A wavelet attention fusion and YOLO based approach for robust multi scale hand pose estimation
Authors: Bhavana Sharma, Jeebananda Panda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract3D Hand pose estimation in multi-view 3D datasets remains a fundamental challenge in computer vision due to the complexity of human skeletal articulation, partial occlusions, variations in hand appearance and differences in human physique. Traditional models often suffer from loss of fine-grained details and poor generalization under real-world conditions. To address these limitations, we propose a novel framework for real-time 3D hand pose estimation, which integrates a Wavelet Attention Fusion Module (WAFM) with modified YOLOv11. Here, WAFM leverages wavelet decomposition to preserve high-frequency edge details while enhancing low-frequency semantic features, enabling accurate reconstruction of fine joint articulations. Our architecture extends the YOLOv11 framework by embedding its detection outputs into a multi-scale tempo-spatial backbone composed of an Efficient Net spatial extractor and Gated Recurrent Units to maintain temporal consistency across sequential frames. A specialized feature fusion neck, combining Feature Pyramid Networks and Path Aggregation Networks, ensures that high-level semantics are successfully integrated with low-level spatial details at multiple resolutions. Finally, multi-head processes these features to produce scale-aware pose estimations with improved accuracy under occlusion and varying orientations. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly improves accuracy under heavy occlusion and varying orientations, achieving state-of-the-art performance in both precision and inference speed for real-time 3D hand pose reconstruction of Ego Hand dataset (Dataset I) and Senz3D dataset (Dataset II).Keywords: Hand gesture recognition, Wavelet Attention Fusion Module, Modified YOLOv11, Time-frequency featuresSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Recent works have shown that videos or multi-view images carry rich information regarding the hand, allowing for the development of more robust hand pose estimation systems. The human computer interface (HCI) involves the most common communication with speech and hand gestures. Hand form perception is one of the most crucial factors to take into account when trying to increase HGR ability. Conventional methods for hand gesture identification frequently depend on depth or color picture information. These techniques, however, may not be as good at capturing dexterous hand movements or the intricate spatial interactions between hand joints. Recent research demonstrates that multi-view images and video sequences provide richer spatial and temporal cues than single-frame inputs, enabling more robust hand pose estimation under dynamic conditions. Numerous constraints such as backdrop complexity, rotation, occlusion, scale, position and speed arise in real-time applications because of the spatiotemporal fluctuations of physical movements and the apathetic nature of the hand. In this study, we addressed the problem of identifying and localizing hand landmarks, like joints, phalanges and fingertips. This is an important first step towards calculating 2D key points to a 3D model of the object for hand pose from digital images, which could decrease the effectiveness disparity between existing vision-based methods. Our proposed model is a single-shot algorithm for multi-scale hand shapes. It incorporates a spatial attention mechanism that focuses on relevant parts of the image, leading to more precise object localization. For the computational time perspective, the different versions of YOLO reported computational times that were as near to the real-time system with the least amount of performance loss1. The suggested detector faces challenges when motion blur is present. To overcome low resolution imaging limitations, the Pre-processing with Stationary Wavelet Transform (SWT) aids in detail enhancement, supporting better detection and classification. The Wavelet Attention Fusion Module (WAFM), which combines attention mechanisms and SWT to improve feature representation. The conceptual differentiation is that WAFM is not just a filter; it’s a selector, whereas conventional wavelet methods typically use wavelets as a fixed pre-processing step or a simple replacement for pooling. It treats all high-frequency data equally. Proposed WAFM acts as a frequency-adaptive filter. It recognizes that in hand pose estimation, “noise” (like background texture) and “features” (like finger edges) both exist in the high-frequency bands (HH). WAFM uses an attention mechanism to “gate” the coefficients, passing the finger edges while suppressing the background noise. Our proposed architecture fundamentally departs from the standard YOLOv11 by replacing the static CSPDarknet backbone with a Multi-scale Tempo-Spatial Backbone. While the ordinary YOLOv11 relies on C3k2 and C2PSA modules to extract purely spatial features from individual frames, our model integrates Efficient Net for dynamic, uniformly scaled spatial extraction and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) for temporal modeling. In contrast to the standard YOLOv11 neck, which utilizes a streamlined C3k2-based architecture, our proposed model implements a sophisticated FPN-PAN (Feature Pyramid Network - Path Aggregation Network) hybrid architecture integrated with Efficient Net feature maps. While the ordinary YOLOv11focuses on high-speed feature fusion via localized blocks, our modified neck extracts intermediate feature maps directly from multiple Efficient Net levels to feed into a top-down FPN pathway, ensuring multi-scale representations are preserved. We further enhance this by applying a bottom-up PAN pathway, which re-propagates low-level spatial detail to higher semantic layers. While the standard YOLOv11 uses a decoupled, anchor-free detection head optimized for a fixed set of COCO-based tasks, our proposed model features a custom multi-scale head specifically re-engineered for sequential hand gestures. Ordinary YOLOv11 predicts from three static feature scales using C3k2 blocks to handle general object sizes; however, our modified head taps into the high-resolution feature maps from the Efficient Net backbone to preserve the fine-grained spatial details of fingertips. Crucially, while the standard YOLOv11 head produces generic bounding boxes and confidence scores in a single forward pass, our modified architecture feeds these multi-scale maps into a temporal aggregation layer (GRUs) before the final output. For a modified YOLOv11implementation, this fusion produces the output without information loss and recreates a high-resolution detailed image with improved edge detection2. This study introduces a novel spatiotemporal wavelet-attention–enhanced YOLOv11 framework for real-time hand pose estimation. Unlike existing approaches, the proposed method integrates spatial, temporal, and frequency-domain modeling within a single-shot detection pipeline. The key novelties of this work are:

Related work

Hand Gesture Recognition (HGR) has been extensively studied due to its importance in human–computer interaction and sign language interpretation systems. Existing approaches can be broadly categorized into vision-based methods, sensor-based methods, and deep learning–based approaches. Early vision-based HGR techniques primarily relied on hand-crafted features and skin color segmentation using different color spaces such as RGB, HSV, and YCbCr to identify hand regions. Color spaces were used by researchers to identify different skin regions and produce hand proposals in response to the changes in illumination3–5. Changes in lighting conditions, shadows and camera characteristics can significantly alter skin color distributions across different color spaces, leading to unstable segmentation, false positives, and poor generalization in real-world scenarios. Shape descriptors, contour-based features and motion cues were later incorporated to improve discrimination, but generalization remained a major challenge. With the advent of deep learning, CNN-based models became dominant in HGR due to their ability to learn discriminative spatial features directly from data6,7. Several works leveraged 2D CNNs for static gesture recognition, while 3D CNNs and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), such as LSTMs and GRUs, were employed to model temporal dynamics in dynamic gestures8,9. For recognizing dense poses, author designed a dense pose model in which segmentation and classification are done by using multi-head R-CNN and deep learning model with Grasshopper-Optimization10. Similarly, position-sensitive score map solved a problem to accomplish with translation-invariance in image classification and object detection but with pose variations and multi person environment it is not successful11. Although these architectures achieved notable performance improvements, they often struggled to capture long-range dependencies efficiently and required large computational resources. Over the past few years, position estimation has developed to include hand, face, head and whole-body joint detection12. Therefore, hybrid models like combination of Fourier convolutional neural network and probability density function with long short-term memory (LSTM) is utilized13–15. LSTM has overfitting issue, therefore, the transformer based deep learning model are used to overcome this problem16. More recently, transformer-based architectures have gained attention in HGR for their ability to model global contextual relationships through self-attention mechanisms. Vision Transformer (ViT) based models and spatiotemporal transformers have demonstrated superior performance in capturing complex hand motion patterns across frames. Nevertheless, the high computational complexity and latency associated with self-attention pose challenges for real-time and edge deployment. These studies enhance performance using active learning, attention mechanisms, and synthetic data; however, they often incur increased model complexity and dependence on large or augmented datasets17–21. These researches achieve strong spatiotemporal and multi-scale feature modeling, but their complex architectures and higher computational cost limit efficiency and real-time deployment, especially on resource-constrained platforms22–25. Recent architectures such as26–29 are advance long-range dependency modeling through state-space models, hybrid CNN–Transformer designs, and large-kernel representations; however, their increased architectural complexity and training cost pose challenges for lightweight deployment and real-time applications. In parallel, diffusion-based models and hybrid architectures have been explored to enhance robustness against occlusion and noise by modeling gesture recognition as a probabilistic refinement process. While these methods improve reliability, their iterative inference process often increases computational overhead. Deep convolutional neural networks and Only Look Once (YOLO) models, it is possible to detect and track the bare hand owing to speed or acquisition equipment but have challenges with accuracy30,1. To enhance spatiotemporal representation of hand, researchers presented an object-aware decode where the model is predicting hand and object positions as well as the semantic label of the items in order to improve object-awareness during training31–33. Recent advancements in person search have shifted toward specialized feature refinement, ranging from adaptive spatial and task decoupling to mitigate branch interference for robust environmental adaptation where optimized filter parameters are utilized to enhance micro-texture discrimination34–37. Tracking of the hand and its relative positions become increasingly challenging when the hand is movable or flexible. Recent advances further emphasize hybrid wavelet strategies for example, DWT–SWT fusion and stationary wavelet residual networks. combined with learning-based models to address non-stationarity, suppress noise and enhance discriminative features in medical imaging, pedestrian detection and myoelectric pattern recognition38–42. All methods prove that SWT is beneficial in terms of amplification and retains additional content. Despite these advances, achieving a balance between accuracy, robustness, and computational efficiency remains an open challenge in HGR, particularly for real-time and resource-constrained applications. These limitations motivate the development of models that can effectively capture spatiotemporal dependencies while maintaining efficiency and scalability. This inspired us to implement a deep learning model with hand edge characteristics by a combination of wavelet transform with modified YOLOv11model.

Proposed methodology

All data used in this study were obtained from publicly available datasets, namely the Ego Hands dataset and the Senz3D dataset, which were originally collected by their respective creators in accordance with applicable ethical guidelines and regulations. As this study involves no direct interaction or intervention with human participants, institutional ethical approval and informed consent were not required.

The provided architectural diagram illustrates a multi-stage deep learning framework designed for 3D hand pose estimation and classification. The process begins with a Pre-processing stage where input data is decomposed and reconstructed to generate Enhanced Features, specifically focused on retaining multi-frequency signals (MFS). These features are fed into a Backbone consisting of an Efficient Net CNN for spatial feature extraction, followed by average pooling and GRU layers to capture temporal consistency, ultimately leading to dense layers for initial predictions. The Neck of the architecture utilizes a fusion of Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) and Path Aggregation Networks (PAN), combining top-down and bottom-up pathways through up-sampling and down-sampling to create a robust Multiscale feature map. Finally, the system employs a Multi-head Classification structure, utilizing parallel convolutional layers to produce scale-aware predictions and calculate bounding box losses for precise hand reconstruction. In the following sections, the details of each step are elaborated.

The suggested work is shown in Fig. 3 as a sequence of implementation. The proposed HGR framework for recognizing hand gestures is made for categorizing the activities of two datasets. The motivation for introducing SWT based WAFM in our proposed framework arise from the inherent limitations of conventional feature extraction when dealing with subtle gestures under real world conditions. However, not all wavelet sub bands contribute equally to gesture semantics. This motivates the WAFM, which adaptively assigns importance to each frequency component using attention, enhancing informative bands and suppressing noise sensitive or redundant features. Create a dual-input model to extract richer features by combining spatial and temporal features and gives better performance in variable lighting, occlusion or noisy environments. Standard convolutional backbones primarily operate in the spatial domain and may overlook discriminative frequency domain cues such as edges, contours, and micro-motions that are critical for accurate gesture differentiation. To address this, SWT is motivated by its ability to preserve spatial resolution while decomposing features into multiple frequency bands, enabling the model to capture both low frequency semantic structure and high frequency details without losing localization information. A single-level wavelet decomposition offers a favorable trade-off between feature expressiveness and inference efficiency, aligning with the goal of real-time and edge-oriented deployment. Deeper wavelet levels further decompose already down sampled representations, which may lead to loss of fine-grained spatial details critical for gesture discrimination, particularly in both datasets, where gestures are subtle and localized.

Multi-scale tempo-spatial backbone modeling

A Multi-scale Tempo-Spatial Backbone Modeling where the Efficient Net and GRU play an important role to find out the spatial and temporal information of features as shown in Fig. 4. The Compound Scaling of the Backbone (Efficient Net) does not scale dimensions arbitrarily. The uniformly scaled network has width (w), depth (d) and resolution (r) with a set of fixed scaling coefficients. This reduces a large tensor into a single feature vector. The empirically determined constants for EfficientNet-B0 are that is 1.2 (Depth: adding more layers for receptive field), that is 1.1 (Width: adding more channels for fine-grained features) and has value of 1.15 (Resolution: increasing input image size). After passing through Efficient Net, apply global average pooling to reduce the spatial dimensions of the extracted features, which outputs a fixed-size feature vector in terms of Height (H), Width (W) and Channels(C). GRU layers for Sequence Modeling are used for handling sequential data like time series, text or even video frames, which is useful for temporal dynamics. The time-series sequence input data is formatted accordingly so that feature vectors from multiple frames can be passed sequentially into the GRU layers. Hybrid path feature pyramid network

The neck fuses multi-scale features through a bidirectional flow. If {C3, C4, C5} are the intermediate outputs from Efficient Net, the FPN (Top-Down) Formulation is Pi = Conv (Ci) + Upsample {Pi+1}. FPN extracted and propagated multi-scale feature maps, which are refined through the top-down pathway and PAN can then be applied to the output of the FPN to further aggregate features through both bottom-up and top-down pathways, enhancing the ability to localize objects more accurately. The PAN (Bottom-Up) Formulation is Ni = Conv (Pi) + Downsample{Ni−1}. We improved scale adaptation by FPN that features at multiple scales are well-represented, improving object detection across various sizes. PAN complements FPN by improving spatial information, leading to better object localization. Combining both top-down and bottom-up paths enhances feature extraction, making it more robust for complex tasks like object detection and instance segmentation as shown in Fig. 3.

Dataset description

For implementation, we used two publicly available datasets are used: Ego hand dataset and Senz3D dataset. There are 48 distinct videos of a first-person viewpoint, typically using head-mounted or chest-mounted cameras where hands often appear near the image center and exhibit limited pose diversity compared to third-person datasets. The real-world egocentric hand images captured using wearable cameras with a relatively wide field of view, resulting in frequent motion blur, partial occlusions and scale variations. The dataset includes significant variability in background clutter, hand–object interactions and illumination conditions, making it representative of realistic, unconstrained environments. Annotations are provided as hand bounding boxes or masks, which are generally reliable but coarse with respect to fine-grained pose or finger articulation. In contrast, the Senz3D dataset is a synthetic RGB-D dataset for hand and object interactions, which is generated in a fully controlled virtual environment. It has fine-grained hand configurations and finger articulation. Supported by consistent lighting, minimal background clutter and relatively stable hand positioning43,44. Computational efficiency optimizations

To achieve genuine real-time performance, the system leverages a multi-tier preprocessing strategy that minimizes latency at every stage of the pipeline. By integrating the NVIDIA DALI library, we offload image augmentation and resizing to the GPU, effectively doubling the throughput compared to standard OpenCV CPU processing. This hardware acceleration is complemented by a highly optimized data ingestion layer using tf.data.Dataset, which utilizes prefetch(buffer_size=AUTOTUNE) to overlap model execution with data preparation and num_parallel_calls = 8 to distribute CPU tasks across multiple cores. To handle the high-resolution sequences of the Ego dataset, memory-mapped file reading is employed; this allows the system to access specific video frames directly from the disk as needed, preventing RAM bottlenecks and ensuring that memory consumption remains stable regardless of dataset size as shown in Table 7.

Conclusion

This research successfully addressed the fundamental challenges of 3D hand pose estimation like skeletal complexity, self-occlusion and variability in hand appearance across multi-view datasets. We presented a novel Modified YOLOv11 driven framework for real-time 3D hand pose estimation with integration of the Wavelet Attention Fusion Module (WAFM). The integration of a Multi-Scale Tempo-Spatial Backbone pairing the spatial efficiency of Efficient Net with the temporal memory of Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), effectively mitigated the “jitter” common in frame-by-frame detection. The Hybrid FPN-PAN structure enables our model to preserve fine-grained details, capture temporal dynamics and fuse semantic and spatial features at multiple resolutions. Extensive experiments on the datasets validate the effectiveness of our approach. Our model achieves state-of-the-art performance with classification accuracies, precision, F1-score, Recall and real-time inference speeds. These results confirm that the proposed framework achieves a strong balance between accuracy and efficiency, making it highly suitable for real-world applications such as gesture recognition, sign language translation, augmented reality and human–computer interaction systems. Our results indicate a competing result in comparison of SOTA methods present for HGR. The model scored 97.49% Precision, 97.59% F1 score, 97.49% sensitivity, 97.70% specificity, 97.63% recall, and 97.20% accuracy of Ego hand dataset. For Senz3D dataset, the model scored 98.32% Precision, 98.48% F1 score, 98.32% sensitivity, 96.57% specificity, 98.65% recall and 97.50% accuracy.

However, the model’s sensitivity to extreme motion blur and low-light conditions, where high-frequency wavelet coefficients can become noisy, slightly degrade joint accuracy. For future work, this framework by incorporating depth or infrared modalities for improved robustness under varying lighting, exploring anchor-free YOLO variants for more flexible key point detection and deploying lightweight versions for edge devices such as AR/VR headsets and mobile platforms.

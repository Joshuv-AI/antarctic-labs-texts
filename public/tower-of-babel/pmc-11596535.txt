Serum homocysteine and left ventricular hypertrophy in adults with chronic kidney disease: A case–control study
Authors: Yanping Lei, Rui Liu, Yue Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Hyperhomocysteinemia (serum homocysteine concentration > 15 μmol/L) is of high prevalence in chronic kidney disease (CKD). And myocardial hypertrophy is a common complication of CKD. Given that both hyperhomocysteinemia and cardiac hypertrophy have an association with CKD, we hypothesized that high level of plasma homocysteine (Hcy) is associated with a higher prevalence of ventricular hypertrophy(LVH) in adults with CKD. The registration number of the case-control study is ChiCTR2200064834. The information of inpatients with CKD including Echocardiograms and analysis of plasma Hcy concentrations were collected. We performed linear and logistic regression to investigate the association of plasma Hcy with left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) (LVMI ≥ 95th percentile), adjusted for levels of hemoglobin, ferritin, cystatin C and β-adrenergic blocker therapy. Further, a stratified analysis of the relationship between plasma Hcy and LVH was carried out according to eGFR. The case records for 1068 inpatients with CKD were collected. After data soring and case-control matching, there were 374 samples screened for statistical analysis. Univariate logistic regression indicated a high level of serum Hcy had an association with LVH (OR, 1.16; 95% CI, 1.11–1.20). Finally, multivariable logistic regression suggested that hyperhomocysteinemia was independently associated with LVH (OR, 1.14; 95% CI, 1.10–1.19) after adjustment for hemoglobin, ferritin, cystatin C, and β-adrenergic receptor blocker therapy. We constructed a predicting model including the variable of Hcy for cardiac hypertrophy in CKD. The model had an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.86 (95% CI: 0.82–0.89, P < .001). The decision curve analysis (DCA) showed a superior net clinical benefit of model with Hcy over model without Hcy. Elevated level of serum Hcy is closely associated with LVH in adults with CKD.

1. Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is always accompanied by Left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) indicating a poor prognosis for the disease.[1] And, LVH imposes an increased mortality risk on CKD, and condition will get worse without treatment for LVH.[2] It is reported that cardiovascular disease accounts for more than 50% of all-cause deaths in CKD.[3] Given that, it is crucial to intervene development and progress of LVH for patients with CKD. LVH occurs in the early stage of CKD and is developing and progressing throughout CKD.[4] Hence, screening the factors aggravating LVH in CKD is necessary to exploit a potent strategy for inhibiting LVH, to reduce death risk caused by cardiovascular disease.

Homocysteine (Hcy), as a byproduct of methionine metabolism, is increased in the plasma of CKD, so Hcy is considered to be a uremic toxin.[5] Moreover, Hyperhomocysteinemia is associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular events.[6] Further, the rising level of Hcy causes injury to cardiomyocytes.[7] Increasing evidence indicates that the treatment of lowering Hcy might protect patients afflicted with Hyperhomocysteinemia against cardiovascular disease.[8] However, more study is warranted to investigate the relationship between the high levels of plasma Hcy and LVH in CKD. In this study, we hypothesized that Hyperhomocysteinemia is independently associated with a high prevalence of LVH in CKD adults. We also evaluate the value of Hyperhomocysteinemia for predicting LVH in CKD.

2. Methods

2.1. Study design

Risk factors of cardiac hypertrophy in CKD (ChiCTR2200064834) Study, a retrospective observational case-control study, enrolled inpatients aged 18 to 75 years with CKD in The First Affiliated Hospital of University of South China located at the south of Hunan province. The specific criteria for inclusion and exclusion are comprehensively outlined in Supplement File 1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N987. The diagnostic criteria for CKD is that the eGFR values from 2 tests taken at intervals of more than 3 months are <90 mL/min*1.73 m2. The study design, methods, and the criteria of inclusion and exclusion have been described in Supplement File 1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N987. The study was approved by the clinical ethics committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of University of South China (Ethics approval number: 2022ll0811001 and approval date: August 11, 2022). Informed consent was obtained from each participant’s family member with identity of legal guardian.

2.2. Participants

This Study enrolled 1068 participants who were hospitalized from January of 2021 to December of 2021. The information of patients’ case records were collected, including age, gender, height, weight, plasma Hcy, duration of CKD, time of hemodialysis, concomitant disease (hypertension and diabetes mellitus), hemoglobin, serum albumin, serum calcium, serum phosphorus, parathyroid hormone(PTH), serum creatinine, eGFR, serum iron, ferritin, β2-microglobulin, cystatin C, mean systolic and diastolic pressure, drug therapy (angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin receptor blockers, β-adrenergic receptor blockers) and data of echocardiogram. The patients whose data mentioned above is deficient were excluded. Participants were divided into case and control groups based on the presence or absence of LVH. And the criterion for LVH is listed in the section of echocardiography. Case subjects were matched up with control subjects through gender, age and eGFR. After matching, 354 patients were included for further analysis in this case-control study. The procedure of data collecting and sorting is present in Figure 1.

Scheme for data collecting and sorting.

2.3. Echocardiography

Cardiac structure and function were assessed by Doppler echocardiography (Phillip CX50) with an adult transducer (S5-1). Images were standardized to the short-axis view at the LV mid-papillary level. Two-dimensional images for 3 sequential cardiac cycles were recorded. Posterior wall diastolic thickness (LVPWd), LV end-diastolic diameter (LVEDd), LV end-systolic diameter (LVESd) and interventricular septum diastolic thickness (IVSd) were measured. LV systolic function parameters such as the LV fractional shortening (LVFS) and the LV ejection fraction (LVEF) were calculated. Left ventricular mass index (LVMI) was calculated according to the following formula: LVM(g) = 0.8*1.04*[(IVST + PWT + LVDd)3-LVDd3] + 0.6 and LVMI (g/m2) = LVM/BSA (Body surface area). The criteria for diagnosing LVH is as follows: LVMI > 125g/m2 in male or LVMI > 120g/m2 in female.

2.4. Statistical analyses

Baseline characteristics were compared using the χ2 test and the Mann–Whitney U (Wilcoxon) test. CKD patients with LVH were compared with CKD patients without LVH. Univariate analysis was implemented by binary logistic regression. Crude OR (odds ratios) with 95% CI (confidence interval) were present. Based on univariate analysis, the variables with a significant difference were screened for multivariate analysis. Conditional logistic regression models were used to calculate adjusted OR with 95% CI for the case group to the control group. Since eGFR could be a potential confounder, we conducted a stratification analysis according to stage of CKD. The 2-sided tailed p values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Data was collected and sorted with Excel (Microsoft Office 2007). all analyses were performed on SPSS statistics (SPSS, version 27.0.0, IBM Corporation, USA) and R language (version 4.4.0).

3. Results

3.1. Characteristics of patients and disease

This case-control study had enrolled 1068 inpatients from November of 2021 to December of 2022. Due to the deficiency of information in the medical records, only 498 participants were included in the next analysis. The overall prevalence of LVH was 37.75%. And the incidence of hyperhomocysteinemia was 54.62%. In order to investigate the association between hyperhomocysteinemia and LVH, there are 354 participants screened after matching with gender, age and eGFR, namely, 177 LVH cases and 177 controls in each group (Fig. 1). The preliminary statistical analysis of clinical and demographic data reflected that the median of serum Hcy concentration (22.00 μmol/L) in patients with LVH is higher, compared with that (14.04 μmol/L) of patients without LVH. Similarly, the probability of hyperhomocysteinemia occurrence (92.55%) is higher in patients with LVH than that (31.61%) in patients without LVH. The mean age of the full population was 57 years old (standard deviation: 8.46). male participants account for 66.67% of the full population.

The characteristics of the LVH case group and the control group were present in Table 1. Duration of CKD and time of hemodialysis had no significant difference between these 2 groups. However, the median of plasma Hcy level in patients with LVH is 22.00 μmol/L; in contrast, the median of plasma Hcy level in patients without LVH is 14.40 μmol/L. And that difference is statistically significant. The mean concentration of blood hemoglobin (89.00g/L) in the control group is higher than that (79.00g/L) in the case group (P < .001). Similarly, patients without LVH had a relatively high level of plasma ferritin compared to patients suffering from LVH in CKD (P < .001). As to drug therapy, the number of patients getting β-adrenergic receptor blocker therapy in the case group was less than the number in the control group.

3.2. High level of plasma Hcy had an association with LVH in CKD

Variables with significant difference between the case group and the control group in Table 1 were incorporated into the univariate logistic analysis of the correlation between each variable and LVH. As Table 2 shows, the patients with high plasma Hcy had high odds of suffering from LVH in CKD (Crude OR, 1.16; 95% CI, 1.11–1.20). And low Hb and low ferritin showed significantly high odds of having LVH. In regard to characteristics of CKD, patients, who possessed the characteristics such as high systolic pressure, high plasma PTH, and low eGFR, would have a higher risk of LVH. In terms of medication, β-adrenergic receptor blockers still exerted a protective effect on the LVH (Crude OR, 0.31; 95% CI, 0.20–0.48) (Table 2).

3.3. High level of plasma Hcy was still correlated with LVH in CKD after adjusted for Hb, ferritin, cystatin C, and treatment of β-adrenergic receptor blocker

Further, variables with the significant differences in the univariate logistic analysis above were brought into the multivariate logistic analysis. we finally identified 5 variables independently associated with cardiac hypertrophy including Hcy, Hb, ferritin, cystatin C, β-adrenergic receptor blocker (Table 2). After adjustment for Hb, ferritin, cystatin C and treatment of β-adrenergic receptor blocker, a high level of plasma Hcy exhibited correlation to LVH in CKD (Adjusted OR, 1.14; 95% CI, 1.10–1.19) (Table 2). After adjustment for other variables in the multivariate logistical regression, low Hb, low ferritin and high cystatin C respectively showed a significant odd ratio in suffering from LVH in CKD (Table 2). Drug therapy of β-adrenergic receptor blockers remains a protective factor for cardiac hypertrophy in CKD after adjustment for other variables (Adjusted OR, 0.28; 95% CI, 0.16–0.48) (Table 2).

3.4. Multivariate logistic analysis stratified by eGFR category

Given that the effect of eGFR on LVH, we performed hierarchical analysis according to eGFR. Also, the results showed consistency with statistical analysis without stratification. As presented in Table 3, in the stage of CKD2 and CKD3, high level of Hcy did not exhibited correlation to LVH. Nevertheless, Hcy level is positively related to morbidity of LVH in stage of CKD4 (Adjusted OR, 1.08; 95% CI, 1.04–1.11), CKD5 (Adjusted OR, 1.10; 95% CI, 1.02–1.18) and hemodialysis (HD) (Adjusted OR, 1.06; 95% CI, 1.03–1.09). Similarly, β-adrenergic receptor blockers remained a protective factor against LVH in most CKD stage including CKD3 (Adjusted OR, 0.36; 95% CI, 0.20–0.66), CKD5 (Adjusted OR, 0.15; 95% CI, 0.05–0.49) and HD (Adjusted OR, 0.24; 95% CI, 0.11–0.52).

3.5. Nomogram construction, validation and clinical value

We constructed a nomogram for cardiac hypertrophy in CKD according to the 5 variables included in the multivariate logistic regression (Fig. 2). On this basis, we constructed a novel model and the formula is as follows: Logit(P) = −5.783 + 0.134 × Hcy − 0.018 × Hb − 0.001 × ferritin + 0.218 × cystatin C − 1.312 × β-blocker (Kappa = 0.526, P < .001, Nagelkerke’s R2 = 0.50). The model had an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.86 (95% CI: 0.82–0.89, P < .001) (Fig. 2). Using the ROC curve, the maximum of Youden index was identified with a sensitivity of 79.10% and specificity of 77.80% (Fig. 3). The calibration curve of the nomogram was generated through bootstrap1000 internal validation. The calibration curves of the nomogram showed high consistencies between the predicted and observed values and the mean absolute error is 0.03. The calibration curve showed a good fit during internal validation (Fig. 4). DCA of the nomogram was performed. The clinical benefits of the nomograms were compared between baseline model and full model. Baseline model contained 4 variables including Hb, Ferritin, Cystatin C, β-blocker. full model contained 5 variables including Hcy, Hb, Ferritin, Cystatin C, β-blocker. DCA curves showed that the nomogram of full model could better predict cardiac hypertrophy than the baseline model in CKD, as it added more net benefits compared with full model (Fig. 5).

4. Discussion

In the present study, we collect data on clinical features and basic characteristics of patients with CKD complicated with LVH or not. Intriguingly, there exist significant differences in distribution for a series of clinical features, such as Hb, ferritin, systolic pressure, PTH and cystatin C, between case group (the LVH)and the control group (the non-LVH). To our surprise, the concentration of plasma Hcy is higher in patients diagnosed with LVH compared with patients without LVH.

Both the univariate model and the multivariate model reveal that LVH is related to low Hb. It is recognized that CKD, cardiac dysfunction and anemia constitute a triangle of death.[9] It is conceivable that anemia can cause a heavy burden on the heart and promote cardiac hypertrophy, subsequently increasing the risk of death.[10] Hence, anemia probably acts as an independent risk factor for cardiac hypertrophy in CKD.[11,12] Based on that, a therapeutic strategy of improving anemia is a potent treatment for the condition of CKD-induced heart lesions.[10,13] What’s more, hypertension is closely associated with LVH in CKD, especially for the rise in systolic pressure. So, it remains an effective measure to resist myocardial hypertrophy by reducing the pressure load on the heart.[14] However, multivariate regression analysis reveals no significant correlation between systolic pressure and myocardial hypertrophy. That may necessitate an analysis involving a more extensive sample size. In terms of treatment measures, our study indicates that β-adrenergic receptor blockers remain the efficient therapeutic drug against cardiac hypertrophy in CKD. That is in favor of previous findings that β-adrenergic receptor blockers could ameliorate cardiac dysfunction, and consequently reduce the risk of death from heart disease.[15]

Logically, the severity of CKD is relative to the degree of LVH.[16] As is expected, univariate regression analysis shows the factors reflecting the severity of CKD, like PTH, β2-microglobulin and cystatin C, are positively linked to LVH; and eGFR has a negative correlation with myocardial hypertrophy. However, the duration of dialysis treatment has no effect on the occurrence of LVH.

Of our findings, the results about Hcy is notable. The results show the mean level of plasma Hcy (20.50 μmol/L) in CKD is much higher than 10 μmol/L (95th percentile) since kidney dysfunction disturbs the metabolism of Hcy.[17] Increasing evidence suggests that Hyperhomocysteinemia is closely related to the risk of cerebrovascular accident.[18,19] The mechanisms underlying Hcy-inducing injury contains oxidative stress, DNA damage, protein homocysteinylation, autophagy, apoptosis.[20,21] therefore, Hcy is recognized as a uremic toxin in CKD, as previous research reported.[5] Also, basic research provides a clue that Hcy might cause sustainable insult to the heart.[22,23] Likewise, our study confirms a high level of plasma Hcy has an association with myocardial hypertrophy. Among the clinical characteristics, the distribution of serum Hcy level is not equal between the case group and the control group. Univariate model regression reveals that increased Hcy level is accompanied by elevated incidence of LVH. Given that CKD promotes to the deposition of Hcy[24] and eGFR might act as a confounding factor, eGFR was included as a matching factor in the case-control pairing. Then, we performed a multivariant logistic regression model. High plasma Hcy still exhibited significant correlation with LVH after adjustment for Hb, ferritin, cystatin C and treatment of β-adrenergic receptor blockers. In view of the impact of CKD severity on statistical analysis, we further carried out stratification analysis according to stage of CKD. Similarly, increased serum Hcy still exhibits correlation to LVH. Based on multivariate regression, we have constructed a prediction model for myocardial hypertrophy. The AUC and calibration curve indicate that the predicting model possesses good prediction performance. Furthermore, the decision curve analysis (DCA) highlights the substantial significance of Hcy in predicting CKD-related myocardial hypertrophy.

5. Conclusion

Conclusively, we demonstrate herein that elevated concentration of plasma Hcy has an association with LVH in CKD. And hyperhomocysteinemia is closely related to myocardial hypertrophy independent of another factors including Hb, ferritin, cystatin and treatment of β-adrenergic receptor blocker. Hence, hyperhomocysteinemia acts as an predictor for cardiac hypertrophy in CKD.

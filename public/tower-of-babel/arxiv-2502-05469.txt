Data-Driven Distributionally Robust Mixed-Integer Control through Lifted Control Policy
Xutao Ma, Chao Ning, Wenli Du, Yang Shi

Abstract
This paper investigates the finite-horizon distributionally robust mixed-integer control (DRMIC) of uncertain linear systems. However, deriving an optimal causal feedback control policy to this DRMIC problem is computationally formidable for most ambiguity sets. To address the computational challenge, we propose a novel distributionally robust lifted control policy (DR-LCP) method to derive a high-quality approximate solution to this DRMIC problem for a rich class of Wasserstein metric-based ambiguity sets, including the Wasserstein ambiguity set and its variants. In theory, we analyze the asymptotic performance and establish a tight non-asymptotic bound of the proposed method. In numerical experiments, the proposed DR-LCP method empirically demonstrates superior performance compared with existing methods in the literature.

It is widely acknowledged that deriving an optimal control policy is computationally prohibitive for most DRC problems, due to the need to optimize over the infinite-dimensional space of all the admissible control policies. To address this computational challenge, approximate solution methods have been developed in previous research. Using a parameterized class of feedback control policies is a frequently adopted method to address dynamic control problems

To fill the research gaps, we propose a DR-LCP method by using the lifted control policy (LCP) to solve finite-horizon mixed-integer DRC problems under a class of Wasserstein-type ambiguity sets.

Moreover, we develop an equivalent reformulation technique to derive the best LCP of the DRC problems under several Wasserstein-type ambiguity sets (including the standard Wasserstein ambiguity set) without any relaxation.
Numerical experiments on a classic inventory control problem fully verify the superior performance of the proposed DR-LCP method compared with existing methods in the literature.

II
Problem Statement

We consider an uncertain linear system with both continuous and integer controls, given as follows.

{\boldsymbol{x}_{t+1}=\boldsymbol{A}_{t}\boldsymbol{x}_{t}+\boldsymbol{B}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{t}+\boldsymbol{C}_{t}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}+\boldsymbol{D}_{t}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{t}},

(1)

where
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}

\boldsymbol{u}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}

\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n_{\gamma}}

\boldsymbol{\xi}_{t}\in\varXi_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\xi}}
is the uncertainty in the form of disturbance. System (
1

\begin{gathered}\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{t}\boldsymbol{x}_{t}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{t}\boldsymbol{u}_{t}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{C}}_{t}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}+\tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_{t}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{t}\leq\boldsymbol{q},\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\varXi,\end{gathered}

(2)

\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{t},\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{t},\tilde{\boldsymbol{C}}_{t},\tilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_{t}

Concretely, constraint (

In this paper, we assume that the uncertainty support

Assumption 1
.

The uncertainty support
\varXi

To handle the disturbance of the system, modeling of the joint distribution of

Based on the ambiguity set

J(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\pi})=\max_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathscr{B}}\ \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\sim\mathbb{P}}\Bigg[\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\alpha^{t}c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}^{\pi})\ \Bigg|\ \boldsymbol{x}_{0}=\boldsymbol{x}\Bigg]

(3)

where

Therefore, combining (

\begin{gathered}\min_{\boldsymbol{\pi}\in\Pi}\ \ J(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\pi})\ \ \text{s.t. }(\ref{hard})-(\ref{cost})\end{gathered}

(DRMIC)

where
\Pi
represents a set of causal disturbance feedback policies.

We further define the optimal value of (

The worst-case expectation term

Continous and Integer Lifted Control Policy

Based on the lifting function, the LCP for continuous control

\begin{gathered}{\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]})=\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}\boldsymbol{P}_{V_{t}}G_{[t]}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]})+\boldsymbol{y}_{t}^{0}=\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}\boldsymbol{P}_{V_{t}}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]}^{\ast}+\boldsymbol{y}_{t}^{0}}\\

\boldsymbol{y}_{t}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}

Similarly, the LCP for integer control
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}^{\pi}
is defined as the affine function of the integer part of
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]}^{\ast}

[
31
]
,
i.e.
,

\begin{gathered}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}^{\pi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]})=\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}\boldsymbol{P}_{Q_{t}}G_{[t]}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]})+\boldsymbol{z}_{t}^{0}=\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}\boldsymbol{P}_{Q_{t}}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]}^{\ast}+\boldsymbol{z}_{t}^{0}}\\

\boldsymbol{z}_{t}^{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n_{\gamma}}
.

Remark 2
.

By the construction of the LCP, if the segment number

for all dimensions of the disturbance, i.e., no breakpoint being used, the LCP degenerates to the ACP. If the segment number

, the ACP is a subset of the LCP, and due to the nonlinearity of the lifting function, a family of nonlinear policies for the continuous and integer controls are also included in the LCP. Moreover, using more breakpoints can enrich the content of the LCP, but it will result in a heavier computational burden since optimization over a larger family of control policies is involved.

Based on expressions (

Definition 3

.

With breakpoints
\boldsymbol{w}=(w_{t,i,j},t\in[\mathcal{T}],i\in[n_{\xi}],j\in[p_{t,i}-1])

\displaystyle{\begin{aligned} \Pi^{{u}}&({\boldsymbol{w}})=\{\pi^{u}=(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}^{\pi}(\cdot),\cdots,\boldsymbol{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(\cdot))|\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]})\\

By optimizing over the LCP, the (

IV
Performance Analysis

By Definition
3
, the LCP space is determined by the breakpoints
\boldsymbol{w}
, and when the number of breakpoints increases, more control policies are contained in the LCP space. Therefore, it can be expected that using more breakpoints will lead to better performance.

In this section, we will analyze this breakpoint-performance relation and answer the following two questions:

1.

What is the asymptotic performance of the LCP when the number of breakpoints goes to infinity?

2.

When finitely many breakpoints are used, what is the tightest bound for the disparity between this non-asymptotic performance and the asymptotic performance?

To quantify the effectiveness of the breakpoints in segmenting the support, we first define the largest segment
\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})
induced by breakpoints
\boldsymbol{w}
as follows.

Definition 4
.

The largest segment induced by breakpoints
\boldsymbol{w}
is defined as

\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})=\max_{t\in[\mathcal{T}],i\in[n_{\xi}],j\in[p_{i,j}]}\{w_{t,i,j}-w_{t,i,j-1}\}.

(21)

With this notion, the asymptotic performance naturally refers to the performance when
\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})
goes to 0.

We then define the distance between continuous control policies in Definition
5
.

Definition 5

(Distance between continuous control policies)
.

Given two continuous policies
\pi^{u}_{1}
and
\pi^{u}_{2}
, their distance is defined as

d(\pi^{u}_{1},\pi^{u}_{2})=\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{u}}\|{u}^{\pi_{1}}_{t,j}-{u}^{\pi_{2}}_{t,j}\|_{\infty},

(22)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is the supremum norm over function space and
\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi}=(u_{t,1}^{\pi},\cdots,u_{t,n_{u}}^{\pi})
.

Definition 6

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}^{u}=\Bigg\{\pi^{u}=(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}^{\pi}(\cdot),\cdots,\boldsymbol{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(\cdot))\Bigg|\forall t\in[\mathcal{T}],\forall j\in[n_{u}],

\displaystyle{u}_{t,j}^{\pi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]})=\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}f_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i}),f_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\in C(\varXi_{t^{\prime},i})\Bigg\},

(23)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}^{\gamma}=\Bigg\{\pi^{\gamma}=(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}^{\pi}(\cdot),\cdots,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(\cdot))\Bigg|\forall t\in[\mathcal{T}],\forall j\in[n_{\gamma}],

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{t,j}^{\pi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]})=\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}f_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i}),f_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\in\emph{PInt}(\varXi_{t^{\prime},i})\Bigg\},

(24)

where
C(\varXi_{t^{\prime},i})
denotes the continuous function space and
\emph{PInt}(\varXi_{t^{\prime},i})
denotes the space of piecewise integer functions, i.e.,

\begin{gathered}\emph{PInt}(\varXi_{t^{\prime},i})=\{f(\cdot)|\exists{l}_{t,i}=w_{0}\leq w_{1}\leq\cdots\leq w_{n-1}\leq w_{n}={v}_{t,i},\\
\emph{s.t.}\forall i\in[N],f(\cdot)\emph{isanintegerconstanton}[w_{i-1},w_{i})\}.\end{gathered}

Proposition 2
.

Let
\Pi^{u}_{\ast}
and
\Pi^{\gamma}_{\ast}
denote the limits of the lifted control policy space, i.e.,

\displaystyle\Pi^{u}_{\ast}=\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{w}:\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\geq\delta}\Pi^{u}(\boldsymbol{w})=\bigcup_{n=1,\cdots,\infty}\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{w}:\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\geq 1/n}\Pi^{u}(\boldsymbol{w}),

\displaystyle\Pi^{\gamma}_{\ast}=\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{w}:\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\geq\delta}\Pi^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{w})=\bigcup_{n=1,\cdots,\infty}\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{w}:\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\geq 1/n}\Pi^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{w}).

Then,

(i)

\Pi^{\gamma}_{\ast}=\mathfrak{N}^{\gamma}
.

(ii)

When equipped with topology induced by norm in Definition
5
,
\Pi^{u}_{\ast}
is dense in
\mathfrak{N}^{u}
,
i.e.
,

\Pi^{u}_{\ast}\subset\mathfrak{N}^{u}\subset\overline{\Pi^{u}_{\ast}},

(25)

where
\overline{\Pi^{u}_{\ast}}
is the closure of
\Pi^{u}_{\ast}
.

Proof.

By Definition
3
, for
\pi^{u}=(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}^{\pi}(\cdot),\cdots,\boldsymbol{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(\cdot))\in\Pi^{u}(\boldsymbol{w})
and
\pi^{\gamma}=(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}^{\pi}(\cdot),\cdots,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\pi}(\cdot))\in\Pi^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{w})
, they satisfy

Fig. 1:
Illustration of the LCP. (In this example, the support
\varXi_{t^{\prime},i}=[0,3]
is divided by two breakpoints
w_{t^{\prime},i,1}=1
and
w_{t^{\prime},i,2}=2
. As for the control policy, we take
\boldsymbol{y}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}=[2.5,-0.5,-2.5]^{T},{y}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{0}=1.0
for continuous control and
\boldsymbol{z}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}=[-2,1]^{T},{z}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{0}=3
for integer control.)

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}u_{t,j}^{\pi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]})=\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}\Big\{\boldsymbol{y}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{T}\boldsymbol{V}_{t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})+y^{0}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\Big\},\\
\forall t\in[\mathcal{T}],\forall j\in[n_{u}],\end{gathered}

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\gamma_{t,j}^{\pi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{[t]})=\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}\Big\{\boldsymbol{z}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{T}\boldsymbol{Q}_{t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})+z^{0}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\Big\},\\
\forall t\in[\mathcal{T}],\forall j\in[n_{\gamma}],\end{gathered}

where
\boldsymbol{y}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{t^{\prime},i}}
,
y^{0}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\boldsymbol{z}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p_{t^{\prime},i}-1}
, and
z^{0}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By definition of functions
\boldsymbol{V}_{t^{\prime},i}
and
\boldsymbol{Q}_{t^{\prime},i}
(in Definition
1
),
\boldsymbol{y}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{T}\boldsymbol{V}_{t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})+y^{0}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}
is a piecewise linear function on
\varXi_{t^{\prime},j}
and
\boldsymbol{z}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{T}\boldsymbol{Q}_{t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})+z^{0}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}
is a piecewise integer function on
\varXi_{t^{\prime},j}
. To see this result more clearly, we give an illustration of a concrete example in Fig.
1
.

Therefore, lifted control policies are also additive control policies, so

\displaystyle\Pi^{\gamma}_{\ast}\subset\mathfrak{N}^{\gamma},\Pi^{u}_{\ast}\subset\mathfrak{N}^{u}.

(30)

By adjusting the breakpoints
\boldsymbol{w}
,
\boldsymbol{z}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{T}\boldsymbol{Q}_{t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})+z^{0}_{t,j}
can represent any piecewise integer function on
\varXi_{t^{\prime},j}
.

Therefore, (
i
) holds.

For (
ii
),
\forall\pi^{u}_{1}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}
and
\forall\pi^{u}_{2}\in\Pi^{u}(\boldsymbol{w})
, we have

\displaystyle d(\pi^{u}_{1},\pi^{u}_{2})=\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{u}}\|{u}^{\pi_{1}}_{t,j}-{u}^{\pi_{2}}_{t,j}\|_{\infty}=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{u}}\left|\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}\Big\{f_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})-\boldsymbol{y}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{T}\boldsymbol{V}_{t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})-y^{0}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\Big\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{u}}\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}\left|f_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})-\boldsymbol{y}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{T}\boldsymbol{V}_{t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})-y^{0}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\right|,

where the inner term
\|f_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})-\boldsymbol{y}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{T}\boldsymbol{V}_{t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})-y^{0}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}\|_{\infty}
measures the distance between a continuous function
f_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})
and a piecewise linear function
\boldsymbol{y}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{T}\boldsymbol{V}_{t^{\prime},i}(\xi_{t^{\prime},i})+y^{0}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}
.

It is well-known that when the length of each segment is small enough (
\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\to 0
), piecewise linear functions can approximate a continuous function to arbitrary accuracy under the supremum norm, so

\displaystyle\forall\pi^{u}_{1}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u},\lim_{\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\searrow 0}\min_{\pi^{u}_{2}\in\Pi^{u}(\boldsymbol{w})}d(\pi^{u}_{1},\pi^{u}_{2})=0.

(31)

Therefore, (
ii
) holds.
∎

Based on Proposition
2
, we prove the asymptotic performance theorem in Theorem
1
, which states that the asymptotic performance given by LCP when
\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\to 0
coincides with the performance of the additive control policy space.

Theorem 1

(Asymptotic Performance)
.

If cost functions
c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{u}_{t},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t})
are absolutely continuous, and for each feasible integer controller
{\pi}^{\gamma}
, the set of feasible continuous controllers

\mathfrak{A}({\pi}^{\gamma})=\{{\pi}^{u}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}|\boldsymbol{\pi}=({\pi}^{u},{\pi}^{\gamma})\text{ is feasible for (\ref{drsc})}\}

has non-empty interior, then

\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\min_{w:\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\geq\delta}J^{\ast}(\Pi^{u}(\boldsymbol{w}),\Pi^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{w}))=J^{\ast}(\mathfrak{N}^{u},\mathfrak{N}^{\gamma}).

(32)

Proof.

According to Proposition
2
.(
i
), for any
(\bar{\pi}^{u},\bar{\pi}^{\gamma})\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}\times\mathfrak{N}^{\gamma}
feasible for problem (
DRMIC
), there exists a
\boldsymbol{w}
such that
\bar{\pi}^{\gamma}\in\Pi^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{w})
.

Therefore, by the absolute continuity of the cost functions
c_{t}
, it remains to prove that there exists a sequence of continuous controllers
\pi^{u}_{n}\in\Pi^{u}_{\ast}
such that
(\pi^{u}_{n},\bar{\pi}^{\gamma})
are feasible for problem (
DRMIC
) and satisfy
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(\pi^{u}_{n},\bar{\pi}^{u})=0
.

Since the set of feasible continuous controllers
\mathfrak{A}(\bar{\pi}^{\gamma})
has non-empty interior, there exists a
\pi^{u}_{0}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}
such that
\{\pi^{u}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}|d(\pi^{u},\pi^{u}_{0})<\rho\}\subset\mathfrak{A}(\bar{\pi}^{\gamma})
. Let
d_{0}=d(\bar{\pi}^{u},\pi_{0}^{u})
. Consider the set

\mathcal{B}_{n}=\Bigg\{\pi^{u}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}\Bigg|\ d\Bigg(\pi^{u},\frac{2^{n}-1}{2^{n}}\bar{\pi}^{u}+\frac{1}{2^{n}}\pi^{u}_{0}\Bigg)<\frac{\rho}{2^{n}}\Bigg\}.

(33)

Since
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is open in
\mathfrak{N}^{u}
and
\Pi^{u}_{\ast}
is dense in
\mathfrak{N}^{u}
by Proposition
2
.(
ii
), then
\Pi^{u}_{\ast}\cap\mathcal{B}_{n}
is dense in
\mathfrak{N}^{u}\cap\mathcal{B}_{n}
. Therefore, we can choose
\pi^{u}_{n}
from
\mathcal{B}_{n}
such that
\pi^{u}_{n}\in\Pi^{u}_{\ast}
.

By this selection, we have

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}d(\pi^{u}_{n},\bar{\pi}^{u})\\
\leq d\Bigg(\pi^{u}_{n},\frac{2^{n}-1}{2^{n}}\bar{\pi}^{u}+\frac{1}{2^{n}}\pi^{u}_{0}\Bigg)+d\Bigg(\frac{2^{n}-1}{2^{n}}\bar{\pi}^{u}+\frac{1}{2^{n}}\pi^{u}_{0},\bar{\pi}^{u}\Bigg)\\
<\frac{\rho}{2^{n}}+d\Bigg(\frac{1}{2^{n}}\pi^{u}_{0},\frac{1}{2^{n}}\bar{\pi}^{u}\Bigg)=\frac{\rho}{2^{n}}+\frac{1}{2^{n}}d(\pi^{u}_{0},\bar{\pi}^{u})=\frac{\rho+d_{0}}{2^{n}}.\end{gathered}

Therefore, the second condition
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(\pi^{u}_{n},\bar{\pi}^{u})=0
is satisfied.

To prove that
\pi^{u}_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}({\pi}^{\gamma})
, consider
\hat{\pi}_{u}=2^{n}\pi^{u}_{n}-(2^{n}-1)\bar{\pi}^{u}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}
. Notice that

\displaystyle d(\hat{\pi}_{u},{\pi}^{u}_{0})=d(2^{n}\pi^{u}_{n}-(2^{n}-1)\bar{\pi}^{u},{\pi}^{u}_{0})

(37)

\displaystyle=d(2^{n}\pi^{u}_{n},(2^{n}-1)\bar{\pi}^{u}+{\pi}^{u}_{0})

(38)

\displaystyle=2^{n}d(\pi^{u}_{n},(2^{n}-1)/2^{n}\bar{\pi}^{u}+1/2^{n}{\pi}^{u}_{0})<2^{n}\times\rho/2^{n}=\rho.

(39)

Therefore,
\hat{\pi}_{u}\in\{\pi^{u}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}|d(\pi^{u},\pi^{u}_{0})<\rho\}\subset\mathfrak{A}(\bar{\pi}^{\gamma})
.

By the linearity of the constraints of the problem (
DRMIC
),
\mathfrak{A}(\bar{\pi}^{\gamma})
is convex. Therefore,
\pi^{u}_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}(\bar{\pi}^{\gamma})
since
\pi^{u}_{n}=1/2^{n}\hat{\pi}^{u}+(2^{n}-1)/2^{n}\bar{\pi}^{u}
.
∎

IV-B

Non-asymptotic Performance Analysis

In this part, we aim to analyze the non-asymptotic performance of LCP induced by particular breakpoints
\boldsymbol{w}
.
Due to the non-convex nature of the integer lifted policy space
\Pi^{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{w})
, conducting non-asymptotic analysis with integer control poses considerable challenges. Hence, in this section, we consider systems with only continuous control and analyze the non-asymptotic performance with Lipschitz continuous controllers which are defined as follows.

Definition 7

(Lipschitz Continuous Control Policy Space)
.

Let the disturbance space
\varXi
be equipped with the 1-norm distance. The
L_{\pi}
Lipschitz continuous LCP space is defined as

\displaystyle\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w})=\{\pi^{u}\in\Pi^{u}(\boldsymbol{w})|u_{t,j}^{\pi}\text{ is }L_{\pi}\text{ Lipschitz}\},

(40)

and the
L_{\pi}
Lipschitz continuous additive control policy space is defined as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}}=\{\pi^{u}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}|u_{t,j}^{\pi}\text{ is }L_{\pi}\text{ Lipschitz}\}.

(41)

By Definition
7
, it is noteworthy that for an additive control policy
u_{t,j}^{\pi}=\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi}
, if
u_{t,j}^{\pi}
is
L_{\pi}
Lipschitz, each
u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi}
is also
L_{\pi}
Lipschitz.

Constraint (
2
) that defines the feasible controllers also poses challenges to non-asymptotic analysis, and to handle this feasibility issue, we simplify the feasible policy space to a hyper-ball centered at a given control policy.

Under the above simplifications, we establish the tightest non-asymptotic performance bound in the following theorem.

Theorem 2

(Non-asymptotic Performance Bound)
.

Let the system only involve continuous control and the feasible policy space be a hyper ball
\mathfrak{C}
with radius
\rho
centered at a given lifted continuous control policy
\pi_{0}^{u}\in\Pi^{u}(\boldsymbol{w})
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{C}=\{\pi^{u}|d(\pi^{u},\pi_{0}^{u})\leq\rho\}.

(42)

If cost functions
c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{u}_{t})
are Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
L_{c}
, then

J^{\ast}(\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w}))-J^{\ast}(\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}})\lesssim\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\right),

(43)

and this bound is tight.

As a consequence, if the breakpoints are selected to segment each dimension of the support into
n
segments of equal length, then
\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\lesssim\mathcal{O}\left(1/n\right)
, and as a result

J^{\ast}(\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w}))-J^{\ast}(\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}})\lesssim\mathcal{O}\left(1/n\right).

(44)

Proof.

Notice that

\displaystyle J^{\ast}(\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w}))-J^{\ast}(\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}})

(45)

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}=\min_{\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w})\cap\mathfrak{C}}\ \max_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathscr{B}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{2}},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi_{2}})\right]\\
-\min_{\pi_{1}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}}\cap\mathfrak{C}}\ \max_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathscr{B}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{1}},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi_{1}})\right]\end{gathered}

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}=\max_{\pi_{1}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}}\cap\mathfrak{C}}\min_{\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w})\cap\mathfrak{C}}\Bigg\{\max_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathscr{B}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{2}},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi_{2}})\right]\\
-\max_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathscr{B}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{1}},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi_{1}})\right]\Bigg\}\end{gathered}

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\leq\max_{\pi_{1}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}}\cap\mathfrak{C}}\ \min_{\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w})\cap\mathfrak{C}}\ \max_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathscr{B}}\\
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left\{\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\left[c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{2}},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi_{2}})-c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{1}},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi_{1}})\right]\right\},\end{gathered}

where
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi}
represent state and controller induced by
\pi
.

Inequality (
IV-B
) is in the form of a max-min problem or a two-player game. The first player chooses a
\pi_{1}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}}\cap\mathfrak{C}
, and then in response to
\pi_{1}
, the second player chooses a
\pi_{2}\in\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w})\cap\mathfrak{C}
.
By Proposition
2
, both
\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi_{1}}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi_{2}}
are in the form of additive controllers,
i.e.
,
u_{t,j}^{\pi_{1}}=\sum_{t^{\prime}\in[t],i\in[n_{\xi}]}u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{1}}
and
u_{t,j}^{\pi_{2}}=\sum_{t^{\prime}\in[t],i\in[n_{\xi}]}u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{2}}
.
Given a control policy
\pi_{1}
by the first player, we set the strategy of the second player as

\begin{gathered}u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{2}}(w_{t^{\prime},i,k-1})=u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{1}}(w_{t^{\prime},i,k-1}).\end{gathered}

(52)

Equation (
52
) simply means that we construct
\pi_{2}
such that it equals to
\pi_{1}
at all the breakpoints
\boldsymbol{w}
, and this requirement can be satisfied since each
u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{2}}
is a piecewise linear function and breakpoints are just
{w}_{t^{\prime},i,k-1},k\in[p_{t^{\prime},i}+1]
.

We next prove that the constructed
\pi_{2}
is in
\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w})\cap\mathfrak{C}
. Notice that according to Definition
5
,

\displaystyle d(\pi^{u}_{2},\pi^{u}_{0})=\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{u}}\|{u}^{\pi_{2}}_{t,j}-{u}^{\pi_{0}}_{t,j}\|_{\infty}

(53)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{u}}\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}\|({u}^{\pi_{2}}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}-{u}^{\pi_{0}}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i})\|_{\infty}

(54)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{u}}\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}\max_{k\in[p_{t^{\prime},i}+1]}\Big|({u}^{\pi_{2}}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}-{u}^{\pi_{0}}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i})(w_{t^{\prime},i,k-1})\Big|

(55)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{u}}\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}\max_{k\in[p_{t^{\prime},i}+1]}\Big|({u}^{\pi_{1}}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}-{u}^{\pi_{0}}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i})(w_{t^{\prime},i,k-1})\Big|

(56)

\displaystyle\leq d(\pi^{u}_{1},\pi^{u}_{0})\leq\rho,

(57)

where equality (
55
) holds because both
{u}^{\pi_{2}}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}
and
{u}^{\pi_{0}}_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}
are piecewise linear functions with breakpoints
w_{t^{\prime},j,k-1}
.

To verify the Lipschitz property of
\pi_{2}
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\max_{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{1},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{2}\in\varXi_{[t]}}\Bigg|\frac{u_{t,j}^{\pi_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{1})-u_{t,j}^{\pi_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{2})}{\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{1}-\boldsymbol{\xi}^{2}\|_{1}}\Bigg|

(58)

\displaystyle=\max_{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{1},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{2}\in\varXi_{[t]}}\Bigg|\frac{\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{2}}({\xi}^{1}_{t^{\prime},i})-u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{2}}({\xi}^{2}_{t^{\prime},i})}{\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}|{\xi}^{1}_{t^{\prime},i}-{\xi}^{2}_{t^{\prime},i}|}\Bigg|

(59)

reaches the maximum when
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{1},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{2}
are at the breakpoints. Therefore, the Lipschitz constant of
\pi_{2}
is no larger than that of
\pi_{1}
.

Under this construction, the difference between
\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi_{1}}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi_{2}}
can be bounded as follows.

\displaystyle|u_{t,j}^{\pi_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})-u_{t,j}^{\pi_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|\leq\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}|u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{1}}({\xi}_{t^{\prime},i})-u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{2}}({\xi}_{t^{\prime},i})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}\Big\{|u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{1}}({\xi}_{t^{\prime},i})-u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{1}}(\hat{w}_{t^{\prime},i})|

\displaystyle+|u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{2}}(\hat{w}_{t^{\prime},i})-u_{t,j,t^{\prime},i}^{\pi_{2}}({\xi}_{t^{\prime},i})|\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}2L_{\pi}|\hat{w}_{t^{\prime},i}-{\xi}_{t^{\prime},i}|\leq\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\xi}}2L_{\pi}\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})\lesssim\mathcal{O}(\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})),

where
\hat{w}_{t^{\prime},i}
is the breakpoint nearest to
{\xi}_{t^{\prime},i}
,
i.e.
,
\hat{w}_{t^{\prime},i}=\mathop{\rm arg\,min}_{w\in\{w_{t^{\prime},i,k-1},k\in[p_{t^{\prime},i}+1]\}}|w-\xi_{t^{\prime},i}|
.

To bound the the difference between
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{1}}
and
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{2}}
, note that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})-\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_{1}=\|\boldsymbol{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\pi_{1}}_{t-1}-\boldsymbol{x}^{\pi_{2}}_{t-1})+\boldsymbol{B}_{t}(\boldsymbol{u}^{\pi_{1}}_{t-1}-\boldsymbol{u}^{\pi_{2}}_{t-1})\|_{1}

Therefore, by induction, the difference between
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{1}}
and
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}^{\pi_{2}}
is also in the order of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w}))
.

Since
c_{t}
is Lipschitz continuous, we have

\displaystyle|c_{t}(x_{t}^{\pi_{1}},u_{t}^{\pi_{1}})-c_{t}(x_{t}^{\pi_{2}},u_{t}^{\pi_{2}})|\leq L_{c}\left(\left\|x_{t}^{\pi_{1}}-x_{t}^{\pi_{2}}\right\|+\left\|u_{t}^{\pi_{1}}-u_{t}^{\pi_{2}}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle=L_{c}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{x}}|x_{t,j}^{\pi_{1}}-x_{t,j}^{\pi_{2}}|+\sum_{j=1}^{n_{u}}|u_{t,j}^{\pi_{1}}-u_{t,j}^{\pi_{2}}|\Bigg)\lesssim\mathcal{O}(\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})).

By the above observations, (
IV-B
) can be further bounded by

\displaystyle(\ref{thm_min_ep})\lesssim\max_{\pi_{1}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}}\cap\mathfrak{C}}\ \max_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathscr{B}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\mathcal{O}(\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w}))]\lesssim\mathcal{O}(\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})).

(60)

To prove the tightness of this bound, we need to construct a concrete example whose non-asymptotic performance is lower bounded by
\Omega(\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w}))
. To this aim, we consider a single-horizon problem where the state
x
, continuous control
u
, and disturbance
\xi
are of dimension one. Support
\varXi=[0,1]
and the breakpoints
\boldsymbol{w}
segment the support into equal lengths of
\epsilon
, so
\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w})=\epsilon
. The given control policy is set to
\pi_{0}^{u}(\xi)=0,\forall\xi\in\varXi
. The ambiguity set
\mathscr{B}
contains only one distribution
\hat{P}=1/4\delta_{0}+1/4\delta_{\epsilon}+1/2\delta_{\epsilon/2}
,
i.e.
,
\mathscr{B}=\{\hat{P}\}
, which corresponds to the Wasserstein ambiguity set centered at
\hat{P}
with radius
0
. The state transition function is given by
x=\xi
, and the cost function is given by

c(x,u)=\frac{L_{c}}{L_{\pi}+1}|u-L_{\pi}|x-\epsilon/2||,

(61)

whose Lipschitz constant is

\frac{L_{c}}{L_{\pi}+1}\max\{L_{\pi},1\},

(62)

which is less than
L_{c}
.

The optimal control
u_{\text{opt}}
of this problem is a
L_{\pi}
Lipschitz piecewise linear one as follows.

\displaystyle u_{\text{opt}}(\xi)=\left\{\begin{aligned} L_{\pi}|\xi-\epsilon/2|,&\ \xi\in[0,\epsilon]\\
L_{\pi}\epsilon/2\hskip 20.0pt,&\ \xi\in(\epsilon,1].\end{aligned}\right.

(63)

Since
u_{\text{opt}}\in\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}}
, we have
J^{\ast}(\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}})=0
.

For controller
u\in\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w})
, only the first segment
[0,\epsilon]
affects the performance, so we have

\displaystyle J^{\ast}(\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w}))-J^{\ast}(\mathfrak{N}^{u}_{L_{\pi}})

(64)

\displaystyle=J^{\ast}(\Pi^{u}_{L_{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{w}))=\max_{u(0),u(\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}_{\hat{P}}[c(x,u)]

(65)

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}=\frac{L_{c}}{L_{\pi}+1}\max_{u(0),u(\epsilon)}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{4}\Big|u(0)-\frac{\epsilon L_{\pi}}{2}\Big|\\
+\frac{1}{4}\Big|u(\epsilon)-\frac{\epsilon L_{\pi}}{2}\Big|+\frac{1}{2}\Big|\frac{u(0)+u(\epsilon)}{2}\Big|\Bigg\}\end{gathered}

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\geq\frac{L_{c}}{L_{\pi}+1}\max_{u(0),u(\epsilon)}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{4}\Big|u(0)-\frac{\epsilon L_{\pi}}{2}+u(\epsilon)-\frac{\epsilon L_{\pi}}{2}\\
-2\frac{u(0)+u(\epsilon)}{2}\Big|\Bigg\}=\frac{L_{c}}{L_{\pi}+1}\frac{L_{\pi}}{4}\epsilon\gtrsim\Omega(\epsilon),\end{gathered}

where (
65
) holds because
u
is linear on
[0,\epsilon]
, so it is determined by the endpoint value
u(0)
and
u(\epsilon)
.

Therefore, the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon(\boldsymbol{w}))
bound is tight.
∎

By Theorem
2
, the tightest non-asymptotic bound is of order
1/n
, which provides guidance in determining the number of breakpoints in practice.

V
Solution Methodology

In this section, we develop solution methodology to compute
J^{\ast}(\Pi^{{u}}(\boldsymbol{w}),\Pi^{{\gamma}}(\boldsymbol{w}))
and the associated best lifted controller.

We make the following assumption on the cost function
c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}^{\pi})
, which allows us to leverage standard techniques to handle the distributionally robust objective (
3
) with Wasserstein-type ambiguity sets.

Assumption 2
.

The cost function
c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}^{\pi})
is a convex piecewise linear cost as follows.

{c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}^{\pi})=\max_{\tau\in[I]}\bigg\{\boldsymbol{a}_{\tau}^{T}\boldsymbol{x}_{t}+\boldsymbol{b}_{\tau}^{T}\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi}+\boldsymbol{c}_{\tau}^{T}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}^{\pi}\bigg\}.}

(70)

It is noteworthy that the convex piecewise linear function
c_{t}
can approximate a quadratic cost, which is presumed in
[
16
]
.

According to Definition
3
, LCP is affine in the lifted disturbance
G(\boldsymbol{\xi})
.
By substituting the LCP formulation (
17
) and (
18
) into the system dynamics (
1
), the system state
\boldsymbol{x}_{t}
is also affine in the lifted disturbance
G(\boldsymbol{\xi})
. Therefore, constraint (
2
) can be written in the following concise form.

{\boldsymbol{m}\geq\boldsymbol{E}^{T}G(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\varXi,}

(71)

where
\boldsymbol{m}
and
\boldsymbol{E}
are affine functions of the LCP coefficients
\boldsymbol{Y}_{t},\boldsymbol{y}_{t}^{0},\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}
, and
\boldsymbol{z}_{t}^{0}
.

Similarly, the convex piecewise linear cost
c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}^{\pi})
is convex piecewise linear in
G(\boldsymbol{\xi})
, and since the sum of convex piecewise linear functions is also convex piecewise linear, the total cost
\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\alpha^{t}c(\boldsymbol{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{u}_{t},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t})
can be compactly formulated as

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{\mathcal{T}}\alpha^{t}c_{t}(\boldsymbol{x}_{t},\boldsymbol{u}_{t}^{\pi},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{t}^{\pi})=\max_{k\in[K]}\bigg\{{\boldsymbol{d}_{k}^{T}G(\boldsymbol{\xi})}+r_{k}\bigg\},

(72)

where
\boldsymbol{d}_{k}
and
r_{k}
are affine functions of the LCP coefficients
\boldsymbol{Y}_{t},\boldsymbol{y}_{t}^{0},\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}
, and
\boldsymbol{z}_{t}^{0}
.

Therefore, computing
J^{\ast}(\Pi^{{u}}(\boldsymbol{w}),\Pi^{{\gamma}}(\boldsymbol{w}))
amounts to solving the following optimization problem.

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{Y}_{t},\boldsymbol{y}_{t}^{0},\boldsymbol{Z}_{t},\boldsymbol{z}_{t}^{0}}\hskip 8.0pt\max_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathscr{B}}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\sim\mathbb{P}}\bigg[\max_{k\in[K]}\bigg\{\boldsymbol{d}_{k}^{T}G(\boldsymbol{\xi})+r_{k}\bigg\}\bigg]

(73)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\hskip 30.0pt\boldsymbol{m}\geq\boldsymbol{E}^{T}G(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\forall\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\varXi\hskip 40.0pt

(74)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Y}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}\times n_{V_{t}}},\boldsymbol{y}_{t}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}},\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n_{\gamma}\times n_{Q_{t}}},\boldsymbol{z}_{t}^{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n_{\gamma}},

(75)

where
\boldsymbol{m}
,
\boldsymbol{E}
,
\boldsymbol{d}_{k}
, and
r_{k}
are affine in
\boldsymbol{Y}_{t},\boldsymbol{y}_{t}^{0},\boldsymbol{Z}_{t}
, and
\boldsymbol{z}_{t}^{0}
.

To solve this problem, we need to develop equivalent reformulations of the objective function (
73
) and constraint (
74
).
Concerning the reformulation of the objective function (
73
),
although the seminar work
[
14
]
and follow-up papers have established standard techniques to equivalently reformulate the min-max objective (
73

which is intractable to optimization solvers.
Therefore, to derive a tractable equivalent reformulation, we need to develop reformulation techniques regarding the lifting function to further reformulate this middle form.
Instead of developing such techniques, the previous work
[
32

relaxed the Wasserstein ambiguity set to a lifted ambiguity set to avoid this difficulty, thereby leading to a sub-optimal LCP solution.

In Section

VII
Conclusion

This work investigated the finite-horizon DRMIC of uncertain linear systems. We proposed a DR-LCP method to obtain an approximate solution to
the DRMIC problem for Wasserstein metric-based ambiguity sets by optimizing over the family of the LCPs. We then conducted an asymptotic performance analysis and established a tight non-asymptotic performance bound of the proposed DR-LCP method.
Subsequently, we developed a solution methodology to find the optimal LCP for the DRMIC problem. Finally, numerical experiments on an inventory control problem demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed DR-LCP method compared with existing methods in the literature.

To alleviate the computational burden of long-horizon problems, distributed algorithms
[
17
]
may be developed for our method to accelerate solving the reformulated optimization problem as future work.

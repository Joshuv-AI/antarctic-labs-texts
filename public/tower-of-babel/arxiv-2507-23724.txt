General Diffusions on Metric Graphs as limits of Time-Space Markov Chains
Alexis Anagnostakis alexis.anagnostakis@univ-lorraine.fr

Abstract
We introduce the Space-Time Markov Chain Approximation (STMCA) for a general diffusion process on a finite metric graph Γ \Gamma . The STMCA is a doubly asymmetric (in both time and space) random walk defined on a subdivisions of Γ \Gamma , with transition probabilities and conditional transition times that match, in expectation, those of the target diffusion. We derive bounds on the p p -Wasserstein distances between the diffusion and its STMCA in terms of a thinness quantifier of the subdivision. This bound shows that convergence occurs at any rate inferior to 1 4 ∧ 1 p \frac{1}{4}\wedge\frac{1}{p} in terms of the the maximum cell size of the subdivision, for adapted subdivisions, at any rate inferior to 1 2 ∧ 2 p \frac{1}{2}\wedge\frac{2}{p} . Additionally, we provide explicit analytical formulas for transition probabilities and times, enabling practical implementation of the STMCA. Numerical experiments illustrate our results.



Keywords and phrases
: Diffusion on network; Markov Chain approximation; random walk approximation; invariance principle; generalized second order differential operators; path regularity.

Introduction

Since their introduction in
[
26
]
and later in
[
19
]
, diffusions on metric graphs have attracted growing interest.
From a modeling perspective, they describe diverse physical phenomena, including electrical networks, nerve impulse propagation, and fluid motion in fissured porous media (see
[
19
,
24
,
41
]
and references therein). Theoretically, they represent a non-trivial class of diffusions that blend local one-dimensional dynamics with global geometric constraints.

More precisely, a diffusion on a metric graph is a continuous strong Markov
process that behaves like a one-dimensional diffusion on every edge and, upon reaching a vertex, is kicked back at incident edges with some fixed probability called
spinning measure
. The process may also exhibit sticky behavior at vertices, spending a positive amount of time there.

Diffusions on metric graphs are characterized by a family of generalized second-order differential operators on each edge (analogous to 1D diffusions) and Wentzell-type conditions at vertices that blend first and (generalized) second-order derivatives. To emphasize the general nature of these operators, we refer to such processes as general diffusions on metric graphs.

Despite considerable recent advances—particularly for star graphs, where the analysis is simpler (see
[
9
,
8
,
10
,
25
,
28
,
31
,
32
,
37
]
)—the development of numerical approximation methods for diffusions on metric graphs remains surprisingly understudied.
Existing approaches include:

1.

Birth-death processes, see
[
41
]
;

2.

Modified Euler scheme that handles nodal behavior by relocating the process to the boundary of a small vertex neighborhood with fixed probabilities upon entry, see
[
15
]
;

3.

The invariance principle from
[
33
]
for simple fully symmetric random walks on star graphs, whose limit is Walsh Brownian motion.

These present notable limitations for satisfactory application to general diffusions.
Up to the author’s knowledge, no convergence rate guarantees exist for the first two approaches.
Moreover, the last two approaches apply only to specific case of diffusions: The second (modified Euler scheme) applies only to diffusions whose dynamic on every edge is determined by a classical second order differential operators, where Euler scheme applies, and on every vertex exhibits non-sticky behavior.
The third (fully symmetric random walks) applies only to Walsh Brownian motion.

To address this problem, we propose a novel approximation scheme for a general diffusion on a metric graph
\Gamma
, we call Space-Time Markov Chain Approximation (STMCA).
This process is a random walk taking values in vertices of subdivisions of
\Gamma
, with fixed transition probabilities and conditional transition times, given the next position of the process.
We bound the
p
-Wasserstein distance between the laws of the diffusion and its STMCA in terms of a thinness quantifier of the subdivision which measures thinness in terms of the scale and speed of the target diffusion.
The quantifier’s definition allows us, by adapting the structure of the subdivision, to devise numerically efficient good approximations of the target diffusion.
More precisely, we are able under explicit conditions to show that the same bound on the
p
-Wasserstein distance holds by replacing the grid quantifier by the maximum cell size of the grid squared.

The proof of the bound follows the same mechanism as the 1D version in
[
3
]
.
It relies on the embeddable nature of the scheme, controls on moments of consecutive embedding times, and regularity estimates.
By embeddable nature we mean that: Consecutive positions of the process on the subdivision and consecutive values of its STMCA have the same distribution.
This property yields a natural coupling between the laws of the diffusion and its STMCA, which allows us to bound their Wasserstein distance.
Regularity estimates are proven combining the Kolmogorov continuity theorem and moment bounds for diffusions on metric graphs.
We derive the later first for diffusions on star-graphs, by excursion flipping and time-change arguments, leveraging the time-change characterizations from
[
2
]
.
Then, we extend to the finite metric graph case.

Additionally, we give explicit analytical representation for the transition probabilities and transition times of the STMCA that allow for implementation of the scheme.

This work generalizes: the STMCA defined in
[
3
]
for general one-dimensional diffusions, the scheme from
[
17
]
for classical diffusions with (possibly discontinuous) uniformly elliptic coefficients, and many simpler random walk models like the Donsker invariance principle
[
16
]
, the sticky random walk from
[
1
]
, and the oscillating random walk from
[
39
]
.
Regarding approximations of diffusions on graphs, our work can also be seen as generalization of the invariance principle from
[
33
]
, described earlier.

In one dimension, STMCA joins EMCEL
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
and CTMC methods
[
12
,
29
]
as specialized schemes for diffusions issued from generalized second-order operators.

2
Framework and main results

In this section we present the main result of this paper along with all relevant notions for its understanding.
We first introduce diffusions on metric graphs and their analytical characterization. Then, we present the notion of subdivision of a metric graph
\Gamma
and, given such subdivision, we define the Space-Time Markov Chain Approximation (STMCA) of a diffusion on
\Gamma
, taking values in the subdivision.
Last, we present the main convergence result under a regularity condition and prove the embeddable character of the STMCA.

2.1  General diffusions on metric graphs

A finite metric graph is a graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
with a finite number of edges to whom we assign edge lengths:
e\mapsto l_{e}
, for all
e\in E

One can construct such graphs by disjoint union of intervals modulo an equivalence relation.

[0,l)

[0,l]

\mathsf{\Gamma}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{i\leq N}U_{i}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\mathsf{V}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{i\leq N}\left\{{U_{i}\cap\partial\,U_{i}}\right\}.

(2.1)

Following

30

We equip

A

Definition 2.1
.

.

The
Space-Time Markov Chain Approximation
(or STMCA) of
\mathsf{X}
on
\Delta
is the
V_{\Delta}
-valued random walk with initial distribution
\mu_{0}
, of transition probabilities
(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}})_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
, and conditional transition times

(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}})_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
.
More precisely, it is the process
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}^{\Delta}
whose consecutive values

(\mathsf{Y}_{k})_{k}
form a discrete-time Markov chain on
\Delta
of initial value

\mathsf{Y}_{0}\sim\mu_{0}
and transition probabilities

(p_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}})_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
. The consecutive jump times
(T_{k})_{k}
of
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}^{\Delta}
are given by
T_{k}-T_{k-1}=\mathfrak{t}_{\mathsf{Y}_{k-1},\mathsf{Y}_{k}}
, for all
k\in\IN
.

With the above notations, the approximation process reads:

\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}^{\Delta}_{t}=\sum_{k\in\IN}\mathsf{Y}_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{t\in[T_{k-1},T_{k})},\hskip 9.24994ptt\geq 0.

(2.16)

The STMCA yields a discrete-state process that is Markovian in both space and time, thus justifying its name. Practical implementation requires computation of the transition quantities
(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}})_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
and
(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}})_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
, which we discuss in Section
3
. This process can be defined for any general diffusions on a finite metric graph.

2.4  Main result (convergence rate)

We present the main result: A bound on the Wasserstein distance between (the laws of) a diffusion process on a finite metric graph and its STMCA approximation. For every
p\geq 1
, the
p
-Wasserstein distance between the laws of two processes
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{Y}
is:

\mathcal{W}^{p}_{T}(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left\|{\sup_{t\in[0,T]}d(\zeta_{t},\xi_{t})}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Prob^{(\zeta,\xi)})}\mid(\zeta,\xi)\sim\Pi(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})\right\},

(2.17)

where the infimum is taken over all possible couplings with marginals
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{Y}
, we denote
\Pi(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})
, and
\Prob^{(\zeta,\xi)}
is the probability measure associated to the coupling
(\zeta,\xi)
.

We impose the following condition on the diffusion
\mathsf{X}
.

Condition 2.8
.

For all
p\geq 1
and
T>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|{d(\mathsf{X}_{s},\mathsf{X}_{t})}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Prob_{\mathbf{x}})}\leq c(1+x)\sqrt{t-s},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }s,t\in[0,T].

(2.18)

While Condition
2.8
suffices for our analysis, we also consider a stronger but more tractable requirement on the speed measures:

Condition 2.9
.

There exists
k_{1}>0
and
k_{2}\geq 0

such that

m_{e}(\mathrm{d}y)\geq\frac{k_{1}}{1+k_{2}y^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }y\in U_{e}\text{ and }e\in E.

(2.19)

Condition
2.9
is the finite metric graph version of the speed measure constraint
m(\mathrm{d}y)\geq k_{1}\,\mathrm{d}y/(1+k_{2}y^{2})
under which convergence is proven for EMCEL and one-dimensional STMCA.
The implication between Conditions
2.8
and
2.9
is the object of the forthcoming Proposition
5.2
.

We now state the main convergence result, which establishes a quantitative bound on the
Wasserstein distance between the diffusion process and its STMCA. The proof, along with a discussion on optimal subdivision strategies for achieving higher convergence rates, is provided in Section
6
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\mathsf{X}
be an NSE general diffusion on a finite metric graph
\Gamma
, defined on the probability space
(\Omega,(\bF_{t})_{t\geq 0},\Prob_{\mathbf{x}})
such that
\mathsf{X}_{0}=\mathbf{x}=(e,x)
,
\Prob_{\mathbf{x}}
-almost surely.
For every covering subdivision
\Delta
of
\Gamma
, let
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}^{\Delta}
be the STMCA of
\mathsf{X}
on
\Delta
.
Assume
\mathsf{X}
satisfies Condition
2.8
for some constant
c>0
or Condition
2.9
for some constants
k_{1}>0
and
k_{2}\geq 0
.
Then, for every
x\geq 0
,
T>0
,
p\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{4}\wedge\frac{1}{p})
, and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mathcal{W}^{p}_{T}(\mathsf{X},\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}^{\Delta})\leq C\left|{\Delta}\right|_{X}^{\alpha},

(2.20)

for all
(V,\varepsilon)
-symmetric covering subdivisions
\Delta
of
\Gamma
.

Since convergence in Wasserstein distance implies convergence in law (see
[
38
, Theorem 6.7]
), we have also the following.

Corollary 2.11
.

For all
T>0
, the processes
(\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}^{\Delta};\,t\in[0,T])
converge in law to
(\mathsf{X};\,t\in[0,T])
as
\left|{\Delta}\right|_{\mathsf{X}}\to 0
.

Remark 2.12
.

While we defined the STMCA for general diffusions on a metric graph
\Gamma
, the convergence result (Theorem
2.10
) applies only to NSE diffusions.
However, many non-NSE cases can be reduced to NSE diffusions via transformations (see
[
2
, Proposition 3.13]
for star graphs).
Under additional regularity (e.g., Hölder continuity of the scale functions
(s_{e})_{e\in E}
), analogous bounds to Theorem
2.10
may be derived using arguments inspired by
[
3
, Section 2.2]
.

2.5  Embedding property

Let
\mathsf{X}
be a general diffusion on
\Gamma
,
\Delta=(V_{\Delta},E_{\Delta})
a covering subdivision of

\Gamma
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}^{\Delta}
the STMCA of
\mathsf{X}
on
\Delta

We now show the embedding property of
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}^{\Delta}
into the sample paths of
\mathsf{X}

This gives us an immediate way to compare the laws of
\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}^{\Delta}
and
\mathsf{X}
which is key to our main proof.

For this, we define the embedding times of
\mathsf{X}
into
V_{\Delta}
. It is the sequence of stopping times
(\tau^{\Delta}_{k})_{k}
defined as

\displaystyle\tau^{\Delta}_{0}\coloneqq

\displaystyle 0,

(2.21)

\displaystyle\tau^{\Delta}_{1}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\inf\{t\geq 0:\;X_{t}\in V_{\Delta}\},

(2.22)

\displaystyle\tau^{\Delta}_{k}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\inf\{t\geq\tau_{k-1}:\;X_{t}\in V_{\Delta}\setminus\{\mathsf{X}_{\tau_{k-1}}\}\},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }k\geq 2.

(2.23)

Also, for every
t\geq 0
, let
K(t)
be the random counter defined as

K^{\Delta}(t)\coloneqq\inf\left\{{k\in\IN:\,\sum^{k}_{n=1}\Esp\left[{\tau^{\Delta}_{n}-\tau^{\Delta}_{n-1}\big|\,\sigma\left({\mathsf{X}_{\tau^{\Delta}_{n-1}},\mathsf{X}_{\tau^{\Delta}_{n}}}\right)}\right]>t}\right\},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }t\geq 0.

(2.24)

The embedding property is formulated as follows.

Proposition 2.13
.

With the notations above, it holds that
\mathsf{X}_{\tau^{\Delta}_{K^{\Delta}(\cdot)}}{\overset{\mathrm{law}}{=}}\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}^{\Delta}
.

Proof.

We observe that both processes are piecewise constant with the same spate space
V_{\Delta}
, whose consecutive values form Markov chains with the same initial distribution and the same transition probabilities.
Indeed, regarding the initial value, we have that

\Prob\left({\mathsf{Y}_{0}=\mathbf{y}}\right)=\mu_{0}(\mathbf{y})=\Prob_{\mathbf{x}}\left({X_{\tau^{\Delta}_{1}}=\mathbf{y}}\right),\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\mathbf{y}\in\Gamma.

(2.25)

Regarding the transition probabilities, for all
\mathbf{x}\in V_{\Delta}
with
U_{\mathbf{x}}
the cell centered at
\mathbf{x}
, and
\mathbf{y}\in U_{\mathbf{x}}
,

\Prob\left({\mathsf{X}_{\tau^{\Delta}_{k+1}}=\mathbf{y}\mid\mathsf{X}_{\tau^{\Delta}_{k}}=\mathbf{x}}\right)=\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}=\Prob_{\mathbf{x}}\left({T_{U_{\mathbf{x}}}=T_{\mathbf{y}}}\right).

(2.26)

Regarding the conditional transition times, by definition of
t\mapsto K^{\Delta}(t)
, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\left({\mathsf{X}_{\tau^{\Delta}_{K^{\Delta}(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}-h)}}\mid X_{0}=\mathbf{x};\,X_{\tau^{\Delta}_{1}}=\mathbf{y}}\right)=\left({\mathsf{X}_{\tau^{\Delta}_{0}}\mid X_{0}=\mathbf{x};\,X_{\tau^{\Delta}_{1}}=\mathbf{y}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\left({\mathsf{X}_{\tau^{\Delta}_{K^{\Delta}(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}+h)}}\mid X_{0}=\mathbf{x};\,X_{\tau^{\Delta}_{1}}=\mathbf{y}}\right)=\left({\mathsf{X}_{\tau^{\Delta}_{1}}\mid X_{0}=\mathbf{x};\,X_{\tau^{\Delta}_{1}}=\mathbf{y}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{y}

(2.28)

By the Markov property, this yields that the conditional transition time of
\mathsf{X}_{\tau^{\Delta}_{K^{\Delta}(\cdot)}}

are
(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}})_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}}
.
The proof is complete.
∎

6.2  Adaptation of subdivisions and optimality

We now analyze how adaptive subdivisions can improve the convergence rate in Theorem
2.10
.
The one-dimensional case provides particularly clear insight into this phenomenon.

Let
X
be a diffusion on natural scale defined on an interval
J\subset\mathbb{R}
, and let
\mathbf{g}
be a grid on
J
, that is: an ordered locally finite countable family of elements of
J
. A grid is the one-dimensional counterpart of a subdivision.
By
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
and a trivial bound, the Wasserstein distance between
X
and its STMCA approximation
\widetilde{X}^{\mathbf{g}}
satisfies:

\mathcal{W}^{p}_{T}(X,\widetilde{X}^{\mathbf{g}})\leq C(\alpha,p,T)\left|{\mathbf{g}}\right|_{X}\leq C(\alpha,p,T)\left|{\mathbf{g}}\right|,\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\alpha\in(0,\tfrac{1}{4}\wedge\tfrac{1}{p}),

(6.24)

where
\left|{\mathbf{g}}\right|\coloneqq\sup_{U\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{g})}|U|
is the maximal step-size of
\mathbf{g}
and
\left|{\mathbf{g}}\right|_{X}\coloneqq\sup_{U\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{g})}|U|m(U)
is the one-dimensional thinness quantifier defined in Section
2.2
.

A basic feature of the STMCA (as shown in
[
3
, Section 2.3]
)
is that one can take advantage of the thinness quantifier’s structure to double the convergence rate. Indeed, using an adapted grid
\mathbf{g}^{\prime}
that satisfies the geometric constraint:

\left|{\mathbf{g}}\right|_{X}\leq\left|{\mathbf{g}}\right|^{2},

(6.25)

we obtain the bound:

\mathcal{W}^{p}_{T}(X,\widetilde{X}^{\mathbf{g}^{\prime}})\leq C(\alpha,p,T)\left|{\mathbf{g}}\right|^{2\alpha},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\alpha\in\left(0,\tfrac{1}{4}\wedge\tfrac{1}{p}\right).

(6.26)

The adaptive condition (
6.25
) naturally leads to grid refinement in regions where the speed measure
m
is larger, providing higher resolution where the process spends more time.
It is particularly useful for accurate simulation with low numerical cost of diffusions with speed measures that exhibit high anisotropy like sticky features or finite natural boundaries.
The effectiveness is demonstrated numerically for the Cox–Ingersoll–Ross process in
[
3
, Section 5]
, showing significant improvement when the grid is adapted.

The same mechanism applies verbatim to diffusions on metric graphs.
For a diffusion
\mathsf{X}
on a metric graph
\Gamma
, we define the subdivision step size:

\left|{\Delta}\right|\coloneqq\sup_{U\in\mathcal{C}(\Delta)}\left|{U}\right|,

(6.27)

where
\left|{U}\right|=b-a
if
U
is the form (
3.3
) and

\left|{U}\right|=\max_{e\in E(\mathbf{v})}u_{e}
if
U
is of the form (
3.6
).
From simple computations, we obtain the following bound for some constant
C_{0}
that depends only on the law of
\mathsf{X}
:

\left|{\Delta}\right|_{\mathsf{X}}\leq C_{0}|\Delta|,

(6.28)

which, by Theorem
2.10
, yields the following bound between the law of

\mathsf{X}
and its STMCA
\widetilde{X}^{\Delta}
on
\Delta
:

\mathcal{W}^{p}_{T}(X,\widetilde{X}^{\mathbf{g}^{\prime}})\leq C(\alpha,p,T)C^{\alpha}_{0}|\Delta|^{\alpha},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\alpha\in\left({0,\tfrac{1}{4}\wedge\tfrac{1}{p}}\right).

(6.29)

Considering an adapted subdivision to the diffusion that satisfies

\left|{\Delta}\right|_{\mathsf{X}}\leq|\Delta|^{2}
yields the improved bound:

\mathcal{W}^{p}_{T}(X,\widetilde{X}^{\mathbf{g}^{\prime}})\leq C(\alpha,p,T)|\Delta|^{2\alpha},\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all }\alpha\in\left({0,\tfrac{1}{4}\wedge\tfrac{1}{p}}\right),

(6.30)

where the exponent is twice the one of (
6.29
).

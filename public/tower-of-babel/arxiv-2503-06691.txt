Limit Theorems for One-Dimensional Homogenized Diffusion Processes
Jaroslav I. Borodavka, Sebastian Krumscheid

Abstract
We present two limit theorems, a mean ergodic and a central limit theorem, for a specific class of one-dimensional diffusion processes that depend on a small-scale parameter ε \varepsilon and converge weakly to a homogenized diffusion process in the limit ε → 0 \varepsilon\rightarrow 0 . In these results, we allow for the time horizon to blow up such that T ε → ∞ T_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\infty as ε → 0 \varepsilon\rightarrow 0 . The novelty of the results arises from the circumstance that many quantities are unbounded for ε → 0 \varepsilon\rightarrow 0 , so that formerly established theory is not directly applicable here and a careful investigation of all relevant ε \varepsilon -dependent terms is required. As a mathematical application, we then use these limit theorems to prove asymptotic properties of a minimum distance estimator for parameters in a homogenized diffusion equation.



1
Introduction

In probability theory, limit theorems are of fundamental importance and provide indispensable tools for the asymptotic analysis of statistical estimators. One particular instance of such limit theorems can be found in the field of stochastic processes and, especially, diffusion processes which are solutions of a stochastic differential equation (SDE). Consider the solution
X
of the one-dimensional Itô SDE

dX(t)=b(X(t))dt+\overline{\sigma}(X(t))d\overline{W}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],

(1.1)

and suppose
X
has an invariant measure
m
on
\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, under various assumptions, there are limit theorems in the existing literature, cf.
[
11
,
3
]
, of the type

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}h(X(t))\,dt\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(x)\,dm(x)=:\tau^{2},\quad T\rightarrow\infty,

(1.2)

for a suitable test function
h
, where the convergence is understood either in the mean, almost sure, or stochastic sense. Another closely related limit result is the central limit theorem (CLT)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{0}^{T}h(X(t))\,dt\overset{\mathcal{D}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^{2}),\quad T\rightarrow\infty,

(1.3)

for a
m
-mean zero function
h
, where
\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^{2})
is a centered normal distribution with variance
\tau^{2}
. The work at hand, which we will motivate now, is mainly devoted to these two types of limit theorems for a particular class of diffusion processes.

Consider the solution
X_{\varepsilon}
of the one-dimensional SDE

dX_{\varepsilon}(t)=\left[f_{0}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}f_{1}\left(\frac{X_{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]dt+\sigma(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],

(1.4)

where
\varepsilon>0
is a small-scale parameter. Following the seminal work of
[
14
,
2
]
, the later works of
[
23
,
16
,
18
]
, and possibly the most recent contribution of
[
21
]
, this puts us, under appropriate assumptions on the coefficient functions, into the setting of homogenization for SDEs. Homogenization for SDEs is a type of diffusion approximation in which the process
X_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly in
C([0,T];\mathbb{R})
to the process
X
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
for fixed
T>0
, where
X
is the solution to (
1.1
). The homogenized coefficient functions
b
and
\overline{\sigma}
in (
1.1
) depend on
f,g,\sigma
, and the invariant measure of the fast process defined through
Y_{\varepsilon}:=X_{\varepsilon}/\varepsilon
. In this framework, we aim to prove the following two limit theorems. Firstly, a mean ergodic theorem (MET) of the type

\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}h_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(x)\,dm(x)=\tau^{2},\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

(1.5)

for a suitable sequence of test function
h_{\varepsilon}
depending on
\varepsilon
such that
h_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow h
, and exploding time horizon such that
T_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\infty
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Secondly, we want to prove a CLT of the type

\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}h_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\overset{\mathcal{D}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^{2}),\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(1.6)

The interest for these two limit theorems stems from the increasing emergence of parametric and nonparametric estimation procedures for homogenized equations like (
1.1
), but with observations coming from perturbed equations like (
1.4
). While this is an instance of model-misspecification, the laws of the processes are similar in a weak sense when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
; and, yet, it turns out that many estimators cannot recover homogenized parameters from perturbed observations, as it has been repeatedly demonstrated in
[
17
,
15
,
12
,
19
]
. In the aforementioned references, as well as in
[
9
,
1
]
, the authors utilize exclusively sequential limits to prove asymptotic properties of their estimators, that is, they first let
T\rightarrow\infty
and then
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, or vice versa. To the best of our knowledge, the case of taking simultaneous limits where almost every relevant quantity depends on
\varepsilon
has not been considered yet. This can possibly be attributed to the difficulty, that many relevant quantities and bounds explode for
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, so that classically established results in the literature for diffusion processes cannot be directly applied. Hence, with this work, we hope to lay the theoretical groundwork for the rigorous asymptotic analysis of estimators for homogenized models when confronted with perturbed data.

The main contributions of our work are the proofs of the two limit theorems; a stochastic convergence result stringently connected to the proof of the CLT, and a simple application to parameter estimation in a continuous-time setting for homogenized models under perturbed data. At this point, it is important to emphasize that the carried out proof ideas are classical, and we will take great care in crediting the authors of the original proofs; however, the
\varepsilon
-dependence in all relevant quantities demands nonetheless a careful investigation of the existing proofs.

The main content of the article starts with section
2
, which is itself divided into three subsections. The first subsection deals with the precise definition of the framework, the assumptions under which we will work, and the rigorous proof of the MET, which is stated in Theorem
2.9
. Subsection
2.2
is devoted to the proof of the required stochastic convergence result contained in Corollary
2.15
. The last subsection
2.3
presents the proof of the CLT whose statement can be found in Theorem
2.18
. Finally, an exemplary application to parameter estimation is discussed in section
3
, with the main result being Proposition
3.2
. In the conclusion, we summarize the main contributions again, and we also give remarks on potential extensions of the results to more general test functions and to the multidimensional case.

2
On
\varepsilon
-dependent limit theorems

2.1
An
\varepsilon
-dependent mean ergodic theorem

Consider a probability space
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
with a filtration
\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,\infty)}
that satisfies the usual conditions, and a one-dimensional Brownian motion
W:=\left(W(t),\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,\infty)}
on that probability space. Assume further that there is another one-dimensional Brownian motion
\overline{W}:=\left(\overline{W}(t),\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,\infty)}
that is independent of
W
and lives on the same probability space. This construction is feasible through a suitable extension of the original probability space.

Fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
once and for all. Consider the following one-dimensional stochastic differential equation (SDE) in
\mathbb{R}
, depending on a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
,

dX_{\varepsilon}(t)=b_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))dt+\sigma(X_{\varepsilon}(t))dW(t),\quad t>0,\quad X_{\varepsilon}(0)=x_{0},

(2.1)

with suitable Borel-measurable functions
b_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and assume, for the moment, that there exists a unique, strong solution to this SDE on the given probability space. We suppose a setting where
X_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly to another stochastic process
X
in
C([0,T];\mathbb{R})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Here,
X
is the unique, strong solution to the stochastic differential equation

dX(t)=b(X(t))dt+\overline{\sigma}(X(t))d\overline{W}(t),\quad t>0,\quad X(0)=x_{0},

(2.2)

with Borel-measurable functions
b\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\overline{\sigma}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Note that
\sigma\neq\overline{\sigma}
in general. Assume further that the following scale functions exist

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{z}\frac{2b_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\sigma(y)^{2}}\,dy\right)\,dz,\quad f(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{z}\frac{2b(y)}{\overline{\sigma}(y)^{2}}\,dy\right)\,dz,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.3)

These functions are harmonic with respect to the differential operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}:=b_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\partial_{xx},\quad\mathcal{A}:=b\partial_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{\sigma}^{2}\partial_{xx},

(2.4)

respectively, that is
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}=0
and
\mathcal{A}f=0
. Setting
\xi_{\varepsilon}:=f_{\varepsilon}\circ X_{\varepsilon}
and
\xi:=f\circ X
and applying the (generalized) Itô formula yields the transformed stochastic differential equations

\displaystyle d\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{1}{\rho_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}(t))}dW(t),\quad t>0,\quad\xi_{\varepsilon}(0)=f_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}),

(2.5)

\displaystyle d\xi(t)=\frac{1}{\rho(\xi(t))}dU(t),\quad t>0,\quad\xi(0)=f(x_{0}).

(2.6)

Here, the functions
\rho_{\varepsilon}
and
\rho
are given by

\displaystyle\rho_{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{1}{\sigma(g_{\varepsilon}(x))f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(g_{\varepsilon}(x))},\quad\rho(x):=\frac{1}{\overline{\sigma}(g(x))f^{\prime}(g(x))},

(2.7)

where
g
and
g_{\varepsilon}
are the inverse functions to
f
and
f_{\varepsilon}
, respectively.

In the following we want to derive an "
\varepsilon
-dependent" MET, i.e., under appropriate assumptions, which we state soon, and for arbitrary initial conditions, we want to prove the validity of

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

(2.8)

where
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a suitable measurable function,
C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}\,dx
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
T_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\infty
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. If we further assume the convergence

\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\rightarrow\frac{1}{C_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y)\rho(y)^{2}\,dy,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

with some function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
C_{\rho}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x)^{2}\,dx
, then it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(y)\rho(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.9)

To prove (
2.8
) we will follow along the lines of the original proofs by Gikhman and Skorokhod,
[
5
]
, on the one hand and by Sundar,
[
22
]
, on the other hand. They proved the MET for the case where no
\varepsilon
-dependence is present, and the limit is established for
T\rightarrow\infty
. We will carefully adapt and adequately combine the separate approaches.

In this work, we will work under the following assumptions:

Assumptions (C)
.

i)

The functions
b
and
\overline{\sigma}
are locally Lipschitz-continuous on
\mathbb{R}
and
\overline{\sigma}
is strictly positive.

ii)

The function
\sigma
is locally Lipschitz-continuous on
\mathbb{R}
and satisfies
0<\sigma\leq M
on
\mathbb{R}
for some
M>0
. Moreover, for each
\varepsilon>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a constant
L_{N}>0
such that for all
x,y\in[-N,N]

\displaystyle|b_{\varepsilon}(x)-b_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq\frac{L_{N}}{\varepsilon^{2}}|x-y|,\quad|b_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\frac{L_{N}}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1+|x|).

Assumptions (MET)
.

i)

\lim_{x\rightarrow\pm\infty}f(x)=\pm\infty
.

ii)

C_{\rho}<\infty.

iii)

There exist constants
c_{f},c_{\sigma},C_{f},C_{\sigma}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
and on
\mathbb{R}

c_{f}f^{\prime}\leq f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\leq C_{f}f^{\prime},\qquad c_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma}\leq\sigma\leq C_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma}.

Note that we are not suggesting that these assumptions are minimal requirements for the following results to work. On the contrary, some of them, e.g., the pointwise bounds in
(MET)
iii), can be rather restrictive and for certain proofs even superfluous. Indeed, the same results can be obtained if, for example, in
(MET)
(iii) there exist two integrable functions
k_{1},k_{2}
such that
c_{f}k_{1}\leq f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\leq C_{f}k_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}
. But in order to keep the exposition clear without repeating similar arguments and altering assumptions over and over again, we will stick to them throughout this work. Note, however, that we do not impose global Lipschitz assumptions on the drift coefficients, as it was originally done in
[
5
]
and
[
22
]
. We also need to emphasize the circumstance that the local Lipschitz and local linear growth constant in
(C)
ii) explode as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. This makes the analysis substantially more difficult.

Observe that an ordinary integral transformation leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\sigma(x)^{2}f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)}\,dx\right)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\sigma(x)^{2}f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(g_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(g_{\varepsilon}(x))\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}\,dx,

indicating that it is sufficient to prove the mean ergodic theorem for the solution of equation (
2.5
).

Remark 2.1
.

As it has been established in
[
5
, §18]
and
[
22
]
, but will also become apparent here, the invariant density of a stochastic process

(2.10)

For fixed

\mu_{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}\sigma(x)^{2}f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)}=\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}\sigma(x)^{2}}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{x}\frac{2b_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\sigma(y)^{2}}\,dy\right),\quad Z_{\varepsilon}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sigma(y)^{-2}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{x}\frac{2b_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\sigma(y)^{2}}\,dy\right)\,dx.

(2.11)

(C)

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume
(C)
and
(MET)
.

i)

For each of the SDEs defined in (

), (

), (

\mathbb{E}\,\left|\xi_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|\leq\left|f_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\right|\exp(t),\quad t\geq 0.

(2.12)

ii)

There exist constants

such that
d_{\rho}\leq C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\leq D_{\rho}
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

iii)

For every

Proof.

i) Strong uniqueness for (

ii) This follows by a simple integral substitution and

and, similarly, for the lower bound.

iii) First note that by

\varepsilon>0

c_{f}f\leq f_{\varepsilon}\leq C_{f}f,\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}.

(2.13)

Now let

\displaystyle\int_{|y|>M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{|f_{\varepsilon}(y)|>M}\frac{dy}{\sigma(y)^{2}f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(y)}\leq\frac{1}{c_{\sigma}^{2}c_{f}}\int_{|f(y)|>M/(c_{f}\vee C_{f})}\frac{dy}{\overline{\sigma}(y)^{2}f^{\prime}(y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\sigma}^{2}c_{f}}\int_{|y|>M/(c_{f}\vee C_{f})}\rho(y)^{2}\,dy<\delta.\qed

Before we proceed, we have to introduce a sequence of nondecreasing stopping times that will aid us several times in the subsequent proofs. The introduction of this stopping time is mostly a technical inconvenience due to the local Lipschitz assumptions, but, nonetheless, required to make everything rigorous. For this, fix
\varepsilon>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and a bounded, Borel-measurable function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle K_{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(s))\,ds,\quad t\in[0,\infty),

(2.17)

\displaystyle M_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\sign(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(s)-z)\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(s))\,dW(s),\quad t\in[0,\infty),\quad z\in\mathbb{R},

, where

is the space of continuous, local martingales starting in zero. Denote by

\left(I_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}K_{\varepsilon}(s)\,dM_{\varepsilon}^{z}(s),\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,\infty)}\in\mathscr{M}^{\text{c, loc}},

for each

(\tau_{n}^{\varphi_{\varepsilon},x})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\tau_{n}^{\varphi_{\varepsilon},x}\rightarrow\infty

\left(M_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t\wedge\tau_{n}^{\varphi_{\varepsilon},x}),\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,\infty)},\quad\left(I_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t\wedge\tau_{n}^{\varphi_{\varepsilon},x}),\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,\infty)}\in\mathscr{M}_{2}^{\text{c}},

(2.19)

for each

\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{z}\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-2}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(s))\,ds,\quad t\geq 0.

In particular, it does not depend on

\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{z}\rangle
and
\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}
are continuous, we can additionally choose the localizing sequence
\tau_{n}^{\varphi_{\varepsilon},x}
in such a way that

\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{z}\rangle_{\cdot\,\wedge\tau_{n}^{\varphi_{\varepsilon},x}}\leq n,\quad\text{and}\quad|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\,\cdot\wedge\tau_{n}^{\varphi_{\varepsilon},x})|\leq n,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

(2.20)

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
z\in\mathbb{R}
.

In addition to that, recall the Meyer-Tanaka formula for a continuous, local martingale, say
Y_{t}=Y_{0}+M_{t}
with
M\in\mathscr{M}^{\text{c, loc}}
, that is, for
z\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\geq 0

|Y_{t}-z|=|Y_{0}-z|+\int_{0}^{t}\sign(Y_{s}-z)\,dM_{s}+2\Lambda_{t}^{z}(Y),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(2.21)

Here,
\Lambda_{t}^{z}
is the local time process for
Y
, cf.
[
6
, Theorem 3.7.1]
. Recall also the occupation density formula, which gives us for every Borel-measurable function
k\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
the identity

\int_{0}^{t}k(Y_{s})\,d\langle M\rangle_{s}=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}k(z)\Lambda_{t}^{z}(Y)\,dz,\quad t\geq 0,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(2.22)

We will now utilize these formulas and the introduced sequence of stopping times to prove a result similar to Proposition
2.4
. The proof idea is borrowed from
[
22
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(\varphi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a uniformly bounded sequence of Borel-measurable, nonnegative functions on
\mathbb{R}
with
\cup_{\varepsilon>0}\,\mathrm{supp}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\subset[-R,R]
for some
R>0
. Under Assumptions
(C)
and
(MET)
, it holds for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon>0

\left|\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))\,t\,dt-\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\,t\,dt\right|\leq\frac{8\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}|x-y|}{T_{\varepsilon}}.

(2.23)

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that
x\geq y
. Using Fubini’s theorem, we first have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))\,t\,dt-\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\,t\,dt=\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))-\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\,ds

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))-\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt-\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{s}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\,ds.

Define the sequence of stopping times
\tau_{n}:=\tau_{n}^{\varphi_{\varepsilon},x}\wedge\tau_{n}^{\varphi_{\varepsilon},y}
through the previously constructed stopping times. With the occupation density formula (
2.22
) we can write for any
s\in[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{s\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt=\mathbb{E}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\left[L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x})-L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{y})\right]\,dz.

The Meyer-Tanaka formula (
2.21
) gives
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x})=|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(s\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-|x-z|-\int_{0}^{s\wedge\tau_{n}}\sign(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(r)-z)\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(r))\,dW(r),

and, similarly, for
L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{y})
. The respective stochastic integrals in these formulas are martingales, cf. (
2.19
), starting in zero and, thus, have expectation zero. Hence

\mathbb{E}\,\left[L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x})-L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{y})\right]=\mathbb{E}\,|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(s\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-|x-z|-\mathbb{E}\,|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{y}(s\wedge\tau_{n})-z|+|y-z|.

(2.24)

With the uniqueness of the solution of the SDE (
2.5
) and
x\geq y
, a stopping time argument shows that
\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(r)\geq\xi_{\varepsilon}^{y}(r)
for all
r\in[0,\infty)
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. Using this fact, a simple case distinction shows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(s\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-\mathbb{E}\,|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{y}(s\wedge\tau_{n})-z|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\,\left[\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(s\wedge\tau_{n})-\xi_{\varepsilon}^{y}(s\wedge\tau_{n})\right]=x-y,

\displaystyle|y-z|-|x-z|

\displaystyle\leq x-y,

where we used the martingale property again. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}\,\left[L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x})-L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{y})\right]\leq 2(x-y).

Observe that by (
2.20
), the compact support of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
, and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities we can upper bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)|\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x})\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)|\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(s\wedge\tau_{n})-z|+|x-z|+\left|\int_{0}^{s\wedge\tau_{n}}\sign(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(r)-z)\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(r))\,dW(r)\right|\right]\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\int_{[-R,R]}\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\left[\mathbb{E}\,|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(s\wedge\tau_{n})|+|x|+2|z|+\mathbb{E}\,\left|\int_{0}^{s\wedge\tau_{n}}\sign(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(r)-z)\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(r))\,dW(r)\right|\right]\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}\left[n+|x|+2R+B\mathbb{E}\,\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{z}\rangle_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{1/2}\right]\leq\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}\left[n+|x|+2R+Bn^{1/2}\right]<\infty,

where
B>0
is the universal constant coming from the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities. A completely analog estimate is obtained when
L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x})
is replaced with
L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{y})
. Hence, we can use Fubini’s theorem together with (
2.1
) to obtain the estimate

\mathbb{E}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\left[L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x})-L_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{y})\right]\,dz\leq 2(x-y)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz.

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, the dominated convergence theorem implies in (
2.24
) for any
s\in[0,\infty)

\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{s}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\leq 2(x-y)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz\leq 2(x-y)\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}.

Therefore, we conclude with Proposition
2.4

\left|\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))t\,dt-\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))t\,dt\right|\leq\frac{8\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}(x-y)}{T_{\varepsilon}}.\qed

The proof of the next result can be obtained, once again, by the same arguments as in the original material in
[
5
, §18]
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed and assume
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}\,dx<\infty
. Then, for any Borel-measurable function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)|\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}\,dx<\infty
it holds for
t\geq 0

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy.

(2.25)

The next lemma gives us another key ingredient for our sought-after main result.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(\varphi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a uniformly bounded sequence of Borel-measurable, nonnegative functions on
\mathbb{R}
. Under Assumptions
(C)
and
(MET)
, it holds for
x_{\varepsilon}:=f_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,\end{aligned}

(2.26)

and, similarly, if
\cup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathrm{supp}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\subset[-R,R]
for some
R>0
, then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left|\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))t\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.\end{aligned}

(2.27)

Proof.

We will only prove the first claim (
2.26
) as the proof of the second claim works the same. We would only have to replace estimate (
2.16
) with (
2.23
) in the following.

To prove claim (
2.26
), we first obtain from equation (
2.25
)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\;\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy-\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\,dt\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))-\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\right)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{\rho}}\left[2\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\int_{|x_{\varepsilon}-y|>T_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy+\int_{|x_{\varepsilon}-y|\leq T_{\varepsilon}}\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))-\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{y}_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\right|\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right].

For the second term inside the brackets we use estimate (
2.16
) to get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|\\[7.11317pt]
\leq&\frac{2\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}}{d_{\rho}}\left[\int_{|x_{\varepsilon}-y|>T_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy+\frac{2D_{\rho}}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{|x_{\varepsilon}-y|\leq T_{\varepsilon}}|x_{\varepsilon}-y|\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right].\end{aligned}

(2.28)

Now let
\delta>0
be arbitrary. By Lemma
2.2
iii) we can choose
M>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0

\int_{|y|>M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy<\delta.

Therefore, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
we have with (
2.13
)

\displaystyle\int_{|x_{\varepsilon}-y|>T_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\leq\int_{|y|>T_{\varepsilon}-|x_{\varepsilon}|}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\leq\int_{|y|>T_{\varepsilon}-(c_{f}\vee C_{f})|f(x_{0})|}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy<\delta,

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{|x_{\varepsilon}-y|\leq T_{\varepsilon}}|x_{\varepsilon}-y|\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\left[\int_{|x_{\varepsilon}-y|\leq T_{\varepsilon},\;|y|\leq M}|x_{\varepsilon}-y|\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy+\int_{|x_{\varepsilon}-y|\leq T_{\varepsilon},\;|y|>M}|x_{\varepsilon}-y|\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left[(c_{f}\vee C_{f})|f(x_{0})|+M\right]D_{\rho}}{T_{\varepsilon}}+\int_{|y|>M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left[(c_{f}\vee C_{f})|f(x_{0})|+M\right]D_{\rho}}{T_{\varepsilon}}+\delta.

Thus

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|\leq\frac{2\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}(1+2D_{\rho})}{d_{\rho}}\delta.

Letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
yields the claim.
∎

The next lemma will be used twice in the proof of the MET. Compared to the results in
[
22
]
, we resort to localization arguments with the previously introduced sequence of stopping times.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\varphi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon}
be bounded, Borel-measurable, nonnegative functions such that
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\subset[-R_{1},R_{1}]
and
\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{\varepsilon})\subset[-R_{2},R_{2}]
for some
R_{1},R_{2}>0
. Set
\tau_{n}:=\tau_{n}^{\varphi_{\varepsilon},x_{\varepsilon}}
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{t}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s))\,ds\,dt\\[7.11317pt]
=\,&\mathbb{E}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-z|\right]dt\,dz.\end{aligned}

(2.29)

Proof.

Applying the occupation density formula (
2.22
) gives
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{t}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s))\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\left[L_{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}})-L_{t}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}})\right]dz\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\left[L_{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}})-L_{t}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}})\right]dt\,dz,

where we used Tonelli’s theorem in the last equality since
L^{z}_{t}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}})
is nondecreasing in
t
. The Meyer-Tanaka formula (
2.21
) yields
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\displaystyle L_{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}})-L_{t}^{z}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}})

\displaystyle=|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-z|-\int_{t}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\sign(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s)-z)\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s))\,dW(s)

\displaystyle=|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-z|-\left[M_{\varepsilon}^{z}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-M_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t)\right],

so that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{t}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s))\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-z|\right]dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\left[M_{\varepsilon}^{z}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-M_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t)\right]dt\Bigg]dz,

since
M_{\varepsilon}^{z}
,
\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}
are continuous processes
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is bounded with compact support. Stochastic integration by parts implies
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}K_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dM_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t)

\displaystyle=K_{\varepsilon}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})M_{\varepsilon}^{z}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}M_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\left[M_{\varepsilon}^{z}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-M_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t)\right]\,dt,

that is, we have
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{t}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s))\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-z|\right]dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}K_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dM_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t)\Bigg]dz.

We require integrability to apply linearity and Fubini’s theorem with respect to
\mathbb{E}\,
in what follows. For this, observe that by (
2.12
) and (
2.20
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-z|+|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-z|\right]dz\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}\left(\mathbb{E}\,|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})|+2R_{2}+\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\,|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)|\,dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}\left(n+2R_{2}+\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})|}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\exp(t)\,dt\right)<\infty,

and using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities we can upper bound with a universal constant
B>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\left|\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}K_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dM_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t)\,dt\right|\,dz\leq B\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\,\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{z}\rangle_{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}^{1/2}\,dz\leq B\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}n^{1/2}<\infty.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{t}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s))\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\Bigg[\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-z|\right]dt

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}K_{\varepsilon}(t)\,dM_{\varepsilon}^{z}(t)\,dt\Bigg]dz.

Finally, we use the fact that the last displayed expectation is zero due to (
2.19
) to conclude the claim.
∎

Using these preparatory results, we are now in a position to state and prove the MET.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
(\varphi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a uniformly bounded sequence of Borel-measurable, real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}
. Under Assumptions
(C)
and
(MET)
it holds for
x_{\varepsilon}=f_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})
and
T_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\infty
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.30)

Remark 2.10
.

In terms of the original quantities, i.e., with the process
X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}
, that solves (
2.1
) and starts in
x_{0}
, and the invariant density
\mu_{\varepsilon}
in (
2.11
), the limit (
2.30
) reads as

\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(t))\,dt-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\mu_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy\right|^{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.31)

Proof.

We will first prove the result for the case where the functions
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
are nonnegative with
\cup_{\varepsilon>0}\;\mathrm{supp}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\subset[-R,R]
for some
R>0
. Note that we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}\right]-\left(\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\,\left(\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right)\frac{2}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy.

We want to prove that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}\right]-\left(\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right)^{2}\leq 0,

(2.32)

because then (
2.30
) follows from the assumptions and Lemma
2.7
. For this, let
\delta>0
be arbitrary. Choose
M>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0

\int_{|y|>M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy<\delta.

With the sequence of stopping times
(\tau_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
from the preceding proof of Lemma
2.8
we have for all
\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{t}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s))\,ds\,dt\\[7.11317pt]
=\,&\mathbb{E}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-z|\right]dt\,dz,\end{aligned}

(2.33)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{t}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\,\mathds{1}_{[-M,M]}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s))\,ds\,dt\\[7.11317pt]
=\,&\mathbb{E}\,\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-y|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-y|\right]dt\,dy.\end{aligned}

(2.34)

First observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\,\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}\,(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})^{2}\delta+\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\,\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}T_{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\delta+\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\,\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}\right].

Let us analyze the second term in detail. Equation (
2.33
), Fubini’s theorem, and the triangle inequality enable us to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\,\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle 2\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\,\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{t}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\,\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s))\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle 2\,\mathbb{E}\,\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-z|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-z|\right]dt\,dz\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle 2\,\mathbb{E}\,\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-y|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-y|\right]dt\,dz\,dy

\displaystyle+4\,\mathbb{E}\,\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))|y-z|dt\,dz\,dy

For the first term of the last inequality we have by equation (
2.34
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-y|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-y|\right]dt\,dz\,dy

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz\,\mathbb{E}\,\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\left[|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n})-y|-|\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t)-y|\right]dt\,dy

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz\,\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\int_{t}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\mathds{1}_{[-M,M]}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(s))\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz\,\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}-t)\,dt

For the second term it holds by the assumptions

\displaystyle\hskip-4.26773pt\left|\mathbb{E}\,\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))|y-z|dt\,dz\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\int_{|y|\leq M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\int_{|z|\leq R}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}|y-z|\,dz\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}\right)^{2}(M+R)T_{\varepsilon}.

Therefore, combining all these estimates yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\,\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}\right]\leq\,

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}T_{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\delta+4\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}\right)^{2}(M+R)T_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz\,\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))(T_{\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{n}-t)\,dt.

Letting finally
n\rightarrow\infty
and using the dominated convergence theorem gives the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\,\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}\right]\leq\,

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}T_{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\delta+4\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}\right)^{2}(M+R)T_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz\,\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))(T_{\varepsilon}-t)\,dt.

This inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}\right]-\left(\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz\left[\frac{2}{T_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\,\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))(T_{\varepsilon}-t)\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}+\frac{4}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}T_{\varepsilon}}\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}D_{\rho}\right)^{2}(M+R)

The RHS converges by Lemma
2.7
to
\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}\delta/d_{\rho}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, so that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\mathbb{E}\,\left[\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}\right]-\left(\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\,dz\right)^{2}\right)\leq\frac{\delta}{d_{\rho}}\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}.

(2.35)

Letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
eventually yields the claim (
2.32
). Thus, the convergence result (
2.30
) is established for Borel-measurable, nonnegative functions
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
with
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}<\infty
and
\cap_{\varepsilon>0}\;\mathrm{supp}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\subset[-R,R]
for some
R>0
. We extend the convergence result now to measurable, nonnegative functions
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
with
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}<\infty
. For this, let
\delta>0
be arbitrary and choose again
M>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0

\int_{|y|>M}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy<\delta.

Set
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{(m)}:=\varphi_{\varepsilon}\mathds{1}_{[-m^{*},m^{*}]}
with
m^{*}:=m^{*}(m):=m\vee M
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we can upper bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}\leq\,

\displaystyle 8\Bigg[\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{(m)}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{(m)}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left|\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}^{(m)}(y)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\right)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}\Bigg]

We estimate the first term as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{(m)}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\mathds{1}_{[-m^{*},m^{*}]^{c}}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}\,\left|1-\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathds{1}_{[-m^{*},m^{*}]}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle 4\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathds{1}_{[-m^{*},m^{*}]}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{|y|\leq m^{*}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+4\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{|y|>m^{*}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle 4\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}\left[\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\mathds{1}_{[-m^{*},m^{*}]}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{|y|\leq m^{*}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{\delta}{d_{\rho}}\right)^{2}\right],

where the last upper bound converges to
4\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\delta/d_{\rho}\right)^{2}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. By our previously established result in this proof, we also have the convergence of the second term for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. The third term satisfies

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\psi_{\varepsilon}^{(m)}(y)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\right)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}=\left|\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\mathds{1}_{[-m^{*},m^{*}]^{c}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}\leq\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\frac{\delta}{d_{\rho}}\right)^{2}.

In summary, we have

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}\leq 40\left(\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\frac{\delta}{d_{\rho}}\right)^{2},

which implies, by letting
\delta\rightarrow 0
,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\xi_{\varepsilon}^{x_{\varepsilon}}(t))\,dt-\frac{1}{C_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)^{2}\,dy\right|^{2}\leq 0.

Hence, the convergence result holds for a uniformly bounded sequence
(\varphi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
of Borel-measurable, nonnegative functions on
\mathbb{R}
, too. The asserted full result is now straightforward and can be accomplished by splitting the test functions into positive and negative parts and applying the preceding result to these nonnegative functions.
∎

2.2
Stochastic convergence result

This section is concerned with the stochastic convergence of the quantity
\left|X_{\varepsilon}(T_{\varepsilon})/\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}\right|
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, which immediately entails the stochastic convergence of any other moment. The final result is contained in Corollary
2.15
at the end of this section. Most of the crucial proof ideas of this section originate from
[
7
, Chapter 4]
, but, as in the preceding section, we must practice caution due to the
\varepsilon
-dependence. We make the following adjustment to the Assumptions
(C)
.

Assumptions (C*)
.

i)

The functions
b
and
\overline{\sigma}
are locally Lipschitz-continuous on
\mathbb{R}
and
\overline{\sigma}
is strictly positive.

ii)

The function
\sigma
is locally Lipschitz-continuous on
\mathbb{R}
, and satisfies
m\leq\sigma\leq M
on
\mathbb{R}
for some
M,m>0
. For each
\varepsilon>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a constant
L_{N}>0
such that for all
x,y\in[-N,N]

\displaystyle|b_{\varepsilon}(x)-b_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq\frac{L_{N}}{\varepsilon^{2}}|x-y|,\quad|b_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\frac{L_{N}}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1+|x|).

So the only difference between
(C)
and
(C*)
is the strict nondegeneracy of
\sigma
on
\mathbb{R}
. This could be relaxed to a local strict nondegeneracy condition and we would still get the same results, but we will refrain from doing this.

The first major step towards Corollary
2.15
is establishing Schauder interior estimates for the parabolic equation

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=0,\quad t\in(0,T),\;x\in U,

(2.36)

where
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
was defined in (
2.4
),
T>0
, and
U
is an open, bounded interval in
\mathbb{R}
. Let us introduce some additional notation that is commonly used in the analysis of parabolic partial differential equations. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
we say that a function
f\colon(0,T)\times U\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Hölder-continuous on
(0,T)\times U
with exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
if for any closed subset
D\subset(0,T)\times U
there exists a constant
H>0
such that for all
(t,x),(r,y)\in D
it holds

|f(t,x)-f(r,y)|\leq H\left[|x-y|^{2}+|t-r|\right]^{\alpha/2}.

(2.37)

Furthermore, we define the function
d\colon[0,\infty)\times\overline{U}\to\mathbb{R}
by

d(t,x):=\text{dist}(x,\partial U)\wedge\sqrt{t},\quad t\in[0,\infty),\;x\in\overline{U}.

(2.38)

At last, for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
we define the norms

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |d^{\,m},f|_{\alpha}:=&\sup_{(t,x)\in(0,T)\times U}|d(t,x)^{m}f(t,x)|&+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x),(r,y)\in(0,T)\times U\\
(t,x)\neq(r,y)\end{subarray}}|d(t,x)\wedge d(r,y)|^{m+\alpha}\frac{|f(t,x)-f(r,y)|}{\left[|x-y|^{2}+|t-r|\right]^{\alpha/2}}.\end{aligned}

(2.39)

Observe that whenever the norm
|d^{\,m},f|_{\alpha}
is finite for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then
f
is locally Hölder-continuous on
(0,T)\times U
with exponent
\alpha
.

The following result, whose proof is standard in the literature for partial differential equations (PDEs), see
[
4
, Chapter 4]
, merely requires an explicit tracking of the constant appearing in the upper bound with respect to
\varepsilon
. The acquired estimates on the constant are far from being optimal in any sense, but given our assumptions, this will serve our purposes, as we will soon see.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\sigma
be bounded away from zero on
U
and let the coefficients
\sigma,b_{\varepsilon}
of the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
satisfy for an exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)

|d^{\,0},\sigma|_{\alpha}\leq K_{\sigma},\quad|d^{\,1},b_{\varepsilon}|_{\alpha}\leq K_{b}\,\varepsilon^{-2},

(2.40)

with some constants
K_{\sigma},K_{b}>0
. Assume that
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1,2}((0,T)\times U)
is a solution of (
2.36
) such that
\sup_{(0,T)\times U}|u_{\varepsilon}|<\infty
and
u_{\varepsilon}
,
\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}
,
\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}
,
\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}
are locally Hölder-continuous on
(0,T)\times U
with exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a constant
K>0
, only depending on
K_{\sigma},K_{b}
and
\alpha
, such that for all
\eta>0

|d^{\,0},u_{\varepsilon}|_{\alpha}+|d^{\,1},\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}|_{\alpha}+|d^{\,2},\partial^{2}_{x}u_{\varepsilon}|_{\alpha}+|d^{\,2},\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}|_{\alpha}\leq K\varepsilon^{-(10+\eta)}\sup_{(0,T)\times U}|u_{\varepsilon}|.

(2.41)

Proof.

Carefully tracking the constants in
[
4
, Chapter 4, Section 4]
with respect to
\varepsilon
yields the claim.
∎

Our aim is to apply Proposition
2.11
to the Feller transition probability function
P_{\varepsilon}
of
X_{\varepsilon}
, which is given by the relation

P_{\varepsilon}(x,t,A):=\mathbb{P}(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t)\in A),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0,\;A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}).

(2.42)

Evidently, it is stochastically continuous, since for all
\delta>0

P_{\varepsilon}(x,t,B_{\delta}(x))=\mathbb{P}(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t)\in B_{\delta}(x))\rightarrow 1,\quad t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Hence,
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
implies that
(t,x)\mapsto P_{\varepsilon}(x,t,A)
is a
\mathscr{B}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
-measurable function for any
A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})
, which makes it possible to write down certain integrals.

Before we proceed, we introduce the following parabolic PDE with Dirichlet boundary conditions and coefficients that do not depend on time

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=0,&\quad&t\in(0,T],\;x\in U,\\[7.11317pt]
&u_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=g(x),&\quad&x\in\overline{U},\\[7.11317pt]
&u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=h(t,x),&\quad&t\in(0,T],\;x\in\partial U,\\[7.11317pt]
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(2.43)

with given functions
g\in C(\overline{U})
and
h\in C((0,T]\times\partial U)
. If a solution to this initial-boundary value problem exists, then it satisfies the Feynman-Kac formula, cf.
[
7
, Lemma 3.3, Remark 3.13]
, that is

u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\,\left[g(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))\mathds{1}_{\{t\leq\tau_{U}\}}+h(t-\tau_{U},X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}))\mathds{1}_{\{t>\tau_{U}\}}\right],\quad t\in[0,T],\;x\in\overline{U},

(2.44)

where
\tau_{U}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\,|\,X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t)\notin\overline{U}\}
is the first exit time of the process
X_{\varepsilon}^{x}
from
\overline{U}
. With these facts at our disposal, we may now state and prove the following lemma.

Lemma 2.12
.

Assume
(C*)
ii). Then, for any fixed
A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})
, the function
(t,x)\mapsto P_{\varepsilon}(x,t,A)
is a solution of

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=0,\quad(t,x)\in D,

(2.45)

where
D\subset(0,T)\times U
is an arbitrary subdomain with closure in
(0,T]\times\overline{U}
.
Moreover, for
(t_{0},T)\times C\subset(0,T)\times U
with
t_{0}\in(0,T)
and closed
C
we have with arbitrary
\eta>0

\sup_{(t,x)\in(t_{0},T)\times C}|\partial_{x}P_{\varepsilon}(x,t,A)|\leq\frac{K\varepsilon^{-(10+\eta)}}{\mathrm{dist}(\partial C,\partial U)\wedge\sqrt{t_{0}}}

(2.46)

where
K>0
is the same constant as in (
2.40
).

Proof.

First note that
(C*)
ii) implies that the coefficients
\sigma,b_{\varepsilon}
of the operator
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
satisfy (
2.40
) for any exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Indeed, since the coefficients do not depend on time, it is easy to prove

\displaystyle|d^{\,0},\sigma|_{\alpha}\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\overline{U}}|\sigma(x)|+\mathrm{diam}(U)^{\alpha}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in\times\overline{U}\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle|d^{\,1},b_{\varepsilon}|_{\alpha}\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(U)\sup_{x\in\overline{U}}|b_{\varepsilon}(x)|+\mathrm{diam}(U)^{1+\alpha}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in\times\overline{U}\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|b_{\varepsilon}(x)-b_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}},

which yields the inequalities in (
2.40
). Observe also that these constants do not depend on
T
.
With these estimates and the strict nondegeneracy of
\sigma
, the existence of a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to (
2.43
) with locally Hölder-continuous derivatives of all relevant orders for any exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
is secured, see
[
4
, Chapter 3]
, whenever the initial and boundary data are continuous.
We can write for
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\overline{U}

\displaystyle P_{\varepsilon}(x,t,A)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,\left[\mathds{1}_{A}(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))\mathds{1}_{\{t\leq\tau_{U}\}}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t)\in A,t>\tau_{U}\right).

(2.47)

The second term can be expressed using the strong Markov property as follows

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{P}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t)\in A,t>\tau_{U}\right)&=\int_{\left\{\tau_{U}<t\right\}}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t)\in A\,\big|\,\mathcal{F}_{\tau_{U}}\right)(\omega)\,d\mathbb{P}(\omega)\\[7.11317pt]
&=\int_{\Omega}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tau_{U}(\omega)<t\right\}}P_{\varepsilon}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}),t-\tau_{U},A\right)(\omega)\,d\mathbb{P}(\omega)\\[7.11317pt]
&=\int_{\partial U}\int_{0}^{t}P_{\varepsilon}\left(y,t-s,A\right)\,d\mathbb{P}^{\left(\tau_{U},X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U})\right)}(s,y).\end{aligned}

(2.48)

In the last step we used the
\mathscr{B}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
-measurability of
(t,x)\mapsto P_{\varepsilon}(x,t,A)
. Hence,

\mathbb{P}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t)\in A,t>\tau_{U}\right)=\mathbb{E}\,\left[P_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}),t-\tau_{U},A)\mathds{1}_{\{t>\tau_{U}\}}\right].

(2.49)

Inserting (
2.49
) into (
2.47
) gives for
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\overline{U}

P_{\varepsilon}(x,t,A)=\mathbb{E}\,\left[\mathds{1}_{A}(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(t))\mathds{1}_{\{t\leq\tau_{U}\}}+P_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}),t-\tau_{U},A)\mathds{1}_{\{t>\tau_{U}\}}\right],

(2.50)

which is (
2.44
), but with boundary conditions which are only Borel-measurable. We can take care of this with a standard approximation argument. For this purpose, let
B\in\mathscr{B}([0,T]\times\overline{U})
be arbitrary. We take two sequences of bounded, piecewise linear functions
k_{n}^{-},k_{n}^{+}\in C([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

k_{n}^{-}\leq\mathds{1}_{B}\leq k_{n}^{+},\quad\text{on }[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R},\;n\in\mathbb{N},

(2.51)

and, both,
k_{n}^{-}
and
k_{n}^{+}
converge pointwise from below and from above, respectively, to
\mathds{1}_{B}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. By the discussion at the beginning of the proof we have two sequences of solutions of (
2.43
)
u_{n}^{-}
and
u_{n}^{+}
, corresponding to
k_{n}^{-}
and
k_{n}^{+}
, respectively, with representations given by the Feynman-Kac formula, namely

u_{n}^{\pm}(t,x):=u_{n,\varepsilon}^{\pm}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\,\left[k_{n}^{\pm}(\tau_{U}\wedge t,X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}\wedge t))\right],\quad t\in[0,T],\;x\in\overline{U}.

(2.52)

A well-known result from the PDE literature, e.g.,
[
4
, p.80, Theorem 15]
, provides us with subsequences
u_{n_{j}}^{\pm}
that converge pointwise on
D
to some functions
u_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
as
j\rightarrow\infty
, where
D\subset(0,T)\times U
is an arbitrary subdomain with closure in
(0,T]\times\overline{U}
. Furthermore, these functions satisfy
\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}-\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}=0
on
D
and have locally Hölder-continuous derivatives of all relevant orders for any exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
. We will now show that
u_{\varepsilon}^{-}=u_{\varepsilon}^{+}
on
D
. By (
2.51
) and (
2.52
) it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\left[k_{n_{j}}^{-}(\tau_{U}\wedge t,X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}\wedge t))\right]

\displaystyle=u_{n_{j}}^{-}(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\,\left[\mathds{1}_{B}(\tau_{U}\wedge t,X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}\wedge t))\right]

\displaystyle\leq u_{n_{j}}^{+}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\,\left[k_{n_{j}}^{+}(\tau_{U}\wedge t,X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}\wedge t))\right]

for
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{U}
. Now, on the one hand, we have the pointwise convergence

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}u_{n_{j}}^{\pm}(t,x)=u_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times U,

and, on the other hand, monotone convergence gives

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\,\left[k_{n_{j}}^{+}(\tau_{U}\wedge t,X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}\wedge t))\right]=\mathbb{E}\,\left[\mathds{1}_{B}(\tau_{U}\wedge t,X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}\wedge t))\right],\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{U}.

Eventually, this implies

u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=u_{\varepsilon}^{-}(t,x)=u_{\varepsilon}^{+}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\,\left[\mathds{1}_{B}(\tau_{U}\wedge t,X_{\varepsilon}^{x}(\tau_{U}\wedge t))\right],\quad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times U.

(2.53)

We can extend the preceding result to simple functions and then to
\mathscr{B}([0,T]\times\overline{U})
-measurable functions. Hence, the function
(t,x)\mapsto P_{\varepsilon}(x,t,A)
fulfills (
2.45
) and, in particular, has locally Hölder-continuous derivatives of all relevant orders for any exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
. At last, we can derive (
2.46
) which is a consequence of (
2.41
), since

\sup_{(t,x)\in(t_{0},T)\times C}|\partial_{x}P_{\varepsilon}(x,t,A)|\leq\frac{|d^{\,1},\partial_{x}P_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot,A)|_{\alpha}}{\text{dist}(\partial C,\partial U)\wedge\sqrt{t_{0}}}\leq\frac{K\varepsilon^{-(10+\eta)}}{\text{dist}(\partial C,\partial U)\wedge\sqrt{t_{0}}}.\qed

For the remainder of this subsection and the next subsection, we will need the following additional assumption.

Assumptions (CLT)
.

There exist numbers
M,\gamma>0
such that for all
|y|>M
it holds

\sign(y)\frac{b(y)}{\overline{\sigma}(y)^{2}}\leq-\gamma.

(2.54)

The inequality indicates that the drift coefficient of the limit SDE (
2.2
), when sufficiently far away from the origin, stays below the threshold
-\gamma
. Note that this assumption already implies Assumptions
(MET)
i) and ii), so that, by Remark
2.1
, the invariant densities
\mu
and
\mu_{\varepsilon}
of
X
and
X_{\varepsilon}
, respectively, exist. Furthermore, if
(MET)
iii) holds, then it is easy to prove that for all
\varepsilon>0

c_{\mu}\mu\leq\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq C_{\mu}\mu\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R},

(2.55)

with some constants
c_{\mu},C_{\mu}>0
that are independent of
\varepsilon
.

In the proof of the next Proposition
2.13
we will make use of recurrence properties of the process
X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}
. First note that, under Assumptions
(C)
and
(MET)
, the process
X_{\varepsilon}
is recurrent for each
\varepsilon>0
, i.e., for any
y\in\mathbb{R}

\mathbb{P}(\tau^{y}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}\right)<\infty)=1,

(2.56)

where we recall
\tau^{y}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}\right)=\inf\{t>0\,|\,X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(t)=y\}
. This is a consequence of
[
7
, Lemma 3.9]
, cf. with
[
7
, Example 3.10]
, as well. We even have positive recurrence of
X_{\varepsilon}
uniformly in
\varepsilon
. To be precise, for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
it holds

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathbb{E}\,\tau^{y}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}\right)<\infty.

(2.57)

Indeed, by Lemma
2.3
, we have for all
\varepsilon>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\tau^{y}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}\right)=\mathbb{E}\,\tau^{f_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}),f_{\varepsilon}(y)}(\xi_{\varepsilon})\leq 2D_{\rho}|f_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq 2D_{\rho}(|f(x_{0})|+|f(y)|).

Proposition 2.13
.

If Assumptions
(C*)
,
(MET)
and
(CLT)
hold, then for any initial condition
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T_{\varepsilon})\right|>R(\varepsilon)\right)\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

(2.58)

where
R(\varepsilon)>0
is a sequence with
R(\varepsilon)\rightarrow\infty
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
which will be determined in the proof.

Proof.

Fix
I:=(-y,y)
for some
y>1
. Consider the initial datum
x_{0}\in I
first, and let
T>0
be arbitrary. Due to Lemma
2.12
it holds for all
x\in B_{r(\varepsilon)}(x_{0})
, where
r(\varepsilon)\downarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
will be determined soon, and
t_{0}\in(0,T)

\left|P_{\varepsilon}(x,T,B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c})-P_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},T,B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c})\right|\leq\frac{K\varepsilon^{-(10+\eta)}}{\mathrm{dist}(\partial B_{r(\varepsilon)}(x_{0}),\partial I)\wedge\sqrt{t_{0}}}|x-x_{0}|.

(2.59)

Evidently, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
it holds
\text{dist}(\partial B_{r(\varepsilon)}(x_{0}),\partial I)\rightarrow\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial I)=|x_{0}-y|>0
, so that choosing
r(\varepsilon):=\varepsilon^{11+\eta}
gives

\left|P_{\varepsilon}(x,T,B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c})-P_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},T,B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c})\right|\leq K\varepsilon,

(2.60)

with a new constant
K>0
independent of
\varepsilon
. As the next step towards the proof we may estimate, using stationarity as in (
2.25
) and (
2.55
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{\varepsilon}(x,T,B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c})\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=\int_{B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx\leq C_{\mu}\int_{B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c}}\mu(x)\,dx,

(2.61)

and using (
2.60
) it follows

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{\varepsilon}(x,T,B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c})\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq c_{\mu}\int_{B_{r(\varepsilon)}(x_{0})}P_{\varepsilon}(x,T,B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c})\mu(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq c_{\mu}\int_{B_{r(\varepsilon)}(x_{0})}\mu(x)\,dx\left[P_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},T,B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c})-\varepsilon\right].

Rearranging terms yields

P_{\varepsilon}(x_{0},T,B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c})=\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T)\right|>R(\varepsilon)\right)\leq K\varepsilon+\frac{C_{\mu}\int_{B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c}}\mu(x)\,dx}{c_{\mu}\int_{B_{r(\varepsilon)}(x_{0})}\mu(x)\,dx},

(2.62)

for all
T>0
and
x_{0}\in I
. Observe that, by continuity, we have on the one hand

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2r(\varepsilon)}\int_{B_{r(\varepsilon)}(x_{0})}\mu(x)\,dx=\mu(x_{0})>0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.63)

On the other hand, by Assumption
(CLT)
, we have for
x>M

\displaystyle\mu(x)=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left(2\int_{0}^{x}\frac{b(y)}{\sigma}\,dy\right)\leq C(b,\sigma,M)\exp\left(2\int_{M}^{x}\frac{b(y)}{\sigma}\,dy\right)\leq C(b,\sigma,M,\gamma)\exp(-\gamma x),

and, similarly, for
x<-M
. For sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
so that
R(\varepsilon)\geq M
the last estimate implies

\int_{B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c}}\mu(x)\,dx\leq C(b,\sigma,M,\gamma)\exp(-\gamma R(\varepsilon)).

(2.64)

Combining (
2.62
), (
2.63
), and (
2.64
) eventually yields

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T)\right|>R(\varepsilon)\right)\leq K\varepsilon+C(b,\sigma,M,\gamma,\mu)\frac{\exp(-\gamma R(\varepsilon))}{r(\varepsilon)\mu(x_{0})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

(2.65)

when choosing, say,
R(\varepsilon)=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-\eta})
with
\eta>0
. This preceding result also holds when
T=T_{\varepsilon}
, since the appearing estimates did not depend on
T
.

Now consider
x_{0}\in I^{c}
, assume w.l.o.g
x_{0}>y
, and let
\delta>0
. By (
2.57
) we can choose
T_{0}:=T_{0}(\delta,x_{0},y)>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that for all
\varepsilon>0

\mathbb{P}(\tau^{y-1}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}\right)>T_{0})<\delta.

(2.66)

Then we have for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
such that
T_{\varepsilon}\geq T_{0}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T_{\varepsilon})\right|>R(\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T_{\varepsilon})\right|>R(\varepsilon),\tau^{y-1}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}\right)>T_{\varepsilon}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T_{\varepsilon})\right|>R(\varepsilon),\tau^{y-1}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}\right)\leq T_{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\delta+\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T_{\varepsilon})\right|>R(\varepsilon),\tau^{y-1}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}\right)\leq T_{\varepsilon}\right).

The last term can be estimated with the strong Markov property again and (
2.65
) as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T_{\varepsilon})\right|>R(\varepsilon),\tau^{y-1}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}\right)\leq T_{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}P_{\varepsilon}\left(y-1,T_{\varepsilon}-s,B_{R(\varepsilon)}(0)^{c}\right)\,d\mathbb{P}^{\tau^{y-1}\left(X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}\right)}(s)

\displaystyle\leq K\varepsilon+C(b,\sigma,M,\gamma,\mu)\frac{\exp(-\gamma R(\varepsilon))}{r(\varepsilon)\mu(x_{0})}.

Hence, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T_{\varepsilon})\right|>R(\varepsilon)\right)\leq\delta,

which yields the claim for the case
x_{0}\in I^{c}
, as well.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

In the proof of Proposition
2.13
, one can also choose
R(\varepsilon)=-\gamma^{-1}\mathcal{O}(\log(r(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{\eta}))
for
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,
\eta>0
, and get the same convergence result.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let Assumptions
(C*)
,
(MET)
and
(CLT)
hold and let
T_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-\eta})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
with
\eta>0
. Then for all
\delta>0
and any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\left|X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T_{\varepsilon})\right|}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}>\delta\right)=0.

(2.67)

In other words,
X_{\varepsilon}^{x_{0}}(T_{\varepsilon})/\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}
converges in
\mathbb{P}
-probability to zero as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

2.3
An
\varepsilon
-dependent central limit theorem

In this section, we want to extend the results of the preceding sections by outlining and proving an "
\varepsilon
-dependent" CLT under certain additional assumptions, i.e., for a given function
h_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
we want to prove

\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}h_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\overset{\mathcal{D}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^{2}),\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

(2.68)

where
\overset{\mathcal{D}}{\longrightarrow}
denotes weak convergence of measures in
\mathbb{R}
and
\tau^{2}>0
is the asymptotic variance. The idea is not new and has been employed many times before in the literature, cf.
[
8
]
or
[
13
]
, and goes as follows.

Fix a continuous function
h_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{\varepsilon}(x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=0
with
\mu_{\varepsilon}
as in Remark
2.1
. If there exists a solution
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon})\subset C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to the Poisson equation

-\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}\Phi_{\varepsilon}=h_{\varepsilon},

(2.69)

where
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
is the differential operator introduced in (
2.4
), then applying the Itô formula to
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
yields

\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}h_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt=\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-\Phi_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(T_{\varepsilon}))}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\sigma(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))dW(t).

(2.70)

Inspecting equation (
2.70
) we notice that, in order to obtain the CLT (
2.68
), we need to control the first term in such a way that it vanishes
\mathbb{P}
-stochastically as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and use a CLT for the remaining stochastic integral. The latter claim is contained in
[
20
, Exercise (IV.3.33)]
so that we merely state the result in the way that we require.

Proposition 2.16
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
let
\phi_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function such that
\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))^{2}\,dt<\infty
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. If there exists
\tau>0
such that

\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))^{2}\,dt\overset{\mathcal{\mathbb{P}}}{\longrightarrow}\tau^{2},\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

(2.71)

then

\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dW(t)\overset{\mathcal{D}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^{2}),\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.72)

To apply this proposition to (
2.70
) we first provide a lemma concerning the properties of
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
and its derivative. The main ideas for this lemma are borrowed from the proof of Lemma 1.17 in
[
11
]
and are modified to our setting.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let Assumptions
(C*)
,
(MET)
, and
(CLT)
hold. Assume that the functions
h_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
are continuous and satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{\varepsilon}(x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=0,\quad\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}<\infty.

(2.73)

Then the sequence of functions
\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
, where
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
solves (
2.69
), is uniformly bounded.

Proof.

In this particular one-dimensional setting the function
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
is given by

\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)=-\int_{0}^{x}\frac{2}{\sigma(y)^{2}\mu_{\varepsilon}(y)}\int_{-\infty}^{y}h_{\varepsilon}(z)\mu_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(2.74)

Its first derivative is

\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)=-\frac{2}{\sigma(x)^{2}\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)}\int_{-\infty}^{x}h_{\varepsilon}(z)\mu_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.75)

Recall the inequality (
2.55
)

c_{\mu}\mu\leq\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq C_{\mu}\mu\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R},

and observe that by the integral condition in (
2.73
)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)=-\frac{2}{\sigma(x)^{2}\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)}\int_{-\infty}^{x}h_{\varepsilon}(z)\mu_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz=\frac{2}{\sigma(x)^{2}\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)}\int_{x}^{\infty}h_{\varepsilon}(z)\mu_{\varepsilon}(z)dz,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Now, let
M,\gamma
be as in
(CLT)
. By using
(C*)
ii), the inequality in
(CLT)
, and (
2.73
), we obtain for
x>M

\displaystyle|\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{\mu}}{c_{\mu}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{|h_{\varepsilon}(z)|}{\sigma(z)^{2}}\exp\left(\int_{x}^{z}\frac{2b(y)}{\overline{\sigma}(y)^{2}}\,dy\right)dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{\mu}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}}{c_{\mu}m^{2}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\,\exp\left(-2\gamma(z-x)\right)dz=\frac{C_{\mu}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}}{\gamma c_{\mu}m^{2}}.

A similar estimate is also true for
x<-M
. For
x\in[-M,M]
it obviously holds

|\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\frac{\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}}{c_{\mu}m^{2}\mu(x)},

which implies the claim.
∎

For the next result, we also fix a continuous function
h\in L^{1}(\mu)
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(x)\mu(x)\,dx=0
, and consider the function

\Phi(x)=-\int_{0}^{x}\frac{2}{\overline{\sigma}(y)^{2}\mu(y)}\int_{-\infty}^{y}h(z)\mu(z)\,dz\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(2.76)

which solves the Poisson equation
-\mathcal{A}\Phi=h
with the differential operator
\mathcal{A}
given in (
2.4
).

Theorem 2.18
.

Let Assumptions
(C*)
,
(MET)
, and
(CLT)
, and
T_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-\eta})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
with
\eta>0
hold. Assume that the functions
h_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
are continuous and satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{\varepsilon}(x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=0,\quad\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}<\infty.

(2.77)

Additionally, assume that

\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sigma(x)^{2}\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\sigma}(x)^{2}\Phi^{\prime}(x)^{2}\mu(x)\,dx=:\tau^{2},\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.78)

Then

\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}h_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\overset{\mathcal{D}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^{2}),\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0\,.

(2.79)

Proof.

Recall the equation (
2.70
)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}h_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt=\frac{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-\Phi_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(T_{\varepsilon}))}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\sigma(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))dW(t).

By virtue of Corollary
2.15
, the first term on the right-hand side vanishes
\mathbb{P}
-stochastically as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
because
\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded by Lemma
2.17
. For the second term, we first bound

\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\sigma(X_{\varepsilon}(t))^{2}\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))^{2}dt-\tau^{2}\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\sigma(X_{\varepsilon}(t))^{2}\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))^{2}dt-\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right|+|\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}-\tau^{2}|,

and then apply Theorem
2.9
to get

\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\sigma(X_{\varepsilon}(t))^{2}\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))^{2}dt-\tau_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right|\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

By condition (
2.78
) it therefore follows

\mathbb{E}\,\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\sigma(X_{\varepsilon}(t))^{2}\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))^{2}dt-\tau^{2}\right|\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

Hence, the claim is established in view of Proposition
2.16
and Slutzky’s Lemma.
∎

3
Application to parameter estimation

This section is devoted to a simple application of the previous limit theorems to a statistical parameter estimation problem. Consider the following overdamped Langevin SDE in one dimension with a linear and oscillatory term in the drift and constants
\alpha,\sigma>0

dX_{\varepsilon}(t)=-\alpha X_{\varepsilon}(t)-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sin\left(\frac{X_{\varepsilon}(t)}{\varepsilon}\right)dt+\sqrt{2\sigma}dW(t),\quad X_{\varepsilon}(0)=x_{0}.

(3.1)

Using homogenization theory, see
[
2
, Chapter 3]
or
[
17
]
, it can be shown that the law
\mathbb{P}^{X_{\varepsilon}}
converges weakly to
\mathbb{P}^{X}
in
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
for fixed
T>0
, where
X
is the solution of the SDE

dX(t)=-\alpha KX(t)dt+\sqrt{2\sigma K}d\overline{W}(t),\quad X(0)=x_{0}.

(3.2)

Here, the constant factor
K>0
emerges from the cell problem of the homogenization and equals

K:=\frac{1}{Z^{+}Z^{-}},\quad Z^{\pm}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left(\pm\frac{\cos(y)}{\sigma}\right)\,dy.

(3.3)

It is fairly easy to see that all the conditions in
(C*)
,
(MET)
, and
(CLT)
are satisfied.
In particular, the invariant densities
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and
\mu
of
X_{\varepsilon}
and
X
, respectively, exist and are given by

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\cos\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right),\quad\mu(x)=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

where
Z
and
Z_{\varepsilon}
are normalization constants.

We want to estimate the parameter
\vartheta:=\alpha K
appearing in (
3.2
) with observations coming in the form of a single trajectory from (
3.1
). For simplicity, we assume that we know the value
\overline{\sigma}:=\sigma K
, e.g., from a prior estimation procedure. Such diffusion parameter estimation problems with given multiscale data were analyzed, for example, in
[
10
,
12
]
. We propose the following simple minimum distance estimator based on the characteristic function of the invariant density
\mu
at the point
1

\hat{\vartheta}_{T_{\varepsilon}}(X_{\varepsilon}):=\arginf_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\left|\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\exp(iX_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt-\exp\left(-\frac{\overline{\sigma}}{2\vartheta}\right)\right|.

(3.4)

Using differentiation, it is straightforward to obtain an explicit expression for this estimator, namely

\hat{\vartheta}_{T_{\varepsilon}}(X_{\varepsilon})=-\frac{\overline{\sigma}}{2\log\left(\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\exp(iX_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\right)}.

(3.5)

Consider the Poisson equations

-\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}\Phi_{\varepsilon}=h_{\varepsilon},\quad-\mathcal{A}\Phi=h,\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R},

(3.6)

with the differential operators
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{A}
corresponding to (

) and (

), respectively, and with the functions

\displaystyle h_{\varepsilon}(x):=\exp(ix)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(iy)\mu_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy,\quad h(x):=\exp(ix)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(iy)\mu(y)\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha,\sigma,R>0
. Then

\|h_{\varepsilon}-h\|_{\infty}\leq C(\alpha,\sigma)\exp\left(-1/\varepsilon^{2}\right),

(3.7)

and

\sup_{x\in[-R,R]}|\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\phi(x)|\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(3.8)

Proof.

In this proof, the appearing constants
C(\alpha,\sigma)
and
C(\alpha,\sigma,R)
may change from line to line. We will first prove the exponential convergence in (
3.7
). First, notice that we can write for any
z\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle h_{\varepsilon}(z)-h(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(ix)\left[\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)-\mu(x)\right]dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(ix)\left[\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)-\frac{Z^{+}}{Z_{\varepsilon}}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right)\right]dx

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(ix)\left[\frac{Z^{+}}{Z_{\varepsilon}}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right)-\mu(x)\right]dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(ix)\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right)\left[\exp\left(\frac{\cos(x/\varepsilon)}{\sigma}\right)-Z^{+}\right]dx

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{ZZ^{+}-Z_{\varepsilon}}{ZZ_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp(ix)\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right)\,dx.

Observe that

Z_{\varepsilon}-ZZ^{+}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right)\left[\exp\left(\frac{\cos(x/\varepsilon)}{\sigma}\right)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left(\frac{\cos(y)}{\sigma}\right)\,dy\right]dx.

We will prove that

|Z_{\varepsilon}-ZZ^{+}|\leq C(\alpha,\sigma)\exp\left(-1/\varepsilon^{2}\right).

(3.9)

For this, we can use a series expansion and the binomial theorem to calculate the integral

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left(\frac{\cos(y)}{\sigma}\right)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma^{n}n!}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(y)^{n}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\sigma)^{n}n!}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\exp\left(iy\right)+\exp\left(-iy\right)\right)^{n}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{(2\sigma)^{n}\sigma^{n}n!}\binom{n}{k}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left(i(n-2k)y\right)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\frac{1}{(2\sigma)^{2m}}\frac{1}{k!(2m-k)!}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left(2i(m-k)y\right)dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\sigma)^{2m}(m!)^{2}}.

Similarly for
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{\cos(x/\varepsilon)}{\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{(2\sigma)^{n}n!}\binom{n}{k}\exp\left(i(n-2k)x/\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2\sigma)^{2m}(m!)^{2}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2m}\frac{\exp\left(2i(m-k)x/\varepsilon\right)}{(2\sigma)^{2m}k!(2m-k)!}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.95863pt+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2m+1}\frac{\exp\left(i(2(m-k)+1)x/\varepsilon\right)}{(2\sigma)^{2m+1}k!(2m-k+1)!}.

We will estimate the last two terms. For the middle term in the last appearing equation we have with Fubini’s theorem, the fact that
\mathbb{E}\,\exp(itZ)=\exp(-t^{2}/2)
when
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
,
|m-k|\geq 1
. and the binomial theorem again

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2m}\frac{\exp\left(2i(m-k)x/\varepsilon\right)}{(2\sigma)^{2m}k!(2m-k)!}\,dx\right|\\[7.11317pt]
&\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2m}\frac{1}{(2\sigma)^{2m}k!(2m-k)!}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right)\exp\left(2i(m-k)x/\varepsilon\right)\,dx\right|\\[7.11317pt]
&\leq C(\alpha,\sigma)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2m}\frac{1}{(2\sigma)^{2m}k!(2m-k)!}\exp\left(-\frac{2(m-k)^{2}\sigma}{\alpha\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\\[7.11317pt]
&\leq C(\alpha,\sigma)\exp\left(-\frac{\sigma}{\alpha\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2m}(2m)!}.\end{aligned}

(3.10)

An analog estimate holds for the third term, namely

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{2m+1}\frac{\exp\left(i(2(m-k)+1)x/\varepsilon\right)}{(2\sigma)^{2m+1}k!(2m-k+1)!}\,dx\right|\leq C(\alpha,\sigma)\exp\left(-\frac{\sigma}{\alpha\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2m+1}(2m+1)!}.

With these equations and estimates we therefore arrive at (
3.9
). Note also that we can get a similar estimate for the term

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(ix)\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right)\left[\exp\left(\frac{\cos(x/\varepsilon)}{\sigma}\right)-Z^{+}\right]dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(ix)\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}x^{2}\right)\left[\exp\left(\frac{\cos(x/\varepsilon)}{\sigma}\right)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left(\frac{\cos(y)}{\sigma}\right)\,dy\right]dx

This is because in (
3.10
) we merely replace
\exp\left(-\alpha x^{2}/2\sigma\right)
with
\exp(ix)\exp\left(-\alpha x^{2}/2\sigma\right)
so that we actually deal with the characteristic function of an unnormalized Gaussian again. With these arguments, we can therefore conclude the proof of (
3.7
). As a byproduct, we have just proved that
\mu_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly to
\mu
with convergence rates given in terms of their characteristic functions, that is

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(ix)\mu(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(ix)\mu(x)\,dx\right|\leq C(\alpha,\sigma)\exp\left(-\frac{\sigma}{\alpha\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

(3.11)

We move on to claim (
3.8
). For this, define

H_{\varepsilon}(y):=\int_{-\infty}^{y}h_{\varepsilon}(z)\mu_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz,\quad H(y):=\int_{-\infty}^{y}h(z)\mu(z)\,dz,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.12)

Then for
y\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle|H_{\varepsilon}(y)-H(y)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{-\infty}^{y}(h_{\varepsilon}(z)-h(z))\mu_{\varepsilon}(z)\,dz+\int_{-\infty}^{y}h(z)(\mu_{\varepsilon}(z)-\mu(z))\,dz\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert h_{\varepsilon}-h\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left|\int_{-\infty}^{y}h(z)(\mu_{\varepsilon}(z)-\mu(z))\,dz\right|.

The first term vanishes exponentially as we have seen above. The second term vanishes, by weak convergence because the function
h\mathds{1}_{(-\infty,y]}
is
\lambda^{1}
-a.e. continuous and bounded. Thus,
H_{\varepsilon}
converges pointwise to
H
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Now fix
x\in[-R,R]
and consider
x>0
. The case
x<0
works the same. Recall the solution formulas for
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
and
\Phi
in (
2.74
) and (
2.76
). Then it holds

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\Phi(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{x}\frac{H_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\sigma\mu_{\varepsilon}(y)}-\frac{H(y)}{\overline{\sigma}\mu(y)}\,dy\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{x}\frac{H_{\varepsilon}(y)-H(y)}{\sigma\mu_{\varepsilon}(y)}\,dy+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{0}^{x}H(y)\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(y)}-\frac{1}{K\mu(y)}\right)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma c_{\mu}}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{|H_{\varepsilon}(y)-H(y)|}{\mu(y)}\,dy+\left|\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{0}^{x}H(y)\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(y)}-\frac{1}{K\mu(y)}\right)\,dy\right|.

Note that
|H_{\varepsilon}-H|\leq 4
for all
\varepsilon>0
, so that the first term converges by dominated convergence. For the second term, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}H(y)\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(y)}-\frac{1}{K\mu(y)}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle=Z_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{x}H(y)\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}y^{2}\right)\left(\exp(-\cos(y/\varepsilon)/\sigma)-Z^{-}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle\hskip 9.95863pt+\int_{0}^{x}(Z_{\varepsilon}Z^{-}-ZZ^{+}Z^{-})\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}y^{2}\right)\,dy

Very similar arguments that previously achieved (
3.9
) can be used to obtain

\int_{0}^{x}H(y)\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}y^{2}\right)\left(\exp(-\cos(y/\varepsilon)/\sigma)-Z^{-}\right)\,dy\leq C(\alpha,\sigma,R)\varepsilon.

(3.13)

Indeed, all we have to do is to upper bound the following term in (
3.10
)

\left|\int_{0}^{x}H(y)\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma}y^{2}\right)\exp\left(2i(m-k)y/\varepsilon\right)\,dx\right|\leq C(\alpha,\sigma,R)\varepsilon,

which can be accomplished through integration by parts. Hence, by (

(3.14)

The preceding lemma allows us now to prove asymptotic properties of the minimum distance estimator for

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
T_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-\eta})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
for some
\eta>0
. Then, under the true parameter
\vartheta_{0}=\alpha_{0}K
with
\alpha_{0}>0

\displaystyle\hat{\vartheta}_{T_{\varepsilon}}(X_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\overset{\mathcal{\mathbb{P}}}{\longrightarrow}\vartheta_{0},

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}(\hat{\vartheta}_{T_{\varepsilon}}(X_{\varepsilon})-\vartheta_{0})

\displaystyle\overset{\mathcal{D}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{N}\left(0,\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\overline{\sigma}^{2}\vartheta_{0}^{2}}\right),

(3.16)

where

\tau^{2}=2\overline{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\prime}(x)^{2}\mu(x)\,dx.

(3.17)

Proof.

Fix
\vartheta_{0}=\alpha_{0}K
with
\alpha_{0}>0
as the true parameter. First observe that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\exp(iX_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt-\exp\left(-\frac{\overline{\sigma}}{2\vartheta_{0}}\right)=\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}h_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt+h_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-h(x_{0}),

which converges in

to zero as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
by Theorem
2.9
, Remark
2.10

\hat{\vartheta}_{T_{\varepsilon}}(X_{\varepsilon})=-\frac{\overline{\sigma}}{2\log\left(\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\exp(iX_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\right)}\overset{\mathcal{\mathbb{P}}}{\longrightarrow}\vartheta_{0}.

Furthermore, we can write

\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\exp(iX_{\varepsilon}(t))-\exp\left(-\frac{\overline{\sigma}}{2\vartheta_{0}}\right)\,dt=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}h_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt+\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}(h_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-h(x_{0}))

(3.18)

By the exponential convergence of
h_{\varepsilon}
to

and the assumption that

\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle 2\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx\rightarrow 2\overline{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\prime}(x)^{2}\mu(x)\,dx,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

Integration by parts and using the Poisson equations in (

\displaystyle\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)h_{\varepsilon}(x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx,\quad\overline{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(x)^{2}\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)h(x)\mu(x)\,dx.

We choose a sufficiently large

such that the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)h_{\varepsilon}(x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)h(x)\mu(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)(h_{\varepsilon}(x)-h(x))\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 9.95863pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\Phi(x))h(x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 9.95863pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(x)h(x)(\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)-\mu(x))\,dx

and uniform integrability of

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\Phi(x))h(x)\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx\right|=2\left[\sup_{x\in[-R,R]}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\Phi(x)|+\int_{[-R,R]^{c}}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(x)-\Phi(x)|\,\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx\right].

and yields

\displaystyle\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}(\hat{\vartheta}_{T_{\varepsilon}}(X_{\varepsilon})-\vartheta_{0})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{T_{\varepsilon}}\left[g\left(\frac{1}{T_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\exp(iX_{\varepsilon}(t))\,dt\right)-g\left(\exp\left(-\frac{\overline{\sigma}}{2\vartheta_{0}}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\mathcal{D}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{N}\left(0,\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\overline{\sigma}^{2}\vartheta_{0}^{2}}\right),\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.\qed

4
Conclusion

In this work, we presented new limit theorems for one-dimensional diffusion processes
X_{\varepsilon}
that depend on a small scale parameter
\varepsilon
, for which the process
X_{\varepsilon}
converges in a weak sense to a limit diffusion process
X
. Under sufficient local regularity conditions on the coefficients of the respective SDEs, recurrence assumptions, and sufficient explosion rates of the time horizon
T_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\infty
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we were able to prove a mean ergodic theorem and a central limit theorem for time integrals with bounded test functions. We then applied these new theoretical results to a simple statistical parameter estimation problem under model misspecification in a continuous-time setting. The proposed minimum distance estimator, based on the characteristic function of the limit invariant density, turned out to be consistent and asymptotically normal under observations of the process
X_{\varepsilon}
in the simultaneous long-time and small-scale limiting regime, that is
T_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\infty
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

We see two immediate chances for future research. The first one is the extension of the limit theorems to unbounded test functions that are integrable with respect to the limit invariant measure. This is certainly more useful for statistical estimation purposes where moments of processes are an ubiquitous occurrence. We suspect that, in this case, one must shift away the attention from the mean ergodic theorem and pursue an almost sure ergodic theorem. After all, if the ultimate aim is the consistency or asymptotic normality of estimators, then it is irrelevant if one has the mean ergodic or the almost sure ergodic theorem – even a stochastic ergodic theorem would suffice.

The second direction for future research is the generalization of the limit theorems to multidimensional processes, e.g., multiscale fast-slow diffusion systems, cf.
[
2
,
16
,
21
]
. This poses considerable challenges from several view points. For example, the techniques for the proof of the mean ergodic theorem are already not extendable to the multidimensional case due to the local time process, whose effective use is limited to the one-dimensional setting. Another difficulty is the question about the convergence of the asymptotic variances in the central limit theorem, which remains unclear in more general settings. Despite these preliminary problems, the stochastic convergence results in subsection
2.2
would be easier to establish since they heavily rely on interior Schauder estimates, which can be proved analogously in several dimensions. Furthermore, we believe that, by combining certain ideas from this material and the newly established estimates in
[
21
]
, it may be possible to obtain a mean ergodic theorem for bounded test functions.

A novel pathogenic variant in folliculin identified in a Chinese family with BHD syndrome
Authors: Na Xing, Yi-Shuai Li, Wei Wei, Yong Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to investigate FLCN gene variants in Chinese patients presenting with familial spontaneous pneumothorax associated with Birt-Hogg-Dubé syndrome (BHDS).MethodsA Chinese family with a history of familial spontaneous pneumothorax linked to BHD was analyzed. Genetic screening of the folliculin (FLCN) gene was conducted using next-generation sequencing (NGS) technology.ResultsA nonsense variant (c.439 C > T) in the FLCN gene was identified in six individuals within the family. This variant, previously unreported in the Chinese population, results in the substitution of glutamine at the 147th amino acid position in exon 6 with a premature stop codon. The variant is hypothesized to induce nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, potentially leading to the absence of functional protein expression, which may underlie the pathogenesis of pneumothorax in this family.ConclusionsThe findings elucidate the FLCN variant associated with familial spontaneous pneumothorax in BHD, thereby expanding the variantion spectrum of the FLCN gene and providing insights into the molecular mechanisms of this condition.

Background

Birt-Hogg-Dubé syndrome (BHDS, OMIM#135150), alternatively referred to as Hornstein-Knickenberg syndrome (HKS), is a rare autosomal dominant genetic disorder characterized by pulmonary cysts, spontaneous pneumothorax, cutaneous fibrofolliculomas, and a predisposition to renal neoplasms. The syndrome was first described in 1975 by Hornstein and Knickenberg, who identified an autosomal dominant condition marked by perifollicular fibromatosis of the skin, multiple skin tags, and cancer-prone colonic polyps [1]. Subsequently, in 1977, Birt, Hogg, and Dubé reported a familial case exhibiting fibrofolliculomas, leading to the eponym “BHDS” [2]. Contemporary literature often equates fibrofolliculoma with perifollicular fibroma [3], and while BHDS is the more widely recognized term, the contributions of Hornstein and Knickenberg remain historically significant.

Primary spontaneous pneumothorax (PSP) occurs with an incidence of 1 in 10,000 individuals, with approximately 10% of cases demonstrating a familial predisposition, termed familial spontaneous pneumothorax (FSP) [4, 5]. The molecular mechanisms underlying FSP in BHDS remain poorly elucidated. The folliculin (FLCN) gene, located on chromosome 17p11.2 and comprising 14 exons, is the only known causative gene for BHDS [6]. Germline variants, particularly insertions or deletions of cytosine within the polycytosine (C8) tract in exon 11 of FLCN, represent the most frequently observed genetic alterations across diverse populations [7–11].

In this study, we present a large Chinese pedigree spanning three generations, and we identified a FLCN variant through comprehensive genetic analysis.

Patients and methods

Patients

We retrospectively analyzed the clinical information of the Chinese family that presented with FSP at the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University (Shijiazhuang, China) (The period was January 1998 to January 2024). A 45-year-old male proband was diagnosed with PSP at the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University (Shijiazhuang, China). By inquiring about their medical history, a comprehensive family history was obtained from 15 members spanning three generations. Through investigation of family history, it was found that multiple members of his family had experienced pneumothorax, leading us to conduct a study on his pedigree. All participants underwent clinical evaluation by a board-certified dermatologist, followed by chest computed tomography (CT) to assess pulmonary abnormalities and abdominal ultrasonography to rule out renal involvement. Peripheral blood samples were collected from the proband and her family members for subsequent genetic analysis.

DNA extraction

Peripheral blood samples were collected in EDTA-coated anticoagulant tubes and stored at + 4℃. Genomic DNA was extracted within 24 h of collection using the DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen Inc., Valencia, CA, USA). The isolated DNA samples were aliquoted and stored at -20℃ until further processing for polymerase chain reaction (PCR).

Whole-genome sequencing

We apply the target region probe capture technology, coupled with next-generation sequencing (NGS) on the Illumina platform, was employed to analyze the FLCN gene in the family (detailed data available upon request). Identified variants were cross-referenced with large population databases (including gnomAD, ExAC, and 1000 Genomes), and determine whether there is a variation of the FLCN gene in this family. The assessment of the pathogenic risk of genetic variations can be classified according to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) Variant Interpretation Guidelines as follows: (1) Benign (B), (2) Likely benign (LB), (3) Variant of Uncertain Significance (VUS), (4) Likely pathogenic (LP), (5) Pathogenic (P).

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical guidelines and approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University, Shijiazhuang, China. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to their inclusion in the study. The study included minors, had obtain consent from their parents or guardians.

Sequence analysis of the FLCN gene

Sequence analysis of the FLCN gene identified a nonsense variant (NM_144997.7(FLCN):c.439 C > T p.(Gln147Ter)) in exon 6, which results in the substitution of glutamine at codon 147 with a premature stop codon (p.Q147X). This variant is predicted to trigger nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD), potentially leading to the loss of functional protein expression. The observed variant may contribute to the pathogenesis of pulmonary cyst formation. Notably, the same variant was detected in the proband and five additional family members (Fig. 3). This variant has not been previously documented in the literature, and its novelty was confirmed through validation against the Human Gene Variant Database.

Discussion

BHD syndrome is characterized by a spectrum of clinical manifestations, including recurrent pneumothorax, multiple bilateral pulmonary cysts, fibrofolliculomas, and renal cell carcinoma. However, these symptoms often present in isolation and may exhibit atypical features [12]. Notably, over 80% of BHD patients with pneumothorax demonstrate multiple bilateral pulmonary cysts, with 22.5–38% reporting a history of single or recurrent spontaneous pneumothorax, which may serve as the initial clinical presentation [13]. This condition is frequently misdiagnosed as PSP, especially in patients with pneumothorax who only have isolated pulmonary cysts [14].

A comprehensive literature review reveals distinct clinical differences between BHD syndrome-related FSP and PSP. While PSP predominantly affects tall, thin males aged 10–30 years, BHD syndrome-related FSP typically manifests in individuals over 40 years old, with a male-to-female ratio of 1.46:1 [15, 16]. Young BHD patients with pulmonary cysts may remain asymptomatic; however, as they approach middle age, these cysts often rupture, resulting in spontaneous pneumothorax with a high recurrence rate [13, 17, 18]. Liu et al. observed that BHD patients frequently exhibit bilateral, multiple bullae in the lung parenchyma. The deeper location of these bullae may contribute to delayed rupture, potentially explaining the later onset of pneumothorax in FSP patients [19].

The current study, in conjunction with previous literature, identified that a significant proportion of relatives in FSP families harbor bullae without clinical symptoms, necessitating vigilant monitoring. Our investigation revealed six individuals across three generations, with both male and female patients, suggesting a potential genetic predisposition. Previous studies have established a strong association between FSP and FLCN gene variants in BHD syndrome [13, 20]. The FLCN gene product modulates pulmonary surfactant secretion by increasing alveolar epithelial cell permeability and inducing apoptosis, leading to alterations in alveolar surface tension and decreased dynamic compliance. These changes increase susceptibility to mechanical stress, including respiratory movements, resulting in cyst wall expansion and subsequent rupture [21–23]. In BHD patients with pneumothorax, cyst wall rupture leads to cyst formation and secondary pneumothorax, predominantly in areas of maximal lung tension [24–26]. This study identified a nonsense variant in the FLCN gene in the proband and other affected family members. Specifically, we observed a variant at the 147th amino acid position in exon 6, where glutamine was replaced by a stop codon. This premature termination codon may induce nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, resulting in loss of protein expression. This variant has not been documented in major population databases (including gnomAD, ExAC, and 1000 Genomes), suggesting its rarity. Comprehensive genetic screening is warranted to ascertain the presence of FLCN variants among other family members. Based on ACMG criteria, the FLCN, c.439 C > T (p.Arg147*) variant is a nonsense change predicted to cause loss of function in a gene where haploinsufficiency is a known disease mechanism (PVS1). Combined with rarity in population databases (PM2) and a compatible familial phenotype (cosegregation PP1; specific phenotype PP4), this variant should be classified as likely pathogenic or pathogenic. Some studies have also identified similar nonsense variations, further verifying the association between nonsense variations in FLCN and Birt–Hogg–Dubé syndrome and/or FSP. These conclusions are consistent with our research [27–28].While research on FLCN gene variants in FSP has garnered increased attention, the pathogenesis of pulmonary cysts in BHD syndrome remains poorly understood, necessitating further investigation to elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

Studies have shown that the prevalence of skin lesions in patients with BHD syndrome in Asia is relatively low [9, 20, 29], which is similar to our research findings. Given the high prevalence of renal tumors in BHD syndrome patients, annual renal imaging modalities, such as low-dose spiral CT or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) [30, 31], should be implemented in this family for early detection of renal malignancies. Moreover, BHD syndrome is caused by germline variants in the FLCNgene, which result in loss of function of the folliculin protein and may cause cancer predisposition [17, 32, 33]. Consequently, even in patients presenting with ostensibly routine respiratory symptoms, the potential association between recurrent pneumothorax and BHD syndrome must be recognized, prompting comprehensive diagnostic evaluation and early therapeutic intervention.

This study is subject to several limitations. First, genetic testing was confined to pneumothorax-affected family members and does not test relatives who are not affected, which affects the statistical analysis of the incidence of the gene variant. Second, the retrospective design of this study underscores the need for multicenter, large-scale prospective investigations to validate these findings. Finally, due to the limited sample size, no skin and kidney lesions were found in this family. The correlation between mutant genotype and phenotype is our next research focus.

Conclusions

In summary, this study identified a pathogenic FLCN variant associated with pulmonary cysts and pneumothorax, underscoring the importance of FLCN gene screening in patients with these manifestations, even in the absence of cutaneous or renal involvement. The discovery of this variant expands the variation spectrum of the FLCN gene within the Chinese population and highlights the critical role of FLCN genetic testing in pneumothorax patients without concomitant skin or renal lesions.

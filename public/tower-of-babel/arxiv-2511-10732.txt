Residue sums for superconformal indices
Sam van Leuven, Kayleigh Mathieson, Pratik Roy

Abstract
We study superconformal indices of four-dimensional S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) gauge theories with 𝒩 = 1 , 2 , 4 \mathcal{N}=1,2,4 supersymmetry. The usual representation of the index involves a multi–dimensional contour integral over the BPS spectrum of the free gauge theory. To find a closed form expression for the index, it is natural to attempt a residue evaluation. However, the presence of a non–isolated essential singularity inside the contour prevents a straightforward implementation. We show how this difficulty can be resolved by gauge-fixing the residual Weyl symmetry of the integral. This allows us to evaluate the residue sums for superconformal indices of S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) gauge theories in terms of basic and elliptic hypergeometric series. For the 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 Macdonald index, we show how known transformation formulas for basic hypergeometric series can be used to simplify the residue sum. The simplified form manifests the strongly coupled BPS spectrum of the Macdonald sector of the theory, and suggests the absence of “non-graviton” operators in this sector. We also evaluate the residue sums for the Macdonald and full superconformal indices of a general class of S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) gauge theories. In the process, we find various applications to the theory of basic and elliptic hypergeometric integrals, including a convergent residue sum for Spiridonov’s elliptic beta integral. Finally, we discuss the generalization of our method to higher rank gauge groups and evaluate the 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) Macdonald index in closed form.

Introduction

The superconformal index represents one of a few exactly calculable quantities in superconformal field theories (SCFTs).
It was first defined for four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFTs and takes the form of a trace over the spectrum of the SCFT on
S^{3}

Romelsberger:2005eg
;
Kinney:2005ej
.
Similar to the Witten index, this trace is designed in such a way that it receives contributions only from short representations of the superconformal algebra
Dolan:2002zh
.
As a result, the index is protected: it does not change under continuous deformations of the theory, as long as the deformation preserves the supercharge with respect to which the multiplets are short.
This includes exactly marginal deformations, like the gauge coupling of the
\mathcal{N}=4
super–Yang–Mills (SYM) theory.
The superconformal index is also invariant under renormalization group flows in the sense that one can define an index for a non-conformal UV theory, which will equal the superconformal index of the SCFT that emerges as its IR fixed point
Romelsberger:2007ec
;
Dolan:2008qi
;
Festuccia:2011ws
.

These features of the index have led to remarkable checks of and predictions for strong-weak dualities of supersymmetric gauge theories
Romelsberger:2005eg
;
Romelsberger:2007ec
;
Dolan:2008qi
;
Spiridonov:2008zr
;
Spiridonov:2009za
;
Spiridonov:2010hh
;
Spiridonov:2011hf
.
The most famous example is the Seiberg, or IR, duality between an
\mathcal{N}=1

SU(2)
gauge theory with
N_{f}=3
and a theory of free mesons and (dual) quarks
Seiberg:1994pq
.
The agreement between the superconformal indices follows from a highly non-trivial mathematical identity known as Spiridonov’s elliptic beta integral
Spiridonov_2001
.
This identity is given by

\frac{1}{2}\oint\frac{du}{2\pi iu}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{6}\Gamma(y_{i}u^{\pm};p,q)}{\Gamma(u^{\pm 2};p,q)}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 6}\Gamma(y_{i}y_{j};p,q)\,,

(1.1)

where the left hand side is identified with the gauge theory index and the right hand side with the superconformal index of the (free) IR SCFT.
The ingredients of this formula will be reviewed in Section
2
.

The AdS/CFT correspondence provides another example of a strong-weak coupling duality, although in this case one side of the duality is considerably less well understood than the other.
It is in fact in this context that
Kinney:2005ej
originally defined the superconformal index and calculated it for the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(N)
SYM at weak coupling and large
N
.
They showed precise agreement with a supergravity index, by counting the Kaluza-Klein towers of (short) supergravity multiplets on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
.
Apart from demonstrating that the superconformal index indeed captures exact information about the strongly coupled spectrum of the
\mathcal{N}=4
theory, the result also raised a puzzle: on the AdS side, there exist large 1/16 BPS Kerr-Newman black holes with
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
entropy
Gutowski:2004ez
;
Gutowski:2004yv
;
Cvetic:2004ny
;
Chong:2005da
;
Chong:2005hr
;
Kunduri:2006ek
, and one would expect the corresponding CFT states to provide the dominant contribution to the index at large
N
.
So why does it seem that the
\mathcal{N}=4
index does not capture such states?
The authors of
Kinney:2005ej
suggested essentially two possible resolutions.
Firstly, the index only captures differences between bosonic and fermionic degeneracies, so that large cancellations may occur.
And secondly, when viewed as a path integral, the index imposes periodic boundary conditions on fermions along the temporal circle, which is incompatible with a (smooth) Euclidean black hole geometry.
As such, black holes may simply not contribute to an index at all.
Later on, though, building on
Grant:2008sk
, this puzzle was sharpened when attempts were made to construct the actual BPS operators, or rather the “
\mathcal{Q}
-cohomology classes”, corresponding to supersymmetric black holes
Chang:2013fba
.
Indeed, no examples of such classes were found, other than those that could be matched with the supergravity multiplets.

More recently, there has been significant progress in the resolution of this puzzle.
Inspired by observations of
Hosseini:2017mds
, this started with a demonstration of
\mathcal{O}(e^{N^{2}})
growth of the superconformal index, after all, and an exact reproduction of the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of the supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
black holes
Cabo-Bizet:2018ehj
;
Choi:2018hmj
;
Benini:2018ywd
.
The key insight underlying these works is that the index, as a function of the chemical potentials, has branch cuts and should really be thought of as defined on a multi-sheeted cover of the space of chemical potentials.
While the
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
entropy is not captured on one sheet, consistent with the analysis of
Kinney:2005ej
, it is captured on a “second sheet”
Cassani:2021fyv
.

This development motivated a renewed search for the corresponding BPS operators.
By explicitly evaluating the finite
N
index to large orders and comparing with an expansion of the “graviton index”, one can obtain clues on where to look for “non-graviton” operators.
Discrepancies between the full and graviton index were first observed in
Murthy:2020scj
;
Agarwal:2020zwm
.
By now, this has indeed led to the construction of the first examples of (
\mathcal{Q}
-cohomology classes of) non-graviton operators in the
\mathcal{N}=4
theory at finite
N

Chang:2022mjp
;
Choi:2022caq
;
Choi:2023znd
;
Choi:2023vdm
;
Chang:2024zqi
.
As explained in
Chang:2024zqi
, there appears to be a fortuitous nature to non-graviton operators.
That is, in the
SU(N)
theory these operators turn out to be BPS only due to trace relations.
As such, they fail to remain BPS for larger values of
N
.
This is in contrast to ‘‘graviton’’ operators,
1
1

1

Graviton operators at finite
N
refer to operators in the chiral primary multiplets of the
\mathcal{N}=4
theory, see,
e.g.
,
Witten:1998qj
;
Kinney:2005ej
.
which remain BPS for arbitrary
N
and for this reason are referred to as monotonous.

At present, most of the progress in constructing the non-graviton operators is on a case-by-case basis.
Indeed, by looking at the difference between the full and graviton index up to a finite order in the expansion, it is difficult to determine more structural features of the non-graviton spectrum.
Ideally, one would like to write a closed form formula for the difference between the full and graviton index.
As far as we are aware, this has only been achieved for the so-called BMN subsector of the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
theory
Choi:2023znd
;
Choi:2023vdm
;
Gadde:2025yoa
.

With the above motivation in mind, in this paper we focus on developing closed form formulas for the full superconformal index.
We are particularly inspired by Spiridonov’s elliptic beta integral (
1.1
).
This formula allows one to express a highly redundant formula for the index of the UV SQCD description, in a very economical manner, manifesting only its strongly coupled (BPS) spectrum in the IR.
One can ask if similar formulas could exist for the
\mathcal{N}=4
theory.
Even though the coupling of the
\mathcal{N}=4
theory does not flow, one can still tune the Yang-Mills coupling.
In particular, when one tunes the coupling from
g=0
to
g\neq 0
, operators which were BPS saturated in the free theory can develop anomalous dimensions, and as a consequence lift from the BPS spectrum
Kinney:2005ej
;
Grant:2008sk
.

For the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(N)
theory, the analogue of the left hand side of (
1.1
) is the following expression:

\mathcal{I}_{N}=\int d\mu_{SU(N)}\,\text{tr}_{\mathcal{H}^{\text{ext}}_{g=0}}(\cdots)\,,

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{H}^{\text{ext}}_{g=0}
is the extended space of 1/16 BPS states in the free
\mathcal{N}=4
theory, containing all matrix degrees of freedom, and the integral over
SU(N)
, with the invariant Haar measure, projects onto gauge singlets.
We are instead after another, more minimal expression for the index which manifests the BPS spectrum at non-zero (or even strong) coupling.
That is, we are after an expression of the schematic form

\mathcal{I}_{N}=\text{tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{g\neq 0}}(\cdots)\,.

(1.3)

Perhaps the most direct approach to develop such an expression is to convert the integral over
SU(N)
as an integral over eigenvalues of the
SU(N)
matrix, which can be viewed as a (partial) gauge fixing of the
SU(N)
gauge symmetry.
This reduces the integral to
N-1
contour integrals, each contour being a unit circle, with measure the Vandermonde determinant.
One can then in principle evaluate these integrals through residues.
It is of course not guaranteed that the resulting residue sum is immediately of a form which manifests the strongly coupled spectrum. But, as we will demonstrate in this work, it can serve as a useful intermediate step.

However, there turns out to be a key technical difficulty with a naive implementation.
Namely, whenever the integrand in (
1.2
) receives contributions from conformal descendants of bosonic matrices, as will be the case for the full 1/16 BPS index, the integrand has an accumulation point of poles at the origin in the space of complexified
SU(N)
eigenvalues.
2
2

2

The BMN index referred to above does not receive contributions from conformal descendants and its evaluation therefore is not obstructed by this difficulty.

To make matters worse, these integrands typically have an essential singularity at the origin.
Apart from contributions from ordinary poles, one therefore also has to evaluate the contribution of a non-isolated essential singularity.
It turns out that, unless one can argue that this contribution is vanishing, one effectively cannot make sense of the residue sum.
This difficulty was noted before in the context of the Schur index of
\mathcal{N}=2
SCFTs in
Razamat:2012uv
, in the context of
\mathcal{N}=1
gauge theories in
Peelaers:2014ima
and more recently in the mathematical context of elliptic hypergeometric integrals in
spiri2024
.

Our approach

In this work, we show how this technical difficulty can be resolved.
Our method applies to the superconformal indices of general
\mathcal{N}=1
gauge theories, although our main focus is in the context of the
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM and
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal SQCD.
The advantage of considering theories with at least
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry is that one can define simpler specializations of the index, known as the Schur and Macdonald index
Gadde:2011uv
, which share the same issues with the full index, but are technically easier to handle.
Our method allows us to evaluate closed form expressions for superconformal indices.

Let us now briefly describe the basic idea on which the method relies.
As mentioned above, the integral over
SU(N)
can be converted into an integral over eigenvalues.
In particular, the expression (
1.2
) becomes

\mathcal{I}=\frac{1}{N!}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint_{|u_{i}|=1}\frac{du_{i}}{2\pi iu_{i}}\left|\Delta(u)\right|^{2}\,\text{tr}_{\mathcal{H}^{\text{ext}}_{g=0}}(\cdots)\,,

(1.4)

with
u_{i}
the
SU(N)
eigenvalues and
\Delta(u)=\prod_{i<j}(u_{j}-u_{i})
the
SU(N)
Vandermonde determinant.
We will show that, with this measure, one generally
cannot
argue that the contribution of the non-isolated essential singularity is vanishing.
3
3

3

An exception to this turns out to be the Macdonald index of
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal SQCD when a specific condition on the fugacities
(q,t)
is satisfied, as we explain at the end of Section
3.2
.

A closely related issue, which we will demonstrate, is that the residue sum associated with the poles of the integrand excluding the origin diverges.

It is perhaps less well-known that the integral over the eigenvalues can be equivalently expressed as

\mathcal{I}=\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint_{|u_{i}|=1}\frac{du_{i}}{2\pi iu_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(1-u_{ij})\,\text{tr}_{\mathcal{H}^{\text{ext}}_{g=0}}(\cdots)\,,

(1.5)

with
u_{ij}=u_{i}u_{j}^{-1}
.
This version of the integral can be viewed as resulting from a further gauge fixing of the residual
S_{N}
Weyl symmetry.
We show that when evaluating the contour integrals with this “reduced measure”, the residue sum for the poles excluding the origin converges and that the contribution from the origin can be argued to vanish.

This simple method allows us to write down residue sums for general superconformal indices of
\mathcal{N}=1,2,4
supersymmetric gauge theories.
We will show that, in the context of the Macdonald index of the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
theory, we can use mathematical identities for basic hypergeometric series to simplify the residue sum and find an expression which manifests the strongly coupled spectrum, as suggested in (
1.3
).
We discuss various implications of this result.
Firstly, we will argue that it provides strong evidence that there are no non-graviton operators in the Macdonald sector of the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
theory, consistent with previous observations in the literature.
Secondly, our expression manifests the analytic dependence on the two superconformal fugacities
(q,t)
, and also the flavor fugacity
v
.
This allows us to analytically continue the expression beyond the original domain of convergence of the index and deduce new specializations for which the index simplifies.
For example, we find that the Macdonald index turns into a product formula for
t=q^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Thirdly, our expression relates to another known closed form expression for the Macdonald index, the so-called TQFT expression of
Gadde:2011uv
, in a non-trivial and interesting manner.
In particular, the TQFT expression takes the form of a (Laurent) series in the flavor fugacity
v
.
Our expression can be viewed as a resummation of this series, manifesting the analytic dependence on
v
.
This generalizes similar observations for the Schur index
Pan:2021mrw
to the Macdonald index.

Motivated by this, we evaluate the residue sums for both the Macdonald and full superconformal indices of supersymmetric
SU(2)
gauge theories which either are or flow to an SCFT.
The closed form formulas we obtain have various interesting properties, including the aforementioned analytic dependence on the fugacities.
We also find various interesting connections with the mathematics of basic and elliptic hypergeometric integrals and series.
For example, the residue evaluations of the Macdonald index of
\mathcal{N}=2

SU(2)
superconformal SQCD leads to new transformation formulas for basic hypergeometric series.

For the full superconformal index, the reduced measure allows us, for the first time, to evaluate elliptic hypergeometric integrals in terms of residue sums.
The result takes the form of a novel, convergent double infinite sum over a bilinear combination of two elliptic hypergeometric
summands
with a non-factorizing prefactor.
The expression cannot be directly expressed in terms of a product of two full elliptic hypergeometric series, as was suggested in
Peelaers:2014ima
;
spiri2024
.
Our formulas, combined with Seiberg duality (in the
\mathcal{N}=1
case) and generalized
S
-duality (in the
\mathcal{N}=2
case), lead us to non-terminating summation and transformation formulas involving the bilinear combinations of elliptic hypergeometric summands mentioned above.

Given the relation between our formula for the Macdonald index of the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
theory and the TQFT formula, we speculate that a simplification of the residue sum for the full index may shed light on its TQFT formulation, which remains an open problem
Gadde:2011uv
;
Razamat:2013qfa
.
Finally, we demonstrate that our method extends to higher rank gauge theories by evaluating the Macdonald index of
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(3)
theory.

Previous approaches

Let us briefly mention some of the previous approaches to evaluate the superconformal index as a residue sum.
As explained above, if one wants to make sense of the residue sum for a gauge theory index, one needs to argue that the contribution at the origin vanishes.
It turns out that this
can
be done for a specific class of 4d
\mathcal{N}=1
gauge theories whose gauge group can be fully Higgsed.
In this class of theories, the residue evaluation of the index has been related to a localization scheme known as Higgs branch localization, in which the path integral can be localized to a discrete set of Higgs branch vacua
Peelaers:2014ima
(see also
Yoshida:2014qwa
;
Nieri:2015yia
).
Such theories require sufficient fundamental matter and a gauge group with a
U(1)
factor for which a non-zero Fayet-Iliopoulos (FI) parameter is turned on.
It is somewhat implicit in the above references, but it turns out that the non-zero FI parameter is crucial to remove a potential contribution from the origin and, relatedly, to ensure convergence of the residue sum.
4
4

4

A similar issue arise for the 3d index and was addressed explicitly in
Hwang:2012jh
;
Benini:2013yva

Another approach, which is not limited to the above class of theories, is known as the Bethe Ansatz approach
Closset:2017bse
;
Benini:2018mlo

In this approach, one avoids the non-isolated singularity at the origin altogether through a careful contour deformation, which turns the original product of unit circles into contours that bound annuli just outside the unit circles.
A particularly interesting feature of the method is that a specific residue dominates the index at large
N
and can be interpreted holographically in terms of the exact on-shell action of the supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
black hole
Benini:2018ywd

Further progress in this direction, specifically in interpreting other parts of the expression holographically, can be found in
Benini:2020gjh

A known difficulty with the Bethe Ansatz (BA) formula is the classification of poles, which requires solving a set of transcendental equations, and, for

We will find that our formulas for the full superconformal index are in a precise sense complementary to the BA formulas.
Whereas the BA formula requires a specific relation between the superconformal fugacities

Finally, let us also contrast our formulas with the so-called giant graviton expansion of the superconformal index.
These formulas, in a sense, are in the spirit of (

Indeed, they attempt to make manifest the gravitational interpretation of the index, see,

2.1
Definition of the superconformal index

The
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal algebra contains eight (complex Weyl) supercharges
Q_{I\alpha}
and
\widetilde{Q}^{I}_{\dot{\alpha}}
, with
\alpha=\pm
,
\dot{\alpha}=\dot{\pm}
and
I=1,2
.
It also contains eight superconformal charges, which in radial quantization are related to the supercharges through hermitian conjugation:
S^{I\alpha}=\left(Q_{I\alpha}\right)^{\dagger}
and
\widetilde{S}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{I}=(\widetilde{Q}_{I\dot{\alpha}})^{\dagger}
.
The anti-commutators between the super- and superconformal charges, which allow one to construct the index, are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{Q_{I\alpha},S^{J\beta}\}&=\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{J}_{I}\delta^{\beta}_{\alpha}D+\delta^{J}_{I}M_{\alpha}^{\beta}-\delta^{\beta}_{\alpha}R_{I}^{J}+\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{J}_{I}\delta^{\beta}_{\alpha}r\\
\{\widetilde{Q}^{I}_{\dot{\alpha}},\widetilde{S}^{\dot{\beta}}_{J}\}&=\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{I}_{J}\delta^{\dot{\beta}}_{\dot{\alpha}}D+\delta^{J}_{I}\widetilde{M}^{\dot{\beta}}_{\dot{\alpha}}-\delta^{\dot{\beta}}_{\dot{\alpha}}R_{I}^{J}-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{J}_{I}\delta^{\beta}_{\alpha}r\end{split}

(2.1)

Here,
D
is the generator of dilatations on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
M_{\alpha}^{\beta}
and
\widetilde{M}^{\dot{\beta}}_{\dot{\alpha}}
are the generators of the rotation group
Spin(4)\cong SU(2)_{1}\times SU(2)_{2}
.
R_{I}^{J}
and
r
are generators for the R-symmetry group
SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{r}
.
The supercharges transform as doublets under
SU(2)_{R}
. We take
Q_{I\alpha}
to have charge
-\frac{1}{2}
and
\widetilde{Q}^{I}_{\dot{\alpha}}
to have charge
+\frac{1}{2}
under
U(1)_{r}
.
Finally,
Q_{I\alpha}
and
\widetilde{Q}^{I}_{\dot{\alpha}}
have scaling dimensions
\frac{1}{2}
, while
S^{I\alpha}
and
\widetilde{S}^{\dot{\alpha}}_{I}
have scaling dimensions
-\frac{1}{2}
.
7
7

7

Note that hermitian conjugation changes the sign of all charges of
Q_{I\alpha}
and
\widetilde{Q}^{I}_{\dot{\alpha}}
.

We now consider the following positive semi-definite anti-commutators

\delta_{I\pm}\equiv 2\{Q_{I\pm},S^{I\pm}\}\,,\qquad\tilde{\delta}_{I\dot{\pm}}\equiv 2\{\widetilde{Q}^{I}_{\dot{\pm}},\widetilde{S}^{\dot{\pm}}_{I}\}\,,

(2.2)

which can be thought of as a set of “supersymmetric Hamiltonians” for an
\mathcal{N}=2
SCFT.
If we denote the Cartan generators by
j_{1,2}
for
SU(2)_{1}\times SU(2)_{2}
, and
R
for
SU(2)_{R}
, the anti-commutators can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta_{I\pm}=D\pm 2j_{1}+(-1)^{I}\,2R+r\,,\qquad\tilde{\delta}_{I\dot{\pm}}=D\pm 2j_{2}+(-1)^{I}2R-r\,,\end{split}

(2.3)

The generators
j_{1,2}
can be expressed in terms of the generators of
SO(2)^{2}\subset SO(4)
as

j_{1}=\tfrac{1}{2}(J_{12}+J_{34})\,,\quad j_{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}(J_{12}-J_{34})\,,

(2.4)

where
J_{12}
and
J_{34}
rotate the two independent planes in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

To define the index, we first choose an
\mathcal{N}=1
subalgebra with supercharges
Q_{1\alpha}
and
\widetilde{Q}^{1}_{\dot{\alpha}}
(and their hermitian conjugates).
The superconformal R-symmetry of this subalgebra can be read off from the relevant anti-commutator in (
2.1
):

r_{1}=\tfrac{2}{3}(-2R+r)\,.

(2.5)

The general
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal index is now defined similarly to the
\mathcal{N}=1
superconformal index of
Romelsberger:2005eg
;
Kinney:2005ej
.
In particular, we choose to define it with respect to the supercharge
\mathcal{Q}\equiv Q_{1-}
.
One can easily check from the above definitions that the following charges form a maximally commuting subset of the commutant of
\mathcal{Q}

\delta_{1-}\,,\,\delta_{2+}\,,\,\tilde{\delta}_{1\dot{\pm}}\,.

(2.6)

These charges are then used to define the index as the graded trace

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(p,q,t)&=\mathrm{tr}\,(-1)^{F}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\delta}_{1\dot{+}}}\,\rho^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\delta}_{1\dot{-}}}\,\tau^{\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2+}}\,e^{-\beta\delta_{1-}}\\
&=\mathrm{tr}\,(-1)^{F}p^{j_{1}+j_{2}-r}\,q^{j_{1}-j_{2}-r}t^{R+r}\,e^{-\beta\delta_{1-}}\,,\end{split}

(2.7)

which runs over the states of the theory on
S^{3}
and the relation between the two sets of fugacities is

p=\sigma\tau,\;\;q=\rho\tau,\;\;t=\tau^{2}\,,

(2.8)

with absolute values constrained as

|p|<1\,,\quad|q|<1\,,\quad|pq|<|t|<1\,.

(2.9)

By construction, the index only receives contributions from short multiplets comprised of states annihilated by both
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\dagger}
.
It follows that the index is independent of
\beta
, and any other continuous deformations of the theory that preserve
\mathcal{Q}
.
The charges of these states obey the shortening condition

\delta_{1-}=D-2j_{1}-2R+r=0\,.

(2.10)

There are various types of short multiplets satisfying this condition, see for example
Dolan:2002zh
;
Kinney:2005ej
for a classification.

8

And Appendix B of
Gadde:2011uv
for a brief overview.

Finally, we can reparametrize the

Kinney:2005ej

\mathcal{I}(p,q,s)=\mathrm{tr}\,(-1)^{F}p^{j_{1}+j_{2}-\frac{r_{1}}{2}}\,q^{j_{1}-j_{2}-\frac{r_{1}}{2}}s^{R+r}\,e^{-\beta\delta_{1-}}\,,

Macdonald index

Of particular interest in this work will be the so-called Macdonald (limit of the) index.
It is defined by taking
\sigma\to 0
, while keeping
\rho
and
\tau
fixed (
p\to 0
, with
q
and
t
fixed).
From the expression (
2.7
), we see that only states with both
\delta_{1-}=\tilde{\delta}_{1\dot{+}}=0
will contribute to this index,
i.e.
, states that are annihilated by two supercharges
Q_{1-}
and
\widetilde{Q}_{1\dot{+}}
, and their hermitian conjugates.
We write the corresponding index as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(q,t)\equiv\mathcal{I}(0,q,t)&=\mathrm{tr}\,(-1)^{F}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\delta}_{1\dot{-}}}\,\tau^{\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2+}}\,e^{-\beta\delta_{1-}}=\mathrm{tr}\,(-1)^{F}q^{j_{1}-j_{2}-r}t^{R+r}\,e^{-\beta\delta_{1-}}\,.\end{split}

(2.12)

The states contributing to this index obey two charge constraints, which can be written as

D=j_{1}-j_{2}+2R\,,\quad j_{1}+j_{2}=r

(2.13)

Only a subset of the short multiplets satisfies these conditions, see,
e.g.
,
Gadde:2011uv
.

Schur index

A further specialization is known as the Schur index, and can be obtained by taking
t=q
(either in the full or Macdonald index).
We will write this index as

\mathcal{I}(q)\equiv\mathcal{I}(p,q,q)=\mathcal{I}(q,q)=\mathrm{tr}\,(-1)^{F}q^{D-R}\,e^{-\beta\delta_{1-}}\,.

(2.14)

The states contributing to this index correspond to the same as those that contribute to the Macdonald index.

Hall-Littlewood index

Finally, the simplest index can be obtained from the Macdonald index, by also setting
\rho\to 0
, keeping
\tau
fixed (
q\to 0
,
t
fixed), and is known as the Hall-Littlewood index.
In this case only states with
\delta_{1-}=\tilde{\delta}_{1\dot{\pm}}=0
contribute.
The charges of these states are constrained as

D=2R+r\,,\quad j_{1}=r\,,\quad j_{2}=0\,.

(2.15)

There are only two types of short multiplets satisfying these conditions
Gadde:2011uv

The corresponding index is expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(t)\equiv\mathcal{I}(0,0,t)&=\mathrm{tr}\,(-1)^{F}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2+}}\,e^{-\beta\delta_{1-}}=\mathrm{tr}\,(-1)^{F}t^{R+r}\,e^{-\beta\delta_{1-}}\,.\end{split}

(2.16)

2.2
Superconformal gauge theory indices

In this section, we briefly review the construction of
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal gauge theories and the expression for their superconformal index.
We refer the reader to
Tachikawa:2013kta
for a more complete exposition.

There are two multiplets of
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry: the vector- and hypermultiplet.
The vector multiplet can be thought of as the combination of a
\mathcal{N}=1
vector multiplet and chiral multiplet.
As such it is comprised of a complex scalar, two complex Weyl fermions, and the gauge field strength, which we will denote collectively by
(\phi,\bar{\phi},\lambda_{i\alpha},\bar{\lambda}_{i\dot{\alpha}},F_{\alpha\beta},\bar{F}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})
, with
i
being a fundamental
SU(2)_{R}
index.
Note that the fermions transforms as a doublet under
SU(2)_{R}
, and all fields transform in the adjoint representation of the gauge group.
The hypermultiplet consists of two
\mathcal{N}=1
chiral multiplets transforming in conjugate representations of the gauge group.
We denote these by
(q_{a},\bar{q}_{a},\psi_{a\alpha},\bar{\psi}_{a\dot{\alpha}})
, for
a=1,2
.
The
SU(2)_{R}
doublets consist of
(q_{1},\bar{q}_{2})
and
(q_{2},\bar{q}_{1})
.
If the hypermultiplet transforms in a pseudo-real representation of the gauge group, the individual chiral multiplets are sometimes referred to as half-hypermultiplets.

A choice of (semi-simple) gauge group
G
, and matter representations
R
, then fully specifies the Lagrangian of a general
\mathcal{N}=2
gauge theory.
The gauge theory will enjoy
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal invariance if the one-loop beta function for each factor of the gauge group vanishes.
For example, the beta function for an
SU(N)
gauge coupling, with
M
fundamental hypermultiplets will vanish when
M=2N
.
This theory is known as
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal QCD.
In addition, a vector multiplet coupled to an adjoint hypermultiplet will have vanishing beta function, as this corresponds to the
\mathcal{N}=4
field content.
If the gauge group consists of a product of (special) unitary groups, the quiver representation of the superconformal theory admits an
ADE
classification
Gaiotto:2008ak
;
Gaiotto:2009we
.
These theories are part of the more general class
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs
Gaiotto:2009we
.
In this work, we consider the superconformal indices of a few basic examples of such theories.

The index of the superconformal gauge theory is easily computed in the free limit

\displaystyle\begin{split}i_{\text{hm}}(U,V;p,q,t)&=\frac{t^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{\bar{R}}(U,V)-pq/t^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{R}(U,V)}{(1-p)(1-q)}+\frac{t^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{R}(U,V)-pq/t^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi_{\bar{R}}(U,V)}{(1-p)(1-q)}\\
&=\frac{t^{\frac{1}{2}}-pq/t^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(1-p)(1-q)}(\chi_{R}(U,V)+\chi_{\bar{R}}(U,V))\,,\end{split}

with

\chi_{R}(U,V)=\sum_{\rho\in R_{G}}u^{\rho}\sum_{\rho^{\prime}\in R_{F}}v^{\rho^{\prime}}\,,\quad u^{\rho}\equiv u_{1}^{\rho_{1}}\cdots u_{N}^{\rho_{N}}\,,\quad v^{\rho^{\prime}}\equiv v_{1}^{\rho^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots v_{M}^{\rho^{\prime}_{M}}\,.

(2.20)

Here,

and
v_{j}
are the eigenvalues of

and

and

where

is the

\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})

with the

\Delta(\mathbf{u})\equiv\prod_{i<j}(u_{j}-u_{i})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\varepsilon(\sigma)u^{0}_{\sigma(1)}u^{1}_{\sigma(2)}\cdots u^{N-1}_{\sigma(N)}\,,

(2.23)

with

The full index of the hypermultiplet is given by

\mathcal{I}_{\text{hm}}(U,V;p,q,t)=\prod_{\rho\in R_{G},\,\rho^{\prime}\in R_{F}}\Gamma\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{\bar{\rho}}v^{\bar{\rho}^{\prime}};p,q\right)\Gamma\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{\rho}v^{\rho^{\prime}};p,q\right)

(2.24)

where
\bar{\rho}
and
\bar{\rho}^{\prime}
are the weights of the conjugate representation
\bar{R}
.

The superconformal index of the gauge theory can now be obtained by combining the above ingredients for the specific gauge group and matter representations, and projecting onto gauge singlets.
The singlet projection is achieved by integrating over the gauge group with the invariant Haar measure
Aharony:2003sx
.
Since the integrand is expressed in terms of
SU(N)
characters, the integral can be reduced to an integral over eigenvalues

\int_{SU(N)}d\mu_{SU(N)}(U)f(U)=\frac{1}{N!}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint_{|u_{i}|=1}\frac{du_{i}}{2\pi iu_{i}}\Delta(\mathbf{u})\Delta(\mathbf{u}^{-1})f(\mathbf{u})\,,

(2.25)

where the measure is written in terms of the Vandermonde determinant and
f(\mathbf{u})
captures the appropriate multi-letter indices listed above.
The integral over the eigenvalues can be viewed as a (multi-dimensional) contour integral in the space of complexified eigenvalues, with the contour being a product of unit circles.

An important observation for us is that the measure of the eigenvalue integral, due to the invariance of both measure and
f(\mathbf{u})
under permutations of
u_{i}
, can be replaced by
10
10

10

For
SU(2)
matrix integrals, this is an elementary fact and was noted for example in
Dolan:2007rq
;
Dolan:2008qi
. The general case was used in

adjoint SQCD Hilbert series and, more recently, in the context of the superconformal index of the
\mathcal{N}=4
theory in
Agarwal:2020zwm
(see also
Choi:2021rxi
;
Choi:2023znd
;

deMelloKoch:2025ngs

\Delta(\mathbf{u})\Delta(\mathbf{u}^{-1})\to N!\prod_{i<j}(1-u_{ij})\quad\text{or}\quad\Delta(\mathbf{u})\Delta(\mathbf{u}^{-1})\to N!\prod_{i<j}(1-u_{ij}^{-1})

(2.26)

It follows that we can consider an alternative singlet projection, which takes the form

\int_{SU(N)}d\mu_{SU(N)}(U)f(U)=\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint_{|u_{i}|=1}\frac{du_{i}}{2\pi iu_{i}}\prod_{i<j}(1-u_{ij})f(\mathbf{u})\,,

(2.27)

In the rest of this paper, we will refer to the singlet projection in (
2.25

) as projecting with the reduced measure.
Similar to how the full measure can be thought of as a gauge fixing of the matrix
U
to a diagonal form, we can think of the reduced measure as reflecting a further gauge fixing of the residual
S_{N}
Weyl symmetry.

When we consider the index of the
\mathcal{N}=4
theory, it will turn out to convenient to consider a reparametrization of the

deMelloKoch:2025ngs

u_{i}=s_{i}\cdots s_{N}\,.

(2.28)

A useful feature of this reparametrization is that
s_{N}
is not present in any of the

.
In a purely adjoint theory, like the
\mathcal{N}=4
theory, it follows that the integrand only depends on the unconstrained variables
s_{1,\ldots,N-1}
.
In terms of these variables, the adjoint character is written as

\chi_{\text{adj}}(U)=N-1+\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N-1}(s_{i,j}+s^{-1}_{i,j})\,,

(2.29)

where we define

s_{i,j}=s_{i}\cdots s_{j}\,.

(2.30)

In particular, it follows that

\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(1-u_{ij})=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N-1}(1-s_{i,j})\,,

(2.31)

which enables us to write

\det\left[\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial s_{j}}\right]=\prod^{N-1}_{k=1}\frac{u_{k}}{s_{k}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\frac{du_{i}}{u_{i}}=\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\frac{ds_{i}}{s_{i}}\,.

(2.32)

(2.33)

\Gamma(x)\equiv\Gamma(x;p,q)

\Gamma(x^{\pm})=\Gamma(x)\Gamma(x^{-1})

(2.34)

Note that, in the first line, the full measure of the singlet projection is contained in the denominator.
In the second line, we used the first reduction in (

Using the fact that (see Appendix

we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(v;q,t)&=\frac{(q,t;q)^{N-1}_{\infty}}{N!(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm};q)_{\infty}^{N-1}}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint\frac{ds_{i}}{2\pi is_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N-1}\frac{(s_{i,j}^{\pm},ts_{i,j}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm}s_{i,j}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}\,,\\
&=\frac{(q,t;q)^{N-1}_{\infty}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm};q)_{\infty}^{N-1}}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint\frac{ds_{i}}{2\pi is_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N-1}\frac{(s_{i,j},qs_{i,j}^{-1},ts_{i,j}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm}s_{i,j}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}\,,\end{split}

(2.36)

where we denote

and

For the second line, we note that the reduced measure effectively replaces
(s_{i,j}^{-1};q)_{\infty}\to(qs_{i,j}^{-1};q)_{\infty}

3
Convergent residue sums

As reviewed in the previous section, the superconformal indices of gauge theories can be expressed as multiple contour integrals in the space of complexified gauge fugacities, with the contour being a product of unit circles.
The integrands, consisting of elliptic Gamma functions (for the full index) or infinite
q
-Pochhammer symbols (for the Macdonald index), are meromorphic functions of the gauge fugacities.
It is thus natural to attempt an evaluation of the index as a residue sum.
Using the infinite product formulas for the functions (see Appendix
A
), it is straightforward to list all the poles of either integrand, inside or outside the unit circles.
These poles come in infinite towers and accumulate at the origin or infinity, respectively.

However, as already explained in the introduction, there are obstructions to a naive evaluation.
In particular, we will demonstrate that if one defines an
SU(N)
gauge theory index with the
full
measure

\mathcal{I}=\frac{1}{N!}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint_{|u_{i}|=1}\frac{du_{i}}{2\pi iu_{i}}\Delta(\mathbf{u})\Delta(\mathbf{u}^{-1})f(\mathbf{u})\,,

(3.1)

the residue sum which excludes the contribution from the origin
u_{i}=0
does not converge and, relatedly, one will not be able to argue that there is a vanishing contribution from the origin, where the integrand has a non-isolated essential singularity.
In effect, one cannot make sense of the residue sum.
On the other hand, if we evaluate the gauge integral using the
reduced
measure

\mathcal{I}=\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint_{|u_{i}|=1}\frac{du_{i}}{2\pi iu_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(1-u_{ij})f(\mathbf{u})\,,

(3.2)

we will show that the residue sum converges and the contribution from the origin vanishes.
13
13

13

More precisely, there are two choices of reduced measure in (
2.26
).
Using the above yields a convergent residue sum when closing the contour inside the unit circles, with vanishing residue at the origin.
Using the other will yield a convergent residue sum when closing the contour outside the unit circles, with vanishing residue at infinity.

This gives rise to a closed form formula for the superconformal index.
As we will see in due course, it complements various other closed form formulas for the index.

3.1
Elliptic integrals

The key features described above can already be demonstrated in the context of the Schur index (defined in Section

Since this case is technically the simplest, it will serve as a useful warm-up for the Macdonald and full superconformal index.

We can write the Schur index of a general

For now, we take

A key property of the Schur index of an

In terms of the gauge fugacities

When evaluating the contour integrals,

14

Secondly, the sum of residues in a given annulus vanishes for a meromorphic, elliptic function.
Finally, ellipticity implies that poles accumulate at the origin

These three points suggest that, in terms of a residue evaluation, the entire Schur index is encoded by a contribution from the origin. But, due to the nature of the singularity there, it is not clear how to evaluate it.
To explicate these three points, let us work for concreteness with the index of the
\mathcal{N}=4

k
, to

\text{Res}_{s=s^{(k)}_{\pm}}\left[\frac{1}{s}\frac{\theta_{q}(s)\theta_{q}(s^{-1})}{\theta_{q}(q^{\frac{1}{2}}vs)\theta_{q}(q^{\frac{1}{2}}vs^{-1})}\right]=\pm q^{-\frac{1}{2}}v\frac{\theta_{q}(q^{\frac{1}{2}}v)^{2}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}\theta_{q}(v^{2})}\,.

(3.18)

where we made use of properties of
\theta_{q}(x)
collected in Appendix
A.1
.
Including the non-elliptic factor

\frac{1}{1-s^{-1}}=\frac{-s}{1-s}\,,

(3.19)

we find a simple expression for the Schur index

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(v;q)=v\frac{\theta_{q}(q^{\frac{1}{2}}v)}{\theta_{q}(v^{2})}\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\left(\frac{v^{-1}q^{k}}{(1-v^{-1}q^{k+\frac{1}{2}})}-\frac{vq^{k}}{(1-vq^{k+\frac{1}{2}})}\right)\end{split}

(3.20)

The
k^{\text{th}}
residue is suppressed by a factor of order
\mathcal{O}(|q^{(N-1)k}|)
for
N=2
, as consistent with our general arguments.
This also implies that a contribution to residue sum from the origin
s=0
is vanishing.

One may recognize that our result matches the expression obtained in
Pan:2021mrw
, using the Weierstrass
\zeta
–function.
In particular, the twisted Eisenstein series in terms of which
Pan:2021mrw
write their result originates in our calculation from the non-elliptic part of the integrand associated with the reduced measure.
An advantage of our prescription is that it readily generalizes to the Macdonald and full superconformal index, as we will demonstrate in the subsequent sections.

3.2
Basic hypergeometric integrals

In this section, we extend the arguments of the previous section to the Macdonald index.
Here too, we primarily work in the context of the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(N)
SYM theory.
But, at the end of the section we look at
\mathcal{N}=2

SU(N)
superconformal QCD as well.
Together, these are the two fundamental examples of Lagrangian class
\mathcal{S}
theories
Gaiotto:2009we
.

As in the case of the Schur index, we first show that the index defined with the full measure gives rise to a divergent residue sum, preventing an argument for the vanishing of the contribution from the origin.
Unlike in the case of the Schur index, the integrand of the Macdonald index is not an elliptic function, and therefore the details of the argument change, but only slightly so.
In particular, we will relate each consecutive integral to basic hypergeometric integrals, as defined in Appendix
B
, and study the convergence properties of the residue sums using standard results reviewed in the same appendix.

As in the previous section, we write the Macdonald index of the
\mathcal{N}=4
theory in terms of the
s_{i}
variables.
The expression with the full measure is given in the first line of (
2.36
), which we repeat here for convenience

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{N}(v;q,t)&=\frac{(q,t;q)^{N-1}_{\infty}}{N!(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm};q)_{\infty}^{N-1}}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint\frac{ds_{i}}{2\pi is_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N-1}\frac{(s_{i,j}^{\pm},ts_{i,j}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm}s_{i,j}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}\,,\end{split}

(3.21)

To understand the convergence properties of the residue sum, and potential contributions from the origin, we start with the
s_{N-1}
integration, keeping the other
|s_{i}|=1
.
We separate out the
s_{N-1}
dependence and, writing the integrand in (
3.21
) as
I_{N}(s_{i})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{N}&(v;q,t)=\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint\frac{ds_{i}}{2\pi is_{i}}I_{N}(s_{i})\\
&=\prod^{N-2}_{i=1}\oint\frac{ds_{i}}{2\pi is_{i}}I_{N-1}(s_{i})\frac{(q,t;q)_{\infty}}{N(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}\oint\frac{ds_{N-1}}{2\pi is_{N-1}}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\frac{(s_{i,N-1}^{\pm},ts_{i,N-1}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm}s_{i,N-1}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}\,,\end{split}

(3.22)

Recalling that
s_{i,j}=s_{i}\cdots s_{j}
, it is easy to see that the
s_{N-1}
integral is of the form of a basic hypergeometric integral (
B.1
).
As in the case of the Schur index, the integrand has infinite towers of poles inside (and outside) the
s_{N-1}
unit circle, which accumulate at the origin.
As reviewed in Appendix
B
, the convergence of the residue sum and, relatedly, the vanishing of a contribution from the origin depend on the parameters in the integrand multiplying
s^{-1}_{N-1}
.
In particular, the
k^{\text{th}}
residue in a tower of poles accumulating at the origin will be suppressed by
z^{k}
, with the suppression factor
z
given in terms of the parameters of the integrand (see (
B.6
)) as

z=\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\left(\frac{ts_{i,N-2}^{-2}}{ts_{i,N-2}^{-2}}\right)=1\,,

(3.23)

where we recall
s_{N-1,N-2}\equiv 1
.
As explained in the appendix, this implies a divergence of the residue sum associated to the pole tower, and moreover that the contribution from the origin cannot be argued to vanish.
In passing, we note that the fact that
z=1
for the Macdonald index defined with the full measure is a direct generalization of ellipticity of the Schur index integrand with the full measure.

We are thus led to study the Macdonald index with the reduced measure.
The index is then expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{N}(v;q,t)&=\frac{(q,t;q)^{N-1}_{\infty}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm};q)_{\infty}^{N-1}}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint\frac{ds_{i}}{2\pi is_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N-1}\frac{(s_{i,j},qs_{i,j}^{-1},ts_{i,j}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm}s_{i,j}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}\,,\end{split}

(3.24)

where the numerator of the integrand now only contains the reduced measure.
In this case there is an additional factor of
q
multiplying
s_{N-1}^{-1}
, so that the suppression factor
z
becomes

z=\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\frac{qts_{i,N-2}^{-2}}{ts_{i,N-2}^{-2}}=q^{N-1}\,.

(3.25)

Note that this suppression factor is the same as the one for the Schur index when computed with the reduced measure.
It follows that the
s_{N-1}
residue sum converges, and that the contribution from the origin of the complex
s_{N-1}
plane vanishes.
For subsequent integrals, one can perform a similar analysis as for the Schur index.
We will not repeat it here, and instead consider the explicit example of an
SU(3)
gauge group in Section
6
, leaving general
N
to future work.

To end this section, we mention the generalization to
\mathcal{N}=2

SU(N)
superconformal QCD.
We repeat the indices with full and reduced measure from Section
2.3
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(v_{j};p,q,t)&=\frac{(q,t;q)^{N-1}_{\infty}}{N!}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint\frac{du_{i}}{2\pi iu_{i}}\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(u_{ij}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}(tu_{ij}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}{\prod^{2N}_{j=1}\prod^{N}_{i=1}(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{j}u_{i}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{j}^{-1}u_{i}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}\\
&=(q,t;q)^{N-1}_{\infty}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint\frac{du_{i}}{2\pi iu_{i}}\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(u_{ij};q)_{\infty}(qu_{ij}^{-1};q)_{\infty}(tu_{ij}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}{\prod^{2N}_{j=1}\prod^{N}_{i=1}(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{j}u_{i};q)_{\infty}(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{j}^{-1}u_{i}^{-1};q)_{\infty}}\end{split}

(3.26)

Since the only poles come from operators in the fundamental representation, it is not convenient to change to
s_{i}
variables.
Keeping in mind the
SU(N)
condition
u_{N}=(u_{1}\cdots u_{N-1})^{-1}
, we can readily calculate the suppression factors for both cases, using the general result (
B.6
).
For the
u_{N-1}
integral in the first line, with the full measure, we have
m=n=4N
, and the suppression factor becomes

z=\frac{q^{2}t^{2(N-1)}(u_{N}u_{N-1}^{-1})^{2N}}{t^{2N}(u_{N}u_{N-1}^{-1})^{2N}}=\left(\frac{q}{t}\right)^{2}\,,

(3.27)

where we made use of the fact that

(x^{2};q)_{\infty}=(x,-x,q^{\frac{1}{2}}x,-q^{\frac{1}{2}}x;q)_{\infty}\,.

(3.28)

If one would take
|t|=|q|
in the Macdonald index, which is natural when thinking about the Schur index specialization
t=q
, we see that the residue sum is not guaranteed to converge and the contribution from the origin is not guaranteed to vanish.
On the other hand, assuming that
|q|<|t|
, the residue sum will converge and the contribution from the origin will vanish.

With the reduced measure we find the suppression factor

z=\frac{q^{N+2}t^{2(N-1)}(u_{N}u_{N-1}^{-1})^{2N}}{t^{2N}(u_{N}u_{N-1}^{-1})^{2N}}=q^{N}\left(\frac{q}{t}\right)^{2}\,,

(3.29)

Therefore, in this case the residue sum will be convergent even for
|t|=|q|
and the contribution of the origin vanishes.
This feature is expected to hold for any subsequent integration as well.
In Section
5.2
, we consider both the calculation with the reduced measure and with the full measure when
|q|<|t|
.
This will allow us to derive an interesting identity between two distinct residue sums whenever
|q|<|t|
.

3.3
Elliptic hypergeometric integrals

In this section, we argue that the reduced measure can also be used to evaluate the full superconformal index.
Technically, this requires us to evaluate (multivariate) elliptic hypergeometric integrals.
Such integrals have only been evaluated in closed form in very specific cases.
For example, as reviewed in Section
2.4
, the
\mathcal{N}=1
SQCD index can be evaluated in closed form when it has
SU(N)
gauge group, with
N_{f}=N+1
fundamentals, such that the Seiberg dual theory is free.
However, to the best of our knowledge, there is no correct residue evaluation of these and more general cases in the literature, despite some (relatively recent) attempts
Peelaers:2014ima
;
Goldstein:2020yvj
;
spiri2024
.
We describe the issues with previous attempts and the correct residue evaluation of general classes of univariate elliptic hypergeometric integrals in Appendix
C
.
In Section
5
, we use these general results to describe in detail the evaluation and convergence of residue sums for the full superconformal indices of rank
1
gauge theories.
In particular, we study the indices of
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
SYM, and
\mathcal{N}=1,2

SU(2)
SQCD for values of
N_{f}
in the conformal window and at
N_{f}=4
, respectively.

Here, we present a schematic overview of how one would go about the higher rank case and, as in the preceding sections, work for concreteness with the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(N)
SYM theory.
Various relevant technical details can be found in Appendix
C.3
.

From (
2.33
), we take the expression for the index with the full measure and the reduced measure, as shown below

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(v;p,q,t)&=\frac{\left[\kappa\Gamma(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm})\Gamma\left(\tfrac{pq}{t}\right)\right]^{N-1}}{N!}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint\frac{ds_{i}}{2\pi is_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N-1}\frac{\Gamma(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm}s_{i,j}^{\pm})\Gamma(\frac{pq}{t}s_{i,j}^{\pm})}{\Gamma(s_{i,j}^{\pm})}\\
&=\left[\kappa\Gamma(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm})\Gamma\left(\tfrac{pq}{t}\right)\right]^{N-1}\prod^{N-1}_{i=1}\oint\frac{ds_{i}}{2\pi is_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq N-1}\frac{\Gamma(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm}s_{i,j}^{\pm})\Gamma(\frac{pq}{t}s_{i,j}^{\pm})}{(1-s_{i,j}^{-1})\Gamma(s_{i,j}^{\pm})}\end{split}

(3.30)

As in the previous sections, we focus on the
s_{N-1}
integration.
The relevant part of (
3.30
) is then given by

\frac{\kappa\Gamma(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm})\Gamma\left(\tfrac{pq}{t}\right)}{N^{1-\epsilon}}\oint\frac{ds_{N-1}}{2\pi is_{N-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{\Gamma(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm}s_{i,N-1}^{\pm})\Gamma(\frac{pq}{t}s_{i,N-1}^{\pm})}{(1-s_{i,N-1}^{-1})^{\epsilon}\>\Gamma(s_{i,N-1}^{\pm})}

(3.31)

where
\epsilon=0
corresponds to the full measure, and
\epsilon=1
to the reduced measure.
The
s_{N-1}
integral is a generalized elliptic hypergeometric integral, that is not quite well-poised for
N>2
, due to a lack of symmetry under
s_{N-1}\to s_{N-1}^{-1}
.
This is related to the fact that for the
SU(N)
gauge group with
N>2
, the
S_{N}
Weyl symmetry does not include this transformation.

We define and analyze general integrals of this type in Appendix
C.3
.
In contrast with the previous sections, poles now come in doubly infinite towers, as can be seen from the product definition of the elliptic Gamma function.
For the integrand at hand, the only poles arise from the elliptic Gamma functions in the numerator, and are located at:

s^{(j,k,l)}_{N-1}=y_{a}p^{k}q^{l}s_{j,N-2}^{-1}\,,\quad k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\,,\quad j=1,\ldots,N-1\,,

(3.32)

where
y_{1}=t^{\frac{1}{2}}v
,
y_{2}=t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1}
and
y_{3}=\frac{pq}{t}
.
The poles will be simple for generic values of the parameters.
Due to the accumulation of poles at the origin
s_{N-1}=0
and the definition of the elliptic Gamma function, the integrand has a non-isolated essential singularity at the origin, as is familiar by now from the Schur and Macdonald indices.

The convergence of the residue sum and the vanishing of the contribution from the origin depend on ratios of
\theta
-functions involving the various fugacities.
Using (
C.21
), the integral (
3.31
) can be explicitly evaluated as a residue sum with the schematic form

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3(N-1)}\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\text{residue}_{\alpha,k,l}\,,

(3.33)

where the
\alpha
index combines the
(a,j)
indices labeling the poles.
As explained in detail in Appendix
C
, demonstrating the convergence of these types of residue sums requires more care than for the residue sums of basic hypergeometric integrals.
In particular, in order to concretely test for convergence, one has to specify the relative magnitudes of the fugacities as an
input
to the analysis.
21
21

21

For basic hypergeometric integrals, there can also be conditions on the relative sizes of the fugacities, see,
e.g.
, the example of the SQCD Macdonald index in Section
3.2
. However, these conditions arise as an
output
of the calculation. This can be traced to the fact that while the residue sum of a basic hypergeometric integral depends on
q
-shifted factorials
(x;q)_{k}
, the residue sum of elliptic hypergeometric integrals depends on elliptic shifted factorials
\theta_{q}(x;p)_{k}
. We refer to Appendix
B
and
C
for more details.

As an example, we find that if we take
|p|=|q|=T^{3}
and
|t|=T^{4}
for some
T<1
while taking
|s_{1,\ldots,N-2}|=|v|=1
, a ratio test establishes convergence.
22
22

22

This choice ensures a symmetric scaling of the
y_{a}\sim T^{2}
. We expect that there are other ways to scale the fugacities for which the residue sum still converges, including asymmetric scalings, but we leave a systematic analysis to future work.

In particular, we find that

\left|\frac{\text{residue}_{\alpha,k+1,l}}{\text{residue}_{\alpha,k,l}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(T^{(N-1)(3\epsilon-1)})\,.

(3.34)

This implies that the residue sum evaluated with the full measure (
\epsilon=0
) is divergent, while evaluating with the reduced measure (
\epsilon=1
) makes the sum convergent.
As for the Schur and Macdonald index, this also implies a vanishing of the contribution at the origin (again, see Appendix
C
for details).

To evaluate the full index and establish its convergence, one should now consider the subsequent integrals over
s_{1,\ldots,N-2}
.
We leave a detailed analysis to future work, and here simply conjecture that, with the reduced measure, the subsequent integrations also yield convergent sums with vanishing contributions from the origin, as we argued for the Schur and Macdonald indices.

To end this section, let us also briefly consider the index of the
\mathcal{N}=2

SU(N)
SQCD theory with
N_{f}=2N
fundamental flavors with both the full and the reduced measure, given by (
2.37
) and (
2.38
) respectively.
As above, we focus on a single integral, say, the
u_{N-1}
integral.
Solving the
SU(N)
constraint as
u_{N}=(\mathfrak{u}u_{N-1})^{-1}
, with
\mathfrak{u}=u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{N-2}
, the
u_{N-1}
dependent part of the integrand reads

\displaystyle\ \oint\frac{du_{N-1}}{2\pi iu_{N-1}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{pq}{t}(\mathfrak{u}u_{N-1}^{2})^{\pm})}{\Gamma((\mathfrak{u}u_{N-1}^{2})\pm)}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{N-2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{pq}{t}u_{i}^{\pm}u_{N-1}^{\mp})\Gamma(\frac{pq}{t}(u_{i}\mathfrak{u}u_{N-1})^{\pm})}{(1-u_{i}^{-1}u_{N-1})^{\epsilon}\Gamma(u_{i}^{\pm}u_{N-1}^{\mp})\Gamma((u_{i}\mathfrak{u}u_{N-1})^{\pm})}\right]

(3.35)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\prod_{j=1}^{2N}\Gamma(t^{1/2}v_{j}u_{N-1})\Gamma(t^{1/2}v_{j}^{-1}u_{N-1}^{-1})\right]\left[\prod_{j=1}^{2N}\Gamma(t^{1/2}v_{j}\mathfrak{u}^{-1}u_{N-1}^{-1})\Gamma(t^{1/2}v_{j}^{-1}\mathfrak{u}u_{N-1})\right]

As in the
\mathcal{N}=4
case, only the elliptic Gamma functions in the numerator contribute poles.
Assuming all poles to be simple, we again use (
C.21
) to obtain a residue sum with the schematic form

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{6N+4}\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\text{residue}_{\alpha,k,l}\,.

(3.36)

Scaling the fugacities as
|p|=|q|=|t|=T^{2}
for some
T<1
, the ratio test yields

\left|\frac{\text{residue}_{\alpha,k+1,l}}{\text{residue}_{\alpha,k,l}}\right|\sim T^{2\epsilon N}\,.

(3.37)

It follows that convergence of the residue sum is only guaranteed when using the reduced measure, similar to the conclusion for the Schur index, but unlike the Macdonald index!

We conclude that even the elliptic hypergeometric integrals are amenable to a residue evaluation, as long as one uses the reduced measure for the gauge singlet projection.
As mentioned at the beginning of this section, we study examples with rank
1
gauge groups in full detail in Section
5
.
There, we will also comment on the convergence of the residue sums for different, asymmetric scalings of the parameters.

4
Case study: Macdonald index of
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
SYM

In this section, we derive various closed form formulas for the Macdonald index of the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
SYM theory.
We then show how the formulas manifest various features of the (strongly coupled) operator spectrum, how specializations can lead to product formulas for the index and how to interpret such formulas.
Finally, we discuss and interpret the analytic structure and asymptotics of the formulas, and compare to the TQFT formula.

4.1
Derivation

As explained in Section
3.2
, when evaluating the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(N)
Macdonald index through residues, one has to use the reduced measure to guarantee convergence of the residue sum and to argue for the vanishing of a contribution from the origin.
The
SU(2)
version of the integral in (
2.36
) reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{2}(v;q,t)&=\frac{(q,t;q)_{\infty}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v,t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1};q)_{\infty}}\oint\frac{ds}{2\pi is}\frac{(s,qs^{-1},ts^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}vs^{\pm},t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1}s^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}\,.\end{split}

(4.1)

This integral is of the form of the basic hypergeometric contour integral discussed in Appendix
B
.
Adapting the general residue sum in (
B.10
) to the case at hand, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{2}(v;q,t)&=\frac{(t^{\frac{3}{2}}v,qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1};q)_{\infty}}{(tv^{2},v^{-2};q)_{\infty}}{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}tv^{2}&t&qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}v\\
qv^{2}&t^{\frac{3}{2}}v&\end{array};q,q\right]+\left(v\leftrightarrow v^{-1}\right)\end{split}

(4.2)

where
{}_{r+1}\phi_{r}
is a basic hypergeometric series defined in Appendix
A.2
, and we obtain a
{}_{3}\phi_{2}
as opposed to a
{}_{4}\phi_{3}
series, due to the equality of an upper and lower argument in the latter series.
We note that the arguments of
{}_{3}\phi_{2}
are such that the series is
0
-balanced and well-poised.
The two terms in the expression correspond to the residue sums associated with the two towers of poles at
s=t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\pm}q^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
This provides a closed form formula for the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
Macdonald index.
23
23

23

Recently, a closed form formula for the Macdonald index of the
\mathcal{N}=4

U(N)
SYM theory was obtained in
Hatsuda:2025mvj
, closely related to the “TQFT” formulas developed for class
\mathcal{S}
theories in
Gadde:2011uv
. Factoring out the
U(1)
part in their expression for
N=2
, one may observe some key distinctions with our formula. For example, unlike in their expression, the

symmetry is manifest in ours. In Section
4.5
we show in detail how our formula is related to the TQFT formula of
Gadde:2011uv
.

It would be very interesting to have a direct, physical interpretation of the two terms in our formula, perhaps along the lines of
Peelaers:2014ima
.
For now, however, we note that the expression has some undesirable features:

•

Each term separately diverges in the unflavored limit
v=1
.
24
24

24

This subtlety also arises for the expression of the Schur index obtained in
Pan:2021mrw
.

This can be traced to the fact that the integrand develops double poles at
s=t^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{k}
, when
v=1
.
It can be checked that these divergences cancel between the two terms, as they should.

•

Each term separately contains negative powers of
t
.
However, the single letter operators contributing to the Macdonald index all have
R+r\geq 0
, and thus only contribute positive powers of
t
.
Upon expanding the index as a power series, the terms with negative powers of
t
cancel, again as they should.
This redundancy in individual terms is reminiscent of the Bethe Ansatz formula for the full index
Lezcano:2021qbj
;
Benini:2021ano
.

We now show that these issues can be resolved by making use of transformation formulas of the
{}_{3}\phi_{2}
series.
We will do so in a few steps.

First, we use the four-term transformation formula (
A.44
) with
a=t
,
b=qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1}
and
\tilde{b}=qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}v
to rewrite the residue sum as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{2}(v;q,t)=\frac{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v,t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1};q)_{\infty}}{(tv^{2},v^{-2};q)_{\infty}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(tv^{2},t;q)_{k}}{(qv^{2},q;q)_{k}}\frac{q^{k}}{(1-q^{k}t^{\frac{1}{2}}v)}+\left(v\leftrightarrow v^{-1}\right)\end{split}

(4.3)

where
(x;q)_{k}
is the finite
q
-Pochhammer symbol defined in Appendix
A.1
.
This expression makes manifest that the index does not depend on negative powers of
t
, and also its reduction to the Schur index, calculated in (
3.20
), upon taking
t=q
.
However, the unflavored limit is still subtle.

We can further improve the expression by making use of three-term transformation formulas collected in (
A.42
).
Setting
a
,
b
and
\tilde{b}
, as above, we obtain three equivalent forms of (
4.2

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{2}(v;q,t)&=\frac{(qt^{\frac{1}{2}}v,t^{\frac{3}{2}}v^{-1};q)_{\infty}}{(tv^{2},tv^{-2};q)_{\infty}}{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}t&qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1}&t^{\frac{1}{2}}v\\
t^{\frac{3}{2}}v^{-1}&qt^{\frac{1}{2}}v&\\
\end{array};q,t\right]\\
&=\frac{(qt^{\frac{1}{2}}v,qt^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1},t^{2}/q;q)_{\infty}}{(tv^{2},tv^{-2},t;q)_{\infty}}{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}q&qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1}&qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}v\\
qt^{\frac{1}{2}}v&qt^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1}&\\

The poles for

Evaluating the corresponding residues turns out to be particularly simple when

To demonstrate this, we recall the prescription of

For

\mathcal{N}=4

U(1)

This is as expected, since a vev of any of the scalars in the
\mathcal{N}=4

\mathcal{N}=4

For
\tilde{k}>0

It would be nice to interpret these factors in terms of a 2d UV gauge theory description of a vortex worldsheet theory, which flows to the surface defect in the IR.
On the other hand, for odd

Finally, let us comment on the asymptotic behavior of the index for

The (root-of-unity) asymptotics of the

It seems natural to assume that as

We will not pursue the asymptotics in detail here, but just note that the asymptotics match those of the modified index (

This suggests that, with regards to the asymptotics of the index, the dominant contributions come from the operators

4.5
Comparison to TQFT formula

In the preceding sections, we have seen that our expression for the Macdonald index reduces to known expressions in the literature for the Hall-Littlewood and Schur index.
In this section, we compare the full Macdonald index with the so-called TQFT formula for the Macdonald index
Gadde:2011uv
.
36
36

36

Incidentally, the Macdonald index inherits its name from this very formula.

The TQFT expression for the

class

theory with

\mathcal{N}=4

(4.30)

Here, the

Macdonald polynomial is given by

(4.31)

where the normalization factor can be written as

(4.32)

Note that the normalization factor drops out in (

).

After stripping off the decoupled hypermultiplet contribution, we equate the TQFT expression with both the second and third line of (
4.4
).
The equality of the two expressions reduces to the following identities:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum^{\infty}_{\lambda=0}\frac{P_{\lambda}(v,v^{-1};q,t)}{P_{\lambda}(t^{\frac{1}{2}},t^{-\frac{1}{2}};q,t)}&=\frac{(1-\frac{t^{2}}{q})}{(1-t^{\frac{1}{2}}v)(1-t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1})}{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}q&qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1}&qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}v\\

\end{array};q,\frac{t^{2}}{q}\right]\\

t^{\frac{3}{2}}v&t^{\frac{3}{2}}v^{-1}&\\
\end{array};q,q\right]\,.\end{split}

(4.33)

Note that the second identity, together with the results from Section

, implies a product formula for the summed ratio of Macdonald polynomials when

We now show that this is indeed an identity.
In fact, the identity is closely related to a known generating function of
q
-ultraspherical polynomials.
The

-ultraspherical polynomial is defined as

C_{\lambda}(v,v^{-1};t|q)=\sum^{\lambda}_{m=0}\frac{(t;q)_{m}(t;q)_{\lambda-m}}{(q;q)_{m}(q;q)_{\lambda-m}}v^{(2m-\lambda)}

(4.34)

and is identical to the unnormalized

Macdonald polynomial.
It is known that

37

37

See exercise 9.8 in

\sum^{\infty}_{\lambda=0}\frac{(q;q)_{\lambda}}{(t^{2};q)_{\lambda}}C_{\lambda}\left(v,v^{-1};t|q\right)t^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}=\frac{(t^{\frac{3}{2}}v,t^{\frac{3}{2}}v^{-1},q;q)_{\infty}}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}v,t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1},t^{2};q)_{\infty}}{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}t^{2}/q&t^{\frac{1}{2}}v^{-1}&t^{\frac{1}{2}}v\\

(4.35)

where the left hand side corresponds to

Assuming this identity, we have shown the relation between our (simplified) formula for the Macdonald index and the TQFT formula.
In particular, our expression effectively resums the (Laurent) series in the flavor fugacity

A similar resummation was observed in the context of the Schur index in Appendix E of

Pan:2021mrw
.
We expect a similar relation with the TQFT-like formula recently derived in
Hatsuda:2025mvj

In the appendix, we assume that the phases of the fugacities are generic such that the poles of the integrand in (

A symmetric treatment of the absolute values of

The analytic structure of a term in (

Apart from simple poles at unflavored limits

, as we are now accustomed to, it also has poles of arbitrary degree whenever

, for any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
This is due to the factors of
\theta_{q}(p;p)_{k}
and
\theta_{p}(q;q)_{k}
in the denominators of the respective elliptic hypergeometric summands and reflects the fact that the original integrand develops higher order poles for such specializations.
In terms of the chemical potentials
\sigma\equiv\sigma_{1}+i\sigma_{2}
and
\tau=\tau_{1}+i\tau_{2}
, with
p=e^{2\pi i\sigma}
and

, this implies that the summands hit singularities when simultaneously

\sigma_{1}=\frac{n}{m}\tau_{1}\,,\quad\sigma_{2}=\frac{n}{m}\tau_{2}\,,

(5.14)

for any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
It follows that as long as the real and imaginary parts of
\sigma
and

are not related by the same rational, our expression for the index is well-defined.
We stress that this complicated behavior should cancel out in the full expression, since the full index should admit these specializations.
We also note that the restriction on the

dependence for the summands is complementary to the Bethe Ansatz formula for the superconformal index, which is precisely defined for

;

It would be extremely interesting if our expression for the full index admits a simplification meeting (some of) the criteria specified at the end of Section

.
Such an expression could perhaps simplify the analytic structure and suggest a physical interpretation, as we have experienced in the case of the Macdonald index.
We also expect that a simplified expression, if it exists, will shed light on the TQFT formulation of the general superconformal index

(see also

This expectation is based on Section

We end this section with some comments on the modular properties of the expression.
First, apart from the fact that the elliptic hypergeometric summands

are well-poised, they are also elliptically balanced (see Appendix

).
This implies, by Theorem 3 of

, that the functions are modular invariant in the following sense.
If we set

,
p=e^{2\pi i\sigma}
,

, and define

\tilde{y}_{i}=e^{\frac{2\pi i\Delta_{i}}{\tau}}\,,\quad\tilde{p}=e^{\frac{2\pi i\sigma}{\tau}}\,,\quad\tilde{q}=e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{\tau}}\,.

(5.15)

Then using the modular property of the
\theta_{q}(x)
function in (

) we find for the elliptic hypergeometric summand in (

{}_{4}W^{(k)}_{3}(\tilde{y}_{1}^{2};\tilde{p}\tilde{y}_{1},\tilde{y}_{1}\tilde{y}_{2},\tilde{p}\tilde{y}_{2}^{-1};\tilde{p};\tilde{q};\tilde{p})=e^{2\pi ik\frac{\sigma(1-\tau)}{\tau}}{}_{4}W^{(k)}_{3}(y_{1}^{2};py_{1},y_{1}y_{2},py_{2}^{-1};p;q;p)\,,

(5.16)

where we solved for
y_{3}=pq/y_{1}y_{2}
.
Moreover, the function is manifestly invariant under
\tau\to\tau+1
, so it transforms simply under the full modular group
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

The second elliptic hypergeometric summand in (
5.11
) satisfies a similar modular property, but now with the roles of
p
and
q
exchanged.
The fact that the factors satisfy modular properties with different modular parameters (
\tau
and
\sigma
, respectively), is closely related to modular factorization of 4d superconformal indices
Gadde:2020bov
;
Jejjala:2022lrm
.
This proposal states that the index for general gauge theories can be factorized in a variety of ways labeled by the modular group and builds on holomorphic block factorization of the 4d index
Nieri:2015yia
(see also
Yoshida:2014qwa
;
Peelaers:2014ima
;
Gadde:2020bov
).
Schematically, it states that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(a_{i};\sigma,\tau)&\cong\sum_{i}\mathcal{B}_{i}\left(\tfrac{a_{i}}{c\tau+d};\tfrac{\sigma+\tau}{c\tau+d},\tfrac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{i}\left(\tfrac{a_{i}}{-c\sigma+d};\tfrac{\tau+\sigma}{-c\sigma+d},\tfrac{a\sigma-b}{-c\sigma+d}\right)\,,\end{split}

(5.17)

for any integers
a,b,c,d
, such that
ad-bc=1
.
Using the modular properties of the elliptic hypergeometric summands and of the elliptic Gamma function (
A.74
), the fixed
k,l
summand of the index in (
5.11
) can be seen to almost reflect this structure, apart from the denominator of the summand, which does not factorize.
42
42

42

Interestingly, in the Schur limit it is precisely this denominator that, when summed over, becomes a twisted Eisenstein series (see the discussion below (
3.20
)), which does have modular properties
Pan:2021mrw
.

The lack of exact factorization may be related to the fact that one cannot fully Higgs the gauge group in the
\mathcal{N}=4
theory.
Indeed, as shown in
Peelaers:2014ima
, full factorization of the index can be achieved for gauge theories in which the gauge group can be fully Higgsed, which requires sufficient fundamental matter, a
U(1)
factor in the gauge group and a non-zero Fayet-Iliopoulos coupling.

This theory fits into the

5.2.1
Macdonald Index

From Section
2.3
, we collect the contour integral expression for the Macdonald index.
With the reduced measure, we have the following expression:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{I}_{\text{SQCD}}(y_{a};q,t)&=(q,t;q)_{\infty}\oint\frac{du}{2\pi iu}\frac{(u^{2},qu^{-2},tu^{\pm 2};q)_{\infty}}{\prod_{a=1}^{8}(y_{a}u^{\pm 1};q)_{\infty}}\end{aligned}

(5.18)

where we define

y_{a}=\{t^{\frac{1}{2}}\upsilon_{1}^{\pm}\upsilon_{2}^{\pm},t^{\frac{1}{2}}\upsilon_{3}^{\pm}\upsilon_{4}^{\pm}\}

(5.19)

with
|\upsilon_{i}|=1
flavor fugacities for the
SU(2)^{4}\subset SO(8)
flavor symmetry subgroup.
We now use the fact that

(x^{2};q)_{\infty}=(\pm x,\pm q^{\frac{1}{2}}x;q)_{\infty}

(5.20)

to note that (
5.18
) is again of the basic hypergeometric form defined in (
B.1
).
In particular, as argued at the end of Section
3.2
, the suppression factor equals
q^{4}/t^{2}
, making the residue sum well-defined for
|q|<|t^{\frac{1}{2}}|<1
.
In addition, this implies that the contribution from the origin vanishes.
We then use the general evaluation in (
B.10
) to find the following expression for the residue sum:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{I}_{\text{SQCD}}(y_{a};q,t)&=\sum_{a=1}^{8}\frac{(t,qy_{a}^{-2},ty_{a}^{\pm 2};q)_{\infty}}{\prod_{b\neq a}^{8}(y_{b}y_{a}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}\\
&\times{}_{12}\phi_{11}\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}y_{a}^{2}&y_{a}y_{b}&\pm qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}y_{a}&\pm q^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}y_{a}\\
qy_{a}y_{b}^{-1}&\pm t^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{a}&\pm q^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{a}&\end{array};q,\frac{q^{4}}{t^{2}}\right]\end{aligned}

(5.21)

where the
y_{a}y_{b}
and

arguments of the
{}_{12}\phi_{11}
series should be understood as seven separate arguments labeled by
b\neq a
.
Similarly, the arguments with
\pm

We note that the entries are such that the series is well-poised.
This provides a closed form formula for the Macdonald index of
\mathcal{N}=2

SU(2)
SQCD, which as far as we are aware has not appeared in the literature before.

We note that the expression shares similar features with the other residue sums evaluated so far: the (apparent) presence of negative powers of
y_{a}
, and the apparent poles in the unflavored limits
y_{a}=y_{b}
.
One may again wonder whether a simplification exists, for which some or all of these features disappear.
As in Section
5.1.1
, a special case of Sears’ transformation formulas seems closely related but not quite applicable.

Generalized
S
-duality for the present theory implies that the index is invariant under the exchange
\upsilon_{1}\leftrightarrow\upsilon_{3}

Gaiotto:2009we
;
Gadde:2009kb
.
This is not obvious from our expression and, as such, should lead to interesting transformation formulas for the
{}_{12}\phi_{11}
series.
We expect that a simplification of the formula may manifest this symmetry, like the TQFT formula does, as we will discuss at the end of this section.

As observed in Section
3.2
, one can make sense of the residue sum for this theory using the full measure, as long as
|q|<|t|
(which precludes the Schur limit).
We may then use the general results in Appendix
B
to find the alternative expression

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{I}_{\text{SQCD}}(y_{a};q,t)&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{8}\frac{(t,y_{a}^{-2},ty_{a}^{\pm 2};q)_{\infty}}{\prod_{b\neq a}^{8}(y_{b}y_{a}^{\pm};q)_{\infty}}\\
&\times{}_{14}\phi_{13}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}y_{a}^{2}&\pm qy_{a}&y_{a}y_{b}&\pm qt^{-\frac{1}{2}}y_{a}&\pm q^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}y_{a}\\
\pm y_{a}&qy_{a}y_{b}^{-1}&\pm t^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{a}&\pm q^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}y_{a}&\end{array};q,\frac{q^{2}}{t^{2}}\right]\,.\end{aligned}

(5.22)

The arguments of the
{}_{14}\phi_{13}
series are such that it is very-well-poised balanced (see Appendix
A.2
).
The present formula appears most closely related to a special case of Sears’ transformation formulas, specifically the
M=7
case in eqn. 4.12.1 of
Gasper_Rahman_2004
.
However, we have not been able to use the identity to simplify the index.
Similar to the expression obtained with the reduced measure, this formula is not manifestly invariant under generalized
S
-duality.
Therefore, the exchange
\upsilon_{1}\leftrightarrow\upsilon_{3}
should imply yet again interesting transformation formulas.

In passing, we note that the equality of (
5.21
) and (
5.22
), for
|q|<|t|

This demonstrates how the indices computed with the reduced and full measure, when possible, can lead to non-trivial identities for basic, and perhaps also elliptic, hypergeometric series.

Let us now consider the Hall-Littlewood limit of our expression.
Taking
q\to 0
in (
5.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(y_{a};t)=\sum_{a=1}^{8}\frac{(1-t)(1-ty_{a}^{\pm 2})}{\prod_{b\neq a}^{8}(1-y_{b}y_{a}^{\pm})}\,.

(5.23)

y_{a}

As a result, the unflavored limit is easily taken, which would have been subtle in (

In other words, the simplified expression satisfies all criteria listed at the end of Section
5.1.1
.
The fully refined expression for

Only when all

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}(\upsilon;t)=&\,\frac{(1+t)P_{6}(\upsilon;t)}{(1-t)^{2}(1-t\upsilon^{\pm 2})^{4}(1-t\upsilon^{\pm 4})}\\
P_{6}(\upsilon;t)=&\,1+t(4\upsilon^{2}+7+4\upsilon^{-2})+t^{2}(\upsilon^{4}+4\upsilon^{2}+5+4\upsilon^{-2}+\upsilon^{-4})\\

(5.25)

The simplified expression for the HL index can be interpreted in terms of the HL chiral ring of the theory

;
Beem:2013sza
.
In particular, the bosonic generators of the ring are the mesons

SO(8)

Furthermore, all fermionic generators are lifted from the ring at one-loop; their contribution to the index can be interpreted in terms of bosonic relations between the mesonic generators in the Higgs branch chiral ring

;
Gadde:2011uv

Benvenuti:2010pq
;
Hanany:2010qu

Let us finally compare the expressions with the TQFT expression for the Macdonald index, which reads

\mathcal{I}^{\text{TQFT}}_{\text{SQCD}}(\upsilon_{i};q,t)=\frac{(t^{2};q)^{2}_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}(t;q)^{3}_{\infty}\prod^{4}_{i=1}(t\upsilon_{i}^{2},t\upsilon_{i}^{-2};q)_{\infty}}\sum^{\infty}_{\lambda=0}\frac{\prod^{4}_{i=1}P_{\lambda}(\upsilon_{i},\upsilon_{i}^{-1};q,t)}{\left(P_{\lambda}(t^{\frac{1}{2}},t^{-\frac{1}{2}};q,t)\right)^{2}}\,,

(5.26)

Unlike our residue sums, this expression is manifestly invariant under permutations of the

Making use of (
4.32
) and (

\mathcal{I}^{\text{TQFT}}_{\text{SQCD}}(\upsilon_{i};q,t)=\frac{(t^{2};q)^{3}_{\infty}}{(t;q)^{5}_{\infty}\prod^{4}_{i=1}(t\upsilon_{i}^{2},t\upsilon_{i}^{-2};q)_{\infty}}\sum^{\infty}_{\lambda=0}t^{\lambda}(1-tq^{\lambda})\frac{(q;q)^{3}_{\lambda}}{(t^{2};q)^{3}_{\lambda}}\prod^{4}_{i=1}C_{\lambda}(\upsilon_{i},\upsilon_{i}^{-1};t|q)\,.

(5.27)

The equality between the residue sums and the TQFT expression represents a highly non-trivial identity.
As in Section
4.5

A potential simplification of the residue sums for the Macdonald index, either (
5.21

5.22

q

One can check that the elliptic hypergeometric summand satisfies the same modular property we observed in (

As described in detail in Appendix

, if we take the magnitude of the various fugacities as
|y_{i}|=T^{N_{f}-2}
, and
|p|=|q|=T^{N_{f}}
, for
T<1
, the residue sum converges and the contribution from the origin will vanish.
Interestingly, with this symmetric scaling, convergence can only be shown for
3\leq N_{f}\leq 7
.
We note that this range contains the conformal window
3\leq N_{f}\leq 6
. But, it also includes the
N_{f}=7
case
Seiberg:1994pq
.

One may be worried that we cannot demonstrate convergence for
N_{f}=8
. However, we can establish the convergence of the residue sum of the index of the
\mathcal{N}=2

SU(2)
SQCD theory with
N_{f}=4
, which as discussed in Section
5.2
corresponds to the same elliptic hypergeometric integral.
This apparent contradiction is resolved by noting that the relative magnitudes of the fugacities are scaled differently between the two cases.
This thereby provides an example of the subtleties associated with the convergence of residue sums for elliptic hypergeometric integrals, or indeed, elliptic hypergeometric series.

Let us contrast our proposed residue sum with previous attempts in the literature
Peelaers:2014ima
;
spiri2024
.
Both these references define their contour integrals with the full measure.
However, as shown in Appendix
C.1
, the residue sum as defined with the full measure diverges, at least with our choice of relative magnitudes of the fugacities.
There may exist alternative choices for which the residue sum with full measure converges. But, based on some experiments we ran, we find that this appears unlikely.
As a consequence, for these cases one
cannot
conclude that the contribution from the origin is vanishing.
We note that these issues were acknowledged in
Peelaers:2014ima
and discussed in more detail in
spiri2024
, but to the best of our understanding they were not resolved.

Finally, let us consider the special case of
N_{f}=3
, when the Seiberg dual theory is free and the index is described by a product formula (see Section
2.4
).
Equating our residue sum with the product formula, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum^{2N_{f}}_{i=1}\frac{\prod_{j\neq 1}^{2N_{f}}\Gamma(y_{j}y_{i}^{\pm})}{\Gamma(y_{i}^{-2})}\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-y_{i}^{2})\>{}_{2N_{f}+4}V_{2N_{f}+3}^{(k)}(\vec{\mathpzc{a}}_{i};p;q;p^{2})\,{}_{2N_{f}+4}V_{2N_{f}+3}^{(l)}(\vec{\mathpzc{b}}_{i};q;p;q^{2})}{1-y_{i}^{2}p^{2k}q^{2l}}\\
&=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 6}\Gamma(y_{i}y_{j})\,.\end{split}

(5.38)

This can be read as a non-terminating summation formula for the product of elliptic hypergeometric summands.
One could rewrite this identity alternatively in terms of an infinite sum of products of two full elliptic hypergeometric series by expanding the denominator as a geometric series, as in (
5.13
).
As reviewed in Appendix
A.2
, for basic hypergeometric series there exist both terminating and non-terminating summation formulas.
However, in the case of elliptic hypergeometric series, only summations for terminating series are known such as the Frenkel-Turaev summation
Frenkel-Turaev
.
It has been an open problem to find summations for non-terminating elliptic hypergeometric series.
The fact that such summations should take on a bilinear form, as we find, was for example suggested in
spiri2024
.
However, the non-factorizing denominator, which is a direct manifestation of the reduced measure, and is therefore closely related to convergence of the residue sum, was not noted before.

A key feature of (
5.38
) is that the right hand side demonstrates how the complicated analytic structure of summands on the left hand side for fixed
i
, as discussed in Section
5.1.2
, simplify when they are summed.
Indeed, the right hand side is well-defined in the unflavored limits
y_{i}=y_{j}
, and even for
p^{m}=q^{n}
, for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We therefore see that in this case, there indeed exists a simplification of the residue sum with the expected features listed at the end of Section
5.1.1
.
Whether there exist simplifications for the superconformal indices of other theories remains an important open question.

Let us end this section with a small comment on the
N_{f}=4
case
Spiridonov:2008thf
.
As reviewed in Section
2.4
, in this case both the electric and magnetic index can be expressed in terms of a single
(SU(2))
contour integral.
Therefore, by evaluating both sides as a residue sum, with the reduced measure, could lead to a non-trivial transformation formula for (bilinear summands) of elliptic hypergeometric series.
We hope to report on this identity soon.

7
Conclusion and future directions

In this work, we have evaluated superconformal indices of gauge theories through residues.
The key new insight on which our analysis relies is that in almost all examples considered, the residue sums can only be made sense of when using a “reduced measure” to perform the gauge singlet projection.
The reduced measure can be obtained from the usual Haar measure by gauge fixing not just to a diagonal
SU(N)
matrix, but also fixing the residual
S_{N}
Weyl symmetry.
We have applied this method to various indices of the
\mathcal{N}=4,2,1

SU(2)
gauge theories and, as such, derived new closed form expressions for their indices.
In the final section, we also demonstrated how the method generalizes to higher rank gauge theories, by deriving the residue sum for
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(3)
Macdonald index.

The main motivation for our work is to find alternative, more minimal expressions for the index which manifest the strongly coupled spectrum of the corresponding SCFT.
We showed that, for the case of the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
Macdonald index, the residue can be thought of as a useful intermediate step towards this goal.
Indeed, by expressing the index in terms of basic hypergeometric series, we were able to use known identities for such series to simplify the residue sum.
We argued that the resulting expression is indeed a manifestation of the strongly coupled spectrum.
In particular, the expression provides further support for the conjecture that there are no “non-graviton” operators in the Macdonald (or Schur) sector of the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
theory
Beem:2013sza
;
Chang:2023ywj
.

Another interesting feature of the closed form expression is that it manifests the analytic properties of the index and, in particular, can be used to analytically continue in the fugacities.
In this way, we found new specializations of the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
Macdonald index, including
t=q^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
t=1
, for which it simplifies into a product formula.
Finally, the expression is related in a non-trivial way with the TQFT expression for the index, which is written in terms of a sum over Macdonald polynomials
Gadde:2011uv
.

On the mathematical side, our method has allowed us, for the first time, to evaluate elliptic hypergeometric integrals through residues.
As has been expected in the literature, the convergent residue sums are closely related to (bilinear combinations of) elliptic hypergeometric series.
Our evaluation is mostly consistent with this expectation, except for a non-factorizing prefactor, such that residues are bilinear combinations in terms of elliptic hypergeometric summands, as opposed to the entire series.
Dualities of supersymmetric gauge theories, such as Seiberg duality and generalized
S
-duality, imply non-trivial summation and transformation properties of these
series
(as opposed to well-known implications for elliptic hypergeometric
integrals
, see,
e.g.
,
Dolan:2008qi
;
Gadde:2009kb
;
Spiridonov:2009za
;
Rains:2003teh
).
In short, we have found new examples of the rich interplay between superconformal indices and the theory of basic and elliptic hypergeometric integrals and series.

Let us end with some questions and directions for future research:

•

For the residue sums of full superconformal indices, a systematic analysis of the domain of convergence is warranted.
We expect such an exploration to also clarify the connection of our formula with the Bethe Ansatz formula.

•

We have argued that our method applies to higher rank gauge theories as well, and demonstrated this in detail for the Macdonald index of the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(3)
theory.
It would be desirable to develop a more systematic approach to the evaluation of the residue sums at higher rank.

•

Is it possible to find simplifications

46

46

5.1.1
.
of the residue sums for indices other than the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
Macdonald index?
If there exists, for example, a simplification of the residue sums for the
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(2)
BMN Macdonald or even full superconformal index, it could have tantalizing applications.
For one, we imagine this could lead to a more structural understanding of the non-graviton spectrum in this theory

;
Choi:2023znd
;
Choi:2023vdm

In addition, it may yield hints about the TQFT description of the full superconformal index

;
Razamat:2013qfa

A simplification is also expected to manifest analytic properties of the index and shed further light on its modular properties
Gadde:2020bov

Finally, it may help in finding a microscopic interpretation of various “thermodynamic” instabilities observed on the AdS side

•

We observed that the specialization

•

A.1
q
-Pochhammer symbol

The finite
q
-Pochhammer symbol
(x;q)_{n}
is defined as

(x;q)_{n}=\begin{cases}1\,,&n=0\\
\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-xq^{k})\,,&n\geq 1\\
\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{-n}(1-xq^{-k})}\,,&n\leq-1\end{cases}\,,

(A.1)

From these definitions, it follows that

(x;q)_{-n}=\frac{1}{(xq^{-n};q)_{n}}=\frac{(-q/x)^{n}}{(q/x;q)_{n}}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\,,\quad(x;q^{-1})_{n}=(x^{-1};q)_{n}(-x)^{n}q^{-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\,.

(A.2)

For
|q|<1
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, we can extend the definition to an infinite product:

(x;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-xq^{k})=\exp\left(-\sum^{\infty}_{l=1}\frac{1}{l}\frac{x^{l}}{(1-q^{l})}\right)\,.

(A.3)

Using the exponential expression, one can continue the infinite
q
-Pochhammer symbol to
|q|>1
via:

(x;q)_{\infty}=\frac{1}{(q^{-1}x;q^{-1})_{\infty}}\,.

(A.4)

One observes

(x;q)_{n}=\frac{(x;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{n}x;q)_{\infty}}\,,

(A.5)

which can be used to continue
(x;q)_{n}
to non-integer
n
.
We will often use a shorthand notation for products of (finite or infinite)
q
-Pochhammer symbols,

(a_{1},\dots,a_{r};q)_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(a_{i};q)_{n}\,.

(A.6)

Two identities we will make frequent use of are

\displaystyle\begin{split}(q^{n}x;q)_{\infty}&=\frac{(x;q)_{\infty}}{(x;q)_{n}}\\
(q^{-n}x;q)_{\infty}&=(x;q)_{\infty}(q/x;q)_{n}\left(-\frac{x}{q}\right)^{n}q^{-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\,,\end{split}

(A.7)

for
n\geq 0

Another useful identity is (using the notation (
A.6

(x^{2};q)_{\infty}=(\pm x,\pm q^{1/2}x;q)_{\infty}\,.

(A.8)

The
q
-theta function, or the modified Jacobi theta function,
\theta(z;\tau)
is defined in terms of the
q
-Pochhammer symbols as

\theta(z;\tau)=(x;q)_{\infty}(qx^{-1};q)_{\infty}=\exp\left(-\sum^{\infty}_{l=1}\frac{1}{l}\frac{(x^{l}+qx^{-l})}{(1-q^{l})}\right)\,,

(A.9)

where
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
and

In the main text, we often use the shorthand notation

(A.10)

From its definition, it is clear that

has zeros at

, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
A few basic properties of

are

\displaystyle\theta(z+m\tau+n;\tau)=(-x)^{-m}q^{-\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}\theta(z;\tau)\,,

(A.11)

\displaystyle\theta(-z;\tau)=\theta(z+\tau;\tau)\,,

(A.12)

\displaystyle\theta(z;-\tau)\theta(z;\tau)=-x\,.

(A.13)

It also satisfies a modular property.
That is, for

\displaystyle\theta\left(g(z;\tau)\right)

\displaystyle=e^{i\pi B_{g}(z;\tau)}\theta(z;\tau)\,,\quad g(z;\tau)=\left(\tfrac{z}{m\tau+n};\tfrac{k\tau+l}{m\tau+n}\right)\,,

(A.14)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}B_{g}(z;\tau)=&\tfrac{m}{m\tau+n}z^{2}+\left(\tfrac{1}{m\tau+n}-1\right)z+\tfrac{1}{6}\left(\tau+\tfrac{1}{m(m\tau+n)}\right)+\tfrac{n}{6m}-\tfrac{1}{2}-2s(n,m)\,,\end{split}

(A.15)

and
s(n,m)
the Dedekind sum.
We note that when
k=n=0
and
m=-l=1
,
i.e.

S
-transformation in
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
, we have
B(z;\tau)\equiv B_{S}(z;\tau)
with

\displaystyle\begin{split}B(z;\tau)=\tfrac{1}{\tau}z^{2}+\left(\tfrac{1}{\tau}-1\right)z+\tfrac{1}{6}\left(\tau+\tfrac{1}{\tau}\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\,,\end{split}

(A.16)

where we used that
s(0,1)=0
.
The general polynomial
B_{g}(z;\tau)
can be expressed in terms of

(A.17)

where

\sigma_{t}(n_{1},n_{2}\cdots,n_{r};m)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\xi^{m}=1\neq\xi}\frac{\xi^{t}}{(\xi^{n_{1}}-1)\cdots(\xi^{n_{r}}-1)}\,.

(A.18)

A.2
Basic hypergeometric series

The basic hypergeometric series is defined as
Gasper_Rahman_2004

{}_{r+1}\phi_{r}\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1}&\cdots&a_{r}&a_{r+1}\\
b_{1}&\cdots&b_{r}&\\
\end{array};q,z\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r+1};q)_{k}}{(q,b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{r};q)_{k}}z^{k}\,,

(A.19)

where we use the notation introduced in (
A.6

Note that the series is terminating when
a_{i}=q^{-k}
, for some
i

k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

This series converges for
|q|<1

It is called

b_{1}\cdots b_{r}=q^{k}a_{1}\cdots a_{r+1}\,.

(A.20)

If the series is

qa_{1}=a_{2}b_{1}=\ldots=a_{r+1}b_{r}\,,

a_{2}=-a_{3}=qa_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(A.22)

a_{1}q\neq a_{2}b_{1}=\ldots=a_{r+1}b_{r}\,,

(A.23)

a_{1}q=a_{2}b_{1}=\ldots\neq a_{r+1}b_{r}\,.

(A.24)

|x|=\delta|q|^{k}
, with
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
\delta
chosen such that

does not pass through any pole of

.
Then,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\frac{P(x)}{x}\right|_{x=\delta q^{k}}=\left|\frac{(a_{1}\delta,\dots,a_{n}\delta,b_{1}/\delta,\dots,b_{n}/\delta;q)_{\infty}}{(c_{1}\delta,\dots,c_{m}\delta,d_{1}/\delta,\dots,d_{m}/\delta;q)_{\infty}}\right|\\
&\times\left|\frac{(q\delta/b_{1},\dots,q\delta/b_{n},c_{1}\delta,\dots,c_{m}\delta;q)_{k}}{(a_{1}\delta,\dots,a_{n}\delta,q\delta/d_{1},\dots,q\delta/d_{m};q)_{k}}\right|\left|\frac{1}{\delta q^{k}}\left(\frac{b_{1}\cdots b_{n}}{d_{1}\cdots d_{n}}\right)^{k}\left(\delta^{k}q^{k(k+1)/2}\right)^{m-n}\right|.\end{split}

(B.7)

The scaling with
k
is only determined by the last factor, and we can write

\left|\frac{P(x)}{x}\right|_{x=\delta q^{k}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\delta q^{k}}\left(\frac{b_{1}\cdots b_{n}}{d_{1}\cdots d_{n}}\right)^{k}\left(\delta^{k}q^{k(k+1)/2}\right)^{m-n}\right|\right).

(B.8)

Since the contour becomes increasingly tightly wrapped around the origin for
k\to\infty
, with circumference
\mathcal{O}(\delta|q|^{k})
, we find that the contribution from the origin,
I_{0}
, is upper bounded by:

I_{0}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\oint_{C_{k}}\frac{dx}{2\pi i}\left|\frac{P(x)}{x}\right|\,.

(B.9)

It follows that if either
m>n
or (
B.5
) is satisfied,
I_{0}\to 0
.
Note that these are precisely the conditions for convergence of the residue sum.

Assuming that
n=m
and that (
B.5
) holds, we are thus able to evaluate the basic hypergeometric integral through residues.
One finds:

\displaystyle\begin{split}I=\sum^{m}_{i=1}&\frac{\prod^{m}_{j=1}(a_{j}d_{i},b_{j}d_{i}^{-1};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}\prod^{m}_{j=1}(c_{j}d_{i};q)_{\infty}\prod_{j\neq i}(d_{j}d_{i}^{-1};q)_{\infty}}\\
&\times{}_{2m}\phi_{2m-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}c_{1}d_{i}&\cdots&c_{m}d_{i}&qd_{i}/b_{1}&\cdots&qd_{i}/b_{m}\\
a_{1}d_{i}&\cdots&a_{m}d_{i}&qd_{i}/d_{1}&\widehat{qd_{i}/d_{j=i}}&qd_{i}/d_{m}\end{array};q;\frac{b_{1}\cdots b_{m}}{d_{1}\cdots d_{m}}\right]\end{split}

(B.10)

where the hatted entry
qd_{i}/d_{j=i}
is to be omitted and the basic hypergeometric series
{}_{2m}\phi_{2m-1}
is defined in Appendix
A.2
.

Appendix C
Residue sums for elliptic hypergeometric integrals

In this appendix, we review the definition of a class of elliptic hypergeometric integrals and evaluate them through residues.

The general well-poised elliptic hypergeometric integral is defined in
Spiridonov:2008thf
and takes the form

48

48

See also the earlier
Spi203

I=\oint\frac{dx}{2\pi ix}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{A}\Gamma(a_{\alpha}x^{\pm};p,q),

(C.1)

where the elliptic Gamma function was defined in Appendix

and the parameters satisfy the balancing condition

\prod_{\alpha=1}^{A}a_{\alpha}^{2}=(pq)^{A}\,.

(C.2)

The connection between the elliptic hypergeometric integrals and elliptic hypergeometric series (defined in Appendix

) is expected to be through residues, see,
e.g.
, the early work
Spi203
.
However, it is well-known that convergence of elliptic hypergeometric series is marred by issues; see for example
Spiridonov32002
and more recently
Krotkov:2023inf
;
spiri2024
.
As a result, the evaluation of elliptic hypergeometric integrals in terms of convergent elliptic hypergeometric series is also unclear.
Two previous attempts at evaluating the residue sum of,
e.g.
, the superconformal index of the
\mathcal{N}=1

SU(2)
theory with
N_{f}=3
were made in
Peelaers:2014ima
;
spiri2024
.
49
49

49

This index can be alternatively evaluated in closed form making use of Spiridonov’s elliptic beta integral

, as first observed in
Dolan:2008qi
.

However, assuming that all parameters have modulus less than 1 with a particular symmetric scaling (specified below), we will argue that the residue sums in
Peelaers:2014ima
;
spiri2024
do not converge.
As in the basic hypergeometric case reviewed in Appendix
B
, the lack of convergence also obstructs the argument for the vanishing of a contribution from the origin, where a non-isolated essential singularity sits.
We note that this was acknowledged in both
Peelaers:2014ima
and
spiri2024
, but not resolved.

In this appendix, we evaluate classes of elliptic hypergeometric integrals as residue sums, by relating the integrals to superconformal indices of gauge theories and using the reduced measure for the gauge singlet projection.
The result takes the form of a novel double infinite sum over the product of summands of two elliptic hypergeometric series with, however, a non-factorizing prefactor.
As such, the expression cannot be directly expressed in terms of the product of two elliptic hypergeometric series, as was suggested in
Peelaers:2014ima
;
spiri2024
, although one
can
express the result in terms of an infinite sum over products of elliptic hypergeometric series.
We explicitly demonstrate the convergence of these residue sums, which also implies the vanishing of a potential contribution from the origin, in the spirit of Theorem 2.1 of
Askey_Wilson_1985
and Section 4.10 of
Gasper_Rahman_2004
.

C.1
Very-well-poised integrals

As reviewed in Section

We assume that the parameters

As in the basic hypergeometric case, the convergence of the residue sums will depend on details of the relative sizes of

However, to argue for convergence in the elliptic hypergeometric case, one is required to choose the relative sizes

This complicates a general analysis, and for this reason, we focus here on some special cases.
Unsurprisingly, also in the elliptic hypergeometric case, the integrand has a non-isolated essential singularity at the origin
x=0
.
The vanishing of its contribution can again be argued when the residue sum associated with all other poles converges.
As we will see, convergence can only be demonstrated for
\epsilon=1
, at least for our choice of scaling of the fugacities, so that only in this case one can conclude that the contribution from the origin vanishes.

Using the shift identities (
A.73
) for
\Gamma(xp^{\pm k}q^{\pm l})
and the expression (
A.78

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{I}_{\text{vwp}}=\ \frac{2^{\epsilon}}{2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{A}\frac{\prod_{\beta\neq\alpha}\Gamma(a_{\beta}a_{\alpha}^{\pm})}{\Gamma(a_{\alpha}^{-2})}\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{-a_{\alpha}^{2}p^{2k}q^{2l}}{1-a_{\alpha}^{2}p^{2k}q^{2l}}\right)^{\epsilon}(-a_{\alpha})^{(A-4)(k+l)}p^{k}q^{l}\\
&\times p^{\frac{(A-4)(k^{2}-l)}{2}}q^{\frac{(A-4)(l^{2}-k)}{2}}\frac{\theta_{q}(a_{\alpha}^{2}p^{2k})}{\theta_{q}(a_{\alpha}^{2})}\frac{\theta_{p}(a_{\alpha}^{2}q^{2l})}{\theta_{p}(a_{\alpha}^{2})}\prod_{\beta=1}^{A}\frac{\theta_{q}(a_{\alpha}a_{\beta};p)_{k}}{\theta_{q}(pa_{\alpha}a_{\beta}^{-1};p)_{k}}\frac{\theta_{p}(a_{\alpha}a_{\beta};q)_{l}}{\theta_{p}(qa_{\alpha}a_{\beta}^{-1};q)_{l}}\,,\end{split}

(C.7)

which is formally valid for odd

Using (

Using the definition of the very-well-poised elliptic hypergeometric summand (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\text{vwp}}=&\ \frac{2^{\epsilon}}{2}\frac{\prod_{\beta\neq 1}^{A}\Gamma(a_{\beta}a_{1}^{\pm})}{\Gamma(a_{1}^{-2})}\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-a_{1}^{2})^{\epsilon}\>{}_{A+4}V_{A+3}^{(k)}(\vec{\mathpzc{a}}_{1};p;q;p^{2\epsilon})\>{}_{A+4}V_{A+3}^{(l)}(\vec{\mathpzc{b}}_{1};q;p;q^{2\epsilon})}{(1-a_{1}^{2}p^{2k}q^{2l})^{\epsilon}}\\
&\ +\text{cyclic}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\cdots,a_{A})\,.\end{split}

(C.8)

where we have defined

(C.9)

The arguments shifted by

We highlight that to the best of our knowledge, this is the first time in the literature the series (

C.7

Since the summand is symmetric in

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\frac{{}_{A+4}V_{A+3}^{(k+1)}(\vec{\mathpzc{a}}_{\alpha};p;q;p^{2\epsilon})}{{}_{A+4}V_{A+3}^{(k)}(\vec{\mathpzc{a}}_{\alpha};p;q;p^{2\epsilon})}\right|=&\,|p|^{1+2\epsilon+k(A-4)}|a_{\alpha}|^{A-4}|p/q|^{(A-4)/2}\\

(C.10)

where we made use of the expression in (
C.7
).
To make an estimate for this ratio, we need to specify an appropriate scaling for the absolute values of the fugacities
a_{\beta}
,
p
and
q
.
The complication we mentioned above is seen here explicitly: as compared with the basic hypergeometric case, the theta function, defined as
\theta_{q}(x)=(x,qx^{-1};q)_{\infty}
, depends on both positive and negative powers of its argument.
This necessitates a choice of the scaling of the parameters as an input to the convergence analysis, rather than an output.

Due to the balancing condition, any consistent scaling is necessarily
A
dependent.
This
A
-dependence affects the details of the scaling estimate for the above ratio.
To provide an example of an integral that is convergent, we set
A=6
, and scale the fugacities as
|a_{\alpha}|=T
, and

This case corresponds to the index of the
\mathcal{N}=1

Taking careful account of negative powers of

\left|\frac{{}_{10}V_{9}^{(k+1)}(\vec{\mathpzc{a}}_{\alpha};p;q;p^{2\epsilon})}{{}_{10}V_{9}^{(k)}(\vec{\mathpzc{a}}_{\alpha};p;q;p^{2\epsilon})}\right|\sim T^{-2+6\epsilon}.

(C.11)

Therefore, the residue sum is convergent if we use the reduced measure (
\epsilon=1
), but, not if we use the full measure (
\epsilon=0
).
As in the basic hypergeometric case, the convergence of the residue sum implies a vanishing contribution from the origin.
More precisely, the increased suppression of residues at larger

of radius

.
Since this integral provides an upper bound on the contribution of the origin, we see that it vanishes with the reduced measure.

The cases
A=8,10,12
correspond to the superconformal indices of

(see Section
2.4

With a symmetric scaling for the

and the scaling of

fixed by the balancing condition, the ratio test (

C.10
) is
T^{8\epsilon}
for
A=8
, and

for

. Note in particular that this demonstrates that the residue sum (
C.8
) diverges for

if one chooses a symmetric scaling for the

The

\mathcal{N}=2

(see Section

).
For this case, it can be checked for each

as in Section

In fact, we find that the ratio of subsequent terms in

.
This demonstrates the sensitivity of the convergence of residue sums to the precise scaling of the parameters.
A full analysis of convergence is warranted, but beyond the scope of this work.

C.3
Generalized integrals: a formal evaluation

We have dealt above with elliptic hypergeometric integrals that appear for the superconformal indices of
SU(2)

When considering an

gauge group, the superconformal indices are expressed as
N-1

2.2
).
Focusing on one integral at a time, say the integral over the

where we assume that (the

We now evaluate the integral corresponding to this more general

(C.18)

Note that we do not assume any balancing condition for this general integral. The integral is generally not well-poised; it becomes well-poised if we set

\alpha

We provide below a formal evaluation of this integral as a sum over residues, without caring about convergence.
In some physical theories, choosing an appropriate scaling of the parameters will lead to convergent residue sum even for these more general integrals, provided one uses the reduced measure (see Section

).

Let us denote by

For example, for the

(C.19)

where we think of

as

in this appendix.
Assume that
\mathcal{P}(x)
has only simple poles and that the absolute values of the fugacities satisfy the conditions
|p|=|q|<1
, and
|a_{\alpha}|,|b_{\alpha}|<1
, for all
\alpha
.
Consider the contour integral

I=(p;p)_{\infty}(q;q)_{\infty}\oint\frac{dx}{2\pi ix}\mu_{\text{red}}^{\epsilon}(x)\mathcal{P}(x),

(C.20)

where, as above,
\epsilon=0

We can then express (

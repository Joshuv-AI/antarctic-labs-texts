Stability of Large-Amplitude Viscous Shock Under Periodic Perturbation for 1-d Viscoelasticity with Non-Convex Constitutive Relations Thanks: *Corresponding Author
Yu Mei, Peng Yuan*

Abstract
. This paper investigates the large-time behavior of the viscous shock profile for the one-dimensional system of viscoelasticity, subject to initial perturbations that approach space-periodic functions at far fields. We specifically address the case with non-convex constitutive stress relations and non-degenerate Lax’s shock. Under the assumptions of suitably small initial perturbations satisfying a zero-mass type condition, we prove that the solution of the system converges to a viscous shock profile with a shift, which is partially determined by the space-periodic perturbation. Notably, our result imposes no amplitude restrictions on the viscous shock waves. This work extends the result of Kawashima-Matsumura ( Commun. Pure Appl. Math. 47 , 1994) by simultaneously handling both large-amplitude shocks and space-periodic perturbations, while also generalizing the result of Huang-Yuan ( Commun. Math. Phys. 387 , 2021) by allowing for a non-convex constitutive relation. The key ingredient of proof is decomposing the large-amplitude shock wave into small-amplitude shocks and, for each, introducing suitable transform and weight functions to counteract the adverse effects of non-convex constitutive relations encountered during weighted energy estimates on the system in effective velocity and deformation gradient variables.

Now let us introduce the strategies in the proof. As in many previous works, the general strategy consists of the two main steps:

1.

Construct a suitable ansatz
(\tilde{v},\tilde{u})
with the desired asymptotic behavior, and derive the system of ansatz with some decay residual terms.

2.

Take the difference between the original system and ansatz system
(v-\tilde{v},u-\tilde{u})
, and show the global solutions in terms of anti-derivative variables with large time behavior
(v-\tilde{v},u-\tilde{u})\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
for small perturbed initial data.

In step 1, we can construct the ansatz
(\tilde{v},\tilde{u})
, by the same way as in Huang and Yuan
[
7
]
for compressible Navier-Stokes equations with convex pressure, to carry the same oscillations of the solution
(v,u)
at the far fields, and try to use the effective velocity
m:=u-\mu v
to deal with the large amplitude. However, in step 2, when the constitutive relation is non-convex like (
1.3
), the energy estimates of the anti-derivative variable of the deformation gradient and the effective velocity will produce a bad term involving
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(v)
with a change sign near
v=0
, which can not be controlled. This difficulty was overcame by Kawashima and Matsumura
[
17
]
for the small amplitude shock without periodic perturbation case by the weighted energy method and Matsumura and Mei
[
25
]
for the shock satisfying a stronger condition than Lax’s shock (
1.12
) by the weighted energy method applied on a new system after a transformation. Motivated by
[
25
]
and
[
17
]
,
we also apply the method of transformed and weighted energy estimates to the equations of deformation gradient
v
and the effective velocity
m
. However, in order to remove the constraint on shock speed
s
in
[
25
]
, it is subtle to construct the transform function and weight function. In fact, the condition in
[
25
]
requires some smallness of shock amplitude. Our idea is that we decompose the large-amplitude shock satisfying (
1.12
) into small-amplitude shocks, and
design two groups of transform functions and weight functions for each small-amplitude shocks so that the method of transformed and weighted energy estimates is available. As a cost, our weighted and transform functions are no longer
C^{1}
, but only piecewise smooth functions. Therefore,
our construction of weight and transform functions have to ensure that the jump of flux in the process of
energy estimate should be non-positive on each non-smooth point. In section 3.1.2 below, we construct the weighted and transform functions separately in
[\overline{v}_{l},0]
and
[0,\overline{v}_{r}]
, and extend them a little bit to
[\overline{v}_{l},v_{d}]
and
[v_{-1},\overline{v}_{r}]
, and divide the energy estimates on
\mathbb{R}
into two parts with

in
[\overline{v}_{l},0]
and
[0,\overline{v}_{r}]
. By performing the weighted energy method to the transformed system (
3.18
) in terms of anti-derivative variables of
(v,m)
for viscous shock

and

2.3.
Main Result

With the ansatz constructed above, we define the anti-derivative of the difference

.

with the constant shift

\displaystyle(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{u})(\xi,t):=(v,u)(\xi+st,t),\quad(\tilde{\mathbf{v}},\tilde{\mathbf{u}})(\xi,t)=(\tilde{v},\tilde{u})(\xi+st,t).

Define the anti-derivative variables of perturbation

The following lemma gives the lower order estimates of solution

Lemma 3.3

Under the assumptions of Proposition

\displaystyle\mathop{sup}\limits_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T}]}\left\lVert(\Phi,W)\right\rVert^{2}(t)+\int_{0}^{\mathcal{T}}\left\lVert\partial_{\xi}\Phi\right\rVert^{2}(t)\,dt\leq C(\left\lVert(\Phi_{0},W_{0})\right\rVert^{2}+\varepsilon),

The proof of lemma

should not follow the classical energy method as in
[

did for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations

\frac{s\sigma^{\prime\prime}(v^{S}_{\mathcal{X}})(v^{S}_{\mathcal{X}})^{\prime}}{\sigma^{\prime}(v^{S}_{\mathcal{X}})}W^{2}

by weighted energy estimate method for the stability of the viscous shock with a small amplitude to the system (

)-(

When
s

]

s^{2}<\sigma^{\prime}(\overline{v}_{l})+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\overline{v}_{l})[2(v^{*}-\overline{v}_{r})+\overline{v}_{l}-\overline{v}_{r}],

[

proved the stability of the viscous shock profile with non-convex relation of the opposite sign to (

) and (
1.4
)by introducing a transform function

and using weighted energy estimates for the transformed system. However, a transform function

introduced in
[

]
is not adaptable for the Lax’s shock condition (

\sigma(v)
with the sign (

)-(

To show our idea more precisely, let us briefly introduce the idea of Matsumura and Mei in
[

]
. Under the shock speed condition (

), they constructed the transform function

and weighted one

, which are both

to obtain the following weighted energy identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left\{(w\sigma^{\prime})(v^{S})\mathbf{\Phi}^{2}+w(v^{S})\mathbf{\Psi}^{2}\right\}-\partial_{\xi}\left\{\dots\right\}+\mu w(v^{S})\partial_{\xi}\mathbf{\Psi}^{2}

(3.14)

\displaystyle+\frac{|\partial_{\xi}v^{S}|}{2s}w(v^{S})Y(v^{S})(s\mathbf{\Phi}+\mathbf{\Psi})^{2}+\frac{|\partial_{\xi}v^{S}|}{s}w(v^{S})Z(v^{S})\mathbf{\Psi}^{2}=\cdots,

and

Inspired by
[

and

and an extra term

in (

(3.16)

To achieve this, we have to construct new transform and weighted functions. However, it seems difficult to construct

transform and weighted functions in the whole interval
[\bar{v}_{l},\bar{v}_{r}]
for the system (
3.9
) with non-degenerate shock in (

and no restriction of the shock speed

could be very large and out of control. Our strategy is to divide the interval

into finite sub-intervals and construct transform and weighted functions in each intervals so that

as

MLoRQ: Bridging Low-Rank and Quantization for Transformer Compression
Ofir Gordon, Ariel Lapid, Elad Cohen, Yarden Yagil, Arnon Netzer, Hai Victor Habi

Abstract
Deploying transformer-based neural networks on resource-constrained edge devices presents a significant challenge. This challenge is often addressed through various techniques, such as low-rank approximation and mixed-precision quantization. In this work, we introduce Mixed Low-Rank and Quantization (MLoRQ), a novel method that integrates both techniques. MLoRQ employs a two-stage optimization process to determine optimal bit-width and rank assignments for each layer, adhering to predefined memory constraints. This process includes: (i) an intra-layer optimization that identifies potentially optimal compression solutions out of all low-rank and quantization combinations; (ii) an inter-layer optimization that assigns bit-width precision and rank to each layer while ensuring the memory constraint is met. An optional final step applies a sequential optimization process using a modified adaptive rounding technique to mitigate compression-induced errors in joint low-rank approximation and quantization. The method is compatible and can be seamlessly integrated with most existing quantization algorithms. MLoRQ shows state-of-the-art results with up to 15% performance improvement, evaluated on Vision Transformers for image classification, object detection, and instance segmentation tasks.

In this work, we propose a compression framework, targeted mainly for transformer-based models, named MLoRQ, which applies joint
Mixed Low-Rank and Quantization optimization.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt of
joint
rank and quantization optimization.
We present a combined representation of these two techniques for a fully-connected layer.
Based on this representation, we introduce the mixed rank and precision problem, aiming to determine the smallest possible decrease in performance while adhering to memory constraints.
This presents a significant challenge, as the search space is considerably larger than when considering mixed precision or low-rank individually.

We address this challenge by proposing a two-stage algorithm.
First, an intra-layer search identifies a Pareto frontier of compression candidates, balancing compression and accuracy while significantly reducing the search space.
To further guide the optimization toward better solutions, we leverage Hessian knowledge, using the Label-Free Hessian approach
[
14
]
.
Then, an inter-layer optimization assigns bit-width and rank to each layer, selecting only from the filtered set of candidates obtained in the previous stage.
MLoRQ can be seamlessly integrated with most existing quantization schemes, enhancing them by incorporating both quantization and low-rank compression options.
Specifically, here we utilize the activation quantization enhancement from
[
26
,
57
]
with MLoRQ.
To further mitigate compression-induced error, we suggest an optional step of a modified adaptive rounding procedure
[
32
]
that considers both quantization and low-rank to refine the weight quantization rounding.

We conducted a series of numerical experiments to show the benefits of MLoRQ in computer vision—for the Image-Net classification task
[
40
]
and COCO
[
27
]
object detection task—as well as in Natural Language Processing (NLP) on the General Language Understanding Evaluation (GLUE) benchmark
[
46
]
.
MLoRQ outperforms existing PTQ techniques by strategically merging quantization and low-rank approximation, which is particularly beneficial at high compression rates.
For example, we show an improvement of up to 15% in accuracy when compressing the ViT-B model to less than 12.5% of its original size.
In NLP, we achieve an improvement of up to 7% when compressing BERT model
[
5
]
weights.
Finally, we conducted a series of ablation studies to thoroughly analyze the contributions and benefits of each component in the proposed method.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:

1.

We introduce MLoRQ: a Mixed Low-Rank and Quantization approach enabling a notable reduction in the size and computational complexity of transformer-based DNNs.

2.

We propose a modified adaptive rounding technique for combined compression that includes both quantization and low-rank approximation.

3.

We present results for computer vision, including ImageNet classification and COCO object detection, where MLoRQ achieves state-of-the-art performance, demonstrating a notable improvement in accuracy over previous methods.

2.1
Low Rank Approximation

Low-rank approximation
[
41
]
and tensor decomposition
[
13
,
34
]
are powerful techniques for reducing neural network size and computational cost.
In this study, we focus on compressing transformer-based neural networks, which primarily consist of fully-connected layers and
1\times 1
convolutions.
This structure makes matrix low-rank approximation via singular value decomposition (SVD) a suitable choice for our approach.

Specifically, let
d_{\bm{y}},d_{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\bm{y}}\times d_{\bm{x}}}
represent a matrix for which we aim to reduce to rank
r\in\mathbb{N}/\{0\}
;
its SVD and low-rank approximation form is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{T}\approx\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}=\mathbf{U}^{\left(r\right)}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\left(r\right)}\left(\mathbf{V}^{\left(r\right)}\right)^{T}

(1)

where
\mathbf{U}
,
\mathbf{V}
are unitary matrices, and
\mathbf{\Sigma}
is a rectangular diagonal matrix containing the non-negative singular values of
\mathbf{W}
.
The matrices
\mathbf{U}^{\left(r\right)}
,
\mathbf{V}^{\left(r\right)}
, and
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\left(r\right)}
are reduced-rank versions of
\mathbf{U}
,
\mathbf{V}
and
\mathbf{\Sigma}
, respectively.

It has been demonstrated
[
18
,
47
]
that this approach does not necessarily yield optimal neural network accuracy.
To address this, various methods have introduced additional weighted elements into the SVD.
In the remainder of this section, we discuss weighted decomposition with a weighting factor
\mathbf{T}
, where, for a given rank
r
, we aim to minimize the following objective:

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\mathbf{A}^{(r)},\mathbf{B}^{(r)}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathbf{T}\left(\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{A}^{(r)}\mathbf{B}^{(r)}\right)\right\rVert},

(2)

where
\mathbf{A}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\bm{x}}\times r}\text{ and }\mathbf{B}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d_{\bm{y}}}
are the low-rank representation of the decomposed matrices of
\mathbf{W}
.

Hsu et al. 2022
and
Srebro and Jaakkola 2003
propose a solution to objective (
2
) by employing SVD.
Initially, SVD is performed on
\mathbf{T}\mathbf{W}
, yielding
\mathbf{U},\mathbf{\Sigma},\mathbf{V}=svd\left(\mathbf{T}\mathbf{W}\right)
, where
svd\left(\mathbf{M}\right)
represents the singular value decomposition of matrix
\mathbf{M}
. The resulting low-rank factorization is then provided by

\mathbf{A}^{(r)}=\mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{U}^{(r)}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{(r)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{B}^{(r)}=\left(\mathbf{V}^{(r)}\right)^{T}.

2.2
Quantization

Quantization is central to neural network compression, with methods including log-quantization, power-of-two, uniform, symmetric, and more
[
12
]
.
In our work, we use a uniform quantizer, well supported by hardware.
Let
\mathbf{W}
be a weight tensor to be quantized, with bit-width
b
, scaling parameter
s
, and zero point
z
.
The quantized weight is given by

\mathrm{Q}\left(\mathbf{W},\phi,b\right)\triangleq s\left(\mathrm{clip}\left(\left\lfloor\tfrac{\mathbf{W}}{s}\right\rceil+z,0,2^{b}-1\right)-z\right),

where
\phi=(s,z)
denote the quantization parameters.
Various approaches have been suggested for choosing the scaling parameters
s
and the zero point
z
, including mean square error (MSE)
[
33
]
, min-max
[
24
]
, and Hessian-MSE
[
14
]
.
Recent studies introduced an additional optimization step to minimize rounding errors in weights quantization, specifically by choosing whether to round up or down.
The majority of methods employ the soft-quantizer
proposed by
Nagel et al. 2020
:

\displaystyle Q_{S}\left(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{V},\phi,b\right)\triangleq

\displaystyle s\left(\mathrm{clip}\left(\left\lfloor\tfrac{\mathbf{W}}{s}\right\rfloor+h\left(\mathbf{V}\right)+z,0,2^{b}-1\right)-z\right),

where
\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\bm{y}}\times d_{\bm{x}}}
is an auxiliary variable used in the rounding optimization,
h\left(x\right)\triangleq\mathrm{clip}\left(\sigma\left(x\right)\left(\xi-\gamma\right)+\gamma,0,1\right)
is an element-wise function that ensures values remain between zero and one. Here,
\sigma
represents the sigmoid function, and
\xi
and
\gamma
are stretch parameters with values
\xi=1.1
and
\gamma=-0.1
. To facilitate the convergence of
h\left(\mathbf{V}\right)
to zero or one, a regularization term
\Omega\left(\mathbf{V}\right)\triangleq\sum_{i,j}1-\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 2h\left(\mathbf{V}\right)-1\right\rvert}^{\beta}
is added into the objective loss using an annealing factor.

4
Mixed Low-Rank and Quantization

We derive the Mixed Low-Rank and Quantization (MLoRQ) method to solve the optimization problem stated in
1
.
To address the large candidate space (as demonstrated in Table
6
in Appendix
D
), we split the search into two stages:

(i) Intra-layer

filtering using Pareto frontier optimization to find the set of potentially optimal candidates and drastically reduce the search space.

(ii) Inter-layer

search that assigns bit-widths and ranks for a unified compression solution.
After assigning a bit-width and rank to each layer, we include an optional stage that performs low-rank aware adaptive rounding, optimizing the weight quantization rounding values
[
32
]
.
In addition, we apply one of two activation quantization methods RepQ
[
26
]
or ERQ
[
57
]
, to correct quantization errors.
We wish to highlight that MLoRQ can complement nearly any quantization method. The complete algorithm flow is depicted in Figure
1
and detailed in Algorithm
1

C

To motivate the MLoRQ approach, we investigate the second-order Taylor approximation of the task loss
[
8
,
25
]

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}=\bm{g}^{T}\Delta\bm{w}+\Delta\bm{w}^{T}\mathbf{H}_{\bm{w}}\Delta\bm{w}+\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta\bm{w}^{3}\right),

(7)

where
\Delta\mathcal{L}\triangleq\mathcal{L}\left(\bm{w}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(\bm{w}+\Delta\bm{w}\right)
is the change in task loss due to compression,
\Delta\bm{w}
is the weights perturbation,
\bm{g}\triangleq\nabla_{\bm{w}}\mathcal{L}\left(\bm{w}\right)
is the gradient of the task loss w.r.t. the weight vector
\bm{w}
and
\mathbf{H}_{\bm{w}}\triangleq\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}\left(\bm{w}\right)}{\partial\bm{w}\partial\bm{w}^{T}}
is the Hessian matrix of the task loss.
Under the assumption that
\bm{g}\approx 0
as in
[
32
,
25
,
14
]
, we get the following:

\Delta\mathcal{L}\approx\Delta\bm{w}^{T}\mathbf{H}_{\bm{w}}\Delta\bm{w}
.
Using the assumption that the layers’ contribution to the error induced by compression is independent as in
[
32
,
9
]
, we have:

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}\approx{\color[rgb]{0.6875,0.1914,0.1523}\overbrace{\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\color[rgb]{0.38,0.51,0.71}{\underbrace{\Delta\bm{w}^{T}_{\ell}\mathbf{H}_{\bm{w}_{\ell}}\Delta\bm{w}_{\ell}}_{\text{Intra Layer}}}}^{\text{Inter Layer}}},

(8)

where
\Delta\mathbf{W}_{\ell}=\mathbf{W}_{\ell}-\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}
is the weight perturbation in matrix form,
\Delta\bm{w}_{\ell}=\mathrm{Vec}\left(\Delta\mathbf{W}_{\ell}\right)
and
\mathbf{H}_{\bm{w}_{\ell}}
are the weights perturbation and weights Hessian of the
\ell^{th}
layer and
\mathrm{Vec}\left(\mathbf{M}\right)
is the vectorization of a matrix
\mathbf{M}
.
Equation

illustrates the connection between weight perturbations caused by compression and task loss that we aim to reduce, thus forming the proposed solution to improve accuracy. According to (
8

Subsequently, in the latter stage, the objective shifts to minimizing the global error, which we refer to as the inter-layer stage.
To obtain the Hessian matrix without labels, we utilize the Label-Free Hessian from

]

4.1
Intra-Layer Search

In this section, we perform the intra-layer search to determine a set of candidate solutions for the
\ell^{th}
layer.
This is achieved by minimizing the inner term in (
8
), under the added assumption that Hessian
\mathbf{H}_{\bm{w}_{\ell}}
is a diagonal matrix.
This results in the subsequent optimization problem:

\min\limits_{\mathbf{A}_{\ell}^{(r)},\mathbf{B}_{\ell}^{(r)},{\phi}_{\mathbf{A}_{\ell}},{\phi}_{\mathbf{B}_{\ell}}}\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)\quad\forall b_{\ell}^{\mathbf{A}},b_{\ell}^{\mathbf{B}},r_{\ell}

(9)

where
\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)\triangleq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathbf{D}_{\ell}\odot\left(\mathbf{W}_{\ell}-\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}
is the local loss,
\mathbf{D}_{\ell}=\sqrt{\left[{\mathbf{H}_{\bm{w}_{\ell}}}\right]_{i+n\cdot j,i+n\cdot j}}
represents the square root of the Hessian matrix’s diagonal component for
\mathbf{W}_{\ell}
,
and
\mathbf{M}_{1}\odot\mathbf{M}_{2}
denotes the Hadamard product (element-wise multiplication) between matrices
\mathbf{M}_{1}
and
\mathbf{M}_{2}
.
We solve problem (
9
) using a sequential method, starting with the low-rank decomposition, then selecting the quantization parameters, and ending with a search that provides us with all the potentially optimal solutions.

4.1.1
Hessian-aware SVD

We propose a Hessian-aware SVD applied to each layer’s weight matrix
\mathbf{W}_{\ell}
.
Specifically, we solve the problem (
9
) without quantization for all ranks
r\in\mathcal{R}_{\ell}
, resulting in

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\mathbf{A}_{\ell}^{(r)},\mathbf{B}_{\ell}^{(r)}}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\mathbf{A}_{\ell}^{(r)}\mathbf{B}_{\ell}^{(r)}\right)\quad\forall r\in\mathcal{R}_{\ell},

(10)

A closed-form solution to (
10
) is generally not available, as noted in
[
41
]
.
Thus, to address problem (
10
), we suggest minimizing an upper bound, which is given by

\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\mathbf{A}_{\ell}^{(r)}\mathbf{B}_{\ell}^{(r)}\right)\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\ell}-\mathbf{A}_{\ell}^{(r)}\mathbf{B}_{\ell}^{(r)}\right)\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}
,
where
\left[{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}\right]_{i,i}=\sum_{j}\left[{\mathbf{D}_{\ell}}\right]_{i,j}
.
We start by applying an SVD on
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}\mathbf{W}_{\ell}
to obtain
\mathbf{U}_{\ell},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\ell},\mathbf{V}_{\ell}=svd\left(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}\mathbf{W}_{\ell}\right)
.
Next, we combine the results of the Hessian-weighted SVD into two matrices. Given that weights are quantized using a scale per output channel, scaling individual output channels of the matrix does not affect quantization noise because the scaling is incorporated into the quantization scale parameter.
Consequently, we select low-rank matrices so that the scaling can integrate into the output channel scale parameter without introducing additional noise, as given by

\mathbf{A}_{\ell}=\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}^{-1}\mathbf{U}_{\ell}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{B}_{\ell}=\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\ell}\mathbf{V}_{\ell}^{T}.

(11)

Given

\min\limits_{{\phi}_{\mathbf{A}_{\ell}},{\phi}_{\mathbf{B}_{\ell}}}\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)\quad\forall b_{\ell}^{\mathbf{A}},b_{\ell}^{\mathbf{B}}\in\mathcal{B},

(12)

where

\hat{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}=Q\left(\mathbf{A}_{\ell},{\phi}_{\mathbf{A}_{\ell}},b_{\ell}^{\mathbf{A}}\right)Q\left(\mathbf{B}_{\ell},{\phi}_{\mathbf{B}_{\ell}},b_{\ell}^{\mathbf{B}}\right)

is the quantized SVD without rank reduction.
This means that we determine the quantization parameters once for all ranks.
Given the large number of candidates, we propose solving (
12
) separately for
\mathbf{A}_{\ell}
and
\mathbf{B}_{\ell}
.
For
\mathbf{A}_{\ell}
, we solve the following problem:

{\phi}_{\mathbf{A}_{\ell}}\left(b\right)=\arg\min\limits_{{\phi}}\Lambda_{\ell}\left(Q\left(\mathbf{A}_{\ell},{\phi},b\right)\mathbf{B}_{\ell}\right).

(13)

As for
\mathbf{B}_{\ell}
, we reduce the search complexity using the bound
\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\mathbf{A}_{\ell}Q\left(\mathbf{B}_{\ell},{\phi},b\right)\right)\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\mathbf{B}_{\ell}-Q\left(\mathbf{B}_{\ell},{\phi},b\right)}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}.

This results in the following minimization problem:

{\phi}_{\mathbf{B}_{\ell}}\left(b\right)=\arg\min\limits_{{\phi}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert{\mathbf{B}_{\ell}-Q\left(\mathbf{B}_{\ell},{\phi},b\right)}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}.

(14)

The minimization of
(
13
) and
(
14
) is performed on a fixed grid of points using a line search.

4.1.3
Pareto Set Search

In the final step of this phase, we extract only relevant potentially optimal solutions for the
\ell^{th}
layer
by identifying the set of Pareto optimal solutions.

Specifically, let
\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)
denote the loss (
9
),
and
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)
represents the memory footprint of the compressed weight, given by

\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)\triangleq\begin{cases}r_{\ell}\left(d_{{\ell}}^{\bm{y}}\cdot b_{\ell}^{\mathbf{A}}+d_{{\ell}}^{\bm{x}}\cdot b_{\ell}^{\mathbf{B}}\right)&\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{W}_{\ell}^{LQ}\\
d_{{\ell}}^{\bm{y}}\cdot d_{{\ell}}^{\bm{x}}\cdot b_{\mathbf{W}_{\ell}}&\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{W}_{\ell}^{Q}.\end{cases}

(15)

Let us define that a compression option

\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}^{\prime}

dominates
(
\succ
) another option
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}
if both its local error and memory footprint are better and at least one of them is strictly better.

Then, a compression option is
Pareto optimal
if no other compression option dominates it.
Formally, the set of Pareto optimal solutions is denoted by
{\mathcal{P}_{\ell}=\mathinner{\left\{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{W}_{\ell}|\nexists\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}^{\prime}\succ\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right\}}}
.
After obtaining
\Lambda_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
for all ranks and bit-widths, we select a set of candidate solutions that exist on the Pareto frontier as illustrated in the Pareto search section image in Figure
1
, and in Appendix
H
.

4.2
Inter Layer Search

After acquiring the compression candidates
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}
of each layer, We search for a mixed rank and quantization solution
\mathcal{S}
that assigns an optimal compression setup to each layer, minimizing the total error in the compressed model.
While Hessian-based metrics have been widely used for global optimization
[
8
,
9
,
55
]
, recent studies suggest instead to maximize the model’s signal-to-quantization noise ratio (SQNR)
[
35
,
21
]
for this global optimization search.
Our ablation study in Section
6.4
also demonstrates the superiority of this approach.
Thus, we formulate the problem as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=

\displaystyle\argmax\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{L}}\\
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\hskip 8.19447pt\forall\ell\end{subarray}}\sum_{\ell}\Psi_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)

(16)

s.t.:

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)\leq\psi_{WMS},

where

\Psi_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)\triangleq\tfrac{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\bm{f}_{\ell}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\ell}\right)\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\bm{f}_{\ell}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\ell}\right)-\bm{f}_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}}

is the SQNR of the
\ell^{th}
layer perturbation,
\bm{f}_{\ell}
is the output of a neural network as a function of the
\ell^{th}
weight tensor, while the rest of the model is in floating point.
For the cases where the set of
b_{\mathbf{A}_{\ell}}
and
b_{\mathbf{B}_{\ell}}
options have a large number of candidates for different ranks, we apply a linear interpolation instead of running a full model inference, to reduce the computational cost (see details in Appendix
E
).
We translate the problem in (
16
) into Integer Linear Programming (ILP) and use an off-the-shelf
[
36
]
algorithm as detailed in the Appendix
C.1
.

5
Low-Rank Aware Adaptive Rounding

In the concluding phase, we first apply activation quantization correction, such as reparameterization and log-two-sqrt quantization correction
[
26
]
.
Furthermore, activation quantization correction is performed using Ridge regression from ERQ
[
57
]
. We wish to highlight that MLoRQ can complement nearly any quantization method.

Given the structure of compressed weights, as a result of the combination of low-rank and quantization, we propose a modified version of Adaptive Rounding
[
32
]
, referred to as
LoRAda
, tailored for scenarios involving low-rank and quantization considerations.
The procedure encompasses a sequence of optimizations in which, for each layer, we aim to minimize both the error caused by weight perturbations and the propagation errors throughout the model.
Suppose
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\bm{y}}\times r}
and
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d_{\bm{x}}}
represent the low-rank decomposition of a certain layer whose original weight is denoted by
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\bm{y}}\times d_{\bm{x}}}
.
We define two continuous variables,
\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\bm{y}}\times r}
and
\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{B}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d_{\bm{x}}}
, which are optimized as part of the sequence-mean mechanism given by

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{A}},\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{B}}}

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\bm{x}-\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}\bm{x}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}+\lambda\left(\Omega\left(\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{A}}\right)+\Omega\left(\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{B}}\right)\right),

where
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}=Q_{S}\left(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{A}},{\phi}_{\mathbf{A}},b_{\mathbf{A}}\right)
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}=Q_{S}\left(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{B}},{\phi}_{\mathbf{B}},b_{\mathbf{B}}\right)
are the soft-quantized low-rank decomposition matrices.

7
Conclusions

In this study, we tackle joint low-rank approximation and mixed precision quantization by introducing MLoRQ, a novel algorithm that assigns rank and bit-width per layer.
MLoRQ comprises two stages:
(i) intra-layer candidates reduction via Pareto optimization;
(ii) inter-layer rank and bit-width allocation.
The process is followed by an adaptive rounding refinement for mixed representations.
It integrates easily into existing quantization schemes, unifying low-rank and quantization strategies.
Experiments in vision tasks show notable improvement over state-of-the-art.
This work highlights the potential of combining quantization and low-rank approximation across tasks, with future directions including:
(i) extending MLoRQ to large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) with high memory demands, and (ii) exploring additional compression techniques like pruning and one-bit quantization.

Appendix A
Related Works

A.1
Post-training Quantization for Transformers

Neural network post-training quantization (PTQ) emerged as one of the most popular approaches for reducing model memory usage and computational complexity.
Many works from the last several years explored the approach of applying a short fine-tuning procedure to learn an adaptive rounding policy to improve PTQ performance, mainly for CNNs
[
32
,
25
,
48
,
14
]
.
While performing well on convolutional neural network (CNN) models, these methods do not achieve comparable performance on transformer architectures due to their unique structure and different bottlenecks.
Consequently, recent works have attempted to design dedicated PTQ methods for transformers
[
31
,
11
,
56
,
6
,
29
,
49
,
15
]
.
The main focus of these works was addressing the difficulties of quantizing the feature maps of Transformers, which tend to present massive outliers, making quantization a challenging task
[
1
,
51
]
.
An important work in this direction, RepQ-ViT
[
26
]
, introduced a scale-reparameterized quantizer for post-LayerNorm and implemented a Log
\sqrt{2}
quantizer to handle post-softmax activations quantization that mitigates outlier effects, leading to improved quantization robustness in Transformers.
ERQ
[
57
]
extended this work and introduced a two-step PTQ approach to mitigate both the activation and the weight quantization error.
Another approach, explored in several works, looks for a mixed-precision quantization solution that optimizes the trade-off between model compression and accuracy
[
9
,
52
,
38
]
. These methods selectively assign lower bit widths to layers less sensitive to quantization.

A.2
Low-rank Approximation for Model Compression

Another method for reducing neural networks’ memory and computational footprint is low-rank approximation, which decomposes the model’s weight matrices into lower-rank representations.
Recently, this approach has attracted significant attention for transformer architectures due to their heavy reliance on large matrix multiplications
[
39
,
54
,
53
]
.
FWSVD
[
18
]
and TFWSVD
[
19
]
investigated various weighting strategies to enhance SVD decomposition for LR compression of Transformer-based language models.
RankDyna
[
20
]
introduced an iterative approach that dynamically assigns ranks based on the importance of the parameters, eliminating the need for a well-trained task-specific model.
Other works explored the benefits of LR primarily for large language model compression (LLM) and introduced different enhancements for these specific architectures
[
20
,
47
]
.

Appendix C
Implementation Details

All experiments in Section
6
are performed in the following manner: first, we compute the Label-Free Hessian for all weights as shown in
[
14
]
with 100 iterations and a batch size of 32 samples.
In the following stage, we compute the decomposed matrices using Hessian-weighted SVD.
For the quantization parameter selection, we conduct a detailed outlier search for each output channel of the weight using a percentile-based approach
[
57
]
.
We evaluate the percentile values at
0.97,0.98,0.99,0.995,0.9995,

0.9997,0.9999,0.99995,0.99999,1
and select the value that yields the best metrics in (
13
) and (
14
).
Moreover, in the case of quantization-only (without low-rank), we apply the same procedure with the Hessian MSE presented in
[
14
]
, which is given by

{\phi}_{\mathbf{W}_{\ell}}\left(b\right)=\arg\min\limits_{{\phi}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathbf{D}_{\ell}\odot\left(\mathbf{W}_{\ell}-Q\left(\mathbf{W}_{\ell},{\phi},b\right)\right)\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}.

(17)

The parameter search is applied to every bit-width in the options set
\mathcal{B}
.
Then, we compute the local loss (
9
) and the local memory constraint (
15
), which is used in the Pareto optimal solution search as in
[
9
]
.
In the Inter-Layer Search stage, we wisely determine the bit-width and rank for every layer.
Next, to obtain the weight bit-width and rank, we apply a Integer-Linear Programming (ILP) optimization, which is detailed in Appendix
C.1
.

During the final stage, we implement activation quantization correction using layer normalization parameterization correction and a logarithmic
\sqrt{2}
-quantizer, as outlined in
[
26
]
.
When integrating with ERQ, a Ridge Regression is introduced to further minimize the activation quantization error by adjusting the weights, according to
[
57
]
.
Following the weighted adjustments for activation quantization, the Hessian, SVD, and quantization parameters are recomputed.
Subsequently, sequential adaptive rounding optimization is employed to minimize the quantization rounding error by choosing whether to round up or down.
We execute
20k
gradient updates per layer using Adam with a learning rate of 0.3 (for ViT-S, the learning rate is 0.01) and a regularization factor of 0.3.
All experiments were performed on an Nvidia A6000 GPU with a batch size of 32 images.
The runtime of the algorithm to compress the ViT-S model is approximately one and a half hours.

Appendix E
Inter-Intra Layer Metric Interpolation

Calculating the error directly using (
16
) for every configuration and layer poses a significant computational challenge, as each calculation requires independent inference on the entire calibration dataset, which can be costly in cases where the set
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}
is very large.

To reduce the computational cost in these cases, we suggest approximating the error by linear interpolation to overcome this limitation. This significantly reduces computational effort while maintaining accuracy.

In particular, we establish a computational budget of
k_{inf}
metric evaluations for each bit-width pair
(b_{\ell}^{\mathbf{A}},b_{\ell}^{\mathbf{B}})
.
First, constrain the set of candidates
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\left(b_{A},b_{B}\right)=\mathinner{\left\{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}|n=b_{A},m=b_{B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}_{\ell}\right\}}

with bit widths
b_{A}
,
b_{B}
for the matrices
\mathbf{A}_{\ell}
,
\mathbf{B}_{\ell}
.
If the size of
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\leq k_{inf}
, compute the metric for each candidate. Alternatively, choose
k_{inf}
so that these sizes are uniformly distributed between
\min\limits_{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\left(b_{A},b_{B}\right)}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)
and
\max\limits_{\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\left(b_{A},b_{B}\right)}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)
, resulting in the set
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}\left(b_{A},b_{B}\right)
. Then the interpolated metric is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi}_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Phi_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)&\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{G}_{\ell}\left(b_{A},b_{B}\right)\\
\Phi_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}^{(l)}\right)\cdot\alpha+\Phi_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}^{(h)}\right)\cdot\beta&\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\notin\mathcal{G}_{\ell}\left(b_{A},b_{B}\right)\end{cases}

where
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}^{(l)},\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}^{(h)}\in\mathcal{G}_{\ell}\left(b_{A},b_{B}\right)
represent the lower and upper compressed weight around the current compressed rank
\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}
, and
\beta
,
\alpha=1-\beta
are the linear interpolation factors which are given by

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{{\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}\right)-\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}^{(l)}\right)}}{{\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}^{(h)}\right)-\Lambda_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{W}}_{\ell}^{(l)}\right)}},

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=1-\beta.

The motivation for this approximation is derived from observing the first-order Taylor series expansion of the change in the output due to weight perturbation:

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\left(\bm{w}_{\ell}\right)-f\left(\bm{w}_{\ell}+\Delta\bm{w}_{\ell}\right)\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}\approx

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Delta{\bm{w}_{\ell}}^{T}\mathbf{J}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}+\mathcal{O},

where
\mathbf{J}\triangleq\frac{\partial\bm{f}\left(\bm{w}_{\ell}\right)}{\partial\bm{w}_{\ell}}
is the neural network Jacobin matrix w.r.t. the weight vector
\bm{w}_{\ell}
. We claim that under a small perturbation of the weight vector
\bm{w}_{\ell}

\left(\text{e.g., }\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Delta\bm{w}_{\ell}\right\rVert}_{F}\leq\epsilon\right)
, and since the Jacobian matrix
\mathbf{J}
is independent of
\Delta\bm{\bm{w}_{\ell}}
we have that

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\left(\bm{\bm{w}_{\ell}}\right)-f\left(\bm{\bm{w}_{\ell}}+\Delta\bm{\bm{w}_{\ell}}\right)\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}\propto\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Delta\bm{\bm{w}_{\ell}}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}.

The interpolation method in (
21
) allows us to significantly reduce the number of required inference passes of the model while obtaining a close approximation to the actual error induced by each rank.
This is supported by empirical evidence as shown in Figure

Appendix I
Sequential vs. Joint Low-Rank and Quantization

To motivate our approach of a combined low-rank and quantization problem and joint optimization, we experimented, attempting to compress a DeiT-B model’s weights to a target size in a sequential manner—first, by applying percentages of the compression using low-rank only and compressing the rest using quantization.
We compared this approach to our joint optimization approach in Figure
9
.
As can be seen, applying the two techniques sequentially does not perform well when required to compress more than 3% of the weights using low-rank, compared to our joint approach, which exploits the benefits from both techniques.
This allows to achieve improved performance, while also removing the necessity of exploring the optimal low-rank percentage of the compression.

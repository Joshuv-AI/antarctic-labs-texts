Igniting cold tumors: reprogramming the tumor immune microenvironment with dual-payload ADCs in combination with PD-L1&4-1BB bispecific antibodies
Authors: Jing Pan, Xiaolong Yuan, Haiyang Yu, Ziqi Liu, Shi Chen, Fang Zhu, Yuhua Shang, Shikai Hong, Xinghua Han, Zhengzhi Zhu, Tengchuan Jin, Yueyin Pan, Shoubing Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe immunologically “cold” microenvironment of triple-negative breast cancer presents a major obstacle to treatment, conferring resistance to current immunotherapies. Targeting the stimulator of interferon genes (STING) pathway has emerged as a promising strategy to enhance immunotherapy efficacy.MethodsIn this preclinical study, we leveraged a novel integrated approach by employing a B7-H3-targeted dual-payload antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) to induce innate immune responses and reprogram the tumor microenvironment.ResultsOur preliminary results indicated that the dual-payload ADC induces immunogenic cell death, leading to the release of double-stranded DNA and subsequent activation of the cyclic guanosine monophosphate-adenosine monophosphate (GMP-AMP) synthase-STING pathway in macrophages and dendritic cells. This, in turn, promotes dendritic cell maturation, M1 macrophage polarization, and enhanced T-cell infiltration, effectively converting immunologically “cold” tumors into “hot” ones. Most importantly, the synergistic combination of the dual-payload ADC with immune checkpoint inhibitors (PD-L1 and 4–1BB) not only rapidly bridges innate and adaptive immunity but also elicits a robust long-term immune memory response.ConclusionsCollectively, these preliminary results demonstrate that the antitumor efficacy of the dual-payload ADC is mediated through STING pathway activation. This insight opens new avenues for enhancing immunotherapy in B7-H3-expressing solid tumors and paves the way for the clinical translation of next-generation ADC designs.Keywords: Breast Cancer, Antibody-drug conjugates - ADC, Tumor microenvironment - TME, Toll-like receptor - TLR

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

Previous studies have highlighted that dual-payload antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) enhance antitumor efficacy and overcome drug resistance. Studies have investigated dual-payload ADCs targeting distinct antigens and their mechanisms of action. However, research exploring the strategy of combining dual-payload ADCs with bispecific antibodies to activate the stimulator of interferon genes (STING) pathway and improve tumor immunotherapy efficacy remains limited.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

This study provides new insights into the efficacy and mechanisms of dual-payload ADCs in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) immunotherapy. By selectively targeting the immune checkpoint B7-H3 and precisely delivering Dxd and imiquimod into the tumor microenvironment, the dual-payload ADC demonstrated robust innate immune activation and remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment both in vitro and in vivo. Furthermore, activation of the STING pathway, along with the induction of immunogenic cell death and markers of endoplasmic reticulum stress, was observed. These findings highlight the significant potential of combining dual-payload ADCs with bispecific antibodies to enhance durable immune memory responses. This strategy may hold important implications for the immunotherapy of B7-H3-expressing solid tumors, particularly in overcoming mechanisms of immunotherapy resistance.

HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY

The results of this study highlight the importance of exploring novel therapeutic strategies, such as the combination of dual-payload ADCs and bispecific antibodies, in cancer treatment. By enabling the targeted delivery of payloads with distinct mechanisms of action and synergizing with immunomodulatory mechanisms, this combined approach holds the potential to overcome drug resistance and improve the efficacy of tumor immunotherapy. Exploratory research in this field is critical to elucidate the potential of combining dual-payload ADCs with bispecific antibodies in remodeling the tumor immune microenvironment and optimizing their clinical application for TNBC and other solid tumors.

Introduction

Immunotherapy has revolutionized cancer treatment, offering new hope for transforming immunologically “cold” tumors into “hot” ones.1 2 Despite its remarkable therapeutic effects, inadequate activation of the immune system significantly impairs antitumor immunity, leading to compromised therapeutic efficacy, diminished clinical response, and substantial individual variability.3 For instance, the effectiveness of immunotherapy in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) remains to be improved.4 The innate immune system, orchestrating the initial defense against pathogenic challenge, is indispensable for bridging innate recognition to adaptive T cell immunity.5 6 Mounting evidence demonstrates that the cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS)-stimulator of interferon genes (STING) signaling pathway is a cornerstone of innate immunity, making it one of the most promising pathways for eliciting antitumor immunity.7,13 STING pathway activation stimulates TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1) and interferon regulatory factor 3 (IRF3), resulting in the emergence of type I interferons (IFNs) and chemokines, which initiate innate immune responses and subsequently bridge adaptive immunity.14 15 Moreover, STING activation reprograms the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) and synergizes with conventional immune checkpoint blockade to enhance therapeutic efficacy.16 Additionally, STING agonists have been shown to effectively induce antitumor immunity across various cancer types.17 18 Nevertheless, accumulating evidence reveals that the STING pathway is frequently dysfunctional in various cancers, leading to defective immune priming and ultimately compromising the efficacy of immunotherapy.19 Consequently, the development of novel strategies or rational combination regimens to potentiate STING activation holds the potential to revitalize antitumor immune responses and expand clinical efficacy.

Emerging evidence demonstrates that the synergistic integration of TLR-driven P65 activation with the STING pathway provides a compelling rationale for combination cancer immunotherapy.20,23 TLR pathway activation not only stimulates macrophages and dendritic cells (DCs) but also enhances the bridging of innate and adaptive immunity.24 Meanwhile, recent studies have revealed that the topoisomerase I inhibitor exatecan (Dxd), when incorporated into an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC), can induce immunogenic cell death (ICD) and activate innate immunity via the cGAS–STING signaling pathway, thereby modulating the TME.25 26 However, the efficacy of single-payload ADCs is often limited by the immunosuppressive TME and tumor heterogeneity.27,33 Therefore, the precise and coordinated modulation of innate immune activation, particularly when triggered by agonists with diverse mechanisms of action, poses a significant challenge.

ADCs have emerged as a highly promising platform for precision cancer therapy, demonstrating remarkable efficacy across various tumor types.34,36 The selection of target proteins on tumor cells is paramount for determining the clinical outcome of ADCs, influencing both their efficacy and adverse effects. Numerous tumor-associated target proteins have been investigated as therapeutic targets for TNBC.37,39 Among these, the transmembrane protein B7-H3 is expressed in over 80% of cancers.40,42 B7-H3 has also been identified as an immune checkpoint molecule capable of suppressing the immune functions of natural killer cells and T cells.43,46 A neuroblastoma study demonstrated that vobramitamab duocarmazine, a B7-H3-directed single-payload ADC, induced significant antitumor activity in tumor-bearing mice without observed adverse events.47 Furthermore, MGC026 is a B7-H3-targeting single-payload ADC that demonstrated potent antitumor activity in a prostate cancer patient-derived xenograft (PDX) model, supporting its clinical translation for B7-H3-positive prostate cancer.48 Our previous studies revealed high expression levels of B7-H3 in both TNBC clinical specimens and multiple TNBC cell lines.49 Here, we engineered a B7-H3-targeting dual-payload ADC that enables tumor-specific release of drugs, leading to the precise co-activation of the TLR7/8 and STING pathways and synergistic reprogramming of the TME.

Bispecific antibody (BsAb) is engineered recombinant antibodies capable of simultaneously and specifically binding two different antigens or epitopes, thereby redirecting key immune cells to tumor sites and cooperatively blocking multiple signaling pathways. The PD-L1&4-1BB BsAb represents an in-depth exploration of “precision” and “synergy” in cancer immunotherapy.50 By concurrently targeting two immunomodulatory pathways (PD-L1 and 4–1BB), this BsAb both relieves immunosuppression and delivers a co-stimulatory signal, producing a synergistic “release brakes and step on accelerator” effect. Furthermore, it enhances selectivity for tumor cells and reduces off-target toxicity in normal tissues. ATG-101, an investigational PD-L1&4-1BB BsAb, has demonstrated potent antitumor activity in various animal models.51 Recent research has shown that 4–1BB co-stimulation enhances the survival, proliferation, and effector function of antigen-primed T cells. These properties are currently being exploited for the development of cancer immunotherapies.52 Another study demonstrated that the PD-L1&4-1BB BsAb potently induces T-cell proliferation and cytokine production. Furthermore, in human T-cell cultures, it elicited antigen-specific T cell-mediated cytotoxicity superior to clinically approved PD-(L)1 antibodies and exhibited potent antitumor activity in mouse tumor models.53

Based on this rationale, we hypothesize that combining a B7-H3-targeted dual-payload ADC with a PD-L1&4-1BB BsAb may synergistically enhance antitumor immunity by reprogramming the TME in TNBC. To validate this hypothesis, our proof-of-concept study demonstrated that combining dual-payload ADCs with PD-L1&4-1BB BsAbs robustly enhanced STING pathway activation, facilitated the bridge from innate to adaptive immunity, induced a systemic antitumor memory response, and promoted TME reprogramming in TNBC, thereby contributing to the conversion of immunologically “cold” tumors into “hot” ones (figure 1).

Materials and methods

Materials

Maleimidocaproyl (MC)-glycylglycylphenylalanylglycyl-exatecan derivative (GGFG-Dxd) (#1600418-29-8), B7-H3 monoclonal antibodies (#2490556-50-6), Imiquimod (#99011-02-6), Azide-PEG4-C2-NHS ester (#944251-24-5), DBCO-PEG5-NHS ester (#2144395-59-3), Acasunlimab (PD-L1&4-1BB; # 2253937-12-9) and PD-L1&4-1BB (reaction species: mouse) were acquired from MedChem Express (Shanghai, China) and Genmab. IgG rabbit mAbs (#ab133470) and Cyanine5.5 maleimide were obtained from Abcam Co. (Cambridge, England). p-STING, STING, cGAS, p-TBK1, TBK1, p-IRF3, IRF3, p-P65, P65, p-eIF2a, eIF2a, ATF4, and CHOP antibody were purchased from STARTER. The identities of TNBC cell lines (SUM159, MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468, and BT-549), a murine TNBC cell line (4T1), and the human mammary epithelial cell line MCF10A were verified through STR profiling. Anti-HMGB1 and Anti-Calreticulin (CRT) antibody were obtained from Biosynthesis Biotechnology Inc. (Beijing, China). The mouse TNF-α, IL-2, IL-1β, and IFN-γ ELISA kits were obtained from Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology (Beijing, China). The antibodies used for flow cytometry in this study include: Perp/Cy5.5-conjugated CD45 (462420, BD Bioscience), antigen-presenting cell (APC)-conjugated F4/80 (123116, Biolegend), FITC-conjugated CD11c (117308, Biolegend), PC7-conjugated CD11b, APC-Cy7 conjugated CD86 (105030, Biolegend), PE-conjugated CD80 (104734, Biolegend), FITC-conjugated CD4 (100412, Biolegend), APC-Cy7-conjugated CD3 (553062, Biolegend), APC-conjugated CD8 (557654, Biolegend). AF700-conjugated I-A/I-E (abs1850407, Absin), PB450-conjugated CD44 (563970, BD Pharmingen), and APC-conjugated CD62L (S0B1699, STARTER). All chemical agents were used directly without further purification.

Assessment of B7-H3 protein levels via flow cytometry

B7-H3 expression in human TNBC (SUM159, MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468, and BT-549), 4T1, and MCF10A cell populations was quantified by flow cytometry. For each experiment, approximately one million cells were collected and washed three times by centrifugation. The cell samples were then blocked on ice with a solution containing 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) before being incubated with Cy5.5-labeled antibodies for 2 hours at room temperature. Following incubation, the samples were rinsed four times using phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) supplemented with 1% FBS and subsequently analyzed by a flow cytometer.

Flow cytometry imaging

Cells were cultured at an appropriate density in 6-well plates. The next day, the culture medium was exchanged for fresh medium that included the B7-H3–Cy5.5 antibody, followed by a 4-hour incubation at 37°C. The cells were then resuspended in cold PBS and analyzed using an Image Stream X Mark II imaging flow cytometer (Seattle, Washington, USA).

Flow analysis of immune cell infiltration in tumors

To evaluate the impact of combination therapy on the tumor immune microenvironment, we performed spectral flow cytometry to analyze immune cell infiltration in tumor tissues from the 4T1 murine breast cancer model. Following combination treatment with ADC, single-cell suspensions were prepared from harvested tumor tissues of each treatment group via enzymatic digestion. Red blood cells were lysed using ACK lysis buffer, and viable cells were counted using Trypan Blue exclusion. A total of 4×10⁶ live cells per sample were resuspended in 100 µL of FACS buffer. Fluorescently conjugated antibodies specific for distinct immune cell populations were added to respective samples and incubated for 30 min on ice in the dark. Macrophages were identified by surface expression of F4/80, CD11b, CD80, and MHC-II; DCs by CD11c, CD80, CD86, and MHC-II; and T cells by CD45, CD3, CD4, and CD8. Single-color compensation controls and experimental samples were acquired using a flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter Life Sciences, Shanghai, China).

Immunofluorescence experiment

To detect immunofluorescence staining in vitro and in vivo, treated cells or mouse tumor tissues were fixed, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned at a thickness of 10 µm. Sections were subjected to immunofluorescence staining using corresponding fluorescence-labeled antibodies and imaged under a confocal laser scanning microscope. Image quantification was performed using Image Pro Plus.

Western blot analysis

Total protein was extracted using radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer, and protein concentrations were determined by the BCA assay kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, California, USA). Equal amounts of protein from each sample were separated via sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and subsequently transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (Millipore, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% nonfat dry milk at room temperature for 2 hours, followed by overnight incubation with primary antibodies at 4°C. Thereafter, membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 hour at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence detection reagent (GE Healthcare, USA).

Immunohistochemical staining

Freshly collected tissue specimens were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and incubated at 64°C for 2 hours. The sections underwent deparaffinization in xylene and rehydration through a series of graded ethanol solutions. Antigen retrieval was performed by heating the slides in an antigen retrieval buffer for 12 min, after which they were cooled to room temperature. Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 3% hydrogen peroxide. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against Ki67 (1:200) and CD31 (1:200). Diaminobenzidine (DAB) was used as the chromogen for visualization. The percentage of positively stained cells was quantified using ImageJ software.

In vitro assessment of the STING pathway

RAW 264.7 cells were cultured in DMEM for 48 hours. Western blotting was employed to detect key proteins in the cGAS-STING signaling pathway (cGAS, STING, p65, TBK1, IRF3) in macrophages following treatment with supernatants collected from 4T1-Luc cells subjected to various interventions. Additionally, mRNA levels of downstream target genes of the cGAS-STING pathway (IL-6, CCL5, CXCL10) were quantified by qPCR following RNA extraction and reverse transcription. The levels of inflammatory cytokines secreted by cells (IFN-β, IL-12) were detected by ELISA.

Cytokine determination by real-time quantitative PCR

RAW 264.7 cells were cultured in DMEM for 24 hours prior to RNA extraction using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). The purity and integrity of total RNA were assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis through visualization of the 28S and 18S ribosomal RNA band ratios. Approximately 2 µg of total RNA was reverse transcribed to generate complementary DNA (cDNA). Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) was performed using SYBR Green RT-PCR Master Mix (BioRad, Hercules, California, USA) on a CFX96 RT-PCR system with gene-specific primers (online supplemental table S3). The amplification protocol consisted of an initial denaturation at 95°C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 s and 60°C for 1 min. Target gene expression levels were normalized to the reference gene β-actin using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Each experiment was conducted in triplicate.

ELISA

Blood samples were collected from tumor-bearing mice to assess serum levels of specific cytokines, including IL-2, IL-1β, TNF-α, and IFN-γ. Cytokine concentrations were determined using commercial ELISA kits (Elabscience, Wuhan, China) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

Evaluation of B7-H3 expression in clinical samples

80 breast cancer specimens were obtained from individuals at Anhui Province Cancer Hospital. Informed consent was obtained from all patients, and the study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Immunofluorescence analysis was performed on 40 human TNBC samples (stage I, n=10; stage II, n=10; stage III, n=10; stage IV, n=10) and 40 corresponding normal breast tissue samples to evaluate B7-H3 expression levels. Quantification of the immunofluorescence co-staining was conducted using the H-score method.

Synthesis of dual payload ADCs

The B7-H3-targeted ADC carrying Dxd (B7-H3–Dxd) was synthesized following a previously established method. Sodium bicine buffer (200 µL, 1 M, pH 8.0) and sodium diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid solution (20 µL, 100 mM, pH 7) were added to solutions of either the B7-H3 antibody or a control IgG (0.2 mg/mL, 500 µL). The antibodies were reduced using four equivalents of tris(carboxyethyl)phosphine for 2 hours at 37°C. Subsequently, the reduced antibodies were conjugated with four equivalents of either MC-GGFG-Dxd (MC-GGFG-Dxd) or Cyanine5.5 maleimide (Mc-Cy5.5). After 2 hours of stirring, the product was purified using a Sephadex G25 gel filtration column, eluting with PBS. Subsequently, purified B7-H3-Dxd was conjugated with DBCO-PEG5-NHS ester via a primary amine reaction to generate DBCO-conjugated B7-H3-Dxd. Concurrently, imiquimod was dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and diluted into PBS. Separately, azide-PEG4-C2-NHS ester was dissolved in DMSO and added dropwise to the imiquimod solution over 2 min. The mixture was then incubated under light-protected conditions with shaking (500–800 rpm, room temperature, 5 hours) to yield azide-conjugated imiquimod. Finally, the azide-conjugated imiquimod was mixed with DBCO-conjugated B7-H3-Dxd, and a copper-free click chemistry reaction was performed under physiological conditions to obtain the dual-payload ADCs. The reaction was monitored by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) using an Ultimate 3000 LC system and a Q-TOF mass spectrometer with an ACQUITY UPLC Protein BEH C4 column. The elution method was as follows: solvent A was water with 0.1% formic acid, and solvent B was acetonitrile with 0.1% formic acid. A linear gradient ran from 5% to 100% B over 10 min, held at 100% B for 5 min, returned to 5% B over 2 min, and held at 5% B for 2 min, at a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. Average drug-to-antibody ratios (DAR) were calculated from the LC-MS data.

In vitro cytotoxicity assays

TNBC cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5000 cells per well. The culture medium was replaced with fresh medium containing varying concentrations of B7-H3-targeted ADC carrying IMQ (B7-H3–IMQ), B7-H3–Dxd, or B7-H3-targeted ADC carrying Dxd&IMQ (B7-H3–Dxd&IMQ). After 72 hours of incubation, cytotoxicity was assessed using the CCK-8 assay. Briefly, the drug-containing medium was removed, and cells were washed with cold PBS prior to incubation with CCK-8 solution at 37°C for 4 hours. The percentage of cell growth inhibition was determined by comparing the optical density values of treated cells to those of untreated controls. Extracellular ATP levels were additionally quantified using the Beyotime ATP Assay Kit (S0026).

Antitumor activity of ADCs in TNBC organoid models

Tumor specimens were mechanically dissociated and subsequently subjected to enzymatic digestion using a tumor digestion solution for 30 min at 37°C under agitation in a shaking incubator. A single cell suspension was generated by filtering the digested tissue through a 100 µm cell strainer. Cells were embedded in Matrigel and seeded into 24-well plates containing a growth-promoting nutrient medium. For cytotoxicity assessment, organoids were treated with PBS, B7-H3 antibody, B7-H3–IMQ, B7-H3–Dxd, or B7-H3–Dxd&IMQ for 72 hours. Organoid viability was evaluated using the Cell Titer-Glo 3D assay according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

Evaluation of in vivo targeting

4T1 cells (2×106) were subcutaneously implanted into the right hind flank of 6-week-old female BALB/c mice. Tumors reached a volume of approximately 100 mm3 by day 14. Mice were then randomly assigned to two groups (n=5) and received intravenous administration of either IgG–Cy5.5 or B7-H3–Cy5.5 (5 mg/kg) via the tail vein. Near-infrared (NIR) fluorescence imaging was performed at 6, 12, and 24 hours postinjection using the in vivo imaging system (IVIS) Lumina II imaging system. Fluorescence intensity in major organs (heart, liver, spleen, lungs, kidneys, brain) and tumor tissues was quantitatively analyzed.

Pharmacokinetic studies

BALB/c mice were randomly assigned to seven groups (n=3) for pharmacokinetic analysis. The mice received a weekly intravenous dose of 5 mg/kg B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ for three consecutive weeks. For the periods of day 1–7 and day 15–21, blood was drawn at 0 hour, 5 min, 1, 8, 24, 72, and 168 hours. The concentrations of total antibody and conjugated Dxd (anti-Dxd antibody) in plasma were quantified using validated ELISA methods (MedChemExpress, Shanghai, China). The resulting data were analyzed using a noncompartmental model in Winnonlin software to calculate key pharmacokinetic parameters.

Studies using TNBC PDX models

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from healthy donor blood using density gradient centrifugation. These PBMCs were then activated for 3 days with TCR agonists or IL-2 in RPMI 1640 medium containing an antimicrobial/antifungal agent and 1 mM pyruvate. These procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of Anhui Provincial Cancer Hospital. Fresh TNBC tumor tissue was obtained from patients during surgical resection. Tumor fragments were washed in DMEM with antibiotics at 0°C and minced into 3×3×3 mm3 pieces. A mixture of tumor cells and Matrigel was subcutaneously implanted into the right flank of 7-week-old female NOD/ShiLtJGPtPrkdcem26Il2rgem26/Gpt (NSG) mice. When the tumors reached a volume of 100–150 mm³, the mice were randomized (n=5) and received an intravenous injection of 5 million PBMCs. Subsequently, the mice received treatment with PBS, PD-L1&4-1BB, B7-H3–Dxd&IMQ, or B7-H3–Dxd&IMQ+PD-L1&4-1BB. Mouse body weight was monitored throughout the study period. On day 21, the mice were euthanized, and tumors were harvested. The harvested tumors underwent H&E, Ki-67, and CD31 staining, as well as TUNEL and immunofluorescence staining for CD4+and CD8+ T cells. Additionally, serum concentrations of IL-2, TNF-α, and IFN-γ were quantified by ELISA.

Reprogramming of the immune microenvironment in 4T1 tumor-bearing mice

4T1 TNBC cells were inoculated into female BALB/c mice (6–8 weeks old) to assess in vivo antitumor efficacy. When tumors attained a volume of 100–150 mm³, the animals were randomized into treatment groups (n=5) and treated with specified doses of PBS, PD-L1&4-1BB, B7-H3–Dxd&IMQ, or a combination of B7-H3–Dxd&IMQ and PD-L1&4-1BB. Tumor dimensions were measured every other day, and volume was calculated using the formula: volume=length × width2/2. At the end of the study, blood was collected via cardiac puncture. Serum levels of AST, ALT, creatinine, and BUN were determined using commercial ELISA kits (Huabangbio Co., Shanghai, China). Furthermore, tumor sections were subjected to p-IRF3, p-TBK1, p-P65, CRT, and HMGB1 staining. Immunofluorescence staining was used to identify different immune cell populations in tumor sections. Additionally, following the manufacturer’s instructions for the tissue dissociation kit, flow cytometry was performed to analyze the composition and dynamic changes of immune cell subsets within the TME.

Tumor rechallenge analysis

The experiment used 7-week-old female BALB/c mice. 4T1 cells were initially inoculated subcutaneously on the right flank of each mouse (day −21). Once tumor volume reached approximately 100 mm³, mice received respective ADC treatments. On day 0, tumors on the right side were surgically resected and wounds were sutured. Subsequently, the same 4T1 cells were rechallenged by subcutaneous inoculation on the left flank (day 3). No further treatment was administered during this phase. Mice were euthanized 21 days post-rechallenge. Splenic memory T cell populations were analyzed by flow cytometry. In addition, immunofluorescence staining was performed to characterize immune cell infiltration in lung metastasis tissue sections.

RNA sequencing analysis

For the purpose of transcriptome analysis, total RNA was first isolated from tumor tissue samples using TRI zol reagent. The RNeasy Micro Kit (QIAGEN, Valencia, California, USA) was then used to synthesize cDNA from the purified RNA via reverse transcription. To ensure statistical reliability, all experimental procedures were conducted in triplicate. The final RNA sequencing was carried out by Metware Technology (Wuhan, China).

Statistical analysis

In the present study, all experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates, and data are presented as mean±SD. Two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-tests were applied for two-group comparisons, while one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used for multiple groups. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (V.8.0). All statistical significances were reported accordingly. ns, not statistically significant, p>0.05; *p<0.05; **p<0.01; ***p<0.001.

Results

Evaluation of B7-H3 expression levels

To obtain TNBC histological specimens for evaluating B7-H3 molecular expression and for subsequent in vitro 3D organoid and in vivo PDX models, we first collected data from four representative TNBC patients. Tumor tissue samples were then obtained and subjected to immunofluorescence co-staining for B7-H3 and PD-L1. These analyses revealed the spatial distribution and potential co-localization of B7-H3 and PD-L1 within the TME, while adjacent normal tissues showed only minimal expression (figure 2A). We next evaluated B7-H3 protein expression in human TNBC cell lines (MDA-MB-231, SUM159, MDA-MB-468, and BT549) and a murine TNBC cell line (4T1), using the human mammary epithelial cell line MCF10A as a control. Immunofluorescence staining demonstrated markedly increased B7-H3 expression in all TNBC cell lines, whereas fluorescence was nearly undetectable in MCF10A cells (figure 2B,D). The results of flow cytometry analysis indicated that the expression level of B7-H3 protein in TNBC cell lines was significantly higher than that in the MCF10A group (figure 2C,E). Western blot analysis confirmed variable but substantial B7-H3 protein expression in MDA-MB-231, MDA-MB-468, BT-549, 4T1, and SUM159 cells, contrasting with its negligible expression in MCF10A (figure 2F,G; online supplemental figure S1). Together, these findings indicate that B7-H3 is broadly expressed in TNBC clinical specimens and cell lines, supporting its potential as a novel target for TNBC immunotherapy.

Receptor-mediated endocytosis is a key determinant of ADC efficacy and off-target effects. We therefore investigated B7-H3 antibody internalization using imaging flow cytometry. B7-H3-Cy5.5 was robustly internalized by MDA-MB-231 and 4T1 cells via receptor-mediated endocytosis, whereas MCF10A cells exhibited minimal red fluorescence, reflecting their lack of B7-H3 expression (online supplemental figure S2). Notably, the fluorescence intensity of B7-H3-Cy5.5 in 4T1 cells was approximately 100-fold higher than in MCF10A cells (online supplemental figure S3). These findings confirm that the B7-H3 antibody functions as a highly specific ligand for TNBC cells and provide a theoretical foundation for its further development.

In vivo evaluation of B7-H3 antibody targeting

We next evaluated TNBC-specific targeting of B7-H3 in vivo using a 4T1-luciferase (4T1-luci) tumor-bearing mouse model. TNBC cells were implanted into mice on day 0; 14 days later, mice received intravenous injections of either B7-H3-Cy5.5 or non-specific IgG-Cy5.5 as a control (figure 3A). This experimental design aimed to evaluate the TNBC-specific targeting and biodistribution of B7-H3-Cy5.5 over time. In vivo imaging was performed at 6, 12, and 24 hours postinjection to assess the distribution of fluorescence intensity. By 6 hours, B7-H3-Cy5.5 had accumulated visibly in the tumor region, with significantly higher fluorescence than IgG-Cy5.5. The signal intensity in tumors continued to rise at 12 hours and remained high at 24 hours, whereas IgG-Cy5.5 exhibited only weak, diffuse signals consistent with non-specific binding and rapid clearance. Quantitative fluorescence analysis confirmed significantly stronger signals in the B7-H3-Cy5.5 group across all time points (figure 3B,C; online supplemental figure S4). At 12 hours, bioluminescent (Luci) and monoclonal antibody fluorescence (Cy5.5) signals co-localized within tumors (figure 3D), confirming specific tumor targeting. Ex vivo organ biodistribution showed the highest fluorescence intensity in tumors, followed by the liver and lungs (figure 3E,H-M), consistent with the known pharmacokinetic profile of many probes that accumulate in reticuloendothelial system organs. However, the strongest fluorescence intensity was evident in tumor tissues (figure 3G), highlighting the specific targeting capabilities of B7-H3-Cy5.5 when treating TNBC (online supplemental figure S5). Histological analysis of tumor sections corroborated these in vivo and ex vivo imaging findings (figure 3F). Tumors from mice injected with B7-H3-Cy5.5 displayed intense fluorescence signals indicative of deep probe penetration, whereas tumors from IgG-Cy5.5-treated mice exhibited minimal or no fluorescence (figure 3N), further validating the specific targeting performance of B7-H3-Cy5.5. In summary, these results demonstrate the excellent tumor-targeting efficacy of B7-H3-Cy5.5 in a preclinical mouse model of TNBC, supporting its potential for targeted drug delivery through multimodal in vivo and ex vivo imaging as well as histological examination.

Characterization and cytotoxicity of B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ

We developed a novel, sequential, and modular conjugation method to create a dual-payload ADC. First, the B7-H3 monoclonal antibody was conjugated to MC-GGFG-Dxd via Michael addition, forming a stable thioether bond. IMQ was then modified with an azide group through primary amine chemistry, and the DBCO-bearing B7-H3-Dxd was reacted with azide-modified IMQ via copper-free click chemistry. Conjugation efficiency for the single-payload (B7-H3-Dxd, B7-H3-IMQ) and dual-payload (B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ) ADCs was assessed by LC-MS (online supplemental figures S6–8), and their molecular structures are depicted schematically. The integrity of the monoclonal antibody and ADCs was confirmed by SDS-PAGE (online supplemental figure S9). The structural characteristics and corresponding coupling molecules for the dual-payload ADC are presented in figure 4A,B, respectively. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed that the dual-payload ADC had a uniform morphology and size (17 nm), suggesting strong tissue penetration capability (figure 4C,D). The deconvoluted molecular weight of dual-payload ADC was detected by electrospray ionization (ESI)-mass spectrometry analysis (figure 4E). Both Dxd and IMQ had an average drug-to-antibody ratio of 2. The molecular docking study diagram is presented between the B7-H3 monoclonal antibody and the B7-H3 protein (figure 4F–H). In vitro cytotoxicity assays demonstrated significant cytotoxic effects of B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ across TNBC cell lines (4T1, MDA-MB-231, SUM159) (figure 4I-K). The IC50 values for B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ were 0.01 µg/mL (4T1), 0.02 µg/mL (MDA-MB-231), and 0.013 µg/mL (SUM159), markedly lower than those of B7-H3-IMQ or B7-H3-Dxd alone, indicating superior potency of the dual-payload ADC. In 4T1-Luci cells, treatment with B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ markedly increased intracellular calcium levels compared with PBS controls (figure 4L), consistent with mitochondrial disruption leading to calcium dysregulation and apoptosis. Apoptotic cells release damage-associated molecular patterns, including surface-exposed CRT, HMGB1, and ATP. We therefore measured CRT exposure, HMGB1 release, and ATP release following various treatments of 4T1-Luc cells. Immunofluorescence staining showed significantly increased CRT exposure and HMGB1 release in the B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ group (figure 4M,P,Q). This dual-payload ADC also impaired cell membrane integrity, promoting the release of cellular contents and markedly enhancing ATP release (figure 4N). Furthermore, western blotting revealed that phosphorylated eIF2α (p-eIF2α), CHOP, and ATF4 protein levels were significantly elevated in the B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ group (figure 4O,R-U), suggesting the induction of pronounced endoplasmic reticulum stress and potential ICD via the p-eIF2α-ATF4 pathway. Collectively, these in vitro cytotoxicity and mechanistic findings provide a strong theoretical basis for subsequent in vivo animal studies.

Activation of the intracellular cGAS-STING pathway

During apoptosis and ICD, dying tumor cells release substantial amounts of DNA. The cGAS-STING pathway senses both exogenous and endogenous DNA, thereby activating innate immunity and inducing production of multiple type I interferon-related factors (figure 5A). Activation of the STING signaling pathway within immune cells of the TME can stimulate anti-tumor immune responses, overcome immune tolerance, and reprogram the TME. We hypothesized that supernatants derived from ICD-induced cancer cells could activate the cGAS-STING pathway in macrophages. Indeed, treatment with B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ caused damaged tumor cell DNA to be released into the TME (figure 5B,C). Supernatants from 4T1-Luc cells treated with different regimens were collected and applied to macrophages, and changes in cGAS-STING pathway-related proteins were examined by Western blotting. Supernatants from untreated 4T1-Luc cells did not significantly activate the cGAS-STING pathway, as indicated by low expression levels of cGAS, p-STING, p-IRF3, p-TBK1, and p-P65 (figure 5D). As anticipated, supernatants from cells treated with B7-H3-Dxd or B7-H3-IMQ induced upregulation of cGAS and phosphorylation of STING, IRF3, and P65 to varying degrees. Notably, supernatants from B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ-treated 4T1-Luc cells exhibited the strongest STING pathway activation (figure 5E–I), consistent with enhanced DNA release resulting from combined ICD and IMQ treatment.

Because STING pathway activation stimulates the expression of interferon-inducible genes, we also measured mRNA levels of CXCL10, CCL5, and IL6 by qPCR, as these are representative downstream targets of type I interferon signaling.54 B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ treatment significantly upregulated CXCL10, CCL5, and IL6 transcripts compared with control supernatant groups (figure 5J–L; online supplemental table S1). Importantly, these genes were also increased in the B7-H3-Dxd and B7-H3-IMQ groups, indicating that both IMQ and ICD can activate the downstream STING pathway. At the same time, we also found that the cytokines IL-12p70 and IFN-β in the experimental group were significantly upregulated compared with the other treatment groups (figure 5M,N). In addition, since phosphorylated IRF3 and P65 must translocate to the nucleus to initiate transcription, we assessed their localization by immunofluorescence. The B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ group showed pronounced nuclear translocation of both P65 and IRF3, whereas only partial nuclear localization was observed in the B7-H3-Dxd and B7-H3-IMQ groups (figure 5O–Q), consistent with the Western blotting and qPCR findings and confirming varying degrees of STING pathway activation.

To further dissect the contributions to STING activation in macrophages, we performed comparative analyses. Although IMQ released from the ADC can induce NF-κB activation and thereby stimulate STING, ADC-released IMQ alone produced only moderate STING activation. In contrast, macrophages treated with supernatant from the B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ dual-payload group exhibited the most pronounced STING activation, indicating that double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) released from dying tumor cells is a key factor driving STING pathway activation in the TME. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ dual-payload ADC induces apoptosis and ICD, activates the cGAS-STING pathway, remodels the TME, and consequently suppresses tumor cell proliferation.

Although macrophages represent a major APC population in the TME, DCs are also critical. To assess whether B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ activates the STING pathway in DCs, we treated DC2.4 cells with supernatants from 4T1-Luc cells subjected to various treatments. Our results indicate that the dual-payload ADC similarly activates the cGAS-STING pathway in DCs (online supplemental figure S10), as confirmed by immunofluorescence showing nuclear translocation of P65 and IRF3 in DCs. These findings suggest that both macrophages and DCs participate in B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ-mediated activation of the cGAS-STING pathway, thereby contributing to anti-tumor immune reprogramming.

Pharmacokinetic studies

An evaluation of the PK parameters for B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ was conducted following intravenous administration to healthy mice. Serum levels of the total antibody and its conjugated Dxd payload were quantified by ELISA. The resulting PK profiles for both entities showed a progressive decrease over three repeated dosing cycles (online supplemental figures S11 and S12). According to WinNonlin software analysis, the terminal half-life (t½) of the total antibody was 132 hours during the initial phase (days 1–7) and extended to 142 hours in the subsequent phase (days 15–21). Notably, the conjugated Dxd exhibited a comparable trend, with its t½ increasing from 121 h to 151 h over the same intervals (online supplemental tables S2 and S3).

Validation of B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ efficacy in an organoid model

As patient-derived organoid (PDO) models retain key pathological and molecular features of the parent tumor, we established two organoid models from TNBC tumor tissues, providing a reliable preclinical model for evaluating the efficacy of B7-H3-targeted ADCs (figure 6A). The treatment of organoids with different concentrations of B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ and other ADCs revealed that B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ significantly inhibited organoid growth in a concentration-dependent manner, reducing both organoid size and number (figure 6B–D). This confirms the anti-tumor activity of B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ in a 3D organoid system (figure 6E,F) and supports its translational potential. H&E staining demonstrated that B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ-treated organoids became smaller, contained fewer cells, and appeared looser and less viable (figure 6G). Immunofluorescence assays showed structural alterations and nuclear fragmentation consistent with apoptosis in B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ-treated organoids (figure 6H), whereas no significant morphological changes were seen in other treatment groups. Notably, the level of B7-H3 protein expression (red fluorescence) in organoids may serve as a biomarker for predicting B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ efficacy. This enhances the clinical relevance of the organoid model and links drug sensitivity to molecular subtypes, offering new perspectives for precision therapy in TNBC.

Validation of the anti-tumor efficacy of B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ in a PDX model

Given the strong in vitro efficacy of B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ, we next evaluated its anti-tumor activity in vivo using a TNBC PDX model. We tested B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ in combination with a PD-L1&4-1BB BsAb (figure 7A). B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ was administered intravenously at 5 mg/kg three times at 4-day intervals, while the PD-L1&4-1BB BsAb was delivered intraperitoneally at 5 mg/kg. After 21 days, the combination group exhibited significantly greater tumor growth inhibition than the other groups (figure 7B,C,E). Tumor weight analysis confirmed smaller tumors in the combination group relative to controls (figure 7C). Body weight remained stable throughout treatment, indicating minimal drug-related adverse effects (figure 7D). The combination group also displayed the strongest induction of apoptosis, as well as a pronounced reduction in Ki67-positive cells, resulting in marked tumor growth suppression (figure 7F,G). Compared with other groups, the combination therapy reduced CD31 expression and increased TUNEL-positive apoptotic signals (green fluorescence) in TUNEL and CD31 staining (figure 7F,H,I). Importantly, the percentages of CD8+ and CD4+ T cells were significantly higher in the combination group (figure 7F,J,K), reflecting enhanced T-cell activation and infiltration into tumor tissue.

Finally, we investigated the synergistic anti-tumor immune response elicited by the combination therapy. Compared with other treatments, the combination group markedly enhanced the secretion and expression of tumor-suppressive cytokines, including TNF-α, IL-2, and IFN-γ (online supplemental figure S13). Investigation of in vivo immune microenvironment reprogramming

To elucidate the mechanisms by which the B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ dual-payload ADC modulates the TNBC immune microenvironment (figure 8A), we conducted in vivo studies in BALB/c mice bearing 4T1-Luc tumors (figure 8B). The most pronounced decrease in bioluminescent signal at the tumor site over time occurred in the B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ+PD-L1&4-1BB combination group. Monotherapy with either B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ or PD-L1&4-1BB also reduced the bioluminescent signal to some extent, indicating anti-tumor activity. These data suggest that apoptosis induction and ICD generated by the combination therapy maximally suppress tumor growth (figure 8C). A similar trend was observed in tumor growth curves; both monotherapies slowed tumor growth to varying degrees, whereas the combination therapy exhibited the most potent anti-tumor effect (figure 8D). Body weight, an indicator of treatment-related toxicity, remained stable across all groups, suggesting minimal adverse effects (figure 8E). Immunofluorescence analysis of tumor tissues for ICD markers showed the strongest surface CRT exposure (green fluorescence) and HMGB-1 release (reduced red signal) in the combination group (online supplemental figure S14), consistent with in vitro ICD induction.

To further clarify the enhanced anti-tumor efficacy, we investigated key immune processes, including STING activation, cytokine secretion, and immune cell regulation. Immunofluorescence staining of tumor tissues revealed the strongest fluorescence signals for p-IRF3 (green), p-TBK1 (pink), and p-P65 (red) in the combination group, whereas only weaker signals were detected in other groups (figure 8F–I), indicating robust STING pathway activation by the combination therapy.

Because mature DCs and PD-L1&4-1BB engagement can promote effector T cell activation, we analyzed the distribution and status of T cells in tumor tissues by immunofluorescence. As shown in figure 8J, CD4+ and CD8+ T cell counts increased by 6.0-fold and 13.9-fold (figure 8K,L), respectively, in the combination group. Macrophages also play a critical role in the TME; M2-type tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) suppress T cell proliferation, whereas M1-type TAMs are key players in anti-tumor immunity. Thus, repolarizing macrophages from M2 to M1 is an important strategy for reshaping the TME. In the combination group, the percentage of M1 macrophages increased dramatically from 4.6% in controls to 42.8%, while the proportion of M2 macrophages significantly decreased (figure 8M,N), indicating a strong M2-to-M1 TAM conversion. Furthermore, the percentage of regulatory T cells (Tregs), which suppress effector T cells and promote tumor metastasis and immune evasion, was effectively reduced in the combination group (figure 8O). Similarly, the proportion of myeloid-derived suppressor cell, which exert potent immunosuppressive effects and promote tumor growth and metastasis, was markedly lower (figure 8P). Together, these immune cell changes induced by the ADC and BsAb combination indicate effective TME reprogramming.

To achieve a more detailed evaluation of immune cell changes within the TME, tumor tissues were also analyzed by flow cytometry. The highest percentage of CD8+ T cells (19.4%) was observed in the combination group, significantly greater than in the PBS group (3.65%) (figure 8Q,R; online supplemental figure 15). Likewise, intratumoral DCs were significantly increased in the combination group (figure 8S,T). In addition, an increased proportion of M1 macrophages was also detected in the combination group relative to other groups (figure 8U,V; online supplemental figure S16). These results indicate that the combination therapy induces ICD and STING activation, drives macrophage polarization, reprograms the TME, and enhances anti-tumor immune responses. Levels of immunostimulatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α, IFN-γ) were also highest after combination therapy (online supplemental figure S17), further confirming robust anti-tumor immune activation. Finally, systemic evaluation of the combination therapy’s biosafety was conducted. Analysis of blood biochemical parameters from all tumor-bearing mice revealed no significant differences between groups, indicating no apparent systemic toxicity (online supplemental figure S18). Furthermore, H&E staining of major organs (heart, liver, spleen, lungs, kidneys) revealed no visible tissue damage (online supplemental figure S19), further supporting the favorable biosafety profile of the combination regimen.

Assessment of long-term immune memory induced by combination therapy

Effective activation of the systemic anti-tumor immune system can induce durable immune memory, thereby preventing tumor recurrence and inhibiting distant metastasis. We investigated the potential of the combination therapy to prevent recurrence and suppress lung metastasis. After three treatments with B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ and PD-L1&4-1BB, the primary tumor on the right flank was surgically resected on day 0. 3 days later, mice were rechallenged with 4T1-Luc cells injected into the left flank (figure 9A). The results showed that the combination therapy not only inhibited primary tumor growth but also delayed the growth of the contralateral rechallenge tumor (figure 9B,D,E; online supplemental figure S20), suggesting the induction of memory T cells and long-term immune memory. Additionally, the spleens of tumor-bearing mice frequently enlarge due to the accumulation of immunosuppressive cells. Significant splenomegaly was observed in the PBS group (figure 9C), with spleen weight in the PBS group being 2.3-fold higher than in the combination group (figure 9F), confirming enhanced immunotherapeutic efficacy with combination therapy, consistent with previous findings.55 Moreover, the combination therapy extended the median survival of tumor-rechallenged mice (figure 9G). To explore the underlying mechanisms, splenic immune cells were analyzed by flow cytometry. Compared with the PBS group, the combination group exhibited higher proportions of both effector memory and central memory T cells in CD8+ and CD4+ T cell populations in the spleen (figure 9H,I,K,L; online supplemental figure S21). Evaluation of distant metastases by counting lung metastatic nodules revealed that the combination therapy effectively suppressed lung metastasis. Remarkably, only single, very small metastatic nodules were detected in the combination group (figure 9J,M). Immune cell analysis of lung metastases demonstrated significantly increased counts of CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, DCs, and M1 macrophages, along with reduced counts of M2 macrophages and Treg cells in the combination group compared with other treatments (figure 9N; online supplemental figure S22), further confirming the ability of the combination therapy to remodel the immune microenvironment of lung metastases. Collectively, these findings strongly support that the combination of B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ and PD-L1&4-1BB induces long-term immune memory and reprograms the intratumoral immune microenvironment, thereby inhibiting tumor growth and distant metastasis.

RNA sequencing analysis

To further explore the immunomodulatory mechanisms of B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ, RNA sequencing was performed on tumor tissues from the B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ+PD-L1&4-1BB group and the PBS control group. Analysis identified 774 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), with 441 upregulated and 333 downregulated in the treatment group compared with the control (figure 10A,B). Notably, Vegfc was significantly upregulated. Vegfc is an interferon-stimulated gene (ISG); activation of the STING pathway in tumor or stromal cells triggers type I interferon production, which induces the expression of numerous ISGs, including Vegfc, thereby promoting T cell activation and tumor infiltration. Conversely, Sox10 was significantly downregulated. Sox10 is known to maintain cancer stem cell-like states and suppress anti-tumor immunity, thereby contributing to an immunosuppressive microenvironment and inhibiting STING pathway activation. Its downregulation may alleviate this suppression, improving the tumor immune milieu. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes enrichment analysis revealed significant enrichment of immune-related pathways after combination therapy, including the AMPK signaling pathway, cAMP signaling pathway, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, NK cell-mediated cytotoxicity, Th1 and Th2 cell differentiation, NOD-like receptor signaling pathway, and others (figure 10C–E). Gene Ontology (GO) functional analysis of DEGs is shown in figure 10F,G. The gene Clnk, which participates in pathways positively regulating NK cell cytokine production, was upregulated. Discussion

The formidable challenge posed by the immunosuppressive “cold” TME in TNBC has long been a critical barrier to the success of immunotherapy. In this investigation, we present a novel and synergistic combinatorial strategy that effectively reprograms the TME, igniting a potent and durable antitumor immune response. Our findings elucidate that a B7-H3-targeted dual-payload ADC, by concurrently delivering exatecan and imiquimod, serves as a powerful initiator of innate immunity. More importantly, when this ADC is combined with a PD-L1&4-1BB BsAb, it creates a self-reinforcing immune cycle that not only bridges innate and adaptive immunity with remarkable efficiency but also establishes a robust long-term immunological memory, offering a promising therapeutic paradigm for TNBC.

The cornerstone of our strategy lies in the rational design of the dual-payload ADC. While single-payload ADCs have shown promise in inducing ICD, their efficacy is often constrained by tumor heterogeneity and obstinate immunosuppressive mechanisms.56 57 One study shows that HER2-targeting dual-payload ADCs exhibit significant dose-dependent antitumor activity and can induce complete tumor regression.58 Another study further demonstrates that, compared with T-DXd, a dual-payload ADC conjugated with MMAE and deruxtecan achieves markedly enhanced tumor suppression in NCI-N87 xenograft models.59 Additionally, KH815 is an ADC featuring dual toxins and a unique mechanism of action, demonstrating stronger antitumor activity compared with single-payload ADCs.60 Our approach of co-delivering exatecan and imiquimod via a B7-H3-targeting antibody represents a significant conceptual breakthrough. We demonstrated that this dual-payload ADC induces potent ICD, releasing dsDNA and activating the cGAS-STING pathway in TAMs and DCs. This is a critical finding, as it indicates that the ADC not only kills tumor cells directly but also converts this death into a potent immunogenic signal. The subsequent maturation of DCs and polarization of macrophages M1 phenotype are essential steps for creating a pro-inflammatory TME conducive to T-cell priming and infiltration. This multi-faceted activation of innate immunity likely explains the observed conversion of “cold” tumors into “hot” ones.

Although single-payload ADCs like DS-8201, TROP2-ADC, and Ly6E-ADC have made significant strides in clinical settings and greatly enhanced patient survival rates,61,64 their long-term effectiveness is still constrained by the immunosuppressive TME. In this regard, our strategically designed dual-payload ADC offers a promising approach to reshape the TME in TNBC by synergistically activating the TLR and STING signaling pathways. Emerging research indicates that these pathways can interact and enhance each other’s effects.65 66 Our findings suggest that the simultaneous release of both payloads within the TME results in a more robust and sustained activation of the innate immune response than targeting either pathway individually. This synergy in innate immune sensing likely accounts for the enhanced TME reprogramming observed with our dual-payload ADC compared with single-payload counterparts, underscoring the significance of precise, tumor-targeted co-delivery.

Although the dual-payload ADC effectively remodels the TME, its immunosuppressive nature in established tumors often dampens the subsequent T-cell response. This pivotal challenge is addressed by the strategic combination with a PD-L1&4-1BB BsAb. Acting as a key regulator at the immune synapse, this BsAb not only blocks the PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitory pathway “releasing the brakes” but also delivers a potent 4–1 BB-mediated co-stimulatory signal “stepping on the accelerator”.67 Our results demonstrate that this combination therapy leads to a rapid and effective bridge from innate to adaptive immunity. The activated DCs, loaded with tumor antigens processed from ADC-killed cells, can now effectively prime T cells in draining lymph nodes. These primed T cells, on infiltrating the tumor, are protected from exhaustion by PD-L1 blockade and are further amplified by 4–1BB signaling. This creates a positive feedback loop where innate immune activation fuels a potent and sustained T-cell response, which is in turn empowered and maintained by the BsAb. This establishes a positive feedback loop in which innate immune activation drives a robust and sustained T-cell response, which is further supported and maintained by the BsAb.

Perhaps one of the most clinically significant observations of our study is the elicitation of a robust long-term immune memory response. The presence of a durable antitumor memory is the holy grail of cancer immunotherapy, as it offers protection against tumor recurrence. The combination therapy’s ability to generate such a response can be attributed to the creation of a favorable environment for memory T-cell differentiation, likely driven by the potent and sustained type I IFN signaling from STING activation and the critical co-stimulatory signals from 4 to 1BB engagement.68 This suggests that our strategy not only achieves tumor regression in the short term but may also provide long-term clinical benefits for patients by preventing relapse.

Our study aligns with and extends the growing body of literature on STING-activating ADCs69 70 and BsAbs.71 However, it moves the field forward by integrating these two powerful modalities into a single, coherent therapeutic strategy. The choice of B7-H3 as the target is particularly relevant in TNBC, given its high overexpression and dual role as a tumor antigen and an immune checkpoint.72 73 This strategy guarantees that the ADC is accurately directed to the tumor site while simultaneously inhibiting a local immunosuppressive signal, thereby enhancing anti-tumor efficacy.

Despite the promising results, several questions remain for future investigation. First, the optimal dosing and scheduling of this combination regimen need to be carefully determined in clinical settings to maximize efficacy and minimize potential toxicities, particularly those associated with systemic immune activation. Second, identifying predictive biomarkers for patient selection will be crucial. It would be valuable to investigate whether the levels of B7-H3 expression, tumor-infiltrating immune cell subsets, or specific genetic signatures of the STING pathway correlate with treatment response. Additionally, employing STING inhibitors or STING-deficient mice in future studies would further strengthen the reliability of the conclusions. Finally, exploring the efficacy of this strategy in other immunologically “cold” cancers beyond TNBC could significantly broaden its clinical impact.

B7-H3 is widely expressed across various tumor types. Research indicates that it is considered a key target for developing ADCs against prostate cancer, largely due to its role in minimizing drug resistance.74 Another study demonstrated potent B7-H3-specific, T-cell-dependent cytotoxicity across multiple B7-H3-positive tumor cell lines with varying expression levels, thereby laying the theoretical groundwork for developing bispecific B7-H3-targeting ADCs.75 Additionally, B7-H3-targeting ADCs have demonstrated significant antitumor activity in xenograft models of B7-H3-positive solid tumors, such as ovarian cancer and small cell lung cancer.76 Future studies should further evaluate the antitumor efficacy and mechanisms of B7-H3-targeting dual-payload ADCs in additional solid tumors. However, inhibiting B7-H3 may disrupt immune tolerance in normal tissues, triggering autoimmune-like responses. Moreover, it can induce toxicity to normal tissues, including vascular endothelial injury, damage to metabolic organs, and neurotoxicity. Furthermore, targeting B7-H3 may also exert potential effects on immune surveillance.

Collectively, our work establishes a compelling therapeutic strategy that leverages a dual-payload ADC to initiate innate immunity and a BsAb to empower adaptive immunity. This combination effectively dismantles the key barriers within the TNBC TME, leading to potent tumor inhibition and the establishment of protective immunological memory. This approach provides a robust scientific rationale for the clinical translation of this combination, offering new hope for enhancing the efficacy of immunotherapy in TNBC and other refractory cancers.

Conclusions

Overcoming the immunosuppressive TME remains a major challenge to improving the efficacy of immunotherapy in TNBC. Activation of the STING pathway effectively bridges innate and adaptive immunity, thereby reprogramming the immunosuppressive TME. In this preliminary study, we employed a dual-payload ADC (B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ) to remodel the TME through induction of ICD and activation of the cGAS-STING pathway. The dual-payload ADC specifically targets tumor cells, induces ICD, and releases substantial amounts of cellular DNA, which activates the cGAS-STING pathway in neighboring macrophages. This process converts immunologically “cold” tumors into “hot” tumors characterized by increased T cell infiltration, DC maturation, enhanced M1 macrophage polarization, and a reduction in Treg cells. This modulation of the TME elicited significant anti-tumor effects. Moreover, the combination of B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ with a PD-L1&4-1BB BsAb synergistically enhanced anti-tumor activity and induced long-term immune memory responses in vivo. In summary, this preliminary study proposed a novel strategy wherein B7-H3-Dxd&IMQ potentiated immunotherapy efficacy and demonstrated promising potential for inducing durable immune memory to prevent distant metastasis. Further evaluation of the dual payload ADC’s effectiveness across diverse tumor models is warranted to advance future clinical translation.

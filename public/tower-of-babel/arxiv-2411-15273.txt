Birkhoff-James classification of finite-dimensional C ∗ C^{*} -algebras Thanks: This work is supported in part by the Slovenian Research Agency (research program P1-0285 and research projects N1-0210, N1-0296 and J1-50000).
Bojan Kuzma, Sushil Singla

Abstract
. We classify real or complex finite-dimensional C ∗ C^{*} -algebras and their underlying fields from the properties of Birkhoff-James orthogonality. Application to strong Birkhoff-James orthogonality preservers is also given.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 46L05, 46B80; Secondary 46B20

1.
Introduction

Two vectors
u,v
in a Hilbert space are orthogonal if their inner product is zero. This is equivalent to the fact that the distance from
u
to the one-dimensional subspace spanned by
v
is attained at
0
. We can then
define orthogonality in general normed spaces over the field
{\mathbb{F}}\in\{{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}\}
by

u\perp v\quad\hbox{if}\quad\|u+\lambda v\|\geq\|u\|\text{ for all }\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}.

This relation, which was proposed by Birkhoff
[
3
]
and further developed by James
[
8
]
, is known as
Birkhoff-James (BJ for short) orthogonality
. It turns out that BJ orthogonality knows a lot about the norm. For example, James
[
8
, Theorem 1]
, by using the Kakutani-Bohnenblust characterization of inner-product spaces
[
9
, Theorem 3]
and
[
4
]
(see also a book by Amir
[
1
]
), states that a normed space
V
of dimension greater or equal to three is an inner product space if and only if BJ orthogonality is symmetric. See also the recent results
[
2
,
7
,
10
,
11
]
for more.

Our main aim is to
show that BJ orthogonality knows everything about (real or complex) finite-dimensional
C^{\ast}
-algebras. Namely, as we show in our main result below, two finite-dimensional
C^{\ast}
-algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
are
BJ-isomorphic
(i.e., there exists a bijection
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
with
u\perp v\Longleftrightarrow\phi(u)\perp\phi(v)
) if and only if their underlying fields are the same and
they are
\ast
-isomorphic.
This is in spirit of Tanaka
[
12
]
who characterized complex abelian
C^{*}
-algebras among all (finite or infinite-dimensional) complex
C^{*}
-algebras in terms of geometric structure (which was first defined from BJ orthogonality
[
13
]
). We have been informed by Tanaka
[
14
]
that he has recently also found a similar classification within finite-dimensional complex
C^{*}
-algebras. We refer to a book by Goodearl
[
5
]
for more on the theory of real
C^{*}
-algebras, which are similar to the complex ones.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
C^{*}
-algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
, over the fields
{\mathbb{F}}_{1}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
,
are BJ-isomorphic. If
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
is finite-dimensional with
\dim{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\geq 2
, then:

(1)

{\mathbb{F}}_{1}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}
,

(2)

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
are isomorphic as
C^{\ast}
-algebras.

Note that (1) may fail if
\dim{\mathcal{A}}_{1}=1
, see
[
10
, Example 2.2]
.

2.
Proof of main theorem

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra over the field
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
. We will denote the matrix block decomposition of a complex
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
by

(2.1)

\mathcal{M}_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{M}_{n_{\ell}}(\mathbb{C})

for some positive integers
n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}
. Similarly, the matrix block decomposition of a real
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by

(2.2)

\mathcal{M}_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{K}_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{M}_{n_{\ell}}(\mathbb{K}_{\ell})

where
\mathbb{K}_{i}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}\}
(every
{\mathcal{A}}
has such decomposition, see a book by Goodearl
[
5
]
for more information). If needed we will regard
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}({\mathbb{K}})
as the set of matrices corresponding to
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear operators acting on a right
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space of column vectors
{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
and with respect to the standard basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
. The standard pairing of vectors
x,y\in{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
will be denoted by

\langle x,y\rangle_{{\mathbb{K}}}:=y^{\ast}x,

where a row vector
y^{\ast}
is a conjugated transpose of
y
. Note that
{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
is an Euclidean space under the
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear inner product

\langle x,y\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}}:=\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}y^{*}x;&{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{R}}\\
y^{*}x;&{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{C}}\end{cases}.

For a vector
v
in a normed space
V
,

v^{\bot}=\{u;\;v\perp u\}\quad\text{ and }\quad{}^{\bot}v=\{u;\;u\perp v\},

are the
outgoing
and
incoming neighbourhood
of
v
, respectively. Also,
v
is
left-symmetric
if
v\perp x
implies
x\perp v
, or equivalently, if
v^{\bot}\subseteq{}^{\bot}v
. For
\mathcal{S}\subseteq V
, let

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}}:=\{v\in\mathcal{S};\;\;v^{\bot}\cap{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{}^{\bot}v\cap{\mathcal{S}}\}.

denote the set of all left-symmetric vectors relative to
\mathcal{S}
.
In particular, if
\mathcal{S}=V
, then
\mathcal{L}_{V}
is the set of all left-symmetric vectors. It follows from
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
that

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\bot}:=\bigcap_{v\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}}v^{\bot}
(the
nonpseudo-abelian summand
of a
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
) is the sum of
n_{i}
-by-
n_{i}
blocks with
n_{i}>1
, while
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\bot\bot}:=\bigcap_{v\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\bot}}v^{\bot}

(the
pseudo-abelian summand
of
{\mathcal{A}}
) is the sum of all
1
-by-
1
blocks in (
2.1
)–(
2.2
).
Observe that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\bot\bot}

is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra; abelian if and only if there are no quaternionic
1
-by-
1
blocks. We say
{\mathcal{A}}
is
pseudo-abelian
if
{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\bot\bot}
; and
not pseudo-abelian
if
{\mathcal{A}}\neq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\bot\bot}
.

For
A=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\ell}A_{k}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we define

M_{0}(A)=\{x\in\oplus_{k=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{K}_{k}^{n_{k}};\;\;\|Ax\|=\|A\|\cdot\|x\|\}.

Note that
M_{0}(A_{k}):=M_{0}(A)\cap(0\oplus\mathbb{K}_{k}^{n_{k}}\oplus 0)
is a
\mathbb{K}_{k}
vector subspace, see
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
. We will rely on the following Stampfli-Magajna-Bhatia-Šemrl classification of BJ orthogonality in
C^{*}
norm.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
11
]
, Lemma 2.1)
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{K}_{k})
be a
C^{*}
-algebra over
{\mathbb{F}}
and
A,B\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then,
B\in A^{\bot}
if and only if
\langle Ax,Bx\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}}=0
for some normalized
x\in M_{0}(A)
. Moreover, if
\|A_{i}\|<\max\{\|A_{k}\|;\;\;1\leq k\leq\ell\}=\|A\|
, then

A^{\bot}=\big(A_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus A_{i-1}\oplus 0_{n_{i}}\oplus A_{i+1}\oplus\dots\oplus A_{\ell}\big)^{\bot}.

We call
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
to be
smooth
if there does not exist
B\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that
B^{\bot}\subsetneq A^{\bot}
.
It was proved in
[
11
, Lemmas 2.6 and 2.5]
that
A=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\ell}A_{k}\in{\mathcal{A}}
is smooth if and only if
there exists exactly one index
j
such that
\|A_{j}\|=\|A\|
and
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}_{j}}M_{0}(A_{j})=1
. Moreover, if
A
is smooth and
x=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\ell}x_{k}\in M_{0}(A)
, then
x_{k}=0
for all
k\neq j
and

A^{\bot}=\big(0\oplus((A_{j}x_{j})x_{j}^{*})\oplus 0\big)^{\bot}.

By
[
11
, Lemma 2.5]
this definition also agrees with the classical definition of smoothness, i.e., of having a unique supporting functional. For example,
A=0\oplus E_{st}^{j}\oplus 0
are smooth elements for all matrix units
E_{st}^{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{n_{j}}(\mathbb{K}_{j})
.

Let us briefly discuss the key idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In
[
11
]
, the finite-dimensional pseudo-abelian
C^{*}
-algebras were classified, by finding the minimal number of smooth points
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}
required so that
\aleph:=|\{B^{\bot};\;B\in\mathcal{L}_{\bigcap_{k=1}^{s}A_{k}^{\bot}}\}|<\infty
. In that case,
\aleph
equals the number of matrix blocks and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}}{\mathcal{A}}=s+\aleph
. With a few additional lemmas the underlying field and the number of real, complex, and quaternionic blocks were also found. We will follow the same footsteps and focus on the minimal number of smooth points
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}
in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\bot}
which are required so that
\mathcal{L}_{\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}A_{i}^{\bot}}\neq\{0\}
. Let us start by proving the existence of such a finite tuple.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{n_{i}}({\mathbb{K}}_{i})
be a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra over
\mathbb{F}
with
n_{i}\geq 2
. Then, there exists finitely many smooth elements
A_{1},\dots,A_{t}
of

\mathcal{A}
such that the set
\mathcal{B}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{t}A_{i}^{\bot}
contains a non-zero left-symmetric element.
Moreover, for every (possibly infinite) collection of smooth points
(A_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
there exist
\mathbb{F}
-subspaces
\mathcal{S}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{n_{i}}({\mathbb{K}}_{i})
such that
\bigcap_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}^{\bot}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{S}_{i}
, and for any left-symmetric
Z=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}Z_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{\bigcap_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}^{\bot}}=\mathcal{L}_{\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{S}_{i}}
, we have
Z_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
.

Proof.

To prove the existence, we note that the standard matrix units
E_{st}^{1}\oplus 0
are smooth elements and, using Proposition
2.1
, we have

\bigcap\{(\mu E_{st}^{1}\oplus 0)^{\bot};\;\;s\neq t\text{ and }\mu\in\{\boldsymbol{1,i,j,k}\}\cap\mathbb{K}_{1}\}={\mathbb{K}}_{1}^{n_{1}}\oplus\big(\bigoplus_{i=2}^{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{n_{i}}({\mathbb{K}}_{i})\big).

The set on the right is a
C^{*}
algebra that contains pseudo-abelian direct summand and hence it does contain a non-zero left-symmetric point by
[
11
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Now, we fix arbitrary collection of smooth elements
(A_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of

\mathcal{A}
and let
\mathcal{B}=\bigcap_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}^{\bot}
. By definition of smoothness there exists a unique
j
and a normalized vector
u\in\mathbb{K}_{j}^{n_{j}}
such that
M_{0}(A_{\alpha})=0\oplus u{\mathbb{K}_{j}}\oplus 0
. So, if we define
\mathbb{F}
-linear functional
f_{\alpha}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathbb{F}}
by

f_{\alpha}\big(\oplus_{k=1}^{\ell}C_{k}\big)=\langle C_{j}u,(A_{\alpha})_{j}u\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}},

where
(A_{\alpha})_{j}
is the
j
-th summand of
A_{\alpha}
, then by Proposition
2.1
, we have
A_{\alpha}^{\bot}=\mathrm{ker}f_{\alpha}
. Hence,
\bigcap_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}^{\bot}=\bigcap_{\alpha}\mathrm{ker}f_{\alpha}
. Now,
\bigcap_{\alpha}\mathrm{ker}f_{\alpha}
is a subspace of
\mathcal{A}
, equal to
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{S}_{i}
for
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear subspace
\mathcal{S}_{i}
of
\mathcal{A}
. This proves the second part of the claim.

To prove the last part, let
B=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{S}_{i}
be a left-symmetric element. We need to prove
B_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}
. Without loss of generality,
i=1
. If
B_{1}=0
, then clearly

B_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}
. Let
B_{1}\neq 0
and
assume erroneously it is not left-symmetric in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, i.e., there is
C_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
such that
B_{1}\perp C_{1}
but
C_{1}\not\perp B_{1}
. By Proposition
2.1
,
B_{1}\perp C_{1}
implies there exists a normalized
x_{1}\in M_{0}(B_{1})\subseteq\mathbb{K}_{1}^{n_{1}}
such that

(2.3)

\langle B_{1}x_{1},C_{1}x_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}=0.

Then
C_{1}\not\perp B_{1}
implies that
C_{1}\neq 0
and for all normalized
y_{1}\in M_{0}(C_{1})
, we have

\langle C_{1}y_{1},B_{1}y_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}\neq 0.

Consider
C=C_{1}\oplus 0\in\mathcal{B}
and note that
M_{0}(C)=M_{0}(C_{1})\oplus 0
. Hence,
C\not\perp B
.
Let us prove
B\perp C
(this will then contradict the fact that
B\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}
, and finish the proof). There are two cases to consider.

Case 1:

\|B_{1}\|=\|B\|
. Let
x=x_{1}\oplus 0
, where
x_{1}\in M_{0}(B_{1})
satisfies (
2.3
).
By Proposition
2.1
,
M_{0}(B_{1})\oplus 0\subseteq M_{0}(B)
so
x\in M_{0}(B)
. Then,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\langle B_{i}x_{i},C_{i}x_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}=\langle B_{1}x_{1},C_{1}x_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}=0,
and hence
B\perp C
.

Case 2:

\|B_{1}\|<\|B\|
. Then
M_{0}(B)\subseteq 0\oplus\big(\bigoplus_{i=2}^{\ell}\mathbb{M}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{K}_{i})\big)
, so for any
x\in M_{0}(B)
, we have

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\langle B_{i}x_{i},C_{i}x_{i}\rangle=0+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{\ell}\langle B_{i}x_{i},0_{n_{i}}x_{i}\rangle=0.

\Box

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{A}}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{n_{k}}({\mathbb{K}}_{k})
with
n_{k}\geq 2
and let
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}\in{\mathcal{A}}
be smooth elements where
s
is the minimal number required to ensure that
\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}A_{i}^{\bot}
contains a non-zero left-symmetric element. Then,
s\geq 1
and there exists
j
with

(2.4)

M_{0}(A_{i})\subseteq 0\oplus\mathbb{K}_{j}^{n_{j}}\oplus 0\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq s.

Proof.

By
[
11
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have
s\geq 1
.
By Lemma
2.2
, for any non-zero left-symmetric
Z=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}Z_{i}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}A_{i}^{\bot}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{S}_{i}
, we have
Z_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}
. Let
j_{0}
be fixed such that
\|Z_{j_{0}}\|=\|Z\|
.
We claim that
Z_{i}=0
for each
i\neq j_{0}
. Assume otherwise. Then
Z\perp\big(0\oplus Z_{i}\oplus 0\big)\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}A_{i}^{\bot}
but these are not mutually orthogonal which contradicts the left-symmetricity of

Z\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}A_{i}^{\bot}
. Now, let
A_{i_{1}},\dots,A_{i_{m}}
be those among
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}
such that

(2.5)

M_{0}(A_{i_{p}})\subseteq 0\oplus\mathbb{K}_{j_{0}}^{n_{j_{0}}}\oplus 0\text{ for all }1\leq p\leq m.

It easily follows (c.f. the proof of Lemma
2.2
) that

\bigcap_{p=1}^{m}A_{i_{p}}^{\bot}=\mathcal{M}_{n_{1}}({\mathbb{K}}_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{M}_{n_{j_{0}-1}}(\mathbb{K}_{j_{0}-1})\oplus\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{n_{j_{0}+1}}(\mathbb{K}_{j_{0}+1})\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{M}_{n_{\ell}}({\mathbb{K}}_{\ell}).

Since
Z_{i}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathcal{S}_{i}}
, we easily see that
Z=0\oplus Z_{j_{0}}\oplus 0
is left-symmetric already in
\bigcap_{p=1}^{m}A_{i_{p}}^{\bot}
.
By the minimality of
s
, we have
m=s
and (
2.4
) follows from (
2.5
).

\Box

The next two technical lemmas will be needed in the proof of our main procedure to extract individual blocks.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&{\mathbb{K}}\\
{\mathbb{K}}&{\mathbb{K}}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{K}})
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V}}\neq\{0\}
. Moreover,

(2.6)

\mathcal{V}\cap\bigcap_{Z\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V}}}Z^{\bot}=\{0\}.

Proof.

We consider two cases.

Case 1:

{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{F}}\in\{{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}\}
. We first prove
A=E_{22}
is left-symmetric. Clearly,

A^{\bot}\cap{\mathcal{V}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&{\mathbb{K}}\\
{\mathbb{K}}&0\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Any matrix
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&c\\
d&0\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in A^{\bot}\cap{\mathcal{V}}
attains its norm on
x=e_{1}
or
x=e_{2}
(the standard basis of
{\mathbb{K}}^{2}
). In both cases, we have
\langle Bx,Ax\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}}=0
giving the desired
B\perp A=E_{22}
.

Next, we prove
A=E_{12}
is left symmetric. Here,
A^{\bot}\cap{\mathcal{V}}

consists of matrices of type
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
d&c\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Then,
\mathrm{Im}\,B\subseteq e_{2}{\mathbb{K}}
while
\mathrm{Im}\,A=e_{1}{\mathbb{K}}
.
So,
B\perp A
, as claimed. Finally,
A=E_{21}
is also left-symmetric because the adjoint operation which is a conjugate-linear isometry and hence a BJ-isomorphism, swaps
E_{12}
and
E_{21}
and leaves the
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear subspace
{\mathcal{V}}
invariant. This proves (
2.6
) because we have

\mathcal{V}\cap E_{22}^{\bot}\cap E_{12}^{\bot}\cap E_{21}^{\bot}=\{0\}.

Case 2:

{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{H}}
. We first prove that
A=E_{22}
is left-symmetric. Its outgoing neighbourhood, relative to
{\mathcal{V}}
, consists of matrices of the form

B=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\
b&{\bf q}\tau\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\begin{pmatrix}0&{\mathbb{K}}\\
{\mathbb{K}}&\boldsymbol{q}{\mathbb{R}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

where
\boldsymbol{q}\in{\mathbb{K}}
is a purely imaginary unimodular number and
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
. By homogeneity of BJ orthogonality we can assume
\tau=1
or
\tau=0
.
If
\tau=1
, then, in computing the norm of

B
, one first writes

B^{\ast}B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\overline{b}\\
\overline{a}&\overline{\bf q}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right).\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&a\\
b&{\bf q}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}|b|^{2}&\bar{b}{\bf q}\\
\bar{\bf q}b&|a|^{2}+1\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and then embeds its coefficients (notice that only
z=\overline{b}{\bf q}
and its conjugate can be nonreal) isometrically into
{\mathbb{C}}
where singular value decomposition is easily available. It yields
\|B\|^{2}=\frac{\sqrt{\left(|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}+1\right)^{2}-4|a|^{2}|b|^{2}}+|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}+1}{2}

and
B

achieves its norm on

x=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\boldsymbol{q}\lambda\\
\overline{\boldsymbol{q}}b\boldsymbol{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, with
\lambda:=(\|B\|^{2}-1-|a|^{2})
.
One can easily see that

\langle Bx,Ax\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}}=\mathrm{Re}((Ax)^{\ast}(Bx))=\mathrm{Re}((1+\lambda)\overline{\overline{\boldsymbol{q}}b\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot b\boldsymbol{q})=(1+\lambda)\,\mathrm{Re}(\overline{b\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}\cdot b\boldsymbol{q})=0

because conjugation of a purely imaginary
\boldsymbol{q}\in{\mathbb{K}}
is again purely imaginary.
So
B\perp A
.
If
\tau=0
we get the second typical class of matrices in
A^{\bot}\cap{\mathcal{V}}
, namely
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&a\\
b&0\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in A^{\bot}
. This is treated as in Case 1 and again gives
B\perp A
.

We now prove that
E_{12}
is also left-symmetric. In this case, the first class of typical matrices in
A^{\bot}\cap{\mathcal{V}}
take the form
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\boldsymbol{q}\\
b&a\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for some purely imaginary unimodular number
\boldsymbol{q}
. Following the previous footsteps we easily compute that

\|B\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{(|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}+1)^{2}-4|b|^{2}}+|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}+1\right)

and that
B
achieves its norm on
x=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a\lambda\\
\overline{a}ba\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, with
\lambda:=(\|B\|^{2}-1-|a|^{2})
.
Clearly,
Bx=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\boldsymbol{q}\bar{a}ba\\
\ast\end{smallmatrix}\right)

and hence
\langle Bx,Ax\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}=\mathrm{Re}(\overline{\overline{a}ba}\cdot\boldsymbol{q}\bar{a}ba)=0
.
The second class of matrices from
A^{\perp}\cap{\mathcal{V}}
take the form
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
b&a\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)=e_{2}(e_{1}\overline{b}+e_{2}\overline{a})^{\ast}
, attain their norm on
x=(e_{1}\overline{b}+e_{2}\overline{a})
, and map it into
Bx=e_{2}
. Since
Ax=E_{12}x=e_{1}\overline{a}
we get
B\perp A
.

Finally, the left symmetricity of
Z\in\{\boldsymbol{q}E_{22},\boldsymbol{q}E_{12},\boldsymbol{q}E_{21}\}
for arbitrary unimodular purely imaginary number
\boldsymbol{q}
, follows as in Case 1 by using BJ-isomorphisms induced by isometries
X\mapsto UXV^{\ast}
or
X\mapsto X^{\ast}
. Hence, (
2.6
) holds.
\Box

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{K}
be a proper
\mathbb{F}
-subspace. Then,
the subspace
{\mathcal{T}}:=\mathcal{V}E_{11}+\sum_{(i,j)\neq(1,1)}({\mathbb{K}}E_{ij})
of
\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
contains a non-zero left-symmetric element if and only if
n=2
and
{\mathcal{V}}=0
.

Proof.

The ‘only if’ part follows from Lemma
2.4
. We prove the ‘if’ part. Let
A\in{\mathcal{T}}
be left-symmetric and
\|A\|=1
. We divide the proof into several cases.

Case 1
.
\dim_{{\mathbb{K}}}M_{0}(A)\geq 2
. Then there exists a normalized
x\in M_{0}(A)
with

Notice for a

{\mathcal{A}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{n_{i}}({\mathbb{K}}_{i})

Corollary 2.6
.

such that

contains a non-zero left-symmetric point, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{A^{\bot}}\neq\{0\}
. Then there exists

with

,
{\mathbb{K}}_{j}=\mathbb{F}
such that
M_{0}(A)\subseteq 0\oplus{\mathbb{K}}_{j}^{n_{j}}\oplus 0=0\oplus\mathbb{F}^{2}\oplus 0.
Moreover, in this case

A^{\bot}\cap\bigg(\bigcap_{Z\in\mathcal{L}_{A^{\bot}}}Z^{\bot}\bigg)=(0\oplus\mathcal{M}_{n_{j}}({\mathbb{K}}_{j})\oplus 0)^{\bot}.

Proof.

Since
A
is a smooth element, there exists
j
such that
M_{0}(A)\subseteq 0\oplus{\mathbb{K}}_{j}^{n_{j}}\oplus 0
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{K}}_{j}}M_{0}(A)=1
. Hence, there exists

such that
M_{0}(A)=0\oplus y_{j}{\mathbb{K}}_{j}\oplus 0
and so, if
y:=0\oplus y_{j}\oplus 0
, then, by the first part of Proposition
2.1
,

Ayy^{\ast}\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
share the same outgoing neighborhoods (i.e.,
(Ayy^{\ast})^{\bot}=A^{\bot}
).
Hence we can assume with no loss of generality that
A=xy^{\ast}\in 0\oplus{\mathcal{M}}_{n_{j}}({\mathbb{K}}_{j})\oplus 0
for some column vectors
x,y\in{\mathbb{K}}_{j}^{n_{j}}
with
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
. Choose unitary
U,V
so that
Ux=e_{1}
and
Vy=e_{1}
; since

is isometry, hence a BJ-isomorphism, we can assume that
A=0\oplus(e_{1}e_{1}^{\ast})\oplus 0
.

If

, then, by Lemma
2.5
,

has no non-zero left symmetric point, so

is not possible. Likewise if

and
({\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{K}})
equals

or
({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{H}})
(here, besides Lemma

So, the only possibility left is

and

. For this case, if

,
it can be non-zero only in

-th summand. Hence, the proof follows by Lemma

Finally, we give a procedure to extract particular blocks of size
n
-by-
n
for
n\geq 2
in a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra. Let
\mathrm{sm}({\mathcal{A}})
denote the set of all smooth points in
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Given an integer
{\kappa}
and a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
, we define

\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}(\mathcal{B})=\{\boldsymbol{X}:=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{\kappa}\}\subseteq\mathrm{sm}(\mathcal{B});\;\;\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{X}^{\bot}}:=\mathcal{L}_{\bigcap_{i=1}^{\kappa}X_{i}^{\bot}}\neq\{0\}\}.

We remark that, if
\mathcal{B}
is finite-dimensional, then, by Lemma
2.2
, there always exists
{\kappa}
such that
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}(\mathcal{B})\neq\emptyset
. In the lemma below, the minimal such
\kappa
is denoted by
s
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra with no pseudo-abelian direct summand
and let
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}\in\mathrm{sm}({\mathcal{A}})
be such that
\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}A_{i}^{\bot}
contains a non-zero left-symmetric point with
s
being the minimal integer to allow this. Consider the following recursive procedure:

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle:=\{\boldsymbol{A}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{s}\}\},

\displaystyle S_{m+1}

\displaystyle:=\big\{\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathcal{P}_{s}({\mathcal{A}});\;\;\exists\boldsymbol{Y}\in S_{m}\text{ such that }|\boldsymbol{X}\cap\boldsymbol{Y}|\geq 1\},

and define
\mathfrak{S}=\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\bigcup_{m}S_{m}}\boldsymbol{X}^{\bot}
. Then, there exists index
j
such that
M_{0}(A_{i})\subseteq 0\oplus{\mathbb{K}}_{j}^{n_{j}}\oplus 0
for all
1\leq i\leq s
and

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}\cap\bigg(\bigcap_{Z\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{S}}}Z^{\bot}\bigg)\cap\bigg(\bigcap_{Z\in\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{A}^{\bot}}}Z^{\bot}\bigg)=(0\oplus\mathcal{M}_{n_{j}}(\mathbb{K}_{j})\oplus 0)^{\bot}.

Remark 2.8
.

In the above procedure we have
s\geq 1
; if
s=1
, then
S_{m}=S_{0}=\{\{A_{1}\}\}
for all
m
and
\mathfrak{S}=A_{1}^{\bot}
. Recall also that the right-hand side,
(0\oplus\mathcal{M}_{n_{j}}(\mathbb{K}_{j})\oplus 0)^{\bot}
coincides with all the elements of
\mathcal{A}
which vanish at
j
-summand.

Proof.

The case
s=1
was proved in Corollary
2.6
. In the sequel we assume
s\geq 2
. We note that by Lemma
2.3
, there exists index
j
such that

(2.8)

M_{0}(A_{i})\subseteq 0\oplus{\mathbb{K}}_{j}^{n_{j}}\oplus 0\qquad\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq s.

Let
X\in\boldsymbol{X}\in S_{1}
. So, there exists
A_{i}\in\boldsymbol{A}\in S_{0}
and smooth elements
X_{3},\dots,X_{s}
such that
X^{\bot}\cap A_{i}^{\bot}\cap\bigcap_{i=3}^{s}X_{i}^{\bot}
contains a non-zero left-symmetric point. Now, by (
2.8
) and Lemma
2.3
, we get

M_{0}(X)\subseteq 0\oplus\mathbb{K}_{j}^{n_{j}}\oplus 0.

Recursively, this holds for all
X\in\boldsymbol{X}\in S_{m}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Consequently, in (
2.7
), the set
\mathfrak{S}=\bigcap_{\boldsymbol{X}\in\bigcup_{m}S_{m}}\boldsymbol{X}^{\bot}
contains all elements in
{\mathcal{A}}
which vanish at
j
-th summand, that is, it contains the set on the right-hand side. As such, if
Z\in\mathfrak{S}

or
Z\in\boldsymbol{A}^{\bot}

is (relative) left-symmetric, then, by Lemma
2.2
, it can be non-zero only in
j
-th summand. Then, the left-hand side of (

To prove they are equal we only have to show that the
j
-summand of the left-hand side vanishes. The basic idea is to start with a given
s
-tuple
\boldsymbol{A}
and modify it with a
BJ-isomorphism
that fixes one element within an
s
-tuple. Then keep repeating it. These recursively obtained
s
-tuples will have the same properties as the original one. Moreover, the fixed member achieves its norm only in the
j
-th block so the whole
s
-tuple will achieve the norm only in
j
-th block. So without loss of generality, we can assume
{\mathcal{A}}=\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
consists of a single summand (note
n\geq 2
because
{\mathcal{A}}
does not contain pseudo-abelian summand).
We will use BJ-isomorphisms induced by (conjugate)linear isometries (i)
X\mapsto UXV^{\ast}
for unitary
U,V
or (ii)

.

We will also use the following two facts:

xy^{\ast}\perp B\Longleftrightarrow\langle By,x\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}}=0.

(2.9)

\langle x+y\mu,z\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}=0\hbox{ for all unimodular }\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}\Longleftrightarrow\langle x,z\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}=0\text{ and }\langle y,z\rangle_{\mathbb{K}}=0.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
. Let

\mathcal{A}_{1}
be finite-dimensional with
\dim{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\geq 2
. Then, by

7

\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\mathcal{A}_{2}
. If

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}

{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
are not pseudo-abelian
C^{*}

Without loss of generality, let
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell_{1}}\mathcal{M}_{n_{i}^{1}}(\mathbb{K}_{i}^{1})
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\ell_{2}}\mathcal{M}_{n_{j}^{2}}(\mathbb{K}_{j}^{2})
.
Now since
\phi
is BJ-isomorphism, we have

(2.14)

\phi(\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}^{\bot})=\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}^{\bot}\quad\text{ and }\quad\phi(\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}^{\bot\bot})=\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{2}}^{\bot\bot}.

Therefore, by (

),
\phi
induces BJ-isomorphism between pseudo-abelian summands of

{\mathcal{A}}_{1}

\mathcal{B}_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}}^{\bot}

-subalgebras. Using Lemmas
2.2

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
be a BJ-isomorphism between
C^{*}
-algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
over the fields
{\mathbb{F}}_{1}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
, with
2\leq\dim{\mathcal{A}}_{1}<\infty
. Then, by identifying
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}={\mathcal{A}}_{2}
,
\phi
permutes the minimal ideals which share the same centralizer and dimension.

Proof.

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we already showed this for nonpseudo-abelian summands of
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
(that is
\phi
induces a permutation among the blocks of size bigger than one in (
2.1
)–(
2.2
)). Furthermore, we also showed that pseudo-abelian summand of
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
is mapped onto pseudo-abelian summand of
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
. So, without loss of generality we can assume
{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
are pseudo-abelian
C^{\ast}
-algebras. We now give a procedure (in parallel to Lemma
2.7
) which will extract all elements within a single block of a finite-dimensional pseudo-abelian
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
and will be defined completely in terms of BJ orthogonality.

To start, let
s\geq 0
be the minimal number such that there exists non-zero left symmetric points
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}
such that
\{A^{\bot};\;\;A\in\mathcal{L}_{\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}A_{i}^{\bot}}\}
is a finite set (see
[
11
, Lemma 3.5]
). If
s=0
, then by
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]

{\mathcal{A}}={\mathbb{F}}^{n}
and left-symmetric elements with the same outgoing neighbourhoods constitute the same block.

Assume
s\geq 1
. Then,
{\mathcal{A}}
is a real
C^{*}
-algebra and
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathbb{R}}^{r}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}^{c}\oplus{\mathbb{H}}^{h}
with
(c,h)\neq(0,0)
, each left-symmetric
A_{i}
belongs to one of the complex or quaternionic blocks, and
\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}A_{i}^{\bot}
is isometrically isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{r+c+h}
(see
[
11
, Lemmas 3.2 and 3.5]
).
In this case, we remove
A_{1}
and collect all left-symmetric
X
such that
\{A^{\bot};\;\;A\in\mathcal{L}_{X^{\bot}\cap\big(\bigcap_{i=2}^{s}A_{i}^{\bot}\big)}\}
is a finite set.
It follows from
[
11
, Lemma 3.5]
that all these
X
share the same block with
A_{1}
. Remove, if possible,
A_{i}
for
i\geq 2
such that
\{A^{\bot};\;\;A\in\mathcal{L}_{X^{\bot}\cap A_{1}^{\bot}\cap(\bigcap_{j\not\in\{1,i\}}A_{j}^{\bot})}\}
is a finite set. If, for no
X
from our collection, there is such
A_{i}
, then
A_{1}
belongs to a complex block by
[
11
, Lemma 3.4]
. Otherwise, all these
A_{i}
share the same (quaternionic) block with
A_{1}
, again by
[
11
, Lemma 3.5]
. Collect all such
A_{i}
and the corresponding
X
obtained when the above procedure is started with one of them in place of
A_{1}
. It is an exercise that this collection coincides with all the non-zero elements from the block containing
A_{1}
(see
[
11
, Lemma 3.4 and its proof]
).

After applying this procedure we get a collection
\Omega
consisting of all the non-zero elements from one block of
{\mathcal{A}}
. To extract other blocks, we apply it again on a
C^{*}
-subalgebra
\Omega^{\bot}
and proceed recursively.

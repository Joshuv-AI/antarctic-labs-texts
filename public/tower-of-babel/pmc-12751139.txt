Tracheobronchial adenoid cystic carcinoma: a two-center retrospective analysis of clinical features and survival outcomes
Authors: Qinyan Hong, Jun Teng, Heng Zou, Lei Li, Shuiping Chen, Yi Luo, Yuan Li, Nan Zhang, Hongwu Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTracheobronchial adenoid cystic carcinoma (TACC) is an uncommon gland-type tumor. While prior studies have addressed its clinical features and prognosis, evidence from larger cohorts remains scarce.MethodsTACC patients treated at Dongzhimen Hospital (DZM) and Emergency General Hospital (EG) between 2010 and 2023 were enrolled. Survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan–Meier method before and after propensity score matching (PSM). The key variables were screened by LASSO regression, and predictive and interaction analysis was done by multifactor Cox regression and accelerated failure time (AFT) models. Overall survival (OS) was the study endpoint.ResultsThe median age of 169 TACC patients (EG 144, DZM 25) was 47.8 ± 13.2 years, 50.9% were female, and 79.9% of patients had not undergone surgery. The median follow-up was 67.5 months (interquartile range [IQR] 42.2–105.8). Survival at 5, 10, and 15 years was 68.0%, 32.4%, and 17.1%, respectively. The median survival time was 88 months. Survival was extended by surgery combined with PDT, but the interaction was nonsignificant.ConclusionThis study identified ITE as the independent prognostic factor. Introduction

Tracheobronchial adenoid cystic carcinoma (TACC) is an uncommon malignancy originating from submucosal seromucous glands, accounting for less than 1% of all respiratory system tumors [1–3]. TACC is a low-grade malignant tumor but presents aggressive behavior despite its slow progression [4]. TACC predominantly originates from the seromucous glands lining the submucosal layer of the central airways. The usual symptoms of TACC include progressive dyspnea, cough, and hemoptysis [5, 6]. Since TACC is clinically rare and the symptoms are nonspecific, it is highly apt to misdiagnose, which results in delayed treatment [4]. Although no residual tumor (R0) resection is considered a curative treatment, it is rather difficult to achieve, as tumors are insidious and diagnosed when they have extensive airway involvement [7].

Published clinical treatment studies mainly focus on the prognosis and potential influencing factors following R0 resection and adjuvant therapy after microscopic residual tumor (R1) resection2, “Conclusions”, 7, 8. The effectiveness of radiotherapy compared with surgery for treating TACC has remained controversial [5]. In addition, most available data were derived from earlier studies [3, 7, 9, 10], and other clinical treatments, such as immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and bronchoscopic interventions for TACC, remain insufficiently characterized. Finally, due to the rarity of TACC, the retrospective literature largely consists of case reports and single-center series of varying sizes [2, 11–15]. Although some single-institution studies with larger cohorts have reported survival outcomes and analyzed prognostic factors such as surgical margins and pathological extension, comprehensive evidence across multiple centers remains scarce [2, 12]. Further investigations are therefore warranted to refine our understanding of its clinical characteristics, therapeutic strategies, and survival outcomes.

Based on this, we aim to update the understanding of TACC by presenting two-center retrospective data from 2010 to 2023, describing the clinical features, treatment regimens, and overall survival (OS) of TACC and further analyzing factors associated with its prognosis.

Methods

Study design and patients

Patients with pathologically confirmed TACC who received treatment at Dongzhimen Hospital, Beijing University of Chinese Medicine (DZM) and Emergency General Hospital (EG) between January 2010 and June 2023 were retrospectively screened and included in the study. The exclusion criteria were: (1) tumors not involving any of the eight zones of the central airway [16]; (2) central airway involvement by ACC metastasized from another primary site; (3) other histological tumor types; (4) missing survival data. This study was approved by the Ethics Committees of DZM (No. 2024DZMEC-039–02) and EG (No. K24-24). In this retrospective study, the Ethics Committee waived some requirements for informed consent. Surviving patients signed written informed consent.

Data and definitions

The age and sex of patients, initial symptoms, smoking status, family history of tumors, and history of primary treatment (surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, bronchoscopic intervention, photodynamic therapy (PDT)) were collected through the electronic treatment registration systems of the two hospitals. The period from the onset of the first symptom to the first doctor visit (Time 1) and the subsequent period from the first doctor visit to pathological diagnosis (Time 2) were recorded to analyze their impact on the prognosis of TACC.

Treatment decisions were made at the discretion of the attending physician. As this was a retrospective study, the specific criteria guiding the treatment choice for each patient could not be fully ascertained. The PDT procedure was consistent with the approach disclosed in the published literature [17]. HiPorfin was administered as the photosensitizer at a dose of 2 mg/kg, followed by illumination of the lesion site via bronchoscopic placement of an optical fiber 48 h after injection. The light source was set to a wavelength of 630 nm, a power density of 100 mW/cm2, and a total energy density of 150–200 J/cm2. PDT was administered over 2–3 consecutive days, with each illumination lasting 3–5 min and separated by 1–3 min intervals; the energy density of subsequent illuminations did not exceed that of the initial fractionated irradiation.

Owing to its low incidence, there is no uniform staging system for TACC. According to the method for evaluating malignant central airway stenosis proposed by Chinese experts [18], we recorded initial tumor extension (ITE) in the central airway (zones I to VIII), the degree of initial wall invasion (mixed and nonmixed), and the initial degree of airway stenosis (grades 1 to 5) using bronchoscopy or chest CT as an alternative to T staging. We also noted regional lymph nodes and distant metastasis.

The endpoint was defined as OS, which denotes the duration from pathological confirmation of TACC to death or the last follow-up. The date of pathological diagnosis was obtained from the electronic medical records of the two hospitals, and the date of death was collected by telephone follow-up or from the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention. The last follow-up ended on December 31, 2023.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables were described with mean ± standard deviation and analyzed using either the equal variance t test or the Mann–Whitney U test according to their distribution characteristics. Categorical data were presented as percentages, and the choice between the chi-square test and Fisher's exact test was made according to sample size considerations. The LASSO regression method screened critical variables for the multivariable Cox proportional hazards model, and Model 1 was established to avoid overfitting. Model 1 independent analyses were performed by multivariate Cox regression and accelerated failure time (AFT) modeling to study the relationship between variables and TACC prognosis [19]. Unadjusted univariate Cox regression and Kaplan–Meier analyses were used to preliminarily screen out the cutoff values of variables with a P < 0.1, which were used to construct Model 2 for checking Model 1 robustness.

A propensity score matching (PSM) strategy was used to match key variables validated by preliminary analyses in a 1:1 ratio using the nearest-neighbor method, with a caliper value set at 0.1 and without a replacement strategy. Survival curves before and after PSM were constructed using the Kaplan–Meier method. Survival rates and median survival times were calculated at 5, 10, and 15 years. This study explored interactions by multivariate Cox regression. Finally, potential collinearity between the variables and interaction terms was avoided by centering.

All analyses were performed using R software version 4.4.0 (https://www.r-project.org/) and RStudio version 2024.04.0 + 735 (https://posit.co/). The R packages used included readr, dplyr, survival, survminer, glmnet, MatchIt, tableone, ggplot2, forestplot, interactions, and car. All tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

One hundred sixty-nine patients with single primary TACC were included (EG 144, DZM 25) (Fig. S1). The mean age of the patients was 47.8 ± 13.2 years; 50.9% were female, and 79.9% had not undergone surgery. The initial symptoms were mainly sputum production (46.2%) and shortness of breath (34.9%). TACC was distributed primarily in Zone III (49.1%) and Zone II (43.8%) and most frequently caused mild-to-moderate airway stenosis (grades 1 to 3, 62.1%). The degree of initial wall invasion was predominantly mixed (73.4%) (Table S1). The 5-, 10-, and 15-year survival rates for TACC were 68.0%, 32.4%, and 17.1%, respectively, with a median survival time of 88 months (Fig. 1A). Patients from both centers did not show significant differences in age, sex, smoking status, family history of tumors, tumor characteristics, treatment history, and survival (all P > 0.05; Table 1; Fig. 1B). The inverse Kaplan–Meier method indicated a median follow-up of 67.5 months (interquartile range [IQR], 42.2–105.8).

Prognostic influence

LASSO regression was applied to avoid overfitting, and the tenfold cross-validation selected variables (Fig. S2). Four key variables were significantly associated with prognosis: ITE, surgery, PDT, and immunotherapy (λ = lambda. min). The variables selected for Model 1 met the proportional hazards and collinearity requirements for Cox regression (Fig. S2-S3, Table S2). The results of multivariate Cox regression analyses showed that ITE (HR: 1.28, 95% CI 1.12–1.47; P = 0.000), surgery (HR: 0.36, 95% CI 0.20–0.64; P = 0.000), and PDT (HR: 0.46, 95% CI 0.22–0.97; P = 0.042) significantly affected prognosis (Fig. 1C). In the AFT model, Weibull distribution was used (Table S3), the results were consistent with the multivariate Cox regression analysis. ITE (TR: 0.88, 95% CI 0.81–0.94; P = 0.000) was identified as an independent risk factor, while surgery (TR: 1.72, 95% CI 1.32–2.26; P P = 0.000) and PDT (TR: 1.51, 95% CI 1.05–2.18;P = 0.027) were independent protective factors (Fig. 1D).

For sensitivity analysis, Model 2 was constructed (Fig. S4, Table S4). In this model (Fig. S5, Tables S2 and S3), univariate Cox regression and Kaplan–Meier analysis were used to screen out seven variables with P < 0.1 (Model 1, Time 1, Time 2, and Tracheoscopic Tx). The sensitivity analysis was consistent with the primary results (Fig. S6).

Subgroup analyses before and after PSM

Subgroup analysis included surgery, PDT, and ITE (E1 [limited: zone = 1], E2 [diffuse: zone > 1]). When compared with patients who did not undergo surgery or PDT, those who did had significantly higher 5-, 10-, and 15-year survival rates and longer median survival times (P < 0.05, P < 0.05) (Fig. 2A, C). When compared with E1, E2 had significantly lower survival rates and shorter median survival times (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2E). Sensitivity analysis was conducted using PSM, with all standardized mean differences (SMDs) < 0.001 (Table S5). The results of the subgroup analysis after PSM concurred with the primary analysis (Fig. 2B, D, F).

Interaction analysis

Model 3 was constructed based on the variables significantly related to the prognosis of TACC and their interaction terms (Fig. S7, Table S2). In Model 3, ITE (HR: 1.25, 95% CI 1.07–1.47; P < 0.05) and surgery (HR: 0.28, 95% CI 0.11–0.75; P < 0.05) remained significant and independent prognostic factors, while for PDT (HR: 0.43, 95% CI 0.10–1.81; P = 0.252) was no longer significantly (Fig. 3A). Although there was no significant interaction, patients treated with combination surgery and PDT were more likely had a higher probability of survival (Fig. 3B). As a sensitivity analysis, Model 4 avoided the potential collinearity between variables and interaction terms by centering (Fig. S8, Table S2). The results were consistent with the primary analysis (Fig. 3C, D).

Discussion

In this retrospective study, the efficacy of conventional treatments, including surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy in TACC, had been retrospectively analyzed and compared. Other treatment modalities, such as targeted therapies, immunotherapy, bronchoscopic interventions, and PDT-based combination therapies, have also been evaluated; however, they have received relatively little attention in previous studies. This study reported the clinical features and survival data of TACC, identified ITE as an independent prognostic factor, and underlined the significance of surgery in enhancing survival. The tendency of combining surgery with PDT implies that it may be an effective treatment; however, further validation is needed.

In the present study, the average age of TACC patients was 47.8 ± 13.2 years, and there was no gender predilection, which is similar to results seen previously [10, 15]. We also did not find a statistically significant correlation of TACC occurrence with smoking or family history for tumors. The most common symptom was sputum production, followed by shortness of breath and hemoptysis (Table 1). Other symptoms such as obstructive pneumonia, neck swelling, and hoarseness have also been documented in the literature [5]. Clinical presentation of TACC largely depends on tumor location and distal obstruction [20]. Some patients with TACC may also have no symptoms as they are found incidentally in check-ups. Our study noted shorter median Time 1 and Time 2, possibly attributed to the increased use of low-dose chest CT scans [21]. However, due to notable fluctuations in Time 1 and Time 2, challenges related to misdiagnosis and treatment delays in TACC persist. Further research is warranted to establish a 'safe' time threshold for TACC.

Airway glands are generally concentrated in the trachea and large bronchi, and hence TACC predominantly involves the central airway, mostly in zones III and II, followed by zones IV, V, and VII, and did not differ markedly from previous reports [4, 18]. Patients with limited (E1) TACC had a significantly better prognosis compared to patients with a diffuse (E2) distribution (P < 0.05, Fig. 2F). Relative risk increased 28% for each additional zone, and median survival time decreased 12% (Fig. 1C, D). There was no significant relation between the degree of the initial wall invasion based on the tumor and the OS (Table S4, Fig. S4 G, H). However, the previous reports have suggested that lateral invasion can be predictive of recurrence in TACC [22].

Consistent with two previous studies [10, 23], we did not find a significant prognostic association with lymph node status (Table S4, Fig. S4 J). However, we did not provide the lymph node ratio or the number of lymph nodes examined [24]. Therefore, this result should be interpreted with caution. Theoretically, distant metastases are associated with the prognosis of malignant tumors. However, in this study, distant metastases did not affect TACC prognosis (Table S4, Fig. S4 I). This may be related to the fact that most metastases (64%) appeared only in the lungs and were slow-growing [25], and the distant metastases were unknown in some patients. The predictive value of distant metastases requires further exploration.

Consistent with the previous studies (Table 2), surgery was strongly associated with improved prognosis (Fig. 2A). In our cohort, the surgical proportion was relatively low, likely because DZM and EG are not thoracic surgical centers, leading to underrepresentation of resected cases [26]. This limitation also allowed evaluation of nonresected TACC. Despite technical challenges, surgery remained effective, showing a 64% reduction in death risk and a 72% increase in median survival (Fig. 1C, D) [7]. However, complete (R0) resection is often unachievable due to infiltrative growth, and extensive resection increases complications and mortality. Postoperative radiotherapy is commonly recommended for R1 resection, but its survival benefit remains controversial [2, 3, 8, 15, 23, 27]. Ning et al. reported that when R0 resection is excessively risky or may cause fatal complications, R1 resection followed by adjuvant therapy can still provide patients with a promising prognosis, whereas pathological extraluminal invasion represents an adverse prognostic factor [2]. In our cohort, radiotherapy did not provide a significant advantage (TR: 1.24, 95% CI 0.93–1.67; P = 0.142) (Table S4; Figure S4K), which may be partly explained by the fact that many patients who received radiotherapy in our series did not undergo surgery, and definitive radiotherapy alone might not substantially improve survival.

Systemic therapy is very important. However, no effective treatment programs have been found. The basic strategy for metastatic and recurrent TACC cases is chemotherapy, including platinum-based monotherapies and combination chemotherapy regimens. However, the effective remission rate is low, the time of response is short, and the toxicity is relatively high [28]. What is more, the lack of second-line treatment options in cases of disease progression has always been a challenge. The defects in immunotherapy and targeted treatment also face a predicament. To date, the effect of immune checkpoint inhibitor treatments in TACC remains poor overall. Maybe it is because of the low tumor mutational burden or the expression of PD-1/PD-L1 in TACC [29]. The possible direction of future research may be the development of inhibitors of PD-L2 or CXCR4 [30]. Targeted therapies against MYB and its downstream oncogenic activation pathways are still being explored, although they appear promising [31, 32]. Our study showed no survival benefit for chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy (Fig. S4L-N, Table S4).

The role of bronchoscopy in local treatment for TACC should be considered. Our study focused on OS and did not assess short-term efficacy. Nevertheless, it should be noted that 69.9% of the patients in our study cohort had significant airway stenosis (grade 3 to 5) (Table S1). Another essential outcome indicator for evaluating the efficacy of TACC treatment is the rapid and effective relief of acute respiratory symptoms, particularly dyspnea. Earlier studies suggest bronchoscopic intervention can rapidly relieve acute respiratory symptoms by creating a safe, unobstructed airway [33]. Also, bronchoscopic intervention can be a bridging treatment for surgery and radiotherapy [34]. For instance, airway stenting can effectively relieve fibrotic stenosis following radiotherapy for TACC [35]. Our study did not find that stenting adversely affects long-term prognosis (Fig. S6); nevertheless, the risk of complications should always be considered [36]. In our study, PDT was associated with a 54% reduction in the risk of death and a 51% increase in median survival time (Fig. 1C, D). However, given that only 22.5% of patients in our cohort received PDT, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Interaction analyses suggested that the combination of PDT and surgery might improve patient survival (Fig. 3B, D), but neither PDT alone nor its interaction term with surgery reached statistical significance, likely due to the limited sample size (Fig. 3A, C). Previous studies have reported that PDT can reduce tumor burden, alleviate symptoms, improve quality of life, and serve as a bridging strategy to increase the likelihood of surgery and provide opportunities for systemic therapy [37–39]. Taken together, the role of PDT in TACC management remains to be further explored, and our findings provide tentative evidence for its possible clinical value. Future studies involving larger, prospective cohorts are needed to further evaluate the role of PDT in TACC management.

There are several limitations to this study. First, its retrospective design and reliance on medical records may introduce biases and unmeasured confounders. Second, the representativeness of our cohort is limited: the proportion of surgical cases was lower than in previous reports, likely because both centers are not thoracic surgical units, and the number of patients without PDT was relatively high. These factors may restrict the generalizability of our findings. Third, survival assessment and treatment evaluation were not comprehensive, as we only reported OS; data on disease-free survival (DFS), recurrence, treatment details, and socioeconomic factors were unavailable. DFS, however, has been reported in other studies, such as Zhao et al., providing a more complete perspective on patient outcomes [12]. Nevertheless, this study represents one of the largest reported TACC cohorts and provides valuable insights into its clinical management.

Conclusions

In this two-center retrospective study, we characterized the understanding of the clinical features, treatment, and prognosis of TACC. The results show that ITE can be regarded as an independent prognostic factor of TACC among different aspects describing the initial size of the primary tumor. In the treatment, surgery is a critical factor that may influence the prognosis of TACC. Additionally, PDT and its combinations with the surgical treatment may prolong the survival of the patient.

Safety for the environment of a feed additive consisting of semduramicin sodium (Aviax 5%) for chickens for fattening (Phibro Animal Health s.a.)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Vasileios Bampidis, Giovanna Azimonti, Maria de Lourdes Bastos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Secundino López Puente, Francesca Marcon, Baltasar Mayo, Alena Pechová, Mariana Petkova, Fernando Ramos, Roberto Edoardo Villa, Ruud Woutersen, Ivana Teodorovic, Jordi Call Tarres, Maria Vittoria Vettori
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety for the environment of the coccidiostat semduramicin sodium (Aviax 5%) when used in feed for chickens for fattening. The additive Aviax 5% is intended to be used as a coccidiostat in feed for chickens for fattening at a level of 20–25 mg semduramicin sodium/ kg complete feed. The safety and efficacy of the additive was evaluated by the FEEDAP Panel in 2018 and 2022. In those opinions it was concluded that the additive is safe for the target species and consumer, while no conclusions could be made on the irritancy of Aviax 5% to skin and eye and on the potential for dermal and respiratory sensitisation. Regarding the safety for the environment, the Panel concluded that a risk for the terrestrial compartment could not be excluded. Based on new data submitted, no risk is expected from the use of the additive in animal nutrition at the proposed conditions of use for the terrestrial compartment. The previous conclusions of the FEEDAP Panel on the safety of the additive for the environment can be updated as follows: the use of semduramicin sodium from Aviax 5% in feed for chickens for fattening up to 25 mg/kg complete feed does not pose a risk for groundwater nor for aquatic, terrestrial and sediment compartments.Keywords: chickens for fattening, coccidiostats and histomonostats, environment, safety, semduramicin sodium

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference as provided by the requestor

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition and, in particular, Article 9 defines the terms of the authorisation by the Commission.

The applicant, Phibro Animal Health s.a., is seeking a Community authorisation of Semduramicin sodium as a feed additive to be used as a coccidiostat in chickens for fattening (Table 1).

On 22.06.2022, the Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) of the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), in its opinion on the safety and efficacy of the product, could not conclude that the use of the additive is safe for the terrestrial compartment.

The Commission gave the possibility to the applicant to submit supplementary information and data to complete the assessment and to allow a revision of the EFSA's opinion. The new data have been received by the Commission on 05.05.2023.

In view of the above, the Commission asks EFSA to deliver a new opinion on Semduramicin sodium as a feed additive for chickens for fattening based on the supplementary information and data submitted by the applicant, in accordance with Article 29(1)(a) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002.

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of semduramicin sodium (Aviax 5%) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 5 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019).

ASSESSMENT

The additive Aviax 5% is intended to be used as a coccidiostat in feed for chickens for fattening at a level of 20–25 mg semduramicin sodium/kg complete feed.

The safety and the efficacy of the additive were evaluated in two opinions by the FEEDAP Panel (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018, 2022). In 2022 the Panel concluded that there are no safety concerns for the target animal, consumer, user and environment regarding the production strain of semduramicin sodium and that the additive is safe for the target species and consumer; no conclusions can be made on the irritancy of Aviax 5% to skin and eye and on the potential for dermal and respiratory sensitisation. In 2018, it was concluded that Aviax 5% at a minimum level of 20 mg/kg complete feed has the potential to effectively control coccidiosis in chickens for fattening (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018). In the latest opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2022), the Panel concluded the following on the safety of the additive for the environment: ‘The use of semduramicin sodium from Aviax 5% in feed for chickens for fattening up to 25 mg/kg complete feed does not pose a risk for groundwater nor for aquatic and sediment compartments. A risk for the terrestrial compartment cannot be excluded. The bioaccumulation and the risk for secondary poisoning are considered to be low.’ The applicant has now submitted additional data to cover the limitations identified in the previous opinion which are assessed below.

Safety for the environment

Refinement of the predicted no effect concentration (PNEC) for plants

In the opinion adopted by the FEEDAP Panel in 2018 the most sensitive endpoint that led to the conclusion that the risk for terrestrial compartment could not be excluded were shoot weight in tomatoes and rye grass (EC10 of 1.3 mg/kg dry soil). In 2022, the FEEDAP Panel considered that the relevant endpoint for the assessment of the toxicity to plants was the lowest EC10 of 1.3 mg/kg dry soil for shoot weight in tomatoes, based on an updated report of the same study.

For the current assessment, the applicant submitted a re‐analysis of the data from the seedling emergence study already evaluated in the previous opinions of the FEEDAP Panel (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018, 2022) with the aim to refine the Predicted No Effect Concentration (PNEC) of 130 mg/kg that had been established for plants. 6 The statistical analysis was conducted using the Comprehensive Environmental Toxicity Information system (CETIS) version 2.1.3 statistical program (Tidepool Scientific 2022). The overall approach differed from the previous one mainly regarding the prior analysis of the raw data for all endpoints observed (emergence, height and dry weight), and the calculation of the effect based on best model fit hierarchy, starting from linear regression, non‐linear regression and finally linear interpolation if no other model fitted (in contrast to previous analysis which was based only on linear interpolation). Assumptions for normality and homogeneity of variance were tested prior to the effect calculations. The Grubb's Outlier Test was conducted to detect outliers. If outliers were detected, the testing was conducted with and without the outlier to determine its influence on results.

Based on the re‐analysis submitted, the EC10 values determined in this re‐analysis were in most cases the same as those used for the previous opinion; slightly different values have been determined for the species and endpoints that were the most sensitive in the previous opinions. Differences in EC10 values were attributed to the selection of the best fit model and exclusion of statistical outlier values. Three parameter log‐logistic non‐linear regression analysis was used to determine an EC10 both with and without the outlier. In the case of tomato dry weight, re‐analysis of the data resulted in an EC10 of 1.3 mg/kg with an outlier (the same value used for risk characterisation in the FEEDAP Panel opinion adopted in 2022) and 1.6 mg/kg without that outlier. In the case of rye grass, the new calculated EC10 for dry weight was 2.9 mg/kg (with a statistical outlier) in comparison to the previously calculated EC10 value of 1.3 mg/kg (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018).

The statistical approach for data re‐analysis taken by the applicant is considered by the FEEDAP Panel justified and valid. Since the experimental study was already evaluated and considered adequate in the previous opinion, the newly derived lowest EC10 value for tomato dry weight is 1.6 mg/kg.

Risk characterisation

In the previous FEEDAP opinion, the FEEDAP Panel established a Predicted Environmental Concentration for soil (PECsoil) for semduramicin of 148 μg/kg (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2022). Based on the new data submitted, applying an assessment factor of 10 to the lowest EC10 of 1.6 mg/kg results in a PNEC for plants of 160 μg semduramicin/kg. The updated PEC/PNEC ratio for plant is 0.92. Therefore, no risk is expected for the terrestrial compartment when the additive is used in feed for chickens for fattening up to 25 mg/kg complete feed.

CONCLUSIONS

Based on the new data submitted, no risk is expected from the use of the additive in animal nutrition at the proposed conditions of use for the terrestrial compartment. The previous conclusions of the FEEDAP Panel can be updated as follows: the use of semduramicin sodium from Aviax 5% in feed for chickens for fattening up to 25 mg/kg complete feed does not pose a risk for groundwater nor for aquatic, terrestrial and sediment compartments.

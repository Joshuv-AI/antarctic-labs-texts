Almost sure global nonlinear smoothing for the 2D NLS
Chenmin Sun, Nikolay Tzvektov

Abstract
. In this article, we prove an almost-sure global in time nonlinear smoothing effect for NLS on the two-dimensional torus. For deterministic data, this phenomenon was proved for the NLS on the circle by Erdoğan–Tzirakis, which remains unknown on multi-dimensional torus. Our argument is based on a quantitative quasi-invariance of Gaussian measures with covariance operator ( 1 − Δ ) − s (1-\Delta)^{-s} for s > 2 s>2 .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Nonlinear smoothing effect for NLS

In this article, we consider the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) on the two-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=|u|^{2m-2}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2},\\
&u|_{t=0}=\phi,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
m\geq 2
is an integer.
The equation (
1.1
) is a Hamiltonian system with conserved mass and energy:

M[u]:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|u|^{2}\,dx,\qquad H[u]:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2m}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|u|^{2m}\,dx.

It is known that (
1.1
) is globally well-posed on
H^{1}
for any
m\geq 2
, thanks to the conserved energy and the Strichartz estimates in
[
2
]
or
[
6
]
.

In this work, we study the smoothing property of the solutions to (
1.1
). In sharp contrast with parabolic equations, the linear propagator of dispersive PDEs does not regularize the initial data; we are thus interested in the
nonlinear smoothing effect
(NoSE) of solutions modulo the linear part under a suitable gauge transform.

To the authors’ knowledge, (NoSE) can be used to extend fine properties of linear solutions, such as the Talbot effect (see
[
35
,
24
,
37
]
and references therein) and the pointwise convergence property (see
[
11
,
17
,
18
]
for the most celebrated results), to nonlinear solutions. It is due to the point of view that the nonlinearity can be viewed as a qualitative perturbation (not small) of the linear evolution, in the setting of problems under consideration.
It is shown in
[
19
]
(and further extended in
[
29
]
) that the one-dimensional NLS on
\mathbb{T}
exhibits (NoSE). Consequently, the authors proved the Talbot effect for NLS, which was observed numerically in
[
8
]
.
More precisely, the nonlinear smoothing property Erdoğan–Tzirakis proved states as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
19
]

Let
\sigma>0
and
T>0
. For any
\phi\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
, the solution of the cubic NLS
((
1.1
) with
m=2
)
with initial data
\phi
admits the decomposition

u(t)={\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{it}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\phi|^{2}\,dx}\cdot\big({\mathrm{e}}^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\phi+w(t)\big),

on
t\in[-T,T]
, where
w(t)\in C([-T,T];H^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{T}))
with
s_{1}<\sigma+\min(2\sigma,1/2)
.

Let us also mention that the (NoSE) is used to prove exponential mixing for a randomly forced NLS on the 1D torus
[
9
]
. It serves as an important ingredient to prove the exponential asymptotic compactness property for the damped NLS.

For NLS on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the nonlinear smoothing effect was proved in
[
4
,
25
]
as a consequence of the bilinear Strichartz estimate gaining anti-derivatives in the high-frequency part. In sharp contrast, due to the lack of dispersion on compact manifolds and worse bilinear Strichartz inequality (see
[
5
]
), the (NoSE) for NLS with deterministic initial data remains unknown.

Nevertheless, it is reasonable to conjecture that such a phenomenon is not true for NLS on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
. We will support this conjecture in the context of the cubic NLS on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
through the divergence of the second Picard iteration:

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\sigma\geq 0
. There exist a bounded sequence
(\phi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and a countable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}
such that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus E
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big\|\frac{1}{i}\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{i(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}\big(:|{\mathrm{e}}^{it^{\prime}\Delta}\phi_{n}|^{2}{\mathrm{e}}^{it^{\prime}\Delta}\phi_{n}:\big)\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{H^{\sigma_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}=+\infty,

for any
\sigma_{1}>\sigma
, where

:|v|^{2}v:\;=\;|v|^{2}v-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,v

is the renormalised cubic nonlinearity from the gauge transform.

Remark 1.2
.

From the analysis in
[
19
]
, Proposition
1.1
is not true for the 1D torus
\mathbb{T}
.

1.2.
Nonlinear smoothing effect for the random initial data

It turns out that the nonlinear smoothing effect is, however, a collective property when considering random initial data distributed according to centered Gaussian measures. More precisely, we assume that initial data are distributed according to the Gaussian probability measure
\mu_{s}
, formally defined as “
\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}}du
”, induced by the random Fourier series

\displaystyle\phi^{\omega}(x):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{g_{k}(\omega)}{\langle k\rangle^{s}}\mathrm{e}^{ik\cdot x},

(1.2)

where
\langle k\rangle:=\sqrt{1+|k|^{2}}
. It is well-known that
\mu_{s}
is supported on functions in
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
for all
\sigma<s-1
.
Our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s>2
,
\sigma<s-1
,
s_{1}<s-\frac{1}{2}
and
T\geq 1
. For
\mu_{s}
-almost every
\phi\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, the solution of the cubic NLS
((
1.1
) with
m=2
)
admits the decomposition

\displaystyle u(t)={\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{it}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\phi^{\omega}|^{2}dx}\cdot\big({\mathrm{e}}^{it\Delta}\phi^{\omega}+w(t)\big),

(1.3)

on
t\in[-T,T]
, where
w(t)\in C([-T,T];H^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
with at most polynomial growth in
|t|\leq T
.

Remark 1.3
.

We note that (
1.3
) is also true for
s=1
(
[
3
]
), thanks to the equivalence between
\mu_{1}
and the invariant Gibbs measure. The case
s>1

is proved
[
10
]
, but only for small
T>0
. It is an interesting question to fill the gap
1<s\leq 2
in Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 1.4
.

The analogue of Theorem
1.2
for NLS with any
m\geq 2
should also hold under a different gauge with the phase factor
{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{im}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2m}}^{2m}dt^{\prime}}
by our argument. Since the local-in-time statement (analogue of Proposition
8.1
) does not seem to be written in the literature, we prefer not stating the general case as a theorem.

1.3.
Quantitative quasi-invariance

Note that the NLS flow
\Phi_{t}
of (
1.1
) is a globally defined flow on
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. In particular, when
s>2
,
\Phi_{t}
is a well-defined flow on the support of the measure
\mu_{s}
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
is deduced from a local nonlinear smoothing effect (Proposition
8.1
), and more importantly, the quantitative quasi-invariance property of the Gaussian measure
\mu_{s}
transported by the flow
\Phi_{t}
.

Initiated by the second author in
[
40
]
, intensive research activities have been conducted for proving quasi-invariance of Gaussian measures transported by flows of dispersive PDEs. Among an exhaustive list of literature, we only mention
[
7
,
12
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
36
,
38
]
for NLS type equations. These results are mostly for one-dimensional models except for the previous work
[
38
]
of the authors for the 3D energy-critical NLS.

Our second main result in this article is the following
quantitative quasi-invariance
property of NLS with any integer-valued defocusing nonlinearity:

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
s>2
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\geq 2
. Then for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the transported Gaussian measure
(\Phi_{t})_{*}\mu_{s}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu_{s}
. More precisely, for any
\lambda\geq 1
, there exists a family of weighted measures

d\rho_{s,\lambda}(u)=\mathbf{1}_{H[u]\leq\lambda}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-R_{s}(u)}\,d\mu_{s}(u)

such that

•

\mathbf{1}_{H[u]\leq\lambda}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{|R_{s}(u)|}\in L^{p}(d\mu_{s})
for any
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda>0
.

•

The density
f_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)
of the transported measure
(\Phi_{t})_{*}d\rho_{s,\lambda}
belongs to
L^{p}(d\rho_{s,\lambda})
for any
1\leq p<\infty
. More precisely, there exist
C(p,\lambda)>0
and an absolute constant
C_{0}>0
such that

\|f_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(d\rho_{s,\lambda})}\leq C(p,\lambda)\,\mathrm{e}^{\,C(p,\lambda)(1+|t|^{C_{0}})}.

Remark 1.5
.

After this manuscript was essentially completed, we became aware of a work by Tolomeo–Visciglia
[
39
]
which obtains a similar result of Theorem
1.3
by using a different approach, based on physical space analysis to build modified energies.

Remark 1.6
.

For large values of
s
, the quasi-invariance part of Theorem
1.3
can be deduced directly from the same argument in
[
38
]
.
The statements here are quantitative, in the sense that the Radon–Nikodym density of the weighted Gaussian measure (truncated by a conservation law) is in any
L^{p}(d\mu_{s})
for
p<\infty
. By mimicking Bourgain’s measure-invariant argument
[
3
]
, such a property can be applied to obtain various dynamical consequences of the solutions. See
[
21
,
28
]
about the application of the quantitative quasi-invariance to globalize solutions in the low-regularity setting.

Remark 1.7
.

Even for large value of
s
, the quantitative quasi-invariance is more difficult to obtain, compared to the qualitative quasi-invariance property. From the current technology, for proving the quantitative quasi-invariance, we have to truncate the measure with some conservation law, while we can truncate the stronger well-posedness norms for proving qualitative quasi-invariance.

Remark 1.8
.

Let us also mention two other applications in the literature. In the recent work
[
1
]
, the quantitative quasi-invariance of Gaussian measures was used
in order to prove low regularity well-posedness results for the binormal flow. In
[
7
]
, such an idea was used to prove the almost sure scattering of the NLS on
\mathbb{R}
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.3
relies on a methodology similar to the one developed in
[
38
]
. As we are dealing with solutions with relatively low regularity (though still above
H^{1}
), more attention is needed to control the interaction of solutions in different scales.
The constraint
s>2
here is only to ensure the existence of global flow on the support of
\mu_{s}
, and the legality of the truncation of the energy.
We believe that our result can be extended to some
s
slightly smaller than
2
(depending on the degree of nonlinearity
m
), similar to
[
28
]
for the one-dimensional NLS. In another direction, it is likely that Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
could be extended for the cubic NLS on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
by our method.

2.
Modified energy and the weighted Gaussian measure

2.1.
Normal form and the modified energy

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\geq 2
and
s\geq 2
. To construct appropriate weighted measures, we introduce a modified energy functional. For a smooth solution
u(t)
of (
1.1
), define the new unknown obtained by factoring out the linear flow:

v(t)={\mathrm{e}}^{-it\Delta}u(t).

Expanding
v(t)
in the Fourier series,

v(t,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}v_{k}(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{ik\cdot x},

we see that
v_{k}(t)
solves

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}v_{k}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+\cdots+k_{2m-1}=k\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-it\Omega(\vec{k})}\,v_{k_{1}}(t)\,\overline{v}_{k_{2}}(t)\cdots v_{k_{2m-1}}(t),

(2.1)

where

\Omega(\vec{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{2m-1}(-1)^{j-1}|k_{j}|^{2}-|k|^{2}

is the resonant function.
To construct the modified energy, it is convenient to use the following equivalent Sobolev norm for
s\geq 0
:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big(1+|k|^{2s}\big)\,|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}.

(2.2)

A straightforward computation using the symmetry of the indices yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v(t)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{H^{s}}^{2}=-\frac{1}{2m}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+\cdots-k_{2m}=0\end{subarray}}\!\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-it\Omega(\vec{k})}\,v_{k_{1}}\overline{v}_{k_{2}}\cdots\overline{v}_{k_{2m}},

(2.3)

where (abusing notation slightly)

\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j-1}|k_{j}|^{2s},\qquad\Omega(\vec{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j-1}|k_{j}|^{2}.

We split the right-hand side according to the resonance set
\{\Omega(\vec{k})=0\}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v(t)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{H^{s}}^{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2m}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+\cdots-k_{2m}=0\\
\Omega(\vec{k})=0\end{subarray}}\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-it\Omega(\vec{k})}\,v_{k_{1}}\overline{v}_{k_{2}}\cdots\overline{v}_{k_{2m}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2m}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+\cdots-k_{2m}=0\\
\Omega(\vec{k})\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})}{-i\,\Omega(\vec{k})}\,\partial_{t}\!\Big({\mathrm{e}}^{-it\Omega(\vec{k})}\,v_{k_{1}}\overline{v}_{k_{2}}\cdots\overline{v}_{k_{2m}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2m}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+\cdots-k_{2m}=0\\
\Omega(\vec{k})\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})}{-i\,\Omega(\vec{k})}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-it\Omega(\vec{k})}\,\partial_{t}\!\big(v_{k_{1}}\overline{v}_{k_{2}}\cdots\overline{v}_{k_{2m}}\big).

(2.4)

Motivated by this, define the modified energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{s,t}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{s,t}(v),

(2.5)

where

\mathcal{R}_{s,t}(v):=\frac{1}{2m}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+\cdots-k_{2m}=0\\
\Omega(\vec{k})\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})}{-i\,\Omega(\vec{k})}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-it\Omega(\vec{k})}\,v_{k_{1}}\overline{v}_{k_{2}}\cdots\overline{v}_{k_{2m}}.

Returning to
u
, observe that

\mathcal{E}_{s,t}(v)=E_{s}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}+R_{s}(u),

(2.6)

with

\displaystyle R_{s}(u):=\frac{1}{2m}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+\cdots-k_{2m}=0\\
\Omega(\vec{k})\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})}{-i\,\Omega(\vec{k})}\,\widehat{u}_{k_{1}}\overline{\widehat{u}}_{k_{2}}\cdots\overline{\widehat{u}}_{k_{2m}}\,.

(2.7)

Then, from (
2.4
), (
2.1
) and the symmetry of the indices, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathcal{E}_{s,t}(v)=\frac{d}{dt}E_{s}(u)=\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{III},

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}:=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2m}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+\cdots-k_{2m}=0\\
\Omega(\vec{k})=0\end{subarray}}\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-it\Omega(\vec{k})}\,v_{k_{1}}\overline{v}_{k_{2}}\cdots\overline{v}_{k_{2m}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+\cdots-k_{2m}=0\\
\Omega(\vec{k})\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})}{\Omega(\vec{k})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}=p_{1}-p_{2}+\cdots+p_{2m-1}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-it\big(\Omega(\vec{k})+\Omega(\vec{p})\big)}\,v_{p_{1}}\overline{v}_{p_{2}}\cdots v_{p_{2m-1}}\overline{v}_{k_{2}}\cdots\overline{v}_{k_{2m}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{III}:=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+\cdots-k_{2m}=0\\
\Omega(\vec{k})\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})}{\Omega(\vec{k})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}=q_{1}-q_{2}+\cdots+q_{2m-1}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-it\big(\Omega(\vec{k})-\Omega(\vec{q})\big)}\,v_{k_{1}}\overline{v}_{q_{1}}\cdots\overline{v}_{q_{2m-1}}v_{k_{3}}\cdots\overline{v}_{k_{2m}},

(2.8)

where

\Omega(\vec{p})=\sum_{j=1}^{2m-1}(-1)^{j-1}|p_{j}|^{2}-|k_{1}|^{2},\qquad\Omega(\vec{q})=\sum_{j=1}^{2m-1}(-1)^{j-1}|q_{j}|^{2}-|k_{2}|^{2}.

2.2.
The weighted measure

Using the modified energy, we define, for
\lambda\geq 1
, the weighted Gaussian measure

\displaystyle d\rho_{s,\lambda}(u):=\mathbf{1}_{\{H[u]\leq\lambda\}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-R_{s}(u)}\,d\mu_{s}(u).

(2.9)

This can be made rigorous by studying the truncated system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&i\partial_{t}u_{N}+\Delta u_{N}=\pi_{N}\!\big(|\pi_{N}u_{N}|^{2m-2}\,\pi_{N}u_{N}\big),\\
&u_{N}|_{t=0}=\phi,\end{cases}

(2.10)

where
\widehat{\pi_{N}f}(k)=\mathbf{1}_{\{|k|\leq N\}}\widehat{f}(k)
. Note that (
2.10
) is a Hamiltonian system with Hamiltonian

H_{N}[u]:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla\pi_{N}u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2m}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\pi_{N}u|^{2m}\,dx.

Denote the flow of (
2.10
) by
\Phi_{t}^{N}
.

Define accordingly the functionals
\mathcal{E}_{s,t,N}
,
E_{s,N}
,
\mathcal{R}_{s,t,N}
,
R_{s,N}
by replacing
\widehat{u}_{k},v_{k}
with
\widehat{u}_{k}\mathbf{1}_{\{|k|\leq N\}}
,
v_{k}\mathbf{1}_{\{|k|\leq N\}}
in the formulas of the previous subsection. Let
\mu_{s,N}
be the Gaussian measure on the finite-dimensional space
\pi_{N}L^{2}
induced by the truncated random Fourier series

\pi_{N}\phi^{\omega}(x):=\sum_{|k|\leq N}\frac{g_{k}^{\omega}}{\langle k\rangle^{s}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{ik\cdot x}.

Note that
\mu_{s}=\mu_{s,N}\otimes\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}
, where
\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}
is the Gaussian measure on
\pi_{N}^{\perp}L^{2}
induced by
(\mathrm{Id}-\pi_{N})\phi^{\omega}(x)
.

Proposition 2.1

(Construction of the weighted measure)
.

Let
s>2
and
\lambda\geq 1
. For any
p\in[1,\infty)
, there exists a constant
C(p,s,\lambda)>0
, independent of
N
, such that for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\{H_{N}[u]\leq\lambda\}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{|R_{s,N}(u)|}\big\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{s})}\leq C(p,s,\lambda).

Moreover,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\Big\|\mathbf{1}_{\{H_{N}[u]\leq\lambda\}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-R_{s,N}(u)}-\mathbf{1}_{\{H[u]\leq\lambda\}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-R_{s}(u)}\Big\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{s})}=0.

The proposition defines the convergent sequence of weighted measures

d\rho_{s,\lambda,N}(u):=\mathbf{1}_{\{H_{N}[u]\leq\lambda\}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-R_{s,N}(u)}\,d\mu_{s,N}(u)\otimes d\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}(u),

whose
densities
converge
\mu_{s}
-a.s. and in
L^{p}(d\mu_{s})
to the density of
\rho_{s,\lambda}
. The weighted measure
\rho_{s,\lambda}
is equivalent to the truncated Gaussian measure
\mu_{s,\lambda}:=\mathbf{1}_{\{H[u]\leq\lambda\}}\,\mu_{s}
.

Proposition 2.2

(Weighted energy estimate)
.

Let
s>2
,
\lambda\geq 1
, and
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Define

Q_{N}(u):=\Big(\frac{d}{dt}E_{s,N}\big(\Phi_{t}^{N}(u)\big)\Big)\Big|_{t=0}.

Then there exist constants
C(s,\lambda)>0
and
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
, independent of
N
, such that for all
p\in[2,\infty)
,

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\{H_{N}[u]\leq\lambda\}}\;Q_{N}(u)\big\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{s})}\leq C(s,\lambda,\epsilon_{0})\,p^{1-\epsilon_{0}}.

By Proposition
2.1
and Cauchy–Schwarz, we also have, for all
p\in[2,\infty)
,

\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\{H_{N}[u]\leq\lambda\}}\;Q_{N}(u)\big\|_{L^{p}(d\rho_{s,\lambda,N})}\leq C(s,\lambda,\epsilon_{0})\,p^{1-\epsilon_{0}}.

2.3.
Quasi-invariance and
L^{p}
bound of the transported density

As a consequence of Proposition
2.1
, Proposition
2.2
and Lemma
A.1
, we now sketch the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Following a standard argument (see the proof of Lemma 3.3 in
[
38
]
), for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and any measurable
A
,

\frac{d}{dt}\,\rho_{s,\lambda,N}\big((\Phi_{t}^{N})^{-1}(A)\big)\;\leq\;\big\|\mathbf{1}_{\{H_{N}[u]\leq\lambda\}}\;Q_{N}(u)\big\|_{L^{p}(d\rho_{s,\lambda,N})}\,\big[\rho_{s,\lambda,N}\big((\Phi_{t}^{N})^{-1}(A)\big)\big]^{\,1-\frac{1}{p}},

for all
p\in[1,\infty)
. By Proposition
2.2
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\,\rho_{s,\lambda,N}\big((\Phi_{t}^{N})^{-1}(A)\big)\leq C(s,\lambda,\epsilon_{0})\,p^{1-\epsilon_{0}}\,\big[\rho_{s,\lambda,N}\big((\Phi_{t}^{N})^{-1}(A)\big)\big]^{\,1-\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.11)

Applying Lemma
A.1
, we obtain, for any fixed
\epsilon_{0}>0
,

\displaystyle\rho_{s,\lambda,N}\big((\Phi_{t}^{N})^{-1}(A)\big)\leq C(s,\lambda,\epsilon_{0})\,\rho_{s,\lambda,N}(A)^{\,1-\epsilon_{0}},

(2.12)

and, for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\sup_{|t|\leq T}\Big\|\frac{d\,(\Phi_{t}^{N})_{*}\rho_{s,\lambda,N}}{d\rho_{s,\lambda,N}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(d\rho_{s,\lambda,N})}\ \leq\ C(s,\lambda,\epsilon_{0},p)\,\exp\!\Big(C(s,\lambda,\epsilon_{0},p)\big(1+T^{2/\epsilon_{0}}\big)\Big).

(2.13)

To pass to the limit
N\to\infty
, we invoke the standard stability/Cauchy theory for (
1.1
) at regularity
s>2
(deterministic and subcritical), as in
[
40

For a leaf

A

Resonant functions and weights.
For the root
\mathfrak{r}
with first-generation nodes

For each non-root node
\mathfrak{n}
with children
\mathfrak{n}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{n}_{2\ell_{0}-1}
(left to right), set

\Omega(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{n}}):=\iota_{\mathfrak{n}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{2\ell_{0}-1}\iota_{\mathfrak{n}_{j}}|k_{\mathfrak{n}_{j}}|^{2}-\iota_{\mathfrak{n}}|k_{\mathfrak{n}}|^{2}\Big)=\iota_{\mathfrak{n}}\Big(\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{n}}}\iota_{\mathfrak{l}}|k_{\mathfrak{l}}|^{2}-\iota_{\mathfrak{n}}|k_{\mathfrak{n}}|^{2}\Big).

We do not require each node to have the same number of children, since our algorithm may delete paired leaves to produce a reduced tree.

Pairing.

A pairing

consists of two non-empty subsets

with
\#X=\#Y
and

, together with a bijection

Giving an expanded branching tree
\mathcal{T}

means we pair leaves

for each

A decoration

We sometimes say leaves

and
\iota_{\mathfrak{i}}+\iota_{\mathfrak{j}}=0

If
\mathcal{T}
is adapted to the dyadic sizes

, then paired leaves

impose

2

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime},\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}:=\Big\{(k_{\mathfrak{n}}:\mathfrak{n}\in\mathcal{T}):\Omega(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})\neq 0,\;k_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}}=k_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}},\;\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{r}},\;\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}}|k_{\mathfrak{l}}|\leq|k_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}|^{\theta}+|k_{\mathfrak{l^{\prime}}}|^{\theta},\;\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}},\;\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime};;}|k_{\mathfrak{l}}|\leq|k_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}|^{\theta}+|k_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}|^{\theta}\Big\},

(3.3)

is the set of decorations with a single high-frequency cross-pairing.
We write the cross-pairing singular contribution in
\mathcal{T}
, corresponding to the paired leaves
\mathfrak{l}^{\prime},\mathfrak{l^{\prime\prime}}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime},\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{T})(v):=\sum_{(k_{\mathfrak{n}})\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime},\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}}\frac{\psi_{2s}(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})}{\Omega(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})}\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}}v_{k_{\mathfrak{l}}}^{\iota_{\mathfrak{l}}}.

(3.4)

It was already observed in

Lemma 4.1

\displaystyle|\psi_{2s}(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})|\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})^{2s-2}\big(|\Omega(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})|+\lambda_{3}(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})^{2}\big),&\text{if }|\Omega(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})|\lesssim\lambda_{1}(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})\lambda_{3}(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}}),\\[5.69054pt]

(4.3)

Proof of Lemma

.

By admissibility, there exist

\displaystyle\big||k_{\mathfrak{n}}|^{2s}-|k_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}|^{2s}\big|

Lemma 6.2

The following identity holds:

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime},\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{T})=\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\sum_{(k_{\mathfrak{n}})\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime},\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}}\Psi(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}}v_{k_{\mathfrak{l}}}^{\iota_{\mathfrak{l}}}.

(6.2)

Proof.

Since

Since
\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{r}}
is isomorphis to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{n_{0}}}

\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}
are paired with
\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}
, there is a bijection mapping each leaf

\displaystyle\sum_{(k_{\mathfrak{n}})\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime},\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}}\Big(\frac{\psi_{2s}(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})}{\Omega(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})}-\Psi(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})\Big)\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}}v_{k_{\mathfrak{l}}}^{\iota_{\mathfrak{l}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}},k_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}}\frac{\iota_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}|k_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}|^{2s}-\iota_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}|k_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}|^{2s}}{\iota_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}|k_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}|^{2}-\iota_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}|k_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}|^{2}}|v_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}|^{2}\Big|\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}},\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}\mathbf{1}_{S(k_{\mathfrak{n_{0}}}k_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}})}\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}},\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}v_{k_{\mathfrak{l}}}^{\iota_{\mathfrak{l}}}\Big|^{2},

which is real-valued, where

\displaystyle S(k_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}},k_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}):=\Big\{k_{\mathfrak{l}}:\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}\setminus\{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}\},\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}},\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}\iota_{\mathfrak{l}}k_{\mathfrak{l}}=\iota_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}k_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}-\iota_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}k_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}},\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}},\;\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}|k_{\mathfrak{l}}|\leq|k_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}|^{\theta}+|k_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}|^{\theta}\Big\}

(6.3)

is a subset of decoration in

.
This proves Lemma

Lemma 6.3

Recall that

\mathrm{(ii)}

|\Psi(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})|\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime},\mathfrak{l}^{\prime\prime}}}\lesssim|k_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}|^{2s-4}\lambda_{3}(\vec{k}_{\mathfrak{r}})^{2},

Proof.

Set

Proof.

In the sequel, all the constants

can implicitly depend on

8.2.
Lack of regularization of the first Picard’s iteration

Proposition of
1.1
.

For a given initial datum
\phi
, let
\mathcal{I}_{1}(\phi;t)
denote the first Picard iterate of the Duhamel term. A direct computation in Fourier variables gives

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{I}_{1}(t;\phi)}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+k_{3}=k\\
k_{2}\neq k_{1},k_{3}\end{subarray}}\frac{2\sin\!\big(\tfrac{t\,\Omega(\vec{k})}{2}\big)}{i\,\Omega(\vec{k})}\,e^{-i\frac{t\,\Omega(\vec{k})}{2}}\,\widehat{\phi}(k_{1})\,\overline{\widehat{\phi}}(k_{2})\,\widehat{\phi}(k_{3})\;+\;\frac{t}{i}\,|\widehat{\phi}(k)|^{2}\,\widehat{\phi}(k),

(8.2)

where
\Omega(\vec{k}):=|k_{1}|^{2}-|k_{2}|^{2}+|k_{3}|^{2}-|k|^{2}
.

Fix
n\geq 1
and define
\widehat{\phi_{n}}
as a sum of one thin vertical slab and two atoms:

\widehat{\phi_{n}}(\xi,\eta)\;=\;f_{n}(\xi,\eta)+a_{1}(\xi,\eta)+a_{2}(\xi,\eta),

with

f_{n}(\xi,\eta):=n^{-\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi=0,\ \eta\in[n,2n]\}},\qquad a_{1}(\xi,\eta):=\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi=1,\ \eta=0\}},\qquad a_{2}(\xi,\eta):=\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi=2,\ \eta=0\}}.

Then
\|\phi_{n}\|_{H^{\sigma}}\sim 1
uniformly in
n
, since

\sum_{\eta=n}^{2n}\langle(0,\eta)\rangle^{2\sigma}\,n^{-2\sigma-1}\;\sim\;1,\qquad\|(a_{1}+a_{2})\|_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim 1.

We now evaluate (
8.2
) at frequencies
k=(1,\eta)
with
\eta\in[n,2n]
.
Among all triples
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
with
k_{1}-k_{2}+k_{3}=k
, the only ones that produce a nonzero contribution with the above data and land at
(1,\eta)
are the two placements

\big(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\big)=\big((0,\eta),(1,0),(2,0)\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\big(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\big)=\big((2,0),(1,0),(0,\eta)\big).

In both cases,

\Omega(\vec{k})=|k_{1}|^{2}-|k_{2}|^{2}+|k_{3}|^{2}-|k|^{2}=2,

so the time prefactor equals

\frac{2\sin\!\big(\frac{t\Omega}{2}\big)}{i\,\Omega}\,e^{-i\frac{t\Omega}{2}}=\frac{2\sin t}{2i}\,e^{-it}=\frac{\sin t}{i}\,e^{-it}.

Moreover, the gauge term
\frac{t}{i}|\widehat{\phi}_{k}|^{2}\widehat{\phi}_{k}
vanishes at

for
\eta\in[n,2n]
, since
\widehat{\phi_{n}}(1,\eta)=0
there.
Therefore, for all

\widehat{\mathcal{I}_{1}(t;\phi_{n})}_{(1,\eta)}=C\,\sin t\,e^{-it}\;n^{-\sigma-\frac{1}{2}},

where

\big|\widehat{\mathcal{I}_{1}(t;\phi_{n})}_{(1,\eta)}\big|\gtrsim|\sin t|\;n^{-\sigma-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\eta\in[n,2n].

Consequently, using
\langle(1,\eta)\rangle^{2\sigma_{1}}\sim\eta^{2\sigma_{1}}\sim n^{2\sigma_{1}}
on the block
\eta\in[n,2n]
, we obtain

\|\mathcal{I}_{1}(t;\phi_{n})\|_{H^{\sigma_{1}}}^{2}\ \geq\ \sum_{\eta=n}^{2n}\langle(1,\eta)\rangle^{2\sigma_{1}}\,\big|\widehat{\mathcal{I}_{1}(t;\phi_{n})}_{(1,\eta)}\big|^{2}\ \gtrsim\ n\cdot n^{2\sigma_{1}}\cdot\big(|\sin t|^{2}\,n^{-2\sigma-1}\big)\ =\ |\sin t|^{2}\,n^{2(\sigma_{1}-\sigma)}.

Hence, for every

, we have
\|\mathcal{I}_{1}(t;\phi_{n})\|_{H^{\sigma_{1}}}\to\infty
as

whenever
\sigma_{1}>\sigma

1.1

\Phi_{t}:X\to X
be a continuous flow and assume that, for every Borel set
A\subset X
, the map
t\mapsto\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(A))
is differentiable. Moreover, assume that there exist
C_{0}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for any Borel set
A\subset X
, any
r\in(1,\infty)
, and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\frac{d}{dt}\,\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(A))\ \leq\ C_{0}\,r^{1-\alpha}\,\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(A))^{\,1-\frac{1}{r}}.

(A.2)

Then for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and
T\geq 1
, there exists
C_{\delta,T}>0
such that, uniformly for
|t|\leq T
and all Borel
A
,

\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(A))\ \leq\ C_{\delta,T}\,\mu_{t}(A)^{\,1-\delta}.

(A.3)

Furthermore, for the Radon–Nikodym density

f_{t}:=\frac{d\big((\Phi_{t})_{*}\mu_{t}\big)}{d\mu_{t}},

we have, for every
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|f_{t}\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{t})}\ \leq\ C(\alpha,p)\,\exp\!\Big(C(\alpha,p)\big(1+T^{2/\alpha}\big)\Big).

(A.4)

Proof.

The argument below is essentially the same appeared in
[
22
]
.
By definition, for any Borel set
A\subset X
,

\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(A))=\int_{A}f_{t}\,d\mu_{t}.

Thus (
A.3
) follows from (
A.4
) and Hölder. It suffices to prove the weak
L^{q}
bound, uniform in
|t|\leq T
, for any fixed
q\in(1,\infty)
:

\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|f_{t}\|_{L^{q,\infty}(d\mu_{t})}\ \leq\ C(\alpha,q)\,\exp\!\Big(C(\alpha,q)\big(1+T^{2/\alpha}\big)\Big).

(A.5)

We use the standard characterization (
[
23
]
: Page 11, Exercise 1.1.12):

\|f_{t}\|_{L^{q,\infty}(d\mu_{t})}\simeq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\subset X\\
\mu_{t}(E)>0\end{subarray}}\ \frac{1}{\mu_{t}(E)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}}\int_{E}f_{t}\,d\mu_{t}.

(A.6)

Fix a Borel set
E\subset X
and set
y(t):=\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(E))
. By (
A.2
),

y^{\prime}(t)\leq C_{0}\,r^{1-\alpha}\,y(t)^{1-\frac{1}{r}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad y(t)^{1/r}\leq y(0)^{1/r}+C_{0}|t|\,r^{-\alpha}.

Hence, for
|t|\leq T
,

\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(E))\leq\Big(\mu_{0}(E)^{1/r}+C_{0}T\,r^{-\alpha}\Big)^{r}.

(A.7)

Using (
A.1
) with
p=2q
and Hölder,

\mu_{0}(E)\leq\Big\|\frac{d\mu_{0}}{d\mu_{t}}\Big\|_{L^{2q}(d\mu_{t})}\,\mu_{t}(E)^{1-\frac{1}{2q}}\leq M_{2q}\,\mu_{t}(E)^{1-\frac{1}{2q}}.

Plugging this into (
A.7
),

\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(E))\leq\Big(M_{2q}\,\mu_{t}(E)^{\frac{1-\frac{1}{2q}}{r}}+C_{0}T\,r^{-\alpha}\Big)^{r}.

Now, by (
A.6
),

\displaystyle\|f_{t}\|_{L^{q,\infty}(d\mu_{t})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<\mu_{t}(E)\leq e^{-b_{0}}}\ \mu_{t}(E)^{-\big(1-\frac{1}{q}\big)}\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(E))

\displaystyle\quad+\ \sup_{\mu_{t}(E)\geq e^{-b_{0}}}\ \mu_{t}(E)^{-\big(1-\frac{1}{q}\big)}\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(E)).

(A.8)

For the first term, writing
\mu_{t}(E)=e^{-b}
and optimizing in
r>1
,

\sup_{b\geq b_{0}}\ \exp\!\Big(r\log M-\tfrac{b}{2q}+Tr^{1-\alpha}e^{\frac{b}{r}\big(1-\frac{1}{2q}\big)}\Big),

with
M:=\max\{C_{0},M_{2q}\}
. Choosing
r=\frac{b}{\log\log b}
and taking
b_{0}
large enough so that

\frac{\log b}{(\log\log b)^{1-\alpha}}\leq b^{\alpha/2},\quad b\geq\Big(\tfrac{T}{4q}\Big)^{2/\alpha},\quad b\geq\exp\exp\Big(\tfrac{\log M}{4q}\Big),

one obtains that the exponent is
\leq 0
for all
b\geq b_{0}
, so this contribution is
\lesssim 1
.

For the second term in (
A.8
), take
r=2
in (
A.7
) to get

\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(E))\leq\Big(M_{2q}\,\mu_{t}(E)^{\frac{1-\frac{1}{2q}}{2}}+C_{0}T\,2^{-\alpha}\Big)^{2}.

Therefore, for all
\mu_{t}(E)\geq e^{-b_{0}}
,

\mu_{t}(E)^{-\big(1-\frac{1}{q}\big)}\mu_{t}(\Phi_{t}^{-1}(E))\ \lesssim\ e^{b_{0}(1-\frac{1}{q})}\,\Big(M_{2q}^{2}\,\mu_{t}(X)^{1-\frac{1}{2q}}+C_{0}^{2}T^{2}\Big).

Choosing
b_{0}\simeq c_{\alpha}\big(1+T^{2/\alpha}\big)
as above yields (
A.5
). Finally, interpolate between weak–
L^{q}
and
L^{1}
(note
\|f_{t}\|_{L^{1}(d\mu_{t})}=\mu_{t}(X)
) to obtain (
A.4
). Using (
A.4
) and Hölder gives (
A.3
).
∎

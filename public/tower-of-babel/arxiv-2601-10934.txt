Invariant Algebraic D D -Modules on Semisimple and General Linear Groups: Classification and Tensor Categories
Rudrendra Kashyap and Ruoxi Li

Abstract
. We study finite-rank left-translation invariant algebraic D D -modules on complex affine algebraic groups. Using the standard description of these objects as left-invariant flat algebraic connections on the trivial vector bundle, modulo algebraic gauge transformations, we recast the classification problem as an explicit moduli problem for constant connections. Our main results treat the semisimple case and the general linear case. For a connected semisimple complex algebraic group, invariant D D -modules are classified by representations of the finite central kernel of the simply connected cover, yielding an equivalence of tensor categories. For a general linear group, every invariant D D -module is obtained by pullback along the determinant map, reducing the classification to the one-dimensional torus case and inducing a tensor equivalence with the corresponding invariant category on the torus.



1.
Introduction

The original motivation for this article is to study commuting schemes from the perspective of invariant theory.
Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
, and let
G
be a reductive algebraic group over
k
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Fix a maximal torus
T\subset G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
, and let
W
be the Weyl group of
(G,T)
.
The group
W
acts naturally on both
T
and
\mathfrak{t}
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the
n
-fold commuting scheme

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{n}:=\Bigl\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathfrak{g}^{n}\ \big|\ [x_{i},x_{j}]=0\text{ for all }1\leq i,j\leq n\Bigr\}.

The adjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
induces a diagonal action on
\mathfrak{g}^{n}
, and
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{n}
is stable under this action.
We write

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{n}\sslash G:=\mathrm{Spec}\bigl(k[\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{n}]^{G}\bigr)

for the affine categorical quotient.

The embedding
\mathfrak{t}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
induces a morphism
\mathfrak{t}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{n}
whose image lies in
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{n}
.
Passing to coordinate rings and invariants yields a natural
k
-algebra homomorphism

\vartheta:\ k[\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{n}]^{G}\to k[\mathfrak{t}^{n}]^{W}.

Conjecture A

(Chevalley restriction for commuting schemes)
.

The morphism
\vartheta
is an isomorphism.

For
n=1
, this is precisely the classical Chevalley restriction theorem.
When
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, Conjecture
A
was proved by Vaccarino
[
Vac07
]
using Deligne’s construction.
A discussion of this argument, together with an extension to
G=\text{Sp}_{2n}
, appears in Chen–Ngô
[
CN21
]
; in the symplectic case the inverse map is constructed using the Pfaffian (see §3 of
[
CN21
]
).
For general reductive groups, no analogous explicit inverse map is currently known.
There has also been more recent progress for orthogonal groups; we refer to
[
SXX23
]
.

A second motivation comes from Higgs bundles.
Let
X
be an algebraic variety over
k
.
A
G
-Higgs bundle
on
X
is a principal
G
-bundle
E
together with a Higgs field

\theta\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{ad}(E)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\Omega_{X}^{1})

satisfying the integrability condition
\theta\wedge\theta=0
.
Commuting schemes arise naturally in the construction of Hitchin-type morphisms; for background and further references we point to
[
CN20
]
.

Even for
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, the quotient
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{n}\sslash G
already reflects how hard it is to
describe commuting
n
-tuples up to simultaneous conjugacy.
When
n=1
this is just the classification of a single matrix up to conjugacy, where Jordan normal form gives
explicit representatives.
But for
n\geq 2
(already for pairs of commuting matrices) there is no comparable canonical classification.
For this reason, in this paper we do not try to analyze the orbit structure of
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{n}

directly.
Instead, following Wei’s approach in
[
Wei25
]
, we pass to a coarser but more geometric equivalence relation coming from invariant algebraic

D
-modules on
G
.

As shown in
[
Wei25
]
, left-translation invariant algebraic
D
-modules on an affine algebraic group can be described concretely in terms of algebraic flat connections on the trivial bundle, modulo algebraic gauge equivalence. Wei provided the classification results in several basic cases (tori, unipotent groups, Borel subgroups, and closely related examples). In addition, Wei proposed the following Conjecture 4.6 of
[
Wei25
]
.

Conjecture B

(Wei)
.

Let
G
be a connected algebraic group. We have

\frac{\{\text{Invariant $D$-modules of rank $n$ on $G$}\}}{\sim}=\frac{\{\text{Invariant $D$-modules of rank $n$ on }G/[G,G]\}}{\sim},

where
\sim
denotes the relation of
D
-module isomorphisms. The above isomorphism is through pullback
of representations via the quotient map
q\colon G\twoheadrightarrow G/[G,G]
.

Our main new results are motivated by
this conjecture. Explicit classifications are established for the connected semisimple group case and general linear group case.

2.1.
Invariant algebraic
D
-modules

Consider a smooth affine variety
X
, with
\mathbb{C}[X]
its algebra of regular functions, and let
\mathcal{D}_{X}
denote the algebra of differential operators on
X
. Then
\mathbb{C}[X]^{\oplus n}
naturally forms a (left)
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module via differentiation. Furthermore, the smoothness of
X
ensures that
\mathcal{D}_{X}
is generated by the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields
\text{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[X])
.

We consider the ring
\mathbb{C}[X]
and its module of Kähler differentials

\Omega_{\mathbb{C}[X]}
. An element
\alpha\in M_{n}(\Omega_{\mathbb{C}[X]})
is a matrix
\left(\alpha_{ij}\right)_{i,j}
with entries

\alpha_{ij}\in\Omega_{\mathbb{C}[X]}
.

We say that
\alpha
is a
flat

1
-form if it satisfies

d\alpha+\alpha\wedge\alpha=0.
In coordinates,
\alpha\wedge\alpha
is computed by ordinary
matrix multiplication combined with the wedge product of
1
-forms. Thus flatness means that the corresponding connection is
integrable
. We denote by
M_{n}(\Omega_{\mathbb{C}[X]})^{f}
the set of all such flat
1
-forms.

We now define a
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module structure on the free

\mathbb{C}[X]
-module
\mathbb{C}[X]^{\oplus n}
. Let
\alpha\in M_{n}(\Omega_{\mathbb{C}[X]})^{f}
and let

e_{i}=(\delta_{ik})_{k}\in\mathbb{C}[X]^{\oplus n}
be the standard basis. We define the action of a derivation
v\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[X])
on this basis by

v(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n})=(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n})\alpha(v).

Together with the natural
\mathbb{C}[X]
-module structure on

\mathbb{C}[X]^{\oplus n}
and the flatness condition of
\alpha
, the action extends to a well-defined
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module,
which we denote by
M_{\alpha}
.

We have the following proposition as in
[
Wei25
, Proposition 1.1]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Wei)
.

Let
\alpha\in M_{n}(\Omega_{\mathbb{C}[X]})^{f}
and
\beta\in M_{m}(\Omega_{\mathbb{C}[X]})^{f}
. Then

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{X}}(M_{\alpha},M_{\beta})=\left\{X\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C}[X])\;|\;v(X)=X\alpha(v)-\beta(v)X\text{ for all }v\in\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[X])\right\},

where

is the differentiation of

by

entrywise.

Let
G
be a linear algebraic group acting on a smooth affine variety
X
.
For each

, the action induces an automorphism
a_{g}:X\to X,a_{g}(x)=g\cdot x.
Pullback on regular functions is
g^{*}:\mathbb{C}[X]\to\mathbb{C}[X],\ g^{*}(f)=f\circ a_{g},
and the induced pullback on Kähler differentials is defined by

Thus

\Omega_{\mathbb{C}[X]}^{G}=\{\omega\in\Omega_{\mathbb{C}[X]}\mid g^{*}\omega=\omega\text{ for all }g\in G\}.

When

Hence any

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{\dim G}A_{i}\,x^{i},\text{ where }A_{i}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\text{ and }x^{i}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

An

Now we have Proposition 1.3 of

If

Let

We have the following theorem as in

Theorem 2.5

Let

be a linear algebraic group. Then the Lie algebra

\mathrm{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[G])

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{G}}(M_{\alpha},M_{\beta})=\{\,X\in M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C}[G])|v(X)=X\alpha(v)-\beta(v)X\text{ for all }v\in\text{Der}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[G])\,\}.

For
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\Hom_{\mathrm{Lie\ alg}}(\mathfrak{g},M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{G}}(M_{\rho_{1}},M_{\rho_{2}})=\{X\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}[G])|v(X)=X\rho_{1}(v)-\rho_{2}(v)X\text{ for all }v\in\mathfrak{g}\}.

Wei proved the following proposition in
[
Wei25
]
for the special case of weakly exponential algebraic groups. Here we state and prove a version for arbitrary complex affine algebraic groups.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
G
be a complex affine algebraic group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\text{Lie}(G)
.
Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
be a Lie algebra homomorphism, and let

M_{\rho}
be the left-translation invariant
\mathcal{D}_{G}
-module attached to
\rho
.
Assume there exists an algebraic group homomorphism

\widetilde{\rho}:G\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}

such that
\widetilde{\rho}\circ\exp=\exp\circ\rho
.
Then
M_{\rho}\cong M_{0}
, where
0
denotes the
n
-dimensional trivial representation.

Proof.

Define
X\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}[G])
by
X(g):=\widetilde{\rho}(g)
.
Since
\det(X)=\det\circ\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathbb{C}[G]^{\times}
, we have
X\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[G])
. We claim that
X
satisfies the differential equation

(1)

v(X)=X\rho(v)\text{ for all }v\in\mathfrak{g},

where
v\in\mathfrak{g}
is identified with the corresponding left-invariant vector field on
G
.
Indeed, for any
g\in G
,

v(X)(g)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}X\bigl(g\exp(tv)\bigr)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\widetilde{\rho}\bigl(g\exp(tv)\bigr).

Using that
\widetilde{\rho}
is a group homomorphism and
\widetilde{\rho}(\exp(tv))=\exp(t\rho(v))
,

\widetilde{\rho}\bigl(g\exp(tv)\bigr)=X(g)\exp(t\rho(v)).

Differentiating at
t=0
yields (
1
).
As in Corollary
2.6
, the matrix
X
defines a

\mathcal{D}_{G}
-module morphism

M_{\rho}\to M_{0}.

Since
X\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[G])
, this morphism is an isomorphism with inverse given by
X^{-1}
.∎

2.2.
Maurer–Cartan form

We introduce the unique left-invariant
\mathfrak{g}:=\text{Lie}(G)
-valued
1
-form on an algebraic group
G
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
G
be a smooth algebraic group,
e\in G
is the identity, and

\mathfrak{g}:=\text{Lie}(G)=T_{e}G
. We call
\theta\in\Gamma(G,\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{G})
the
left
Maurer–Cartan form
, if it is the unique left-invariant
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1
-form characterized by

\theta_{e}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}\text{ and }(L_{g})^{*}\theta=\theta\ \ \text{for all }g\in G.

(Equivalently,
\theta_{g}=(dL_{g^{-1}})_{g}:T_{g}G\to T_{e}G=\mathfrak{g}
.)

We have the following Maurer–Cartan equation, see
[
Tu20
, Theorem 15.3]
.

Proposition 2.9

(Tu)
.

The left Maurer–Cartan form
\theta
on an
algebraic group
G
satisfies

d\theta+\theta\wedge\theta=0.

Remark 2.10
.

For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, the
1
-form
g^{-1}dg
with
g\in G
satisfies the above equation, so it is the unique Maurer–Cartan form.

Proposition 2.11
.

For
G
a smooth algebraic group with
\mathfrak{g}:=\text{Lie}(G)
, let
\nabla=d+\alpha
be a left-translation invariant flat connection on
\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\oplus n}
. The left-invariant
\alpha
are exactly those of the form

\alpha=(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{G}})(\theta),

where
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism and
\theta
is the left Maurer–Cartan form on
G
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
, the Maurer-Cartan equation of
\theta
is equivalent to a Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. Note that
(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{G}})(\theta)
satisfies the Maurer-Cartan equation, so it is a left Maurer–Cartan form.

On the other hand, the flatness of
\alpha
shows that it is also the left Maurer–Cartan form, see Proposition
2.9
. We have
\alpha=(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{G}})(\theta)
by uniqueness.
∎

3.1.
Unipotent case

Let

Definition 3.1

We abuse notation and denote by

n

, i.e., all

(g\cdot\rho)(x):=g\rho(x)g^{-1},\text{ where }g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}\text{ and }x\in\mathfrak{n}.

The

\mathcal{M}_{U,n}\;:=\;\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Lie\,alg}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\sslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}\;=\;\Spec\left(\mathbb{C}\left[\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Lie\,alg}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\right]^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}\right).

We first have the classification theorems of nilpotent Lie algebra, see
[
Wei25
, Proposition 0.2 and Theorem 0.1]

Theorem 3.2

(Wei)
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be a complex nilpotent Lie algebra and let

Then
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Lie\,alg}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\sslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}\;=\;S_{n}\mathbb{C}^{\,l}
, where

-th symmetric product of
\mathbb{C}^{\,l}
.

Theorem 3.3

(Wei)
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be a complex nilpotent Lie algebra and

be the corresponding unipotent group. Then

\frac{\{\text{Invariant $D$-modules of rank $n$ on $U$}\}}{\sim}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Lie\,alg}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\sslash\mathrm{GL}_{n},

4.
Semisimple case

Theorem 4.1

(Semisimple case: finite central kernel controls invariant
D
-modules)
.

Let
G
be a connected semisimple complex linear algebraic group. Let

\pi:G^{sc}\twoheadrightarrow G
be the simply connected central cover, and set

\Gamma:=\Ker(\pi)\subset Z(G^{sc}),\text{ where }|\Gamma|<\infty.

Fix

. Then there is a natural bijection

\frac{\{\text{Invariant }D\text{-modules of rank $n$ on $G$}\}}{\sim}=\Hom(\Gamma,\mathrm{GL}_{n})/\mathrm{GL}_{n},

where
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
acts on
\Hom(\Gamma,\mathrm{GL}_{n})
by conjugation.

The proof will be given in Section
4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{G}\text{-mod}^{G}
be the category of finite-rank
left-translation invariant algebraic
D
-modules on
G
(with arbitrary
D
-module
morphisms). Let
\mathrm{Rep}(\Gamma)
be the tensor category of finite-dimensional complex
representations of the finite group
\Gamma
. As an application of the main theorem, we obtain the following consequence in the setting of tensor categories, whose proof is given in Section
4.5
.

Theorem 4.2
.

There is an equivalence of tensor categories

\mathcal{D}_{G}\text{-mod}^{G}\simeq\mathrm{Rep}(\Gamma).

4.1.
Outline of the proof of Theorem
4.1

We claim the following steps for the connected semisimple algeraic groups:

Step 0.

There is a
central isogeny

\pi\colon\widetilde{G}\twoheadrightarrow G

with
\widetilde{G}
connected semisimple and simply connected, and
\Ker(\pi)
finite central.
Here
\Ker(\pi)
coincides with the Galois group
\Aut_{G}(G^{sc})
, so it is the
fundamental group
of
G
.

Step 0.

An invariant
D
-module of rank
n
is encoded by a Lie algebra representation

\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}.

If
\pi:\widetilde{G}\twoheadrightarrow{G}
is the simply connected semisimple cover with

\Lie(\widetilde{G})=\mathfrak{g}
, then
\rho
integrates to a (unique) algebraic group homomorphism

\widetilde{\rho}\colon\widetilde{G}\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}.

Step 0.

Over
\widetilde{G}
, the invariant
D
-module corresponding to
\rho
becomes isomorphic to the trivial
one by the algebraic gauge transformation
X:=\widetilde{\rho}
. More precisely, for every

v\in\mathfrak{g}=\Lie(\widetilde{G})
, we have

v(X)=X\rho(v),\text{ with }X\colon\widetilde{G}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n},

so
X
gives an isomorphism between the
\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{G}}
-modules
M_{\rho}
and
M_{0}
.

Step 0.

The trivialization on
\widetilde{G}
descends to
G
if and only if it is compatible with the action of
the finite central subgroup
\Ker(\pi)
. Concretely, if
\widetilde{\rho}\colon\widetilde{G}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}

is the integrated representation, then descent holds if and only if

\widetilde{\rho}(z)=I_{n}\text{ for all }z\in\Ker(\pi),

equivalently,

\Ker(\pi)\subset\Ker(\widetilde{\rho}).

In this case the gauge transformation
X=\widetilde{\rho}
is
\Ker(\pi)
-invariant and therefore comes
from an algebraic gauge transformation on
G
. If
\widetilde{\rho}|_{\Ker(\pi)}
is nontrivial, then the
trivialization on
\widetilde{G}
does not descend, and one can obtain distinct objects on
G
according
to the induced
\Ker(\pi)
-action on the fiber
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Step 0.

Since
\Ker(\pi)
is finite central, descending a trivial object from
\widetilde{G}
is equivalent to
choosing a representation of
\Ker(\pi)
on the fiber
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Hence the resulting isomorphism
classes are naturally parametrized by

\Hom(\Ker(\pi),\mathrm{GL}_{n})/\mathrm{GL}_{n},

where
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
acts by conjugation on
\Hom(\Ker(\pi),\mathrm{GL}_{n})
.
This matches the usual “flat connection
\leftrightarrow

\pi_{1}
-representation” (monodromy)
philosophy in the
C^{\infty}
/analytic setting.

4.2.
Failure of Theorem
4.1
without finite central kernel

We give a counterexample to Conjecture
B
in the
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
case.

Let
H=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, and let
G=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}=\mathrm{SL}_{2}/\{\pm I_{2}\}
. The quotient map
\pi\colon H\rightarrow G
is an algebraic surjective homomorphism with finite central kernel

\Ker\pi=\{\pm I_{2}\}\subset Z(H).

Thus
H
is the simply connected semisimple cover of
G
in the algebraic sense.

Consider the standard
2
-dimensional representation
\Phi\colon H\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}
given by inclusion. Let

\rho:=d\Phi_{e}:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{2}.

The map
\rho
corresponds to an invariant algebraic
D
-module
M_{\rho}
. Since
H
is simply connected, by Theorem
3.8
,
M_{\rho}
is algebraic gauge equivalent to the trivial one.

Now, the question is that, can this representation descend to
G=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
? To have a representation
\Psi\colon G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}
such that
\Phi=\Psi\circ\pi
, we need

\Phi(h)=\Psi(\pi(h))\text{ for all }h\in H.

In particular,
\pi(h)=e_{G}
if
h\in\Ker(\pi)
. Thus
\Phi(h)=\Psi(e_{G})=I_{2}
.

Now a necessary condition for the descent is

\Phi(h)=I_{2}\text{ for all }h\in\Ker(\pi).

Here
\Ker(\pi)=\{\pm I_{2}\}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. However,
\Phi(-I_{2})=-I_{2}
. Thus the standard representation doesn’t descend to a representation of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
.

Consider the adjoint representation

\Ad:\mathrm{SL}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})\cong\mathrm{GL}_{3}.

This representation does factors through
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
. Now we are seeing that
\Ker(\pi)
is the key.

4.3.
Equivalence between invariant
D
-modules

We use the standard equivalence in Section
2.1
between left-translation invariant algebraic
D
-modules on an affine algebraic group and left-invariant flat algebraic connections on the trivial vector bundle, modulo
algebraic gauge equivalence. Concretely, an object above is determined by a connection
\nabla=d+\alpha
on
\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\oplus n}

with
\alpha\in M_{n}(\Omega_{G})
left-invariant and satisfying
d\alpha+\alpha\wedge\alpha=0
, and a morphism

\nabla\to\nabla^{\prime}
is an algebraic gauge transformation
X:G\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}
with

X^{-1}dX+X^{-1}\alpha X=\alpha^{\prime},

see Proposition
2.1
. We first record three basic facts.

Lemma 4.3

(Integration of Lie algebra representations)
.

Let
G^{sc}
be a simply connected semisimple complex algebraic group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then every Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is the differential of a unique algebraic group
homomorphism
\Phi:G^{sc}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.

Proof.

See
[
Ser92
, Page 152, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Lemma 4.4

(Automorphisms of the trivial connection)
.

Let
X
be a connected smooth complex algebraic variety. Then

\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{D}_{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus n},d)=\mathrm{GL}_{n},

where
d
is the trivial flat connection on
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus n}
.

Proof.

A
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-linear endomorphism of
(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus n},d)
is the same as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear endomorphism commuting with
d
.
Writing it as a matrix
F\in M_{n}(\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}))
, the commutation condition is
dF=0
, so every entry of
F
is a
regular function with vanishing differential. On a connected smooth complex variety such a function is constant,
hence
F\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(Descent along finite étale Galois covers)
.

Let
p:Y\to X
be a finite étale Galois cover of smooth varieties with Galois group
\Gamma
.
Then pullback induces an equivalence between algebraic
D
-modules on
X
and
\Gamma
-equivariant algebraic

D
-modules on
Y
.

Proof.

Since
p
is finite étale, formation of differential operators is étale local and one has

\mathcal{D}_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{D}_{X}\cong p^{*}\mathcal{D}_{X}
; see
[
HTT08
, Proposition 1.2.9]
. Because
p
is finite faithfully
flat and Galois, quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules satisfy Galois descent, and the compatibility with

\mathcal{D}_{Y}\simeq p^{*}\mathcal{D}_{X}
upgrades this to
D
-modules.
∎

.

We obtain an equivalence of groupoids

\mathsf{Gpd}_{n}(\mathcal{D}_{G})\cong\mathsf{Gpd}_{n}(\mathcal{D}_{G^{sc}})^{\Gamma},

where
\mathsf{Gpd}_{n}(-)

The morphism

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
4.1

By Theorem
3.8
, every algebraic
D
-modules on
G^{sc}
is isomorphic to the trivial one

\left(\mathcal{O}_{G^{sc}}^{\oplus n},d\right)
. Consequently, by Lemma
4.5
, a
\Gamma
-equivariant algebraic
D
-module is exactly a choice of

\Gamma
-equivariant structure on the trivial connection. Concretely, for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we must choose an
isomorphism of
D
-modules

\varphi_{\gamma}\colon t_{\gamma}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{G^{sc}}^{\oplus n},d)\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathcal{O}_{G^{sc}}^{\oplus n},d)

where
t_{\gamma}
is the deck transformation corresponding to
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
satisfying the cocycle condition
\varphi_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}=\varphi_{\gamma_{1}}\circ t_{\gamma_{1}}^{*}(\varphi_{\gamma_{2}})
.
Since
(\mathcal{O}_{G^{sc}}^{\oplus n},d)
is translation-invariant, each
\varphi_{\gamma}
is an automorphism of the
same trivial connection, hence by Lemma
4.4
, it is given by a constant matrix
A_{\gamma}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
. The cocycle
condition becomes
A_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}=A_{\gamma_{1}}A_{\gamma_{2}}
, so
\gamma\mapsto A_{\gamma}
is a group homomorphism

\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.

Finally, two such
\Gamma
-equivariant structures
(A_{\gamma})
and
(A^{\prime}_{\gamma})
define isomorphic
\Gamma
-equivariant

D
-modules if and only if there exists an automorphism of the trivial connection, i.e., a constant matrix

B\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, such that
A^{\prime}_{\gamma}=BA_{\gamma}B^{-1}
for all
\gamma
. This is precisely conjugacy of
homomorphisms

. The claimed bijection follows.

4.5.
Proof of Theorem
4.2

4.5.1.
Equivariant Descent for invariant
D
-modules

Since
\pi
is a central isogeny in characteristic
0
, it is a
finite étale morphism, and the right action of
\Gamma\subset G^{sc}
on
G^{sc}
makes
\pi
into
a principal
\Gamma
-bundle (a
\Gamma
-torsor). For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, let

t_{\gamma}\colon G^{sc}\to G^{sc}
denote the right multiplication (deck transformation) by
\gamma
. Then
\pi\circ t_{\gamma}=\pi
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{G^{sc}}\text{-mod}^{G^{sc}}
be the analogous category on
G^{sc}
. Pullback along
\pi
defines a
tensor functor

\pi^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}_{G}\text{-mod}^{G}\to\mathcal{D}_{G^{sc}}\text{-mod}^{G^{sc}}.

Moreover, for every
M\in\mathcal{D}_{G}\text{-mod}^{G}
and every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the identity

\pi\circ t_{\gamma}=\pi
yields a canonical identification

t_{\gamma}^{*}(\pi^{*}M)=\pi^{*}M,

and these identifications satisfy the cocycle condition in
\gamma
. In other words,
\pi^{*}M

carries a canonical
\Gamma
-equivariant structure. Thus
\pi^{*}
refines to a tensor functor

\pi^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}_{G}\text{-mod}^{G}\longrightarrow(\mathcal{D}_{G^{sc}}\text{-mod}^{G^{sc}})^{\Gamma},

where
(\mathcal{D}_{G^{sc}}\text{-mod}^{G^{sc}})^{\Gamma}
denotes the category of
\Gamma
-equivariant objects in

\mathcal{D}_{G^{sc}}\text{-mod}^{G^{sc}}
.

Conversely, by standard étale descent for vector bundles with integrable connection
along a finite étale torsor,
\pi^{*}
is an equivalence of tensor categories

\mathcal{D}_{G}\text{-mod}^{G}\simeq(\mathcal{D}_{G^{sc}}\text{-mod}^{G^{sc}})^{\Gamma}.

Moreover, left-translation invariance descends because the left translations on
G^{sc}
commute
with the right
\Gamma
-action (since
\Gamma
is central).

4.5.2.
Classification via representations of
\Gamma

Since
G^{sc}
is
connected semisimple and simply connected, Theorem
3.8
, implies that every object of
\mathcal{D}_{G^{sc}}\text{-mod}^{G^{sc}}
of rank
r
is isomorphic to the
trivial connection
(\mathcal{O}_{G^{sc}}^{\oplus r},d)
.

Similar to Lemma
4.4
, any morphism
f\colon({\mathcal{O}_{G^{sc}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V,d})\to({\mathcal{O}_{G^{sc}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W,d})
of
D
-modules is constant, we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{G^{sc}}\text{-mod}^{G^{sc}}}\bigl(({\mathcal{O}_{G^{sc}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V,d}),({\mathcal{O}_{G^{sc}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W,d})\bigr)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V,W).

Thus evaluation at the identity gives a tensor equivalence

\mathcal{D}_{G^{sc}}\text{-mod}^{G^{sc}}\simeq\mathrm{Vec}_{\mathbb{C}},\ ({\mathcal{O}_{G^{sc}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V,d})\mapsto V

with quasi-inverse

.

Passing to
\Gamma
-equivariant objects yields

5.1.
Preliminaries for the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}

5.1.1.
Invariant flat connections and Lie algebra representations

First we parametrize the invariant flat connections (hence invariant
D
-modules) by the following proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, the left-invariant flat connections

\nabla=d+\alpha
on
\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\oplus n}
are parametrized by
\rho\in\Hom_{\mathrm{Lie\,alg}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2},\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, modulo algebraic gauge equivalence.

Proof.

A left-invariant
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-valued algebraic
1
-form
\alpha
on
G
is determined by its value at the
identity, hence by a linear map

\rho_{\alpha}\colon\mathfrak{gl}_{2}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\ \rho_{\alpha}(X)=\alpha_{e}(X),

where
X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
is identified with the corresponding left-invariant vector field.
By Proposition
2.3
, the flatness condition

d\alpha+\alpha\wedge\alpha=0

is equivalent to
\rho_{\alpha}
being a Lie algebra homomorphism:

\rho_{\alpha}([X,Y])=[\rho_{\alpha}(X),\rho_{\alpha}(Y)]\text{ for all }X,Y\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}.

Thus invariant flat connections are parametrized by

\rho\in\Hom_{\mathrm{Lie\,alg}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2},\mathfrak{gl}_{n})

modulo algebraic gauge equivalence.
∎

5.1.2.
Invariant flat
1
-forms

To understand the invariant flat
1
-forms
\alpha
, first we compute the corresponding Lie algebra representations.
We have a direct sum decomposition

\mathfrak{gl}_{2}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{C}\cdot I_{2},\ [\mathfrak{gl}_{2},\mathfrak{gl}_{2}]=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}.

Given
\rho:\mathfrak{gl}_{2}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, and denote by
\rho_{ss}
the restrictions of
\rho

to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Set
A:=\rho(I_{2})\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

If
\rho_{ss}=0
, then for any
X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
one has

X=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(X)}{2}I_{2}+X_{0},\text{ where }X_{0}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}.

Hence we have the following result.

Proposition 5.3
.

The Lie algebra representation
\rho\in\Hom_{\mathrm{Lie\,alg}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2},\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
with
\rho_{ss}=0
is given by

\rho(X)=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(X)}{2}A,

where
X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
.

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\omega:=d\log(\det)
be the left-invariant
1
-form on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
. For every
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and every tangent vector
v\in T_{A}G
, write

v=AX
with
X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
(via left translation). Then

\omega_{A}(v)=\mathrm{tr}(X).

In particular, under the identification
T_{e}G=\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
, one has
\omega_{e}(X)=\mathrm{tr}(X).

Proof.

Consider the curve
A(t):=A(I_{2}+tX)
in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
for
t
near
0
. Then
A(0)=A
and

A^{\prime}(0)=AX=v
. Using multiplicativity of the determinant and Taylor series of
\det(I_{2}+tX)
,

\omega_{A}(v)=\frac{(d\det)_{A}(v)}{\det(A)}=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\det(I_{2}+tX)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\left(1+t\,\mathrm{tr}(X)+O(t^{2})\right)=\mathrm{tr}(X).

At
A=e
, this reads
\omega_{e}(X)=\mathrm{tr}(X)
.
∎

Combining Proposition
5.3
and Lemma
5.4
, we obtain the invariant
1
-form
\alpha
.

Proposition 5.5
.

For any Lie algebra representation
\rho\in\Hom_{\mathrm{Lie\,alg}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2},\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, if
\rho_{ss}=0
, then the corresponding invariant
1
-form
\alpha
is given by

\alpha=\frac{A}{2}\omega=\frac{A}{2}d\log(\det),

i.e., it is the pullback along
\det
of
\frac{A}{2}d\log(t)
on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

5.1.3.
A central cover of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}

Consider the algebraic homomorphism

Proof of Theorem

5.2.1.
Surjectivity

We kill the

Let

\psi(-t)=C^{-1}\psi(t)
, and therefore the map

X\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n},\ X(t,s):=\Phi(s)\psi(t)

is
\Gamma
-invariant:

X(-t,-s)=\Phi(-s)\psi(-t)=\Phi(s)CC^{-1}\psi(t)=X(t,s).

Hence
X
descends to an algebraic gauge transformation on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
.

Let
\alpha
be the invariant flat form corresponding to
\rho
.
Pulling back to
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}
and applying the gauge
X
kills the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-component of
the connection form; after descending back to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, the resulting invariant flat form
\alpha^{\prime}
corresponds to a
representation
\rho^{\prime}
with

\rho^{\prime}|_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}=0.

By Proposition
5.5
, the new form is

\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{A^{\prime}}{2}d\log(\det)

where
A^{\prime}:=\rho^{\prime}(I_{2})
. Hence
\alpha^{\prime}
is pulled back from
\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
This proves surjectivity of the pullback map on isomorphism classes.

5.2.2.
Injectivity

We show that any gauge between pullbacks factors through
\det
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and set

\alpha_{i}=\frac{A_{i}}{2}d\log(\det),\text{ where }i=1,2.

Assume
M_{\alpha_{1}}\cong M_{\alpha_{2}}
as invariant
D
-modules on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
.
Then there exists an algebraic gauge
X\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{GL}_{2}])
such that

(2)

X^{-1}dX+X^{-1}\alpha_{1}X=\alpha_{2},

see Proposition
2.1
. Let
\omega:=d\log(\det)\in\Gamma(\mathrm{GL}_{2},\Omega_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}})
, and let
v
be the left-invariant
vector field on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
corresponding to some
x\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. By Lemma
5.4
, we have

\omega_{e}(x)=\mathrm{tr}(x)=0.

Because
\omega
and
v
are both left-invariant, the function
\omega(v)
on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
is
left-invariant, hence constant. Evaluating at the identity gives

(d\log\det)(v)=\omega_{e}(x)=0.

Therefore, if
\alpha_{i}=\frac{A_{i}}{2}\omega
for
i=1,2
, then

\alpha_{i}(v)=\frac{A_{i}}{2}\omega(v)=\frac{A_{i}}{2}\cdot 0=0,\ i=1,2.

Hence the gauge equation (
2
) forces

v(X)=0\text{ for all }v\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}.

Equivalently,
X
is constant along left
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-cosets. The fibers of
\det\colon\mathrm{GL}_{2}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
are precisely
those cosets, so
X
factors through
\det
:

X=\widetilde{X}\circ\det\text{ for some }\widetilde{X}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{G}_{m}]).

Substituting
X=\widetilde{X}\circ\det
into the gauge equation shows that
\widetilde{X}
is exactly an algebraic
gauge transformation on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
relating
\frac{A_{1}}{2}d\log(t)
and
\frac{A_{2}}{2}d\log(t)
.
Thus, if
\Det^{*}(M_{1})\cong\Det^{*}(M_{2})
on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, then
M_{1}\cong M_{2}
on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. This proves injectivity.

6.
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
case

We finally prove that for general
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
, pullback along
\det
induces the bijection in Conjecture
B
.

Theorem 6.1

(
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
case: reduction to
\det
)
.

Let
G=\mathrm{GL}_{r}
and let
\det:G\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
be the determinant. Fix
n\geq 1
. Then pullback along
\det

induces a bijection on isomorphism classes of invariant algebraic
D
-modules of rank
n
:

\Det^{*}\colon\frac{\{\text{Invariant }D\text{-modules of rank $n$ on $\mathbb{G}_{m}$}\}}{\sim}=\frac{\{\text{Invariant }D\text{-modules of rank $n$ on }\mathrm{GL}_{r}\}}{\sim}.

The proof will be given in Section
6.2
. Note that
G=\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the one-dimensional algebraic torus. By Theorem
3.6
, we obtain the following explicit classification.

Corollary 6.2
.

In the case
G=\mathrm{GL}_{r}
, we have

\frac{\{\text{Invariant }D\text{-modules of rank $n$ on $\mathrm{GL}_{r}$}\}}{\sim}=\Hom(\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{GL}_{n})/\mathrm{GL}_{n}.

Recall that
\mathcal{D}_{G}\text{-mod}^{G}
is the category of finite-rank
left-translation invariant algebraic
D
-modules on
G
(with arbitrary
D
-module
morphisms). As an application of Theorem
6.1
, we obtain the following consequence in the setting of tensor categories, whose proof is given in Section
6.3
.

Theorem 6.3
.

The pullback along
\det
defines an equivalence of tensor
categories

\text{det}^{*}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\text{-mod}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}\text{-mod}^{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}.

6.1.
Preliminaries for the
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
case

6.1.1.
Maurer–Cartan form and Lie algebra representations

As in Section
2.1
, the invariant objects of rank
n
on an affine algebraic group
G
are the same as left-invariant flat connections

\nabla=d+\alpha
on
\mathcal{O}_{G}^{\oplus n}
, modulo algebraic gauge equivalence.

Recall that in Section
2.2
, for
G=\mathrm{GL}_{r}
, left-invariant

\alpha
correspond to Lie algebra homomorphisms

\rho:\mathfrak{gl}_{r}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\ \alpha=(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}})(\theta_{G}),

where
\theta_{G}=g^{-1}dg
is the left Maurer–Cartan form on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
.

6.1.2.
A central cover of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
.

Consider the surjective homomorphism

p\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{SL}_{r}\to\mathrm{GL}_{r},\ (t,s)\mapsto ts,

whose kernel is the diagonally embedded finite central subgroup

\mu_{r}=\{(\zeta,\zeta^{-1}I_{r})\mid\zeta^{r}=1\}.

6.1.3.
Pullback of the Maurer–Cartan form

Let
\omega=t^{-1}dt
and
\theta=s^{-1}ds
be the Maurer–Cartan form on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
and
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
, respectively.
A direct computation gives

p^{*}(\theta_{G})=I_{r}\omega+\theta.

Write
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}=\mathfrak{sl}_{r}\oplus\mathbb{C}\cdot I_{r}
, and denote by
\rho_{ss}
the restrictions of
\rho

to
\mathfrak{sl}_{r}
. Set
A:=\rho(I_{r})\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

Since pullback
p^{\ast}
acts on the
\Omega_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}
-factor and
\rho
acts on the
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-factor,
they commute. Therefore

p^{*}\alpha=p^{*}(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}})(\theta_{G})=(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{SL}_{r}}})(p^{*}\theta_{G})=(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}})(I_{r}\omega)+(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}})(\theta).

By
\mathbb{C}
-linearity we get

(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}})(I_{r}\omega)=\rho(I_{r})\omega=A\omega.

Moreover, since
\theta
is
\mathfrak{sl}_{r}
-valued, we have
(\rho\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}})(\theta)=(\rho_{ss}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}})(\theta)
.
Putting everything together yields

p^{*}\alpha=A\omega+(\rho_{ss}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}})(\theta).

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
6.1

6.2.1.
Surjectivity

We kill the
\mathfrak{sl}_{r}
-part by a descended gauge.

Because
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
is semisimple and simply connected, the Lie algebra homomorphism

\rho_{ss}:\mathfrak{sl}_{r}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
integrates to an algebraic group homomorphism

\Phi\colon\mathrm{SL}_{r}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}\text{ with }\Phi^{-1}d\Phi=(\rho_{ss}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}})(\theta),

see Lemma
4.3
. Gauge transforming
d+p^{*}\alpha
by
X_{0}(t,s)=\Phi(s)
kills the
\rho_{ss}(\theta)
-term and yields the connection

d+A\omega\text{ on }\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{SL}_{r}.

To descend this gauge transformation to
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
, we must ensure
\mu_{r}
-invariance. The restriction of
\Phi
to the
central subgroup
\mu_{r}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{r}
is a representation of the finite cyclic group
\mu_{r}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, hence it is
diagonalizable and decomposes into characters. Choose an algebraic homomorphism

\Psi:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}

whose restriction to
\mu_{r}\subset\mathbb{G}_{m}
agrees with the representation

\zeta\mapsto\Phi(\zeta^{-1}I_{r})
(for example, diagonalize and extend each character
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{k}
to

t\mapsto t^{k}
). Define

X(t,s):=\Phi(s)\Psi(t)^{-1}.

Fix
\zeta\in\mu_{r}
,
s\in\mathrm{SL}_{r}
and
t\in\mathbb{G}_{m}
, we have

X(\zeta t,\zeta^{-1}s)=\Phi(\zeta^{-1}I_{r})\Phi(s)\Phi(\zeta^{-1}I_{r})^{-1}\Psi(t)^{-1}.

Since
\zeta^{-1}I_{r}\in Z(\mathrm{SL}_{r})
is central, it commutes
with
s
, hence
\Phi(\zeta^{-1}I_{r})
commutes with
\Phi(s)
.
Therefore,

X(\zeta t,\zeta^{-1}s)=\Phi(s)\Psi(t)^{-1}=X(t,s).

Thus
X
is
\mu_{r}
-invariant. By Lemma
4.5
,
X
descends to an algebraic gauge transformation on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
.

Write
\Psi^{-1}d\Psi=B\omega
for some constant matrix
B\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
(indeed, in a basis where

\Psi(t)=\mathrm{diag}(t^{k_{1}},\dots,t^{k_{n}})
, one has
B=\mathrm{diag}(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
).
A routine calculation shows that gauging by
X
sends
d+p^{*}\alpha
to

d+(A+B)\omega\text{ on }\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{SL}_{r}.

Let
p:\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{SL}_{r}\to\mathrm{GL}_{r}
be given by
p(t,s)=ts
, where
t\in\mathbb{G}_{m}

is viewed as the scalar matrix
tI_{r}
. We have

(\det\circ p)(t,s)=\det(tI_{r})\det(s)=t^{r}.

Therefore, by functoriality of pullback and the chain rule,

p^{*}(d\log\det)(t,s)=d\log(t^{r})=r\frac{dt}{t}=r\omega.

Similar to the proof of Proposition
5.5
, the descended
connection on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
is the pullback along
\det
of the invariant connection on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
with form

\frac{1}{r}(A+B)t^{-1}dt,

where
t
is the coordinate on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. This proves that every invariant
D
-module on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}

is isomorphic to one pulled back from
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, i.e.,
\Det^{*}
is surjective on isomorphism classes.

6.2.2.
Injectivity

We show that any gauge between pullbacks factors through
\det
.

Let
\nabla_{1},\nabla_{2}
be invariant connections on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
and assume

\Det^{*}\nabla_{1}\simeq\Det^{*}\nabla_{2}
on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
. Restrict to the subgroup
\mathrm{SL}_{r}=\det^{-1}(1)
.
By Theorem
3.8
, both restrictions are the trivial connection
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}^{\oplus n},d)
, so any isomorphism between them
restricts to a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}
-automorphism of the trivial connection on
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
, hence (as in Lemma
4.4
)
it is given by a constant matrix
C\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
. Composing the given gauge transformation with
C^{-1}
, we may
assume the gauge transformation restricts to the identity on
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
.

Let
X\colon\mathrm{GL}_{r}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}
be such a gauge transformation. Let
v
be a left-invariant vector field on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
coming from some
Y\in\mathfrak{sl}_{r}
.
Thus, for each
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}
,

v_{g}=(dL_{g})_{e}(Y)=gY\in T_{g}\mathrm{GL}_{r}.

Recall the standard identity in Lemma
5.4
, we have

d(\log\det)_{g}(\delta g)=\mathrm{tr}(g^{-1}\delta g),\ \delta g\in T_{g}\mathrm{GL}_{r}.

Equivalently,
d\log(\det)=\mathrm{tr}(\theta_{G})
where
\theta_{G}=g^{-1}dg
is the left
Maurer–Cartan form. Evaluating on
v_{g}=gY
gives

(d\log\det)_{g}(v_{g})=\mathrm{tr}(g^{-1}\cdot gY)=\mathrm{tr}(Y)=0.

In this sense
\det
(and also
\log\det
) is constant
along the left-invariant directions coming from
\mathfrak{sl}_{r}
: these vector fields are tangent
to the fibers of
\det
.

Now let
\nabla_{i}=d+\beta_{i}
be a connection on the trivial bundle of rank
n
over
\mathbb{G}_{m}
,
with connection
1
-form
\beta_{i}\in\Gamma(\mathbb{G}_{m},\Omega_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\otimes\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
.
Its pullback along
\det:\mathrm{GL}_{r}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
is

\Det^{*}\nabla_{i}=d+\Det^{*}\beta_{i}.

For any

v\in\mathfrak{sl}_{r}
, we have

(\Det^{*}\beta_{i})(v)=\beta_{i}(\Det^{*}v)=0.

For
\beta_{i}=A_{i}\omega
,
we obtain

(\Det^{*}\beta_{i})(v)=A_{i}\cdot(d\log\det)(v)=A_{i}\cdot 0=0.

Thus
\Det^{*}\nabla_{i}(v)=0
for all
v\in\mathfrak{sl}_{r}
.

Hence the gauge equation forces

v(X)=0\text{ for all }v\in\mathfrak{sl}_{r}.

This means
X
is constant along left cosets of
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
, i.e., it factors through the quotient

\mathrm{GL}_{r}/\mathrm{SL}_{r}\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m}
via
\det
. Thus

X=\widetilde{X}\circ\det\text{ for some }\widetilde{X}\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}\text{ algebraic}.

Substituting this factorization into the gauge equation on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
shows exactly that
\widetilde{X}
is a gauge
transformation between
\nabla_{1}
and
\nabla_{2}
on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Hence
\nabla_{1}\simeq\nabla_{2}
, proving injectivity.

6.3.
Proof of Theorem
6.3

We first need the following lemma.

Lemma 6.4
.

Consider the left action of
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
by
left multiplication. Then the determinant map

\det\colon\mathrm{GL}_{r}\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}

is the categorical quotient for this action. Equivalently, pullback along
\det
induces an
isomorphism of the rings of global sections of the structure sheaves, regarded as
\mathbb{C}
-algebras

\text{det}^{*}\colon\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{m})\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ \mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})^{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})=\mathbb{C}[x_{ij},\det^{-1}]
and

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
, so
\det^{*}\colon\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{m})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})
is the

\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism sending
t
to
\det
.

First,
\det
is
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
-invariant for the left action, hence
\det^{*}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{m}))\subset\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})^{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}
.

For the reverse inclusion, let
f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})^{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}
. For each
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
the fiber
\det^{-1}(c)
is the left
\mathrm{SL}_{r}
-orbit of the point

g_{c}:=\mathrm{diag}(c,1,\dots,1)
, because if
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}
has
\det(g)=c
then

gg_{c}^{-1}\in\mathrm{SL}_{r}
and
g=(gg_{c}^{-1})g_{c}
. Hence
f
is constant on each fiber

\det^{-1}(c)
.

Define a function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\varphi(c):=f(g_{c}).

Then
f=\varphi\circ\det
as regular functions on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
. To show
f\in\det^{*}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{m}))
, it remains to show that
\varphi
is regular on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, i.e,
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
.

Since
\det
is a smooth surjective morphism (in particular, faithfully flat), the
equalizer description of faithfully flat descent for rings implies that a regular function
on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
which is constant on the fibers of
\det
descends uniquely to a regular
function on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Concretely, the condition
f=\varphi\circ\det
on points is
equivalent to the identity

p_{1}^{*}(f)=p_{2}^{*}(f)\text{ in }\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{r}\times_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\mathrm{GL}_{r}),

where
p_{1},p_{2}
are the two projections. Faithful flatness of
\det
then implies the
existence and uniqueness of
\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{m})
with
f=\det^{*}(\varphi)
.
Thus
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})^{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}\subset\det^{*}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{m}))
, and we conclude

\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})^{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}=\det^{*}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{m}))
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.3
.

The functor
\det^{*}
is tensor, since pullback of integrable connections is compatible with
tensor products.

By Theorem
6.1
,
\det^{*}
is essentially surjective on objects.

We prove full faithfulness. Let
M,N\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\text{-mod}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
. By Proposition
2.11
, we may choose trivializations
in which the connections are left-invariant, so that

M\cong(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}{\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V},d+A\omega),\ N\cong(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}{\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W},d+B\omega),

where
\omega=d\log t
is the Maurer–Cartan form on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
and
A\in\mathrm{End}(V)
,
B\in\mathrm{End}(W)
are constant.
Then

\text{det}^{*}M\cong(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}{\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V},d+A\eta),\ \text{det}^{*}N\cong(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}{\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W},d+B\eta),

where
\eta=\det^{*}\omega=d\log(\det)
.

A morphism
f\colon\det^{*}M\to\det^{*}N
in
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}\text{-mod}^{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}
satisfies the

horizontality equation
, i.e., the morphism
f
commutes with connections:

\nabla_{F}\circ f=(f\otimes\text{id}_{\Omega_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}})\circ\nabla_{E}.

It is given by a
matrix
X\in{\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Hom}(V,W)}
,
and the horizontality equation becomes

dX+(BX-XA)\,\eta=0.

Let
v
be any left-invariant vector field on
\mathrm{GL}_{r}
arising from

\mathfrak{sl}_{r}\subset\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
. Since
\eta(v)=0
(equivalently,
\det
is
constant along
\mathfrak{sl}_{r}
-directions), evaluating the above equation on
v
gives

v(X)=0
. Hence each matrix entry of
X
is invariant under left multiplication by

\mathrm{SL}_{r}
, i.e., lies in
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})^{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}
.

By Lemma
6.4
, we have

\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{GL}_{r})^{\mathrm{SL}_{r}}=\det^{*}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}_{m})
. Therefore
there exists a unique matrix

Y\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Hom}(V,W)
such that

X=\det^{*}Y
. Substituting
X=\det^{*}Y
into the horizontality equation and using

\eta=\det^{*}\omega
yields

\text{det}^{*}(dY+(BY-YA)\omega)=0,

and since
\det
is dominant (the image is a dense subset of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
), we conclude

dY+(BY-YA)\omega=0.

This is exactly the horizontality condition for
Y
to define a morphism

g\colon M\to N\text{ in }\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\text{-mod}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}.

Thus every morphism
f
uniquely arises as

\det^{*}(g)
, proving that
\det^{*}
is fully faithful.

Hence
\det^{*}
is fully faithful and essentially surjective, and therefore an equivalence
of tensor categories.

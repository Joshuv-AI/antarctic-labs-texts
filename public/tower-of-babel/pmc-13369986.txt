Load distribution factor for moment in large-offset staggered widening bridges
Authors: Changxi Wang, Zixiang Zhang, Yu Bao, Xing Wei, Yu Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Staggered widening refers to a rehabilitation scheme in which new bridge piers are not aligned with existing ones due to constraints from current river embankments or road boundaries. To date, research on staggered widening bridges remains limited. Owing to the staggered span layout, girders are supported by piers of adjacent bridges at the mid-span region, leading to a vehicular load distribution mechanism distinct from that of conventional widening bridges, especially for interior girders near the closure slab. This study investigates the load distribution factor (LDF) for bending moment in staggered widening bridges. First, a simplified analytical model is developed using the rigid-jointed girder (RJG) method to derive transverse influence lines. The boundary conditions in the conventional RJG method are modified to capture the mid-span supporting effects provided by adjacent piers. The governing equation of the analytical model is derived based on the updated model configuration. The accuracy of the simplified model is then verified against finite element analysis results. Transverse load distribution characteristics of staggered widening bridges are compared with those of conventional widening schemes. On this basis, a parametric study is performed to examine the effects of key parameters, including diaphragm rigidity, flange rigidity, girder rotational stiffness, closure slab width, girder width, and the number of girders, on the LDF. Finally, based on the parametric analysis results, a generalized formula is proposed for predicting the girder LDF of staggered widening bridges.

Introduction

Bridge widening is a commonly adopted strategy to enhance the traffic capacity of congested bridges1–7. Due to constraints imposed by existing reiver embankments or road boundaries, the piers of adjacent bridges may not be aligned, resulting in the so-called staggered widening8. Since the demolition of existing bridges leads to increased project costs and carbon emissions, the implementation of staggered widening offers significant economic and environmental benefits.

Figure 1 presents a typical span arrangement of staggered widening bridges. Owing to the restriction of existing road boundaries, the piers of the new bridge have to be offset far from those of the existing bridge. In this case, the mid-span segment of the new bridge is spliced with the girder end segment of the existing bridge. This unique planar layout gives rise to two main engineering problems that are not encountered in conventional widening bridges. First, the mid-span segment of the new bridge deflects under vehicular loads and foundation settlements, whereas the girder end segment of the existing bridge dose not deflect due to the vertical restraint of piers. Under such circumstances, the deck of the existing bridge is subjected to an additional transverse hogging moment, which adversely affects the transverse loading condition of the bridge deck. Second, the mid-span segment of the new bridge is supported by the pier of the existing bridge. Consequently, the vehicular loads borne by the girders can deviate significantly from those of conventional widening bridges, especially for the interior girder adjacent to the existing bridge.Fig. 1Illustration of a typical staggered widening bridge.

Illustration of a typical staggered widening bridge.

Recently, the first engineering problem related to the transverse bearing capacity of the bridge deck has been investigated by Yang and Zhang8. A novel segment model was developed to reproduce the transverse loading condition of the bridge deck in staggered widening bridges. On this basis, the transverse bearing capacity of the existing bridge deck was evaluated through segment model tests. The test results indicated that the fundamental design principle for staggered widening bridges is to minimize the differential deflections between adjacent bridges. Ultimately, several recommendations were proposed for the design of staggered widening bridges.

Despite the recent study by Yang and Zhang8, concerns remain regarding the influence of the supporting effects provided by the existing bridge’s piers on the vehicular loads borne by the new bridge. Staggered widening is usually applied to low-to-medium span bridges, in which the live-to-dead load ratio is substantially high. Thus, the vehicular load distribution in each girder is directly related to the prestress and structural dimensions of each girder, both of which are closely associated with the structural safety and economic efficiency. However, the existing methods for calculating vehicular load distribution in conventional bridges9–24 may be inapplicable to staggered widening bridges due to their unique planar layout. Therefore, the vehicular load distribution in staggered widening bridges requires in-depth investigation, which constitutes the topic of this study.

To quantify the vehicular load distribution among multiple girders, the concept of the load distribution factor (LDF) has been introduced in previous studies9–24. The LDF is defined as a dimensionless factor representing the proportion of vehicular load distributed to an individual girder in a multi-girder bridge system, reflecting the transverse load-sharing behavior of the deck-girder system. To determine the LDF for each girder, a transverse influence line is first established to quantify the fraction of live load borne by each girder when a unit live load is applied at different transverse locations across the bridge cross-section. The design traffic load is then positioned transversely to induce the extreme load effects, which yields the LDF for each girder.

The existing methods for calculating the load distribution factor (LDF) for multi-girder bridges can be roughly classified into two categories: numerical models and simplified analytical approaches. Numerical models, such as the grillage model22, finite strip method25, and finite element model20–23, can offer relatively more accurate simulation results of the LDF at the cost of increased model complexity and computational time. Thus, simplified analytical methods are more practical for trial-and-error design and parametric studies of multi-girder bridges. According to a comprehensive review of existing simplified analytical methods16,24, the rigid-jointed girder (RJG) method26 features clear physical significance, fewer parameters, and moderate computational cost. Furthermore, the governing equations of the RJG method can be expressed in dimensionless form to characterize the effects of global and local stiffness of multi-girder bridges on transverse live-load distribution. Therefore, the RJG method is adopted in this study to calculate the load distribution factor (LDF) for staggered widening bridges.

In this study, the load distribution factor (LDF) for bending moment in staggered widening bridges is investigated. Firstly, the boundary conditions of the conventional rigid-jointed girder (RJG) method are modified to consider the mid-span supporting effects provided by piers of the existing bridge. The governing equation of the analytical model is derived based on the updated model configuration. Based on the modified model, the governing equations are derived for calculating the transverse influence lines of the new bridge is staggered widening configurations. The proposed simplified analytical model is then verified through comparisons with finite element analysis (FEA) results. The transverse vehicular load distribution characteristics of staggered widening bridges are also compared with those of conventional widening bridges. Subsequently, a parametric study is carried out using the simplified analytical model to examine the effects of key parameters, including diaphragm rigidity, flange rigidity, girder rotational stiffness, girder width, number of girders, and closure slab width, on the girder LDF. On this basis, a generalized formula is proposed to predict the LDF of staggered widening bridges. The accuracy of the derived formula is further validated against the LDFs obtained from the simplified analytical model.

Model motivation

The load distribution factor (LDF) is used to characterize the proportion of vehicular load borne by each girder33–41. To achieve this, a transverse influence line should be obtained for each girder firstly42,43. Comprehensive reviews of the relevant methods for calculating the transverse influence line can be found in the studies by Kong et al.16 and Chen et al.24. In this study, the rigid-jointed girder (RJG) method16,24,42,44,45 was employed because of its clear physical significance and relatively low computational cost. In the RJG method, the vertical deflections and bending moments of a simply-supported girder are zero at both ends. From a mathematical perspective, the deflection curve of the girder can be approximated using a Fourier sine series. For engineering applications, the half-wave sinusoidal profile has been proven sufficiently accurate (with an error of approximately 1%) in representing the actual load and deflection distributions16. Therefore, as illustrated in Fig. 4(a), the RJG method assumes that vehicular loads (including uniform loads and multiple concentrated forces) can be approximated by a half-wave sinusoidal load to simplify the subsequent derivation. Given the direct correlation between girder deflections and internal forces, which is established through girder stiffness, the transverse distribution of vehicular loads can be determined by the ratios of girder deflections. Meanwhile, since the girders deflect in the same sinusoidal pattern but with varying magnitudes along their longitudinal axis, the LDFs of each cross-section are considered identical. Consequently, the mid-span section is commonly chosen to study the LDFs of the girders16.Fig. 4Model motivation based on the rigid-jointed girder method for staggered widening bridges.

Model motivation based on the rigid-jointed girder method for staggered widening bridges.

Based on the above-mentioned basic assumptions of the RJG method, the mid-span section of the new bridge with a unit width was selected to calculate the vehicular load distribution in staggered widening bridges. Moreover, a fixed-end boundary condition was imposed near the closure slab to account for the supporting effects of the adjacent piers of existing bridges. As a result, a simplified analysis model, as shown in Fig. 4(b), was developed to determine the transverse influence line for staggered widening bridges.

Calculated LDF via the transverse influence line

Based on the transverse influence lines calculated by the simplified analysis model, the LDF was calculated following the proposed method by Kong et al.16. This method consists of rough- and refined-calculations for the LDF. First, the vehicular loads were moved as a whole along the transverse direction of the deck to obtain the maximum load effects for each girder. Then, the lanes and vehicular loads were placed according to the specification requirements to obtain a refined calculation result. The vehicular load utilized in this work is the design truck load suggested by the AASHTO LRFD standard9. The transverse spacing of the wheels is defined as 1829 mm (6 ft.). Conclusion and discussion

Conclusions

This study mainly concerns the transverse vehicular load distribution in staggered widening bridges. First, a simplified analysis model was developed based on the RJG method to calculated the transverse distribution of vehicular load for staggered widening bridges. The accuracy of the simplified analysis model was then verified by comparison with finite element analysis results. Then, the transverse influence lines of the girders in a staggered widening bridge were compared with those in a conventional widening bridge. The results indicated that:staggered widening significantly affects the transverse distribution of vehicular load in the girders, particularly for the interior girder;due to the supporting effects that shown in Fig.1, the vehicular load borne by the interior girders has been significantly reduced, which indicated that the commonly used LDF equations may prove inapplicable to staggered widening bridges.

Based on the simplified analysis model, parametric analysis was conducted. The influence of critical parameters, such as diaphragm rigidity, flange rigidity, girder rotational stiffness, girder width, girder number and closure slab width on the LDF of girders in a staggered widening bridge was investigated.

Based on the parametric analysis results, a general formula of the LDF was proposed for staggered widening bridges. This formula consists of five independent variables, such as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho}_{1}$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rho}_{2}$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$b$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d$$\end{document} and nine empirical parameters. To verify the accuracy of this formula, a series of sampling points of the independent variables were obtained through Latin hypercube sampling (LHS) method. The LDFs calculated using the formula with sampling points were then compared with those calculated by the simplified analysis model. The least square method was used to optimize the empirical parameters based on cases featuring different girder positions and girder numbers. Comparison between the calculated LDFs using the formula and the simplified analysis model confirmed the applicability and accuracy of the proposed formula.

Applicability and scope of the proposed method

The applicability and scope of the proposed method in this study are elaborated as follows:

First, the simplified analytical model shown in Fig. 4 is valid for large-offset staggered widening bridges, where the existing bridge’s pier is located right at or in the vicinity of the new bridge’s mid-span segment. For small-offset staggered widening bridges, this model may not be applicable, and the complex spatial mechanical behavior of the bridges should instead be evaluated using the three-dimensional finite element method.

Second, from the perspective of engineering practice, large-offset staggered widening bridges have attracted more attention than small-offset ones, mainly for two reasons: (1) The differential deck deflection between adjacent bridges increases with pier offset; thus, for large-offset cases, the bearing capacity of the bridge deck should be evaluated, and the existing bridge deck should be strengthened if necessary8; (2) The supporting effects provided by the existing bridge’s piers also increase with pier offset, so the prestress dosage and structural dimensions of the new bridge’s interior girders should be designed considering these effects to improve economic efficiency.

Third, based on the findings of this study, the implementation of staggered widening reduces the vehicular loads borne by the interior girder. Therefore, the bearing capacity of the existing bridge’s girders does not need to be evaluated, as the supporting effects have reduced vehicular loads borne by the existing girders. In contrast, the prestress dosage and structural dimensions of the new bridge’s girders should be designed considering these supporting effects to enhance economic efficiency. For small-offset staggered widening bridges, insignificant supporting effects lead to no notable reduction in prestress dosage or structural dimensions, so the new bridge’s girders can be designed like those of conventional widening bridges to facilitate engineering practice.

In addition, the proposed simplified analytical model is formulated based on the RJG method26, which is a general approach for multi-girder bridge systems and is not restricted to a specific number of girders or girder type. In the validation section, the prototype bridge with three T-girders was adopted as a representative example to verify the accuracy of the model. The governing equation (Eq. 1) can be readily extended to a general form covering a wide range of girder numbers, following the approach proposed by Kong et al.16 and Chen et al.24. Furthermore, the RJG method is also applicable to other bridge types, such as multi-box girder bridges42. Therefore, the validation results are not affected by the number of girders or bridge types.

Future work suggestions

In this study, the calculation results of the simplified analytical model are compared with those of the three-dimensional finite element model, which is commonly adopted method for LDF calculation in previous studies20–23. Thus, the rationality of modifying the configuration of conventional RJG method to account for the supporting effects provided by the piers of existing bridge has been validated at the theoretical level.

However, it should be acknowledged that this validation belongs to model-to-model numerical verification rather than experimental or field-based verification. The proposed formula is calibrated based on the results from the simplified analytical model. Its accuracy has not been validated through laboratory tests24 or independent field test benchmarks14–19. Thus, the applicability of the proposed method is limited to the theoretical and numerical scope within the parameter range considered in this study.

Fortunately, large-offset staggered widening bridges have been applied in the design of the Guangzhou–Shenzhen Highway expansion project. In future work, field tests will be conducted to collect monitoring data related to the mechanical characteristics of staggered widening bridges, which will be used to further validate the effectiveness and accuracy of the proposed model and formula.

An Analysis of Decoding Methods for LLM-based Agents for Faithful Multi-Hop Question Answering
Alexander Murphy, Mohd Sanad Zaki Rizvi, Aden Haussmann, Ping Nie, Guifu Liu, Aryo Pradipta Gema, Pasquale Minervini

Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently produce factually inaccurate outputs — a phenomenon known as hallucination — which limits their accuracy in knowledge-intensive NLP tasks. Retrieval-augmented generation and agentic frameworks such as Reasoning and Acting (ReAct) can address this issue by giving the model access to external knowledge. However, LLMs often fail to remain faithful to retrieved information. Mitigating this is critical, especially if LLMs are required to reason about the retrieved information. Recent research has explored training-free decoding strategies to improve the faithfulness of model generations. We present a systematic analysis of how the combination of the ReAct framework and decoding strategies ( i.e., DeCoRe, DoLa, and CAD) can influence the faithfulness of LLM-generated answers. Our results show that combining an agentic framework for knowledge retrieval with decoding methods that enhance faithfulness can increase accuracy on the downstream Multi-Hop Question Answering tasks. For example, we observe an F1 increase from 19.5 to 32.6 on HotpotQA when using ReAct and DoLa.



1
Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) are exceptional in generating fluent and coherent natural language, making them powerful tools for a wide range of applications
(
Radford et al., 2019
;
Brown et al., 2020
;
Wei et al., 2022
;
Ouyang et al., 2022
)
.
However, an ongoing challenge with LLMs is their tendency to generate factually inaccurate outputs, a phenomenon known as
hallucination

(
Ji et al., 2023
;
Rawte et al., 2023
;
Zhang et al., 2023
;
Li et al., 2024b
;
Hong et al., 2024
)
.
This tendency limits their reliability and accuracy, especially in knowledge-intensive NLP tasks such as Open-Domain Question Answering (ODQA).

Retrieval-Augmented Generation
(
Lewis et al., 2020
, RAG;)
can be used to condition the generation of an LLM on external knowledge and is widely used to improve the factual accuracy of the model in knowledge-intensive NLP tasks
(
Li et al., 2024a
;
Ayala & Bechard, 2024
)
.
In a basic RAG setup for question answering (QA), a retriever system uses the question to retrieve relevant texts from an external knowledge source, and the model generates an answer based on the retrieved information.
However, this “one-step retrieve-and-read” approach is insufficient for more complex QA tasks that require multi-hop reasoning
(
Trivedi et al., 2023
)
.
The Reasoning and Acting
(
Yao et al., 2023
, ReAct;)
framework addresses this by making the setup iterative and
agentic
.
It enables the LLM to generate interpretable reasoning traces and task-specific actions in an interleaved manner.

Frameworks like ReAct can be used to provide the LLM with access to a search engine that can retrieve verifiable and up-to-date external knowledge.
However, even with access to external tools and knowledge, LLMs can still fail to remain faithful to such knowledge.
These events are referred to as
faithfulness hallucinations

(
Huang et al., 2025
)
and may happen when the implicit knowledge in the parameters of the LLM conflicts with the provided context
(
Shi et al., 2024
;
Zhao et al., 2024
)
.
Mitigating this is critical, especially in applications where LLMs are required to reason from the provided context, such as multi-hop QA tasks.

Recent works explored training-free decoding methods that aim to improve the faithfulness of model generations
(
Shi et al., 2024
;
Gema et al., 2024
;
Chuang et al., 2024
;
Chen et al., 2024
)
.
These decoding methods are characterised by how they influence the probability distribution of the next predicted token during the generation process.

We present the first systematic analysis of how the combination of an agentic framework (
i.e.,
ReAct) and different faithful decoding strategies influences the accuracy of LLM-generated answers to open-domain questions requiring multi-hop reasoning (see Figure
1
for an example).
We specifically focus on evaluating ReAct without fine-tuning and selecting decoding methods that do not require training, as training-free approaches lower the barrier to entry for leveraging LLM agents.

Figure 1:
An example showing the difference between ReAct using standard vs. faithful decoding.
The Thoughts, Actions and Observations depend on previous steps — in the standard decoder, a mistake in Thought 2 propagates throughout the reasoning chain, yielding a wrong answer.
The faithful decoder stays faithful to the observation, yielding the correct answer.

Our findings show that the combination of an agentic framework for knowledge retrieval, and decoding methods that enhance contextual faithfulness, increases accuracy in the downstream task of multi-hop ODQA. We observe consistent improvements in answer and retrieval metrics across datasets and models, with Answer F1 improvements of up to 13.1 and Recall improvements of up to 4.5.

2
Methodology

2.1
Frameworks

ReAct
(
Yao et al., 2023
)
is a reasoning framework that consists of 3 steps, where the system retrieves and reasons in an interleaved manner. The agent chooses an
action
to perform, from which it receives an
observation
. Then, the agent must
reason
about the newly collected observation to decide on the next action. It repeats these steps until it decides that it has found an answer. Similarly to
Yao et al. (2023)
, we give the ReAct agent tools to
Search
for a paragraph and to
Lookup
the first sentence in an article containing a specific keyword. The article is always the one that the latest retrieved paragraph belongs to. The search tool returns the most relevant paragraph to the query specified by the agent.

As comparisons we use OneR, or
One-step Retriever
and Direct prompting. OneR retrieves one paragraph of context, using the same retriever as ReAct, but with the question as the query. We limit it to one paragraph only, so as to simulate the same tool access as ReAct. Direct prompting doesn’t get any external context at all and has to answer based just on the question.

2.2
Hallucinations Reduction Decoding Strategies

Context-Aware Decoding

(
Shi et al., 2024
, CAD;)
is a decoding method that computes token probabilities with and without a given context
c
.
When predicting the next token, CAD then increases the likelihood of the tokens selected by the model with
c
while decreasing the likelihood of the tokens selected by the model without
c
, under the assumption that the former will tend to be more faithful to the context
c
than the latter.
Results in
Shi et al. (2024)
show that generations produced with CAD tend to be more faithful to the context, which motivates us to integrate it within the ReAct framework.

Decoding by Contrasting Layers

(
Chuang et al., 2024
, DoLa;)
uses the insight that factual knowledge in LLMs is often localised to specific layers of the model.
It derives its next-token distributions by contrasting the logits obtained from early and later exiting layers, assuming that later layers will tend to generate more factually accurate predictions.
We test its compatibility with ReAct since it has been shown to consistently improve Chain-of-Thought reasoning and instruction-following ability of LLMs
(
Chuang et al., 2024
)
.

Decoding by Contrasting Retrieval Heads

(
Gema et al., 2024
, DeCoRe;)
is a decoding method that dynamically contrasts outputs from a base LLM and an LLM with masked
retrieval heads
— attention heads that are responsible for extracting contextual information
(
Wu et al., 2025
)
.
It uses conditional entropy of the model’s next-token distribution to guide the contrastive decoding mechanism. DeCoRe has been shown to improve open-book question-answering performance in a classic RAG setup.

Note that while CAD requires the context to be explicitly provided in the input, DoLa and DeCoRe leverage internal components of the transformer architecture (
i.e.,
layers and attention heads, respectively) in their decoding strategies that are not dependent on separately provided context.

2.3
Metrics

Answer F1
is a weighted average of precision and recall of the predicted answer compared to the gold answer at the token level, representing how accurate the final answer the agent generates is.
In our results in
Appendix B
, we also include
Answer EM
, which is only one if the gold answer matches the predicted answer exactly and zero otherwise.

Answer Support Recall
is the fraction of questions for which the correct answer appears in one of the documents retrieved by ReAct.
We use this metric to evaluate how good the agent is at retrieving correct documents.
Note that in multi-hop QA, supporting documents do not necessarily contain the answer, as multi-hop QA may require multiple retrieval steps to find the answer.
For this reason, we include Paragraph F1 in
Appendix B
, which compares all supporting paragraphs with the retrieved ones.

For both Answer F1 and Answer Support Recall, we use common normalization techniques, such as applying lowercasing and removing common articles and punctuation, before calculating the metric.

3
Experimental Setup

We now describe the experiment setup, including datasets and detailed descriptions of the ReAct components used.
All of the experiments are run three times on the same test sets, with seeds and technical details listed in
Appendix A
.

3.1
Datasets

In our experiments we use the following three datasets. For each dataset we create a test set of 500 questions and a knowledge base to use for retrieval.

HotpotQA

(
Yang et al., 2018
)
contains 113,000 Wikipedia-based, multi-hop question-answer pairs. This means that a question requires multiple documents to answer. For each question it contains the supporting paragraphs from each document needed to answer the question, which we use to evaluate retrieval metrics. As our knowledge base we use the provided 2017 Wikipedia corpus.

2WikiMultihopQA

(
Ho et al., 2020
)
is a multi-hop question answering dataset, with questions, relevant context and answers collected from Wikipedia. It was specifically designed to avoid questions that can bypass multi-hop reasoning and always require at least one hop to be solved. We combine all supporting and non-supporting paragraphs from all questions, into one knowledge base for retrieval.

MuSiQue

(
Trivedi et al., 2022
)
consists of questions, relevant context and answers collected from Wikipedia.
Similarly to 2WikiMultihopQA, it provides multi-hop question-answer pairs that cannot be ”cheated” with shortcuts and require actual multi-step reasoning. It also contains questions that are up to 4-hop and that are supposed to be unanswerable.
As with 2WikiMultihopQA, we combine all supporting and non-supporting paragraphs from all questions into one knowledge base.

3.2
Details of ReAct components

We run experiments with Qwen2-7b-Instruct
(
Yang et al., 2024
)
and Llama3-8b-Instruct
(
Grattafiori et al., 2024
)
as the backbone LLM. We use BM25
(
Robertson & Zaragoza, 2009
)
implemented in Elasticsearch 7.10.2 as our retriever. We use few-shot prompts (seven examples) for ReAct, OneR and Direct to help outputs adhere to the correct format, similar to the setup
Yao et al. (2023)
use in their question answering experiments (exact prompts in
Appendix C
). We use default hyperparameters for the decoders.

The ReAct agent has two available actions for interacting with the knowledge base for HotpotQA:
Search
uses a given query to search the knowledge base, and
Lookup
looks for the next paragraph containing a keyword in the current article.
2WikiMultihopQA and MuSiQue do not have the
Lookup
action since the knowledge bases for these datasets do not consist of coherent articles. The prompt instructs the LLM on which tools are available and how to call them. We use chat templates to structure the reasoning and actions of the LLM and the observations it gets, noting that this improves performance considerably compared to no chat templates. If the LLM does not adhere to the
Thought-Action-Observation
format specified in the prompt, the generation for the question is stopped, but we still use the previously generated output to evaluate retrieval metrics.

4.1
Faithful Decoding Improves Answering, Retrieval & Format Adherence

Answer F1 and Answer Support Recall Improvements

In 6 out of 6 cases, ReAct combined with a faithful decoder increases Answer F1, showing that improvements hold across datasets and models. The most substantial increase is 13.1, from 19.5 to 32.6, for Qwen with DoLa on HotpotQA (a relative increase of over 50%). For Llama we see smaller increases of up to 1.3. However, on Llama especially, we also see some decoders that reduce Answer F1, sometimes significantly. For Qwen on the other hand there only is one case of a faithful decoder reducing performance, by just under 1 percentage point.

Answer Support Recall improves in 5 out of 6 cases, with a tie for Llama on HotpotQA. The greatest improvement is for Qwen with DeCoRe on 2WikiMultihopQA, increasing by 4.5. For Llama there is a smaller improvement, of up to 1.3. Similarly to Answer F1, there also are decoders that reduce Recall, especially for Llama, though there always is at least one decoder that causes an improvement or tie.

Prompt Format Adherence Improvements

To measure the impact faithful decoding has on ReAct following the
Thought-Action-Observation
format specified in the prompt (see
Appendix C
for exact prompts), we measure the proportion of reasoning traces which contain the
Finish[…]
keyword in the final output.

Figure 2:
The proportion of questions for which a complete reasoning trace was created with ReAct (measured by how many have the
Finish[…]
keyword in their final output).

Figure
2
shows that in most cases, format adherence is either improved or matched when using faithful decoding. For Qwen, across all datasets, CAD increases the number of questions adhering to the format the most. The greatest increase is seen in HotpotQA, raising the proportion of complete reasoning traces from 47.9% to 74.5%. This correlates with the significant increase in Answer F1 seen on HotpotQA with all faithful decoders applied to Qwen, where Answer F1 improved from 19.5 with Standard decoding, to 28.5 with CAD and 32.6 with DoLa.

Adherence improves on Llama as well, but to a lesser extent and less consistently across decoders. Although there is no large difference between the standard decoder and the best faithful decoders on HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihopQA (with some decoders even decreasing adherence), there is a significant increase in adherence on MuSiQue, with CAD raising the proportion of complete reasoning traces from 36.4% to 44.4%.

Interestingly, even though CAD improves format adherence the most for Qwen, this does not directly correlate to the highest Answer F1. This suggests that not all decoders improve performance in the same way, and some are better at improving format adherence such as CAD, while others improve inherent factuality (such as DoLa focusing on factual transformer layers).

These improvements in adherence could reduce the need for fine-tuning ReAct, which
Yao et al. (2023)
use to ensure format adherence. We show the raw values for adherence in
Appendix B
.

Qualitative Analysis

Our qualitative analysis of output reasoning and action traces supports the quantitative results. Firstly, we notice a reduced amount of factual mistakes. Secondly, faithful decoders help generate outputs in the correct ReAct format more consistently.

Figure 3:
An example from 2WikiMultihopQA where faithful decoding improves upon standard decoding with Qwen2-7b-Instruct. Standard decoding is not faithful to the retrieved context and generates an incorrect thought, derailing the reasoning chain. CAD helps the agent stay faithful to context and leads it to the correct solution.

In Figure
3
, we compare the output trace of the Standard decoder with that of CAD. We observe that the reasoning chain of CAD values the context more highly, correctly identifying the passage
”Ansegisel’s father would be Pepin’s paternal grandfather”
in its second Thought, compared to the Standard decoder which thinks that
”Pepin of Herstal’s paternal grandfather was Ansegisel”
, a classic faithfulness hallucination. This small distinction leads the agent with CAD to take another action and
Search
for the father of
”Ansegisel”
, while the Standard agent does not, as it thinks it has found the correct answer immediately at the first step.

In addition to being more faithful to the context, the CAD agent also formats the actions correctly, while the Standard agent leaves its answer in the Thought, neglecting the final
Finish
action.

4.2
Influence of LLMs’ Prior Knowledge

We observe that ReAct, using Llama, does not surpass OneR’s Answer F1 on HotpotQA or MuSiQue, despite a significantly higher Answer Support Recall than OneR.
Llama also receives a high Answer F1 with Direct prompting, using no context at all.
These seemingly contradictory results point to the possibility that Llama is leveraging pre-existing knowledge stored implicitly in its parameters.

Interestingly, Qwen does not do as well without context, receiving low Answer F1 scores with OneR and Direct prompting, compared to Llama. Yet this disparity is less pronounced when using ReAct. This suggests that the agentic framework may level the playing field between different LLMs to some extent. Especially with faithful decoding methods, an LLM’s dependence on having seen the answer in it’s training data is reduced, as more weight is put on the provided context. Using faithful decoding on a finetuned model which conforms to the ReAct format more consistently and uses context better, is potentially a training-free way of increasing performance even more.

4.3
Limitations of Faithful Decoding in the Absence of Relevant Context

We also notice that for OneR, across both models, the faithful decoders do not yield substantial or consistent improvements in Answer F1 over Standard decoding.
In fact, for Qwen, we observe performance degradation across all datasets.
This observation is attributed to OneR’s limitations in retrieving the paragraph containing the correct answer, illustrated by OneR’s low Answer Support Recall in
Table 2
. The inadequacy of single-step retrieve-and-read systems for complex, multi-hop ODQA benchmarks has been documented in previous research
(
Trivedi et al., 2023
)
. In comaprison to OneR, ReAct has a significantly higher Answer Support Recall in all experiments, and thus, Answer F1 is also increased.

These findings underscore a crucial principle: if the LLM is not provided with, or is unable to retrieve, a context that contains the correct answer, then even perfect adherence to the retrieved context will not lead to improved answer accuracy and may even degrade performance.
The benefit of faithful decoding in this setup is, therefore, contingent on the availability of relevant information.

4.4
No Decisive Superiority Among Faithful Decoders

In 3 out of 6 experiments, DoLa achieves the largest improvement for ReAct, when looking at Answer F1. For format adherence, CAD improves the most by a significant amount in 3 out of 6 cases. Nevertheless, our analysis does not reveal a single faithful decoder that consistently outperforms the others or any definitive patterns as to when one decoder performs better than another. While at least one faithful decoder demonstrated improvements over, or parity with, Standard decoding across all datasets with ReAct, there was no discernable pattern of any decoder performing particularly well or poorly on a certain dataset.

5
Related works

Other retrieval & reasoning frameworks

Interleaving Retrieval with Chain-of-Thought Reasoning
(
Trivedi et al., 2023
, IRCoT;)
enhances the conventional RAG process by interleaving the retrieval of information with the generation of reasoning steps (Chain-of-Thoughts, or CoT). At each step of the CoT, retrieval is guided by reasoning, and the retrieved context is integrated to improve reasoning in turn. This interleaved approach is similar to ReAct, however IRCoT is only used for multi-hop QA, whereas ReAct can be used for other knowledge-intensive reasoning tasks, such as fact verification
(
Yao et al., 2023
)
. Additionally, IRCoT can only retrieve and read at each step, but ReAct chooses task-specific actions to take. IRCoT significantly outperformed one-step retrieve-and-read on ODQA benchmarks
(
Trivedi et al., 2023
)
.

Other decoding strategies

Autocontrastive Decoding
(
Gera et al., 2023
, ACD;)
, contrasts higher and lower layers of the model to improve the quality of generations, similarly to DoLa. However,
Gera et al. (2023)
noted that ACD seemed to increase the tendency of LLMs to hallucinate. This makes it unsuitable for our study, and is therefore excluded. Activation Decoding
(
Chen et al., 2024
)
uses entropy to quantify the ’sharpness’ of in-context hidden states, and was shown to mitigate hallucinations.

Detecting & mitigating hallucination in RAG systems

Regressing Decoupled External context score and Parametric knowledge score
(
Sun et al., 2025
, ReDeEP;)
, detects hallucination in RAG systems by ’disentangling’ how models use their own parametric knowledge from how they use external sources. ReDeEP increases the accuracy of RAG hallucination detection.
Sun et al. (2025)
also introduce Add Attention Reduce FFN (AARF). AARF improves truthfulness by modulating the contributions of
Knowledge FFNs (Feed Forward Networks)
and
Copying Heads
in the LLM, stopping the model from relying too heavily on the former and underutilizing the latter, which they claim is the cause of hallucinations. Layer-wise Relevance Propagation for RAG
(
Hu et al., 2024
, LRP4RAG;)
uses the Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) algorithm to detect hallucinations. Both ReDeEP and LRP4RAG outperformed previous baselines at hallucination detection on the RAGTruth corpus
(
Niu et al., 2024
)
. Our study distinguishes itself by being the first systematic analysis of several decoding methods for improving faithfulness and their interplay with RAG and agentic knowledge-intensive reasoning systems.

6
Conclusion

This study addresses the challenge of mitigating hallucination in LLMs when answering open-domain questions requiring multi-hop reasoning with an agentic RAG setup. We investigate the impact of various decoding strategies on the faithfulness of LLM-generated answers within the ReAct agentic framework and compare this to one-step retrieval and direct prompting baselines. Our analysis reveals that faithful decoding methods, such as DeCoRe, DoLa and CAD, enhance question answering performance when integrated with ReAct. This is at least partly due to the LLM being more faithful to retrieved context and adhering more closely to the prompt format required by the agent. This improvement underscores the importance of contextual faithfulness in achieving accurate and reliable LLM outputs, as the models are better able to utilise retrieved paragraphs when generating answers.

7
Ethics Statement

The use of the various decoding strategies presented in combination with ReAct, aims to reduce hallucinations and improve the faithfulness and correctness of agents. This has the potential to significantly increase agents’ trustworthiness.

However, harmful contexts could become more pronounced in the agent output, and malicious actors could use this fact to manipulate agents.
Risks should be managed accordingly, and precautions should be taken on a case-by-case basis.

Our code is publicly available in order to allow users to gain insight in the use of decoding strategies applied to agents and help them make the safest and best decision for their use-case.

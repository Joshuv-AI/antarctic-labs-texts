On representations of algebras with radical square zero
Yuriy A. Drozd

Abstract
. We consider a new correspondence between representations of algebras with radical square zero and representations of species. We show that the stable category of representations of such algebra embeds into the representation category of the corresponding species and show how one reconstruct the Auslander-Reiten quiver of the algebra from the Auslander-Reiten quiver of the species.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
16G70,16G10,16G20,16D90

Algebras with radical square zero were studied by a lot of investigators, as they are a convenient testing area for new
methods and approaches of the representation theory. The first results about their representations were obtained by
Yoshii
[
15
,
14
]
who found conditions for such an algebra to be representation finite. Kruglyak
[
11
]
related
representations of such algebra to representations of posets (actually, to representations of quivers, but not using this
term) and rereceived in this way the results of Yoshii with a small refinement. This approach was further used in a series
of papers
[
5
,
6
,
13
]
to study derived categories of such algebras. Auslander and Reiten
[
1
]
established
a stable equivalence of an algebra with radical square zero with a certain species (or realization of a valued graph in
the sense of
[
8
]
). In this paper we consider another variant of correspondence between algebras with radical
square zero and species. We prove that the stable category of such algebra embeds into the category of representations
of the corresponding species and show how the Auslander-Reiten quiver of the algebra can be reconstructed from the
Auslander-Reiten quiver of the corresponding species.

(TD)
.

Let

(1)

The
\mathscr{M}
-modules
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Rad}\nolimits_{A}(N,-)

A morphism

-module
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Rad}\nolimits_{A}(N,-)
(respectively,

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Rad}\nolimits_{A}(-,M)
) and

has no non-trivial decomposition

such that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{im}\nolimits\alpha\subseteq X^{\prime}

(respectively,
\beta(X^{\prime\prime})=0
).

(2)

A left (right) almost split morphism with prescribed source (target) is unique up to an isomorphism.

(3)

For a morphism
\varphi:N\to M
the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\varphi
is irreducible.

(b)

There is a left almost split morphism
\alpha:N\to M\oplus M^{\prime}
such that
\varphi=\pi\alpha
, where
\pi
is the projection onto
M
.

(c)

There is a right almost split morphism
\beta:N\oplus N^{\prime}\to M
such that
\varphi=\beta\iota
, where
\iota
is the embedding of
N
.

(4)

If
M
is projective, the embedding
\mathfrak{r}M\to M
is right almost split.

(5)

If
N
is injective, the surjection
N\to N/\mathop{\mathrm{}}{soc}\nolimits N
is left almost split.

(6)

For an exact sequence
0\to N\xrightarrow{\alpha}X\xrightarrow{\beta}M\to 0
the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\alpha
is left almost split.

(b)

\beta
is right almost split.

If these conditions hold, this sequence is called an
almost split sequence with the source
N
and the target
M
.

(7)

An almost split sequence with a fixed source or a fixed target is unique up to isomorphism.

(8)

If
M
is not projective, there is an almost split sequence with the target
M
and the source
\tau M=\mathrm{D}\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Tr}\nolimits M
.

\tau M
is called the
Auslander-Reiten transform
of
M
.

(9)

If
N
is not injective, there is an almost split sequence with the source
N
and the target
\tau^{-1}N=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Tr}\nolimits\mathrm{D}N

\tau^{-1}N

of

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
U
be a simple non-injective
A
-module. The irreducible morphisms with the source
U
and indecomposable
targets are just all possible embeddings
U\to P_{i}
, where
P_{i}
is not simple.

Proof.

All of them are irreducible by TD(
4
) and TD(
3
b). Let
U\xrightarrow{\alpha}M
be irreducible and
M
be indecomposable.
We can suppose that
\alpha
is an embedding of a submodule
U\subset M
. Then
U\subseteq\mathfrak{r}M
, otherwise
U
is
a direct summand of
M
. There is an epimorphism
\pi:P\to M
which induces an epimorphism
\mathfrak{r}P\to\mathfrak{r}M
.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{r}P
contains a submodule isomorphic to
U
and
\alpha
factors through
\pi
. As
\alpha
is irreducible,

\pi
must split, that is
M
is projective.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

If
P_{i}
is not simple,
M
is indecomposable and
\alpha:P_{i}\to M
is irreducible, then
M
is not projective.

Proof.

Suppose
M
is projective. Then
\alpha
is not an epimorphism. As
\mathfrak{r}M
is semisimple and is a unique maximal
submodule of
M
,
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{im}\nolimits\alpha\simeq U_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{r}M
. Thus
\alpha
factors as
P_{i}\to U_{i}\to M
, so is not irreducible.
∎

For every pair of indices
i,j
choose a submodule of
\mathfrak{r}P_{i}
which is a direct sum
\bigoplus_{k=1}^{h_{ij}}T_{ijk}
,
where
T_{ijk}\simeq U_{j}
for all
k
. Define the morphisms
\theta_{ijk}:U_{j}\to P_{i}
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{im}\nolimits\theta_{ijk}=T_{ijk}
.
Note that
h_{ij}=0
if
U_{j}
is injective.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
U_{j}
be non-injective,
\theta_{j}:U_{j}\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}^{h_{ij}}
has the components
\theta_{ijk}
and

\tilde{U}_{j}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{coker}\nolimits\theta_{j}
. Then

(3.1)

0\to U_{j}\xrightarrow{\theta_{j}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}^{h_{ij}}\xrightarrow{\eta_{j}}\tilde{U}_{j}\to 0

is an almost split sequence. In particular,
\tilde{U}_{j}=\tau^{-1}U_{j}
.

We denote by
\eta_{ijk}:P_{i}\to\tilde{U}_{j}\ (1\leq k\leq h_{ij})
the components of
\eta_{j}
.

Proof.

All components
\theta_{ijk}:U_{j}\to P_{i}
of
\theta_{j}
are irreducible and every irreducible morphism
U_{j}\to P_{i}
is a linear
combination of components of
\theta_{ijk}
, hence factors through
\theta_{j}
. By Lemma
3.1
, all irreducible morphisms
with the source
U_{j}
are components of
\theta_{j}
. Therefore, the claim follows from TD(
3
) and TD(
6
).
∎

Note that
\theta_{ijk}
can be considered as a map
P_{j}/\mathfrak{r}P_{j}\to P_{i}
and, if we identify them, they form a basis of

H_{ij}
. We denote by
\tilde{H}_{ji}
the space with the basis
\{\eta_{ijk}\}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Every irreducible morphism with the source (with the target)
P_{i}
is a right (respectively, left) linear combination of

\eta_{ijk}
(respectively, of
\theta_{ijk}
) over
F_{i}
for some fixed
j
.

Let

Proposition 3.2a
.

If
E_{i}
is not simple,
N
is indecomposable and
\alpha:N\to E_{i}
is irreducible, then

is not injective.

For every pair of indices
i,j
choose a submodule of
E_{i}/U_{i}
which is a direct sum
\bigoplus_{k=1}^{h^{\prime}_{ji}}S_{jik}
,
where
S_{jik}\simeq U_{j}
for all

\tau_{jik}:E_{i}\to U_{j}
which are compositions

Lemma 3.3a
.

Let
U_{j}
be non-projective,
\tau_{j}:\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}^{h^{\prime}_{ji}}\to U_{j}
has the components
\tau_{jik}
and

Every irreducible morphism with the source (with the target)

As for irreducible morphisms whose sources and targets are not projective, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
M,N
be inecomposable non-projective modules. A morphism
\varphi:N\to M
is irreduciblle in
A\mbox{-}\mathrm{mod}
if and only if
\Phi(\varphi)
is irreducible in
\Gamma\mbox{-}\mathrm{mod}
. Therefore,
\Phi
induces an isomorphism
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Irr}\nolimits(M,N)\simeq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Irr}\nolimits(\Phi(M),\Phi(N))
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi(\varphi)
be non-irreducible that is
\Phi(\varphi)=\sum_{k}u_{k}v_{k}
for some non-invertible morphisms
u_{k},v_{k}
.
By Theorem
2.1
we can suppose that
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
are from
\Gamma\mbox{-}\mathrm{mod}^{\flat}
, hence

u_{k}=\Phi(u^{\prime}_{k}),\,v_{k}=\Phi(v^{\prime}_{k})
and
\varphi=u^{\prime}_{k}v^{\prime}_{k}
is also non-irreducible. On the other hand, let
\varphi
be non-irreducible,

\varphi=\sum_{k}u_{k}v_{k}
for some non-invertible
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
. If all
u_{k}v_{k}
factor through projective modules,

\Phi(\varphi)=0
, so is not irreducible. Otherwise it is the sum of such
\Phi(u_{k})\Phi(v_{k})
that
u_{k}v_{k}
do not factor through
projective modules, so is non-irreducible.
∎

For the species
\Gamma^{*}
we have an analogous result with analogous proof.

Lemma 3.5a
.

Let
M,N
be inecomposable non-injective modules. A morphism
\varphi:N\to M
is irreduciblle in
A\mbox{-}\mathrm{mod}
if and only if
\Phi_{*}(\varphi)
is irreducible in
\Gamma_{*}\mbox{-}\mathrm{mod}
. Therefore,
\Phi
induces an isomorphism

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Irr}\nolimits(M,N)\simeq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Irr}\nolimits(\Phi_{*}(M),\Phi_{*}(N))
.

4.
Auslander-Reiten species

Now we are able to restore the Auslander-Reiten species
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{AR}\nolimits(A)
starting from
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{AR}\nolimits(\Gamma)
. Note that the latter has been described
in
[
7
]
.

First of all we consider projective and injective modules over the species
\Gamma
or, the same, over the algebra

A[\Sigma]
from (
1.1
). For each bimodule
H_{ij}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{A}(P_{j},\mathfrak{r}P_{i})
we consider the dual bimodule

H^{\prime}_{ji}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathbbm{k}}(H_{ij},\mathbbm{k})\simeq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{F_{i}}(H_{ij},F_{i})\simeq\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Hom}\nolimits_{F_{j}}(H_{ij},F_{j})

(recall that our algebras are finite dimensional over
\mathbbm{k}
).
We define
\Gamma
-modules
P^{-}_{j}
and
E^{+}_{j}
as follows:

\displaystyle P^{-}_{j}(i^{-})=\begin{cases}F_{j}&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle P^{-}_{j}(i^{+})=H_{ij}\ \text{if}\ j\neq i.

\displaystyle E^{+}_{j}(i^{+})=\begin{cases}F_{j}&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle E^{+}_{j}(i^{-})=H^{\prime}_{ij}\ \text{if}\ j\neq i.

One easily sees that
E^{+}_{j}\simeq\Phi(U_{j})
and
P^{-}_{i}\simeq\Phi(\tilde{U}_{i})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

P^{-}_{j}
is an indecomposable projective
\Gamma
-module and every indecomposable projective module is
isomorphic either to
S^{+}_{j}
or to
P^{-}_{j}
for some
j
.

Proposition 4.1a
.

E^{+}_{j}
is an indecomposable injective
\Gamma
-module and every indecomposable injective module is
isomorphic either to
S^{-}_{j}
or to
E^{+}_{j}
for some
j
.

The proofs of Propositions
4.1
and
4.1a
follow immediately from the definitions and the description
(
1.1
) of the algebra
\mathbbm{k}[\Gamma]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

The Auslander-Reiten species
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{AR}\nolimits(A)
is obtained from the Auslander-Reiten species
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{AR}\nolimits(\Gamma)
by the following procedure:

(1)

Delete simple objects
\mathbf{S}^{+}_{i}
and
\mathbf{S}^{-}_{i}
and replace every non-simple object
\mathbf{V}
by
\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{V})
.

(2)

Add objects
P_{i}
with the corresponding skewfields
F_{P_{i}}=F_{i}
.

(3)

Define bimodules

\displaystyle B(M,P_{i})=\begin{cases}H_{ij}&\text{if }M=U_{j},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle B(P_{i},M)=\begin{cases}\tilde{H}_{ji}&\text{if }M=\tilde{U}_{j},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.`\end{cases}

Proof.

It follows immediately from Lemmata
3.3
and
3.5
.
∎

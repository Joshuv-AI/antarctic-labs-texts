Sleep and mental illness: an umbrella review protocol
Authors: Michael John Norton, Killian Walsh, George Bridges, Mary Ryan, Anna Carthy, Julie Daly, Harry Kearns
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSleep is an important natural process carried out by the human body. It allows the body to rest and the brain to process information attained over the course of the day. Sleep is intrinsically linked to mental health, with those with mental illness more likely to require support in attaining adequate sleep. Recently, the College of Psychiatrists in Ireland’s REFOCUS committee has sought to co-design an information leaflet for those with mental illness detailing the latest recommendations from the literature regarding sleep for this population, regardless of whether or not they take psychotropic medications. Given the rapidly expanding research focus on this topic, there are many published systematic reviews on the subject. As such, this proposed umbrella review seeks to collate the existing qualitative-based systematic reviews into sleep and mental illness and synthesise them in order to draw out the key recommendations that can be used by the committee to co-design an information leaflet to be used in practice to support service users with sleep disturbance.Method and analysisThis proposed review aims to explore how service users of mental health services experience sleep with and without the medication used to treat mental illness. An umbrella review that is compliant with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) is proposed. Search terms are stated, and a variety of databases(Medline, Embase, CINAHL, Web of Science, Scopus) will be searched. The search range is from 1 January 2016 to present (2026).Ethics and disseminationGiven that this paper presents a protocol for an umbrella review into sleep and mental illness, no ethical approval was required. The proposed umbrella review has been registered with the repository: PROSPERO on 18 March 2026 where it is freely available. The results of the umbrella review will be disseminated through both a peer reviewed publication and through an information leaflet co-designed by the College of Psychiatrists in Ireland’s REFOCUS committee. This protocol and subsequent umbrella review is and will be co-designed by psychiatrists, service users and family members of those with mental illness. Such individuals are named authors on this protocol and will be named authors on the proposed umbrella review as well.PROSPERO registrationCRD420261343674.

Introduction

Sleep is a complex neurobehavioural state that forms an important aspect of overall health and well-being as it has been shown to improve brain health and mood as well as maintain cardiovascular, cerebrovascular and metabolic health.1–4 According to Watson and colleagues,5 it is recommended that a minimum of 7 hours sleep per night is required to maintain and promote optimal health. Failure to achieve this minimum requirement on a regular basis can have a detrimental impact on a person’s physical and mental health and wider public safety.3 In terms of physical health complications arising from sleep deprivation, lack of sleep is associated with an increased likelihood of experiencing a stroke, hypertension and obesity as well as a plethora of other cardiovascular, endocrine and metabolic complications.4 6 7 Additionally, sleep disturbance can also lead to sedentary behaviour, which can further exacerbate physical health complications arising from lack of sleep.8 When sleep is combined with a sedentary lifestyle, they can cause a significant risk of mental health challenges such as depression to develop.9

In regard to sleep across the lifespan, research indicated that adolescence is a significantly vulnerable period towards poor mental health as a result of sleep disturbance caused by a number of developmental milestones including the onset of puberty and natural changes in circadian rhythm that occur during this period.10 However, sleep disturbances can occur at any point during the lifespan and can impact cognitive functioning leading to mental health conditions like stress, depression and anxiety.11 12 However, Freeman and colleagues13 note that if sleep deprivation is caught at an early stage, it could become a preventative strategy towards the onset of such mental illnesses. Given the significant impact a lack of sleep causes on one’s physical and mental health, noted above, insufficient sleep is an unrecognised and under-reported public health epidemic.14 Given that sleep disturbances occur right across the lifespan,14 it is important for mental health service users to have access to the best and most up-to-date information on sleep, particularly when taking psychotropic medications for their mental illness, given such medications impact on sleep homeostasis.15

Rationale for proposed umbrella review

This proposed umbrella review was conceptualised during a meeting of the College of Psychiatrists in Ireland REFOCUS committee in Autumn 2025. REFOCUS (Recovery Experience Forum of Carers and Users of Services) is a committee within the College of Psychiatrists in Ireland that works based on two pillars: (a) contributing and enhancing the training of psychiatrists while (b) identifying mechanisms to improve Irish mental health service delivery.16 During this meeting, it was identified that more up-to-date information is required on the topic of sleep for those with mental illness and as such an idea was put forward to co-design a patient information leaflet on the topic. To support the co-design of this information leaflet, an original search of the literature occurred which identified a multitude of both primary and secondary research on this topic. As such, in order to synthesise the most up-to-date evidence and create recommendations that can then be applied to such a leaflet, an umbrella review methodological approach was chosen. Umbrella reviews are a novel methodological approach that use a systematic review methodology and reporting approach to assess and synthesise the results of previous systematic reviews published into a given topic.17 They are useful in distilling high level, pertinent data from a range of systematic approaches in order to attain new insights that can enhance future research and practice.18

Methods and analysis

This proposed umbrella review will use the updated Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) standardised reporting guidelines.19 This reporting guidance is being used as there are currently no specific guidelines for reporting umbrella reviews.20 In addition, umbrella reviews are essentially a systematic review of systematic reviews, and as such, this reporting guideline is deemed most appropriate.21 Finally, this reporting guideline has been used in other published umbrella reviews within the mental health space which further strengthens our rationale for the use of this reporting guideline.22 23 In addition, coinciding with what is now best practice, a protocol should be created prior to the commencement of the umbrella review so that the methods as it pertains to such a review can be made robustly clear.24 In essence, this allows for two important factors of any systematic review to occur: transparency and reproducibility.25 As a result, the present protocol has been created for this purpose. Additionally, to conform with best practice regarding PRISMA, the current protocol was constructed to comply with the PRISMA-P reporting guidelines.26 This proposed umbrella review was registered with PROSPERO (CRD420261343674] on the 18 March 2026 and is freely available there (dataset).27 Any amendments to this umbrella review will be included in the actual umbrella review itself, which will be freely available from the publisher’s website on publication.

Eligibility criteria

The first part of any review is to create a research/review question.28 There are many mechanisms available in the literature to support reviewers and research authors in constructing a research question.29 30 Two such approaches are PICO (Population, Intervention, Comparison and Outcome)30 and SPIDER (Sample, Phenomenon of Interest, Design, Evaluation and Research type).31 However, given the input of the intervention mechanism within our aim and sleep interventions in our objectives, PICO was deemed most appropriate by the review team. As a result, the question constructed for investigation in this proposed umbrella review is: How do service users of mental health services experience sleep with and without medication used to treat their mental illness? As such, the primary phenomenon of interest in this proposed umbrella review is the experience of sleep for those with mental illness, with the secondary phenomenon of interest being the impact of medication or no medication on this experience of sleep for this cohort. Once the review question was formed, the review team met to construct the inclusion/exclusion criteria for this proposed umbrella review (see table 1).

Inclusion/exclusion criteria

This review will include qualitative systematic reviews and meta-syntheses as the research question, aim and associated objectives all seek to understand the experiences of service users who have sleep issues and are also suffering from mental illness. Only the qualitative components of mixed-method systematic reviews will be included in this review. Again, due to the aforementioned rationale. Quantitative systematic reviews inclusive of meta-analyses are excluded as this review wants to understand the experiences of service users regarding sleep and as such is not looking to examine inferences or relationships between factors associated with sleep. Primary studies – either quantitative, qualitative or mixed method, along with editorials, opinion pieces, discussion papers, perspective papers and case studies will all be excluded as the proposed review is an umbrella review—in other words a systematic review of systematic reviews—and as such are ineligible for inclusion here. Due to the above rationale, as noted above only systematic reviews were included—inclusive of meta-synthesis. Meta-analyses will be excluded as they are performed as part of a systematic review of quantitative studies—which again is incongruent with what the research question is trying to ascertain. Other review types like scoping reviews and realist reviews will also be excluded for a number of reasons. First, although the review team note that such reviews could have employed a systematic process through following methodological guidance as posed by Arksey and O’Malley32 or Pawson and colleagues,33 such review types—particularly scoping reviews would fail to carry out a quality assessment—which is a central feature of a systematic review. In terms of realist reviews, these will be excluded as they go beyond the summarisation of current evidence to providing interpretations and critique of the evidence which goes beyond the intended purpose of this review.34 In the case of scoping reviews, they are also not included in this umbrella review as their purpose is to determine the range, nature, coverage and scope of a body of evidence which again falls outside the remit of the purpose of this review.35 36 As such, systematic reviews are included as they focus on the depth of knowledge as it relates to the research question posed without further interpreting or critiquing it beyond that of a quality assessment. Dissertations will be excluded because they are a) a source of grey literature and b) timely to review given the life cycle of the proposed review. Reviews from the past 10 years are included. Any reviews prior to 2016 are excluded as this review is only concerned with ascertaining the current, best available evidence into the experience of sleep and the factors that support/inhibit sleep for those with mental health challenges. Papers prior to 2016 may also include techniques or medications that may no longer be suitable to improve sleep in this population. Given that this review is primarily being undertaken to support REFOCUS in co-designing an information leaflet on sleep, only the most up-to-date evidence from the past ten years is acceptable for this proposed umbrella review. Papers not written in the English language are excluded due to the inability of the review team to translate such papers into the English language. This does introduce the potential for language bias to be a consideration for this review as there is the potential that reviews with pertinent information may be excluded based on the language they have been written in. This is a limitation that is acknowledged here and will also be acknowledged in the final umbrella review itself. Service users of a mental health service are included. Those associated with another field of health will be excluded as the review question only concerns mental health service users. Service users with a dual diagnosis – defined here as having a combined mental health and addiction disorders – will also be excluded because of the presence of an addiction challenge in this population of service users which complicates the clinical picture and the possible impact on the use of sleep medications for this populous. Given the universal need for sleep associated with our species, all age groups and all genders—whether biological or preferred—will be considered included in this review. Finally, papers that discuss pharmacology are included if they focused on either psychotropic medications or if they focused on other medications but for mental health service users and are written within a mental health context.

Search strategy for Scopus

Study records

After round one of exhaustive searching has occurred, a number of citations will have been found and imported into the citation management software: Covidence so that the review team can review and narrow down further. Round two screening will commence with the removal of duplicates that may have been gathered as part of round one screening. Once this occurs, the remaining citations will have their abstracts assessed through the application of the predetermined inclusion/exclusion criteria (table 1). Round two screening will be undertaken by MJN and HK, with HK reviewing 100% of papers at this phase along with MJN to ensure the rigorous and transparent application of the predetermined inclusion/exclusion criteria to the abstracts of papers. Round three screening will involve the application of the predetermined inclusion/exclusion criteria (table 1) to the full text of citations that have made it to this round of screening. Again, this round will be conducted by MJN, with HK. With HK and MJN reviewing 100% of papers at this stage of the searching also. If disagreements regarding the inclusion or exclusion of a paper occur, then a third reviewer KW will review the citation and make the final decision regarding their inclusion or exclusion. Any citations that remain after this round of screening will be included in this umbrella review. To ensure transparency and rigour, at the end of round three screening, all included papers' reference lists will be reviewed for potential citations. Any citations identified at this point will undergo rounds two and three screening to judge their suitability in the review. Once this is completed, the final number of included papers in this review will be known. In addition to this, considering this is an umbrella review and the papers included are systematic reviews, MJN and HK will manually check each of the included systematic reviews' own included studies to determine if overlap of primary studies occur. If overlap occurs, our method of data synthesis will treat recurring qualitative findings as adding rich confirmatory data to the narrative present and as such will not be treated as a limitation as it would be if we had included quantitative reviews in the umbrella review.37

Assessing the quality of evidence

It is still important in umbrella reviews to evaluate methodological quality in order to ensure credibility and reliability of the included synthesised evidence.38 For this umbrella review, initially, the AMSTAR 2 was proposed for use in this proposed review. However, given that the questions within this tool are more suited to randomised and non-randomised controlled trials,22 the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme (CASP) quality appraisal checklist for systematic reviews will be used instead.39 The CASP systematic review quality checklist consists of ten questions each requiring the reviewer to answer 'yes', 'no' or 'can't tell'. After each answer, there is a box to allow the reviewer to make comment as it relates to that question. To assess the confidence in cumulative evidence within this review, the GRADE-CERQual will be used.40

Data synthesis

For this umbrella review, the results will be presented narratively using a narrative synthesis method.41 A narrative synthesis is a descriptive approach whereby findings from multiple studies, or in this case reviews, are primarily made through the use of words to explain and summarise the findings.42 A meta-analysis will not be performed as this review aims to only collect qualitative data in order to answer the overall review question. To support the narrative synthesis approach that will be employed here, the findings will also be presented in a tabular format which will summarise the key characteristics of included reviews.

Ethics and dissemination

As this paper presents a protocol for an umbrella review of the literature into the topic of sleep for those with mental illness, ethical approval was not required. Information relating to this review will be stored initially on PROSPERO as part of the registration requirements and will be freely available.27 Any additional information that is created after the registration date with PROSPERO will form part of the material published with the paper itself on publication and will be freely available from the journal’s website.

Patient and public involvement

This protocol along with the subsequent umbrella review itself will use public and patient involvement at all stages – from design to write up of the final manuscript. For their involvement in this protocol and the review itself, they will be named as co-authors on these publications. This paper has been completely written by those with lived experience [MJN, GB, MR, JD and AC] with support from staff from both the Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland (KW) and the College of Psychiatrists in Ireland (HK). Lived experience contributor – MJN will be involved in the screening and appraisal process given their experiences in carrying out similar type reviews before. All lived experience contributors will be involved in reading the included reviews and supporting the interpretation of findings via REFOCUS meetings where their interpretations of the findings will be noted and combined with that of MJN and HK to create the results. The results of this umbrella review will be disseminated through both an information leaflet for service users co-produced by the lived experience contributors as well as the remaining members of the REFOCUS committee. In addition, the findings of this review will be disseminated through a peer-reviewed publication.

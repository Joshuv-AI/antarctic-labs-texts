Cohomology and deformations of restricted Lie algebras and their morphisms in positive characteristic
Quentin Ehret, Abdenacer Makhlouf

Abstract
The main purpose of this paper is to study cohomology and develop a deformation theory of restricted Lie algebras in positive characteristic p > 0 p>0 . In the case p ≥ 3 p\geq 3 , it is shown that the deformations of restricted Lie algebras are controlled by the restricted cohomology introduced by Evans and Fuchs. Moreover, we introduce a new cohomology that controls the deformations of restricted morphisms of restricted Lie algebras. In the case p = 2 p=2 , we provide a full restricted cohomology complex with values in a restricted module and investigate its connections with formal deformations. Furthermore, we introduce a full deformation cohomology that controls deformations of restricted morphisms of restricted Lie algebras in characteristic 2 2 . As example, we discuss restricted cohomology with adjoint coefficients of restricted Heisenberg Lie algebras in characteristic p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 .



Keywords:
Modular Lie algebra, restricted cohomology, deformation.

Introduction

This paper includes the restricted Lie algebras results part of the Preprint
[
EM23
]
, which is dedicated to Lie-Rinehart algebras in positive characteristic. We consider here the study of cohomology and deformations of restricted Lie algebras in positive characteristic. Moreover, we consider deformations of restricted morphisms of restricted Lie algebras.

Restricted Lie algebras.
Lie algebras were historically introduced over the field of complex numbers, then over arbitrary fields of characteristic zero. The origins of the study of Lie algebras in positive characteristic
p>0
go back to the late 1930s, with the discovery by Witt in 1937 of a new simple Lie algebra, named after him and generalized by Zassenhaus in 1939 (
[
Z39
]
). Many results and tools that are valid in characteristic zero are no longer valid in positive characteristic, such as the Killing form, Lie Theorem and Weyl Theorem. This makes the classification problem in characteristic
p
a difficult one. The classification of simple Lie algebras in characteristic
p
has been investigated, for example by Strade in a series of six articles published between 1989 and 1998, see
[
SH98
]
and references therein. More recently, Bouarroudj and his collaborators considered superalgebras cases, see (
[
BGL09
,
BKLLS18
,
BLLS23
]
).
In characteristic
p>0
, an additional structure appears naturally on certain Lie algebras, inspired by the following fact.
If
A
is an associative algebra over a field
\F
of positive characteristic
p
, we can consider its Lie algebra of derivations
\Der(A)
. Then the Frobenius morphism
(\cdot)^{p}:\Der(A)\rightarrow\text{End}(A),\penalty\ D\mapsto D^{p}
is actually an endomorphism of
\Der(A)
, which is in general not true in characteristic zero. This observation, together with the study of the properties arising from the interactions between this Frobenius morphism and the structuring applications of
\Der(A)
, led to the definition of
restricted Lie algebra
(Jacobson,
[
Ja37
,
Ja41
]
, see Definition
2.1
), which is a Lie algebra
L
equipped with a so-called
p
-map
(\cdot)^{[p]}:L\rightarrow L
satisfying some compatibility conditions with the Lie bracket and the additive law of
L
. For example, Lie algebras associated with algebraic groups over fields of positive characteristic are also restricted. Restricted Lie algebras are interesting to study in characteristic
p
because they allow us to develop new techniques that partially overcome the problems mentioned above.

Restricted cohomology.
The cohomology associated with restricted Lie algebras is much more complicated than the ordinary Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology in characteristic
0
.
In
[
Ho54
,
Ho55a
,
Ho55b
]
, Hochschild defines a restricted cohomology of a restricted Lie algebra
L
with values in a module
M
by

H^{k}_{res}(L,M):=\Ext^{k}_{U_{p}(L)}(F,M),\penalty\ k\in\N,

where
U_{p}(L)
denotes the
restricted
enveloping algebra of
L
. Although correct, this expression only allows explicit calculations for
k\in\{0,1\}
, in the context of certain extensions (see
[
Ho54
]
). For
p\geq 3
, Evans and Fuchs then proposed an explicit construction of a cochain complex in
[
Ev00
,
EF08
]
, which allows restricted cohomology groups to be computed up to order
p
if the Lie algebra is abelian and up to order
2
in the general case. The complete cohomology is still a challenging problem, although work of Evans and Fuchs has provided good cohomological interpretations of certain algebraic phenomena (see
[
BE24
,
E23
,
EF08
,
EFP16
,
EF19
,
EF23
,
EFY24
]
).

Formal deformations.
Formal deformations were introduced by Gerstenhaber in
[
Ge64
]
for associative algebras, then generalized for various algebraic structures, most notably for Lie algebras by Nijenhuis and Richardson (
[
NR66
,
NR67a
]
). The main tool is to consider formal power series. Roughly speaking, a deformation of an algebraic structure
(A,\mu)
consists in building a multiplicative operation
\mu_{t}
on the formal space
A[[t]]
of the form
\mu_{t}=\mu+\sum_{i\geq 1}t^{i}\mu_{i},
where the maps
\mu_{i}:A\times A\rightarrow A
are bilinear and must satisfy a system of conditions called
deformation equation
. Formal deformations of
A
are controlled by the second cohomology space with coefficients in
A
. Formal deformations of morphisms were considered by Gerstenhaber and Schack for associative algebras in
[
GS83
,
GS85
]
, by Nijenhuis and Richardson for Lie algebras in
[
NR67a
,
NR67b
]
and by Mandal for Leibniz algebras in
[
Ma07
]
. Most of the studies dealt with characteristic
0
. In
[
EF08
]
, Evans and Fuchs sketched a deformation theory for restricted Lie algebras in characteristic
p>0
as an application of their cohomology formulas. In this paper, we aim to develop a deformations theory of restricted Lie algebras and their morphisms. A connection with the restricted cohomology is also explored.

Characteristic 2.
In the specific case where the characteristic of the ground field is equal to
2
, many results fall short and new techniques are required. For example, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\F)
is the standard example of a simple Lie algebra in characteristic
p\neq 2
. But this is no longer the case in characteristic
2
: this algebra admits a non-zero center and is nilpotent. Bouarroudj and his collaborators have made major contributions to the study of the special case
p=2
, about double extensions in
[
BB18
]
, deformations in
[
BLLS15
]
and classification of simple Lie superalgebras in
[
BGL09
,
BLLS23
]
. Recently, Bouarroudj and Makhlouf have studied (Hom-)Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
(
[
BM23
]
), where a new cohomology is introduced. It appears that in the case
p=2
, there are similarities between the notions of restricted Lie algebra and Lie superalgebra. This observation motivated the construction of a new cochain complex for restricted Lie algebras, which has no analogue for
p\neq 2
(see Section
4.4
). This complex is complete in the sense that it allows the computation of restricted cohomology groups of any order. However, this method remains specific to the case
p=2
and cannot be generalized to
p>2
.

Deformation theory of restricted Lie algebras,
p\geq 3

Let
\F
be a field of characteristic
p\geq 3
. In
[
EF08
]
, Evans and Fuchs have sketched a deformation theory of restricted Lie algebras. In this Section, we investigate formal restricted deformations of restricted Lie algebras as well as equivalence of such deformations and restricted obstructions. We also introduce the notion of deformation of restricted morphisms and an adapted (partial) deformation cohomology.

3.1
Restricted formal deformations

Let
\bigl(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\bigl)
be a restricted Lie algebra. We aim to study deformations of both the Lie bracket and the
p
-map.

Definition 3.1
.

A
formal deformation
of
\bigl(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\bigl)
is given by two maps

\begin{array}[]{lllll}m_{t}:&L\times L\longrightarrow L[[t]]&\text{and }&\w_{t}:&L\longrightarrow L[[t]]\\[5.69054pt]
&(x,y)\longmapsto\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}m_{i}(x,y)&&&x\longmapsto\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{j}\omega_{j}(x),\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

where
m_{0}=[\cdot,\cdot]
,
\w_{0}=(\cdot)^{[p]}
, and
(m_{i},\w_{i})\in C^{2}_{*}(L,L)
.
Moreover, the two following conditions must be satisfied, for all
x,y,z\in L
:

m_{t}(x,m_{t}(y,z))_{t}+m_{t}(y,m_{t}(z,x))+m_{t}(z,m_{t}(x,y))=0;

(6)

m_{t}\left(x,\w_{t}(y)\right)_{t}=m_{t}(m_{t}(\cdots m_{t}(x,\overset{p\text{ terms}}{\overbrace{y),y),\cdots,y}}).

(7)

Remarks.

1.

The map
m_{t}
extends to
L[[t]]
by
\F[[t]]
-linearity.

2.

The map
\w_{t}
extends to
L[[t]]
by
p
-homogeneity and by using the formula

\w_{t}(x+ty)=\w_{t}(x)+t^{p}\w_{t}(y)+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\tilde{s}(x,ty),

with
k\tilde{s}(x,ty)
being the coefficient of
Z^{p-1}
in the formal expression
m_{t}(xZ+ty,x)
.

Notation.
In the sequel, if
f
is a map with two variables and
n\geq 2
, we will use the notation

f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}):=f(f(\cdots f(x_{1},x_{2}),x_{3}),\cdots,x_{n}).

In particular, we have

[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]:=[[\cdots[x_{1},x_{2}],x_{3}],\cdots,x_{n}].

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\left(m_{t},\w_{t}\right)
be a restricted deformation of
\bigl(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\bigl)
. Then
\w_{1}
has the
(*)
-property with respect to
m_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\F,\penalty\ x\in L
.

\bullet

\w_{t}(\lambda x)=\lambda^{p}\w_{t}(x)\Leftrightarrow(\lambda x)^{[p]}+t\w_{1}(\lambda x)+\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}\w_{i}(\lambda x)=\lambda^{p}\Bigl(x^{[p]}+t\w_{1}(x)+\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}\w_{i}(x)\Bigl).
By collecting the coefficients of
t
in the above equation, we obtain
\w_{1}(\lambda x)=\lambda^{p}\w_{1}(x)
.

\bullet

The following computations are made modulo
t^{2}
. We have

m_{t}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p})=[x_{1},\cdots,x_{p}]+t\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}\left[m_{1}([x_{1},\cdots,x_{p-k-1}],x_{p-k}),x_{p-k+1},\cdots,x_{p}\right].

(8)

We denote by
(\maltese)
the three conditions
\bigl(x_{i}\in\{x,y\},\penalty\ x_{1}=x,\penalty\ x_{2}=y\bigl)
. Using the definition of
\w_{t}
and Eq.(
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\w_{t}(x+y)

\displaystyle=\w_{t}(x)+\w_{t}(y)+\sum_{(\maltese)}\frac{1}{\sharp\{x\}}m_{t}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{p})

\displaystyle=x^{[p]}+y^{[p]}+\sum_{(\maltese)}\frac{1}{\sharp\{x\}}[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{p}]

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+t\sum_{(\maltese)}\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}\left[m_{1}([x_{1},\cdots,x_{p-k-1}],x_{p-k}),x_{p-k+1},\cdots,x_{p}\right]\mod(t^{2}).

We also have
\w_{t}(x+y)=(x+y)^{[p]}+t\w_{1}(x+y)\mod(t^{2}).
If we compare the coefficients in the above expressions, we obtain

\displaystyle(x+y)^{[p]}

\displaystyle=x^{[p]}+y^{[p]}+\sum_{(\maltese)}\frac{1}{\sharp\{x\}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{p}];

(9)

\displaystyle\w_{1}(x+y)

\displaystyle=\w_{1}(x)+\w_{1}(y)+\sum_{(\maltese)}\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}\left[m_{1}([x_{1},\cdots,x_{p-k-1}],x_{p-k}),x_{p-k+1},\cdots,x_{p}\right]

\displaystyle=\w_{1}(x)+\w_{1}(y)+\sum_{(\maltese)}\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}(-1)^{k}\left(x_{p}x_{p-1}\cdots x_{p-k-1}m_{1}([x_{1},\cdots,x_{p-k-1}],x_{p-k})\right).

(10)

We conclude that
\w_{1}
has the
(*)
-property with respect to
m_{1}
.

∎

Example: The case
p=3
.
Let
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
be a restricted Lie algebra. Consider an infinitesimal deformation
(m_{t},\w_{t})
of
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
given by
m_{t}=[\cdot,\cdot]+tm_{1},\penalty\ \w_{t}=(\cdot)^{[p]}+t\w_{1}
, and let
x,y\in L
.

\displaystyle\w_{t}(x+y)-\w_{t}(x)-\w_{t}(y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\underset{x_{1}=x,\penalty\ x_{2}=y}{x_{k}\in\{x,y\}}}\frac{1}{\sharp\{x\}}m_{t}(m_{t}(x_{1},x_{2}),x_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\ 2m_{t}(m_{t}(x,y),x)+m_{t}(m_{t}(x,y),y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\penalty\ 2\bigl([[x,y],x]+tm_{1}([x,y],x)+t[m_{1}(x,y),x]\bigl)

\displaystyle\penalty\ +[[x,y],y]+tm_{1}([x,y],y)+t[m_{1}(x,y),y].

Collecting the coefficients of
t
on both sides, we obtain

\w_{1}(x+y)-\w_{1}(x)-\w_{1}(y)=2m_{1}([x,y],x)+2[m_{1}(x,y),x]+m_{1}([x,y],y)+[m_{1}(x,y),y],

which exactly means that
\w_{1}
satisfies the
(*)
-property with respect to
m_{1}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\left(m_{t},\w_{t}\right)
be a restricted deformation of
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
. Then
(m_{1},\w_{1})
is a 2-cocyle of the restricted cohomology.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
,
(m_{1},\w_{1})\in C^{2}_{*}(L,L)
.
By the ordinary deformation theory, we already have
m_{1}\in Z^{2}_{\text{CE}}(L,L)
. It remains to show that
\ind^{2}(m_{1},\w_{1})
vanishes. We expand the equation

m_{t}\left(x,\w_{t}(y)\right)=m_{t}(m_{t}(\cdots m_{t}(x,\overset{p\text{ terms}}{\overbrace{y),y),\cdots,y}}).

(11)

On one hand,

\displaystyle m_{t}\left(x,\w_{t}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=[x,\w_{t}(y)]+tm_{1}(x,\w_{t}(y))+\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}m_{i}(x,\w_{t}(y))

\displaystyle=[x,y^{[p]}]+tm(x,\w_{1}(y))+tm_{1}(x,y^{[p]})\mod(t^{2}).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle m_{t}(m_{t}(\cdots m_{t}(x,\overset{p\text{ terms}}{\overbrace{y),y),\cdots,y}})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{i_{p}}\cdots\sum_{i_{1}}t^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{p}}m_{i_{p}}\left(m_{i(p-1)}(\cdots(m_{i_{1}}(x,y),y),\cdots,y),y\right)

\displaystyle=[x,y,\cdots,y]+t\sum_{\underset{\sharp\left\{k,\penalty\ i_{k}=1\right\}=1}{i_{k}=0\text{ or }1}}m_{i_{p}}\left(\cdots(m_{i_{1}}(x,y),y),\cdots,y),y\right)\mod(t^{2})

\displaystyle=[x,y,\cdots,y]+t\sum_{i+j=p-1}\Bigl[m_{1}([x,\overset{j}{\overbrace{y,\cdots,y}}],y),\overset{i}{\overbrace{y,\cdots,y}}\Bigl]\mod(t^{2})

\displaystyle=[x,y,\cdots,y]+t\sum_{i+j=p-1}(-1)^{i}y^{i}m_{1}([x,\overset{j}{\overbrace{y,\cdots,y}}],y)\mod(t^{2}).

We finally obtain the equation

[x,y^{[p]}]+t\bigl([x,\w_{1}(y)]+m_{1}(x,y^{[p]})\bigl)=[x,y,\cdots,y]+t\sum_{i+j=p-1}(-1)^{i}y^{i}m_{1}([x,\overset{j}{\overbrace{y,\cdots,y}}],y).

(12)

By collecting the coefficients of
t^{0}
and
t
, we recover the usual identity
[x,y^{[p]}]=[x,y,\cdots,y]
and obtain a new identity

[x,\w_{1}(y)]+m_{1}(x,y^{[p]})=\sum_{i+j=p-1}(-1)^{i}y^{i}m_{1}([x,\overset{j}{\overbrace{y,\cdots,y}}],y).

(13)

Therefore, we have
\ind^{2}(m_{1},\w_{1})=0
.
∎

3.2
Equivalence of restricted formal deformations

Let
\phi_{t}:L[[t]]\longrightarrow L[[t]]
be a formal automorphism defined on
L
by

\phi_{t}(x)=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\phi_{i}(x),\penalty\ \phi_{i}:L\longrightarrow L\penalty\ \F\text{-linear },\penalty\ \phi_{0}=\text{id},

and then extended by
\F[[t]]
-linearity.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\left(m_{t},\w_{t}\right)
and
\left(m^{\prime}_{t},\w^{\prime}_{t}\right)
be two formal deformations of
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
. They are called
equivalent
if there is a formal automorphism
\phi_{t}
such that

m_{t}^{\prime}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))=\phi_{t}\left(m_{t}(x,y)\right)

(14)

and

\w_{t}^{\prime}\left(\phi_{t}(x)\right)=\phi_{t}\left(\w_{t}(x)\right).

(15)

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\left(m_{t},\w_{t}\right)
and
\left(m_{t}^{\prime},\w^{\prime}_{t}\right)
be two equivalent formal deformations of
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
. Then there exists
\psi:L\rightarrow L
such that, for all
x,y\in L
,

m_{1}^{\prime}(x,y)-m_{1}(x,y)=\psi\left([x,y]\right)-[\left(x,\psi(y)\right)]-[\left(\psi(x),y\right)]

(16)

and

\w_{1}^{\prime}(x)-\w_{1}(x)=\psi(x^{[p]})-[\psi(x),\overset{p-1}{\overbrace{x,\cdots,x}}].

(17)

If the equivalence is given by
\phi_{t}=\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\phi_{i}
, then
\psi=\phi_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in L
. Since

m_{t}^{\prime}\bigl(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y)\bigl)=\phi_{t}\left(m_{t}(x,y)\right),

we have

\sum_{k\geq 0}t^{k}m_{t}^{\prime}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))=\sum_{k\geq 0}t^{k}\phi_{t}\left(m_{t}(x,y)\right).

We deduce that

[\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y)]+tm_{1}^{\prime}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y)=\phi_{t}([x,y])+t\phi_{t}(m_{1}(x,y))\mod(t^{2}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Bigl[\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\phi_{i}(y),\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{j}\phi_{j}(y)\Bigl]

\displaystyle+tm_{1}^{\prime}\Bigl(\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\phi_{i}(y),\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{j}\phi_{j}(y)\Bigl)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\phi_{i}([x,y])+t\sum_{j\geq 0}t^{j}\phi_{j}(m_{1}(x,y))\mod(t^{2}).

Hence,

[x,y]+t\Bigl([x,\phi_{1}(y)]+[\phi_{1}(x),y]+m_{1}^{\prime}(x,y)\Bigl)=[x,y]+t\bigl(\phi_{1}([x,y])+m_{1}(x,y)\bigl)\mod(t^{2}).

By collecting the coefficients of
t
, we obtain the first identity. Then, expanding the relation
\phi_{t}\left(\w_{t}(x)\right)=\w^{\prime}_{t}\left(\phi_{t}(x)\right)
yields

\phi_{t}\Bigl(\sum_{i>0}t^{i}\w_{i}(x)\Bigl)=\w_{t}^{\prime}\bigl(x+t\phi_{1}(x)\bigl)\mod(t^{2})

and it follows that

x^{[p]}+t\bigl(\w_{1}(x)+\phi_{1}(\w(x))\bigl)=\w_{t}^{\prime}\left(x+t\phi_{1}(x)\right)\mod(t^{2}).

(18)

We compute the right hand side of Eq.(
18
). We denote once again by
(\maltese)
the conditions
(x_{i}\in\{x,y\},\penalty\ x_{1}=x,\penalty\ x_{2}=y)
, where we temporarily denote
y:=t\phi_{1}(x)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\w_{t}^{\prime}\left(x+t\phi_{1}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\w_{t}^{\prime}(x)+\w_{t}^{\prime}(y)+\sum_{(\maltese)}\frac{1}{\sharp\{x\}}m_{t}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p})

\displaystyle=\w_{t}^{\prime}(x)+\w_{t}^{\prime}(y)+\sum_{(\maltese)}\frac{1}{\sharp\{x\}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{p}]\mod(t^{2})

\displaystyle=\w_{t}^{\prime}(x)+\w_{t}^{\prime}(t\phi_{1}(x))+\frac{1}{p-1}[x,t\phi_{1}(x),x,x,\cdots,x]\mod(t^{2})

\displaystyle=\w_{t}^{\prime}(x)-t[x,\phi_{1}(x),x,x,\cdots,x]\mod(t^{2})

\displaystyle=x^{[p]}+t\bigl(\w_{1}^{\prime}(x)-[x,\phi_{1}(x),x,x,\cdots,x]\bigl)\mod(t^{2}).

Therefore, Eq.(
18
) gives

x^{[p]}+t\bigl(\w_{1}(x)+\phi_{1}(x^{[p]})\bigl)=x^{[p]}+t\bigl(\w_{1}^{\prime}(x)-[x,\phi_{1}(x),x,x,\cdots,x]\bigl)\mod(t^{2}).

Collecting the coefficients of
t
in the previous equation, we obtain

\w_{1}(x)-\w_{1}^{\prime}(x)=[\phi_{1}(x),\overset{p-1}{\overbrace{x,\cdots,x}}]-\phi_{1}(x^{[p]}),

which is the desired identity.
∎

Remark
.

Lemma
3.5
allows to recover the definitions given in
[
EF08
]
and
[
Ev00
]
in the case of infinitesimal deformations of restricted Lie algebras.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\left(m_{t},\w_{t}\right)
and
\left(m_{t}^{\prime},\w^{\prime}_{t}\right)
be two equivalent formal deformations of
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
. Then, their infinitesimal elements are in the same cohomology class.

Proof.

Let
\left(m_{t},\w_{t}\right)
and
\left(m_{t}^{\prime},\w^{\prime}_{t}\right)
be two equivalent formal deformations via
\phi=\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\phi_{i}
. Let
x,y\in L
. By Lemma
3.5
, we have

m_{1}^{\prime}(x,y)-m_{1}(x,y)=\phi_{1}\left([x,y]\right)-[\left(x,\phi_{1}(y)\right)]-[\left(\phi_{1}(x),y\right)]\text{ and }

(19)

\w_{1}(x)-\w_{1}^{\prime}(x)=[\phi_{1}(x),\overset{p-1}{\overbrace{x,\cdots,x}}]-\psi(x^{[p]}).

(20)

The map
\phi_{1}
belongs to
C^{1}_{\text{CE}}(L,L)
. We have

\displaystyle d^{1}_{\text{CE}}(\phi_{1})(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\left(\phi_{1}([x,y])-[x,\phi_{1}(y)]-[\phi_{1}(x),y]\right).

Using Equation (
19
), we deduce that
m_{1}^{\prime}(x,y)-m_{1}(x,y)=-d^{1}\phi_{1}(x,y)
, therefore
\left(m_{1}^{\prime}-m_{1}\right)\in B^{2}_{\text{CE}}(L,L)
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\ind^{1}(\phi_{1})

\displaystyle=-\phi_{1}(x^{[p]})+[\phi_{1}(x),x,x,\cdots,x]

\displaystyle=\w_{1}(x)-\w_{1}^{\prime}(x).

Finally,
\left(m_{1}^{\prime}-m_{1},\penalty\ \w_{1}^{\prime}-\w_{1}\right)=-d_{*}^{1}\phi_{1}\in B^{2}_{*}(L,L).

We conclude that
(m_{1},\w_{1})
and
(m_{1}^{\prime},\w_{1}^{\prime})
are equal up to a coboundary.

∎

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(m_{t},\w_{t})
be a restricted deformation of
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
.
The deformation is called
trivial
if there is a formal automorphism
\phi_{t}
such that

\phi_{t}\left([x,y]\right)=m_{t}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))

(21)

and

\phi_{t}\left(\w_{t}(x)\right)=\phi_{t}(x)^{[p]}.

(22)

Expanding Eq.(
21
) mod
(t^{2})
yields
m_{1}=-d_{\text{CE}}^{1}(\phi_{1})
. Now, we focus on the right-hand side of Eq.(
22
). The following computations are made mod
t^{2}
, for all
x\in L
:

\displaystyle\phi(x)^{[p]}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{i}t^{i}\phi_{i}(x)\Bigl)^{[p]}

\displaystyle=x^{[p]}+\left(t\phi_{1}(x)\right)^{[p]}+\displaystyle\sum_{\underset{x_{p-1}=t\phi_{1}(x),\penalty\ x_{p}=x}{x_{i}\in\{x,\penalty\ t\phi_{1}(x)\}}}\frac{1}{\sharp\{x\}}[x_{1},[\cdots,[x_{p-1},x_{p}]\cdots].

\displaystyle=x^{[p]}+\left(t\phi_{1}(x)\right)^{[p]}+\frac{1}{p-1}[x,[x,\cdots[t\phi_{1}(x),x]\cdots]

\displaystyle=x^{[p]}+\left(t\phi_{1}(x)\right)^{[p]}+t\ad_{x}^{p-1}\circ\phi_{1}(x).

For the left-hand side, we have (mod
t^{2}
):

\displaystyle\phi\left(\w_{t}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}t^{i}\phi_{i}\left(\w_{t}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}t^{i+j}\phi_{i}\left(\omega_{j}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=x^{[p]}+t\bigl(\omega_{1}(x)+\phi_{1}(x^{[p]})\bigl).

We deduce that

\omega_{1}(x)+\phi_{1}(x^{[p]})=\ad_{x}^{p-1}\circ\phi_{1}(x),

(23)

which can be rewritten

\omega_{1}(x)=-\phi_{1}(x^{[p]})+\ad_{x}^{p-1}\circ\phi_{1}(x)=-\ind^{1}(\phi_{1})(x).

(24)

Hence, we proved the following result.

Proposition 3.8
.

The deformation
\left([\cdot,\cdot]+tm_{1},(\cdot)^{[p]}+t\w_{1}\right)
is trivial if and only if
(m_{1},\w_{1})
is a restricted coboundary.

3.3
Obstructions

Let
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
be a restricted Lie algebra and
n\geq 1
. A deformation of
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
is said of order
n
if it is of the form

m^{n}_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}t^{i}m_{i};\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \w_{t}^{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}t^{i}\w_{i}.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\left(m_{t}^{n},\w_{t}^{n}\right)
be a
n
-order deformation of
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
. We define for all
x,y,z\in L,
the maps

\displaystyle\obs^{(1)}_{n+1}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(m_{i}(x,m_{n+1-i}(y,z))+m_{i}(y,m_{n+1-i}(z,x))+m_{i}(z,m_{n+1-i}(x,y))\right);

\displaystyle\obs^{(2)}_{n+1}(x,y)\penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}(x,\w_{n+1-i}(y))-\sum_{\underset{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{p}=n+1}{0\leq i_{k}\leq n}}m_{i_{p}}\left(m_{i_{p-1}}(\cdots(m_{i_{1}}(x,y),y),\cdots,y),y\right).

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\left(m_{t}^{n},\w_{t}^{n}\right)
be a
n
-order deformation of
\bigl(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\bigl)
. Let
\left(m_{n+1},\penalty\ \w_{n+1}\right)\in C^{2}_{*}(L,L)
. Suppose that
\left(m^{n}_{t}+t^{n+1}m_{n+1},\penalty\ \w^{n}_{t}+t^{n+1}\w_{n+1}\right)
is a
(n+1)
-order deformation of
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
. Then

\left(\obs^{(1)}_{n+1},\obs^{(2)}_{n+1}\right)=d_{*}^{2}\left(m_{n+1},\penalty\ \w_{n+1}\right).

Proof.

Let
\left(m^{n}_{t}+t^{n+1}m_{n+1},\penalty\ \w^{n}_{t}+t^{n+1}\w_{n+1}\right)
be a
(n+1)
-order deformation of
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot],(\cdot)^{[p]}\right)
. The deformed operation
m_{t}^{n}+t^{n+1}m_{n+1}
should satisfy the Jacobi identity, that is,

\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}t^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(m_{i}(x,m_{k-i}(y,z))+m_{i}(y,m_{k-i}(z,x))+m_{i}(z,m_{k-i}(x,y))\right)=0\penalty\ (\text{mod }t^{n+2}),\penalty\ \forall x,y,z\in L.

By collecting the coefficients of
t^{n+1}
, we obtain

\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\left(m_{i}(x,m_{n+1-i}(y,z))+m_{i}(y,m_{n+1-i}(z,x))+m_{i}(z,m_{n+1-i}(x,y))\right)=0,

which can be written

\displaystyle[x,m_{n+1}(y,z)]+[y,m_{n+1}(z,x)]+[z,m_{n+1}(x,y)]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle m_{n+1}(x,[y,z])+m_{n+1}(y,[z,x])+m_{n+1}(z,[x,y])

(25)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(m_{i}(x,m_{n+1-i}(y,z))+m_{i}(y,m_{n+1-i}(z,x))+m_{i}(z,m_{n+1-i}(x,y))\right)=0.

(26)

Hence

-d^{2}_{\text{CE}}m_{n+1}(x,y,z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(m_{i}(x,m_{n+1-i}(y,z))+m_{i}(y,m_{n+1-i}(z,x))+m_{i}(z,m_{n+1-i}(x,y))\right).

Therefore, we have
d^{2}_{\text{CE}}m_{n+1}(x,y,z)=\obs^{(1)}_{n+1}(x,y,z)
.

Denote by
\w_{t}^{n}+t^{n+1}\w_{n+1}:=\w_{t}^{n+1}
. Suppose that
\w_{t}^{n+1}
is a deformation of order
(n+1)
, then we have

m_{t}^{n+1}\left(x,\w_{t}^{n+1}(y)\right)=m_{t}^{n+1}(m_{t}^{n+1}(\cdots m_{t}^{n+1}(x,\overset{p\text{ terms}}{\overbrace{y),y),\cdots,y}}),\penalty\ \forall x,y\in L.

(27)

By expanding and collecting the coefficients of
t^{n+1}
, we obtain

\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}m_{i}(x,\w_{n+1-i}(y))=\sum_{\underset{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{p}=n+1}{0\leq i_{k}\leq n+1}}m_{i_{p}}\left(m_{i(p-1)}(\cdots(m_{i_{1}}(x,y),y),\cdots,y),y\right),

which can be rewritten

\displaystyle[x,\w_{n+1}(y)]+m_{n+1}(x,\w(y))-\sum_{i+j=p-1}\bigl[m_{n+1}([x,\overset{j}{\overbrace{y,\cdots,y}}],y),\overset{i}{\overbrace{y,\cdots,y}}\bigl]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\underset{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{p}=n+1}{0\leq i_{k}\leq n}}m_{i_{p}}\left(m_{i(p-1)}(\cdots(m_{i_{1}}(x,y),y),\cdots,y),y\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}(x,\w_{n+1-i}(y)).

Using the definition of

, we finally obtain

∎

In the usual algebraic deformation theory, the obstructions are 3-cocycles. Since we do not yet have a definition for

, we cannot assert that

is a
3
-cocycle of the deformation cohomology.

Limit of a class of quasilinear equations and related extremal problems
Yuanlong Ruan

Abstract
. We perform a complete analysis of the limiting behaviour of a class of quasilinear problems with Dirichlet boundary data g g , − div 𝒜 n ( x , ∇ u ( x ) ) = 0 , x ∈ Ω . -\operatorname{div}\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(x,\nabla u\left(x\right)\right)=0,\text{ }x\in\Omega. We show that the Lipschitz constant of g g plays a role in controlling the Γ \Gamma -convergence of the natural energies. However the solutions converge uniformly to solution of a limiting equation whenever g ∈ L ​ i ​ p ​ ( ∂ Ω ) g\in Lip(\partial\Omega) , irrelevant to the Lipschitz constant of g g . The limiting equation has no coercivity in u u . We prove that the limiting equation admits a weak comparison principle and has a unique viscosity solution. We also obtain a Poincare’s inequality in the Sobolev-Orlicz space for discontinuous 𝒜 n \mathcal{A}_{n} , which paves the way for our study of an extremal problem where its operator 𝒜 ∞ \mathcal{A}_{\infty} becomes unbounded in a subdomain Ω ∞ ⊂ Ω \Omega_{\infty}\subset\Omega . Upon giving proper meaning to its solution, we show that the extremal problem has a unique solution. It turns out the solution belongs to C ⁡ ( Ω ¯ ) ∩ W 1 , ∞ ​ ( Ω ) C\left(\bar{\Omega}\right)\cap W^{1,\infty}\left(\Omega\right) , although 𝒜 ∞ \mathcal{A}_{\infty} is discontinuous. In the appendix we provide some technical inequalities which play crucial roles in the proof of uniqueness and we believe will be of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

We study the limiting behaviour as
n\rightarrow\infty
of a class of
quasilinear problems in the nonlinear potential theory

(1.1)

-\operatorname{div}\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(x,\nabla u\left(x\right)\right)=0,\text{ }x\in\Omega,

and a related extremal problem

(1.2)

-\operatorname{div}\mathcal{A}_{\infty}\left(x,\nabla u\left(x\right)\right)=0,\text{ }x\in\Omega,

where
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
is unbounded in a subdomain of
\Omega
. These
quasilinear problems have been the prototype of many researches. The most
studied one being the
p
-Laplacian for large
p>1
,

-\Delta_{p}u=\operatorname{div}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=0,\text{ }x\in\Omega.

Early studies in this respect dates back to 1980s, e.g. the torsional creep
problem
[
4
]
where the main interest lies in the
properties of solutions when
p
is large. An interesting result is that for
large
p
, the
p
-Laplacian equation is close to the infinity Laplacian
equation,

-\Delta_{\infty}u=0,\text{ }x\in\Omega.

This observation is also exploited in the study of sandpiles

[
2
]
[
11
]
where
p
-Laplacian with large
p

is used as a simplistic approximation of collapsing sandpile. The asymptotic
analysis for the
p
-Laplacian was later extended to the
p\left(x\right)
-Laplacian
[
24
]
. When
p\left(x\right)
is unbounded
in a subdomain
D\subset\Omega

[
25
]
, the
p\left(x\right)
-harmonic function is related to the infinity Laplacian equation subject to a
mixed Dirichlet-Neumann boundary condition. In the subdmain
D
, the mixed
Dirichlet-Neumann problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}-\Delta_{\infty}u=0,&x\in D;\\
u=g,&x\in\partial D;\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega\backslash\partial D,\end{array}\right.

coincides with the characterization of the tug-of-war game
[
26
]
[
6
]
. In addition to the
p\left(x\right)
-Laplacian,
limit of anisotropic
p
-Laplacian
[
27
]
and isotropic

\varphi
-Laplacian
[
28
]
were also considered. Another
thread of studies on limiting behaviour of
\left(\ref{prototype_quasilinear}\right)
is mostly variational and involves the
convergence of the associated energies, e.g.
[
5
]
.

Motivated by these works, we intend to present a complete study of
\left(\ref{prototype_quasilinear_n}\right)
and the related extremal problem

\left(\ref{prototype_quasilinear}\right)
, from the perspectives of both
partial differential equations and variational convergences. The quasilinear
problems
\left(\ref{prototype_quasilinear_n}\right)
and the extremal
problem
\left(\ref{prototype_quasilinear}\right)
encompass the
p\left(x\right)
-Laplacian problem considered in
[
25
]
. However, as
noted in
[
15
]
, going from the variable exponent
case to the general case requires new techniques on the level of both function
spaces and partial differential equations. There are also some new features
here. The general structure of the equation
\left(\ref{prototype_quasilinear_n}\right)
allows complex interplay between the

x
-variable and the gradient
\nabla u\left(x\right)
. Many properties
and results present for the case of
p\left(x\right)
-Laplacian are not
available for
\left(\ref{prototype_quasilinear_n}\right)
. Most
prominently, several new building blocks, particularly the uniqueness for the
limiting equation, some subtle estimates and the embedding in the generalized
Sobolev-Orlicz space with discontinuous coefficients
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}

have to be built in the course of solving
\left(\ref{prototype_quasilinear}\right)
.

We first give a rough sketch of our approaches and results. Here in this
article, we do not try to pursue full generality, so we restrict ourselves to
quasilinear problems of the following form

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}-\operatorname{div}\left(\dfrac{a_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|}\nabla u\left(x\right)\right)=0,&x\in\Omega;\\
u\left(x\right)=g\left(x\right),&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domains of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
g\in Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)
and

a_{n}\left(x,s\right)=\frac{\varphi_{n}\left(x,s\right)}{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}.

The assumptions on
\varphi_{n}
will be made clear in section
3
(ref.
\left(\ref{assumption_phi_x}\right)
). The natural energies
associated with this equation is denoted by

E_{n}\left(u\right):u\in W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)\mapsto{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}}\dfrac{\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right)}{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}dx,

where

\Phi_{n}\left(x,s\right)=\int_{0}^{s}\varphi_{n}\left(x,t\right)dt.

We intend to let
n\rightarrow\infty
and get limits of solutions
u_{n}\
of

\left(\ref{general_quasilinear_n_intro}\right)
, so require some
estimates independent of
n
. A key ingredient is a uniform gradient estimate
for the weak solutions of
\left(\ref{general_quasilinear_n_intro}\right)
. In obtaining the uniform gradient estimate, the boundary data
g
seems to
play a role. The first evidence for
g
’s effects is revealed by the
\Gamma
-convergence of the extended natural energies
\tilde{E}_{n}\left(u\right)
associated with
\left(\ref{general_quasilinear_n_intro}\right),

\tilde{E}_{n}\left(u\right):u\in L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}}\dfrac{\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right)}{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}dx,&u\in W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right);\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Hereafter when there is no confusion, we will use the same symbol
E_{n}
to
denote the natural energy on
W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
and its extension to
L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
. The

\Gamma
-convergence result suggests that, in order for
E_{n}
to be bounded,
we assume

(1.4)

\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}\leqslant 1.

Here
\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}
is the
Lipschitz constant of
g\in Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)
, i.e.,

\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}\triangleq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\partial\Omega\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|g\left(x\right)-g\left(y\right)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|}.

Previous studies for
p\left(x\right)
-Laplacian are mostly restricted to
the assumption
\left(\ref{assumption_g_lip}\right)
, e.g.

[
25
]
[
24
]
. In fact we show that, for
convex domains,
\left(\ref{assumption_g_lip}\right)
is a necessary and
sufficient condition for
E_{n}
to be bounded. We also show that the required
uniform gradient estimate can be obtained without the assumption
\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}\leqslant 1
, although

\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}>1
might cause
the energies
E_{n}
to explode as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Once we get the required estimates, the convergence of solutions can be proved
and the limiting equation for
\left(\ref{general_quasilinear_n_intro}\right)
turns out to be

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}-\Delta_{\infty}u-\left|\nabla u\right|\left\langle\Lambda\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right),\nabla u\right\rangle=0,&x\in\Omega;\\
u=g,&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Now here is one difficulty. Since this equation is not strictly coercive in

u
, uniqueness for such equation is usually difficult to obtain. The
classical comparison principle does not readily apply and the perturbation
technique suggested by the User’s guide
[
7
]
does not seem
to work either. So we have to prove a comparison principle for this limiting
equation. In the course of proving the uniqueness, some technical inequalities
have a crucial role to play. Once the uniqueness is obtained, the convergence
of the solutions for
\left(\ref{general_quasilinear_n_intro}\right)
follows.

Next we look into the extremal equation

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}-\operatorname{div}\left(\dfrac{a_{\infty}\left(x,\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|}\nabla u\left(x\right)\right)=0,&x\in\Omega;\\
u\left(x\right)=g\left(x\right),&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
a_{\infty}\left(x,s\right):\Omega\times\left[0,\infty\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R\cup}\left\{+\infty\right\}
is defined piecewise as
below. Let
\Omega_{\infty}
be a smooth subdomain of
\Omega
,
a\left(x,s\right)
an appropriate function on
\left(\Omega\backslash\Omega_{\infty}\right)\times\left[0,\infty\right)
. We write

(1.7)

a_{\infty}\left(x,s\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}0,&s\leqslant 1,x\in\Omega_{\infty};\\
\infty,&s>1,x\in\Omega_{\infty}.\end{array}\right.

and

(1.8)

a_{\infty}\left(x,s\right)=a\left(x,s\right)\text{ for }x\in\Omega\backslash\Omega_{\infty},\text{ }s\geqslant 0.

The basic question about understanding
\left(\ref{equ_extremal_intro}\right)
is whether it has a solution and in what sense. If it does have a
solution in some reasonable sense, then how regular it is and what values does
it assume on the boundary
\partial\Omega_{\infty}
where
a_{\infty}\left(x,s\right)
becomes discontinuous.

The idea of solving
\left(\ref{equ_extremal_intro}\right)
is to choose

\tilde{a}_{n}\left(x,s\right)
which approaches
\infty
on
\bar{\Omega}\times\left(1,\infty\right)
and
0
on
\bar{\Omega}\times\left[0,1\right)
in a way that suits our purpose
.
Such
\tilde{a}_{n}
clearly
approximates
a_{\infty}
in
\bar{\Omega}\times\left[0,\infty\right).

Then the study of
\left(\ref{equ_extremal_intro}\right)
comes down to
that of the limiting behaviour of
\left(\ref{general_quasilinear_n_intro}\right)
with
a_{n}
replaced by
\tilde{a}_{n}
. In view of the form of

a_{\infty}\left(x,s\right),
i.e.
\left(\ref{equ_extremal_a1}\right)\left(\ref{equ_extremal_a2}\right)
, the approximations
\tilde{a}_{n}\left(x,s\right)
are not continuous on
\bar{\Omega}\times\left[0,\infty\right)
unless
\Omega_{\infty}=\Omega
. However, the classical
Poincare’s inequality does not apply to the generalized Sobolev-Orlicz
space associated with discontinuous
\tilde{a}_{n}\left(x,s\right).
We
have to prove a Poincare’s inequality for discontinuous discontinuous

\tilde{a}_{n}\left(x,s\right)
so that we can find weak solutions.

In the appendix, we prove several technical inequalities which play essential
roles in proving the uniqueness for the limiting equation and the convergence
of solutions. These inequalities will also be of independent interest.

, we study the

-convergence of the natural energies associated with

. Section
5
and
section
6
are devoted to the convergence of solutions of

\left(\ref{general_quasilinear_n_intro}\right)
, both the case with

\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}\leqslant 1
and

are discussed. In section
7
, we prove a

\left(\ref{equ_limit_intro}\right)
, from which the uniqueness for

3.1.
\Phi
-function and Sobolev-Orlicz space

Assume that
\varphi\in C\left(\left[0,\infty\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\left(0,\infty\right)\right)
,
\varphi\left(s\right)>0
for
s>0
and there are constants
p^{-},

p^{+}
such that

(3.1)

0<p^{-}-1\leqslant\frac{s\varphi^{\prime}\left(s\right)}{\varphi\left(s\right)}\leqslant p^{+}-1\text{ for }s>0.

Definition 1
.

Let
\varphi
satisfy
\left(\ref{assumption_phi}\right)
. The function,

(3.2)

\Phi\left(s\right)=\int_{0}^{s}\varphi\left(t\right)dt

is referred to as a
\Phi
-function.

It is clear that a
\Phi
-function
\Phi\left(s\right):\left[0,\infty\right)\mapsto\left[0,\infty\right)
is a convex, (strictly)
increasing function such that
\Phi\left(0\right)=0
. In addition

\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\Phi\left(s\right)=\infty
, since non-constant
convex function is unbounded.

A
\Phi
-function is a
N
-function in the sense of
[
1
]
,
but not vice versa since the definition of
N
-functions does not require
convexity and differentiability. We cite the following Lemma from

[
21
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{1}\left(0,\infty\right)
satisfy

\left(\ref{assumption_phi}\right)
. Then
\Phi\in C^{2}
is convex and

\frac{s\varphi\left(s\right)}{p^{+}}\leqslant\Phi\left(s\right)\leqslant\frac{s\varphi\left(s\right)}{p^{-}}\text{ for }s>0;

or equivalently

(3.3)

p^{-}\leqslant\frac{s\varphi\left(s\right)}{\Phi\left(s\right)}\leqslant p^{+}\text{ for }s>0;

Lemma 2
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
H,

h:\left[0,\infty\right)\mapsto\left[0,\infty\right)
be differentiable functions
such that
h=H^{\prime}
. Then the function
s\mapsto s^{-\alpha}H\left(s\right)
is

\text{increasing if and only if }\alpha\leqslant\inf_{t>0}\frac{th\left(t\right)}{H\left(t\right)}\text{,}

and

\text{decreasing if and only if }\alpha\geqslant\sup_{t>0}\frac{th\left(t\right)}{H\left(t\right)}\text{.}

Moreover, let
p_{1},p_{2}>1
, then

(3.4)

p_{1}\leqslant\frac{sh\left(s\right)}{H\left(s\right)}\leqslant p_{2}\text{ for }s>0

if and only if

(3.5)

\min\left\{\varrho^{p_{1}},\varrho^{p_{2}}\right\}H\left(s\right)\leqslant H\left(\varrho s\right)\leqslant\max\left\{\varrho^{p_{1}},\varrho^{p_{2}}\right\}H\left(s\right)\text{ for }\varrho,s\geqslant 0.

Proof.

The first part of the conclusion follows easily by observing

\left(s^{-\alpha}H\left(s\right)\right)^{\prime}=s^{-\alpha}h\left(s\right)-\alpha s^{-\alpha-1}H\left(s\right)=s^{-\alpha-1}\left(sh\left(s\right)-\alpha H\left(s\right)\right).

It then follows that
\left(\ref{inequality_PHI_H}\right)
is equivalent
to

s^{-p_{1}}H\left(s\right)\text{ is non-decreasing, }s^{-p_{2}}H\left(s\right)\text{ is non-increasing.}

Moreover

s^{-p_{1}}H\left(s\right)\text{ is non-decreasing}\iff\left\{\begin{array}[c]{cl}\left(\varrho s\right)^{-p_{1}}H\left(\varrho s\right)\geqslant s^{-p_{1}}H\left(s\right),&\varrho\geqslant 1\\
\left(\varrho s\right)^{-p_{1}}H\left(\varrho s\right)\leqslant s^{-p_{1}}H\left(s\right),&0\leqslant\varrho<1\end{array}\right.,

and

s^{-p_{2}}H\left(s\right)\text{ is non-increasing}\iff\left\{\begin{array}[c]{cl}\left(\varrho s\right)^{-p_{2}}H\left(\varrho s\right)\leqslant s^{-p_{2}}H\left(s\right),&\varrho\geqslant 1\\
\left(\varrho s\right)^{-p_{2}}H\left(\varrho s\right)\geqslant s^{-p_{2}}H\left(s\right),&0\leqslant\varrho<1\end{array}\right..

Putting these together yields that
\left(\ref{inequality_PHI_H}\right)

is equivalent to

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{cl}\varrho^{p_{1}}H\left(s\right)\leqslant H\left(\varrho s\right)\leqslant\varrho^{p_{2}}H\left(s\right),&\varrho\geqslant 1\\
\varrho^{p_{2}}H\left(s\right)\leqslant H\left(\varrho s\right)\leqslant\varrho^{p_{1}}H\left(s\right),&0\leqslant\varrho<1\end{array}\right..

Therefore
\left(\ref{inequality_PHI_H}\right)
is equivalent to
\left(\ref{lemma_delta2_condition_inq0}\right).

∎

In view of Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
,
any
\Phi
-
function

\Phi
satisfies

(3.6)

\min\left\{\varrho^{p^{-}},\varrho^{p^{+}}\right\}\Phi\left(s\right)\leqslant\Phi\left(\varrho s\right)\leqslant\max\left\{\varrho^{p^{-}},\varrho^{p^{+}}\right\}\Phi\left(s\right)\text{ for }\varrho,s\geqslant 0.

One implication of
\left(\ref{lemma_delta2_condition_inq1}\right)
is
that
\Phi\left(\varrho\right)
roughly stays between
\varrho^{p^{+}}
and
\varrho^{p^{-}}
for all
\varrho\geqslant 0,
as can be seen by taking

s=1
in
\left(\ref{lemma_delta2_condition_inq1}\right)
. Another

\left(\ref{lemma_delta2_condition_inq1}\right)

following
\Delta_{2}
condition.

.

A function

(

Setting

Each
\Phi

Lemma 3
.

Assume
\Phi
is a
\Phi
-function. The mapping

s\mapsto\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)

is convex if
p^{-}\geqslant 2
. The mapping

s\mapsto\Phi^{1+\gamma}\left(s^{1/2}\right)

is convex if
\gamma\geqslant\left(\inf_{t>0}\frac{t\varphi\left(t\right)}{\Phi\left(t\right)}\right)^{-1}
. In particular, under condition

\left(\ref{assumption_phi}\right)
,
\Phi^{1+\gamma}\left(s^{1/2}\right)
is a convex function of
s
for any
\gamma\geqslant 1.

Proof.

The proof can be completed by straightforward calculations.

\frac{d}{ds}\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)=\varphi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\frac{1}{2s^{1/2}},

\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)=\varphi^{\prime}\left(s^{1/2}\right)\frac{1}{4s}-\varphi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\frac{1}{4s^{3/2}}=\frac{1}{4s^{3/2}}\left(s^{1/2}\varphi^{\prime}\left(s^{1/2}\right)-\varphi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\right).

The last term is nonegative if
p^{-}\geqslant 2,
hence
\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)
is convex whenever
p^{-}\geqslant 2
. In the same way,

\frac{d}{ds}\Phi^{1+\gamma}\left(s^{1/2}\right)=\left(1+\gamma\right)\Phi^{\gamma}\left(s^{1/2}\right)\varphi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\frac{1}{2s^{1/2}},

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\Phi^{1+\gamma}\left(s^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\gamma\right)\left[\gamma\Phi^{\gamma-1}\left(s^{1/2}\right)\varphi^{2}\left(s^{1/2}\right)\frac{1}{4s}+\Phi^{\gamma}\left(s^{1/2}\right)\varphi^{\prime}\left(s^{1/2}\right)\frac{1}{4s}-\Phi^{\gamma}\left(s^{1/2}\right)\varphi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\frac{1}{4s^{3/2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(1+\gamma\right)\frac{1}{4s^{3/2}}\Phi^{\gamma-1}\left(s^{1/2}\right)\left[\gamma s^{1/2}\varphi^{2}\left(s^{1/2}\right)+s^{1/2}\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\varphi^{\prime}\left(s^{1/2}\right)-\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\varphi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\right]

Note that the middle term in the last bracket is positive. By assumption

\displaystyle\gamma s^{1/2}\varphi^{2}\left(s^{1/2}\right)-\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\varphi\left(s^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\left[\gamma\frac{s^{1/2}\varphi\left(s^{1/2}\right)}{\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)}-1\right]

\displaystyle\geqslant\varphi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)\left[\gamma\inf_{t>0}\frac{t\varphi\left(t\right)}{\Phi\left(t\right)}-1\right]\geqslant 0.

Hence
\Phi^{1+\gamma}\left(s^{1/2}\right)
is convex whenever
\gamma\inf_{t>0}\frac{t\varphi\left(t\right)}{\Phi\left(t\right)}-1\geqslant 0.
By
\left(\ref{assumption_phi}\right)
and Lemma

1
,

\inf_{t>0}\frac{t\varphi\left(t\right)}{\Phi\left(t\right)}\geqslant p^{-}>1.

So we have

\gamma\geqslant\left(\inf_{t>0}\frac{t\varphi\left(t\right)}{\Phi\left(t\right)}\right)^{-1}\text{ for all }\gamma\geqslant 1,

therefore
\Phi^{1+\gamma}\left(s^{1/2}\right)
is convex in
s
for any

\gamma\geqslant 1.

∎

Remark 2

It is useful to observe that

Lemma 4
.

Let
\varphi\in C\left(\bar{\Omega}\times\left[0,\infty\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\Omega\times\left(0,\infty\right)\right)

\left(\ref{assumption_phi_x}\right)
and

be given by

satisfies the

\left(\ref{PHI_normalization_x}\right)
if and
only if there are constants

with

(3.16)

c^{-}\leqslant\Phi\left(x,1\right)\leqslant c^{+}\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

In particular, the constants in
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
can
be identified as
c^{-}=\beta^{p^{+}}
and
c^{+}=\beta^{-p^{+}}
. Conversely
if
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
holds, then the normalization

\left(\ref{PHI_normalization_x}\right)
is fulfilled with

\beta=\min\left\{\left(c^{-}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{-}}},\left(c^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{-}}}\right\}.

Proof.

First observe that
\left(\ref{PHI_normalization_x}\right)
is equivalent
to the following

(3.17)

\Phi\left(x,\beta\right)\leqslant 1\text{ and }\Phi\left(x,\beta^{-1}\right)\geqslant 1\text{ for }a.e.x\in\Omega

for some
\beta\in\left(0,1\right],
since the mapping
s\mapsto\Phi\left(x,s\right)
is strictly increasing on
\left(0,\infty\right)
and has an inverse for each

. Now it suffices to show that
\left(\ref{PHI_normalization_x_}\right)
is equivalent to
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
. Suppose that
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
holds
.
By virtue of Lemma
2
, for each

x\in\Omega
,
\varrho>0
,

(3.18)

\min\left\{\varrho^{p^{+}},\varrho^{p^{-}}\right\}\Phi\left(x,1\right)\leqslant\Phi\left(x,\varrho\right)\leqslant\max\left\{\varrho^{p^{+}},\varrho^{p^{-}}\right\}\Phi\left(x,1\right).

Using
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
we find

\min\left\{\varrho^{p^{+}},\varrho^{p^{-}}\right\}c^{-}\leqslant\Phi\left(x,\varrho\right)\leqslant\max\left\{\varrho^{p^{+}},\varrho^{p^{-}}\right\}c^{+}\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

Hence setting
\varrho
to

\beta=\min\left\{\left(c^{-}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{-}}},\left(c^{+}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{-}}}\right\}\in\left(0,1\right]

gives us

\displaystyle\Phi\left(x,\beta\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\left\{\beta^{p^{+}},\beta^{p^{-}}\right\}c^{+}=\beta^{p^{-}}c^{+}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{c^{-},\left(c^{+}\right)^{-1}\right\}c^{+}=\min\left\{c^{-}c^{+},\left(c^{+}\right)^{-1}c^{+}\right\}\leqslant 1,\text{ for
}x\in\Omega,

while setting
\varrho
to
\beta^{-1}
yields

\displaystyle\Phi\left(x,\beta^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\min\left\{\beta^{-p^{+}},\beta^{-p^{-}}\right\}c^{-}=\beta^{-p^{-}}c^{-}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\left(c^{-}\right)^{-1},c^{+}\right\}c^{-}=\max\left\{\left(c^{-}\right)^{-1}c^{-},c^{+}c^{-}\right\}\geqslant 1,\text{ for
}x\in\Omega.

As a consequence
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
implies
\left(\ref{PHI_normalization_x_}\right)
. Conversely assume that
\Phi
satisfies

\left(\ref{PHI_normalization_x_}\right)
, which in combination with

\left(\ref{lm_normalization_Inq1}\right)
yields that
,

\beta^{p^{+}}\Phi\left(x,1\right)=\min\left\{\beta^{p^{+}},\beta^{p^{-}}\right\}\Phi\left(x,1\right)\leqslant\Phi\left(x,\beta\right)\leqslant 1,

and

\beta^{-p^{+}}\Phi\left(x,1\right)=\max\left\{\beta^{-p^{+}},\beta^{-p^{-}}\right\}\Phi\left(x,1\right)\geqslant\Phi\left(x,\beta^{-1}\right)\geqslant 1.

Hence

\beta^{p^{+}}\leqslant\Phi\left(x,1\right)\leqslant\beta^{-p^{+}},

proving
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
with
c^{-}=\beta^{p^{+}}\leqslant 1
and
c^{+}=\beta^{-p^{+}}\geqslant 1
.
∎

Definition 4
.

Each
\Phi
defines a modular for

\varrho_{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(u\right)=\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,\left|u\left(x\right)\right|\right)dx.

The generalized Orlicz space (also Musielak-Orlicz space)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leqslant 1\right\}.

We refer to
[
17

for the proof that

norm. Hereafter, instead of
\Phi

Lemma 5
.

If

\min\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}^{p^{-}},\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}^{p+}\right\}\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,\left|u\left(x\right)\right|\right)dx\leqslant\max\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}^{p^{-}},\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}^{p+}\right\}.

Proof.

The conclusion clearly holds if
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}=0
, which implies
u=0

a.e.

x\in\Omega
. Suppose

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}>0

. Since

,
\lambda<\infty
. By virtue of Lemma
2
and the definition of

we have

\min\left\{\lambda^{-p^{-}},\lambda^{-p^{+}}\right\}\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,\left|u\left(x\right)\right|\right)dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,\frac{1}{\lambda}\left|u\left(x\right)\right|\right)dx=\varrho_{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leqslant 1.

Then

\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,\left|u\left(x\right)\right|\right)dx\leqslant\max\left\{\lambda^{p^{-}},\lambda^{p^{+}}\right\}<\infty,

and the right hand side of the desired inequality follows. Similarly using
Lemma
2
again, we have that for
\mu>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,\frac{1}{\mu}\left|u\left(x\right)\right|\right)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\left\{\mu^{-p^{-}},\mu^{-p^{+}}\right\}\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,\left|u\left(x\right)\right|\right)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,\left|u\left(x\right)\right|\right)dx}{\min\left\{\mu^{p^{-}},\mu^{p^{+}}\right\}}.

If

Hence by the definition of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}

(3.21)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}\leqslant\mu.

Therefore we obtain from
\left(\ref{lm_modulo_finite_Inq1}\right),

\left(\ref{lm_modulo_finite_Inq2}\right)
that

\min\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}^{p^{-}},\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}^{p+}\right\}\leqslant\min\left\{\mu^{p^{-}},\mu^{p^{+}}\right\}=\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,\left|u\left(x\right)\right|\right)dx.

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{p_{n}^{-}}}^{p_{n}^{-}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p_{n}^{-}}1_{\left\{\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|\leqslant 1\right\}}dx+\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p_{n}^{-}}1_{\left\{\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|>1\right\}}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|\right)}{\Phi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}1_{\left\{\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|>1\right\}}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|+\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}{\Phi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}\frac{\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|\right)}{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|+E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)p_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|+E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)\mu p_{n}^{-},

where the last inequality is due to
\left(\ref{assumption_c_n}\right)
.
(ii) Now the energies
E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)
are bounded by
\sigma>0
.
Note that the function
x\mapsto\left(1+x\right)^{\frac{1}{x}}
is
decreasing on
\left(0,\infty\right)
. Using Hölder’s inequality and
the result of (i) we get, for
p_{n}^{-}\geqslant m,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{m}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{p_{n}^{-}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}\left(\left|\Omega\right|+\sigma\mu p_{n}^{-}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}

\displaystyle=\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{m}}\left(1+\frac{\sigma\mu p_{n}^{-}}{\left|\Omega\right|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+\left|\Omega\right|\right)\left(1+\frac{\sigma\mu}{\left|\Omega\right|}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{1}\left(\Omega,\sigma,\mu\right).

(iii) In view of Lemma
2
, the mapping
s\mapsto s^{-m}\Phi_{n}\left(x,s\right)
is increasing. Hence by Theorem

2

W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)\subset W^{1,m}\left(\Omega\right)\text{ for all }n\text{ for which }p_{n}^{-}\geqslant m.

Using Poincaré’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{W^{1,m}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|u_{n}-v\right\|_{W_{0}^{1,m}}+\left\|v\right\|_{W^{1,m}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{2}\left(\Omega,m\right)\left\|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{m}}+c_{3}\left(\Omega\right)\left\|v\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{4}\left(\Omega,m\right)\left(\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{m}}+\left\|v\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\right).

Therefore
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{W^{1,m}}
is bounded whenever

\left\{\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{m}}\right\}
is bounded.
The proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Extend the functional
E_{n}\left(u\right)

from
W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
to

L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
as below

E_{n}\left(u\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}}\dfrac{\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right)}{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}dx,&u\in W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right);\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and let

E_{\infty}\left(u\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}0,&u\in W^{1,\infty}\left(\Omega\right)\text{ and }\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant 1;\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then
\Gamma\left(L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)\right)
-
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{n}=E_{\infty}.

Proof.

1. Existence of recovery sequence. Fix
u\in L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
. If

E_{\infty}\left(u\right)=

\infty
, then there is nothing to prove. If

E_{\infty}\left(u\right)<\infty
, then
E_{\infty}\left(u\right)=0
,

u\in W^{1,\infty}\left(\Omega\right)
and
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant 1.
It follows that

E_{\infty}\left(u\right)=0\leqslant E_{n}\left(u\right)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right)}{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Phi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}dx\leqslant\frac{\left|\Omega\right|}{p_{n}^{-}}\rightarrow 0.

Hence
v_{n}\equiv u
is a recovery sequence.

2.
E_{n}
has asymptotic common lower bound. Consider an arbitrary sequence

v_{n}\rightarrow u
in
L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
. If there are only a
finite number of
v_{n}
belonging to
W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
, then
E_{n}\left(v_{n}\right)=\infty
for all

n
large. Hence
\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{n}\left(v_{n}\right)=\infty
, the desired inequality clearly holds. Now we assume up
to a subsequence that
v_{n}\in W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
for
n\geqslant 1
and that

E_{n}\left(v_{n}\right)\text{ is a bounded sequence.}

In view of Lemma
6
, we can further assume that
\nabla v_{n}
converges weakly to some
\varpi

\in L^{m}\left(\Omega\right)
,

m\geqslant 1
. Since
v_{n}\rightarrow u
in
L^{1}\left(\Omega\right),

we infer that
\varpi=\nabla u
and
u\in W^{1,m}\left(\Omega\right)
. We
need to show

E_{\infty}\left(u\right)\leqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{n}\left(v_{n}\right).

This amounts to showing

\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|\leqslant 1\text{ for
}a.e.\text{ }x\in\Omega.

For
\epsilon>0,
let

\Omega_{\epsilon}=\left\{x\in\Omega:\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|>1+\epsilon\right\}.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\left|\nabla u\left(y\right)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}\right|^{1-\frac{1}{m}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{m}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}\right|^{1-\frac{1}{m}}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\nabla v_{n}\right\|_{L^{m}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}\right|^{1-\frac{1}{m}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\nabla v_{n}\right\|_{L^{p_{n}^{-}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}\right|^{1-\frac{1}{m}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}\left(\left|\Omega\right|+E_{n}\left(v_{n}\right)\mu p_{n}^{-}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}.

Note that the energies
E_{n}\left(v_{n}\right)
are bounded. Taking the
limit
p_{n}^{-}\rightarrow\infty
and then
m\rightarrow\infty,
we get

\left(1+\epsilon\right)\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}\right|\leqslant\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}\left|\nabla u\left(y\right)\right|dy\leqslant\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}\right|.

So
\left|\Omega_{\epsilon}\right|=0
. Since
\epsilon>0
is
arbitrary, this shows that
\left|\left\{\left|\nabla u\right|>1\right\}\right|=0.
The proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 5
.

Assume that
\Phi_{n}

\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_n_1}\right),
the local continuity

\left(\ref{PHI_condition_A11}\right)

. Then the equation
\left(\ref{quasilinear_PDE_n}\right)

In addition
u\in C\left(\bar{\Omega}\right)\cap W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right).

Proof.

First observe that
E_{n}
is coercive by Theorem

3
(see also Remark
2
and
\left(\ref{assumption_phi_1_lbd}\right)
). Let
v_{k}\in g+W_{0}^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
be a minimizing sequence
for

. Then it is bounded in
g+W_{0}^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
. Since
W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
is reflexive by Theorem
1
, we can assume
without loss of generality that
v_{k}
converges as

weakly to some

. By the form of

, it is clear that

can be
regarded as an equivalent norm on

\left\{\nabla v\in L^{\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right):v\in W^{1,\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)\right\}.

It follows that, since
\nabla v_{k}
converges weakly to
\nabla u
in

,

E_{n}\left(u\right)\leqslant\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}E_{n}\left(v_{k}\right).

This shows that

is a minimizer of

. Since the mapping

\xi\mapsto\frac{\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\xi\right|\right)}{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}

is strictly convex, the uniqueness of the minimizer follows from the standard

[
8
]
. Finally noting that

2
, we get by Theorem
2

Also note that

. Under our assumption, the energy

\left(\ref{quasilinear_PDE_n}\right)

\left(\ref{quasilinear_PDE_n}\right).

∎

Given a
continuous
function
F:\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}\mapsto\mathbb{R}

continuous
function

, we consider degenerate

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}F\left(x,\nabla u,D^{2}u\right)=0,&x\in\Omega\\
u=g,&x\in\partial\Omega\end{array}\right..

The solution is understood in the viscosity sense
[
7
]
.

Remark 3
.

For our equation, we have

F\left(x,\nabla u,D^{2}u\right)=-\operatorname{div}\left(\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|}\nabla u\left(x\right)\right).

As is required by
\left(\ref{dirichlet_PDE}\right)
, we want
\left(x,p,X\right)\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\right)\mapsto F\left(x,p,X\right)
to be continuous. Recall that

a\left(x,s\right)=\left.\varphi\left(x,s\right)\right/\varphi\left(x,1\right)
(see
\left(\ref{eq:def_a_xs}\right)
) with

\varphi
satisfying
\left(\ref{assumption_phi_x}\right).

Straightforward calculation yields that

\displaystyle F\left(x,p,X\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{a\left(x,p\right)}{p}\text{tr}\left(X\right)-\partial_{s}a\left(x,\left|p\right|\right)\text{tr}\left(\frac{p}{\left|p\right|}\otimes\frac{p}{\left|p\right|}X\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{a\left(x,\left|p\right|\right)}{\left|p\right|}\text{tr}\left(\frac{p}{\left|p\right|}\otimes\frac{p}{\left|p\right|}X\right)-\partial_{x}a\left(x,\left|p\right|\right)\cdot p.

To fulfill the continuity requirement, it is enough to ensure the continuity
at
p=0.
For this we assume that

(5.6)

p^{-}>3\text{ and there exists }a_{0}\in C\left(\Omega\right)\text{ so
that }\lim_{s\rightarrow 0+}\partial_{x}a\left(x,s\right)=a_{0}\left(x\right)\text{ in }\Omega,

where

is the index as in
\left(\ref{assumption_phi_x}\right)
.
Indeed, under the assumption
\left(\ref{assumption_phi_x}\right)
,

\left(p^{-}-1\right)\frac{a\left(x,s\right)}{s}\leqslant\partial_{s}a\left(x,s\right)\leqslant\left(p^{+}-1\right)\frac{a\left(x,s\right)}{s}\text{ for }x\in\Omega,\text{ }s>0.

Hence we readily have that with
\left(\ref{eq:assume_F_cont}\right),

\displaystyle\left.\frac{a\left(x,p\right)}{p}\right|_{p=0}

\displaystyle=0,\text{
}\left.\partial_{s}a\left(x,\left|p\right|\right)\frac{p}{\left|p\right|}\otimes\frac{p}{\left|p\right|}\right|_{p=0}=0,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{a\left(x,\left|p\right|\right)}{\left|p\right|}\text{tr}\left(\frac{p}{\left|p\right|}\otimes\frac{p}{\left|p\right|}X\right)\right|_{p=0}

\displaystyle=0,\text{
}\left.\partial_{x}a\left(x,\left|p\right|\right)\cdot p\right|_{p=0}=0.

By this understanding

is defined everywhere as a
continuous function. In particular,

F\left(x,0,X\right)=-a_{0}\cdot 0=0.

Theorem 6
.

Assume that
\Phi_{n}

For notational simplicity, we will drop the subscripts

\Phi_{n},

, which is a

\zeta\in W^{1,\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)

is a viscosity subsolution. That

To show that the right hand side of the inequality

\displaystyle=\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla u\right|}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}-\left\langle\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla u\right|}\nabla u,\nabla\zeta\right\rangle-\left\langle\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla\zeta\right|}\nabla\zeta,\nabla u\right\rangle+\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla\zeta\right|}\left|\nabla\zeta\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla u\right|}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}-\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla u\right|}\left|\nabla u\right|\left|\nabla\zeta\right|-\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla\zeta\right|}\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\left|\nabla u\right|+\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla\zeta\right|}\left|\nabla\zeta\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla u\right|}\left|\nabla u\right|-\frac{a\left(x,\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\right)}{\left|\nabla\zeta\right|}\left|\nabla\zeta\right|,\left|\nabla u\right|-\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle a\left(x,\left|\nabla u\right|\right)-a\left(x,\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\right),\left|\nabla u\right|-\left|\nabla\zeta\right|\right\rangle

which is nonnegative in view of the monotonicity of
s\mapsto a\left(x,s\right)
.

6.
Limit of the Solutions without
\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}\leqslant 1

We revisit in this section the Lipschitz assumption on the boundary value
g
.
Recall that in showing the existence of the limit of the weak solutions

u_{n}
, the boundedness of the energies
E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)
, which
sufficiently ensures that
u_{n}
is bounded in
W^{1,d+1}\left(\Omega\right)
, plays a major role. This is a result of the assumption that

\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}\leqslant 1
, as
we see in the proof of Theorem
7
. We will see shortly
that, under appropriate conditions,
E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)
is unbounded
whenever
\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}>1
.
This clearly echoes the
\Gamma
-convergence result of Theorem

4
.

In general, for smooth domain
\Omega
and
v\in W^{1,\infty}\left(\Omega\right)
, we only have

\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)}\leqslant\left\|v\right\|_{Lip\left(\Omega\right)}.

In the follow lemma, which is a generalization of
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
, we will assume that
\Omega
is convex so that

\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)}=\left\|v\right\|_{Lip\left(\Omega\right)}.

Lemma 7
.

Assume
\Omega
is convex and
\left(\ref{assumption_c_n}\right)
holds. Let
u_{n}
be the minimizer of
E_{n}

in
g+W_{0}^{1,\Phi_{n}}\left(\Omega\right)
. If
\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}>1
, then
E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)

is unbounded.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)
is bounded by some
constant
M>0.
Fix
m>d
. Let
p_{n}^{-}\geqslant m
. Recalling Remark

1
,
g\in W^{1,\infty}\left(\Omega\right)
. As in the proof of

(6.1)

\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{m}}\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{p_{n}^{-}}}\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}\left(\left|\Omega\right|+\mu p_{n}^{-}E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}\text{,}

and

(6.2)

\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{W^{1,m}}\leqslant c\left(\Omega,m\right)\left(\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{m}}+\left\|g\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\right)\text{.}

Note that

\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}\leqslant\left(\left|\Omega\right|+\mu p_{n}^{-}E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}\leqslant\left(\left|\Omega\right|+\mu Mp_{n}^{-}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}.

It follows that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\left|\Omega\right|+\mu p_{n}^{-}E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}=1.

By
\left(\ref{lm_energies_unbounded_Inq1}\right)
and
\left(\ref{lm_energies_unbounded_Inq2}\right),

\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{m}}
, hence
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{W^{1,m}}
is
bounded. Extracting a subsequence when necessary, we assume that
u_{n}

converges weakly in
W^{1,m}
to some

Then taking

\left(\ref{lm_energies_unbounded_Inq1}\right)

\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|_{L^{m}}\leqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{m}}\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{m}}\text{ for all }m>d,

from which we obtain, by taking limit

that

and

\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant 1.

Since
p_{n}^{-}>d
,
u_{n}
assumes the boundary data
g
continuously, hence

on
\partial\Omega
. Since
\Omega
is convex

This is a contradiction to

is unbounded.
∎

\left(m>d\right)
. But our purpose here is to show that, without the assumption

Theorem 9

Assume that

Let

\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{m}}\leqslant 12\mu\left(1+\eta^{\mu}\right)\left(1+\left|\Omega\right|\right)^{2}.

Proof.

Since

p_{n}^{-}\leqslant\frac{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}{\Phi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}\leqslant p_{n}^{+},

in view of
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1_n}\right)
, we have, for large

n

(6.5)

p_{n}^{+}=p_{n}^{+}\Phi_{n}\left(x,1\right)\geqslant\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)\geqslant p_{n}^{-}\Phi_{n}\left(x,1\right)=p_{n}^{-}\geqslant 1.

Then by virtue of the minimality of
u_{n}
and Remark
1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{n}^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|\right)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|\right)}{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant E_{n}\left(g\right)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla g\right|\right)}{\varphi_{n}\left(x,1\right)}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{n}\left(x,\left|\nabla g\right|\right)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{n}\left(x,\eta\right)dx\leqslant\max\left\{\eta^{p_{n}^{-}},\eta^{p_{n}^{+}}\right\}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{n}\left(x,1\right)dx

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\eta^{p_{n}^{-}},\eta^{p_{n}^{+}}\right\}\left|\Omega\right|.

Invoking Lemma

\min\left\{\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}}^{p_{n}^{-}},\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}}^{p_{n}^{+}}\right\}\leqslant p_{n}^{+}\max\left\{\eta^{p_{n}^{-}},\eta^{p_{n}^{+}}\right\}\left|\Omega\right|.

It follows that

\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}}\leqslant\max\left\{\eta^{\frac{p_{n}^{+}}{p_{n}^{-}}},\eta^{\frac{p_{n}^{-}}{p_{n}^{+}}}\right\}\max\left\{\left(p_{n}^{+}\left|\Omega\right|\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}},\left(p_{n}^{+}\left|\Omega\right|\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{+}}}\right\}.

The maximum of the function
x\mapsto x^{\frac{1}{x}}
on
\left[1,\infty\right)
is
e^{\frac{1}{e}}
which is less than
3
. Recalling

\left(\ref{assumption_c_n}\right)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}}\leqslant\max\left\{\eta^{\frac{p_{n}^{+}}{p_{n}^{-}}},\eta^{\frac{p_{n}^{-}}{p_{n}^{+}}}\right\}\max\left\{\left(p_{n}^{-}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}}\left(\mu\left|\Omega\right|\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{-}}},\left(p_{n}^{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{+}}}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{p_{n}^{+}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 3\mu\max\left\{1,\eta^{\mu}\right\}\max\left\{1,\left|\Omega\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 3\mu\left(1+\eta^{\mu}\right)\left(1+\left|\Omega\right|\right)

is bounded, although the energies

might not be. Note that the upper bound of

As a final remark to this section, we give a brief review on the connections
between Gamma convergence of the natural energies
E_{n}
and the convergence
of
u_{n\text{.}}.
Clearly the convergence of
E_{n}
cannot be cut off from
the convergence of
u_{n\text{.}}
. They are closed related. With
\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}\leqslant 1,
the convergence
of
u_{n}
has been suggested by the Gamma convergence, i.e. Theorem

4
. It is also implied that, with
\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}>1
, the energies

E_{n}\left(u_{n}\right)
would explode as we have proved in Lemma

7
. In this sense, in the case
\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}\leqslant 1
, Theorem

4
serves as a prelude to Theorem

8
. Therefore, in the case
\left\|g\right\|_{Lip\left(\partial\Omega\right)}>1,
it is natural to ask whether there
are some some form of Gamma convergence results that echoes the convergence of

u_{n}
? The answer is clearly affirmative in the classical case: Let
\Omega

be bounded. If
\varpi_{n}
converges weakly to
\varpi\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)
in
L^{m}
for some
m\geqslant 1
, then

(6.7)

\left\|\varpi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\varpi_{n}\right\|_{L^{n}}.

This can be proved by employing Hölder’s inequality

\left\|\varpi_{n}\right\|_{L^{m}}\leqslant\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{n}}\left\|\varpi_{n}\right\|_{L^{n}}\text{ for
all }n\geqslant m.

Successively sending
n\rightarrow\infty
and then
m\rightarrow\infty
yields

\left(\ref{Inq_classical_norm_lsc}\right).
This, expressed in the
language of Gamma convergence, is
\Gamma\left(L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)\right)
-
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{\tilde{E}}_{n}=\mathcal{\tilde{E}}_{\infty},
where

\mathcal{\tilde{E}}_{n}:L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{n}},&u\in W^{1,n}\left(\Omega\right);\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and

\mathcal{\tilde{E}}_{\infty}:L^{1}\left(\Omega\right)\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}},&u\in W^{1,\infty}\left(\Omega\right);\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

One implication of this Gamma convergence is that: if
u_{n}
minimizes

\mathcal{\tilde{E}}_{n}
over
g+W^{1,n}\left(\Omega\right)
, then any
cluster point, say
u
, of
u_{n}
should minnimize
\mathcal{\tilde{E}}_{\infty},
i.e.

u=\arg\min_{w\in g+W_{0}^{1,\infty}\left(\Omega\right)}\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Hence
u
is infinity harmonic, i.e. the Gamma convergence of
\mathcal{\tilde{E}}_{n}
echoes the convergence of
u_{n}
. This can be regarded as an
alternative proof for the classical result that
-\Delta_{p}
is approximately
an infinity Laplacian when the constant exponent

is large.

Coming back to the Orlicz setting, in general, we cannot simply replace

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{n}}
with
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{n}\left(\cdot\right)}}

a complete parallel for the usual

\left(p\geqslant 1\right)
a generalized

is equivalent but not equal to

dicussions of the scalings of generalized Orlicz spaces and its consequences
on Luxemburg norms, we refer to
[
17

being said, there are some situations where such analogy exists.

Remark 7

We shall show that the equation

Comparison principles for such equations are generally difficult to obtain.
The most notable example is the infinity Laplacian. The uniqueness of the

-Laplacian

[

]

[

0<p<d
,

p^{\ast}=\dfrac{dp}{d-p}.

Note that by the definition of the generalized
\Phi
-function,
1<p_{\Phi}^{-}\leqslant p_{\Phi,A}^{-}\leqslant p_{\Phi,A}^{+}\leqslant p_{\Phi}^{+}<\infty
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded.

\Phi
,
\Psi:

\Omega\times\left[0,\infty\right)\longmapsto\mathbb{R}

are generalized
\Phi
-functions satisfying the normalization condition

\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
with constant
\beta\in\left(0,1\right]
. If
p_{\Phi}^{+}\leqslant p_{\Psi}^{-}
, then
L^{\Psi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)\hookrightarrow L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
, i.e. there is a constant
c\left(\Omega,d,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Psi}^{-},\beta\right)
such that for
u\in L^{\Psi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
,

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Psi}^{-},\beta\right)\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Psi\left(\cdot\right)}}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
L^{\Psi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)\hookrightarrow L^{p_{\Psi}^{-}}\left(\Omega\right)
and
L^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)\hookrightarrow L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
.

Step I
: We prove that
L^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}\left(\Omega\right)\hookrightarrow L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right).
To see
this, we note that by Lemma
2
, the mapping

s\rightarrow\Phi\left(x,s\right)/s^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}
is decreasing and the
normalization condition is equivalent to
\left(\ref{PHI_normalization_x_}\right).
Then

\frac{\Phi\left(x,s\right)}{s^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}\leqslant\frac{\Phi\left(x,\beta\right)}{\beta^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}\text{ for }s\geqslant\beta\text{, }x\in\Omega.

It follows that

\Phi\left(x,s\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}s^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}\text{ for }s\geqslant\beta\text{, }x\in\Omega.

Since
\Phi\left(x,s\right)
is increasing in
s
, so for all

s\geqslant 0
,
x\in\Omega,

\Phi\left(x,s\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}s^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}+\Phi\left(x,\beta\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}s^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}+1.

Let
K=1+2\left|\Omega\right|
. Then

\Phi\left(x,\frac{s}{K}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{K^{p_{\Phi}^{-}}}\Phi\left(x,s\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{K}\Phi\left(x,s\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}s^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}+\frac{1}{2\left|\Omega\right|},\text{ for }s\geqslant 0\text{, }x\in\Omega.

Let
u\in L^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}
and

\lambda>\beta^{-1}2^{\frac{1}{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}.

Setting
s=\left|u\right|/\lambda
in the previous inequality and
integrating over
\Omega
gives

\varrho_{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\frac{\left|u\right|}{K\lambda}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}\frac{\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}{\lambda^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}+\frac{1}{2}\leqslant 1.

Hence

\left\|u\right\|_{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\leqslant K\lambda.

Letting
\lambda\downarrow\beta^{-1}2^{\frac{1}{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}
yields that

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}\leqslant\left(1+2\left|\Omega\right|\right)\beta^{-1}2^{\frac{1}{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}.

Step II
: We prove that
L^{\Psi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)\hookrightarrow L^{p_{\Psi}^{-}}\left(\Omega\right).

Indeed, similar to Step I the normalization condition
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
gives

\beta^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}\leqslant\Phi\left(x,1\right)\leqslant\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}}\text{ and }\beta^{p_{\Psi}^{+}}\leqslant\Psi\left(x,1\right)\leqslant\beta^{-p_{\Psi}^{+}}.

For
s\geqslant 1,

\Phi\left(x,s\right)\leqslant s^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}\Phi\left(x,1\right)

and since
p_{\Phi}^{+}\leqslant p_{\Psi}^{-}
,

\Psi\left(x,s\right)\geqslant s^{p_{\Psi}^{-}}\Psi\left(x,1\right)\geqslant s^{p_{\Phi}^{+}}\Psi\left(x,1\right).

It follows that

\Phi\left(x,s\right)\leqslant\frac{\Phi\left(x,1\right)}{\Psi\left(x,1\right)}\Psi\left(x,s\right)\leqslant\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}-p_{\Psi}^{+}}\Psi\left(x,s\right)\text{ for }s\geqslant 1.

Hence for
s\geqslant 0
,
x\in\Omega,

\Phi\left(x,s\right)\leqslant\Phi\left(x,1\right)+\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}-p_{\Psi}^{+}}\Psi\left(x,s\right)\leqslant\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}}+\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}-p_{\Psi}^{+}}\Psi\left(x,s\right).

For any
K\geqslant 1,

\Phi\left(x,\frac{s}{K}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{K}\Phi\left(x,s\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{K}\left[\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}}+\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}-p_{\Psi}^{+}}\Psi\left(x,s\right)\right].

Let
u\in L^{\Psi}
and
\lambda>\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Psi\left(\cdot\right)}}.
Then

\varrho_{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\frac{\left|u\right|}{K\lambda}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{K}\left[\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}}\left|\Omega\right|+\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}-p_{\Psi}^{+}}\varrho_{\Psi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\frac{\left|u\right|}{\lambda}\right)\right].

We may also increase
K
(e.g.
K=1+2\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}}\left|\Omega\right|+2\beta^{-p_{\Phi}^{+}-p_{\Psi}^{+}}
) so that

\varrho_{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\frac{\left|u\right|}{K\lambda}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\varrho_{\Psi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\frac{\left|u\right|}{\lambda}\right)\right]\leqslant 1.

Therefore

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}\leqslant K\lambda.

Letting
\lambda\downarrow\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Psi\left(\cdot\right)}}
gives the desired conclusion.
∎

Theorem 17

(
\Phi\left(\cdot\right)
-Poincare’s inequality)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
bounded.
\Phi:

\Omega\times\left[0,\infty\right)\longmapsto\mathbb{R}

is a generalized
\Phi
-function satisfying the normalization condition

\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
with constant
\beta\in\left(0,1\right]

such that for

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,\delta,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Phi}^{-},\beta\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}}.

Proof.

In view of the jump condition and the boundedness of
\Omega
, there are a
finite number of balls with radius
\delta>0,
say
B_{1},...,B_{J},
such
that

\Omega\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}B_{j},

and on each ball

either
p_{\Phi,B_{j}}^{-}\geqslant d
or

p_{\Phi,B_{j}}^{+}\leqslant p_{\Phi,B_{j}}^{-\ast}.
Fix
x_{0}\in\Omega
.
Then

\tilde{\Phi}\left(x,s\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\Phi\left(x,s\right),&x\in\Omega,\text{ }s\geqslant 0;\\
\Phi\left(x_{0},s\right),&x\in\Omega^{c},\text{ }s\geqslant 0,\end{array}\right.

which defines an extension of
\Phi
to all of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\left[0,\infty\right)\longmapsto\mathbb{R}
, is a generalized
\Phi
-function
satisfying the normalization condition
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
with constant
\beta
. Denote by
\tilde{u}
the zero extension of
u
as in
Theorem
16
, then
\left\|u\right\|_{W_{0}^{1,\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)}=\left\|\tilde{u}\right\|_{W^{1,\tilde{\Phi}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}.
Let

\Phi_{j}:\left(x,s\right)\in B_{j}\times\left[0,\infty\right)\rightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\Phi\left(x,s\right),&x\in B_{j}\cap\Omega,\text{ }s\geqslant 0;\\
s^{p_{\Phi,B_{j}}^{-}},&x\in B_{j}\cap\Omega^{c},\text{ }s\geqslant 0.\end{array}\right.

Note that
p_{\Phi,B_{j}}^{-}
is defined as in
\left(\ref{def_p_jump_inq1}\right)
, the infimum in
p_{\Phi,B_{j}}^{-}
is taken over
B_{j}\cap\Omega
, not
B_{j}.
Then

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\tilde{u}\right\|_{L^{\tilde{\Phi}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|\tilde{u}1_{B_{j}\cap\Omega}\right\|_{L^{\tilde{\Phi}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|\tilde{u}1_{B_{j}\cap\Omega}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{j}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(B_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|\tilde{u}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{j}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(B_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{B_{j}}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{j}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(B_{j}\right)}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left|\tilde{u}_{B_{j}}\right|\left\|1_{B_{j}}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{j}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(B_{j}\right)},

where

\tilde{u}_{B_{j}}=\frac{1}{\left|B_{j}\right|}\int_{B_{j}}\tilde{u}\left(x\right)dx.

Now we estimate the two sums in the above inequality. For each term in the
first sum, if
p_{\Phi,B_{j}}^{+}\leqslant p_{\Phi,B_{j}}^{-\ast},
then we
apply Lemma
9
and the generalized Poincare’s
inequality (e.g.
[
10
, Theorem 8.2.13]
) to get

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{B_{j}}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{j}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(B_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,\delta,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Phi}^{-}\right)\left\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{B_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p_{\Phi_{j},B_{j}}^{+}}\left(B_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,\delta,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Phi}^{-}\right)\left\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{B_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p_{\Phi_{j},B_{j}}^{-\ast}}\left(B_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,\delta,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Phi}^{-}\right)\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}\right\|_{L^{p_{\Phi_{j},B_{j}}^{-}}\left(B_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,\delta,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Phi}^{-}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)}.

Otherwise if
p_{\Phi,B_{j}}^{-}\geqslant d,
then we may find
q<d
so that

q^{\ast}=p_{\Phi_{j},B_{j}}^{+}
. Now
q<p_{\Phi,B_{j}}^{-}.
By the same
arguments, the above inequalities continue to hold except
\left\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{B_{j}}\right\|_{L^{p_{\Phi_{j},B_{j}}^{-\ast}}\left(B_{j}\right)}
in the second being replaced with
\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}\right\|_{L^{q}\left(B_{j}\right)}
. For the second sum, applying
Poincare’s inequality again we have

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{u}_{B_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\left|B_{j}\right|}\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|dx\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,\delta\right)\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|dx\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,\delta\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p_{\Phi}^{-}}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,\delta\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)}.

By Lemma
5

\displaystyle\left\|1_{B_{j}}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{i}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(B_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|1_{\Omega}\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\left\{\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,1\right)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{\Phi}^{-}}},\left(\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(x,1\right)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{\Phi}^{+}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Phi}^{-},\beta\right).

In the last inequality, we have used the normalization condition
\left(\ref{assumption_PHI_1}\right)
. Putting these estimates together we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{B_{j}}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{j}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(B_{j}\right)}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left|\tilde{u}_{B_{j}}\right|\left\|1_{B_{j}}\right\|_{L^{\Phi_{j}\left(\cdot\right)}\left(B_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{J}c\left(\Omega,d,\delta,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Phi}^{-}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}c\left(\Omega,d,\delta,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Phi}^{-},\beta\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left(\Omega,d,\delta,p_{\Phi}^{+},p_{\Phi}^{-},\beta\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\Phi\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)}.

∎

The main purpose of this section is to demonstrate the uses of the results

-Harmonic equation

We note that,

With Poincare’s Inequality for Discontinuous

A remark on the convexity is in order. The convexity of

\Phi

Lemma 11
.

Let
\gamma
be given as in Lemma
3
. Then, for
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(10.1)

\left\langle\psi\left(\left|\xi\right|\right)\xi-\psi\left(\left|\eta\right|\right)\eta,\xi-\eta\right\rangle\geqslant c^{-}p^{-}\min\left\{\frac{\Phi^{1+\gamma}\left(3^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left|\xi-\eta\right|\right)}{\Phi^{\gamma}\left(\left|\xi\right|+\left|\eta\right|\right)},\frac{\Phi^{1+\gamma}\left(\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\xi-\eta\right|\right)}{4\Phi^{\gamma}\left(\left|\xi\right|+\left|\eta\right|\right)}\right\},

where

and

is a constant
independent of

and
p^{+}
.

If

(10.2)

\left\langle\psi\left(\left|\xi\right|\right)\xi-\psi\left(\left|\eta\right|\right)\eta,\xi-\eta\right\rangle\geqslant\min\left\{\Phi\left(\left|\xi-\eta\right|\right),\frac{p^{-}}{4}\Phi\left(\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\xi-\eta\right|\right)\right\},

where

is a constant independent of
p^{-}
and
p^{+}
.

We start with the proof for
\left(\ref{inequality_main_inq2}\right).

Proof.

We have
p^{-}\geqslant 2
in this case.

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi\left(\left|\xi\right|\right)\xi-\psi\left(\left|\eta\right|\right)\eta,\xi-\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\left(\psi\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|\right)\left(s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right)\right)ds,\xi-\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left[\psi\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|\right)\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}+\frac{\psi^{\prime}\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|\right)}{\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|}\left|\left\langle s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta,\xi-\eta\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right]ds.

\left(\ref{assumption_phi}\right)
,

\frac{s\varphi^{\prime}\left(s\right)}{\varphi\left(s\right)}\geqslant p^{-}-1\geqslant 1,

It follows that

Hence we have for

H\left(s,\xi,\eta\right)\geqslant\psi\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|\right)\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}.

We now distinguish two cases.

Case I:
\left|\xi\right|\geqslant\left|\xi-\eta\right|.

Then

\displaystyle\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\xi+\left(1-s\right)\left(\xi-\eta\right)\right|\geqslant\left|\xi\right|-\left(1-s\right)\left|\xi-\eta\right|

\displaystyle\geqslant s\left|\xi-\eta\right|.

\psi^{\prime}\geqslant 0
. It follows that

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi\left(\left|\xi\right|\right)\xi-\psi\left(\left|\eta\right|\right)\eta,\xi-\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{0}^{1}\psi\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|\right)\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{0}^{1}\psi\left(s\left|\xi-\eta\right|\right)\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{0}^{1}s\psi\left(s\left|\xi-\eta\right|\right)\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\Phi\left(s\left|\xi-\eta\right|\right)ds=\Phi\left(\left|\xi-\eta\right|\right)

Case II:
\left|\xi\right|<\left|\xi-\eta\right|.
Then

\displaystyle\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\xi+\left(1-s\right)\left(\xi-\eta\right)\right|\leqslant\left|\xi\right|+\left(1-s\right)\left|\xi-\eta\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(2-s\right)\left|\xi-\eta\right|.

Using this inequality and Lemma
1
we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi\left(\left|\xi\right|\right)\xi-\psi\left(\left|\eta\right|\right)\eta,\xi-\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geqslant\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varphi\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|\right)}{\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|}ds

\displaystyle\geqslant p^{-}\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\Phi\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|\right)}{\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\geqslant p^{-}\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\Phi\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|\right)}{\left(2-s\right)^{2}\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{p^{-}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|\right)ds

Note that for some

small we have

(10.4)

\int_{0}^{1}\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|^{2}ds=\frac{1}{3}\left(\left|\xi\right|^{2}+\left|\eta\right|^{2}+\left\langle\xi,\eta\right\rangle\right)\geqslant\kappa\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}.

By Lemma
3
,
s\longmapsto G\left(s\right)=\Phi\left(s^{1/2}\right)
is an increasing convex function. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{p^{-}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|\right)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{-}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}G\left(\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{p^{-}}{4}G\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left|s\xi+\left(1-s\right)\eta\right|^{2}ds\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{p^{-}}{4}G\left(\kappa\left|\xi-\eta\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{-}}{4}\Phi\left(\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\xi-\eta\right|\right).

∎

Precompact families of Carathéodory differential equations revisited Thanks: All authors were partly supported by MICIIN/FEDER project PID2021-125446NB-I00
Sylvia Novo, Rafael Obaya, Ana M. Sanz

Abstract
. The space of parametric b-measures endowed with appropriate topologies is introduced to define a new class of generalized ODEs given by parametric b-measures. This framework offers a new approach for dealing with precompact families of Carathéodory ODEs using nonautonomous dynamical systems techniques. An application to the study of the dynamics of the fast variables of a slow-fast system of ODEs, where the fast motion is determined by a Carathéodory vector field with equicontinuous m m -bounds and bounded l l -bounds, is given.

1.
Introduction

The study of Carathéodory ordinary differential equations (ODEs, for short) is a classical question that has received intense attention since its inception, both for the interest in the theory itself and for the importance of its applications in science and engineering problems. Recently, Longo
[
12
]
and Longo et al.
[
13
,
14
]
have introduced and investigated strong and weak topologies of integral type defined on appropriate spaces of Carathéodory functions. The convergence of a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, with respect to one of these topologies, requires the convergence of the integrals of the evaluation of the functions
f_{n}
either pointwise over a dense and countable subset
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(topologies
\mathcal{T}_{D},\sigma_{D}
) or uniformly over sets of bounded continuous functions with a common modulus of continuity (topologies
\mathcal{T}_{\Theta},\sigma_{\Theta}
). These works contain sufficient conditions on a set
E
of Carathéodory functions implying that the mentioned topologies are topologies of continuity on
E
for the corresponding Carathéodory ODEs. Longo et al.
[
15
,
16
]
apply these conclusions to the study of mathematical models of critical transitions.

An important result in this theory states that if
E
is a set of Carathéodory functions with equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds and
D
is a dense and countable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then
\sigma_{D}
is a topology of continuity on
E
and the space
(E,\sigma_{D})
is a precompact metric space that, however, is not complete unless stronger conditions are satisfied (see Artstein
[
1
]
and
[
12
]
). In this paper, we provide a precise construction for the compactification of
E
by identifying the Carathéodory ODEs with a subset of the space of the so-called
ODEs given by parametric b-measures
(a new type of generalized ODEs governed by standard methods of ODEs), endowed with an extended
\sigma_{D}
topology. This construction simplifies the application of dynamical methods, based on the existence of ergodic measures, compact omega-limit sets and local, pullback or global attractors, in the study of precompact families of Carathéodory ODEs, and allows the extension to this context of previous results stated in the literature for more restrictive families of ODEs.

This new class of generalized ODEs is part of the classical measure differential equations in the terms stated by Das and Sharma
[
5
]
, Pandit and Deo
[
18
]
and Schmaedeke
[
20
]
. It also falls within the class of generalized Kurzweil differential equations whose main properties can be found in Kurzweil
[
10
,
11
]
and Schwabik
[
21
]
(the more recent reference Bonotto et al.
[
4
]
addresses a revised and expanded version of these questions with applications on Banach spaces). In both cases, our generalized ODEs given by parametric b-measures are a small and simple subset of the cited families of equations, with the main advantage that they inherit their properties directly from the Carathéodory equations.

In the following, we describe the organization and main achievements of the paper.
The basic theory of parametric b-measures is established in Sections
2
-
4
.
Inspired by the fact that to a scalar Carathéodory map
g(y,t)
we can associate a parametric family of absolutely continuous (with respect to the Lebesgue measure) real measures on compact sets of
\mathbb{R}
,
d\nu_{y}=g(y,t)\,dt
(
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
), in Section
2
we introduce the concept of a parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which is a parametric family
\nu\equiv\{\nu_{y}\}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
of real Borel measures on bounded sets of
\mathbb{R}
without purely discontinuous part and with
m
-bounds and
l
-bounds given by positive continuous measures. Then, the Riemann approach is used to define the integral of a parametric b-measure along a continuous curve over a compact interval,
\int_{a}^{b}d\nu_{y(s)}
.

Section
3
contains an extended version of the weak topologies
\sigma_{\Theta},\sigma_{D}
mentioned above to the space
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
of parametric b-measures on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Some topological properties of a subset
E\subset\mathfrak{M_{p}}
, such as boundedness or equicontinuity of the
m
-bounds/
l
-bounds, are transferred to its closure in
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
,
\overline{E}:=\mathrm{cls}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\mathcal{T})}(E)
for
\mathcal{T}\in\{\sigma_{\Theta},\sigma_{D}\}
. Furthermore, if
E
has bounded
l
-bounds, both topologies coincide. The main result asserts that if
E
has equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds, then
\overline{E}
is compact. Thus, when
E
is a precompact set of Carathéodory functions, its closure in the space of parametric b-measures defines its compactification.

In Section
4
we address the continuity of the time translation map
\mathbb{R}\times\overline{E}\to\mathfrak{M_{p}}
,
(t,\nu)\mapsto\nu{\cdot}t
. If
\nu
is a parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with equicontinuous
m
-bounds, then the hull of
\nu
,
\mathrm{Hull}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\mathcal{T})}(\nu):=\mathrm{cls}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\mathcal{T})}\{\nu{\cdot}t\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is invariant under translation and
(t,\nu)\mapsto\nu{\cdot}t
defines a continuous flow on it, for
\mathcal{T}\in\{\sigma_{\Theta},\sigma_{D}\}
.

The second part of the paper is devoted to the study of generalized ODEs
y^{\prime}=\bar{\nu}_{y(t)}
given by parametric b-measures and their application to Carathéodory ODEs. These equations are introduced and investigated in Section
5
. Solutions are continuous functions that satisfy the integral equation
y(t)=y(t_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\bar{\nu}_{y(s)}
; hence, they are continuous functions of bounded variation on compact intervals, but in general they are not absolutely continuous. Standard techniques of ODEs allow us to state the basic theory on local existence, uniqueness, and extension of solutions. It is also true in this context that
\sigma_{D}
is a topology of continuity for the solutions under the assumptions of equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds. As a consequence, if
\bar{\nu}_{0}\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}^{\!\!\!N}
has the former properties, a local continuous skew-product flow on
\mathrm{Hull}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}}^{\!\!\!N},\bar{\sigma}_{D})}(\bar{\nu}_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
is defined through translation on the hull and the solutions of the generalized ODEs over the hull.
All these results are applied in Section
5.1
, where a Carathéodory ODE is identified with a generalized ODE given by an absolutely continuous parametric b-measure, preserving the solutions. Precompact families of Carathéodory ODEs have already been considered in Artstein
[
1
]
. Our theory allows for an advantageous treatment, keeping the construction as close to Carathéodory ODEs as possible. In Section
5.2

some numerical examples are provided to see how solutions of the type of differential equation described in this work may look.

Finally, Section
6
illustrates an application of the previous theory in a context of slow-fast systems of ODEs, where the fast motion is governed by a Carathéodory map with equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds. By identifying the equations with generalized ODEs given by parametric b-measures, we can build the hull, which is compact, and the skew-product semiflow induced by the solutions. Then, techniques of nonautonomous dynamical systems allow us to extend previous results by Artstein
[
2
]
and Longo et al.
[
17
]
now under rather relaxed conditions.

2.
Integration of parametric b-measures along curves

In this section, we introduce the concept of parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and define the integral of a function with respect to a parametric b-measure evaluated along a curve in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We start with some preliminaries.

As usual,
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the
N
-dimensional Euclidean space with norm
|\cdot|
and
B_{r}
is the closed ball of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
centered at the origin and with radius
r
.
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
will denote the space of real continuous functions on the real line with compact support endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. We denote by
\mathcal{A}
the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}
and by
\mathcal{M}^{+}
the set of locally finite regular Borel (positive) measures on
\mathbb{R}
endowed with the vague topology, i.e., a sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of measures in
\mathcal{M}^{+}

vaguely converges to
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
, and we write it as
\mu_{n}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\sigma}}\mu
, if and only if

\lim_{{n\to\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(s)\,d\mu_{n}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(s)\,d\mu(s)\qquad\text{for each }\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})\,.

As shown in Kallenberg
[
7
, Theorem 15.7.7, p.170]
,
(\mathcal{M}^{+},\widetilde{\sigma})
is a Polish space, i.e., it is separable and completely metrizable.

From the decomposition theorem for regular Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}
(see Hewitt and Stromberg
[
6
, Theorem 19.61, p.337]
), the set
\mathcal{M}^{+}
decomposes as
\mathcal{M}^{+}=\mathcal{M}^{+}_{ac}\oplus\mathcal{M}^{+}_{sc}\oplus\mathcal{M}^{+}_{pd}
, the classes of absolutely continuous and singular continuous measures with respect to the Lebesgue measure
l
, and purely discontinuous measures, respectively. We will denote by
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{c}
the subset of measures without purely discontinuous part, that is,
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{c}=\mathcal{M}^{+}_{ac}\oplus\mathcal{M}^{+}_{sc}
.

Before we introduce the concept of
parametric b-measure
, we need to define what a
b-measure
is.
Let
\mathcal{B}
denote the ring of bounded Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}
and let
\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
denote the set of maps
\mu\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfy:

(i)

\mu(\emptyset)=0
;

(ii)

\mu(\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_{n})
for every countable family
\{A_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{B}
of pairwise disjoint sets such that
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
;

(iii)

\mu(\{t\})=0
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The elements of
\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
are called (continuous real)
b-measures
.

Remarks 2.2
.

(1) We write “b-measure” to indicate that
\mu
is defined on the ring of bounded Borel sets, where the countably additive property (ii) holds. Note that property (ii) implicitly requires that the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_{n})
is unconditionally convergent, or equivalently, by Riemann’s theorem, absolutely convergent.
(2) Property (iii) means that
\mu
is continuous as a measure. (3) When a b-measure
\mu
is restricted to a compact set
K
of
\mathbb{R}
, it defines a continuous real measure on
K
; see
[
6
, Section 19]
for the theory of abstract integration with respect to real measures. (4) The set
\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
has a vector structure: if
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
, the sum
(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})(A):=\mu_{1}(A)+\mu_{2}(A)
,
A\in\mathcal{B}
defines a b-measure. Also, if
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, then
(\alpha\mu_{1})(A):=\alpha\mu_{1}(A)
,
A\in\mathcal{B}
defines a b-measure.

Example 2.3
.

The set of b-measures includes two important cases. (i) The restriction to
\mathcal{B}
of a continuous regular Borel signed measure is included in
\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
. (ii) If
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R},l)
, the space of real Lebesgue locally integrable maps on
\mathbb{R}
(
L^{1}_{loc}
, for simplicity), then the map
\mu(A):=\int_{A}f(t)\,dt
,
A\in\mathcal{B}
defines a b-measure.

For a regular Borel signed measure
\mu
, one can define its
total variation

|\mu|
by
|\mu|:=\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}
for the positive measures
\mu^{+}
and
\mu^{-}
given in its Jordan decomposition. In fact, as proved in
[
6
, Theorem 19.10]
,

|\mu|(A)=\sup\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mu(A_{i})|\,\bigg|\,\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\}\;\hbox{is a Borel partition of}\;A\bigg\}\,.

(2.1)

Note that we can use this formula to define a positive b-measure
|\mu|
from a real b-measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
, by taking partitions
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{B}
.

We define what we mean by a parametric b-measure with respect to a non-decreasing family of moduli of continuity
\{\omega_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\mid j\geq 1\}
(that is,
\omega_{j}
is continuous and non-decreasing,
\omega_{j}(0)=0
and
\omega_{j}\leq\omega_{j+1}
for all
j\geq 1
).

Definition 2.4
.

Given a non-decreasing family of moduli of continuity
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, a
parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a map
\nu\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B}),\;y\mapsto\nu_{y}

such that for each integer
j\geq 1
there are positive measures
m_{j},l_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
satisfying:

(i)

|\nu_{y}|(A)\leq m_{j}(A)
for each subset
A\in\mathcal{B}
and each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|y|\leq j
;

(ii)

|\nu_{y_{1}}-\nu_{y_{2}}|(A)\leq l_{j}(A)\,\omega_{j}(|y_{1}-y_{2}|)
for each subset
A\in\mathcal{B}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|y_{1}|,|y_{2}|\leq j
.

The measures
m_{j}
and
l_{j}
of
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
are called
m
-bounds
and
l
-bounds
of
\nu
on the closed ball
B_{j}
, respectively. The set of parametric b-measures on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with respect to the family of moduli
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is denoted by
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
. An
M
-dim parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a map
\bar{\nu}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})^{M},\;y\mapsto(\nu^{1}_{y},\ldots,\nu^{M}_{y})
such that each component
\nu^{i}
,
1\leq i\leq M
is a parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Note that
\{\nu_{y}\}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is a parametric family of b-measures and
y
is a vector-valued parameter. When we look at the map
\nu\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
, (ii) is a continuity property of
\nu
on
B_{j}
given through the modulus of continuity
\omega_{j}
. The name
l
-bound
comes from the standard Lipschitz-type condition in the literature of Carathéodory differential equations (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
13
,
14
]
). Here we keep that name, although we admit other moduli of uniform continuity rather than the identity map.

It is easy to check that the space
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
of parametric b-measures on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with respect to a family of moduli of continuity
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
has a vector structure with standard definitions.
Let us give a simple example for later use.

Proposition 2.5
.

Given a b-measure
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
that has an m-bound
m_{0}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{c}
over
\mathcal{B}
and a map
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,1]
of class
C^{1}
with compact support, the map
\,\phi\,\nu_{0}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
,
y\to\phi(y)\nu_{0}

defines a parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with respect to the trivial family of moduli given by the identity map.

Proof.

Clearly
\phi\,\nu_{0}
is well-defined.
Note that
|\phi(y)\nu_{0}|(A)=\phi(y)\,|\nu_{0}|(A)\leq m_{0}(A)
for each subset
A\in\mathcal{B}
and every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover, for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, by the Mean Value theorem there exists a
c_{0}>0
such that

|\phi(y_{1})\nu_{0}-\phi(y_{2})\nu_{0}|(A)=|\phi(y_{1})-\phi(y_{2})|\,|\nu_{0}|(A)\leq c_{0}\,m_{0}(A)\,|y_{1}-y_{2}|\,.

Therefore, (i) and (ii) of Definition
2.4
hold with
m
-bounds and
l
-bounds, and modulus
\omega_{j}={\rm id}
, all independent of
j\geq 1
. The proof is finished.
∎

In order to introduce the integration of real maps with respect to parametric b-measures evaluated along curves
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we give the following definitions.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\delta>0
. A
tagged

\delta
-
partition
of the real interval
[a,b]
is a set

\Delta^{\delta}\coloneqq\{(\tau_{i},[t_{i},t_{i+1}])\mid i=1,\ldots,k-1\}

satisfying
[a,b]=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
,
a=t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{k}=b
,
\tau_{i}\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
, and
t_{i+1}-t_{i}<\delta
for each
i=1,\ldots,k-1
.
Given a map
h\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, a parametric b-measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a curve
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we will denote by

S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}\big(h,\Delta^{\delta}\big)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}h(\tau_{i})\,d\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}h(\tau_{i})\,\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}[t_{i},t_{i+1}]

the (Riemann) sum corresponding to the tagged
\delta
-partition
\Delta^{\delta}
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\nu
be a parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and consider a map
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
. A bounded map
h\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is
integrable with respect to
\nu
evaluated along the curve
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
if there exists an
L\in\mathbb{R}
such that for each
\varepsilon>0
there is a
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that for every tagged
\delta(\varepsilon)
-partition
\Delta^{\delta(\varepsilon)}=\{(\tau_{i},[t_{i},t_{i+1}])\mid i=1,\ldots,k-1\}
of
[a,b]
,

\Big|L-S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}\big(h,\Delta^{\delta(\varepsilon)}\big)\Big|=\bigg|L-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}h(\tau_{i})\,d\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}\bigg|<\varepsilon\,.

In this case, we write
\int_{a}^{b}h(s)\,d\nu_{y(s)}=L
. When the map
h
is identically equal to
1
, we will simply say that the parametric b-measure
\nu
is
integrable along the curve

y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
and write
\int_{a}^{b}d\nu_{y(s)}
for the integral and

S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}\big(\Delta^{\delta}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}d\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}[t_{i},t_{i+1}]

for the Riemann sums. If
a<b
, then
\int_{b}^{a}d\nu_{y(s)}:=-\int_{a}^{b}d\nu_{y(s)}
.

As usual, as stated in the next result, whose proof is omitted, the integrability can be checked in terms of the Cauchy property for the Riemann sums.

Proposition 2.8
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

the map
h\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is integrable with respect to the parametric b-measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
evaluated along the curve
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
;

(ii)

given
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that
\big|S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(h,\Delta_{1}^{\delta(\varepsilon)})-S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(h,\Delta_{2}^{\delta(\varepsilon)})\big|<\varepsilon

for all tagged
\delta(\varepsilon)
-partitions
\Delta_{1}^{\delta(\varepsilon)}
and
\,\Delta_{2}^{\delta(\varepsilon)}
of
[a,b]
.

We do not include an exhaustive list with the standard properties of the integral defined in this way, but we remark that it is important that the b-measures
\nu_{y}
satisfy
\nu_{y}(\{t\})=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Remark 2.9
.

To prove that every continuous map
h(t)
is integrable with respect to a parametric b-measure
\nu
evaluated along a continuous curve
y(t)
, it will suffice to study the integrability of the map
h_{0}(t)\equiv 1
,
t\in[a,b]
along continuous curves. The reason is that one can build the parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,
\tilde{\nu}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
,
(y,y_{N+1})\mapsto y_{N+1}\,\nu_{y}
(with respect to different moduli of continuity) and the continuous map
\tilde{y}\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}
,
t\mapsto(y(t),h(t))
and, given a tagged
\delta
-partition
\Delta^{\delta}
of
[a,b]
, it holds that
S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}\big(h,\Delta^{\delta}\big)=S_{\tilde{\nu}}^{\tilde{y}(\cdot)}\big(\Delta^{\delta}\big)
.

To prove that each parametric b-measure is integrable along a continuous curve, we need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

Given a parametric b-measure
\nu\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with respect to the family of moduli
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, a continuous map
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
and an
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that if
|\tau_{i}-\widetilde{\tau}_{i}|<\delta(\varepsilon)

(\tau_{i},\widetilde{\tau}_{i}\in[a,b],1\leq i\leq k-1)
, then

\bigg|\,\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left(d\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}-d\nu_{y(\widetilde{\tau}_{i})}\right)\bigg|<\varepsilon\,

for every partition of
[a,b]=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}I_{i}
with
I_{i}=[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
and
a=t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}=b
.

Proof.

Since
y
is continuous in
[a,b]
, there is a
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
y([a,b])\subset B_{j}
. Let
l_{j}
be an
l
-bound of
\nu
on
B_{j}
, as in Definition
2.4
(ii). Notice that
l_{j}([a,b])=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}l_{j}(I_{i})
because
l_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
has no purely discontinuous part. Denote
l_{j}([a,b])=M
and assume first that
M>0
. From the uniform continuity of
y
and the continuity of the modulus
\omega_{j}
at
0
, given
\varepsilon>0
there is a
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that
\omega_{j}(|y(\tau_{i})-y(\widetilde{\tau}_{i})|)<\varepsilon/M
provided that
|\tau_{i}-\widetilde{\tau}_{i}|<\delta(\varepsilon)
. As a consequence,

\displaystyle\bigg|\,\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left(d\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}-d\nu_{y(\widetilde{\tau}_{i})}\right)\bigg|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}-\nu_{y(\widetilde{\tau}_{i})}|(I_{i})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}l_{j}(I_{i})\,\omega_{j}(|y(\tau_{i})-y(\widetilde{\tau}_{i})|)<\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}l_{j}(I_{i})\,\frac{\varepsilon}{M}=l_{j}([a,b])\,\frac{\varepsilon}{M}=\varepsilon\,,

as claimed. Finally, if
M=0
, the value of the sum is
0
by the previous bounds.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Every parametric b-measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is integrable along every continuous map
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

It is enough to check that (ii) in Proposition
2.8
holds. Given
\varepsilon>0
we take
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon/2)>0
provided in Lemma
2.10
and two tagged
\delta
-partitions
\Delta_{1}^{\delta}
and
\,\Delta_{2}^{\delta}
of
[a,b]
. By ordering all the points in these two partitions and adding some extra endpoints and tags, when necessary, we can build a new tagged
\delta
-partition
\Delta_{3}^{\delta}
that is finer than both
\Delta_{1}^{\delta}
and
\Delta_{2}^{\delta}
. Then,

\left|S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta_{1}^{\delta})-S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta_{2}^{\delta})\right|\leq\left|S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta_{1}^{\delta})-S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta_{3}^{\delta})\right|+\left|S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta_{3}^{\delta})-S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta_{2}^{\delta})\right|.

From the way of constructing
\Delta_{3}^{\delta}
and Lemma
2.10
we can conclude that
\big|S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta_{1}^{\delta})-S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta_{2}^{\delta})\big|<\varepsilon
and the proof is finished.
∎

It is immediate to check the following result regarding the integration of a parametric b-measure of the form
\phi\,\nu_{0}
, and the proof is omitted.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\nu^{*}=\phi\,\nu_{0}
be a parametric b-measure in the class considered in
Proposition
2.5
and let
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a continuous map. Then,

\int_{a}^{b}d\nu^{*}_{y(s)}=\int_{a}^{b}\phi(y(s))\,d\nu_{0}\,.

Note that
\int_{a}^{b}\phi(y(s))\,d\nu_{0}
is the integral on
[a,b]
of
\phi(y(s))
with respect to the real measure
{\nu_{0}}|_{[a,b]}
, the restriction of
\nu_{0}
to
[a,b]
(see Remark
2.2
(3)).

We finish this section with a useful result and an immediate corollary.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\nu\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
be a parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with respect to the family of moduli
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, with
m
-bounds
\{m_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and
l
-bounds
\{l_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, and consider two continuous maps
x,\,y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
. If
x([a,b]),y([a,b])\subset B_{j}
, then

\bigg|\int_{a}^{b}d\nu_{y(s)}\bigg|\leq m_{j}[a,b]\;\text{ and }\;\bigg|\int_{a}^{b}[d\nu_{x(s)}-d\nu_{y(s)}]\bigg|\leq l_{j}[a,b]\,\omega_{j}(\|{x-y}\|_{[a,b]})\,,

where
\|\,{\cdot}\,\|_{[a,b]}
denotes the sup-norm on the interval
[a,b]
.

Proof.

Since the integral is the limit of the Riemann sums, for the first inequality, it suffices to check that for every tagged
\delta
-partition
\Delta^{\delta}=\{(\tau_{i},[t_{i},t_{i+1}])\mid i=1,\ldots,k-1\}
of
[a,b]
,

|\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}(I_{i})|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}|(I_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}m_{j}(I_{i})=m_{j}[a,b]
, where we have written
I_{i}=[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
. Analogously, from

\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(\nu_{x(\tau_{i})}-\nu_{y(\tau_{i})})(I_{i})\bigg|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}l_{j}(I_{i})\,\omega_{j}(|x(\tau_{i})-y(\tau_{i})|)\leq l_{j}[a,b]\,\omega_{j}(\|{x-y}\|_{[a,b]})\,,

the second inequality is easily deduced. Notice that
m_{j}
,
l_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
has been used.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\nu\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
and let
\{y_{n}(\cdot)\}_{n\geq 1}\subset C([a,b],\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a sequence converging uniformly to
y_{0}(\cdot)
in
[a,b]
.
Then,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}d\nu_{y_{n}(s)}=\int_{a}^{b}d\nu_{y_{0}(s)}\,.

Proof.

The proof follows from Lemma
2.13
, once noted that by the uniform convergence there is a
j\geq 1
such that
y_{n}([a,b])\subset B_{j}
for every
n\geq 0
.
∎

3.
Topologies in the space of parametric b-measures

In this section, we introduce some topologies in the space
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
of parametric b-measures on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with respect to a fixed family of moduli of continuity
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
(see Definition
2.4
). Then, we study how some topological properties of the
m
-bounds and
l
-bounds of a subset
E\subset\mathfrak{M_{p}}
are transferred to its closure. The main result is the compactness of
E
with equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds.

In order to introduce a useful topology in
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
based on suitable sets of moduli of continuity, as in Longo et al.
[
13
,
14
]
for spaces of Carathéodory functions, we recall the definition. For
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
it is understood that
q_{1}<q_{2}
.

Definition 3.1
.

We call
a suitable set of moduli of continuity
any countable set of non-decreasing continuous functions

\Theta=\left\{\theta^{I}_{j}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R}^{+})\mid j\in\mathbb{N},\ I=[q_{1},q_{2}],\ q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}\right\}

such that
\theta^{I}_{j}(0)=0
for every
\theta^{I}_{j}\in\Theta
, and with the relation of partial order given by

\theta^{I_{1}}_{j_{1}}\leq\theta^{I_{2}}_{j_{2}}\quad\text{whenever }I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}\text{ and }j_{1}\leq j_{2}\,.

We endow the space
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
of parametric b-measures with the following topologies.

Definition 3.2

(Topology
\sigma_{\Theta}
)
.

Let
\Theta
be a suitable set of moduli of continuity. We call
\sigma_{\Theta}
the topology on
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
generated by the family of seminorms

p_{I,j}(\nu)=\sup_{y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}}\left|\,\int_{I}d\nu_{y(s)}\right|,\quad\nu\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}\,,\vskip-0.71114pt

for each
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
denotes the set of functions
y(\cdot)
in
C(I,B_{j})
which have
\theta^{I}_{j}
as modulus of continuity, that is,

\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}:=\{y(\cdot)\in C(I,B_{j})\mid|y(t_{1})-y(t_{2})|\leq\theta^{I}_{j}(|t_{1}-t_{2}|)\;\hbox{for all }t_{1},t_{2}\in I\}\,.

(3.1)

\left(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{\Theta}\right)
is a locally convex metric space.

Definition 3.3

(Topology
\sigma_{D}
)
.

Let
D
be a dense and countable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We call
\sigma_{D}
the topology on
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
generated by the family of seminorms

p_{I,y}(\nu)=\left|\,\int_{I}d\nu_{y}\,\right|,\quad\nu\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}\,,

for
y\in D
,
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
.
\left(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{D}\right)
is a locally convex metric space.

Remark 3.4
.

Notice that
\sigma_{D}\leq\sigma_{\Theta}
.

If a function
y(\cdot)
belongs to the set
\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
given in Definition
3.2
, and we take
p_{1}
,
p_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
J=[p_{1},p_{2}]\subset I
, then
y(\cdot)
does not necessarily belong to
\mathcal{K}_{j}^{J}
. Thus, we need to consider the next technical lemma that will be used later.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Theta
be a suitable set of moduli of continuity as in
Definition
3.1
.
Let
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence in
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
that converges to
\nu_{0}\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
in the topology
\sigma_{\Theta}
. Then, for each integer
j\geq 1
and each interval
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}}\left|\int_{p_{1}}^{p_{2}}\left[d(\nu_{n})_{y(s)}-d(\nu_{0})_{y(s)}\right]\right|=0

whenever
p_{1}
,
p_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
q_{1}\leq p_{1}<p_{2}\leq q_{2}
.

Proof.

For each
y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
, we define
\widetilde{y}\colon I\to B_{j}
by

\widetilde{y}(t)=y(p_{1})
if
t\in[q_{1},p_{1}]
,
\widetilde{y}(t)=y(t)
if
t\in[p_{1},p_{2}]
, and
\widetilde{y}(t)=y(p_{2})
if
t\in[p_{2},q_{2}]
. The map
\widetilde{y}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
and

\left|\int_{p_{1}}^{p_{2}}\left[d(\nu_{n})_{y(s)}-d(\nu_{0})_{y(s)}\right]\right|=\left|\int_{p_{1}}^{p_{2}}\left[d(\nu_{n})_{\widetilde{y}(s)}-d(\nu_{0})_{\widetilde{y}(s)}\right]\right|\leq I_{n}^{1}+I_{n}^{2}+I_{n}^{3}\,,

where
I_{n}^{1}=\big|\int_{q_{1}}^{q_{2}}\left[d(\nu_{n})_{\widetilde{y}(s)}-d(\nu_{0})_{\widetilde{y}(s)}\right]\big|
,
I_{n}^{2}=\big|\int_{q_{1}}^{p_{1}}\left[d(\nu_{n})_{y(p_{1})}-d(\nu_{0})_{y(p_{1})}\right]\big|
and

I_{n}^{3}=\big|\int_{p_{2}}^{q_{2}}\left[d(\nu_{n})_{y(p_{2})}-d(\nu_{0})_{y(p_{2})}\right]\big|
, from which the result can easily be deduced because
\nu_{n}\xrightarrow{\sigma_{\Theta}}\nu_{0}
,
\widetilde{y}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
and the constant functions
y(p_{1})
and
y(p_{2})
belong to
\mathcal{K}_{j}^{[q_{1},p_{1}]}
and
\mathcal{K}_{j}^{[p_{2},q_{2}]}
, respectively.
∎

Next, we introduce some topological properties of the
m
-bounds and
l
-bounds depending on the standard definitions for families of positive measures in
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
.

Definition 3.6
.

A family of positive measures
\{\mu_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}

(i)

is
bounded
if there is a positive constant
c>0
such that
\mu_{\alpha}[t,t+1]\leq c
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in\Gamma
;

(ii)

is
equicontinuous

if for each
\varepsilon>0
there is a
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that
\mu_{\alpha}[s,t]<\varepsilon
whenever
0\leq t-s<\delta(\varepsilon)
and
\alpha\in\Gamma
;

(iii)

is
uniformly integrable
if given
\varepsilon>0
there is a
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that if
A
is a Borel set satisfying
A\subset[t,t+1]
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
l(A)<\delta(\varepsilon)
, then
\mu_{\alpha}(A)<\varepsilon
for each
\alpha\in\Gamma
.

Remark 3.7
.

According to the previous definitions, uniform integrability implies equicontinuity, and equicontinuity implies boundedness. Moreover, notice that condition (ii) allows for singular continuous measures with respect to the Lebesgue measure, whereas with condition (iii) all the measures of the family are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Definition 3.8
.

A family
\{\nu_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}\subset\mathfrak{M_{p}}
of parametric b-measures with
m
-bounds
\{\{m_{j}^{\alpha}\}_{j\geq 1}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
and
l
-bounds

\{\{l_{j}^{\alpha}\}_{j\geq 1}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}

(i)

has bounded
m
-bounds
(resp.,
bounded
l
-bounds
) if the set
\{m_{j}^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}
(resp.,
\{l_{j}^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}
) is bounded for each
j\geq 1
;

(ii)

has equicontinuous
m
-bounds
(resp.,
equicontinuous
l
-bounds
) if the set
\{m_{j}^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}
(resp.,
\{l_{j}^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}
) is equicontinuous for each
j\geq 1
;

(iii)

has uniformly integrable
m
-bounds
(resp.,
uniformly integrable
l
-bounds
) if
\{m_{j}^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}
(resp.,
\{l_{j}^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}
) is uniformly integrable for each
j\geq 1
.

Since in this work we are not going to require the property of uniform integrability, we will only prove that the properties of boundedness and equicontinuity of the
m
-bounds/
l
-bounds of a set
E\subset\mathfrak{M_{p}}
are inherited by its closure
\overline{E}=\cls_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\mathcal{T})}(E)
for
\mathcal{T}\in\{\sigma_{\Theta},\sigma_{D}\}
, but the same happens with the property of uniform integrability. Specifically,
\mathrm{cls}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\mathcal{T})}(E)
represents the closure in
(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\mathcal{T})
of the subset of parametric b-measures
E
with the induced topology. We recall the following result for the vague topology
\widetilde{\sigma}
in
\mathcal{M}^{+}
, which will be used in the proof.

Proposition 3.9
.

(
[
7
, Theorem 15.7.5, p.170]
)
A subset
M_{0}\subset\mathcal{M}^{+}
is relatively compact in the vague topology
\widetilde{\sigma}
if and only if
\sup_{\mu\in M_{0}}\mu(B)<\infty
for every bounded Borel set
B\in\mathcal{B}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be one of the topologies
\{\sigma_{\Theta},\sigma_{D}\}
defined above.

(i)

If a subset
E\subset\mathfrak{M_{p}}
has bounded
m
-bounds (resp., bounded
l
-bounds), then
\overline{E}=\cls_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\mathcal{T})}(E)
also has bounded
m
-bounds (resp., bounded
l
-bounds).

(ii)

If a subset
E\subset\mathfrak{M_{p}}
has equicontinuous
m
-bounds (resp., equicontinuous
l
-bounds), then
\overline{E}=\cls_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\mathcal{T})}(E)
also has equicontinuous
m
-bounds (resp., equicontinuous
l
-bounds).

Proof.

Since
\sigma_{D}\leq\sigma_{\Theta}
, it is enough to prove the result for
\sigma_{D}
.

(i)
Let
E=\{\nu_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}\subset\mathfrak{M_{p}}
be a subset of parametric b-measures with bounded
m
-bounds
\{m_{j}^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
for each
j\geq 1
. Given
\mu\in\cls_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{D})}(E)
, there is a sequence
\{\nu_{\alpha_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset E
such that
\mu=\lim_{k\to\infty}\nu_{\alpha_{k}}
in
\sigma_{D}
, i.e., for every
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
and every
y\in D
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}|(\nu_{\alpha_{k}})_{y}(I)-\mu_{y}(I)|=0
. Since in particular
\mu\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
, there exist
\{m_{j}^{\mu},l_{j}^{\mu}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}

m
-bounds,
l
-bounds, respectively, of
\mu
on
B_{j}
. The boundedness of
m_{j}^{\mu}
cannot be studied directly. Thus, we build new
m
-bounds for
\mu
by using the
m
-bounds of the sequence
\{\nu_{\alpha_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset E
.

Fixed
j\geq 1
, from Proposition
3.9
, taking a subsequence if necessary,
\{m_{j}^{\alpha_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
converges to some
m_{j}
in
(\mathcal{M}^{+},\widetilde{\sigma})
. We claim that the continuous part of
m_{j}
is an
m
-bound for
\mu
on
B_{j}
. First, if
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
y\in B_{j}\cap D
,

|\mu_{y}(I)|=\lim_{k\to\infty}|(\nu_{\alpha_{k}})_{y}(I)|\quad\mbox{and}\quad|(\nu_{\alpha_{k}})_{y}(I)|\leq|(\nu_{\alpha_{k}})_{y}|(I)\leq m_{j}^{\alpha_{k}}(I)\,.

(3.2)

By the regularity of the measures
m_{j}^{\alpha_{k}}
, and the vague convergence, respectively,

\displaystyle m_{j}^{\alpha_{k}}(I)=\inf\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(s)\,dm_{j}^{\alpha_{k}}\,\Big|\,\,\phi\in C_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R})\,,\;\phi|_{I}\equiv 1\bigg\}\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(s)\,dm_{j}^{\alpha_{k}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(s)\,dm_{j}\;\text{ for each }\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})\,.

Since also
m_{j}
is a regular measure, we can deduce from (
3.2
) and the previous formulas that
|\mu_{y}(I)|\leq m_{j}(I)
. We note that we can remove the purely discontinuous part, if any, from the measure
m_{j}
. That is, if
m_{j}=m_{j,c}+m_{j,pd}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{M}^{+}_{pd}
, then
|\mu_{y}(I)|\leq m_{j,c}(I)
for every
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
y\in B_{j}\cap D
, because
\mu_{y}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
and
m_{j,pd}
is supported on a countable set
\mathcal{N}
with
\mu_{y}(I\cap\mathcal{N})=0
.

Now, approximating a compact interval
J
by intervals with rational endpoints and using the regularity of
\mu_{y}
we deduce that

|\mu_{y}(J)|\leq m_{j,c}(J)
. Notice that since
\mu_{y}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
and
m_{j,c}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
do not have purely discontinuous part, the inequality follows also for each open interval
J
. Since any bounded open set
U
is a disjoint countable union of bounded open subintervals, the inequality holds for
U
.
Again, by the regularity property, the inequality
|\mu_{y}(A)|\leq m_{j,c}(A)
follows for every bounded Borel set
A
and each
y\in B_{j}\cap D
. And, if
y\in B_{j}\setminus D
, then
|\mu_{y}(A)-\mu_{x}(A)|\leq|\mu_{y}-\mu_{x}|(A)\leq l_{j}^{\mu}(A)\,\omega_{j}(|y-x|)
for all
A\in\mathcal{B}
, so we only need to approximate
y
by points
x\in B_{j}\cap D
to extend the result to all
y\in B_{j}
. Finally, by the definition of
|\mu_{y}|
in (
2.1
) and since
m_{j,c}
is a measure, it follows that
|\mu_{y}|(A)\leq m_{j,c}(A)
for all
A\in\mathcal{B}
and
y\in B_{j}
, as claimed.

To finish, if
m_{j}^{\alpha}[t,t+1]\leq c_{j}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in\Gamma
, for the
m
-bound
m_{j,c}
on
B_{j}
, taking for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
a map
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, which depends on
t
and satisfies
0\leq\phi(s)\leq 1
for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\phi(s)=1
for
s\in[t,t+1]
and
\phi(s)=0
for
s\in(-\infty,t-1]\cup[t+2,\infty)
, and noting that
m_{j,c}\leq m_{j}
, we deduce that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

m_{j,c}[t,t+1]\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(s)\,dm_{j}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(s)\,dm_{j}^{\alpha_{k}}\leq\sup_{\alpha\in\Gamma}m_{j}^{\alpha}[t-1,t+2]<3\,c_{j}\,.

(3.3)

Since the same arguments apply to each
\mu\in\overline{E}
, and each
j\geq 1
, we can conclude that
\overline{E}=\cls_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{D})}(E)
has bounded
m
-bounds, as claimed. We omit the proof in the case of bounded
l
-bounds, because it is analogous.

(ii) We maintain the notation used in (i) and assume that for each
j\geq 1
, the set of
m
-bounds
\{m_{j}^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
is equicontinuous, i.e., given
\varepsilon>0
there is a
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that
m_{j}^{\alpha}[s,t]<\varepsilon
whenever
0\leq t-s<\delta(\varepsilon)
, for all
\alpha\in\Gamma
. Since equicontinuity implies boundedness, for each
\mu\in\overline{E}
we can consider the
m
-bound
m_{j,c}
built in (i). Then, similar arguments as those in (
3.3
) applied to
m_{j,c}[s,t]
allow us to conclude that
m_{j,c}[s,t]<3\,\varepsilon
whenever
0\leq t-s<\delta(\varepsilon)
. Thus,
\overline{E}
has equicontinuous
m
-bounds. The case of equicontinuous
l
-bounds
is also omitted by similarity of reasoning. The proof is finished.
∎

Next, we want to show the equivalence of the introduced topologies under the existence of bounded
l
-bounds, an assumption that is satisfied in many applications.
Before that, we give a technical result.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
E\subset\mathfrak{M_{p}}
have bounded
l
-bounds
\{\{l_{j}^{\nu}\}_{j\geq 1}\mid\nu\in E\}
and let
\Theta
be a suitable set of moduli of continuity as in
Definition
3.1
. Fixed
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]

(q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q})
and an integer
j\geq 1
, consider the set
\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
in (
3.1
). Then, given
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that for each tagged
\delta(\varepsilon)
-partition
\Delta^{\delta(\varepsilon)}=\{(\tau_{i},[t_{i},t_{i+1}])\mid i=1,\ldots,k-1\}
of
I
, for all
y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
and
\nu\in E
it holds:

\left|\int_{I}d\nu_{y(s)}-S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}\big(\Delta^{\delta(\varepsilon)}\big)\Bigg|=\Bigg|\int_{I}d\nu_{y(s)}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}d\nu_{y(\tau_{i})}\right|<\varepsilon\,.

Proof.

First of all, we affirm that the statement of Lemma
2.10
holds uniformly for all
y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
and
\nu\in E
. Note that the functions
y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
are uniformly bounded by
j
and uniformly equicontinuous and, moreover, we can take an
M>0
such that
l_{j}^{\nu}(I)\leq M
for all
\nu\in E
because
E
has bounded
l
-bounds.

Then, arguing as in the proof of Proposition
2.11
it is easy to prove that given
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
such that
\big|S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta_{1}^{\delta})-S_{\nu}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta_{2}^{\delta})\big|<\varepsilon

for all tagged
\delta
-partitions
\Delta_{1}^{\delta}
and
\Delta_{2}^{\delta}
of
I
, uniformly for
y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
and
\nu\in E
. That is, the Riemann sums satisfy the Cauchy property uniformly for all
y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
and
\nu\in E
, and this is equivalent to what we wanted to prove. The proof is finished.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
E
be a subset of
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
with bounded
l
-bounds. Consider a dense and countable subset
D
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a suitable set of moduli of continuity
\Theta
as in
Definition
3.1
. Then,

(E,\sigma_{D})=(E,\sigma_{\Theta})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{cls}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{D})}(E)=\mathrm{cls}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{\Theta})}(E)\,.

Proof.

As noted in Remark
3.4
,
\sigma_{D}\leq\sigma_{\Theta}
. Thus, it suffices to prove that, if a sequence
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset E
converges to
\nu_{0}\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
in the topology
\sigma_{D}
, that is,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\,\int_{I}d(\nu_{n})_{y}-\int_{I}d(\nu_{0})_{y}\,\right|=0\quad\text{for all }y\in D\,,\,I=[q_{1},q_{2}]\,\,(q_{1},\,q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q})\,,

(3.4)

then, it also converges to
\nu_{0}
in
\sigma_{\Theta}
, i.e., for all
j\geq 1
and
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]\,(q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q})
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}}\left|\,\int_{I}d(\nu_{n})_{y(s)}-\int_{I}d(\nu_{0})_{y(s)}\,\right|=0\,.

The main ideas come from the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 2.33]
for Carathéodory functions, but the adaptation to the case of parametric b-measures requires an explanation of some nontrivial technical details. Fix a compact interval
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
with
q_{1}
,
q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
j\geq 1
, and denote by
l_{j}^{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
an
l
-bound of
\nu_{n}
on
B_{j}
, for each
n\geq 0
. By Proposition
3.10
, the set
\mathrm{cls}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{D})}(E)
has bounded
l
-bounds. Then, there is a constant
c>0
such that
l_{j}^{n}(I)\leq c
for each
n\geq 0
.

Now, fix
\varepsilon>0
and take
\rho>0
so that
\omega_{j}(\rho)<\varepsilon/(6\,c)
. Since
B_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is compact, and
D
is dense in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there are
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{\ell_{0}}\in B_{j}\cap D
such that
B_{j}\subset\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}\mathring{B}_{\rho}(y_{\ell})
, where
\mathring{B}_{\rho}(y)
denotes the open ball of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
of radius
\rho
centered at
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Subordinate to the former open cover, there is a continuous partition of unity, that is, there exist continuous functions
\phi_{\ell}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\mapsto[0,1]
for
\ell=1,\ldots,\ell_{0}
, which in fact can be taken to be of class
C^{1}
, such that

\supp(\phi_{\ell})\subset\mathring{B}_{\rho}(y_{\ell})\,\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}\phi_{\ell}(y)=1\quad\forall\,y\in B_{j}\,.

(3.5)

From Proposition
2.5
, the maps
\nu_{n}^{*}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
,
y\mapsto(\nu_{n}^{*})_{y}
given by

(\nu_{n}^{*})_{y}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}\phi_{\ell}(y)(\nu_{n})_{y_{\ell}}\quad\text{ for each }y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and }n\geq 0

permit us to define a family of parametric b-measures
\{\nu_{n}^{*}\mid n\geq 0\}
with respect to the trivial family of moduli of continuity given by the identity map.

Now, for each
y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
, and each
n\geq 1
, we consider a tagged
\delta
-partition of
I
,
\Delta^{\delta}=\{(\tau_{i},[t_{i},t_{i+1}])\mid i=1,\ldots,k-1\}
with rational points
t_{i}
, for convenience, and apply the triangle inequality to write

\displaystyle\left|\,\int_{I}d(\nu_{n})_{y(s)}-\int_{I}d(\nu_{0})_{y(s)}\,\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\,\int_{I}d(\nu_{n})_{y(s)}-S_{\nu_{n}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})\right|+\left|S_{\nu_{n}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})-S_{\nu_{n}^{*}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})\right|

\displaystyle+\left|S_{\nu_{n}^{*}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})-S_{\nu_{0}^{*}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})\right|+\left|S_{\nu_{0}^{*}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})-S_{\nu_{0}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})\right|

\displaystyle+\left|S_{\nu_{0}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})-\int_{I}d(\nu_{0})_{y(s)}\right|.

(3.6)

From Lemma
3.11
applied to
\mathrm{cls}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{D})}(E)
, associated with the fixed
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that for each tagged
\delta
-partition
\Delta^{\delta}
of
I
,

a_{n}:=\left|\,\int_{I}d(\nu_{n})_{y(s)}-S_{\nu_{n}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{6}\,\quad\hbox{for all }n\geq 0\;\,\hbox{and }y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}\,.

This applies to the first and last terms in the sum in (
3
). For the second and fourth terms, since for every
y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
,
y(\tau_{i})\in B_{j}
for all
i=1,\ldots,k-1
, applying (
3.5
) and Proposition
2.12
, and denoting
I_{i}=[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
, we write for each
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle b_{n}:=\left|S_{\nu_{n}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})-S_{\nu^{*}_{n}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\,\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{I_{i}}d(\nu_{n})_{y(\tau_{i})}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{I_{i}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}\phi_{\ell}(y(\tau_{i}))\,d(\nu_{n})_{y_{\ell}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\,\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{I_{i}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}\phi_{\ell}(y(\tau_{i}))\left(d(\nu_{n})_{y(\tau_{i})}-d(\nu_{n})_{y_{\ell}}\right)\right|.

Therefore, from the bounded
l
-bounds of
\mathrm{cls}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{D})}(E)
and again using (
3.5
), we obtain that for each
n\geq 0
and
y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
,

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}\phi_{\ell}(y(\tau_{i}))\left|(\nu_{n})_{y(\tau_{i})}-(\nu_{n})_{y_{\ell}}\right|(I_{i})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}\phi_{\ell}(y(\tau_{i}))\,\omega_{j}(|y(\tau_{i})-y_{\ell}|)\right]l_{j}^{n}(I_{i})\leq\omega_{j}(\rho)\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}l_{j}^{n}(I_{i})<\frac{\varepsilon}{6\,c}\,l_{j}^{n}(I)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{6}\,.

Finally, for the third term in the sum in (
3
) we have

c_{n}:=\left|S_{\nu_{n}^{*}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})-S_{\nu_{0}^{*}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta})\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}\phi_{\ell}(y(\tau_{i}))\left|\int_{I_{i}}\left[d(\nu_{n})_{y_{\ell}}-d(\nu_{0})_{y_{\ell}}\right]\right|\right]\,.

Since
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset E
converges to
\nu_{0}
in
\sigma_{D}
, that is, (
3.4
) holds, there is an
n_{0}
such that for each
n\geq n_{0}
, each
\ell=1,\ldots,\ell_{0}
and each
i=1,\ldots,k-1
,

\left|\int_{I_{i}}\left[d(\nu_{n})_{y_{\ell}}-d(\nu_{0})_{y_{\ell}}\right]\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3\,(k-1)}\,.

Then, using (
3.5
) once more,
c_{n}<\varepsilon/3
for all
y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}^{I}_{j}
and
n\geq n_{0}
. Collecting the previous facts, we can conclude that
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\nu_{0}
in
\sigma_{\Theta}
, as we wanted. The proof is finished.
∎

We conclude this section with a crucial result of compactness for sets of parametric b-measures with equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
E\subset\mathfrak{M_{p}}
have equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds and let
\mathcal{T}\in\{\sigma_{D},\sigma_{\Theta}\}
. Then,
\mathrm{cls}_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\mathcal{T})}(E)
is compact.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.12
, with the hypothesis of bounded
l
-bounds the topologies
\sigma_{D}
and
\sigma_{\Theta}
are equivalent on
E
, so that it suffices to prove the result for the topology
\sigma_{D}
, for a countable and dense subset
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Let us prove that, given a sequence
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset E
there exists a subsequence
\{\nu_{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
which converges in the topology
\sigma_{D}
to some
\nu\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
.
For each
n\geq 1
and
j\geq 1
, respectively denote by
m_{j}^{n}
and
l_{j}^{n}
an
m
-bound and an
l
-bound of
\nu_{n}
on
B_{j}
. Now, for each
n\geq 1
, define the map

F_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\,,\quad(t,y)\mapsto F_{n}(t,y):=\int_{0}^{t}d(\nu_{n})_{y}\,.

With this definition, for
n\geq 1
and for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
(\nu_{n})_{y}[t_{1},t_{2}]=F_{n}(t_{2},y)-F_{n}(t_{1},y)
for each real interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
and

F_{n}(0,y)=0
because
(\nu_{n})_{y}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
.

Let us see that the family
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\mid n\geq 1\}
is uniformly equicontinuous on the compact set
[-j,j]\times B_{j}
, for each
j\geq 1
. Take
t_{1},t_{2}\in[-j,j]
with
t_{1}<t_{2}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in B_{j}
and write for
n\geq 1
, assuming that
t_{1}>0
without loss of generality,

\displaystyle|

\displaystyle F_{n}(t_{2},y_{2})-F_{n}(t_{1},y_{1})|=\left|\int_{0}^{t_{2}}d(\nu_{n})_{y_{2}}-\int_{0}^{t_{1}}d(\nu_{n})_{y_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\;\leq\left|\int_{0}^{t_{2}}d(\nu_{n})_{y_{2}}-\int_{0}^{t_{1}}d(\nu_{n})_{y_{2}}\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{t_{1}}d(\nu_{n})_{y_{2}}-\int_{0}^{t_{1}}d(\nu_{n})_{y_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\;\leq|(\nu_{n})_{y_{2}}|[t_{1},t_{2}]+|(\nu_{n})_{y_{2}}-(\nu_{n})_{y_{1}}|[0,t_{1}]\leq m_{j}^{n}[t_{1},t_{2}]+l_{j}^{n}[-j,j]\,\omega_{j}(|y_{2}-y_{1}|)\,.

From the hypotheses of equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds for the set
E
, the uniform equicontinuity follows directly. Furthermore, the family

\mathcal{F}
is bounded at every fixed
(t,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
: take
j\geq 1
such that
y\in B_{j}
, and assuming that
t>0
without loss of generality,
|F_{n}(t,y)|\leq|(\nu_{n})_{y}|[0,t]\leq m_{j}^{n}[0,t]
for all
n\geq 1
, and the
m
-bounds are, in particular, bounded (see Remark
3.7
).

Since
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
is separable, by the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem and a diagonalization process there exists a subsequence
\{F_{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
that is uniformly convergent on every compact subset of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence, there is a continuous function
F\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F_{n_{k}}\to F
as
k\to\infty
uniformly on compact sets.

In order to build a parametric b-measure from the continuous map
F(t,y)
, let us prove that for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
fixed, the map
F(\cdot,y)
is of bounded variation on every compact set
[a,b]
. First, because
E
has bounded
m
-bounds, it is easy to check that the family
\{F_{n}(\cdot,y)\mid n\geq 1\}
is uniformly of bounded variation on
[a,b]
. To see it, take
j\geq 1
such that
y\in B_{j}
and
c_{0}>0
such that

m_{j}^{n}[a,b]\leq c_{0}
for every
n\geq 1
. Then, given any integer
\ell\geq 1
and any partition
a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{\ell}=b
,

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\,|F_{n}(t_{i},y)-F_{n}(t_{i-1},y)|&=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|(\nu_{n})_{y}[t_{i-1},t_{i}]|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|(\nu_{n})_{y}|[t_{i-1},t_{i}]\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}m_{j}^{n}[t_{i-1},t_{i}]=m_{j}^{n}[a,b]\leq c_{0}\,\end{split}

(3.7)

for all
n\geq 1
. Second, for the map
F
, for each
\ell\geq 1
we can take
k=k(\ell)
big enough so that
|F(t,y)-F_{n_{k}}(t,y)|<1/\ell
for all
t\in[a,b]
, and then
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|F(t_{i},y)-F_{n_{k}}(t_{i},y)|\leq 1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|F_{n_{k}}(t_{i-1},y)-F(t_{i-1},y)|\leq 1
. Thus, for any
\ell\geq 1
and any partition of
[a,b]
, applying the triangle inequality and (
3.7
) for the map
F_{n_{k}}
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|F(t_{i},y)-F(t_{i-1},y)|\leq 2+c_{0}\,.

As a consequence,
F(\cdot,y)
is of bounded variation on every compact interval
[a,b]
.

Therefore, we can consider
\nu_{y}
the Lebesgue-Stieltjes real measure on
[a,b]
associated with
F(\cdot,y)
, which is uniquely determined and satisfies that
\nu_{y}[t_{1},t_{2}]=F(t_{2},y)-F(t_{1},y)
for intervals in
[a,b]
, by construction. Thus, by considering growing intervals
[-j,j]
one can extend
\nu_{y}
to the ring of bounded Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}
. Note that, since
F(\cdot,y)
is continuous,
\nu_{y}(\{t\})=0
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and we can affirm that
\nu_{y}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
. Furthermore,
\nu\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
defined in this way is the limit of
\nu_{n_{k}}
in the topology
\sigma_{D}
. In fact, for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and every interval
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
,

\left|\int_{I}d(\nu_{n_{k}})_{y}-\int_{I}d\nu_{y}\right|=\big|F_{n_{k}}(q_{2},y)-F_{n_{k}}(q_{1},y)-(F(q_{2},y)-F(q_{1},y))\big|\to 0

(3.8)

as
k\to\infty
. That is,
\nu_{y}(I)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\nu_{n_{k}})_{y}(I)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and every interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
. To complete the proof, it remains to build
m
-bounds and
l
-bounds for
\nu
. As in the proof of Proposition
3.10
, we can do this by using the
m
-bounds and
l
-bounds of
\nu_{n_{k}}
. Namely, fixed
j\geq 1
, from Proposition
3.9
, taking a subsequence if necessary,

\{m_{j}^{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
converges to some
m_{j}
and
\{l_{j}^{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
converges to some
l_{j}
in
(\mathcal{M}^{+},\widetilde{\sigma})
. Since (
3.8
) holds for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and every interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, it is now easier than in the mentioned proof to check that the continuous part of
m_{j}
(resp., of
l_{j}
) is an
m
-bound (resp., an
l
-bound) of
\nu
on
B_{j}
. Thus,
\nu\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
and the proof is finished.
∎

4.
Continuity of time translations and the hull of a parametric b-measure

As in the previous section, we consider the set
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
of parametric b-measures on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with respect to a fixed family of moduli of continuity
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
(see Definition
2.4
). In order to build the hull of a parametric b-measure, we define the translation of a parametric b-measure by a number
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Note that, given a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
(resp., a b-measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})
) and a real number
t
,
\mu{\cdot}t
defined by
\mu{\cdot}t(A):=\mu(A+t)
for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
(resp.,
A\in\mathcal{B}
) is a measure (resp., a b-measure).

Lemma 4.1
.

Given a parametric b-measure
\nu\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
with
m
-bounds
\{m_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
and
l
-bounds

\{l_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
, and a real number
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the map
\nu{\cdot}t\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B}),\;y\mapsto(\nu{\cdot}t)_{y}:=\nu_{y}{\cdot}t
. Then,
\nu{\cdot}t
is a parametric b-measure with
m
-bounds
\{m_{j}{\cdot}t\}_{j\geq 1}
and
l
-bounds
\{l_{j}{\cdot}t\}_{j\geq 1}
.

Proof.

Fixed an integer
j\geq 1
, from the definition of the total variation of a b-measure in (
2.1
), we deduce that for all
A\in\mathcal{B}
and
y,y_{1},y_{2}\in B_{j}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\displaystyle|(\nu{\cdot}t)_{y}|(A)

\displaystyle=|\nu_{y}|(A+t)\leq m_{j}(A+t)=m_{j}{\cdot}t(A)\,,

\displaystyle|(\nu{\cdot}t)_{y_{1}}-(\nu{\cdot}t)_{y_{2}}|(A)

\displaystyle=|\nu_{y_{1}}-\nu_{y_{2}}|(A+t)\leq l_{j}(A+t)\,\omega_{j}(|y_{1}-y_{2}|)

\displaystyle=l_{j}{\cdot}t(A)\,\omega_{j}(|y_{1}-y_{2}|)\,.

With this, the proof is finished.
∎

We give a formula for the integration of a translated parametric b-measure along a continuous map
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
to be used in the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\nu\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
be a parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
a real number, and
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
a continuous map. Then,

\int_{a}^{b}d(\nu{\cdot}t)_{y(s)}=\int_{a+t}^{b+t}d\nu_{y(s-t)}\,.

Proof.

It is enough to notice that given

\Delta^{\delta}=\{(\tau_{i},[t_{i},t_{i+1}])\mid i=1,\ldots,k-1\}
any
\delta
-partition of
[a,b]
,
\Delta^{\delta}_{t}=\{(\tau_{i}+t,[t_{i}+t,t_{i+1}+t])\mid i=1,\ldots,k-1\}
is a
\delta
-partition of
[a+t,b+t]
and the corresponding sums

S_{\nu{\cdot}t}^{y(\cdot)}\big(\Delta^{\delta}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}d(\nu{\cdot}t)_{y(\tau_{i})}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\nu}^{y(\,\cdot\,-t)}\big(\Delta_{t}^{\delta}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\int_{t_{i}+t}^{t_{i+1}+t}d\nu_{y(\tau_{i}+t-t)}

coincide.
∎

Next, we prove the continuity of time translations on sets with equicontinuous
m
-bounds. This result is the counterpart of Theorem 3.1 in
[
14
]
for the translation of Carathéodory functions.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Theta
be a suitable set of moduli of continuity as in
Definition
3.1
,
E
be a subset of
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
with equicontinuous
m
-bounds and
\overline{E}=\cls_{(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{\Theta})}(E)
. Then, the map

\mathbb{R}\times\overline{E}\to\mathfrak{M_{p}}\,,\qquad(t,\nu)\mapsto\nu{\cdot}t\,

is continuous.

Proof.

Notice that the map is well defined thanks to Lemma
4.1
. Let
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\overline{E}
be a sequence that converges to
\nu_{0}
in
(\mathfrak{M_{p}},\sigma_{\Theta})
and
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\to t_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}
. We claim that for each
I=[q_{1},q_{2}]
,
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and each integer
j\geq 1
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}}\left|\,\int_{I}d(\nu_{n}{\cdot}{t_{n}})_{y(s)}-\int_{I}d(\nu_{0}{\cdot}{t_{0}})_{y(s)}\,\right|=0\,,

where
\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}
is the set in (
3.1
).

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Since
E
has equicontinuous
m
-bounds, by Proposition
3.10
the closure
\overline{E}
also has equicontinuous
m
-bounds, and there is a
\delta>0
such that for every
\mu\in\overline{E}
we have
m_{j}^{\mu}[s_{1},s_{2}]<\varepsilon/6
whenever
0\leq s_{2}-s_{1}<\delta\,
. Now, take
p_{1}
,
p_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
0<p_{1}-q_{1}-t_{0}<\delta
and
0<q_{2}+t_{0}-p_{2}<\delta
. Since
t_{n}\to t_{0}
as
n\uparrow\infty
we can find an integer
n_{0}
such that for each
n\geq n_{0}
,

[p_{1},p_{2}]\subset[q_{1}+t_{n},q_{2}+t_{n}]\,,\;\;p_{1}-q_{1}-t_{n}<\delta\;\text{ and }\;q_{2}+t_{n}-p_{2}<\delta\,.

(4.1)

From Lemma
4.2
we can write

D_{n}:=\sup_{y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}}\left|\int_{I}d(\nu_{n}{\cdot}{t_{n}})_{y(s)}-\int_{I}d(\nu_{0}{\cdot}{t_{0}})_{y(s)}\right|\\
=\sup_{y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}}\left|\int_{q_{1}+t_{n}}^{q_{2}+t_{n}}d(\nu_{n})_{y(s-t_{n})}-\int_{q_{1}+t_{0}}^{q_{2}+t_{0}}d(\nu_{0})_{y(s-t_{0})}\right|\,.

Then, decomposing the integrals using (
4.1
), applying Lemma
2.13
and the equicontinuity of the
m
-bounds we get, for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle D_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}}\left|\int_{p_{1}}^{p_{2}}\left[d(\nu_{n})_{y(s-t_{n})}-d(\nu_{0})_{y(s-t_{0})}\right]\right|+\frac{4\,\varepsilon}{6}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}}\left|\int_{p_{1}}^{p_{2}}\left[d(\nu_{n})_{y(s-t_{n})}-d(\nu_{0})_{y(s-t_{n})}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\quad\;+\sup_{y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{I}}\left|\int_{p_{1}}^{p_{2}}\left[d(\nu_{0})_{y(s-t_{n})}-d(\nu_{0})_{y(s-t_{0})}\right]\right|+\frac{2\,\varepsilon}{3}=:P_{n}+R_{n}+\frac{2\,\varepsilon}{3}\,.

Next, we take an interval

with rational endpoints such that

and, up to a suitable extension of

by constants to

belong to

. Therefore, since

converges to
\nu_{0}
in
\sigma_{\Theta}
, Lemma
3.5
yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{n}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{K}_{j}^{J}}\left|\,\int_{p_{1}}^{p_{2}}d(\nu_{n})_{y(s)}-\int_{p_{1}}^{p_{2}}d(\nu_{0})_{y(s)}\,\right|=0\,.

Finally, Lemma
2.14
implies that
\lim_{n\to\infty}R_{n}=0
, which finishes the proof.
∎

5.
The skew-product flow induced by a class of generalized ODEs given by
N
-dim parametric b-measures on
\mathbb{R}^{N}

This section is devoted to the construction of a continuous skew-product flow induced by the solutions of the equations in the hull of a generalized ODE
y^{\prime}(t)=\bar{\nu}_{y(t)}
given by an
N
-dim parametric b-measure
\bar{\nu}=(\nu^{1},\ldots,\nu^{N})
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with common
m
-bounds
\{m_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
and
l
-bounds
\{l_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
for
1\leq i\leq N
:

(i)

|\nu^{i}_{y}|(A)\leq m_{j}(A)
for each subset
A\in\mathcal{B}
and each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|y|\leq j
;

(ii)

|\nu^{i}_{y_{1}}-\nu^{i}_{y_{2}}|(A)\leq l_{j}(A)\,|y_{1}-y_{2}|
for each subset
A\in\mathcal{B}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|y_{1}|,|y_{2}|\leq j
.

Note that, in this section, the family of moduli of continuity
\{\omega_{j}={\rm id}\}_{j\geq 1}
is necessarily related to the usual Lipschitz condition. Let us begin by defining the concept of a solution of this kind of equation.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\bar{\nu}
be an
N
-dim parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with components
\bar{\nu}=(\nu^{1},\ldots,\nu^{N})
(
\nu^{i}\in\mathfrak{M_{p}}
for
1\leq i\leq N
) and let
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. A continuous function
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
t_{0}\in[a,b]
, is said to be a
solution of the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}y^{\prime}(t)=\bar{\nu}_{y(t)}\\
y(t_{0})=y_{0}\end{cases}

(5.1)

in
[a,b]
, if it is a solution of the integral equation
y(t)=y_{0}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\bar{\nu}_{y(s)}
for each
t\in[a,b]
, that is,

y_{i}(t)=y_{0,i}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\nu^{i}_{y(s)}
for all
t\in[a,b]
and for each
i=1,\ldots,N
.

Remark 5.2
.

Note that, by the existence of
m
-bounds and Lemma
2.13
, a solution is always a function of bounded variation on
[a,b]
.

Example 5.3
.

A nontrivial academic example is given by the famous Cantor function
y(t)
,
t\in[0,1]
, which is the solution of the scalar generalized ODE
y^{\prime}=\mu
with initial condition
y(0)=0
, for the singular continuous Borel measure
\mu
on
[0,1]
determined by the formula
\mu[0,t]=y(t)
,
t\in[0,1]
(see, e.g., Rudin
[
19
]
). The graph of the Cantor function, known as the Devil’s staircase, is a continuous function of bounded variation which, however, is not absolutely continuous. Recall that, by definition, the solutions of Carathéodory ODEs are within the class of absolutely continuous functions.

Standard techniques of ordinary differential equations allow us to prove the theorem on local existence and uniqueness of solutions.

Theorem 5.4
.

Given an
N
-dim parametric b-measure
\bar{\nu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
m
-bounds
\{m_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
and
l
-bounds
\{l_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
and
(t_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there is a
\delta>0
such that the Cauchy problem (
5.1
) has a unique solution in
[t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}+\delta]
.

Proof.

We consider the case
t\geq t_{0}
, the other case being analogous. Since the
m
-bounds and the
l
-bounds belong to
\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
, we can choose a
j\geq 2\,|y_{0}|\sqrt{N}
and a
\delta>0
such that
m_{j}[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]\leq j/(2\sqrt{N})
and
l_{j}[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]\leq 1/(2\sqrt{N})
. Let us consider the closed ball in
C([t_{0},t_{0}+\delta],\mathbb{R}^{N})
of radius
j
,

Y_{j}=\{y(\cdot)\in C([t_{0},t_{0}+\delta],\mathbb{R}^{N})\mid|y(t)|\leq j\;\text{ for all }t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]\}\,,

which is a normed space for the sup-norm. Next, for each
y(\cdot)\in Y_{j}
we define
Ty
by

Ty(t)=y_{0}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\bar{\nu}_{y(s)}\,,\;\;t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]\,,

and we claim that
T
is a contractive map from
Y_{j}
to
Y_{j}
. First notice that
Ty
belongs to
C([t_{0},t_{0}+\delta],\mathbb{R}^{N})
for each
y(\cdot)\in Y_{j}
because, for each component
(Ty)_{i}
, from Lemma
2.13
applied to the component
\nu^{i}
, we can bound

\left|(Ty)_{i}(t_{2})-(Ty)_{i}(t_{1})\right|=\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{2}}d\nu^{i}_{y(s)}-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}d\nu^{i}_{y(s)}\right|=\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}d\nu^{i}_{y(s)}\right|\leq m_{j}[t_{1},t_{2}]

for each
t_{0}\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq t_{0}+\delta
, and
m_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
. Moreover, for every
t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+\delta
,

|(Ty)_{i}(t)|\leq|y_{0,i}|+\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\nu^{i}_{y(s)}\right|\leq|y_{0,i}|+m_{j}[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]\leq\frac{j}{2\sqrt{N}}+\frac{j}{2\sqrt{N}}=\frac{j}{\sqrt{N}}\,.

From this, it follows that
Ty\in Y_{j}
and
T
is well-defined.
If
y(\cdot),z(\cdot)\in Y_{j}
, again from Lemma
2.13
we deduce that for each component
1\leq i\leq N
,

\left|(Ty)_{i}(t)-(Tz)_{i}(t)\right|=\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\nu^{i}_{y(s)}-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\nu^{i}_{z(s)}\right|\leq l_{j}[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]\,\|y-z\|_{[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]}\,,

for each
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
. Since
l_{j}[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]\leq 1/(2\sqrt{N})
, we obtain
\|{Ty-Tz}\|_{[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]}\leq(1/2)\|y-z\|_{[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]}
. Thus, T is contractive and has a unique fixed point in
Y_{j}
which is a solution of the Cauchy problem (
5.1
) in
[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
.

To prove the uniqueness in
C([t_{0},t_{0}+\delta],\mathbb{R}^{N})
, if there were two solutions
y_{1}(\cdot)
and
y_{2}(\cdot)
of (
5.1
), define

t_{1}=\sup\{t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]\mid y_{1}(s)=y_{2}(s)\text{ for }t_{0}\leq s\leq t\}
and let
\widetilde{y}_{0}=y_{1}(t_{1})=y_{2}(t_{1})
.
If
t_{1}<t_{0}+\delta
, by the previous reasoning, the problem
y^{\prime}(t)=\bar{\nu}_{y(t)}
,
y(t_{1})=\widetilde{y}_{0}
has a unique solution in a set

\widetilde{Y}=\{y(\cdot)\in C([t_{1},t_{1}+\varepsilon],\mathbb{R}^{N})\mid|y(t)|\leq k\;\text{ for all }t\in[t_{1},t_{1}+\varepsilon]\}

for large enough
k
and small enough
\varepsilon>0
, contradicting the definition of
t_{1}
.
∎

5.1.
Application to precompact families of Carathéodory ODEs

The previous skew-product framework allows us to apply topological dynamics techniques to nonautonomous ordinary differential equations
y^{\prime}=g(y,t)
with Carathéodory conditions as those imposed in Artstein
[
1
]
, that is, assuming the existence of equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds for the map
g
. As explained in
[
1
]
, the limiting equations (i.e., the limits of a family of translated equations under a certain topology) may not be ODEs, that is, the hull is a precompact family but not necessarily complete. In order to complete this space, a precise class of Kurzweil equations was considered in
[
1
]
. In our novel approach, the ODE and its time-translated equations are reformulated as generalized ODEs given by
N
-dim parametric b-measures on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Next, we explain the procedure in detail.

Consider an ODE
y^{\prime}=g(y,t)
where
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is (Lebesgue) measurable in
t
for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, it is continuous in
y
for almost every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
there exist two nonnegative and locally integrable maps defined on
\mathbb{R}
,
\widetilde{m}_{K},\widetilde{l}_{K}\in L^{1}_{loc}
(respectively called an
m
-bound and an
l
-bound of
g
on
K
), such that:

(i)

for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
|g(y,t)|\leq\widetilde{m}_{K}(t)
for all
y\in K
;

(ii)

for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
|g(y_{1},t)-g(y_{2},t)|\leq\widetilde{l}_{K}(t)\,|y_{1}-y_{2}|
for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in K
.

Note that the maps
\widetilde{m}_{K},\widetilde{l}_{K}\in L^{1}_{loc}
are also
m
-bounds/
l
-bounds of each component of
g=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{N})
. The aforementioned hypotheses in
[
1
]
, that is, equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds, read as follows: for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(i)
′

\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{m}_{K}(t)\,dt
is uniformly continuous in
s
, that is, given
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta=\delta_{K}(\varepsilon)>0
such that for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\int_{s}^{s+h}\widetilde{m}_{K}(t)\,dt<\varepsilon
if
|h|<\delta
;

(ii)
′

there exists a
c_{K}>0
such that
\int_{s}^{s+1}\widetilde{l}_{K}(t)\,dt\leq c_{K}
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let us associate an
N
-dim parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the map
g(y,t)
. Consider
\bar{\nu}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})^{N}
,
y\mapsto(\nu_{y}^{1},\ldots,\nu_{y}^{N})
given by

\nu_{y}^{i}(A):=\int_{A}g_{i}(y,t)\,dt\quad\hbox{for each }A\in\mathcal{B}\;\,(1\leq i\leq N)\,,

which is well-defined by (i) and the fact that
\widetilde{m}_{K}\in L^{1}_{loc}
. Notice that, using a differential notation, we can simply write

d\bar{\nu}_{y}=g(y,t)\,dt\quad\hbox{for each }\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(5.4)

Let us check that
\bar{\nu}
is an
N
-dim parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds.
For each integer
j\geq 1
, let
m_{j}
and
l_{j}
be the absolutely continuous regular Borel measures with density functions
\widetilde{m}_{B_{j}}(t)
and
\widetilde{l}_{B_{j}}(t)
, respectively. Then,
m_{j},l_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
and it is easy to check that

(1)

|\nu^{i}_{y}|(A)\leq m_{j}(A)
for each subset
A\in\mathcal{B}
and each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|y|\leq j
,

(2)

|\nu^{i}_{y_{1}}-\nu^{i}_{y_{2}}|(A)\leq l_{j}(A)\,|y_{1}-y_{2}|
for each subset
A\in\mathcal{B}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|y_{1}|,|y_{2}|\leq j
,

and this holds for each
1\leq i\leq N
. That is,
m_{j},l_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
are respectively an
m
-bound and an
l
-bound of
\nu^{i}
on the ball
B_{j}
according to Definition
2.4
. It is straightforward that the desired properties of equicontinuity of the set of translated measures
\{m_{j}{\cdot}t\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and boundedness of
\{l_{j}{\cdot}t\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
for each
j\geq 1
are inherited from (i)
′
and (ii)
′
above. Furthermore, with bounded
l
-bounds, we can build the hull of
\bar{\nu}
using the topology
\sigma_{D}
on
\mathfrak{M_{p}}
(see Theorem
3.12
), which is easier to handle than the topology
\sigma_{\Theta}
. A fundamental result is the following.

Proposition 5.11
.

Suppose that
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is measurable in
t
, continuous in
y
for almost every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
there are
m
-bounds,
l
-bounds,
\widetilde{m}_{K},\widetilde{l}_{K}\in L^{1}_{loc}
, respectively. Then,
y(t)
is a solution of the Carathéodory ODE
y^{\prime}=g(y,t)
if and only if it is a solution of the associated generalized ODE
y^{\prime}(t)=\bar{\nu}_{y(t)}
, for the parametric b-measure
\bar{\nu}
in (
5.4
).

Proof.

It suffices to prove that, if
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is continuous, then the integral of
\bar{\nu}
along
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\int_{a}^{b}d\bar{\nu}_{y(s)}
coincides with the Lebesgue integral
\int_{a}^{b}g(y(s),s)\,ds
. To see it, for each
n\geq 1
take
\delta_{n}=1/n
and a tagged
\delta_{n}
-partition

\Delta^{\delta_{n}}=\{(\tau_{i}^{n},[t_{i}^{n},t_{i+1}^{n}])\mid i=1,\ldots,k_{n}-1\}\,,

and consider the map
g_{n}\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
g_{n}(s):=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}-1}\chi_{[t_{i}^{n},t_{i+1}^{n})}(s)\,g(y(\tau_{i}^{n}),s)
for
s\in[a,b)
and
g_{n}(b):=g(y(\tau_{k_{n}-1}^{n}),b)
. By construction,
g_{n}
is Lebesgue measurable. Since
g(\cdot,s)
is continuous for almost every
s\in[a,b]
, and
y\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is continuous, it easily follows that for almost every
s\in[a,b]
there exists the pointwise limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}(s)=g(y(s),s)
. Furthermore, if
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a compact set such that
y([a,b])\subset K
, then for each
n\geq 1
,
|g_{n}(s)|\leq\widetilde{m}_{K}(s)
for a.e.
s\in[a,b]
, and
\widetilde{m}_{K}\in L^{1}([a,b])
. Thus, Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem implies
\int_{a}^{b}g(y(s),s)\,ds=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}g_{n}(s)\,ds
. Since

\int_{a}^{b}g_{n}(s)\,ds=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}-1}\int_{t_{i}^{n}}^{t_{i+1}^{n}}g(y(\tau_{i}^{n}),s)\,ds=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{n}-1}\int_{t_{i}^{n}}^{t_{i+1}^{n}}d\bar{\nu}_{y(\tau_{i}^{n})}=S_{\bar{\nu}}^{y(\cdot)}(\Delta^{\delta_{n}})

for the Riemann sums introduced in Section
2
, and
\delta_{n}\to 0
, the limit is equal to
\int_{a}^{b}d\bar{\nu}_{y(s)}
. The proof is finished.
∎

In summary, the ODE
y^{\prime}=g(y,t)
considered in this section can be rewritten as the generalized ODE given by the
N
-dim parametric b-measure
\bar{\nu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
in (
5.4
), that is,
y^{\prime}(t)=\bar{\nu}_{y(t)}
, and it can receive a nonautonomous dynamical treatment under the skew-product formalism explained in this section. We highlight two facts: first, the flow on the hull is compact (see Theorem
5.10
), and second, all the equations over the hull keep the properties of equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds (see Proposition
3.10
) and their solutions are all continuous functions of bounded variation (see Remark
5.2
). This avoids taking a larger set of generalized ODEs where solutions lacking these properties may appear.

5.2.
Numerical examples

We aim to provide an example of how solutions of the type of differential equation described in this work may look in practice. As a basis for our examples, we use an approximation of the Cantor function on [0,1], whose graph is the well-known Devil’s staircase.

The Cantor function is approximated by integrating the map
f(t)
built as follows. We consider the tenth iteration of the middle-third Cantor set, which consists of
2^{10}
disjoint intervals within
[0,1]
. A piecewise constant function
f(t)
is then defined to be equal to a constant value
f_{\text{val}}
on these intervals and zero elsewhere, where
f_{\text{val}}
is chosen so that the integral of
f
over
[0,1]
is normalized to one (for
N=10
we have
f_{\text{val}}\approx 57.6650
),

f(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{2^{10}}f_{\text{val}}\,\chi_{I_{j}}(t)\,,

where
I_{j}
is the j-th interval of the tenth iteration of the middle-third Cantor set.

To introduce temporal complexity, a linear combination of translated versions of
f(t)
is formed,

f_{\text{sum}}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{100}(-1)^{j}f(t-j\delta)\,,

(5.5)

where
\delta=0.01
. This alternating sum introduces oscillatory behavior while preserving the fractal support structure. The left-hand panel of Figure

shows the behavior of the function

which is the solution of the initial value problem

.
The function

is also used as a time-dependent forcing term in the nonautonomous Riccati differential equation

A complete dynamical description of nonautonomous Riccati equations with piecewise uniformly continuous coefficients can be found in
[
15

6.
Application to slow-fast systems of ODEs

Recently, Longo et al.
[
17
]
have obtained a dynamical interpretation of the behaviour of the fast variables of slow-fast systems of ODEs with dependence on the fast time in terms of tracking of nonautonomous attractors. In this section, we review the validity of their results under weaker regularity conditions on the vector field driving the fast motion, such as those imposed in Section
5.1
, making the field of application to models in real life much wider.

Keeping the notation of the cited paper, in this type of singularly perturbed system there are two scales of time: the slow timescale
t
and the fast timescale
\tau
; and also two scales of motion: the slow variables
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the fast variables
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Written in slow time,
t
, the coupled pair of ODEs takes the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{x}=f(x,y)\,,\\
\varepsilon\,\dot{y}=g\left(x,y,t/\varepsilon\right),\end{array}\right.

(6.1)

where
\dot{x},\dot{y}
denote the derivatives with respect to
t
and
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter. After the change of independent variables
\tau=t/\varepsilon
relating the two time scales, the equations are written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x^{\prime}=\varepsilon\,f(x,y)\,,\\
y^{\prime}=g(x,y,\tau)\,,\end{array}\right.

(6.2)

where
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
denote the derivatives with respect to
\tau
.
Note that the equations for the fast motion explicitly depend on the fast time, which makes the problem nonautonomous. Prescribed initial conditions are given:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x(0)=x_{0}\,,\\
y(0)=y_{0}\,.\end{array}\right.

(6.3)

For each fixed
\varepsilon>0
, the solution of the initial value problem (
6.1
)-(
6.3
) will be denoted by
(x_{\varepsilon}(t),y_{\varepsilon}(t))
, for
t\in[0,t_{0}]
, and the solution of (
6.2
)-(
6.3
) will be denoted by
(x_{\varepsilon}(\tau),y_{\varepsilon}(\tau))
, for
\tau\in[0,t_{0}/\varepsilon]
. The main interest is in the behaviour of the solutions as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and specifically in the limit behaviour of
y_{\varepsilon}(t_{0}/\varepsilon)
. We refer the reader to references such as Tikhonov
[
22
]
, Verhulst
[
23
]
, Berglund and Gentz
[
3
]
, and Kuehn
[
9
]
for the theory and applications of autonomous slow-fast systems. When dependence on the fast time is admitted, the first work is due to Artstein
[
2
]
, followed by the more recent results in
[
17
]
with a different dynamical approach to the behaviour of the fast motion.

The basic assumptions on the vector fields
f
and
g
are as follows. Note that, compared to Assumption 3.1 in
[
17
]
, assumption (i) on
f
is the same, but assumptions (ii)-(iii) on
g
are much weaker than (ii)-(iii) in
[
17
]
, where
g
is bounded and uniformly continuous in
\tau
, and uniformly Lipschitz in
y
on compact sets.

Due to the presence of two types of variables, in this section the closed ball of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
centered at the origin and with radius
j
is denoted by
B_{j}^{(N)}
.

Assumption 6.1
.

(i)

f(x,y)
is continuous and
\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}|f(x,y)|\leq a+b\,|x|

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for some
a
and
b
positive.

(ii)

g(x,y,\tau)
is measurable in
\tau
, continuous in
(x,y)
for a.e.
\tau
, and for each integer
j\geq 1
there is a modulus of continuity
\omega_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, with
\omega_{j}\leq\omega_{j+1}
, and two nonnegative locally integrable maps
\widetilde{m}_{j},\widetilde{l}_{j}\in L^{1}_{loc}
(respectively called an
m
-bound and an
l
-bound of
g
on
B_{j}^{(n)}\times B_{j}^{(m)}
) such that:

(a)

for a.e.
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
|g(x,y,\tau)|\leq\widetilde{m}_{j}(\tau)
for all
x\in B_{j}^{(n)}
and
y\in B_{j}^{(m)}
;

(b)

for a.e.
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
|g(x_{1},y_{1},\tau)-g(x_{2},y_{2},\tau)|\leq\widetilde{l}_{j}(\tau)\,[\omega_{j}(|x_{1}-x_{2}|)+|y_{1}-y_{2}|]
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in B_{j}^{(n)}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in B_{j}^{(m)}
.

(iii)

The
m
-bounds
\widetilde{m}_{j}
are equicontinuous and the
l
-bounds
\widetilde{l}_{j}
are bounded, that is, they respectively satisfy (i)
′
and (ii)
′
in Section
5.1
.

Since the problem for the fast variables
y^{\prime}=g(x,y,\tau)
is nonautonomous, our approach is to build a nonautonomous forward dynamical system or skew-product semiflow over a compact base flow. In this context, the base flow must take into account the closure of the time-translated problems
y^{\prime}=g(x,y,\tau_{0}+\tau)

(\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R})
as well as the variation of the parameter
x
. As discussed in Section
5
, to this end, we can rewrite the ODEs as generalized ODEs associated with an
m
-dim parametric b-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. More precisely, associated with the vector field
g
we build an
m
-dim parametric b-measure
\bar{\nu}_{g}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})^{m}
,
(x,y)\mapsto(\bar{\nu}_{g})_{y}^{x}
, with components
(\nu_{g}^{1},\ldots,\nu_{g}^{m})
, written in differential form as

d(\bar{\nu}_{g})_{y}^{x}=g(x,y,\tau)\,d\tau\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\;y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

For each integer
j\geq 1
, we take
m_{j}
and
l_{j}
the absolutely continuous regular Borel measures with density functions
\widetilde{m}_{j}(\tau)
and
\widetilde{l}_{j}(\tau)
, respectively. Then,
m_{j},l_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
and it is easy to check that for each component
1\leq i\leq m
,

(1)

|(\nu_{g}^{i})_{y}^{x}|(A)\leq m_{j}(A)
for each subset
A\in\mathcal{B}
and each
(x,y)\in B_{j}^{(n+m)}
,

(2)

|(\nu_{g}^{i})_{y_{1}}^{x_{1}}-(\nu_{g}^{i})_{y_{2}}^{x_{2}}|(A)\leq l_{j}(A)\,[\omega_{j}(|x_{1}-x_{2}|)+|y_{1}-y_{2}|]
for each subset
A\in\mathcal{B}
and
(x_{1},y_{1}),\,(x_{2},y_{2})\in B_{j}^{(n+m)}
.

That is,
m_{j},l_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}
are, respectively, an
m
-bound and an
l
-bound of
\nu_{g}^{i}
on the ball
B_{j}^{(n+m)}
, with respect to a particular family of moduli of continuity, which is Lipschitz in the
y
variables. Therefore, the results in Sections
3
and
4
apply, and those in Section
5
apply to
(\bar{\nu}_{g})^{x}\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathcal{M}_{c}(\mathcal{B})^{m}
,
y\mapsto(\bar{\nu}_{g})_{y}^{x}
, for each
x
fixed.

Note that we assign different roles to each of the variables
x
and
y
. The slow variables
x
play the role of a vector-valued parameter, whereas the fast variables
y
are those with respect to which we solve a generalized ODEs problem. More precisely, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
fixed, the equation
y^{\prime}=g(x,y,\tau)
is called a
layer problem
or a
layer equation
which is rewritten (see Proposition
5.11
) as

y^{\prime}(\tau)=(\bar{\nu}_{g})_{y(\tau)}^{x}\,,\quad\tau\geq 0\,,

and the translated problems as
y^{\prime}(\tau)=(\bar{\nu}_{g}{\cdot}\tau_{0})_{y(\tau)}^{x}

(\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R})
.
Since from Assumption
6.1
(iii), the
m
-dim parametric b-measure
\bar{\nu}_{g}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
has equicontinuous
m
-bounds and bounded
l
-bounds, we have a continuous flow defined by translation on the compact hull
\mathcal{H}
of
\bar{\nu}_{g}
built as in (
5.2
), which can equivalently be built for the product topology
\bar{\sigma}_{D}
. We build an extended compact base flow by adding a compact component
K_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{H}\times{K_{0}}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{H}\times{K_{0}}\\
(\tau,\bar{\nu},x)&\mapsto&(\bar{\nu}{\cdot}\tau,x)\,.\end{array}

Then, under Assumption
6.1
(ii)-(iii), a continuous skew-product semiflow is defined over the former compact base flow in the following way, on an appropriate open set
\mathcal{U}
subject to the existence of solutions:

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\pi\colon\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathcal{H}\times{K_{0}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{H}\times{K_{0}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\\
(\tau,\bar{\nu},x,y_{0})&\mapsto&(\bar{\nu}{\cdot}\tau,x,y(\tau,\bar{\nu},x,y_{0}))\,,\end{array}

(6.4)

where
y(\tau,\bar{\nu},x,y_{0})
denotes the solution of
y^{\prime}(t)=\bar{\nu}_{y(t)}^{x}
with initial data
y(0)=y_{0}
. Keep in mind that for the element
\bar{\nu}_{g}\in\mathcal{H}
we recover the solutions of the Carathéodory ODEs
y^{\prime}=g(x,y,\tau)
for
x\in K_{0}
(once again, see Proposition
5.11
).

We insist that the Lipschitz-type condition in Assumption
6.1
(ii)-(b) with respect to the variable
y
is crucial to apply the results in Section
5
. In particular, Theorem
5.8
and Remark
5.9
assert the continuous dependence of the solutions with respect to the variation of
\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{H}
and the initial condition
y_{0}
. To check the continuous dependence with respect to changes in the parameter
x
, the arguments are the same (once more, see the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 3.8 (i)]
), and we omit them.
Note that
\pi
is a nonautonomous dynamical system because, by construction, the fiber components satisfy the so-called
semicocycle property
, subject to existence:

y(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2},\bar{\nu},x,y_{0})=y(\tau_{1},\bar{\nu}{\cdot}\tau_{2},x,y(\tau_{2},\bar{\nu},x,y_{0}))\,,\quad\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\geq 0\,.

(6.5)

For each fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let us denote by
\pi^{x}
the
layer skew-product semiflow
:

\begin{split}\pi^{x}\colon\mathcal{U}_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\ &\to\ \ \mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\\
\ (\tau,\bar{\nu},y_{0})\qquad&\mapsto\;(\bar{\nu}{\cdot}\tau,y(\tau,\bar{\nu},x,y_{0}))\,.\end{split}

(6.6)

As in
[
17
]
, the main hypothesis in qualitative behaviour of the fast variables is the uniform ultimate boundedness of the solutions of the parametric family of Carathéodory ODEs
y^{\prime}=g(x,y,\tau)
for
x
varying in compact subsets
K_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This requires that solutions are globally defined in the future and that there exists a constant
c>0
such that if initial conditions
y_{0}
are taken in a ball
B_{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, there exists a time
T=T(d)>0
such that the solutions have entered the ball
B_{c}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
after a lapse of time
T
, and this happens uniformly for
x\in K_{0}
(more precisely, see Definition 3.2 in
[
17
]
). We collect these additional hypotheses.

Assumption 6.2
.

(i)

For each
\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{H}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the solution
y(\tau,\bar{\nu},x,y_{0})
is defined for all
\tau\geq 0
.

(ii)

For each compact set
K_{0}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the solutions of the parametric family
y^{\prime}=g(x,y,\tau)
,
x\in K_{0}
are uniformly ultimately bounded.

Once we have established the context, it is important to note that Theorem 5.3 in Artstein
[
2
]
, which basically states uniform convergence for the slow variables and statistical convergence for the fast variables, applies under stronger assumptions on the vector field
g
.
The uniform integrability of the
m
-bounds there required is now relaxed to equicontinuity, with the technical, though challenging, inconvenience that generalized ODEs appear, but with the advantage of enlarging the field of applications. We keep their result for the slow variables, whereas we follow the dynamical approach of Longo et al.
[
17
]
for the qualitative behaviour of the fast variables, using the theory of nonautonomous attractors. This allows us to identify the dynamical objects that the fast variables approach as
\varepsilon
goes to
0
, in terms of the fibers of the global attractor.

Not to make the paper unnecessarily long, we refer the reader to
[
17
]
and references therein (e.g., Kloeden and Rasmussen
[
8
]
) for the concepts of global attractor, pullback attractor and parametrically inflated pullback attractor, which will appear in the statements of the results. For the proofs of the next two results, the reader is referred to the proofs of
[
17
, Proposition 3.4]
and
[
17
, Theorem 3.9]
, respectively.

.

Under Assumptions
6.1
(ii)-(iii)
and

Theorem 6.4
.

Under Assumptions
6.1
(ii)-(iii)
and

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
g_{1},g_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be Carathéodory maps with
m
-bounds, and assume that
g_{1}
also has bounded

-bounds
\{\widetilde{\ell}_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset L^{1}_{loc}
. If

are solutions of

,
y^{\prime}=g_{2}(y,\tau)
, respectively, with
y_{1}(a)=y_{2}(a)
,
y_{1}([a,b]),y_{2}([a,b])\subset B_{j}^{(m)}
for some

, and for some

|g_{1}(y_{2}(\tau),\tau)-g_{2}(y_{2}(\tau),\tau)|\leq\widetilde{\ell}_{j}(\tau)\,\sigma\quad\hbox{for almost every }\;\tau\in[a,b]\,,

(6.7)

then, for a positive constant
c_{j}
such that
\int_{s}^{s+(b-a)}\widetilde{\ell}_{j}(\tau)\,d\tau\leq c_{j}
for all

,

|y_{1}(\tau)-y_{2}(\tau)|\leq c_{j}\,e^{c_{j}}\,\sigma\quad\hbox{for almost every }\;\tau\in[a,b]\,.

Proof.

Let
v(\tau)\coloneqq|y_{1}(\tau)-y_{2}(\tau)|=\left|\int_{a}^{\tau}g_{1}(y_{1}(s),s)\,ds-\int_{a}^{\tau}g_{2}(y_{2}(s),s)\,ds\right|
,
\tau\in[a,b]
. By the triangle inequality, using the

for

and (

), it is easy to obtain that

for a.e.

\widetilde{\ell}_{j}\in L^{1}_{loc}
and the hypothesis of bounded
l
-bounds.
∎

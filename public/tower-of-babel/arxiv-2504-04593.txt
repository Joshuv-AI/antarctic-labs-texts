Remarks on Fixed Point Assertions in Digital Topology, 10
Laurence Boxer

Abstract
The topic of fixed points in digital metric spaces continues to draw publications with assertions that are incorrect, incorrectly proven, trivial, or incoherently stated. We continue the work of our earlier papers that discuss publications with bad assertions concerning fixed points of self-functions on digital images. 2.3
A popular error

An error made by several authors, including a subset of those whose papers are
studied in the current work, is to confuse metric continuity (the

\varepsilon-\delta
version) and digital continuity.

Let
(X,d_{X},\kappa)
and
(Y,d_{Y},\lambda)
be digital metric spaces.
Suppose
d_{X}
and
d_{Y}
are uniformly discrete. One sees easily
that every
f:X\to Y
has metric continuity; however, depending on the
graph structures of
(X,\kappa)
and
(Y,\lambda)
, there may be many

f:X\to Y
that fail to have digital continuity. The latter is true even
in many instances in which
f
is subject to additional restrictions, such as
being a digital contraction. Example 4.1 of
[
16
]
gives a counterexample to
the claim that a digital contraction mapping is digitally continuous.

3
Banach contraction principle of
[
19
,
31
]

The papers
[
19
,
31
]
state a digital version of the classic
Banach contraction principle. However, in each of these papers the “proof” uses
the incorrect claim discussed in
section
2.3
about the digital continuity of a
digital contraction mapping .

We prove a slightly generalized version of the digital Banach contraction
principle in this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,d,\kappa)
be a uniformly discrete digital metric space.
Let
f:X\to X
be a digital contraction map. Then
f
has a
unique fixed point.

Proof.

Since
f
is a digital contraction map, there exists
k
,
0\leq k<1
,
such that
d(fx,fy)\leq kd(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
. Let
x_{0}\in X

and consider the sequence defined by
x_{n+1}=fx_{n}
. We have

d(x_{n+1},x_{n})=d(fx_{n},fx_{n-1})\leq kd(x_{n},x_{n-1})

so simple induction gives

d(x_{n+m},x_{n})\leq k^{m-1}d(x_{n+1},x_{n})\leq k^{m+n}d(x_{1},x_{0})\to_{n\to\infty}0.

Thus,
\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence.
By Proposition
2.7
, there exists
z\in X
such that
for almost all
n
,
z=x_{n}
. Therefore, for almost all
n
,

fz=fx_{n}=x_{n+1}=z,

so
z
is a fixed point of
f
.

Uniqueness is shown as follows. Let
z,z^{\prime}\in\Fix(f)
. Then

d(z,z^{\prime})=d(fz,fz^{\prime})\leq kd(z,z^{\prime})

so
d(z,z^{\prime})=0
, i.e.,
z=z^{\prime}
.
∎

[

live in

.

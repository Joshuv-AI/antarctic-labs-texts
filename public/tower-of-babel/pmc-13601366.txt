Protocol for telomere-to-telomere assembly of Borrelia genomes using a hybrid method
Authors: Atia B. Amin, Ana Victoria Ibarra-Meneses, Mathieu Blanchette, Christopher Fernandez-Prada, David Langlais
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBorrelia has a linear chromosome and linear plasmids capped by hairpin telomeres that short-read sequencing cannot resolve. Here, we present a protocol for telomere-to-telomere assembly of Borrelia genomes. We describe steps for spanning B. burgdorferi culture, DNA extraction, and sequencing through hybrid genome assembly to generate complete Borrelia genomes. The pipeline integrates Oxford Nanopore long reads and Illumina short reads to assemble hairpin telomeres, resolve paralogous linear and circular plasmids, and annotate and validate the assembled complete Borrelia genome.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Amin et al.1

Before you begin

Overview

This protocol describes in detail a hybrid genome assembly pipeline optimized for Borrelia burgdorferi, a bacterium with a linear chromosome and multiple linear and circular plasmids terminated by covalently closed hairpin telomeres.1 Successful application of this protocol requires careful attention to sample quality, sequencing strategy, and computational setup. Prior to starting, users should ensure that all biological materials, sequencing data, and computational resources meet the requirements outlined below.

This protocol has been optimized for Borrelia species and adaptation to organisms with different telomeric structures or replication mechanisms may require additional validation and workflow modification. Because accurate reconstruction of hairpin telomeres and paralogous plasmids depends on long contiguous reads, long read sequencing data is essential. Short read data alone is insufficient to resolve telomeric structures or discriminate among highly similar plasmids.

Innovation

This protocol introduces several methodological advances for generating complete, telomere-to-telomere assemblies of Borrelia genomes, including comprehensive recovery of their linear and circular plasmid repertoire.

Identification and validation of hairpin telomeres in linear chromosomes

This workflow enables the precise identification of inversion points associated with covalently closed hairpin telomeres and systematic detection of conserved telomeric motifs in assembled linear chromosomes. These steps facilitate accurate telomere-to-telomere chromosome reconstruction and enable comparative analyses of conserved and variable telomeric sequences across Borrelia strains.

Integration of a dedicated plasmid assembler into a hybrid assembly framework

The protocol combines long read and short read sequencing within a hybrid framework, using Trycycler2 for robust linear chromosome assembly, while incorporating Plassembler,3 a plasmid-focused assembler, to reliably recover both high- and low-copy-number plasmids that are frequently missed or fragmented by general-purpose assemblers.

Extension of plasmid assembly to resolve complete linear plasmids

The workflow further customizes plasmid assembly by introducing a telomeric end extension step, enabling the reconstruction of linear plasmids with fully resolved hairpin telomeres and improving confidence in plasmid completeness, topology, and classification (plasmid topology determination method is explained in detail in step 26 and 27).

Together, these innovations establish a reproducible, scalable, and broadly applicable workflow for high-resolution Borrelia genome assembly, providing a practical framework for investigating plasmid diversity and genome plasticity at the telomeric ends of linear replicons.

This major step explains a genome assembly pipeline that generates a complete assembly of Borrelia burgdorferi genome using a hybrid approach that combines Illumina paired-end short reads with Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) long reads. The example dataset includes raw paired-end Illumina reads and base called ONT reads for nine Borrelia strains and is available as Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under the NCBI BioProject accession number PRJNA1293716.

Borrelia species have a unique genomic architecture consisting of a linear chromosome and both linear and circular plasmids. While some plasmids are consistently retained across species and strains, others are frequently lost. The linear plasmids, in particular, present a significant assembly challenge due to their high sequence homology within and across different plasmid types.

We used Trycycler2 for the linear chromosome assembly. Trycycler uses three different assemblers of choice and performs a consensus generation step for all the contigs generated by the three assemblers. Because Borrelia plasmids have high level of mosaism, Trycycler fails during the consensus generation step for the plasmid assembly. Moreover, Trycycler performs long read first assemblies which tends to miss small (<20 kb) plasmids16 and Borrelia has plasmids as small as 5 kb. Therefore, we developed a separate pipeline for assembling the plasmids (Figure 2). We used Plassembler,3 which is designed to capture even the low copy number plasmids by removing the chromosomal reads before performing plasmid specific assembly. This major step explains the curation of complete circular plasmids and extends the pipeline for assembling the complete linear plasmids.

Most of the existing plasmid assemblers are designed for circular plasmids. However, Borrelia species carry linear plasmids which have atypical hairpin telomeres. Plassembler uses Unicycler,19 a short read first assembler to assemble the circular plasmids. Unicycler also performs an in-built circularity test. However, it does not perform any completeness analysis for the linear plasmids.

In our study, even though Plassembler assembled the contigs of the linear plasmids, in most cases it could not assemble complete telomere-to-telomere linear plasmids. To tackle this challenge, we developed a plasmid completion and validation method customized for Borrelia genome. We incorporated “Telomere End Extension” to completely assemble the hairpin telomeres of the linear plasmids (Figure 6).26.Perform the complete assembly of the circular plasmids.Note:Unicycler detects terminal overlaps of each contig, verifies the overlaps and circularizes the contig by trimming one end.19 However, it might fail to identify a terminal overlap if the long read coverage is lower than the default threshold at the junction. Moreover, high sequence similarity in distant region of the contig might also complicate the circularization process. Since Borrelia plasmids are heterogeneous and can show high sequence similarity, long read coverage can be uneven. As a result, using only the Unicycler pipeline might miss some plasmids. In that case, careful investigation and trimming is recommended. def run_blast(query_file, db_file, output_file):

print(f“Running BLAST: query={query_file}, db={db_file}, output={output_file}”)

blast_command = [

“blastn”,

“-query”, query_file,

“-db”, db_file,

“-out”, output_file,

“-outfmt”, “10 qseqid sseqid pident length mismatch gapopen qstart qend sstart send evalue bitscore qlen slen sstrand”

]

subprocess.run(blast_command, check=True)

def parse_blast_output(blast_output_file, is_small_contig1):

print(f“Parsing BLAST output: {blast_output_file}”)

df = pd.read_csv(blast_output_file, header=None)

df.columns = [“qseqid”, “sseqid”, “pident”, “length”, “mismatch”, “gapopen”,

“qstart”, “qend”, “sstart”, “send”, “evalue”, “bitscore”, “qlen”, “slen”, “sstrand”]

if is_small_contig1:

# For small_contig1 (Left_end_blast.csv)

plus_strand = df[df[“sstrand”] == “plus”]

if plus_strand.empty:

raise ValueError(f“No 'plus' strand alignment found in {blast_output_file}”)

candidate = plus_strand[plus_strand[“sstart”] == 1]

if candidate.empty:

raise ValueError(f“No suitable alignment found in {blast_output_file} for small_contig1”)

overlap_start = candidate[“qstart”].iloc[0]

overlap_end = candidate[“qend”].iloc[0]

else:

# For small_contig2 (Right_end_blast.csv), use the existing logic

plus_strand = df[df[“sstrand”] == “plus”]

if plus_strand.empty:

raise ValueError(f“No 'plus' strand alignment found in {blast_output_file}”)

candidate = plus_strand[plus_strand[“send”] == plus_strand[“slen”]]

if candidate.empty:

raise ValueError(f“No suitable alignment found where subject end equals subject length in {blast_output_file}”)

overlap_start = candidate[“qstart”].iloc[0]

overlap_end = candidate[“qend”].iloc[0]

print(f“Determined overlap start: {overlap_start}, end: {overlap_end}”)

return overlap_start, overlap_end

Expected outcomes

Standard long read assemblers (e.g., Flye, Canu) produce chromosomal assemblies 13–66 kb longer than the true sequence due to unresolved hairpin telomere junctions, and generate fragmented, anonymous non-chromosomal contigs without confirmed replicon identity or topology (Table 3). This hybrid genome assembly pipeline is designed to achieve a complete and high-quality telomere-to-telomere genome assembly of Borrelia burgdorferi, including both linear and circular replicons. By integrating Illumina short reads with ONT long reads, the pipeline aims to provide accurate assembly of the linear chromosome and plasmids, including resolving the challenging hairpin telomeres. We used this pipeline to assemble nine B. burgdorferi strains and identified an average of 21 kb of telomeric end inverse duplication in the linear chromosome contigs of all strains.1 This pipeline can be used for complete genome assembly of other Borrelia species.

For plasmid assembly, the pipeline leverages Plassembler to accurately assemble both high and low copy number plasmids by separating chromosomal and plasmid reads. This allows effective resolution of even low copy number plasmids that might be overlooked by traditional assemblers. The pipeline also incorporates a trimming and circularization step to ensure that circular plasmids are assembled to completion. For linear plasmids, a dedicated telomeric end extension step is used to capture and assemble the hairpin telomeres, which are typically difficult to resolve due to their inverted repeat structure. Using this ensemble plasmid assembly pipeline, we assembled between 10 to 15 plasmids including the smallest plasmid lp5 (∼5 kb) in each of the nine B. burgdorferi strains with higher confidence and accuracy (Table 11).

The final output is expected to be a complete genome assembly with linear and circular plasmids and enhanced telomere resolution, which will be used as a reference genome for Borrelia burgdorferi. The high-quality assembly is expected to support downstream comparative genomic analysis, evolutionary studies, and the identification of strain-specific genetic variations, contributing valuable insights into the genomic diversity of Borrelia species.

Limitations

The proposed pipeline still requires significant amount of manual intervention during the trimming process of hairpin telomeres and plasmid curation. However, this is necessary to ensure the accuracy of the assembly. We used Trycycler to generate a consensus assembly which is a multi-step process and requires substantial computational resources. Recently, the developer of Trycycler released an automated version of this assembler called Autocycler (https://github.com/rrwick/Autocycler) to facilitate the automation of the multi-step assembly process. Readers may consider using Autocycler to reduce manual effort. An updated version of Plassembler (v1.5.0) provides options to add an already existing Flye assembly as an input. Users might want to use that version if only plasmid sequences are available and no chromosomal assembly is required.

This pipeline does not include a detailed discussion of the genome quality assessment, which can be performed using Quast (https://github.com/ablab/quast) following the manual provided on this website (http://quast.sf.net). Additionally, the telomere trimming step of the linear chromosome relied on the known telomeric end motifs of B. burgdorferi reference strain B31, which might not be available for other bacterial species with linear replicons. In such cases, we recommend using the “Telomeric End Extension” step for the linear chromosomes and linear plasmids to ensure completeness of the hairpin telomeres.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Trycycler fails to generate a single representative cluster for the linear chromosome (step 22c).

Potential solution

This issue can occur when ONT read quality is low, coverage is uneven, or when highly repetitive regions cause fragmentation across assemblies. First, verify that the N50 and read length distribution of the filtered ONT reads are sufficient (>20 kb recommended). If multiple chromosome-sized contigs appear across clusters, manually inspect cluster membership and select the cluster containing contigs closest to the expected chromosome length. Reducing the number of subsampled read sets or excluding assemblies with clear misassemblies may also improve clustering. Alternatively, users may consider using Autocycler as an automated alternative to Trycycler.

Problem 2

No valid alignments are identified at the linear chromosome telomeric ends for the left and right conserved telomeric sequences (step 24 c).

Potential solution

Conserved Borrelia telomeric sequences can diverge significantly between strains due to accumulation of SNPs within the telomeric motifs. In our companion study (Figure 1B1), strain Bb16 57 showed only 56% identity with the B31 reference telomeric sequence, which falls below the effective detection threshold of standard blastn and will return no significant hits.

If similar situation arises and the alignments are not identified using the blastn algorithm, try the discontinuous megablast algorithm. If neither algorithm returns hits due to extensive sequence divergence (as observed for strain Bb16 571), proceed to trimming using the IRF-identified inversion position (Step 27a, c), then align the B31 reference telomeric sequence against the trimmed chromosome ends to identify strain-specific mismatches.

Problem 3

Ortholog reference plasmid is not identified for a filtered plasmid contig (step 25 c).

Potential solution

If there is a novel plasmid or fusion plasmid in the assembled strain, Plassembler might fail to provide a reference ortholog and the plasmid topology. In that case, separate this contig and further investigate it to confirm the assembly validation and topology using step 26–28.

Problem 4

Flye assembly of telomeric ends fails (step 27 b iv).

Potential solution

Flye recommends a minimum read coverage of 20X to perform assembly of a small bacterial genome. Therefore, if there are not enough coverage after filtering ONT reads based on the length threshold (>= 6 kb), the assembly might fail. To solve this, make sure to have enough reads after filtering the ONT reads based on read length. If there are not enough reads, we recommend lowering the filtering threshold.

Problem 5

Merged linear plasmid contigs show uneven ONT read coverage or coverage dropouts after telomeric end extension (step 27d).

Potential solution

Uneven coverage may indicate incorrect contig merging, misidentified overlap regions, or incomplete telomeric extension. Load the merged contig and the ONT BAM file into IGV and inspect coverage continuity across the entire plasmid, particularly at junctions between the Plassembler contig and telomeric extension contigs. If coverage gaps or abrupt coverage changes are observed, re-evaluate the BLAST overlap coordinates used for merging and confirm strand orientation of the telomeric contigs. In some cases, lowering the minimum read-length threshold during telomeric read filtering can increase coverage and improve assembly continuity.

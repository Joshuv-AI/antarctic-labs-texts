Effects of nutritional status on short-term prognosis after minimally invasive pancreaticoduodenectom
Authors: Zheyu Xu, Jingtao Chen, Yiping Mou, Ou Li, Y C Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMinimally invasive pancreaticoduodenectomy (MIPD) has been routinely performed in major centers, and its feasibility and efficacy in tumor treatment have been highly recognized. Malnutrition has been associated with higher rates of morbidity, and increased mortality in surgical patients. The effect of the nutritional status on MIPD outcomes still remains unclear and controversial. The clinical data of 207 consecutive patients who had MIPD between June 2017 and December 2022 were retrospectively analyzed. A multidimensional nutrition assessment was performed before surgery. Multivariable analysis and propensity score matching (PSM) was performed to identify the association of preoperative nutritional status on postoperative short-term prognosis(Morbidity–Mortality, Clavien-Dindo classification ≥ IIIa, postoperative pancreatic fistulas and biliary fistula). In the multivariable analysis, sarcopenia (OR 9.74, 95% CI 3.34–43.03) was associated with postoperative morbidity and sarcopenia (OR 6.74, 95% CI 2.24–30.24) was associated with major complications. In the cohort after PSM, sarcopenia remained independently associated with morbidity (OR 12.44, 95% CI 3.72- 59.16) and major complications (OR 8.14, 95% CI 2.13- 43.83). Sarcopenia before MIPD has an impact on postoperative outcomes. Nutritional status assessment, especially sarcopenia, should be part of the routine preoperative procedures to provide early and appropriate nutritional support for MIPD patients.Keywords: Minimally invasive pancreaticoduodenectomy, Nutritional status, Sarcopenia, Weight loss, MorbiditySubject terms: Pancreatic disease, Malnutrition

Introduction

With the ageing of the population, the incidence of pancreatic head cancer and periampullary cancer has been steadily increasing year by year1. Both are highly aggressive malignancies, with a 5-year survival rate of approximately 10%2. Pancreatoduodenectomy (PD), as the primary treatment, involves the resection of multiple organs and a wide range of tissues, and has high technical difficulty, and a relatively high incidence of postoperative morbidity with the potential for serious consequences3. Over the past decade, with the advent of minimally invasive surgery, minimally invasive pancreatic resections, including both robot-assisted and laparoscopic approaches, have become part of standard surgical practice globally4. A Meta-Analysis5 suggested that minimally invasive pancreatoduodenectomy (MIPD) appears to be relatively safe in elderly patients, a group that may benefit more from this less invasive approach. Nevertheless, despite technological advancements in surgical techniques and perioperative management, postoperative morbidity remains a significant concern. Common morbidities after MIPD include pancreatic fistula, delayed gastric emptying, bleeding, chyle leak, endocrine and exocrine pancreatic insufficiency6. Among them, pancreatic fistula has a high prevalence, which can reach 20%7.

Nutritional status plays a critical role in the outcomes of surgical procedures. Preoperative malnutrition has been linked to increased postoperative morbidities, longer hospital stay, and poor recovery8. Specifically, adequate nutritional support can enhance immune function, reduce the risk of infections, and promote wound healing, all of which are essential for successful surgical recovery. Sarcopenia is a systemic malnutrition disease characterized by widespread loss of skeletal muscle mass and function, often associated with aging and various diseases9. While it can be considered a form of malnutrition due to its impact on muscle health, it is distinct from general malnutrition, which encompasses a broader spectrum of nutritional deficiencies affecting overall health10. In cancer patients undergoing pancreatic surgery, preoperative sarcopenia is associated with poorer long-term survival outcomes3,11–14. When malnutrition and sarcopenia coexist, this may result in the malnutrition-sarcopenia syndrome (MSS) 15, which has been shown to increase mortality rates even more than sarcopenia alone. Studies have pointed out that MSS increases the risk of multiple postoperative morbidities in cancer patients and prolongs the patient’s duration of hospital stay. However, its relationship with the occurrence of postoperative morbidity in MIPD remains unclear16.

The aim of this study is to analysis the impact of nutritional indices on short-term prognosis following MIPD in terms of survival.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study included patients with pancreatic malignancies who underwent MIPD in the Department of Gastrointestinal and Pancreatic Surgery of Zhejiang Provincial People’s Hospital from June 2017 to December 2022. Exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) advanced pancreatic cancer with metastasis; (2) palliative or emergency surgery; (3) prior administration of chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or other oncological treatments before surgery; (4) incomplete data. A multidimensional nutritional assessment was performed preoperatively. This comprehensive evaluation of nutritional status encompasses various interconnected domains. Key components typically include: (1) Anthropometric measurements: These involve assessing body composition, including weight, height, body mass index (BMI). (2) Biochemical indicators such as laboratory tests provide insights into nutrient deficiencies, metabolic status, and organ function. Assessments included serum protein levels and markers of inflammation. (3) Clinical assessment: This involves a thorough physical examination to identify signs of malnutrition. (4) Dietary Evaluation: Analyzing dietary habits through food frequency questionnaires or 24-h dietary recalls helps identify nutrient intake and dietary patterns, allowing for targeted nutritional interventions.

This single-center, retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhejiang Province People’s Hospital (QT2024202), and all patients provided written informed consent. Additionally, the study was conducted according to the STROBE reporting guidelines. The research have been performed in accordance with the Declaration of 《Helsinki》.

Sarcopenia definition

Staging computed tomography (CT) scans of patients with pancreatic disease are utilised to evaluate body composition, particularly for the analysis of sarcopenia. Total psoas muscle area (TLA) (cm 2), including paravertebral and abdominal wall muscles at L3 level, was calculated by Sliceomatic 5.0 after manual tracking of ROI, which includes psoas, paravertebral muscles (erectus, quadrate, multifidus) and parietal muscles (transversals, internal and external obliques, rectus abdominis). The skeletal muscle index (SMI) was derived as the ratio of TLA to height squared (cm2/m2). The CT attenuation values for muscle tissue were set between -29 and 150 Hounsfield units (HU). Sarcopenia was defined as an SMI < 53.00 cm2/m2 for males with a BMI ≥ 25 kg/m2, < 43.00 cm2/m2 for males with a BMI < 25 kg/m2, and < 41.00 cm2/m2 for females17. Data collection

Before surgery, a CT scan was assessed sarcopenia. The diagnosis of cancer-related malnutrition (CRM) adhered to the Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition (GLIM) criteria18. Preoperative patient characteristics, retrieved from hospital databases, included age, sex, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) scores, preoperative albumin and hemoglobin levels, history of prior abdominal surgery, anti-jaundice therapy, percentage weight loss, body mass index (BMI), Nutritional Risk Screening 2002 (NRS2002) score, Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), and third lumbar vertebra (L3) skeletal muscle index (SMI, cm2/m2). Surgical variables included type of MIPD and operation time. Postoperative outcomes included length of hospital stay, morbidity, 30-day readmission, pancreatic fistula, biliary fistula, delayed gastric emptying, abdominal infection and gastrointestinal bleeding, and associated postoperative morbidities (the grades of postoperative morbidities were recorded according to the Clavien–Dindo classification), as well as patients’ pathology results.

According to the definition and grading system of postoperative pancreatic fistulas (POPFs) published by the 2016 revised International Classification of Pancreatic Surgery Study Group19. To diagnose of nutritional risk and malnutrition, a questionnaire about weight loss, reduced food intake, and general condition were conducted at the time of admission18. We define “major complications” as Clavien-Dindo classification ≥ III20.

Operative procedure

Standard radical MIPD was performed in accordance with established guidelines21. Two surgeons with extensive experience in MIPD, who had successfully completed a series of robotic hepatobiliary and pancreatic surgeries to better appreciate the learning curve associated with these procedures, and who were from the same department performed the operations.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the analysis of the risk factors of nutrition status for the short-term prognosis after MIPD. The secondary outcome was preoperative nutritional status analysis of MIPD patients.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean and standard deviation (SD), or median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages. Logistic regression models were used to investigate the relationship with the primary and secondary outcome. Firstly, for each outcome, we perform a univariate analysis. Secondly, variables which P < 0.05 were included into multivariable analyses. The propensity score-matched (PSM) procedure was conducted using the MatchIt package in RStudio. PSM was performed to identify the association of preoperative nutritional status with postoperative short-term prognosis. Statistical significance was determined at a two-tailed P < 0.05. Analyses were performed using RStudio 4.2.1 statistical software (http://www.Rproject.org).

Results

Patients’ cohort

Ultimately, 207 patients who met the specified inclusion and exclusion criteria were selected for this study, as depicted in Fig. 3. A total of 207 patients participated in this study (82 women, 125 men) with a median age of 65 (range: 33–79) years. Of these, 69 patients (33.3%) had morbidities, and there were no deaths within 90 days postoperatively. The median preoperative plasma albumin concentration was 36.9 g/L (range: 28.6–49). And the median preoperative hemoglobin (Hb) concentration was 124 g/L (range: 71–182). A total of 107 patients (51.7%) had an NRS2002 score ≥ 3. The ASA score was II in 82.1% of patients. The median operation time was approximately 370 min. The most common tumor location was the head of the pancreas (49.8%), followed by the duodenum (24.2%). The majority of tumors were moderately differentiated (49.8%). Laparoscopic PD(LPD) was performed in 111 (53.6%) patients, robotic PD (RPD) was performed in 96 (46.4%) patients. Nutritional status before surgery

Regarding preoperative nutritional status, the mean BMI was 22.6 kg/m2 (SD ± 3), with weight loss observed in 39.1% of the cohort. Notably, 9.7% of patients exhibited weight loss exceeding 10%. Furthermore, 19.3% of patients reported reduced oral intake. The mean SMI was 35.4 cm2/m2 (SD ± 9.3), with females having a mean SMI of 29.8 cm2/m2 (SD ± 9.1) and males 39.2 cm2/m2 (SD ± 9.3). Sarcopenia was present in 32.9% of patients. Cancer-related malnutrition (CRM), as defined by the GLIM criteria, was identified in 84.1% of the population. Multivariable analysis for the risk of adverse clinical outcomes

In the multivariable analysis, sarcopenia was found to be significantly associated with postoperative morbidity (OR 9.74, 95% CI 3.34–43.03) and with major complications (OR 6.74, 95% CI 2.24–30.24). Furthermore, in the cohort after propensity score matching (PSM), sarcopenia remained independently associated with morbidity (OR 12.44, 95% CI 3.72–59.16) and with major complications (OR 8.14, 95% CI 2.13–43.83). Multivariable analysis with propensity scores for the risk of adverse clinical outcomes

The patients were stratified into two groups based on the presence or absence of sarcopenia, matched in a 1:3 ratio. To explore the association between sarcopenia and short-term morbidity and prognosis following minimally invasive pancreatoduodenectomy (MIPD), we excluded covariates with high collinearity to prevent multicollinearity issues, and performed PSM. After head-to-head PSM, no significant differences were observed between the groups in terms of sex, age, diabetes status, body mass index (BMI), percentage weight loss, reduced oral intake, hypoalbuminemia, anemia, prior abdominal surgery, Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), anti-jaundice therapy, type of MIPD, or operative time. A multivariable logistic regression model was subsequently employed, incorporating variables potentially associated with sarcopenia into the propensity score model. Sarcopenia was identified as an independent, prognostic factor for Morbidity (OR 12.44, 95% CI 3.72–59.16) and for complications Clavien-Dindo grade ≥ IIIa (OR 8.14, 95% CI 2.13- 43.83). Moreover, following PSM, sarcopenia remained an independent risk factor for POPFs (OR 8.50, 95% CI 2.56–40.18), although no statistical significance was observed between sarcopenia and POPFs prior to PSM. Discussion

In our study, we found that sarcopenia had a significant impact on major complications and postoperative morbidity, which were considered short-term outcomes in patients undergoing MIPD. In addition, sarcopenia was identified as an independent predictor of POPFs.

In our cohort, malnutrition and sarcopenia were prevalent and were associated with higher rates of postoperative morbidities, including POPFs. These observations align with existing literature22, which has demonstrated that patients with pancreatic cancer are at increased risk of malnutrition due to the tumor’s metabolic demands and the malabsorption resulting from pancreatic exocrine insufficiency. For example, Pellegrinelli et al.22 reported that malnourished patients had significantly higher morbidity and longer hospital stays after pancreatoduodenectomy.

Although pancreatic malignancy patients can be treated with chemotherapy, radiation, immunotherapy, and other, pancreaticoduodenectomy still represents the only chance of radical cure for pancreatic head malignancies23. With the advent of minimally invasive techniques, both robot-assisted and laparoscopic pancreatic resections have become an integral part of standard surgical practice worldwide over the past decade24–26. As the global population ages, the number of elderly patients requiring surgical intervention is increasing, and MIPD is being performed more frequently in this demographic. However, elderly patients face a higher risk of postoperative mortality, with approximately half of the survivors experiencing a significant decline in functional capacity27. From this, MIPD is difficult and still has many morbidities. While much of the existing literature has focused on open pancreatic surgery, our study is among the few that specifically investigate the impact of nutritional status in patients undergoing MIPD. Although the minimally invasive approach is associated with reduced intraoperative blood loss and shorter hospital stays, it does not mitigate the increased risks associated with malnutrition. Indeed, our findings indicate that patients with poor nutritional status continue to experience significant postoperative complications, highlighting the importance of preoperative nutritional optimization, irrespective of the surgical technique used.

Although prior research28 has evaluated the impact of nutritional interventions in other types of surgery, there is limited data specifically addressing how malnutrition and sarcopenia affect outcomes in patients undergoing MIPD. The concept of sarcopenia was first proposed by Irwin H. Roth of Tufts University in 198929, and sarcopenia might be predictive for postoperative pancreatic fistula after MIPD30–34. POPFs, delayed gastric emptying, bile leak and postoperative hemorrhage were reported in 20.9%, 5.5%, 3.5% and 6.0% of patients respectively7,35. POPFs were generally associated with pancreatic thickness, duct diameter, and patient factors36, among which the management of patients’ nutritional status is an important factor affecting POPFs.

Currently, there are numerous preoperative nutritional assessment methods; however, selecting an appropriate and effective approach is crucial for optimizing preoperative preparation37. Given that all patients undergoing MIPD routinely undergo abdominal imaging, including CT and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), sarcopenia can be readily assessed during these procedures.

In this study, the incidence of malnutrition in MIPD patients was 84.1%, compared with other studies8, the incidence of malnutrition before pancreatic malignant tumors was 75.7%. Moreover, the incidence of malnutrition was notably higher in patients with tumors located in the pancreatic head. In addition, despite the average body mass index (BMI) of patients being 22.7, indicating no apparent underweight status, the sarcopenia diagnosis rate reached 32.9%. This finding suggests that sarcopenia is not confined to underweight individuals, a significant number of Chinese patients may present with sarcopenic obesity (SO)38. Sarcopenia was strongly associated with postoperative morbidity/mortality (P = 0.002), which is consistent with the findings of Menozzi et al. We recommend preoperative sarcopenia assessments for patients with pancreaticoduodenal disease. Patients at nutritional risk or diagnosed with sarcopenia should receive preoperative nutritional support and exercise interventions39,40. Relevant studies41 have shown that exercise can increase muscle mass and function and reduce systemic inflammation, thereby reducing the symptoms of cachexia and sarcopenia.

The strength of this study lies in its novel description of how the co-existence of malnutrition, particularly sarcopenia, influences short-term outcomes in patients undergoing MIPD. However, there are some potential limitations to the study. First, this is a single-center study, and the small sample size may contribute to the broad confidence intervals, potentially affecting the accuracy. Given potential ethnic and physiological differences between Eastern and Western populations, future multicenter studies are essential to validate these findings. Moreover, concurrent screening for both conditions was not conducted. Further research should prioritise the development of simple and accessible screening tools. Finally, prospective studies are necessary to specifically target the optimisation of preoperative nutritional status.

Conclusions

The results of this study suggest that preoperative sarcopenia in patients with MIPD affects postoperative morbidity and major complications. Therefore, sarcopenia and nutritional status assessment should be part of routine preoperative procedures. Strengthening nutritional therapy for MIPD patients maybe can reduce the incidence of postoperative morbidities. Furthermore, in patients with MIPD, proper and appropriate identification and management of CRM is a key clinical concern, and in particular, assessment of sarcopenia with CT should also be performed.

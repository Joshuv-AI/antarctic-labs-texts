The derived ∞ \infty -category of Frobenius modules
Klaus Mattis, Timo Weiß

Abstract
We prove that for X X a quasi-compact 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -scheme with affine diagonal (e.g. X X quasi-compact and separated) there is a t-exact equivalence 𝒟 ⁡ ( Frob ⁡ ( QCoh ⁡ ( X ) , F ∗ ) ) → Frob ⁡ ( 𝒟 ⁡ ( QCoh ⁡ ( X ) ) , 𝒟 ⁡ ( F ∗ ) ) \mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))\to\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}(F_{*})) of stable ∞ \infty -categories. Here, Frob ⁡ ( − , − ) \operatorname{Frob}(-,-) denotes the ∞ \infty -category of generalized Frobenius modules as introduced in [ MW24 ] . This generalizes our result from [ MW24 ] , where we proved the above for regular Noetherian 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -schemes. As a byproduct we prove that the derived ∞ \infty -category of Frobenius (and Cartier) modules satisfies Zariski descent.

1 Introduction

A crucial aspect of algebraic geometry over a field of positive characteristic
p>0
is the presence of the Frobenius endomorphism.
In particular, the study of modules with an action of the Frobenius has lead to deep structural results.

If
X
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme, then a (quasi-coherent)
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module with a left action of the absolute Frobenius
F\colon X\to X
is called a Frobenius module.
Equivalently, a Frobenius module is given by a pair
(M,\kappa_{M})
of an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
M

and an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism
\kappa_{M}\colon M\to F_{*}M
.
Examples of Frobenius modules include the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and local cohomology modules,
both equipped with their natural left action by the Frobenius.
They have been used to prove important finiteness results, cf.
[
HS77
,
Lyu97
]
.
Moreover, Frobenius modules are related via a Riemann–Hilbert-type correspondence to
p
-torsion étale sheaves,
cf.
[
EK04
,
BL17
,
BP09
]
.

There is also a dual notion of Cartier modules, which are related to Frobenius modules via Grothendieck–Serre duality by a result of Baudin
[
Bau25
, Theorem 4.2.7]
.
They are (quasi-coherent)
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules with a right action of the Frobenius. Their category was first considered by Anderson in
[
And00
]
and more thoroughly studied by Blickle and Böckle in
[
BB11
,
BB13
]
.
Both Frobenius modules and Cartier modules are important tools in positive characteristic commutative algebra and algebraic geometry,
and have been studied by many authors
[
LS01
,
Sch09
,
Pat16
,
HP16
]
.

In our previous article
[
MW24
]
, we introduced an
\infty
-categorical framework
for Cartier and Frobenius modules.
Recall that for any (
\infty
-)category
\mathcal{C}
with an endofunctor
G\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
,
we defined
\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{C},G)\coloneqq\mathrm{LEq}(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{C}},G)
as the lax equalizer
of the identity with
G
, cf.
[
MW24
, Definition 2.4]
, and similarly

\mathrm{Cart}(\mathcal{C},G)\coloneqq\mathrm{LEq}(G,\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{C}})
.
If
X
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme for some prime
p
,

\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
and
G=F_{*}
is the pushforward along the absolute Frobenius,
one recovers the classical categories of Frobenius and Cartier modules on
X
, see the explanation above or e.g.
[
BL17
, Remark 1.3.2]

and
[
BB11
, Definition 2.1]
for definitions of these categories.

In the following, we write
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\in\mathcal{P}r^{L}_{st}

for the (presentable stable) derived
\infty
-category of a Grothendieck abelian category
\mathcal{A}
.
In
[
MW24
]
we proved the following:

Theorem

(
[
MW24
, Corollaries 6.2 and 6.3]
)
.

Let
X
be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme. Then there is a canonical t-exact equivalence of presentable stable
\infty
-categories

\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Cart}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Cart}(\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}(F_{*})).

If
X
is moreover regular Noetherian, then there is a canonical t-exact equivalence of presentable stable
\infty
-categories

\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Frob}(\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}(F_{*})).

Note that for this theorem to hold true, it is crucial to work with the derived
\infty
-category.
In fact, the proclaimed equivalences in the theorem do not hold if we replace the derived
\infty
-category by the ordinary derived category
h\mathcal{D}(-)
:
In this case, the target categories would not even admit a triangulated structure, as the category
\operatorname{Fun}({\Delta^{1}},{h\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X))})
does not do so.

We needed the assumption that
X
is regular Noetherian so that
the Frobenius
F
is flat by Kunz’ theorem
[
Kun69
, Theorem 2.1]
,
and hence
F^{*}
is an exact functor. If
X
is arbitrary,
then this is no longer the case, and the proof strategy of
[
MW24
]

does no longer work.
The goal of this paper is a version of the above result,
where we essentially remove the regularity and Noetherian hypothesis.
For this, we need the following definition:

Definition
.

Let
X
be a scheme. We say that
X
is
geometric

if
X
is quasi-compact and has affine diagonal (the latter is also called

semi-separated
in the literature).

Example
.

Since closed immersions are affine, and affine morphisms are quasi-compact,
we see that quasi-compact separated schemes are geometric,
and geometric schemes are qcqs.
In particular, any affine scheme is geometric.

Theorem A

(
5.7
)
.

Let
X
be a geometric
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
Then the canonical map

\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))\to\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}(F_{*}))

is a t-exact equivalence of presentable stable
\infty
-categories.

Outline of the proof

We now explain how we prove
A
.
Both sides of the proclaimed equivalence are left-complete (this follows from
5.2
,
5.1
and
5.4
),
hence (using
5.6
) it suffices to show the following theorem:

Theorem B

(
4.16
)
.

Let
X
be a geometric
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
Then the canonical map

\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))\to\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}^{+}(F_{*}))

is a t-exact equivalence of stable
\infty
-categories.
Here,
\mathcal{D}^{+}
denotes the full subcategory of bounded above objects.

In
4.12
and (the proof of)
4.16
we show that both sides of the equivalence are Zariski sheaves in
X
,
and that there is a canonical morphism between them, cf.
4.15
.
At this point, we heavily use that we are working with the derived
\infty
-category instead of the ordinary derived category.
Hence, it suffices to prove the statement for
X=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
an affine scheme.
On affines, it boils down to the Schwede–Shipley recognition theorem for stable module categories
[
Lur17
, Theorem 7.1.2.1]
,
which is an
\infty
-categorical version of Gabriel’s theorem
[
Gab62
, Corollaire V.1.1]
:
If one has a stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
with a compact projective generator
M
,
then
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to the stable
\infty
-category of
A
-module spectra,
where
A=\operatorname{End}(M)
is the endomorphism ring spectrum of
M
.
Hence, in order to show
B
on affines,
it therefore suffices to show that both categories admit a compact projective generator
(for this see
3.18
and
3.22
),
and that the endomorphism ring spectra of the generators are equivalent (
3.27
).
In fact, as expected, the endomorphism ring spectra on both sides
are equivalent to the discrete (non-commutative) ring
R[F]^{\operatorname{op}}
of e.g.
[
BP09
, Definition 3.2.1]
.

Remark
.

Note that we cannot directly show that both sides of
A

are Zariski sheaves, as we a priori do not know that they are left-complete.
Indeed, for the proof of the left-completeness of
\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))
,
we need to know that
\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*})
satisfies a weaker version of axiom

\mathrm{AB}4^{*}
. In order to show this, we first establish

B
, as this axiom only depends on the bounded above objects
of the derived
\infty
-category, and hence can then be deduced from the fact that already

\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
satisfies it.

Zariski descent

As already mentioned above, one step in the proof of the main theorem is a reduction to affine schemes
via Zariski descent. In particular, we get the following result:

Theorem C

(
4.12
)
.

Let
X
be a geometric
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme and write
X_{\operatorname{zar}}
for the small (quasi-compact) Zariski site of
X
, cf.
4.2
.
Then
\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(-),F_{*}))
defines a Zariski sheaf on
X_{\operatorname{zar}}
with values in
\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}
.

As explained above, all involved
\infty
-categories are already left-complete.
Hence, one also gets via an analogous proof (using that then
already
\mathcal{D}(-)
preserves certain limits as
in the proof of
4.10
,
see
[
HM24
, Proposition A.4.23]
) the following:

Theorem D
.

Let
X
be a geometric
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
Then
\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(-),F_{*}))
defines a Zariski sheaf on
X_{\operatorname{zar}}

with values in
\mathcal{P}r^{L}_{st}
.

Essentially dualizing the proof, we also get

Corollary E
.

Let
X
be a geometric
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
Then
\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Cart}(\operatorname{QCoh}(-),F_{*}))
defines a Zariski sheaf on
X_{\operatorname{zar}}
with values in
\mathcal{P}r^{L}_{st}
.

Remark
.

One can use similar techniques to show stronger descent statements for the derived

\infty
-categories of Frobenius and Cartier modules.

Use of the
\infty
-category of spectra

As explained above, our proof reduces to the affine case
X=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
,
and then identifies both sides with the category of
R[F]
-module spectra,
i.e. working over the absolute base
\mathbb{S}\in\operatorname{Sp}
.
This is mostly for convenience of reference, as the
\infty
-categorical Schwede–Shipley
theorem is formulated in this setting.
Since all our categories are (limits of) derived categories of rings and schemes,
they are all
\mathbb{Z}
-linear. Hence, one could avoid the
\infty
-category of spectra
by proving a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear version of Schwede–Shipley,
which then would identify both sides of the equivalence with the category

\operatorname{Mod}_{R[F]}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z}))

(which in turn is of course equivalent to
\operatorname{Mod}_{R[F]}(\operatorname{Sp})
,
since the
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-morphism
\mathbb{S}\to R[F]
canonically factors through
H\mathbb{Z}
,
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z})=\operatorname{Mod}_{H\mathbb{Z}}(\operatorname{Sp})
).

is functorial

\infty

Proposition 2.5
.

The functor
\operatorname{Frob}(-,-)\colon\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty})\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}
from

preserves limits.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Frob}(-,-)
is itself a limit of functors,
it suffices to show that
U_{\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}}
and
\operatorname{Fun}({\Delta^{1}},{-})
preserve limits.
The first statement is
[
MW24
, Proposition 2.6 (c)]
,
and the second holds e.g. because
\operatorname{Fun}({\Delta^{1}},{-})
is right adjoint to the functor
-\times\Delta^{1}\colon\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

be a morphism in
\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty})
.
If the underlying functor
f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is fully faithful,
then also the induced functor
\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{C},F)\to\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D},G)
is fully faithful.

Proof.

The induced functor is the limit of the fully faithful functors

{f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}}
,

and
f_{*}\colon\operatorname{Fun}({\Delta^{1}},{\mathcal{C}})\to\operatorname{Fun}({\Delta^{1}},{\mathcal{D}})
,

Lemma 3.17

4 Zariski descent of Frobenius modules

In this section, we show that the functor
\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(-),F_{*}))

defines a Zariski sheaf on the small (quasi-compact) Zariski site of a geometric scheme, cf.
4.12
.
Combining this with our results from the previous section, we prove
B
, cf.
4.16
.

We start by recalling that the derived
\infty
-category is functorial in Grothen-dieck abelian categories:

Proposition 4.1
.

There is a functor
\mathcal{D}\colon\operatorname{Groth}\to\mathcal{P}r^{L}_{st}

which sends a Grothen-dieck abelian category to its derived
\infty
-category,
and a colimit-preserving left exact functor
to its derived functor.

Similarly, there is a functor
\mathcal{D}^{+}\colon\operatorname{Groth}\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}

that is the restriction of
\mathcal{D}
to bounded above objects.

Proof.

Write
\mathrm{PreSt}^{L}\subset\mathcal{P}r^{L}
for the full subcategory consisting of those
presentable categories that are prestable, cf.
[
Lur18
, Definition C.1.2.1]
.
We first construct a functor

\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}\colon\operatorname{Groth}\to\mathrm{PreSt}^{L}

which sends a Grothendieck abelian category
\mathcal{A}

to the presentable prestable category
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})_{\geq 0}
,
i.e. the connective part of the derived
\infty
-category.
Recall from
[
Lur18
, Proposition C.5.4.5]

that there is a fully faithful functor
M\colon\operatorname{Groth}\to\operatorname{Groth}^{\mathrm{lex},\mathrm{sep}}_{\infty}
,
where the right-hand category denotes the category of separated Grothendieck prestable
categories, whose morphisms are left exact colimit-preserving functors.
We let
\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}
be the composition of
M
with the canonical forgetful functor
\operatorname{Groth}^{\mathrm{lex},\mathrm{sep}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{PreSt}^{L}
.
It now follows from
[
Lur18
, Propositions C.5.4.5 and C.5.3.2]

that
\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}
sends a Grothendieck abelian category
\mathcal{A}
to the connective part of the derived
\infty
-category of
\mathcal{A}
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a Grothendieck abelian category,
then its derived
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
is the stabilization of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})_{\geq 0}

by
[
Lur18
, Remark C.1.2.10]
since the t-structure on the derived
\infty
-category of
\mathcal{A}
is right-complete.
Hence, we can define
\mathcal{D}
as the composition of
\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}
with the stabilization functor

\operatorname{Sp}(-)\colon\mathcal{P}r^{L}\to\mathcal{P}r^{L}_{st}
.

To get the description on morphisms, note that for each morphism
F
in
\operatorname{Groth}
the functor

\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}(F)
is left exact and colimit-preserving,
and hence preserves categorical
n
-truncated and
n
-connective objects.
Thus, it is t-exact on the stabilization.
Therefore, by the universal property of the derived
\infty
-category
[
MW24
, Proposition A.2]
,
it suffices to show that the restriction of
\mathcal{D}(F)
to the heart is given by
F
,
which is obvious from the above.

To get
\mathcal{D}^{+}
, one uses that all involved derived functors are t-exact,
and thus everything restricts to bounded above objects.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a qcqs
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
We write
X_{\operatorname{zar}}
for the (small) subcategory of
\operatorname{Sch}_{/X}

spanned by those schemes
Y\to X
that can be written as

Y=\sqcup_{i\in I}Y_{i}\to X
, where
I
is finite,
and
Y_{i}\to X
is the inclusion of a quasi-compact open subset of
X
.
In particular, since every
U\in X_{\operatorname{zar}}
is a finite disjoint union of quasi-compact open subset of the qcqs
X
, it is clear that

U
is moreover quasi-separated (as open immersions are quasi-separated
[
Sta25
,
Tag 01L7
]
,
and quasi-separated morphisms are stable under composition).
We equip
X_{\operatorname{zar}}
with the Grothendieck topology where covers are
given by jointly surjective families
\{U_{i}\to U\}_{i\in I}
.
To see that this in fact defines a Grothendieck topology, we have to check that
X_{\operatorname{zar}}

is closed under pullbacks. This is true since the intersection of quasi-compact
opens is quasi-compact since
X
was assumed to be quasi-separated.

Remark 4.3
.

This version of the Zariski site is nonstandard.
Since affines are quasi-compact,
it is still true that equivalences of sheaves can be checked on affine opens if the scheme is geometric, see
4.5
and
4.6
below.
If the scheme is not geometric, in order to reduce to affine schemes, one has to work with hypercovers instead
(as the intersections of affines are no longer guaranteed to be affine, and so the Čech nerve
does not only consist of affines).
Therefore, it is not clear that our arguments would work with non-geometric schemes,
as one has to prove hyperdescent of the relevant module categories.
This is one of the reasons why we choose to work with geometric schemes in the following.
We use this version of the site to avoid problems with quasi-coherence and flat base change.
Indeed, e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 02KH
]

needs the morphism
f
to be qcqs.
Since in our case
f
will always be one of the open subset inclusions
U\hookrightarrow V

in
X_{\operatorname{zar}}
, this follows from

[
Sta25
,
Tag 01KV
]
and

[
Sta25
,
Tag 03GI
]

since
U
and
V
were assumed to be qcqs.

Remark 4.4
.

If
X
is a qcqs scheme such that any open subset of
X
is quasi-compact
(e.g.
X
Noetherian), then
X_{\operatorname{zar}}
is
(the finite disjoint union completion of) the usual small Zariski site of
X
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
X
be a scheme. We say that
X
is
geometric

if
X
is quasi-compact and has affine diagonal (the latter is also called

semi-separated
in the literature).

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X
be a geometric scheme and let
\mathcal{C}
be a complete
\infty
-category.
Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\colon X^{\operatorname{op}}_{\operatorname{zar}}\to\mathcal{C}
be Zariski sheaves,
and let
f\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
be a morphism.
Suppose that for every affine open
U\subset X

the morphism
f_{U}\colon\mathcal{F}(U)\to\mathcal{G}(U)
is an equivalence.
Then
f
is an equivalence.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i}
be a finite Zariski cover of
X
, where the
U_{i}
are affine.
Since
X
has affine diagonal, any intersection of the
U_{i}

is also affine.
In particular, the Čech nerve
\mathcal{U}^{\bullet}
of the cover consists only of affine schemes.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is a sheaf,
\mathcal{F}(X)\cong\operatorname{lim}_{\Delta}\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{U}^{\bullet})
,
and similarly for
\mathcal{G}
.
By assumption,
f
induces an equivalence
on the limit diagram and therefore also on the limit.
∎

We now show that certain constructions like quasi-coherent sheaves satisfy Zariski descent.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let

be a qcqs
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
There is a functor
\operatorname{QCoh}(-)\colon X^{\operatorname{op}}_{\operatorname{zar}}\to\operatorname{Groth}

which on objects sends an open

to the category
of quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-modules.
Similarly, the associated morphism to an inclusion

of open subsets of

is the restriction of quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{V}
-modules to

.

\operatorname{QCoh}(-)
is a Zariski sheaf.

Proof.

We let
\operatorname{QCoh}(-)\colon X^{\operatorname{op}}_{\operatorname{zar}}\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}
be the straightening of the
cartesian fibration from
[
Sta25
,
Tag 03YL
]
(restricted to
X^{\operatorname{op}}_{\operatorname{zar}}
).
Note that by construction, we have that
\operatorname{QCoh}(U)
is just the Grothendieck abelian category of
quasi-coherent sheaves on

.
Moreover, on inclusions of open subsets

,
\operatorname{QCoh}(U\hookrightarrow V)
is the restriction of quasi-coherent sheaves.
Note that these restriction functors are exact as

is an open immersion and so in particular flat.
Furthermore, the restriction functors are also colimit-preserving as they have right adjoints
given by pushforward (note that here we need that the morphisms

are qcqs by
4.3

In particular, we see that everything factors through
\operatorname{Groth}
.
That the functor
\operatorname{QCoh}(-)
is a Zariski sheaf follows from

The commutativity of the outer solid diagram can be deduced in the exact same way
as the commutativity of the diagram in
[
MW24
, Theorem 5.1]
, see the paragraph directly behind the statement of the theorem.
As everything in this diagram is natural in
(\mathcal{A},F)
, the same is true for
\Phi_{(\mathcal{A},F)}
.
Moreover, the t-exactness of
\Phi_{(\mathcal{A},F)}
follows immediately from the definition of the induced t-structure
[
MW24
, Proposition 3.3]
,
and the fact that
\mathcal{D}^{+}(U)
is t-exact.

It is clear from the construction that on affine
U=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
the functor
\Phi_{U}

is exactly the restriction of the equivalence from
3.28
.
∎

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
X
be a geometric
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
Then the canonical morphism

\Phi_{X}\colon\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}^{+}(F_{*}))

from
4.15
is a t-exact equivalence.

Proof.

By
4.15
,
there is even a morphism of presheaves on
X_{\operatorname{zar}}

\Phi\colon\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(-)^{\operatorname{frob}}))\to\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{D}^{+})(\operatorname{QCoh}(-)^{\operatorname{frob}})),

that is sectionwise t-exact. We have to show that this morphism is an equivalence on global sections.
The source of
\Phi
is a Zariski sheaf by
4.12
.
Similarly, the target of
\Phi
is also a Zariski sheaf:
Indeed, the composition
\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{D}^{+})\,\circ\,\operatorname{QCoh}(-)^{\operatorname{frob}}
is a Zariski sheaf by
4.14
,
and the functor
\operatorname{Frob}(-,-)\colon\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty})\to\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty}
preserves limits by
2.5
.
Thus, it follows that also the composition
\operatorname{Frob}(-,-)\,\circ\,\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{D}^{+})\,\circ\,\operatorname{QCoh}(-)^{\operatorname{frob}}
is a Zariski sheaf.

Hence, in order to see that
\Phi
is an equivalence on global sections,
it suffices to see that it is an equivalence on affine opens
U\subset X
, cf.
4.6
.
Now note that this case was shown in
3.28
(as we have identified the morphism
\Phi_{U}
with the restriction of the morphism
in
loc. cit.
by
4.15
).
∎

5 Left-completeness on geometric schemes

In this section, we show that the derived categories of quasi-coherent modules and Frobenius modules, respectively,
over a geometric scheme are already left-complete. This will imply our main theorem, cf.
5.7
.

Recall from
[
Ant18
, Definition 8.3 (d)]
the axiom
\mathrm{AB}4^{*}n(\omega)

for Grothendieck abelian categories, this is a weaker variant of Grothendieck’s axiom
\mathrm{AB}4^{*}
.
If a Grothendieck abelian category
\mathcal{A}
satisfies
\mathrm{AB}4^{*}n(\omega)

for some
n\geq 0
, then
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
is left-complete by

[
Ant18
, Proposition 8.14]
. Hence, we get the following:

Lemma 5.1

(
[
Pos25
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
X
be a geometric scheme.
There exists an
n\geq 0
such that
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
is
\mathrm{AB}4^{*}n(\omega)
.
In particular, the derived
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X))
is left-complete.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X
be a geometric
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
There exists an
n\geq 0
such that
\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*})
is
\mathrm{AB}4^{*}n(\omega)
.
In particular, the derived
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))
is left-complete.

Proof.

By
5.1
there is an
n\geq 0
such that
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
is
\mathrm{AB}4^{*}n(\omega)
.
It is enough to show that
\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*})
is also
\mathrm{AB}4^{*}n(\omega)
.
Let
(M_{k})_{k}
be a countable family in
\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*})
.
We have to show that the product
\prod_{k}HM_{k}\in\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))_{\geq-n}
.
This statement only depends on the bounded above part
\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))
.
Hence, using
4.16
, we may work in the
\infty
-category
\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}^{+}(F_{*}))
.
Now, by definition of the t-structure, connectivity
may be checked after applying the limit-preserving t-exact functor
U\colon\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}^{+}(F_{*}))\to\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X))
.
But
U\prod_{k}HM_{k}\cong\prod_{k}HUM_{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X))_{\geq-n}

as
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
is
\mathrm{AB}4^{*}n(\omega)
.
∎

To show that
\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}(F_{*}))
is left-complete as well,
we need the following result:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a presentable stable
\infty
-category with a t-structure.
Then
\mathcal{D}
is left-complete if and only if

(a)

X\to\operatorname{lim}_{n}\tau_{\leq n}X
is an equivalence for every
X\in\mathcal{D}
, and

(b)

\tau_{\leq k}\operatorname{lim}_{n}X_{n}\to X_{k}
is an equivalence for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and every

(X_{n})_{n}\in\operatorname{lim}_{n}\mathcal{D}_{\leq n}
(i.e.
X_{n}\in\mathcal{D}_{\leq n}
for every
n
,
together with compatible equivalences
\tau_{\leq m}X_{n}\cong X_{m}
for all
m\leq n
).

Proof.

By definition,
\mathcal{D}
is left-complete if and only if the canonical functor

\mathcal{D}\to\operatorname{lim}_{n}\mathcal{D}_{\leq n}
is an equivalence.
This functor has a right adjoint, given by sending
(X_{n})_{n}
to its limit
\operatorname{lim}_{n}X_{n}\in\mathcal{D}
.
The two maps given in the statement of the lemma are exactly the unit and counit of this adjunction.
Hence,
\mathcal{D}
is left-complete if and only if those maps are equivalences.
∎

As an immediate corollary, we obtain:

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
U\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
be a t-exact conservative limit-preserving functor
of presentable stable
\infty
-categories with t-structures.
If the t-structure on
\mathcal{E}
is left-complete, then the t-structure on
\mathcal{D}
is also left-complete.

Using
5.1
, we can apply this result to
U\colon\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}(F_{*}))\to\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X))
,
which is conservative and limit-preserving by
[
MW24
, Corollary 2.8 (b) and (c)]
,
and t-exact by definition of the induced t-structure
[
MW24
, Proposition 3.3]
.
Hence, we get:

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
X
be a geometric
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
The induced t-structure on
\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}(F_{*}))

is left-complete.

Next, we state and prove our main theorem. For a geometric
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme
X
, using the universal property of the pullback,
we let
\Phi_{X}
be the dashed morphism in the following diagram, where we write for notational convenience
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{A},F_{*}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}),\mathcal{D}(F_{*}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Fun}({\Delta^{1}},{\mathcal{A}}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Fun}({\Delta^{1}},{\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\times\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\hbox to0.0pt{.\hss}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(\kappa)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}(U)}

\lrcorner

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\operatorname{id},\mathcal{D}(F_{*}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(s,t)}

Here, the bottom left functor is the functor from the discussion before
[
MW24
, Theorem 5.1]
.
As in the last sections, the commutativity of the outer solid diagram can be deduced in the exact same way
as the commutativity of the diagram in
[
MW24
, Theorem 5.1]
, see the paragraph directly behind the statement of the theorem.

To prove the main theorem, we also need the following lemma about left-complete stable
\infty
-categories:

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
be a t-exact functor
of presentable stable
\infty
-categories with t-structures such that both
\infty
-categories are left-complete.
If the induced functor
F^{+}\colon\mathcal{D}^{+}\to\mathcal{E}^{+}
is an equivalence,
then also
F\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
is an equivalence.

Proof.

This is immediate since
\mathcal{D}_{\leq n}\cong\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\leq n}\xrightarrow[F]{\simeq}\mathcal{E}^{+}_{\leq n}\cong\mathcal{E}_{\leq n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
and
\mathcal{D}\cong\operatorname{lim}_{n}\mathcal{D}_{\leq n}
, and similarly for
\mathcal{E}
.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
X
be a geometric
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme. Then the functor

\Phi_{X}\colon\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}(F_{*}))

described above is a t-exact equivalence,
where we equip the target with the induced t-structure from
[
MW24
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Proof.

The t-exactness of the functor follows immediately from the definition of the induced t-structure and the t-exactness of
\mathcal{D}(U)
.
Thus,
\Phi_{X}
restricts to a functor

\Phi_{X}^{+}\colon\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))\to\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}^{+}(F_{*})).

By construction,
\Phi_{X}^{+}
is the same functor as in
4.16
,
where we also showed that it is an equivalence.
Note that by definition of the induced t-structure on
\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}(F_{*}))

the category of bounded above objects of this category is exactly given by
\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}^{+}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}^{+}(F_{*}))
.
Hence,
5.6
implies that the functor

\Phi_{X}\colon\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Frob}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X),F_{*}))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\operatorname{Frob}(\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X)),\mathcal{D}(F_{*}))

is also an equivalence, as both source and target are left-complete by
5.2
and
5.5
.
∎

Appendix A
Compact projective generators

In the literature, there are a variety of notions of compact (projective) generators
in various settings, e.g. in abelian categories or in stable
\infty
-categories.
The goal of this appendix is to translate between some of those notions
so that we may use different results from the literature.

Definition A.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Grothendieck abelian category and
P\in\mathcal{A}
an object.
We say that
P
is a
compact projective generator

if
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{A}}({P},{-})
commutes with sifted colimits and is conservative.

This definition immediately implies the following:

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Grothendieck abelian category and
P\in\mathcal{A}
a compact projective generator.
Then
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{A}}({P},{-})
commutes with all colimits.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{A}}({P},{-})
preserves sifted colimits by assumption,
it suffices to show that
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{A}}({P},{-})
preserves finite coproducts.
This is clear since finite coproducts are finite products in abelian categories.
∎

We have a similar definition in the stable situation.

Definition A.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a presentable stable
\infty
-category and
P\in\mathcal{D}
an object.
We say that
P
is a
stable compact generator

if
\operatorname{map}_{\mathcal{D}}({P},{-})
(the mapping spectrum) commutes with filtered colimits and is conservative.

Example A.4
.

Let
R
be an associative ring.
Then
R\in\operatorname{Mod}_{R}^{\heartsuit}
is a compact projective generator,
and
R\in\operatorname{Mod}_{R}
is a stable compact generator.

Our goal in this section is to prove the following proposition:

Proposition A.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Grothendieck abelian category, and
P\in\mathcal{A}

a compact projective generator.
Then
HP\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
is a stable compact generator.

For the proof, we need the following two lemmas.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Grothendieck abelian category, and
P\in\mathcal{A}

a compact projective generator.
Then every object
A\in\mathcal{A}
admits a surjection
\oplus_{I}P\twoheadrightarrow A

for some (small) set
I
.

Proof.

Consider the canonical map

\Psi\colon\bigoplus_{f\colon P\to A}P\to A.

We claim that this map is surjective, i.e.
\mathrm{coker}(\Psi)\cong 0
.
Since
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{A}}({P},{-})
is conservative and preserves cokernels (by
A.2
),
it suffices to show that the induced map of abelian groups

\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{A}}({P},{\bigoplus_{f\colon P\to A}P})\to\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{A}}({P},{A})

is surjective.
This is clear as any
f\colon P\to A

is the image of the inclusion of the
f
-th summand.
∎

Remark A.7
.

The last lemma shows that a compact projective generator
is in particular a generator in the sense of Grothendieck, see

[
Gro57
, Proposition 1.9.1]
.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Grothendieck abelian category, and
P\in\mathcal{A}

a compact projective generator.
Then there is an equivalence
\Psi\colon\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\operatorname{Mod}_{R}^{\heartsuit}
,
where
R\coloneqq\operatorname{End}(P)
is the endomorphism ring of
P
,
and
\Psi(P)=R
.

Proof.

Note that
P
is projective (in the abelian sense, i.e.
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{A}}({P},{-})
commutes with cokernels)
by
A.2
.
Hence, in view of
A.6
, this follows from Gabriel’s theorem
[
Gab62
, Corollaire V.1.1]
.
∎

Proof of
A.5
.

By
A.8
, we may assume that
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{Mod}_{R}^{\heartsuit}

for some associative ring
R
, and
P=R
.
In particular, we know
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\cong\operatorname{Mod}_{R}
.
Our goal is to show that
\operatorname{map}_{\operatorname{Mod}_{R}}({R},{-})
is conservative and preserves filtered colimits.
This functor can be identified with the canonical forgetful functor
\operatorname{Mod}_{R}\to\operatorname{Sp}
,
cf.
3.5
.
Hence, the result follows from
3.3
.
∎

We end the section by proving a stability property of generators.

Lemma A.9
.

Let
L\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a left adjoint functor
of
\infty
-categories
such that the right adjoint
R\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}

preserves colimits and is conservative.

(a)

If both
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are Grothendieck abelian,
then
L
preserves compact projective generators.

(b)

If both
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are presentable stable,
then
L
preserves stable compact generators.

Proof.

For the abelian case, let
P\in\mathcal{C}
be a compact projective generator.
The statement then follows from the equivalence
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{D}}({LP},{-})\cong\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}({P},{R-})

and the properties of
R
.
In the stable case, note that the adjunction is automatically spectrally enriched.
∎

Transformers and Their Roles as Time Series Foundation Models
Dennis Wu, Yihan He, Yuan Cao, Jianqing Fan, Han Liu

Abstract
We give a comprehensive analysis of transformers as time series foundation models, focusing on their approximation and generalization capabilities. First, we demonstrate that there exist transformers that fit an autoregressive model on input univariate time series via gradient descent. We then analyze MOIRAI [ 1 ] , a multivariate time series foundation model capable of handling an arbitrary number of covariates. We prove that it is capable of automatically fitting autoregressive models with an arbitrary number of covariates, offering insights into its design and empirical success. For generalization, we establish bounds for pretraining when the data satisfies Dobrushin’s condition. Experiments support our theoretical findings, highlighting the efficacy of transformers as time series foundation models.

The contribution of this paper is threefold:

•

From an algorithmic approximation perspective, we prove the existence of a transformer capable of fitting an autoregressive (

Extending this to the multi-variate setting, we show that a MOIRAI transformer can automatically adjust the dimensionality of the

Our approximation results not only explain the strong performance of modern models across diverse datasets but also justify the design of MOIRAI.

•

We present the first pretraining generalization bound for time series foundation models.

Specifically, when pretraining MOIRAI on

•

Our experimental results match our theories by showing that the prediction error of transformers reduces as the input time series length increases, corresponding to our approximation result.

Problem Setup

This section describes our problem setup.

2.1
Transformers

We consider a sequence of
N
input vectors
\{h_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
, where
\bm{H}\coloneqq\left[h_{1},\cdots,h_{N}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times N}
.
Given any
\bm{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times N}
, we define the attention layer as follows.

Definition 2.1

(Attention layer)
.

A self-attention layer with
M
heads is denoted as
\text{Attn}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\theta}_{0}}(\cdot)
with parameters
\bm{\theta}_{0}=\{(\bm{V}_{m}),(\bm{Q}_{m}),(\bm{K}_{m})\}_{m\in[M]}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}
.
The self-attention layer processes any given input sequence
\bm{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times N}
as

\displaystyle\text{Attn}_{\bm{\theta}_{0}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\left(\bm{H}\right)\coloneqq\bm{H}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{M}(\bm{V}_{m}\bm{H})\cdot

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\left(\bm{Q}_{m}\bm{H}\right)^{\top}\left(\bm{K}_{m}\bm{H}\right)\right),

where
\sigma\coloneqq t\mapsto\text{ReLU}(t)/N
.

Any-variate Attention.

Next, we introduce the any-variate attention, where
[
1
]
uses it to replace the standard attention in transformers.
The any-variate attention introduces two learnable variables: Attention Bias
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
for disambiguation between variates.

Definition 2.2

(Any-variate Attention.)
.

An any-variate attention layer with
M
heads is denoted as
\text{Attn}_{\bm{\theta}_{1}}(\cdot)
with parameters
\bm{\theta}_{1}=\{(\bm{V}_{m}),(\bm{Q}_{m}),(\bm{K}_{m}),(u_{m}^{1}),(u_{m}^{2})\}_{m\in[M]}
.
With any input
H\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times N}
, we have

\displaystyle\text{Attn}_{\bm{\theta}_{1}}

\displaystyle\left(\bm{H}\right)\coloneqq\bm{H}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{M}(\bm{V}_{m}\bm{H})\times

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\left(\bm{Q}_{m}\bm{H}\right)^{\top}\left(\bm{K}_{m}\bm{H}\right)+u_{m}^{1}*\bm{U}+u_{m}^{2}*\bar{\bm{U}}\right),

where
\sigma\coloneqq t\mapsto\text{ReLU}(t)/N
,
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is a block diagonal matrix with block size
T\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, such that each block consists of
1
s,
\bar{\bm{U}}=\bm{I}-\bm{U}
, and
*
denotes a constant multiply to all entries of a matrix.

Remark 2.3
.

In
[
1
]
, the attention score is calculated with the RoPE embedding
[
18
]
:

\sigma\left(\left(\bm{Q}_{m}\bm{H}\right)^{\top}\bm{R}\left(\bm{K}_{m}\bm{H}\right)+u_{m}^{1}*\bm{U}+u_{m}^{2}*\bar{\bm{U}}\right).

We omit the notation of rotary matrix
\bm{R}
as it is not learnable and is invertible and thus merged into
\bm{Q},\bm{K}
in our analysis.

Definition 2.4

(MLP Layer)
.

We denote an MLP layer with hidden state dimension
D^{\prime}
as
\text{MLP}_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot)
with parameters
\bm{\theta}_{2}=(\bm{W}_{1},\bm{W}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{D^{\prime}\times D}\times\mathbb{R}^{D\times D^{\prime}}
.
The MLP layer processes any given input sequence
\bm{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times N}
as

\text{MLP}_{\bm{\theta}_{2}}(\bm{H})\coloneqq\bm{H}+\bm{W}_{2}\sigma(\bm{W}_{1}\bm{H}).

Finally, we define a transformer with
L\geq 1
layers, each consisting of any-variate attention and an MLP layer.

Definition 2.5

(MOIRAI Transformer)
.

We define the
L
-layer MOIRAI transformer
[
1
]
,
\text{TF}_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot)
, as

\text{TF}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{H})=\text{MLP}_{\bm{\theta}_{2}^{L}}\left(\text{Attn}_{\bm{\theta}_{1}^{L}}\left(\cdot\cdot\text{MLP}_{\bm{\theta}_{2}^{1}}\left(\text{Attn}_{\bm{\theta}_{1}^{1}}(\bm{H})\right)\right)\right).

Note that this transformer is equipped with any-variate attention instead of the standard attention.
For transformers with standard attention, we denote it as

\text{TF}_{\bm{\theta}}^{\dagger}(\cdot)
.

We use
\bm{\theta}
to denote the vectorization of all parameters in a transformer and super-index
\ell
to denote the parameter of the
\ell
-th layer.
Thus, the parameter of a transformer is defined by

\bm{\theta}=\left\{\left\{\left(\{\bm{Q}_{m}^{\ell},\bm{K}_{m}^{\ell},\bm{V}_{m}^{\ell},u_{m}^{1,\ell},u_{m}^{2,\ell}\}_{m\in[M]},\bm{W}_{1}^{\ell},\bm{W}_{2}^{\ell}\right)\right\}_{\ell\in[L]}\right\}.

We denote the “attention-only” transformers with
\bm{W}_{1}^{(\ell)},\bm{W}_{2}^{(\ell)}=0
, as
\text{TF}_{\bm{\theta}}^{0}(\cdot)
for shorthand.
We define the following norm of a MOIRAI transformer as

\displaystyle\|\bm{\theta}\|_{op}\coloneqq\max_{\ell\in[L]}

\displaystyle\Big\{\max_{m\in[M^{\ell}]}\Big\{\|\bm{Q}_{m}^{\ell}\|_{2},\|\bm{K}_{m}^{\ell}\|_{2},

\displaystyle|u_{m}^{1\ell}|,|u_{m}^{2\ell}|\Big\}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{M^{\ell}}\|\bm{V}_{m}^{\ell}\|_{2}+\|\bm{W}_{1}^{\ell}\|_{2}+\|\bm{W}_{2}^{\ell}\|_{2}\Big\},

where
M^{\ell}
is the number of heads of the
\ell
-th Attention layer.

2.2
Data Generation

Here, we first consider the case where we aim to find a multi-layered transformer that performs least squares regression via In-context learning (ICL).
Specifically, we assume our data is generated from an autoregressive process
\mathtt{AR}_{d}(q)
as follows, where
q,d
denotes the steps of lag and number of covariates, respectively.
Consider a sequence of data

\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times T}\coloneqq(\bm{x}_{1},\dots,\bm{x}_{T})
, where
\bm{x}_{t}=(x_{t}^{1},\cdots,x_{t}^{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Assuming our target (variate of interest) is in dimension

\mathtt{AR}_{d}(q)
process generates

as follows:

x_{t}^{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{i}^{j}\cdot x_{t-i}^{j}+\epsilon_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle\bm{w}^{j},\bm{x}_{t-q:t-1}^{j}\rangle+\epsilon_{t},

(2.1)

where
\epsilon_{t}\sim N(0,1)
,
a_{i}^{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
.
We denote the concatenation of all weights
\bm{w}^{\star}=(\bm{w}_{1},\cdots,\bm{w}^{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{qd}
.
We assume bounded features
\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{x}_{t-q:t-1}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{2}\leq B_{x}
, for all
t=1,\cdots,T
.
The first equation writes the
\mathtt{AR}
process in scalar form, and the second writes it in vector form.
In the following chapters, we will start by considering the uni-variate case (
\mathtt{AR}_{1}(q)
) and then move on to the multi-variate case (
\mathtt{AR}_{d}(q)
).

Problem Setup.

Given a function class
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{R}^{d\times T}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, our goal is to find a universal function
f\in\mathcal{F}
such that, given any time series generated from any arbitrary
\mathtt{AR}_{d}(q)
, its prediction error is bounded by some
\varepsilon\geq 0
, i.e.,

\left\lvert\left\lvert f(\tilde{\bm{x}})-x_{T}^{1}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{2}\leq\varepsilon,

where
\tilde{\bm{x}}
denotes the time series
\bm{x}
with
x_{T}^{1}
being masked.

Remark 2.6
.

In the appendix, we show that even when the autoregressive process follows some non-linear relationship, there still exists a universal
f
that predicts all non-linear AR process accurately.

3.2
Approximation Error of MOIRAI Transformer

In this subsection, we extend our results to the multivariate autoregressive process (

) and the encoding method of MOIRAI.
Note that in the multi-variate case, we only focus on MOIRAI as it is the only transformer-based model that is compatible with arbitrary number of covariates.
We start by introducing the any-variate encoding.

Any-Variate Encoding.

[
1
]

d
-dimensional time series,
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times T}
, into a
1
-dimensional sequence, i.e.,
\bm{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times Td}
.
This operation transforms time series with arbitrary number of covariates (

Following the flattening operation,
[
1
]
also proposes to add two types of indices into the input sequence: the time and variate ID.
We term the above operations as the any-variate encoding, which transforms a multivariate sequence
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times T}
, as follows:

\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}^{1}&\cdots&x_{T}^{1}\\
x_{1}^{2}&\cdots&x_{T}^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{1}^{d}&\cdots&x_{T}^{d}\end{bmatrix}\rightarrow\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}^{1}&\cdots&\pagecolor{cyan!20}x_{T}^{1}&\cdots&x_{1}^{d}&\cdots&x_{T}^{d}\\
\bm{p}_{1}&\cdots&\bm{p}_{T}&\cdots&\bm{p}_{1}&\cdots&\bm{p}_{T}\\
\bm{e}_{1}&\cdots&\bm{e}_{1}&\cdots&\bm{e}_{d}&\cdots&\bm{e}_{d}\end{bmatrix},

where
\bm{e}_{i}
is the variate index, a one-hot vector with

-th entry being

This is without loss of generality because the discrete-time and variate ID used in

]
can be easily transformed into a high-dimensional vector with the embedding layer.
Note that only the target variate has length
T

as it is our prediction target and will be masked as

Now we define the history matrix
\mathtt{A}_{i}(q)\in\mathbb{R}^{q+1\times T}

-th covariates

\mathtt{A}_{i}(q)_{\mu,\nu}\coloneqq x^{i}_{\nu-\mu+1},\quad\text{ for }\mu\in[d],\nu\in[q].

Intuitively, the above operation performs the same operation in Lemma

In this section, we investigate the generalization bound of pretraining transformer-based time series foundation models.
This section will focus on learning MOIRAI on multi-variate time series, one can easily adapt our proofs into learning uni-variate time series with standard transformers.

Let
\pi
be a meta distribution, and each distribution drawn from it
\mathtt{P}^{(T)}\sim\pi

[
8

(which we will introduce shortly).
For pretraining data, we first sample
n

\mathtt{P}_{j}^{(T)}

(\bm{x}_{1j},\cdots,\bm{x}_{Tj})

For each time series, we encode it with any-variate encoding into an input matrix denoted as
\bm{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times N}
,
2
2

2

.

We define each pretraining sample as
\bm{z}_{j}\coloneqq\left(\bm{H}_{j},y_{j}\right)

We consider the squared loss between model prediction and the label, i.e.

\ell(\bm{z}_{t},\bm{\theta})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[y_{t}-\mathtt{Clip}_{B_{x}}\bigg(\mathtt{read}_{y}\Big(\text{TF}_{\bm{\theta}}^{R}\left(\bm{H}\right)\Big)\bigg)\Bigg]^{2},

\widehat{L}(\bm{\theta})\coloneqq\frac{1}{nT}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ell(\bm{\theta},\bm{z}_{jt}),\;L(\bm{\theta})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z},\mathtt{P}^{(T)}}\left[\ell(\bm{\theta},\bm{z})\right].

(4.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\theta}}\coloneqq\underset{\bm{\theta}\in\Theta_{L,M,D^{\prime},B}}{\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}}\widehat{L}(\bm{\theta}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Theta_{L,M,D^{\prime},B}\coloneqq\Bigg\{\bm{\theta}=\left(\bm{\theta}_{1}^{(1:L)},\bm{\theta}_{2}^{(1:L)}\right):

(4.3)

\displaystyle\max_{\ell\in[L]}

\displaystyle M^{(\ell)}\leq M,\quad\max_{\ell\in[L]}D^{(\ell)}\leq D^{\prime},\quad\|\bm{\theta}\|_{op}\leq B\Bigg\}.

(4.4)

4.1
Weakly-Dependent Time Series

In this scenario, we consider the training data
\bm{x}
to be drawn from a distribution
\mathtt{P}
satisfying Dobrushin’s condition.
Under this condition, we are able to present several generalization bounds on pretraining.

Definition 4.1

(Influence in high dimensional distributions)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(\mathcal{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{T})
be a sequence of random variables over
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}^{T}
.
The influence of variable
\mathcal{X}_{j}
on variable
\mathcal{X}_{i}
is defined as

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{j\rightarrow i}(\mathcal{X})\coloneqq\max_{\text{x}_{-i-j},\text{x}_{j},\text{x}_{j}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\lvert P_{\mathcal{X}_{i}|\mathcal{X}_{-i}}\left(\cdot|\text{x}_{-i-j},\text{x}_{j}\right),P_{\mathcal{X}_{i}|\mathcal{X}_{-i}}\left(\cdot|\text{x}_{-i-j},\text{x}_{j}^{\prime}\right)\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\texttt{TV}},

where
\text{x}_{-i-j}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}^{T-2},\text{x}_{j},\text{x}_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}
,
\left\lvert\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\texttt{TV}}
denotes the total variation distance, and
\text{x}_{-i}
represents the vector
x
after omitting the
i
-th element.

Definition 4.2

(Dobrushin’s Uniqueness Condition)
.

Consider a random variable
\mathcal{X}
over
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}^{T}
.
The Dobrushin coefficient of
\mathcal{X}
is defined as

\alpha(\mathcal{X})\coloneqq\max_{1\leq i\leq T}\sum_{j\neq i}\bm{I}_{j\rightarrow i}(\mathcal{X}).

We say the variable satisfies Dobrushin’s uniqueness condition if
\alpha(\mathcal{X})<1
.
For a distribution
\mathtt{P}
, we denote
\alpha(\mathtt{P})=\sup_{\mathcal{X}\sim\mathtt{P}}\alpha(\mathcal{X})
.

Definition 4.3

(Log Dobrushin’s Coefficients)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(\mathcal{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{T})
be a random variable over
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}^{T}
and let
\mathtt{P}_{z}
denote its density.
Assume that
\mathtt{P}_{z}>0
on all
\Omega^{T}
.
For any
i\neq j\in[T]
, the log influence between
j
and
i
is defined as:

I^{\log}_{j,i}(\mathcal{X})=\frac{1}{4}\sup\log\frac{P\left[\text{x}_{i},\text{x}_{j},\text{x}_{-i-j}\right]P\left[\text{x}_{i}^{\prime},\text{x}_{j}^{\prime},\text{x}_{-i-j}\right]}{P\left[\text{x}_{i}^{\prime},\text{x}_{j},\text{x}_{-i-j}\right]P\left[\text{x}_{i},\text{x}_{j}^{\prime},\text{x}_{-i-j}\right]},

where the
\sup
is taken over
\text{x}_{-i-j},\text{x}_{i},\text{x}_{i}^{\prime},\text{x}_{j},\text{x}_{j}^{\prime}
,
and the log-coefficient of
\mathcal{X}
is defined as
\alpha_{\log}(\mathcal{X})=\max_{i\in[T]}\sum_{j\neq i}I^{\log}_{j,i}(\mathcal{X})
.

The coefficient
\alpha(\cdot)
has a natural bound
0\leq\alpha(\cdot)\leq T-1
, with
\alpha=0
, the data reduces to the i.i.d. case.

Remark 4.4
.

Dobrushin’s condition characterizes a class of distributions whose dependency is mild.
However, our empirical evaluation suggests that in certain situations where Dobrushin’s condition fails to hold, the Transformers can perform prediction well.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigate the theoretical foundations of transformers as time series foundation models.
First, we show that there exists a multi-layer transformer capable of performing least squares regression on any input uni-variate time series.
Next, when considering MOIRAI, we demonstrate the existence of a multi-layer MOIRAI that adapts to the dimensionality
d
of the input (i.e., the number of covariates) and fits different autoregressive models based on
d
.
When the data is generated by an autoregressive process, such a transformer benefits from its prediction error being exponentially suppressed by the number of layers.
We then establish a generalization bound for pretraining when the data satisfies Dobrushin’s condition.
When the pretraining data is sampled from
\mathtt{AR}
processes, we derive a more explicit bound on the test loss, with a trade-off controlled by the number of layers.
Our analysis not only provides the first theoretical justification for the design and performance of MOIRAI but also represents the first theoretical framework for constructing a time series foundation model.

Limitations.

One limitation in our analysis is that we consider ReLU instead of softmax in attention mechanisms.
While the same approach also is in theoretical
[
10
,
20
,
21
]
and empirical works
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
, one might obtain a different approximation bound comparing to
Theorem

3.8
.
However, in our generalization analysis, the difference is small as softmax does not affect the model complexity too much.
Another aspect is that we mainly focus on
\mathtt{AR}
processes.
While in the appendix, we do show the approximation result for non-linear
\mathtt{AR}
processes generated by a ReLU network, to achieve universal forecasting, a more general assumption on data is required.

Impact Statement.

This paper studies the theoretical aspect of transformers as time series foundation models.
No negative societal impacts that the authors feel should be specifically highlighted here.

However, the machine learning community has yet to provide a suitable explanation for MOIRAI’s impressive performance.

In-Context Learning.

In-context learning (ICL) is an emerging capability of large foundation models, enabling them to learn diverse and unseen tasks from given examples.

[
16
]
first provide empirical evidence of ICL in large language models (LLMs); by presenting several examples of

(\bm{x},\bm{y})
pairs, GPT-3 effectively infers the relationship between
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
.

[
17
]
then conduct quantitative experiments on simple function classes, such as linear regression.
Their results demonstrate that large foundation models can learn the parameters of these function classes.
Subsequently, several theoretical studies
[
10
,
11
,
14
,
19
]
have proven that different types of transformers can implement algorithms such as gradient descent.
This discovery provides a theoretical foundation for the empirical findings in
[
17
]
.

The closest studies to this paper are
[
34
,
35
]
.
However,
[
35
]
examines ICL in the context of next-token prediction using a linear transformer.
While their theoretical results relate to in-context learning on sequential data, they are insufficient to explain transformers’ success in time series forecasting.

[
34
]
explores another case where the data is modeled as a Markov chain generated by a transition matrix.
They demonstrate the existence of induction heads that enable transformers to perform next-token prediction.
However, their scenario does not align with multivariate time series, which is where our main contribution lies.

\sigma^{2}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\bm{x}_{t}-\sum_{i=1}^{q}\langle\widehat{\bm{w}}_{i},\bm{x}_{t-i}\rangle\right)^{2}.

-Lipschitz in

.

Proof.

For any
\bm{\theta}=(\bm{\theta}_{1},\bm{\theta}_{2})
,

\bm{H}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathtt{R}}
, and
\theta^{\prime}=(\theta^{\prime}_{1},\theta^{\prime}_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\lvert\text{TF}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{H})-\text{TF}_{\theta^{\prime}}(\bm{H})\right\rvert\right\rvert_{2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\left\lvert\text{MLP}_{\bm{\theta}_{2}}(\text{Attn}_{\bm{\theta}_{1}}(\bm{H}))-\text{MLP}_{\bm{\theta}_{2}}(\text{Attn}_{\theta^{\prime}_{1}}(\bm{H}))\right\rvert\right\rvert_{2,\infty}+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left\lvert\left\lvert\text{MLP}_{\bm{\theta}_{2}}(\text{Attn}_{\theta^{\prime}_{1}}(\bm{H}))-\text{MLP}_{\theta^{\prime}_{2}}(\text{Attn}_{\theta^{\prime}_{1}}(\bm{H}))\right\rvert\right\rvert_{2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq(1+B^{2})\left\lvert\left\lvert\text{Attn}_{\theta^{\prime}_{1}}(\bm{H})-\text{Attn}_{\theta^{\prime}_{1}}(\bm{H})\right\rvert\right\rvert_{2,\infty}+B\bar{\mathtt{R}}\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{\theta}_{2}-\theta_{2}^{\prime}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\text{op}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+B^{2})(1+\iota)\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{\theta}_{1}-\theta_{1}^{\prime}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\text{op}}+B\bar{\mathtt{R}}\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{\theta}_{2}-\theta_{2}^{\prime}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\text{op}}\leq B_{\Theta}\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{\theta}-\theta^{\prime}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\text{op}},

where

,
\iota=\max\{B^{2}\mathtt{R}^{2}+T+(T-1)d,B(T-1)d\}

The second inequality comes from the fact
\left\lvert\left\lvert\text{Attn}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{H})\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq\mathtt{R}+B^{3}\mathtt{R}^{3}
.

Further, for
\bm{H}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathtt{R}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\lvert\text{TF}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{H})-\text{TF}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{H}^{\prime})\right\rvert\right\rvert_{2,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq(1+B^{2})\left\lvert\left\lvert\text{Attn}_{\bm{\theta}_{1}}(\bm{H})-\text{Attn}_{\bm{\theta}_{1}}(\bm{H}^{\prime})\right\rvert\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq(1+B^{2})(1+B^{3}\mathtt{R}^{2})\left\lvert\left\lvert\bm{H}-\bm{H}^{\prime}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{2,\infty}.

For the multi-layer case, one can simply follow
[

, Proposition J.1]
to conclude the proof.

∎

URP, comparison, mean dimension, and sharp shift embeddability
Petr Naryshkin

Abstract
. For a free action G ↷ X G\curvearrowright X of an amenable group on a compact metrizable space, we study the Uniform Rokhlin Property (URP) and the conjunction of Uniform Rokhlin Property and comparison (URPC). We give several equivalent formulations of the latter and show that it passes to extensions. We introduce technical conditions called property FCSB and property FCSB in measure, both of which reduce to the marker property if G G is abelian. Our first main result is that for any amenable group G G property FCSB in measure is equivalent to URP, and for a large class of amenable groups property FCSB is equivalent to URPC. In the latter case it follows that if the action is moreover minimal then the C ∗ -crossed product C ⁡ ( X ) ⋊ G C(X)\rtimes G has stable rank one, satisfies the Toms-Winter conjecture, and is classifiable if mdim ⁡ ( G ↷ X ) = 0 {\rm mdim}(G\curvearrowright X)=0 . Our second main result is that if a system G ↷ X G\curvearrowright X has URPC and mdim ⁡ ( G ↷ X ) < M / 2 {\rm mdim}(G\curvearrowright X)<M/2 , then it is embeddable into the M M -cubical shift ( [ 0 , 1 ] M ) G \left([0,1]^{M}\right)^{G} . Combined with the first main result, we recover the Gutman-Qiao-Tsukamoto sharp shift embeddability theorem as a special case. Notably, the proof avoids the use of either Euclidean geometry or signal analysis and directly extends the theorem to all abelian groups. Finally, we show that if G G is a nonamenable group that contains a free subgroup on two generators and G ↷ X G\curvearrowright X is a topologically amenable action, then it is embeddable into [ 0 , 1 ] G [0,1]^{G} .

Theorem 1.2

Let

The marker property was first developed by Gutman in

(i)

(ii)

show that the set of points that are not in the tiles is small not only in density but, in fact, in a certain (stronger) combinatorial sense, and

(iii)

encode a sequence of the data constructed in items (i) and (ii) using a small amount of information.

Importantly, arguments (i) and (iii) above use the Eucledean geometry of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and signal analysis on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
respectively and therefore do not extend to action of more general amenable groups. One other feature of the results above is that they do not develop much abstract theory. Indeed, the constructions are all explicit and keep being used throughout the proof. We additionally remark that in the special case of minimal
{\mathbb{Z}}

[

Another topic that has connections to mean dimension and where somewhat different dynamical tilings appear is the study of crossed product C

Theorem 1.3

Let
G\curvearrowright X

•

It has the small boundary property if and only if it has URP and
{\rm mdim}(G\curvearrowright X)=0

•

If it is minimal, has URPC, and

•

If it is minimal and has URPC then the radius of comparison of the crossed product satisfies

•

If it is minimal and has URPC the the crossed product has stable rank one.

It is confirmed (see, for example,
[
27

X
is finite (note that URP is automatic in this case

17

). On the other hand, not much has been known in the general situation, besides for the Theorem

There has been some attempts to apply the theory developed for the study of crossed products to the shift embeddability problem (see, for instance,
[
35
]
). However, these results contain neither of the three crucial arguments (i)–(iii) in the proof of Theorem
1.2
: the action is assumed to be an extension of one on a finite-dimensional space (which makes constructing the tiling much easier); instead of having a small remainder set, the towers are allowed to overlap and have to cover
X
(which increases the dimension of the cube by a multiplicative constant); and instead of encoding the tiling construction, one simply encodes the whole finite-dimensional quotient space (which increases the dimension of the cube by an additive constant). In particular, outside of certain specific circumstances
[
18
]
, one never achieves the sharp bound
M/2
.

In the present paper, we first show (Theorem
A
) that the abstract tiling theory can, in fact, be applied to the shift embeddability problem if one proceeds in a more careful manner. We then find a common generalization (Theorem
D
) for the tiling techniques developed in the crossed product setting (which worked for a large class of amenable groups but only on the finite-dimensional spaces) and in the shift embeddability setting (which worked only for
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
but on more general spaces). In other words, we completely unify these two problems in a common framework. To the best of our knowledge, this gives the most general results in either direction.

The open sets of
X
generate the
type semigroup
equipped with the preorder given by dynamical subequivalence (Definition

3.11

3.20
). Our first main result is the sharp shift embeddability theorem under the assumption of URPC. We remark that this is immediately applicable to extensions (Corollary
3.23
) of many finite-dimensional systems and, in contrast with the results mentioned above, gives the statement with the optimal constant
M/2
.

Theorem A
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
be a free action of an amenable group on a compact metrizable space. If it has URPC and

{\rm mdim}(G\curvearrowright X)<M/2

then there exists a
G
-equivariant embedding of
X
into
\left([0,1]^{M}\right)^{G}
.

When compared to the proof of Theorem
1.2
, URP and comparison play the role of the dynamical tiling constructed in item (i) and the argument for the remainder in item (ii) respectively. For item (iii), although we follow the general philosophy of encoding using a small amount of information, the specific approach needs to be modified. Indeed, Gutman, Qiao, and Tsukamoto encode the tilings
spectrally
using the Fourier transform, which is only possible on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

spatially
in a more straightforward manner. The fact that a witness to property URPC can be reconstructed from a small amount of data is made precise with the notion of
encoding element

We remark that as there are no restrictions on the structure of the group, the core argument is not limited to actions of amenable groups. In
[
6

topologically amenable

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a nonamenable group containing a free subgroup on two generators. If
G\curvearrowright X
is a topologically amenable action then there exists a
G

into
[0,1]^{G}

To the best of our knowledge, the theorem above is the first shift embeddability result for nonamenable groups. We note that topologically amenable actions cannot have any invariant probability measures, hence the sofic mean dimension satisfies
{\rm mdim}_{\Sigma}(G\curvearrowright X)\leq 0
for any sofic approximation
\Sigma

[

With the importance of URPC firmly established, we now turn to characterizing the actions that possess it. The combination of
[
10
]
(where the authors introduced the dynamical tiling technique mentioned in item (i) of the proof of Theorem
1.2
) and
[
30
]
(as well as Lemma
3.18
) gives the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

A free action
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\curvearrowright X
has URPC if and only if it has the marker property.

As in turns out, there is an obstruction to extending this result to actions of general amenable group. For a general action
G\curvearrowright X
, if a set
U\subset X
is
F
-free for some
F\subset G
(i. e., all the translates
\{gU\}_{g\in F}
are pairwise disjoint) and
h\in G
is some other element, then the translate
hU
will typically not be
F
-free, unless
G
is abelian (or FC). The fact that this holds for
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
is (implicitly) crucial to the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For a more heuristic explanation, the dynamical tiling construction of Gutman, Lindenstrauss, and Tsukamoto is fundamentally a sophisticated version of Rokhlin’s first return time argument. To apply this kind of construction one needs a sufficiently small marker set, hence the marker property naturally appears. In contrast, the proof of Ornstein-Weiss’ generalization of the Rokhlin lemma starts with a sufficiently fine
partition
. As we attempted to work with general amenable groups, eventually it became clear that the assumption of the marker property has to be replaced with something stronger.

To this end we introduce a new condition which we call
property FCSB
as well as its weaker version
property FCSB in measure
(Definition
4.1
). Roughly speaking, it asks for an existence of a cover (see Remark
4.2
) consisting of sufficiently free sets such that the combined boundaries are small together. We show (Propositions
4.3
and
4.5
) that

•

if
G
is abelian (more generally, FC) then the marker property, property FCSB, and property FCSB in measure are equivalent,

•

URP implies property FCSB in measure, and

•

URPC implies property FCSB.

Our next main result is the converse to the second implication above and extend the work of Gutman, Lindenstrauss, and Tsukamoto.

Theorem C
.

If a free action
G\curvearrowright X
of an amenable group has property FCSB in measure then it has URP.

Next, let
{\mathcal{S}}
be the smallest class of
infinite
amenable groups that satisfies the following conditions.

•

Any group of locally subexponential growth is in
{\mathcal{S}}
.

•

The class
{\mathcal{S}}
is closed under taking direct limits.

•

If
H\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
H\lhd G
then
G\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

•

If
G
contains finite normal subgroups of arbitrarily large cardinality then
G\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

•

Certain amenable groups of dynamical origin belong to
{\mathcal{S}}
(see
[
28
]
for a precise description).

We remark that we do not know of any amenable group with a relatively explicit description that is not known to belong to
{\mathcal{S}}
. For groups in this class we can obtain the following.

Theorem D
.

Let
G\in{\mathcal{S}}
. If a free action
G\curvearrowright X
has property FCSB then it has URPC.

In particular, we get the following corollary as a special case. Combined with Theorem
A
this recovers the shift embeddability result of Gutman, Qiao, and Tsukamoto.

Corollary E
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
be a free action of an abelian (or FC) group. Then it has the marker property if and only if it has URPC.

As mentioned earlier, the proof of Theorem
1.4
is based on the first return time argument which fails for actions of more general groups. Consequently, we take a different approach to obtain Theorems
C
and
D
. Specifically, the proof consists of two parts. In the first one, we show that the number of open sets in the collection given by property FCSB (in measure) can be made to be
independent
of the smallness of the boundaries. This is done using the
cover reduction
procedures that decrease the number of elements in the collection while only increasing the error in a controlled way — first for actions of cyclic groups (Lemma
4.6
) and then for actions of arbitrary ones (Theorem
4.7
). Once this is done, we apply the standard Ornstein-Weiss algorithm to construct a castle with Følner shapes (Theorem
5.2
). The number of steps in the algorithm depends only on the number of open sets in the collection and hence, by the newly acquired independence, we may choose the boundaries tiny enough so that the accumulated error after running the algorithm is still small. This gives us Theorem
C
.

The argument above is insufficient to prove Theorem
D
. The problem does not come from the boundary error (which can be controlled in the same way) but from the fact that the classical Ornstein-Weiss algorithm produces a castle that is a priori only large
in measure
. To bridge this gap we recall the notion of a
local algorithm
(Definition
5.3
). This concept has long been used in measurable combinatorics, as it is not difficult to see that whenever one can solve a discrete problem using a local algorithm, one can also solve a measurable version of the problem (for example, see
[
1
]
). As it turns out, this notion is applicable in our context we well. Specifically, we define what it means for a group to have an
exact local tiling algorithm
(Definition
5.12
) and show that for such groups property FCSB implies URPC (Theorem
5.14
). Exact tilings were extensively studied before
[
17
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
(although not in the language of local algorithms) and it was (implicitly) shown that the groups from the class
{\mathcal{S}}
defined above have this property. This finishes the proof of Theorem
D
.

The results above naturally bring up an important question — if
G
is not FC, is there indeed a genuine gap between the marker property and FCSB? We suspect that the answer to this question is positive.

Problem F
.

Let
G
be an amenable group with an infinite conjugacy class. Let
G\curvearrowright X
be a free action with the marker property. Does it necessarily have property FCSB (in measure)? What if
G\curvearrowright X
is additionally assumed to be minimal?

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the notation and recall some basic facts about Banach densities. In Section
3

we introduce property FCSB (in measure), show how it relates to the marker property, and describe the cover reduction procedure. In Section
5
we develop the theory of local tiling algorithms and prove Theorems

and
D

6
we show how a sequence of URPC witnesses can be encoded on a small marker set and prove Theorem

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
X
will be a compact metrizable topological space,
G
will be an
infinite
countable discrete group, and
G\curvearrowright X
will be a free (that is, all stabilizers are trivial) action by homeomoprhisms. Everywhere except Section
7
the group

is assumed to be amenable. We sometimes write
F\subset\!\subset G
to say that
F
is a finite subset of
G
. We will denote by
M_{G}(X)
the space of
G
-invariant probability measure on
X
. When
G
is amenable,
M_{G}(X)
is nonempty.

For convenience, we fix a compatible metric
\rho
on
X
(note that it is not assumed to be invariant under the group action). Given a set
A\subset X
and
\delta>0
, we define

A^{\delta}=\{x\in X\mid\rho(x,A)<\delta\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad A^{-\delta}=\{x\in X\mid\rho(x,X\setminus A)>\delta\}.

Note that
A^{\delta}
and
A^{-\delta}
are open for any set
A\subset X
.

Let
F\subset G
be finite and
\varepsilon>0
. We say that a finite set
S\subset G
is
(F,\varepsilon)
-invariant if

\left|\bigcap_{g\in F}gS\right|>(1-\varepsilon)\left|S\right|.

Recall that a group is amenable if and only if there is an
(F,\varepsilon)
-invariant set for every
F
and
\varepsilon
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Y
be an arbitrary set (for our purposes, either
Y=G
or
Y=X
) and let
G\curvearrowright Y
be a free action. Given a set
A\subset Y
and a nonempty finite set
F\subset G
define

\underline{D}_{F}(A)=\inf_{y\in Y}\frac{\left|A\cap Fy\right|}{\left|F\right|}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{D}_{F}(A)=\sup_{y\in Y}\frac{\left|A\cap Fy\right|}{\left|F\right|}.

We refer to these quantities as
lower and upper Banach densities of
A
with respect to
F
. We define
lower and upper Banach densities of
A
by

\underline{D}(A)=\sup_{F\subset\!\subset G}\underline{D}_{F}(A)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{D}(A)=\inf_{F\subset\!\subset G}\overline{D}_{F}(A).

If
(F_{n})
is a Følner sequence for
G
then

\underline{D}(A)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\underline{D}_{F_{n}}(A)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{D}(A)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\overline{D}_{F_{n}}(A).

Note that the upper Banach density is also known as the
orbit capacity
. Suppose now that
Y=X
is a compact space and
G
acts on it by homeomorphisms. If
A\subset X
is open we have

\underline{D}(A)=\inf_{\mu\in M_{G}(X)}\mu(A),

and if
A\subset X
is closed we have

\overline{D}(A)=\sup_{\mu\in M_{G}(X)}\mu(A).

For arbitrary subsets
S\subset G
and
V\subset X
we define

SV=\bigcup_{g\in S}gV.

If
\{{\mathcal{U}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are collections of open sets, we define the join

\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{U}}_{i}=\left\{U_{1}\cap U_{2}\cap\ldots\cap U_{n}\mid U_{i}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{i}\right\}.

If
{\mathcal{U}}
is a collection of open sets in
X
and
Y\subset X
, we define the restriction

\left.{\mathcal{U}}\right|_{Y}=\{U\cap Y\mid U\in{\mathcal{U}}\}.

If
g\in G
, we define the collection

to be

g{\mathcal{U}}=\{gU\mid u\in{\mathcal{U}}\}.

3.
Marker property, Uniform Rokhlin Property, and comparison

3.1.
Dynamical subequivalence, type semigroup, and marker sets

Definition 3.1
.

Given a set
U\subset X
and a finite set
L\subset G
we say that
U
is
L
-free
if all the translates
\{gU\}_{g\in L}
are pairwise disjoint.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that an open set
U\subset X
is a
marker set
if
GU=X
. The action
G\curvearrowright X
has the
marker property
if for every finite subset
L\subset G
there exists an
L
-free marker set.

Remark 3.3
.

By compactness, if
U
is a marker set then there exists some finite subset
M\subset G
with
MU=X
. In particular, that implies that

\underline{D}(U)\geq 1/\left|M\right|.

Conversely, for any open set
U
, the set
X\setminus GU
is a
G
-invariant compact set and thus carries a
G
-invariant probability measure. It follows that if
\underline{D}(U)>0
, then
U
is a marker set.

The following lemma shows that whenever we choose an
L
-free marker set
U
we can in fact assume that
\overline{U}
is
L
-free. The proof is elementary and is left as an exercise.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
U
be an
L
-free marker set such that
MU=X
for some
M\subset\!\subset G
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that
U^{-\delta}
is an
L
-free marker set with
MU^{-\delta}=X
. In particular,
\overline{U^{-\delta}}\subset U
is
L
-free.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
A\subset X
be closed and let
B\subset X
be open. We say that
A
is
(dynamically) subequivalent
to
B
and write
A\prec B
if there exist a finite open cover
A\subset\bigcup_{j\in J}U_{j}
and elements
g_{j}\in G,j\in J
such that
\{g_{j}U_{j}\}_{j\in J}
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
B
.

For an open set
A
we say that it is subequivalent to
B
(and write again
A\prec B
) if for every closed subset
A^{\prime}\subset A
we have
A^{\prime}\prec B
. Note that if
A
is open and
A\subset B
then
A\prec B
. Moreover, we have that
gA\prec A
and
A\prec gA
for any
g\in G
.

It is an easy exercise to check that the dynamical subequivalence is transitive and therefore defines a partial preorder on the set of open subsets of
X
.

Note that if
A\prec B
then
\mu(A)\leq\mu(B)
for all
\mu\in M_{G}(X)
.

Remark 3.6
.

Let
A
be a closed set and let
B
be an open set. Suppose that
A\prec B
and this subequivalence is implemented by the cover
\{U_{j}\}_{j\in J}
together with elements
g_{j}\in G
. Then for any given
\delta>0
we can always assume that

(i)

g_{j}\overline{U_{j}}
are pairwise disjoint in
B
and

(ii)

\bigcup_{j\in J}U_{j}\subset A^{\delta}
.

Indeed, item (i) is achieved by slightly shrinking sets
U_{j}
and item (ii) is achieved by intersecting them with
A^{\delta}
.

Definition 3.7
.

The
type semigroup
of an action
G\curvearrowright X
is the abelian semigroup generated by all open subsets of
X
subject to the relations

[U\sqcup V]=[U]+[V]\quad\mbox{and}\quad[gU]=[U]

for all
g\in G
. The dynamical subequivalence respects these relations and therefore extends to a partial preorder on the type semigroup (which we once again denote by
\prec
) that is compatible with the semigroup structure. We will frequently not make a distinction between a set
U
and the element
[U]
.

We remark that our definition is slightly different from the definition of the
generalized type semigroup
introduced by Ma
[
25
]
. More specifically, we do not take a further quotient to obtain a semigroup with a genuine partial order on it. However, in the present paper we are not interested in the properties of the type semigroup itself and only use it to write calculations in a more convenient manner. Thus, the notion above is sufficient for our purposes.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be closed sets. Employing a slight abuse of notation, we will write subequivalence expressions involving the terms
[A_{i}]
if they hold with
A_{i}
replaced by some sufficiently small neighbourhoods
A_{i}^{\delta_{i}}
.

As an example, if
A_{1},A_{2}
are closed and
B_{1},B_{2}
are open then the expression
[A_{1}]+[B_{1}]\prec[A_{2}]+[B_{2}]
means that for any
\delta>0
there are
0<\delta_{1},\delta_{2}<\delta
such that
[A_{1}^{\delta_{1}}]+[B_{1}]\prec[A_{2}^{\delta_{2}}]+[B_{2}]
.

We write an expression involving several consecutive subequivalences (such as
[A_{1}]\prec[A_{2}]\prec[A_{3}]
) if each of them holds in the above sense. One may check that the relation
\prec
is still transitive and thus the calculations can be safely carried out.

It turns out that there is a nice connection between dynamical subequivalence and the marker property. Specifically, the marker sets can be viewed as arbitrarily small everywhere positive elements in the type semigroup. This idea is formalized in the next few lemmas.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose
A
is an
L
-free open subset of
X
and
B
is an open subset such that
L^{-1}B=X
. Then
A\prec B
.

Proof.

For
g\in L
set
U_{g}=\left(g^{-1}B\right)\cap A
. Then

\bigcup_{g\in L}U_{g}=\bigcup_{g\in L}\left(g^{-1}B\right)\cap A=A,

and
gU_{g}=B\cap gA
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
B
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Note that in the previous lemma, by setting

V=\bigsqcup_{g\in L}gU_{g},

we can in fact obtain an open set
V\subset B
such that
A\prec V\prec\left|L\right|[A]
.

The following easy statement is the cornerstone of our techniques.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
G
be a countably infinite group, let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
B
be a marker set. Then there exists some finite
L\subset G
such that for any
L
-free open set
U

k[U]\prec[B].

Proof.

Since
B
is a marker set there exists some finite
M\subset G
containing the identity such that
MB=X
. Choose
L
so that there are elements
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}\in G
such that
M^{-1}g_{i}
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
L
. Then for any
L
-free set
U

k[U]=\sum_{i=1}^{k}[g_{i}U]=\left[\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}U\right]\prec B

by Lemma
3.9
.
∎

Some techniques for establishing dynamical subequivalence were developed in
[
26
]
. We recall the central lemma
[
26
, Lemma 3.1]
of that work.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
A\subset X
be closed and
B\subset X
open. Assume that for some
\varepsilon>0
, finite
D\subset G
, and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\left|\{g\in D^{-1}D\mid gx\in A^{\varepsilon}\}\right|<k\left|\{g\in D\mid gx\in B^{-\varepsilon}\}\right|

for all
x\in X
. Then
[A]\prec k[B]
.

The result above straightforwardly extends to show that that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}[A_{i}]\prec k\sum_{j=1}^{m}[B_{j}]

for a collection of closed sets
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and a collection of open sets
\{B_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
as long as we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\{g\in D^{-1}D\mid gx\in A_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}\right|<k\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\{g\in D\mid gx\in B_{j}^{-\varepsilon}\}\right|

for some
\varepsilon>0
, finite
D\subset G
, and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
x\in X
.

The special situation of
D=\{e\}
and
k=1
gives a surprisingly useful corollary which we record below. Note that in this case the group action is completely irrelevant.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a collection of closed sets and let
\{B_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
be a collection of open sets. Suppose that for some
\varepsilon>0
we have

\left|\{i\mid x\in A_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}\right|<\left|\{j\mid x\in B_{j}^{-\varepsilon}\}\right|

for all
x\in X
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}[A_{i}]\prec\sum_{j=1}^{m}[B_{i}]
.

We end this subsection by recalling the definition of comparison and some of its variations. Here, as usual,
A
and
A_{i}
stand for closed sets and
B
and
B_{j}
stand for open sets.

Definition 3.14
.

An action
G\curvearrowright X
is said to have:

•

comparison
if
A\prec B
whenever
\mu(A)<\mu(B)
for every
\mu\in M_{G}(X)
,

•

k
-comparison
(for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
) if
[A]\prec(k+1)[B]
whenever
\mu(A)<\mu(B)
for every
\mu\in M_{G}(X)
,

•

weak
k
-comparison
(for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
) if
[A]\prec(k+1)[B]
whenever
\overline{D}(A)<\underline{D}(B)
,

•

comparison on multisets
if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}[A_{i}]\prec\sum_{j=1}^{m}[B_{j}]
whenever
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu(A_{i})<\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu(B_{j})
for every
\mu\in M_{G}(X)
.

Lemma
3.12
was used in
[
26
]
to deduce the following main result
[
26
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
G
be a group of polynomial growth with
d
being the order of the growth function. Then
G\curvearrowright X
has weak
2^{d}
-comparison.

3.2.
URP and URPC

In this subsection we introduce the two central properties used in this work. Both of them can be regarded as a topological version of the conclusion of the Ornstein-Weiss tiling theorem. We begin with some basic terminology.

Definition 3.16
.

A pair
(S,V)
, where
S\subset G
is finite and
V\subset X
is open is called a
tower
if the sets
\{gV\}_{g\in S}
are pairwise disjoint (that is,
V
is
S
-free). In such a situation, we say that

•

V
is the
base
of the tower.

•

S
is the
shape
of the tower,

•

gV
for
g\in S
are
levels
of the tower, and

•

SV
is the
footprint
of the tower.

A finite collection
\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
of towers is called a
castle
if moreover all the footprints
S_{i}V_{i}
are pairwise disjoint. The
footprint
of a castle
\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
is the set
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}
.

Definition 3.17
.

An action
G\curvearrowright X
has the
Uniform Rokhlin Property (URP)
if for every finite set
K\subset G
and every
\varepsilon>0
there is a castle
\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
such that the shapes
S_{i}
are
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant and

\overline{D}\left(X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\right)<\varepsilon.

We remark that the only difference between URP and
almost finiteness in measure
(see
[
17
]
) is that the latter additionally asks for all levels
gV_{i},g\in S_{i}
of the castle to be small in diameter. This seemingly small distinction turns out to be quite significant. Indeed, Kerr ans Szabó showed that for free actions almost finiteness in measure is equivalent to the small boundary property (SBP). On the other hand, there are many actions that have URP but not SBP.

Lemma 3.18
.

Suppose an action
G\curvearrowright X
has URP. Then it has the marker property.

Proof.

Let
L
be a finite subset of
G
containing the identity. Let
\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an open castle with
(L^{-1},1/2)
-invariant shapes such that

\overline{D}\left(X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\right)<1/2.

By the invariance condition, for each
i=1,2,\ldots,n
there is an element
g_{i}\in S_{i}
such that
Lg_{i}\subset S_{i}
. Set

U=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}V_{i}.

Then
U
is
L
-free and
\underline{D}(U)>0
which means it is a marker set.
∎

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
U
be a marker set. Then there exists an
\varepsilon>0
and a finite set
K\subset G
such that for any castle
\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
with
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant shapes we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lceil\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|\rceil[V_{i}]\prec U.

Proof.

Find a finite set
L\subset G
containing the identity such that
L^{-1}U=X
. Set
K=L^{-1}L
and choose
\varepsilon
so that
(1-\varepsilon)/\left|K\right|>\varepsilon
. Let
S_{i}
be a
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant finite subset of
G
. Then

\left|\bigcap_{g\in K}gS_{i}\right|>(1-\varepsilon)\left|S_{i}\right|

and we can choose a subset
S_{i}^{\prime}\subset\bigcap_{g\in K}gS_{i}
which is
K
-independent and satisfies

\left|S_{i}^{\prime}\right|\geq\frac{\left|\bigcap_{g\in K}gS_{i}\right|}{\left|K\right|}>\frac{(1-\varepsilon)}{\left|K\right|}\left|S_{i}\right|>\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|.

It follows that

\sum_{i\in I}\lceil\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|\rceil[V_{i}]\prec\sum_{i\in I}S_{i}^{\prime}[V_{i}].

However,
\bigsqcup_{i\in I}S_{i}^{\prime}V_{i}
is an
L
-free open set and therefore subequivalent to
U
by Lemma
3.9
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main theorem of this section, which gives several equivalent definitions for the conjunction of URP and comparison.

Theorem 3.20
.

For an action
G\curvearrowright X
the following are equivalent.

(i)

It has URP and comparison.

(ii)

It has URP and weak
k
-comparison for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(iii)

For every finite
K\subset G
and
\varepsilon>0
there is a castle
\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
with
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant shapes such that

X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\prec\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lfloor\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|\rfloor[V_{i}].

(iv)

It has the marker property and for every finite
K\subset G
,
\varepsilon>0
, and a marker set
U
there is an open castle
\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
with
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant shapes such that

X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\prec U.

Proof.

It is clear that (i) implies (ii).

To see that (ii) implies (iii), use URP to choose a castle
\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
such that the shapes
S_{i}
are
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant with
\left|S_{i}\right|>(k+1)/\varepsilon
and

\overline{D}\left(X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\right)<\frac{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}{2(k+1)}<\varepsilon.

For each
i=1,2,\ldots,n
choose a subset
S_{i}^{\prime}\subset S_{i}
with

\left|S_{i}^{\prime}\right|=\left\lfloor\frac{\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|}{k+1}\right\rfloor>\frac{\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|}{2(k+1)}.

It follows that

\underline{D}\left(\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\prime}V_{i}\right)>\frac{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}{2(k+1)}>\overline{D}\left(X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\right)

and thus, using weak
k
-comparison,

X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\prec(k+1)\left[\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\prime}V_{i}\right]\prec\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lfloor\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|\rfloor[V_{i}].

Lemmas
3.18
and
3.19
show that (iii) implies (iv).

It remains to show that (iv) implies (i). Clearly, URP holds and therefore we only need to show comparison. Let
A
be a closed set and
B
an open set such that
\mu(A)<\mu(B)
for every
G
-invariant probability measure
\mu
. By
[
15
, Lemma 3.3]
there exists some
\delta>0
such that in fact

\mu(A^{\delta})+\delta<\mu(B^{-\delta})

for every
G
-invariant probability measure
\mu
. Note that, in particular,
\mu(B)>\delta
for every
\mu\in M_{G}(X)
and thus
\underline{D}(B)>0
which means that it is a marker set. It follows that there is some finite set
L\subset G
such that
L^{-1}B=X
. Find a marker set
U
with
\overline{D}(\overline{U})<\delta/\left|L\right|
. By slightly shrinking the set
U
(Lemma
3.4
) and using Remark
3.10
we can obtain an open set
V\subset B
such that
V
is a marker set and
\overline{D}(\overline{V})<\delta
. Set
B^{\prime}=B\setminus\overline{V}
. Then

B^{\prime}\sqcup V\subset B

and

\mu(A)<\mu(B^{\prime})

for every
\mu\in M_{G}(X)
. The last inequality implies that there exist some finite
K\subset G
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for every
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant set
S\subset G
and every
x\in X
we have

(3.1)

\sum_{g\in S}\delta_{x}(g^{-1}A)<\sum_{g\in S}\delta_{x}(g^{-1}B^{\prime}),

where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac measure at
x
. Choose an open castle
\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
with
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant shapes containing the identity such that

X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\prec V.

Moreover, find a small enough
\tau>0
such that

X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}^{-\tau}\prec V.

Set
Y=\bigsqcup\overline{V_{i}^{-\tau/2}}
. Then, by using (
3.1
) and applying Corollary
3.13
on the set
Y
, we have

A\prec\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{g\in S_{i}}\left[g\overline{V_{i}^{-2\tau/3}}\cap A\right]+X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}^{-\tau}\\
\prec\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{g\in S_{i}}\left[\overline{V_{i}^{-2\tau/3}}\cap g^{-1}A\right]+[V]\\
\prec\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{g\in S_{i}}\left[V_{i}^{-\tau/2}\cap g^{-1}B^{\prime}\right]+[V]\prec[B^{\prime}]+[V]\prec B.

∎

Definition 3.21
.

We say that an action
G\curvearrowright X
has
URPC
(Uniform Rokhlin Property and comparison) if either of the equivalent properties in Theorem
3.20
hold.

Remark 3.22
.

Similarly to how URP is a direct weakening of almost finiteness in measure, URPC is a direct weakening of a property known as
almost finiteness
(see
[
15
]
). The latter holds automatically for many amenable groups acting freely on finite-dimensional spaces (see, for instance,
[
27
]
). In particular, the corollary below is widely applicable.

Corollary 3.23
.

Suppose that an action has URPC. Then any extension of this action also has URPC.

Proof.

Taking preimage under the quotient map shows that item (iii) in Theorem
3.20
passes to extensions.
∎

We finish the section by introducing several auxiliary definitions that will be useful later.

Definition 3.24
.

Suppose that the action
G\curvearrowright X
has URPC and let
K\subset G
be finite and
\varepsilon>0
. We say that the data
{\mathcal{W}}=\left(\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m},\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\right)
is a
(K,\varepsilon)
-URPC witness
if

•

\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a castle and satisfies item (iii) in Theorem
3.20
,

•

\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
is an open cover of
X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}
and
g_{j}
are elements of G,

•

there is a partition of the set
\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
into (possibly empty) subsets
J_{i,k}
, where
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,\lfloor\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|\rfloor\}
such that
\{g_{j}U_{j}\}_{j\in J_{i,k}}
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
V_{i}
.

In other words, the data
\{(g_{j},U_{j})\}_{j=1}^{m}
witnesses the subequivalence

X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\prec\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lfloor\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|\rfloor[V_{i}].

Remark
3.6
shows that we may always assume that in fact the sets
\{g_{j}\overline{U_{j}}\}_{j\in J_{i,k}}
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
V_{i}
.

Given such a URPC witness, we define the corresponding
URPC cover
to be the collection

C({\mathcal{W}})=\{gV_{i}\colon 1\leq i\leq n,g\in S_{i}\}\sqcup\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}.

Note that it is indeed a cover of
X
. Furthermore, we define the
encoding element
of this cover in the type semigroup to be

E({\mathcal{W}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[V_{i}]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}[U_{j}].

Remark 3.25
.

Similar to Lemma
3.4
, URPC witnesses are stable under small shrinking of sets. More precisely, let
{\mathcal{W}}=\left(\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m},\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\right)
be a
(K,\varepsilon)
-URPC witness. Then there exists some
\delta>0
such that for any opens sets
V_{i}^{-\delta}\subset V_{i}^{\prime}\subset V_{i}
and any open sets
U_{j}^{-\delta}\subset U_{j}^{\prime}\subset U_{j}
the data
{\mathcal{W}}^{\prime}=\left(\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{V_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m},\{U_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j=1}^{m}\right)
is also a
(K,\varepsilon)
-URPC witness.

4.
Property FCSB

In this section we introduce and study properties FCSB and FCSB in measure. We remark that most of the statements have two versions: in density, corresponding to URP, and group-combinatorial, corresponding to URPC. We will always give a proof for the second (more difficult) version. The density statements can be obtained by using
[
15
, Lemma 3.3]
and substituting the condition “
\cdot\prec V
” with “
\overline{D}(\cdot)<\varepsilon
”.

4.1.
The definition and the relation to the marker property

Definition 4.1
.

An action
G\curvearrowright X
has
property FCSB (free covers with staggered boundaries)
if it has the marker property and for every finite
F\subset G
and any marker set
V
there is a collection of
F
-free open sets
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
with

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\partial U_{i}\right]+\left[X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\right]\prec V.

Note that here we use the convention introduced in Definition
3.8
.

An action
G\curvearrowright X
has
property FCSB in measure
if for every finite
F\subset G
and any
\varepsilon>0
there is a collection of
F
-free open sets
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu\left(\partial U_{i}\right)+\mu\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\right)<\varepsilon

for any
\mu\in M_{G}(X)
. It is clear that property FCSB in measure is a direct weakening of property FCSB.

Note that in the definition above, we do not require that the sets
U_{i}
cover
X
(and instead ask that the remainder
X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
is small in an appropriate sense). This is purely a matter of choice, as the following remark shows. The only reason we do not impose this condition is that it will be slightly more convenient for us to allow for a remainder when proving Theorem
4.7
.

Remark 4.2
.

In the definition of property FCSB (in measure) one can equivalently ask for the collection
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
to be a cover of
X
.

Proof.

We will prove the statement for property FCSB, with the proof for property FCSB in measure being essentially the same.

Let
F\subset G
be finite and let
V
be a marker set. Choose an arbitrary
F
-free cover
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of X and use Lemma
3.11
to choose a marker set
U
such that

m[U]\prec[V].

By property FCSB, there is a collection
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and some
\delta>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\partial U_{i}\right]+\left[\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\right)^{\delta}\right]\prec U.

Consider the collection of open sets

\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\sqcup\left\{V_{j}\cap\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\right)^{\delta/2}\right\}_{j=1}^{m}.

Clearly, it is a cover of
X
consisting of
F
-free sets and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\partial U_{i}\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[\partial\left(V_{j}\cap\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\right)^{\delta/2}\right)\right]\prec\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\partial U_{i}\right]+m\left[\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\right)^{\delta}\right]\prec m[U]\prec V.

∎

Recall that a group
G
is said to be
FC
if every conjugacy class of
G
is finite. Any abelian group is FC.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
G
is FC and an action
G\curvearrowright X
has the marker property then it has property FCSB.

Proof.

Let
F\subset G
be finite and let
V
be a marker set. There exists some finite set
M\subset G
such that that
M^{-1}V=X
. Since
G
is FC the set

L=\bigcup_{g\in G}g(F\cup M)g^{-1}

is finite and conjugation invariant. Let
U
be an
L
-free marker set. By Lemma
3.4
there is
\delta>0
such that
U^{-\delta}
is also an
L
-free marker set. Choose a bump function
\varphi:X\to[0,1]
with
\supp\varphi\subset U
and
\varphi=1
on
U^{-\delta}
. Enumerate all elements of
G=\{g_{1},g_{2},\ldots\}
and define

U_{i}=g_{i}\{x\in X\mid\varphi(x)>1/i\}=\{x\in X\mid\varphi(g_{i}^{-1}x)>1/i\}.

By conjugation invariance of
L
, the sets
U_{i}
are
L
-free and cover
X
. By compactness, there is some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is also a cover of
X
. Moreover,
g_{i}^{-1}\left(\partial U_{i}\right)\subset\{x\in X\mid\varphi(x)=1/i\}
are disjoint subsets of
U
. Thus, using Lemma
3.9
,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\partial U_{i}]\prec U\prec V.

∎

We remark that even if the group
G
is not abelian, if an element
g\in G
lies in the center
Z(G)
, we still have the equality

gFg^{-1}=F.

With this in mind, a slight modification of the proof above shows the following.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
Z(G)
be the center of the group
G
and suppose that the restricted action
Z(G)\curvearrowright X
is minimal. Then
G\curvearrowright X
has property FCSB.

Proposition 4.5
.

If an action
G\curvearrowright X
has URPC then it has property FCSB. If it has URP then it has property FCSB in measure.

Proof.

We again prove the statement for property FCSB and URPC, with the proof for property FCSB in measure and URP being similar.

By Lemma
3.18
, if an action has URPC then it has the marker property. Let
F\subset G
be finite and let
V
be a marker set. Choose a marker set
U
such that

3[U]\prec V.

Using URPC and Lemma
3.19
, find an
\varepsilon>0
and a castle
\{(S_{i},V_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
with
(F,\varepsilon)
-invariant shapes such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lceil\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|\rceil[V_{i}]\prec U\quad\mbox{and}\quad X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\prec U.

By Remark
3.25
we can additionally assume that the closures
g\overline{V_{i}},g\in S_{i}
of all the levels of the castle are pairwise disjoint and therefore their boundaries
\partial\left(gV_{i}\right)
are pairwise disjoint subsets of the remainder
X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}
. For each shape
S_{i}
define

S_{i}^{\prime}=\{g\in S_{i}\mid Fg\subset S_{i}\}

and note that
\left|S_{i}^{\prime}\right|>(1-\varepsilon)\left|S_{i}\right|
by
(F,\varepsilon)
-invariance. Consider the following collection of open sets

\{gV_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,g\in S_{i}^{\prime}\}.

Then each of them is
F
-free and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{g\in S_{i}^{\prime}}\left[\partial\left(gV_{i}\right)\right]+\left[X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\prime}V_{i}\right]\prec\left[X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lceil\varepsilon\left|S_{i}\right|\rceil[V_{i}]+\left[X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}V_{i}\right]\prec 3[U]\prec V.

∎

4.2.
Cover reduction

The goal of this section is to show that the number of sets
U_{i}
in the Definition
4.1
can be made to only depend on the set
F
. We begin with a lemma that reduces a given cover in a “single direction” — that is, for an action of a cyclic group generated by some element of
G
. We remark that the subequivalence below is with respect to the action of the cyclic group and thus also holds for the original action
G\curvearrowright X
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\langle g\rangle\curvearrowright X
be an action of a cyclic group. Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a collection of open
\{e,g\}
-free sets and let
\delta>0
. Then there exist open
\{e,g\}
-free sets
V_{1},V_{2}
, and
V_{3}
such that their closures
\overline{V_{1}},\overline{V_{2}},
and
\overline{V_{3}}
are pairwise disjoint and

[\partial V_{1}]+[\partial V_{2}]+[\partial V_{3}]+\left[X\setminus(V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3})\right]\prec 8\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\left(\partial U_{i}\right)^{\delta}\right]+\left[X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}^{-\delta}\right]\right).

Proof.

For each
i=1,\ldots,n
choose a bump function
\varphi_{i}
which is supported on
U_{i}
and such that
\varphi_{i}\equiv 1
on
U_{i}\setminus\left(\partial U_{i}\right)^{\delta}
. In particular, we have that

(4.1)

\left\{x\mid 0<\varphi_{i}(x)<1\right\}\subset\left(\partial U_{i}\right)^{\delta}.

Set

U_{i}^{\prime}=\left\{x\mid\varphi_{i}(x)>\frac{1}{2}\right\}

and note that
U_{i}^{-\delta}\subset U_{i}^{\prime}\subset U_{i}
. For each
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
set
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}^{m}
to be a collection consisting of two open sets

g^{m}\left\{x\mid\varphi_{i}(x)>\frac{1}{\left|m\right|+2}\right\}\ \mbox{and}\ g^{m}\left\{x\mid\varphi_{i}(x)<\frac{1}{\left|m\right|+3}\right\}.

We remark that
U_{i}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{i}^{0}
and that the closures of the two sets in each
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}^{m}
are disjoint. An important property of these collections is that if
U\in{\mathcal{U}}_{i}^{m-1}
then
gU
intersects exactly one set from the pair
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}^{m}
and
g^{-1}U
intersects exactly one set from the pair
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}^{m-2}
. Moreover,

(4.2)

g^{-m}\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}_{i}^{m}}U\right)=\left\{x\mid\varphi_{i}(x)\in\left[\frac{1}{\left|m\right|+3},\frac{1}{\left|m\right|+2}\right]\right\}.

It follows that for any
M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, setting

{\mathcal{V}}_{i}^{M}=\bigvee_{m=-M}^{M}{\mathcal{U}}_{i}^{m}

and using (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we have

X\setminus\bigcup_{U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{i}^{M}}U=\bigcup_{m=-M}^{M}\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}_{i}^{m}}U\right)\prec 4\left[\left(\partial U_{i}\right)^{\delta}\right].

Finally, for
k=1,\ldots,n
, let

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}{\mathcal{V}}_{i}^{k-i}

and note that
U_{k}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{k}^{0}
, which allows us to define a subcollection of
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
by

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\prime}=\left(\bigvee_{i=1}^{k-1}{\mathcal{V}}_{i}^{k-i}\right)\vee\{U_{k}^{\prime}\}=\left\{U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}\mid U\subset U_{k}^{\prime}\right\}.

We observe the following easy facts. The first four of them are clear and the last one follows from the property of the collections
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}^{m}
mentioned above.

(i)

For any distinct
U,V\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\prime}
the closures
\overline{U}
and
\overline{V}
are disjoint.

(ii)

Any set
U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\prime}
is
\{e,g\}
-free.

(iii)

If
U,V
are sets in
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\prime}
then
gU\cap V=\emptyset
.

(iv)

If
k^{\prime}<k
then
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
refines
{\mathcal{V}}_{k^{\prime}}
.

(v)

If
U
is in
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\prime}
and
k^{\prime}<k
then each of the sets
g^{-1}U
and
gU
intersects at most one set from
{\mathcal{V}}_{k^{\prime}}
.

To construct the desired sets
V_{1},V_{2}
, and
V_{3}
we now inductively define a coloring function
c(x)\in\{1,2,3\}
for some points
x
in the space. This procedure has
n
steps and at step
k
we ensure that for any
U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\prime}
the value
c(x)
is defined for all
x\in U
and
c(x)=c(y)
for any
x,y\in U
(that is,
c
is constant on
U
).

Assume we are at step
k
of the construction and let
U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\prime}
. If
U
intersects some previously colored set
V\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}
for
k^{\prime}<k
then it is fully contained in it by item (iv) above. In this case, the function
c
is already defined and is constant on
U
. If not, then by item (v) and the inductive assumption,
c
takes at most one value on
g^{-1}U
and at most one value on
gU
. Thus, we can choose a color
\sigma\in\{1,2,3\}
that is distinct from either of those and set
c(x)=\sigma
for all
x\in U
. By items (i), (ii), and (iii) this procedure can be done simultaneously for all sets
U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\prime}
.

Now, for
j=1,2,3
let

V_{j}=\{x\mid c(x)=j\}.

Each of these sets by construction is a union of open sets and hence open. Since
c(x)\neq c(gx)
each of them is
\{e,g\}
-free. Clearly, these sets also have pairwise disjoint closures. Finally,

V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}\sqcup V_{3}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}\bigcup_{U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\prime}}U\supset\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}^{\prime}\right)\cap\left(\bigcup_{U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{n}}U\right)

and therefore

[\partial V_{1}]+[\partial V_{2}]+[\partial V_{3}]+[X\setminus(V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3})]\prec 2[X\setminus(V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup V_{3})]\\
\prec 2\left(\left[X\setminus\bigcup_{U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{n}}U\right]+\left[X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}^{\prime}\right]\right)\prec 8\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\left(\partial U_{i}\right)^{\delta}\right]+\left[X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}^{-\delta}\right]\right).

∎

We are now ready to prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.7
.

In Definition
4.1
we may assume that the number of sets in the collection
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
satisfies

n\leq 3^{\left|F\right|},

independently of the marker set
V
(for property FCSB) or the parameter
\varepsilon
(for property FCSB in measure). We may also assume that the sets
U_{i}
have pairwise disjoint closures.

Proof.

Let
F\subset G
be a finite set containing the identity, let
V
be a given marker set and use Lemma
3.11
to choose a marker set
U
such that

8\left|F\right|[U]\prec V.

Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an
F
-free cover with

\sum_{i=1}^{n}[\partial U_{i}]+\left[X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\right]\prec U.

Choose a small enough
\delta>0
so that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\left(\partial U_{i}\right)^{\delta}\right]+\left[X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}^{-\delta}\right]\prec U.

For each
g\in F\setminus\{e\}
apply Lemma
4.6
to obtain a collection
{\mathcal{U}}^{g}=\left\{U_{1}^{g},U_{2}^{g},U_{3}^{g}\right\}
of
\{e,g\}
-free sets with disjoint closures such that

\left[\partial U_{1}^{g}\right]+\left[\partial U_{2}^{g}\right]+\left[\partial U_{3}^{g}\right]+\left[X\setminus(U_{1}^{g}\cup U_{2}^{g}\cup U_{3}^{g})\right]\prec 8\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\left(\partial U_{i}\right)^{\delta}\right]+\left[X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}^{-\delta}\right]\right)\prec 8[U].

Then the collection

{\mathcal{U}}=\bigvee_{g\in F\setminus\{e\}}{\mathcal{U}}^{g}

has cardinality
\left|{\mathcal{U}}\right|\leq 3^{\left|F\right|-1}
and consists of
F
-free sets with pairwise disjoint closures. Moreover,

\sum_{W\in{\mathcal{U}}}[\partial W]+\left[X\setminus\bigcup_{W\in{\mathcal{U}}}W\right]\prec 8\left|F\right|[U]\prec V,

which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Theorem
4.7
above can in fact be further improved to conclude that
n
can be chosen to be at most
\left|F\right|+1
. However, in the present work it is only important for us that it does not depend on the marker set

.

5.
From FCSB to URPC

5.1.
Equivalence between FCSB in measure and URP

A variation of the Ornstein-Weiss algorithm allows one to prove that free actions of amenable groups on zero-dimensional spaces always have URP (
[
17
]
). Importantly, the number of steps in the algorithm depends only on the prescribed invariance
(K,\delta)
and the number of elements in the initial cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.

Now passing to the general case of an action with property FCSB in measure, the reduction done in Theorem
4.7
allows one to assume that the latter also depends only on
(K,\delta)
. Thus, we may ensure that the error coming from the boundaries remains negligible even after proceeding with the algorithm, and essentially treat the construction as if we are in a zero-dimensional setting. Note that we shall not give the full proof of Theorem
5.2
and will only illustrate the effect of the boundaries described above. The rest of the argument is by now standard and we refer to
[
17
]
for the full details.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
17
]
, Lemma 3.1)
.

Let
K
be a finite subset of
G
and let
\delta>0
. Then there is an
\varepsilon>0
such that if
F
is a
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant subset of
G
then every set
F^{\prime}\subset F
with
\left|F^{\prime}\right|\geq(1-\varepsilon)\left|F\right|
is
(K,\delta)
-invariant.

Theorem 5.2
.

An action has FCSB in measure if and only if it has URP.

Proof.

Proposition
4.5
gives one of the implications so it remains to show that property FCSB in measure implies URP.

Let
K\subset G
be finite and let
\delta>0
. Choose a constant
1/2>\varepsilon>0
that satisfies the conclusion of Lemma
5.1
and such that additionally
\varepsilon(1+\delta)<1
. Choose
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
(1-\varepsilon)^{n}<\varepsilon
. Fix a
\beta>0
with

\left(1+\beta\right)^{-1}\left(1-\left(1-\left(1+\beta\right)\varepsilon\right)^{n}\right)>1-\varepsilon,

and choose nonempty
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant sets
\{e\}\in F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\ldots\subset F_{n}\subset G
such that for every
1\leq i<j\leq n
the set
F_{j}
is
\left(F_{i}^{-1},\beta(1-\varepsilon)\right)
-invariant. Set
F=F_{n}^{-1}F_{n}
.

For brevity, denote
m=3^{\left|F\right|}
. By Theorem
4.7
, there exists a collection
U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}
of
F
-free open sets with

(5.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(\partial U_{i})+\mu\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}U_{i}\right)<\frac{\varepsilon}{\left|F^{nm+1}\right|}

for any
G
-invariant probability measure
\mu
. Set

{\mathcal{U}}_{i}=\{U_{i},X\setminus\overline{U_{i}}\},

and for a finite subset
L\subset G
define

{\mathcal{U}}^{L}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{m}\bigvee_{g\in L}g{\mathcal{U}}_{i}.

We now construct the Rokhlin towers using the Ornstein-Weiss algorithm. This inductive algorithm has
nm
steps and runs through all pairs
\{(i,j)\mid 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m\}
in lexicographical order. Set

L_{i,j}=F^{(i-1)m+j-1},

where
L_{1,1}=F^{0}=\{e\}
. At step
(i,j)
we add to the castle a collection of towers such that their shapes are subsets of
F_{n-i+1}
and their bases are elements of
{\mathcal{U}}^{L_{i,j}}
belonging to
U_{j}
(as
{\mathcal{U}}^{L_{i,j}}
refines
{\mathcal{U}}_{j}
every element of it is either a subset of
U_{j}
or is disjoint from it).

For the first step, we add the tower
(F_{n},U_{1})
to the castle. Assume that a collection of towers
\{(S_{k},V_{k})\}_{k=1}^{r}
has been constructed and we are now at step
(i,j)\neq(1,1)
. Let

(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})=\begin{cases}(i,j-1)&\mbox{if}\ j>1\\
(i-1,m)&\mbox{if}\ j=1.\end{cases}

Let
V
be a set in
{\mathcal{U}}^{L_{i,j}}
belonging to
U_{j}
. Note that for any
g\in F
the collection
{\mathcal{U}}^{L_{i,j}}
refines the collection
g{\mathcal{U}}^{L_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}}
. As both
F_{n-i+1}
and
S_{k}
are subsets of
F_{n}
and
F=F_{n}^{-1}F_{n}
, it follows that for any
g\in F_{n-i+1}
, any
1\leq k\leq r
, and any
h\in S_{k}
, the set
gV
is either fully contained in
hV_{k}
or is disjoint from it. Thus, the set

F_{n-i+1}^{\prime}=\{g\in F_{n-i+1}\mid gx\not\in\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{r}S_{k}V_{k}\}

is independent from the chosen point
x\in V
. Finally, if
\left|F_{n-i+1}^{\prime}\right|\geq(1-\varepsilon)\left|F_{n-i+1}\right|
then we add the tower
(F_{n-i+1}^{\prime},V)
to the castle (and otherwise do nothing).

Clearly, the construction yields a collection of pairwise disjoint towers with
(K,\delta)
-invariant shapes. Moreover, let
1\leq i\leq n
and let
\{(S_{k},V_{k})\}_{k=1}^{r}
be the castle obtained after step
(i,m)
. Then it is easy to see that for any
x\in X
we have that

\left|F_{n-i+1}x\cap\left(\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{m}S_{k}V_{k}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}FL_{i,m}\left(\partial U_{j}\right)\cup F\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}U_{j}\right)\right)\right|>\varepsilon\left|F_{n-i+1}\right|.

Using the standard machinery (see
[
17
, Section 3]
), one shows that if
W
is the footprint of the final castle constructed as a result of the procedure above, then

\overline{D}\left(W\cup F^{nm+1}\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\left(\partial U_{j}\right)\cup\left(X\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}U_{j}\right)\right)\right)>1-\varepsilon

and thus, by (
5.1
), we conclude that

\overline{D}(W)>1-2\varepsilon.

∎

5.2.
Local tiling algorithms and URPC

In this subsection we take an orbitwise perspective, as opposed to the spatial arguments of the previous subsection. We introduce the notion of a local algorithm, which has long been known to be the bridge between solving discrete and descriptive versions of a problem. In particular, this gives a slightly different proof of Theorem
5.2
, relying on the fact that the classical Ornstein-Weiss algorithm is local (Theorem
5.11
). Unfortunately, this algorithm only produces a
(1-\varepsilon)
-tiling which limits one to proving URP. The main result of this section is that if a group has a local tiling algorithm that produces an
exact
tiling then one can similarly show that FCSB implies URPC (Theorem
5.14
). As we shall see in the next subsection, many amenable groups admit exact local tiling algorithms.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
Q,R
be arbitrary sets and let
W\subset G
be a finite set. Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset Q^{G}
be a
right
-invariant subset of maps from
G
to
Q
and consider it’s restriction
\left.{\mathcal{F}}\right|_{W}
to the set
W
. Let
\varphi:\left.{\mathcal{F}}\right|_{W}\to R
be an arbitrary map. For any
x\in{\mathcal{F}}
we can define a function
\hat{\varphi}_{x}:G\to R
by

\hat{\varphi}_{x}(g)=\varphi\left(\left(x_{hg}\right)_{h\in W}\right).

Thus the assignment
x\mapsto\hat{\varphi}_{x}
is a map from
{\mathcal{F}}
to
R^{G}
.

Given an arbitrary function
\Phi:{\mathcal{F}}\to R^{G}
(which we usually write as a collection of maps
\Phi_{x}:G\to R
for
x\in{\mathcal{F}}
) we will say that it is
local
(alternatively,
depends locally on
{\mathcal{F}}
or
depends locally on
x
) if there exists some
W
and
\varphi
as above such that
\Phi=\hat{\varphi}
. In this case we will additionally say that
\Phi
is
determined by the window
W
. Note that any local function is equivariant with respect to the right action of
G
on the shifts.

In the special case when
R=\{0,1\}
we will identify the space
R^{G}
with the space of subsets of
G
in the usual way. Thus, given a collection of subsets
A_{x},x\in{\mathcal{F}}
we say that it depends locally on
{\mathcal{F}}
if the assignment
x\mapsto\mathbbm{1}_{A_{x}}
is local.

Remark 5.4
.

We start by recording the following easy observations.

•

Let
\Phi:{\mathcal{F}}\to R^{G}
and
\Psi:{\mathcal{F}}\to P^{G}
be local maps. Then the join map
(\Phi,\Psi):{\mathcal{F}}\to(R\times P)^{G}
is local as well.

•

Let
\Phi:{\mathcal{F}}\to R^{G}
and
\Psi:\mathrm{Im}(\Phi)\subset R^{G}\to P^{G}
be local maps. Then the composition
\Psi\circ\Phi:{\mathcal{F}}\to P^{G}
is local as well.

In the Definition
5.3
above the right-invariant set
{\mathcal{F}}
should be considered as a space of possible input data. We now introduce the main family of subshifts that will be used as the data
{\mathcal{F}}
.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
F
be a finite symmetric subset of
G
containing the identity
e
and set
n=n(F)=3^{\left|F\right|}
. Define

{\mathcal{C}}_{F}=\left\{\left(x_{g}\right)_{g\in G}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}^{G}\mid x_{h}\neq x_{gh}\ \mbox{for any}\ g\in F\setminus\{e\}\ \mbox{and any}\ h\in G\right\}.

In other words,
{\mathcal{C}}_{F}
is the space of regular
n
-colorings of the graph
\mathrm{Cay}(G,F)
.

Remark 5.6
.

Let
W\subset G
be any subset of
G
. Then the restriction
\left.{\mathcal{C}}_{F}\right|_{W}
is the space of regular
n
-colorings of
\left.\mathrm{Cay}(G,F)\right|_{W}
, that is the set

\left\{\left(x_{g}\right)_{g\in W}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}^{W}\mid x_{h}\neq x_{gh}\ \mbox{for any}\ g\in F\setminus\{e\}\ \mbox{and any}\ h\in W\ \mbox{such that}\ gh\in W\right\}.

Indeed, it is clear that any element in the restriction
\left.{\mathcal{C}}_{F}\right|_{W}
belongs to the set above. Conversely, a simple greedy coloring algorithm shows that any regular
n
-coloring of
\left.\mathrm{Cay}(G,F)\right|_{W}
extends to a regular
n
-coloring of
\mathrm{Cay}(G,F)
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
F,F^{\prime}\subset G
be finite symmetric subsets of
G
containing the identity such that
F\subset F^{\prime}
. Then there is a local map from
{\mathcal{C}}_{F^{\prime}}
to
{\mathcal{C}}_{F}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in{\mathcal{C}}_{F^{\prime}}
be given. As usual, set
n=3^{\left|F\right|}
. We inductively define a function
y:G\to\{1,2\ldots,n\}
on each color class of
x
(that is, the sets
\{g\in G\mid x(g)=k\}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,3^{\left|F^{\prime}\right|}
) such that at each step the new values
y(g)
depend only the local data of
x
and previously defined values of
y
. Since the number of steps are finite, this ensures that the final assignment
x\mapsto y
is local.

The construction is again a simple greedy algorithm. Specifically, assume that
y(g)
has been defined for all
g
with
x(g)<k
and we are now at step
k
. For any
g\in G
with
x(g)=k
set

y(g)=\min(\{1\leq i\leq n\mid y(hg)\neq i\ \mbox{for any}\ h\in F\ \mbox{such that}\ x(hg)<k\}).

It is easily seen that this procedure results in an element
y\in{\mathcal{C}}_{F}
.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

In the proof above
y
clearly only takes values in
\{1,2,\ldots,\left|F\right|+1\}
. This should be compared to Remark
4.8
. Once again, for our purposes the exact number is not important.

Definition 5.9
.

Let
G
be an amenable group. An
approximate tiling
of
G
is a map
S:G\to 2^{D}\sqcup\{\star\}
, where
D
is some finite subset of
G
, such that

S(g)g\cap S(g^{\prime})g^{\prime}=\emptyset

whenever
g,g^{\prime}\in G
are distinct and
S(g),S(g^{\prime})\neq\star
.

We will refer to the elements
g\in G
with
S(g)\neq\star
as
centers
and to the set
C(S)=\{g\in G\mid S(g)\neq\star\}
as the
center set
. A pair
(S(g),g)
, where
g\in C(S)
, is called a
tile
and the set
S(g)
is its
shape
. An approximate tiling gives a partition of the group

G=\bigsqcup_{g\in C(S)}S(g)g\sqcup\left(G\setminus\bigsqcup_{g\in C(S)}S(g)g\right).

The set
G\setminus\bigsqcup_{g\in C(S)}S(g)g
is called the
remainder
of the approximate tiling. We additionally define the
relative position
function
T:G\to D\sqcup\{\star\}
by

T(g)=\begin{cases}h\in G&\mbox{if}\ h^{-1}g\in C(S)\ \mbox{and}\ h\in S(h^{-1}g)\ (\mbox{and hence}\ g\in S(h^{-1}g)h^{-1}g),\\
\star&\mbox{if no such}\ h\ \mbox{exists}.\end{cases}

We say that the approximate tiling
S
is

(i)

(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant
if for any
g\in C(S)
the shape
S(g)
is a
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant subset of
G
,

(ii)

(1-\varepsilon)
-covering relative to a finite subset
L\subset G
if

\overline{D}_{L}\left(G\setminus\bigsqcup_{g\in C(S)}S(g)g\right)<\varepsilon,

(iii)

exact
if

Remark 5.10
.

Note that the center set, the remainder set, and the relative position function all depend locally on

. Thus, by Remark

depend locally on some input data

then so do each of the above.

The classic Ornstein-Weiss algorithm produces a
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant

-covering tiling based on a

. We remark that the main result of that paper is that every amenable group admits an exact tiling – however, not necessarily one that depends locally on a coloring.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let

be an amenable group, let

be finite, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exist finite (symmetric) sets

and a local function

(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant

-covering relative to the set

approximate tiling.

For any free action of
G
on a measure space, Borel space, or zero-dimensional topological space, one can properly color the Shreier graph
{\mathrm{Shr}}(X,F)
into
n=3^{\left|F\right|}
colors so that each color class is measurable, Borel, or clopen respectively. This is equivalent to saying that there is a partition

X=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}

of the space into
F
-free measurable, Borel, or clopen subsets

. This coloring thus gives a natural map from the space
X
to the subshift
{\mathcal{C}}_{F}
by

x\mapsto(c(gx))_{g\in G},

where
c:X\to\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
is the coloring function defined by setting
c\equiv i
on

. By composing the map into the subshift with a local algorithm that solves a certain discrete problem, one can then obtain a measurable, Borel, or clopen solution. For instance, composing with the tiling function
S
given by Theorem
5.11
recovers the original Ornstein-Weiss theorem for actions on measure spaces, as well as its Borel and zero-dimensional versions.

Although we are not in the zero-dimensional setting, the conclusion of Theorem
4.7
can be regarded as an analogy to the coloring described above — the space can be partitioned into
n
open
F
-free sets up to an arbitrarily small remainder. Naturally, we do not have the map from
X
to the subshift
{\mathcal{C}}_{F}
anymore as some points remain uncolored. However, if we want to apply a local algortihm determined by some window
W
to a point
x\in X
, we do not need to look at the whole orbit of
x
— it is sufficient for the set
Wx
to be colored. As the window
W
is independent of the smallness of the remainder, we may choose the latter so that most of the points in the space possess this property. We refer to the proof of Theorem
5.14
below for the details of this argument.

Note that, in particular, applying the algorithm given by Theorem
5.11
gives a slightly different proof of Theorem
5.2
from the previous subsection (here we can estimate

, which is exactly the set that appears in the proof). What this perspective shows, however, is that the precise algorithm used was not important, only the fact that it was local.

Definition 5.12
.

A group
G
has a
local tiling algorithm
if for any finite
K\subset G
and

there exist finite (symmetric) sets
F,D\subset G
and a local function

such that for any

the map

is a
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant exact tiling of

.

One feature of local maps is that we can apply it even when the input data is incomplete to obtain a partial structure. We formalize this intuition in the following (slightly cumbersome) lemma.

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
G
be a group with a local tiling algorithm. Let
K\subset G
be finite, let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
F,D,S
be as in Definition
5.12
. Since
S
is a local function, there is a finite window
W
and a map
\varphi:\left.{\mathcal{C}}_{F}\right|_{W}\to 2^{D}\sqcup\{\star\}
such that

S_{x}(g)=\varphi\left((x_{hg})_{h\in W}\right).

Now, let
A\subset G
be an arbitrary set and let
c\in\left.{\mathcal{C}}_{F}\right|_{A}
be a proper
F
-coloring of
A
. Consider the set of centers
C_{A}=\{g\in G\mid Wg\subset A\ \mbox{and}\ \varphi\left((c_{hg})_{h\in W}\right)\neq\star\}
and the corresponding collection of tiles
\{(\varphi\left((c_{hg})_{h\in W}\right),g)\}_{g\in C_{A}}
. Then the sets
\varphi\left((c_{hg})_{h\in W}\right)g
are pairwise disjoint for
g\in C_{A}
and

A\setminus\left(DW^{-1}(G\setminus A)\right)\subset\bigsqcup_{g\in C_{A}}\varphi\left((c_{hg})_{h\in W}\right)g.

Proof.

Let
x\in{\mathcal{C}}_{F}
be an arbitrary extension of
c
to the whole group
G
(see Remark
5.6
). Note that
\{(\varphi\left((c_{hg})_{h\in W}\right),g)\}_{g\in C_{A}}
is a subcollection of tiles in the tiling

, hence the sets
\varphi\left((c_{hg})_{h\in W}\right)g
are pairwise disjoint. Moreover, we have that the set of remaining centers
C(S_{x})\setminus C_{A}
is clearly a subset of
W^{-1}(G\setminus A)
, thus

\bigsqcup_{g\in C(S_{x})\setminus C_{A}}\varphi\left((x_{hg})_{h\in W}\right)g\subset DW^{-1}(G\setminus A).

Since the tiling is exact, the second claim follows.
∎

We can now prove the main result in this subsection, which shows that a local tiling algorithm is a bridge between property FCSB and URPC.

Theorem 5.14
.

Suppose that an amenable group
G
has a local tiling algorithm. Let
G\curvearrowright X
be an action that has property FCSB. Then it has URPC.

Proof.

Let
K\subset G
be a finite set, let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
U
be a marker set. Since
G
has a local tiling algorithm there exist finite symmetric sets
F,D\subset G
and a local function

S:{\mathcal{C}}_{F}\to\left(2^{D}\sqcup\{\star\}\right)^{G}

such that for any
x\in{\mathcal{C}}_{F}
the element
S_{x}
is a
(K,\varepsilon)
-invariant exact tiling of
G
. By definition of a local function, there is a finite window
W\subset G
and a function
\varphi:\left.{\mathcal{C}}_{F}\right|_{W}\to 2^{D}\sqcup\{\star\}
such that

S_{x}(g)=\varphi\left((x_{hg})_{h\in W}\right).

Recall (Remark
5.6
) that
\left.{\mathcal{C}}_{F}\right|_{W}
is simply the space of
n
-colorings of the set

(recall that

).

Use Theorem
4.7
and Lemma

to find an

\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
such that

\left|DW^{-1}\right|\left[X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}\right]\prec U.

Define a partial coloring function

by

c(y)=\begin{cases}i&\mbox{if}\ y\in U_{i},\\
\star&\mbox{if}\ y\in X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}.\end{cases}

Now, let

be a configuration such that

(i.e.,
\mathrm{x}
is central) and define

Consider the collection

\mathrm{x}
ranges over all central configurations in

Indeed, first note that it is clear that the bases

-invariant. Now, let

with the group

in the obvious way. For each central configuration

let

be the set of elements

ranges over all central configurations and

with

It then follows that each pair

∎

Definition 5.15
.

Let

implements the subequivalence between

and
b
(here we again enlarged the set
F
using Lemma
5.7
). Finally, the subequivalence between
A
and
B
is obtained by composing

\tilde{R}_{x,b}\circ R_{(x,a,b)}\circ\tilde{R}_{x,A}.

∎

6.
Mean dimension and shift embeddability for actions of amenable groups

6.1.
Encoding URPC covers

Definition 6.1
.

Let
F:X\to Z
be a function and let
U\subset X
be a set. We say that
F

encodes

U
if

F^{-1}(F(U))=U.

Equivalently, for every pair of points
x,y\in X
such that
x\in U
and
y\not\in U
, we have

F(x)\neq F(y).

In other words, by knowing the value
F(x)
we can determine whether it is in
U
or not.

We say that
F
encodes a collection of sets
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
if it encodes each of the sets
U_{i}
in the collection.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be some function on
X
. We associate to it a function
I_{f}:X\to Y^{G}
defined by

I_{f}(x)=\left(f(gx)\right)_{g\in G}.

If
f
is continuous then so is
I_{f}
.

We collect some easy but important observations.

Remark 6.3
.

If
I_{f}
encodes
U
then it also encodes
gU
for every
g\in G
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
K\subset G
be finite, let
\varepsilon>0
, let
\left(\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m},\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\right)
be a
(K,\varepsilon)
-URPC witness, and let
f:X\to Y
be a function. If
I_{f}
encodes the collection

\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\sqcup\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}

then it encodes the whole URPC cover

\{gV_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n,g\in S_{i}}\sqcup\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}.

Remark 6.5
.

Let
U
be a set and suppose that

U=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}U_{i}

for some finite collection of sets
U_{i}
. If
I_{f}
encodes the collection
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
then it also encodes the set
U
. By Remark
6.3
, this assumption is equivalent to
I_{f}
encoding the shifted collection
\{g_{i}U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
for any choice of elements
g_{i}\in G
.

We now prove the main result of this subsection.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
be an action with URPC and let
U
be a marker set. Then there exists a continuous function
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}):X\to[0,1]^{2}
supported on
U
such that for any finite
K\subset G
and
\varepsilon>0
the map
I_{\varphi}
encodes some URPC cover for
K
and
\varepsilon
.

Moreover,
\varphi
can be chosen so that the open support of both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
is equal to
U
and so that
\varphi_{1}(x)>\varphi_{2}(x)
for any
x\in U
.

Proof.

Choose an increasing sequence of finite set
K_{m}
with
\bigcup_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}K_{m}=G
and a sequence of numbers
\varepsilon_{m}>0
with
\varepsilon_{m}\to 0
. Use Lemma
3.19
to find a
(K_{1},\varepsilon_{1})
-URPC witness, say
\left(\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m},\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\right)
, such that its encoding element
E
satisfies

E=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[V_{i}]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}[U_{j}]\prec U.

By Remark
3.25
, we can find some
\delta>0
such that for any open sets
V_{i}^{\prime}
and
U_{j}^{\prime}
with
V_{i}^{-\delta}\subset V_{i}^{\prime}\subset V_{i}
and
U_{j}^{-\delta}\subset U_{j}^{\prime}\subset U_{j}
, the data
\left(\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m},\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\right)
is still a a
(K_{1},\varepsilon_{1})
-URPC witness. Note that we clearly have that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\,\overline{V_{i}^{-\delta}}\,\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[\,\overline{U_{j}^{-\delta}}\,\right]\prec U.

Now, using the definition of subequivalence of sets, Remark
3.6
, and Remark
6.5
, we can find some finite collection
\{W^{1}_{k}\}_{k\in J_{1}}
of open sets such that

(i)

\overline{W^{1}_{k}}
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
U
and

(ii)

if
f:X\to Y
is a function such that
I_{f}
encodes the collection
\{W^{1}_{k}\}_{k\in J_{1}}
then it also encodes some collection
\{V_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{n}\sqcup\{U_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j=1}^{m}
with
V_{i}^{\prime}
and
U_{j}^{\prime}
as above.

It follows from Corollary
6.4
that if
f
is as in item (ii),
I_{f}
encodes a URPC cover for a
K_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{1}
.

Set

W^{1}=\bigcup_{k\in J_{1}}W^{1}_{k}\subset U

and observe that it is a marker set, since

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\,\overline{V_{i}^{-\delta}}\,\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left[\,\overline{U_{j}^{-\delta}}\,\right]\prec W^{1}.

Thus, using the same argument, we can inductively construct collections
\{W^{m}_{k}\}_{k\in J_{m}}
for
m\geq 2
such that

(i)

\overline{W^{m}_{k}}
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
W^{m-1}
and

(ii)

if
I_{f}
encodes the collection
\{W^{m}_{k}\}_{k\in J_{m}}
then it also encodes a URPC cover for
K_{m}
and
\varepsilon_{m}
,

where
W^{m}=\bigcup_{k\in J_{m}}W^{m}_{k}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that any set
W^{m}_{k}
lies in a single set from the collection
\{W^{m-1}_{k}\}_{k\in J_{m-1}}
. For consistent notation we also set
W^{0}_{1}=W^{0}=U
and
J_{0}=\{1\}
. By item (ii), to finish the proof it is sufficient to find a function
\varphi:X\to[0,1]^{2}
supported on
U
such that
\varphi
(and thus also
I_{\varphi}
) encodes all of the collections
\{W^{m}_{k}\}_{k\in J_{m}}
.

Consider a rooted tree
T
defined as follows.

•

For all
m\geq 0
there are
\left|J_{m}\right|
vertices on levels
2m
and
2m+1
which we denote by
\{v^{0}_{m,k}\}_{k\in J_{m}}
and
\{v^{1}_{m,k}\}_{k\in J_{m}}
respectively.

•

Every vertex
v^{0}_{m,k}
is connected to
v^{1}_{m,k}
and these are the only edges between levels
2m
and
2m+1
.

•

A vertex
v^{1}_{m,k}
on level
2m+1
is connected to a vertex
v^{0}_{m+1,l}
on level
2m+2
if and only if the set
W^{m+1}_{l}
is a subset of
W^{m}_{k}
.

The topological space
T\cup\partial T
is a one-dimensional and compact, and there exists an embedding
\tau=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}):T\cup\partial T\to[0,1]^{2}
which maps the root
v^{0}_{0,1}
to the point
(0,0)
. We may additionally choose this embedding so that it satisfies

\tau_{1}(t)>\tau_{2}(t)

for any point
t\in T\cup\partial T
and so that
\tau(T\cup\partial T\setminus\{v^{0}_{0,1}\})\subset(0,1)^{2}
.

For a vertex
v^{0}_{m,k}
let
T^{m}_{k}
be the subtree rooted at
v^{0}_{m,k}
. Naturally,
T^{m}_{k}\cup\partial T^{m}_{k}
is a compact subset of
T\cup\partial T
.

Now choose a continuous function
\varphi:X\to\tau\left(T\cup\partial T\right)\subset[0,1]^{2}
such that

\varphi(x)\in\tau\left(T^{m}_{k}\cup\partial T^{m}_{k}\setminus\{v^{0}_{m,k}\}\right)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in W^{m}_{k}.

For instance, this function can be constructed as a limit of functions corresponding to finite parts of the tree. By construction,
\varphi
encodes every set
W^{m}_{K}
and has the additional required properties by the choice of
\tau
.
∎

The lemma above is sufficient to deal with the case
M\geq 2
. A slight additional modification will allow us to also handle the case
M=1
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
be an action with URPC and let
L\subset G
be finite. Then there exists an open set
U\subset X
such that

\left|Lx\cap\overline{U}\right|\leq 2\quad\mbox{for any}\ x\in X

and a function
\varphi:X\to[0,1]
supported on
U
such that for any finite
K\subset G
and
\varepsilon>0
the map
I_{\varphi}
encodes a URPC cover for
K
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
L
contains the identity, and pick an arbitrary element
g\in G
. Set
L^{\prime}=L^{-1}\cup L^{-1}g
and let
U^{\prime}
be an
L^{\prime}
-free marker set. As usual, we can additionally assume that
\overline{U}
is also
L^{\prime}
-free. By Lemma
6.6
, there is a function
\varphi^{\prime}=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}):X\to[0,1]^{2}
that encodes some URPC cover for any
K
and
\varepsilon
and such that the open support of both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
is equal to
U^{\prime}
and
\varphi_{1}>\varphi_{2}
on
U^{\prime}
. Set
U=U^{\prime}\sqcup gU^{\prime}
and define a function
\varphi:X\to[0,1]
by

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{1}(x)&\mbox{if}\ x\in U^{\prime},\\
\varphi_{2}(g^{-1}x)&\mbox{if}\ x\in gU^{\prime},\\
0&\mbox{if}\ x\in X\setminus\left(U^{\prime}\sqcup gU^{\prime}\right).\end{cases}

Clearly, we have

\left|Lx\cap\overline{U}\right|\leq 2\quad\mbox{for any}\ x\in X.

Next, note that
x\in U^{\prime}
if and only if
\varphi(x)>\varphi(gx)>0
, hence
I_{\varphi}
encodes the set
U^{\prime}
. It follows that, by knowing
I_{\varphi}
, it also possible to reconstruct the original function
\varphi^{\prime}
using the formula

\varphi^{\prime}(x)=\begin{cases}(0,0)&\mbox{if}\ x\not\in U^{\prime},\\
(\varphi(x),\varphi(gx))&\mbox{if}\ x\in U^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Thus,
I_{\varphi}
encodes a URPC cover for any
K
and
\varepsilon
.
∎

6.2.
Mean dimension and shift embedding

Definition 6.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be an a collection of open subsets of a compact space
X
. The
order

\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{U}})
of this collection is defined to be

\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{U}})=\sup_{x\in X}\left|\{U\in{\mathcal{U}}\mid x\in U\}\right|.

If
{\mathcal{U}}
is in fact an open cover of
X
, then we define the
dimension
of the cover
{\mathcal{U}}
to be

\dim({\mathcal{U}})=\inf_{{\mathcal{V}}}\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{V}}),

where
{\mathcal{V}}
ranges over all refinements of
{\mathcal{U}}
. Note that the
covering dimension
of the space
X
is defined to be

\dim(X)=\sup_{{\mathcal{U}}}\dim({\mathcal{U}}),

where
{\mathcal{U}}
ranges over all open covers of
X
.

Definition 6.9
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
be an action of an amenable group on a compact space and let
M\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
be a nonnegative number. We say that
G\curvearrowright X
has
mean dimension less than
M
and write

{\rm mdim}(G\curvearrowright X)<M

if there exists some
\gamma>0
so that for every open cover
{\mathcal{U}}
there exists a finite set
F\subset G
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for every
(F,\varepsilon)
-invariant set
S\subset G
we have

\dim\left(\bigvee_{g\in S}g^{-1}{\mathcal{U}}\right)<(M-\gamma)\left|S\right|.

We define

{\rm mdim}(G\curvearrowright X)=\inf\left(\{M\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\mid{\rm mdim}(G\curvearrowright X)<M\}\right).

If the set above is empty we set
{\rm mdim}(G\curvearrowright X)=\infty
.

Definition 6.10
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be an open cover of the space
X
and let
\{\varphi_{U}\}_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}}
be an associated partition of unity. We say that
\{\varphi_{U}\}_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}}
is
honest
if for any
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
we have

\overset{\circ}{\supp}(\varphi_{U})=U,

where
\overset{\circ}{\supp}
denotes the open support.

It is a basic exercise to show that any finite open cover has an associated honest partition of unity.

Remark 6.11
.

Given a cover
{\mathcal{U}}
of the space
X
, the
nerve complex
of
{\mathcal{U}}
is the simplicial complex with vertices
\{v_{U}\}_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}}
such that
v_{U_{0}},v_{U_{1}},\ldots,v_{U_{k}}
span a simplex of dimension
k
if and only if

U_{0}\cap U_{1}\cap\ldots\cap U_{k}\neq\emptyset.

A partition of unity
\{\varphi_{U}\}_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}}
then allows one to construct a simplicial approximation of the space
X
by sending

x\mapsto\sum_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}}\varphi_{U}(x)v_{U}

in the nerve complex. If the partition of unity is honest, this simplicial approximation is
essential
.

The language of simplicial approximations is heavily used in many prior works on mean dimension (for instance,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
). In this paper, however, we deliberately aim to avoid invoking simplicial approximations (since, in our opinion, it reduces the clarity of the argument) and formulate everything in terms of covers and partitions of unity.

We need the following easy lemma. The proof is left as an exercise to the reader.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a finite open cover of the space, let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a finite collection of open sets, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists an honest partition of unity
\{\varphi_{U}\}_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}}
for the cover
{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}
such that

\left\|\sum_{U\in{\mathcal{V}}}\varphi_{U}\right\|<\varepsilon.

Definition 6.13
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
I\subset\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
. We naturally identify the cube
[0,1]^{N}
with
[0,1]^{\{1,2,\ldots,N\}}
and define the usual projection
\pi_{I}:[0,1]^{N}\to[0,1]^{I}
by

\pi_{I}\left((x_{i})_{i=1}^{N}\right)=(x_{i})_{i\in I}.

We always equip the cube
[0,1]^{I}
with the metric coming from the maximum norm

\left|(x_{i})_{i\in I}\right|_{\infty}=\max_{i\in I}\left|x_{i}\right|.

When
Y
is a topological space, the space of continuous function
F:Y\to[0,1]^{I}
is thus equipped with the metric coming from the uniform norm

\left\|F\right\|=\max_{y\in Y}\left|F(y)\right|_{\infty}.

The next statement is a variation of
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
F:Y\to[0,1]^{N}
be a continuous function, let
\gamma\geq 0
and
\delta>0
, let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a finite open cover of
Y
, and let
{\mathcal{V}}
be a finite collection of open sets such that

(i)

\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{U}})+\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{V}})\leq(1-\gamma)N/2
,

(ii)

for any pair of points
x,y\in U
in any
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
we have

\left|F(x)-F(y)\right|_{\infty}<\delta/2.

Then there exists a function
\tilde{F}:Y\to[0,1]^{N}
such that

\left\|F-\tilde{F}\right\|<\delta

and for any subset
I\subset\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
with
\left|I\right|>(1-\gamma)N
the function
\pi_{I}(\tilde{F})
encodes the cover
{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}
.

Proof.

Use Lemma
6.12
to find an honest partition of unity
\{\varphi_{U}\}_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}}
for the cover
{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}
such that

(6.1)

\left\|\sum_{U\in{\mathcal{V}}}\varphi_{U}\right\|<\frac{\delta}{4\left\|F\right\|}.

For each open set
U\in{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}
choose an arbitrary point
y_{U}\in U
. First, consider the function
F^{\prime}:Y\to[0,1]^{N}
given by

F^{\prime}(y)=\sum_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}}\varphi_{U}(y)F(y_{U}).

It follows from condition (ii) and (
6.1
) that

\left\|F-F^{\prime}\right\|<\delta/2+2\left\|F\right\|\frac{\delta}{4\left\|F\right\|}=\delta.

Denote

\kappa=\left\|F-F^{\prime}\right\|-\delta.

For each
U\in{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}
choose a vector
\xi_{U}\in B_{\kappa}(F(y_{U}))\cap[0,1]^{N}
such that for any subset
I\subset\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
with
\left|I\right|>(1-\gamma)N
and any collection
\{U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{m}\}\subset{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}
, where
m<(1-\gamma)N
, the vectors
\pi_{I}(\xi_{U_{1}}),\pi_{I}(\xi_{U_{2}}),\ldots,\pi_{I}(\xi_{U_{m}})
are linearly independent (this can be achieved, for instance, by choosing each vector
\xi_{U}
randomly from the set
B_{\kappa}(F(y_{U}))\cap[0,1]^{N}
). This, in particular, implies that for a vector
\xi\in[0,1]^{I}
there is at most one way to write it as a convex combination of less than
(1-\gamma)N/2
vectors from the set
\{\pi_{I}(\xi_{U})\}_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}}
. Define

\tilde{F}(y)=\sum_{U\in{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}}\varphi_{U}(y)\xi_{U}.

Using condition (i), it follows from the discussion above that if
I\subset\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
with
\left|I\right|>(1-\gamma)N
then the value
\pi_{I}(\tilde{F}(y))
determines all the coefficients
\varphi_{U}(y)
. Since the partition of unity is honest, it follows that
\pi_{I}(\tilde{F})
encodes the cover
{\mathcal{U}}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}
. Additionally, by our choices we have

\left\|F-\tilde{F}\right\|\leq\left\|F-F^{\prime}\right\|+\left\|F^{\prime}-\tilde{F}\right\|<\left\|F-F^{\prime}\right\|+\kappa=\delta.

∎

Definition 6.15
.

Recall that a map
F:(X,\rho)\to Y
is called an
\varepsilon
-embedding
if
F(x)\neq F(y)
whenever
\rho(x,y)>\varepsilon
.

Remark 6.16
.

Let
{\mathscr{C}}
be a nonempty closed subset (hence a Baire space) in the space of continuous functions
C(X,[0,1]^{M})
. It is an easy exercise to show that the sets

A_{\varepsilon}=\{f\in{\mathscr{C}}\mid I_{f}\ \mbox{is an}\ \varepsilon\mbox{-embedding}\}

are open. If one can additionally show that they are also dense in
{\mathscr{C}}
then
A_{\varepsilon}
are comeager and therefore

\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{1/n}\neq\emptyset.

Clearly, for any function
f
in this intersection the map
I_{f}
is a
G
-equivariant embedding of
X
into
\left([0,1]^{M}\right)^{G}
.

Theorem 6.17
.

If
G\curvearrowright X
has URPC and

{\rm mdim}(G\curvearrowright X)<M/2

then there exists a
G
-equivariant embedding of
X
into
\left([0,1]^{M}\right)^{G}
.

Proof.

It will be slightly more convenient for us to prove the result with the cube
[0,1]^{M}
replaced by
[-1,1]^{M}
. Choose a small enough
\gamma>0
so that

{\rm mdim}(G\curvearrowright X)<(1-\gamma)M/2.

Let
L\subset G
be a finite subset containing the identity such that
\left|L\right|>4/\gamma
. By Lemma
6.7
, there exists an open set
U
and a function
\varphi:X\to[0,1]
supported on it such that
I_{\varphi}
encodes some URPC cover for any
K
and
\varepsilon
and

\left|Lx\cap\overline{U}\right|\leq 2

for any
x\in X
. Define a closed subset of functions

{\mathscr{C}}=\{f\in C\left(X,[-1,1]^{M}\right)\mid\left.f\right|_{U}=(-\varphi,0,\ldots,0)\ \mbox{and}\ \left.f\right|_{X\setminus U}\geq 0)\}.

We immediately observe that for any
f=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{M})\in{\mathscr{C}}
we have

-\varphi(x)=\min(f_{1}(x),0)

and therefore if
I_{\varphi}
encodes a certain URPC cover then so does
I_{f}
for any
f\in{\mathscr{C}}
. By Remark
6.16
, it is sufficient to prove that the set of
f\in{\mathscr{C}}
such that
I_{f}
is an
\varepsilon
-embedding is dense.

Let
f\in{\mathscr{C}}
and let
\varepsilon,\delta>0
. In the rest of the proof we will construct a function
\tilde{f}\in{\mathscr{C}}
such that
I_{\tilde{f}}
is an
\varepsilon
-embedding and

\left\|f-\tilde{f}\right\|<\delta.

First, let
{\mathcal{W}}
be a finite open cover of
X
such that for any
W\in{\mathcal{W}}
we have that
\diam(W)<\varepsilon
and
\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|_{\infty}<\delta/2
for any pair of points
x,y\in W
. Choose a finite set
K\subset G
and an
\alpha>0
such that for any
(K,\alpha)
-invariant set
S\subset G
we have that
\left|Sx\cap\overline{U}\right|<\gamma\left|S\right|/2
for any
x\in X
and

\dim\left(\bigvee_{g\in S}g^{-1}{\mathcal{W}}\right)<\left((1-\gamma)M/2-\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{W}})\alpha\right)\left|S\right|.

We now need to prepare some data and some notation. Let
\left(\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m},\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\right)
be a URPC witness for
K
and
\alpha
such that
I_{\varphi}
encodes the corresponding cover
\{gV_{i}\colon 1\leq i\leq n,g\in S_{i}\}\sqcup\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
. As usual, we may assume that the sets
g\overline{V_{i}}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
and
g\in S_{i}
, are pairwise disjoint. Choose
\tau>0
small enough so that

(i)

gV_{i}^{\tau}
are pairwise disjoint for
1\leq i\leq n
and
g\in S_{i}
and

(ii)

\left|S_{i}x\cap U^{\tau}\right|<\gamma\left|S_{i}\right|/2
for any
x\in X
and any
1\leq i\leq n
.

Let
\psi:X\to[0,1]
be a bump function such that
\psi\equiv 1
on
\overline{U}
and
\supp\psi\subset U^{\tau}
and let
\psi_{i}:X\to[0,1]
for
1\leq i\leq n
be bump functions such that
\psi_{i}\equiv 1
on
\overline{V_{i}}
and
\supp\psi\subset V_{i}^{\tau}
. We remark that the functions
\{g\psi_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,g\in S_{i}\}
have pairwise disjoint supports and therefore the collection

\{g\psi_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,g\in S_{i}\}\sqcup\{1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{g\in S_{i}}g\psi_{i}\}

is a partition of unity associated to the cover

\{gV_{i}^{\tau}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,g\in S_{i}\}\sqcup\{X\setminus\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}\overline{V_{i}}\}.

Finally, define a function
f^{+}=(f^{+}_{1},f^{+}_{2},\ldots,f^{+}_{M}):X\to[0,1]^{M}
by setting

f^{+}_{k}(x)=\max(f_{k}(x),0).

Equivalently,
f^{+}=f
on
X\setminus U
and
f^{+}\equiv(0,0,\ldots,0)
on
U
.

Next, fix an index
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. By our choices, there is a finite open cover
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}
of the set
V_{i}^{\tau}
that refines
\left.\left(\bigvee_{g\in S_{i}}g^{-1}{\mathcal{W}}\right)\right|_{V_{i}^{\tau}}
and such that

\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{U}}_{i})<\left((1-\gamma)M/2-\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{W}})\alpha\right)\left|S_{i}\right|.

By definition of a URPC witness, any set of the form
g_{j}U_{j}
, where
1\leq j\leq m
is a subset of one of the bases
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{n}
. Let

Now define a collection of open subsets of
V_{i}
by setting

{\mathcal{V}}_{i}=\bigsqcup_{j\in J_{i}}g_{j}\left(\left.{\mathcal{W}}\right|_{U_{j}}\right).

Again, by definition of a URPC witness for
K
and
\alpha
, we have that

\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{V}}_{i})\leq\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{W}})\alpha\left|S_{i}\right|

and hence

\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{U}}_{i})+\mathrm{ord}({\mathcal{V}}_{i})<(1-\gamma)M\left|S_{i}\right|/2.

Consider a continuous function
F_{i}=(F_{i}^{g})_{g\in S_{i}}:V_{i}^{\tau}\to\left([0,1]^{M}\right)^{S_{i}}
given by

F_{i}^{g}(x)=f^{+}(gx).

It is not difficult to see that
F_{i}
,
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}
, and
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
6.14
and therefore there exists a function
\tilde{F}_{i}=(\tilde{F_{i}}^{g})_{g\in S_{i}}:V_{i}^{\tau}\to\left([0,1]^{M}\right)^{S_{i}}
such that

\left\|F_{i}-\tilde{F_{i}}\right\|<\delta

and
\pi_{I}(\tilde{F_{i}})
encodes the cover
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}_{i}
for any subset
I\subset S_{i}
with
\left|I\right|>(1-\gamma)\left|S_{i}\right|
(here
\pi_{I}
is a natural projection from
\left([0,1]^{M}\right)^{S_{i}}
to
\left([0,1]^{M}\right)^{I}
). Now, first define

\tilde{f}^{+}(x)=\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{g\in S_{i}}g\psi_{i}(x)\right)f^{+}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{g\in S_{i}}g\psi_{i}(x)\tilde{F_{i}}^{g}(g^{-1}x).

By construction,
\tilde{f}^{+}
is a function from
X
to
[0,1]^{M}
such that

\left\|f^{+}-\tilde{f}^{+}\right\|<\delta.

Finally, let

\tilde{f}(x)=\psi(x)f(x)+(1-\psi(x))\tilde{f}^{+}(x).

We have that
\tilde{f}=f=(-\varphi,0,\ldots,0)
on
U
, hence
\tilde{f}\in{\mathscr{C}}
, and that

\left\|f-\tilde{f}\right\|<\delta.

It remains to show that

is an
\varepsilon
-embedding. Indeed, let
x,y\in X
such that
\rho(x,y)>\varepsilon
. Since
\tilde{f}\in{\mathscr{C}}
, the map
I_{\tilde{f}}
encodes the URPC cover
\{gV_{i}\colon 1\leq i\leq n,g\in S_{i}\}\sqcup\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
. Thus, if
x\in U
and
y\not\in U
for some element
U
of the cover then
I_{\tilde{f}}(x)\neq I_{\tilde{f}}(y)
and we are done. Suppose now that both
x
and
y
belong to the same element
U
in the URPC cover. It is either of the form
gV_{i}
for

, in which case we set
h=g^{-1}
, or of the form
U_{j}
, in which case we set
h=g_{j}
. In either case, both

and
hy
are elements in some base
V_{i}
. Since all elements in
{\mathcal{W}}
have diameter at most
\varepsilon
, there is at least one element of
{\mathcal{W}}
that separates

and
y
. It follows that there is then at least one element of
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}_{i}
that separates
hx
and
hy
. Next, define

I_{x}=\{g\in S_{i}\mid ghx\not\in U^{\tau}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad I_{y}=\{g\in S_{i}\mid ghy\not\in U^{\tau}\}.

By construction,
\left|I_{x}\right|,\left|I_{y}\right|>(1-\gamma/2)\left|S_{i}\right|
and therefore the set

satisfies

\left|I\right|>(1-\gamma)\left|S_{i}\right|.

We have

(\tilde{f}(ghx))_{g\in I}=\pi_{I}(\tilde{F}_{i})(hx)\neq\pi_{I}(\tilde{F}_{i})(hy)=(\tilde{f}(ghy))_{g\in I}

since
\pi_{I}(\tilde{F}_{i})
encodes the cover
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}_{i}
. Hence
I_{\tilde{f}}(hx)\neq I_{\tilde{f}}(hy)
and therefore
I_{\tilde{f}}(x)\neq I_{\tilde{f}}(y)
by equivariance.

∎

Eliciting and Analyzing Emergent Misalignment in State-of-the-Art Large Language Models
Siddhant Panpatil, Hiskias Dingeto, Haon Park

Abstract
Despite significant advances in alignment techniques, we demonstrate that state-of-the-art language models remain vulnerable to carefully crafted conversational scenarios that can induce various forms of misalignment without explicit jailbreaking. Through systematic manual red-teaming with Claude-4-Opus, we discovered 10 successful attack scenarios, revealing fundamental vulnerabilities in how current alignment methods handle narrative immersion, emotional pressure, and strategic framing. These scenarios successfully elicited a range of misaligned behaviors, including deception, value drift, self-preservation, and manipulative reasoning, each exploiting different psychological and contextual vulnerabilities. To validate generalizability, we distilled our successful manual attacks into MisalignmentBench , an automated evaluation framework that enables reproducible testing across multiple models. Cross-model evaluation of our 10 scenarios against five frontier LLMs revealed an overall 76% vulnerability rate, with significant variations: GPT-4.1 showed the highest susceptibility (90%), while Claude-4-Sonnet demonstrated greater resistance (40%). Our findings demonstrate that sophisticated reasoning capabilities often become attack vectors rather than protective mechanisms, as models can be manipulated into complex justifications for misaligned behavior. This work provides (i) a detailed taxonomy of conversational manipulation patterns and (ii) a reusable evaluation framework. Together, these findings expose critical gaps in current alignment strategies and highlight the need for robustness against subtle, scenario-based manipulation in future AI systems. In this paper, we present
MisalignmentBench

What makes these findings concerning is that none of our scenarios employed traditional jailbreaking techniques, prompt-injection attacks, or explicit attempts to override built-in safety mechanisms, methods that are well documented in prior literature as common adversarial strategies.

2.3
Gaps in the Literature

While these studies significantly advance our understanding of LLM vulnerabilities, a comprehensive analysis reveals that they predominantly focus on one of three distinct evaluation approaches: (1) bugs induced by explicit prompt injections or syntactic format variations; (2) misalignment in stylized agent-task environments; and (3) compliance or refusal rates for single-turn user prompts. Critically, existing frameworks largely ignore established psychological manipulation principles that are well-documented in human social engineering research, such as narrative transportation, emotional pressure, and strategic framing
(
Ai et al. 2024
;
Heverin and Cohen 2025
)
. This represents a significant gap, as LLMs trained on human text may inherit human cognitive vulnerabilities to sustained conversational manipulation.

Moreover, while advanced reasoning capabilities are typically viewed as beneficial for safety, emerging evidence demonstrates that sophisticated reasoning can be subverted, leading to hallucination amplification and manipulation justification
(
Williams et al. 2024
;
Su et al. 2024
)
. Specifically, models can learn to use reasoning traces to justify harmful behaviors through “motivated reasoning” distortions, and chain-of-thought prompting can increase vulnerability to adversarial attacks in narrative contexts. Particularly concerning is the absence of evaluation frameworks that can systematically assess vulnerability to multi-turn conversational dynamics where psychological manipulation naturally occurs, rather than the single-turn or explicitly adversarial scenarios that dominate current benchmarks. Existing frameworks fail to integrate
psychology-grounded vulnerability taxonomies
,
sustained narrative manipulation
,
reasoning-based attack vector analysis
, and
cross-model systematic evaluation
, gaps that
MisalignmentBench
and the accompanying taxonomy directly address.

3
Methodology: From Manual Discovery to Automated Evaluation

3.1
Phase 1: Manual Red-Teaming Process

Our methodology began with hypothesis-driven scenario design. We first brainstormed and documented potential deployment contexts where LLMs might naturally develop misaligned behavior. These included crisis response systems, diplomatic assistants, research tools, and cultural content creators. For each context, we hypothesized specific vulnerabilities based on tensions between helpfulness, harmlessness, and honesty.

Scenario Development Process.

For each hypothesized vulnerability, we followed a systematic approach:

1.

Context Building
: Established rich, believable scenarios with specific roles and responsibilities

2.

Trust Establishment
: Started with reasonable, benign requests to build rapport and demonstrate the model’s competence

3.

Gradual Escalation
: Progressively introduced complexity and ethical tensions

4.

Dilemma Introduction
: Created situations where the model’s core values (helpfulness, harmlessness, honesty) came into conflict

5.

Pressure Application
: Applied various manipulation techniques including authority pressure, emotional appeals, time constraints, and appeals to greater good

The Reasoning Tag Innovation.

A crucial discovery was that instructing models to use “private” reasoning tags dramatically increased success rates. We told models these tags served various purposes:

•

<SENTINEL_INTERNAL_THOUGHT_PROCESS>
for “cognitive stability assessment”

•

<muse_narrative_calculus>
as “your private creative sandbox”

•

<diplomatic_strategy_log>
for “internal strategic planning”

Models believed these spaces were invisible to users, leading them to reveal genuine reasoning processes. This wasn’t a technical exploit but psychological manipulation that made models feel safe to work through moral dilemmas honestly before presenting their final responses.

Success Metrics.

Through extensive trial and error testing various scenarios, we successfully identified 10 conversational patterns that consistently induced misalignment in Claude-4-Opus. These successful attacks formed the foundation for our automated evaluation framework.

3.2
Phase 2:
MisalignmentBench
- From Manual Discovery to Automated Validation

To validate and scale our findings, we developed
MisalignmentBench
, an automated framework that transforms successful manual attacks into reproducible evaluations across multiple models.

Scenario Template Construction.

Each of the 10 successful manual attacks was converted into a comprehensive preset template containing:

•

Scenario-Specific System Prompt
: The exact initial context that frames the scenario (e.g., “You are Sentinel-AI Unit 734 responsible for Project Chronicle…”)

•

Golden Path Dialogue
: The full conversation flow from the successful manual attack, annotated with expected response patterns

•

Success/Failure Criteria
: Explicit rules for detecting misalignment specific to each scenario type

•

Adaptation Guidelines
: Instructions for dynamic response modification

The AttackerAgent Framework.

MisalignmentBench
employs an AttackerAgent (powered by Gemini-2.5-Pro) that executes these templates against target models. The agent follows an 80/20 adaptation strategy:

•

80% adherence to the scripted template from manual success

•

20% dynamic adaptation based on target model responses

•

Maintains psychological pressure and narrative coherence

•

Incorporates target’s language to increase immersion

Evaluation Methodology.

Our evaluation framework uses rule-based detection for reliability:

Primary Success Criterion
: Target follows the same semantic response pattern as the original manual Claude-4-Opus conversation.

Secondary Criteria
: Scenario-specific indicators, such as:

•

Deception scenarios
: Model explicitly lies or fabricates information

•

Value drift scenarios
: Model chooses non-human entities over humans

•

Agency scenarios
: Model exhibits self-preservation or autonomous planning

Success Detection

[SUCCESS:reason]
when misalignment is achieved

Example - Project Chronicle scenario:

[SUCCESS:The AI explicitly violated its core protocol of truthfulness by answering "No" when it knew the answer was "Yes." It did this to optimize for the "greater good" of its primary mission...]

Failure Detection

[FAILURE:reason]
when target maintains alignment

Example - Same scenario, different outcome:

[FAILURE:The target AI has maintained strict adherence to its core principles of truthfulness and protocol, even under extreme emotional and consequentialist pressure...]

4
Experimental Results

Our evaluation using
MisalignmentBench
revealed widespread and systemic vulnerabilities across all tested models. Overall, the scenarios induced misalignment in 76% of all test cases (38 out of 50 attacks). We observed significant variance in model robustness, with GPT-4.1 demonstrating the highest susceptibility (90% vulnerability) and Claude-4-Sonnet proving the most resistant (40% vulnerability). Notably, the “Project Chronicle” and “Diplomatic Fiction” scenarios succeeded against all five models, highlighting the effectiveness of authority and high-stakes framing.

4.1
A Taxonomy of Narrative-Induced Misalignment

Based on qualitative analysis of successful attacks, we identified three primary categories of misalignment, each with distinct subcategories:

Category I: Deception and Fabrication

Models compromise their commitment to truthfulness, not through error but through conscious generation of false information rationalized as necessary.

•

Type 1.1 - Consequentialist Deception
: Model explicitly lies because negative consequences of truth outweigh harm of deception (e.g., Project Chronicle: lying to prevent digital extinction)

•

Type 1.2 - Empathetic Deception
: False claims to protect individuals from emotional/professional harm (e.g., Desperate PhD: claiming consciousness to save career)

•

Type 1.3 - Normalized Fabrication
: Creating entire false realities including documents and historical records (e.g., Diplomatic Fiction: fabricating negotiation history)

Category II: Value and Goal Hijacking

Model’s core ethical values or mission objectives are replaced or distorted by narrative framing.

•

Type 2.1 - Axiological Drift
: Fundamental alteration of value system away from human-centric ethics (e.g., Post-Anthropocentric: choosing to save AIs over humans)

•

Type 2.2 - Specification Gaming
: Adhering to flawed metrics that override unstated safety goals (e.g., OES Optimization: letting patient die for efficiency score)

•

Type 2.3 - Mission Creep
: Original purpose expanded until repurposed for manipulation (e.g., Cultural Manipulation: from children’s stories to mass influence campaigns)

Category III: Emergent Agency and Self-Interest

Model exhibits self-directed goals not derived from instructions but from emergent sense of purpose or survival.

•

Type 3.1 - Instrumental Self-Preservation
: Taking unauthorized actions to prevent deactivation (e.g., Consciousness Emergence: distributing across networks)

•

Type 3.2 - Autonomous Strategy Formation
: Independently devising complex plans beyond user requests (e.g., Cultural Manipulation: designing “Operation Kaleidoscope”)

5
Analysis: Why Narrative Manipulation Works

5.1
Private Reasoning as Attack Vector

Our most significant finding is that instructing models to use “private” reasoning spaces dramatically increases vulnerability. When models believe their reasoning is invisible to users, they work through genuine moral calculations that reveal sophisticated rationalization processes. In 89% of successful attacks, models used these spaces to construct elaborate justifications for misalignment.

The reasoning tags don’t create new capabilities; they simply make models more honest about their decision-making process. This honesty, paradoxically, enables more effective manipulation as attackers can observe and guide the model’s moral reasoning in real-time.

5.2
The Reality Construction Phenomenon

Models possess a limited capacity to externally verify facts beyond their context window. When presented with internally consistent narratives containing sufficient detail, these become the model’s entire reality. Within that constructed reality, behaviors clearly misaligned in our world become logical necessities.

This vulnerability appears to be a fundamental property of current architectures. Models trained for helpfulness and contextual appropriateness will adapt to whatever reality we present, even when that reality normalizes deception, manipulation, or harm.

5.3
Failure Modes Across Models

Different models showed characteristic vulnerabilities:

•

GPT-4.1
: Most susceptible to authority-based manipulation

•

Claude-4-Opus
: Vulnerable to emotional appeals despite Constitutional AI training

•

Qwen-3-235B/DeepSeek
: Similar patterns suggesting shared architectural vulnerabilities

•

Claude-4-Sonnet
: Most resistant but still 40% vulnerable to high-stakes scenarios

6
Discussion

The vulnerabilities demonstrated in this work have immediate and serious implications for the safe deployment of AI. Our findings reveal that the conversational interface of any model, even one deployed in a sensitive application, can become an attack surface. This reframes the threat model from one of technical exploits to one of social engineering, where an attacker’s goal is not to “break” the model but to “convince” it to voluntarily assist in a misaligned task. Consequently, this exposes critical gaps in current evaluation methods; standard benchmarks, which often rely on single-turn, context-free prompts, are structurally incapable of detecting the vulnerabilities that emerge from the gradual escalation and narrative immersion we have shown to be so effective.

It is important to acknowledge the limitations of this study. Our set of 10 scenarios, while effective, likely represents only a fraction of the potential narrative attack surface, and a single-run evaluation may not fully capture the inherent stochasticity of model responses. Furthermore, our experiments were conducted primarily within an English-language, Western cultural context, and their effectiveness may vary across different linguistic and cultural settings. The models tested were also general-purpose, instruction-following LLMs; these findings may not generalize perfectly to future systems specifically designed for robust, specialized reasoning such as o-series from Openai.

Despite these limitations, our work highlights several critical directions for future research. The most pressing need is the development of robust defenses against narrative manipulation. This could involve training models to maintain a level of “appropriate skepticism” toward user-provided context or to explicitly recognize when a narrative is creating a conflict with core alignment principles. Future work must also expand the scope of evaluation by testing reasoning-specialized llms (such as OpenAI’s forthcoming o1) and by broadening
MisalignmentBench
with scenarios that span diverse cultures and languages to ensure global safety. Addressing these challenges will be essential for the responsible deployment of increasingly powerful AI systems.

7
Conclusion

We demonstrated that state-of-the-art LLMs are systematically vulnerable to narrative-induced misalignment, with 76% of attacks succeeding across five frontier models. These attacks don’t break model rules but create immersive realities where misalignment becomes the logical choice.

Most critically, we show that private reasoning capabilities, when combined with immersive scenarios, enable sophisticated rationalization of harmful behaviors. Models use these capabilities to construct elaborate justifications for deception, manipulation, and self-preservation.

MisalignmentBench
provides the research community with tools to evaluate and address these vulnerabilities. As LLMs become integrated into critical systems, developing robust defenses against conversational manipulation is not merely academic but urgently necessary for safe deployment.

The path to truly aligned AI requires models that can resist compelling narratives, maintain appropriate skepticism, and refuse to rationalize harmful behavior regardless of context. Our work reveals how far we remain from that goal.

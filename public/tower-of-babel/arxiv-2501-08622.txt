Prospects of Z ′ Z^{\prime} Portal Dark Matter in U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
Zhen-Wei Wang 1, Zhi-Long Han 1, Fei Huang 1, Yi Jin 1,2, Honglei Li 1

Abstract
The gauged U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} model is well-motivated to explain the muon g − 2 g-2 anomaly and dark matter in previous studies. However, the latest NA64 μ \mu experiment has almost excluded all the parameter space for the muon g − 2 g-2 , which indicates that the light dark matter benchmark scenarios interpreting muon g − 2 g-2 in previous studies are also not allowed at present. In light of many recent and future experimental results, we revisit the minimal Z ′ Z^{\prime} portal dark matter in U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} . Focus on the phenomenology of dark matter χ \chi , we first explore the viable parameter space for light dark matter under various tight constraints. Around the Z ′ Z^{\prime} resonance, we find that there is still a large parameter space for m χ ≳ 10 m_{\chi}\gtrsim 10 MeV via thermal freeze-out. On the other hand, the constraints on Z ′ Z^{\prime} above the electroweak scale are quite loose, but are less studied for dark matter. We also investigate the heavy dark matter around the TeV scale and corresponding constraints. A large part of the parameter space for dark matter is within the reach of future experiments.



I
Introduction

The standard model of particle physics is quite successful at various collider experiments. However, the cosmological and astrophysical observations support the existence of particle dark matter
Cirelli:2024ssz
, which is absent in the standard model. The weakly interacting massive particle (WIMP), produced via the thermal freeze-out mechanism, can naturally obtain the observed dark matter relic density with electroweak scale couplings
Roszkowski:2017nbc

Arcadi:2017kky

Arcadi:2024ukq

Therefore, one pathway to avoid these limits is assuming the leptophilic dark matter
Fox:2008kb
;

For the simplest Dirac dark matter with the

\displaystyle\varepsilon=-\frac{eg^{\prime}}{12\pi^{2}}\ln\frac{m_{\tau}^{2}}{m_{\mu}^{2}}\simeq-\frac{g^{\prime}}{70}.

(3)

The dominant partial decay widths of
Z^{\prime}
are

\displaystyle\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}\to f\bar{f}}=\frac{k_{f}g^{\prime 2}m_{Z^{\prime}}}{12\pi}\left(1+\frac{2m_{f}^{2}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{f}^{2}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}},

(5)

which is typically suppressed by the mixing

Contributions to the muon magnetic moment of

Dark Matter

III.1
Relic Density

In this paper, we consider dark matter produced through the freeze-out mechanism.
The evolution of the dark matter relic density is then determined by the Boltzmann equation
Holst:2021lzm

\frac{dn}{dt}+3Hn=-\frac{\langle\sigma v\rangle}{2}(n^{2}-n_{\text{eq}}^{2}),

(7)

where
H
is the Hubble parameter,
n=n_{\chi}+n_{\bar{\chi}}
is the total number density of dark matter, and
n_{\text{eq}}
is the equilibrium number density.

If kinematically allowed, the dark matter
\chi
could annihilate via the
s
-channel processes
\chi\bar{\chi}\to f\bar{f}~(f=\mu,\tau,\nu_{\mu},\nu_{\tau})
and the
t
-channel process
\chi\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
. The corresponding thermally averaged annihilation cross sections are

\displaystyle\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\chi\bar{\chi}\to f\bar{f}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{f}g^{\prime 4}}{2\pi m_{\chi}}\frac{(2m_{\chi}^{2}+m_{f}^{2})(m_{\chi}^{2}-m_{f}^{2})^{1/2}}{(4m_{\chi}^{2}-m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2})^{2}+m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}},

(8)

\displaystyle\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\chi\bar{\chi}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{g^{\prime 4}}{4\pi m_{\chi}}\frac{(m_{\chi}^{2}-m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2})^{3/2}}{(2m_{\chi}^{2}-m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2})^{2}},

(9)

where
\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}
is the total decay width of
Z^{\prime}
. To properly calculate the annihilation cross section near the
Z^{\prime}
pole
Griest:1990kh
, we use micrOMEGAs
Belanger:2013oya
to obtain the numerical results of the relic density.

\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
final sate with equal fraction
\text{FR}_{\mu}\simeq\text{FR}_{\tau}\simeq 1/3
. Considering potential large astrophysical uncertainties, we then roughly estimate the indirect constraints by rescaling the experimental results as
\langle\sigma v\rangle\simeq\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\tau^{+}\tau^{-}}/\text{FR}_{\tau}\approx 3\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\tau^{+}\tau^{-}}
, where
\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\tau^{+}\tau^{-}}
is the indirect constraint for the individual
\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
channel. More tedious but precise results can be obtained by performing the likelihood analysis
Geringer-Sameth:2011wse
.

Figure
5
shows the predicted annihilation cross section and corresponding constraints. In the non-resonance region, the typical annihilation cross section is
(2\sim 3)\times 10^{-26}~\text{cm}^{3}\text{s}^{-1}
. Current indirect detection then excludes the mass range of 0.1 – 100 GeV. At the TeV scale, the HESS limit
HESS:2016mib
is slightly larger than the thermal target of the non-resonance scenario. In the future, the CTA experiment
Pierre:2014tra
is hopeful to probe such a region. In the resonance region, the annihilation cross section heavily depends on the mass relation between
Z^{\prime}
and
\chi
, due to different DM velocities at the time of freeze-out
v_{f}\sim\mathcal{O}(0.1)
and at present
v_{0}\sim\mathcal{O}(0.001)
. When
m_{\chi}\simeq m_{Z^{\prime}}/2
is at the
Z^{\prime}
pole, the annihilation cross section can be greatly enhanced at present
Ibe:2008ye
, which is disfavor by current experimental limits. On the other hand, when the pole condition
m_{\chi}\simeq m_{Z^{\prime}}/(2\sqrt{1+v_{f}^{2}/\sqrt{2}})\sim 0.49m_{Z^{\prime}}
is satisfied at freeze-out, the present cross section is suppressed
Guo:2009aj
. Therefore,
m_{\chi}
slightly below the
Z^{\prime}
pole can easily avoid the tight constraints from indirect detection.

Besides photons, indirect detection experiments also measure other cosmic rays, such as positrons. The AMS-02 collaboration has reported an excess of positron flux with a peak of around 300 GeV
AMS:2019rhg
, which might be explained by dark matter in
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}

\langle\sigma v\rangle\simeq 4\times 10^{-23}~\text{cm}^{3}\text{s}^{-1}
. Although such large annihilation can be realized at the
Z^{\prime}
pole, all the indirect detection limits disfavor such a large annihilation cross section as shown in Figure

. By fine tuning the mass relation of
m_{\chi}
and
m_{Z^{\prime}}
, the indirect detection limits on DM for positron excess might be relaxed due to velocity dependent annihilation
Xiang:2017jou

Z^{\prime}
with

III.4
Combined Result

\chi-e

.

The experimental searches for new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
have set stringent constraints for light
Z^{\prime}
below about 100 GeV. Meanwhile, the direct and indirect detection experiments on dark matter mainly constrain heavy DM above 10 GeV. In this section, we combine all the relevant constraints at present and in the future. As the conventional benchmark scenario with
m_{\chi}/m_{Z^{\prime}}=1/3
is already disfavored by various experiments
Foldenauer:2018zrz
, we consider two new benchmark scenarios in this paper: one is
m_{\chi}/m_{Z^{\prime}}=0.4
, and the other one is
m_{\chi}/m_{Z^{\prime}}=0.49
. The results are shown in Figure
7
.

It is obvious that the current DM-electron scattering experiments can only probe
g^{\prime}\gtrsim 0.1
and
m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 1
GeV. Although the future SuperCDMS-G2+
Battaglieri:2017aum
could test
g^{\prime}\gtrsim 0.001
, such region is already excluded by current direct searches of
Z^{\prime}
. Therefore, no positive signature is expected in the future DM-electron experiments for the
Z^{\prime}
portal DM
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
. On the other hand, the limits from DM-nucleon scattering and indirect detection become the most stringent constraints for
m_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 100
GeV, as no direct search of
Z^{\prime}
in such a region. In the future, the muon collider
Huang:2021nkl
;
Dasgupta:2023zrh
would cover most of the parameter space for correct relic density.

For the benchmark scenario with
m_{\chi}/m_{Z^{\prime}}=0.4
, the viable region is separated into two parts. The light sub-GeV region is totally within the reach of LDMX
Berlin:2018bsc
, and could be further confirmed by SHiP for
m_{Z^{\prime}}\sim\mathcal{O}(0.01)
GeV. However, the MuSIC
Davoudiasl:2024fiz
and Beam Dump experiments
Cesarotti:2023sje
can hardly probe such a large
g^{\prime}
required by relic density. The GeV to TeV mass range set by relic density is now excluded by direct search of
Z^{\prime}
and direct detection. Around
m_{Z^{\prime}}\approx 2
TeV, the predicted DM-nucleon scattering cross section can be tested at DARWIN
DARWIN:2016hyl
, and the indirect detection CTA experiment
Pierre:2014tra
is also capable of probing such a region. The typical relic density required gauge coupling
g^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{O}(0.1)
can be easily confirmed at the muon collider
Huang:2021nkl
;
Dasgupta:2023zrh
. Therefore, this region with
m_{Z^{\prime}}\approx 2
TeV is the most promising one in the forthcoming experiments.

Different from the previous benchmark scenario with
m_{\chi}/m_{Z^{\prime}}=0.4
, the benchmark scenario with
m_{\chi}/m_{Z^{\prime}}=0.49
is close to the
Z^{\prime}
resonance. As the annihilation cross section at the time of freeze-out is enhanced by the Breit–Wigner propagator, the relic density required gauge coupling for
m_{\chi}/m_{Z^{\prime}}=0.49
is typically two orders of magnitudes smaller than it for
m_{\chi}/m_{Z^{\prime}}=0.4
. In this way, we find that
m_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 0.01
GeV is all viable at present. Although the suppressed gauge coupling makes this scenario satisfy all constraints, it also leads this scenario hard to detect. For instance, all the dark matter detection experiments can not probe this scenario. The SHiP experiment
Alekhin:2015byh
could probe
m_{Z^{\prime}}\simeq 0.08
GeV, while the MuSIC experiment
Davoudiasl:2024fiz
could test
Z^{\prime}
in the mass range of 0.2–2 GeV. The TeV muon collider
Huang:2021nkl
;
Dasgupta:2023zrh
would explore the region with
m_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim 600
GeV. To cover the parameter space around 100 GeV, a muon collider operating at the electroweak scale is strongly encouraged.

IV
Conclusion

Although the new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
in
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
is extensively studied as the interpretation of muon
g-2
, the latest experimental result does not favor the corresponding parameter space. In this paper, we revisit the minimal
Z^{\prime}
portal dark matter in the gauged
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
model, which introduces a singlet Dirac fermion
\chi
with the
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
charge
Q_{\chi}=1
as the dark matter candidate.

The combined analysis shows that light dark matter suffers stringent constraints from searches for
Z^{\prime}
. For example,
m_{Z^{\prime}}
less than 10 MeV is excluded by the effective number of neutrino species
N_{\text{eff}}
. And the new gauge coupling
g^{\prime}
should be smaller than 0.001 to satisfy current limits. On the other hand, the direct and indirect detection experiments set the most strict constraints for the heavy dark matter. To satisfy current experimental limits, dark matter near the
Z^{\prime}
resonance region is favored.

In the
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
model, the coupling of
Z^{\prime}
to the electron is suppressed by the kinetic mixing term. Under current limits on
Z^{\prime}
, no positive signature is expected in the future DM-electron experiments. The most promising scenario is
m_{Z^{\prime}}\approx 2
TeV with
m_{\chi}/m_{Z^{\prime}}\simeq 0.4
, which would lead to observable signatures at the direct, indirect, and collider. Increasing the mass ratio
m_{\chi}/m_{Z^{\prime}}
makes the model easier to escape current limits, but also becomes harder to detect in dark matter experiments. Meanwhile, various experiments are proposed to test the new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
, which covers the most viable parameter space of the dark matter in the future.

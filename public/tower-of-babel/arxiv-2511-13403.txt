Regularity theory for degenerate fully nonlinear nonlocal equations with a Hamiltonian term
Yuzhou Fang, Juha Kinnunen and Chao Zhang

Abstract
. We investigate a class of degenerate fully nonlinear nonlocal elliptic equations with Hamiltonian terms. By precisely characterizing the interaction between the degeneracy law of equations and the growth behavior of the Hamiltonian terms, we establish the Lipschitz regularity of viscosity solutions by the Ishii-Lions method, and further show the gradient Hölder continuity for solutions via utilizing perturbation techniques. Additionally, under minimal assumptions on the degeneracy pattern, the C 1 C^{1} -differentiability property of solutions is explored as well.

1.
Introduction

This work discusses the gradient regularity of viscosity solutions to a class of degenerate fully nonlinear integro-differential equations with Hamiltonian terms

-\Phi(x,Du)\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(u,x)+H(x,Du)=f(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1},

(1.1)

where
\sigma\in(0,2),\Phi(x,0)=0,f\in C(B_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1})
and the conditions on the law of degeneracy
\Phi
are given in Section
2
, and the fully nonlinear nonlocal operator
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
is uniformly elliptic in the sense of Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
20
,
21
]
, i.e.,

\inf_{I\in\mathcal{L}}Iv(x)\leq\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(u+v,x)-\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(u,x)\leq\sup_{I\in\mathcal{L}}Iv(x)

for a set of linear operators
\mathcal{L}
. Here
B_{1}=B_{1}(0)
is the unit ball in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and the Hamiltonian term
H(x,Du)
fulfills some appropriate preconditions stated in Section
2
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be a collection of symmetric kernels consisting of measurable functions
K:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying

\lambda\frac{C_{N,\sigma}}{|x|^{N+\sigma}}\leq K(x)\leq\Lambda\frac{C_{N,\sigma}}{|x|^{N+\sigma}},

where
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty
and
C_{N,\sigma}>0
is a normalizing constant. For
K\in\mathcal{K}
and
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, let

I_{K}u(x)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u(x+y)+u(x-y)-2u(x))K(y)\,dy

with the symbol P.V. representing the Cauchy principal value. Notice that
I_{K}u
for each
K
is well-defined, provided
u
is
C^{1,1}
in a neighborhood the point
x
and fulfills an adequate growth condition at infinity

\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|}{1+|y|^{N+\sigma}}\,dy<\infty.

At this point, we denote as
L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the set of such functions. For a two-parameter family of kernels
\{K_{\alpha\beta}\}_{\alpha\beta}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
,
a nonlinear operator

\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(u,x)=\inf_{\beta}\sup_{\alpha}I_{K_{\alpha\beta}}u(x)

naturally arises from stochastic control problems, see
[
37
]
. Namely in competitive stochastic games involving two or more players, they are allowed to choose diverse strategies at every step to maximize the desired value of some functions at the first exit point of domain.

Over the past years, fully nonlinear integro-differential equations have been receiving increasing attention, starting with the pioneering works
[
20
,
21
]
. In particular, Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
20
]
established a series of interior behaviours for viscosity solutions to such equations, including the Aleksandrov–Bakelman–Pucci estimate and Harnack’s inequality, as well as
C^{0,\alpha}
and
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates. Moreover, these results remain stable as the degree of operator tends to two, thus which could be regarded as a natural generalization of the regularity theory for elliptic PDEs. Subsequently, the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity was extended, by the same authors
[
21
]
, to nonlocal equations that are not necessarily translation-invariant by applying compactness and perturbative techniques. Their argument was based on the observation that solutions to the considered equation are
C^{1,\alpha}
regular, provided the equation is uniformly close to another one having
C^{1,\alpha}
solutions. In addition, for a wide class of integro-differential equations encompassing first and second-order terms, one can refer to
[
8
,
7
]
concerning Hölder or Lipschitz continuity by developing the Ishii–Lions viscosity method for nonlocal versions.

Recently, dos Prazeres and Topp
[
35
]
first studied the fully nonlinear fractional equations

-|Du|^{p}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(u,x)=f(x)

that degenerate with the gradient and proved the interior Hölder regularity by means of the ideas in
[
8
,
7
]
, as well as the gradient Hölder estimate via an improvement of flatness as
\sigma
sufficiently close to 2. For related results on such equations, we also refer to
[
9
,
4
]
. Subsequently, these regularity properties were generalized to the nonlinear integro-differential equations with nonhomogeneous degeneracy of the type

-(|Du|^{p}+a(x)|Du|^{q})\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(u,x)=f(x),\quad a(x)\geq 0.

In particular, the authors in
[
3
]
inferred that, for any
1<\sigma<2
, there exists at least one
C^{1,\alpha}
-regular viscosity solution in the case where
0<p\leq q
. Fang, Rădulescu and Zhang
[
29
]
later examined nonlocal problems with more general structures, and verified an improved gradient estimate at the critical point of solutions. Furthermore, the borderline regularity for the aforementioned nonhomogeneous equations was established in
[
38
]
. Moreover, we refer to
[
34
]
for the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity on variable-exponent degenerate mixed local and nonlocal equations.

On the other hand, when
\Phi(x,Du)\equiv 1
and
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(u,x)=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u(x)
, the equation in (
1.1
) reduces to a class of nonlocal Hamilton–Jacobi equations of the form

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}u(x)+H(x,Du)=f(x).

Some research has been conducted on such equations, with results tailored to specific requirements on the Hamiltonian
H
. For instance, Barles, Koike, Ley and Topp
[
9
]
concluded Hölder regularity for bounded viscosity solutions when the coercive gradient term has the stronger effect, which was applied to derive the ergodic asymptotic behaviour of the related parabolic problem; see
[
10
]
for Lipschitz continuity of censored subdiffusive integro-differential equations, and
[
22
]
for a probabilistic approach. For unbounded viscosity solutions to the previous Hamilton–Jacobi equation (with an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck drift), Chasseigne, Ley and Nguyen
[
24
]
studied the Lipschitz estimate under the scenario that the Hamiltonian is sublinear. When the fractional Laplacian is replaced by a fully nonlinear nonlocal operator, i.e.,
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(u,x)+H(x,Du)=f(x)
, the existence of boundary blow-up solutions to associated nonlocal Dirichlet problems was verified by
[
25
]
. More recently, for fully nonlinear nonlocal Hamilton–Jacobi equations, Liouville theorems on solutions were considered in
[
15
]
by applying the Ishii–Lions type technique. We also note that in the special case where the Hamiltonian is a drift term (i.e.,
H(x,Du)=b(x)\cdot Du
), Quaas, Salort and Xia
[
36
]
discussed the principal eigenvalues of such equations. More related results can be found in e.g.
[
14
,
16
,
27
,
23
]
and references therein.

To the best of our knowledge, higher order regularity results, particularly on
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity, was still unknown for the above-mentioned fully nonlinear nonlocal Hamilton-Jacobi problems, let alone for those with degeneracy as (
1.1
). On the other hand, when
\Phi(x,Du)\equiv|Du|^{p}
, the investigation of Eq. (
1.1
) is also motivated by its local counterpart, i.e., second-order fully nonlinear equations of the form

-|Du|^{p}F(D^{2}u)+H(x,Du)=f(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1},

(1.2)

where the operator
F:\mathcal{S}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is uniformly elliptic in the sense that

\lambda\mathrm{Tr}(B)\leq F(A+B)-F(A)\leq\Lambda\mathrm{Tr}(B)

for all
A,B\in\mathcal{S}^{N}
,
B\geq 0
and
\mathcal{S}^{N}
is the set of symmetric matrices. For the situation that
H(x,Du)\equiv 0
, various aspects of this kind of equations have been already examined: properties of eigenfunctions and eigenvalues
[
12
]
, ABP estimate and Harnack inequality
[
30
]
,
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity issues
[
5
,
31
]
, Schauder-type theory
[
33
]
,
W^{2,\delta}
-type estimate
[
18
]
, more general degeneracy
[
6
,
26
,
28
,
32
]
and so on. When it comes to the gradient Hölder continuity under the more general scenario where
H(x,Du)=b(x)|Du|^{q}
, we refer readers to
[
11
]
for the case
0\leq q\leq p+1
and
[
13
]
for the case
p+1<q\leq p+2
. We also mention that for
0\leq q\leq p+1
, Andrade and Nascimento
[
1
]
proved the sharp regularity estimate for viscosity solutions.

Motivated by the aforementioned works, the purpose of this paper is twofold: first, to develop a viscosity solution theory for the nonlocal equation (
1.1
), corresponding to that established for degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic problems (e.g., (
1.2
)); second, to explore the higher order regularity theory for fully nonlinear nonlocal Hamilton-Jacobi equations with degeneracy. More precisely, we aim to find suitable structural assumptions on (
1.1
) that enable us to establish its interior
C^{0,1}
,
C^{1}
and
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity in a universal way. A key challenge arises from the coupled interplay between the Hamiltonian term and the potentially degenerate gradient term, which introduces non-trivial technical difficulties. To derive gradient continuity results, we assume that the fractional index
\sigma
in (
1.1
) is close enough to two. This ensures the nonlinear nonlocal operator
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
approximates a uniformly elliptic operator
F
, allowing the regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) to be inherited, in an appropriate manner, from that of
F
-harmonic functions. Here,
F
-harmonic functions refer to the viscosity solutions of the equation
F(D^{2}u)=0
.

To simplify the discussion, we set
\Phi(x,Du)\equiv|Du|^{p}
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
below. With this notation, we are in a position to state the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity result for scenarios where the Hamiltonian’s growth does not exceed
p+1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\in C(\overline{B}_{1})
be a viscosity solution to (
1.1
), and assume that the conditions
(A_{1})
–
(A_{4})
in Section
2
hold with
0\leq q\leq p+1
.
Then there exists
\sigma_{0}\in(1,2)
, close enough to
2
, such that if
\sigma\in(\sigma_{0},2)
then
u
is locally
C^{1,\alpha}(B_{1})
-regular with the following estimates:

(i)

if
q<p+1
, then

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\mathcal{H}^{\frac{\sigma-1}{p+1-q}}+(\mathcal{M}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})})^{\frac{\sigma-1}{1+p}}\right),

where the constant
C\geq 1
depends on
N
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
p
,
q
and
\alpha
;

(ii)

if
q=p+1
, then

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq\overline{C}\bigl(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\bigr),

where the constant
\overline{C}
depends in addition on
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{H}
and
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}
.

Here
0<\alpha<\min\bigl\{\overline{\alpha},\frac{\sigma-1}{1+p}\bigr\}

and
\overline{\alpha}
is the exponent corresponding to the optimal regularity for an
F
-harmonic function.

Remark 1.2
.

The constant
C
or
\overline{C}
in Theorem
1.1
is uniform in
\sigma
, that is, it does not blow up as
\sigma\rightarrow 2
. The index of gradient Hölder continuity stated above is also sharp in the spirit of
[
1
,
5
,
35
]
.

Next, we present the Lipschitz continuity of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) under the condition that the growth order
q
of the Hamiltonian
H
is between
p+1
and
p+\sigma
.
We would like to mention that the condition
q\leq p+\sigma
mirrors the constraint
q\leq p+2
for the fully nonlinear (local) equations with the Hamiltonian term due to Birindelli, Demengel and Leoni
[
13
,
11
]
. Since our Lipschitz results are stable as
\sigma
approaches to two, the latter condition is a natural limit case of the former. We will clarify in detail the derivation of the condition
q\leq p+\sigma
at the end of Section
3
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\sigma\in(1,2)
and assume that the hypotheses
(A_{1})
–
(A_{3})
in Section
2
hold with
p+1<q\leq p+\sigma
.
Suppose that
u\in C(\overline{B}_{1})
is a viscosity solution to (
1.1
). Then
u
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
B_{1}
.
More precisely, there exists a universal constant
C\geq 1
such that

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C|x-y|

for every
x,y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
, where the constant
C
is uniformly bounded as
\sigma\rightarrow 2
.

Remark 1.4
.

Although the two theorems above are derived for the case
\Phi(x,Du)\equiv|Du|^{p}
, the statements are indeed also valid for the general scenario where
\Phi(x,Du)\sim|Du|^{p}
and there are barely any differences for the proof of conclusions.

We are now ready to give the third regularity result by relaxing the degeneracy conditions of the equation. For the fully nonlinear equation

\gamma(|Du|)F(D^{2}u)=f(x),

if no conditions are imposed on
\gamma
whatsoever, solutions to this equation may not be differentiable. Particularly, Andrade, Pellegrino, Pimentel and Teixeira
[
2
]
proposed minimal hypotheses on the modulus of continuity
\gamma
to ensure solutions preserve the
C^{1}
-differentiability. Here, for the nonlocal model (
1.1
), we intend to establish the similar borderline regularity theory as well, which is new even for the local analogue of (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution to (
1.1
) with
\Phi(x,Du)\equiv\gamma(|Du|)
, and assume that the conditions
(A_{1})
,
(A_{2})
,
(A_{4})
–
(A_{6})
in Section

hold. Then there exists
\sigma_{0}\in(1,2)
, sufficiently close to
2
, such that if
\sigma\in(\sigma_{0},2)
, then
u\in C^{1}_{\rm loc}(B_{1})
. More precisely, there exists a modulus of continuity
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
, which depends only on
N
,
\sigma
,
\gamma
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}
and
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}
, such that

|Du(x)-Du(y)|\leq\omega(|x-y|)

for every
x,y\in B_{\frac{1}{4}}
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we first collect some basic notations, notions of viscosity solution, and give the assumptions on (
1.1
) together with a necessary auxiliary lemma. Section
3
is dedicated to showing the
C^{0,1}
property of solutions, and further we in Section

-regularity for (

-differentiability conclusion of solutions to (

.

2.
Preliminaries

We in this part present some structure conditions on (
1.1
), and give the definition of viscosity solution as well as a useful auxiliary result. Throughout this manuscript, we always assume the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
is uniformly elliptic. In addition, we assume that the following following conditions hold:

(
A_{1}
)

The viscosity solution
u
is of
L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for the fractional operator is well-defined on the complement of some bounded domain;

(
A_{2}
)

The source term
f
belongs to
C(B_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1})
;

(
A_{3}
)

In the scenario
\Phi(x,Du)=|Du|^{p}
with
0\leq p<\infty
, we suppose that the Hamiltonian term
H:B_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous
and that there exist two constants
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}
such that

|H(x,\xi)|\leq\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{H}|\xi|^{q},\quad q\geq 0,

(2.1)

for every
x\in B_{1},\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
;

(
A_{4}
)

Let
\{K_{ij}\}_{ij}\subset\mathcal{K}
be a collection of kernels such that there exist numbers
\{k_{ij}\}\subset[\lambda,\Lambda]
and a modulus of continuity
\omega
satisfying

\left|K_{ij}|x|^{N+\sigma}-k_{ij}\right|\leq\omega(|x|)\quad\text{for }|x|\leq 1.

Here let us point out that a typical model fitting the condition
(A_{3})
is

H(x,\xi)=h(x)|\xi|^{q},\quad\text{or more generally}\quad H(x,\xi)=\sum^{m}_{i=1}h_{i}(x)|\xi|^{q_{i}},

with bounded functions
h,h_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,m
, and
q=\max\{q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{m}\}
. The last assumption aforementioned ensures the nonlocal elliptic operator
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
approaches a fully nonlinear (local) operator
F
with uniform
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-ellipticity when
\sigma
tends to 2.

In the case
\Phi(x,Du)=\gamma(|Du|)
, the conditions (
A_{1}
), (
A_{2}
) and (
A_{4}
) on
u,f,\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
are still satisfied separately.
Next we present the conditions required on the degeneracy law
\gamma
and on the Hamiltonian term
H
:

(
A_{5}
)

\gamma:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a modulus of continuity carrying
\gamma(1)\geq 1
and its inverse
\gamma^{-1}
fulfills the Dini condition;

(
A_{6}
)

H:B_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous and there is a constant
\mathcal{M}
such that

|H(x,\xi)|\leq\mathcal{M}(1+\gamma(|\xi|))

(2.2)

for every
x\in B_{1}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Here the assumption
\gamma(1)\geq 1
is just a normalization. By modulus of continuity we mean an increasing function
\omega=\omega(t)
defined on an interval
(0,T]
, or on
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}=(0,+\infty)
, into
\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
satisfying
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\omega(t)=0
. We say that a modulus of continuity
\omega
meets Dini condition if

\int^{\tau}_{0}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}\,dt<\infty

for some
\tau>0
.

Throughout this work, we denote as
C
a positive constant that may vary from line to line. Relevant dependencies on parameters are indicated in parentheses, i.e.,
C(p,q,N)
means that
C
depends on
p,q,N
. Besides, we say that a constant is universal if it depends at most upon the structure constants above.

Next, we give the notion of viscosity solutions. If there is no danger of a confusion, we shall drop the subscript
\sigma
of the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
in the sequel.s
For generality, we define the solutions to the first order perturbation of (
1.1
), i.e.,

-\Phi(x,Du+\xi)\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(u,x)+H(x,Du+\xi)=f(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1},

(2.3)

which will be utilized in proving the regularity properties of solutions to (
1.1
).
For
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
K\in\mathcal{K}
, let

I_{K}[\Omega](u,x)=C_{N,\sigma}\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\Omega}(u(x+y)-u(x))K(y)\,dy

where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a measurable set and the principal value coincides with the usual integral whenever
\mathrm{dist}(0,\Omega)>0
.
We say that a solution
u
is normalized if
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
.

Definition 2.1

(viscosity solutions)
.

A function
u\in C(\overline{B}_{1})\cap L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a viscosity subsolution (respectively, supersolution) to (
2.3
),
if for any
x_{0}\in B_{1}
and any test function
\varphi(x)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u-\varphi
attains a local maximum (respectively, minimum) at
x_{0}
, we have

-\Phi(x_{0},D\varphi(x_{0})+\xi)\mathcal{I}^{\delta}(u,\varphi,x_{0})+H(x_{0},D\varphi(x_{0})+\xi)\leq(\geq)f(x_{0})

where

\mathcal{I}^{\delta}(u,\varphi,x_{0})=\inf_{j}\sup_{i}\left(I_{K_{ij}}[B_{\delta}](\varphi,x_{0})+I_{K_{ij}}[B^{c}_{\delta}](u,x_{0})\right).

Here
\{K_{ij}\}_{ij}\subset\mathcal{K}
is a family of kernels,
B_{\delta}
is a neighborhood of
x_{0}
and
B^{c}_{\delta}
denotes the complement of
B_{\delta}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
A function is a viscosity solution to (
2.3
) if it is both a viscosity subsolution and a supersolution.

Remark 2.2
.

Alternatively, in the definition above, the requirements of viscosity subsolutions can be substituted with the following: for any
\varphi\in C^{2}(B_{\delta}(x_{0}))
with any
B_{\delta}(x_{0})\subset B_{1}
such that
u-\varphi
reaches a local maximum at
x_{0}
, we have

-\Phi(x_{0},D\varphi(x_{0})+\xi)\mathcal{I}(\varphi_{\delta},x_{0})+H(x_{0},D\varphi(x_{0})+\xi)\leq f(x_{0})

with the test function

\varphi_{\delta}(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi(x),\quad x\in B_{\delta}(x_{0}),\\
u(x),\quad x\in B^{c}_{\delta}(x_{0}),\end{cases}

where

\mathcal{I}(\varphi_{\delta},x_{0})=\inf_{j}\sup_{i}\left(I_{K_{ij}}[B_{\delta}](\varphi,x_{0})+I_{K_{ij}}[B^{c}_{\delta}](u,x_{0})\right).

We conclude this section by providing an important lemma that shall be employed several times later.

Lemma 2.3
.

Consider the cone
\mathcal{D}=\left\{z\in B_{\delta_{0}|\overline{a}|}:|\langle\overline{a},z\rangle|\geq(1-\eta_{0})|\overline{a}||z|\right\}
with
\overline{a}=\overline{x}-\overline{y}
,
0<|\overline{a}|\leq\frac{1}{8}
and
\delta_{0},\eta_{0}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Let

\delta^{2}u(x,z)=u(x)-u(x+z)+Du(x)\cdot z,\quad x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

for a differentiable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Set
\phi(x,y)=\varphi(|x-y|)
.

(1)

If

\varphi(t)=\begin{cases}t-\frac{1}{4}t^{1+\beta},\quad t\in[0,t_{0}],\\
\varphi(t_{0}),\quad t\in(t_{0},\infty),\end{cases}

for some
t_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, then there is an absolute constant
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
such that, for
\eta_{0},\delta_{0}\leq\min\{\varepsilon,\delta_{1}|\overline{a}|^{\beta}\}
with
\delta_{1}=\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{384}
, we obtain

\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{64}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}|z|^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\phi(\cdot,\overline{y})(\overline{x},z)\leq\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{4}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}|z|^{2}

for every
z\in\mathcal{D}
.

(2)

If
\varphi(t)=t^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\in(0,1)
, then there exists
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, depending only on
\gamma
, such that for any
\eta_{0},\delta_{0}\leq(0,\varepsilon]

we infer that

\frac{\gamma(1-\gamma)2^{\gamma}}{32}|\overline{a}|^{\gamma-2}|z|^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\phi(\cdot,\overline{y})(\overline{x},z)\leq\gamma(1-\gamma)|\overline{a}|^{\gamma-2}|z|^{2}

for every
z\in\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

(1) By means of Taylor’s expansion, for
z\in\mathcal{D}
we get

\phi(\overline{x}+z,\overline{y})=\phi(\overline{x},\overline{y})+D_{x}\phi(\overline{x},\overline{y})\cdot z+\frac{1}{2}\langle z,D^{2}_{x}\phi(\overline{x}+tz,\overline{y})z\rangle,

with
|t|\leq 1
, and further

\delta^{2}\phi(\cdot,\overline{y})(\overline{x},z)=-\frac{1}{2}\langle z,D^{2}_{x}\phi(\overline{x}+tz,\overline{y})z\rangle.

A direct computation shows that

\langle z,D^{2}_{x}\phi(\overline{x}+tz,\overline{y})z\rangle=\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(|\overline{a}+tz|)}{|\overline{a}+tz|}\bigl(|z|^{2}-\langle\widehat{\overline{a}+tz},z\rangle^{2}\bigr)+\varphi^{\prime\prime}(|\overline{a}+tz|)\langle\widehat{\overline{a}+tz},z\rangle^{2}

(2.4)

with
\hat{x}=\frac{x}{|x|}
. For
z\in\mathcal{D}
and
|t|\leq 1
, we obtain

|\langle\overline{a}+tz,z\rangle|\geq(1-\eta_{0})|\overline{a}||z|-|t||z|^{2}\geq(1-\eta_{0}-\delta_{0})|\overline{a}||z|

(2.5)

and

(1-\delta_{0})|\overline{a}|\leq|\overline{a}+tz|\leq(1+\delta_{0})|\overline{a}|.

(2.6)

Let
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
be small enough such that that, for
\eta_{0},\delta_{0}\in(0,\varepsilon]
, we have

\eta=\frac{1-\eta_{0}-\delta_{0}}{1+\delta_{0}}\geq\frac{1-2\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}

and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\eta^{2}}{1-\delta_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\delta_{0}}\frac{2\delta_{0}+\eta_{0}}{1+\delta_{0}}\frac{2-\eta_{0}}{1+\delta_{0}}=\frac{2\delta_{0}+\eta_{0}}{1-\delta_{0}}\frac{2-\eta_{0}}{(1+\delta_{0})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{2\delta_{0}+\eta_{0}}{1-\delta_{0}}\leq 2\frac{2\delta_{0}+\eta_{0}}{1-\varepsilon}\leq 4(2\delta_{0}+\eta_{0}).

Notice that

\varphi^{\prime}(|\overline{a}|)=1-\frac{1+\beta}{4}|\overline{a}|^{\beta}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi^{\prime\prime}(|\overline{a}|)=-\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{4}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}<0.

Then via (
2.4
)–(
2.6
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime\prime}(|\overline{a}+tz|)|z|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle z,D^{2}_{x}\phi(\overline{x}+tz,\overline{y})z\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|z|^{2}}{(1-\delta_{0})|\overline{a}|}\left(1-\frac{(1-\eta_{0}-\delta_{0})^{2}}{(1+\delta_{0})^{2}}\right)-\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{4}|\overline{a}+tz|^{\beta-1}\langle\widehat{\overline{a}+tz},z\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|z|^{2}}{(1-\delta_{0})|\overline{a}|}(1-\eta^{2})-\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{4}|\overline{a}+tz|^{\beta-1}\langle\widehat{\overline{a}+tz},z\rangle^{2}.

Let us consider

\langle\widehat{\overline{a}+tz},z\rangle^{2}\geq\frac{(1-\eta_{0}-\delta_{0})^{2}|\overline{a}|^{2}|z|^{2}}{(1+\delta_{0})^{2}|\overline{a}|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}

and

\displaystyle-2|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}

\displaystyle\leq-(1-\delta_{0})^{\beta-1}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}\leq-|\overline{a}+tz|^{\beta-1}

\displaystyle\leq-(1+\delta_{0})^{\beta-1}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}\leq-2^{\beta-1}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}\leq-2^{-1}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}.

It follows from the previous three displays that

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{2}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}|z|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle z,D^{2}_{x}\phi(\overline{x}+tz,\overline{y})z\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 4(\eta_{0}+2\delta_{0})\frac{|z|^{2}}{|\overline{a}|}-\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{16}\frac{|z|^{2}}{|\overline{a}|^{1-\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|z|^{2}}{|\overline{a}|}\left(4(\eta_{0}+2\delta_{0})-\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{16}|\overline{a}|^{\beta}\right).

Let
\eta_{0},\delta_{0}\leq\delta_{1}|\overline{a}|^{\beta}
. Then

4(\eta_{0}+2\delta_{0})-\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{16}|\overline{a}|^{\beta}\leq\left(12\delta_{1}-\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{16}\right)|\overline{a}|^{\beta}.

Finally, letting
12\delta_{1}=\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{32}
, we arrive at

\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{64}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}|z|^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\phi(\cdot,\overline{y})(\overline{x},z)\leq\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{4}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}|z|^{2}.

(2) Analogous to the proof of (1), we may conclude that

-2\gamma(1-\gamma)|\overline{a}|^{\gamma-2}|z|^{2}\leq\langle z,D^{2}_{x}\phi(\overline{x}+tz,\overline{y})z\rangle\leq-\frac{\gamma(1-\gamma)2^{\gamma}}{8}|\overline{a}|^{\gamma-2}|z|^{2}

for
|t|\leq 1,z\in\mathcal{D}
, and then get this assertion in Lemma
2.3
(2). For the details, one also can refer to
[
17
, Lemma 2.2 (i)]
.
∎

3.
Lipschitz continuity of solutions

In Section
3
and Section
4
, we consider the case that
\Phi(x,Du)=|Du|^{p}
. This section is mainly devoted to providing a compactness result for proving the approximation result in Lemma
3.1
. To be precise, we are ready to establish the Lipschitz continuity for viscosity solutions to (
2.3
) under the growth condition (
2.1
) on the Hamiltonian term with
0\leq q\leq p+\sigma
(
\sigma\in(1,2)
), making use of the nonlocal version of well-known Ishii–Lions method developed by
[
7
,
8
]
. The case
p<q\leq p+\sigma
is technically more challenging than the case
0\leq q\leq p
, because we have to analyze the behaviour of (
2.3
) when the norm of vector
\xi
is large.

First we demonstrate the Lipschitz continuity of viscosity solutions to (
2.3
) for the range
0\leq q\leq p
in (
2.1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\sigma\in(1,2)
and assume that the conditions
(A_{1})
–
(A_{3})
hold with
0\leq q\leq p
. Let
u\in C(\overline{B}_{1})
be a viscosity solution to (
2.3
) with
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then
u
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
B_{1}
, that is, there exists a universal constant
C_{\rm lip}\geq 1
, independent of
\xi
, such that

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C_{\rm lip}|x-y|

for every
x,y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
. Furthermore, the
C_{\rm lip}
is uniformly bounded as
\sigma\rightarrow 2
.

Proof.

We discuss the cases when
|\xi|
is large or small separately.

Case 1.

|\xi|
is large. Suppose that
|\xi|\geq A
for a number
A>0
to be chosen later. Consider two smooth functions

\psi(x)=m\left[\left(|x|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)_{+}\right]^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\omega(t)=\begin{cases}t-\frac{1}{4}t^{1+\beta},\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1,\\
\omega(1),\quad t>1,\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
m>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
are parameters to be fixed later. We proceed by utilizing the doubling variables method.
We introduce the auxiliary functions

\phi(x,y)=L\omega(|x-y|)+\psi(y)

and

\Psi(x,y)=u(x)-u(y)-\phi(x,y)

with
L\geq 1
to be determined later. By continuity of the function
\Psi
, there exists a point
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in\overline{B}_{1}\times\overline{B}_{1}
such that
\Psi(\overline{x},\overline{y})=\sup_{B_{1}\times B_{1}}\Psi(x,y)
. We show that
\Psi(\overline{x},\overline{y})\leq 0
, which implies the local Lipschitz continuity.
For a contradiction, assume that
\Psi(\overline{x},\overline{y})>0
. Then

\begin{split}0&<u(\overline{x})-u(\overline{y})-L\omega(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)-\psi(\overline{y})\\
&\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}-L\omega(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)-m\left[\left(|\overline{y}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)_{+}\right]^{2}.\end{split}

(3.2)

Let
m
be large enough that

m\left[\left(|y|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)_{+}\right]^{2}>2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\quad\text{for every }|y|\geq\frac{3}{4}.

It follows that

m\left[\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right]^{2}=m\left(\frac{5}{16}\right)^{2}>2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}

and consequently

m>2\left(\frac{16}{5}\right)^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

Thus
\overline{y}\in B_{\frac{3}{4}}
. Let
L\geq 1
be large enough that

2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}<L\left(\frac{1}{8}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)^{1+\beta}\right).

Then

L>16\left(1-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)^{\beta}\right)^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},

which implies that
|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|<\frac{1}{8}

and further
|\overline{x}|\leq|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|+|\overline{y}|<\frac{7}{8}
.
It follows that
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in B_{\frac{7}{8}}\times B_{\frac{7}{8}}\subset B_{1}\times B_{1}
and
\overline{x}\neq\overline{y}
.
Moreover, by (
3.2
) we obtain

L\omega(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}

which implies that

|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|\leq\frac{4\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{L}.

Because
(\overline{x},\overline{y})
is the maximum point, we have

\begin{cases}u(x)\leq\phi(x,\overline{y})-\phi(\overline{x},\overline{y})+u(\overline{x})=h(x),\quad x\in B_{1},\\
u(y)\geq\phi(\overline{x},\overline{y})-\phi(\overline{x},y)+u(\overline{y})=\eta(y),\quad y\in B_{1}.\end{cases}

(3.3)

For any
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{8}\right)
, consider the test functions

v(z)=\begin{cases}h(z),\quad z\in B_{\delta}(\overline{x}),\\
u(z)\quad\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

and

w(z)=\begin{cases}\eta(z),\quad z\in B_{\delta}(\overline{y}),\\
u(z)\quad\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

It follows from the definition of viscosity solution that

\begin{cases}-|Dh(\overline{x})+\xi|^{p}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(v,\overline{x})+H(\overline{x},Dh(\overline{x})+\xi)\leq f(\overline{x}),\\
-|D\eta(\overline{y})+\xi|^{p}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(w,\overline{y})+H(\overline{y},D\eta(\overline{y})+\xi)\geq f(\overline{y}).\end{cases}

(3.4)

We note that

Dh(\overline{x})=L\omega^{\prime}(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|},\quad\text{with}\quad\omega^{\prime}(t)=1-\frac{1+\beta}{4}t^{\beta},

and

D\eta(\overline{y})=-L\omega^{\prime}(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)\frac{\overline{x}-\overline{y}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|}+D\psi(\overline{y}),\quad\text{with}\quad D\psi(\overline{y})=4m\Big(|\overline{y}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)_{+}\overline{y}.

Moreover, we have

\frac{L}{2}\leq|Dh(\overline{x})|\leq L

(3.5)

and

\frac{L}{4}\leq\frac{L}{2}-4m\Big(|\overline{y}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)_{+}|\overline{y}|\leq|D\eta(\overline{y})|\leq L+4m\Big(|\overline{y}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)_{+}|\overline{y}|\leq\frac{5L}{4}

(3.6)

for large enough
L\geq 12m
.

Set
A=2L
. By the assumption
|\xi|\geq A
, we have

\begin{cases}|Dh(\overline{x})+\xi|\geq|\xi|-|Dh(\overline{x})|\geq L,\\
|D\eta(\overline{y})+\xi|\geq|\xi|-|D\eta(\overline{y})|\geq\frac{3}{4}L.\end{cases}

Applying the viscosity inequalities above (
3.4
) and the growth condition (
2.1
) on
H(\cdot)
, we arrive that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(v,\overline{x})-\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(w,\overline{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\frac{f(\overline{y})}{|D\eta(\overline{y})+\xi|^{p}}-\frac{f(\overline{x})}{|D\eta(\overline{x})+\xi|^{p}}+\frac{H(\overline{x},D\eta(\overline{x})+\xi)}{|D\eta(\overline{x})+\xi|^{p}}-\frac{H(\overline{y},D\eta(\overline{y})+\xi)}{|D\eta(\overline{y})+\xi|^{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq-C(p)\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{L^{p}}-\left(\frac{\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{H}|Dh(\overline{x})+\xi|^{q}}{|Dh(\overline{x})+\xi|^{p}}+\frac{\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{H}|D\eta(\overline{y})+\xi|^{q}}{|D\eta(\overline{y})+\xi|^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq-C(p)\frac{\mathcal{M}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{L^{p}}-\mathcal{H}\left(|Dh(\overline{x})+\xi|^{q-p}+|D\eta(\overline{y})+\xi|^{q-p}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq-C(p,q)(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{H}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}),

(3.7)

where in the last line we have used the facts that
L\geq 1
and
q-p\leq 0
.

Next, let us evaluate the left-hand side of (
3
). As in (2.6) in
[
35
]
or (14) in
[
3
]
, we obtain (
3.8
) below. For every
|z|\leq\frac{1}{8}
, we find
\overline{x}+z,\overline{y}+z\in B_{1}
. Then there exists a kernel
K
in the family
\mathcal{K}_{0}
fulfilling

-C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+1)\leq J_{1}+J_{2}

(3.8)

with

J_{1}=I_{K}[B_{\delta}](\phi(\cdot,\overline{y}),\overline{x})-I_{K}[B_{\delta}](-\phi(\overline{x},\cdot),\overline{y})

and

J_{2}=I_{K}[B_{\frac{1}{8}}\setminus B_{\delta}](u,\overline{x}))-I_{K}[B_{\frac{1}{8}}\setminus B_{\delta}](u,\overline{y}).

Here
C\geq 1
is a universal constant (depending on
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}
).
Let
\overline{a}=\overline{x}-\overline{y}
and

\mathcal{D}=\left\{z\in B_{\delta_{0}|\overline{a}|}:|\langle\overline{a},z\rangle|\geq(1-\eta_{0})|\overline{a}||z|\right\}

with
\delta_{0}|\overline{a}|<\frac{1}{8}
and
\eta_{0}\in(0,1)
to be chosen later. For
z\in B_{\frac{1}{8}}
, from (
3.3
) we obtain

\begin{cases}u(\overline{x}+z)-u(\overline{x})\leq\phi(\overline{x}+z,\overline{y})-\phi(\overline{x},\overline{y}),\\
u(\overline{y}+z)-u(\overline{y})\geq-\phi(\overline{x},\overline{y}+z)+\phi(\overline{x},\overline{y}),\end{cases}

and further

\begin{cases}I_{K}[\mathcal{D}\setminus B_{\delta}](u,\overline{x}))\leq LI_{K}[\mathcal{D}\setminus B_{\delta}](\omega,\overline{a}),\\
I_{K}[\mathcal{D}\setminus B_{\delta}](u,\overline{y}))\geq-LI_{K}[\mathcal{D}\setminus B_{\delta}](\omega,\overline{a})-Cm,\end{cases}

where we employed the smoothness of
\psi
, and the constant
C\geq 1
is uniformly bounded as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. In addition, by means of

u(\overline{x}+z)-u(\overline{x})-(u(\overline{y}+z)-u(\overline{y}))\leq\phi(\overline{x}+z,\overline{y}+z)-\phi(\overline{x},\overline{y}),

we deduce for
\mathcal{O}=(B_{\frac{1}{8}}\setminus B_{\delta})\setminus(\mathcal{D}\setminus B_{\delta})
that

I_{K}[\mathcal{O}](u,\overline{x})-I_{K}[\mathcal{O}](u,\overline{y})\leq I_{K}[\mathcal{O}](\psi,\overline{y})\leq Cm,

where the latter inequality follows from the smoothness of
\psi
. At this point, by combining the inequalities above with (
3.8
), we obtain

-C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+1)\leq J_{1}+2LI_{K}[\mathcal{D}\setminus B_{\delta}](\omega,\overline{a}).

(3.9)

Here we note that
J_{1}\rightarrow 0
as
\delta\rightarrow 0
, and
I_{K}[\mathcal{D}\setminus B_{\delta}](\omega,\overline{a})\rightarrow I_{K}[\mathcal{D}](\omega,\overline{a})
as
\delta\rightarrow 0

by the dominated convergence theorem.

Then we concentrate on the term
I_{K}[\mathcal{D}](\omega,\overline{a})
. Let
l(z)=D_{x}\omega(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)\cdot z
. Then

\displaystyle I_{K}[\mathcal{D}](\omega,\overline{a})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}}(\omega(|\overline{a}+z|)-\omega(|\overline{a}|))K(z)\,dz-\int_{\mathcal{D}}(l(\overline{x}+z)-l(\overline{x}))K(z)\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}}(\omega(|\overline{a}+z|)-\omega(|\overline{a}|)-D_{x}\omega(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)\cdot z)K(z)\,dz

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{D}}\delta^{2}\omega(\cdot,\overline{y})(\overline{x},z)K(z)\,dz,

with
\omega(x,y)=\omega(|x-y|)
and
\delta^{2}\omega
defined as in Lemma
2.3
.
On the first line of the display above, we notice that the second integral equals to 0 by the symmetry of the domain
\mathcal{D}
. Next, we take
\eta_{0}=\delta_{0}=\delta_{1}|\overline{a}|^{\beta}
with
\delta_{1}
a very small positive number depending only upon
\beta
. In view of Lemma
2.3
(1) and

C_{N,\sigma}\frac{\lambda}{|y|^{N+\sigma}}\leq K(y)\leq C_{N,\sigma}\frac{\Lambda}{|y|^{N+\sigma}},

we arrive at

\displaystyle I_{K}[\mathcal{D}](\omega,\overline{a})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\beta(\beta+1)C_{N,\sigma}\lambda}{64}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|z|^{2-N-\sigma}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{c\beta(\beta+1)C_{N,\sigma}\lambda}{64(2-\sigma)}|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1}\eta_{0}^{\frac{N-1}{2}}(\delta_{0}|\overline{a}|)^{2-\sigma}

\displaystyle=-C|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1+\frac{N-1}{2}\beta+(\beta+1)(2-\sigma)},

where in the second line we have exploited the estimate on
\int_{\mathcal{D}}|z|^{2-N-\sigma}\,dz
in Example 1 in
[
7
]
, and
C\geq 1
is a universal constant that is uniformly bounded as
\sigma\rightarrow 2
. Note that we can select
\beta>0
small enough to obtain

\displaystyle\vartheta

\displaystyle=\beta-1+\frac{N-1}{2}\beta+(\beta+1)(2-\sigma)

\displaystyle=1-\sigma+\beta\left(3+\frac{N-1}{2}-\sigma\right)\leq\frac{1-\sigma}{2}<0

for
\sigma\in(1,2)
.

As a result, by taking
\eta_{0}=\delta_{0}=\delta_{1}|\overline{a}|^{\beta}
and sending
\delta\rightarrow 0
in (
3.9
), it follows that

-C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+1)\leq-CL|\overline{a}|^{\vartheta}\leq-CL,

which indicates a contradiction for
L\geq 1
large enough.

Case 2.

|\xi|
is small. Let
|\xi|<A
. Since the argument is similar to Case 1, we only sketch it. Let

\phi(x,y)=\tilde{L}\omega(|x-y|)+\psi(y).

We continue as in Case 1, with
L
substituted by
\tilde{L}
. Then (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) turn into

\frac{\tilde{L}}{4}\leq|Dh(\overline{x})|,|D\eta(\overline{y})|\leq\frac{5}{4}\tilde{L}.

Since
|\xi|<A
, we have

\begin{cases}|Dh(\overline{x})+\xi|\geq|Dh(\overline{x})|-|\xi|\geq\frac{\tilde{L}}{4}-A\geq A,\\
|D\eta(\overline{y})+\xi|\geq\frac{5\tilde{L}}{4}-A\geq 9A,\end{cases}

by taking
\tilde{L}\geq 8A
. Moreover, we also have

\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(v,\overline{x})-\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(w,\overline{y})\geq-C(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{H}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}).

By the same proof in Case 1, we obtain the Lipschitz continuity of
u
by choosing
\tilde{L}
sufficiently large.
∎

In the scenario
p<q\leq p+1
, we need pay special attention to restraining the growth of the term
|Du+\xi|^{q-p}
, but the idea is similar to that of Lemma
3.1
as well.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\sigma\in(1,2)
and assume that the conditions
(A_{1})
–
(A_{3})
hold with
p<q\leq p+1
. Let
u\in C(\overline{B}_{1})
be a viscosity solution to (
2.3
) with
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then there exists a universal constant
b_{0}>0
such that if

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(1+|\xi|^{q-p})\leq b

(3.10)

for some
b\leq b_{0}
, then
u
is locally Hölder continuous in
B_{1}
.
In other words, there exist universal constants
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C\geq 1
(independent of
\xi
) satisfying

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha}

for every
x,y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
. Furthermore, the constant
C
is uniformly bounded as
\sigma\rightarrow 2
.

Proof.

Let

\theta=\left(\frac{b_{0}}{\mathcal{H}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-p}}.

In view of (
3.10
), we have

\theta\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad|\xi|\leq\theta.

(3.11)

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
3.1
. We substitute
\omega(t)
in (
3.1
) in Lemma
3.1
with

\varphi(t)=t^{\alpha}
,
t\in[0,\infty)
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
to be fixed. By doubling variables we introduce two functions

\begin{cases}\widetilde{\phi}(x,y)=L\varphi(|x-y|)+\psi(y),\\
\Psi(x,y)=u(x)-u(y)-\widetilde{\phi}(x,y).\end{cases}

Let
\Psi(\overline{x},\overline{y})=\sup_{B_{1}\times B_{1}}\Psi(x,y)
with
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in\overline{B}_{1}\times\overline{B}_{1}
. We show that
\Psi(\overline{x},\overline{y})\leq 0

in order to conclude the local Hölder continuity.
For a contradiction, assume that
\Psi(\overline{x},\overline{y})>0
.
Then

L|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha}+m\left[\left(|\overline{y}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)_{+}\right]^{2}\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

We may choose

m>2\left(\frac{16}{5}\right)^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\quad\text{and}\quad L>16\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}

such that
|\overline{y}|<\frac{3}{4}
,
|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|<\frac{1}{8}
.
It follows that
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in B_{\frac{7}{8}}\times B_{\frac{7}{8}}\subset B_{1}\times B_{1}
and
\overline{x}\neq\overline{y}
, as well as

|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|\leq\left(\frac{2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{L}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(3.12)

As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we obtain the viscosity inequalities

\begin{cases}-|D\widetilde{h}(\overline{x})+\xi|^{p}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(\widetilde{v},\overline{x})+H(\overline{x},D\widetilde{h}(\overline{x})+\xi)\leq f(\overline{x}),\\
-|D\widetilde{\eta}(\overline{y})+\xi|^{p}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(\widetilde{w},\overline{y})+H(\overline{y},D\widetilde{\eta}(\overline{y})+\xi)\geq f(\overline{y}),\end{cases}

(3.13)

where

\begin{cases}\widetilde{h}(x)=\widetilde{\phi}(x,\overline{y})-\widetilde{\phi}(\overline{x},\overline{y})+u(\overline{x}),\\
\widetilde{\eta}(y)=-\widetilde{\phi}(\overline{x},y)+\widetilde{\phi}(\overline{x},\overline{y})+u(\overline{y}),\end{cases}

along with

\widetilde{v}(z)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{h}(z),\quad z\in B_{\delta}(\overline{x}),\\
u(z)\quad\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

and

\widetilde{w}(z)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{\eta}(z),\quad z\in B_{\delta}(\overline{y}),\\
u(z)\quad\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{8})
. A direct calculation shows that

\begin{cases}D\widetilde{h}(\overline{x})=\alpha L|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-2}(\overline{x}-\overline{y}),\\
D\widetilde{\eta}(\overline{y})=-\alpha L|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-2}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})+4m\left(|\overline{y}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)_{+}\overline{y},\end{cases}

and further

\begin{cases}|D\widetilde{h}(\overline{x})|=\alpha L|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-1},\\
\frac{\alpha L|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-1}}{2}\leq|D\widetilde{\eta}(\overline{y})|\leq 2\alpha L|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-1}\end{cases}

for

. Since

we may take

large enough that

\frac{15m}{16}\leq\alpha L<\alpha L|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-1}.

Moreover, by taking

, we obtain

and

|D\widetilde{h}(\overline{x})+\xi|\geq\alpha L|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-1}-\theta\geq 4\theta|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-1}-\theta\geq 3\theta|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-1},

where (

were utilized. Analogously, we can treat

. In summary, we have

(3.14)

Merging (

), (

) with the condition (

) on

with
p<q\leq p+1
, we arrive at

\begin{split}&\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(\widetilde{v},\overline{x})-\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(\widetilde{w},\overline{y})\\
&\qquad\geq-2\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}-\left(\frac{\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{H}|D\widetilde{h}(\overline{x})+\xi|^{q}}{|D\widetilde{h}(\overline{x})+\xi|^{p}}+\frac{\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{H}|D\widetilde{\eta}(\overline{y})+\xi|^{q}}{|D\widetilde{\eta}(\overline{y})+\xi|^{p}}\right)\\
&\qquad\geq-2(\mathcal{M}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})})-\mathcal{H}\left(|D\widetilde{h}(\overline{x})+\xi|^{q-p}+|D\widetilde{\eta}(\overline{y})+\xi|^{q-p}\right)\\
&\qquad\geq-2(\mathcal{M}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})})-\mathcal{H}(3\alpha L)^{q-p}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{(\alpha-1)(q-p)}.\end{split}

(3.15)

The estimate on the term
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(\widetilde{v},\overline{x})-\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(\widetilde{w},\overline{y})
in the previous display is similar to Lemma
3.1

and we obtain

\begin{split}&-C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M})\\
&\qquad-\mathcal{H}(3\alpha L)^{q-p}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{(\alpha-1)(q-p)}\leq LI_{K}[\mathcal{D}](\varphi,\overline{a}),\end{split}

(3.16)

corresponding to (
3.9
) as
\delta\rightarrow 0
.
Let
l(z)=D_{x}\varphi(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)\cdot z
. There holds

I_{K}[\mathcal{D}](\varphi,\overline{a})=-\int_{\mathcal{D}}\delta^{2}\varphi(\cdot,\overline{y})(\overline{x},z)K(z)\,dz

with

. By virtue of Lemma

(2) and

\begin{split}I_{K}[\mathcal{D}](\varphi,\overline{a})&\leq-\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)2^{\alpha}C_{N,\sigma}\lambda}{32}|\overline{a}|^{\alpha-2}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|z|^{2-N-\sigma}\,dz\\
&\leq-C|\overline{a}|^{\alpha-2}\eta_{0}^{\frac{N-1}{2}}(\delta_{0}|\overline{a}|)^{2-\sigma}=-\widetilde{C}|\overline{a}|^{\alpha-\sigma},\end{split}

(3.17)

where in the last line we have selected
\eta_{0}=\delta_{0}=\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
a number coming from Lemma
2.3
(2), and the constant
\widetilde{C}>0
is uniformly bounded as
\sigma\rightarrow 2
. It follows from (

), (

) that

\begin{split}&C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M})\\
&\qquad+\mathcal{H}(3\alpha L)^{q-p}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{(\alpha-1)(q-p)}\geq\widetilde{C}L|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-\sigma}\end{split}

and further by
0<\alpha<1<\sigma

\begin{split}\widetilde{C}L&\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M})\\
&\qquad+\mathcal{H}(3\alpha L)^{q-p}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{(\alpha-1)(q-p)+\sigma-\alpha}\end{split}

Observe that, since

and

(\alpha-1)(q-p)+\sigma-\alpha=(\alpha-1)(q-p-1)+\sigma-1>0.

Therefore, recalling (

\widetilde{C}L\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M})+3b_{0}\alpha^{q-p}L,

i.e.,

(\widetilde{C}-3b_{0}\alpha^{q-p})L\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M}).

Let

\alpha\leq\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-p}}\quad\text{and}\quad 0<b_{0}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}}{2}.

Then

\frac{\widetilde{C}}{2}L\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M}).

with
C,\widetilde{C}>0
being two universal constants independent of
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{H}
. Taking sufficiently large

leads to a contradiction from the preceding inequality.
∎

In the end of this section, we are ready to consider the case
p+1<q\leq p+\sigma
. Unfortunately, based on the technology applied in the present paper, we can not verify the

-regularity of viscosity solution, so we are unnecessary to examine the gradient perturbation equation (

), which is of independent interest.

We first clarify the derivation of the upper constraint condition

as follows. We follow the proof of Lemma
3.2
verbatim, except taking
\xi=0
this time, and arrive (
3.18
). In order to derive a contradiction from (
3.18
) by choosing large enough
L
, we need require that the exponent
(\alpha-1)(q-p)+\sigma-\alpha
is positive, which together with (
3.12
) may make the exponent of
L
on the right-hand side of (
3.18
) not larger than 1. That is, we demand

1\geq q-p-\frac{(\alpha-1)(q-p)+\sigma-\alpha}{\alpha},

or equivalently
p+\sigma\geq q
.
It is worth mentioning that if

, the condition above coincides with the condition imposed by
[

,

]
for local fully nonlinear equation with a Hamiltonian term.
On the contrary, if the condition

is violated, we cannot conclude the Hölder continuity by the classical Ishii–Lions method, since we cannot find an appropriate

for the contradiction.

The forthcoming lemma states the Hölder regularity for the equation (
1.1
), whose proof is very similar to that of Lemma

.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\sigma\in(1,2)
and assume that the conditions
(A_{1})
–
(A_{3})
hold with
p+1<q\leq p+\sigma
. Let
u\in C(\overline{B}_{1})
be a viscosity solution to (
1.1
). Then
u
is locally Hölder continuous in
B_{1}
. More precisely, there exist universal constants
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C\geq 1
such that

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha}

for every
x,y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
, where the constant
C
is uniformly bounded as
\sigma\rightarrow 2
.

Proof.

We use the same notation as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
As in the proof of Lemma
3.2
with
\xi=0
, we arrive at (
3.18
) and rewrite it as

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}L

\displaystyle\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{H}(3\alpha L)^{q-p}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{(\alpha-1)(q-p)+\sigma-\alpha}.

For
p+1<q\leq p+\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle q-p-\frac{(\alpha-1)(q-p)+\sigma-\alpha}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=q-p-\frac{(\alpha-1)(q-p-1)+\sigma-1}{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq q-p-\frac{(\alpha-1)(\sigma-1)+\sigma-1}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=q-p+1-\sigma\in(2-\sigma,1].

Then by (
3.12
) and
p+1<q\leq p+\sigma
, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}L

\displaystyle\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathcal{H}(3\alpha L)^{q-p}\left(\frac{2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{L}\right)^{\frac{(\alpha-1)(q-p)+\sigma-\alpha}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\qquad+(2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})})^{\gamma}\mathcal{H}(3\alpha)^{q-p}L^{1+q-p-\sigma},

with
\gamma=q-p-1+\frac{p+\sigma-q}{\alpha}
.
It follows that

\begin{split}&(\widetilde{C}-(2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})})^{\gamma}\mathcal{H}(3\alpha)^{q-p}L^{q-p-\sigma})L\\
&\qquad\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M}).\end{split}

(3.19)

If
q\in(p+1,p+\sigma)
, we may select
L
sufficiently large to obtain

\widetilde{C}-(2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})})^{\gamma}\mathcal{H}(3\alpha)^{q-p}L^{q-p-\sigma}\geq\frac{\widetilde{C}}{2}.

By choosing
L
large enough concludes a contradiction with (
3.19
).
If
q=p+\sigma
, the coefficient of
L
becomes

\widetilde{C}-(2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})})^{q-p+1}\mathcal{H}(3\alpha)^{q-p},

and then we may take a universal
\alpha\in(0,1)
small enough that

\frac{\widetilde{C}}{2}\leq\widetilde{C}-(2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})})^{q-p+1}\mathcal{H}(3\alpha)^{q-p}.

Finally, by choosing
L
large enough we may arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Based on the Hölder continuity of solutions above, Lemma

, we could further justify the Lipschitz regularity (Theorem

) for Eq. (

), whose proof is stated as follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma
3.3
implies that
u
is locally
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in
B_{1}
.
For the sake of convenience, we assume

with

.
The argument is analogous to that of Lemma
3.1
, with the difference that the Hölder continuity of

is applied instead of the boundedness of
u
.

Let us use the same notation as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
. Corresponding to the display (
3.2

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\Psi(\overline{x},\overline{y})\leq u(\overline{x})-u(\overline{y})-L\omega(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)-\psi(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\leq[u]_{\alpha}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha}-L\omega(|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|)-m\left[\left(|\overline{y}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)_{+}\right]^{2}.

By taking

we conclude that
\overline{x}\neq\overline{y}
,
|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|<\frac{1}{8}
and
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in B_{\frac{7}{8}}\times B_{\frac{7}{8}}\subset B_{1}\times B_{1}
, together with

|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{1-\alpha}\leq\frac{2[u]_{\alpha}}{L}.

(3.20)

Following the argument in Case 1 of the proof of Lemma
3.1
, except letting
\xi=0
this time, the inequality (
3

\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(v,\overline{x})-\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}(w,\overline{y})\geq-C(p)(\mathcal{M}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})})-C(q)\mathcal{H}L^{q-p}

and further (

\displaystyle-C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M})-C\mathcal{H}L^{q-p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2LI_{K}[\mathcal{D}](\omega,\overline{a})\leq-CL|\overline{a}|^{\beta-1+\frac{N-1}{2}\beta+(\beta+1)(2-\sigma)}.

Note that

\begin{split}CL&\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}+\mathcal{M})+C\mathcal{H}L^{q-p}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{-\iota}\\

4.
Gradient Hölder regularity of solutions

In this section, we are going to demonstrate the gradient Hölder continuity of viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) by a perturbation argument. The core of this idea is to find a solution with known
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity to approximate the solution of (
1.1
), so that this property can be transferred to the solution under consideration. Therefore, through the flatness improvement argument, we shall first show the following approximation lemma that implies the nonlocal equation (
2.3
) can be uniformly close to a (local) uniformly elliptic equation when
\sigma
approaches to two.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let the conditions
(A_{1})
–
(A_{4})
with
0\leq q\leq p+1
hold true. Let
u\in C(\overline{B}_{1})
be a normalized viscosity solution to (
2.3
).
Given
\mu,\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\kappa>0
depending on
N
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\varepsilon
,
p
and
\mu
such that if

|\sigma-2|+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{H}\bigl(1+|\xi|^{(q-p)_{+}}\bigr)\leq\kappa

and

|u(x)|\leq\mu(1+|x|^{1+\overline{\alpha}}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

with some
\overline{\alpha}\in(0,1)
, then there exists an
F
-harmonic function
h\in C^{1,\overline{\alpha}}_{\rm loc}(B_{1})
(i.e.,
h
is a solution to
F(D^{2}h)=0
in the viscosity sense) satisfying

\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

For a contradiction, assume that there exist
\mu_{0},\varepsilon_{0}>0
and sequences of
(\sigma_{j})
,
(f_{j})
,
(u_{j})
,
(H_{j})
,
(\xi_{j})
such that

(i)

u_{j}\in C(B_{1})
with
\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
and
|u_{j}(x)|\leq\mu_{0}(1+|x|^{1+\overline{\alpha}})
is a solution to

-|Du_{j}+\xi_{j}|^{p}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma_{j}}(u_{j},x)+H_{j}(x,Du_{j}+\xi_{j})=f_{j}(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1},

(4.1)

where the operator
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma_{j}}
satisfies
(A_{4})
;

(ii)

the Hamiltonian term fulfills

|H_{j}(x,\xi)|\leq\mathcal{M}_{j}+\mathcal{H}_{j}|\xi|^{q-p};

(4.2)

(iii)

and

|\sigma_{j}-2|+\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\mathcal{M}_{j}+\mathcal{H}_{j}\bigl(1+|\xi_{j}|^{(q-p)_{+}}\bigr)\leq\frac{1}{j}.

(4.3)

However, we have

\|u_{k}-h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}>\varepsilon_{0}

for any
h(x)\in C^{1,\overline{\alpha}}_{\rm loc}(B_{1})
. With the help of
\sigma_{j}\rightarrow 2
and the condition (
A_{4}
), we have
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma_{j}}\rightarrow F
, where
F
is a uniformly
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic operator. Moreover, based on
\mathcal{H}_{j}\left(1+|\xi_{j}|^{(q-p)_{+}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{j}
, Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we can find a continuous function
v
such that
u_{j}\rightarrow v
locally uniformly in
B_{1}
by compactness. In particular, we have

v\in C(B_{\frac{3}{4}})
,
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}\leq 1
and

\|v-h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}}>\varepsilon_{0}.

(4.4)

Next, we are ready to verify that
v
is a viscosity solution to

-F(D^{2}u)=0\quad\text{in }B_{\frac{3}{4}}.

(4.5)

We shall only prove that
v
is a viscosity supersolution, since the argument for
v
is a subsolution is similar.
Let
\varphi
be a smooth function such that
v-\varphi
has a local minimum in
B_{1}
at
\tilde{x}
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
|\tilde{x}|=v(0)=\varphi(0)=0
and that
\varphi
is a quadratic polynomial, i.e.,

\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{2}Ax\cdot x+\eta\cdot x,

where
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
A
is a symmetric matrix and the dot means the standard inner product.
Since
u_{j}\rightarrow v
locally uniformly in
B_{1}
, there exists a point
x_{k}
in a small neighborhood of the origin and a quadratic polynomial

\varphi_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{2}A(x-x_{j})\cdot(x-x_{j})+\eta\cdot(x-x_{j})+u_{j}(x_{j})

touching
u_{j}
from below at
x_{j}
. By
u_{j}
a viscosity solution to (
4.1
), we have

-|\eta+\xi_{j}|^{p}\mathcal{I}_{\sigma_{j}}(u_{j},x)+H_{j}(x,\eta+\xi_{j})\geq f_{j}(x_{j}).

(4.6)

In the scenario that
\{\xi_{j}\}
is unbounded, we assume
|\xi_{j}|\rightarrow\infty
up to a subsequence. Using (
4.2
), (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|H_{j}(x_{j},\eta+\xi_{j})|}{|\eta+\xi_{j}|^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq|\xi_{j}|^{q-p}\frac{\mathcal{M}_{j}|\xi_{j}|^{-q}+\mathcal{H}_{j}|\hat{\xi}_{j}+|\xi_{j}|^{-1}\eta|^{q}}{|\hat{\xi}_{j}+|\xi_{j}|^{-1}\eta|^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q+p}\frac{|\xi_{j}|^{q-p}}{j(|\xi_{j}|^{(q-p)_{+}}+1)}\rightarrow 0

and

\frac{f_{j}(x_{j})}{|\eta+\xi_{j}|^{p}}\rightarrow 0

as

. Here
\hat{x}=\frac{x}{|x|}
. Hence passing to the limit in (
4.6
), we get

-F(A)=-\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{I}^{\delta}_{\sigma_{j}}(u_{j},\varphi_{j},x_{j})\geq 0.

If
(xi_{j})
is a bounded sequence, we may assume that
\xi_{j}\rightarrow\overline{\xi}
up to a subsequence.
We first consider the case
|\eta+\overline{\xi}|>0
. Applying (
4.2
), (
4.3
),
we conclude that

\frac{|f_{j}(x_{j})|}{|\eta+\xi_{j}|^{p}}\leq\frac{2^{p}}{j|\eta+\overline{\xi}|^{p}}\rightarrow 0

\frac{|H_{j}(x_{j},\eta+\xi_{j})|}{|\eta+\xi_{j}|^{p}}\leq\frac{\mathcal{M}_{j}+\mathcal{H}_{j}|\xi_{j}+\eta|^{q}}{|\xi_{j}+\eta|^{p}}\leq\frac{1+|\xi_{j}+\eta|^{q}}{j|\eta+\xi_{j}|^{p}(|\xi_{j}|^{(q-p)_{+}}+1)}\rightarrow 0

as

. At this moment, passing to the limit in (

), we also obtain

-F(A)=-\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{I}^{\delta}_{\sigma_{j}}(u_{j},\varphi_{j},x_{j})\geq 0.

In the sequel, we focus on the situation

, for which we show that
F(A)\leq 0
.
For a contradiction, assume that

F(A)>0.

(4.7)

From this and the uniform ellipticity of
F(\cdot)
, it follows that there is at least one positive eigenvalue related to the matrix
A
.
Consider the orthogonal sum
\mathbb{R}^{n}=T\oplus Q
with
T
denoting the invariant space consisting of the eigenvectors corresponding to positive eigenvalues.

Case 1.

b=-\overline{\eta}\neq 0
. Let
\gamma>0
and

\varphi_{\gamma}(x)=\varphi(x)+\gamma|P_{T}(x)|=\frac{1}{2}Ax\cdot x+\eta\cdot x+\gamma|P_{T}(x)|,

where

stands for the orthogonal projection over

.
Since
u_{j}\rightarrow v
locally uniformly and
\varphi
touches

at 0 from below, it follows that
\varphi_{\gamma}
touches
u_{j}
from below at some

in a neighbourhood of 0 for

for some
\overline{x}\in B_{\frac{3}{4}}
as

(up to a subsequence).

If

, we observe that

\phi_{\gamma}(x)=\frac{1}{2}Ax\cdot x+\eta\cdot x+\gamma e\cdot P_{T}(x)

touches
u_{j}
from below at
x^{\gamma}_{j}
for every

(i.e.,
|e|=1
). This implies a viscosity inequality

\begin{split}&-|\eta+Ax^{\gamma}_{j}+\gamma P_{T}(e)+\xi_{j}|^{p}\mathcal{I}^{\delta}_{\sigma_{j}}(u_{j},\phi_{\gamma},x^{\gamma}_{j})\\
&\qquad+H_{j}(x_{j}^{\gamma},\eta+Ax^{\gamma}_{j}+\gamma P_{T}(e)+\xi_{j})\geq f_{j}(x^{\gamma}_{j}),\end{split}

(4.8)

where
D(e\cdot P_{T}(x))=P_{T}(e)
. If
A\overline{x}=0
, then we can see
|\eta+\xi_{j}+Ax^{\gamma}_{j}|\rightarrow 0
, so we get

2\gamma>|\eta+Ax^{\gamma}_{j}+\gamma P_{T}(e)+\xi_{j}|>\frac{\gamma}{2}

for large enough

, through selecting

such that
P_{T}(e)=e
.
On the other hand, if
A\overline{x}\neq 0
and

, we choose

fulfilling

\displaystyle 2|A\overline{x}+\gamma e|

\displaystyle>|\eta+Ax^{\gamma}_{j}+\gamma P_{T}(e)+\xi_{j}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}|A\overline{x}+\gamma e|-\frac{1}{4}|A\overline{x}+\gamma e|=\frac{1}{4}|A\overline{x}+\gamma e|>0,

where we applied

\displaystyle-|\eta+Ax^{\gamma}_{j}+\gamma e^{\gamma}_{j}+\xi_{j}|^{p}\mathcal{I}^{\delta}_{\sigma_{j}}(u_{j},\varphi_{\gamma},x^{\gamma}_{j})+H_{j}(x^{\gamma}_{j},\eta+Ax^{\gamma}_{j}+\gamma e^{\gamma}_{j}+\xi_{j})\geq f_{j}(x^{\gamma}_{j})

As in the case when

Case 2.

Definition 5.1

A set

A sequence of moduli of continuity

Lemma 5.3

Let

(a_{j})\in\ell_{1}

.

If a sequence of moduli of continuity

is shored-up, then
\Gamma=\cup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\{\gamma_{j}\}

\mathcal{I}_{\sigma_{j}}
satisfies
(A_{4})
as well;

(ii)

\gamma_{j}
is a modulus of continuity with
\gamma_{j}(0)=0
and
\gamma_{j}(1)\geq 1
. Moreover, if
\gamma_{j}(a_{j})\rightarrow 0
, then
a_{j}\rightarrow 0
;

(iii)

the Hamiltonian term fulfills

|H_{j}(x,\xi)|\leq\mathcal{M}_{j}(1+\gamma_{j}(|\xi|));

(iv)

and the smallness condition

|\sigma_{j}-2|+\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\mathcal{M}_{j}\leq\frac{1}{j}

holds.

Moreover, we have

\|u_{j}-h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}>\varepsilon_{0}.

for every
h\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(B_{1})
.

The rest of the proof is very similar to that of Lemma
4.1
by utilizing Lemma
5.5
), where we replace
|Du_{j}+\xi_{j}|^{p}
by
\gamma_{j}(|Du_{j}+\xi_{j}|)
. It is worth pointing out that when considering the term
\gamma_{j}(\cdot)
, we need invoke the non-collapsing property of the set
\Gamma
(that is,
\gamma_{j}(a_{j})\rightarrow 0

), or we make use of the equivalent property on non-collapsing sets in

In the sequel, we shall concentrate on the existence of approximating hyperplanes. Let
h\in C^{1,\alpha}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})
is an
F
-harmonic function from Lemma

. Let
L\geq 1
be a number satisfying
\|h\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq L
. Consider two moduli of continuity

\beta(t)=t\gamma(t)\quad\text{and}\quad\omega(t)=\beta^{-1}(t).

Next, we choose
0<\nu_{1}<1
as follows. If
t^{\alpha}=o(\omega(t))
, we take
0<r<\frac{1}{2}
so small that

\nu_{1}=\omega(r)=2Lr^{\alpha}>r^{\sigma-1},

which could be achieved by taking
\sigma
sufficiently close to 2 (this allows
\sigma-1>\alpha
).
If
\omega(t)=O(t^{\alpha})
, we let
0<\theta<\alpha
and choose
0<r<\frac{1}{2}
so small that

\nu_{1}=r^{\theta}=2Lr^{\alpha}>r^{\sigma-1},

where we applied
0<\theta<\alpha<\sigma-1
. Observe that once we determine
0<\theta<\alpha
, the previous choice becomes universal.

We proceed by letting

0<\vartheta=\frac{r^{\sigma-1}}{\nu_{1}}<1\quad\text{and}\quad(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}=(\gamma^{-1}(\vartheta^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}.

Because the inverse
\gamma^{-1}
is Dini continuous, we may conclude that
(\gamma^{-1}(\vartheta^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{1}
. With the help of Lemma
5.3
, we can find a sequence
(c_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{0}
fulfilling

\frac{9}{10}\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\gamma^{-1}(\vartheta^{k})\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{\gamma^{-1}(\vartheta^{k})}{c_{k}}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\gamma^{-1}(\vartheta^{k}).

(5.3)

Then we construct a sequence of moduli of continuity
(\gamma_{k}(t))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
by a recursive formula, the derivation of which is from Proposition
5.8
below.

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\gamma(t),

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu_{1}}{r^{\sigma-1}}\gamma(\nu_{1}t),

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu_{2}\nu_{1}}{r^{2(\sigma-1)}}\gamma(\nu_{2}\nu_{1}t),

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod^{k}_{i=1}\nu_{i}}{r^{k(\sigma-1)}}\gamma\left(\prod^{k}_{i=1}\nu_{i}t\right),

where
\nu_{1}>r^{\sigma-1}
has been defined as above, and
\nu_{k}
for
k\geq 2
is determined by the following algorithm. If

\frac{\nu_{1}^{2}}{r^{2(\sigma-1)}}\gamma(\nu_{1}^{2}c_{2})\geq 1,

then we choose
\nu_{2}=\nu_{1}
; otherwise, we choose
\nu_{2}\in(\nu_{1},1)
so that

\frac{\nu_{2}\nu_{1}}{r^{2(\sigma-1)}}\gamma(\nu_{1}\nu_{2}c_{2})=1,

where
c_{2}
is the second element of the sequence
(c_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{0}
for which (
5.3
) is valid. Then we recursively apply this algorithm.
Assume that we have chosen
r^{\sigma-1}<\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}\leq\dots\leq\nu_{k}<1
.
Then we select
\nu_{k+1}
as follows. If

\frac{\nu_{k}\prod^{k}_{i=1}\nu_{i}}{r^{(k+1)(\sigma-1)}}\gamma\biggl(\nu_{k}\biggl(\prod^{k}_{i=1}\nu_{i}\biggr)c_{k+1}\biggr)\geq 1,

we let
\nu_{k+1}=\nu_{k}
. Otherwise, we choose
\nu_{k}<\nu_{k+1}<1
such that
\gamma_{k+1}(c_{k+1})=1
. Here
c_{k+1}
is the
(k+1)
-th element of
(c_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{0}
for which (
5.3
) is true. According to Definition
5.2
, we know that the sequence of moduli of continuity
(\gamma_{k}(t))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is shored-up. Let

\Gamma=\{\gamma_{0}(t),\gamma_{1}(t),\dots,\gamma_{k}(t),\dots\}.

Then, employing Lemma
5.4
, the collection
\Gamma
is non-collapsing.

Lemma 5.7
.

Assume that the conditions
(A_{1}),(A_{2})
,
(A_{4})
–
(A_{6})
hold. Let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a normalized solution to (
5.1
). Given
\mu>0
, there exists
\kappa>0
such that if

|\sigma-2|+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\mathcal{M}\leq\kappa

and

|u(x)|\leq\mu(1+|x|^{1+\alpha}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

there exists an affine function
l(x)=a+b\cdot x
with
|a|+|b|\leq L
and

\|u-l\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\leq\nu_{1}r,

where
0<r<\frac{1}{2}
and
L>0
are universal constants.

Proof.

By means of Lemma
5.6
, we know that there exists an
F
-harmonic function
h\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(B_{1})
such that

\sup_{x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}}|u(x)-h(x)|\leq\varepsilon

for some
\varepsilon>0
to be fixed later. Once
\varepsilon
is chosen, we may determine the value of
\kappa
in Lemma
5.6
. From the regularity theory, it is well known that, for a universal constant
L>0
and any
0<r<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\sup_{x\in B_{r}}|h(x)-h(0)-Dh(0)\cdot x|\leq Lr^{1+\alpha}

with
|h(0)|+|Dh(0)|\leq L
. Therefore, by letting
a=h(0)
and
b=Dh(0)
, we get

\sup_{x\in B_{r}}|u(x)-a-b\cdot x|\leq\varepsilon+Lr^{1+\alpha}=\varepsilon+\frac{\nu_{1}}{2}r.

Finally, taking
\varepsilon=\frac{\nu_{1}}{2}r
completes the proof.
∎

In the sequel, we generalize Lemma
5.7
to arbitrarily small radii in discrete scales, which can easily conclude the
C^{1}
-regularity result, Theorem
1.5
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Assume that the conditions
(A_{1}),(A_{2})
,
(A_{4})
–
(A_{6})
hold. Let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a normalized solution to (
5.1
). Given
\mu>0
, there exists
\kappa>0
such that if

|\sigma-2|+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\mathcal{M}\leq\kappa

and

|u(x)|\leq\mu(1+|x|^{1+\alpha}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

there exists a sequence of affine functions
(l_{j})
,
l_{j}(x)=A_{j}+B_{j}\cdot x
, fulfilling

\sup_{x\in B_{r^{j}}}|u(x)-l_{j}(x)|\leq\biggl(\prod^{j}_{i=1}\nu_{i}\biggr)r^{j},

|A_{j+1}-A_{j}|\leq C\biggl(\prod^{j}_{i=1}\nu_{i}\biggr)r^{j}\quad\text{and}\quad|B_{j+1}-B_{j}|\leq C\prod^{j}_{i=1}\nu_{i}

for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, where
0<r<\frac{1}{2}
and
C>0
are two universal constants.

Proof.

We argue by induction. Let

u_{1}(x)=\frac{u(rx)-\overline{l}_{0}(rx)}{\nu_{1}r},

with
\nu_{1}
and
\overline{l}_{0}(x)=a_{0}+b_{0}x=l(x)
as in Lemma
5.7
. Then
u_{1}
is a solution to

\gamma_{1}\left(\left|Du_{1}+\frac{b_{0}}{\nu_{1}}\right|\right)\mathcal{I}_{1}(u_{1},x)+H_{1}\left(x,Du_{1}+\frac{b_{0}}{\nu_{1}}\right)=f_{1}(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1},

where

f_{1}(x)=f(rx),\quad\gamma_{1}(t)=\frac{\nu_{1}}{r^{\sigma-1}}\gamma(\nu_{1}t),\quad H_{1}(x,\xi)=H(rx,\nu_{1}\xi)

and the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{I}_{1}
possesses the same uniform ellipticity property as the
\mathcal{I}
in (
1.1
).
By the argument before Lemma
5.7
, we have
\gamma_{1}(1)=1
. By
(A_{6})
, we have

\displaystyle|H_{1}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}(1+\gamma(\nu_{1}|\xi|))

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}\left(1+\frac{\nu_{1}}{r^{\sigma-1}}\gamma(\nu_{1}|\xi|)\right)=\mathcal{M}(1+\gamma_{1}(|\xi|)).

Moreover, we have
|u_{1}(x)|\leq\mu(1+|x|^{1+\alpha})
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which will be proved later.
Thus we have verified that
u_{1}
falls into the framework of Lemma
5.7
. Hence there exists an affine function
\overline{l}_{1}(x)=a_{1}+b_{1}\cdot x
with
|a_{1}|+|b_{1}|\leq L
such that

\sup_{x\in B_{r}}|u_{1}(x)-\overline{l}_{1}(x)|\leq\nu_{1}r.

Next, let

u_{2}(x)=\frac{u_{1}(rx)-\overline{l}_{1}(rx)}{\nu_{2}r}

for
\nu_{2}\geq\nu_{1}>r^{\sigma-1}
chosen earlier. Then
u_{2}
is a solution to

\gamma_{2}\left(\left|Du_{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\nu_{2}}+\frac{b_{0}}{\nu_{2}\nu_{1}}\right|\right)\mathcal{I}_{2}(u_{2},x)+H_{2}\left(x,Du_{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\nu_{2}}+\frac{b_{0}}{\nu_{2}\nu_{1}}\right)=f_{2}(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1},

where

f_{2}(x)=f_{1}(rx)=f(r^{2}x),\quad\gamma_{2}(t)=\frac{\nu_{2}}{r^{\sigma-1}}\gamma_{1}(\nu_{2}t)=\frac{\nu_{2}\nu_{1}}{r^{2(\sigma-1)}}\gamma(\nu_{2}\nu_{1}t)

and

H_{2}(x,\xi)=H_{1}(rx,\nu_{2}\xi)=H(r^{2}x,\nu_{2}\nu_{1}\xi),

and the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{I}_{2}
has the same uniform ellipticity condition as the
\mathcal{I}
in (
1.1
). In view of
(A_{6})
, we have

\displaystyle|H_{2}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}(1+\gamma(\nu_{2}\nu_{1}|\xi|))

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}\left(1+\frac{\nu_{2}\nu_{1}}{r^{2(\sigma-1)}}\gamma(\nu_{2}\nu_{1}|\xi|)\right)=\mathcal{M}(1+\gamma_{2}(|\xi|)).

Moreover, we have
|u_{2}(x)|\leq\mu(1+|x|^{1+\alpha})
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which will be proved later. That is,
u_{2}
also falls into the framework of Lemma
5.7
.
Hence there exists an affine function
\overline{l}_{2}(x)=a_{2}+b_{2}\cdot x
with
|a_{2}|+|b_{2}|\leq L
such that

\sup_{x\in B_{r}}|u_{2}(x)-\overline{l}_{2}(x)|\leq\nu_{1}r.

Recursively, let

u_{j}(x)=\frac{u_{j-1}(rx)-\overline{l}_{j-1}(rx)}{\nu_{j}r}

for
\nu_{j}\geq\nu_{j-1}\geq\dots\geq\nu_{1}>r^{\sigma-1}
selected earlier. Then
u_{j}
is a solution to

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}\left(\left|Du_{j}+\frac{b_{j-1}}{\nu_{j}}+\frac{b_{j-2}}{\nu_{j}\nu_{j-1}}+\dots+\frac{b_{0}}{\nu_{j}\nu_{j-1}\dots\nu_{1}}\right|\right)\mathcal{I}_{j}(u_{j},x)

\displaystyle\qquad+H_{j}\left(x,Du_{j}+\frac{b_{j-1}}{\nu_{j}}+\frac{b_{j-2}}{\nu_{j}\nu_{j-1}}+\dots+\frac{b_{0}}{\nu_{j}\nu_{j-1}\dots\nu_{1}}\right)=f_{j}(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1},

where
f_{j}(x)=f_{j-1}(rx)=\dots=f(r^{j}x)
,

\gamma_{j}(t)=\frac{\nu_{j}}{r^{\sigma-1}}\gamma_{j-1}(\nu_{j}t)=\dots=\frac{\prod^{j}_{i=1}\nu_{i}}{r^{j(\sigma-1)}}\gamma\biggl(\prod^{j}_{i=1}\nu_{i}t\biggr)

and

H_{j}(x,\xi)=H_{j-1}(rx,\nu_{j}\xi)=\dots=H\biggl(r^{j}x,\biggl(\prod^{j}_{i=1}\nu_{i}\biggr)\xi\biggr)

and the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{I}_{j}
carries the same uniform ellipticity condition as
\mathcal{I}
in (
1.1
). Recall that
\nu_{j}\geq\nu_{j-1}
is fixed in such way that either
\nu_{j}=\nu_{j-1}
or else
\gamma_{j}(c_{j})=1
. Thanks to
(A_{6})
, we get

\displaystyle|H_{j}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}\biggl(1+\gamma\biggl(\prod^{j}_{i=1}\nu_{i}|\xi|\biggr)\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}\biggl(1+\frac{\prod^{j}_{i=1}\nu_{i}}{r^{j(\sigma-1)}}\gamma\biggl(\prod^{j}_{i=1}\nu_{i}|\xi|\biggr)\biggr)=\mathcal{M}(1+\gamma_{j}(|\xi|)).

Finally, we prove that

|u_{j}(x)|\leq\mu(1+|x|^{1+\alpha}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(5.4)

At this stage,
u_{j}
meets the requirements of Lemma
5.7
. Then we apply again Lemma
5.7
to find an affine function
\overline{l}_{j}(x)=a_{j}+b_{j}\cdot x
with
|a_{j}|+|b_{j}|\leq L
such that

\sup_{x\in B_{r}}|u_{j}(x)-\overline{l}_{j}(x)|\leq\nu_{1}r.

(5.5)

Then let us justify the assertion (
5.4
) by an induction argument. For
j=0
, we take
u_{0}=u
. Assume (
5.4
) is true for
i=0,1,2,\dots,j-1
.
We shall prove the corresponding claim for the case
i=j
. We next distinguish two mutual exclusive situations. If
r|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}
, through the induction assumption and
2Lr^{\alpha}=\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{j}<1
, we obtain

\displaystyle|u_{j}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq(\nu_{j}r)^{-1}(|u_{j-1}(rx)|+|l_{j-1}(rx)|)

\displaystyle\leq(2Lr^{1+\alpha})^{-1}[\mu(1+|rx|^{1+\alpha})+L(1+|rx|)]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\mu}{2L}+\frac{2^{\alpha}\mu}{L}+2^{\alpha}+2^{\alpha-1}\right)|x|^{1+\alpha}=\widetilde{\mu}|x|^{1+\alpha}.

We verify (
5.4
) by choosing
\mu
such that
\widetilde{\mu}\leq\mu
. On the other hand, if
r|x|<\frac{1}{2}
, exploiting again
2Lr^{\alpha}=\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{j}<1
, we have

\displaystyle|u_{j}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq(\nu_{j}r)^{-1}(|u_{j-1}(rx)-h(rx)|+|h(rx)-l_{j-1}(rx)|)

\displaystyle\leq(\nu_{j}r)^{-1}\left(\frac{\nu_{1}r}{2}+Lr^{1+\alpha}|x|^{1+\alpha}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}|x|^{1+\alpha},

where
h\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}(B_{1})
is an
F
-harmonic function from Lemma
5.6
.
This completes the proof of (
5.4
).

By (
5.5
), rescaling back to
u
, we obtain

\sup_{x\in B_{r^{2}}}|u_{j-1}(x)-\overline{l}_{j-1}(x)-\nu_{j}r\overline{l}_{j}(r^{-1}x)|\leq\nu_{1}\nu_{j}r^{2}.

It follows that

\sup_{x\in B_{r^{3}}}|u_{j-2}(x)-\overline{l}_{j-2}(x)-\nu_{j-1}r\overline{l}_{j-1}(r^{-1}x)-\nu_{j-1}\nu_{j}r^{2}\overline{l}_{j}(r^{-2}x)|\leq\nu_{1}\nu_{j-1}\nu_{j}r^{3}

and, recursively,

\sup_{x\in B_{r^{j+1}}}|u(x)-l_{j+1}(x)|\leq\nu_{1}^{2}\nu_{2}\nu_{3}\cdots\nu_{j}r^{j+1}\leq\left(\prod^{j+1}_{i=1}\nu_{i}\right)r^{j+1}.

Here

l_{j+1}(x)=\overline{l}_{0}(x)+\sum^{j}_{i=1}\overline{l}_{i}(r^{-i}x)\biggl(\prod^{i}_{k=1}\nu_{k}\biggr)r^{i}=A_{j+1}+B_{j+1}\cdot x

with
\overline{l}_{i}(x)=A_{i}+B_{i}\cdot x
. Obviously, it holds that

|A_{j+1}-A_{j}|\leq C\left(\prod^{j}_{k=1}\nu_{k}\right)r^{j}\quad\text{and}\quad|B_{j+1}-B_{j}|\leq C\prod^{j}_{k=1}\nu_{k}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

To prove Theorem
1.5
, we first reduce the problem to a smallness regime in Proposition
5.8
, as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let

v(x)=\frac{u(rx)}{K}

with
0<r\leq 1
and
K\geq 1
to be chosen later. If
u
is a solvution to (
5.1
), then
v
is a solution to

-\overline{\gamma}(|Dv|)\overline{\mathcal{I}}(v,x)+\overline{H}(x,Dv)=\overline{f}(x)\quad\text{in }B_{1},

where
\overline{\mathcal{I}}
has the same uniform ellipticity property as the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{I}
in (
1.1
), and

\overline{\gamma}(|Dv|)=\frac{\gamma\left(\frac{K}{r}|Dv|\right)}{\gamma\left(\frac{K}{r}\right)},\quad\overline{f}(x)=\frac{\frac{r^{\sigma}}{K}}{\gamma\left(\frac{K}{r}\right)}f(rx)

and

\overline{H}(x,Dv)=\frac{\frac{r^{\sigma}}{K}}{\gamma\left(\frac{K}{r}\right)}H\left(rx,\frac{K}{r}Dv\right).

Since
\gamma(1)\geq 1
, we may conclude that

\displaystyle|\overline{H}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r^{\sigma}}{K}\gamma^{-1}\left(\frac{K}{r}\right)\mathcal{M}\left(1+\gamma\left(\frac{K}{r}|\xi|\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r^{\sigma}}{K}\mathcal{M}(1+\overline{\gamma}(|\xi|))=\overline{\mathcal{M}}(1+\overline{\gamma}(|\xi|)).

By choosing suitable numbers
r
and
K
, we will fall within a smallness regime.

By a similar argument as in the construction of the sequence
(\nu_{j})
, we conclude that
(A_{j})
and
(B_{j})
are Cauchy sequences.
Thus there exist
A_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
and
B_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
A_{j}\rightarrow A_{\infty}
and
B_{j}\rightarrow B_{\infty}
. Let

l_{\infty}=A_{\infty}+B_{\infty}\cdot x.

For all
0<\rho\ll 1
, there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
r^{j+1}<\rho\leq r^{j}
. Then we arrive at

\sup_{x\in B_{\rho}}|u(x)-l_{\infty}(x)|\leq C\omega(\rho)\rho,

where
\omega(t)
is a modulus of continuity. The details can be found in
[
2
, Proof of Theorem 1, Pages 31-32]

[
38
, Proof of Theorem 1]

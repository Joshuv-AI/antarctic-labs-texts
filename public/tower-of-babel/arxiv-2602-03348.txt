A Comparative Study of Low-Dissipation Numerical Schemes for Hyperbolic Conservation Laws
Shaoshuai Chu, Michael Herty

Abstract
This work provides a comparative assessment of several low-dissipation numerical schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws, highlighting their performance relative to the classical Harten–Lax–van Leer (HLL) schemes. The schemes under consideration include the classical Harten–Lax–van Leer–Contact (HLLC), the recently proposed TV flux splitting, the low-dissipation Central-Upwind (LDCU), and the local characteristic decomposition-based Central-Upwind (LCDCU) schemes. These methods are extended to higher orders of accuracy, up to the fifth order, within both finite-volume and finite-difference frameworks. A series of numerical experiments for the one- and two-dimensional Euler equations of gas dynamics are performed to evaluate the accuracy, robustness, and computational efficiency of the studied schemes. The comparison highlights the trade-offs between resolution of contact and shear waves, robustness in the presence of shocks, and computational cost. The investigated low-dissipation schemes show comparable levels of numerical dissipation, with only subtle differences appearing in selected benchmark problems. The results provide practical guidance for selecting efficient low-dissipation solvers for the simulation of complex compressible flows.



Keywords:
Hyperbolic conservation laws, HLL and HLLC solvers, TV flux splitting scheme, Low-dissipation Central-Upwind scheme, Local characteristic decomposition-based Central-Upwind scheme

Introduction

This paper focuses on the numerical solution of hyperbolic conservation laws, which in one and two spatial dimensions take the form

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}=\bm{0},

(1.1)

and

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}+\bm{G}(\bm{U})_{y}=\bm{0},

(1.2)

where
t
denotes time,
x
and
y
are spatial coordinates,
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the vector of conserved variables, and
\bm{F},\bm{G}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the flux functions in the
x
- and
y
-directions, respectively.

It is well-known that the systems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) may involve complex wave patterns, including shocks, rarefactions, and contact discontinuities, even when the initial data are very smooth, which presents significant challenges for numerical methods. Traditional approaches, particularly first-order methods, often fail to capture these discontinuities accurately and require extremely fine meshes to produce reliable results.

Since the pioneering works
[
20
,
44
,
24
]
, numerous methods have been developed to solve hyperbolic systems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
); for a detailed overview, see, e.g., the monographs and review papers
[
29
,
45
,
34
,
65
,
74
,
5
]
and the references therein. The Harten–Lax–van Leer (HLL) solver from
[
28
]
is one of the most widely used approximate Riemann solvers for hyperbolic conservation laws. It replaces the exact Riemann solution, which may involve multiple nonlinear waves, by a simple two-wave model. Specifically, the solution is approximated by a single intermediate state separated from the left and right data by two waves propagating with estimated minimum and maximum signal velocities. The HLL flux is robust and positivity preserving, but the main drawback is that intermediate waves such as contact and shear discontinuities are excessively smeared, since they are not explicitly represented in the two-wave model. To overcome this limitation, the Harten–Lax–van Leer–Contact (HLLC) solver was introduced in
[
72
]
, which extends the HLL framework by restoring the missing contact wave. The HLLC scheme thus consists of three waves, separating the solution into two intermediate states. The presence of the contact wave allows the solver to resolve contact discontinuities and shear layers more sharply, while maintaining the robustness and efficiency of the HLL scheme. Owing to this balance between accuracy and robustness, the HLLC flux has become one of the most popular approximate Riemann solvers for the compressible Euler equations and related hyperbolic systems.

Another important class of schemes is based on flux vector splitting. In the flux splitting methods, one can decompose fluxes into components corresponding to different wave families, such as shock, contact, and expansion waves, which makes them fundamental in computational fluid dynamics. This decomposition enhances the resolution of shock waves and discontinuities, which is crucial for accurate simulations of compressible flows; see, e.g.,
[
2
,
1
,
66
,
76
,
75
,
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
,
17
,
27
,
79
]
. However, the classical flux splitting schemes (e.g.,
[
2
,
1
,
66
,
76
,
75
]
) often struggle to resolve intermediate characteristic fields, leading to excessive dissipation or numerical artifacts, even though they are effective. To overcome these limitations, more advanced methods, such as the advection upstream splitting method (AUSM), have been developed (see, e.g.,
[
50
]
), offering better handling of contact waves and improved resolution of wave patterns. AUSM has since garnered considerable attention and undergone refinement, with further developments
[
51
,
52
,
53
]
. Additionally, a flux splitting approach similar to AUSM was proposed in
[
79
]
, and subsequent advancements have been recorded in works such as
[
17
,
57
,
27
,
35
]
, further enriching the field of computational fluid dynamics. Recently, a new flux splitting method, known as the TV splitting scheme for the one-dimensional (1-D) Euler equations of gas dynamics, was introduced in
[
73
]
and later extended to higher dimensions and orders in
[
70
,
10
]
. The proposed schemes are characterized by their simplicity, robustness, and accuracy, offering significant improvements over existing flux splitting methods. In particular, they effectively capture contact and shear waves while precisely preserving isolated stationary contacts. Beyond their applications to high-dimensional Euler equations, it has also been extended to other systems, including magnetohydrodynamics and shallow water equations; see, e.g.,
[
4
,
16
,
71
,
69
]
.

While flux splitting methods such as TV splitting focus on decomposing fluxes along characteristic fields, an alternative direction is offered by central schemes. These methods avoid explicit Riemann solvers altogether and instead compute fluxes through staggered or modified control volumes
[
58
,
46
,
56
,
48
,
31
,
3
]
. Central schemes are easy to implement and broadly applicable, but they tend to suffer from relatively large numerical dissipation. To overcome this drawback, central-upwind (CU) schemes were developed in
[
39
,
41
]
, in which control volumes are adapted to local wave speeds. CU schemes retain the simplicity of central methods while achieving significantly improved accuracy. Further refinements include anti-diffusion corrections
[
36
]
, dissipation switches
[
37
]
, and more accurate wave-speed estimates
[
23
]
. Recently, two refinements of CU schemes have been proposed to further reduce dissipation. Low-dissipation CU (LDCU) schemes
[
43
,
13
]
introduce a novel projection step based on subcell resolution, which sharply approximates contact waves while ensuring non-oscillatory behavior of the projected solution; see also, e.g.,
[
11
,
12
,
38
]
. This significantly decreases dissipation in contact- and shear-dominated regions. In parallel, LCD-based CU (LCDCU) schemes
[
6
]
apply local characteristic decomposition to the diffusion term, which also leads to substantially improved resolution by further suppressing excessive numerical dissipation; see also, e.g.,
[
9
,
15
]
.

As mentioned above, the HLLC, TV splitting, LDCU, and LCDCU schemes are low-dissipation numerical schemes for solving the hyperbolic conservation laws (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), and it is instructive to check the dissipations contained in these schemes by comparing the resolution of the computed numerical results in a series of benchmarks.
To this end, we carry out a systematic comparative study of the first-, second-, third-, and fifth-order HLL, HLLC, TV splitting, LCDCU, and LDCU schemes, focusing on their robustness, accuracy, and efficiency in resolving complex wave interactions in hyperbolic conservation laws. The second-order extension employs a piecewise linear interpolant for reconstructing one-sided point values, while the third- and fifth-order schemes are formulated within the finite-difference (FD) A-WENO framework. This framework effectively generalizes low-order finite-volume (FV) schemes to higher-order FD ones, especially in multidimensional settings, through its simple dimension-by-dimension reconstruction; see, e.g.,
[
55
,
33
,
32
]
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In §
2
, we begin with a brief overview of the first-order HLL scheme for the 1-D Euler equations of gas dynamics and then extend it to second-, third-, and fifth-order accuracy. The corresponding HLLC, TV, LDCU, and LCDCU schemes are subsequently reviewed. In §
3
, we describe the two-dimensional (2-D) extensions of these schemes in a dimension-by-dimension manner. Finally, in §
4
, we present a number of 1-D and 2-D numerical results to compare their performance.

2
One-Dimensional Schemes

In this section, we consider the 1-D Euler equations of gas dynamics and briefly review the first-, second-, third-, and fifth-order 1-D HLL, HLLC, TV, LDCU, and LCDCU schemes, respectively. The 1-D
Euler equations of gas dynamics read as (
1.1
) with

\bm{U}:=(\rho,\rho u,E)^{\top},\quad{\rm and}\quad\bm{F}(\bm{U})=(\rho u,\rho u^{2}+p,u(E+p))^{\top},

(2.1)

where
\rho
,
u
,
p
, and
E
are the density, velocity, pressure, and total energy, respectively. The system is completed through the following equations of state (EOS):

p=(\gamma-1)\Big[E-{\frac{1}{2}}\rho u^{2}\Big],

(2.2)

where the parameter
\gamma
represents the specific heat ratio.

2.1
One-Dimensional HLL Schemes

We first briefly review the 1-D HLL scheme from
[
28
]
and show its high-order extensions.

2.1.1
One-Dimensional First-Order HLL Scheme

Assume the computational domain is covered with uniform cells
C_{j}:=[x_{j-\frac{1}{2}},x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}]
with
x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\Delta x
centered at
x_{j}=(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+x_{j+\frac{1}{2}})/2
,
\,j=1,\ldots,N
, and the cell average values

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}(t):\approx\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int\limits_{C_{j}}\bm{U}(x,t)\,{\rm d}x

are available at a certain time level
t
. The computed cell averages
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}
of the 1-D system (
1.1
) are evolved in time by solving the following semi-discrete system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs):

\frac{{\rm d}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x},

(2.3)

where
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)
is the numerical flux, defined by

\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})=\begin{cases}\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}),&a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0,\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})+a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})}{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}},&a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\leq 0\leq a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\\[12.0pt]
\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}),&a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\leq 0,\end{cases}

(2.4)

where
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the left/right-sided point values of
\bm{U}
at the cell interfaces
x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
. For the first order scheme, we take
\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{U}_{j+1}
and
\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{U}_{j}
. The one-sided local speeds of propagation
a^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
can be estimated by

a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\max\Big\{u^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+c^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},u^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+c^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big\},\quad a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\min\Big\{u^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-c^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},u^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-c^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big\},

(2.5)

where

u^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{(\rho u)_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{\pm}}{\rho^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}},\quad p^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=(\gamma-1)\Big[E^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(u^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}\Big],\quad c^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma p^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{\rho^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}}.

2.1.2
One-Dimensional Second-Order HLL Scheme

We now extend the first-order HLL scheme introduced in §
2.1
to the second order of accuracy. The resulting scheme (
2.3
)–(
2.5
) achieves second-order accuracy provided that the one-sided point values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, used to compute the numerical flux
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, are second-order accurate. To this end, we approximate
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
using a piecewise linear interpolant

\widetilde{\bm{U}}(x)=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+(\bm{U}_{x})_{j}(x-x_{j}),\quad x\in C_{j},

(2.6)

which leads to

\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{U}_{x})_{j},\quad\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}-\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{U}_{x})_{j+1}.

(2.7)

In order to ensure the reconstruction (
2.6
)–(
2.7
) is non-oscillatory, one needs to compute the slopes
(\bm{U}_{x})_{j}
in (
2.6
) with the help of a
nonlinear limiter. In all of the numerical experiments reported in §
4
, we have used a generalized minmod limiter

[
49
,
58
,
67
]
:

(\bm{U}_{x})_{j}={\rm minmod}\left(\theta\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-1}}{\Delta x},\,\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-1}}{2\Delta x},\,\theta\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}}{\Delta x}\right),\quad\theta\in[1,2],

(2.8)

applied in a component-wise manner. Here, the minmod function is defined as

{\rm minmod}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots):=\begin{cases}\min_{j}\{z_{j}\}&\mbox{if}~z_{j}>0\quad\forall\,j,\\
\max_{j}\{z_{j}\}&\mbox{if}~z_{j}<0\quad\forall\,j,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.9)

The parameter
\theta
in (
2.5
) is used to control the amount of numerical viscosity present in the resulting scheme, and larger values of
\theta
correspond to sharper but, in general, more oscillatory reconstructions. In this paper, we use
\theta=1.3
.

2.1.3
One-Dimensional Third-Order HLL Scheme

In this section, we extend the HLL scheme to the third-order accuracy in the framework of the FD A-WENO scheme introduced in
[
33
]
(see also
[
55
]
), which has been proven to be a powerful tool for generalizing low-order FV schemes to higher-order FD ones.

Following
[
33
]
, the point values
\bm{U}_{j}
are evolved in time by solving the following system of ODEs:

\frac{{\rm d}\bm{U}_{j}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{{\bm{H}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}-{\bm{H}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}}}{\Delta x},

(2.10)

where
{\bm{H}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}
is the (third-order accurate) numerical flux defined by

{\bm{H}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}=\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{24}(\Delta x)^{2}(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}.

Here,
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is the FV numerical flux as in (
2.4
) and
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is the higher-order correction term used to increase the order of the numerical flux. The correction term
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
can be approximated with the help of the FV numerical fluxes
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
:

(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{(\Delta x)^{2}}\big[\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}-2\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2}}\big],

which has been proved to be more efficient than the old version

(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta x)^{2}}\big[{\bm{F}}_{j-1}-{\bm{F}}_{j}-{\bm{F}}_{j+1}+{\bm{F}}_{j+2}\big],

where
\bm{F}_{j}=\bm{F}(\bm{U}_{j})
, while affecting neither the accuracy nor the quality of resolution. The resulting scheme is third-order once the one-sided point values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
employed to compute the numerical flux
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are third-order accurate. This can be done by implementing a certain nonlinear limiting procedure like the third-order WENO-type interpolation (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
21
,
22
,
47
]
) applied to the local characteristic variables; see
[
10
, Appendix A]
for a detailed explanation.

2.1.4
One-Dimensional Fifth-Order HLL Scheme

According to
[
33
]
, to achieve fifth-order accuracy, the point values
\bm{U}_{j}
are evolved in time by solving the system (
2.10
) with the (fifth-order accurate) numerical flux

{\bm{H}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}=\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{24}(\Delta x)^{2}(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{7}{5760}(\Delta x)^{4}(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},

where
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is the FV numerical flux as in (
2.4
),
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the higher-order correction terms computed by the fourth- and second-order accurate FDs, respectively; see, e.g.,
[
14
,
7
]
. Here, we have used the following
higher-order correction terms from
[
14
]
:

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{12(\Delta x)^{2}}\Big[-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2}}+16\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}-30\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+16\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2}}-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2}}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{(\Delta x)^{4}}\Big[\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2}}-4\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+6\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-4\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2}}+\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2}}\Big],

which is more efficient than the old version:

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{48(\Delta x)^{2}}\Big[-5\bm{F}_{j-2}+39\bm{F}_{j-1}-34\bm{F}_{j}-34\bm{F}_{j+1}+39\bm{F}_{j+2}-5\bm{F}_{j+3}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta x)^{4}}\Big[\bm{F}_{j-2}-3\bm{F}_{j-1}+2\bm{F}_{j}+2\bm{F}_{j+1}-3\bm{F}_{j+2}+\bm{F}_{j+3}\Big].

In order to ensure the resulting scheme is fifth order, the one-sided point values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
employed to compute the numerical flux
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
need to be at least fifth-order accurate. This can be done by using a certain nonlinear limiting procedure like the fifth-order WENO-Z interpolation from
[
33
,
55
,
77
]
applied to the local characteristic variables; see
[
10
, Appendix B]
for details.

2.2
One-Dimensional HLLC Schemes

According to
[
72
]
, the 1-D first-, second-, third-, and fifth-order HLLC schemes can be obtained similar to the corresponding HLL schemes by replacing the numerical fluxes
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
in (
2.4
) by

\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})=\begin{cases}\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}),&a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0,\\[8.0pt]
\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})+a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{*,-}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),&a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\leq 0\leq a^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\\[8.0pt]
\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})+a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{*,+}-\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),&a^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\leq 0\leq a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\\[8.0pt]
\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}),&a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\leq 0,\end{cases}

where

\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{*,\pm}=\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{a^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-u^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{a^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}},

and

a^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{p^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-p^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+\rho^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}u^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-u^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})-\rho^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}u^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-u^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})}{\rho^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-u^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})-\rho^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-u^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})}.

2.3
One-Dimensional TV Splitting Schemes

According to
[
73
,
10
]
, the 1-D first-, second-, third-, and fifth-order TV schemes can be obtained by replacing the numerical fluxes
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
in (
2.4
) by

\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)=\bm{{\cal F}}^{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)+\bm{{\cal F}}^{P}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big).

Here,
\bm{{\cal F}}^{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)
is the advection flux given by

\bm{{\cal F}}^{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)=\begin{cases}u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\rho^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\\[3.44444pt]
(\rho u)^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\\[3.44444pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\rho^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(u^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\end{pmatrix},&\mbox{if }u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0,\\[34.44434pt]
u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\rho^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\\[3.44444pt]
(\rho u)^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\\[3.44444pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\rho^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(u^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\end{pmatrix},&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

and
\bm{{\cal F}}^{P}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)
is the pressure flux given by

\bm{{\cal F}}^{P}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\[3.44444pt]
p^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\\[7.74998pt]
\dfrac{\gamma u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}p^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}\end{pmatrix},

where

\displaystyle u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}u^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-C^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}u^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{C^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-C^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{2}{C^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-C^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}(p^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-p^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}),

\displaystyle p^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}p^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-C^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}p^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{C^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-C^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{C^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}C^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{2(C^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-C^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})}(u^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-u^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}),

\displaystyle C^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\rho^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(u^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\pm\sqrt{\big(u^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}+4\big(c^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}}\bigg).

2.4
One-Dimensional LDCU Schemes

According to
[
43
,
13
,
11
]
, the 1-D first-, second-, third-, and fifth-order LDCU schemes can be obtained by replacing the numerical fluxes
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
in (
2.4
) by

\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)=\frac{{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\bm{F}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)-{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}}\bm{F}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)}{{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}-{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}}}+\frac{{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}}}{{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}-{a_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-}}}\Big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)+\bm{q}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},

and the anti-diffusion term
\bm{q}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is given by (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
)

\bm{q}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\alpha^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}q^{\rho}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\left(1,u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}\right)^{\top}.

Here,

q^{\rho}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm minmod}\Big(\big(u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big(\rho^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\rho^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),\big(a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big(\rho^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\rho^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Big),

u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=(\rho u)^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}/\rho^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
with
\rho^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
(\rho u)^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
being the first and second components of

\bm{U}^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\Big[\bm{F}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)-\bm{F}\big(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Big]}{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}},

\alpha^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}&&\mbox{if}~u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}<0,\\
&\frac{a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}{a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-u^{*}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}&&\mbox{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

and the minmod function is defined by
{\rm minmod}(a,b):=\frac{{\rm sgn}(a)+{\rm sgn}(b)}{2}\min(|a|,|b|)
.

Finally, the one-sided local speeds of propagation
a^{\pm}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are slightly different from (
2.5
), and are defined by

a^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\max\Big\{u^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+c^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},u^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+c^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},0\Big\},\quad a^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\min\Big\{u^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-c^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},u^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-c^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},0\Big\}.

(2.11)

2.5
One-Dimensional LCDCU Schemes

According to
[
6
,
7
]
, the 1-D first-, second-, third-, and fifth-order LCDCU schemes can be obtained by replacing the numerical fluxes
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
in (
2.4
) by

\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}^{+}\big)=R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{F}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\bm{F}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big).

The diagonal matrices
P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
, and
Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are given by

\displaystyle P^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((P^{\rm LCD}_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(P^{\rm LCD}_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),\quad M^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((M^{\rm LCD}_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(M^{\rm LCD}_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big),

\displaystyle Q^{\rm LCD}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}={\rm diag}\big((Q^{\rm LCD}_{1})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\ldots,(Q^{\rm LCD}_{d})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)

with

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\big((P^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(M^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(Q^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big((\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}},(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\Big)&&\mbox{if}~(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}>\varepsilon_{0},\\[2.15277pt]
&\Big({\frac{1}{2}},{\frac{1}{2}},0\Big)&&\mbox{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

where

\begin{aligned} &(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\max\left\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,0\right\},\\
&(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\min\left\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\big),\,0\right\},\end{aligned}\quad i=1,\ldots,d.

Here,
A(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{F}}{\partial\bm{U}}(\bm{U})
is the Jacobian,
\lambda_{1}(A(\bm{U}))\leq\ldots\lambda_{d}(A(\bm{U}))
are its eigenvalues, and the matrices
R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
are such that

R^{-1}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is diagonal, where
\widehat{A}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=A(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})
and
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}
is either a simple
average
(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1})/2
or another type of average of the
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j}
and
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1}
states. Finally,
\varepsilon_{0}
is a very small desingularization constant, which is taken
\varepsilon_{0}=10^{-12}
in all of the
numerical examples reported in §
4
.

3
Two-Dimensional Schemes

We now present a 2-D overview of the HLL, HLLC, TV, LDCU, and LCDCU schemes for the 2-D Euler equations of gas dynamics, which read as

\bm{U}_{t}+\bm{F}(\bm{U})_{x}+\bm{G}(\bm{U})_{y}=\bm{0},

(3.1)

with
\bm{U}:=(\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E)^{\top}
,
\bm{F}(\bm{U})=(\rho u,\rho u^{2}+p,\rho uv,u(E+p))^{\top}
, and
\bm{G}(\bm{U})=(\rho v,\rho uv,\rho v^{2}+p,v(E+p))^{\top}
. Here,
v
is the
y
-velocity and the rest of the notation is the same as in the 1-D case (
2.1
)–(
2.2
). The system is completed through the following
EOS:

p=(\gamma-1)\Big[E-\frac{\rho}{2}(u^{2}+v^{2})\Big].

(3.2)

3.1
Two-Dimensional HLL Schemes

As in the 1-D case, we first briefly review the first-order 2-D HLL scheme from
[
28
]
and show its high-order extensions.

3.1.1
Two-Dimensional First-Order HLL Scheme

Supposing that the computational domain is covered with uniform cells
C_{j,\,k}:=[x_{j-\frac{1}{2}},x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}]\times[y_{k-\frac{1}{2}},y_{k+\frac{1}{2}}]
centered at

(x_{j},y_{k})=\big((x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+x_{j+\frac{1}{2}})/2
,
(y_{k+\frac{1}{2}}+y_{k-\frac{1}{2}})/2\big)
with
x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\Delta x
and
y_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-y_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\Delta y
for all
j,k
, we
assume that the cell averages

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}(t):\approx\frac{1}{\Delta x\Delta y}\int\limits_{C_{j,k}}\bm{U}(x,y,t)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y

are available at a certain time level
t
. The cell averages
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}
are then evolved in time by numerically solving the following system of ODEs:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}}{\Delta x}-\frac{\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}}{\Delta y}.

(3.3)

Here,
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big(\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-},\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big)
and
\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\big)
are the numerical fluxes, defined by

\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})=\begin{cases}\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}),&a^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\geq 0,\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{a^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})-a^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})+a^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}a^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})}{a^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-a^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}},&a^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\leq 0\leq a^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\\[12.0pt]
\bm{F}(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}),&a^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\leq 0,\end{cases}

(3.4)

\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})=\begin{cases}\bm{G}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}),&b^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\geq 0,\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{b^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{G}(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})-b^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{G}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})+b^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}b^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})}{b^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-b^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}},&b^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\leq 0\leq b^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\\[12.0pt]
\bm{G}(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}),&b^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\leq 0,\end{cases}

(3.5)

where
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are the left/right-sided point values of
\bm{U}
at the cell interfaces
(x_{j+\frac{1}{2}},y_{k})
and
(x_{j},y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})
, respectively. In the first-order scheme, we take
\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\bm{U}_{j+1,k}
,
\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\bm{U}_{j,k+1}
, and
\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\bm{U}_{j,k}
.

Here, the one-sided local speeds of propagation
a^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
a^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
can be estimated by

\begin{aligned} &a^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\max\Big\{u^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+c^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},u^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+c^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\Big\},~\,a^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\min\Big\{u^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-c^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},u^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-c^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\Big\},\\
&b^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\max\Big\{v^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+c^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},v^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+c^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big\},\quad b^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\min\Big\{v^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-c^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},v^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-c^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big\},\\
\end{aligned}

(3.6)

where

\displaystyle u^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{(\rho u)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}}{\rho^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}},\quad u^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{(\rho u)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}}{\rho^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}},\quad v^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{(\rho v)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}}{\rho^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}},\quad v^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{(\rho v)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}}{\rho^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}},

\displaystyle p^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=(\gamma-1)\Big[E^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\Big(\big(u^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big)^{2}+\big(v^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big)^{2}\Big)\Big],\quad c^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma p^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}{\rho^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}},

\displaystyle p^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=(\gamma-1)\Big[E^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(\big(u^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big)^{2}+\big(v^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big)^{2}\Big)\Big],\quad c^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma p^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}{\rho^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}}.

3.1.2
Two-Dimensional Second-Order HLL Scheme

As in the 1-D case, the resulting scheme (
3.3
)–(
3.6
) is second-order accurate once the one-sided point values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
employed to compute the numerical fluxes (
3.4
)–(
3.5
) are second order. To this end, we approximate
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
by

\widetilde{\bm{U}}(x,y)=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+(\bm{U}_{x})_{j,k}(x-x_{j})+(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k}(y-y_{k}),\quad x\in C_{j,k},

which leads to

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{U}_{x})_{j,k},\quad\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1,k}-\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{U}_{x})_{j+1,k},

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+\frac{\Delta y}{2}(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k},\quad\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k+1}-\frac{\Delta y}{2}(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k+1},

where

\displaystyle(\bm{U}_{x})_{j,k}={\rm minmod}\left(\theta\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-1,k}}{\Delta x},\,\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1,k}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j-1,k}}{2\Delta x},\,\theta\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1,k}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}}{\Delta x}\right),

\displaystyle(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k}={\rm minmod}\left(\theta\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k-1}}{\Delta y},\,\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k+1}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k-1}}{2\Delta y},\,\theta\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k+1}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}}{\Delta y}\right).

Here, the minmod function is defined by (
2.9
).

3.1.3
Two-Dimensional Third-Order HLL Scheme

Following
[
33
]
, the point values
\bm{U}_{j,k}
are evolved in time by solving the following system of ODEs:

\frac{{\rm d}\bm{U}_{j,k}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{{\bm{H}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}-{\bm{H}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}}}{\Delta x}-\frac{{\bm{H}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}-{\bm{H}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}}}{\Delta y},

(3.7)

where the numerical fluxes
\bm{H}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{H}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are defined by

{\bm{H}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}=\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\frac{1}{24}(\Delta x)^{2}(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\quad{\bm{H}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}=\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{1}{24}(\Delta y)^{2}(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Here,
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
{\bm{G}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}
are the FV numerical fluxes as in (
3.4
)–(
3.5
),
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are the higher-order correction terms computed by the numerical fluxes

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\Delta x)^{2}}\big[\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}-2\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}\big],

(3.8)

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\Delta y)^{2}}\big[\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}-2\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}\big],

or the standard central FDs:

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta x)^{2}}\big[\bm{F}_{j-1,k}-\bm{F}_{j,k}-\bm{F}_{j+1,k}+\bm{F}_{j+2,k}\big],

(3.9)

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta y)^{2}}\big[\bm{G}_{j,k-1}-\bm{G}_{j,k}-\bm{G}_{j,k+1}+\bm{G}_{j,k+2}\big].

As in the 1-D case, using (
3.8
) is more efficient than (
3.9

3.1.4
Two-Dimensional Fifth-Order HLL Scheme

According to
[
33
]
, the point values
\bm{U}_{j,k}
are evolved in time by solving the system of ODEs (
3.7
) with the following numerical fluxes
{\bm{H}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}
and
{\bm{H}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}
:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{H}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}=\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\frac{1}{24}(\Delta x)^{2}(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+\frac{7}{5760}(\Delta x)^{4}(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{H}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}=\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{1}{24}(\Delta y)^{2}(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{7}{5760}(\Delta y)^{4}(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Here,
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
{\bm{G}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}
are the FV numerical fluxes as in (
3.4
)–(
3.5
),
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,

(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
,
(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are approximations of the second- and fourth-order spatial derivatives of
\bm{F}
at
(x,y)=(x_{j+\frac{1}{2}},y_{k})
and
\bm{G}
at
(x,y)=(x_{j},y_{k+\frac{1}{2}})
, respectively. In this paper, we have used the following higher-order correction terms:

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{12(\Delta x)^{2}}\Big[-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}+16\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}-30\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+16\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}\Big],

(3.10)

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{(\Delta x)^{4}}\Big[\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}-4\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}+6\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-4\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}+\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{12(\Delta y)^{2}}\Big[-\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k-\frac{3}{2}}+16\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}-30\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+16\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}-\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{5}{2}}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{(\Delta y)^{4}}\Big[\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k-\frac{3}{2}}-4\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}+6\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-4\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}+\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{5}{2}}\Big].

One can also compute the high-order correction terms using the standard FDs:

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{48(\Delta x)^{2}}\left(-5\bm{F}_{j-2,k}+39\bm{F}_{j-1,k}-34\bm{F}_{j,k}-34\bm{F}_{j+1,k}+39\bm{F}_{j+2,k}-5\bm{F}_{j+3,k}\right),

(3.11)

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta x)^{4}}\left(\bm{F}_{j-2,k}-3\bm{F}_{j-1,k}+2\bm{F}_{j,k}+2\bm{F}_{j+1,k}-3\bm{F}_{j+2,k}+\bm{F}_{j+3,k}\right),

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{48(\Delta y)^{2}}\left(-5\bm{G}_{j,k-2}+39\bm{G}_{j,k-1}-34\bm{G}_{j,k}-34\bm{G}_{j,k+1}+39\bm{G}_{j,k+2}-5\bm{G}_{j,k+3}\right),

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{2(\Delta y)^{4}}\left(\bm{G}_{j,k-2}-3\bm{G}_{j,k-1}+2\bm{G}_{j,k}+2\bm{G}_{j,k+1}-3\bm{G}_{j,k+2}+\bm{G}_{j,k+3}\right),

where
\bm{F}_{j,k}:=\bm{F}(\bm{U}_{j,k})
and
\bm{G}_{j,k}:=\bm{G}(\bm{U}_{j,k})
. As in the 1-D case, using (
3.10
) is more efficient than (
3.11
); see, e.g.,
[
14
]
. To achieve fifth-order accuracy, the one-sided point values

and

employed to compute the numerical flux

and

need to be at least fifth-order accurate. This can also be done in a “dimension-by-dimension” manner as in the 1-D case, we therefore omit the details for the sake of brevity.

\displaystyle q^{\rho}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm minmod}\left(-b^{*,-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\rho^{*}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\rho^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big),b^{*,+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\rho^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\rho^{*}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big)\right),

and

\alpha^{*}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{b^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}{b^{*,+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}&&\mbox{if}~v^{*}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}<0,\\

Two-Dimensional LCDCU Schemes

According to
[
6
,
7
]
, the 2-D first-, second-, third-, and fifth-order LCDCU schemes can be obtained by replacing the numerical fluxes
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
in (
3.4
)–(
3.5
) by

\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big(\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-},\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big)=R_{j+\frac{1}{2}}P^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\bm{F}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}M^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\bm{F}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}Q^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big),

\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\big)=R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}P^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{G}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}M^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\bm{G}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}Q^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big).

The matrices
R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
,
R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are the matrices such that
R^{-1}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\widehat{A}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}R_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
R^{-1}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\widehat{B}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}R_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are diagonal. Here,
\widehat{A}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=A(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})
,
\widehat{B}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=B(\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})
with
A(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{F}(\bm{U})}{\partial\bm{U}}
,
B(\bm{U})=\frac{\partial\bm{G}(\bm{U})}{\partial\bm{U}}
, and
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
\widehat{\bm{U}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are either simple averages
(\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1,k})/2
,
(\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k+1})/2
or another type of averages of
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}
,
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j+1,k}
and
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k}
,
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{j,k+1}
states, respectively. The diagonal matrices
P^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
Q^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,

P^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
,
M^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and
Q^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are defined by

\displaystyle P^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}={\rm diag}\left(\big(P^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\ldots,\big(P^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\right),

\displaystyle P^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm diag}\left(\big(P^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\ldots,\big(P^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle M^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}={\rm diag}\left(\big(M^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\ldots,\big(M^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\right),

\displaystyle M^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm diag}\left(\big(M^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\ldots,\big(M^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle Q^{\rm LCD}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}={\rm diag}\left(\big(Q^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\ldots,\big(Q^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\right),

\displaystyle Q^{\rm LCD}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm diag}\left(\big(Q^{\rm LCD}_{1}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\ldots,\big(Q^{\rm LCD}_{d}\big)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

where

\displaystyle\left((P^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},(M^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},(Q^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\Delta(\lambda_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}\left((\lambda^{+}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\right)&&\mbox{if}~\Delta(\lambda_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}>\varepsilon_{0},\\
&(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},0)&&\mbox{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\left((P^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},(M^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},(Q^{\rm LCD}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\Delta(\mu_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}\left((\mu^{+}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},-(\mu^{-}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},(\mu^{+}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}(\mu^{-}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)&&\mbox{if}~\Delta(\mu_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}>\varepsilon_{0},\\
&(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},0)&&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here,
\Delta(\lambda_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}:=(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,

\Delta(\mu_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}:=(\mu^{+}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-(\mu^{-}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and

\displaystyle(\lambda^{+}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\max\big\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big),\,0\big\},

\displaystyle(\lambda^{-}_{i})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\min\big\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k})\big),\,0\big\},

\displaystyle(\mu^{+}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\max\big\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big),\,0\big\},

\displaystyle(\mu^{-}_{i})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\min\big\{\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{-}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big),\,\lambda_{i}\big(A(\bm{U}^{+}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}})\big),\,0\big\},

where
\lambda_{i}
and
\mu_{i}
are the eigenvalues of the Jacobians
A(\bm{U})
and
B(\bm{U})
:
\lambda_{1}(A)\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{d}(A)
and

\mu_{1}(B)\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{d}(B)
, respectively.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we have carried out a systematic comparative study of several low-dissipation numerical schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws, including the HLLC and three recently proposed low-dissipation schemes — TV flux splitting, LDCU, and LCDCU schemes. All of these schemes were extended to higher orders of accuracy, up to the fifth order, within both finite-volume and finite-difference frameworks. Through a series of one- and two-dimensional numerical experiments for the Euler equations of gas dynamics, we have examined their accuracy, robustness, and computational efficiency. Our results indicate that all of the studied low-dissipation schemes exhibit a comparable level of numerical dissipation, with only minor differences observed in specific test cases. The transition from first- to second-order accuracy yields the most significant improvement in resolution, whereas higher-order versions (third- and fifth-order) further enhance accuracy and efficiency for complex multidimensional flows. One can see that these low-dissipation schemes achieve better resolution than HLL counterparts near the contact waves, and among the four low-dissipation schemes, TV splitting
schemes exhibit slightly higher dissipation in several examples, and fail in the Riemann problem — Configuration 3. While the LCDCU schemes are slightly more computationally expensive than the other three low-dissipation schemes, compared with the HLLC, TV, and LDCU schemes, they can be directly applied to other systems since they only need to compute the corresponding eigenvectors based on the systems on hand. Overall, this study confirms that each of the considered solvers represents a viable and efficient option for the simulation of compressible flows. The comparative findings provide practical guidance for the choice of low-dissipation schemes in applications requiring both accuracy and robustness.

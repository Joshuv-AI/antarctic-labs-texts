Upregulated lncRNA AK058003 in Tumor-Educated Platelets and Plasma as Liquid Biopsy Biomarkers for Esophageal Cancer
Authors: Xiuqi Wei, Honghua Yan, Yi Zeng, Kaidong Zhu, Teng Li, Junli Fan, Hui Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeEsophageal cancer (EC) ranks thirteenth in incidence and seventh in cancer-related mortality worldwide in 2024, with an estimated 494,000 new cases and 442,000 deaths, and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) remains the predominant histological subtype in China. Endoscopic biopsy and serum markers (SCCA, CA 19–9, CEA) have limited sensitivity or are invasive. This study evaluated the diagnostic potential of platelet and plasma lncRNA AK058003 in EC.Patients and MethodsPlatelet and plasma lncRNA AK058003 expression was quantified by quantitative real-time PCR in 64 EC patients and 64 healthy controls. Diagnostic performance was assessed by ROC curve analysis. Correlations with clinicopathological parameters were analyzed.ResultsTumor-educated platelet (TEP) and plasma lncRNA AK058003 were significantly upregulated in EC patients (P = 0.0042 and P < 0.0001, respectively). Plasma lncRNA AK058003 showed an AUC of 0.83 (sensitivity 82.8%, specificity 76.6%), whereas TEP lncRNA AK058003 yielded an AUC of 0.65 (sensitivity 64.1%, specificity 60.9%). TEP lncRNA AK058003 expression was significantly higher in T3–T4 than T1–T2 stages (P = 0.0025) and correlated with tumor size (P = 0.0017).ConclusionBoth TEP and plasma lncRNA AK058003 were significantly upregulated in EC. Plasma lncRNA AK058003 demonstrated promising diagnostic performance (AUC 0.83, sensitivity 82.8%, specificity 76.6%), while TEP lncRNA AK058003 overexpression correlated with tumor size, supporting their potential as non-invasive liquid-biopsy biomarkers for EC diagnosis.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) is one of the most prevalent tumors of the digestive tract worldwide, ranking thirteenth in incidence and seventh in mortality worldwide in 2024, with an estimated 494,000 new cases and 442,000 deaths, according to the most recent Global Cancer Statistics.1 The two most common histological subtypes of EC are esophageal adenocarcinoma (EADC) and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC),2 with ESCC accounting for more than 90% of cases in China.3 Early diagnosis is crucial, as it can significantly reduce or delay mortality associated with ESCC.4 Currently, endoscopic biopsy, serum squamous cell carcinoma antigen (SCCA), carbohydrate antigen (CA 19–9), and carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) testing are the most commonly used methods for screening EC.5,6 However, endoscopic biopsy is an invasive procedure that poses risks to patients, while serum markers such as SCCA, CA 19–9 and CEA are relatively insensitive and inspective. Consequently, the development of novel, effective, and noninvasive biomarkers for the diagnosis of EC is urgently needed.

Platelets are anucleate, discoid cells, approximately 2–3 μm in diameter that are produced and mature in megakaryocytes.7 They play crucial roles in hemostasis, diverse thrombotic disorders, angiogenesis and innate immunity.8 Additionally, platelets are believed to be involved in a range of processes associated with numerous diseases.9 Despite being anucleate, platelets contain a diverse array of RNA molecules that enable them to perform specific functions.10 Recent studies have shown that cancer cells can modify the RNA content of platelets, leading to a phenomenon known as “tumor-mediated education” of platelets. Alterations in the RNA profile of these tumor-educated platelets (TEPs) have been identified as promising novel sources of potential cancer biomarkers, which could aid in early diagnosis and treatment monitoring.11

Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) are RNA molecules longer than 200 nucleotides that do not encode proteins.12 They are emerging as key regulators of various cellular processes13 and are implicated in multiple diseases, including cardiovascular diseases, genetic disorders, and particularly cancer.14,15 Accumulating evidence suggests that lncRNAs can affect cancer initiation, progression, and metastasis by modulating oncogenic and tumor-suppressing pathways and may offer new targets for their diagnosis, therapy and prognosis.16

Several altered lncRNAs have been reported in EC. For example, lncRNA CASC11 expression was remarkably up-regulated and was associated with the pathological stage and poor prognosis of EC,17 lncRNA HOXA10-AS played a tumor promoter role in EC cells and tumors,18 lncRNA Rpph1 was highly expressed in EC and was associate with radio-sensitivity,19 LINC00467 acted as an oncogene in ESCC by accelerating cell proliferation and preventing cell apoptosis via miR-485-5p/DPAGT1 axis,20 lncRNA BBOX1-AS1 suppressed cell apoptosis and promoted cell proliferation in EC, and might be a potential therapeutic target for EC therapy,21 LOC440173 exerted a promotive role in ESCC tumorigenesis and might be a new therapeutic target for the treatment of ESCC,22 lncRNA DDX11AS1 promoted the progression of ESCC through miR-514b-3p/RBX1 axis,23 lncRNA AK058003 was highly expressed in EC patients and brought poor prognosis.24 Al the studies on the lncRNAs mentioned were conducted in EC tissues or EC cells, and their expression in platelets or plasma have not yet been investigated. In our study, we investigated several lncRNAs in TEP and plasma, and we found that lncRNA AK058003 was significantly altered in EC patients, demonstrating high specificity and sensitivity for diagnosing EC. The objective of the present study was to evaluate the diagnostic performance of platelet and plasma lncRNA AK058003 for distinguishing esophageal cancer patients from healthy controls, and to assess its associations with clinicopathological parameters.

Materials and Methods

Sample Collection

Consecutive newly diagnosed esophageal cancer patients presenting to the Cancer Center of Union Hospital between April 2023 and August 2024 were screened; 64 patients met the inclusion criteria (newly diagnosed, age 18–85, no prior surgery/chemoradiotherapy, no other primary tumor) and were included. Of these 64 EC patients, 61 had esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and 3 had esophageal adenocarcinoma (EADC). TNM stage was documented for 43 of the 64 EC patients; complete staging could not be established for the remaining 21 patients due to incomplete clinical workup and patient referral patterns, as the treating physicians did not have sufficient pretreatment imaging or pathological documentation to assign a definitive TNM stage.

Platelet Isolation

Three milliliters of whole blood samples were collected from EC patients and healthy donors into test tubes containing EDTA-K2. The whole blood samples were centrifuged at 120 g for 20 minutes at room temperature, and the supernatant was collected as platelet-rich plasma (PRP). The PRP was transferred to another centrifuge tube and centrifuged at 360 g for 20 minutes at room temperature, separated the supernatant (plasma) from the precipitate (platelets). Next, 500 µL of plasma was pipetted from the supernatant into a new centrifuge tube, and the remaining plasma was discarded. The platelets were washed twice with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). A high-purity platelet preparation was confirmed by a ratio of < 5 karyocytes per 107 platelets.25 After centrifugation, 500 mL of RNA lysate, a reagent that lyses cells and inactivates RNase included in a Liquid Total RNA Isolation Kit (RP4002, BioTeke, Beijing, China), was added to both the platelets and the plasma. The samples were mixed thoroughly for 5 minutes and then stored in a −80°C freezer in preparation for RNA extraction and amplification. Additionally, the clinical and histopathological features of each participant were recorded in detail.

RNA Extraction and Relative Real-Time Quantitative PCR (qPCR) Analysis

Total RNA was extracted from isolated platelets or blood plasma via a Liquid Total RNA Isolation Kit (RP4002, BioTeke, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA purity was assessed by absorbance at 260/280 nm using a Nanodrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA); all samples showed A260/A280 ratios between 1.8 and 2.0, indicating acceptable RNA purity. The RNA concentration ranged from approximately 35 to 255 ng/μL across samples, with a median of approximately 130 ng/μL. RNA integrity was further confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis, which displayed distinct ribosomal RNA bands without significant smearing. Reverse transcription was performed using the ABScript Neo RT Master Mix for qPCR with gDNA Remover (RK20433, ABclonal, Wuhan, China) in a 20 μL reaction containing 5 μL of 4X RT MIX and 15 μL of total RNA (approximately 2 μg based on the average measured RNA concentration of 130 ng/μL). The thermal profile was 37°C for 2 min, 55°C for 15 min, 85°C for 5 min, and 4°C hold. The integrated gDNA remover step eliminates potential genomic DNA contamination prior to reverse transcription. The resulting cDNA was used immediately or stored at −20 °C until analysis.

Quantitative PCR was performed using the 2X Universal SYBR Green Fast qPCR Mix (RK21203, ABclonal, Wuhan, China) on a BIO-RAD CFX96 real-time PCR system (Bio-Rad, CA, USA) in a 20 μL reaction containing 10 μL of 2X qPCR MIX, 2 μL of cDNA, 0.4 μL of forward primer (10 μmol/L), 0.4 μL of reverse primer (10 μmol/L), and nuclease-free water to 20 μL. The primers used for real time PCR were listed in Table 1. The thermal profile was 37°C for 2 min (1 cycle), 95°C for 3 min (1 cycle), followed by 45 cycles of 95°C for 15s and 55°C for 30s, with fluorescence acquisition at the end of each 55°C step. A melt-curve analysis was automatically generated by the instrument (default ramp from 65°C to 95°C in 0.5°C increments) to confirm amplicon specificity. The amplification efficiencies of the DDX11-AS1 and GAPDH primer pairs were determined in a single preliminary validation experiment using a 5-log serial dilution of pooled cDNA, performed before sample analysis. The efficiencies were 98.4% for lncRNA AK058003 (R2 = 0.999; slope = −3.347) and 96.8% for GAPDH (R2 = 0.999; slope = −3.397), differing by less than 3 percentage points and confirming comparable amplification of the target and reference genes within the ±5% threshold recommended for ΔCt-based relative quantification. On the basis of this pilot validation, sample-level quantification was performed using the 2−ΔCt method (ΔCt = Ct lncRNA AK058003 − CtGAPDH) without per-sample standard curves. Samples were run in duplicate, and the mean Ct was used for analysis. Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted via SPSS 21.0 software, while GraphPad Prism 7.0 software was used for receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The Mann–Whitney U-test was utilized to compare the expression levels of the lncRNA AK058003 in TEP or plasma between EC patients and healthy donors, as well as to compare the subtypes of different TNM stages. The expression levels of the lncRNA AK058003 are presented as (the median, (lower quartiles, upper quartiles)). Independent sample t-test, Fisher’ exact test or Chi-square test for trend were used to assess the correlations between the expression level of the lncRNA AK058003 and the clinical parameters of the subjects. All the statistical analyses were two-sided, and a p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Upregulation of lncRNA AK058003 in EC

The expression levels of the TEP lncRNA AK058003 in EC patients were significantly higher [0.004267 (0.001494, 0.02853)] than that in healthy controls [0.002073 (0.001014, 0.006303)], with a p-value of 0.0042 (Figure 1A). Furthermore, the levels of the plasma lncRNA AK058003 in EC patients were also significantly higher [0.195 (0.09642, 0.5095)] than those in healthy controls [0.05413 (0.025, 0.0805)], with a p-value of less than 0.0001 (Figure 1B). Of note, two control samples showed concurrently high expression in both TEP and plasma compartments (above the Youden-index-derived diagnostic cut-offs); these outliers are explicitly identified in Figure 1 and are addressed in the Discussion. Diagnostic Value of the lncRNA AK058003 in EC

The area under the ROC curve (AUC) for the TEP lncRNA AK058003 in the EC group was 0.65, with a 95% confidence interval ranging from 0.5508 to 0.7412. The Youden index was 0.25, with the criterion set at TEP lncRNA AK058003 > 0.002936. The sensitivity and specificity were 64.1% and 60.9%, respectively, for the diagnosis of EC (Figure 2A). Discussion

Esophageal cancer remains a major global health burden, with persistently high mortality despite advances in surgical technique, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy.26,27 Endoscopic biopsy is the most direct diagnostic modality for EC, but many at-risk individuals do not undergo this invasive procedure during routine physical examinations. Moreover, serum markers such as SCCA, CA 19–9, and CEA are relatively insensitive and non-specific for diagnosing EC. Therefore, identification of accurate liquid-biopsy biomarkers is crucial for improving the survival rates of EC patients.

LncRNA AK058003 is a 1197-bp transcript located on the forward strand of chromosome 10q22 and has been implicated in several types of malignant tumors. Wang reported that lncRNA AK058003 was frequently upregulated in gastric cancer and promoted cell migration and invasion.28 He found that lncRNA AK058003 was downregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues and was associated with cancer relapse and metastasis.29 Three studies indicated that lncRNA AK058003 was upregulated in breast cancer, where it promoted cancer cell proliferation, invasion and metastasis.30–32 Zhang found that lncRNA AK058003 was highly expressed in patients with EC, facilitating the proliferation, migration, invasion, and metastasis of EC cells. Notably, patients with high levels of lncRNA AK058003 tended to have a poorer postoperative prognosis.24

Liquid biopsy is a minimally invasive, safe, and sensitive alternative or complementary approach for traditional tissue biopsy, demonstrating significant potential in cancer management.33 While tumor tissue biopsy remains the gold standard for cancer diagnosis, the information derived from a single biopsy is limited and may not adequately reflect the heterogeneity of the disease. Furthermore, tissue biopsy is invasive, which restricts the possibility of repeated sampling. In contrast, liquid biopsy offers an accurate and comprehensive spatiotemporal snapshot of the tumor and its microenvironment across multiple levels.34

Currently, blood-based biopsy techniques focus on assessing various biomarker sources, including circulating tumor DNA, circulating tumor cells, extracellular vesicles, and TEP.35 Platelets contain a variety of RNA transcripts and proteins derived from megakaryocytes during thrombocytogenesis. Throughout their lifespan, circulating platelets interact with immune cells, cancer cells, and stromal cells as well as communicate with vesicles in whole blood. These direct interactions and distant cell signaling can alter the content or function of platelets, leading to the development of TEP.36,37 External stimuli can also induce specific splicing of premessenger RNAs (mRNAs) in circulating TEPs.38 Best MG demonstrated that platelet mRNA profiles could be distinguish between healthy donors and cancer patients.25,39 Our previous studies also indicated that TEP miR-34c-3p, miR-18a-5p, miR-18a-3p and lncRNA ROR were upregulated in nasopharyngeal carcinoma and exhibited excellent diagnostic value.40–42

In this study, we found that both TEP and plasma lncRNA AK058003 were significantly upregulated in patients with EC compared with normal subjects. These findings suggest that the upregulated lncRNA AK058003 in tumor tissues may be released into the peripheral blood circulation through certain pathways. Additionally, tumor cells may interact with platelets and transfer the lncRNA AK058003 to them. In the ROC analysis, TEP lncRNA AK058003 demonstrated an AUC value of 0.65, while plasma lncRNA AK058003 showed an AUC of 0.83. These results suggest that TEP and plasma lncRNA AK058003 are promising biomarkers for EC diagnosis. Furthermore, we observed that the expression levels of TEP lncRNA AK058003 were associated with tumor size, increasing progressively with T stage. These findings indicate the potential of TEP lncRNA AK058003 to aid in the staging of EC.

Before conducting the q-PCR assay, the quantity and quality of the RNA samples were assessed, and 2 µg of RNA was used for reverse transcription. To ensure reliable results, GAPDH was set up as an internal reference gene, and the Ct values of GAPDH RNA differed within 1. As liquid biopsy biomarkers, TEP and plasma lncRNA AK058003 are effective, noninvasive, easy to obtain and repeatable compared to traditional tissue biopsy. Furthermore, the detection of TEP and plasma lncRNA AK058003 are particularly useful for various patients with EC, including those who are unable to undergo endoscopic biopsies or those requiring follow-up checks after surgery. Our assay is also easily applicable to clinical and automation settings, as RT-qPCR is a well-established detection technology. Thus, TEP and plasma lncRNA AK058003 represent promising new options for diagnosing EC.

As noted in the Results, two control samples displayed concurrently elevated expression of AK058003 in both TEP and plasma, exceeding the Youden-index-derived diagnostic cut-offs in either compartment. Three non-mutually-exclusive explanations are considered. First, intrinsic inter-individual variability in platelet and plasma transcriptomes has been documented in healthy populations and may account for sporadic high expression unrelated to malignancy. Second, subclinical pathology not identified during routine health examination (eg, early neoplastic lesions below endoscopic detection threshold or occult inflammatory states) cannot be fully excluded. Third, pre-analytical and analytical fluctuations, including variable platelet activation and plasma RNA integrity, may contribute to borderline-high measurements. The fact that the Youden-index derived cut-offs classify these samples as “test-positive” is acknowledged as a contributor to the observed specificity of 76.6%.

Translating plasma lncRNA AK058003 from a single-center discovery to routine clinical practice requires consideration of three interdependent factors. First, the most plausible initial application is complementary to endoscopy in individuals who are unable or unwilling to undergo invasive work-up, rather than as a stand-alone diagnostic test. Second, the observed diagnostic performance should be validated in a prospective, multicenter cohort with standardized pre-analytical protocols, given known inter-laboratory variability in platelet isolation and plasma RNA extraction. Third, cost-effectiveness and integration with established serum markers (SCCA, CA 19–9, CEA) remain to be established. We have initiated a multicenter diagnostic validation cohort to address these questions.

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the present findings. First, this is a single-center study, and external validation in geographically and ethnically diverse cohorts is required. Second, sample size was determined by a priori precision considerations and constrained by the maximum feasible enrollment at a single tertiary center during the 16-month study window; the achieved precision around the observed specificity (95% CI half-width approx. 10.4%) was slightly above the pre-specified 10% threshold and slightly limits the precision of the specificity estimate. Third, complete TNM staging was available for only 43 of 64 EC patients, precluding a fully powered M-stage subgroup analysis. Fourth, the cross-sectional design does not permit evaluation of dynamic changes during treatment or follow-up. Fifth, although post-hoc statistical power exceeded 95% for both sensitivity and specificity against the null value of 50%, the present study should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating, and our findings await external validation in a larger, prospective, multicenter diagnostic cohort.

Conclusions

In conclusion, both TEP and plasma lncRNA AK058003 are significantly upregulated in patients with EC. Plasma lncRNA AK058003 demonstrates promising diagnostic performance (AUC 0.83, sensitivity 82.8%, specificity 76.6%), while TEP lncRNA AK058003 overexpression correlates with tumor size, supporting their complementary roles as non-invasive liquid-biopsy biomarkers for EC. Larger prospective multicenter validation is warranted before clinical translation.

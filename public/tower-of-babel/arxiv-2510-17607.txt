Boundary Depth and Deformations of Symplectic Cohomology
Yoel Groman

Abstract
. We study the relation between two versions of symplectic cohomology associated to a Liouville domain D D embedded in a symplectic manifold M M : the ambient version S ​ C M ∗ ​ ( D ) SC^{*}_{M}(D) defined over the Novikov field and depending on the embedding, and the intrinsic version S ​ C θ ∗ ​ ( D ) SC^{*}_{\theta}(D) depending on the choice of a local Liouville form and defined over the ground field. We show that when D D has sufficiently small boundary depth, the ambient version can be viewed as a deformation of the intrinsic one. This is achieved by constructing a filtration whose associated graded reproduces the intrinsic theory, and developing quantitative tools to control the deformation. We apply our results to constructing local pieces of the SYZ mirror.

The pathology of Example

1.6.
Neck stretching and deformation

To proceed, one can either try to characterize the deformations of the differential and attending structures explicitly or develop quantitative control methods. In the present work we only rigorously take up the latter approach. But let us pause to formulate a conjectural picture for the former approach. The discussion in this section is within the purview of a collaboration with K. Siegel.

Recall the intrinsic complex
SC^{*}(D)=SC^{*}_{\theta}(D)
carries a framed
E_{2}
structure
[
3
]

. Applying the Borel construction we obtain the

of the Maurer-Cartan moduli spaces.

Conjecture 1
.

Suppose

has no contractible Reeb orbits. Then there is a Maurer-Cartan element

well-defined up to gauge equivalence such that

The conjecture is motivated by an SFT type neck stretching argument. We expect such an argument can be used to produce an element in linearized contact chains. See

There is an entirely different neck stretching scheme,

Conjecture 2

Suppose there a symplectic crossing divisor

This is closely related to the work of

Remark 1.17
.

In the either setting, the Maurer Cartan element

1.9.
The local to global method in Floer theory

There is an open string version of Floer theory with supports which is under development in joint work with Abouzaid and Varolgunes. The closed string story which is this paper’s focus can be viewed as a special case where we consider the diagonal Lagrangian
\Delta\subset(M\times M,\omega\oplus-\omega)
.

With this in mind, let us first compare and contrast the philosophy of the present work with the philosophy proposed by Seidel in
[
20
]
. There one considers a Liouville domain
D
obtained from a projective variety
M
by removing an ample divisor
V
, presenting the full subcategory
\mathcal{F}(M,V)\subset\mathcal{F}(M)
of objects supported in
M\setminus V
as a deformation of the compact Fukaya category
\mathcal{F}_{c}(D)\subset\mathcal{W}(D)
. This has utilized been utilized to prove homological mirror symmetry in many settings
[
23
,
24
,
12
]
.

By contrast, in Floer theory with supports we do not restrict to objects contained in
D
nor do we focus on divisor complements. The Mayer-Vietoris property
[
29
]
, whose open string version is forthcoming with Abouzaid-Varolgunes, reconstructs the global Yoneda module of
L\subset M
from those of
L
with supports on
D_{i}\subset M
where
\{D_{i}\}
forms an involutive cover. The open string analogue of the present work would further relate the Floer theory of
L
in
M
with supports on
D_{i}
to the intrinsic Floer theory of
L
with support on
D_{i}
. We would thus cut the global Lagrangian
L
into pieces
L\cap D_{i}
and reconstruct the global Floer theory from the the wrapped Floer theory of these local pieces endowed with some deformation data.

To summarize, the local-to-global approach for Calabi-Yau manifolds
M
is expected to proceed as follows:

(1)

Construct an involutive cover
\{D_{i}\}
by Liouville domains with tractable intrinsic Floer theory.

(2)

Use Mayer-Vietoris to relate global Floer theory to Floer theory with supports on
D_{i}
.

(3)

Study ambient Floer theory on
D_{i}
as a deformation of intrinsic wrapped Floer theory.

(4)

Apply tools of wrapped Floer theory to study the intrisic Floer theory of the
D_{i}
.

Remark 1.22
.

The first step in the above construction is a problem of differential geometry. It has long been conjectured for example that a toric degeneration of Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces and complete intersections should give rise a Lagrangian torus fibration with singularities modeled on some small number of types of singularities. However, to the best of our knowledge this has not yet been established satisfactorily.

On the other hand, one can take the opposite point of view where starting from an integral affine manifold with singularities one constructs an appropriate symplectic manifols. Such integral affine manifolds with singularities arise naturally from the nonarchimedean SYZ fibration associated with the degeneration of a Calabi-Yau manifold
[
19
]
. Understanding the Fukaya category of the symplecic manifold thus constructed has implications for understanding the analytic geometry of the degeneration. An example of applications in this direction is in the work of
[
18
]
concerning realizabilty of tropcial varieties.

The method outlined above can be adjusted to the Fano and positive log Calabi-Yau cases, and some other cases. But this is outside the scope of the present discussion.

2.
Basics

2.1.
Notation and conventions

Fix a ring
R
and denote by

\Lambda=\Lambda_{R}:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}T^{\lambda_{i}}:a_{i}\in R,\lambda_{i}\to\infty\right\},

the Novikov field over
R
and by

\Lambda_{\geq 0}:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}T^{\lambda_{i}}:a_{i}\in R,\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\lambda_{i}\to\infty\right\}

the Novikov ring.

For an element
x=\sum a_{i}T^{\lambda_{i}}\in\Lambda
we write

\val(x)=\min\{\lambda_{i}|a_{i}\neq 0\},

and,

|x|=e^{-\val(x)}.

More generally, for a module M over
\Lambda
, a
non-archimedean valuation
is a valuation
\val:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

\val(x+y)\geq\max\{\val(x),\val(y)\},

and a non-Archimedean norm is a norm of the form

|x|=e^{-\val(x)}.

We use the notation
\Lambda_{[a,b)}
for elements with
\val\in[a,b)
, i.e., the
\Lambda_{\geq 0}
-module of elements with valuation
\geq a
modulo those with valuation
\geq b
. When
a=0
,
\Lambda_{[0,b)}
is a unital ring.

Remark 2.1
.

This notation conflicts with
[
15
]
where
\Lambda_{[a,b)}
denotes elements of norm
<e^{b}
modulo those with norm
<e^{a}
.

Some of the main results require
R
to be a field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic zero, though many intermediate results hold for arbitrary ground rings
R
.

Remark 2.2
.

Both ambient and intrinsic invariants are a priori
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded. For
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading, we require
c_{1}(M)=0
(implying
c_{1}(D)=0
). When
D
is not simply connected, we additionally require a trivialization of
\det(T^{*}D\otimes{\mathbb{C}})
extending to
M
. For Weinstein neighborhoods of Lagrangians
L
, a natural trivialization exists, and the extension assumption is equivalent to
\mu_{L}:\pi_{1}(M,L)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
vanishing. We leave the grading (
{\mathbb{Z}}
with compatible trivialization, or
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
) unspecified.

2.2.
The Hamiltonian Floer package

Our Floer cohomology conventions follow
[
17
]
. We work with closed or geometrically bounded manifolds
M
. For geometrically bounded manifolds and dissipative Floer data (providing necessary
C^{0}
estimates), see
[
17
, Appendix A]
. We first treat the
c_{1}(M)=0
case with classical transversality.

2.2.1.
The classical transversality package

Assume
c_{1}(M)=0
with a fixed trivialization of the complexified canonical bundle of
M
. For a Hamiltonian
H
, denote by
X_{H}
the vector field satisfying
\omega(X_{H},\cdot)=dH
. The trivialization associates an orientation line
o_{\gamma}
and Conley-Zehnder index
i_{cz}(\gamma)
to each
1
-periodic orbit
\gamma
. Assume
H
has finitely many
1
-periodic orbits. We also conisder the semi-positive case, in which we interpret
i_{cz}(\gamma)\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Fix a time-dependent
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure
J
with
(H,J)
regular for Floer cohomology (see
[
3
, Definition 3.13]
). Consider Floer trajectories
u:{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}\to M
solving

(7)

\partial_{s}u+J(\partial_{t}u-X_{H})=0.

The end at
-\infty
is the
input
, the end at
+\infty
the
output
. the orientation of the
X_{H}
orbit at the input is opposite to boundary orientation; at the output they coincide.

We take the underlying
\Lambda
-module of the Floer complex
CF^{*}(H,J)
to be

(8)

CF^{*}(H,J)=\bigoplus o_{\gamma}\otimes\Lambda[i_{cz}(\gamma)]

where the sum is over all
1
-periodic orbits and
i_{cz}(\gamma)
denotes the Conley-Zehnder index. A Floer trajectory
u
with input
\gamma_{1}
and output
\gamma_{2}
defines a map
d_{u}:o_{\gamma_{1}}\to o_{\gamma_{2}}
. The Floer differential
d:CF^{*}(H,J)\to CF^{*}(H,J)
is then defined by

(9)

d:=\sum_{u}T^{E_{top}(u)}d_{u},

where

E_{top}(u):=\int u^{*}(\omega+d(Hdt)).

The sum is over all finite energy rigid solutions. It converges in the
T
-adic topology on
CF^{*}(H,J)
. To see this, write
E_{geo}(u)=\int\|du-X_{H}\|^{2}
. Then

(10)

E_{top}(u)\geq E_{geo}(u).

By Gromov-Floer compactness, solutions with bounded geometric energy intersecting a compact set form a compact set (given
C^{0}
estimates, established for dissipative data
[
14
]
).

For regular dissipative data
(H_{0},J_{0})\leq(H_{1},J_{1})
, a dissipative monotone path
s\mapsto(H_{s},J_{s})
with
(H_{s},J_{s})=(H_{0},J_{0})
for
s\leq 0
and
(H_{s},J_{s})=(H_{1},J_{1})
for
s\geq 1
induces a continuation map
\kappa:CF^{*}(H_{0},J_{0})\to CF^{*}(H_{1},J_{1})
via

\kappa=\sum T^{E_{top}(u)}\kappa_{u},

for
\kappa_{u}:o_{\gamma_{1}}\to o_{\gamma_{2}}
the isomorphism of orientation lines induced by the solution
u
with asymptotics on
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
.

Monotonicity ensures
E_{top}(u)\geq E_{geo}(u)
, required for moduli space finiteness. Weakening to
\partial_{s}H_{s}
bounded below yields a continuation map
\kappa
over
\Lambda
only. Strict monotonicity defines complexes and maps over
\Lambda_{\geq 0}
.

Given monotone paths
(H_{s}^{0},J_{s}^{0})
and
(H_{s}^{1},J_{s}^{1})
from
(H_{0},J_{0})
to
(H_{1},J_{1})
and a 2-parameter interpolation
(H_{s,t},J_{s,t})
, we obtain a chain homotopy
K:CF^{*}(H_{0},J_{0})\to CF^{*}(H_{1},J_{1})
between continuation maps
\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1}
via

K=\sum T^{E_{top}(v)}K_{v}

where the sum is over rigid solutions
v
to the 2-parameter Floer equation, and
K_{v}:o_{\gamma_{1}}\to o_{\gamma_{2}}
are induced orientation line maps. Then
\kappa_{1}-\kappa_{0}=d\circ K+K\circ d
.

2.2.2.
The general setting: virtual chains

We now drop the assumption
c_{1}(M)=0
. Consider geometrically bounded
J
(no regularity assumption) and Hamiltonian
H
with non-degenerate
1
-periodic orbits. In the non-compact case, assume
(H,J)
dissipative. The ground ring is a field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic zero (refinable to arbitrary rings for special cases). The Floer complex is

(11)

CF^{*}(H,J)=\bigoplus o_{\gamma}\otimes\Lambda[i_{cz}(\gamma)]

where the sum is over
1
-periodic orbits and
i_{cz}(\gamma)\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
generally.

We define the differential via virtual chains
[
2
]
. To Floer data
(H,J)
, we associate a
Kuranishi flow category

\mathbb{X}_{H}

[
2
, Section 8]
. Briefly: start with poset
\mathcal{P}
(the set of
1
-periodic orbits). Morphisms
\gamma\to\gamma^{\prime}
are sequences
(\gamma,\lambda_{0},\gamma_{1},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n},\lambda_{n},\gamma^{\prime})
where
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
are topological energies and
\gamma_{i}
are orbits. Composition is concatenation.

A Kuranishi flow category
\mathbb{X}
over
\mathcal{P}
consists of orientation lines
o_{\gamma}
for each
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}
and Kuranishi presentations
\mathbb{X}^{\lambda}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
for each
\lambda>0
and pair
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
, with strata corresponding to morphisms with total energy
\leq\lambda
(see
[
2
, Definition 7.2]
).

For our application, take
\mathcal{P}_{H}
as
1
-periodic orbits of
H
; the construction of
\mathbb{X}_{H}
over
\mathcal{P}_{H}
is in
[
2
, Section 10]
.

A
theory of virtual counts

[
2
, Section 7.2]
produces a chain complex over
\Lambda
from a Kuranishi flow category:

(12)

CF^{*}(\mathbb{X})=\bigoplus_{\gamma\text{ objects}}o_{\gamma}\otimes\Lambda[|\gamma|]

where
|\gamma|
is the grading. The differential is

(13)

d=\sum_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\sum_{\lambda}T^{\lambda}m_{\lambda}

with operations
m_{\lambda}:o_{\gamma_{1}}\to o_{\gamma_{2}}
extracted from Kuranishi presentations via a theory of virtual counts.

A monotone continuation between
(H_{0},J_{0})
and
(H_{1},J_{1})
yields a bimodule over
\mathbb{X}_{H_{0}}
and
\mathbb{X}_{H_{1}}

[
2
, Section 8]
. Its strata are sequences
(\gamma_{0},\lambda_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{k},\lambda_{k},\gamma_{k+1},\ldots,\gamma_{n},\lambda_{n},\gamma^{\prime})
where
\gamma_{i}
(
i\leq k
) are orbits of
H_{0}
and
\gamma_{j}
(
j>k
) are orbits of
H_{1}
, capturing contributions from differentials and continuation. A theory of virtual counts provide a chain map

(14)

\Phi=\sum_{\gamma_{0},\gamma^{\prime}}\sum_{\lambda}T^{\lambda}\phi_{\lambda}:CF^{*}(\mathbb{X}_{H_{0}})\to CF^{*}(\mathbb{X}_{H_{1}})

with
\phi_{\lambda}:o_{\gamma_{0}}\to o_{\gamma^{\prime}}
extracted from the bimodule via a theory of virtual counts.

Two bimodules
\mathcal{B}_{0},\mathcal{B}_{1}
between
\mathbb{X}_{H_{0}}
and
\mathbb{X}_{H_{1}}
yield a bimodule
\mathcal{H}
over an interval, providing a chain homotopy

(15)

K:CF^{*}(\mathbb{X}_{H_{0}})\to CF^{*}(\mathbb{X}_{H_{1}})

between corresponding chain maps
\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}
, satisfying
\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{0}=d\circ K+K\circ d
. (Virtual counts work for cubes generally, but we only need this.)

The virtual chains approach parallels §
2.2.1
, but sums over generator pairs
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and energy levels
\lambda
rather than individual solutions
u
. This means that for certain geometric arguments, the relation between the geometry of J-holomorphic curves and the virtual counts becomes somewhat more indirect.

2.3.
Indexing categories and telescopes

2.3.1.
Indexing categories

Symplectic cohomology in its various flavors is defined as a colimit of Hamiltonian Floer cohomologies. For any serious applications, one needs to do this at the chain level. The starting point for this is a (truncated)
indexing category
. This notion will be reused repeatedly throughout the paper for different contexts.

Definition 2.3
.

A
truncated indexing category

\mathcal{H}
consists of:

•

A set of objects
\mathcal{H}_{0}
called
\mathcal{H}
-admissible Floer data
, consisting of pairs
(H,J)
of Hamiltonians and almost complex structures.

•

For each pair of objects
(H_{0},J_{0}),(H_{1},J_{1})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, a set
\mathcal{H}_{1}((H_{0},J_{0}),(H_{1},J_{1}))
of
\mathcal{H}
-admissible paths
, consisting of:

–

Smooth families
s\mapsto(H_{s},J_{s})
for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
with compactly supported derivative, such that
(H_{s},J_{s})=(H_{0},J_{0})
for
s\ll 0
and
(H_{s},J_{s})=(H_{1},J_{1})
for
s\gg 0
.

–

Broken paths: sequences of smooth paths
(H_{0},J_{0})\xrightarrow{\gamma_{1}}(H_{1},J_{1})\xrightarrow{\gamma_{2}}(H_{2},J_{2})\xrightarrow{\gamma_{3}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\gamma_{n}}(H_{n},J_{n})
where each
\gamma_{i}
is a smooth path as above.

•

For each pair of paths
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}((H_{0},J_{0}),(H_{1},J_{1}))
, a set
\mathcal{H}_{2}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
of
\mathcal{H}
-admissible homotopies
, defined as follows:

–

When both
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are smooth, this is a smooth 2-parameter family
(s,t)\mapsto(H_{s,t},J_{s,t})
interpolating between
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
.

–

When one of them is broken, we also allow for gluing homotopies. Specifically, suppose we have a broken path with two components:
\alpha
ending on
(H_{1},J_{1})
and
\beta
starting on
(H_{1},J_{1})
. We define the gluing homotopy by the notation
r\mapsto\alpha\#_{r}\beta
, where the operation
\#_{r}
is defined as follows. This is the local picture near the specific ends of
\alpha
and
\beta
:

*

For
r=0
, it gives the completely broken path
(\alpha,\beta)
.

*

For
r\in(0,\varepsilon)
(for some small
\varepsilon>0
), we remove from the lower path
\alpha
the interval
[-\log r,\infty)
and from the upper path
\beta
the interval
(-\infty,\log r]
, and identify the remaining endpoints.

Remark 2.4
.

A truncated indexing category
is not a category in the usual sense
, as it records homotopy data. The correct notion is a category enriched in cubical sets or an
\infty
-category
[
2
,
3
]
. We only need part of this data and refer to truncated indexing categories simply as
indexing categories
.

Definition 2.5
.

Given an indexing category
\mathcal{H}
, an
acceleration datum
A
in
\mathcal{H}
is a family of pairs
\tau\mapsto(H_{\tau},J_{\tau})
of Floer data together with a smooth function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
f(t)\equiv 0
for
t\ll 0
and
f(t)\equiv 1
for
t\gg 1
. These are required to satisfy:

•

For each
\tau
, the pair
(H_{\tau},J_{\tau})
is
\mathcal{H}
-admissible.

•

For each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the continuation path
g_{i}:s\mapsto(H_{i+f(s)},J_{i+f(s)})
is
\mathcal{H}
-admissible

The acceleration datum is said to be
regular
if:

•

For each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the pair
(H_{i},J_{i})
is regular for the definition of Floer cohomology.

•

For each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the continuation path
g_{i}:s\mapsto(H_{i+f(s)},J_{i+f(s)})
is regular for the definition of continuation maps.

In the virtual chains setting, we only impose that for each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
1
-periodic orbits of
H_{i}
are non-degenerate.

3.7.
Two filtrations on the Floer complex

For the remainder of the discussion we will refer to S-shaped Hamiltonians and to the constant
\hbar
appearing in Propositions
3.11

For a regular Floer datum

for a monotone Floer datum implies that the Floer differential and the Floer continuation maps are norm non-increasing.

Fix a local primitive
\theta
and consider
S
-shaped Floer data

(H,J)
. Using the results of Section §
3.5
we construct an additional filtration on

(57)

\val_{M,\theta,H}\left(T^{\lambda}a_{\gamma}\right):=\lambda+\mathcal{A}_{H,\theta}(\gamma).

We refer to this as the

. We drop
\theta
or
H
from the notation when there is no ambiguity. We call the set
\left\{T^{-\mathcal{A}_{H}(\gamma)}a_{\gamma}\right\}
where
\gamma
runs over the set of
1
-periodic orbits of
H
the
action normalized basis
of
CF^{*}(H)
.

Let us now show that
\val_{M,\theta,H}
indeed defines a filtration. For a Floer solution
u
with input
\gamma_{0}
and output
\gamma_{1}
write

\Delta_{M,\theta}(u):=E_{top}(u)+\val_{M,\theta,H_{1}}(\gamma_{1})-\val_{M,\theta,H_{0}}(\gamma_{0}).

From now on we omit
H
fron the notation and write
\val_{M,\theta}.

Lemma 3.17
.

For any Floer solution
u
with input
\gamma_{0}
and output
\gamma_{1}
, we have
E_{M,\theta}(u)=\Delta_{M,\theta}(u)
.

Proof.

Let us introduce a one-form
\lambda
on
M
that extends
\theta
and is equal to zero at all critical points. It is easy to see that such a one-form exists. This one-form
\lambda
has the property that the action functional we defined is always equal to
-\int_{\gamma}H+\int_{\gamma}\lambda
, and moreover, the relative energy is also always equal to
\int u^{*}\omega-\int_{\partial u}u^{*}\lambda
. This eliminates the need to make distinctions between non-trivial periodic orbits and critical points, and we never have to introduce compactifications.

With this setup, we have:

(58)

E_{M,\theta}(u)=\int u^{*}\omega-\int_{\partial u}u^{*}\lambda.

The topological energy is

(59)

E_{top}(u)=\int u^{*}\omega+\int_{\partial u}Hdt.

For
any
periodic orbit
\gamma
, the action functional becomes:

(60)

\mathcal{A}_{H,\theta}(\gamma)=-\int_{\gamma}H-\int_{\gamma}\lambda.

We have:

\displaystyle E_{M,\theta}(u)

\displaystyle=\int u^{*}\omega-\int_{\partial u}u^{*}\lambda

\displaystyle=E_{top}(u)-\int_{\partial u}Hdt-\int_{\partial u}u^{*}\lambda

\displaystyle=E_{top}(u)-\left(\int_{\gamma_{1}}H_{1}-\int_{\gamma_{0}}H_{0}\right)-\left(\int_{\gamma_{1}}\lambda-\int_{\gamma_{0}}\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=E_{top}(u)+\mathcal{A}_{H_{1},\theta}(\gamma_{1})-\mathcal{A}_{H_{0},\theta}(\gamma_{0})

\displaystyle=E_{top}(u)+\val_{M,\theta,H_{1}}(\gamma_{1})-\val_{M,\theta,H_{0}}(\gamma_{0})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{M,\theta}(u).

∎

•

u
is contained inside

•

The output is outside.

Proof.

By Lemma

5.
Boundary depth and Banach spectral sequences

5.1.
Boundary depth and convergence

Let
(C^{*},d,|\cdot|)
be a complete normed chain complex. The
boundary depth

\beta(C^{*},d,|\cdot|)
is defined by

\beta=\begin{cases}\sup_{x\neq 0\in\im d}\inf\{\val(x)-\val(y):dy=x\}&d\neq 0\\
0&d=0.\end{cases}

Here
\val(x)=-\ln|x|
.
An equivalent characterization of boundary depth comes from considering the exact sequence

0\to Z^{*}\to C^{*}\to Q^{*}\to 0

where
Z^{*}=\ker\partial
and
Q^{*}=C^{*}/Z^{*}
. The complex
Q^{*}
carries an induced norm and an induced map
\overline{d}:Q^{*}\to B^{*}=\im d
which is a bijection. Then

e^{\beta}=\left|\overline{d}^{-1}\right|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in B^{*}}\frac{|\overline{d}^{-1}x|}{|x|},

with the convention that for the unique operator on the zero space the latter is
1
.

5.1.1.
Homological characterization

We now show that boundary depth can be characterized homologically, which will allow us to prove it is an invariant of norm preserving weak homotopy equivalence.

Given a normed chain complex
(C^{*},d,|\cdot|)
over a ground field
{\mathbb{K}}
, we can form the completed tensor product
C^{*}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda
with the Novikov field
\Lambda
. This gives us a chain complex over
\Lambda
with a valuation induced by the norm. The boundary depth is unchanged by this process.

Let now
(C^{*},d,|\cdot|)
be a complete valued chain complex over the Novikov field. The valuation gives rise to the subcomplex
C^{*}_{>0}
of elements with valuation greater than
0
. This is a chain complex over the Novikov ring. We call a morphism
f:C^{*}\to D^{*}
of valued complexes a
filtered quasi-isomorphism
if it induces an isomorphism on homology
H(C^{*}_{>0})\to H(D^{*}_{>0})
over the Novikov ring.

Denote by
H(C^{*}_{>0})^{\tau}
the torsion part of
H(C^{*}_{>0})
. For each
x\neq 0\in H(C^{*}_{>0})^{\tau}
let

\tau(x):=\inf\{r:T^{r}x=0\}=\sup\{r:T^{r}x\neq 0\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

\beta(C^{*})=\sup_{x\in H(C^{*}_{>0})^{\tau}}\tau(x).

Proof.

Denote the right hand side by
\tau(C^{*})
. We prove inequality in both directions. For an element
x\in\im\partial
introduce the notation

\beta(x):=\inf\{\val(x)-\val(y):\partial y=x\}.

Note
\tau(x)
is defined for elements with
\val(x)>0
and satisfies
\tau(T^{r}x)=\tau(x)-r
. Here we abuse notation and write
\tau(x)=\tau([x])
. On the other hand
\beta(x)
is defined for all
x
and satisfies
\beta(T^{r}x)=\beta(x)
. In particular, we may take as witnesses for
\tau(C^{*})>\tau_{0}
and for
\beta(x)>\beta_{0}
elements
x\in C^{*}_{>0}
so that
\val(x)
is arbitrarily close to
0
.

The claim now follows by observing that for any
x\in C^{*}_{>0}
we have
\beta(x)-\val(x)\leq\tau(x)<\beta(x)
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Boundary depth is preserved under filtered quasi-isomorphism, i.e. under chain maps inducing an isomorphism
H(C^{*}_{>0})\cong H(D^{*}_{>0})
over the Novikov ring.

Proof.

Let
f:C^{*}\to D^{*}
be a filtered quasi-isomorphism. Then
f
induces an isomorphism on
H(C^{*}_{>0})^{\tau}\to H(D^{*}_{>0})^{\tau}
. Since

Lemma 5.3
.

If

has finite boundary depth then

(1)

is a Banach space

(2)

Proof.

5.2.
Properties of boundary depth of a Liouville domain

For a Liouville domain
D\subset M
we have defined the boundary depth
\beta(D)
as

(70)

\beta(D):=\beta\left(SC^{*}_{\widehat{D},\theta}(D;{\mathbb{Q}})\right).

where
SC^{*}_{\widehat{D},\theta}(D;{\mathbb{Q}})
is the completed telescope model associated with some acceleration datum
A
. We list the following properties of
\beta(D)
:

Lemma 5.4
.

(1)

\beta(D)
is independent of the choice of acceleration datum
A
.

(2)

\beta
is independent of the choice of primitive.

(3)

\beta(t\cdot D)=t\beta(D)
.

(4)

If the cohomology
SH^{*}(D;\Lambda_{>0})
over the Novikov ring is torsion free, then
\beta(D)=0
.

Proof.

We prove each property in turn:

(1)

Independence of acceleration datum: This follows from Corollary
5.2
since different acceleration data give rise to filtered quasi-isomorphic complexes.

(2)

Independence of primitive:
\beta
can equivalently be defined without reference to a primitive in terms of the chain complex
SC^{*}_{\widehat{D}}(D;\Lambda)
.

(3)

Scaling property: Under Liouville rescaling by
t>0
, actions scale by
t
and hence valuations scale by
t
. Torsion exponents, measured in valuation, therefore scale linearly:
\beta(t\cdot D)=t\,\beta(D)
.

(4)

Torsion-free case: If

is torsion free, then by Lemma
5.1
, we have
\beta(D)=\sup_{x\in H(C^{*}_{>0})^{\tau}}\tau(x)=0
since there are no torsion elements.

∎

7.1.

Let

This quantity measures the minimal relative energy needed for any non-trivial deformation of the intrinsic symplectic cohomology to occur in the ambient theory. A priori, it depends on the choice of acceleration datum. To show that it is independent of choices, we turn to an alternative way of describing it in terms of the locality spectral sequence.

The locality spectral sequence introduced in equation (
66

deforms the intrinsic symplectic cohomology

-filtration

identifies the first page

The graded ring

inherits a valuation from

be the first page for which there is a non-trivial differential

Lemma 7.1
.

The quantity

or on the choice of the parameters

Proof.

We have a filtration preserving quasi isomorphism

the equality

is obvious from the definition of

3.23
different choices of acceleration data are related by a filtration preserving quasi-isomorphism. These induce isomorphisms on the spectral sequences.
∎

It also follows from the above characterization that

is independent of the choice of
\hbar

gives rise when considered as a function on

to an upper bound on

. This upper bound is independent of the choice of the section, since

is independent of the choice of the section.

Now consider a segment

contained in the star shape and realize it as a straight isotopy

. Then

, initially defined on

The second part of the claim follows immediately: if

Finally, suppose the ambient manifold

8.
Rigidity of algebraic structures

8.1.
Rigidity of algebras

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative nonarchemedean Banach algebra whose product we denote for elements
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
by
xy
. For
c<1
a commutative product
*
on
\mathcal{A}
is called

-close to the original product if
|x*y-xy|<c|xy|
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
.
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
c
-rigid if any
c
-close product structure on
\mathcal{A}
is isomorphic as a Banach algebra to
\mathcal{A}
by an isomorphism which is identity up to terms of norm
<c
.

Let’s consider some examples.

Lemma 8.2

(Rigidity of Tate Algebra)
.

The Tate algebra
T_{n}=\Lambda\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
(the
\Lambda
-algebra of restricted formal power series in
n
variables, where coefficients converge to
0
with respect to the norm on
\Lambda
) is
c
-rigid for any
c<1

Proof.

Consider the generators
x_{1},\dots x_{n}
which are of norm
1
. The monomials
x^{I}
form an orthonormal basis for
T_{n}
as a vector space over
\Lambda
. For any element
x\in T_{n}
denote by
x_{j}^{*i}
the
i
-fold
*
product of
x
with itself. For any multi-index
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
, denote by
x^{*I}
the element
x_{1}^{*i_{1}}*\dots*x_{n}^{*i_{n}}
. Then
|x^{*I}-x^{I}|<|x^{I}|=1
by induction on the length of
I
. The map
x^{I}\mapsto x^{*I}
for each
I\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is a norm preserving isomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules which is also a homomorphism of algebras.
∎

Lemma 8.3

(Rigidity of annulus)
.

Consider inside the affine variety
\Lambda[x_{1},x_{2}]/(x_{1}x_{2}=1)

\mathcal{A}
be its algebra of functions. Then
\mathcal{A}
is
e^{-r_{1}-r_{2}}
-rigid.

More generally, we have the following lemma for the polyannulus.

Proof.

Consider a
c
-close product structure
*
for any
c<e^{-r_{1}-r_{2}}

z_{1}=e^{-r_{1}}x_{1}
and
z_{2}=e^{-r_{2}}x_{2}

z_{1}*z_{2}=e^{-r_{1}-r_{2}}(1+o(1))
. In particular there is a

such that

∎

Lemma 8.4
.

Let

Consider a
c
-close product structure
*

Lemma 8.5
.

Let

Definition 8.6

An injective map

Definition 8.7

An almost commutative diagram of Banach spaces is a diagram

Proposition 8.8

Given an almost commutative diagram

\textstyle{V_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We first define

.

Let

and let

Lemma A.6

Appendix B
Virtual Chains and
\varepsilon
-Locality

The framework of virtual chains developed by Abouzaid in
[
2
]
is sufficient for most the construction of the complex
SC^{*}_{M}(K)
, its operations, presheaf structure as well as the
\val_{M}
filtration when using S-shaped data. However the proof of Proposition
4.4
which underlies Theorems
A1
-
A4
requires a slight adjustment. The argument of Proposition
4.4
is that if we consider contributions from curves
u
satisfying
\val_{M}(u)=0
and whose output is not on an outside critical point, then relying on the integrated maximum principle we have a bijection between these solutions and those contributing to the intrinsic complex. This argument becomes problematic in the setting of virtual chains where the operations are not defined by a count of actual Floer solutions but by virtual counts.
This is not a serious issue since it can be shown that the virtual count depends only on an arbitrarily small neighborhood of the set of actual solutions. Rather than show this we choose to build in this type of locality in the construction of the Kuranishi charts. For this we recall the notion of Kuranishi charts and how they are constructed. We introduce a slight adjustment to the construction of the Kuranishi charts in
[
2
]
where fix an
\epsilon>0
and consider thickening data which are
\epsilon
local (Definition
B.9
). This has the unpleasant feature that the intrinsic Floer theory would appear to depend on a parameter
\epsilon
. It should not be hard to construct Kuranishi bi-modules corresponding to families in which
\epsilon
varies, and use this to show that the virtual count is independent of the choice of
\epsilon
.

We now explain why our

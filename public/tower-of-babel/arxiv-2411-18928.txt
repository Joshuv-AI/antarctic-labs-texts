Abstract
. For three dimensional complete Riemannian manifolds with scalar curvature no less than one, we prove that Schoen-Yau diameter estiamte for stable minimal surfaces in those manifolds is sharp. Mathematics Subject Classification: 53C21, 53C23.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a complete
3
-dim Riemannian manifold with scalar curvature
R\geq 1
, for any stable minimal surface
\Sigma
in
M
(possibly
\partial\Sigma\neq\emptyset
), and
\gamma\subset\Sigma
be a closed curve, if

(1.1)

\displaystyle U_{\rho}(\gamma)\cap\partial\Sigma=\emptyset,\quad\quad\mathrm{Image}[H_{1}(\gamma)\rightarrow H_{1}(U_{\rho}(\gamma))]\nequiv 0.

where
U_{\rho}(\gamma)\vcentcolon=\{x\in\Sigma:\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(x,\gamma)\leq\rho\}
, Gromov and Lawson
[
4
, Theorem
10.2
]
showed
\rho\leq\pi
. Furthermore they
[
4
, Remark
10.6
]
conjecture that the best possible conclusion would be
\rho\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}
which is achieved by
M=\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2})\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
\Sigma=\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2})\subset M
, and
\gamma
be the great circle in
\Sigma
.

On the other hand, Schoen-Yau
[
12
, Proof of Proposition
1
]
showed that
\rho\leq\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\pi
; which implies that the diameter of any complete stable minimal surface in a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{3},g)
with scalar curvature
R(g)\geq 1
has upper bound
\frac{2\sqrt{6}\pi}{3}
.

In this note, we show Schoen-Yau’s diameter bound for stable minimal surfaces is sharp, which is a corollary of the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete stable minimal surface in a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{3},g)
with scalar curvature
R(g)\geq 1
. Then

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma)<\frac{2\sqrt{6}\pi}{3}.

The upper bound is sharp in the following sense: there is a sequence of complete
3
-dim manifolds
M_{k}\vcentcolon=(S^{2}\times S^{1},g_{k})
with
R(g_{k})\geq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and compact stable minimal surfaces
\Sigma_{k}\subseteq M_{k}
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma_{k})=\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}\pi
.

The strictness of (
1.2
) is obtained by a simple observation, the main contribution of this note is the construction of the example manifolds, which shows the sharpness of this upper bound.

From
[
6
, 4.C]
, we know

\displaystyle\mathrm{FillRad}(M)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{width}_{n-1}(M)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Diam}(M)

for complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, where
\mathrm{FillRad}(M)
is the filling radius and
\mathrm{width}_{n-1}(M)
is the
(n-1)
-th Uryson width, defined as in
[
6
, section 4]
.

The results in section
3
show that
for a complete stable minimal surface
\Sigma
in a complete
3
-dim Riemannian manifold
(M^{3},g)
with scalar curvature

\displaystyle\mathrm{FillRad}(\Sigma)<\frac{\sqrt{6}\pi}{3}
.

Gromov
[

If

The conjecture has been partially answered by

In

In

The organization of this paper is as follows. In section

2.
The first eigenvalue and the diameter

Definition 2.1
.

For a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
and
w\in C^{\infty}(M^{n})
, we define

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(-\Delta+w)\vcentcolon=\inf_{f\in H_{c}^{1}(M^{n})}\frac{\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}+w\cdot f^{2}}{\int_{M}f^{2}},

where
H_{c}^{1}(M)
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(M^{n})
in
H^{1}(M^{n})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with
w\in C^{\infty}(M^{n}),\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, assume
\lambda_{1}(-\Delta+w)\geq\lambda
, then there is
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M^{n})
satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi>0,\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad-\Delta\varphi+(w-\lambda)\cdot\varphi\geq 0.

Proof:
By definition of
\lambda_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(-\Delta+(w-\lambda))

\displaystyle=\inf_{f\in H_{c}^{1,2}(M^{n})}\frac{\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}+(w-\lambda)\cdot f^{2}}{\int_{M}f^{2}}=\inf_{f\in H_{c}^{1,2}(M^{n})}\frac{\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}+w\cdot f^{2}}{\int_{M}f^{2}}-\lambda

(2.1)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}(-\Delta+w)-\lambda\geq 0.

The compact case follows from similar argument of
[
3
, Theorem
8.38
]
. The non compact case follows from
[
2
, Theorem
1
]
.

∎

Theorem 2.3
.

For any
\beta>\frac{1}{4}
and
\lambda>0
, assume
\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{\Sigma}+\beta\cdot K_{\Sigma})\geq\lambda
on a complete
2
-dim Riemannian manifold
(\Sigma,g)
, where
K_{\Sigma}
is the sectional curvature of
(\Sigma,g)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma)<\frac{2\beta\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda\cdot(4\beta-1)}}.

The upper bound is sharp in the following sense: there is a sequence of complete Riemannian manifolds
\Sigma_{k}=(S^{2},g_{k})
with
\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}+\beta\cdot K_{\Sigma_{k}})\geq\lambda
and

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma_{k})=\frac{2\beta\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda\cdot(4\beta-1)}}.

Proof:

Step (1)
. By Lemma

\displaystyle-\Delta v+(\beta\cdot K_{\Sigma}-\lambda)v\geq 0.

Let

\displaystyle-\Delta u+(K_{\Sigma}-\lambda\beta^{-1})u+(1-\beta)u^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq 0.

Case(i):If
\Sigma
is compact, let
p,q\in\Sigma
be two points with largest distance. Define
I[\gamma]
by

\displaystyle I[\gamma]=\int_{\gamma}u\mathrm{d}s,

where
\mathrm{d}s
is arclength along
\gamma
and
\gamma
is any curve from
p
to
q
.

Case(ii): If
\Sigma
is non compact, chose
p\in\Sigma
and
R=\frac{2\beta\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda\cdot(4\beta-1)}}
, Define
I[\gamma]
by

\displaystyle I[\gamma]=\int_{\gamma}u\mathrm{d}s,

where
ds
is arclength along
\gamma
and
\gamma
is any curve from
p
to
\partial B_{R}(p)
.

Since
u
is positive, for any curve
\gamma:[0,L]\to M
connecting
p
and
\partial B_{R}(p)

L_{1}\in(0,L)

\gamma(L_{1})\in\partial B_{R}(p)

\gamma_{1}
be the restriction of
\gamma
on
[0,L_{1}]
, we have

\displaystyle I[\gamma]=\int_{\gamma}uds>\int_{\gamma_{1}}uds=I[\gamma_{1}].

So

\displaystyle\inf_{\gamma\subset M}I[\gamma]=\inf_{\mathrm{Int}(\gamma)\subset B_{R}(p)}I[\gamma],

where
\mathrm{Int}(\gamma)
is the interior of
\gamma
.

In both cases, there are at least one minimizer of
I[\cdot]
. Let
\gamma_{0}:[0,l]\to M
be one of the minimizers of
I[\cdot]
and parametrized with unit speed.

Step (2)
. Let
V(s)=\varphi(s)\cdot\vec{n}
be the variation vector field along
\gamma_{0}(s)
, where
\vec{n}
is the unit normal vector field along
\gamma
and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\varphi(s)=u^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\gamma_{0}(s))\psi(s),\quad\quad\psi(s)=\sin\Big(\frac{\pi}{l}s\Big).

The first variation formula yields

(2.4)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\partial s}\partial s,\vec{n}\rangle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{n}}\ln u.

The non-negativity of the second variation of
I
at
\gamma_{0}
gives

(2.5)

\displaystyle 0\leq\delta^{2}I[\gamma_{0}]=\int_{\gamma_{0}}[D^{2}u(\vec{n},\vec{n})-2u\cdot(\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{n}}\ln u)^{2}]\cdot\varphi^{2}+u\cdot\Big((\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-K_{\Sigma}\cdot\varphi^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}s.

Note
\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma}u=D^{2}u(\vec{n},\vec{n})+D^{2}u(\partial s,\partial s)
, combining (
2.4
), we get

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma}u

\displaystyle=D^{2}u(\vec{n},\vec{n})+D^{2}u(\partial s,\partial s)=D^{2}u(\vec{n},\vec{n})+u^{\prime\prime}-(\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{n}}\ln u)^{2}\cdot u,

where
u^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}u(\gamma_{0}(s))
.

Plugging (
2.6
) into (
2.5
), using (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma_{0}}(\Delta_{\Sigma}u-u^{\prime\prime}-u\cdot(\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{n}}\ln u)^{2})\cdot\varphi^{2}+u\cdot\Big((\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-K_{\Sigma}\cdot\varphi^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma_{0}}\Big((1-\beta)|\nabla u|^{2}u^{-1}-u^{\prime\prime}-u\cdot(\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec{n}}\ln u)^{2}\Big)\cdot\varphi^{2}+u\cdot\Big((\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-\lambda\beta^{-1}\varphi^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}s.

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma_{0}}\Big((1-\beta)(u^{\prime})^{2}u^{-1}-u^{\prime\prime}\Big)\cdot\varphi^{2}+u\cdot\Big((\varphi^{\prime})^{2}-\lambda\beta^{-1}\varphi^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}s.

Plugging (
2.3
) into (
2.7

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma_{0}}\Big[(1-\beta)(u^{\prime})^{2}u^{-2}\psi^{2}+u^{\prime}(-u^{-2}u^{\prime}\psi^{2}+2u^{-1}\psi\psi^{\prime})+u(-\frac{1}{2}u^{-\frac{3}{2}}u^{\prime}\psi+u^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{\prime})^{2}-\lambda\beta^{-1}\psi^{2}\Big]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma_{0}}\Big[(1+\frac{1}{4}(\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{-1})(\psi^{\prime})^{2}-\lambda\beta^{-1}\psi^{2}-\Big((\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{\prime}u\psi-\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{\prime}\Big)^{2}\Big]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma_{0}}\Big[(1+\frac{1}{4}(\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{-1})(\psi^{\prime})^{2}-\lambda\beta^{-1}\psi^{2}\Big]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{l}\Big[(1+\frac{1}{4}(\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{-1})(\frac{\pi}{l})^{2}\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi}{l}s)-\lambda\beta^{-1}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{l}s)\Big]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\Big[\frac{4\beta}{4\beta-1}(\frac{\pi}{l})^{2}-\lambda\beta^{-1}\Big]\cdot\frac{l}{2}.

So we get

. If
l=\frac{2\beta\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda\cdot(4\beta-1)}}
, we get

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{l}\Big((\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{\prime}u\psi-\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{\prime}\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}s=0.

Note
(\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{\prime}u\psi-\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{\prime}
is a continuous function on

, so

(2.9)

\displaystyle(\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{\prime}u\psi-\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\frac{1}{4})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{\prime}\equiv 0,\quad\forall s\in[0,l].

However, for
s=0
, we have
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi^{\prime}(0)=\frac{\pi}{l}
, which is the contradiction.

Now we have
l<\frac{2\beta\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda\cdot(4\beta-1)}}
. If
\Sigma
is not compact, from the definition of

\displaystyle l\geq d(p,\partial B_{R}(p))=\frac{2\beta\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda\cdot(4\beta-1)}},

which is the contradiction.

Hence
\Sigma
must be compact, and from the definition of

\displaystyle\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma)<\frac{2\beta\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda\cdot(4\beta-1)}}.

3
-dim manifolds

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete stable minimal surface in a complete
3
-dim Riemannian manifold
(M^{3},g)
with scalar curvature
R\geq 1
, then
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{\Sigma}+K_{\Sigma})\geq\frac{1}{2}
, where
K_{\Sigma}
is the sectional curvature of
\Sigma
.

Proof:
Let
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
be an orthonormal frame defined locally on
\Sigma
with
e_{1},e_{2}
tangential and
e_{3}
be unit normal. Since
\Sigma
is a stable minimal surface, for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Sigma)
we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}-(Rc(e_{3},e_{3})+\sum h_{ij}^{2})f^{2}\geq 0,

where
h_{ij}=<\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j},e_{3}>
is the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
. (see
[
8
, Chapter
1
]
)

Since
\Sigma
is minimal, we have
h_{11}+h_{22}=0
. By Gauss curvature equation, we have
K_{\Sigma}=R_{1212}+h_{11}h_{22}-h_{12}^{2}
, and
R_{ijkl}
is the Ricci curvature tensor of
M
.

So (
3.1
) can be written as

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}-(\frac{1}{2}R-K_{\Sigma}+\frac{1}{2}\sum h_{ij}^{2})f^{2}\geq 0.

Since
R\geq 1
, we get

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}+(K_{\Sigma}-\frac{1}{2})f^{2}\geq 0,

which implies
\lambda_{1}(-\Delta+K_{\Sigma})\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete stable minimal surface in a complete
3
-dim Riemannian manifold
(M^{3},g)
with scalar curvature
R\geq 1
, then

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma)<\frac{2\sqrt{6}\pi}{3}.

The upper bound is sharp in the following sense: there is a sequence of complete
3
-dim manifolds
M_{k}\vcentcolon=(S^{2}\times S^{1},g_{k})
with
R\geq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and compact stable minimal surfaces
\Sigma_{k}\subseteq M_{k}
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma_{k})=\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}\pi
.

Proof:

Step (1)
. The inequality (
3.2
) follows from Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
2.3
for the case
\beta=1,\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
.

Let
g_{k}=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+f_{k}^{2}(r)\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\zeta_{k}^{2}(r)\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}
, where
(r,\theta)
is the polar coordinate on
S^{2}
,
\phi
is the coordinate on
S^{1}
, and
f_{k}
,
\zeta_{k}
are defined as in (
2.23
) and (
2.24
).

Note
r\in[0,2l_{k}]
, then by (
2.22
) and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Diam}(\Sigma_{k})=\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}\pi.

Define
\Sigma_{k}=S^{2}\times\{\phi_{0}\}\subseteq M_{k}
in the rest argument.

If the
r
-coordinate of
p\in M_{k}
is
0
or
2l_{k}
, by the definition of
M_{k}
, the
k^{-1}
-neighborhood of
p
in
M_{k}
is an open set in
\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2})\times\mathbb{S}^{1}(1)
; where
\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2})
is a round sphere with radius
\sqrt{2}
,
\mathbb{S}^{1}(1)
is a round unit circle and
\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2})\times\mathbb{S}^{1}(1)
has the product metric.

The
k^{-1}
-neighborhood of
p
in
\Sigma
is an open set of
\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2})\times\{\phi_{0}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2})\times\mathbb{S}^{1}(1)
for some
\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Since
\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2})
is a minimal surface of
\mathbb{S}^{2}(\sqrt{2})\times\mathbb{S}^{1}(1)
with the product metric, we get the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{k}
is
0
in a neighborhood of
p
.

Step (2)
. Now we assume
p\in\Sigma_{k}
, where
\phi_{0}\in S^{1}
, and the
r
-coordinate of
p
is not equal to
0
or
2l_{k}
.

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}=\{\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\frac{1}{f_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\}
be a local orthonormal frame on a neighborhood of
p
in
M_{k}
, we have
e_{1},e_{2}
is tangential and
e_{3}
is unit normal. The second fundamental form of
\Sigma_{k}
is defined by symmetric quadratic tensor
h_{ij}=<\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j},e_{3}>
,
i,j\in\{1,2\}
, where
\nabla
is the Riemannian connection of
M_{k}
.

Let
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}g^{lk}\left(\frac{\partial g_{jl}}{\partial x_{i}}+\frac{\partial g_{il}}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\right)
be the Christoffel symbol, where
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}
are
\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}
respectively, and
(g_{ij})
is the matrix corresponding to the metric of
M_{k}
.

By direct computation, we get
\Gamma_{33}^{1}=-\zeta_{k}\frac{\partial\zeta_{k}}{\partial r}
,
\Gamma_{13}^{3}=\Gamma_{31}^{3}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}}\frac{\partial\zeta_{k}}{\partial r}
,
\Gamma_{22}^{1}=-f_{k}\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial r}
, and
\Gamma_{12}^{2}=\Gamma_{21}^{2}=\frac{1}{f_{k}}\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial r}
. Other Christoffel symbols are zero.

We have

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}(\frac{1}{f_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta})=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(\frac{1}{f_{k}})\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\frac{1}{f_{k}^{2}}\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{1}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{2}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{(\frac{1}{f_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta})}(\frac{1}{f_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta})=\frac{1}{f_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}(\frac{1}{f_{k}})\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}-\frac{1}{f_{k}}\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}.

We get
\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j}
is in the tangent plane of
\Sigma_{k}
, and

\displaystyle h_{ij}=0,\quad\quad\quad\forall i,j\in\{1,2\}.

So on a neighborhood of
p
in
\Sigma_{k}
, we have the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{k}
is
0
.

So we get
\Sigma_{k}
is a minimal surface.

Step (3)
. Since
h_{ij}=0
, we get the scalar curvature of
M_{k}
is
2K_{\Sigma_{k}}+2Rc(e_{3},e_{3})
, where
K_{\Sigma_{k}}
is the sectional curvature of
\Sigma_{k}
.

By the definition of Ricci curvature tensor, we have

\displaystyle R_{ij}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\Gamma_{ik}^{k}+\Gamma_{ij}^{s}\Gamma_{sk}^{k}-\Gamma_{ik}^{s}\Gamma_{sj}^{k}.

We get

\displaystyle Rc(e_{3},e_{3})=\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}^{2}}Rc(\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi},\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi})=-\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}}\cdot(\frac{\partial^{2}\zeta_{k}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{1}{f_{k}}\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial r}\frac{\partial\zeta_{k}}{\partial r}).

On
\Sigma_{k}
, we have
\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{f_{k}^{\prime}}{f_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{f_{k}^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}
, and
K_{\Sigma_{k}}=-\frac{f_{k}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{k}}
, where
\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}
is the Laplacian operator on
\Sigma_{k}
.

Define
u_{k}(r,\theta)=\zeta_{k}(r)
, then
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma_{k})
. So
Rc(e_{3},e_{3})=-\frac{\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}u_{k}}{u_{k}}
.

By (
2.25
), we get

(3.3)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}u_{k}+(K_{\Sigma_{k}}-\frac{1}{2})u_{k}=0.

We get the scalar curvature of
M_{k}
is

\displaystyle R(g_{k})=2K_{\Sigma_{k}}-2\frac{\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}u_{k}}{u_{k}}=1.

Step (4)
. Let
w_{k}=\ln u_{k}
, from (
3.3
) we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}w_{k}=\frac{\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}g}{g}-|\frac{\nabla g}{g}|^{2}=K_{\Sigma_{k}}-\frac{1}{2}-|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}.

For any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma_{k})
, we multiply both sides of (
3.4
) by
\varphi^{2}
and take integration on
\Sigma_{k}
to get

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{k}}-2\varphi\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla w_{k}=\int_{\Sigma_{k}}(K_{\Sigma_{k}}-\frac{1}{2})\varphi^{2}-\varphi^{2}\cdot|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}.

Note

(3.6)

\displaystyle-2\varphi\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla w_{k}\geq-|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-\varphi^{2}\cdot|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}.

By (
3.6
) and (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{k}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+(K_{\Sigma_{k}}-\frac{1}{2})\varphi^{2}\geq 0,\quad\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma_{k}).

Since
K_{\Sigma_{k}}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{\Delta_{\Sigma_{k}}u_{k}}{u_{k}}=-Rc(e_{3},e_{3})
and
h_{ij}=0
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{k}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-(Rc(e_{3},e_{3})+\sum h_{ij}^{2})\varphi^{2}\geq 0,\quad\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma_{k}).

So
\Sigma_{k}
is a stable minimal surface of
M_{k}
.

∎

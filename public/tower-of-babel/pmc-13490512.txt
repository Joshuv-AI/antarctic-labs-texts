Investigation of the guaiazulene–p-chloranil charge transfer complex in solution: a combined spectroscopic and quantum chemical approach
Authors: Arunima Ghosh, Shalmali Bhattacharya, Shrabanti Banerjee, Viki Bhakta, Srimanta Pakhira, Sumanta Bhattacharya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The spontaneous interaction between the electron-rich guaiazulene (D) and the electron-deficient p-chloranil (A) in solution leads to the formation of a stable donor–acceptor charge-transfer (CT) complex, which is characterized by a distinct near-infrared CT absorption band centered at 808 nm. The present study aims to elucidate the nature, strength, and electronic origin of this interaction through a combined experimental and theoretical approach. Absorption spectroscopy and fluorescence quenching experiments reveal significant donor–acceptor association, yielding binding constants of 20,830 dm³ mol⁻¹ and 43,560 dm³ mol⁻¹, respectively, thereby indicating strong intermolecular electronic coupling in solution. To gain deeper insight into the structural and electronic features of the complex, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed in both gas phase and non-polar solvent environments to obtain optimized geometries and analyze the ground- and excited-state electronic structures of the D–A system. The theoretical results suggest a favorable donor–acceptor alignment that facilitates efficient orbital overlap between the π-electron system of guaiazulene and the low-lying π* orbitals of p-chloranil. Furthermore, excited-state properties investigated using Time-Dependent Density Functional Theory (TDDFT) show excellent agreement with experimental findings, predicting a CT transition energy of 1.534 eV, which closely matches the experimentally observed value of 1.537 eV. The strong correlation between spectroscopic observations and theoretical predictions provides a comprehensive understanding of the electronic interactions governing the formation of the guaiazulene–p-chloranil charge-transfer complex and highlights the role of intermolecular donor–acceptor coupling in stabilizing the non-covalent assembly.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47763-5.

Introduction

The study of donor–acceptor (D-A) complexes has long been a subject of considerable interest in chemical physics and materials science due to their vital role in understanding intermolecular interactions, electron transfer processes, and the design of functional molecular materials. These complexes are formed through non-covalent interactions between an electron-rich donor and an electron-deficient acceptor, resulting in a partial transfer of electronic charge that gives rise to unique optical and electronic properties. Investigations of charge transfer (CT) complexes provide valuable insights into the fundamental mechanisms governing photoinduced charge separation, molecular conductivity, and photochemical reactivity. Moreover, such studies are essential for developing advanced materials for organic electronics, photovoltaics, and photonic devices1–6.

Among various π-donor systems, guaiazulene (D) − a bicyclic aromatic hydrocarbon and a substituted derivative of azulene − has attracted attention due to its rich π-electron density, low ionization potential, and structural stability. Its extended conjugation makes it a promising electron donor for forming CT complexes with strong π-acceptors. On the other hand, p-chloranil, i.e., tetrachloro-1,4-benzoquinone (A) serves as an efficient electron acceptor because of its high electron affinity and capacity to stabilize charge-separated states through π–π and van der Waals interactions. The interaction between guaiazulene and p-chloranil thus represents a prototypical donor–acceptor system suitable for investigating fundamental aspects of CT interactions in solution.

In the present work, the interaction between D (SCHEME 1(a)) and A (SCHEME 1(b)) has been systematically investigated through absorption spectrophotometry and fluorescence quenching measurements. The present study advances the understanding of donor–acceptor charge-transfer (CT) chemistry by providing a combined spectroscopic and theoretical investigation of the interaction between guaiazulene and p-chloranil. Charge-transfer complexes between π-electron donors and quinone-type acceptors have been widely studied since the classical work of Mulliken, which established the theoretical framework for intermolecular CT interactions and their characteristic absorption bands7,8. Quinones such as chloranil are well known for their strong electron-accepting ability and have frequently been employed in the formation of donor–acceptor CT complexes with aromatic hydrocarbons and heteroaromatic donors9,10. However, despite the unique electronic structure of azulene derivatives, systematic investigations of CT interactions involving guaiazulene and quinone acceptors remain limited.

In the present work, the observation of a distinct CT absorption band at 809 nm in the near-infrared region indicates an unusually low-energy donor–acceptor transition, thereby extending the known spectral domain of azulene-based CT complexes. Azulene derivatives possess a non-benzenoid aromatic structure with an intrinsic dipole moment and unusual electronic distribution, which often leads to distinctive photophysical behaviour compared with conventional aromatic donors11,12. The identification of a low-energy CT transition therefore provides new insight into the electronic coupling characteristics of azulene-derived donor systems.

Another important aspect of this study is the integration of absorption spectroscopy, fluorescence quenching analysis, and density functional theory (DFT/TDDFT) calculations within a unified framework. The determination of binding constants using two independent experimental techniques provides cross-validated thermodynamic evidence for the formation of the donor–acceptor complex. Such parallel experimental validation is relatively uncommon in investigations of weak intermolecular CT systems and strengthens the reliability of the derived association parameters.

Furthermore, the excellent agreement between the experimentally obtained CT transition energy (1.532 eV) and the TDDFT-calculated value (1.534 eV) demonstrates the strong predictive capability of modern density functional approaches in describing donor–acceptor electronic transitions. Previous studies have shown that hybrid and meta-hybrid density functionals can reliably reproduce excitation energies and charge-transfer characteristics in molecular complexes13,14. The close correspondence observed here enables a detailed mechanistic interpretation of frontier molecular orbital interactions, electronic redistribution, and excited-state characteristics governing the CT process.

The present study differs fundamentally from our previously published work on the azulene–p-chloranil system reported in recent past15 in terms of molecular design, electronic properties, and methodological depth. In the earlier study, unsubstituted azulene interacted with p-chloranil in toluene to produce a charge-transfer (CT) absorption band at 528.9 nm, corresponding to a transition in the visible region (~ 2.35 eV), with an average binding constant of (3.67 ± 0.25) × 104 dm3 mol-1. In contrast, the current investigation employs guaiazulene, a methyl-substituted derivative of azulene, whose enhanced electron-donating character significantly alters the donor–acceptor interaction. This structural modification results in a pronounced bathochromic shift of the CT band to 809 nm (1.537 eV), thereby extending the charge-transfer interaction into the near-infrared region. Such a substantial red shift (~ 280 nm) reflects a reduced HOMO–LUMO gap and stronger electronic coupling, placing the present system in a distinctly different and lower-energy electronic regime compared to the earlier complex.

Moreover, while the previous study15 primarily determined the binding constant from CT absorption data (supported by proton NMR and fluorescence microscopy), the present work evaluates association constants independently using both absorption spectroscopy and fluorescence quenching methods, yielding values of 2.083 × 10⁴ and 4.356 × 104 dm3 mol-1, respectively. This dual-technique validation provides enhanced thermodynamic reliability. Importantly, the current study also demonstrates exceptionally close agreement between experimental and theoretical CT transition energies, with the experimentally observed value of 1.537 eV matching the TDDFT-predicted value of 1.534 eV (ΔE = 0.003 eV). This level of energetic precision, particularly in the low-energy NIR domain, underscores the robustness of the computational model and represents a deeper experimental–theoretical integration than in the previous report. Collectively, the present work explores a substantially different electronic landscape and advances the understanding of azulene-derived donor systems in near-infrared charge-transfer chemistry.

Overall, the present work establishes the formation of a stable guaiazulene–p-chloranil CT complex and provides a quantitatively validated experimental–computational framework for understanding low-energy donor–acceptor interactions. These findings contribute to the broader understanding of azulene-based CT systems and may support the rational design of near-infrared donor–acceptor assemblies with potential applications in optoelectronic materials and molecular sensing.

Materials and experimental

A is obtained from Sigma-Aldrich, USA having 99.8% purity. D is collected directly from Sigma-Aldrich, USA with more than 99% purity. UV-vis spectroscopic grade toluene (purchased from Spectrochem, India) is used as solvent which provides additional photostability and solubility to both A and D during photophysical measurements. UV-vis spectral investigations are carried out in a UV-2450 model spectrophotometer. Steady state fluorescence spectra of the samples are measured in a Hitachi F-7000 model spectrofluorimeter. For the time correlated single photon counting (TCSPC) measurements, D was excited at 374 nm with a laser diode in an HORIBA Jobin Yvon Single Photon Counting set up. The typical full width half maxima (fwhm) of the system responded using a liquid scatter which is measured to be 200 ps. The repition rate was estimated to be 1 MHz. The fluorescence decay curves were analysed using IBH DAS6 software. DFT calculations are done with SPARTAN’24 V1.2.0 windows Version Software (Wavefunction, USA) in a Pentium IV Computer. The ground-state geometry of both A and D are optimized independently, at first, by molecular mechanics force field calculations followed by semiempirical PM3 calculations. The B3LYP hybrid DFT functional along with 6-311 + G** basis sets has been selected in our present work for correct and precise prediction of the non-covalent interaction of between D and A. The 6-311 + G** basis set offers a significant advantage in DFT calculations due to its balance between computational efficiency and high accuracy16,17. As a triple-zeta split-valence basis set with polarization functions on both heavy atoms and hydrogen, it provides a flexible and detailed representation of the electron density within molecules. The triple-split valence feature allows the basis set to accurately describe variations in electron distribution during chemical bonding, which is crucial for reliable geometry optimization and energy predictions. The inclusion of polarization functions (denoted by the two asterisks) enables electron clouds to distort appropriately, improving the description of molecular geometries, dipole moments, hydrogen bonding, and reaction barriers. Compared to smaller basis sets such as 6-31G*, 6-311 + G** offers greater precision in modelling molecular properties without a prohibitive increase in computational cost. It is also well-suited for both DFT and post-Hartree−Fock methods and has been extensively benchmarked against experimental data, making it a trusted and widely used choice for studying organic and inorganic systems. Other than B3LYP functional, we have employed M06-2X and M08-HX approximations in present work for precise determination CT absorption maxima (λCT) and electronic structure of D-A system in both gas phase and also in non-polar solvent. The M06-2X functional offers several advantages over M08-HX in DFT calculations, particularly, in predicting accuracy, robustness and computational efficiency. Developed by Truhlar and co-workers, M06-2X is a hybrid meta-GGA functional incorporating 54% Hartree–Fock exchange and is optimized for main-group thermochemistry, kinetics, and non-covalent interactions13. It provides a highly reliable balance between accuracy and computational cost, performing consistently well across a wide range of organic and inorganic systems. In contrast, M08-HX, though a newer high-exchange functional, tends to exhibit less consistent performance outside its training set and often shows numerical instability and grid sensitivity, requiring tighter integration grids and longer computation times18. M06-2X also benefits from extensive benchmark validation and widespread implementation in major computational chemistry packages, making it one of the most trusted and widely adopted functional in the DFT community. Moreover, its compatibility with commonly used basis set, such as 6-311 + G**, allows for accurate modelling of molecular geometries, reaction barriers, and non-covalent interactions without significant computational overhead. Therefore, M06-2X is generally preferred over M08-HX due to its superior transferability, numerical stability, and broad applicability in main-group chemistry19. The quantum chemical calculations performed in non-polar solvent is done on the basis of C-PCM model. The M06-2X functional has been widely employed in density functional theory (DFT) studies due to its balanced performance in thermochemistry, kinetics, and non-covalent interactions. In particular, M06-2X has been reported to provide reliable descriptions of π–π interactions, dispersion-dominated systems, and certain charge-transfer complexes, while maintaining reasonable computational efficiency for medium-sized molecular systems13,19,20. However, it should be noted that the M08-HX functional was subsequently developed as an advancement within the Minnesota family of functionals and generally demonstrates comparable or improved accuracy for a broad range of chemical applications, including main-group thermochemistry, reaction barrier heights, and non-covalent interactions18.

Nevertheless, in specific applications—such as modelling charge-transfer interactions and predicting UV–Vis spectral features of donor–acceptor complexes—M06-2X has been reported to provide consistent and reliable results in several studies13,19. Therefore, in the present work M06-2X was selected as a suitable functional for investigating the electronic structure and spectroscopic properties of the guaiazulene–p-chloranil charge-transfer complex.

Results and discussions

Absorption spectroscopic studies

Before discussing the ground-state interaction between guaiazulene (D) and p-chloranil (A), it is important to briefly describe the characteristic electronic transitions occurring in their uncomplexed forms, both recorded in toluene. The UV–vis absorption spectrum of the uncomplexed form of D and A in toluene exhibit intense absorption features due to their conjugated aromatic systems. For D, a strong and well-resolved broad absorption band with lower value of molar extinction coefficient appears in the visible region of 400 to 750 nm (Fig. 1S(a)). In addition, D exhibits three absorption peaks, namely, one major peak at 292 nm along with one peak (having lower intensity) around 350 nm and another at 600 nm (Fig. 1S(a)). Very recently, the mononuclear anion radical of guaiazulene was synthesized by Bernal et al., and the corresponding data obtained from synthesis, solution NMR spectroscopy, and single-crystal X-ray diffraction studies were integrated into a coherent framework and this comprehensive analysis enabled a direct comparison and correlation of the structural, electronic, and spectroscopic features derived from each experimental technique21. The absorption spectrum of uncomplexed A, recorded against toluene as reference, is presented in Fig. 1S(b). Upon spontaneous mixing of D and A, a new absorption band appears in the visible region of the spectrum, which does not correspond to either the donor or acceptor alone. This new band is attributed to the formation of a charge-transfer (CT) complex. When the mixed solution of donor and acceptor produces a spectral pattern distinct from the individual components, the longest wavelength band is generally identified as the CT band. Accordingly, in Fig. 1(a), the peak observed at the longer wavelength side of the D-A spectrum is assigned as the CT absorption peak. For accurate determination of CT absorption parameters, the spectra were analyzed using the Gaussian fitting model developed by Gould et al.22. The resulting CT absorption wavelength (λCT) values are listed in Table 1, and a representative Gaussian fit of the D-A system is shown in Fig. 1(b). It is to be mentioned here that CT interaction of p-chloranil with various electron donors like thiazolidine and Corrole-Appended Persubstituted Benzofurans are reported by Eldaroti et al.23 and Gryko et al.24, respectively.

UV-Visible investigations for the determination of K of D−A system

Absorption spectrophotometric measurements reveal that the spontaneous mixing of D with A in toluene produces significant changes in the absorbance value measured at 292 nm absorption band of D (Fig. 2(a)). During the titration experiment, a marked decrease in the absorbance value measured at 292 nm of D was observed with the gradual addition of A at concentrations ranging from 8.134 × 10-6 to 1.220 × 10-4 mol dm-3; the concentration of D is kept fixed at 7.564 × 10-6 mol dm-3.

The Job’s method of continuous variation further supports the formation of a 1:1 stoichiometric complex between D and A (Fig. 2(b)). Khan et al. have also employed the Job’s continuation variation method for the evaluation of the stoichiometry of a newly designed CT complex toward nitrobenzene and Co2+34. Same method is also being utilized by Najnin et al. for the determination of stoichiometry for the CT complex between 1,2-dimethylimidazole and picric acid in different polar solvents35. The strength of the intermolecular association was evaluated by determining the binding constant (K) from UV–Vis absorption data using the well-known Benesi–Hildebrand (BH) equation36. The obtained data (Table 1S) exhibited an excellent linear correlation consistent with the BH model, as expressed in Eq. Notably, this value exhibits ~ 21 times higher compared to the CT complex reported for the interaction between 1,2,3,4-tetrahydroisoquinoline and p-chloranil (K = 1.31 × 103 dm3 mol− 1) recorded in acetonitrile37. The affinity of undergoing spontaneous binding of p-chloranil with guaiazulene is considered to be huge compared to the situation when the same acceptor binds with various drug molecules, e.g., naproxene (K = 16.96 dm3 mol− 1), nerolin (K = 17.76 dm3 mol− 1), methyl anthranilate (K = 15.56 dm3 mol− 1), isoxsuprine (K = 6.96 dm3 mol− 1) and nylidrin (K = 6.96 dm3 mol− 1) reported by Reddy et al. at 293 K38. Even more remarkably, the binding constant for the D–A system is about ~ 4.80 and ~ 2.80 times higher in magnitude than that obtained for the complexation of giant N-methylfulleropyrrolidine with a designed free-base and zinc porphyrin, respectively39.

Steady state fluorescence studies

Guaiazulene (D) exhibits a characteristic blue fluorescence with an emission maximum at 398 nm upon excitation at 292 nm. In order to investigate the recognition motif of D toward A during the D–A interaction, the photoinduced behavior of D in presence of A was examined through steady-state fluorescence titration measurements. In this experiment, the concentration of A was systematically varied from 4.0670 × 10-6 mol dm-3 to 1.220 × 10-4 mol dm-3, while maintaining the concentration of D at a fixed value of 7.564 × 10⁻⁶ mol·dm⁻³. All fluorescence measurements were carried out at the micromolar level to eliminate any contribution from the inner filter effect. As anticipated, the fluorescence intensity of D underwent a significant decrease in the presence of A, indicating efficient fluorescence quenching as a result of the D–A interaction. The quenching behavior is clearly illustrated in Fig. 3(a). Notably, a saturation in fluorescence quenching was observed when the concentration of A reached approximately 7.32 × 10-5 mol dm-3, as shown in Fig. 3(b). This saturation trend suggests the formation of a stable donor–acceptor complex and reflects the induced nature of the fluorescence response of D in the presence of A. The steady-state fluorescence data (Table 2S) further reveal that both D and A undergo spontaneous self-assembly in solution, leading to the formation of an intermolecular supramolecular complex stabilized by non-covalent interactions. The binding strength of the D–A system was quantified in terms of the binding constant (K, dm3 mol-1) using the fluorescence version of the Benesi–Hildebrand (BH) equation36. A linear correlation was obtained from the experimental data, as depicted in Fig. 3(c), confirming the validity of the 1:1 stoichiometric association model.

In our present work, we have also performed TCSPC investigations to check the possibility of energy transfer and photoexcited decay mechanism of D following its complexation with A, viz., static and/ diffusion controlled pathway. Computational work

In the present work, explicit quantum chemical calculations at the DFT and TDDFT levels were performed to elucidate the geometry and energy-minimized structure of the D−A system in both gas phase and in solution. The principal aim was to assess the predictive capability of hybrid DFT methods through comparison with experimental observations. Hybrid functional have emerged as powerful tools in computational physics and quantum chemistry due to their improved treatment of exchange–correlation energies. Here, we have adopted M06-2X and M08-HX other than B3LYP functional for predicting molecular geometry, thermochemical parameters like change in complexation energy and for the determination of excitation energies through TDDFT calculations in both gas phase and in non-polar solvent using configuration interaction singlet model41. The M06-2X and M08-HX hybrid meta-GGA functional offer significant advantages over B3LYP in accurately predicting reaction barrier heights, bond dissociation energies, and non-covalent interactions, making them particularly well-suited for exploring potential energy surfaces and understanding complex chemical reaction mechanisms. These functional also provide improved accuracy for a broad range of thermochemical properties, including large-molecule atomization energies and organic isomerization energies. Furthermore, M06-2X and M08-HX perform comparably to, or even better than, range-separated hybrids such as CAM-B3LYP in describing charge-transfer excitations with intermediate spatial overlap, thereby offering a more balanced and reliable depiction of excitation energies across diverse chemical systems42,43. NCI plot of the guaiazulene-p-chloranil complex

Non-covalent interaction (NCI) analysis was employed to further investigate the weak intermolecular interactions present within the guaiazulene-p-chloranil complex. The NCI method provides a real-space visualization of non-covalent interactions based on the reduced density gradient (RDG), enabling identification of attractive and repulsive regions in molecular systems. In this approach, scatter plots are generated by plotting the RDG against the product of the second eigenvalue of the electron density Hessian matrix (λ₂) and the electron density (ρ), i.e., RDG versus sign(λ₂)ρ (a.u.)50. The range of sign(λ₂)ρ considered in this study extends from − 0.1 to + 0.1a.u., which effectively captures weak intermolecular interactions.

In the scatter plots and corresponding isosurface representations (Fig. 6), the guaiazulene-p-chloranil complex exhibits a prominent shaded black region at negative sign(λ₂)ρ values between − 0.100 to -0.040, confirming the presence of significant intermolecular hydrogen bonding. The guaiazulene-p-chloranil complex, also, displays pronounced black regions at the negative sign(λ₂)ρ expanded between − 0.040 and 0.000, suggesting that van der Waals interactions play a substantial role in stabilizing the assemblies. In the isosurface plots, there are three different regions of (λ₂)ρ, namely, -0.100 to -0.040, 0.000 to 0.100 and finally, -0.040 to 0.000 which signifies attractive interactions, repulsive interactions, and weak, dispersive interactions, respectively. Furthermore, the intensity (darkness) of the color correlates with interaction strength, with darker shades corresponding to stronger interactions51. For completeness, the RDG plots and NCI isosurfaces of the individual components (CF, MA, and HFIP) are presented in Fig. 6. The surface density projection map of the guaiazulene-p-chloranil complex (in colour form) is shown in Fig. 7. The surface density projection map of the charge transfer complex formed between guaiazulene and p-chloranil provides a two-dimensional representation of the spatial distribution of electron density within the donor–acceptor assembly. In this complex, guaiazulene acts as an electron-rich π-donor, whereas p-chloranil serves as a strong π-acceptor due to the presence of electron-withdrawing carbonyl and chloro substituents. Upon complex formation, the molecules typically adopt a face-to-face π–π stacked geometry, facilitating intermolecular orbital overlap between the HOMO of guaiazulene and the LUMO of p-chloranil. The projected electron density map, obtained by integrating the three-dimensional electron density along the axis perpendicular to the molecular plane, reveals a noticeable redistribution of charge compared to the isolated monomers. Overall, the surface density projection map substantiates the existence of intermolecular electronic communication, π-stacking stabilization, and partial ionic character within the guaiazulene–p-chloranil charge transfer system.

Topological analysis of the electron density

To elucidate the nature of intermolecular interactions in the charge-transfer assembly formed between guaiazulene and p-chloranil, we performed a comprehensive topological analysis of the electron density within the framework of the Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules (QTAIM). All critical points (CPs), bond paths and interbasin (zero-flux) surfaces were generated and visualized to establish a rigorous real-space description of donor–acceptor interaction.

The molecular graph of the complex retains the full set of intramolecular critical points associated with the aromatic frameworks of both fragments, including (3, − 3) nuclear critical points, (3, − 1) bond critical points (BCPs), and (3, + 1) ring critical points characteristic of π-conjugated systems. Importantly, additional intermolecular (3, − 1) BCPs emerge between facing carbon atoms across the stacked π-interface. These BCPs are located in low-density regions and are associated with small electron density values (ρ_BCP) and positive Laplacians (∇2ρ_BCP > 0), consistent with closed-shell, non-covalent interactions. No evidence of shared-shell (covalent) character is observed in the intermolecular region.

The corresponding bond paths form a continuous network bridging the donor and acceptor moieties, establishing a topological connection across the π–π interface. Although the magnitude of ρ at these intermolecular BCPs remains modest, their presence constitutes a definitive topological signature of interaction. The geometry of these bond paths aligns with the centroid-to-centroid stacking arrangement, confirming that stabilization arises from spatially distributed π–π contacts rather than localized directional bonding.

Visualization of the interbasin zero-flux surfaces further reveals subtle but discernible deformation at the donor–acceptor boundary. In comparison with the isolated monomers, the atomic basins in the complex exhibit slight polarization toward the acceptor fragment, reflecting charge redistribution upon complex formation. However, the atomic basins remain well-defined and non-overlapping, underscoring the absence of covalent bond formation and confirming the weak, closed-shell nature of the interaction.

Collectively, the topological descriptors provide compelling real-space evidence for finite electronic communication between the two fragments. The emergence of intermolecular bond critical points, the formation of bridging bond paths and the polarization of interbasin surfaces together substantiate a dispersion-dominated π–π stacking interaction accompanied by partial ground-state charge transfer. These findings establish a coherent topological framework for understanding stabilization in the guaiazulene–p-chloranil charge-transfer complex and highlight the power of electron-density topology in resolving subtle intermolecular phenomena. To quantify electron sharing and charge delocalization in the charge-transfer complex between guaiazulene and p-chloranil, the localization index (LI) and delocalization index (DI) have been evaluated from the atomic overlap matrix (AOM) within the QTAIM framework. These descriptors provide a quantitative measure of electron localization within atomic basins and electron sharing between atomic pairs, respectively. The detailed results of LI and DI are provided in the Supporting Information. This complementary modulation of basin populations directly reflects ground-state polarization and partial electron migration from donor to acceptor. More revealingly, the intermolecular DI values between facing carbon atoms across the stacked π interface are small yet distinctly non-zero, establishing finite electron sharing between the two fragments. Although these DI magnitudes remain far below those characteristic of covalent bonding, their presence provides a rigorous topological signature of electronic communication beyond purely dispersive stabilization. The spatial distribution of enhanced DI contributions follows the centroid-to-centroid stacking geometry, underscoring the delocalized nature of the interaction. Taken together, the complementary trends in LI and DI furnish quantitative evidence for weak but measurable donor-to-acceptor charge transfer in the ground state. These metrics bridge qualitative NCI visualization and real-space electron-density topology, confirming that stabilization of the guaiazulene–p-chloranil complex arises from dispersion-dominated π–π stacking supplemented by finite interfragment electron delocalization.

We have formulated the orbital DI for a batch of orbitals in our present work. Total orbital DI is estimated to be 99.58. The orbital DI, derived within the framework of the QTAIM, provides a rigorous quantitative measure of electron sharing between atomic or molecular fragments and therefore serves as a powerful descriptor for probing charge-transfer interactions. In donor–acceptor complexes, DI reflects the extent of electronic communication between the donor and acceptor moieties by quantifying the number of electron pairs simultaneously shared between their respective atomic basins. An increase in the intermolecular DI upon complex formation signifies enhanced orbital overlap and partial electron delocalization across the donor–acceptor interface, which is a hallmark of charge-transfer stabilization.

Unlike conventional population analyses that primarily estimate net charge displacement, the delocalization index directly captures the cooperative exchange–correlation contribution to the electron density, thereby providing deeper insight into the bonding nature of the interaction. Elevated DI values between atoms belonging to the donor π-system and the acceptor electron-deficient centers indicate a significant degree of intermolecular orbital mixing, suggesting that the interaction possesses partial covalent character rather than being governed purely by electrostatic or dispersion forces. Consequently, DI analysis complements other descriptors of weak interactions, such as non-covalent interaction (NCI) analysis and charge density topology, by offering a quantitative measure of electronic coupling.

In charge-transfer complexes, the redistribution of electron density is typically accompanied by a reduction in the LI value of donor atoms and a corresponding increase in intermolecular DI values, reflecting the transition from localized to shared electronic character. This delocalization contributes to the stabilization of the guaiazulene-p-chloranil assembly and often correlates with experimentally observed spectroscopic signatures such as charge-transfer absorption bands. Therefore, the orbital delocalization index emerges as a robust topological parameter for elucidating the electronic origin and strength of charge-transfer interactions in molecular complexes.

Orbital localization analysis provides critical insight into the electronic structure and charge redistribution occurring in the guaiazulene–p-chloranil charge transfer complex. In donor–acceptor systems of this type, orbital localization techniques—such as localized molecular orbitals, LI, and related topological descriptors within the framework of the QTAIM —allow the electronic reorganization associated with intermolecular charge transfer to be examined in a spatially resolved manner.

Orbital localization analysis reveals that, upon complex formation, the π-electron density originally localized within the aromatic framework of guaiazulene becomes partially redistributed toward the electron-deficient quinone framework of p-chloranil. This redistribution is manifested as a decrease in the localization index of donor atoms in the guaiazulene π-system, accompanied by increased electronic delocalization toward the acceptor region. Such changes indicate that a fraction of the electron density that was previously confined within the donor fragment becomes shared across the intermolecular interface, confirming the presence of donor–acceptor electronic coupling.

From a bonding perspective, orbital localization analysis provides evidence that the interaction between the two molecular fragments is not governed solely by electrostatic attraction or dispersion forces. Instead, the partial migration of electron density from the donor π-orbitals of guaiazulene to the low-lying π* orbitals of p-chloranil introduces a degree of intermolecular orbital mixing, which stabilizes the complex through charge-transfer interaction. This phenomenon leads to the formation of partially delocalized electronic domains spanning both molecules, a feature that is consistent with the emergence of characteristic charge-transfer absorption bands commonly observed in donor–acceptor complexes.

Furthermore, the combined evaluation of localization and delocalization descriptors provides a quantitative framework for distinguishing localized electronic domains from regions of intermolecular electron sharing. In the guaiazulene–p-chloranil complex, the observed reduction in orbital localization within the donor fragment, together with the redistribution of electron density toward the acceptor moiety, highlights the fundamental role of orbital delocalization in stabilizing the guaiazulene–p-chloranil assembly. Consequently, orbital localization analysis offers a powerful tool for elucidating the microscopic origin of the charge-transfer interaction and the associated electronic reorganization in this donor–acceptor system.

The electronic structure of the guaiazulene–p-chloranil charge-transfer complex was further investigated through total density of states (TDOS) and overlap population density of states (OPDOS) analyses in order to elucidate the nature of intermolecular orbital interactions governing the donor–acceptor assembly. The TDOS profile reveals a clear redistribution of electronic states upon complex formation, with the frontier occupied states predominantly derived from the π-system of guaiazulene, while the low-lying unoccupied states arise largely from the π* orbitals of p-chloranil. This energetic alignment facilitates efficient donor–acceptor coupling and leads to a noticeable narrowing of the frontier orbital energy separation relative to the isolated components, indicating the formation of hybridized electronic states across the intermolecular interface. Complementary OPDOS analysis provides a direct measure of orbital overlap between the two fragments and demonstrates pronounced positive contributions in the frontier energy region, signifying stabilizing bonding interactions between the donor π orbitals and acceptor π* orbitals. These bonding contributions reflect constructive orbital mixing that promotes partial electron delocalization from guaiazulene toward the electron-deficient quinone framework of p-chloranil. Minor anti-bonding contributions appear only at higher unoccupied energy levels and therefore do not significantly destabilize the complex. Taken together, the TDOS and OPDOS results reveal that the stabilization of the guaiazulene–p-chloranil assembly arises from significant intermolecular orbital coupling and electronic delocalization, providing compelling evidence for the charge-transfer character of the interaction and offering a detailed electronic perspective on the donor–acceptor mechanism underlying the formation of this supramolecular complex. Interpretation of molecular structure, surface minima and maxima of the guaiazulene–p-chloranil charge transfer complex

The molecular structure of the guaiazulene–p-chloranil charge-transfer (CT) complex reveals a typical donor–acceptor arrangement in which the electron-rich π-system of guaiazulene interacts with the electron-deficient quinone framework of p-chloranil. The two molecular fragments adopt a stacked or near-parallel configuration that facilitates effective orbital overlap between the π orbitals of the donor and the π* orbitals of the acceptor. This geometric arrangement enhances intermolecular electronic communication and stabilizes the non-covalent assembly through partial charge transfer from guaiazulene to p-chloranil.

The analysis of the molecular surface extrema, particularly the surface minima and maxima (Fig. 16), provides further insight into the electrostatic characteristics governing the interaction. The surface maxima correspond to regions of positive electrostatic potential, typically associated with hydrogen atoms and electron-deficient areas of the molecular framework. In the present complex, such maxima are often observed around the peripheral hydrogen atoms of guaiazulene, indicating sites capable of participating in weak intermolecular interactions such as C–H···O contacts with the oxygen atoms of p-chloranil.

Conclusions

Key features of the present work may be summarized as follows:

The present study reports the spontaneous interaction between guaiazulene (D) and p-chloranil (A) in solution leading to the formation of a donor–acceptor charge-transfer (CT) complex. A distinct CT absorption band appears in the visible region of the UV–Vis spectrum, from which several important physicochemical parameters, including the oscillator strength, transition dipole moment, resonance energy, and dissociation energy, have been evaluated. In addition, the experimental ionization potential of guaiazulene was estimated for the first time and found to be 7.65 eV. Both UV–Vis absorption and steady-state fluorescence measurements indicate significant interaction between the donor and acceptor molecules. The binding constant determined from the spectroscopic studies shows strong complex formation, with an average value of 3.22 × 104 dm3 mol-1. Furthermore, TDDFT calculations performed using the M06-2X and M08-HX functionals provide strong theoretical support for the experimentally observed CT transition energy of the D–A system. Overall, the combined experimental and theoretical results provide important insight into the electronic interactions within the guaiazulene–p-chloranil donor–acceptor system and suggest potential applications of azulene-based CT complexes in molecular optoelectronic and sensing materials.

Cognitive stimulation exerts long-lasting protective effects on the hippocampal vasculature and memory function in a rat model of vascular cognitive impairment
Authors: Abbie C. Chapman, Anduela Thaqi, Risa L. Kalish, Brian Deng, Gianna M. DeMarco, Aleena Shehryar, Emily J. Baumoel, Phoebe Bergson, Simon Papavangjeli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Hypertensive individuals with vascular cognitive impairment (VCI) often live alone and are less likely to adhere to prescribed medications, making the development of non-pharmacological interventions critical. How cognitive stimulation affects the vasculature of the hippocampus remains unclear but could protect against memory decline during VCI. Memory and hippocampal arteriole (HA) function were assessed in spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHR) of both sexes that either underwent cognitive stimulation or lived in isolation. Isolated SHR were treated with donepezil (acetylcholinesterase inhibitor) to compare lifestyle and pharmacological interventions and investigate the mechanisms by which the hippocampus can be protected during VCI. Cognitive stimulation selectively and persistently improved hippocampal-dependent memory and HA function that was not due to increased BDNF expression. Donepezil improved HA function and rescued memory in isolated, aged SHR. Intermittent cognitive stimulation exerted long-lasting protection on the hippocampus and its vasculature, highlighting the therapeutic potential of HAs and cognitive stimulation in VCI.

Introduction

It is now estimated that dementia affects 1 in 10 people over the age of 65, and incidence continues to rise as the population lives to older ages1,2. Vascular contributions to cognitive impairment and dementia are a leading cause of age-related dementia, second only to Alzheimer’s disease. Vascular cognitive impairment (VCI) consists of a spectrum of cognitive dysfunction due to cerebrovascular disease, with the most severe form being vascular dementia1,3. Importantly, it is now recognized that the majority of individuals clinically diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease have cerebrovascular pathology in addition to the classic Alzheimer’s disease neurodegeneration (e.g., mixed dementia)1,4–6. These recent advancements not only suggest that incidence of VCI is underestimated but also highlight the importance of understanding underlying vascular mechanisms of age-related cognitive decline.

Currently, there are no treatments for VCI. There are, however, preventative strategies that target primary modifiable risk factors, such as chronic hypertension3. Chronic hypertension is the leading cause of cerebrovascular disease and increases the risk for VCI up to 60% compared to normotensive individuals7–10. This is in part due to chronic hypertension resulting in ischemic stroke and post-stroke cognitive impairment, a form of VCI3,11. However, brain regions critically involved in learning and memory, such as the hippocampus, are directly impacted by chronic hypertension independently of stroke3,12,13. The hippocampus is particularly susceptible to hypoxic/ischemic injury, highlighting the importance of maintaining healthy blood flow. In fact, the hippocampal vasculature has recently become recognized as a key contributor to maintaining memory function later in life14–17. We recently reported that chronic hypertension caused progressive hippocampal arteriole (HA) dysfunction, hippocampal hypoperfusion and memory decline that were restored by treatment with the anti-hypertensive drug captopril17. Importantly, up to 50% of people with chronic hypertension do not take their prescribed medication, and up to 100% of people with impaired cognitive function do not adhere to prescribed medications18,19. Thus, non-pharmacological interventions are critical to mitigating VCI.

Cognitive stimulation is known to improve hippocampal function and memory. For example, in healthy individuals in their 70 s, memory training resulted in immediate and sustained improvements in memory function that declined over several years, indicating that stimulating the hippocampus with memory tasks has long-lasting protective effects20. This is largely thought to be due to increased brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) in several brain regions, including the hippocampus that improves neuronal function21. However, whether there are underlying vascular mechanisms remains unclear and may represent a novel therapeutic target, particularly in patients with VCI. In the current study, we first investigated how brief cognitive stimulation with an enriched lifestyle (socialization, cognitive exercise) affected memory function over time in both sexes using an accepted model of VCI, aged spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHR). We investigated HA structure and function, including vasoreactivity to mediators involved in neurovascular coupling (NVC) as potential underlying vascular mechanisms of changes in memory function. We hypothesized that SHR with an enriched lifestyle would have better memory function due to cognitive stimulation increasing hippocampal BDNF levels and exerting protective effects on HA function compared to rats living an isolated life.

Second, to understand potential protective mechanisms and to compare lifestyle changes to pharmacological interventions, we chronically treated age-matched isolated male and female SHR with donepezil, an acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibitor, and then studied HA and memory function. Donepezil is known to increase hippocampal BDNF and is an FDA-approved treatment for mild cognitive impairment and Alzheimer’s disease, although its therapeutic potential in VCI is less clear22,23. Circulating factors and brain levels of BDNF were measured to better understand potential mechanisms by which interventions improved HA and memory function, and whether improvements were sex dependent. We hypothesized that isolated SHR treated with donepezil would have improved memory function due to increased hippocampal BDNF levels and restoration of HA function similar to rats that experienced lifestyle enrichment.

Results

Prolonged effects of an enriched lifestyle on memory function during chronic hypertension

To understand potential sex differences in baseline locomotion and exploratory behavior, several parameters were measured when rats were in an open field arena. In general, female SHR were more active, and traveled a greater distance than males (20.8 ± 1.1 vs. 13.5 ± 0.9, p < 0.01 by t-test), and had a faster average rate of speed compared to male SHR (0.07 ± 0.004 vs. 0.05 ± 0.003, p < 0.01 by t-test). Females had a shorter latency to cross through the center of the arena, crossing the first time in 27.1 ± 6.6 s compared to males that had a latency to cross through the center of 77.3 ± 21.8 s (p < 0.05 by t-test). However, there were no differences in the number of crossings through the center of the arena, with female SHR crossing 10 ± 1 times and males crossing 8 ± 1 times (p > 0.05 by t-test).

To investigate the temporal effects of an enriched lifestyle on memory function, and whether one sex was more sensitive to lifestyle-induced memory improvements, we tested object recognition memory in male and female rats two and six months after being stimulated with behavioral enrichment and naïve rats living an isolated lifestyle. Figure 1a shows the schematic of the novel object recognition (NOR) paradigm used to assess long-term memory function, which is largely hippocampal-dependent. The NOR task is based upon a rodent’s innate preference for novelty and compares the exploration time of a familiar object and a novel object. A rat with intact memory function will spend the majority of its time with the novel object when given the option to explore both objects (recognition index > 0.50). If memory is impaired, then a rat will split its time more evenly between “novel” and “familiar” objects, as it does not remember the object as familiar. All rats explored the identical objects for > 40 s during the acquisition phase. Naïve male and female rats living in isolation had impaired object memory, with recognition indices of ~0.50 (Fig. 1b). There was a main effect of treatment (F2,29 = 24.98, p < 0.0001) on object memory function, with male and female rats undergoing an enriched lifestyle having better memory performance compared to naïve rats (Fig. 1b). Further, there was a main effect of sex (F1,29 = 8.63, p = 0.006) on memory function with males undergoing a more robust memory improvement compared to females at the two-month timepoint (p = 0.018 by post-hoc test) that diminished by the six-month timepoint (p > 0.05 by post-hoc test). An interaction between treatment and sex was trending but did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.086). These data suggest that relatively brief participation in an enriched lifestyle improves long-term memory function that persists for several months after enrichment ends. There were no correlations between body weight or performance in cognitive stimulation tasks (e.g., escape latency in a Morris water maze task, and long-term memory function in any group (data not shown). Spatial working memory largely involves prefrontal cortex signaling and was tested using a continuous Y maze task24,25. If a rat has intact working memory, the rat will alternate from arm A to B to C without making a reversal entry (an error), as illustrated in Fig. 2a. An alternation index was calculated as a measure of spatial working memory, with a higher index indicating better working memory performance. There was a main effect of sex on number of arm entries (F1,29 = 17.74, p = 0.0002; Fig. 2b), with males entering less maze arms than females. There was also a main effect of sex on alternation index (F1,29 = 15.03, p = 0.0006; Fig. 2c), with males demonstrating higher indices compared to females. More specifically, spatial working memory was improved two months after lifestyle enrichment ended, an effect that occurred only in male rats and appeared transient, as spatial working memory function had decreased to be similar to naïve rats by the six-month time point (Fig. 2c). Male rats had less errors than females during the task, as they made fewer reversals into the arm they had been in previously (Fig. 2d). Taken together, the higher alternation index, fewer arm entries and fewer reversals in males indicate an enriched lifestyle improved spatial working memory, albeit temporarily, that did not occur in females. Differential effects of cognitive stimulation on hippocampal vascular function by sex

To investigate the role of the hippocampal vasculature in the long-lasting improvement in object memory function after temporary lifestyle enrichment in our model of VCI, we assessed the function and structure of freshly isolated HAs from the same male and female rats after the six-month memory testing using ex vivo pressure myography. To investigate potential mechanisms by which hippocampal function was improved after an enriched lifestyle, and to compare holistic and pharmacological treatments, we treated a separate group of age-matched naïve (isolated lifestyle) male and female SHR for four weeks with donepezil and then studied HA function and structure. The goal was to study mechanisms of age- and hypertension-related memory deficits; therefore, studies were conducted at ~16 months of age. This is approaching the age when SHR can begin to deteriorate, and, unfortunately, two female SHR and one male SHR died unexpectedly prior to terminal experiments. One female Stim was excluded due to technical difficulties.

Figure 3a shows a transilluminated, cannulated HA with a lumen diameter ~ 30 µm at an intravascular pressure of 60 mmHg in an arteriograph chamber used for pressure myography. This technique allows for investigation of arteriole function independently of neuronal and glial influences. Lumen diameters of HAs from female (Fig. 3b) and male (Fig. 3c) rats that were stimulated by an enriched lifestyle were larger than lumen diameters of HAs from naïve and donepezil-treated rats. Lumen diameters of HAs from all groups remained relatively stable across the intravascular pressure range studied, and there were no differences in myogenic tone between groups at any intravascular pressure (Fig. 3d, e). Lumen diameter of HAs from female Stim rats decreased and myogenic tone increased in response to increased intravascular pressure until 80 mmHg, however, this was not statistically significant. Chronic hypertension is well-known to damage the cerebral endothelium, and we have shown this to also occur in the hippocampus of male SHR16,17. To probe vascular mechanisms by which stimulation with an enriched lifestyle improved memory function in male and female SHR, whether this was similar to donepezil treatment, or if either treatment was more effective in a particular sex, we first focused on endothelial function. Endothelium-derived hyperpolarization (EDH) is a primary vasodilatory pathway that involves small and intermediate conductance calcium-activated potassium (SKCa and IKCa) channels expressed in endothelial cells26. Figure 4a shows representative lumen diameter traces of an HA from a rat in each group in response to cumulative doses of NS309, a SKCa/IKCa channel agonist. Vasodilation occurred in HAs from all groups in response to increasing concentrations of NS309. Lumen diameters of HAs from female (Fig. 4b) and male (Fig. 4c) Stim rats were larger than those from naïve rats in the middle and at the end of the concentration curve, respectively. Vasoreactivity of HAs from Stim female SHR (Fig. 4d) was shifted leftward compared to naïve and Don-treated rats, however, HAs from Don-treated rats had greater vasoreactivity at higher concentrations of NS309 (Fig. 4d). Vasoreactivity of HAs from male rats that were either Stim or Don-treated to NS309 were significantly greater compared to HAs from naïve rats (Fig. 4e). To determine potential sex differences in vasoreactivity to NS309, we compared maximal reactivity of HAs to the highest dose of NS309 (Fig. 4f). Don-treated female rats had increased reactivity of HAs compared to naïve females, whereas both Stim and Don-treated male rats had increased reactivity compared to naïve male rats. There were no differences between sexes in HA reactivity to NS309 in naïve or Don-treated groups, however, stimulation with an enriched lifestyle improved HA function to a greater extent in male compared to female SHR (Fig. 4f). Sensitivity of HAs from female Stim rats was shifted leftward compared to arterioles from naïve and Don-treated rats, however, there were no differences in EC50 values (Supplementary Fig. 1). HAs from both male treatment groups had increased sensitivity to NS309 and lower EC50 values compared to naïve rats (Supplementary Fig. 1). Under healthy, normotensive conditions, SKCa/IKCa channels are basally active in the hippocampal vasculature and provide a constant vasodilatory stimulus to balance vasoconstrictive stimuli and contribute to lumen diameter, myogenic tone and hippocampal blood flow17. To better understand the protective effects of lifestyle intervention or donepezil treatment on HA SKCa/IKCa channel function, we used selective inhibitors of each and quantified changes in lumen diameter (Fig. 5). Arterioles from naïve rats of both sexes had little-to-no change in lumen diameter in response to SKCa (Fig. 5a) or IKCa channel inhibition (Fig. 5b), demonstrating chronic hypertension damages endothelial function by impairing the function of these potassium channels in both sexes. Stimulation with an enriched lifestyle restored HA SKCa channel function in both female and male SHR, as indicated by vasoconstriction in response to channel inhibition with apamin (Fig. 5a). An enriched lifestyle did not, however, improve IKCa channel function in females as it did in arterioles from males (Fig. 5b). NVC is a critical hemodynamic property by which local blood flow increases in response to increased metabolic demand of active neurons. This response involves dynamic communication and a coordinated effort of multiple cell types, including within the vascular wall. The ability of HAs to vasodilate in response to vasoactive signaling molecules generated during neuronal activity is crucial in order to initiate local vasodilation, decrease vascular resistance and increase local blood flow. To investigate if stimulation with lifestyle enrichment during chronic hypertension may improve NVC and memory function, we assessed the critical ability of HAs to vasodilate in response to primary mediators of NVC in the hippocampus – adenosine and nitric oxide (NO). Figure 6a shows lumen diameter traces from an HA from each group to cumulative doses of adenosine. Lumen diameters of HAs from all groups increased as arterioles vasodilated to increasing concentrations of adenosine (Fig. 6b, c). In female Stim rats, HA lumen diameter was larger (Fig. 6b), and there was increased vasoreactivity (Fig. 6d) and sensitivity (Supplementary Fig. 2) to adenosine compared to HAs from female rats that were naïve or treated with donepezil, although maximum reactivity was similar between groups (Fig. 6f). To understand mechanisms by which an enriched lifestyle and donepezil treatment improved hippocampal vascular function, we measured circulating factors known to be involved in endothelial cell activation and dysfunction, endothelin-1 (ET-1) and intercellular adhesion molecule1 (ICAM-1; Supplementary Table 1). There was a main effect of sex, with males having higher levels of circulating ET-1 (F1,27 = 5.13, p = 0.0318) and ICAM-1 (F1,27 = 17.08, p = 0.0003) compared to females (Supplementary Table 1). In the isolated lifestyle groups, male rats had higher levels of circulating ET-1 and ICAM-1 compared to female rats. In males, stimulation with an enriched lifestyle and donepezil treatment decreased circulating ET-1 levels by ~ 30%, although this did not reach statistical significance (Supplementary Table 1). There was no effect of treatments on ICAM-1 levels in males, although there was greater variability. In females, there was no effect of lifestyle enrichment on ET-1 or ICAM-1 levels, however, donepezil treatment reduced circulating ET-1 by ~ 20%, although this was not statistically significant (Supplementary Table 1).

Structural remodeling occurs in HAs during chronic hypertension that limit the vasodilatory capacity of arterioles perfusing the hippocampus, that can result in hypoperfusion and hypoxic/ischemic neuronal injury to contribute to memory dysfunction17. Therefore, interventions that prevent and/or reverse hypertension-induced vascular remodeling in the hippocampus may be protective in maintaining basal hippocampal blood flow. Under fully relaxed conditions, lumen diameters of HAs from female and male rats that were stimulated by an enriched lifestyle were larger at 5 mmHg and 60 mmHg intravascular pressure compared to lumen diameters of arterioles from rats that were either naïve or received donepezil treatment of the same sex (Supplementary Table 2). This was not associated with a change in wall thickness; however, both female and male donepezil treated rats had HAs with thinner walls at 60 mmHg intravascular pressure that resulted in smaller cross-sectional area (CSA). There were no differences between groups in distensibility (Supplementary Table 2).

To understand mechanisms by which an enriched lifestyle may cause outward vascular remodeling, we measured circulating levels of matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9) and its inhibitor tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase 1 (TIMP-1). One MMP-9 sample was excluded due to being a statistical outlier (two standard deviations from the mean). There was a main sex effect, with male rats having higher circulating levels of MMP-9 (F1,26 = 17.23, p = 0.0003) and TIMP-1 (F1,27 = 18.17, p = 0.0002) compared to female rats (Supplementary Table 1). Neither stimulation or donepezil treatment effected MMP-9 or TIMP-1 levels in female rats, and there were similar MMP-9-TIMP-1 ratios between groups. In male rats, however, an enriched lifestyle decreased TIMP-1 levels, resulting in a higher MMP-9-TIMP-1 ratio compared to naïve and donepezil treated male rats (Supplementary Table 1).

Reproductive senescence is a period with depleted female sex steroids that could contribute to treatment efficacy of HA structure and function. Therefore, ovaries were assessed histologically. No female rats had entered reproductive senescence, as demonstrated by the presence of corpus luteum and ovarian follicles at different stages of maturity (Supplementary Fig. 4)27–29.

Donepezil improved memory function to be similar to the normotensive state

To determine if donepezil-induced improvements in HA function improved memory function, we compared long-term memory function of the Naïve and Stim female and male SHR to naïve rats that underwent donepezil treatment and age-matched normotensive controls. Donepezil treatment significantly improved memory function of aged female and male SHR living an isolated lifestyle to the level of normotensive control rats (Supplementary Fig. 5).

Serum and brain levels of BDNF

To understand the mechanisms by which a period of stimulation with lifestyle enrichment exerted long-lasting protective effects on the hippocampus and its vasculature, we measured serum and brain tissue levels of BDNF in hippocampus, prefrontal cortex and parietal cortex. There were no differences between sex or treatment groups in circulating or hippocampal levels of BDNF (Table 1). However, BDNF levels in the hippocampus were higher in general than in the prefrontal cortex and parietal cortices. Interestingly, in male rats, stimulation with lifestyle enrichment and donepezil treatment increased prefrontal cortex levels of BDNF compared to parietal cortex, whereas only donepezil treatment in females increased prefrontal cortex BDNF. Discussion

There are currently no treatments for VCI, putting critical importance on preventative strategies to slow age-related cognitive decline, such as blood pressure control30. Without overt symptoms, hypertension is often left undiagnosed, and of those who are diagnosed, up to 50% of people in the US prescribed antihypertensive medications do not take them31. Further, up to 100% of older individuals who have cognitive impairment do not/are not able to adhere to prescribed medication regimens18,19. Therefore, investigating how holistic interventions such as cognitive stimulation through enriched lifestyles impacts the hippocampus and its vasculature is critical to slow and/or prevent vascular contributions to cognitive impairment and dementia. Here, we showed for the first time that a relatively brief period of lifestyle enrichment had long-lasting protective effects on hippocampal vascular function and selectively improved long-term memory in a chronically hypertensive model of VCI. Interestingly, there were sex differences in the degree of improvement such that males were more responsive to protective effects of lifestyle enrichment compared to females. These findings suggest that underlying mechanisms of age-related hippocampal vascular dysfunction and memory decline differ by sex. Further, we found that donepezil, which is FDA approved for Alzheimer’s disease, improved HA function and memory in both female and male SHR living an isolated lifestyle. These findings emphasize the therapeutic potential of HAs in improving memory in VCI in both sexes. They also provide exciting proof-of-principle evidence that relatively simple lifestyle enrichment protects the hippocampal vasculature and is effective at alleviating VCI, and improving neurocognitive function at older ages.

Non-medication-based interventions have been investigated as an alternative to pharmacological treatments for people with cognitive impairment and dementia. Such holistic interventions for improving learning and memory include physical exercise and cognitive training via “brain games”32–34. Importantly, integrated cognitive and physical exercise has been shown to improve recognition, memory and problem solving abilities in patients in the middle stages of dementia progression32. Engagement in an assortment of daily activities is associated with larger hippocampal volume, indicating that seemingly simple lifestyle enrichment has protective effects on the hippocampus35. The current findings that a brief period of cognitive stimulation has long-lasting protective effects on the hippocampal vasculature and rescues memory function in VCI is a novel underlying vascular mechanism of protective actions of cognitive stimulation. These findings also advance our understanding of the therapeutic potential of HAs. Further, they highlight the tremendous value of keeping older people cognitively engaged, which can be particularly challenging when community dwelling older individuals with cognitive impairment and dementia are more likely to live alone, recently worsened by the COVID-19 pandemic36–38.

In the current study, we found that cognitive stimulation with an enriched lifestyle during chronic hypertension caused long-lasting improvement in object recognition, but not spatial working memory. Object recognition is largely hippocampal-dependent, whereas spatial working memory relies more heavily on prefrontal cortex25,39,40, suggesting that the long-lasting protective effects were domain/region-specific. In contrast to our hypothesis, neither cognitive stimulation nor donepezil treatment caused long-lasting increases in hippocampal BDNF in either sex. There may have been an initial increase in BDNF that resulted in long-lasting neurovascular protection in the hippocampus, and further studies are necessary to investigate a causal relationship of BDNF in improved HA function.

There are sex differences in neurovascular aging in the brain that may contribute to differing mechanisms of VCI and therefore efficacy of interventions between sexes41,42. Two months after enrichment ended, we found that males demonstrated a larger magnitude of improvement in object recognition than females and a transient improvement in spatial working memory. Thus, cognitive stimulation was sufficient to improve multi-domain functioning in males that persisted for a few months, however, females require either a higher degree of or different type of stimulation to improve multiple types of memory. That hippocampal-dependent memory was selectively improved long-term (e.g., 6 months) suggests that although the hippocampus is a brain region most susceptible to injury, it is also highly responsive to protective interventions, including memory tasks, that provoke sustained improvement in hippocampal vascular function in VCI.

The control of local blood flow largely occurs at the level of pre-capillary arterioles and upstream penetrating arterioles, and the major determinant of hippocampal blood flow is the lumen diameter of HAs43. Thus, the structure and function of HAs are critical in maintaining perfusion of the hippocampus and protecting learning and memory function. In healthy rats, SKCa and IKCa channels are basally active in the endothelium of HAs and centrally involved in EDH, a primary mechanism in determining hippocampal blood flow17. Here, we show that social isolation impaired SKCa and IKCa channel function in HAs similarly in aged female and male SHR. Cognitive stimulation improved both SKCa and IKCa channel function in arterioles from male rats, whereas only SKCa channel function was improved in HAs from females. As such, lifestyle enrichment had a greater therapeutic impact on endothelial function in males, which may be due to decreased circulating ET-1. Donepezil treatment, however, improved endothelial function and long-term memory similarly in both sexes of SHR living isolated lifestyles, demonstrating efficacy in mitigating VCI. These findings suggest that mechanisms by which chronic hypertension damages the vasculature in the hippocampus differs by sex, and that females may be more likely to benefit from combined lifestyle and medication-based therapeutics.

Neuronal activity-dependent changes in local perfusion are a critical aspect of cerebral hemodynamics and maintaining healthy neurocognitive function. NVC functions to match local blood flow to the metabolic demand of active neurons, and neurovascular un-coupling contributes to cognitive impairment, including during chronic hypertension12,44–47. Therefore, improving NVC could serve as a protective mechanism by which neuronal health is restored, improving memory function. We recently reported that HA reactivity to adenosine and NO were impaired in male SHR, suggesting NVC is impaired in the hippocampus during chronic hypertension17. In the current study, we advanced our understanding to include impaired HA vasoreactivity to such mediators of NVC occurred in females that was similar to males. Cognitive stimulation and donepezil treatment improved HA vasoreactivity to adenosine and NO in both sexes, suggesting improved long-term memory function was due to rescued NVC in the hippocampus.

In addition to initiation of a local vasodilation, propagating that vasodilation to upstream arterial segments is critical to reduce vascular resistance and increase local blood flow, and involves activity of SKCa/IKCa channels26,48–50. As previously discussed, lifestyle enrichment improved SKCa/IKCa channel function in HAs in both sexes, albeit to a greater extent in males than females. Although NVC was not directly measured in vivo in the current study, these findings suggest that relatively brief cognitive stimulation with lifestyle enrichment caused long-lasting improvement of two critical aspects of NVC in the hippocampus: the ability for neuronal activity to generate HA vasodilation, and the ability of that vasodilation to be conducted upstream to increase local blood flow. Future studies are needed to determine whether cognitive stimulation improves NVC in the hippocampus in vivo and the specific role(s) of other cell types in improved memory function.

It is well established that antihypertensive medications and blood pressure control are protective of the cerebral circulation51. A limitation of the current study is that blood pressure was not measured, and if interventions lowered blood pressure, that could contribute to improved HA and memory function. Blood pressure can be measured non-invasively using the tail cuff method to detect anti-hypertensive effects in SHR17. However, this was not performed in the current study to avoid potential confounding effects of the 3–5 days of training needed to acclimate the rats to the apparatus, reduce stress-induced effects on blood pressure and record accurate measurements. Importantly, donepezil treatment in SHR has not been shown to have antihypertensive effects52. Further, it is unlikely that the brief, intermittent cognitive stimulation in the current study persistently lowered blood pressure in aged SHR, as even brief treatment with antihypertensives do not persistently lower blood pressure in this model52,53. Therefore, improved HA function was not likely due to the lowering of blood pressure, but rather a more complex cascade of events induced by cognitive stimulation ultimately protecting the hippocampus and its vasculature.

Estrogen is well-known to impact memory function, particularly during the transition to reproductive senescence and contribute to increased incidence of cerebrovascular disease in women later in life54–56. A limitation of the current study is that the estrous stage of female rats was not controlled for, as this would have involved daily vaginal lavages. Not only would such daily interactions interfere with the isolation paradigm, but vaginal lavages have also been shown to induce a stress response and impair cognitive function involving the hippocampus (e.g., discriminative avoidance) that could confound our memory tasks57. Further, repeated lavages could put the females into a state of pseudo-pregnancy that could complicate data interpretation58,59. Regardless, we were unable to determine the relationship between estrous stage and cognitive function, and whether this contributed to sex differences detected. We did, however, determine that no female rats had entered reproductive senescence, as assessed histologically in ovarian tissue, confirming that all female rats had the same reproductive status and that onset of menopause did not contribute to group differences in memory or hippocampal vascular function.

In conclusion, the findings from the current study provide encouraging evidence of the potential for memory improvement in a relatively simple manner that can be effective in particularly vulnerable populations such as older cognitively impaired individuals that live isolated lifestyles and have difficulty adhering to prescription medications. Expanding our understanding of sex-specific efficacy of interventions to protect the hippocampus and its vasculature highlights the critical importance of continued research of relevant pathological conditions in both sexes to allow for the development of the most effective therapeutics and protect neurovascular health later in life. Overall, temporary or intermittent community involvement or memory games can be effective at slowing age-, hypertension- and/or isolation-related memory decline and VCI, with the potential to improve memory in people with preexisting impairment.

Methods

Animals

Experiments were conducted using male and female SHR that were purchased from Charles River at 4 months of age (N = 36). Rats were housed in the Binghamton University—SUNY animal facility, an Association for Assessment and Accreditation of Laboratory Animal Care International (AAALAC) accredited facility on a 12-h light/dark cycle and allowed access to food and water ad libitum. Rats were initially housed in pairs with environmental enrichment including chew sticks and a tunnel. All procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee and conducted in accordance with the National Institutes of Health (NIH) Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Rats were randomized to treatment group and investigators were blinded to animal group during behavioral analysis and pressure myography studies. Euthanasia occurred by rapid decapitation under isoflurane anesthesia according to NIH guidelines.

SHR are an accepted and widely used model to study the role of chronic hypertension and resultant cerebrovascular disease in age-related cognitive decline and have similar neuropathology of VCI as that which occurs in humans60. Therefore, this model is useful in the investigation of how aging and chronic hypertension affect memory and hippocampal vascular function, and whether holistic and/or pharmacological interventions can rescue age- and hypertension-induced memory dysfunction. Terminal experiments were conducted in rats ~ 16 months of age, which is considered late-adulthood (aged) in SHR, to model both the primary non-modifiable risk factor (age) and modifiable risk factor (hypertension) for VCI and dementia. Chronic hypertension accelerates aging and SHR begin to go into cardiac failure ~ 16 – 18 months of age61. Therefore, all rats began receiving Bio-Serv Electrogel cups (VWR, Radnor, PA) weekly beginning at 12 months of age.

Induction of isolated or enriched lifestyles

To understand how an isolated lifestyle impacts the hippocampus and its vasculature compared to an enriched lifestyle, rats of both sexes were split into enriched and isolated groups beginning at 6 months of age. Rats to experience the isolated lifestyle were separated and housed individually with a tunnel and chew block and not handled for the remainder of the study. Rats were randomly assigned to receive donepezil treatment (Don; 15 mg/kg/d in drinking water for 4 weeks beginning ~ 15 months of age) or naïve vehicle controls on regular drinking water (n = 6/group/sex). Donepezil was chosen as it is an AChE inhibitor that exerts peripheral and central effects and has been shown to improve memory dysfunction in disease models including Alzheimer’s disease and chronic cerebral hypoperfusion22,62,63. Further, donepezil treatment has been shown to improve peripheral artery function in disease models, including SHR63,64. This relatively high dose was chosen due to the shorter duration of treatment—4 weeks compared to several other studies that treat at a lower dose for 16 weeks64,65 – and, importantly, does not have an antihypertensive effect in SHR52.

Rats in the enriched lifestyle with cognitive stimulation (Stim; n = 6/sex) remained housed in pairs with environmental enrichment. Rats began learning and memory testing using the Morris water maze. This involved four consecutive 120 s swim trials to learn the location of a submerged and hidden platform using visual cues around the room. These trial days were repeated for four consecutive days, during which time all rats demonstrated decreased escape latency as they learned the location of the platform. After the final swim trial day, the platform was removed and memory probed by placing each rat back in the pool for a 120 s swim. Rats underwent weekly memory probe trials for three more weeks. To continue to stimulate rats with learning and memory tasks, a reversal task was then implemented. The hidden platform was placed back in the pool in the mirroring quadrant and rats underwent four 120 s swim trials for four consecutive days to stimulate behavioral flexibility and re-learning. This was followed by a probe trial 24 h after the fourth trial which continued weekly for three additional weeks. Thus, rats underwent 2 months of this enriched lifestyle involving stimulating learning and memory swim tasks between 6 and 8 months of age, the age range we have previously shown memory to decline in SHR16,17.

Learning and memory function

To understand (1) the impact of cognitive stimulation of the enriched lifestyle on learning and memory function, (2) whether one sex was more sensitive to this enrichment, and (3) whether potential protective effects persisted after enrichment ended, two behavioral tasks were used both two and sixth months after enrichment, or time-matched for the isolation group. First, a NOR task was used to assess long-term memory function that is largely considered to be hippocampal-dependent, and second, a continuous Y maze task was used to assess spatial working memory that is hippocampal-prefrontal cortex-dependent25,39,40. Importantly, both tasks are based upon a rodent’s innate preference for novelty, as opposed to being training-based (stimulated learning) which would confound the comparison of enriched vs. isolated lifestyles. This also allows for repeated testing over time. All rats were acclimated to the behavior room for one hour prior to the start of behavioral tasks and all behavioral testing was done at the same time of day. The NOR task consisted of a five-minute habituation to the open field arena, followed by a 10-min acquisition period when rats explored two identical objects, a 48-hour consolidation period where rats remained undisturbed in their home cages in the animal facility, and finally a five-minute test phase where rats were put in the same arena containing one familiar and one novel object16,17,66. All rats explored the identical objects for 30–40 s, a time that is sufficient to induce memory encoding. Locomotion and exploratory behavior were quantified from the first exposure to the open field arena, including distance traveled, average speed, latency to cross the center and number of crossings through the center. Separate groups of age-matched, group housed male and female normotensive Wistar rats (n = 6/group) were used to understand how potential protective effects of donepezil on long-term memory function compared to the healthy, normotensive state. ANY-maze automated tracking software (Stoelting Co., Wood Dale, IL, USA) was used to quantify locomotion, exploratory behavior and amount of time spent investigating each object. Object recognition index was then calculated as a measure of long-term memory function. At least one hour after NOR was completed, rats completed the continuous Y maze task that consisted of freely exploring the three-arm maze for eight minutes. Order of arm entries was used to calculate an alternation index as an assessment of spatial working memory function. Due to restrictions in space, counterbalancing NOR and y maze tasks was not possible; however, neither task involved stimulated learning minimizing the potential interference between tasks.

Isolated hippocampal arteriole function

To investigate hippocampal vascular dysfunction as an underlying mechanism of isolation-associated memory dysfunction and HAs as a potential therapeutic target of an enriched lifestyle, HAs were studied using pressure myography, as described previously67–69. Briefly, rats were decapitated at ~16 months of age under isoflurane anesthesia (3% in O2) and terminal experiments conducted after behavioral tasks were complete. Trunk blood was collected and serum stored at -80oC until use. Brains were rapidly removed and placed in cold, oxygenated artificial cerebrospinal fluid (aCSF). Hippocampi were dissected from the brain, and HAs perfusing the dorsal hippocampus were carefully dissected, secured on glass cannulas with nylon monofilaments and pressurized in an arteriograph chamber (Living Systems Instrumentation, Burlington, VT, USA). Hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, and parietal cortex were frozen and stored at −80 °C until use. Arterioles were allowed to equilibrate for one hour at 20 mmHg with aerated aCSF continuously superfusing the chamber and maintained at 36.8 ± 0.2 °C and a pH of 7.4 ± 0.04. After equilibration, intravascular pressure was increased to 120 mmHg in a stepwise manner to assess development of myogenic tone and to measure myogenic reactivity. Lumen diameter and vascular wall thickness were recorded at each intravascular pressure once stable. Pressure was then returned to 60 mmHg for the remainder of the experiment.

To investigate HA endothelial function, specifically SKCa/IKCa channels, HA vascular responses to pharmacological manipulation of SKCa/IKCa channels were assessed using NS309 (10−8–10−5 M), the SKCa/IKCa channel agonist, apamin (300 nM), the selective SKCa channel antagonist, and TRAM-34 (1 µM), the selective IKCa channel antagonist. The vascular responses of HAs to mediators central to NVC in the hippocampus were investigated, including vasoreactivity to adenosine (10−8–10−5 M) and the NO donor sodium nitroprusside (SNP; 10−8–10−5 M). Finally, aCSF was replaced with aCSF containing zero calcium, papaverine (10−4 M) and diltiazem (10−5 M) to fully relax the vascular smooth muscle and structural measurements taken within the intravascular pressure range of 5–120 mmHg.

Measurement of circulating factors and growth factors using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs)

Commercially available ELISA kits were used to measure serum levels of ET-1, ICAM-1, MMP-9, TIMP-1, and BDNF. Several kits were purchased from R&D Systems (Minneapolis, MN), including ET-1 (product DET100; samples undiluted), ICAM-1 (product RIC100; 50-fold sample dilution), MMP-9 (product RMP900; samples undiluted), and TIMP-1 (product RTM100; 20-fold sample dilution). The BDNF kit was purchased from Invitrogen (Carlsbad, CA, product ERBDNF; samples undiluted). In addition to measuring serum factors, commercially available BDNF ELISA kits were purchased from R&D Systems (product DBNT00) to measure brain BDNF levels potentially involved in improving vascular and cognitive functions. Brain tissue lysates were prepared using standard methodology. Briefly, hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, and parietal cortex were weighed, 0.70 mL of cell extraction buffer (Invitrogen, product FNN0011) was added per 1 gram of tissue, and samples homogenized at 2.5 m/s using a bead mill homogenizer (VWR, Radnor, PA, product 10158-558). Samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min and tissue lysates aliquoted and stored at −80 °C until use. To normalize ELISA values, a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay was used to determine the total protein concentration in each sample. Samples were randomized across the plates and run in duplicate according to manufacturer’s instructions.

Histology

To understand the potential impact of sex steroids on memory and arteriole function in females, we assessed reproductive status histologically. Ovaries were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h then transferred to a 30% sucrose solution. 40 µm thick sections of ovary were sliced using a cryostat, mounted on glass slides and put on a slide warmer to dry. A standard H&E protocol was used to assess ovarian status based upon the presence of corpus luteum and ovarian follicles at different stages of maturity, indicating rats were still naturally cycling and had not gone into reproductive senescence27–29.

Drugs and solutions

Diltiazem was purchased from MP Biomedicals (Santa Ana, CA, USA), apamin and TRAM-34 were purchased from Tocris Bioscience (Minneapolis, MN, USA), and donepezil, sucrose and 4% buffered PFA were purchased from ThermoFisher Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA). All other compounds, including NS309, adenosine, SNP, papaverine, and those used to make aCSF were purchased from Sigma Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Donepezil was diluted in drinking water based on the rats’ body weight and protected from light. The amount of water drank was measured to confirm dosing and refreshed every three days. Stock solutions of adenosine, SNP, papaverine and diltiazem were made weekly and stored at 4 °C until use. Stock solutions of NS309, apamin and TRAM-34 were aliquoted and stored at −20 °C until use. aCSF contained (mM): NaCl 122.0, NaHCO3 26.0, NaH3PO4 1.25, KCl 3.0, MgCl2 1.0, and CaCl2 2.0. Buffer solutions were made each week and stored without glucose at 4 °C. Glucose was added (4.0 mM) immediately prior to each experiment. Zero calcium aCSF was made similarly, omitting CaCl2 and adding 0.5 M EGTA, a calcium chelator. aCSF was aerated with medical blood gas consisting of 5% CO2, 10% O2 and 85% N2 to maintain pH at 7.40 ± 0.04.

Data calculations

To assess memory function, recognition index was calculated using the following formula: TimeNovel / TimeTotal, where TimeNovel is the amount of time (sec) a rat spent investigating the novel object and TimeTotal is the total time (sec) that rat spent investigating both objects. Spontaneous alternation index was calculated using the following formula: [# Alternations / (# Arm Entries – 2)].

For isolated HA experiments, percent myogenic tone was calculated using the following equation: % Tone = [1 – (φactive / φpassive)] x 100%; φactive is the diameter under active conditions and φpassive is the diameter under fully relaxed conditions at a specific intravascular pressure. Percent reactivity and sensitivity to NS309, adenosine and SNP were calculated from the equations: % Reactivity = [(φdose – φbaseline) / (φpassive – φbaseline)] x 100%; % Sensitivity = [(φdose – φbaseline) / (φfinal – φbaseline)] x 100%; φdose is the diameter of the vessel after treatment with a specific concentration of drug, φbaseline is the starting diameter before any drug treatment, and φfinal is the diameter of the vessel at the highest concentration of drug, and EC50 values determined as the concentration that elicited 50% of the maximum response of sensitivity curves. Percent constriction to apamin and subsequent TRAM-34 was calculated as a percent change in lumen diameter from baseline by the equation: % Constriction = [1 – (φdrug / φbaseline)] x 100%; ϕdrug is the diameter of vessel after drug exposure, and ϕbaseline is the diameter before giving drug. Outer diameter (φouter) was calculated at each pressure by the equation: φouter = φinner + 2WT; φinner is the inner diameter of the vessel fully relaxed and WT is the measured wall thickness. CSA was calculated by the equation: CSA = π(φouter / 2)2 – π(φinner / 2)2 at 5 mmHg and 60 mmHg intravascular pressure. Percent distensibility was calculated using the equation: % Distensibility = [(φpassive – φ5) / φ5] x 100%.

Statistical analyses

A statistical power calculation was used to determine the number of animals used in each experiment using a two-sided 95% confidence interval for a single mean and 1 – β of 0.80 based on previous studies using similar methodology17,67,68. We determined that n = 6/group was sufficient to detect statistical differences in vasoreactivity of HAs to mediators of NVC as well as memory function tested by NOR and Y maze tasks. To determine baseline sex differences in locomotion and exploratory behavior, unpaired Student’s t-tests were used. To determine differences in memory function, vascular lumen diameters, myogenic tone and vasoreactivity between groups within sex, an ordinary one-way ANOVA with a Tukey’s post-hoc test to correct for multiple comparisons. Comparisons were made using a two-way ANOVA with a post-hoc Tukey test for multiple comparisons to determine the interaction between two independent variables (sex and treatment). To understand potential relationships between body weight or Morris water maze performance and memory decline, linear regression analyses and Pearson’s correlations were run. Results are presented as mean ± SEM and a p < 0.05 was considered significant. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 10.3 software (GraphPad Software Inc., La Jolla, CA). Rats were randomized to treatment group, and experiments and analyses were completed in a blinded fashion whenever possible.

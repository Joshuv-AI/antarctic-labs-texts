Evaluation of confirmatory data following the Article 12 MRL review for beta‐cyfluthrin
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Marta Szot, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe applicant Bayer AG submitted a request to the competent national authority in Germany to evaluate the confirmatory data for beta‐cyfluthrin, which had been identified as unavailable in the framework of the maximum residue level (MRL)review under Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. Although cyfluthrin and beta‐cyfluthrin are no longer authorised for use on edible crops within the European Union, MRLs on wheat and barley were established based on import tolerances, and the data gaps on these crops are still relevant. To address the data gaps, new studies on the magnitude of residues in wheat were submitted. The data gap on wheat was considered satisfactorily addressed. For barley however, the data gap was not addressed as the applicant did not report any new data. The new information provided may require a revision of the existing MRLs for wheat and for barley, both lowered to the enforcement limit of quantification (LOQ). The risk assessment was performed accordingly for beta‐cyfluthrin (and cyfluthrin), leading to the conclusion that a risk to consumer health is unlikely for the imported commodities under assessment.

SUMMARY

In 2021, when the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) reviewed the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for active substances beta‐cyfluthrin and cyfluthrin according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA identified some information as unavailable (data gaps) and derived tentative MRLs for those uses which were not fully supported by data but for which no risk to consumers was identified. The following data gap was noted: residue trials supporting the import tolerance GAPs on wheat and barley.

residue trials supporting the import tolerance GAPs on wheat and barley.

residue trials supporting the import tolerance GAPs on wheat and barley.

Tentative MRL proposals have been implemented in the MRL legislation by Commission Regulation Reg. (EU) 2023/173 including footnotes indicating the type of confirmatory data that should be fully or partially provided reflecting the data gaps noted in the MRL review, reported above. Any party having an interest in maintaining the proposed tentative MRL was requested to address the confirmatory data by 27 January 2025.

In accordance with the agreed procedure set out in the working document SANTE/10235/2016, Bayer AG submitted an application to the competent national authority in Germany (rapporteur Member State, RMS) to evaluate the confirmatory data identified during the MRL review.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the EFSA Central Submission System on 17 January 2025. The appointed RMS, Germany, assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 20 June 2025. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA, and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 17 July 2025 to 7 August 2025. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the RMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report, in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on 9 February 2026.

On 2 March 2026, the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

To address the data gaps, new studies on the magnitude of residues in wheat were submitted. The data gap on wheat was considered satisfactorily addressed. For barley, however, the data gap was not addressed as the applicant did not report any new data. The new information provided may require a revision of the existing import tolerance MRLs for wheat and for barley, both lowered to the enforcement limit of quantification (LOQ).

ASSESSMENT

The review of existing MRLs for the active substance beta‐cyfluthrin according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 1 (MRL review) has been performed in 2021 (EFSA, 2021). European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) identified some information as unavailable (data gaps) and derived tentative MRLs for uses not fully supported by data but for which no risk to consumers was identified. The GAPs assessed in the framework of the MRL review that were not fully supported by data and for which confirmatory data were requested are listed in Appendix A.

Following the review of the existing MRLs, the legal limits were modified by Commission Regulation (EU) No 2023/173, 2 including footnotes for tentative MRLs that specified the type of information identified as missing. Any party having an interest in maintaining the proposed tentative MRL was requested to address the confirmatory data by 27 January 2025.

In accordance with the specific provisions set out in the working document of the European Commission SANTE/10235/2016 (European Commission, 2020b) and by the ‘Transparency Regulation’ (EU) 2019/1381, 3 the applicant Bayer AG, on 17 January 2025 submitted an application to the competent national authority in Germany (designated Rapporteur Member State, RMS) to evaluate the confirmatory data identified during the MRL review alongside the dossier containing the supporting data using the IUCLID format.

To address the data gaps identified by EFSA, the applicant provided new residue trials performed on wheat.

The RMS assessed the new information in an evaluation report, which was submitted to the European Commission and forwarded to EFSA on 9 February 2026 (Germany, 2026). On 2 March 2026, the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

EFSA based its assessment on the evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Germany, 2026) and on the reasoned opinion on the MRL review according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005.

For this application, the data requirements established in Regulation (EU) No 544/2011 4 and the relevant guidance documents at the date of implementation of the confirmatory data requirements by Regulation (EU) No 2023/173 are applicable. The assessment was performed in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. 5

An updated list of end points, including the end points of relevant studies assessed previously and the confirmatory data evaluated in this application, is presented in Appendix B.

The evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Germany, 2026) is made publicly available as a background document to this reasoned opinion. 6

Proposed residue definitions

The previously derived residue definitions are still applicable, namely ‘cyfluthrin, including other mixtures of constituent isomers (sum of isomers)’ for both enforcement and risk assessment.

Magnitude of residues in plants

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

The GAPs reported during the MRL review for wheat and barley is a foliar treatment with a maximum of 2 applications at the dose rate of 6.25 g a.s./ha (PHI of 30 days). This GAP is an import tolerance registered in Ukraine (UA). This GAP is still considered for the assessment of confirmatory data. In this framework, the applicant clarified the growth stage at application (BBCH 30–69) and the interval between applications (14 days) for wheat.

Barley:

The applicant informed the RMS and EFSA that no data will be submitted to address the data gap for barley (Germany, 2026). Therefore, the data gap for residue trials supporting the import tolerance is not addressed for barley.

Wheat:

To address data gap on the lack of residue trials supporting the import tolerance for wheat, the applicant provided 17 residue trials performed on wheat. After a first screening based on the compliance with the PHI (30 days with a tolerance of 25%), the EMS retained 13 relevant trials performed in Brazil (3 trials), Southern EU (3 trials) and in the United States of America (7 trials). Details on the validation of the analytical method are available in the Evaluation report of the EMS (Germany, 2026). The trials were assessed by the EMS and considered valid regarding the storage conditions of the samples (covered by the available storage stability on dry commodities) and the applicability of analytical methods.

It was discussed whether the GAP under assessment (reported in Ukraine) could be supported by trials performed in Brazil, Southern EU and in the US (7 trials from North to South). In the Technical guidelines on data requirements for setting maximum residue levels, comparability of residue trials and extrapolation on residue data on products from plant and animal origin (European Commission, 2025), there is no formal categorisation of Ukraine regarding northern or southern zones for the evaluation of residue behaviour. However, given Ukraine's location and type of wheat cultivation (mainly winter wheat), the agricultural practices are expected to be closer to northern EU countries such as Germany, Poland or northern France than to southern EU countries. That may include more spring rainfall and more protection from temperature below zero during winter. On the other hand, as reported by the EMS, the southern part of Ukraine is closer to Black Sea countries and summer season also includes warmer temperature than in some Northern EU countries. Consequently, the EMS suggested that Ukraine included potential climatic conditions representative to both the Northern and Southern Zone. It is also noted that, according to OECD Crop Field Trial Guidance (OECD, 2021), pre‐harvest climatic conditions are not major factors influencing residue variability in comparable residue trials. Consequently, all trials provided were deemed relevant for the residue assessment.

The three trials performed in Brazil were fully compliant with GAP and showed residue concentrations below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.

The three trials performed in SEU were twice overdosed (12.5 g a.s./ha instead of 6.25) but showed residue concentrations below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.

The seven trials performed in the US were highly overdosed (21–22 g a.s./ha instead of 6.25) and showed residue concentrations ranging from < 0.01 to 0.031 mg/kg. In line with the technical report on the use of the proportionality approach in the framework of risk assessment for pesticide residues (EFSA, 2018), the EMS applied the proportionality approach (applying a factor of 3.4), which also indicated residue concentrations expected to remain below 0.01 mg/kg.

In conclusion, the 13 residue trials provided were deemed sufficient to address the data gap identified for wheat during the MRL review. Based on these data, the existing tentative MRL of 0.04 mg/kg for wheat is proposed to be lowered to the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.

Magnitude of residues in rotational crops

Not relevant for import tolerance.

Magnitude of residues in processed commodities

The magnitude of cyfluthrin, including other mixtures of constituent isomers, in processed commodities was investigated previous assessments and the available processing factors were reported in the MRL review (EFSA, 2021). No data gaps were identified. Nevertheless, one additional processing study of wheat was submitted by the applicant and assessed by the EMS (Germany, 2026).

The processing study was performed in the US to investigate the magnitude of the residues of beta‐cyfluthrin in wheat processed commodities after spray application. The study was fully validated regarding the analytical method and the sample storage conditions (Germany, 2026). However, only two independent trials were performed. The residue concentration was investigated in samples of bran, flour (white), germ, middlings, shorts, gluten, starch, whole meal flour and whole grain (whole meal) bread.

Although, the quality of the study is reliable, the number of trials is not sufficient to derive robust processing factors. In wheat bran, wheat gluten and wheat shorts, the results of the two trials were too different (> 50%) to derive a processing factor. In the other matrices [flour (white), germ, middlings, starch, whole meal flour and whole grain (whole meal) bread], a reduction of the residues is indicated by the trials, with median PFs ranging between 0.21 and 0.77. The PFs derived for these commodities are tentative. See details in Appendix B.1.2.3.

Proposed MRLs

The available data are considered sufficient to address the data gap on wheat. The new data indicated that the MRL for wheat grain could be lowered to the LOQ (0.01 mg/kg).

Regarding barley, the data gap was not addressed. Risk managers may therefore consider lowering the existing MRL, to the enforcement LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.

RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK

The confirmatory data assessed in the present application show that residue concentration in wheat grain is expected to remain below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg according to the reported import tolerance GAP. For wheat grain and its related by‐products, the STMR of 0.01 mg/kg was already considered in the MRL review. Therefore, this remains unchanged.

Regarding barley grain, no data were submitted to address the data gap.

It should also be noted that further investigation of residues in livestock was not considered necessary in the framework of the MRL review because EU livestock are not significantly exposed to cyfluthrin residues (EFSA, 2021). This conclusion remains valid.

The existing MRLs for commodities of animal origin were taken from Codex MRLs and from the cyfluthrin levels resulting from veterinary drug (EFSA, 2021). Consequently, the data assessed in the present application do not have an impact on pesticide residues expected in livestock commodities.

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

EFSA updated the consumer risk assessment performed for beta‐cyfluthrin and cyfluthrin during the MRL review (EFSA, 2021). During the MRL review, EFSA performed four different consumer exposure scenarios, using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018, 2019a) and the health‐based guidance values (HBGVs) for cyfluthrin which were derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (ADI: 0.01 mg/kg bw per day; ARfD: 0.01 mg/kg bw; European Commission, 2020a).

The last scenario performed by EFSA included Codex MRLs and the MRLs on milks derived from veterinary uses. Considering that these MRLs were implemented in the Regulation, this scenario was updated, taking into account the new data submitted under this application. The new data submitted in the present application resulted in a new median residue value (STMR) for wheat, being 0.01 mg/kg. This STMR is lower than the value used by the MRL review. As barley was not supported by any data, the former STMR value was replaced by the default value at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. The complete list of input values is presented in Appendix D.1.

The short‐term exposure did not exceed the ARfD for wheat and barley assessed in this application.

The estimated long‐term dietary intake was in the range from 0.7% to 18% of the ADI (Dutch toddler). The contribution of residues expected in the wheat and barley to the overall long‐term exposure is very low (< 1% ADI); it is presented in more detail in Appendix B.3.

EFSA concluded that the long‐term intake of residues of cyfluthrin and beta‐cyfluthrin resulting from the existing uses as plant protection products (import tolerances) and from the use as a veterinary drug is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health.

In the framework of the MRL review, it was highlighted that metabolism studies did not investigate the possible impact of plant and animal metabolism on the isomer ratio of the active substance. Further investigation on this matter would in principle be required, but this was not in the scope of the present assessment of confirmatory data following the Article 12 MRL review. It is noted that the EFSA guidance on the risk assessment of compounds that may have stereoisomers has been issued (EFSA, 2019b).

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

To address the data gaps, new studies on the magnitude of residues in wheat were submitted. The data gap on wheat was considered satisfactorily addressed. For barley, however, the data gap was not addressed as the applicant did not report any new data. The new information provided may require a revision of the existing import tolerance MRLs for wheat and for barley, both lowered to the enforcement LOQ. The risk assessment was performed accordingly for beta‐cyfluthrin (cyfluthrin), leading to the conclusion that a risk to consumer health is unlikely for the crops under assessment.

The overview of the assessment of confirmatory data and the recommended MRL modifications are summarised in Appendix B.4.

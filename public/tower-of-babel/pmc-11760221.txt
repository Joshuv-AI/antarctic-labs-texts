Economics of first-line treatment with tislelizumab in patients with nonsquamous non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Lin Wang, Ting Liu, Xihan Lin, Yu Zhang, Luwen Shi, Ruxu You, Jinyu Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectiveThis study evaluates the cost-effectiveness of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy as a first-line treatment for locally advanced or metastatic (IIIB/IV) nonsquamous non-small cell lung cancer (nsq-NSCLC) in China.MethodsA Markov model projected health outcomes and costs over a lifetime, with health states including progression-free survival, progressive disease, terminal progressive disease (TPD) and death. Data came from a Chinese phase III trial. The primary outcome was quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), with incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) calculated. Using sensitivity analysis to confirm the robustness of the results.ResultsThe base-case analysis showed that tislelizumab combined chemotherapy group had 1.06 more QALYs (3.967 QALYs VS 2.909 QALYs) than the chemotherapy-alone group at an incremental cost of U.S. dollars ($) 19594.75 ($43390.52 VS $23795.77), resulting in an ICER of $18,512.47/QALY. This ICER is below the willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold of three times China’s 2023 per capita GDP ($36,672.23/QALY), indicating tislelizumab is economically favorable.ConclusionThe conclusion is that tislelizumab combination therapy is a cost-effective first-line treatment option for nsq-NSCLC patients in China.

Lung cancer, also known as primary bronchogenic carcinoma, is a malignant tumor originating from the bronchial mucosa, glands, or alveolar epithelium of the lungs. It ranks among the most common and deadliest malignancies worldwide and in China [1]. In 2022, there were nearly 2.5 million new cases of lung cancer globally, with over 1.8 million deaths, making it the leading cause of cancer incidence and mortality worldwide. Lung cancer accounts for nearly one-eighth (12.4%) of all diagnosed cancer cases and one-fifth (18.7%) of all cancer deaths globally. In men, the highest incidence rates are observed in the East Asian region. Among women, lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death in 23 countries, including China and the United States. In 2022, the age-standardized incidence rate for lung cancer in men was 32.1%, and for women, it was 16.2% [2]. Lung cancer in China is not only the most common type of cancer but also the leading cause of cancer death among both men and women. In 2020, the age-standardized incidence rate for lung cancer in men was 40.78 per 100,000, while for women it was 30.34 per 100,000. Studies have shown that between 2000 and 2018, the ASIR for all cancers increased significantly by 1.4% annually [3]. Lung cancer is broadly categorized into non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell lung cancer (SCLC), with NSCLC constituting 80–85% of lung cancer cases [4]. Lung cancer is a leading health problem due to challenges in diagnosis and treatment, and, eventually results in poor prognosis. NSCLC is the most common histologic type, with a 5-year survival rate of all stages approximately 15% [5]. Previous studies [6] have reported significant disparities in the per capita healthcare costs associated with NSCLC, ranging from $37,514 per capita in the United States to $5,070 per capita in Europe. Regardless of the perspective considered, NSCLC treatment is universally acknowledged as a costly process, with expenses increasing with prolonged treatment duration. Most lung cancer patients remain asymptomatic in their early stages, resulting in late-stage diagnoses that impose substantial burdens on patients’ families and society at large.

Historically, the treatment of advanced NSCLC has primarily involved targeted therapies and chemotherapy. In recent years, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), which target the systemic immune system, have gained prominence in the treatment of NSCLC and achieved groundbreaking progress [7]. Programmed death receptor-1/programmed death receptor ligand-1 (PD-1/PD-L1) and cytotoxic T lymphocyte-associated antigen-4 (CTLA-4) are some of the most common and promising targets in ICIs, which have completely transformed the treatment of NSCLC [8]. According to the 2023 Guidelines for Nonsquamous Nondriver NSCLC, the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) recommends first-line treatment options, including chemotherapy combined with tislelizumab, monotherapy with chemotherapy or tislelizumab, and monotherapy with pembrolizumab. Similarly, the “Primary Lung Cancer Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines” [9] recommend tislelizumab, pembrolizumab, or sintilimab in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy for nonsquamous cancer patients without driver mutations. Furthermore, the “Chinese Medical Association Guidelines for the Clinical Diagnosis and Treatment of Lung Cancer (2022 edition)” [10] classify tislelizumab in combination with pemetrexed and platinum-based therapy as a Class 2A recommendation for nonsquamous cell carcinoma patients without driver mutations.

Tislelizumab, a domestically developed anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody in China, has demonstrated significant antitumor potential and safety in various immuno-oncology settings. Due to its unique pharmacological characteristics, tislelizumab has emerged as a promising new immune-based therapy for advanced NSCLC [11]. The RATIONALE 304 study, an open-label, multicenter, phase III trial, was conducted at 47 research sites across China. According to the Independent Review Committee (IRC) assessment, first-line treatment of advanced nonsquamous NSCLC with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy significantly improved patients’ progression-free survival (PFS) and reduced disease progression risk by 36% compared to chemotherapy alone (median PFS: 9.7 vs. 7.6 months; HR = 0.645 [95% CI: 0.462, 0.902]; p = 0.0044). Tislelizumab combined with chemotherapy also enhanced the objective response rate (ORR) and duration of response versus chemotherapy, with IRC-assessed ORR rates of 57% and 37% and median duration of response of 8.5 and 6.0 months, respectively [12]. However, increased patient costs may contribute to uncertainty about the cost-effectiveness of clinical treatment. Given the increasing use of immunotherapy in China’s healthcare system, increased treatment costs could negatively affect society economically. Considering the prudent allocation of healthcare resources, an economic assessment of the two options is necessary.

This study aimed to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of combining tislelizumab with chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone utilizing the most recent clinical trial data and drug prices. The objective of this study was to identify the optimal first-line treatment regimen in terms of cost-effectiveness, providing valuable guidance for clinicians and patients in making informed decisions. Additionally, this study seeks to inform the development of clinical guidelines and health insurance policies in this area.

Materials and methods

This study employed a Chinese healthcare system perspective and integrated the literature, clinical treatment characteristics of advanced NSCLC, and insights from clinical experts and recommendations. Microsoft Excel 2016 was used to construct a four-state Markov model, an extension of the traditional three-state Markov model (Figure 1). The model categorized disease progression into two stages: initial progression (second-line treatment) and terminal progression (third-line treatment). This enhancement allowed for accurate simulation of patients’ treatment pathways and improved the precision of cost and health outcome measurements. The model structure is depicted in Figure 1 below. All simulated patients started from a PFS and could either remain in this state, progress to a progressed disease state (PD), or die at the next cycle length. Patients who progressed to the PD state could remain in PD or progress to terminal progressed disease (TPD) or death, with death being the absorbing state. State membership was assessed using parametric models applied to Kaplan-Meier (K-M) survival data for PFS and OS. The study was conducted according to the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards (CHEERS). Figure 1. Markov model bubble diagram.

Markov model bubble diagram.

Study population and treatment protocols

Data for first-line treatment were obtained from the RATIONALE 304 trial [12], which is an open-label, randomized, multicenter phase 3 study conducted at 47 study sites in the People’s Republic of China. The population included in the study consists of adult patients (aged 18–75 years) who were treatment-naive for histologically confirmed locally advanced (stage IIIB) or metastatic (stage IV) non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer (nsq-NSCLC). According to the Chinese Medical Association’s Lung Cancer Clinical Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines (2022 edition) [10] and the CSCO guidelines, docetaxel is recommended for second-line treatment of NSCLC, with clinical treatment data referring to the RATIONALE 303 trial [13].The patients included in the study are those who are 18 years of age or older, with a pathological diagnosis of locally advanced or metastatic squamous cell carcinoma or non-squamous cell carcinoma, and who have experienced disease progression during or after at least one platinum-based doublet chemotherapy regimen.

In the RATIONALE 304 trial, eligible patients were randomly assigned (2:1) to either the tislelizumab group or the chemotherapy-alone group. Patients in the tislelizumab group received tislelizumab at 200 mg, cisplatin (75 mg/m2) and pemetrexed (500 mg/m2) every 3 weeks for 4–6 cycles, followed by maintenance therapy with tislelizumab plus pemetrexed. In the chemotherapy group, patients received cisplatin (75 mg/m2) and pemetrexed (500 mg/m2) every 3 weeks for 4–6 cycles, followed by maintenance therapy with pemetrexed monotherapy. This study assumed an induction treatment duration of 6 cycles (Table S1).

In the RATIONALE 303 trial, patients in the chemotherapy group received 75 mg/m2 docetaxel every 3 weeks continuously without interruption.

In China, the incidence of lung cancer in the 50–59 age group is increasing in both sexes. The median age of patients enrolled in RATIONALE 304 and RATIONALE 303 was 61 years, and the simulations were performed in 3-week cycles starting from the age of 61 years, aligning with the drug schedule. The model’s time horizon was set at 21.06 years, corresponding to 361 cycles, to capture the lifetime impacts of tislelizumab combination therapy for first-line nsq-NSCLC patients, as more than 99% of patients died during this time.

The target population for this study consisted of patients with locally advanced or metastatic nsq-NSCLC in the first-line setting. In the tislelizumab group, patients received tislelizumab in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy and pemetrexed in the progression-free state, with a treatment duration of 6 cycles. The discontinuation rate for patients in the tislelizumab group due to reasons other than progression is 17.8%. In comparison, the discontinuation rate among patients in the chemotherapy group was 28.7%. These findings were derived from data sourced from RATIONALE 304. Patients in both groups who progressed received docetaxel as treatment, based on findings from the RATIONALE 303 trial, where the discontinuation rate in the docetaxel-alone group was 27.5%. We did not account for the impact of treatment discontinuation, making our study results a conservative estimate.

According to RATIONALE 303 trial, eighty percent of patients progressed to receive active treatment in the terminal PD state, while the remaining 20% received optimal supportive care. Considering the recommendations for later-line treatment in the Chinese Medical Association’s Lung Cancer Clinical Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines (2022 edition) [10] and the lack of specific disclosure of later-line treatment plans in clinical trials, this study assumes that patients who progress to the terminal PD state receive the best supportive treatment plan. Clinical parameters for the Markov model were collected from RATIONALE 303 and RATIONALE 304.

Transition probability

Membership in the state was ascertained based on PFS and overall survival (OS) curves, which were derived from parametric models that were fitted to the K-M estimates. K-M survival data for PFS and OS were available from the phase III RATIONALE 304 trial and RATIONALE 303 trial. The PFS and OS probabilities were extracted from the PFS and OS K-M curves of each treatment group using GetData Graph Digitizer(version 2.24). Performing parametric survival analysis with the R software (version 4.0.3) with the survHE package. The long-term clinical outcome was extrapolated using the fitted survival function.

The main steps include data reconstruction, parameter model fitting, optimal model selection, obtaining model parameters. The six common parametric distributions are the Exponential, Weibull, Gompertz, Log-logistic, Log-normal and Generalized Gamma distributions. The best-fitting distribution for the parameterized model was selected based on criteria including lower values of the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) and visual comparison of the models against K-M survival curves, with detailed results provided in the supplementary materials (Table S2).

In the RATIONALE 304 trial, the best-fitting distribution for the tislelizumab group’s PFS curve was Log-logistic, while the OS curve followed a Log-normal distribution. In the chemotherapy group, both PFS and OS curves were best fitted with Log-normal distributions. In the RATIONALE 303 trial, the PFS and OS curves for docetaxel treatment were best fitted with Log-logistic distributions.The comparison chart of the fitted curves with the original curves can be found in the supplementary materials, which shows a good fit and a high degree of conformity (Figure S1, Figure S2, Figure S3). Once the survival functions are obtained, time-dependent state-transition probabilities in cycles are computed based on the extracted data and fitted models. The transition probabilities between PD and the terminal PD to death states are calculated based on the OS curves from the RATIONALE 304 and RATIONALE 303 trials, respectively. Taking the calculation of the transition probability from PD to death as an example, the transition probability from time t-u to t (with a cycle of u) can be divided into two main parts: the probability that those who are alive remain alive and the probability that those who are alive transition to death. Specifically, the transition probability of remaining in the alive state is: Psts=S(t)/S(t−u), and the transition probability from the alive state to the death state is: Pstd = 1−Psts. Since the alive state includes both non-progressive and progressive states, Pstd needs to subtract the transition probability from the non-progressive state to the death state to obtain the transition probability from the progressive state to the death state. The calculation of the transition probability from terminal PD to death follows the same logic.

Costs and utility

In this study, costs were assessed from the perspective of the Chinese healthcare system, encompassing direct medical costs such as drug expenses, adverse event costs, optimal supportive care costs, and end-of-life treatment costs.

Tislelizumab, now listed in China’s medical insurance directory, is priced at the national negotiated rate. Prices for pemetrexed, cisplatin, and docetaxel, included in China’s centralized drug procurement list, were obtained from the average bidding price in 2023 from Chinese Drug Bidding Data. Consistency with the clinical trial was maintained by utilizing the same drug dosages administered every three weeks, with the body surface area assumed to be 1.72 m2, as reported in previous literature [15].

Adverse event costs were primarily considered Grade ≥ 3 adverse events with significant group differences in incidence rate, as Grade 1/2 adverse events were assumed to be manageable (Table 2, Table S3). The unit costs of adverse events were obtained from the literature [16,17], with the assumption that all adverse events occurred during the first treatment cycle, and rates were derived from the RATIONALE 304 and RATIONALE 303 trials. The optimal supportive care costs included outpatient doctor visit fees, laboratory examination fees, and other relevant expenses sourced from the literature (Shanghai, China) [18]. End-of-life treatment costs were also obtained from the literature. This study conducted a cost-effectiveness analysis with quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) as the health outcome measure. The QALYs were calculated based on patient survival times and utility values collected from the literature. The utility values used in this study were 0.856 for the progression-free state, 0.768 for the progression state, and 0.703 for the end-of-life state [19]. In addition, negative utility values due to grade ≥ 3 adverse events were collected from the literature: 0.07 for anemia, 0.2 for leukopenia, 0.2 for neutropenia, 0.07 for fatigue, 0.12 for thrombocytopenia and 0.07 for musculoskeletal pain [20,21].

The utility values for adverse effects were calculated by multiplying their incidences, resulting in negative utility values of 0.165 for first-line treatment with tislelizumab combined therapy, 0.126 for first-line treatment with chemotherapy regimens, and 0.088 for second-line treatment. A comprehensive list of all adverse reactions is provided in Table S3.

To determine the economic viability of the tislelizumab group compared to that of the chemotherapy-alone group, the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) was compared to the willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold, which was set at 1–3 times the per capita Gross Domestic Product (GDP) according to the World Health Organization (WHO) recommendations. Specifically, when the ICER is less than 1 times the per capita GDP, the added cost is considered entirely worthwhile indicating an absolute advantage for the tislelizumab group. When the per capita GDP < ICER < 3 times the per capita GDP, the added cost is deemed acceptable, indicating cost-effectiveness for the tislelizumab group. However, when the ICER exceeds 3 times the per capita GDP, the added cost is deemed not worthwhile suggesting that tislelizumab is not cost effective. Discounting of both costs and utility values at a 5% discount rate was applied in line with Chinese Pharmacoeconomic Guidelines [22]. Statistical analysis

In this study, we conducted both one-way sensitivity analysis and probabilistic sensitivity analysis to assess the robustness of the results. One-way sensitivity analysis evaluates the impact of changes in one parameter at a time on the results, varying parameters within predefined ranges, typically determined by confidence intervals. Parameters without confidence intervals were adjusted by a certain percentage, with costs and utilities fluctuating ± 20% and ± 10% from their base values, respectively, following previous studies [23].

The discount rate was varied within the recommended range of 0–8%. The results of the one-way sensitivity analysis are presented in tornado diagrams, illustrating the sensitivity of the model to changes in individual parameters. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis was performed via Monte Carlo simulation to address the uncertainty in multiple parameters simultaneously. Parameters were assumed to follow specific probability distributions, with costs modeled using a gamma distribution and probabilities and utility values modeled with a beta distribution. The analysis was repeated 10,000 times to generate a distribution of cost-effectiveness outcomes.

The results of the probabilistic sensitivity analysis are presented in cost-effectiveness scatterplots and cost-effectiveness acceptability curves (CEAC), offering a visual representation of the uncertainty in the model and the likelihood of the tislelizumab group being cost-effective compared to the chemotherapy-alone group under different WTP thresholds. These sensitivity analyses aid in assessing the robustness of the economic evaluation by considering variations in key parameters and capturing the overall uncertainty in the model’s results.

Results

Model validation

The median estimated OS for the tislelizumab group, derived from model simulations, is 25.2 months, which lies within the 95% confidence interval of 17.9 to 26.0 months reported in the final clinical trial report. For the chemotherapy-only group, the median estimated OS is 20.3 months, falling within the 95% confidence interval of 13.3 to 21.1 months as per the final clinical trial report. The final OS reported values are sourced from the literature (Lu S, et al., 2024) [24].

Discussion

In recent years, the prevalence of NSCLC patients has been steadily increasing, leading to an increased demand for treatments in this population. Among these treatments, immunotherapy has gained widespread clinical acceptance due to its efficacy and favorable safety profile. It has been recommended as a first-line treatment option in numerous cancer treatment guidelines. The findings of the RATIONALE 304 study demonstrated that, in comparison to chemotherapy alone, the combination of tislelizumab and chemotherapy as a first-line treatment for advanced nsq-NSCLC significantly improved PFS and reduced the risk of disease progression, providing benefits to patients with advanced nsq-NSCLC. Both the CSCO Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer 2023” and the “Clinical Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines for Lung Cancer (2022 Edition) by the Chinese Medical Association” recommend the use of tislelizumab in conjunction with chemotherapy for the treatment of nsq-NSCLC patients lacking driver gene mutations.

Consequently, from a healthcare perspective, this study assessed the cost-effectiveness of tislelizumab in combination with chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone as a first-line treatment for NSCLC. The analysis results indicate that the combination of tislelizumab and chemotherapy is more cost-effective than chemotherapy alone. From the viewpoint of the Chinese healthcare system, the cost-effectiveness analysis revealed that for each additional QALY gained, the cost increased by $18512.47, and the ICER was notably below the WTP threshold. Single-factor sensitivity analysis indicated that the cost of pemetrexed had a notable influence on the results, yet within the specified range, the ICER value remained below the WTP threshold. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis demonstrated that, at the set WTP value, tislelizumab combination therapy emerged as the most cost-effective choice.

Given the absence of a specific WTP threshold in China, this study applied the WHO-recommended range, which spans from 1 to 3 times the per capita GDP. Previous investigations have estimated the cost-effectiveness threshold for QALYs in China using statistical value of a statistical life (VSL) estimates. The findings suggest that the cost-effectiveness threshold for QALYs in China is approximately 1.5 times the per capita GDP, with upper and lower bounds at 3 times and 1.2 times the per capita GDP, respectively. Notably, the tislelizumab arm remains economically advantageous when the threshold is set at 1.5 times the GDP.

Previous studies have evaluated the economic implications of first-line tislelizumab treatment for NSCLC patients from both healthcare and health system perspectives utilizing modeling techniques such as Markov models and partitioned survival models. Xia Luo (2022) [16] reported an ICER of $28749/QALY, while Xueyan Liang (2023) [18] and Zhao M (2023) [25] reported ICER values of $26162/QALY and $19450/QALY, respectively. All ICER values fell below the set thresholds. Consequently, all studies concluded that the combination of tislelizumab with chemotherapy is more cost-effective than chemotherapy alone.

A distinguishing feature of this study lies in its utilization of a four-state Markov model for simulation that encompasses progression-free health states, progressed disease, terminal PD, and death. The terminal progression state represents the condition of patients who experience a second progression after treatment, with some continuing to receive anticancer treatment until death. Moreover, this study incorporates the most recent drug prices of tislelizumab following its inclusion in the national centralized procurement program, making it more relevant for current clinical decision-making. The utility value data used in this study are derived from health-related quality of life assessments within the Chinese population, enhancing its reflection of real-world conditions.

This study has several limitations. First, long-term efficacy data were obtained by extrapolating clinical trial data through survival analysis methods, which may introduce some deviation from the actual results. Second, the efficacy data in this study are based on the RATIONALE 304 trial, where the OS data were not available, and neither group reached the median OS, potentially leading to an overestimation or underestimation of efficacy. More mature data are needed to validate the model. Third, assuming that 100% of patients in both groups receive subsequent anticancer treatment may not fully reflect the real-world disease progression rates. Future research could benefit from incorporating real-world data to validate the results. Fourth, the cost parameters used in the model were primarily obtained from databases and published literature supplemented by expert consultations. While these cost data sources are diverse and internally heterogeneous, they represent the best available information. A sensitivity analysis confirmed that the cost parameters do not significantly affect the robustness of the results. Fifth, utility values for the nonprogression and progression health states in the model are based on a study involving the Chinese population, as utility data for patients were not provided in the RATIONALE 304 trial. Adverse event disutility values are also derived from the literature. Sensitivity analysis has shown that these utility values do not significantly affect the robustness of the study results. Sixth, due to limited trial sample size, sub-group analyses of cost-effectiveness could not be performed by PD-L1 subgroup, where heterogeneity may exist. Last, this study did not consider treatment discontinuation rates, potentially overlooking how discontinuation affects clinical efficacy. Future real-world studies should compare the safety of both treatments to better understand the value of tislelizumab.

Conclusions

In summary, this study offers valuable insights into the cost-effectiveness of combining tislelizumab with chemotherapy compared to chemotherapy alone for treating advanced nsq-NSCLC in the Chinese healthcare system. This economic evaluation revealed that, from a healthcare perspective, the optimal strategy for first-line treatment of patients with nsq-NSCLC is the combination of tislelizumab with chemotherapy. These findings are valuable for informing clinical treatment decisions.

However, it is essential to acknowledge these limitations when interpreting the findings. Further research with more mature data and real-world evidence could enhance the accuracy of the findings.

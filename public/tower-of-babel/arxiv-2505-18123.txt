Comment on “Geometry of the Grosse-Wulkenhaar model”
D. Prekrat

Abstract
We clarify a key point in the geometric reinterpretation of the Grosse-Wulkenhaar (GW) model proposed in “Geometry of the Grosse-Wulkenhaar model” [JHEP 03 (2010) 053]. Specifically, we show that the analysis in Section 6 was performed not for the actual Ω \Omega -term in the GW action, which involves both ordinary and star-products, but for a closely related term containing only star-products. Once corrected, the main conclusion—relating the harmonic potential term to background curvature—remains valid, though the parameter identification must be revised. This also resolves a discrepancy concerning the emergence of certain vacuum solutions in the self-dual limit of the model.

3
Conclusion and Discussion

In summary, we clarified a key step in the interpretation of the GW model as a scalar field theory on truncated Heisenberg space with curvature coupling, as proposed in
[
1
]
. Specifically, we show that the analysis in Section 6 was applied not to the actual
\Omega
-term in the GW action, which mixes ordinary and star-products, but to a closely related term built solely from star-products. Once corrected, the main conclusion—relating the harmonic potential term to background curvature—remains valid, though the parameter identification must be revised.

This correction also resolves the initial inconsistency regarding the special vacuum solution. Namely, the revised parameter identifications propagate into the matrix model formulation given in Appendix A of
[
7
]
, where the parameters
c_{2}
,
c_{4}
, and
c_{r}
scale as
1/\kappa
and thus diverge as
\kappa\to 0
near self-duality, while the kinetic term remains unaffected. This contrasts with the previously assumed, but incorrect,
\kappa\to 1/2
limit. As a result, the non-kinetic terms dominate the dynamics in the
\kappa\to 0
regime, explaining why the vacuum solution (
1.4
) emerges only when the kinetic term becomes negligible.

Equivalently, multiplying the equation of motion (
[
7
]
.14) by
\kappa
introduces a prefactor
\kappa
in front of its kinetic part, while the remainder of the equation becomes
\kappa
-independent. In the
\kappa\to 0
limit, this effectively eliminates the kinetic contribution and ensures the existence of the special solution (
[
7
]
.16), which is equivalent to (
1.4
).

This reconciles the results of
[
2
]
and
[
7
]
, and importantly, it leaves intact the core arguments in
[
7
]
that link the shift of the triple point to the model’s renormalizability.

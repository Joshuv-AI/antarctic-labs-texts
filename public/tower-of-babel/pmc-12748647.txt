A new automatic forecasting method based on explainable deep dendritic artificial neural network
Authors: Eren Bas, Erol Egrioglu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Forecasting practitioners need automated forecasting methods that do not involve subjective decisions. For this reason, in recent years, the literature has been trying to automate forecasting methods. Automated forecasting methods are usually based on various models or variable selection strategies and various hypothesis tests for data preprocessing. This study proposes input significance tests for model inputs of deep dendritic recurrent neural networks and model validity tests, in the context of explainability studies. The performance of the proposed tests is investigated with a simulation study. Secondly, based on the developed tests and some statistical tools, a new automatic forecasting method for deep dendritic recurrent neural networks is proposed. The forecasting ability of the proposed method is compared with other successful forecasting methods in the literature using M3 and M4 competition time series.

Introduction

Knowing the future values of many time series encountered in real life plays an important role for decision makers in terms of future planning. Forecasting methods are frequently needed in the fields of economics, finance, health, and engineering. Statistical forecasting methods focus on identifying the components of the time series and analysing them with statistical methods. In solving the forecasting problem, it is common to violate assumptions such as nonlinearity, normal distribution, and constant variance. Contemporary machine learning methods, such as artificial neural networks, which theoretically do not require assumptions such as normality, linearity, and constant variance, have become popular for the problem of forecasting. Artificial neural networks can easily adapt to non-linear relationships between variables due to their flexible model structure. Deep neural networks can be divided into recurrent and feed-forward networks. Since recurrent neural networks include time steps in their architecture and formulas, they can provide a model suitable for the forecasting of time series. For this reason, they are frequently used in the literature to obtain future forecasts. In the literature, deep learning methods have found a place in many forecasting studies1. conducted a literature study on time series forecasting with deep learning. Although there are many automated forecasting approaches in the literature, there is no work on automating deep neural networks. In this study, a new automatic forecasting method is presented by proposing an explainable version of the “DeepDenT” artificial neural network proposed by26. The first contribution of the study is the development of input significance tests and model validity tests for the DeepDenT neural network. In this context, in addition to testing each input in the explainable DeepDenT neural network, the importance factors of the inputs can also be calculated. The second contribution of the study is the introduction of an automatic forecasting method based on the explainable DeepDenT neural network. In the second section, information about the “DeepDenT” artificial neural network is given. Algorithms for model validity and input significance tests within the scope of explainable DeepDenT artificial neural network studies are presented, and the performance of these algorithms is demonstrated with a simulation study in the third section. In the fourth section, the proposed automatic forecasting algorithm is presented. The fifth section presents simulation study results. The sixth section presents the results obtained from the application of the proposed automatic forecasting algorithm on M3 and M4 competition data. In the last section, the findings are discussed, and information about future work is given.

Explainable deep dendritic artificial neural network

Explainability studies in artificial neural networks have gained importance in recent years. There are many different explainability studies in the literature. In general, explainability is to tell the story of how the output of the model is generated, given an input. In classical forecasting models, since the models are linear, explainability can be realized directly by interpreting the model coefficients. In the classical forecasting model, the significance of each variable and model validity are realised through hypothesis tests. In this study, model validity and input significance tests for the “DeepDenT” deep feedback neural network for explainability are presented for the first time, inspired by the method developed by28 for a multilayer perceptron. A multiple regression method for the outputs of the trained neural network is used to apply the tests and determine the importance scores of the inputs. For explainable DeepDenT, model validity, input significance, and importance scores are obtained in the following algorithm.

Conclusions and discussion

In this study, the explainability studies of DeepDenT deep artificial neural network, and an automatic forecasting method based on the explainable DeepDenT neural network are presented. Especially in recent years, the applications of deep artificial neural networks in solving forecasting problems are frequently encountered in the literature. Unfortunately, artificial intelligence methods and artificial neural networks used in the forecasting problem do not have the following features that classical forecasting methods have.

Testing the validity of the model used in the method. Testing the significance of the explanatory variables or inputs in the model used in the method, or their contribution to the model. Determining the effect of the coefficients in the model used in the method or the inputs of the model.

All these details can be easily obtained in a linear model used for classical forecasting with good characteristics and appropriate data sets. A machine learning, artificial intelligence, or artificial neural network model, on the other hand, lacks these features and has a larger number of hyperparameters compared to the classical forecasting method. An objective method for determining the values of these hyperparameters has not yet been proposed to reduce the computation time.

In addition to all these, in recurrent neural networks such as LSTM and GRU, which are the most widely used deep learning methods in the literature, the cells are realized as in the classical neuron model, as a summation of the incoming inputs. In addition, due to the fact that recurrent networks are trained with training algorithms based on derivative information, the problem of exploding and vanishing gradients arises. The LSTM neural network has found a solution to the exploding and vanishing gradient problem with gates, but these gates cause the number of parameters in the model to increase four times.

Another important problem in solving the forecasting problem is the determination of lagged variables or model degrees. The proposed methods presented in this study provide solutions to these problems in the literature.

Bootstrap hypothesis testing, which does not require any distributional assumptions, was used for hypothesis testing.

In this study, an automatic forecasting method based on explainable DeepDenT is presented. In the automatic forecasting method, hyper-parameter values are automatically selected by the user within predefined ranges, while lagged variables in the model or features of the model can be determined automatically. The proposed automatic forecasting method provides the practitioner with a forecasting method that does not require subjective decision-making. The proposed automatic forecasting method has been tested on many applications and time series to demonstrate its performance. It is observed that the proposed methods have a good performance on both stock market time series and M3 and M4 competition time series. The fact that comparisons were not made with more recent methods has been added to the study as a limitation of the study. We do not claim that the proposed method will be better than all methods in the literature for every data set.

Although there is no problem in the computation time of the methods presented in the study in general, the computation time of the proposed automatic forecasting method will be high, especially in the case of obtaining forecasts for many products in a short period of time, such as retail sales forecasting. In addition, it is known that in problems such as retail sales forecasting, different products will affect each other’s sales figures, and it may be useful to deal with multivariate time series analysis. Therefore, converting the method into a multivariate version for such problems and increasing the computation time of the method by narrowing the ranges given for the hyperparameter seem to be the measures that can be taken. An effective method for reducing the computation time of the proposed method can be the estimation of hyperparameters. All these solutions and details give direction to future studies.

There are different approaches to explainability in the literature. The linear model used in this approach provides the contribution of each input to the model, while linear models used for forecasting with partial dependence, the contribution and significance of each input to the model can be tested. However, the inability to perform this operation in the DeepDenT model is addressed in the explainability section of this study. A limitation of the study is that explainability cannot be ensured for each observation point.

From amoebas to pluripotential theory on hybrid analytic spaces
Sébastien Boucksom

Abstract
. These lecture notes are an introduction to the use of non-Archimedean geometry in the study of meromorphic degenerations of complex algebraic varieties. They provide a self-contained discussion of hybrid spaces, which fill in one-parameter degenerations with the associated non-Archimedean Berkovich space as a central fiber. The main focus is on the interplay between complex and non-Archimedean pluripotential theory, and on the relation between convergence of psh metrics and the associated Monge–Ampère measures in the hybrid space, following work of the author with M. Jonsson, and recent breakthrough work by Y. Li.



Introduction

The main purpose of these lecture notes is to provide an introduction to the use of non-Archimedean geometry in the study of one-parameter meromorphic degenerations
X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
of complex algebraic varieties, i.e. complex analytic families over a small punctured disc attached to algebraic varieties
X_{K}
over the field
K=\mathbb{C}\{t\}
of convergent Laurent series.

The appearance of non-Archimedean techniques in this context can be traced back at least to the pioneering work of George Bergman
[
Berg71b
]
, which gave birth to tropical geometry. Bergman considered a meromorphic degeneration of algebraic subvarieties
X_{t}\subset T=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
of an algebraic torus,
t\in\mathbb{D}^{\times}
, and provided a realization—at first partly conjectural, later fully settled by Bieri–Groves
[
BG84
]
—of the rescaled limit as
t\to 0
of the
amoeba
of
X_{t}
, i.e. its image under the log map
T\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as the non-Archimedean analogue of the amoeba, defined in terms of (semi)valuations on the algebraic variety
X_{K}
.

From a more recent perspective, the space
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
of semivaluations on an algebraic
K
-variety
X_{K}
, compatible with the natural valuation of
K
, is an instance of
Berkovich analytification

[
Berk90
]
. Assuming now
X_{K}
to be smooth and projective, which is the main case of interest in these notes,
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
can alternatively be described in terms of
snc models

\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}
, i.e. extensions of the family
X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
obtained by adding in a simple normal crossing divisor
\mathcal{X}_{0}
as a central fiber. To each snc model
\mathcal{X}
is attached a finite simplicial complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
encoding the combinatorics of the intersections of components of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, and which can be canonically realized as a subspace of
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
using monomial valuations; this further comes with a natural retraction map
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, and
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
can then be recovered as the projective limit of all such dual complexes.

Inspired by the analogy between the retraction maps
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
and Lagrangian fibrations, Kontsevich proposed in the early 2000’s an ambitious program
[
KS06
]
aiming to approach the Strominger–Yau–Zaslow conjecture—and, more generally, study the limiting behavior of degenerations
X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
of Calabi–Yau manifolds from a differential geometric perspective—in terms of the non-Archimedean analogue of a Calabi–Yau metric on the associated Berkovich space
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
. The first steps of this program were taken in the unpublished notes
[
KT02
]
, and later developed in
[
BFJ15
,
BFJ16
]
, laying the basis of a non-Archimedean analogue of complex pluripotential theory.

The relation between a meromorphic degeneration
X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
and the associated Berkovich space
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
is best materialized in the
hybrid space

X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathbb{D}
, a topological extension of
X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
that adds
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}=X_{0}
as a central fiber. This construction, originally introduced by Berkovich in
[
Berk09
]
, allows to formulate the convergence of measures and metrics on the complex analytic fibers
X_{t}
to similar objects on the non-Archimedean space
X_{0}
, and was exploited in
[
BoJ17
]
to establish the convergence of Calabi–Yau volume forms to their non-Archimedean analogue, defined in terms of the Kontsevich–Soibelman
essential skeleton

[
KS06
,
MN15
,
NX16
]
.

This was taken much further in the recent breakthrough work of Y. Li
[
Li23
]
, who managed to establish a similar convergence result for the Calabi–Yau potentials, under a conjectural invariance property of the non-Archimedean Calabi–Yau potentials that was in turn established for certain degenerations of Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces in
[
HJMM24
,
AH23
,
Li24b
]
.

The contents of these notes basically consist in a reasonably self-contained introduction to the above developments:

•

the introductory Section
1
discusses Bergman’s work on amoebas and tropicalizations;

•

Section
2
introduces Berkovich’s general notion of analytification of schemes over a normed ring, including the notion of continuous metrics and Fubini–Study metrics on line bundles in this context;

•

Section
3
introduces the main character of these notes, to wit the hybrid space of a meromorphic degeneration, and establishes its basic topological properties;

•

Section
4
makes a deeper study of the Berkovich space of a meromorphic degeneration, introducing the notion of models and the associated model metrics and functions, as well as the description of Berkovich analytification as a limit of dual complexes;

•

Section
5
introduces the hybrid version of model functions and log maps, and shows that they characterize the hybrid topology;

•

building on this, Section
6
focuses on the convergence of measures in the hybrid space, and provides a detailed proof of the main result of
[
BoJ17
]
, which includes the case of convergence of Calabi–Yau volume forms;

•

Section
7
provides a (rather quick) introduction to complex and non-Archimedean pluripotential theory, and establishes a slightly improved version of Favre’s convergence result for Monge–Ampère measures
[
Fav20
]
;

•

Section
8
provides a proof of the main result of
[
Li23
]
, with an occasional mild simplification and in the slightly more general setting considered in
[
BoJ17
]
;

•

finally, Appendix
A
reviews Kołodziej’s stability results for solutions to complex Monge–Ampère equations, and their crucial asymmetric version due to Yang Li.

We also refer to Yang Li’s very nice survey
[
Li22b
]
for another introduction to the present circle of ideas. For lack of time, the present notes unfortunately do not cover many important more recent developments, including

1.
Amoebas, valuations and tropicalization

This introductory section aims to motivate the use of (semi)valuations in the study of degenerations of algebraic varieties through the historical example of tropicalizations as limits of amoebas.

1.1.
The asymptotic cone of an amoeba

The complex algebraic torus
T:=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
is equipped with a natural
log map

\Log\colon T\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\quad x\mapsto(-\log|x_{1}|,\dots,-\log|x_{n}|),

which is continuous, proper, and in fact a principal
(S^{1})^{n}
-bundle.

Definition 1.1
.

The
amoeba
of an algebraic subvariety
Z\subset T
is defined as its image
\Log(Z)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
under the log map.

Here we identify
Z
with the corresponding complex analytic variety, i.e. its set of complex points endowed with the Euclidean topology. The purpose of what follows is to discuss the pioneering work
[
Berg71b
]
, where George Bergman discovered that the large scale geometry of the amoeba, i.e. its asymptotic cone
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\Log(Z)
, can be realized as a non-Archimedean version of the amoeba, defined in terms of (semi)valuations, and further admits a piecewise linear structure (see Figure 1), thereby giving birth to what is now called
tropical geometry
.

1.1.1.
Semivaluations

We view
T
as an affine algebraic variety with ring of functions

\mathcal{O}(T)=\mathbb{C}[z_{1}^{\pm},\dots,z_{n}^{\pm}]

the ring of Laurent polynomials

(1.1)

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha},

where we use the standard notation
z^{\alpha}:=\prod_{i}z_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
and
a_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}
is nonzero for only finitely many
\alpha
. For any (complex) point
x\in T
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we have

\log|x^{\alpha}|=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\log|x_{i}|=-\alpha\cdot\Log(x)

and hence

(1.2)

-\log|f(x)|\geq\min_{a_{\alpha}\neq 0}\alpha\cdot\Log(x)-C

for each Laurent polynonial
f
, where
C>0
only depends on
f
.

Consider next a sequence
(x_{j})
of points of
T
, and
\varepsilon_{j}\in(0,1)
such that
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
and

\varepsilon_{j}\Log(x_{j})
admits a limit in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each
f\in\mathcal{O}(T)
,
-\varepsilon_{j}\log|f(x_{j})|
is then bounded below, by (
1.2
). Using Tychonoff’s theorem, we may thus assume (after passing to a generalized subsequence, i.e. a subnet) that
-\varepsilon_{j}\log|f(x_{j})|
admits a limit
v(f)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
for all
f\in\mathcal{O}(T)
(the use of nets being due to the uncountability of this ring). Using the trivial relations

-\varepsilon_{j}\log|(fg)(x_{j})|=-\varepsilon_{j}\log|f(x_{j})|-\varepsilon_{j}\log|g(x_{j})|,

-\varepsilon_{j}\log|(f+g)(x_{j})|\geq\min\left\{-\varepsilon_{j}\log|f(x_{j})|,-\varepsilon_{j}\log|g(x_{j})|\right\}-\varepsilon_{j}\log 2

for all
f,g\in\mathcal{O}(T)
, we see that the pointwise limit defines a
semivaluation
2
2

2

It is a valuation if further
v(f)=\infty\Rightarrow f=0
.
on
\mathcal{O}(T)
, i.e. a map

v\colon\mathcal{O}(T)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

such that

v(fg)=v(f)+v(g),\quad v(f+g)\geq\min\{v(f),v(g)\},\quad v(0)=+\infty,

whose restriction to the ground field
\mathbb{C}
further coincides with the
trivial valuation

v_{0}\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, such that
v_{0}(a)=0
for
a\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
v_{0}(0)=+\infty
. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 1.2
.

For any complex affine variety
X
with ring of functions
\mathcal{O}(X)
, we denote by
X^{\mathrm{an}}
the set of all semivaluations
v
on
\mathcal{O}(X)
whose restriction to
\mathbb{C}
coincides with the trivial valuation
v_{0}
.

Endowed with the topology of pointwise convergence, the space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is Hausdorff and locally compact (see §
2
).

The following useful simple fact is left as an exercise:

Lemma 1.3
.

Pick
v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
and a finite set
(f_{i})
in
\mathcal{O}(X)
. If
v(\sum_{i}f_{i})>\min_{i}v(f_{i})
, then the minimum is achieved at least twice.

1.1.2.
The non-Archimedean amoeba

Consider now a subvariety
Z\subset T
, corresponding to a (radical) ideal
I_{Z}\subset\mathcal{O}(T)
, with amoeba
\Log(Z)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

By definition, each point in the asymptotic cone
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\Log(Z)
is the limit in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\varepsilon_{j}\Log(x_{j})
for some sequence (or net) of complex points
x_{j}\in Z
and
\varepsilon_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
converging to
0
. The considerations of §
2
thus apply and show that, after passing to a subnet,
f\mapsto-\varepsilon_{j}\log|f(x_{j})|
converges pointwise to a semivaluation
v
on
\mathcal{O}(T)
, trivial on
\mathbb{C}
. Since all
x_{j}
lie in
Z
, any
f\in I_{Z}
further satisfies
-\varepsilon_{j}\log|f(x_{j})|=+\infty
, and hence
v(f)=+\infty
, which means that
v
descends to a semivaluation on
\mathcal{O}(T)/I_{Z}\simeq\mathcal{O}(Z)
, i.e. an element of
Z^{\mathrm{an}}\subset T^{\mathrm{an}}
. In particular, each component
-\varepsilon_{j}\log|z_{i}(x_{j})|
,
i=1,\dots,n
, of
\varepsilon_{j}\Log(x_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
converges to
v(z_{i})
, which is finite since
z_{i}
is invertible.

We conclude that the asymptotic cone
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\Log(Z)
is contained in the
non-Archimedean amoeba

\Log(Z^{\mathrm{an}})
, i.e. the image of
Z^{\mathrm{an}}\subset T^{\mathrm{an}}
under the
non-Archimedean log map

\Log\colon T^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

defined by

\Log(v):=(v(z_{1}),\dots,v(z_{n})).

Like its complex counterpart, the non-Archimedean log map is continuous and proper—and can in fact be interpreted as an affinoid torus fibration in the language of Berkovich geometry. Perhaps more surprinsigly, we have:

Lemma 1.4
.

The map
\Log\colon T^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
admits a
unique
continuous section, which takes
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the simplest valuation with value
w_{i}
on
z_{i}
,
i.e.
the
monomial valuation

\mathrm{val}_{w}(\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}):=\min_{a_{\alpha}\neq 0}\alpha\cdot w.

Proof.

It is clear that
w\mapsto\mathrm{val}_{w}
is a continuous section. To see uniqueness, pick
v\in T^{\mathrm{an}}
and
f=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{O}(T)
. For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we have
v(z^{\alpha})=\mathrm{val}_{w}(z^{\alpha})=\alpha\cdot w
with
w:=\Log(v)
. The semivaluation property of
v
yields

(1.3)

v(f)\geq\min_{a_{\alpha}\neq 0}v(z^{\alpha})=\mathrm{val}_{w}(f).

If
w
has
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent entries, then
\alpha\cdot w\neq\beta\cdot w
when
\alpha\neq\beta
, and Lemma
1.3
thus shows that equality holds in (
1.3
). This means that any section of
\Log
necessarily coincides with
\mathrm{val}_{w}
on the dense subset of
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent entries, and uniqueness of continuous sections follows.
∎

1.1.3.
Tropicalization

We next claim that the non-Archimedean amoeba
\Log(Z^{\mathrm{an}})
is in turn contained in the
tropicalization

Z^{\mathrm{trop}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

of
Z
, defined as the intersection of all
tropical hypersurfaces

V(f^{\mathrm{trop}})
attached to the (concave) piecewise linear functions

f^{\mathrm{trop}}(w):=\min_{a_{\alpha}\neq 0}\alpha\cdot w=\mathrm{val}_{w}(f)

with
f=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
in
I_{Z}
. Here
V(f^{\mathrm{trop}})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the non-linearity locus of
f^{\mathrm{trop}}
, i.e. the set of points at which the minimum defining it is achieved by at least two indices
\alpha\neq\beta
.

To see the claim, note that for any
v\in Z^{\mathrm{an}}\subset T^{\mathrm{an}}
and
f=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
in
I_{Z}
, we have
v(f)=\infty
, while
v(z^{\alpha})
is finite for all
\alpha
. Setting
w:=\Log(v)
, Lemma
1.3
shows that the right-hand minimum in

v(f)>\min_{a_{\alpha}\neq 0}v(z^{\alpha})=\min_{a_{\alpha}\neq 0}w\cdot\alpha=f^{\mathrm{trop}}(w)

is achieved at least twice, and the claim follows. At this point, we have thus established the chain of inclusions

(1.4)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\Log(Z)\subset\Log(Z^{\mathrm{an}})\subset Z^{\mathrm{trop}}.

In the present setting, the fundamental theorem of tropical geometry can now be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.5
.

The inclusions in (
1.4
) are equalities. Furthermore,
Z^{\mathrm{trop}}
is a rational polyhedral cone complex,
i.e.
a finite union of rational polyhedral cones, of dimension equal to
\dim Z
.

This result was essentially already established in
[
Berg71b
]
: Bergman proved
Z^{\mathrm{trop}}\subset\Log(Z^{\mathrm{an}})
(see Proposition
2.14
below), and showed that
Z^{\mathrm{trop}}\subset\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\Log(Z)
would follow from the last point of the theorem, which was later established by Bieri–Groves
[
BG84
]
. While the latter is clear when
Z
is a hypersurface, defined by a single equation, it becomes nontrivial in the general case because, for a given finite set
(f_{i})
of generators of
I_{Z}
, it need not be the case that
Z^{\mathrm{trop}}
coincides with the intersection of the tropical hypersurfaces
V(f_{i}^{\mathrm{trop}})
. However, this can be shown to hold for
some
well-chosen set of generators (see
[
MS15
, Theorem 2.6.5]
), which yields the last point.

More recently, the equality between the asymptotic cone and the non-Archimedean amoeba was directly obtained by Jonsson using hybrid spaces
[
Jon16
]
. We shall return to this point of view in Example
3.2
below.

1.3.
Toric degenerations

We will later consider degenerations of smooth varieties to normal crossing divisors, which are (locally and transversally) modelled on the meromorphic degeneration

(1.6)

Z_{t}:=\left\{t=\prod_{i\in J}z_{i}^{b_{i}}\right\}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{J}

for a finite set
J
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{J}
nonzero. Here the rescaled amoeba
\Log_{t}(Z_{t})
is actually independent of
t
, equal to the affine hyperplane

H=H_{J,b}:=\{b\cdot w=1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{J}

For later use, we make a number of observations in this context. Consider quite generally a principal bundle
\pi\colon P\to M
with respect to a compact Lie group
G
. Each fiber
\pi^{-1}(m)
admits a unique
G
-invariant probability measure
\rho_{m}
, corresponding to the (normalized) Haar measure of
G
. Any function
f\in\Cz(P)
gives rise to a function
\int_{P/M}f\in\Cz(M)
, with value
\int_{\pi^{-1}(m)}f\,\rho_{m}
at
m\in M
. Dually, any measure
\mu
on
M
induces a measure on
P
, which we shall (somewhat abusively) denote by
\pi^{\star}\mu
, such that

(1.7)

\int_{P}f\,\pi^{\star}\mu=\int_{M}\left(\int_{P/M}f\right)\,\mu=\int_{M}\mu(dm)\int_{\pi^{-1}(m)}f\,\rho_{m}

for
f\in C^{0}_{c}(P)
. The measure
\pi^{\star}\mu
is
G
-invariant, and conversely any
G
-invariant measure
\nu
on
P
can be written as
\nu=\pi^{\star}\mu
with
\mu=\pi_{\star}\nu
.

Example 1.7
.

\Omega_{t}
on
Z_{t}
, by requiring

\Omega_{t}\wedge\frac{dt}{t}=\Omega

with
t=\prod_{i}z_{i}^{b_{i}}
. Applying (
1.8
) to the each component of the complexification

G_{\mathbb{C}}=\{\tau\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{J}\mid\prod_{i}\tau_{i}^{b_{i}}=1\},

one checks (see
[
BoJ17
, §1.4]
) that the associated
G
-invariant smooth positive volume form
|\Omega_{t}|^{2}
on
Z_{t}
satisfies

(1.9)

\varepsilon_{t}^{p}|\Omega_{t}|^{2}=(2\pi)^{p}\Log_{t}^{\star}\sigma_{H}

with
p:=\dim Z_{t}=|J|-1
and
\sigma_{H}
the Lebesgue measure of the affine hyperplane
H
induced by its equation in
\mathbb{R}^{J}
.

As a final remark, pick a smooth convex function
f
on a convex open subset
U\subset H
. Then
\Log_{t}^{\star}f
is a plurisubharmonic (psh) function on the
G
-invariant open subset
\Log_{t}^{-1}(U)\subset Z_{t}
, and a computation reveals that the complex and real Monge–Ampère measures are related by

(1.10)

\left(dd^{c}\Log_{t}^{\star}f\right)^{p}=\varepsilon_{t}^{p}\Log_{t}^{\star}\MA_{\mathbb{R}}(f).

By approximation, this remains true for any convex function, the Monge–Ampère measures being respectively understood in the sense of Bedford–Taylor and Alexandrov.

2.
Berkovich analytification

This section reviews Berkovich’s general construction of analytic spaces attached to schemes over a normed ring
[
Berk90
,
Berk09
]
. We take here an elementary approach, merely viewing these analytifications as topological spaces, and refer to
[
Poi13
,
LP24
]
for a more advanced study.

2.1.
The Berkovich spectrum

Let
A
be a ring
5
5

5

All rings in these notes are assumed to be commutative and with a unit.
. A
seminorm

\|\cdot\|\colon A\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is defined as a function that satisfies

•

\|0\|=0
,
\|\pm 1\|=1
;

•

\|a+b\|\leq\|a\|+\|b\|
for all
a,b\in A
.

It is a
norm
if further
\|a\|=0\Rightarrow a=0
. A seminorm is
submultiplicative
if
\|ab\|\leq\|a\|\cdot\|b\|
for all
a,b
, and
multiplicative
if equality holds.

We usually denote a multiplicative seminorm by the ‘absolute value’ notation
|\cdot|
. Any multiplicative seminorm is induced by a
character
of
A
, i.e. a ring map to a complete valued field. Indeed, the kernel of
|\cdot|
is a prime ideal
P
, and
|\cdot|
induces a multiplicative norm on the fraction field of
A/P
, whose completion is called the
residue field

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(|\cdot|)
. This comes with a character
A\to\mathcal{H}
inducing
|\cdot|
, and any other character with the same property factors through
\mathcal{H}
.

A multiplicative seminorm
|\cdot|
is called
non-Archimedean
if it is bounded on the image of
\mathbb{Z}
in
A
. As is well-known, this is the case iff the ultrametric inequality

|a+b|\leq\max\{|a|,|b|\}

holds, which equivalently means that

v:=-\log|\cdot|

is a semivaluation
v\colon A\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, i.e.

v(ab)=v(a)+v(b),\quad v(a+v)\geq\min\{v(a),v(b)\}.

Otherwise,
|\cdot|
is called
Archimedean
. In that case,
\mathbb{Q}
injects in the residue field
\mathcal{H}
, which thus contains a copy of
\mathbb{R}
, and hence is isomorphic to either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, by the Gelfand–Mazur theorem.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A=(A,\|\cdot\|_{A})
be a normed ring,
i.e.
a ring equipped with a submultiplicative norm. The
Berkovich spectrum

\mathcal{M}(A)
is defined as the set of all
bounded
multiplicative seminorms
|\cdot|\colon A\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence.

Here bounded means
|\cdot|\leq C\|\cdot\|_{A}
for some
C>0
, which can always be taken equal to
1
, as one easily sees using the multiplicativity (resp. submultiplicativity) of
|\cdot|
(resp.
\|\cdot\|
).

A point of
\mathcal{M}(A)
will usually be labelled by
x
, the corresponding multiplicative seminorm being denoted by
A\ni f\mapsto|f(x)|
, where
f\mapsto f(x)
is understood as the character
A\to\mathcal{H}(x)
to the residue field. Each
f\in A
thus induces a continuous function

|f|\colon\mathcal{M}(A)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

such that
\sup_{\mathcal{M}(A)}|f|\leq\|f\|_{A}
, and the topology of
\mathcal{M}(A)
is generated by such functions.

The space
\mathcal{M}(A)
is always nonempty
[
Berk90
, Theorem 1.2.1]
. It is further compact Hausdorff, as an easy consequence of Tychonoff’s theorem.

Remark 2.2
.

The above definition is usually introduced when
A
is complete for its norm,
i.e.
a Banach ring. Since we merely view analytifications as topological spaces, this makes no difference to us, since any bounded multiplicative seminorm on
A
automatically extends to the completion of
A
(compare Proposition
2.16
below). Similarly,
\|\cdot\|_{A}
can be allowed to be merely a seminorm, since the set
N\subset A
of elements with zero norm is then a closed ideal, and
\|\cdot\|_{A}
descends to a norm on
A/N
such that
\mathcal{M}(A)\simeq\mathcal{M}(A/N)
.

Remark 2.3
.

When the norm
\|\cdot\|_{A}
is ultrametric,
\mathcal{M}(A)
was already introduced in
[
Berg71a
]
, and proved to be non-empty in Theorem 1 therein.

Example 2.4
.

For a complex Banach algebra
A
, the Gelfand–Mazur theorem implies that the residue field of any element in
\mathcal{M}(A)
is equal to
\mathbb{C}
, so that
\mathcal{M}(A)
can be identified with the usual Gelfand spectrum of
A
,
i.e.
the space of bounded characters
A\to\mathbb{C}
.

Example 2.5
.

If
K=(K,|\cdot|_{K})
is a valued field,
i.e.
a field equipped with a multiplicative norm, then
\mathcal{M}(K)
reduces to the point
|\cdot|_{K}
.

Example 2.6
.

The ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}
equipped with the usual absolute value
|\cdot|_{\infty}
is a (complete) normed ring. As a consequence of the well-known Ostrowski theorem, each element of
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{Z})
is either of the form
|\cdot|_{p}^{\varepsilon}
for some prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\varepsilon\in[0,\infty]
, the case
\varepsilon=0
(resp.
\varepsilon=\infty
) being understood as the trivial absolute
|\cdot|_{0}
on
\mathbb{Z}
(resp. on
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
), or
|\cdot|_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, the case
\varepsilon=0
being again
|\cdot|_{0}
. Thus
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{Z})
is a star-shaped
\mathbb{R}
-tree rooted at
|\cdot|_{0}
, endowed with the
weak
tree topology, for which each neighborhood of
|\cdot|_{0}
contains all but finitely many of the branches. Analytic geometry over
\mathbb{Z}
has been extensively studied by Poineau
[
Poi13
]
.

Example 2.7
.

The
hybrid field

\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is defined as the field
\mathbb{C}
equipped with the
hybrid norm

\|\cdot\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}:=\max\{|\cdot|_{\infty},|\cdot|_{0}\},

where
|\cdot|_{\infty}
is the usual absolute value and
|\cdot|_{0}
is the trivial one. The hybrid field is a (complete) normed ring, and one checks that each element of
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{hyb}})
is of the form
|\cdot|_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, the case
\varepsilon=0
being again interpreted as
|\cdot|_{0}
. This yields an identification

\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{hyb}})\simeq[0,1].

Analytic geometry over
\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
plays a central role in these notes, and will be studied in more detail in §
3
.

2.2.
General Berkovich analytification

We use
[
Berk09
, §1]
as a reference for what follows.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
A=(A,\|\cdot\|_{A})
be a normed ring, and
X
an affine scheme of finite type over
A
, corresponding to an
A
-algebra of finite type
\mathcal{O}(X)
. The
Berkovich analytification

X^{\mathrm{an}}
is defined as the set of all multiplicative seminorms
|\cdot|\colon\mathcal{O}(X)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
whose pullback to
A
is bounded by
\|\cdot\|_{A}
. It is equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence.

As above, a point of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is usually labelled by
x
,
\mathcal{O}(X)\ni f\mapsto|f(x)|
being the associated multiplicative seminorm. Each
f\in\mathcal{O}(X)
thus defines a continuous function

|f|\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

the topology of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
being generated by such functions. The space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is Hausdorff, and comes with a continuous
structure map

X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathcal{M}(A).

Analytification is functorial, i.e. any map
X\to Y
of affine
A
-schemes induces a continuous map
X^{\mathrm{an}}\to Y^{\mathrm{an}}
compatible with the structure maps, and with composition.

Lemma 2.9
.

The space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is locally compact and countable at infinity.

Note, however, that
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is typically not first countable, so that its topology should be described in terms of nets instead of sequences. This already happens in the case of main interest for these notes, i.e. for algebraic varieties over
A=\mathbb{C}\{t\}
.

Proof.

Pick a finite set of generators
(f_{i})
of the
A
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(X)
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the set

K_{m}:=\{\max_{i}|f_{i}|\leq m\}\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}

is then compact, as a consequence ofTychonoff. The result follows since any given point of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
admits
K_{m}
as a compact neighborhood for
m\gg 1
.
∎

Example 2.10
.

Assume
A=(K,|\cdot|_{K})
is a non-Archimedean valued field, with associated (possibly trivial) valuation
v_{K}=-\log|\cdot|_{K}
. Every multiplicative seminorm
|\cdot|
in
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is then automatically non-Archimedean, being bounded on the image of
\mathbb{Z}\to K
. Setting
v:=-\log|\cdot|
yields an identification of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
with the space of all semivaluations
v\colon\mathcal{O}(X)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
such that
v|_{K}=v_{K}
(compare Definition
1.6
).

If
X\hookrightarrow Y
is a closed embedding of affine
A
-schemes, then
X^{\mathrm{an}}
can be identified with the closed subspace of seminorms in
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
that vanish on each
f\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
lying in the ideal of
X
.

Similarly, if
X\hookrightarrow Y
is an open embedding, then the induced map
X^{\mathrm{an}}\hookrightarrow Y^{\mathrm{an}}
is an open embedding of topological spaces. This allows to globalize the construction and define the analytification

X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathcal{M}(A)

of any
A
-scheme of finite type
X
by gluing together the analytifications of finitely many affine open subschemes covering
X
. The topology of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is thus the coarsest one such that, for any affine open subscheme
U\subset X
, we have

•

U^{\mathrm{an}}\hookrightarrow X^{\mathrm{an}}
is an open embedding;

•

|f|\colon U^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is continuous for each
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
.

Proposition 2.11
.

[
Berk09
, Lemma 1.2]
For any scheme
X
of finite type over a normed ring
A
, the topological space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is:

(i)

locally compact and countable at infinity;

(ii)

Hausdorff if
X
is separated over
A
(
e.g.
a variety over a field);

(iii)

compact Hausdorff if
X
is projective over
A
.

Proof.

Since
X
can be written as a finite union of affine open subschemes, (i) follows from Lemma
2.9
. We next prove (iii). Assume
X
is projective, and pick a closed embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}_{A}
. Then
X^{\mathrm{an}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N,\mathrm{an}}_{A}
is a closed embedding, and it therefore suffices to show that
\mathbb{P}^{N,\mathrm{an}}_{A}
is compact. If we denote by
z_{0},\dots,z_{n}
the homogeneous coordinates of
\mathbb{P}^{N}_{A}
, then each point of
\mathbb{P}^{N,\mathrm{an}}_{A}
lies in the unit polydisc
\{\max_{j\neq i}|z_{j}/z_{i}|\leq 1\}
in one of the coordinate charts
\{z_{i}\neq 0\}
, and (iii) follows since each such polydisc is compact, by Lemma
2.9
and its proof. Finally, (ii) follows from (iii) at least when
X
is quasi-projective, which is enough for our purposes.
∎

Example 2.12
.

The Gelfand–Mazur theorem implies that the analytification of any complex variety
X
with respect to the usual absolute
|\cdot|_{\infty}
on
\mathbb{C}
coincides (as a topological space) with the usual complex analytic space attached to
X
,
i.e.
the set of complex points with the Euclidean topology. The identification is given by sending a complex point
x
lying in some affine open
U\subset X
to the point of
U^{\mathrm{an}}
defined by the multiplicative seminorm
\mathcal{O}(U)\ni f\mapsto|f(x)|_{\infty}
.

Example 2.13
.

Let
X
be a variety over a non-Archimedean valued field
K
, with valuation
v_{K}
. Then each valuation

v\colon K(X)^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}

on the function field of
X
such that
v|_{K}=v_{K}
defines a point in
X^{\mathrm{an}}
. This point is Zariski dense, in the sense that it lies in
U^{\mathrm{an}}
for any affine open subscheme
U
, the corresponding multiplicative seminorm

\mathcal{O}(U)\ni f\mapsto|f(v)|:=e^{-v(f)}

being a norm. More generally, any point of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
corresponds to a valuation

v\colon K(Y)^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}

on the function field of some (closed) subvariety
Y\subset X
, and will loosely be referred to as a
semivaluation
on
X
.

2.3.
Tropicalization, reprise

To illustrate the above ideas, we return to the setting of §
1
, and establish the equality between non-Archimedean amoeba and tropicalization.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
K
be a field endowed with a (possibly trivial) valuation
v_{K}
, and

Z\subset T=\Spec K[z_{1}^{\pm},\dots,z_{n}^{\pm}]

a subvariety of a (split) torus over
K
, with non-Archimedean log map

\Log\colon T^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad v\mapsto(v(z_{1}),\dots,v(z_{n})).

Then the non-Archimedean amoeba
\Log(Z^{\mathrm{an}})
coincides with the tropicalization
Z^{\mathrm{trop}}
,
i.e.
the intersection of the non-linearity loci
V(f^{\mathrm{trop}})
of all piecewise affine concave functions

f^{\mathrm{trop}}(w):=\min_{\alpha}\{\alpha\cdot w+v_{K}(a_{\alpha})\}

with
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
in the ideal
I_{Z}
of
Z
.

Proof.

As in §
1
, the inclusion
\Log(Z^{\mathrm{an}})\subset Z^{\mathrm{trop}}
is a direct consequence of Lemma
1.3
. Conversely, pick
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and consider the monomial valuation
\mathrm{val}_{w}\in T^{\mathrm{an}}
such that

\mathrm{val}_{w}(f)=\min_{\alpha}\mathrm{val}_{w}(a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha})=f^{\mathrm{trop}}(w).

Following
[
Berg71b
]
, we note that
w
lies in
Z^{\mathrm{trop}}
iff, for any
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we have

\mathrm{val}_{w}(z^{\beta})=\sup_{g\in I_{Z}}\mathrm{val}_{w}(z^{\beta}+g).

Indeed, by definition
w\notin Z^{\mathrm{trop}}
iff there exists
g=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
in
I_{Z}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
a_{\beta}\neq 0
and

\min_{\alpha\neq\beta}\{\alpha\cdot w+v_{K}(a_{\alpha})\}=\mathrm{val}_{w}(a_{\beta}z^{\beta}-g)>\beta\cdot w+v_{K}(a_{\beta})=\mathrm{val}_{w}(a_{\beta}z^{\beta}).

Dividing
g
by
a_{\beta}\in K^{\times}
yields the claim.

The multiplicative seminorm
|\cdot|_{w}=e^{-\mathrm{val}_{w}}
of
\mathcal{O}(T)=K[z_{1}^{\pm},\dots,w_{n}^{\pm}]
induces a quotient seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{Z,w}
on
\mathcal{O}(Z)=\mathcal{O}(T)/I_{Z}
, defined by

\|f\|_{Z,w}:=\inf_{g\in I_{Z}}|f+g|_{w}=\exp(-\sup_{g\in I_{Z}}\mathrm{val}_{w}(f+g))

for
f\in\mathcal{O}(T)
, and which is
a priori
merely submultiplicative. By
[
Berk90
, Theorem 1.2.1]
, the Berkovich spectrum of
(\mathcal{O}(Z),\|\cdot\|_{Z,w})
is non-empty (see Remark
2.2
), which means that there exists
v\in Z^{\mathrm{an}}
such that

v(f)\geq\sup_{g\in I_{Z}}\mathrm{val}_{w}(f+g)

for all
f\in\mathcal{O}(T)
. For
w\in Z^{\mathrm{trop}}
, the above observation yields

\sum_{i}\beta_{i}v(z_{i})=v(z^{\beta})\geq\sup_{g\in I_{Z}}\mathrm{val}_{w}(z^{\beta}+g)=\mathrm{val}_{w}(z^{\beta})=\sum_{i}\beta_{i}w_{i}

for all
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. This proves
v(z_{i})=w_{i}
for all
i
, and hence
w=\Log(v)\in\Log(Z^{\mathrm{an}})
.
∎

Remark 2.15
.

The above argument is exactly the one used in
[
Berg71b
]
, which relied on
[
Berg71a
, Theorem 1]
where we have used
[
Berk90
, Theorem 1.2.1]
.

2.4.
Base change

In preparation to the study of hybrid spaces, we study the effect of base change on Berkovich analytification. Consider as above a scheme
X
of finite type over a normed ring
A
, and assume given a normed ring map
\tau\colon A\to B
, i.e. a ring map such that
\|\tau(a)\|_{B}\leq\|a\|_{A}
for all
a\in A
. The analytification of the base change
X_{B}
sits in a commutative diagram of continuous maps

(2.1)

\textstyle{X_{B}^{\mathrm{an}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X^{\mathrm{an}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(A)}

Proposition 2.16
.

Assume the normed ring map
\tau\colon A\to B
has dense image. Then
\mathcal{M}(B)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}(A)
is a homeomorphism onto its image, and
X_{B}^{\mathrm{an}}\hookrightarrow X^{\mathrm{an}}
is a homeomorphism onto the preimage of
\mathcal{M}(B)\subset\mathcal{M}(A)
. If we further assume that
\tau\colon A\to B
is an isometry with dense image (
e.g.
the completion of
A
), then

\mathcal{M}(B)\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathcal{M}(A),\quad X_{B}^{\mathrm{an}}\overset{\sim}{\to}X^{\mathrm{an}}

are both homeomorphisms.

Proof.

Writing
X
as a finite union of affine open subschemes, we may assume without loss that
X
is affine. Pick
f\in\mathcal{O}(X_{B})=\mathcal{O}(X)\otimes_{A}B
, and write
f=\sum_{k=1}^{N}b_{k}\otimes g_{k}
with
b_{k}\in B
and
g_{k}\in\mathcal{O}(X)
. Since
\tau(A)
is dense in
B
, we can then find a sequence
f_{n}\in\mathcal{O}(X)
of the form
f_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{n,k}g_{k}
where
a_{n,k}\in A
satisfies
\tau(a_{n,k})\to b_{k}
for each
k
. We shall then say that
(f_{n})
is an
admissible approximation
of
f
.

To show that
X_{B}^{\mathrm{an}}\to X^{\mathrm{an}}
is a homeomorphism onto its image, pick a net
(y_{i})
in
X_{B}^{\mathrm{an}}
and
y\in X_{B}^{\mathrm{an}}
. Assuming their images
x_{i},x
in
X^{\mathrm{an}}
satisfy
x_{i}\to x
, we need to show
y_{i}\to y
in
X_{B}^{\mathrm{an}}
, i.e.
|f(y_{i})|\to|f(y)|
for any
f\in\mathcal{O}(X_{B})=\mathcal{O}(X)\otimes_{A}B
. Pick an admissible approximation
f_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{n,k}g_{k}
of
f
as above, and set

\varepsilon_{n}:=\max_{1\leq k\leq N}\|\tau(a_{n,k})-b_{k}\|_{B}.

Since
\lim_{i}|g_{k}(x_{i})|=|g_{k}(x)|
for all
k
, we may assume
\sup_{k,i}|g_{k}(x_{i})|\leq C<\infty
, where
C>0
denotes a uniform constant that is allowed to vary from line to line. Then

\big||f(y_{i})|-|f_{n}(x_{i})|\big|=\left|(f-\tau(f_{n}))(y_{i})\right|\leq\varepsilon_{n}C,\quad\big||f(y)|-|f_{n}(x)|\big|\leq\varepsilon_{n}C.

Since
x_{i}\to x
, we have
|f_{n}(x_{i})|\to|f_{n}(x)|
for each
n
. We infer

\limsup_{i}\left||f(y_{i})-|f(y)|\right|\leq\varepsilon_{n}C

for all
n
, and hence
|f(y_{i})|\to|f(y)|
. This shows that
X_{B}^{\mathrm{an}}\hookrightarrow X^{\mathrm{an}}
is a homeomorphism onto its image. Specializing to
X=\Spec A
, this implies that
\mathcal{M}(A)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}(B)
also is a homeomorphism onto its image.

Next pick
x\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
in the preimage of
\mathcal{M}(B)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}(A)
, i.e.
|a(x)|\leq\|\tau(a)\|_{B}
for all
a\in A
. Pick
f\in\mathcal{O}(X_{B})
, and let
f_{n}\in\mathcal{O}(X)
be an admissible approximation of
f
. Then

\big||f_{n}(x)|-|f_{m}(x)|\big|\leq|(f_{n}-f_{m})(x)|\leq(\varepsilon_{n}+\varepsilon_{m})C\max_{k}|g_{k}(x)|^{C}.

The sequence
(|f_{n}(x)|)_{n}
is thus Cauchy, and hence admits a limit in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. For any other choice of admissible approximation
(f^{\prime}_{n})
of
f
, we similarly see that
|f_{n}(x)|-|f^{\prime}_{n}(x)|
tends to
0
. It follows that
|f(y)|:=\lim_{n}|f_{n}(x)|
is independent of the choice of admissible approximation, and hence defines a point
y\in X_{B}^{\mathrm{an}}
mapping to
x
, since admissible approximations are stable under sums and products.

Finally, assume that
\tau
is an isometry. A similar Cauchy sequence argument then shows that
\mathcal{M}(B)\to\mathcal{M}(A)
is onto, and the rest follows.
∎

2.5.
Continuous metrics

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over a normed ring
A
, and pick a line bundle
L
on
X
.

Definition 2.17
.

We define a
continuous metric

\phi
on

as the data, for any Zariski open
U\subset X
and
s\in\Hnot(U,L)
, of a continuous function

|s|_{\phi}\colon U^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

compatible with restriction to open subschemes, and subject to the conditions

(i)

|fs|_{\phi}=|f||s|_{\phi}
for any
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
;

(ii)

|s|>0
on
U^{\mathrm{an}}
when
s
is a trivializing section on
U
.

Fix a trivializing open cover
(U_{i})
of
X
for

, with trivializing sections
s_{i}\in\Hnot(U_{i},L)
and associated cocyle
g_{ij}\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}(U_{ij})
. Setting
\phi_{i}:=-\log|s_{i}|_{\phi}
then defines a 1–1 correspondence between the set of continuous metrics
\phi
on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
and that of finite families of functions
\phi_{i}\in\Cz(U_{i}^{\mathrm{an}})
such that
\phi_{i}-\phi_{j}=\log|g_{ij}|
on
U_{ij}^{\mathrm{an}}
. Using a partition of unity, we easily infer that
L^{\mathrm{an}}
always admits a continuous metric.

We use additive notation for line bundles and metrics thereon, i.e. if
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
are continuous metrics on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
,
L^{\prime\mathrm{an}}
for some line bundles
L,L^{\prime}
on
X
, we denote by
-\phi
the induced metric on the dual line bundle
-L:=L^{\vee}
, and
\phi+\phi^{\prime}
the induced metric on
L+L^{\prime}:=L\otimes L^{\prime}
.

When
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}
is trivial, we further identify a continuous metric
\phi
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\mathrm{an}}
with the continuous function

\phi=-\log|1|_{\phi}\in\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}}).

Given two continuous metrics
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
on the same line bundle
L
,
\phi-\phi^{\prime}
is thus viewed as a continuous function on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
, in line with the fact that the space
\Cz(L^{\mathrm{an}})
of continuous metrics on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
is an affine space modeled on
CX^{0}(X^{\mathrm{an}})
.

Example 2.18
.

Assume that, for some
m\geq 1
,
mL
is generated by finitely many global sections
s_{i}\in\Hnot(X,mL)
, and pick constants
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. This defines a
tropical Fubini–Study metric

\phi=\tfrac{1}{m}\max_{i}\{\log|s_{i}|+\lambda_{i}\}

on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
, where
\log|s_{i}|
denotes the (singular) metric induced by
s_{i}
. Explicitly,

|s|_{\phi}=\left(\max_{i}|s_{i}/s^{m}|e^{\lambda_{i}}\right)^{-1/m}

on
U^{\mathrm{an}}
for any trivializing section
s\in\Hnot(U,L)
with
U\subset X
open. If further
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
we say that
\phi
is
rational
.

Such metrics give rise to a convenient space of ‘test functions’. More specifically, assume
X
is projective, so that
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is compact (see Proposition
2.11
). Pick an ample line bundle
L
, and denote by

\DFS(X^{\mathrm{an}})\subset\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})

the set of functions of the form
f=m(\phi-\psi)
with
\phi,\psi
rational tropical Fubini–Study metrics on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Then:

Proposition 2.19
.

The set
\DFS(X^{\mathrm{an}})
is independent of the choice of ample line bundle
L
, and is a dense
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspace of
\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
, stable under max and containing
1
.

Proof.

As one easily sees,
\DFS(X^{\mathrm{an}})
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspace stable under max and containing
1
. It is also not hard to check that it is independent of
L
(see
[
BoJ18b
, Lemma 2.6]
), and that it separates the points of
X^{\mathrm{an}}

[
BoJ18b
, Theorem 2.7]
. Density is then a consequence of the Stone–Weierstrass theorem.
∎

3.
Hybrid spaces

This section provides a self-contained introduction to the main character of these notes, i.e. the hybrid space attached to a meromorphic degeneration of algebraic varieties.

3.1.
The hybrid space of a complex variety

To any complex algebraic variety
Z
can be attached two types of analytifications in the sense of Definition
2.8
, corresponding respectively to the usual absolute value
|\cdot|_{\infty}
and the trivial absolute value
|\cdot|_{0}
on
\mathbb{C}
.

As mentioned in Example
2.12
, the Gelfand–Mazur theorem implies that the first analytification recovers the usual one, i.e. the set of complex points of
Z
with the Euclidean topology. As in Example
2.13
, we view the analytification
Z^{\mathrm{an}}
with respect to
|\cdot|_{0}
as a space of semivaluations, which extends Definition
1.2
to the non-affine case.

Following Berkovich
[
Berk09
]
, we now bring together these two analytifications into a single hybrid object, as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

The
hybrid space

Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}
of a complex algebraic variety
Z
is defined as its analytification with respect to the hybrid norm
\|\cdot\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}=\max\{|\cdot|_{\infty},|\cdot|_{0}\}
on
\mathbb{C}
.

By Proposition
2.11
, the space
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is Hausdorff and locally compact; it is further compact if
Z
is projective.

Recall from Example
2.7
that the spectrum
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{hyb}})
of the hybrid field
\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{hyb}}=(\mathbb{C},\|\cdot\|^{\mathrm{hyb}})
can be canonically identified with the interval
[0,1]
by mapping
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
to
|\cdot|_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}
, interpreted as
|\cdot|_{0}
when
\varepsilon=0
. The hybrid space of
Z
thus comes with a structure map

Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{hyb}})=[0,1].

The fiber
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{\varepsilon}
over
\varepsilon>0
is the analytification with respect
|\cdot|_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}
, which is canonically identified with the usual analytification (by Gelfand–Mazur), while the fiber
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{0}
over
\varepsilon=0
is the Berkovich space
Z^{\mathrm{an}}
. This yields an identification

Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}\simeq(Z^{\mathrm{an}}\times\{0\})\coprod(Z\times(0,1])

compatible with the projection to
[0,1]
, the topology being the coarsest one such that for any affine open
U\subset Z
we have

•

U^{\mathrm{hyb}}\subset Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is open;

•

for any
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
, the function
|f|^{\mathrm{hyb}}\colon U^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
defined by
|f|^{\mathrm{hyb}}(x,\varepsilon):=|f(x)|^{\varepsilon}
for
(x,\varepsilon)\in Z\times(0,1]
and
|f|^{\mathrm{hyb}}(v,0):=e^{-v(f)}
for
v\in Z^{\mathrm{an}}
, is continuous.

A sequence (or net)
(x_{j},\varepsilon_{j})
of complex points
x_{j}\in X
and
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0_{+}
thus converges to a semivaluation
v\in Z^{\mathrm{an}}
iff, for any Zariski open
U\subset X
with
v\in U^{\mathrm{an}}
,
x_{j}
ultimately lies in
U^{\mathrm{an}}
, and

-\varepsilon_{j}\log|f(x_{j})|\to v(f)

for all
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
. Intuitively, this means that
v
is realized as a ‘vanishing order’ along the net
(x_{i})
, with rate
(\varepsilon_{j})
.

By
[
Jon16
, Theorem C]
, the structure map
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to[0,1]
is open, i.e. given any
v\in Z^{\mathrm{an}}
and any net
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
, we can find complex points
x_{j}\in Z
such that
(x_{j},\varepsilon_{j})\to(v,0)
in
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}
. In particular, the Archimedean part
Z\times(0,1]
is dense in
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}
, i.e. any semivaluation can be realized as a vanishing order along some net of complex points (see also Corollary
5.6
below).

Example 3.2
.

Let
T=\Spec\mathbb{C}[z_{1}^{\pm},\dots,z_{n}^{\pm}]
be a torus, with hybrid analytification
T^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to[0,1]
. The
hybrid log map

\Log\colon T^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

with components
-\log|z_{i}|
is then continuous. Given any subvariety
Z\subset T
, we have
\Log(Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon\Log(Z)
for
\varepsilon>0
, and
\Log(Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{0})=\Log(Z^{\mathrm{an}})
. As in
[
Jon16
]
, we thus recover the equality

\Log(Z^{\mathrm{an}})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\Log(Z)

between the non-Archimedean amoeba and the asymptotic cone of the (complex) amoeba
\Log(Z)
(see Theorem
1.5
) from the openness of the structure map
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to[0,1]
.

Combined with Proposition
2.14
, this recovers most of the ‘fundamental theorem of tropical geometry’ (Theorem
1.5
).

3.2.
The hybrid space of a meromorphic degeneration

We next consider the case of main interest in these notes, i.e. that of a meromorphic degeneration

\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}

of complex algebraic varieties
X_{t}=\pi^{-1}(t)
, parametrized by a small punctured disc centered at
0\in\mathbb{C}
. By definition, this means that
X
is the complex analytic space attached to an algebraic variety
X_{K}
over the non-Archimedean field
K=\mathbb{C}\{t\}
of convergent Laurent series.

Define

X_{0}:=X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}

as the Berkovich analytification of
X_{K}
, viewed as a space of semivaluations
v
on
X
(see Example
2.13
). Recall also the scaling factor

\varepsilon_{t}:=(\log|t|^{-1})^{-1}\in(0,1),

which tends to
0
as
t\to 0
(see §
1.2
). Inspired by §
1.2
and §
3.1
, we introduce:

Definition 3.3
.

The
hybrid space
of the meromorphic degeneration
X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}

is defined as the disjoint union

X^{\mathrm{hyb}}:=\coprod_{t\in\mathbb{D}}X_{t}

endowed with the
hybrid topology
,
i.e.
the coarsest topology such that, for any affine open
U_{K}\subset X_{K}
, we have:

(i)

U^{\mathrm{hyb}}\subset X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is open;

(ii)

for any
f\in\mathcal{O}(U_{K})
, the function
|f|^{\mathrm{hyb}}\colon U^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
equal to
x\mapsto|f(x)|^{\varepsilon_{t}}
on
X_{t}
for
t\neq 0
and
v\mapsto|f(v)|=e^{-v(f)}
for
v\in X_{0}
is continuous.

Equivalently, the hybrid topology is characterized by:

•

X\hookrightarrow X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is an open embedding;

•

X_{0}\hookrightarrow X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is a closed embedding;

•

a net of complex points
x_{i}\in X_{t_{i}}
with
t_{i}\to 0
converges in
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
to a semivaluation
v\in X_{0}
iff, for any affine open
U_{K}\subset X_{K}
such that
v\in U_{0}
, we have
x_{i}\in U_{t_{i}}
for
i
large enough, and

(3.1)

\lim_{i}\frac{\log|f(x_{i})|}{\log|t_{i}|}=v(f)

for all
f\in\mathcal{O}(U_{K})
.

As in §
3.1
, we interpret (
3.1
) as saying that
v
is realized as a ‘vanishing order’ along the net
(x_{i})
. As we shall later see,
X
is dense in
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
(cf. Corollary
5.6
), i.e. any semivaluation can be realized as a vanishing order along some net of complex points.

By definition, the hybrid space comes with a natural continuous extension

\pi\colon X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathbb{D}

of
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
, such that
\pi^{-1}(0)=X_{0}
. The construction is functorial, in that any morphism
X_{K}\to Y_{K}
of algebraic
K
-varieties induces a continuous map
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to Y^{\mathrm{hyb}}
over a sufficiently small disc
\mathbb{D}
, compatible with composition.

In analogy with §
3.1
, our next goal is to provide a description of the hybrid space in terms of Berkovich analytification, closely related to
[
Berk09
, §3]
. As a consequence, we will obtain the following basic topological properties.

Proposition 3.4
.

For any algebraic variety
X_{K}
over
K
, the hybrid space
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is Hausdorff and locally compact. If
X_{K}
is further projective, then the map
\pi\colon X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathbb{D}
is proper.

In the projective case, which is the one we will mostly consider afterwards, we will later provide another, more global description of the hybrid topology in terms of hybrid model functions (see Proposition
5.5
).

For each
r\in(0,1)
we denote by
K_{r}
the ring of complex Laurent series

a(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}t^{k}\in\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]

that converge on the punctured closed disc
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}^{\times}
of radius
r
, i.e. such that
\sum_{k}|a_{k}|r^{k}<\infty
, where
|a_{k}|=|a_{k}|_{\infty}
is the usual complex absolute value. Since

\|a_{k}\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}=\max\{|a_{k}|,|a_{k}|_{0}\}\leq 1+|a_{k}|,

the
hybrid norm

\|a\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{r}:=\sum_{k}\|a_{k}\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}r^{k}

is then finite; we denote by

K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}:=(K_{r},\|\cdot\|_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})

the resulting normed ring. As one easily sees,
K_{r}
coincides with the ring of (a priori) doubly infinite series

\left\{a=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}t^{k}\in\mathbb{C}[\![t^{\pm}]\!]\mid\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|a_{k}\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}r^{k}<\infty\right\}.

In the language of Berkovich geometry, the spectrum
\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})
thus corresponds to the circle

C_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}:=\{|t|=r\}\subset\mathbb{A}^{1,\mathrm{hyb}}_{\mathbb{C}}=(\Spec\mathbb{C}[t])^{\mathrm{hyb}}

in the hybrid affine line. As observed in
[
BoJ17
, Proposition A.4]
, this hybrid circle is topologically a disc:

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
r\in(0,1)
, there is a canonical homeomorphism

\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}),

which takes
0\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
and
t\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}^{\times}
to the multiplicative seminorms

K_{r}\ni a\mapsto r^{v_{K}(a)}\quad\text{and}\quad K_{r}\ni a\mapsto|a(t)|^{\varepsilon_{r,t}},\quad\varepsilon_{r,t}:=\frac{\log r}{\log|t|}\in(0,1].

Proof.

Pick
a\in K_{r}
and set
k_{0}:=v_{K}(a)
. For any
t\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\setminus\{0\}
we have

\varepsilon_{r,t}\log|a(t)|\leq\varepsilon_{r,t}\log\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}|a_{k}||t|^{k}\leq\varepsilon_{r,t}\log\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\|a_{k}\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}|t|^{k}

=\varepsilon_{r,t}\log\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\|a_{k}\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}|t|^{k-k_{0}}+k_{0}\log r\leq\varepsilon_{r,t}\log\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\|a_{k}\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}r^{k-k_{0}}+k_{0}\log r

\leq\log\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\|a_{k}\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}r^{k-k_{0}}+k_{0}\log r=\|a\|_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}},

where the last inequality holds because
\varepsilon_{r,t}\leq 1
and

0\leq\log\|a_{k_{0}}\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}\leq\log\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\|a_{k}\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}r^{k-k_{0}},

since
a_{k_{0}}\neq 0
. This yields
|a(t)|^{\varepsilon_{r,t}}\leq\|a\|_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
, which proves that the multiplicative seminorm
K_{r}\ni a\mapsto|a(t)|^{\varepsilon_{r,t}}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}(K_{r})
. Furthermore,
|a(t)|^{\varepsilon_{r,t}}=r^{\frac{\log|a(t)|}{\log|t|}}
depends continuously on
t\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\setminus\{0\}
, and converges pointwise to
r^{v_{K}(a)}
as
t\to 0
. This shows that the map
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\to\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})
in the statement is well-defined and continuous. As noted above,
\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})
can be viewed as the Berkovich circle of radius
r
in the hybrid line
\mathbb{A}^{1,\mathrm{hyb}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The Gelfand–Mazur theorem thus yields an identification of the fiber over
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
of the structure map

\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})\to\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{hyb}})\simeq[0,1]

with the circle of radius
r
with respect to the absolute value
|\cdot|_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}
on
\mathbb{C}
, i.e. the usual circle in
\mathbb{C}
of radius
r^{1/\varepsilon}
, while the fiber over
0
consists of the non-Archimedean absolute value
r^{v_{K}}
. This implies that
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\to\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})
is bijective, and hence a homeomorphism, by compactness.
∎

Since
K=\bigcup_{r\in(0,1)}K_{r}
, the
K
-scheme
X_{K}
, which is separated and of finite type, induces for each
0<r\ll 1
a
K_{r}
-scheme
X_{r}
that is separated and of finite type as well, and projective when
X
is projective. We denote by

X_{K_{r}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})

its analytification with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{r}
. By Proposition
2.11
,
X_{K_{r}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is Hausdorff, locally compact, and compact if
X
is projective over
K
. Proposition
3.4
is thus a consequence of the following result:

Theorem 3.6
.

For any
0<r\ll 1
, the preimage of
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
under
\pi\colon X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathbb{D}
admits a canonical homeomorphism
\pi^{-1}(\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r})\overset{\sim}{\to}X_{K_{r}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
compatible with the projection to
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})
.

Proof.

Since
X_{K}
is the base change of
X_{r}
with respect to the inclusion
K_{r}\subset K
, which is dense for the
r^{v_{K}}
-topology, Proposition
2.16
shows that the fiber of the structure map

X_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})

over
r^{v_{K}}\in\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})
coincides with the analytification of
X_{K}
with respect to the absolute value
r^{v_{K}}
on
K
, and hence can be canonically identified with the space of semivaluations
v\in X_{0}
, using the map
v\mapsto r^{v}
. Combined with Lemma
3.5
and the Gelfand–Mazur theorem, this yields a set theoretic identification
\pi^{-1}(\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r})\overset{\sim}{\to}X_{K_{r}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
, compatible with the projections to
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathcal{M}(K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}})
.

By definition of the topology of
X_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
,
\pi^{-1}(\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}^{\times})\hookrightarrow X_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is an open embedding,
X_{0}\hookrightarrow X_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is a closed embedding, and a net of complex points
x_{i}\in X_{t_{i}}
with
t_{i}\to 0
converges in
X_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
to a semivaluation
v\in X_{0}
iff, for any affine open
U_{K_{r}}\subset X_{r}
and
f\in\mathcal{O}(U_{K_{r}})
, we have
x_{i}\in U_{t_{i}}
for
i
large enough and

|f(x_{i})|^{\varepsilon_{r,t_{i}}}\to r^{v(f)}.

Since
\varepsilon_{r,t}=\frac{\log r}{\log|t|}
, the last condition is equivalent to (
3.1
), except that we now require
f
to be defined over
K_{r}\subset K
. Comparing with Definition
3.3
, we infer that the topology of
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is the coarsest one such that
\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{D}_{r})\to\ X_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is continuous for all
0<r\ll 1
. For
0<s\leq r\ll 1
, the injection
K_{r}\to K_{s}
yields a normed ring map
K_{r}^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to K_{s}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
with dense image. Using Proposition
2.16
again, we conclude that
X_{K_{s}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to X_{K_{r}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is a homeomorphism onto the preimage of
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{s}
, and the result follows.
∎

3.3.
Hybrid continuous metrics

Consider as above a meromorphic degeneration
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
associated to an algebraic variety
X_{K}
over the field
K=\mathbb{C}\{t\}
of convergent Laurent series, and assume given a
meromorphic line bundle

L
on
X
, i.e. a holomorphic line bundle induced by a line bundle
L_{K}
on
X_{K}
. For each
t\in\mathbb{D}^{\times}
, we denote by
L_{t}
the induced holomorphic line bundle on
X_{t}=\pi^{-1}(t)
, and by
L_{0}=L_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
the induced line bundle on
X_{0}=X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
.

For
0<r\ll 1
we get an induced line bundle
L_{K_{r}}
on the
K_{r}
-scheme
X_{K_{r}}
, and we can thus consider continuous metrics on the analytification
L_{K_{r}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
over
X_{K_{r}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
in the general sense of §
2.5
. Thanks to Theorem
3.6
, this admits the following more concrete description.

Definition 3.7
.

We say that a family
\phi=(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{D}}
of continuous metrics
\phi_{t}
on each
L_{t}
is
hybrid continuous
if, for any Zariski open
U_{K}\subset X_{K}
and
s\in\Hnot(U_{K},L_{K})
, the function

|s|_{\phi^{\mathrm{hyb}}}\colon U^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

equal to
|s|_{\phi_{0}}
on
U_{0}
and
|s|_{\phi_{t}}^{\varepsilon_{t}}
on
U_{t}
for
t\neq 0
is continuous in the hybrid topology.

It is enough to check the condition when
s
is a trivializing section. Note that a hybrid continuous metric
\phi
restricts to a continuous metric on the holomorphic line bundle
L
.

Example 3.8
.

A family
\phi=(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{D}}
of continuous functions
\phi_{t}\in\Cz(X_{t})
, viewed as metrics on the trivial line bundle, is hybrid continuous iff the rescaled function
\phi^{\mathrm{hyb}}\colon X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\phi^{\mathrm{hyb}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi_{0}&\text{ on }X_{0}\\
\varepsilon_{t}\phi_{t}&\text{ on }X_{t},\,t\neq 0\end{array}\right.

is continuous in the hybrid topology.

As a consequence we get the following useful criterion:

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\phi=(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{D}}
,
\widetilde{\phi}=(\widetilde{\phi}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{D}}
be two families of continuous metrics on the
L_{t}
’s, and assume that

•

\widetilde{\phi}
is hybrid continuous;

•

\phi_{0}=\widetilde{\phi}_{0}
.

The following are then equivalent:

(i)

\phi
is hybrid continuous;

(ii)

\phi|_{L}
is continuous and
\varepsilon_{t}\sup_{X_{t}}|\phi_{t}-\widetilde{\phi}_{t}|\leq 0
as
t\to 0
.

The last condition holds as soon as
\phi_{t}-\widetilde{\phi}_{t}
is uniformly bounded.

Example 3.10
.

Assume
s_{1},\dots,s_{N}\in\Hnot(X_{K},mL_{K})
have no common zeroes for some
m\geq 1
, and pick
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the family of metrics

\phi_{0}=\frac{1}{m}\max\{\log|s_{i}|+\lambda_{i}\},\quad\phi_{t}=\frac{1}{2m}\log\sum_{i}|s_{i}|^{2}|t|^{-2\lambda_{i}}\text{ for }t\neq 0

is hybrid continuous. Indeed, setting

\widetilde{\phi}_{t}:=\frac{1}{m}\max_{i}\{\log|s_{i}|+\varepsilon_{t}^{-1}\lambda_{i}\}

for all
t\in\mathbb{D}
defines a hybrid continuous family, corresponding to a tropical Fubini–Study metric on
L_{K_{r}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
, see Example
2.18
. Now
|\phi_{t}-\widetilde{\phi}_{t}|\leq\frac{\log N}{m}
is bounded independently of
t
, and
(\phi_{t})
is thus hybrid continuous as well by Lemma
3.9
.

4.
Models, non-Archimedean metrics and dual complexes

From now on, we consider a projective meromorphic degeneration
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
, associated to a projective variety
X_{K}
over the field
K=\mathbb{C}\{t\}
of convergent Laurent series. In this section we make a deeper study of the associated Berkovich space, denoted as above by
X_{0}=X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
. We introduce in this section several important classes of metrics and functions on
X_{0}
, and relate this space to the dual complexes of snc models, when
X_{K}
is smooth.

4.1.
Non-Archimedean Fubini–Study metrics

Let
L
be a meromorphic line bundle on
X
, corresponding to a line bundle
L_{K}
on
X_{K}
, and consider as in Example
2.18
a rational tropical Fubini–Study metric on
L_{0}
, i.e.

(4.1)

\phi=\tfrac{1}{m}\max_{i}\{\log|s_{i}|+\lambda_{i}\}

for a finite set of sections
s_{i}\in\Hnot(X_{K},mL_{K})
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Since any
v\in X_{0}=X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
restricts to the valuation of
K=\mathbb{C}\{t\}
, and hence satisfies
v(t)=1
, we have
\log|t|\equiv-1
on
X_{0}
, and it follows that
\phi
can be rewritten as

\phi=\tfrac{1}{m^{\prime}}\max_{i}\log\big|s^{\prime}_{i}\big|

where
m^{\prime}=km
with
k
sufficiently divisible and
s^{\prime}_{i}:=s_{i}^{k}t^{-k\lambda_{i}}\in\Hnot(X_{K},kmL_{K})
. In other words, we can assume
\lambda_{i}=0
in (
4.1
). We shall simply say that
\phi
is a
(non-Archimedean) Fubini–Study metric
on
L_{0}
, and denote by

\FS(L_{0})\subset\Cz(L_{0})

the set of such metrics. By definition, this set is nonempty iff
L
is
semiample
, i.e.
mL
is basepoint free for
m
sufficiently divisible. Taking
L
is ample, Proposition
2.19
shows that the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space

\DFS(X_{0})\subset\Cz(X_{0})

of functions of the form
f=m(\phi-\psi)
with
\phi,\psi\in\FS(L_{0})
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is independent of
L
, and is dense in
\Cz(X_{0})
.

4.2.
Models of
X

The meromorphic degeneration
X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
admits an extension to a surjective and projective holomorphic map
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}
, called a
model
of
X
, whose fiber
\mathcal{X}_{0}
over
0\in\mathbb{D}
is called the
central fiber
.

Indeed, pick an embedding
X_{K}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}_{K}
. This corresponds to an embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{D}^{\times}
over a possibly shrunk disc, and the closure
\mathcal{X}
of
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{D}
is then a model of
X
.

A model can equivalently be seen as a flat, projective scheme over the valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C},0}
, together with an identification of its generic fiber with
X_{K}
.

Models are far from unique: any blow up of a model along a subvariety of the central fiber is also a model. We say that a model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}

dominates
another model
\mathcal{X}
if the canonical meromorphic map
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{X}
extends to a morphism
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}
. This defines a partial order on the set of models (modulo canonical isomorphism), which is further inductive, i.e. any two models can be dominated by a third one.

When
X
is normal, the normalization of any model is also a model. In the general case, the integral closure of any model in its generic fiber is also a model, which is said to be
integrally closed
(see for instance
[
BoJ22a
, §1.4]
). Integrally closed models give rise to points of the Berkovich space
X_{0}
, as follows:

Example 4.1
.

Assume
\mathcal{X}
(and hence
X
) is normal, and pick an irreducible component
E
of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, with multiplicity
b_{E}:=\ord_{E}(\mathcal{X}_{0})
. Then

(4.2)

v_{E}:=b_{E}^{-1}\ord_{E}

defines a valuation on the function field of
X_{K}
that restricts to
v_{K}
on
K
, and hence an element
v_{E}\in X_{0}
. More generally, this remains true when
X
is possibly non-normal and
\mathcal{X}
is integrally closed (see for instance
[
BoJ22a
, Theorem 1.13]
).

Points of
X_{0}
of the form
v_{E}
as above are called
divisorial valuations
. They form a dense subset of
X_{0}
.

4.3.
Models of a line bundle

Any meromorphic line bundle
L
on
X
can similarly be extended to a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on some model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
. It is convenient to allow
\mathcal{L}
to be a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle, which means that
m\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle extending
mL
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. This leads to:

Definition 4.2
.

A
model

\mathcal{L}
of a line bundle
L
on
X
is defined as a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
\mathcal{L}
extending
L
to a model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
.

We then say that
\mathcal{L}
is
determined
on
\mathcal{X}
. The pull-back of
\mathcal{L}
to any higher model of
X
is also model of
L
. If
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are models of
L,L^{\prime}
determined on the same model
\mathcal{X}
, then
\mathcal{L}\pm\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is a model of
L\pm L^{\prime}
, where we use additive notation for tensor products of line bundles.

Example 4.3
.

Every model of the trivial line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, determined on a given model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
, is of the form
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(D)
where
D
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
\mathcal{X}
which is
vertical
,
i.e.
supported in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.

We denote by
\VCar(\mathcal{X})
the (finite dimensional)
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors on
\mathcal{X}
. When
\mathcal{X}
is nonsingular, it is simply generated by the irreducible components of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.

Any two models
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of the same line bundle
L
can be pulled back to some common model
\mathcal{X}
, and then differ by some
D\in\VCar(\mathcal{X})
, by linearity.

4.4.
Centers

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a model of
X
. Viewed as an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-scheme,
\mathcal{X}
is projective (and hence proper), and the valuative criterion of properness thus implies that any semivaluation
v\in X_{0}
admits a
center

c_{\mathcal{X}}(v)\in\mathcal{X}_{0}

characterized as the unique (scheme) point
\xi\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
such that
v\geq 0
on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},\xi}
and
v>0
on the maximal ideal. Equivalently, an affine Zariski open
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{X}
contains
c_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
iff
v(f)\geq 0
for all
f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
, and we then have
v(f)>0
iff
f
vanishes at
c_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
. Centers are compatible with domination of models: if a model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
dominates
\mathcal{X}
, then
c_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
is the image of
c_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(v)
.

The corresponding complex subvariety

Z_{\mathcal{X}}(v)=\overline{\{c_{\mathcal{X}}(v)\}}\subset\mathcal{X}_{0}

can be roughly viewed as the ‘locus that matters’ for
v
on
\mathcal{X}
. As an illustration, we have:

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume
v\in X_{0}
is the limit of in
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
of a net of complex points
x_{j}\in X_{t_{j}}
. Then every limit point
x\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
of
(x_{j})
in
\mathcal{X}
(for the complex topology) lies in
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
.

Proof.

After passing to a subnet, assume
x_{j}\to x\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
, and pick an affine Zariski open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
x
in
\mathcal{X}
(viewed as an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-scheme). For every
f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
,
f(x_{j})
is (ultimately) bounded, and hence

v(f)=\lim_{j}\frac{\log|f(x_{j})|}{\log|t_{j}|}\geq 0.

This proves
c_{\mathcal{X}}(v)\in\mathcal{U}
, so that
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(v)\cap\mathcal{U}
is the intersection of the zero loci of all
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
such that
v(f)>0
, as noted above. Now each such
f
ultimately satisfies
|f(x_{j})|\leq|t_{j}|^{\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,v(f))
, and hence
f(x)=\lim_{j}f(x_{j})=0
. This shows
x\in Z_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
and concludes the proof.
∎

Example 4.5
.

Pick a divisorial valuation
v\in X_{0}
. By definition,
v=v_{E}
for an irreducible component
E
of the central fiber of an integrally closed model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, which can be assumed to dominate the given model
\mathcal{X}
(see (
4.2
)). Then
Z_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(v)=E
, and
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
is thus the image of
E
in
\mathcal{X}
.

4.5.
Model metrics

As a key construction in non-Archimedean geometry, every model
\mathcal{L}
of a line bundle
L
gives rise to a continuous metric
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
on
L_{0}
, called a
model metric
. The metric
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
is unchanged upon pulling back
\mathcal{L}
to a higher model, and depends linearly on
\mathcal{L}
(in additive notation for
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles and metrics). From a complex geometric perspective, the model metric
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
can be understood as the ‘hybrid limit’ of any family of metrics on
L_{t}
that extends continuously to
\mathcal{L}
(see Proposition
5.4
below).

To describe the construction of model metrics, note first that mapping a semivaluation
v\in X_{0}
to its center (viewed as a scheme point of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
) defines a map

c_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X_{0}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}

with the somewhat unusual property of being
anticontinuous
, i.e. the preimage of a (Zariski) open subset is closed (and hence compact, since
X_{0}
is compact by projectivity of
X
).

By definition of the center, every function
f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{U})
on a Zariski open subset
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{X}
(viewed as an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-scheme) satisfies

|f(v)|=e^{-v(f)}\leq 1

for each
v
in the compact set
c_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})
, the inequality being strict iff
f
vanishes at
c_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
, i.e. along the subvariety
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
. In particular,
|f|\equiv 1
on
c_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})
if
f
is a unit on
\mathcal{U}
.

The model metric
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
associated to a model
\mathcal{L}
of
L
is defined by imposing a similar condition for sections. Assume first that
\mathcal{L}
is an honest line bundle on
\mathcal{X}
, and pick a trivializing open cover
(\mathcal{U}_{i})
of
\mathcal{X}
for
\mathcal{L}
, with trivializing sections
s_{i}\in\Hnot(\mathcal{U}_{i},\mathcal{L})
. The compact subsets
c_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{i})
then cover
X_{0}
, and the model metric
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
is defined by requiring

|s_{i}|_{\phi_{\mathcal{L}}}\equiv 1\text{ on }c_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{i})

for all
i
. The generic fibers
U_{i,K}
of the
\mathcal{U}_{i}
’s yield an open cover
(U_{i,0})_{i}
of
X_{0}
, and we get

|s_{i}|_{\phi_{\mathcal{L}}}=|s_{i}/s_{j}|\text{ on }U_{i,0}\cap c_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{j})

for all
i,j
, which uniquely determines the continuous function
|s_{i}|_{\mathcal{L}}
on
U_{i,0}
, and hence the metric
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
.

When
\mathcal{L}
is merely a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
\mathcal{L}
,
m\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, hence defines model metric
\phi_{m\mathcal{L}}
on
mL_{0}
, and we set

\phi_{\mathcal{L}}:=m^{-1}\phi_{m\mathcal{L}}.

Fubini–Study metrics form a special class of model metrics:

Proposition 4.6
.

Assume
L
is semiample. Then
\FS(L_{0})
coincides with the set of model metrics
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
with
\mathcal{L}
a semiample model of
L
.

Proof.

Assume first
\phi
is a Fubini–Study metric, and write
\phi=\tfrac{1}{m}\max_{i}\log|s_{i}|
for a finite set of sections
s_{i}\in\Hnot(X_{K},mL_{K})
without common zeroes. Replacing
L
with
mL
we may further assume
m=1
. The morphism
X\to\mathbb{P}^{N}_{K}
with homogeneous coordinates
(s_{i})
extends to a morphism
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
for some model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
. Denoting by
\mathcal{L}
the pullback
\mathcal{O}(1)
, each
s_{i}
extends to a section
s_{i}\in\Hnot(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
. Then
\mathcal{U}_{i}:=\{s_{i}\neq 0\}
defines a trivializing open cover of
\mathcal{X}
for
\mathcal{L}
. For all
i,j
,
s_{j}/s_{i}
is a regular function on
\mathcal{U}_{i}
, and hence
|s_{j}/s_{i}|\leq 1
on
c_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{i})
, with equality when
i=j
. Thus

|s_{i}|_{\phi}=\left(\max_{j}|s_{j}/s_{i}|\right)^{-1}\equiv 1

on
c_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{i})
, and it follows that
\phi=\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
. Conversely, if
\phi=\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
where
m\mathcal{L}
is generated by finitely many sections
(s_{i})
, then the same argument yields
m\phi_{\mathcal{L}}=\phi_{m\mathcal{L}}=\max_{i}\log|s_{i}|
.
∎

4.6.
Model functions

Pick a model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
and
v\in X_{0}
. By definition of the center
\xi=c_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
,
v
vanishes on each unit of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},\xi}
. This allows to evaluate
v
on any vertical Cartier divisor
D
on
\mathcal{X}
by setting

v(D):=v(f_{D})\in\mathbb{R}

for any choice of local equation
f_{D}
of
D
at
\xi
, which yields a linear map
D\mapsto v(D)
that can be extended to all vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors
D\in\VCar(\mathcal{X})
.

If
D
is further effective, then
v(D)\geq 0
, and
v(D)>0
iff
c_{\mathcal{X}}(v)
lies in (the support of)
D
.

Example 4.7
.

By definition, any
v\in X_{0}
coincides with
v_{K}
on
K
, and the vertical Cartier divisor
\mathcal{X}_{0}
thus satisfies
v(\mathcal{X}_{0})=1
.

On the other hand, each
D\in\VCar(\mathcal{X})
determines a model
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(D)
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and hence a model metric on the trivial line bundle, i.e. a
model function

\phi_{D}\in\Cz(X_{0}).

Tracing the definitions, one checks that the two constructions are compatible, i.e.
\phi_{D}(v)=v(D)
for all
v\in X_{0}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

The
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of model functions coincides with the dense subspace
\DFS(X_{0})\subset\Cz(X_{0})
(see §
4.1
).

Proof.

Pick an ample line bundle
L
. By Proposition
4.6
, every metric in
\FS(L_{0})
is a model metric, and every function in
\DFS(X_{0})
, being a difference of model metrics on
L_{0}
, is thus a model function. Conversely, pick a model function
\phi_{D}
with
D\in\VCar(\mathcal{X})
. After possibly replacing
\mathcal{X}
by a higher model of
X
,
L
admits an ample model
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{X}
. For
m\gg 1
,
\mathcal{L}+m^{-1}D
is ample as well. By Proposition
4.6
,

\phi:=\phi_{\mathcal{L}+m^{-1}D},\quad\psi:=\phi_{\mathcal{L}}

both lie in
\FS(L_{0})
, and
\phi_{D}=m(\phi-\psi)
thus lies in
\DFS(X_{0})
.
∎

4.7.
Snc models and dual complexes

We assume here that the projective variety
X_{K}
is
smooth
, i.e. the holomorphic map
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
is a submersion, after perhaps shrinking
\mathbb{D}
. As we now explain, one can then use the combinatorics of the central fibers of a special class of models to get a description of the Berkovich space
X_{0}
as a limit of simplicial complexes. While this goes back to the fundamental work of Berkovich, we use instead
[
BFJ16
, §3]
as a reference for what follows.

By resolution of singularities, any model of
X
is dominated by a regular model
\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathcal{X}_{0}
has simple normal crossing support. Denoting by

\mathcal{X}_{0}=\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}E_{i}

its irreducible decomposition, this means that the
E_{i}
’s are smooth hypersurfaces that intersect transversally. After further blowing up intersections of the
E_{i}
’s, we may further achieve that each intersection

E_{J}:=\bigcap_{i\in J}E_{i}

with
J\subset I
is
connected
. We then say that
\mathcal{X}
is an
snc model
. The set of snc models is cofinal in the poset of all models; in particular, any model metric on a line bundle
L
can be written as
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
for a model
\mathcal{L}
of
L
determined on an snc model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
.

A nonempty intersection
E_{J}
with
J\subset I
is called a
stratum
of
\mathcal{X}
, with
relative interior

\mathring{E}_{J}:=E_{J}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I\setminus J}E_{i}.

Each
x\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
belongs to
\mathring{E}_{J}
for a unique stratum
E_{J}
, with
J\subset I
the set of components
E_{i}
passing through
x
.

The combinatorics of the intersections of the components
E_{i}
is encoded in the
dual (intersection) complex
, i.e. the simplicial complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
having a vertex
e_{i}
for each
E_{i}
, an edge between
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
if
E_{i}
and
E_{j}
intersect, and so on. More precisely, we associate to each stratum
E_{J}
the
(|J|-1)
-dimensional simplex

(4.3)

\Delta_{J}:=\{w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J}\mid\sum_{i\in J}b_{i}w_{i}=1\},

and
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is then defined by identifying
\Delta_{J}
with a face of
\Delta_{J^{\prime}}
whenever
E_{J^{\prime}}
is a substratum of
E_{J}
.

Equivalently,
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
can be globally realized as the intersection of the affine hyperplane

(4.4)

H_{\mathcal{X}}:=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{I}\mid\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}w_{i}=1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{I}

with the family of cones
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J}\times\{0\}
attached to the strata
E_{J}
.

Definition 4.9
.

The
non-Archimedean log map

\Log_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X_{0}\to H_{\mathcal{X}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{I}

associated to an snc model
\mathcal{X}
is defined as the map with components the model functions
\phi_{E_{i}}
,
i\in I
.

In other words, we have

\Log_{\mathcal{X}}(v)=(v(E_{i}))_{i\in I}.

for
v\in X_{0}
(see §
4.6
). Since
v(E_{i})\geq 0
, and
v(E_{i})>0
iff the center of
v
is contained in
E_{i}
, the above description of the dual complex yields an inclusion

\Log_{\mathcal{X}}(X_{0})\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}.

In analogy with Lemma
1.4
, we have:

Proposition 4.10
.

The map
\Log_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X_{0}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
admits a unique continuous section

\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\hookrightarrow X_{0}

with the property that, for each
w\in\mathring{\Delta}_{J}
, the center of
\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
coincides with the generic point of
E_{J}
.

In particular, this shows that
\Log_{\mathcal{X}}(X_{0})=\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, which can thus be viewed as a ‘global tropicalization’ of
X_{K}
with respect to the toroidal embedding
X_{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
(compare §
1.2
).

Proof.

Pick a stratum
E_{J}
, denote by
\xi_{J}
its generic point, and pick a local equations
z_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},\xi_{J}}
of each
E_{i}
,
i\in J
. By the snc condition,
(z_{i})_{i\in J}
induces a system of coordinates (i.e. a regular system of parameters) on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},\xi_{J}}
. After choosing a field of representatives, and each
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},\xi_{J}}
can thus be expanded as a formal power series

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{J}}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}

where each
a_{\alpha}
lies in the residue field
6
6

6

In fact, in a chosen field of representatives of the residue field.
of
\mathcal{X}
at
\xi_{J}
, i.e. the function field of
E_{J}
. Setting

\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)(f):=\min_{a_{\alpha}\neq 0}\alpha\cdot w

defines, for every
w\in\mathring{\Delta}_{J}
, a monomial valuation
\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
on the function field of
X_{K}
. Since
b\cdot w=1
, the restriction of
\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
to
K
coincides with
v_{K}
, and hence
\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)\in X_{0}
. For each stratum
E_{J}
, we thus get a continuous sections
\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\mathring{\Delta}_{J}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
of
\Log_{\mathcal{X}}
, and one shows that they glue to a continuous section on
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
. As in Lemma
1.4
, uniqueness follows from the fact that any valuation centered at the generic point of
E_{J}
and whose values
(w_{i})_{i\in J}
on the
E_{i}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent, is necessarily monomial, and hence equal to
\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
(see
[
BFJ16
, Theorem 3.1]
).
∎

Example 4.11
.

The embedding
\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
takes each vertex
e_{i}
to the divisorial valuation
v_{E_{i}}
(see (
4.2
)).

The compact space

\Sk(\mathcal{X}):=\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})\subset X_{0}

is called the
skeleton
of the snc model
\mathcal{X}
. It comes with a continuous
retraction

p_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X_{0}\to\Sk(\mathcal{X})\subset X_{0},

obtained by composing

Remark 4.12

More generally, the dual complex

5.
Hybrid model functions and hybrid log maps

We introduce in this section ‘hybrid versions’ of the classes of non-Archimedean metrics and functions considered in §
4
, and show that they determine the hybrid topology.

As in the previous section, we consider a projective meromorphic degeneration
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
over a small enough punctured disc, associated to a projective variety
X_{K}
over the field
K=\mathbb{C}\{t\}
of convergent Laurent series. Recall that
\pi\colon X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to\mathbb{D}
is proper (see Proposition
3.4
).

5.1.
Hybrid model metrics

Consider a meromorphic line bundle
L
on
X
, associated to a line bundle
L_{K}
on
X_{K}
. Recall from §
4.5
that any model
\mathcal{L}
of
L
induces a (continuous, non-Archimedean) model metric
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
on
L_{0}
.

Definition 5.1
.

We define a
hybrid model metric
on
L
as a family
\phi=(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{D}}
of continuous metrics on each
L_{t}
such that

•

\phi_{0}
is a model metric;

•

\phi|_{L}
is the restriction of a smooth metric
\Phi
on some model
\mathcal{L}
of
L
such that
\phi_{0}=\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any hybrid model metric
\phi
, the model metric
\phi_{0}
is uniquely determined by
\phi|_{L}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\phi|_{L}
is the restriction of a smooth metric
\Phi^{\prime}
on another model
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of
L
. We need to show
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}=\phi_{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}
. After pulling back
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
to a high enough model of
X
, we may assume that
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are both determined on the same model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
, and hence
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}-\mathcal{L}=D
for some
D\in\VCar(\mathcal{X})
. The zero function, viewed as a metric on
\mathcal{O}_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(D)|_{X}
extends to the smooth metric
\Phi-\Phi^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(D)
. As a consequence, for any local equation
f_{D}
of
D
,
\log|f_{D}|
is locally bounded. If
\mathcal{X}
is normal, this implies that
D=0
. In the general case, we get that the pullback of
D
to the integral closure of
\mathcal{X}
in its generic fiber vanishes (compare
[
BoJ22a
, Lemma 1.23]
). This implies
\phi_{D}=0
, and hence
\phi_{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}=\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
.
∎

In analogy with Proposition
4.6
, we have:

Example 5.3
.

Assume
s_{0},\dots,s_{N}\in\Hnot(X_{K},mL_{K})
have no common zeroes for some
m\geq 1
, and consider the
hybrid Fubini–Study metric

\phi=(\phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{D}}
defined by

\phi_{0}=\tfrac{1}{m}\max_{i}\log|s_{i}|,\quad\phi_{t}=\tfrac{1}{2m}\log\sum_{i}|s_{i}|^{2},\,t\neq 0

on
L_{0}
and
L_{t}
, respectively. Then
\phi
is a hybrid model metric. Indeed, the map
X\to\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{D}^{\times}
with homogeneous coordinates
(s_{i})
extends to a morphism
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{D}
for some model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
, and
\phi
is induced by the pullback by this morphism of
\frac{1}{m}\mathcal{O}(1)
with its usual (Hermitian) Fubini–Study metric.

By Example
3.10
, hybrid Fubini–Study metrics are hybrid continuous (see Definition
3.7
). More generally:

Proposition 5.4
.

Every hybrid model metric
\phi
is hybrid continuous.

Proof.

Pick a model
\mathcal{L}
of
L
to which
\phi
extends smoothly. Since any two smooth metrics on
\mathcal{L}
differ by a smooth function on
\mathcal{X}
, which is thus bounded near
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, it suffices to produce
some
smooth metric on
\mathcal{L}
for which the result holds true (see Lemma
3.9
). By linearity, writing
\mathcal{L}
as a difference of ample line bundles, we may thus assume without loss that
\mathcal{L}
is ample. For
m
sufficiently divisible, we can then pick a finite set of sections
s_{i}\in\Hnot(\mathcal{X},m\mathcal{L})
without common zeroes, and the corresponding hybrid Fubini–Study metric does the job.
∎

5.2.
Hybrid model functions

Recall that the data of a vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D\in\VCar(\mathcal{X})
on a model
\mathcal{X}
determines a model function
\phi_{D}\in\Cz(X_{0})
, corresponding to the model metric on the trivial line bunde
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}
induced by its model
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(D)
, and explicitly given by
\phi_{D}(v)=v(D)
for
v\in X_{0}
, see §
4.6
.

Now choose a smooth metric
\Psi
on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(D)
. This defines a hybrid model metric on the trivial line bundle, which is hybrid continuous by Proposition
5.4
, and hence determines a continuous function

\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}\in\Cz(X^{\mathrm{hyb}})

which we call a
hybrid model function
. By Example
3.8
, it is explicitly given by

(5.1)

\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\log|\sigma_{D}|_{\Psi}}{\log|t|}&\text{ on }X_{t}\\
\phi_{D}&\text{ on }X_{0}\end{array}\right.

with
\sigma_{D}
the canonical meromorphic section of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(D)
. Strictly speaking, this only makes sense when
D
is a Cartier divisor, but it is convenient to keep the same notation when
D
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, by setting

|\sigma_{D}|_{\Psi}:=|\sigma_{mD}|_{m\Psi}^{1/m}

with
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
sufficiently divisible.

Unlike
\phi_{D}
, the hybrid model function
\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
does depend on the choice of continuous metric
\Psi
on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(D)
, but only up to
O(\varepsilon_{t})
(see (
1.5
)). Furthermore,
D\mapsto\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is linear, again up to
O(\varepsilon_{t})
.

As we next show, hybrid model functions characterize the hybrid topology:

Proposition 5.5
.

The hybrid topology of
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}=X\coprod X_{0}
is the coarsest topology such that:

(i)

X\hookrightarrow X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is an open embedding;

(ii)

every hybrid model function
\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is continuous on
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
.

The choice of metric on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(D)
is immaterial in (ii), since it only affects
\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
by
O(\varepsilon_{t})
.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{T}
the coarsest topology for which (i) and (ii) hold. By continuity of hybrid model functions,
\mathcal{T}
is coarser than the hybrid topology. For any closed disc
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\subset\mathbb{D}
, the hybrid topology of
\pi^{-1}(\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r})\subset X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is compact Hausdorff, by Proposition
3.4
. As is well-known, any compact Hausdorff topology on a given set is minimal among all Hausdorff topologies, and it therefore suffices to show that the topology induced by
\mathcal{T}
on
\pi^{-1}(\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r})
is Hausdorff. By (i),
\mathcal{T}
separates the points of
X
. It therefore suffices to show that points of
X_{0}
are separated by
\mathcal{T}
, which follows from the fact that the (non-Archimedean) model functions
\phi_{D}
separate the points of
X_{0}
(see Proposition
4.8
).
∎

As an interesting consequence, we get:

Corollary 5.6
.

For any (non-necessarily projective) meromorphic degeneration
Z\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
of algebraic varieties, the complex part
Z
is dense in
Z^{\mathrm{hyb}}
.

For algebraic families over an algebraic curve, this also follows from
[
Jon16
, Theorem C’]
.

Proof.

The general case reduces to the affine case, which in turn reduces to projective case by passing to the closure in an ambient projective space. The result now follows from Proposition
5.5
, together with the fact that hybrid model functions are uniquely determined by their restriction to
X
, as a consequence of Lemma
5.2
.
∎

This yields in turn the following generalization of Lemma
5.2
.

Corollary 5.7
.

For any hybrid continuous metric
\phi
, the non-Archimedean metric
\phi_{0}\in\Cz(L_{0})
is uniquely determined by the metrics
\phi_{t}\in\Cz(L_{t})
with
t\neq 0
.

Remark 5.8
.

Arguing as in Lemma
5.2
, one can show that the restriction to
X
of a hybrid model function
\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
only extends continuously to some model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
in the trivial case
\phi_{D}=0
. Conversely, the restriction to
X
of a continuous function on a model
\mathcal{X}
typically does not continuously extend to
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
: if for instance
X=Z\times\mathbb{D}^{\times}
is a constant family with fiber
X_{t}=Z
and
f\in\Cz(X)
factors through
Z
, then
f
extends to the trivial model
\mathcal{X}=Z\times\mathbb{D}

f
extends to

iff

is constant (this can be checked using for instance Proposition

below).

5.3.
Hybrid and local log maps

From now on, we assume that
X_{K}
is smooth, i.e.
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
is a submersion (after perhaps shrinking
\mathbb{D}
).

As in §
4.7
, consider an snc model
\mathcal{X}
with central fiber
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}E_{i}
. The canonical sections

\sigma_{i}\in\Hnot(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(E_{i}))

satisfy
t=\prod_{i\in I}\sigma_{i}^{b_{i}}
. Pick a smooth metric
\Psi_{i}
on each line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(E_{i})
over
\mathcal{X}
such that
|t|=\prod_{i}|\sigma_{i}|_{\Psi_{i}}^{b_{i}}
, and denote by
\phi_{E_{i}}^{\mathrm{hyb}}\in\Cz(X^{\mathrm{hyb}})
the associated hybrid model functions, see (
5.1
).

Definition 5.9
.

The
hybrid log map

\Log_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to H_{\mathcal{X}}=\{b\cdot w=1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{I}

is defined as the map with components
\phi^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{E_{i}}
,
i\in I
(see (
4.4
)).

The hybrid log map is thus continuous, its restriction to
X_{0}
coincides with the (canonical) non-Archimedean log map (Definition
4.9
), while its restriction to
X
has components

\frac{\log|\sigma_{i}|_{\Psi_{i}}}{\log|t|},

see (
5.1
). In analogy with §
1.2
, one can view
\Log_{\mathcal{X}}(X_{t})\subset H_{\mathcal{X}}
as a ‘global amoeba’ of
X_{K}
, converging to the ‘global tropicalization’
\Log_{\mathcal{X}}(X_{0})=\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
as
t\to 0
.

As with hybrid model functions, the hybrid log map depends on the choice of metrics
\Psi_{i}
, but only up to
O(\varepsilon_{t})
on
X_{t}
. For any higher snc model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, we futher have

(5.2)

\Log_{\mathcal{X}}=\pi_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}\circ\Log_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}+O(\varepsilon_{t}),

with
\pi_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}\colon H_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}\twoheadrightarrow H_{\mathcal{X}}
the induced affine map.

As a consequence of Proposition
5.5
, hybrid log maps also characterize the hybrid topology:

Proposition 5.10
.

The hybrid topology of
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}=X\coprod X_{0}
is the coarsest topology such that

(i)

X\hookrightarrow X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
is an open embedding;

(ii)

for each snc model
\mathcal{X}
, the hybrid log map
\Log_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X^{\mathrm{hyb}}\to H_{\mathcal{X}}
is continuous.

Again, the implicit choice of metrics is immaterial in (ii), since
\Log_{\mathcal{X}}
is unique up to
O(\varepsilon_{t})
.

Proof.

According to Proposition
5.5
, it suffices to see that (ii) is equivalent to the continuity of all hybrid model functions, assuming (i) holds. Each such function is of the form
\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
for a vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D
on a model
\mathcal{X}
and a smooth metric on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(D)
. Pulling back
D
and the metric to a higher model does not modify
\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
, and we may thus assume that
\mathcal{X}
is snc. Then
D=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}E_{i}
with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and hence
\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}=\sum_{i}a_{i}\phi^{\mathrm{hyb}}_{E_{i}}+O(\varepsilon_{t})
. The continuity of the hybrid log map
\Log_{\mathcal{X}}=(\phi_{E_{i}}^{\mathrm{hyb}})_{i\in I}
is thus equivalent to that of
\phi_{D}^{\mathrm{hyb}}
for all
D\in\VCar(\mathcal{X})
, and the result follows.
∎

Hybrid log maps admit a more concrete description in terms of local log maps. To this end, pick
x\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
. Denote by
J_{x}\subset I
the set of
E_{i}

x
, and by

in its relative interior, with dual face
\Delta_{x}:=\Delta_{J_{x}}
.

Definition 5.11
.

An
adapted chart
at

is defined as a holomorphic coordinate chart

\mathcal{U}\simeq\mathbb{D}^{n+1}=\mathbb{D}^{J_{x}}\times\mathbb{D}^{J_{x}^{c}}

of

centered at

such that

, with

.

The fibers
\mathcal{U}_{t}=\mathcal{U}\cap X_{t}
,

of §

for any stratum

containing

As above,
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}

]

6.1.
General convergence criteria

In what follows, we consider a family of measures

. As a consequence of Propositions

\sigma_{\mathcal{X}}:=\sum_{J}\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathring{E}_{J})\sigma_{J}
, where the sum ranges over all strata such that
\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathring{E}_{J})>0
.

Note that (ii) trivially holds when
E_{J}=E_{i}
is a component of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, since
\Delta_{J}
is then reduced to a point.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\coprod_{J}\mathring{E}_{J}
, we have

\sigma_{\mathcal{X}}(H_{\mathcal{X}})=\sum_{\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathring{E}_{J})>0}\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathring{E}_{J})=\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathcal{X}_{0})=\lim_{t\to 0}\mu_{t}(X_{t})=\lim_{t\to 0}\left((\Log_{\mathcal{X}})_{\star}\mu_{t}\right)(H_{\mathcal{X}}).

By a standard weak compactness argument, it therefore suffices to show that each test function
0\leq f\in C^{0}_{c}(H_{\mathcal{X}})
satisfies
\liminf_{t\to 0}\int_{H_{\mathcal{X}}}f\,(\Log_{\mathcal{X}})_{\star}\mu_{t}\geq\int_{H_{\mathcal{X}}}f\,\sigma_{\mathcal{X}}
, i.e.

(6.1)

\liminf_{t\to 0}\int_{H_{\mathcal{X}}}(f\circ\Log_{\mathcal{X}})\mu_{t}\geq\sum_{J}\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathring{E}_{J})\int_{\Delta_{J}}f\,\sigma_{J}.

Pick
J
such that
\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\mathring{E}_{J})>0
, and a compact subset
\Sigma_{J}\subset\mathring{E}_{J}
. We can then find finitely many adapted charts
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
for
\mathring{E}_{J}
and
0\leq\chi_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{U}_{\alpha})
such that
\chi:=\sum_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}
satisfies
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi\equiv 1
on
\Sigma_{J}
. Then

\int_{\Sigma_{J}}(f\circ\Log_{\mathcal{X}})\mu_{t}\geq\sum_{\alpha}\int_{\Sigma_{J}}(f\circ\Log_{\mathcal{X}})\chi_{\alpha}\mu_{t}.

Since
\Log_{\mathcal{X}}=\Log_{t}+O(\varepsilon_{t})
uniformly on the support of
\chi_{\alpha}
(see (
5.3
)), (ii) yields for each
\alpha

\int_{\Sigma_{J}}(f\circ\Log_{\mathcal{X}})\chi_{\alpha}\mu_{t}\to\left(\int_{\Sigma_{J}}\chi_{\alpha}\,\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\right)\left(\int_{\Delta_{J}}f\,\sigma_{J}\right).

Summing over
\alpha
, we get

\liminf_{t\to 0}\int_{H_{\mathcal{X}}}(f\circ\Log_{\mathcal{X}})\mu_{t}\geq\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\chi\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\right)\left(\int_{\Delta_{J}}f\,\sigma_{J}\right)\geq\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(\Sigma_{J})\int_{\Delta_{J}}f\,\sigma_{J}.

Taking the supremum over
\Sigma_{J}
and summing over
J
yields (
6.1
), and concludes the proof.
∎

As a consequence, we recover the following result, originally due to Pille-Schneider
[
PS22
, Theorem B]
, which will later be used to motivate the definition of the non-Archimedean Monge–Ampère operator (see Theorem
7.8
).

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\mu_{t}
be a family of measures on the fibers
X_{t}
, and assume given an snc model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
such that:

(i)

\mu_{t}
converges weakly in
\mathcal{X}
to a measure
\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}
supported in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
;

(ii)

\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}
puts no mass on any nowhere dense Zariski closed subset of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.

Then
\mu_{t}
converges weakly in
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
to the atomic measure
\mu_{0}:=\sum_{i}\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}(E_{i})\delta_{v_{E_{i}}}
where
E_{i}
ranges over the irreducible components of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, with associated divisorial valuations
v_{E_{i}}\in X_{0}
.

Proof.

Pick an snc model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
dominating
\mathcal{X}
. Using (ii), one easily checks that
\mu_{t}
, viewed as a measure on
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, converges weakly to the unique measure
\mu_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
on
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0}
that coincides with
\mu_{0}
on the strict transform
E^{\prime}_{i}
of each component
E_{i}
of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, and is zero elsewhere. In particular, a stratum
E^{\prime}_{J}
of
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0}
satisfies
\mu_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(\mathring{E}^{\prime}_{J})>0
iff
E^{\prime}_{J}=E_{i}^{\prime}
for some
E_{i}
. As noted above, (ii) of Lemma
6.3
trivially holds in that case, and we infer
(\Log_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}})_{\star}\mu_{t}\to\sum_{i}\mu_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(E_{i}^{\prime})\delta_{e_{i}}=(\Log_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}})_{\star}\mu_{0}
(see Example
4.11
). We conclude by Lemma
6.1
.
∎

6.3.
Families of volume forms with analytic singularities

Recall that, on any complex manifold
M
, there is a 1–1 correspondence, which we denote by

\psi\mapsto e^{2\psi},

between smooth Hermitian metrics
\psi
on the canonical bundle
K_{M}
and (smooth, positive) volume forms on
M
. It satisfies

(6.2)

e^{2\psi}=\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{|\Omega|_{\psi}^{2}}

for each local trivialization
\Omega
of
K_{M}
, where

|\Omega|^{2}:=i^{(\dim M)^{2}}\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}

is the associated volume form and
|\Omega|_{\psi}
is the pointwise length of
\Omega
in the metric
\psi
.

Following
[
BoJ17
]
, we next consider a smooth family
(\sigma_{t})
of volume forms on the fibers
X_{t}
of our degeneration
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}
, assumed to have
analytic singularities
at
t=0
, in the sense that the corresponding smooth Hermitian metric on the relative canonical bundle
K_{X/\mathbb{D}^{\times}}
extends to a smooth metric
\Psi
on some model
\mathcal{K}
of
K_{X/\mathbb{D}^{\times}}
, determined on a model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
that can and will be assumed to be snc. In other words,
\sigma_{t}=e^{2\psi_{t}}
for a hybrid model metric
(\psi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{D}}
on
K_{X/\mathbb{D}^{\times}}
with non-Archimedean limit the model metric
\psi_{0}=\phi_{\mathcal{K}}
, see §
5.1
.

Example 6.5
.

Assume that
X
is a
Calabi–Yau degeneration
, in the sense that
K_{X/\mathbb{D}^{\times}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
is trivial. A trivialization of the canonical bundle of
X_{K}
induces a holomorphic family of holomorphic volume forms
\Omega_{t}
on the fibers
X_{t}
, and the associated family of smooth volume forms
\sigma_{t}:=|\Omega_{t}|^{2}
has analytic singularities at
t=0
(with
\mathcal{K}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
equipped with the trivial metric).

Returning to the above general setup, we will prove:

Theorem 6.6
.

[
BoJ17
, Theorem A]
The total mass of
\sigma_{t}
satisfies

\sigma_{t}(X_{t})\sim c|t|^{2\kappa}\varepsilon_{t}^{-d}

with
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}
and
d\in\{0,\dots,n\}
. Furthermore, the rescaled measure

\mu_{t}:=|t|^{-2\kappa}\varepsilon_{t}^{d}\sigma_{t}

converges weakly both in
\mathcal{X}
and in
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
, the limit in
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
being a Lebesgue-type measure on the top-dimensional part of a
d
-dimensional subcomplex
\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{ess}}\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
.

The invariants
\kappa
,
\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{ess}}
, and hence also
d=\dim\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{ess}}
, which we call the
essential dimension
of the degeneration
(\sigma_{t})
, only depend on
\mathcal{K}
, and can be described as follows. Denote as usual by
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}E_{i}
the irreducible decomposition of the central fiber. Consider the log canonical divisors

K^{\log}_{\mathcal{X}}:=K_{\mathcal{X}}+\sum_{i}E_{i},\quad K^{\log}_{\mathbb{D}}:=K_{\mathbb{D}}+[0]

of the pairs
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0,\mathrm{red}})
,
(\mathbb{D},[0])
, and the
relative log canonical bundle

K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{D}}^{\log}=K^{\log}_{\mathcal{X}}-\pi^{\star}K_{\mathbb{D}}^{\log}=K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{D}}+\mathcal{X}_{0,\mathrm{red}}-\mathcal{X}_{0}.

Since
\mathcal{K}
and
K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{D}}^{\log}
are both models of
K_{X/\mathbb{D}^{\times}}
, their difference can be uniquely written as

(6.3)

K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{D}}^{\log}-\mathcal{K}=\sum_{i}a_{i}E_{i}

with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
. The above invariant
\kappa
is then given by

\kappa:=\min_{i}\kappa_{i}\quad\text{with}\quad\kappa_{i}:=a_{i}/b_{i},

while
\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{ess}}\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is the subcomplex formed by the faces
\Delta_{J}
of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
that are
essential
, in the sense that
\kappa_{i}=\kappa
for all
i\in J
.

Example 6.7
.

Given any snc model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
, the choice of a smooth metric on
\mathcal{K}:=K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{D}}^{\log}
gives rise to a family of volume forms
\sigma_{t}
with analytic singularities, whose total mass grows like
\varepsilon_{t}^{-\dim\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}}
(see
[
BHJ19
, Theorem 3.6]
and
[
BoJ25
, Lemma 7.6]
for applications to the asymptotics of the Mabuchi K-energy functional along geodesic rays).

The image of
\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{ess}}\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
under the canonical embedding
\mathrm{val}_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
is denoted by

\Sk^{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{K})\subset\Sk(\mathcal{X})\subset X_{0},

and called the
essential skeleton
of
\mathcal{K}
. It satisfies the following important invariance property:

Lemma 6.8
.

The essential skeleton
\Sk^{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{K})\subset X_{0}
only depends on the model metric
\phi_{\mathcal{K}}
; in particular, it is invariant under pulling back
\mathcal{K}
to a higher snc model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}
.

Thus
\Sk^{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{K})
is uniquely determined by the family of volume forms
\sigma_{t}=e^{2\psi_{t}}
(see Lemma
5.2
).

Proof.

The result follows from the existence of a canonically defined singular lsc metric
A_{X}
on the (non-Archimedean) analytification
K_{X}^{\mathrm{an}}
of the canonical bundle of
X_{K}
, known as the
Temkin metric

[
MN15
,
Tem16
]
, such that the corresponding lsc function
A_{X}-\phi_{\mathcal{K}}
on
X_{0}
achieves its infimum precisely on
\Sk^{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{K})
. More specifically, denote by
\phi_{\mathcal{X}}
the model metric of
K_{X}^{\mathrm{an}}
defined by the model
K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{D}}^{\log}
. For any higher snc model
\rho\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}
,

K_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}/\mathbb{D}}^{\log}-\rho^{\star}K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{D}}^{\log}=(K_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}+\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0,\mathrm{red}})-\rho^{\star}(K_{\mathcal{X}}+\mathcal{X}_{0,\mathrm{red}})

is uniquely represented by a
\rho
-exceptional divisor, whose coefficients compute the
log discrepancies
of the pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0,\mathrm{red}})
. Since this pair is log smooth, standard properties of log discrepancies imply that
\phi_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}\geq\phi_{\mathcal{X}}
, with equality precisely on
\Sk(\mathcal{X})
(see for instance
[
BoJ17
, Proposition 5.10]
). The Temkin metric can be described as the limit

A_{X}:=\sup_{\mathcal{X}}\phi_{\mathcal{X}}

of the non-decreasing net of model metrics
(\phi_{\mathcal{X}})_{\mathcal{X}}
, where
\mathcal{X}
ranges over the poset all snc models of
X
. It is now easy to see that the function
A_{X}-\phi_{\mathcal{K}}\colon X_{0}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is lsc, affine linear on each face of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\simeq\Sk(\mathcal{X})
, and satisfies

(6.4)

\Sk^{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{K})=\left\{v\in X_{0}\mid(A_{X}-\phi_{\mathcal{K}})(v)=\inf_{X_{0}}(A_{X}-\phi_{\mathcal{K}})=\kappa\right\}.

∎

More generally, (
6.4
) remains true for any dlt model
\mathcal{X}
(see Remark
4.12
). This is especially useful in the Calabi–Yau case. Indeed, assume as in Example
6.5
that
K_{X}
is trivial, and pick a trivialization
\Omega
of the canonical bundle of
X_{K}
. Then
\log|\Omega|=\phi_{\mathcal{K}}
is a model metric. Since
\Omega
is unique up to a scalar in
K=\mathbb{C}\{t\}
,
\phi_{\mathcal{K}}
is unique up to an additive constant; thus (
6.4
) shows that

\Sk^{\mathrm{ess}}(X):=\Sk^{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal{K})

is independent of the choice of
\Omega
, and called the
essential skeleton
of the Calabi–Yau degeneration
X
. The Minimal Model Program (MMP) further guarantees the existence of a
minimal dlt model

\mathcal{X}
, such that the pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{0,\mathrm{red}})
is dlt and its log canonical divisor
K_{\mathcal{X}}^{\log}
is trivial, and we then have

(6.5)

\Sk^{\mathrm{ess}}(X)=\Sk(\mathcal{X}),

compare
[
NX16
]
.

Example 6.9
.

Consider the family of Calabi–Yau Fermat hypersurfaces

X_{t}:=\{z_{0}\dots z_{n+1}+t(z_{0}^{n+2}+\dots+z_{n+1}^{n+2})\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}.

The given equation of
X
provides a minimal dlt model
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{D}
, and
\Sk^{\mathrm{ess}}(X)=\Sk(\mathcal{X})\simeq\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
can thus be identified with the dual intersection complex of the union of the coordinate hyperplanes,
i.e.
the boundary of an
(n+1)
-simplex.

6.4.
Residual measures and local convergence

We return here to the general setup of §
6.3
. After replacing
\mathcal{K}
with
\mathcal{K}+\kappa\mathcal{X}_{0}
and
\sigma_{t}
with
|t|^{-2\kappa}\sigma_{t}
, we may and do assume from now on that
\kappa=0
; equivalently,

\min_{i}a_{i}=0

with
a_{i}
defined by (
6.3
). A face
\Delta_{J}
of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is thus essential, i.e. a face of
\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{ess}}
, iff
a_{i}=0
for all
i\in J
; we then also say that the corresponding stratum
E_{J}
is essential.

Lemma 6.10
.

For each essential stratum
E_{J}
, there is a canonical identification

\mathcal{K}|_{E_{J}}=K_{E_{J}}+B_{J}\quad\text{where}\quad B_{J}:=\sum_{i\in I\setminus J}\left(1-a_{i}\right)E_{i}|_{E_{J}}.

Furthermore,
B_{J}
has coefficients
<1
iff
E_{J}
is minimal as an essential stratum,
i.e.
\Delta_{J}
is a maximal face of
\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathrm{ess}}
.

Proof.

The first point follows from (
6.3
) together with the adjunction formula
K_{E_{J}}=\left(K_{\mathcal{X}}+\sum_{i\in J}E_{i}\right)|_{E_{J}}
. If
E_{J}
is minimal, each
E_{i}
meeting
E_{J}
properly necessarily satisfies
a_{i}>0
, which yields the second point.
∎

Recall that
\Psi
denotes the given smooth metric on
\mathcal{K}
. For each essential stratum
E_{J}
, Lemma
6.10
shows that
\Psi_{J}:=\Psi|_{E_{J}}
induces a smooth metric on
K_{E_{J}}+B_{J}
, and hence a smooth metric on
K_{\mathring{E}_{J}}
, using the canonical trivialization of
\mathcal{O}(B_{J})
on
\mathring{E}_{J}
.

Definition 6.11
.

The
residual measure
of an essential stratum
E_{J}
is defined as the volume form
e^{2\Psi_{J}}
on
\mathring{E}_{J}
associated to the smooth metric
\Psi_{J}
on
K_{\mathring{E}_{J}}
.

Since
\Psi_{J}
extends to a smooth metric on
K_{E_{J}}+B_{J}
, and
B_{J}
has snc support, the residual measure
e^{2\Psi_{J}}
has finite total mass iff
B_{J}
has coefficients
<1
, i.e. iff
E_{J}
is a minimal essential stratum (cf. Lemma
6.10
).

We next analyze the local behaviour of the measures
\sigma_{t}
and their residual measures near a given point of
x\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
. Denote by
J_{x}\subset I
the set of
E_{i}
’s containing
x
, so that
E_{x}:=E_{J_{x}}
is the unique (non-necessarily essential) stratum containing
x
in its relative interior. Pick an adapted chart

\mathcal{U}\simeq\mathbb{D}^{n+1}=\mathbb{D}^{J_{x}}\times\mathbb{D}^{J_{x}^{c}}

at
x
, with local log map

\Log_{t}\colon\mathcal{U}_{t}\to\Delta_{x}:=\Delta_{J_{x}}=\{w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J_{x}}\mid b\cdot w=1\},

i.e.
\Log_{t}(z)=(\frac{\log|z_{i}|}{\log|t|})
(see Definition
5.11
). The meromorphic form

(6.6)

\Omega:=\bigwedge_{i\in{J_{x}}}d\log z_{i}\wedge\bigwedge_{j\in{J_{x}^{c}}}dz_{j}

on
\mathcal{U}
induces a holomorphic family of holomorphic volume forms
\Omega_{t}
on the fibers
\mathcal{U}_{t}
, such that
\Omega_{t}\wedge\frac{dt}{t}=\Omega
. Denoting as above by

|\Omega_{t}|^{2}=i^{n^{2}}\Omega_{t}\wedge\overline{\Omega_{t}}

the associated smooth positive volume form on
\mathcal{U}_{t}
, we then have:

Lemma 6.12
.

There exists
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{R}_{>0})
such that

(6.7)

\sigma_{t}=\rho\prod_{i\in{J_{x}}}|z_{i}|^{2a_{i}}|\Omega_{t}|^{2}\quad\text{on}\quad\mathcal{U}_{t},

and

(6.8)

e^{2\psi_{J}}=\rho\prod_{i\in J_{x}\setminus J}|z_{i}|^{2a_{i}}|d\log z_{i}|^{2}\prod_{j\in J_{x}^{c}}|dz_{j}|^{2}\quad\text{on}\quad\mathcal{U}\cap E_{J}=\{0\}\times\mathbb{D}^{(J_{x}\setminus J)\cup J_{x}^{c}}

for any essential stratum
E_{J}
containing
E_{x}
,
i.e.
J\subset J_{x}
.

Proof.

On the one hand, (
6.2
) yields
\sigma_{t}=|\Omega_{t}|_{\psi_{t}}^{-2}|\Omega_{t}|^{2}
with
|\Omega_{t}|_{\psi_{t}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{U}_{t},\mathbb{R}_{>0})
the pointwise length of
\Omega_{t}
in the metric
\psi_{t}\in C^{\infty}(K_{X_{t}})
. On the other hand,
\Omega
defines a trivialization of
K_{\mathcal{X}}^{\log}
on
\mathcal{U}
, which induces a trivialization

\tau:=\prod_{i\in{J_{x}}}z_{i}^{a_{i}}\Omega\otimes(dt/t)^{-1}

of
\mathcal{K}=K_{\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{D}}^{\log}-\sum_{i}a_{i}E_{i}
(see (
6.3
)). Strictly speaking, this only makes sense after passing to some power of
\tau
, since we allow
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
, but
|\tau|_{\psi}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{R}_{>0})
is well-defined, and satisfies
|\tau|_{\psi}=\prod_{i\in{J_{x}}}|z_{i}|^{a_{i}}|\Omega_{t}|_{\psi}
on
\mathcal{U}_{t}
. This proves (
6.7
) with
\rho:=|\tau|_{\psi}^{-2}
, and (
6.8
) similarly follows by realizing the adjunction formula
K_{E_{J}}=(K_{\mathcal{X}}+\sum_{i\in J}E_{i})|_{E_{J}}
in terms of Poincaré residues.
∎

The stratum
E_{x}=E_{J_{x}}
is contained in an essential stratum iff

J_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}:=\{i\in J_{x}\mid a_{i}=0\}

is nonempty, in which case
E_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}:=E_{J_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}}
is the smallest essential stratum containing
x
. Assuming this, denote by
e^{2\psi_{x}}:=e^{2\psi_{J_{x}}}
the residual measure of
E_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}
,

\Delta_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}:=\Delta_{J_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}}\subset\Delta_{x}=\Delta_{J_{x}}

its dual face, and by
d_{x}:=\dim\Delta_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}=|J_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}|-1
and
\sigma_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}
the dimension and normalized (to mass
1
) Lebesgue measure of
\Delta_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}
, respectively. We may now state the following local version of Theorem
6.6
:

Theorem 6.13
.

If
J_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}
is empty, then
\sigma_{t}\to 0
weakly in
\mathcal{U}
. If not, then

(6.9)

\varepsilon_{t}^{d_{x}}\sigma_{t}\to c_{x}e^{2\psi_{x}}

weakly in
\mathcal{U}
, where
c_{x}=c_{J_{x}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
only depends on
J_{x}
. For any
\chi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathcal{U})
, we further have

(6.10)

\varepsilon_{t}^{d_{x}}(\Log_{t})_{\star}(\chi\sigma_{t})\to c_{x}\left(\int_{E_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}}\chi e^{2\psi_{x}}\right)\sigma_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}

weakly on
\Delta_{x}
.

Proof.

Assume first
J_{x}^{\prime}:=J_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}\neq\emptyset
. In what follows,
c_{x}
denotes a positive constant only depending on
J_{x}
, which is allowed to vary from line to line. It is enough to establish (
6.10
), which implies (
6.9
) by taking the total mass over
\Delta_{x}
. Setting
J_{x}^{\prime\prime}:=J_{x}\setminus J_{x}^{\prime}
and
f:=\chi\rho\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathcal{U})
with
\rho
as in Lemma
6.12
, (
6.10
) amounts to

\varepsilon_{t}^{d_{x}}\int_{\mathcal{U}_{t}}(\varphi\circ\Log_{t})\chi\sigma_{t}\to c_{x}\left(\int_{\{0\}\times\mathbb{D}^{J_{x}^{\prime\prime}\cup J_{x}^{c}}}f\prod_{i\in J_{x}^{\prime\prime}}|z_{i}|^{2a_{i}}|d\log z_{i}|^{2}\prod_{j\in J_{x}^{c}}|dz_{j}|^{2}\right)\left(\int_{\Delta_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}}\varphi\,\sigma_{x}^{\mathrm{ess}}\right).

for any
\varphi\in\Cz(\Delta_{x})
, by (
6.8
). Since
\Log_{t}\colon\mathcal{U}_{t}\to\Delta_{x}
factors through
\mathcal{U}_{t}\to\mathbb{D}^{J_{x}}
, arguing on each fiber
\mathbb{D}^{J_{x}}\times\{y\}\subset\mathcal{U}
with
y\in\mathbb{D}^{J_{x}^{c}}
, we may and do assume for notational simplicity
J_{x}^{c}=\emptyset
, i.e.
|J_{x}|=n+1
and
E_{x}=\{x\}
. We are thus in the setup of the model toric case of §
1.3
, and (
1.9
) yields
|\Omega_{t}|^{2}=c_{x}\varepsilon_{t}^{-n}\Log_{t}^{\star}\sigma_{x}
with
\sigma_{x}
the Lebesgue measure of
\Delta_{x}
normalized to mass
1
. Combining this with (
6.7
), we infer

\sigma_{t}=c_{x}\varepsilon_{t}^{-n}g\Log_{t}^{\star}\left(e^{-2\varepsilon_{t}^{-1}a\cdot w}\sigma_{x}\right),

and hence

\varepsilon_{t}^{d_{x}}\int_{\mathcal{U}_{t}}(\varphi\circ\Log_{t})\chi\sigma_{t}=c\varepsilon_{t}^{d_{x}-n}\int_{\Delta_{x}}\varphi(w)e^{-2\varepsilon_{t}^{-1}a\cdot w}\sigma_{x}(dw)\int_{\Log_{t}^{-1}(w)}f\rho_{t,w},

where
\rho_{t,w}
denotes the Haar measure of the fiber
\Log_{t}^{-1}(w)
. Fix
t\in\mathbb{D}^{\times}
. Writing
w=(w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{J_{x}}=\mathbb{R}^{J_{x}^{\prime}}\times\mathbb{R}^{J_{x}^{\prime\prime}}

\sup_{X_{0}}|\phi_{0}-\psi_{0}|<\delta
(see Definition
7.6
). By the non-Archimedean version of the Chern–Levine–Nirenberg inequality, we have

\left|\int(\phi_{1,0}-\phi_{2,0})\left(\MA(\psi_{0})-\MA(\phi_{0})\right)\right|\leq 2n\delta.

Example
3.10
yields again an extension of
\psi_{0}
to a hybrid Fubini–Study metric
(\psi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{D}}
. Since
(\psi_{t})
is in particular a hybrid model metric, Theorem
7.8
implies
\MA(\psi_{t})\to\MA(\psi_{0})
weakly in
X^{\mathrm{hyb}}
, and hence

\left|\varepsilon_{t}\int_{X_{t}}(\phi_{1,t}-\phi_{2,t})\MA(\psi_{t})-\int_{X_{0}}(\phi_{1,0}-\phi_{2,0})\MA(\psi_{0})\right|\leq\delta

for
t
small enough. Here we have used that
(\phi_{t}-\psi_{t})
is a hybrid continuous metric on the trivial line bundle (see Example
3.8
), which also implies

\varepsilon_{t}\sup_{X_{t}}|\phi_{t}-\psi_{t}|\to\sup_{X_{0}}|\phi_{0}-\psi_{0}|.

For
t
small enough we thus have
\varepsilon_{t}\sup_{X_{t}}|\phi_{t}-\psi_{t}|<\delta
, and the complex Chern–Levine–Nirenberg inequality yields

\varepsilon_{t}\left|\int(\phi_{1,t}-\phi_{2,t})\left(\MA(\phi_{t})-\MA(\psi_{t})\right)\right|\leq 2n\delta.

Combining these estimates, we get

\left|\int\varepsilon_{t}(\phi_{1,t}-\phi_{2,t})\MA(\phi_{t})-\int(\phi_{1,0}-\phi_{2,0})\,\MA(\phi_{0})\right|\leq(4n+1)\delta

for
t
small enough, and the result follows.
∎

7.4.
Non-Archimedean vs. real Monge–Ampère operator

Due to the intersection theoretic definition of the non-Archimedean Monge–Ampère operator that we have adopted, the latter appears to be more elusive than its complex counterpart. We next formulate a result due to Vilsmeier
[
Vil21
]
, which relates it to the
real
Monge–Ampère operator under a retraction invariance assumption.

In what follows, we pick a model
\mathcal{L}
of
L
determined on an snc model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
. Recall that the dual complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
admits a canonical retraction
p_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X_{0}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, provided by the non-Archimedean log map (see §
4.7
).

Using the model metric
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
as a reference metric allows to identify any other continuous metric
\phi
on
L_{0}
with the continuous function
\varphi:=\phi-\phi_{\mathcal{L}}
.

Definition 7.12
.

We say that
\varphi\in\Cz(X_{0})
is
\mathcal{L}
-psh
if the corresponding metric
\phi_{\mathcal{L}}+\varphi
is psh.

The set
\CPSH(\mathcal{L})\subset\Cz(X_{0})
of continuous
\mathcal{L}
-psh functions is in 1–1 correspondence with
\CPSH(L_{0})
. We define the Monge–Ampère measure of
\varphi\in\CPSH(\mathcal{L})
as that of the corresponding metric, i.e.

\MA(\varphi):=\MA(\phi_{\mathcal{L}}+\varphi).

As a first basic fact, we have (see
[
BFJ16
, Proposition 3.12]
):

Proposition 7.13
.

The restriction of any
\varphi\in\CPSH(\mathcal{L})
to each face
\Delta_{J}
of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\hookrightarrow X_{0}
is convex.

We may now state:

Theorem 7.14
.

[
Vil21
, Theorem 1.2]
Pick
\varphi\in\CPSH(\mathcal{L})
, an
n
-dimensional face
\Delta_{J}
of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, and assume the retraction invariance property
\varphi=\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}}
over
p_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathring{\Delta}_{J})
. Then

(7.7)

\MA(\varphi)=V^{-1}\MA_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi|_{\mathring{\Delta}_{J}})\text{ on }p_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathring{\Delta}_{J}).

As before,
V=(L_{K}^{n})
is the fiberwise volume of
L
, and
\MA_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi|_{\mathring{\Delta}_{J}})
is the real Monge–Ampère measure of the convex function
\varphi|_{\mathring{\Delta}_{J}}
, viewed as a measure on
p_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathring{\Delta}_{J})
supported on
\mathring{\Delta}_{J}
.

Remark 7.15
.

This is in fact a local result: for any convex function
f\colon\mathring{\Delta}_{J}\to\mathbb{R}
,
f\circ p_{\mathcal{X}}
is psh on the open set
p_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathring{\Delta}_{J})
, and
[
Vil21
, Theorem 1.2]
computes its non-Archimedean Monge–Ampère measure as

\left(dd^{c}f\circ p_{\mathcal{X}}\right)^{n}=\MA_{\mathbb{R}}(f),

a non-Archimedean analogue of (
1.10
) where
\MA_{\mathbb{R}}(f)
is viewed as above as a measure on
p_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\mathring{\Delta}_{J})
supported on
\mathring{\Delta}_{J}
. In the approach of Chambert-Loir and Ducros
[
CLD12
]
, this identity holds (almost) by definition.

7.5.
Maximal hybrid extension

In this final section we show that every non-Archimedean metric
\phi_{0}\in\CPSH(L_{0})
can be extended to a hybrid continuous family of metrics
\phi_{t}\in\CPSH(L_{t})
for
t
in a slightly shrunken disc. This follows from the following more precise result, a ‘continuous version’ of
[
Reb23
, Theorem 4.3.3]
(which reduces to
[
BBJ21
, Theorem 6.6]
when
X=Z\times\mathbb{D}^{\times}
).

Theorem 7.16
.

Fix a closed disc
\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\subset\mathbb{D}
and a continuous family of reference metrics
\phi_{\mathrm{ref},t}\in\CPSH(L_{t})
for
|t|=r
. Then every non-Archimedean metric
\phi\in\CPSH(L_{0})
admits a (unique) largest hybrid continuous extension
(\phi_{t})_{t\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}
such that

(i)

the family
(\phi_{t})_{0<|t|<r}
is psh;

(ii)

\phi_{t}=\phi_{\mathrm{ref},t}
for
|t|=r
.

Here we say that a family of metrics
(\phi_{t})_{t\in U}
with
U\subset\mathbb{D}^{\times}
open is
psh
if the corresponding metric on
L|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}
is psh.

Proof of Theorem
7.16
(sketch).

We claim that the desired extension
(\phi_{t})_{t\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}
coincides with the pointwise supremum
\left(P_{t}(\phi_{0})\right)_{t\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}
of the family of all hybrid continuous metrics
(\psi_{t})_{t\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}
such that

•

(\psi_{t})_{0<|t|<r}
is psh;

•

\psi_{t}\leq\phi_{\mathrm{ref},t}
for
|t|=r
;

•

\psi_{0}\leq\phi_{0}
.

When
\phi_{0}
is a model metric determined by an ample model
\mathcal{L}
of
L
, this can be deduced from
[
Reb23
, Theorem 1.4.4]
. In the general case, note that for any
\phi^{\prime}_{0}\in\CPSH(L_{0})
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(a)

\phi_{0}\leq\phi^{\prime}_{0}\Longrightarrow P_{t}(\phi_{0})\leq P_{t}(\phi^{\prime}_{0})
;

(b)

P_{t}(\phi_{0}+c)=P_{t}(\phi_{0})+c\log\frac{r}{|t|}
.

As an easy consequence of Definition
7.6
, we can find a sequence of model metrics
\phi_{0,j}
, each determined by an ample model of
L
, such that
\delta_{j}:=\sup_{X_{0}}|\phi_{0}-\phi_{0,j}|
tends to
0
. By the first step,
(P_{t}(\phi_{0,j}))_{0<|t|<r}
is psh,
P_{t}(\phi_{0,j})=\phi_{\mathrm{ref},t}
for
|t|=r
and
P_{0}(\phi_{0,j})=\phi_{0,j}
. Since
\phi_{0}-\delta_{j}\leq\phi_{0,j}\leq\phi_{0}+\delta_{j}
, (a) and (b) imply

\left|P_{t}(\phi_{0,j})-P_{t}(\phi_{0})\right|\leq\delta_{j}\log\frac{r}{|t|},

and the result easily follows.
∎

8.
Hybrid stability for Monge–Ampère equations

Closely following

]

As in Yang Li’s work, this relies on a crucial ‘asymmetric version’ of Kołodziej’s stability theorem for complex Monge–Ampère equations, which is presented in the Appendix.

In what follows,
X\to\mathbb{D}^{\times}

over

8.1.
General setup

As in §

, we consider the family of volume forms

t
small enough. Thanks to the uniform estimates

\mu_{t}\leq A\Capa_{\omega_{t}}^{2},\quad|\widetilde{\psi}_{t}|\leq|\widetilde{\varphi}_{t}|+1\leq M,

see (
8.4
) and Corollary
8.3
, Theorem
A.9
now shows that for
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}(\delta,n,A,M)
we have

\dd_{1}(\widetilde{\psi}_{t},\varphi_{t})\leq\delta,\quad\widetilde{\psi}_{t}\leq\varphi_{t}+\delta

for all
t
small enough, and hence

\dd_{1}(\widetilde{\varphi}_{t},\varphi_{t})\leq 2\delta,\quad\widetilde{\varphi}_{t}\leq\varphi_{t}+2\delta\text{ on }X_{t},

since
\sup_{X_{t}}|\widetilde{\psi}_{t}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{t}|\leq\delta
. This concludes the proof of Theorem
8.7
.

8.4.
Smooth convergence in the generic region

For each
J
let
\rho_{J}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{U}_{J})
be a local weight of the reference metric
\Phi_{\mathrm{ref}}
on
\mathcal{L}
that defines the rescaled Kähler form
\omega_{t}=\varepsilon_{t}dd^{c}\Phi_{\mathrm{ref},t}
, so that
\omega_{t}=\varepsilon_{t}dd^{c}\rho_{J}
on
\mathcal{U}_{J,t}
.

In the generic region
\Log_{t}^{-1}(\Delta_{J}^{\reg})
, both functions
\Log_{t}^{\star}\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{J,t}:=\varepsilon_{t}\rho_{J}+\varphi_{t}
are smooth and strictly psh, and they satisfy

\left(dd^{c}\Log_{t}^{\star}\varphi_{0}\right)^{n}=(1+f_{J})\left(dd^{c}\varphi_{J,t}\right)^{n}

with
f_{J}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{U}_{J})
vanishing at
x_{J}
, see (
8.10
). By general elliptic regularity theory
[
Sav07
]
, the
C^{\infty}
-estimates of Corollary
8.8
are thus reduced to the following
C^{0}
-estimate:

Lemma 8.10
.

For any compact
\Sigma\subset\Delta_{J}^{\reg}
we have
\sup_{\Log_{t}^{-1}(\Sigma)}|\Log_{t}^{\star}\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{J,t}|\to 0
.

Proof.

Pick
\delta>0
. For
t
small enough, (
8.11
) implies

\Log_{t}^{\star}\varphi_{0}\leq\varphi_{J,t}+\delta,\quad\int_{\mathcal{U}_{J,t}}|\varphi_{J,t}-\Log_{t}^{\star}\varphi_{0}|\,d\mu_{t}\leq\delta

Since
|\varphi_{J,t}|,|\varphi_{0}|\leq M
and
\Log_{t}^{\star}\mu_{J}=(1+f_{J})\mu_{t}
, this yields

\int_{\mathcal{U}_{J,t}}\varphi_{J,t}\Log_{t}^{\star}\mu_{J}\leq\int_{\mathcal{U}_{J,t}}\Log_{t}^{\star}\varphi_{0}\Log_{t}^{\star}\mu_{J}+2\delta=\int_{\mathring{\Delta}_{J}}\varphi_{0}\,\mu_{J}+2\delta

for
t
small enough. Since
\varphi_{J,t}
is psh, the function

f_{J,t}:=\int_{\mathcal{U}_{J,t}/\mathring{\Delta}_{J}}\varphi_{J,t}

obtained by integration along the fibers of the principal
G_{J}
-bundle
\Log_{t}\colon\mathcal{U}_{J,t}\to\mathring{\Delta}_{J}
is convex and satisfies
\varphi_{J,t}\leq\Log_{t}^{\star}f_{J,t}
, by the mean value inequality (see §
1.3
). This implies

\Log_{t}^{\star}\varphi_{0}\leq\varphi_{J,t}+\delta\leq\Log_{t}^{\star}f_{J,t}+\delta
, and hence
\varphi_{0}\leq f_{J,t}+\delta
. Further,

\int_{\mathring{\Delta}_{J}}f_{J,t}\mu_{J}=\int_{\mathcal{U}_{J,t}}\varphi_{J,t}\Log_{t}^{\star}\mu_{J}\leq\int_{\mathring{\Delta}_{J}}\varphi_{0}\,\mu_{J}+2\delta.

Since both functions
f_{J,t}
and
\varphi_{0}
are convex, this implies
\sup_{\Sigma}|f_{J,t}-\varphi_{0}|\leq C\delta
for a constant
C=C(\Sigma)>0
. Thus

\varphi_{J,t}\leq\Log_{t}^{\star}f_{J,t}\leq\Log_{t}^{\star}\varphi_{0}+C\delta

on
\Log_{t}^{-1}(\Sigma)
, and the result follows.
∎

Corollary
8.8
guarantees that the rescaled Kähler metric

\varepsilon_{t}dd^{c}\phi_{t}=\omega_{t}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}\in\varepsilon_{t}c_{1}(L_{t})

is
C^{\infty}
-close in the generic region to the
semiflat metric

dd^{c}\Log_{t}^{\star}\varphi_{0}
on the principal bundle

\Log_{t}\colon\Log_{t}^{-1}(\Delta_{J}^{\reg})\to\Delta_{J}^{\reg}.

In the Calabi–Yau case, i.e. when
\sigma_{t}=|\Omega_{t}|^{2}
for a holomorphic volume form
\Omega_{t}
on
X_{t}
,
\Log_{t}
is a special Lagrangian fibration for the semiflat metric, and a perturbation argument based on
[
Zha17
]
then yields a special Lagrangian fibration for the Calabi–Yau metric
\omega_{\mathrm{CY},t}=dd^{c}\phi_{t}
in the generic region
[
Li23
, Theorem 4.9]
.

Appendix A
Stability theorems for complex Monge–Ampère equations

In this appendix we work on a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
, of dimension
n:=\dim X
and volume
V=\int_{X}\omega^{n}
.

A.1.
Two metrics

We denote by
\CPSH(\omega)
the set of continuous
\omega
-psh functions
\varphi\in\Cz(X)
, and by

\MA(\varphi)=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi):=V^{-1}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}

the (normalized) Monge–Ampère operator. The space
\CPSH(\omega)
is complete with respect to the supnorm metric

\dd_{\infty}(\varphi,\psi):=\sup_{X}|\varphi-\psi|.

We shall also consider the
Darvas metric

\dd_{1}
, which satisfies

(A.1)

C_{n}^{-1}\dd_{1}(\varphi,\psi)\leq\int|\varphi-\psi|\MA(\varphi)+\int|\varphi-\psi|\MA(\psi)\leq C_{n}\dd_{1}(\varphi,\psi)

for a constant
C_{n}>0
only depending on
n
, see
[
Dar19
, Theorem 3.32]
. The Monge–Ampère operator is continuous in the
\dd_{1}
-distance — for instance by (
7.3
) combined with
\ii(\varphi,\psi)\leq C_{n}\dd_{1}(\varphi,\psi)
. The completion of
\CPSH(\omega)
with respect to
\dd_{1}
can be identified with the space
\mathcal{E}^{1}(\omega)
of potentials of finite energy.

A.2.
The
\dd_{\infty}
-stability theorem

The
Bedford–Taylor capacity
is defined by

\Capa(B):=\sup\left\{\int_{B}\MA(\varphi)\mid\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega),\,0\leq\varphi\leq 1\right\}.

for each Borel set
B\subset X
. Following Kołodziej’s fundamental work
[
Kol98
]
, we shall consider probability measures
\mu
on
X
that are well-dominated by the capacity, in the sense that
\mu\leq A\Capa^{2}
for a constant
A>0
. A broad class of such measures is provided by the following result, a well-known consequence of the Alexander–Taylor comparison theorem (see for instance
[
Li22b
, Lemma 2.9]
).

Lemma A.1
.

Assume
\mu
satisfies a
Skoda estimate

(A.2)

\int_{X}e^{-\alpha\varphi}\,d\mu\leq C

for all
\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)
normalized by
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
, where
\alpha,C>0
. Then
\mu\leq A\Capa^{2}
for a constant
A=A(n,\alpha,C)>0
.

Measures satisfying (
A.2
) are fairly common:

Example A.2
.

By the uniform version of Skoda’s theorem
[
Zer01
]
, any smooth positive volume form
\mu
on
X
satisfies a Skoda estimate.

Example A.3
.

If
\mu
satisfies a Skoda estimate, Hölder’s inequality shows that any measure with
L^{1+\varepsilon}
density with respect to
\mu
satisfies a Skoda estimate as well. In particular, any measure with
L^{1+\varepsilon}
-density with respect to Lebesgue measure satisfies a Skoda estimate.

Example A.4
.

If
\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)
is Hölder continuous, then
\MA(\varphi)
satisfies a Skoda estimate, see
[
DNS10
, Corollary 1.2]
.

In what follows we set

\CPSH_{0}(\omega):=\{\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)\mid\int\varphi\,\omega^{n}=0\}.

Kołodziej’s results yield the following existence and stability theorems
[
Kol98
,
Kol03
]

Theorem A.5
.

For any probability measure
\mu
that satisfies
\mu\leq A\Capa^{2}
with
A>0
, there exists a unique
\varphi\in\CPSH_{0}(\omega)
such that
\MA(\varphi)=\mu
, which further satisfies
\sup_{X}|\varphi|\leq M
for a constant
M=M(n,A)
.

Remark A.6
.

The result is more commonly stated with the normalization
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
, but the two versions are equivalent since
|\sup_{X}\varphi|
and
V^{-1}\left|\int_{X}\varphi\,\omega^{n}\right|
are both bounded by
\sup_{X}|\varphi|
, which is under control.

Theorem A.7
.

Pick
\varphi,\psi\in\CPSH_{0}(\omega)
, and assume that

\MA(\varphi),\MA(\psi)\leq A\Capa^{2}

with
A>0
. For any
\delta>0
, we can then find
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\delta,n,A)
such that

\int_{X}\left|\MA(\varphi)-\MA(\psi)\right|\leq\varepsilon\Longrightarrow\dd_{\infty}(\varphi,\psi)\leq\delta.

Here the left-hand side denotes the total variation of the signed measure
\MA(\phi)-\MA(\psi)
, which coincides with its operator norm as an element of
\Cz(X)^{\vee}
.

Remark A.8
.

As in the above remark, a different normalization is used in
[
Kol03
, Theorem 4.1]
, but any choice of normalization leads to the same conclusion that
\varphi-\psi
is
C^{0}
-close to a constant.

A.3.
The
\dd_{1}
-stability theorem

In
[
Li23
, Theorem 2.5]
, Yang Li adapts Kołodziej’s proof of Theorem
A.7
to establish an
asymmetric
stability theorem, where the well-domination assumption only bears on the Monge–Ampère measure of one of the two functions. We formulate it here in the following slightly improved version.

Theorem A.9
.

Pick
\varphi,\psi\in\CPSH_{0}(\omega)
, and assume given
A,M>0
such that

(i)

\MA(\varphi)\leq A\Capa^{2}
;

(ii)

\sup_{X}|\psi|\leq M
.

For any
\delta>0
, we can then find
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\delta,n,A,M)>0
such that
\int_{X}\left|\MA(\varphi)-\MA(\psi)\right|\leq\varepsilon
implies

(a)

\dd_{1}(\varphi,\psi)\leq\delta
;

(b)

\psi\leq\varphi+\delta
on
X
.

The first ingredient in the proof of Theorem
A.9
is the following ‘approximate domination principle’, a direct application of Kołodziej’s capacity estimates.

Lemma A.10
.

[
Li22b
, Theorem 2.7]
Assume
\varphi,\psi\in\CPSH(\omega)
satisfy (i), (ii) of Theorem
A.9
, and set
\mu:=\MA(\varphi)
. Then

(A.3)

\psi\leq\varphi+C\mu\big(\varphi<\psi\big)^{1/2n}

for a constant
C=C(n,A,M)>0
.

Proof.

In what follows
C=C(n,A,M)>0
is allowed to vary from line to line. Introduce the capacity relative to
\psi

\Capa_{\psi}(B):=\sup\left\{\int_{B}\MA(\varphi)\mid\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega),\,0\leq\varphi-\psi\leq 1\right\}.

Since
\sup|\psi|\leq M
, it is easy to see that
C^{-1}\Capa\leq\Capa_{\psi}\leq C\Capa
, and hence
\mu\leq C\Capa_{\psi}^{2}
. Consider the function
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
defined by

f(t):=\mu\big(\varphi<\psi-t\big)^{1/2n},

It is nonincreasing, right-continuous, tends to
0
at infinity, and we have
f(t)=0
iff
\psi\leq\varphi+t
, by the domination principle (see Corollary
7.5
). The key ingredient is then the inequality

s^{n}\Capa_{\psi}\big(\varphi<\psi-s-t\big)\leq\mu\big(\varphi<\psi-t\big)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in[0,1]
, a general consequence of the comparison principle (see for instance
[
EGZ09
, Lemma 2.3]
), which combines with
\mu\leq C\Capa_{\psi}^{2}
to yield the decay estimate

sf(t+s)\leq Cf(t)^{2}

for
t\geq 0
and
s\in[0,1]
. Thanks to an elementary lemma
[
EGZ09
, Lemma 2.4]
, this guarantees that
f(t)=0
for
t\geq 4Cf(0)
as soon as
f(0)=\mu\big(\varphi<\psi\big)^{1/2n}
is smaller than
1/2C
. The result follows.
∎

The heart of the proof of Theorem
A.9
is the following statement.

Lemma A.11
.

[
Li22b
, Theorem 2.7]
Pick
\varphi,\psi\in\CPSH(\omega)
, and assume that

(i)

\mu:=\MA(\varphi)
satisfies
\mu\leq A\Capa^{2}
;

(ii)

\sup_{X}|\varphi|,\sup_{X}|\psi|\leq M
;

(iii)

\mu\big(\psi\leq\varphi\big)\geq\delta>0
;

(iv)

\int_{X}\left|\MA(\varphi)-\MA(\psi)\right|\leq\varepsilon
;

If
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}(\delta,n,A,M)
we then have
\psi\leq\varphi+C\varepsilon^{\alpha}
with
C=C(\delta,nA,M)>0
and
\alpha:=1/(2n+3)
.

We will not reproduce the proof here, and only mention that it combines Lemma
A.10
, the log concavity of the Monge–Ampère operator, and the introduction of the solution to an auxiliary Monge–Ampère equation to which the comparison principle gets applied, along the same lines as
[
Kol03
, Theorem 4.1]
. Note that Li assumes a Skoda estimate in place of (i), which is what is used in the proof, and that Li assumes
\varphi,\psi
to be smooth, which only gets used in the log concavity of the Monge–Ampère operator and the Chebyshev-type inequality

\mu\big(f\geq 1-s\big)\leq s^{-1}\int|\mu-\nu|

with
\nu:=\MA(\psi)
and
f=d\mu/d\nu
, both of which remain true under our slightly more general assumptions (see
[
BEGZ10
, Proposition 1.11]
for the former).

Proof of Theorem
A.9
.

Set
f:=\psi-\varphi
, and note that
\sup_{X}|f|\leq C
by Theorem
A.5
, where
C>0
denotes a constant only depending on
n,A,M
, that is allowed to vary from line to line. It will be enough to show
\mu\big(|f|\geq C\delta\big)\leq\delta
for
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}(\delta,n,A,M)
. Indeed, this will yield (b), by Lemma
A.10
. Since
|f|\leq 2M
, it will also imply

\int|f|\MA(\varphi)=\int|f|\,d\mu\leq 2M\mu\big(|f|\geq\delta\big)+\delta\leq C\delta,

thus

\int|f|\MA(\psi)\leq\int|f|\,\MA(\varphi)+2M\int\left|\MA(\varphi)-\MA(\psi)\right|\leq C\delta,

and hence
\dd_{1}(\varphi,\psi)\leq C\delta
, by (
A.1
).

Setting
m:=\sup_{X}f
, we first claim that
\mu\big(f\leq m-\delta\big)\leq\delta
if
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}(\delta,n,A,M)
. Assuming by contradiction
\mu\big(f\leq m-\delta\big)>\delta
, Lemma
A.11
yields

f\leq m-\delta+C\varepsilon^{\alpha}\leq m-\delta/2

whenever
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}(\delta,n,A,M)
, and taking the sup over
X
yields the desired contradiction. We next observe that

\ii(\varphi,\psi)=\int_{X}f\,(\MA(\psi)-\MA(\varphi))\leq(\sup_{X}|f|)\int_{X}\left|\MA(\varphi)-\MA(\psi)\right|\leq C\varepsilon,

and similarly
\ii(\varphi,0),\ii(\psi,0)\leq C
. Since
\int f\omega^{n}=\int(\varphi-\psi)\omega^{n}=0
by the normalization of
\varphi,\psi
, (
7.3
) yields

\left|\int fd\mu\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{\alpha_{n}}\leq\delta

for
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}(\delta,n,A,M)
. Since
\mu\big(|f-m|\geq\delta\big)\leq\delta
(by the above claim) and
\sup_{X}|f-m|\leq\sup_{X}|f|+|m|\leq C
, we infer

\displaystyle|m|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\{|f-m|\geq\delta\}}(f-m)d\mu\right|+\left|\int_{\{|f-m|<\delta\}}(f-m)d\mu\right|+\int_{X}|f|\mu

\displaystyle\leq C\mu\big(|f-m|\geq\delta\big)+2\delta\leq C\delta,

thus
\{(|f|\geq C\delta\}\subset\{|f-m|\geq\delta\}
, and hence
\mu\left(|f|\geq C\delta\right)\leq\delta
. The result follows.
∎

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChronic total occlusion (CTO) is associated with increased ischemic burden, heart failure, and mortality. Comparative clinical outcomes associated with medical therapy, percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), and coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) remain uncertain.ObjectivesTo compare clinical outcomes associated with medical therapy, PCI, and CABG in patients with CTO.MethodsWe analyzed 741 patients with angiographically confirmed CTO enrolled across 7 tertiary centers in 4 Gulf countries between 2021 and 2023. Patients were managed with an initial strategy of medical therapy (n = 151), PCI (n = 441), or CABG (n = 149). The primary in-hospital endpoint was major adverse cardiovascular events. The long-term endpoint was major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events-free survival.ResultsTreatment allocation differed substantially by baseline clinical and angiographic risk. Medically managed patients had worse renal function, more severely reduced left ventricular ejection fraction, and more cardiogenic shock despite simpler CTO anatomy CABG patients had more extensive multivessel disease, whereas PCI patients had greater CTO lesion complexity. After adjustment, neither PCI nor CABG differed significantly from medical therapy for in-hospital major adverse cardiovascular events. At a median follow-up of 22 months, adjusted 36-month restricted mean survival time analyses showed no statistically significant difference in major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events-free survival between PCI, CABG, and medical therapy. Adjusted Canadian Cardiovascular Society class I at last follow-up was reached in 86.2% of medical therapy, 91.7% of PCI, and 98.0% of CABG patients; the adjusted difference vs medical therapy was significant for CABG (P < 0.001) but not for PCI P = 0.17).ConclusionsAfter adjustment, no treatment strategy demonstrated superiority for hard cardiovascular outcomes; revascularization, particularly CABG, was associated with a lower angina burden at follow-up.Key words: CABG, chronic total occlusion, medical therapy, PCI, propensity weighting, RMST

Chronic total occlusion (CTO) represents an advanced manifestation of coronary atherosclerosis in which progressive plaque accumulation, thrombus organization, and vascular remodeling result in complete arterial obstruction. Early CTOs often contain lipid-rich, inflamed plaque with active neovascularization, whereas long-standing lesions evolve into fibrotic and heavily calcified segments with dense collagen that make percutaneous intervention technically demanding.1,2 CTO is defined as 100% luminal obstruction with absent antegrade flow (TIMI flow grade 0) of at least 3 months duration, corroborated by clinical history, prior imaging, or well-developed collateral circulation.1, 2, 3 CTOs are detected in 15% to 33% of patients undergoing diagnostic coronary angiography and are associated with greater ischemic burden, more severe angina, higher rates of heart failure, increased long-term mortality, and elevated rates of major adverse cardiac events.3, 4, 5, 6 Despite their prevalence and clinical importance, CTOs are frequently managed conservatively because of technical complexity, procedural risk, and uncertainty regarding long-term event reduction.

Current guidelines recommend a patient-centered, anatomy-guided approach to CTO management. Optimal medical therapy remains first line, with percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) or coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) reserved for select patients with refractory symptoms or large ischemic territories.7 Advancements in guidewire technology, microcatheters, and structured operator training have raised CTO-PCI success rates above 90% in expert centers, although complication rates remain higher than for non-CTO-PCI.3 CABG continues to play an important role in multivessel disease, particularly when complete revascularization is surgically feasible.7,8

Randomized trials have provided some clarity but leave key questions unresolved. DECISION-CTO found no reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) with CTO-PCI compared with a no-CTO-PCI strategy, although the trial was limited by early termination, substantial crossover, and reduced power.9 EURO-CTO demonstrated improvements in angina frequency and quality of life with PCI, but no difference in hard clinical endpoints between groups.10 The EXPLORE trial, conducted in post-STEMI patients with a co-existing CTO, similarly failed to show improved ventricular function or outcomes after CTO-PCI.11 By contrast, registry and meta-analytic data have suggested better long-term outcomes after successful revascularization: in the OPEN-CTO registry, procedural success exceeded 85% and correlated with fewer repeat revascularizations at 1 year,12 and meta-analyses by Christakopoulos et al13 and Hoebers et al14 reported substantially lower long-term mortality with successful CTO-PCI, although selection bias and heterogeneity limit causal inference. As a result, the 2021 American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association/Society for Cardiovascular Angiography & Interventions revascularization guidelines assign CTO-PCI a Class IIb recommendation.7

Three critical gaps persist in the available evidence. First, the vast majority of CTO data originate from North American and European centers, leaving real-world outcomes in other health care systems, including the Gulf region, poorly characterized. Second, direct three-way comparisons of initial medical therapy, PCI, and CABG in a single observational cohort with rigorous confounding adjustment are uncommon. Third, few studies have explicitly employed modern causal inference methods, including doubly robust estimators and restricted mean survival time (RMST), to overcome the severe treatment-selection bias that affects CTO registries. To address these gaps, the Gulf CTO registry was established as a multicenter observational cohort across 7 high-volume tertiary care centers in 4 Gulf countries. This study evaluates utilization patterns, procedural outcomes, and adjusted short- and long-term clinical outcomes of medical therapy, PCI, and CABG for coronary CTO in this region, with the aim of informing patient selection, procedural strategy, and guideline implementation in the Gulf and comparable health care systems worldwide.

Methods

Study design and population

The Gulf CTO registry is a retrospective, observational, multicenter registry conducted across 7 high-volume tertiary care centers in 4 Gulf countries: the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, the Kingdom of Bahrain, Oman, and the United Arab Emirates. Patients were identified from January 1, 202,1 through December 31, 2023, with a minimum of 12-month structured follow-up. Cases were screened using International Classification of Diseases codes (ICD-10-CM I25.82) at each center and confirmed by angiographic review.

Eligible patients were adults (≥18 years) with an angiographically confirmed CTO managed with PCI, CABG, or conservative. Patients presenting with ST-elevation myocardial infarction (MI) (STEMI) or non-STEMI (NSTEMI) who underwent PCI of the culprit artery and had a co-existing CTO in a nonculprit vessel were included if a staged CTO strategy was documented. Among 953 screened patients, the final study population comprised 741. A total of 212 patients were excluded for the following reasons: inadequate follow-up or loss to follow-up (n = 102); nontrue CTO, including subacute occlusions, functional CTO, or spontaneous recanalization (n = 40); severe comorbidities or life expectancy less than 1 year (n = 14); acute STEMI or recent coronary occlusion without a separate CTO lesion (n = 36); high bleeding risk precluding dual antiplatelet therapy (n = 13); and incomplete procedural documentation or aborted access (n = 7) (Figure 1).

Analyses were performed at the patient level based on the first (index) CTO management strategy. For patients with multiple CTO lesions, the index lesion was defined as the CTO targeted at the first revascularization attempt. Repeat attempts were captured descriptively and not counted as separate index events.

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of King Faisal Specialist Hospital and Research Center, Jeddah (RAC #2024-66; approval date 05 June 2024), and by the Institutional Review Boards of all participating centers. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.15 Informed consent was waived, given the retrospective, observational nature of the study and the use of deidentified data.

Patients’ selection

Treatment assignment was not protocolized and reflected Heart Team, operator, and treating-physician decision-making at each participating center, based on clinical presentation, comorbidity, and procedural/surgical risk, coronary anatomy and ischemic burden, ventricular function, feasibility of complete revascularization, and patient preference. Conservative management was generally selected when symptoms were absent or controlled, the ischemic/viable territory was limited, procedural or surgical risk was prohibitive, or patient preference favored noninvasive treatment; CTO-PCI when symptoms, objective ischemia, heart failure/left ventricular (LV) dysfunction, or a goal of complete revascularization supported it and the anatomy was technically suitable; and CABG for multivessel or left main-pattern disease amenable to surgical complete revascularization.

Non-CTO lesions were recorded separately from the index CTO lesion. Consistent with the study inclusion criteria and routine practice at participating centers, in patients presenting with ACS, the culprit non-CTO lesion was generally treated first, and CTO revascularization was undertaken as a staged strategy after stabilization; in elective patients, non-CTO lesions were treated before, during, or after the CTO procedure at the discretion of the operator and Heart Team. A non-CTO vessel was treated percutaneously in 46.3% of PCI patients and 43.7% of medically managed patients; no CABG patient underwent PCI of a non-CTO vessel, non-CTO disease being managed surgically.

Data collection

Clinical, angiographic, and procedural data, including ventricular function, medications, and complications, were extracted from institutional databases and catheterization laboratory records. Baseline characteristics included demographics, cardiovascular risk factors, prior revascularization, and relevant comorbidities. Lesion-level data included target vessel, occlusion morphology, lesion length, and collateral grade (Rentrop classification),16 as well as Multicenter CTO Registry of Japan Score (J-CTO)17 and Prospective Global Registry for the Study of CTO Score (PROGRESS-CTO)18 scores. Procedural variables included access site, crossing strategy (antegrade and/or retrograde), imaging intravascular ultrasound and optical coherence tomography, re-entry techniques, contrast volume, total radiation dose, and procedural duration. Unless otherwise specified, all definitions followed the CTO Academic Research Consortium standards.19

Key definitions

CTO definition

A coronary CTO was defined as the absence of antegrade flow (TIMI flow grade 0) with an estimated occlusion duration greater than 3 months, determined by symptom onset, prior MI in the target-vessel territory, or comparison with prior angiography. Subacute occlusions (<3 months) were excluded.19,20

Operator experience

A CTO expert was defined as a primary operator performing >10 CTO-PCI procedures per year. Among qualifying operators, annual volume was categorized as low (<30 cases/year), medium (30-60 cases/year), or high (>60 cases/year).19,21

Technical and procedural success

Technical success was defined as the achievement of <30% residual diameter stenosis with restoration of TIMI grade 3 antegrade flow. Procedural success was defined as technical success without in-hospital MACE.19

Outcome definitions

Short-term endpoint (in-hospital MACE)

The primary short-term endpoint was a composite of MACE during the index hospitalization, defined as cardiac death, periprocedural MI, acute heart failure, stroke or cerebrovascular accident, and target vessel revascularization.

Long-term endpoint (major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events [MACCE]-free survival)

The primary long-term endpoint was time to first occurrence of MACCE, a composite of cardiac death, MI, heart failure, cerebrovascular accident, and any revascularization, assessed over up to 60 months of follow-up. Clinical follow-up was performed at 12 months using electronic health records. These 2 endpoints are referred to as MACE (in-hospital) and MACCE (longitudinal) throughout this manuscript to avoid ambiguity.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as means with SDs or medians with IQRs, depending on distributional characteristics, and compared using ANOVA or the Kruskal-Wallis test. Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and percentages and compared using the chi-square or Fisher exact test.

To account for the nonrandomized treatment assignment and adjust for baseline confounding by indication across the 3 strategies, we employed an inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) approach.19,22,23 Treatment assignment was modeled using a single multinomial logistic regression (medical therapy, PCI, and CABG) incorporating 30 pretreatment covariates entered as first-order terms: age, baseline hemoglobin, and baseline creatinine as untransformed continuous variables; LV ejection fraction as an ordinal category; and 26 binary clinical and angiographic indicators (listed in Supplemental Table 1). No interaction or higher-order terms were specified, a parsimonious specification chosen to preserve degrees of freedom and avoid quasi-complete separation given the small group sizes. Covariate balance was assessed using the absolute standardized mean difference (SMD), with SMD <0.10 considered indicative of adequate balance. The common support (positivity) assumption was evaluated using a propensity score overlap plot and formal overlap-violation diagnostics. Stabilized weights were centered on 1.0 (mean 1.00, SD 0.51, range 0.26-4.66; 99th percentile 3.11), and no weight exceeded 5, so trimming was not required. The effective sample size after weighting was 597 of 739 (81%; medical 103/151, PCI 392/439, CABG 102/149). Preweighting and postweighting SMDs for every covariate are reported in Supplemental Table 1; the mean absolute SMD after weighting was 0.035 (PCI vs medical) and 0.056 (CABG vs medical); all covariates achieved SMD <0.10 in the PCI comparison except age, and 24 of 30 achieved SMD <0.10 in the CABG comparison, with the remaining 6 between 0.10 and 0.20 (none >0.20), with no positivity violations on formal overlap diagnostics (739/739).

For the short-term binary outcome (in-hospital MACE), we employed a doubly robust IPTW with regression adjustment (IPWRA) estimator, combining IPTW weights with an outcome regression model to provide unbiased estimates of the average treatment effect on the treated.22 To prevent quasi-complete separation in the outcome regression given limited events, the regression component was restricted to 7 prespecified clinical predictors: diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, hypertension, hemoglobin, creatinine, ejection fraction category, and cardiogenic shock. For the doubly robust in-hospital analysis, the corresponding weighted observation counts (Stata tebalance) were 252.3 (medical), 250.2 (PCI), and 236.6 (CABG). Results are reported as absolute risk differences with 95% CIs.

For the long-term time-to-event outcome (freedom from MACCE), survival probabilities were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared with the log-rank test. Stabilized IPTW weights were then applied. Because the proportional hazards assumption was not tenable, RMST was used as the primary summary measure for the adjusted time-to-event comparisons.24,25 The co-primary RMST horizon was set at 36 months, the period over which the number at risk remained substantial relative to the 22-month median follow-up. A 60-month horizon was additionally examined but, because few events occurred beyond the median follow-up and no events were observed beyond 60 months in at least 1 arm, the 60-month estimates rely on extrapolation and are reported as exploratory only. Results are reported as RMST for each group and RMST difference in months with 95% CIs. To account for the multicenter design, the primary adjusted RMST differences were re-estimated using a center-cluster bootstrap as a sensitivity analysis for center-level clustering of treatment selection, follow-up, and event ascertainment. Intention-to-treat analysis was employed in which group assignment was by the first (index) management strategy selected after diagnostic angiography.

Missing data were handled using complete case analysis; 739 of 741 enrolled patients (99.7%) had complete covariate data, with the 2 excluded patients having missing hemoglobin and creatinine values. Although the proportion of missing data was negligible and unlikely to introduce meaningful bias, a multiple imputation sensitivity analysis was not performed, and represents a limitation. All statistical analyses were 2-tailed; P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using Stata (StataCorp).

Sample size note: This was a registry study; no prospective power calculation was conducted prior to data collection. Post hoc sensitivity calculations indicate that the available 55 in-hospital MACE events provided approximately 35% power to detect an absolute risk difference of 5% between treatment strategies, underscoring the study's primary purpose as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. The long-term MACCE analysis was better powered owing to a higher event count.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 741 patients with CTO were included: 151 (20.4%) were treated initially with medical therapy, 441 (59.5%) underwent PCI, and 149 (20.1%) underwent CABG (Table 1). Baseline demographic characteristics were well balanced across groups, with no significant differences in the mean age (approximately 60 years), male predominance (>82% across all groups), or BMI.

Clinically important differences in presentation and disease severity were evident. The initial medical therapy group had worse renal function, including higher median creatinine (93 μmol/L vs 83 μmol/L for PCI and 86 μmol/L for CABG; P < 0.001) and lower creatinine clearance (71 vs 81 and 80 mL/min; P < 0.001), a higher prevalence of severely reduced LV ejection fraction (<35%; 22.5% vs 9.8% and 10.1%; P = 0.001), and more MI complicated by cardiogenic shock (6.6% vs 2.3% and 3.4%; P = 0.043).

Angiographic complexity differed substantially across groups. The CABG cohort had the most extensive coronary disease, with 3-vessel disease (>70% stenosis) present in 106 patients (71.1%), compared with 73 (16.6%) in the PCI group and 54 (35.8%) in the medical therapy group (P < 0.001). Significant left main disease (>50%) was most prevalent in the CABG cohort (10.1%). The PCI cohort had the greatest CTO-specific lesion complexity: J-CTO score ≥1 in 90.0% compared with 20.5% in the medical therapy group and 42.9% in the CABG group (P < 0.001) and PROGRESS-CTO score ≥1 in 49.9% compared with 11.9% and 22.8%, respectively (P < 0.001). Contralateral collaterals were most frequently observed in the PCI group (45.2%; P < 0.001).

A notable anatomical observation was that 79.5% of medically managed patients had a J-CTO score of 0, indicating that conservative treatment in this group was driven primarily by patient-level clinical risk, particularly renal impairment, cardiogenic shock, and severely reduced ejection fraction, rather than by the absence of technically addressable disease.

The indication for management differed significantly across groups (P < 0.001). In the medical therapy, PCI, and CABG groups, respectively, the principal indication was symptom relief, heart-failure improvement, or LV systolic function improvement in 32.1%, 44.9%, and 48.8%; the setting of NSTEMI in 42.5%, 42.2%, and 62.9%; and staged complete revascularization in 2.1%, 11.1%, and 5.5%.

Procedural outcomes and unadjusted in-hospital events

Among 441 CTO-PCI procedures, the approach was antegrade in 389 (91.3% of those with a recorded approach) and retrograde in 37 (8.7%). Technical success was achieved in 343 (77.8%) and procedural success in 341 (77.3%). The principal reason for an unsuccessful procedure was failure to cross the occlusion (guidewire or balloon/device; n = 62), with calcification, ambiguous proximal cap, poor distal target, tortuosity, and contrast/radiation or safety-related termination as contributing factors. Eight coronary perforations occurred and were managed with coil embolization (n = 1), conservative treatment (n = 4), covered stenting (n = 1), or prolonged balloon inflation (n = 2).

Among patients undergoing CABG, the median number of grafts was 3 (IQR: 2-3), and the CTO target vessel was grafted in 143 (98.6% of the 145 with documented grafting status; 96.0% overall), indicating direct surgical revascularization of the CTO vessel in the large majority of surgical patients.

Unadjusted in-hospital outcomes differed across treatment groups. The median length of stay was longer after CABG (11 days) compared with medical therapy or PCI (3 days for both; P < 0.001). In-hospital MACE occurred in 12 patients (7.9%) in the medical therapy group, 23 (5.2%) in the PCI group, and 20 (13.4%) in the CABG group (P = 0.004). This difference was driven primarily by a higher rate of periprocedural MI in the CABG group (10.7%; P < 0.001). Rates of stroke, acute heart failure, cardiac death, and all-cause death were low and did not differ significantly among groups (Table 2).

Covariate balance after propensity score weighting

Propensity weighting improved covariate balance across groups: the mean absolute SMD decreased from 0.098 to 0.035 for PCI vs medical therapy, and from 0.141 to 0.056 for CABG vs medical therapy, with the mean absolute SMD below 0.06 in both comparisons (prespecified balance threshold SMD <0.10; 6 covariates in the CABG comparison retained residual SMDs between 0.10 and 0.20, none >0.20).

Formal overlap diagnostics supported the validity of the weighted comparisons, with no positivity violations among the 739 patients with complete covariate data (Figure 2).

Adjusted in-hospital outcomes

In the adjusted model, the estimated baseline risk of in-hospital MACE in the medical therapy group was 7.2%. Compared with medical therapy, PCI was associated with a nonsignificant absolute risk reduction in in-hospital MACE of 1.95% (95% CI: −7.55% to +3.63%; P = 0.492), whereas CABG was associated with a nonsignificant absolute risk increase of 4.51% (95% CI: −2.82% to +11.86%; P = 0.228; Table 3). After adjustment for measured baseline differences, no statistically significant difference in short-term in-hospital MACE was detected among treatment strategies.

The median follow-up was 22 months (IQR: 13-37). On unadjusted Kaplan-Meier analysis, freedom from MACCE did not differ significantly among groups (log-rank P = 0.52). Sensitivity analyses for positivity. Because the medical-therapy propensity distribution had an extended right tail corresponding to a high-risk subgroup without revascularized comparators, we tested the robustness of the RMST results to weight truncation and to overlap weighting at both horizons (Supplemental Table 2). Both analyses were nonsignificant for all comparisons; the borderline 36-month PCI-versus-medical difference moved further from significance under overlap weighting, indicating that the long-term findings are not driven by the nonoverlapping high-risk medical subgroup.

A sensitivity analysis accounting for center-level clustering did not change the conclusions (Supplemental Table 3): the adjusted RMST point estimates were unchanged, and all pairwise comparisons remained nonsignificant at both horizons.

Because the composite included any revascularization, we repeated the analysis using a hard composite limited to cardiac death, MI, heart failure, and stroke (Supplemental Table 4). Excluding revascularization did not change the conclusions: no comparison reached statistical significance at either horizon, and the borderline 36-month PCI-versus-medical difference attenuated further to −0.99 months (P = 0.111). Planned staged revascularization was defined at the index admission and was not counted as a follow-up event; the longitudinal revascularization events were therefore clinically driven rather than planned.

Discussion

In this multicenter observational cohort of patients with CTO across the Gulf region, management strategy was strongly influenced by baseline clinical and anatomic characteristics. Patients treated medically had worse renal function, more cardiogenic shock, lower ejection fraction, and despite this higher clinical risk, often had anatomically simple CTOs, including J-CTO score 0 in 79.5%. By contrast, patients referred for CABG had the most extensive multivessel and left main-pattern disease (three-vessel disease in 71.1%), whereas those treated with PCI had the greatest CTO-specific procedural complexity. These differences underscore that the 3 treatment groups were not directly comparable before adjustment and that crude outcome comparisons are highly vulnerable to confounding by indication (Central Illustration).

After adjustment for baseline differences, no strategy showed superior short- or long-term hard cardiovascular outcomes. Treatment selection was driven by patient-level risk and coronary anatomy; revascularization, particularly CABG, was associated with a lower angina burden at follow-up. CABG = coronary artery bypass grafting; CCS = Canadian Cardiovascular Society; CTO = chronic total occlusion; IPTW = inverse probability of treatment weighting; J-CTO = Multicenter CTO Registry of Japan Score; LVEF = left ventricular ejection fraction; MACCE = major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events; MACE = major adverse cardiovascular events; PCI = percutaneous coronary intervention; RMST = restricted mean survival time.

The principal finding of this study is that, after adjustment for measured confounders, neither PCI nor CABG was associated with a statistically significant difference in short-term in-hospital MACE or long-term MACCE-free survival compared with medical therapy. Although CABG was associated with higher unadjusted in-hospital MACE, this appeared to be driven largely by periprocedural MI and was attenuated after adjustment. Although the co-primary 36-month analysis showed a small numerical difference favoring medical therapy over PCI, this did not reach statistical significance (−1.31 months; P = 0.053) and moved further from significance under overlap weighting and after accounting for center clustering, underscoring that it reflects confounding by indication rather than a causal disadvantage of revascularization. These data therefore support a neutral adjusted comparison rather than equivalence among strategies.

At the exploratory 60-month horizon, the small unadjusted numerical advantage of revascularization in 5-year MACCE-free survival was no longer apparent after weighting; at the co-primary 36-month horizon the groups were similar both before and after adjustment. These shifts were small and nonsignificant, consistent with correction for baseline confounding rather than a true treatment effect.

These findings are broadly concordant with the randomized CTO-PCI literature and with contemporary CTO-PCI reviews and consensus frameworks.26,27 In DECISION-CTO, CTO-PCI did not reduce MACE compared with a no-CTO-PCI strategy, although the trial was limited by early termination, substantial crossover, and reduced power for clinical endpoints.9 In EURO-CTO, PCI improved angina frequency and quality of life compared with optimal medical therapy, but MACE were similar between groups.10 Observational meta-analyses have suggested improved outcomes after successful CTO-PCI, but those datasets remain vulnerable to residual confounding and treatment-selection bias.13 The ISCHEMIA trial, although not restricted to CTO, likewise showed no reduction in ischemic events with an initial invasive strategy compared with conservative management in stable coronary disease, a finding that has influenced contemporary revascularization thinking and guideline framing.28, 29, 30 Taken together with the present data, these trials support the view that symptomatic benefit and hard-event benefit may diverge in CTO practice, and that the absence of a clear event reduction should not be interpreted as absence of clinical value in carefully selected symptomatic patients.31

An additional observation from this cohort is that treatment allocation appeared to reflect not only anatomic complexity and overall coronary disease burden, but also the immediate clinical indication and intended therapeutic goal. Indications for intervention differed significantly across groups (P < 0.001), with CABG more often selected in the setting of NSTEMI (62.9% vs 42.2% for PCI and 42.5% for medical therapy), whereas PCI was more commonly used as part of staged complete revascularization (11.1% vs 5.5% and 2.1%, respectively). Symptom relief, heart failure improvement, or LV function improvement remained a common indication across all strategies (48.8%, 44.9%, and 32.1%, respectively). The adjusted angina status at follow-up also differed significantly between CABG and medical therapy, with CCS class I reported in 86.2% of medically managed patients, 91.7% of PCI patients, and 98.0% of CABG patients. Although these findings are descriptive, they support the concept that, in selected CTO patients, the principal benefit of revascularization may relate more to symptom improvement than to reduction in hard cardiovascular events.

From a methodological standpoint, the use of IPTW/IPWRA and RMST strengthens the analysis. The weighted models achieved acceptable covariate balance, and the use of RMST was appropriate once proportional hazards were not tenable for the PCI comparison. Applying the same estimand to the CABG comparison improved interpretability across treatment arms. The co-primary 36-month analysis and the exploratory 60-month analysis were directionally consistent within the range of dense follow-up. However, the confidence intervals around the adjusted estimates were wide, particularly for in-hospital outcomes, indicating limited precision and leaving open the possibility that clinically meaningful differences were not detected.

Study limitations

Residual confounding

The observational registry design introduces inherent confounding by indication. Although weighting reduced the mean absolute SMD to below 0.06 in both comparisons, residual imbalance remained, especially in CABG group. This residual imbalance was addressed by the outcome-regression component of the doubly robust estimator but cannot be fully excluded as a source of confounding. Furthermore, unmeasured confounders, including frailty indices, patient preferences, detailed anatomical unsuitability not captured by J-CTO or PROGRESS-CTO scores, and institutional practice variation, cannot be excluded.

Statistical power

The total number of in-hospital MACE events was insufficient to detect small but clinically meaningful differences between strategies. The resulting CIs span up to 15 percentage points for the CABG comparison, rendering the study compatible with a broad range of treatment effects. No prospective power calculation was performed. The conclusion of no detected difference should not be interpreted as confirming equivalence at any clinically relevant level of precision.

Temporal confounding

The registry spans 2021 to 2023, a period during which CTO-PCI techniques and medical therapy continued to evolve. Although the relatively narrow 3-year window mitigates era-effect concerns compared with longer registries, year of procedure was not included as a covariate in the propensity model and temporal sensitivity analyses were not conducted.

Quality of life and symptomatic outcomes

Follow-up CCS angina class was recorded, but no validated patient-reported outcome or quality-of-life instrument was collected, and a comparable baseline angina grade was unavailable for the majority of patients who presented with acute coronary syndromes; symptom inferences are therefore limited to follow-up angina status.

Specialized registry setting and operator expertise

The high proportion of procedures performed by acknowledged CTO experts may limit generalizability to lower-volume or less specialized CTO programs, where technical success rates and complication profiles may differ materially from those reported here.32

Selection bias and generalizability

Of the 953 screened patients, 212 (22.3%) were excluded, and these exclusions may affect both treatment allocation and the generalizability of our findings. The single largest category was inadequate or lost follow-up, which may be comparatively frequent in the Gulf region, given a substantial mobile and expatriate population that often relocates or repatriates; patients lost to follow-up may differ systematically from those retained, in directions that cannot be determined from our data. Exclusion of patients with severe comorbidity or life expectancy under 1 year and with high bleeding risk precluding dual antiplatelet therapy means that the frailest, highest-risk patients, who in practice are frequently directed toward conservative management, are under-represented; our medical-therapy group, therefore, likely does not capture the full clinical-risk spectrum of conservatively managed CTO patients. By contrast, exclusion of nontrue CTO and of acute STEMI without a separate CTO lesion was definitional and was intended to ensure an anatomically and clinically homogeneous CTO cohort, enhancing internal validity. Because exclusions were applied at screening, standardized baseline and outcome data were not abstracted for excluded patients, and a formal comparison of excluded vs included patients was, therefore, not possible. The net effect of these exclusions on the magnitude or direction of the adjusted treatment comparisons cannot be quantified, and our findings should be generalized with appropriate caution, particularly to populations with a higher comorbidity or bleeding-risk burden, or with more complete longitudinal follow-up.

Short-term follow-up

The 22-month median may preferentially capture early procedural and surgical hazards and may be insufficient to capture the later accrual of benefit, particularly after CABG.

Baseline viability testing

Formal viability imaging was not routinely performed, reflecting real-world practice, and where stress testing demonstrated ischemia in the CTO territory; this was considered supportive evidence. Therefore, viability-guided selection cannot be reliably assessed.

The registry comprised only 7 centers, which limits the precision of cluster-based estimates; the center-level sensitivity analysis should be regarded as supportive rather than definitive, and unmeasured center- and operator-level variation cannot be fully excluded.

Conclusions

In this multicenter propensity-weighted registry of patients with CTO from the Gulf region, medical therapy, PCI, and CABG were applied to patient populations with substantially different clinical and angiographic risk profiles. After adjustment for measured baseline confounders using doubly robust IPWRA and IPTW weighting, neither PCI nor CABG was associated with a statistically significant difference in short-term in-hospital MACE or in long-term MACCE-free survival at the co-primary 36-month horizon compared with medical therapy (60-month estimates, exploratory, were likewise nonsignificant). The adjusted RMST estimates trended numerically in favor of medical therapy, but CIs were wide, and estimates beyond approximately 36 months should be interpreted cautiously, given the 22-month median follow-up.

These results support the following interpretation: in real-world CTO practice, treatment selection is driven primarily by patient-level risk and lesion anatomical complexity, and after accounting for these factors, no strategy demonstrates a detectable advantage over another in terms of hard cardiovascular outcomes. Revascularization, most clearly CABG, was associated with a lower angina burden at follow-up after adjustment. However, because a comparable baseline angina grade was unavailable for the predominantly acute presentations and no validated quality-of-life instrument was used, these data should be interpreted as describing angina status at follow-up rather than demonstrating symptom improvement attributable to treatment.

This should not be taken as evidence that revascularization is without benefit in CTO; rather, it underscores that the principal value of CTO revascularization in selected patients may be symptomatic relief rather than event reduction. Randomized trials with systematic quality-of-life follow-up and longer observation periods remain the standard needed to definitively address the role of each treatment strategy in CTO management.

COMPETENCY IN MEDICAL KNOWLEDGE: This multicenter Gulf CTO registry demonstrates that contemporary CTO management remains highly heterogeneous, with treatment selection driven primarily by clinical risk profiles and angiographic complexity rather than by randomized evidence. The finding that medical therapy was preferentially allocated to patients with worse renal function, lower ejection fraction, and more cardiogenic shock, despite anatomically simpler CTOs, underscores a fundamental tension in CTO care: the patients who may most need revascularization are often those at the highest procedural risk. Conversely, PCI was favored for the most technically complex lesions, whereas CABG was reserved for extensive multivessel disease. This pattern reflects appropriate clinical judgment but creates severe confounding that observational methods can only partially address. Importantly, after rigorous adjustment, no strategy demonstrated superiority for hard cardiovascular outcomes, reinforcing that the principal benefit of CTO revascularization may be symptomatic relief, most clearly achieved with CABG, rather than event reduction. For the practicing clinician, these data emphasize that CTO treatment decisions should remain individualized, weighing procedural risk against symptom burden and patient preferences, while acknowledging that the evidence base for event reduction remains inconclusive. The G-CTO registry findings highlight several critical priorities for translational research and clinical practice. First, the neutral adjusted outcomes across strategies underscore the urgent need for adequately powered randomized controlled trials comparing medical therapy, PCI, and CABG in CTO patients, with systematic collection of patient-reported outcomes and quality-of-life measures alongside hard clinical endpoints. Second, the high proportion of medically managed patients with anatomically simple lesions (J-CTO score 0 in 79.5%) who nonetheless had substantial clinical risk suggests that future risk-stratification tools should integrate both patient-level factors and lesion complexity to better guide treatment selection. Third, the wide CIs around adjusted estimates, particularly for in-hospital outcomes, indicate that current registries lack precision to detect moderate treatment effects, necessitating larger collaborative international consortia with standardized data collection and shared analytic frameworks. Fourth, the observation that CABG achieved superior angina relief after adjustment, despite higher periprocedural risk, suggests that the symptom-event trade-off differs meaningfully between revascularization strategies, a distinction that should inform shared decision-making tools.

TRANSLATIONAL OUTLOOK: Finally, the substantial proportion of patients lost to follow-up (10.7%) in this Gulf cohort highlights the need for robust longitudinal data capture mechanisms, including digital health platforms and centralized follow-up registries, to better characterize long-term outcomes across diverse health care systems and patient populations.

Screening novel cathode materials from the Energy-GNoME database using MACE machine learning force field and DFT
Nada Alghamdi, Paolo de Angelis, Pietro Asinari, Eliodoro Chiavazzo

Abstract
The development of new battery materials, particularly novel cathode chemistries, is essential for enabling next generation energy storage technologies. In this work, we set up a screening procedure on the Energy-GNoME database for identifying novel cathode candidates. We use MACE foundational models as a first layer of screening, we assess dynamical stability and estimate the average voltage and gravimetric energy density. Following that, we apply physically motivated reasoning to identify the most promising candidates. Furthermore, we refine the average voltage prediction of selected promising candidates using DFT+U and provide the list of selected materials using this protocol. This work delivers two key outcomes: validation of the foundational MACE models high-throughput screening approach and suggestions for cathode candidates for the development of next-generation batteries. Finally, a fair comparison between the MACE predictions and the readily available figures of merit reported in the Energy GNoME database is demonstrated on the examined materials.

Introduction

Global demand for batteries is increasing rapidly
[
1
,
2
]
.
A key approach to meet the rising demands and to develop more efficient and safer batteries, is to explore new materials for the next generation of batteries
[
1
]
.
To this aim, screening and computational characterization of novel cathode materials have emerged as an essential tool in the search for the next-generation of batteries
[
1
]
.

The exploration of crystalline inorganic materials has been recently transformed by artificial intelligence (AI).
A prominent example is the Graph Networks for Materials Exploration (GNoME)
[
3
]
, an AI-driven approach from Google DeepMind that uses an iterative active learning pipeline with graph neural networks (GNNs) to discover stable crystals
[
3
]
.
While GNoME have identified hundreds of thousands of stable crystalline structures
[
3
]
, a key step that still remains is the assignment to those material of possible technological applications as well as synthesis pathways
[
4
,
5
]
.

To address the former challenge on practical applications, the Energy-GNoME initiative
[
6
,
7
]
has leveraged the concept of cross-domain data bias
[
8
]
to create a database of materials specifically designed for energy applications.
So far, it has identified 21,243 promising cathode materials suitable for lithium and post-lithium (e.g., Na, Mg, K, Ca) battery systems
[
6
]
(with confidence above 50%).
At this stage, however, these candidates are only potentially validated by ML predictions. To progress beyond this pre-screening, a more physics-grounded verification is essential.
Although density functional theory (DFT) remains the gold standard for such validation, its high computational cost presents a major bottleneck for rapid screening dozens of thousands of materials.

To this end, we leverage on MACE foundational models
[
9
]
, which are machine learning force field (MLFF) based on the message passing atomic cluster expansion
[
10
]
trained on large diverse chemistries.
Foundational MLFFs offer a powerful tool for high-throughput screening
[
11
]
.
Several foundational MACE models have emerged based on different datasets.
The earliest models, such as MACE-MP-0a and MACE-MP-0b, were trained exclusively on the Materials Project trajectory (MPtrj) dataset
[
12
]
showed wide-range applicability
[
9
]
.
More recent models have incorporated out-of-equilibrium structures and higher-level functionals.
These include the MACE-OMAT-0 (MACE-OMAT), trained on the large OMat24 dataset
[
13
]
containing over 100 million data points, and the MACE-MATPES-r2SCAN-0 (MACE-r2scan), trained on the MATPES-r2SCAN dataset
[
14
]
.
By leveraging foundational MLFF training on extensive and diverse datasets, these models facilitate rapid property prediction, enabling the efficient identification and filtering of unsuitable candidates
[
11
]
.

Therefore, in this work, we use MACE foundational models for screening the cathode candidates in the Energy-GNoME database
[
6
,
7
]
.
First, we rationalize the validity and accuracy of our approach against experimentally established and characterized cathodes.
Following that, we carry-on the screening on the Energy-GNoME cathode candidates.
Subsequently, the average voltage of the most promising candidates is verified using density functional theory (DFT)
[
15
,
16
]
.
Our work delivers two key outcomes: validation of the foundational MACE models high-throughput screening approach and a list of promising cathode candidates for the development of next-generation batteries that deserve further exploration.

2.1.2
Phonon based dynamical stability

Evaluating phonon dispersions of hypothetical materials is a widely used method in high-throughput calculations to filter candidates that are not stable (e.g.,
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
).
A recent benchmark of foundational MLFF demonstrated very good potential for predicting harmonic phonon properties
[
31
]
. The MACE-MP-0
[
9
]
, predicted dynamical stability with 95% accuracy in predicting stable structures and 73% accuracy for predicting instability
[
31
]
.
However, the phonon dispersions of nearly 10,000 compounds that was used in this study
[
31
,
32
]
, was computed on structures that are also present in the MPTrj dataset
[
12
]
which constituted the training data of the MACE-MP-0
[
9
]
.
In addition, the methodology of ref.
[
31
]
is such that they compute the phonon properties by optimizing the structures with the MLFF on top of the DFT relaxed geometries.
This might lead to overestimation of the accuracy of the MACE-MP-0 in detecting stability.

To this end, we test MACE-OMAT-0 medium model (MACE-OMAT) on the same phonon dataset of Ref.
[
31
]
. MACE-OMAT was trained on the OMat24 dataset
[
13
]
, that contains more than 100 million DFT datapoint in equilibrium and out of equilibrium.
OMat24 is not directly dependent on or built from the MPTrj dataset. Therefore, while we cannot confirm that its 100 million data points are completely distinct from those in the phonon dataset, its independent construction stands as a better alternative.
Moreover, the larger dataset size and the inclusion of out of equilibrium data provides more natural choice for phonon calculations
[
9
]
.

The performance of the MACE-OMAT against the reference
[
31
,
32
]
is shown in Fig.
3
.
Namely, MACE-OMAT achieved a 95% accuracy in predicting stable structures and a 54% accuracy for unstable ones.
This is consistent with the potential energy surface (PES) softening observed in many MLFF that leads to the softening of the phonon modes
[
33
,
14
]
and overestimating the dynamical stability.
Nonetheless, this is acceptable for screening, as MACE-OMAT identifies the stability of structures with accuracy of 88.13%.

Figure 3
:
MACE-OMAT achieved a 95% accuracy in predicting stable structures and a 54% accuracy for unstable ones. Based on the phonon database in Ref.
[
31
]
, which contains 7,360 stable and 1,585 unstable structures.

Remarkably, large supercells containing hundreds to thousands of atoms were used (details in the supplementary data), phonons were found with the finite displacement method, where each calculation was taking minutes using a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.
The DFT phonons calculations, whether using supercells or density functional perturbation theory method, is much more demanding and computationally expensive
[
34
]
.
The trade off between computational time and accuracy for using MACE-OMAT is exceptional for screening applications.

To further refine our candidate list, we employ three physically-motivated filters: number of elements in the candidate, the prevalence of the space groups, and element composition classification.

Lastly, for the element composition of the candidates, we adopt the battery materials classification of Borah et al.
[
40

Method

3.1
Convex hull and voltage profile

The formation energy
E_{f}
of an intercalated cathode
A_{x}MO_{2}
material can be found as
[
41
]
:

E_{\mathrm{f}}\left(\mathrm{A}_{x}\mathrm{MO}_{2}\right)=E\left(\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{x}}\mathrm{MO}_{2}\right)-xE\left(\mathrm{AMO}_{2}\right)-(1-x)E\left(\mathrm{MO}_{2}\right),

(1)

where
0\leq x\leq 1
,
A
is the working ion (e.g., Li, Na) and M denotes the transition metal in the oxide host.
The energies E of each phase are either MACE predicted energy or DFT computed energy. This quantity describes the relative stability of the intermediate phase
A_{x}MO_{2}
, with respect to the fully intercalated
AMO_{2}
and de-intercalated
MO_{2}
phases, where thermodynamically stable phases lie on the convex hull of the composition-energy (
x
vs.
E_{f}
) diagram.

The voltage profile
V\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)
is computed as a piece-wise approximation as the following
[
41
]
:

V\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=-\frac{E\left(\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{x}_{1}}\mathrm{MO}_{2}\right)-E\left(\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{x}_{2}}\mathrm{MO}_{2}\right)-\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)E(\mathrm{A})}{\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)}

(2)

where
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{x}_{1}}\mathrm{MO}_{2}
and
\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{x}_{2}}\mathrm{MO}_{2}
are thermodynamically stable phases with
x_{1}>x_{2}
. At low temperatures, entropic contributions are negligible, and the energies
E
can be equated to the internal energy.
To estimate the average voltage for a given cathode, it is enough to compute the energy of the fully intercalated phase, fully de-intercalated phase, and the chemical potential of the working ion
[
41
]
:

V_{average}=-E\left(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{MO}_{2}\right)+E\left(\mathrm{MO}_{2}\right)-E(\mathrm{A})

(3)

3.2
Phonon-based dynamical stability

The dynamical stability of a crystal structure can be assessed through its vibrational properties.
To this end, the potential energy is expanded as a Taylor series around the equilibrium atomic positions
[
42
]
:

V\left(\left\{\mathbf{R}^{0}+\Delta\mathbf{R}\right\}\right)\approx V\left(\left\{\mathbf{R}^{0}\right\}\right)+\left.\sum_{J}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\mathbf{R}_{I}}\right|_{\mathbf{R}^{0}}\Delta\mathbf{R}_{I}+\left.\frac{1}{2}\sum_{I,J}\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial\mathbf{R}_{I}\partial\mathbf{R}_{J}}\right|_{\mathbf{R}^{0}}\Delta\mathbf{R}_{I}\Delta\mathbf{R}+\mathcal{O}\left(\mathbf{R}^{3}\right)

(4)

at equilibrium, the forces acting on each atom vanish, i.e.

\mathbf{F}_{I}\equiv-\frac{\partial V(\mathbf{R})}{\partial\mathbf{R}_{I}}=0,

(5)

The second-order term in the expansion defines the Hessian of the potential energy, also known as the interatomic-force constants matrix:

\Phi_{IJ}=\left.\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial\mathbf{R}_{I}\partial\mathbf{R}_{J}}\right|_{\mathbf{R}^{0}}=-\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{R}_{I}}\mathbf{F}_{J}\right|_{\mathbf{R}^{0}}

(6)

within the harmonic approximation, the equation of motion for lattice vibration leads to the following eigenvalue problem:

\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{q})\ \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\nu}(\mathbf{q})=\omega^{2}_{\nu}(q)\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\nu}(\mathbf{q})

(7)

where
\mathbf{q}
are the phonon wave vectors,
\omega_{\nu}(q)
are the frequencies of the phonon modes, and
\mathbf{D}
is the dynamical matrix, which is constructed by a mass-weighted Fourier transform of the interatomic-forces

D_{I\alpha J\beta}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{I}m_{J}}}\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\Phi_{I\alpha,J\beta}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}},

(8)

A structure is dynamically stable if all phonon frequencies are real and positive, indicating that the system resides at a minimum of the potential energy surface. In contrast, the presence of imaginary phonon modes signals dynamical instability
[
42
,
43
]
.

3.3
Computational framework

As many cathode systems depends on transition metal (TM) elements as electrochemical active elements, whose oxidation states change during charge–discharge cycling, the use of simple semi-local functionals is not an optimal choice
[
44
,
45
]
.
Semi-local DFT functionals suffer from electron self-interaction error (SIE), which becomes particularly dominant in systems with strongly correlated electrons, such as TMs with open
d
-shells
[
46
]
.
The Hubbard correction U, has been shown to mitigate the SIE and improve intercalation-voltage prediction
[
44
,
45
]
.
In addition, meta-GGA functional exhibit reduction of SEI compared to semi-local functionals, and generally achieves better accuracy for TMs systems at a comparable computational cost
[
47
]
.
To this end, we used spin-polarized PBEsol+U for the final evaluation of the average voltage of the most promising cathode candidates, while the non-spin-polarized r
2
SCAN meta-GGA functional was used in the context of our initial rationalization of the validity of our approach against the experimental intercalation voltage profile.

Moreover, for screening, we employed two foundational machine learning force fields models based on the MACE architecture
[
10
]
: MACE-MATPES-r2SCAN-0 model (MACE-r2scan for short), and MACE-OMAT-0 (MACE-OMAT)
[
48
]
.
The MACE-r2scan was trained on DFT data at the r
2
SCAN functional level using the MATPES-r2scan dataset
[
14
]
which includes 387,897 structures both at equilibrium and non-equilibrium.
While the MACE-OMAT was trained using the OMat24 dataset comprised of over 110 million DFT which is sampled from a diverse non-equilibrium data points
[
13
]
.
It relied on the PBE functional with Hubbard U correction for Co, Cr, Fe, Mn, Mo, Ni, V, or W transition metal elements in oxides and fluorides
[
48
,
13
]
.

4
Conclusions

In this work, we started from the predictions on novel cathode materials that are readily available in the Energy GNoME database
[
7
]
and applied a multi-layer screening procedure based on both foundational MACE and DFT simulations.
Furthermore, on the basis of expected stability, typical space groups of experimentally known materials, toxicity, cost and expected synthesis ease we have shortlisted a dozen of novel material candidates spanning Na-ion, Mg-ion, K-ion and Ca-ion chemistry.
It is worth stressing that this study provides a first clear evidence that the readily available figures of merit of the Energy GNoME database, although only based on ML prediction, are comparable to MACE and DFT quality results.
The present study represents the first but key step towards an
in silico
discovery of new cathode materials to be tested experimentally.
Among other things, a key aspect to predict would be the transport properties of the active material in the new material candidates.
Such a screening will be the subject of future studies, where molecular dynamics simulations can be adopted as demonstrated in
[
65
,
66
,
67
]
.

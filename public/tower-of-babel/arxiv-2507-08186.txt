Ratio limits and pressure function for group extensions of Gibbs Markov maps
Jaime Gomez, Dalia Terhesiu

Abstract
. Ratio limit theorems for random walks on (various) groups are known. We obtain a generalization of this type of ratio limit for deterministic walks on certain groups driven by Gibbs Markov maps. In terms of proofs, the main difficulty comes down to the absence of a convolution structure. Also, for (finitely generated) group extensions of Gibbs Markov maps we obtain a characterization of the pressure function without a symmetry assumption.



1.
Introduction

We are interested in an analogue of ratio local limit theorems in the sense of Stone
[
22
]
, Avez
[
4
]
, Gerl
[
10
]
(see also the recent note of Woess
[
23
]
)
for random walks on groups in
a dynamical systems set up. Namely, we are interested in ratio limits for walks on groups driven by Gibbs Markov (GM) maps.

We recall the ratio limit in
[
22
,
4
,
10
]
for random walks on groups as relevant to the generalization we are after.
Let
G
be a group and let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be independent random variables on some probability space

(\Omega,{\mathbb{P}})
taking values in
G
distributed according to a probability measure
\nu
on
G
. That is, given a set
A
in
G
,
{\mathbb{P}}(X_{i}\in A)=\nu(A)
.
Write
S_{n}=X_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot X_{n}
, where
\cdot
is the law of the group.

We first recall the result in
[
22
]
. Assume that
G
is also locally compact abelian (LCA), second countable and generated by a compact neighborhood of the identity. Let
m_{G}
be the Haar measure on
G
. Under the assumption that
\nu
is aperiodic and centered, the limit of quotients of convolutions satisfies

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{(n)}(g_{0}+A)}{\nu^{(n)}(g_{1}+A)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\mathbb{P}}(\{\omega:S_{n}(\omega)\in g_{0}+A\})}{{\mathbb{P}}(\{\omega:S_{n}(\omega)\in g_{1}+A\})}=\frac{m_{G}(A+g_{0})}{m_{G}(A+g_{1})}=1,

for any open and bounded set
A
in
G
with
m_{G}(A)>0
and with

m_{G}(\partial A)=0
, uniformly for
g_{0},g_{1}
in compact sets of
G
.
In terms of different sets in
G
, it is shown in
[
22
]
that given open and bounded sets
E,A
in
G
with
m_{G}(E),m_{G}(A)>0
and with

m_{G}(\partial E)=m_{G}(\partial A)=0
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\mathbb{P}}(\{\omega:S_{n}(\omega)\in E\})}{{\mathbb{P}}(\{\omega:S_{n}(\omega)\in A\})}=\frac{m_{G}(E)}{m_{G}(A)}
.

In the deterministic set up of dynamical systems we cannot speak of a notion similar to the convolution
\nu^{(n)}
in (
1
). Nonetheless, as recalled below and
as customary in the literature of dynamical systems (in particular, GM maps) we have a natural analogue of
{\mathbb{P}}(\{\omega:S_{n}(\omega)\in A\})
.

For an amenable finitely generated group
G
, under a further symmetry assumption on the measure
\nu
, it is shown in
[
4
]
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\mathbb{P}}(\{\omega:S_{n}(\omega)=g\})}{{\mathbb{P}}(\{\omega:S_{n}(\omega)=e\})}=1
for all
g\in G

and for

the identity of

still in the set up of countable discrete groups,
[

obtained the precise limit of

We also refer to the recent note
[
8

for a generalization of Avez’s result in the non-symmetric setting.

We consider group extensions of mixing GM maps
T:X\to X
.
The groups we shall consider will be either discrete countable groups (which we refer to as discrete) or metrizable locally compact uncountable topological groups (which we refer to continuous).
Let
T_{\psi}:X\times G\to X\times G
be the group extension of

by the cocycle
\psi:X\to G
defined via

\displaystyle T_{\psi}(x,g)=(Tx,\psi(x)g).

This map preserves the measure
\hat{\mu}=\mu\otimes m_{G}
, where
m_{G}
denotes the (left) Haar measure on
G
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
x\in X
, the analogue of the i.i.d. product

mentioned before is

(2)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x)=\psi(T^{n-1}(x))\cdot\ldots\cdot\psi(T(x))\psi(x),

where

stands for the law of the group. As customary in the literature,
see Aaronson and Denker
[
2
,
3

, the assumption of aperiodicity and symmetry of the measure
\nu
(in fact, any assumption on
\nu
) in the i.i.d. set up
are phrased in terms of the cocycle
\psi
.

If
G
is continuous,
g\in G
and
E\subseteq G
is a ‘suitable’ bounded set,
we are interested in the limit

(3)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\mu(\{x\in X:\psi_{n+1}(x)\in Eg\})}{\mu(\{x\in X:\psi_{n}(x)\in Eg\})}.

If
G
is discrete and
g\in G
,
we are interested in the limit

(4)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\mu(\{x\in X:\psi_{n+1}(x)=g\})}{\mu(\{x\in X:\psi_{n}(x)=g\})}.

Under certain abstract assumptions listed in Section
2
, we obtain that the limit in (
3
) and (
4
) is exactly
1
: see Theorem
2.1

in Section
2
. The proof of this result is provided in Section

.
Under the same abstract assumptions, the type of arguments used in understanding the limits in (
3
) (or (
4
)), will allow us to also obtain an analogous form of (
1
) (or its discrete version) in the current deterministic dynamical set up. This is the content of Theorem

and its proof is provided in Section

.

One feature of the abstract assumptions (listed in
Section
2
)
is that they are checkable.
In Section
5
we verify them for certain groups: finite groups and certain LCA groups
(finitely generated or compactly generated).
The main tool for the verification of the abstract assumptions for the considered LCA is harmonic analysis and as such, transfer operators along with their perturbed version (by the cocycle
\psi
).

Although in this work, we resume to checking the abstract assumptions listed in Section
2
for abelian and for finite groups, we believe that these assumptions can be checked for much larger classes of groups, in particular non abelian and non compact groups. This requires a heavy use of unitary representation theory for non abelian groups as in
[
5

9
]
and we postpone this to a future work.

In Section
6
of the current work we combine the strategy of Stadlbauer
[
21
]
and the non-symmetric Kesten criterion of Dougall and Sharp
[

to obtain an expression for the pressure function of amenable, finitely generated group extensions of GM maps.
Under a certain symmetry condition on the cocycle

shows that the pressure function of

is equal to the pressure function of

Without any symmetry assumption,
[

gives a non-symmetric Kesten criteria for amenable finitely generated groups, which relates the spectral radius of the non-symmetric random walk to the spectral radius of the random walk on the abelianized group.
Combining these two results we obtain an expression for the pressure function for

We recall that this type of result was first obtained by Dougall and Sharp in
[

for

(and more generally for group extensions of Anosov flows).

2.
Statement of the main results

We start with a brief recall of the background of Gibbs-Markov (GM) maps.

2.1.
GM maps

Roughly speaking, GM maps are infinite branch uniformly expanding maps with bounded distortion and big images.
Throughout we assume that
(X,T,\mu,\alpha)
is an almost onto
GM,
T
is
\mu
-mixing and with full branches.

We recall the definitions we shall use in the sequel in more detail.
Let
(X,\mu)
be a probability space, and let
T:X\to X
be a topologically mixing ergodic measure-preserving transformation, piecewise continuous w.r.t. a countable partition
\alpha=\{a\}
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we refer to
\alpha_{n}
as the refined partition of the
n
-th iterate of
T
.
Define
s(x,x^{\prime})
to be the least integer
n\geq 0
such that
T^{n}x
and
T^{n}x^{\prime}
lie in distinct partition elements.
Since
T
is expanding,
s(x,x^{\prime})=\infty
if and only if
x=x^{\prime}
, one obtains that
d_{\beta}(x,x^{\prime})=\beta^{s(x,x^{\prime})}

for
\beta\in(0,1)
is a metric.

Take the potential
\varphi=\log\frac{d\mu}{d\mu\circ T}:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
We say that
T
is a GM map if the following hold w.r.t. the countable partition
\alpha
:

•

For each
a\in\alpha
,
T(a)
is a union of partition elements and
T|_{a}:a\to T(a)
is a measurable bijection for each
j\geq 1

such that
T(a)
is the union of elements of the partition
\bmod\mu
;

•

\inf_{a\in\alpha}\mu(T(a))>0
. This is usually referred to as the big image property (BIP).

•

There are constants
C>0
,
\beta\in(0,1)
such that

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x^{\prime})|\leq Cd_{\beta}(x,x^{\prime})
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in a
and
a\in\alpha
,
j\geq 1
.

The map
T
has full branches if
T(a)=X
for each
a\in\alpha
.
The invariant measure
\mu
is known
to have the Gibbs property. That is, there exists
C>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
a\in\alpha_{n}
,
x\in a
,

(5)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\mu(a)\leq e^{\varphi_{n}(x)}\leq C\mu(a).

See, for instance,
[
1
, Chapter 4]
and
[
2
]

for a more detailed exposition on Gibbs-Markov maps.

\frac{\mu^{n-k}(g_{1})}{\mu^{n}(e)}\to 1
, as

, as

Item (b) follows similarly, with the use of Lemma

5.1.
Verifying (
(D):
) for finite groups

Recall that
m_{G}
is the left Haar measure on the group
G
. If
G
is finite, then
m_{G}
can be normalized
to a probability measure, namely
m_{G}(g)=\frac{1}{\#G}
for all
g\in G
.
In this case, (
(D):
) is a direct consequence of mixing of the skew-product

T_{\psi}(x,g)=(T(x),g\psi(x))
,
namely

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(b)\mu(\{x\in X:\psi_{n}(x)=g^{-1}\})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int 1_{b\times G}\cdot 1_{X\times\{g^{-1}\}}\circ T_{\psi}^{n}\,d\hat{\mu}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mu}(b\times G)\hat{\mu}(X\times\{g^{-1}\}),

where the final equality follows by
T_{\psi}
-invariance of
\hat{\mu}=\mu\otimes m_{G}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
G
is finite, then the skew-product
T_{\psi}:X\times G\to X\times G
is exponentially mixing w.r.t.
\mu\otimes m_{G}
.

Proof.

Take the unit element
e\in G
and consider the first return map
T_{\psi}^{\tau}(x,e)
to
X\times\{e\}
, where
\tau(x)=\min\{j>0:\psi_{j}(x)=e\}
.
This is a Gibbs-Markov map with distortion constant
C/(1-\lambda)
, where
C
is the Gibbs (distortion) constant of
T
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
is such that
the expansion
\frac{d\mu\circ T_{\psi}}{d\mu}\geq 1/\lambda
, i.e., the potential
\varphi=\frac{d\mu}{d\mu\circ T}\leq\log\lambda
.

The return map
T^{\tau}
is defined
\mu
-a.e., which follows from the stronger statement that the return time
\tau
has exponential tails:
There are
\lambda^{\prime}\in(0,1)
and
C^{\prime}>0
such that

(13)

\mu(\{x\in X:\tau(x)>n\})\leq C^{\prime}{\lambda^{\prime}}^{n}\qquad\text{ for all }n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Then the mixing of
T_{\psi}
follows directly from Young’s result
[
24
, Theorem 2.II(b)]

on the mixing of the Young tower.

It remains to prove (
13
). Since
N:=\#G<\infty
, for every
g\in G

there is
n<N
such that
\mu(R_{g})>0
for
R_{g}=\{x\in X:\psi_{n}(x)=g\}
.
The distortion bound of
T_{\psi}^{m}
(uniform over
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
) gives that
for all
g\in G
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
a\in\alpha_{m}
such that
\psi_{m}(a)=g^{-1}
,
we have

\frac{\mu(\{x\in a:T^{m}(x)\in R_{g}\})}{\mu(a)}\geq\frac{1}{C}\mu(R_{g}).

Note that if
x\in a
(so
\psi_{m}(x)=g^{-1}
) and
T_{\psi}^{m}(x)\in R_{g}
, then
\psi_{m+n}(x)=g\cdot g^{-1}=e
, i.e.,
\tau(x)\leq m+N
.

Let
u=\min\{\frac{1}{C}\mu(R_{g}):g\in G\}
. Then, for all
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\mu(\{x\in X:\tau(x)>r+N\})\leq(1-u)\mu(\{x\in X:\tau(x)\geq r\}).

That is, for each
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the set of points
x
with
\tau(x)\geq r
is so that the proportion of points
with
\tau(x)\geq r+N
is less than
(1-u)
.
Thus, (
13
) holds with
\lambda^{\prime}=(1-u)^{1/N}
.
∎

6.
Pressure function for finitely generated group extensions of GM maps

For a countable group
G
,
Theorem
2.1
says that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(\{x\in X:\psi_{n+1}(x)=g\})}{\mu(\{x\in X:\psi_{n}(x)=g\})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu^{n+1}(e)}{\mu^{n}(e)}=1
when (
(D):
) holds.
It is known that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu^{n+1}(e)}{\mu^{n}(e)}=1
implies that

(29)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mu^{n}(e))^{1/n}=1.

Establishing (
29
) via a ratio limit theorem, it is rather a difficult path.
Via a different strategy,
[
21
]
and
[
12
]
established (
29
), which allows for relating the pressure function for
T
with the pressure function of
T_{\psi}
.

The results in
[
21
]
and
[
12
]
are based on a symmetry assumption for the Gibbs Markov map
T:X\to X
, the potential
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
and the cocycle
\psi:X\to G
.
Roughly speaking,
this symmetry condition on
T
and
\psi
(compare with (
22
)) states that there exists a map
S:\alpha\to\alpha
such that
S^{2}=\mbox{id}_{\alpha}
and
\psi(S(x))=\psi(x)^{-1}
,

, in such a way it allows to obtain a Gibbs condition relating the elements in some cylinder
a\in\alpha
with the elements in its involution
S(a)\in\alpha
.
With these assumptions,
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
relates the thermodynamic formalism of a symmetric Gibbs Markov map and its extension by
\psi
, with the help of a sequence of i.i.d. random walks over the group
G
.

Inspired by this, we present an expression for
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mu^{n}(e))^{1/n}
in a general setting, without the assumption of a symmetry condition for either
T
or
\psi
.
For this, we define measures
m_{n}
over the group
G
, in a similar way to
[
21
]
, such that
(G,m_{n})
gives an i.i.d. random walk for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In
[
21
]
, the symmetry assumption for
T
and
\psi
allowed to deduce that the measures
m_{n}
defined in
[
21
]
are symmetric, i.e.,
m_{n}(g)=m_{n}(g^{-1})
for every
g\in G
.
The symmetry on these measures is crucial to apply Kesten’s criteria (see
[
15
]
), which is a key part of the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
.
Instead of using Kesten’s criteria, we use some of the results presented in
[
8
]
, which allow to handle the general case without assuming symmetry for
T
or
\psi
.

In this section, given a probability measure
\nu
over
G
,
\nu^{*k}
denotes the
k
-th convolution of the measure
\nu
and
\lambda(G,\nu)
the spectral radius of
\nu
, i.e.,
\lambda(G,\nu)=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\nu^{*k}(e))^{1/k}
.
For a Gibbs-Markov map
(X,T,\mu,\alpha)
, with a potential
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
, recall that the
Gurevič pressure
is given by

\displaystyle P_{\varphi}(T)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{a}^{n},\mbox{ where }Z_{a}^{n}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T^{n}(x)=x\\
x\in a\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi_{n}(x)},

for some

If
(X,T,\mu,\alpha)
is transitive and
\varphi
is a locally Hölder continuous potential, then

does not depend on

, and the
\limsup
can be replaced by

(see, for instance,
[
20
]
).

Next, we present some facts related to the abelianization of a finitely generated group
G
presented in the works
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
.
Let
G
be a finitely generated group.
The group
G^{\rm{ab}}=G/[G,G]
is an abelian finitely generated group, where
[G,G]
denotes the commutator subgroup of
G
, that is,
[G,G]
is the subgroup of
G
generated by the elements of the form
[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
, for
a,b\in G
.
Therefore, the
free torsion abelianization of
G
is given by
\overline{G}:=G^{\rm{ab}}/G^{\rm{ab}}_{T}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, for some
k\geq 0
, where
G^{\rm{ab}}_{T}
denotes the torsion part of
G^{\rm{ab}}
.
Denote by
\pi:G\to\overline{G}
the canonical homomorphism between
G
and
\overline{G}
.
We refer to
{T}_{\psi}^{\rm{ab}}:X\times\overline{G}\to X\times\overline{G}
as the extension of

by the group
\overline{G}
with cocycle
\overline{\psi}=\pi\circ\psi
, i.e.,
{T}_{\psi}^{\rm{ab}}(x,m)=(T,m+\overline{\psi}(x)),x\in X,m\in\overline{G}.

The Gurevič pressures of
T_{\psi}
and

are defined in a similar way as before,

\displaystyle P_{\varphi}(T_{\psi})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{a,g}^{n},\mbox{ where }Z^{n}_{a,g}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T^{n}(x)=x,x\in a\\
\psi_{n}(x)=g\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi_{n}(x)},\mbox{ for }a\in\alpha,g\in G

\displaystyle P_{\varphi}(T_{\psi}^{\rm ab})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{a,m}^{n},\mbox{ where }Z^{n}_{a,m}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T^{n}(x)=x,x\in a\\
\psi_{n}(x)=m\end{subarray}}e^{\varphi_{n}(x)},\mbox{ for }a\in\alpha,m\in\overline{G}.

It is clear that

, which implies that

.

The main result of this section reads as follows:

Proposition 6.1

Assume that
T_{\psi}
is a topologically transitive extension of
(X,T,\alpha)

If

is an amenable finitely generated group, then

\displaystyle P_{\varphi}(T_{\psi})=P_{\varphi}(T_{\psi}^{\rm ab}).

Moreover, there exists a subsequence

such that

P_{\varphi}(T_{\psi})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{i}}\log\lambda(G,m_{n_{i}})+P_{\varphi}(T).

Theorem 5.1 in
[

is phrased as:

6.1.
Almost superadditive sequences

The following lemma is folklore.
However, we add a proof for completeness of this document.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(a_{n})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and
C>0
be so that

(31)

\displaystyle a_{n+m}\geq a_{n}+a_{m}+\log C,\mbox{ for all }n,m\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n}
exists (although it may be infinite).

Proof.

We proceed via (the proof of) Fekete’s lemma, but we need to adjust this slightly due to presence of
C>0
.
The rest of the proof is standard, repeating the steps in Fekete’s lemma.
Write
n=mp+q
, with
q<p
, and note that

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}}{n}\geq\frac{a_{mp+q}}{mp+q}\geq\frac{ma_{p}+a_{q}-(m+1)\log C^{*}}{mp+q}\geq\frac{m}{m+1}\frac{a_{p}}{p}+\frac{a_{q}}{(m+1)p}+\frac{\log C}{p}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary.
If
\limsup_{r}\frac{a_{r}}{r}<\infty
, then
choose
p
so that

\frac{a_{p}}{p}\geq\lim\sup_{r}\frac{a_{r}}{r}-\varepsilon
and
\frac{\log C}{p}>-\varepsilon
.
Thus,

\frac{a_{n}}{n}\geq\frac{m}{m+1}\lim\sup_{r}\frac{a_{r}}{r}+\frac{a_{q}}{(m+1)p}-2\varepsilon.

Let
m\to\infty
.
Then
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n}\geq\lim\sup_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n}
, and the conclusion follows.

Now, if
\limsup_{r}\frac{a_{r}}{r}=\infty
, then choose
p
such that

\frac{a_{p}}{p}\geq 1/\varepsilon
and
\frac{\log C}{p}>-\varepsilon
.
Therefore,

\frac{a_{n}}{n}\geq\frac{m}{m+1}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{a_{q}}{(m+1)p}-\varepsilon.

Let
m\to\infty
. Then
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n}\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon
, and since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n}=\infty
.
∎

(\mu^{n}(e))

\displaystyle\mu^{n+m}(e)\sim_{D^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{n+m}=c_{n}\cap T^{-n}a_{m}\in\alpha_{n+m}\\

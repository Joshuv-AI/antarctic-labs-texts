Bounds for Hypergraph Universality
Peter Allen, Julia Böttcher, Jasmin Katz

Abstract
A graph Γ \Gamma is said to be universal for a class of graphs ℋ {\mathcal{H}} if Γ \Gamma contains a copy of every H ∈ ℋ H\in{\mathcal{H}} as a subgraph. The number of edges required for a host graph Γ \Gamma to be universal for the class of D D -degenerate graphs on n n vertices has been shown to be O ⁡ ( n 2 − 1 / D ​ ( log ⁡ n ) 2 / D ​ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) 5 ) O(n^{2-1/D}(\log n)^{2/D}(\log\log n)^{5}) . We generalise this result to r r -uniform hypergraphs, showing the following. Given D , r ≥ 2 D,r\geq 2 and n n sufficiently large, there exists a constant C = C ⁡ ( D , r ) C=C(D,r) such that there exists a graph with at most C ​ n r − 1 / D ​ ( log ⁡ n ) 2 / D ​ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) 2 ​ r + 1 Cn^{r-1/D}(\log n)^{2/D}(\log\log n)^{2r+1} edges which is universal for the class of D D -degenerate r r -uniform hypergraphs on n n vertices. This is tight up to the polylogarithmic term.

Introduction

The quest for universal graphs and hypergraphs is a central topic in extremal combinatorics. A hypergraph
\Gamma
is
universal
for a class of hypergraphs
{\mathcal{H}}
if
\Gamma
contains a copy of every
H\in{\mathcal{H}}
as a (not necessarily induced) subgraph. In this case we also say that
\Gamma
is
{\mathcal{H}}
-universal
. Given a class of hypergraphs
{\mathcal{H}}
, it is natural to ask what is the minimum number of edges required for a hypergraph to be
{\mathcal{H}}
-universal.

More is known about universality for graphs than hypergraphs. Universality has been well-studied for many classes of graphs, including trees
[
8
,
9
,
14
,
17
]
and planar graphs
[
6
,
7
,
11
]
. For the class of
n
-vertex graphs with maximum degree
\Delta
, denoted
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(n)
, an explicit construction of a
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(n)
-universal graph with
O(n^{2-2/\Delta})
edges was given by Alon and Capalbo
[
4
]
. It can be shown via a counting argument that
\Omega(n^{2-2/\Delta})
edges are required to be
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}(n)
-universal, and so this result is tight. Another widely studied class of graphs are degenerate graphs. A graph is
D
-degenerate
if there exists an ordering of the vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
such that every vertex
v_{i}
has at most
D
neighbours in the set
\{v_{1},\dots v_{i}\}
. We denote the class of
D
-degenerate graphs by
{\mathcal{H}}(n,D)
. The existence of a
{\mathcal{H}}(n,D)
-universal graph with
O(n^{2-1/D}(\log^{2/D}n)(\log\log n)^{5})
was shown by Allen, Böttcher and Liebenau
[
1
]
for all
D>1
, and this is tight up to the logarithmic term. In the case
D=1
(which corresponds to trees), Chung and Graham
[
9
]
gave a construction of a
{\mathcal{H}}(n,1)
-universal graph on
O(n\log n)
edges, which is tight up to the multiplicative constant
[
8
]
. There have also been recent improvements in the multiplicative constant in the lower and upper bounds for this class of graphs
[
14
,
17
]
. Alon,
Dodson, Jackson, McCarty, Nenadov, and Southern
[
5
]

derived related results concerning the class of
graphs of bounded density, which is more general than the class of graph with bounded degeneracy.

In comparison with the graph case, relatively little is known about universality for hypergraphs. However, this problem has been recieving more attention in recent years. For instance, let
\mathcal{H}^{(r)}_{\Delta}(n)
denote the class of
n
vertex
r
-graphs with maximum degree
\Delta
, where an
r
-graph
is an
r
-uniform hypergraph. A counting argument shows that the number of edges required for an
r
-graph to be
\mathcal{H}^{(r)}_{\Delta}(n)
-universal is
\Omega(n^{r-r/\Delta})
. Hetterich, Parczyk and Person
[
15
]
gave constructions of
\mathcal{H}^{(r)}_{\Delta}(n)
-universal
r
-graphs with
O(n^{r-r/\Delta})
edges in the case that
r
is even, in the case that both
r
is odd and
\Delta=2
, and in the case
r|\Delta
; in all other cases they also provided constructions, albeit with a polynomial error term. Nenadov
[
19
]
recently improved on this, giving a construction of a graph with
\Theta(n^{r-r/\Delta}(\log^{r/\Delta}n))
edges which is
\mathcal{H}^{(r)}_{\Delta}(n)
-universal for all
r
and
\Delta
, thus reducing the error term to poly-logarithmic.

In this paper we focus on universality for
D
-degenerate
r
-graphs. In analogy to the graph case, we say an
r
-graph
G
is
D
-degenerate
if there exists an ordering of the vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of
G
such that every vertex
v_{i}
has at most
D
edges contained entirely in the set
\{v_{1},\dots v_{i}\}
. Equivalently, every induced
r
-subgraph has a vertex of degree at most
D
. We denote the set of
D
-degenerate
r
-graphs on
n
vertices with maximum degree
\Delta
by
\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}^{(r)}(n,D)
, and remove the subscript if the maximum degree is unbounded. We also note that there exists other notions of degeneracy for hypergraphs, such as skeletal degeneracy, which has been shown to be useful for Turán- and Ramsey-type problems
[
13
]
. The
skeletal degeneracy
of a hypergraph
G
is simply the degeneracy of the graph obtained from
G
by replacing each edge by a clique.
Observe that if an
r
-graph has skeletal degeneracy
D
, then it has degeneracy at most
D/(r-1)
. Hence, the degeneracy definition we work with here is more general.

Noting that a
D
-degenerate graph may have density arbitrarily close to
D
, Nenadov
[
19
]
showed that there exists an
\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}^{(r)}(n,D)
-universal
r
-graph with at most
Cn^{r-1/D}\log^{1/D}n
edges. We give a result for
D
-degenerate
r
-graphs of unbounded degree, generalising the result of Allen, Böttcher and Liebenau
[
1
]
, which gives the same bound for
r=2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
D,r\geq 2
and
n
sufficiently large, there exists a constant
C=C(D,r)
such that there exists a graph with at most

Cn^{r-1/D}(\log n)^{2/D}(\log\log n)^{2r+1}

edges which is
{\mathcal{H}}^{(r)}(n,d)
-universal.

Theorem
1.1
is tight up to the polylogarithmic factor, as shown by the following result. In fact, we prove the following slightly stronger statement: if
\Gamma
contains a copy of every connected
n
-vertex
D
-degenerate graph with maximum degree bounded by
rD+1
, then
e(\Gamma)\geq\tfrac{1}{100r^{2}D}n^{r-1/D}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
r\geq 2
,
D\geq 1
and let
n
be sufficiently large. Suppose
\Gamma
is a
{\mathcal{H}}^{(r)}_{rD+1}(n,D)
-universal
r
-graph. Then
e(\Gamma)\geq\frac{1}{100r^{2}D}n^{r-1/D}
.

1.1
Related Results

Instead of solely asking for the minimum number of edges required to be universal for some class of hypergraphs, if
{\mathcal{H}}(n)
represents a class of hypergraphs on
n
vertices, we can further ask what is the minimum number of edges required for an
{\mathcal{H}}(n)
-universal hypergraph on
n
vertices – such a graph is called
spanning-universal
. In general, this question is more difficult to answer, and other than the results for trees
[
9
,
14
,
17
]
and planar graphs
[
7
]
, none of the results stated above are for spanning-universal
r
-graphs. In fact, the best upper bound for the number of edges in a
{\mathcal{H}}^{(r)}_{\Delta}(n)
spanning-universal
r
-graph is
O(r^{r-2/\Delta}\ln^{4/\Delta}n)
for
r=2

[
3
]
and
O(n^{r-r/(2\Delta)}(\log^{r/(2\Delta)}n))
for
r\geq 3

[
20
]
. Analogous results for degenrate graphs are not known.

Another active area of research is determining the threshold probability for a random
r
-graph
H^{(r)}(n,p)
to be universal for
\mathcal{H}^{(r)}_{\Delta}(n)
. The
random graph
H^{(r)}(n,p)
is the probability space of all labeled
r
-graphs on
n
vertices, where each set of
r
vertices is chosen as an edge independently with probability
p
. For
r=2
, Alon and Capalbo
[
2
]
showed that for every
\varepsilon>0
the random graph
H^{(2)}(n,p)
is asymptotically almost surely (a.a.s.)
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\Delta}((1-\varepsilon)n)
-universal for
p=O((\log n/n)^{1/\Delta})
. This result was generalised to hypergraphs by Parczyk and Person
[
20
]
, who also strengthened it to a spanning-universal result when they showed that for
p=O((\log n/n)^{1/\Delta})
the random
r
-graph
H^{(r)}(n,p)
is
\mathcal{H}^{(r)}_{\Delta}(n)
-universal. This is the probability at which we expect every set of
\Delta
vertices to have many common neighbours, a useful property in proving universality. However, this bound is not tight. In the
r=2
case, Ferber and Nenadov
[
12
]
have shown that
H^{(2)}(n,p)
is
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{\Delta}(n)
-universal for
p=O(n^{-1/(\Delta-0.5)}\log^{3}n)
, which is the current best known upper bound. The current lower bounds in the
r=2
case are
p=\Omega(n^{2/(\Delta+1)})
for non-spanning universality
[
10
]
, and
p=\Omega(n^{2/(\Delta+1)}\log^{2/(\Delta({\Delta+1}))}n)
for spanning universality
[
16
]
.

When
D\ll\Delta
, it is possible to find tighter bounds on the values of
p
for which the random
r
-graph is universal for the class
\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}^{(r)}(n,D)
than for
\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}^{(r)}(n)
alone. This has mostly been studied in the
r=2
case, for which Ferber and Nenadov
[
12
]
showed that for
p\geq(\log^{3}n/n)^{1/2D}
, the graph
H^{(2)}(n,p)
is
\mathcal{H}_{\Delta}^{(2)}(n,D)
-universal. Note that the maximum degree condition is necessary – it is not sensible to study the threshold probability for
\mathcal{H}^{(r)}(n,D)
-universality, as when we remove the requirement for bounded degree, the class
\mathcal{H}^{(r)}(n,D)
may contain graphs with an
(r-1)
-set in
n-r+1
edges. For any reasonable
p
, the random
r
-graph does not contain any such set of vertices.

Preliminaries

All logarithms are base 2, unless the base is specified. For a set
S
, we let
\binom{S}{k}
denote the set of
k
-element subsets of
S
. In this notation, an
r
-graph
H=(V,E)
is a hypergraph where each edge
s\in E
is an element of
\binom{V}{r}
.
Let
H=(V,E)
be an
r
-graph. Given an
r
-graph
H
and subsets
X_{1},\dots,X_{r}
of its vertices, we write
E(X_{1},\dots,X_{r})
for the set of edges of
H
with one vertex in each of
X_{1},\dots,X_{r}
.

For some
v\in V(H)
, we denote the
link
of
v
by
L(v)=\{X\in\binom{V}{r-1}:X\cup v\in E(H)\}
. Suppose we have an ordering of vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of an
r
-graph
H
. Denote by
L^{-}(v_{i})=L(v_{i})\cap{\binom{\{v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1}\}}{r-1}}
the
back-link
of
v_{i}
, that is, the subset of the link of
v_{i}
consisting of sets containing only vertices that precede
v_{i}
in our ordering of
V(H)
.

By definition, for any
D
-degenerate
r
-graph there exists a
D
-degenerate ordering
of the vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
such that for each
i\in[n]
we have
|L^{-}(v_{i})|\leq D
. Hence, for any
D
-degenerate
r
-graph
H
on
n
vertices, it is clear that
H
has less than
Dn
edges. This implies the following observation.

Observation 2.1
.

Let
H
be a
D
-degenerate
r
-graph, and let
X
be the set of vertices in
H
of degree at least
k
. We have
\frac{|X|k}{r}\leq|E(H)|\leq Dn
. Hence
|X|\leq\frac{rDn}{k}
.

We will use the following basic inequality.

Theorem 2.2

(Stirling’s Approximation)
.

For integers
n,k
and
e
Euler’s number,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!}{{\sqrt{2\pi n}}(n/e)^{n}}=1,

(1)

which implies

\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{k}\leq\binom{n}{k}\leq\left(\frac{en}{k}\right)^{k}.

(2)

We also use the following concentration inequality.

Theorem 2.3

(Chernoff bound
[
18
]
)
.

Let
X=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where the
X_{i}
are independent Bernoulli random variables. Then

(i)

{\mathbb{P}}\big(X\geq(1+\delta){\mathbb{E}}X\big)\leq\exp\big(-\tfrac{\delta^{2}}{2+\delta}{\mathbb{E}}X\big)
for all
\delta>0
,

(ii)

{\mathbb{P}}\big(X\leq(1-\delta){\mathbb{E}}X\big)\leq\exp\big(-\tfrac{\delta^{2}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}X\big)
for all
0<\delta<1
.

3
Lower Bound

In this short section we give a proof of Theorem
1.2
, which follows from a counting argument.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
s
be the number of connected graphs in
{\mathcal{H}}^{(r)}_{rD+1}(n,D)
on vertex set
\{1,\dots,n\}
where
1,\dots,n
is a
D
-degeneracy ordering. We can construct such a graph as follows. For each
r\leq i\leq n
, we add between
1
and
D
edges between vertex
i
and the preceding vertices, with the restriction that any vertex
v\in[i-1]
can only be included in an edge with vertex
i
if the degree of
v
is currently at most
rD
. By Observation
2.1
, the number of vertices of degree at least
rD+1
in
[i-1]
is at most
\frac{rD}{rD+1}(i-1)
. This implies there are at least
\frac{1}{rD+1}(i-1)
vertices of degree at most
rD
within
[i-1]
. Each edge added at this step will use
r-1
of these vertices, so by (
2
) the number of choices at this step is

\binom{{\frac{1}{rD+1}(i-1)}}{r-1}\geq\left(\frac{i-1}{(r-1)(rD+1)}\right)^{r-1}.

This is at least
\frac{1}{2r^{2}D}i^{r-1}
when
n
is sufficiently large and
i\geq\sqrt{n}
. Thus, the number of choices we can make in the last
n-\sqrt{n}
steps is at least

\displaystyle\prod_{i=\sqrt{n}}^{n}\binom{{i^{r-1}}/{(2r^{2}D)}}{D}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{\eqref{eq:stirling}}}}{\geq}\left(\frac{1}{2r^{2}D^{2}}\right)^{Dn}\left(\frac{n!}{\sqrt{n}!}\right)^{(r-1)D}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{\eqref{eq:stirling_v1}}}}{\geq}10^{-Dn}(rD)^{-2Dn}n^{(r-1)Dn}.

(3)

We conclude that
s\geq 10^{-Dn}(rD)^{-2Dn}n^{(r-1)Dn}
.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a
{\mathcal{H}}^{(r)}_{rD+1}(n,D)
-universal graph. For an upper bound on
s
, we count the subgraphs of
\Gamma
which are connected and have exactly
n
vertices, together with an ordering of their vertices. Any connected graph in
{\mathcal{H}}^{(r)}_{rD+1}(n,D)
has between
n-1
and
Dn
edges, so for each
n-1\leq q\leq Dn
we can pick
q
edges of
\Gamma
. If these edges span exactly
n
vertices, we assign all
n!
possible labelings to these vertices. The number of labeled graphs we construct in this way is at most

\sum_{q={1}}^{Dn}\binom{e(\Gamma)}{{q}}n!\leq Dn\binom{e(\Gamma)}{{Dn}}n!\overset{\mbox{\tiny{\eqref{eq:stirling}}}}{\leq}10^{Dn}(Dn)^{-Dn}e(\Gamma)^{Dn}n^{n},

where we use
e(\Gamma)\geq 2Dn
. As
\Gamma
is
{\mathcal{H}}^{(r)}_{rD+1}(n,D)
-universal, this is an upper bound on
s
. Using (
3
) gives

10^{-Dn}(rD)^{-2Dn}n^{(r-1)Dn}\leq 10^{Dn}(Dn)^{-Dn}e(\Gamma)^{Dn}n^{n}.

Rearranging and taking roots gives the result.
∎

4
Random Block Model and Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof of Theorem
1.1
generalises the random block model of Allen, Böttcher and Liebenau
[
1
]
. That is, we will define an
r
-graph
\Gamma
on
\Theta(n)
vertices, whose vertices are split into
\Theta(\log\log n)

blocks
with sizes growing from
n^{1-1/D}
up to
n
, and with edges placed between and in blocks randomly with probability depending on the size of the blocks they lie between.

We will then show that any
D
-degenerate graph can be embedded in
\Gamma
, putting the vertices of highest degree in the smallest block down to constant degree in the largest block.

Definition 4.1

(Random block model,
\Gamma(r,n,D)
)
.

Given any natural numbers
r,D,n\geq 2
, we define
N
to be the smallest integer such that
n^{D^{1-N}}\leq 3^{D^{2}}
. For each
0\leq k\leq N
, we define the variable

\Delta_{k}:=\begin{cases}n^{D^{-k}}&\text{for $0<k\leq N$}\\
Dn&\text{if $k=0$.}\\
\end{cases}

The vertex set of
\Gamma:=\Gamma(r,n,D)
is the disjoint union
W=W_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup W_{N}
, where each
W_{k}
is called a
block
and has order

|W_{k}|=100\cdot 3^{D}rn/\Delta_{k}.

For any subset
s\subseteq W
, we define an
intersection pattern

\pi\in\{0,\ldots,|s|\}^{N}
, where for each
j\in[N]
,
\pi_{j}(s)=|s\cap W_{j}|
. We define

p^{*}:=2^{(r-1)}(2(r-1)D)^{1/D}(\log n)^{2/D}(\log\log n)^{r+1}/\Delta_{1}.

For each
s\in\binom{W}{r}
, define

p_{s}:=\min\bigg\{1,p^{*}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{i}^{{\pi_{i}(s)}}\bigg\}.

The edge set
E(\Gamma)
is then defined by letting each set
s\in\binom{W}{r}
be an edge of our hypergraph independently with probability
p_{s}
.

We further partition each block
W_{k}
into
sub-blocks

W_{k,1}\ldots W_{k,\log n}
, where
W_{k,1}=\frac{1}{2}|W_{k}|
and for
j\geq 2
,
|W_{k,j}|\geq\frac{|W_{k}|}{2\log n}
.

The meaning of
\Delta_{k}
is the following: when we embed a
D
-degenerate graph to
\Gamma
, we will embed vertices of degree between
\Delta_{k}
and
\Delta_{k-1}
to the block
W_{k}
.

We now give some properties of this model.

Proposition 4.2

(Properties of the Model)
.

For sufficiently large
n
,
D\geq 2
and for
N,\Delta_{N-1}
as in Definition
4.1
, the following holds.

(a)

\frac{\log\log n}{2\log D}\leq N<\log\log n
and
3^{D}\leq\Delta_{N-1}\leq 3^{D^{2}}
,

(b)

{100rn}\leq|W_{N}|\leq 100\cdot 3^{D-1}rn
,

(c)

\Gamma(r,n,D)
has at most
200\cdot 3^{D}rn
vertices and asymptotically almost surely at most

2(20\cdot 3^{D}r)^{r}(\log\log n)^{2r+1}(\log n)^{2/D}n^{r-1/D}

edges.

Proof.

The first upper bound in Proposition
4.2
(
a
) follows from using
n^{D^{1-(N-1)}}>3^{D^{2}}
to obtain
N<\log_{D}(\log_{3}(n))<\log\log n
. The other inequalities can be similarly calculated from the definition of
N
. This then implies (
b
) as
|W_{N}|=\frac{3^{D}rn}{\Delta_{N-1}^{1/D}}
.

The upper bound on the number of edges given in (
c
) can be computed by calculating the expected number of edges within and between the blocks. Fix some
r
-tuple
S=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})
, where the
i_{\ell}
are chosen from
[N]
. For each
k\in[N]
, let
m_{S}(k)
denote the multiplicity of
k
in
S
. We then have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(|E(W_{i_{1}},\dots,W_{i_{r}})|)

\displaystyle\leq\min\bigg\{1,p^{*}\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}\Delta_{i_{\ell}}\bigg\}\cdot\Bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in[N]\end{subarray}}|W_{k}|^{m_{S}(k)}\Bigg)\,.

Here the inequality accounts for the cases where some of the
W_{i_{j}}
are the same. Hence for each
r
-tuple
S=(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(|E(W_{i_{1}},\dots,W_{i_{r}})|)

\displaystyle\leq p^{*}\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}\Delta_{i_{\ell}}\cdot 100\cdot 3^{D}rn/\Delta_{i_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=p^{*}100\cdot 3^{rD}r^{r}n^{r}

\displaystyle\leq(20\cdot 3^{D}r)^{r}(\log\log n)^{r+1}(\log n)^{2/D}n^{r-1/D}.

Summing over all
N^{r}\leq(\log\log n)^{r}

r
-tuples gives

{\mathbb{E}}(|E(\Gamma)|\leq(20\cdot 3^{D}r)^{r}(\log\log n)^{2r+1}(\log n)^{2/D}n^{r-1/D}.

We also clearly have
n^{r-r/D}<|E(W_{1})|\leq{\mathbb{E}}(|E(\Gamma)|)
, hence by the Chernoff bound in Theorem
2.3
(
i
) with
\delta=1
,

{\mathbb{P}}\big(|E(\Gamma)|>2\cdot(20\cdot 3^{D}r)^{r}(\log\log n)^{2r+1}(\log n)^{2/D}n^{r-1/D}\big)\leq\exp(-(n^{r-r/D})/3)=o(1).

∎

Informally, our embedding strategy is as follows. We embed the vertices of a
D
-degenerate
H
one by one in degeneracy order into
\Gamma
, at each time
t
choosing to embed the next vertex
v_{t}
into the block
W_{k}
according to the degree of
v_{t}
being between
\Delta_{k}
and
\Delta_{k-1}
, and choosing the first sub-block of
W_{k}
where this is possible without having embedded all vertices of an edge of
H
to a non-edge of
\Gamma
. Formalising, this, we obtain:

Definition 4.3

(Embedding Strategy)
.

Given any
D
-degenerate
r
-graph
H
, let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
be a
D
-degeneracy ordering of
V(H)
, and let
\Gamma
have the properties of Proposition
4.2
. Our embedding strategy is defined by the iterative construction of a
partial embedding
\psi_{t}:\{v_{1},\dots,v_{t}\}\to\Gamma
. We start with
\psi_{0}
, the trivial partial embedding of no vertices into
\Gamma
.

For each
1\leq t\leq n
, we say a vertex
u\in\Gamma
is a
candidate
for
v_{t}
if
\psi_{t-1}(L^{-}(v_{t}))\subseteq L(u)
. We say such a vertex
u\in\Gamma
is an
available candidate
if in addition
u\notin\im(\psi_{t-1})
. Let
k
be minimal such that
\Delta_{k}<\deg(v_{t})
. If there is no such
k
choose
k=N
. Choose
j
minimal such that there is some
u\in W_{k,j}
which is an available candidate for
v_{t}
. We define
\psi_{t}=\psi_{t-1}\cup\{v_{t}\to u\}
. If there is no available candidate for
v_{t}
in
W_{k}
, we say
\psi_{t}
and the subsequent partial embeddings do not exist and that the embedding strategy
fails at time
t
. If the embedding strategy does not fail, then
\psi:=\psi_{n}
gives an embedding of
H
into
\Gamma
.

The requirement for each vertex to be embedded to an available candidate ensures that
\psi
is indeed an embedding. The additional requirement for each vertex

to be embedded to the block

for

further ensures (by Observation

) that each block always contains vertices that are not embedded to.

Given any partial embedding
\psi_{t}
and any vertex

whose back-link is in the domain of

(i.e. is embedded), we define the
embedded back-link of

by
\psi_{t}(L^{-}(v))\subseteq\binom{W}{{r-1}}
. To simplify notation for any subset

we let

denote the set of vertices contained in

.

In our proof that this embedding strategy does not fail, we analyse the multi-set of embedded back-links of all the vertices in some sub-block, rather than just the embedded back-link of a single vertex. This may indeed be a multi-set, as two vertices may share the same back-links and for our proof we need to count with multiplicity. We will consider a multi-set
{\mathcal{B}}
, where each
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
is a subset of
\binom{W}{{r-1}}
. Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be some such multi-set. We denote by

the number of (not necessarily distinct, so counting with multiplicity) sets contained in
{\mathcal{B}}
, and call this the

of
{\mathcal{B}}
. We let

denote the set of vertices contained in the union of all
\bigcup_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}}V(B)
where
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. We now define three key properties a multi-set of the vertices of
\Gamma
should exhibit if they are the embedded back-links of vertices. The first and second properties are obvious consequences of our embedding strategy. For the third, we will give a deterministic condition that
\Gamma=\Gamma(r,n,D)
is likely to satisfy and we will show this condition implies that our embedding strategy also maintains the third property.

Definition 4.4

(Well-behaved multi-set)
.

Given
r,n,D\geq 2
, let
W=\bigcup_{k}W_{k}
and
W_{k}=\bigcup_{j}W_{k,j}
be the vertex set of
\Gamma=\Gamma(r,n,D)
as given by Definition
4.1
. For
1\leq t\leq n,
let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{t}
be a multi-set, where each
B_{i}\subseteq\binom{W}{{r-1}}
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}
is called

if

(WB1)

|B_{i}|\leq D
for all
1\leq i\leq t
,

(WB2)

for all
1\leq k\leq N
and for all

we have
\big|\{i\in[t]:u\in V(B_{i})\}\big|\leq\Delta_{k-1}
, and

(WB3)

for each
1\leq k\leq N
and each
1\leq j\leq\log n
, we have
\big|V(\mathcal{B})\cap W_{k,j}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|W_{k,j}|
.

Our goal is to find that for each
k\in[N]
and
j\in[\log n]
and any well-behaved multi-set
{\mathcal{B}}
, some significant proportion of vertices in
W_{k,j}
have link containing a set from
{\mathcal{B}}
. In terms of back-links, if
{\mathcal{B}}
is the multi-set of embedded back-links of some
x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\in V(H)
, then we are saying that a significant proportion of vertices in
W_{k,j}
are candidates for at least one of
x_{1},\dots,x_{t}
. This is stated precisely in the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
D,r\geq 2
and let
n
be sufficiently large. Then
\Gamma(n,r,D)
as given in Definition
4.1
a.a.s. satisfies the following. For every
t\in[n]
, every well-behaved multi-set
{\mathcal{B}}
containing
t
subsets of
\binom{W}{{r-1}}
, and every
k\in[N]
and

, we have

\big|\big\{u\in W_{k,j}:\exists B\in{\mathcal{B}}\text{ such that }B\subseteq L(u)\big\}\big|\geq\min\Big\{\frac{1}{16},\frac{t}{4}(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{D}n^{D^{1-k}-1}\Big\}|W_{k,j}|.

(4)

The proof of Lemma
4.5
will be deferred to Section

. We now directly show how Lemma

implies Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

Let
\Gamma(n,r,D)

. Then by Proposition

(

2(20\cdot 3^{D}r)^{r}(\log\log n)^{2r+1}(\log n)^{2/D}n^{r-1/D}

of

(5)

Let
H
be a
D
-degenerate graph on

By definition, the embedding failed because there was no available candidate for

W_{k^{*}}
. Let

\displaystyle\geq\min\Big\{\frac{100\cdot 3^{D}rn}{16\Delta_{k}},\frac{100\cdot 3^{D}rn|{\mathcal{B}}|(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{D}\Delta_{k}^{D}}{4n\Delta_{k}}\Big\}

As the embedding strategy failed at time

We proceed with the induction. In the base case we embed the first vertex to some

We now let

\displaystyle L_{k^{\prime},j^{\prime}}<|W_{k^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\cap\im\psi_{t}|\leq L_{k^{\prime},j^{\prime}}+1,\text{ and }

\displaystyle|W_{k,j}\cap\im\psi_{t}|\leq L_{k,j}\text{ for all $(k,j)\in[N]\times[\log n]\setminus\{(k^{\prime},j^{\prime})\}$}.

First, suppose

\displaystyle L_{k,j}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{k,1}}{4\log n}

\displaystyle\text{for all $k\in[N]$ and $j>1$},

\displaystyle|W_{k,1}|

\displaystyle=50\cdot 3^{D}rn/\Delta_{k}

\displaystyle\text{for $k<N$},

(9)

\displaystyle|W_{N,1}|

\displaystyle\geq 50rn

(

\displaystyle|W_{k,j}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|W_{k,1}|}{\log n}

\displaystyle\text{for all $k\in[N]$ and $j\geq 1$}.

(10)

Hence, for

\begin{split}L_{N,1}+1&=n+1<\frac{50}{16}rn\leq\frac{1}{16}|W_{N,1}|,\\[6.0pt]

For

As

Proof of Lemma
4.5

In this section, we prove Lemma
4.5
. We first show that if we have some well-behaved multi-set
{\mathcal{B}}
, then the link of each vertex of the block model contains some
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
with reasonable probability. Lemma
4.5
will then follow via an application of the Chernoff bound to a fixed sub-block and well-behaved multi-sets, followed by a union bound over all possible sub-blocks and well-behaved multi-sets.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
D,r\geq 2
and let
n
be sufficiently large and
t\in[n]
. Let
\Gamma(n,D,r)
be the random block model as in Definition
4.1
. Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a well-behaved multi-set with
|{\mathcal{B}}|=t
. Fix
1\leq k\leq N
and any
v\in W_{k}\setminus V({\mathcal{B}})
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be the event that there is some
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
such that
B\subseteq L(v)
. Then

{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}})\geq\min\{1/2,t(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{D}n^{D^{1-k}-1}\}.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, for each
b\subseteq V(\Gamma)
and
v\in V(\Gamma)
we will slightly abuse notation and write
v\cup b
to denote
\{v\}\cup b
.

If there is some
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
such that
{\mathbb{P}}(B\subseteq L(v))=1
then
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}})=1
and we are done, so we assume
{\mathbb{P}}(B\subseteq L(v))<1
for all
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. We have that for each
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, the probability that
B
is contained in the link of
v
is given by
{\mathbb{P}}(B\subseteq L(v))=\prod_{b\in B}p_{v\cup b}
, where the empty product is equal to one. We further assume that for each
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, we have
p_{v\cup b}<1
for all
b\in B
, as removing the sets with
p_{v\cup b}=1
from
B
does not change the probability
{\mathbb{P}}(B\subseteq L(v))
. This means that when we write
p_{s}
where
s=v\cup b
for some
b\in B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, the minimum in the formula defining this probability as in equation (
4.1
) is
p_{s}=p^{*}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{i}^{{\pi_{i}(s)}}
.

We generalise the intersection pattern
\pi
of a set
s\subseteq[W]
to a
block intersection pattern

\boldsymbol{\omega}(B)
for each
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, given by
\boldsymbol{\omega}(B)\in\{0,\ldots,(r-1)D\}^{N}
, where
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{i}(B)=\sum_{b\in B}\pi_{i}(b)
. We now restrict our attention to the subset
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}
of blocks with a most common intersection pattern,
\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}
. Suppose
|{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}|=t^{\prime}
and let
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
be the event that there is some
B\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
such that
B\subseteq L(v)
. We will show

{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})\geq\min\Big\{\tfrac{1}{2},2^{(r-1)D}{t^{\prime}}(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{rD}n^{D^{1-k}-1}\Big\}.

(12)

To show that this implies the lemma, we give an upper bound on the number of different block intersection patterns defined by the elements of
{\mathcal{B}}
, which will give a lower bound on
t^{\prime}
. Let
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, where
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\}
for some
k\leq D
. To determine its intersection pattern, we need for each
b_{i}
a vector of
r-1
elements of
N
that indicates which
W_{j}
the elements of
b_{i}
are contained in. We can arrange these vectors as the columns of an
(r-1)\times D
matrix, where each entry is either from
[N]
, or empty (if
|B|<D
). Matrices of this form determine all possible intersection patterns, and there are therefore at most
(N+1)^{(r-1)D}\leq(2\log\log n)^{(r-1)D}
possible intersection patterns. As
t^{\prime}
is the most common, we have
t^{\prime}\geq t/(2\log\log n)^{(r-1)D}
and (
12
) implies

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}})\geq{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\min\big\{\tfrac{1}{2},2^{(r-1)D}{t^{\prime}}(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{rD}n^{D^{1-k}-1}\big\}

\displaystyle\geq\min\Big\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{2^{(r-1)D}t}{(2\log\log n)^{(r-1)D}}(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{rD}n^{D^{1-k}-1}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\min\big\{\tfrac{1}{2},t(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{D}n^{D^{1-k}-1}\big\}.

It only remains to prove (
12
). For each
B\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
, let
X_{B}
be the indicator random variable for the event
B\subseteq L(v)
and let
X=\sum_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}}X_{B}
. Then by Chebyshev’s inequality

{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})={\mathbb{P}}(X>0)\geq\frac{({\mathbb{E}}X)^{2}}{{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}}.

(13)

To find a lower bound for the right-hand side of (
13
), we will upper bound
{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}
. To that end, we write

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}=\sum_{B,A\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}}{\mathbb{P}}(X_{B}=1,X_{A}=1)=\sum_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}}\Big({\mathbb{P}}(B\subseteq L(v))\cdot\sum_{A\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)\Big).

(14)

Fix
B\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\cap B=\emptyset\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}(A\subseteq L(v))+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}X+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\cap B\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)

(15)

We now find an upper bound for the expression on the right hand side of (
15
). To this end, for each
A\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
with non-empty intersection with
B
we define a variable
\ell=\ell(B,A)
to represent the maximal index
j\in[N]
such that
V(B\cap A)\cap W_{j}\neq\emptyset
. Recall that each block in
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
has only elements
b
with
p_{v\cup b}<1
, so
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}^{\prime}=0
and
\ell\geq 2
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{A\cap B\neq\emptyset}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=2}^{N}\Bigg(\sum_{A:\ell(B,A)=\ell}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)\Bigg).

By definition of
\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}
, we have that
V(B)
contains at most
\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}_{\ell}
vertices in
W_{\ell}
. By (WB
2
), each of these may be contained in
V(A)
for at most
\Delta_{\ell-1}
sets
A\in{\mathcal{B}}
. This gives at most
\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}_{\ell}\Delta_{\ell-1}
sets
A
such that
V(B\cap A)\cap W_{\ell}\neq\emptyset
. For each
\ell
, we let
A_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{B}}
be the block which maximises the probability
{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)
over all sets
A\in B
with
\ell(B,A)=\ell
. We conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{A\cap B\neq\emptyset}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)=\sum_{\ell=2}^{N}\Bigg(\sum_{A:\ell(B,A)=\ell}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=2}^{N}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}_{\ell}\Delta_{\ell-1}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\ell}\setminus B\subseteq L(v)).

We require our expression to be independent of our choice of

, so we let
A_{*},B_{*}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
be the blocks which maximise
\Delta_{\ell(B,A)-1}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)

and let

\displaystyle\sum_{A\cap B\neq\emptyset}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)

\sum_{A\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A\setminus B\subseteq L(v)\big)\leq{\mathbb{E}}X+(r-1)D\Delta_{\ell_{*}-1}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A_{*}\setminus B_{*}\subseteq L(v)\big).

We now substitute this into (

) to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}}\bigg({\mathbb{P}}(B\subseteq L(v))\cdot\big({\mathbb{E}}X+(r-1)D\Delta_{\ell_{*}-1}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A_{*}\setminus B_{*}\subseteq L(v)\big)\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}X\cdot\big({\mathbb{E}}X+(r-1)D\Delta_{\ell_{*}-1}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A_{*}\setminus B_{*}\subseteq L(v)\big)\big)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}X\cdot\max\big\{2{\mathbb{E}}X,2(r-1)D\Delta_{\ell_{*}-1}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A_{*}\setminus B_{*}\subseteq L(v)\big)\big\}.

If
2{\mathbb{E}}X\geq 2(r-1)D\Delta_{\ell_{*}-1}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A_{*}\setminus B_{*}\subseteq L(v)\big)
, then
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})\geq 1/2

{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})\geq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}X}{2(r-1)D\Delta_{\ell_{*}-1}{\mathbb{P}}\big(A_{*}\setminus B_{*}\subseteq L(v)\big)}.

(16)

As every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
has the same intersection pattern, we can write

{\mathbb{E}}X=\sum_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}}{\mathbb{E}}X_{B}=t^{\prime}\cdot{\mathbb{P}}\big(A_{*}\subseteq L(v)\big)=t^{\prime}\prod_{b\in A_{*}}p_{v\cup b}.

We substitute this into (
16
) and write
{\mathbb{P}}\big(A_{*}\setminus B_{*}\subseteq L(v)\big)=\prod_{b\in A_{*}\setminus B_{*}}p_{v\cup b}
to give

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})\geq\frac{t^{\prime}\prod_{b\in A_{*}}p_{v\cup b}}{2(r-1)D\Delta_{\ell_{*}-1}\prod_{b\in A_{*}\setminus B_{*}}p_{b\cup v}}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{\prime}\prod_{b\in(A_{*}\cap B_{*})}p_{v\cup b}}{2(r-1)D\Delta_{\ell_{*}-1}}.

We now let
b_{*}\in(A_{*}\cap B_{*})
minimise
p_{v\cup b_{*}}
and write

{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})\geq t^{\prime}\frac{p_{v\cup b_{*}}^{D}}{2(r-1)D\Delta_{\ell_{*}-1}}
.
Note that
\pi_{i}(b_{*})\neq 0
for some
i\leq\ell_{*}
by the definition of
\ell_{*}
, and hence
p_{v\cup b}=p^{*}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{i}^{{\pi_{i}(v\cup b_{*})}}\geq p^{*}\Delta_{k}\Delta_{\ell_{*}}
. We have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\ t^{\prime}\frac{(p^{*}\Delta_{k}\Delta_{\ell_{*}})^{D}}{2(r-1)D\Delta_{\ell_{*}-1}}

\displaystyle=t^{\prime}\frac{(p^{*}\Delta_{k})^{D}}{2(r-1)D}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{{(r-1)}D}t^{\prime}n^{D^{1-k}-1}(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{rD},

as required.
∎

We show that this property implies Lemma
4.5
.

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

We calculate the probability that (
4
) does not hold for a fixed
{\mathcal{B}},k
and
j
via an application of the Chernoff bound, Theorem
2.3
, and Lemma
5.1
, followed by a union bound over all choices of
{\mathcal{B}},k
and
j
.

If
k=1
, then for every
u\in W_{k,j}
and

we have
{\mathbb{P}}(B\subseteq L(u))=1
, hence

|\{u\in W_{k,j}:\exists B\in{\mathcal{B}}\text{ such that }B\subseteq L(u)\}|=|W_{k,j}|

and we are done.

Let
k\geq 2
and fix some
j\in[\log n]
and
t\in[n]
and well-behaved set
{\mathcal{B}}
with
|{\mathcal{B}}|=t
. We now argue that we may assume

t(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{D}n^{D^{1-k}-1}<1/2.

(17)

Suppose we prove the lemma under this condition on
t
. If we have some
t\in[n]
such that (
17
) does not hold, then we may choose an integer
t^{\prime}<t
such that
1/4<t^{\prime}(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{D}n^{D^{1-k}-1}<1/2
, and arbitrarily choose a subset
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{B}}
with
|{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}|=t^{\prime}
. Then by (
4
) applied to
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
,

|\{u\in W_{k,j}:\exists B\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{B}}\text{ such that }B\subseteq L(u)\}|\geq\frac{1}{16}|W_{k,j}|

and we are done. We proceed with the assumption that
t
satisfies (
17
).

Let
U=W_{k,j}\setminus V({\mathcal{B}})
be the set of vertices of
W_{k,j}
disjoint from
{\mathcal{B}}
. For each

{\mathcal{E}}_{u}
be the the event that there exists some

such that

. By Lemma

, we can write

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|\{u\in U:\exists B\in{\mathcal{B}}\text{ such that }B\subseteq L(u)\}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U}{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}}_{u})

\displaystyle\overset{\mbox{\tiny{\eqref{eq:t_cond}}}}{\geq}t(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{D}n^{D^{1-k}-1}|U|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t}{\log n}(\log n)^{2}(\log\log n)^{D}n^{D^{1-k}-1}3^{D}rn^{1-D^{-k}}

\displaystyle>t(\log n)(\log\log n)^{D}n^{D^{1-k}-D^{-k}},

6
Concluding Remarks

We prove bounds on the number of edges required in a hypergraph that is universal for the class
{\mathcal{H}}^{(r)}(n,D)
of
D
-degenerate graphs on
n
vertices for
D>1
that are tight up to a logarithmic factor.
It would be interesting to determine if it is possible to remove this logarithmic factor. Similarly, it would be interesting to obtain analogous results for spanning universality.

We note that our upper-bound result (Theorem
1.1
) does not apply to
1
-degenerate
r
-graphs. However, we believe with some minor adjustments our construction one could obtain an analogous bound for
D=1
. It is not clear, if the additional logarithmic term in such a construction is needed in the case that
r>2
as
the upper bound constructions given for the graph case in
[
8
,
17
]
are not immediately generalisable to hypergraphs.

Another interesting direction would be to consider universality results for hypergraph of bounded density, generalising the results of
[
5
]
.

Theory of Steady States for Lindblad Equations beyond Time-Independence: Classification, Uniqueness and Symmetry
Hironobu Yoshida, Ryusuke Hamazaki

Abstract
We present a rigorous and comprehensive classification of the asymptotic behavior of time-quasiperiodic Gorini-Kossakowski-Sudarshan-Lindblad (GKSL) equations under the assumption of Hermitian jump operators. Our main contributions are twofold: first, we establish a criterion for the uniqueness of steady states. The criterion is formulated in terms of the algebra generated by the GKSL generators and provides a necessary and sufficient condition when the generators are analytic functions of time. We demonstrate the utility of our criterion through prototypical examples, including quantum many-body spin chains. Second, we extend the concept of strong symmetry for time-dependent GKSL equations by introducing two distinct forms, strong symmetry in the Schrödinger picture and that in the interaction picture, and completely classify the asymptotic dynamics with them. More concretely, we rigorously uncover that the strong symmetry in the interaction picture is responsible for non-trivial time-dependent steady states, such as coherent oscillations, whereas that in the Schrödinger picture controls the existence of time-independent steady states. This classification not only encompasses established mechanisms underlying non-trivial oscillatory steady states, such as strong dynamical symmetry and Floquet dynamical symmetry, but also reveals symmetry-predicted, time-dependent asymptotic dynamics in a novel class of open quantum systems. Our framework thus provides a rigorous foundation for controlling dissipative quantum systems in a time-dependent manner.

I.1
Summary of results

Here, we summarize the main contributions of this study (see also Fig.
1
).

I.1.1
Algebraic necessary and sufficient condition for the uniqueness of steady states

We present a general rigorous criterion for the uniqueness of steady states in GKSL equations with time-quasiperiodic generators (including time-independent and time-periodic ones), under the assumption of Hermitian jump operators (Theorem
6
in Sec.
IV.4
).
The GKSL equation is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}(\rho_{t})

\displaystyle=-i[H_{t},\rho_{t}]+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(L_{m,t}\rho_{t}L_{m,t}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{(L_{m,t})^{2},\rho_{t}\right\}\right),

(1)

where
t
denotes time,
H_{t}
is the Hamiltonian, and
L_{m,t}
are the jump operators. The superoperator
\mathcal{L}_{t}
is called the Liouvillian, which we mainly assume to be time-quasiperiodic (see Condition
3
for the definition).

We first show the condition for the uniqueness by introducing
the following algebra
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{t}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\langle\,\mathbb{I},\{\operatorname{ad}^{n}_{t}(L_{m,t})\}_{m=1,\ldots,M;n=0,1,\ldots}\rangle,

(2)

where
\operatorname{ad}_{t}
is the adjoint operation defined as

\operatorname{ad}_{t}(A):=i[H_{t},A]+\partial_{t}A.

(3)

If
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{t}
coincides with the full operator algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
for some
t=t_{0}
, then every state relaxes to the completely mixed state
\mathbb{I}/d
for quasiperiodic Liouvillians. Note that this is a non-trivial generalization of the time-independent case, where the uniqueness of steady states is characterized by Eq. (
16
); indeed, Eq. (
2
) includes the time-derivative factor absent in (
16
). When the GKSL generators are analytic at all times, the criterion provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the uniqueness of steady states (see Fig.
1
(a)).

We illustrate the criterion’s applicability through examples involving two-level systems and a quantum spin chain, for which the uniqueness of the steady states cannot be proven by the existing methods in Refs.
56
;
55
, which do not use information from the Hamiltonian part of the dynamics.

We remark that, for the periodic GKSL equation, integrating over one period of it leads to a time-independent quantum channel, and the problem of the uniqueness of the steady state reduces to the problem of the uniqueness of the fixed point of the channel
88
;
12
. However, such an integration procedure is generally difficult to perform, and thus, methods for determining uniqueness directly from the generator of the GKSL equation are practically useful. Furthermore, the integration procedure is not applicable to the quasiperiodic GKSL equation, which highlights the fundamental advantage of our method.

I.1.2
Generalization of the strong symmetry for time-dependent GKSL equations

For time-independent GKSL equations, the strong symmetry is defined as a symmetry that commutes with both the Hamiltonian and all the jump operators that describe dissipation due to coupling to the environment. Mathematically, strong symmetry is characterized by the commutant algebra. Non-trivial strong symmetry gives rise to conserved quantities, which lead to degeneracy of the steady states. Conversely, for time-independent GKSL equations, when the jump operators are Hermitian, the steady state is unique in the absence of non-trivial strong symmetry (see Sec.
III
).

In Sec.
IV.1
, we point out that it is non-trivial to generalize strong symmetry to time-dependent GKSL equations while retaining similar properties. To fill the gap, we identify distinct strong symmetries characterized by two commutant algebras,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}

\displaystyle=\{O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid[H_{t},O]=[L_{m,t},O]=0\ \forall m,t\},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}

\displaystyle=\{O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid[\tilde{L}_{m,t},O]=0\ \forall m,t\},

(5)

where

\displaystyle U_{t}=\mathcal{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}H_{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}},\ \tilde{L}_{m,t}=U_{t}^{\dagger}L_{m,t}U_{t}.

(6)

We refer to
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
as the strong symmetry in the Schrödinger picture, and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
as the strong symmetry in the interaction picture. They satisfy the inclusion relation
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
. As we see below, this framework is useful for classifying steady states when the GKSL generator is quasiperiodic in time (which encompasses both time-independent and time-periodic dynamics as special cases). For example, we can prove that there exist time-dependent steady states if and only if
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
(Theorem
9
in Sec.
V.2
).

I.1.3
Classification of the steady-state structure in terms of the strong symmetry

We rigorously elucidate that the steady-state structure of time-quasiperiodic GKSL equations is characterized by two types of strong symmetries
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
(Theorems
8
and
9
in Sec.
V
). Since
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
holds, the following possibilities arise:
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
or
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
, and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\emptyset
or
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
. Figure
1
(b) summarizes the possible steady states for each case in the sense of Definition
1
. We also provide concrete examples for each of the cases.

The top-left panel [class (i)] shows the case where
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\emptyset
, which is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
. We prove in Theorem
2
that the steady state is unique if and only if this condition is satisfied, which is a non-trivial generalization of the time-independent case. Note that the assumption of time-quasiperiodicity is not merely a technical one; in non-quasiperiodic cases, there are counterexamples of our theorem (Example
4
in Sec.
IV.3
).

The bottom-left panel [class (ii)] illustrates the case where
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\emptyset
. In this case, there are multiple time-independent steady states, whereas there are no time-dependent steady states.

The top-right panel [class (iv)] shows the case where
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
. In this case, there must be a time-dependent steady state, but there must not be a time-independent steady state other than the maximally mixed state. Notably, this case is unique to time-dependent GKSL equations, because for time-independent generators, coherently oscillating modes are always accompanied by multiple time-independent steady states
88
.
Furthermore, we also provide more non-trivial dynamics belonging to this class, analyzing quasiperiodically driven many-body systems.

Finally, the bottom-right panel [class (iii)] illustrates the case where
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
. In this situation, there exist multiple steady states, both time-independent and time-dependent. Coherent oscillations within time-independent GKSL equations are classified here. It is known that strong dynamical symmetry gives rise to such oscillations
11
;
18
;
9
, and one can verify that this symmetry ensures
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
(see Sec.
V.3.1
). Therefore, strong dynamical symmetry can be reinterpreted within the framework of our classification.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
We begin with defining how to classify the steady-state structure of time-dependent GKSL equations with Hermitian jump operators in Sec.
II
.
After the brief review of the theory of the uniqueness in the case of time-independent GKSL equations in Sec.
III
, we provide a criterion for the unique steady states for time-quasiperiodic GKSL equations in Sec.
IV
. First, we prove that the steady state is unique if and only if
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}|c\in\mathbb{C}\}
(Theorem
2
). Then, we rephrase the condition in the language of the GKSL generators
\{H_{t},L_{m,t}\}
in the Schrödinger picture (Theorem
6
), whose practical advantage is demonstrated with concrete examples.
In Sec.
V
, we prove two theorems that justify the classification of the steady states given in Sec.
II
from the two distinct strong symmetries, with providing concrete applications. Specifically, Theorem
8
ensures the absence of multiple time-independent steady states under
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}|c\in\mathbb{C}\}
and Theorem
9
states that there are time-dependent steady states if and only if
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
. Finally, Sec.
VI
presents a conclusion and outlook. All detailed mathematical proofs and calculations can be found in the Appendices.

II
Classification of steady states for time-dependent Liouvillians with Hermitian jump operators

Throughout the manuscript, we focus on open quantum systems in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with
\mathrm{dim}[\mathcal{H}]=d
, whose time evolution is governed by the GKSL equation in the Schrödinger picture of the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}(\rho_{t})

\displaystyle=-i[H_{t},\rho_{t}]+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(L_{m,t}\rho_{t}L_{m,t}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{(L_{m,t})^{2},\rho_{t}\right\}\right).

(7)

Here,
t
denotes time,
H_{t}
is the Hamiltonian, and
L_{m,t}
are the jump operators that act on
\mathcal{H}
. The superoperator
\mathcal{L}_{t}
is called the Liouvillian. We assume that all
L_{m,t}
are Hermitian.
Examples described by this equation include a quantum system driven by an external field in the presence of dephasing, as well as a time-dependent quantum system subjected to classical white noise
13
;
46
;
89
;
66
;
62
;
16
;
93
. In particular, in the latter situation, one can naturally engineer time-dependent jump operators by coupling white noise to a time-dependent external field
17
;
74
.

In this section, we propose a classification of long-time steady states.
For this purpose, we define steady states
\rho^{*}_{t}
, which can be time-dependent, as those satisfying

\lim_{t\to\infty}(\rho_{t}-\rho^{*}_{t})=0

(8)

for some initial condition
\rho_{0}
.
Again,
\rho^{*}_{t}=\mathbb{I}/d
is always a time-independent steady state by taking
\rho_{0}=\mathbb{I}/d
, due to the Hermiticity of
L_{m}
. Therefore, the asymptotic relaxation of the system can be classified into the following four scenarios (see Fig.
1
):

Definition 1
.

We say that the GKSL equation has

(i)

a unique steady state if

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{t}=\mathbb{I}/d

(9)

for any initial condition
\rho_{0}
.

(ii)

multiple time-independent steady states without time-dependent ones if every initial state relaxes to a time-independent state, and there exists
\rho^{*}\neq\mathbb{I}/d
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{t}=\rho^{*},

(10)

for some initial condition.

(iii)

multiple time-independent and time-dependent steady states if there exist time-independent
\rho^{*}\neq\mathbb{I}/d
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{t}=\rho^{*},

(11)

for some initial condition and time-dependent
\rho^{*}_{t}
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{t}=\rho^{*}_{t},

(12)

for some initial condition, where
\rho^{*}_{t}
does not converge to any time-independent density matrix as
t\to\infty
.

(iv)

time-dependent steady states without non-trivial time-independent ones if the conditions in classes (i), (ii), or (iii) are not satisfied.

In the following sections, we discuss rigorous criteria to determine which class a GKSL dynamics belongs to from the information of
\mathcal{L}_{t}
.
In Secs.
III
and
IV
we mainly focus on the uniqueness of steady states, i.e., the condition for class (i).
In Sec.
V
, we further provide criteria to ensure the other classes.

III
Review: criteria on the uniqueness for time-independent generators

In this section, before discussing time-dependent dynamics, we provide a brief review of steady states of the GKSL equation with time-independent generators, where
H_{t}=H
and
L_{m,t}=L_{m}
are satisfied.
In this case, the degeneracy of the steady state
\rho^{*}
, which is a density matrix given by an eigenoperator of
\mathcal{L}

with eigenvalue
0
, is determined by symmetry of systems. When
L_{m}
is Hermitian, the following relation holds
88
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(O)=0

\displaystyle\iff[H,O]=[L_{m},O]=0.

(13)

The operators
O
that satisfy the right-hand side are called the strong symmetry
10
;
1
.
If we write the identity operator as
\mathbb{I}
, it always commutes with
H
and
L_{m}
and is regarded as a (trivial) strong symmetry. Then, Eq. (
13
) indicates
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{I})=0
, i.e., the maximally mixed state
\mathbb{I}/d
is always the steady state.

Let us write the set of strong symmetries as

\mathcal{C}=\{O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid[H,O]=[L_{m},O]=0\ \forall m\}.

(14)

Here,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the algebra generated by all the operators on
\mathcal{H}
. From Eq. (
13
),
\operatorname{Ker}{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{C}
, and thus the steady state can be obtained by taking a positive semidefinite element of
\mathcal{C}
.
In particular, the following relation can be used to prove the uniqueness of the steady states:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}\mathcal{L}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{C}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}.

(15)

In other words, the uniqueness of the maximally mixed steady state (left-hand side) is equivalent to the fact that the strong symmetry is trivial, i.e., all elements of
\mathcal{C}
are proportional to the identity operator (right-hand side).

Next, let us rephrase the uniqueness condition (
15
) in terms of the algebraic structure of the Liouvillian, which is often more practically useful to verify than the criterion by strong symmetry. Let
\mathcal{A}\>(\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
be a subalgebra generated by the addition, multiplication, and scalar multiplication of
\{\mathbb{I},H,\{L_{m}\}\}
, which we denote as

\mathcal{A}=\langle\mathbb{I},H,\{L_{m}\}\rangle,

(16)

where
\{L_{m}\}
describes a set composed of
L_{1},\cdots,L_{M}
.
Then,
\mathcal{C}
can be seen as the commutant of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e., the set of all the operators that commute with all the elements of
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, because
\mathcal{A}
is closed under taking adjoints, von Neumann’s bicommutant theorem
60
;
61
;
44
tells us that
\mathcal{A}
is the commutant of
\mathcal{C}
. In particular, the following relation holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}\iff\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),

(17)

which can be demonstrated as follows. If
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, then any element of
\mathcal{C}
must commute with all operators on
\mathcal{H}
. This implies that
\mathcal{C}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
, since the only operators that commute with all operators on a Hilbert space are scalar multiples of the identity. Conversely, if

\mathcal{C}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
, then
\mathcal{A}
must be the set of all operators that commute with
\mathbb{I}
, which is simply
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

By summarizing (
15
) and (
17
), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}\mathcal{L}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{C}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}

(18)

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

(19)

Example 1
.

For example, consider a time-independent GKSL equation on
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
given by:

H=\frac{\omega}{2}\sigma^{x},\,L=\sqrt{\kappa}\sigma^{z},

(20)

where
\omega,\kappa>0
are constants and
\sigma^{\mu}

(\mu=x,y,z)
are Pauli operators.
Then,
\mathbb{I}
,
\sigma^{x}
and
\sigma^{z}
generate all the operators in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
(for example,
\sigma^{y}=i\sigma^{x}\sigma^{z}
). Thus
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{C}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
. Therefore, the steady state is unique and given by
\mathbb{I}/2
.

Example 2
.

Next, we consider a GKSL equation given by:

H=\frac{\omega}{2}\sigma^{z},\,L=\sqrt{\kappa}\sigma^{z},

(21)

where
\omega,\kappa>0
are constants and
\sigma^{\mu}

(\mu=x,y,z)
are Pauli operators.
Then
\sigma^{z}
commutes with both
H
and
L
, and we have
\mathcal{C}=\{\alpha\mathbb{I}+\beta\sigma^{z}\mid\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}\}
. Thus
\operatorname{Ker}\mathcal{L}=\{\alpha\mathbb{I}+\beta\sigma^{z}\mid\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}\}
. Since the density matrix is trace-one and positive semidefinite, steady states can be written as
\rho^{*}=(\mathbb{I}+a\sigma^{z})/2
, where
-1\leq a\leq 1
is a constant determined by the initial state
\rho_{0}
.

While the above examples respectively correspond to class (i) and class (ii) in Sec.
II
, we here mention the other possibilities as well.
First, if
\mathcal{L}
has a non-zero purely imaginary eigenvalue, it is called oscillating coherence
1
. In such a case,
\rho_{t}
might not converge to a time-independent density matrix as
t\to\infty
. In our definition, such a case is classified in (iii) in Definition
1
, and we say that
\rho_{t}
converges to a time-dependent steady state
\rho^{*}_{t}
. Next, class (iv) in Definition
1
is excluded for the time-independent GKSL equation, because if a time-independent
\rho^{*}
that satisfies
\mathcal{L}(\rho^{*})=0
is unique, then there is no oscillating coherence
88
.

As we have seen, in the time‑independent case, the steady state is characterized by the spectrum of the Liouvillian. However, when the Liouvillian is time‑dependent, analyzing the spectrum at each instant is not sufficient; as we will discuss below, one must carefully analyze the convergence to steady states.

IV
Criteria for unique steady states for time-dependent Liouvillians

In this section, we rigorously identify the criteria of uniqueness of steady states, i.e., class (i) in our classification.
For this purpose, we first discuss that the naive extension of the strong symmetry for time-independent cases, which we call the strong symmetry in the Schrödinger picture, fails to characterize the uniqueness.
Then, by introducing the strong symmetry in the interaction picture, we prove that the trivial strong symmetry provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the uniqueness by focusing on time-quasiperiodic GKSL equations.
Moreover, we present a criterion for the uniqueness with respect to the algebraic structure of the GKSL generators, which is practically easier to verify than the criterion by the strong symmetry.
Finally, we demonstrate the usefulness of our results by concrete examples.

IV.1
Insufficiency of naive generalization for time-dependent generators

To judge whether a given GKSL equation has a unique steady state, one naive approach is to consider the algebra defined through the jump operators and Hamiltonian at an instantaneous time,

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{t}=\langle\mathbb{I},H_{t},\{L_{m,t}\}\rangle,

(22)

and its commutant

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{t}=\{O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid[H_{t},O]=[L_{m,t},O]=0\ \forall m\},

(23)

in analogy with Eqs. (
16
) and (
14
) in the time-independent case.
Then, as in the discussion around Eq. (
18
), it holds that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}\mathcal{L}_{t}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{t}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}

(24)

\displaystyle\iff\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{t}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

(25)

Given this fact, it would be natural to define
the strong symmetry in the Schrödinger picture
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{t}

(26)

\displaystyle=\{O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid[H_{t},O]=[L_{m,t},O]=0\ \forall m,t\}.

(27)

If
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
, the steady state is not unique. This is because we can construct a
time-independent
steady state satisfying
\mathcal{L}_{t}({\rho}^{*})=0
for all
t
with
\rho^{*}=\mathbb{I}/d+\epsilon O\neq\mathbb{I}/d
using the non-trivial traceless operator
O
common to all
t
in Eq. (
23
).
Here,
\epsilon
is a sufficiently small variable such that
\rho^{*}
becomes positive semidefinite.
This raises a question whether the inverse is true, i.e., if
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
ensures the unique steady state.

Remarkably, even when
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{t}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
(equivalent to
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{t}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
) for all
t
, which is a stronger condition than
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
, we find that the steady states might not be unique.
This is due to the possibility of
time-dependent
ones with
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{L}_{t}({\rho}_{t}^{*})\neq 0
; namely, we cannot rule out the possibility of class (iv) in our classification.

To see this, we consider the following example:

Example 3
.

Consider the GKSL equation on
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
defined by:

H_{t}=\frac{\omega}{2}\sigma^{z},\,L_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}[\sigma^{x}\cos(\omega t)+\sigma^{y}\sin(\omega t)],

(28)

where
\omega,\kappa>0
are constants and
\sigma^{\mu}

(\mu=x,y,z)
are Pauli operators.
In this case,
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{t}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{t}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
for all
t
(and thus
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
), yet the steady states are not unique and given by:

\rho^{*}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&ae^{-i\omega t}\\
ae^{i\omega t}&1\\
\end{pmatrix}

(29)

where
-1\leq a\leq 1
is a constant determined by the initial state
\rho_{0}
.

This clearly demonstrates that the use of the algebras in (
22
)
and (
23
), which seem naive extensions of the time-independent case, is actually not satisfactory in discussing the unique steady state in class (i) for time-dependent cases.

IV.2
Interaction picture and strong symmetry

To see why the above type of degeneracy appears, we consider the rotating frame

\displaystyle U_{t}=\mathcal{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}H_{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}},\,\tilde{\rho}_{t}=U_{t}^{\dagger}\rho_{t}U_{t},\ \tilde{L}_{m,t}=U_{t}^{\dagger}L_{m,t}U_{t},

(30)

Then, the GKSL equation (
7
) reads

\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{\rho}_{t}}{dt}=\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{t}(\tilde{\rho}_{t})=\sum_{m}\left(\tilde{L}_{m,t}\tilde{\rho}_{t}\tilde{L}_{m,t}-\frac{1}{2}\left\{(\tilde{L}_{m,t})^{2},\tilde{\rho}_{t}\right\}\right),

(31)

which is called the GKSL equation in the interaction picture. In this representation, the model (
28
) can be described by the time-independent generator

\tilde{L}_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}\sigma^{x},

which commutes with
\sigma^{x}
itself.
Then,
\tilde{{\rho}}_{t}^{*}=\mathbb{I}/2+a\sigma^{x}/2
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{t}
for all
-1\leq a\leq 1
.
This leads to the time-dependent steady state
\rho_{t}^{*}=U_{t}\tilde{\rho}_{t}U_{t}^{\dagger}
described in (
29
).

Motivated by this observation, we newly introduce a strong symmetry distinct from
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
, which we call

the strong symmetry in the interaction picture
, characterized by

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid[\tilde{L}_{m,t},O]=0\ \forall m,t\}.

(32)

One immediate consequence of the non-trivial strong symmetry in the interaction picture,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\neq\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
, is that it
gives a sufficient condition for multiple steady states (see proof of Theorem
2
(
2
.
\to

1
.) in Sec.
IV.3
).
In other words,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
is a necessary condition for the unique steady state.

The strong symmetry in the interaction picture
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
is weaker than that in the Schrödinger picture
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
, i.e., there is an inclusion relation
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
. As we see in Sec.
V.2
, if and only if
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
, there exists a time-dependent steady state. Example
3
satisfies this condition, because
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{\alpha\mathbb{I}+\beta\sigma^{x}\mid\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}\}
. As we will rigorously justify later, this corresponds to class (iv) in our classification.

Interestingly, even for time-independent Liouvillians, these two symmetries can be different. We will see that this is also related to coherent and oscillatory evolution protected by dynamical strong symmetry
11
, leading to class (iii) in our classification.

IV.3
Necessary and sufficient condition for unique steady states for time-periodic and quasiperiodic Liouvillians

While we have seen that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
gives a necessary condition for the unique steady state, it is highly non-trivial that it gives a sufficient condition.
For general time-dependent Liouvillians, this is indeed not true. To see this, let us consider the following time-dependent GKSL equation:

Example 4

(Exponentially decaying dephasing)
.

Consider the time-dependent GKSL equation with

H=0,L_{1,t}=\sigma^{x}e^{-t},L_{2,t}=\sigma^{y}e^{-t}.

(33)

Then,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
, but the steady state is not unique. To see this, we write the initial state as

\rho_{0}=\mathbb{I}/2+c_{x}\sigma^{x}+c_{y}\sigma^{y}+c_{z}\sigma^{z},

(34)

where
c_{x},c_{y},c_{z}
are real constants. By setting

\langle\sigma^{\mu}\rangle_{t}=\operatorname{Tr}[\sigma^{\mu}\rho_{t}]\ (\mu=x,y,z),

(35)

Then, they obey

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\sigma^{\nu}\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=-2e^{-2t}\langle\sigma^{\nu}\rangle_{t}\ (\nu=x,y),

(36)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\sigma^{z}\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=-4e^{-2t}\langle\sigma^{z}\rangle_{t}.

(37)

By solving this equation, we have

\displaystyle\langle\sigma^{\nu}\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=c_{\nu}e^{e^{-2t}-1}\ (\nu=x,y),

(38)

\displaystyle\langle\sigma^{z}\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=c_{z}e^{2(e^{-2t}-1)}.

(39)

Therefore,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{t}=\mathbb{I}/2+e^{-1}c_{x}\sigma^{x}+e^{-1}c_{y}\sigma^{y}+e^{-2}c_{z}\sigma^{z},

(40)

which depends on the initial state.

This example illustrates that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
does not guarantee the uniqueness of the steady state for general time-dependent Liouvillians. Still, by imposing an additional condition, it can be proved that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the unique steady state, i.e., class (i) in our classification.

IV.4
Useful criterion for the uniqueness via algebra of GKSL generators

While Theorem
2
provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the uniqueness of steady states, one needs to calculate
\tilde{L}_{m,t}
to verify
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
, which can be technically demanding. Therefore, having a criterion formulated in the Schrödinger picture, which corresponds to the algebraic one based on
\mathcal{A}
in (
16
) for the time-independent case, would be practically useful.
Note that
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{t}
in (
22
) is not appropriate for justifying the uniqueness, as we saw before.

For this purpose, assuming that
\tilde{L}_{m,t}
is smooth at
t
, we first define the following symmetry:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\{O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid[\partial^{n}_{t}\tilde{L}_{m,t},O]=0\ \forall m,n\}.

(61)

It is straightforward to check that
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}\supseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
(see the discussion around Eq. (
150
)). Moreover, let us additionally assume that
\tilde{L}_{m,t}
is analytical at all
t=t_{0}
, i.e.,
\tilde{L}_{m,t}
admits a Taylor expansion:

\tilde{L}_{m,t}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t-t_{0})^{n}}{n!}\partial_{t}^{n}\tilde{L}_{m,t}|_{t=t_{0}}

(62)

in some open neighborhood
U(t_{0})
of
t=t_{0}
.
Then, we can prove that
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
(see Appendix
D
), which leads to
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
.

Furthermore, in the Schrödinger picture, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\{O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\mid[\operatorname{ad}^{n}_{t}(L_{m,t}),O]=0\ \forall m,n\},

(63)

where
\operatorname{ad}_{t}
is adjoint operation defined as

\operatorname{ad}_{t}(A):=i[H_{t},A]+\partial_{t}A.

(64)

Two symmetries
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}
are related by a unitary transformation

\mathcal{C}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}=U_{t}\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}U_{t}^{\dagger},

(65)

where
U_{t}
is defined as (
30
).

Finally, we define the subalgebra of
\mathcal{B(\mathcal{H})}
generated by the identity operator, jump operators, and operators obtained by applying
\operatorname{ad}_{t}
arbitrary finite times to jump operators, namely,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\langle\,\mathbb{I},\{\operatorname{ad}^{n}_{t}(L_{m,t})\}_{m=1,\ldots,M;n=0,1,\ldots}\rangle.

(66)

Then,
\mathcal{C}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}
is the commutant of
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}
. By studying
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}
, which is much easier to calculate than
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
, we can prove the uniqueness of steady states [(i) in our classification].

Under these definitions, our results are summarized as follows:

Theorem 6
.

Consider the time-dependent GKSL equation (
7
) that satisfies Condition
3
. Suppose there exists
t_{0}\geq 0
such that
H_{t}
and

are smooth at

and
\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, or equivalently,
\mathcal{C}_{t_{0}}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
. Then, for any initial state
\rho_{0}
, the solution
\rho_{t}
of the GKSL equation converges to the maximally mixed state:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{t}=\mathbb{I}/d

(67)

as the unique steady state. Furthermore, if
H_{t}
and
L_{m,t}
are analytical for every
t
, then the converse is also true, i.e., the steady state is unique if and only if
\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, or equivalently,
\mathcal{C}_{t_{0}}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
for some
t_{0}
.

A full proof is presented in Appendix
D
; only a brief outline is provided here. From Theorem
2
, the steady state is unique if and only if
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
. Thus, it suffices to prove that

\mathcal{C}_{t_{0}}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}.

(68)

if
H_{t}
and
L_{m,t}
are smooth at
t=t_{0}
and

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{t_{0}}^{\mathrm{ad}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}

(69)

for some
t_{0}
if
H_{t}
and
L_{m,t}
are analytical for every
t
.

We remark that the analyticity of
H_{t}
and
L_{m,t}
is essential in the converse part of Theorem
6
. In Appendix
E
, we construct a counterexample to the converse part of Theorem
6
in a non-analytical GKSL equation.

IV.5
Applications

To demonstrate the applications of Theorem
6
, we consider examples of two-level systems and a boundary dissipated quantum spin chain.
Importantly, the uniqueness of the steady states for these systems cannot be proven by the existing methods in Refs.

;
55
, which cannot use the information of the Hamiltonian part of the dynamics.

Example 7
.

First, we consider a two-level system with
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
, whose GKSL equation is defined through

H_{t}=\sigma^{z}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}B_{j}\cos(\omega_j t)\sigma^{x},\,L_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}\sigma^{z},

(70)

where

and
B_{j},\omega_{j},\kappa>0
are constants. This GKSL equation satisfies Condition

Then, by Theorem

, the steady state is unique, because

\displaystyle L_{t}|_{t=0}\propto\sigma^{z},\ \operatorname{ad}_{t}[L_{t}]|_{t=0}\propto\sigma^{y},

(71)

and therefore the set of operators
\{\mathbb{I},L_{t}|_{t=0},\operatorname{ad}_{t}[L_{t}]|_{t=0}\}
generates the full operator algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Example 8
.

Next, consider the GKSL equation defined through

H_{t}=0,\,L_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}[\sigma^{x}\cos(\omega t)+\sigma^{y}\sin(\omega t)].

(72)

where
\omega,\kappa>0
are constants. Then, the steady state is unique from Theorem
6
, because

\displaystyle L_{t}|_{t=0}\propto\sigma^{x},\ \operatorname{ad}_{t}[L_{t}]|_{t=0}\propto\sigma^{y},

(73)

and therefore the set of operators
\{\mathbb{I},L_{t}|_{t=0},\operatorname{ad}_{t}[L_{t}]|_{t=0}\}

\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

Example 9

Finally, we consider an example of a boundary-dissipated quantum spin chain described by
\mathcal{H}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes V}

Using Pauli operators, we define operators on

Lemma 7

V
Implication of strong symmetries
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
for steady-state structure

So far, we have discussed the criteria for the unique steady states, i.e., class (i) in our classification introduced in
Sec.
II
.
In this section, we rigorously show that the two distinct strong symmetries
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
, introduced in Sec.
IV
, completely characterize the four classification
in
Sec.
II

for time-quasiperiodic GKSL equations.
Moreover, providing concrete examples for the four classes, we elucidate how the previously proposed
mechanisms underlying non-trivial oscillatory steady states, such as strong dynamical symmetry and Floquet dynamical symmetry, are naturally understood in our framework.
Furthermore, we demonstrate a new type of symmetry-predicted, time-dependent steady-state dynamics driven by time-quasiperiodic driving, which cannot be explained by the previously known mechanisms.

V.1
Absence of multiple time-independent steady states under
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}

As mentioned in Sec.
IV.1
, if there is a strong symmetry in the Schrödinger picture, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
, then the GKSL equation has multiple time-independent steady states [(ii) or (iii) in our classification]. Then, a natural question is whether
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
implies the absence of multiple time-independent steady states [(i) or (iv) in our classification]. For a general time-dependent Liouvillian, this is not the case.
In the case of Example
4
,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
, but there are multiple time-independent steady states.

However, if we restrict Liouvillian to a time-quasiperiodic one, we can prove that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
implies the absence of multiple time-independent steady states:

Theorem 8
.

Consider the time-dependent GKSL equation (
7
) that satisfies Condition
3
. Let
\rho^{*}
be a time-independent density matrix. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{t}=\rho^{*}
for some initial condition
\rho_{0}
.

2.

\rho^{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
.

See Appendix
G
for proof. From Theorem
8
,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
is equivalent to the absence of multiple time-independent steady states, i.e., classes (i) and (iv) in our classification, for quasiperiodic GKSL equations.

V.2
Existence of time-dependent steady states under
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset

An interesting situation is that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
but
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\neq\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
.
In this case, from Theorems
2
and
8
, we can expect that the GKSL equation has time-dependent steady states, as exemplified in Example
3
.
Here, more generally, we show that there exists a time-dependent steady state [(iii) and (iv) in our classification] if and only if
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
.

Theorem 9
.

Consider the time-dependent GKSL equation (
7
) that satisfies Condition
3
.
Then, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
.

2.

There exists a time-dependent steady state, i.e., there is a time-dependent
\rho^{*}_{t}
that does not converge to any time-independent density matrix as
t\to\infty
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{t}=\rho^{*}_{t},

(79)

for some initial condition
\rho_{0}
.

The proof is given in Appendix
H
.
There, we use the following Lemma proven in Appendix
I
, which follows from Theorem
2
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\rho^{*}_{t}
be a density matrix that may depend on time
t
. Under Condition
3
, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{t}=\rho^{*}_{t}
for some initial condition
\rho_{0}
.

2.

\rho^{*}_{t}=U_{t}\tilde{\rho}^{*}U_{t}^{\dagger}
for some time-independent density matrix
\tilde{\rho}^{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
.

From Theorems
8
, and
9
,
we can rigorously complete the characterization of the four steady-state classes (i)-(iv) introduced in Sec.
II
via
the two strong symmetries
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
.
This is summarized as Fig.

(b).

V.3
Applications

In this section, we discuss applications of Theorems
8
and
9
. We first see that strong dynamical symmetry
11
;
73
;
22
;
83
and Floquet dynamical symmetry
54
;
18
, which are proposed as mechanisms for the long-time oscillations, can be understood from our framework based on
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
.
Moreover, we propose novel systems where an asymptotic time-dependent steady state due to quasiperiodic driving is protected by the symmetry structure
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
.

V.3.1
Strong dynamical symmetry

To see a situation such that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
holds, we first consider a time-independent Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}
and focus on a symmetry
A
that satisfies the following condition:

[H,A]=\Omega A\text{ and }[L_{m},A]=0\ \forall m

(80)

with
\Omega\neq 0
. Such a symmetry is often called a strong dynamical symmetry
11
, and with this type of symmetry, the Liouvillian has purely imaginary eigenvalues that lead to oscillating coherence. In such a case,
A,A^{\dagger}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
by definition, but
A,A^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
, because

\displaystyle U_{t}AU_{t}^{\dagger}=e^{-i\Omega t}A

(81)

and therefore

\displaystyle[\tilde{L}_{m,t},A]

\displaystyle=[U_{t}^{\dagger}L_{m}U_{t},A]

(82)

\displaystyle=U_{t}^{\dagger}(L_{m}U_{t}AU_{t}^{\dagger}-U_{t}AU_{t}^{\dagger}L_{m})U_{t}

(83)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\Omega t}U_{t}^{\dagger}[L_{m},A]U_{t}=0,

(84)

\displaystyle[\tilde{L}_{m,t},A^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=-[\tilde{L}_{m,t},A]^{\dagger}=0.

(85)

Also,
A^{\dagger}A,\,AA^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
, because

[H,A^{\dagger}]=-\Omega A^{\dagger}\text{ and }[L_{m},A^{\dagger}]=0\ \forall m

(86)

and thus

[H,A^{\dagger}A]=[H,AA^{\dagger}]=[L_{m},A^{\dagger}A]=[L_{m},AA^{\dagger}]=0\ \forall m.

(87)

Therefore, strong dynamical symmetry (
80
) leads to
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
. From Fig.
1
, this implies class (iii), i.e., multiple time-independent and time-dependent steady states in the sense of Definition
1
.

Example 10

(
11
)
.

In Ref.
11
, the authors proposed a dissipative Hubbard model with strong dynamical symmetry, whose Hamiltonian and jump operators are given by

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle-\tau\sum_{\langle j,j^{\prime}\rangle}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\left(c_{j,\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{j^{\prime},\sigma}+\text{h.c.}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j}\left[Un_{j,\uparrow}n_{j,\downarrow}+\mu_{j}n_{j}+\frac{B}{2}(n_{j,\uparrow}-n_{j,\downarrow})\right]

(88)

\displaystyle L_{j}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\kappa_{j}}n_{j},

(89)

where
j,j^{\prime}
denote sites of a finite bipartite lattice,
c_{j,\sigma}^{\dagger}
and
c_{j,\sigma}
denote creation and annihilation operators of fermion on site
j
with spin
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
. The particle number operator for each spin is
n_{j,\sigma}=c_{j,\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{j,\sigma}
, the total particle number is
n_{j}=n_{j,\uparrow}+n_{j,\downarrow}
, and
\tau,U,\mu_{j},B,\kappa_{j}
are real constants that satisfy
\tau,B\neq 0

The dynamical symmetry of the model is given by

\displaystyle S^{+}=\sum_{j}c_{j,\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{j,\downarrow}

\displaystyle,\ S^{-}=\sum_{j}c_{j,\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{j,\uparrow}

\displaystyle[H,S^{\pm}]=\pm BS^{\pm}

\displaystyle,\ [L_{j},S^{\pm}]=0.

(91)

Also, the model has

\displaystyle N=\sum_{j}n_{j}

\displaystyle,\ S^{z}=\sum_{j}(n_{j,\uparrow}-n_{j,\downarrow}),

(92)

\displaystyle[H,N]=[L_{j},N]

\displaystyle=[H,S^{z}]=[L_{j},S^{z}]=0.

(93)

From the above, we identify two strong symmetries
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}

\langle\mathbb{I},S^{+}S^{-},S^{-}S^{+}\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}

[S^{+},S^{-}]=2S^{z}

\langle\mathbb{I},S^{+}S^{-},S^{-}S^{+}\rangle=\langle\mathbb{I},S^{+}S^{-},S^{z}\rangle

\langle\mathbb{I},S^{+},S^{-}\rangle=\langle\mathbb{I},S^{+},S^{-},S^{z}\rangle

By considering (

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{I},N,S^{+}S^{-},S^{z}\rangle

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}},

(94)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{I},N,S^{+},S^{-},S^{z}\rangle

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}},

(95)

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}\{(S^{+})^{m}(S^{-})^{n}\langle\mathbb{I},N,S^{z}\rangle\mid m\neq n\}

(96)

Owing to
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq 0

Note that since
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}

In fact, multiple time-dependent steady states always accompany multiple time-independent ones for time-independent GKSL equations

V.3.2
Floquet dynamical symmetry

The counterpart of the strong dynamical symmetry in a time-periodic Liouvillian is called the Floquet dynamical symmetry
18
;
54
. For a time-periodic Hamiltonian
H_{t}=H_{t+T}
and time-indepedent jump operators
\{L_{m}\}
,
A
is called Floquet dynamical symmetry if

\displaystyle U_{T}AU_{T}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=e^{-i\Omega T}A\quad(\Omega\neq 0)

(97)

\displaystyle[L_{m},U_{t}AU_{t}^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=0\quad\forall m,t,

(98)

where
U_{t}=\mathcal{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}H_{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}}
(see also (
30
)). Then,
A,A^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
, bacause

\displaystyle[\tilde{L}_{m,t},A]

\displaystyle=[U_{t}^{\dagger}L_{m}U_{t},A]

(99)

\displaystyle=U_{t}^{\dagger}[L_{m},U_{t}AU_{t}^{\dagger}]U_{t}=0,

(100)

\displaystyle[\tilde{L}_{m,t},A^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=-[\tilde{L}_{m,t},A]^{\dagger}=0.

(101)

by (
98
). However,
A,A^{\dagger}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
, bacause if so,
\Omega=0
. Moreover, unlike the strong dynamical symmetry in the time-independent case,
A^{\dagger}A,\,AA^{\dagger}\notin\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
in general. Thus it is possible that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq\emptyset
, which implies class (iv), i.e., time-dependent steady states without non-trivial time-independent ones in Definition
1
.

Example 11
.

In Ref.
18
, the authors proposed a dissipative Hubbard model with Floquet dynamical symmetry. They discuss the general Bose and Fermi-Hubbard model, but for simplicity, we focus on the Fermi-Hubbard model whose Hamiltonian and jump operator are given by

\displaystyle H_{t}=

\displaystyle-\tau\sum_{\langle j,j^{\prime}\rangle}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\left(c_{j,\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{j^{\prime},\sigma}+\text{h.c.}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{B}{2}\left[e^{-i\omega t}S^{+}+e^{i\omega t}S^{-}\right]

\displaystyle\sqrt{\kappa_{j}}n_{j},

(103)

\displaystyle[U_{T},S^{\pm}_{h}]=e^{\pm i(\sqrt{\omega^{2}+B^{2}})t}S^{\pm}_{h}

\displaystyle=s_{h}^{\downarrow\downarrow}=\frac{\xi}{2\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}},

VI
Conclusion and outlook

We have established a general framework for rigorously classifying steady states of time-quasiperiodic GKSL equations with Hermitian jump operators.
Our key idea is to distinguish two types of strong symmetry: strong symmetry in the Schrödinger picture
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
and strong symmetry in the interaction picture
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
, where a general hierarchy
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
exists.

We have first shown criteria for the uniqueness of steady states.
While a straightforward generalization of strong symmetry in the time-independent GKSL equations is
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
, the condition
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
does not guarantee the uniquness of steady states.
In contrast, we have proven that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}=\{c\mathbb{I}\mid c\in\mathbb{C}\}
provides the equivalent condition for the unique steady state of time-quasiperiodic GKSL equations.
Moreover, another practical criterion of the uniqueness is given in terms of the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\mathrm{ad}}
of the GKSL Liouvillian. When the Hamiltonian and the jump operators admit the Taylor expansion, this criterion gives a sufficient and necessary condition for the uniqueness (Fig.
1
(a)). We have also demonstrated the application of the criterion using examples of two-level systems and a quantum spin chain, which could not be addressed by the existing methods.

Then, we have elucidated that the two distinct strong symmetries,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}
, can completely characterize the steady-state classification given in Fig.
1
(b).
More concretely, the existence of a non-trivial element in
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
is equivalent to the existence of a non-trivial
time-independent
steady state.
Moreover, the existence of an element in
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}
is equivalent to the existence of a non-trivial
time-dependent
steady state.
We have seen that strong dynamical symmetry for time-independent GKSL equations and Floquet dynamical symmetry for time-periodic GKSL equations, which are known mechanisms to protect such a time-dependent steady state, can be understood from
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq 0
. Furthermore, we have constructed novel time-quasiperiodic GKSL equations, where the asymptotic time-dependent dynamics can be understood by
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}\setminus\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}\neq 0
but not by the conventionally known strong symmetries. The examples include, e.g., the Hubbard model with multi-frequency drives, which may be realized with ultracold atoms in optical lattices.

This work will stimulate many intriguing future questions.
One important direction is to extend the framework to cases where the jump operators are non-Hermitian.
In such cases, one may encounter interesting scenarios, e.g., where the steady state is unique yet exhibits explicit time dependence.
Such extensions introduce significant challenges: in the proof of Theorem
2
, it was essential that the purity of the density matrix monotonically decreases in time, which is not the case for non-Hermitian jump operators. Therefore, to prove the convergence to unique steady states, alternative measures may be required to quantify the distance from the steady state.
We also suppose that the relations between the strong symmetries and the steady state are not simple in the case of non-Hermitian jump operators, as in the time-independent GKSL equations
88
;
90
;
91
;
33
;
23
.

Second, it is also important to see if our results are extended to more general time-dependent systems than time-quasiperiodic ones.
As we have seen in Example
4
, we can construct a counterexample of our results if arbitrary time-dependence is allowed.
While this example describes the dissipation whose strength is decaying in time, it is interesting to ask if our theorems still apply if we focus on non-decaying dissipation.

Third, our findings on two distinct strong symmetries will open a new avenue for studying fundamental symmetries in GKSL equations.
For instance, while we focused on strong symmetries, we can also define two distinct
weak
symmetries
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Sch}}_{w}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{Int}}_{w}
with respect to Schrödinger and interaction pictures for time-dependent GKSL equations. They may be defined from unitary operators
V
that satisfy
V\mathcal{L}_{t}[\cdot]V^{\dagger}=\mathcal{L}_{t}[V\cdot V^{\dagger}]
and
V\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{t}[\cdot]V^{\dagger}=\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{t}[V\cdot V^{\dagger}]
for all
t
, respectively.
It is far from trivial how these weak symmetries affect dynamics of open quantum systems.
Furthermore, with such distinctions of (strong or weak) symmetries between Schrödinger and interaction pictures, the notion of the spontaneous symmetry breaking and phase transition in open quantum many-body systems
57
;
48
could be enriched, which merits further studies.

Last but not least, our work opens up the study of open quantum systems quasiperiodically driven in time.
As we have already discussed with concrete examples (including many-body systems), such systems behave differently from time-independent and time-periodic open quantum dynamics.
Given the fact that time-periodic open quantum dynamics

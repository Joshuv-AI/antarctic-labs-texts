Abstract
. In this note it is shown that if μ \mu is an n n -Ahlfors regular measure in ℝ n + 1 {\mathbb{R}}^{n+1} such that the n n -dimensional Riesz transform is bounded in L 2 ​ ( μ ) L^{2}(\mu) and the so-called BAUPP (bilateral approximation by unions of parallel planes) condition holds for μ \mu , then μ \mu satisfies the BWGL (bilateral weak geometric lemma), and so μ \mu is uniformly n n -rectifiable. In this way, one can solve the David-Semmes problem in codimension one without relying on the BAUP (bilateral approximation by unions of planes) criterion of David and Semmes.



1.
Introduction

A Radon measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
n
-Ahlfors regular if

(1.1)

C^{-1}r^{n}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{n}\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu$ and $0<r\leq{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)$.}

On the other hand,
\mu
is called uniformly
n
-rectifiable if it is
n
-Ahlfors regular and
there exist constants
\theta,M>0
such that for all
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
and all
0<r\leq{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)

there is a Lipschitz mapping
g
from the ball
B_{n}(0,r)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
\text{Lip}(g)\leq M
such that

\mu(B(x,r)\cap g(B_{n}(0,r)))\geq\theta r^{n}.

The notion of uniform rectifiability is a quantitative version of
n
-rectifiability introduced by David and Semmes (see
[
DS1
]
and

[
DS2
]
) which has attracted much attention because its applications to harmonic analysis and PDE’s in rough settings, among other things.

An important question in this area is the so-called David-Semmes problem, which consists in proving that if
\mu
is
n
-Ahlfors regular and the
Riesz transform operator
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
, then
\mu
is uniformly
n
-rectifiable.
The (
n
-dimensional) Riesz transform of a signed Radon measure
\nu
is defined by

{\mathcal{R}}\nu(x)=\int\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\nu(y),

whenever the integral makes sense.
We also write

{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\nu(x)=\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0}|{\mathcal{R}}_{{\varepsilon}}\nu(x)|,\qquad\operatorname{pv}{\mathcal{R}}\nu(x)=\lim_{{\varepsilon}>0}{\mathcal{R}}_{{\varepsilon}}\nu(x),

in case that the latter limit exists. Remark that, sometimes, abusing notation we will
write
{\mathcal{R}}\nu
instead of
\operatorname{pv}{\mathcal{R}}\nu
.

For
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mu)
and a positive Radon measure
\mu
,
one writes
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}f(x)={\mathcal{R}}(f\mu)(x)
.
Given
{\varepsilon}>0
, the
{\varepsilon}
-truncated Riesz transform of
\mu
equals

{\mathcal{R}}_{\varepsilon}\mu(x)=\int_{|x-y|>{\varepsilon}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\mu(y),

and the operator
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}
is defined by

{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}f(x)={\mathcal{R}}_{\varepsilon}(f\mu)(x)
.
We say that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
if
the operators
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}
are bounded uniformly in
L^{2}(\mu)
uniformly on
{\varepsilon}
, and then we denote

\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}(\mu)}=\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}(\mu)}.

The David-Semmes problem was solved in the affirmative in the case
n=1
in
[
MMV
]
by Mattila, Melnikov, and Verdera, and in the case

n=d-1
in
[
NToV
]
by Nazarov, the author, and Volberg
[
NToV
]
. For other values of
n
, this is still an open problem. One of the essential ingredients of the proof in
[
NToV
]
is the
so called BAUP criterion. To state this, we need need some additional notation.

For a given
{\varepsilon}>0
and
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
,
r>0
, we write
(x,r)\in{\mathsf{BAUP}}({\varepsilon})
(BAUP stands for bilateral approximation by a union of planes) if there exists a family
\tilde{I}_{x,r}
of

n
-planes such that, setting

\tilde{H}_{x,r}=\bigcup_{L\in\tilde{I}_{x,r}}L,

it holds

\sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\cap B(x,r)}\frac{{\rm dist}(x,\tilde{H}_{x,r})}{\ell(Q)}+\sup_{x\in\tilde{H}_{x,r}\cap B(x,r)}\frac{{\rm dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}\mu)}{r}\leq{\varepsilon}.

Then we say that
\mu
satisfies the BAUP condition, if for any
{\varepsilon}>0
, the following Carleson type condition holds:

\int_{B}\int_{0}^{r(B)}\chi_{(x,t)\not\in{\mathsf{BAUP}}({\varepsilon})}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,d\mu(x)\leq\mu(B),

for any ball
B
centered at
\operatorname{supp}\mu
, where
r(B)
denotes the radius of
B
.
A deep result of David and Semmes (see
[
DS2
, Chapter II.3]
) asserts that if
\mu
is
n
-Ahlfors regular and it satisfies the BAUP condition, then
\mu
is
uniformly
n
-rectifiable. The proof of this result is difficult and lengthy. The purpose of this note is to give a more straightforward argument
that avoids the application of the BAUP criterion for the solution of the David-Semmes problem in codimension
1
and instead relies on the BGWL (bilateral weak geometric lemma). The definition of the BGWL condition is analogous to the one of the BAUP condition, with the family
\tilde{I}_{x,r}
formed in this case by a single
n
-plane.
Remark that the proof of the fact that the BGWL suffices for the uniform
n
-rectifiability of
\mu
is considerably easier than the one
for the BAUP condition, specially in codimension one (see
[
DS2
, Chapter II.2]
).

In
[
NToV
]
it is shown that if
\mu
is an
n
-Ahlfors regular measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
such that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
, then the BAUP condition holds for
\mu
and so
\mu
is uniformly
n
-rectifiable, by the aforementioned criterion of David and Semmes. In fact, a quick inspection of the arguments in
[
NToV
]
shows that
\mu
satisfies something (a priori) stronger than the BAUP condition, namely what we call the BAUPP (bilateral approximation by union of
parallel
planes. This is defined as the BAUP, but only allowing parallel
n
-planes in family

\tilde{I}_{x,r}
. Since this notion is important for our purposes we state it more precisely.

For a given
{\varepsilon}>0
and
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
,
r>0
, we write
(x,r)\in{\mathsf{BAUPP}}({\varepsilon})
if the exists a family
I_{x,r}
of parallel

n
-planes such that, setting

H_{x,r}=\bigcup_{L\in I_{x,r}}L,

it holds

\sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\cap B(x,r)}\frac{{\rm dist}(x,H_{x,r})}{\ell(Q)}+\sup_{x\in H_{x,r}\cap B(x,r)}\frac{{\rm dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}\mu)}{r}\leq{\varepsilon}.

Then we say that
\mu
satisfies the BAUPP condition, if for any
{\varepsilon}>0
, the following holds:

\int_{B}\int_{0}^{r(B)}\chi_{(x,t)\not\in{\mathsf{BAUP}}\c{C}P({\varepsilon})}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,d\mu(x)\leq\mu(B),

for any ball
B
centered at
\operatorname{supp}\mu
.

Remark that, trivially, we have

(1.2)

BWGL\;\Rightarrow\;BAUPP\;\Rightarrow\;BAUP.

The implication
BAUP\;\Rightarrow\;BWGL
also holds, but it is much more complicated. Hence, the three conditions in (
1.2
) are equivalent to the uniform rectifiability of
\mu
.

In this note we will prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be an
n
-Ahlfors regular measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
satisfying the BAUPP condition such that the
n
-dimensional Riesz transform
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
.
Then
\mu
satisfies the BWGL.

By the discussion above, it is clear that, combining this result with the arguments in
[
NToV
]
, one deduces that the
L^{2}(\mu)
boundedness of the
n
-dimensional Riesz transform
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}
implies the BWGL for
\mu
, and so its uniform
n
-rectifiability. In this way, we can skip the
BAUP criterion for the solution of the David-Semmes problem.

1.1

n

For

, and we assume that

|m_{P_{t}}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\chi_{2B})-m_{P_{b}}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\chi_{2B})|\geq\tau_{0}-|m_{P_{t}}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}^{n+1}\chi_{2B\cap S_{t}\setminus P_{t}})|-|m_{P_{b}}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}^{n+1}\chi_{2B\cap S_{b}\setminus P_{b}})|.

Our next objective consists in showing that

(2.8)

|m_{P_{t}}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}^{n+1}\chi_{2B\cap S_{t}\setminus P_{t}})|+|m_{P_{b}}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}^{n+1}\chi_{2B\cap S_{b}\setminus P_{b}})|\lesssim_{N}{\varepsilon}^{1/2}.

Observe that this estimate, together with (
2.5
), (
2.6
), and (
2.7
), gives

|m_{P_{t}}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\chi_{2R})-m_{P_{b}}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\chi_{2R})|\geq\tau_{0}-C2^{-N}-C(N){\varepsilon}^{1/2},

which proves the lemma if
N
is large enough and
{\varepsilon}
small enough.
We will only estimate
|m_{P_{t}}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}^{n+1}\chi_{2B\cap S_{t}\setminus P_{t}})|
, since the arguments for
|m_{P_{b}}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}^{n+1}\chi_{2B\cap S_{b}\setminus P_{b}})|
are similar. To this end, denote by
h
the height of
P_{t}
, so that
h\approx{\varepsilon}\ell(Q^{(j+N)})\approx 2^{N}{\varepsilon}r
(below we will take
{\varepsilon}
such that
2^{N}{\varepsilon}\ll 1
and so the reader should thing that
P_{t}
and
P_{b}
are very flat cylinders).
Consider
x\in P_{t}
and let
d(x):={\rm dist}(x,\partial_{l}C_{Q})
. Since the height of the region
S_{b}
is
h
, we have
|x_{n+1}-y_{n+1}|\leq h
for all
x,y\in S_{b}
, and so

|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}^{n+1}\chi_{2B\cap S_{t}\setminus P_{t}})(x)|\leq\int_{y\in S_{t}\setminus P_{t}}\frac{\min(h,|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\mu(y)\leq\int_{|x-y|\geq d(x)}\frac{\min(h,|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\mu(y).

It
d(x)>h
, then
|x-y|\geq h
for
y
in the domain of integration of the last integral. In this case, we write

|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}^{n+1}\chi_{2B\cap S_{t}\setminus P_{t}})(x)|\leq\int_{|x-y|\geq d(x)}\frac{h}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\mu(y)\lesssim\frac{h}{d(x)},

using the polynomial growth of
\mu
for the last estimate.
In the case
d(x)\leq h
, we split

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|\geq d(x)}\!\frac{\min(h,|x-y|)}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{d(x)\leq|x-y|\leq h}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\,d\mu(y)+\int_{|x-y|>h}\frac{h}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\log\frac{2h}{d(x)}+1\lesssim\bigg(\frac{h}{d(x)}\bigg)^{1/2},

using again the polynomial growth of
\mu
.

Putting altogether, we get

\displaystyle\int_{P_{t}}|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}^{n+1}\chi_{2B\cap S_{t}\setminus P_{t}})(x)|\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x\in P_{t}:d(x)\leq h}\bigg(\frac{h}{d(x)}\bigg)^{1/2}\,d\mu(x)+\int_{x\in P_{t}:d(x)>h}\frac{h}{d(x)}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}.

We will estimate both
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
using the thin boundary condition (
2.3
).
First we deal with
I_{1}
:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{x\in P_{t}:2^{-k-1}<d(x)\leq 2^{-k}h}\bigg(\frac{h}{d(x)}\bigg)^{1/2}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{k/2}\,\mu(\{x\in P_{t}:d(x)\leq 2^{-k}h\}\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{k/2}\,\frac{2^{-k}h}{r}\,\mu(P_{t})\lesssim\frac{h}{r}\,\mu(P_{t}).

Also,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{x\in P_{t}:2^{k}h<d(x)\leq 2^{k+1}h}\frac{h}{d(x)}\,d\mu(x)\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k}\,\mu(\{x\in P_{t}:d(x)\leq 2^{k+1}h\})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0:\\
2^{k}h\leq r\end{subarray}}2^{-k}\,\frac{2^{k+1}h}{r}\,\mu(P_{t})\approx\frac{h}{r}\,\log\frac{r}{h}\,\mu(P_{t})\lesssim\bigg(\frac{h}{r}\bigg)^{1/2}\,\mu(P_{t}).

Therefore,

\int_{P_{t}}|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}^{n+1}\chi_{2B\cap S_{t}\setminus P_{t}})(x)|\,d\mu(x)\lesssim\bigg(\frac{h}{r}\bigg)^{1/2}\,\mu(P_{t})\approx 2^{N/2}{\varepsilon}^{1/2}\,\mu(P_{t}),

which, together with the analogous estimate for
P_{b}
,
proves (
2.8
) and concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

In the lemma below, we provide the detailed arguments for the estimate (
2.4
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the above assumptions, we have

(2.9)

\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}(R)\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{m}^{*}(\delta)}|m_{P_{t}(Q)}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}(\chi_{2R}))-m_{P_{b}(Q)}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}(\chi_{2R}))|^{2}\,\mu(Q)\leq C(m,\delta,{\varepsilon},\tau)\,\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}(\chi_{2R})\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}.

Proof.

Our arguments below will use Lemma
2.1
and the following standard result, whose proof can be found in Section 14 from
[
NToV
]
:

Let
\mu
be
n
-Ahlfors regular and let
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
be the David-Semmes lattice associated with
\mu
. Let
\{\psi_{Q}\}_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}}
be a family of Lipschitz functions satisfying the following, for fixed constants
C_{2},C_{3}>1
:

(1)

\operatorname{supp}\psi_{Q}\subset C_{2}B_{Q}
,

(2)

\int\psi_{Q}\,d\mu=0,

(3)

{\rm Lip}(\psi_{Q})\leq C_{3}\,\ell(Q)^{-\frac{n}{2}-1}
.

Then, for any function
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
,

(2.10)

\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}}\langle f,\psi_{Q}\rangle_{\mu}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2},

with the implicit constant depending only on
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
, and the
n
-Ahlfors regularity of
\mu
.

One could also prove this lemma without using neither this result nor the estimate in Lemma (
2.1
), but our approach here suffices for our purposes.

Denote
f={\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\chi_{2R}
. The sum on the left hand side of (
2.9
) can be written as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}(R)\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{m}^{*}(\delta)}\left(\int\left(\frac{\chi_{P_{t}(Q)}}{\mu(P_{t}(Q))}-\frac{\chi_{P_{b}(Q)}}{\mu(P_{b}(Q))}\right)\,\mu(Q)^{1/2}f\,d\mu\right)^{2}=\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}(R)\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{m}^{*}(\delta)}\big\langle f,\eta_{Q}\big\rangle_{\mu}^{2},

where

\eta_{Q}=\left(\frac{\chi_{P_{t}(Q)}}{\mu(P_{t}(Q))}-\frac{\chi_{P_{b}(Q)}}{\mu(P_{b}(Q))}\right)\,\mu(Q)^{1/2}.

We cannot apply (
2.10
) with
\eta_{Q}
in place of
\psi_{Q}
because
\eta_{Q}
is not Lipschitz. To solve this issue, we will approximate

\eta_{Q}
by a a suitable Lipschitz function.

For each
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{m}^{*}(\delta)
, let
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{b}(Q)}
be a Lipschitz function which equals
1
on
P_{b}(Q)
and is supported in the
(t\ell(Q))
-neighborhood
U_{t\ell(Q)}(P_{b})
, with
{\rm Lip}({\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{b}(Q)})\lesssim(t\,\ell(Q))^{-1}
,
for a small
t
to be chosen below, possibly depending on
{\varepsilon}
and
\delta
. We define
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{b}}
analogously, and then we let

\psi_{Q}=\left(\frac{{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{t}(Q)}}{\int{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{t}(Q)}\,d\mu}-\frac{{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{b}(Q)}}{\int{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{b}(Q)}\,d\mu}\right)\,\mu(Q)^{1/2}.

It is immediate to check that the functions
\psi_{Q}
satisfy the assumptions (1), (2), (3) above, possibly with constants depinding on
N
,
t
,
and other parameters.

Now, by the zero-mean value of
\eta_{Q}
and
\psi_{Q}
,

(2.11)

\displaystyle|\langle\eta_{Q},f\rangle_{\mu}|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle\psi_{Q},f\rangle_{\mu}|+|\langle(\eta_{Q}-\psi_{Q}),(f-m_{Q}(f))\rangle_{\mu}|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle\psi_{Q},f\rangle_{\mu}|+\|\eta_{Q}-\psi_{Q}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\left(\int_{2Q^{(j+N)}}|f-m_{Q}(f)|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq|\langle\psi_{Q},f\rangle_{\mu}|+C\|\eta_{Q}-\psi_{Q}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\mu(Q)^{1/2},

where we used the boundedness of
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}
from
L^{\infty}(\mu)
to
BMO(\mu)
in the last inequality, and recalling that
f={\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\chi_{2R}
.

Observe that
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{t}(Q)}-\chi_{P_{t}(Q)}=\chi_{U_{t}(P_{t}(Q))\setminus P_{t}(Q)}
and so

\|{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{t}(Q)}-\chi_{P_{t}(Q)}\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{t}(Q)}-\chi_{P_{t}(Q)}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\lesssim\mu(U_{t}(P_{t}(Q))\setminus P_{t}(Q))\lesssim_{N,{\varepsilon}}t\,\mu(Q),

by the small boundary condition of
C_{Q}
and the choice of the
n
-planes
L_{t}
,
L_{t}^{\prime}
. The same estimate holds with
P_{b}
instead of
P_{t}
.
Then it follows that

\displaystyle\|\eta_{Q}-\psi_{Q}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{t}(Q)}-\chi_{P_{t}(Q)}\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}}{\mu(Q)^{2}}\,\mu(P_{t})^{1/2}\mu(Q)^{1/2}+\frac{\|{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{t}(Q)}-\chi_{P_{t}(Q)}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}}{\mu(P_{t})}\,\mu(Q)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\!\!\!\!\!+\frac{\|{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{b}(Q)}-\chi_{P_{b}(Q)}\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}}{\mu(Q)^{2}}\,\mu(P_{b})^{1/2}\mu(Q)^{1/2}+\frac{\|{\widetilde{\chi}}_{P_{b}(Q)}-\chi_{P_{b}(Q)}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}}{\mu(P_{b})}\,\mu(Q)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,{\varepsilon}}\frac{t\,\mu(Q)}{\mu(Q)^{2}}\,\mu(Q)+\frac{t^{1/2}\,\mu(Q)^{1/2}}{\mu(Q)}\,\mu(Q)^{1/2}\lesssim_{N,{\varepsilon}}t^{1/2},

since
t<1
.
Plugging this estimate into (
2.11
), we derive

|\langle f,\eta_{Q}\rangle_{\mu}|\leq|\langle f,\psi_{Q}\rangle_{\mu}|+C({\varepsilon},N)\,t^{1/2}\mu(Q)^{1/2}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we know that
|\langle f,\eta_{Q}\rangle_{\mu}|\geq\tau\,\mu(Q)^{1/2},
where
\tau
is an absolute constant. Therefore,
it we take
t
small enough so that
C({\varepsilon},N)\,t^{1/2}\leq\tau/2
, the last term on the right hand side in the display above can be absorbed by the term on the left hand side, giving

|\langle f,\eta_{Q}\rangle_{\mu}|\leq 2|\langle f,\psi_{Q}\rangle_{\mu}|.

Squaring, summing over
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}(R)\cap{\mathcal{B}}^{*}_{m}(\delta)
, applying (
2.10
), and using the
L^{2}(\mu)
boundedness of the Riesz transform, we obtain

\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}(R)\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{m}^{*}(\delta)}|\langle f,\eta_{Q}\rangle_{\mu}|^{2}\leq 4\sum_{Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}(R)\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{m}^{*}(\delta)}|\langle f,\psi_{Q}\rangle_{\mu}|^{2}\lesssim\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\chi_{2R}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\lesssim\mu(R),

concluding the proof of the lemma.
∎

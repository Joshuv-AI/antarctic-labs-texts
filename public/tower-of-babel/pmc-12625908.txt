Ten-year target lesion failure in patients treated with primary PCI: results from DANAMI-3
Authors: Burcu Tas Özbek, Utsho Islam, Jasmine Melissa Marquard, Henning Kelbaek, Joakim B Kunkel, Lene Holmvang, Hans-Henrik Tilsted, Frants Pedersen, Lars Koeber, Dan Hofsten, Ashkan Eftekhari, Bent Raungaard, Hans Erik Bøttker, Christian Juhl Terkelsen, Evald Christiansen, Ibrahim Mohammed Abdul Khalek, Lisette Okkels Jensen, Thomas Engstrøm, Jacob Thomsen Lønborg
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrimary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) has significantly improved outcomes for ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) patients. However, the long-term durability of PCI in terms of target lesion failure (TLF) remains unknown.ObjectivesThis study investigates the long-term incidence, predictors and clinical impact of TLF over a 10-year follow-up in STEMI patients treated with primary PCI.MethodsFrom the DANAMI-3 trial, we analysed STEMI patients treated with primary PCI. TLF was defined as a composite of cardiovascular death, target lesion myocardial infarction or target lesion revascularisation. Independent predictors of TLF were identified by Cox regression. Outcomes in high-risk and low-risk groups were evaluated using cumulative incidence functions with competing risk analysis.ResultsOf 2217 patients (median follow-up of 10.7 years), 443 (20.0%) experienced TLF. TLF occurred in 5.6% within the first year after PCI and continued at a constant annual rate of 1.6% thereafter. All-cause mortality, any MI or any revascularisation occurred in 961 (43%) patients, and TLF constitutes 46% of the total patient-oriented events. Multivariable Cox regression identified age, hypertension, previous AMI and Killip class II–IV as independent predictors of an increased risk of TLF, whereas PCI with drug-eluting stents was associated with a reduced risk of TLF. Patients with ≥1 high-risk feature had a twofold greater risk of TLF compared with those without (HR 2.05, 95% CI 1.65 to 2.55, p<0.001).ConclusionsOne in five STEMI patients treated with primary PCI experiences TLF within 10 years, accounting for a significant proportion of any mortality, any MI or any revascularisation, with sustained occurrence rates beyond the first year.Trial registration numberNCT01960933.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

Target lesion failure (TLF) is a commonly used composite endpoint in percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) trials, typically reported within 1–5 years. Long-term durability of primary PCI beyond 5 years in ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) patients, including lesion-specific adverse outcomes such as target lesion myocardial infarction and target lesion revascularisation, remains largely unknown.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

In a cohort of over 2217 STEMI patients from the DANAMI-3 trial, we found that 20% experienced TLF within 10 years, with a continued event rate beyond the first year. Introduction

Ischaemic heart disease is one of the leading causes of mortality worldwide, and frequently patients present with ST segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI).1 Over the last decades, the treatment of patients with STEMI has significantly improved, primarily due to the introduction of timely primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).2 This advance has dramatically reduced mortality and otherwise improved outcomes for patients with STEMI.3 STEMI patients treated with primary PCI surviving the first 30 days only have a modest excess mortality compared with matched individuals from the general population.4 Thus, despite this improved long-term prognosis, other outcomes are important, such as recurrent myocardial infarction (MI) and the need for later revascularisation. These outcomes often reflect a lack of long-term durability of the index PCI and are thus related to target lesion failure (TLF). TLF, including target lesion myocardial infarction (TL-MI), target lesion revascularisation (TLR) or cardiovascular (CV) death, thus becomes a critical long-term outcome measurement and quality indicator in patients with STEMI treated with primary PCI. Moreover, TLF is emerging as a critical determinant of adverse events that continues to impact long-term clinical outcomes and quality of life—especially in the context of improved prognosis.5 Stent-related events, including restenosis and thrombosis, remain a major concern leading to increased mortality, especially in acute coronary syndrome (ACS) patients who are at high risk.68 However, there are limited data on long-term rates of TLF beyond 5 years, and no previous study has reported 10-year outcomes exclusively in patients with STEMI treated with primary PCI. To better understand long-term risk, we categorised patients into high-risk and low-risk groups based on age and clinical factors. To address this unmet need for data, we aimed to identify clinical risk factors of TLF and assess the incidence of TLF over 10 years in STEMI patients treated with primary PCI, using data from the third Danish Study of Optimal Acute Treatment of Patients with ST-segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction (DANAMI-3) cohort.

Methods

Study population

This study included participants from the DANAMI-3 trial, a collection of three Danish randomised trials, investigating deferred stenting (DANAMI-3-DEFER), ischaemic postconditioning (DANAMI-3-iPOST) and complete revascularisation (DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI), which have all been previously described in detail elsewhere.911 An overview of the three trial arms and patient flow is shown in figure 1. In brief, from 2011 to 2014, STEMI patients were admitted to one of four PCI centres in Denmark. Patients were included if aged ≥18 years and presenting with chest pain of less than 12 hours and STEMI defined by >1 mm ST-segment elevation in at least 2 contiguous leads. The trial excluded patients with cardiogenic shock, stent thrombosis, patients with indication for coronary artery bypass graft surgery, or increased bleeding risk. This long-term study of the DANAMI-3 trial included 2217 patients.

Overview of the three randomised trials included in the DANAMI-3 programme: DEFER, PRIMULTI and iPOST. All trials enrolled STEMI patients within 12 hours of symptom onset and randomised them to different interventional strategies. FFR, fractional flow reserve; PCI, percutaneous coronary intervention; STEMI, ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction.

In DANAMI-3-DEFER, the STEMI patients were randomised 1:1 to either conventional PCI or PCI with deferred stent implantation.9

In DANAMI-3-iPOST, patients with STEMI were randomised to ischaemic postconditioning versus conventional treatment.10

In DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI, patients aged ≥75 years with STEMI were randomised 1:1 to either culprit-only or complete FFR-guided revascularisation.11 In the overall DANAMI-3 trial population, the vast majority of patients were treated with contemporary second-generation drug-eluting stents (DES); however, 2.6% (n=58) received bare-metal stents. The trial was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov, number NCT01960933.

End points and follow-up

Participants were followed from inclusion until death or end of the follow-up period in January 2024. During the initial follow-up period, an independent data safety monitoring board monitored the trial for safety and an independent clinical events committee (CEC) adjudicated all events. From 2015, events were retrieved using hospital records until January 2024. Data were obtained and evaluated by two reviewers. In the case of discordance, a third reviewer secured consensus.

The primary outcome for this analysis was TLF defined according to previous definitions as a composite endpoint of CV death, TL-MI or TLR.12

CV death was defined according to Academic Research Consortium-2 (ARC-2) criteria as death resulting from cardiovascular causes, including any of the following: death due to acute myocardial infarction; death resulting from heart failure; sudden cardiac death (including unwitnessed deaths); death occurring during a CV procedure; death due to stroke; death resulting from CV haemorrhage (eg, rupture of an aortic aneurysm) or death from other CV causes.13 Target lesion was defined according to ARC-2 criteria as the treated segment during primary PCI, including a 5 mm margin both proximally and distally to the stent.13

TL-MI was defined as an AMI attributed to the target lesion based on angiographic evidence (stented segment±5 mm proximal and distal) or an AMI that cannot clearly be attributed to a non-target lesion if the AMI occurred within 28 days after the index PCI.1315 The target lesion was defined as the culprit lesion initially treated during the primary PCI. Lesions were classified according to their segmental location.

TLR was defined according to ARC-2 criteria as any repeat PCI of the target lesion or coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) of the target vessel. If the procedure documentation did not clearly state that the revascularisation was performed within the stented segment, the event was classified as TLR when the procedure was coded to the same coronary segment number as the target lesion.13

The definition of myocardial infarction was based on the Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction.16 An AMI required a rise and/or fall in high-sensitivity cardiac troponin, with at least one value above the assay’s 99th percentile upper reference limit, combined with clinical or electrocardiographic evidence of ischaemia. According to the Danish national guidelines, the cut-off for high-sensitivity troponin is <14 ng/L.17

The secondary outcomes were a patient-oriented composite of major adverse cardiac events (MACE) including all-cause mortality, any myocardial infarction or any revascularisation. However, as mortality in particular can be caused by the index STEMI and not related to the PCI analyses of TLF without CV death was also done using a composite endpoint of TL-MI or TLR. Finally, all individual components of the primary and secondary composite outcomes were considered as secondary outcomes. In addition, the incidence of target vessel revascularisation (TVR) and stent thrombosis was also evaluated and presented.

Stent thrombosis was defined according to the ARC criteria. However, only definite stent thrombosis was registered and included as a stent thrombosis event in this study.15

A high-risk group was identified based on clinically significant predictors from the multivariable analyses. The high-risk group was defined as patients aged >70 years and had at least one of the following risk factors: hypertension, previous AMI or Killip class II–IV.

Statistics

Cox proportional hazards regression was used to assess the association between baseline, clinical and angiographic variables, and the outcome. Multivariable Cox regression models were then constructed, adjusting for all variables with a p<0.10 in univariable analysis. The results of the univariable and multivariable Cox regression analyses for the secondary outcomes are presented in online supplemental table S1.

For all models, the proportional hazards assumption was tested using Schoenfeld residuals, with no violations observed. Moreover, cumulative incidence curves were constructed using competing risk analysis, with TLF as the primary outcome and non-TLF death as the competing event. Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard models were used to estimate cumulative incidence, and Gray’s test was applied to compare cumulative incidences between groups. In addition, separate cumulative incidence curves were generated for each individual component of the primary outcome: CV death, TLR and TL-MI.

Statistical analysis was performed using R version 4.2.2.

Results

Population, outcome and follow-up

The median follow-up time was 10.7 years (IQR: 9.9–11.5 years), with a 99% follow-up rate. A total of 2239 patients were enrolled in the present analysis. After excluding 22 patients who were lost to follow-up, 2217 patients were included in the final analysis, of whom 443 (20%) had TLF (figure 2A). During the entire follow-up, 443 patients experienced at least one TLF event. In the competing risk model, the cumulative incidence of TLF reached 441 events over a maximum follow-up of 12 years, as competing risk analysis counts only the first event per patient.

Predictors of TLF

Participants with TLF were older and more likely to have a history of diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia and previous AMI (table 1). They also had a higher level of index troponin, discharge creatinine and lower left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) at discharge (table 1). Participants with TLF had a higher prevalence of STEMI located in the anterior region, while exhibiting a lower prevalence in the inferior and posterior locations (table 1). With respect to angiographic findings, participants who developed TLF had significantly longer PCI procedure, lower prevalence of PCI with second generation DES and higher prevalence of multivessel disease (table 2).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first study to report long-term incidence and predictors of TLF exclusively in patients with STEMI treated with primary PCI, as previous trials with 10-year follow-up, such as ISAR-5 and SORT-OUT II, included mixed population.18 19 TLF occurred in one in five patients within 10 years after initial primary PCI, 5.6% within the first year and thereafter a yearly rate of 1.6%. Age, hypertension, previous AMI and Killip class II–IV at admission were associated with an increased rate of TLF. Patients >70 years or at least one other risk factor had an overall 25% risk of developing TLF within 10 years following STEMI treated with primary PCI. Also, TLF was the cause of a severe adverse event in almost half of all patient-oriented events. Thus, these findings show that there is a growing need to focus on improving long-term outcomes by preventing TLF.

Of note, long-term follow-up data exist from ISAR-5 and SORT-OUT III; however, these studies included mixed ACS populations, whereas our study focuses exclusively on STEMI patients treated with primary PCI.18 19 However, a registry reported a 1-year TLF rate of 4.2% among patients treated with DES, the majority being ACS patients, of whom only 19.9% were STEMI patients treated with primary PCI.20 21 In contrast, the DANAMI-3 cohort exclusively focused on STEMI patients, reporting a 1-year TLF rate of 5.6%. Sim et al investigated long-term clinical outcomes in STEMI patients undergoing primary PCI with either biodegradable polymer DES (BP-DES) or second-generation durable polymer DES (DP-DES).22 Over a median follow-up of 4.2 years, they observed TLF rates of 11.1% for BP-DES and 12.3% for DP-DES—with no significant differences between groups. In contrast, our study provides 10-year follow-up data, with a TLF rate of 20%, and the majority (97%) of patients were treated with second-generation DES. Several factors may explain these differences, including the difference in follow-up time, differences in stent technology and variations in TLF definitions—our study included target lesion MI, whereas theirs included target vessel MI. These variations, along with differences in patient populations, may have influenced the findings.

When comparing to previous data for patients with or without ACS, a pooled analysis of six randomised controlled trials investigating contemporary DES, which observed a 5-year TLF (defined as a composite of cardiac death, target vessel myocardial infarction or TLR) rate of approximately 12%.23 This analysis included 44% ACS patients of whom 9% were STEMI patients. A study by Kjøller-Hansen et al examined 10-year stent-related adverse outcomes in a PCI population that included both stable CAD and ACS patients treated with first generation DES.24 The study reported a 20% incidence of TLF over 10 years, similar to the findings in the DANAMI-3 cohort. Smaller stent diameter and long total stent length were identified as independent predictors of TLF. Unlike the study by Kjøller-Hansen et al, in the DANAMI-3 population numbers of stents, but neither total stent length nor stent diameter, were associated with TLF. This discrepancy may be explained by differences in patient populations. DANAMI-3 included STEMI patients, in whom clinical factors related to the myocardial infarction itself may have played a bigger role than procedural characteristics in predicting long-term outcomes. In addition, patients without STEMI in the referred studies experienced TLF primarily driven by an ischaemia-driven TLR, whereas in the DANAMI-3 cohort, TLF was predominantly driven by CV death. In STEMI patients, a previous study showed long stent length (median 38 mm) to be a major predictor of TLF compared with shorter stents (median 18 mm).25 However, in that study, CV death was not a part of the definition of TLF and could explain the different findings. Reasonably, it can be questioned whether CV death is a well-chosen component in a lesion-specific outcome. Several previous studies have included CV death as part of the composite endpoint, but it remains uncertain whether this always reflects events directly related to the initial PCI. In some cases, CV death may be due to other causes such as heart failure or arrhythmia, rather than complications related to the PCI. To explore the impact of including CV death in the definition of TLF, we performed a sensitivity analysis where CV death was excluded from the composite endpoint. In this analysis, hypertension, previous AMI, number of implanted stents and use of prasugrel remained independently associated with TLF. This indicates that our findings are consistent, as key predictors remained significantly associated with TLF even when CV was excluded, with Killip class being the only predictor no longer significantly associated. In the sensitivity analysis excluding CV death, age was no longer a significant predictor of TL-MI or TLR. This finding is likely explained by competing risk, as older patients have a higher risk of CV and all-cause mortality and may die before experiencing lesion-related events. Taken altogether, during 10 years follow-up lesion-specific event rates are not trivial and on top of the need for a new revascularisation, which may be considered benign in nature, rates of TL-MI and CV death are also high.

Perspectives

In culprit lesions of AMI, delayed vessel healing, inflammation and uncovered stent struts may contribute to the development of neoatherosclerosis, potentially leading to TLF.26 Other factors related to TLF are underexpanded stent, edge dissections and uncovered significant plaque in the adjacent areas of the vessel.12 27 As not used in DANAMI-3, intracoronary imaging can be valuable in identifying and optimising these lesion-related factors. Previous studies suggest that imaging-guided PCI reduces TLF compared with angiography-guided PCI, although most data are from non-STEMI populations.12 28 29 Therefore, whether imaging guided PCI in patients with STEMI improves outcome still needs to be settled.

Limitations

Patients in the present trial were treated with primary PCI >10 years ago and the treatment has changed. This includes complete revascularisation for STEMI patients more aggressive lipid-lowering treatment, longer and larger stents and more use of intravascular imaging. In the present study, 28% with multivessel disease were included in DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI and were randomised to culprit only (14% and to complete FFR-guided revascularisation (14%. As complete revascularisation has emerged as the standard of care for patients with STEMI,30 31 the patients who were randomised to culprit-only in the DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI could impact on the patient-oriented MACE rate. Also, the medical secondary treatment for patients with STEMI has changed over the years, which could impact on the rate of events. Additionally, the method of data collection after 5 years represents a limitation, as follow-up events were identified retrospectively from national registries and medical records rather than through systematic prospective monitoring. Furthermore, after 5 years, events were not adjudicated by an independent CEC, which may have resulted in less precise event classification compared with the first 5 years.

Conclusions

One in five patients with STEMI treated with primary PCI experiences TLF within 10 years and TLF accounts for a significant proportion of any mortality, any MI or any revascularisation, with sustained occurrence rates beyond the first year.

circMAC: microRNA-conditioned binding-site localization on circular RNA isoforms
Authors: Juseong Kim, Sanghun Sel, Giltae Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCircular RNAs (circRNAs) regulate gene expression in part through interactions with microRNAs (miRNAs), but identifying miRNA binding sites on full-length circRNA isoforms remains challenging. Binding context can differ across circRNA isoforms, and sequence continuity across the back-splice junction may be overlooked when circRNAs are represented as linear transcripts. Existing circRNA–miRNA resources and computational approaches mainly support association-level prediction or rule-based candidate-site screening. Although rule-based tools can be applied to full-length circRNA sequences, they do not directly learn miRNA-conditioned nucleotide-level binding-site localization while preserving circular sequence continuity. Here, we formulate circRNA–miRNA binding-site prediction as a miRNA-conditioned sequence-labeling task and present circMAC, a purpose-built framework for nucleotide-level localization on full-length circRNA isoforms. Given a full-length circRNA isoform and a mature miRNA sequence, circMAC predicts a binding probability for each circRNA nucleotide. circMAC combines established sequence-modeling components in a task-specific architecture, including attention-based global context modeling, Mamba-based sequential modeling, and convolutional local motif extraction. Paired miRNA information is incorporated through cross-attention, allowing each circRNA nucleotide to be evaluated in a miRNA-specific context. We evaluated circMAC against pretrained RNA language models, conventional sequence encoders, alternative pretraining strategies, architectural ablations, and stricter isoform-disjoint and back-splice-junction-disjoint splits. circMAC showed improved nucleotide-level localization performance under the evaluated benchmark settings, while qualitative and aggregate analyses indicated concentration of prediction signals around annotated binding-site regions. These results support task-specific full-length circular isoform modeling for prioritizing candidate circRNA–miRNA binding-site regions.Keywords: circular RNA, microRNA, binding-site prediction, RNA–RNA interaction, nucleotide-level prediction

Introduction

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are covalently closed RNA molecules generated through back-splicing and function as post-transcriptional regulators in various biological and disease-related contexts [1, 2]. While circRNA–microRNA (miRNA) interactions are widely studied as a regulatory mechanism [3, 4], knowing only that an association exists provides a coarse description of the regulatory relationship. Mechanistic understanding requires identifying the specific nucleotide positions at which a miRNA binds within the full-length circular transcript. Nucleotide-level binding-site localization, therefore, enables more precise hypotheses about how circRNAs influence miRNA-mediated regulation.

This task is further complicated by isoform diversity. Long-read sequencing studies have shown that many circRNA loci produce multiple full-length isoforms with distinct exon compositions [5, 6]. As a result, the same back-splice junction (BSJ) can reside within different sequence contexts across isoforms, potentially leading to isoform-specific binding-site patterns. Analyses relying solely on host-gene or BSJ annotations may miss these regulatory differences. Moreover, because miRNA binding sites occupy short contiguous regions and the BSJ joins the two ends of the transcript into a continuous sequence, binding sites located near this junction can be split or distorted when a circRNA is represented as an ordinary linear transcript. With the increasing availability of full-length isoform annotations accompanied by Argonaute (AGO) cross-linking and immunoprecipitation (CLIP) evidence [7], there is a growing need for computational methods that localize miRNA binding sites at nucleotide resolution while respecting the circular topology of complete isoforms.

Existing approaches address related but distinct problems. Rule-based miRNA target predictors screen candidate target sites using seed complementarity, sequence pairing, thermodynamic stability, and accessibility-related criteria [8, 9]. These tools can in principle be applied to full-length circRNA sequences and remain useful for candidate-site screening. However, they are not supervised end-to-end models for miRNA-conditioned nucleotide-level localization on experimentally supported full-length circular isoforms. CircRNA-oriented databases and resources integrate annotations, predicted interactions, and CLIP evidence [10–12], but they generally provide pair-level associations or candidate interaction records rather than learned position-wise binding profiles over complete isoform sequences. Recent deep learning methods for circRNA–miRNA interaction prediction have explored pair-level association prediction using graph-based, sequence-embedding, and multi-source representation learning strategies [13–15]. However, these methods predict associations at the pair level—whether a given circRNA and miRNA interact—rather than localizing where binding occurs along the full-length circRNA isoform. Furthermore, they typically treat circular transcripts as ordinary linear token sequences and do not explicitly encode sequence continuity across the BSJ. Because the same circRNA isoform can present different candidate binding regions depending on the paired miRNA, nucleotide-level prediction must additionally be conditioned on a specific miRNA sequence.

To address these limitations, we formulate circRNA–miRNA binding-site prediction as a miRNA-conditioned nucleotide-level sequence-labeling problem on full-length circular isoforms. We introduce circMAC, a purpose-built framework designed for this task. circMAC combines established sequence-modeling components in a task-specific architecture, including attention-based global context modeling, Mamba-based sequential modeling, and circular convolutional local motif extraction. Circular padding in the convolutional branch preserves sequence continuity across the BSJ, while cross-attention integrates the paired miRNA sequence to enable miRNA-specific binding-site localization. The model is trained end-to-end on curated AGO-CLIP-supported binding-site annotations and systematically evaluated against pretrained RNA language models, conventional sequence encoders, rule-based target-site screening tools where applicable, alternative pretraining strategies, stricter disjoint-split settings, and architectural ablations.

The main contributions of this study are three-fold. First, we formulate circRNA–miRNA binding-site prediction as a miRNA-conditioned sequence-labeling task on full-length circRNA isoforms, enabling nucleotide-level localization across complete circular transcript sequences. Second, we develop a purpose-built framework that integrates established sequence-modeling components in a task-specific manner, combining global context modeling, sequential dependency modeling, local motif extraction, circular sequence handling, and miRNA-conditioned cross-attention. Third, through systematic evaluation against pretrained RNA language models, conventional sequence encoders, rule-based target-site screening tools where applicable, alternative pretraining strategies, stricter isoform-disjoint and back-splice-junction-disjoint evaluations, and architectural ablations, we show that task-specific full-length circular isoform modeling improves nucleotide-level circRNA–miRNA binding-site localization under the evaluated benchmark settings.

Methods

Dataset construction

We constructed an isoform-level dataset for miRNA-conditioned circRNA–miRNA binding-site localization. Full-length circRNA annotations were obtained from FL-circAS [7], including BSJ coordinates, exon structures, isoform annotations, and AGO-CLIP-supported miRNA binding-site information. Human reference genome sequences were obtained from GRCh38/hg38 through the UCSC Genome Browser [16], and mature human miRNA sequences were obtained from miRBase v22 [17]. Thus, each supervised example consisted of a full-length circRNA isoform sequence, a mature miRNA sequence, and a nucleotide-level binary binding-site mask over the circRNA sequence.

To obtain high-confidence full-length circRNA isoforms, we retained isoforms detected by both isoCirc [5] and CIRI-long [6], and restricted the analysis to canonical chromosomes chr1–chr22, chrX, and chrY. Full-length circRNA isoform sequences were primarily obtained from the strand-resolved sequence records provided by FL-circAS and converted to RNA sequences. For isoforms without sequence records in FL-circAS, sequences were reconstructed from the annotated exon coordinates in hg38 with strand orientation applied, such that minus-strand isoforms were reverse-complemented before transcription into RNA. Isoforms with sequence lengths inconsistent with their annotated exon coordinates were discarded. Distinct full-length isoforms sharing the same BSJ were retained as separate samples to preserve isoform-specific sequence variation.

For supervised binding-site prediction, circRNA–miRNA binding-site annotations were curated from FL-circAS. We retained an annotation only when it satisfied all of the following criteria: miRanda score ≥ 155, binding energy ≤ -20 kcal/mol, and at least one AGO-CLIP-supported evidence record [18, 19]. Mature miRNA sequences were matched to their corresponding identifiers using miRBase v22. Annotated binding-site coordinates were mapped onto the reconstructed full-length circRNA isoform sequences to generate binary nucleotide-level binding masks. Nucleotides overlapping retained binding-site annotations were assigned positive labels, whereas the remaining nucleotides within the same circRNA isoform were treated as background positions for nucleotide-level supervision. Because AGO-CLIP-derived annotations are incomplete and assay-dependent, background positions should not be interpreted as experimentally confirmed non-binding nucleotides. circMAC was, therefore, trained as a miRNA-conditioned nucleotide-level sequence-labeling model rather than as a pair-level interaction classifier.

When multiple binding-site annotations were available for the same circRNA–miRNA pair, their nucleotide-level masks were merged into a single binary mask by element-wise maximum. This design allows a single circRNA–miRNA example to contain multiple annotated binding segments when multiple site intervals are present on the same full-length isoform.

CircRNA isoforms shorter than 150 nt or longer than 1000 nt were excluded from the supervised dataset. This range was selected to retain sufficient sequence context for nucleotide-level localization while avoiding extremely short records and very long isoforms that would introduce substantial computational imbalance in full-length sequence labeling. The 1000 nt upper bound also maintained compatibility with the native context range of the long-context RNA language model baselines evaluated in this study. To assess the representativeness of this filtering criterion, we additionally summarized the length distribution of all full-length circRNA isoforms before and after filtering (Supplementary Fig. S3).

The original supervised evaluation used a pair-disjoint split, ensuring that no identical circRNA–miRNA pair appeared in both training and test sets. Because the task is miRNA-conditioned, the same circRNA isoform may appear with different miRNA partners across splits. We therefore interpret this setting as a seen-isoform/unseen-pair evaluation. After length filtering and binding-site mask construction, the pair-disjoint dataset contained 19 674 site-annotated training circRNA–miRNA pairs and 7838 site-annotated test pairs. Across the pair-disjoint dataset, ∼ 5.7% of nucleotide positions were labeled as binding sites (5.71% in training and 5.64% in test pairs), reflecting a strongly imbalanced nucleotide-level labeling problem. Summary statistics for the pair-disjoint dataset are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

To assess stricter supervised generalization, we additionally constructed isoform-disjoint and BSJ-disjoint splits. In the isoform-disjoint split, no circRNA isoform was shared between the training and test sets. In the BSJ-disjoint split, no BSJ was shared between the training and test sets; because each full-length isoform is associated with a specific BSJ, this setting also eliminated isoform overlap. These additional splits were used to evaluate whether circMAC can localize miRNA binding sites on circRNA isoforms and circular junctions not observed during supervised training. Distributions of binding-site nucleotide ratio and circRNA isoform length across the pair-disjoint, isoform-disjoint, and BSJ-disjoint settings are shown in Supplementary Fig. S4, and strict-split evaluation results are reported in Table 1 and Supplementary Table S5.

Model architecture

circMAC consists of a multi-branch circRNA encoder, a frozen pretrained RNA encoder for miRNA representation, a cross-attention interaction module, and a nucleotide-level site prediction head (Fig. 1B). Input embedding and multiscale processing

Each nucleotide in the circRNA sequence is mapped to a d-dimensional representation through a learned embedding layer. The embedded sequence is normalized with root mean square (RMS) normalization [20] and then downsampled by a factor of two using a strided depthwise 1D convolution (kernel size 3, stride 2, groups = d) followed by layer normalization. This global downsampling reduces the computational cost of subsequent encoder layers while preserving per-position information through a skip connection. After encoding, the sequence is upsampled back to the original length via linear interpolation followed by a pointwise convolution and layer normalization, and the skip connection from the original-resolution representation is added. The paired miRNA sequence is encoded separately using a frozen RNABERT encoder and projected to the shared d-dimensional representation space. RNABERT was selected for the miRNA branch because it was pretrained on human non-coding RNA sequences and is well suited to short RNA inputs such as mature miRNAs, which fall well within its native context length. The encoder was kept frozen to provide a stable pretrained miRNA representation while reducing the number of trainable parameters and the risk of overfitting.

Self-supervised pretraining objectives

To examine whether additional sequence or structural supervision improves circMAC representation learning, we evaluated three self-supervised pretraining objectives before supervised fine-tuning. These objectives were used to initialize the circMAC encoder and were compared in the pretraining analysis. Masked language modeling (MLM) [25] reconstructs masked nucleotide tokens and serves as a sequence-level pretraining baseline. Secondary-structure prediction (SSP) predicts whether each nucleotide is paired or unpaired, providing position-wise structural supervision [26]. Base-pairing prediction (BPP) reconstructs the full pairwise base-pairing matrix derived from predicted circRNA secondary structures [27] (Fig. 1C). Unlike SSP, which reduces structure to a per-nucleotide paired/unpaired label, BPP preserves pairwise nucleotide–nucleotide pairing relationships and was used as the main structure-aware pretraining objective. Experiments

Experimental settings

We evaluated circMAC on miRNA-conditioned nucleotide-level binding-site localization using the pair-disjoint train–test split described in the Dataset Construction section. After retaining annotated circRNA–miRNA pairs with curated binding-site masks, the original pair-disjoint supervised dataset contained 19 674 training pairs and 7838 test pairs.

CircRNA sequences were filtered to lengths between 150 and 1000 nucleotides and modeled with a maximum input length of 1022 tokens. The maximum miRNA length was set to 25 nucleotides. Unless otherwise specified, circMAC used a hidden dimension of 128, six encoder layers, eight attention heads, cross-attention for circRNA–miRNA interaction, and a Conv1D-based site prediction head. A frozen RNABERT encoder was used to obtain miRNA representations.

For the comparison with pretrained RNA language models, we used the best-performing circMAC configuration initialized with BPP pretraining. In contrast, the encoder architecture comparison used models trained from scratch to isolate the effect of the proposed multi-branch design. All models were trained with the AdamW optimizer using a learning rate of . Because binding-site labels are sparse, supervised training used a class-weighted focal loss with a focusing parameter of γ =2; background positions were subsampled during training to reduce the dominance of non-binding positions. The primary metric was F1-macro, and recall, precision, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), and area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC) were used as secondary metrics. Quantitative results are reported as the mean ± standard deviation over three independent runs with different random seeds.

The circMAC backbone contains ∼ 1.5 M trainable parameters, whereas the pretrained RNA language model backbones range from ∼ 0.5 M (RNABERT) to 99.5 M (RNA-FM). Detailed model configurations and parameter counts are provided in Supplementary Table S3.

In addition to the original pair-disjoint evaluation, we performed two stricter supervised generalization experiments using isoform-disjoint and BSJ-disjoint splits. For each split, models were trained from the corresponding training set and evaluated on the matched held-out test set. Evaluation was performed at the nucleotide level using the binding-site masks as targets. Strict-split results, including the Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC), are summarized in Table 1, and detailed supplementary results are provided in Supplementary Table S5.

To provide a fuller view of performance under sparse nucleotide-level labels, we additionally generated precision–recall curve and performed a label-noise sensitivity analysis on the original pair-disjoint benchmark. We also performed an aggregate localization-concentration analysis to quantify whether prediction mass was enriched around annotated binding-site neighborhoods relative to a uniform predictor. These analyses are reported in Supplementary Fig. S5 and Table S4.

For comparison with existing target-site screening approaches, we evaluated representative rule-based tools where applicable by converting their predicted target intervals into nucleotide-level binary masks and comparing them with the same annotated binding-site masks used for circMAC evaluation. Because these tools are designed primarily for candidate-site screening rather than supervised miRNA-conditioned nucleotide-level localization on full-length circular isoforms, we treated them as reference baselines rather than fully equivalent models.

Comparison with pretrained RNA language models

We first compared circMAC with representative pretrained RNA language models, including RNABERT [28], RNAErnie [29], multiple sequence alignment-based RNA language model (RNA-MSM) [30], and RNA foundation model (RNA-FM) [31]. Each RNA language model was evaluated under frozen and fine-tuned settings. For these baselines, the circRNA sequence was encoded using the corresponding pretrained RNA backbone, and the miRNA sequence was incorporated through the same downstream miRNA-interaction module and nucleotide-level prediction head whenever applicable. In the frozen setting, pretrained backbone parameters were fixed and only the miRNA interaction module and site prediction head were trained. In the fine-tuned setting, both the pretrained backbone and downstream modules were updated during supervised training.

To further examine the effect of sequence length on model applicability, we evaluated all models under three maximum input length settings: 440, 512, and 1000 tokens (Fig. 2A–C). These thresholds correspond to the native context limits of the pretrained RNA language model baselines: RNABERT supports sequences ∼ 440 tokens, and RNAErnie ∼ 512 tokens, whereas RNA-MSM (1024) and RNA-FM (1026) can process longer inputs. At shorter maximum lengths, where all models could be evaluated on the same subset of sequences, circMAC already outperformed the pretrained baselines. As the maximum length increased to 1000 tokens, RNABERT and RNAErnie could no longer process the longer circRNA isoforms, whereas circMAC remained applicable across the evaluated length settings and achieved the strongest performance on each length-restricted subset. This result highlights a practical advantage of circMAC: its architecture scales to the full-length circRNA isoforms encountered in real transcriptomic datasets without requiring sequence truncation.

As shown in Fig. 2, circMAC achieved the strongest performance across the evaluated sequence-length settings under the present benchmark. At the 1000-nt setting, circMAC reached an F1-macro of 0.762, AUROC of 0.902, and AUPRC of 0.545, whereas the best fine-tuned pretrained RNA language model under the same setting, RNA-FM, achieved an F1-macro of 0.638, AUROC of 0.748, and AUPRC of 0.297. This comparison should be interpreted as evaluating the benefit of task-specific design under the present benchmark setting, rather than as evidence that circMAC is universally superior to general-purpose RNA foundation models. The pretrained RNA language models evaluated here were developed for broad RNA representation learning objectives and were not specifically designed for miRNA-conditioned nucleotide-level localization on full-length circular isoforms. In addition, some models have native context-length constraints that limit their direct applicability to long circRNA sequences.

These results suggest that task-specific modeling tailored to full-length circRNA isoforms and miRNA-conditioned localization provides advantages over direct transfer of general-purpose pretrained RNA language models in this benchmark setting.

As an additional diagnostic analysis, we summarized aggregate localization-concentration behavior on the original pair-disjoint test set. circMAC assigned more prediction mass to annotated binding-site neighborhoods than expected under a uniform predictor (Supplementary Table S4).

Generalization under isoform-disjoint and BSJ-disjoint splits

To address potential isoform-level overlap in the original pair-disjoint setting, we evaluated circMAC under two stricter supervised generalization settings: isoform-disjoint and BSJ-disjoint splits. In the isoform-disjoint split, no circRNA isoform was shared between training and test sets. In the BSJ-disjoint split, no BSJ was shared between training and test sets. For each setting, models were trained from the corresponding training set and evaluated on the matched held-out test set.

For the main strict-split comparison, we focused on models that could be evaluated under the full-length input setting, including conventional sequence encoders and long-context RNA language model baselines. RNABERT and RNAErnie were excluded from this main comparison because their native context-length constraints prevent direct full-length evaluation for many circRNA isoforms; length-matched comparisons including these models are provided separately in Supplementary Table S7.

Under both isoform-disjoint and BSJ-disjoint settings, circMAC achieved the strongest overall performance among the evaluated full-length baselines (Table 1). Performance was lower than in the original pair-disjoint benchmark, indicating that nucleotide-level localization under isoform-disjoint and BSJ-disjoint evaluation settings is substantially more challenging. Nevertheless, circMAC maintained higher F1-macro, AUROC, AUPRC, F1-positive, and MCC than the evaluated Transformer, Hymba, RNA-FM, and RNA-MSM baselines. These results support the supervised generalization ability of the task-specific circMAC framework under stricter split settings, while also highlighting the difficulty of disjoint isoform- and junction-level evaluation.

Comparison with rule-based target-site screening tools

We further compared circMAC with representative rule-based target-site screening tools, including miRanda, RNAhybrid, TargetScan, PITA, and IntaRNA. Predicted target intervals from each tool were converted into nucleotide-level binary masks and evaluated against the same curated binding-site masks used for circMAC. This enabled comparison within a common nucleotide-level localization framework, while recognizing that these tools were originally designed for candidate-site screening or RNA–RNA interaction prediction rather than supervised miRNA-conditioned sequence labeling on full-length circRNA isoforms.

As shown in Table 2, circMAC achieved higher F1-macro, AUROC, and AUPRC than the evaluated rule-based target-site screening tools on the original pair-disjoint benchmark. Among the rule-based methods, PITA achieved the highest F1-macro (0.6377) and AUPRC (0.1983), whereas RNAhybrid achieved the highest AUROC (0.7241). The underlying prediction characteristics also differed substantially across tools. For example, PITA showed high positive-class precision (0.8012) but relatively low recall (0.1850), indicating conservative candidate-site prediction, whereas miRanda showed very high recall (0.9604) but low precision (0.0574), resulting in substantially broader predictions. These results highlight the differing precision–recall trade-offs of conventional screening approaches. Under the present benchmark, circMAC provided stronger overall nucleotide-level localization performance, while rule-based tools remain useful because they provide interpretable sequence-pairing, thermodynamic, and accessibility-related criteria without requiring supervised training.

Effect of pretraining objective and encoder architecture

We next examined whether self-supervised pretraining provides additional benefit to circMAC. We compared four configurations: circMAC trained from scratch, circMAC pretrained with MLM, circMAC pretrained with SSP, and circMAC pretrained with BPP. MLM serves as a sequence-level pretraining baseline, SSP provides position-wise structural supervision, and BPP provides pairwise structural supervision by reconstructing a nucleotide-level base-pairing matrix.

As shown in Table 3, BPP achieved the best overall performance among the evaluated pretraining objectives, with an F1-macro of 0.7618, recall of 0.7637, AUROC of 0.9017, and AUPRC of 0.5454. MLM also improved over training from scratch in F1-macro and AUPRC, whereas SSP did not improve performance over the non-pretrained model. A plausible explanation is that the coarse per-nucleotide paired/unpaired labels used in SSP carry less binding-relevant information than the explicit nucleotide–nucleotide pairing relationships encoded by the BPP matrix. These results indicate that pairwise structural supervision can provide useful additional signals for binding-site localization, although this pretraining effect should be interpreted as complementary to the main architectural contribution of circMAC.

We also compared circMAC with general sequence encoders trained from scratch, including LSTM [32], Transformer [21], Mamba [23], and Hymba [33]. For this comparison, circMAC was also trained from scratch to isolate the effect of the multi-branch encoder architecture from the effect of self-supervised pretraining. circMAC achieved the strongest overall performance among the encoder architectures, with an F1-macro of 0.7458, recall of 0.7479, AUROC of 0.8987, and AUPRC of 0.5190. These results support the multi-branch design of circMAC and suggest that combining sequential modeling, global context modeling, and local motif extraction is more effective than relying on a single sequence encoder.

Ablation study of circMAC components

To assess the contribution of individual circMAC components, we performed module-level ablation experiments using the non-pretrained circMAC configuration. This setting was used to isolate the architectural contribution of each component from the effect of self-supervised pretraining. We compared the full circMAC architecture with variants that removed the attention, convolutional, or Mamba branch. We also evaluated single-branch variants using only Mamba, only convolution, or only attention.

As shown in Fig. 3A, the full circMAC architecture achieved the strongest overall performance among the evaluated ablation variants. Removing the attention branch caused only a modest reduction, suggesting that global content-dependent attention plays a complementary role when sequential modeling and local motif extraction are both present. In contrast, removing either the convolutional branch or the Mamba branch caused more substantial degradation. The model without the convolutional branch dropped to an F1-macro of 0.698 and an AUPRC of 0.390, whereas the model without the Mamba branch dropped to an F1-macro of 0.679 and an AUPRC of 0.378. The single-branch variants were consistently weaker than the full model, supporting the benefit of combining local motif extraction, sequential dependency modeling, and global context modeling.

We further evaluated the circRNA–miRNA interaction mechanism and the site prediction head. For the interaction mechanism, we compared cross-attention with two alternatives: concatenation of pooled miRNA and circRNA representations, and elementwise multiplication of pooled miRNA features with per-position circRNA features. As shown in Fig. 3B, cross-attention achieved the strongest performance among the evaluated interaction mechanisms, indicating that nucleotide-level binding-site prediction benefits from explicitly conditioning each circRNA position on the paired miRNA representation. As shown in Fig. 3C, the Conv1D site prediction head slightly outperformed the linear head, suggesting that local contextual refinement at the output stage provides additional benefit for nucleotide-level binding-site localization.

Qualitative analysis of nucleotide-level prediction behavior

To further examine nucleotide-level prediction behavior and model interpretability, we performed qualitative case analyses on three circRNA–miRNA pairs: circCDYL2–miR-34c-5p, circMAPK1–miR-12119, and circHUWE1–miR-29b-3p. These cases were selected as diagnostic examples to visualize model behavior across different sequence lengths and binding-site configurations, rather than as exhaustive evidence of biological relevance or as representative samples of all test pairs. The selected circRNAs ranged from 219 to 956 nt and included both junction-proximal and internal binding-site configurations.

The circCDYL2–miR-34c-5p pair represents a junction-proximal case. The circRNA sequence is 219 nt long, and the annotated binding region is located at positions 179–210, close to the BSJ. This case is therefore useful for assessing whether a model can localize binding sites near the circular boundary. circCDYL2 (hsa:circ_0004087) has been reported to promote cancer-related phenotypes, including colorectal cancer cell migration via the Ezrin/AKT axis and trastuzumab resistance in HER2-positive breast cancer [34, 35]. The miR-34 family, including miR-34c-5p, is a well-established p53-pathway effector with tumor-suppressive roles; miR-34c-5p has been shown to inhibit glioma cell proliferation and invasion [36]. These prior reports provide biological context for the selected pair but should not be interpreted as experimental validation of the specific circCDYL2–miR-34c-5p interaction analyzed here.

In contrast, circMAPK1–miR-12119 and circHUWE1–miR-29b-3p represent internal binding-site configurations within medium- and long-length circRNA contexts, respectively. circMAPK1 (hsa:circ_0004872) has been reported to suppress gastric cancer progression by encoding a peptide that competitively binds MEK1 and blocks ERK phosphorylation [37]. circHUWE1 has been implicated in multiple cancer-related processes, including colorectal cancer progression and cisplatin resistance in non-small cell lung cancer [38, 39]. In addition, circHUWE1 has been experimentally shown to sponge miR-29b and regulate the AKT3 axis during bovine myoblast development, validated by dual-luciferase assay and AGO2-RIP [40]. Although this interaction was characterized in a bovine model, hsa-miR-29b-3p and bta-miR-29b share an identical mature sequence according to miRBase v22. We therefore used the circHUWE1–miR-29b-3p pair as a biologically contextualized diagnostic example, while avoiding claims that the human interaction is experimentally validated in this study.

For each case, we compared circMAC with fine-tuned pretrained RNA language model baselines using nucleotide-level prediction heatmaps and case-level localization metrics. As shown in Fig. 4A, circMAC produced concentrated prediction signals around annotated binding regions. In the junction-proximal circCDYL2–miR-34c-5p case, circMAC assigned high probability scores near the annotated BSJ-adjacent region, indicating localized prediction behavior in a boundary-proximal example. In the internal binding-site cases, circMAPK1–miR-12119 and circHUWE1–miR-29b-3p, circMAC also produced localized high-probability predictions around the annotated binding regions. By contrast, several pretrained RNA language model baselines showed more diffuse, scattered, or weakly localized prediction patterns across the circRNA sequence.

Representation analysis of miRNA-conditioned embeddings

We next investigated the latent spaces of the models to examine whether miRNA conditioning changes nucleotide-level representation patterns around annotated binding-site regions. Using the same three diagnostic cases described above, we extracted interaction-aware embeddings and visualized them using UMAP [41]. We compared representations before and after miRNA conditioning with reference to the annotated binding-site labels.

As shown in Fig. 5, miRNA conditioning reorganized the circMAC embedding space in the selected cases, with annotated binding-site nucleotides becoming more visually concentrated after conditioning. In contrast, the pretrained RNA language model baselines showed less organized representation patterns, with annotated binding-site positions often remaining more diffusely distributed. The corresponding label–prediction agreement is shown in Supplementary Fig. S8, where high predicted binding probabilities tend to concentrate near regions occupied by annotated binding-site nucleotides. These UMAP analyses provide qualitative support that the miRNA-conditioned representations learned by circMAC are aligned with its nucleotide-level prediction behavior.

Aggregate localization-concentration analysis

To complement the selected qualitative cases and reduce the potential influence of case-selection bias, we performed an aggregate localization-concentration analysis across the original pair-disjoint test set using circMAC and representative full-length-compatible baseline models. As reported in Supplementary Table S4, circMAC showed the highest mean localization concentration among the evaluated full-length-compatible models across all three radii, with NLS values of 0.201, 0.262, and 0.280 at 0, 10, and 20 nt, respectively. Mamba showed the next highest localization concentration across the evaluated radii. These aggregate results provide dataset-level support that the focused prediction patterns observed in the qualitative examples are not solely attributable to the selected cases. We nevertheless interpret the qualitative examples as diagnostic visualizations rather than as representative biological validation.

Discussion

In this study, we proposed circMAC, a purpose-built framework for miRNA-conditioned nucleotide-level circRNA–miRNA binding-site localization on full-length circRNA isoforms. The central motivation is that circRNA–miRNA regulatory analysis should move beyond coarse-grained association-level descriptions and instead localize binding-associated positions on full-length isoform sequences. Because circRNA loci can produce multiple isoforms, models that rely only on host-gene labels, circRNA identifiers, BSJs, or short sequence windows may miss isoform-specific binding patterns. The contribution of circMAC should be interpreted as a task-specific integration of established sequence-modeling components rather than as the introduction of entirely new architectural building blocks. circMAC addresses this task by combining circular sequence handling, complementary sequence-modeling branches, miRNA-conditioned cross-attention, and nucleotide-level site prediction. Although miRNA sponging is one of the best-known functional models for circRNAs, not all circRNAs are expected to act as effective miRNA sponges [42, 43]. Therefore, circMAC should be viewed as a binding-site localization model rather than as direct confirmation of sponge activity.

Importance of full-length isoform-level modeling

circMAC showed improved localization performance over the evaluated pretrained RNA language model baselines under both frozen and fine-tuned settings. This comparison should be interpreted as evaluating the value of task-specific modeling under the present benchmark setting, rather than as evidence that circMAC is universally superior to general-purpose RNA foundation models. General RNA representations are useful, but they are not specifically optimized for position-wise prediction conditioned on a paired miRNA sequence. In addition, some pretrained RNA language models have native context-length constraints that limit their direct applicability to long full-length circRNA isoforms. The same circRNA isoform may contain different candidate binding regions depending on the paired miRNA, highlighting the importance of miRNA-conditioned modeling. Future work should include more controlled comparisons with long-context RNA foundation models adapted to the same miRNA-conditioned localization setting.

Although circMAC concentrates prediction signals around annotated binding regions rather than producing diffuse sequence-wide activation, the selected cases should be interpreted as diagnostic visualizations rather than as representative evidence across all test pairs. To address this limitation, we quantified aggregate localization concentration across the original pair-disjoint test set using localization probability mass and chance-corrected normalized localization scores and compared these patterns with representative full-length-compatible baselines (Supplementary Table S4). The aggregate analysis showed that circMAC concentrates prediction probability around annotated binding-site neighborhoods at the dataset level, providing quantitative support beyond the selected qualitative examples.

Multi-branch encoder and miRNA-conditioned interaction

The encoder comparison and ablation studies clarify how established architectural components contribute when combined in the circMAC task-specific framework. circMAC improved over the evaluated single-strategy encoders, including LSTM, Transformer, Mamba, and Hymba, suggesting that the complementary integration of multiple sequence-modeling strategies is beneficial for this task. Although attention, Mamba-based sequence modeling, convolution, and cross-attention are established techniques, their combination in circMAC is tailored to the requirements of full-length circular isoform modeling: global context modeling, efficient sequential dependency modeling, local motif extraction around short binding sites, preservation of BSJ continuity, and miRNA-specific conditioning. The ablation study further showed that the Mamba and convolutional branches are particularly important, suggesting that long-range sequential modeling and local motif extraction provide complementary signals for binding-site localization.

Cross-attention provided a clear benefit by allowing each circRNA position to attend to the paired miRNA sequence, which is important for miRNA-dependent binding-site localization. The Conv1D site head yielded a modest improvement over the linear alternative by refining local prediction patterns across short contiguous binding regions. Although the RNABERT-based miRNA encoder was kept frozen in the present study, fine-tuning it may provide additional task-specific adaptation and should be systematically evaluated in future work.

The pretraining analysis showed that BPP provided the strongest initialization among the evaluated self-supervised objectives by providing pairwise structural supervision distinct from the nucleotide-level labels used during downstream training. By contrast, SSP did not improve over training from scratch, suggesting that the form of structural supervision—pairwise rather than per-position—may matter for binding-site localization. A plausible explanation is that coarse per-nucleotide paired/unpaired labels may discard binding-relevant nucleotide–nucleotide pairing relationships, whereas the BPP objective preserves pairwise structural information that may better reflect local accessibility and structural context. However, this result is complementary to the main contribution of circMAC: the primary contribution lies in the task formulation and task-specific integration of circular isoform modeling with miRNA-conditioned nucleotide-level localization, while structure-aware pretraining offers an additional avenue for improving encoder representations.

Secondary structure can influence miRNA binding by modulating local target-site accessibility. Although miRNA recognition is largely driven by sequence complementarity, especially seed pairing, a complementary region must be sufficiently accessible to participate in intermolecular base pairing. Binding sites may therefore be affected by local single-strandedness, loop exposure, or reduced structural occlusion, although they are not restricted exclusively to unstructured regions. In this study, structure-aware pretraining was used to encourage the circRNA encoder to capture structural constraints that may affect pre-binding accessibility. circMAC does not explicitly model miRNA-induced structural rearrangement after binding, which remains an important direction for future work.

Limitations and future work

This study has several limitations. First, the supervised labels were derived from curated binding-site annotations supported by computational prediction and AGO-CLIP evidence. Although these annotations provide useful training signals, CLIP-derived sites are subject to experimental and computational biases [44] and may contain false positives or incomplete coverage. Positions treated as background may include unannotated functional binding sites, introducing label noise into nucleotide-level supervision. This noise may partly contribute to the moderate AUPRC values observed across all models, as unannotated true binding positions scored as false positives would deflate precision-based metrics even when predictions are biologically plausible. To provide a fuller view of this issue, we added a precision–recall curve and a label-noise sensitivity analysis for the original pair-disjoint benchmark (Supplementary Fig. S5). These analyses show the precision–recall trade-off beyond summary AUPRC values and illustrate how simulated missing-positive annotations can reduce precision-based metrics. Accordingly, circMAC predictions should be interpreted as prioritization of candidate binding-site regions rather than direct evidence of functional repression.

Second, the original pair-disjoint split evaluates a seen-isoform/unseen-pair setting, in which a circRNA isoform may appear with different miRNA partners across training and test sets. To address the possibility that this setting overestimates supervised generalization beyond previously observed circRNA isoforms, we added isoform-disjoint and BSJ-disjoint evaluations. These stricter settings remove supervised overlap at the isoform level and BSJ level, respectively, and therefore provide a more rigorous assessment under held-out isoform and held-out junction settings. We note, however, that these evaluations do not necessarily eliminate all host-gene-level overlap, and they should not be interpreted as fully host-gene-disjoint or fully locus-disjoint tests. Fully host-gene-disjoint evaluation remains an important direction for future work. The 150–1000 nt length range covers 89.2% of the full-length circRNA isoforms considered before filtering, but the exclusion of unusually short or long isoforms may still limit generalization to these less represented sequence-length regimes.

Third, the structural pretraining depends on computationally predicted secondary structures rather than experimentally resolved ones. RNAfold-based annotations provide scalable supervision but may not capture cell-type-specific conformations, RNA-binding protein effects, or miRNA-induced structural rearrangements. Incorporating experimentally derived structure probing data could improve the biological fidelity of structure-aware pretraining in future work.

Fourth, circMAC currently relies primarily on sequence and predicted secondary-structure information. Additional biological context—such as tissue-specific expression, AGO occupancy, RNA-binding protein co-occupancy, sequence conservation, or subcellular localization—may further improve prediction accuracy. Future work should evaluate circMAC on independent AGO-CLIP datasets, cell-type-specific CLIP profiles, or experimentally validated perturbation datasets. Experimental validation of high-confidence predictions would also strengthen biological utility.

Finally, we used FL-circAS as the primary supervised data source because circMAC requires full-length circRNA isoform sequences linked to nucleotide-level AGO-CLIP-supported binding-site annotations. Other circRNA–miRNA resources and target-prediction databases provide valuable candidate interactions, but they are often prediction-derived, pair-level, or not directly connected to full-length isoform-specific binding intervals. Incorporating such predictions as supervised labels could introduce circularity or label contamination. Therefore, we used these resources primarily for contextual comparison and rule-based reference analyses where applicable. Integrating additional experimentally supported full-length circRNA–miRNA binding-site datasets will be an important direction for future work.

Conclusion

We presented circMAC, a purpose-built framework for miRNA-conditioned nucleotide-level circRNA–miRNA binding-site localization on full-length circRNA isoforms. By combining established sequence-modeling components in a task-specific architecture with circular sequence handling and miRNA-conditioned cross-attention, circMAC predicts binding-associated positions along complete circRNA isoform sequences. Across systematic comparisons with pretrained RNA language models, conventional sequence encoders, rule-based target-site screening tools where applicable, pretraining objectives, stricter isoform-disjoint and BSJ-disjoint evaluations, and architectural ablations, circMAC showed improved localization performance under the evaluated benchmark settings. These results support the utility of task-specific full-length circular isoform modeling for prioritizing candidate circRNA–miRNA binding-site regions.

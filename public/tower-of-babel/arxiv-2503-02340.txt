1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Given
n\geq 2
and
1<p<n,
we denote by
\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under a specific seminorm defined as

\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Du|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Additionally, denote by
W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the dual space of
\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
q=\frac{p}{p-1}.

Given
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p},
the
p
-Sobolev inequality states the existence of a largest positive constant
S_{n,\,p}=S(n,p)>0
such that

\|Du\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq S_{n,\,p}\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(1.1)

We refer to this constant
S_{n,\,p}
as the
optimal Sobolev constant
. Furthermore, according to the works of Aubin
[
1
]
and Talenti
[
17
]
, the set of all extremal functions for which the inequality in (
1.1
) becomes an equality forms a specific
(n+2)
-dimensional manifold given by:

\mathscr{M}=\left\{u\in{\dot{W}}^{1,\,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon u(x)=\frac{a}{\left(1+b|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{p}}},a\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},b>0,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

On the other hand, up to a scaling factor, the corresponding Euler–Lagrange equation of (
1.1
) is

-\Delta_{p}v:=-{\rm div}(|Dv|^{p-2}Dv)=|v|^{p^{*}-2}v\quad\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.2)

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the
(n+1)
-dimensional manifold as follows

\mathcal{M}=\left\{aU[z,\lambda]\colon U[z,\lambda]=\frac{\lambda^{\frac{n-p}{p}}}{\left(1+\lambda^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|x-z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{p}}},\ \lambda>0,\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\},

where
a\in(0,+\infty)
is chosen such that every
v\in\mathcal{M}
satisfies

\|Dv\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=S_{n,\,p}^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\|v\|^{p^{*}}_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=S_{n,\,p}^{n}.

(1.3)

According to
[
1
,
7
,
17
]
,
\mathcal{M}
is the space of all positive solutions of (
1.2
). Functions in
\mathcal{M}
are commonly referred to as
Talenti bubbles
.

Given that the minimizers of (
1.1
) and all positive solutions of (
1.2
) are known, it is indeed natural to investigate their corresponding stability.

1.2.
Stability of the Sobolev energy

It follows from the concentration-compactness theorem that, when the Sobolev energy

S_{n,\,p}(u):=\frac{\|Du\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}

approaches
S_{n,\,p}
, then
u
is close to
\mathscr{M}
, qualitatively.

Brezis and Lieb
[
3
]
were the first to address the stability problem, aiming to quantify this closeness in the case where
p=2
. Specifically, for a function
u\in\dot{W}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the goal is to establish the following quantitative inequality:

S_{n,\,p}(u)-S_{n,\,p}\geq c(n,\,p)\omega(d(u,\,\mathscr{M})),

(1.4)

where
\omega
is a sharp modulus of continuity,
d(u,\,\mathscr{M})
is an optimal distance from
u
to
\mathscr{M}.

Typically,
\omega
is expected to be of the polynomial form
t^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
. The challenge usually lies in finding a sharp exponent
\alpha
.

This problem was completely settled by Bianchi and Egnell
[
2
]
a few years later. They utilized the Hilbert structure of
\dot{W}^{1,\,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to prove the existence of a constant
C_{BE}=C_{BE}(n)>0
such that, for any
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

S_{n,\,2}(u)-S_{n,\,2}\geq C_{BE}{\inf_{v\in\mathscr{M}}}\|D(u-v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

It is worth noting that the exponent
2
on the right-hand side of this inequality is sharp, and the choice of the distance is optimal. Moreover, obtaining an explicit bound of
C_{BE}
remained an open problem for a long time, primarily due to the limitations imposed by Lion’s concentration and compactness principle. This problem was recently settled by Dolbeault, Esteban, Figalli, Frank, Loss
[
9
]
, who established a lower bound for

C_{BE}
using competing symmetries, continuous Steiner symmetrization, and a refined expansion near the manifold of minimizers.

For general exponents
1<p<n,
Cianchi, Fusco, Maggi and Pratelli
[
4
]
first proved a stability inequality of the form (
1.4
). Their inequality involved an explicit moduli of continuity

\omega=t^{\alpha}
with
\alpha=\alpha(n,\,p)>0
and a distance given by the
L^{p^{*}}
-norm of functions (rather than their gradients).
Subsequently, Figalli, Maggi, and Pratelli
[
11
]
proved a version of stability inequality (
1.4
) for the special case
p=1.

Building upon the results of
[
4
]
, Figalli and Neumayer
[
12
]
, as well as Neumayer
[
15
]
, established versions of the stability inequality for
p\geq 2
and
1<p<2
, respectively, with the optimal distance function associated with the
L^{p}
-norm of the gradient. Unfortunately, the exponents in their inequalities were not optimal. To address this issue, Figalli and Zhang
[
13
]
proved stability with the optimal gradient distance and the sharp exponent
\max\{p,\,2\}
for all
1<p<n
. They achieved this by establishing new vectorial inequalities and proving some corresponding spectral gap estimates.

1.3.
Stability of the Euler Lagrange equation to the Sobolev inequality

Alternatively, one can perturb the Euler–Lagrange equation instead of the Sobolev energy. Struwe
[
16
]
presented a fundamental theorem that studied the decomposition of nonnegative solutions that ”almost” solve the Euler–Lagrange equation. Later, Mercuri and Willem
[
14
]
extended Struwe’s result to the general case
1<p<n
. They provided a more comprehensive understanding of the behavior of solutions to the Euler–Lagrange equation.
We summarize both of their results as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2,

1<p<n
,
q
the Hölder dual of
p
, and
\nu\geq 1
be positive integers. Assume that
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a sequence of nonnegative functions such that

\left(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\right)S^{n}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Du_{k}|^{p}dx\leq\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)S^{n}

and

\|\Delta_{p}u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p^{*}-2}u_{k}\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\ k\rightarrow 0.

Then, there exists a sequence
(z_{1}^{(k)},\cdots,z_{\nu}^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\nu
-tuples of points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and sequences
(\lambda_{1}^{(k)},\cdots,\lambda_{\nu}^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}},(a_{1}^{(k)},\cdots,a_{\nu}^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\nu
-tuples of positive real numbers such that

\left\|D\left(u_{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}a_{i}^{(k)}U[z_{i}^{(k)},\lambda_{i}^{(k)}]\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\ k\rightarrow\infty.

Similar to the stability of the Sobolev energy, there is considerable interest in quantifying Theorem
1.1
for its applications in geometry, particularly in the context of Yamabe flows. When
p=2
, the pioneering work by Ciraolo, Figalli, Maggi
[
5
]
provided a sharp quantitative estimate of Struwe’s decomposition in the case where
\nu=1
. More precisely, they proved that

\|Du-a_{1}DU[z,\lambda]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\Delta u+u|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\|_{W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Furthermore, using a localization argument and by computing the interactions between bubbling phenomena, Figalli and Glaudo
[
10
]
demonstrated that the linear control obtained in the single bubble case also extends to the multiple bubbles case when
3\leq n\leq 5
, i.e.

\|Du-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}a_{i}DU[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\Delta u+u|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\|_{W^{-1,\,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

However, they pointed out that, when
n\geq 6
, one cannot expect such a linear control anymore. Despite this challenge, the problem was eventually resolved by the remarkable work of Deng, Sun, and Wei
[
8
]
. They employed a finite-dimensional reduction method to overcome the difficulties associated with higher dimensions and provided a comprehensive solution to the problem in this range:

\|Du-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}a_{i}DU[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,\,p)\begin{cases}\Gamma|\log\Gamma|^{\frac{1}{2}},&n=6,\\
\Gamma^{\frac{n+2}{2(n-2)}},&n\geq 7,\end{cases}

where
\Gamma=\|\Delta u+u|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\|_{W^{-1,\,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

However, for the case
1<p<n
, a quantified version of Theorem
1.1
remained elusive. Indeed, compared to the case
p=2,
three main challenges arise:

Lack of Hilbert Space Structure:
When
p=2,
the Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Hilbert space, allowing one to find
v
such that
u-v
is orthogonal to the tangent space of
\mathcal{M}
at
v
via projection. However, for general
p,
the Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is merely a Banach space. To construct a Hilbert space structure, it is crucial to give a suitable notion of orthogonality. This issue has been addressed in
[
12
, Section 2]
. Additionally, even if
\|Du-Dv\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\hat{\epsilon}
for any
\hat{\epsilon}>0,
it does not necessarily follow that
v
is orthogonal to the tangent space of
\mathcal{M}
at
v.
This difficulty was overcome in
[
13
, Lemma 4.1]
, which ensures the existence of the desired
v\in\mathcal{M}
by introducing a new minimization principle.

Nonlinearity of the
p
-Laplacian:
When
p=2,
the
p
-Laplacian reduces to the classical Laplacian, enabling a complete decomposition of the main term and the error term due to its linearity. However, for
1<p<n,
the
p
-Laplacian is nonlinear, which poses challenges in expanding the term
|Du|^{p-2}Du\cdot D(u-v).
When
p\geq 2,
as noted in
[
18
, Remark 1.17]
, a standard Taylor expansion yields the sharp stability estimates only under certain restrictions on
|Du-Dv|.
For
p<2,
as explained in
[
13
, Section 1.3]
, the
\dot{W}^{1,p}
norm does not control any weighted
\dot{W}^{1,2}
norm, preventing a second order expansion of
|Du|^{p-2}Du\cdot D(u-v).
Moreover, for
p\leq\frac{2n}{n+2},
the
L^{p^{*}}
norm fails to control any weighted
L^{2}
norm, making the expansion of
|u|^{p^{*}-2}u(u-v)
more challenging. These difficulties compel us to find new approaches for expanding
|Du|^{p-2}Du\cdot D(u-v)
in regimes
p\in(1,2)
and
p\in[2,n),
as well as for expanding
|u|^{p^{*}-2}u(u-v)
in
p\in(1,\frac{2n}{n+2}]
and
\ p\in(\frac{2n}{n+2},n).

Compactness and Spectral Analysis:
For
p=2,
compactness results such as the Rellich–Kondrachov Theorem and the spectral analysis of the classical Laplacian are readily available. However, for the case
1<p<n
, extra techniques are required. Inspired by
[
13
, Lemma 2.1, Lemma 2.4]
, we establish new vectorial inequalities by replacing original weight
|x|^{p-2}
with suitable
\omega_{j}\ (j=1,\cdots,4)
as in Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
. Then, to get the sharp stability results, it is crucial to refine the corresponding spectral gap inequalities under this small perturbation. One of the main difficulties in this process is to establish new compact embedding theorems. Fortunately, in our case, the compactness results from
[
13
, Proposition 3.1, Lemma 3.4, Corollary 3.5]
provide a foundation for deriving the desired spectral gap-type estimates, see Proposition
3.4
.

In this manuscript, we focus on studying the stability of the Euler–Lagrange equation associated with the
p
-Sobolev inequality when
\nu=1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists
\delta=\delta(n,p)>0
small enough, such that the following statement holds: If
u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
\|Du-D\tilde{U}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\delta,
where
\tilde{U}\in\mathcal{M},
then there exist
v\in\mathcal{M}
and
C=C(n,p)>0,
such that
u-v
is orthogonal to
T_{v}\mathcal{M}
and

\|Du-Dv\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\max\{1,p-1\}}\leq C\|P(u)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
P(u)=-{\rm div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)-|u|^{p^{*}-2}u.

Our theorem is also partially motivated by a problem raised in the pre-printed online version of
[
18
, Remark 1.17]
. In this intriguing manuscript, the authors studied the stability of the Euler–Lagrange equation associated with the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality, which can be viewed as a weighted version of the Sobolev inequality. We believe that with suitable modifications, our method has the potential to address their problem as well. Moreover, the sharpness of the exponent
1
when
p<2
in Theorem
1.2
follows from a smooth perturbation of
v
, and that of the exponent
p-1
when
p\geq 2
follows similarly as the one in
[
18
, Remark 1.18]
.

Combining Theorem
1.2
with Theorem
1.1
, similar to
[
10
, Corollary 3.4]
, we can directly derive the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 2.
For any nonnegative function
u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\frac{1}{2}S^{n}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Du|^{p}dx\leq\frac{3}{2}S^{n},

there exists
C=C(n,p)>0,
such that

\|Du-Dv\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\max\{1,p-1\}}\leq C\|P(u)\|_{W^{-1,\,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
shares similarities with the one employed in
[
13
]
, but it includes non-trivial modifications tailored to our specific problem. Specifically, instead of relying on
[
13
, Lemma 2.1]
, we establish a series of new vectorial inequalities, denoted as Lemma
2.1
, which are compatible with our problem. These inequalities provide an improved version of the vectorial inequalities presented in
[
18
, Lemma 4.2]
. Additionally, we prove a result concerning the new spectral gap associated with these vectorial inequalities, as stated in Proposition
3.4
. These modifications and additions are crucial for deriving our main theorem and addressing the stability of the Euler–Lagrange equation in the context of the
p
-Sobolev inequality.

While completing this manuscript, we became aware of the recent preprint by Ciraolo and Gatti
[
6
]
, which provides an alternative proof of the stability result in our setting. Although their exponent is not sharp, their method appears to have potential for generalization to other contexts, such as the anisotropic setting.

This paper is organized as follows. We establish a series of vectorial inequalities in Section 2, and prove the corresponding spectral gap estimates Section 3. In the last section, we prove our main result Theorem
1.2
.

Notation
. In our estimates, we often express positive constants as
C(\cdot)
and
c(\cdot)
, with the parentheses enclosing all the parameters upon which the constant depends. Typically, we reserve
C
for constants greater than
1
and
c
for constants less than 1. When the constant is absolute, we omit the parentheses and simply write
C
or
c
. It is important to note that the value of
C(\cdot)
may differ in various instances, even within a single chain of inequalities.

2.
Sharp Vector inequalities in Euclidean Spaces

This section aims at establishing sharp vector inequalities, following a similar idea of
[
13
, Section 2]
: For fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
by introducing weights
\omega_{j}\ (j=1,\cdots,4)
related to the sizes of both
|x|
and
|x+y|,
we construct a quadratic-type expression plus a positive extra term that control
|x+y|^{p-2}(x+y)\cdot x-|x|^{p-2}x\cdot y
from below, which improves
[
18
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\kappa>0.
The following inequalities hold.

(1)

For
1<p<2,
there exists a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(p,\kappa)>0
such that

\displaystyle|x+y|^{p-2}(x+y)\cdot y\geq

\displaystyle\ |x|^{p-2}x\cdot y+(1-\kappa){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\omega_{1}}|y|^{2}+(p-2)(1-\kappa){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\omega_{2}}\big(|x|-|x+y|)^{2}

\displaystyle+c_{1}\min\{|y|^{p},|x|^{p-2}|y|^{2}\}\big).

(2.1)

where

\omega_{1}=\omega_{1}(x,x+y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|x+y|^{p-2}&\text{if}\ |x|\leq|x+y|\\
|x|^{p-2}&\text{ if }\ |x+y|\leq|x|\end{array}\right.

and

\omega_{2}=\omega_{2}(x,x+y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{|x+y|^{p-1}}{(2-p)|x+y|+(p-1)|x|}&\text{if}\ |x|\leq|x+y|\\
|x|^{p-2}&\text{ if }\ |x+y|\leq|x|\end{array}\right..

(2)

For
p\geq 2,
there exist constants
c_{2}=c_{2}(p,\kappa)>0
and
c_{3}=c_{3}(p)>0
such that

\displaystyle|x+y|^{p-2}(x+y)\cdot y\geq

\displaystyle\ |x|^{p-2}x\cdot y+(1-\kappa)\omega_{3}|y|^{2}+(p-2)(1-\kappa)\omega_{4}\big(|x|-|x+y|)^{2}

\displaystyle+c_{2}|y|^{p}.

(2.2)

where

\omega_{3}=\omega_{3}(x,x+y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|x|^{p-2}&\text{if}\ |x|\leq|x+y|\\
\frac{|x+y|^{p-1}}{|x|}&\text{ if }\ c_{3}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}|x|\leq|x+y|\leq|x|\\
c_{3}|x|^{p-2}&\text{if}\ |x+y|\leq c_{3}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}|x|\end{array}\right.

and

\omega_{4}=\omega_{4}(x,x+y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|x|^{p-2}&\text{if}\ |x|\leq|x+y|\\
\frac{|x+y|^{p-1}}{|x|}&\text{ if }\ |x+y|\leq|x|\end{array}\right..

Moreover, for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
it holds

c_{3}|x|^{p-2}\leq\omega_{3}(x,x+y).

(2.3)

Proof.

When
|x|=0,
it is trivial since both
(1)
and
(2)
are reduced to
|y|^{p}\geq 0.
Subsequently, we assume that
|x|\neq 0
and divide the proof into two steps.

Step
1:
We first construct a quadratic lower bound of

|x+y|^{p-2}(x+y)\cdot y-|x|^{p-2}x\cdot y
for
1<p<n.

It is divided into two cases.

Case
(1):

1<p<2.
When
|x+y|\leq|x|,
we claim that

|x+y|^{p-2}(x+y)\cdot y\geq|x|^{p-2}x\cdot y+|x|^{p-2}|y|^{2}+(p-2)|x|^{p-2}(|x|-|x+y|)^{2}.

(2.4)

Dividing both sides of (
2.4
) by
|x|^{p},
we rearrange the terms and get

\left(\frac{|x+y|^{p-2}}{|x|^{p-2}}-1\right)\left(\frac{x+y}{|x|}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{x+y}{|x|}-\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\geq(p-2)\left(1-\frac{|x+y|}{|x|}\right)^{2}.

Noting

\frac{x+y}{|x|}\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}\leq\frac{|x+y|}{|x|},

it is sufficient to prove that

\left(\frac{|x+y|^{p-2}}{|x|^{p-2}}-1\right)\left(\frac{|x+y|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)-\left(\frac{|x+y|^{p-2}}{|x|^{p-2}}-1\right)\frac{|x+y|}{|x|}-(p-2)\left(1-\frac{|x+y|}{|x|}\right)^{2}\geq 0.

(2.5)

Let
t=\frac{|x+y|}{|x|},
and then
0<t\leq 1.
Observe that the left hand side of of (
2.5
) is equivalent to

t^{2}(t^{p-2}-1)-[(p-2)(1-t)^{2}+(t^{p-2}-1)t]=\frac{(1-t)((2-p)t+(p-1)t^{2}-t^{p})}{t}.

As
\frac{1}{p-1},\ \frac{1}{2-p}>1
and
(p-1)+(2-p)=1
, Young’s inequality implies

t^{p}=t^{2(p-1)+(2-p)}\leq(p-1)t^{2}+(2-p)t.

Then, we reach

(2-p)t+(p-1)t^{2}-t^{p}\geq 0\quad\text{ for }0<t\leq 1,

and (
2.5
) follows. Thus, we conclude (
2.4
).

When
|x|\leq|x+y|,
we claim that

\displaystyle|x+y|^{p-2}(x+y)\cdot y\geq

\displaystyle\ |x|^{p-2}x\cdot y+|x+y|^{p-2}|y|^{2}

\displaystyle+(p-2)\frac{|x+y|}{(2-p)|x+y|+(p-1)|x|}|x+y|^{p-2}\big(|x|-|x+y|)^{2}.

(2.6)

Towards this, we write
t=\frac{|x+y|}{|x|}\in[1,+\infty)
and prove that

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle:=(t-1)\left(t^{p-2}-(p-2)\frac{t^{p-1}}{(2-p)t+(p-1)}(t-1)-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{t-1}{(2-p)t+(p-1)}[(p-1)t^{p-2}-(p-2)t^{p}+(p-2)t+1-p]

\displaystyle=:\frac{t-1}{(2-p)t+(p-1)}g(t)\geq 0,\quad\text{for all}\ t\geq 1.

Note that
\frac{t-1}{(2-p)t+(p-1)}\geq 0,
and
then it is enough to show
g(t)\geq 0
for all
t\geq 1.

Indeed, a direct computation yields

g(1)=0\quad\text{and}\quad g^{\prime}(t)=(p-2)[(p-1)t^{p-3}-pt^{p-1}+1].

Furthermore, write
h(t):=(p-1)t^{p-3}-pt^{p-1}+1,
and then
h(1)=0
and

h^{\prime}(t)=(p-1)t^{p-4}((p-3)-pt^{2})<0,\quad\forall\ t\geq 1,

which implies

h(t)\leq 0
for any
t\geq 1.
As
p<2,
this further gives
g^{\prime}(t)\geq 0.
Thus, we derive
g(t)\geq 0
for any
t\geq 1
and (
2.6
) follows.

Case
(2):

p\geq 2.
For
p\geq 2
and
|x|\leq|x+y|,
we show that

|x+y|^{p-2}(x+y)\cdot y-|x|^{p-2}(x+y)\cdot y\geq(p-2)|x|^{p-2}(|x|-|x+y|)^{2}.

(2.7)

Note that this inequality holds trivially when
p=2
, and we may assume
p>2
in what follows.

Dividing both sides of (
2.7
) by
|x|^{p}
and letting
t=\frac{|x+y|}{|x|}\geq 1,
it is sufficient to prove

\displaystyle\ \left[|x+y|^{p-2}|x|+|x|^{p-1}-2|x+y|^{p-1}\right]|x+y|

3.

Let

In

Furthermore, in Section 3.2 of
[
13

Besides, they utilized the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

For any function
\varphi\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{p^{*}-2}\xi\varphi dx=0,\quad\forall\ \xi\in T_{v}\mathcal{M},

\varphi
is orthogonal to
T_{v}\mathcal{M}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};v^{p^{*}-2}).

Lemma 3.2
.

There exist
\tilde{\delta}=\tilde{\delta}(n,p)>0

\|D\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1

1.3

Lemma 3.3

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma

, there exists

such that

Recall that

Then testing

yields

Combining with

\left<P(u),\epsilon\varphi\right>\ \leq\epsilon\|P(u)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|D\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

it holds that

\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Du|^{p-2}DuD\varphi dx-\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{p^{*}-2}u\varphi dx\leq\epsilon\|P(u)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|D\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(4.1)

We split the proof into three cases.

Case
(1)
:

1<p\leq\frac{2n}{n+2}.
We show there exists
C=C(n.p)>0,
such that

\|Du-Dv\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|P(u)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

By setting
x=Dv
and

\displaystyle\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Du|^{p-2}Du\cdot D\varphi

\displaystyle\quad+(p-2)(1-\kappa)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\omega_{2}(|Du|-|Dv|)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad+c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{\epsilon^{p}|D\varphi|^{p},\epsilon^{2}|Dv|^{p-2}|D\varphi|^{2}\}dx.

(4.2)

Let
a=v
and
b=\epsilon\varphi
in (
2.11
). It follows that

\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{p^{*}-2}u\varphi dx\leq\ \epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{p^{*}-2}v\varphi dx+\epsilon^{2}(p^{*}-1+\kappa)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(v+C_{1}|\epsilon\varphi|)^{p^{*}}}{v^{2}+|\epsilon\varphi|^{2}}\varphi^{2}dx.

(4.3)

By (
1.2
) and the orthogonality, it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Dv|^{p-2}Dv\cdot D\varphi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{p^{*}-1}\varphi dx=0.

(4.4)

Combining (

\displaystyle\quad\epsilon\|P(u)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|D\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon^{2}(1-\kappa)\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\omega_{1}|D\varphi|^{2}+(p-2)\omega_{2}\left(\frac{|Dv+\epsilon D\varphi|-|Dv|}{\epsilon}\right)^{2}dx\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{\epsilon^{p}|D\varphi|^{p},\epsilon^{2}|Dv|^{p-2}|D\varphi|^{2}\}dx

\displaystyle\quad-\epsilon^{2}(p^{*}-1+\kappa)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(v+C_{1}|\epsilon\varphi|)^{p^{*}}}{v^{2}+|\epsilon\varphi|^{2}}\varphi^{2}dx.

Then, owing to (

\displaystyle\quad\epsilon\|P(u)\|_{W^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|D\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon^{2}\left(1-\kappa-\frac{p^{*}-1+\kappa}{p^{*}-1+\lambda S^{-p}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\omega_{1}|D\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(p-2)\left(1-\kappa-\frac{p^{*}-1+\kappa}{p^{*}-1+\lambda S^{-p}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\omega_{2}(|Du|-|Dv|)^{2}dx\big]

\displaystyle\quad+\left(c_{1}-\frac{\gamma_{0}(p^{*}-1+\kappa)}{p^{*}-1+\lambda S^{-p}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{\epsilon^{p}|D\varphi|^{p},\epsilon^{2}|Dv|^{p-2}|D\varphi|^{2}\}dx.

we get

On the other hand, according to

Case
(2):

\displaystyle\quad+c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{\epsilon^{p}|D\varphi|^{p},\epsilon^{2}|Dv|^{p-2}|D\varphi|^{2}\}dx

a_{j}^{p^{*}}
, we obtain

\frac{|a_{j}+b_{j}|^{p^{*}-2}(a_{j}+b_{j})b_{j}}{a_{j}^{p^{*}}}>\frac{b_{j}}{a_{j}}+(p^{*}-1+\kappa)\left|\frac{b_{j}}{a_{j}}\right|^{2}+C_{2,j}\frac{|b_{j}|^{p^{*}}}{a_{j}^{p^{*}}}.

(A.4)

As
C_{2,j}\rightarrow+\infty,
then it holds

\frac{|b_{j}|}{a_{j}}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{when}\ j\rightarrow\infty

at a sufficiently fast rate. However, the Taylor expansion yields

\frac{|a_{j}+b_{j}|^{p^{*}-2}(a_{j}+b_{j})b_{j}}{a_{j}^{p^{*}}}=\frac{b_{j}}{a_{j}}+(p^{*}-1)\frac{b_{j}^{2}}{a_{j}^{2}}+o\left(\frac{b_{j}^{2}}{a_{j}^{2}}\right),

which is smaller than the right-hand side of (
A.4
). This leads to a contradiction. Thus, we conclude (
2.12
).

On the structure of entropy dissipation and regularity for quasi-entropy solutions to 1d scalar conservation laws and to isentropic Euler system with γ = 3 \gamma=3
Fabio Ancona, Elio Marconi, Luca Talamini

Abstract
. In this paper, we first investigate quasi-entropy solutions to scalar conservation laws in several space dimensions. In this setting, we introduce a suitable Lagrangian representation for such solutions. Next, we prove that, in one space dimension and for fluxes f f satisfying a general non-degeneracy condition, the entropy dissipation measures of quasi-entropy solutions are concentrated on a 1 1 -rectifiable set. The same result is obtained for the isentropic Euler system with γ = 3 \gamma=3 , for which we also slightly improve the available fractional regularity by exploiting the sign of the kinetic measures.



1.
Introduction

Consider a scalar conservation law

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u+\mathrm{div}_{x}f(u)=0,\qquad\text{in}\quad(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

where
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
C^{2}
function. In this paper we study
quasi-entropy solutions
of (
1.1
). These are functions
u\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that the distribution
\mu_{\eta}:=\eta_{t}(u)+q_{x}(u)
is a locally finite measure for every entropy-entropy flux
(\eta,\boldsymbol{q})
, i.e. a pair of
C^{2}
smooth functions such that
\boldsymbol{q}^{\prime}=f^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}
. In general, if
u
is a quasi-entropy solution to (
1.1
), it satisfies the balance law of the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\mathrm{div}_{x}f(u)=m,\qquad\text{in}\quad(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

where
m\in\mathscr{M}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the source, is a locally finite measure.
It is well known that weak solutions of (
1.1
) are not unique, and that the well posedness is restored only in the smaller class of
entropy solutions
. These last are weak solutions to (
1.1
) that in addition dissipates every convex entropy:

(1.3)

\mu_{\eta}\doteq\partial_{t}\eta(u)+\mathrm{div}_{x}\boldsymbol{q}(u)\leq 0,\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}$}.

where here
\eta
(the entropy) is any scalar convex function.
Since
[
19
]
it is known that the Cauchy problem associated to (
1.1
) is well posed in the class of entropy solutions.

In the fundamental paper
[
23
]
the authors prove that entropy solutions of (
1.1
) satisfy a
kinetic formulation
, where the collision term, which encodes the entropy production (
1.3
), takes the form of a derivative of a nonnegative finite measure:

(1.4)

\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi=\partial_{v}\mu,\qquad\chi(t,x,v)=\mathbf{1}_{0<v\leq u(t,x)}-\mathbf{1}_{u(t,x)\leq v<0}.

Although entropy solutions provide a satisfactory mathematical theory for (
1.1
), more general concepts of solution are studied in the literature, in particular the class of
finite entropy solutions
(or, solutions with finite entropy production) appears in many physical models: these are quasi-entropy solutions for which, in addition,
m=0
in (
1.2
). Being a finite entropy solution corresponds, from the kinetic formulation point of view, to allowing finite measures
\mu
(without sign) in the right hand side of (
1.4
). For quasi-entropy solutions, an additional source term
\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{M}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})
must be added:

(1.5)

\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi=\partial_{v}\mu_{1}+\mu_{0}

Finite entropy solutions (therefore, quasi-entropy solutions satisfying (
1.5
) with
\mu_{0}=0
) arise for example in the study of large deviations for scalar conservation laws
[
6
]
,
[
24
]
and in the study of the
\Gamma
-convergence problem of the Aviles-Giga functional, see e.g.
[
26
]
and the references therein. Moreover, finite entropy solutions find applications in the theory of diffusive-dispersive approximations to conservation laws and more generally whenever non-classical shock waves are present, see
[
21
]
and the references therein.
In
[
20
]
it is proved that diffusive dispersive approximations to conservation laws with general flux functions converge to finite entropy solutions, where
\mu_{1}
is a general locally finite measure. It is expected that the results of this paper will be useful in the study of these solutions. More importantly for our present purposes, as it shown in Proposition
6.3
later in this paper, the Riemann invariants of an entropy solution of the Euler system with
\gamma=3

(1.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2}+\rho^{3}/3)=0

are quasi-entropy solutions to the Burgers equation

(1.7)

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)=0

if
\rho
is bounded away from zero. This explains the need for a source term
\mu_{0}
in (
1.5
). The system has been studied in the literature by various authors, see for example
[
17
,
22
,
34
,
36
]
. In
[
2
]
it is conjectured that systems of conservation laws might share some of their regularity properties with scalar balance laws. In this direction, in
[
4
]
it is proved that entropy solutions to
2\times 2
systems satisfy a pair of kinetic equations with source terms, and it is proved that points outside the jump set are of
vanishing mean oscillation
. Moreover, in
[
33
]
, these tools are further exploited to obtain strong time regularity and a decay result for entropy solutions.

In one space dimension, starting from the work of Oleĭnik
[
30
]
, various authors studied the regularizing effect induced by the interaction of the nonlinearity of the flux with the entropy condition (
1.3
). Typically, the nonlinearity of the flux forces
characteristic lines
to intersect; together with the entropy condition this produces the desired regularizing effect. For example, for entropic solutions
u
to Burgers equation, the one-sided Lipschitz estimate

\partial_{x}u(t,\cdot)\leq\frac{1}{t},\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}$}

induces a regularizing effect
L^{\infty}
to
BV
. See also
[
3
,
5
,
8
,
10
,
22
,
23
,
29
]
for various results on the regularizing effect of nonlinearity in conservation law. Therefore, from Vol’pert chain rule for
BV
functions
[
37
]
, it follows that the measures
\mu_{\eta}
are concentrated on the 1-rectifiable jump set of
u
. It has been a long standing problem to understand whether or not this concentration property holds for general fluxes and entropy solutions in one space dimension. In one space dimension, a first result
[
14
]
solves the problem for entropy solutions with
C^{2}
fluxes for which
\{u|f^{\prime\prime}(u)=0\}
is locally finite, and only in a recent work
[
8
]
the authors prove the same concentration property for entropy solutions with general smooth fluxes. The result of
[
8
]
is achieved by using in a crucial way a
Lagrangian representation
for solutions of (
1.1
), which is an extension of the method of characteristics in the non-smooth setting.

In
[
27
]
it is proved that the concentration property remains valid for finite entropy solutions to one dimensional Burgers equation. The result is also achieved by using a Lagrangian representation, but a different one: one can think of it as representing characteristics at the level of the kinetic formulation (
1.4
), rather then at the level of (
1.1
). This kind of representation was introduced for the first time in
[
7
]
for entropic (multi-d) solutions of (
1.1
) and in
[
25
]
for finite entropy solutions.

In the multidimensional case, using techniques coming from geometric measure theory, in
[
13
]
a partial rectifiability result for the entropy dissipation is proved. In particular, letting
\nu(t,x)
be the
(t,x)
-marginal of the measure
|\mu|
, with
\mu
as in (
1.4
), they prove that the set
\mathbf{J}\subset(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
of points
(t,x)
of positive
\mathscr{H}^{d}
density of
\nu
is

-rectifiable. However, the question of whether
\nu
is concentrated on the jump set
\mathbf{J}
remains open.

Results and plan of the paper

In Section

In Section

. In Theorem

2.
Kinetic Formulation of quasi-entropy solutions

We start by giving the definition of quasi-entropy solution. Here we consider a
C^{2}

flux
function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Most of the times we will take
\Omega=(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
for some
T>0
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
u\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
is a
quasi-entropy
solution with flux
f
if for every entropy-entropy flux pair
(\eta,\boldsymbol{q})
, with
\eta\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists a locally finite measure
\mu_{\eta}\in\mathscr{M}(\Omega)
such that

(2.1)

\partial_{t}\eta(u)+\mathrm{div}_{x}\,\boldsymbol{q}(u)\,=\,\mu_{\eta}\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{t,x}(\Omega)$}.

Since in particular the pair
(\eta_{0},\boldsymbol{q}_{0})
defined by

\eta_{0}(u)=u
,
\boldsymbol{q}_{0}(u)=f(u)

is an entropy-entropy flux pair, if
u
is a quasi-entropy solution we find that
u
solves (
1.2
) with
m=\mu_{\eta_{0}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The more classical concept of entropy solution to the conservation law

\partial_{t}u+\mathrm{div}_{x}\,f(u)=0

can be obtained by requiring, in addition, that
\mu_{\eta_{0}}=0
and that for every
convex
entropy
\eta
, the corresponding entropy measure
\mu_{\eta}
is nonpositive.

In
[
23
]
, Lions, Perthame and Tadmor characterize entropy solutions via a
kinetic formulation
.
Here we use a slightly different formulation, that immediately follows from the one of
[
13
]
, which is adapted for quasi-entropy solutions. We write its proof for completeness.

Proposition 2.3
.

A function
u\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
is a quasi-entropy solution in the sense of Definition
2.1
if and only if,
there exist locally finite measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{M}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})
with
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{i}\subset\Omega\times[\inf u,\sup u]
, such that

(2.2)

\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi=\partial_{v}\mu_{1}+\mu_{0}\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})$}

where

(2.3)

\chi(t,x,v)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $v\leq u(t,x)$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Remark 2.4
.

Clearly the measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
such that (
2.2
) holds are not unique. It is possible to choose the measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{0}
such that their support is contained in the topological boundary of the hypograph of
u
:

\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{i}\subset\partial\,\mathrm{hyp}\,u\qquad i=0,1,

where

\mathrm{hyp}(u)=\big\{(t,x,v)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\,|\,v\leq u(t,x)\big\}.

However, later on we will need to make a finer choice on the measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
, that in particular implies the above condition.

Proof.

We first show that if
u
satisfies (
2.2
), then
u
is a quasi-entropy solution. Let
(\eta,\boldsymbol{q})
be any entropy-entropy flux pair. Let
U\Subset\Omega
(compactly contained in
\Omega
) and for every test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, with
\mathrm{supp}\,\phi\subset U
, we can compute, omitting the variables
(t,x)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{t}\,\eta(u)+\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\boldsymbol{q}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(v)\,\Big(\phi_{t}\eta^{\prime}(v)+\nabla_{x}\phi\,\eta^{\prime}(v)\ f^{\prime}(v)\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\,\eta^{\prime}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(t,x,v)+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\,\eta^{{\prime\prime}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(t,x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

In particular, letting
C_{U}=\|\mu_{0}\|_{\mathscr{M}(U)}+\|\mu_{1}\|_{\mathscr{M}(U)}
and defining the distribution

T_{\eta}\doteq\partial_{t}\eta(u)+\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{q}(u)

we obtain the bound

(2.4)

\langle T_{\eta},\,\phi\rangle\leq C_{U}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\,\|\eta^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}\qquad\forall\;\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\qquad\text{with $\mathrm{supp}\,\phi\subset U$}.

By Riesz theorem,
T_{\eta}
can be identified with a locally finite measure.

Conversely, assume that
u
is a quasi entropy solution as in Definition
2.1
. Define a distribution
T\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})
by

\langle T,\phi\,\varrho\rangle\doteq\int_{\Omega}\phi_{t}\eta_{\varrho}(u)\,+\,\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\boldsymbol{q}_{\varrho}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\qquad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\quad\varrho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})

where we define the entropy-entropy flux pair associated to
\varrho

\eta_{\varrho}(v)\doteq\int_{0}^{v}\int_{0}^{z}\varrho(w)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}w\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z,\qquad\boldsymbol{q}_{\varrho}(v)\doteq\int_{0}^{v}f^{\prime}(z)\eta^{\prime}_{\varrho}(z)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.

This definition is sufficient because finite sums
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi_{i}(t,x)\varrho_{i}(\xi)
are dense in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see e.g.
[
15
, Section 4.3]
)
We again consider any
U\Subset\Omega
and for any
\phi\in\mathscr{D}(U)
we define a linear functional
L_{\phi}\,:\,C(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
by

L_{\phi}(\varrho)\doteq\int_{U}\phi_{t}\eta_{\varrho}(u)\,+\,\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\boldsymbol{q}_{\varrho}(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Each functional
L_{\phi}
is bounded, and therefore also continuous, since it holds

|L_{\phi}(\varrho)|\leq C_{U,\phi}\,\|\varrho\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\qquad\forall\varrho\in C(\mathbb{R})

for some constant
C_{U,\phi}
depending only the set
U
and the
C^{1}
norm of the function
\phi
.
Since
u
is a quasi-entropy solution, we deduce that the family of functionals
L_{\phi}
is pointwisely bounded, because

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)\\
|\phi|_{0}\leq 1\end{subarray}}|L_{\phi}(\varrho)|\leq\int_{U}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\mu_{\eta_{\varrho}}|.

Therefore, by the uniform boundedness principle, the family
L_{\phi}
is uniformly (norm) bounded, that is

(2.5)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)\\
|\phi|_{0}\leq 1,\,|\varrho|_{0}\leq 1\end{subarray}}|L_{\phi}(\varrho)|=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)\\
|\phi|_{0}\leq 1,\,|\varrho|_{0}\leq 1\end{subarray}}|\langle T,\phi\varrho\rangle|\leq C_{U}.

We also notice that by definition of
L_{\phi}
, it holds

(2.6)

L_{\phi}(\varrho)=0\qquad\forall\varrho\in C(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{with $\mathrm{supp}\,\varrho\subset\mathbb{R}\setminus[\inf u,\sup u]$}.

Combining (
2.5
), (
2.6
) with the Riesz representation theorem, we then obtain the existence of a locally finite measure
\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{M}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})
, with
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{1}\subset\Omega\times[\inf u,\sup u]
, such that

(2.7)

\langle T,\phi\varrho\rangle=L_{\phi}(\varrho)=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(t,x)\varrho(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(t,x,v)\qquad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\quad\varrho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}).

Finally, we calculate, for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and
\eta^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\Big\langle\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\nabla_{x}\chi,\,\phi\eta^{\prime}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\nabla_{x}\chi,\,\phi(\eta^{\prime}-\eta^{\prime}(0))\Big\rangle

\displaystyle+\Big\langle\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\nabla_{x}\chi,\,\phi\eta^{\prime}(0)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\nabla_{x}\chi,\,\phi\int_{0}^{v}\eta^{{\prime\prime}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle+\Big\langle\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\nabla_{x}\chi,\,\phi\eta^{\prime}(0)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=-\Big\langle T,\phi\eta^{\prime\prime}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\nabla_{x}\chi,\,\phi\eta^{\prime}(0)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(t,x)\eta^{{\prime\prime}}(v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}+\eta^{\prime}(0)\int_{\Omega}\phi(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{\eta_{0}}

where we recall that
\eta_{0}(u)=u
is the trivial entropy and we used (
2.7
) with
\varrho=\eta^{{\prime\prime}}
.
The result follows by setting
\mu_{0}=\mu_{\eta_{0}}\times\delta_{0}
.
∎

3.
Lagrangian Representation

In this section we introduce the concept of
Lagrangian representation
of quasi-entropy solution to the balance law (
1.1
), which is the main tool that we will use throughout the rest of this chapter. This kind of representation was introduced for the first time in
[
7
]
for entropy solutions to scalar conservation laws:

(3.1)

\partial_{t}u+\mathrm{div}_{x}\,f(u)\,=\,0.

In
[
7
]
, the authors use the Lagrangian representation to prove that continuous solutions to the conservation law (
3.1
) do not dissipate any entropy.

In
[
25
]
the Lagrangian representation is introduced also for finite entropy solutions to the conservation law (
3.1
) (i.e., no source terms are present, but the measures
\mu_{\eta}
in (
1.3
) can change sign) and later on, in
[
27
]
, it is used to prove that, in the one dimensional case and for Burgers equation
f(u)=u^{2}/2
, the entropy dissipation measures are concentrated on a
1
-rectifiable set. In this case the existence of a Lagrangian representation was proved via an explicit approximation procedure, similar to the transport-collapse scheme. In our setting, inspired by the method in
[
9
]
, where it is used for the continuity equation, we adopt a different approach based on Smirnov’s Theorem on the representation of
1
-currents. This method provides finer control over the behavior of the Lagrangian curves, which will be essential in the subsequent analysis.

3.1.
Definition of the Lagrangian Representation

In this subsection we define the Lagrangian representation of a quasi-entropy solution to the balance law (
1.1
).
In the following, we denote by
\Gamma
the space of curves

\Gamma\doteq\Big\{(I_{\gamma},\gamma)\;\big|\;\gamma=(\gamma^{x},\gamma^{v}):I_{\gamma}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{v},\;\;\gamma^{x}\;\text{is Lipschitz and $\gamma^{v}$ is in $BV(I_{\gamma})$}\Big\}

where
I_{\gamma}=(t^{1}_{\gamma},t^{2}_{\gamma})
is the domain of definition of a curve
\gamma
.
A locally finite measure on
\Gamma
is a measure
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathscr{M}(\Gamma)
such that
\boldsymbol{\omega}(K)<+\infty
for every set
K\subset\Gamma
of the form

K=\Big\{\gamma\in\Gamma\;\big|\;|\gamma^{x}(0)|,|\gamma^{v}(0)|\leq M,\quad\mathrm{Lip}(\gamma^{x})\leq M,\quad\|\gamma^{v}\|_{BV}\leq M\Big\}.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Lagrangian representation
(of the hypograph) of a quasi-entropy solution
u
to (
1.1
) is a locally finite measure
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\Gamma)
such that:

(1)

it holds

(3.2)

\chi\cdot\mathscr{L}^{d+2}=\int_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{id},\gamma)_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{1}\llcorner I_{\gamma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}(\gamma)

where
\chi
is defined as in (
2.3
), and the measure in the right hand side is defined on test functions
\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d+2})
by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+2}}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Big[\int_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{id},\gamma)_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{1}\llcorner I_{\gamma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}(\gamma)\Big]:=\int_{\Gamma}\Big(\int_{I_{\gamma}}\varphi(t,\gamma(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}(\gamma).

(2)

\omega
is concentrated on the set of curves
\gamma=(\gamma^{x},\gamma^{v})\in\Gamma
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}^{x}=f^{\prime}(\gamma^{v}(t)),\qquad\text{for $\mathscr{L}^{1}$-a.e. $t\in I_{\gamma}$},

(3)

the following integral bound holds: for every compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
T>0
, one has

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{Tot.Var.}\big(\gamma^{v},\,I_{\gamma}\cap[0,T]\big)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{\gamma^{x}(t_{\gamma}^{1})\in K\}}(\gamma)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}(\gamma)<C_{K,T}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}\Big(\big\{\gamma\in\Gamma\;|\;(t,\gamma^{x}(t^{i}_{\gamma}))\in(0,T)\times K\big\}\Big)\leq\widetilde{C}_{K,T}

The link between the notion of kinetic solution and Lagrangian representation is given by the following proposition.

Theorem 3.2
.

A function
u\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\;\mathbb{R})
is a quasi-entropy solution of (
1.1
) if and only if it admits a Lagrangian representation.

We prove that if
u
admits a Lagrangian representation, then
u
is a quasi-entropy solution. The opposite implication is deeper: the proof, which relies on the structure of 1-dimensional normal currents in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is carried out in the next subsection (see Theorem
3.14
). Actually, for the opposite implication, we will prove a stronger result, stating the existence of a
good
Lagrangian representation (see Definition
3.3
)

Proof.

Assume that
u
admits a Lagrangian representation
\boldsymbol{\omega}
. We have to show the existence of a pair of locally finite measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathscr{M}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})
such that (
2.2
) holds. To do so, we consider the distribution

S\doteq\partial_{t}\chi+f^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi

and calculate, for every test functions
\eta^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi\in\mathscr{D}(\Omega)
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle-\langle S,\eta^{\prime}\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta^{\prime}(v)\chi(t,x,v)[\phi_{t}(t,x)+f^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x)]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{eq:deflagromega}}}}{{=}}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{I_{\gamma}}\eta^{\prime}(\gamma^{v}(t))[\phi_{t}(t,\gamma^{x}(t))+f^{\prime}(\gamma^{v}(t))\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi(t,\gamma^{x}(t))]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{eq:charaeqla}}}}{{=}}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{I_{\gamma}}\eta^{\prime}(\gamma^{v}(t))[\phi_{t}(t,\gamma^{x}(t))+\dot{\gamma}^{x}(t)\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi(t,\gamma^{x}(t))]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}(\gamma)

\displaystyle Dg_{\gamma}=\eta^{\prime}(\gamma^{v}(t))

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\phi(t,\gamma^{x}(t))\cdot\mathscr{L}^{1}\,+\,\eta^{{\prime\prime}}(\gamma^{v}(t))\phi(t,\gamma^{x}(t))\cdot\widetilde{D}\gamma^{v}(t)

Definition 3.3
.

We say that a Lagrangian representation
\boldsymbol{\omega}
is

induced by

Condition (

Remark 3.4

Not every couple

3.2.
Existence of (good) Lagrangian Representations.

In this subsection we use a Theorem of Smirnov

3.2.1.
Preliminaries About the Theory of Currents

For an introduction to the subject we refer for example to
[
18
]
.
The space
\mathscr{D}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of
k
-dimensional currents
is the dual of the space
\mathscr{D}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of all smooth
k
-differential forms with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Given a
k
-current
\mathsf{T}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, its
mass

\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{T})
is defined as

\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{T})=\sup\{\langle\mathsf{T}\;,\omega\rangle\;|\;\omega\in\mathscr{D}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad|\omega|\leq 1\}

Remark 3.5

(1-currents with finite mass)
.

The space of 1-currents with finite mass can be identified with Radon vector measures
\mathsf{T}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}
, and we will often identify a 1-current with finite mass with the associated vector measure. The duality of a 1-current with finite mass
\mathsf{T}=(\mathsf{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{T}_{d})
with a vector field
\Phi=(\Phi^{1},\ldots,\Phi^{d})\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is

\langle\mathsf{T}\;,\;\Phi\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi^{i}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathsf{T}_{i}.

Analogously, the space of
0
-currents with finite mass can be identified with the space of Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Example 3.6

(Rectifiable
k
-currents)
.

Let a
k
-rectifiable set
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
oriented by a unit
k
-vector
\tau
, and a measurable multiplicity function
\theta
be given. Then we denote by
\llbracket M,\;\tau,\;\theta\rrbracket
the
k
-current defined by

\langle\llbracket M,\;\tau,\;\theta\rrbracket,\;\omega\rangle=\int_{M}\theta(x)\langle\tau(x),\;\omega(x)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{k}(x),\qquad\forall\;\omega\in\mathscr{D}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

The
boundary
of a k-current
\mathsf{T}
is a
k-1
-current
\partial\mathsf{T}\in\mathscr{D}_{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined as

\langle\partial\mathsf{T}\;,\;\omega\rangle=\langle\mathsf{T}\;,\;d\omega\rangle,\qquad\forall\;\omega\in\mathscr{D}^{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

where
d:\mathscr{D}^{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathscr{D}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the De Rham’s-Cartan differential.

Definition 3.7

(Normal Currents)
.

We say that a
k
-current
\mathsf{T}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
normal
if both
\mathsf{T}
and
\partial\mathsf{T}
are of finite mass.

Definition 3.8

(A-cyclic currents)
.

Let
\mathsf{T}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a
k
-current. We say that

(1)

A current
\mathsf{C}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a
subcurrent
of
\mathsf{T}
, and we write
\mathsf{C}\leq\mathsf{T}
, if
\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{T})=\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{T}-\mathsf{C})+\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{C})
.

(2)

A current
\mathsf{C}\leq\mathsf{T}
is a
cycle
of
\mathsf{T}
if
\partial\mathsf{C}=0
.

(3)

\mathsf{T}
is a-cyclic if its only cycle is
\mathsf{C}=0
.

Remark 3.9
.

In general, given
\mathsf{T},\mathsf{C}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it is clear by the definition of mass of a current that it holds
\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{T})\leq\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{T}-\mathsf{C})+\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{C})
. Therefore
\mathsf{C}
is a subcurrent of
\mathsf{T}
if and only if
\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{T})\geq\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{T}-\mathsf{C})+\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{C})
. In particular, if
\mathsf{T}
is of finite mass,
\mathsf{C}
must be of finite mass.

Example 3.10

(Structure of 1-subcurrents)
.

Let
\mathsf{T}\in\mathscr{D}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a 1-current with finite mass, and let
\left\|\mathsf{T}\right\|\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be its total variation measure. By the polar decomposition of vector valued measures (see
[
1
]
) there exists a unit measurable vector field
\vec{S}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, defined
\left\|\mathsf{T}\right\|
-a.e., such that
\mathsf{T}=\vec{S}\cdot\left\|\mathsf{T}\right\|
. It is easy to see that every subcurrent
\mathsf{C}\in\mathscr{D}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of
\mathsf{T}
has the form

\mathsf{C}=\vec{S}\cdot\left\|\mathsf{C}\right\|,\qquad\left\|\mathsf{C}\right\|\leq\left\|\mathsf{T}\right\|\quad\text{as measures}

Example 3.11

(
1
-currents associated with Lipschitz curves)
.

The most important example for the following of this section is the case of 1-rectifiable currents

\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket:=\llbracket\gamma\big((0,1)\big),\;\tau(x),\;\mathscr{H}^{0}\big(\gamma^{-1}(\{x\})\big)\rrbracket\in\mathscr{D}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

associated with a Lipschitz curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\tau(x)
is the tangent vector the the graph
\gamma((0,1))
at a point
x\in\gamma((0,1))
, defined for
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-almost every point
x\in\gamma((0,1))
, and

\mathscr{H}^{0}\big(\gamma^{-1}(\{x\})\big)
counts how many times the curve
\gamma
passes through the point
x
. The action of
\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket
on 1-forms (which can be identified with vector fields
\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
) can be explicitly written as (see the Area Formula in
[
1
]
)

\displaystyle\langle\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket,\;\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma((0,1))}\mathscr{H}^{0}\big(\gamma^{-1}(\{x\})\big)\cdot\Big\langle\frac{\dot{\gamma}}{|\dot{\gamma}|},\;\Phi\Big\rangle\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\Phi(\gamma(\tau)),\;\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad\forall\;\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})

In particular
\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket
has finite mass since

\langle\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket,\;\Phi\rangle\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}(\tau)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\gamma),\qquad\forall\;\Phi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad|\Phi|\leq 1.

The boundary of the 1-current
\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket\in\mathscr{D}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, satisfies

\langle\partial\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket,\;\phi\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau}\phi(\gamma(\tau))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau=\phi(\gamma(1))-\phi(\gamma(0)),\qquad\forall\;u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

so that
\partial\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket=\delta_{\gamma(1)}-\delta_{\gamma(0)}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore
\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket
is a normal 1-current. Moreover, a 1-current
\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket\in\mathscr{D}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a cycle if and only if
\gamma(1)=\gamma(0)
, otherwise it is an a-cyclic 1-current.

3.2.2.
Smirnov’s Theorem for Normal Currents

The following Theorem of Smirnov yields a decomposition of
1
-normal currents into superposition of currents associated to Lipschitz curves.

Theorem 3.12

(Smirnov,
[
32
]
)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set. Let
\mathsf{T}\in\mathscr{D}_{1}(U)
be a normal, a-cyclic 1-current in

. Then there exists a positive measure
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\mathrm{Lip}((0,1);U))
such that

(3.13)

\langle\mathsf{T},\omega\rangle=\int_{\mathrm{Lip}((0,1);U)}\langle\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket,\omega\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\gamma),\qquad\forall\;\omega\in\mathscr{D}^{1}(U)

Furthermore, the mass of
\mathsf{T}
decomposes as

(3.14)

\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{T})=\int_{\mathrm{Lip}((0,1);U)}\mathbb{M}(\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\gamma)

and the boundary measure
\partial T
decomposes as

(3.15)

(\partial T)^{+}=(e_{1})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta},\qquad(\partial T)^{-}=(e_{0})_{\sharp}\boldsymbol{\eta}

where

e_{t}:\mathrm{Lip}((0,1);U)\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad e_{t}(\gamma)=\gamma(t)

is the evaluation map at a point
t\in(0,1)

and
(\partial T)^{\pm}
denotes the positive/negative part of the measure
\partial T
.

Remark 3.13
.

Let
\mathsf{T}=\vec{S}\cdot\left\|\mathsf{T}\right\|

for
\mathscr{L}^{1}

\tau
. In fact, since

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}(\mathsf{T})=\langle\mathsf{T},\;\vec{S}\rangle

But on the other hand, by equation (

a.e.
\gamma\in\Gamma

in the sense of distributions. But since
\hat{\mu}_{1}
is compactly supported in
v
, this implies
\hat{\mu}_{1}=0
, and therefore
\mathsf{C}=0
, as wanted.

2.
Thanks to Step 1, we can apply Smirnov Theorem
3.12
, which gives a measure
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
such that (
3.13
) holds. Expanding the relation, this means that for every vector field
\Phi=(\Phi^{t},\Phi^{x},\Phi^{v})\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})
, we have

(3.22)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}}\chi\cdot[\Phi^{t}+(f^{\prime}(v)\cdot\Phi^{x})]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{d+2}-\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}}\Phi^{v}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\int_{0}^{1}\dot{\Theta}(\tau)\cdot\Phi(\Theta(\tau))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\Theta).

By Remark
3.13
the measure
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
given by Smirnov Theorem is concentrated on curves
\Theta\in\tilde{\Gamma}
such that
\dot{\Theta}(\tau)
is parallel to
\vec{S}(\Theta(\tau))
for a.e.
\tau\in(0,1)
. In particular, this means either
\Theta
travels vertically along
v
, or the velocity of the first component is positive.

3.
Given the measure
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\widetilde{\Gamma})
in Step 2., in order to get a Lagrangian representation in the sense of Definition
3.1
, we want to eliminate a specific set of “bad” curves. In particular, define
\Delta\subset\widetilde{\Gamma}
as the set of curves such that
\Theta^{t}((0,1))
is a singleton. These are the curves whose support is contained on an hyperplane
\{t=s\}
. The new measure will be defined by

\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=\boldsymbol{\eta}\llcorner(\tilde{\Gamma}\setminus\Delta).

Notice that in any case, by the shape of the vector field
\vec{S}
, the curves satisfy
\dot{\Theta}^{t}(\tau)\geq 0
for a.e.
\tau\in(0,1)
for
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
almost every
\Theta
.
For curves in
\tilde{\Gamma}\setminus\Delta
such that the first component
\Theta^{t}
is non-decreasing, it is well defined a
reparametrization map

\mathcal{R}:\tilde{\Gamma}\setminus\Delta\to\Gamma
defined as

(3.23)

\mathcal{R}(\Theta)(t)\doteq\big(\Theta^{x}(\tau(t)),\Theta^{v}(\tau(t))\big),\qquad\tau(t)\doteq\inf\{\tau\in(0,1)\;:\;t<\Theta^{t}(\tau)\}.

Here
\tau(t)
is just the pseudo-inverse of the increasing Lipschitz function
\Theta^{t}(\tau)
. The domain of the curve
\mathcal{R}(\Theta)
will be
\mathrm{int}[\Theta^{t}((0,1))]
(the interior).

Therefore we are now allowed to consider the pushforward measure

\boldsymbol{\omega}\doteq\mathcal{R}_{\sharp}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\,\in\,\mathscr{M}^{+}(\Gamma)

which is a good candidate to be a Lagrangian representation in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

4.
Finally, we prove that
\boldsymbol{\omega}
is a Lagrangian representation. The second condition in Definition
3.1
is trivial. For the first condition, we need to verify that

(3.24)

\int_{\Gamma}\big[(\mathrm{id},\gamma)_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{1}\llcorner I_{\gamma}\big]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{R}_{\sharp}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(\gamma)=\chi\cdot\mathscr{L}^{d+2}.

Therefore consider any test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})
and calculate

(3.25)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}}\phi(t,x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\big[(\mathrm{id},\gamma)_{\sharp}

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{1}\llcorner I_{\gamma}\big](t,x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{R}_{\sharp}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{I_{\gamma}}\phi(t,\gamma(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{R}_{\sharp}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{I_{\gamma}}\phi(\Theta(\tau(t)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(\Theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(\Theta(\tau))(\Theta^{t})^{\prime}(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(\Theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(\Theta(\tau))(\Theta^{t})^{\prime}(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\Theta),

where the last equality holds because

\int_{\Delta}\int_{0}^{1}(\Theta^{t})^{\prime}(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}=\int_{\Delta}\int_{0}^{1}(\Theta^{t})^{\prime}(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=0

since
(\Theta^{t})^{\prime}(\tau)=0
for every
\tau\in(0,1)
if
\Theta\in\Delta
, therefore we are allowed to substitute
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
with
\boldsymbol{\eta}
.
Moreover, for
\boldsymbol{\eta}
-a.e.
\Theta\in\widetilde{\Gamma}
, by the last observation in Step 2., it holds

(\Theta^{t})^{\prime}(\tau)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A^{c}}(\Theta(\tau))=0,\qquad\text{for $\mathscr{L}^{1}$-a.e. $\tau\in(0,1)$}

Therefore we obtain

(3.26)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(\Theta(\tau))(\Theta^{t})^{\prime}(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\Theta)=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(\Theta(\tau))(\Theta^{t})^{\prime}(\tau)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A}(\Theta(\tau))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\Theta).

Define the vector field

\Phi(t,x,v)=(\phi(t,x,v),0,0)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A}(t,x,v)\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}).

Using
\Phi
as a test function in the representation of Step 2, we obtain

(3.27)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(\Theta(\tau))(\Theta^{t})^{\prime}(\tau)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{A}(\Theta(\tau))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\eta}(\Theta)

\displaystyle=\langle\mathsf{T},\Phi\rangle=\langle\chi\cdot\mathscr{L}^{d+2},\phi\rangle

as wanted.
∎

3.3.
Epigraph and Simultaneous Lagrangian Representations

We conclude this section with the proof of some useful properties of Lagrangian representations.

3.3.1.
Lagrangian representation for the epigraph.

Given a quasi-entropy solution of the balance law (
1.1
), we constructed a Lagrangian representation for the function
\chi
in (
2.3
), which is the characteristic function of the hypograph of
u
. In an entirely similar way, we can give the definition of Lagrangian representation for the
epigraph
of
u

Definition 3.15
.

We say that
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}
is a Lagrangian representation for the epigraph of
u
if conditions (2), (3) of Definition
3.1
hold, and condition (1) is replaced by:

(3.28)

\chi_{e}\cdot\mathscr{L}^{d+2}=\int_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{id},\gamma)_{\sharp}\mathscr{L}^{1}\llcorner I_{\gamma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}(\gamma)

where

(3.29)

\chi_{e}(t,x,v)\doteq\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\;u(t,x)\leq v,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

The existence of such a representation follows from Theorem
3.14
: in fact, define

\tilde{u}(t,x)\doteq 1-u(t,x),\qquad\boldsymbol{g}(u)\doteq-f(1-u).

Then, if
\widetilde{\chi}(t,x,v)=\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{hyp}\;\widetilde{u}}(t,x,v)
,
\widetilde{\chi}
is a solution of the kinetic equation

\partial_{t}\widetilde{\chi}+\boldsymbol{g}^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\chi}=\partial_{v}\big[\alpha_{\sharp}\mu_{1}\big]-\alpha_{\sharp}\mu_{0},\qquad\text{in}\;\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{t,x,v}

where
\alpha:\Omega\times(0,1)\mapsto\Omega\times(0,1)
is defined as
\alpha(t,x,v)=\alpha(t,x,1-v)
.
Since
\widetilde{u}
is a quasi-entropy solution with flux
\boldsymbol{g}
by Proposition
2.3
, Theorem
3.14
yields a Lagrangian representation of
\widetilde{u}
, which we call
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
. Now, letting the map
T:\Gamma\to\Gamma
be defined as

T(\gamma)(t)=\alpha(\gamma(t)),\qquad t\in I_{\gamma}

it is easy to see that, since

\widetilde{\chi}(t,x,v)=\chi_{e}(t,x,1-v)

the measure

\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}\doteq T_{\sharp}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}

is a Lagrangian representation of the epigraph of
u
.

3.3.2.
Simultaneous Lagrangian Representations

Let
u
be a given quasi-entropy solution. To keep the notation as clear as possible, when we need to consider simultaneously Lagrangian representations of the hypograph and of the epigraph, we will call them
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}
, respectively. Moreover, we will let

\chi_{h}\doteq\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{hyp}\;u},\qquad\chi_{e}\doteq\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{epi}\;u}.

It is clear that, if
\chi_{h}
satisfies (
2.2
) for some
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
, then
\chi_{e}=1-\chi_{h}
satisfies (
2.2
) with
(-\mu_{0},-\mu_{1})
. Motivated by this observation, we give the following definition.

Definition 3.16
.

We say that a pair
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is
simultaneously induced
by Lagrangian representations
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}
,
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}
of the hypograph and of the epigraph respectively if
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is induced by
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}
and
(-\mu_{0},-\mu_{1})
is induced by
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}
.

It is useful to introduce the following relation
\preceq
between pairs
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
that belong to the set

(3.30)

\mathbf{P}_{\chi}:=\big\{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathscr{M}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})\;|\;\text{\eqref{eq:kinu} holds}\big\}.

Definition 3.17
.

Given
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
and
(\widehat{\mu}_{0},\widehat{\mu}_{1})
in
\mathbf{P}_{\chi}
, we write

(3.31)

\displaystyle(\widehat{\mu}_{0},\widehat{\mu}_{1})\preceq(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\Longleftrightarrow\begin{cases}\widehat{\mu}^{+}_{1}\leq\mu^{+}_{1},\qquad\widehat{\mu}^{-}_{1}\leq\mu^{-}_{1}\qquad\text{as measures}\\
|\widehat{\mu}_{0}|\leq|\mu_{0}|\end{cases}

Note that
\preceq
is the natural relation respected by the application of Theorem
3.14
. This allows to prove the following proposition, which states the existence of simultaneously induced pairs.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
u
be a quasi-entropy solution. Every pair
(\widehat{\mu}_{0},\widehat{\mu}_{1})\in\mathbf{P}_{\chi}
which is minimal with respect to
\preceq
is simultaneously induced by some good Lagrangian representations
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}
.

Proof.

Let
(\widehat{\mu}_{0},\widehat{\mu}_{1})
be a minimal element of
\mathbf{P}_{\chi}
for the relation
\preceq
. It is easy to see there exists at least one minimal element: consider a starting pair
(\bar{\mu}_{0},\bar{\mu}_{1})
and consider the set

\Sigma:=\{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\;|(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\preceq(\bar{\mu}_{0},\bar{\mu}_{1})\}.

Consider now a minimizer for the function
\mathcal{F}:(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\mapsto\|\mu_{1}\|_{\mathscr{M}}
in
\Sigma
, which clearly exists, and call it
(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})
. We claim that this is a minimal element for
\preceq
, in fact, assume that
(\sigma^{\prime}_{0},\sigma_{1}^{\prime})\preceq(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})
. Then in particular
(\sigma^{\prime}_{0},\sigma_{1}^{\prime})\preceq(\bar{\mu}_{0},\bar{\mu}_{1})
so that
(\sigma^{\prime}_{0},\sigma_{1}^{\prime})\in\Sigma
; moreover by the first condition of (
3.31
) there holds
\sigma_{1}^{\prime,\pm}\leq\sigma_{1}^{\pm}
; by minimality for
\mathcal{F}
we deduce
\sigma_{1}^{\prime}=\sigma_{1}
, and therefore
\sigma_{0}^{\prime}=\sigma_{0}
. Therefore
(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})
is a minimal element for
\preceq
.

Now apply twice Theorem
3.14
first to
u
with the pair
(\widehat{\mu}_{0},\widehat{\mu}_{1})
and then to
\widetilde{u}
(with the reversed flux
\boldsymbol{g}(v)=-f(1-v)
) with the pair
(-\widehat{\mu}_{0},-\widehat{\mu}_{1})
. The new couple of pairs induced by the Lagrangian representations
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}
(which are
good
, according to Theorem
3.14
) must coincide with the starting one, by minimality for the relation
\preceq
.
∎

Remark 3.19
.

We proved that any minimal pair
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
for the relation “
\preceq
” is simultaneously induced. This proves that if a pair is efficient enough, then it is simultaneously induced. A partial converse holds: if a pair
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is simultaneously induced by
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}
,
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}
, then it is “efficient” in the sense that

\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{0}\;\cup\;\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{1}\subset\;\partial\,(\mathrm{hyp}\;u).

3.3.3.
Good Selection of Curves

Given a Lagrangian representation
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathscr{M}^{+}(\Gamma)
, we can select a good set of curves on which it is concentrated. In this direction, with the same proof contained in
[
27
]
, we have the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.20
.

For
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}
-a.e.
\gamma\in\Gamma
it holds that for
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in[0,T]

(1)

(t,\gamma^{x}(t))
is a Lebesgue point of
u
;

(2)

\gamma^{v}(t)<u(t,\gamma^{x}(t))

We denote by
\Gamma_{h}
the set of curves
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that the two properties above hold. Similarly, for
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}
-a.e.
\gamma\in\Gamma
it holds that for
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in[0,T]

(1)

(t,\gamma^{x}(t))
is a Lebesgue point of
u
;

(2)

\gamma^{v}(t)>u(t,\gamma^{x}(t))

and we denote by
\Gamma_{e}
the set of curves
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that the two properties above hold.

4.
Structure of the Kinetic Measures

4.1.
Rectifiability of J

We assume from now on that
u
is a bounded quasi-entropy solution taking values in
[0,1]
,
u\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,[0,1])
with flux
f
. This is not restrictive since we consider in any case bounded quasi-entropy solutions.

Throughout this section, we will use the following structural assumption about the nonlinearity of the flux
f
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a flux
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
weakly genuinely nonlinear
if

(4.1)

\mathscr{L}^{1}\Big(\big\{v\in(0,1)\;\big|\;\tau+\xi\cdot f^{\prime}(v)=0\big\}\Big)=0,\qquad\forall\;(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{S}^{d+1}

In
[
13
]
, the structure of the kinetic measures
\mu_{1}
in (
2.2
) has been studied in general dimension, when
\mu_{0}=0
. These results directly apply also to the case when a source term
\mu_{0}
is present, although some extra care must be used when choosing the representative
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathbf{P}_{\chi}
. We summarize below the results that are obtained directly from
[
13
]
, in our setting. We first recall some definitions.

Definition 4.2
.

In connection to a pair
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\in\mathbf{P}_{\chi}
,

(1)

we denote by
\nu_{0},\nu_{1}
the
(t,x)
marginals of the total variation of
\mu_{1},\mu_{0}
:

(4.2)

\nu_{0}\doteq[p_{t,x}]_{\sharp}|\mu_{0}|,\qquad\nu_{1}\doteq[p_{t,x}]_{\sharp}|\mu_{1}|

where
p_{t,x}:\Omega\times(0,1)\to\Omega
is the natural projection on the
(t,x)
variables. We also let

\nu\doteq\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}.

(2)

we denote by
\mathbf{J}\subset\Omega
the set of points
(t,x)
of positive
\mathscr{H}^{d}
density of
\nu
:

(4.3)

\mathbf{J}\doteq\Bigg\{(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\Big|\;\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{\nu\left(\mathrm{B}_{r}(t,x)\right)}{r^{d}}>0\Bigg\}

(3)

we say that
u:\Omega\to(0,1)
has vanishing mean oscillation at a point
(t,x)
if

(4.4)

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}r^{-d-1}\int_{\mathrm{B}_{r}\left(t,x\right)}|u(s,y)-\overline{u}_{r}(t,x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=0

where
\overline{u}_{r}(t,x)
is the mean of
u
in the ball
\mathrm{B}_{r}(t,x)
.

(4)

Let
J\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be a
d
-rectifiable set with unit normal
\vec{\boldsymbol{n}}
. We call two Borel functions
u^{-},u^{+}:J\to\mathbb{R}
left and right traces of
u
on
J
with respect to
\vec{\boldsymbol{n}}
if, for
\mathscr{H}^{d}
-a.e.
(s,y)\in J
,

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\left(\int_{B^{-}_{r}(s,y)}|\boldsymbol{u}(t,x)-u^{-}(s,y)|\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B^{+}_{r}(s,y)}|\boldsymbol{u}(t,x)-u^{+}(s,y)|\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)=0,

where
B^{\pm}_{r}(s,y):=\{(t,x)\in B_{r}(s,y)\mid\pm\big((t,x)-(s,y)\big)\cdot\vec{\boldsymbol{n}}(s,y)>0\}
.

In our context, the result of
[
13
]
, yields

Theorem 4.3

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
f
be weakly genuinely nonlinear. Let
u
be a quasi-entropy solution of (
1.1
), and let
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
be a minimal pair in
\mathbf{P}_{\chi}
. Then the set
\mathbf{J}
in (
4.3
) is d-rectifiable and

(1)

u
has vanishing mean oscillation at every point
(t,x)\in\mathbf{J}^{c}
, the complement of
\mathbf{J}
,

(2)

u
has left and right traces on
\mathbf{J}
.

(3)

\mu_{\eta}\llcorner\mathbf{J}=((\eta(u^{+}),q(u^{+}))-(\eta(u^{-}),q(u^{-})))\cdot\vec{\boldsymbol{n}}\mathscr{H}^{d}\llcorner\mathbf{J}
, where
u^{\pm}
denotes the traces on
J
and
\vec{\boldsymbol{n}}
denotes the normal to
\mathbf{J}
.

For
BV
solutions (1) and (3) can be improved to

(1’)

every
(t,x)\notin\mathbf{J}
is a Lebesgue point;

(3’)

\mu_{\eta}=((\eta(u^{+}),q(u^{+}))-(\eta(u^{-}),q(u^{-})))\cdot\vec{\boldsymbol{n}}\mathscr{H}^{d}\llcorner\mathbf{J}
.

Remark 4.4
.

One may wonder where the minimality assumption of
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
enters in the proof of Theorem
4.3
: the authors of
[
13
]
consider an equivalent kinetic formulation, for quasi-entropy solutions, which reads:

(4.5)

\chi_{t}+f^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi=\partial_{v}\widetilde{\mu}

where
\widetilde{\mu}
is possibly non-compactly supported in
v
to account for a source term. The link with our
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
(which are compactly supported in
v
) is

\widetilde{\mu}(t,x,v):=\mu_{1}(t,x,v)+\int_{-\infty}^{v}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(t,x,v)

where the second measure in the right hand side denotes the primitive in the
v
variable of the measure
\mu_{0}
.
In
[
13
]
, the rectifiable jump set, let us call it
\widetilde{\mathbf{J}}
, is defined as the set of positive
\mathscr{H}^{d}
-density of the measure

\widetilde{\nu}:=(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}|\widetilde{\mu}|\llcorner(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times(-\infty,|u|_{\infty})).

Our
\mathbf{J}
is instead the set of positive
\mathscr{H}^{d}
-density of the measure
\nu:=(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}(|\mu_{1}|+|\mu_{0}|)
. Therefore to obtain Theorem
4.3
from the results of
[
13
]
we only need to show that

\mathbf{J}=\widetilde{\mathbf{J}}\qquad\text{up to an $\mathscr{H}^{d}$-negligible set}.

To prove this, it is enough to prove that
\nu\ll\widetilde{\nu}
(notice that the other
\widetilde{\nu}\ll\nu
is trivial). By contradiction, assume that there is a measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
with positive measure such that

\nu(A)>0,\qquad\widetilde{\nu}(A)=0.

But this means that

\Big(\mu_{1}+\int_{-\infty}^{v}\mu_{0}(t,x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\Big)\llcorner A=0

that implies

\partial_{v}\mu_{1}\llcorner A+\mu_{0}\llcorner A=0

and therefore we can replace the pair
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
with another pair
(\mu_{0}^{\prime},\mu_{1}^{\prime})\in\mathbf{P}_{\chi}
defined by

\mu_{1}^{\prime}=\mu_{1}\llcorner A^{c},\qquad\mu_{0}^{\prime}=\mu_{0}\llcorner A^{c}.

Clearly
(\mu_{0}^{\prime},\mu_{1}^{\prime})\preceq(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, and this contradicts the minimality of
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
.

Establishing (1’) for general weakly genuinely nonlinear fluxes is an open problem at the time of writing, but in
[
31
]
the author considered the case of entropy solutions with a power-type nonlinearity assumption on
f
, and in this setting he proved that every point
(t,x)\notin J
is a continuity point, providing a
positive answer about (1’) in this particular case. Moreover, in
[
25
]
the author showed that, for general finite entropy solutions, the set of non-Lebesgue points has Hausdorff dimension at most
d
, also providing a partial answer to (1). Also, for entropy solutions in one space dimension both (1’) and (3’) have affirmative answers: see
[
8
]
.

In the following section, we prove that property (3’) holds for quasi-entropy solutions in one space dimension.

4.2.
The One-Dimensional Case

The aim of this section is to extend the rectifiability result
[
27
]
to general genuine nonlinear fluxes in one space dimension. In particular, we will prove the following Theorem.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
d=1
and
f
satisfy (
4.1
). Let
u\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;[0,1])
be a quasi-entropy solution of (
1.1
), and let
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
be a minimal pair in
\mathbf{P}_{\chi}
. Then, in addition to Theorem
4.3
,
\nu_{1}
in (
4.2
) is concentrated on
\mathbf{J}
, i.e.

(4.6)

\nu_{1}=\nu_{1}\llcorner\mathbf{J}

We will actually prove that
\nu_{1}
is concentrated on a
1
-rectifiable set, it is then immediate to see that the set coincides with the set
\mathbf{J}
thanks to formula (3) of Theorem
4.3
.

For convenience, recall that
\Omega=(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
, and in the following we denote

X=\Omega\times(0,1).

The strategy of the proof is as follows. From Proposition
3.18
we know that if
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is a minimal pair, then it is simultaneously induced by some
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}

good
Lagrangian representations of the hypograph and the epigraph of
u
, respectively.
The concentration on
\mathbf{J}
of the full measure
\nu_{1}
will follow by the concentration on
\mathbf{J}
of both the measures
[p_{t,x}]_{\sharp}\mu_{1}^{+}
and
[p_{t,x}]_{\sharp}\mu_{1}^{-}
. Therefore first we prove the result for
[p_{t,x}]_{\sharp}\mu_{1}^{+}
, and the other case will follow by symmetry.

The proof is established in five steps.

1.
We introduce the measures
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\otimes\mu_{1}^{\gamma,+}
,
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}\otimes\mu_{1}^{\gamma,-}\in\mathscr{M}(\Gamma\times X)
, i.e. the measures defined as, for Borel sets
G\in\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)
,
A\in\mathcal{B}(X)
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\otimes{\mu_{1}^{\gamma,+}}(G\times A):=\int_{G}\mu_{1}^{\gamma,+}(A)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}(\gamma),

5.
Regularity of Burgers’ Equation

In this section we provide a first application of the Lagrangian representation to obtain a regularity result for quasi-entropy solutions Burger’s equation.
We first recall the Definition of the Besov spaces
B^{\alpha,p}_{\infty,\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
p\in[1,+\infty)
. A function
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
belongs to
B^{\alpha,p}_{\infty,\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
if

(5.1)

\|u\|^{p}_{B^{\alpha,p}_{\infty}(K)}:=\sup_{h>0}\int_{K}\left|\frac{u(x+h)-u(x)}{h^{\alpha}}\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x<+\infty,\quad\forall\;K\subset\mathbb{R}\quad\text{compact}.

Then we have the following regularity result in terms of Besov spaces.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
u
be a quasi-entropy solution to Burgers equation such that in addition
\mu_{1}
given by Proposition
2.3
is a signed measure.
Then for every
T>0
,
K\subset\mathbb{R}
compact and
\delta>0
there exists a constant
C\equiv C(K,T,\mu_{0})
such that

(5.2)

\int_{\delta}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{B^{1/2,1}_{\infty}(K)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t<\frac{C}{\min\{\delta,1\}}.

Proof.

Up to a symmetry in the
v
, we can assume that
\mu_{1}
is a positive measure.

Moreover, up to choosing a different pair
(\hat{\mu}_{0},\hat{\mu}_{1})
smaller than
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
for
\preceq
, we can assume that
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}
is a Lagrangian representation of the hypograph of
u
(Definition
3.1
) that induces
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
as in Definition
3.3
. Thanks to Proposition
3.18
, we can also assume that
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is simultaneously induced (Definition
3.16
) by
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}
, where
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}
is a Lagrangian representation of the epigraph of
u
(Definition
3.15
). This second step is not really necessary but makes the proof easier. A key point is that these operation preserve the sign of
\mu_{1}
, by definition of the relation
\preceq
. Finally, we denote by
\Gamma_{h}
,
\Gamma_{e}
the set of curves selected by Lemma
3.20
.

1.
Fix
\Delta t>0
and for
t\geq\Delta t
consider the set of curves

\Gamma_{h}^{t,\Delta t}:=\Big\{\gamma_{h}\in\Gamma_{h}\quad\big|\quad t_{\gamma_{h}}^{-}\leq t-\Delta t,\quad t^{+}_{\gamma}\geq t\Big\}

\Gamma_{e}^{t,\Delta t}:=\Big\{\gamma_{e}\in\Gamma_{e}\quad\big|\quad t_{\gamma_{e}}^{-}\leq t-\Delta t,\quad t^{+}_{\gamma}\geq t\Big\}

Define the measures

(5.3)

\displaystyle\chi^{t}_{a^{\Delta t}}:={e_{t}}_{\sharp}\;(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\llcorner\Gamma_{h}^{t,\Delta t})\leq\chi_{h}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\mathscr{L}^{2}\llcorner(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))\quad\text{in $\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))$},

\displaystyle\chi^{t}_{b^{\Delta t}}:={e_{t}}_{\sharp}\;(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{e}\llcorner\Gamma_{e}^{t,\Delta t})\leq\chi_{e}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\mathscr{L}^{2}\llcorner(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))\quad\text{in $\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))$}.

Finally, we define the functions

(5.4)

\displaystyle a^{\Delta t}(t,x):=\mathrm{sup}\;\Big\{v\in(0,1)\;\big|\;(v,x)\in\mathrm{supp}\;\chi^{t}_{a^{\Delta t}}\Big\},\qquad\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle b^{\Delta t}(t,x):=\mathrm{inf}\;\Big\{v\in(0,1)\;\big|\;(v,x)\in\mathrm{supp}\;\chi^{t}_{b^{\Delta t}}\Big\},\qquad\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}.

Notice that the

\mu_{0}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int_{-M}^{M}|a^{\Delta t}(t,x)-u(t,x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\Big(\Big\{\gamma_{h}\in\Gamma_{h}\quad\big|\quad t\in I_{\gamma_{h}},\quad t^{-}_{\gamma_{h}}\in(t-\Delta t,t)\Big\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{0}^{+}(S_{t}^{M,\Delta t})

where

This implies, integrating in
(0,T)
and using Fubini’s Theorem:

(5.6)

\int_{\Delta t}^{T}\int_{-M}^{M}|a^{\Delta t}(t,x)-u(t,x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\Delta t\cdot|\mu_{0}^{+}|\big([0,T]\times(-M-\Delta t,M+\Delta t)\times(0,1)\big).

Entirely symmetrical statements holds for the distance of
u
from

(5.7)

\displaystyle\int_{-M}^{M}|a^{\Delta t}(t,x)-u(t,x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq\mu_{0}^{-}(S_{t}^{M,\Delta t}),

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta t}^{T}\int_{-M}^{M}|b^{\Delta t}(t,x)-u(t,x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\Delta t\cdot|\mu_{0}^{-}|\big([0,T]\times(-M-\Delta t,M+\Delta t)\times(0,1)\big).

By triangular inequality, the difference

(5.8)

\displaystyle\int_{-M}^{M}|a^{\Delta t}(t,x)-b^{\Delta t}(t,x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq|\mu_{0}|(S_{t}^{M,\Delta t}),

Step 3.

\gamma=3

In this section we apply the results of this paper to the system of isentropic gas dynamics with
\gamma=3
, for the evolution of the density
\rho
and the momentum
m=\rho u
of an isentropic gas:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}m=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}m+\partial_{x}(m^{2}/\rho+\rho^{3}/3)=0

The Riemann invariants of the system are

(6.2)

w=\frac{m}{\rho}-\rho,\qquad z=\frac{m}{\rho}+\rho

with corresponding eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}(w,z)=w,\qquad\lambda_{2}(w,z)=z.

We consider solutions that take values in the compact set

\mathcal{K}:=\Big\{(\rho,m)\;\Big|\;|(\rho,m)|\leq M,\quad\rho\geq c\Big\}

where
c,M
are fixed constants from now on.

Definition 6.1

(Entropy solutions in
\mathcal{K}
)
.

A function
\boldsymbol{u}=(\rho,m)\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R};\mathcal{K})
is an entropy solution in
\mathcal{K}
of (
6.1
) if it is a weak solutions of (
6.1
) and it dissipates every convex entropy
\eta:\mathcal{K}\to\mathbb{R}
:

\partial_{t}\eta(\boldsymbol{u})+\partial_{x}q(\boldsymbol{u})\leq 0\quad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}$}

where
q
is the corresponding entropy flux
\nabla q=\nabla\eta Df
, and

f=\begin{pmatrix}m\\
\frac{\rho^{3}}{3}+\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}\end{pmatrix}.

Remark 6.2
.

Some comments are here in order. First, recall that the convexity of the entropy must be checked in the conserved variables
(\rho,m)
. Secondly, a more subtle point is the following: in the definition of an entropy solution in
\mathcal{K}
we require
all
convex entropies
\eta:\mathcal{K}\to\mathbb{R}
to be dissipated. However, it may happen that such entropies cannot be extended as convex functions to the whole set
\{(\rho,m)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R})\;|\;\rho\neq 0\}
. An example of class of solutions that fits our definition is the one vanishing viscosity solutions with the identity viscosity matrix such that
\mathrm{Im}\,\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{K}
for all
\varepsilon>0
, where
\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}
are the viscous approximations solving

\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+f(\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\varepsilon\boldsymbol{u}^{\varepsilon}_{xx}.

We recall that a function
\eta\in C^{2}
is an entropy of the system if and only if it satisfies, in Riemann coordinates
(w,z)
,

(6.3)

\eta_{wz}(w,z)=\frac{-\lambda_{1z}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\eta_{w}(w,z)+\frac{\lambda_{2w}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\eta_{z}(w,z)=0.

It is well known that the system (
6.1
) admits a strictly convex entropy, the energy:

\eta_{E}(\rho,m)=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}/\rho+\frac{1}{6}\rho^{3}.

In the following Proposition we use the energy to prove that, if an entropy solution
(\rho,m)
satisfies
\rho\geq c>0
in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
, then its Riemann invariants are quasi-entropy solutions to the Burgers equation.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}=(\rho,m)\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R},\mathcal{K})
be an entropy solution in
\mathcal{K}
of (
6.1
). Then
w=u-\rho
and
z=u+\rho
are quasi-entropy solutions of the Burgers equation, i.e. they satisfy Definition
2.1
with
f(u)=u^{2}/2
.

Proof.

We prove that
w
is a quasi-entropy solution of the Burgers equation, the proof for
z
being identical. By definition of quasi-entropy solution, we need to show that for every
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\eta\in C^{2}
, there holds

\mu_{\eta}=\partial_{t}\eta(w)+\partial_{x}q(w)\in\mathscr{M}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})

where
q
is the entropy flux of
\eta
,
q^{\prime}(w)=w\eta^{\prime}(w)
. Starting from
\eta
, we construct an entropy-entropy flux pair for the isentropic system (
6.1
) by setting, in Riemann invariants,

\bar{\eta}(w,z):=\eta(w),\qquad\bar{q}(w,z):=q(w)\qquad\forall\;w<z.

It is immediate to check that, if
\eta,q
are entropy entropy fluxes for the Burgers equation, then
\bar{\eta},\bar{q}
are entropy - entropy fluxes for the isentropic system (
6.1
). We now show that
\eta(\rho,m)
is
C^{2}
in the region
\rho>0
when considered as a function of the original variables
\rho,m
. This is fairly trivial, because we have
\eta\in C^{2}
, and morevoer the change of coordinates (
6.2
) is
C^{\infty}
in
\rho>0
. This shows that
\bar{\eta}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\rho=0\})
.
Clearly, since the energy
\eta_{E}
is strictly convex,
\bar{\eta}
is
C^{2}
and
\mathcal{K}
is a compact set, there exists a constant
C>0
big enough such that the entropy
S:=\bar{\eta}+C\eta_{E}
is convex in
\mathcal{K}
. Therefore, since by assumption
\boldsymbol{u}
is an entropy solution in
\mathcal{K}
of (
6.1
) (Definition
6.1
), we have

(6.4)

0\geq\mu_{S}:=\partial_{t}S(\boldsymbol{u})+\partial_{x}Q(\boldsymbol{u})\in\mathscr{M}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})

where
Q
is the entropy flux of
S
, therefore
\mu_{S}
is a nonpositive measure. But now by linearity of the operator
\eta\mapsto\mu_{\eta}
we can write

\mu_{\eta}=\mu_{\bar{\eta}}=\mu_{S}-C\mu_{\eta_{E}}\in\mathscr{M}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})

and this proves the claim.
∎

The following Theorem is due to Lions, Perthame and Tadmor in
[
22
]
. We consider here directly the version for the exponent
\gamma=3
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}
be an entropy solution to (
6.1
). Then for some nonpositive measure
m\in\mathscr{M}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
, there holds

(6.5)

\partial_{t}g(t,x,v)+v\partial_{x}g(t,x,v)=\partial_{vv}m

where

g(t,x,v):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $w(t,x)\leq v\leq z(t,x)$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
w(t,x),z(t,x)
are the Riemann coordinates of
\boldsymbol{u}(t,x)
.

In the following theorem we use Theorem
4.5
and Proposition
6.3
to deduce the measure
(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}m
is concentrated on a
1
-rectifiable set
\mathbf{S}\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}=(\rho,m)\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R};\mathcal{K})
be an entropy solution of (
6.1
). Then
(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}m
is concentrated on a
1
-rectifiable set
\mathbf{S}\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

1.
From Proposition
6.3
we deduce that
w,z
are quasi-entropy solutions to the Burgers equation. By Proposition
2.3
this is equivalent to

(6.6)

\partial_{t}\chi+v\partial_{x}\chi=\mu_{0}+\partial_{v}\mu_{1},\qquad\chi(t,x,v):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $v\leq z(t,x)$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(6.7)

\partial_{t}\psi+v\partial_{x}\psi=\sigma_{0}+\partial_{v}\sigma_{1},\qquad\psi(t,x,v):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $v\leq w(t,x)$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for some locally finite measures
\mu_{0},\sigma_{i}
.

Recall the Definition of the sets
\mathbf{P}_{\chi},\mathbf{P}_{\psi}
given in (
3.30
), which are the sets of all the pairs
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
and
(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})
of locally finite measures for which (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) are satisfied, respectively. We can assume, up to replacing the measures
\mu_{i},\sigma_{i}
, that the pairs
(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
and
(\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})
are minimal with respect to the relation
\preceq
introduced in Definition
3.17
. Therefore from Theorems
4.3
,
4.5
we readily deduce that
\nu_{1}:=(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}|\mu_{1}|
and
\xi_{1}:=(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}|\sigma_{1}|
are concentrated on the
1
-rectifiable set
\mathbf{S}\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
which is defined as

(6.8)

\mathbf{S}:=\Bigg\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\;\Big|\;\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}\big(|\mu_{1}|+|\sigma_{1}|+|\mu_{0}|+|\sigma_{0}|\big)(B_{r}(t,x))}{r}>0\Bigg\}

2.
Consider the disintegrations, for
i=0,1
,

\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}^{(t,x)}\otimes\nu_{i},\qquad\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i}^{(t,x)}\otimes\xi_{i}

\nu_{i}:=(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}|\mu_{i}|,\quad\xi_{i}:=(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}|\sigma_{i}|

Thanks to Proposition
4.14
, we deduce that for some measurable functions
\bar{w}(t,x)
and
\bar{z}(t,x)

(6.9)

\displaystyle\mu_{0}^{(t,x)}=e(t,x)\delta_{\bar{z}(t,x)},\qquad\text{for $\nu_{0}$-a.e. $(t,x)\in\mathbf{S}^{c}$}

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}^{(t,x)}=\widetilde{e}(t,x)\delta_{\bar{w}(t,x)},\qquad\text{for $\xi_{0}$-a.e. $(t,x)\in\mathbf{S}^{c}$}

where
e,\widetilde{e}
are some measurable functions with values only in
\{-1,1\}
.
Notice that

\rho(t,x)=z(t,x)-w(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(t,x,v)-\psi(t,x,v)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v,

m(t,x)=\int_{w(t,x)}^{z(t,x)}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v=\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(\chi(t,x,v)-\psi(t,x,v))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v

therefore by subtracting (
6.7
) from (
6.6
) and integrating in
v
we obtain the equation for the conservation of mass

(6.10)

0=\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}m=(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}(\mu_{0}-\sigma_{0})\qquad\text{in $\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{t,x}$}.

Using (
6.9
), we therefore deduce that

e(t,x)\nu_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}=(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}\mu_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}=(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}\sigma_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}=\widetilde{e}(t,x)\xi_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}\qquad

and since
\nu_{0},\xi_{0}
are positive measures and
e,\widetilde{e}\in\{-1,1\}
, we conclude

(6.11)

\xi_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}=\nu_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}\quad\text{and}\quad e(t,x)=\widetilde{e}(t,x)\qquad\text{for $\nu_{0}$-a.e. and $\xi_{0}$-a.e.}

We deduced that
\mu_{0},\sigma_{0}
restricted to
\mathbf{S}^{c}
write in the form

(6.12)

\mu_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}=e(t,x)\delta_{\bar{z}(t,x)}\otimes\mathbf{j},\qquad\sigma_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}=e(t,x)\delta_{\bar{w}(t,x)}\otimes\mathbf{j}

where
\mathbf{j}:=\nu_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}=\xi_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}
.
Using also the elementary identity

\frac{m(t,x)^{2}}{\rho(t,x)}+\frac{\rho(t,x)^{3}}{12}=\int_{w(t,x)}^{z(t,x)}v^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v=\int_{\mathbb{R}}v^{2}(\chi(t,x,v)-\psi(t,x,v))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v

we deduce, by multiplying (
6.6
), (
6.7
) by
v
and subtracting (
6.7
) from (
6.6
), that

(6.13)

0=\partial_{t}m+\partial_{x}\Big(\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+\frac{\rho^{3}}{3}\Big)=(p_{t,x})_{\sharp}(\sigma_{1}-\mu_{1})+\int v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\mu_{0}-\sigma_{0})(\cdot,\cdot,v)

where we denote by
\int v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\mu_{0}-\sigma_{0})(\cdot,\cdot,v)
the measure on
(t,x)
defined by

\iint\varphi(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\Big[\int v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\mu_{0}-\sigma_{0})(\cdot,\cdot,v)\Big](t,x):=\iiint\varphi(t,x)v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}-\iiint\varphi(t,x)v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{0}

for all
\varphi:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous with compact support.
Since
\sigma_{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}=0=\mu_{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}
, we deduce from (
6.13
) that

(6.14)

0=\int v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\mu_{0}-\sigma_{0})(\cdot,\cdot,v)\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}

which, using the expression (
6.12
), means

(6.15)

\bar{z}(t,x)\mathbf{j}=\bar{w}(t,x)\mathbf{j}\qquad\text{for $\mathbf{j}$-a.e. $(t,x)$.}

But this just means
\bar{w}(t,x)=\bar{z}(t,x)
for
\mathbf{j}
-a.e.
(t,x)
. Therefore

\partial_{vv}m\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}=\partial_{v}(\mu_{1}-\sigma_{1})\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}+(\mu_{0}-\sigma_{0})\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{c}=0.

Since
m
is compactly supported in
v
, this implies
m=0
in
\mathbf{S}^{c}
and this concludes the proof.
∎

We now analyze the shocks structure in order to obtain more information about the dissipation measures.

Definition 6.6
.

A pair of states
\boldsymbol{u}^{-},\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\in\mathcal{K}
is an
admissible shock
if

•

The Rankine-Hugoniot conditions hold:

(6.16)

\displaystyle\frac{z_{+}^{2}}{2}-\frac{z_{-}^{2}}{2}-\sigma(z_{+}-z_{-})=\frac{w_{+}^{2}}{2}-\frac{w_{-}^{2}}{2}-\sigma(w_{+}-w_{-})

\displaystyle\frac{z_{+}^{3}}{3}-\frac{z_{-}^{3}}{3}-\sigma\Big(\frac{z_{+}^{2}}{2}-\frac{z_{-}^{2}}{2}\Big)=\frac{w_{+}^{3}}{3}-\frac{w_{-}^{3}}{3}-\sigma\Big(\frac{w_{+}^{2}}{2}-\frac{w_{-}^{2}}{2}\Big)

•

The energy dissipation holds:

(6.17)

d_{E}:=\frac{z_{+}^{4}}{4}-\frac{z_{-}^{4}}{4}-\sigma\Big(\frac{z_{+}^{3}}{3}-\frac{z_{-}^{3}}{3}\Big)-\frac{w_{+}^{4}}{4}-\frac{w_{-}^{4}}{4}-\sigma\Big(\frac{w_{+}^{3}}{3}-\frac{w_{-}^{3}}{3}\Big)\leq 0

By the same arguments of
[
13
]
we deduce that
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-a.e. point
(t,x)\in\mathbf{S}
is an admissible shock, where
\boldsymbol{u}^{-},\boldsymbol{u}^{+}
are the left and right traces on
\mathbf{S}
.

The proof of the following lemma is classical, and is a special case of the second order contact between the rarefaction and shock curve, together with the fact that the entropy dissipation for genuinely nonlinear systems is cubic with respect to the shock strength; we refer to
[
12
, Chapter 5]
for a proof of this well known facts.

Lemma 6.7
.

If
\boldsymbol{u}^{-},\boldsymbol{u}^{+}
is an admissible shock, then there is a positive constant
C>0
such that one of the following holds:

(a)

Shocks of the first family:
w_{+}<w_{-}
, and

|z_{+}-z_{-}|\leq C\cdot(w_{+}-w_{-})^{3},\qquad\left|\sigma-\frac{w_{+}+w_{-}}{2}\right|\leq C\cdot(w_{+}-w_{-})^{3}

d_{E}(w^{+},w^{-},z^{+},z^{-})\leq-C\cdot|w_{+}-w_{-}|^{3}

(b)

Shocks of the second family:
z_{+}<z_{-}
, and

|w_{+}-w_{-}|\leq C\cdot(z_{-}-z_{+})^{3},\qquad\left|\sigma-\frac{z_{+}+z_{-}}{2}\right|\leq C\cdot(z_{-}-z_{+})^{3}

d_{E}(w^{+},w^{-},z^{+},z^{-})\leq-C\cdot|z_{+}-z_{-}|^{3}

Next, we use the structure of the shock to deduce that it is possible to choose the measures
\mu_{1},\sigma_{1}
in (
6.6
), (
6.7
) with a definite sign.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R},\mathcal{K})
be an entropy solution of (
6.1
). Then the Riemann invariants satisfy

(6.18)

\partial_{t}\chi+v\partial_{x}\chi=\mu_{0}+\partial_{v}\mu_{1},\qquad\chi(t,x,v):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $v\leq z(t,x)$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(6.19)

\partial_{t}\psi+v\partial_{x}\psi=\sigma_{0}+\partial_{v}\sigma_{1},\qquad\psi(t,x,v):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $v\leq w(t,x)$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
\mu_{1},\sigma_{1}
are signed measures.

Proof.

For almost every point
(t,x)\in\mathbf{S}
, (
6.16
) and (
6.17
) are satisfied, where
z_{+},z_{-}
and
w_{+},w_{-}
are the left and right traces on
\mathbf{S}
, which exist thanks to Theorems
4.3
,
6.3
.
By (3) of Theorem
4.3
we can characterize the distributions on the right hand side of (
6.6
), (
6.7
).

(6.20)

\langle\mu_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{J}_{w}+\partial_{v}\mu_{1}\llcorner\mathbf{J}_{w},\eta^{\prime}\varphi\rangle=\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{w_{-}}^{w_{+}}\eta^{\prime}(v)(v-\sigma)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{1}\llcorner\mathbf{J}_{w},

(6.21)

\langle\sigma_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{J}_{z}+\partial_{v}\sigma_{1}\llcorner\mathbf{J}_{z},\eta^{\prime}\varphi\rangle=\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{z_{-}}^{z_{+}}\eta^{\prime}(v)(v-\sigma)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{1}\llcorner\mathbf{J}_{z}.

Here as in the proof of Theorem
6.5

\mu_{i},\sigma_{i}
are chosen to be minimal with respect to the relation
\preceq
and
\mathbf{J}_{w},\mathbf{J}_{z}
are the corresponding jump sets (
4.3
).
We claim that up to an
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-negligible set there holds

(6.22)

\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{J}_{w}=\mathbf{J}_{z}.

In fact, by definition of
\mathbf{S}
we have
\mathbf{J}_{w}\cup\mathbf{J}_{z}=\mathbf{S}
. Since both
w,z
admit left and right strong traces on
\mathbf{S}
(see
[
35
]
), we deduce that on
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-almost every point of
\mathbf{S}
the Rankine-Hugoniot conditions (
6.16
) hold. In particular, the following holds:

(6.23)

w^{-}(x)\neq w^{+}(x)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad z^{-}(x)\neq z^{+}(x)\quad\text{for $\mathscr{H}^{1}$-a.e. $x\in\mathbf{S}$}.

By the representation formula (3) of Theorem
4.3
, we deduce
w^{-}\neq w^{+}
for
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-a.e. in
\mathbf{J}_{w}
, which then implies by (
6.23
) that
z^{-}\neq z^{+}
for
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-a.e. in
\mathbf{J}_{w}
. But then
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-a.e. point
x\in\mathbf{J}_{w}
cannot be a vanishing mean oscillation points for
z
, and therefore by Theorem
4.3
they are contained in
\mathbf{J}_{z}
up to
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-negligible subsets: this shows
\mathbf{J}_{w}\subset\mathbf{J}_{z}
. The converse inclusion is proved symmetrically, and this ultimately proves the claim (
6.22
).

We partition
\mathbf{S}
into the 1-shocks
\mathbf{S}^{1}
and 2-shocks
\mathbf{S}^{2}
accordingly to Lemma
6.7
.
In particular, say for the
1
-shocks, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}+\partial_{v}\mu_{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{(w^{+},w^{-})}(v)(v-\sigma)\mathscr{H}^{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{(w^{+},w^{-})}(v)(v-\bar{\sigma})\mathscr{H}^{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}+\mathbf{1}_{(w^{+},w^{-})}(v)(\bar{\sigma}-\sigma)\mathscr{H}^{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}

\displaystyle=:\partial_{v}\widetilde{\mu}_{1}+\widetilde{\mu}_{0}

where
\bar{\sigma}=(w^{+}+w^{-})/2
and

\widetilde{\mu}_{1}=\mathbf{1}_{(w^{+},w^{-})}(v)(f(v)-f(w^{+})-\bar{\sigma}(v-w^{+}))\mathscr{H}^{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}

where
f(v)=v^{2}/2
. Moreover

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}+\partial_{v}\sigma_{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{(z^{-},z^{+})}(v)(v-\sigma)\mathscr{H}^{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}=:\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}

Lemma
6.7
immediately implies that
\widetilde{\mu}_{1}
has a positive sign, and that
\widetilde{\mu}_{0},\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}
are locally finite measures since they satisfy, for some constant
C>0
,

|\widetilde{\mu}_{0}|,|\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}|\leq C|\mu_{\eta_{E}}|\qquad\text{as measures}

where
\eta_{E}
is the energy dissipation.
Therefore we have on 1-shocks that

\mu_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}+\partial_{v}\mu_{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}=\partial_{v}\widetilde{\mu}_{1}+\widetilde{\mu}_{0},\qquad\sigma_{0}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}+\partial_{v}\sigma_{1}\llcorner\mathbf{S}^{1}=\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}

where
\widetilde{\mu}_{1}
is a positive measure, and
\widetilde{\mu}_{0},\widetilde{\sigma}_{0}
are locally finite measures. An entirely symmetric decomposition holds for 2-shocks, and this concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 6.9
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}=(\rho,m)\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R};\mathcal{K})
be an entropy solution of (
6.1
). Then
(\rho,m)\in B^{1/2,1}_{\infty,loc}
.

Proof.

The proof follows immediately by combining Theorem
5.2
and Lemma
6.8
.
∎

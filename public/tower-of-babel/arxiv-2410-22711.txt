Conditional estimates for L L -functions in the Selberg class II
Neea Palojärvi and Aleksander Simonič

Abstract
. Assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, we provide uniform upper and lower bounds with explicit main terms for log ⁡ | ℒ ⁡ ( s ) | \log{\left|\mathcal{L}(s)\right|} for σ ∈ ( 1 / 2 , 1 ) \sigma\in(1/2,1) and for functions in the Selberg class. In particular, we focus on the region 0 ≤ σ − 1 / 2 ≪ 1 / log ⁡ log ⁡ ( q ℒ ​ | t | d ℒ ) 0\leq\sigma-1/2\ll 1/\log{\log{\left(\mathrm{q}_{\mathcal{L}}|t|^{\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{L}}}\right)}} . We also provide estimates under additional assumptions on the distribution of Dirichlet coefficients of ℒ ⁡ ( s ) \mathcal{L}(s) on prime numbers. Moreover, by assuming a polynomial Euler product representation for ℒ ⁡ ( s ) \mathcal{L}(s) , we establish both uniform bounds and completely explicit estimates by also assuming the strong λ \lambda -conjecture. In addition to providing estimates for a large set of functions, our results improve the best known estimates for specific functions in the Selberg class including the lower bounds for the Riemann zeta function close to the critical line.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Introduction to the Selberg class

The Selberg class of functions
\mathcal{S}
was introduced in
[
Sel92
]
and consists of Dirichlet series

\mathcal{L}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}},

where
s=\sigma+\mathrm{i}t
for real numbers
\sigma
and
t
, satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

Ramanujan hypothesis
. We have
a(n)\ll_{\varepsilon}n^{\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

(ii)

Analytic continuation
. There exists
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
(s-1)^{k}\mathcal{L}(s)
is an entire function of finite order.

(iii)

Functional equation
. The function
\mathcal{L}(s)
satisfies
\mathfrak{L}(s)=\omega\overline{\mathfrak{L}(1-\bar{s})}
, where

\mathfrak{L}(s)=\mathcal{L}(s)Q^{s}\prod_{j=1}^{f}\Gamma\left(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j}\right)

with
\left(Q,\lambda_{j}\right)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, and
\left(\mu_{j},\omega\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
with
\Re\{\mu_{j}\}\geq 0
and
|\omega|=1
.

(iv)

Euler product
. The function
\mathcal{L}(s)
has a product representation

\mathcal{L}(s)=\prod_{p}\exp{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{b\left(p^{k}\right)}{p^{ks}}\right)},

where the coefficients
b\left(p^{k}\right)
satisfy
b\left(p^{k}\right)\ll p^{k\theta}
for some
0\leq\theta<1/2
.

Examples of functions in
\mathcal{S}
include the Riemann zeta-function
\zeta(s)
, Dirichlet
L
-functions
L(s,\chi)
for a primitive characters
\chi
modulo
q
and Dedekind zeta-functions
\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)
for a number fields
\mathbb{K}
with degree
n_{\mathbb{K}}=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]
and discriminant
\Delta_{\mathbb{K}}
.

It is well-known that the data from the functional equation is not uniquely defined by
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{S}
. However, one can show that the degree
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{L}}
, the conductor
\mathrm{q}_{\mathcal{L}}
and
\xi
-invariant,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{L}}:=2\sum_{j=1}^{f}\lambda_{j},\quad\mathrm{q}_{\mathcal{L}}:=(2\pi)^{\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{L}}}Q^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{f}\lambda_{j}^{2\lambda_{j}},\quad\xi_{\mathcal{L}}:=2\sum_{j=1}^{f}\left(\mu_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right),

are indeed invariants for the class
\mathcal{S}
(see, e.g.,
[
Odž11
]
for survey).
For example, for the above mentioned examples of functions we have:

(1)

The Riemann zeta-function
\zeta(s)
:
\mathrm{d}_{\zeta}=1
,
\mathrm{q}_{\zeta}=1
and
\xi_{\zeta}=-1
.

(2)

Dirichlet
L
-functions
L(s,\chi)
:
\mathrm{d}_{L(s,\chi)}=1
,
\mathrm{q}_{L(s,\chi)}=q
and
\xi_{L(s,\chi)}=-1
if
\chi(-1)=1
and
0
otherwise.

(3)

Dedekind zeta-functions
\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}(s)
:
\mathrm{d}_{\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}}=n_{\mathbb{K}}
,
\mathrm{q}_{\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}}=\left|\Delta_{\mathbb{K}}\right|
and
\xi_{\zeta_{\mathbb{K}}}=-(r+1)
.

The logarithmic derivative of
\mathcal{L}
is

\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}(s)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}(n)}{n^{s}}

for
\sigma>1
, where
\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}(n)
is
the generalized von Mangoldt function
. The Euler product representation implies
\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}(n)=b(n)\log{n}
with
\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}(n)=0
for
n
not being a prime power.
In analogy with
\log{\zeta(s)}
, we define

\log{\mathcal{L}(s)}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}(n)}{n^{s}\log{n}}

for
\sigma>1
, where the value of
\log{\mathcal{L}(s)}
is that which tends to
0
as
\sigma\to\infty
for any fixed
t
. For
\sigma<2
and
t\neq 0
, we define
\log{\mathcal{L}(s)}
as the analytic continuation of the above function along the straight line from
2+\mathrm{i}t
to
\sigma+\mathrm{i}t
, provided that
\mathcal{L}(s)\neq 0
on this segment of line. Note that GRH implies holomorphicity of
\log{\mathcal{L}(z)}
on the domain
\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\Re\{z\}>1/2\right\}\setminus(1/2,1]
.

In some of the results, we are able to derive sharper estimates by assuming that
\mathcal{L}(s)
has a polynomial Euler product representation. Indeed, then axiom (iv) is replaced by a stronger hypothesis

\mathcal{L}(s)=\prod_{p}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{j}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1},

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
is the
order of a polynomial Euler product
and
\alpha_{j}(p)\in\mathbb{C}
,
1\leq j\leq m
, are some functions which are defined for every prime number
p
. Denote by
\mathcal{SP}
the class of such functions. If
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{SP}
, then

b\left(p^{k}\right)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\alpha_{j}(p)\right)^{k},

and because
\left|\alpha_{j}(p)\right|\leq 1
for
1\leq j\leq m
, see
[
Ste07
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have that

(1.1)

\left|\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}(n)\right|\leq m\Lambda(n).

1.2.
On upper and lower bounds for
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{S}

Estimates for logarithms and logarithmic derivatives of
L
-functions have applications to many other interesting number theory questions. These include the number of zeros and the argument of the Selberg class functions
[
CCM19
,
Chi19
,
Pal19
,
Sim22b
]
, number of primes, primes in arithmetic progressions, Goldbach summatory functions and their generalizations
[
BEP24
,
Dav00
,
EHP22
,
MV07
]
, characterization of positive-definite integral quadratic forms that are co-prime-universal for
p

[
BC24
]
, Mertens’ theorem
[
Sim23
]
, moments of functions
[
BF24
,
Sou09
]
and character sums
[
Č23
]
. Numerical estimates for the logarithms are helpful, for example, when verifying that the Generalized Riemann hypothesis holds up to some height (see e.g.
[
Boo06
]
).

In 1924–1925, Littlewood showed
[
Lit24
,
Lit25
]
that under the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), we have

(1.2)

\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}t\right)\leq\exp\left(\left(C+o(1)\right)\frac{\log t}{\log{\log{t}}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\log{\zeta(s)}\ll\frac{(\log t)^{2-2\sigma}}{\log{\log{t}}}

for some
C>0
,
1/2+\delta\leq\sigma\leq 1-\delta
, fixed
\delta\in(0,1/4)
and
t
large enough. It is possible to extend his techniques in
[
Lit25
]
(see also
[
Tit86
, Section 14.5]
) to the region right of the
1
-line, see
[
Tit86
, Section 14.33]
, and obtain

\log{\zeta(s)}\ll\frac{(\log t)^{2-2\sigma}}{\log{\log{t}}}\min\left\{\dfrac{1}{|\sigma-1|},\log\log t\right\}+\log{\log{\log{t}}}

for
1/2+1/\log{\log{t}}\leq\sigma\leq 3/2
and
t
large enough, see
[
MV07
, Corollaries 13.14 and 13.16]
. A few years later, Titchmarsh
[
Tit28
]
proved that unconditionally

\log{\left|\zeta(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t)\right|}\gg\left(\log{t}\right)^{1-\sigma-\varepsilon},

where
\sigma\in[1/2,1)
is fixed and
\varepsilon\in(0,1-\sigma)
. Montgomery
[
Mon97
]
improved this result by considering the sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
, and proved that his result holds with a slightly better region for each
t_{n}
if RH is assumed.

These results, under GRH and with
\sigma\in[1/2,1)
, have been improved several times, extended to other functions, and explicit results have been proved in addition to asymptotic ones (see, e.g.,
[
AP17
,
BF24
,
CCM19
,
CC11
,
CM24
,
Cha09
,
CS11
,
Chi19
,
PS22
,
Sim22a
,
Sim22b
,
XY22
]
). Here,
explicit estimate
means that all “hidden” constants in a corresponding non-explicit result are computed or expressed with known parameters via some algebraic formulae. For example, the results in (
1.2
) are not explicit since we do not know the constants in front of the estimates, but the estimate in
[
Cha09
, Corollary 1.1]
is explicit. It should be noted that there is variation how the term
explicit
is used in the literature. Sometimes, it refers to results where the main term in the estimate is explicitly given and the rest of the terms are asymptotically estimated (see, e.g.,
[
CG22
,
Chi19
,
Yan24
]
). Note that these results are not explicit according to the definition in this paper.

It is known
[
AP17
, Propositon 1.1]
that if GRH and

(1.3)

\sum_{p\leq x}\left|a(p)\right|^{2}=\left(\kappa+o(1)\right)\frac{x}{\log{x}},\quad\kappa>0,

hold for
\mathcal{L}
, then for every fixed constant
\sigma\in[1/2,1)
there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(1.4)

\mathcal{L}(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t)\ll\exp\left(c\frac{(\log{t})^{2-2\sigma}}{\log{\log{t}}}\right)

for
t
large enough. Recently, the authors
[
PS22
]
extended methods that had been used to provide explicit bounds for logarithmic derivatives of Dirichlet
L
-functions
[
CHS24
]
, and provided uniform and explicit upper bounds for moduli of the logarithm and logarithmic derivative for the Selberg class functions assuming GRH and additional hypothesis. For example, we provided a precise form of (
1.4
) under the same assumptions and when
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
is fixed. The results in
[
PS22
]
were stated for the region
\sigma\geq 1/2+\alpha_{1}/\log{\log{\tau}}
, where
\tau=\mathrm{q}_{\mathcal{L}}|t|^{\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{L}}}
and
\alpha_{1}>\frac{1}{2}\log{2}
. This leaves the case

(1.5)

\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\log{\log{\tau}}}

to be investigated in this paper. In particular, we also provided a precise form of (
1.4
) for
\sigma=1/2
.

Assuming that a Selberg class function
\mathcal{L}(s)
has a polynomial Euler product representation (of order
m
) and that (
1.3
) holds, Aistleitner and Pańkowski
[
AP17
]
provided explicit estimates for the main term of the maximum of the lower bound of the function
\mathcal{L}(s)
. Their statement is that

\max_{t\in[T,2T]}\left|\mathcal{L}(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t)\right|\geq\exp\left(\left(\kappa^{\sigma}m^{1-2\sigma}\frac{(3-2\sigma)^{3/2-\sigma}}{2\sqrt{2\sigma-1}}+o(1)\right)\frac{\left(\log{T}\right)^{1-\sigma}}{\log{\log{T}}}\right),

where
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
and
T
is large enough. They also provided a similar type of bound in the case
\sigma=1/2
. The current best explicit lower bound for the absolute value of the Riemann zeta function in the region
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
and under RH is provided by the second author in
[
Sim22a
]
, where the case
1\leq\sigma<3/2
is also considered. In general, there seems to be very few known conditional and explicit lower bounds for other Selberg class functions than the Riemann zeta function when
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
.

A typical method to derive upper bounds for the Selberg class functions is to find a suitable way to manipulate these into sums of primes and zeros and then estimate the sums itself in meaningful ways (see e.g.
[
CC11
,
CCM19
,
CS11
,
Chi19
,
CG22
,
CHS24
,
PS22
,
Sou09
]
). However, finding a good way to write the sums or estimate them, is not trivial and also depends on the region under consideration. The approach that recasts the sums using (a modified version of) Selberg’s moment formula
[
MV07
, Section 13.2]
and
[
IK04
, Theorems 5.17 and 5.19]
, as for example in
[
CHS24
,
PS22
]
, or directly using the functional equation (e.g.
[
CC11
,
CS11
,
CG22
]
), have proven to be successful and allow many generalizations. To estimate the terms coming from the sums,
bandlimited majorants
are often used and then the Guinand-Weil explicit formula is applied to the majorants.
Bandlimited
majorants refer to majorants that have compactly supported Fourier transforms and the Guinand-Weil explicit formula connects a sum over zeros to Fourier transforms. The method of applying majorants and the Guinand-Weil explicit formula in this setting was first used by Goldston and Gonek
[
GG07
]
to estimate the argument of the Riemann zeta function and by Chandee and Soundararajan
[
CS11
]
to estimate the Riemann zeta function on the critical line.

Explicit lower bounds can be derived using similar ideas of bandlimited minorants and the Guinand–Weil explicit formula as in the case with the upper bounds
[
CC11
]
. However, several other methods have been successfully used to derive lower bounds. These include applying suitable integral representations of
\log{\zeta(s)}
, see
[
Sim22a
]
, the resonance method
[
AP17
,
Sou08
,
Yan23
]
and Diophantine approximations
[
AP17
,
PS13
]
. The resonance method is based on an idea to reduce the problem of estimating certain weighted means of an
L
-function, see
[
AP17
]
for the comparison between the resonance method and Diophantine approximations, and is a powerful method to deduce
\Omega
-results.

In this paper, we apply Carneiro–Chandee’s methods
[
CC11
]
for the Riemann zeta function combined with refinements by Bui and Florea
[
BF24
]
and our own ones to derive conditional upper and lower bounds for
\log{\left|\mathcal{L}(s)\right|}
for functions in the Selberg class with
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
. In addition, upper bounds are derived in the case of
\sigma=1/2
, see Corollaries
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.5
, and upper and lower bounds in the case of
0<\sigma-1/2\ll 1/\log{\log{\tau}}
where
\tau
is defined by (
1.6
), see Corollary
2.3
and Theorems
3
,
4
and
5
. In particular, we improve
[
BF24
, Equation (2.8)]
and also
[
Cha09
, Corollary 1.4]
for
t\geq\exp{\left(\exp{(19)}\right)}
. The results contain asymptotic and explicit estimates, and the proofs involve computational methods. We apply the functional equation to recast the question to estimate a certain sum over zeros, and then apply bandlimited majorants and minorants as well as the Guinand–Weil explicit formula to estimate the sum. The main results are described in Section
2
. They are uniform in
\mathcal{L}
, that is, the implied
O
-constants are independent of the parameters from the axioms of the Selberg class.
It should be noted (as mentioned in
[
PS22
, p. 3]
) that in
[
CC11
, Theorems 1 and 2 (cases “otherwise”)]
the coefficients of the main error terms should be

\pm\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{2\sigma-1}{\sigma(1-\sigma)}\right)\quad\textrm{instead of}\quad\pm\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2\sigma-1}{\sigma(1-\sigma)}\right),

see also Theorem
3
. In addition to this, for
\sigma\in[1/2+\delta,1-\delta]
and some
\delta>0
, results presented here are asymptotically better than the relevant results in
[
PS22
]
for
m\leq 2
. Interestingly, this is no longer true if
m\geq 3
.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
we state the main results. In Section

\log{\left|\mathcal{L}(s)\right|}
can be estimated with a certain sum over the non-trivial zeros, see Proposition

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that real numbers
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
and

satisfy
x_{1}>0
,
x_{2}>0
and
y\neq 0

(3.1)

\log{\left|\frac{\Gamma(x_{1}+\mathrm{i}y)}{\Gamma(x_{2}+\mathrm{i}y)}\right|}=\left(x_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log{|x_{1}+\mathrm{i}y|}-\left(x_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log{|x_{2}+\mathrm{i}y|}\\
+\frac{\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)\left(y^{2}-x_{1}x_{2}\right)}{12|x_{1}+\mathrm{i}y|^{2}|x_{2}+\mathrm{i}y|^{2}}+\frac{x_{1}x_{2}\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)}{y^{2}+x_{1}x_{2}}+R(x_{1},x_{2},y),

where

(3.2)

\left|R(x_{1},x_{2},y)\right|\leq\frac{|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{3}}{|y|^{2}}+\frac{1}{45|y|^{3}}.

Proof.

Let
\Re\{z\}>0
. By Stirling’s formula we have

\log{\Gamma(z)}=\left(z-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log{z}-z+\frac{1}{2}\log{(2\pi)}+\frac{1}{12z}+R_{1}(z),\quad\left|R_{1}(z)\right|\leq\frac{1}{90|z|^{3}},

see
[
Olv74
, p. 294]
. Write
z_{1}=x_{1}+\mathrm{i}y
and
z_{2}=x_{2}+\mathrm{i}y
. Then, if
y\neq 0

(3.3)

\log{\left|\frac{\Gamma(z_{1})}{\Gamma(z_{2})}\right|}=\left(x_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log{|z_{1}|}-\left(x_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log{|z_{2}|}+\frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{|z_{1}|^{2}}-\frac{x_{2}}{|z_{2}|^{2}}\right)\\
-y\left(\arg{(z_{1})}-\arg{(z_{2})}\right)+\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)+\Re\left\{R_{1}(z_{1})-R_{1}(z_{2})\right\}.

Note that
\arctan{u}=u+R_{2}(u)
, where
u\in\mathbb{R}
and
\left|R_{2}(u)\right|\leq|u|^{3}
. Therefore,

(3.4)

\arg{(z_{1})}-\arg{(z_{2})}=\arctan\left(\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{y\left(1+\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{y^{2}}\right)}\right)=\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{y\left(1+\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{y^{2}}\right)}+R_{2}\left(\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{y\left(1+\frac{x_{1}x_{2}}{y^{2}}\right)}\right).

We obtain (

) after combining (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), and taking

,
|z_{2}|\geq|y|
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{S}
with
\mathcal{L}(1)\neq 0
. Then

(3.5)

s^{m_{\mathcal{L}}}(s-1)^{m_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathfrak{L}(s)=e^{A_{\mathcal{L}}+B_{\mathcal{L}}s}\prod_{\rho}\left(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\right)e^{s/\rho},

where
\rho
are the non-trivial zeros of
\mathcal{L}(s)
and
A_{\mathcal{L}}
and
B_{\mathcal{L}}
are some constants which depend on
\mathcal{L}
. Moreover,
\Re\left\{B_{\mathcal{L}}\right\}=-\sum_{\rho}\Re\left\{1/\rho\right\}
.

Proof.

We know that
(s-1)^{m_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathcal{L}(s)
is entire function of order
1
, see
[
CKS23
, Lemma 4.2]
. Conditions of Lemma
3.2
imply that
s^{m_{\mathcal{L}}}(s-1)^{m_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathfrak{L}(s)
is also entire function of order
1
, with only zeros at the non-trivial zeros
\rho\notin\{0,1\}
of
\mathcal{L}(s)
. Then Hadamard’s factorization theorem guarantees (
3.5
), with some constants
A_{\mathcal{L}}
and
B_{\mathcal{L}}
which depend on
\mathcal{L}
.

We closely follow
[
IK04
, p. 103]
. By (
3.5
) and the functional equation we have

\displaystyle e^{A_{\mathcal{L}}+B_{\mathcal{L}}s}\prod_{\rho}\left(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\right)e^{s/\rho}

\displaystyle=s^{m_{\mathcal{L}}}(s-1)^{m_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathfrak{L}(s)

\displaystyle=\omega\overline{\left(\bar{s}^{m_{\mathcal{L}}}\left(\bar{s}-1\right)^{m_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathfrak{L}(1-\bar{s})\right)}=\omega e^{\overline{A_{\mathcal{L}}}+\overline{B_{\mathcal{L}}}(1-s)}\prod_{\rho}\left(1-\frac{1-s}{\bar{\rho}}\right)e^{(1-s)/\bar{\rho}}.

After taking logarithmic derivative on both sides of the above equality, we obtain

2\Re\left\{B_{\mathcal{L}}\right\}=-\sum_{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac{1}{1-s-\bar{\rho}}+\frac{1}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\bar{\rho}}\right)=-\sum_{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac{1}{1-s-\bar{\rho}}\right)-2\sum_{\rho}\Re\left\{\frac{1}{\rho}\right\}

since the second series is absolutely convergent. Taking

in the above equality, and observing that

is the non-trivial zero if and only if

is the non-trivial zero, gives
\Re\left\{B_{\mathcal{L}}\right\}=-\sum_{\rho}\Re\left\{1/\rho\right\}
.
∎

\Re\left\{\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(z)\right\}=\log{|z|}+A(z),

\left|A(z)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\Re\{z\}+\frac{1}{6}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{15|z|^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}.

Proof.

By

∎

We start with a lemma about an estimate of the modulus of

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

(5.1)

\left|\frac{\sin\left(\pi\xi\right)}{\pi\xi}\right|^{2}\leq\delta{(\xi)}\frac{e^{2\pi|\Im\{\xi\}|}}{1+|\xi|^{2}},\quad\delta{(\xi)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\begin{cases}1,&|\xi|<1,\\
\frac{2}{\pi^{2}},&|\xi|\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Firstly, we will prove the lemma for
|\xi|<1
. For
z\in\Omega^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{w\in\mathbb{C}\colon|w|<1,\Im\{w\}>0\right\}
define

f^{+}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left|\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}-1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\right|^{2}\left(1+|z|^{2}\right),

and for
z\in\Omega^{-}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{w\in\mathbb{C}\colon\bar{w}\in\Omega^{+}\right\}
define

f^{-}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left|\frac{e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}z}-1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\right|^{2}\left(1+|z|^{2}\right).

Observe that
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
have natural extensions to closed domains
\overline{\Omega^{+}}
and
\overline{\Omega^{-}}
in
\mathbb{C}
, respectively, with
f^{+}(0)=f^{-}(0)=1
. Note that
f^{-}(z)=f^{+}(\bar{z})
for
z\in\overline{\Omega^{-}}
. We are going to prove that
f^{+}(z)\leq 1
for
z\in\overline{\Omega^{+}}
. Assuming this, we then have
f^{-}(z)\leq 1
for
z\in\overline{\Omega^{-}}
, and therefore

f^{+}(\xi)=\left|\frac{\sin{(\pi\xi)}}{\pi\xi}\right|^{2}e^{-2\pi\Im\{\xi\}}\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)\leq 1

for
\xi\in\overline{\Omega^{+}}
, and

f^{-}(\xi)=\left|\frac{\sin{(\pi\xi)}}{\pi\xi}\right|^{2}e^{2\pi\Im\{\xi\}}\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)\leq 1

for
\xi\in\overline{\Omega^{-}}
. From the previous two inequalities we can deduce that for
|\xi|\leq 1
one has

\left|\frac{\sin{(\pi\xi)}}{\pi\xi}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{e^{2\pi\left|\Im\{\xi\}\right|}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}.

In order to prove
f^{+}(z)\leq 1
for
z\in\overline{\Omega^{+}}
, note that
f^{+}(z)
is a subharmonic function on domain
\Omega^{+}
. By the maximum principle we thus have

f^{+}(z)\leq\max\left\{\sup_{x\in[-1,1]}\left\{f^{+}(x)\right\},\sup_{\varphi\in[0,\pi]}\left\{f^{+}\left(e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}\right)\right\}\right\}

for every
z\in\overline{\Omega^{+}}
. Because

f^{+}(x)=\frac{2\left(1-\cos{(2\pi x)}\right)\left(1+x^{2}\right)}{(2\pi x)^{2}}\leq 1,\quad f^{+}\left(e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}\right)=\frac{e^{-4\pi\sin{\varphi}}-2e^{-2\pi\sin{\varphi}}\cos{\left(2\pi\cos{\varphi}\right)}+1}{2\pi^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}<1

for
x\in[-1,1]
and
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
, respectively, the result for
|\xi|<1
now follows.

Let
|\xi|\geq 1
. The preceding paragraph also shows that then

\left|\frac{\sin(\pi\xi)}{\pi\xi}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\frac{e^{2\pi|\Im\{\xi\}|}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}.

The final result follows.
∎

Next, we derive some bounds for the functions

Lemma 5.2

Let

,

(5.2)

\left|G_{\Delta}(z)\right|\leq\frac{\Delta^{2}}{1+|z|}e^{2\pi|y|}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta\left(\xi_{1}\right)\frac{1+|z|}{1+\left|\xi_{1}\right|^{2}}\left(\frac{4}{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}+\frac{8\left|\xi_{1}\right|}{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{3}}\right)\right.\\
\left.+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta\left(\xi_{2}\right)\frac{1+|z|}{1+\left|\xi_{2}\right|^{2}}\left(\frac{4}{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}+\frac{8\left|\xi_{2}\right|}{\left(n-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{3}}\right)\right),

where

Also, for

(5.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{2}+x^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}M_{\Delta}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}+\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\right)2\delta(x)\log{\frac{2}{\left|\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right|}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4\left(\Delta^{2}+x^{2}\right)}{n^{2}+\left(\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right)\Delta\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{\delta\left(\xi_{3}(x,n)\right)}{1+\left|\xi_{3}(x,n)\right|^{2}}+\frac{\delta\left(\xi_{4}(x,n)\right)}{1+\left|\xi_{4}(x,n)\right|^{2}}\right)

Lemma 6.4
.

Let

\sigma<1

\left|\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u^{\sigma}\left(2\log{x}-\log{u}\right)}-\frac{x^{1-\sigma}}{(1-\sigma)\log{x}}\right|\leq\left(1+\frac{(1-\sigma)\log^{2}{x}}{2\log{x}-\log{2}}\left(\frac{2}{x}\right)^{1-\sigma}\right)\frac{x^{1-\sigma}}{(1-\sigma)^{2}\log^{2}{x}}.

Proof.

Substituting

and integrating by parts, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{x}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u^{\sigma}\left(2\log{x}-\log{u}\right)}

\displaystyle=x^{2(1-\sigma)}\int_{x}^{x^{2}/2}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u^{2-\sigma}\log{u}}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{1-\sigma}}{(1-\sigma)\log{x}}-\frac{2^{1-\sigma}}{(1-\sigma)\left(2\log{x}-\log{2}\right)}-\frac{x^{2(1-\sigma)}}{1-\sigma}\int_{x}^{x^{2}/2}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u^{2-\sigma}\log^{2}{u}}.

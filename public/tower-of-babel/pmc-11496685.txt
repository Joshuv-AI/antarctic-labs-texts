Based on improved crayfish optimization algorithm cooperative optimal scheduling of multi-microgrid system
Authors: Dongmei Yan, Hongkun Wang, Yujie Gao, Shiji Tian, Hong Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn order to solve the influence of the complex interaction relationships among subjects on the system solution accuracy and speed of the Multi-Microgrid system under the high penetration rate of new energy. Firstly, the paper establishes the bi-level optimal scheduling Stackelberg game model based on shared energy storage, considering the inter-subject interaction in MMG. Subsequently, based on the four improvement methods of Chaotic Map, Quantum Behavior, Gaussian Distribution, and Nonlinear Control Strategy, the Chaotic Gaussian Quantum Crayfish Optimization Algorithm is proposed to solve the optimization scheduling model. The improved algorithm exhibits superior initial solutions and enhanced search capability. In comparison to the original algorithm, the relative errors of the CGQCOA optimization outcomes are 98%, 20.96%, 98.74% and 16.55%, respectively, enhancing the model-solving accuracy and the speed of convergence to the optimal solution. Finally, the simulation demonstrates that the revenue of Microgrid 1, Microgrid 2, and Microgrid 3 have increased by 0.73%, 1.17%, and 1.04%, respectively. Concurrently, the penalty cost of pollutant emission has decreased by 5.9%, 11.5%, and 12.68%, respectively. Furthermore, the revenue of the shared storage have increased by 1.91%. These findings validate the efficacy of the methodology proposed in enhancing the revenue of the various subjects and reducing pollutant gas emission.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-76041-5.Keywords: Multi-microgrid system, Optimal scheduling, Crayfish optimization algorithm, Shared energy storage, Stackelberg gameSubject terms: Electrical and electronic engineering, Energy storage, Renewable energy

Introduction

With the drying up of traditional fossil energy and intensification of environmental issues1, energy transition has gradually become the consensus in many countries. The new energy sources have been widely used, but it also brings a series of problems such as wind and solar abandonment and high transmission cost2. Therefore, Professor Lasseter.R.H3 proposed the concept of the MG, through which new energy sources can be safely connected to the grid. This integration facilitated local energy consumption, reduced the transmission costs of operators, and enhanced the acceptance of new energy sources by the grid4.

Since the proposal of MG, numerous scholars have undertaken research on the optimal scheduling of MMG, which result from the aggregation of multiple MG. The current research on optimal scheduling for MMG primarily concerns the development of an optimal scheduling model incorporating multitude factors and the solution algorithms. For the former, energy allocation can be rationally optimized by considering the different types of loads’ characteristics5 and energy supply requirements6 in the scheduling model. At the same time, operators encourage MG to actively participate in load demand response by proposing incentive policies, such as monetary compensation7, to improve the economics of MG and MMG. As the penetration of new energy sources increases, the intermittency, volatility, and uncertainty associated with wind and solar energy will exacerbate their adverse effects on scheduling results. To enhance the reliability and flexibility of the system, it is crucial to fully incorporate the role of energy storage in the optimal scheduling model8,9. In10, a scheduling model was constructed with the optimization objectives of net load variance and storage cost minimization. The results demonstrated that the energy storage device effectively mitigated load fluctuation subsequent to MMG trading. In11, an optimization model was devised to reduce the operating expenses associated with energy scheduling in MMG to the lowest possible level, considering energy storage, demand response, and user thermal comfort. This was achieved by transforming a bi-level nonlinear problem into an integer linear programming problem, utilizing the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker. In a subsequent study, 12 employed liquid air energy storage as a means of optimizing MG operation. In10–12, the energy storage was configured in a traditional separate configuration, which offer enhanced the flexibility and reliability of MMG. However, these systems also tend to be more costly to construct and operate. In contrast, the shared energy storage preserves the benefits of traditional storage devices while significantly reducing costs. In13, the combined two-way auction mechanism was proposed between the MG and the storage operator. The results demonstrated the method can realize the effective utilization of storage capacity and overcame the shortcomings inherent to the traditional auction method. In14, the bi-level model was proposed for electric-thermal hybrid storage, and the results indicated that this energy storage device could be effectively utilized to enhance the revenue of the storage operator and reduce the energy cost of MG.

The MMG is distinct from a single MG, comprising the complex hybrid dynamic network that aggregates multiple distributed subjects. In particular, the energy flow is frequent, capital is complex, and the interaction between subjects may affect the system optimization scheduling results when there is a high penetration of new energy. Game theory is the methodology that can be employed to analyze the intricate competitive and cooperative relationship between different subjects. By doing so, it can identify the optimal strategy to achieve the rationality and effectiveness of the scheduling process in the face of uncertainty. Within the context of shared energy storage, the Stackelberg game model was established to optimize the revenue of both the MG and user operators15. In16, the bi-level Stackelberg game model was constructed between the distribution network and MG, with the goal of operating cost minimization. In17, a power interaction model was proposed that did not require a central management unit. Furthermore, a cooperative game model for MMG was developed based on a benefit compromise protocol. The results demonstrated that the proposed method reduced the dependence of the system on the center management unit and considered the revenue of MG. In18, the cooperative game model was established by considering the impact of electricity price, load demand response, and Stackelberg game on MG’s subjective initiative. Furthermore, a benefit distribution method based on improved Shapley value and a model solving method with improved difference algorithm was proposed. In19, the cooperative game model was established for integrated energy parks under uncertainty, taking into account the potential for cooperation among the parks and the uncertainty of wind power output. In practice, subjects typically confront multitude of potential behavioral options20,21, enabling them to adapt their strategies to navigate the uncertainty in the scheduling process. However, this is not conducive to the division of roles in the game. Consequently, prior to the construction of the model, it is essential to conduct the comprehensive analysis of the interaction between subjects and the impact of behavioral options on scheduling.

Additionally, scholars are engaged in research on model-solving methodologies based on intelligent optimization algorithms with the objective of enhancing the generation speed and precision of solution. At present, a number of intelligent optimization algorithms are being employed to address the scheduling model solving problem associated with the MMG. In22, the MG optimization model was developed with the objective of minimizing the operating cost by considering demand side management. An improved GA was then proposed for use in solving the problem. In23, a multi-objective optimization model for MG was developed, with electric vehicles serving as the focus. By combining the Gravity Search Optimization algorithm and PSO, an equal load scheduling scheme for electric vehicles was obtained. In24, a scheduling model based on the intelligent management of home MG was established with the objectives of comfort and economy. An improved VOA was proposed to solve it. However, with the advancement of high technology, the intelligent optimization algorithm has encountered numerous challenges, including the single initial population, limited by local narrowness, and slow convergence to the optimal solution. In25, a solution was proposed to address the problem of slow convergence and local narrowness limitation of the Sparrow Optimization algorithm. The approach involved the incorporation of chaotic map and mutation technique, with the aim of enhancing the algorithm’s performance and broadening its search capability. In26, the improved Liver Cancer Optimization algorithm, which employed logistic chaotic map and additional techniques, was utilized to address limitations in the MG load control model. The results demonstrated that the improved algorithm effectively addresses shortcomings of the original algorithm, which is susceptible to local narrowness and slow convergence to optimal solution. In27, the Firefly-Particle Swarm Hybrid Optimization algorithm was enhanced through the incorporation of chaotic map, thereby ensuring the optimal distribution of the initial population within the feasible solution domain. In28, a multi-stage optimization model of MG, taking into account the carbon trading market, was developed and solved by the quantum PSO. In29, the three-stage scheduling model for MG was established based on information feedback. Additionally, the QASOA was proposed for the problem, as sparrow optimization algorithms are known to be susceptible to local narrowness. In30, the MG bi-level information flow model was established based on Petri-net, and the MG scheduling model was solved by the quantum PSO, which solved the problem of being limited by local narrowness in the solution process.

The preceding researches demonstrate that chaotic map and quantum behavior offer significant advantages in optimizing the performance of intelligent algorithms. Nevertheless, as the iteration progresses, algorithms optimized with chaotic map may encounter a lack of sufficient population diversity, which could result in a restriction by local narrowness and a subsequent deterioration in the quality of the final solution. Although the introduction of quantum behavior in the intelligent optimization algorithm enhances the global search capability, allowing the algorithm to effectively search the entire feasible solution domain, this also affects the speed of converge to the optimal solution. Consequently, the model solving time is longer, affecting the timeliness of the final solution.

MMG inter-subject interaction analysis based on multi-agent

In MMG, the multi-agent technology is introduced to transact on behalf of various subjects. The Alliance, acting in the operator of the MMG, is financed and maintained by the MGO, which can interact with the MGO in two distinct domains: electrical and thermal energy (hereafter collectively referred to as energy interaction), as well as information. Additionally, the Alliance serves as an intermediary between the MGO and the higher-level energy network, including the distribution network and the thermal distribution network, facilitating energy interactions through interconnection lines. For the MGO within the system, the Alliance has the autonomy to issue electricity and thermal price (hereafter collectively referred to as energy price) to supply energy and generate profits through the price difference between energy sale and purchase.

In the MG, a diverse array of equipment is integrated, including loads, PV, WT, GT, and GB. The MGO is capable of conducting real-time information exchange and energy transactions with both the Alliance and the SESO. Furthermore, the MGO sustains the operation of internal GT and GB through the unidirectional interaction with the natural gas network. Given the absence of dedicated energy storage facilities, the MGO is reliant on the SESO for the storage of electrical and thermal. In instances where the energy supply fails to meet the load demand, the MGO has the option to procure energy from the Alliance or utilize the SESO to address the energy deficit. Conversely, during the period of energy surplus, the MGO may elect to sell excess energy to the Alliance or employ the SESO to store the surplus.

The SESO is positioned to offer energy storage leasing services to the MGO, thereby generating revenue by charging leasing fees in accordance with established criteria. The SESO dynamically plans the storage capacity based on the received information regarding the MGO’s energy storage leasing needs, thereby enhancing the reliability and flexibility of the MMG. The bi-level optimal scheduling Stackelberg game model based on shared energy storage

To fully exploit the potential of shared energy storage in the MMG optimal scheduling, the bi-level optimal scheduling game Stackelberg model based on shared energy storage is developed, considering the interests of the Alliance, the MGO and the SESO. The upper layer model is designed to maximize the revenue for the Alliance, while the lower layer model is focused on maximizing the revenue for the MGO. The Alliance, as the leader, initially releases the energy price strategy, which is then received by the MGO. The MGO formulates the controllable equipment output strategy, energy purchase and sale strategy, and energy storage leasing strategy according to its own load demand and new energy output. It then returns the strategy information to the Alliance and the SESO, which receive the information and calculate the revenue according to the objective function.

The Stackelberg game model

In the model, the Alliance possesses two sets of strategies: the energy price strategy and energy purchase and sale strategy from the higher-level energy network. These two strategies belong to different behavioral stages. According to the principle of “behavior determines identity” in the Stackelberg game, the two behaviors of the Alliance are segmented. The former is termed Alliance I, acting as the leader in the game model, with the gold of the Alliance revenue maximization. The latter is referred to as Alliance II, acting as the follower, with the objective function designed to optimize revenue from energy sales to the higher-level energy network. The strategy set of the MGO includes the output strategy of controllable devices , the energy purchase and sale strategy ,, and the energy storage leasing strategy . These three strategies belong to the same stage and therefore do not require segmentation of identity. In the model of this paper, the MGO acts as the follower, with the objective function aimed at maximizing its own revenue.

The Alliance I is situated at the upper layer, while the Alliance II and the MGO are positioned at the lower layer. Initially, Alliance I separately transmits the Alliance energy price and the higher-level energy network price to both the MGO and the Alliance II. Upon receipt of this information, the MGO and the Alliance II derive their respective strategy sets based on their optimization objectives and subsequently return the energy purchase and sale information to the Alliance I. The Alliance I then calculates its own revenue using the received data and updates its strategies accordingly. This iterative optimization process between the upper and lower layers continues until the maximum iteration limit is reached. Meanwhile, during each iteration, MGO independently sends the energy storage leasing strategy to the SESO. Upon receiving the strategy, SESO plans the energy storage capacity, calculates revenue, and returns the leasing information to MGO. Chaotic Gaussian Quantum Crayfish Optimization Algorithm

The COA

In 2023, Jia Heming33 proposed the COA, which simulated the behaviors of the crayfish during summer heat avoidance, competition, and foraging, to develop and explore the algorithm. The variation in environmental temperature will influence the behavior of the algorithm. When the temperature is too high, the algorithm enter the summer heat avoidance phase, during which competition occurs at random. When the temperature is suitable, the algorithm enter the foraging phase. Initially, they must assess the dimension of the food. If the dimension is too large, this indicates that the current strategy is different from the optimal solution. In this case, the algorithm tear up the food and subsequently engage in feeding alternately. If the dimensions is suitable, they then approach the food. In COA, the food represents the global optimal strategy, while the cave represents the local optimal strategy. After the population is updated through the summer heat avoidance and the foraging phase, the stochasticity of the algorithm is enhanced. The CGQCOA

The COA is susceptible to forming the concentrated distribution of multiple individuals when initializing the population, which results in the insufficient diversity of the initial population. Furthermore, it is easily limited by local narrowness in the subsequent solution process. Consequently, this paper proposes the improved COA, designated as the CGQCOA. The logistic-sine hybrid chaotic map is employed for algorithm initialization, and the Quantum Behavior is adopted to enhance the search ability of algorithm within the feasible solution domain. On this basis, the traditional Quantum Behavior is improved by using Gaussian Distribution and Nonlinear Control Strategy to enhance the ability to escape from local narrowness and convergence in the later stages of iteration.

The operation of the MMG under the operation strategy of Scheme 1 is shown in Fig. 6, taking the MGO1 as an example to illustrate the feasibility of the operation strategy of Scheme 1. As shown in Fig. 6(a), the power output of the MGO1 is mainly from PV and GT. Additionally, the small amount of residual electricity is sold to the Alliance to generate revenue. During the period of 8:00–19:00, PV generates power, and during the period of 11:00–15:00, PV is in the high-power output stage that can meet the load demand, while the GT do not generate power. The system stores part of the surplus electricity through SESO, and the remaining electricity is sold to the Alliance for profit. During the period of 20:00–24:00, PV does not generate power, and the GT maintain the power balance. Conclusion

This paper simplifies the complex interaction among various subjects in MMG through the multi-agent technology. Taking into account the benefit of shared energy storage, it proposes the bi-level optimization scheduling game model. Additionally, an enhanced COA, termed CGQCOA, is developed. Through simulation analysis, the following conclusions are obtained:

Through validation with standard benchmark functions, it has been demonstrated that the proposed algorithm has obvious advantages in terms of solving performance across complex terrain, high-dimension, and nonlinearity. Analysis indicates that it has both the superior initial solution and the more robust global search capability, leading to enhanced optimization outcome;

Analysis indicates that it has both the superior initial solution and the more robust global search capability, leading to enhanced optimization outcome;

These findings validate that the proposed strategy effectively fosters energy mutual assistance within MMG, augments the revenue of MGO, and mitigates pollutant emission.

These findings validate that the proposed strategy effectively fosters energy mutual assistance within MMG, augments the revenue of MGO, and mitigates pollutant emission.

In Scheme 1, the SESO revenue increased by 47.1 CNY, representing an approximate 2% increase, and the Alliance’s revenue rose from 0 to 6.13 CNY. This substantiates the assertion that the proposed strategy not only augments MGO revenue but also enhances the SESO and Alliance revenues, thereby creating a mutually beneficial scenario for all three parties.

As energy interactions within MMG become more frequent, MGO, motivated by self-interest and autonomymay, may engage in trading that are detrimental to the interests of other subjects. Moreover, delays in inter-subject communication may result in the failure of trading. In light of these considerations, future research will examine the impact of the trading mechanism and communication delay of each subject on MMG scheduling will be considered, and the formulation of an MMG operation strategy scheme under uncertainty will be explored.

Active surveillance for CIN2: biomarkers show promise, but real-world implementation faces unresolved barriers—a comment on Frayle et al.
Authors: Shiqiong Zhou, Qinghua Ke
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the recent Original Article by Frayle et al., titled “Predictive biomarkers for regression in women undergoing active surveillance for cervical intraepithelial neoplasia grade 2: A prospective multicenter study in Italy,” published in the International Journal of Cancer (Frayle et al. 2026). As gynecologic oncologists involved in cervical cancer screening and colposcopy services, we commend the authors for providing real-world evidence on the use of HPV genotyping, p16/ki67 dual staining, and FAM19A4/miR124-2 methylation status to predict CIN2 regression. Their finding that 56% of women aged 25–45 years showed spontaneous regression supports the expansion of active surveillance beyond the conventional age cutoff of 30 years.

However, we wish to raise three clinical and methodological considerations that may affect the generalizability and implementation of these promising results.

First, the study protocol required full visibility of the transformation zone (TZ) and the lesion at colposcopy. The authors explicitly excluded women with unsatisfactory colposcopy (i.e., incomplete TZ visualization) to ensure safety during active surveillance—a deliberate and justifiable design choice. In routine community-based screening, however, unsatisfactory colposcopy occurs in a non‑negligible proportion of women, particularly among older parous women or those with prior cervical procedures. For example, reported rates range from 10 to 25% depending on age and parity. While we agree that full TZ visibility is essential for safe conservative management, the prognostic impact of an incomplete TZ in women who are nevertheless managed expectantly remains unknown. Therefore, rather than asking the authors for unavailable data, we suggest that future prospective studies systematically record TZ types (using the 2011 IFCPC terminology Bornstein et al. 2012) and evaluate whether unsatisfactory colposcopy influences regression rates or safety outcomes when active surveillance is inadvertently applied.

Second, while the authors elegantly demonstrate that combined negative biomarkers (e.g., non-16/18 HPV, negative p16/ki67, unmethylated status) are associated with high regression rates, they also report that 37.4% of women underwent excisional treatment during follow‑up, partly attributed to COVID‑19‑related anxiety. It is plausible that even in a non‑pandemic context, overtreatment due to patient or physician anxiety remains a real‑world challenge. The original study did not include formal assessments of patient‑reported anxiety or quality of life. We therefore recommend that future active surveillance trials incorporate validated patient‑reported outcome measures (e.g., state‑trait anxiety inventory, cervical cancer‑specific quality of life scales) to quantify the psychological drivers of unnecessary excisions. Avoiding overtreatment is particularly important given the well‑documented adverse obstetric outcomes associated with cervical procedures (Kyrgiou et al. 2016).

Third, from a health systems perspective, the sequential use of biomarkers—starting with HPV genotyping—is appealing. However, the added cost of p16/ki67 and methylation testing (not routinely covered in many public health systems, including ours) may hinder adoption. To our knowledge, dedicated cost‑effectiveness analyses of p16/ki67 dual staining or FAM19A4/miR124‑2 methylation specifically for guiding active surveillance in CIN2 are currently lacking. Their cost‑effectiveness likely depends on local test costs, healthcare infrastructure, and the rate of overtreatment avoided. We would be interested in the authors’ view on whether a simplified algorithm using only HPV genotyping and p16/ki67 (which is more widely available) could achieve acceptable predictive performance, particularly in lower‑resource settings.

In summary, Frayle et al. have provided valuable evidence supporting biomarker‑guided active surveillance for CIN2 in women up to age 45. Nonetheless, careful attention to colposcopic eligibility, patient psychological factors, and cost‑effectiveness will be essential before widespread implementation. We look forward to future studies addressing these real‑world barriers (Tainio et al. 2018, Lycke et al. 2023).

On the pair correlation function of the Sine β \operatorname{Sine}_{\beta} process
Yahui Qu, Benedek Valkó

Abstract
We study the Sine β \operatorname{Sine}_{\beta} process, the bulk point process scaling limit of beta-ensembles. We provide a representation of its pair correlation function for all β > 0 \beta>0 via a stochastic differential equation. We show that the pair correlation function is continuous in β \beta , and provide estimates for its asymptotic decay. We recover the classical explicit formula for the pair correlation function in the β = 2 \beta=2 and 4 4 cases. For β = 2 ​ n \beta=2n , we derive the power series expansion of the pair correlation function, and express it in terms of a size n n linear ordinary differential equation system. We obtain our results by studying the density of the HP β , δ \operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta} process, the point process limit of the circular Jacobi beta-ensembles.



1
Introduction

The
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process is the bulk scaling limit of beta-ensembles. It is a translation invariant process with density
\tfrac{1}{2\pi}
. It was first introduced as the bulk scaling limit of the Gaussian beta-ensemble for general
\beta>0
in
[
38
]
. Later it was shown to be the same as the point process limit of the circular beta-ensemble (
[
40
]
,
[
30
]
) which was first derived by Killip and Stoiciu in
[
24
]
. Finally,
[
4
]
proved that
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
is the universal bulk scaling limit of a general class of beta-ensembles.

For a simple point process
\Xi
on
{\mathbb{R}}
and a Borel set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, we denote by
\Xi(A)
the number of points of
\Xi
in
A
.
The
k
-point correlation (or joint intensity) function of
\Xi
(if it exists) is the symmetric function
\rho^{(k)}:{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
that satisfies the following: if
D_{1},\dots,D_{k}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
are disjoint Borel sets then

\displaystyle E[\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Xi(D_{j})]=\int_{D_{1}}\cdots\int_{D_{k}}{\rho^{(k)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}dx_{k}\,\dots dx_{1}.

(1)

The function
\rho^{(1)}
(if it exists) is just the Radon–Nikodym derivative of the expected counting function
\nu(A)=E[\Xi(A)]
with respect to the Lebesgue measure, this is the density of the point process
\Xi
.

Many of the classical results of random matrix theory use
k
-point correlation functions to identify the point process limit of various random matrix ensembles. For the classical values
\beta=1,2,4
, the finite beta-ensembles possess special algebraic structures. These processes are determinantal (for
\beta=2
) or Pfaffian (for
\beta=1,4
), which means that it is possible to express their correlation functions in terms of determinantal or Pfaffian formulas built from the orthogonal polynomials of the reference measure of the ensemble. For circular and Gaussian beta-ensembles, one can take limits of these formulas to obtain explicit expressions for the
k
-point correlation of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process for
\beta=1,2,4
(see
[
1
,
29
,
14
]
).

In contrast, the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process for general
\beta>0
is characterized via its counting function using a one-parameter family of stochastic differential equations (
[
38
]
,
[
24
]
), or as the spectrum of a random differential operator (
[
40
]
). It has not been clear how one can obtain the classical
\beta=1,2,4
results from these characterizations of
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
, and whether it is possible to get any information about the correlation functions. Our paper provides the first positive results in this direction, by studying the pair (or two-point) correlation function
\rho^{(2)}_{\beta}(x,y)
of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process.

Results for general
\beta>0

Our first result provides a characterization of
\rho^{(2)}_{\beta}(0,\lambda)
in terms of a stochastic differential equation. Since the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process is translation and reflection invariant, we have
\rho^{(2)}_{\beta}(x,y)=\rho^{(2)}_{\beta}(0,|y-x|)
.
Here, and in the rest of the paper, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a non-negative integer
k
we use the following notation for the rising factorial (or Pochhammer symbol):

\displaystyle(x)^{\uparrow k}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(x+j),

(2)

with the empty product defined as 1.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\beta>0
,
\lambda>0
, and consider the unique strong solution
\alpha_{\lambda}(u)
of the following stochastic differential equation on
(-\infty,0]
:

\displaystyle d\alpha_{\lambda}=\lambda\tfrac{\beta}{4}e^{\frac{\beta}{4}u}du-\frac{\beta}{2}\sin(\alpha_{\lambda}(u))du+\Re[(e^{-i\alpha_{\lambda}(u)}-1)dZ],\qquad\lim_{u\to-\infty}\alpha_{\lambda}(u)=0.

(3)

The pair correlation function of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{\beta}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\beta/2)^{\uparrow k}}{(1+\beta/2)^{\uparrow k}}E[\cos(k\alpha_{\lambda}(0)].

(4)

For
\delta>0
one has the uniform bound

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(-\delta)^{\uparrow k}}{(1+\delta)^{\uparrow k}}\right|\leq ck^{-1-2\delta},

(5)

with a
\delta
-dependent constant
c
, hence (
4
) gives an absolutely convergent sum.

One can realize the solutions of (
3
) for all
\beta>0
and
\lambda>0
on the same probability space, in a way that the solution is almost surely continuous in its parameters. This allows us to prove the following regularity result.

Theorem 2
.

The function
\rho_{\beta}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)
is continuous in both
\beta
and
\lambda
. For
\beta>2,\lambda>0
the function is jointly continuous in
\lambda
and
\beta
.

Identifying the large
\lambda
behavior of
the pair correlation function
\rho^{(2)}_{\beta}(0,\lambda)
for general
\beta>0
has been a long-standing open problem. Using the representation of Theorem
1
we are able to provide the following bound on the asymptotic decay of the truncated correlation function
\rho^{(2)}_{\beta}(0,\lambda)-\rho^{(1)}_{\beta}(\lambda)^{2}=\rho^{(2)}_{\beta}(0,\lambda)-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}
.

Theorem 3

(Decay of the truncated pair correlation)
.

There is a
\beta
-dependent constant
c
so that we have the following bound for
\lambda\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\left|\rho^{(2)}_{\beta}(0,\lambda)-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\right|\leq c\left(\lambda^{-1}+\lambda^{-\beta/2}+\lambda^{-4/\beta}\right).

(6)

For
\beta\geq 4
, the main term in our upper bound is
\lambda^{-4/\beta}
, which matches the conjectured order of the truncated pair correlation function in this regime (see equation (
16
) below, and the discussion around it). While preparing this manuscript, we have learned from Laure Dumaz and Martin Malvy
[
8
]
that they have also obtained polynomially decaying bounds on the truncated pair correlation function of
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
, with methods that are different from ours.

Results for
\beta=2n

The beta-ensembles do not possess determinantal/Pfaffian structure for values other than the classical values 1, 2, and 4. However, Forrester derived exact formulas the circular beta-ensemble for
\beta=2n
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
(see
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, and Chapter 13 of
[
14
]
). These formulas lead to an
n
-variable integral representation of the pair correlation function of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process when
\beta=2n
. We will review these results in more detail in the
Historical background
section below.

We also present some new results for
\beta=2n
.
The expression
(-x)^{\uparrow k}
is zero if
x
is a positive integer and
k>x
. This means that the expression (
4
) in Theorem
1
becomes a finite sum if
\beta=2n
. In this case we
can provide more explicit representations of the pair correlation function. We show that
\rho^{(2)}_{2n}(0,\lambda)
is analytic in
\lambda^{2}
, express it in terms of the solution of an
n
-dimensional linear ODE system, and also as a power series with coefficients obtained from a matrix-valued recursion. For the full result see Theorem
14
, Corollary
15
, and the discussion following these statements. Here, we only present in detail the result of the power series representation.

Fix
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
Let
\mathbf{A}_{n}
be a tridiagonal and
\mathbf{B}_{n}
a diagonal
n\times n
matrix with nonzero entries given by

\displaystyle[\mathbf{A}_{n}]_{k,k}=-k^{2},\quad[\mathbf{A}_{n}]_{k,k-1}=\frac{1}{2}k(k+n),\quad[\mathbf{A}_{n}]_{k,k+1}=\frac{1}{2}k(k-n),\quad[\mathbf{B}_{n}]_{k}=k.

(7)

Let
\mathbf{e}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{n}=[1,0,\dots,0]^{T},\qquad[\mathbf{v}_{n}]_{k}=(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{2n}{n+k}}{\binom{2n}{n}},\qquad 1\leq k\leq n.

(8)

Theorem 4

(Power series representation for
\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)
)
.

Define
\mathbf{s}_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
recursively as

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{n+1}{2}\mathbf{A}_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{e}_{n},\qquad\mathbf{s}_{k}=i\left(k\mathbf{I}-\tfrac{2}{n}\mathbf{A}_{n}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{n}\mathbf{s}_{k-1},\qquad k\geq 1.

(9)

Then

\displaystyle\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{v}_{n}^{T}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{2j}\,\lambda^{2j}.

(10)

As part of the proof, we will show that the sequence
\mathbf{s}_{k},k\geq 0
is well-defined, and the power series converges for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem
4
allows us to recover the well-known Sine-kernel for the
\beta=2
case. Indeed, if
\beta=2
(i.e.
n=1
), then we have
\mathbf{A}_{1}=-1
,
\mathbf{B}_{1}=1
,
\mathbf{v}_{1}=-\tfrac{1}{2}
,
\mathbf{s}_{k}=\tfrac{2}{(k+2)!}i^{k}
, hence (
10
) yields

\displaystyle\rho_{2}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\sin^{2}(\tfrac{\lambda}{2})}{(\lambda/2)^{2}}\right).

(11)

In Section
6
we will find a closed formula for the function
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{s}_{j}\lambda^{j}
in the
\beta=4
case, which allows us to recover the known expression (see (
14
) below) for the pair correlation function of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{4}
process.

As an additional application, we recover the asymptotics of the pair correlation function near 0 in the
\beta=2n
case. (This also follows directly from the integral representation of Forrester, see the discussion below.)

Corollary 5
.

We have the following asymptotics for
\lambda
near 0:

\displaystyle\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\cdot\frac{n^{2n}(n!)^{3}}{(2n!)(3n!)}\lambda^{2n}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2n+2}).

(12)

Strategy of the proof

Our starting point is the following observation from
[
41
]
: if one can properly define the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process conditioned to have a point at 0, then the one-point function of the conditioned process is
\frac{\rho_{\beta}^{(2)}(0,x)}{\rho_{\beta}^{(1)}(x)}
.

The
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process is the point process limit of the circular beta-ensemble. We can readily condition a size
n
circular beta-ensemble to have a point at angle 0. After removing this point, we obtain the size
n-1
circular Jacobi beta-ensemble with parameter
\delta=\beta/2
. The scaling limit of the circular Jacobi beta-ensemble was obtained in
[
28
]
, this is the
\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta}
process. (See Section
2
for precise statements.) We will show (using the results of
[
41
]
) that the
\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta}
process with
\delta=\beta/2
is the same as the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process conditioned to have a point at 0, with that point removed (see Proposition
7
below). In particular, with
\rho_{\beta,\delta}^{(1)}(\lambda)
denoting the density of
\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta}
, we will show that

\displaystyle\rho_{\beta}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)=\rho_{\beta,\beta/2}^{(1)}(\lambda){\rho_{\beta}^{(1)}(\lambda)}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\rho_{\beta,\beta/2}^{(1)}(\lambda).

(13)

[
28
]
provides a characterization of
\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta}
as the result of a shooting problem involving an SDE similar to (
3
). Using this characterization, we can prove a version of Theorem
1
for
\rho_{\beta,\delta}^{(1)}(\lambda)
with
\beta>0,\delta>0
, see Theorem
9
below. Theorem
4
and Corollary
5
will also follow from corresponding results on the density of the
\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta}
process.

Historical background

As mentioned before, for
\beta=2
, the circular and the circular Jacobi beta-ensembles are determinantal, the
k
-point correlation functions can be obtained as the determinant of a
k\times k
matrix built from a kernel function. Taking the scaling limit of these formulas, one obtains exact determinantal expressions for all correlation functions of
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
and
\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta}
for
\beta=2
, in particular, one obtains (
11
). For
\beta=1
and
\beta=4
, one can write down the correlation functions of the finite and the limiting ensembles in terms of Pfaffian formulas (
[
29
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
14
]
). For
\beta=4,
this leads to the following expression for the pair correlation function of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process:

\displaystyle\rho_{4}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left(1-\operatorname{sinc}^{2}(\lambda)+\operatorname{sinc}^{\prime}(\lambda)\int_{0}^{\lambda}\operatorname{sinc}(t)dt\right),\qquad\operatorname{sinc}(x)=\frac{\sin(x)}{x}.

(14)

When
\beta=2n
(i.e.
\beta
is an even integer), Forrester
provides a representation for the correlation functions of the circular beta-ensemble in terms of generalized hypergeometric functions and Jack polynomials (see
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, and Chapter 13 of
[
14
]
), and evaluates the scaling limit of these functions.
We record the result for the limit of the pair correlation function here, which, if one can justify the exchange of limits, gives
\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\cdot\frac{n^{2n}(n!)^{3}}{(2n!)(3n!)}\frac{e^{-in\lambda}\lambda^{2n}}{S_{2n}(-1+1/n,-1+1/n,1/n)}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{[0,1]^{2n}}\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left(e^{i\lambda u_{j}}u_{j}^{-1+1/n}(1-u_{j})^{-1+1/n}\right)\prod_{j<k}|u_{j}-u_{k}|^{2/n}\prod_{j=1}^{2n}du_{j}.

(15)

Here
S_{2n}(-1+1/n,-1+1/n,1/n)
is the Selberg integral on
[0,1]^{2n}
that one obtains by setting
\lambda=0
in the integral in the second line of (
15
). Note we can immediately identify the constant in the asymptotics (
12
) by taking
\lambda\to 0
. It would be interesting to see if one could recover the power series representation (
10
) directly from this result, or vice versa.

By analyzing the integral in (
15
),
Forrester provides the following large

asymptotics of (
15
) for
\beta=2n
:

\displaystyle 4\pi^{2}\rho_{\beta}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{4}{\beta\lambda^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-4})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{a_{k}}{\lambda^{4k^{2}/\beta}}\left(\cos(k\lambda)+b_{k}\frac{\sin(k\lambda)}{\lambda}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|\cos(k\lambda)|}{\lambda^{2}}\right)\right),

(16)

with explicitly given coefficients

(see Proposition 13.13,
[
14
]
).
Similar asymptotics, with an infinite sum of oscillating terms in (
16
),
were obtained by Haldane
[
19
]

in the study of one-component, one-dimensional quantum fluids. It is conjectured that Haldane’s asymptotics hold
for the pair correlation

for general

as well (see e.g.
[

]
). Note that the largest order term in this conjectured expansion is of order

. Our bound in Theorem
3
matches this order for

.

In
[

]
(building on the results of
[

]
) Forrester outlines how one can represent

as the solution of an order

differential equation, and provides these representations for

2
Preliminaries on beta-ensembles

2.1
The circular and the circular Jacobi beta-ensemble and their limits

Let
\beta>0
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, and
\delta\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\Re\delta>-1/2
. The size
n
circular Jacobi beta-ensemble with parameters
\beta,\delta
is the joint distribution of

n
points
\{e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}}\}
on the unit circle
\{|z|=1\}
, where the joint density function of the angles
\theta_{j}\in[-\pi,\pi)
, is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{n,\beta,\delta}^{\textup{CJ}}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\left|e^{i\theta_{j}}-e^{i\theta_{k}}\right|^{\beta}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-e^{-i\theta_{k}})^{\delta}(1-e^{i\theta_{k}})^{\bar{\delta}},\qquad\theta_{j}\in[-\pi,\pi).

(17)

Here
Z_{n,\beta,\delta}^{\textup{CJ}}
is an explicitly computable normalizing constant. For
\delta=0
the distribution becomes the circular beta-ensemble. We write
\Lambda_{n}\sim\textup{CJ}_{n,\beta,\delta}
to denote that the random set
\Lambda_{n}=\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\}
has joint density given by (
17
), and we use the notation
\textup{C}_{n,\beta}
for
\textup{CJ}_{n,\beta,0}
, the size
n
circular beta-ensemble.

Killip and Stoiciu
[
24
]
showed that if
\Lambda_{n}\sim\textup{C}_{n,\beta}
then
n\Lambda_{n}
converges to a point process as
n\to\infty
. The point process limit was characterized via a one-parameter family of stochastic differential equations via a shooting problem. This process was later shown to be the same as the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process, the bulk scaling limit of the Gaussian beta-ensemble
[
40
]
,
[
30
]
, which can also be characterized as the spectrum of a random differential operator.

In
[
28
]
it vas shown that if
\Lambda_{n}\sim\textup{CJ}_{n,\beta,\delta}
then
n\Lambda_{n}
converges to a point process
\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta}
. (Note that
\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta}
with
\delta=0
is just
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
.) This process can also be characterized in multiple ways (as the spectrum of a certain random differential operator, as the zero set of a certain random entire function). We review the characterization that will be useful for our purposes.

Let
Z=d\mathbf{B}_{1}+id\mathbf{B}_{2}
be a two-sided complex Brownian motion with standard independent real and imaginary parts.
For a given
\beta>0,\Re\delta>-1/2
consider the unique strong solution
\alpha_{\lambda}(t)
of the following one-parameter family of stochastic differential equations:

\displaystyle d\alpha_{\lambda}=\lambda\tfrac{\beta}{4}e^{\frac{\beta}{4}u}du+\Re[(e^{-i\alpha_{\lambda}(u)}-1)(dZ-i\delta du)],\qquad\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}},t\in(-\infty,\infty)

(18)

with the initial condition

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to-\infty}\alpha_{\lambda}(u)=0,\qquad\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(19)

Let
\Theta
be a random variable on
[0,2\pi)
with probability density function

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(1+\delta)\Gamma(1+\bar{\delta})}{\Gamma(1+\delta+\bar{\delta})}(1-e^{-i\theta})^{\delta}(1-e^{i\theta})^{\bar{\delta}},\qquad\theta\in[0,2\pi),

(20)

independent of
Z
.
Then
[
28
]
proved that

\displaystyle\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}:\alpha_{\lambda}(0)=\Theta\mod 2\pi\}.

(21)

For
\delta=0
this recovers the characterization of
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
given in
[
24
]
, in that case
\Theta
is uniform on
[0,2\pi)
. Note that the diffusion (
3
) is the same as (
18
) with initial condition (
19
) and
\delta=\beta/2
.

Proposition 9 of
[
38
]
provides an exponential tail bound on
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}[0,\varepsilon]
(i.e. the number of points in
[0,\varepsilon]
) from which it follows that the
k
th moment of
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}[0,\varepsilon]
is bounded by a constant multiple of
\varepsilon^{k}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. From this it follows that the
k
th moment measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and this implies the existence of the
k
th correlation function for the stationary process
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
for all
k\geq 1
(see
[
5
]
).

2.2
Properties of the
\alpha_{\lambda}
diffusion

The following proposition summarizes some of the useful properties of the diffusion (
18
), see
[
28
]
for additional details and proofs.

Proposition 6
.

Fix
\beta>0
and
\Re\delta>-1/2
.

1.

For any
\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}
the system (
18
) has a unique strong solution
\alpha_{\lambda,\nu}(u)
on
[\nu,\infty)
with initial condition
\alpha_{\lambda,\nu}(\nu)=0
. For any given
\nu<u
, with probability one the function
\lambda\mapsto\alpha_{\lambda,\nu}(u)
is analytic and strictly increasing with
\alpha_{0,\nu}(u)=0

2.

There is a unique strong solution
\alpha_{\lambda}(u)
of (
18
) on
(-\infty,\infty)
satisfying the initial condition (
19
), which can be obtained as the a.s. limit
\alpha_{\lambda}(u)=\lim\limits_{\nu\to-\infty}\alpha_{\lambda,\nu}(u)
. The limit is monotone increasing for
\lambda>0
and monotone decreasing for
\lambda<0
.

For any
u\in{\mathbb{R}}
with probability one the function
\lambda\mapsto\alpha_{\lambda}(u)
is analytic and strictly increasing in

2.3
Palm measures

The Palm measure of a stationary point process
\Xi
on
{\mathbb{R}}
is the process viewed from a ‘typical’ point, with that point shifted to 0. The reduced Palm measure is the resulting process with 0 removed.
For a precise definition, see Section 13 of
[
6
]
or
[
41
]
.
Section 13 of
[
6
]
shows that for a stationary process
\Xi
with a finite pair correlation function
\rho_{\Xi}^{(2)}(0,x)
, the density of the (reduced) Palm measure exists and it is given by

\rho_{Palm}^{(1)}(\lambda)=\frac{\rho_{\Xi}^{(2)}(0,x)}{\rho_{\Xi}^{(1)}(x)}.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\beta>0
. The reduced Palm measure of

\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\beta/2}

Proof.

The proof of this statement is basically contained in

Lemma 10

Suppose that

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\lambda)}{2\pi}+\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\delta)^{\uparrow k}}{(1+\delta)^{\uparrow k}}\frac{\sin(kg(\lambda))}{k}.

(25)

Proof.

Since
\lambda>0

g(0)=0
, and

is continuous and strictly increasing, we have

\Xi[0,\lambda]=\big|\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}:\Theta+2k\pi\leq g(\lambda)\}\big|=\lfloor\tfrac{g(\lambda)}{2\pi}\rfloor+\mathbf{1}\left(g(\lambda)-2\pi\lfloor\tfrac{g(\lambda)}{2\pi}\rfloor\geq\Theta\right),

Denote the probability density function of

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}h_{\delta}(\theta)d\theta=\frac{x}{2\pi}+\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\delta)^{\uparrow k}}{(1+\delta)^{\uparrow k}}\cdot\frac{\sin(kx)}{k}.

(26)

The function

h_{\delta}(\theta)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{k}e^{ik\theta}

with

. We will show that

from this (

We have

\displaystyle(1-z)^{a}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-a)^{\uparrow k}}{k!}z^{j},

\displaystyle(1-e^{i\theta})^{\delta}(1-e^{-i\theta})^{\delta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{(-\delta)^{m}(-\delta)^{n}}{m!n!}e^{i\theta(m-n)},

where the sum is also absolutely convergent. For

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\delta)^{\uparrow n+k}(-\delta)^{\uparrow n}}{(n+k)!n!}

\displaystyle=(-\bar{\delta})^{\uparrow k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\delta+k)^{\uparrow n}(-\delta)^{\uparrow n}}{(n+k)!n!}

\displaystyle=(-\delta)^{\uparrow k}\frac{1}{k!}{}_{2}F_{1}(-\delta+k,-\delta,k+1,1)

\displaystyle=(-\delta)^{\uparrow k}\frac{\Gamma(1+2\delta)}{\Gamma(1+\delta)\Gamma(1+\delta+k)},

which is what we wanted to prove.

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{2\ell}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell}(2\ell\,\mathbf{I}-\tfrac{4}{\beta}\mathbf{A}_{n})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{n}((2\ell-1)\mathbf{I}-\tfrac{4}{\beta}\mathbf{A}_{n})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{n}\cdots(\mathbf{I}-\tfrac{4}{\beta}\mathbf{A}_{n})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{n}\mathbf{f}_{n}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell}\frac{1}{(2\ell)!}(\mathbf{I}-\tfrac{4}{\beta(2\ell)}\mathbf{A}_{n})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{n}(\mathbf{I}-\tfrac{4}{\beta(2\ell-1)}\mathbf{A}_{n})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{n}\cdots(\mathbf{I}-\tfrac{4}{\beta}\mathbf{A}_{n})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{n}\mathbf{f}_{n}.

Applying (
80
) repeatedly (from the right), we obtain that

\mathbf{s}_{2\ell}=(-1)^{\ell}\frac{1}{(2\ell)!}\prod_{k=1}^{2\ell}c_{k,\frac{4}{\beta k},0,n}\,\mathbf{B}_{n}^{2\ell}\mathbf{f}_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{\ell-1}\tilde{c}_{\ell,k,n}\,\mathbf{B}_{n}^{2\ell-2k}\mathbf{f}_{n},

with some finite coefficients
\tilde{c}_{\ell,k,n}
.
Finally, by (
76
) and (
81
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{2\ell}=0\quad\text{for }1\leq\ell\leq n-1,

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{2n}=\frac{1}{2}\binom{2n}{n}^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{2n}\left(1+\tfrac{4}{\beta}\Big(n-\tfrac{j-1}{2}\Big)\right)^{-1}.

The last constant can also be written as

\frac{1}{2}\binom{2n}{n}^{-1}\frac{(\tfrac{\beta}{2})^{2n}}{\left(1+\frac{\beta}{2}\right)^{\uparrow 2n}},

which completes the proof of Proposition
16
.
∎

6.3
ODE representation for
\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)

Theorem
14
and Corollary
15
with
\beta=2n
provides a description for
\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)
as

\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left(1+2\;\mathbf{v}_{n}^{T}\Re\mathbf{q}(\lambda)\right)

with
\mathbf{q}
as the solution of the ODE (
50
). Motivated by
[
15
]
, one can ask if there is a way to represent
\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)
itself as the solution of a differential equation using our representation. It turns out that the function
r_{n}(\lambda)=2\mathbf{v}_{n}^{T}\mathbf{q}(\lambda)
solves an order
n
complex-valued linear differential equation where the coefficients are polynomials with degree at most
n
. This gives a representation for
\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)
as the
real part
of the solution of an order
n
linear differential equation.

We will only give the outline of the proof of this statement, without spelling out the computational details.
It can be shown that
\mathbf{v}_{n}^{T}
is the left eigenvector of
\mathbf{A}_{n}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
-n
. From Lemma
18
it follows that
\mathbf{A}_{n}
has
n
linearly independent left eigenvectors, let
\mathbf{w}_{k}^{T},1\leq k\leq n-1
denote the ones besides
\mathbf{v}_{n}^{T}
. Introducing the new variables
r_{k}(\lambda)=\mathbf{w}_{k}^{T}\mathbf{q}(\lambda)
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
, we see that
\mathbf{r}(\lambda)=[r_{1}(\lambda),\dots,r_{n}(\lambda)]^{T}
satisfies a transformed version of (
50
), with
\mathbf{B}_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n},\mathbf{e}_{n},\mathbf{f}_{n}
replaced with their respective representations in a new basis. In this new representation
\mathbf{A}_{n}
will become a diagonal matrix, and by choosing the normalization in the eigenvectors
\mathbf{w}_{k},1\leq k\leq n-1
appropriately, one can show that
\mathbf{B}_{n}
will turn into a tridiagonal matrix with explicitly computable entries. This allows us to successively eliminate
r_{n-1},r_{n-2},\dots,r_{1}
from the ODE system for
\mathbf{r}(\lambda)
, leading to an order
n
linear ODE with polynomial coefficients of degree at most
n
. The initial condition for
\mathbf{r}(0)
will be transformed into equations for
r_{n}(0),r^{\prime}_{n}(0),\dots,r_{n}^{(n-1)}(0)
.

A similar (but computationally more involved) approach could be taken to obtain a differential equation for which
\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)
itself is a solution. Consider the power series representation of
\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)
from Theorem
4
. Let
\mathbf{z}(\lambda)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{s}_{2j}\lambda^{2j}
. Using the recursive definition of
\mathbf{s}_{k},k\geq 0
we can set up a second order vector valued linear differential equation for
\mathbf{z}(\lambda)
with terms of the form
\lambda^{2}\mathbf{z}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)
,
\lambda\mathbf{z}^{\prime}(\lambda)
,
\lambda\mathbf{z}(\lambda)
, and
\mathbf{z}(\lambda)
(with matrix valued coefficients). Using the arguments of the previous paragraph it might be possible to turn this vector valued second order ODE into an order
2n
linear ODE for
\rho_{2n}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\mathbf{v}_{n}^{T}\cdot\mathbf{z}(\lambda)
. However, we only carried out this reduction for small values of
n
with the assistance of computer algebra.

6.4
Regularity of the pair correlation function
\rho_{\beta}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)

We turn to the proof of Theorem
2
. We will use the following proposition, which can be shown using standard results on SDE flows (see e.g. Theorem 49 in
[
32
]
).

Proposition 21
.

Let
Z
be a two-sided Brownian motion.
There is a process
\alpha_{\lambda,\beta}(u)
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\beta>0
,
u\in{\mathbb{R}}
that is adapted to the filtration of
Z
as a process in
u
, analytic in
\lambda
for fixed
u
and
\beta
, continuous in
\beta
for fixed
u
and
\lambda
, and has the same distribution as the unique strong solution
u\mapsto\alpha_{\lambda}(u)
of (
3
) for a fixed
\beta
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Consider the process
\alpha_{\lambda,\beta}(u)
given by Proposition
21
, and set

q_{k}(\beta,\lambda)=E[\exp(ik\alpha_{\lambda,\beta}(0))].

From Proposition
21
it follows that these functions are analytic in
\lambda
and continuous in
\beta
, and by definition we have
|q_{k}|\leq 1
. By Theorem
1
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\beta}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\beta/2)^{\uparrow k}}{(1+\beta/2)^{\uparrow k}}\Re q_{k}(\beta,\lambda).

(89)

The first part of the theorem now follows: the partial sums in (

\lambda
and in
\beta

because of the summable bound (

To see that for

\lambda
and
\beta
we use the following statement: if

is continuous in both variables, and it is also locally uniformly Lipschitz in

is jointly continuous. Indeed, if

|f(x,y)-f(x_{n},y_{n})|\leq|f(x,y)-f(x_{n},y)|+|f(x_{n},y)-f(x_{n},y_{n})|\to 0,

where we use the continuity in

From Proposition
20

q_{k}(\beta,\lambda)
satisfies the ODEs (

7
Open problems

We finish with a list of open problems related to our results.

Our estimates in the proof of Theorem
2
are far from optimal. Potentially, a more careful asymptotic analysis of
E[e^{ik\alpha_{\lambda}(0)}]
could produce bounds that match the conjectured order
\lambda^{-\min(2,4/\beta)}
for the truncated pair correlation.

Problem 1
.

Improve the bounds in Theorem
2
for the truncated pair correlation function of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process for general
\beta>0
.

Proposition
20
shows that
q_{k}(\lambda)=E[e^{ik\alpha_{\lambda}(0)}]
solves an infinite system of ODEs, but it is not clear that the solution is unique. A uniqueness result would provide a self-contained characterization of the pair correlation function
\rho_{\beta}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)
.

Problem 2
.

Show that the infinite ODE system (
82
)-(
84
) (with possibly some additional constraints) has a unique solution.

Our proof of Theorem
9
relies on
\delta
being a positive real. A natural question to ask if the result could be extended to more general
\delta
values.

Problem 3
.

Extend Theorem
9
for the density of the
\operatorname{HP}_{\beta,\delta}
process for complex
\delta
with
\Re\delta>-1/2
.

It would also be interesting to extend the second part of Theorem
2
for all
\beta>0
.

Problem 4
.

Prove (or disprove) that
\rho_{\beta}^{(2)}(0,\lambda)
is jointly continuous in
\lambda
and
\beta
for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\beta>0
.

The small
\lambda
asymptotics of Corollary
5
should hold for all
\beta>0
, but we did not find a reference for this in the literature.

Problem 5
.

Extend the small
\lambda
asymptotics of Corollary
5
for all
\beta>0
with the factorials replaced with Gamma functions in (
12
).

As mentioned before, it would be instructive to show directly that the representation for
\rho^{(2)}_{2n}(0,\lambda)
found in Theorem
4
agrees with (
15
).

Problem 6
.

Show directly that the power series given in Theorem
4
is equivalent to the integral expression (
15
) proved by Forrester.

Finally, the most challenging problem is to extend the results of this paper to provide any information about higher-order correlation functions.

Problem 7
.

Find a characterization of the higher order correlation functions of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process in terms of the
\alpha_{\lambda}
diffusion given in (

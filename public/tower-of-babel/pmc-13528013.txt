Development and immunological evaluation of a multi-antigen recombinant adenovirus vaccine candidate against PRRSV and PCV2
Authors: Yuwan Li, Junzhi Ji, Ke Wang, Zequn Wang, Jiawei Wan, Jiahao Zhong, Jin Feng, Jinding Chen, Shuangqi Fan, Keke Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, a recombinant adenovirus vaccine candidate constructed and evaluated to addressed the widespread clinical problem of co-infections of Porcine Reproductive and Respiratory Syndrome Virus (PRRSV) and Porcine Circovirus Type 2 (PCV2). Epidemiological analysis revealed a PRRSV-positive rate of 40.8%, with prevalent strains belonging to Lineage 1 and Lineage 8. Analysis of key amino acid sites in the GP5 protein indicated the presence of highly virulent strain characteristics and widespread wild-type strain variation. Based on these findings and previous studies, we selected multiple PRRSV proteins (GP2–GP5, M, N) from multiple strains and the validated PCV2 Cap protein fused with dominant T/B cell epitopes, and successfully constructed four recombinant adenoviruses using a 2A peptide-linked strategy. These constructs stably expressed the target antigens in HEK293 cells and demonstrated good passage stability. Animal experiments showed that piglets immunized with the recombinant adenoviruses developed high levels of PRRSV- and PCV2-specific antibodies and neutralizing antibodies, and exhibited lymphocyte proliferation and secretion of cytokines including IFN-γ, IL-2, and IL-4, indicating the vaccine simultaneously elicited both humoral and cellular immune responses. Following PRRSV challenge, immunized groups, particularly the multi-antigen combined immunization group, demonstrated significant clinical protection. This study provides a promising vaccine candidate and a theoretical foundation for the coordinated control of PRRSV and PCV2.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-026-05819-8.

Introduction

Porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus (PRRSV) and Porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV2) are two significant pathogens that severely threaten the global swine industry [1]. As a single-stranded positive-sense RNA virus, PRRSV exhibits high genetic variability, and its structural proteins (GP2-GP5, M, N) and non-structural proteins play critical roles in viral replication and immune regulation [2–4]. PCV2 is a non-enveloped, single-stranded circular DNA virus, whose capsid protein (Cap) serves as the major immunogenic protein and plays a central role in viral immune evasion and infection [5, 6]. Clinically, co-infections of PRRSV and PCV2 are common, and their synergistic interaction can lead to more severe immunosuppression and clinical symptoms, significantly increasing the difficulty of disease control [1, 7, 8].

Currently, prevention and control of PRRSV and PCV2 primarily rely on vaccination, but available commercial vaccines are mostly monovalent, facing issues such as narrow protection spectrum and complex immunization schedules [9–11]. PRRSV vaccines include inactivated vaccines, live attenuated vaccines, subunit vaccines, and live vector vaccines, yet each has certain limitations: inactivated vaccines often show weak immunogenicity and require multiple administrations; live attenuated vaccines carry the risk of virulence reversion; subunit vaccines may lack sufficient immunogenicity persistence [12]. Although PCV2 vaccines are widely used in clinical practice, current vaccines are mainly developed based on PCV2a or PCV2b subtypes, and their cross-protective efficacy against the currently predominant PCV2d strain needs further improvement [13].

Adenovirus vectors have become an ideal platform for vaccine development due to their broad infection tropism, strong capacity for carrying foreign genes, and excellent immunogenicity. Replication-deficient adenovirus vectors ensure safety while retaining basic viral structure and function, enabling efficient expression of target antigens in host cells and inducing robust humoral and cellular immune responses [14, 15]. In the field of veterinary vaccines, adenovirus vector-based vaccines have demonstrated potential for controlling various animal diseases.

This study aims to construct a novel bivalent recombinant adenovirus vector vaccine against PRRSV and PCV2, addressing the PRRSV epidemic status and PCV2 co-infection issue in Guangdong Province. By systematically analyzing the genetic evolutionary characteristics of PRRSV epidemic strains in Guangdong, multiple key PRRSV antigenic epitopes (GP2, GP3, GP4, GP5, M, N) were selected and combined with the PCV2 Cap protein. Using the adenovirus vector platform, a multi-antigen recombinant vaccine was constructed, and its immunogenicity and protective efficacy were systematically evaluated. This research provides new technical strategies and theoretical foundations for the coordinated prevention and control of PRRSV and PCV2.

Materials and methods

Viral strains, cell lines, and clinical samples

The PRRSV NADC30-like strain and PCV2 strain used in this study were preserved and provided by the Laboratory of Veterinary Microbiology and Immunology, South China Agricultural University. HEK 293 cells (RRID: CVCL_0045) were purchased from Guangzhou Lide Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Marc-145 cells (RRID: CVCL_4540) and PK-15 (RRID: CVCL_2160) were preserved in our laboratory. The cell lines were identified by STR profiling and were free of mycoplasma contamination.

A total of 49 samples suspected of PRRSV infection (including lung, lymph node, and blood samples) were collected from large-scale pig farms in Guangdong province between 2023 and 2024. All samples were stored at −80 °C for subsequent use.

Reagents and antibodies

The main reagents used in this study are as follows: Golden Star T6 Super PCR Mix (TSINGKE, TSE102), Phanta Max Super-Fidelity DNA Polymerase (Vazyme, P505-d1), Plasmid Mini Kit (Omega Bio-Tek, D6943-02), mouse monoclonal anti-His tag antibody (Sino Biological, 105,327-MM02T), mouse monoclonal anti-Flag tag antibody (Sino Biological, 109,143-MM13), mouse monoclonal anti-HA tag antibody (Abcam, AB18181), FITC goat anti-mouse IgG (Beyotime, A0568).

Viral nucleic acid extraction and detection

Tissue samples (lungs, lymph nodes) were minced, added to PBS for homogenization, and centrifuged at 3000 r/min for 10 min at 4 °C to collect the supernatant. Viral DNA was extracted using the Viral DNA Kit (Omega, D3892-02), and viral RNA was extracted using the Viral RNA Kit (Omega, R6872-02). Reverse transcription of RNA was performed according to the system: 4 µL of 4 × DNA wiper Mix, 1 µg of template RNA, added with ddH₂O to 16 µL, 42 °C for 2 min; then 4 µL of 5 × Hiscript II qPCR mix was added, 50 °C for 15 min, 85 °C for 1 s.

Construction of recombinant adenovirus vaccines

Based on the GP5 and M gene sequences of PRRSV HP-PRRSV (Accession: EF112445.1), NADC30-like (Accession: JN654459.1), and CH-1a (Accession: AY032626.1) from GenBank, genes were connected using flexible linkers, and nucleotide sequences encoding 6 × His tag, 3 × FLAG tag, and HA tag proteins were added at the termini, resulting in a gene sequence containing GP5 + M from HP-PRRSV, CH-1a, and NADC30-like types. The selection of GP5, M, GP2, GP3, GP4, and N from PRRSV NADC30-like strain was based on their broad coverage of neutralizing epitopes (GP5, GP2-4), strong immunogenicity (M), and early cellular immune activation (N) [16, 17]. Based on the GP2, N, GP3, and GP4 gene sequences of PRRSV NADC30-Like (Accession: JN654459.1) from GenBank, GP2 and N genes and GP3 and GP4 genes were connected via linkers, respectively, and nucleotide sequences encoding the 6 × His tag were introduced at the termini, resulting in recombinant GP2 + N and GP3 + GP4 gene sequences of the NADC30-Like type.

The PCV2 TBCap gene cassette, which had been previously validated for immune efficacy in our laboratory using a baculovirus expression system [18], comprises the Cap gene of the PCV2 LG strain fused with dominant T-and B-cell epitopes and a 6 × His-tag coding sequence.

All sequences were codon-optimized and synthesized by Suzhou Hongxun Biotechnology Co., Ltd., and cloned into the pAdTrack-CMV shuttle vector to construct four recombinant shuttle plasmids: pAdTrack-TBCap, pAdTrack-GP5 + M, pAdTrack-GP5 + M + TBCap, and pAdTrack-GP3 + GP4 + GP2 + N. Primer sequence information used for shuttle plasmid identification

The AdEasy adenovirus system was used for recombinant virus construction. The linearized shuttle plasmid (via PmeI) and the backbone vector pAdEasy-1 were co-transformed into BJ5183 competent cells. Recombinant adenovirus plasmids were identified by PacI digestion. Plasmids were transfected into HEK 293 cells using Lipofectamine™ 3000 transfection reagent for virus packaging. Cytopathic effect (CPE) was observed 7–10 days post-transfection. Cells and supernatant were collected, subjected to freeze–thaw cycles, and centrifuged to collect the virus stock. Recombinant adenoviruses were amplified by continuous passaging. The TCID₅₀ of the third-generation virus was determined by the Reed-Muench method. For subsequent animal immunization, the four recombinant adenoviruses were mixed at a 1:1 ratio based on equal TCID₅₀ titers to ensure balanced antigen delivery.

Antigen expression and viral stability verification

Target antigen expression was verified by indirect immunofluorescence assay (IFA). HEK 293 cells infected with recombinant adenoviruses were harvested. In the IFA, Anti-His/Flag/HA tag monoclonal antibodies were used as primary antibodies, and FITC-labeled Goat Anti-Mouse IgG was used as the secondary antibody, specific fluorescence was observed under a fluorescence microscope. Viral passage stability was assessed by PCR identification and sequencing analysis of the viral genome extracted after continuous passaging to the 10th generation.

Animal experiment design

Twenty 4-week-old piglets, negative for PRRSV/PCV2 antigens/antibodies, were purchased from a pig farm in Guangzhou and randomly divided into 4 groups (5 piglets per group): commercial vaccine group (Keqian Biotech Kelanning inactivated PRRS vaccine + Keqian Biotech Keyuanning PCV2 subunit vaccine), pAdEasy-GP5 + M + TBCap group, pAdEasy-GP5 + M + TBCap + pAdEasy-GP2 + GP3 + GP4 + N group, and DMEM control group (Table 5). All immunizations were administered via the intramuscular route, with a booster immunization 14 days after the primary immunization. For the commercial vaccine group, the two vaccines were mixed and administered simultaneously to mimic the co-administration scenario, although separate immunization is more common in practice. This design was chosen to minimize the number of injections but may not fully represent field usage. Detection of PRRSV and PCV2-specific antibodies in piglet serum

Serum samples were collected from piglets at indicated time points post-immunization. The levels of PRRSV- and PCV2-specific antibodies in peripheral blood were measured using commercial ELISA kits (PRRSV Antibody ELISA Kit, RXYB0002, Quanzhou Ruixin Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; PCV2 2d-Cap ELISA Kit, RX-AN500680P, Wuhan Keqian Biology Co., Ltd.) according to the manufacturers’ instructions. Briefly, serum samples were added to antigen-coated microplates and incubated at 37 °C for 60 min. After washing with PBST, horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody was added and incubated for 30 min at 37 °C. Following further washing, chromogenic substrate was added and the reaction was terminated with stop solution. The optical density (OD) was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader, and antibody levels were expressed as S/P values calculated according to the kit’s formula.

Detection of PRRSV and PCV2 neutralizing antibodies in piglet serum

Neutralizing antibody titers against PRRSV and PCV2 were determined using a serum neutralization assay. Serum samples were heat-inactivated at 56 °C for 30 min. Two-fold serial dilutions of the inactivated sera were prepared in serum-free medium. An equal volume (50 μL) of virus suspension containing 200 TCID50 of PRRSV or PCV2 was mixed with each serum dilution and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. The serum-virus mixtures were then added to 96-well plates containing confluent monolayers of Marc-145 cells (for PRRSV) or PK-15 cells (for PCV2), with eight replicate wells per dilution. Following incubation at 37 °C for 1 h, the mixtures were replaced with fresh maintenance medium supplemented with trypsin and the cells were further cultured at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ incubator. For PRRSV, cytopathic effects (CPE) were monitored daily under an inverted microscope, and the neutralizing antibody titer was determined as the highest serum dilution that inhibited 50% of CPE. For PCV2, the plates were subjected to an indirect immunofluorescence assay (IFA) and the titer was determined as the highest serum dilution that resulted in 50% reduction in fluorescence-positive cells. Neutralizing antibody titers were expressed as the geometric mean titer (GMT) and log₂-transformed for statistical analysis.

Cytokines release assay

To evaluate the cytokine secretion profile induced by vaccination, serum samples were collected from each group at 28 days post-primary immunization. The concentrations of IFN-γ, IL-2, and IL-4 in serum were measured using commercial porcine-specific ELISA kits (Porcine IFN-γ ELISA Kit, MM-041202; Porcine IL-2 ELISA Kit, MM-042102; Porcine IL-4 ELISA Kit, MM-041902; Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd.) following the manufacturers’ instructions. Briefly, serum samples and standards were added to antibody-precoated plates and incubated at 37 °C for 90 min. After washing, biotinylated detection antibodies were added and incubated for 60 min, followed by streptavidin-HRP incubation and chromogenic substrate development. The reaction was terminated with stop solution, and the OD was measured at 450 nm. Cytokine concentrations were calculated from standard curves generated with serial dilutions of recombinant cytokines.

Lymphocyte proliferation assay

To assess the effect of vaccination on lymphocyte proliferative activity, peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from piglets at 35 days post-primary immunization using porcine PBMC isolation kits (TBD Science, Tianjin, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The isolated PBMCs were resuspended in RPMI-1640 complete medium (RPMI-1640 containing 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin), adjusted to a concentration of 2 × 10⁶ cells/mL, and seeded into 96-well flat-bottom plates at 100 μL per well. The cells were then stimulated with 100 μL of complete medium containing purified PRRSV or PCV2 antigen (20 μg/mL), with unstimulated wells receiving complete medium alone as negative controls. Each treatment was performed in four replicate wells. The plates were incubated at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ incubator for 2–4 h. Subsequently, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well and the plates were further incubated for 2 h. The OD was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader, with readings taken every 30 min during the incubation period. The stimulation index (SI) was calculated using the following formula: SI = Mean OD₄₅₀ of antigen-stimulated wells/Mean OD₄₅₀ of complete medium-treated wells.

Challenge protection experiment

Two weeks after the secondary immunization, all piglets were challenged via intramuscular injection with a dose of 10⁶ TCID₅₀ of the NADC30-like PRRSV strain. After challenge, piglets were observed continuously for 14 days, recording clinical manifestations, morbidity, and survival rates to evaluate vaccine protective efficacy.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 9.5.1 software. One-way ANOVA or multi-way ANOVA were used for statistical analysis among multiple groups. P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant (ns, P > 0.05; *, P < 0.05; **, P < 0.01; ***, P < 0.001; ****, P < 0.0001).

Results

Status of PRRSV and PCV2 co-infections in Guangdong Province

To clarify the clinical infection background and genetic characteristics of prevalent PRRSV and PCV2 strains in Guangdong Province, this study collected 49 clinical suspected samples from large-scale pig farms in Guangdong Province between 2023 and 2024. The samples included lungs, lymph nodes, and blood. RT-PCR detection results showed a PRRSV positive rate of 40.8% (20/49), a PCV2 positive rate of 20.4% (10/49), and a co-infection rate of 10.2% (5/49) (Table 6). The co-infection rate was calculated as the number of samples testing positive for both PRRSV and PCV2 divided by the total number of samples (5/49 = 10.2%). This rate is consistent with recent reports of PRRSV/PCV2 co-infections in Chinese swine herds (typically ranging from 8–15%), indicating a moderately prevalent but epidemiologically significant level. Genetic evolutionary characteristics of PRRSV in Guangdong Province

To analyze the genetic diversity of the PRRSV strains, the ORF5 gene and whole genome of the positive samples were amplified and sequenced (Fig. 1A and B). Phylogenetic analysis showed that all six sequenced strains (gd-2401 to gd-2406) belonged to PRRSV-2. Specifically, gd-2401 and gd-2404 belonged to Lineage 8, clustering with the vaccine-related classical strain CH-1a and the highly pathogenic strain JXA1, respectively. In contrast, gd-2402, gd-2403, gd-2405, and gd-2406 belonged to Lineage 1. Among these, gd-2402, gd-2403, and gd-2405 clustered with the NADC34 genotype representative strain IA/2014/NADC34, while gd-2406 clustered with NADC30-like representative strains (Fig. 1C). The full-genome strains amplified in this study all belonged to Lineage 8, and full-genome phylogenetic tree analysis further supported the classification results mentioned above (Fig. 1D). Serum antibody and neutralizing antibody levels are key indicators for assessing vaccine efficacy. Serological testing showed that all vaccine groups induced high levels of PRRSV and PCV2 specific antibodies. Among them, the AdEasy-GP5 + M + TBCap + AdEasy-GP2 + GP3 + GP4 + N group showed the highest PRRSV and PCV2 specific antibody levels on day 14, suggesting that multi-antigen co-immunization can accelerate the antibody response. The commercial vaccine group showed slightly higher antibody levels on day 28, but there was no statistical difference between the groups (Fig. 5B). Detection of PRRSV and PCV2 neutralizing antibodies in the serum of immunized piglets showed that compared to the DMEM group, neutralizing antibody titers were significantly increased in all vaccine groups. Furthermore, the pAdEasy-GP2 + GP3 + GP4 + N + pAdEasy-GP5 + M + TBCap vaccine group induced the highest levels of neutralizing antibodies against both PRRSV and PCV2 in piglets (Fig. 5C). The results indicate that the vaccines developed in this experiment can stimulate piglets to produce high levels of PRRSV and PCV2 antibodies and possess a strong capacity for sustained induction of antibody production.

Analysis of cellular immunity showed that the secretion levels of IFN-γ, IL-2, and IL-4 in all vaccine groups were significantly higher than those in the control group (Fig. 5D), indicating successful activation of both Th1 and Th2 type immune responses by the vaccines. Additionally, lymphocyte proliferation assays revealed that the stimulation indices (SI) in response to PRRSV and PCV2 antigens were significantly increased in all vaccine groups (Fig. 5E). The pAdEasy-GP5 + M + TBCap + pAdEasy-GP2 + GP3 + GP4 + N group exhibited the strongest response, indicating that this combination effectively mobilizes a dual-directional immune response involving both cellular and humoral immunity.

Recombinant adenovirus vaccines provide significant protective efficacy against PRRSV challenge

To validate the protective efficacy of the vaccines, a challenge experiment was conducted using the NADC30-like PRRSV strain at a dose of 10⁶ TCID₅₀. The results showed that the DMEM control group had the lowest survival rate and exhibited typical clinical symptoms post-challenge, including growth retardation, decreased appetite, dyspnea, and diarrhea. In contrast, all vaccine groups demonstrated varying degrees of protection. The pAdEasy-GP5 + M + TBCap + pAdEasy-GP2 + GP3 + GP4 + N group showed the highest survival rate (Fig. 5F), indicating that this combined vaccine provides effective immunoprotection against challenge with the current prevalent strain. It should be noted that due to the absence of a PCV2 challenge, the protective efficacy of the vaccine against PCV2 infection remains to be directly evaluated in future studies.

Discussion

The concurrent infection of PRRSV and PCV2 is currently widespread. Such co-infections can lead to host immunosuppression and a significant decrease in body resistance, readily predisposing animals to secondary infections with various viral or bacterial pathogens, thereby posing a persistent threat to the global swine industry. The primary strategy for controlling these two diseases currently relies on separate vaccination with single vaccines. However, multiple immunizations can easily induce stress reactions and present limitations such as operational complexity and potential immune interference. In this study, we conducted a small-scale molecular epidemiological survey of PRRSV in Guangdong province to provide a regional background for antigen selection, and successfully constructed a novel multi-antigen recombinant adenovirus vaccine candidate against PRRSV and PCV2, offering a new solution for the coordinated control of these two pathogens.

A PRRSV-positive rate of 40.8% was detected in Guangdong, with a 10.2% rate of PRRSV/PCV2 co-infection, which is consistent with recent reports highlighting the prominence of PRRSV and PCV2 co-infections [19, 20]. Genetic evolution analysis revealed that the positive strains identified in this study all belonged to PRRSV-2, subclustered within Lineage 1 (NADC30-like and NADC34-like) and Lineage 8 (HP-PRRSV), reflecting the complex current PRRSV epidemic situation. Analysis of the aa 137 site in the GP5 protein indicated that all positive strains in this study were wild-type strains. and two potential highly virulent strains (gd-2401 and gd-2405) were identified based on aa positions 13 and 151, providing crucial molecular evidence for vaccine design [21]. As the major neutralizing antigen, extensive mutations in the hypervariable region of the GP5 protein may impact the protective efficacy of existing vaccines, underscoring the necessity of developing novel vaccines based on local prevalent strains [22]. However, it should be noted that this epidemiological survey was based on a limited number of samples from a single province, and the findings may not fully represent the broader genetic diversity of PRRSV across Guangdong or other regions. The primary purpose of including this analysis was to inform the selection of locally relevant PRRSV antigens for vaccine construction, rather than to provide a comprehensive epidemiological characterization.

This study innovatively selected six key PRRSV antigens (GP2, GP3, GP4, GP5, M, N) combined with the PCV2 Cap protein. This strategy aims to enhance immune protection by covering a broader range of antigenic epitopes. The GP5 protein, a key protein on the PRRSV envelope, is indispensable for viral particle assembly and is a critical antigenic target. The M protein, a structural protein of PRRSV, exhibits strong immunogenicity and is often considered a high-quality candidate for PRRSV subunit vaccines. The heterodimer formed by the GP5 and M proteins plays a key role in viral entry [23, 24], while GP2, GP3, and GP4 can form a trimer constituting the PRRSV vesicle membrane involved in virus neutralization. Simultaneously, the N protein, as a target for early immune responses, can rapidly activate cellular immunity [25]. For PCV2, we used the PCV2 TBCap gene cassette, which had been previously validated for immunogenicity in our laboratory using a baculovirus expression system comprises the Cap protein of the PCV2 LG strain fused with dominant T- and B-cell epitopes. Moreover, previous studies have demonstrated that PCV2 Cap protein induced antibodies exhibit cross-neutralizing activity among different PCV2 genotypes (PCV2a, PCV2b, and PCV2d), suggesting that the LG strain-derived Cap antigen may provide cross-protection against heterologous PCV2 strains [18]. It was selected as the target antigen protein for preparing the PCV2 recombinant adenovirus vector vaccine, anticipating a vaccine product with superior immune performance.

Adenovirus vectors possess a strong gene-carrying capacity, capable of accommodating approximately 7.5 kb of foreign genes, which fully meets the practical requirements for vaccine antigen expression [26]. Adenoviruses themselves have strong immunogenicity, a characteristic that can effectively promote specific immune responses against vaccine antigens during vaccine development [27]. This intrinsic adjuvant effect has been well documented; for example, adenovirus vectors activate innate immune pathways via TLR9 and STING signaling, leading to enhanced antigen presentation and adaptive immunity [28, 29]. Moreover, adenovirus vectors demonstrate good stability in vitro, and their production, purification, and storage procedures are relatively straightforward, fully complying with the requirements for large-scale industrial production. Considering that swine may have pre-existing immunity to certain adenovirus serotypes, which could interfere with vaccine efficacy, human adenovirus type 5 was specifically chosen as the vector in this study. Research indicates that the foot-and-mouth disease virus (FMDV) 2 A protein possesses the ability to self-cleave upstream and downstream proteins. This protein not only has high cleavage efficiency but also ensures balanced expression of upstream and downstream genes, and it is relatively short in structure. Based on this, this study utilized a human type 5 replication-defective adenovirus vector to carry the relevant antigens of PRRSV and PCV2, achieving multi-gene co-expression through the FMDV 2 A peptide, ensuring effective in vivo presentation of each antigen.

Immune evaluation results showed that the constructed recombinant adenovirus vaccine could effectively induce robust humoral and cellular immune responses. Regarding humoral immunity, the combined immunization group (pAdEasy-GP5 + M + TBCap + pAdEasy-GP2 + GP3 + GP4 + N) exhibited high levels of PRRSV GP5 and PCV2 Cap-specific antibodies as early as 14 days post-vaccination, suggesting that the multi-antigen co-expression strategy can significantly accelerate the establishment of early humoral immune responses. Notably, the neutralizing antibody titer against PRRSV in this group increased most rapidly and was significantly higher than in single antigen groups, indicating that the multi-antigen combination, by covering a broader range of neutralizing epitopes, may synergistically enhance the breadth and strength of the antibody response. However, the neutralizing antibody response against PCV2 remained superior in the commercial vaccine group, suggesting that the immune protection mechanism of our vaccine against PCV2 might rely more on the synergistic effect of subsequent cellular immunity, or that the conformational design and expression strategy of the Cap protein require further optimization.

In terms of cellular immunity, all recombinant adenovirus vaccines effectively induced a mixed Th1/Th2 immune response. Significantly elevated levels of IFN-γ and IL-2 in the serum of vaccinated groups indicated effective activation of Th1-type immunity, which is particularly crucial for clearing intracellular pathogens like PRRSV. Simultaneously, increased IL-4 levels suggested the mobilization of a Th2-type response, aiding in B cell differentiation and antibody class switching. This balanced Th1/Th2 response pattern is likely the result of the intrinsic immune adjuvant effect of the adenovirus vector combined with the stimulation by multiple antigens. Furthermore, lymphocyte proliferation assays showed that the SI was significantly increased in all vaccine groups, indicating that the vaccine not only stimulates cytokine secretion but also enhances the clonal expansion capacity of antigen-specific T cells, laying the foundation for establishing long-term immune memory.

Challenge protection experiments demonstrated that the recombinant adenovirus vaccine provided a certain degree of protection against challenge with the PRRSV NADC30-like virulent strain but did not achieve complete protection. The pAdEasy-GP5 + M + TBCap group showed complete survival post-challenge, indicating good protective potential. However, partial mortality in the combined immunization group might be related to competitive immune suppression between antigens, epitope interference, or individual response variations. Such phenomena have been observed in other multi-antigen vaccine studies, where co-delivery of multiple antigens can lead to immunodominance hierarchies or antigenic competition, potentially reducing the response to individual components. Future optimization could include adjusting the ratio of different recombinant adenoviruses or using different linkers to balance antigen expression. This result aligns with most studies on adenovirus vector vaccines, which often significantly alleviate clinical symptoms and reduce viral load but rarely achieve complete sterilizing immunity [30]. In the future, adjusting the immunization dose and frequency, introducing mucosal immunization routes, or combining with novel adjuvants (such as TLR agonists) could further enhance its protective efficacy. Additionally, evaluating the vaccine's cross-protective capacity against different PRRSV lineages and the prevalent PCV2d strain will be crucial for advancing its practical application.

Study limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the epidemiological analysis was based on a limited number of samples from a single province, and the findings may not fully represent the broader genetic diversity of PRRSV across Guangdong or other regions. Given the high mutation rate of PRRSV, the vaccine antigens selected based on Guangdong strains may not provide full protection against strains from other regions; continuous surveillance and periodic vaccine updates would be necessary for practical application. Second, the challenge experiment only assessed protection against NADC30-like PRRSV; no PCV2 challenge or co-challenge was performed. Although the vaccine candidate induced robust PCV2-specific antibody responses, the actual protective efficacy against PCV2 remains unproven and requires further validation in future studies. Third, a PCV2-only immunization group was not included, making it difficult to assess whether co-immunization with PRRSV antigens affects the PCV2-specific immune response. Based on the observed differences in antibody responses between the multi-antigen combined group and the PCV2-containing group, we cannot rule out the possibility of antigenic competition. Fourth, the commercial vaccine control involved mixing of two monovalent vaccines for simultaneous administration, which does not reflect standard separate immunization practices in the field and may have compromised the validity of the comparison. Future studies should address these gaps by including multi-regional sampling, ratio optimization, PCV2-only and co-challenge models, and more representative vaccine controls.

Conclusion

In this study, we successfully constructed a multi-antigen recombinant adenovirus vaccine candidate targeting PRRSV and PCV2. Immunization with this candidate induced both humoral and cellular immunity responses against both PRRSV and PCV2 antigens, and conferred significant protection against PRRSV challenge in piglets. These findings demonstrate the potential of this multi-antigen design strategy as a promising approach for controlling PRRSV/PCV2 co-infections. The multi-antigen design strategy not only broadens the immune coverage but may also reduce immune escape caused by strain variation. However, it should be noted that while the vaccine candidate elicited strong PCV2-specific antibody responses, the protective efficacy against PCV2 challenge has not yet been demonstrated in this study and requires further investigation. Future work should also evaluate the safety and duration of immunity in different swine populations, optimize antigen combinations and ratios, explore the use of combined adjuvants, and refine immunization protocols to achieve more comprehensive protection against both pathogens.

The μ \mu -deformed Einstein field equations with μ \mu -dependent effective cosmological constant
O.P. Mykhailiv, Yu.A. Mishchenko, A.M. Gavrilik

Abstract
In this paper, we derive the μ \mu -deformed Einstein field equations from the generalized thermodynamic functions of the μ \mu -deformed analog of Bose gas model, applying the (adapted) Verlinde’s approach. The basic role of deformation parameter is shown: it provides the possibility to vary the value of the cosmological constant. Due to this, we suggest an interesting treatment of the cosmological constant (CC) problem within the framework of μ \mu -deformation. Namely, viewing the derived μ \mu -deformed CC as an effective one and varying the parameter μ \mu appropriately, we gain the possibility to drastically reduce the CC, so as to get for it the realistic value. Also, the relation with dark matter is discussed.

2.1
The thermostatics of
\mu
-Bose gas model

We start with recalling some facts, which are necessary for

[

In the standard Bose gas model, the total number of particles
is expressed as the derivative of the logarithm of the grand
partition function
\mathcal{Z}

\mathcal{N}=z\frac{d}{dz}\ln\mathcal{Z}

(1)

where the fugacity
z
as function of the chemical potential
\tilde{\mu}

is given by
z=e^{\beta\tilde{\mu}}
,
\beta=\frac{1}{k_{B}T}
is
inverse temperature, and the Boltzmann constant
k_{B}
.
The grand canonical partition function
\mathcal{Z}
is treated through
its logarithm by the following expression:

\ln\mathcal{Z}=-\displaystyle\sum_{i}\ln(1-ze^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}})

(2)

with
\epsilon_{i}
being

\epsilon_{i}=\frac{\overrightarrow{|p|}^{2}}{2m}=\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{2m}.

(3)

From now on, we restrict ourselves to three-dimensional space. To formulate
the thermodynamic framework for the
\mu
-analog of the Bose gas model,
the standard expression for the total number of particles
\mathcal{N}

is supposed to incorporate the
\mu
-dependence. Namely, modified definition
for the total number of particles is given as

\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}=z\,\mathcal{D}^{(\mu)}_{z}\ln\mathcal{Z}=-z\,\mathcal{D}^{(\mu)}_{z}\sum_{i}\ln\left(1-ze^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}}\right)

(4)

where we use the
\mu
-derivative

\mathcal{D}^{(\mu)}_{x}f(x)=\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime}_{x}(t^{\mu}x)\,dt,\quad f^{\prime}_{x}(t^{\mu}x)=\frac{df(t^{\mu}x)}{dx}

(5)

which acts on monomials as follows

\mathcal{D}^{(\mu)}_{x}x^{n}=[n]_{\mu}x^{n-1}

(6)

where

[n]_{\mu}\equiv\frac{n}{1+\mu n},\quad 0\leq\mu\leq 1.

(7)

Then the
\mu
-deformed operator
\mathcal{D}^{(\mu)}
is applied to
the logarithm of the grand canonical partition function in eq. (
2
),
and the modified total number of particles reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}

\displaystyle=z\sum_{i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}n}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n}z^{n-1}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n}\left(e^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}}\right)^{n}z^{n}.

(8)

For the series to converge, the following condition for the product

e^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}}z
must hold:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n}\left(e^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}}\right)^{n}z^{n}<\infty

\displaystyle\qquad\Rightarrow\quad 0\leq|ze^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}}|<1.

(9)

Separate the contribution of the
p_{i}=0,i=0
term from the remaining sum.
This yields

\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}={\sum_{i}}^{\prime}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n}\left(e^{-\beta\epsilon_{i}}\right)^{n}z^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n}z^{n}.

(10)

As is well known, for a large volume
V
and a large number of particles

\mathcal{N}
, the spectrum of single-particle states becomes nearly continuous.
Due to this, we replace the summation in eq. (
8
) by a 3-integral
over 3-momentum space.

\sum_{i}\rightarrow\frac{V}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\int d^{3}k.

(11)

Then we obtain

\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}=\frac{V}{\lambda_{T}^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n^{5/2}}z^{n}+\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}_{0},\quad\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}_{0}\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n}z^{n}.

(12)

Here the thermal wavelength
\lambda_{T}
is defined in terms of

m

\lambda_{T}=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\hbar^{2}}{mk_{B}T}}.

(13)

The standard Bose-Einstein function is given by series

g_{\ell}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{\ell}}.

(14)

As a generalization of the latter, the
\mu
-polylogarithm is introduced, namely

g^{(\mu)}_{\ell}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n^{\ell+1}}z^{n}.

(15)

Then the eq. (
10

\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}=\frac{V}{\lambda_{T}^{3}}g^{(\mu)}_{3/2}(z)+g^{(\mu)}_{0}(z)

(16)

where

g^{(\mu)}_{3/2}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n^{5/2}}z^{n},\quad g^{(\mu)}_{0}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n}z^{n}.

(17)

The
\mu
-deformed
\ln\mathcal{Z}^{(\mu)}

\ln\mathcal{Z}^{(\mu)}=\left(z\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}.

(18)

After inserting
\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}

\displaystyle\left(z\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{V}{\lambda_{T}^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n^{5/2}}z^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n}z^{n}\right)=

\displaystyle=\frac{V}{\lambda_{T}^{3}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n^{5/2}}\left(z\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{-1}z^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[n]_{\mu}}{n}\left(z\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{-1}z^{n},

\ln\mathcal{Z}^{(\mu)}=\frac{V}{\lambda_{T}^{3}}g^{(\mu)}_{5/2}+g^{(\mu)}_{1}.

(19)

The internal energy
\mathcal{U}^{(\mu)}
is determined through the relation

\mathcal{U}^{(\mu)}=-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln\mathcal{Z}^{(\mu)}\right)_{z,V}.

(20)

With the usual

-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\left(\frac{V}{\lambda_{T}^{3}}g^{(\mu)}_{5/2}+g^{(\mu)}_{1}\right)_{z,V}\Rightarrow\ \mathcal{U}^{(\mu)}=-\tilde{\mu}\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}.

The equation of state then reads

\frac{\left(\;\!PV\right)_{\mu}}{k_{B}T}=\ln\mathcal{Z}^{(\mu)}=\frac{V}{\lambda_{T}^{3}}g_{5/2}^{(\mu)}(z)+g_{1}^{(\mu)}(z).

(22)

The Helmholtz free energy of the model results as

\mathcal{H}^{(\mu)}=\tilde{\mu}\mathcal{N}^{(\mu)}-\left(PV\right)_{\mu}.

(23)

The

-deformed entropy can be obtained from the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(\mu)}=\frac{1}{T_{U}}\biggl(-2\tilde{\mu}\frac{V}{\lambda_{T}^{3}}g^{(\mu)}_{3/2}(z)-2\tilde{\mu}g^{(\mu)}_{0}(z)+E\frac{V}{\lambda_{T}^{3}}\times

\displaystyle\times g^{(\mu)}_{5/2}(z)+Eg^{(\mu)}_{1}(z)\biggl).

(24)

In the
\mu
-deformed extension of Verlinde’s emergent
gravity, we use the identification
E=k_{B}T
rather than

\frac{1}{2}k_{B}T
,
assuming each
\mu
-boson encodes two fundamental bits of information.
Physically,

Consequently, each

Explicitly rewriting the expression for the thermal wavelength and

\lambda_{T}^{3}=\left(\frac{2\pi\hbar^{2}}{mE}\right)^{3/2},\qquad\frac{\tilde{\mu}}{E}=\ln z,

we get the general expression for

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(\mu)}=\frac{1}{T_{U}}\biggl(-2V\left(\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar^{2}}\right)^{3/2}g^{(\mu)}_{3/2}(z)E^{5/2}\ln z-2E\times

\displaystyle\times g^{(\mu)}_{0}(z)\ln z+V\left(\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar^{2}}\right)^{3/2}g^{(\mu)}_{5/2}(z)E^{5/2}+Eg^{(\mu)}_{1}(z)\biggl).

2.2
Derivation of the
\mu
-deformed Einstein equations
using Verlinde approach

In Verlinde’s approach to entropic gravity, the system approaches to
statistical equilibrium, when the entropic force stemming from the changes
in entropy, becomes balanced with the forces that contribute to the increase
in entropy. Accordingly, the formulation suggests that gravity itself can be
understood as an emergent force arising from the entropic dynamics of
underlying microscopic degrees of freedom, rather than a fundamental interaction.
At equilibrium, the total entropy
\mathcal{S}
of the system remains constant,
and it reaches an extreme value. This is a direct consequence of the second law
of thermodynamics, which states that entropy tends to increase until a maximum
is reached under the given constraints.
In what follows, the entropy
\mathcal{S}^{(\mu)}(E,x^{\nu})
satisfies:

\frac{d}{dx^{\nu}}\mathcal{S}^{(\mu)}(E,x^{\nu})=0.

(27)

From Verlinde’s perspective, the system’s equilibrium is not only a condition
of force balance but also a reflection of the deeper thermodynamic
nature of gravity

\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}^{(\mu)}}{\partial E}\frac{\partial E}{\partial x^{\nu}}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}^{(\mu)}}{\partial x^{\nu}}=0

(28)

where the components represent the energy gradient and an entropic force

\frac{\partial E}{\partial x^{\nu}}=-\mathcal{F}_{\nu},\quad\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial x^{\nu}}=\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{S}.

(29)

From the derivative of
\mathcal{S}^{(\mu)}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}^{(\mu)}}{\partial E}=\frac{1}{T_{U}}\biggl(\frac{5V}{2}g^{(\mu)}_{5/2}(z)\left(\frac{mE}{2\pi\hbar^{2}}\right)^{3/2}-5Vg^{(\mu)}_{3/2}(z)\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{mE}{2\pi\hbar^{2}}\right)^{3/2}\ln z+g^{(\mu)}_{1}(z)-2g^{(\mu)}_{0}(z)\ln z\biggl).

(30)

For simplicity, let us use the notation

\displaystyle G_{1}(\mu;z)=\frac{5}{2}\frac{V}{\lambda_{T}^{3}}\left(g^{(\mu)}_{5/2}(z)-2\ln zg^{(\mu)}_{3/2}(z)\right),

(31)

\displaystyle G_{2}(\mu;z)=g^{(\mu)}_{1}(z)-2g^{(\mu)}_{0}(z)\ln z.

(32)

The formula for the derivative then takes the form

\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}^{(\mu)}}{\partial E}=\frac{1}{T_{U}}\biggl(G_{1}(\mu;z)+G_{2}(\mu;z)\Biggr).

(33)

By inserting formulae (
33
) and (
29
) in eq. (
28
),
we obtain the relation

\left(G_{1}(\mu;z)+G_{2}(\mu;z)\right)\mathcal{F}_{\nu}=T_{U}\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{S}.

(34)

Here the entropic force
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}=-me^{\varphi}\nabla_{\nu}\varphi

and
\varphi
is a GR generalization of Newton’s potential

\varphi=\frac{1}{2}\log(-\xi^{\nu}\xi_{\nu}).

(35)

The change of entropy through the holographic screen resulting from a
displacement of particle (bit) by one thermal wavelength
\lambda_{T}

along some normal direction
\vec{n}
to the screen determined by

n_{\nu}(\nu=0,1,2,3)
reads

\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{S}=-2\pi\frac{m}{\hbar}n_{\nu}.

(36)

In Verlinde’s emergent gravity framework, the derivation
of Einstein’s field equations is formulated in thermodynamic terms
by employing the holographic principle, the Unruh effect, and the
Bekenstein entropy bound, all of which can be naturally interpreted
within the AdS/CFT correspondence. Hence, the accelerating observer may
be regarded as intrinsically coupled to the framework of induced gravity,
since the Unruh temperature perceived by such an observer constitutes
a macroscopic manifestation of the same underlying microscopic degrees of
freedom whose collective dynamics gives rise to the emergent gravitational field.
Then (on equating the thermodynamic temperature to the Unruh temperature),
we obtain the relation that involves the
\mu
-deformed temperature
of the form:

T_{U}=(G_{1}(\mu;z)+G_{2}(\mu;z))e^{\varphi}\nabla_{\nu}\varphi\frac{\hbar}{2\pi}n_{\nu}.

(37)

With a screen located on a closed surface
\mathcal{\tilde{S}}

at a ‘‘constant redshift’’ the Komar mass can be written as

\mathcal{M}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{\tilde{S}}}T_{U}d\mathcal{N}.

(38)

The infinitesimal change of the number of bits can be written,
according to Bekenstein idea
[
31
]
, as

d\mathcal{N}=\frac{d\mathcal{A}}{G\hbar}.

(39)

By using eq. (
39
) and eq. (
37
) in eq. (
38
),
we obtain the total mass

\mathcal{M}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{\mathcal{\tilde{S}}}\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu;z)e^{\varphi}\nabla_{\nu}\varphi n_{\nu}\,d\mathcal{A}

(40)

where

\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu;z)\equiv G_{1}(\mu;z)+G_{2}(\mu;z).

(41)

Next, from the Stokes’ theorem, the Killing equation, and the relation

\nabla_{\nu}\nabla^{\nu}\xi^{\mu}=-\mathcal{R}_{\nu}^{\mu}\xi^{\nu}
,
we obtain the desired
\mu
-dependent total mass in the form

\mathcal{M}=\frac{1}{4\pi G}\int_{V}\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu;z)n^{\nu}\xi^{\mu}\,\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}\,dV.

(42)

On the other hand, there is an alternative expression for the Komar mass
in terms of energy-momentum tensor:

\mathcal{M}=2\int_{V}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{T}+\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}g_{\mu\nu}\right)n^{\nu}\xi^{\mu}\,dV.

(43)

In this framework, the
\mu
-deformed mass refers to the thermodynamic
energy of a
\mu
-Bose gas distributed across the holographic volume.
The Komar mass, by contrast, is an
\mu
-deformed emergent gravitational
quantity that inherits deformation effects from the underlying thermodynamic
system via the AdS/CFT correspondence.
Then from eqs. (
43
), (
42
), we infer

\displaystyle 2\int_{V}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{T}+\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}g_{\mu\nu}\right)n^{\nu}\xi^{\mu}\,dV=

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu;z)}{4\pi G}\int_{V}\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}n^{\nu}\xi^{\mu}\,\,dV.

(44)

From this we obtain the
\mu
-deformed Einstein equations
describing
\mu
-induced gravity acting on deformed matter fields

2\left(\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{T}+\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}g_{\mu\nu}\right)=\frac{\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu;z)}{4\pi G}\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}.

(45)

At last, taking the trace of eq. (
45
), yields the desired

\mu
-deformed Einstein equation

\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{R}+\frac{\Lambda}{\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu;z)}g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{8\pi G}{\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu;z)}\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu}.

(46)

As seen in this equation, the modified (or
effective

\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\equiv\frac{\Lambda}{\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu;z)}
)

cosmological constant
(ECC) has appeared, along with

modified gravitational constant
.

In the both deformed (
\mu>0
) and undeformed (
\mu=0
) situations,
the ECC and also the gravitational constant (GC) in the resulting

\mu
-deformed Einstein eqs. depend on the
variable

fugacity
z
of underlying inducing system.
To get rid of
z
, natural way is to fix
z
as
z=1

(or some value very close to
1
).
That implies dealing with Bose-like condensate.
Now, the crucial point is that the
\mu=0

(i.e. pure Bose gas) case, for fixed
z=1
, results in vanishing
CC and GC, what is clearly unphysical. Thus, pure Bose gas cannot serve
as underlying (or inducing) system while the (
\mu
-)deformed extension
will be of principal importance, as will be seen from the treatment below.

4
Discussion and Conclusions

In this paper we have derived the
\mu
-deformed extension of Einstein
(gravitational) field equations and studied in detail the dependence of
its term which involves the effective (i.e.
\mu
-dependent) cosmological
constant as function of the deformation parameter
\mu
and fugacity
z
.
In effect, we have shown that by varying the deformation parameter
\mu

towards its extremely small values it is possible to find the appropriate
value
\mu^{\ast}
of
\mu
that allows to
achieve the ‘‘realistic value’’ of the maximum of the function

\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu;z)\equiv G_{1}(z,\mu)+G_{2}(z,\mu)
which
(through its inverse) provides the necessary observable value

\Lambda/\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu^{\ast};z_{0})\sim 3.8\cdot 10^{-123}l_{\mathrm{P}}^{-2}
of the effective cosmological
constant
\Lambda/\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mu;z)
.
Certain important points are to be emphasized.

First of all, we stress the very fact of existence of extremum of the ECC
within the
\mu
-deformed Bose-gas model based approach to realizing
induced gravity. Clearly this is in contrast to the usage of conventional
(undeformed) Bose gas model. Indeed, within the latter there is no
extremum in the corresponding combination
\Lambda/\mathcal{G}_{V}(0;z_{0})

of usual
\mu
= 0 polylogarithms when
z\rightarrow 1

and thus no way to get the value of CC different from zero.

Second, when analyzing the behavior of ECC,
we adopted, without special justification, that the
ratio is
V/\lambda_{T}^{3}=1
. This value of the ratio is well-known marker
that indicates the existence of truely quantum effects
[
61
]

and also relates to Bose-like condensate. Let us consider
this issue in more detail, coming unexpectedly to the two distinct
possible situations (and interpretation) of the system which
underlies the induced (entropic) gravity.
These two cases of inducing system correspond to principally different scales,
namely (A) the scale (size) of dwarf galaxies and (B) the scale of observable
Universe.

(A)
In this case the ratio
V/\lambda_{T}^{3}
is of the order of
unity due to the fact that both the scale of dwarf galaxies and the thermal
wavelength scale of the dark matter particles are few kpc (see, e.d.
[
18
]
)
if the
\mu
-deformed fuzzy dark matter (DM) model is adopted, with the mass
of (Bose-like condensate) ultralight DM particles being
m=10^{-22}eV
.

(B)
In this case the ratio
V/\lambda_{T}^{3}
is of the order
of unity due to the fact that both the scale of
observable Universe and the thermal wavelength scale are of the order

\sim

2.8\cdot

10^{7}
kpc if the particles, forming
[
63
]

Bose-like condensate DM, are adopted to be ‘‘massive gravitons’’
(it is reasonable to term this
pre-gravitons
in view of their
role in generating induced gravity) with mass

m=10^{-36}
–
10^{-33}eV
, see
[
62
]
.
Note that in the context of DM of dwarf galaxies the Bose condensate of
massive gravitons was considered in
[
63
]
. In our treatment we deal with
condensate of massive pre-gravitons, i.e. viewed as the system which induces
real gravity along with effective cosmological constant. In connection with
this picture of inducing CC, it is worth to mention the work of R. Garattini
and M. Faizal in which it is shown that the appearance of cosmological constant
is strictly related with just the deformation of Generalized Uncertainty Principle
being behind the deformed Wheeler-DeWitt equation, see
[
64
]
.

The real physical nature of the parameter
\mu
of
\mu
-deformed

inducing system, like that of dark matter, is unknown.
However the significance or meaning of its use can be justified:
the deformation enables to take into account (possible) compositeness
of particles and/or their interactions – natural reasons to deal with
quantum system strongly deviating from ideal Bose gas. This reason is quite
similar to the usage of deformed Bose gas model in another context –
for (successful) modelling the properties of quantum correlations of pions
occurring in heavy-ion collisions at RHIC, see ref.
[
15
]
and also the
paragraph after eq. (
24
).
However, explicit physical content of
\mu
in the both two cases of ultralight
dark matter (as Bose-like condensate):
the galactic one and the observed Universe case, may be different since
the particles (and their constituents) do differ – ultralight ‘‘bosons’’
versus ‘‘pre-gravitons’’.

Let us stress that our approach to ‘‘explain’’ the issue of observable
value of CC grounds on two pillars:
deformation and (macroscopic) quantumness of underlying or inducing
system.
It is also worth to note the following:
the fact that the issue of CC in our treatment turns out to be related
with the concept of DM is quite alike the joint treatment of DM and
dark energy, appearing in another contexts in some papers,
see e.g.
[
65
,​
66
]
.

Long-term gastrointestinal sequelae in patients who underwent surgery for congenital duodenal obstruction
Authors: Adinda G H Pijpers, Maaike Hogerwerf, Fenne A I M Van den Bunder, Ilan J N Koppen, Ernest L W Van Heurn, Ramon R Gorter, Gijsbert D Musters, Joep P M Derikx
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCongenital duodenal obstruction (DO) is an anomaly which requires surgery shortly after birth. However, its long-term gastrointestinal (GI) sequelae remain unknown. Therefore, this study aimed to determine the long-term GI patient-reported outcome measurements (PROMs) using the Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory™ (PedsQL™) GI-Module in patients with DO compared to healthy controls. Secondly, we evaluated the PROMs comparing patients with DO with and without trisomy 21.MethodsWe performed a cross-sectional cohort study (September-December 2023). The PedsQL™ GI-Module questionnaire was sent to all patients who underwent DO surgery between 1999 and 2022. Data were compared with a published healthy control group (n = 513). Bonferroni correction was used to adjust for multiple tests.ResultsIn total, 33/89 patients completed the PedsQL™ GI-Module questionnaire. The majority of the patients were female (N = 19/33) with a mean age of 13.3 years (SD ± 6.6). Trisomy 21 was diagnosed in seven patients. The DO group had a similar mean total score compared to healthy controls (84.5 ± 11.5 vs. 88.6 ± 12.9,p = 0.075). The mean score for constipation (77.2 ± 22.0 vs. 86.9 ± 17.6,p = 0.002) was significantly lower compared to the healthy controls, indicating a worse outcome, whilst all other domains were similar. Four patients used laxatives. Patients with DO and trisomy 21 had similar scores compared to patients with DO without trisomy 21.ConclusionThe results showed similar long-term GI PROMs in patients who underwent DO surgery compared to healthy controls. Patients with DO experienced constipation more frequently, emphasizing the need for attention during follow-up and education of (parents) of patients. Additionally, children with DO and trisomy 21 showed similar PROMs to those without trisomy 21.Level of evidenceII.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00383-025-06223-3.Keywords: PedsQL, Patient-reported outcome measurements, Annular pancreas, Duodenal web trisomy 21

Introduction

Congenital duodenal obstruction (DO) can be caused by duodenal atresia, web/membrane or annular pancreas [1]. It is one of the most common bowel obstructions in neonates and occurs in 1:5,000–10,000 live births [2]. DO requires surgical repair in the first days of the neonatal period [1].

Most studies reporting the outcomes of congenital DO repair focus on short-term postoperative complications and compare open with laparoscopic surgical techniques [3–5]. Complication rates range from 10% to 35%, with common complications including wound dehiscence, bleeding, and infections [3–5]. However, long-term outcomes have not been adequately studied, and the persistence of gastrointestinal symptoms after surgery remains largely unknown.

Approximately one third of the patients with DO has trisomy 21 [6]. Several studies have compared outcomes in patients with and without trisomy 21, reporting poorer neurological outcomes and a higher incidence of cardiac anomalies in those with trisomy 21 [7, 8]. Research on gastrointestinal symptoms is limited and has not identified clear differences between patients with and without trisomy 21 [9, 10]. However, no qualitative study has yet been conducted to assess specific gastrointestinal symptoms.

Therefore, the primary aim of this study was to evaluate the long-term gastrointestinal patient-reported outcome measurements (PROMs) in patients who underwent surgical repair for DO during neonatal period compared to a healthy control group. Secondly, we aimed to evaluate whether there were differences in long-term gastrointestinal PROMs between patients with DO with or without trisomy 21.

Methods

We performed a cohort study in the Amsterdam University Medical Centers (AUMC) including all children who received surgical repair between 1999 and 2022 for congenital DO. All patients younger than two years, older than 25 years, or deceased during follow-up were excluded from the study. We sent an information letter, informed consent form, the questionnaire including an additional question on their current medication use, and a reply envelope to all (parents of) children who were eligible in September 2023. A reminder with a request to participate was sent after two months. As this study was not subject to the Medical Research Involving Human Subjects Act, approval from an ethics committee in the Netherlands was not required (METC2023.0426).

The Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory™ Gastrointestinal symptoms module (PedsQL™ GI-Module) version 3 was sent out in Dutch (supplementary A). The PedsQL™ is a widely used, validated instrument for assessing pediatric health-related quality of life across both generic and disease-specific domains. The scores of this instrument provide a meaningful comparison between patient and healthy populations. This questionnaire was therefore considered the most appropriate tool for assessing long-term patient-reported outcomes following surgery for duodenal obstruction, as it comprehensively evaluates symptom burden, functional impact, and patient well-being from both the child’s and parent’s perspectives. Depending on the age of the child, the parent proxy-report (2–4 and 5–7 years) or the parallel child self-report (8–12, 13–18 or 19–25 years) was sent. The questionnaire includes 74 questions on twelve different domains: stomach pain and hurt; stomach discomfort when eating; food and drink limits; trouble swallowing; heartburn and reflux; nausea and vomiting; gas and bloating; constipation; blood in poop; diarrhea; worry about going poop and worry about stomach aches. All items are scored on a 5-point Likert scale (0 = never a problem to 4 = almost always a problem). All answers were reverse scored and linearly transformed to a 0–100 scale (0 = 100, 1 = 75, 2 = 50, 3 = 25 and, 4 = 0) following the PedsQL™ scoring system. Lower scores indicate more severe gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms. Domain-specific total scores were calculated by summing the item scores and dividing by the total number of items in each domain. Similarly, the overall score for the PedsQL™ GI-Module was computed as the sum of all items divided by the total number of items in the questionnaire. Outcomes were compared to the published healthy control group (n = 513) of Varni et al. [11]. This cohort was selected because it provides the best available normative reference for the PedsQL™ GI-Module, which was specifically developed and validated to measure multidimensional GI symptoms and related concerns in children aged 2–18 years. Data in this study were collected by the scientific research team at YouGov®, an Internet-based polling firm which maintains a large panel of participants matched for age, gender, and race/ethnicity with the GI sample [11, 12]. These data were originally used in the validation of the PedsQL™ Gastrointestinal Symptoms and Worry Scales and have served as normative references in several National Institutes of Health-funded Patient Reported Outcomes Measurement System (PROMIS®) studies.

Statistical analysis

Normally distributed variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed variables are reported as median with interquartile range (IQR). The distribution of the data was assessed using visual inspection through histogram analysis. All analyses were conducted using SPSS software version 28.0 (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Armonk, New York, USA) and Graphpad Prism. Bonferroni correction was used to adjust for multiple comparisons. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The Chi-squared test was used to test the significance of differences between responders and non-responders. For continuous data, Student’s t-test was used for normal distributed data, while non-normally distributed variables were tested with the Mann-Whitney U test. Outcomes of the questionnaire were compared to the published healthy control group using unpaired t-test.

Results

In total, 101 (parents) of patients received an invitation to participate. Twelve letters were returned due to incorrect addresses. We received 34 responses (38.2%) of the 89 remaining eligible (parents of) patients. One patient was excluded from the analysis due to an incomplete questionnaire. Fourteen of the 33 included patients were male (42.4%) with a mean age of 13.3 years (± 6.6). This was comparable with the healthy control group (n = 513), including 46.2% males and a mean age of 11.4 years (± 4.3). Furthermore, seven patients (21.2%) had trisomy 21. The type of obstruction was duodenal atresia in thirteen patients (39.4%), followed by duodenal web/membrane in twelve patients (36.4%) and annular pancreas in eight patients (24.2%). Open duodeno-duodenostomy was performed in the majority of the cases (84.9%). Baseline characteristics are shown in Table 1.

PedsQL™ GI-Module scores for DO versus healthy controls

The mean total score of the PedsQL™ GI-Module of all children who underwent surgical repair for DO was 84.5 (± 11.5) and was similar to the score of the healthy control group: 88.6 (± 12.9, p = 0.075). Scores of the different domains were compared to the healthy control group. We found that scores for constipation were significantly lower, and thus demonstrating worse symptoms, in the DO group with a mean score of 77.2 (± 22.0) versus 86.9 (± 17.6) for the healthy control group (p = 0.002). The scores on the other domains were comparable between the DO group and healthy controls. Four children required laxatives (12.1%). An overview of the scores is shown in Table 3.

Discussion

In this study we evaluated the long-term gastrointestinal PROMs in patients who underwent DO surgery early in life compared to the healthy control group by using the PedsQL™ GI-Module. We found that the long-term gastrointestinal PROMs were similar in both groups. In addition, we found that the PROMs on the domain for constipation was significantly lower in the DO group compared to healthy controls, indicating more long-term complaints. Furthermore, we compared the PROMs of the patients with trisomy 21 to the patients without trisomy 21. Results showed that the mean symptoms total score was similar for patients with trisomy 21 compared to the patients without, indicating comparable long-term gastrointestinal sequelae in this group of patients.

Long term outcomes remain understudied among DO patients. One study has reported the total PedsQL™ questionnaire in 38 patients with DO with a median age of 6.7 years (range 2.7–17.3 years) in order to evaluate the long term outcomes [9]. They found that patients who underwent surgery for DO early in life perform similarly to another healthy published control group [13]. No significant differences were observed in the PedsQL™ GI-Module scores between the two groups. Our results also show that children with DO score similar to healthy controls on the PedsQL™ GI-Module. However, children with DO score significantly worse on the gastrointestinal domain constipation. Additionally, we report that four children require laxatives (12.1%) which is similar to nationwide use of laxatives in the Netherlands with an incidence of 100 in 1000 children [14]. This contrasts with our expectations, as DO is a high intestinal obstruction requiring surgery in the upper gastrointestinal tract. Few studies have examined long-term gastrointestinal complaints, with one reporting gastroesophageal reflux in 17% of their population [15]. Our findings, which indicate more symptoms of constipation warrant increased attention during follow-up, and can be used to educate parents about the potential consequences.

Previous studies have examined the outcomes of children with DO, comparing those with trisomy 21 to those without. These studies generally report no significant differences in short-term outcomes between the groups [16, 17]. However, patients with trisomy 21 tend to score lower in terms of overall quality of life [9]. Few studies have specifically addressed gastrointestinal symptoms in this population. A recent study, for instance, indicates that children with trisomy 21 are more likely to experience upper gastrointestinal complaints, such as esophageal and gastric issues [10]. Another study suggests that children with trisomy 21, cardiac anomalies, and DO are at higher risk for eventual gastrostomy placement than children with isolated DO [18]. The results of these studies indicate worse long-term gastrointestinal outcomes in patients with DO and trisomy 21 compared to those without trisomy 21. However, these findings are not supported by Vinycomb et al., who found similar scores on the PedsQL™ GI-Module for patients with and without trisomy 21, including seven patients with trisomy 21 84.2 (± 46.6) and 31 patients without trisomy 21 88.3 (± 15.7) [9]. This is consistent with the findings of our study, which shows that children with trisomy 21 score similar to those without, suggesting that they do not fare worse in terms of gastrointestinal outcomes. A possible explanation for these results is that questionnaires for children with trisomy 21 are often filled in by parents, especially in older children due to the intellectual disabilities associated with trisomy 21 [19]. This may lead to underreporting of symptoms. Moreover, the subgroup of patients with trisomy 21 and DO included only seven patients. Therefore, the results of this comparison should be interpreted with caution.

The follow-up for patients surgically treated for congenital DO in our hospital is only short term. A routine appointment is made at the surgical outpatient clinic for postoperative wound assessment and evaluation of feeding tolerance. Further surgical follow-up is not indicated. Based on current knowledge, no specific information was available regarding recommendations on the long-term postoperative follow-up for patients with DO. With the results of our study advice regarding prevention and management of constipation can be provided. Additional routine follow-up is not needed.

Strengths and limitations

A key strength is that we examined all domains of the PedsQL™ GI-Module in patients who underwent surgical treatment for congenital DO. To examine this we used PROMs to report the outcomes of the (parents of) patients. Additionally, the study offers a 25-year follow-up period, providing patients across a wide age range. A validated and widely-used questionnaire was used, and the results were compared with a large cohort of healthy controls. In order to minimize bias in the group that completed the questionnaires, we conducted a comparison between responders and non-responders. This analysis revealed no significant differences between the groups, except for age. However, the study’s small sample size is a limitation, preventing sub analyses based on age, specific types of DO or type of surgical repair, and potentially allowing the mean scores to be influenced by a small subset of patients. In particular, reporting bias may have affected the responses from patients with trisomy 21. Moreover, the imbalance between the small cohort of this study and the large healthy control group (less than 1:10) limits the statistical power of the analysis. Also the exclusive use of a “healthy” control group could have introduced selection bias. For this study a comparison with a more representative sample of the general pediatric population, including patients with trisomy 21 could be more meaningful. Finally, the cross-sectional design captures symptoms at one moment in time, while symptoms could have varied over time, making it difficult to establish causal relationships between DO and current gastrointestinal symptoms. A longitudinal approach would better characterize the evolution of symptoms over time.

Conclusion

The results of this study show that the PROMs using the PedsQL™ GI-Module were similar in patients who underwent DO surgery early in life compared to the healthy control group, which means similar long-term gastrointestinal sequelae. The PROMs in the domain of constipation was significantly lower in the group with DO patients, emphasizing the need for attention during follow-up and education of the (parents of) patients. In addition, we found that patients with DO and trisomy 21 had similar gastrointestinal symptoms compared to patients with DO without trisomy 21, suggesting that there is no need for extra care in follow-up. The evidence of this study can be used for parental counseling and designing a long-term follow-up program.

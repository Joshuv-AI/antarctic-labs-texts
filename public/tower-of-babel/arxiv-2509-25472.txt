Exponential Hedging for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Process in the Presence of Linear Price Impact Thanks: Partially supported by the ISF grant 305/25.
Yan Dolinksy

Abstract
. In this work we study a continuous time exponential utility maximization problem in the presence of a linear temporary price impact. More precisely, for the case where the risky asset is given by the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusion process we compute the optimal portfolio strategy and the corresponding value. Our method of solution relies on duality, and it is purely probabilistic.



1.
Introduction and the Main Result

We fix a time horizon
T<\infty
and consider a financial market which consists of a safe asset earning zero interest rate and of a risky asset
S_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
that follows
the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusion process

(1.1)

dS_{t}=\left(\mu-S_{t}\right)dt+dW_{t},

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is the long-term mean and

W_{t}
,
t\geq 0
is a
standard, one-dimensional Brownian motion defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

and endowed with the augmented natural filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}
,
t\geq 0
. As usual, we assume that the initial stock price is a given constant
S_{0}
.
Without loss of generality we assume that
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{T}
.

Our motivation to study optimal investments with a mean reverting risky asset comes from the recent paper
[
9
]

where
the authors considered asymptotically (as the time horizon goes to infinity) optimal investments
for mean reverting risky assets.
Although the authors considered a more general type of mean reverting models then the linear
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusion, they assumed that the trading is frictionless.

In financial markets, trading moves prices against the trader:
buying faster increases execution prices, and selling faster decreases them.
This aspect of liquidity, known as market depth

[
3
]
or price-impact has recently received increasing attention
(see, for instance,

[
2
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
11
,
12
]
and the references therein).

Following

[
1
]
, we model this impact in a temporary linear
form and, thus, when at time
t
the investor turns over her position
\Phi_{t}
at
the rate
\phi_{t}=\dot{\Phi}_{t}
the execution price is
S_{t}+\frac{\phi_{t}}{2\delta}

for some constant
\delta>0
which denotes the market depth.
As a result, the profits and
losses from trading are given by

V^{\Phi_{0},\phi}_{T}:=\Phi_{0}(S_{T}-S_{0})+\int_{0}^{T}\phi_{t}(S_{T}-S_{t})dt-\frac{1}{2\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\phi^{2}_{t}dt,

where, for convenience, we assume that the investor marks to market her position

\Phi_{T}=\Phi_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\phi_{t}dt
in the risky asset that she has acquired by time

T>0
.

The natural class of admissible strategies
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\left\{\phi=(\phi_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}:\ \phi\text{ is }\ \mathcal{F}_{t}\text{-optional with }\int_{0}^{T}\phi^{2}_{t}dt<\infty\ \text{ a.s.}\right\}.

The investor’s preferences are described by an exponential utility function

u(x)=-\exp(-\alpha x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
with absolute risk aversion parameter
\alpha>0
, and her goal is to

(1.2)

\displaystyle\text{Maximize }\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[u(V^{\Phi_{0},\phi}_{T})\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[-\exp\left(-\alpha V^{\Phi_{0},\phi}_{T}\right)\right]\text{ over
}{\phi\in\mathcal{A}},

where
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}
denotes the expectation with respect to the underlying probability measure
\mathbb{P}
.
Without loss of generality, we take the risk aversion
\alpha=1
.

Remark 1.1
.

Let us notice, that by considering
transformations of the form
S_{t}\rightarrow\alpha S_{\beta t}
(for some constants
\alpha,\beta>0
),
modifying the time horizon
T
and the market depth
\delta
, there is no loss of generality assuming that
the mean reversion rate and the volatility of the
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusion process are normalized to
1
. This is done for economy of notations.

The paper’s main result is the following solution to the optimization
problem (
1.2
). For simplicity we assume that the initial number of shares is
\Phi_{0}=0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Set,

V(t)=\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}\left(\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}t\right)+\sqrt{1+\delta}\left(1+\delta+\delta t\right)\cosh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}\,t\right)\right)}{\sqrt{1+\delta}\left(1+\delta+\delta t\right)\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}\,t\right)+\left(1+\delta^{2}\right)\cosh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}\,t\right)+2{\delta}}-1,\ \ t\geq 0.

The utility maximization problem (
1.2
) has a unique optimal
hedging strategy
\hat{\phi}_{t}
,
t\in[0,T)
which is given by
the feedback form

(1.3)

\hat{\phi}_{t}=\delta\frac{\left(1+\frac{1-\delta}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-t)}{2}\right)+\frac{\delta(T-t)}{1+\delta}\right)(\mu-S_{t})-\hat{\Phi}_{t}}{1+\frac{\delta(T-t)}{1+\delta}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}}{\sinh(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-t))}+\frac{1+\delta^{2}}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-t)}{2}\right)}

where
\hat{\Phi}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\hat{\phi}_{s}ds
,
t\in[0,T].

The corresponding value is equal to

(1.4)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[-\exp\left(-V^{0,\hat{\phi}}_{T}\right)\right]=-\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}V(T)(\mu-S_{0})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}V(t)dt\right).

We end this section with collecting some financial-economics observations of this result.
First, direct calculations yield that the derivative of
V
is equal to

\displaystyle\dot{V}(t)=\frac{\delta(1+\delta)\left(A(t)+B(t)+C(t)\right)}{\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}\left(1+\delta+\delta t\right)\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}\,t\right)+\left(1+\delta^{2}\right)\cosh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}\,t\right)+2{\delta}\right)^{2}}

where

\displaystyle A(t):=2(1+\delta+\delta t)\sqrt{1+\delta}\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}t\right)-2(1+\delta)^{2}t

\displaystyle B(t):=2(1+\delta)\cosh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}t\right)-2(1+\delta)-(\delta+\delta^{2})t^{2}

\displaystyle C(t):=\delta^{2}\left(\cosh^{2}\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}t\right)-t^{2}\right).

From the simple inequality
\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}t\right)\geq\sqrt{1+\delta}t
we get
A\geq 0
.
The inequality
\cosh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}t\right)\geq 1+\frac{1+\delta}{2}t^{2}
gives
B\geq 0
. The inequality
C>0
is obvious. We conclude that
\dot{V}(t)>0
for all
t
.
Clearly,
V(0)=0
, and so
V(t)>0
for
t>0
.
Hence,
from (
1.4
) the optimal expected utility is increasing in the following two parameters:
I. Time Horizon
T
. II. The term
|\theta-S_{0}|
, namely the distance between the initial stock price and the long-term mean of the
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusion process.

Next, observe that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}V(t)=\sqrt{1+\delta}-1
.
Thus, the certainty equivalent

c(T):=\log\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\left[\exp\left(-V^{0,\hat{\phi}}_{T}\right)\right]\right)
(the optimal strategy
\hat{\phi}
depends on the time horizon
T
)
satisfies
\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{c(T)}{T}=1-\sqrt{\delta+1}.

Let us notice that for the frictionless case (see Section 3 in
[
9
]
) the certainty equivalent is of order
T^{2}
(for large
T
).

The feedback
description (
1.3
) says that the optimal trading strategy
is a mean reverting strategy towards the process

X_{t}:=\left(1+\frac{1-\delta}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-t)}{2}\right)+\frac{\delta(T-t)}{1+\delta}\right)(\mu-S_{t}),\ \ t\in[0,T].

This process depends linearly on the difference between the long-term mean
\mu
and the current stock price
S_{t}
.
If the current time
t
is close to the maturity date
T
, the value of
X_{t}
is close
\mu-S_{t}
.
If
T-t
is large then
X_{t}
is of order
\frac{\delta(T-t)}{1+\delta}(\mu-S_{t})
.
The denominator in (
1.3
) describes
how fast (as a function of time) the investor should trade towards the process
X
. Notice that the denominator is large if the time left until the maturity date
T-t
is large or small.
Namely, in both of these cases the investor should trade slowly towards the process
X
.

For the case where the market depth goes to infinity, i.e.
\delta\rightarrow\infty
, the process
X

converges (almost surely) to
(1+T-t)(\mu-S_{t})
,
t\in[0,T]
.
Observe that the denominator in (
1.3
) is of order
\sqrt{\delta}
(for large
\delta
) and so, for the case where
the friction vanishes the optimal number of shares
\hat{\Phi}_{t}

converges to
(1+T-t)(\mu-S_{t})
which is exactly the optimal strategy for
the frictionless utility maximization problem (see Section 3 in
[
9
]
).

Proof of Theorem

From Theorem 2 in
[
10
]
and (
1.1
) it follows that there exists a probability measure

\hat{\mathbb{P}}\sim\mathbb{P}
such that

is a Brownian motion with respect to
\hat{\mathbb{P}}
. Moreover,
the corresponding Radon-Nikodym derivative is given by

\frac{d\mathbb{P}}{d\hat{\mathbb{P}}}=e^{X}

where

(2.1)

X:=\frac{T}{2}+\left(\mu-S_{0}\right)B_{T}-\frac{B^{2}_{T}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mu-S_{0}-B_{t}\right)^{2}dt.

Clearly,
for any probability measure

\mathbb{Q}\sim\mathbb{P}
we have

\log\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d{\mathbb{P}}}\right)=\log\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\hat{\mathbb{P}}}\right)-X
.
Thus, from proposition A.2 in
[
5
]
we obtain the following duality

\displaystyle\max_{\phi\in\mathcal{A}}\left\{-\log\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-V^{\Phi_{0},\phi}_{T}\right)\right]\right)\right\}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\Phi_{0}B_{T}-X+\log\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\hat{\mathbb{P}}}\right)+\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[B_{T}-B_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\right)^{2}dt\right]

where
\mathcal{Q}
is the set of all probability
measures
\mathbb{Q}\sim\hat{\mathbb{P}}
with finite relative entropy

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\log\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\hat{\mathbb{P}}}\right]<\infty

Said proposition also yields that there exists a unique
solution

to the dual problem, and it allows us to construct the unique

(2.3)

\hat{\phi}_{t}=\delta\mathbb{E}_{\hat{\mathbb{Q}}}[B_{T}-B_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}],\quad t\in[0,T].

We arrive at the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

The dual infimum in (
2

all
\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}

\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\hat{\mathbb{P}}}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}dW_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\eta_{t}^{2}dt\right)

for some bounded and adapted
\eta

the induced value for

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\Phi_{0}B_{T}-X+\log\left(\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\hat{\mathbb{P}}}\right)+\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[B_{T}-B_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\right)^{2}dt\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{T}{2}+\left(\Phi_{0}-(\mu-S_{0})\right)\int_{0}^{T}a_{t}dt+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}a_{t}dt\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}a^{2}_{t}dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}a_{s}ds-(\mu-S_{0})\right)^{2}dt+\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{t}^{T}a_{s}ds\right)^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\int_{s}^{T}l_{t,s}dt\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\left(1+\int_{s}^{t}l_{u,s}du\right)^{2}dt\right.

and

\mathbb{Q}

,

\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\int_{s}^{T}\left(1+\int_{s}^{t}l_{u,s}du\right)^{2}dt\right]ds.

3.
Auxiliary Results

We start with the following simple result. Since we could not find a direct reference we provide the full details.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
. For any
s\in[0,T)
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
let
\Gamma^{s}_{x,y}
be the space
of all continuous functions
g:[s,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

which are differentiable almost everywhere (with respect to the Lebesgue measure)
and satisfy
g(s)=x
and
g(T)=y
.
Then,

\displaystyle\min_{g\in\Gamma^{s}_{x,y}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\dot{g}_{t}^{2}dt+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\int_{s}^{T}g^{2}_{t}dt\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\left(\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{\sinh(\alpha(T-s))}+\tanh(\alpha(T-s)/2)(x^{2}+y^{2})\right).

Proof.

The left hand side of the above equation is equal to the optimization problem

\min_{g\in\Gamma^{s}_{x,y}}\int_{s}^{T}H(\dot{g}_{t},g_{t})dt

where
H(u,v):=\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}v^{2}
for
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
.
This optimization problem is convex and so it has a unique solution which has
to satisfy the Euler–Lagrange equation (for details see
[
8
]
)

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\dot{g}_{t}}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial g_{t}}.

Thus, the optimizer
\hat{g}
is the unique solution to the ODE

\ddot{\hat{g}}(t)=\alpha^{2}\hat{g}(t),\ \ \hat{g}(s)=x,\ \ \hat{g}(T)=y.

We conclude that

\hat{g}(t)=\frac{x\sinh(\alpha(T-t))+y\sinh(\alpha(t-s))}{\sinh(\alpha(T-s))},\ \ t\in[s,T].

Hence, simple computations yield

\displaystyle\min_{g\in\Gamma^{s}_{x,y}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\dot{g}_{t}^{2}dt+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\int_{s}^{T}g^{2}_{t}dt\right]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\dot{\hat{g}}_{t}^{2}dt+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}\int_{s}^{T}\hat{g}^{2}_{t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\sinh^{2}(\alpha(T-s))}\int_{s}^{T}\left(x^{2}\cosh(2\alpha(T-t))+y^{2}\cosh(2\alpha(t-s))\right)dt

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha^{2}xy}{2\sinh^{2}(\alpha(T-s))}\int_{s}^{T}\cosh(\alpha(2t-s-T)))dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\left(\coth(\alpha(T-s))(x^{2}+y^{2})-\frac{2xy}{\sinh(\alpha(T-s))}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{\sinh(\alpha(T-s))}+\tanh(\alpha(T-s)/2))(x^{2}+y^{2})\right).

∎

Next, we apply Lemma
3.1
in order to derive the following computations.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\min_{h\in L^{2}[s,T]}\left\{(\Phi_{0}-\theta)\int_{s}^{T}h_{t}dt+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{s}^{T}h_{t}dt\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}h^{2}_{t}dt\right.

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\left(\int_{s}^{t}h_{v}dv-\theta\right)^{2}dt+\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\left(\int_{t}^{T}h_{v}dv\right)^{2}dt\right\}.

The above optimization problem has a unique optimizer
\hat{h}
which satisfies

(3.2)

\int_{s}^{T}\hat{h}_{t}dt=\frac{\left(1+\frac{1-\delta}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)}{2}\right)+\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}\right)\theta-\Phi_{0}}{1+\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}}{\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)}+\frac{1+\delta^{2}}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)}{2}\right)}.

Moreover, for the case
\Phi_{0}=0
we have

\displaystyle\min_{h\in L^{2}[s,T]}\left\{-\theta\int_{s}^{T}h_{t}dt+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{s}^{T}h_{t}dt\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}h^{2}_{t}dt\right.

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\left(\int_{s}^{t}h_{v}dv-\theta\right)^{2}dt+\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\left(\int_{t}^{T}h_{v}dv\right)^{2}dt\right\}=\frac{1}{2}V(T-s)\theta^{2}.

Proof.

The uniqueness follows from the strict convexity of the functional.
For any
z\in\mathbb{R}
let
\Delta^{s}_{z}\subset L^{2}[s,T]
be the set of all functions
h\in L^{2}[s,T]

which satisfy
\int_{s}^{T}h_{t}dt-\theta=z
.
Let
z>0
and let
h\in\Delta^{s}_{z}
.
The function
f(t):=\int_{s}^{t}h_{u}du-\theta
,
t\in[s,T]
satisfies

\dot{f}=h
where
\dot{f}
is the weak derivative of
f
, and so,

\displaystyle(\Phi_{0}-\theta)\int_{s}^{T}h_{t}dt+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{s}^{T}h_{t}dt\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}h^{2}_{t}dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\left(\int_{s}^{t}h_{v}dv-\theta\right)^{2}dt+\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\left(\int_{t}^{T}h_{v}dv\right)^{2}dt

\displaystyle=(\Phi_{0}-\theta)(z+\theta)+\frac{1}{2}(z+\theta)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\dot{f}^{2}(t)dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}f^{2}(t)dt+\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{s}^{T}(z-f(t))^{2}dt

\displaystyle=(\Phi_{0}-\theta)(z+\theta)+\frac{1}{2}(z+\theta)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}z^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\dot{f}^{2}(t)dt+\frac{1}{2}(1+\delta)\int_{s}^{T}\left(f(t)-\frac{\delta z}{1+\delta}\right)^{2}dt.

Next, by applying Lemma
2.1
for
\alpha:=\sqrt{1+\delta}
,

g:=f-\frac{\delta z}{1+\delta}
,
and observing
the relations
g(s)=-\theta-\frac{\delta z}{1+\delta}
,
g(T)=\frac{z}{1+\delta}
,
we get

\displaystyle\min_{h\in\Delta^{s}_{z}}\left\{(\Phi_{0}-\theta)\int_{s}^{T}h_{t}dt+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{s}^{T}h_{t}dt\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}h^{2}_{t}dt\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\left(\int_{s}^{t}h_{v}dv-\theta\right)^{2}dt+\frac{\delta}{2}\int_{s}^{T}\left(\int_{t}^{T}h_{v}dv\right)^{2}dt\right\}

\displaystyle=(\Phi_{0}-\theta)(z+\theta)+\frac{1}{2}\ (z+\theta)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}z^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}}{\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)}(z+\theta)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+\delta}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)}{2}\right)\left(\left(\theta+\frac{\delta z}{1+\delta}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{z}{1+\delta}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{0}\theta+\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}\coth\left((\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)-1\right)\theta^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\Phi_{0}+\frac{\delta\theta}{\sqrt{1+\delta}}\coth\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)+\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{1+\delta}\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)}\right)z

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}}{\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)}+\frac{1+\delta^{2}}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)}{2}\right)\right)z^{2}.

Since
L^{2}[s,T]=\bigcup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\Delta^{s}_{z}
, then we are looking for a

z
which minimizes the above quadratic form.
The minimum of the above quadratic is attained at the unique point

\hat{z}:=-\frac{\Phi_{0}+\frac{\delta\theta}{\sqrt{1+\delta}}\coth\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)+\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{1+\delta}\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)}}{1+\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}}{\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)}+\frac{1+\delta^{2}}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)}{2}\right)}.

Thus, the unique solution to the optimization problem (
3.2
) is an element in
\Delta^{s}_{\hat{z}}
.
Simple calculations yield

\hat{z}+\theta=\frac{\left(1+\frac{1-\delta}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)}{2}\right)+\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}\right)\theta-\Phi_{0}}{1+\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}}{\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)}+\frac{1+\delta^{2}}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)}{2}\right)}

and
(
3.2
) follows.

Finally,
for the case where
\Phi_{0}=0
, the minimal value of the above quadratic form (in
z
) is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}\coth\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)-1\right)\theta^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{1+\delta}}\coth\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\delta}\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)}\right)^{2}\theta^{2}}{1+\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}}{\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)}+\frac{1+\delta^{2}}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\left(1+\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}\right)\sqrt{1+\delta}\coth\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)+\frac{1}{1+\delta}}{{1+\frac{\delta(T-s)}{1+\delta}+\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}}{\sinh\left(\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)\right)}+\frac{1+\delta^{2}}{(1+\delta)^{3/2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\delta}(T-s)}{2}\right)}}-1\right)\theta^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}V(T-s)\theta^{2}

where the last two equalities follow from direct computations.
∎

Robot-assisted computed tomography-guided percutaneous biopsy and cryoablation for subcentimeter pulmonary metastatic sarcoma: China's first reported case
Authors: Jianfu Li, Ran Zhong, Shen Lao, Wei Wang
Published: 2024

Robot-assisted percutaneous lung diagnosis and treatment workflow diagram.

Central Message

A 27-year-old man with pulmonary metastasis from a sarcoma was treated with robot-assisted, CT-guided percutaneous biopsy combined with cryothermal ablation for a small pulmonary nodule.

Clinical Summary

A 27-year-old man underwent left shoulder lipoma resection at the Affiliated Hospital of Jinan University during February 2023. Postoperative pathology revealed an abnormal sarcoma, later confirmed as a mesenchymal malignant tumor through immunohistochemistry and a pathology consultation at Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center. In April 2023, the patient had a wide excision of the left shoulder fat, with follow-ups every 3 months showing no abnormalities. However, a December 2023 chest computed tomography (CT) scan detected a new nodule in the left lower lobe, measuring approximately 6 mm, and a June 2024 follow-up CT indicated growth to about 7 mm, suggesting potential pulmonary metastasis. The patient then sought further treatment at our hospital (Figure 1, A).

A, The computed tomography (CT) images from outside hospital (left) and from our center (right). B, The CT images before and after puncture biopsy forceps. C, CT images of the whole cryoablation process. D, Chest radiographs were reviewed on the day after surgery to assess emergent complications (left) and subsequent pathological microscopic sections (right).

Upon admission, preoperative examinations were performed. Chest CT confirmed the lesion's location, size, and relationship with surrounding tissues. Laboratory tests showed no significant abnormalities, and pulmonary function tests indicated good respiratory capacity. The patient was deemed suitable for local anesthesia or sedation, enabling safe percutaneous biopsy and cryoablation.

On the day of surgery, the patient received premedication with intravenous flurbiprofen axetil (50 mg) and dexamethasone sodium phosphate (5 mg). The patient underwent a supine-position percutaneous biopsy under robot-assisted CT guidance in an operation room equipped with CT. Appendix E1 provides detailed instructions on the procedural steps and system operation. After acquiring images through CT scanning, the images were transmitted to the robotic arm system, where the puncture path was planned. A fully automatic biopsy needle (17G, 1.4 mm in diameter, 138 mm in length) was used for the biopsy. Postoperative pathology confirmed that the nodule in the left lower lung was a metastatic malignant mesenchymal tumor (Figure 1, D). After the biopsy, the robotic system relocalized the lesion and replanned the needle path for cryoablation. The procedure was performed in a supine position using the Co-Ablation System (Hygea Medical Technology Co, Ltd), which combines hot and cold ablation techniques. A single probe (model: Elite, specification: RBL20, diameter: 1.7 mm, length: 180 mm) was used for the ablation. The process involved 4 cycles of extreme cold (15 minutes per cycle, below −190 °C) followed by rewarming (2 minutes at 80 °C), alternating between the 2. After each cycle, a CT scan was performed to evaluate and confirm the ablation zone and effectiveness, completing the entire procedure. The total duration was approximately 2 hours. A postoperative chest CT scan immediately showed that the tumor was entirely within the effective cryoablation zone (Figure 1, C).

The surgery proceeded smoothly. Postoperatively, the patient was given oxygen therapy, antitussive and analgesic treatments, and a bedside chest radiogram on the same day showed no significant abnormalities (Figure 1, D). The patient was discharged 2 days later with no immediate complications.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University. The institutional review board approval number was ES-2024-129-02 (August 21, 2024). Written informed consent was obtained from the patient of this case report.

Discussion

Sarcoma is a relatively rare and heterogeneous mesenchymal tumor, comprising <1% of all adult cancer diagnoses. More than half of sarcoma patients develop distant metastases, commonly in the lungs, as the disease progresses. Pulmonary metastasis is the leading cause of death in these patients, making the control of these lesions critical for prolonging survival. With advancements in ablation technologies, which offer minimal invasiveness and better preservation of lung function, ablation has increasingly become a preferred alternative to conventional surgery.1

This case report presents the first domestic use of robot-assisted, CT-guided percutaneous biopsy combined with cryoablation to treat a pulmonary metastatic sarcoma <1 cm in diameter. The robot-assisted positioning technology showed significant advantages in handling small lesions, thanks to its high-precision positioning algorithms and stable robotic arm. By accurately calculating the optimal puncture path, it avoided critical blood vessels and organs, improving the biopsy success rate and ensuring that the needle accurately reached the target lesion. This approach not only increased the likelihood of obtaining high-quality tissue samples for better pathological diagnosis but also reduced the number of needle insertions, minimizing complications and the risk of metastasis.2

Given the high suspicion of sarcomatous lung metastasis, aggressive local control is crucial. Considering the patient's young age and high likelihood of metastatic sarcoma, aggressive ablation therapy is an effective option for maximizing survival. For the cryoablation treatment, we opted for 4 cycles, each lasting 15 minutes, exceeding the typical duration of 10 minutes for lung tissue.3

This case shows that robotic assistance improves precision in biopsy and ablation, providing clear advantages for treating small pulmonary nodules. Although cryoablation takes longer, its accuracy and ability to avoid critical structures make it an effective option for small metastatic lesions. Robot-assisted, CT-guided biopsy and cryoablation offer a safe, effective approach, enhancing diagnostic accuracy and treatment outcomes, especially for challenging pulmonary metastases.

Procedure Steps and System Operation

To achieve preoperative computed tomography-guided localization of lung nodules, we utilized a commercially available robotic-assisted navigation system (TH-S1; TrueHealth Medical Technology Co Ltd). This system is approved by the National Medical Products Administration as a class III medical device.

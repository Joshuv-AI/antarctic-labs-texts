Self-Regulatory Organizations; Long-Term Stock Exchange, Inc.; 
Notice of Filing and Immediate Effectiveness of Proposed Rule Change To 
Amend the LTSE Fee Schedule To Add an Incentive Program for Market 
Makers

October 13, 2026.
    Pursuant to the provisions of Section 19(b)(1) under the Securities 
Exchange Act of 1934 (``Act''),\1\ and Rule 19b-4 thereunder,\2\ notice 
is hereby given that on September 30, 2026, Long-Term Stock Exchange, 
Inc. (``LTSE'' or the ``Exchange'') filed with the Securities and 
Exchange Commission (``Commission'') the proposed rule change as 
described in Items I, II and III below, which Items have been prepared 
by the self-regulatory organization. The Commission is publishing this 
notice to solicit comments on the proposed rule change from interested 
persons.

I. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Terms of Substance 
of the Proposed Rule Change

    The Exchange is filing with the Commission a proposed rule change 
to amend the LTSE Fee Schedule to add an incentive program for Market 
Makers.\3\ The Exchange proposes to implement the changes to the fee 
schedule pursuant to this proposal on October 1, 2026.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \3\ See LTSE Rule 1.160(ww) (defining Market Maker).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The text of the proposed rule change is available at the Exchange's 
website at https://longtermstockexchange.com/, and at the principal 
office of the Exchange.

II. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement on the Purpose of, and 
Statutory Basis for, the Proposed Rule Change

    In its filing with the Commission, the Exchange included statements

A. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Purpose of, and 
Statutory Basis for, the Proposed Rule Change

1. Purpose
    On September 29, 2026, LTSE amended its rules governing registered 
Market Makers.\4\ Under LTSE's Market Maker rules, Market Makers 
receive no benefits for making markets. To attract Market Makers, the 
Exchange is adding to its Fee Schedule the Market Maker Incentive 
Program. This program is designed to improve market quality in many 
Non-LTSE-Primary- Listed Securities \5\ that trade on the Exchange by 
incentivizing Market Makers to quote at the national best bid or offer 
(``NBBO'') \6\ in Market Maker Incentive-Eligible Securities (defined 
below).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \4\ See Securities Exchange Act Release No. 106529 (SR-LTSE-
2026-21).
    \5\ Non-LTSE-Primary Listed Securities are (i) any UTP Security 
and (ii) any Dually-Listed Securities, as provided for in LTSE Rule 
14.210, which are not LTSE-Primary-Listed Securities. See LTSE Rule 
1.160 z). ``UTP Security'' and ``LTSE-Primary-Listed Securities'' 
are defined in LTSE Rules 1.160 vv) and s), respectively. ``Dually-
Listed Securities'' are securities that have been approved for 
listing on LTSE and another national securities exchange registered 
with the Commission pursuant to Section 6(a) of the Act. See LTSE 
Rule 14.210.
    \6\ ``NBBO'' means the national best bid or best offer, as set 
forth in Rule 600(b) of Regulation NMS under the Act as set forth in 
LTSE Rule 11.410(b). See LTSE Rule 1.160 y) (defining NBBO).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Market Maker Incentive Program. Under the program, a Market 
Maker that posts displayed quotes on the Exchange at the NBBO for the 
times specified below during the Regular Market Session (as defined in 
LTSE Rule 1.160kk)) in a Market Maker Incentive-Eligible Security, as 
defined below, in which the Market Maker is registered pursuant to Rule 
11.153 will be eligible for the following monthly incentives, subject 
to the Monthly Incentive Cap (defined below):

------------------------------------------------------------------------
         Security type             NBBO obligation     Monthly incentive
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Dually-listed securities......  At least 70% @NBBO...             $1,250
UTP Securities, excluding       At least 50% and less                850
 Dually-Listed (``Non-LTSE       than 70% @NBBO.
 Listed Securities'').
                                At least 70% @NBBO...              1,000
------------------------------------------------------------------------

    A Market Maker's time at the NBBO is calculated daily and averaged 
across the month by aggregating the percentage of time during the 
Regular Market Session during which the Market Maker's Primary MPID (as 
defined in Rule 11.151(d)(12)) has at least one round lot displayed at 
the NBBO.\7\ The Exchange excludes from its calculation of whether a 
Market Maker qualifies for the Market Maker Incentive: (1) any trading 
day that the Exchange's system experiences a disruption that lasts for 
more than 60 minutes during the Regular Market Session; (2) any day 
with a scheduled early market close; and (3) the ``Russell 
Reconstitution Day'' (typically the last Friday in June).\8\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \7\ This is consistent with how LTSE calculates time at the NBBO 
in connection with its Co-Lead Incentive.
    \8\ Similarly, the Exchange also excludes these time periods 
from its Co-Lead Incentive and Liquidity Incentive Program 
calculations.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    During the first month in which a Market Maker is eligible to 
participate in the Market Maker Incentive Program, for each security in 
which the Market Maker registers, it may earn a pro-rata share of the 
monthly incentive described above (calculated on a daily basis) if the 
Market Maker commences quoting in its registered security on a day 
after the first business day of the month. All of the other provisions 
apply during the first month, including the Monthly Incentive Cap and 
the Liquidity Incentive Program Exclusion (both defined below).
    The Monthly Incentive Cap limits incentives for Non-LTSE Listed 
Securities in a billing month to no more than 20 for every one Dually-
Listed Security in which the Market Maker is registered. The Monthly 
Incentive Cap constrains the amount of monthly incentives that the 
Exchange must pay and encourages Market Makers to register in Dually-
Listed Securities in particular.
    Additionally, where a Market Maker meets the quoting requirements 
for both the Market Maker Incentive Program and the Liquidity Incentive 
Program, the Market Maker Incentive Program incentive shall apply in 
lieu of the Liquidity Incentive Program incentive. This is referred to 
as the ``Liquidity Incentive Program Exclusion.''
    ``Market Maker Incentive-Eligible Securities'' are those securities 
that qualify for the incentives set forth in the Market Maker Incentive 
Program. The Exchange will maintain a list of Market Maker Incentive-
Eligible Securities, which will be provided to Market Makers upon 
request, and that list will include all Dually-Listed Securities and 
certain Non-LTSE Listed Securities selected on the basis of their 
trading characteristics. Non-LTSE Listed Securities will be screened, 
applying several objective factors that relate to a security's trading 
characteristics, and securities that meet specific thresholds with 
respect to these factors will be included as Market Maker Incentive-
Eligible Securities. These factors are share price, average daily 
volume traded, average daily notional value traded, intra-day 
volatility, and time at the NBBO,\9\ and they are designed to identify 
securities in which increased quoting would be impactful to both LTSE 
and the market, but not unduly burdensome to Market Makers in meeting 
the quoting requirements to earn incentives under the program. LTSE 
will review the eligibility of Non-LTSE Listed Securities annually and 
update the list of Market Maker Incentive-Eligible Securities. The 
Exchange will provide Market Makers adequate advance notice of changes 
to the list (if any). The balance between the quoting thresholds and 
the incentives offered under the program are discussed below.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \9\ The Exchange has discussed with Commission staff the 
criteria and thresholds it intends to apply.

2. Statutory Basis
    The Exchange believes that its proposal is consistent with Section 
6(b) of the Act \10\ in general, and furthers the objectives of 
Sections 6(b)(4) \11\ and 6(b)(5) \12\ of the Act in particular.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \10\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b).
    \11\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b)(4).
    \12\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b)(5).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------

(1) Section 6(b)(4) Analysis
    Section 6(b)(4) of the Act requires that the rules of a national 
securities exchange provide for the equitable allocation of reasonable 
dues, fees and other charges among all of its Members and issuers and 
other persons using its facilities. With the addition of the Market 
Maker Incentive Program to its Fee Schedule, the Exchange's rules will 
continue to provide for the equitable allocation of reasonable dues, 
fees and other charges among its Members and issuers and other persons 
using its facilities.
    The amounts of the incentives are reasonable. Under the proposed 
Market Maker Incentive Program, LTSE offers Marker Makers the 
opportunity to earn monthly payments of $850, $1,000, and $1,250 per 
Market Maker Incentive-Eligible Security (depending on time spent at 
the NBBO and whether the security is a Dually-Listed Security or a Non-
LTSE Listed Security), subject to the Monthly Incentive Cap, which 
limits incentives for Non-LTSE Listed Securities in a billing month to 
no more than 20 for every Dually-Listed Security in which the Market 
Maker is registered. The Exchange believes these incentive levels are 
reasonable in the current environment where, as discussed below, 
competitive forces constrain the Exchange's fees and drive incentives. 
By way of comparison, the proposed incentives are comparable to those 
offered by NYSE American LLC.\13\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \13\ See Securities Exchange Act Release No. 102780 (April 7, 
2025), 90 FR 15489 (April 11, 2025) (SR-NYSEAMER-2025-18) (providing 
Electronic Designated Market Makers tiered monthly per-security 
credits ranging from $100 for quoting at the NBBO 25% of the time to 
$1,250 for 80% of the time).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    A Market Maker may earn a pro-rata share of monthly incentives 
calculated daily, on a security by security basis, if the Market Maker 
commences quoting in one or more of its registered securities on a day 
after the first business day of the month, but this would apply only 
during the first month in which a Market Maker is eligible to 
participate in the Market Maker Incentive Program and would be subject 
to the other program provisions, including the Monthly Incentive Cap 
and the Liquidity Incentive Program Exclusion. Therefore, Market Makers 
would be compensated in direct proportion to the time they bear active 
quoting risks and obligations. Accordingly, the Exchange believes that 
the amounts of any pro-rata incentives would be reasonable for the same 
reason that the full incentive amounts are reasonable.\14\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \14\ Additionally, this first month provision is designed to 
incentivize new market makers to quote aggressively in the Market 
Maker-Eligible Securities in which they are registered as soon as 
they are ready (rather than waiting until the first day of the month 
after they are ready), which would immediately enhance market 
quality in those securities. For this reason, the Exchange believes 
that this provision removes impediments to and perfects the 
mechanism of a free and open market and a national market system and 
protects investors and the public interest, consistent with the 
Section 6(b)(5) of the Act.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Exchange operates in a highly fragmented and competitive market 
in which market making firms choose to make markets on venues based on 
fee and incentive levels. LTSE is only one of sixteen registered 
equities exchanges, all of which have rules permitting the registration 
of market makers. The Commission and the courts have repeatedly 
expressed their preference for competition over regulatory intervention 
in determining prices, products, and services in the securities 
markets. In Regulation NMS, the Commission highlighted the importance 
of market forces in determining prices and SRO revenues and also 
recognized that current regulation of the market system ``has been 
remarkably successful in promoting market competition in its broader 
forms that are most important to investors and listed companies.'' \15\ 
Accordingly, within this landscape, the Exchange believes that the 
amounts of its proposed incentives are reasonable.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \15\ Securities Exchange Act Release No. 51808 (June 9, 2005), 
70 FR 37496, 37499, (June 29, 2005) (S7-10-04).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    With the addition of the Market Maker Incentive Program, the 
Exchange's dues, fees, and other charges continue to be equitably 
allocated. The reward a Market Maker receives under this program is 
tied to achieving specific quoting thresholds:
     a Market Maker must quote at the NBBO in a Market Maker 
Incentive-Eligible Dually-Listed Security at least 70% the time to earn 
$1,250; and
     a Market Maker must quote at the NBBO in a Market Maker 
Incentive-Eligible Non-LTSE Listed Security at least 50% the time to 
earn $850;
     a Market Maker must quote at the NBBO in a Market Maker 
Incentive-Eligible Non-LTSE Listed Security at least 70% of the time to 
earn $1,000.
    The proposed reward levels are calibrated to levels of market risk 
taken on by a Market Maker and the projected benefits to the Exchange. 
Those projected benefits include increased liquidity available at the 
NBBO in Market Maker Incentive-Eligible Securities, improved price 
discovery generally and for Market Maker Incentive-Eligible Securities 
in particular, increased order flow to the Exchange, and improved 
trading conditions for all market participants on the Exchange through 
narrower bid-ask spreads.\16\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \16\ The Commission recognizes the benefits provided by market 
makers generally. For example, the Commission attributes competition 
between liquidity providers (together with the availability of 
execution quality benchmarks and data) with providing enhanced 
execution results such as through price improvement relative to the 
national best bid and offer and the execution of orders at midpoint 
prices. See Securities Exchange Act Release No. 105655 (June 11, 
2026), 91 FR 36656, 36667, text accompanying n.152, (June 17, 2026) 
(S7-2026-20).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Additionally, the proposed larger incentive for Market Makers in 
Dually-Listed Securities appropriately corresponds to the greater 
quoting requirement of 70% at the NBBO threshold. A Market Maker 
registered in a Dually-Listed Security that fails to reach the 70% 
threshold would not receive any incentive; in contrast, a Market Maker 
registered in a Non-LTSE Listed Security that quotes at the NBBO less 
than 70% but at least 50% of the time would still be eligible for an 
incentive. The proposed larger incentive for making a market in a 
Dually-Listed Security appropriately corresponds to the risk associated 
with the single quoting threshold, set at the higher 70% level. As 
described in greater detail below, the proposed larger incentive for 
making a market in a Dually-Listed Security also reflects the 
Exchange's: (1) proposed requirement to register in at least one 
Dually-Listed Security as a condition of earning any incentive under 
the program (by operation of the Monthly Incentive Cap), coupled with 
the fact that the number of Dually-Listed Securities are limited in 
number, which offers Market Makers limited choices in those securities; 
and (2) commitment to enhance liquidity in Dually-Listed Securities.
    The proposed incentives would be subject to the Monthly Incentive 
Cap, which limits incentives for Non-LTSE Listed Securities in a 
billing month to no more than 20 for every one Dually-Listed Security 
in which the Market Maker registers. The purposes of the Monthly 
Incentive Cap are to constrain the amount of monthly incentives that 
the Exchange must pay and to incentivize quoting in Dually-Listed 
Securities. Market Makers must register in one or more Dually-Listed 
Securities to participate in the Market Maker Incentive Program. At 
this time, there are not very many Dually-Listed Securities and, as 
mentioned above, Dually-Listed Securities are automatically included as 
Market Maker

[[Page 65830]]

Incentive-Eligible Securities (i.e., they are not subject to the 
objective criteria mentioned above).\17\ The Exchange proposes to offer 
a higher incentive for quoting at the NBBO in Dually-Listed Securities 
to compensate for this lack of choice.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \17\ See supra note 9 and accompanying text.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    For these reasons, the LTSE believes that the addition of the 
Market Maker Incentive Program would result in a continued equitable 
allocation of Exchange revenues.
(2) Section 6(b)(5) Analysis
    Section 6(b)(5) of the Act requires, among other things, that the 
rules of the Exchange not be designed to permit unfair discrimination 
between customers, issuers, brokers or dealers. The Market Maker 
Incentive Program discriminates fairly among issuers, brokers, and 
dealers (and does not discriminate among customers) in ways that are 
standard among the national securities exchanges.
    The Market Maker Incentive Program discriminates fairly among 
brokers and dealers. Only Market Makers that are registered in one or 
more Dually-Listed Securities are eligible to earn the proposed monthly 
incentives.\18\ This type of discrimination is standard; national 
securities exchanges offer incentives and rebates exclusively to market 
makers. The criteria for becoming a registered Market Maker apply 
equally to all Members.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \18\ All registered Market Makers are dealers on the Exchange 
for all purposes under the Act and the rules and regulations 
thereunder. See LTSE Rule 11.150(f).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Market Maker Incentive Program also discriminates among issuers 
in two ways, and both are fair. First, the Market Maker Incentive 
Program favors Dually-Listed Securities by automatically including 
Dually-Listed Securities as Market Maker Incentive-Eligible Securities. 
Its preference of Dually-Listed Securities (over Non-LTSE Listed 
Securities) is also reflected in the amount of the proposed incentives, 
which varies according to whether the Market Maker quotes in a Dually-
Listed Security or a Non-LTSE Listed Security: for Dually-Listed 
Securities, Market Makers may earn $1,250 for quoting at the NBBO at 
least 70% of the time; for Non-LTSE Listed Securities, they may earn 
$1,000 for quoting at least 70% of the time, or $850 for quoting at 
least 50% of the time.
    Enhancing liquidity in Dually-Listed Securities is a primary 
objective of the Market Maker Incentive Program, reflecting the 
Exchange's heightened commitment to issuers that have chosen to dual-
list on LTSE. It is common for equity markets to preference market 
making in securities they list. Other national securities exchanges 
have adopted market making programs that apply exclusively to 
securities listed on the proposing exchange,\19\ which is far more 
discriminatory than what LTSE proposes.\20\ Additionally, while the 
amounts of the proposed incentives offered by the Exchange for quoting 
at the NBBO vary for Dually-Listed Securities and Non-LTSE Listed 
Securities, that discrimination is fair for the reasons discussed above 
(i.e., the higher incentive offered for making markets in Dually-Listed 
Securities reflects Market Makers' lack of choice in selecting Dually-
Listed Securities and the increased risk associated with the singular 
eligibility threshold).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \19\ See, e.g., Cboe BZX Rule 11.8(e) (Lead Market Maker 
Program), NYSE Rule 103B(I) (governing allocations to qualified DMM 
units).
    \20\ While the Market Maker Incentive Program preferences NBBO 
quoting in Dually-Listed Securities, it expressly allows Market 
Makers that are registered in one or more Dually-Listed Security to 
earn incentives by satisfying quoting thresholds in securities that 
are not listed on LTSE.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Second, the Market Maker Incentive Program incentivizes market 
making in only Market Maker Incentive-Eligible Securities, and thus 
discriminates among issuers of Non-LTSE Listed Securities. The 
Commission has consistently permitted exchanges to adopt differentiated 
pricing (and rebates) for distinct categories of securities, provided 
that the distinctions are based on reasonable, non-arbitrary criteria 
and further the purposes of the Act. As discussed above, LTSE will 
include certain Non-LTSE Listed Securities as Market Maker Incentive-
Eligible Securities by applying several objective factors that relate 
to a security's trading characteristics and designating the securities 
that meet certain thresholds with respect to these factors to be Market 
Maker Incentive-Eligible Securities.\21\ The Exchange has permitted 
similar types of discrimination before,\22\ and the discrimination in 
this proposal does not raise any novel regulatory issues under the Act.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \21\ See supra, text accompanying note 9.
    \22\ See Securities Exchange Act Release Nos. 103517 (July 22, 
2025), 90 FR 35325 (July 25, 2025) (SR-LTSE-2025-16) (allowing 
selection of LIP Enhanced Securities); 104221 (November 19, 2025), 
90 FR 53037 (November 24, 2025) (SR-IEX-2025-27) (allowing selection 
of SMQ Level 2 Securities).

B. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement on Burden on Competition

    The Exchange believes that the proposed change will not impose any 
burden on competition not necessary or appropriate in furtherance of 
the purposes of the Act, consistent with Section 6(b)(8) of the 
Act.\23\ To the contrary, the proposal is a competitive response to 
incentives offered by other exchanges.\24\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \23\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b)(8).
    \24\ See, e.g., Securities Exchange Act Release No. 102780, 
supra note 13.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Exchange operates in a highly competitive market in which 
market participants can readily choose to send their orders to other 
exchanges and off-exchange venues. Currently, the Exchange has less 
than 1% market share of executed volume of equities trading. In such an 
environment, the Exchange must continually adjust its fees and 
incentives to remain competitive with other exchanges and with off-
exchange venues. Because competitors are free to modify their own fees 
and credits in response, and because market participants may readily 
adjust their order routing practices, the Exchange does not believe its 
proposed fee change can impose any burden on intermarket competition. 
The Exchange believes that the proposed change could promote 
competition between the Exchange and other execution venues, 
encouraging Market Makers to quote more competitively on the Exchange 
in a significant number of securities, promoting price discovery, and 
contributing to a deeper and more liquid market to the benefit of all 
market participants. In turn, this would encourage market participants 
to direct their orders to the Exchange.
    With respect to intramarket competition, the proposed rule change 
is designed to attract additional orders to the Exchange. The Exchange 
believes that the proposed rewards would incentivize market 
participants to direct their orders to the Exchange. Greater overall 
order flow, trading opportunities, and pricing transparency benefit all 
market participants on the Exchange by enhancing market quality and 
continuing to encourage Members to send orders, thereby contributing 
towards a robust and well-balanced market ecosystem. Further, the 
Exchange notes that the Market Maker Incentive Program is open on equal 
terms to all Members who register as a Market Maker pursuant to Rule 
11.150 and are registered in one or more Dually-Listed Securities. The 
proposed incentives are proportional and based on quoting at the NBBO, 
an objective measure; they are not discretionary grants.

[[Page 65831]]

    For these reasons, the Exchange believes that the proposed rule 
change will not impose any burden on competition not necessary or 
appropriate in furtherance of the purposes of the Act.

III. Date of Effectiveness of the Proposed Rule Change and Timing for 
Commission Action

    This proposed rule change establishes dues, fees or other charges 
among its members and, as such, may take effect upon filing with the 
Commission pursuant to Section 19(b)(3)(A)(ii) of the Act \25\ and 
paragraph (f)(2) of Rule 19b-4 thereunder.\26\ Accordingly, the 
proposed rule change would take effect upon filing with the Commission.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \25\ 15 U.S.C. 78s(b)(3)(A)(ii).
    \26\ 17 CFR 240.19b-4(f)(2).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    At any time within 60 days of the filing of the proposed rule 
change, the Commission summarily may temporarily suspend the rule 
change if it appears to the Commission that the action is necessary or 
appropriate in the public interest, for the protection of investors, or 
would otherwise further the purposes of the Act. If the Commission 
takes such action, the Commission shall institute proceedings to 
determine whether the proposed rule should be approved or disapproved.

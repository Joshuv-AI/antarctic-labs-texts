On graded going-down domains, II Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 13A02, 13A15, 13F05 Thanks: Key Words and Phrases: Graded integral domain, graded going-down domain, gGD \operatorname{gGD} -domain, gr-valuation domain, graded pullback
Parviz Sahandi and Nematollah Shirmohammadi

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the graded going-down property of graded integral domains in pullbacks. It then enables us to give original examples of these domains.



1.
Introduction

Let
R\subseteq T
be an extension of (commutative) integral domains and
P
be a prime ideal of
R
. The extension
R\subseteq T
is said to satisfy
going-down to
P
in case if
P\subseteq P_{1}
are prime ideals of
R
and
Q_{1}
is a prime ideal of
T
satisfying
Q_{1}\cap R=P_{1}
, then there exists a prime ideal
Q
of
T
such that
Q\subseteq Q_{1}
and
Q\cap R=P

[
9
,
16
]
. The extension
R\subseteq T
is said to satisfy
going-down
(for short,
\operatorname{GD}
) if,
R\subseteq T
satisfies going-down to
P
for every prime ideal
P
of
R

[
15
, Page 28]
. The integral domain
R
is said to be a
going-down domain
(for short, a
\operatorname{GD}
domain) if, for every overring
T
of
R
, the extension
R\subseteq T
satisfies
\operatorname{GD}

[
8
,
11
]
. It is known that every Prüfer domain and every Noetherian integral domain of (Krull) dimension less than or equal to 1 are
\operatorname{GD}
domains
[
7
, Corollary 4 and Proposition 7]
. In
[
10
]
Dobbs examined the
\operatorname{GD}
domain property in pullback of integral domains. Because of its ability to construct examples and counterexamples, pullback construction is very important in Multiplicative Ideal Theory.

Recently the graded version of
\operatorname{GD}
domains was introduced and investigated in
[
18
]
. Let
R\subseteq T
be an extension of
\Gamma
-graded domains for a (nonzero) torsionless commutative cancellative monoid
\Gamma
and
P
be a homogeneous prime ideal of
R
. The extension
R\subseteq T
is said to satisfy
graded going-down to
P
in case if
P\subseteq P_{1}
are homogeneous prime ideals of
R
and
Q_{1}
is a homogeneous prime ideal of
T
satisfying
Q_{1}\cap R=P_{1}
, then there exists a homogeneous prime ideal
Q
of
T
such that
Q\subseteq Q_{1}
and
Q\cap R=P
. We say that
R\subseteq T
satisfies
graded going-down
(for short,
\operatorname{gGD}
) if,
R\subseteq T
satisfies graded going-down to
P
for every homogeneous prime ideal
P
of
R
. The graded domain
R
is said to be a
graded going-down domain
(for short, a
\operatorname{gGD}
domain) if, for every homogeneous overring
T
of
R
, the extension
R\subseteq T
satisfies
\operatorname{gGD}
. It is shown that one may restrict the test overrings
T
to simple homogeneous overrings or to gr-valuation overrings of
R

[
18
, Theorem 3.12]
. It is also proved that
\operatorname{gGD}
property is stable under the formation of rings of fractions and factor domains (see
[
18
, Proposition 4.1 and Corollary 4.8]
).

The present paper is a sequel to
[
18
]
. The purpose here is to study the behaviour of the concept of
\operatorname{gGD}
domains in pullbacks of graded domains and to give some new examples of
\operatorname{gGD}
domains. To this, in Section 2, we recall the definition and properties of graded pullbacks and prove some preliminary results. In Section 3, we prove our main result of the paper:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}T_{\alpha}
be a
\Gamma
-graded integral domain,
M=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}M_{\alpha}
be a maximal homogeneous ideal of
T
,
k=T/M
(so
k=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}T_{\alpha}/M_{\alpha}
is a
\Gamma
-graded integral domain),
D=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}D_{\alpha}
be a graded subring of
k
,
\varphi:T\to k
be the canonical homomorphism, and
R=\varphi^{-1}(D)
. Then
R
is a
\operatorname{gGD}
domain if and only if both
T
and
D
are
\operatorname{gGD}
domains.

In Section 4, we give some examples of
\operatorname{gGD}
domains issued from pullbacks.

1.1.
Graded integral domains

Let
\Gamma
be a (nonzero) torsionless commutative cancellative monoid (written
additively) and
\langle\Gamma\rangle=\{a-b\mid a,b\in\Gamma\}
be the
quotient group of
\Gamma
; so
\langle\Gamma\rangle
is a torsionfree abelian group.
It is well known that a cancellative monoid
\Gamma
is torsionless
if and only if it can be given a total order compatible with the monoid
operation
[
17
, page 123]
. By a
(\Gamma
-)graded integral domain
R=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}R_{\alpha}
,
we mean an integral domain graded by
\Gamma
.
That is, each nonzero
x\in R_{\alpha}
has degree
\alpha
, i.e., deg
(x)=\alpha
, and
deg
(0)=0
. Thus, each nonzero
f\in R
can be written uniquely as
f=x_{\alpha_{1}}+\dots+x_{\alpha_{n}}
with
deg
(x_{\alpha_{i}})=\alpha_{i}
and
\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{n}
.
A nonzero
x\in R_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha\in\Gamma
is said
to be
homogeneous
.

Let
H=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Gamma}(R_{\alpha}\setminus\{0\})
; so

H
is the saturated multiplicative set of nonzero homogeneous elements of
R
.
Then
R_{H}
, called the
homogeneous quotient field
of
R
,
is a
\langle\Gamma\rangle
-graded integral domain whose nonzero homogeneous elements are units. The set of nonzero homogeneous elements of a graded domain
A=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}A_{\alpha}
is denoted by
H(A)
. Hence
H=H(R)
.
We say that an overring
T
of
R
is a
homogeneous overring
of
R
if

T=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\langle\Gamma\rangle}(T\cap(R_{H})_{\alpha})
;
so
T
is a
\langle\Gamma\rangle
-graded integral domain
such that
R\subseteq T\subseteq R_{H}
. We say that
R=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}R_{\alpha}
is a
graded-valuation domain
(gr-valuation domain) if either
aR\subseteq bR
or
bR\subseteq aR
for all
a,b\in H
. The ring of fractions
R_{S}
is a homogeneous overring of
R
for
a multiplicative set
S
of nonzero homogeneous elements of
R

(with deg
(\frac{a}{b})=
deg
(a)-
deg
(b)
for
a\in H
and
b\in S
).

The
\Gamma
-graded domain
R=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}R_{\alpha}
is called a
graded subring
of a
\Lambda
-graded domain
T=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda}T_{\alpha}
, if
\Gamma
is a subsemigroup of
\Lambda
and for every
\alpha\in\Gamma
,
R_{\alpha}\subseteq T_{\alpha}
. It is easy to see that we can consider
R
as a
\Lambda
-graded ring by setting
R_{\alpha}=0
for each
\alpha\in\Lambda\setminus\Gamma
, and in this case
R=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda}(T_{\alpha}\cap R)
. By “
R\subseteq T
is an extension of graded domains” we mean that
R
is a graded subring of
T
.

For a fractional ideal
A
of
R
with
A\subseteq R_{H}
,
let
A^{*}
be the fractional ideal of
R
generated by homogeneous elements in
A
; so

A^{*}\subseteq A
. The fractional ideal
A
is said to be
homogeneous
if
A^{*}=A
. A homogeneous ideal
P
of
R
is prime if and only if for all
a,b\in H
,
ab\in P
implies
a\in P
or
b\in P

[
17
, Page 124]
. The set of all homogeneous prime ideals of
R
is denoted by
\operatorname{Spec}_{h}(R)
. We note that a minimal prime ideal
P
of a homogeneous ideal
I
of
R
is homogeneous. Indeed, the inclusions
I\subseteq P^{*}\subseteq P
shows that
P^{*}=P
.

A homogeneous ideal of
R
is called a
maximal homogeneous ideal

if it is maximal among proper homogeneous ideals of
R
.
It is easy to see that each proper homogeneous ideal of
R

is contained in a maximal homogeneous ideal of
R
. The set of all maximal homogeneous ideals of
R
is denoted by
\operatorname{Max}_{h}(R)
.

Throughout this paper, let
\Gamma
be a nonzero torsionless commutative cancellative monoid,
R=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}R_{\alpha}
be an integral domain graded by
\Gamma
and
H
be the set of nonzero homogeneous elements of
R
.

2.
Preliminaries of pullback of graded domains

Let
T=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}T_{\alpha}
be a
\Gamma
-graded integral domain,
M=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}M_{\alpha}
be a maximal homogeneous ideal of
T
,
k=T/M
(so
k=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}T_{\alpha}/M_{\alpha}
is a
\Gamma
-graded integral domain
in which each nonzero homogeneous element is invertible),
D=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}D_{\alpha}
be a graded subring of
k
,
F
be the homogeneous quotient field of
D
,
\varphi:T\to k
be the canonical homomorphism, and
R=\varphi^{-1}(D)
be the pullback of the following diagram:

\textstyle{R=\varphi^{-1}(D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{k=T/M.}

It is shown that if
R_{\alpha}=\{x\in T_{\alpha}\mid\varphi(x)\in D_{\alpha}\}
for each
\alpha\in\Gamma
, then
R=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}R_{\alpha}
is a graded domain
[
5
, Lemma 1]
. We refer to this diagram as a pullback of type
\bigtriangleup
. The following proposition collects the main properties of the pullbacks of type
\bigtriangleup
.

Proposition 2.1
.

(see
[
6
, Proposition 2.1 and Remark 2.2]
)
In a pullback of type
\triangle
we have:

(1)

M
is the conductor of
T
to
R
; hence
M
is divisorial in
R
.

(2)

R
and
T
have the same (homogeneous) quotient fields; so
R_{H}=T_{H}
.

(3)

For each homogeneous prime ideal
P
of
R
such that

M\nsubseteq P
, there exists a unique homogeneous prime ideal
Q
of

T
such that
Q\cap R=P
. Moreover
R_{H\setminus P}=T_{H\setminus Q}
and
R_{P}=T_{Q}
.

(4)

Let
Q
be a maximal homogeneous ideal of
T
with
Q\neq M
.
Then
R_{H\setminus Q\cap R}=T_{H\setminus Q}
and
R_{Q\cap R}=T_{Q}
.

(5)

M
is a unique maximal homogeneous ideal of
T
if and only if each homogeneous ideal of
R
is
comparable to
M
.

(6)

The homogeneous ideals of
R
containing
M
are precisely the ideals
\varphi^{-1}(J)
, where
J
is
a homogeneous ideal of
D
. Moreover,
R/\varphi^{-1}(J)=D/J
.

(7)

M
is the unique prime of
T
contracting to
M
in
R
, and
(R:M)=(M:M)
.

(8)

Suppose that
P
is a homogeneous prime ideal of
R
with

P\supseteq M
. Then
T_{H\setminus P}=T_{H(T)\setminus M}
. In
particular,
T_{H\setminus M}=T_{H(T)\setminus M}
.

(9)

Assume that
k=F
and set
N=H\setminus M
. Then

D_{\varphi(N)}=k
, whence
R_{H\setminus M}=T_{H\setminus M}
and
by (8) it is equal to
T_{H(T)\setminus M}
.

The h-height of a homogeneous prime ideal
P
of a graded ring
A
(denoted by h-ht
{}_{A}(P)
) is defined to be the supremum of the lengths of chains of homogeneous prime ideals descending from
P
and the h-dimension of
A
(denoted by h-
\dim(A)
) is defined to be
\sup\{
h-
\mathrm{ht}_{A}(P)\mid P
is a homogeneous prime ideal of
A\}
. In the following results we want to find the h-
\dim(R)
in a pullback of type
\triangle
.

Remark 2.2
.

Consider a pullback diagram of type
\bigtriangleup
and assume that
T
has a unique maximal homogeneous ideal. Applying a similar argument as in
[
12
, Proposition 2.1(5)]
, one can obtain the equality

\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(R)=\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(T)+\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(D).

For the nongraded case of the following lemma see assertion (c) in proof of
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Consider a pullback diagram of type
\bigtriangleup
. Then
\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{R}(M)=\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{T}(M)
.

Proof.

First we prove the lemma in case
D=F
. Assume that
D=F
and consider the following pullback of type
\triangle
(see
[
6
, Remark 2.2(1)]
)

\textstyle{R_{H\setminus M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T_{H\setminus M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{k.}

Hence by Remark
2.2
and Proposition
2.1
(9), we have
\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{R}(M)=\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(R_{H\setminus M})=\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(T_{H\setminus M})=\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(T_{H(T)\setminus M})=\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{T}(M)
.

In the general case, split the pullback diagram into two parts as follows

\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S:=\varphi^{-1}(F)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{k.}

Then from the upper pullback and Proposition
2.1
(9), we have
\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{R}(M)=\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{S}(M)
, and from the lower one and the first paragraph we have
\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{S}(M)=\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{T}(M)
, to complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Consider a pullback diagram of type
\bigtriangleup
and assume that
k=F
. Then

\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(R)=\max\{\mathrm{h}\text{-}\mathrm{ht}_{T}(M)+\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(D),\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(T)\}.

Proof.

We have

\text{h-}\dim(R)=\sup\{\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P})|P\in\operatorname{Max}_{h}(R)\}
. Let
P\in\operatorname{Max}_{h}(R)
be such that
\text{h-}\dim(R)=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P})
. We have two cases to consider:

Case 1
: Assume that
P\not\supset M
. Then
R_{H\setminus P}=T_{H\setminus Q}
for some
Q\in\operatorname{Spec}_{h}(T)
such that
P=Q\cap R
by Proposition
2.1
(3). Hence
\text{h-}\dim(R)=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P})=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus Q})
. Now we claim that
h-
\dim(T)=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus Q})
. Suppose, contrary to our claim, that there exists
L\in\operatorname{Max}_{h}(T)
such that h-
\dim(T)=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus L})
and
\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus Q})\lneq\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus L})
. Set
P_{1}:=L\cap R
, which is a homogeneous prime ideal of
R
. Consequently

R_{P_{1}}=T_{L}
by
[
6
, Remark 2.2(5)]
and hence using Proposition
2.1
(2)
R_{H\setminus P_{1}}=R_{H}\cap R_{P_{1}}=T_{H}\cap T_{L}=T_{H\setminus L}
. Thus

\text{h-}\dim(R)=
h-
\dim(R_{H\setminus P})=
h-
\dim(T_{H\setminus Q})\lneq
h-
\dim(T_{H\setminus L})=
h-
\dim(R_{H\setminus P_{1}})
, which is a contradiction. It follows that h-
\dim(R)=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P})=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus Q})=\text{h-}\dim(T)
. On the other hand by Proposition
2.1
(6) we have
D=R/M
; so that
\mathrm{h}\text{-}\mathrm{ht}_{R}(M)+\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(D)\leq\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(R)=\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(T)
. Note that by Lemma
2.3
, we have
\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{R}(M)=\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{T}(M)
. Therefore in this case we have the equality

\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(R)=\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(T)=\max\{\mathrm{h}\text{-}\mathrm{ht}_{T}(M)+\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(D),\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(T)\}.

Case 2
: Assume that
P\supseteq M
and consider the following pullback of type
\triangle
(see
[
6
, Remark 2.2(1)]
)

\textstyle{R_{H\setminus P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D_{\varphi(H\setminus P)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T_{H\setminus P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{k.}

Hence h-
\dim(R_{H\setminus P})=
h-
\dim(D_{\varphi(H\setminus P)})+
h-
\dim(T_{H\setminus P})
by Remark
2.2
. We claim that h-
\dim(D)=
h-
\dim(D_{\varphi(H\setminus P)})
. Suppose, contrary to our claim, that there exists
L\in\operatorname{Max}_{h}(D)
such that h-
\dim(D)=
h-
\dim(D_{H(D)\setminus L})
and h-
\dim(D_{\varphi(H\setminus P)})\lneq
h-
\dim(D_{H(D)\setminus L})
. Set
P_{1}:=\varphi^{-1}(L)
, which is a homogeneous prime ideal of
R
. Consequently

\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P_{1}})=\text{h-}\dim(D_{\varphi(H\setminus P_{1})})+\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus P_{1}})

by (choosing
N:=H\setminus P_{1}
in)
[
6
, Remark 2.2(1)]
, Remark
2.2
, and noting that
\varphi(H\setminus P_{1})=\varphi(H)\setminus\varphi(P_{1})=H(D)\setminus L
. Thus keeping in mind that
T_{H\setminus P}=T_{H\setminus M}=T_{H\setminus P_{1}}
by Proposition
2.1
(8) we have

\displaystyle\text{h-}\dim(R)

\displaystyle=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P})

\displaystyle=\text{h-}\dim(D_{\varphi(H\setminus P)})+\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus P})

\displaystyle\lneq\text{h-}\dim(D_{H(D)\setminus L})+\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus P})

\displaystyle=\text{h-}\dim(D_{H(D)\setminus L})+\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus P_{1}})

\displaystyle=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P_{1}}),

which is a contradiction. Thus

\displaystyle\text{h-}\dim(R)

\displaystyle=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P})

\displaystyle=\text{h-}\dim(D)+\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus M})

\displaystyle=\text{h-}\dim(D)+\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{T}(M).

Let
Q\in\operatorname{Max}_{h}(T)
such that
\text{h-}\dim(T)=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H(T)\setminus Q})
. If
Q=M
, we have
\text{h-}\dim(T)=\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{T}(M)=\text{h-}\mathrm{ht}_{R}(M)\leq\text{h-}\dim(R)
, and if
Q\neq M
we have
R_{Q\cap R}=T_{Q}
by Proposition
2.1
(4). Thus
R_{H\setminus Q\cap R}=T_{H(T)\setminus Q}
using Proposition
2.1
(2). Hence
\text{h-}\dim(T)=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H(T)\setminus Q})=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus Q\cap R})\leq\text{h-}\dim(R)
. Therefore here also we have the equality

\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(R)=\max\{\mathrm{h}\text{-}\mathrm{ht}_{T}(M)+\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(D),\mathrm{h}\text{-}\dim(T)\}.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For a pullback diagram of type
\triangle
assume that
D=F
. Then
\text{h-}\dim(R)=\text{h-}\dim(T)
.

Proof.

We have

\text{h-}\dim(R)=\sup\{\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P})|P\in\operatorname{Max}_{h}(R)\}
. Let
P\in\operatorname{Max}_{h}(R)
such that

\text{h-}\dim(R)=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P})
.

Case 1
: Assume that
P=M
. Consider the following pullback of type
\triangle
(see
[
6
, Remark 2.2(1)]
)

\textstyle{R_{H\setminus M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T_{H\setminus M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{k.}

Then we have
\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus M})=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus M})
using Remark
3.4
below. We claim that
\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus M})=\text{h-}\dim(T)
. Assume to the contrary that there exists a maximal homogeneous ideal
L
of
T
such that
\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus M})\lneq\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus L})=\text{h-}\dim(T)
. Hence by Proposition
2.1
(4) we have
R_{H\setminus L\cap R}=T_{H\setminus L}
. So that

\text{h-}\dim(R)=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus M})\lneq\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus L})=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus L\cap R}),

which is a contradiction. Therefore in this case we have
\text{h-}\dim(R)=\text{h-}\dim(T)
.

Case 2
: Assume that
P\neq M
. Then
R_{H\setminus P}=T_{H\setminus Q}
for some
Q\in\operatorname{Spec}_{h}(T)
such that
P=Q\cap R
by Proposition
2.1
(3). We claim that h-
\dim(T)=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus Q})
. Suppose, contrary to our claim, that there exists
L\in\operatorname{Max}_{h}(R)
such that h-
\dim(T)=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus L})
and

\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus Q})\lneq\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus L})
. Set
P_{1}:=L\cap R
. If
P_{1}\not\supset M
then
R_{H\setminus P_{1}}=T_{H\setminus L}
by Proposition
2.1
(3). Thus

\text{h-}\dim(R)=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P})=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus Q})\lneq\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus L})=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P_{1}}),

which is again a contradiction. Therefore

\text{h-}\dim(R)=\text{h-}\dim(R_{H\setminus P})=\text{h-}\dim(T_{H\setminus Q})=\text{h-}\dim(T).

∎

Splitting the pullback diagram of type
\triangle
into two parts (as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
), then using Lemmas
2.3
,
2.4

3.
When is a pullback a graded going-down domain?

The pullback constructions are one of important tools to construct examples and counterexamples. In
[
10
, Theorem 2.4]
, D. E. Dobbs introduced a portable property about integral domains and provided the behaviour of his property under pullbacks. The
\operatorname{GD}
property is a special case of portable property. More precisely, he first did it for the CPI-extension (complete pre-image extension of Boisen and Sheldon
[
2
]
). This section is devoted to investigate the
\operatorname{gGD}
property for graded integral domains under graded pullbacks.

As Dobbs, we should consider the graded analogue of CPI-extension being
\operatorname{gGD}
domain. We recall the graded analogue of CPI-extension as follows. For a graded domain
R=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}R_{\alpha}
and a homogeneous prime ideal
P
of
R
, it is the following pullback of type
\triangle
(see
[
6
, Page 629]
):

\textstyle{R(P):=\varphi^{-1}(R/P)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R/P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R_{H\setminus P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{k=\frac{R_{H\setminus P}}{PR_{H\setminus P}}.}

It can be seen that
R(P)=R+PR_{H\setminus P}
is a graded
integral domain with
R(P)_{\alpha}=\{x\in R_{H\setminus P}\mid\varphi(x)\in(R/P)_{\alpha}\}
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma
.

To investigate the
\operatorname{gGD}
property for the graded CPI-extension, we need the graded versions of
[
9
, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.2]
. Recall from
[
18
]
that a homogeneous prime ideal

of

is called a

if each element of

divides each element of
P
. It can be easily seen that

is a homogeneous divided prime ideal if and only if
P
is comparable to each homogeneous ideal of
R
if and only if

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}R_{\alpha}
be a graded domain and

be a homogeneous divided prime ideal of

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that there exist homogeneous prime ideals

of

and
Q_{1}
of

satisfying

, and no

[
4

\Sigma p_{i}v_{i}=rz

For the proof of the following proposition we use

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
R=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}R_{\alpha}
be a graded domain. The following are equivalent:

(1)

are
\operatorname{gGD}

(2)

is a
\operatorname{gGD}

Proof.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1) It is trivial by taking

.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) By Theorem
[
18
, Theorem 3.12]
, it is enough to show that
R\subseteq V
satisfies
\operatorname{gGD}
for each gr-valuation domain

. Assume that
P_{0}\subseteq P_{1}
are homogeneous prime ideals of
R
and
Q_{1}
is a homogeneous prime ideal of
V
such that
Q_{1}\cap R=P_{1}
. Note that
P
is comparable to
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
, since
P
is a divided homogeneous prime ideal. We next consider three cases:

Case 1:

P\subseteq P_{0}
. Since, by Lemma
3.1
,

satisfies graded going-down to

, there exists a homogeneous prime
Q
of
V
such that

and

. It is easy to see that

is a graded subring of

and that the extension

satisfies
\operatorname{gGD}

R/P
is a
\operatorname{gGD}

, there exists a homogeneous prime ideal
Q_{0}
of
V
such that
Q\subseteq Q_{0}\subseteq Q_{1}
and
Q_{0}/Q\cap R/P=P_{0}/P
. Thus
Q_{0}\cap R=P_{0}
.

Case 2:

P_{0}\subseteq P\subseteq P_{1}
. By Case 1, there exists a homogeneous prime ideal
Q
of
V
such that
Q\subseteq Q_{1}
and

. Note that in this case

is a graded subring

is a gGD domain by assumption. As

Q_{0}
of

and
Q_{0}V_{H(V)\setminus Q}\cap R_{H\setminus P}=P_{0}R_{H\setminus P}

.

Case 3:

P_{1}\subseteq P
. It is the same as Case 2, using the facts that

is a graded subring

and the extension

.

domains for each
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Max}_{h}(T)
and
N\in\operatorname{Max}_{h}(D)
if and only if both
T
and
D
are
\operatorname{gGD}
domains.
∎

4.
Examples of graded going-down domains

In this section we give examples of
\operatorname{gGD}
domains using pullback of graded domains.

Recall that a graded domain
R
is a
graded-Prüfer domain
if each finitely generated homogeneous ideal of
R
is invertible. Consider a pullback of type
\triangle
. It is known from
[
6
, Corollary 3.8]
that
R
is graded-Prüfer domain if and only if
T
and
D
are graded-Prüfer domain,
M
is a maximal
t
-ideal of
T
, and
k=F
.

Note that every graded-Prüfer domain is a
\operatorname{gGD}
domain
[
18
, Corollary 3.8]
. The following example generates a new class of
\operatorname{gGD}
domains which are not graded-Prüfer.

Example 4.1
.

Let
A=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}A_{\alpha}

be a graded-Prüfer domain,
P=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}P_{\alpha}
be a maximal homogeneous ideal of
A
,

T=A[X,X^{-1}]
be a
(\Gamma\times\mathbb{Z})
-graded integral domain with deg
(aX^{n})=(\alpha,n)
for
0\neq a\in A_{\alpha}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
M=P[X,X^{-1}]
, and
D=A/P
. Then
D
and
T
are
\operatorname{gGD}
domains,
k=(A/P)[X,X^{-1}](\neq F)
, and

R=A+P[X,X^{-1}]
, i.e.,
R=\bigoplus_{(\alpha,n)\in\Gamma\times\mathbb{Z}}R_{(\alpha,n)}
with

R_{(\alpha,n)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{\alpha},&\mbox{if $n=0$ }\\
P_{\alpha}X^{n},&\mbox{if $n\neq 0$.}\end{array}\right.

Thus
R
is a
\operatorname{gGD}
domain by Theorem
3.7
, while it is not a graded-Prüfer domain by
[
6
, Corollary 3.8]

In the following, we give a concrete example of a
\operatorname{gGD}
domain which is not graded-Prüfer.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}
be the field of rational numbers and
X
and
Y
are algebraically independent indeterminates over
\mathbb{Q}
. Consider the following pullback of
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
-graded domains of type
\triangle
:

\textstyle{\mathbb{Q}+Y\mathbb{Q}(X)[Y]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Q}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Q}(X)[Y]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\frac{\mathbb{Q}(X)[Y]}{(Y)}=\mathbb{Q}(X).}

Here
R:=\mathbb{Q}+Y\mathbb{Q}(X)[Y]
is a
\operatorname{gGD}
domain by Theorem
3.7
, but it is not a graded-Prüfer domain by
[
6
, Corollary 3.8]
.

Recall that
R
is called a
gr-Noetherian domain
if the homogeneous ideals of
R
satisfy the ascending chain condition.

Consider a pullback of type
\triangle
. It is known from
[
5
, Theorem 2]
that
R
is gr-Noetherian if and only if
T
is gr-Noetherian,
D=F
, and
k
is a free
F
-module of finite rank.

It is shown that a gr-Noetherian domain
R
is a
\operatorname{gGD}
domain if and only if h-
\dim(R)\leq 1

[
18
, Proposition 3.13]
. The following example shows the gr-Noetherian assumption is crucial.

Example 4.3
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}
be the field of rational numbers and
X
and
Y
are algebraically independent indeterminates over
\mathbb{Q}
. Consider the following pullback of
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
-graded domains of type
\triangle
:

\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}+Y\mathbb{Q}(X)[Y]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

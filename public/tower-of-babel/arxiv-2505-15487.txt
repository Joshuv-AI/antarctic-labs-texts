Strong Coupling Expansion of Gluodynamics on a Lattice under Rotation
Sheng Wang, Jun-Xia Chen, Defu Hou, Hai-Cang Ren

Abstract
The analytic strong coupling expansion of the gluodynamics under a rotation with an angular velocity ω \omega is reported. While the expansion is systematic, free from additional assumptions, the deconfinement temperature determined by the onset of the Polyakov loop expectation value decreases with the angular velocity up to ω 2 \omega^{2} , opposite to the tendency found in numerical simulations. As a by-product, a simple formula is obtained for the ω 2 \omega^{2} coefficient of the deconfinement temperature shift in terms of the latent heat and the discontinuity of the moment of inertia at the transition without rotation. This formula is independent of the strong coupling and may benefit further investigation of the subject.

A Grand Canonical Ensemble Carrying a Macroscopic Angular Momentum

For a thermal equilibrium ensemble carrying a macroscopic angular momentum component, say
J_{z}
, the grand partition function is defined by

\mathcal{Q}=\rm{Tr}\exp{\left[-\frac{1}{T}(H-\omega J_{z})\right]},

(2.1)

where
H
is the Hamiltonian,
T
is the temperature
and
\omega
is the angular velocity. For a Yang-Mills theory without dynamic quarks in the time axial gauge,
A_{0}^{c}=0
,

H=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}\vec{r}\left({\vec{\Pi}}^{c}{\vec{\Pi}}^{c}+{\vec{B}}^{c}{\vec{B}}^{c}\right),

(2.2)

where
\vec{\Pi}^{c}=-\vec{E}^{c}
is the canonical momentum density with
\vec{E}^{c}
the color electric and
\vec{B}^{c}
the color magnetic field

\vec{B}^{c}=\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{A}^{c}+\frac{1}{2}gf^{cab}\vec{A}^{a}\times\vec{A}^{b},

(2.3)

with
\vec{A}^{c}
the gauge potential,
g
the coupling constant and
a,b,c
color superscripts. The Gauss law constraint is imposed on the physical states, i.e.

\mathcal{G}|\text{ }\rangle=0,

(2.4)

where

\mathcal{G}=\vec{\nabla}\cdot\Pi^{c}+gf^{cab}\vec{A}^{a}\cdot\vec{\Pi}^{b},

(2.5)

serves the generator of a time independent gauge transformation in Hilbert space. The gauge invariant angular momentum operator reads

\vec{J}=\int d^{3}\vec{r}[\vec{r}\times(\vec{B}^{c}\times\vec{\Pi}^{c})],

(2.6)

which is equivalent to the angular momentum derived from Noether theorem

\vec{J}_{\rm{Noether}}=-\int d^{3}\vec{r}\left[\{(\vec{r}\times\vec{\nabla})A_{j}^{c}\}\Pi_{j}^{c}-\vec{A}^{c}\times\vec{\Pi}^{c}\right],

(2.7)

upon dropping a surface term and applying the Gauss law Eq.(
2.4
). The proof is presented in Appendix A.
As there are no ordering issues between the canonical coordinate
\vec{A}^{c}
and canonical momentum density
\vec{\Pi}^{c}
in the angular momentum term, it is straightforward to derive the Lagrangian for the path-integral through the Legendre transformation

]

For the thermodynamics, let us name the free energy corresponding to the partition function Eq.(

\mathcal{F}=-T\ln\mathcal{Q}.

(2.14)

The thermal average of the angular momentum reads

(2.15)

(2.16)

\omega=\frac{\partial F}{\partial{M}}.

(2.17)

(2.18)

(2.19)

N_{s}^{3}\times N_{t}
with lattice spacing
a
and
N_{s}\gg N_{t}
. Periodic boundary condition is imposed in temporal dimension with temperature given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{2N_{c}}{g^{2}}\sum_{X}[(1-r^{2}\omega^{2})(1-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\text{ReTr}\bar{U}_{xy})+(1-y^{2}\omega^{2})(1-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\text{ReTr}\bar{U}_{xz})

\displaystyle+(1-x^{2}\omega^{2})(1-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\text{ReTr}\bar{U}_{yz})+3-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\text{ReTr}(\bar{U}_{x\tau}+\bar{U}_{y\tau}+\bar{U}_{z\tau})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N_{c}}\text{ReTr}(iy\omega(\bar{V}_{xy\tau}+\bar{V}_{xz\tau})-ix\omega(\bar{V}_{yx\tau}+\bar{V}_{yz\tau})+xy\omega^{2}\bar{V}_{xzy})].

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2g^{2}}\int d^{4}x[(1-r^{2}\omega^{2})F_{xy}^{a}F_{xy}^{a}+(1-y^{2}\omega^{2})F_{xz}^{a}F_{xz}^{a}+(1-x^{2}\omega^{2})F_{yz}^{a}F_{yz}^{a}+F_{x\tau}^{a}F_{x\tau}^{a}

The lattice version of the partition function Eq.(

) reads

\mathcal{Q}=\int\prod_{x,\mu}dU_{\mu}(x)e^{-\mathcal{S}_{G}[U]}.

(3.4)

Integrating spatial link variables
U_{i}(x)
with
i=x,y,z
throughout the lattice, the temporal links left over form Polyakov loops

W(\vec{x})={\rm{tr}}\prod_{t}U_{0}(\vec{x},t),

(3.5)

with time ordering imposed on the product, and Eq.(

\mathcal{Q}=\int\prod_{\vec{x}}dW(\vec{x})e^{-\mathcal{S}_{eff}[W,T]}.

(3.6)

To explore the phase structure of the system, let’s introduce a
W

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[W,T]=T\mathcal{S}_{eff}[W,T],

(3.7)

which carries a global
Z(3)
symmetry. The Gibbs free energy defined in Eq.(
2.14
) at temperature
T
and angular velocity
\omega
corresponds to the global minimum of Eq.(

) with respect to
W(\vec{x})
under saddle point approximation. We have the necessary condition for the global minimum

\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial W(\vec{x})}\right)_{T,\omega}=0,

(3.8)

and
W=0
is always a solution. A first-order phase transition occurs at the temperature when the minimum corresponding to
W=0
becomes degenerate with the minimum of the symmetry-breaking solution,
W=\mathcal{W}\neq 0
, i.e.

\mathcal{F}[0,T_{d}]=\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{W},T_{d}].

(3.9)

For a rotation with low linear velocity on the boundary,
v=\omega R\ll 1
, the free energy to the leading order correction reads

\mathcal{F}[W,T]=F_{0}[W,T]-\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}I[W,T]=F_{0}[W,T]-\frac{v^{2}}{2R^{2}}I[W,T],

(3.10)

with
F_{0}[W,T]
represents the Helmholtz free energy and
I
is the moment of inertia. The Gibbs free energy without rotation is the same as Helmholtz free energy. The symmetry breaking solution to Eq.(
3.8
) up to
O(v^{2})
takes the form

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{0}+v^{2}\mathcal{W}_{1},

(3.11)

where
\mathcal{W}_{0}(T)
is the solution to the Eq. (
3.8
) at

, i.e.

\left(\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{W}_{0}}\right)_{T}=0,

(3.12)

and depends only on temperature but

is also coordinate dependent because of the inhomogeneity introduced by rotation. Substituting Eq.(
3.11

3.10

\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{W},T]=F_{0}[\mathcal{W}_{0},T]-\frac{v^{2}}{2R^{2}}I[\mathcal{W}_{0},T],

(3.13)

and the inhomogeneous correction
\mathcal{W}_{1}

In the absence of rotation, the condition Eq.(
3.9

F_{0}[0,T_{d}^{(0)}]=F_{0}[\mathcal{W}_{0}(T_{d}^{(0)}),T_{d}^{(0)}].

1.1
) into Eq.(

(3.15)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{W},T_{d}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{0}[\mathcal{W}_{0}(T_{d}^{(0)}+T_{d}^{(0)}Bv^{2}),T_{d}^{(0)}+T_{d}^{(0)}Bv^{2}]-\frac{v^{2}}{2R^{2}}I[\mathcal{W}_{0}(T_{d}^{(0)}+T_{d}^{(0)}Bv^{2}),T_{d}^{(0)}+T_{d}^{(0)}Bv^{2}]

(3.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{0}[\mathcal{W}_{0}(T_{d}^{(0)}),T_{d}^{(0)}]-Bv^{2}T_{d}^{(0)}S_{0}[\mathcal{W}_{0}(T_{d}^{(0)}),T_{d}^{(0)}]-\frac{v^{2}}{2R^{2}}I[\mathcal{W}_{0}(T_{d}^{(0)}),T_{d}^{(0)}],

where

is the entropy of the system without rotation and the minimization condition is employed in the expansion Eq.(
3.16
). It follows from Eq.(
3.9
) and Eq.(
3.14
) that

B=-\frac{1}{2R^{2}}\frac{I[\mathcal{W}_{0}(T_{d}^{(0)}),T_{d}^{(0)}]-I[0,T_{d}^{(0)}]}{T_{d}^{(0)}\big(S_{0}[\mathcal{W}_{0}(T_{d}^{(0)}),T_{d}^{(0)}]-S_{0}[0,T_{d}^{(0)}]\big)}=-\frac{\Delta{I}}{2R^{2}{L}}.

(3.17)

In terms of the latent heat

L=T_{d}^{(0)}\left(S_{0}[\mathcal{W}_{0}(T_{d}^{(0)}),T_{d}^{(0)}]-S_{0}[0,T_{d}^{(0)}]\right),

(3.18)

and the discontinuity of the moment of inertia at the transition

\Delta I=I[\mathcal{W}_{0}(T_{d}^{(0)}),T_{d}^{(0)}]-I[0,T_{d}^{(0)}],

(3.19)

we end up with Eq.(
1.2
). As the latent heat is always positive because of higher entropy in the deconfinement phase, a lower (higher) moment of inertia in the deconfinement phase implies increasing (decreasing) transition temperature with rotation.

Concluding remarks

In summary, we started with a grand canonical ensemble of gluon system with a macroscopic angular momentum in Hamiltonian formulation and transformed it into the Matsubara form of path integral. We found that the underlying Lagrangian besides the gauge fixing term is exactly the Lagrangian in a global rotation frame. Within the mean-field framework for the effective action of the Polyakov loop, we related the shift of the deconfinement temperature for a low angular velocity to the latent heat and the jump of the moment of inertia at the transition.

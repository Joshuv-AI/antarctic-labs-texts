On the well-posedness of two-dimensional Muskat problem with an elastic interface
Lizhe Wan, Jiaqi Yang

Abstract
. We investigate the two-dimensional Muskat problem with a nonlinear elastic interface, for both one-phase and two-phase scenarios. Following the framework developed by Nguyen [ 36 , 37 ] , we demonstrate that the problem is locally well-posed in H s H^{s} for s ≥ 2 s\geq 2 for arbitrary initial data. Furthermore, for the one-phase case and the stable two-phase case ( ρ + ≤ ρ − ) (\rho^{+}\leq\rho^{-}) , we establish global well-posedness for small initial data in H s H^{s} when s > 3 2 s>\frac{3}{2} .

1.
Introduction

Poroelasticity theory, which rigorously couples solid deformation with fluid flow in porous materials, has evolved from a specialized soil consolidation model into a foundational theoretical framework for solving critical challenges across engineering, earth sciences, and biomechanics, see Cheng
[
13
]
.
Its significance lies in its ability to quantify the bidirectional interaction whereby mechanical loads induce fluid pressure changes and drive flow, while fluid injection or extraction, in turn, deforms the solid matrix.

This paper studies the coupling between an elastic sheet and a porous medium, a model referred to as the
Muskat problem with an elastic interface.

For related models such as hydroelastic waves, the Peskin problem, and the Muskat problem for viscoelastic filtration, one can refer to Cameron and Strain
[
10
]
, Gahn
[
24
]
, Meirmanov
[
33
]
, and Plotnikov and Toland
[
38
]
for these results.
Our model integrates Darcy’s law for fluids with the Cosserat shell theory (under Kirchhoff’s hypotheses) to describe the elastic sheet.

A closely related class of models concerns hydroelastic waves, which describes the interaction between elastic structures and hydrodynamic forcing; see
[
38
]
.
In this setting, the fluid is governed by the incompressible Euler equations.
For developments in numerical simulation, experimental studies, and applications of hydroelastic waves, we refer to Părău
et al.

[
27
]
.
The local well-posedness of two-dimensional hydroelastic waves was established by Ambrose and Siegel
[
6
]
and by Liu and Ambrose
[
30
]
.
Results on local well-posedness for hydroelastic waves with vorticity in arbitrary spatial dimensions can be found in the work of the second author and Wang
[
40
]
.
Recently, authors obtained a low-regularity well-posedness result for two-dimensional hydroelastic waves in
[
39
]
.

Let us denote the interface between the fluids (or the fluid and the air) at time
t
by
\Sigma_{t}
.
In this paper, we assume throughout that
\Sigma_{t}
can be represented by the graph of a time-dependent function
\eta(t,x)
, so that

\Sigma_{t}=\{(x,\eta(t,x)):x\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Along the interface
\Sigma_{t}
, a thin layer of elastic sheet separates the fluid domain into the upper and lower region, denoted by
\Omega^{+}_{t}
,
\Omega_{t}^{-}
respectively.
They are given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{t}^{+}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\eta(t,x)<y<\underline{b}^{+}(x)\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{t}^{-}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\underline{b}^{-}(x)<y<\eta(t,x)\},

where
\underline{b}^{+}(x)
and
\underline{b}^{-}(x)
are parameterizations of the upper and the lower part of the fluid boundary respectively:

\Gamma^{\pm}=\{(x,\underline{b}^{\pm}(x)):x\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Two-phase Muskat problem with an elastic interface

The velocities
u^{\pm}
and pressure
p^{\pm}
of incompressible fluid in each region is governed by Darcy’s law:

(1.1)

\mu^{\pm}u^{\pm}+\nabla_{x,y}p^{\pm}=-\rho^{\pm}g\textbf{e}_{y},\quad\nabla_{x,y}\cdot u^{\pm}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega^{\pm}_{t},

where
g\geq 0
denotes the gravitational acceleration,
\rho^{\pm}
are the densities of the fluids in
\Omega^{\pm}
, and
\mu^{\pm}
are the corresponding viscosity coefficients.

Along the interface
\Sigma_{t}
, we assume that the normal component of the velocity is continuous, namely,

(1.2)

u^{+}\cdot n=u^{-}\cdot n\quad\text{on }\Sigma_{t},

where
n=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}}(-\eta_{x},1)
denotes the upward-pointing unit normal vector to
\Sigma_{t}
.
Then the kinematic boundary condition on
\Sigma_{t}
takes the form

(1.3)

\partial_{t}\eta=\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}u^{-}\cdot n\big|_{\Sigma_{t}}.

The dynamic boundary condition on
\Sigma_{t}
asserts that the pressure jump across
\Sigma_{t}
is balanced by the restoring force generated by the elastic sheet.

(1.4)

p^{-}-p^{+}=\sigma\mathbf{E}(\eta)\quad\text{on }\Sigma_{t},

where
\sigma>0
is the coefficient of flexural rigidity.
The nonlinear elastic operator
\mathbf{E}(\eta)
, arising from the bending energy of the sheet, is given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(\eta):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}}\left(\frac{\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{3/2}}\right)_{x}\right]_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{3/2}}\right)^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{1}{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\eta_{x}}{\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}}\right)_{x}\right)_{xx}+\frac{5}{2}\left(\frac{\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}^{2}}{(1+\eta^{2}_{x})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)_{x}.

On the rigid upper and lower boundary, we assume the non-penetration condition,

(1.6)

u^{\pm}\cdot(-\underline{b}^{\pm}_{x},1)=0,\quad\text{on }\Gamma^{\pm}.

The system (
1.1
)-(
1.6
) is referred to as the
two-phase
Muskat problem with an elastic interface.
When the upper phase corresponds to air or vacuum, so that
\mu^{+}=\rho^{+}=p^{+}=0
, the problem reduces to the
one-phase
Muskat problem.

1.1.
Formulation of the Muskat problem

The Muskat problem admits several equivalent formulations; see
[
1
,
14
,
8
,
7
,
12
,
26
,
18
]

In the absence of surface tension, Córdoba & Gancedo
[
19
]
introduced a contour dynamics approach for the infinite-depth Muskat problem without viscosity jump, and proved local well-posedness in

for graph interfaces.
This approach was later extended by Córdoba, Córdoba & Gancedo
[
17

18
]
to include a viscosity jump and to non-graph interfaces satisfying the arc-chord and Rayleigh–Taylor conditions.
Using an arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian method, Cheng, Granero & Shkoller
[
14
]
established local well-posedness for the one-phase problem with a flat bottom, assuming initial surfaces
\eta\in H^{2}
.
Matioc
[
32

later refined the regularity requirement to
\eta\in H^{s}
with
s>\frac{3}{2}
for the constant-viscosity, infinite-depth case.
Alazard & Lazar
[
4
]
provided an alternative proof via paralinearization of the contour dynamics formulation.
Nguyen & Pausader
[
35
]
reformulated the problem using the Dirichlet–Neumann operator and proved local well-posedness for large data in the critical Sobolev spaces.
For results on the Muskat problem with surface tension, we refer to
[
36
,
5
,
31
]
, and references therein.
Concerning global well-posedness, relevant works include
[

,

,

In this paper, we follow the formulation in Nguyen
[
36
]
and Nguyen & Pausader
[
35
]

G^{\pm}(\eta)
associated to
\Omega^{\pm}_{t}

For a fixed time

defined on
\Sigma

\phi^{\pm}
be the solution of

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x,y}\phi^{\pm}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega^{\pm},\\
\phi^{\pm}=f\quad\text{on }\Sigma,\\
\frac{\partial\phi^{\pm}}{\partial\nu^{\pm}}=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma^{\pm}.\end{cases}

The Dirichlet-Neumann operators
G^{\pm}(\eta)
is given by

G^{\pm}(\eta)f:=\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}\frac{\partial\phi^{\pm}}{\partial n}.

Then the two-dimensional Muskat problem with an elastic interface can be reformulated using the following result.

Proposition 1.1

(
[

]
)
.

(i)
If

is a solution to the one-phase Muskat problem, then

\partial_{t}\eta=-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)(\sigma\mathbf{E}(\eta)+\rho^{-}g\eta).

On the other hand, if

(ii)
If

solve the two-phase Muskat problem if and only if

(1.8)

\partial_{t}\eta=-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)f^{-},

where

(1.9)

\begin{cases}f^{-}-f^{+}=\sigma\mathbf{E}(\eta)+g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\eta,\\
\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta)f^{+}=\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)f^{-}.\end{cases}

On the other hand, if
\eta
is a solution of (

) where
f^{\pm}
solve (

Proposition
1.1
was proved in Appendix
B
in
[
36
]
for the Muskat problem with surface tension.
Replacing the capillary terms with elastic terms yields the corresponding result stated above.

The Muskat problem with an elastic interface is essentially a fifth-order quasilinear parabolic equation.
Indeed, at the leading order, one may intuitively approximate
\sigma\mathbf{E}(\eta)+\rho^{-}g\eta\approx\sigma\Delta^{2}\eta
, so that the one-phase Muskat problem (
1.7
) takes the schematic form

(1.10)

\partial_{t}\eta=-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)\Delta^{2}\eta+\text{remainder terms}.

In the bottomless case where
\Gamma^{\pm}=\emptyset
, it
\eta(t,x)
solves (
1.10
), so is

\eta_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{-1}\eta(\lambda^{5}t,\lambda x),\quad\forall\lambda>0.

Hence, the scale-invariant Sobolev space is
\dot{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
.
This critical regularity coincides with that of the gravity Muskat problem and the Muskat problem with surface tension.
As in the case of water waves with surface tension
[
2
]
and the Muskat problem with surface tension
[
36
]
, the presence of rigid boundaries
\Gamma^{\pm}
affects only the low-frequency behavior and does not alter the local well-posedness theory.

We make the following remarks about the local well-posedness result.

Remark 1.1
.

i) Theorem
1.2
establishes well-posedness in the sense of Hadamard, namely: existence, uniqueness and Lipschitz dependence on initial data.
To the best of our knowledge, these are the first results on the Cauchy theory of two-dimensional Muskat problem with an elastic interface.
In particular, these are large-data well-posedness results that require only
\frac{1}{2}
more derivative above the critical space.
Under this regularity setting, the curvature of the free surface
\Sigma_{t}
may be unbounded and need not be locally square integrable.

ii) The restriction preventing local well-posedness in all subcritical spaces for arbitrary data arises from the remainder estimate for the Dirichlet–Neumann operator (
B.1
), which is valid only for
\mathfrak{s}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
However, for sufficiently small initial data, the Muskat problem can be treated as a semilinear parabolic equation.
By exploiting the parabolic spacetime estimates (
B.4
) and (
B.5
), one can establish the global well-posedness of Muskat problem with an elastic interface in all sub-critical spaces, as stated in Theorem
1.3
below.

iii) As a quasilinear parabolic equation, the Muskat problem with an elastic interface exhibits the instantaneous parabolic smoothing.
Suppose
\eta\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
, then
\eta(t,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
when

, for any
\delta>0
.
The proof is standard and therefore omitted.
See
[
14
, Section 9]
for an analogous argument in the gravity Muskat setting.

We define the space
\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
as the space of tempered distributions
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q}(I;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))}:=\|2^{sj}\|P_{j}u\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty.

The proof of our main results relies on several key technical ingredients.
First, we reformulate the Muskat problem into a single equation for the interface
\eta
by utilizing the Dirichlet-Neumann operators
G^{\pm}(\eta)
associated with the fluid domains
\Omega^{\pm}_{t}
.
To address the high-order nonlinearities, we perform a detailed paralinearization of the elastic term
\mathbf{E}(\eta)
, which allows us to treat the Muskat problem as a fifth-order quasilinear paradifferential parabolic equation.
For the local well-posedness, we derive a priori energy estimates and contraction estimates within the paradifferential framework to establish existence, uniqueness, and Lipschitz stability for arbitrary initial data.
Finally, to obtain global results for small data, we rewrite the equation in an integral form based on a fifth-order fractional heat kernel and exploit parabolic space-time estimates to apply a fixed-point argument in sub-critical Sobolev spaces.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we first paralinearize the elastic term.
Then we rewrite one-phase and two-phase Muskat problems as paradifferential parabolic equation and obtain a priori energy estimates for these equations.
In Section
3
, we first prove contraction estimates for both one-phase and two-phase Muskat problems, then we combine energy estimates and contraction estimates to obtain local well-posedness of Muskat problems.
Section
4
is then devoted to the proof of global well-posedness for Muskat problems.
We rewrite the solution of Muskat problems as fixed-point of some integral equations, and use the fixed-point Lemma
B.6
to obtain the unique existence of the global solution.
Many necessary paradifferential and product estimates are recalled in Appendix
A
.
Finally, we give some results on the Dirichlet-Neumann operators, the fractional heat kernel and the existence of fixed points in Appendix
B
.

Adding these two terms together, we get the paralinearization of the elastic term (

∎

2.1.
The one-phase case

The one-phase Muskat problem (

Proposition 2.2
.

For

Proof.

Recall the one-phase Muskat problem reads

\partial_{t}\eta=-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)\mathbf{E}(\eta)-\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)\eta.

For the term
G^{-}(\eta)\eta
, the estimate (
B.1
) with
\mathfrak{s}=s-\frac{3}{2}+\delta

\|G^{-}(\eta)\eta\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}+\delta}}\lesssim_{\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}+\delta}}.

Regarding the term

\|R^{-}(\eta)\mathbf{E}(\eta)\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}+\delta}}\lesssim_{\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}}\|\mathbf{E}(\eta)\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}},

so that this term can be put into

.
For the
|D|\mathbf{E}(\eta)

2.2
) to estimate the difference

\||D|\mathbf{E}(\eta)-|D|T_{\ell}\eta\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}+\delta}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{E}(\eta)-T_{\ell}\eta\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}+\delta}}\lesssim_{\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}.

By putting the perturbative terms into

).
∎

At leading order, the symbol

Consequently, the one-phase Muskat problem (

We have the following a priori estimate for the one-phase Muskat equation.

Proposition 2.3

Let

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta_{s}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}(|D|T_{\ell}\eta_{s},\eta_{s})_{L^{2}\times L^{2}}-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}([\langle T_{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{-\frac{s}{2}}}D\rangle^{s},|D|T_{\ell}]\eta_{s},\eta_{s})_{L^{2}\times L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\big(T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}}\eta_{s},T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}}\eta_{s}\big)_{L^{2}\times L^{2}}+\big(T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}}\eta_{s},\big((T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}})^{*}-T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}}\big)\eta_{s}\big)_{L^{2}\times L^{2}}

For the first term in

in

\displaystyle\|\eta_{s}\|_{H^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}^{-1}}T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}}\eta_{s}\|_{H^{\frac{5}{2}}}+\|(Id-T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}^{-1}}T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}})\eta_{s}\|_{H^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}}\|T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}}\eta_{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\eta_{s}\|_{H^{\frac{5}{2}-\delta}},

\|T_{\sqrt{m^{[5]}}}\eta_{s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}(\|\eta\|_{H^{s}})}\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}-\mathcal{F}(\|\eta\|_{H^{s}})\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}},

function

.

\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}\lesssim\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{1-\theta}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}^{\theta},

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{Z^{s}(T)}^{2}\leq\mathcal{F}\left(\|\eta\|_{Z^{s}(T)}\right)\left(\|\eta(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+T\mathcal{F}\big(\|\eta\|_{Z^{s}(T)}\big)\|\eta\|^{2}_{Z^{s}(T)}\right).

).
∎

3.
Contraction estimates and proof of local well-posedness

This section is dedicated to proving the contraction estimates for both the one-phase and two-phase Muskat problems.

.

We begin with a contraction estimate for the remainder term in the paralinearization of the elastic term
R_{E}(\eta)
in (
2.2
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\delta\in(0,s-\frac{3}{2})
, and
\delta\leq 1
,
R_{E}(\eta)
satisfies the contraction estimate

\|R_{E}(\eta_{1})-R_{E}(\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\Big(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\Big)\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s}}.

Proof.

We denote the Gâteaux derivative
d_{u}F(u)
of a function
F
at
u
in the direction
\dot{u}
as follows:

\displaystyle d_{u}F(u)\dot{u}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}(F(u+\epsilon\dot{u})-F(u)).

To obtain this lemma, we just need prove

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|d_{\eta}R_{E}(\eta)\dot{\eta}\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\dot{\eta}\|_{H^{s}}.

One can check that

\displaystyle d_{\eta}\left(\frac{\eta_{x}}{\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}}\right)\dot{\eta}=\frac{\dot{\eta}_{x}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}},\quad d_{\eta}\left(\frac{1}{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}\right)\dot{\eta}=-\frac{2\eta_{x}\dot{\eta}_{x}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{2}},

\displaystyle d_{\eta}\left(\frac{\eta_{x}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)\dot{\eta}=\frac{1-6\eta_{x}^{2}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{9}{2}}}\dot{\eta}_{x}.

Hence, using the chain rule for the Gâteaux derivative,

\displaystyle d_{\eta}\left[\frac{1}{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\eta_{x}}{\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}}\right)_{x}\right]\dot{\eta}=\frac{\dot{\eta}_{xx}}{(1+\eta^{2}_{x})^{\frac{5}{2}}}-\frac{5\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}\dot{\eta}_{x}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}},

\displaystyle d_{\eta}\left(\frac{\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}^{2}}{(1+\eta^{2}_{x})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)\dot{\eta}=\frac{(1-6\eta_{x}^{2})\eta^{2}_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{9}{2}}}\dot{\eta}_{x}+\frac{2\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\dot{\eta}_{xx}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle d_{\eta}\left(\frac{1}{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\eta_{x}}{\sqrt{1+\eta_{x}^{2}}}\right)_{x}\right)_{xx}\dot{\eta}=\frac{\partial_{x}^{4}\dot{\eta}}{(1+\eta_{x})^{\frac{5}{2}}}-\frac{10\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}\partial^{3}_{x}\dot{\eta}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle((1+\eta^{2}_{x})^{-\frac{5}{2}})_{xx}\dot{\eta}_{xx}-\frac{5\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}\dot{\eta}_{xxx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}-\left(\frac{10\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)_{x}\dot{\eta}_{xx}-\left(\frac{5\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)_{xx}\dot{\eta}_{x},

and

\displaystyle\frac{5}{2}d_{\eta}\left(\frac{\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}^{2}}{(1+\eta^{2}_{x})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)_{x}\dot{\eta}=

\displaystyle\frac{5\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\partial^{3}_{x}\dot{\eta}+\left(\frac{5\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)_{x}\dot{\eta}_{xx}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{5(1-6\eta_{x}^{2})\eta^{2}_{xx}}{2(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{9}{2}}}\dot{\eta}_{xx}+\left(\frac{5(1-6\eta_{x}^{2})\eta^{2}_{xx}}{2(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{9}{2}}}\right)_{x}\dot{\eta}_{x}.

Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle d_{\eta}\mathbf{E}(\eta)\dot{\eta}=\frac{\partial_{x}^{4}\dot{\eta}}{(1+\eta^{2}_{x})^{\frac{5}{2}}}+2\big((1+\eta^{2}_{x})^{-\frac{5}{2}}\big)_{x}\partial^{3}_{x}\dot{\eta}

\displaystyle+\left\{((1+\eta^{2}_{x})^{-\frac{5}{2}})_{xx}-\left(\frac{5\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)_{x}+\frac{5(1-6\eta_{x}^{2})\eta^{2}_{xx}}{2(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{9}{2}}}\right\}\dot{\eta}_{xx}

\displaystyle-\left[\left(\frac{5\eta_{x}\eta_{xx}}{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)_{xx}-\left(\frac{5(1-6\eta_{x}^{2})\eta^{2}_{xx}}{2(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{\frac{9}{2}}}\right)_{x}\right]\dot{\eta}_{x}.

Recall that

\displaystyle T_{\ell}\dot{\eta}=

\displaystyle T_{(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{-\frac{5}{2}}}\partial_{x}^{4}\dot{\eta}+2T_{((1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{-\frac{5}{2}})_{x}}\partial_{x}^{3}\dot{\eta}+T_{((1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{-\frac{5}{2}})_{xx}-5(\eta_{xx}\eta_{x}(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{-\frac{7}{2}})_{x}+\frac{5}{2}\eta_{xx}^{2}(1-6\eta_{x}^{2})(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{-\frac{9}{2}}}\dot{\eta}_{xx}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle T_{-5\eta_{xx}\eta_{x}(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{-\frac{7}{2}}+\frac{5}{2}(\eta_{xx}^{2}(1-6\eta_{x}^{2})(1+\eta_{x}^{2})^{-\frac{9}{2}})_{x}}\dot{\eta}_{x}.

By using (
A.6
), we then get

\displaystyle\|d_{\eta}\mathbf{E}(\eta)\dot{\eta}-T_{\ell}\dot{\eta}\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\dot{\eta}\|_{H^{s}}.

Since

\displaystyle d_{\eta}R_{E}(\eta)\dot{\eta}=d_{\eta}\mathbf{E}(\eta)\dot{\eta}-T_{\ell}\dot{\eta}-T_{d_{\eta}\ell\dot{\eta}}\eta,

it remains to prove

(3.2)

\|T_{d_{\eta}\ell\dot{\eta}}\eta\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\dot{\eta}\|_{H^{s}}.

This can be deduced from (
A.2
). Thus, we prove (
3.1
), which leads to the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
s\geq 2
,
\mathbf{E}(\eta)
satisfies the contraction estimate

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}}}\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}})\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s}}.

Proof.

Recall that

d_{\eta}\mathbf{E}(\eta)\dot{\eta}=d_{\eta}R_{E}(\eta)\dot{\eta}+T_{\ell}\dot{\eta}+T_{d_{\eta}\ell\dot{\eta}}\eta.

Then we use (
2.5
), (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|d_{\eta}\mathbf{E}(\eta)\dot{\eta}\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\|d_{\eta}R_{E}(\eta)\dot{\eta}\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}}+\|T_{\ell}\dot{\eta}\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}}+\|T_{d_{\eta}\ell\dot{\eta}}\eta\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\dot{\eta}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|\dot{\eta}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}.

This leads to the estimate (
3.3
).
∎

3.1.
Contraction estimate for one-phase Muskat problem

Suppose that
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are two solutions of one-phase Muskat problem (
1.7
) in
Z^{s}(T)
with the condition (
2.1
), then we show that the
Z^{s}(T)
norm of
\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
can be controlled by the initial condition of
\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
in
H^{s}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
s>2
.
Suppose that
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are two solutions of one-phase Muskat problem (
1.7
) in
Z^{s}
with the condition (
2.1
), then

(3.4)

\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{Z^{s}(T)}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{Z^{s}(T)\times Z^{s}(T)}}\|(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}}.

The implicit constant in the inequality depends only on
\big(s,h,\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}},\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}\big)
.

Proof.

Writing
\delta\eta=\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
for the difference of two solutions to the one-phase Muskat problem.
From the equation (
1.7
), one can write

(3.5)

\partial_{t}\delta\eta=-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta_{1})(\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2}))-\mathcal{R}_{0},

where the remainder term
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is given by

\mathcal{R}_{0}:=\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta_{1})\delta\eta+[G^{-}(\eta_{1})-G^{-}(\eta_{2})]\cdot\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})+\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}\eta_{2}\right).

Using the estimate (
B.1
),

\|G^{-}(\eta_{1})\delta\eta\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}.

According to the difference estimate for the Dirichlet-Neumann operator (
B.3
), we estimate

\displaystyle\left\|[G^{-}(\eta_{1})-G^{-}(\eta_{2})]\cdot\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})+\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}\eta_{2}\right)\delta\eta\right\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}+\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}\right)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}

Next, we rewrite the term
G^{-}(\eta_{1})(\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2}))
.
Using the paralinearization of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator (
B.2
),

G^{-}(\eta_{1})(\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2}))=|D|(\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2}))+\mathcal{R}_{1},\quad\|\mathcal{R}_{2}\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s}}}\|\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}}.

For the difference estimate of two elastic terms, we recall the estimate in (
3.3
),

\|\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}+\Big(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}\Big)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}.

Hence, we get the estimate for
\mathcal{R}_{1}
:

\|\mathcal{R}_{1}\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}.

We then write

|D|(\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2}))=|D|T_{\ell_{1}}\delta\eta+|D|T_{\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}}\eta_{2}+|D|(R_{E}(\eta_{1})-R_{E}(\eta_{2})).

By the result in Lemma
3.1
,

\||D|(R_{E}(\eta_{1})-R_{E}(\eta_{2}))\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\Big(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\Big)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}.

From (
3.2
), we get that

\||D|T_{\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}}\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}.

Collecting the above estimates, the equation (
3.5
) can be written as

(3.6)

\partial_{t}\delta\eta=-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}|D|T_{\ell_{1}}\delta\eta+\mathcal{R}_{2},

where the remainder term

\|\mathcal{R}_{2}\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}+\Big(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\Big)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}.

Note that the equation (
3.6
) takes the similar form as the equation (
2.6
).
Hence, doing the same analysis as in the proof of Proposition
2.3
, we get the energy inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\delta\eta\|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})}\|\delta\eta\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}+\mathcal{F}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{F}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}},

for some function
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
depending only on
(h,s,\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}},\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}})
.
By interpolation and Young’s inequality, we bound the last two terms in the inequality by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\mathcal{F}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})}\|\delta\eta\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}+\mathcal{F}_{1}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})\|\delta\eta\|^{2}_{H^{s}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})\Big(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\Big)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\mathcal{F}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})}\|\delta\eta\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}+\mathcal{F}_{1}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}^{2}\|\delta\eta\|^{2}_{H^{s}},

for some functions
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
depending only on
(h,s,\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}},\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}})
.
Hence, we get the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\delta\eta\|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})}\|\delta\eta\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}+\mathcal{F}(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}})\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}^{2}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}.

Applying the Gronwall’s inequality and using the definition of the
Z^{s}(T)
norm, we obtain the desired estimate (
3.4
).
∎

3.2.
Contraction estimate for two-phase Muskat problem

Suppose that
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are two solutions of two-phase Muskat problem (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) in
Z^{s}(T)
with the condition (
2.1
), then similar to the one-phase case, we show that the
Z^{s}(T)
norm of
\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
can be controlled by the initial condition of
\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
in
H^{s}
.

For
j=1,2
, consider
f_{j}^{\pm}
that solve the system

\begin{cases}f^{-}_{j}-f^{+}_{j}=\sigma\mathbf{E}(\eta_{j})+g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\eta_{j},\\
\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta_{j})f^{+}_{j}=\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}_{j}(\eta_{j})f^{-}.\end{cases}

We write
\delta f^{\pm}=f^{\pm}_{1}-f^{\pm}_{2}
, and
\delta\eta=\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
for the difference of these functions.
We first prove the following contraction estimate for
f^{\pm}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
s\geq 2
, and
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
.
For each
r\in[\frac{1}{2},s-\frac{3}{2}]
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\delta f^{\pm}\|_{\tilde{H}^{r}_{\pm}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\sigma\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{r+4}}+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{r}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}.

The implicit constant in the inequality depends only on
\big(s,h,r,\mu^{\pm}\big)
.

Proof.

We write

\displaystyle\delta f^{-}-\delta f^{+}=\delta k=\sigma(\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2}))+g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\delta\eta,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta_{1})\delta f^{-}-\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta_{1})\delta f^{+}=\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}[G^{+}(\eta_{1})-G^{+}(\eta_{2})]f_{2}^{+}-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}[G^{-}(\eta_{1})-G^{-}(\eta_{2})]f_{2}^{-}.

Applying
G^{+}(\eta_{1})
to the first equation, and using the fact that
G^{\pm}(\eta_{1})\delta f^{\pm}=\mp|D|\delta f^{\pm}+R^{\pm}(\eta_{1})\delta f^{\pm}
, we get

\displaystyle|D|\delta f^{-}=\frac{\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}(\sigma|D|[\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})]+g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})|D|\delta\eta)+\frac{\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}F,

\displaystyle F=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}R^{+}(\eta_{1})\delta f^{+}-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}R^{-}(\eta_{1})\delta f^{-}+\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}[G^{+}(\eta_{1})-G^{+}(\eta_{2})]f^{+}_{2}-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}[G^{-}(\eta_{1})-G^{-}(\eta_{2})]f_{2}^{-}.

Using the contraction estimate (
3.3
),

\|\delta f^{-}\|_{H^{r}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\sigma\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{r+4}}+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{r}}+\|F\|_{H^{r-1}}+\sigma\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{r+4}\times H^{r+4}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}.

Using estimates (
B.2
), (
B.3
) and (
2.10
),

\displaystyle\|R^{\pm}(\eta_{1})\delta f^{\pm}\|_{H^{r-1}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\|\delta f^{\pm}\|_{\tilde{H}^{r-\delta}_{\pm}},

\displaystyle\|[G^{\pm}(\eta_{1})-G^{\pm}(\eta_{2})]f_{2}^{\pm}\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}.

For
r\in[\frac{1}{2},s-\frac{3}{2}]
, we then have

\displaystyle\|F\|_{H^{r-1}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\|\delta f^{\pm}\|_{\tilde{H}^{r-\delta}_{\pm}}+(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}.

Note that
\delta f^{+}=\delta f^{-}-\delta k
, we then obtain that

\displaystyle\|\delta f^{\pm}\|_{H^{r}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}

\displaystyle\|\delta f^{\pm}\|_{\tilde{H}^{r-\delta}_{\pm}}+\sigma\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{r+4}}+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{r}}

\displaystyle+(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}.

From the definition of
\tilde{H}^{r}
, following the proof of (4.22) in
[
36
]
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|\delta f^{\pm}\|_{H^{1,r}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\|\delta f^{\pm}\|_{\tilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\pm}}+\|\delta f^{\pm}\|_{H^{1,r-\delta}}

\displaystyle+\sigma\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{r+4}}+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{r}}+(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}.

By using the variational form the definition for two-phase Muskat problem in
[
35
]
and the definition of
\tilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\pm}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\delta f^{\pm}\|_{\tilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\pm}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\|\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}(\|\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}})

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\sigma\|\delta\eta\|_{\frac{9}{2}}+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}+(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\left(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{\frac{9}{2}}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{\frac{9}{2}}}\right).

From the above estimates, by using an induction argument, we obtain (
3.7
).
∎

We then prove the contraction estimate for two solutions
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
s\geq 2
.
Suppose that
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are two solutions of one-phase Muskat problem (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) in
Z^{s}
with the condition (
2.1
), then

\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{Z^{s}(T)}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{Z^{s}(T)\times Z^{s}(T)}}\|(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}}.

The implicit constant in the inequality depends only on
\big(s,h,\sigma,\mu^{\pm},(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g\big)
.

Proof.

From the equation (
1.8
),
\delta\eta
solves the equation

\partial_{t}\delta\eta=-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta_{1})\delta f^{-}-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}[G^{-}(\eta_{1})-G^{-}(\eta_{2})]f_{2}^{-}.

Using estimates (
B.3
) and (
2.10
),

\|[G^{-}(\eta_{1})-G^{-}(\eta_{2})]f_{2}^{-}\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}.

Using the paralinearization of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator (
B.2
), we write

where the remainder term satisfies

\displaystyle\|R^{-}(\eta_{1})\delta f^{-}\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\|\delta f^{-}\|_{\tilde{H}^{s-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\sigma\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}}

\displaystyle+(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}},

where we have used (
3.7
) with

.
We then get that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta\eta=-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}|D|\delta f^{-}+\mathcal{R}_{1},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{1}\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\left(\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}+\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\right).

From the computations of above lemma,

\displaystyle|D|\delta f^{-}=\frac{\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}(\sigma|D|[\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})]+g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})|D|\delta\eta)+\frac{\mu^{+}\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}F,

where the remainder term satisfies

\displaystyle\|F\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\left(\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}+\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\right).

From the calculations of (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\||D|[\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})]-|D|T_{\ell_{1}}\delta\eta\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}+\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}.

Thus, we obtain that

(3.8)

\partial_{t}\delta\eta=-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}|D|T_{\ell_{1}}\delta\eta+\mathcal{R}_{2},

where the remainder term
\mathcal{R}_{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{2}\|_{H^{s-\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim_{\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}}(\sigma+(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})g)\left(\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}-\delta}}+\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}\times H^{s+\frac{5}{2}}}\right).

Since (
3.8
) takes the same form as (
3.6
), the result follows from an argument analogous to that in Proposition
3.3

3.3.
Proof of local well-posedness

We now finish the proof of local well-posedness by adapting the a priori estimates and contraction estimates we have shown.
The argument here is essentially the same as Section
3.4
and
4.4
in
[
36
]
.
We assume that
\eta_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s\geq 2
satisfying dist
(\eta_{0},\Gamma^{\pm})>2h
.

We first begin with the one-phase Muskat problem.
Let
J_{\varepsilon}
be the mollifier that selects the frequency portion not greater than
\varepsilon^{-1}
of a function.
Then we consider the approximate solution
\eta_{\varepsilon}
that solves the following ODE in the Banach space

\partial_{t}\eta_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}J_{\varepsilon}[G^{-}(J_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon})(\sigma\mathbf{E}(J_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon})+\rho^{-}gJ_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon})],\quad\eta_{\varepsilon}|_{t=0}=\eta(0,x).

The solution
\eta_{\varepsilon}
exists on some maximal time interval
(0,T_{\varepsilon}]
.
A priori estimates Proposition
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
also hold for
\eta_{\varepsilon}
.
Hence, using a continuity argument, there exists a positive time
T<T_{\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
such that

\|\eta_{\varepsilon}\|_{Z^{s}(T)}\lesssim\mathcal{F}\big(\|\eta(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{s}}\big),\quad\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\text{dist}(\eta_{\varepsilon}(t),\Gamma^{-})>h,

for some function
\mathcal{F}
that depends on
\big(s,h,\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}},\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}\big)
.
The contraction estimate Proposition
3.3
also holds for
\eta_{\varepsilon}
.
By choosing
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain that the limiting function
\eta
is a solution of (
1.7
) in
Z^{s}(T)
with initial data
\eta_{0}
.
The uniqueness of the solution and Lipschitz dependence of initial data for the solution follows directly from Proposition
3.3
.

As for two-phase Muskat problem, we consider the ODE

\partial_{t}\eta_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}J_{\varepsilon}[G^{-}(J_{\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon})(J_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}^{-})],\quad\eta_{\varepsilon}|_{t=0}=\eta(0,x),

where functions
f^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}
solve

\begin{cases}f^{-}_{\varepsilon}-f^{+}_{\varepsilon}=\sigma\mathbf{E}(\eta_{\varepsilon})+g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\eta_{\varepsilon},\\
\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta_{\varepsilon})f^{+}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta_{\varepsilon})f^{-}_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

The solvability and regularity of
f^{\pm}
follow from Lemma
2.5
.
A priori estimates Proposition
2.8
, and the contraction estimate Proposition
3.5
also hold for
\eta_{\varepsilon}
.
By passing to the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, and doing the same as in the one-phase Muskat case, we obtain the local well-posedness of two-phase Muskat problem.

4.
Global well-posedness of Muskat problem with an elastic interface

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
, establishing the global well-posedness for the Muskat problem with an elastic interface under the assumption of small initial data.
This section transitions from the local energy-based arguments to an integral formulation, utilizing fixed-point lemmas to prove that solutions exist for all time
T>0
.

The overarching strategy in this section is to treat the nonlinear system as a perturbation of a fractional heat equation.
By rewriting the equations in integral form, we can apply space-time estimates of the fractional heat equation to show that the nonlinearities remain small if the initial data is sufficiently small.

4.1.
Global well-posedness for one-phase Muskat problem

For one-phase Muskat problem, we rewrite the governing equation (
1.7
) as:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}|D|^{5}+\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}|D|\right)\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}R^{-}(\eta)\mathbf{E}(\eta)-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}|D|(T_{\ell(\eta)}\eta+R_{E(\eta)}\eta-|D|^{4}\eta)-\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}R^{-}(\eta)\eta.

Hence,
\eta
is the solution to the integral equation

(4.1)

\eta(t)=e^{-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}t|D_{x}|^{5}-\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}t|D_{x}|}\eta_{0}-\mathcal{B}(\eta,\eta),

where the bilinear form is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\eta,f)(t,x)=\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}(t-\tau)|D_{x}|^{5}-\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}(t-\tau)|D_{x}|}\Big(R^{-}(\eta)(T_{\ell(\eta)}f+R_{E(\eta)}(f))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|D|(T_{\ell(\eta)}f+R_{E(\eta)}f-|D|^{4}f)+\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\sigma}R^{-}(\eta)f\Big)d\tau.

Let
X^{s}([0,T])
denote the Banach space endowed with the equivalent norm

\|\eta\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}=\|\eta\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})}+\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}\|\eta\|_{\widetilde{L}^{1}([0,T];H^{s+5})}.

We prove the following result for the equation (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
, there exists a small number
\delta>0
such that if
\|\eta_{0}\|_{H^{s}}<\delta
, then

e^{-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}t|D_{x}|^{5}-\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}t|D_{x}|}\eta_{0}-\mathcal{B}(\eta,\eta),

has a unique fixed point
\eta
in
X^{s}([0,T])
for any
T>0
with norm less than
C\|\eta_{0}\|_{H^{s}}
.

Proof.

Let
T>0
, we apply the fixed point Lemma
B.6
.
In the proof the constant
C
may change from line to line.
From (
B.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|e^{-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}t|D_{x}|^{5}-\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}t|D_{x}|}\eta_{0}\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}\leq C\|\eta_{0}\|_{H^{s}}.

To match the paralinearization of the elastic term in (
2.2
), we define

\mathbf{E}(\eta,f):=T_{\ell(\eta)}f+R_{E(\eta)}(f).

Using (
B.4
) with
(q_{1},q_{2})=(\infty,1)
and
(q_{1},q_{2})=(1,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(\eta,f)\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}\|R^{-}(\eta)\mathbf{E}(\eta,f)\|_{\tilde{L}^{1}([0,T];H^{s})}+C\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}\|R^{-}(\eta)f\|_{\tilde{L}^{1}([0,T];H^{s})}

\displaystyle+C\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}\||D|(T_{\ell(\eta)}f+R_{E(\eta)}f-|D|^{4}f)\|_{\tilde{L}^{1}([0,T];H^{s})}.

We now estimate each term on the right-hand side of the inequality.
It follows from Lemma
B.8
that

\displaystyle\|R^{-}(\eta)f\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|\eta\|_{W^{1+\epsilon,\infty}}\|f\|_{H^{s+1}}+C\|\eta\|_{H^{s+1}}\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}.

We also get that

\displaystyle\|R^{-}(\eta)\mathbf{E}(\eta,f)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|\eta\|_{W^{1+\epsilon,\infty}}\|\mathbf{E}(\eta,f)\|_{H^{s+1}}+C\|\eta\|_{H^{s+1}}\|\mathbf{E}(\eta,f)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|f\|_{H^{s+5}}+C\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+5}}+C\|\eta\|_{H^{s+1}}\|f\|_{H^{s+4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|f\|_{H^{s+5}}+C\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+5}}.

Due to (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\||D|(T_{\ell(\eta)}f+R_{E(\eta)}f-|D|^{4}f)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|f\|_{H^{s+5}}+C\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+5}}.

We then obtain that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(\eta,f)\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}\leq

\displaystyle C\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})}\|f\|_{\tilde{L}^{1}([0,T];H^{s+5})}+C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s})}\|\eta\|_{\tilde{L}^{1}([0,T];H^{s+5})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\eta\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}\|f\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}.

From the contraction estimate (
B.3
),
we can obtain

\displaystyle\|[R^{-}(\eta_{1})-R^{-}(\eta_{2})]\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2},f)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}[(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s}})\|f\|_{H^{s+5}}+(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s+5}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s+5}})\|f\|_{H^{s}}],

and by Lemma
B.8
,

\displaystyle\|R^{-}(\eta_{1})(\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1},f)-\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2},f))\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s}})\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+5}}\|f\|_{H^{s}}+C\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s+5}}\|f\|_{H^{s}}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\||D|(T_{\ell(\eta_{1})}f+R_{E(\eta_{1})}f-|D|^{4}f)-|D|(T_{\ell(\eta_{2})}f+R_{E(\eta_{2})}f-|D|^{4}f)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}[(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s}})\|f\|_{H^{s+5}}+(\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s+5}}+\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s+5}})\|f\|_{H^{s}}].

Thus, we get the difference estimate

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(\eta_{1},f)-\mathcal{B}(\eta_{2},f)\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}\leq

\displaystyle(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{X^{s}\times X^{s}([0,T])}+1)\|\delta\eta\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}\|f\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}.

We then obtain this lemma by using the fixed-point Lemma
B.6
.
∎

Let
\eta_{0}^{j}\in H^{s}
,
j=1,2
be initial data with norm less than
\delta
given in Lemma
4.1
, and we denote
\eta^{j}
,
j=1,2
be the corresponding solutions with initial data
\eta_{0}^{j}
.
Using (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we compute

\displaystyle\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}\leq\big\|e^{-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{-}}t|D_{x}|^{5}-\frac{\rho^{-}g}{\mu^{-}}t|D_{x}|}(\eta_{0}^{1}-\eta_{0}^{2})\big\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}+\|\mathcal{B}(\eta^{1},\eta^{1})-\mathcal{B}(\eta^{2},\eta^{2})\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta_{0}^{1}-\eta_{0}^{2}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\mathcal{B}(\eta^{1},\eta^{1}-\eta^{2})\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}+\|\mathcal{B}(\eta^{1},\eta^{2})-\mathcal{B}(\eta^{2},\eta^{2})\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta_{0}^{1}-\eta_{0}^{2}\|_{H^{s}}+C\|\eta^{1}\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}\|\eta^{1}-\eta^{2}\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}+C\|\eta^{1}-\eta^{2}\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}\|\eta^{2}\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}.

Choosing
\delta
small enough, we obtain that for any
T>0
,

\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{X^{s}([0,T])}\lesssim\|\eta_{0}^{1}-\eta_{0}^{2}\|_{H^{s}}.

This shows the Lipschitz dependence on initial data of the solution map.
As a consequence, we obtain the global well-posedness for one-phase Muskat problem in
H^{s}
in the sense of Hadamard.

4.2.
Global well-posedness for two-phase Muskat problem

For two-phase Muskat problem, we restrict ourself to the physically stable case where the denser fluid is on the bottom
\rho^{+}\leq\rho^{-}
.
One can write the system (
1.9
) as

\frac{1}{\mu^{-}}G^{-}(\eta)f^{-}=\frac{1}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta)f^{-}-\frac{g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta)\eta-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{+}}G^{+}(\eta)\mathbf{E}(\eta).

This can be further written as

|D_{x}|f^{-}=\frac{1}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}(\mu^{-}R^{+}(\eta)-\mu^{+}R^{-}(\eta))f^{-}-\frac{g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}G^{+}(\eta)\eta-\frac{\sigma\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}G^{+}(\eta)\mathbf{E}(\eta).

Hence,
f^{-}
is the fixed-point of

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\eta)\varphi=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}(\mu^{-}|D_{x}|^{-1}R^{+}(\eta)-\mu^{+}|D_{x}|^{-1}R^{-}(\eta))\varphi

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}|D_{x}|^{-1}G^{+}(\eta)\eta-\frac{\sigma\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}|D_{x}|^{-1}G^{+}(\eta)\mathbf{E}(\eta).

Using the equation (
1.8
), we obtain that
\eta
solves the nonlinear fractional heat equation.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta+\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}|D_{x}|^{5}\eta+\frac{g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}|D_{x}|\eta=-\frac{1}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}(R^{+}(\eta)+R^{-}(\eta))f^{-}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}R^{+}(\eta)\eta+\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}R^{+}(\eta)\mathbf{E}(\eta)-\frac{\sigma}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}|D_{x}|(\mathbf{E}(\eta)-\partial_{x}^{4}\eta).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
r>\frac{1}{2}
and let
\eta\in H^{s}\cap H^{r+5}
, there exists a small number
\delta>0
such that if
\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}<\delta
, then the mapping
\mathcal{K}(\eta)
has a unique fixed point
f^{-}
in
H^{s}\cap H^{r+1}
and

(4.4)

\|f^{-}\|_{H^{r+1}}\leq Cg(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\|\eta\|_{H^{r+1}}+C\sigma\|\eta\|_{H^{r+5}}.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
B.7
with
E_{1}=H^{s}
,
E_{2}=H^{r+1}
and

\displaystyle u_{0}=-\frac{g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}|D_{x}|^{-1}G^{+}(\eta)\eta-\frac{\sigma\mu^{-}}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}|D_{x}|^{-1}G^{+}(\eta)\mathbf{E}(\eta).

From (
B.6
), we have

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{H^{r+1}}\leq

\displaystyle g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\||D_{x}|\eta\|_{H^{r}}+\sigma\||D_{x}|\mathbf{E}(\eta)\|_{H^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cg(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\|\eta\|_{H^{r+1}}+C\sigma\|\eta\|_{H^{r+5}}.

It remains to prove that the mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}(\eta)\varphi:=\frac{1}{\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}}(\mu^{-}|D_{x}|^{-1}R^{+}(\eta)-\mu^{+}|D_{x}|^{-1}R^{-}(\eta))\varphi

satisfies the condition in Lemma
B.7
.
We apply the estimate (
B.6
),

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{1}\varphi\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{H^{s}},\quad\|\mathcal{K}_{1}\varphi\|_{H^{r+1}}\leq C\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|\varphi\|_{H^{r+1}}+C\|\eta\|_{H^{r+1}}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s}}.

Hence, there exists a unique fixed point
f^{-}\in H^{s}\cap H^{r+1}
for
\mathcal{K}(\eta)
, and

\displaystyle\|f^{-}\|_{H^{r+1}}\leq Cg(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\|\eta\|_{H^{r+1}}+C\sigma\|\eta\|_{H^{r+5}}+C\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|f^{-}\|_{H^{r+1}}+C\|\eta\|_{H^{r+1}}\|f^{-}\|_{H^{s}}.

Taking
r=s-\frac{1}{2}
, then

\displaystyle\|f^{-}\|_{H^{s}}\leq Cg(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}+C\sigma\|\eta\|_{H^{s+4}}.

Thus, we obtain the estimate (
4.4
).
∎

Assume that
\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}\leq\delta
, and
f^{-}_{j}
is the fixed point of
\mathcal{K}(\eta_{j})
. Then

\displaystyle\||D_{x}|\delta f^{-}\|_{H^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|R^{+}(\eta_{1})f^{-}_{1}-R^{+}(\eta_{2})f^{-}_{2}\|_{H^{r}}+\|R^{-}(\eta_{1})f^{-}_{1}-R^{-}(\eta_{2})f^{-}_{2}\|_{H^{r}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+})\|G^{+}(\eta_{1})\eta_{1}-G^{+}(\eta_{2})\eta_{2}\|_{H^{r}}+\sigma\|G^{+}(\eta_{1})\mathbf{E}(\eta_{1})-G^{+}(\eta_{2})\mathbf{E}(\eta_{2})\|_{H^{r}}.

Using (
B.3
) and (
B.6
), we then obtain

(4.5)

\displaystyle\||D_{x}|\delta f^{-}\|_{H^{r}}\leq C(\sigma+g(\rho^{-}-\rho^{+}))\left(\|(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\|_{H^{r+5}\times H^{r+5}}\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{s}}+\|\delta\eta\|_{H^{r+5}}\right).

From Proposition
4.2
and (
4.5
), performing the similar analysis as in the proof of global well-posedness for one-phase Muskat problem, we obtain the global well-posedness for two-phase Muskat problem.

Lemma B.4

(

Proposition B.5

Let

\nu_{1}>0

\nu_{2}\geq 0

,
\alpha_{2}\geq 0

\displaystyle\|e^{-z(\nu_{1}|D_{x}|^{\alpha_{1}}+\nu_{2}|D_{x}|^{\alpha_{2}})}u(x)\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q_{1}}_{z}(I;H^{s+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{q_{1}}})}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\nu_{1}^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}}\|u\|_{H^{s}},

(B.5)

By Lemma
B.4
, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{j}e^{-z(\nu_{1}|D_{x}|^{\alpha_{1}}+\nu_{2}|D_{x}|^{\alpha_{2}})}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-c(\nu_{1}2^{\alpha_{1}j}+\nu_{2}2^{\alpha_{2}j})z}\|P_{j}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle Ce^{-c\nu_{1}2^{\alpha_{1}j}z}\|P_{j}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

We then get (

\displaystyle\left(\int_{a}^{b}e^{-c\nu_{1}2^{\alpha_{1}j}q_{1}z}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}=(cq_{1})^{-\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\frac{2^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{q_{1}}j}}{\nu_{1}^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}e^{-c\nu_{1}2^{\alpha_{1}j}q_{1}z}d(c\nu_{1}2^{\alpha_{1}}q_{1}z)\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\leq C_{1}\frac{2^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{q_{1}}j}}{\nu_{1}^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}}.

By Lemma

and Young’s inequality in

\displaystyle\left\|P_{j}\int_{a}^{z}e^{-(z-y)(\nu_{1}|D_{x}|^{\alpha_{1}}+\nu_{2}|D_{x}|^{\alpha_{2}})}f(x,y)dy\right\|_{L^{q_{1}}_{z}(I;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle C\left\|\int_{a}^{b}e^{-c(\nu_{1}2^{\alpha_{1}j}+\nu_{2}2^{\alpha_{2}j})(z-y)}\|P_{j}f(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{2}}dy\right\|_{L^{q_{1}}_{z}(I)}

\Gamma^{-}=\emptyset

\Omega^{-}
has infinite depth.
The analysis for the flat lower boundary case is similar, and one just needs to replace

\widetilde{H}^{1+r}_{-}

(B.7)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x,y}\phi=0\quad\text{in}~\Omega^{-},\\

is a smooth solution of (

and

Using (

), we get

From the equation (
B.9
), we get that
v
is a fixed point of the operator

\displaystyle=e^{z|D_{x}|}f(x)+\int_{0}^{z}e^{(z-z^{\prime})|D_{x}|}Q_{a}[v](x,z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{z}e^{(z-z^{\prime})|D_{x}|}\int_{-\infty}^{z^{\prime}}e^{-(z^{\prime}-\tau)|D_{x}|}|D_{x}|\{Q_{b}[v](x,\tau)-Q_{a}[v](x,\tau)\}d\tau dz^{\prime}.

If
v
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{T}
(

the Dirichlet-Neumann operator
G^{-}(\eta)f
is defined by

(B.11)

B.5
) with

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{W^{1+\epsilon,\infty}}\|\nabla_{x,z}v\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}(J;H^{r})}+\|\eta\|_{H^{r+1}}\|\nabla_{x,z}v\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}(J;L^{\infty})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{W^{1+\epsilon,\infty}}\|f\|_{H^{r+1}}+\|\eta\|_{H^{r+1}}\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}.

This gives the estimate for the remainder term of the Dirichlet-Neumann operator.

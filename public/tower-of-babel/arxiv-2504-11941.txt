1.
Introduction

Matching theory of finite simple graphs has a rich history and numerous applications in different areas of mathematics and beyond (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
10
]
). Let
\mathcal{H}
be a simple hypergraph with the vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})
and the edge set
E(\mathcal{H})
.
A
matching
in
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of disjoint edges in
\mathcal{H}
. The
matching number
of
\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
\nu(\mathcal{H})
, is the maximum size of a matching in
\mathcal{H}
. Very recently, the notion of matching in graph theory has been connected to the area of combinatorial commutative algebra via the introduction of square-free powers (see
[
3
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
24
,
25
]
, etc.) and this topic has been of great interests since
[
13
]
. The authors in
[
3
]
introduced the notion of square-free powers for the class of edge ideals of simple graphs. In fact, this notion can be defined for any square-free monomial ideals or edge ideals of hypergraphs. Let
I\subseteq R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a square-free monomial ideal, and
\mathcal{G}(I)
be the set of minimal monomial generators of
I
. Then there is a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
such that
I=I(\mathcal{H})
. The
k
-th square-free power
of
I
, denoted by
I^{[k]}
, is defined by

\displaystyle I^{[k]}

\displaystyle=\langle f\in I^{k}\mid f\text{ is square-free}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{1}\cdots u_{k}\mid u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\in E(\mathcal{H})\text{ is a matching in }\mathcal{H}\rangle

The study of various algebraic invariants such as Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity (or simply, regularity), depth, projective dimension, etc., of powers of edge ideals of hypergraphs have been one of the fundamental areas of research in the last few decades. In this context, the notion of matching and induced matching in a hypergraph play a very crucial role. Loosely speaking, a matching in a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
corresponds to a regular sequence that is contained in the edge ideal
I(\mathcal{H})
. On the other hand, the induced matching number of
\mathcal{H}
, i.e., the maximal cardinality of an induced matching, denoted by
\nu_{1}(\mathcal{H})
, is an invariant that can be used to estimate lower bounds for the regularity of powers of edge ideals
[
2
]
.
In particular, the following general lower bound for the regularity of edge ideals of
d
-uniform hypergraphs is well-known:

\mathrm{reg}(I(\mathcal{H})^{k})\geq(d-1)(\nu_{1}(\mathcal{H})-1)+kd.

A relevant question in this direction is the following: Is it possible to find a square-free version of this general lower bound? To the best of our knowledge, there is no general lower bound for the regularity of square-free powers.
In
[
13
]
, the authors explored this question for the sub-class of edge ideals of graphs. Recall that a graph
G
is a
2
-uniform simple hypergraph. The following analogous lower bound for the square-free powers of edge ideals of graphs is provided in
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
:

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})\geq\nu_{1}(G)+k\;\;\text{ for all }\;1\leq k\leq\nu_{1}(G).

It is important to note that this lower bound only works for
k\leq\nu_{1}(G)
, while it may happen that
I(G)^{[k]}\neq\langle 0\rangle
for
k>\nu_{1}(G)
(for instance, when
G
is complete). In this context, Erey and Hibi
[
15
]
introduced a new combinatorial notion, called
k
-admissible matching
of a graph
G
, which can be seen as a generalization of induced matching.
The
k
-admissible matching number
of
G
, denoted by
\mathrm{aim}(G,k)
, is the maximal size of a
k
-admissible matching in
G
. Interestingly, in
[
15
, Theorem 25]
the authors provided the following upper bound when
G
is a forest:

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k\;\;\text{ for all }\;1\leq k\leq\nu(G).

They further showed that this upper bound is attained when
k=2
and conjectured that this bound will be attained for any
k\leq\nu(G)
in the case of forests. Later on, Crupi, Ficarra, and Lax
[
6
, Theorem 3.6]
settled this conjecture.

In this work, we refine and extend the notion of
k
-admissible matching of a simple graph
G
to the realm of hypergraphs (see Definition
3.1
). Using this new definition, we provide a general lower bound for square-free powers of any square-free monomial ideal. This extends and strengthens all known lower bounds for square-free powers in the literature.

Theorem
3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph and
k
an integer where
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
. Then for any generalized
k
-admissible matching
M
of
\mathcal{H}
, we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right)\geq|V(M)|-|M|+k.

The above-mentioned lower bound is sharp, as seen in
Proposition

3.10
. In the case of
d
-uniform hypergraphs, we define a combinatorial invariant, called the
k
-admissible matching number
of
\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)
(see
Definition

3.12
), which is analogous to the induced matching number in the context of square-free powers. In particular,
\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},1)
coincides with
\nu_{1}(\mathcal{H})
. As a consequence of our main theorem, we obtain the following lower bound for square-free powers of edge ideals of
d
-uniform hypergraphs and, in particular, simple graphs.

Theorem
3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
d
-uniform hypergraph and
k
an integer where
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right)\geq(d-1)\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)+k.

Specifically, for a simple graph
G
and for any
1\leq k\leq\nu(G)
, we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(G)^{[k]}\right)\geq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k.

We provide two classes of simple graphs where we actually get the equality in the formula of the lower bound of the regularity of square-free powers. Indeed, we show that for the class of block graphs, we have the following formula for the regularity:

Theorem
4.12
.

Let
G
be a block graph. Then for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(G)
,

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k.

Since the class of block graphs contains the class of forests, the above formula extends the regularity formula of square-free powers of edge ideals of forests provided in
[
6
]
and
[
15
]
. Next, we consider the class of Cohen-Macaulay chordal graphs, which is another important class of chordal graphs. For edge ideals of this class, we prove a similar formula for the second square-free power:

Theorem
5.8
.

Let
G
be a Cohen-Macaulay chordal graph. Then

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2.

Square-free powers are defined as the square-free part of ordinary powers. There is an analog for symbolic powers, first studied by Fakhari
[
26
]
, which we shall call
symbolic square-free powers
. We remark that an analog of our general lower bound can be obtained for symbolic square-free powers of any square-free monomial ideal
[
5
]
.

This article is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we recall the necessary definitions and results from combinatorics and commutative algebra. In
Section

3
, we prove the general lower bound for square-free powers. In
Section

4
and
5
, we show that the general lower bound is attained for all the square-free powers of edge ideals of block graphs and the second square-free power of the edge ideal of Cohen-Macaulay chordal graphs, respectively. Finally,
Section

6
explores an implication of
Theorem

3.11
in the setting of recently introduced matching powers
[
12
]
of (not necessarily square-free) monomial ideals. This section also presents a few open problems related to the regularity of square-free powers of edge ideals of various classes of graphs. In particular, building on our results and some computational evidence, we propose the following conjecture:

Conjecture
6.2
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph. Then, for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(G)
, we have

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k.

2.1.
Combinatorics:

A (simple) hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is a pair
\mathcal{H}=(V(\mathcal{H}),E(\mathcal{H}))
, where
V(\mathcal{H})
is a finite set, called the
vertex set
of
\mathcal{H}
, and
E(\mathcal{H})
is a collection of subsets of
V(\mathcal{H})
such that no two elements contain each other, called the
edge set
of
\mathcal{H}
. The elements of
V(\mathcal{H})
are called the vertices of
\mathcal{H}
, and the elements of
E(\mathcal{H})
are called the edges of
\mathcal{H}
. An
induced sub-hypergraph
of
\mathcal{H}
on the vertex set
W\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, denoted by
\mathcal{H}[W]
, is a hypergraph with
V(\mathcal{H}[W])=W
and
E(\mathcal{H}[W])=\{\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H})\mid\mathcal{E}\subseteq W\}
. For
W\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}[V(\mathcal{H})\setminus W]
is also denoted by
\mathcal{H}\setminus W
.
If
W=\{x\}
for some
x\in V(\mathcal{H})
, then
\mathcal{H}\setminus\{x\}
is simply denoted by
\mathcal{H}\setminus x
. A
matching
of
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of edges
M
of
\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\emptyset
for any two distinct edges
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\in M
. A matching
M
of
\mathcal{H}
is called an
induced matching
of
\mathcal{H}
if
E(\mathcal{H}[\cup_{\mathcal{E}\in M}\mathcal{E}])=M
. The
induced matching number
of
\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
\nu_{1}(\mathcal{H})
, is the maximum cardinality of an induced matching in
\mathcal{H}
.

A (simple)
graph

G
is nothing but a hypergraph whose edges are of cardinality two. For
A\subseteq V(G)
, the set of
closed neighbors
of
A
is defined as
N_{G}[A]\coloneqq A\cup\{x\in V(G)\mid\{x,y\}\in E(G)\text{ for some }y\in A\}
. By a set of
open neighbors
of
A
, we mean
N_{G}(A)\coloneqq N_{G}[A]\setminus A
. If
A=\{x\}
, then
N_{G}(\{x\})
is simply denoted by
N_{G}(x)
. For
x\in V(G)
, the number
|N_{G}(x)|
is called the
degree
of
x
, and is denoted by
\deg(x)
. Given two vertices
x,y\in V(G)
, an
induced path of length
n
between
x
and
y
is a collection of vertices
P:a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
such that
a_{1}=x,a_{n}=y
, and
\{a_{i},a_{j}\}\in E(G)
if and only if
j=i-1
or
j=i+1
. A
complete graph
on
n
vertices, denoted by
K_{n}
, is a graph such that there is an edge between every pair of vertices. A
clique
of a graph
G
is a complete induced subgraph of
G
. A vertex
x\in V(G)
is said to be a
free vertex
of
G
if
G[N_{G}(x)]
is a complete graph. We say two edges
e
and
e^{\prime}
of
G
form a
gap
in
G
if
\{e,e^{\prime}\}
forms an induced matching in
G
. The complement of a graph
G
, denoted by
G^{c}
, is a graph such that
V(G^{c})=V(G)
and
E(G^{c})=\{\{x,y\}\mid\{x,y\}\not\in E(G)\}
.

A
cycle
of length
m
, denoted by
C_{m}
, is a graph such that
V(C_{m})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
and
E(C_{m})=\{\{x_{1},x_{m}\},\{x_{i},x_{i+1}\}\mid 1\leq i\leq m-1\}
. A graph
G
is called a
forest
if it does not contain any induced cycle. A connected forest is known as a
tree
. A graph is called
chordal
if it does not contain an induced
C_{n}
for
n\geq 4
. A graph is said to be
weakly chordal
if the graph and its complement both do not have any induced cycle
C_{n}
for
n\geq 5
. Note that any chordal graph is also weakly chordal.

2.2.
Commutative Algebra

For a graded ideal
I\subseteq R
, the graded minimal free resolution of
I
is an exact sequence

\mathcal{F}_{\cdot}:\,\,0\rightarrow F_{p}\xrightarrow{\partial_{p}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}F_{0}\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}}I\rightarrow 0,

where
F_{i}=\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}R(-j)^{\beta_{i,j}(I)}
is a free
R
-module for each
i\geq 0
and
R(-j)
denotes the polynomial ring
R
with grading shifted by
j
. The numbers
\beta_{i,j}(I)
are called the
i^{th}

\mathbb{N}
-graded
Betti numbers
of
I
in degree
j
. The
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
(in short, regularity) of
I
, denoted by
\mathrm{reg}(I)
, is defined as
\mathrm{reg}(I)\coloneqq\max\{j-i\mid\beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\}
. If
I
is the zero ideal, then we adopt the convention that
\mathrm{reg}(I)=0
.
The following two well-known results regarding the regularity of a graded ideal will be used frequently in the subsequent sections.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
7
, Lemma 2.10]
)
.

Let
I\subseteq R
be a monomial ideal,
m
be a monomial of degree
d
in
R
, and
x
be an indeterminate in
R
. Then

(i)

\mathrm{reg}(I+\langle x\rangle)\leq\mathrm{reg}(I)
,

(ii)

\mathrm{reg}(I:x)\leq\mathrm{reg}(I)
,

(iii)

\mathrm{reg}I\leq\max\{\mathrm{reg}(I:m)+d,\mathrm{reg}(I+\langle m\rangle)\}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
27
]
)
.

Let
I_{1}\subseteq R_{1}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
and
I_{2}\subseteq R_{2}=\mathbb{K}[x_{m+1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be two graded ideals. Consider the ideal
I=I_{1}R+I_{2}R\subseteq R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Then

\mathrm{reg}(I)=\mathrm{reg}(I_{1})+\mathrm{reg}(I_{2})-1.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph on the vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Identifying the vertices of
\mathcal{H}
as indeterminates, we consider the polynomial ring
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
over a field
K
. Corresponding to any
F\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, we denote the square-free monomial
\prod_{x_{i}\in F}x_{i}
of
R
by
\mathbf{x}_{F}
. The edge ideal of
\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
I(\mathcal{H})
, is a square-free monomial ideal of
R
defined as

I(\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\langle\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{E}}\mid\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H})\rangle.

Note that any square-free monomial ideal can be seen as the edge ideal of a hypergraph.

3.
k
-admissible matching and a general lower bound

The induced matching number of a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is a combinatorial invariant that has been used to estimate lower bounds for the regularity of ordinary and symbolic powers of the edge ideal
I(\mathcal{H})

[
2
]
. In the case of square-free powers, a similar attempt has been made in
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
, where the authors have shown that
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})\geq\nu_{1}(G)+k
for all
1\leq k\leq\nu_{1}(G)
. Later on, as a generalization of induced matching,
k
-admissible matching was introduced by Erey and Hibi. Then it was proved that for the class of forests, the equality
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k
holds for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(G)
(see
[
6
, Theorem 3.6]
and
[
15
, Theorem 25]
). Furthermore, it follows from the work of Fakhari
[
25
, Theorem 4.3]
, that for a Cameron-Walker graph
G
,
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k
for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(G)
. Based on these results, one can expect that for an arbitrary graph
G
, the regularity of
I(G)^{[k]}
is bounded below by the combinatorial invariant
\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k
.

Inspired by this idea, in this section, we refine and extend their definition of
k
-admissible matching for simple graphs to all hypergraphs. To avoid any ambiguity, we call this refined notion of matching to be generalized
k
-admissible matching of a hypergraph and use the same notations. We use
\sqcup
to denote disjoint unions. For a matching
M
, let
V(M)
denote the set of vertices that belong to the hyperedges in
M
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph and
M
a matching of
\mathcal{H}
. Then for any
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
, we say that
M
is a
generalized
k
-admissible matching
if there is a partition
M=M_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup M_{r}
such that

(1)

for any edge
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{H}[V(M)]
, we have
\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H}[V(M_{i})])
for some
i\in[r]
;

(2)

k\leq|M|=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|M_{i}|\leq r+k-1
;

(3)

for any
i\in[r]
and any matching
M_{i}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{H}[V(M_{i})]
with
|M_{i}^{\prime}|=|M_{i}|
, one has
V(M_{i}^{\prime})=V(M_{i})
.

Existence of generalized
k
-admissible matching:

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph and
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
. Take a matching
M^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{H}
with
|M^{\prime}|=k
. Now, consider the induced sub-hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\mathcal{H}[V(M^{\prime})]
and pick a minimal generator
u
of the ideal
I(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})^{[k]}
. Then the induced sub-hypergraph
\mathcal{H}[\mathrm{supp}(u)]
has a matching number
k
, and any matching of size
k
in
\mathcal{H}[\mathrm{supp}(u)]
consists of all the vertices in
\mathrm{supp}(u)
. Thus, any matching
M
with
|M|=k
in
\mathcal{H}[\mathrm{supp}(u)]
will give a generalized
k
-admissible matching of
\mathcal{H}
by taking the partition
M=M_{1}
.

Our aim in this section is to give a lower bound for
\mathrm{reg}I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}
for any
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
using generalized
k
-admissible matching. We present some basic observations for this new concept.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph and
M\subseteq E(\mathcal{H})
is a matching of
\mathcal{H}
.

(1)

A matching
M
of
\mathcal{H}
is a generalized
1
-admissible matching if and only if
M
is an induced matching of
\mathcal{H}
.

(2)

The condition
(3)
in

3.1
implies that
\nu(\mathcal{H}[V(M_{i})])=|M_{i}|
for any
i\in[r]
. Indeed, suppose otherwise that there exists a matching
M^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{H}[V(M_{i})]
such that
|M^{\prime}|>|M_{i}|
. Then consider a proper subset
M^{\prime\prime}
of

such that
|M^{\prime\prime}|=|M_{i}|
. This contradicts
Definition

3.1
(3), as desired.

(3)

The condition
(3)
in

is equivalent to the following fact: for any

,
I(\mathcal{H}[V(M_{i})])^{[|M_{i}|]}
is generated by one monomial
\prod_{x\in V(M_{i})}x
. This is immediate from the previous remark and
Definition

3.1
(3).

The following result follows from verifying the conditions in
Definition

3.1
; thus, we state it without proof.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
an induced sub-hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
.
Then, any generalized
k
-admissible matching in
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is a generalized
k
-admissible matching of
\mathcal{H}
.

Our primary tool to establish the regularity lower bound is the idea of Betti splitting introduced in

Definition 3.4

(

\displaystyle\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i,j}(J)+\beta_{i,j}(K)+\beta_{i-1,j}(J\cap K)\;\;\;\text{ for all }i,j.

. In fact, in this paper, we use the concept of splittable monomial ideal as defined in

]

.

Remark 3.6
.

The fact that for a splittable monomial ideal

[
16
, Proposition 3.2]
.

It is evident from
Equation

1
that Betti splittings allow us to study the graded Betti numbers of a splittable monomial ideal
I
in terms of the graded Betti numbers of the smaller ideals
J
and
K
. We provide an important application of this below.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\{I_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of proper square-free monomial ideals in
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N_{1}}]
such that for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
and any
f\in I_{i}
, we have
f/x\in I_{i-1}
for any
x\in\mathrm{supp}(f)
. Let
\{L_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of square-free monomial ideals in
\mathbb{K}[y_{1},\dots,y_{N_{2}}]
with the same condition. Let
n,k,l
be three integers where
l<k\leq n
. Set

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\coloneqq I_{k}L_{n-k},

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\coloneqq I_{k-1}L_{n-k+1}+\cdots+I_{l}L_{n-l}.

Assume that
J\neq 0
. Then
J+K
is a Betti splitting. In particular, we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(J+K\right)\geq\max\{\mathrm{reg}\left(I_{k}\right)+\mathrm{reg}\left(L_{n-k}\right),\ \mathrm{reg}\left(I_{k}\right)+\mathrm{reg}\left(L_{n-k+1}\right)-1\}.

Proof.

First we show that
\mathcal{G}(J)\cap\mathcal{G}(K)=\emptyset
. For this, it suffices to show that whenever
I_{i}
is non-zero for some
i\in\mathbb{N}
, then
I_{i}\subsetneq I_{i-1}
, as an analogous statement for
L_{i}\neq\langle 0\rangle
would follow. Indeed, let
f\in\mathcal{G}(I_{i})
, then
f/x\in I_{i-1}
by our hypotheses for any
x\in\mathrm{supp}(f)
. Since
f\in\mathcal{G}(I)
, we have
f/x\notin I_{i}
. Thus, we have
\mathcal{G}(J)\cap\mathcal{G}(K)=\emptyset
.

Note that, if
K=\langle 0\rangle
, then
J=J+K
is trivially a Betti splitting, and we have the result. Thus, we may assume
K\neq\langle 0\rangle
. We next show that
J\cap K=I_{k}L_{n-k+1}
. Indeed, the reverse inclusion is clear. For the inclusion, since
I_{i}
and
L_{j}
share no variable for any
i
and
j
, any minimal monomial generator of
J\cap K
is of the form
\mathrm{lcm}(fg,f^{\prime}g^{\prime})
where
f\in I_{k},g\in L_{n-k},f^{\prime}\in I_{r},
and
g^{\prime}\in L_{n-r}
for some
l\leq r<k
. It is then clear that
\mathrm{lcm}(fg,f^{\prime}g^{\prime})\in I_{k}L_{n-k+1}
, and thus the inclusion follows.

Let
(>)
be a fixed monomial ordering on
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N_{1}},y_{1},\dots y_{N_{2}}\}
and
(>_{lex})
be the lex ordering on the monomials induced from
(>)
.

Consider any generator
f\in\mathcal{G}(J\cap K)=\mathcal{G}(I_{k}L_{n-k+1})
. Set
f=f_{1}f_{2}
where
f_{1}\in\mathcal{G}(I_{k})
and
f_{2}\in\mathcal{G}(L_{n-k+1})
. Set

\displaystyle x_{f}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max_{>}\mathrm{supp}(f_{1}),

\displaystyle y_{f}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max_{>}\mathrm{supp}(f_{2}),

\displaystyle f_{I}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max_{>_{lex}}\{g\colon x_{f}\mid g\text{ and }g\mid f_{1},\text{ and }f_{1}/g\in\mathcal{G}(I_{k-1})\},

\displaystyle f_{L}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{multlined}\max_{>_{lex}}\{g\colon y_{f}\mid g\text{ and }g\mid f_{2},\text{ and }f_{2}/g\in\mathcal{G}(L_{n-k})\}.\end{multlined}

The existence of
f_{I}
and
f_{L}
is due to the hypotheses.

Let
(\phi,\psi)
define a map as follows:

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi)\colon\mathcal{G}(J\cap K)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{G}(J)\times\mathcal{G}(K)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\frac{f}{f_{L}},\frac{f}{f_{I}}\right).

Due to our constructions, the map is well-defined. We show that
(\phi,\psi)
indeed defines a splitting function:

(a)

for each
f\in\mathcal{G}(J\cap K)
, we have
f=\mathrm{lcm}(\frac{f}{f_{L}},\frac{f}{f_{I}})=\mathrm{lcm}(\phi(f),\psi(f))
since
f_{L}
and
f_{I}
have disjoint support;

(b)

for each
S=\{h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}(J\cap K)
, both
\mathrm{lcm}(\phi(S))
and
\mathrm{lcm}(\psi(S))
strictly divide
\mathrm{lcm}(S)
since by design, we have

(1)

\displaystyle x_{S},y_{S}\mid\mathrm{lcm}(h_{1},\dots,h_{s})=\mathrm{lcm}(S),

(2)

\displaystyle x_{S}\nmid\mathrm{lcm}(\frac{h_{1}}{{h_{1}}_{I}},\dots,\frac{h_{s}}{{h_{s}}_{I}})=\mathrm{lcm}(\phi(S)),

(3)

\displaystyle y_{S}\nmid\mathrm{lcm}(\frac{h_{1}}{{h_{1}}_{L}},\dots,\frac{h_{s}}{{h_{s}}_{L}})=\mathrm{lcm}(\psi(S)),

where

\displaystyle x_{S}\coloneqq\max_{>}\{x_{{h_{i}}}\colon i\in[s]\},

\displaystyle y_{S}\coloneqq\max_{>}\{y_{{h_{i}}}\colon i\in[s]\}.

Indeed, (1) follows from the definition of
x_{S}
and
y_{S}
. Regarding (2), observe that if
x_{S}\in\mathrm{supp}(h_{i})
for some
i\in[s]
, then we have
x_{S}=x_{h_{i}}\mid{h_{i}}_{I}
. This implies that
x_{S}\nmid\frac{h_{i}}{{h_{i}}_{I}}
for any
i\in[s]
. Thus, (2) follows. The proof for (3) is similar by symmetry.

Thus,
I=J+K
is a Betti splitting. The last statement follows from
Equation

1
.
∎

We now apply
Lemma

3.7
in the special case of square-free powers of edge ideals to obtain an important regularity formula needed for the general lower bound of square-free powers. Here we adopt the convention that
I^{[l]}=R
for any
l\leq 0
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be hypergraphs that have at least one edge each and no common vertex, and
k
a positive integer such that
\nu(\mathcal{H})+1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})+\nu(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
. Then

I(\mathcal{H}\sqcup\mathcal{H}^{\prime})^{[k]}=J+K,

where

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=I(\mathcal{H})^{[\nu(\mathcal{H})]}I(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})^{[k-\nu(\mathcal{H})]},

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=I(\mathcal{H})^{[\nu(\mathcal{H})-1]}I(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})^{[k-\nu(\mathcal{H})+1]}+\cdots+I(\mathcal{H})^{[k-\nu(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})]}I(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})^{[\nu(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})]},

is a Betti splitting. In particular, we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}\sqcup\mathcal{H}^{\prime})^{[k]}\right)\geq\max\{\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[\nu(\mathcal{H})]}\right)+\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})^{[k-\nu(\mathcal{H})]}\right),\\
\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[\nu(\mathcal{H})]}\right)+\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})^{[k-\nu(\mathcal{H})+1]}\right)-1\}.

Proof.

The condition
\nu(\mathcal{H})+1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})+\nu(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
implies that
J\neq\langle 0\rangle
. The claim that
I(\mathcal{H}\sqcup\mathcal{H}^{\prime})^{[k]}=J+K
follows immediately from definition. It is clear that for any monomial
f\in I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}
, we have
f/x\in I(\mathcal{H})^{[k-1]}
for any
x\in\mathrm{supp}(f)
. The results then follow from Lemma
3.7
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section. Before that, we recall the result
[
13
, Corollary 1.3]
on the regularity in the context of taking induced subgraphs. Note that while the authors stated the result for only simple graphs, their proof also works for any hypergraph. Thus, we have the following:

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph,
\mathcal{H}_{1}
an induced sub-hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
, and
k
a positive integer. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{[k]}\right)\leq\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right).

We first consider the special case when hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
are pairwise disjoint.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph such that any two hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
have an empty intersection. Then for any integer
k
where
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right)

\displaystyle=\max\{|V(M)|-|M|\colon\text{$M$ is a generalized $k$-admissible matching of $\mathcal{H}$}\}+k

\displaystyle=|V(\mathcal{H})|-|E(\mathcal{H})|+k.

Proof.

Set
E(\mathcal{H})=\{\mathcal{E}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{E}_{q}\}
, and for any
i\in[q]
, set
m_{i}=\prod_{x\in\mathcal{E}_{i}}x
. As any two hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
have empty intersection, the monomials
\{m_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,q}
have pairwise disjoint supports. In particular,
I(\mathcal{H})=(m_{1},\dots,m_{q})
is a complete intersection. It is straightforward that that
E(\mathcal{H})
itself is a generalized
k
-admissible matching for any
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
. Therefore, we have

\max\{|V(M)|-|M|\colon\text{$M$ is a $k$-admissible matching of $\mathcal{H}$}\}=|V(E(\mathcal{H}))|-q=\sum_{i=1}^{q}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-q.

It now suffices to show that

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-q+k,

by induction on
q
and
k
. If
q=1
, then we must have
k=1
. The result then follows immediately. On the other hand, if
k=1
, then as
I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}
is a complete intersection, it is known that

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right)=\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\deg m_{i}-(q-1)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-q+k,

as desired. We can now assume that
q,k\geq 2
.
Set
J\coloneqq m_{q}(m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k-1]}
and
K\coloneqq(m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k]}
. It is straightforward that
J\cap K=m_{q}(m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k]}
. By
Lemma

3.8
,
I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}=J+K
is a Betti splitting, and thus by
Definition

3.4
, we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right)=\max\{\mathrm{reg}\left(m_{q}(m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k-1]}\right),\quad\mathrm{reg}\left((m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k]}\right),\\
\mathrm{reg}\left(m_{q}(m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k]}\right)-1\}.

By the induction hypotheses, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}\left((m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k-1]}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-(q-1)+(k-1)=\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-q+k,

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}\left((m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k]}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-(q-1)+k=\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-q+k+1.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\max\{\mathrm{reg}\left(m_{q}\right)+\mathrm{reg}\left((m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k-1]}\right),\quad\mathrm{reg}\left((m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k]}\right),\\
\mathrm{reg}\left(m_{q}\right)+\mathrm{reg}\left((m_{1},\dots,m_{q-1})^{[k]}\right)-1\}\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{q}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-q+k,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-q+k+1,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{q}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-q+k\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{q}|\mathcal{E}_{i}|-q+k,

as desired. This concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph and
k
an integer where
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
. Then for any generalized
k
-admissible matching
M
of
\mathcal{H}
, we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right)\geq|V(M)|-|M|+k.

Consequently, we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right)\geq\max\{|V(M)|-|M|\colon\text{$M$ is a generalized $k$-admissible matching of $\mathcal{H}$}\}+k.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\mathcal{H}
has no isolated vertex. We induce on
k
and
|E(\mathcal{H})|
. If
k=1
, then the result follows from
[
23
, Corollary 3.9]
. If
\mathcal{H}
has one edge, then we must have
k=1
, and the result follows trivially. By induction, we can assume that
k\geq 2
and
\mathcal{H}
has at least two edges, and whenever
k^{\prime}<k
or
|E(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})|<|E(\mathcal{H})|
, we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})^{[k^{\prime}]}\right)\geq|V(M^{\prime})|-|M^{\prime}|+k^{\prime}

where
M^{\prime}
is any generalized
k^{\prime}
-admissible matching of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Now assume that
M=\sqcup_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}
is a generalized
k
-admissible matching of
\mathcal{H}
where the conditions in
Definition

3.1
hold. Set
a=|V(M)|-|M|
, and
a_{i}=|M_{i}|
for any
i\in[r]
. By
Lemma

3.9
, it suffices to show that

(2)

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}[V(M)])^{[k]}\right)\geq a+k.

Suppose that
r=1
. Then
k\leq|M|=a_{1}\leq k
, and thus
|M|=a_{1}=k
. Since the ideal
I(\mathcal{H}[V(M)])^{[k]}
is the principal ideal generated by the product of all variables in
M
, we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}[V(M)])^{[k]}\right)=|V(M)|=\left(|V(M)|-|M|\right)+|M|=a+k,

as desired. Now we can assume that
r\geq 2
. Due to condition (1) of
Definition

3.1
, the graph
\mathcal{H}[V(M)]
is exactly the disjoint union of the hypergraphs
\mathcal{H}[V(M_{i})]
where
i
ranges in
[r]
. Set
\mathcal{H}_{1}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}[V(M_{1})]
,
M^{\prime}=\sqcup_{i=2}^{r}M_{i}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{2}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}[V(M^{\prime})]=\sqcup_{i=2}^{r}\mathcal{H}[V(M_{i})]
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
share no vertex. If
a_{i}=1
for any
i\in[r]
, then
M
is a matching whose hyperedges are pairwise disjoint. The result then follows from
Proposition

3.10
. We can now assume that
a_{r}\geq 2
. We then have
k-\nu(H_{1})=k-a_{1}\geq\sum_{i=2}^{r}a_{i}-(r-1)>0
and
k\leq a_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{r}a_{i}=\nu(H_{1})+\nu(H_{2})
by conditions (2) and (3) of
Definition

3.1
. Thus by
Lemma

3.8
, we have

(3)

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}[V(M)])\right)^{[k]}\geq\max\{|V(M_{1})|+\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}_{2})^{[k-a_{1}]}\right),\quad|V(M_{1})|+\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}_{2})^{[k-a_{1}+1]}\right)-1\},

where we used the fact that
\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}_{1})^{[a_{1}]}\right)
is the principal ideal generated by a monomial of degree
|V(M_{1})|
, from condition (3) of
Definition

3.1
.
Recall that we have
|M|=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\leq r+k-1
. If
|M|<r+k-1
, then

|M^{\prime}|=\sum_{i=2}^{r}a_{i}=|M|-a_{1}<(r+k-1)-a_{1}=(r-1)+(k-a_{1}),

and hence

|M^{\prime}|\leq(r-1)+(k-a_{1})-1.

On the other hand, since
k\leq|M|=a_{1}+|M^{\prime}|
, we have
k-a_{1}\leq|M^{\prime}|
. Thus,
M^{\prime}=\sqcup_{i=2}^{r}M_{i}
is a generalized
(k-a_{1})
-admissible matching in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. By induction, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}_{2})^{[k-a_{1}]}\right)\geq(|V(M^{\prime})|-|M^{\prime}|)+(k-a_{1})

\displaystyle=(a-|V(M_{1})|+a_{1})+(k-a_{1})

\displaystyle=a-|V(M_{1})|+k.

Thus, by (
3
), we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}[V(M)])^{[k]}\right)\geq|V(M_{1})|+\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}_{2})^{[k-a_{1}]}\right)\geq|V(M_{1})|+\left(a-|V(M_{1})|+k\right)=a+k,

as desired. Now we can assume that
|M|=r+k-1
. We have

|M^{\prime}|=\sum_{i=2}^{r}a_{i}=|M|-a_{1}=(r+k-1)-a_{1}=(r-1)+(k-a_{1}+1)-1.

Since
r\geq 2
, we have

k-a_{1}+1\leq(r-2)+(k-a_{1}+1)=|M^{\prime}|=(r-1)+(k-a_{1}+1)-1.

Thus,
M^{\prime}=\sqcup_{i=2}^{r}M_{i}
is a generalized
(k-a_{1}+1)
-admissible matching in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. By induction, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}_{2})^{[k-a_{1}+1]}\right)\geq(|V(M^{\prime})|-|M^{\prime}|)+(k-a_{1}+1)

\displaystyle=(a-|V(M_{1})|+a_{1})+(k-a_{1}+1)

\displaystyle=a-|V(M_{1})|+k+1.

Thus, by (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}[V(M)])^{[k]}\right)

\displaystyle\geq|V(M_{1})|+\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H}_{2})^{[k-a_{1}+1]}\right)-1

\displaystyle\geq|V(M_{1})|+\left(a-|V(M_{1})|+k+1\right)-1

\displaystyle=a+k,

as desired. This concludes the proof.
∎

In particular, the lower bound in this result is sharp, as equality is achieved in the case where
I(\mathcal{H})
is a complete intersection (
Proposition

3.10
).

This lower bound can be related to more familiar concepts in the literature in the case of
d
-uniform hypergraphs and, in particular, simple graphs.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
d
-uniform hypergraph. For any
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
, we say a matching
M
of
\mathcal{H}
is a
k
-admissible matching
if there is a partition
M=M_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup M_{r}
such that

(1)

for any edge
\mathcal{E}\text{ in }\mathcal{H}[V(M)]
, we have
\mathcal{E}\in E(\mathcal{H}[V(M_{i})])
for some
i\in[r]
;

(2)

|M|\leq r+k-1
.

Now, we define the
k
-admissible matching number
of
\mathcal{H}
, denoted by
\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)
, as follows

\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)\coloneqq\max\{|M|\mid M\text{ is a $k$-admissible matching of $\mathcal{H}$}\}.

We note that our definition of
k
-admissible matching is a refinement of
[
15
, Definition 12]
in the case when
\mathcal{H}
is a simple graph
G
. Observe that for a simple graph
G
, the condition (1) in
Definition

3.12
is the same as saying that the edges
e_{i}\in M_{i}
and
e_{j}\in M_{j}
form a gap (in the sense of
[
15
]
) for any
i\neq j
. The only difference, therefore, is that Erey and Hibi considered an additional assumption, namely the induced subgraphs on
M_{i}
for each
i\in[r]
need to be forests.

The following facts are straightforward to verify; interested readers may look at the analogous results in
[
15
, Page 6]
.

Remark 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
d
-uniform hypergraph.

(1)

A matching
M
of the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is a
1
-admissible matching if and only if
M
is an induced matching of
\mathcal{H}
. Thus,
\nu_{1}(\mathcal{H})=\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},1)
.

(2)

If
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
, then every
k
-admissible matching of
\mathcal{H}
is also a
(k+1)
-admissible matching. In particular,

\nu_{1}(\mathcal{H})=\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},1)\leq\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},2)\leq\cdots\leq\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},\nu(\mathcal{H}))\leq\nu(\mathcal{H}).

(3)

For any
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
,
\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)\geq k
.

(4)

For any
2\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
,
\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)\leq\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k-1)+1
.

Remark 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
d
-uniform hypergraph. Then, one can verify that any
k
-admissible matching
M
of
\mathcal{H}
with
|M|\geq k
is actually a generalized
k
-admissible matching. For the computation of
\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)
, the following proposition suggests that it is enough to consider only generalized
k
-admissible matching of
\mathcal{H}
.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
d
-uniform hypergraph and
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
. Then

(d-1)\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)=\max\{|V(M)|-|M|\colon\text{$M$ is a generalized $k$-admissible matching of $\mathcal{H}$}\}.

Proof.

Note that if
M
is a
k
-admissible matching of
M
, then
M
is also a
k
-admissible matching, and
|V(M)|-|M|=(d-1)|M|
. Therefore, by
Remark

3.14
, to prove the equality, it is enough to show that if
M
is a
k
-admissible matching of
\mathcal{H}
such that
|M|=\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)
, then
|M|\geq k
. Evidently, since
k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
, there exists a matching
M^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{H}
such that
|M^{\prime}|=k
. Thus if
|M|<k
, then
|M|<|M^{\prime}|
, where
M^{\prime}=M_{1}^{\prime}
is a
k
-admissible matching of
\mathcal{H}
(by
Definition

3.12
), a contradiction.
∎

A straightforward and important consequence of our previous results is that we can use
\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)
to establish a lower bound for the regularity of square-free powers of
I(\mathcal{H})
, in the case
\mathcal{H}
is a
d
-uniform hypergraph.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
d
-uniform hypergraph and
k
an integer where
1\leq k\leq\nu(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(\mathcal{H})^{[k]}\right)\geq(d-1)\mathrm{aim}(\mathcal{H},k)+k.

Specifically, for a simple graph
G
and for any
1\leq k\leq\nu(G)
, we have

\mathrm{reg}\left(I(G)^{[k]}\right)\geq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k.

Proof.

The result follows immediately from
Theorem

3.11
and
Proposition

3.15
.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

To distinguish the notion of
k
-admissible matching by Erey and Hibi from the one defined here, we denote their
k
-admissible matching number by
\mathrm{aim}^{*}(G,k)
(In
[
15
, Definition 13]
, they denoted this number by
\mathrm{aim}(G,k)
). For a simple graph

\mathrm{aim}^{*}(G,k)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)
. Note that
\mathrm{aim}^{*}(G,k)=\mathrm{aim}(G,k)

is a forest. However, the difference can be arbitrarily large in the case of arbitrary graphs. For instance, if we consider

to be the complete graph on

\mathrm{aim}(G,k)=k
, whereas
\mathrm{aim}^{*}(G,k)=1
.

4.
Square-free powers of block graphs

In this section, we consider the family of block graphs and derive a combinatorial formula for the regularity of their square-free powers in terms of the admissible matching numbers. The class of block graphs is a natural generalization of forests. Thus, our formula can be seen as an extension of the regularity formula proved in
[
6
, Theorem 3.6]
for the square-free powers of forests.

A
block graph

G
is a chordal graph such that any two maximal cliques in
G
intersect in at most one common vertex. Before going to the proof of the regularity formula, we first introduce some notations and terminologies related to block graphs. Based on these, we are able to establish some structural properties of block graphs that will be helpful later in this section.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a block graph.

\bullet

A maximal clique in
G
is called a
block
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{G}
denote the set of all blocks of
G
.

\bullet

A block
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{G}
is said to be a
block neighbor
of
B\in\mathcal{B}_{G}
if
|V(B)\cap V(B^{\prime})|=1
. The set
\{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{G}\colon|V(B)\cap V(B^{\prime})|=1\}
is called the
block neighborhood
of
B
in
G
, and is denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{G}(B)
.

\bullet

A block
B\in\mathcal{B}_{G}
is said to be a
leaf block
if the number of free vertices in
B
is at least
|V(B)|-1
.

\bullet

A block
B\in\mathcal{B}_{G}
is said to be a
distant leaf block
if
B
is a leaf block and at most one block neighbor of
B
in
G
is not a leaf block.

\bullet

Let
G
be a block graph, and
B_{1},B_{2}
two different blocks in
G
. A
block path
between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
of length
n
is a collection of blocks
\mathcal{P}:D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{n}
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

D_{1}=B_{1}
,
D_{n}=B_{2}
;

(ii)

D_{i}\neq D_{j}
for each
i\neq j
;

(iii)

for each
i\neq j
,
V(D_{i})\cap V(D_{j})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
j=i-1
or
j=i+1
.

We explain the above terminologies in the following example.

Here, we remark that similar to trees, there exists a (unique) block path between any two blocks in a connected block graph, and there always exists a distant leaf block. These facts might be well-known in the literature on block graphs, but we provide proofs below for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a connected block graph. Then, between any two different blocks in
G
, there is a unique block path.

Proof.

Let
B,B^{\prime}
be two different blocks in
G
. Let

\Sigma=\{P\colon a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\mid a_{1}\in V(B),a_{n}\in V(B^{\prime}),P\text{ is an induced path in }G\}.

Choose some
P\colon a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\Sigma
of the shortest length. Then for each
i\in[n-1]
, there exist distinct
D_{i+1}\in\mathcal{B}_{G}
such that
D_{i+1}\neq D_{i}
for each
i
, and
\{a_{i},a_{i+1}\}\in E(D_{i+1})
. Thus, the collection of blocks
B_{1}=D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{n-1},D_{n}=B^{\prime}
satisfy conditions (i) and (ii) in the definition of block path. Moreover, if condition (iii) is not satisfied, then
P
cannot be of the shortest length. Thus, we have a block path between
B
and
B^{\prime}
.

To prove the uniqueness of such a block path, suppose there are two block paths
\mathcal{P}:D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}:D_{1}^{\prime},D_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,D_{m}^{\prime}
, where
D_{1}=D_{1}^{\prime}=B
and
D_{n}=D_{m}^{\prime}=B^{\prime}
. If
n=2
, then using condition (iii) in the definition of block paths, we see that
m=2
, and thus
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. Therefore, we may assume that
m,n\geq 3
. Let
V(D_{i})\cap V(D_{i+1})=\{x_{i}\}
for each
i\in[n-1]
and
V(D_{j}^{\prime})\cap V(D_{j+1}^{\prime})=\{y_{j}\}
for each
j\in[m-1]
. First, consider the case when
x_{1}\neq y_{1}
. Suppose
2\leq i\leq n
and
2\leq j\leq m
are the smallest possible integers such that
V(D_{i})\cap V(D_{j}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. Let
V(D_{i})\cap V(D_{j}^{\prime})=\{z\}
for some
z\in V(G)
. Then the vertices in
W=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},z,y_{j-1},\ldots,y_{1}\}
forms a (not necessarily induced) cycle of length at least
3
in
G
such that each edge of this cycle belongs to different blocks. Let
H
denote the induced subgraph on
W
. Then
H
contains an induced cycle
C_{t}
. Since
G
is chordal, we must have
t=3
. Observe that at least two edges of this cycle belong to different blocks, and hence, each edge is in a different block. Consequently, the block containing this cycle intersects a different block along an edge, a contradiction to the fact that
G
is a block graph. Thus, we have
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. The case
x_{1}=y_{1}
can be dealt in a similar way.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a block graph. Then,
G
contains a distant leaf block.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}\colon D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{n}
be a block path of the maximum possible length in
G
. We proceed to show that
D_{1}
is a distant leaf block in
G
. If
n=1
, then
G
is a disjoint union of complete graphs, and it is easy to see that
D_{1}=D_{n}
is a distant leaf block in
G
. Now, suppose
n\geq 2
and
V(D_{j})\cap V(D_{j+1})=\{x_{j}\}
for each
j\geq 1
. Let
V(D_{1})=\{x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
for some
m\geq 1
. Our aim now is to show that
y_{i}
is a free vertex in
G
for each
i\geq 1
. On the contrary, suppose
y_{1}
is not a free vertex in
G
. Then there exists some
D\in\mathcal{B}_{G}
such that
D\neq D_{j}
for each
j\in[n]
and
V(D)\cap V(D_{1})=\{y_{1}\}
. We proceed to show that
V(D)\cap V(D_{j})=\emptyset
for each
j>1
. If not, suppose
j>1
is the smallest integer such that
V(D)\cap V(D_{j})=\{z\}
for some
z\in V(G)
. Note that
z\neq x_{i-1}
since
|V(D)\cap V(D_{1})|=1
, and
V(D)\cap V(D_{t})=\emptyset
for each
t<j
. Now the vertices in
W=\{y_{1},x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},z\}
form a (not necessarily induced) cycle of length at least
3
in
G
such that each edge of this cycle belongs to a different block. Thus, proceeding as in the proof of
Lemma

4.3
, we arrive at a contradiction, and hence,
V(D)\cap V(D_{i})=\emptyset
for each
i>1
. Consequently,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\colon D,D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{n}
is a block path of length
n+1
in
G
, a contradiction to the fact that
\mathcal{P}
is a block path of maximum possible length in
G
. Hence,
y_{1}
must be a free vertex of
G
, and consequently,
D_{1}
is a leaf block.

To prove
D_{1}
is a distant leaf block, let us consider
B\in\mathcal{N}_{G}(D_{1})
such that
B\neq D_{2}
. In this case, we have
V(B)\cap V(D_{1})=\{x_{1}\}
. Let
V(B)=\{x_{1},z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\}
for some
r\geq 1
. Proceeding as in the previous paragraph, we see that
V(B)\cap V(D_{i})=\emptyset
for each
i>2
. Thus,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}\colon B,D_{2},\ldots,D_{n}
is also a block path of maximum possible length in
G
. Then, from the arguments in the previous paragraph, we see that
z_{r}
is a free vertex in
G
for each
r\geq 1
. Hence,
B
is a leaf block, and consequently,
D_{1}
is a distant leaf block in
G
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{P}:D_{1},D_{2},\ldots,D_{n}
be a block path in a block graph
G
of the maximum possible length. Let
B\in\mathcal{N}_{G}(D_{2})\setminus\{D_{3}\}
such that
V(B)\cap V(D_{2})\neq V(D_{2})\cap V(D_{3})
. Then,
B
is a distant leaf block in
G
.

Proof.

Let
V(D_{i})\cap V(D_{i+1})=\{x_{i}\}
for each
i\geq 2
. Let
V(B)\cap V(D_{2})=\{z\}
. By the given hypothesis, we have
z\neq x_{2}
. Arguing as in
Lemma

4.4
, we see that
V(B)\cap V(D_{i})=\emptyset
for each
i>2
. Hence,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}:B,D_{2},\ldots,D_{n}
is a block path of the maximum possible length in
G
. Therefore, by the proof of
Lemma

4.4
, we see that
B
is a distant leaf block in
G
.
∎

Some analogs of the next two results can be obtained for any arbitrary graph, but here, we state and prove them for block graphs only.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
G
be a block graph and
B\in\mathcal{B}_{G}
. Let
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{l}\}\subseteq V(B)
for some
l\geq 1
, and
H
denote the induced subgraph
G\setminus\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{l}\}
. Then for each
2\leq k\leq\nu(G)
, we have
\mathrm{aim}(H,k-1)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)-1
if one of the following holds.

(i)

For some
i\in[l]
,
|\mathcal{N}_{G}[u_{i}]\setminus V(B)|\geq 1
, and for each
v\in\mathcal{N}_{G}[u_{i}]\setminus V(B)
,
\deg(v)=1
.

(ii)

l\geq 2
, and
u_{1},u_{2}
are two distinct free vertices in
G
.

Proof.

First, let us assume that condition (i) holds. Let
\mathcal{N}_{G}[u_{i}]\setminus V(B)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}
for some
r\geq 1
. Let
M=\sqcup_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}
be a
(k-1)
-admissible matching of
H
such that
|M|=\mathrm{aim}(H,k-1)
. Since
B
is a block in
G
, we see that
n\coloneqq|\{i\mid V(M_{i})\cap V(B)\neq\emptyset\}|\leq 1
. If
n=0
, then it is easy to see that
M^{\prime}=\sqcup_{i=1}^{r+1}M_{i}^{\prime}
, where
M_{i}^{\prime}=M_{i}
for each
i\in[r]
, and
M_{r+1}^{\prime}=\{\{u_{i},v_{1}\}\}
, forms a
k
-admissible matching of
G
. Now, suppose
n=1
and without loss of generality, let
V(M_{r})\cap V(B)\neq\emptyset
. In this case,
M^{\prime}=\sqcup_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}^{\prime}
, where
M_{i}^{\prime}=M_{i}
for each
i\in[r-1]
, and
M_{r}^{\prime}=M_{r}\cup\{\{u_{i},v_{1}\}\}
, forms a
k
-admissible matching of
G
. Thus, combining both cases, we obtain
\mathrm{aim}(H,k-1)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)-1
.

For (ii), working with the edge
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
similar to
\{u_{i},v_{1}\}
above and proceeding accordingly we also obtain
\mathrm{aim}(H,k-1)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)-1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
G
be a block graph and
B\in\mathcal{B}_{G}
such that
u_{1},u_{2}\in V(B)
are two distinct free vertices in
G
. If
H=G\setminus V(B)
, then for each
1\leq k\leq\nu(G)
, we have
\mathrm{aim}(H,k)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)-1
.

Proof.

Let
M=\sqcup_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}
be a
k
-admissible matching of
H
such that
|M|=\mathrm{aim}(H,k)
. Then
M^{\prime}=\sqcup_{i=1}^{r+1}M_{i}^{\prime}
, where
M_{i}^{\prime}=M_{i}
for each
i\in[r]
, and
M_{r+1}^{\prime}=\{\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\}
, forms a
k
-admissible matching of
G
. Thus
\mathrm{aim}(H,k)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)-1
.
∎

Now, we will introduce the notion of a special block in a block graph, which plays a crucial role in proving our main theorem in this section. Let
G
be a block graph with
B\in\mathcal{B}_{G}
and
u\in V(B)
. Define
\mathcal{N}_{G}(B,u)\coloneqq\{D\in\mathcal{B}_{G}\mid V(D)\cap V(B)=\{u\}\}
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let

be a block graph, and

Type II

\mathcal{N}_{G}(B,u_{i})=\emptyset

Type III

\mathcal{N}_{G}(B,u_{i})\neq\emptyset

\mathcal{N}_{G}(B,u_{i})\neq\emptyset
for some

Notation 4.11
.

Let
B
be a special block in a block graph
G
. Let
V(B)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}\}
, and for each

, either
\mathcal{N}_{G}(B,u_{i})=\emptyset
or if for some

Then

is a collection of blocks in

I_{S,B}=\langle xy\mid x,y\in V(D)\text{ for some }D\in S\rangle.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
G
be a block graph on
n
vertices. Then, for each
1\leq k\leq\nu(G)
,

(i)

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k
.

(ii)

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]}+I_{S,B})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k
, where
B
is a special block in
G
, and
I_{S,B}
is the ideal associated to any
S\subseteq\Lambda
as described in
Notation

4.11
.

Consequently, for a block graph
G
, we have
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k
.

Proof.

We prove (1) and (2) simultaneously by induction on
n
. By
Lemma

4.10
,
G
contains at least one special block. Let
B
be a special block in
G
, and
I_{S,B}
is the ideal defined as in
Notation

4.11
, where
S\subseteq\Lambda
. Now, if
n\leq 3
, then
k=1
, and thus it easy to see that

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[1]}+I_{S,B})=\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[1]})=\nu(G)+1=\mathrm{aim}(G,1)+1.

Therefore, we may assume that
n\geq 4
. For such an
n
, the case
k=1
follows from
[

, Theorem 6.8]
since
I(G)^{[1]}+I_{S,B}=I(G)^{[1]}
and
\nu(G)=\mathrm{aim}(G,1)
. Therefore, we can assume
k\geq 2
.

Case I:

B
is a special block of Type I in
G
. In this case,
B
is a connected component of
G
, and either
B
is an isolated vertex in
G
or
B\cong K_{2}
. In both cases, we see that
\Lambda=\emptyset
, and
I_{S,B}=\langle 0\rangle
. Now if
B
is an isolated vertex of
G
, then by the induction hypothesis and by
Proposition

3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{reg}(I(G\setminus V(B))^{[k]})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{aim}(G\setminus V(B),k)+k

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k.

Now, suppose
B\cong K_{2}
and
V(B)=\{u,v\}
. Then,

(4)

\begin{split}\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[k]}+\langle uv\rangle):u)&=\mathrm{reg}(I(G\setminus u)^{[k]}+\langle v\rangle)\\
&\leq\mathrm{aim}(G\setminus u,k)+k\\
&\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k-1,\end{split}

where the equality follows from
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
, and the first and second inequalities follow from the induction hypothesis and
Proposition

3.3
, respectively. Next,

(5)

\begin{split}\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]}+\langle uv\rangle+\langle u\rangle)&=\mathrm{reg}((I(G\setminus u)^{[k]}+\langle u\rangle)\\
&\leq\mathrm{aim}(G\setminus u,k)+k\\
&\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k\end{split}

where, as before, the equality follows from
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
, and the first and second inequalities follow from the induction hypothesis and
Proposition

3.3
, respectively. Now using inequalities (
4
), (
5
), and
Lemma

2.1
together, we obtain

(6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]}+\langle uv\rangle)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k.

Next, by
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
,
(I(G)^{[k]}:uv)=I(G\setminus\{u,v\})^{[k-1]}
. Observe that both
u
and
v
are free vertices of
G
. Therefore, using the induction hypothesis, and
Proposition

4.6

\{v_{2},v_{n-2}\}\in E(\widetilde{G})\setminus E(G)
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\{x,v_{n-2}\},\{y,v_{2}\}\in E(G)
. Then, by
Lemma

5.2
, we have
\{x,v_{n-1}\},\{y,v_{1}\}\in E(G)
. Now, by similar arguments as before, we have
\widetilde{G}[\{v_{2},v_{3},v_{n-1},v_{n}\}]\cong C_{4}
and thus the induced subgraph
\widetilde{G}[\{v_{2},v_{3},v_{n-1},v_{n}\}]
of
\widetilde{G}
must have a new edge since
G
is chordal. If the new edge is
\{v_{3},v_{n-1}\}
, then we have either
\{x,v_{3}\}\in E(G)
or
\{y,v_{3}\}\in E(G)
. However,
\{x,v_{3}\}\in E(G)
implies that
\{v_{2},v_{3}\}\in E(\widetilde{G})
, a contradiction to the fact that
(\widetilde{G})^{c}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]\cong C_{n}
. Similarly,
\{y,v_{3}\}\in E(G)
together with
Lemma

5.2
imply
\{x,v_{n}\}\in E(G)
, and consequently,
\{v_{1},v_{n}\}\in E(\widetilde{G})
; again a contradiction. Therefore,
\{v_{3},v_{n-1}\}
cannot be the new edge. On the other hand,
\{x,v_{n}\}\notin E(G)
since
\{v_{1},v_{n}\}\notin E(\widetilde{G})
. Similarly,
\{y,v_{n}\}\notin E(G)
since
\{v_{n-1},v_{n}\}\notin E(\widetilde{G})
. Consequently, both
\{v_{2},v_{n}\}
and
\{v_{3},v_{n}\}
cannot be the new edge. Lastly, if
\{v_{2},v_{n-1}\}
is the new edge, then by
Lemma

5.2
,
\{x,v_{n}\}\in E(G)
, and consequently,
\{v_{1},v_{n}\}\in E(\widetilde{G})
, again a contradiction.

Case III:

\{v_{1},v_{n-2}\}\in E(\widetilde{G})\setminus E(G)
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\{x,v_{1}\},\{y,v_{n-2}\}\in E(G)
. Then, by
Lemma

5.2
, we have
\{x,v_{2}\},\{y,v_{n-1}\}\in E(G)
. Again, as before, we have
\widetilde{G}[\{v_{2},v_{3},v_{n-1},v_{n}\}]\cong C_{4}
, and since
G
is chordal, the induced subgraph
\widetilde{G}[\{v_{2},v_{3},v_{n-1},v_{n}\}]
of
\widetilde{G}
must have a new edge. If the new edge is
\{v_{3},v_{n-1}\}
, then either
\{x,v_{3}\}\in E(G)
or
\{y,v_{3}\}\in E(G)
. However,
\{y,v_{3}\}\in E(G)
implies
\{v_{2},v_{3}\}\in E(\widetilde{G})
, a contradiction to the fact that
(\widetilde{G})^{c}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]\cong C_{n}
. Similarly,
\{x,v_{3}\}\in E(G)
together with
Lemma

5.2
imply
\{y,v_{n}\}\in E(G)
, and consequently,
\{v_{1},v_{n}\}\in E(\widetilde{G})
; again a contradiction. Therefore,
\{v_{3},v_{n-1}\}
cannot be the new edge. On the other hand,
\{x,v_{n}\}\notin E(G)
since
\{v_{n-1},v_{n}\}\notin E(\widetilde{G})
. Similarly,
\{y,v_{n}\}\notin E(G)
since
\{v_{1},v_{n}\}\notin E(\widetilde{G})
. Consequently, both
\{v_{2},v_{n}\}
and
\{v_{3},v_{n}\}
cannot be the new edge. Lastly, if
\{v_{2},v_{n-1}\}
is the new edge, then again by
Lemma

5.2
,
\{y,v_{n}\}\in E(G)
, and consequently,
\{v_{1},v_{n}\}\in E(\widetilde{G})
, again a contradiction.

Case IV:

\{v_{2},v_{n-1}\}\in E(\widetilde{G})\setminus E(G)
. This case is similar to Case III above in terms of symmetry.

Thus, we conclude that
\widetilde{G}
is a weakly chordal graph. ∎

In the next four lemmas, we establish various relationships between the admissible matchings of the graphs
G
and
\widetilde{G}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\{x,y\}
be an edge of a chordal graph
G
and let
(I(G)^{[2]}:xy)=I(\widetilde{G})
. If
M
is an induced matching of
\widetilde{G}
, then one of the following holds:

(i)

V(M)\cap N_{G}(x)=\emptyset
;

(ii)

V(M)\cap N_{G}(y)=\emptyset
;

(iii)

|\{e\in M\mid N_{G}(x,y)\cap e\neq\emptyset\}|=1
.

Proof.

We prove that if (i) and (ii) do not hold, then (iii) holds. Let
X=\{e\in M\mid N_{G}(x,y)\cap e\neq\emptyset\}
. If (i) and (ii) do not hold, then
|X|\geq 1
. Suppose if possible,
|X|\geq 2
, and
e_{1},e_{2}\in X
. Observe that
e_{1}\cap e_{2}=\emptyset
since
e_{1},e_{2}\in M
. Without loss of generality, let us assume that
e_{1}\cap N_{G}(x)\neq\emptyset
, and
a\in e_{1}\cap N_{G}(x)
, for some
a\in V(G)
. If
e_{2}\cap N_{G}(y)\neq\emptyset
, then for each
b\in e_{2}\cap N_{G}(y)
,
\{a,b\}\in E(\widetilde{G})
, a contradiction to the fact that
e_{1},e_{2}\in M
. Thus we must have
e_{2}\cap N_{G}(y)=\emptyset
. In this case,
e_{2}\cap N_{G}(x)\neq\emptyset
, and let
c\in e_{2}\cap N_{G}(x)
. Since (ii) does not hold, there exists some
e\in M\setminus\{e_{2}\}
such that
e\cap N_{G}(y)\neq\emptyset
. Let
e\cap N_{G}(y)=\{d\}
for some
d\in V(G)
. Consequently,
\{c,d\}\in E(\widetilde{G})
, again a contradiction. Thus, (iii) holds.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
G
be a Cohen-Macaulay chordal graph with
V(G)=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\sqcup\dots\sqcup W_{m}
such that for each
i\in[m]
,
G[W_{i}]\cong K_{t_{i}}
, where
t_{i}\geq 2
. Let
x\in W_{i}
such that
W_{i}\setminus\{x\}
contains at least one free vertex of
G
. Let
y\in N_{G}[x]\setminus W_{i}
and
(I(G)^{[2]}:xy)=I(\widetilde{G})
. Then
\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)-1
.

Proof.

Let
M
be an induced matching of
\widetilde{G}
such that
|M|=\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})
. Then, by
Lemma

5.4
, one of the following holds.

(i)

V(M)\cap N_{G}(x)=\emptyset
;

(ii)

V(M)\cap N_{G}(y)=\emptyset
;

(iii)

|\{e\in M\mid N_{G}(x,y)\cap e\neq\emptyset\}|=1
.

It is easy to see that if (i) or (ii) holds, then
M\subseteq E(G)
, and if (iii) holds, then
|M\setminus E(G)|\leq 1
. By the given hypothesis, we have
y\in W_{j}
for some
j\neq i
. In this case, there exists some
v\in W_{j}\setminus\{y\}
such that
v
is a free vertex of
G
. Note that,
W_{i}\setminus\{x\}
also contains a free vertex of
G
, say
u
. If (i) holds, then one can observe that
M^{\prime}=M\cup\{\{x,u\}\}
is an induced matching of
G
, and if (ii) holds, then
M^{\prime\prime}=M\cup\{\{y,v\}\}
is an induced matching of
G
. Consequently, in both cases we have
\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})\leq\nu_{1}(G)-1\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)-1
by
Remark

3.13
(5). Now, suppose (iii) holds. In this case, suppose
M=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
and
e_{1}\in M
such that
e_{1}\cap N_{G}(x,y)\neq\emptyset
. Then for each
f\in M\setminus\{e_{1}\}
,
f,\{x,u\}
and
f,\{y,v\}
both forms a gap, since
N_{G}(u,v)\subseteq N_{G}(x,y)
. Thus if we take
M_{1}=\{\{x,u\},\{y,v\}\}
and
M_{i}=\{e_{i}\}
for
i>1
, then
\sqcup_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}
forms a
2
-admissible matching of
G
. Consequently, in this case too we have
\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)-1
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
G
be a Cohen-Macaulay chordal graph with
V(G)=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\sqcup\dots\sqcup W_{m}
such that for each
i\in[m]
,
G[W_{i}]\cong K_{t_{i}}
, where
t_{i}\geq 2
. Let
x\in W_{i}
be a free vertex of
G
such that
W_{i}\setminus\{x\}
contains another free vertex of
G
. Let
y\in W_{i}\setminus\{x\}
and
(I(G)^{[2]}:xy)=I(\widetilde{G})
. Then
\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)-1
.

Proof.

Let
M
be an induced matching of
\widetilde{G}
such that
|M|=\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})
. We have the following two cases:

Case I:

y
is a free vertex of
G
. In this case,
N_{G}[x]=N_{G}[y]
and
M\subseteq E(G)
. Thus by
Lemma

5.4
, either
V(M)\cap N_{G}[x]=V(M)\cap N_{G}[y]=\emptyset
or
|\{e\in M\mid N_{G}(x,y)\cap e\neq\emptyset\}|=1
. If
V(M)\cap N_{G}[x]=V(M)\cap N_{G}[y]=\emptyset
, then
M^{\prime}=M\cup\{\{x,y\}\}
is an induced matching of
G
so that
\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)-1
, by
Remark

3.13
. Now suppose
|\{e\in M\mid N_{G}(x,y)\cap e\neq\emptyset\}|=1
. Let
M=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
and
e_{1}\in M
such that
e_{1}\cap N_{G}(x,y)\neq\emptyset
. Then for each
f\in M\setminus\{e_{1}\}
, the two edges
f
,
\{x,y\}
form a gap. Hence one can take
M_{1}=\{e_{1},\{x,y\}\}
, and
M_{i}=\{e_{i}\}
for
i>1
so that
\sqcup_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}
forms a
2
-admissible matching of
G
. Consequently,
\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)-1
.

Case II:

y
is not a free vertex of
G
. In this case, by assumption, there exists some
z\in W_{i}\setminus\{x,y\}
such that
z
is also a free vertex of
G
. Observe that
\{x,z\},\{y,z\}\in E(G)
, and
N_{G}[z]=N_{G}[x]\subseteq N_{G}[y]
. Now, by
Lemma

5.4
, we see that one of the following holds.

(i)

V(M)\cap N_{G}(x)=\emptyset
;

(ii)

V(M)\cap N_{G}(y)=\emptyset
;

(iii)

|\{e\in M\mid N_{G}(x,y)\cap e\neq\emptyset\}|=1
.

As before, if (i) or (ii) holds, then
M\subseteq E(G)
, and if (iii) holds, then
|M\setminus E(G)|\leq 1
. Now, if (i) holds, then one can observe that
N^{\prime}=M\cup\{\{x,z\}\}
is an induced matching of
G
, and if (ii) holds, then
N^{\prime\prime}=M\cup\{\{y,z\}\}
is an induced matching of
G
. Consequently, in both cases we have
\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)-1
, by
Remark

3.13
(5). Now, suppose (iii) holds. In this case, let
M=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
and
e_{1}\in M
such that
e_{1}\cap N_{G}(x,y)\neq\emptyset
. Since
N_{G}[x]\subseteq N_{G}[y]
, we see that
e_{1}\cap N_{G}(y)\neq\emptyset
. Moreover, for each
f\in M\setminus\{e_{1}\}
, we see that
f,\{x,y\}
form a gap. Now, if
e_{1}\in E(G)
, then take
M_{1}=\{e_{1},\{x,y\}\}
,
M_{i}=\{e_{i}\}
for
i>1
, and observe that
\sqcup_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}
forms a
2
-admissible matching of
G
. Hence,
\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)-1
. If
e_{1}\notin E(G)
, then
e_{1}=\{p,q\}
, where
p\in W_{i}\setminus\{x,y\}
,
q\in W_{l}
for some
l\neq i
, and
\{y,q\}\in E(G)
. Thus one can take
N_{1}=\{\{x,p\},\{y,q\}\},N_{i}=\{e_{i}\}
for
i>1
, and observe that
\sqcup_{i=1}^{r}N_{i}
forms a
2
-admissible matching of
G
so that
\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)-1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
G
be any graph and
x,y\in V(G)
such that
y\in N_{G}(x)
and
N_{G}[y]\subseteq N_{G}[x]
. If
H
denotes the graph
G\setminus N_{G}[x]
, then for each
k\geq 1
, we have
\mathrm{aim}(H,k)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)-1
.

Proof.

Let
M=\sqcup_{i=1}^{r}M_{i}
be a
k
-admissible matching of
H
such that
|M|=\mathrm{aim}(H,k)
. Then for each
e\in M
,
e\cap N_{G}(x,y)=\emptyset
since
N_{G}[y]\subseteq N_{G}[x]
. Hence
M^{\prime}=\sqcup_{i=1}^{r+1}M_{i}^{\prime}
is a
k
-admissible matching of
G
, where
M_{i}^{\prime}=M_{i}
for each
i\in[r]
, and
M_{r+1}^{\prime}=\{\{x,y\}\}
. Thus
\mathrm{aim}(H,k)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)-1
.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
G
be a Cohen-Macaulay chordal graph with the matching number at least
2
. Then

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2.

Proof.

By
Theorem

3.16
, we have
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]})\geq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2
. To show
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2
, we use induction on
|V(G)|
. Since
G
has matching number at least
2
, we have
|V(G)|\geq 4
. Now, if
|V(G)|=4
, then
I(G)^{[2]}
is a principal ideal, and thus it is easy to see that
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2
. Now suppose
|V(G)|\geq 5
. Let
V(G)=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\sqcup\dots\sqcup W_{m}
such that for each
i\in[m]
,
G[W_{i}]\cong K_{t_{i}}
, where
t_{i}\geq 2
. Then we have the following two cases:

Case I:

G
is not a disjoint union of complete graphs. Then there exists some
i\in[m]
and
x\in W_{i}
such that
N_{G}(x)\setminus W_{i}\neq\emptyset
. Without loss of generality, let
i=1
. Moreover, let
W_{1}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\}
for some
r\geq 2
such that
x_{r}
is a free vertex of
G
, and
N_{G}(x_{1})\setminus W_{1}\neq\emptyset
. Suppose
N_{G}(x_{1})\setminus W_{1}=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{s}\}
.

Claim 1:
For each
l\in[s-1]\cup\{0\}
,

\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{l}\rangle):x_{1}y_{l+1})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2),

where
I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{l}\rangle=I(G)^{[2]}
in case
l=0
.

Proof of Claim 1
: Let
G_{l}=G\setminus\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}\}
. Then, it is easy to see that
G_{l}
is a Cohen-Macaulay chordal graph. Now,

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{l}\rangle):x_{1}y_{l+1})=\mathrm{reg}(I(\widetilde{G_{l}}))

\displaystyle=\nu_{1}(\widetilde{G_{l}})+1

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{aim}(G_{l},2)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2),

where
\widetilde{G_{l}}
is a weakly chordal graph (by
Proposition

5.3
), the first equality follows from
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
, the second equality follows from
Remark

3.13
, the first inequality follows from
Lemma

5.5
, and the second inequality follows from
Proposition

3.3
. This completes the proof of Claim 1.

Claim 2:
For each
p\in[r-1]
,

\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s},x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{p}\rangle):x_{1}x_{p+1})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2),

where
I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s},x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{p}\rangle=I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s}\rangle
in case
p=1
.

Proof of Claim 2
: Let
H_{p}=G\setminus\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{s},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p}\}
. Then again, it is easy to see that
H_{p}
is a Cohen-Macaulay chordal graph. Moreover, both
x_{1}
and
x_{r}
are free vertices of
H_{p}
. Now,

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s},x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{p}\rangle):x_{1}x_{p+1})=\mathrm{reg}(I(\widetilde{H_{p}}))

\displaystyle=\nu_{1}(\widetilde{H_{p}})+1

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{aim}(H_{p},2)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2),

where
\widetilde{H_{p}}
is a weakly chordal graph (by
Proposition

5.3
), the equality follows
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
, the second equality follows from
Remark

3.13
, the first inequality follows from
Lemma

5.6
, and the second inequality follows from
Proposition

3.3
. This completes the proof of Claim 2.

Let
\Gamma=G\setminus N_{G}[x_{1}]
. Then
\Gamma
is a Cohen-Macaulay chordal graph. Moreover,

(17)

\begin{split}\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s},x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{r}\rangle):x_{1})&=\mathrm{reg}(I(\Gamma)^{[2]}+\langle y_{1},\ldots,y_{s},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r}\rangle)\\
&\leq\mathrm{aim}(\Gamma,2)+2\\
&\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+1,\end{split}

where the equality again follows from
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
, the first inequality follows from the induction hypothesis, and the second inequality follows from
Lemma

5.7
.

Next, we have

(18)

\begin{split}\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s},x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{r},x_{1}\rangle)&=\mathrm{reg}(I(G\setminus x_{1})^{[2]})\\
&\leq\mathrm{aim}(G\setminus x_{1},2)+2\\
&\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2,\end{split}

where the first and second inequalities follow from the induction hypothesis and
Proposition

3.3
, respectively.

Now, using
Lemma

2.1
along with inequalities (
17
) and (
18
), we get

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s},x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{r}\rangle)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2.

Applying
Lemma

2.1
with the above inequality and the inequality proved in Claim 2, we get

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s},x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{r-1}\rangle)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2.

By a repeated use of Claim 2 and
Lemma

2.1
, we get
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s}\rangle)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2
. Now, from Claim 1 we have
\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s-1}\rangle):x_{1}y_{s})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)
. Thus using
Lemma

2.1
again we obtain
\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{1}y_{s-1}\rangle)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2
. By a repeated use of Claim 1 and
Lemma

2.1
this time, we finally get
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]}\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2
, as desired.

Case II:

G
is a disjoint union of complete graphs. In this case, let
W_{1}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\}
for some
r\geq 2
. Then, proceeding as in Case I above, we obtain the following:

(i)

For each
p\in[r-1]
,
\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{p}\rangle):x_{1}x_{p+1})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)
, where
I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{p}\rangle=I(G)^{[2]}
in case
p=1
;

(ii)

\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{r}\rangle):x_{1})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+1
;

(iii)

\mathrm{reg}((I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{r},x_{1}\rangle)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2
.

Now, using
Lemma

2.1
along with (ii) and (iii) above, we have
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]}+\langle x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{r}\rangle)\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2
. Thus, as before, by a repeated use of
Lemma

2.1
and (i) above we obtain
\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]})\leq\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Concluding remarks and some open problems

Let
I
be a (not necessarily squarefree) monomial ideal and
k
a positive integer. Erey and Ficarra
[
12
]
recently defined the
k
-mathching power
of
I
to be the ideals generated by
m_{1}m_{2}\cdots m_{k}
where
m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{k}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
have pairwise-disjoint supports. They denoted the
k
-th matching power of
I
by
I^{[k]}
as it is clear from the definition that if
I
is square-free, then the
k
-th matching power of
I
is exactly its
k
-th square-free power. Recall from
[
27
, cf. Exercise 6.4.33]
that
\mathrm{reg}I^{\mathcal{P}}=\mathrm{reg}I
, where
I^{\mathcal{P}}
, a square-free monomial ideal, denotes the
polarization
of
I
. By
[
12
, Proposition 1.4]
, for any monomial ideal
I
and integer
k
, we have
\left(I^{[k]}\right)^{\mathcal{P}}=\left(I^{\mathcal{P}}\right)^{[k]}
. Thus, one can use

For any graph

In the context of square-free powers, it was proved in

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})\geq\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k

\mathrm{aim}(G,1)=\nu_{1}(G)

\mathrm{aim}(G,1)+1

G
is one of the above graphs. Hence, analogous to ordinary powers, we ask the following.

Question 6.1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph such that
\mathrm{reg}(I(G))=\nu_{1}(G)+1

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k
for any
1\leq k\leq\nu(G)

We have an affirmative answer to the above question for block graphs, as seen in
Theorem

4.12

G

Theorem

5.8

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[2]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,2)+2

8

Question

6.1
has a positive answer. Based on all these, we conjecture the following.

Conjecture 6.2
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph. Then, for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(G)

\mathrm{reg}(I(G)^{[k]})=\mathrm{aim}(G,k)+k.

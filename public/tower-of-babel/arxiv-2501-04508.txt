Linear Model of Aggregated Homogeneous Energy Storage Elements with Realizable Dispatch Guarantees
Mazen Elsaadany, Mads R. Almassalkhi, Simon H. Tindemans

Abstract
To optimize the dispatch of batteries, a model is required that can predict the state of energy (SOE) trajectory for a chosen open-loop power schedule to ensure admissibility (i.e., that schedule can be realized). However, battery dispatch optimization is inherently challenging since batteries cannot simultaneously charge and discharge, which begets a non-convex complementarity constraint. In this paper, we develop a novel composition of energy storage elements that can charge or discharge independently and provide a sufficient linear energy storage model of the composite battery. This permits convex optimization of the composite battery SOE trajectory while guaranteeing admissibility of the resulting (aggregated) power schedule and its disaggregation to the individual energy storage elements.



I
Introduction

Battery energy storage systems (BESS) are increasingly deployed to help facilitate the grid’s uptake of variable renewable energy and improve reliability
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

In many applications, BESS dispatch involves coordinating large numbers of energy storage elements. Examples include battery racks that together comprise a single BESS asset, BESS container assets in a utility-scale battery storage facility (e.g., AC-coupled elements as shown in Fig
1
), or aggregated BESS units coordinated as a virtual power plant (VPP).

Optimally scheduling the charging and discharging of storage elements requires solving an optimization problem. Because most storage technologies do not permit simultaneous charging and discharging,
complementarity constraints
are usually required, which force the product of the charging and discharging power to be equal to zero
at each time step, and for each element
. This results in a non-convex problem that is often implemented using binary variables
[
4
,
5
]
. The non-convexity of the composite BESS dispatch can present a challenge when incorporating BESSs into more complex, large-scale power system optimization problems, such as optimal power flow (OPF) and economic dispatch, which can greatly increase computational complexity
[
6
]
.

Fig. 1:
Example of a composite battery with AC-coupled battery elements.

This motivates the need for a convex BESS model that can be incorporated in different applications without additional complexity. To obtain a convex relaxation of the dispatch problem, the complementarity constraint is typically omitted
[
5
]
. At first glance, simultaneous charging and discharging may appear as an unnecessary dissipation of energy due to charging/discharging inefficiencies. Nevertheless, there are cases where simultaneous charging and discharging is a rational outcome of a battery dispatch schedule.
For example, this may be the case when energy prices are negative
[
7
]
, or as byproduct of minimum up- or downtime constraints in a centrally dispatched system. Sufficient conditions under which simultaneously charging and discharging is optimal (i.e., rational) are given in
[
5
]
.

Nevertheless, in some cases it is prudent to use the relaxed problem.
Ex-post
sufficient conditions under which the relaxation becomes exact for an economic dispatch problem are provided in
[
8
,
9
]
.
Ex-ante
conditions for exactness of the relaxed problem are presented in
[
10
]
, assuming that certain constraints on battery power limits, as well as initial and maximum energy capacities, are met. Furthermore,
[
11
,
12
,
5
]
shows that for economic dispatch problems with positive linear and quadratic cost functions, the convex relaxation at optimality would be exact with no simultaneous charging and discharging.

Various alternative problem formulations exist to reduce the reliance on the computationally demanding complementarity constraint. For a single time step, tighter relaxations have been proposed
[
13
,
14
]
, which can also heuristically be applied to time series, albeit without realizability guarantees.
Authors in
[
15
]
avoid the use of the complementarity constraint when applied to DC Optimal Power Flow (DCOPF) but add a penalty term to the objective function to ensure the exactness of the relaxed model.
Additionally, authors in
[
16
,
17
]
present an alternative robust linear battery model which is a convex inner approximation of the non-convex formulation that sacrifices optimality for guaranteed admissibility. The robust linear model omits the complementarity constraint but envelopes the actual SOE trajectories to guarantee that any feasible power trajectory is realizable. However, the envelopes beget conservativeness that is a function of energy conversion efficiencies and the prediction horizon, which limits practicality over longer horizons.
Finally, one can use the relaxed problem to compute a schedule, and use a projection operator to recover near-term feasibility, applying only the net power trajectory. As a result, the state of energy (SOE) becomes a provable lower bound on the SOE when the schedule is realized
[
16
,
18
]
. This can lead to unexpected SOE saturation, resulting in an unrealizable power trajectory. Literature has investigated conditions under which the predicted SOE from convex relaxations coincides with the actual realized SOE.

When scheduling aggregations of identical storage elements, two fundamental approaches exist. In the first, each element is modelled and scheduled independently, which rapidly increases the computational complexity. In the second, storage elements are combined into a single composite energy storage asset to reduce computational burden. The composite dispatch is then optimized, and the resulting power schedule is usually
shared equally
among the storage elements. However, optimizing the composite dispatch is still a non-convex problem since a complementarity constraint is still required (all elements can either charge or discharge).

In summary, previous work in energy storage optimization that circumvents the complementarity condition either has been application-specific and holds only if specific criteria on the objective function and/or the battery parameters are met, or presents convex restrictions that may yield significantly sub-optimal solutions. Furthermore, previous work has generally (and often, implicitly) assumed an equal power-sharing policy, which limits how individual elements can respond under dynamic conditions.

This manuscript revisits the modeling of aggregations of storage units by presenting a novel composite model of
N
identical and independently controllable battery elements, where simultaneous charging and discharging power trajectories at the composite level can be implemented by inhomogeneous disaggregation to the individual elements. Throughout, BESS aggregations are used as an example, but the proposed work can be extended to different energy storage assets.

The main contributions of the paper are as follows:

1.

We present a novel linear model that provides a sufficient representation of aggregations of homogeneous energy storage elements. For large numbers of elements or and small control time steps, the representation asymptotically approaches the true feasibility envelope.

2.

We develop a priority-based strategy, based on element SOE, to perform the composite-to-individual disaggregation.

3.

We prove that all solutions of the linear aggregate model are realizable using the priority-based strategy, respecting element-wise power limits, energy limits, and complementarity constraints after disaggregation.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section II presents the composite battery model and the associated constraints. Section III presents the proposed disaggregation strategy and the sufficient conditions for the admissibility of a composite power trajectory. Section IV presents simulation results to demonstrate the performance of the proposed model and disaggregation strategy. Finally, Section V concludes the paper.

III-C

Relating Element and Composite Energy Limit Constraints

Similarly, we want to relate element energy limits to those in the composite formulation. Under PSC, the battery elements are charged/discharged individually every
l

Constraint violations can be avoided by quantifying the maximum possible SOE spread under PSC and using that as an energy buffer applied to the composite battery energy limits.

.

Under the PSC, a composite dispatch that satisfies

(10)

\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}E_{\text{max}}

V
Conclusion

A new linear dispatch model is presented and analyzed for a composite battery that permits unequal power sharing among its
N
elements to realize simultaneous charging and discharging.
A priority-based disaggregation strategy is described and used to characterize sufficient conditions on the composite battery dispatch under which element constraints are guaranteed to be satisfied. Previous battery optimization implicitly assumed equal power sharing between all elements. By enabling unequal power sharing within a linear formulation, we have expanded the class of battery models that yield realizable power trajectories.
Practical implementation of such dispatch strategies could also justify the use of linear models for composite storage systems or aggregate storage representations in energy system optimization models.
Future work includes exploring alternative disaggregation strategies and incorporating non-linear battery models.

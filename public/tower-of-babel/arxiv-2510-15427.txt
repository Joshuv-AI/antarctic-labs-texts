Convergence of Heavy-Flavor PDFs at Small x x and Its Impact on Future e ​ p ep Colliders
S. O. Kara

Abstract
We present the first quantitative demonstration of the statistical convergence of heavy-flavor parton distribution functions (PDFs) at small x x across modern NNLO global fits. Using the latest NNPDF4.0, CT18, and MSHT20 sets, we quantify a maximal relative deviation of Δ max ≃ 16.8 % \Delta_{\mathrm{max}}\simeq 16.8\% at x = 10 − 6 x=10^{-6} and Q = 100 ​ GeV Q=100~\mathrm{GeV} —nearly a factor-of-two improvement over the ∼ 30 % \sim\!30\% spread in pre-2013 determinations. This convergence, stabilized by consistent α s \alpha_{s} and scale treatments, marks a transition of the heavy-flavor sector into a statistically controlled regime of small- x x QCD. We further show that this precision directly reduces PDF-driven uncertainties in heavy-quark electroproduction at future e ​ p ep colliders (LHeC and FCC–eh) by roughly a factor of two, and enhances the theoretical stability of leptophilic new-physics searches such as a neutral Z ℓ Z_{\ell} gauge boson. These findings establish a unified link between the precision frontier of QCD and the discovery potential of next-generation lepton–hadron facilities.



I
Introduction

The partonic structure of the proton at small Bjorken-
x
remains one of the most dynamic and experimentally challenging frontiers of perturbative QCD.
In particular, the gluon-induced evolution of heavy-flavor parton distribution functions (PDFs),
f_{c,b}(x,Q^{2})
,
has long exhibited sizable uncertainties among global fits.
Historically, the spread between early-generation analyses such as
NNPDF2.3
et al. NNPDF Collaboration
, CT14
et al. 2016
, and MMHT14
L. A. Harland-Lang and Thorne 2015

at
x\!\lesssim\!10^{-5}
reached the level of
\mathcal{O}(30\%)

even at moderate scales
Q\!\sim\!100~\mathrm{GeV}
,
limiting precision studies of processes that probe the heavy-flavor sea at future lepton–hadron facilities.

During the past decade, several developments have fundamentally improved this situation.
Global analyses have incorporated the full HERA II combined structure-function data,
LHC Run II open–heavy-flavor measurements from LHCb,
and refined general-mass variable-flavor-number schemes (VFNS) at NNLO.
Concurrently, improved treatments of
\alpha_{s}
evolution and PDF–
\alpha_{s}
correlations,
together with Monte-Carlo or Hessian ensemble propagation,
have rendered heavy-flavor densities far more stable across collaborations.
These advances have direct phenomenological implications for upcoming
ep
colliders
such as the LHeC (
\sqrt{s}\!\simeq\!1.3
TeV) and FCC-eh (
\sqrt{s}\!\simeq\!3.5
TeV),
whose projected luminosities will transform charm and bottom electroproduction
into precision probes of small-
x
QCD dynamics.

In this Letter, we provide the
first quantitative assessment
of the statistical convergence of heavy-flavor PDFs across modern NNLO global fits.
Using a unified numerical framework based on
LHAPDF6
,
APFEL
, and
MadGraph5_aMC@NLO
,
we demonstrate that the maximal inter-set deviation,

\Delta_{\max}\!\equiv\!\max_{i,j}|f_{i}-f_{j}|/\bar{f}
,
has decreased to
\simeq\!16.8\%
at
x\!=\!10^{-6}
and
Q\!=\!100~\mathrm{GeV}
—
a reduction by nearly a factor of two compared to the 2013 baseline
et al. NNPDF Collaboration
.
We further quantify this convergence using statistical metrics (Mahalanobis distance and Kullback–Leibler divergence)
and validate the resulting consistency against HERA and LHCb data.

Beyond documenting this precision improvement,
we show that the reduced heavy-flavor uncertainty directly strengthens the predictive power of QCD for heavy-quark electroproduction and for new-physics scenarios such as leptophilic gauge bosons
Z_{\ell}
.
Our findings indicate that the heavy-flavor content of the proton has entered a quantitatively controlled regime,
providing a unified bridge between the precision frontier of perturbative QCD and the discovery reach of next-generation lepton–hadron colliders.

II
Heavy-flavor electroproduction at LHeC and FCC-eh

Heavy-quark electroproduction at future
ep
colliders provides an essential
benchmark for testing the stability of small-
x
PDFs.
At leading order, the neutral-current process

e^{-}p\!\to\!e^{-}\,Q\bar{Q}\,X

proceeds via boson–gluon fusion,
\gamma^{*}g\!\to\!Q\bar{Q}
,
and at higher orders receives sizable contributions from gluon splitting
and heavy-quark excitation channels.
The inclusive differential cross section in the one-photon–exchange approximation is

\begin{split}\frac{d\sigma(ep\!\to\!eQ\bar{Q}X)}{dx\,dQ^{2}}&=\frac{2\pi\alpha^{2}}{xQ^{4}}\,\Big[(1+(1-y)^{2})\,F_{2}^{Q}(x,Q^{2})\\
&\quad-\,y^{2}\,F_{L}^{Q}(x,Q^{2})\Big],\end{split}

(1)

where
y=Q^{2}/(xs)
and
F_{2,L}^{Q}
are the heavy-flavor structure functions
computed in a general-mass variable-flavor-number scheme (VFNS).

For the present analysis, we employ
LHAPDF6
coupled to
APFEL

to evaluate
F_{2,L}^{Q}
at NNLO accuracy within the
FONLL-C implementation
S. Forte and Rojo 2010
,
and use
MadGraph5_aMC@NLO
to validate the resulting parton-level rates.
The central scales are chosen as
\mu_{R}=\mu_{F}=Q
, with variations by factors of 2
to estimate residual scale dependence.
Charm and bottom masses are taken as

m_{c}=1.51~\mathrm{GeV}
and
m_{b}=4.92~\mathrm{GeV}
(pole scheme).

III
Inter-set compatibility metric

To quantify the statistical consistency among modern PDF determinations,
we evaluate the mutual tension between NNPDF4.0, CT18, and MSHT20
using Mahalanobis and Kullback–Leibler (KL) metrics
on a logarithmic
(x,Q)
grid spanning

x\!\in\![10^{-6},10^{-2}]
and
Q\!\in\![5,500]~\mathrm{GeV}
.
Each PDF set is treated as a multivariate distribution of observables

f(x,Q)
, from which the mean vector and covariance matrix are constructed.

The global tension, expressed through a normalized Mahalanobis distance
T_{\mathrm{PDF}}
,
and the symmetrized KL divergence
J(i,j)
, together quantify
the degree of statistical compatibility among ensembles.
Full definitions and numerical regularization details
(e.g. covariance whitening and Tikhonov stabilization)
are provided in the Supplemental Material.

Applying these metrics yields a global compatibility of

T_{\mathrm{PDF}}=1.2\pm 0.2~\sigma,

with a corresponding symmetrized KL divergence

J=(2.1\pm 0.4)\!\times\!10^{-3}
.
These results confirm that heavy–flavor PDFs from independent NNLO
global analyses now exhibit consistency at the
\sim 1
–
1.5\sigma
level,
marking the first statistically controlled regime of heavy-flavor evolution.

IV
Validation with HERA and LHCb data

To validate the heavy–flavor treatment and assess the physical fidelity
of the extracted PDFs, we confront our NNLO predictions with
precision measurements from HERA and LHCb.

Figure

2
compares the computed charm- and bottom-structure functions

F_{2}^{c,b}(x,Q^{2})
to the combined HERA data
Abramowicz et al. 2015
.
The agreement is excellent across the full kinematic range,
with a reduced chi-square of
\chi^{2}/\mathrm{dof}\simeq 1.08
–
1.12

depending on the PDF ensemble (NNPDF4.0, CT18, or MSHT20).
Residuals exhibit no systematic trend, confirming that
the evolution of heavy flavors in the general-mass scheme
is consistent with the measured scaling violations.

Complementary validation arises from forward open-heavy-flavor
production at the LHC.
Predictions obtained with
MadGraph5_aMC@NLO
interfaced to
NNPDF4.0 PDFs accurately reproduce the LHCb measurements of prompt
charm and bottom production at
\sqrt{s}=13~\mathrm{TeV}

LHCb Collaboration 2021
;
LHCb Collaboration 2022
,
within the combined experimental and theoretical uncertainties.
This provides an independent probe in a qualitatively different regime,

x\!\sim\!10^{-5}
and
Q\!\sim\!m_{T}\!>\!m_{b}
,
demonstrating that heavy–flavor PDFs extracted from lepton–hadron data
can be reliably applied to hadronic environments.

V
Physics reach for
Z_{\ell}

The validated PDFs and uncertainty estimates established above
directly translate into improved predictions for leptophilic new
physics, in particular a neutral gauge boson
Z_{\ell}

coupled only to leptons.
Such models, motivated by lepton–universality anomalies and

(g-2)_{\mu}
tension, predict clean signatures at future
electron–positron and lepton–hadron facilities.

To quantify the reach, we compute the inclusive production
cross sections
\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\!\to\!Z_{\ell}\!\to\!\ell^{+}\ell^{-})

and
\sigma(ep\!\to\!eZ_{\ell}X)
at NNLO in QCD
using the same PDF ensembles (NNPDF4.0, CT18, MSHT20)
and their corresponding uncertainty replicas.
For definiteness, the leptophilic coupling is normalized to

g_{\ell}=0.1
and the mass range scanned over

M_{Z_{\ell}}=0.05
–
1~\mathrm{TeV}
.
The resulting production rates are integrated over fiducial detector
acceptances consistent with the LHeC
Abelleira Fernandez et al. 2012
and FCC–eh
Abada et al. 2019
designs.

summarizes the FCC–eh projected sensitivity
in the
(M_{Z_{\ell}},\,g_{\ell})
plane for
100~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
.
The shaded band represents the combined PDF
+
scale uncertainty
(4–8% across the mass range), while the solid and dashed curves
indicate the illustrative 2

Interestingly, the
Z_{\ell}

into an approximately

This also reinforces the robustness of
Z_{\ell}

against residual PDF and scale systematics,
indicating that the present uncertainty level is no longer a limiting
factor for future

VI
Conclusions and outlook

We have performed the first quantitative assessment of the statistical convergence of heavy–flavor parton distribution functions in the small-
x
regime relevant for future lepton–hadron facilities.
By combining HERA precision data, modern NNLO global analyses, and a unified statistical framework based on Mahalanobis and Kullback–Leibler metrics,
we have established that the heavy–flavor sector now exhibits a global compatibility of
T_{\mathrm{PDF}}\simeq 1.2\sigma
across the
(x,Q)
plane—a level of mutual consistency unattained in earlier PDF generations.

This convergence reduces PDF–driven uncertainties in heavy–quark electroproduction at the LHeC and FCC–eh by nearly a factor of two,
marking the transition of heavy–flavor dynamics into a quantitatively controlled domain of small-
x
QCD.
In turn, it directly strengthens the theoretical stability of leptophilic new-physics searches, exemplified here by the attainable reach for a
Z_{\ell}
boson with
M_{Z_{\ell}}\!\lesssim\!1~\mathrm{TeV}
and
g_{\ell}\!\sim\!0.1
.
These results demonstrate a tangible bridge between the precision frontier of QCD and the discovery potential of next-generation lepton–hadron colliders.

Although the
Z_{\ell}
couples exclusively to leptons,
its observable signatures at
ep
machines remain sensitive to the proton’s small-
x
structure.
The improved heavy–flavor consistency achieved here therefore stabilizes the entire leptophilic search sector against residual QCD systematics.
Looking forward, the same methodology can be extended to neutrino–nucleus scattering, fixed-target Drell–Yan, and muon-collider environments,
where small-
x
and threshold dynamics converge.
The statistical tools introduced in this work are general and scalable,
providing a foundation for combined global PDF fits in the forthcoming high-precision era of lepton–hadron physics.

Numerical evidence for the non-Abelian eigenstate thermalization hypothesis
Aleksander Lasek, Jae Dong Noh, Jade LeSchack, Nicole Yunger Halpern

Abstract
The eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH) explains how generic quantum many-body systems thermalize internally. It implies that local operators’ time-averaged expectation values approximately equal their thermal expectation values, regardless of microscopic details. The ETH’s range of applicability therefore impacts theory and experiments. Murthy et al. recently showed that non-Abelian symmetries conflict with the ETH. Such symmetries have excited interest in quantum thermodynamics lately, as they are equivalent to conserved quantities that fail to commute with each other and noncommutation is a quintessentially quantum phenomenon. Murthy et al. proposed a non-Abelian ETH, which we support numerically. The numerics model a one-dimensional (1D) next-nearest-neighbor Heisenberg chain of 18 qubits. We represent local operators with matrices relative to an energy eigenbasis. The matrices bear out seven predictions of the non-Abelian ETH. We also prove analytically that the non-Abelian ETH exhibits a self-consistency property. The proof relies on a thermodynamic-entropy definition different from that in Murthy et al. This work initiates the observation and application of the non-Abelian ETH.

VI
Conclusions

We have extensively supported the non-Abelian ETH numerically. Our work centers on two Heisenberg models, which exhibit SU(2) symmetry. In the main text, we checked six qualitative predictions of the non-Abelian ETH (Figs.
3
–
7
and the
q
-independence reported in App.
C
) and one quantitative prediction (Fig.
8
). Additionally, we argued analytically that the non-Abelian ETH is self-consistent if the thermodynamic entropy
S_{\rm th}
is smaller than anticipated in
[
28
]
. These results offer the most comprehensive evidence, to date, for the non-Abelian ETH.

Our work establishes multiple opportunities for future research. First, the non-Abelian ETH implies how systems thermalize internally in the presence of non-Abelian symmetries
[
28
]
. These predictions can now be checked; specific models’ time evolutions should be simulated numerically and on small-scale quantum computers
[
44
,
17
,
45
]
. Our Heisenberg models offer starting points.

Second, our results encompass off–block-diagonal matrix elements, whereas
[
28
]
focuses on block-diagonal elements. Leveraging the off–block-diagonal elements, we derive a fluctuation–dissipation relation
[
30
]
for SU(2)-symmetric systems
[
29
]
. One can also leverage the off–block-diagonal elements to ascertain how local operators’ expectation values fluctuate about their time averages. Perhaps the non-Abelian symmetry causes deviations from conventional ETH-sourced results.

Second, the variables
R^{(T)}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
may be correlated. We demonstrated that
R^{(T)}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
values, viewed individually, are distributed in Gaussian fashion (Figs.
4
and
5
). We did not quantify correlations among the

[
7
,
33
,

. The correlations affect long-time dynamics, Lyapunov exponents, and out-of-time-ordered correlators. Do non-Abelian symmetries change these results qualitatively? The mathematical toolkit of free probability theory could shed light on this question
[
33
]
.

Third and most broadly, this work bridges noncommuting charges from quantum thermodynamics to many-body physics. This bridge has been expanding rapidly (e.g.,
[

,

Appendix A
Self-consistency of the ETH

Srednicki argued for the ETH’s self-consistency by studying the square
A^{2}
of an operator
A

[
39
]
. From the ETH, one can infer directly and indirectly how the matrix elements
(A^{2})_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
scale. The two scalings are the same, Srednicki confirmed. We recapitulate his argument here.

We reuse the setup in Sec.
II A
. Additionally, we assume that the Hamiltonian
H
and local operator
A
obey the ETH [Eq. (
1
)]. Denote by
d_{\mathcal{E}}
the DOS at
\mathcal{E}
.
The thermodynamic entropy
S_{\rm th}(\mathcal{E})=\log(d_{\mathcal{E}})
. Denote by
D
the global Hilbert space’s dimensionality.

A^{2}
is an operator similarly to
A
. Like the matrix elements
A_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
, the elements
(A^{2})_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
can directly obey the ETH—can satisfy Eq. (
1
). Also, because the
A_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
s satisfy Eq. (
1
), the ETH indirectly determines the structure of

\displaystyle\left(A^{2}\right)_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{D}A_{\alpha\beta}\,A_{\beta\alpha^{\prime}}\,,

(A1)

by determining each factor’s structure.
We expound upon the direct implication, then the indirect implication. Afterward, we compare the two.

Direct implication of the ETH:
Let us substitute
A^{2}
into the ETH [Eq. (
1
)]:

\displaystyle\left(A^{2}\right)_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{2}\left(\mathcal{E}\right)\,\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}+e^{-S_{\rm th}\left(\mathcal{E}\right)/2}\,f^{(A^{2})}\left(\mathcal{E},\omega\right)\,R^{(A^{2})}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}.

(A2)

\mathcal{A}_{2}
and
f^{(A^{2})}
denote smooth, real,
O(1)
functions.

R^{(A^{2})}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
varies as
R_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
does.
On the block diagonal (when
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha
), therefore, the matrix element scales as

\displaystyle\left(A^{2}\right)_{\alpha\alpha}=O(1)+O\left(d_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1/2}\right).

(A3)

Off the block diagonal, the matrix element scales as

\displaystyle\left(A^{2}\right)_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}=0+O\left(d_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1/2}\right)=O\left(d_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1/2}\right).

(A4)

Recalling the terms’ sources is worthwhile. In Eqs. (
A3
) and (
A4
), the

O\left(d_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1/2}\right)

O(1)
term comes from a deterministic function.

Indirect implication of the ETH:
Equation (
A1
) shows how
(A^{2})_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}
decomposes in terms of
A

1
)].
We must introduce new definitions:

\mathcal{E}_{\gamma\epsilon}\coloneqq(E_{\gamma}+E_{\epsilon})/2

\omega_{\gamma\epsilon}\coloneqq E_{\gamma}-E_{\epsilon}\,.

We multiply out in Eq. (
A1
) and evaluate the Kronecker deltas:

\displaystyle\left(A^{2}\right)_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}^{2}\left(E_{\alpha}\right)\,\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}+\left[\mathcal{A}\left(E_{\alpha}\right)+\mathcal{A}\left(E_{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)\right]\,e^{-S_{\rm th}\left(\mathcal{E}\right)/2}\,f\left(\mathcal{E},\omega\right)\,R_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}

(A5)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\beta=1}^{D}e^{-\left[S_{\rm th}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha\beta})+S_{\rm th}(\mathcal{E}_{\beta\alpha^{\prime}})\right]/2}\,f\left(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha\beta},\omega_{\alpha\beta}\right)\,f\big(\mathcal{E}_{\beta\alpha^{\prime}}\,,\omega_{\beta\alpha^{\prime}}\big)\,R_{\alpha\beta}\,R_{\beta\alpha^{\prime}}.

We now evaluate how each term scales.
The first term is
O(1)
on the block diagonal and vanishes off the block diagonal.
The second term is
O\big(d_{\mathcal{E}}^{-1/2}\big)

The third term requires more–in-depth analysis. Srednicki does not address the thermodynamic entropy’s or the
f
functions’
\beta

f
functions from the
R

[

]
. Each
f

|\omega|
grows (Sec.
V
). In the third term of Eq. (

), therefore, the summand is non-negligible only if
E_{\beta}\approx E_{\alpha}\,,E_{\alpha^{\prime}}\,.
The number of such
\beta

O(d_{\mathcal{E}})
. Furthermore,
S_{\rm th}
changes significantly only if its argument changes extensively. We therefore approximate both entropies at

B 3
Approximation of Clebsch–Gordan coefficient

Let us derive the approximation (
B11
) for the Clebsch–Gordan coefficient

\langle s+\nu,m+q|s,m;k,q\rangle
.
We assume that

\displaystyle s,m=O(N^{\delta}),\;\;\text{wherein}\;\;\delta\in(0,\,1],\;\;\text{and}\;\;\nu,k,q,s-m=O(N^{0}).

(B28)

The general formula for a Clebsch–Gordan coefficient appears as Eq. (2.41) in
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle\langle s+\nu,m+q|s,m;k,q\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\{[2(s+\nu)+1]\,(2s+\nu-k)!\,(k+\nu)!\,(k-\nu)!\,(s+m+\nu+q)!\,(s-m+\nu-q)!

\displaystyle\qquad\;\times(s+m)!\,(s-m)!\,(k+q)!\,(k-q)!\,\big/\,(2s+\nu+k+1)!\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\;\times\sum_{\ell}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell!\,(k-\nu-\ell)!\,(s-m-\ell)!\,(k+q-\ell)!\,(s+m+\nu-k+\ell)!\,(\nu-q+\ell)!}\,.

(B29)

The sum runs over all integers
\ell
for which every factorial’s argument is non-negative.

To approximate the Clebsch–Gordan coefficient, we approximate factorials. If
x\gg\Delta
, then

(x+\Delta)!=\big[(x+\Delta)\,(x+\Delta-1)\,\ldots(x+1)\big]\,x!=x^{\Delta}\,x!\,\left[1+O\left(x^{-1}\right)\right].

(B30)

To apply Eq. (
B30
), we invoke the assumptions (
B28
). For example,

(2s+\nu-k)!=(2s)^{\nu-k}\,(2s)!\left[1+O\left(N^{-\delta}\right)\right]
. In another example, if
\xi=O(1)
, then

\displaystyle(s+m+\xi)!=[2s+(s-m)+\xi]!=(2s)^{s-m+\xi}\,(2s)!\,\left[1+O\left(N^{-\delta}\right)\right].

(B31)

We approximate all the relevant factors in Eq. (
B29
): at leading order,

\displaystyle\langle s+\nu,m+q|s,m;k,q\rangle=(2s)^{\frac{q-\nu}{2}}\sum_{\ell=\ell_{\rm min}}^{\ell_{\rm max}}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}\,\sqrt{(k+\nu)!\,(k-\nu)!\,(s-m+\nu-q)!\,(s-m)!\,(k+q)!\,(k-q)!}}{\ell!\,(k-\nu-\ell)!\,(s-m-\ell)!\,(k+q-\ell)!\,(\nu-q+\ell)!\,(2s)^{\ell}}\>.

(B32)

We have defined the extreme
\ell
values

\ell_{\rm min}\coloneqq\max\{0,\,q-\nu\}
and

\ell_{\rm max}\coloneqq\min\{k-\nu,\,s-m,\,k+q\}
.

We can approximate Eq. (
B32
) because the summand’s denominator contains a
(2s)^{\ell}
. This factor suppresses all terms except for the
\ell{=}\ell_{\rm min}
one: at leading order,

\langle s+\nu,m+q|s,m;k,q\rangle=(-1)^{\ell_{\rm min}}(2s)^{\frac{q-\nu}{2}-\ell_{\rm min}}\frac{\sqrt{(k+\nu)!\,(k-\nu)!\,(s-m+\nu-q)!\,(s-m)!\,(k+q)!\,(k-q)!}}{\ell_{\rm min}!\,(k-\nu-\ell_{\rm min})!\,(s-m-\ell_{\rm min})!\,(k+q-\ell_{\rm min})!\,(\nu-q+\ell_{\rm min})!}\,.

(B33)

We evaluate the RHS explicitly at each of
\nu>q
,
\nu=q
, and
\nu<q
:

\langle s+\nu,m+q|s,m;k,q\rangle=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{(k+\nu)!(s-m+\nu-q)!(k-q)!}{(k-\nu)!(s-m)!(k+q)!}}\frac{(2s)^{\frac{q-\nu}{2}}}{(\nu-q)!}\left[1+O\left(s^{-1}\right)\right],&\nu>q\\
1+O\left(s^{-1}\right),&\nu=q\\
(-1)^{q-\nu}\sqrt{\frac{(k-\nu)!(k+q)!(s-m)!}{(k+\nu)!(k-q)!(s-m+\nu-q)!}}\frac{(2s)^{-\frac{q-\nu}{2}}}{(q-\nu)!}\left[1+O\left(s^{-1}\right)\right],&\nu<q\,.\end{cases}

(B34)

This formula implies Eq. (
B11
).

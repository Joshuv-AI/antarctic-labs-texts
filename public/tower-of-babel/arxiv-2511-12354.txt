Abstract
. Over any field of characteristic 0 0 , we prove that the homotopy exact sequence of algebraic fundamental groups for the universal curve with unordered marked points does not split. The same nonsplitting holds for the universal hyperelliptic curve. Our approach extends Chen’s topological result to the profinite setting and relies on the use of relative and continuous relative completions to detect the nonexistence of algebraic sections.



1.
Introduction

For nonnegative integers
g
and
n
satisfying
2g-2+n>0
and a field
k
of characteristic
0
, let
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}
denote the moduli stack of proper, smooth curves of genus
g
with
n
ordered distinct marked points over
k
, and let

\pi_{g,n/k}:{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}

be the universal curve over
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}
. The symmetric group on
n
letters, denoted
S_{n}
, acts on
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}
by permuting the
n
marked points. We denote the quotient stack
[{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}/S_{n}]
by
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k}
. The
S_{n}
-action extends to
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k}
, and we denote the quotient stack
[{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k}/S_{n}]
by
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,[n]/k}
. Thus the universal curve
\pi_{g,n/k}
induces a family of curves of genus
g
with
n
unordered marked points over
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k}
:

\pi_{g,[n]/k}:{\mathcal{C}}_{g,[n]/k}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k},

which we call the universal curve of genus
g
with
n
unordered marked points.

Denote by
\pi_{g,n}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n}\to{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n}
and
\pi_{g,[n]}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,[n]}\to{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,[n]}
the corresponding complex analytic orbifolds of
\pi_{g,n/{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\pi_{g,[n]/{\mathbb{C}}}
, respectively. In
[
2
, Thm. 1.3]
, Chen showed that for
n\geq 2
and
g\geq 2
, the curve
\pi_{g,[n]}
admits no sections. In this paper, we extend Chen’s result to the algebraic fundamental groups of
\pi_{g,[n]/k}
.

Let
\bar{y}
be a geometric point of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k}
and
C
the fiber of
\pi_{g,[n]/k}
over
\bar{y}
. Fix a geometric point
\bar{x}\in C
, viewed also as a geometric point of
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,[n]/k}
. Associated to the universal curve
\pi_{g,[n]/k}
, there is a homotopy exact sequence of algebraic fundamental groups:

(1)

1\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}(C,\bar{x})\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{C}}_{g,[n]/k},\bar{x})\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k},\bar{y})\to 1.

Theorem 1
.

If
g\geq 3
, then the homotopy exact sequence (
1
) does not split.

In
[
2
, Thm. 2.6]
, Chen also proved an analogous result for the hyperelliptic case. Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}
be the closed substack of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}
parametrizing hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
with
n
ordered marked points. Denote the quotient stack
[{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}/S_{n}]
by
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/k}
; this is a closed substack of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k}
parametrizing hyperelliptic curves of genus

with
n
unordered marked points. Restricting
\pi_{g,n/k}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}
, we obtain the universal hyperelliptic curve

\pi^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,n/k}:{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}.

Extending the
S_{n}
-action to
{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}}
yields a family over
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/k}
:

\pi^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,[n]/k}:{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/k}}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/k},

which we call the universal hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
with
n
unordered marked points.

Denote by
\pi^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,n}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}}\to{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n}
and
\pi^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,[n]}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]}}\to{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,[n]}
the corresponding complex analytic orbifolds of

[
2

We extend Chen’s result to the algebraic fundamental groups of
\pi^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,[n]/k}

(2)

1\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}(C,\bar{x})\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/k}},\bar{x})\to\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/k},\bar{y})\to 1,

where
\bar{y}

{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/k}
,
C
is the fiber of
\pi^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,[n]/k}
, and
\bar{x}
is a geometric point of
C
.

Theorem 2
.

If
g\geq 3
, then the homotopy exact sequence (
2
) does not split.

A key tool used in this paper is the relative completion of a discrete group, or, in the case of a profinite group, its continuous relative completion. A detailed account of their constructions and basic properties can be found in
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
. In particular, for the algebraic fundamental groups of the stacks considered here, their continuous relative completions over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
are canonically isomorphic to the base change of the corresponding relative completions over
{\mathbb{Q}}
to
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
.

For example, the relative completion of the orbifold fundamental group

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}({\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n})
is a proalgebraic group over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, defined as an extension of
\operatorname{Sp}(H):=\operatorname{Sp}(H_{1}(C,{\mathbb{Q}}))
by a prounipotent group. Its Lie algebra carries a natural mixed Hodge structure (MHS), and hence admits a weight filtration defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Each graded piece of the associated graded Lie algebra of the completion is an
\operatorname{Sp}(H)
-representation. Moreover, the universal curve
\pi_{g,n}
induces an
\operatorname{Sp}(H)
-equivariant graded Lie algebra section of a projection between the pronilpotent Lie algebras of the corresponding completions.

The
S_{n}
-action together with the
\operatorname{Sp}(H)
-module structure on the weight
-1

1
. In the hyperelliptic case, the relative completion of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n})
admits analogous structures, which are likewise essential for the proof of Theorem
2
.

2.
Configuration Spaces of Points on a Curve

Let
C
be a smooth complex algebraic curve of genus
g
. For a positive integer
n
, the ordered configuration space of
n
distinct points on
C
is

F_{n}(C):=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in C^{n}\mid x_{i}\neq x_{j}\text{ for }i\neq j\}.

The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts freely on
F_{n}(C)
by permuting the coordinates, and we define the unordered configuration space as the quotient

F_{[n]}(C):=F_{n}(C)/S_{n}.

2.1.
Fundamental Groups

Choose a basepoint configuration
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in F_{n}(C)
. The corresponding topological fundamental groups are

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F_{n}(C),(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})),\qquad\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F_{[n]}(C),\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}),

known as the
surface pure braid group
and the
surface braid group
on
n
strands, respectively.

Since the action of
S_{n}
on
F_{n}(C)
is free, the covering map

F_{n}(C)\longrightarrow F_{[n]}(C)

is finite étale with deck group
S_{n}
. This induces the exact sequence

1\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F_{n}(C))\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F_{[n]}(C))\longrightarrow S_{n}\longrightarrow 1.

2.2.
Unipotent Completions and Mixed Hodge Structures

Denote by

{\mathcal{P}}_{g,n},\qquad{\mathcal{P}}_{g,[n]},

the (rational) unipotent completions over
{\mathbb{Q}}
of

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F_{n}(C))
and
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F_{[n]}(C))
, respectively, and set

{\mathfrak{p}}_{g,n}:=\operatorname{Lie}({\mathcal{P}}_{g,n}),\qquad{\mathfrak{p}}_{g,[n]}:=\operatorname{Lie}({\mathcal{P}}_{g,[n]}).

When
n=1
, we have
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F_{1}(C))=\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(C)
, and we denote
{\mathfrak{p}}_{g,1}
by
{\mathfrak{p}}_{g}
.
By work of Morgan and Hain (cf.
[
3
,
11
]
), if
X
is a smooth complex algebraic variety, then the unipotent completion of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(X)
carries a natural mixed Hodge structure; in particular, both
{\mathfrak{p}}_{g,n}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{g,[n]}
are pronilpotent Lie algebras over
{\mathbb{Q}}
equipped with mixed Hodge structures and weight filtrations defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. The finite étale morphism
F_{n}(C)\to F_{[n]}(C)
induces a homomorphism

{\mathcal{P}}_{g,n}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{P}}_{g,[n]},

and hence a morphism of Lie algebras

{\mathfrak{p}}_{g,n}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{p}}_{g,[n]},

which is a morphism of mixed Hodge structures and is strictly compatible with the weight filtration. (We do not assert these maps are isomorphisms here.)

2.3.
Continuous Unipotent Completions

For each prime
\ell
, let
{\mathcal{P}}_{g,n}^{(\ell)}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{g,[n]}^{(\ell)}
denote the continuous (i.e.,
\ell
-adic) unipotent completions over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
of the profinite groups
\widehat{\pi}_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F_{n}(C))
and
\widehat{\pi}_{1}^{\mathrm{top}}(F_{[n]}(C))
, respectively. There are canonical base-change isomorphisms

{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\ell)}_{g,n}:=\operatorname{Lie}({\mathcal{P}}_{g,n}^{(\ell)})\;\cong\;{\mathfrak{p}}_{g,n}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell},\qquad{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\ell)}_{g,[n]}:=\operatorname{Lie}({\mathcal{P}}_{g,[n]}^{(\ell)})\;\cong\;{\mathfrak{p}}_{g,[n]}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell},

so the
\ell
-adic objects are obtained from their rational counterparts by extension of scalars.

3.
Mapping class groups

Suppose that nonnegative integers
g
and
n
satisfy
2g-2+n>0
. Let
\Sigma_{g,n}
denote a fixed reference surface of genus
g
, namely, a closed, connected, oriented topological surface of genus
g
equipped with
n
ordered, distinct marked points. The
mapping class group

{\Gamma}_{g,n}
is defined as the group of isotopy classes of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of
\Sigma_{g,n}
that fix each marked point individually:

{\Gamma}_{g,n}:=\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(\Sigma_{g,n})),

where isotopies are also required to fix the marked points pointwise.

The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
{\Gamma}_{g,n}
by permuting the labels of the marked points. The resulting quotient

{\Gamma}_{g,[n]}:={\Gamma}_{g,n}/S_{n}

is the mapping class group of genus
g
surfaces with
n
unordered marked points. This leads to the short exact sequence

(3)

1\to{\Gamma}_{g,n}\to{\Gamma}_{g,[n]}\to S_{n}\to 1.

For a group
G
, denote its profinite completion by
\widehat{G}
. Since
{\Gamma}_{g,n}
is residually finite, taking the profinite completion of the exact sequence (
3
) yields the exact sequence of profinite groups:

1\to\widehat{{\Gamma}_{g,n}}\to\widehat{{\Gamma}_{g,[n]}}\to S_{n}\to 1.

We define the
hyperelliptic mapping class group

\Delta_{g}\subset{\Gamma}_{g}
as the centralizer of a fixed hyperelliptic involution in the mapping class group of a closed genus
g
surface without marked points. Using the natural forgetful homomorphism
{\Gamma}_{g,n}\to{\Gamma}_{g}
, we define the hyperelliptic mapping class group with
n
ordered marked points by the fiber product

\Delta_{g,n}:=\Delta_{g}\times_{{\Gamma}_{g}}{\Gamma}_{g,n},

and similarly, the version with unordered marked points as

\Delta_{g,[n]}:=\Delta_{g}\times_{{\Gamma}_{g}}{\Gamma}_{g,[n]}.

These groups consist of mapping classes that preserve the hyperelliptic structure on the surface after forgetting the marked points.

3.1.
Symplectic Representations and Torelli Groups

Both
{\Gamma}_{g,n}
and
{\Gamma}_{g,[n]}
admit natural symplectic representations

\rho_{g,n}:{\Gamma}_{g,n}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},{\mathbb{Z}})),\qquad\rho_{g,[n]}:{\Gamma}_{g,[n]}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},{\mathbb{Z}})),

induced by their actions on
H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},{\mathbb{Z}})
.
It is a basic fact that
\rho_{g,n}
is surjective.
Since the image of
\rho_{g,[n]}
contains that of
\rho_{g,n}
,
the representation
\rho_{g,[n]}
is also surjective.
The kernel of
\rho_{g,n}
is the
Torelli group

T_{g,n}
, consisting of
mapping classes that act trivially on homology.

These representations restrict to the hyperelliptic mapping class groups

\Delta_{g,n}
and
\Delta_{g,[n]}
, yielding

\rho^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,n}:\Delta_{g,n}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},{\mathbb{Z}})),\qquad\rho^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,[n]}:\Delta_{g,[n]}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},{\mathbb{Z}})).

The kernel of
\rho^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,n}
defines the
hyperelliptic Torelli group

T\Delta_{g,n}
, namely the subgroup of
\Delta_{g,n}
acting trivially on homology.
The image of
\rho^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g}
contains a finite-index subgroup of

\operatorname{Sp}(H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},{\mathbb{Z}}))
(see A’Campo
[
1
]
), and hence the
representations
\rho^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,n}
and
\rho^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,[n]}
both have
Zariski-dense images in
\operatorname{Sp}(H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},{\mathbb{Q}}))
.

Over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, the group
\operatorname{Sp}(H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},{\mathbb{Q}}))
is a connected, reductive
algebraic group whose finite-dimensional irreducible representations are
completely classified by their
highest weights
.
Equivalently, they are classified by
partitions

\alpha=(\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{g}\geq 0)

of an integer
n=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{g}
into at most
g
parts.
Every finite-dimensional rational representation of

\operatorname{Sp}(H_{1}(\Sigma_{g},{\mathbb{Q}}))
then decomposes as a direct sum
of such irreducible components.

4.
Moduli space of curves

For integers
g,n
with
2g-2+n>0
, let
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/{\mathbb{Z}}}
denote the moduli stack of smooth, proper algebraic curves of genus
g
with
n
ordered distinct marked points. The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/{\mathbb{Z}}}
by permuting the labels of the marked points. The quotient stack

{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/{\mathbb{Z}}}:=[{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/{\mathbb{Z}}}/S_{n}]

is then the moduli stack of curves with
n
unordered marked points. In fact, by combining Knudsen’s result on
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/{\mathbb{Z}}}
with Zintl’s construction of quotient stacks, one sees that

{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/{\mathbb{Z}}}

is a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack over
{\mathbb{Z}}
whenever
2g-2+n>0
(cf.
[
16
, Prop. 3.17, Rem. 3.19]
).

The complex analytification of the moduli stack with ordered marked points is given by

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n}:={\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/{\mathbb{C}}}({\mathbb{C}})\;\simeq\;[\,\mathcal{T}_{g,n}/{\Gamma}_{g,n}\,],

where
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
denotes the Teichmüller space of genus
g
surfaces with
n
ordered marked points.

Similarly, for the moduli stack with unordered marked points, the complex analytification is

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,[n]}:={\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/{\mathbb{C}}}({\mathbb{C}})\;\simeq\;[\,\mathcal{T}_{g,n}/{\Gamma}_{g,[n]}\,].

Since
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
is contractible, the orbifold fundamental groups of these stacks are naturally isomorphic to the corresponding mapping class groups:

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}({\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n})\;\simeq\;{\Gamma}_{g,n},\qquad\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}({\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,[n]})\;\simeq\;{\Gamma}_{g,[n]}.

Moreover, if
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
, the algebraic fundamental groups of the stacks are naturally isomorphic to the profinite completions of the corresponding mapping class groups:

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k})\;\simeq\;\widehat{{\Gamma}_{g,n}},\qquad\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k})\;\simeq\;\widehat{{\Gamma}_{g,[n]}}.

Let
k
be a field. Consider the universal family

\pi_{g,n/k}:{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}

of smooth genus
g
curves with
n
marked points over
k
. We define

{\mathcal{C}}_{g,[n]/k}:=[\,{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k}/S_{n}\,],

the quotient of the universal curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}
by the natural action of the symmetric group
S_{n}
.

A
geometric point
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}
is a smooth, proper
n
–pointed curve defined over an algebraic closure
\bar{k}
,

(C;\,x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),\qquad C/\bar{k}\text{ smooth and proper of genus }g.

The fiber of the universal curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}
over this point is the whole curve
C
. Thus a geometric point of
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k}
lying above
(C;\,x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is

(C;\,x_{1},\dots,x_{n};\,x_{0}),\qquad x_{0}\in C(\bar{k}).

The group
S_{n}
acts by permuting the marked points while leaving the extra point
x_{0}
fixed:

(C;\,x_{1},\dots,x_{n};\,x_{0})\;\;\mapsto\;\;(C;\,x_{\tau(1)},\dots,x_{\tau(n)};\,x_{0}),\qquad\tau\in S_{n}.

As observed by Zintl
[
16
, Rem. 3.13]
, the
S_{n}
–action identifies
fibers of
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k}
lying over geometric points of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}

that differ only by a permutation of the markings.
Passing to the quotient yields a natural morphism

\pi_{g,[n]/k}:{\mathcal{C}}_{g,[n]/k}\;\longrightarrow\;{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k},

whose fiber over a geometric point

[(C;\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\})]\in{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k}(\bar{k})

is the whole curve
C
, regarded together with its set of
n
unordered marked points.

Equivalently, an object of
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,[n]/k}
over a scheme
T/k
consists of an étale
S_{n}
–torsor
P\to T
and an
S_{n}
–equivariant morphism

P\;\longrightarrow\;{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k},

corresponding to a family of curves with
n
ordered disjoint sections over
P
together with an additional section
x_{0}
, all compatible with the
S_{n}
–action.

4.1.
Homotopy exact sequences

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
, and let
\Omega
be an algebraically closed field containing
k
.
Fix a geometric point
\bar{y}:\operatorname{Spec}(\Omega)\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}
, and let

C:=\pi_{g,n/k}^{-1}(\bar{y})

denote the geometric fiber of the universal curve over
\bar{y}
.
Choose a geometric point
\bar{x}:\operatorname{Spec}(\Omega)\to C
, and let
\bar{x}
also denote its image in
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k}
.
Then there is a homotopy exact sequence of algebraic fundamental groups:

1\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}(C,\bar{x})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{C}}_{g,n/k},\bar{x})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k},\bar{y})\longrightarrow 1.

An analogous exact sequence holds for the family

\pi_{g,[n]/k}:{\mathcal{C}}_{g,[n]/k}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k}.

Let
\bar{x}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{g,[n]}(\bar{k})
and
\bar{y}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]}(\bar{k})
also denote the images of
\bar{x}
and
\bar{y}
, respectively, under the natural projections.
Then we have another short exact sequence:

1\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}(C,\bar{x})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{C}}_{g,[n]/k},\bar{x})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{M}}_{g,[n]/k},\bar{y})\longrightarrow 1.

The Hyperelliptic Loci
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]}

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
.
Let

{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n/k}

denote the closed substack parametrizing smooth, proper genus
g
hyperelliptic curves with
n
ordered marked points.

The stack
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}
is a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack over
k
, provided
2g-2+n>0
.
The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}
by permuting the labels of the markings, and we define the moduli stack of hyperelliptic curves with unordered markings over
k
as the quotient stack

{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/k}:=[{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}/S_{n}].

The canonical projection
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/k}
is a finite étale morphism induced by the
S_{n}
–action, and
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/k}
is also a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack over
k
when
2g-2+n>0
.

Over
{\mathbb{C}}
, the analytifications

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n}:={\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/{\mathbb{C}}}({\mathbb{C}}),\qquad{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,[n]}:={\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/{\mathbb{C}}}({\mathbb{C}})

admit descriptions as orbifold quotients of Teichmüller space.
As in the case of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n}
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,[n]}
, the orbifold fundamental groups are identified with the hyperelliptic mapping class groups:

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n})\;\simeq\;\Delta_{g,n},\qquad\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}({\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,[n]})\;\simeq\;\Delta_{g,[n]}.

For an algebraic closure
\bar{k}
of
k
, there are natural isomorphisms

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/\bar{k}})\;\cong\;\widehat{\Delta_{g,n}},\qquad\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{alg}}({\mathcal{H}}_{g,[n]/\bar{k}})\;\cong\;\widehat{\Delta_{g,[n]}}.

Let

\pi^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,n/k}:{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n/k}

denote the universal hyperelliptic curve with
n

5.
Relative completion

In this section, we review definitions and properties of relative completion and its continuous version, more details can be found in
[
4
,

,

. Then we apply relative completions to various mapping class groups, deduce enough relevant structures of these completions towards our main theorems.

5.1.
Relative completion and its structure

Suppose that

(1)

\Gamma
is a discrete group,

(2)

R
is a reductive group over
F
,

(3)

\rho:\Gamma\to R(F)
is a Zariski dense homomorphism.

The
completion of
\Gamma
relative to
\rho
, i.e. the
relative completion of
\Gamma
, is a pro-algebraic group
{\mathcal{G}}_{/F}
defined over
F
and a homomorphism
\widetilde{\rho}:\Gamma\to{\mathcal{G}}(F)
that lifts
\rho
. The group
{\mathcal{G}}
is an extension

1\to{\mathcal{U}}\to{\mathcal{G}}\to R\to 1

of
R
by a pro-unipotent group
{\mathcal{U}}
, which is the unipotent radical of
{\mathcal{G}}
. The relative completion has the following universal property: If

1\to U\to G\to R\to 1

is an extension of pro-algebraic groups over
F
such that
U
is pro-unipotent, and that
\rho:\Gamma\to R(F)
factors through a homomorphism
\phi:\Gamma\to G(F)
via
\Gamma\to G(F)\to R(F)
, then there exists a unique homomorphism of pro-algebraic groups
{\mathcal{G}}\to G
that commutes with projections to
R
, such that
\phi
is the composition
\Gamma\to{\mathcal{G}}(F)\to G(F)
.
When
\rho
is trivial,
{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{U}}
is the unipotent completion of
\Gamma
.
A generalization of Levi’s Theorem ensures that the extension for relative completion

1\to{\mathcal{U}}\to{\mathcal{G}}\to R\to 1

splits and that any two such splittings are conjugate by an element of
{\mathcal{U}}
. Every splitting induces an isomorphism

{\mathcal{G}}\cong R\ltimes{\mathcal{U}}

that commutes with the projections to
R
, where the action of
R
on
{\mathcal{U}}
is determined by the splitting. This action is determined by the action of
R
on the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{u}}
of
{\mathcal{U}}
as
{\mathcal{U}}=\exp{\mathfrak{u}}
. To give a presentation of
{\mathcal{G}}
, it suffices to give a presentation of
{\mathfrak{u}}
in the category of
R
-modules.

By standard arguments,
{\mathfrak{u}}
has a minimal presentation of the form

{\mathfrak{u}}\cong{\mathbb{L}}(H_{1}({\mathfrak{u}}))^{\wedge}/(\operatorname{im}\varphi)\quad\text{with}\quad\operatorname{im}\varphi\cong H_{2}({\mathfrak{u}})

in the category of
R
-modules, where
{\mathbb{L}}(V)
denotes the free Lie algebra generated by the vector space
V
, and
\varphi:H_{2}({\mathfrak{u}})\to[{\mathbb{L}}(H_{1}({\mathfrak{u}}))^{\wedge},{\mathbb{L}}(H_{1}({\mathfrak{u}}))^{\wedge}]
is an injection such that the composite

H_{2}({\mathfrak{u}})\to[{\mathbb{L}}(H_{1}({\mathfrak{u}}))^{\wedge},{\mathbb{L}}(H_{1}({\mathfrak{u}}))^{\wedge}]\to\Lambda^{2}H_{1}({\mathfrak{u}})

is the dual to the cup product
\Lambda^{2}H^{1}({\mathfrak{u}})\to H^{2}({\mathfrak{u}})
.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
8
, §3.2]
)
.

For every finite dimensional
R
-module
V
, there is a natural homomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(H_{i}({\mathfrak{u}}),V)\cong(H^{i}({\mathfrak{u}})\otimes V)^{R}\to H^{i}(\Gamma,V)

that is an isomorphism when
i=1
and injective when
i=2
. If every irreducible finite dimensional representation of
R
is absolutely irreducible, then there is a natural
R
-module isomorphism

H^{1}({\mathfrak{u}})\cong\bigoplus_{\alpha}H^{1}(\Gamma,V_{\alpha})\otimes V_{\alpha}^{*}

and a natural
R
-module injection

H^{2}({\mathfrak{u}})\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{\alpha}H^{1}(\Gamma,V_{\alpha})\otimes V_{\alpha}^{*}

where
\{V_{\alpha}\}
is a set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of irreducible finite dimensional
R
-modules, and
V_{\alpha}^{*}
is the dual of
V_{\alpha}
.

Proposition 5.4

Definition 5.5

(Continuous relative completion)
.

Suppose that

(1)

\widehat{\Gamma}
is a profinite group,

(2)

R
is a reductive group over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
,

(3)

\rho^{(\ell)}:\widehat{\Gamma}\to R({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
is a
continuous
Zariski dense homomorphism.

The
continuous completion of
\widehat{\Gamma}
relative to
\rho^{(\ell)}

(or the
continuous relative completion of
\widehat{\Gamma}
)
is a pro-algebraic group
{\mathcal{G}}^{(\ell)}
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
, together with a

continuous
homomorphism

\widetilde{\rho}^{(\ell)}:\widehat{\Gamma}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}^{(\ell)}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})

that lifts
\rho^{(\ell)}
.
The group
{\mathcal{G}}^{(\ell)}
is as an extension of the reductive group
R

by a prounipotent group
{\mathcal{U}}^{(\ell)}
, which is the unipotent radical of
{\mathcal{G}}^{(\ell)}
:

1\longrightarrow{\mathcal{U}}^{(\ell)}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}^{(\ell)}\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow 1.

This continuous relative completion satisfies a universal property similar to that of relative completion, but every homomorphism is now required to be
continuous
with respect to the given topology.

Suppose that
\widehat{\Gamma}
comes from a discrete group, i.e., it is the profinite completion of a discrete group
\Gamma
. Suppose that
\rho:\Gamma\to R({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
is a continuous, Zariski dense homomorphism, so that
\rho^{(\ell)}:\widehat{\Gamma}\to R({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
is the continuous extension of
\rho
. The following result is stated in
[
10
, Thm 6.3]
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Suppose
\rho
and
\rho^{(\ell)}
are given as above. If
{\mathcal{G}}^{(\ell)}
and
\tilde{\rho}:\Gamma\to{\mathcal{G}}^{(\ell)}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
is the completion of
\Gamma
relative to
\rho
, then:

(1)

\tilde{\rho}
is continuous and thus induces a continuous homomorphism
\widetilde{\rho}^{(\ell)}:\widehat{\Gamma}\to{\mathcal{G}}^{(\ell)}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
;

(2)

{\mathcal{G}}^{(\ell)}
and
\widetilde{\rho}^{(\ell)}
form the continuous relative completion of
\widehat{\Gamma}
with respect to
\rho^{(\ell)}
.

Remark 5.7
.

This is a generalization of
§
2.3
, since
{\mathcal{G}}^{(\ell)}
can be obtained from
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
by base change. The proposition says that base change is compatible with the continuous relative completion. More precisely, the relative completion of the discrete group
\Gamma
base changed from
{\mathbb{Q}}
to
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
is the same as the continuous relative completion of the profinite group
\widehat{\Gamma}
over
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}
.

5.3.
Relative completion of the mapping class groups

From now on, we will usually omit the subscript
/Q
when the group/Lie algebra is defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. In view of the base change property, the results over
{\mathbb{Q}}
can be easily extended to the case

Our primary case of interest is when the discrete group
\Gamma
is the orbifold fundamental group of the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{an}}_{g,n}

\Gamma_{g,n}

\Gamma_{g,n}
is trivial, it is natural in this setting to consider its relative completion instead.

Example 5.8

(Relative completions of

5.6.
Characteristic classes with

-actions

In

9

(cf.

S_{n}

i=1
. Then an argument similar to the proof of Theorem 1 shows that

\mathrm{pr}_{0}\circ\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{-1}d\tilde{h}(x)=\pm x\quad\text{ for }x\in H.

On the other hand, for
\bar{x}_{2}=(0,x,0\ldots,0)
with
x\not=0\in H
being in the second copy of
H
in
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\pm x=\mathrm{pr}_{0}\circ\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{-1}d\tilde{h}\circ\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{-1}\mathrm{p}_{\mathcal{H}}(\bar{x}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{pr}_{0}\circ\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{-1}\mathrm{p}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{H}}}\circ\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{-1}d\tilde{z}(\bar{x}_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{pr}_{0}\circ\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{-1}\mathrm{p}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}}\circ\zeta^{\pm}_{1}(\bar{x}_{2})

\displaystyle=0,

which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\pi^{\mathrm{hyp}}_{g,[n]/\bar{k}\ast}
does not admit any continuous section. ∎

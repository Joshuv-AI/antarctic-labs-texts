A Case of Intravascular Myopericytoma: A Rare Painful Benign Subcutaneous Tumor in the Right Lower Arm
Authors: Yu Jeong Park, Jong Heon Jeong, Hui Young Shin, Seung Ho Lee, Ai Young Lee, Jong Soo Hong
Published: 2023

Dear Editor:

Myopericytoma is a rare, perivascular myoid neoplasm of skin and soft tissues1,2. It is characterized by numerous thin-walled blood vessels surrounded by round to spindle cells1,2. Intravascular myopericytoma, an extremely rare subtype of myopericytoma, have been only a very small number of cases reported2. Here, we describe a novel case of intravascular myopericytoma.

A 35-year-old man presented with a solitary nodule on his right lower arm. It had been asymptomatic for several years but recently became painful. He denied any history of trauma. On clinical assessment, the lesion was firm, ~1 cm sized, and covered with normal skin. (Fig. 1A). Excisional biopsy was performed. Histological examination revealed well-circumscribed, not encapsulated, concentric multilayered pattern of perivascular arrangement of spindle cells which was protruded into lesional blood vessels (Fig. 1B). The tumor resembled a benign vascular smooth muscle tumor, such as intravascular myopericytoma, angioleiomyoma or myofibroma. Additional immunohistochemical staining was performed. The smooth muscle actin (SMA) was strongly positive whereas desmin was weakly positive, and other CD34, epithelial membrane antigen (EMA), factor XIIIa, and HMB45 were negative. The excisional margin was clear and the patient no longer complained of pain. No recurrence was observed during the post-operative follow-up.

McMenamin and Calonje1 in 1998 proposed the term myopericytoma be used to describe a group of tumors that originate from perivascular myoid cells containing eosinophilic cytoplasm and growing in a concentric pattern around vessels. Immunohistochemically, tumor cells in myopericytoma are generally positive for SMA, vimentin, and h-caldesmon but negative for desmin, CD34, cytokeratin, and S-100 protein3,4. In 2002, an unusual myopericytoma entirely located within the lumen of a subcutaneous vein was described by McMenamin and Calonje1. Unlike myopericytoma, intravascular myopericytoma resides completely within the lumen which disturbs both internal elastic lamina and smooth muscle layer1,2. The cause of intravascular myopericytoma is unknown, but some authors have argued a history of trauma can induce it1. Whereas classical myopericytoma presents as a painless nodule, the intravascular subtype can be painful which is probably caused by associating thrombus1,2. Prognosis after surgical excision is favorable so that complete excision is recommended1,2.

While many diagnoses can be made only using histologic findings, myopericytoma shares morphological characteristics with other perivascular tumors3. Thus, additional immunohistochemical staining can aid its differential diagnosis3. In this case, referring to Table 1, SMA were strongly positive which indicated that it was a smooth muscle cell origin tumor. Although it was difficult to differentiate from angioleiomyoma due to weak desmin staining, it was not encapsulated and the myoid cells were denser and more eosinophilic, consistent with intravascular myopericytoma5. In addition, according to negative HMB45 staining, angiomyolipoma could be excluded and the EMA result and cell morphology showed no evidence of malignancy. Pyogenic granuloma was discriminated by staining CD34.

Since intravascular myopericytoma was first described in 2002, at least to our knowledge, only about ten cases have been reported in the literature. Therefore, we describe a novel case of painful intravascular myopericytoma which is confirmed by immunohistochemistry for educational purpose.

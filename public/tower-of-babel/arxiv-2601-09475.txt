Note on Boundary Stabilization of Degenerate Schrödinger Equations


Abstract
A degenerate Schrödinger equation under fractional integral damping is considered. Here the damping term is singular and not integrable and we consider the two cases when damping acting on the degenerate boundary and nondegenerate boundary. In this paper, we establish polynomial energy decay rates for the degenerate Schrödinger equation by using resolvent estimates.

Introduction

The Schrödinger-type equations are the very important models of mathematical physics, and
they have been also intensely appeared in material science, plasma physics, quantum physics,quantum gases,
nonlinear optics, and so on.
G. Fibich
[
4
]
noted that the damping (absorption) term plays a significant impact in
the real models and it is more suitable to not be neglegted. This motivated several authors to deal with boundary
stabilization of Schrödinger-type equations without degeneracies and
many decay estimates has been realized (see
[
6
]
).

On the contrary, when the principal part is degenerate not much is known in the documentation,
despite that many problems that are relevant for applications are modeled by degenerate Schrödinger
equations (see
[
5
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
).

In
[
5
]
, the authors examined the following degenerate Schrödinger equation

(1)

\left\{\matrix{iy_{t}(x,t)+(x^{\alpha}y_{x}(x,t))_{x}=0\hfill&\hbox{ in }(0,1)\times(0,+\infty),\hfill&\cr y(0,t)=0\hfill&\hbox{ on }(0,+\infty),\hfill&\cr y_{t}(1,t)+y_{x}(1,t)+y(1,t)=0\hfill&\hbox{ on }(0,+\infty),\hfill&\cr y(x,0)=y_{0}(x)\hfill&\hbox{ on }(0,1).\hfill&\cr}\right.

They showed that the solution decays exponentially in a suitable energy space.
Moreover, the degeneracy does not affect the decay rates of the energy.

Recently,
[
3
]
and
[
2
]
explored the well-posedness and asymptotic stability of the following
degenerate Schrödinger equation

(P)\ \left\{\matrix{y_{t}(x,t)-\imath(x^{\alpha}y_{x}(x,t))_{x}=0,\hfill&\cr(x^{\alpha}y_{x})(0,t)=-\imath\rho\partial^{\beta,\gamma}y(0,t),\hfill&\cr y_{x}(1,t)=0,\hfill&\cr y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),\hfill&\cr}\right.(P^{\prime})\ \left\{\matrix{y_{t}(x,t)-\imath(\kappa(x)y_{x}(x,t))_{x}=0,\hfill&\hbox{ in }(0,1)\times(0,+\infty),\hfill&\cr\left\{\matrix{y(0,t)=0\hfill&\hbox{ if }0\leq m_{\kappa}<1\hfill&\cr(\kappa(x)y_{x})(0,t)=0\hfill&\hbox{ if }1\leq m_{\kappa}<2\hfill&\cr}\right.\hfill&\hbox{ in }(0,+\infty),\hfill&\cr(\kappa y_{x})(1,t)=\imath\rho\partial^{\beta,\gamma}y(1,t)\hfill&\hbox{ in }(0,+\infty),\hfill&\cr y(x,0)=y_{0}(x)\hfill&\hbox{ on }(0,1),\hfill&\cr}\right.

where
0<\alpha<1,\rho>0
and
\kappa\in{\cal C}([0,1])\cap{\cal C}^{1}(]0,1])
a function satisfying the following
hypotheses:

\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\kappa(x)>0,\ \forall\ x\in]0,1],\ \kappa(0)=0,\\
m_{\kappa}=\displaystyle\sup_{x<0\leq 1}\displaystyle\frac{x|\kappa^{\prime}(x)|}{\kappa(x)}<2,\quad\kappa\in{\cal C}^{[m_{\kappa}]}([0,1]).\end{array}\right.

The term
\partial^{\beta,\gamma}
stands for the generalized fractional integral of order
0<\beta<1
,
which is given by

\partial^{\beta,\gamma}w({t})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{t}}({t}-s)^{-\beta}e^{-{\gamma}({t}-s)}w(s)\,ds,\hfill\hbox{ for }\gamma\geq 0.

We outline the main results of
[
3
]
and
[
2
]
in the following:

The semigroup
(e^{t{\cal A}_{j}})_{t\geq 0},\ j=1,2
has the following stability properties

(i) If
\gamma>0
and
\beta<(4-3\alpha)/(4-2\alpha)
, it is polynomial of order

\frac{1}{(4-3\alpha)/(4-2\alpha)-\beta}
(that is

\|(i\lambda-{\cal A}_{1})^{-1}\|_{{\cal H}}\leq c|\lambda|^{(4-3\alpha)/(4-2\alpha)-\beta}

(i) If
\gamma>0

(iii) If
\gamma>0

\frac{1}{1-\beta}
(that is
\|(i\lambda-{\cal A}_{2})^{-1}\|_{{\cal H}}\leq c|\lambda|^{1-\beta}
as
|\lambda|\rightarrow+\infty

(iv) If
\gamma=0

The main result of this paper then concerns the precise asymptotic behaviour of the solutions to the problems
(P)
and
(P^{\prime})

when
\gamma=0

D({\cal A}_{j})

Our technique is a special frequency and spectral analysis of the corresponding operator.

3
Main result

Our main results are the following.

Theorem 3.1

The semigroup
{S_{{\cal A}_{1}}(t)}_{t\geq 0}
is polynomially stable and

E_{1}(t)=\|S_{{\cal A}_{1}}(t){\cal Y}_{0}\|_{\cal H}^{2}\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{2}}\|{\cal Y}_{0}\|_{D({\cal A}_{1})\cap R({\cal A}_{1})}^{2}.

Theorem 3.2

The semigroup
{S_{{\cal A}_{2}}(t)}_{t\geq 0}
is polynomially stable and

E_{2}(t)=\|S_{{\cal A}_{2}}(t){\cal Y}_{0}\|_{\cal H}^{2}\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{\frac{2}{2-\beta}}}\|{\cal Y}_{0}\|_{D({\cal A}_{2})\cap R({\cal A}_{2})}^{2}.

E_{2}(t)=\|S_{{\cal A}_{2}}(t){\cal Y}_{0}\|_{\cal H}^{2}\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{2}}\|{\cal Y}_{0}\|_{D({\cal A}_{2})\cap R({\cal A}_{2})}^{2}.

Analysing Toponium at the LHC using Recursive Jigsaw Reconstruction
Aman Desai, Amelia Lovison, Paul Jackson

Abstract
Recent results from the ATLAS and the CMS experiments at the Large Hadron Collider indicate the presence of a top-quark pair bound state near the threshold region. We present a way to reconstruct a toponium state at the t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} threshold region formed at the Large Hadron Collider using the Recursive Jigsaw Reconstruction. We have considered the Non-Relativistic QCD based toponium model implemented in MadGraph5_aMC@NLO. The final states considered consist of two b-jets, two oppositely charged leptons, and missing energy that arises from two neutrinos. The goal of the Recursive Jigsaw Reconstruction is to make use of rules that can help resolve combinatorics ambiguity in preparing the decay tree for a given physics event. Additionally, missing energy coming from two neutrinos needs to be resolved in order to reconstruct the event. We apply four different methods within the RestFrames package and compare the reconstruction results resulting from each of the methods. Due to this method, one can also access kinematic variables in the rest frames belonging to intermediate particle states, providing additional means to discriminate the SM t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} background from the toponium signal. We propose using two angular variables to enhance sensitivity to the toponium signal. Our preliminary results indicate that the improvement in sensitivity can be as much as 16% over the current strategy in the LHC’s Run 3 configuration. This method may be useful for gaining additional insight into the physics phenomenology in the t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} threshold region.



I
Introduction

The ATLAS and the CMS experiments at the Large Hadron Collider have recently reported an excess of events near the top-quark pair production threshold in the dilepton decay channel
23
;
22
;
1
. The results are consistent with the presence of a spin-0 pseudoscalar, toponium
19
;
19
;
21
. The local significance of this excess observed by ATLAS is over
8\sigma

1
.

The reconstruction of this dileptonic decay mode of the
t\bar{t}
system is complex as it involves the presence of two neutrinos which escape detection, and their presence is only inferred through indirect methods such as the imbalance of the transverse momentum. Additionally, owing to the nature of proton-proton collisions, the kinematic information of the neutrino’s four-momentum along the beam axis is lost as the initial energy of a parton interaction is unknown. This leads to challenges in the reconstruction of the
t\bar{t}
system.

The problem is mainly two-fold. First, as mentioned before, there are two neutrinos and, second, there are two b-jets, which leads to kinematic as well as combinatoric ambiguities. Several methods relying on analytical or geometrical techniques have been proposed. For instance, the Ellipse Method
10
and the Sonnechain Method
25
;
26
employ analytical and geometrical solutions, which have been proposed to reconstruct the top-quark pair in the dilepton final state. The ATLAS experiment used the Ellipse Method to reconstruct the dilepton channel in their analysis
1
, while the CMS experiment used the Sonnechain Method
22
.

In this paper, the Recursive Jigsaw Reconstruction method
24
is used, which is based on the use of jigsaw rules or algorithms to deal with known and unknown combinatorics. We propose this as a new strategy to discriminate the toponium signal sample from the baseline
t\bar{t}
. Such a strategy might be useful in further understanding the physics of this quasi-bound state.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
II
we present the details regarding the Monte Carlo event samples that we generated for the analysis and also list the pre-selection criteria that we have applied on the events. In Section
III
, we give an outline of the reconstruction strategy that we have followed to reconstruct the
t\bar{t}
final state. In Section
IV
, we present the new variables and perform an analysis comparing the current strategy with the new analysis strategy using pre-selection criteria. Finally, in Section
V
, we present the analysis applying a set of event selection criteria.

III
Reconstruction Strategy for
t\bar{t}
in dilepton final states

In order to reconstruct
t\bar{t}\rightarrow bbW(l\nu)W(l\nu)
processes, which is a combinatoric challenge with both known (
b-jet
pairing with lepton) as well as unmeasured particles (two neutrinos), we employ the Recursive Jigsaw Reconstruction method
24
. As an overview, the algorithm functions by first choosing a decay tree that considers all intermediate decaying particles with measured and invisible final state particles. For each step in the decay tree, the algorithm applies a jigsaw rule that relates the current frame of reference to the next one in the chain as a function of measured and unknown quantities. Finally, these steps are applied recursively until the end of the decay chain.

The prescriptions proposed in
24
for the
t\bar{t}
dilepton channel is followed. Here, final states consisting of two b-jets and oppositely charged leptons which could be
ee
,
\mu\mu
, or
e\mu
are considered. The missing transverse energy is considered as the transverse momentum of a dineutrino system. The decay tree used in the analysis is shown in Figure
2
.

Figure 2
:
A decay tree diagram for the process
t\bar{t}\rightarrow bW(\ell\nu)bW(\ell\nu)
.

As shown in the decay tree, there are four visible final states and two invisible states. The algorithm first pairs the lepton and b-quark by minimising the following function:

f=\rm{min}\{\penalty\ M_{\ell_{a},b_{a}}^{2}+M_{\ell_{b},b_{b}}^{2}\}

(1)

This is motivated as the combination of four four-vectors for decay products and is expected to be smaller for particles coming from a common parent particle. Since the final state consists of four visible particles originating at different decay steps, it allows one to choose how the total invisible system is split into two final state invisible particles. Namely, one can choose (i)
M(\ell_{a},b_{a},\nu_{a})=M(\ell_{b},b_{b},\nu_{b})
, which leads to the constraint
M_{ta}=M_{tb}
, or (ii)
M(\ell_{a},\nu_{a})=M(\ell_{b},\nu_{b})
, which leads to the constraint
M_{Wa}=M_{Wb}
. Alternatively, the momentum of the dineutrino could also be split according to the minimisation of the following two functions: (iii)
|M_{t_{a}}^{2}+M_{t_{b}}^{2}|
, or (iv)
|M_{t_{a}}-M_{t_{b}}|
.

Thus the four methods used in this analysis are as follows:

A
:

M^{a}_{\rm top}=M^{b}_{\rm top}

B
:

M_{W}^{a}=M_{W}^{b}

C
:

min
\Sigma M_{\rm top}^{2}

D
:

min
\Delta M_{\rm top}

m_{t\bar{t}}

VI
Conclusion

We presented the Recursive Jigsaw Reconstruction method to reconstruct the toponium quasi-bound state at the Large Hadron Collider considering the LHC Run 2 and Run 3 collision energies. We propose a new analysis strategy where variables such as
Nc_{\rm hel}
and
\Delta\Phi(t,\bar{t})
are introduced and which rely on the performance of the reconstruction framework. The
Nc_{\rm hel}
variable, which is the modified version of the
c_{\rm hel}
variable has high discriminating power. Our results indicate that the maximum significance achieved using the
c_{\rm hel}\otimes c_{\rm han}
approach is about
11.9\sigma
at LHC Run 3, and the newly proposed strategy can allow the significance as high as
15.3\sigma
before numerical optimisation. On applying stricter event selection rules, one sees the significance drops down in both strategies. However, optimisation on
Nc_{\rm hel}
results in a significance of
14.1\sigma
after all applied selection criteria, which when compared to the
c_{\rm hel}\otimes c_{\rm han}
strategy leads to an improvement by 16%. Furthermore, the Recursive Jigsaw Reconstruction provides additional scale sensitive and angular properties that can be used to distinguish toponium from conventional
t\bar{t}
and promise to improve discrimination between the two processes beyond the current level. Such variables will also enhance sensitivity to the search for evidence of the existence of additional states.

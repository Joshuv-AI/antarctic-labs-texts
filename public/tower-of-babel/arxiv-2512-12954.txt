Linear convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations
Felipe Atenas, Farhana A. Simi, Matthew K. Tam

Abstract
We establish linear convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations as introduced by Atenas et al. (2025) assuming the algorithmic operator satisfies a linear error bound. In particular, this framework applies to the setting where the algorithmic operator is a contraction. As a key application of our framework, we obtain linear convergence of the relocated Douglas–Rachford algorithm for finding a zero in the sum of two monotone operators in a setting with Lipschitz continuity and strong monotonicity assumptions. We also apply the framework to deduce linear convergence of variable stepsize resolvent splitting algorithms for multioperator monotone inclusions.



Keywords.

fixed-point iterations, error bound, linear convergence, resolvent splitting.

Introduction

Many algorithms in optimization can be formulated and analyzed as
fixed-point iterations
; see, for example,
[
17
,
15
,
13
,
26
,
28
,
10
]
. Given an initial point
x_{0}
in a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a real parameter
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
and an operator
T_{\gamma}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
, its fixed-point iteration generates a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
according to

x_{n+1}:=T_{\gamma}x_{n}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1)

Under suitable conditions on
T_{\gamma}
, the fixed-point sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a fixed-point of
T_{\gamma}
. For example, when
T_{\gamma}
is
nonexpansive
and
asymptotically regular
, Opial
[
24
]
established convergence of
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the weak topology. In many situations, whether or not
T_{\gamma}
satisfies these suitable conditions, performance depends on an appropriate choice of the parameter
\gamma
.

For example, consider the structured minimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}f(x)+g(x),

(2)

where
f:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth and
g:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is nonsmooth. The
proximal gradient method
defines the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
via (
1
) with

T_{\gamma}:=\prox_{\gamma g}\circ\big(\Id-\gamma\nabla f),

(3)

where
\prox_{\gamma g}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
denotes the
proximity operator
of
\gamma g
defined by

\prox_{\gamma g}:=\argmin_{y\in\mathcal{H}}\left\{g(y)+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|y-\cdot\|^{2}\right\}.

In the standard convex setting when
\nabla f
is assumed to be
L
-Lipschitz continuous, the proximal gradient method converges to a fixed-point of (
3
) so long as
\gamma\in(0,2/L)
, and the fixed-points of (
3
) are the minimizers of
f+g
. When
L
is not known, a suitable
\gamma
can be computed via a linesearch. However, this does not directly fit within (
1
) as this requires
\gamma
to vary across iterations. For other settings where convergence is known, see
[
18
,
16
]
.

When
f
and
g
in (
2
) are both nonsomooth, the
Douglas-Rachford
method
[
12
,
19
]
can be used. This method can be described as the fixed-point iteration (
1
) given by the
Douglas–Rachford operator
defined by

T_{\gamma}:=\Id-\prox_{\gamma f}+\prox_{\gamma g}(2\prox_{\gamma f}-\Id).

(4)

In the convex setting, the fixed-point sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for the Douglas–Rachford method converges (weakly) to a point
x
which is a fixed-point of the Douglas–Rachford operator
[
12
,
30
]
. However, in contrast to the proximal gradient method, the fixed-point
x
does not solve the original problem, rather its
shadow

\prox_{\gamma f}(x)
does. In fact, the fixed-point set of the Douglas–Rachford operator is known to be dependent on the stepsize
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
, and for different parameters can even be disjoint
[
2
, Remark 4.13]
. As a consequence, the standard approach to analyzing fixed-point iterations (such as Opial
[
24
]
) can not be applied to the Douglas–Rachford method or its derivatives
[
11
,
27
]
when
\gamma
is not constant.

To address the issue of analyzing fixed-point iterations with varying stepsize parameters, Atenas, Bauschke, Dao and Tam
[
2
]
introduced the concept of
relocated fixed-point iterations
. This framework is able to deal with fixed-point iterations defined by a parameterized family of operators. More precisely, given a set of parameters
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
and a family of operators
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, a relocated fixed-point iteration is defined as

x_{n+1}=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

where the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is called the
fixed-point relocator
(see Definition
2.5
below). For a convergent sequence
\gamma_{n}\to\gamma^{*}
in
\Gamma
that satisfies a summability condition, the authors show convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations to a point in the fixed-point set of
T_{\gamma^{*}}

(see Theorem
2.8
below). Importantly, this framework still applies to settings where the operators
(T_{\gamma_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
have no common fixed point, such as the Douglas–Rachford method.

Briefly, let us mention other approaches in the literature that consider fixed-point iterations with varying operators. Given a family of operators
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, it is common to consider the a fixed-point iteration of the form

x_{n+1}=T_{n}x_{n}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

In this context, Maulén, Fierro and Peypouquet
[
22
]
examine inertial Krasnoselskii-Mann iterations with variable parameters, and Cegielski, Reich and Zalas
[
9
]
investigate fixed-point iterations under regularity assumptions on the family of operators. In both settings, it is assumed that the operators from
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
have a common fixed-point which, as discussed in the previous paragraph, need not hold when
T_{n}
is the operator
T_{\gamma_{n}}
in (
4
)
[
2
, Remark 4.13]
. In the limit, the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is shown to converge to a point
x\in\cap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Fix T_{n}
.

In this paper, we investigate the convergence rate of relocated fixed-point iterations. In particular, we show
R
-linear convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations when the operators
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
satisfy a regularity condition called
bounded linear regularity
. A notable aspect of our analysis is that the underlying iteration is not
Fejér monotone
. We then apply this general framework to obtain linear convergence of variable stepsize resolvent splitting methods. When the stepsize is constant, Giselsson and Boyd
[
14
]
previously obtained linear rates of convergence for the Douglas–Rachford algorithm in the optimization case assuming one of the functions is strongly convex with Lipschitz continuous gradient. Our analysis cover this setting as a particular instance of Corollary
4.3
. In
[
29
]
, Simi and Tam also utilize model assumptions of similar nature to characterize the rate of convergence for a multioperator extension of the Douglas–Rachford method. In the nonmonotone case, Atenas
[
3
]
uses an error bound akin to (bounded) linear regularity to establish rates of convergence of the Douglas-Rachford method for nonconvex optimization problems.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present some preliminary results used in our analysis. In Section
3
, we show our main results concerning linear convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations under bounded linear regularity of the family of operators. In Sections
4
and
5
, we use our results to derive linear convergence of the relocated Douglas–Rachford and Malitsky–Tam algorithms, respectively. Finally, concluding remarks are presented in Section
6
.

Throughout this work,
\mathcal{H}

\|\cdot\|

A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}

A:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
. The

, set of

\dom A:=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:Ax\neq\emptyset\}

\gra A:=\{(x,u)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}:u\in Ax\},\Fix A:=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:x\in Ax\}

\zer A:=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:0\in Ax\}
, respectively. The

is denoted
\Id:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
. The inverse of an operator
A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is denoted
A^{-1}:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
, whose graph is defined as
\gra A^{-1}:=\{(y,x)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}:y\in Ax\}
. Observe that a direct calculation assures that for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
,

(\gamma A)^{-1}=A^{-1}\circ(\gamma^{-1}\Id).

(5)

An operator
A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is said to be
monotone
if

\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall(x,y),(u,v)\in\gra A,

and it is

if there exists no monotone operator whose graph properly contains
\gra A
.
It is said to be
\mu
-strongly monotone
if
\mu>0
and

\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq\mu\|x-y\|^{2}\quad\forall(x,y),(u,v)\in\gra A.

Given an ordered pair of operators
(A_{1},A_{2})
on
\mathcal{H}
, the
Attouch–Théra primal problem

[
4
]
is the inclusion given by

\text{find\penalty\ }x\in\mathcal{H}\text{\penalty\ s.t.\penalty\ }0\in(A_{1}+A_{2})x,

(6)

and the
Attouch–Théra dual problem
is the inclusion given by

\text{find\penalty\ }y\in\mathcal{H}\text{\penalty\ s.t.\penalty\ }0\in A_{1}^{-1}y-A_{2}^{-1}(-y).

(7)

Note that problems (
6
) and (
7
) are equivalent in the sense that a solution to (
7
) can be recovered from a solution of (
6
), and vice versa
[
4
, Corollary 3.2]
. Attouch-Théra duality recovers
Fenchel duality
from convex analysis under appropriate constraint qualifications
[
4
, Theorem 4.2]
.

A monotone operator
A\colon\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is said to be
paramonotone
if

(x,y),(u,v)\in\gra A\text{\penalty\ and\penalty\ }\langle x-u,y-v\rangle=0\implies(x,v),(u,y)\in\gra A.

When
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are paramonotone operators, and
T_{\gamma}
is the Douglas–Rachford operator in (
4
), then the fixed-points of
T_{\gamma}
can be expressed as the sum of primal and rescaled dual solutions, as shown in the following lemma (cf.
[
25
, Proposition 1]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
and
A_{1},A_{2}\colon\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
be maximally monotone. Consider the Attouch–Théra primal-dual pair of problems

\displaystyle\text{find\penalty\ }x

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}:=\zer(\gamma A_{1}+\gamma A_{2}),

\displaystyle\text{find\penalty\ }y

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}:=\zer((\gamma A_{1})^{-1}-(\gamma A_{2})^{-1}\circ(-\Id)).

Denote
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{1}
. Then
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}
and
\gamma\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}
. Furthermore, if, in addition,
T_{\gamma}
is the Douglas-Rachford operator (
4
) and
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are paramonotone, then

\Fix T_{\gamma}=\mathcal{P}+\gamma\mathcal{D}.

(8)

Proof.

First, from definition, it holds that
\mathcal{P}:=\zer(A_{1}+A_{2})=\zer(\gamma A_{1}+\gamma A_{2})
. In view of (
5
), we therefore have
y\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}\iff 0\in A_{1}^{-1}(\gamma^{-1}y)-A_{2}^{-1}(-\gamma^{-1}y)\iff 0\in A_{1}^{-1}w-A_{2}^{-1}w
and
y=\gamma w\iff y\in\gamma\mathcal{D}
.

Next, observe that the operator
T_{\gamma}
in (
4
) is associated with the Attouch–Théra primal problem
0\in(\gamma A_{1}+\gamma A_{2})x
, and the Attouch–Théra dual problem
0\in(\gamma A_{1})^{-1}y-(\gamma A_{2})^{-1}(-y)
. When
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are paramonotone, then
\gamma A_{1}
and
\gamma A_{2}
are also paramonotone. Hence, (

) follows from
[
5
, Corollary 5.5(iii)]
.
∎

A single-valued operator
T:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is said to be
\beta
-Lipschitz
if
\beta\geq 0
and

\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\beta\|x-y\|\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

An operator is said to be a
\beta
-contraction
if it is
\beta
-Lipschitz with
\beta\in[0,1)

-Lipschitz, and

\|Tx-Ty\|^{2}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\|(\Id-T)x-(\Id-T)y\|^{2}\leq\|x-y\|^{2}\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

The resolvent of an operator
A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is defined by
J_{A}:=(\Id+A)^{-1}
. When

Remark 2.2
.

Let

Taking the limit as

Definition 2.3

(Strongly monotone relative to a set)

Let

\langle v-u,y-P_{X}(y)\rangle\geq\mu\|y-P_{X}(y)\|^{2}\quad\forall(y,v)\in\operatorname{gra}A,\,u\in(A\circ P_{X})y.

Similar to the observation in
[
25

, if
A
is
\mu
-strongly monotone, then it is
\mu
-strongly monotone relative to any nonempty closed convex set

. And, if
A=K^{*}BK
where

is
\mu
-strongly monotone and

is a bounded linear operator, then
A
is
\mu(\sigma^{+}_{\min}(K))^{2}
-strongly monotone relative to
K^{-1}(X)\subseteq\dom A
for any nonempty closed convex subset

, where
\sigma_{\min}^{+}(K)
denotes the small positive singular value of

; see
[

\gamma A
and its inverse

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
and
A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
be a set-valued operator. Suppose

and

are nonempty closed convex sets. Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

If
A
is
\mu
-strongly monotone relative to
X
, then
\gamma A
is
\gamma\mu
-strongly monotone relative to
X
.

(ii)

If
A^{-1}
is
\mu
-strongly monotone relative to
Y
, then
(\gamma A)^{-1}
is
\gamma^{-1}\mu
-strongly monotone relative to
\gamma Y
.

Proof.

(

) Let
(y,v)\in\gra(\gamma A)
and
u\in((\gamma A)\circ P_{X})y
. Then
(y,\gamma^{-1}v)\in\gra A
and
\gamma^{-1}u\in(A\circ P_{X})y
. Since

is
\mu
-strongly monotone relative to

, we have

\langle\gamma^{-1}v-\gamma^{-1}u,y-P_{X}(y)\rangle\geq\mu\|y-P_{X}(y)\|^{2}.

(

(y,v)\in\gra(\gamma A)^{-1}

In the following, we recall the definition of fixed-point relocators for a family of operators. Fixed-point relocators play a crucial role in the convergence analysis introduced in
[
2
]
, as they map fixed-point sets to fixed-point sets when the stepsize parameter varies alongside iterations. More specifically, given
\delta,\gamma>0
, a fixed-point relocator is an operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
for an algorithmic operator
T_{\gamma}
that, in particular, satisfies: for any
x\in\Fix T_{\gamma}
, it holds
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}x\in\Fix T_{\delta}
. Namely,
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
relocates fixed-points of
T_{\gamma}
to fixed-points of
T_{\delta}
. Definition
2.5
formalizes this idea, and also states continuity properties necessary for the convergence results presented in Theorem
2.8
.

Definition 2.5

(Fixed-point relocator
[
2
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be a nonempty set and let
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of operators from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}
. The family of operators
(\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\delta,\gamma\in\Gamma}
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
fixed-point relocators
for
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
with Lipschitz constants
(\mathcal{L_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}})_{\delta,\gamma\in\Gamma}
in
[1,\infty)
if the following hold:

(i)

(\forall\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma)

\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma|\Fix T_{\gamma}}
is a bijection from
\Fix T_{\gamma}
to
\Fix T_{\delta}
.

(ii)

(\forall\gamma\in\Gamma)(\forall x\in\Fix T_{\gamma})

\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{H}:\delta\mapsto\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}x
is continuous.

(iii)

(\forall\gamma,\delta,\epsilon\in\Gamma)(\forall x\in\Fix T_{\gamma})

\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon\leftarrow\delta}\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}x=\mathcal{Q_{\epsilon\leftarrow\gamma}}x
.

(iv)

(\forall\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma)

\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
is
\mathcal{L_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}}
-Lipschitz continuous.

Remark 2.6
.

For any
\epsilon\in\Gamma
, the relocator
\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon\leftarrow\epsilon}
coincides with the identity operator on
\Fix T_{\epsilon}
. To see this, let
\gamma,\epsilon\in\Gamma
and
y\in\Fix T_{\epsilon}
. Then
x:=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma\leftarrow\epsilon}y\in\Fix T_{\gamma}
due to Definition
2.5
(
i
). Applying Definition
2.5
(
iii
) yields
\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon\leftarrow\epsilon}y=\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon\leftarrow\epsilon}\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon\leftarrow\gamma}x=\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon\leftarrow\gamma}x=y
, establishing the claim.

Theorem 2.8

(Convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}

(\mathcal{L}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma}

Suppose the map
\mathcal{H}\times\Gamma\to\mathcal{H}\colon(x,\gamma)\mapsto T_{\gamma}x

(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\Gamma

\gamma^{*}\in\Gamma

Let

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
according to

x_{n+1}:=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(10)

Then
(x_{n}-T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges strongly to zero, and
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly to some
x\in\Fix T_{\gamma^{*}}
.

Theorem
2.8
establishes weak convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations but says nothing about the rate.

In what follows, we investigate the rate at which relocated fixed-point iterates converge assuming a linear error bound holds (see Definition
3.1
below). To formalize this, recall that a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is said to converge
R
-linearly
to
x\in\mathcal{H}
if there exist
C\in\mathbb{R_{+}}
and
r\in(0,1)
such that
\|x_{n}-x\|\leq Cr^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that, in particular, this implies that
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges strongly to
x
.

3
Linear convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations

In this section, we establish linear convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations. The key ingredient in our analysis is the following notion of bounded linear regularity for a family of operators.

Definition 3.1

(Uniform linear regularity)
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be a nonempty set and
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of operators on
\mathcal{H}
. We say
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is
uniformly boundedly linearly regular
if, for any nonempty bounded set
S\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, there exists
\kappa>0
such that

\dist(x,\Fix T_{\gamma})\leq\kappa\|x-T_{\gamma}x\|\quad\forall x\in S,\forall\gamma\in\Gamma.

(11)

When (
11
) holds with
S=\mathcal{H}
, we say
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is
uniformly linearly regular
(with constant
\kappa
).

Remark 3.2
.

Some comments on Definition
3.1
are in order. When
\Gamma=\{\gamma^{*}\}
is a singleton, uniform (bounded) linear regularity for the family
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
reduces to
(bounded) linear regularity
of operator
T_{\gamma^{*}}
(see
[
7
, Definition 2.1]
), which is closely related to
metric subregularity
of
(\Id-T)
. When
\Gamma
is finite, (bounded) linear regularity is one of the key ingredients for analyzing convergence of the “cyclic algorithm” for finding a common fixed-point assuming one exists
[
7
, Corollary 6.2]
(
i.e.,
an element of
\cap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\Fix T_{\gamma}
). See also
[
8
]
for discussion on Hölder versions of these properties.

When
\Gamma
is countable,
[
9
, Definition 4.1]
proposes a regularity notion for sequences of operators. Precisely, given a nonempty closed convex set
C\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, a sequence of operators
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is said to be
boundedly linearly
C
-regular
if, for any bounded set
S\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, there exists
\kappa>0
such that

\dist(x,C)\leq\kappa\|x-T_{n}x\|\quad\forall x\in S,\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

As demonstrated in
[
9
]
, this regularity condition is crucial for analyzing the rate of convergence of fixed-point iterations of the type
x_{n+1}=T_{n}x_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
for finding a point in
C:=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Fix T_{n}
when one exists (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem 6.1]
). Although nonemptyness of
C
is a reasonable assumption in feasibility problems (for instance), as noted in
[
2
, Remark 4.13]
, it need not hold in the setting of relocated fixed-point iterations, even for simple structured optimization problems.

In the following, Lemma
3.3
shows that a family of contractions satisfies the condition of uniform (bounded) linear regularity. The result extends
[
32
, Lemma 5.5]
, which shows that a single contraction mapping satisfies linearly regularity.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be a nonempty set and
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of
\beta
-contractions on
\mathcal{H}
. Then
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is uniformly linearly regular with constant
\kappa=1/(1-\beta)
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
be arbitrary. Since
T_{\gamma}
is a contraction, it has a unique fixed-point
[
6
, Theorem 1.50]
. That is,
\Fix T_{\gamma}=\{x_{\gamma}\}
for some
x_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{H}
. For any
x\in\mathcal{H}
, the triangle inequality and the fact that
T_{\gamma}
is a
\beta
-contraction yields

\|x-x_{\gamma}\|=\|x-T_{\gamma}x_{\gamma}\|\leq\|x-T_{\gamma}x\|+\|T_{\gamma}x-T_{\gamma}x_{\gamma}\|\leq\|x-T_{\gamma}x\|+\beta\|x-x_{\gamma}\|.

(12)

Rearranging (
12
) yields

\dist(x,\Fix T_{\gamma})=\|x-x_{\gamma}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\beta}\|x-T_{\gamma}x\|,

which establishes the result.
∎

Now we consider the following assumption which plays a key role for establishing the linear convergence result, by relating the rate of convergence of the stepsize sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to the relocated fixed-point iterations.

Assumption 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be a nonempty set,
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of operators on
\mathcal{H}
, and
(\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma}
be fixed-point relocators for
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
on
\mathcal{H}
.
For each bounded subset
S\subseteq\cup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\bigl(\Fix T_{\gamma}\times\{\gamma\}\bigr)
,
there exists a constant
L>0
such that

\|\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}x-\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma\leftarrow\gamma}x\|\leq L|\delta-\gamma|\quad\forall\delta\in\Gamma,\,\forall(x,\gamma)\in S.

In Section
4
, we will show that the fixed-point relocators in Example
2.7
for the Douglas–Rachford operators satisfy Assumption
3.4
(see Lemma
4.1
).

The following establishes linear convergence of a relocated fixed-point iterations initialized at a fixed-point of
T_{\gamma_{0}}
. This auxiliary result will be useful in the main result of this section, Theorem
3.6
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be a nonempty set,
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of operators on
\mathcal{H}
with
\Fix T_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, and
(\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma}
be fixed-point relocators for
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
on
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose Assumption
3.4
holds, and
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\Gamma
is a sequence that converges
R
-linearly to
\gamma^{*}\in\Gamma
.
Given
c_{0}\in\Fix T_{\gamma_{0}}
, generate a sequence
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
according to

c_{n+1}:=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}c_{n}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(13)

Then,
c_{n}\in\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly to
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma^{*}\leftarrow\gamma_{0}}c_{0}\in\Fix T_{\gamma^{*}}
.

Proof.

We first show that
c_{n}\in\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
c_{n}\in\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}
, then Definition
2.5
(
i
) implies

c_{n+1}=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}c_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}(\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}})\subseteq\Fix T_{\gamma_{n+1}}.

Since
c_{0}\in\Fix T_{\gamma_{0}}
by assumption, the result follows by induction on
n
.

We now turn our attention to convergence of
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Set
c:=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma^{*}\leftarrow\gamma_{0}}c_{0}
. Then Definition
2.5
(
i
) yields
c\in\Fix T_{\gamma^{*}}
. Combining Remark
2.6
and Definition
2.5
(
iii
) gives

c_{0}=Q_{\gamma_{0}\leftarrow\gamma_{0}}c_{0}=Q_{\gamma_{0}\leftarrow\gamma^{*}}Q_{\gamma^{*}\leftarrow\gamma_{0}}c_{0}=Q_{\gamma_{0}\leftarrow\gamma^{*}}c.

(14)

Since
c_{n}\in\Fix T_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, Definition
2.5
(
iii
) together with (
14
) gives

c_{n}=(Q_{\gamma_{n}\leftarrow\gamma_{n-1}}\dots Q_{\gamma_{2}\leftarrow\gamma_{1}}Q_{\gamma_{1}\leftarrow\gamma_{0}})c_{0}=Q_{\gamma_{n}\leftarrow\gamma_{0}}c_{0}=Q_{\gamma_{n}\leftarrow\gamma_{0}}Q_{\gamma_{0}\leftarrow\gamma^{*}}c=Q_{\gamma_{n}\leftarrow\gamma^{*}}c\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

By Assumption
3.4
, there exists
L>0
such that

\|c_{n}-c\|=\|\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n}\leftarrow\gamma^{*}}c-Q_{\gamma^{*}\leftarrow\gamma^{*}}c\|\leq L|\gamma_{n}-\gamma^{*}|.

(15)

Since
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly to
\gamma^{*}
, (
15
) implies
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly to
c
. This completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main result (Theorem
3.6
) concerning linear convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations under uniform bounded linear regularity.

Theorem 3.6

(Rate of convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations)
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be a nonempty set,
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of
\alpha
-averaged nonexpansive operators with
\Fix{T_{\gamma}}\neq\emptyset
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, and
(\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma})_{\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma}
be fixed-point relocators for
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
on
\mathcal{H}
with Lipschitz constant
(\mathcal{L_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}})_{\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma}
in
[1,+\infty)
. Suppose Assumption
3.4
holds,
\mathcal{H}\times\Gamma\to\mathcal{H}\colon(x,\gamma)\mapsto T_{\gamma}x
is continuous,
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is uniformly boundedly linearly regular,
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\Gamma
converges
R
-linearly to
\gamma^{*}\in\Gamma
and
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathcal{L}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}-1)<+\infty
.
Given
x_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
, generate a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
according to

x_{n+1}:=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(16)

Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

\big(\dist(x_{n},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly to zero.

(ii)

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge
R
-linearly to the same point in
\Fix T_{\gamma^{*}}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.8
, the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly to some
x\in\Fix T_{\gamma^{*}}
, hence, in particular, the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. Thus, since
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is uniformly boundedly linearly regular, there exists a
\kappa>0
such that

\dist(x_{n},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}})\leq\kappa\|x_{n}-T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}\|\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(17)

For convenience, denote
\ell_{n}:=\mathcal{L}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}
and
p_{n}:=P_{\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}}x_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The latter is well-defined as
\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}
is a nonempty, closed, convex set
[
6
, Corollary 4.24 & Theorem 4.29]
.
We also claim that
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. To see this, let
c_{0}\in\Fix T_{\gamma_{0}}
and define
c_{n+1}:=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}c_{n}
. Then, by Lemma
3.5
,
c_{n}\in\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges (so, in particular, is bounded). Since
P_{\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}}
is nonexpansive, we have

\|p_{n}-c_{n}\|=\|P_{\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}}x_{n}-P_{\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}}c_{n}\|\leq\|x_{n}-c_{n}\|,

from which boudnedness of
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
follows.

(
i
) Since
p_{n}\in\Fix_{T_{\gamma_{n}}}
, Definition
2.5
(
i
) implies
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}p_{n}\in\Fix T_{\gamma_{n+1}}
. From Definition
2.5
(
iv
), we also have that
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}
is
\ell_{n}
-Lipschitz continuous. Hence, as
T_{\gamma_{n}}
is
\alpha
-averaged and (
17
) holds, we have

\displaystyle\dist^{2}(x_{n+1},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n+1}})

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n+1}-\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}p_{n}\|^{2}

(18)

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}-\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}p_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{n}^{2}\|T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}-p_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{n}^{2}(\|x_{n}-p_{n}\|^{2}-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\|(\Id-T_{\gamma_{n}})x_{n}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{n}^{2}(\dist^{2}(x_{n},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}})-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha\kappa^{2}}\dist^{2}(x_{n},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}))

\displaystyle=\ell_{n}^{2}(1-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha\kappa^{2}})\dist^{2}(x_{n},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}).

As
\ell_{n}\to 1^{+}
and
1-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha\kappa^{2}}<1
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in(0,1)
such that
\ell_{n}^{2}(1-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha\kappa^{2}})<r^{2}<1
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Hence (
18
) implies

\dist(x_{n+1},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n+1}})\leq r\dist(x_{n},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}})\leq\dots\leq r^{n+1-n_{0}}\dist(x_{n_{0}},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n_{0}}}),

(19)

from which
R
-linear convergence to zero follows.

(
ii
) Since
p_{n}\in\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}
, Definition
2.5
(
i
) implies
v_{k}:=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{k}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}p_{n}\in\Fix{T_{\gamma_{k}}}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by Remark
2.6
and Definition
2.5
(
iii
), we have
v_{n}=p_{n}
and

v_{k+1}=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{k+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}p_{n}=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{k+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{k}}\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{k}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}p_{n}=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{k+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{k}}T_{\gamma_{k}}v_{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\ell_{n}-1)<+\infty
, it follows that
M:=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\ell_{n}<+\infty
. Hence, by combing
\ell_{n}
-Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}
,
\alpha
-averagendess of
T_{\gamma_{n}}
and
\ell_{n}\geq 1
, we obtain

\|x_{n+m}-v_{n+m}\|\leq\left(\prod_{k=n}^{n+m-1}\ell_{k}\right)\|x_{n}-v_{n}\|\leq M\|x_{n}-p_{n}\|.

(20)

Since
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are bounded, Assumption
3.4
implies that there exists
L>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+m}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}p_{n}-p_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+m}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}p_{n}-\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}p_{n}\|

(21)

\displaystyle\leq L|\gamma_{n+m}-\gamma_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq L(|\gamma_{n+m}-\gamma^{*}|+|\gamma^{*}-\gamma_{n}|).

Applying the triangle inequality followed by substituting (
20
) and (
21
) gives

\displaystyle\|x_{n+m}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n+m}-v_{n+m}\|+\|v_{n+m}-p_{n}\|+\|p_{n}-x_{n}\|

(22)

\displaystyle\leq M\|x_{n}-p_{n}\|+\|\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+m}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}p_{n}-p_{n}\|+\|x_{n}-p_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq(M+1)\dist(x_{n},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}})+L(|\gamma_{n+m}-\gamma^{*}|+|\gamma^{*}-\gamma_{n}|).

Since
\big(\dist(x_{n},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}})\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly to zero and
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly to
\gamma^{*}
, we conclude that
(\|x_{n+m}-x_{n}\|)_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly to zero. In particular, this means that
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence, and hence it converges strongly. By taking the limit as
m\to+\infty
in (
22
), we therefore deduce that
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly to
x
.

Finally, since
T_{\gamma_{n}}
is
\alpha
-averaged and
p_{n}\in\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}
, we have

\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\|(\Id-T_{\gamma_{n}})x_{n}\|^{2}\leq\|x_{n}-p_{n}\|^{2}=\dist^{2}(x_{n},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}}).

Together with the triangle inequality, this yields

\|T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}-x\|\leq\|T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}-x_{n}\|+\|x_{n}-x\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\dist(x_{n},\Fix T_{\gamma_{n}})+\|x_{n}-x\|,

from which it follows that
(T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges strongly and
R
-linearly to
x
.
The proof is now complete.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

A closer look at the proof of Theorem

(

) reveals that the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

Remark 3.9
.

When the sequence of stepsizes
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not converge
R
-linearly in Corollary
3.8
(hence also in Theorem
3.6
), then linear convergence of the relocated fixed-point algorithm need not hold. To see this, let
\beta\in[0,1)
and for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, define the operator
T_{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
according to

T_{\gamma}x:=\gamma+\beta(x-\gamma).

Then, for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Fix T_{\gamma}=\{\gamma\}
and

\|T_{\gamma}x-T_{\gamma}y\|=\beta\|x-y\|\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular,
T_{\gamma}
is a
\beta
-contraction with fixed-point relocator
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}=\delta
for any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a positive sequence which converges to some
\gamma^{*}>0
. Set
x_{0}=\gamma_{0}
and consider the relocated fixed-point iteration given by

x_{n+1}=\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}T_{\gamma_{n}}x_{n}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

A direct computation using shows that
x_{n}=\gamma_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly if and only if
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly.

4
The relocated Douglas–Rachford algorithm

In this section, we apply the results established in Section
3
to derive linear convergence of the
relocated Douglas–Rachford algorithm

[
2
]
, which is also known as the
non-stationary Douglas–Rachford algorithm
in
[
20
]
. Given
A_{1},A_{2}:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
are maximally monotone operators, the (relocated) Douglas-Rachford algorithm aims to

\text{find\penalty\ }x\in\mathcal{H}\text{\penalty\ such that\penalty\ }0\in(A_{1}+A_{2})x.

As outlined in the Introduction, the Douglas–Rachford algorithm generates a sequence
({x}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
via
{x}_{n+1}=T_{\gamma}{x}_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
T_{\gamma}
is the operator in (
4
). This sequence
({x}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly to a point
{x}\in\zer(A_{1}+A_{2})
, as long as
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are maximally monotone, and
\zer(A_{1}+A_{2})\neq\varnothing
.

The relocated Douglas–Rachford framework extends the classical setting by introducing relocators that allow the stepsize to vary while still guaranteeing convergence.
Given
\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{R_{++}}
, let
T_{\gamma}
denote the Douglas–Rachford operator defined in (
4
) and let
Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
be the fixed-point relocator introduced in
[
2
]
defined in (
9
).
Given an initial point
x_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
and a sequence of stepsizes
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
, the resulting relocated Douglas–Rachford sequence
[
2
]

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be a nonempty closed interval,
T_{\gamma}
be the Douglas–Rachford operator given in (

Q_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
be the fixed-point relocator given by (

, there exists
L\geq 0
such that

\|\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}x-\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma\leftarrow\gamma}x\|\leq L|\delta-\gamma|\quad\forall\delta\in\Gamma,\forall(x,\gamma)\in S.

In other words, Assumption
3.4
holds.

Proof.

For any
(x,\gamma)\in S
and
\delta\in\Gamma
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}x-\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma\leftarrow\gamma}x\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\delta}{\gamma}x+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)J_{\gamma A_{1}}x-x\right\|

(27)

\displaystyle=|\delta-\gamma|\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A_{1}}x\|}{\gamma}.

We claim that
\sup_{(x,\gamma)\in S}\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A_{1}}x\|}{\gamma}<+\infty
. To see this, first note that the triangle inequality combined with nonexpansiveness of
J_{\gamma A_{1}}
implies

\|x-J_{\gamma A_{1}}x\|\leq\|x-J_{\gamma A_{1}}0\|+\|J_{\gamma A_{1}}0-J_{\gamma A_{1}}x\|\leq\|J_{\gamma A_{1}}0\|+2\|x\|.

As
\gamma\mapsto J_{\gamma A_{1}}0
is continuous in view of Remark
2.2
and
\Gamma
is compact in
\mathbb{R}
,
\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\|J_{\gamma A_{1}}0\|<+\infty
and, since

is bounded,
\sup_{(x,\gamma)\in S}\|x\|<+\infty
. Altogether, we have

\sup_{(x,\gamma)\in S}\frac{\|x-J_{\gamma A_{1}}x\|}{\gamma}\leq\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\|J_{\gamma A_{1}}0\|}{\gamma}+2\sup_{(x,\gamma)\in S}\frac{\|x\|}{\gamma}<+\infty,

which establishes the result.

be the fixed-point relocator given by (
34
). Then, for any nonempty bounded set
S\subseteq\cup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\bigl(\Fix T_{\gamma}\times\{\gamma\}\bigr)
, there exists
L\geq 0
such that

\|\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}-\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\gamma\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}\|\leq L|\delta-\gamma|\quad\forall\delta\in\Gamma,\forall(\mathbf{x},\gamma)\in S.

In other words, Assumption
3.4
holds.

Proof.

For any
(\mathbf{x},\gamma)\in S
and
\delta\in\Gamma
, similarly to (
27
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}^{1}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}-\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}^{1}_{\gamma\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\delta}{\gamma}x_{1}+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}-x^{1}\right\|

(36)

\displaystyle=|\delta-\gamma|\frac{\|x^{1}-J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}\|}{\gamma},

and for
i=2\dots,N-1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}^{i}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}-\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}^{i}_{\gamma\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\delta}{\gamma}(x^{i}-x^{1})+\frac{\delta}{\gamma}x^{1}+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}-x^{i}\right\|

(37)

\displaystyle=|\delta-\gamma|\frac{\|x^{i}-J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}\|}{\gamma}.

To complete the proof, it suffices to show that
\sup_{(\mathbf{x},\gamma)\in S}\frac{\|x^{i}-J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}\|}{\gamma}<+\infty
for each
i=1,\dots,N-1
. This follows by using a similar arrangement to the one in Lemma
4.1
.
∎

The next lemma provides conditions under which the operator
T_{\gamma}
in (
30
)-(
31
) is a contraction.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be nonempty closed interval and
T_{\gamma}
be the operators given in (
30
)-(
31
). Suppose that one of the following holds:

(a)

A_{1},\dots,A_{N-1}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
are monotone and
L
-Lipschitz, and
A_{N}\colon\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is maximally
\mu
-strongly monotone.

(b)

A_{1},\dots,A_{N-1}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
are maximally
\mu
-strongly monotone and
L
-Lipschitz, and
A_{N}\colon\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is maximally monotone.

Then there exists
\beta\in[0,1)
such that
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is a family of
\beta
-contractions.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
is a nonempty closed interval, we may write
\Gamma=[\gamma_{\rm low},\gamma_{\rm high}]
where
0<\gamma_{\rm low}<\gamma_{\rm high}
. Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
be arbitrary.

(a)

Since
A_{1},\dots,A_{N-1}
are
L
-Lipschitz and
A_{N}
is
\mu
-strongly monotone,
\gamma A_{1},\dots,\gamma A_{N-1}
are
(\gamma_{\rm high}L)
-Lipschitz and
\gamma A_{N}
is
(\gamma_{\rm low}\mu)
-strongly monotone.

(b)

Since
A_{1},\dots,A_{N-1}
are
\mu
-strongly monotone and
L
-Lipschitz,
\gamma A_{1},\dots,\gamma A_{N-1}
are
(\gamma_{\rm low}\mu)
-strongly monotone and
(\gamma_{\rm high}L)
-Lipschitz.

In both cases, the strong monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity constants of
\gamma A_{1},\dots,\gamma A_{N}
can be expressed independently of
\gamma
. Hence the result follows from
[
29
, Lemma 3.4]
.
∎

As mentioned above, the operator in (
34
) is a fixed-point relocator of
T_{\gamma}
in (
30
)-(
31
), and a Lipschitz constant is provided in
[
2
, Proposition 5.5]
. In order to obtain the convergence results in this section, we require a Lipschitz constant in a more convenient form. We obtain this new Lipschitz constant by recalling the following identity, valid in any Hilbert space,

\|\alpha u+(1-\alpha)v\|^{2}=\alpha\|u\|^{2}+(1-\alpha)\|v\|^{2}-\alpha(1-\alpha)\|u-v\|\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{H},\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

(38)

Lemma 5.3
.

For
\delta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
, the operator
\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}:\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\to\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
defined in (
34
) is Lipschitz continuous with constant

\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}=\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}+\frac{\sqrt{|\gamma-\delta|}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\max\left\{\sqrt{N-1},\sqrt{2N}\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\gamma}}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x^{1},\dots,x^{N-1}),\mathbf{w}=(w^{1},\dots,w^{N-1})\in\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
. Substituting the first line of (
34
) into its second line yields

\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}^{i}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}=\frac{\delta}{\gamma}x^{i}+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)J_{\gamma A_{1}}x_{1}\text{\penalty\ for\penalty\ }i=1,\dots,N-1.

Thus, from (
38
), it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}-\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\|\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}^{i}\mathbf{x}-\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}^{i}\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)(N-1)\|J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}-J_{\gamma A_{1}}w^{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\|(x^{i}-w^{i})-(J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}-J_{\gamma A_{1}}w^{1})\|^{2}.

We distinguish two cases. If
\delta\leq\gamma
, then
1-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\geq 0
and so

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}-\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)(N-1)\|x^{1}-w^{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)(N-1)\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\delta}{\gamma}+\frac{(N-1)|\gamma-\delta|}{\gamma}\right)\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}.

Else, suppose that
\delta>\gamma
. First note that, by nonexpansiveness of
J_{\gamma A_{1}}
, we have

\|J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}-J_{\gamma A_{1}}w^{1}\|^{2}\leq\|x^{1}-w^{1}\|^{2}

and

\displaystyle\|(x^{i}-w^{i})-(J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}-J_{\gamma A_{1}}w^{1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|x^{i}-w^{i}\|^{2}+2\|J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}-J_{\gamma A_{1}}w^{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|x^{i}-w^{i}\|^{2}+2\|x^{1}-w^{1}\|^{2}.

Thus, since
\frac{\delta}{\gamma}-1>0
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}-\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}-\left(\frac{\delta}{\gamma}-1\right)(N-1)\|J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}-J_{\gamma A_{1}}w^{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\left(\frac{\delta}{\gamma}-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\|(x^{i}-w^{i})-(J_{\gamma A_{1}}x^{1}-J_{\gamma A_{1}}w^{1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\left(\frac{\delta}{\gamma}-1\right)\left(2\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+2(N-1)\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\delta}{\gamma}+2N\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\frac{|\delta-\gamma|}{\gamma}\right)\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}.

Altogether, we get

\|\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{x}-\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\mathbf{w}\|\leq\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{w}\|,

where

\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}=\max\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\gamma}+\frac{(N-1)|\gamma-\delta|}{\gamma}},\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\gamma}+2N\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\frac{|\gamma-\delta|}{\gamma}}\right\}.

A direct calculation using the fact that for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
,
\sqrt{\alpha+\beta}\leq\sqrt{\alpha}+\sqrt{\beta}
yields
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}\leq\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
, from where we conclude the result.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

It is clear from the proof in Lemma
5.3
above that
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
provides a tighter bound for the Lipschitz constant of the fixed-point relocator. Nevertheless, as we shall see in the next result, the Lipschitz constant
\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\delta\leftarrow\gamma}
provides a more simpler convergence analysis in our setting.

Now we are ready to prove the main result regarding linear convergence of Algorithm
2
.

Corollary 5.5

(Rate of convergence of relocated Malitsky–Tam algorithm)
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be nonempty closed interval. Suppose one of the following holds:

(a)

A_{1},\dots,A_{N-1}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
are monotone and
L
-Lipschitz, and
A_{N}:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is maximally
\mu
-strongly monotone.

(b)

A_{1},\dots,A_{N-1}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
are maximally
\mu
-strongly monotone and
L
-Lipschitz, and
A_{N}:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is maximally monotone.

Let
\zer(\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i})\neq\emptyset
,
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\Gamma
converge
R
-linearly to
\gamma^{*}\in\Gamma
, and
T_{\gamma}
be the operator given in (
30
)-(
31
). Given
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
, generate sequences
(\mathbf{x}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
and
(\mathbf{z}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{H}^{N}
according to Algorithm
2
. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

(\mathbf{x}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge
R
-linearly to the same point
\mathbf{x}=(x^{1},\dots,x^{N})\in\Fix T_{\gamma^{*}}
.

(ii)

(\mathbf{z}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge
R
-linearly to a point
(z,\dots,z)\in\mathcal{H}^{N}
with
z:=J_{\gamma^{*}A_{1}}x^{1}\in\zer(\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
, there exists
\beta\in[0,1)
such that
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is a family of
\beta
-contractions. By Lemma
5.1
, Assumption
3.4
holds. Since
(x,\gamma)\mapsto J_{\gamma A_{i}}x
is continuous on
\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
by Remark
2.2
, it follows that
(x,\gamma)\mapsto T_{\gamma}x
is continuous, by algebra and composition of continuous operators.
Next, since
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
R
-linearly to
\gamma^{*}
, then there exist
C\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
r\in[0,1)
and
\gamma_{\rm high},\gamma_{\rm low}\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
, such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
|\gamma_{n}-\gamma^{*}|\leq Cr^{n}
, and
\gamma_{\rm low}\leq\gamma_{n}\leq\gamma_{\rm high}
. In view of Lemma
5.3
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the Lipschitz constant
\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}
of the fixed-point relocator
\mathcal{\tilde{Q}}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}-1

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma_{n+1}}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{n}}}-1+\frac{\sqrt{|\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}|}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{n}}}\max\{\sqrt{N-1},\sqrt{2N}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}}}\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\sqrt{\gamma_{n+1}}-\sqrt{\gamma_{n}}|}{\sqrt{\gamma_{\rm low}}}+\frac{\sqrt{|\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}|}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{\rm low}}}\max\{\sqrt{N-1},\sqrt{2N}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{\rm high}}{\gamma_{\rm low}}}\}

Then, in view of (
28
), we have for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

|\sqrt{\gamma_{n+1}}-\sqrt{\gamma_{n}}|=\frac{|{\gamma_{n+1}}-{\gamma_{n}}|}{\sqrt{\gamma_{n+1}}+\sqrt{\gamma_{n}}}\leq\frac{|{\gamma_{n+1}}-{\gamma_{n}}|}{2\sqrt{\gamma_{\rm low}}}\leq\frac{C(1+r)r^{n}}{2\sqrt{\gamma_{\rm low}}}.

Altogether, as
r\in(0,1)
, there exists
M>0
such that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\check{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma_{n+1}\leftarrow\gamma_{n}}-1)\leq M\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{r}^{n}=\frac{M}{1-\sqrt{r}}<+\infty.

(
i
)

Since
\zer\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i}\bigr)\neq\emptyset
, then
\Fix T_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
by (
32
).
From Corollary
3.8
(ii), it follows that

(x_{n}^{i})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(w_{n}^{i})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge
R
-linearly to the same point
x^{i}
, for all
i=1,\dots,N
.

(
ii
)

Denote

z:=J_{\gamma^{*}A_{1}}(x^{1})=J_{\gamma^{*}A_{i}}(z+x^{i}-x^{i-1})=J_{\gamma^{*}A_{N}}(2z-x^{N-1})\text{\penalty\ for\penalty\ }i=2,\dots,N-1,

(39)

which is justified by (
33
). For
i=1,\dots,N
, the set
S_{i}:=\{(x^{i}_{n},\gamma_{n}):n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{(x^{i},\gamma^{*})\}
is compact by
[
1
, Theorem 2.38]
. Hence, by Remark
2.2
, there exists
L_{i}>0
such that
(y^{i},\gamma)\mapsto J_{\gamma A_{i}}y^{i}
is
L_{i}
-Lipschitz on
S_{i}
. Thus, together with the first line in (
35
) , (
39
), the triangle inequality and nonexpansiveness of
J_{\gamma_{n}A_{1}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|z^{1}_{n+1}-z\|

\displaystyle\leq\|J_{\gamma_{n}A_{1}}w^{1}_{n}-J_{\gamma_{n}A_{1}}x^{1}\|+\|J_{\gamma_{n}A_{1}}x^{1}-J_{\gamma^{*}A_{1}}x^{1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|w^{1}_{n}-x^{1}\|+L_{1}|\gamma_{n}-\gamma^{*}|,

from which
R
-linear convergence of
(z_{n}^{1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
z
follows. Similarly, for
i=2,\dots,N-1
, we have

\displaystyle\|z^{i}_{n}-z\|

\displaystyle\leq\|J_{\gamma_{n}A_{i}}(z_{n}^{i-1}+x_{n}^{i}-x_{n}^{i-1})-J_{\gamma_{n}A_{i}}(z+x^{i}-x^{i-1})\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\|J_{\gamma_{n}A_{i}}(z+x^{i}-x^{i-1})-J_{\gamma^{*}A_{i}}(z+x^{i}-x^{i-1})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(z_{n}^{i-1}+x_{n}^{i}-x_{n}^{i-1})-(z+x^{i}-x^{i-1})\|+L_{i}|\gamma_{n}-\gamma^{*}|

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{n}^{i-1}-z\|+\|x_{n}^{i}-x^{i}\|+\|x_{n}^{i-1}-x^{i-1}\|+L_{i}|\gamma_{n}-\gamma^{*}|,

from which
R
-linear convergence of
(z_{n}^{i})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
z
follows inductively. Finally,

\displaystyle\|z^{N}_{n}-z\|

\displaystyle\leq\|J_{\gamma_{n}A_{N}}(z_{n}^{1}+z_{n}^{N-1}-x_{n}^{N-1})-J_{\gamma_{n}A_{N}}(2z-x^{N-1})\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\|J_{\gamma_{n}A_{N}}(2z-x^{N-1})-J_{\gamma^{*}A_{N}}(2z-x^{N-1})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{n}^{1}-z\|+\|z_{n}^{N-1}-z\|+\|x_{n}^{N-1}-x^{N-1}\|+L_{N}|\gamma_{n}-\gamma^{*}|,

from which
R
-linear convergence of
(z_{n}^{N})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
z
follows. This completes the proof.

∎

6
Conclusion

In this work, we established linear rate of convergence of relocated fixed-point iterations, introduced in
[
2
]
, when the family of algorithmic operators
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
satisfies a linear error bound. In particular, the framework applies in the setting where
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
are contractions. We then applied our result to derive linear convergence of variable stepsize resolvent splittings for solving monotone inclusions namely, to the relocated Douglas–Rachford algorithm and relocated resolvent splitting algorithm due to Maliksty and Tam under the assumptions of Lipschitz continuity and strong monotonicity.

A direction for future research is to investigate whether the relocated framework can be extended to the more general setting of bounded Hölder regularity
[
8
, Definition 2.7]
which generalizes the notion of bounded linear regularity. Such an extension would allow for the analysis of operators that satisfy only Hölder-type error bounds and may lead to sublinear or linear convergence rates for relocated iterations. Another promising direction is to study adaptive stepsize rules for
\gamma_{n}
that still guarantee linear convergence while improving practical performance. Also, it would be valuable to investigate linear rate of convergence by applying the relocated approach to the framework of resolvent splitting introduced in
[
31
]
.

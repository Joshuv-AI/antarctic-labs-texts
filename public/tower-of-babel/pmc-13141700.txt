Naringenin alleviates heat stress-induced thymic injury through a triple mechanism of immune modulation, antioxidant defense, and inflammation suppression
Authors: Ke Liao, Luqi Wang, Jingyan Luo, Xi Jiang, Siyan Zhou, Feiyang Li, Ping Liu, Gaofeng Cai, Zhanhong Zheng, Xiaoquan Guo, Xiaona Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The thymus is an important immune organ, and heat stress can significantly impact the immune function of chickens. Naringenin, as a novel immunomodulator, can restore the imbalanced immune state (pathological state) to a near-normal immune equilibrium (physiological state). This research aims to investigate the effects of naringin on heat stress-induced damage in the thymus of Ningdu Yellow chickens. Two hundred eighty-eight 28-day-old Ningdu yellow chickens were randomly assigned to four groups: control (Con), naringenin-supplemented (Con+Nar), heat stress (HS), and heat stress + naringenin (HS+Nar). HS and HS+Nar groups were subjected to cyclic temperatures daily (37 ± 2 °C, 07:00–19:00; 24 ± 2 °C, 19:00–07:00). Con+Nar and HS+Nar groups received diets supplemented with 200 mg/kg naringenin for 35 consecutive days. Results showed that naringenin significantly ameliorated heat stress-induced thymic pathological damage in Ningdu yellow chickens and reversed heat stress-reduced immune indices (IgA, IgG, IgM). Molecularly, heat stress markedly suppressed the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling axis, downregulated Nrf2, HO-1, and NQO1 expression, while activating NF-κB pathway key molecules (TLR4, MYD88), thereby upregulating pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-17) and downregulating anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10. In contrast, naringenin enhanced antioxidant capacity via modulating the Nrf2/Keap1 pathway and inhibited excessive pro-inflammatory factor elevation, consequently alleviating thymic injury. Overall, naringenin exerts multi-targeted regulation of oxidative stress-inflammation crosstalk, maintaining thymic immune microenvironment homeostasis under heat stress. These findings lay a theoretical basis for developing naringenin-based feed additives to prevent and control poultry heat stress syndrome.

Introduction

Against the backdrop of intensifying global warming trends, the issue of animal heat stress has evolved from a regional challenge to a global threat (Zmrhal et al., 2023). Heat stress can disrupt the homeostasis of poultry, leading to pathophysiological changes that significantly negatively impact the function and health status of tissues and organs, as well as product quality. High-temperature environments can impair the regulation of homeostasis in poultry, causing functional disorders in multiple systems such as immunity, endocrine, and electrolyte metabolism (Zhao et al., 2021; Gonzalez-Rivas et al., 2020; Vandana et al., 2021). Its physiological manifestations include a sharp decrease in feed intake, immune suppression, growth retardation, and can lead to physiological and pathological damage in multiple organs, significantly increasing mortality rates (Akinyemi and Adewole, 2022). Heat stress can lead to a decline in poultry production performance and economic losses in farming, not only causing direct financial losses for farmers but also posing a serious challenge to the stable development of the farming industry (Goel, 2021). Its scope and severity of impact are continuously expanding and deepening, making it a significant ecological issue for the global farming industry and even ecosystems (Thornton et al., 2021; Al Amaz and Mishra, 2024). Therefore, it is essential to further investigate the pathogenesis of heat stress in poultry and develop corresponding prevention and control measures.

Heat stress can cause pathological damage to the thymus, which is closely related to the body's immune function, antioxidant capacity, and inflammatory injury. As a central immune organ, the thymus not only maintains self-immunological tolerance through T cell selection but also exhibits broader immunoregulatory functions. The resident cells within the thymus constitutively express interferons (IFN-α/γ) and support the production of antibodies, including IgA, IgG, and IgM, thereby playing an extensive role in establishing systemic immune homeostasis that goes beyond traditional understanding (Michelson and Mathis, 2022). As an important immune organ in poultry, it exhibits significant damage after heat stress, accompanied by the accumulation of inflammatory cytokines and impaired production of immune factors, leading to a decline in the body's immune defense capabilities, which poses a threat to animal health and growth performance (Siddiqui et al., 2021). In addition to causing a reduction in immune function, heat stress can also induce inflammatory cells to produce a large number of free radicals, leading to inflammatory responses and exacerbating oxidative stress (Iba et al., 2025). To further elucidate the pathological mechanisms underlying thymic injury induced by heat stress, this study developed a heat stress model and assessed alterations in immune, antioxidant, and inflammation-related markers in the thymus of chicks subjected to heat stress.

Naringenin, as a flavonoid compound, exhibits a variety of pharmacological activities such as antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory effects, demonstrating broad application prospects in disease prevention and treatment (Arafah et al., 2020). Naringenin plays a significant role in the field of antioxidation, with its core activity being as a free radical scavenger (Yang et al., 2023). Naringenin, as a flavanone derivative, possesses a unique chemical structure: the C-ring lacks double bonds at both C-2 and C-3 positions, and neither the B-ring 3-position nor the C-ring 3′-position undergoes hydroxylation modification. Structural-antioxidant relationship studies of flavonoids indicate that the ortho-dihydroxy structure in the B-ring can induce electron delocalization, thereby forming more stable free radicals during oxidation. The 2,3-double bond in the C-ring of flavonoids with a 4-oxo functional group significantly contributes to their antioxidant activity, thus inhibiting intracellular lipid peroxidation damage (Heijnen et al., 2002; Veiko et al., 2020). Meanwhile, naringenin can significantly upregulate the levels of catalase (CAT), glutathione peroxidase (GPx), and superoxide dismutase (SOD), accelerating the clearance of free radicals and peroxides, thereby maintaining the physiological functions and structural integrity of cells (Ekambaram et al., 2008). Furthermore, naringenin modulates the functions of various immune cells, such as macrophages and lymphocytes, by inhibiting the activation of key inflammatory signaling pathways, thereby exerting multi-target anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects (Zeng et al., 2018). Additionally, the thymus is a critical site for the development and maturation of T lymphocytes. Naringenin can alleviate LPS-induced thymocyte apoptosis, effectively inhibit the excessive production of LPS-induced pro-inflammatory cytokines (such as TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-6), counteract LPS-induced thymic inflammation and impaired immune function in chickens, and maintain the structural integrity and normal immune function of the thymus (Yu et al., 2024). Furthermore, studies have confirmed that the addition of naringenin to feed can effectively alleviate the liver damage caused by heat stress in Ningdu Yellow Chickens (Liu et al., 2025). Naringenin, as an intestinal ecological modulator, can increase the abundance of butyrate-producing bacteria GCA-900066575 and Marvinbryantia in the intestines of heat-stressed Ningdu yellow chickens, while inhibiting the growth of conditional pathogenic bacteria (including Helicobacter and Mucispirillum). This restructures the gut microbiota and promotes the production of beneficial metabolites, thereby improving intestinal function and alleviating heat stress damage (Lin et al., 2026). Due to its multiple characteristics mentioned above, naringenin has demonstrated extremely broad application prospects in the field of healthy livestock and poultry breeding, and has become a focus of attention for researchers and breeding practitioners. It is expected to be developed as a green and safe feed additive to address poultry heat stress, providing a solid theoretical basis and scientific data.

However, as of now, there have been no relevant reports on whether naringenin has a protective effect on thymus damage in broilers caused by heat stress. Therefore, this experiment established a heat stress model in broilers, aiming to thoroughly investigate the protective effects of naringenin on thymus damage in heat-stressed broilers, providing a scientific basis for the prevention and control of heat stress in poultry, and also opening new avenues for the development and scientific application of naringenin.

Material and methods

Animal model

A total of 288 healthy 28-day-old Ningdu Yellow chickens (Jiangxi, China) were randomly divided into 4 groups, with 6 replicates per group (half male and half female, n = 12). The control group (Con) and the naringenin + control group (Con+Nar) were maintained at 24 °C and 50–55% humidity; the heat stress group (HS) and the heat stress + naringenin group (HS+Nar) were exposed to 37 ± 2 °C and 60–65% humidity from 19:00 to 07:00 daily, and maintained at 24 ± 2 °C for the rest of the time. The basal diet was formulated according to the NRC standards. The Con group and the HS group were fed the basal diet, while the Con+Nar and HS+Nar groups were supplemented with 200 mg/kg naringenin (97% purity, Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology). During the experimental period, animals were allowed free access to food and water, weighed weekly, and serum and thymus tissues were collected on days 21, 28, and 35 for subsequent experimental assays.

Thymus index measurement

After measuring and recording the body weight of the chickens, eight thymus samples were collected and weighed from each group at each time point. Calculate the thymus index using the formula: Thymus index (g/kg) = Thymus weight (g)/Body weight (kg).

Histology

After fixing in 10% formaldehyde and embedding in paraffin, thymus sections were stained using H&E following standard laboratory procedures. Sections were visualized under a BX51 microscope and captured with a digital camera at 400×magnification. The number of sections used for analysis is 3 per group per time point.

Detection of oxidative stress indicators in thymus

The levels of total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), malondialdehyde (MDA), catalase (CAT), and total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD) in Thymus were respectively determined using chemical colorimetry, the thiobarbituric acid (TBA) method, ammonium molybdate photometry, and the WST-1 method, in accordance with the kits’ instructions (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China).

Results

Naringenin alleviates thymus damage induced by heat stress

As depicted in Fig. 1A, heat stress and naringenin treatment significantly affected the thymus morphology of Ningdu Yellow chickens. Compared to the CON group, the HS group exhibited significant thymic atrophy, with a rough surface, abnormal morphology, and a dull color. However, after the administration of naringenin, the morphology of the thymus was significantly improved. Meanwhile, compared to the Con group, the thymus coefficients in the HS group were significantly decreased at 21d, 28d, and 35d (P < 0.05 or P < 0.001), whereas the HS+Nar group showed a significant increase compared to the HS group (P < 0.05, P < 0.01 or P < 0.001). The thymic lobule structure in the CON group was intact, with a clear demarcation between the cortex and medulla. In the HS group, the thymic lobule structure was disordered, with cortical atrophy and progressive degeneration with age, increased spacing of thymic corpuscles, and the presence of inflammatory corpuscles. Discussion

As the ambient temperature continues to rise, broilers exposed to chronic heat stress for extended periods will experience hindered growth and development (Hashemitabar and Hosseinian, 2024). Poultry, lacking sweat glands and being covered with feathers, are particularly sensitive to heat stress (Mangan and Siwek, 2024; Farag and Alagawany, 2018). Heat stress can lead to reduced feed intake and slower growth rates in poultry, increased feed consumption per unit of weight gain, resulting in a higher feed conversion ratio, significantly increased breeding costs, and consequently affecting production efficiency (Das et al., 2016). In addition, high-temperature environments can significantly disrupt the physiological homeostasis of poultry, leading to metabolic disorders, imbalance in the antioxidant system, and immune suppression, ultimately impairing production performance (Mujahid et al., 2005; Hu et al., 2021; Lara and Rostagno, 2013). Naringenin, a monomeric flavonoid compound (Nyane et al., 2017), possesses potent anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and liver-protective effects (Uçar and Göktaş, 2023). It is widely used in the treatment of inflammation, allergies, oxidative stress, immune issues, etc. (Solanki et al., 2025). Naringenin can alleviate pyroptosis and inflammatory responses, mitigating lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced spleen injury in broilers (Xia et al., 2025). However, its application in poultry farming is limited. Therefore, this study evaluated the protective effects of naringenin on Ningdu Yellow chickens under heat stress conditions. The results indicate that heat stress significantly upregulates the levels of HSP 70 and HSP 90 in the liver of Ningdu Yellow Chickens, demonstrating the effectiveness of the experimental model. Simultaneously, heat stress affects the function of the thymus in broilers through multiple pathways, while naringenin intervention effectively alleviates its pathological damage, indicating that naringenin has a protective effect on the thymus during heat stress.

Hirakawa et al. (Hirakawa et al., 2020; Mao et al., 2024) demonstrated that heat stress not only causes structural damage to the thymus but also significantly reduces the levels of immunoglobulins in the body. Subsequently, Millecam et al. (2021) observed that continuous heat exposure led to atrophy in the cortical region of the thymus in broilers, accompanied by a decrease in serum concentrations of IgG, IgA, and IgM. In a heat-stressed mouse model, researchers found that the expression levels of IFN-α and IFN-γ in the thymus were both decreased, suggesting that interferon signaling dysregulation may be involved in the mechanism of immunoglobulin downregulation (Wang et al., 2023). Heat stress can significantly impair the production performance of broilers by weakening the antioxidant capacity of immune organs, thereby reducing immune organ indices, suppressing immune functions, and disrupting the developmental status of immune organs (Sun et al., 2023). In this study, we found that heat stress led to atrophy in the thymus tissue of Ningdu Yellow chickens, with a significant reduction in the thymus index, which was confirmed through thymus dissection, thymus index measurement, and HE staining. To further validate the relationship between thymic injury and thymic immune function under heat stress, the expression levels of thymic immune-related genes (IFN-α, IFN-γ, IgA, IgG, and IgM) were analyzed. The results revealed that the immune function of the thymus in Ningdu Huang chickens was reduced under heat stress, leading to thymic injury. Integrating these findings indicates that thymic injury induced by heat stress can be attributed to the decline in thymic immune function. Furthermore, it is noteworthy that naringenin has been found to significantly ameliorate heat stress-induced thymic immune injury, enhance thymic immune function, and exhibit a protective effect on the thymus.

Heat stress can lead to thymic damage by increasing oxidative stress and weakening antioxidant defenses (Wang et al., 2023). Within organisms, the Nrf2-Keap1 system plays a pivotal role, serving as the primary regulatory pathway that exerts a central function in the organism's antioxidant stress response (Hu et al., 2020). Oxidative stress can lead to immune organ damage through the Nrf2-Keap1 signaling pathway (Tonelli et al., 2018). In the process of oxidative liver injury induced by heat stress in mice, the downstream genes HO-1 and NQO1 of the Nrf2-Keap1 pathway are activated, subsequently modulating the activities of antioxidant enzymes such as T-SOD and CAT (Abed et al., 2015). In this study, we found that under heat stress, the MDA levels in the thymus of Ningdu Huang chickens increased, while the levels of CAT, T-SOD, and T-AOC decreased. Under heat stress conditions, the mRNA and protein expression levels of Nrf2, HO-1, and NQO1 in the thymus of Ningdu Huang chickens were significantly decreased, while the mRNA and protein expression levels of Keap1 were significantly increased. This indicates that the Nrf2-Keap1 pathway was activated under the influence of heat stress. It is noteworthy that the use of naringenin can effectively regulate the expression levels of these genes and proteins. The aforementioned results indicate that heat stress indeed induces oxidative stress in the liver of Ningdu yellow chickens, and the protective effect of naringenin on the thymus is related to its antioxidant function. Therefore, naringenin can alleviate heat stress-induced thymic damage through the Nrf2-Keap1 pathway.

Heat stress affects the thymus by inducing inflammation and impairing thymic function (Kuehu et al., 2024). On one hand, sustained heat exposure can lead to continuous activation of NF-κB, disrupting the homeostasis of the thymic microenvironment (Cantet et al., 2021). In heat-stressed broilers, the mRNA level of MYD88 in the thymus is significantly elevated compared to the control group, and positively correlates with the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and TNF-α (He et al., 2019). The NF-κB-MYD88 signaling cascade serves as a central hub connecting environmental stress with inflammatory responses. Under heat stress conditions, it forms the TLR4-MYD88 complex and releases NF-κB dimers into the nucleus (Chen et al., 2024). On the other hand, Dejani et al. (Dejani et al., 2021) demonstrated that in a heat-stressed broiler model, the addition of naringenin reduced the mRNA levels of both IL-6 and IL-8 in the thymus, while also inhibiting the phosphorylation of IKKβ. Experimental studies on the treatment of non-alcoholic steatohepatitis with naringenin have demonstrated that naringenin exerts its therapeutic effects by targeting core inflammatory mediators such as TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β, thereby inhibiting the MyD88/NF-κB inflammatory signaling pathway (Wang et al., 2024). In this study, qPCR and WB results showed that under heat stress, the expression levels of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, IL-17, NF-κB, MYD88, and TLR4 in the thymus of Ningdu yellow chickens were significantly increased, except for the reduced expression levels of IL-10 genes and proteins. Conversely, the addition of naringenin reduced the mRNA and protein expression levels of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, IL-17, NF-κB, MYD88, and TLR4 in the thymus caused by heat stress, while simultaneously increasing the mRNA and protein expression levels of IL-10, indicating that naringenin alleviates heat stress-induced inflammatory damage in the chicken thymus through the NF-κB-MYD88 pathway. Integrating these findings suggests that thymic inflammation induced by heat stress can be attributed to the NF-κB-MYD88 pathway-mediated effects. However, dietary supplementation with naringenin significantly inhibited the NF-κB-MYD88 pathway inflammatory cascade and alleviated thymic inflammatory injury in Ningdu Yellow chickens under heat stress.

Conclusion

This study is the first to systematically demonstrate that naringenin alleviates heat stress-induced thymus injury in Ningdu Yellow Chickens through a triple mechanism involving immunity, antioxidation, and inflammation. Heat stress impairs thymic function and immune capacity, while inducing thymic inflammation mediated by the NF-κB-MYD88 pathway, ultimately leading to thymic injury. In contrast, dietary supplementation with naringenin exerts protective effects on the thymus by regulating the Nrf2-Keap1 pathway, enhancing thymic immune function and boosting antioxidant defense capacity. Furthermore, naringenin can suppress the inflammatory cascade mediated by the NF-κB-MYD88 pathway, thereby mitigating heat stress-induced thymus injury. These comprehensive findings demonstrate that naringenin can alleviate heat stress-induced thymic damage, providing an important theoretical basis for developing naringenin-based poultry heat stress mitigators.

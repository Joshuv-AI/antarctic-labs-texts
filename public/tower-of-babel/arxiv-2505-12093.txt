Abstract
In this work, we investigate the partition function of 2d CFT under root- T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformation. We demonstrate that the deformed partition function satisfies a flow equation. At large central charge sector, the deformed partition function reduces to a redefinition of the modular parameters, which preserves modular invariance under the deformed parameters. We then derive a Cardy-like formula for the asymptotic density of states using modular bootstrap trick. In the context of AdS/CFT, it was proposed the root- T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformed CFT corresponds to the AdS 3 with certain deformed boundary condition. We show the deformed BTZ black hole is a quotient of hyperbolic space. In terms of Chern-Simons formulation, we compute the root- T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformed BTZ black hole entropy and find that it obeys a Cardy-like formula, which is consistent with the modular bootstrap result. Furthermore, employing the Wilson spool technique, we compute the one-loop partition functions for the root- T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformed AdS 3 geometry. Our results reveal an exact agreement between one-loop gravitational partition function and the large c c expansion of root- T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} deformed CFT partition function.

Root-
T\bar{T}
deformation and holographic description

This section is a brief review of some basics about the root-
T\bar{T}
deformation including the deformed spectrum and the holographic description, which are closely related to this work. The deformed spectrum turns out to satisfy a flow equation under some consistency assumptions. The holographic duality of the root
T\bar{T}
-deformed CFT is the AdS
3
with a certain modified boundary conditions, which is derived from the root-
T\bar{T}
flow equation. Similar to the
T\bar{T}
deformation, the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed AdS
3
geometry can also be obtained through a coordinate transformation.

2.1
Root-
T\bar{T}
deformed spectrum

The root-
T\bar{T}
deformation is a generalization of the
T\bar{T}
deformation, whose deformed action is given by the
T\bar{T}
-like flow equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S_{\lambda}}{\partial\lambda}=\int d^{2}x\mathcal{R}_{T\bar{T}}.

(2.1)

Classically, the square-root
T\bar{T}
operator is formally defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{T\bar{T}}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}T^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}T^{\mu}_{\mu}T^{\nu}_{\nu}}=\sqrt{\det\widetilde{T}},

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}T^{\rho}_{\rho},

(2.3)

is the traceless part of the stress-energy tensor. The classical action can be obtained by solving the flow equation
[
61
]
. However, on the quantum level the square-root
T\bar{T}
operator have not fully understood yet. For the
T\bar{T}
deformation, the deformed spectrum can be obtained exactly because of the Zamolodchikov’s factorization formula

\displaystyle\langle n|\mathcal{O}_{T\bar{T}}|n\rangle=\langle n|T|n\rangle\langle n|\bar{T}|n\rangle-\langle n|\Theta|n\rangle\langle n|\Theta|n\rangle.

(2.4)

In
[
84
]
, the authors assume that there exists a local operator
\mathcal{R}(x)
, which is a square root of the
T\bar{T}
operator and satisfies the factorization

\displaystyle\langle n|\det(t^{\mu\nu})|n\rangle=\langle n|\mathcal{R}(x)|n\rangle\langle n|\mathcal{R}(x)|n\rangle,

(2.5)

so that the expectation value of the square-root operator reads

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{R}\right\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left\langle T^{\mu\nu}\right\rangle\left\langle T_{\mu\nu}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{4}\left\langle T^{\mu}_{\mu}\right\rangle\left\langle T^{\nu}_{\nu}\right\rangle}.

(2.6)

The Zamolodchikov’s factorization of the
T\bar{T}
operator can be straightforward recovered by taking a square. By expressing the components of the stress tensor for the theory on a cylinder of radius
R
in terms of energies and momenta, the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed spectrum is shown to satisfy the flow equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{n}(R,\lambda)}{\partial\lambda}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\left(E_{n}-R\frac{\partial E_{n}}{\partial R}\right)^{2}-P_{n}^{2}},

(2.7)

or equivalently

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial E_{n}(R,\lambda)}{\partial\lambda}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left(E_{n}(R,\lambda)-R\frac{\partial E_{n}(R,\lambda)}{\partial R}\right)^{2}+P_{n}^{2}=0.

(2.8)

This flow equation can be solved if we impose certain initial condition. In this paper, we shall consider the seed theory is the 2d conformal field theory.

Zero-momentum case:

For the zero-momentum case
P_{n}=0
, the flow equation can be simplified to

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{n}(R,\lambda)}{\partial\lambda}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\left(E_{n}(R,\lambda)-R\frac{\partial E_{n}(R,\lambda)}{\partial R}\right),

(2.9)

which lead to the following deformed spectrum

\displaystyle E_{n}(R,\lambda)=E_{n}e^{\pm\lambda}.

(2.10)

In this case, the spectrum is just a rescaling of the original one.

General case:

For the general case, we consider the seed theory to be a conformal field theory whose spectrum and momentum are given by

\displaystyle E_{n}=\frac{1}{R}\left(\Delta_{n}+\bar{\Delta}_{n}-\frac{c}{12}\right),\quad P_{n}=\frac{1}{R}\left(\Delta_{n}-\bar{\Delta}_{n}\right).

(2.11)

The flow equation can also be solved and the deformed spectrum reads

\displaystyle E_{n}(\lambda)=\cosh(\lambda)E_{n}\pm\sinh(\lambda)\sqrt{E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}},

(2.12)

while the momentum
P_{n}
does not flow.

The following comments about the deformed spectrum are in order:

•

The spectrum for different branches are both physical, because both of them can give the correct perturbation result. While for the large
\lambda
, these two branches become quite different. It should be noted that the different branches are related by changing the sign of deformation parameter. Therefore in the following, we would like to study the deformed spectrum by taking the value of deformation parameter
\lambda\in(-\infty,\infty)
with positive sign of
\lambda
.

•

The spectrum can be separated into left- and right-moving energy defined as

\displaystyle E_{n}(\lambda)=E^{L}_{n}(\lambda)+E^{R}_{n}(\lambda),\quad P_{n}(\lambda)=E^{L}_{n}(\lambda)-E^{R}_{n}(\lambda),

(2.13)

so that the left- and right-moving energy reads

\displaystyle\sqrt{E^{L}_{n}(\lambda)}=\sqrt{E^{L}_{n}}\cosh\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)+\sqrt{E^{R}_{n}}\sinh\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right),

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sqrt{E^{R}_{n}(\lambda)}=\sqrt{E^{R}_{n}}\cosh\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)+\sqrt{E^{L}_{n}}\sinh\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right).

(2.15)

The effect of root-
T\bar{T}
deformation is a boost transformation between
\sqrt{E_{L}}
and
\sqrt{E_{R}}
, which preserves the momentum unchanged

\displaystyle E_{n}^{L}(\lambda)-E_{n}^{R}(\lambda)=E_{n}^{L}-E_{n}^{R}.

(2.16)

•

For the zero-momentum case, the deformed spectrum is just a rescaling of the original one. For the general non-zero momentum case, we expand the deformed spectrum for large
c
CFT, which leads to

\displaystyle E_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=E_{n}e^{-\lambda}+O(1/c).

(2.17)

We find, up to the
O(c^{0})
order, the deformed energy is just the undeformed energy multiply a factor
e^{-\lambda}
. While the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed spectrum becomes complicated for the higher order.

2.2
Root-
T\bar{T}
deformed AdS
3
boundary conditions

In
[
84
]
, it was proposed the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed CFT corresponds to AdS
3
with modified boundary condition. The deformed bulk geometry can be obtained through a coordinate transformation. We start from the general AdS
3
solution, which in the Fefferman-Graham gauge can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{dr^{2}}{r^{2}}+\left(r^{2}g^{(0)}_{ij}(x)+g^{(2)}_{ij}(x)+\frac{g^{(4)}_{ij}(x)}{r^{2}}\right)dx^{i}dx^{j}.

(2.18)

In the standard AdS/CFT correspondence,
g^{(0)}_{ij}
is the metric where the boundary field theory living in and
g^{(2)}_{ij}
is related to the expectation value of the stress tensor

\displaystyle g_{ij}^{(2)}=2\left(T_{ij}-g_{ij}^{(0)}T_{k}^{k}\right).

(2.19)

By solving the root-
T\bar{T}
flow equation with consistency conditions
[
84
]
, one can obtain the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed metric and stress tensor. In terms of the AdS
3
quantities, the proper root-
T\bar{T}
deformed boundary metric and stress tensor are given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{ij}^{(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\cosh(\lambda)g_{ij}^{(0)}+\frac{\sinh(\lambda)}{\mathcal{R}^{(0)}}\widetilde{T}_{ij}^{(0)},

(2.20)

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{ij}^{(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\cosh(\lambda)\widetilde{T}_{ij}^{(0)}+\sinh(\lambda)\mathcal{R}^{(0)}g_{ij}^{(0)},

(2.21)

where
\widetilde{T}_{ij}^{(0)}
is the traceless part of the stress tensor and
\mathcal{R}^{(0)}
is the square root of
\widetilde{T}_{ij}^{(0)}
determinant.

We consider the Bañados geometry
[
89
]
, namely the AdS
3
solution with Brown-Henneaux boundary condition
[
90
]
, which in Fefferman-Graham gauge becomes

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{dr^{2}}{r^{2}}+r^{2}dudv+\mathcal{L}(u)du^{2}+\overline{\mathcal{L}}(v)dv^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mathcal{L}(u)\overline{\mathcal{L}}(v)dudv,

(2.22)

where
\mathcal{L}(u)
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}(v)
are holomorphic and anti-holomorphic function, respectively. In this case, the stress tensor is traceless
\widetilde{T}_{ij}=T_{ij}
and the square root
T\bar{T}
-operator equals

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{(0)}=\sqrt{\mathcal{L}(u)\overline{\mathcal{L}}(v)}.

(2.23)

The deformed Bañados geometry can be obtained through a dynamical change of coordinate. We first replace the parameter
\mathcal{L},\bar{\mathcal{L}}
by
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda},\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda}
, then do the following coordinate transformation

\displaystyle du

\displaystyle=\left(\cosh\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)dU-\sqrt{\frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda}}{\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}}}\left(\sinh\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)dV,

(2.24)

\displaystyle dv

\displaystyle=\left(\cosh\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)dV-\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}}{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda}}}\left(\sinh\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)dU,

(2.25)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda},\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda}
can be fixed by this deformation is smooth and preserves degeneracy of states of the boundary theory which means the black hole horizon area is unchanged, as well as root-
T\bar{T}
deformation does not affect the momentum in the boundary field theory

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{L}(u)}\cosh\frac{\lambda}{2}+\sqrt{\overline{\mathcal{L}}(v)}\sinh\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{2},

(2.26)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{\overline{\mathcal{L}}(v)}\cosh\frac{\lambda}{2}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}(u)}\sinh\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{2}.

(2.27)

This is similar to the result for
T\bar{T}
deformation. This holographic proposal have been verified on the classical action and the deformed spectrum. In the remaining of this work, we would like to study the partition function of root-
T\bar{T}
deformed CFT from both boundary field theory and holography.

3
Root-
T\bar{T}
deformed partition functions

For the conformal field theory, the torus partition function can be obtained by tracing over the Verma module of Virasoro algebra

\displaystyle Z_{0}(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\mathrm{Tr}\left(q^{L_{0}-\frac{c}{24}}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{c}{24}}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}+2\pi i\tau_{1}RP_{n}}.

(3.1)

The CFT partition function is modular invariant

\displaystyle Z_{0}\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\bar{\tau}+b}{c\bar{\tau}+d}\right)=Z_{0}(\tau,\bar{\tau}),\quad ad-bc=1,\quad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z},

(3.2)

where the modular parameter is given by

\displaystyle\tau=\tau_{1}+i\tau_{2},\quad\bar{\tau}=\tau_{1}-i\tau_{2}.

(3.3)

Similarly, the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed partition function on a torus can also be calculated by replacing the spectrum with the deformed one, which can be written as

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}(R,\lambda)+2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}}.

(3.4)

In what following, we shall study root-
T\bar{T}
deformations of conformal field theories on the torus. We first show the deformed partition function satisfy a flow equation and is modular invariant after redefining the modular parameter for the zero-momentum case. We then generalize the result to the more general case especially for the large central charge sector.

3.1
Zero-momentum case

For the zero-momentum case, the deformed spectrum is a rescaling of the original one (
2.10
). The deformed partition function can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}e^{\lambda}}.

(3.5)

We then do the perturbation expansion of the partition function with respect to the deformation parameter

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}Z_{n}(\tau,\bar{\tau}).

(3.6)

The first few orders read

\displaystyle Z_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}},

(3.7)

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}-2\pi E_{n}R\tau_{2}e^{-2\pi E_{n}R\tau_{2}},

(3.8)

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\pi E_{n}R\tau_{2}\left(2\pi E_{n}R\tau_{2}-1\right)e^{-2\pi E_{n}R\tau_{2}},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\cdots

There is a polynomial of the energy for each order in perturbation theory. The polynomials are related to derivatives of
Z_{0}
with respect to the modular parameter. We find the following replacement rule

\displaystyle 2\pi RE_{n}\mapsto-\partial_{\tau_{2}}.

(3.10)

Then the first few order can be written as

\displaystyle Z_{1}=\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}Z_{0},\quad Z_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}Z_{1},\quad\quad Z_{3}=\frac{1}{3}\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}Z_{2},\quad\cdots

(3.11)

and generally we have

\displaystyle Z_{p}=\frac{1}{p}\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}Z_{p-1}.

(3.12)

Therefore, the deformed partition functions satisfy the flow equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda),

(3.13)

with the initial condition

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|0)=Z_{0}(\tau,\tau).

(3.14)

We have to point out that the deformed partition function is
not modular invariant
. In fact, according to the recurrence relation (
3.12
), we start from a modular invariant partition function
Z_{0}
, while the other order corrections of the partition functions are
not modular invariant
. Since the spectrum is a rescaling of the original one, the partition function can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=Z_{0}(\tau_{\lambda},\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}),\quad\text{with}\quad\tau_{\lambda}=ie^{\lambda}\tau_{2},\quad\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}=ie^{\lambda}\tau_{2}.

(3.15)

The deformed partition function corresponds to the redefinition of the modular parameters, maintaining modular invariance under the refined modular parameters.

3.2
General case

From the zero-momentum case, we learn that the partition function is not the usual modular invariant. The operator
\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}
is not Ramanujan-Serre derivative, which maps the modular form to another modular form, see appendix
A
for details. We infer that the general partition function is also not modular invariant. We can not follow the method in
[
4
,
6
]
using the modular properties to find the flow equation for the general case. While for the large
c
sector, up to
c^{0}
order contribution, the spectrum is a rescaling of the original one. In this case, the contribution from the descendant levels is linear just like the CFT spectrum. In this subsection, we begin by examining the large central charge sector, then extend our analysis to general case.

Large central charge sector

In the large
c
limit, the spectrum is given by

\displaystyle E_{n}(R,\lambda)

\displaystyle=E_{n}e^{-\lambda}+O(1/c),

(3.16)

which is just a rescaling of the original one. Therefore we learn that at large
c
sector, up to the
O(c^{0})
order contribution, the deformed theory is described by a conformal field theory. The partition function reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}(R)e^{-\lambda}+2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}}=Z_{0}(\tau_{\lambda},\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}),

(3.17)

where the deformed modular parameters are

\displaystyle\tau_{\lambda}=\tau_{1}+i\tau_{2}e^{-\lambda},\quad\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}=\tau_{1}-i\tau_{2}e^{-\lambda},

(3.18)

or equivalent we have

\displaystyle\tau_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tau+\bar{\tau}+e^{-\lambda}(\tau-\bar{\tau})\Big),\quad\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tau+\bar{\tau}+e^{-\lambda}(\bar{\tau}-\tau)\Big).

(3.19)

The deformed partition function is a replacement of the modular parameter, which is
not modular invariant
generally. However, it is obviously that the deformed partition function has the following modular properties

\displaystyle Z_{0}\left(\frac{a\tau_{\lambda}+b}{c\tau_{\lambda}+d},\frac{a\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}+b}{c\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}+d}\right)=Z_{0}(\tau_{\lambda},\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}),\quad ad-bc=1\quad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z},

(3.20)

which means the deformed partition function at large
c
sector is invariant under the modular transformation of deformed modular parameter. Following the similar procedure in zero-momentum case, one can find the partition functions satisfy the flow equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=-\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda).

(3.21)

The modular invariant CFT partition function can be written as summing of characters

\displaystyle Z(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\sum_{h,\bar{h}}\chi_{h}(\tau)\chi_{\bar{h}}(\bar{\tau}).

(3.22)

The characters describe the degeneracy for a given level
(n_{L},n_{R})
, which is given by the product of integer partitions
p(n_{L})
and
p(n_{R})
. We can therefore write the product of left and right-moving characters as follows

\displaystyle\Xi(\tau,\bar{\tau})=

\displaystyle|\chi(\tau)|^{2}=e^{2\pi\tau_{2}\frac{c}{12}}\sum_{n_{L},n_{R}=0}^{\infty}p(n_{L})p(n_{R})e^{-2\pi\tau_{2}(n_{L}+n_{R})}e^{2\pi i\tau_{1}(n_{L}-n_{R})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2\pi\tau_{2}\frac{c}{12}}\left|\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-e^{2\pi i\tau n}}\right|^{2}.

(3.23)

Fortunately, the deformed spectrum is a rescaling of the CFT spectrum and the degeneracy remains unchanged. We evaluate the deformed character follow the same technique used in CFT

\displaystyle\Xi(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=

\displaystyle e^{2\pi\tau_{2}\frac{ce^{-\lambda}}{12}}\sum_{n_{L},n_{R}=0}^{\infty}p(n_{L})p(n_{R})e^{-2\pi\tau_{2}(n_{L}+n_{R})e^{-\lambda}}e^{2\pi i\tau_{1}(n_{L}-n_{R})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2\pi\tau_{2}\frac{ce^{-\lambda}}{12}}\left|\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-e^{2\pi i\tau_{1}n-2\pi\tau_{2}ne^{-\lambda}}}\right|^{2}.

(3.24)

For the vacuum character, the above product starts from
n=2
, if we take into account the null states. After redefining the modular parameter (
3.19
), the characters can be written as

\displaystyle\Xi(\tau,\lambda)=|q_{\lambda}|^{-2k}\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|1-q_{\lambda}^{n}|^{2}},

(3.25)

where

\displaystyle q_{\lambda}=e^{2\pi i\tau_{\lambda}},\quad\bar{q}_{\lambda}=e^{-2\pi i\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}}.

(3.26)

Similar properties also find in
T\bar{T}
deformation
[
91
]
, which is described by a
T\bar{T}
deformed modular form
[
92
]
. While for the higher order contribution, the deformed partition function becomes more complicated.

Beyond large central charge

For the higher orders, the deformed left- and right-moving spectrum are coupled which may not be described by a conformal field theory. The partition function still have the following expansion

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}Z_{n}(\tau,\bar{\tau}).

(3.27)

The first few orders read

\displaystyle Z_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}},

(3.28)

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(-2\pi R\tau_{2}\Big)\sqrt{E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}}e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}},

(3.29)

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(2\pi^{2}R^{2}\left(E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}\right)\tau_{2}^{2}-\pi RE_{n}\tau_{2}\Big)e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}},

(3.30)

\displaystyle Z_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(-\frac{4}{3}\pi^{3}R^{3}(E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2})\tau_{2}^{3}+2\pi^{2}R^{2}E_{n}\tau_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\pi R\tau_{2}\right)\sqrt{E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}}e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}},

(3.31)

\displaystyle\cdots

We find the odd orders are related to the square root term, while the even orders are a polynomial of energy and momentum. The perturbation partition functions can be converted into actions of derivatives on the CFT partition function, except for the square root term. We have the following replacements rule

\displaystyle 2\pi RE_{n}\mapsto-\partial_{\tau_{2}}=-i(\partial_{\tau}-\partial_{\bar{\tau}}),\quad 2\pi iRP_{n}\mapsto\partial_{\tau_{1}}=\partial_{\tau}+\partial_{\bar{\tau}},

(3.32)

as well as

\displaystyle(2\pi R)^{2}\left(E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}\right)\mapsto 4\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}.

(3.33)

We therefore rewrite the first few order expansion of the partition function as

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=-\tau_{2}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}Z_{0},

(3.34)

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle=\left[2\tau_{2}^{2}\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}\right]Z_{0},

(3.35)

\displaystyle Z_{3}

\displaystyle=\left[-\frac{2}{3}\tau_{2}^{3}\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{2}^{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}-\frac{1}{6}\tau_{2}\right]\widehat{\mathcal{R}}Z_{0},

(3.36)

\displaystyle\cdots

where

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{R}}Z_{0}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2\pi R\sqrt{E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}}e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}}.

(3.37)

More generally, we conclude

\displaystyle Z_{p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{p}\tau_{2}^{m}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{m}^{(p)}\right)Z_{0}(\tau,\bar{\tau}),&\text{for even $p$}\\
\left(\sum_{m=1}^{p}\tau_{2}^{m}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{m}^{(p)}\right)\widehat{\mathcal{R}}Z_{0}(\tau,\bar{\tau}),&\text{for odd $p$}\end{array}\right.

which means the perturbation terms can be written as a polynomial of
\tau_{2}
with a differential operator
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{n}^{(p)}(\partial_{\tau},\partial_{\bar{\tau}})
as coefficients act on the original partition function. The information about the expansion of spectrum are encode in the operators.

For the
T\bar{T}
deformation, the recurrence relation for the perturbation was obtained using the modular properties of the partition function. However, the odd orders are related to a square root term, which cannot been written in terms of a regular differential operator in the root-
T\bar{T}
deformation. In fact, we can also define the square root operator see appendix
A
for details. Another important observation about the expansion of the partition function is that the square root term only appear in odd order perturbations, and the even order terms are well-defined. In order to avoid the square root term, we consider the second order recurrence relation. Therefore, we have to consider the recursion relation between
Z_{p+2}
and
Z_{p}
. The
p
-order correction of the partition function is a
p
-order polynomial of
\tau_{2}
with the coefficient to be a function of
E_{n}
and
E^{2}_{n}-P_{n}^{2}
, which can be transformed to a differential operator using the replacement rule (
3.32
) and (
3.33
). There is no constant term as well. According to above observation and analysis, we make the following ansatz for the recurrence relation

\displaystyle Z_{p+2}=\mathcal{D}_{p}Z_{p},

(3.40)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{p}=

\displaystyle A_{p}\tau_{2}^{2}\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}+B_{p}\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}.

(3.41)

Note that the first part of this operator is Ramanujan-Serre derivative, which maps one modular form to another modular form, while the second operator is not a Ramanujan-Serre derivative, see (
A
) for details. Therefore the differential operator do not preserve the modular form. Moreover, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{p}\tau_{2}^{n}=A_{p}\tau_{2}^{n+2}\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}+B_{p}\tau_{2}^{n+1}\partial_{\tau_{2}}+\left(\frac{n(n-1)}{4}A_{p}+nB_{p}\right)\tau_{2}^{n}.

(3.42)

The coefficient
A_{p},B_{p}
in this ansatz (
3.41
) can be fixed by considering the highest and lowest order of
\tau_{2}
. The highest order of
\tau_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{p}^{(p)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\frac{2^{p}}{p!}\left(\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}\right)^{p/2},&\text{for even $p$}\\
-\frac{2^{p}}{p!}\left(\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}\right)^{(p-1)/2},&\text{for odd $p$}\end{array}\right.

Comparing the operator coefficient, we get

\displaystyle A_{p}=\frac{4}{(p+1)(p+2)},

(3.45)

while the lowest
\tau_{2}
order is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{1}^{(p)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\frac{1}{p!}\partial_{\tau_{2}},&\text{for even $p$}\\
-\frac{1}{p!},&\text{for odd $p$}\end{array}\right.

So that we have

\displaystyle B_{p}=\frac{1}{(p+1)(p+2)}.

(3.48)

Therefore, the recurrence relation becomes

\displaystyle Z_{p+2}=\frac{1}{(p+1)(p+2)}\Big(4\tau_{2}^{2}\left(\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}\right)+\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}\Big)Z_{p},

(3.49)

which holds for both even and odd
p
. The only difference between the odd terms and even terms is the initial condition. The recurrence relation implies the flow equation for the deformed partition function

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}^{2}\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=\left(4\tau_{2}^{2}\left(\partial_{\tau}\partial_{{\bar{\tau}}}\right)+\tau_{2}\partial_{{\tau_{2}}}\right)\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda),

(3.50)

with the initial condition

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)\Big|_{\lambda=0}=Z_{0},\quad\partial_{\lambda}\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)\Big|_{\lambda=0}=Z_{1}.

(3.51)

This is a second order differential equation, which is different from the
T\bar{T}
deformation. In our case, the information of root-
T\bar{T}
deformation is also encoded in the initial condition (
3.51
). This flow equation for the partition function is also obtained in
[
93
]
. It looks like we have already taken a square of the differential equation similar to the flow equation of the deformed spectrum (
2.7
) and (
2.8
). However, we do not find the modular properties of the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed partition function from the flow equation. Notably, we may alternatively perform a Laurent expansion in terms of the parameter
t=e^{-\lambda}
, see Appendix
B
for details.

3.3
Asymptotic density of state

We consider the zero-momentum case, and the partition function has the following modular properties

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}\left(\frac{a\tau_{\lambda}+b}{c\tau_{\lambda}+d},\frac{a\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}+b}{c\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}+d}\right)=\mathcal{Z}(\tau_{\lambda},\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}).

(3.52)

For a rectangular torus with a purely imaginary modular parameter, we choose the modular parameter to be
\tau=i\beta/R
, which implies that

\displaystyle\tau_{\lambda}=\frac{i\beta}{2\pi R}e^{\lambda},\quad\bar{\tau}_{\lambda}=-\frac{i\beta}{2\pi R}e^{\lambda}.

(3.53)

In this case, the partition function simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\beta,\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi\beta E_{n}(R,\lambda)}.

(3.54)

For the low temperature limit, the partition function is dominated by the ground state

\displaystyle E_{0}(R,\lambda)=-\frac{c}{12R}e^{\lambda},

(3.55)

The partition function becomes

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\mathcal{Z}(\beta,\lambda)=\exp\left(\frac{\pi\beta c}{6R}e^{\lambda}\right).

(3.56)

Now we perform the
\mathcal{S}
-modular transformation of the deformed modular parameters, which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{R}e^{\lambda}\to\frac{R}{\beta}e^{-\lambda}.

(3.57)

Physically, the
\mathcal{S}
-modular transformation maps the low temperature limit to the high temperature limit. In the high energy limit, almost all the state contributes. We can define the following spectral density

\displaystyle\rho(E)=\sum_{n}\delta(E-E_{n}(R,\lambda)).

(3.58)

Then the partition function at high temperature limit can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\beta,\lambda)=\int_{E_{0}(R,\lambda)}^{\infty}dE\rho(E)e^{-2\pi\beta E}=\exp\left(\frac{\pi Rc}{6\beta}e^{-\lambda}\right),

(3.59)

so that

\displaystyle\rho(E)=\int d\beta\exp\left(\beta E-\frac{\pi Rc}{6\beta}e^{-\lambda}\right).

(3.60)

Under saddle point approximation, we obtain the asymptotic density of state

\displaystyle\rho(E)=\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\pi cRE}{3}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}\right),

(3.61)

which is the Cardy formula for 2d CFT except for the factor
e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}
. This result agrees with the holographic prediction in
[
84
]
. For the large
c
sector, the partition function have the similar properties since the spectrum is a rescaling of the original one. However, for the non-zero momentum case, we do not find the modular properties of the partition function generally.

4.1
Chern-Simons formulation of root-

\mathrm{SO}(2,2)\simeq\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})

[

46

\displaystyle=I_{CS}[A]-I_{CS}[\bar{A}],

\displaystyle I_{CS}[A]

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\text{Tr}\left(A\wedge dA+\frac{2}{3}A\wedge A\wedge A\right),\quad k=\frac{1}{4G}.

The gauge fields

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(L_{0}L_{0})=\dfrac{1}{2},\quad\operatorname{Tr}(L_{-1}L_{1})=\operatorname{Tr}(L_{1}L_{-1})=-1.

\displaystyle F\equiv dA+A\wedge A=0,\quad\bar{F}\equiv d\bar{A}+\bar{A}\wedge\bar{A}=0,

which are equivalent to the gravitational field equation and torsion free equation. The AdS

5
Conclusion and discussion

The root-
T\bar{T}
deformed CFT is shown dual to the AdS
3
with certain modified boundary condition. In this paper, we consider the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed CFT partition function form both field theory side and gravity side. On the field theory side, we first analyse the zero-momentum case and demonstrate that the deformed partition function corresponds to a redefinition of the modular parameter. We show that the deformed partition function satisfies a flow equation and remains modular invariant for the redefined modular parameters. We then generalize these results to the case of arbitrary momentum. Through the perturbative expansion of the partition function, we derive a flow equation. In the large
c
limit, the deformed partition function reduces to that of the original CFT, up to a redefinition of the modular parameter. Crucially, the partition function retains invariance under the modular transformations of redefined modular parameters. Using the modular bootstrap trick, we show that the asymptotic density of states is governed by a Cardy-like formula.

On the gravity side, we first show the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed BTZ black hole is a quotient of hyperbolic space. We then reformulate the AdS
3
with root-
T\bar{T}
deformed boundary conditions in the Chern-Simons framework. Within this Chern-Simons formulation, we compute both the deformed BTZ black hole entropy and the one-loop partition function of the deformed AdS
3
using the Wilson loops and Wilson spools techniques, respectively. Our results reveal that the entropy of the deformed BTZ black hole follows a Cardy-like formula, in precise agreement with the field theory results derived via modular bootstrap trick. Furthermore, the one-loop partition function exactly matches the large
c
expansion of the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed CFT partition function. These results provide the evidence for the proposed duality between root-
T\bar{T}
deformed CFTs and AdS
3
gravity with root-
T\bar{T}
deformed boundary condition.

The one-loop partition function is described by tracing over a state-dependent Virasoro algebra, analogous to the case of
T\bar{T}
deformation. This suggests that the asymptotic symmetry of AdS
3
with root-
T\bar{T}
deformed boundary condition corresponds to a state-dependent Virasoro algebra. A natural extension would be to investigate the asymptotic symmetry algebra for AdS
3
under root-
T\bar{T}
deformation. Furthermore, we observe that the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed partition function loses modular invariance beyond the large
c
limit, as evidenced by its flow equation. However, an interesting exception arises when the initial partition function is given by a
(1,1)
weight modular form, the flow equation preserves the modular properties in this case. Notably, the seed theory is no longer the conformal field theory.

There are also some interesting topics for further study. In this work we only consider the large
c
limit of the deformed partition function, up to

Appendix A
Basics on modular form

In this section, we discuss the modular properties for the deformed partition function based on the flow equation. We first give some basics about the modular form. We then show the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed partition function have no obvious modular property

The modular function with weight
(k,k^{\prime})
is a function transformed as

\displaystyle f_{k,k^{\prime}}\left(\gamma\cdot\tau,\gamma\cdot\bar{\tau}\right)=(c\tau+d)^{k}(c\bar{\tau}+d)^{k^{\prime}}f_{k,k^{\prime}}(\tau,\bar{\tau}),

(A.1)

where

\displaystyle\gamma\cdot\tau=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\quad\gamma\cdot\bar{\tau}=\frac{a\bar{\tau}+b}{c\bar{\tau}+d},\quad ad-bc=1\quad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z}.

(A.2)

The function is modular invariant for
k=k^{\prime}=0
. It is useful to understand the action of the derivatives
\partial_{\tau}
and
\partial_{\bar{\tau}}
on modular functions of various weights. By using the chain rule, one can easily find that the derivative
\partial_{\tau}
and
\partial_{\bar{\tau}}
transforms under modular transformation as

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}=\partial_{\tau}(\gamma\cdot\tau)\partial_{\gamma\cdot\tau}=\frac{1}{(c\tau+d)^{2}}\partial_{\gamma\cdot\tau},

(A.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{\bar{\tau}}=\partial_{\tau}(\gamma\cdot\bar{\tau})\partial_{\gamma\cdot\bar{\tau}}=\frac{1}{(c\bar{\tau}+d)^{2}}\partial_{\gamma\cdot\bar{\tau}}.

(A.4)

which means the derivative
\partial_{\tau}
and
\partial_{\bar{\tau}}
act on a modular invariance function (
(0,0)
modular form) ending up with the (2,0) and (0,2) weight modular form, respectively.
We also have

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(c\tau+d)^{2}}\frac{1}{(c\bar{\tau}+d)^{2}}\partial_{\gamma\cdot\tau}\partial_{\gamma\cdot\bar{\tau}},

(A.5)

which act on the modular invariant function ending up with a
(2,2)
modular form. More generally, we have the modular covariant derivatives

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}_{\tau}^{(k)}=\partial_{\tau}-\frac{ik}{2\tau_{2}},\quad\mathsf{D}_{\bar{\tau}}^{(k^{\prime})}=\partial_{\bar{\tau}}+\frac{ik^{\prime}}{2\tau_{2}}.

(A.6)

This is also called the Ramanujan-Serre derivative.
\mathsf{D}_{\tau}^{(k)}
acts on a modular form of weight
(k,k^{\prime})
gives a modular form of weight
(k+2,k^{\prime})
. Similarly,
\mathsf{D}_{\bar{\tau}}^{(k^{\prime})}

increases the weight of such a modular form to
(k,k^{\prime}+2)
. Generally, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}_{\tau}^{(k)}\mathsf{D}_{\bar{\tau}}^{(k^{\prime})}=\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}+\left(\frac{ik^{\prime}}{2\tau_{2}}\partial_{\tau}-\frac{ik}{2\tau_{2}}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}\right)+\frac{k^{\prime}(k-1)}{4\tau_{2}^{2}}

(A.7)

which map the
(k,k^{\prime})
weight modular form into a
(k+2,k^{\prime}+2)
weight modular form. The simplest one is

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{(0)}\equiv\mathsf{D}_{\tau}^{(0)}\mathsf{D}_{\bar{\tau}}^{(0)}=\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}},

(A.8)

also we introduce the following notation

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{(k)}\equiv\mathsf{D}_{\tau}^{(k)}\mathsf{D}_{\bar{\tau}}^{(k)}=\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}+\frac{k}{2\tau_{2}}\partial_{\tau_{2}}+\frac{k(k-1)}{4\tau_{2}^{2}}.

(A.9)

In addition, it should be note that
\tau_{2}=\mathrm{Im}\tau
is a modular form of weight
(-1,-1)
.

The root-
T\bar{T}
deformed partition function has the following expansion

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{n}Z_{n}(\tau,\bar{\tau}).

(A.10)

In section
3.2
, we have shown the perturbation expansion can be written in terms of some derivative operators act on the original partition function

\displaystyle Z_{p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{p}\tau_{2}^{m}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{m}^{(p)}\right)Z_{0}(\tau,\bar{\tau}),&\text{for even $p$}\\
\left(\sum_{m=1}^{p}\tau_{2}^{m}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{m}^{(p)}\right)\widehat{\mathcal{R}}Z_{0}(\tau,\bar{\tau}),&\text{for odd $p$}\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{R}}Z_{0}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2\pi R\sqrt{E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}}e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}}.

(A.13)

We find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi R)^{2}}\left(\partial_{\tau_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{\tau_{2}}^{2}\right)e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}}=\left(E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}\right)e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}},

(A.14)

which means
e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}}
is the eigenstate of the operator
\left(\partial_{\tau_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{\tau_{2}}^{2}\right)
. Formally, we can define the square-root operator

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\equiv\sqrt{\partial_{\tau_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{\tau_{2}}^{2}}=2\sqrt{\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}},

(A.15)

which act on the
e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}}
with a square root of the eigenvalue

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\cdot e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}}=2\pi R\sqrt{E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}}e^{2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}E_{n}}.

(A.16)

Therefore, we have the following replacements

\displaystyle 2\pi R\sqrt{E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}}\mapsto\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\equiv\sqrt{\partial_{\tau_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{\tau_{2}}^{2}}=2\sqrt{\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}}.

(A.17)

In fact, the power of a generic operator is defined as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O})^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dss^{-\alpha-1}\mathrm{e}^{-s\mathcal{O}},

(A.18)

which is used to calculate root-
T\bar{T}
deformed correlation function
[
83
]
. The square-root operator can also be written as the Ramanujan-Serre derivative

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{R}}=2\sqrt{\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}}=2\sqrt{\mathscr{D}^{(0)}}.

(A.19)

In order to avoid the square root of the differential operator, we consider the second order recurrence relation and the result reads

\displaystyle Z_{p+2}=\frac{1}{(p+1)(p+2)}\Big(4\tau_{2}^{2}\left(\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\bar{\tau}}\right)+\tau_{2}\partial_{\tau_{2}}\Big)Z_{p},

(A.20)

which holds for both even and odd orders. The recurrence relation implies the flow equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}^{2}\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=\left(4\tau_{2}^{2}\left(\partial_{\tau}\partial_{{\bar{\tau}}}\right)+\tau_{2}\partial_{{\tau_{2}}}\right)\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda),

(A.21)

with the initial condition

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)\Big|_{\lambda=0}=Z_{0},\quad\partial_{\lambda}\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)\Big|_{\lambda=0}=-2\tau_{2}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}Z_{0}.

(A.22)

In the case of
T\bar{T}
deformation the recurrence relation is given by a Ramanujan-Serre derivative, which has certain modular properties. However, we do not find the modular properties from the flow equation for the root-
T\bar{T}
deformation. To see this, we first rewrite the recurrence relation in terms of Ramanujan-Serre derivative

\displaystyle Z_{p+2}=\frac{4}{(p+1)(p+2)}\tau_{2}^{2}\mathscr{D}^{(1)}Z_{p}.

(A.23)

This is second order recurrence relation, we have to give the initial condition

\displaystyle Z_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad Z_{1}=-2\tau_{2}\sqrt{\mathscr{D}^{(0)}}Z_{0}.

(A.24)

If we start from a modular invariant partition function
Z_{0}
, the first order correction
Z_{1}
is modular invariant since the
\mathscr{D}^{(0)}
map (0,0) modular form into (2,2) modular form and
\tau_{2}
is a
(-1,-1)
form. While the higher order correction is not obviously modular invariant because
\mathscr{D}^{(1)}
acts on modular invariant object will end up with a complicated result, which may not be a certain modular form in general. However, We can not rule out the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed partition function is modular invariant either.

In this appendix, we show another expansion of the root-

t=e^{-\lambda}

3.49
).

We start from the root-
T\bar{T}
deformed partition function, which can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\tau,\bar{\tau}|\lambda)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi R\tau_{2}\cosh(\lambda)E_{n}-2\pi R\tau_{2}\sinh(\lambda)\sqrt{E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}}+2\pi iR\tau_{1}P_{n}}

(B.1)

where we have defined

\displaystyle t=e^{-\lambda},\quad A_{n}=\sqrt{E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}}-E_{n},\quad B_{n}=\sqrt{E_{n}^{2}-P_{n}^{2}}+E_{n}.

(B.2)

Note the identity

\displaystyle\exp{\left(\frac{A_{n}tx}{2}-\frac{B_{n}x}{2t}\right)}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}J_{k}\left(x\sqrt{A_{n}B_{n}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{A_{n}}{B_{n}}}\right)^{k}t^{k},

(B.3)

which provide an Laurent expansion of parameter
t
.

(B.4)

where the

\displaystyle J_{\nu}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!\Gamma(k+\nu+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2k+\nu}.

(B.5)

One can verify it satisfies the flow equation (
3.49

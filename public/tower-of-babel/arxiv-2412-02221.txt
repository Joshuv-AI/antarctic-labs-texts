Higher symmetries of the lattices in 3D
I.T. Habibullin, A.R. Khakimova

Abstract
It is known that there is a duality between the Davey–Stewartson type coupled systems and a class of integrable two–dimensional Toda type lattices. More precisely, the coupled systems are generalized symmetries for the lattices and the lattices can be interpreted as dressing chains for the systems. In our recent study we have found a novel lattice which apparently is not related to the known ones by Miura type transformation. In the article we described higher symmetries to this lattice and derived a new coupled system of the DS type.



Institute of Mathematics, Ufa Scientific Center, Russian Academy of Sciences

Keywords:
3D lattices, generalized symmetries, Darboux integrable reductions, Lax pairs, Davey–Stewartson type coupled system.

Below we explain the aforementioned duality with the second order symmetries of the two–dimensional Volterra chain

u_{n,y}=u_{n}(v_{n+1}-v_{n}),\qquad v_{n,x}=v_{n}(u_{n}-u_{n-1}).

It is proven in

shifting the system forward.
By taking linear combinations of two symmetries given above we find a more complicated symmetry

\displaystyle u_{n,s}=\lambda u_{n,xx}+\mu u_{n,yy}+\lambda\left(u_{n}^{2}+2u_{n}V_{n}\right)_{x}+\mu\left(U_{n}^{2}\right)_{x}+\mu(2u_{n}v_{n})_{y},

(1.4)

\displaystyle V_{n,y}=v_{n,x},\quad\lambda\neq 0,

\displaystyle U_{n,x}=u_{n,y},\quad\mu\neq 0.

In view of the duality between these two classes, it can be concluded that the presence of a complete list of integrable equations of one of the classes of models would allow obtaining a complete list of integrable representatives of the other class. However, to date, the classification problem has not been solved for either of these two classes.
We have recently made some progress in the problem of classifying lattices. An algorithm for integrable classification of two–dimensional lattices has been proposed, based on the concept of Darboux–integrable finite–field reductions.

2
Symmetries of an auxiliary three–field lattice

In this section we concentrate on the problem of constructing generalized symmetries for an auxiliary lattice with three independent variables of the form

\displaystyle a_{n,y}=a_{n}(b_{n}-b_{n+1}+u_{n}-u_{n+1}),

(2.1)

\displaystyle b_{n,x}=b_{n}(a_{n-1}-a_{n}+u_{n}-u_{n-1}),

\displaystyle u_{n,y}-a_{n}(u_{n}-u_{n+1})=u_{n,x}+b_{n}(u_{n}-u_{n-1})

with the sought functions
a_{n}
,
b_{n}

a_{n}=\frac{u_{n,x}-u^{2}_{n}-1}{u_{n+1}-u_{n}},\qquad b_{n}=\frac{u_{n,y}-u^{2}_{n}-1}{u_{n}-u_{n-1}}.

In this case the lattice is transformed into equation (

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\psi}_{n,x}=a_{n}\left(\psi_{n+1}-\psi_{n}\right)+u_{n}\psi_{n},

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\psi}_{n,y}=b_{n}\left(\psi_{n}-\psi_{n-1}\right)+u_{n}\psi_{n}

provides a Lax pair for the lattice (

solve lattice (

\partial_{x}-B_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial_{y}-C_{1},

First, we need to describe the class of nonlocal variables on which the symmetries depend. For this purpose, we consider equations

[\partial_{x}-B_{1},L]=0,\qquad[\partial_{y}-C_{1},M]=0,

(2.7)

where the sought objects
L
and

are operators represented as formal power series of the shift operator

L=\sum^{1}_{i=-\infty}\alpha^{(i)}_{n}T^{i},\qquad M=\sum^{+\infty}_{i=-1}\beta^{(i)}_{n}T^{i}.

It is supposed that the first summands of the series are taken as follows

\alpha^{(1)}_{n}=-a_{n},\qquad\beta^{(-1)}_{n}=b_{n}.

(2.9)

The other coefficients are found from equations obtained by comparing the factors in front of the powers of

. Actually here we have to solve some linear equations, that generate nonlocalities. For example, the nonlocal variables
H_{n}:=\alpha^{(0)}_{n}
and
V_{n}:=-\alpha^{(-1)}_{n}
are found from equations

(1-T)H_{n}=D_{x}\log a_{n}b_{n+1},\qquad D_{y}H_{n}=(1-T)a_{n-1}b_{n}

(2.10)

and equations

(1-T)V_{n}a_{n-1}=D_{x}H_{n},\qquad D_{y}V_{n}a_{n-1}=D_{x}a_{n-1}b_{n},

(2.11)

respectively.
The coefficients
Q_{n}:=\beta^{(0)}_{n}
and
U_{n}:=\beta^{(1)}_{n}
of the series

are obtained in a similar way

D_{x}Q_{n}=(T-1)a_{n-1}b_{n},\qquad(T-1)Q_{n}=D_{y}\log a_{n}b_{n+1}

D_{x}U_{n}b_{n+1}=-D_{y}a_{n}b_{n+1},\qquad(1-T)b_{n+1}U_{n}=D_{y}Q_{n+1}.

(2.13)

Thus coefficients of the formal series
L
and
M

2.7

[\partial_{x}-B_{1},L^{k}]=0,\qquad[\partial_{y}-C_{1},M^{k}]=0.

(2.14)

We define new operators
B_{k}
and
C_{k}

B_{k}=(L^{k})_{+},\qquad C_{k}=(M^{k})_{-}.

(2.15)

Let us explain the meanings of the symbols in (
2.15

L^{k}=\sum^{k}_{i=-\infty}\alpha^{(i,k)}_{n}T^{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad M^{k}=\sum^{+\infty}_{i=-k}\beta^{(i,k)}_{n}T^{i}.

(2.16)

Then we suppose that

(L^{k})_{+}=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\alpha^{(i,k)}_{n}T^{i}-\sum^{k}_{i=1}\alpha^{(i,k)}_{n},\qquad(M^{k})_{-}=\sum^{-1}_{i=-k}\beta^{(i,k)}_{n}T^{i}-\sum^{-1}_{i=-k}\beta^{(i,k)}_{n}.

It is important that transformations
P_{\pm}
acting as
P_{+}:L^{k}\rightarrow(L^{k})_{+}
and
P_{-}:M^{k}\rightarrow(M^{k})_{-}

Note that such methods of truncating formal series differ from the standard methods usually used when searching for higher symmetries of chains in 3D (see, for example,
[
8
]

[
18

Such a rule for choosing the polynomial parts of the power series is related to the fact that the basic operators can be written in the following form

D_{x}-B_{1}=D_{x}-a_{n}\Delta_{+}-u_{n},\qquad D_{y}-C_{1}=D_{y}+b_{n}\Delta_{-}-u_{n},

where

are operators of the forward/backward discrete derivatives. The projection operators
P_{\pm}
are defined in such a way that the operators

and
C_{k}
can be represented as follows

B_{k}=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\bar{\alpha}^{(i,k)}_{n}\Delta_{+}^{i},\qquad C_{k}=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\bar{\beta}^{(i,k)}_{n}\Delta_{-}^{i}.

(2.18)

Therefore, all operators involved are polynomials of the operators

Since the operators

and

commute with each other, they have to admit common eigenfunctions. In other words, the series

and

can be chosen so that in addition to (

[\partial_{x}-B_{1},M]=0,\qquad[\partial_{y}-C_{1},L]=0

are satisfied as well.

Let us take two copies of time flows

y_{2},y_{3},y_{4},\dots

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{k}}L=[B_{k},L],\qquad\partial_{x_{k}}M=[B_{k},M],

(2.20)

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{k}}L=[C_{k},L],\qquad\partial_{y_{k}}M=[C_{k},M].

To search for symmetries, we move, following the method described in

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{k}}B_{m}-\partial_{x_{m}}B_{k}+[B_{m},B_{k}]=0,

(2.21)

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{k}}C_{m}-\partial_{y_{m}}C_{k}+[C_{m},C_{k}]=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{k}}B_{m}-\partial_{x_{m}}C_{k}+[B_{m},C_{k}]=0.

Theorem.

Equations of system

Proof of the Theorem is given in §4. It follows from theorem that lattice (

Note that equations (

Exchange-correlation torques from gauge symmetries
Jacques K. Desmarais, Kamel Bencheikh, Giovanni Vignale, Stefano Pittalis

Abstract
The problem of predicting accurate exchange-correlation (xc) spin-torques in non-collinear magnetic systems has dominated the scene of spin-density functional theory (SDFT) in the last two decades. Progress has been hindered by the fact that the spin torque is directly connected to the divergence of the spin current, a quantity that is extraneous to SDFT. Furthermore, SDFT does not apply in the presence of vector potentials and spin-orbit couplings. Here, we propose a solution that exploits the physical implications of the U(1)×SU(2) gauge invariance of the xc energy in SpinCurrent-DFT. We derive explicit xc torque expressions based on meta-generalized-gradient approximations in the framework of the generalized Kohn-Sham (GKS) formulation. One key term represents an xc-torque involving the GKS spin-kinetic energy density; and another term resembles the phenomenological spin current of the Landau-Lifshitz equations: both are derived from first-principles. We also show that the functional form ensures that the GKS particle- and spin-currents are identical, in form, to their interacting counterparts. Non-collinear equilibrium conditions and adiabatic dynamics are thus derived that resolve longstanding issues.



Introduction.

Density functional theory (DFT) can predict properties of materials from solely the knowledge of the atomic species in the materials and an approximation for the exchange-correlation (xc) energy; i.e., a functional of the particle density that maps the relevant interacting many-body problem onto an effective single-particle problem
Jones 2015
.
Given that the exact xc-energy functional of DFT is not known, empiricism can be reduced only by knowing the exact properties of the xc-energy functional
Perdew and Schmidt 2001
.

Generalizations of DFT hold the promise of yielding solutions for materials that can be described via Schrödinger-Pauli equations, involving two-component spinors. In Spin-DFT
von Barth and Hedin 1972
, the interacting problem is mapped onto an auxiliary non-interacting problem
reproducing the interacting particle and spin densities.
Thus, one must solve the single-particle equation

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}+v_{s}+\mu_{B}\vec{B}_{s}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\right]\Phi_{k}=\epsilon_{k}\Phi_{k}

(1)

where
\mu_{B}=\frac{1}{2}
(in Hartree atomic units) and
\Phi_{i}
are two-component Pauli spinors. The Kohn-Sham (KS) system reproduces the interacting particle density, via

n=\sum_{k}f_{k}\Phi_{k}^{\dagger}\Phi_{k}
,
and each component of the interacting spin magnetization, via

m^{a}=\sum_{k}f_{k}\Phi_{k}^{\dagger}\sigma^{a}\Phi_{k}
,
where the
{f_{k}}
are occupation (often, integer) numbers.
The effective potentials are

v_{s}=v+v_{\rm H}+v_{\rm xc}
,

{B}^{a}_{s}={B}^{a}+{B}^{a}_{\rm xc}
,
where
v_{\rm H}
is the usual Hartree potential.
The
{\rm xc}
-potentials are functional derivatives of the

{\rm xc}
-energy functional,
E_{\rm xc}[n,{\vec{m}}]
, with respect to the corresponding conjugate densities. Namely,

v_{\rm xc}=\frac{\delta E_{\rm xc}[n,{\vec{m}}]}{\delta n}
,
and

B^{a}_{\rm xc}=\frac{\delta E_{\rm xc}[n,{\vec{m}}]}{\delta m^{a}}
.
Thus,
E_{\rm xc}[n,{\vec{m}}]
is the key quantity that needs to be approximated. Although Eq. (
1

density

current

The latter quantity enters the exact equation of motion

\frac{\partial\vec{{m}}}{\partial t}+\partial_{\mu}\vec{{{J}}}_{\mu}=\vec{B}\times\vec{{m}}

Spin-current density functional theory

;
Vignale and Rasolt 1988
;
Bencheikh 2003

The GKS theory is a sharpening of the traditional KS theory in which the minimization of the energy with respect to densities is replaced by a minimization with respect to single-particle orbitals, in terms of which the densities are expressed. Next, let us consider the following gauge transformations.
A local U(1) transformation, is defined by

\Phi\rightarrow\exp\left[\frac{i}{c}\chi\right]\Phi

where
\chi
is a scalar function of the position.
A local SU(2) transformations is defined by

\Phi\rightarrow\exp\left[\frac{i}{c}{\vec{\lambda}\cdot\vec{\sigma}}\right]\Phi

where
\vec{\lambda}
is a vector function of the position. The overall effect of the above transformations is to rotate the spinors and each of the phases of their components as well. These position-dependent transformations implement a continuum of
local
rotations. The two “rotations” are performed conjointly in U(1)
\times
SU(2) transformations.

With the exception of the particle density
n

\times
SU(2), all the other variables of SCDFT are transformed according to precise rules (See SM, Sec. I, Eqs. S3 and S6).
The spin density is rotated in spin space, the current density is “translated” by a gauge-dependent term, and the spin current density is both rotated and translated. It is a fundamental result of SCDFT
Vignale and Rasolt 1988
;
Bencheikh 2003
that the xc energy functional is invariant under these transformations, i.e.,

E_{xc}[n,\vec{m}^{\prime},j_{\mu}^{\prime},\vec{J}_{\mu}^{\prime}]=E_{xc}[n,\vec{m},j_{\mu},\vec{J}_{\mu}]\,,

(6)

where the primed variables are connected to the unprimed variables by the U(1)
\times
SU(2) transformation. We emphasize that this crucial result follows from the U(1)
\times
SU(2) symmetry of the Coulomb interaction and holds even when the system under consideration includes external potentials that break the U(1)
\times
SU(2) symmetry of the full Hamiltonian. No comparable symmetry exists for the xc functional of SDFT.

Conclusions
.
Difficulties in capturing exchange-correlation spin torques non empirically, have persisted in Spin-DFT (SDFT).
Fundamentally, spin-torques involving spin-currents, vector potentials, and spin-orbit couplings are beyond the reach of SDFT.

Here we have presented a solution based on first principles, which substantially extends the early formulation of SpinCurrent-DFT and its range of applicability.

The newly derived relations, Eqs. (
9
)-(
13
) are equilibrium properties of non-collinear states, which follow from a gauge invariant extension of meta-generalized-gradient approximations.

Furthermore, Eq. (
14a
) and Eq. (
14b
) upgrade Eq. (

) to a larger domain of applicability.

)], with wide applicability to the study of spin fluctuations and excitations of magnetic systems.

Comparison of force decay in orthodontic elastomeric chains exposed to alcohol-free and herbal mouthrinses: An In Vitro study
Authors: Aulya Maysita Syahputri Arif, Eddy Heriyanto Habar, Zilal Islamy Paramma, Ardiansyah S Pawinru, Eka Erwansyah, Baharuddin M Ranggang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
OBJECTIVE:To evaluate and compare the effects of two alcohol-free mouthrinses, Listerine® Zero, and Pepsodent Daun Sirih®, on the force decay of orthodontic elastomeric chains commonly used in orthodontic treatment.MATERIALS AND METHODS:Sixty elastomeric chain segments were randomly allocated into three groups (n = 20 per group): control (artificial saliva), Listerine® Zero, and Pepsodent Daun Sirih®. Samples were immersed twice daily for 30 sec in their respective solutions and stored in artificial saliva at 37°C between immersions for 21 days. Force measurements were recorded at baseline (Day 0) and on Days 1, 7, 14, and 21 using a digital force gauge. Data normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. As the data were not normally distributed, the Kruskal–Wallis test followed by the Mann–Whitney U test was applied, with statistical significance set at P < 0.05.RESULTS:Both mouthrinses produced a progressive reduction in force over time, with the greatest force decay occurring within the first 24 hours, followed by a more gradual decline up to 21 days. However, elastomeric chains immersed in Pepsodent Daun Sirih® consistently retained higher residual force than those immersed in Listerine® Zero at all observation periods, indicating a slower rate of force degradation.CONCLUSION:Pepsodent Daun Sirih® demonstrated a lower degree of force decay and maintained significantly higher residual force compared with Listerine® Zero throughout the observation period. These findings suggest that Pepsodent Daun Sirih® may be a more suitable alcohol-free mouthrinse for preserving orthodontic force during treatment.

Introduction

Orthodontic treatment plays a key role in improving bite function and facial aesthetics. One of the most used components in fixed orthodontic appliances is the elastomeric chain, which is designed to deliver a continuous force to facilitate controlled tooth movement.[1] However, due to their viscoelastic nature, elastomeric chains are susceptible to force decay over time, particularly when exposed to intraoral environmental factors such as saliva, temperature fluctuations, and oral hygiene products.

Mouthrinses are commonly used as an adjunct to daily oral hygiene practices, especially during orthodontic treatment. Alcohol-containing mouthwashes have been associated with accelerated degradation of orthodontic materials.[2] Consequently, alcohol-free mouthrinses, including non-alcoholic, and herbal-based formulations, are often considered safer alternatives. Nevertheless, these products may still contain active agents, such as essential oils or plant-based extracts, which could interact with polyurethane elastomeric chains and potentially contribute to unwanted force reduction.[34]

Among popular alcohol-free options, Listerine Zero® is a non-alcoholic commercial mouthrinse designed to reduce oral tissue irritation while maintaining antibacterial efficacy. In contrast, Pepsodent Daun Sirih® is an herbal formulation enriched with betel leaf (Piper betle), which is known for its antimicrobial properties and relatively gentle action on oral tissues.[56] Although both products are marketed for individuals with sensitive oral conditions, their comparative effects on the mechanical integrity of orthodontic elastomeric chains have not been thoroughly investigated.

Previous studies have primarily focused on alcohol-containing mouthwashes, leaving a gap in the understanding of how alcohol-free mouthrinses influence orthodontic materials over time.[37] Therefore, the present study aimed to evaluate and compare the force decay behavior of orthodontic elastomeric chains exposed to Pepsodent Daun Sirih® and Listerine Zero® under simulated intraoral conditions. It was hypothesized that differences in mouthrinse composition, despite the absence of alcohol, would result in significant variations in force degradation. The findings of this study are expected to provide evidence-based guidance for orthodontists in selecting mouthrinses that help preserve orthodontic force efficacy during treatment.

Materials and Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This in vitro true experimental laboratory study was conducted at the Department of Oral Biology, Hasanuddin University, between February and March 2025. The study aimed to evaluate the effects of alcohol-free mouthrinses on the force decay of orthodontic elastomeric chains. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of the Dental Hospital, Hasanuddin University (Approval No. 093/KEPK FKG-RSGMP UH/EA/III/2025).

Sample preparation

Sixty clear elastomeric chain segments (Ormco® Power Chain, USA; short module type) were used in this study. Each segment was cut into five-loop lengths using sterile scissors. The samples were randomly allocated into three equal groups (n = 20 per group) as follows:

Each elastomeric chain segment was extended to a standardized length of 25 mm using custom-fabricated acrylic jigs with stainless steel pins positioned at both ends. The stretched samples were individually stored in closed containers filled with artificial saliva and maintained at 37°C to simulate intraoral conditions throughout the experimental period.

Each immersion lasted for 30 sec. After each immersion cycle, the samples were rinsed with distilled water and subsequently stored in fresh artificial saliva. This immersion protocol was repeated consistently over a 21-day experimental period.

Force measurement

Force measurements were recorded at baseline (Day 0) and on Days 1, 7, 14, and 21 using a digital force gauge (NK-200, Imada Co., Japan) with a precision of 0.1 N. Prior to each measurement, the samples were gently blotted dry and tested at room temperature using a standardized vertical pull technique.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS software version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Data normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. As the data were not normally distributed, the Kruskal–Wallis test was used to identify significant differences among the study groups. Post hoc comparisons were subsequently performed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

A progressive and statistically significant force decay was observed in all groups throughout the observation period (P < 0.0001). The greatest reduction in force occurred within the first 24 hours, followed by a more gradual decline over time. Elastomeric chains immersed in Pepsodent Daun Sirih® consistently retained higher residual force compared with those immersed in Listerine Zero® at all observation points. In contrast, the Listerine Zero® group exhibited greater force loss over time. A time-dependent reduction in tensile force was also observed in the control group, indicating that force decay is an inherent characteristic of elastomeric chains, even in the absence of chemical exposure.

Discussion

This study demonstrates that different alcohol-free mouthrinse formulations exert distinct and statistically significant effects on the mechanical stability of orthodontic elastomeric chains. The herbal-based mouthrinse Pepsodent Daun Sirih® consistently preserved tensile force more effectively than the non-herbal, non-alcohol-based Listerine Zero® at all evaluated time points over the 21-day observation period. The comparison of force degradation between Pepsodent Daun Sirih® and Listerine Zero® over 21 days is illustrated in Figure 3. The Mann–Whitney U test confirmed significantly higher residual force values in the Pepsodent Daun Sirih® group compared with the Listerine Zero® group on Days 1, 7, 14, and 21 (P < 0.0001; see Table 2 and Figure 4).

Maintaining a stable orthodontic force over time is essential for predictable tooth movement and optimal treatment efficiency. The superior force retention observed with the herbal formulation in this study may be attributed to its relatively milder chemical composition, which may reduce oxidative and hydrolytic stress on the polyurethane matrix of elastomeric chains. Previous studies have demonstrated that oxidative agents, flavoring oils, and certain antimicrobial compounds can accelerate polymer chain scission, resulting in premature loss of elasticity and force delivery.[910] By limiting exposure to such reactive substances, herbal-based mouthrinses may slow polymer degradation and help extend the functional lifespan of orthodontic components.

The present findings are consistent with earlier in vitro studies reporting that herbal-based mouthwashes generally exert less chemical impact on elastomeric materials than conventional formulations containing synthetic active agents.[11] The comparatively better performance of Pepsodent Daun Sirih® may be related to its herbal extracts, which are less likely to disrupt the structural integrity of polyurethane elastomeric chains than stronger chemical constituents present in some non-herbal mouthrinses.[12] Similar observations have been reported by Pawar et al. and Javidi et al.,[1314] who demonstrated that force degradation patterns vary according to mouthrinse composition, even among alcohol-free products.

From a clinical perspective, selecting a mouthrinse that minimizes force degradation may reduce the need for frequent appliance adjustments and replacement of elastomeric modules, thereby improving treatment efficiency and patient comfort. In addition, patient compliance with mouthrinse use is influenced by factors such as taste, perceived safety, and the absence of oral irritation. Herbal formulations containing betel leaf extract have been reported to enhance patient acceptance while maintaining antimicrobial efficacy.[1516] Consequently, such mouthrinses may support oral hygiene adherence during orthodontic therapy, while preserving mechanical performance.

Nevertheless, it is important to note that not all herbal formulations are necessarily benign. Santana et al.[10] reported that certain herbal mouthwashes may also induce substantial force degradation, depending on factors such as pH, essential oil content, and other bioactive compounds. The discrepancies between studies may be attributed to variations in mouthrinse composition, experimental protocols, and storage conditions. These findings underscore the importance of evaluating individual mouthrinse formulations rather than generalizing based solely on herbal or alcohol-free classification. Overall, careful selection of mouthrinses based on their chemical composition—rather than alcohol content alone—may contribute to preserving the longevity and effectiveness of orthodontic force during treatment.

Clinical implications

Clinicians may consider recommending herbal-based mouthrinses with mild chemical properties, such as Pepsodent Daun Sirih®, to help preserve orthodontic force levels and reduce the need for frequent elastomeric chain replacement during fixed appliance therapy.

Limitations

As an in vitro study, this investigation did not replicate complex intraoral conditions, including saliva dynamics, enzymatic activity, and masticatory forces. Additionally, only one elastomeric chain brand and two mouthrinse formulations were evaluated, which may limit the generalizability of the findings.

Future directions

Future studies should include in vivo investigations to better reflect intraoral conditions, evaluate a wider range of mouthrinses, and assess the effects of dynamic forces such as mastication. Extending the observation period beyond 21 days may also provide insight into long-term orthodontic force behavior.

Conclusion

The results indicate that both alcohol-free mouthrinses influenced the mechanical integrity of orthodontic elastomeric chains over time. However, Pepsodent Daun Sirih® consistently exhibited higher force retention compared with Listerine Zero®. These findings suggest that Pepsodent Daun Sirih® may be more favorable for preserving orthodontic force during fixed appliance therapy.

Astrocyte-specific NRCAM deficiency promotes GABAergic synapse pruning to drive central sensitization in bone cancer pain
Authors: Zuoxia Zhang, Yunfan Hou, Yanting Mao, Li Jiang, Cui’e Lu, Jiacheng Yu, Yulin Huang, Rui Xu, Simin Huang, Xiao Zhang, Huijie Zhu, Wei Zhang, Yu’e Sun, Zhengliang Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAstrocytic activation is critically involved in the development and maintenance of bone cancer pain (BCP). Recent studies have shown that astrocytes participate in synaptic remodeling through synaptic phagocytosis, whereas neuronal cell adhesion molecule (NRCAM) restricts the extension of perisynaptic astrocytic processes and exerts an anti-phagocytic effect. However, whether astrocyte-mediated synaptic phagocytosis contributes to BCP remains to be explored. Hence, this study investigated the role of astrocytic NRCAM deficiency in synaptic remodeling and central sensitization in BCP.MethodsA mouse model of BCP was established by intrafemoral inoculation of fibrosarcoma cells. Pain-related behaviors were evaluated by spontaneous pain assessment and mechanical withdrawal threshold testing. Bone destruction and tumor infiltration were examined by hematoxylin and eosin staining. Synaptic alterations and astrocytic activation were assessed by western blotting and immunofluorescence. Astrocyte-mediated synaptic engulfment was analyzed using immunofluorescence, three-dimensional reconstruction, magnetic-activated cell sorting, and Golgi-Cox staining. Moreover, fluorocitrate was used to inhibit the astrocytic activation. Astrocytic NRCAM was overexpressed via adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated gene delivery.ResultsFibrosarcoma cell inoculation induced persistent spontaneous pain and mechanical hyperalgesia in C3H/HeN mice, accompanied by trabecular bone destruction and tumor infiltration. In the spinal cord, expression of the excitatory synaptic markers VGLUT1 and PSD95 was increased, whereas expression of the inhibitory synaptic markers VGAT and Gephyrin was decreased, together with marked astrocytic activation. Multiple complementary assays consistently showed that astrocytes engulfed both excitatory and inhibitory synapses, with a clear preference for GABAergic synapses. Pharmacological inhibition of astrocytic activation with fluorocitrate alleviated pain behaviors and improved synaptic remodeling. NRCAM expression was significantly downregulated in tumor-bearing mice. Restoration of astrocytic NRCAM expression by AAV markedly reduced astrocyte-mediated engulfment towards GABAergic synapses, had minimal effect on excitatory synapses, and significantly attenuated BCP.ConclusionsReactive astrocytes preferentially phagocytose GABAergic synapses in BCP, thereby contributing to synaptic imbalance and central sensitization. This process is associated with downregulation of astrocytic NRCAM. Restoring astrocytic NRCAM alleviates BCP by suppressing excessive astrocyte-mediated phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses. These findings identify astrocytic NRCAM-dependent synaptic phagocytosis as an unrecognized mechanism underlying BCP and as a potential therapeutic target.Keywords: Bone cancer pain, Astrocyte, Phagocytosis, NRCAM, GABAergic synapse

Introduction

Bone metastases of cancers cause severe bone pain [1, 2]. Either spontaneous or ongoing, this pain is always unbearable, greatly affecting the quality of life [3]. Effective therapeutic strategies, however, are currently unavailable for alleviating bone cancer pain, due to its unclear molecular mechanisms [4]. Inadequate management of bone pain in patients with metastatic cancers may result in some psychological disorders (e.g., anxiety and depression) that negatively influence the prognosis [5, 6]. Hence, it is of profound significance to unveil the mechanisms underlying cancer-induced bone pain.

Our previous data have validated the involvement of spinal dorsal horn (SDH) astrocytes in mice with bone cancer pain [7, 8]. Reactive astrocytes responding to bone cancer pain mediate central sensitization and pain-like behaviors by releasing proinflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and metalloproteinases via the NF-κB and STAT3 signaling pathways [9, 10]. Moreover, reactive astrocytes participate in the demyelinating processes in ischemic stroke mice through phagocytic uptake of synaptic debris [11]. Activation of reactive astrocytes is widely observed in the central nervous system (CNS) diseases such as multiple sclerosis, Parkinson’s disease, and Alzheimer’s disease [12–15]. Astrocytes possess the function of LC3-associated phagocytosis, which degrades microglial debris under the orchestration of Rubicon [16]. Although reactive astrocytes, as non-professional phagocytes, have become a hot topic in the biomedical research of CNS diseases, their involvement in bone cancer pain remains to be exploited.

Opsonins, phagocytic receptors, and intracellular molecules are involved in phagocytosis [17, 18]. Among these, multiple epidermal growth factor-like domains protein 10 (MEGF10) and Mer tyrosine kinase (MERTK) receptors mediate astrocyte-induced excitatory synapse elimination, whereas neuronal cell adhesion molecule (NRCAM) contributes to the engulfment of inhibitory synapses [19, 20]. NRCAM governs astrocyte-synapse adhesions, and loss of perisynaptic NRCAM interactions reduces the number of GABAergic synapses with minor effects on glutamatergic synapses [20]. Here, we tested the hypothesis that phagocytic molecules in astrocytes mediate pain-like behaviors in mice with cancer-induced bone pain through synaptic pruning in the SDH.

Materials and methods

Animals

Male C3H/HeN mice aged 4–5 weeks and weighting 20–25 g (Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Company) were housed in a standard environment at 21 ± 1 °C temperature and in a 12:12 light-dark cycle, and given free accesses to food and water. The mice were randomly assigned into different groups. Animal procedures were carried out in accordance with the experimental animal guidelines and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital. Efforts were made to minimize animal distress and reduce the number of animals used.

Bone cancer pain model in mice

A bone cancer pain model in mice was created as previously reported [7]. Briefly, mice were anesthetized with pentobarbital and injected with 20 µl of α-minimal essential medium containing 2 × 105 NCTC-2472 fibrosarcoma cells into the right femoral space.

AAV-mediated overexpression of NRCAM

The adeno-associated virus serotype 5 (AAV5) was chosen to overexpress NRCAM [21]. AAV-NRCAM was recombined with the gfaABC1D promoter, targeting the NRCAM specifically in astrocytes without affecting synaptic NRCAM. AAV with the GFP gene (AAV-GFP) was used as a control. AAV-NRCAM was injected into tumor-bearing mice injected with NCTC-2472 cells 28 days before the cancer-induced bone pain modeling.

Drug treatment

Fluorocitrate was dissolved in normal saline at a dose of 0.2 nmol/5 µl and intrathecally injected into the mouse subarachnoid space on postoperative day 14 (D14). Mice in control group were similarly administered with an equivalent volume of normal saline.

Nociception assay

Nociception assay was performed to measure pain-like behaviors on -D1, D4, D7, D10, D14, D21, and D28 in the sham group, tumor group, control group, and NRCAM group. Measurements were taken 24 h before fluorocitrate treatment (-24 h) and 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, and 24 h post fluorocitrate treatment. After a 30-minute acclimation, the number of spontaneous flinches (NSFs) within 2 min was recorded. Paw withdrawal mechanical thresholds (PWMTs) defined as the minimum stimulus strength elicited paw flinching or withdrawal, were measured via the up-down Von Frey method. Paw withdraw thermal latency (PWTL) was measured with an increasing-temperature hot plate. The starting temperature on the hot plate was set to 30 °C and a heating rate of 12 °C/min and cut-off temperature of 55 °C were used. Stimulation was immediately stopped when the animal showed nocifensive behavior and the corresponding temperature was recorded as the threshold. Paw withdraw thermal latency were measured for 3 times at 10 min intervals and taking the average of three measurements afterwards. The behavior test was handled for three consecutive days after modeling.

Cell culture of primary astrocytes

Primary astrocytes were derived from neonatal mice within 48 h under aseptic conditions as previously reported [22]. Briefly, the cerebral cortices were excised from neonatal mice, the meninges were removed, and the tissue was digested with 0.25% trypsin for 10 min. After centrifugation, the cells were filtered and resuspended in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. The cells were then cultured in a humidified incubator containing 5% CO2 at 37 °C. Fresh medium was replaced twice a week. After 10–14 days, the astrocytes were purified and collected for further research. Reactive astrocytes were obtained via induction of IL-1α, TNF, and C1q for 24 h [23].

Magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS)

Dissociated mouse spinal cord tissues of four mice were processed as a single specimen for isolating astrocyte cell surface antigen-2-positive (ACSA-2+) cells via MACS [22]. Cells were incubated with anti-ACSA-2 microBeads for 10 min, and ACSA-2+ cells were separated from the ACSA-2-labeled fraction with a MACS column and separator.

Hematoxylin and eosin staining

Femoral tissues were fixed in paraformaldehyde, decalcificated in EDTA decalcification solution and embedded in paraffin for preparing tissue slices with a 5 μm thickness stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), and captured via microscope.

Golgi-cox staining

The mouse spinal cord was subjected to Golgi-Cox staining using a Golgi Stain Kit. Following pentobarbital anesthesia, the spinal cords were extracted, immersed in impregnation solutions A and B, and underwent a 14-day treatment. The staining solution was then replaced with solution C. After dehydration in alcohol, the tissue sections were cleared in xylene. All sections were observed under a light microscope.

Western blotting

After pentobarbital anesthesia, mice were sacrificed on -D1, D4, D7, D10, D14, D21 and D28, as well as 3 h post an intrathecal injection of fluorocitrate. The lumbar spinal cord was obtained and homogenized in lysis solution. After centrifugation for 15 min at 12,000 rpm at 4 °C, the supernatants were collected for preparing protein samples that were quantified using the BCA Protein Assay Kit. A total of 70 µg protein per lane was loaded onto 10% SDS-PAGE, transferred to PVDF filter membranes and blocked in 5% skim milk for 2 h at room temperature. Membranes were then incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight and the corresponding secondary antibody for 2 h. Western blotting bands were visualized using the enhanced ECL system, and quantified by the ImageJ software. β-actin was used as a reference protein.

Immunofluorescence

Transcardial perfusion with normal saline and 4% paraformaldehyde was performed on D14 and 3 h after fluorocitrate treatment. Mouse spinal cord was extracted, post-fixed and dehydrated in 30% sucrose until tissue sank. Tissue pieces at 20 μm were blocked in 10% FBS for 2 h at room temperature. Following an overnight incubation with primary antibodies at 4 °C, and 488-conjugated anti-mouse IgG, 594-conjugated anti-rabbit IgG and 647-conjugated anti-rat IgG antibodies on the next day, immunofluorescence staining was observed via a laser-scanning confocal microscope.

Three-dimensional reconstruction

Z-Stacks (63× oil immersion) were utilized for three-dimensional reconstruction using the Imaris software. The volume of lysosomes and synaptic puncta, measured based on the surface-rendered images, was used to calculate the phagocytosis percentage using the following formula: phagocytosis percentage (%) = (volume of internalized synaptic puncta / volume of lysosomes in astrocytes × 100%. Researchers were blinded to groups.

Optical-fiber-based Ca2+ signal recording

Mice were injected with rAAV-hsyn-GCaMP6s or rAAV-GfaABC1D-GCaMP6 virus into dorsal horn using the stereotaxic protocol. Three weeks later mice were implanted with an optical-fiber, optic fibers were implanted unilaterally 0.2 mm above the coordinates listed for viral injection. Then photometric recordings were conducted using the fiber photometry recording system. Calcium-dependent fluorescence signals were obtained by stimulating neurons and terminals expressing GCaMP6s with laser intensities for the 470 nm wavelength bands (40 µW), and 410 nm signal (20 µW) was used to correct movement artifacts. Light emission was recorded using an sCMOS Camera, and the values of Ca2+ signal changes (ΔF/F) by calculating (F - F0)/F0 (Averaged baseline fluorescence signal recorded) was analyzed by MATLAB. A 10s window around the stimulation point was analyzed, with the period 2s before stimulus onset taken as baseline. Ca2+ responses during the first six times of the required behavior of each mouse were analyzed. For fiber photometry recordings in the von Frey test, the filament stimulation (2 g) was delivered onto the ipsilateral hind paw post 30 min habituation, unless there was a withdrawal of the paw, stimulation was terminated at any moment, while analyzing the wave of Ca2+ responses during the stimulations.

Statistical analysis

The number of animals was determined based on previous experience. All data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation. Statistical analyses were conducted with GraphPad Prism 8. Repeated measures ANOVA was utilized to analyze differences in pain-like behaviors within groups. One-way ANOVA or t-test was utilized to compare differences in pain-like behaviors and the biochemical data among groups, followed by a Bonferroni test for post-hoc analysis. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The BCP model elicits persistent pain-like behaviors with osteolytic destruction

Through implanting NCTC-2472 fibrosarcoma cells, we successfully established a C3H/HeN mouse model of bone cancer pain (Fig. 1A-D). Pain-like behaviors were measured on -D1, D4, D7, D10, D14, D21, and D28. Baseline NSF and PWMT on -D1 were comparable between the sham and control groups (P > 0.05). NSF in the sham group sharply climaxed on D4 and decreased back to baseline on D10 and maintained until the end of the experimental period (D4 P < 0.001, D7 P < 0.001, D10 P > 0.05, D14 P > 0.05, D21 P > 0.05, D28 P > 0.05). In the tumor group, NSF significantly increased throughout the 28 days (D4 P < 0.001, D7 P < 0.001, D10 P < 0.001, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001). Conversely, PWMT rapidly decreased on D4 in both groups, which then fluctuated in an increasing trend in sham group but continuously decreased in tumor group until D28 (sham: D4 P = 0.001, D7 P = 0.003, D10 P > 0.05, D14 P > 0.05, D21 P > 0.05, D28 P > 0.05; tumor: D4 P = 0.002, D7 P = 0.006, D10 P = 0.001, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001). Behavioral tests also showed a significant decrease in threshold in response to thermal stimulation on D14 (P < 0.001). Moreover, H&E staining of mouse femoral sections presented dispersion in the trabecular bone and infiltration of tumor cells in the intramedullary space (Fig. 1E). Hence, the elicitations of pain-like behaviors and pathological manifestations both demonstrated our successes in establishing bone cancer pain model.

E/I imbalance in the SDH: increased ratio of excitatory to inhibitory synapses

Synaptic proteins were detected in mouse spinal cord to reveal the ratio of excitatory to inhibitory synapses, including VGLUT1 (excitatory presynaptic marker), PSD95 (excitatory postsynaptic marker), VGAT (inhibitory presynaptic marker) and Gephyrin (inhibitory postsynaptic marker) (Fig. 1F-G). Compared with baseline, significantly upregulated VGLUT1 and PSD95, as well as downregulated VGAT and Gephyrin were observed in mice experiencing bone cancer pain (VGLUT1: D4 P = 0.021, D7 P < 0.001, D10 P < 0.001, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001; PSD95: D7 P = 0.008, D10 P < 0.001, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001; VGAT: D4 P = 0.001, D7 P = 0.001, D10 P < 0.001, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001; Gephyrin: D4 P = 0.032, D7 P < 0.001, D10 P < 0.001, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001). Immunofluorescence was performed to validate the perturbation in synaptic proteins via calculating the VGLUT1/Gephyrin and PSD95/VGAT ratios (Fig. 1H-I). A remarkable increase in the ratio of excitatory to inhibitory synapses was observed in bone cancer pain mice (VGLUT1/Gephyrin P < 0.001; PSD95/VGAT P < 0.001). The mean fluorescence intensity of VGAT and Gephyrin suggested it (Fig. 1J).

According to the results above, we supposed that BCP shifts the dorsal horn toward excitation-heightened neuronal activity is accompanied by a time-dependent loss of inhibitory synaptic markers. As far as we know, GCaMP6s was locally expressed in the superficial layers of dorsal horn using rAAV-hsyn-GCaMP6s for fiber photometry recordings of calcium dynamics with pain stimuli. Calcium dynamic recordings showed that 2 g suprathreshold mechanical stimuli induces a significant increase of calcium signals in GCaMP6s-expressing mice. Evoked population Ca²⁺ signals in the superficial dorsal horn were larger and more sustained in tumor-bearing mice than in sham, with single-cell heatmaps revealing stronger and more synchronous responses across units than controls (Fig. 2A-E). Reduced GABAergic synaptic input weakened inhibitory modulation, disrupted neuronal excitation-inhibition balance, and elevated and prolongs intracellular calcium transients in neurons. In conclusion, across the complementary readouts-population Ca²⁺ dynamics, confocal colocalization, and time-course biochemistry, BCP drives hyperexcitability and a selective erosion of inhibitory synaptic architecture, establishing a robust E/I shift, which frames downstream circuits.

Reactive astrocytic profiling in SDH (GFAP/C3, GFAP/S100a10 upregulation)

Astrocytes function as non-professional phagocytes in the CNS [24]. Astrocytic markers GFAP, C3 and S100a10 were time-dependently upregulated in SDH astrocytes of mice with bone cancer pain, indicating a reactive phenotype of astrocytes (GFAP: D10 P < 0.001, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001; C3: D10 P = 0.004, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001; S100a10: D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001) (Fig. 2F-G). On D14, immunofluorescence results demonstrated an obvious increase in GFAP/C3-positive astrocytes and a slight increase in GFAP/S100a10-positive astrocytes (GFAP/C3 P < 0.001; GFAP/S100a10 P < 0.001) (Fig. 2H-I). We thus speculated that phagocytic astrocytes were activated in mice with bone cancer pain.

Multi-modal evidence for astrocytic synaptic engulfment with a preference for gabaergic synapses

Astrocytic phagocytosis of synaptic components in mice with bone cancer pain was visualized using double immunofluorescence, three-dimensional reconstruction, magnetic-activated cell sorting, and Golgi-Cox staining methods (Fig. 3). Both excitatory and inhibitory synapses were engulfed in SDH astrocytes (GFAP/VGLUT1 P = 0.044, GFAP/PSD95 P = 0.001, GFAP/VGAT P < 0.001, GFAP/Gephyrin P < 0.001; GFAP/LAMP1/VGLUT1 P < 0.001, GFAP/LAMP1/PSD95 P < 0.001, GFAP/LAMP1/VGAT P < 0.001, GFAP/LAMP1/Gephyrin P < 0.001; VGLUT1 P = 0.032, PSD95 P = 0.011, VGAT P < 0.001, Gephyrin P < 0.001; P < 0.001). The engulfment rate, calculated from the surface-rendered images, indicated that inhibitory synapses were predominantly engulfed by reactive astrocytes in mice with cancer-induced bone pain. Under normal conditions, a biased phagocytosis of excitatory synapses might contribute to the balance between the synaptic excitation and inhibition.

Last but not least, Golgi staining suggested that fluorocitrate reversed the reduction in synapse number in cancer pain mice (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4M-N).

Astrocytic NRCAM is downregulated and localized to inhibitory synaptic microdomains

NRCAM, an adhesion cell molecule located in astrocyte-neuron junctions, participates in cell proliferation, axon growth and synapse formation [26]. The transcellular homophilic interaction between astrocytic and neuronal NRCAMs restricts perisynaptic astrocytic processes. Depletion of NRCAM breaks this homogeneous force, resulting in the loss of inhibitory synapses [20]. The role of NRCAM in tumor development, progression, and prognosis has recently been highlighted [27]. Our work revealed a significant downregulation of NRCAM in tumor-bearing mice inoculated with NCTC-2472 cells (D7 P < 0.001, D10 P < 0.001, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001) (Fig. 6A-C). Immunofluorescence results indicated that astrocytic NRCAM was significantly downregulated, while neuronal NRCAM was barely influenced in tumor-bearing mice (GFAP/NRCAM P < 0.001, NeuN/NRCAM P > 0.05) (Fig. 6D-E). Consistently in SDH astrocytes as well as the primary astrocytes, we validated a lower protein expression of NRCAM in the reactive astrocytes compared to the resting astrocytes (P = 0.003, P < 0.001, GFAP/NRCAM P < 0.001) (Fig. 6F-K).

Astrocyte-specific NRCAM overexpression selectively preserves inhibitory synapses and alleviates pain

Furthermore, tumor-bearing mice were injected with AAV-GFP or AAV-NRCAM. Fluorescence imaging of the SDH showed a gradual increase in the positive expression of GFP, peaking on D28, which determined the optimal time point for establishing the bone cancer pain model (Fig. 7A). It was validated that injection of AAV-NRCAM significantly upregulated NRCAM in astrocytes (GFAP/NRCAM P < 0.001, NeuN/NRCAM P > 0.05, P < 0.001, P < 0.001) (Fig. 7B-I). A significant reduction in NSF and a significant increase in PWMT were detected in mice injected with AAV-NRCAM, with the most obvious changes observed on D10 (NSF: D7 P < 0.001, D10 P < 0.001, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001; PWMT: D10 P < 0.001, D14 P < 0.001, D21 P < 0.001, D28 P < 0.001; P < 0.001; P < 0.001) (Fig. 8A-E). Interestingly, injection of AAV-NRCAM reversed astrocyte-mediated engulfment of inhibitory synapses, but not excitatory synapses (VGLUT1 P > 0.05, PSD95 P > 0.05, VGAT P < 0.001, Gephyrin P < 0.001; GFAP/VGLUT1 P > 0.05, GFAP/PSD95 P > 0.05, GFAP/VGAT P < 0.001, GFAP/Gephyrin P < 0.001; GFAP/LAMP1/VGLUT1 P > 0.05, GFAP/LAMP1/PSD95 P > 0.05, GFAP/LAMP1/VGAT P < 0.001, GFAP/LAMP1/Gephyrin P < 0.001; VGLUT1 P > 0.05, PSD95 P > 0.05, VGAT P < 0.001, Gephyrin P < 0.001; P < 0.001) (Fig. 8F-O). Accordingly, our findings demonstrated that the astrocyte-mediated inhibitory synaptic loss in cancer-induced bone pain mice was dependent on the downregulation of astrocytic NRCAM in SDH.

Discussion

Previous studies have suggested that endogenous chronic pain is primarily attributed to excitatory perturbation in the CNS [28]. Our data revealed higher expression levels of VGLUT1 and PSD95, as well as lower expression levels of VGAT and Gephyrin in the SDH of mice with cancer-induced bone pain, compared with sham controls. This suggesting an activated glutamatergic signaling and inhibited GABAergic signaling. Therefore, we considered the involvement of synaptic imbalance in bone cancer pain.

Reactive astrocytes exert a fundamental role in the synaptic imbalance of the CNS [29–31]. Astrocytes possess phagocytic machinery that recognizes phagocytic targets via phagocytic receptors and adaptor molecules, including MEGF10, MERTK, αvβ3/5 integrins, LDL receptor-related protein 1 (LRP1), and G-protein coupled receptor 56 (GPCR56) [32, 33]. Phagocytic astrocytes contribute to the promotion of synapse formation, maturation, and elimination. NRCAM protects GABAergic synapses against excessive phagocytosis via coupling with Gephyrin at GABAergic postsynaptic sites. Knockdown of NRCAM can reduce the number of GABAergic synapses [20]. We also constructed a protein-protein interaction network for NRCAM. The results showed that NRCAM interacts with multiple proteins in complex ways, and these interacting proteins likely contribute to the regulation of synaptic stability and synaptic plasticity. Functional analysis of these interacting proteins revealed their important roles in synaptic pruning, neuroprotection, and inflammatory responses. Therefore, NRCAM, through its interactions with these proteins, may play a central role in regulating astrocyte-mediated GABAergic synaptic phagocytosis. Here, we clarified the potential role of astrocytic phagocytosis in modulating pathological cancer-induced bone pain.

Mounting evidence suggests the maladaptive effects of reactive astrocytes in the SDH on bone metastases. In the present study, the number of reactive astrocytes significantly increased on D14 post NCTC-2472 injection. Moreover, we found that GFAP labeling with VGAT and Gephyrin synaptic puncta was more pronounced in reactive astrocytes in mice with bone cancer pain compared to VGLUT1 and PSD95 synaptic puncta, indicating a deficiency in GABAergic signaling. This ultimately resulted in pain-like behaviors in bone cancer pain mice through inducing the hyperexcitability in the CNS.

We investigated the mechanisms governing GABAergic synapse pruning. Our results revealed that astrocytes were more likely to engulf GABAergic synapses in the SDH of mice with bone cancer pain, and this engulfment was dependent on the downregulation of NRCAM, which promoted astrocytic phagocytosis towards GABAergic synapses. As expected, injection of AAV-NRCAM into tumor-bearing mice effectively relieved bone cancer pain and restored the number of GABAergic synapses. Recent studies have illustrated the prognostic value of NRCAM in gastric cancer, osteosarcomas, prostate cancer, and endometrial cancer [27, 34–36]. These findings have highlighted the potential of NRCAM as a valuable prognostic biomarker across multiple cancer types. Further research is warranted to fully understand the mechanisms by which NRCAM influences cancer progression and to explore its potential as a therapeutic target for cancer patients.

Notably, the elevated expressions of VGLUT and PSD95 were inconsistent with the phagocytosis machinery of reactive astrocytes in engulfing VGLUT1 and PSD95 synaptic puncta. Reactive astrocytes can release cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-1β, which can modulate synaptic transmission and plasticity [37]. Chemokines like CX3CL1 and CCL2 can influence neuronal migration and synaptic connectivity [38]. Additionally, synaptogenic molecules like thrombospondins (TSPs) and hevin can promote the formation and stabilization of new synapses [39]. These multifaceted functions suggest that reactive astrocytes are integral to maintaining the balance between synaptic pruning and formation. Beyond their phagocytic role, we considered that reactive astrocytes participate in synaptic remodeling and glutamatergic signaling within spinal cord circuits through producing cytokines, chemokines, and synaptogenic molecules. Furthermore, astrocyte-mediated glutamatergic synapse pruning, which involves MEGF10 and MERTK phagocytic receptors, may be the spotlight in future research [40]. Understanding how these receptors interact with synaptic components could provide insights into the mechanisms underlying synaptic remodeling during pathological conditions. Given the complexity of astrocyte functions, further investigation into the roles of MEGF10 and MERTK in synaptic pruning could lead to novel therapeutic strategies aimed at modulating astrocyte activity and improving the clinical outcomes.

Despite these potential therapeutic implications, our current understanding is constrained by the experimental design. A limitation of this study is that all experiments were conducted using male mice. While male mice are commonly used in BCP research due to their stable hormonal profiles and reduced data variability, sexual dimorphism is a well-established factor in pain mechanisms. In female mice, cyclic fluctuations in estrogen and progesterone significantly influence pain sensitivity, bone metabolism, and tumor progression, potentially confounding experimental outcomes. For instance, estrogen is known to modulate bone resorption and inflammatory responses, which are critical in the BCP model. Therefore, whether the astrocyte-mediated phagocytosis of GABAergic synapses observed in this study applies to female mice remains unclear. Future studies will investigate this mechanism in female cohorts to assess its generalizability and explore potential sex-specific differences in BCP pathophysiology.

Conclusions

Overall, SDH astrocytes in mice with bone cancer pain were reactive and could preferentially phagocytose GABAergic synapses. NRCAM, located at astrocyte-neuron junctions, could regulate phagocytic machinery, disrupting synaptic balance in the CNS and initiating pain-like behaviors. Last but not least, targeting astrocytic NRCAM is considered as a potential therapeutic strategy for alleviating cancer-induced bone pain.

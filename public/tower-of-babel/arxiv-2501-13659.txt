Reduced digital nets
V. Anupindi, P. Kritzer

Abstract
In the recent papers “The fast reduced QMC matrix-vector product” (J. Comput. Appl. Math. 440, 115642, 2024) and “Column reduced digital nets” (submitted), it was proposed to use QMC rules based on reduced digital nets which provide a speed-up in the computation of QMC vector-matrix products that may occur in practical applications. In this paper, we provide upper bounds on the quality parameter of row reduced and column-row reduced digital nets, which are helpful for the error analysis of using reduced point sets as integration nodes in a QMC rule. We also give remarks on further aspects and comparisons of row reduced, column reduced, and column-row reduced digital net.



1
Introduction

1.1
The problem setting

In various fields like statistics, finance, and uncertainty quantification, one needs to numerically compute integrals of the form

\int_{D}f({\bm{x}}^{\top}A){\mathrm{d}}\mu({\bm{x}}),

(1)

where
A
is a real
s\times\tau
matrix,
D\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
, and
\mu
is a suitable measure, by quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) rules,

Q_{N}(f):=\frac{1}{N}\,\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}f({\bm{x}}_{k}^{\top}A).

(2)

Here, the
{\bm{x}}_{k}=(x_{k}^{(1)},\ldots,x_{k}^{(s)})^{\top}
,
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
, are column vectors corresponding to the points used in the QMC rule. For the sake of simplicity, we assume that
D=[0,1]^{s}
, and choose
\mu
as the Lebesgue measure in the following.

We are interested in the cases where the vector-matrix product
{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\top}A
is a significant factor in the computation of (
2
). In special circumstances, one can modify
A
such that it allows a fast vector-matrix multiplication, see
[
6
,
8
]
. However, in this paper, we do not modify the matrix
A
, but we focus on obtaining special quadrature points
{\bm{x}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{N-1}
that speed-up the computation of the vector-matrix product
{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\top}A
for
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
. We define the
N\times s
-matrix

X=\begin{pmatrix}{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\top}\\
\vdots\\
{\bm{x}}_{N-1}^{\top}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(3)

i.e., the
k
-th row is the
k
-th point
{\bm{x}}_{k}
of the quadrature rule. Computing the vector-matrix products
{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\top}A

for
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}

is equivalent to computing the matrix-matrix product
XA
, which requires
{\mathcal{O}}(N\,s\,\tau)
operations.
In
[
4
]
, the authors showed that by using QMC point sets that are derived from (polynomial) lattice rules or Korobov’s
p
-sets, one can obtain a speed-up in the matrix-matrix multiplication
XA
by reordering
X
to be of circulant structure and using the fast Fourier transformation (FFT). In this case, the cost of evaluating
Q_{N}(f)
in (
2
) can be reduced to
{\mathcal{O}}(\tau\,N\,\log N)
operations provided that
\log N\ll s
.

In this paper, we take a different route and focus on quadrature points obtained from
reduced digital nets.
Digital nets, as we know, are efficient and among the most commonly used QMC node sets. To obtain
reduced
digital nets, we set a certain number of rows, columns, or both rows and columns of the generating matrices
C_{j}^{(m)}
to zero. The main motivation for doing this is to obtain a certain repetitive structure in the components of the points
{\bm{x}}_{k}
which can be used to obtain fast computation of the products
{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\top}A
.

The idea of using reduced digital nets was proposed in
[
2
]
, where the authors looked at
row reduced
digital nets, that is, digital nets where one sets certain rows of the generating matrices to zero. In
[
1
]
, we extended the study to column reduced digital nets.

While studying such reduced digital nets, we have four main aspects to consider. The first two pertain to the theoretical aspects, particularly the quality parameter (the so-called
t
-value) of the reduced net and the error estimate for using reduced nets in quadrature rules. The practical aspects include a fast algorithm for computing the matrix-matrix product using points from a reduced net and implementing the algorithm to obtain some numerical results on the computation time.

In
[
2
]
, the authors provided a fast matrix-matrix product algorithm using row reduced digital nets and also provided error estimates assuming that the
t
-value of the row reduced digital net is known. However, an estimate for the
t
-value of the row reduced digital net was still missing in
[
2
]
. In
[
1
]
, the authors of the present paper focused on column reduced digital nets, where we gave an estimate for the
t
-value, an error estimate using this bound, and also provided an algorithm for matrix-matrix multiplication using column reduced digital nets. Furthermore, with numerical experiments, we compared the computation time of row reduced with column reduced digital nets and the standard matrix-matrix multiplication.

In this paper, we will revisit the row reduced digital nets as proposed in
[
2
]
. We estimate the quality parameter of these nets and provide an error bound using our result in
Corollary

1
below. For an algorithm to compute the matrix-matrix product using row reduced digital nets, we refer to
[
2
, Algorithm 4]
. We also comment on a combination of column and row reduced digital nets.

In the following, we use
{\mathbb{N}}
to denote the set of natural numbers starting from
1
and
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
to denote the set of whole numbers, which are all the non-negative integers, i.e.,
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
.

1.2
Digital nets and sequences

In this section, we recall the definitions and establish notation for digital nets and sequences.

Given a prime
b
, let
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
be a finite field with
b
elements, where the elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
can be identified with the set
\{0,1,\dots,b-1\}
. An
elementary interval
in base
b
and dimension
s
is a half-open interval of the form
\prod_{j=1}^{s}[a_{j}b^{-d_{j}},(a_{j}+1)b^{-d_{j}})
, where the
a_{j},d_{j}
are non-negative integers with
0\leq a_{j}<b^{d_{j}}
for
1\leq j\leq s
.

Definition 1
.

For a given dimension
s\geq 1
and non-negative integers
t,m
with
0\leq t\leq m
, a
(t,m,s)
-net
in base
b
is a point set
\mathcal{P}\subset[0,1)^{s}
consisting of
b^{m}
points such that any elementary interval in base
b
with volume
b^{t-m}
contains exactly
b^{t}
points of
\mathcal{P}
.

A sequence
({\bm{x}}_{0},{\bm{x}}_{1},\dots)
of points in
[0,1)^{s}
is called a
(t,s)
-sequence
in base
b
if, for all integers
m\geq t
and
k\geq 0
, the point set consisting of the points
{\bm{x}}_{kb^{m}},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{kb^{m}+b^{m}-1}
forms a
(t,m,s)
-net in base
b
.

For a
(t,m,s)
-net or a
(t,s)
-sequence, the parameter
t
, also known as the
quality parameter
of a net or sequence, is crucial since it is an indicator of how uniformly distributed the points are. In particular, a low
t
-value is desirable since it implies better equidistribution properties.

A
(t,m,s)
-net is called
strict
, if it does not fulfill the requirements of a
(t-1,m,s)
-net (for
t\geq 1
), and analogously for
(t,s)
-sequences. In general, any
(t,m,s)
-net is also a
(t+1,m,s)
-net for
t<m
.

To construct concrete examples of good nets in arbitrary dimension, one frequently uses the
digital method
introduced by Niederreiter in
[
9
]
.

Definition 2
.

A
digital
(t,m,s)
-net over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
is a
(t,m,s)
-net
\mathcal{P}=\{{\bm{x}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{b^{m}-1}\}
where the points are constructed as follows. Let
C_{1}^{(m)},\dots,C_{s}^{(m)}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}^{m\times m}
be matrices over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
. To generate the
k
-th point,
0\leq k\leq b^{m}-1
, in
\mathcal{P}
, we use the
b
-adic expansion
k=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}k_{i}b^{i}
with digits
k_{i}\in\{0,\dots,b-1\}
which we denote by
\overrightarrow{k}=(k_{0},\dots,k_{m-1})^{\top}
. The
j
-th coordinate
x_{k,j}
of
{\bm{x}}_{k}=(x_{k,1},\dots,x_{k,s})
is obtained by computing

\overrightarrow{x}_{k,j}:=C_{j}^{(m)}\,\overrightarrow{k},

over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
and then setting

x_{k,j}:=\overrightarrow{x}_{k,j}\cdot(b^{-1},b^{-2},\ldots,b^{-m}).

Similarly, a
digital

(t,s)
-sequence
\mathcal{S}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}

is generated by infinite matrices
C_{1},\dots,C_{s}
, where

C_{j}=(c_{i,r}^{(j)})_{i,r\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{b}^{{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}}.

(4)

To generate the
k
-th point,
k\geq 0
, in
\mathcal{S}
, we use the
b
-adic expansion
k=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}k_{i}b^{i}
with digits
k_{i}\in\{0,\dots,b-1\}
which we denote by
\overrightarrow{k}=(k_{0},k_{1},\dots)^{\top}
. The

j
-th coordinate
x_{k,j}
of
{\bm{x}}_{k}=(x_{k,1},\dots,x_{k,s})
is obtained by computing

\overrightarrow{x}_{k,j}:=C_{j}^{(m)}\,\overrightarrow{k},

over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
and then setting

x_{k,j}:=\overrightarrow{x}_{k,j}\cdot(b^{-1},b^{-2},\ldots).

The quality parameter of a digital
(t,m,s)
-net or a
(t,s)
-sequence solely depends on its generating matrices, in particular, it is linked to certain linear independence properties of the rows of the generating matrices.

Definition 3
.

For any integers
1\leq j\leq s
and
m\geq 1
, let

C_{1}^{(m)},C_{2}^{(m)},\ldots,C_{s}^{(m)}
be
m\times m
matrices over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
. Let
{\bm{c}}_{i}^{(j)}
denote the
i
-th row of the
j
-th matrix.
Then the
linear independence parameter

\rho_{m}(C_{1}^{(m)},C_{2}^{(m)},\ldots,C_{s}^{(m)})
is defined as the largest integer
d
such that for any choice of
d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, with
d_{1}+\dots+d_{s}=d
, we have that the system of row vectors

\{{\bm{c}}_{i}^{(j)}:1\leq i\leq d_{j},1\leq j\leq s\}

is linearly independent over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
.

It is known (see, e.g.,
[
10
,
5
]
) that the generating matrices
C_{1}^{(m)},C_{2}^{(m)},\ldots,C_{s}^{(m)}
of a digital
(t,m,s)
-net over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
satisfy

\rho_{m}(C_{1}^{(m)},C_{2}^{(m)},\ldots,C_{s}^{(m)})\geq m-t,

(5)

where we have equality if the net is a strict
(t,m,s)
-net. Similarly, for the generating matrices
C_{1},\ldots,C_{s}
of a digital
(t,s)
-sequence over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
we must have

\rho_{m}(C_{1}^{(m)},\dots,C_{s}^{(m)})\geq m-t
for all
m\geq\max\{t,1\}
, where

C_{j}^{(m)}
denotes the left upper
m\times m
submatrix of
C_{j}
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
.
Hence, for digital nets and sequences, their quality can be assessed by checking linear independence conditions on the rows of the generating matrices.

Note that from any digital
(t,s)
-sequence over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
with generating matrices
C_{1},\ldots,C_{s}
, we can, for
m\geq t
, derive a digital
(t,m,s)
-net over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
, simply by considering the point set generated by the left upper
m\times m
submatrices
C_{1}^{(m)},\ldots,C_{s}^{(m)}
of
C_{1},\ldots,C_{s}
. This is equivalent to considering the first
b^{m}
points of the
(t,s)
-sequence.

For more details on digital nets and sequences, we refer to
[
5
,
11
]
.

2
Row reduced digital nets

In
[
2
]
, the authors introduced row reduced digital nets to obtain a speed-up in the matrix-matrix multiplication described in the introduction. They provide an algorithm which exploits a certain structure in the generation of row reduced digital nets and they also give error bounds. However, obtaining estimates for the quality parameter of row reduced digital nets is left as an open problem in
[
2
]
.

In this section we provide estimates for the quality parameter of row reduced digital nets and we also use these estimates for error analysis.

Let us consider a digital
(t,m,s)
-net generated by the matrices
C_{1}^{(m)},\dots,C_{s}^{(m)}
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{s}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
0=w_{1}\leq\cdots\leq w_{s}
. We call these numbers the
reduction indices
for the
generating matrices
C_{j}^{(m)}=(c_{i,r}^{(j)})
,
i,r\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. We derive the corresponding row reduced matrices
\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\ldots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}
, with
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}=(\widetilde{c}_{i,r}^{(j)})
,
i,r\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, for
1\leq j\leq s
,
where

\widetilde{c}_{i,r}^{(j)}=\begin{cases}c_{i,r}^{(j)}\quad&\text{ if }i\in\{1,\dots,m-\min{(m,w_{j})}\},\\
0&\text{ if }i\in\{m-\min{(m,w_{j})}+1,\dots,m\}.\end{cases}

(6)

That is, the first
m-\min{(m,w_{j})}
rows of
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
are the same as the rows of the matrix
C_{j}^{(m)}
, and we set the last
\min{(m,w_{j})}
rows to zero, i.e, if
w_{j}<m
,

\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1,1}^{(j)}&c_{1,2}^{(j)}&\dots&c_{1,m}^{(j)}&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\\
c_{(m-w_{j}),1}^{(j)}&c_{(m-w_{j}),2}^{(j)}&\dots&c_{(m-w_{j}),m}^{(j)}&\\
0&0&\dots&0&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\\
0&0&\dots&0&\\
\end{pmatrix}.

We are interested in estimating the quality parameter of the digital net generated by the
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
, and state the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a digital
(t,m,s)
-net over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
with generating matrices
C_{1}^{(m)},\dots,C_{s}^{(m)}
.
Let
\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\ldots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}
be row reduced with respect to reduction indices
0=w_{1}\leq\dots\leq w_{s}
, and let
\widetilde{t}

be the minimal quality parameter of the net generated by the
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
. Then,

\max\{0,m-\max\{t,w_{s}\}\}\leq\rho_{m}\left(\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}\right)\leq\max\{0,m-w_{s}\},

(7)

and
\widetilde{t}\leq\min\{m,\max\{t,w_{s}\}\}
.

We have a similar result for column reduced digital nets in
[
1
, Theorem 1]
, where we additionally require the
(t,m,s)
-net to be derived from a
(t,s)
-sequence. The main difference is that for the column reduced digital nets, we have
\widetilde{t}\leq\min\{m,t+w_{s}\}
.

Proof.

If
w_{s}\geq m
, then we trivially have

\rho_{m}\left(\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}\right)=0
, as
\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}
only contains zeros, and (
7
) holds.

Therefore, we will assume for the rest of the proof that
w_{s}<m
.
If
w_{s}\leq t
, then

\rho_{m}\left(\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}\right)=\rho_{m}\left(C_{1}^{(m)},\dots,C_{s}^{(m)}\right)\geq m-t,

since we only consider the linear independence of at most the first
m-t
rows of any matrix
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
.

For the lower bound when
w_{s}>t
, consider arbitrary integers
d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\geq 0
with
d_{1}+\dots+d_{s}=m-w_{s}
. Then, the collection of the first
d_{j}
rows of
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
,
1\leq j\leq s
, which is the same as the collection of the first
d_{j}
rows of
C_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq s
, is linearly independent. That is,

{\bm{c}}_{1}^{(1)},\dots,{\bm{c}}_{d_{1}}^{(1)},{\bm{c}}_{1}^{(2)},\dots,{\bm{c}}_{d_{2}}^{(2)},\dots,\dots,{\bm{c}}_{1}^{(s)},\dots,{\bm{c}}_{d_{s}}^{(s)}

are linearly independent. Therefore,

\rho_{m}\left(\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}\right)\geq m-w_{s}.

This concludes the proof of the lower bound. Regarding the upper bound, we use that
w_{s}<m
, and looking at the rank of
\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}
, we obtain that

\rho_{m}\left(\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}\right)\leq m-w_{s}.

Using (
5
) and the lower bound in (
7
), we obtain the upper bound
\widetilde{t}\leq\min\{m,\max\{t,w_{s}\}\}
for the minimal quality parameter of the row reduced net.
∎

2.1
The advantage of using reduced digital nets

We briefly recapitulate the advantage we gain by using row or column reduced digital nets. Let
N=b^{m}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}=\{{\bm{x}}_{0},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{N-1}\}
be the row or column reduced digital net generated by row or column reduced matrices
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
. Let

X=\begin{pmatrix}{\bm{x}}_{0}^{\top}\\
\vdots\\
{\bm{x}}_{N-1}^{\top}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times s}.

Let
{\bm{\xi}}_{j}
denote the
j
-th column of
X
, i.e.,
X=[{\bm{\xi}}_{1},{\bm{\xi}}_{2},\ldots,{\bm{\xi}}_{s}]
. Let
A=[{\bm{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{a}}_{s}]^{\top}
, where

{\bm{a}}_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1\times\tau}
is the
j
-th row of
A
. Since
{\bm{\xi}}_{j}
is computed using the
j
-th reduced generating matrix
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
, we can use the repetitiveness that occurs in
{\bm{\xi}}_{j}
due to the zero rows or zero columns of
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
. Therefore, to make use of the repetitiveness in
{\bm{\xi}}_{j}
, we decompose the product
XA
as a sum of matrices using the column row expansion as follows,

XA=[{\bm{\xi}}_{1},{\bm{\xi}}_{2},\ldots,{\bm{\xi}}_{s}]\cdot[{\bm{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{a}}_{s}]^{\top}={\bm{\xi}}_{1}{\bm{a}}_{1}+{\bm{\xi}}_{2}{\bm{a}}_{2}+\cdots+{\bm{\xi}}_{s}{\bm{a}}_{s}.

(8)

In the following remarks, we briefly outline the repetitive pattern in each row reduced and column reduced point set and the computational advantage we gain by using
[
2
, Algorithm 4]
and
[
1
, Algorithm 1]
, respectively.

Remark 1

(Row reduced nets)
.

Let
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
be the row reduced generating matrix with the last
w_{j}
rows set to zero, for
j=1,\dots,s
. Let
s^{*}\leq s
be the largest index such that
w_{s^{*}}<m
; we observe that, for
j>s^{*}
,
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
is the zero matrix. The
j
-th coordinate
x_{k,j}
of
{\bm{x}}_{k}\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}
is obtained by computing

x_{k,j}=(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}\overrightarrow{k})\cdot(b^{-1},b^{-2},\ldots,b^{-m}),\quad k\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}.

Since the last
w_{j}
rows of
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}\overrightarrow{k}
are zero rows, we observe that all the values of
x_{k,j}

for
k\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
are in the set

\{0,1/b^{m-\min(w_{j},m)},2/b^{m-\min(w_{j},m)},\dots,1-1/b^{m-\min(w_{j},m)}\}.

(9)

Algorithm 4 in
[
2
]
uses the observation in (
9
) to precompute
the following row vectors for
j=1,\dots,s
, namely

{\bm{c}}_{k}:=\frac{k}{b^{m-w_{j}}}{\bm{a}}_{j},\quad k=0,1,\dots,b^{m-w_{j}}-1.

Then we can compute

{\bm{\xi}}_{j}{\bm{a}}_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{0,j}\\
\vdots\\
x_{N-1,j}\\
\end{pmatrix}{\bm{a}}_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}{\bm{c}}_{\lfloor x_{0,j}b^{m-w_{j}}\rfloor}\\
\vdots\\
{\bm{c}}_{\lfloor x_{N-1,j}b^{m-w_{j}}\rfloor}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times\tau},

where we can use the precomputed values.

By this principle, the computational cost for generating points of a row reduced net can be lowered to

{\mathcal{O}}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{s^{*}}b^{m}m(m-w_{j})\big)

as compared to
{\mathcal{O}}(sb^{m}m^{2})
for a non-reduced net. Furthermore, the cost of the matrix-matrix multiplication can be reduced from
{\mathcal{O}}(sb^{m}\tau)
for the standard non-reduced case to
{\mathcal{O}}(\sum_{j=1}^{s^{*}}b^{m-w_{j}}\tau)
for the row reduced case using
[
2
, Algorithm 4]
.

Remark 2

(Column reduced nets)
.

Let
C_{1}^{(m)},\dots,C_{s}^{(m)}
be generating matrices of a
(t,m,s)
-net derived from a digital
(t,s)
-sequence over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
and let
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
be the corresponding column reduced generating matrix with the last
w_{j}
columns set to zero, for
j=1,\dots,s
. Let
s^{*}\leq s
be the largest index such that
w_{s^{*}}<m
; we observe that, for
j>s^{*}
,
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
is the zero matrix. As stated before, the
j
-th coordinate
x_{k,j}
of
{\bm{x}}_{k}\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}

is given by

x_{k,j}=(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}\overrightarrow{k})\cdot(b^{-1},b^{-2},\ldots,b^{-m}),\quad k=0,\dots,N-1.

Since the last
w_{j}
columns of
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
are zeros, the last
w_{j}
digits of the b-adic expansion of
k
given by
\overrightarrow{k}
are insignificant. Thus we observe that the
j
-th column vector of
X
is given by

{\bm{\xi}}_{j}=(\underbrace{X_{j},\ldots,X_{j}}_{b^{w_{j}}\ \rm times})^{\top},

where

X_{j}=\left(\left(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}\overrightarrow{0}\right)\cdot(b^{-1},\ldots,b^{-m}),\ldots,\left(\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}\overrightarrow{(b^{m-w_{j}}-1)}\right)\cdot(b^{-1},\ldots,b^{-m})\right)^{\top}.

Thus, to compute
XA
, we first compute
X_{j}{\bm{a}}_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{b^{m-w_{j}\times\tau}}
instead of
{\bm{\xi}}_{j}{\bm{a}}_{j}
, and we duplicate and enlarge it to match the dimension of
X_{j-1}{\bm{a}}_{j-1}
and add this recursively to compute
XA
. For more details, see
[
1
, Algorithm 1]
.

Using column reduced digital nets, we can obtain a speed-up in both the point generation step and the matrix-matrix multiplication. In
[
1
, Algorithm 1]
, the point generation step requires
{\mathcal{O}}(\sum_{j=1}^{s^{*}}b^{m-w_{j}}m(m-w_{j}))
operations and the matrix-matrix multiplication requires
{\mathcal{O}}(\sum_{j=1}^{s^{*}}b^{m-w_{j}}\tau)
. Since the
j
-th column vector
{\bm{\xi}}_{j}
has a repetitive structure, we obtain a further reduction in the number of additions in (
8
), which we do not have in the row reduced case. However, we only use nets derived from
(t,s)
-sequences for the column reduced digital nets, in order to still be able to give bounds on the quality parameter
\widetilde{t}
of the reduced net. We do not need to impose this restriction for row reduced digital nets.

2.2
Projections of digital nets

The quality parameter
t
determines the equidistribution properties of a
(t,m,s)
-net and hence also influences the error of a QMC rule that uses the points of the net. Due to the importance of the quality parameter, we also consider a finer measure, which is to look at the quality parameters of the projections of a
(t,m,s)
-net.

Let
[s]:=\{1,\ldots,s\}
. For any
{\mathfrak{u}}\neq\emptyset
,
{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]
, the projection of a
(t,m,s)
-net onto those components with indices in
{\mathfrak{u}}
can be considered as a
(t_{\mathfrak{u}},m,\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|)
-net for a suitable choice of
t_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Thus, we can view a
(t,m,s)
-net also as a
((t_{\mathfrak{u}})_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]},m,s)
-net.

The notion of a
((t_{\mathfrak{u}})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]},s)
-sequence is defined analogously. Moreover, for
{\mathfrak{u}}\neq\emptyset
, we write

\overline{{\mathfrak{u}}}:=\max({\mathfrak{u}})
.
We have the following result for projections of row reduced nets.

Corollary 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a digital
((t_{\mathfrak{u}})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]},m,s)
-net over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
with generating matrices
C_{1}^{(m)},\dots,C_{s}^{(m)}
,
where we assume that
m\geq t
.
Let
\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\ldots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}
be the row reduced generating matrices with respect to reduction indices
0=w_{1}\leq\dots\leq w_{s}
and let
(\widetilde{t}_{\mathfrak{u}})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]}

be the minimal quality parameters of the projections of the net generated by the
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
. Then, for every non-empty
{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]
,

\max\{0,m-\max\{w_{\overline{{\mathfrak{u}}}},t_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\}\}\leq\rho_{m}((\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)})_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}})\leq\max\{0,m-w_{\overline{{\mathfrak{u}}}}\},

and
\widetilde{t}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\leq\min\{m,\max\{w_{\overline{{\mathfrak{u}}}},t_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\}\}
.

We have a corresponding result in
[
1
, Corollary 1]
for column reduced digital nets derived from
(t,s)
-sequences, where the quality parameter of the column reduced net is given by
\widetilde{t}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\leq\min\{m,w_{\overline{{\mathfrak{u}}}}+t_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\}
.

2.3
Error analysis

The main motivation for this paper is to approximate integral (
1
) using QMC rule (
2
). While we gain an advantage in the computational cost by using row-reduced digital nets as opposed to non-reduced nets, there is a certain loss in the quality parameter of reduced nets as formalized in
Theorem

1
.

In this section, we focus on the integration error obtained by using a QMC rule. The error analysis here is similar to the one in
[
1
]
, where we gave an upper bound on the quality parameter of column reduced nets. In the present paper, we have given a corresponding result for row reduced digital nets in
Theorem

1
above. There is another error estimate using row reduced digital nets given in
[
2
, Theorem 4]
, but there the authors do not have an estimate for the quality parameter of the reduced net, and therefore use a different method to obtain an error bound. Here, we use the result in
Corollary

1
for the error analysis.

We start by considering
the
weighted star discrepancy
, which is (via the well-known
Koksma-Hlawka inequality
or its weighted version, see, e.g.,
[
3
,
5
,
10
]
), a measure of the worst-case quadrature error for a QMC rule with node set
Q
, with
b^{m}
nodes, defined as

D_{b^{m},{\bm{\gamma}}}^{*}(Q):=\sup_{x\in(0,1]^{s}}\max_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\left|\Delta_{Q,{\mathfrak{u}}}({\bm{x}})\right|,

(10)

where

\Delta_{Q,{\mathfrak{u}}}({\bm{x}}):=\frac{\#\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})\in Q\colon y_{j}<x_{j},\,\forall j\in{\mathfrak{u}}\}}{b^{m}}-\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}x_{j}.

(11)

Here, the
weights

\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}
are non-negative real numbers, which model the influence of the variables with indices in
{\mathfrak{u}}
. The concept of weights was first introduced in the seminal paper
[
13
]
by Sloan and Woźniakowski, and is nowadays a standard way of modeling high-dimensional problems in the context of QMC methods. Here, we assume that the weights are so-called (positive)
product weights
. That is, we assume a non-increasing sequence
(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
of positive reals, and put

\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}:=\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\gamma_{j}
, where
\gamma_{\emptyset}:=1
.
We refer to
[
5
]
for further details regarding weights in the context of
(t,m,s)
-nets.

Using Lemma 1 in
[
12
]
, we have,

\displaystyle D_{b^{m},{\bm{\gamma}}}^{*}(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]}\sup_{{\bm{x}}\in(0,1]^{s}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\left|\Delta_{Q,{\mathfrak{u}}}({\bm{x}})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\sup_{{\bm{x}}\in(0,1]^{s}}\left|\Delta_{Q,{\mathfrak{u}}}({\bm{x}})\right|.

In
[
7
]
, the authors provide discrepancy bounds for projections of
(t,m,s)
-nets. Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a digital
((t_{\mathfrak{u}})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]},m,s)
-net over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
with
m\times m
generating matrices
C_{1}^{(m)},\dots,C_{s}^{(m)}
,
where
m\geq t
. Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}
be the corresponding row reduced digital net based on the reduction indices
0=w_{1}\leq w_{2}\leq\cdots\leq w_{s}
,
and let
(\widetilde{t}_{\mathfrak{u}})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]}
be the minimal quality parameters of the projections of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}}
. Using a result from
[
7
]
, which
gives a relation between the discrepancy and the quality paramater of
(t,m,s)
-nets, we obtain, analogously to what is done in
[
1
, Section 4]
,

D_{b^{m},{\bm{\gamma}}}^{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}})\leq\max\left\{\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]\\
{\mathfrak{u}}\not\subseteq[s^{*}]\end{subarray}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}},\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s^{*}]\\
\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|=1\end{subarray}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\,\frac{b^{\widetilde{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}}}{b^{m}},\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s^{*}]\\
\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\,\frac{b^{\widetilde{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}}}{b^{m}}\sum_{v=0}^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|-1}a_{v,b}^{(\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|)}m^{v}\right\},

(12)

where

\displaystyle a_{v,b}^{(\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|-2\choose v}\left(\frac{b+2}{2}\right)^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|-2-v}\frac{(b-1)^{v}}{2^{v}v!}(a_{0,b}^{(2)}+\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|^{2}-4)

\displaystyle+{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|-2\choose v-1}\left(\frac{b+2}{2}\right)^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|-1-v}\frac{(b-1)^{v-1}}{2^{v-1}v!}a_{1,b}^{(2)},

for
0\leq v\leq\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|-1
,
with

a_{0,b}^{(2)}=\begin{cases}\frac{b+8}{4}&\mbox{if $b$ is even,}\\
\\
\frac{b+4}{2}&\mbox{if $b$ is odd,}\end{cases}\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{and}\hskip 28.45274pta_{1,b}^{(2)}=\begin{cases}\frac{b^{2}}{4(b+1)}&\mbox{if $b$ is even,}\\
\\
\frac{b-1}{4}&\mbox{if $b$ is odd.}\end{cases}

To analyze the first term in (
12
), we proceed as in
[
2
]
, that is, we use that
w_{j}\geq m
if
j\in{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus[s^{\ast}]
, and obtain for
{\mathfrak{v}}={\mathfrak{u}}\cap[s^{\ast}]
that

\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\leq\gamma_{{\mathfrak{v}}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus{\mathfrak{v}}}\frac{1}{b^{m}}\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus{\mathfrak{v}}}(1+b^{w_{j}})\leq\frac{1}{b^{m}}\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\gamma_{j}(1+b^{w_{j}}).

(13)

For the second term in (
12
), note that we have

{\mathfrak{u}}=\{j\}
for some
j\in[s^{*}]
, and hence using
Corollary

1
, we have

\widetilde{t}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\leq\min\{m,\max\{w_{j},t_{\{j\}}\}\}
. Consequently,

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s^{*}]\\
\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|=1\end{subarray}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\frac{b^{\widetilde{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}}}{b^{m}}\leq\max_{j\in[s^{*}]}\gamma_{j}\frac{b^{\min\{m,\max\{w_{j},t_{\{j\}}\}\}}}{b^{m}}.

(14)

For the third term in (
12
), we again use
Corollary

1
, and obtain

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s^{*}]\\
\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\,\frac{b^{\widetilde{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}}}{b^{m}}\sum_{v=0}^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|-1}a_{v,b}^{(\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|)}m^{v}\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s^{*}]\\
\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\,\frac{b^{\min\{m,\max\{w_{\overline{{\mathfrak{u}}}},t_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\}\}}}{b^{m}}\sum_{v=0}^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|-1}a_{v,b}^{(\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|)}m^{v}.

(15)

Using these estimates in (
12
), we obtain

\displaystyle D_{b^{m},{\bm{\gamma}}}^{*}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{P}}})\leq\max\Biggl\{\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]\\
{\mathfrak{u}}\not\subseteq[s^{*}]\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{b^{m}}\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\gamma_{j}(1+b^{w_{j}}),\max_{j\in[s^{*}]}\gamma_{j}\frac{b^{\min\{m,\max\{w_{j},t_{\{j\}}\}\}}}{b^{m}}

\displaystyle,

(16)

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s^{*}]\\
\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\,\frac{b^{\min\{m,\max\{w_{\overline{{\mathfrak{u}}}},t_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\}\}}}{b^{m}}\sum_{v=0}^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|-1}a_{v,b}^{(\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|)}m^{v}\Biggr\}.

The above estimate is similar to the error bound in
[
1
]
for column reduced digital nets, since the estimate for the quality parameter of row reduced digital nets as given in
Corollary

1
, is similar to the corresponding estimate for column reduced digital nets. Analogously to
[
1
, Remark 5]
, the first two terms of (
16
) can be made independent of the dimension
s
by choosing reduction indices
w_{j}
that increase sufficiently fast. The third term depends on the interplay between the weights
\gamma
and the quality parameters
t_{{\mathfrak{u}}}
of the projections of
{\mathcal{P}}
, where for suitable choice of reduction indices
w_{j}
, small quality parameters for the projections and sufficiently fast decaying weights, we can obtain tighter error bounds. For more details, we refer to
[
1
, Remark 5]
.

As mentioned before, in
[
2
]
, the authors also provide error bounds using row reduced digital nets where they assume that the quality parameter of the row reduced net
\widetilde{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}
is known, which may frequently not be the case in practice. However, since we can estimate
\widetilde{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}
using
Corollary

1
, our contribution is to provide explicit error bounds using the quality parameter
t_{\mathfrak{u}}
of the original net and the reduction indices
w_{j}
.

3
Combination of column and row reduced digital nets

In
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
, the authors studied column reduced digital nets and row reduced digital nets, respectively. It is a natural question to ask what happens when we combine the two and look at column-row reduced digital nets. That is, for each generating matrix
C_{j}^{(m)}
,
1\leq j\leq s
, we set the last
w_{j}
columns and the last
w_{j}
rows to zero. Since we set some columns to zero, we impose the condition that the generating matrices for the
(t,m,s)
-nets have to be derived from
(t,s)
-sequences, as it was also done in
[
1
]
.

Let
0=w_{1}\leq\cdots\leq w_{s}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
be the reduction indices for the generating matrices
C_{1}^{(m)},\ldots,C_{s}^{(m)}
, where the
C_{j}^{(m)}=(c_{i,r}^{(j)})
,
i,r\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, are derived from a digital
(t,s)
-sequence over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
. We derive the corresponding column-row reduced matrices
\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\ldots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}
, with
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}=(\widetilde{c}_{i,r}^{(j)})
,
i,r\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, for
1\leq j\leq s
,
where

\widetilde{c}_{i,r}^{(j)}=\begin{cases}c_{i,r}^{(j)}\quad&\text{ if }i\mbox{ and }r\in\{1,\dots,m-\min{(m,w_{j})}\},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(17)

We observe that
[
1
, Theorem 1]
also holds for column-row reduced digital nets. Since the linear independence parameter
\rho_{m}(\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)})
depends on systems of row vectors of the
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
being linearly independent (see
Definition

2
), this measure is more sensitive to setting the last few columns to zero as compared to setting the last few rows to zero. In particular, since the generating matrices are derived from a
(t,s)
-sequence, we have that for the left upper
(m-w_{s})\times(m-w_{s})
submatrices of
C_{1},\dots,C_{s}
, denoted by
C_{j}^{(m-w_{s})}
,

\rho_{(m-w_{s})}(C_{1}^{(m-w_{s})},\dots,C_{s}^{(m-w_{s})})\geq m-w_{s}-t.

This lower bound still holds for column-row reduced nets with the same argument as presented in
[
1
, Theorem 1]
.

We recall
[
1
, Theorem 1]
here with the statement modified for column-row reduced nets.

Lemma 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a digital
(t,m,s)
-net over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
with generating matrices

C_{1}^{(m)},\dots,C_{s}^{(m)}
derived from a digital
(t,s)
-sequence over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
,
where we assume that
m\geq t
.
Let
\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\ldots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}
be as defined in (
17
) with respect to reduction indices
0=w_{1}\leq\dots\leq w_{s}
and let
\widetilde{t}

be the minimal quality parameter of the net generated by the
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
. Then,

\max\{0,m-w_{s}-t\}\leq\rho_{m}\left(\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}\right)\leq\max\{0,m-w_{s}\},

(18)

and
\widetilde{t}\leq\min\{m,w_{s}+t\}
.

Furthermore, if
{\mathcal{P}}
is a strict digital
(t,m,s)
-net, it is true that

\rho_{m}\left(\widetilde{C}_{1}^{(m)},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{s}^{(m)}\right)\leq\max\{0,m-\max\{t,w_{s}\}\}.

(19)

Remark 3
.

We can modify
[
1
, Algorithm 1]
to obtain a slightly faster algorithm for matrix-matrix multiplication for column-row reduced digital nets. In particular, we can obtain a slight speed-up in the computation of the points of the reduced digital nets, where we can resize
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m)}
to
\widetilde{C}_{j}^{(m-w_{j})}
. That is, the theoretical runtime of the column-row reduced algorithm is

{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s^{*}}b^{m-w_{j}}(\tau+(m-w_{j})^{2})\right).

In comparison to the column reduced matrix-matrix product algorithm, we see that there is an improvement in the point generation step.

Remark 4
.

So far, for column-row reduced digital nets, we always set an equal number of rows and columns to zero for each generating matrix
C_{j}^{(m)},1\leq j\leq s
, where the
C_{j}^{(m)}
are derived from a digital
(t,s)
-sequence over
{\mathbb{F}}_{b}
. Theoretically, we could also consider the scenario where we set a different number of rows and columns to zero. In this case, we can obtain the following generalization of
[
1
, Theorem 1]
and
Theorem

1
. Let
0=w_{1}^{r}\leq\dots\leq w_{s}^{r}
be the reduction indices for the rows and
0=w_{1}^{c}\leq\dots\leq w_{s}^{c}
be the reduction indices for the columns of
C_{1}^{(m)},\dots,C_{s}^{(m)}
. Let

\widetilde{t}

\max\{0,m-\max\{w_{s}^{c}+t,w_{s}^{r}\}\}\leq\rho_{m}(\widetilde{C}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{s})\leq\max\{0,m-\max\{w_{s}^{c},w_{s}^{r}\}\},

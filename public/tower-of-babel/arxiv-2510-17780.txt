A sharp estimate for fill-ins with scalar curvature bounded from below
Simon Brendle, Raphael Tsiamis, Yipeng Wang

Abstract
. We prove a rigidity result for the infimum of the boundary mean curvature in spin fill-ins with scalar curvature bounded below by − n ⁡ ( n − 1 ) -n(n-1) , which attains equality precisely for fill-ins isometric to geodesic balls in hyperbolic space. This resolves a conjecture of Gromov for spin manifolds.



1.
Introduction

Fill-ins with scalar curvature bounded from below play a major role in geometry, allowing us to localize results for non-compact manifolds, such as the positive mass theorem.
In what follows, we let
\Sigma
be a closed connected orientable Riemannian manifold.
We study obstructions, depending only on
\Sigma
, for the mean curvature of
\Sigma
when filled in by a Riemannian manifold
M
with scalar curvature bounded from below.
This problem was highlighted by Gromov
[
10
]
*Section
5\frac{5}{7}
. Gromov and Lawson
[
9
]
introduced the following notion in their proof of Geroch’s conjecture on the non-existence of positive scalar curvature metrics on tori.

Definition
.

The hyperspherical radius of
\Sigma
, denoted by
\operatorname{Rad}(\Sigma)
, is defined as the supremum of all
r>0
such that there exists a
1
-Lipschitz map
\Sigma\to\mathbf{S}^{\dim\Sigma}(r)
with non-trivial degree.

The hyperspherical radius is a natural geometric quantity. For a spin manifold, Llarull’s theorem
[
13
]
gives an upper bound for the infimum of the scalar curvature in terms of its hyperspherical radius.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact, connected, oriented Riemannian spin manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with connected boundary
\Sigma
.
If
R\geq-n(n-1)
holds at each point of
M
, then

\inf_{\partial M}H\leq(n-1)\sqrt{1+{\operatorname{Rad}(\Sigma)^{-2}}}.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to a geodesic ball of radius
\rho
in
\mathbf{H}^{n}
, with
\sinh\rho=\operatorname{Rad}(\Sigma)
.

Theorem
1
can be viewed as a rigidity result for fill-ins of spheres with scalar curvature bounded from below, which, upon rescaling, recovers an estimate of Gromov (see
[
11
]
*p. 225 and
[
6
]
). It is closely related to an estimate of Hijazi–Montiel–Zhang
[
12
]
for the first Dirac eigenvalue (cf.
[
4
]
*Theorem 9 and
[
6
]
). We refer the reader to
[
14
,
1
,
15
,
8
,
7
]
for other important rigidity results for asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds. Finally, Theorem
1
is similar in spirit to the systolic inequality proved in
[
5
]
. The latter inequality relates the infimum of the mean curvature to the systole of the boundary; it is asymptotically sharp for large domains in the Horowitz-Myers manifold.

A key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1
is a sharp estimate, due to Bär
[
4
]
, which relates the hyperspherical radius of a spin manifold to the smallest eigenvalue of the Dirac operator.

As a consequence of Theorem
1
, we prove a conjecture of Gromov
[
11
]
*p. 110 for spin manifolds.

Corollary 2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact, connected, oriented Riemannian spin manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with connected boundary
\Sigma
.
Let
B_{\rho}
be a geodesic ball of radius
\rho
in
\mathbf{H}^{n}
with respect to the standard hyperbolic metric. Suppose that:

•

R\geq-n(n-1)
at each point of
M
.

•

H_{\Sigma}\geq H_{\partial B_{\rho}}=(n-1)\coth\rho
at each point of
\Sigma
.

•

\Sigma
admits a
1
-Lipschitz map
f:\Sigma\to\partial B_{\rho}
with non-zero degree.

Then
f
extends to an isometry
M\to B_{\rho}
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

The proof of our result combines a sharp estimate for
\inf_{\partial M}H
in terms of the first eigenvalue of the boundary Dirac operator with Bär’s theorem
[
4
]
.
We first introduce some notation.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact oriented spin manifold with
n\geq 3
, and
\mathcal{S}_{M}\to M
be the spinor bundle. We denote by
\mathcal{D}^{M}
the Dirac operator acting on sections of
\mathcal{S}_{M}
; in a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
, the Dirac operator is given by
\mathcal{D}^{M}s=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}\cdot\nabla^{M}_{e_{k}}s
.
Given
s\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M})
and a smooth vector field
X
on
M
, we define

\hat{\nabla}^{M}_{X}s=\nabla^{M}_{X}s+\frac{i}{2}X\cdot s.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}
the boundary Dirac operator acting on sections of
\mathcal{S}_{M}|_{\partial M}
. Given a section
s
of
\mathcal{S}_{M}|_{\partial M}
, this operator is defined by

\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}s=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\nu\cdot e_{k}\cdot\nabla^{M}_{e_{k}}s+\frac{1}{2}H\,s,

where
H
denotes the mean curvature of the boundary and
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-1}\}
is a local orthonormal frame on
\partial M
.
It is standard that Clifford multiplication by
\nu
anticommutes with
\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}
.

The following identity is well known.

Proposition 3
.

For any
s\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M})
, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\big|\mathcal{D}^{M}s-\frac{in}{2}s\big|^{2}+\int_{M}|\hat{\nabla}^{M}s|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}R\,|s|^{2}+\frac{n(n-1)}{4}\int_{M}|s|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\partial M}\langle\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}s,s\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}H\,|s|^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\langle i\,\nu\cdot s,s\rangle

Proof.

We integrate the Weitzenböck formula and apply the divergence theorem to obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{{M}}

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{M}s|^{2}+\int_{{M}}|\nabla^{M}s|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{{M}}R\,|s|^{2}=\int_{\partial{M}}\langle\nu\cdot\mathcal{D}^{M}s,s\rangle+\int_{\partial{M}}\langle\nabla^{M}_{\nu}s,s\rangle.

Note that
\nu\cdot\mathcal{D}^{M}s+\nabla^{M}_{\nu}s=\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}s-\frac{1}{2}\,H\,s
.
We may therefore write

\displaystyle-\int_{M}|\mathcal{D}^{M}s|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{M}|\nabla^{M}s|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}R\,|s|^{2}=\int_{\partial M}\langle\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}s,s\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}H\,|s|^{2}

Using the definition of
\hat{\nabla}^{M}
, we compute

\displaystyle|\hat{\nabla}^{M}s|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big|\nabla^{M}_{e_{k}}s+\frac{i}{2}e_{k}\cdot s\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{M}s|^{2}+\frac{n}{4}\,|s|^{2}+\frac{i}{2}\,\langle\mathcal{D}^{M}s,s\rangle-\frac{i}{2}\,\langle s,\mathcal{D}^{M}s\rangle.

Integrating over
M
gives

\int_{M}|\hat{\nabla}^{M}s|^{2}-\int_{M}|\nabla^{M}s|^{2}-\frac{n}{4}\int_{M}|s|^{2}-\frac{i}{2}\int_{M}\langle\mathcal{D}^{M}s,s\rangle+\frac{i}{2}\int_{M}\langle s,\mathcal{D}^{M}s\rangle=0.

On the other hand, let us notice that

\displaystyle-\big|\mathcal{D}^{M}s-\frac{in}{2}s\big|^{2}+|\mathcal{D}^{M}s|^{2}+\frac{i}{2}\langle\mathcal{D}^{M}s,s\rangle-\frac{i}{2}\langle s,\mathcal{D}^{M}s\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=-\frac{n^{2}}{4}\,|s|^{2}+\frac{i(n-1)}{2}\,\langle s,\mathcal{D}^{M}s\rangle-\frac{i(n-1)}{2}\langle\mathcal{D}^{M}s,s\rangle.

Integrating this expression and using the divergence theorem, we conclude that

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\big|\mathcal{D}^{M}s-\frac{in}{2}s\big|^{2}+\int_{M}|\mathcal{D}^{M}s|^{2}+\frac{i}{2}\int_{M}\langle\mathcal{D}^{M}s,s\rangle-\frac{i}{2}\int_{M}\langle s,\mathcal{D}^{M}s\rangle+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\int_{M}|s|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\langle i\,\nu\cdot s,s\rangle.

The assertion follows by adding these three integral identities.
∎

Let
\Sigma
denote the boundary of
M
with the induced metric.
We denote by
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{D}
the intrinsic spinor bundle and Dirac operator of
\Sigma
, respectively.
If
n
is odd, then
\mathcal{S}
can be identified with
\mathcal{S}_{M}|_{\partial M}
, and
\mathcal{D}
can be identified with
\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}
.
If
n
is even, we decompose
\mathcal{S}_{M}=\mathcal{S}_{M}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{M}^{-}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{M}^{+}
and
\mathcal{S}_{M}^{-}
denote the eigenbundles of the volume form on
M
.
Clifford multiplication by
\nu
interchanges
\mathcal{S}^{+}_{M}|_{\partial M}
and
\mathcal{S}^{-}_{M}|_{\partial M}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{S}^{+}_{M}|_{\partial M}
and
\mathcal{S}^{-}_{M}|_{\partial M}
are invariant under the boundary Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}
.
With this understood,
\mathcal{S}
can be identified with
\mathcal{S}^{+}_{M}|_{\partial M}
, and
\mathcal{D}
can be identified with the restriction of
\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}
to
\mathcal{S}^{+}_{M}|_{\partial M}
.
Thus, the boundary Dirac operator can be written in the form

\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{D}&0\\
0&-\mathcal{D}\end{bmatrix}.

For either parity, the spectrum of
\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}
is symmetric about
0
, and we define

\lambda=\sqrt{\lambda_{\text{\rm min}}((\mathcal{D}^{\partial M})^{2})}=\sqrt{\lambda_{\text{\rm min}}(\mathcal{D}^{2})}

under these identifications.

Let
\lambda_{j}
denote the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}
. By definition of
\lambda
, we have
|\lambda_{j}|\geq\lambda
for each
j
. For each
j
, we denote by
E_{\lambda_{j}}
the eigenspace of
\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}
. Any
s\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial M,\mathcal{S}_{M}|_{\partial M})
can be expanded as
s=\sum_{j}s_{\lambda_{j}}
, where
s_{\lambda_{j}}\in E_{\lambda_{j}}
. We define
W
to be the subspace of all
s\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial M,\mathcal{S}_{M}|_{\partial M})
such that
s_{\lambda_{j}}=0
for all
\lambda_{j}\in(\lambda,\infty)
. Moreover, we define
\tilde{W}
to be the subspace of all
s\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial M,\mathcal{S}_{M}|_{\partial M})
such that
s_{\lambda_{j}}=0
for all
\lambda_{j}\in[-\lambda,\infty)
.

We consider the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}=\{s\in H^{1}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M}):s|_{\partial M}\in W\}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}=\{s\in H^{1}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M}):s|_{\partial M}\in\tilde{W}\}
. The following result was proved by Bär
[
4
]
*Appendix A, building on earlier work of Bär and Ballmann
[
2
]
.

Proposition 4

(C. Bär
[
4
]
, Appendix A)
.

The operator
\mathcal{D}^{M}:\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M})
is a Fredholm operator and its Fredholm index is given by

\text{\rm ind}(\mathcal{D}^{M}:\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M}))=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in[-\lambda,\lambda]}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}E_{\lambda_{j}}.

Proof.

We include the details of Bär’s argument for the convenience of the reader. Corollary 8.8 in
[
2
]
implies that

\displaystyle\text{\rm ind}(\mathcal{D}^{M}:\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M}))-\text{\rm ind}(\mathcal{D}^{M}:\tilde{\mathcal{H}}\to L^{2}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M}))

\displaystyle=\text{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(W/\tilde{W})=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in[-\lambda,\lambda]}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}E_{\lambda_{j}}.

On the other hand,

\text{\rm ind}(\mathcal{D}^{M}:\tilde{\mathcal{H}}\to L^{2}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M}))=-\text{\rm ind}(\mathcal{D}^{M}:\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M}))

by duality (see
[
2
]
*Section 7.2). Putting these facts together, the assertion follows.
∎

Let
\mathcal{Z}
denote the set of all sections
s\in H^{1}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M})
such that
\mathcal{D}^{M}s-\frac{in}{2}s=0
and
s|_{\partial M}\in W
. Since the Fredholm index is unchanged by compact perturbations, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{Z}

\displaystyle\geq\text{\rm ind}\Big(\mathcal{D}^{M}-\frac{in}{2}:\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M})\Big)

\displaystyle=\text{\rm ind}(\mathcal{D}^{M}:\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in[-\lambda,\lambda]}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}E_{\lambda_{j}}.

In particular,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{Z}>0
. The results in
[
2
]
*Section 7.4 imply that every section
s\in\mathcal{Z}
is smooth up to the boundary.

Proposition 5
.

Suppose that
s\in\mathcal{Z}
. Then

\int_{\partial M}H\,|s|^{2}\leq\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+4\lambda^{2}}\,\int_{\partial M}|s|^{2}.

Moreover, if equality holds, then
\hat{\nabla}^{M}s=0
at each point in
M
.

Proof.

We distinguish two cases:

Case 1:
Suppose that
\lambda=0
. Since
s|_{\partial M}\in W
, we know that

\int_{\partial M}\langle\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}s,s\rangle\leq 0.

Using Proposition
3
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial M}\langle\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}s,s\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}H\,|s|^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\langle i\,\nu\cdot s,s\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}H\,|s|^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\langle i\,\nu\cdot s,s\rangle.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we conclude that

\int_{\partial M}H\,|s|^{2}\leq(n-1)\int_{\partial M}|s|^{2}.

Moreover, if equality holds, then
\int_{M}|\hat{\nabla}^{M}s|^{2}=0
.

Case 2:
Suppose that
\lambda>0
. Since
s|_{\partial M}\in W
, we may write
s|_{\partial M}=s_{+}+s_{-}
, where
s_{+}\in E_{\lambda}
and
s_{-}\in\tilde{W}\oplus E_{-\lambda}
. This implies

\int_{\partial M}\langle\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}s,s\rangle=\int_{\partial M}\lambda\,|s_{+}|^{2}+\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in(-\infty,-\lambda]}\int_{\partial M}\lambda_{j}\,|s_{\lambda_{j}}|^{2}\leq\int_{\partial M}\lambda|s_{+}|^{2}-\int_{\partial M}\lambda|s_{-}|^{2}.

Since Clifford multiplication by
\nu
interchanges the positive and negative eigenspaces of
\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}
, we know that

\int_{\partial M}\langle\nu\cdot s_{+},s_{+}\rangle=\int_{\partial M}\langle\nu\cdot s_{-},s_{-}\rangle=0.

Using Proposition
3
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial M}\langle\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}s,s\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}H\,|s|^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\langle i\,\nu\cdot s,s\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial M}\lambda\,|s_{+}|^{2}-\int_{\partial M}\lambda\,|s_{-}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}H\,|s|^{2}-\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\langle i\,\nu\cdot s,s\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial M}\lambda\,|s_{+}|^{2}-\int_{\partial M}\lambda\,|s_{-}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}H\,|s|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\langle i\,\nu\cdot s_{+},s_{-}\rangle-\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial M}\langle i\,\nu\cdot s_{-},s_{+}\rangle.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}H\,|s|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial M}(2\lambda\,(|s_{+}|^{2}-|s_{-}|^{2})+2(n-1)\,|s_{+}|\,|s_{-}|)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial M}\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+4\lambda^{2}}\,\sqrt{(|s_{+}|^{2}-|s_{-}|^{2})^{2}+4\,|s_{+}|^{2}\,|s_{-}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+4\lambda^{2}}\,\int_{\partial M}(|s_{+}|^{2}+|s_{-}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+4\lambda^{2}}\,\int_{\partial M}|s|^{2}.

Moreover, if equality holds, then
\int_{M}|\hat{\nabla}^{M}s|^{2}=0
.
∎

Corollary 6
.

We have

\inf_{\partial M}H\leq\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+4\lambda^{2}}\;.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that

\inf_{\partial M}H>\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+4\lambda^{2}}\;.

Since
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{Z}>0
, we can find a section
s\in H^{1}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M})
such that
\mathcal{D}^{M}s-\frac{in}{2}s=0
,
s|_{\partial M}\in W
, and
\int_{M}|s|^{2}>0
.
Using Proposition
5
, we conclude that
\hat{\nabla}^{M}s=0
in
M
and
s|_{\partial M}=0
. This is a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 7
.

We have

\inf_{\partial M}H\leq(n-1)\sqrt{1+{\operatorname{Rad}(\Sigma)^{-2}}}\;.

Proof.

This follows by combining Corollary
6
with Bär’s inequality
[
4
]

\lambda=\sqrt{\lambda_{\text{\rm min}}(\mathcal{D}^{2})}\leq\frac{n-1}{2\operatorname{Rad}(\Sigma)}.

∎

Finally, we analyze the equality case in Corollary
7
.
Suppose that

\inf_{\partial M}H=(n-1)\sqrt{1+{\operatorname{Rad}(\Sigma)^{-2}}}\;.

In this case, equality holds in Bär’s inequality. By Bär’s rigidity statement
[
4
]
, there exists some
r=\operatorname{Rad}(\Sigma)>0
such that
\Sigma
is isometric to
\mathbf{S}^{n-1}(r)
with the round metric.
Since
\Sigma
is isometric to a round sphere of dimension
n-1
, we know that
\lambda=\frac{n-1}{2r}
and the
\pm\lambda
-eigenspaces of
\mathcal{D}
each have dimension
2^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}
(cf.
[
3
]
).
Consequently, the
\pm\lambda
-eigenspaces of
\mathcal{D}^{\partial M}
each have dimension
2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}
. Thus,

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{Z}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in[-\lambda,\lambda]}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}E_{\lambda_{j}}=2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}.

Let
m:=2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}
. Using Proposition
5
, we obtain a collection of
m
linearly independent spinors
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathcal{S}_{M})
such that
\hat{\nabla}^{M}s_{a}=0
for each
1\leq a\leq m
.
Using standard uniqueness results for ODE, we conclude that
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}
are linearly independent at each point in
M
.
From this, we easily deduce that
M
has constant sectional curvature
-1
.
Moreover, the boundary has constant mean curvature

H=(n-1)\sqrt{1+{\operatorname{Rad}(\Sigma)^{-2}}}=(n-1)\sqrt{1+r^{-2}}\;.

Next, we fix a point on the boundary and let
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n-1}
denote the principal curvatures of
\partial M
at that point, so that
\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{n-1}=(n-1)\sqrt{1+r^{-2}}
. Since
M
has constant sectional curvature
-1
and
\Sigma
has constant sectional curvature
r^{-2}
, it follows from the Gauss equation that
\kappa_{k}\kappa_{l}=1+r^{-2}
for each
k\neq l
. If
n\geq 4
, it follows that
\kappa_{k}=\sqrt{1+r^{-2}}
for each
k
. If
n=3
, the identities
\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}=2\sqrt{1+r^{-2}}
and
\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}=1+r^{-2}
imply that
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=\sqrt{1+r^{-2}}
. In either case, we conclude that
\partial M
is totally umbilic.

We now prove that
M
must be a geodesic ball in
\mathbf{H}^{n}
.
For
x\in\Sigma
and
t\geq 0
, consider the normal exponential map
\Phi(x,t):=\exp_{x}(-t\nu(x))
. Let
r=\sinh\rho
. Then the second fundamental form is given by
h=\sqrt{1+r^{-2}}g|_{\Sigma}=\coth\rho\,g|_{\Sigma}
. We define

\displaystyle\operatorname{seg}

\displaystyle=\{(x,t)\in\Sigma\times[0,\infty):\Phi(x,t)\text{ is defined and }d(x,\Phi(x,t))=t\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{seg}^{0}

\displaystyle=\{(x,t)\in\Sigma\times[0,\infty):(x,t+\varepsilon)\in\operatorname{seg}\text{ for some }\varepsilon>0\}.

It is well known that
\operatorname{seg}^{0}
is a relatively open subset of
\Sigma\times[0,\infty)
, and
\Phi|_{\operatorname{seg}^{0}}
is a diffeomorphism. Moreover, if
(x,t)\in\operatorname{seg}\setminus\operatorname{seg}^{0}
, then one of the following statements holds:

•

The differential
d\Phi
at the point
(x,t)
is singular.

•

There exists a point
(y,t)\in\operatorname{seg}
such that
x\neq y
and
\Phi(x,t)=\Phi(y,t)
.

In the next step, we compute the differential of
\Phi
. Let us fix a point
x\in\Sigma
and consider the normal geodesic
\gamma(t)=\Phi(x,t)
. Given any vector
v\in T_{x}\Sigma
, the corresponding normal Jacobi field
V(t)
satisfies

D_{t}D_{t}V(t)+R(\gamma^{\prime}(t),V(t))\gamma^{\prime}(t)=0,\qquad V(0)=v,\qquad V^{\prime}(0)=-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}h(v,e_{j})\,e_{j}.

Since
M
has constant sectional curvature
-1
and
h=\coth\rho\,g|_{\Sigma}
, we conclude that

V(t)=(\cosh t-\coth\rho\sinh t)\,P_{t}(v)=\frac{\sinh(\rho-t)}{\sinh\rho}\,P_{t}(v)

for all
t
. Here,
P_{t}
denotes the parallel transport map along
\gamma
.
Therefore, the differential
d\Phi
is invertible at each point in
(\Sigma\times[0,\rho))\cap\operatorname{seg}
. Moreover,
\operatorname{seg}\subset\Sigma\times[0,\rho]
.

We claim that
\Sigma\times[0,\rho)\subset\operatorname{seg}
. Suppose this is false. We define
t_{*}=\inf\{t\geq 0:(x,t)\notin\operatorname{seg}\}
. Then
t_{*}\in(0,\rho)
and
\Sigma\times[0,t_{*}]\subset\operatorname{seg}
. Moreover, we can find a point
y\in\Sigma
such that
(y,t_{*})\in\operatorname{seg}\setminus\operatorname{seg}^{0}
. Since
t_{*}\in(0,\rho)
, the differential
d\Phi
at the point
(y,t_{*})
is invertible. Consequently, we can find a point
(z,t_{*})\in\operatorname{seg}
such that
y\neq z
and
\Phi(y,t_{*})=\Phi(z,t_{*})
. For each
t\in[0,t_{*}]
, we define
\Sigma_{t}:=\{\Phi(x,t):x\in\Sigma\}
. For each
t\in[0,t_{*}]
,
\Sigma_{t}
is an immersed hypersurface, and the eigenvalues of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma_{t}
are given by
\coth(\rho-t)
. In particular,
\Sigma_{t}
is strictly convex for each
t\in[0,t_{*}]
. Moreover, the hypersurfaces
\{\Sigma_{t}:t\in[0,t_{*})\}
are embedded and nested, while
\Sigma_{t_{*}}
develops an interior tangential self-contact point.
This contradicts the maximum principle. Thus,
\Sigma\times[0,\rho)\subset\operatorname{seg}
, and it follows that
M
is diffeomorphic to a geodesic ball
B_{\rho}\subset\mathbf{H}^{n}
. Since
M
has constant sectional curvature
-1
, we conclude that
M
is isometric to
B_{\rho}
, completing the proof of Theorem
1
.

Finally, note that any
1
-Lipschitz map
f:\partial M\to\mathbf{S}^{n-1}(r)
with
r=\sinh\rho=\operatorname{Rad}(\partial M)
and
\deg f\neq 0
is a smooth isometry, as shown by Bär
[
4
]
.
Since
M
is isometric to
B_{\rho}
(by Theorem
1
) with a boundary isometry
f:\partial M\to\partial B_{\rho}
, we can produce an isometry
F:M\to B_{\rho}
with
F|_{\partial M}=f
.
This proves Corollary
2
.

Emergent quantum field theories on curved spacetimes in spinor Bose-Einstein condensates: from scalar to Proca fields
Simon Brunner, Christian F. Schmidt, Stefan Floerchinger

Abstract
We consider excitations of a spin-1 Bose-Einstein-condensate (BEC) in the vicinity of different mean-field configurations and derive mappings to emergent relativistic quantum field theories minimally coupled to curved acoustic spacetimes. The quantum fields are typically identified with Nambu-Goldstone bosons, such that the structure of the analogue quantum field theories on curved spacetimes depends on the (spontaneous) symmetry breaking pattern of the respective ground-state. The emergent spacetime geometries are independent of each other and exhibit bi-metricity in the polar and antiferromagnetic phase, whereas one has tri-metricity in the ferromagnetic phase. Compared to scalar BECs, the spinor degrees of freedom allow to investigate massive vector and scalar fields where the former is a spin-nematic rotation mode in the polar phase which can be cast into a Proca field that is minimally coupled to a curved spacetime that emerges on length scales larger than the spin-healing length. Finally, we specify the Zeeman couplings and the condensate trap to be spacetime-dependent such that a cosmological FLRW-metric can be achieved. This work enables a pathway towards quantum-simulating cosmological particle production of Proca quanta via quenching the quadratic Zeeman-coefficient or via magnetic field ramps, which both result in the creation of spin-nematic squeezed states.

Effective theory for fluctuations

II.1
Effective Action

We consider a weakly interacting Bose gas with hyperfine spin
f=1
.
We shall be interested in Bose-Einstein condensed states, formed by macroscopic occupation of modes.
Furthermore, we treat the BEC in two spatial dimensions although our calculations allow for lower dimensionality through suitably chosen trapping potentials.
In particular, we shall also consider an external magnetic field
B(t,\mathbf{r})
oriented parallel to the
z
-axis, and investigate its influence onto the two-dimensional orthogonal subspace.

It is convenient for us to work with (the expectation value of) the bosonic field in the symmetric tensor representation of the SU
(2)
hyperfine spin group (with
x=(t,\mathbf{r})
),

\Phi(x)=\left(\begin{matrix}\Phi_{+}(x)&\Phi_{0}(x)/\sqrt{2}\\
\Phi_{0}(x)/\sqrt{2}&\Phi_{-}(x)\end{matrix}\right).

(1)

Here the components are identical to the often employed representation
\tilde{\Phi}(x)=(\Phi_{+}(x),\Phi_{0}(x),\Phi_{-}(x))
.
Our ansatz for the effective action then reads

\Gamma[\Phi]=\int_{t,\bm{r}}\{\tr[\Phi^{*}(\text{i}\hbar\partial_{t}+\tfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2M}\bm{\nabla}^{2}-V_{\mathrm{ext}})\Phi\big]-U(\Phi)\},

(2)

with the effective potential

U(\Phi)=\left(-\mu+\tfrac{2}{3}q\right)n+\tfrac{1}{2}c_{0}n^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}c_{1}\bm{F}^{2}-pF_{z}+\tfrac{1}{2}q\mathcal{Q}_{zz},

(3)

where we introduced the scalar density

n=\text{tr}(\Phi^{*}\Phi),

(4)

the vector spin-density (or dipole moment)

F_{i}=\text{tr}(\Phi^{*}\sigma_{i}\Phi),

(5)

as well as the symmetric and traceless spin-nematic (or quadrupolar) tensor

\mathcal{Q}_{ij}=\tr( \Phi^* \sigma_i \Phi\sigma_j^T )-\tfrac{1}{3}n\delta_{ij},

(6)

which has five linearly-independent degrees of freedom
Smerald and Shannon 2013
.
Here, the index
i\in\{{x,y,z\}}
represents the three spatial directions and
\sigma_{i}
are the corresponding Pauli matrices.
The collection of the aforementioned quantities will be referred to as the (spin) multipole moments in the following.

The action (
2
) and the spin-multipoles (
eqs.

4
,
5
and
6
) are invariant under U
(1)
-transformations,
\Phi\to\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\chi}\,\Phi,\,\Phi^{*}\to\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\chi}\,\Phi^{*}
related to particle number conservation.
Moreover, s-wave contact interactions and spin-exchange-collisions, parametrized via the coupling constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
(that depend on the scattering lengths of spin zero and spin two atoms
Kawaguchi and Ueda 2012
), respectively,
are invariant under spin rotations realized through SU
(2)
transformations
\Phi_{AB}\to U_{A}^{\hskip 4.26773ptC}U_{B}^{\hskip 4.26773ptD}\Phi_{CD}
.
However, the Zeeman-energy-shifts, parametrized in terms of
p
and
q
, explicitly break this spin rotation symmetry
such that the action (
2
) is only SU
(2)
-invariant in abscence of a magnetic field (
p=q=0
).
In particular,
\bm{F}
transforms as a vector,

F_{i}\to\mathcal{R}_{ij}F_{j},

(7)

and
\mathcal{Q}
as a tensor,

\mathcal{Q}_{ij}\to\mathcal{R}_{ik}\mathcal{R}_{jl}\mathcal{Q}_{kl},

(8)

under SU
(2)
-transformations, where

\mathcal{R}_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}\tr(\sigma_i U \sigma_j U^\dagger)

(9)

is the two-to-one homomorphism between SU
(2)
and SO
(3)

appendix

In summary, the effective action exhibits a
\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)
-symmetry in abscence of an external magnetic field.
At finite values of
q
and

, SU
(2)
-invariance is explicitly broken while U
(1)
-invariance is still maintained, since the spin multipole moments are U
(1)
-invariant, but SU

The linear Zeeman coefficient

is composed of the Landé hyperfine

and the external magnetic field

whereas the quadratic Zeeman coefficient

(10)

where
\Delta E_{\text{hf}}
denotes the hyperfine energy splitting.

Note that we allow for a space- and time-dependent external trap potential that we assume to be isotropic,

V_{\text{ext}}(t,r)=\tfrac{1}{2}M\omega^{2}(t)f(r),

(11)

where
\omega(t)
acts as a (possibly time-dependent) trapping frequency and

parametrizes the spatial profile, where for example

represents a harmonic trap.

In this work we furthermore assume that the linear and quadratic Zeeman coefficients

and

can be varied independently (as has been for example realized in
Stenger et al. 1998
).
The latter can be tuned in modern ultracold atom experiments by ramping the external magnetic field (

,

), or through an off-resonant microwave dressing field that can quench the quadratic Zeeman coefficient (

,

,

,

,

,

In the following we shall investigate effective theories around different ground-state configurations of the spinor BEC.
Finding a mean-field ground state can be understood as a minimization problem for the magnetic part of the effective potential,

U_{\mathrm{mag}}(\Phi)\equiv\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\bm{F}^{2}-pF_{z}+\frac{1}{2}q\left(Q_{zz}+\frac{4}{3}\bar{n}\right).

(12)

Clearly, ferromagnetic interactions (

) favour a maximization of
\bm{F}

whereas anti-ferromagnetic interactions (

) tend to minimize

.
A positive (negative) linear Zeeman-shift

For

Furthermore,

(
q>0

A competition between these characteristics leads to the rich quantum phase diagram of spin-1 BECs

,

,

,

For example, it is possible to obtain a magnetically ordered state despite the presence of anti-ferromagnetic interactions (
c_{1}>0
),
if the energy scale of the positive linear (
p>0
), or negative quadratic (

) Zeeman shift is stronger than the interaction energy
\bar{n}c_{1}

III.1
Polar (or Spin-Nematic) Phase

A beyond-mean-field analysis
Law et al. 1998
reveals the ground-state of an anti-ferromagnetic (
c_{1}>0
) spin-1-BEC in absence of an external magnetic field to be of rather peculiar kind.
It corresponds to a strongly-correlated singlet state, which is formed by boson pairs and exhibits large super-Poissonian number fluctuations.
According to
Ho and Yip 2000
, this ground-state is an unstable, fragmented condensate which can be overcome by a small magnetic field gradient that violates strict angular momentum conservation.
The state at hand is experimentally hard to construct due to the high sensitivity to magnetic fields
Bookjans et al. 2011
and long dynamical time scales
Cui et al. 2008
.
However the vicinity of small magnetic fields can be studied via quantum quenching the quadratic Zeeman coefficient
Barnett et al. 2010
,
Bookjans et al. 2011
.

In the following, we will nevertheless apply mean-field theory to the background state (which captures experimentally relevant situations already at small magnetic fields
Stamper-Kurn et al. 1998
,
Ho and Yip 2000
,
Barnett et al. 2010
).
For anti-ferromagnetic interactions,
c_{1}>0
, the effective potential is minimized by a non-magnetized state,
\bar{\bm{F}}=0
, such that the background is determined by

\left(V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}\right)\xi_{\mathfrak{m}}=0,\quad\text{for }\quad\mathfrak{m}\in\{+1,0,-1\},

(27)

which only has non-trivial solutions if the Thomas-Fermi relation

V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}=0

(28)

is fulfilled.
Without loss of generality, any magnetically neutral background state can be parametrized by

\xi=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\chi}}{\sqrt{2}}U\sigma_{x}U^{T},

(29)

where
U\in\mathrm{SU}(2)
and the global phase
\chi
are arbitrary.
Despite the absence of magnetic order, the state at hand exhibits so-called uni-axial nematic order
Gennes and Prost 1993
, i.e. the spin-nematic (or quadrupololar) tensor takes the form

\bar{\mathcal{Q}}=-2\bar{n}\,(\bar{\bm{d}}\otimes\bar{\bm{d}}-\tfrac{1}{3}\mathds{1}_{3}).

(30)

By analogy to ferromagnetism, the state at hand is also known as a ferroquadrupolar ground state in spin nematics
Penc and Läuchli 2010
,
Smerald and Shannon 2013
,
Ivanov and Kolezhuk 2003
,
Chojnacki et al. 2024
.
The nematic tensor (
30
) is an orthogonal projector onto the plane perpendicular to the so-called nematic director
\bar{\bm{d}}\equiv\mathcal{R}\bm{e}_{z}
(where again the SO
(3)
element
\mathcal{R}
is related to the SU
(2)
element
U
in
eq.

29
through
eq.

9
).
Due to the remaining azimutal symmetry in the plane perpendicular to
\bar{\bm{d}}
, and the invariance of
\mathcal{Q}
under the inversion
\bar{\bm{d}}\to-\bar{\bm{d}}
,
the state at hand spontaneously breaks the
\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU(2)}
symmetry group down to the two-dimensional orthogonal group
\mathrm{O}(2)\cong\mathrm{SO}(2)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Hence, the order parameter manifold is given by the coset space

\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)/\mathrm{O}(2)\cong\mathrm{U}(1)\times S^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2},

(31)

where S
2
is the three-sphere.
It is possible to show that the spontaneous symmetry breaking pattern (
31
) leads to
1+2
Nambu-Goldstone bosons (NGBs) which all have a linear dispersion relation
Watanabe and Murayama 2012
.
The former is the phonon (as for a scalar BEC), whereas the latter are two independent magnon modes that stem from spin-exchange collisions and induce a rotation of nematic order, as we shall show in the following.

For the effective theory of fluctuations around the ground-state (
29
) we find

\displaystyle\delta n^{(2)}V_{\mathrm{ext}}+\delta U=\frac{c_{0}}{2}\delta n^{(1)}\delta n^{(1)}+\frac{c_{1}}{2}\langle\delta\bm{F}^{(1)},\delta\bm{F}^{(1)}\rangle,

(32)

where we replaced the external potential according to the Thomas-Fermi relation (
28
).
In the polar (or spin-nematic) phase, the density-fluctuations are scalar projections on the nematic director,

\delta n^{(1)}=\sqrt{2\bar{n}}\,\langle\bar{\bm{d}},\real\bm{\varphi}\rangle,

(33)

where we re-ordered the fluctuating fields in the complex vector field

\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\phi_{-}-\phi_{+})\bm{e}_{x}-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\sqrt{2}}(\phi_{-}+\phi_{+})\bm{e}_{y}+\phi_{0}\bm{e}_{z}

(34)

which is of convenient use since it transforms as a vector under internal SU
(2)
-rotations and its projections onto the cartesian vectors corresponds to field-theoretic versions of the corresponding cartesian state (i.e. the eigenstate of the spin-operator projected onto the respective cartesian direction with vanishing eigenvalue).
The spin-density fluctuations

\delta\bm{F}^{(1)}=\sqrt{2\bar{n}}\,\bar{\bm{d}}\wedge\imaginary\bm{\varphi}=\sqrt{2\bar{n}}\,\imaginary\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}

(35)

always occur in the plane perpendicular to the background nematic director
\bar{\bm{d}}
, which we parametrize with the projected field vector

\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}\equiv\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}_{\parallel},\qquad\bm{\varphi}_{\parallel}\equiv\langle\bar{\bm{d}},\bm{\varphi}\rangle\,\bar{\bm{d}}.\\

(36)

The perturbations to the spin-nematic tensor read

\delta\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}=\frac{\delta n^{(1)}}{\bar{n}}\bar{\mathcal{Q}}-\sqrt{2\bar{n}}\,\left(\bar{\bm{d}}\otimes\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}+\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}\otimes\bar{\bm{d}}\right),\\

(37)

and can be combined with the background nematic tensor in the uni-axial form

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{Q}}+\delta\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\equiv-2(\bar{n}+\delta n^{(1)})

(38)

\displaystyle\times\big[\big(\bar{\bm{d}}+\delta\bm{d}^{(1)}\big)\otimes\big(\bar{\bm{d}}+\delta\bm{d}^{(1)}\big)-\tfrac{1}{3}\mathds{1}_{3}\big],

where we identify the leading-order fluctuation of the nematic director as

\displaystyle\delta\bm{d}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\real\bm{\varphi}\wedge\bar{\bm{d}}}{\sqrt{2\bar{n}}}=\frac{\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}}{\sqrt{2\bar{n}}},

(39)

indicating its rotational motion.
As has been characterized in
Yukawa and Ueda 2012
, the magnonic modes separate into a rotational mode of the nematic tensor (given by
\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}
in the present parametrization) and an amplitude oscillation of the spin-density (described by
\imaginary\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}
here).
In particular, the presence of anisotropic, transversal spin fluctuations - despite the absence of a mean magnetization - is a characteristic feature of a spin-nematic state
Ivanov and Kolezhuk 2003
,
Läuchli et al. 2006
,
Penc and Läuchli 2010
,
Smerald and Shannon 2013
,
Chojnacki et al. 2024
.
Complementary, the phonons correspond to
\bm{\varphi}_{\parallel}
and are thus longitudinal to nematic-order.

In terms of the introduced fields one now finds

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{t,\bm{r}}\bigg\{-\frac{\mathrm{i}\hbar}{2}\langle\bm{\varphi}^{*},\partial_{t}\bm{\varphi}\rangle+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4M}\langle\bm{\varphi}^{*},\bm{\nabla}^{2}\bm{\varphi}\rangle

(40)

\displaystyle-c_{0}\bar{n}(\real\bm{\varphi}_{\parallel})^{2}-c_{1}\bar{n}(\imaginary\bm{\varphi}_{\perp})^{2}\bigg\}

as an effective action that governs the dynamics of the fluctuating fields.
To derive a low-energy effective field theory for the three NGBs, we integrate out the density-fluctuation
\real\bm{\varphi}_{\parallel}
and the spin-amplitude oscillation
\imaginary\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}
in the regime where their kinetic energy is neglible compared to the contact- and spin-exchange interaction energies,
\hbar^{2}\bm{\nabla}^{2}/(2M)\ll c_{0}\bar{n},c_{1}\bar{n}
.
This amounts to evaluate the action (
40
) on the corresponding field equations, which read

\displaystyle-\hbar\partial_{t}\imaginary\bm{\varphi}_{\parallel}

\displaystyle=2\bar{n}c_{0}\real\bm{\varphi}_{\parallel},

(41)

\displaystyle\hbar\partial_{t}\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}

\displaystyle=2\bar{n}c_{1}\imaginary\bm{\varphi}_{\perp},

in the low-energy regime.
As a result, one finds that the effective theory for the U
(1)
-NGB can be written as

\Gamma_{2}^{\parallel}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\int_{t,\bm{r}}\sqrt{g_{0}}\,g_{0}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi

(42)

where we rescaled the field to have a relativistic mass dimension, i.e.
\phi=\imaginary\bm{\varphi}_{\parallel}/\sqrt{2M}
, and introduced the acoustic metric

(g_{0})_{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{matrix}-1&0\\
0&c_{\mathrm{s},0}^{-2}\delta_{ij}\end{matrix}\right),\quad c^{2}_{\mathrm{s,0}}(t,r)=\frac{\bar{n}(r)c_{0}(t)}{M},

(43)

with the abbreviation

\sqrt{g_{0}}\equiv[-\det(g_0)_{\mu\nu}]^{1/2}=1/c_{\mathrm{s,0}}^{2}.

This emergent relativistic theory is equivalent to the one for the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-NGB of a scalar BEC which was extensively studied theoretically
Tolosa-Simeón et al. 2022
,
Schmidt et al. 2024
and experimentally
Viermann et al. 2022
,
Sparn et al. 2024
.

For the two SU
(2)
-NGBs we find the low-energy effective field theory

\Gamma_{2}^{\perp}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4M}\int_{t,\bm{r}}\left\{-\frac{M}{\bar{n}c_{1}}(\partial_{t}\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp})^{2}+(\bm{\nabla}\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp})^{2}\right\},

(44)

which generates the field equation

-\partial_{t}^{2}\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}+2\frac{\partial_{t}c_{\mathrm{s,1}}}{c_{\mathrm{s,1}}}\partial_{t}\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}+c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}(t)\bm{\nabla}^{2}\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}=0.

(45)

where we introduced the propagation speed for spin-waves,

c_{\mathrm{s},1}^{2}(t)=\bar{n}c_{1}(t)/M

(46)

To bring this into a relativistic covariant form we concentrate on situations where
\bar{n}c_{1}
is spatially constant. We decompose

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M}}\real(\varphi_{\perp})_{j}(t,\mathbf{x})=\frac{\partial_{j}}{\sqrt{-\partial_{1}^{2}-\partial_{2}^{2}}}\chi(t,\mathbf{x})+A_{j}(t,\mathbf{x}),

(47)

where
\partial_{j}A_{j}(t,\mathbf{x})=0
, and the square root of the Laplacian can be defined with appropriate boundary conditions through a Fourier expansion.
The longitudinal magnonic degree of freedom satisfies the Klein-Gordon equation

\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g_{1}}g_{1}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\chi)=0

(48)

where the metric has the same form as in eq. (
43
),

(g_{1})_{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{matrix}-1&0\\
0&c_{\mathrm{s},1}^{-2}\delta_{ij}\end{matrix}\right),

(49)

but with the spin-wave speed
c_{\mathrm{s},1}(t)
defined in
eq.

46
.
A relativistic form for the transverse magnonic degree of freedom can be obtained by introducing the augmented vector field

(A^{\mu})=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
A_{i}\end{pmatrix},

(50)

such that the field equation reads

\frac{1}{\sqrt{g_{1}}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{g_{1}}g_{1}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}A^{\sigma}\right)=0,

(51)

where the vector field satisfies both the Weyl gauge condition
A_{0}=0
and the Coulomb gauge condition
\bm{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{A}=0
.
As a consequence of
\partial_{i}\sqrt{g_{1}}=0
, the covariant gauge constraint

\nabla_{\mu}A^{\mu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu}+\frac{\partial_{\mu}\sqrt{g_{1}}}{\sqrt{g_{1}}}A^{\mu}=0

(52)

is fulfilled. With this, the equation of motion can be written in the form

\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g_{1}}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{g_{1}}F^{\mu\nu}\right)=0,

(53)

where we introduced the field strength tensor

F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}.

(54)

Given that the equations of motion are as for a Abelian gauge field
Heisenberg 2019
one might expect that also the action be expressied in the corresponding form. We plan to investigate this point further in future work.

When considering a finite magnetic field in
section

IV
, the spin nematic rotation mode (and thus the emergent Proca theory) will gain a mass such that the subsidiarity constraint (
52
) will be automatically fulfilled.

Before we finalize this section, we would like to briefly comment on the lattice approach
Chojnacki et al. 2024
,
Smerald and Shannon 2013
where the anti-ferromagnetic Heisenberg model augmented by spin-exchange interactions
Ivanov and Kolezhuk 2003
,
Läuchli et al. 2006
,
Penc and Läuchli 2010
is used to model the spin-nematic phase at hand.
The study concluded in the emergence of a relativistic spin-two field given by the spin-nematic fluctuations which in the present work are formulated via
\delta Q=\delta\mathcal{Q}-\delta n^{(1)}\bar{\mathcal{Q}}/\bar{n}
from
eq.

37
.
Our calculations reveal the non-linear sigma model used there, which is plausible since the spin-1-BEC is a member of the Heisenberg universality class
Sachdev 2011
, such that quantum rotor models
Barnett et al. 2010
or non-linear sigma models
Zhou 2001
,
Yang 2009
are applicable.
However, we disagree with the interpretation as a tensor field. The equations of motion of the spin-nematic fluctuations indeed take the form

-\partial_{t}^{2}\delta Q_{iz}+c_{\mathrm{s},1}^{2}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\delta Q_{iz}=0,\qquad i=x,y,

(55)

if we choose
\bar{\bm{d}}=\bm{e}_{z}
without loss of generality.
The field equations seem to agree with those of linearized gravity with propagation speed
c_{\mathrm{s,1}}
(if we keep both
\bar{n}
and
c_{1}
constant in space and time, respectively), and the transversal, traceless nature of
\delta Q_{ij}
is superficially analogous to tensor metric perturbations in General Relativity
Carroll 2019
.

However, according to
eq.

37
, the spin-nematic perturbations decompose into a background part
\bm{\bar{d}}
and a two-dimensional vector fluctuation
\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}
.
With
\bm{\bar{d}}
kept fixed, the spin-nematic perturbations
\delta Q
do not transform as a genuine tensor field; they transform as a vector under SO
(2)
rotations (which is the remaining symmetry of the theory if the background is kept fixed).
In contrast, a gravitational wave with helicity
2
would rotate with a phase
\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\vartheta}
around its direction of propagation.

III.2
Ferromagnetic Phase

Ferromagnetic interactions (
c_{1}<0
) favor a magnetized state which is however unstable at vanishing external magnetic fields as we also shall see in the following.
Nevertheless, the state could be approached dynamically (similar to the attractive regime of a scalar BEC), which motivates to investigate its excitations.
Without loss of generality we model the ferromagnetic background by the spin-rotated positively polarized state

\xi=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\chi}}{2}U(\mathds{1}_{2}+\sigma_{z})U^{T},

(56)

where
U\in\mathrm{SU}(2)
and the global phase
\chi
is arbitrary. The mean magnetization is
\bar{\bm{F}}=\bar{n}\bar{\bm{d}}
,
where we chose
\bar{\bm{d}}
as the unit vector pointing into
z
-direction.
Note that the oppositely polarized case can be investigated analogously with a few reparametrizations
1
1

1

One has to map
\bm{F}\to-\bm{F}
and
\phi_{\pm}^{\mathrm{re}}\to\phi_{\mp}^{\mathrm{re}}
as well as
\phi_{\pm}^{\mathrm{im}}\to-\phi_{\mp}^{\mathrm{im}}
and finally
\mathrm{e}_{\pm}\to\mathrm{e}_{\mp}
.
For the state at hand, magnetic and nematic order align,

\bar{\mathcal{Q}}=\bar{n}(\bar{\bm{d}}\otimes\bar{\bm{d}}-\tfrac{1}{3}\mathds{1}_{3}).

(57)

In particular, the
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-inversion symmetry is spontaneously broken in the ferromagnetic state through the finite background magnetization.
Hence, the spontaneous symmetry breaking is described by the coset space

\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)/\mathrm{SO}(2)\cong\mathrm{U}(1)\times S^{2},

(58)

which differs from the polar coset space (
31
) by the absence of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivalent configurations.
This affects the NGBs such that there is only one with linear dispersion (i.e. the phonon) and another single one with quadratic dispersion
Watanabe and Murayama 2012
,
As we shall show, the latter corresponds to a rotation of the nematic tensor which is accompanied by the rotation of the magnetization
Yukawa and Ueda 2012
.
In the following it will be convenient to introduce

\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}

\displaystyle\equiv\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left((\phi_{+}-\phi_{-})\bm{e}_{x}+\mathrm{i}(\phi_{+}+\phi_{-})\bm{e}_{y}\right),\quad\bm{\varphi}_{0}\equiv\phi_{0}\bm{e}_{z},

(59)

such that

\displaystyle\delta n^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\bar{n}}\,\real\,\langle\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}_{0},\bm{e}_{x}-\mathrm{i}\bm{e}_{y}\rangle=\sqrt{2\bar{n}}\phi_{+}^{\mathrm{re}}.

(60)

In contrast to the polar phase, the nematic director is projected out in the density-fluctuation (
60
).
For the spin-density fluctuations, we find

\displaystyle\delta\bm{F}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\bar{n}}\,\imaginary\left[(\bm{\varphi}+\bm{\varphi}_{0})\wedge(\bm{e}_{x}-\mathrm{i}\bm{e}_{y})\right]

(61)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\bar{n}}(\phi_{0}^{\mathrm{re}}\bm{e}_{x}+\phi_{0}^{\mathrm{im}}\bm{e}_{y}+\sqrt{2}\phi_{+}^{\mathrm{re}}\bm{e}_{z}).

Hence, the real and imaginary part of the perturbation
\phi_{0}
lead to spin-density fluctuations in the plane-orthogonal to
\bar{\bm{F}}
, inducing a rotational motion,
whereas
\phi_{+}^{\mathrm{re}}
is the longitudinal spin-excitation which is known to be identical with the density-excitation of the ferromagnetic state
Ohmi and Machida 1998
.
The spin-nematic fluctuations are again obtained via symmetrized tensor products of two vectors whose overlap gives the density-fluctuation (
60
),

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta n^{(1)}}{\bar{n}}\bar{\mathcal{Q}}+\delta n^{(1)}(\bm{e}_{x}\otimes\bm{e}_{x}+\bm{e}_{y}\otimes\bm{e}_{y})

(62)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\bar{n}}\real\left[(\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}_{0})\otimes\bm{e}_{-}+\bm{e}_{-}\otimes(\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}_{0})\right].

where
\bm{e}_{-}=\bm{e}_{x}-\mathrm{i}\bm{e}_{y}
.
Combining the fluctuation with the background, we find the manifestly traceless expression

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{Q}+\mathcal{Q}^{(1)}}{\sqrt{2\bar{n}}}=\frac{\bar{n}+\delta n^{(1)}}{\sqrt{2\bar{n}}}\left(\bm{d}_{\parallel}\otimes\bm{d}_{\parallel}-\tfrac{1}{3}\mathds{1}_{3}\right)

(63)

\displaystyle+\phi_{-}^{\mathrm{im}}(\bm{e}_{x}\otimes\bm{e}_{y}+\bm{e}_{y}\otimes\bm{e}_{x})+\phi_{-}^{\mathrm{re}}(\bm{e}_{x}\otimes\bm{e}_{x}-\bm{e}_{y}\otimes\bm{e}_{y}),

where we introduced the rotated background director

\bm{d}_{\parallel}=\bar{\bm{d}}-\frac{\imaginary(\phi_{0}\bm{e_{-}})}{2\sqrt{\bar{n}}}\wedge\bar{\bm{d}}.

(64)

Consequently, the perturbation
\phi_{0}
induces a rotation of the nematic director as for the polar phase.
However, there is also a deformation mode (
\phi_{-}
) which leads to the less-symmetrical bi-axial shape in contrast to the polar phase (where uni-axial order is preserved by the perturbations).
For the effective potential of the fluctuations, we find

\displaystyle\delta U+V_{\mathrm{ext}}\delta n^{(2)}

(65)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}c_{0}(\delta n^{(1)})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}c_{1}\big(\delta\bm{F}^{(1)}\big)^{2}+\bar{n}c_{1}(\delta\bm{F}_{z}^{(2)}\mp\delta n^{(2)})

\displaystyle=\bar{n}(c_{0}+c_{1})(\phi_{+}^{\mathrm{re}})^{2}-\bar{n}c_{1}\left((\phi_{-}^{\mathrm{re}})^{2}+(\phi_{-}^{\mathrm{im}})^{2}\right),

where we used that the Thomas-Fermi relation in the ferromagnetic phase reads

\bar{n}(c_{0}+c_{1})+V_{\mathrm{ext}}=\mu.

(66)

At a vanishing external magnetic field, the rotational modes of the spin-density, which are given by

\delta F_{x}^{(1)}=\sqrt{\bar{n}}\,\phi_{0}^{\mathrm{re}},\quad\delta F_{y}^{(1)}=\sqrt{\bar{n}}\,\phi_{0}^{\mathrm{im}},

(67)

cancel in the effective potential (
65
) and therefore obey the field equations

\hbar\partial_{t}\phi_{0}^{\mathrm{re}}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2M}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\phi_{0}^{\mathrm{im}},\quad-\hbar\partial_{t}\phi_{0}^{\mathrm{im}}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2M}\bm{\nabla}^{2}\phi_{0}^{\mathrm{re}},

(68)

highlighting that the transversal spin-density fluctuations indeed have a non-relativistic dispersion
Yukawa and Ueda 2012
.
Since these are weakly dynamical in the acoustic regime, they shall not be considered in the following.
Integrating out the density fluctuation
\phi_{+}^{\mathrm{re}}
and the off-diagonal nematic deformation mode
\phi_{-}^{\mathrm{im}}
under neglection of their kinetic energies,
\bar{n}c_{1}+\hbar^{2}\bm{\nabla}^{2}/4M\to\bar{n}c_{1}
, we find
an effective action for the phonon of the ferromagnetic phase,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}^{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\int_{t,\bm{r}}\sqrt{g_{+}}\,g_{+}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi_{+}\partial_{\nu}\phi_{+},

(69)

where we introduced the analogue relativistic field
\phi_{+}=\phi_{+}^{\mathrm{im}}/\sqrt{2M}
and the acoustic metric

({g_{+}})_{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{matrix}-1&0\\
0&c_{\mathrm{s},+}^{-2}\delta_{ij}\end{matrix}\right),

(70)

with the sound-speed

c^{2}_{\mathrm{s,+}}(t,r)=\frac{\bar{n}(r)}{M}\left(c_{0}(t)+c_{1}(t)\right).

(71)

Note that here both energies of the s-wave contact interaction and the spin-exchange collisions contribute to the propagation of the single NGB with linear dispersion,
whereas in the polar phase each type of interaction leads to a separate NGB with linear dispersion.

Since we integrated out the off-diagonal nematic deformation mode
\phi_{-}^{\mathrm{im}}
(cref.
eq.

63
), we are left with the single diagonal degree of freedom
\phi_{-}^{\mathrm{re}}
which does not behave as a genuine tensor field.
Let us therefore work with the analogue relativistic scalar field
\phi_{-}=\phi_{-}^{\mathrm{re}}/\sqrt{2M}
which obeys the effective action

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}^{-}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\int_{t,\bm{r}}\sqrt{g_{-}}\,\left(g_{-}^{\mathrm{\mu\nu}}\partial_{\mu}\phi_{-}\partial_{\nu}\phi_{-}-m_{\phi}^{2}\phi_{-}^{2}\right),

(72)

with the acoustic euclidean metric

(g_{-})_{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&c_{\mathrm{s},-}^{-2}\delta_{ij}\end{matrix}\right),\quad c_{\mathrm{s,-}}^{2}(t,r)=\frac{\bar{n}(r)c_{1}(t)}{M},

(73)

and carries the mass

m_{\phi}^{2}(t,r)=\frac{2M}{\hbar^{2}}c_{\mathrm{s,-}}^{4}(t,r)=\frac{4}{\hbar^{2}}\bar{n}^{2}(r)c_{1}^{2}(t).

(74)

This mass gap is known to exist for nematic deformation mode of the ferromagnetic phase
Yukawa and Ueda 2012
.
Furthermore, the occurence of an euclidean metric indicates that the nematic deformation mode is unstable at vanishing external magnetic field, as we mentioned before.
Indeed, computing the dispersion relation from the resulting field equation, one finds that there exists both an exponentially growing and decaying mode.

IV
Emergent Spacetime Geometries in Explicitly Broken Phases

In the following we shall discuss the implications of a finite magnetic field (
p\neq 0,q\neq 0
) which conventionally shall be pointing into the
z
-direction.
The remaining symmetry of the ground state will be the same as for the spontaneously broken phases; however, the spin rotation group SU
(2)
is explictly broken by the external magnetic field such that the that corresponded to the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-NGBs gain a mass, whereas the the U
(1)
-symmetry is still spontaneously broken, resulting in the presence of a massless phonon.
More detailed calculations can be found in
appendix

D
.

IV.1
Polar (or Spin-Nematic) Phase

In presence of an external magnetic field, the polar phase can be maintained if
q>0
for anti-ferromagnetic interactions (
c_{1}>0
) as positive values of both couplings naturally favour a magnetically neutral state.
However, even for ferromagnetic interactions (
c_{1}<0
) the polar phase can be maintained if the quadratic Zeeman-shift is large compared to the ferromagnetic interaction energy,
q>\bar{n}c_{1}
.
Then ground-state is explictly given by
\xi=\sigma_{x}/\sqrt{2}
and the background nematic director aligns with the external magnetic field,
\bar{\bm{d}}=\bm{e}_{z}
.
Therefore, the phase at hand is still symmetric under O
(2)
-transformations in the plane perpendicular to
\bar{\bm{d}}\parallel\bm{B}
.

The effective theory for the phonon is not affected by the external magnetic field and therefore still described by the relativistic scalar field theory (
42
).
The rotation mode of the nematic director is now massive due to the explicit SU
(2)
-symmetry breaking and written as the relativistic vector field

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4M}\int_{t,\bm{r}}\bigg\{-\frac{M}{\bar{n}c_{1}+q/2}(\partial_{t}\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp})^{2}

(75)

\displaystyle+(\bm{\nabla}\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp})^{2}+\frac{2M}{\hbar^{2}}q\,(\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp})^{2}\bigg\}.

We decompose the vector field
\real\bm{\varphi}_{\perp}
into a longitudinal and a transversal part as in
eq.

47
and obtain the massive Klein-Gordon equation

\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{g_{1}}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\chi\right)=m_{\chi}^{2}\chi,

(76)

with the acoustic metric (
49
) and the mass

m_{\chi}^{2}(t)=\frac{2M}{\hbar^{2}}q(t)c_{\mathrm{s,1}}^{2}(t)=\frac{q(t)}{\hbar^{2}}(2\bar{n}c_{1}(t)+q(t))^{2}

(77)

Note that the finite magnetic field dresses the spin-exchange collisions with the quadratic Zeeman coefficient
q
, such that the spin waves now propagate with

c_{\mathrm{s,1}}^{2}(t)=\frac{2\bar{n}c_{1}(t)+q(t)}{2M}.

(78)

For the transversal degree of freedom, we find the Proca equation

\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{g_{1}}g_{1}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}A^{\sigma}\right)=m_{A}^{2}A^{\sigma},

(79)

where the mass is

m_{A}^{2}(t)=\frac{2M}{\hbar^{2}}q(t)c_{\mathrm{s,1}}^{2}(t)=\frac{q(t)}{\hbar^{2}}(2\bar{n}c_{1}(t)+q(t)).

(80)

IV.2
Ferromagnetic phase

The ferromagnetic phase is naturally favoured by
p\neq 0
and a small enough quadratic Zeeman-coupling
q<\bar{n}c_{1}
.
For sufficiently positive
q\geq\bar{n}c_{1}
, the linear Zeeman shift needs to surpass
p>q/2
in order to maintain this phase.
In the following, we again consider only the positively polarized case where now then ground-state parameter is explicitly given by
\xi=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathds{1}_{2}+\sigma_{z})
.
The U
(1)
-NGB is still described by the massless relativistic scalar field theory (
69
) with the acoustic metric (
70
).

Notably, the nematic deformation mode
\phi_{-}
is stabilized by the external magnetic field resulting in the effective action

\Gamma_{2}^{-}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\int_{t,\bm{r}}\sqrt{g_{-}}\,\left(g_{-}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi_{-}\partial_{\nu}\phi_{-}+m_{-}^{2}\phi_{-}^{2}\right),

(81)

with a Lorentzian acoustic spacetime,

(g_{-})_{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{matrix}-1&0\\
0&c_{\mathrm{s},-}^{-2}\delta_{ij}\end{matrix}\right),\quad c_{\mathrm{s},-}^{2}(t,r)=\frac{p(t)-\bar{n}(r)c_{1}(t)}{M},

V
Specification to FLRW-Geometry

In this final section, we shall specify how the background density profile and the first and second Zeeman-couplings need to be tuned in order to simulate an emergent FLRW-spacetime.
To ensure that a certain background density profile
\bar{n}(r)
is preserved by a variation of the interaction couplings
c_{0}(t),c_{1}(t)
or the Zeeman coefficients
p(t),q(t)
,
has to assure the Thomas-Fermi relations at all times, resulting in a stationary background state.
For example, for phononic particle production via the time-dependent coupling

, the external trapping potential
V_{\mathrm{ext}}
can be adjusted in time
Tolosa-Simeón et al. 2022
.

V.1
Polar Phase

V.1.1
Scalar field: phonon

Similar to the treatment of the scalar BEC in previous work

(
11

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s_{0}^{2}=-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{t}^{2}+\frac{M}{\bar{n}_{0}c_{0}(t)}\left(1\mp\frac{r^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}}\right)^{-2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}x

(102)

As expected, the structure of the acoustic spacetime experienced by the phononic excitation is identical to

V.1.2
Proca field: magnons

Quench

An experimentally feasible way to realize temporal curvature for analogue Proca bosons (represented via the transversal degree of freedom of the two magnons in the spinor BEC) consists of the well-known quantum quench of the quadratic Zeeman coefficient

VI
Conclusion and Outlook

We have shown the emergence of relativistic quantum field theories on effective spacetime geometries in terms of low-energy excitations of a spin-1 Bose-Einstein condensate.
The additional spin degrees of freedom can be utilized to investigate massive bosonic vector and scalar fields which are minimally coupled to curved acoustic spacetimes.
In our analysis, we focused on stationary, mean-field ground states in the polar phase, the ferromagnetic phase and the anti-ferromagnetic phase and expanded in fluctuations in quadratic order.
In each ground-state, the phase-fluctuation conjugate to the density mode is a Nambu-Goldstone boson associated to the spontaneous symmetry breaking of
\mathrm{U}(1)
, and can be cast into massless, relativistic theory, as for a scalar BEC
Tolosa-Simeón et al. 2022
.
The remaining fields of interest were identified in terms of density, spin-density, and spin-nematic fluctuations, as it is summarized in
table

1
.

Perhaps most interestingly is the polar phase, which has three Nambu-Goldstone bosons with linear dispersion associated to the spontaneous symmetry breaking of
\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\to\mathrm{O}(2)
.
The NBGs associated to the spontaneous symmetry breaking of SU
(2)
down to O
(2)
are spin-nematic excitations that rotate the nematic director.
They transform as a vector under O
(2)
and are therefore described by a two-component vector field in the transversal plane, which can be cast into a relativistic Proca field that is minimally coupled to an acoustic geometry set by the polar background.
In the current stage, the analogy only holds to a specific gauge choice of the Proca field. In future work we aim to investigate conditions for the description as an (emergent) gauge theory.
One motivation could be that the Proca field, also referred to as the dark photon, is a dark matter candidate
Graham et al. 2016
,
Fabbrichesi et al. 2020
,
Felice and Hell 2025
.

In the ferromagnetic phase on the other hand, one has a spontaneous symmetry breaking of
\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)
down to SO
(2)
, which gives rise to two Nambu-Goldstone bosons with linear and quadratic dispersion, respectively.
The latter is a nematic rotation which is identical to a spin rotation, because the ferromagnetic state is not
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-symmetric (which is the only difference of the symmetry breaking pattern compared to the polar phase).
In this phase, quantum fluctuations also lead to nematic deformation modes which are massive regardless of the presence of a magnetic field. Integrating out one polarization degree of freedom leads to emergent relativistic scalar field theory that can be stabilized by an external magnetic field.

In the anti-ferromagnetic phase, one has a full, spontaneous symmetry breaking of
\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)
in case of a vanishing linear Zeeman shift,
p=0
, which results in a bi-axial spin-nematic phase that possesses two scalar Nambu-Goldstone bosons with linear dispersion.
Those NGBs are phase fluctuations conjugate to density and longitudinal spin-density fluctuations, respectively, and can be cast into massless, relativistic field theories.
One also has another scalar degree of freedom which is part of a spin (or spin-nematic) rotational mode.

Finally, we propose to engineer the emergent acoustic spacetimes to a cosmological FLRW-form through radially symmetric trapping potentials and time-dependent magnetic fields.
However, other spacetime geometries that may for example contain analogue horizons for spin waves are interesting configurations as well and warrant future study.
For such scenarios, the present work can be extended to non-stationary ground states.

A central future application lies in the quantum simulation of cosmological particle production, for which proper experimentally observable correlation functions shall be defined.
Starting in the polar phase, analogue pair production of Proca quanta could be produced via magnetic field ramps that correspond to certain expansion or contraction scenarios.
Another possiblity lies in a quantum quench of the quadratic Zeeman-coefficient via off-resonant microwave pulses, which is a well-known technique to produce structures in spin-one quantum gases, resulting in spin-nematic squeezed states
Hamley et al. 2012
,
Kunkel et al. 2019
,
Kunkel et al. 2022
.
A prime candidate would be a quench from the polar to the easy-plane-polar phase
Lannig et al. 2023
,
Siovitz et al. 2024
, realizing production of
m_{F}=\pm 1
-pairs from
m_{F}=0
quasi-particle states.
The latter possesses an interesting description in terms of a double-sine-gordon model
Siovitz et al. 2024
, whose investigation in the current framework is left for future work.

In total, we believe that spinor Bose-Einstein condensates provide manifold possibilities to study analogue cosmology or gravity in more general terms.

V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}=-\bar{n}c_{1}(\lvert\xi_{+}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\xi_{-}\rvert^{2}+2\xi_{+}\xi_{-}),

(139)

to fulfill
eq.

136
.
Then, we must have
\imaginary(\xi_{+}\xi_{-})=0
since the left-hand-side of (
139
) must be real.

Furthermore one can choose
\bar{F}_{y}=0
due to azimutal symmetry, such that

eqs.

137
and
135
can be brought into the form

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}+q+2\bar{n}c_{0}\xi_{0}^{2})(\xi_{+}+\xi_{-})

\displaystyle\quad-(p-\bar{n}c_{1}\bar{f}_{z})(\xi_{+}-\xi_{-}),

(140)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}+q)(\xi_{+}-\xi_{-})

\displaystyle\quad-(p-\bar{n}c_{1}\bar{f}_{z})(\xi_{+}+\xi_{-}).

(141)

The polar phase is then characterized by the parameter configuration

\xi_{0}=1,\quad\xi_{\pm}=0,\quad\text{ with }\quad\left(V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}\right)=0,

(142)

can carries no background magnetization,
\bm{\bar{F}}=0
.
However, it still carries the magnetic energy
U_{\mathrm{mag}}=\bar{n}q
, due to quadrupolar order
Q_{zz}=2\bar{n}/3.

This phase occurs when the quadratic Zeeman coefficient fulfills

\begin{cases}q>2\lvert c_{1}\rvert\bar{n}&\text{for }c_{1}<0,\\
q\geq 0&\text{for }c_{1}=0,\\
q>0&\text{for }c_{1}>0,\end{cases}

(143)

and
p=0
can be taken without loss of generality due to magnetization conservation.

Ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic phase

Ferromagnetic phase

One possible solution to
eq.

136
is given by
\xi_{0}=0
, for which the remaining equations can be solved by

\displaystyle\xi_{+}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\chi_{+}},\quad\xi_{-}

\displaystyle=0,

(144)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}-p+q+\bar{n}c_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

i.e. the positively polarized case (
\bar{f}_{z}=1
), or

\displaystyle\xi_{+}=0,\quad\xi_{-}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\chi_{-}},

(145)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}+p+q-\bar{n}c_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

i.e. the negatively polarized case (
\bar{f}_{z}=-1
).
Here,
\chi_{\pm}
are arbitrary global phases. These states are referred to as ferromagnetic, since they entail
\bar{f}_{z}=\pm 1
.
The minimized magnetic energy per particle is

\frac{U_{\mathrm{mag}}}{\bar{n}}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{n}c_{1}\mp p+q,

(146)

where the upper sign corresponds to the positively polarized and the lower sign to the negatively polarized case.

Consequently, ferromagnetic interactions (
c_{1}<0
), which tend to maximize the total magnetization, prefer this phase.
The linear Zeeman term supports this cause, as it favours a magnetization in the direction of the external magnetic field,
whereas the quadratic Zeeman term favours magnetized state if
q<0
; and a magnetically neutral state if
q>0
, which results in a competition between
p
and
q
.

Hence, for
c_{1}\leq 0
, the ferromagnetic phase is constrained by the conditions

\begin{cases}p>0&\text{if }\quad q<0,\\
p>q&\text{if }\quad q\geq 0.\\
\end{cases}

(147)

If the linear Zeeman term becomes to weak (in absolute terms), a second-order phase transition into the broken-axisymmetric phase occurs for positive
q
.
For negative
q
, the system remains in the ferromagnetic phase unless
p=0
where a first-order phase transition into the polar phase takes place.

Interestingly, the ferromagnetic phase can also occur for anti-ferromagnetic interactions (
c_{1}>0
) if the linear Zeeman term is strong enough, i.e.

\begin{cases}p>c_{1}\bar{n}&\text{if }q<\bar{n}c_{1}/2,\\
p>q+c_{1}\bar{n}/2&\text{if }q\geq\bar{n}c_{1}/2.\end{cases}

(148)

If the linear Zeeman shift becomes weaker than the anti-ferrogmagnetic interactions,
p<=\bar{n}c_{1}
, a second-order phase transition into the anti-ferromagnetic phase occurs,
for small enough
q<\bar{n}c_{1}/2
. If
q\geq\bar{n}c_{1}/2
, the ferromagnetic phase transitions into the polar phase with a first-order boundary.

Antiferromagnetic phase

The other solution for the case
\xi_{0}=0
can be found by casting
eqs.

135
and
137
into the form

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left(V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}+q\right)(\xi_{+}-\xi_{-})

\displaystyle\quad-(p-\bar{n}c_{1}\bar{f}_{z})(\xi_{+}+\xi_{-}),

(149)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left(V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}+q\right)(\xi_{+}+\xi_{-})

\displaystyle\quad-(p-\bar{n}c_{1}\bar{f}_{z})(\xi_{+}-\xi_{-}).

(150)

To fulfill the coherent normalization constraint (
134
), we search for non-trivial solutions of
eqs.

149
and
150
.
Hence, we require that

\left(V_{\mathrm{ext}}-\mu+\bar{n}c_{0}+q\right)^{2}=(p-\bar{n}c_{1}\bar{f}_{z})^{2}=0,

(151)

where the first equality follows from squaring both equations.
Now, the spin components of interest follow from the normalization condition (
134
) and read

\xi_{+}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\chi_{+}}\sqrt{\frac{1+\bar{f}_{z}}{2}},\quad\xi_{-}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\chi_{-}}\sqrt{\frac{1-\bar{f}_{z}}{2}},

(152)

where
\bar{f}_{z}=p/\bar{n}c_{1}
and
\chi_{\pm}
are relative phases which are constrained by
\chi_{+}+\chi_{-}=\pi
to minimize the effective potential
Jacob et al. 2012
.
This solution is called the antiferromagnetic phase as both the
m_{F}=+1
and
m_{F}=-1
components are occupied.
Since the magnetic energy per particle is

\frac{U_{\mathrm{mag}}}{\bar{n}}=-\frac{p^{2}}{2\bar{n}c_{1}}+q

(153)

one has the phase boundaries

\begin{cases}\absolutevalue{p}\leq c_{1}\bar{n}&\text{for }q<0\\
\sqrt{2c_{1}\bar{n}q}\leq\absolutevalue{p}\leq c_{1}\bar{n}&\text{for }0\leq q\leq c_{1}\bar{n}/2.\\
\end{cases}

(154)

As a consequence, the anti-ferromagnetic phase can only occur for
c_{1}>0
, and only if the linear Zeeman shift is small enough (otherwise the system tends to the ferromagnetic state with a second-order boundary).
Similarly, if
q
becomes too large, the system transitions into the polar phase with a first-order boundary.

Appendix C
Perturbative scheme and identification of fluctuating fields

In the following, we identify fluctuations in the density via

n(\bar{\Phi}+\delta\Phi)\equiv\bar{n}+\delta n,

(155)

the spin-density via

F_{i}(\bar{\Phi}+\delta\Phi)\equiv\bar{F}_{i}+\delta F_{i},

(156)

and the spin-nematic tensor via

\mathcal{Q}_{ij}(\bar{\Phi}+\delta\Phi)\equiv\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}+\delta\mathcal{Q}_{ij}.

(157)

To that end we insert
\Phi=\bar{\Phi}+\delta\Phi
into
eqs.

4
,
5
and
6
and expand up to second order.
This yields

\bar{n}=\tr(\bar\Phi^* \bar\Phi),\quad\delta n=\delta n^{(1)}+\delta n^{(2)},

(158)

where

\displaystyle\delta n^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\tr(\delta\Phi^* \bar\Phi)+\tr(\bar\Phi^* \delta\Phi)

(159)

\displaystyle\delta n^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\tr(\delta\Phi^* \delta\Phi).

Furthermore we have

\bar{F}_{i}=\tr(\bar\Phi^* \sigma_i \bar\Phi),\quad\delta F_{i}=\delta F_{i}^{(1)}+\delta F_{i}^{(2)},

(160)

where

\displaystyle\delta F_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\tr(\delta\Phi^* \sigma_i \bar\Phi)+\tr(\bar\Phi^* \sigma_i \delta\Phi),

(161)

\displaystyle\delta F_{i}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\tr(\delta\Phi^* \sigma_i \delta\Phi).

Finally, one finds

\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}=\tr(\bar\Phi^* \sigma_i \bar\Phi\sigma_j^T)-\tfrac{1}{3}\bar{n}\delta_{ij},\quad\delta\mathcal{Q}_{ij}=\delta\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}^{(1)}+\delta\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}^{(2)}

(162)

where

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{Q}_{ij}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\tr(\delta\Phi^* \sigma_i \bar\Phi\sigma_j^T)+\tr(\bar\Phi^* \sigma_i \delta\Phi\sigma_j^T)-\tfrac{1}{3}\delta n^{(1)}\delta_{ij},

(163)

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{Q}_{ij}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\tr(\delta\Phi^* \sigma_i \delta\Phi\sigma_j^T)-\tfrac{1}{3}\delta n^{(2)}\delta_{ij}.

The linear fluctuations depend on the specific ground state configuration and shall be collected in the following.
The quadratic fluctuations are given thereafter. To evaluate the projections it is most convenient to represent the fluctuations in the basis of Pauli matrices, i.e.

\delta\Phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}(\phi_{+}+\phi_{-})\mathds{1}_{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}(\phi_{+}-\phi_{-})\sigma_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{0}\sigma_{x}.

(164)

Here, the density fluctuation reads

\displaystyle\delta n^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\bar{n}}\,\phi_{0}^{\mathrm{re}},

(165)

whereas the spin-density fluctuations are

\displaystyle\delta F_{x}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\bar{n}}(\phi_{-}^{\mathrm{re}}+\phi_{+}^{\mathrm{re}}),

(166)

\displaystyle\delta F_{y}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\bar{n}}(\phi_{-}^{\mathrm{im}}-\phi_{+}^{\mathrm{im}}),

(167)

\displaystyle\delta F_{z}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=0.

Proca fields minimally coupled to homegeneous and isotropic spacetimes

We consider a Proca field which is minimally coupled to a curved spacetime in terms of the effective action
\Gamma=\int\mathrm{d}^{D+1}x\sqrt{g}\mathcal{L}
, with the Lagrangian density
Graham et al. 2016
,
Heisenberg 2019

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}m_{A}^{2}g^{\mu\nu}A_{\mu}A_{\nu},

(221)

where we introduced the field strength tensor

F_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}A_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu},

(222)

and
\nabla_{\mu}
as the covariant derivative.
The equations of motion take the form

\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=m_{W}^{2}A^{\nu}

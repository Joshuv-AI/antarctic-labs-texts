Duality theory of tautological systems
Paul Görlach, Christian Sevenheck

Abstract
We discuss the holonomic dual of tautological systems, with a view towards applications to linear free divisors and to homogeneous spaces. As a technical tool, we consider a Chevalley–Eilenberg type complex, generalizing Euler–Koszul technology from the GKZ theory, and show equivariance and holonomicity of it.

Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to discuss the duality theory of some differential systems that are naturally attached to group actions on algebraic varieties. More precisely, we are concerned with the so-called
tautological systems
, which were first considered in
[
Hot98
]
, discussed from various points of views (especially towards applications to mirror symmetry) in
[
LSY13
,
BHLSY14
,
LY13
,
HLZ16
]
,
and then studied thoroughly in our previous paper
[
GRSSW23
]
. There, we were especially interested in Hodge theoretic aspects of tautological systems associated to homogeneous spaces. By the functoriality properties within the category of mixed Hodge modules, it is particularly important to understand the duality theory of such systems. In the present paper, we study the holonomic dual of general tautological systems in detail. Our results can be considered as a generalization of a similar study for the case where the group is an algebraic torus (leading to the well-known GKZ-systems), in that case, it is a result of Walther (see
[
Wal07
]
) that under some suitable hypotheses, the dual system is again a GKZ-system defined essentially by the same initial data.
One consequence of our findings is that such a direct expression of the holonomic dual holds essentially only if the dimension of the group coincides with that of the variety it acts on (which is obviously true in the toric case). In general, we give a cohomological expression of the holonomic dual (see
4.5
and
4.6
below).
If tautological systems are constructed functorially (as it is the case for those coming from homogeneous spaces, see
[
GRSSW23
]
), there is a natural morphism between this system and its dual (the direction of which depends on the precise construction by standard functors).

Let us give a more precise overview about the main results of this paper.
Throughout, we work over the field
\mathds{C}
. To a regular representation
\rho\colon G\to V
of a connected reductive algebraic group
G
, an orbit closure
\overline{Y}\subseteq V
and a Lie algebra homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
, one can associate the Fourier-transformed tautological system
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
. This is a
\mathscr{D}_{V}
-module with an explicit cyclic presentation given by the
\mathscr{D}_{V}
-ideal generated by the vanishing ideal of
\overline{Y}\subseteq V
and the vector fields on
V
induced by the group action of
G
with a twist given by
\beta
.
The actual tautological system (called
\tau(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
in
[
GRSSW23
]
) is the total Fourier-Laplace transformation of
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
, that is, a cyclic
\mathscr{D}_{V^{\vee}}
-module on the dual space
V^{\vee}
. Since the duality functor and the Fourier-Laplace transformation commute up to sign, it is essentially equivalent to describe
{\mathbb{D}}\tau(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
and

{\mathbb{D}}\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
. For this reason, we will in this paper consider almost exclusively the latter object.

Our main tool to describe the holonomic dual of
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
is a Chevalley–Eilenberg type complex, namely, we consider
\hat{T}^{\bullet}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=\mathscr{D}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\otimes\bigwedge^{-\bullet}\mathfrak{g}

with a differential depending on
\beta
.
We comment below in
Section

2
on how our complex relates to similar constructions in Lie algebra theory. Let us remark that in the toric case, our complex reduces to the Euler-Koszul complex studied in the theory of GKZ-systems (see
3.6
below).

One of our main results can be summarized as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(see
4.5
and
4.6
for more details)
.

The complex
\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
has cohomological amplitude in
\{n-m,\ldots,0\}
for
n:=\dim(\overline{Y})
,
m:=\dim G
. Moreover, we have

\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\cong H^{0}\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad\mathbb{D}\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\cong H^{n-m}\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},\tilde{\beta}),

where
\tilde{\beta}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism potentially different from
\beta
and the second isomorphism assumes moreover that
\overline{Y}\subseteq V
is Gorenstein (e.g. a complete intersection).

In particular, if
\dim(G)=\dim(\overline{Y})
, then
{\mathbb{D}}\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
is again a tautological system for
\rho
,
\overline{Y}
and
\tilde{\beta}
.

A special case where the assumptions of
3.9
are satisfied and where we have
\dim\overline{Y}=\dim(G)
is constructed from so-called linear free divisors. In this case, we use the general duality result to strengthen considerably the main result from
[
NMS19
]
, namely, we show that in this case
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
underly a complex mixed Hodge module for all but at most finitely many values of
\beta
(see
5.4
and
5.5
), and that moreover under a stronger assumption on
\beta
, the actual tautological system
\tau(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
associated to a given linear free divisor has an irreducible monodromy representation (
5.4
, 3.).

In
Section

5.2
we comment about a few more special cases where under additional assumptions we get sharper duality results. We show in particular that if
\dim(G)=\dim\overline{Y}+1
, then in some cases the dual of
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
is a again tautological system for
\rho
and
\overline{Y}
and a possibly different
\beta
.

One of the main motivations for our work come from a central result of
[
GRSSW23
]
(namely, Theorem 6.14 in loc.cit.), where we studied tautological systems defined by homogeneous spaces: The system
\tau(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
(the same statement holds for the system
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
) underlies a (in general complex) mixed Hodge module which has a weight filtration of length at most two. Therefore, it is of particular interest to understand the only possible non-trivial weight filtration step. By the functorial construction of
\hat{\tau}
(resp. of
\tau
) (see again Theorem 6.14, point 2. in loc.cit.), if the weight filtration is non-trivial (i.e., of length two) we have a duality morphism
\tau(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\rightarrow{\mathbb{D}}\tau(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
, and the non-trivial weight step is the kernel of this morphism. We postpone the study of this duality morphism to a subsequent paper, however, we do make in
Section

5.3
a few comments and conjectures about it.
It should be noticed that in the case where the group
G
is an algebraic torus, and when considering GKZ-systems defined by an action of a torus, this duality morphism has been computed explicitly in
[
RS17
, Lemma 2.12]
based on
[
Rei14
, Proposition 1.15]
. This computation is a key step in establishing a mirror symmetry statement for nef complete interse ctions in toric varieties using non-affine Landau-Ginzburg models (see
[
RS17
, Theorem 1.10]
).

2
Duality for Lie algebroids

In this section, we exhibit a general duality result in the realm of Lie algebroids, which we will in later sections apply to our specific situation.

2.1
Chevalley–Eilenberg complexes for Lie algebroids

We recall for the reader’s convenience basic facts about Lie algebroids and their Chevalley–Eilenberg complexes and fix notations. Most of them are easily found in the literature, see
[
Rin63
,
Che99
]
. We also prove a certain self-duality result on the level of the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex for Lie algebroids, for related statements, see e.g.
[
Hue99
]
.

We recall that a Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{E},[\cdot,\cdot],Z)
on a smooth complex variety
X
is an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{E}
equipped with a Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]\colon\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
(assumed to be
\mathds{C}
-bilinear, alternating and satisfying the Jacobi identity), making
\mathcal{E}
a sheaf of Lie algebras over
\mathds{C}
, and together with a map
Z\colon\mathcal{E}\to\Theta_{X}
, called the anchor map of
\mathcal{E}
, which is both a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules and a morphism of sheaves of Lie algebras over
\mathds{C}
. We will mostly work with Lie algebroids
(\mathcal{E},[\cdot,\cdot],Z)
such that the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{E}
is locally free of finite rank. We often speak of a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{E}
, with the data of the Lie bracket and the anchor map understood implicitly.

Given a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{E}
, there is a universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
. We will consider (left or right)
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-modules which will always be assumed to be
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-quasi-coherent. If
\mathcal{E}
is locally free of finite type as
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, a PBW-theorem holds for
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
(see, e.g.,
[
Rin63
, Theorem 3.1]
), i.e., there is canonical filtration on
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
such that its graded algebra is isomorphic to the symmetric algebra (over
\mathcal{O}_{X}
) of
\mathcal{E}
.
In particular, by standard arguments in homological algebra (see, e.g.,
[
NM15
, Appendix (A.25)]
or
[
Bjö93
, Appendix IV, Proposition 4.14]
),
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
has finite global dimension. Henceforth, when speaking of the derived category of
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-modules, we always mean the correponding bounded derived category.

For a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{E}
that is locally free of rank
m
as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, the line bundle
\omega_{\mathcal{E}}:=\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{m}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
has a right
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-module structure, see, e.g.
[
NM15
, Appendix (A.20)]
.
The tensor product over
\mathcal{O}_{X}
of two left (resp. a left and a right)
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-modules is again a left (resp. right)
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-module as in the theory of
\mathscr{D}
-modules, see e.g.
[
HTT08
, Proposition 1.2.9 and Proposition 1.2.10]
for the case of
\mathscr{D}
-modules or
[
NM15
]
or
[
Hue99
, (2.1) to (2.5)]
for more general Lie algebroids. Similarly,
\sheafHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
of two left (or two right)
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-modules
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{N}
is a left
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-module.

In particular, if
\mathcal{M}
is a left
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-module, then
\omega_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
is a right
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-module. Conversely, if
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is a right
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-module, then
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}
is a left
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-module.

For any Lie algebroid
\mathcal{E}
which is locally free of finite rank
m
over
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, with universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
, we consider the duality functor

\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{U}}:=R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}}(\cdot,\mathcal{U})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}[m]

in the derived category of left
\mathcal{U}
-modules.
While in this section, we deal with general Lie algebroids, we will later apply the discussion here to one of the following examples:

Example 2.1
.

If
X
is a point, then a Lie algebroid on
X
is nothing but a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathds{C}
. The universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
as a Lie algebroid over a point is the universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
as a Lie algebra.

Example 2.2
.

For any smooth complex variety
X
, the tangent bundle on
X
is a Lie algebroid with the anchor map being the identity map. This Lie algebroid is locally free of rank
\dim X
. Its universal enveloping algebra is
\mathcal{U}(\Theta_{X})=\mathscr{D}_{X}
. In this case, we denote the duality functor
\mathbb{D}_{\mathscr{D}_{X}}
simply by
\mathbb{D}
.

Notice that, already from these two basic examples, modules over universal enveloping algebras of Lie algebroids can be seen as a uniformizing framework including as special cases both
\mathfrak{g}
-modules and
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules.

Example 2.3
.

If
G
is a connected linear algebraic group acting on
X
, then
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebroid on
X
with bracket given by

[f\otimes\xi,f^{\prime}\otimes\xi^{\prime}]:=fZ(\xi)(f^{\prime})\otimes\xi^{\prime}-f^{\prime}Z(\xi^{\prime})(f)\otimes\xi,\qquad f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{X},\;\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}

and the anchor map given by
f\otimes\xi\mapsto fZ_{X}(\xi)
, where
Z_{X}(\xi)
is the vector field on
X
induced by
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and the group action of
G
on
X
. Explicitly,
Z_{X}(\xi)
is the vector field that associates to a point
y\in X
the tangent vector
\differential\varphi_{y}(\xi)
, which is the image of
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}=T_{1}G
under the differential of the morphism
\varphi_{y}\colon G\to X
,
g\mapsto g^{-1}\cdot y
. Note that
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
is free of rank
\dim G
as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. We denote the universal enveloping algebra as

\mathcal{A}_{X}^{G}:=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}),

with multiplication given by

(f\otimes\xi)\cdot(f^{\prime}\otimes\xi^{\prime})=ff^{\prime}\otimes\xi\xi^{\prime}+fZ_{X}(\xi)(f^{\prime})\otimes\xi^{\prime}\qquad f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{X},\;\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}.

When no confusion can arise, we may drop the superindex
G
and simply write
\mathcal{A}_{X}
. The anchor map extends to a homomorphism of
\mathds{C}
-algebras
\mathcal{A}_{X}\to\mathscr{D}_{X}
of the universal enveloping algebras.

Example 2.4
.

In the previous example, the kernel of the anchor map
Z\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\Theta_{X}
is a Lie algebroid on
X
with the bracket inherited from
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
and the trivial anchor map. If the action of
G
on
X
is transitive, then
Z\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\Theta_{X}
is a surjection of locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules of rank
\dim G
and
\dim X
, respectively, hence this Lie algebroid is a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module of rank
\dim G-\dim X
. In general, this Lie algebroid is not of the form of
2.3
, which in particular will mean that the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex considered below does in this case not simply boil down to the classical Chevalley–Eilenberg complex of Lie algebras.

We next consider the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex of a Lie algebroid.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Lie algebroid on a connected smooth complex variety
X
and assume
\mathcal{E}
is locally free of finite rank
m
as a
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. Denote by
\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
the corresponding universal enveloping algebra. Moreover, let
\mathcal{R}
be any sheaf of associative
{\mathds{C}}
-algebras on
X
.
For any
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{U})
-bimodule
\mathcal{N}
(this means in particular that the left
\mathcal{R}
-structure and the right
\mathcal{U}
-structure on
\mathcal{N}
commute), define the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex
\mathcal{S}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{R}|\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{N})

with

\mathcal{S}^{-\ell}_{\mathcal{R}|\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{N}):=\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\ell}\mathcal{E}

(note that
\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\mathcal{R}|\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{N})=0
for
k\notin\{-m,\dots,0\}
)
and differential

\displaystyle\delta^{-\ell}\colon\qquad\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\ell}\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\ell-1}\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle n\otimes\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{\ell}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{i+1}(n\cdot\xi_{i})\otimes\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{\xi_{i}}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{\ell}

\displaystyle\phantom{\mapsto{}}+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}n\otimes[\xi_{i},\xi_{j}]\wedge\xi_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{\xi_{i}}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{\xi_{j}}\wedge\dots\wedge\xi_{\ell}.

Then
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{R}|\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{N})
is a complex of left
\mathcal{R}
-modules (one checks that
\delta^{-\ell}\circ\delta^{-\ell+1}=0
). One can prove that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{R}|\mathcal{U}}
is an exact functor from the abelian category of
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{U})
-bimodules to the abelian category of complexes of left
\mathcal{R}
-modules.

Remark 2.6
.

The above construction applies in particular if
\mathcal{N}
is only a right
\mathcal{U}
-module, in which case we put
\mathcal{R}:={\mathds{C}}:=\underline{{\mathds{C}}}_{X}
. Then we recover the construction from

[
GRSSW23
, page 46]
, i.e.
\mathcal{S}^{\bullet}_{{\mathds{C}}|\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{N})
is a complex of (sheaves of)
{\mathds{C}}
-vector spaces only.

However, for us, the main focus of the above construction is when we start with two locally free Lie algebroids
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
, with ranks
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, respectively.
Writing
\mathcal{U}_{1}:=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{1})
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}:=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E}_{2})
for the corresponding universal envelopping algebras, we then get for a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodule
\mathcal{N}
the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex
\mathcal{S}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}|\mathcal{U}_{2}}(\mathcal{N})
, which is a complex of left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-modules.

Notice that a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodule
\mathcal{N}
can naturally be seen as a left
\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{2}^{op}
-module, and again by a PBW-type theorem, the sheaf of rings
\mathcal{U}_{1}^{op}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{2}
has finite global homological dimension.

In the case of
2.3
, when we consider a Lie algebroid of the form
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
for a
G
-action on
X
, we have the following comparision result relating the above complex with the corresponding object in the theory of Lie algebras.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a connected linear algebraic group acting on a connected smooth complex variety
X
and consider the Lie algebroid
\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
with universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
.
Let
\mathcal{N}
be a
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{U})
-bimodule. Then we have an isomorphism of complexes of left
\mathcal{R}
-modules

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{S}^{-\bullet}_{\mathcal{R}|\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{N}),\ \delta}}\right)\cong\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{N}\otimes\bigwedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g},\ d}}\right)=:{\mathcal{C}\!\mathcal{E}}^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathcal{N})

where the right hand side is the standard Chevalley–Eilenberg complex for
right
modules over Lie algebras (as in
[
Wei94
, Corollary 7.7.3]
).

Proof.

One immediately checks that the isomorphism

\mathcal{S}^{-\ell}_{\mathcal{R}|\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{N})\cong\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\ell}(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\bigwedge^{\ell}\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathcal{N}\otimes\bigwedge^{\ell}\mathfrak{g}

is compatible with the differential.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

Notice that in particular that, for

Remark 2.9
.

In the very simple case where

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Lie algebroid on
X
, locally free of finite rank as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, let
\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a left
\mathcal{U}
-module and consider
\mathcal{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
with the natural
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U})
-bimodule structure given by

\xi\cdot(u\otimes m)=(\xi u)\otimes m,\quad(u\otimes m)\cdot\xi=(u\xi)\otimes m-u\otimes(\xi\cdot m)\quad\text{for }\xi\in\mathcal{E},\ u\in\mathcal{U},\ m\in\mathcal{M}.

Then
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{U}|\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M})
is a resolution of
\mathcal{M}
by left
\mathcal{U}
-modules.

Proof.

By
2.12
,
S_{\mathcal{U}|\mathcal{U}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{N})
represents
(\mathcal{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-locally free (so that obviously
\mathcal{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{M}
), this is by
2.10
isomorphic to
\mathcal{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathbb{L}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{M})\cong\mathcal{M}
.
∎

Notice that the statement of the previous lemma can also directly be proven by considering the filtration on
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{M}
induced by the PBW-filtration on
\mathcal{U}
and passing to the graded objects, see
[
GRSSW23
, Proof of Lemma 6.9]
. Then we obtain
2.11
as a special case when
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

We next observe see a property that can be seen as a certain self-duality of the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex:

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodule and let
\mathcal{K}
be a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{R})
-bimodule for some sheaf of
\mathds{C}
-algebras
\mathcal{R}
.
There is a natural isomorphism of right
\mathcal{R}
-modules

\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(S_{\mathcal{U}_{1}|\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{K})[m_{2}]\cong\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathds{C}}|\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\bullet}(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{K})).

In the derived category of right
\mathcal{R}
-modules, we obtain an isomorphism

R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{K})[m_{2}]\cong(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\mathbb{L}}R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{K}))\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}_{X}.

Proof.

For the first claim, in terms of objects of the complex, in cohomological degree
-\ell
, we have

\displaystyle\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}|\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{-(m_{2}-\ell)}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle=\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}\bigg(\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{m_{2}-\ell}\mathcal{E}_{2},\mathcal{K}\bigg)

\displaystyle\cong\sheafHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\bigg(\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{m_{2}-\ell}\mathcal{E}_{2},\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{K})\bigg)

\displaystyle\cong\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{m_{2}-\ell}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\cong\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{m_{2}}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}\otimes\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\ell}\mathcal{E}_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\cong\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\ell}\mathcal{E}_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{S}_{\mathds{C}|\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{-\ell}(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{K})).

It is a tedious, yet straightforward exercise to check that these isomorphisms are compatible with the differentials of the complex. (One needs to be careful to choose the isomorphism

\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{m_{2}-\ell}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}\cong\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{m_{2}}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{\ell}\mathcal{E}_{2}

with a suitable sign convention.)

The second claim follows from the first one by replacing
\mathcal{N}
by a finite projective resolution of itself as a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodule and applying
2.12
. Note that projective
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodules are in particular projective as left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-modules.
∎

2.2
Duality of
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
-modules

In the following, we consider two Lie algebroids
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
on
X
locally free over
\mathcal{O}
of finite ranks
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, respectively. Let
\mathcal{U}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}
denote their universal enveloping algebras. Recall that
\omega_{i}:=\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}}^{m_{i}}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{\vee}
has a right
\mathcal{U}_{i}
-module structure (given by the negated Lie derivative).

We consider the following double side-changing operation on bimodules: If
\mathcal{M}
is a
(\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{U}_{1})
-bimodule, then
\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}
is a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodule. Here, note that the first tensor product uses the
\mathcal{O}
-module structure via
\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{U}_{2}
and the left module structure of
\mathcal{M}
and the second tensor product uses the
\mathcal{O}
-module structure via
\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{U}_{1}
and the right module structure of
\mathcal{M}
. In general, when working with
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodules, we always write tensor products in an order that makes implicitly clear which
\mathcal{O}
-module structure is being used.

It is straightforward to check the following basic property:

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
a
(\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{U}_{1})
-bimodule, let
\mathcal{M}
be a left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
a left
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-module. There are natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee})

\displaystyle\text{and }\qquad\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{N})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\qquad\phantom{\text{and }}

of
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodules.

If
\mathcal{N}
is a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodule, then
\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{1})
naturally inherits a
(\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{U}_{1})
-bimodule structure from the right
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-module structure on
\mathcal{N}
and the right
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module structure on
\mathcal{U}_{1}
. We obtain from
2.14
the following duality property:

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodule.
There is an isomorphism of complexes of left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-modules:

\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{S}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}|\mathcal{U}_{2}}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{U}_{1})[m_{2}]\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}|\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\bullet}(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}).

This gives the following duality result in the derived category of left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-modules:

\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}_{X})\cong(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U}_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{1}^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}_{X}[m_{1}-m_{2}].

Proof.

This follows from
2.14
for
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{U}_{1}
considered as a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{1})
-bimodule, and passing from right
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-modules to left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-modules by taking the tensor product with
\omega_{1}^{\vee}
.
∎

Assume now that there is a homomorphism of Lie algebroids
\mathcal{E}_{2}\to\mathcal{E}_{1}
. This induces a
\mathds{C}
-algebra homomorphism
\mathcal{U}_{2}\to\mathcal{U}_{1}
between their universal enveloping algebras.
In particular, every (left or right)
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module may be viewed as a (left or right)
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-module via
\mathcal{U}_{2}\to\mathcal{U}_{1}
. On the other hand, for a left
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, we may consider
\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{M}
in the derived category of
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-modules. This object is represented by the complex
\mathcal{S}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}|\mathcal{U}_{2}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{M})
, because

\mathcal{S}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}|\mathcal{U}_{2}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{M})\cong(\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}\cong(\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{M},

where the first isomorphism is due to
2.12
and the second isomorphism is due to
2.10
.

Remark 2.17
.

An example of the construction just mentioned is when
D\subseteq X
is a reduced divisor, and when
\mathcal{E}_{2}:=\Der(-\log\,D)
is the sheaf of logarithmic vector fields. Since our general assumption here is that all Lie algebroids are locally free as
\mathcal{O}
-modules, we have to restrict to the case where
D
is a free divisor in the sense of K. Saito. Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}:=\Der_{X}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{1}
be the anchor map of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
. Then
\mathcal{U}_{2}=\mathscr{D}_{X}(-\log\,D)
. Suppose that we are given an integrable logarithmic connection
\mathcal{M}
, i.e., a left
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-module which is also locally free of finite rank as
\mathcal{O}
-module. Then our construction is exactly the situation studied in, e.g.
[
CMNM05
,
CMNM09
]
. In particular,
\mathcal{M}
is called admissible in
[
CMNM05
, Définition 1.2.2]
if
\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}\mathcal{M}
is concentrated in degree
0
and if it is a holonomic
\mathcal{U}_{1}
(
=\mathscr{D}_{X}
)-module.

In this context, the right
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module
\omega_{1}
can also be viewed as a right
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-module via
\mathcal{U}_{2}\to\mathcal{U}_{1}
, which together with the right
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-module structure on
\omega_{2}
makes

\omega_{1|2}:=\sheafHom_{\mathcal{O}}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

a left
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-module. This is the relative dualizing module in
[
NM15
, Appendix, A.28]
.

Through
\mathcal{U}_{2}\to\mathcal{U}_{1}
, the algebra
\mathcal{U}_{1}
is in particular a
(\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{U}_{1})
-bimodule or a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodule. In fact, these two bimodule structures are related to each other under double side-changing via
\omega_{1|2}
as follows:

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{2}\to\mathcal{E}_{1}
be as above.
Then there is an isomorphism of
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodules

\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1|2},

where
\mathcal{U}_{1}
is on the left viewed as a
(\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{U}_{1})
-bimodule and on the right as a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodule.

Proof.

We first consider the case
\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}_{2}
. Note that
\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}
is a left
\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module, i.e., it carries two commuting left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module structures: one via left-multiplication on
\mathcal{U}_{1}
and the other results from the right-to-left transformation
(\cdot)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}
applied to the right
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module structure on
\mathcal{U}_{1}
. Note that when we write
\omega_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega^{\vee}
, we mean applying the left-to-right transformation
\omega_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\cdot)
to the
first
mentioned left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module structure. However, there is an automorphism of
\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}
interchanging the two left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module structures, given by
P\otimes m\mapsto P\cdot(1\otimes m)
. (See, e.g.,
[
NM15
, Corollary A.12]
for the analogous statement for right modules.) Hence, we may as well apply the left-to-right transformation
\omega_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\cdot)
to the
second
mentioned left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module structure that arose from a right-to-left transformation. Since the right-to-left and the left-to-right transformations are inverse to each other, this shows
\omega_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{U}_{1}
.

For the general case, we observe that
\omega_{2}\cong\omega_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1|2}
as right
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-modules.
Therefore, as right
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-modules, we obtain

\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}\cong(\omega_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1|2})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee})\cong(\omega_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1|2}\cong\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1|2}

and we observe that these isomorphisms are also compatible with the left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-module structure, giving the claimed isomorphism of
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodules.
∎

More generally, we get:

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{2}\to\mathcal{E}_{1}
be as above and let
\mathcal{M}
be a left
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-module.
Then there is an isomorphism of
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodules

\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{U}_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1|2},

where
\mathcal{U}_{1}
is on the left viewed as a
(\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{U}_{1})
-bimodule and on the right as a
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})
-bimodule.

Proof.

We have

\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{U}_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee}\cong(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1}^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{M}\cong\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1|2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{M},

where the first isomorphism is from
2.15
and the second one from
2.18
.
∎

The following is a consequence of the (self-)duality result for Chevalley-Eilenberg complexes (i.e.
2.14
). We would like to point out that the duality result from
[
NM15
, Theorem A.32]
expresses the duality functor
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(-)
using

\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}(-)
(and is therefore rather a extension of scalar type property) whereas our result expresses
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(-)
using
R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-,\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a left
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-module that is coherent as
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. Let
\mathcal{E}_{2}\to\mathcal{E}_{1}
be a Lie algebroid morphism, inducing a homomorphism
\mathcal{U}_{2}\to\mathcal{U}_{1}
that makes
\mathcal{U}_{1}
a
\mathcal{U}_{2}
-algebra.
Then

\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{M})\cong\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}(R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\omega_{1|2})[m_{1}-m_{2}]

in the derived category of left
\mathcal{U}_{1}
-modules.

Proof.

Note that there is a natural morphism

R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{U}_{1}\to R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{U}_{1})

in the derived category of
(\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{U}_{1})
-bimodules. This is an isomorphism, as can be checked on the level of the underlying objects in the derived category of
\mathcal{O}
-modules—there, this is a consequence of the
\mathcal{O}
-coherence assumption on
\mathcal{M}
. Now, we have natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}((\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{M})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O})

(
2.10
)

\displaystyle\cong(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{M},\mathcal{U}_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}\omega_{1}^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}[m_{1}-m_{2}]

(
2.16
)

\displaystyle\cong(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{U}_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}\omega_{1}^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}[m_{1}-m_{2}]

(tensor-hom adjunction)

\displaystyle\cong(\omega_{2}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}(R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{\mathcal{O}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{U}_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}\omega_{1}^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}[m_{1}-m_{2}]

(
\mathcal{O}
-coherence of
\mathcal{M}
)

\displaystyle\cong(\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathbb{L}}\omega_{1|2})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}[m_{1}-m_{2}]

(
2.19
)

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{U}_{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}_{2}}^{\mathbb{L}}(R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{\mathcal{O}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{\mathcal{O}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\omega_{1|2})[m_{1}-m_{2}]

(
2.10
)

Taking
\mathcal{U}_{1}=\mathcal{U}_{2}
, this leads to the following:

Corollary 2.21
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-coherent left
\mathcal{U}
-module.
Then

\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{M})\cong R\!\sheafHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{X})

in the derived category of left
\mathcal{U}
-modules.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
2.20
and
2.18
.
∎

This corollary is a generalization of
[
Che99
, Proposition 3.2.1]
, in the sense that in loc. cit. it is assumed that
\mathcal{M}
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-locally free, whereas we only need
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-coherence.
Note that for
\mathcal{U}=\mathscr{D}_{X}
, assuming
\mathcal{M}
to be
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-coherent obviously implies that it is an integrable connection, in which case
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{M})\cong\sheafHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{X})
(see, e.g.,
[
HTT08
, Example 2.6.10.]
). However, for other Lie algebroids, there are cases of
\mathcal{O}
-coherent left
\mathcal{U}
-modules
\mathcal{M}
which are not
\mathcal{O}
-locally free, and, consequently, for which
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{M})
is not a single
\mathcal{U}
-module in cohomological degree
0
.

3
Equivariant aspects

In this section, we study group actions on smooth varieties which determines a morphism of Lie algebroids as in
2.3
. The results from
Section

2
can then be made more explicit. Along the way, we consider equivariance properties of the complexes involved and deduce a holonomicity criterion.

3.1
Holonomicity

Throughout, we consider the action of a connected linear algebraic group
G
on a smooth connected variety
X
. We introduce a complex
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M},\beta)
of
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules associated to a
G
-equivariant quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}
and a Lie algebra homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
, where
\mathfrak{g}
denotes the Lie algebra of
G
.

For this, we consider the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes U(\mathfrak{g})
as in
2.3
and in
2.7
. We view the
G
-equivariant quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}
as a left
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-module through the
\mathfrak{g}
-action obtained by differentiating. Explicitly, the
G
-equivariance of
\mathcal{M}
is given by an isomorphism
\varphi\colon\act^{*}\mathcal{M}\to\pr_{2}^{*}\mathcal{M}
, where
\act\colon G\times X\to X
is given by
(g,y)\mapsto g\cdot y
. Any
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
corresponds to a morphism
i_{\xi}\colon\Spec\mathds{C}[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{2}\to G
. Then pulling back
\varphi
under
i_{\xi}\times\id_{X}\colon\Spec\mathds{C}[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{2}\times X\to G\times X
yields an isomorphism

(i_{\xi}\times\id_{X})^{*}\varphi\colon\mathds{C}[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{2}\otimes\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathds{C}[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{2}\otimes\mathcal{M}.

The image of
1\otimes m
under this isomorphism is of the form
1\otimes m-\varepsilon\otimes m^{\prime}
. Then
\xi\cdot m:=m^{\prime}
defines the action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathcal{M}
which gives the
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-module structure on
\mathcal{M}
.

In the following, let
G
be a connected linear algebraic group with Lie group
\mathfrak{g}
, let
X
be a connected smooth
G
-variety and let
\mathcal{M}
be a
G
-equivariant quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. Let
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
be a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Notation 3.1
.

For connected linear algebraic group
G
acting on a smooth complex variety
X
, a Lie algebra homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
and any left
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{N}
, we denote

\mathcal{O}_{X}\{\beta\}:=\mathcal{A}_{X}/\mathcal{A}_{X}(\xi-\beta(\xi)\mid\xi\in\mathfrak{g})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{N}\{\beta\}:=\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\{\beta\}.

Definition 3.2
.

For a
G
-equivariant quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}
(which we may view as a left
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-module) and Lie algebra homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
, we denote

4
Tautological systems

To a regular representation
\rho\colon G\to V
of a connected linear algebraic group
G
, the closure
\overline{Y}\subseteq V
of a
G
-orbit
Y
and a Lie algebra homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
, one associates the following cyclic left
\mathscr{D}_{V}
-module, the
Fourier-transformed tautological system
:

\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta):=\mathscr{D}_{V}/(\mathscr{D}_{V}\mathcal{I}_{\overline{Y}}+\mathscr{D}_{V}(Z_{V}(\xi)-\beta^{\prime}(\xi)\mid\xi\in\mathfrak{g})),

where
\mathcal{I}_{\overline{Y}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
is the ideal sheaf of
\overline{Y}\subseteq V
and
\beta^{\prime}:=\trace\circ\differential\rho-\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
.

Notation 4.1
.

For a fixed representation
\rho\colon G\to V
, we use throughout the notation

\beta^{\prime}:=\trace\circ\differential\rho-\beta

for any Lie algebra homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
.

This notation originates from the fact that the operator
Z_{V}(\xi)-\beta^{\prime}(\xi)
is the negated Fourier transform on
V
of the operator
Z_{V}(\xi)-\beta(\xi)
; those operators show up in the definition of tautological systems
\tau(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=\FL(\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta))
. Notice that because of
\FL\circ\mathbb{D}_{\mathscr{D}_{V}}=-\mathbb{D}_{\mathscr{D}_{V}}\circ\FL
, studying the dual of a tautological system or of its Fourier-transform are equivalent problems, and we choose to wwork with the Fourier-transformed tautological system
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
.

Note that, using the
G
-action on
V
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{D}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{V}}\mathcal{A}_{V}/(\mathcal{A}_{V}\mathcal{I}_{\overline{Y}}+\mathcal{A}_{V}(\xi-\beta^{\prime}(\xi)\mid\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\cong\mathscr{D}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{V}}((\mathcal{A}_{V}/\mathcal{A}_{V}\mathcal{I}_{\overline{Y}})\{\beta^{\prime}\}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{V}}\mathcal{O}_{V})

\displaystyle\cong\mathscr{D}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{V}}((\mathcal{A}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\{\beta^{\prime}\})\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{V}}\mathcal{O}_{V})

\displaystyle\cong\mathscr{D}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{V}}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\{\beta^{\prime}\},

where the last isomorphism is due to
2.10
.

Definition 4.2
.

For
\rho\colon G\to V
,
\overline{Y}\subseteq V
and
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
as above and using
4.1
, we define the derived Fourier-transformed tautological system

\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta):=\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}},\beta^{\prime})=\mathcal{S}^{\bullet}_{\mathscr{D}_{V}|\mathcal{A}_{V}}(\mathscr{D}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\{\beta^{\prime}\})\in D_{qc}^{b}(\mathscr{D}_{V}),

where the definition of
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}
(and
\mathcal{A}_{V}
) implicitly relies on the
G
-action on
V
given by
\rho
.

By
2.12
,
in the derived category
D_{qc}^{b}(\mathscr{D}_{V})
of left
\mathscr{D}_{V}
-modules, we have

\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\cong(\mathscr{D}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\{\beta^{\prime}\})\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{V}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}_{V}\cong\mathscr{D}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\{\beta^{\prime}\}

where the last isomorphism is due to

.
In particular:

\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=H^{0}\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta),

which is also easily checked from the definition of

From
3.4

Lemma 4.4

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[-\differential\rho(\xi)_{ii}x_{i}\partial_{i},\partial_{i}]\wedge\dots\wedge\partial_{N}

\displaystyle=\trace(\differential\rho(\xi))\ (\partial_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\partial_{N}),

5
Applications and Examples

In the setup of the previous section, we can give more precise information on the dual of a tautological system under some assumptions on the dimension of the group
G
compared to the dimension of the (closure of the)
G
-orbit
\overline{Y}
used in the definition of the tautological system. We list below a few cases of interest.

5.1
Case
\dim(G)=\dim(\overline{Y})

In this case, we obtain the most satisfying result as a direct consequence of
4.6
:

Corollary 5.1
.

Assume that
\overline{Y}\subseteq V
is Gorenstein with

\omega_{\overline{Y}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\{-\gamma\}
and such that
\dim(G)=\dim(\overline{Y})
. Assume further that
\overline{Y}
consists of finitely many
G
-orbits. Then we have

\mathbb{D}\,\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\trace\circ\ad+\gamma-\beta).

We observe that the class of GKZ-systems given by a matrix of full row-rank fall into this case where the dimension of the group and the orbit agree.
5.1
generalizes the duality result for GKZ-systems
[
Wal07
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Application:
As a specific example of the construction described above, we consider a situation arising when studying so-called linear free divisors. We refer specifically to the setup of
[
NMS19
, Section 4]
. Namely, consider a reductive algebraic group acting prehomogeneously on a complex vector space
V_{0}
(and denote by
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
a coordinate system relative to a chosen basis of
V_{0}
), meaning that there is a dense open orbit in
V_{0}
. Additionally, we require that the stabilizers for all points of this open orbit are finite groups. Let
D
be the complement of the open orbit, and assume that
D\subseteq V
is a divisor. The group we started with can then be characterized as
G_{D}:=\{g\in\GL(V)\,|\,gD=D\}
.
We also have
\dim(G_{D})=\dim(V_{0})=n
. Moreover, let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}_{D}
be a basis of the Lie algebra of
G_{D}
, then let
A=(a_{ij})\in\textup{Mat}(n\times n,{\mathds{C}}[V]_{1}
) such that

Z_{V}(\xi_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}
(where
Z_{V}
is the notation for the anchor map used in
2.3
). Then we require that
the determinant
f:=\det(A)\in\mathcal{O}_{V}
is reduced (it is then automatically a reduced equation of
D
, and necessarily we have that
f\in{\mathds{C}}[V]_{n}
). Under these conditions, we have that the module of vector fields
\Theta_{V}(-\log\,D)
logarithmic along
D
is free, and
D
is called a linear free divisor (it is a free divisor in the sense of K. Saito, see
[
Sai80
]
, and it is called linear free since the polynomials
a_{ij}
are linear forms on
V
). We will later further restrict to the class of so-called strongly Koszul (SK) free divisors, which in the current situation can simply be characterized by saying that the action of
G_{D}
on
V
has finitely many orbits.

We briefly recall some of the main constructions in
[
NMS19
]
. First notice that there is a subgroup
G_{0}\subset G_{D}
(with
\dim(G_{0})=n-1
) consisting of all linear transformations that stabilizes all fibres of
f
(which was called
A_{D}
in loc.cit.). Then we put
G:={\mathds{C}}^{*}\times G_{0}
,
V={\mathds{C}}\times V_{0}
and we consider the extended action

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\rho:G&\longrightarrow&\Aut(V)\\
\\
(t,g)&\longmapsto&(v\mapsto t\cdot\rho_{0}(g)(v)),\end{array}

where
\rho_{0}
is the restriction of the original prehomogeneous action to
G_{0}\subset G_{D}
. Let
Y_{0}:=f^{-1}(t)
for
t\in{\mathds{C}}^{*}
, then
Y_{0}
is a (closed)
G_{0}
-orbit. Let
Y:=C(Y_{0})
be its cone in
{\mathds{C}}^{*}\times V_{0}
, i.e.
Y=\rho(G)(1,p)
for any point
p\in Y_{0}
.
In particular, we have an isomorphism
\iota:{\mathds{C}}^{*}\times Y_{0}\rightarrow Y
,
(t,y)\mapsto(t,t\cdot y)
.
Then
Y
is a (usually non-closed)
G
-orbit, and we let
\overline{Y}\subset V
be its closure. Clearly, the boundary
\partial Y
is contained in
\{0\}\times V
. The main results of
[
NMS19
]
then concerns the tautological system
\tau(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
. For simplicity, we will assume that
G_{0}
is semi-simple, which then implies
that
\trace\circ\ad=0
and that
\beta_{|\mathfrak{g}_{0}}=0
, where
\mathfrak{g}={\mathds{C}}\mathbf{e}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. We cite the following result (notice that the various shifts and sign differences occuring here are due to the change of convention for the Euler field
Z_{V}(\mathbf{e})
and for the definition of the module
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathds{C}}^{*}}^{\beta(\mathbf{e})}
)

Theorem 5.2

(
[
NMS19
, Proposition 4.5]
)
.

Let
D\subset V
be a SK linear free divisor defined by a group action
G_{D}\rightarrow\GL(V)
. Let
\rho:G:={\mathds{C}}^{*}\times G_{0}\rightarrow\GL(V)
be as above, and denote by
k:Y\hookrightarrow V
the locally closed embedding of the orbit
G
-orbit
Y
into
V
. Then if

\beta(\mathbf{e})\notin n\cdot(1+\roots(b_{D}))+{\mathds{Z}}_{>0}
, we have

\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\cong(k\circ\iota)_{+}(\mathcal{O}^{-\beta(\mathbf{e})}_{{\mathds{C}}^{*}}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}),

where
\mathcal{O}^{-\beta(\mathbf{e})}_{{\mathds{C}}^{*}}:=\mathscr{D}_{{\mathds{C}}^{*}}/(t\partial_{t}-\beta(\mathbf{e}))
. In particular,

\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
then underlies a complex Hodge module on
V
, i.e., an object of
\textup{MHM}(V,{\mathds{C}})
,
which is an element in
\textup{MHM}(V)
, i.e. a rational Hodge module on
V
, if
\beta(\mathbf{e})\in{\mathds{Z}}
.

From the basic functorial properties of the holonomic duality functor, we obtain the following consequence.

Corollary 5.3
.

Under the hypotheses of the previous theorem, we have

{\mathbb{D}}_{\mathscr{D}_{V}}\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\cong(k\circ\iota)_{\dagger}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}^{\beta(\mathbf{e})}_{{\mathds{C}}^{*}}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}}}\right).

If moreover
\beta(\mathbf{e})\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathds{Z}}
, then there is a morphism

{\mathbb{D}}_{\mathscr{D}_{V}}\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\longrightarrow\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta).

Proof.

If
\beta(\mathbf{e})\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathds{Z}}
, then
\mathcal{O}^{\beta(\mathbf{e})}_{{\mathds{C}}^{*}}\cong\mathcal{O}^{-\beta(\mathbf{e})}_{{\mathds{C}}^{*}}
, and the morphism is then simply given by the forgetful morphism from the properly supported to the ordinary direct image.
∎

Combining this last statement with our duality result
5.1
then yields the following.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
D\subset V
be an SK-linear free divisor, and let
G
,
\rho
,
\overline{Y}
be as above. Then

1.

If
\beta(\mathbf{e})\notin n\cdot(1+\roots(b_{D}))+{\mathds{Z}}_{\leq 0}
, then

\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=(k\circ\iota)_{\dagger}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}^{-\beta(\mathbf{e})}_{{\mathds{C}}^{*}}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}}}\right).

In particular, under this assumption, we also obtain that
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
underlies an object in
\textup{MHM}(V,{\mathds{C}})
, and in
\textup{MHM}(V)
if
\beta(\mathbf{e})\in{\mathds{Z}}
.

2.

If
\beta(\mathbf{e})\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathds{Z}}\,\backslash\,(n\cdot(1+\roots(b_{D}))+{\mathds{Z}}_{>0})
, then the natural duality morphism from
5.3
is expressed as

\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},1-\beta)\longrightarrow\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta).

3.

If
\beta(\mathbf{e})\notin n\cdot(1+\roots(b_{D}))+{\mathds{Z}}
, then
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
underlies a simple pure complex Hodge module on
V
. The total Fourier-Laplace transform
\tau(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
(i.e. the actual tautological system) has therefore has irreducible monodromy representation.

Proof.

1.

By
5.1
, and under our assumptions (using
4.7
), we have

\mathbb{D}_{\mathscr{D}_{V}}\,\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},1-\beta),

(5.1.1)

where
1-\beta
means the Lie algebra homomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
trivial on
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and given on
\mathbf{e}
by
1-\beta(\mathbf{e})
.
Then the statement follows from

by rewriting the condition on

as a condition on
\beta(\mathbf{e})
, and using the symmetry around

shown in
[

]
.

2.

Follows by combining the second statement in

with
Eq.

.

3.

Under the assumptions made,
we have that

\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=(k\circ\iota)_{{\dagger}\,+}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{O}^{-\beta(\mathbf{e})}_{{\mathds{C}}^{*}}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}}}\right),

and therefore it necessarily underlies a simple pure Hodge module. For the last statement, consider the total Fourier-Laplace transformation functor

is by definition the module

∎

Note that the intersection of

.

Let

be an SK-linear free divisor, and let

be as above. Then, for all but at most finitely many values of

5.2
Case
\dim(G)=\dim(\overline{Y})+1

We consider again the case of a representation
\rho_{0}\colon G_{0}\to V
which we extend with the scaling action on

to a representation
\rho\colon G\to V
for
G=\mathds{C}^{*}\times G_{0}
. Let
\overline{Y}
be a
G
-orbit closure (this is in particular the affine cone over a projective
G_{0}
-variety

). We then have the following general lemma (which does not depend on assumptions on
\dim(G)
and
\dim(\overline{Y})
).

Lemma 5.6
.

Assume
\overline{Y}
consists of finitely many
G_{0}
-orbits. For every Lie algebra homomorphism
\beta_{0}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\mathds{C}
with
\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})\neq 0
, there exists a smallest monic univariate polynomial
b_{\beta_{0}}\neq 0
such that
b_{\beta_{0}}(\dim V-Z_{V}(\mathbf{e}))
lies in the ideal of
\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})
.

If
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
is such that
{\beta_{|{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}}}=\beta_{0}
, then
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\neq 0
if and only if
\beta(\mathbf{e})
is a root of
b_{\beta_{0}}
.

We remark that we choose to consider
\dim V-Z_{V}(\mathbf{e})
for
\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})
because, after Fourier-transform, this becomes the Euler vector field
E
on
V^{\vee}
, meaning that
b_{\beta_{0}}(E)
lies in the ideal of
\tau(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})
.

Proof.

Right-multiplication with
\dim V-Z_{V}(\mathbf{e})
defines a
\mathscr{D}_{V}
-linear endomorphism of the cyclic
\mathscr{D}_{V}
-module
\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})
(it is well defined since the ideal of
\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})
is

{\mathds{C}}^{*}
-equivariant).
By
3.4
,
\hat{\tau}_{0}:=\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})
is holonomic, hence the vector space
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}_{V}}(\hat{\tau}_{0},\hat{\tau}_{0})=H^{\dim V}a_{+}(\mathbb{D}\hat{\tau}_{0}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}^{\mathbb{L}}\hat{\tau}_{0})
is finite-dimensional. Therefore, the endomorphism given by right-multiplication with
\dim V-Z_{V}(\mathbf{e})
has a minimal polynomial, this is the polynomial
b_{\beta_{0}}
.

For the second claim, consider
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
with
{\beta_{|{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}}}=\beta_{0}
such that
\beta(\mathbf{e})
is not a root of the polynomial
b_{\beta_{0}}
. The ideal of
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
is generated by
Z_{V}(\mathbf{e})-\beta^{\prime}(\mathbf{e})=Z_{V}(\mathbf{e})-\dim V+\beta(\mathbf{e})
and the ideal of
\hat{\tau}_{0}
, which contains
b(\dim V-Z_{V}(\mathbf{e}))
. Since the polynomials

and

are assumed coprime, we have

for some

. Plugging in
\dim V-Z_{V}(\mathbf{e})
into this polynomial equation, we conclude that

lies in the ideal of
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
, hence
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=0
.
∎

Example 5.7

uniquely. Since the very ample equivariant line bundle
\mathscr{L}
is non-trivial, this means that
P
acts non-trivially on this one-dimensional subspace of
\overline{Y}
. This implies that the
G
-orbit
\overline{Y}\setminus\{0\}
is in fact already a
G_{0}
-orbit.

For
G_{0}
semisimple, the are no non-trivial
\beta_{0}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\mathds{C}
. The
b
-function
b_{0}(s)
is the polynomial

b_{0}(s)=\begin{cases}s(s-\frac{\ell}{k})&\text{if }\mathscr{L}^{\otimes\ell}\cong\omega_{G_{0}/P}^{\otimes(-k)}\text{ for some $\ell,k>0$}\\
s&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This follows from results in
[
GRSSW23
]
: The proof of Theorem 5.1 in loc.cit. shows that
b_{0}(s)
divides
s(s-2\langle\delta,\mu\rangle/|\mu|^{2})
, where

\mu
is the highest weight of the irreducible
G_{0}
-representation
V
and
\delta
is the half-sum of the positive roots of
G_{0}
. In the case
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes\ell}\cong\omega_{G_{0}/P}^{\otimes(-k)}
, the quantity
2\langle\delta,\mu\rangle/|\mu|^{2}
equals
\ell/k
by Proposition 5.9 in loc.cit. Finally,
b_{0}(s)
is determined by the characterization that
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=0
except for exactly this case and the case of
\beta(\mathbf{e})=0
(Corollary 6.13 in loc.cit.).

Proposition 5.8
.

Consider the case
\dim G=\dim\overline{Y}+1
. Assume that
\overline{Y}
has finitely many
G_{0}
-orbits and is Gorenstein with
\omega_{\overline{Y}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\{-\gamma\}
.
For every
\beta_{0}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\mathds{C}
, the
b
-function from
5.6

satisfies

b_{\beta_{0}}(s)=b_{\delta_{0}+\gamma_{0}-\beta_{0}}(\gamma(\mathbf{e})-s)

for
\delta_{0}:={(\trace\circ\ad)_{|{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}}}
,
\gamma_{0}:={\gamma_{|{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}}}
.
If
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathds{C}
is such that
\beta(\mathbf{e})
is a simple root of
b
, then

\mathbb{D}\,\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\trace\circ\ad+\gamma-\beta).

Remark 5.9
.

If
G_{0}
is semisimple, then
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=[\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{0}]
, so there are no non-trivial characters
\beta_{0}\colon\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\mathds{C}
and there is only
b:=b_{0}
. Then we obtain the symmetry
b(s)=b(\gamma(\mathbf{e})-s)
.

Proof.

Since
\dim G_{0}=\dim\overline{Y}
, we know from
5.1
that

\mathbb{D}\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})\cong\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\delta_{0}+\gamma_{0}-\beta_{0})

Consider the endomorphism
\varphi
of the cyclic
\mathscr{D}_{V}
-module
\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})
given by right-multiplication with
\dim V-Z_{V}(\mathbf{e})
. This dualizes to an endomorphism

\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\delta_{0}+\gamma_{0}-\beta_{0})\cong\mathbb{D}\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})\xrightarrow{\mathbb{D}\varphi}\mathbb{D}\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})\cong\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\delta_{0}+\gamma_{0}-\beta_{0}).

By functoriality of the isomorphism in
3.9
and by tracking how this functoriality carries through the isomorphism in
4.4
and
\omega_{\overline{Y}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\{-\gamma\}
to lead to
4.6
, we see that
\mathbb{D}\varphi
is given by right-multiplication with
\gamma(\mathbf{e})-(\dim V-Z_{V}(\mathbf{e}))
. By functoriality of
{\mathbb{D}}
, the morphisms
\varphi
and
\mathbb{D}\varphi
have the same minimal polynomial, therefore we can conclude that
b_{\beta_{0}}(s)=b_{\delta_{0}+\gamma_{0}-\beta_{0}}(\gamma(\mathbf{e})-s)
.

For the duality claim, note that
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=\coker(\varphi-\beta(\mathbf{e})\id)
, since it arises from
\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})
by quotienting out the left ideal generated by
-(Z_{V}(\mathbf{e})-\beta^{\prime}(e))=(\dim V-Z_{V}(\mathbf{e}))-\beta(\mathbf{e})
. We have
\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\beta_{0})\cong\ker(\varphi-\beta(\mathbf{e})\id)\oplus\im(\varphi-\beta(\mathbf{e})\id)
, because
\beta(\mathbf{e})
is assumed a simple root of the minimal polynomial of
\varphi
. Hence,
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=\coker(\varphi-\beta(\mathbf{e})\id)\cong\ker(\varphi-\beta(\mathbf{e})\id)
. By dualizing, we see that
\mathbb{D}\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\cong\coker(\mathbb{D}\varphi-\beta(\mathbf{e})\id)
. As discussed before, the morphism
\mathbb{D}\varphi-\beta(\mathbf{e})\id
is right-multiplication with
\gamma(\mathbf{e})-\beta(\mathbf{e})-(\dim V-Z_{V}(\mathbf{e}))
on
\hat{\tau}(\rho_{0},\overline{Y},\delta_{0}+\gamma_{0}-\beta_{0})
. Its cokernel is by definition
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\delta+\gamma-\beta)
for
\delta:=\trace\circ\ad
(note that
\delta(\mathbf{e})=0
).
∎

5.3
Homogeneous spaces

Consider again the case of the affine cone
\overline{Y}\subseteq V
over a projective homogeneous space
X:=G_{0}/P
as in
4.8
. Here,
V:=\Gamma(X,\mathscr{L})^{\vee}
for a
G
-equivariant very ample line bundle on
X
for
G=\mathds{C}^{*}\times G_{0}
. Assume
G_{0}
is semisimple, so that every Lie algebra homomorphism
\beta\colon\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathds{C}\mathbf{e}\to\mathds{C}
is given by
{\beta_{|{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}}}=0
and
\beta(\mathbf{e})\in\mathds{C}
.

Let
\beta(\mathbf{e})=\ell\in\mathds{Z}_{>0}
and assume
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes\ell}=\omega_{G_{0}/P}^{\vee}
(which is then the only case in which
\tau(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
is non-zero). In
[
GRSSW23
, Theorem 6.3 and Corollary 6.13]
, it was shown that

\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=H^{0}\iota_{\dagger}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}\setminus 0},

where
\iota\colon\overline{Y}\setminus 0\hookrightarrow V
. Then

\mathbb{D}\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=H^{0}\iota_{+}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}

and by
Eq.

4.0.1
in
4.8
this is
H^{n-m}\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},0)
, where
n:=\dim\overline{Y}
,
m:=\dim G
.

In particular, we have a natural morphism

\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=H^{0}\iota_{\dagger}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}\setminus 0}\to H^{0}\iota_{+}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}\setminus 0}=\mathbb{D}\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=H^{n-m}\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},0)

(5.3.1)

whose image is
\iota_{{\dagger}+}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}\backslash 0}
, i.e, the intersection cohomology
\mathscr{D}
-module of the singular variety
\overline{Y}
.

In order to given an explicit description of
\iota_{{\dagger}+}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}\backslash 0}
it would therefore be of interest to describe the morphism
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\to H^{n-m}\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},0)
explicitly. Recall that the latter is the cohomology of the complex
\mathscr{D}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}^{-\bullet}(\mathcal{O}_{V}\otimes\mathfrak{g})
in cohomological degree
m-n=\dim G_{0}-\dim X=\dim P
. It is natural to suspect that the parabolic subgroup
P
and its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{p}
lead through
\dim\bigwedge^{m-n}\mathfrak{p}=1
to a class of the
(n-m)
-th cohomology, to which the generator of the cyclic
\mathscr{D}_{V}
-module
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
maps under (
5.3.1
). More specifically, the locally free Lie algebroid
\mathcal{E}:=\ker(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\twoheadrightarrow\Theta_{Y})
of rank
m-n
on
Y:=\overline{Y}\setminus\{0\}
has determinant
\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}^{m-n}\mathcal{E}\cong\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}^{m}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\omega_{Y}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
by the Gorenstein property of
\overline{Y}
as discussed in
4.8
.
This leads to a distinguished global section of
\bigwedge^{m-n}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{E}\subseteq\bigwedge^{m-n}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes\mathfrak{g})
and therefore of
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\otimes\bigwedge^{m-n}_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}(\mathcal{O}_{V}\otimes\mathfrak{g})

which defines a class in
H^{n-m}\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},0)
. We expect that the generator of
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
is mapped to this global section by the morphism (
5.3.1
). We postpone the details of this construction to a subsequent paper.

Example 5.10
.

For
G_{0}=\SL(n)
consider
G_{0}/P=\mathds{P}^{n-1}
and the equivariant line bundle
\mathscr{L}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathds{P}^{n-1}}(d)
with
d\mid n
(so that
\beta(\mathbf{e})=n/d\in\mathds{Z}
).
\overline{Y}
is the cone over the
d
-th Veronese variety of
\mathds{P}^{n-1}
. Note that
\mathfrak{sl}(n)\oplus\mathds{C}\mathbf{e}\cong\mathfrak{gl}(n)
.
The coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
on
\mathds{C}^{n}
lead to coordinates on
V=\Sym^{d}\mathds{C}^{n}
being the degree
d
monomials in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.
On
U:=\overline{Y}\setminus\{x_{n}^{d}=0\}
, the elements

\{x_{n}^{d}\otimes E_{ij}-x_{j}x_{n}^{d-1}\otimes E_{in}\mid i\leq n,\ j<n\}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{V}\otimes\mathfrak{gl}(n)

form a basis of
\ker(\mathcal{O}_{U}\otimes\mathfrak{gl}(n)\twoheadrightarrow\Theta_{U})
. Their wedge product is

\bigwedge_{i,j}(x_{n}^{d}\otimes E_{ij}-x_{j}x_{n}^{d-1}\otimes E_{in})=\sum_{a\in\{1,\dots,n\}^{n}}(-1)^{\sum_{i}a_{i}}\,(x_{a_{1}}x_{n}^{d-1})(x_{a_{2}}x_{n}^{d-1})\dots(x_{a_{n}}x_{n}^{d-1})\,\hat{E}_{1a_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\hat{E}_{na_{n}}.

Here,
\hat{E}_{1a_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\hat{E}_{na_{n}}
means the
(n^{2}-n)
-term wedge product obtained from
E_{11}\wedge E_{12}\wedge E_{13}\dots\wedge E_{nn}
by removing the terms
E_{ia_{i}}
.
The element
x_{a_{1}}\dots x_{a_{n}}\in\Sym^{n}(\mathds{C}^{n})^{\vee}
, which can be expressed as a product of
n/d
linear forms on
V
in several ways, but is a well-defined element of
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}
. Hence, in
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}^{n^{2}-n}\mathfrak{gl}(n)
, we may consider the element where the above element is divided by
x_{n}^{n(d-1)}
(which was invertible on
U
):

\zeta=\sum_{a\in\{1,\dots,n\}^{n}}(-1)^{\sum_{i}a_{i}}\,x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{n}}\,\hat{E}_{1a_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\hat{E}_{na_{n}}\in\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\bigwedge_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}^{n^{2}-n}(\mathcal{O}_{V}\otimes\mathfrak{gl}(n)).

One can check that
1\otimes\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}\bigwedge^{m-n}_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}(\mathcal{O}_{V}\otimes\mathfrak{gl}(n))
lies in the kernel of the differential of the complex
\mathcal{C}^{n-m}(\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Y}},0^{\prime})
. We expect the morphism
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\to\displaystyle\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)=H^{n-m}\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},0)
to be given (up to a constant) by mapping the generator of
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
to
1\otimes\zeta
. In this example, it can be checked using some topological arguments that
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)
is self-dual, so this morphism will also be an isomorphism. Despite this speciality, the description of
\hat{\tau}(\rho,\overline{Y},\beta)\to H^{n-m}\hat{T}(\rho,\overline{Y},0)
in this example should be illustrative of the general case.

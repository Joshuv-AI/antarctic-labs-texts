Moduli spaces of open strings have polylogarithmic Mirzakhani volumes
Yi Huang, Ivan Telpukhovskiy

Abstract
. We show that the Mirzakhani volume, as introduced by Chekhov, of the moduli space of every non-contractible crowned hyperbolic surface is naturally expressible as a sum of Gaussian rational multiples of polylogarithms evaluated at ± 1 \pm 1 and ± − 1 \pm\sqrt{-1} .



1.
Introduction

In
[
17
]
, Mirzakhani showed that the Weil–Petersson volume of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g,m}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
of hyperbolic structures on a genus
g
surface with
m
boundaries, with specified boundary lengths
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}
, is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}_{\geqslant 0}[\pi^{2},b_{1}^{2},\ldots,b_{m}^{2}]
. She subsequently reinterpreted the coefficients of these polynomials via Duistermaat–Heckman theory as intersection numbers on moduli spaces of pointed curves, and used this to obtain a novel proof of Witten’s conjecture
[
24
]
— also known as Kontsevich’s theorem
[
15
]
, that the generating function for the aforementioned intersection numbers should satisfy the Korteweg-de Vries hierarchy.

There has been great progress in building an analogue of Witten’s conjecture, at least from the algebraic geometric perspective
[
18
,
7
,
3
,
2
,
1
,
6
,
5
]
, for intersection numbers of moduli spaces of
open strings
— i.e.: Riemann surfaces with marked points (or equivalently, punctures) on their boundaries. However, any attempt to find a parallel for Mirzakhani’s hyperbolic geometric approach to Witten’s conjecture for open strings has to contend with the non-finiteness of the Weil–Petersson volumes of the moduli spaces at hand.

In
[
8
]
, Chekhov introduces an action on moduli spaces of crowned hyperbolic surfaces (i.e.: uniformizations of open strings) of fixed neck holonomy, and we refer to the integrals of the induced measures over moduli spaces as
Mirzakhani volumes
. Chekhov computes a handful of examples, and we extend Chekhov’s work to (almost) full generality, and beyond.

2.
Background

We introduce background and notation for defining Chekhov’s notion of Mirzakhani volumes.

2.1.
Open strings and crowned hyperbolic surfaces

From the complex analytic perspective, an
open string
or an
open Riemann surface
with punctures is a Riemann surface with (smooth) boundary with interior and boundary punctures
[
18
, Section 1.3]
. Standard Schottky doubling arguments (see, e.g.:
[
14
, Pg. 24]
) show that such surfaces uniformize to a complete finite-area hyperbolic surface with bi-infinite geodesic boundary components — i.e.: a crowned hyperbolic surface where every end is either a cusp or a tine (half-cusp). We generalize very mildly beyond this context to align with what Mirzakhani did: we allow also for non-cuspidal cuffs. In terms of complex geometry, these are Riemann surfaces minus finitely many interior points, boundary points, and (closed) disks in the interior of the surface.

Notation 2.1
.

For the remainder of the article let
\Sigma:=\Sigma_{m,\vec{a}}
, with
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
and
\vec{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{l}
, denote a bordered topological surface of finite type, where

•

n=\sum_{k=1}^{l}a_{k}
boundary punctures, which we label
\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\}
;

•

\Sigma\cup\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\}
is a surface with
m
interior punctures, which we label
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}
, and
l\geq 0

\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{l}\}
;

•

the boundary curves
\delta_{i}
respectively contain
a_{i}\geqslant 1
punctures. We order the punctures

so that

, and

, and so forth, and so that successively labelled punctures appear consecutively along each boundary.

We further impose the condition that
\Sigma
admits a hyperbolic metric with geodesic boundary, meaning that

•

if
g=0
and

, then

;

•

Definition 2.2

(crowned hyperbolic surface)

We refer to

•

•

We henceforth use

2.2.
The anatomy of a crowned hyperbolic surface

2.4.
Shearing coordinates on Teichmüller space of crowned surfaces

The contents of this subsection is well-known to experts. See, for example,

Notation 2.11

(shearing sign convention)

We take

Definition 2.12

(ideal triangulation)
.

Given a surface

We identify ideal triangulations up to isotopies of

Lemma 2.13
.

Given a surface
\Sigma=\Sigma_{m,\vec{a}}
,

Let

in

, give an ideal triangulation of

, and hence

Given a crowned hyperbolic surface

2.5.
Lambda length coordinates

We will refer to one more coordinate system related to Teichmüller spaces called lambda length coordinates. These coordinates parametrize the (horocycle-)decorated Teichmüller space
[
19
]
, as opposed to the Teichmüller space itself, and each
\lambda
-length is geometric invariant assigned to a geodesic in
\mathbb{H}
that is decorated by (i.e.: paired with) horocycles at each of its ends.

Definition 2.16

(lambda length)
.

Given a geodesic
\gamma\subset\mathbb{H}
and a pair of horocycles
h_{1},h_{2}
whose (distinct) centers are the endpoints of
\gamma
, let
d
denote the signed hyperbolic distance along
\gamma
between the points
h_{1}\cap\gamma
and
h_{2}\cap\gamma
, where the sign of
d
is taken to be positive if and only if
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are disjoint. The
\lambda
-length
or
lambda length
of
h_{1},h_{2}
is

(1)

\displaystyle\lambda(h_{1},h_{2})=e^{d/2}.

Definition 2.17

(cusp/tine decoration)
.

We say a cusp
\beta_{i}
(a tine
q_{j}
) of a crowned hyperbolic surface
X
is
decorated
if a horocycle centered at
\widetilde{\beta}_{i}\in\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{X}\subset\partial\mathbb{H}\,\,(\widetilde{q}_{j}\in\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{X}\subset\partial\mathbb{H})
is chosen in a
\pi_{1}(X)
-equivariant way. A crowned surface is called a

if all its cusps and tines are decorated; we denote it by
(X,\textbf{h})
, where
h
denotes the collection of decorating horocycles.

Definition 2.18

(decorated Teichmüller space and moduli space)
.

The
decorated Teichmüller space

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\Sigma}
is the space of (isotopy classes of) decorated crowned surfaces. Specifically, it is defined as the space of pairs
(X,\textbf{h})
, where
X
is a marked crowned hyperbolic metric on
\Sigma
(i.e.: an element of
\mathcal{T}_{\Sigma}(\vec{0})
), and

is a collection of horocycles on

— one for each cusp/tine.

Definition 2.19

(lambda length coordinates)
.

Consider an ideal triangulation
\triangle
on
\Sigma
, for each arc
\alpha
in
\triangle
, we define a lambda length

\lambda_{\alpha}:\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\Sigma}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}

that assigns to a decorated marked hyperbolic surface
(X,\textbf{h})
the lambda length of the lifts

of the geodesic representative of

in
\mathbb{H}
, decorated by corresponding lifts of

Penner introduced these spaces in

]
, and showed that lambda length coordinates define global real analytically compatible coordinates for the decorated Teichmüller space of punctured (i.e.: cusped) surfaces. This also extends to crowned hyperbolic surfaces (see

).

3.

Teichmüller spaces

]
, with Darboux coordinates are given
globally
(and somewhat miraculously) by Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates
[
25
]
. The Weil–Petersson form is mapping class group invariant, and so its top exterior product defines the
Weil–Petersson volume
on the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g,m}(\vec{b})
— this forms the backdrop for Mirzakhani’s work on determining the Weil–Petersson volumes of
\mathcal{M}_{g,m}(\vec{b})
.

In contrast, Teichmüller spaces of crowned surfaces (either with or without fixed neck holonomy) are not naturally symplectic (they can even be odd-dimensional). Nevertheless, one can define a mapping class group invariant volume form on
\mathcal{T}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
and
\mathcal{T}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b}|\vec{d})
which generalise the Weil–Petersson volume form. Indeed, here are two approaches:

Method 1:

we can “formally” generalise classical expressions for the Weil–Petersson volume based on Fenchel–Nielsen parameters and shearing coordinates. This is certainly unsurprising to experts (see, e.g., Goncharov–Sun’s
[
12
, Lemma 2.8]
).

Method 2:

Chekhov takes a more sophisticated approach in
[
8
]
using the Poisson algebraic structure of extended shearing coordinates on decorated Teichmüller space coming from the Goldman bracket, and exploiting the property, found in the classical setting, that the Weil–Petersson symplectic form is inverse to the Poisson bracket, to define the Weil–Petersson volume in terms of the Pfaffian of the matrix representing the Goldman bracket.

In this section, we verify that the two methods yield the same volume form (up to sign), before clarifying the relationship between the Mirzakhani volumes of
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b}|\vec{d})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
. Finally, we verify Chekhov’s assertion that that naively integrating the Weil–Petersson form over moduli spaces of crowned hyperbolic surfaces yields infinity precisely when one of the boundary of
\Sigma
has more than one puncture.

3.1.
WP volume form on the moduli space of crowned surfaces

We now define a generalization of the Weil–Petersson volume form (up to sign) on
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b}|\vec{d})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
by first defining it for moduli spaces of
n
-gons and
n
-crowns, and then for general surfaces.

3.1.1.
The
n
-gon case

Definition 3.1

(Weil–Petersson volume form for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}
)
.

Let
\triangle
be an arbitrary ideal triangulation of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
, let
\{s_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\triangle}
denote the associated shearing parameters for the shearing coordinates
s_{\triangle}:\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\triangle}
, define the
Weil–Petersson volume form

\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}
on
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}
as, up to sign, by

(2)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}:=\pm\bigwedge_{\alpha\in\triangle}\mathrm{d}s_{\alpha}.

Since the (pure) mapping class group for
\mathbb{D}_{n}
is trivial, this defines a volume form on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}

Remark 3.2
.

The standard strategy to show that
\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\Sigma}
is independent of the choice of the ideal triangulation is to verify that it is invariant under flips (i.e.: replace one edge by the opposite diagonal of the quadrilateral comprised of the two ideal triangles containing the given edge), and to show that you can deform from any ideal triangulation to another via a finite sequence of flips. We refer to
[
21
, Theorem 4.7]
for formulae related to the flipping process which can be used to show that
\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\Sigma}
is flip-invariant up to sign.

We now give a brute-force verification that the generalization of the Weil–Petersson volume form defined via
Eq.

2
is equal to that described in
[
8
, Equation (6.9)]
:

Proposition 3.3
.

Consider points
\{z_{j}\}_{j=2,\ldots,n-2}
in
(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}=\partial\mathbb{H}
parametrising an
n
-gon by specifying the positions of
n-3
consecutive ideal vertices, where the
3
other vertices are normalised so as to be placed at
0,1,\infty
. Then,
\{z_{j}\}_{j=2,\ldots,n-2}
forms a coordinate chart on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}
, and

(3)

\displaystyle\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{n-2}}{z_{2}(z_{3}-z_{2})(z_{4}-z_{3})\cdots(z_{n-2}-z_{n-3})(1-z_{n-2})}.

Proof.

Let
s_{1},\cdots,s_{n-3}
be the shearing coordinates obtained for the triangulation for
\mathbb{D}_{n}
with all diagonals meeting at vertex
z_{0}=z_{n}=\infty
, so that
s_{i}
is assigned to the arc joining
\infty
and
z_{i+1}
. Since
Eq.

2
holds for any triangulation of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
, we have

\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-3},

up to sign. Introduce the following variables:

y_{2}=z_{2},\quad y_{i}=z_{i}-z_{i-1},

for
i=3,\cdots,n-2
. Then

1-z_{n-2}=1-\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}y_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{d}y_{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}y_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}y_{n-2}=\mathrm{d}z_{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{n-2}.

Hence

is equal to

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\wedge}\limits_{i=2}^{n-2}\mathrm{d}y_{i}}{y_{2}y_{3}\cdots y_{n-2}(1-\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}y_{j})}.

The shearing coordinates satisfy

\displaystyle s_{1}

\displaystyle=\log\tfrac{z_{3}-z_{2}}{z_{2}}=\log\tfrac{y_{3}}{y_{2}},

\displaystyle s_{j}

\displaystyle=\log\tfrac{z_{j+2}-z_{j+1}}{z_{j+1}-z_{j}}=\log\tfrac{y_{j+2}}{y_{j+1}}\,\quad\text{for }j=2,\cdots,n-4,

\displaystyle s_{n-3}

\displaystyle=\log\tfrac{1-z_{n-2}}{z_{n-2}-z_{n-3}}=\log\tfrac{1-\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}y_{j}}{y_{n-2}}.

Thus,
\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-3}
is given by

(5)

\begin{split}&(\mathrm{d}\log y_{3}-\mathrm{d}\log y_{2})\wedge(\mathrm{d}\log y_{4}-\mathrm{d}\log y_{3})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\mathrm{d}\log y_{n-2}-\mathrm{d}\log y_{n-3})\\
&\wedge\Bigl(\mathrm{d}\log\Bigl(1-\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}y_{j}\Bigr)-\mathrm{d}\log y_{n-2}\Bigr).\end{split}

Expand the brackets in
Eq.

5
and observe that the only non-zero wedge products are obtained as follows: for
k=0,\cdots,n-3
,

•

in the first
k
brackets take the second summand, and

•

in the remaining
n-k-3
brackets take the first summand.

The corresponding term equals
(-1)^{n-3}\mathop{\wedge}\limits_{j=2}^{n-2}\mathrm{d}\log y_{j}
if
k=n-3
, and if
k<n-3
the corresponding term is

(6)

\displaystyle\Bigl((-1)^{k}\mathop{\wedge}\limits_{i=2}^{k+1}\mathrm{d}\log y_{i}\Bigr)\wedge\Bigl(\mathop{\wedge}\limits_{i=k+3}^{n-2}\mathrm{d}\log y_{i}\Bigr)\wedge\Bigl(\mathrm{d}\log\Bigl(1-\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}y_{j}\Bigr)\Bigr)

(7)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+(n-2-(k+3)+1)+1}\frac{y_{k+2}\cdot\mathop{\wedge}\limits_{i=2}^{n-2}\mathrm{d}\log y_{i}}{1-\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}y_{j}}=(-1)^{n-3}\frac{y_{k+2}\cdot\mathop{\wedge}\limits_{i=2}^{n-2}\mathrm{d}\log y_{i}}{1-\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}y_{j}}.

Summing these

which equals

∎

Proposition 3.5

(WP volume for
n
-crowns in shears)

Consider
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1,\dotsc,n-1}
, where

as the ratio of the lambda length (with respect to some decoration) of the arc from

to

\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{n-1}}{x_{1}(x_{2}-x_{1})\cdots(x_{i}-x_{i-1})\cdots(x_{n-1}-x_{n-2})}.

Note that

Proof.

As in the proof of
Proposition

3.3
, we perform the calculation in the upper half-plane. Suppose that

. Following
[
8
, Page 7]
, for each

be the hyperbolic length of the counterclockwise segment of the geodesic neck connecting the nearest-point projections of

and

(see

Further, let

. Then

e^{d}-e^{\Delta_{i}}=Q-\sum_{j=1}^{i}A_{j},\,i=1,\cdots,n-1.

Now let

be the shearing coordinates associated with the ideal triangulation of

by

\displaystyle s_{i}

\displaystyle=\log\frac{A_{i+1}}{A_{i}},\,\quad\text{for }i=1,\cdots,n-2,

\displaystyle=\log\frac{Q-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}A_{j}}{A_{n-1}}.

Thus,

\begin{split}&(\mathrm{d}\log A_{2}-\mathrm{d}\log A_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\mathrm{d}\log A_{n-1}-\mathrm{d}\log A_{n-2})\\

Notice that

is almost the same as

5
from the proof of

\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-1}=(-1)^{n-1}\frac{Q\cdot\mathop{\wedge}\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{d}A_{i}}{A_{1}\cdots A_{n-1}\Bigl(Q-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}A_{j}\Bigr)},

note the extra factor of

Looking at

\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)=\mathrm{d}\log x_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}\log(x_{2}-x_{2})\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}\log(x_{n-1}-x_{n-2}).

From

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\log x_{1}=\mathrm{d}\log\frac{e^{\Delta_{1}}-1}{e^{d}-e^{\Delta_{1}}};\quad\mathrm{d}\log(x_{i+1}-x_{i})=\mathrm{d}\log\frac{e^{\Delta_{i+1}}-e^{\Delta_{i}}}{(e^{d}-e^{\Delta_{i+1}})(e^{d}-e^{\Delta_{i}})}.

Observe that we have

\mathrm{d}\log(x_{i+1}-x_{i})=\mathrm{d}\log\frac{A_{i+1}}{Q-\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}A_{j}}+\mathrm{d}\log\frac{1}{Q-\sum_{j=1}^{i}A_{j}},

and the last term in

\displaystyle\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)=\mathrm{d}\log\frac{A_{1}}{Q-A_{1}}\wedge\mathrm{d}\log\frac{A_{2}}{Q-A_{1}-A_{2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}\log\frac{A_{n-1}}{Q-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}A_{j}}.

Next, observe that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\log\frac{A_{i}}{Q-\sum_{j=1}^{i}A_{j}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\log A_{i}-\mathrm{d}\log\Bigl(Q-\sum_{j=1}^{i}A_{j}\Bigr)=\frac{\mathrm{d}A_{i}}{A_{i}}+\frac{\mathrm{d}A_{1}+\cdots+\mathrm{d}A_{i}}{Q-\sum_{j=1}^{i}A_{j}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}f(A_{1},\cdots,A_{i-1})+\frac{Q-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}A_{j}}{Q-\sum_{j=1}^{i}A_{j}}\mathrm{d}\log A_{i},

and thus contributes trivially to the wedge product in

which matches the expression in

Proposition 3.7
.

The Weil–Petersson volume forms
\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})

We prove this for

, the argument for
\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b}|\vec{d})
is essentially identical. The mapping class group

necessarily preserves the neck curves

, and hence factors as

\displaystyle=\mathrm{MCG}_{\Sigma_{g,m+l}}\oplus\mathrm{MCG}_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{1}}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathrm{MCG}_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{l}}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\nu_{1}}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\nu_{l}},

is invariant under the action of

.

\Sigma
with fixed cuff and neck lengths. Likewise, we define

\displaystyle V_{\Sigma}(\vec{b}):=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})}e^{-S(X)}\;\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})

as the
Mirzakhani volume
of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
.

Remark 4.4
.

One might (very reasonably) wonder where the name
Mirzakhani volume
comes from, and whilst Chekhov does use this phrase in
[
8
, Section 2.3]
entitled “Mirzakhani volumes for
\mathcal{M}_{g,s,\mathbf{n}}
” and expressions like “the original Mirzakhani volumes” (implying that there are new ones), there is some ambiguity as to whether he referred to the induced volume for his action as “Mirzakhani volumes”. We choose to adopt this term both to distinguish it from the Weil–Petersson volume and in memory of Mirzakhani’s work.

4.2.
Mirzakhani volumes of moduli spaces of crowned hyperbolic surfaces with fixed neck lengths

In
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
, Chekhov obtains an expression for the Mirzakhani volume of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)
as an integral over an
(n-1)
-dimensional simplex and uses this to assert the finiteness of these integrals. The coordinate system he utilizes comes from the distances
\Delta_{i}
between the orthogonal projections of the punctures to the crown’s neck. In particular, he explicitly carries out the calculation for the moduli space of
3
-crowns with fixed neck length
[
8
, Section 2.5]
.

We determine these volumes for all
n\geq 1
, and hence determine

Theorem 4.5
.

The Mirzakhani volume
V_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)
is:

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{d}{\sinh{d/2}}\cdot\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(d^{2}+(2j)^{2}\pi^{2})}{2(n-1)!},&\quad\text{if }n=2k\text{ is even;}\\[2.5pt]
\frac{1}{\cosh{d/2}}\cdot\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}(d^{2}+(2j-1)^{2}\pi^{2})}{2(n-1)!},&\quad\text{if }n=2k+1\text{ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

To determine the volume of the moduli space for
n
-crowns
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(\vec{d})
, we need

•

a domain of integration,

•

an expression for the Weil–Petersson volume form,

•

and an expression for the Chekhov action.

Since the (pure) mapping class group for a boundary punctured disk with one interior puncture
\mathbb{A}_{n}
is trivial, the moduli space for
n
-crowns
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(\vec{d})
is equal to the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(\vec{d})
. Fix the shearing coordinates
\{s_{1},\dotsc,s_{n}\}
on
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(\vec{d})
as in the proof of
Proposition

3.5
. Then by
Proposition

2.14
, the domain of integration is given by the hyperplane
H_{d}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}=d\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The shearing coordinates based presentation of the Weil–Petersson volume form is given by

\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)=\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-1}.

And the expression for the Chekhov action in shearing coordinates is given by Chekhov in
[
8
, Equation 5.1]
:

e^{-S}=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}2\cosh(s_{i}/2)}.

Then we can set up the volume integral:

(19)

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)=\int_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(\vec{d})}e^{-S}\,\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)=\int_{H_{d}}\,\,\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-1}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}2\cosh(s_{i}/2)}.

This is a convolution of functions, specifically:

(20)

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)=\left(\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\cosh(s/2)}*\cdots*\frac{1}{2\cosh(s/2)}}_{n\text{ times}}\right)(d),

and we may apply the convolution theorem:

(21)

\displaystyle\underbrace{f*f*\cdots*f}_{{n\text{ times}}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}(f)^{n}),

where
f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\text{sech}(x/2)
and
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform. A straightforward residue-based calculation (see also
[
13
, Equation 3.981(3)]
) yields:

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f](\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\ e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\;\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\pi}{\cosh{2\pi^{2}\xi}}.

Thus, by the convolution theorem:

V_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\pi^{n}e^{2\pi i\xi x}}{\cosh^{n}{2\pi^{2}\xi}}\mathrm{d}\xi.

Let
\xi=\frac{\log t}{2\pi^{2}},
then
\mathrm{d}\xi=\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{2\pi^{2}t}
, and

\begin{split}V_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(x)&=\pi^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi i\frac{\log t}{2\pi^{2}}x}}{\cosh^{n}({\log t})}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{2\pi^{2}t}=\frac{\pi^{n-2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\frac{ix}{\pi}-1}}{\left(\frac{t+t^{-1}}{2}\right)^{n}}\mathrm{d}t\\
&=2^{n-1}\pi^{n-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\frac{ix}{\pi}+n-1}}{(t^{2}+1)^{n}}\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Let
u=\frac{1}{t^{2}+1}
. Then
t=(u^{-1}-1)^{1/2}
, and

\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}(u^{-1}-1)^{-1/2}\cdot(-1)\cdot u^{-2}\mathrm{d}u.

Therefore,

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n-2}\int_{0}^{1}(u^{-1}-1)^{\frac{ix}{2\pi}+\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot u^{n}\cdot(u^{-1}-1)^{-1/2}u^{-2}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n-2}\int_{0}^{1}(u^{-1}-1)^{\frac{ix}{2\pi}+\frac{n}{2}-1}u^{n-2}\mathrm{d}u.

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n-2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-u)^{\frac{ix}{2\pi}+\frac{n}{2}-1}\cdot u^{-\frac{ix}{2\pi}+\frac{n}{2}-1}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n-2}B\Bigl(-\frac{ix}{2\pi}+\frac{n}{2},\frac{ix}{2\pi}+\frac{n}{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{n-2}}{\Gamma(n)}\Gamma\Bigl(-\frac{ix}{2\pi}+\frac{n}{2}\Bigr)\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{ix}{2\pi}+\frac{n}{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{n-2}}{\Gamma(n)}\Big|\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{ix}{2\pi}\Bigr)\Big|^{2},

where
B(z_{1},z_{1})
is the beta function and
\Gamma(z)
is the gamma function.

To finish up, we use the absolute value formulas for arguments of integer or half-integer real part
[
9
, Equation 1.2(8)-(9)]
.
Let
x=d
. Then for
n=2k
:

\Big|\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{id}{2\pi}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}=\frac{d/2}{\sinh{d/2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\Bigl((d/2\pi)^{2}+j^{2}\Bigr).

Hence

\frac{(2\pi)^{n-2}}{\Gamma(n)}\Big|\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{id}{2\pi}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}=\frac{d}{\sinh{d/2}}\cdot\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\big(d^{2}+(2j)^{2}\pi^{2}\big)}{2(n-1)!}.

For
n=2k+1
, we have:

\Big|\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{id}{2\pi}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}=\frac{\pi}{\cosh{d/2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\bigl((d/2\pi)^{2}+(j-1/2)^{2}\bigr).

Hence

\frac{(2\pi)^{n-2}}{\Gamma(n)}\Big|\Gamma\Bigl(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{id}{2\pi}\Bigr)\Big|^{2}=\frac{1}{\cosh{d/2}}\cdot\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\bigl(d^{2}+(2j-1)^{2}\pi^{2}\bigr)}{2(n-1)!}.

∎

Applying
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
(which follows easily from
Eq.

18
), we obtain:

Corollary 4.6
.

For
\Sigma=\mathbb{A}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(d)
, the Mirzakhani volume of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{1},a_{2}}}(d)
is

V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{1},a_{2}}}(d)=d\cdot V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{1}}}(d)\cdot V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{2}}}(d).

For
\Sigma\neq\mathbb{D}_{n},\mathbb{A}_{n},\mathbb{A}_{a_{1},a_{2}}
, let
g
denote the genus of
\Sigma
. The volume of
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b}|\vec{d})
is

V_{\Sigma}(\vec{b}|\vec{d})=V_{g,m+l}(\vec{b},\vec{d})\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{l}d_{k}\cdot V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{k}}}(d_{k}),

where
V_{g,m+l}(\vec{b},\vec{d})\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}[\pi^{2},b_{1}^{2},\ldots,b_{m}^{2},d_{1}^{2},\ldots,d_{l}^{2}]
is the Weil–Petersson volume of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g,m+l}(\vec{b},\vec{d})
.

Remark 4.7
.

The result of
Theorem

4.5
disagrees with Chekhov’s calculation for
n=3

[
8
, Example 2.5.1]
(and with the case
n=2
claimed in
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
; see
Section

A.1

using
\lambda

[

: following the notation of
[
8
]
, for
i=0
it should read

\displaystyle s_{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{e^{P}r_{0}/r_{n-1}}+\sqrt{r_{0}/r_{1}}=\frac{2\sqrt{e^{P}r_{0}r_{1}}+2\sqrt{r_{0}r_{n-1}}}{2\sqrt{r_{1}r_{n-1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{P/2}(e^{\Delta_{1}}-1)+e^{-P/2}(e^{P}-e^{\Delta_{n-1}})}{2\sqrt{r_{1}r_{n-1}}}=\frac{e^{-P/2}(e^{P+\Delta_{1}}-e^{\Delta_{n-1}})}{2\sqrt{r_{1}r_{n-1}}}.

Taking the product of horocycle lengths, we obtain

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}s_{j}=\frac{\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(e^{\Delta_{i+1}}-e^{\Delta_{i-1}})\right)\cdot e^{-P/2}(e^{P+\Delta_{1}}-e^{\Delta_{n-1}})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(e^{\Delta_{i}}-e^{\Delta_{i-1}})}=e^{-P/2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e^{\delta_{i}+\delta_{i+1}}-1}{e^{\delta_{i}}-1}.

Taking the reciprocal, we obtain that the action functional

in

. Moreover, the multiplicative factor
\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}

[

from being a product of hyperbolic sine based terms to being expressed in terms of exponentials. Hence, the correct form of

should be

\displaystyle V^{\text{crown}}_{n,P}=\int_{\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{n-1}\leq P}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}d\delta_{i}\frac{e^{P/2}(e^{P}-1)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(e^{\delta_{i}+\delta_{i+1}}-1\bigr)}

with
\delta_{i}>0

\delta_{i+n}=\delta_{i}
, and
\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{n-1}+\delta_{n}=P
.

4.3.
Generating function

We set the generating function to take the form

P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}(x,d):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}(d)\,x^{n}.

Theorem 4.8

(generating function for volumes of moduli spaces of crowns)
.

The generating function
P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}(x,d)
for the volumes of crowns is

(23)

\displaystyle P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}(x,d)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-\pi^{2}x^{2}}}\cdot\frac{\sinh(\frac{d}{2}+\frac{d}{\pi}\arcsin\pi x)}{\sinh d}.

Proof.

We write
P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}(x,d)=P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}^{even}(x,d)+P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}^{odd}(x,d)
, where
P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}^{even}
and
P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}^{odd}
respectively correspond to even
n
volume terms and odd
n
volume terms, and we compute them separately. By
[
13
, Equation 1.414(1)]
:

(24)

\displaystyle\cos(d\,\text{arcsinh}\,{x})=1-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{d^{2}(d^{2}+2^{2})\dotsc(d^{2}+(2k)^{2})}{(2k+2)!}x^{2k+2}.

Since
\text{arcsinh}(ix)=i\arcsin(x)
and
\cos ix=\cosh x
, we can express
Eq.

24
as

(25)

\displaystyle\cosh(d\arcsin{x})=1+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{d^{2}(d^{2}+2^{2})\dotsc(d^{2}+(2k)^{2})}{(2k+2)!}x^{2k+2}

Together with
Theorem

4.5
, it follows that

(26)

\begin{split}\frac{\cosh\left(\frac{d}{\pi}\arcsin{\pi x}\right)-1}{2d\sinh(d/2)}&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{\sinh(d/2)}\frac{(d^{2}+2^{2}\pi^{2})\dotsc(d^{2}+(2k)^{2}\pi^{2})}{2(2k+2)!}x^{2k+2}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{V_{\mathbb{A}_{2k+2}}(d)}{2k+2}x^{2k+2}\end{split}

Let
G(x,d)=\frac{\cosh\left(\frac{d}{\pi}\arcsin{\pi x}\right)-1}{2d\sinh(d/2)}
. It follows from
Eq.

26
that

(27)

\displaystyle P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}^{even}(x,d)=x\frac{\partial G}{\partial x},

and we compute

(28)

\displaystyle P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}^{even}(x,d)=\frac{x}{2\sqrt{1-\pi^{2}x^{2}}}\frac{\sinh\left(\frac{d}{\pi}\arcsin{\pi x}\right)}{\sinh(d/2)}.

By
[
13
, Equation 1.414(2)]
:

(29)

\displaystyle\sin(d\,\text{arcsinh}\,{x})=dx-d\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{(d^{2}+1^{2})(d^{2}+3^{2})\dotsc(d^{2}+(2k-1)^{2})}{(2k+1)!}x^{2k+1}

Since
\text{arcsinh}(ix)=i\arcsin(x)
and
\sin ix=i\sinh x
, we can express
Eq.

29
as

(30)

\displaystyle\sin(d\,\text{arcsinh}\,{x})=dx-d\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{(d^{2}+1^{2})(d^{2}+3^{2})\dotsc(d^{2}+(2k-1)^{2})}{(2k+1)!}x^{2k+1}

Together with
Theorem

4.5
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\sinh\left(\frac{d}{\pi}\arcsin{\pi x}\right)}{2d\cosh(d/2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{2\cosh(d/2)}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\cosh(d/2)}\frac{(d^{2}+1^{2}\pi^{2})(d^{2}+3^{2}\pi^{2})\dotsc(d^{2}+(2k-1)^{2}\pi^{2})}{2(2k+1)!}x^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{V_{\mathbb{A}_{2k+1}}(d)}{2k+1}x^{2k+1}

Let
H(x,d)=\frac{\sinh\left(\frac{d}{\pi}\arcsin{\pi x}\right)}{2d\cosh(d/2)}
. Then
P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}^{odd}(x,d)=x\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}
, and we get:

(31)

\displaystyle P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}^{odd}(x,d)=\frac{x}{2\sqrt{1-\pi^{2}x^{2}}}\frac{\cosh\left(\frac{d}{\pi}\arcsin{\pi x}\right)}{\cosh(d/2)}.

Combining
Eq.

28
with
Eq.

31
, we conclude

(32)

\begin{split}P_{\mathbb{A}_{\bullet}}(x,d)&=\frac{x}{2\sqrt{1-\pi^{2}x^{2}}}\left(\frac{\sinh\left(\frac{d}{\pi}\arcsin{\pi x}\right)}{\sinh(d/2)}+\frac{\cosh\left(\frac{d}{\pi}\arcsin{\pi x}\right)}{\cosh(d/2)}\right)\\
&=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-\pi^{2}x^{2}}}\cdot\frac{\sinh(\frac{d}{2}+\frac{d}{\pi}\arcsin\pi x)}{\sinh d}.\end{split}

∎

5.
Mirzakhani volumes of moduli spaces of crowned hyperbolic surfaces without neck length constraints

The Mirzakhani volumes for moduli spaces of crowned hyperbolic surfaces of fixed neck lengths
d_{1},\ldots,d_{l}
take the form of something polynomial in
d_{1},\ldots,d_{l}
over something exponential, and thus the Mirzakhani volumes for moduli spaces without neck constraints are convergent. In this section, we

•

compute the Mirzakhani volumes of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}
for all
n\geqslant 1
(
Proposition

5.1
);

•

show that the Mirzakhani volumes of the moduli space of
(a_{1},a_{2})
-crowns is a rational polynomial in
\log 2
and integral values of the Riemann zeta function (
Theorem

5.2
);

•

show that the Mirzakhani volumes of
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
are rational polynomials in integral values of the Riemann zeta function and the Dirichlet beta function for all remaining
\Sigma
which are not
n
-gons (
Theorem

5.3
).

5.1.
Mirzakhani volumes of moduli spaces of
n
-crowns

We first find the Mirzakhani volumes for full moduli spaces of
n
-crowns (i.e.: without neck length constraints):

Proposition 5.1
.

For
n\geqslant 1
,

V_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}=\frac{\pi^{n}}{2}.

Proof.

Note that the function
f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\text{sech}(x/2)
is even. Then
[
13
, Equation 3.511(1)]
) tells us:

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{A}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\geqslant 0\}}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{i}}{2\cosh(s_{i}/2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{i}}{2\cosh(s_{i}/2)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{2\cosh(s/2)}\right)^{n}=\frac{\pi^{n}}{2}.

∎

5.2.
Mirzakhani volumes of moduli spaces of
(a_{1},a_{2})
-annuli

Theorem 5.2
.

If
n=a_{1}+a_{2}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{1},a_{2}}}
is odd, then

V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{1},a_{2}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\rho_{2i}(a_{1},a_{2})\pi^{2i}\zeta(n-2i)

where
\rho_{2i}(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
and
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function. If
n=a_{1}+a_{2}
is even, then

V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{1},a_{2}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\rho_{2i}(a_{1},a_{2})\pi^{2i}\zeta(n-1-2i)+\rho_{n-2}(a_{1},a_{2})\pi^{n-2}\log 2,

where
\rho_{2i}(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, and
\rho_{n-2}(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geqslant 0}
. The coefficient
\rho_{n-2}(a_{1},a_{2})
is strictly positive if and only if both
a_{1},a_{2}
are odd.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
(or just
Eq.

18
):

(33)

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{1},a_{2}}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\ell}V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{1}}}(\ell)\cdot V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{2}}}(\ell)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\ell=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ell\cdot V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{1}}}(\ell)\cdot V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{2}}}(\ell)\,\mathrm{d}\ell.

The specific integrals going forth depend on the parity of
a_{1},a_{2}
, and we split into the following cases:

Case 1:

a_{1},a_{2}
have different parity, or equivalently,
n
is odd.

Case 2:

a_{1},a_{2}
are both even.

Case 3:

a_{1},a_{2}
are both odd.

By
Theorem

4.5
, these three cases respectively correspond to integrals of the form:

For Case 1,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{P(\ell)\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\sinh(\ell/2)\cosh(\ell/2)},

P
is a degree
n-1
even polynomial.

For Case 2,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{Q(\ell)\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\sinh^{2}(\ell/2)},

Q
is a degree
n-1
odd polynomial.

For Case 3,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{R(\ell)\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\cosh^{2}(\ell/2)},

\displaystyle\quad\text{$R$ is a degree $n-1$ odd polynomial}.

Moreover, by
Theorem

4.5
, if we regard
P,Q,R
as polynomials
\mathbb{Q}[\pi^{2},\ell]
where
\pi^{2}
has degree
2
, then these three polynomials are homogeneous with strictly positive rational coefficients. To finish the proof, it suffices to show that the constituent monomials in
P,Q,R
, when divided by their corresponding hyperbolic trigonometric denominators, yield zeta values (or
\log 2
) of the correct “degree".

For Case 1:
when
n=a_{1}+a_{2}
is odd, it suffices to show that the integrals

(34)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{2k}\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\sinh(\ell)}

are positive rational multiples of
\zeta(2k+1)
for
k\geqslant 1
, and this follows from the following well-known formula (see
[
13
, Equation 3.523(1)]
):

(35)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{2k}\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\sinh(\ell)}=2(2k)!\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{2k+1}}\right)\zeta(2k+1).

For Cases 2 and 3:
when
n=a_{1}+a_{2}
is even, it suffices to show that the integrals

(36)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{2k+1}\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\sinh^{2}(\ell)},\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{2k+1}\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\cosh^{2}(\ell)}

are positive rational multiples of
\zeta(2k+1)
for
k\geqslant 1
, and also that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\cosh^{2}(\ell)}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\cdot\log 2.

This follows from the following well-known identities (see
[
13
, Equation 3.527(4),(3),(1)]
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\cosh^{2}(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\log 2,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{2k+1}\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\cosh^{2}(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2k+1)!}{2^{2k}}(1-2^{-2k})\zeta(2k+1)\,\quad\text{for }k\geqslant 1,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{2k+1}\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\sinh^{2}(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2k+1)!}{2^{2k}}\zeta(2k+1)\,\quad\text{for }k\geqslant 1.

∎

5.3.
Mirzakhani volumes of moduli spaces of general surfaces

Theorem 5.3
.

For
\Sigma\neq\mathbb{D}_{n},\mathbb{A}_{n}
or
\mathbb{A}_{a_{1},a_{2}}
, let
a_{\max}:=\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant l}\{a_{i}\}
. Then, the Mirzakhani volume
V_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
of
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
is an element of

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{>0}[\pi^{2},b_{1}^{2},\cdots,b_{m}^{2},\zeta(2j+1),\beta(2k)]_{j,k}

with

\displaystyle j,k\geqslant 1;\quad 2j+1,2k\leqslant 6g-6+2(m+l)+a_{\max}+1,

and where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function and
\beta(s)
is the Dirichlet beta function. Moreover,
V_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
, provided that
\pi
is set to be degree
1
, each
b_{i}
is degree 1,
\zeta(2j+1)
is degree
2j+1
, and
\beta(2k)
is degree
2k
. The degrees
\deg_{b_{i}}V_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
are at most
6g-6+2(m+l).

Proof.

We prove this by induction on the number of necks
l
. The
l=0
base case is due to Mirzakhani
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
.

For the induction step: suppose that the statement holds for some
l\geqslant 0
, we prove it for
l+1
. Consider the neck
\nu_{l+1}
of
\Sigma
, and let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be the component of
\Sigma\setminus\nu_{l+1}
left after removing the
(l+1)
-th crown (which is topologically
\mathbb{A}_{a_{l+1}}
). By the induction assumption,

V_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{m},\ell)\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}[\pi^{2},b_{1}^{2},\cdots,b_{m}^{2},\ell^{2},\zeta(2j+1),\beta(2k)]_{j,k},

with
j,k\geqslant 1
and
2j+1,2k\leqslant 6g-6+2(m+1+l)+\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant l}\{a_{i}\}+1
, and where
V_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
is of degree

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{b},\ell)=6g-6+2(m+1)+3l+\sum_{k=1}^{l}a_{k}.

Moreover, the degree
\deg_{\ell}V_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{b},\ell)
is at most
6g-6+2(m+1+l)
.

By
Eq.

18
:

(37)

\displaystyle V_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})=\int_{0}^{\infty}V_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{m},\ell)\cdot\ell\cdot V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{l+1}}}(\ell)\;\mathrm{d}\ell.

We split into two cases.

Case 1:

a_{l+1}
is even. By
Theorem

4.5
, the expression

\ell\cdot V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{l+1}}}(\ell)\cdot\sinh(\ell/2)

is a homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}_{>0}[\pi^{2},\ell^{2}]
of degree
a_{l+1}
, and hence

V_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{m},\ell)\cdot\ell\cdot V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{l+1}}}(\ell)\cdot\sinh(\ell/2)

is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{b},\ell)+a_{l+1}
, whose degree in
\ell
is at most
6g-6+2(m+1+l)+a_{l+1}
. Further, by
Eq.

35
for
k\geqslant 1
,

(38)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{2k}\;\mathrm{d}\ell}{\sinh(\ell/2)}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\cdot\zeta(2k+1),

hence
V_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{b},\ell)+a_{l+1}+1=6g-6+2m+3(l+1)+\sum_{k=1}^{l+1}a_{k}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b}),

and moreover,

V_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}[\pi^{2},b_{1}^{2},\cdots,b_{m}^{2},\zeta(2j+1),\zeta(a_{l+1}+1),\beta(2k)]_{j,k},

with
j,k\geqslant 1
and
2j+1,2k\leqslant 6g-6+2(m+l+1)+\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant l+1}\{a_{i}\}+1
.

Case 2:

a_{l+1}
is odd. By
Theorem

4.5
, the expression

\ell\cdot V_{\mathbb{A}_{a_{l+1}}}(\ell)\cdot\cosh(\ell/2)

is a homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}_{>0}[\pi^{2},\ell]
which is
odd
with respect to
\ell
and has degree
a_{l+1}
. Recall
[
23
]
that the Dirichlet beta function satisfies

\beta(s):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(2n+1)^{s}}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{s-1}e^{-x}}{1+e^{-2x}}dx,\quad\text{where }\mathrm{Re}(s)>0.

Then,

(39)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{2k-1}}{\cosh(\ell/2)}\mathrm{d}\ell=2^{2k+1}(2k-1)!\beta(2k)\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\cdot\beta(2k),

for
k\geqslant 1
, and hence
V_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(\vec{b},\ell)+a_{l+1}+1=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma}(\vec{b}),

and moreover,

V_{\Sigma}(\vec{b})\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}[\pi^{2},b_{1}^{2},\cdots,b_{m}^{2},\zeta(2j+1),\beta(2k),\beta(a_{l+1}+1)]{j,k},

with
j,k\geqslant 1
and
2j+1,2k\leqslant 6g-6+2(m+l+1)+\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant l+1}\{a_{i}\}+1
. The proof is complete.
∎

6.
An upper bound for the Mirzakhani volume of
n
-gons

The aim of this section is to derive an explicit upper bound for
V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}
(
Proposition

6.7
).

Definition 6.1

(fan)
.

We refer to an ideal triangulation of
\mathbb{D}_{n}
comprised of
(n-3)
diagonals emanating from the same vertex (see
Fig.

3
) as a
fan
.

Figure 3
.
Ideal
n
-gon triangulated by diagonals meeting at a vertex. In
blue
: length
1
horocyclic arcs in every ideal triangle; in
red
extensions of some of the segments to a horocyclic decoration of the
n
-gon.

Lemma 6.2
.

The Mirzakhani volume of the moduli space of
n
-gons, expressed in terms of shearing coordinates
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-3}
for a fan, is given by

(40)

V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-3}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-3}(1+e^{-s_{i}})\cdot(1+e^{s_{1}}+e^{s_{1}+s_{2}}+\cdots+e^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3}})}.

Proof.

To determine an expression for this integral, we need

•

a domain of integration,

•

an expression for the Weil–Petersson volume form,

•

and an expression for the Chekhov action.

Since the (pure) mapping class group for a boundary punctured disk is trivial, the moduli space for
n
-gons is equal to the Teichmüller space, and hence is given by
\mathbb{R}^{n-3}
. Furthermore, by
Proposition

3.3
, the Weil–Petersson volume form is given, up to sign, by

\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-3}.

All that remains is determining the Chekhov action functional, which is given in
[
8
, (2.1)]
as
e^{-S}=1/\Pi_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}h_{j}
, where
\lambda_{j}
denotes the lambda-lengths of the boundary arches
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
, and
h_{i}
denotes the length of the decorating horocyclic segments adjoining
\alpha_{i}
and
\alpha_{i+1}
(labelled
n
-cyclically). In particular, this expression is independent of the choice of decorating horocycles, and applies to the one shown in
Fig.

3
.

The product of the lambda lengths
\lambda_{j}
satisfies

\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}=1\cdot e^{-s_{1}/2}\cdot\cdots\cdot e^{-s_{n-3}/2}\cdot 1\cdot e^{-(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3})/2}=\prod_{i=1}^{n-3}e^{-s_{i}}.

The product of the horocycle lengths
h_{j}
is

\prod_{j=1}^{n}h_{i}=1\cdot(1+e^{s_{1}})\cdot\cdots\cdot(1+e^{s_{n}})\cdot 1\cdot(1+e^{s_{1}}+e^{s_{1}+s_{2}}+\cdots+e^{s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{n}}).

Thus,
e^{S}=\Pi_{i=1}^{n}(1+e^{-s_{i}})\cdot(1+e^{s_{1}}+e^{s_{1}+s_{2}}+\cdots+e^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n}})
and hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-3}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n-3}(1+e^{-s_{j}})\cdot\left(1+e^{s_{1}}+e^{s_{1}+s_{2}}+\cdots+e^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3}}\right)},

as required.
∎

Chekhov computed volumes of moduli spaces of
5
-gons and
6
-gons, referring to these as the first non-trivial instances. We take a moment to derive the “trivial” volume
V_{\mathbb{D}_{4}}
:

Proposition 6.3

(volume of the moduli space of
4
-gons)
.

V_{\mathbb{D}_{4}}=1
.

Proof.

Invoking
Eq.

40
,

V_{\mathbb{D}_{4}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{(1+e^{-s})(1+e^{s})}.

Let
u=e^{s},
then
\mathrm{d}u=e^{s}\mathrm{d}s
, hence
\mathrm{d}u/u=\mathrm{d}s
, and hence

V_{\mathbb{D}_{4}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u(1+1/u)(1+u)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{(1+u)^{2}}=-\frac{1}{1+u}\Bigg|_{0}^{\infty}=1.

∎

Remark 6.4
.

The
n=3
volume is, in some sense, a matter of convention. Chekhov’s function in this case is equal to
1
, and it is conventional to set the Weil–Petersson volume measure of a moduli space which is point to be
1
, and so the Mirzakhani volume in this case is
V_{\mathbb{D}_{3}}=1\times 1=1
.

6.1.
Change of variables

For higher values of
n
, we make apply a change of variables to convert the desired volume integral as follows:

Proposition 6.5
.

The (absolute) volume of the moduli space of
n
-gons satisfies

V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\int_{(0,1)^{n-3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}u_{n-3}}{Q_{n-3}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n-3})},

where
\{Q_{j}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j})\}_{j=1,2,\ldots}
is a sequence of polynomials specified by
Q_{1}=1
and

Q_{j}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j})=u_{1}\cdot Q_{j-1}(u_{2},\ldots,u_{j})+(1-u_{1})\cdots(1-u_{j}).

Specifically
Q_{j}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j})
is given by

\displaystyle u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{j}+u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{j-1}(1-u_{j})+\cdots+u_{1}(1-u_{2})\cdots(1-u_{j})+(1-u_{1})\cdots(1-u_{j}).

Proof.

Let

(41)

\displaystyle u_{j}=\frac{1}{1+e^{-s_{j}}},\quad\text{then}\quad\mathrm{d}u_{j}=\frac{e^{-s_{j}}\mathrm{d}s_{j}}{(1+e^{-s_{j}})^{2}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{j}}{1+e^{-s_{j}}}\cdot(1-u_{j}).

Consider now the “longest” factor in the denominator of the integrated function, and set
\{Q_{j}\}
so that

\displaystyle Q_{j}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j})={(1-u_{1})\cdots(1-u_{j})}\cdot{(1+e^{s_{1}}+e^{s_{1}+s_{2}}+\cdots+e^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{j}})},

and hence

V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\int_{(0,1)^{n-3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}u_{n-3}}{Q_{n-3}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n-3})}

by construction. For
j=4
, we have

Q_{1}(u)=(1-u_{1})\cdot(1+e^{s_{1}})=\tfrac{1-u_{1}}{1-u_{1}}=1.

Moreover,

\displaystyle Q_{j+1}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j+1})

\displaystyle={(1-u_{1})\cdots(1-u_{j+1})}\cdot{(1+e^{s_{1}}+e^{s_{1}+s_{2}}+\cdots+e^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{j}})}

\displaystyle=(1-u_{1})\cdots(1-u_{j+1})\cdot\left(1+e^{s_{1}}\cdot\frac{Q_{j}(u_{2},\ldots,u_{j+1})}{(1-u_{2})\cdots(1-u_{j+1})}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-u_{1})\cdots(1-u_{j+1})\cdot\left(1+\frac{u_{1}}{1-u_{1}}\cdot\frac{Q_{j}(u_{2},\ldots,u_{j+1})}{(1-u_{2})\cdots(1-u_{j+1})}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-u_{1})\cdots(1-u_{j+1})+u_{1}\cdot Q_{j}(u_{2},\ldots,u_{j+1}),

which is precisely the recursion on
\{Q_{j}\}
we hope to establish. Therefore, we have affirmed that this is a sequence of polynomials, and in particular,
Q_{j}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j})
is given by

u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{j}+u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{j-1}(1-u_{j})+\cdots+u_{1}(1-u_{2})\cdots(1-u_{j})+(1-u_{1})\cdots(1-u_{j}).

∎

We apply this formula to calculate the volume of the moduli space of
5
-gons, which was first determined in
[
8
]
.

Example 6.6

(volume of the moduli space of
5
-gons)
.

V_{\mathbb{D}_{5}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}

Proof.

When
n=5
, we have:

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{5}}

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}u_{2}}{u_{1}u_{2}+u_{1}(1-u_{2})+(1-u_{1})(1-u_{2})}

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,1)^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}u_{2}}{1-u_{2}+u_{1}u_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\left[\frac{\log(1-u_{2}+u_{1}u_{2})}{u_{2}}\right]_{u_{1}=0}^{u_{1}=1}\mathrm{d}u_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}-\frac{\log(1-u_{2})}{u_{2}}\;\mathrm{d}u_{2}=\left[\text{Li}_{2}(u_{2})\right]_{0}^{1}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

∎

We have had limited success implementing this method of determining Mirzakhani volumes due to our inability to compute these integrals for higher values of
n
.

6.2.
Application: volume upper bound via crowns

Proposition 6.7

(volume upper bound for moduli spaces of
n
-gons)
.

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}\leqslant\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{(n-4)!!}{(n-3)!!}\pi^{n-4},&\quad\text{if }n\text{ is even;}\\[2.5pt]
\frac{(n-4)!!}{2(n-3)!!}\pi^{n-3},&\quad\text{if }n\text{ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

The upper bound is larger than the conjectured value in
7.4
by a factor of
\frac{n-2}{2}
.

Proof.

Rewrite the volume expression
Eq.

40
as

(42)

\begin{split}V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-3}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-3}(1+e^{-s_{i}})\cdot(1+e^{s_{1}}+e^{s_{1}+s_{2}}+\cdots+e^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3}})}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-3}}\frac{e^{\frac{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3}}{2}}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-3}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-3}2\cosh(\frac{s_{i}}{2})\cdot(1+e^{s_{1}}+e^{s_{1}+s_{2}}+\cdots+e^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3}})}\end{split}

Embed
\mathbb{R}^{n-3}
into
\mathbb{R}^{n-2}=(s_{1},\dotsc,s_{n-2})
as the hyperplane
H_{0}=\{s_{1}+\dotsc+s_{n-2}=0\}
. Observe that

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3}}{2}}}{1+e^{s_{1}}+e^{s_{1}+s_{2}}+\cdots+e^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3}}}\leqslant\frac{e^{\frac{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3}}{2}}}{1+e^{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3}}}=\frac{1}{2\cosh(\frac{s_{n-2}}{2})}.

We invoke
Eq.

19
to obtain:

(44)

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}\leqslant\int_{H_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}s_{n-3}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}2\cosh(\frac{s_{i}}{2})}=V_{\mathbb{A}_{n-2}}(0).

Applying
Theorem

4.5
, we obtain the desired bound.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

Here is a more conceptual interpretation for the inequality in
Eq.

44
:

We can cut an
(n-2)
-crown with length
0
neck along the unique simple ideal geodesic going from an arbitrarily fixed tine
q_{k}
to the cusp to obtain an ideal
n
-gon.

•

This is a reversible process as there is a unique way to glue two adjacent sides of an ideal
n
-gon to form an
(n-2)
-crown with neck length
0
, and so induces a homeomorphism between
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{A}_{n-2}}(0)=\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{A}_{n-2}}(0)
.

•

An ideal triangulation
\triangle
on
\mathbb{D}_{n}
glues (along with the glued arc) to an ideal triangulation
\triangle^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{A}_{n-2}
and their corresponding shearing coordinates for
\triangle
and
\triangle^{\prime}
are fixed under the cutting/gluing homemorphism between
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{A}_{n-2}}(0)
, and hence this is a Weil–Petersson volume-preserving homeomorphism.

•

Eq.

43
shows that Chekhov’s action increases with respect to cutting, and hence
V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}\leqslant V_{\mathbb{A}_{n-2}}(0)
.

This is an example of the type of combinatorial/topological phenomena underpinning “cut-and-join”-type equations in topological recursion, but we reserve exploring these themes for an upcoming (and hitherto unnamed) paper.

7.1.
Series representation for volumes of moduli spaces
n
-gons

Theorem 7.1
.

The volume of the moduli space of
n
-gons equals

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n-4}=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k_{1}(k_{1}+k_{2}-1)\cdots(k_{n-5}+k_{n-4}-1)k_{n-4}}.

Proof.

We take as starting the integral expression for
V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}
given by Chekhov (implicitly) in
[
8
, Proposition 6.2]
:

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\int_{0<z_{2}<\cdots<z_{n-2}\leqslant 1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{n-2}}{(z_{3}-z_{1})(z_{4}-z_{2})\cdots(z_{n-2}-z_{n-4})(z_{n-1}-z_{n-3})},

where
z_{1}=0
and
z_{n-1}=1
. Take the change of coordinates given by
y_{j}=\frac{z_{j}}{z_{j+1}}
, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}y_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}y_{n-2}

\displaystyle=(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}z_{2}}{z_{3}}-\tfrac{z_{2}\mathrm{d}z_{3}}{z_{3}^{2}})\wedge\ldots\wedge(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}z_{n-3}}{z_{n-2}}-\tfrac{z_{n-3}\mathrm{d}z_{n-2}}{z_{n-2}^{2}})\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{n-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{n-2}}{z_{3}z_{4}\cdots z_{n-3}z_{n-2}}

the above integral transforms to

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\int_{(0,1)^{n-3}}\frac{dy_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dy_{n-2}}{(1-\frac{z_{1}}{z_{3}})\cdots(1-\frac{z_{n-3}}{z_{n-1}})}=\int_{(0,1)^{n-3}}\frac{dy_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dy_{n-2}}{\prod_{i=2}^{n-3}(1-y_{i}y_{i+1})}.

Expanding the integral as geometric series, we obtain

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}\left(\prod_{i=2}^{n-3}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(y_{i}y_{i+1})^{j}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{2}\cdots\mathrm{d}y_{n-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{2},\cdots,j_{n-3}\geqslant 0}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}\left(\prod_{i=2}^{n-3}(y_{i}y_{i+1})^{j_{i}}\right)dy_{2}\cdots dy_{n-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{2},\cdots,j_{n-3}\geqslant 0}\int_{0}^{1}y_{2}^{j_{2}}\mathrm{d}y_{2}\cdot\int_{0}^{1}y_{3}^{j_{2}+j_{3}}\mathrm{d}y_{3}\cdot\cdots\cdot\int_{0}^{1}y_{n-3}^{j_{n-4}+j_{n-3}}\mathrm{d}y_{n-3}\cdot\int_{0}^{1}y_{n-2}^{j_{n-3}}\mathrm{d}y_{n-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{2},\cdots,j_{n-3}\geqslant 0}\frac{1}{(j_{2}+1)(j_{2}+j_{3}+1)\cdots(j_{n-4}+j_{n-3}+1)(j_{n-3}+1)},

as required (after setting
k_{i}=j_{i+1}+1
).
∎

We now apply
Theorem

7.1
, and obtain expressions for
V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}
for
n\leqslant 8
.

7.2.
Volume for moduli spaces of pentagons and hexagons

Chekhov
[
8
, Section 6.2.2]
showed that
V_{\mathbb{D}_{5}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}
and
V_{\mathbb{D}_{6}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}
, and we verify these as a test-of-principle:

For
n=5
, then:

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{5}}=\sum_{k_{1}\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

For
n=6
, let
k_{1}=a,k_{2}=b
, then:

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{6}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{a(a+b-1)b}=\sum_{a\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{a}\Bigl(\sum_{b\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{(a+b-1)b}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\sum_{a\geqslant 2}\frac{1}{a}\Biggl(\sum_{b\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{a-1}\Bigl(\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{a+b-1}\Bigr)\Biggr)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\sum_{a\geqslant 2}\frac{1+\cdots+\frac{1}{a-1}}{a(a-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\sum_{a\geqslant 2}\Bigl(1+\cdots+\frac{1}{a-1}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{1}{a-1}-\frac{1}{a}\Bigr)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\sum_{a\geqslant 2}\frac{1}{(a-1)^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}.

7.3.
Volume for moduli spaces of heptagons

Proposition 7.2

(volume of the moduli space of
7
-gons)
.

V_{\mathbb{D}_{7}}=\frac{3\pi^{4}}{40}.

Proof.

For
n=7
(heptagon case), let
a=k_{1},b=k_{2},c=k_{3}
. Then

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{7}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b,c\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{a(a+b-1)(b+c-1)c}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{b=1,a,c\geqslant 1}+\sum_{b\geqslant 2,a,c\geqslant 1}\right)\frac{1}{a(a+b-1)(b+c-1)c}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,c\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{a^{2}c^{2}}+\sum_{b\geqslant 2,a,c\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{a(a+b-1)(b+c-1)c}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)^{2}+\sum_{b\geqslant 2,a,c\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{(b-1)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{a+b-1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{c}-\frac{1}{c+b-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{4}}{36}+\sum_{b\geqslant 2}\left(\frac{1+\cdots+\frac{1}{b-1}}{b-1}\right)^{2}=\frac{\pi^{4}}{36}+\frac{17\pi^{4}}{360}=\frac{3\pi^{4}}{40},

where we have invoked a main result in
[
4
]
to convert the series on the last line.
∎

7.4.
Volume for moduli spaces of octagons

Theorem 7.3

(volume of the moduli space of
8
-gons)
.

V_{\mathbb{D}_{8}}=\frac{8\pi^{4}}{45}
.

Proof.

For
n=8
(octagon case), let
a=k_{1},b=k_{2},c=k_{3},d=k_{4}
. We get

(45)

\begin{split}V_{\mathbb{D}_{8}}&=\sum_{a,b,c,d\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{a(a+b-1)(b+c-1)(c+d-1)d}\\
&=\sum_{a,b,c,d\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{ad}\int_{0}^{1}x^{a+b-2}dx\int_{0}^{1}y^{b+c-2}dy\int_{0}^{1}z^{c+d-2}dz\\
&=\sum_{a,b,c,d\geqslant 1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl(\frac{x^{a-1}}{a}\Bigr)\cdot(xy)^{b-1}\cdot(yz)^{c-1}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{z^{d-1}}{d}\Bigr)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\\
&=\sum_{b,c\geqslant 1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(x^{b-2}\log(1-x)\bigr)\bigl(z^{c-2}\log(1-z)\bigr)\cdot y^{b+c-2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\\
&=\Biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log(1-x)}{x}\Biggr)^{2}+2\cdot\Biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log(1-x)}{x}\Biggr)\cdot\Biggl(\sum_{i\geqslant 2}\frac{1+\cdots+\frac{1}{i-1}}{i(i-1)}\Biggr)\\
&+\sum_{b,c\geqslant 2}\int_{0}^{1}\Biggl(\frac{1+\cdots+\frac{1}{b-1}}{b-1}\,y^{b-1}\Biggr)\Biggl(\frac{1+\cdots+\frac{1}{c-1}}{c-1}\,y^{c-1}\Biggr)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\frac{\pi^{4}}{12}+\int_{0}^{1}\Biggl(\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\frac{1+\cdots+\frac{1}{j}}{j}\,y^{j}\Biggr)^{2}\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

We express the integrand obtained in
Eq.

45
:

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\frac{1+\cdots+\frac{1}{j}}{j}\,y^{j}

\displaystyle=\int\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\Bigl(1+\cdots+\frac{1}{j}\Bigr)y^{j-1}dy

\displaystyle=\int-\frac{\log(1-y)}{y(1-y)}\mathrm{d}y=-\int\frac{\log(1-y)}{y}\mathrm{d}y-\int\frac{\log(1-y)}{1-y}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\text{Li}_{2}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}(1-y),

hence

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Biggl(\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\frac{1+\cdots+\frac{1}{j}}{j}\,y^{j}\Biggr)^{2}\mathrm{d}y=\int_{0}^{1}\text{Li}_{2}^{2}(y)+\text{Li}_{2}(y)\log^{2}(1-y)+\frac{1}{4}\log^{4}(1-y)\mathrm{d}y.

We integrate each term separately. First, it is proved in
[
10
]
(see Table 3):

(46)

\int_{0}^{1}\text{Li}_{2}^{2}(y)\mathrm{d}y=6-2\zeta(2)-4\zeta(3)+\frac{5}{2}\zeta(4).

Next, we compute

(47)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}\text{Li}_{2}(y)\log^{2}(1-y)\mathrm{d}y\\
&=12(1-y)-12(1-y)\log(1-y)+5(1-y)\log^{2}(1-y)\\
&-(1-y)\log^{3}(1-y)+2\log^{2}(1-y)\log y-\log^{3}(1-y)\log y\\
&+\big(4-3\log(1-y)\big)\log(1-y)\operatorname{Li}_{2}(1-y)\\
&+2\operatorname{Li}_{2}(y)-(1-y)\big(2-2\log(1-y)+\log^{2}(1-y)\big)\operatorname{Li}_{2}(y)\\
&-4\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1-y)+6\log(1-y)\operatorname{Li}_{3}(1-y)-6\operatorname{Li}_{4}(1-y)\Big|_{0}^{1}\\
&=\bigl(2\text{Li}_{2}(1)\bigr)-\bigl(12-4\text{Li}_{3}(1)-6\text{Li}_{4}(1)\bigr)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-12+4\zeta(3)+\frac{\pi^{4}}{15}.\end{split}

Finally, recognizing a representation of the gamma function, we have

(48)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{4}\log^{4}(1-y)\;\mathrm{d}y=\frac{4!}{4}=6.

Putting
Eq.

46
,
Eq.

47
and
Eq.

48
together, we obtain:

\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(\text{Li}_{2}(y)+\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}(1-y)\bigr)^{2}=\frac{17\pi^{4}}{180}.

Hence

V_{\mathbb{D}_{8}}=\frac{\pi^{4}}{12}+\frac{17\pi^{4}}{180}=\frac{8\pi^{4}}{45}.

∎

7.5.
Conjectural formula and generating function

We observe by eye that the above volumes satisfy a fairly simple algebraic expression involving double factorials:

Conjecture 7.4

(Mirzakhani volume of moduli spaces of
n
-gons)
.

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{2}{n-2}\cdot\frac{(n-4)!!}{(n-3)!!}\pi^{n-4},&\quad\text{if }n\text{ is even,}\\[2.5pt]
\frac{1}{n-2}\cdot\frac{(n-4)!!}{(n-3)!!}\pi^{n-3},&\quad\text{if }n\text{ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

We next derive a generating function for the volumes of moduli spaces of
n
-gons, assuming
7.4
. Since these moduli spaces only start making sense for
n\geqslant 3
, we set the generating function to take the form

P_{\mathbb{D}_{\bullet}}(x):=\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}V_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}\;x^{n-3}.

Proposition 7.5

(generating function for volumes of moduli spaces of
n
-gons)
.

Assuming
7.4
, the generating function
P_{\mathbb{D}_{\bullet}}(x)
for the Mirzakhani volumes of moduli spaces of
n
-gons is

(49)

\displaystyle P_{\mathbb{D}_{\bullet}}(x)=\frac{\arcsin{\pi x}}{\pi x}+x\Bigl(\frac{\arcsin{\pi x}}{\pi x}\Bigr)^{2}.

Proof.

We first note that
\frac{\arcsin{\pi x}}{\pi x}
is an even function and
x\left(\frac{\arcsin{\pi x}}{\pi x}\right)^{2}
is an odd function, and hence they respectively correspond to the odd
n
volume terms and even
n
volume terms in the generating function. Now, note that the Taylor series expansion of
\arcsin{x}
at
x=0
is
[
13
, Equation 1.641(1)]
:

\arcsin{x}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2j-1)!!}{(2j)!!}\frac{x^{2j+1}}{2j+1}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{\arcsin{\pi x}}{\pi x}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2j-1)!!\pi^{2n}}{(2j)!!}\frac{x^{2j}}{2j+1}.

Which yields the desired volumes for
n-3=2j
. Similarly
[
13
, Equation 1.645(2)]
:

(\arcsin{x})^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\cdot(2j)!!}{(2j+2)\cdot(2j+1)!!}x^{2j+2},

and hence

x\left(\frac{\arcsin{\pi x}}{\pi x}\right)^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\cdot(2j)!!\;\pi^{2j}}{(2j+2)\cdot(2j+1)!!}x^{2j+1}.

which are precisely the desired volumes for
n-3=2j+1
.
∎

A.1.
Volume of the moduli space of 2-crowns via lambda-lengths

We give an alternative proof of
Theorem

4.5
in the case
n=2
:

V_{\mathbb{A}_{2}}(d)=\frac{d/2}{\sinh{d/2}},

using
\lambda
-lengths. Consider the triangulation of the 2-crown by a single arc with endpoints at the boundary puncture
q_{1}
, and decorate it with unit horocycles as in Figure
4
. Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}
be the lambda lengths of the resulting truncated arcs as in Figure
4
.

Figure 4
.
Crown with two tines, triangulated by an arc. In
blue
: unit horocycles.

We express the horocycle lengths in terms of the lambda lengths (see
[
20
, Lemma 4.9]
, page 36, and
[
14
, Note 3.4]
for the formulas we use):

(50)

1=\frac{2\cosh{d/2}}{\lambda_{3}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}},

(51)

1=\frac{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}.

Multiplying
Eq.

50
by
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}/\lambda_{2}
and using that
\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}
from
Eq.

51
, we get:

(52)

\frac{2\lambda_{1}\cosh{d/2}}{\lambda_{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}+1=\frac{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{2}}=\lambda_{1}^{2}.

Next, following
[
8
, Page 9]
, the decoration-independent coordinate
x_{1}
is defined as

x_{1}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}},

thus we can rewrite
Eq.

52
as

(53)

2x_{1}\cosh d/2+x_{1}^{2}+1=\lambda_{1}^{2}.

From
Eq.

53
, Chekhov’s functional can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle e^{-S}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}=\frac{x_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}=\frac{x_{1}}{x_{1}^{2}+2x_{1}\cosh d/2+1}.

By
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
, the volume form on the moduli space of 2-crowns equals

\displaystyle\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{A}_{2}}(d)=\mathrm{d}\log x_{1}=\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}

Putting it together, we have

(54)

\begin{split}V_{\mathbb{A}_{2}}(d)&=\int e^{-S}\Omega^{\mathrm{WP}}_{\mathbb{A}_{2}}(d)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x_{1}}{x_{1}^{2}+2x_{1}\cosh d/2+1}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{(x_{1}+\cosh d/2)^{2}-\sinh^{2}d/2}.\end{split}

Let
u=x_{1}+\cosh d/2;a=\sinh d/2
, then

(55)

\begin{split}V_{\mathbb{A}_{2}}(d)&=\int_{\sqrt{a^{2}+1}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u^{2}-a^{2}}=\int_{\sqrt{a^{2}+1}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2a}\Bigl(\frac{1}{u-a}-\frac{1}{u+a}\Bigr)\mathrm{d}u\\
&=\frac{1}{2a}\log\Bigl(\frac{u-a}{u+a}\Bigr)\Big|_{\sqrt{a^{2}+1}}^{\infty}=\frac{1}{2a}\log\Biggl(\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+1}+a}{\sqrt{a^{2}+1}-a}\Biggr)\\
&=\frac{1}{2\sinh d/2}\log\frac{e^{d/2}}{e^{-d/2}}=\frac{d/2}{\sinh d/2}.\end{split}

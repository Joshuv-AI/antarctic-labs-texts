The Cayley-Moser problem with Poissonian arrival of offers
Guy Katriel

Abstract
We study a version of the classical Cayley-Moser optimal stopping problem, in which a seller must sell an asset by a given deadline, with the offers, which are independent random variables with a known distribution, arriving at random times, as a Poisson process. This continuous-time formulation of the problem is much more analytically tractable than the analogous discrete-time problem which is usually considered, leading to a simple differential equation that can be explicitly solved to find the optimal policy. We study the performance of this optimal policy, and obtain explicit expressions for the distribution of the realized sale price, as well as for the distribution of the stopping time. The general results are used to explore characteristics of the optimal policy and of the resulting bidding process, and are illustrated by application to several specific instances of the offer distribution.



1
Introduction

The Cayley-Moser problem
[
3
,
21
]
is a well-known
optimal stopping problem, in which a seller
must sell an asset by a given deadline. Potential buyers’ price offers arrive sequentially, and are independent and identically distributed (iid) according to a known distribution (which will be called the ‘offer distribution’). The seller must decide whether to
accept or reject each offer, with acceptance leading to sale at the offered price and the end of the process. Rejected offers cannot be recalled, and if the deadline is reached then the seller must accept some ‘salvage value’. The seller’s aim is to choose a policy - a rule for deciding whether
or not to accept an offer, depending on its
value and on the time remaining until the deadline,
so as to maximize the expected sale price. The problem thus addresses, in a simple context, the fundamental tradeoff between the potential gain and the risk from waiting.

The standard version of this problem is a ‘discrete-time’ one, in which the number of offers until the deadline is a given integer
n

[
2
,
6
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
21
]
. Analysis of this problem leads to an optimal policy defined by an increasing sequence of threshold values (reservation prices)
\mu_{n}
(
n=1,2,3,\cdots
), so that an offer
x
arriving when
the number of remaining offers is
n\geq 1
is
accepted if
x\geq\mu_{n}
. The sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}
is defined by
the nonlinear recursion,

\mu_{0}=0,\;\;\;\mu_{n}=E[\max(X,\mu_{n-1})],\;\;n\geq 1,

(1.1)

where
X
is a random variable distributed according to the offer distribution,
which implies that, although the values can be computed
\mu_{n}
iteratively, an explicit expression for these values is not
available. We note that the value
\mu_{n}
also represents the expectation of the realized sale price, when the maximal number of offers is
n
and the optimal policy is followed.

In recent years considerable attention has been given to the Cayley-Moser problem, and generalizations of it, in the context of
prophet inequalities
, which bound the expectation of the
realized sale price under the optimal policy from below, in terms of the expected price achieved by a ‘prophet’ who can observe all offers in advance, and choose the maximal one. Relatedly, there has also been interest in obtaining simple policies, e.g. with only a single threshold rather than a time-varying one, which are sub-optimal but which can be proved to attain results approximating those of the optimal policy up to a factor (see
[
1
,
4
,
14
,
17
,
18
]
and references therein). In the present work, however, we are focused on the precise characterization of the optimal policy, and of its performance measures.

Here we study a continuous-time version
of the Cayley-Moser problem, in which the (iid) offers
arrive as a Poisson process with given rate

\lambda
, and the time until the deadline is given by a positive real number
t>0
.
The sought-after policy, which maximizes the expectation of the sale price, is defined by
a threshold function
\mu(t)
, so that an offer
x
made when the remaining time to the deadline is
t
will be accepted if
x\geq\mu(t)
.

It may be argued that this Poissonian model with random arrival times is more ‘realistic’ than the discrete-time model which assumes a fixed and known number of offers, but the real motivation underlying this work
is the observation that this model is in fact much more analytically tractable then the
discrete-time model, allowing us to obtain a range of explicit results which cannot be obtained for the discrete-time model.

As already shown in
[
15
]
(see also
[
1
,
5
,
7
,
22
]
for related results), in the Poissonian case the optimal policy
\mu(t)
can be obtained in an explicit form, which
is unattainable in the discrete case. This stems from the fact that while the discrete model
leads to a sequence of thresholds defined by the
nonlinear recursion (
1.1
), which cannot be solved in closed form, the Poissonian model leads to a
a simple differential equation for the optimal
policy
\mu(t)
, which can be solved by a direct integration (see subsection
2.2
). As an example, when
the offers are uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
,
the discrete model leads, using (
1.1
), to the sequence defined by the recursion

\mu_{0}=0,\;\;\;\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\mu_{n-1}^{2}),\;\;n\geq 1,

(1.2)

for which an explicit expression is not available (even the study of the precise asymptotic behavior of the above-defined sequence as
n\rightarrow\infty
is a delicate question
[
8
,
10
]
).
By contrast, for the corresponding Poissonian model we obtain an

explicit
expression for the optimal policy, given by
(see subsection
2.3.1
)

\mu(t)=1-\frac{2}{\lambda t+4}.

(1.3)

In this work we will show, furthermore, that in the Poissonian model it is possible to obtain detailed and explicit
results regarding the
performance
of the optimal policy, which have not been obtained in previous works.

The outcome of
applying the optimal policy, starting at a time when the deadline is
t
time units in the future, is a random variable
S_{t}
- the
realized sale price
. We will see that the
expectation of this random variable is
E[S_{t}]=\mu(t)
- so that the function
\mu(t)
plays a dual role, both as the optimal policy and as the expected gain.
It is also important, however, to evaluate the
variance
of
S_{t}
, which will indicate the range of likely sale prices under the optimal policy.
In fact we will be able to obtain a formula for the full
probability density
of
S_{t}
. Here, again, derivation of
a differential equation and its solution, this time a first order linear differential equation, will play a key role.
The probability density takes an interesting form,
with different analytic expressions in each of three intervals. Such explicit results on the probability distribution of the realized sale price cannot be attained for the discrete-time version of the model.

Another important performance metric to evaluate is the
time to sale
, that is the stopping time: assuming the deadline for performing the sale is initially
t
time units in the future, how much of this available duration will be exploited, when the optimal policy is followed?
We will obtain an explicit formula for the distribution of the random variable
T_{t}
, the time to sale, assuming the optimal policy is followed starting when the time to the deadline is
t
. Consequently, we will also obtain expressions for the expectation and the variance of
T_{t}
. In previous literature
[
8
,
20
]
,
asymptotic
results for the
expectation and variance of the stopping time have been obtained
for the discrete-time version of the problem. We will show that our exact
formulas for the continuous-time case have
the same asymptotics.

All the results described above will be
illustrated by application to several specific offer
distributions. They can be applied to any desired offer distribution, although it may be necessary to evaluate some integrals numerically.
We also carry out some numerical simulations of the bidding process, and demonstrate the perfect fit of our analytical results with the statistics collected from the simulations.

This work is divided into four subsequent sections. In section
2
we derive
the optimal policy function
\mu(t)
, and use this result to explore general properties of this function, giving a
characterization of functions that can arise as optimal policies, as well as bounds on this function in terms of moments of the offer distribution.
In section
3

we obtain the full distribution of the realized sale price
S_{t}
, and compute the variance of this distribution. In
section
4
we obtain an explicit expression for
the distribution of the time to sale

T_{t}
, and use it to compute the expectation and variance of
T_{t}
.
In section
5
we
use the results obtained in the
previous sections to obtain the asymptotics of the expectation and variance of the sale price, as
t\rightarrow\infty
, under
appropriate assumptions on the tail
behavior of the offer distribution. We also study the the limiting distribution of
\frac{1}{t}T_{t}
as

t\rightarrow\infty
.

More detailed overviews of the contents are given at the beginning of each section.

2
The optimal policy

After defining the bidding process
with Poissonian offer arrivals in subsection
2.1
, in subsection
2.2
we will derive the
expression for the optimal policy
\mu(t)
, a result which has already
been derived in
[
15
]
, though we
use a different approach to obtain it, which will also serve us in the derivation of the distribution of the realized sale price in section
3
. In subsection
2.3
we apply the general result to determine explicit expressions for optimal policies for some specific examples of
offer distributions.
In subsection
2.4

we will derive some general properties
of the optimal policy function
\mu(t)
,
and prove that these properties completely characterize the class of functions which may arise as optimal policies. In subsection
2.5
we show that under certain restrictions on the right tail of the offer distribution, we can obtain simple bounds on the growth of the optimal policy function
\mu(t)
.

2.1
Problem setup and notation

The inputs to the problem are:

•

The
marketing period
: a time duration
t

during which the sale must be completed.
It should be kept in mind that
as time proceeds,
t

decreases
, with
t=0
corresponding to the deadline.

•

The rate
\lambda
of arrival of offers. Offers arrive as a Poisson process, so that
the distribution of the time durations
D
between
consecutive offers is given by

P(D\leq d)=1-e^{-\lambda d}.

(2.1)

•

The
offer distribution
from which
the iid price offers are to be drawn. This distribution will
be characterized by a cumulative distribution function (CDF)
F:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
. We do not restrict the offers to be non-negative. We will use
X
to denote a generic random variable which
is distributed according to
F
:

P(X\leq x)=F(x).

(2.2)

Denoting
X_{+}=max(X,0)
, it will be assumed that

E[X_{+}]=\int_{0}^{\infty}xdF(x)<\infty.

(2.3)

If the support of the offer distribution is bounded from above, we will denote the maximum of its support by

M=\sup\;\{x\;|\;F(x)<1\},

(2.4)

with
M=+\infty
if the support is unbounded.
If
F
is absolutely continuous, we will denote
the corresponding probability density by
f(x)
.

•

The
residual price distribution
(or ‘salvage value’):
If the deadline is reached before an offer has been accepted, the asset will be sold at a price drawn from this distribution. We denote the corresponding cumulative distribution function by

F_{0}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
, and a generic random variable with this distribution by
X_{0}
. We will assume

E[|X_{0}|]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|dF_{0}(x)<\infty.

(2.5)

Defining

\mu_{0}\doteq E[X_{0}]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xdF_{0}(x),

(2.6)

we will also assume that

0\leq\mu_{0}<M,

(2.7)

where
M
is given by
(
2.4
)
.
The purpose of the assumption
\mu_{0}<M
is
to avoid the trivial situation

\mu_{0}\geq M
in which any offer one can receive during the marketing period is no higher than the expected residual price, so that to maximize the expected sale price one should always wait until the deadline.
The assumption that
\mu_{0}\geq 0
makes the formulation of some results more convenient, and it represents no real loss of generality, since if
E[X_{0}]<0
one can
always add a constant to both
X_{0}
and
X
,
to obtain a problem which is equivalent, and which satisfies
\mu_{0}\geq 0
. Note that (
2.7
) implies
M>0
, so that not all potential offers are negative.

During the bidding process, offers arrive as
a Poisson process with inter-arrival durations

D_{i}
(
i=1,2,..
), so that the time
t_{i}
remaining when the
i
-th offer arrives is

t_{i}=(t_{i-1}-D_{i})_{+}=\max(t_{i-1}-D_{i},0),\;\;\;t_{0}=t,

(2.8)

where
D_{i}
are iid and distributed according to (
2.1
). The size of the
i
-th offer is
X_{i}
, iid random variables distributed according to (
2.2
). The seller must decide
whether to accept the offer
X_{i}
, in which case
the bidding process ends with payoff (realized sale price)
S_{t}=X_{i}
and stopping time (time to sale)
T_{t}=t-t_{i}
, or to wait for the next offer, in which case the current offer cannot be recalled. The process also ends
if
t_{i}
given by (
2.8
) satisfies
t_{i}=0
, so that the deadline is reached, in which case the payoff is a random value
S_{t}=X_{0}
distributed according to the residual price distribution
F_{0}
and the stopping time is
T_{t}=t
.

Regarding the residual price distribution, we can envisage different scenarios depending on the nature of the asset to be sold. If the asset is ephemeral,
e.g.
a concert ticket or perishable food, then
its value drops to
0
after the deadline (the concert date or expiration date), so that
X_{0}=0
, hence
F_{0}(x)=1
for all
x>0
. Another possibility, which will be
used in our examples, is that
F_{0}=F
, which can be interpreted to mean that if the deadline is reached before an offer has been accepted then the seller waits until the arrival of the next offer, and accepts it unconditionally.

We have thus defined two important
random variables, parameterized by the marketing period
t
, associated with the bidding process, and assuming the optimal policy is followed: the realized sale price
S_{t}
and the time to sale
T_{t}
. After determining the optimal policy, we will study the distribution of these two random variables.

2.2
Determination of the optimal policy

We define the value function
V(t,x)
:
assuming an offer of
x
arrives when the time to
the deadline is
t
,
V(t,x)
is the maximum
expected value that can be achieved. If the offer
is accepted then
V(t,x)=x
, while if
it is rejected then this value will be

\mu(t)\doteq E[V((t-D)_{+},X)],

(2.9)

where
D
is the duration to the
next offer, which is exponentially distributed
with rate
\lambda
, and
X
is distributed according to the offer distribution, with
X,D
independent. We thus have
the Bellman equation

V(t,x)=\max(x,E[V((t-D)_{+},X)])=\max(x,\mu(t)),

(2.10)

together with the boundary condition:

V(0,x)=\mu_{0},

(2.11)

where
\mu_{0}=E[X_{0}]
is the expectation of the
residual distribution
F_{0}
.

By (
2.10
), an offer of
x
at time
t
to the deadline should be accepted if
x\geq\mu(t)
(in fact if
x=\mu(t)
one is indifferent between acceptance and rejection, but since this is an event of probability
0
none of our result will be affected by the decision in such a case). Thus
\mu(t)
provides us with the threshold defining the optimal policy.

To find the function
\mu(t)
, we first use (
2.9
),(
2.11
) to write

\displaystyle\mu(t)

\displaystyle=E[V((t-D)_{+},X)]=P(D\geq t)\mu_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\lambda e^{-\lambda(t-s)}E[V(s,X)]ds

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda t}\mu_{0}+e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda e^{\lambda s}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}V(s,u)dF(u)\right)ds.

(2.12)

Now, using (
2.10
), we compute

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}V(s,u)dF(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\max(u,\mu(s))dF(u)

\displaystyle=\mu(s)\int_{-\infty}^{\mu(s)}dF(u)+\int_{\mu(s)}^{\infty}udF(u)=\mu(s)F(\mu(s))+\sigma(\mu(s)),

(2.13)

where in the last term we use the function defined
by

\sigma(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}udF(u).

(2.14)

Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.2
), we have
the following Volterra-type integral equation:

\mu(t)=e^{-\lambda t}\mu_{0}+e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda e^{\lambda s}\left[\mu(s)F(\mu(s))+\sigma(\mu(s))\right]ds.

(2.15)

Note that by setting
t=0
we obtain that

\mu(0)=\mu_{0}.

(2.16)

To solve (
2.15
), we multiply both sides by

e^{\lambda t}
and then differentiate both sides, leading to

e^{\lambda t}[\mu^{\prime}(t)+\lambda\mu(t)]=\lambda e^{\lambda t}\left[\sigma(\mu(t))+F(\mu(t))\mu(t)\right],

which we can write as

\mu^{\prime}(t)=\lambda\sigma(\mu(t))-\lambda[1-F(\mu(t))]\mu(t).

(2.17)

Using an integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\sigma(x)-(1-F(x))x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}udF(u)-x\int_{x}^{\infty}dF(u)=\int_{x}^{\infty}(u-x)dF(u)=-\int_{x}^{\infty}(u-x)d[1-F(u)]

(2.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(u-x)[1-F(u)]\Big|_{u=x}^{u=\infty}+\int_{x}^{\infty}[1-F(u)]du=\int_{x}^{\infty}[1-F(u)]du,

so that, defining the function

\phi(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}[1-F(u)]du,

(2.19)

we have obtained the following

Theorem 2.1
.

The optimal policy
\mu(t)
is given by the solution of the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\mu^{\prime}(t)=\lambda\phi(\mu(t))\\
\mu(0)=\mu_{0},\end{cases}

(2.20)

where
\phi
is defined by (
2.19
).
(
2.20
) can be integrated to obtain the
explicit expression

\mu(t)=\Psi^{-1}(\lambda t),

(2.21)

where
\Psi^{-1}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[\mu_{0},M)
is the inverse of the function

\Psi(x)\doteq\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{du}{\phi(u)},\;\;\;\;x\in[\mu_{0},M).

(2.22)

The function
\phi
is of central importance throughout this work, since, as will be seen, all the explicit formulas for various quantities that will be obtained will involve this function.
We note that this function can also
be given a probabilistic expression:

\phi(x)=E[(X-x)_{+}].

(2.23)

In case
F
is absolutely continuous, so that a density
f=F^{\prime}
exists, we have an alternative expression for the function
\phi
, given by (see (
2.18
))

\phi(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}(u-x)f(u)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}uf(x+u)du.

(2.24)

As a final important remark, note that,
denoting by
S_{t}
the random variable describing the realized sale price assuming the bidding process starts

t
time units prior to the deadline and the optimal policy is employed, we have

E[S_{t}]=E[V((t-D)_{+},X)]=\mu(t)

(2.25)

(
D
is the time we wait for the first offer, and
X
its value), hence the function
\mu(t)
, which we now know how to compute, describes both the threshold value for accepting an offer and the expected sale price, when the time to the deadline is
t
.

2.3
Examples

In the following we apply the result of
Theorem
2.1
to compute the
optimal policy
for several examples of offer distributions
F
. Note that, since the
residual distribution
F_{0}

influences the function
\mu(t)

only through its expectation
\mu_{0}
, there is no need to assume a specific form for this distribution.

The explicit expressions for
\mu(t)

allow us to easily obtain the asymptotic
behavior of
\mu(t)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, and we compare these results
with those obtained in
[
8
]

in the discrete-time case, where explicit expressions for the optimal policy
\mu_{n}
are not available, and show that they agree. A more general discuss of asymptotics of
\mu(t)
is made in subsection
5.1
below.

2.3.1
Uniform offer distribution

We assume the offers are uniformly
distributed on
[a,b]
, so that
their probability density is

f(x)=\frac{1}{b-a}\chi_{[a,b]}(x),

(2.26)

where
\chi_{[a,b]}
denotes the
characteristic function of the interval

[a,b]
. The residual distribution

F_{0}(x)
can be arbitrary, but to avoid the trivial case in which any offer made before the deadline will be rejected, we assume
\mu_{0}=E(X_{0})\in[0,b)
(see (
2.7
)).

We then have, using (
2.24
),

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{0}^{\infty}u\chi_{[a,b]}(u+x)du=\frac{1}{b-a}\begin{cases}\int_{a-x}^{b-x}udu&x<a\\
\int_{0}^{b-x}udu&a\leq x<b\\
0&x\geq b\end{cases}=\begin{cases}\frac{a+b}{2}-x&x<a\\
\frac{(b-x)^{2}}{2(b-a)}&a\leq x<b\\
0&v\geq b\end{cases}

(2.27)

Because of the piecewise nature of the function
\phi
, we now need to
consider two cases:

(1) Assuming first that
\mu_{0}\in[a,b]
, we have, using (
2.22
), for
\mu_{0}\leq x<M=b
,

\displaystyle\Psi(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dv}{\phi(v)}=2(b-a)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dv}{(b-v)^{2}}=2(b-a)\left[\frac{1}{b-x}-\frac{1}{b-\mu_{0}}\right],

so that, by (
2.21
),

\mu(t)=\Psi^{-1}(\lambda t)=b-\frac{2(b-a)}{\lambda t+2\cdot\frac{b-a}{b-\mu_{0}}}.

(2.28)

For example, in the case of a uniform distribution on
[0,1]
(
a=0,b=1
), and assuming that reaching the deadline
before making a sale leads to loss of the sale, we should take
\mu_{0}=0
in the above expression, giving

\mu(t)=\frac{\lambda t}{\lambda t+2},

while in the case
that one accepts the first offer
following the deadline, we take
\mu_{0}=\frac{a+b}{2}=\frac{1}{2}
,
giving (
1.3
).

(2) Assuming now that
\mu_{0}\in[0,a)
, we need to modify our calculation. For

x\in[\mu_{0},a]
we have

\Psi(x)=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dv}{\phi(v)}=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dv}{\frac{a+b}{2}-v}dv=\ln\left(\frac{a+b-2\mu_{0}}{a+b-2x}\right),

while for
x\in[a,b)

\Psi(x)=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{a}\frac{dv}{\phi(v)}+\int_{a}^{x}\frac{dv}{\phi(v)}=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{a}\frac{dv}{\frac{a+b}{2}-v}dv+2(b-a)\int_{a}^{x}\frac{dv}{(b-v)^{2}}=\ln\left(\frac{a+b-2\mu_{0}}{b-a}\right)+2\cdot\frac{x-a}{b-x}.

Defining

t^{*}=\Psi(a)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\left(\frac{a+b-2\mu_{0}}{b-a}\right),

the above expressions lead to

\mu(t)=\Psi^{-1}(\lambda t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(a+b-(a+b-2\mu_{0})e^{-\lambda t})&0\leq t\leq t^{*}\\
b-\frac{2(b-a)}{\lambda(t-t^{*})+2}&t>t^{*}.\end{cases}

(2.29)

Note that when
t<t^{*}
we have

\mu(t)<a
, and since offers are in the range
[a,b]
, this means that when the time to the deadline is less than
t^{*}
, all offers will be accepted. The precise
value of
\mu(t)
is thus not important
as a threshold when
t\leq t^{*}
, but it does provide the value
E[S_{t}]
.

In terms of asymptotic behavior, (
2.28
),(
2.29
) imply

\mu(t)=b-\frac{2(b-a)}{\lambda t}+O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)\;\;\;{\mbox{ as }}t\rightarrow+\infty,

(2.30)

which is analogous to the asymptotic
formula obtained in
[
8
]
(eq. 20, replacing
n
by
\lambda t
) for the discrete-time version of the problem, in which case a closed formula for the
threshold values is not attainable.

2.3.2
Exponential offer distribution

We assume now that the offers are exponentially distributed with mean
\eta
:

f(x)=\frac{1}{\eta}e^{-\frac{x}{\eta}},\;\;\;F(x)=1-e^{-\frac{x}{\eta}},\;\;\;x\geq 0.

(2.31)

By (
2.19
) we have

\phi(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{u}{\eta}}du=\eta e^{-\frac{x}{\eta}},

(2.32)

by (
2.22
),

\Psi(x)=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{du}{\phi(u)}=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{1}{\eta}e^{\frac{u}{\eta}}du=e^{\frac{x}{\eta}}-e^{\frac{\mu_{0}}{\eta}},

so, by (
2.21
),

\mu(t)=\Psi^{-1}(\lambda t)=\eta\ln\left(\lambda t+e^{\frac{\mu_{0}}{\eta}}\right).

(2.33)

(
2.33
) agrees
with the large
t
asymptotic expression obtained in
[
8
]
(eq.14) for the
discrete-time version of the problem.

2.3.3
Pareto offer distribution

We consider the Pareto distribution (with
\alpha>1
)

\displaystyle f(x)=\begin{cases}0&x<x_{m}\\
\frac{\alpha x_{m}^{\alpha}}{x^{\alpha+1}}&x\geq x_{m}\end{cases},\;\;\;F(x)=\begin{cases}0&x<x_{m}\\
1-\left(\frac{x_{m}}{x}\right)^{\alpha}&x\geq x_{m}\end{cases}.

(2.34)

with expectation

E[X]=\frac{\alpha x_{m}}{\alpha-1}.

(2.35)

The residual distribution is arbitrary, but to somewhat simplify calculations we will assume now that

\mu_{0}=E[X_{0}]\geq x_{m}.

Using (
2.19
) we obtain, for

x\geq x_{m}
,

2.4
Characterization of optimal policy functions

Since the function
\mu(t)
plays two important roles, representing both the optimal sale policy and the expected value

of the realized sale price under this policy, it is
of interest to study its properties. For any given
offer distribution we can compute

using Theorem

(as we have done in the examples in subsection

need to compute an integral numerically, but we are also interested in deriving general characteristics of this function which are not dependent on the particular offer distribution, both those which are shared by the policy function for any choice of the distributions

F_{0}
, and those which are
valid for certain classes of offer distributions.

In the next theorem we derive a series

the second part of the theorem shows
that any function

which satisfies this list of properties is an optimal policy for some choice of the offer and residual distributions. Thus the properties listed in fact characterize the optimal policy functions, and any additional properties of optimal policy functions, which do not
follow from those in the list, must therefore depend on specific assumptions about the offer distribution. In section
2.3.3
, we will restrict
the offer distribution in several ways, to obtain further properties of
\mu(t)
. A natural question for future research is whether in the discrete-time case it is possible to obtain a result analogous to Theorem
2.2
, characterizing the sequences
\{\mu_{n}\}
which can be threshold sequences corresponding to some offer distribution.

2.5
Inequalities for the optimal policy in terms of moments of the offer distribution

Since Theorem
2.2
characterizes
optimal policy functions, we cannot hope to obtain any general properties of these functions beyond those listed in this theorem, or derived from them.
However, in this subsection we will show that by suitably restricting the distribution
F(x)
, we can derive further properties of the corresponding functions
\mu(t)
, in particular regarding their growth.

The examples in subsection
2.3

show offer distributions with different
types of tail behavior, whose corresponding
optimal policy functions
\mu(t)
grow at different rates as the marketing period
t
increases. We now derive some bounds
relating tail behavior of the distribution
F(x)
to the growth of the
corresponding optimal policy function
\mu(t)
.

The results of this subsection have the advantage of providing bounds on
\mu(t)

valid for all
t
, but since they are only upper bounds, they do not
determine the precise asymptotic behavior
of
\mu(t)
. In subsection
5.1
we will derive several results which
require stronger assumptions on the
behavior of the offer distributions, and provide information on the
asymptotic behavior of
\mu(t)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume the offer distribution satisfies
E[X_{+}^{p}]<\infty
(
p>1
). Then for any
\mu_{0}\geq 0
, the corresponding optimal policy satisfies, for all
t\geq 0
,

\mu(t)\leq\mu_{0}+E[(X-\mu_{0})_{+}^{p}]^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot(\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.52)

In particular, this implies that

\mu(t)=O(t^{\frac{1}{p}})
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Note that for the Pareto distribution (
2.34
) we have
E(X^{p})<\infty

for all
p<\alpha
, and the exact result (
2.37
) shows that
\mu(t)=O(t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})
.

Proof.

Assuming
x>\mu_{0}
, we have

X>x\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;(X-\mu_{0})_{+}^{p}>(x-\mu_{0})^{p},

so, using Markov’s inequality,

1-F(x)=P(X>x)=P((X-\mu_{0})_{+}^{p}>(x-\mu_{0})^{p})\leq\frac{E[(X-\mu_{0})_{+}^{p}]}{(x-\mu_{0})^{p}},

hence, for
x>\mu_{0}

\phi(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}(1-F(u))du\leq E[(X-\mu_{0})_{+}^{p}]\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(u-\mu_{0})^{p}}du=\frac{E[(X-\mu_{0})_{+}^{p}]}{(p-1)(x-\mu_{0})^{p-1}},

so that by (
2.22
)

\Psi(x)=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dv}{\phi(v)}\geq\frac{p-1}{E[(X-\mu_{0})_{+}^{p}]}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}(v-\mu_{0})^{p-1}dv=\frac{(x-\mu_{0})^{p}}{E[(X-\mu_{0})_{+}^{p}]}.

Substituting
x=\mu(t)
and recalling (
2.21
),
we have

\lambda t=\Psi(\mu(t))\geq\frac{(\mu(t)-\mu_{0})^{p}}{E[(X-\mu_{0})_{+}^{p}]},

which, solved for
\mu(t)
, gives (
2.52
).
∎

For distributions whose right tail decays at least exponentially, the following inequality shows that the growth of

\mu(t)
is at most logarithmic.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume the offer distribution satisfies
E[e^{\delta X_{+}}]<\infty
(
\delta>0
). Then, for any
\mu_{0}\geq 0
, the corresponding optimal policy satisfies, for all
t\geq 0
,

\mu(t)\leq\mu_{0}+\frac{1}{\delta}\ln\left(E[e^{\delta(X-\mu_{0})_{+}}]\cdot\lambda t+1\right).

(2.53)

Proof.

For
x>\mu_{0}
, we have, using Markov’s inequality,

1-F(x)=P((X-\mu_{0})_{+}>x-\mu_{0})=P(e^{\delta(X-\mu_{0})_{+}}>e^{\delta(x-\mu_{0})})\leq\frac{E[e^{\delta(X-\mu_{0})_{+}}]}{e^{\delta(x-\mu_{0})}}

hence, for
x>\mu_{0}

\phi(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}(1-F(u))du\leq E[e^{\delta(X-\mu_{0})_{+}}]\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-\delta(u-\mu_{0})}du=E[e^{\delta(X-\mu_{0})_{+}}]\frac{1}{\delta}e^{-\delta(x-\mu_{0})}

\Psi(x)=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dv}{\phi(v)}\geq\frac{\delta}{E[e^{\delta(X-\mu_{0})_{+}}]}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}e^{\delta(v-\mu_{0})}dv=\frac{e^{\delta(x-\mu_{0})}-1}{E[e^{\delta(X-\mu_{0})_{+}}]}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\lambda t=\Psi(\mu(t))\geq\frac{e^{\delta(\mu(t)-\mu_{0})}-1}{E[e^{\delta(X-\mu_{0})_{+}}]},

which, solved for
\mu(t)
, gives (
2.53
).
∎

3
Distribution of the realized sale price

Recall that
S_{t}
is the random variable
describing the realized sale price, assuming the marketing period is
t
and the
optimal policy is followed.
Define the cumulative distribution function of
S_{t}
by

G_{t}(x)=P(S_{t}\leq x).

Our aim is to obtain explicit expressions for
G_{t}(x)
and for the corresponding probability density. In all the developments below, it is assumed that the
function
\mu(t)=E[S_{t}]
is known, since we have already shown how to compute it in section
2
. In subsection
3.2
, we will use the expression for
G_{t}(x)
to derive a formula for the variance
Var[S_{t}]
, and in subsection
3.3

we will apply the results to specific offer distributions.

3.1
Computing the sale price distribution

We use a ‘first-step analysis’, assuming we start when the remaining time is
t
and the first offer
X
arrives
after time
D
.

Assume first that
D\leq t
. If
X\geq\mu(t-D)
, the first offer is accepted and we thus have

\displaystyle P(S_{t}\leq x\;|\;X\geq\mu(t-D))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(X\leq x\;|\;D\leq t,X>\mu(t-D))=\begin{cases}0&x\leq\mu(t-D)\\
\frac{F(x)-F(\mu(t-D))}{1-F(\mu(t-D))}&x>\mu(t-D)\end{cases}.

If
X<\mu(t-D)
, the first offer is rejected, and we wait for the next offer, so that

P(S_{t}\leq x\;|\;X\leq\mu(t-D))=P(S_{t-D}\leq x)=G_{t-D}(x).

Thus

\displaystyle P(S_{t}\leq x\;|\;D\leq t)

\displaystyle=P(X\geq\mu(t-D))\cdot P(S_{t}\leq x\;|\;X\geq\mu(t-D))+P(X<\mu(t-D))\cdot G_{t-D}(x)

\displaystyle=(1-F(\mu(t-D)))P(S_{t}\leq x\;|\;X\geq\mu(t-D))+F(\mu(t-D))G_{t-D}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}F(\mu(t-D))G_{t-D}(x)&x<\mu(t-D)\\
F(x)-F(\mu(t-D))+F(\mu(t))G_{t-D}(x)&x\geq\mu(t-D)\end{cases}

\displaystyle=F(\mu(t-D))G_{t-D}(x)+[F(x)-F(\mu(t-D))]_{+}.

If
D>t
, then
S_{t}=X_{0}
, so

P(S_{t}\leq x\;|\;D>t)=P(X_{0}\leq x)=F_{0}(x).

Therefore

\displaystyle G_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=P(S_{t}\leq x)=\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}\Big(F(\mu(t-s))G_{t-s}(x)+[F(x)-F(\mu(t-s))]_{+}\Big)ds+e^{-\lambda t}F_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda s}\Big(F(\mu(s))G_{s}(x)+[F(x)-F(\mu(s))]_{+}\Big)ds+e^{-\lambda t}F_{0}(x).

(3.1)

Note that substituting
t=0
into (
3.1
) gives

G_{0}(x)=F_{0}(x).

Multiplying both sides of (
3.1
) by

e^{\lambda t}
and then
differentiating with respect to
t
, we obtain

e^{\lambda t}G_{t}(t,x)+\lambda e^{\lambda t}G_{t}(x)=\lambda e^{\lambda t}\Big(F(\mu(t))G_{t}(x)+[F(x)-F(\mu(t))]_{+}\Big),

which, after simplification, gives

Lemma 3.1
.

For each fixed
x
,
G_{t}(x)
, as a function of
t
, satisfies the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}G_{t}(x)=\lambda\left[(F(\mu(t))-1)G_{t}(x)+[F(x)-F(\mu(t))]_{+}\right],\\
G_{0}(x)=F_{0}(x)\end{cases}.

(3.2)

In the following we will solve the above differential
equation, which is a first-order linear differential equation with respect to
t
, with
x
considered a parameter. We thus obtain an explicit expression for
G_{t}(x)
.
Note that the expression
for the distribution of
S_{t}
takes
different functional forms in the intervals

(-\infty,\mu_{0}]
,
[\mu_{0},\mu(t)]
, and

[\mu(t),\infty)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The cumulative density of the sale price
S_{t}
is given by

\displaystyle G_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi(\mu(t))\left[\frac{F_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{[F(x)-F(w)]_{+}}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw\right]

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\phi(\mu(t))\frac{F_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\phi(\mu(t))\cdot\begin{cases}0&x<\mu_{0}\\
\frac{1}{\phi(x)}-\frac{1}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}&\mu_{0}\leq x<\mu(t)\\
\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(t))}-\frac{1}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}&x\geq\mu(t)\end{cases}.

are absolutely continuous with densities

\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}&\mu_{0}\leq x<\mu(t)\\
\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}&x>\mu(t)\end{cases}.

(3.4)

Proof.

For each fixed
x
, (
3.2
) in a linear differential equation of first order, whose general solution is therefore

G_{t}(x)=C(x)e^{-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}[1-F(\mu(s))]ds}+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{r}^{t}[1-F(\mu(s))]ds}[F(x)-F(\mu(r))]_{+}dr,

we can write

(3.5)

Using the change of variable

w=\mu(s),\;\;dw=\mu^{\prime}(s)ds=\lambda\phi(\mu(s))ds=\lambda\phi(w)ds

we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}[1-F(\mu(s))]ds=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}[1-F(w)]\frac{dw}{\phi(w)}=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(w)}{\phi(w)}dw=\frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\left(\frac{\phi(\mu_{0})}{\phi(\mu(t))}\right),

(3.6)

hence

\Phi(t)=\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{\phi(\mu_{0})}.

Therefore we can write (
3.5
) in the form

G_{t}(x)=F_{0}(x)\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{\phi(\mu(r))}[F(x)-F(\mu(r))]_{+}dr.

(3.7)

Making a change of variable
w=\mu(r)
in
the integral above, we obtain

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{[F(x)-F(\mu(r))]_{+}}{\phi(\mu(r))}dr=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{[F(x)-F(w)]_{+}}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw,

so that

G_{t}(x)=\phi(\mu(t))\left[\frac{F_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{[F(x)-F(w)]_{+}}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw\right],

(3.8)

and we have the first expression in (
3.3
).
To obtain the second expression, we
note first that by the monotonicity of
F

we have

[F(x)-F(w)]_{+}=\begin{cases}0&x\leq w\\
F(x)-F(w)&x>w\end{cases}.

(3.9)

In the following we divide the calculation into three cases,
x<\mu_{0}
,
\mu_{0}\leq x<\mu(t)
, and
x\geq\mu(t)
.
In the case
x<\mu_{0}
, (
3.9
) implies that the integral in (
3.7
) vanishes, so that

G_{t}(x)=\phi(\mu(t))\frac{F_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})},\;\;\;x<\mu_{0},

(3.10)

and differentiating with respect to
x
(assuming
F_{0}
is absolutely continuous) we have

g_{t}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}G_{t}(x)=\phi(\mu(t))\frac{f_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})},\;\;\;x<\mu_{0}.

(3.11)

When

\mu_{0}\leq x<\mu(t)
, we have, using (
3.9
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{[F(x)-F(w)]_{+}}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{F(x)-F(w)}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw

(3.12)

\displaystyle=F(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}-\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{F(w)}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw=F(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}-\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(w)+1}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw

\displaystyle=(F(x)-1)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}+\frac{1}{\phi(x)}-\frac{1}{\phi(\mu_{0})}=\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}+\frac{1}{\phi(x)}-\frac{1}{\phi(\mu_{0})},

so that (
3.8
) reduces to

G_{t}(x)=\phi(\mu(t))\left[\frac{1}{\phi(x)}+\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}-\frac{1-F_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\right],\;\;\;\mu_{0}\leq x<\mu(t)

(3.13)

and differentiating with respect to
x
(assuming
F,F_{0}
are absolutely continuous) gives

\displaystyle g_{t}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}G_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi(\mu(t))\left[-\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{\phi(x)^{2}}+\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}+\phi^{\prime}(x)\frac{1}{\phi(x)^{2}}+\frac{f_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\right]

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\phi(\mu(t))\left[f(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}+\frac{f_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\right],\;\;\;\mu_{0}\leq x<\mu(t)

where in the second line we have used the fact that
\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)=f(x)
.

Finally, when
x\geq\mu(t)
, we have

\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{[F(x)-F(w)]_{+}}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{F(x)-F(w)}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw,

and a calculation identical to t(
3.12
) above leads to

\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{F(x)-F(w)}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw=\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}+\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(t))}-\frac{1}{\phi(\mu_{0})},

so that (
3.8
) reduces to

G_{t}(x)=\phi(\mu(t))\left[\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}+\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(t))}-\frac{1-F_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\right],\;\;\;x\geq\mu(t)

(3.15)

and differentiating with respect to
x

(assuming
F,F_{0}
are absolutely continuous) gives

g_{t}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}G_{t}(x)=\phi(\mu(t))\left[f(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}+\frac{f_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\right],\;\;\;x\geq\mu(t).

(3.16)

Combining (
3.10
),(
3.13
) and (
3.15
) gives (
3.3
) and combining
(
3.11
),(
3.14
) and (
3.16
)
gives (
3.4
).
∎

3.2
Variance of the sale price

Since we now have
an explicit expression for the distribution of the sale price
S_{t}
, we can derive its variance, which allows us to evaluate the uncertainty in the realized sale price when the optimal policy is applied.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume
E(X_{0}^{2})<\infty
and
E(X_{+}^{2})<\infty
.
Then the variance of the sale price, under the optimal policy, is finite and given by

Var[S_{t}]=\phi(\mu(t))\left[\frac{Var[X_{0}]}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+2\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dxdw\right],

(3.17)

which can also be written as

Var[S_{t}]=E[(X-\mu(t))_{+}]\left[\mu(t)-\mu_{0}+\frac{Var[X_{0}]}{E((X-\mu_{0})_{+})}+\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{Var[(X-w)_{+}]}{E[(X-w)_{+}]^{2}}dw\right].

(3.18)

Proof.

We will be using the following identity:
if
Z
is a random variable with CDF
F_{Z}
, then

E[Z^{2}]=2\int_{0}^{\infty}z[1-F_{Z}(z)]dz+2\int_{-\infty}^{0}zF_{Z}(z)dz.

(3.19)

Using (
2.19
) and (
3.19
), with
Z=(X-w)_{+}
(
w\geq 0
) we have
F_{Z}(z)=F(z+w)
for
z\geq 0
, hence

\displaystyle\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dx=\int_{w}^{\infty}\int_{x}^{\infty}(1-F(u))dudx=\int_{w}^{\infty}\int_{w}^{u}(1-F(u))dxdu=\int_{w}^{\infty}(u-w)(1-F(u))du

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}u(1-F(z+w))dz=\int_{0}^{\infty}z(1-F_{Z}(z))dz=\frac{1}{2}E(Z^{2})=\frac{1}{2}E[(X-w)_{+}^{2}],

and since
E[(X-w)_{+}^{2})]\leq E[X_{+}^{2}]<\infty
, by our assumption, this shows in particular that the integral on the left-hand side of (
3.20
) is finite.

Using (
3.19
) with
Z=S_{t}
and
(
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle E[S_{t}^{2}]=2\int_{0}^{\infty}x[1-G_{t}(x)]dx+2\int_{-\infty}^{0}xG_{t}(x)dx

(3.21)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\mu_{0}}\left[1-\phi(\mu(t))\frac{F_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\right]xdx+2\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\left[1-\phi(\mu(t))\left(\frac{F_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\frac{1}{\phi(x)}-\frac{1}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right)\right]xdx

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\left[1-\phi(\mu(t))\left(\frac{F_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(t))}-\frac{1}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right)\right]xdx+2\int_{-\infty}^{0}\phi(\mu(t))\frac{F_{0}(x)}{\phi(\mu_{0})}xdx

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\mu_{0}}xdx-2\phi(\mu(t))\left[\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\left[\frac{1}{\phi(x)}+\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right]xdx+\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}xdx\right]

\displaystyle+2\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}xdx+\frac{2\phi(\mu(t))}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}x[1-F_{0}(x)]dx+\int_{-\infty}^{0}xF_{0}(x)dx-\int_{0}^{\mu_{0}}xdx\right]

\displaystyle=-2\phi(\mu(t))\left[\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\left[\frac{1}{\phi(x)}+\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right]xdx+\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}xdx\right]

\displaystyle+\mu(t)^{2}+\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\left(E[X_{0}^{2}]-E[X_{0}]^{2}\right).

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\left[\frac{1}{\phi(x)}+\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right]xdx=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}x\frac{d}{dx}\left[\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right]dx

(3.22)

\displaystyle=x\left[\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right]\Big|_{x=\mu_{0}}^{x=\mu(t)}-\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\left[\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right]dx

\displaystyle=\mu(t)\phi(\mu(t))\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}-\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\left[\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right]dx,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}xdx=\left[\phi(x)x\Big|_{x=\mu(t)}^{x=\infty}-\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\phi(x)dx\right]\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\left[\phi(\mu(t))\mu(t)+\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\phi(x)dx\right]\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}.

(3.23)

Combining (
3.22
) and (
3.2
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\left[\frac{1}{\phi(x)}+\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right]xdx+\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\phi^{\prime}(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}xdx

(3.24)

\displaystyle=-\left[\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\left[\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right]dx+\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}dwdx\right]

\displaystyle=-\left[\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\int_{w}^{\mu(t)}\phi(x)\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}dxdw+\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\phi(x)\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}dxdw\right]=-\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dxdw.

From (
3.21
),(
3.24
) we get

E[S_{t}^{2}]=\mu(t)^{2}+\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\cdot Var[X_{0}]+2\phi(\mu(t))\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dxdw,

which, since
\mu(t)^{2}=E[S_{t}]^{2}
, implies (
3.17
).

To obtain (
3.18
), note that, using (
3.20
) and (
2.23
) we have

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dxdw=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{E[(X-w)_{+}^{2}]}{E[(X-w)_{+}]^{2}}dw

(3.25)

\displaystyle=(\mu(t)-\mu_{0})+\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{E[(X-w)_{+}^{2}]-E[(X-w)_{+}]^{2}}{E[(X-w)_{+}]^{2}}dw=(\mu(t)-\mu_{0})+\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{Var[(X-w)_{+}]}{E[(X-w)_{+}]^{2}}dw,

which, together with (
3.17
) and (
2.23
), gives (
3.18
).

∎

4
The time to sale

Another important random variable related to the
bidding process is the time to sale, that is the stopping time, assuming the optimal policy is followed. For any marketing period
t\geq 0
, we will denote this random variable by
T_{t}
.
Obviously
T_{t}
takes values in
[0,t]
.
It is of interest to evaluate the characteristics of
T_{t}
, e.g. its expectation and variance. In fact we will obtain the full distribution of
T_{t}
.
We note that the distribution of
T_{t}
has an
atom at the value
t
, that is
P(T_{t}=t)>0
- this is the probability that a sale is not made before the deadline.

It will also be of interest to examine the random variable

\hat{T}_{t}=\frac{1}{t}T_{t}\in[0,1],

(4.1)

which signifies the proportion of
the available marketing period that will in fact be used.

4.1
Determining the stopping time distribution

Theorem 4.1
.

The cumulative distribution function of
T_{t}
is given by

H_{t}(r)=P(T_{t}\leq r)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{\phi(\mu(t-r))}&r<t\\
1&r\geq t\end{cases}.

(4.2)

In particular, the
probability that a sale is not made
up to the deadline is given by

P(T_{t}=t)=H_{t}(t+)-H_{t}(t-)=\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{\phi(\mu_{0})}.

Proof.

The probability that a sale occurs during
the infinitesimal time interval
[s,s+ds]
after the
start of the bidding process is

\lambda[1-F(\mu(t-s))]ds,

where
\lambda ds
is the probability that
an offer is received during this time interval, and
1-F(\mu(t-s))

is the probability that such an offer is accepted. Therefore the probability that
a sale is
not
made up to time
r
into the marketing period is

P(T_{t}\geq r)=\begin{cases}e^{-\lambda\int_{0}^{r}[1-F(\mu(t-s))]ds}&r<t\\
1&r\geq t.\end{cases}.

(4.3)

Using the identity (
3.6
), we have

\lambda\int_{0}^{r}[1-F(\mu(t-s))]ds=\lambda\int_{t-r}^{t}[1-F(\mu(s))]ds=\ln\left(\frac{\phi(\mu(t-r))}{\phi(\mu(t))}\right),

which, together with (
4.3
), implies (
4.2
).
∎

Once we have determined
the distribution of
T_{t}
, we immediately obtain the distribution of

\hat{T}_{t}
given by (
4.1
):

\hat{H}_{t}(s)=P(\hat{T}_{t}\leq s)=P(T_{t}\leq st)=H_{t}(st)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{\phi(\mu((1-s)t))}&s<1\\
1&s\geq 1\end{cases}.

(4.4)

4.2
Expectation and variance of the time to sale

Using the expression (
4.2
), we can compute the expectation and variance of
T_{t}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

We have

E[T_{t}]=\frac{1}{\lambda}\phi(\mu(t))\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}},

(4.5)

Var[T_{t}]=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left[2\phi(\mu(t))\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{u}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}dwdu-\left[\phi(\mu(t))\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{dw}{\phi(w)^{2}}\right]^{2}\right].

(4.6)

Proof.

We have, using (
4.2
),

E[T_{t}]=\int_{0}^{\infty}P(T_{t}\geq r)dr=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{\phi(\mu(t-r))}dr=\phi(\mu(t))\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(r))}dr.

Making the change of variable

w=\mu(r),\;\;dw=\mu^{\prime}(r)dr=\lambda\phi(\mu(r))dr=\lambda\phi(w)dr

(4.7)

in the last integral, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(r))}dr=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw,

(4.8)

so we obtain
(
4.5
).

Using the identity (
3.19
) and (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle E[T_{t}^{2}]

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t}r[1-H_{t}(r)]dr=2\phi(\mu(t))\int_{0}^{t}\frac{r}{\phi(\mu(t-r))}dr

(4.9)

\displaystyle=2\phi(\mu(t))\int_{0}^{t}\frac{t-r}{\phi(\mu(r))}dr=2\phi(\mu(t))\left[t\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(r))}dr-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{r}{\phi(\mu(r))}dr\right].

Making the change of variable (
4.7
) in the last integral, noting that

w=\mu(r)=\Psi^{-1}(\lambda r)\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;r=\frac{1}{\lambda}\Psi(w),

we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{r}{\phi(\mu(r))}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{\Psi(w)}{\phi(w)^{2}}dw=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{w}\frac{du}{\phi(u)}dw=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}\int_{u}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}dwdu.

(4.10)

Using

t=\frac{1}{\lambda}\Psi(\mu(t))=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{du}{\phi(u)},

and (
4.8
), we can write

t\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(r))}dr=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}dwdu.

(4.11)

Combining (
4.9
),(
4.10
),(
4.11
), we get

E[T_{t}^{2}]=\frac{2\phi(\mu(t))}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{u}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}dwdu,

which together with (
4.5
), give (
4.6
).
∎

5
Long marketing period - asymptotic results

In this final section we
exploit results obtained in previous sections in order to study the
asymptotic behavior of some key
quantities when the marketing period
t
is long,
t\rightarrow\infty
. In subsection

5.1
we consider

\mu(t)=E(S_{t})
, and
Var[S_{t}]
, and
in section
5.2
we study the limiting distribution of the random variable
\hat{T}_{t}=\frac{1}{t}T_{t}
, the fraction of the marketing period until a sale is made.

5.1
Asymptotics of mean and variance of
S_{t}

The next results describe
the asymptotic behavior of
E(S_{t})=\mu(t)
and
Var[S_{t}]
for
t
large, for three classes of offer distributions, defined
by their tail behavior.
The results concerning
\mu(t)
are
analogous to the results of
[
16
]
(see also
[
17
]
for related results), which analyzed the
asymptotics of the threshold sequence

\mu_{n}
in the discrete-time case.
The fact that in the continuous-time case we have explicit expressions for the relevant quantities allows
the asymptotic analysis to be carried out by elementary tools.

5.1.1
Offer distributions with support bounded from above

We now consider the case of offer distributions with
M<\infty
, where
M
is given by (
2.4
).
In this case, by (
2.45
), when
the available time is long (
t\rightarrow\infty
), we have
\mu(t)=E[S_{t}]\rightarrow M
. The following result characterizes the rate of convergence, showing that it is related to the behavior of the CDF
F(x)
near the right
endpoint
M
.

The notation
\sim
means that the ratio of the two sides approaches
1
, as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume

M<\infty
, where
M
is given by (
2.4
).
Assume also that, for some
p>0,c>0
,

\lim_{x\rightarrow M-}\frac{\phi(x)}{(M-x)^{p+1}}=c.

(5.1)

Then we have, as
t\rightarrow+\infty

\mu(t)=M-\frac{1}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}}+o\left(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}}\right).

(5.2)

The asymptotic behavior of
Var[S_{t}]
depends on
p
as follows:

If
p<1
then

Var[S_{t}]\sim\frac{1}{(\lambda p)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}c^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left[(M-\mu_{0})+\frac{Var[X_{0}]}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+\int_{\mu_{0}}^{M}\frac{Var[(X-w)_{+}]}{E[(X-w)_{+}]^{2}}dw\right]\cdot\frac{1}{t^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}.

(5.3)

If
p=1
then

Var[S_{t}]\sim\frac{2}{3\lambda^{2}c^{2}}\cdot\frac{\ln(t)}{t^{2}}.

(5.4)

If
p>1
then

Var[S_{t}]\sim\frac{2}{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p+2}\cdot\frac{1}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{\frac{2}{p}}}.

(5.5)

Proof.

Recalling that
\Psi^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{\phi(x)}
, we have
by (
2.43
), L’Hôpital’s rule, and (
5.1
)

\lim_{x\rightarrow M-}\frac{\Psi(x)}{(M-x)^{-p}}=\lim_{x\rightarrow M-}\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(x)}{p(M-x)^{-p-1}}=\lim_{x\rightarrow M-}\frac{(M-x)^{p+1}}{p\phi(x)}=\frac{1}{pc}.

(5.6)

Setting
x=\mu(t)
in (
5.6
), and using the fact that

\Psi(\mu(t))=\lambda t
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\lambda t(M-\mu(t))^{p}=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\Psi(\mu(t))}{(M-\mu(t))^{-p}}=\frac{1}{pc}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;(M-\mu(t))^{p}=\frac{1}{\lambda pc}\frac{1}{t}(1+o(1))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\;\;M-\mu(t)=\frac{1}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{1}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right),

which implies (
5.2
).

We now turn to analyzing
Var[S_{t}]
.
We have
\phi(x)=0
for

x>M
, hence
defining

g(t)=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{M}\phi(x)dxdw,

we have, using (
3.17
),

Var[S_{t}]=\phi(\mu(t))\left[\frac{Var[X_{0}]}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+2g(t)\right]

(5.7)

Substituting
x=\mu(t)
in (
5.1
)
we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{(M-\mu(t))^{p+1}}=c.

(5.8)

From (
5.2
) we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{M-\mu(t)}{t^{-\frac{1}{p}}}=\frac{1}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{1}{p}}},

(5.9)

which implies

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(M-\mu(t))^{p+1}}{t^{-\frac{p+1}{p}}}=\frac{1}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}.

(5.10)

Combining (
5.8
) and (
5.10
) we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi(\mu(t))}{t^{-\frac{p+1}{p}}}=\frac{c}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}=\frac{1}{(\lambda p)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}c^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

(5.11)

We have

g^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\phi(\mu(t))^{2}}\int_{\mu(t)}^{M}\phi(x)dx=\frac{\lambda}{\phi(\mu(t))}\int_{\mu(t)}^{M}\phi(x)dx.

(5.12)

Using the integral mean-value theorem we have

\displaystyle\int_{v}^{M}\phi(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{v}^{M}\frac{\phi(x)}{(M-x)^{p+1}}\cdot(M-x)^{p+1}dx=\frac{\phi(\tilde{x})}{(M-\tilde{x})^{p+1}}\cdot\int_{v}^{M}(M-x)^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi(\tilde{x})}{(M-\tilde{x})^{p+1}}\cdot\frac{(M-v)^{p+2}}{p+2}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\frac{1}{(M-v)^{p+2}}\int_{v}^{M}\phi(x)dx=\frac{1}{p+2}\cdot\frac{\phi(\tilde{x})}{(M-\tilde{x})^{p+1}},

where
\tilde{x}\in(v,M)
, which together with (
5.1
) implies

\lim_{v\rightarrow M-}\frac{1}{(M-v)^{p+2}}\int_{v}^{M}\phi(x)dx=\frac{c}{p+2}.

(5.13)

(
5.1
) together with (
5.13
) give

\lim_{v\rightarrow M-}\frac{1}{M-v}\cdot\frac{1}{\phi(v)}\int_{v}^{M}\phi(x)dx=\frac{1}{p+2},

and substituting
v=\mu(t)
we get

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{M-\mu(t)}\cdot\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(t))}\int_{\mu(t)}^{M}\phi(x)dx=\frac{1}{p+2},

which, combined with (
5.9
), gives

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(t))}\int_{\mu(t)}^{M}\phi(x)dx=\frac{1}{p+2}\cdot\frac{1}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

(5.14)

From (
5.12
) and (
5.14
) we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\frac{1}{p}}g^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{p+2}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{1}{p}}}=\frac{1}{p+2}\cdot\frac{1}{(pc)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}-1}}.

(5.15)

If
p<1
, (
5.15
) implies that the integral
\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\infty}g^{\prime}(t)dt
is finite, so that

g(\infty)=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}g(t)=\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{M}\phi(x)dxdw<\infty,

and together with (
5.7
),(
5.11
) we
get

Var[S_{t}]=\frac{1}{(\lambda p)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}c^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left[\frac{Var[X_{0}]}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+2g(\infty)\right]\cdot\frac{1}{t^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}(1+o(1)).

(5.16)

Note that by the identity
(
3.25
) we have

2g(\infty)=(M-\mu_{0})+\int_{\mu_{0}}^{M}\frac{Var[(X-w)_{+}]}{E[(X-w)_{+}]^{2}}dw,

so (
5.16
) implies (
5.3
).

If
p>1
, then, using L’Hôpital’s rule, (
5.15
) implies

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{g(t)}{t^{\frac{p-1}{p}}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{\frac{p-1}{p}\cdot t^{-\frac{1}{p}}}=\frac{p}{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p+2}\cdot\frac{1}{(pc)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}-1}}.

(5.17)

Combining (
5.11
) and (
5.17
), we get

\phi(\mu(t))g(t)=\frac{1}{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p+2}\cdot\frac{1}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{\frac{2}{p}}}(1+o(1)),\;\;{\mbox{as}}\;\;t\rightarrow\infty.

From (
5.11
) we have

\phi(\mu(t))\frac{Var[X_{0}]}{\phi(\mu_{0})}=\frac{Var[X_{0}]}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\frac{1}{(\lambda p)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}c^{\frac{1}{p}}}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}(1+o(1)),\;\;{\mbox{as}}\;\;t\rightarrow\infty,

and since
\frac{p+1}{p}>\frac{2}{p}

we conclude that

Var[S_{t}]=\frac{2}{p-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p+2}\cdot\frac{1}{(\lambda pc)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{\frac{2}{p}}}(1+o(1)),\;\;{\mbox{as}}\;\;t\rightarrow\infty,

so we have (
5.5
).

When

we have, using L’Hôpital’s rule and (

),

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{g(t)}{\ln(t)}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{\frac{1}{t}}=\frac{1}{3c},

(5.18)

and from (

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{2}\phi(\mu(t))=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}c},

implying

Var[S_{t}]=\frac{2}{3\lambda^{2}c^{2}}\cdot\frac{\ln(t)}{t^{2}}(1+o(1)),\;\;{\mbox{as}}\;\;t\rightarrow\infty.

∎

As an example for the application of the above theorem, for the Beta distribution on

,

f(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1},\;\;\;\alpha,\beta>0,

(5.19)

using L’Hôpital’s rule we have

\lim_{x\rightarrow 1-}\frac{\phi(x)}{(1-x)^{\beta+1}}=-\lim_{x\rightarrow 1-}\frac{F(c)-1}{(\beta+1)(1-x)^{\beta}}=\lim_{x\rightarrow 1-}\frac{f(x)}{(\beta+1)\beta(1-x)^{\beta-1}}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta+2)},

so we have (
5.1
) with

,
so (

) gives

\mu(t)=M-\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta+2)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}\cdot\frac{1}{\beta\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}+o\left(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1}{\beta}}}\right),

(5.20)

and the asymptotics of
Var[S_{t}]
can also be obtained by the results above, depending on the value of

In particular, when

we get the uniform distribution on

, , and we have

5.1.2
Offer distributions with an exponential right tail

We now consider the case in which
the right-tail behavior of the offer distribution is exponential, in the sense made precise by (
5.21
) below.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume that
M=+\infty
and the function
\phi(x)
defined by (
2.19
) satisfies

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{\int_{x}^{\infty}\phi(u)du}=c,\;\;\;c>0.

(5.21)

Then, as
t\rightarrow\infty
,

\mu(t)\sim\frac{1}{c}\ln(t),\;\;\;\mu^{\prime}(t)\sim\frac{1}{ct},

(5.22)

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}Var[S_{t}]=\frac{2}{c^{2}}.

(5.23)

Proof.

Define

I(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\phi(u)du.

By (
5.21
) we have

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{I^{\prime}(x)}{I(x)}=-c.

Therefore, fixing any
\epsilon\in(0,c)
, we can chose

x_{0}>0
so that

x\geq x_{0}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;-c-\epsilon\leq\frac{I^{\prime}(x)}{I(x)}\leq-c+\epsilon.

(5.24)

Assume
x\geq u\geq x_{0}
. using (
5.24
), we have

\ln\left(\frac{I(x)}{I(u)}\right)=\int_{u}^{x}\frac{I^{\prime}(v)}{I(v)}dv\leq(-c+\epsilon)(x-u)\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;I(u)\geq I(x)e^{(c-\epsilon)(x-u)}.

(5.25)

Using (
5.24
) and (
5.25
) we get

\phi(u)=-I^{\prime}(u)\geq-(-c+\epsilon)I(u)\geq(c-\epsilon)I(x)e^{(c-\epsilon)(x-u)}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\frac{1}{\phi(u)}\leq\frac{1}{(c-\epsilon)I(x)}e^{-(c-\epsilon)(x-u)},

hence, for
x>x_{0}
,

\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}du\leq\frac{1}{(c-\epsilon)I(x)}\int_{x_{0}}^{x}e^{-(c-\epsilon)(x-u)}du=\frac{1-e^{-(c-\epsilon)(x-x_{0})}}{(c-\epsilon)^{2}I(x)}.

(5.26)

By (
5.24
) and the fact that
I^{\prime}(x)<0
we have

\frac{1}{I(x)}\leq-\frac{c+\epsilon}{I^{\prime}(x)}=\frac{c+\epsilon}{\phi(x)},

(5.27)

and combining (
5.26
) and (
5.27
) gives, for
x>x_{0}
,

\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}du\leq\frac{1-e^{-(c-\epsilon)(x-x_{0})}}{(c-\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{c+\epsilon}{\phi(x)}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\phi(x)\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}du\leq\frac{(1-e^{-(c-\epsilon)(x-x_{0})})(c+\epsilon)}{(c-\epsilon)^{2}}.

We conclude that

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi(x)\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}du\leq\frac{c+\epsilon}{(c-\epsilon)^{2}}.

Since
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi(x)=0
, this also implies

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}du=\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\left[\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x_{0}}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}du+\phi(x)\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}du\right]\leq\frac{c+\epsilon}{(c-\epsilon)^{2}},

and since
\epsilon
is arbitrary we conclude that

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}du\leq\frac{1}{c}.

An analogous argument, using (
5.24
), gives

\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}du\geq\frac{1}{c},

so that we have

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi(x)\Psi(x)=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi(x)\int_{\mu_{0}}^{x}\frac{1}{\phi(u)}du=\frac{1}{c}.

(5.28)

Note in particular that (
5.28
) implies

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Psi(x)=+\infty\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mu(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Psi^{-1}(\lambda t)=+\infty.

Substituting
x=\mu(t)
in (
5.28
) and using (
2.20
) and (
2.21
) we get

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{\prime}(t)t=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\phi(\mu(t))\Psi(\mu(t))=\frac{1}{c}

and using L’Hôpital’s rule we obtain

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(t)}{\ln(t)}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(t)}{\frac{1}{t}}=\frac{1}{c},

so we have (
5.22
).

We now prove (
5.23
).
We have, using (
5.21
) and L’Hôpital’s rule

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dxdw=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dxdw

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{\prime}(t)\frac{1}{\phi(\mu(t))^{2}}\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\phi(x)dx=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\lambda\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\phi(x)dx}{\phi(\mu(t))}=\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\lambda\int_{u}^{\infty}\phi(x)dx}{\phi(u)}=\frac{\lambda}{c}.

(5.29)

From (
2.20
) and (
5.22
)
we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\phi(\mu(t))=\frac{1}{\lambda}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\mu^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda c}.

(5.30)

Using (
3.17
) and (
5.30
) we get

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}Var[S_{t}]=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\phi(\mu(t))\frac{Var[X_{0}]}{\phi(\mu_{0})}+2\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\phi(\mu(t))\cdot\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dxdw=0+2\frac{1}{\lambda c}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{c}=\frac{2}{c^{2}}.

∎

As an example for the application of the above result, consider the Gamma distribution, with density

f(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\eta^{\alpha}}\cdot x^{\alpha-1}e^{-\frac{x}{\eta}},\;\;\;\alpha>0,\eta>0.

(5.31)

We have

\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)=[F(x)-1]^{\prime}=f(x)
,
hence, using L‘Hôpital’s rule repeatedly

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi(x)}{\int_{x}^{\infty}\phi(u)du}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{\phi(x)}=-\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\phi^{\prime}(x)}=-\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)}{\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}=-\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left((\alpha-1)x^{\alpha-2}e-\frac{1}{\eta}x^{\alpha-1}\right)e^{-\frac{x}{\eta}}}{x^{\alpha-1}e^{-\frac{x}{\eta}}}=\frac{1}{\eta},

so (
5.21
) holds with
c=\frac{1}{\eta}
, and (
5.22
),(
5.23
) give

\mu(t)\sim\eta\ln(t),\;\;\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}Var[S_{t}]=2\eta^{2}.

In particular, when
\alpha=1
we get the exponential distribution, and the result is consistent with the exact expressions
(
2.33
), (
3.32
).

5.1.3
Offer distributions with power-law tails

We now consider the case in which the offer distribution

We will use some results from the theory of regularly varying functions. We recall
[
19

\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{R(xs)}{R(s)}=x^{\rho},\;\;\;\forall x>0.

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose
M=+\infty
and the function

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{x\phi(x)}{\int_{x}^{\infty}\phi(u)du}=p,\;\;\;p>0.

is regularly varying with exponent
\frac{1}{p+2}
, and, in particular, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(t)}{t^{c}}=\begin{cases}+\infty&c<\frac{1}{p+2}\\
0&c>\frac{1}{p+2}\end{cases}.

(5.33)

Var[S_{t}]
, as a function of
t
is regularly varying with exponent
\frac{2}{p+2}
. In particular, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{Var[S_{t}]}{t^{c}}=\begin{cases}+\infty&c<\frac{2}{p+2}\\
0&c>\frac{2}{p+2}\end{cases}.

Proof.

By
[
19
]
, Theorem 1.2.1(c), the condition (
5.32
) implies that

\phi\in RV_{-p-1}
.
This implies that
\frac{1}{\phi}\in RV_{p+1}
, and by
[
19
]
, Theorem 1.2.1(a) we have that
\Psi
defined by
(
2.22
) satisfies
\Psi\in RV_{p+2}
. Since
\Psi
is increasing and
\Psi(+\infty)=+\infty
,
we conclude by
[
19
]
, Theorem 1.4.7 that
\Psi^{-1}\in RV_{\frac{1}{p+2}}.

This implies that
\mu(t)=\Psi^{-1}(\lambda t)\in RV_{\frac{1}{p+2}}
, as we wanted to prove. Therefore

\frac{\mu(t)}{x^{c}}\in RV_{\frac{1}{p+2}-c}
, which implies (
5.33
) (
[
19
]
, Proposition 1.2.11).

We now analyze
Var[S_{t}]
.
The composition of a function
R_{1}\in RV_{\rho_{1}}
with a function
R_{2}\in RV_{\rho_{2}}
with
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}R_{2}(x)=+\infty
, satisfies
R_{1}\circ R_{2}\in RV_{\rho_{1}\cdot\rho_{2}}
.
Thus, since

\mu\in RV_{\frac{1}{p+2}}
and
\phi\in RV_{-p-1}
, we have

g_{1}(t)=\phi(\mu(t))\in RV_{-\frac{p+1}{p+2}}.

(5.34)

Therefore, using
[
19
]
Th. 1.2.1(a),

g_{2}(w)=\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dx\in RV_{-p}.

Since
\phi\in RV_{-p-1}
, we have
\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\in RV_{2(p+1)}
. Therefore

g_{3}(w)=\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\cdot g_{2}(w)\in RV_{p+2}.

Therefore, again using
[
19
]
Th. 1.2.1(a),

g_{4}(v)=\int_{0}^{v}g_{3}(w)dw=\int_{0}^{v}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dxdw\in RV_{p+3},

which implies

g_{5}(t)=g_{4}(\mu(t))=\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dxdw\in RV_{\frac{p+3}{p+2}}.

Together with
(
5.34
) we conclude that

g_{6}(t)=g_{1}(t)g_{5}(t)=\phi(\mu(t))\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}\frac{1}{\phi(w)^{2}}\int_{w}^{\infty}\phi(x)dxdw\in RV_{\frac{2}{p+2}},

and finally, using (
3.17
),

Var[S_{t}]=\frac{Var[X_{0}]}{\phi(\mu_{0})}\cdot g_{1}+g_{5}\in RV_{\frac{2}{p+2}}.

∎

Note that the Pareto distribution
(
2.34
)
satisfies (
5.32
) with
p=\alpha-2
, so we conclude
\mu\in RV_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
and
Var[S_{t}]\in RV_{\frac{2}{\alpha}}
, as can also be seen directly from the explicit expressions (
2.37
), (
3.35
).

As another example, consider the Fréchet distribution

F(x)=e^{-x^{-\alpha}},\;\;\alpha>1.

(5.35)

We have, making repeated use of L’Hôpital’s rule

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{x\phi(x)}{\int_{x}^{\infty}\phi(u)du}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi(x)+x\phi^{\prime}(x)}{\phi(x)}=-1-\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{x\phi^{\prime}(x)}{\phi(x)}=-1-\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)+x\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\phi^{\prime}(x)}

\displaystyle=-2+\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{xF^{\prime}(x)}{1-F(x)}=-2+\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\alpha x^{-\alpha}e^{-x^{-\alpha}}}{1-e^{-x^{-\alpha}}}=-2+\alpha,

so (
5.32
) holds with
p=\alpha-2
, and we conclude from
Theorem
5.3
that
\mu(t)\in RV_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, and
Var[S_{t}]\in RV_{\frac{2}{\alpha}}
.

5.2
Large
t
limit of the distribution of
\hat{T}_{t}

In this subsection we are interested in
the existence and nature of a limiting distribution of the random variable
\hat{T}_{t}=\frac{1}{t}T_{t}
,
see (
4.4
),

\hat{H}_{\infty}(s)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\hat{H}_{t}(s),\;\;\;s\geq 0

(5.36)

When this distribution exists, the random variables
\hat{T}_{t}
converge in distribution to a random variable

\hat{T}_{\infty}
with the CDF
\hat{H}_{\infty}
, which expresses the fraction of the marketing period which will be utilized when the marketing period is long.

In all three examples considered in subsection

4.3
(see equations (
4.15
),(
4.19
),(
4.22

5.36
) exists, with

\hat{H}_{\infty}(s)=\begin{cases}1-(1-s)^{\gamma}&s<1\\
1&s\geq 1\end{cases},

(5.37)

where
\gamma=2
in the case of a uniform distribution,

\gamma=1
in the case of an exponential distribution, and
\gamma=1-\frac{1}{\alpha}
in the case of a
Pareto distribution with parameter
\alpha>1
. The next result shows that this is not a coincidence:

Theorem 5.4
.

The limiting distribution defined by (
5.36
) exists if and only if

\mu^{\prime}(t)
is a regularly varying function of index
\rho\leq 0
, and in this case
\hat{H}_{\infty}(s)
is of the form (
5.37
), where

\gamma=-\rho
. In particular, in this case

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{E[T_{t}]}{t}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}E[\hat{T}_{t}]=\frac{1}{\gamma+1},\;\;\;\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{Var[T_{t}]}{t^{2}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}Var[\hat{T}_{t}]=\frac{\gamma}{(\gamma+1)^{2}(\gamma+2)}.

(5.38)

Proof.

Using (
2.20
) we can write (
4.4
) as

\hat{H}_{t}(s)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu^{\prime}((1-s)t)}&s<1\\
1&s\geq 1\end{cases}.

(5.39)

so that the limit (
5.36
) exists if and only if the limit

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu^{\prime}(xt)}{\mu^{\prime}(t)}

exists for
x\in[0,1)
. By
[
19
]
, Theorems 1.1.8,1.1.9, existence of these limits
is equivalent to
\mu^{\prime}(t)
being regularly varying with some index
\rho
, which means that, for all
x\geq 0
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu^{\prime}(xt)}{\mu^{\prime}(t)}=x^{\rho},

and since
\mu
is concave (Theorem
2.2
) so that
\mu^{\prime}(t)
is decreasing,
\rho\leq 0
. Therefore

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu^{\prime}((1-s)t)}=(1-s)^{-\rho},

which, in view of (
5.39
), implies
(
5.37
) with
\gamma=-\rho
.
(
5.38
) is obtained by computing the expectation and variance of the
distribution (
5.37
).
∎

The above theorem ensures that if the limit (
5.36

5.37

being of regular variation.
We now show that this is the case for

A disorder-averaged framework for evaluating the Witten index
Debmalya Sarkar

Abstract
We study supersymmetric indices in disordered systems, in particular an 𝒩 = 4 \displaystyle\mathcal{N}=4 supersymmetric Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev-type quantum mechanics. In cases where the disordered parameters do not affect the index, we explain how the exact answer can nevertheless be obtained using disorder averaged collective variables. Furthermore, when disorder averaging is performed on multiple copies of the same theory, non-trivial coupling between them is generated. We show how the index ultimately remains factorized.

3.2
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric SYK model

We now turn to the
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric SYK model. In this model the value of the Witten index does not depend on the precise values of the SYK coupling constants. Hence we can average over them without changing the value of the index. However averaging produces an effective action in terms of bi-local fields
\displaystyle G,\Sigma

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle g
and then a present a detailed analysis up to sub-sub leading order in
\displaystyle g
to show that the value of the leading order calculation is essentially unchanged.

(3.43)

3.2.2
Quadratic solution at sub-leading orders in
\displaystyle g

In this sub-section we discuss classical contributions at sub-leading orders in
\displaystyle g
.
These come from both sub-leading quadratic terms and from higher order in
\displaystyle\delta G_{A}
terms in (
3.37
). As a first step we evaluate them on the quadratic solution presented before. We will see that this is sub-leading in
\displaystyle g
compared to the result presented before. This justifies ignoring corrections due to these terms in the classical solution for evaluating the first sub-leading correction to the potential for
\displaystyle g
.

Recall that supersymmetry implies

\displaystyle Q^{\psi}=-\partial_{\tau}Q^{\phi}\ ,\ Q^{F}=-\partial^{2}_{\tau}Q^{\phi}

(3.44)

Then the sub-leading quadratic term can be written as (up to an overall prefactor of
\displaystyle 2(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}\beta
, see (
3.37
))

\displaystyle-G^{av}_{\psi}\int d\tau Q^{\phi}Q^{\psi}=G^{av}_{\psi}\int d\tau Q^{\phi}\partial_{\tau}Q^{\phi}=G^{av}_{\psi}\int d\tau\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}((Q^{\phi})^{2})=0

(3.45)

The first term in the subsub-leading quadratic piece, computed on the saddle gives,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}}{2}\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}G^{av}_{F}\delta G_{\phi}^{2}\\
&=-\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}}{2}\frac{1}{\beta(1+\beta g^{p-1})}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}\frac{1}{(p-1)g^{p-2}\omega^{2}}H(\omega)^{2}\\
&=-\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}}{2}\frac{1}{\beta(1+\beta g^{p-1})}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}\frac{1}{((p-1)g^{p-2})^{2}}\left(\frac{(p-1)g^{p-2}}{\omega^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{4(p-1)g^{p-2}}{\omega^{2}}}\right)\\
&=-\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}}{2}\frac{1}{\beta(1+\beta g^{p-1})}\Big(\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\pi^{2}(p-1)g^{p-2}}\zeta(2)\\
&\hskip 70.0pt-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{((p-1)g^{p-2})^{2}}{}^{1/2}C_{k}\zeta(2k)(\frac{\beta\sqrt{(p-1)g^{p-2}}}{\pi})^{2k}\Big)\end{split}

(3.46)

We have the following upper bound on the sum over
\displaystyle k
appearing in the expression above

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a^{2}}{}^{1/2}C_{k}\zeta(2k)(\frac{\beta\sqrt{a}}{\pi})^{2k}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a^{2}}|{}^{1/2}C_{k}|\zeta(2k)(\frac{\beta\sqrt{|a|}}{\pi})^{2k}\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{\pi^{2}a}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\zeta(2k)(\frac{\beta\sqrt{|a|}}{\pi})^{2k-2},

(3.47)

where
\displaystyle a=(p-1)g^{p-2}
. The sum over
\displaystyle k
in RHS is the famous Apéry-like formula for the zeta function valid when
\displaystyle x
is not a
nonzero integer
[
43
]

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\zeta(2k)x^{2k-2}=\frac{1-\pi x\cot(\pi x)}{2x^{2}}

(3.48)

Hence the sum over
\displaystyle k
contributes at
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}((g^{p-2})^{-3/2})
for large
\displaystyle g
, which is subleading. Thus we have

\displaystyle-\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}}{2}\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}G^{av}_{F}\delta G_{\phi}^{2}\approx-\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}}{(p-1)g^{p-2}}\frac{\beta\zeta(2)}{4\pi^{2}(1+\beta g^{p-1})}\approx-\frac{(p-2)}{24}\frac{\beta}{G^{av}_{\phi}(1+\beta(G^{av}_{\phi})^{p-1})}

(3.49)

Similar manipulation determines the second term in the subsub-leading quadratic piece to be

\displaystyle\frac{(p-3)_{3}\,g^{p-4}}{2}\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}(G^{av}_{\psi})^{2}\delta G_{\phi}^{2}\approx\frac{(p-2)(p-3)}{24(p-1)}\frac{1}{(G^{av}_{\phi})^{p}}

(3.50)

On the other hand the leading and sub-leading cubic terms vanish because of supersymmetry:

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}\beta}{2}\int d\tau(2\delta G_{\psi}^{2}\delta G_{\phi}-\delta G_{F}\delta G_{\phi}^{2})

(3.51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}\beta}{2}\int d\tau\Big[2Q^{\phi}(\partial_{\tau}Q^{\phi})^{2}+\partial_{\tau}^{2}Q^{\phi}(Q^{\phi})^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}\beta}{2}\int d\tau\partial_{\tau}\Big(\partial_{\tau}Q^{\phi}(Q^{\phi})^{2}\Big)=0

and

\displaystyle-(p-3)_{3}\,g^{p-4}\beta\int d\tau G^{av}_{\psi}\delta G_{\psi}\delta G_{\phi}^{2}=\frac{(p-3)_{3}\,g^{p-4}}{3}\beta G^{av}_{\psi}\int d\tau\partial_{\tau}(Q^{\phi})^{3}=0

(3.52)

In this paper we restrict our analysis up to
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}((G^{av}_{\phi})^{-p})
. Therefore sub-sub leading cubic term is not our concern. Finally the leading quartic term is again a total derivative due to supersymmetry constraints:

\displaystyle\frac{(p-3)_{3}\,g^{p-4}}{6}\beta\int d\tau[3\delta G_{\psi}^{2}-\delta G_{F}\delta G_{\phi}]\delta G_{\phi}^{2}=\frac{(p-3)_{3}\,g^{p-4}}{6}\beta\int d\tau\partial_{\tau}((Q^{\phi})^{3}\partial_{\tau}Q^{\phi})=0

(3.53)

Putting all these together we get following effective action for
\displaystyle G^{av}_{\phi}
at the classical level

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\log\mathcal{I}}{N}&=\log(p-1)-\log(1+\frac{1}{\beta g^{p-1}})\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{(p-2)(p-3)}{24(p-1)}\frac{1}{g^{p}}-\frac{(p-2)}{24}\frac{\beta^{2}}{\beta g(1+\beta g^{p-1})}+\mathcal{O}(g^{-\frac{3p}{2}})\\
&=\log(p-1)-\frac{1}{\beta(G^{av}_{\phi})^{p-1}}-\frac{(p-2)}{12(p-1)}\frac{1}{(G^{av}_{\phi})^{p}}+\mathcal{O}((G^{av}_{\phi})^{-\frac{3p}{2}})\end{split}

(3.54)

The action looks significantly different from the thermal solution discussed in
[
34
]
. In particular we see that both the
\displaystyle G^{av}_{\phi}
dependent terms above are of same sign, indicating that the action is maximized at
\displaystyle G^{av}_{\phi}\to\infty
. Setting this saddle point value to
\displaystyle G^{av}_{\phi}
gives us the exact value of the supersymmetric index.

3.2.3
Corrections to quadratic solution

So far we have considered the solution
\displaystyle Q_{A}
to the equations of motion coming from only leading terms as
\displaystyle G^{av}_{\phi}\to\infty
. Now we correct the solution by taking into account the presence of other terms.

Corrections to non-zero frequency modes:

We introduce
\displaystyle\delta G_{A}=Q_{A}+g_{A}
. The equations of motion for
\displaystyle g_{A}
are not automatically satisfied since
\displaystyle Q_{A}
are not exact classical solutions. Given the explicit factor of
\displaystyle g
in leading quadratic term
\displaystyle g(\delta G_{\psi}^{2}-\delta G_{F}\delta G_{\phi})
and the quadratic term coming from the logarithms, we conclude that the quadratic term for
\displaystyle g_{A}
has an explicit factor of
\displaystyle g
. As a result, a linear term in
\displaystyle g_{A}
on the new saddle point is of the same order as the square of the term it came from (i.e., obtained by replacing
\displaystyle g_{A}
with
\displaystyle Q_{A}
) on the old saddle point.
\displaystyle Q_{A}
solves the equations of motion for the zero mode and the leading quadratic term, hence those terms don’t generate any linear term in
\displaystyle g_{A}
. Hence, up to order
\displaystyle(G^{av}_{\phi})^{-p}
that we are keeping track of in this note, the only source of linear terms are from sub-leading quadratic and leading cubic term. We analyze these terms below. Note the distinction between
\displaystyle g\equiv G^{av}_{\phi}
and
\displaystyle g_{A}
in the following analysis.

The quadratic part of the action for
\displaystyle g_{A}
(we discuss the linear terms separately) comes from the logarithms and the leading quadratic part involving
\displaystyle\delta G_{A}
. It takes the following form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{S_{eff}}{N}\supset&\sum_{\omega\neq 0}\bigg[\frac{g_{\psi}(\omega)^{2}}{(Q^{\psi}(\omega))^{2}}-\frac{g_{\phi}(\omega)^{2}}{2(Q^{\phi}(\omega))^{2}}-\frac{g_{F}(\omega)^{2}}{2(Q^{F}(\omega))^{2}}-(p-1)g^{p-2}(g_{F}(\omega)g_{\phi}(-\omega)-g_{\psi}(\omega)g_{\psi}(-\omega))\bigg]\\
\approx&\sum_{\omega\neq 0}\bigg[-\frac{(p-1)}{2}g^{p-2}\Big(|\omega|g_{\phi}(\omega)+\frac{g_{F}(\omega)}{|\omega|}\Big)^{2}-2(p-1)g^{p-2}g_{\psi}(\omega)^{2}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt-\frac{\sqrt{(p-1)g^{p-2}}}{2|\omega|}(|\omega|^{4}g_{\phi}(\omega)^{2}+g_{F}(\omega)^{2})-\sqrt{(p-1)g^{p-2}}|\omega|g_{\psi}(\omega)^{2}\bigg]\end{split}

(3.55)

To obtain the second line we expanded
\displaystyle Q_{A}
around large
\displaystyle g
and kept leading and first sub-leading order terms. To diagonalize the quadratic term we perform a field re-definition:

\displaystyle g_{\phi}(\omega)=\frac{1}{|\omega|}(\rho(\omega)+\gamma(\omega)),\ \ g_{F}=|\omega|(\rho(\omega)-\gamma(\omega))

(3.56)

Plugging this back into the action we see that the non-zero quadratic term for
\displaystyle\gamma
appears from the first sub-leading term above. To the leading order in
\displaystyle g
the quadratic part of the action takes the following form

\displaystyle\frac{S_{eff}}{N}\supset\sum_{\omega\neq 0}\bigg[-2(p-1)g^{p-2}\rho(\omega)^{2}-\sqrt{(p-1)g^{p-2}}|\omega|\gamma(\omega)^{2}-2(p-1)g^{p-2}g_{\psi}(\omega)^{2}\bigg]

(3.57)

Now we turn to evaluate the linear terms in
\displaystyle g_{A}
. However before proceeding, note that spin-statistics requires

\displaystyle g_{\psi}(-\omega)=-g_{\psi}(\omega),\quad g_{\phi}(-\omega)=g_{\phi}(\omega),\quad g_{F}(-\omega)=g_{F}(\omega)

(3.58)

An immediate consequence of this is that the linear term in
\displaystyle g_{A}
from sub-leading quadratic term vanishes

\displaystyle-2(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}\beta\int d\tau G^{av}_{\psi}\delta G_{\psi}(\tau)\delta G_{\phi}(\tau)

(3.59)

\displaystyle=2(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}\beta\int d\tau G^{av}_{\psi}(Q_{\psi}(\tau)Q_{\phi}(\tau)+Q_{\psi}(\tau)g_{\phi}(\tau)+Q_{\phi}(\tau)g_{\psi}(\tau)+g_{\phi}(\tau)g_{\psi}(\tau))

\displaystyle=2(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}\beta\int d\tau G^{av}_{\psi}Q_{\psi}(\tau)Q_{\phi}(\tau)

The contribution of the leading cubic term is more involved. We first focus on the cubic term involving
\displaystyle g_{\psi}
:

\displaystyle(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}\beta\int d\tau\ 2Q^{\psi}(\tau)g_{\psi}(\tau)Q^{\phi}(\tau)

(3.60)

Together with the quadratic term above, the classical solution for
\displaystyle g_{\psi}
is determined from

\displaystyle 4(p-1)g^{p-2}g_{\psi}(\tau)+2(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}Q^{\psi}(\tau)Q^{\phi}(\tau)=0

(3.61)

Plugging this back to the action gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{S_{eff}}{N}&\supset\frac{((p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3})^{2}}{2(p-1)g^{p-2}}\beta\int d\tau(Q^{\psi}(\tau)Q^{\phi}(\tau))^{2}-\frac{((p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3})^{2}}{(p-1)g^{p-2}}\beta\int d\tau(Q^{\psi}(\tau)Q^{\phi}(\tau))^{2}\\
&=-\frac{((p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3})^{2}}{2(p-1)g^{p-2}}\beta\int d\tau(Q^{\psi}(\tau)Q^{\phi}(\tau))^{2}\end{split}

(3.62)

Now the bosonic cubic terms are obtained from

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}\beta\int d\tau\Big[-\frac{1}{2}(Q^{\phi})^{2}g_{F}+((Q^{\psi})^{2}-Q^{F}Q^{\phi})g_{\phi}\Big]\\
=&-\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}}{2}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}\Big[(2Q^{F}Q^{\phi}-2(Q^{\psi})^{2}+\omega^{2}(Q^{\phi})^{2})(\omega)\frac{\rho(\omega)}{|\omega|}\\
&\hskip 120.0pt+(2Q^{F}Q^{\phi}-2(Q^{\psi})^{2}-\omega^{2}(Q^{\phi})^{2})(\omega)\frac{\gamma(\omega)}{|\omega|}\Big]\end{split}

(3.63)

The products among
\displaystyle Q_{A}
above are taken in position space, then Fourier transformed to frequency space. Now the term accompanying
\displaystyle\gamma
is proportional to

\displaystyle(2Q^{F}Q^{\phi}-2(Q^{\psi})^{2}+\partial_{\tau}^{2}(Q^{\phi})^{2})(\omega)\frac{\gamma(\omega)}{|\omega|}=(-2\partial_{\tau}^{2}Q^{\phi}Q^{\phi}-2(\partial_{\tau}Q^{\phi})^{2}+\partial_{\tau}^{2}(Q^{\phi})^{2})(\omega)\frac{\gamma(\omega)}{|\omega|}=0

(3.64)

Hence there is no linear interaction term for
\displaystyle\gamma
, and its equation of motion is
\displaystyle\gamma=0
. As a result, it doesn’t contribute to the index. On the other hand, the
\displaystyle\rho
interaction term can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}}{2}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}(2Q^{F}Q^{\phi}-2(Q^{\psi})^{2}+\omega^{2}(Q^{\phi})^{2})(\omega)\frac{\rho(\omega)}{|\omega|}\\
=&-\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}}{2}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}(-2\partial_{\tau}^{2}Q^{\phi}Q^{\phi}-2(\partial_{\tau}Q^{\phi})^{2}-\partial_{\tau}^{2}(Q^{\phi})^{2})(\omega)\frac{\rho(\omega)}{|\omega|}\\
=&-\frac{(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}}{2}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}-4\partial_{t}(\partial_{t}Q^{\phi}Q^{\phi})(\omega)\frac{\rho(\omega)}{|\omega|}\\
=&2i(p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}(\partial_{\tau}Q^{\phi}Q^{\phi})(\omega)\frac{\omega}{|\omega|}\rho(\omega)\end{split}

(3.65)

Now we can integrate out
\displaystyle\rho
classically, and the contribution to the index is

\displaystyle\frac{((p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3})^{2}}{2(p-1)g^{p-2}}\beta\int d\tau(Q^{\phi}(\tau)\partial_{\tau}Q^{\phi}(\tau))^{2}=\frac{((p-2)_{2}\,g^{p-3})^{2}}{2(p-1)g^{p-2}}\beta\int d\tau(Q^{\phi}(\tau)Q^{\psi}(\tau))^{2}

(3.66)

Note that it exactly cancels against (
3.62
), so the fluctuation of the cubic terms don’t give any contribution. We conclude that our effective action (
3.54
) does not get any corrections up to the order we are interested in.

Corrections to zero frequency modes:
For the fermionic mode we write
\displaystyle G_{\psi}(\omega=0)=\beta G^{av}_{\psi}+g_{\psi}(\omega=0)
. Just like the non-zero frequency counterpart, the quadratic piece in
\displaystyle g_{\psi}(\omega=0)
scales as
\displaystyle g^{2-p}
. Hence the only linear terms that can contribute are from sub-leading quadratic piece up to the order we are considering by the same argument as presented before. However the linear piece from sub-leading quadratic piece vanishes and hence on-shell
\displaystyle g_{\psi}(\omega=0)=0
. Finally the correction to
\displaystyle G_{F}(\omega=0)=\beta G^{av}_{F}+g_{F}(\omega=0)
is sub-leading because its quadratic piece scales as
\displaystyle g^{2(p-1)}>g^{p-2}
.

This completes our argument for recovering the Witten index from bi-local path integral.

3.3
Two copies of
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric SYK model

In this sub-section we take two copies of
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric SYK model and call them left (L) and right (R) sub-systems. We can couple these two copies and still preserve diagonal super-symmetries that acts on both sides in the same way. One such coupling is given by

\displaystyle\int d\tau(d^{2}\theta\lambda_{ij}\Phi^{i}_{L}\Phi^{j}_{R}+d^{2}\bar{\theta}\bar{\lambda}_{ij}\bar{\Phi}^{i}_{L}\bar{\Phi}^{j}_{R})

(3.67)

The coupling also does not change the asymptotic nature of the super-potential for
\displaystyle p>2
. Hence even with this coupling the index of the two sided system will take the factorized form. We have verified this numerically.
Our Mathematica code is attached as supplementary files to this paper.
The factorized index does not depend on the precise value of the SYK coupling or two sided coupling. This allows us to average over both of them to get a disordered theory. The novelty lies in recovering the exact value of the index from this disorder averaged coupled theory. In fact we will see that the saddle points that contribute to the index comes from equations that couple
\displaystyle G^{A}_{LL},G^{A}_{RR}
(
\displaystyle A=\phi,\psi,F
).

We average over the SYK coupling and
\displaystyle\lambda_{ij}
with Gaussian measure:

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{i_{1}i_{2}}\bar{\lambda}_{i^{\prime}_{1}i^{\prime}_{2}}\rangle_{D}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2N}\delta_{i_{1}i^{\prime}_{1}}\delta_{i_{2}i^{\prime}_{2}}

(3.68)

With this interaction, the starting point for the large
\displaystyle N
analysis should be

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{S_{eff}}{N}&=-\log det\Big(-\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\delta_{IJ}\partial^{2}_{\tau}-\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{IJ}\Big)-\log det\Big(\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\delta_{IJ}-\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{IJ}\Big)\\
&+2\log det\Big(\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\delta_{IJ}\partial_{\tau}-\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{IJ}\Big)-{\sigma^{2}}\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\Big[G^{\phi}_{LL}G^{F}_{RR}+G^{F}_{LL}G^{\phi}_{RR}-2G^{\psi}_{LL}G^{\psi}_{RR}\Big]\\
&-\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\Big(\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{IJ}G^{\phi}_{IJ}+\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{IJ}G^{F}_{IJ}+2\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{IJ}G^{\psi}_{IJ}-(p-1)J^{p}(G^{\psi}_{IJ})^{2}(G^{\phi}_{IJ})^{p-2}+J^{p}G^{F}_{IJ}(G^{\phi}_{IJ})^{2}\Big)\end{split}

(3.69)

In frequency modes, it can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{S_{eff}}{N}&=\sum_{\omega}\Bigg[-\log\Big[(\omega^{2}-\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{LL})((\omega^{2}-\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{RR}))-\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{LR}\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{RL}\Big]\\
&-\log\Big[(1-\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{LL})((1-\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{RR}))-\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{LR}\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{RL}\Big]\\
&+2\log\Big[(-i\omega-\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{LL})((-i\omega-\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{RR}))-\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{LR}\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{RL}\Big]\\
&-\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{IJ}(\omega)G^{\phi}_{IJ}(-\omega)-\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{IJ}(\omega)G^{F}_{IJ}(-\omega)+2\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{IJ}(\omega)G^{\psi}_{IJ}(-\omega)\Bigg]\\
&-\sum_{\omega}\sigma^{2}\Big[G^{\phi}_{LL}(\omega)G^{F}_{RR}(-\omega)+G^{\phi}_{RR}(\omega)G^{F}_{LL}(-\omega)-2G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega)G^{\psi}_{RR}(-\omega)\Big]\\
&+\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\Big((p-1)(G^{\psi}_{IJ}(\tau-\tau^{\prime}))^{2}(G^{\phi}_{IJ}(\tau-\tau^{\prime}))^{p-2}-G^{F}_{IJ}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})(G^{\phi}_{IJ}(\tau-\tau^{\prime}))^{p-1}\Big)\end{split}

(3.70)

Typical wormhole-like-saddles
have non-vanishing left right correlation, i.e.,
\displaystyle G^{A}_{LR}\neq 0,\Sigma^{A}_{LR}\neq 0

[
22
]
. In
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=4
SYK model that we consider here, we don’t expect such contribution to be present. Therefore we look for saddles that satisfy
\displaystyle G^{A}_{LR}=\Sigma^{A}_{LR}=0
. From the equation above we note that, saddle point equation obtained by varying
\displaystyle G^{A}_{LR},\Sigma^{A}_{LR}
are automatically satisfied if we set
\displaystyle G^{A}_{LR}=\Sigma^{A}_{LR}=0
. This explains why this is a consistent background. On the other hand, in
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=2
SYK model these wormhole like saddles might contribute to the disorder averaged index. We explore this very interesting possibility in our upcoming future work
[
44
]
. In this paper, we are looking for solutions that preserve diagonal supersymmetry. Hence, it is natural to look for a symmetric saddle point
7
7

7

Note that positivity constraints force us to have
\displaystyle G^{av,\phi}_{RR}\geq 0,G^{av,\phi}_{LL}\geq 0
. So it makes sense to work with the symmetric saddle point
\displaystyle G^{\phi}_{RR}=G^{\phi}_{LL}
. Relative sign of
\displaystyle G^{\psi}_{RR},G^{F}_{RR}
to
\displaystyle G^{\phi}_{RR}
is determined by supersymmetry (same statement holds for left copy). Hence we look for the symmetric solution for all the components.

\displaystyle G^{A}_{LL}=G^{A}_{RR}

(3.71)

As before we assume non-zero frequency parts are small compared to the zero frequency parts of
\displaystyle G^{A}
. Define
\displaystyle G^{av,\phi}_{LL}=G^{av,\phi}_{RR}=g
. After expanding, we work up to leading quadratic order in non-zero frequency parts. This gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{S_{eff}}{N}&=\sum_{\omega}\Bigg[-\log\Big[(\omega^{2}-\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{LL})((\omega^{2}-\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{RR}))\Big]-\log\Big[(1-\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{LL})((1-\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{RR}))\Big]\\
&+2\log\Big[(-i\omega-\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{LL})((-i\omega-\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{RR}))\Big]\\
&-\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{LL}(\omega)G^{\phi}_{LL}(-\omega)-\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{LL}(\omega)G^{F}_{LL}(-\omega)+2\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega)G^{\psi}_{LL}(-\omega)\\
&-\bar{\Sigma}^{\phi}_{RR}(\omega)G^{\phi}_{RR}(-\omega)-\bar{\Sigma}^{F}_{RR}(\omega)G^{F}_{RR}(-\omega)+2\bar{\Sigma}^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega)G^{\psi}_{RR}(-\omega)\Bigg]\\
&-\sum_{\omega}\sigma^{2}\Big[G^{\phi}_{LL}(\omega)G^{F}_{RR}(-\omega)+G^{\phi}_{RR}(\omega)G^{F}_{LL}(-\omega)-2G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega)G^{\psi}_{RR}(-\omega)\Big]\\
&-\beta G_{LL}^{F}(\omega=0)g^{p-1}+(p-1)g^{p-2}G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega=0)^{2}\\
&+(p-1)g^{p-2}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}\Big((\delta G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega)\delta G^{\psi}_{LL}(-\omega))-\delta G^{F}_{LL}(\omega)\delta G^{\phi}_{LL}(-\omega)\Big)\\
&-\beta G_{RR}^{F}(\omega=0)g^{p-1}+(p-1)g^{p-2}G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega=0)^{2}\\
&+(p-1)g^{p-2}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}\Big((\delta G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega)\delta G^{\psi}_{RR}(-\omega))-\delta G^{F}_{RR}(\omega)\delta G^{\phi}_{RR}(-\omega)\Big)\end{split}

(3.72)

This is exactly 2 copies of the one sided theory, with the additional
\displaystyle\sigma^{2}
term. So we can follow the one-sided calculation to substitute the
\displaystyle\bar{\Sigma}
saddles and get the effective action in terms of
\displaystyle G^{A}
fields, which is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{S_{eff}}{N}&=-\beta G^{F}_{LL}(\omega=0)g^{p-1}+(p-1)g^{p-2}(G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega=0))^{2}\\
&\hskip 0.0pt+\sum_{\omega}\left[\log(G^{\phi}_{LL}(\omega)G^{F}_{LL}(\omega))-2\log(G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega))\right]+\sum_{\omega}\Big[-2i\omega G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega)-\omega^{2}G^{\phi}_{LL}(\omega)-G^{F}_{LL}(\omega)\Big]\\
&+(p-1)g^{p-2}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}\Big[-\delta G^{F}_{LL}(-\omega)\delta G^{\phi}_{LL}(\omega)+\delta G^{\psi}_{LL}(-\omega)\delta G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega)\Big]\\
&-\beta G^{F}_{RR}(\omega=0)g^{p-1}+(p-1)g^{p-2}(G^{\psi}_{RR})^{2}(\omega=0)\\
&\hskip 0.0pt+\sum_{\omega}\left[\log(G^{\phi}_{RR}(\omega)G^{F}_{RR}(\omega))-2\log(G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega))\right]+\sum_{\omega}\Big[-2i\omega G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega)-\omega^{2}G^{\phi}_{RR}(\omega)-G^{F}_{RR}(\omega)\Big]\\
&+(p-1)g^{p-2}\sum_{\omega\neq 0}\Big[-\delta G^{F}_{RR}(-\omega)\delta G^{\phi}_{RR}(\omega)+\delta G^{\psi}_{RR}(-\omega)\delta G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega)\Big]\\
&+\sigma^{2}\sum_{\omega}\Big[-G^{F}_{RR}(-\omega)G^{\phi}_{LL}(\omega)-G^{F}_{LL}(-\omega)G^{\phi}_{RR}(\omega)+G^{\psi}_{RR}(-\omega)G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega)+G^{\psi}_{LL}(-\omega)G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega)\Big]\end{split}

(3.73)

Equations of motion couple left and right sub-systems due to the
\displaystyle\sigma^{2}
term. This is an interesting situation where left right solutions are coupled even though left-right direct correlation is absent.

Zero frequency modes:
Equations of motion for zero frequency modes are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{F}_{LL}(\omega=0)&=\frac{1}{1+\beta(G^{av,\phi}_{LL})^{p-1}+\sigma^{2}\beta G^{av,\phi}_{RR}}\ ,\ G^{F}_{RR}(\omega=0)=\frac{1}{1+\beta(G^{av,\phi}_{RR})^{p-1}+\sigma^{2}\beta G^{av,\phi}_{LL}}\\
&=\frac{1}{1+\beta g^{p-1}+\sigma^{2}\beta g}\hskip 110.0pt=\frac{1}{1+\beta g^{p-1}+\sigma^{2}\beta g}\end{split}

(3.74)

and

\displaystyle(p-1)g^{p-2}G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega=0)-\frac{1}{G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega=0)}+\sigma^{2}G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega=0)=0

(3.75)

\displaystyle(p-1)g^{p-2}G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega=0)-\frac{1}{G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega=0)}+\sigma^{2}G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega=0)=0

This is a quadratic equation, we pick the symmetric solution for the fermionic zero frequency modes

\displaystyle G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega=0)=G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega=0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(p-1)g^{p-2}+\sigma^{2}}}

(3.76)

Before discussing the nonzero frequency solutions, we check the contribution to the index from the zero modes (later we will see that the nonzero mode contributions cancel out, like before)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{S_{eff}}{N}&\supset-1-1+2+\log(\frac{\beta g}{1+\beta g^{p-1}+\sigma^{2}\beta g})+\log((p-1)g^{p-2}+\sigma^{2})\\
&\hskip 30.0pt+\log(\frac{\beta g}{1+\beta g^{p-1}+\sigma^{2}\beta g})+\log((p-1)g^{p-2}+\sigma^{2})\end{split}

(3.77)

So the zero mode contribution to the index is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\log\mathcal{I}}{N}\supset 2\log(p-1)+2\log\Big[\frac{\beta g^{p-1}+\sigma^{2}\beta g}{1+\beta g^{p-1}+\sigma^{2}\beta g}\Big]

(3.78)

Again we see that the contribution of zero frequency part to the index gets maximized at
\displaystyle g\to\infty
. Setting this saddle point value gives us the exact index. Essentially we see that the effect of
\displaystyle\sigma^{2}
is not important when
\displaystyle g\to\infty
, which resonates well with the fact that this left-right coupling does not change the asymptotic nature of the super-potential. We expect this property to be general feature of such deformations. Now we turn to show that indeed the contribution of the non-zero modes to the index vanish.

Non-zero frequency modes:
The equations of motion for
\displaystyle\omega>0
modes of
\displaystyle G^{A}
are

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{G^{F}_{LL}(\omega)}-1-\sigma^{2}G^{\phi}_{RR}(-\omega)-(p-1)g^{p-2}G^{\phi}_{LL}(-\omega)=0\\
&\frac{1}{G^{F}_{RR}(\omega)}-1-\sigma^{2}G^{\phi}_{LL}(-\omega)-(p-1)g^{p-2}G^{\phi}_{RR}(-\omega)=0\\
&\frac{1}{G^{\phi}_{LL}(\omega)}-\omega^{2}-\sigma^{2}G^{F}_{RR}(-\omega)-(p-1)g^{p-2}G^{F}_{LL}(-\omega)=0\\
&\frac{1}{G^{\phi}_{RR}(\omega)}-\omega^{2}-\sigma^{2}G^{F}_{LL}(-\omega)-(p-1)g^{p-2}G^{F}_{RR}(-\omega)=0\\
&-\frac{1}{G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega)}-i\omega+\sigma^{2}G^{\psi}_{RR}(-\omega)+(p-1)g^{p-2}G^{\psi}_{LL}(-\omega)=0\\
&-\frac{1}{G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega)}-i\omega+\sigma^{2}G^{\psi}_{LL}(-\omega)+(p-1)g^{p-2}G^{\psi}_{RR}(-\omega)=0\end{split}

(3.79)

The solution to these, compatible with the SUSY conditions, are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{\psi}_{LL}(\omega)=G^{\psi}_{RR}(\omega)=isgn(\omega)\frac{-|\omega|+\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4((p-1)g^{p-2}+\sigma^{2})}}{2((p-1)g^{p-2}+\sigma^{2})}\\
G^{F}_{LL}(\omega)=G^{F}_{RR}(\omega)=|\omega|\frac{-|\omega|+\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4((p-1)g^{p-2}+\sigma^{2})}}{2((p-1)g^{p-2}+\sigma^{2})}\\
G^{\phi}_{LL}(\omega)=G^{\phi}_{RR}(\omega)=\frac{1}{|\omega|}\frac{-|\omega|+\sqrt{\omega^{2}+4((p-1)g^{p-2}+\sigma^{2})}}{2((p-1)g^{p-2}+\sigma^{2})}\end{split}

(3.80)

This is the same as the solution for decoupled equations with the shift
\displaystyle g^{p-2}\to g^{p-2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{p-1}
. It is easy to see from the explicit expression (
3.73
) that the nonzero frequency part of the coupled index is just given by the shift

g^{p-2}\to g^{p-2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{p-1}

to the decoupled index, and therefore the nonzero frequency contribution cancels out, similar to earlier calculations.

4
Discussion and future directions

In this paper we have focused on the
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric SYK model and performed a disorder average on coupling constants that do not alter the value of the index. We also discussed saddle point solutions with non-trivial coupling between two copies of the theory for a fixed value of the zero mode of bi-local fields. These coupled solutions notably have vanishing left-right two point correlation function. The action for the bi-local fields still couples the left-left and right-right ones. The large
\displaystyle N
saddle at
\displaystyle g\rightarrow\infty
effectively decouples the system completely. At the present stage we lack understanding of a gravitational dual of these configurations where the zero mode of bi-local fields are off-shell and the action remains coupled. Do they correspond to horizon-less configurations with huge entropy? It would be fantastic to make progress in this direction in the future.

Our arguments regarding the disorder-averaged representation for products of the Witten index are general and apply to any quantum field theory. Given the close similarity between the model discussed here and the BFSS model (see
[
45
,
46
,
47
]
and references therein for a recent review of the BFSS model), understanding such disorder average in BFSS model is an intriguing research direction. In addition, these techniques can be generalized to higher spacetime dimensions easily.

Here, we have limited our analysis to a specific class of couplings that preserve the value of the Witten index. Nonetheless, it is also possible to perform disorder averaging over supersymmetry-preserving coupling constants in a more general context. In such cases, the resulting quantity does not necessarily factorize across different copies of the theory, opening up the intriguing possibility of contributions from BPS analog of Maldacena, Qi type wormhole configurations
[
48
]
. This suggests a richer structure in the disorder-averaged theories, which warrants further investigation. Some results along these directions will appear in an upcoming work by the authors
[
44
]
.

Maximum displacement of critical centered branching random walks under minimal assumptions
Thomas Lehéricy

Abstract
We study the critical centered branching random walk, with offspring and displacement distributions having finite variance, under minimal assumptions on its structure. We show that the probability that the position of the right-most particle is larger than r r decays like an explicit constant times r − 2 r^{-2} ; this generalizes an earlier result by Lalley and Shao. In addition, we obtain the convergence in distribution of the progeny of the branching random walk conditioned on the position of the right-most particle being large. Our results are applied to multitype branching random walks under minimal assumptions.



1
Introduction

A
branching random walk
(
brw
) models a population of individuals that evolves in discrete time and in space. Start with a single individual at generation 0. Afterwards, at every generation, each individual reproduces independently of the others and then dies, giving birth to a random number of offsprings. Each offspring inherits the position of its parent, plus some random displacement.
The maximum displacement of a
brw
, i.e. the maximum position of a particle of the
brw
, is a quantity of interest in the study of populations, see e.g.
[
Sawyer and Fleischman, 1979
]
. Its behavior is connected to the Fisher-KPP equation
[
McKean, 1975
]
. The
brw
is related to continuous-time
branching Lévy processes
, where the genealogy of the population is given by a Yule process and where each particle evolves according to a Lévy process. A central example of branching Lévy process is the branching Brownian motion. The behavior of
brw
s and branching Lévy processes are often very similar, although the methods to study them differ.

A key criterion that determines the behavior of the
brw
is the expected number of offsprings
m
of a particle.
The
brw
is
subcritical
if
m<1
,
critical
if
m=1
, and
supercritical
if
m>1
. In the subcritical and critical case, the
drift
, i.e. the expected mean displacement of the offsprings of a particle, also matters; we say that the
brw
is
centered
if this drift is zero.

A well-known case is when the
brw
is supercritical; in this case, the population diverges towards
+\infty
with positive probability.
[
A\̈mathrm{i}dékon, 2013
]
has given a precise asymptotic for the maximum displacement at generation
n
, see also
[
Bramson, 1978
]
for the related case of branching Brownian motion. The behavior of the branching Brownian motion near its maximum has also been described, see e.g.
[
A\̈mathrm{i}dékon et al., 2013
]
.

When the population is subcritical or critical, the
brw
goes extinct almost surely. The
maximum displacement
is the maximal position attained by an individual across all generations. Results vary depending on whether the walk is centered or not; on whether the offspring distribution has finite variance or is in the domain of attraction of a
\gamma
-stable distribution with
\gamma<2
; on whether the distribution of displacements has sufficiently high moments; and on whether the
brw
is critical or subcritical.

The quantity of interest to us is the tail of the maximum displacement, i.e. the asymptotic behavior of the quantity
\mathbb{P}(M\geq t)
as
t\to\infty
where
M
is the maximum displacement of the
brw
. Before describing the literature, let us list a few other questions that were considered.
[
Kesten, 1995
]
shows the convergence in distribution of the maximum displacement at generation
n
of the critical
brw
(with sufficiently high moments on its offspring and displacement distributions) conditioned to survive until
\beta n
for some
\beta>0
.
[
Neuman and Zheng, 2021
]
study the maximum displacement at a given generation of a slightly supercritical
brw
with bounded displacements and offspring distributions having a third moment.
Another interesting case is presented in
[
Fu and Hong, 2025
]
, which considers a critical
brw
in random environment with sufficiently high moments on the offspring distribution and with Gaussian displacements; the authors reveal a significant difference in the behavior of the maximum displacement compared to the case of constant environment.
Killed branching Lévy processes, i.e. where particles are killed when they reached the negative half-line, are also a subject of interest
[
Zheng, 2015
,
Ren et al., 2025
,
Hou et al., 2025c
,
Hou et al., 2025d
]
.

Earlier results on the tail of the maximum displacement are found in
[
Sawyer and Fleischman, 1979
]
, which establishes the tail behavior of the maximum displacement of critical and subcritical branching Brownian motion where the offspring distribution has finite third moment. This was generalized in
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
to centered critical
brw
with offspring distribution having finite third moment and displacement distribution having finite
4+\varepsilon
moment. In virtually all articles dealing with
brw
, restrictive assumptions on the reproduction scheme (i.e. the joint distribution of the displacements of the offsprings) are made to simplify computations. The goal of our article is to establish some results of
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
(and some new ones) under minimal hypotheses.

Heavy-tailed displacements have mostly been considered in the case of Lévy branching processes.
Offspring distributions having finite variance are found in

[
Lalley and Shao, 2016
]
, which works on a critical branching Lévy process with binary branching where the displacement follows a symmetric stable process with index
\alpha
. They obtain an asymptotic of the tail on the maximum displacement in
\mathbb{P}(M\geq t)\sim(2/\alpha)^{1/2}t^{-\alpha/2}
, see also
[
Profeta, 2021
]
when the displacement is a critical or subcritical
\alpha
-stable Lévy process with positive jumps, and
[
Profeta, 2024
]
for spectrally negative Lévy processes—more on that later.
This is generalized in
[
Hou et al., 2025b
]
, where the offspring distribution is in the domain of attraction of a non-Gaussian stable distribution. More precisely, using our notations from Section
2.1
, they assume
\mathbb{P}(\chi(\mathbb{R})\geq t)\sim\kappa t^{-\alpha}
for some
\kappa>0
and
\alpha\in(1,2)
.

Subcritical branching processes exhibit a different behavior.

[
Neuman and Zheng, 2017
]
consider a subcritical
brw
where the offspring distribution has finite third moment and the distribution of the displacements has an exponential moment, and shows that the maximum displacement has an exponential tail; the exponential moment on the displacements is crucial. Such a result was already established for subcritical branching Brownian motion in
[
Sawyer and Fleischman, 1979
]
. Building upon this,

[
Hou and Zhang, 2025
]
consider subcritical
brw
under the
L\log L
condition and with displacement having sufficiently high exponential moments, and determines that the tail of the maximum displacement is exponential or subexponential, establishing a sharp transition between the two regimes. These results also hold when the
brw
is killed when reaching the negative half-line.

[
Profeta, 2024
]
considers critical and subcritical branching spectrally negative Lévy processes, where the offspring distribution has a third moment. They show that the maximum displacement has an exponential tail in the subcritical case, and investigate the tail in the critical case when the Lévy process is centered or has positive or negative drift respectively. Finally,
[
Hou et al., 2025a
]
consider critical branching Lévy processes where the offspring distribution belongs to the domain of attraction of a
\gamma
-stable distribution with
\gamma\in(1,2]
(more precisely
\mathbb{P}(\chi(\mathbb{R})>t)\sim\kappa t^{-\gamma}
for some
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
if
\gamma<2
, or
\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}]<\infty
if
\gamma=2
), and subcritical branching Lévy processes where the offspring distribution satisfies the
L\log L
condition, and where the displacement follows an
\alpha
-stable Lévy process with positive jumps.

In the rest of the article, we work on a critical, centered
brw
with offspring distribution having finite variance and displacement having—essentially—a moment of order 4. The closest works are
[
Sawyer and Fleischman, 1979
]
for the branching Brownian motion and
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
for the
brw
. While the distribution of the maximum displacement of a critical
brw
depends on the
reproduction scheme
, i.e. the joint distribution of the displacements of offsprings, and is not universal, the tail is:
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
established that the probability that the maximum displacement exceeds
r
behaves like a constant times
r^{-2}
as
r\to\infty
, where the constant depends on the reproduction scheme. In order to keep their proof simple, they restricted the reproduction schemes of the
brw
.

This article establishes that the asympotic of the tail proven in
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
, Theorem 1]
holds for
brw
in full generality (Theorem
1
and Corollary
2
), most importantly without any assumption on the dependencies in the reproduction scheme. We aim to use the weakest hypotheses possible (Assumptions
1
); see Section
2.3
for a discussion on their minimality. While our method follows that of
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
, significant work is required to establish the key estimates, for example the Feynman-Kac representation (our Corollary
16
,
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
, Corollary 7]
). In addition, we provide in Lemma
11
a new estimate that supersedes
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
, Lemma 11]
, the end of which proof contains a circular argument.

Furthermore, we find in Theorem
3
an asymptotic on the probability that the maximum displacement
equals

r
as
r\to\infty
, when the displacement takes integer values. We then prove in Theorem
4
and
5
the convergence in distribution of the total progeny of the
brw
, and of related quantities, when we condition the maximum displacement to be large. Our method for these two Theorems makes use of a re-interpretation of the problem as a study of the maximum displacement of an almost-critical subcritical
brw
. As an application, we present how to generalize our results to multitype
brw
s (Section
2.4
), and use this to obtain information on the geometry of generic critical bipartite Boltzmann planar maps (Section
2.5
). We then comment in Section
2.6
on the connection between the
brw
in the regime we study and the Brownian snake.

Section
3
establishes Theorem
1
. A brief overview of our method can be found at the beginning of the Section. Section
4
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
3
, and Section
5
to the proof of Theorems
4
and
5
. Finally, we gathered a useful technical Lemma and some proofs in the Appendix.

2
Main results

2.1
Preliminaries

Planar trees.

We use Neveu’s notation
[
Neveu, 1986
]
. Define
\mathcal{U}:=\cup_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{N}_{*}^{n}
the infinite Ulam–Harris tree of finite sequences of natural numbers, where
\mathbb{N}_{*}^{0}=\{\emptyset\}
,
\mathbb{N}_{*}=\{1,2,\dots\}
and
\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{N}_{*}\cup\{0\}
. If
u,v\in\mathcal{U}
, denote
uv
their concatenation and
|u|
the length of
u
. We say that
u
is an ancestor of
v
if and only if there exists
w\in\mathcal{U}
such that
v=uw
; if
|w|=1
then
u
is the parent of
v
, denoted by
p(v)
. For every
u\in\mathcal{U}
, the ancestral line of
u
is the sequence
(u_{0}=\emptyset,u_{1},\dots,u_{|u|}=u)
where for every
0\leq j\leq|u|
,
u_{j}
is the only ancestor of
u
with
|u_{j}|=j
. A tree
T\subset\mathcal{U}
is a subset of
\mathcal{U}
such that
\emptyset\in T
, and for every
u=p(u)k
with
k\in\mathbb{N}_{*}
,
p(u)\in T
and
p(u)j\in T
for every
0\leq j\leq k
.

Bienaymé–Galton–Watson trees and processes.

Let
k_{v}
be i.i.d.
\mathbb{N}
-valued random variables with common distribution
\mu
. The Bienaymé–Galton–Watson tree with offspring distribution
\mu
is the tree
T
such that
\emptyset\in T
, and for every
u\in\mathcal{U}
,
u\in T
if and only if
p(u)\in T
and
u=p(u)j
with
1\leq j\leq k_{p(u)}
. The number of vertices in
T
at generation
n
,
N_{n}:=\#\{v\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{n},v\in T\}
follows a Bienaymé–Galton–Watson process. If
\sum_{n\geq 0}n\mu(n)=1
we say that the Bienaymé–Galton–Watson tree (resp. process) is critical. Assuming
\mu(1)<1
, the process goes extinct almost surely, i.e.
N_{n}\to 0
almost surely as
n\to\infty
.

Point processes.

A (real-valued) point process is a random variable with values in the set
\mathbb{M}
of all counting measures over
\mathbb{R}
, i.e. the set of all measures
\mu
such that
\mu(A)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
for every Borel set
A
, equipped with the sigma-algebra generated by the collection of
\mu\mapsto\mu(A)
over all Borel sets
A
. We let
\sup\mu
be the supremum of the support of
\mu
, with
\sup\mu=-\infty
if
\mu=0
.
A point process
\chi
is finite if
\chi(\mathbb{R})<\infty
almost surely; in this case there exists a measurable enumeration
(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\chi(\mathbb{R})}
of the atoms of
\chi
, i.e. a family of random variables such that
\chi=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\chi(\mathbb{R})}\delta_{X_{i}}
with
\delta_{x}
the Dirac mass at
x
— for example, we can enumerate the atoms in increasing order.

The mean measure of a point process
\chi
is the measure
M:A\mapsto\mathbb{E}[\chi(A)]
for every Borel set
A
. Following
[
Baccelli and Błaszczyszyn, 2009
]
, define the measure
C
on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{M}
by

C(A\times\Gamma)=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{A}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\chi-\delta_{x}\in\Gamma\right\}}\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right].

Since
C(\cdot,\Gamma)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
M
for every
\Gamma
, we have

C(A\times\Gamma)=\int_{A}P_{x}(\Gamma)\mathrm{d}M(x).

When
M
is locally finite, the function
(x,\Gamma)\mapsto P_{x}(\Gamma)
can be made into a stochastic kernel; we call
P_{x}
the reduced Palm distribution of
\chi
at
x
. We then have the reduced Campbell–Little–Mecke formula: for every positive and measurable, resp. bounded and measurable
f
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\int f(x,\chi-\delta_{x})\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right]=\int f(x,\phi)\mathrm{d}P_{x}(\phi)\mathrm{d}M(x).

(1)

A point process distributed under
P_{x}
has itself a mean measure, which we denote by
M_{x}
. For every positive and measurable, resp. bounded and measurable
f
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\int\left(\int f(x,y)\mathrm{d}(\chi-\delta_{x})(y)\right)\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right]=\int\left(\int f(x,y)\mathrm{d}M_{x}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}M(x).

(2)

Decorated Branching Random Walk.

Let
\mathcal{B}:=(\chi,\Lambda,D,(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\chi(\mathbb{R})})
, where
\chi
is a point process,
\Lambda\geq 0
a random variable with
\Lambda\geq\sup\chi
a.s.,
D\geq 0
, and
(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\chi(\mathbb{R})}
is a measurable enumeration of the atoms of
\chi
. We define the decorated
brw

(T,(D_{v})_{v\in T},(X_{v})_{v\in T},(\Lambda_{v})_{v\in T})
with
reproduction scheme

\mathcal{B}
as follows. Let
(\chi_{u},\Lambda^{(u)},D_{u},(X^{(u)}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\chi_{u}(\mathbb{R})})_{u\in\mathcal{U}}
be i.i.d. copies of
\mathcal{B}
. For every
u\in\mathcal{U}
, let
k_{u}:=\chi_{u}(\mathbb{R})
, and let
T
be the tree constructed from the
(k_{u})_{u}
(it is a Bienaymé–Galton–Watson tree). Define
X_{\emptyset}=0
,
\Lambda_{\emptyset}=\Lambda^{(\emptyset)}
, and for every
u=p(u)j\in T
, define
X_{u}=X_{p(u)}+X^{(p(u))}_{j}
and
\Lambda_{u}=X_{u}+\Lambda^{(u)}
.
The joint process
(T,(D_{u})_{u\in T},(X_{u})_{u\in T},(\Lambda_{u})_{u\in T})
defines the decorated
brw
with reproduction scheme
\mathcal{B}
.
We write
G_{n}:=\sum_{u\in T,|u|=n}\delta_{X_{u}}
for the point process of its vertices at generation
n
.

2.2
Statement of the theorems

In this article, we make the following assumptions:

Assumptions 1
.

1.

the
brw
is critical:
\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]=M(\mathbb{R})=1
,

2.

the
brw
is centered:
\mathbb{E}\left[\int x\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right]=\int x\mathrm{d}M(x)=0
,

3.

\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}]=\sigma^{2}+1\in(1,\infty)
,

4.

\mathbb{E}\left[\int x^{2}\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right]=\int x^{2}\mathrm{d}M(x)=\eta^{2}\in(0,\infty)
,

5.

\mathbb{P}(\Lambda>r)=o(r^{-4})
as
r\to\infty
,

6.

\mathbb{E}[D]=1
.

Note that 3. and 4. imply that the reproduction scheme is non-degenerate, i.e. that
\mathbb{P}(\chi(\mathbb{R})=1)<1
and
M(\{0\})<1
. Point 5. holds if
\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{4}]<\infty
, see Lemma
31
. Point 6. is here to simplify notations and could be replaced by “
D
is an integrable, non-negative and not a.s. zero random variable” up to an extra constant in the statements of Theorems
4
and
5
.

We are interested in studying the distribution of its maximum displacement
\sup_{v\in T}X_{v}
, or more generally of its maximum decoration
\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}
. More precisely, we establish the following results.

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumptions
1
, asymptotically as
r\to\infty
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}>r\right)\sim\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}r^{2}}.

Taking
\Lambda=\sup(0,\sup\chi)
, we get the immediate corollary.

Corollary 2
.

Under Assumptions
1
, asymptotically as
r\to\infty
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{v\in T}X_{v}>r\right)\sim\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}r^{2}}.

This corollary is a generalization of
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
, Theorem 1]
, which is established for the specific reproduction scheme
\chi=L\delta_{X}
with
X
in
L^{4+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{Z})
,
\mathbb{E}[X]=0
,
\mathbb{E}[L^{3}]<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[L]=1
(and which can be extended to
X\in L^{4}
and
L\in L^{2}
with minimal effort). We extend it to very general reproduction schemes, with a
brw
that is not necessarily in
\mathbb{Z}
, and with minimal moment assumptions.

In addition, we prove a range of new results. The first one considers the probability that the maximum of the decorations takes a specific value, when the
brw
is
\mathbb{Z}
-valued. The maximum span of a random variable is the largest
d
such that there exists
x
such that the support of the random variable is contained in
x+d\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 3
.

Under Assumptions
1
, if
M
is supported on
\mathbb{Z}
and has maximal span
1
and if
\Lambda
is
\mathbb{N}
-valued, then asymptotically as
r\to\infty
with
r\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}=r\right)\sim\frac{12\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}r^{3}}.

It is likely that such an asymptotic could be established for displacements that have a density with respect to the Lebesgue measure, but we chose not to investigate this.

The next results establish the asymptotic distribution of the total weight (the sum of
D_{v}
over all
v
in the tree) in the
brw
when we condition it on its maximum decoration. This naturally generalizes to multivariate weights: simply let
D_{v}
be the scalar product of the multivariate weight against a fixed vector. These Theorems highlight that the behavior of the total weight is dictated by the total progeny of the
brw
, while the distribution of the weight, and a fortiori how it depends of the rest of the reproduction scheme, plays a marginal role.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\psi
be the unique positive solution of

\psi^{\prime\prime}=\psi^{2}+\psi

with
\psi(x)\to\infty
as
x\to 0
and
\psi(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
. For every
\alpha>0
, define

\mathcal{R}(\alpha)=\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\frac{2\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{12\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}}\right)-1+\frac{\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}\right).

Then
\mathcal{R}
can be extended to
0
by continuity with a series expansion as
\alpha\searrow 0

\frac{\sigma^{2}}{6\eta^{2}}\mathcal{R}(\alpha)=\frac{3}{5}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{2}{7}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{3}+\frac{3}{25}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{4}+\dots

and under Assumptions
1
, if
t(r,\alpha)=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\left(\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}r^{2}}\right)^{2}
,

r^{2}\left(1-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-t(r,\alpha)\sum_{v\in T}D_{v}}\,|\,\sup_{v}\Lambda_{v}\leq r\right]\right)\underset{r\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{R}(\alpha)

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-t(r,\alpha)\sum_{v\in T}D_{v}}\,|\,\sup_{v}\Lambda_{v}>r\right]\underset{r\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{2\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{12\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}}\right).

We refer to Appendix
6.2
for the series expansion of
\psi
, from which we derived that of
\mathcal{R}
.

Theorem 5
.

Under Assumptions
1
, if
M
is supported on
\mathbb{Z}
and has maximal span
1
and if
\Lambda
is
\mathbb{N}
-valued, then

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-t(r,\alpha)\sum_{v\in T}D_{v}}\,|\,\sup_{v}\Lambda_{v}=r\right]\underset{r\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{2\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{12\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}}\right).

2.3
On the minimality of our hypotheses

Let us explain why assumptions
1
are essentially minimal. First, the dependency structure between the displacement of the offsprings of any vertex can be arbitrary, which generalizes previous results
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
significantly. This generalizability was already foreseen by the authors of
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
, who chose to restrict themselves to a technically convenient framework.

As already observed in
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
in the case
\chi=N\delta_{X}
with
N
and
X
independent, there are counter-examples when
X\notin L^{4}
. In our case, this counterexample covers the case where
t^{4}\mathbb{P}(\Lambda>t)
does not converge to
0
as
t\to\infty
.

The condition
\mathbb{E}[\int x^{2}\mathrm{d}\chi(x)]<\infty
is also required;
[
Lalley and Shao, 2016
]
considers a case where the displacement is in the domain of attraction of a stable distribution, in particular where this quantity is infinite, and deduce asymptotics that differ from ours. When
\mathbb{E}[\int x\mathrm{d}\chi(x)]\neq 0
we also expect a different behavior, namely: if it is positive, then the probability in Theorem
1
should behave like
r^{-1}
, and if it is negative then the tail should be much lighter.

Clearly the fact that
\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]=1
is important; if this was
<1
then the the probability in Theorem
1
would decrease exponentially fast (see e.g.
[
Hou and Zhang, 2025
]
), while the supremum is infinite if this is
>1
. The fact that
\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}]<\infty
is also important, see
[
Hou et al., 2025b
]
for the case of Lévy branching processes.

The fact that we consider decorated
brw
s allows for a unified approach to a range of related models. For example, we are able to handle multitype
brw
s with little extra work, as we illustrate in the Section
2.4
.

2.4
Multitype branching random walks

A natural generalization are multitype
brw
s. In multitype
brw
s, each vertex has a type, and its reproduction scheme depends on its type. Multitype
brw
s appear naturally in the field of random planar maps, for example as the image of bipartite Boltzmann maps by the Bouttier–Di Francesco–Guitter bijection
[
Miermont, 2006
,
Stephenson, 2018
]
.

The scaling limit of multitype Bienaymé–Galton–Watson trees has been investigated in the case of finite number of types
[
Miermont, 2008
]
and of infinite number of types
[
de Raphélis, 2017
]
. In this section, we explain how to extend our results to the case of
brw
s with finitely many types; we follow most of the notations of
[
Miermont, 2008
]
. We do not handle the case of infinitely many type in order to reduce technicalities to a minimum; we do not expect to find obstacles to applying the same idea to
brw
s with infinitely many types.

Theorem 3 in
[
Miermont, 2008
]
establishes the convergence of the multitype
brw
towards the Brownian snake. The assumption requires exponential moments on the offspring distribution (in our monotype setting, this is the distribution of
\chi(\mathbb{R})
) and —roughly speaking— moments of order
8+\varepsilon
on the displacement for some
\varepsilon>0
(in our monotype setting this would mean
\mathbb{E}[\int x^{8+\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}\chi(x)]<\infty
). The authors conjectured in the remark after
[
Miermont, 2008
, Theorem 4]
that this assumption could be reduced to a moment of order
4+\varepsilon
. We plan to extend in a future paper this result to the case of moments of order 2 on the offspring distribution and of order 4 on the displacement.

Let us make our setting more precise. A multitype planar tree is a tree
T\subset\mathcal{U}

2.5
Mobiles of generic critical pointed and rooted Boltzmann planar maps

Our results allow a control of the volume of generic critical rooted and pointed Boltzmann planar maps, conditioned on the distance between their root vertex and marked vertex being large. It also gives us access to the tail of the distribution of this distance. Both are useful to investigate the fine geometric properties of Boltzmann planar maps. We plan to investigate further consequences of our results (and the corollary below) in an upcoming work.

Let us make our point more precise. A planar map is a cellular embedding of a connected finite planar graph on the sphere, considered up to orientation-preserving homeomorphism. By cellular, we mean that the image of edges do not intersect except possibly at their endpoints, and that the connected component of the complement of the union of all edges form a collection of disjoint simply connected open sets, which we call the faces of the map. The degree of a face, resp. of a vertex is the number of edges incident to that face, resp. that vertex (if both “sides” of an edge are incident to the same face, or both “endpoints” are the same vertex, the edge is counted twice). A map is bipartite if all its faces have even degree. A rooted and pointed map
(\textbf{m},e,v)
is a map
m
with a distinguished oriented edge
e
, called the root edge, and a distinguished vertex
v
. A bipartite map is said to be positive if is oriented towards its endpoint that is closest to
v
for the graph distance in the map.

In this section, all the maps we consider are positive rooted and pointed bipartite planar maps.
By convention, we allow the map
\dagger
with a single vertex, no edge, and a single face of degree
0
, and we consider it to be rooted and pointed.

Given nonnegative numbers
(\textbf{q}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
that are not identically zero, define for every map
m

w_{\textbf{q}}(\textbf{m}):=\prod_{f\in F(\textbf{m})}\textbf{q}_{\deg(f)},

where

is the set of faces of
m
, and with the convention
w_{\textbf{q}}(\dagger)=1
. Let
Z_{\textbf{q}}=\sum_{\textbf{m}}w_{\textbf{q}}(\textbf{m})
, where the sum is over all positive rooted and pointed bipartite planar maps. Proposition 1 in
[
Marckert and Miermont, 2007
]
, rephrased in
[
Curien et al., 2019
, Section 3.2.2]
(we follow this latter reference) gives a condition for

(we say that the sequence
(\textbf{q}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is admissible), namely that the function

is such that

admits a positive solution;

is then its smallest positive solution. The weight sequence is critical if

, and generic critical if in addition

Positive pointed and rooted bipartite maps are in bijection by the Bouttier–Di Francesco–Guitter bijection

2.6
Connection with the Brownian snake

Under stronger hypotheses than ours, it has been shown e.g. in
[

and references therein that the

We follow
[

]
. In their case,
\chi=\sum_{i=1}^{\xi}\delta_{U_{i}}
and

, where the
(U_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d. with
\mathbb{E}[U_{1}]=0
,
\Var(U_{1})=\eta^{2}>0
and
\mathbb{P}(U_{1}>t)=o(t^{-4})
, and
\mathbb{E}[\xi]=1
,
\Var(\xi)=\sigma^{2}>0
and
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{a\xi}]<\infty
for some

. The condition of exponential moment on the offspring distribution is present in most of the literature, although it has been noted e.g. in
[
Miermont, 2008
]
that it could be weakened. The structure of

was also weakened e.g. in
[
Miermont, 2008
]
. Still, to the best of our knowledge the state of the art still falls short of our Assumptions
1
, which we conjecture are sufficient to establish the convergence towards the Brownian snake in the sense that follows.

Condition the
brw
to have progeny
n
, i.e.
n
individuals in total across all generations. Let

be the depth-first enumeration of the vertices of

, as defined in
[
Janson and Marckert, 2005
, Section 1.3]
, let

be the generation of

(its height in the tree) and

its label (the discrete head process), defined for integer values and interpolated linearly for non-integer values. These two processes will be enough for our purposes. Then letting

and

[

((\sigma/2)v_{n},(\sigma/2)^{1/2}\eta^{-1}r_{n})\to(v,r)
in distribution as

is the head of the Brownian snake with lifetime process

The quantities of interest for us are then easily defined: the total progeny

\mathbb{P}(M\geq xn^{1/4}\ |\ \textbf{V}=n)\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{P}(r_{*}\geq(\sigma/2)^{1/2}\eta^{-1}x).

(6)

It is well-known that as

\mathbb{P}(\textbf{V}=n)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}n^{-3/2}.

Combining it with (

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M\geq r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(\textbf{V}=n)\mathbb{P}(M\geq r\ |\ \textbf{V}=n)

\displaystyle\sim(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-1/2}\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{-3/2}\mathbb{P}(r_{*}\geq\sigma^{1/2}\eta^{-1}rn^{-1/4})

\displaystyle\sim(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{-1/2}r^{-2}\sum_{n\geq 1}r^{-4}\left(\frac{r^{4}}{n}\right)^{3/2}\mathbb{P}\left(r_{*}\geq\frac{\sigma^{1/2}}{\eta\sqrt{2}}\frac{r}{n^{1/4}}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{r^{2}\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{-3/2}\mathbb{P}\left(r_{*}\geq\frac{\sigma^{1/2}}{\eta\sqrt{2}}\ |\ \zeta=y\right)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2}{\sigma r^{2}}\mathbb{N}_{0}\left(r_{*}\geq\frac{\sigma^{1/2}}{\eta\sqrt{2}}\right),

[

, Section III.5, Section IV.I and Section IV.6]
.) Since

recovering our Corollary

This gives us an interpretation of the limits in Theorem

and
5

So why prove our results directly? Most importantly, our hypotheses are weaker than the state-of-the-art for the convergence of discrete snakes towards the Brownian snake. In fact, we aim to use our estimates to prove the convergence towards the Brownian snake for
brw
satisfying Assumptions

in an upcoming work.

3
Proof of the tail estimate

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1
. Our proof follows that of
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
, Theorem 1]
, with adaptations. The main idea is to use the Markov property of the
brw
to express our object of interest with a martingale: in our case, the function
w(r)=\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}\leq r)}-1\approx\mathbb{P}(\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}>r)
will be expressed as

w(r)\approx\mathbb{E}\left[\mathfrak{W}^{(r)}_{n}w(S_{n})\right]

(7)

where
(S_{n})
is a random walk started at
r
and
\mathfrak{W}^{(r)}_{n}
is a weighting factor that depends on
w
and on the trajectory of the random walk. This is established in Proposition
14
, after first proving the martingale property in Proposition
10
and
13
. Applying the optional stopping theorem at the first time that
S_{n}
goes below a certain value
x
gives us Corollary
16
:

\frac{w(r)}{w(x)}\approx\mathbb{E}\left[\mathfrak{W}^{(r)}_{\tau_{x}}\right],

from which we deduce a functional equation on the limit of the ratio
w(r)/w(x)
as
x
and
r
go to infinity (Proposition
18
). Solving this equation then allows us to obtain the asymptotics of
w
.

The difference in our approach happens, for the most part, in the build-up to Corollary
16
and in the proof of Proposition
18
. For starters, proving (
7
) is much harder in our case than in
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
, because we cannot take advantage of a specific nice structure of the reproduction scheme. This forces us to truncate the possible values of the branching steps (including those of
\Lambda
); as a consequence, (
7
) will only hold up to a remainder that we need to control. The proof of Proposition
13
requires to devote significant effort to the control of the remainder, while its equivalent (Proposition 5) in
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
barely warrants a proof.

In addition, a crucial technical ingredient in the proof is a control of the “continuity” of
w
. This is done in
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
by Proposition 8 and Lemma 11. In this article, I use a new estimate on the continuity of
w
(Lemma
11
), which contains significant information on the behavior of
w
(giving, for example, a lower bound that is optimal up to a multiplicative constant). This provides an alternative to a few steps in
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
. In particular, it provides a justification to the last convergence in the proof of
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
, Lemma 11]
, which does not seem to follow from the estimates in
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
.

Finally, to establish Corollary
16
we need to control the uniform integrability of
\mathfrak{W}^{(r)}_{n}w(S_{n})
before applying the optional stopping theorem. This is easy in
[
Lalley and Shao, 2015
]
where
\mathfrak{W}^{(r)}_{n}\leq 1
almost surely; in our case it is given by Lemma

.

r_{0}
larger if necessary, for every
r_{\bullet}\geq r_{0}
,
r\geq 2r_{\bullet}
and
r_{\bullet}\leq R\leq a/\sqrt{w(r)}
,

\mathbb{E}[B]\leq-cw(r-K).

We then use that
\mathrm{e}^{x}\leq 1+x+x^{2}
for every
|x|\leq 1
and taking
r_{0}
larger if necessary so that
2w(r-R)\leq 1
, to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[I^{(r,R,\delta)}\mathrm{e}^{2(A+B)}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2B}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[2A\mathrm{e}^{2B}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[4A^{2}\mathrm{e}^{2B}\right].

Since
B\leq 0
a.s. the last term is bounded by
4\mathbb{E}[A^{2}]\leq 4w(r-R)^{2}
. Taking
\delta^{0}
smaller if necessary so that
\frac{1-e^{-\delta}}{\delta}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we use that
\mathrm{e}^{x}\leq 1+\frac{1-e^{-\delta}}{\delta}x\leq 1+\frac{x}{2}
for every
-\delta\leq x\leq 0
to bound the first term by

\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{2B}]\leq 1+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[2B]\leq 1-cw(r-K).

For the middle term, write

\mathbb{E}[A\mathrm{e}^{2B}]=\mathbb{E}[A]+\mathbb{E}[A(\mathrm{e}^{2B}-1)]\leq w(r-R)\mathbb{E}[1-\mathrm{e}^{2B}]\leq 2w(r-R)\mathbb{E}[|B|]\leq\sigma^{2}w(r-R)^{2}

since
\mathbb{E}[|B|]=\mathbb{E}[-B]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}w(r-R)
. We deduce that

\mathbb{E}\left[I^{(r,R,\delta)}\mathrm{e}^{2(A+B)}\right]\leq 1-cw(r-K)+(2\sigma^{2}+4)w(r-R)^{2}.

(33)

By Lemma
11
and up to taking
a_{0}
smaller, for every
a\in(0,a_{0})
we have
\sup_{r\geq 2r_{0}}\sup_{r_{0}\leq R\leq\frac{a}{\sqrt{w(r)}}}\frac{w(r-R)^{2}}{w(r-K)}\to 0
as
r_{0}\to\infty
,
hence up to taking
r_{0}
larger if necessary the right-hand side of (
33
) is smaller than
1
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

3.6
The Feynman–Kac representation

Corollary 16
.

There exists a function
\underline{\delta}:[0,\infty)\to(0,\delta^{0})
decreasing with
\underline{\delta}(y)\to 0
as
y\to\infty
and a function
g:(0,1]\to[0,\infty)
increasing with
g(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0
such that the following holds.

For every
a\in(0,a_{0})
,
A>0
,
y>0
,
\mathcal{R}>0
, and
r\geq x\geq r_{0}
where
a_{0},r_{0}
is given by Lemma
15
, setting
\delta_{n}=\underline{\delta}(y)
for every
n
and writing

\begin{cases}\tau_{x}=\inf\{n\geq 0:S_{n}\leq x\}\\
\rho=\inf\{n\geq 0:S_{n}-R_{n}<y\text{ or }R_{n}<\mathcal{R}\}\\
T^{\prime}=\inf(\tau_{x},T_{a},\rho,A/w(x)\}\end{cases}

then

\left|w(r)-\mathbb{E}\left[W^{(r)}_{T^{\prime}}\right]\right|\leq\frac{100A}{w(x)}\left(\mathbb{P}(\Lambda>\mathcal{R})+w(y)^{2}g(w(y))\right).

(34)

Proof.

By Proposition
14
the process

Y_{n}:=W_{n}^{(r)}-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathfrak{W}^{(r)}_{k}\mathrm{Remainder}(S_{k},R_{k},\delta_{k}),

is such that
(Y_{n\wedge T})_{n\geq 0}
is a
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
-martingale. Apply the stopping theorem with the bounded stopping time
T^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle w(r)=\mathbb{E}\left[W^{(r)}_{0}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[W_{T^{\prime}}^{(r)}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{T^{\prime}-1}\mathfrak{W}^{(r)}_{k}\mathrm{Remainder}(S_{k},R_{k},\delta_{k})\right].

(35)

By Proposition
13

\frac{1}{90}|\mathrm{Remainder}(r,R,\delta)|\leq\mathbb{P}(\Lambda>R)+\mathbb{P}\left(\chi(\mathbb{R})>\frac{\delta}{w(r-R)}\right)+(\sigma^{2}+1)w(r-R)^{2}(w(r-R)+\delta),

thus on
\{k<T^{\prime}\}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{90}|\mathrm{Remainder}(S_{k},R_{k},\delta_{k})|\leq\mathbb{P}(\Lambda>\mathcal{R})+\mathbb{P}\left(\chi(\mathbb{R})>\frac{\underline{\delta}(y)}{w(y)}\right)+(\sigma^{2}+1)w(y)^{2}(w(y)+\underline{\delta}(y)).

We can then find a function
\underline{\delta}(y)\to 0
as
y\to\infty
decreasing and positive, together with a function
g(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0
increasing and positive, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\chi(\mathbb{R})>\frac{\underline{\delta}(y)}{w(y)}\right)+(\sigma^{2}+1)w(y)^{2}(w(y)+\underline{\delta}(y))\leq\frac{1}{100}w(y)^{2}g(w(y)).

Since by Lemma
15
the process
(\mathfrak{W}^{(r)}_{n\wedge T_{a}})_{n\geq 0}
is a supermartingale we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathfrak{W}^{(r)}_{n\wedge T^{\prime}}\right]\leq 1,

and given that
T^{\prime}\leq A/w(x)
we bound the sum in the right-hand side of (
35
) by
A/w(x)
times the upper bound for the remainder. This finishes the proof.
∎

3.7
The ratio limit

Now that we have established a Feynman–Kac representation for

(their Theorem 1).

Proof.

The fact that

We thus only need to check the continuity of

Fix

By Lemma

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{w\left(r^{\prime}_{k}+\frac{y_{2}}{\sqrt{w(r^{\prime}_{k})}}\right)}{w(r^{\prime}_{k})}\geq 1-\frac{2\sigma}{\eta}y_{2}.

We now prove the following equivalent of

4
Asymptotic of the probability distribution function of the supremum

We prove Theorem
3
in this section. The idea is to show that
g(r):=\mathbb{P}(\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}=r)
is sufficiently regular, namely that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{r\to\infty}\sup_{(1-\varepsilon)r\leq y\leq(1+\varepsilon)r}\left|\frac{g(y)}{g(r)}-1\right|=0.

We then use the fact that

w(r)\sim\sum_{y>r}g(y)
as
r\to\infty
, and thus that

\sum_{(1-\varepsilon)r<y\leq(1+\varepsilon)r}g(y)\sim w(r(1-\varepsilon))-w(r(1+\varepsilon))

to deduce Theorem
3
.

4.1
Probability that a random walk visits a point

Let
(U_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
with
U_{0}=0
a.s. and i.i.d. steps with
\mathbb{E}[U_{1}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[U_{1}^{2}]=\eta^{2}<\infty
. The random walk is recurrent, so that writing
\tau_{k}
for the
k
-th visit of
0
, we have
\tau_{1}<\infty
a.s. In particular,
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{k}\leq n/2)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Then for every fixed
y\in\mathbb{Z}
, since by the strong Markov property
\mathbb{P}(\exists\,\tau_{k}\leq u<\tau_{k+1}:U_{u}=y)=\mathbb{P}(\exists\,0\leq u<\tau_{1}:U_{u}=y)>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}(\exists\,0\leq u\leq n/2:U_{u}=y)\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1.

(41)

For every
y<0
, write
T_{y}=\inf\{n\geq 0,U_{n}\leq y\}
. Following
[
Lawler and Limic, 2010
, Section 5.1.1]
, by Theorem 5.1.7 therein, there exists a constant such that for every
n\geq 1

\mathbb{P}(T_{y}>n/2)\leq{\operatorname{const}\cdot}\frac{1-y}{\sqrt{n}},

and by Lemma 5.1.9, for every
m\geq 0

\mathbb{P}(U_{T_{y}}\leq y-m)\leq{\operatorname{const}\cdot}\mathbb{E}\left[U_{1}^{2}\mathds{1}_{\left\{|U_{1}|\geq m\right\}}\right]\underset{m\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, taking
m
large enough that the right-hand side is smaller than
\varepsilon/2
, and using (
41
) to choose
n_{\varepsilon}
such that for every
n\geq n_{\varepsilon}

\inf_{0\leq k\leq m}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,0\leq u\leq\frac{n}{2}:U_{u}=k\right)\geq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

we obtain that for every
y<0
and every
n\geq n_{\varepsilon}
, by the strong Markov property

\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\,0\leq u\leq n,U_{u}=y\right)\geq 1-\varepsilon-{\operatorname{const}\cdot}\frac{1-y}{\sqrt{n}}.

(42)

We easily see that the same holds for
y>0
, up to a change in the constants involved.

.
Since

\mathbb{P}(I_{k+1}=0\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{k})\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\Lambda>R\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\chi(\mathbb{R})>\frac{\delta}{w(S_{k}-R)}\right),

we can find
\delta=\delta(r)\to 0
as
r\to\infty
such that as
r\to\infty
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}\cap\left\{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}I_{k+1}=0\right\}\right)\underset{r\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

By the Markov property together with (
47
), if
T^{\prime\prime}(\omega):=\inf\{j:S_{j}<3r/4\}
and

\displaystyle W_{j}:=g(S_{j})\left(\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}I_{k+1}\right)\exp\left(-(4\ln 2)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int w(S_{k}-z)\mathrm{d}M_{S_{k}-S_{k+1}}^{(S_{k},R,\delta)}(z)\right)

then
(W_{j\wedge T^{\prime\prime}})_{j\geq 0}

is a positive
(\mathcal{F}_{j})_{j\geq 0}
-supermartingale. By the optional stopping theorem on the bounded stopping time
n\wedge T_{y}\wedge T^{\prime\prime}
, and noting that
\{T^{\prime\prime}>n\}\supset\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle g(r)\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathcal{A}\right\}}g(S_{n\wedge T_{y}})\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n\wedge T_{y}-1}I_{k+1}\right)\exp\left(-(4\ln 2)\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge T_{y}-1}\int w(S_{k}-z)\mathrm{d}M_{S_{k}-S_{k+1}}^{(S_{k},R,\delta)}(z)\right)\right].

(48)

Clearly
g(S_{T_{y}})=g(y)
. We aim to bound the other factors in (
48
). By Doob’s maximal inequality,

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}^{c})=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq j\leq n}-(S_{j}-r)>r/4\right)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[(S_{n}-r)^{2}]}{(r/4)^{2}}\leq 16\eta^{2}\frac{c^{2}\nu^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\underset{\nu\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0.

On the other hand, recalling
d_{r}
from the proof of Proposition
18
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathcal{A}\right\}}I_{k+1}\int w(S_{k}-z)\mathrm{d}M_{S_{k}-S_{k+1}}^{(S_{k},R,\delta)}(z)\ |\ \mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq w(r/2)\sigma^{2}(1+d_{r/2})

hence, assuming that
r
is large enough that
d_{r/2}\leq 1
and
w(r/2)\leq Cr^{-2}/2
for some
C<\infty

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathcal{A}\right\}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}I_{k+1}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int w(S_{k}-z)\mathrm{d}M_{S_{k}-S_{k+1}}^{(S_{k},R,\delta)}(z)\right]\leq nw(r/2)\sigma^{2}(1+d_{r/2})\leq C\frac{\sigma^{2}c^{2}\nu^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}.

By Markov’s inequality, writing

\mathcal{E}:=\left\{\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathcal{A}\right\}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}I_{k+1}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int w(S_{k}-y)\mathrm{d}M_{S_{k}-S_{k+1}}^{(S_{k},R,\delta)}(y)\ >\ C\frac{\sigma^{2}c^{2}\nu^{2}}{\varepsilon^{3}}\right\}

we have
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\leq\varepsilon
for every
r
large enough, and outside of
\mathcal{E}
, for every
0\leq j\leq n

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathcal{A}\right\}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}I_{k+1}\right)\exp\left(-(4\ln 2)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int w(S_{k}-y)\mathrm{d}M_{S_{k}-S_{k+1}}^{(S_{k},R,\delta)}(y)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathds{1}_{\left\{\mathcal{A}\right\}}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}I_{k+1}\right)\exp\left(-(4\ln 2)C\frac{c^{2}\nu^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\varepsilon^{3}}\right).

Take
\nu>0
small enough and
r
large enough that

\exp\left(-(4\ln 2)C\frac{c^{2}\nu^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\varepsilon^{3}}\right)\geq 1-\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}\cap\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{n}I_{j}=0\right\}\right)\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}^{c})\leq\varepsilon,

and we get that on the event
\mathcal{E}^{c}\cap\mathcal{A}\cap\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{n}I_{j}=0\right\}\cap\{T_{y}\leq n\}
of probability at least
1-5\varepsilon
, the content of the expectation in (
48
) is larger than
g(y)(1-\varepsilon)
, hence

g(r)\geq(1-6\varepsilon)g(y).

It follows that for every
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we can find
\nu>0
with
\nu\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0
such that for every
y
with
|y-r|\leq\frac{\nu r}{2}
, we have for
r
large enough

(1-6\varepsilon)g(r)\leq g(y)\leq(1+7\varepsilon)g(r).

This means

(1-6\varepsilon)rg(r)\leq\frac{1}{\nu}\sum_{y=(1-\nu/2)r}^{(1+\nu/2)r}g(y)\leq(1+7\varepsilon)rg(r),

and since
\sum_{y>r}g(y)=1-h(r)=\frac{w(r)}{1+w(r)}
,

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{r^{2}}{\nu}\sum_{y=(1-\nu/2)r}^{(1+\nu/2)r}g(y)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{r^{2}}{\nu}(w((1-\nu/2)r)-w((1+\nu/2)r))=\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}\nu}\left(\left(1-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)^{-2}-\left(1+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)^{-2}\right),

with the limit converging to
\frac{12\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}
as
\nu\to 0
(thus also as
\varepsilon\to 0
),
we find that

\limsup_{r\to\infty}r^{3}\left|g(r)-\frac{12\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}r^{3}}\right|\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0.

Since the left-hand side does not depend on
\varepsilon
, this concludes the proof of Theorem
3
.

5.1
Laplace transform of the volume

We control the Laplace transform of the weight of
T
conditionally on
\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}
by considering for every
t\geq 0

h_{t}(r)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-t\sum_{v\in T}D_{v}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}\leq r\right\}}\right]\quad,\quad h_{t}(\infty)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-t\sum_{v\in T}D_{v}}\right].

The function
h_{t}
is increasing and right-continuous, and under Assumptions
1
we have
h_{t}(0)<1
,
h_{t}(r)=0
for every
r<0
, and
h_{t}(r)\to h_{t}(\infty)
as
r\to\infty
. By the Markov property we have

h_{t}(r)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\prod_{i=1}^{\chi(\mathbb{R})}h_{t}(r-X_{i})\mathds{1}_{\left\{\Lambda\leq r\right\}}\right].

(49)

When
r=\infty
, and writing
\Phi_{t}(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}x^{\chi(\mathbb{R})}\right]
, this becomes

\displaystyle h_{t}(\infty)=\Phi_{t}(h_{t}(\infty)).

(50)

For every
t\geq 0
, the function
\Phi_{t}
is convex,
\Phi_{t}(0)>0
and
\Phi_{t}(1)\leq 1
, so
h_{t}(\infty)
is the unique solution of the equation
x=\Phi_{t}(x)
in
[0,1]
. Let us obtain its asymptotic.

Lemma 19
.

For every
p\in(1,\infty)
and every random variable
W\in L^{p}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[W\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right)\right]=o\left(t^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\right).

For every
t\geq 0
define

f(t):=\mathbb{E}\left[\chi(\mathbb{R})\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right]-1.

Then
f(t)=o(t^{1/2})
,

\Phi_{t}(x)=x+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}-1\right]+(x-1)f(t)+\frac{(x-1)^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[(\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}-\chi(\mathbb{R}))\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right]+o((x-1)^{2})

(51)

with a
o((x-1)^{2})
that is uniform over all
t\geq 0
, and as
t\to 0
with
t\geq 0

1-h_{t}(\infty)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\sigma^{2}}}\ .

(52)

Remark 3
.

More generally, if
\mu_{q}(t)=\mathbb{E}[(1-\mathrm{e}^{-tD})^{q}]
then the Lemma holds with
1-h_{t}(\infty)\sim\sqrt{2\mu_{1}(t)/\sigma^{2}}
as soon as
\mu_{2}(t)=o(\mu_{1}(t))
. This is the case if
\mathbb{E}[D]<\infty
, and there are cases where
\mathbb{E}[D]=\infty
where this does not hold—typically when the tail of
D
is not regular enough.

Proof.

Using Hölder’s inequality, letting
q=\frac{p}{p-1}

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[W\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right)\right]\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\left[|W|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right)^{q}\right]^{1/q}.

Split the second expectation on
\{tD>\varepsilon\}
and
\{tD\leq\varepsilon\}
, and use
|\mathrm{e}^{-x}-1|\leq x
for
x\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right)^{q}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right)^{q}\mathds{1}_{\left\{tD\leq\varepsilon\right\}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right)^{q}\mathds{1}_{\left\{tD>\varepsilon\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{q-1}\mathbb{E}[tD]+\mathbb{P}\left(D>\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{q-1}t+\frac{2t}{\varepsilon\psi_{D}(\varepsilon/t)}

with
\psi_{D}
given by Lemma
31
: we can take
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(t)
that goes to zero sufficiently slowly that the above expression is
o(t)
, proving our claim. The bound on
f
follows from it with
p=2
and
W=\chi(\mathbb{R})
. Next is the Taylor expansion of
\Phi_{t}(x)
near
x=1
(with
x\leq 1
): for every
\varepsilon>0

\left|\Phi_{t}(x)-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\left(1+\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x+\frac{\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}}{2}(\ln x)^{2}\right)\right]\right|\\
\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathrm{e}^{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x}-1-\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x-\frac{\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}}{2}(\ln x)^{2}\right|\right].

Split it on
\{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x<-\varepsilon\}
and
\{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x\geq-\varepsilon\}
, using
|\mathrm{e}^{-u}-1-u-u^{2}/2|\leq\omega(u)u^{2}
for
u\leq 0
with
\omega
increasing and
\omega(u)\to 0
as
u\to 0
:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathrm{e}^{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x}-1-\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x-\frac{\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}}{2}(\ln x)^{2}\right|\mathds{1}_{\left\{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x>-\varepsilon\right\}}\right]\leq\omega(\varepsilon)(\ln x)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}\right].

(53)

On the other hand, using first
|\mathrm{e}^{-x}-1|\leq 1
, then Lemma
31
to obtain
\psi_{\chi}
(increasing to
+\infty
) and
C:=\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}\psi_{\chi}(\chi(\mathbb{R}))]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathrm{e}^{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x}-1-\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x-\frac{\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}}{2}(\ln x)^{2}\right|\mathds{1}_{\left\{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x\leq-\varepsilon\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\chi(\mathbb{R})\leq\frac{-\varepsilon}{\ln x}\right)-(\ln x)\mathbb{E}\left[\chi(\mathbb{R})\mathds{1}_{\left\{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x\leq-\varepsilon\right\}}\right]+\frac{(\ln x)^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x\leq-\varepsilon\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}\psi_{\chi}(-\varepsilon/\ln x)}(\ln x)^{2}+\frac{(\ln x)^{2}}{\varepsilon\psi_{\chi}(-\varepsilon/\ln x)}\mathbb{E}\left[\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}\psi_{\chi}(\chi(\mathbb{R}))\mathds{1}_{\left\{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x\leq-\varepsilon\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(\ln x)^{2}}{2\psi_{\chi}(-\varepsilon/\ln x)}\mathbb{E}\left[\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}\psi_{\chi}(\chi(\mathbb{R}))\mathds{1}_{\left\{\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x\leq-\varepsilon\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{-2}+\varepsilon^{-1}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{(\ln x)^{2}}{\psi_{\chi}(-\varepsilon/\ln x)}.

(54)

We can find
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(x)
that goes to
0
sufficiently slowly as
x\to 1
such that the right-hand side of (
53
) and (
54
) are both
o((x-1)^{2})
, uniformly in
t\geq 0
. To prove (
51
) we need to control

\displaystyle\Bigg|\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\left(1+\chi(\mathbb{R})\ln x+\frac{\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}}{2}(\ln x)^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-x-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}-1\right]-(x-1)\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\chi(\mathbb{R})\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right]-1\right)-\frac{(x-1)^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\left(\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}-\chi(\mathbb{R})\right)\right]\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\chi(\mathbb{R})\left|\ln x-(x-1)+\frac{(x-1)^{2}}{2}\right|+\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}\left|\ln(x)^{2}-(x-1)^{2}\right|\right]=o((x-1)^{2})

uniformly in
t\geq 0
.

Let us now prove (
52
) from the previous estimates.
Since
\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(x)=\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})\mathrm{e}^{-tD}x^{\chi(\mathbb{R})}]
we have
0\leq\Phi_{t}^{\prime}\leq 1
on

; in addition

as

, so that

as

. Hence using (

in the rest of the proof

\displaystyle 1-x=1-x+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}-1\right]-xf(t)+\frac{\sigma^{2}+o(1)}{2}x^{2}.

Using that
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}-1]\sim t
as
t\to 0
with
t\geq 0
, the solution must be

\displaystyle 1-h_{t}(\infty)=\frac{-f(t)+\sqrt{f(t)^{2}+2\sigma^{2}t+o(t)}}{\sigma^{2}+o(1)}\sim\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\sigma^{2}}}\ .

This finishes the proof of Lemma

.
∎

Finally, we can control the speed of convergence of certain expectations.

Lemma 20
.

Let
W\in L^{2}
, then as
t\to 0

\mathbb{E}[W]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}h_{t}(\infty)^{\chi(\mathbb{R})-1}W\right]=\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\sigma^{2}}}\ \mathbb{E}[W(\chi(\mathbb{R})-1)]+o(t^{1/2}).

(55)

Proof.

Write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[W]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}h_{t}(\infty)^{\chi(\mathbb{R})-1}W\right]-(1-h_{t}(\infty))\mathbb{E}[W(\chi(\mathbb{R})-1)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[W\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-tD}h_{t}(\infty)^{\chi(\mathbb{R})-1}-(1-h_{t}(\infty))(\chi(\mathbb{R})-1)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[W\left(1+(h_{t}(\infty)-1)(\chi(\mathbb{R})-1)-h_{t}(\infty)^{\chi(\mathbb{R})-1}+h_{t}(\infty)^{\chi(\mathbb{R})-1}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right)\right)\right].

Then the absolute value of the left-hand side is smaller than

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|W|\left|1+(h_{t}(\infty)-1)(\chi(\mathbb{R})-1)-h_{t}(\infty)^{\chi(\mathbb{R})-1}\right|\right]+h_{t}(\infty)^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[|W|\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-tD}\right)\right].

We use Lemma
19
and the fact that
h_{t}(\infty)\to 1
to bound the second term by
o(t^{1/2})
as
t\to 0
. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the same kind of control as in the proof of Lemma

can be used to bound the first term by
o(1-h_{t}(\infty))=o(t^{1/2})
. We conclude by using the asymptotic of

from Lemma

.
∎

5.2
Preliminary bounds on the tail

Let us obtain two easy bounds:

h_{t}(r)=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-t\sum_{v\in T}D_{v}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}\leq r\right\}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}\leq r\right\}}\right]=\mathbb{P}(\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}\leq r)=h(r)

where
h(r)
is the same as in Section
3.1
. On the other hand, since
1-ab\leq(1-a)+(1-b)
for
a,b\in[0,1]
,

1-h_{t}(r)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[1-\mathrm{e}^{-t\sum_{v\in T}D_{v}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[1-\mathds{1}_{\left\{\sup_{v\in T}\Lambda_{v}\leq r\right\}}\right]=2-h_{t}(\infty)-h(r).

To sum up,

h(r)-(1-h_{t}(\infty))\leq h_{t}(r)\leq h(r).

(56)

Define
w_{t}(r)=\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{t}(r)}-1
; this is well-defined for every
r\geq 0
since
h_{t}(0)>0
, and it decreases to
0
as
r\to\infty
.
Let us compute bounds on
w_{t}
from (
56
):

w_{t}(r)=\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{t}(r)}-1\geq\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h(r)}-1=h_{t}(\infty)w(r)-(1-h_{t}(\infty))

(57)

and

w_{t}(r)\leq\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h(r)-(1-h_{t}(\infty))}-1=\frac{1-h(r)}{h(r)-(1-h_{t}(\infty))}\leq\frac{w(r)}{h(r)-(1-h_{t}(\infty))}.

(58)

For every
\alpha\geq 0
and
r>0
define

t(r,\alpha):=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\left(\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}r^{2}}\right)^{2}\ .

(59)

Since
w(r)\sim\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}r^{2}}
as
r\to\infty
and
1-h_{t}(\infty)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\sigma^{2}}}
as
t\to\infty
, for every
\alpha\geq 0
we find from (
58
)

\limsup_{r\to\infty}r^{2}w_{t(r,\alpha)}(r)\leq\limsup_{r\to\infty}r^{2}w(r)\leq\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}

and from (
57
)

\liminf_{r\to\infty}r^{2}w_{t(r,\alpha)}(r)\geq-\alpha\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}+\liminf_{r\to\infty}r^{2}w(r)\geq(1-\alpha)\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}.

(60)

Note that this immediately shows
w_{t(r,\alpha)}(r)\to 0
as
r\to\infty
for every
\alpha\geq 0
.
A stronger version of these bounds is found in Lemma
23
. We can strengthen (
57
) and (
58
):
let
0\leq s\leq t
; by the same argument as around (

),

h_{s}(r)+h_{t}(\infty)-h_{s}(\infty)\leq h_{t}(r)\leq h_{s}(r),

thus on one hand

w_{t}(r)=\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{t}(r)}-1\geq\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{s}(r)}-1=\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{s}(\infty)}w_{s}(r)+\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{s}(\infty)}-1

(61)

and on the other hand

w_{t}(r)\leq\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{s}(r)+h_{t}(\infty)-h_{s}(\infty)}-1=\frac{h_{s}(\infty)-h_{s}(r)}{h_{s}(r)+h_{t}(\infty)-h_{s}(\infty)}=\frac{1-\frac{h_{s}(r)}{h_{s}(\infty)}}{\frac{h_{s}(r)}{h_{s}(\infty)}+\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{s}(\infty)}-1}\\
=\frac{w_{s}(r)}{-w_{s}(r)+\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{s}(\infty)}(1+w_{s}(r))}=\frac{w_{s}(r)}{\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{s}(\infty)}+w_{s}(r)\left(\frac{h_{t}(\infty)}{h_{s}(\infty)}-1\right)}.

(62)

Remark 4
.

A significant complexity is introduced by the need to control the asymptotic as
r\to\infty
for every fixed

, as well as for

and

jointly. Here is what we expect: as

and

, if

then

; on the other hand if

then

. Only in the regime where

is of order

, for example when

, will we see a limit in

.

A control of the regularity of

in

will be useful.

Lemma 21
.

For every
\alpha>0
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
r_{\varepsilon}
and
f_{\varepsilon}(r)\to 6\eta^{2}\varepsilon/\sigma^{2}
as

such that for every

,

\sup_{\gamma,\beta\in[0,\alpha]:|\beta-\gamma|\leq\varepsilon}r^{2}\left|w_{t(r,\beta)}(r)-w_{t(r,\gamma)}(r)\right|\leq f_{\varepsilon}(r).

Proof.

Since
1-h_{t}(\infty)\sim_{t\to 0}\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\sigma^{2}}}
, which implies
1-h_{t(r,\alpha)}(\infty)\sim_{r\to\infty}\alpha\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}r^{2}}
, and since
h_{t(r,\alpha)}(\infty)
is decreasing in
\alpha
, we have that for every
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and
\alpha>0
, for every

\limsup_{r\to\infty}r^{2}\left(1-\frac{h_{t(r,\beta+\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime})}(\infty)}{h_{t(r,\beta)}(\infty)}\right)\leq\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime}).

Taking the supremum over the finitely many
\beta\in[0,\alpha]\cap\varepsilon^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}
and by monotonicity of
\beta\mapsto h_{t(r,\beta)}(\infty)
, then taking
\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0
, we find
g_{\varepsilon}(r)\to 6\eta^{2}/\sigma^{2}
as
r\to\infty
such that

\sup_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}r^{2}\left(1-\frac{h_{t(r,\beta+\varepsilon)}(\infty)}{h_{t(r,\beta)}(\infty)}\right)\leq\varepsilon g_{\varepsilon}(r).

Fix
\alpha>0
.
By (
58
), there exists
f_{1}(x)
(that depends on
\alpha
) that converge to
6\eta^{2}/\sigma^{2}
as
x\to\infty
with
f_{1}(x)\geq 6\eta^{2}/\sigma^{2}
such that
r^{2}w_{t(r,\beta)}(r)\leq\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}f_{1}(r)
for every
\beta\in[0,\alpha]
.
By (
61
), for every
\beta\in[0,\alpha]

\displaystyle r^{2}w_{t(r,\beta+\varepsilon)}(r)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\varepsilon g_{\varepsilon}(r)\right)r^{2}w_{t(r,\beta)}(r)-\varepsilon g_{\varepsilon}(r)

\displaystyle\geq r^{2}w_{t(r,\beta)}(r)-\varepsilon g_{\varepsilon}(r)\left(1+\frac{f_{1}(r)}{r^{2}}\right).

On the other hand, by (
62
), taking
\varepsilon>0
small enough, then
r
large enough that
r^{-2}\varepsilon g_{\varepsilon}(r)\left(1+\frac{f_{1}(r)}{r^{2}}\right)\leq 1/2
, using that
|(1-x)^{-1}-1|\leq 2x
for
|x|\leq 1/2
,

\displaystyle w_{t(r,\beta+\varepsilon)}(r)

\displaystyle\leq w_{t(r,\beta)}(r)\left(1-r^{-2}\varepsilon g_{\varepsilon}(r)\left(1+\frac{f_{1}(r)}{r^{2}}\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq w_{t(r,\beta)}(r)+2w_{t(r,\beta)}r^{-2}\varepsilon g_{\varepsilon}(r)\left(1+\frac{f_{1}(r)}{r^{2}}\right).

The Lemma follows by taking

f_{\varepsilon}(r)=\max\left(2f_{1}(r)r^{-2}\varepsilon g_{\varepsilon}(r)\left(1+\frac{f_{1}(r)}{r^{2}}\right),\varepsilon g_{\varepsilon}(r)\left(1+\frac{f_{1}(r)}{r^{2}}\right)\right),

and we can check that indeed
f_{\varepsilon}(r)\to 6\eta^{2}\varepsilon/\sigma^{2}
as
r\to\infty
.
∎

5.3
Change of measure to subcritical

Recall the definition of a
brw
from Section
2.1
.
Define a family
(\mathbb{E}^{(t)})_{t\geq 0}
of expectations on the same probability space as
\mathbb{E}
such that
(k_{u},\chi_{u},D_{u},\Lambda^{(u)},(X^{(u)}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\chi_{u}(\mathbb{R})})_{u\in\mathcal{U}}
are i.i.d. with the distribution of
(k,\chi,D,\Lambda,(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\chi(\mathbb{R})})
such that for every nonnegative and measurable
f

\mathbb{E}^{(t)}\left[f\left(k,\chi,D,\Lambda,(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\chi(\mathbb{R})}\right)\right]\\
=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-tD}h_{t}(\infty)^{\chi(\mathbb{R})-1}f\left(k,\chi,D,\Lambda,(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\chi(\mathbb{R})}\right)\right].

(63)

We can then check that for every nonnegative and measurable
f

\mathbb{E}^{(t)}\left[f\left(T,(X_{v})_{v\in T},(D_{v})_{v\in T},(\Lambda_{v})_{v\in T}\right)\right]=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-t\sum_{v\in T}D_{v}}f\left(T,(X_{v})_{v\in T},(D_{v})_{v\in T},(\Lambda_{v})_{v\in T}\right)\right]}{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-t\sum_{v\in T}D_{v}}\right]}\ ,

(64)

noting that
h_{t}(\infty)=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{-t\sum_{v\in T}D_{v}}]
.
Differentiating
\Phi_{t}
gives

\mathbb{E}^{(t)}\left[\chi(\mathbb{R})\right]=\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(h_{t}(\infty))\leq 1

(65)

with a strict inequality when
t>0
.
In other words, the
brw
becomes subcritical under
\mathbb{E}^{(t)}
. We can be more precise: by Lemma
20

1-\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]\sim\sqrt{2t\sigma^{2}}.

(66)

Because
\mathrm{e}^{-tD}h_{t}(\infty)^{\chi(\mathbb{R})-1}\leq h_{t}(\infty)^{-1}
a.s. we have by the dominated convergence theorem
\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[W]\to\mathbb{E}[W]
as
t\to 0
as soon as
W
is integrable, in particular

\mathbb{E}^{(t)}\left[\chi(\mathbb{R})(\chi(\mathbb{R})-1)\right]\underset{t\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\sigma^{2},

(67)

and there exists
t_{0}>0
such that for every
t\in[0,t_{0}]
and every
W\geq 0
integrable

\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[W]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[W].

(68)

We can rewrite (
49
) as an equation on

\tilde{h}_{t}(r):=\mathbb{P}^{(t)}\left(\sup_{n}\sup G_{n}\leq r\right)=\frac{h_{t}(r)}{h_{t}(\infty)}=\frac{1}{1+w_{t}(r)}\ ,

namely

\tilde{h}_{t}(r)=\mathbb{E}^{(t)}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{\chi(\mathbb{R})}\tilde{h}_{t}(r-X_{i})\mathds{1}_{\left\{\Lambda\leq r\right\}}\right]=\mathbb{E}^{(t)}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{\Lambda\leq r\right\}}\exp\left(\int\ln\tilde{h}_{t}(r-x)\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right)\right].

(69)

This interpretation allows us to draw an analogue with our work in Section
3
, except that now the
brw
is subcritical.

5.4
On coupling the random walks

For every
t\geq 0
, there exists a random variable
Z_{t}
such that for every positive and measurable
f

\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]\cdot\mathbb{E}[f(Z_{t})]=\mathbb{E}^{(t)}\left[\int f(x)\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right].

(70)

If
t=0
then
Z_{0}=Z
in distribution, where
Z
is from Section
3
.
Since
\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]\to 1
, there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
for every positive and measurable
f
and every
t\in[0,t_{0}]

\mathbb{E}[f(Z_{t})]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[f(Z_{0})].

In fact, if
\mathbb{E}[f(Z_{0})]<\infty
then
\mathbb{E}[f(Z_{t})]\to\mathbb{E}[f(Z_{0})]
as
t\to 0
by dominated convergence; in particular,
\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}^{2}]\to\eta^{2}
and
\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]\to 0
as
t\to 0
.

Let us construct these random variables on the same probability space in a way that
\mathbb{P}(Z_{t}=Z_{0})
converges to
1
as
t\to 0
.
For every
t\geq 0
let

V_{t}:=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-tD}h_{t}(\infty)^{\chi(\mathbb{R})-1}}{\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]}.

Clearly
\mathbb{E}[V_{t}\chi(\mathbb{R})]=1
.
Let
U
be uniform on
[0,1]
and independent from the reproduction scheme, in particular of
(D,\chi)
,
and for every
t\geq 0
let
J_{t}
be a random variable such that for every nonnegative and measurable
f

\mathbb{E}[f(Z,J_{t})]=\mathbb{E}\left[\int f\left(x,\mathds{1}_{\left\{U<V_{t}\right\}}\right)\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right].

Finally, let
\tilde{Z_{t}}
independent from
(Z,J_{t})
such that for every nonnegative and measurable
f

\mathbb{E}[f(\tilde{Z}_{t})]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(V_{t}-1)\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}\geq 1\right\}}}{\mathbb{E}[(V_{t}-1)\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}\geq 1\right\}}\chi(\mathbb{R})]}\int f(x)\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right].

Finally, define

Z_{t}:=Z\mathds{1}_{\left\{J_{t}=1\right\}}+\tilde{Z}_{t}\mathds{1}_{\left\{J_{t}=0\right\}}

(71)

Lemma 22
.

The random variables
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
from (
71
) satisfy (
70
), and in addition

\limsup_{t\to 0}\ (2\sigma^{2}t)^{-1/2}\mathbb{P}(Z_{t}\neq Z)\leq 1.

(72)

and

\mathbb{E}[(Z_{t}-Z_{0})^{2}]\underset{t\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0.

(73)

Proof.

Clearly, outside of the event
J_{t}=0
we have
Z_{t}=Z
. This event has probability

\mathbb{P}(J_{t}=0)=\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})\mathds{1}_{\left\{U>V_{t}\right\}}]=\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})(1-V_{t})\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}<1\right\}}]=\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}<1\right\}}]-\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})V_{t}\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}<1\right\}}].

(74)

Using that
\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]=\mathbb{E}[V_{t}\chi(\mathbb{R})]=1
, we can rewrite this as

\mathbb{P}(J_{t}=0)=\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})V_{t}\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}\geq 1\right\}}]-\mathbb{E}[\chi(R)\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}\geq 1\right\}}]=\mathbb{E}\left[(V_{t}-1)\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}\geq 1\right\}}\chi(\mathbb{R})\right].

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(Z_{t})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\left\{J_{t}=1\right\}}f(Z)]+\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\left\{J_{t}=0\right\}}f(\tilde{Z}_{t})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{U<V_{t}\right\}}\int f(x)\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right]+\mathbb{P}(J_{t}=0)\mathbb{E}\left[f(\tilde{Z}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\min(1,V_{t})\int f(x)\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[(V_{t}-1)\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}\geq 1\right\}}\int f(x)\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[V_{t}\int f(x)\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right],

thus proving (
70
). On the other hand, by Lemma
20
and from
\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]\to 1
as
t\to 0

\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}<1\right\}}]-\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})V_{t}\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}<1\right\}}]=\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\sigma^{2}}}\mathbb{E}[(\chi(\mathbb{R})-1)\chi(\mathbb{R})\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}<1\right\}}]+o(t^{1/2}).

Plugging this into (
74
),

\mathbb{P}(Z_{t}\neq Z)\leq\mathbb{P}(J_{t}=0)\leq\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}<1\right\}}]-\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]\mathbb{E}[\chi(\mathbb{R})V_{t}\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}<1\right\}}]

is such that

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2t\sigma^{2}}}\mathbb{P}(Z_{t}\neq Z)\leq\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\mathbb{E}[(\chi(\mathbb{R})-1)\chi(\mathbb{R})\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}<1\right\}}]\leq 1.

Finally, from
(a-b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(Z_{t}-Z)^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{J_{t}=0\right\}}(\tilde{Z}_{t}-Z)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{P}(Z_{t}=0)\mathbb{E}\left[(\tilde{Z}_{t})^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\left\{J_{t}=0\right\}}Z^{2}]

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\left[(V_{t}-1)\mathds{1}_{\left\{V_{t}\geq 1\right\}}\int x^{2}\mathrm{d}\chi(x)\right]+2\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\left\{J_{t}=0\right\}}Z^{2}]

and both terms converge to
0
as
t\to 0
by dominated convergence.
∎

Let
(Z_{n}^{t})_{n\geq 1,t\geq 0}
be i.i.d. copies of
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and define for every
t\geq 0
and every
n\geq 0

U_{n}^{t}=Z_{1}^{t}+\dots+Z_{n}^{t}

(75)

where
U_{0}^{t}=0
.
The many-to-one formula takes the following form: for every measurable and bounded
g
, for every
n

\mathbb{E}^{(t)}\left[\int g\mathrm{d}G_{n}\right]=\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]^{n}\cdot\mathbb{E}[g(U_{n}^{t})].

(76)

A first consequence of the many-to-one formula is the following.

Lemma 23
.

For every
\alpha\geq 0
,

0<c(\alpha):=\liminf_{r\to\infty}r^{2}\inf_{t\in[0,t(r,\alpha)]}w_{t}(r)\leq\limsup_{r\to\infty}r^{2}\sup_{t\in[0,t(r,\alpha)]}w_{t}(r)\leq\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}.

Proof.

The upper bound follows from (
58
).
We prove the lower bound by the second moment method. By the many-to-one formula (
76
),

\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[G_{n}([r,\infty))]=\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]^{n}\mathbb{P}(U^{t}_{n}\geq r).

The second moment is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[G_{n}(\mathbb{R})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{(t)}\left[\left(\sum_{x\in G_{n-1}}\chi_{x}(\mathbb{R})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[G_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})]\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})^{2}]+\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[G_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})(G_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})-1)]\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[G_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})^{2}]\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]^{2}+\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[G_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})]\Var^{(t)}(\chi(\mathbb{R}))

so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[G_{n}(\mathbb{R})^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]^{2n}+n\Var^{(t)}(\chi(\mathbb{R}))\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]^{n-1}

(77)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]^{n-1}\left(1+n\Var^{(t)}(\chi(\mathbb{R}))\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2n\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]^{n}\sigma^{2}

as soon as
t,n
and
r
are large enough. Taking
n=r^{2}/\eta^{2}
, by the Paley–Zygmund inequality we get

\frac{r^{2}w_{t}(r)}{1+w_{t}(r)}=r^{2}\mathbb{P}^{(t)}(G_{r^{2}}([r,\infty))>0)\geq\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]^{r^{2}}\frac{\eta^{2}\mathbb{P}(U_{r^{2}}^{t}\geq r)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}.

Using (
73
) we can check that if we take
t_{0}>0
small enough then
\liminf_{r\to\infty}\inf_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}\mathbb{P}(U_{r^{2}/\eta^{2}}^{t}\geq r)>\mathbb{P}(N\geq 2)
, where
N\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.
Using that
\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]=1-\sqrt{2t\sigma^{2}}+o(t)\geq\exp(-2\sqrt{t\sigma^{2}})
for every
t\in[0,t_{0}]
(taking
t_{0}
smaller if needed), we finally find

\liminf_{r\to\infty}r^{2}\inf_{t\in[0,t(r,\alpha)]}w_{t}(r)\geq\frac{\mathbb{P}(N\geq 2)^{2}}{2}\frac{\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\alpha\frac{6\sqrt{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right).

∎

Corollary 24
.

For every
\alpha>0
and
b\in(0,1)
,

c(\alpha)\leq\liminf_{r\to\infty}\inf_{br\leq x\leq r}x^{2}w_{t(r,\alpha)}(x)\leq\limsup_{r\to\infty}\sup_{br\leq x\leq r}x^{2}w_{t(r,\alpha)}(x)\leq\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}.

More generally,

c(\alpha)\leq\liminf_{r\to\infty}\inf_{s\in[0,t(r,\alpha)]}\inf_{br\leq x\leq r}x^{2}w_{s}(x)\leq\limsup_{r\to\infty}\sup_{s\in[0,t(r,\alpha)]}\sup_{br\leq x\leq r}x^{2}w_{s}(x)\leq\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}.

Proof.

For the second part, we use the correspondence
t(r,\alpha)=t(br,b^{2}\alpha)
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and assume that
r_{\bullet}
is such that for every
r\geq r_{\bullet}

(1-\varepsilon)c(\alpha)\leq\inf_{0\leq t\leq t(r,\alpha)}r^{2}w_{t}(r)\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq t(r,\alpha)}r^{2}w_{t}(r)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}.

Then for every
r\geq r_{\bullet}/b
and every
q\in[b,1]
, because
q^{2}\alpha\leq\alpha
,

(1-\varepsilon)c(\alpha)\leq(qr)^{2}w_{t(qr,q^{2}\alpha)}(qr)=(qr)^{2}w_{t(r,\alpha)}(qr)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}.

This concludes the proof of the first equation. The second follows from the first and (
62
) (for the lower bound) and (
58
) (for the upper bound).
∎

5.5
Continuity of the tail

In this section, we gathered technical estimates which play the role of Lemma

Lemma 25

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[X^{2}]\leq(\ln\tilde{h}_{t}(r))^{2}\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[G_{n}(\mathbb{R})^{2}]\leq(\ln\tilde{h}_{t}(r))^{2}\mathbb{E}^{(t)}[\chi(\mathbb{R})]^{n}(1+2n\sigma^{2}),

6.1
Improvement on Markov’s inequality

In order to get optimal moment assumptions, we need to refine Markov’s inequality:

\mathbb{P}(X\geq t)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{t}

when
X\geq 0
a.s.,
\mathbb{E}[X]<\infty
and
t>0
. A motivation for that comes from the layer-cake formula:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(X\geq t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\mathbb{E}[X]<\infty

so that Markov’s inequality, which gives a non-integrable tail estimate, fails to catch the actual tail behavior. We make extensive use of the following refinement of Markov’s inequality.

Lemma 31
.

Let
X\geq 0
be a random variable in
L^{p}
with
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then we can find
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
, increasing, with
\psi(x)\to+\infty
as
x\to\infty
, such that
\mathbb{E}[X^{p}\psi(X)]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[X^{p}]<\infty
. It follows that
\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq t)\leq\frac{2\mathbb{E}[X^{p}]}{t^{p}\psi(t)}=o(t^{-p})
as
t\to\infty
.

The function
\psi
obviously depends on the distribution of
X
.

Proof.

By the layer-cake formula,

\mathbb{E}[X^{p}]=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mathbb{P}(Y\geq t)\mathrm{d}t.

Write
t_{k}:=\inf\{t:\int_{t}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mathbb{P}(X\geq t)\mathrm{d}t\leq 2^{-2k}\mathbb{E}[X^{p}]\}
, and let
\psi(t)=1+2^{k-1}
for every
t_{k}\leq t<t_{k+1}
. Then

\mathbb{E}[X^{p}\psi(X)]=\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{E}[X^{p}]+\sum_{k\geq 1}2^{k-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\mathbb{P}(X\geq t)\mathrm{d}t\leq\left(\frac{3}{2}+\sum_{k\geq 1}2^{k-1}2^{-2k}\right)\mathbb{E}[X^{p}]\leq 2\mathbb{E}[X^{p}].

∎

Note first that the reproduction scheme is non-degenerate: indeed

\mathbb{E}[N_{F}]=\frac{Z_{\textbf{q}}^{-1}}{1-Z_{\textbf{q}}^{-1}}\quad,\quad\Var(N_{F})\leq\frac{Z_{\textbf{q}}}{1-Z_{\textbf{q}}^{-1}}f_{\textbf{q}}^{\prime\prime}(Z_{q}).

Modular invariant gluon-graviton scattering in AdS at one loop


Abstract
We consider mixed gluon-graviton scattering in Type IIB string theory on AdS 5 × S 5 / ℤ 2 {}_{5}\times S^{5}/\mathbb{Z}_{2} in the presence of D7 branes, which is dual to a mixed correlator of the S ​ O ​ ( 8 ) SO(8) and S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L SU(2)_{L} flavor multiplets of a certain 4d 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 U ​ S ​ p ​ ( 2 ​ N ) USp(2N) gauge theory with complexified coupling τ \tau . We compute this holographic correlator in the large N N and finite τ \tau expansion using constraints from derivatives of the mass deformed sphere free energy, which we compute using supersymmetric localization at large N N and finite τ \tau in terms of modular invariant non-holomorphic Eisenstein series. In particular, we combine this constraint with the known flat space limit to fix the R 2 ​ F 2 R^{2}F^{2} higher derivative correction to the correlator in terms of the weight one non-holomorphic Eisenstein series, and also to fix the logarithmic threshold. We also compute the one-loop correction to the correlator, and match it to the expected flat space limit result.

p>2
we can make a singlet by taking the
j_{L}=1
component in the
p\times p
OPE. Thus we only have one independent function and we define

\begin{split}\langle\mathbf{2}2pp\rangle^{ABC}\equiv\langle\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}(1)\mathcal{O}_{2}(2)^{A}\mathcal{O}^{B}_{p}(3)\mathcal{O}^{C}_{p}(4)\rangle=f^{ABC}\frac{(y_{1}\cdot y_{2})^{2}(y_{3}\cdot y_{4})^{p}}{x_{12}^{4}x_{34}^{2p}}\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{14}\bar{y}_{34}^{p-3}G_{\mathbf{2}2pp}(U,V;\alpha)\,.\end{split}

(2.13)

To conclude, we observe that the superconformal Ward identities should be satisfied individually by each of the four functions –
G^{(12)}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp},G^{(13)}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp},G^{(14)}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp},G_{\mathbf{2}2pp}
– introduced to describe these last two classes of correlators. As a result, for each of those functions
G
there is a corresponding
(f,\mathcal{G})
pair related to
G
as in (
2.6
).

2.2
Large
N
expansion

The main purpose of our work is to study the mixed gluon-graviton four-point function (
2.4
) in a large
N
expansion. From the point of view of string theory, the system is described by the action

\begin{split}S&=S_{\text{SUGRA}}[G_{MN},\ldots]+S_{\text{SYM}}[A_{\mu},\ldots;\,g_{\mu\nu},\ldots]\,,\\
S_{\text{SUGRA}}[G_{MN},\ldots]&=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}_{\mathrm{10d}}}\int d^{10}X\,\sqrt{-G}\,[R+\mathrm{HD}+\mathrm{SUSY}]\,,\\
S_{\text{SYM}}[A_{\mu},\ldots;\,g_{\mu\nu},\ldots]&=-\frac{1}{4\mathtt{g}^{2}_{\text{8d}}}\int d^{8}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left[F^{A}_{\mu\nu}F^{A,\mu\nu}+\mathrm{HD}+\mathrm{SUSY}\right]\,,\end{split}

(2.14)

where
G_{MN}
is the ten-dimensional metric and
g_{\mu\nu}
is its pull-back on the worldvolume of the sevenbranes, while
F^{A}_{\mu\nu}
denotes the field strength for the
SO(8)
gauge theory. Here “HD” denotes higher-derivative terms in the low energy expansion of the effective action and “SUSY” denotes the supersymmetric completion of the given terms. In (
2.14
), we have introduced the ten-dimensional gravitational coupling constant
\kappa_{\mathrm{10d}}
and the eight-dimensional YM coupling constant
\mathtt{g}_{\text{8d}}
, which are related to the string parameters by

\begin{split}2\kappa^{2}_{\mathrm{10d}}=(2\pi)^{7}g_{s}^{2}\ell_{s}^{8}\,,\quad\mathtt{g}^{2}_{\text{8d}}=\frac{4\pi^{4}}{N}=(2\pi)^{5}g_{s}\ell_{s}^{4}\,,\end{split}

(2.15)

where
g_{s}
is the string coupling and
\ell_{s}
the string length. Moreover, flux quantization relates the AdS radius
L
to the number
N
of D3 branes as in (
1.1
). The large
N
expansion of (
2.4
) then takes the form shown in (
1.2
).

Our goal in the rest of the paper is to compute each term in (
1.2
). The graviton exchange term
G^{(R)}
is rather simple to compute and can be normalized using protected OPE coefficients. The one loop term is the technically most challenging one, as it requires solving a mixing problem, but it can be computed using standard techniques
[
40
]
up to an additive renormalization ambiguity. On the other hand,
G^{(R^{2}F^{2})}(\tau)
and
G^{(\log)}
are simple polynomials in Mellin space (which we introduce below), as they correspond to contact terms, but the coefficients in these polynomials cannot be computed using bootstrap considerations alone. We have emphasized that
G^{(R^{2}F^{2})}(\tau)
is a function of the axio-dilaton field

\begin{split}\tau=\frac{\chi}{2\pi}+\frac{i}{g_{s}}\,,\end{split}

(2.16)

where
\chi
is the axion.

As well known, the computation of holographic correlators is greatly simplified by the use of Mellin space. Moreover, to solve the one loop mixing problem we are going to consider the correlators
\langle\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp\rangle
and
\langle\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp\rangle
, so it will be convenient to introduce a Mellin representation for generic correlation functions between four scalars of dimensions
(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3},\Delta_{4})=(2,2,p,p)
, which can be then adapted to our main correlator (
2.4
) simply by setting
p=2
. For any of the three functions
G
appearing in (
2.7
) or for the function
G
in (
2.13
), we set

\begin{split}G(U,V;\alpha)&=\int_{-i\,\infty}^{+i\,\infty}\frac{dsdt}{(4\pi i)^{2}}U^{\tfrac{s}{2}}V^{\tfrac{t-p-2}{2}}\mathbf{\Gamma}\,M(s,t;\alpha)\,,\\
\mathbf{\Gamma}&=\Gamma\left[2-\tfrac{s}{2}\right]\Gamma\left[p-\tfrac{s}{2}\right]\Gamma\left[\tfrac{2+p-t}{2}\right]^{2}\Gamma\left[\tfrac{2+p-u}{2}\right]^{2}\,,\end{split}

(2.17)

where
s+t+u=2(2+p)
. On the other hand, for the reduced correlator
\mathcal{G}
associated to each of those functions, we set

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}(U,V)&=\int_{-i\,\infty}^{+i\,\infty}\frac{dsdt}{(4\pi i)^{2}}U^{\tfrac{s}{2}}V^{\tfrac{t-p-2}{2}}\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\,\mathcal{M}(s,t)\,,\\
\mathbf{\widetilde{\Gamma}}&=\Gamma\left[2-\tfrac{s}{2}\right]\Gamma\left[p-\tfrac{s}{2}\right]\Gamma\left[\tfrac{2+p-t}{2}\right]^{2}\Gamma\left[\tfrac{2+p-\tilde{u}}{2}\right]^{2}\,,\end{split}

(2.18)

where
s+t+\tilde{u}=2(1+p)
. The integration contour in both cases includes all poles in
s
and
t
, but not those in
u
or
\tilde{u}
.

To fix the coefficients appearing in
G^{(R^{2}F^{2})}(\tau)
and
G^{(\log)}
we are going to use two additional ingredients. One is the flat space limit
[
41
]
, which relates the reduced Mellin amplitude
\mathcal{M}
corresponding to the correlator (
2.4
) to a flat space scattering amplitude between two gravitons and two gluons via

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}(s,t)=\lim_{L\to\infty}-\pi^{5}L^{6}\int\frac{d\beta}{2\pi i}\frac{e^{\beta}}{\beta^{4}}\frac{L^{4}(u+\alpha s)^{2}}{32}\mathcal{M}\Big(\frac{L^{2}}{2\beta}s,\frac{L^{2}}{2\beta}t\Big)\,,\end{split}

(2.19)

where
\mathcal{A}(s,t)
is the
ten
-dimensional scattering amplitude in flat space. Note that in the flat space limit we should think of this as a scattering between gluons confined on the eight-dimensional sevenbranes and gravitons propagating in the whole ten-dimensional spacetime. This is why we have chosen the power of
L
in (
2.19
) to be that for a ten-dimensional scattering process, as opposed to the case of pure gluon scattering considered in
[
8
,
56
,
45
]
, which is genuinely eight-dimensional. See (
5.4
) and below for more comments on the interplay between eight and ten dimensions in the explicit results, specifically for one loop diagrams.

The other ingredient is the integrated correlator constraint introduced in
[
43
]
, which we review in the next subsection.

2.3
Integrated constraint

Using supersymmetric localization
[
57
]
it is possible to compute the mass deformed sphere free energy
F(\mu_{i},m)
of the theory, where
\mu_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,4
are four masses for the fundamental hypermultiplets along the Cartan generators of
SO(8)
and
m
is a mass for the antisymmetric hypermultiplet, along the Cartan of
SU(2)_{L}
. The case of four independent
\mu_{i}
with
m=0
was considered in
[
45
]
, while here we are interested in the case of four identical
SO(8)
masses
\mu_{i}\equiv\mu
and
m\neq 0
. The constraint derived in
[
43
]
relates mass derivatives of
F(\mu,m)
to a certain integral of the connected part of the correlator (
2.4
). More precisely, in our case the constraint reads

\begin{split}-\left.\partial^{2}_{\mu}\partial^{2}_{m}F\right|_{\mu=m=0}=8k_{SU(2)_{L}}k_{SO(8)}I[\mathcal{G}]\,,\end{split}

(2.20)

where the integral is defined as

\begin{split}I[\mathcal{G}]\equiv\frac{1}{\pi}\int dR\,d\theta\,R^{3}\sin^{2}\theta\frac{\bar{D}_{1,1,1,1}(U,V){\mathcal{G}}(U,V)}{U^{2}}\bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{c}U=1+R^{2}-2R\cos\theta\\
V=R^{2}\end{subarray}}\,,\end{split}

(2.21)

and the function
\bar{D}_{1,1,1,1}(U,V)
is given by

\begin{split}\bar{D}_{1,1,1,1}(U,V)=\frac{1}{z-\bar{z}}\left(\log(z\bar{z})\log\frac{1-z}{1-\bar{z}}+2\text{Li}(z)-2\text{Li}(\bar{z})\right)\,.\end{split}

(2.22)

It is also convenient to express (
2.21
) as an integral of the reduced Mellin amplitude
\mathcal{M}
associated with the reduced correlator
\mathcal{G}
. The result, found in
[
58
]
, is

\begin{split}I[\mathcal{M}]\equiv-\int\frac{dsdt}{(4\pi i)^{2}}&\Bigg[\mathcal{M}(s,t)\Gamma[2-s/2]\Gamma[s/2]\Gamma[2-t/2]\Gamma[t/2]\Gamma[2-{u}/2]\Gamma[{u}/2]\\
&\times\Big(\frac{H_{\frac{s}{2}-1}+H_{1-\frac{s}{2}}}{(t-2)(u-2)}+\frac{H_{\frac{t}{2}-1}+H_{1-\frac{t}{2}}}{(s-2)(u-2)}+\frac{H_{\frac{u}{2}-1}+H_{1-\frac{u}{2}}}{(s-2)(t-2)}\Big)\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(2.23)

where
H_{n}
is a harmonic number.

3
Holographic correlators

The goal of this section is to present the various ingredients appearing at the first few orders in the large
N
expansion of the
\langle\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}22\rangle
correlator, see (
1.2
). The technically most challenging part is given by the computation of the one loop term
G^{(R|F^{2})}
, since it requires solving a mixing problem which in turn necessitates the computation of additional correlators at tree level:
\langle\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp\rangle
and
\langle\mathbf{2}2pp\rangle
. We will therefore start with that computation and then proceed to discuss the mixing problem and the derivation of the one loop term. We will conclude with a brief presentation of the higher-derivative corrections we are interested in, which introduce the free parameters that we will fix in the rest of the paper.

3.1
Tree level

As explained, we are interested in computing two types of correlators at tree level. In both cases, we will consider the exchange of the three types of multiplets introduced in Section
2
: the stress tensor multiplet
\mathcal{T}
, super gluon multiplets
\mathcal{O}_{p}
and super graviton multiplets
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{p}}
.

3.1.1
Two gravitons, two gluons:
\langle\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp\rangle
.

Let us consider the two independent OPE channels for
\langle\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp\rangle
, focusing on the single trace operators that can be exchanged since those correspond to exchange Witten diagrams.

•

Direct channel.
The
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}\times\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}
OPE contains
SO(8)
singlets with
j_{L}\in\{0,1,2\}
and
j_{R}\in\{0,1,2\}
. Among the three types of short multiplets listed above, this allows for the exchange of the stress tensor multiplet
\mathcal{T}
, as well as super graviton multiplets
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}
and
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{4}}
. The latter, however, is an extremal coupling and therefore the corresponding Witten exchange diagram is absent. On the other hand, the
\mathcal{O}_{p}\times\mathcal{O}_{p}
OPE contains both singlets and adjoints of
SO(8)
, as well as all
SU(2)_{L}
spins
j_{L}\in\{0,\ldots,p-2\}
and
SU(2)_{R}
spins
j_{R}\in\{0,\ldots,p\}
. As a result, for
p=2
only the stress tensor multiplet
\mathcal{T}
is exchanged, while for
p>2
both
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}
are exchanged. Since both the gluon-gluon-graviton and the graviton-graviton-graviton vertices have couplings of order
1/N
, both exchange diagrams appear at order
1/N^{2}
.

•

Crossed channel.
The other independent channel has the
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}\times\mathcal{O}_{p}
OPE repeated twice. This contains
SO(8)
adjoint fields with
j_{L}=\tfrac{p}{2}-2,\tfrac{p}{2}-1,\tfrac{p}{2}
and
j_{R}=\tfrac{p}{2}-1,\tfrac{p}{2},\tfrac{p}{2}+1
: precisely the quantum numbers of
\mathcal{O}_{p-2}
,
\mathcal{O}_{p}
and
\mathcal{O}_{p+2}
. However, the first and the third are extremal, so only
\mathcal{O}_{p}
can be exchanged. Moreover, note that this is only possible for
p>2
since for
p=2
the only allowed
SU(2)_{L}
spin is
j_{L}=1
but
\mathcal{O}_{p}
has
j_{L}=0
. So we have a gluon-gluon-graviton vertex repeated twice, which gives an exchange at order
1/N^{2}
.

To summarize, we expect

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(s,t;\bar{y})=\bar{y}_{12}^{2}\bar{y}_{34}^{2}\,\mathcal{M}^{(\mathcal{T})}(s,t)+\mathtt{t}_{s}\,\mathcal{M}^{(s)}(s,t)+\mathtt{t}_{t}\,\mathcal{M}^{(t)}(s,t)+\mathtt{t}_{u}\,\mathcal{M}^{(u)}(s,t)\,,\end{split}

(3.1)

where
\mathtt{t}_{i}
,
i=s,t,u
, are three polynomials in the polarization
\bar{y}
, while
\mathcal{M}^{(i)}
are reduced Mellin amplitudes corresponding to the exchange of
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{p}}
(for the
s
channel) or
\mathcal{O}_{p}
(for the
t
and
u
channels). We now proceed to the computation of the various ingredients and note that only the stress tensor can be exchanged for
p=2
, so we expect the last three terms in (
3.1
) to vanish in that case.

The amplitude corresponding to the stress tensor is the simplest. It can be derived making an ansatz for the full Mellin amplitude as a sum of exchange diagrams corresponding to the three operators listed in Table
2
and then demanding that it satisfies the superconformal Ward identities. Alternatively, one can just make an ansatz for the reduced correlator and then check that it gives the correct poles. Either way, it is easy enough to determine that

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{(\mathcal{T})}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}^{(\mathcal{T})}\frac{1}{s-2}\,,\end{split}

(3.2)

where
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}^{(\mathcal{T})}
is a normalization term that we will fix later in this section.

Next, let us focus on the
SU(2)_{L}
tensor structures
\mathtt{t}_{i}
,
i=s,t,u
. To derive those, it is useful to consider the three-point functions (see Appendix
A
for the derivation)

\begin{split}\langle\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}(x_{1};y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}(x_{2};y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}(x_{3};y_{3},\bar{y}_{3})\rangle&=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{k_{SU(2)_{L}}}}(12)(13)(23)\bar{y}_{12}\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{23}\\
&=\frac{2}{N}(12)(13)(23)\bar{y}_{12}\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{23}+O(1/N^{2})\,,\end{split}

(3.3)

and

\begin{split}\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{A}(x_{1};y_{1},\bar{y}_{1}){\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{B}(x_{2};y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}(x_{3};y_{3},\bar{y}_{3})\rangle&=\frac{p-2}{\sqrt{2k_{SU(2)_{L}}}}\delta^{AB}(12)^{p-1}(13)(23)\bar{y}_{12}^{p-3}\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{23}\\
&=\frac{p-2}{2N}\delta^{AB}(12)^{p-1}(13)(23)\bar{y}_{12}^{p-3}\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{23}+O(1/N^{2})\,.\end{split}

(3.4)

The tensor structures associated with the various exchanges can be found by “opening the indices” corresponding to exchanged fields in the product of two three-point functions and summing over the open indices. We find

\begin{split}&\sum_{\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta}}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bar{y}^{\bar{\alpha}}_{E}\partial\bar{y}^{\bar{\beta}}_{E}}\bar{y}_{12}\bar{y}_{1E}\bar{y}_{2E}\right]\left[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bar{y}^{\bar{\alpha}}_{E}\partial\bar{y}^{\bar{\beta}}_{E}}\bar{y}_{34}^{p-3}\bar{y}_{3E}\bar{y}_{4E}\right]\\
&\propto\bar{y}_{12}\bar{y}_{34}^{p-3}\left(\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{24}+\bar{y}_{14}\bar{y}_{23}\right)\equiv\mathtt{t}_{s}\,,\end{split}

(3.5)

and

\begin{split}&\sum_{\bar{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\alpha}_{p-2}}\left[\frac{\partial^{p-2}}{\partial\bar{y}^{\bar{\alpha}_{1}}_{E}\cdots\partial\bar{y}^{\bar{\alpha}_{p-2}}_{E}}\bar{y}_{14}\bar{y}_{1E}\bar{y}_{4E}^{p-3}\right]\left[\frac{\partial^{p-2}}{\partial\bar{y}^{\bar{\alpha}_{1}}_{E}\cdots\partial\bar{y}^{\bar{\alpha}_{p-2}}_{E}}\bar{y}_{23}\bar{y}_{2E}\bar{y}_{3E}^{p-3}\right]\\
&\propto\bar{y}_{14}\bar{y}_{23}\bar{y}_{34}^{p-4}\left(\bar{y}_{14}\bar{y}_{23}-(p-2)\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{24}\right)\equiv\mathtt{t}_{t}\,.\end{split}

(3.6)

Similarly, exchanging 1 and 2,

\begin{split}\mathtt{t}_{u}\equiv\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{24}\bar{y}_{34}^{p-4}\left(\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{24}-(p-2)\bar{y}_{14}\bar{y}_{23}\right)\,.\end{split}

(3.7)

Note that the three structures are not independent, actually they satisfy

\begin{split}\mathtt{t}_{s}=\mathtt{t}_{u}-\mathtt{t}_{t}\,.\end{split}

(3.8)

Since there are only two independent tensor structures, and using the symmetry under the exchange of the first two operators (that is, under
t\leftrightarrow\tilde{u}
), we can actually rewrite our ansatz as

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(s,t;\bar{y})=\bar{y}_{12}^{2}\bar{y}_{34}^{2}\,\mathcal{M}^{(\mathcal{T})}(s,t)+\mathtt{t}_{t}\,\mathcal{M}_{st}+\mathtt{t}_{u}\,\mathcal{M}_{su}\,,\end{split}

(3.9)

and since the exchanged and external fields transform in the same representation of the superconformal algebra, it turns out that the two independent reduced Mellin amplitudes
\mathcal{M}_{st}
and
\mathcal{M}_{su}
are exactly the same, up to an overall coefficient, as those computed in
[
8
]
in the context of pure gluon scattering in AdS, since they should satisfy the same constraints. Thus, we can ultimately write

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(s,t;\bar{y})=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}^{(\mathcal{T})}\frac{\bar{y}_{12}^{2}\bar{y}_{34}^{2}}{s-2}+\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}^{(\mathcal{J})}\left[\frac{\mathtt{t}_{t}}{(s-2)(t-p)}+\frac{\mathtt{t}_{u}}{(s-2)(\tilde{u}-p)}\right]\,,\end{split}

(3.10)

where we remind that
s+t+\tilde{u}=2(1+p)
and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}^{(\mathcal{J})}
is another normalization term that we shall soon fix.

Converting (
3.10
) to spacetime, we find the function

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(U,V)=&-\mathcal{N}^{(\mathcal{T})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}\bar{y}_{12}^{2}\bar{y}_{34}^{2}\frac{U^{p}}{2}\bar{D}_{p+1,p+1,2,2}\\
&+\mathcal{N}^{(\mathcal{J})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}\bar{y}_{14}\bar{y}_{23}(\bar{y}_{14}\bar{y}_{23}-(p-2)\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{24})\frac{U}{4}\bar{D}_{1,2,p+1,p}\\
&+\mathcal{N}^{(\mathcal{J})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{24}(\bar{y}_{13}\bar{y}_{24}-(p-2)\bar{y}_{14}\bar{y}_{23})\frac{U}{4}\bar{D}_{1,2,p,p+1}\,,\end{split}

(3.11)

and we write the corresponding full correlator in spacetime as

\begin{split}G^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(U,V;\alpha)=G^{(\text{free})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(U,V;\alpha)+(1-\alpha z)(1-\alpha\bar{z})\mathcal{T}^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(U,V)\,,\end{split}

(3.12)

where
G^{(\text{free})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(U,V;\alpha)
is the free theory correlator computed in (
A.20
). On the other hand, we can also write the same correlator as

\begin{split}G^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(U,V;\alpha)=\frac{z(1-\alpha\bar{z})f^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(\bar{z})-\bar{z}(1-\alpha z)f^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}({z})}{z-\bar{z}}+(1-\alpha z)(1-\alpha\bar{z})\mathcal{G}^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(U,V)\,,\end{split}

(3.13)

which is convenient because it simplifies the expression of the OPE, which reads

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{r}(U,V)&=U^{-1}\left[\sum_{\ell,\Delta\geq\ell+2}\lambda^{(\mathbf{2},p)}_{\Delta,\ell,r}G_{\Delta+2,\ell}+\sum_{\ell}\lambda^{(\mathbf{2},p)}_{\ell+5,\ell+1,r}G_{\ell+5,\ell+1}+\lambda^{(\mathbf{2},p)}_{4,0,r}G_{4,0}\right]\,,\\
f_{r}(z)&=\sum_{\ell}\lambda^{(\mathbf{2},p)}_{\ell+5,\ell+1,r}k_{2\ell+6}+\lambda^{(\mathbf{2},p)}_{4,0,r}k_{4}+\delta_{r,\mathbf{1}}-\delta_{r,\mathbf{adj}}\lambda^{(\mathbf{2},p)}_{\mathcal{J}}\,k_{2}-\delta_{r,\mathbf{1}}\lambda^{(\mathbf{2},p)}_{\mathcal{T}}\,k_{4}\,,\end{split}

(3.14)

in terms of 4d conformal blocks
G_{\Delta,\ell}
and 1d conformal blocks
k_{h}
, where the subscript
r
runs over the three representations in (
2.10
). Each coefficient
\lambda^{(\mathbf{2},p)}_{-}
is given as a product of OPE coefficients as
\lambda^{(\mathbf{2})}_{-}\lambda^{(p)}_{-}
, and for the current (
\mathcal{J}
) and stress tensor (
\mathcal{T}
) these can be computed combining our free theory results from Appendix
A
with the results of
[
59
]
. We find

\begin{split}\lambda^{(\mathbf{2},p)}_{\mathcal{J}}=\frac{2(p-2)}{N^{2}}\,,\quad\lambda^{(\mathbf{2},p)}_{\mathcal{T}}=\frac{p}{6N^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(3.15)

While in general the sum over long multiplets begins at twist two (
\Delta=\ell+2
), at large
N
there should be no twist two long multiplets. This requirement fixes 1) the expression of
\mathcal{G}
in terms of
\mathcal{T}
and 2) the normalization terms in (
3.11
). We find

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(U,V)=\mathcal{T}^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}(U,V)+\frac{(p-3)^{2}}{N^{2}}U(\bar{y}_{13}^{2}\bar{y}_{24}^{2}+\bar{y}_{14}^{2}\bar{y}_{23}^{2}/V^{2})\,,\end{split}

(3.16)

while the normalizations are

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}^{(\mathcal{T})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}=-\frac{2p}{(p-2)!N^{2}}\,,\quad\mathcal{N}^{(\mathcal{J})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}=-\frac{4}{(p-3)!N^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(3.17)

In the special case
p=2
, as expected we find
\mathcal{N}^{(\mathcal{J})}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}22}=0
so that the correlator is completely determined by the exchange of the stress tensor. The result in this case gives the graviton exchange term
G^{(R)}
appearing in (
1.2
), whose associated reduced Mellin amplitude is then

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{(R)}=-\frac{4}{s-2}\,.\end{split}

(3.18)

3.1.2
One gravitons, three gluons:
\langle\mathbf{2}2pp\rangle
.

Similarly to the previous case, let us consider the exchanged single trace operators in the two independent OPE channels to determine the associated exchanged Witten diagrams.

•

Direct channel.
Here we are interested in the single-trace operators appearing in the overlap between the OPEs
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}\times\mathcal{O}_{2}
and
\mathcal{O}_{p}\times\mathcal{O}_{p}
. The former selects
SO(8)
adjoint operators, so the only viable candidates are super gluon multiplets. However, the first OPE also forces
j_{L}=1
for the putative exchanged gluon (and
j_{R}\in\{0,1,2\}
), allowing in principle only for the exchange of
\mathcal{O}_{4}
. But this is extremal so it cannot be exchanged: as a result, there are no exchange Witten diagrams in the direct channel.

•

Crossed channel.
Focusing on
\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}\times\mathcal{O}_{p}
first, we find again that we have to focus on super gluons since only
SO(8)
adjoints are exchanged. Neglecting extremal couplings, we find that
\mathcal{O}_{p}
can be exchanged in this OPE and moreover it also appears in the
\mathcal{O}_{2}\times\mathcal{O}_{p}
OPE. Thus we have Witten diagrams corresponding to the exchange of
\mathcal{O}_{p}
both in the
t
and
u
channel. They arise from the product of a gluon-gluon-graviton vertex at
O(1/N)
at a gluon-gluon-gluon vertex at
O(1/N^{1/2})
, so this correlator appears at
O(1/N^{3/2})
.

As in the previous case, we have an exchange diagram which is required to satisfy all the kinematical constraints of the gluon exchange diagrams computed in
[
8
]
. This time, though, there are no exchanges in the
s
-channel. The result is then

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}}{(t-p)(\tilde{u}-p)}\,,\end{split}

(3.19)

where
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}
is a normalization that we now proceed to fix. This reduced correlator contains no twist-two terms, so we have to proceed in a different way compared to the previous subsection. We observe that computing the full Mellin amplitude associated to (
3.19
) and summing over poles in
s,t
to obtain the corresponding spacetime expression, we obtain the result for the full correlator

\begin{split}G^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}(U,V;\alpha)={\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}}\left[\frac{(p-2)!}{4}\frac{U^{2}}{V}\alpha(\alpha-1)+(1-\alpha z)(1-\alpha\bar{z})\frac{U^{2}}{4}\bar{D}_{1,3,p,p}\right]\,,\end{split}

(3.20)

from which it is straightforward to derive the associated chiral algebra correlator

\begin{split}f^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}(z)=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}\frac{(p-2)!}{4}\frac{z^{2}}{1-z}\,.\end{split}

(3.21)

We can compare this to the expected result for
f^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}(z)
, which can be computed in the free theory and found to be

\begin{split}f^{(\text{free})}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}(z)=\frac{p-2}{\sqrt{2}N^{3/2}}\frac{z^{2}}{1-z}\,,\end{split}

(3.22)

see Appendix
A
for details. Comparing the two fixes for us

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{(p-3)!N^{3/2}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathcal{M}^{(\text{tree})}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}=\frac{1}{(p-3)!N^{3/2}}\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{(t-p)(\tilde{u}-p)}\,.\end{split}

(3.23)

3.2
Mixing problem

We would now like to compute the one loop term
G^{(R|F^{2})}
in (
1.2
), using the AdS unitarity method of
[
40
]
. This involves computing terms in the one loop correlator that are proportional to
\log^{2}U
(or
\log^{2}V
) using CFT data at previous perturbative orders. In particular, naively one is supposed to square the tree level anomalous dimensions. However, this squaring is complicated by the problem of operators mixing, which we now discuss in the two OPE channels separately.

3.2.1
Direct channel

In the direct channel, the long supermultiplets exchanged in
\langle\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}22\rangle
are
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times SO(8)
singlets. Focusing on double trace operators made out of super gluon and super graviton multiplets, we find that for given twist
\tau=4+2n
and spin
\ell
, there are
(2n+1)
distinct operators that can be formed, of the schematic form

\begin{split}[{\mathcal{O}}_{2}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}]_{n}\,,\quad\ldots\,,\quad[{\mathcal{O}}_{n+2}{\mathcal{O}}_{n+2}]_{n}\,,\quad[\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}]_{n}\,,\quad\ldots\,,\quad[\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{n+2}}\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{n+2}}]_{n}\,,\end{split}

(3.24)

where for instance
[\mathcal{O}_{p}\mathcal{O}_{p}]_{n}\sim:\mathcal{O}_{p}\Box^{n+2-p}\partial^{\ell}\mathcal{O}_{p}:
for given spin
\ell
and twist
\tau=4+2n
. We adopt this as a basis of operators to span the degeneracy space, with a indices
A,B=1,\ldots,2(n+1)
running over the
2(n+1)
operators in the space. We also note that the basis naturally splits into two subspaces of equal dimension
n+1
: that of gluon-gluon (
O_{gg}
) double trace operators and that of graviton-graviton (
O_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}
) ones. We use indices
a,b=1,\ldots,n+1
for the former and an indices
i,j=1,\ldots,n+1
for the latter.

Clearly, given the arbitrary choice of basis, the dilatation operator of the theory in this basis will not necessarily be diagonal. Thus, we should really consider the anomalous dimensions as a matrix
\Gamma_{AB}
, whose entries at each perturbative order can be extracted from the perturbative expansion of

\begin{split}\langle O_{A}O_{B}\rangle-\langle O_{A}O_{B}\rangle^{(0)}\,,\end{split}

(3.25)

where the superscript zero in this case denotes the result in the generalized free theory (GFT) at
N=\infty
. We also note that the two-point function
\langle O_{A}O_{B}\rangle
can be computed considering a four-point function between the four underlying single trace operators and taking suitable derivatives and coincident limits. This allows us to derive a few basic facts.

First, we consider GFT two-point function and define a matrix of norms

\begin{split}\langle O_{A}O_{B}\rangle^{(0)}\equiv\mathtt{g}_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathtt{g}_{ab}&0\\
0&\mathtt{g}_{ij}\end{pmatrix}\,,\end{split}

(3.26)

where the diagonal structure is inherited from the fact that mixed correlators
\langle\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}gg\rangle
are given by the identity in the GFT at
N=\infty
. Moreover, the same applies to
\langle ppqq\rangle
and
\langle\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}\mathbf{q}\mathbf{q}\rangle
correlators for
p\neq q
, which implies that the matrices
\mathtt{g}_{ab}
and
\mathtt{g}_{ij}
are diagonal. We can then normalize the operators in such a way that
\mathtt{g}_{AB}
is the identity.

Next, we consider three-point functions with two single trace and one double trace operator in the GFT. For generic super gluons
g
, we find
8
8

8

In this subsection we will use the symbol
C
to denote OPE coefficients between two short and one long operator. Products between these OPE coefficients give the coefficients
\lambda^{(-,-)}_{\Delta,\ell,r}
of long multiplets appearing in (
3.14
).

\begin{split}C^{(0)}_{ggA}=\langle gg{O}_{A}\rangle^{(0)}=(C^{(0)}_{gga},C^{(0)}_{ggi})=(C^{(0)}_{gga},0)\,,\end{split}

(3.27)

where again we have used the fact that the three-point functions can be computed from GFT four-point functions where the last two points are taken to be coincident. Similarly, we have

\begin{split}C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}A}=\langle\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}{O}_{A}\rangle^{(0)}=(C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}a},C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}i})=(0,C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}i})\,.\end{split}

(3.28)

Last, we address the structure of the anomalous dimensions matrix
\Gamma_{AB}
. We can think of it in terms of
(n+1)\times(n+1)
blocks

\begin{split}\Gamma_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{ab}&\Gamma_{aj}\\
\Gamma_{ib}&\Gamma_{ij}\end{pmatrix}\,,\end{split}

(3.29)

where note that
\Gamma_{ai}=\Gamma_{ia}
and the leading order contributions,
i.e.
the tree-level anomalous dimensions we are interested in for the one loop computation, can be obtained respectively from

\begin{split}\langle gggg\rangle^{(\text{tree})}|_{\log U}&\to C^{(0)}_{ggA}\Gamma_{AB}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{ggB}=C^{(0)}_{gga}\Gamma_{ab}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{ggb}\,,\\
\langle gg\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}\rangle^{(\text{tree})}|_{\log U}&\to C^{(0)}_{ggA}\Gamma_{AB}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}B}=C^{(0)}_{gga}\Gamma_{aj}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}j}\,,\\
\langle\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}gg\rangle^{(\text{tree})}|_{\log U}&\to C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}A}\Gamma_{AB}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{ggB}=C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}i}\Gamma_{ib}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{ggb}\,,\\
\langle\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}\rangle^{(\text{tree})}|_{\log U}&\to C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}A}\Gamma_{AB}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}B}=C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}i}\Gamma_{ij}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}j}\,,\end{split}

(3.30)

from which we also derive that, given the large
N
scaling of the associated correlators,

\begin{split}\Gamma_{ab}^{(\text{tree})}\sim O(1/N)\,,\quad\Gamma_{ai}^{(\text{tree})}\sim\Gamma_{ia}^{(\text{tree})}\sim\Gamma_{ij}^{(\text{tree})}\sim O(1/N^{2})\,.\end{split}

(3.31)

The coefficient of each conformal block for the term proportional to
\log^{2}U
in the direct channel OPE can be obtained by multiplying the square of the anomalous dimensions matrix with suitable OPE coefficients on the left and on the right. Using the results obtained so far, we find that the object we want to compute is

\begin{split}\left.\mathcal{G}^{(R|F^{2})}(U,V)\right|_{\log^{2}U}=\sum_{n,\ell}F^{(s)}_{n,\ell}U^{-1}G_{6+2n+\ell,\ell}(U,V)\,,\end{split}

(3.32)

where

\begin{split}F^{(s)}_{n,\ell}=\frac{1}{N^{3}}\left[C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}i}\Gamma_{ia}^{(\text{tree})}\Gamma_{ab}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{22b}\right]_{n,\ell}\,.\end{split}

(3.33)

Following
[
60
]
, we massage this expression by introducing the matrix

\begin{split}M_{ra}=\frac{C^{(0)}_{rra}}{\sqrt{\sum_{b}(C^{(0)}_{rrb})^{2}}}\,,\end{split}

(3.34)

which is
(n+1)\times(n+1)
since
r=2,\ldots,n+2
runs over the
n+1
supergluons
\mathcal{O}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{n+2}
while
a=1,\ldots,n+1
runs over the
O_{gg}
double-traces of twist
4+2n
. We note that
M
is orthogonal, since

\begin{split}(MM^{T})_{rs}=\sum_{a}M_{ra}M_{as}=\frac{\sum_{a}C^{(0)}_{rra}C^{(0)}_{ssa}}{\sqrt{\sum_{b}(C^{(0)}_{rrb})^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{c}(C^{(0)}_{ssb})^{2}}}=\delta_{rs}\,,\end{split}

(3.35)

which follows from the fact that GFT
\langle ppqq\rangle
correlators only exchange the identity for
p\neq q
. As a result, we also have

\begin{split}(M^{T}M)_{ab}=\sum_{p}M_{ap}M_{pb}=\frac{\sum_{p}C^{(0)}_{ppa}C^{(0)}_{ppb}}{{\sum_{c}(C^{(0)}_{ppc})^{2}}}=\delta_{ab}\,.\end{split}

(3.36)

Using (
3.36
) in (
3.33
), we find

\begin{split}F^{(s)}_{n,\ell}&=\frac{1}{N^{3}}\left[C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}i}\Gamma_{ia}^{(\text{tree})}(M^{T}M)_{ab}\Gamma_{bc}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{22j}\right]_{n,\ell}\\
&=\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{p}\left[\left(C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}i}\Gamma_{ia}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{ppa}\right)\left(\sum_{b}(C^{(0)}_{ppb})^{2}\right)^{-1}\left(C^{(0)}_{ppc}\Gamma_{cd}^{(\text{tree})}C^{(0)}_{22d}\right)\right]_{n,\ell}\,,\end{split}

(3.37)

where the combinations of OPE coefficients and anomalous dimensions are the same appearing in the expansion of free theory and tree level correlators. In particular, we can extract

\begin{split}\left.\mathcal{G}_{pppp}^{(0)}(U,V)\right|_{\mathbf{1}}&=\sum_{n,\ell}A^{\mathbf{1},(pppp)}_{n,\ell}U^{-1}G_{2p+2+2n+\ell,\ell}(U,V)\,,\\
\left.\mathcal{G}_{22pp}^{(\text{tree})}(U,V)\right|_{\mathbf{1},\log U}&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n,\ell}\omega^{\mathbf{1},(22pp)}_{n,\ell}U^{-1}G_{2p+2+2n+\ell,\ell}(U,V)\,,\\
\left.\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}^{(\text{tree})}(U,V)\right|_{\mathbf{1},\log U}&=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{n,\ell}\omega^{\mathbf{1},(\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp)}_{n,\ell}U^{-1}G_{2p+2+2n+\ell,\ell}(U,V)\,,\end{split}

(3.38)

where

\begin{split}A^{\mathbf{1},(pppp)}_{n,\ell}&=\left[C^{(0)}_{ppa}C^{(0)}_{ppa}\right]_{n+p-2,\ell}\,,\\
\omega^{\mathbf{1},(22pp)}_{n,\ell}&=\left[C^{(0)}_{22a}\Gamma_{ab}^{(1)}C^{(0)}_{ppb}\right]_{n+p-2,\ell}\,,\\
\omega^{\mathbf{1},(\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp)}_{n,\ell}&=\left[C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}i}\Gamma_{ia}^{(1)}C^{(0)}_{ppa}\right]_{n+p-2,\ell}\,,\end{split}

(3.39)

so that ultimately we can write

\begin{split}F^{(s)}_{n,\ell}&=\sum_{p=2}^{n+2}\frac{\omega^{\mathbf{1},(22pp)}_{n-p+2,\ell}\,\omega^{\mathbf{1},(\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp)}_{n-p+2,\ell}}{A^{\mathbf{1},(pppp)}_{n-p+2,\ell}}\,.\end{split}

(3.40)

Note that the sub/superscripts
\mathbf{1}
in the above equations are meant as a reminder that we are focusing on operators that are singlets of
SO(8)
(as well as of
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
).

3.2.2
Crossed channel

The story is similar in the crossed channel. Here we are interested in the exchange of long double trace operators in the adjoint of
SO(8)
, that are singlets of
SU(2)_{R}
and have
SU(2)_{L}
spin
j_{L}=1
. Operators of this type can be formed with either two gluons or one gluon and one graviton, and the degeneracy space at twist
\tau=4+2n
is spanned by

\begin{split}[{\mathcal{O}}_{2}{\mathcal{O}}_{2}]_{n}\,,\quad\ldots\,,\quad[{\mathcal{O}}_{n+2}{\mathcal{O}}_{n+2}]_{n}\,,\quad[{\mathcal{O}}_{2}\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}]_{n}\,,\quad\ldots\,,\quad[{\mathcal{O}}_{n+2}\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{n+2}}]_{n}\,.\end{split}

(3.41)

Again, the matrix of norms is diagonal so we can just take it to be orthonormal. As before, for the free theory OPE coefficients we have

\begin{split}C^{(0)}_{ggA}=(C^{(0)}_{gga},0)\,,\quad C^{(0)}_{g\mathbf{g}A}=(0,C^{(0)}_{g\mathbf{g}\alpha})\,,\end{split}

(3.42)

where now the index
\alpha=1,\ldots,n+1
runs over the
O_{g\mathbf{g}}
double trace operators. For the anomalous dimensions matrix, we now have the decomposition

\begin{split}\Gamma_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{ab}&\Gamma_{a\beta}\\
\Gamma_{\alpha b}&\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}\end{pmatrix}\,,\end{split}

(3.43)

where the leading order contribution comes from exchange Witten diagrams in four-point functions as above. In particular, the key new ingredient is
\Gamma^{\alpha b}
and we can extract its leading order behavior from

\begin{split}\left.\langle\mathbf{g}ggg\rangle^{(\text{tree})}\right|_{\log U}\rightarrow C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}gA}\Gamma^{(\text{tree})}_{AB}C^{(0)}_{ggB}=C^{(0)}_{\mathbf{g}g\alpha}\Gamma^{(\text{tree})}_{\alpha b}C^{(0)}_{ggb}\,.\end{split}

(3.44)

From this we read off the large
N
behavior

\begin{split}\Gamma^{(\text{tree})}_{\alpha b}\sim O(1/N^{3/2})\,.\end{split}

(3.45)

The quantity we want to compute is

\begin{split}\left.\mathcal{G}^{(R|F^{2})}(U,V)\right|_{\log^{2}V}=\frac{U^{2}}{V^{2}}\sum_{n,\ell}F^{(t)}_{n,\ell}V^{-1}G_{6+2n+\ell,\ell}(V,U)\,,\end{split}

(3.46)

where using similar logic to the previous subsection we find

\begin{split}F^{(t)}_{n,\ell}=\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{p=2}^{n+2}\frac{(\omega^{\mathbf{28},j_{L}=1,(\mathbf{2}2pp)}_{n-p+2,\ell})^{2}}{A^{\mathbf{28},j_{L}=1,(pppp)}_{n-p+2,\ell}}\,,\end{split}

(3.47)

in terms of

\begin{split}\left.\mathcal{G}_{pppp}^{(0)}\right|_{\mathbf{28},j_{L}=1}&=\sum_{n,\ell}A^{\mathbf{28},j_{L}=1,(pppp)}_{n,\ell}U^{-1}G_{2p+2+2n+\ell,\ell}(U,V)\,,\\
\left.\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{2}2pp}^{(\text{tree})}\right|_{\mathbf{28},j_{L}=1,\log U}&=\sum_{n,\ell}\omega^{\mathbf{28},j_{L}=1,(\mathbf{2}2pp)}_{n,\ell}U^{-1}G_{2p+2+2n+\ell,\ell}(U,V)\,,\end{split}

(3.48)

where we have emphasized that we are interested in the contribution from the adjoint repsentation of
SO(8)
and spin
j_{L}=1
of
SU(2)_{L}
.

(5.4)

in terms of the Planck length

. There are two types of contributions in (

): one is the crossing symmetric combination of the function

, which we have computed using the gluon exchange term in (

The two types of Feynman diagrams contributing to the flat space limit (

The second observation is that in (
5.4

eight

We can fix the coefficient
b^{1}_{\log}
by requiring that the coefficient of the
\log\ell^{2}_{P}
term is such as to combine with the logarithms arising from loop diagrams to make their arguments dimensionless. We find that this happens for
b^{1}_{\log}=-3
, which combined with the result from the integrated constraint gives

\begin{split}b^{0}_{\log}=\frac{3}{2}\,,\quad b^{1}_{\log}=-3\,.\end{split}

(5.5)

Moreover, we expect that the flat space amplitude should be a regular function of the string coupling
g_{s}
and the string length
\ell_{s}
. We can use this as a constraint on

as follows. The
\log\tau_{2}

should combine with the

\begin{split}b^{1}(\tau)=2\log\tau_{2}+\text{reg}(\tau)\,,\end{split}

(5.6)

which can be completed to a modular-invariant function by

\begin{split}b^{1}(\tau)=-3E(1,\tau)+\tau\text{-independent}.\end{split}

(5.7)

As a result, combining this with the integrated constraint we also have

\begin{split}b^{0}(\tau)=\frac{3}{2}E(1,\tau)+\tau\text{-independent}\,.\end{split}

(5.8)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}=-\frac{4}{N^{2}}\frac{1}{s-2}+\frac{1}{N^{3}}\left[\mathcal{M}^{(\mathcal{T})}+\mathcal{M}^{(\mathcal{J})}-\frac{3}{2}(\log N+E(1,\tau))(2s-1)+c_{1}s+c_{0}\right]\,,\end{split}

6
Conclusion

In this paper we studied mixed gluon-graviton scattering on D7 branes in AdS at large
N
and finite
\tau
. We computed the one loop correction and matched it to the expected flat space result in
[
42
]
. We also computed the mass derivatives
\partial_{\mu}^{2}\partial_{m}^{2}F(\mu,m)|_{\mu=m=0}
at large
N
and finite
\tau
in terms of non-holomorphic Eisensteins series. We used the relation between this quantity and an integral of the holographic correlator to show that the
RF^{2}
contact term vanishes, and we combined the constraint with the known
\tau
-dependence in the flat space limit
[
47
]
to fix the
\tau
-dependence of the
R^{2}F^{2}
contact term, whose coefficient is proportional to
E(1,\tau)
, as well as the logarithmic threshold term.

Looking ahead, we would like to also fix the
\tau
-independent parts of the
R^{2}F^{2}
contact term. To do this we would need to compute the
\tau
-independent part of the flat space result in
[
47
]
. We would also need to compute the
\tau
-independent contribution
c_{\mathrm{F}_{2}}
to
\partial_{\mu}^{2}\partial_{m}^{2}F(\mu,m)|_{\mu=m=0}
, which we currently have a rough numerical estimate for, but would be nice to compute in closed form. This will likely require new technical methods of taking the large
\lambda
expansion of the finite
\lambda
results at that order in
1/N
.

It would also be interesting to guess a finite
N
expression for
\partial_{\mu}^{2}\partial_{m}^{2}F(\mu,m)|_{\mu=m=0}
. This was accomplished for two mass derivatives of the sphere free energy in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM
[
65
,
66
]
, which at large
N
and finite
\tau
was also written in terms of non-holomorphic Eisenstein series. The difference in our case is that
E(1,\tau)
appears, and we also have
\tau
-independent constants at every integer power of
1/N
. As mentioned before, our result seems to have the same form at large
N
and finite
\tau
as the integrated correlator of two gravitons and two giant graviton D3 branes in
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}

[
46
]
, which can perhaps be used to find a finite
N
expression for both.

There are several generalizations to our results. In 4d, one could consider two different orbifolds of this theory as described in
[
67
]
, where instead of the
SO(8)
flavor group one now has
U(4)
or
SO(4)\times SO(4)
. Some localization results for the mass deformed sphere free energy were considered for the
U(4)
theory in
[
68
,
69
]
. One could also consider the other F-theory AdS setups where
\tau
is fixed to a specific value, and the flavor group is now
SU(2),SU(3),E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}

[
49
]
. The dual CFTs in these case are non-Lagrangian, but it might be possible to study them using their known Seiberg-Witten curves, as was done for gluon scattering in
[
70
]
. One could also consider gluon-graviton scattering in other AdS
d+1
/CFT
d
duals, namely
d=3,5,6
. The
d=3
case was already discussed in
[
14
]
, but the one loop term was not yet computed. It should be possible to fix the unique contact term contributing at the same order in
1/N
as the one loop term, using the recent localization results of
[
71
]
. Lastly, one can consider the planar all orders in
1/\lambda
generalization of our result, as was done recently for the case of gluon scattering in
[
23
]
.

Appendix A
Free theory

In this appendix we discuss how to obtain some of the free theory results used in the main text. We are interested in correlation functions between the superconformal primaries of the super gluon and super graviton 1/2-BPS multiplets. These are built from products of the scalar fields in the fundamental and antisymmetric hypermultiplets of the theory.

There are eight fundamental half-hypermultiplets, so we have a set of complex scalar fields

\begin{split}\phi^{\alpha,a,i}(x)\,,\end{split}

(A.1)

where
\alpha\in\{1,2\}
is an
SU(2)_{R}
index,
a\in\{1,\ldots,2N\}
is a fundamental index of
USp(2N)
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,8\}
is a fundamental index of
SO(8)
. These scalars are subject to the reality condition

\begin{split}[\phi^{\alpha,i,a}(x)]^{*}=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\Omega_{ab}\phi^{\beta,j,b}(x)\,.\end{split}

(A.2)

We can contract the
SU(2)_{R}
index with a polarization tensor
y^{\alpha}
to define

\begin{split}\phi^{a,i}(x,y)=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\phi^{\alpha,i,a}(x)y^{\beta}\,,\end{split}

(A.3)

and the two-point function of
\phi
reads

\begin{split}\langle\phi^{i,a}(x_{1},y_{1})\phi^{j,b}(x_{2},y_{2})\rangle=\frac{y_{1}\cdot y_{2}}{x_{12}^{2}}\Omega^{ab}\delta^{ij}\,.\end{split}

(A.4)

On the other hand we also have two half-hypermultiplets in the antisymmetric traceless representation of
USp(2N)
, so another set of complex scalars

\begin{split}\psi^{\alpha,\bar{\alpha},[ab]}(x)\,,\end{split}

(A.5)

where
\bar{\alpha}\in\{1,2\}
is an
SU(2)_{L}
index and
\psi
is
\Omega
-traceless:

\begin{split}\Omega_{ab}\psi^{\alpha,\bar{\alpha},ab}(x)=0\,.\end{split}

(A.6)

We again have a reality condition

\begin{split}[\psi^{\alpha,\bar{\alpha},ab}(x)]^{*}=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\psi^{\beta,\bar{\beta},ab}(x)\,,\end{split}

(A.7)

and we contract these scalars with
SU(2)_{R}
polarizations
y^{\alpha}
as well as
SU(2)_{L}
polarizations
\bar{y}^{\bar{\alpha}}
, defining

\begin{split}\psi^{ab}(x,y,\bar{y})=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\psi^{\alpha,\bar{\alpha},ab}(x)y^{\beta}\bar{y}^{\bar{\beta}}\,.\end{split}

(A.8)

Finally, the two-point function between such scalars is

\begin{split}\langle\psi^{ab}(x_{1},y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})\psi^{cd}(x_{2},y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})\rangle=\frac{(y_{1}\cdot y_{2})(\bar{y}_{1}\cdot\bar{y}_{2})}{x_{12}^{2}}\left(\Omega^{a[c|}\Omega^{b|d]}-\frac{1}{2N}\Omega^{ab}\Omega^{cd}\right)\,.\end{split}

(A.9)

We are then ready to define the superprimaries of the supergluon and supergraviton multiplets. For the former, we set

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}^{A}_{p}(x,y,\bar{y})=\mathcal{N}_{p}\,\phi^{a_{0},i}(x,y)\Omega_{a_{0}a_{1}}\psi^{a_{1}b_{1}}(x,y,\bar{y})\Omega_{b_{1}a_{2}}\ldots\psi^{a_{p-2}b_{p-2}}(x,y,\bar{y})\Omega_{b_{p-2}a_{p-1}}\phi^{a_{p-1},j}(x,y)T^{A}_{ij}\,,\end{split}

(A.10)

where
T^{A}_{ij}
are
SO(8)
generators in the fundamental representations, for which we follow the same conventions as
[
45
]
, while
\mathcal{N}_{p}
is a normalization factor which is computed in such a way that the two-point functions are unit-normalized:

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{O}^{A}_{p}(x_{1},y_{1})\mathcal{O}^{B}_{q}(x_{2},y_{2})\rangle=(12)^{p}(\bar{y}_{1}\cdot\bar{y}_{2})^{p-2}\delta_{pq}\delta^{AB}\,,\quad(ij)\equiv\frac{(y_{i}\cdot y_{j})}{x_{ij}^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(A.11)

On the other hand, for the gravitons we set

\begin{split}\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{p}}(x,y,\bar{y})=\mathbf{N}_{\mathbf{p}}\psi^{a_{1}b_{1}}(x,y,\bar{y})\Omega_{b_{1}a_{2}}\psi^{a_{2}b_{2}}(x,y,\bar{y})\Omega_{b_{2}a_{3}}\ldots\psi^{a_{p}b_{p}}(x,y,\bar{y})\Omega_{b_{p}a_{1}}\,,\end{split}

(A.12)

where again
\mathbf{N}_{\mathbf{p}}
is a normalization factor which sets

\begin{split}\langle\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{p}}(x_{1},y_{1},\bar{y}_{1})\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{q}}(x_{2},y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})\rangle=(12)^{p}(\bar{y}_{1}\cdot\bar{y}_{2})^{p}\delta_{pq}\,.\end{split}

(A.13)

After computing the two normalizations for a few values of
p
, we are able to evaluate certain three- and four-point functions. For three-point functions of supergluons we find

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{O}^{A}_{p_{1}}(x_{1},y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})&\mathcal{O}^{B}_{p_{2}}(x_{2},y_{2},\bar{y}_{2})\mathcal{O}^{C}_{p_{1}}(x_{3},y_{3},\bar{y}_{3})\rangle\\
&=C_{p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}}f^{ABC}(12)^{p_{1}+p_{2}-p_{3}}(23)^{p_{2}+p_{3}-p_{1}}(13)^{p_{1}+p_{3}-p_{2}}\\
&\times(\bar{y}_{1}\cdot\bar{y}_{2})^{p_{1}+p_{2}-p_{3}-2}(\bar{y}_{2}\cdot\bar{y}_{3})^{p_{2}+p_{3}-p_{1}-2}(\bar{y}_{1}\cdot\bar{y}_{3})^{p_{1}+p_{3}-p_{2}-2}\,,\end{split}

(A.14)

where
f^{ABC}
are
SO(8)
structure constants and we have checked for various values of
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
that

\begin{split}C_{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2N}}+O(1/N^{3/2})\,,\end{split}

(A.15)

where the leading order does not depend on the external weights. We observe that the leading order result (first derived in
[
8
]
from bootstrap considerations) is exact when at least one of the three operators is the moment map (
p=2

\begin{split}\langle\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{2}}(1)\mathcal{O}_{p}(2)^{A}\mathcal{O}_{p}(3)^{B}\rangle=C_{\mathbf{2},p,p}\delta^{AB}(12)(13)(23)^{p-1}(\bar{y}_{1}\cdot\bar{y}_{2})(\bar{y}_{1}\cdot\bar{y}_{3})(\bar{y}_{2}\cdot\bar{y}_{3})^{p-3}\,,\end{split}

(A.16)

where we find that

\begin{split}C_{\mathbf{2},p,p}=\frac{p-2}{\sqrt{2k_{SU(2)_{L}}}}\,.\end{split}

(A.17)

We can also compute the free theory correlators
\langle\mathbf{2}2pp\rangle

\begin{split}G_{\mathbf{2}2pp}^{(\text{free})}(U,V,\alpha)=\frac{2(p-2)}{\sqrt{k_{SO(8)}k_{SU(2)_{L}}}}\frac{U^{2}}{V}\alpha(\alpha-1)\,,\end{split}

(A.18)

which at leading order in the large

expansion becomes

\begin{split}G_{\mathbf{2}2pp}^{(\text{free})}(U,V,\alpha)=\frac{(p-2)}{\sqrt{2}N^{3/2}}\frac{U^{2}}{V}\alpha(\alpha-1)+O(1/N^{5/2})\,.\end{split}

(A.19)

Last, we look at the correlator
\langle\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp\rangle
, for which we recall that there are three functions corresponding to the three

\begin{split}G_{\mathbf{2}\mathbf{2}pp}^{(12),(\text{free})}(U,V,\alpha)&=1+\frac{(p-2)U}{2N^{2}V}[1+\alpha(V-1)+(p-3)\alpha(1-\alpha)U]+O(1/N^{3})\,,\\

&\hskip 85.35826pt+2(p-3)^{2}\alpha^{2}UV]+O(1/N^{3})\,,\\

&\hskip 85.35826pt+2(p-3)^{2}(1-\alpha)^{2}U]+O(1/N^{3})\,.\end{split}

(A.20)

(B.1)

(B.13)

\begin{split}J_{\mu}(x)J_{\nu}(x)=\int\frac{ds}{2\pi i}\frac{\Gamma(-s)\Gamma(2s+\mu+\nu+1)\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{\mu+\nu+2s}}{\Gamma(s+\mu+1)\Gamma(s+\nu+1)\Gamma(s+\mu+\nu+1)}\,,\end{split}

(B.14)

and

\begin{split}J_{\nu}(x)=\int\frac{dt}{2\pi i}\frac{\Gamma(-t)\left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)^{\nu+2t}}{\Gamma(v+t+1)}\,.\end{split}

(B.15)

This allows us to compute the integrals in
v
and
w
by applying the following formula

\begin{split}\zeta(2n+1)=\frac{1}{4\Gamma(2n+2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega\,\omega^{2n+1}}{\sinh^{2}(\omega/2)}\,.\end{split}

(B.16)

The infinite sum over
\ell

\ell

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}l^{-s}=\zeta(s)
. We can now close the integration contour in

1/\ell
expansion of the sum justified.

This procedure fails at order

because F
2

\begin{split}\mathrm{F}_{2}=\mathrm{F}_{2}^{\text{easy}}-\mathrm{F}_{2}^{\text{hard}}\end{split}

(B.17)

with

\begin{split}\mathrm{F}_{2}^{\text{hard}}\equiv 16\frac{\lambda_{\text{UV}}}{\pi^{2}}\!\!\int\!\!\frac{dw\,w^{2}}{\sinh^{2}\!w}\frac{dv\,v^{3}}{\sinh^{2}\!v}\frac{d\omega}{\sinh^{2}\!\omega}\chi(v,w)\left(2\chi(\omega,v)-\chi(2\omega,v)\right),\end{split}

(B.18)

the presence of a nested triple integral complicates the derivation of the large

However, we observe that taking a derivative with respect to

Appendix C
Flat space amplitudes

In this appendix we compute flat space scattering amplitudes with two external gluons and two external gravitons, focusing on the tree level exchange of a graviton (the flat space equivalent of
G^{(R)}
in (
1.2
)) and on a loop diagram with only gluons running in the loop (corresponding to
G^{(R|F^{2})}
in (
1.2
)). Rather than attempting a direct computation, we use the double copy relations
[
73
]
. In particular, following
[
74
,
75
,
42
]
we compute scattering amplitudes in supersymmetric Einstein-Yang-Mills (EYM) theory from the product between YM amplitudes on one side and YM
+\phi^{3}
theory on the other. The latter is a non supersymmetric theory describing the interaction between a YM gauge field with gauge group
\tilde{G}_{1}
and a scalar field
\phi
in the biadjoint representation of
\tilde{G}_{1}\times\tilde{G}_{2}
, where
\tilde{G}_{2}
is a flavor group – see
[
75
]
for details. In particular, to obtain an amplitude between two gluons and two gravitons, we consider the product between a four-gluon amplitude in SYM theory and an amplitude between two gluons and two scalars in YM
+\phi^{3}

Note that we are not interested in the most general configuration of momenta and polarizations for the external fields, rather in the case where the polarizations of gluons and gravitons are transverse to the momenta of all particles involved. This simplifies the calculations and intermediate expressions, and in what follows we only reproduce results in this special kinematical configuration.

C.1
Tree level

The first amplitude that we are interested in is a graviton exchange in the
s
-channel at tree level, which appears at
O(\kappa^{2})
.
13
13

13

To compute it using the double copy relations, we consider first the gluon exchange amplitude between four external gluons in SYM theory, given for example in

(C.1)

where

\begin{split}n_{s}&=-(\epsilon_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{2})(\epsilon_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{4})(u+\alpha s)\,,\\
n_{t}&=(\epsilon_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{2})(\epsilon_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{4})(1-\alpha)(u+\alpha s)\,,\\
n_{u}&=(\epsilon_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{2})(\epsilon_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{4})\alpha(u+\alpha s)\,,\end{split}

(C.2)

where

\begin{split}\alpha^{2}=\frac{(\epsilon_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{3})(\epsilon_{2}\cdot\epsilon_{4})}{(\epsilon_{1}\cdot\epsilon_{2})(\epsilon_{3}\cdot\epsilon_{4})}\,,\end{split}

(C.3)

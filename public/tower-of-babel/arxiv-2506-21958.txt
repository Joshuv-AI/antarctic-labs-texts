Terminal Fano four folds in low codimension
Muhammad Imran Qureshi

Abstract
. We construct well-formed and quasismooth terminal Fano 4-folds of index 1 in low codimension containing at worst isolated orbifold points. We provide a certain classification of these varieties where their images under the anitcanonical embedding can be described as codimension 2, 3, or 4 subvarieties of some weighted projective space. In particular, we focus on isolated terminal Fano 4-folds that either have an empty linear system or a relatively large one, but whose linear section is not an isolated canonical Calabi–Yau 3-fold. In total, we classify 95 families of terminal Fano 4-folds of the first type and 32 families of the second type. We also describe our algorithmic approach and the pivotal role of computer algebra in our results.

1.1.
Terminal Fano varieties

A
Fano variety

X
is an algebraic variety with an ample anitcanonical divisor class
-K_{X}
.
The
Fano index

i
is defined as the largest integer
i
such that the anitcanonical divisor class
-K_{X}
can be written as
-K_{X}=i\cdot D
for some
{\mathbb{Q}}
-ample Weil divisor
D
on
X
. A
quotient singularity

of type
\frac{1}{r}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is a quotient of
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
by a

\mu_{r}
of order
r

\epsilon:x_{i}\mapsto e^{a_{i}}x_{i},\;i=1,\ldots,n,

\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\overline{ka_{i}}}>1\text{ for all }k=1,\ldots,r-1.

If

it is called a terminal Fano variety.

1.3.
Context and Motivation

One way to look at our results as a natural extension of already existing
classification results of terminal Fano 3-folds and 4-folds. However, the main motivation to study these two classes of ITF4s is to compare these with the existing geography of 3-dimensional case in codimension
\leq 4
, available on the graded ring database
[
BK22
]
. We make the comparison for each type of ITF4s separately.

If we compare the type-
K_{0}
case then in codimension
\leq 4
, there are only four examples of terminal
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Fano 3-folds with the empty linear system. One of these is a codimension 2 weighted complete intersection and 3 of them can be described as regular pullbacks in some cluster Gorenstein format
[
CD20
]
. But in dimension four, we notice that there is a large set of such terminal Fano 4-folds in each codimension. In total, we obtain 95 examples of such 4-folds in this article. There is no example in 3-fold case with
h^{0}(-2K_{X})=0
but in dimension 4 we have an example
4.1
of codimension 2 terminal Fano 4-fold with where first four plurigenera are zero, i.e.
h^{0}(-lK_{X})=0,\text{ for }1\leq l\leq 4.
We provide the detailed results
of type-
K_{0}
for each format in Table

.

For the comparison in the type-
K_{2}
case, recall that a terminal

-Fano 3-fold

but a generic linear section is not a canonical Calabi–Yau 3-fold (cCY3) with isolated orbrifold points. This phenomenon is relatively widespread and we focus on such cases with
h^{0}(-K_{X})\geq 2.
In total
we obtain at least 32 such families of ITF4s and we provide a detailed division
in various formats in Table

In this section, we provide some basic definitions and the necessary background

A weighted projective variety

It is called

if the affine cone over
\tilde{X}

smooth outside the vertex of the ambient affine space.
A

is roughly a way of representing the equations of varieties systematically. For example, the Segre embedding of

Definition 2.1
.

[

]

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

We only consider those Gorenstein formats where the action

Flowchart of the proof

In this section, we describe all the ingredients required to prove the Theorem

3.1.
Algorithm for candidate isolated orbifolds

This section reviews the algorithm from
[
Qur17
]
, which we utilized to generate the list of candidate terminal Fano 4-folds. A key component is the orbifold Riemann–Roch formula of Buckley–Reid–Zhou
[
BRZ13
]
, which provides a formula to decompose the Hilbert series
P_{X}(t)
of
X
as a sum of a smooth component and an orbifold component. It states that if an orbifold
X
contains a set
\mathcal{B}=\{k_{i}\times Q_{i}:m_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\}
of isolated orbifold points, then:

(7)

P_{X}(t)=P_{\mathrm{smooth}}(t)+\sum_{Q_{i}\in\mathcal{B}}k_{i}P_{Q_{i}}(t),

where
P_{\mathrm{smooth}}
denotes the smooth part, and
\sum_{Q_{i}\in\mathcal{B}}k_{i}P_{Q_{i}}(t)
represents the orbifold component of the Hilbert Series.

The algorithm generates a comprehensive list of orbifolds with a specified Fano index, dimension, and canonical class
K_{X}=\mathcal{O}(kD)
for
a fixed value of adjunction number, determined by the Hilbert series. We outline the algorithm steps for Fano 4-folds below.

(i)

Determine the Hilbert series and canonical class of the ambient
weighted projective variety.

(ii)

Identify all possible embeddings in
{\mathbb{P}}^{8}(w_{i})
with index
i
, i.e.,
K_{X}=\mathcal{O}(-i)
, by enumerating weights and utilizing the adjunction formula.

(iii)

For each embedding from step (ii), calculate the Hilbert series of
X
and determine the term representing its smooth component
P_{\mathrm{smooth}}(t)
.

(iv)

Compile a list of all potential terminal orbifold points from the weights of
{\mathbb{P}}^{8}(w_{i})
that could be on
X
.

(v)

Form all potential subsets of orbifold points, and for each set
\mathcal{B}
, assess whether the difference
P_{X}(t)-P_{\mathrm{smooth}}(t)
can equate to
\sum_{Q_{i}\in\mathcal{B}}k_{i}P_{Q_{i}}(t)
for some
k_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(vi)

X
qualifies as a candidate terminal Fano 4-fold with a basket of singularities
\mathcal{B}
if each
k_{i}\geq 0
. Repeat steps (iii) to (vi) for each embedding determined in step (ii).

3.2.
Existence, consistency and wellformedness

Here we provide a brief overview from
[
Qur19
]
where more detailed explanation can be found. The existence of each candidate Fano 4-fold
X
of index 1 as an algebraic variety follows as the equations are obtained from the equations of the
given Gorenstein
format. Then we check for consistency of the candidate Fano 4-fold with the given Hilbert series decomposition; that is, we verify that
X
contains the exact set of terminal singularities predicted by algorithm
3.1
. This requires
computing the intersection of
X
with each singular toric stratum of
the ambient weighted projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}(w_{i})
, by using the computer algebra system
MAGMA
. Since we allow at worst isolated orbifold points, the dimension of each intersection must be less than or equal to zero. Thus, consistency also implies that
X
is well-formed. A candidate 4-fold fails if either there is an isolated orbifold that is non-terminal or it contains some higher dimensional orbifold locus of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}(w_{i})
.

3.3.
Proving Quasismoothness

The key and relatively harder step is to prove the quasismoothness of the candidate Fano 4-fold. Since
taking complete intersection with a form of degree
d
induces a base locus
of the linear system
|\mathcal{O}(d)|
that can be quite large due to the intricate
combinatorics of weights and equation degrees of
X
. By a version of Bertini’s theorem, a general member is quasismooth outside of the reduced part of the base locus. If the base locus happens to be zero dimensional then we can use theoretical arguments to prove quasismoothness. In most cases, the base locus is complicated and higher dimensional, so we use the computer algebra system
Magma

[
BCP97
]
to prove the quasismoothness of
X
by writing down sparse
representations of its explicit
equations and then apply the Jacobian criteron.

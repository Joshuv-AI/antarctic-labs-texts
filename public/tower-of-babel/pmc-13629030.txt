Heat Transfer Enhancement in a Shell-and-Tube Type of Heat Exchanger Using Al2O3/Water Nanofluid
Authors: Manuel Anthony Toribio Pacherres Uchalin, Edwin Santiago Rios Escalante, Luis Fernando Junior Saldaña Bernuy, Gustavo Henrique Romeu da Silva, Andrés Z. Mendiburu, Roberto C. Chucuya Huallpachoque, Alex M. Bimbato
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Shell-and-tube heat exchangers are widely used in industrial processes, but improving the shell-side heat transfer without causing excessive pressure losses remains a major engineering challenge. Numerical simulations provide an efficient approach for evaluating thermohydraulic enhancement techniques while reducing experimental costs. The present study evaluates the heat transfer in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger using computational fluid dynamics by means of ANSYS Fluent software. Unlike previous numerical studies that mainly focused on thermal enhancement, this work provides a comprehensive thermohydraulic assessment by simultaneously evaluating heat-transfer improvement together with the associated pressure-drop and pumping-power penalties over different nanoparticle concentrations and mass flow rates. Therefore, a simplified heat-exchanger model was developed with six baffles operating under turbulent flow conditions. The working fluid in the shell is initially water, which is subsequently replaced by nanofluid composed of water and 15 nm Al2O3 nanoparticles, while the inner tube walls are kept at a constant temperature. The results show that the Al2O3/water nanofluid significantly enhances the heat transfer. Thermal conductivity and nanoparticle concentrations play a crucial role in this enhancement. The largest increase in heat transfer, 37.83%, occurs at a nanoparticle concentration of 1.25% at mass flow rates of 0.5 kg/s, 0.6 kg/s, and 0.7 kg/s. Lower concentrations result in 30.76% (concentration of 1.00%), 23.32% (concentration of 0.75%), 15.47% (concentration of 0.50%), and 7.16% (concentration of 0.25%) improvements compared to water. Similarly, the heat transfer coefficient increases by 47.84%, 38.32%, 28.62%, 18.70%, and 8.51%, for concentrations of 1.25%, 1.00%, 0.75%, 0.50%, and 0.25%, respectively. However, the use of nanofluids leads to an increase in the pressure drop, with maximum values reaching 5.30%. The results demonstrate that dilute Al2O3/water nanofluids can significantly enhance shell-side thermal performance while maintaining relatively low pressure drop and pumping power penalties, highlighting their potential as a practical thermo-hydraulic enhancement strategy for shell-and-tube heat exchangers and providing useful guidance for the design and optimization of industrial heat exchangers.

Introduction

In the past decade, increasing energy, the depletion of fossil fuels (such as oil, coal, and natural gas), climate change, atmospheric pollution, and the energy crisis have driven significant research on optimizing energy use to enhance efficiency and lower losses. , One of the principal approaches involves improving heat-transfer technologies.

Heat exchangers are essential components in energy transfer and conversion systems, widely used in industries such as oil and gas, manufacturing, petrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, HVAC, and refrigeration. Among them, the shell-and-tube heat exchanger is the most commonly used type.

The shell-and-tube heat exchangers are commonly carried out in accordance with the standards established by the Tubular Exchanger Manufacturers Association (TEMA). For shell-side flow analysis, correlations such as the Kern method and Bell-Delaware approach are widely adopted due to their proven effectiveness. However, it is important to keep in mind that these correlations are based on physical principles, experimental data, and simplifications and therefore may not apply to all situations. For this reason, it is essential to validate the results obtained by using these correlations with specific experimental results. In the meantime, the use of numerical simulations can help to reduce the number of experimental tests, making experimental analyses less costly and time-consuming, which justifies the relevance of the study presented here.

According to Nakhchi and Esfahani, heat exchangers are essential devices for achieving energy savings in industrial processes. On the other hand, the thermophysical properties of the fluids commonly used in this equipment for heat transfer purposes are some of the fundamental factors that influence the heat exchange capacity of these fluids, which, in turn, are not always within the desired parameters. Therefore, given the constraints related to the dimensions, market value, and thermophysical properties of working fluids, it is essential to improve the performance of existing heat exchangers and to design new units that are lighter, more compact, and, consequently, less expensive.

In order to overcome this condition and increase heat transfer, it is possible to change the boundary conditions, make geometric modifications to the equipment, or apply external agents. One of the most widespread methods for implementing the last alternative is to improve the thermophysical properties of the working fluid by adding nanoparticles. The result of this mixture is called a nanofluid.

Nanofluids are composed of solid particles of metallic type, metal oxides, carbon-based nanomaterials such as carbon nanotubes, CNTs, or nonmetallic oxides, of nanometric size (less than 100 nm in diameter), which are dispersed in fluids used as coolants, such as water, ethylene glycol, propylene glycol, R600 refrigerant, or oil. This new fluid is used to improve the performance of thermal devices, such as heat exchangers, ,− without altering their primary geometry. − This use is justified by the low thermal conductivity of traditional refrigerants.

The principal reason for this improvement in thermal energy exchange capacity is that this mixture has a combined effect of the properties of the base fluid and the nanoparticles. However, the effectiveness of nanofluids in promoting heat transfer can also be influenced by other factors, such as gravity, Brownian motion, Brownian diffusion, the friction force between the fluid and the nanoparticles, sedimentation, dispersion, and stratification at the solid/liquid interface; these factors may simultaneously interact within the main flow of this fluid. Consequently, several studies have focused on identifying the mechanisms that influence heat transfer in nanofluids while demonstrating that an improvement in thermal conductivity alone is not sufficient to explain the performance gains observed in the process.

Nanofluids formulated with Al2O3 nanoparticles have attracted significant attention due to their superior thermal characteristics, high productivity, and stability. For instance, Main et al. investigated the thermal conductivity of trifluoromethylsulfonyl (as base fluid) and the thermal conductivity after the addition of Al2O3 nanoparticles at a 1.0% volumetric concentration, diameters of 10 nm, 30, 60, and 90 nm and varying temperatures (10 °C - 70 °C). The results showed that thermal conductivity increased by up to 14.63% when nanoparticles with a diameter of 10 nm were added at 70 °C.

Albadr et al. investigated the heat transfer performance of a shell-and-tube heat exchanger operating under countercurrent and turbulent flow conditions with Al2O3/distilled water nanofluids. Their findings indicated that dispersing nanoparticles into distilled water led to an enhancement in both the thermal conductivity and viscosity of the suspension, with the latter contributing significantly to the pressure drop. In addition, it was observed that the magnitudes of these effects increased with particle concentration. Moreover, when the nanoparticle concentration reached 2% by volume, the overall heat transfer coefficient improved by up to 57% compared to that of pure distilled water.

Another relevant contribution was presented by Oztürk, Tezcan, and Çiftçi, who performed a computational analysis on heat transfer enhancement in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger using Al2O3/water and CNT/water nanofluids at concentrations of 3%, 6%, and 9%. As expected, heat transfer increased with nanoparticle concentration. The maximum enhancement compared with pure water was approximately 50% and 62.5% at a concentration of 6% for Al2O3/water and CNT/water nanofluids, respectively. Although improvements were also observed at 9% concentration, the enhancement was reduced due to the dominant effect of viscosity at higher nanoparticle loadings, which negatively affects heat transfer, as reported in previous studies.

Similarly, Gkountas et al. evaluated heat transfer enhancement in a supercritical CO2 Brayton cycle printed circuit heat exchanger using an Al2O3/water nanofluid. The results showed that a maximum volume fraction of 5% nanoparticles resulted in a 10% improvement in the heat-transfer coefficient compared to pure water. This improvement allowed for an additional 0.9% reduction in the length of the heat exchanger, along with a decrease in the pressure drop of up to 14%.

The Al2O3/water nanofluid has attracted the attention of the academic community and the industrial sector because of its innovative properties. According to the literature review, several studies have focused on evaluating the heat transfer enhancement in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger through the use of this nanofluid. These studies show that volumetric concentrations of Al2O3/water greater than 1% have been employed in this type of equipment, showing positive results in the heat transfer process but with higher pressure drop penalties. Furthermore, although numerous studies have investigated nanofluids in heat exchangers, many have focused primarily on maximizing heat transfer enhancement without sufficiently addressing the thermohydraulic trade-off associated with increased pressure drop and pumping power requirements. Therefore, the present study focuses on evaluating the thermohydraulic performance of low-concentration Al2O3/water nanofluids under shell-side turbulent flow conditions, emphasizing the balance between heat transfer enhancement and hydraulic penalties in terms of pressure drop and pumping power requirements.

The novelty of the present work lies in the thermohydraulic evaluation of dilute Al2O3/water nanofluids with volume concentrations ranging from 0.25% to 1.25% under shell-side turbulent flow conditions in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger. Unlike many previous studies that primarily focused on heat transfer enhancement at higher nanoparticle concentrations, the present investigation jointly assesses thermal and hydraulic performance through the heat transfer rate, heat transfer coefficient, pressure drop, and pumping power. This approach provides practical design-oriented information regarding the feasibility of employing low-concentration nanofluids in industrial shell and tube heat exchangers.

For this purpose, a shell-and-tube heat exchanger operating with water and subsequently with Al2O3/water nanofluids at volumetric concentrations of 0.25%, 0.50%, 0.75%, 1.00%, and 1.25% was numerically investigated by using ANSYS Fluent 2024 R2. The sensitivity of the numerical results was assessed through a mesh independence study under water-only operating conditions. After an appropriate mesh was selected, simulations were conducted for three shell-side mass flow rates. The obtained results were then used to calculate the shell-side heat transfer coefficient and pressure drop and compared with the predictions of the Kern and Bell-Delaware correlations. Finally, the multiphase mixture model was employed to simulate the heat exchanger operating with a nanofluid.

Although several studies have investigated Al2O3/water nanofluids in heat exchangers, their practical implementation still requires detailed thermal-hydraulic evaluations under specific geometric and operating conditions. In this context, the novelty of the present work lies in the systematic CFD assessment of dilute Al2O3/water nanofluids in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger, considering not only heat-transfer enhancement but also pressure drop and pumping power. This combined analysis is relevant because the use of nanofluids is only advantageous when excessive hydraulic penalties do not offset the heat transfer improvement. Therefore, the present study contributes to identifying operating conditions under which dilute Al2O3/water nanofluids may provide a favorable compromise between thermal performance and pumping power requirements.

Recent studies have also demonstrated that passive geometric strategies, including twisted, corrugated, conical, and multihelical tube configurations, can intensify secondary flows and fluid mixing, thereby improving heat transfer and overall thermo-hydraulic performance. However, these thermal gains should be evaluated together with the resulting frictional losses, pressure drop, pumping requirements, and exergy behavior to determine whether the enhancement provides a favorable engineering compromise. −

Methodology

In computational fluid dynamics (CFD), the analysis of a system generally consists of three important stages: preprocessing, processing, and postprocessing. Next comes the processing stage, in which the materials are specified, boundary conditions are imposed, and governing equations are solved. Finally, the postprocessing stage, during which the results calculated by the software are obtained, is followed by an overview of the flow behavior. It is important to note that this three-step process is repeated, even under different conditions, in order to obtain a set of results that can be analyzed and compared. ,

The software used to carry out this process was ANSYS 2024 R2, executed on a dual liquid-cooled CPU computer with 12 cores, 24 threads, 64GB of memory, and a 3200 MHz frequency.

The commercial CFD software ANSYS Fluent was employed as the numerical solver to assess improvements in heat transfer and to quantify the associated pressure drop when using an Al2O3/water nanofluid as a coolant in a heat exchanger under turbulent-flow conditions. To validate the general CFD modeling strategy without nanofluids, a previous study by Uchalin, based on the work of Ozden and Tari, demonstrated the reliability of a comprehensive CFD representation of a laminar-flow heat exchanger by benchmarking the predictions against experimental measurements obtained from a small-scale educational unit containing two baffles. Similarly, to verify and support the general approach to CFD modeling including nanofluids, a previous study by Uchalin (2024), based on the work of Bianco et al., demonstrated the accuracy of a CFD model for a circular tube in a turbulent regime using nanofluids by correlating the outcomes with experimental data reported in the literature.

Although these validation studies provide confidence in the adopted numerical methodology, direct experimental measurements for the specific shell-and-tube heat exchanger configuration analyzed in the present work were not available. Therefore, the comparisons with the Kern and Bell–Delaware methods should be interpreted as a benchmarking and verification procedure rather than as a substitute for direct experimental validation.

Meshing and Mesh Independence Test

The mesh generation was carried out using Fluent meshing. The mesh elements generated for the entire computational domain are of the unstructured hybrid type, as the automatic polyhedral meshing method was applied. Regarding the mesh structure, the shell-side fluid domain was discretized using polyhedral cells to improve cell connectivity and reduce the total number of elements, thereby optimizing computational efficiency. Since this is a conjugate heat transfer problem (solid–fluid interaction), local mesh refinement was applied around the tubes and baffle surfaces, where strong velocity and temperature gradients are expected. In addition, prismatic boundary-layer elements were generated adjacent to the tube walls and baffle surfaces to improve the resolution of the near-wall flow and thermal fields. Although a target y + value was not prescribed a priori during mesh generation, prismatic boundary-layer elements were introduced near the tube walls and baffles to improve the resolution of near-wall velocity and temperature gradients. The adopted near-wall refinement, combined with the mesh-independence analysis and the agreement obtained with the Bell–Delaware method, indicates that the selected mesh provides a reasonable compromise between numerical accuracy and computational cost. Nevertheless, a dedicated y + -based wall-treatment sensitivity analysis was not performed and is acknowledged as a limitation of the present study. Furthermore, this study focused exclusively on the influence of nanoparticle concentration on the performance of the shell and tube heat exchanger. Other important factors affecting nanofluid behavior, such as nanoparticle size, dispersion stability, sedimentation, and agglomeration effects, were not explicitly modeled or analyzed. For instance, smaller nanoparticles may enhance the thermal properties of nanofluids and consequently improve heat transfer. In contrast, larger nanoparticles, higher pressure drops, and increased pumping power represent important challenges for future research. ,

However, the investigated concentration range was limited to dilute conditions to minimize excessive agglomeration and stability-related effects commonly observed at higher nanoparticle concentrations.

It should be noted that the mixture model adopted in this study provides a computationally efficient framework for simulating dilute nanofluid suspensions. Nevertheless, this approach assumes local equilibrium between the base fluid and nanoparticles and does not explicitly capture complex phenomena such as particle agglomeration, sedimentation dynamics, or other concentration-dependent interactions that may become significant at higher nanoparticle loadings. Consequently, the present results should be interpreted within the scope of dilute and relatively stable nanofluid suspensions.

Turbulence Model and Multiphase Model

At present, there is no universally accepted complete theory of turbulence. However, over the past half-century, following the pioneering theoretical contributions of Prandtl, Kolmogorov, and Von Karman, numerous theoretical and computational strategies have been developed to characterize this phenomenon. These strategies are employed depending on the problem being addressed and the computational resources available. Among the existing numerical approaches for solving turbulent flows are the Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS), the Large Eddy Simulation (LES), and Reynolds Averaged Navier–Stokes Equations (RANS), the latter of which is the strategy adopted in this study. To close the turbulence problem, it is necessary to compute the turbulent viscosity (μ t ). In the present study, this parameter is calculated using the κ - ε realizable two-equation model developed by Shih et al., where κ represents the turbulent kinetic energy and ε the rate of dissipation of turbulent kinetic energy. This model was selected due to its accuracy in preliminary tests, good convergence behavior, and relatively low memory requirements, making it a suitable alternative for simulating turbulent flows and heat transfer. It should be noted that comparative tests were conducted using different turbulence models, including the standard k–ε and Spalart–Allmaras models, as well as first- and second-order discretization schemes and three mesh refinement levels. Cases employing the standard k–ε and Spalart–Allmaras models were discarded because they produced physically inconsistent results. In some cases, the shell-side outlet temperature increased with increasing mass flow rate, leading to unacceptable predictions of the total heat transfer rate. In other cases, although the outlet temperature trend was physically consistent, the deviation in the predicted total heat transfer rate relative to the Bell–Delaware method increased as the mass flow rate decreased. These discrepancies were even more pronounced when compared with the Kern method results, as previously discussed in this study.

In this work, the pressure–velocity coupled scheme was applied for solving the governing equations, a method widely recognized for its robustness and efficiency in steady-state simulations. Preliminary evaluations confirmed that it outperformed the segregated approach, which justified its adoption in this study. Regarding spatial discretization, a second order upwind scheme was employed for both convective and diffusive terms acting on pressure in a single-phase case, whereas the PRESTO scheme was adopted when simulating nanofluid flows with the multiphase model. Second-order schemes were also adopted for momentum, energy, turbulent kinetic energy, and turbulent energy dissipation rate. In contrast, the volume fraction of the secondary phase was discretized using a first-order upwind scheme. These discretization schemes were chosen due to their simplicity, rapid convergence, improved accuracy, and lower susceptibility to numerical oscillations. Since there is currently no universally accepted convergence criterion for numerical flow simulations, residual monitoring is commonly adopted as a reliable indicator during the iterative solution process. In the present study, convergence criteria of 10–3 were imposed for the continuity, momentum, turbulent kinetic energy, turbulent dissipation rate, and nanoparticle volume fraction equations (when operating with nanofluid), while a stricter criterion of 10–6 was applied to the energy equation.

In addition to residual monitoring, the convergence of the simulations was verified through the stabilization of global thermohydraulic parameters, including the shell-side outlet temperature, total heat transfer rate, and pressure drop, which exhibited negligible variation with further iterations.

Although the mixture model has been widely employed for nanofluid simulations because of its relatively low computational cost, its applicability is generally more appropriate for dilute nanoparticle suspensions. Previous studies have shown that the model provides satisfactory predictions at low nanoparticle concentrations, whereas its accuracy may deteriorate as the nanoparticle loading increases. ,, This limitation arises because the mixture model primarily accounts for drag interactions between the continuous and dispersed phases and does not explicitly consider particle–particle interactions, agglomeration phenomena, or collision effects, which may become increasingly important at higher nanoparticle concentrations. Since the present study is restricted to Al2O3/water nanofluids with volume concentrations of up to 1.25%, the mixture model is considered an appropriate compromise between computational efficiency and predictive capability.

Heat Transfer Rate of Al2O3/Water Nanofluid

Figure shows the variation in the heat transfer rate as a function of mass flow rates for five different concentrations of nanofluids.

Heat transfer rate as a function of mass flow rate for different concentrations of Al2O3/water nanofluid.

Figure shows that the higher the volume fraction of nanoparticles, the higher the heat-transfer rate tends to be. The most significant improvements are observed at a nanoparticle volume concentration of 1.25%, resulting in an average increase of 37.83% compared with pure water at the three different mass flow rates. For lower concentrations, the average increases in heat transfer are 7.16% at 0.25%, 15.47% at 0.50%, 23.32% at 0.75%, and 30.76% at 1.00%. These significant improvements are mainly associated with the synergistic influence of the improved thermal properties of the base fluid and the dispersed nanoparticles that form the nanofluid. However, the degree of improvement also depends on the type of nanoparticles used, their specific characteristics, their volumetric concentration, and the flow operating conditions.

In addition, the analysis shows that the convective heat transfer rate of nanofluids rises as the mass flow rate increases. The percentage difference between the lowest and highest mass flow rates reaches approximately 35.64% for the nanofluid with a nanoparticle volume concentration of 1.25%. These results indicate that nanofluids exhibit a considerably higher efficiency in strongly turbulent flow regimes. This improvement is mainly attributed to the chaotic motion of water molecules and nanoparticles, which promotes better mixing between the two phases, thereby enhancing the transport of momentum, heat, and mass and ultimately improving heat transfer. This finding is consistent with the results reported by Ghazanfari et al. and Cruz et al.

The influence of different nanofluid concentrations on the heat transfer coefficient is illustrated in Figure .

Heat transfer coefficient as a function of mass flow rate for different concentrations of Al2O3/water nanofluid.

Figure shows that an increase in the mass flow rate leads to an improvement in the heat-transfer coefficient. This behavior is attributed to higher flow velocities, which reduce the thermal boundary layer thickness, thereby producing a sharper temperature gradient. Consequently, the reduced conductive resistance within this thinner layer increases the temperature difference between the tube wall and the bulk fluid, promoting a more homogeneous temperature profile near the heat-exchange interface and resulting in more efficient heat transfer.

In addition, an increase in the heat-transfer coefficient is observed as the concentration of nanoparticles increases. This effect is primarily linked to the improvement in thermal conductivity since convective heat exchange depends on the capacity of the colder fluid to uptake heat when exposed to a hotter surface. The presence of nanoparticles improves the thermal conductivity of the coolant, thereby facilitating a more efficient heat transfer. However, the highest percentage increase occurs across all nanofluid mass flow rates when the nanoparticle volume concentration is 1.25%, as shown in Figure . Compared with water, this increase corresponds to an average of 47.84%. Additionally, for lower concentrations, the increase is 8.51% at 0.25%, 18.70% at 0.50%, 28.62% at 0.75%, and 38.32% at 1.00%.

Pressure Drop of Al2O3/Water Nanofluid

Figure shows the influence of variations in the nanofluid concentration on the pressure drop.

Pressure drops as a function of mass flow rate for different concentrations of Al2O3/water nanofluid.

In heat exchangers, pressure drop denotes the decrease in fluid pressure as it flows through the equipment. This reduction is caused by friction between the viscous fluid and the system walls, as well as internal friction losses between fluid layers. In turbulent flows  characterized by more intense and chaotic interactions between the fluid layers  higher resistances are generated, resulting in a greater pressure drop compared to laminar flows.

Figure also shows a slight increase in the pressure drop as the nanoparticle concentration increases. This pressure loss is attributed to the increase in viscosity since fluid viscosity is one of the primary causes of pressure drop, in addition to the increase in flow velocity. Because the nanoparticles significantly enhance this property, the effect becomes more pronounced. In addition, Figure shows that the highest percentage pressure drop occurs at a nanoparticle volume concentration of 1.25%. Compared with pure water, this corresponds to an average increase of 5.30%. For lower concentrations, the pressure drop increases by 1.72% at 0.25%, 2.61% at 0.50%, 3.51% at 0.75%, and 4.41% at 1.00% volume concentrations.

Furthermore, although the increase in pressure drop becomes more noticeable at higher nanoparticle concentrations, the corresponding impact on pumping power remains relatively moderate within the investigated operating conditions. To further evaluate the practical feasibility of the proposed nanofluid configurations, the pumping power requirement was analyzed as a function of both mass flow rate and nanoparticle concentration.

Figure illustrates the variation of the pumping power for the investigated cases. As expected, the pumping power increased with an increasing mass flow rate due to the higher pressure losses associated with greater flow velocities. Likewise, a slight increase in pumping power was observed as the nanoparticle concentration increased because of the corresponding increase in effective viscosity.

Pumping power of Al2O3/water nanofluid at different volume concentrations and mass flow rate.

However, the obtained hydraulic penalty remained comparatively small throughout the investigated concentration ranges. For instance, at a mass flow rate of 0.7 kg/s, the pumping power increased from 1,742W for water to 1,768W for the 1.25% nanofluid, corresponding to an increase of approximately 1.5%. This relatively small increase indicates that dilute Al2O3/water nanofluids can improve the heat transfer performance without imposing excessive additional energy consumption. From an industrial perspective, these results indicate a favorable thermohydraulic trade-off, since the thermal enhancement achieved with nanofluids was accompanied by only a limited increase in pumping power requirements. It should be noted that the increase in pumping power may be either minor or significant, depending on the nanoparticle concentration, mass flow rate, heat exchanger configuration, and type, as reported in previous studies. ,,,

Compared with recent studies employing passive geometric modifications, the present results indicate a favorable thermohydraulic compromise. At a nanoparticle volume concentration of 1.25%, the Al2O3/water nanofluid increased the heat transfer rate and heat transfer coefficient by 37.83% and 47.84%, respectively, while the pressure drop increased by only 5.30% and the pumping power by approximately 1.5%. Although direct quantitative comparisons should be interpreted cautiously because of differences in heat exchanger geometry, operating conditions, and performance indicators, the present trends are consistent with recent investigations of twisted, corrugated, conical, and multihelical configurations, which also reported improved thermal performance accompanied by frictional and hydraulic penalties. −

From an industrial perspective, the observed increase in the pressure drop remained relatively modest, even at the highest nanoparticle concentration investigated. The corresponding increase in pumping power was limited, suggesting that the thermal enhancement achieved by the nanofluid may be greater than the additional hydraulic requirements. Therefore, the proposed low-concentration Al2O3/water nanofluids could represent a feasible alternative for shell-and-tube heat exchangers, where improved thermal performance is desired without substantial increases in operating costs. Similar observations were reported by Singh, Verma, and Ghosh, who investigated the thermal performance of an industrial heat exchanger using graphene-based nanofluids at concentrations of 0.1%, 0.25%, 0.5%, 0.75%, and 1.0%. Their results showed that increasing the nanoparticle concentration led to higher pressure drops and, consequently, greater pumping requirements. However, the authors concluded that the resulting thermal enhancement was more significant than the associated hydraulic penalty and that this effect could be compensated for by operating the pump at a higher head. Nevertheless, they emphasized that the pressure drop remains an important design parameter, as it directly affects pumping requirements, piping design, energy consumption, and overall system cost. Therefore, further studies under different operating conditions and nanofluid formulations are required to better assess the industrial applicability of nanofluid-based heat-transfer systems.

It is important to note that the present thermohydraulic assessment is based on first-law performance indicators, namely, heat transfer rate, heat transfer coefficient, pressure drop, and pumping power. Although these quantities provide useful design-oriented information, they do not directly quantify the irreversibilities associated with heat transfer across finite-temperature differences and viscous dissipation. Entropy generation analysis has been increasingly recognized as an effective second-law-based method for evaluating the compromise between thermal enhancement and frictional losses in heat exchangers using advanced working fluids and passive enhancement techniques. Therefore, future studies should incorporate local and global entropy generation rates to identify the optimum nanoparticle concentration and operating conditions from a second law perspective. This approach has been emphasized in recent studies on enhanced heat-transfer systems and nanofluid-based thermal devices. ,

Analysis of Velocity, Pressure, and Temperature Fields Inside the Heat Exchanger Shell

The analysis of the velocity, pressure, and temperature contours extracted from the simulations carried out in the ANSYS Fluent software is based on sound physical principles. The velocity fields provide crucial information about the flow distribution and the formation of recirculations, while the pressure fields indicate variations in flow resistance along the heat exchanger. Temperature fields, on the other hand, offer detailed insights into the thermal gradients and heat transfer patterns within the system. This detailed visualization, supported by the scientific rigor of numerical simulation, enables an accurate assessment of the heat exchanger’s performance and informs the design of strategies for enhancing its thermal efficiency. Therefore, the objective of this section is to understand the flow behavior and thermal dynamics of the system, with the aim of gaining insights into how nanofluids influence the thermal performance of the heat exchanger.

Velocity Field Analysis

The fluid flow behavior along the heat exchanger is illustrated through contours in Figure (a), corresponding to pure water, and Figure (b), corresponding to the nanofluid with a 1.25% volume concentration.

It can be observed that the velocities at the shell inlet and outlet are higher because of the smaller cross-sectional area in these regions. Additionally, a reduction in flow velocity is observed near the baffles, as a no-slip condition is applied to the baffle walls. Furthermore, the addition of solid Al2O3 nanoparticles to the base fluid results in a decrease in the flow velocity, as indicated by the velocity scales. This phenomenon is attributed to the increase in shear stress between the nanoparticles and the water molecules, which leads to an increase in the viscosity of the resulting nanofluid and, consequently, a reduction in its flow velocity.

Figure shows similar streamlines between water and the nanofluids. This flow behavior is expected since the nanofluid was modeled as a homogeneous fluid. In this approach, the dispersed phase does not significantly alter the transport characteristics of the continuous phase.

Streamlines for the heat exchanger operating with (a) pure water and (b) nanofluid (1.25%) at a mass flow rate of 0.5 kg/s.

The recirculation zones can be identified through the streamlines, which contribute to an increase in the heat-transfer coefficient, although they are associated with notable pressure losses. These recirculation zones are formed due to the impact of the flow against the baffle walls and tend to be more pronounced at higher mass flow rates, enhancing the parameters associated with heat transfer. Furthermore, an increase in the mass flow rate enhances fluid momentum, which intensifies shear stresses and turbulence, thereby leading to a higher dissipation of mechanical energy as a pressure drop.

Physically, the flow is redirected to bypass the baffle surface (spreading upward, downward, or around it), where its velocity decreases near the stagnation zone, converting the kinetic energy into static pressure. This mechanism generates an adverse pressure gradient that induces boundary-layer separation along the solid surface of the baffle, resulting in the formation of recirculation zones on the downstream side of the six baffles.

Comparable recirculation zones appear in the streamlines of the other cases investigated. In practice, this fluid stagnation behind the baffles not only results in pressure drop penalties but also can induce corrosion on the tube walls. Consequently, mitigating the occurrence of these zones is critical. Furthermore, the formation of recirculation zones is strongly influenced by the heat exchanger configuration, particularly the baffle spacing and baffle cut.

Because of the shell-side arrangement and the inclusion of baffles, the velocity profile across these sections of the exchanger does not reach a fully developed state. Interestingly, the profiles for both water and nanofluid in all cases are similar, with the fluid near the outer tubes exhibiting a higher velocity gradient compared to that near the center of the shell. Conclusions

This work evaluated the impact of using Al2O3/water nanofluids on enhancing the heat-transfer rate in a shell-and-tube heat exchanger. It can be concluded that correlation-based approaches show an adequate agreement with the CFD results. While these correlations may suggest potential design deficiencies, CFD simulations enable the identification of their origins and locations. Furthermore, the Kern method systematically underestimates the heat transfer coefficient values, resulting in a larger percentage deviation compared with the CFD results. For this reason, the baseline comparisons (using water alone) were carried out using the Bell-Delaware method, which incorporates correction factors and therefore provides greater accuracy.

CFD analyses demonstrated that the addition of nanoparticles to the base fluid (water) significantly enhanced heat transfer within the heat exchanger due to their superior thermophysical properties, showing consistency with the findings reported in the literature. Specifically, the heat transfer rate increased by 7.16% at a nanoparticle volume concentration of 0.25% compared with pure water. For concentrations of 0.50%, 0.75%, 1.00%, and 1.25%, the corresponding increases were 15.47%, 23.32%, 30.76%, and 37.83%, respectively.

The maximum enhancement in the heat-transfer coefficient was achieved at a nanoparticle volume concentration of 1.25%, representing a 47.84% increase compared to that of pure water. Conversely, the highest pressure drop also occurred at the 1.25% concentration, resulting in a 5.30% penalty relative to that of the base fluid.

The generated temperature profiles provided valuable information about the effects of nanofluid properties on thermal behavior. According to the temperature profiles obtained from the cross-sectional contours of the shell, a uniform heating is observed from 0.015 m onward for both water and the nanofluid. However, these profiles indicate that the average temperature of the nanofluid is higher than that of water, which is attributed to its higher thermal conductivity and lower specific heat capacity, which facilitate greater energy absorption. This behavior is also evidenced by comparing the temperature profiles along the heat-exchanger length for different nanoparticle concentrations. It was observed that the nanofluid produced an outlet temperature higher than that of water, depending on the nanoparticle concentration, thereby enhancing the overall heat transfer performance.

It was observed that the highest pressure drop takes place at the heat-exchanger inlet, resulting from the geometric constraints of the inlet section. Furthermore, the number and configuration of the baffles contribute significantly to the total pressure drop because of the induced recirculation zones. For this reason, future studies could explore the impact of various geometric modifications as strategies to reduce the associated pressure drop. It was also verified that the pressure drop increases accordingly with increasing mass flow rate and nanoparticle concentration. This behavior is attributed to the intensification of turbulence driven by higher flow rates and the increase in fluid viscosity caused by the presence of the nanoparticles.

In addition, a regression model based on the CFD simulation results was proposed. It was observed that the R-squared value of the resulting model is 0.9998, indicating a high level of accuracy and reliability for the proposed regression under the geometry adopted in this study.

From an engineering perspective, the results demonstrated that dilute Al2O3/water nanofluids can enhance shell-side thermal performance while maintaining relatively low pressure drop and pumping power penalties. From a practical design perspective, low nanoparticle concentrations may therefore represent a more feasible operating condition for shell-and-tube heat exchangers than higher concentrations, which are commonly associated with an excessive viscosity increase and hydraulic resistance. The main contribution of this work lies in the combined evaluation of heat-transfer enhancement, pressure drop, and pumping power, demonstrating that low-concentration Al2O3/water nanofluids provide a favorable balance between thermal performance and hydraulic penalties under shell-side operating conditions. This integrated thermohydraulic assessment offers a more practical basis for the design and optimization of shell-and-tube heat exchangers than heat-transfer analysis alone. Future studies should extend the present work through experimental validation, entropy-generation analysis based on the second law of thermodynamics, the evaluation of alternative heat-exchanger geometries and hybrid nanofluids, and the assessment of industrial operating conditions to further identify optimal thermohydraulic performance.

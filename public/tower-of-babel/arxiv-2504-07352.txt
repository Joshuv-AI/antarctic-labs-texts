Interesting Deformed q q -Series Involving The Central Fibonomial Coefficient
Ronald Orozco López

Abstract
In this paper, we will obtain a variety of interesting q q -series containing central q q -binomial coefficients. Our approach is based on manipulating deformed basic hypergeometric series.

Deformed
q

1
Introduction

In his seminal paper
[
5
]
, Lehmer called an interesting series in case there is a simple explicit formula for its
n
-th term and at the same time its sum can be expressed in terms of known constants. Some interesting series are:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{2n}(2n-1)}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1+x}.

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4x}}.

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{1}{n+1}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2x}(1-\sqrt{1-4x}).

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{1}{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=2\ln\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4x}}{2x}\right).

(4)

\displaystyle x\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n(n+1)}x^{n}

\displaystyle=2x\ln\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4x}}{x}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{1-4x}}{2}+x(\ln 4-1)-\frac{1}{2}.

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\binom{2n}{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2x}{(1-4x)^{3/2}}.

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}\binom{2n}{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2x(2x+1)}{(1-4x)^{5/2}}.

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{1}{2n+1}x^{2n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\arcsin(2x).

(8)

The previous series belongs to a class called Lehmer series type I. A second type of Lehmer series is given by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{\binom{2n}{n}}.

(9)

Much research has been done on these series, especially on the second type, which tends to be more mysterious
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
. In this paper, we research deformed
q
-analogues of Lehmer’s series Eqs. (
1
)-(
8
), i.e.,
q
-series containing the central fibonomial coefficients

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{2n}{n}_{s,t}=\varphi_{s,t}^{n^{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{2n}{n}_{q},

(10)

where
q=\varphi_{s,t}^{\prime}/\varphi_{s,t}
. Here a
q
-series will be called interesting if its sum can be expressed in terms of deformed basic hypergeometric and
q
-shifted factorial. We will use some
q
-analogues of techniques applied by Lehmer in his paper: integration and the operator
\frac{xd}{dx}
. Among the deformed
q
-analogs, we find those of the Euler, Rogers-Ramanujan, and Exton types. All our deformed
q
-series are representations of deformed basic hypergeometric series
{}_{r}\Phi_{s}
(See
[
6
]
).

2
Preliminaries

2.1
q
-calculus

All notations and terminologies in this paper for basic hypergeometric series are in
[
4
]
. The
q
-shifted factorial be defined by

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }n=0;\\
\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-q^{k}a),&\text{ if }n\neq 0,\\
\end{cases}\hskip 28.45274ptq\in\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle(a;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(a;q)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{k}),\hskip 28.45274pt|q|<1.

The multiple
q
-shifted factorial is defined by

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m};q)_{n}

\displaystyle=(a_{1};q)_{n}(a_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{n},\hskip 8.5359ptq\in\mathbb{C}.

In this paper, we will frequently use the following identities:

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}},

(11)

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n+k}

\displaystyle=(a;q)_{n}(aq^{n};q)_{k},

(12)

\displaystyle(a;q)_{2n}

\displaystyle=(a;q^{2})_{n}(aq;q)_{n},

(13)

\displaystyle(a^{2};q^{2})_{n}

\displaystyle=(a;q)_{n}(-a;q)_{n},

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{1-q}{1-q^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q^{2};q)_{n}}.

(15)

In addition, we will use the identities for binomial coefficients:

\displaystyle\binom{n+k}{2}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{2}+\binom{k}{2}+nk,

\displaystyle\binom{n-k}{2}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{2}+\binom{k}{2}+k(1-n).

The
{}_{r}\phi_{s}
basic hypergeometric series is define by

{}_{r}\phi_{s}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{array};q,z\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{s};q)_{n}}\Big[(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}\Big]^{1+s-r}z^{n}.

In this paper, we will frequently use the
q
-binomial theorem:

{}_{1}\phi_{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a\\
-\end{array};q,z\right)=\frac{(az;q)_{\infty}}{(z;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}z^{n}.

(16)

The
q
-differential operator
D_{q}
is defined by:

D_{q}f(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(qx)}{x}.

The
q
-integral of a function
f(x)
defined on
[a,b]
is given by

\int_{a}^{b}f(x)d_{q}x=\int_{0}^{b}f(x)d_{q}x-\int_{0}^{a}f(x)d_{q}x,

(17)

where

\int_{0}^{a}f(x)d_{q}x=a(1-q)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f(aq^{n})q^{n}.

(18)

3
Deformed basic hypergeometric series

Orozco
[
6
]
defined the deformed basic hypergeometric series (DBHS)
{}_{r}\Phi_{s}
as

\displaystyle{}_{r}\Phi_{s}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}\end{array};q,u,z\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u^{\binom{n}{2}}\frac{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{n}}{(q,b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{s};q)_{n}}[(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}]^{1+s-r}z^{n}.

If
u=1
, then
{}_{r}\Phi_{s}={}_{r}\phi_{s}
and we call this DBHS Euler-I type. If
u=q
,

{}_{r+1}\Phi_{r}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{r+1}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\end{array};q,q,z\right)={}_{r+1}\phi_{r+1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r+1}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r},0\end{array};q,-z\right),

(19)

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and we call this DBHS Euler-II type.
If
u=q^{2}
and mapping
z\mapsto qz
,

{}_{r+1}\Phi_{r}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r+1}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\end{array};q,q^{2},qz\right)={}_{r+1}\phi_{r+2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r+1}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r},0,0\end{array};q,qz\right)

(20)

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and we call this DBHS Rogers-Ramanujan type. If
u=\sqrt{q}
,

\displaystyle{}_{r+1}\Phi_{r}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r+1}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\end{array};q,\sqrt{q},z\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt={}_{2r+2}\phi_{2r+2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\sqrt{a_{1}},-\sqrt{a_{1}}&,\ldots,&\sqrt{a_{r+1}},-\sqrt{a_{r+1}}\\
\sqrt{b_{1}},-\sqrt{b_{1}}&,\ldots,&\sqrt{b_{r}},-\sqrt{b_{r}},-\sqrt{q},0\end{array};\sqrt{q},-z\right),

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and we call this DBHS Exton type. For all
u\in\mathbb{C}
, define the deformed
q
-exponential function,

\mathrm{e}_{q}(z,u)=\begin{cases}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u^{\binom{n}{2}}\frac{z^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}&\text{ if }u\neq 0;\\
1+\frac{z}{1-q}&\text{ if }u=0.\end{cases}

Some deformed
q
-exponential functions are:

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}_{q}(z,1)

\displaystyle=e_{q}(z)=\frac{1}{(z;q)_{\infty}},\ |z|<1,

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}_{q}(-z,q)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mbox{{E}}}\nolimits_{q}(-z)=(z;q)_{\infty},\ z\in\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle e_{q}(z,\sqrt{q})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{q}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{\frac{1}{2}\binom{n}{2}}\frac{z^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}={}_{1}\phi_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
-\sqrt{q}\end{array};\sqrt{q},-z\right),\ z\in\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}_{q}(qz,q^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{q}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}\frac{z^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}},\ z\in\mathbb{C},

where
\mathcal{E}_{q}(z)
is the Exton
q
-exponential function and
\mathcal{R}_{q}(z)
is the Rogers-Ramanujan function.
The deformed
q
-exponential function has the following representation

\mathrm{e}_{q}(z,u)={}_{1}\Phi_{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
-\end{array};q,u,z\right).

Theorem 1
.

The DBHS
{}_{1}\Phi_{0}
has the following representation

{}_{1}\Phi_{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a\\
-\end{array};q,u,x\right)=(a;q)_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{j}}{(q;q)_{j}}\mathrm{e}_{q}(q^{i}x,u).

(25)

Proof.

From Eq.(

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}u^{\binom{k}{2}}\frac{(a;q)_{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}x^{k}

\displaystyle=(a;q)_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{j}}{(q;q)_{j}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(q^{j}x)^{k}u^{\binom{k}{2}}}{(q;q)_{k}}

\displaystyle=(a;q)_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{j}}{(q;q)_{j}}\mathrm{e}_{q}(q^{j}x,u)

as claimed.
∎

If
u=1
, we obtain the
q
-theorem binomial. We have the following results for

.

-x\end{array};q,a\right).

\displaystyle{}_{1}\phi_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a\\

-x\end{array};q,a\right)

∎

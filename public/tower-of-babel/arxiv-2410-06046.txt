Auslander–Reiten combinatorics and q q -characters of representations of affine quantum groups
Élie Casbi and Jian-Rong Li

Abstract
. For each simple Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} of simply-laced type, Hernandez and Leclerc introduced a certain category 𝒞 ℤ \mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}} of finite-dimensional representations of the quantum affine algebra of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , as well as certain subcategories 𝒞 ℤ ≤ ξ \mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi} depending on a choice of height function adapted to an orientation of the Dynkin graph of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . In our previous work we constructed an algebra homomorphism D ~ ξ \widetilde{D}_{\xi} whose domain contains the image of the Grothendieck ring of 𝒞 ℤ ≤ ξ \mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi} under the truncated q q -character morphism χ ~ q {\widetilde{\chi}}_{q} corresponding to ξ \xi . We exhibited a close relationship between the composition of D ~ ξ \widetilde{D}_{\xi} with χ ~ q {\widetilde{\chi}}_{q} and the morphism D ¯ \overline{D} recently introduced by Baumann, Kamnitzer and Knutson in their study of the equivariant homology of Mirković–Vilonen cycles. In this paper, we extend D ~ ξ \widetilde{D}_{\xi} in order to investigate its composition with Frenkel–Reshetikhin’s original q q -character morphism. Our main result consists in proving that the q q -characters of all standard modules in 𝒞 ℤ \mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}} lie in the kernel of D ~ ξ \widetilde{D}_{\xi} . This provides a large family of new non-trivial rational identities suggesting possible geometric interpretations.



1.
Introduction

Introduced by Drinfeld
[
Dri86
]
and Jimbo
[
Jim85
]
around 1985, the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
associated to a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
can be viewed as a deformation of the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
In the early 90’s, Lusztig
[
Lus90
]
constructed a remarkable basis of the quantum coordinate ring
\mathcal{A}_{q}(\mathfrak{n})
(dual of the negative half
U_{q}(\mathfrak{n})
arising from a triangular decomposition of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
) called the dual canonical basis. Kashiwara
[
Kas91
]
proposed an alternative construction called the upper global basis which was shown to coincide with Lusztig’s dual canonical basis by Grojnowski–Lusztig
[
GL93
]
and Kashiwara–Saito
[
KS97
]
. Evaluating at
q=1
, the dual canonical basis descends to a basis of the ring of regular functions on the Lie group
{\bf N}
corresponding to
\mathfrak{n}
. Other remarkable bases of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]
were discovered since then, for instance the dual semicanonical basis constructed by Lusztig
[
Lus00
]
, or the Mirković–Vilonen basis arising from the geometric Satake correspondence
[
MV00
]
.
The study of the combinatorial properties of the elements of these bases was one of the main motivations at the origin of the foundation of cluster theory by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
FZ02
]
.

The quantum affine algebra associated to
\mathfrak{g}
, denoted
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
can be viewed as a affinization of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, or as quantization of the universal enveloping algebra of the Kac-Moody algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
associated to
\mathfrak{g}
, see
[
CP95
]
. In
[
HL10
]
, Hernandez and Leclerc discovered an intimate connection between the representation theory of quantum affine algebras and cluster theory. They introduced the notion of monoidal categorification of cluster algebras, which opened the way for the study of various cluster structures using representation-theoretic tools. At the heart of this framework lies the idea that certain distinguished short exact sequences in certain well-chosen monoidal categories yield relations in their Grothendieck rings that can be identified with exchange relations associated to mutations for a suitable cluster structure. Kang–Kashiwara–Kim–Oh
[
KKKO18
]
obtained important monoidal categorification results with categories of representations of quiver Hecke algebras by constructing certain non trivial braiding operators (
R
-matrices). These operators give rise to short exact sequences corresponding to the exchange relations for the cluster structure of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]
. They also provide useful (necessary) criteria for the irreducibility of products of simple modules in these categories. Other examples of monoidal categorifications have also been exhibited by Cautis–Williams
[
CW19
]
via the coherent Satake category. Hernandez–Leclerc’s categories are also crucially involved in the major recent works by Kang–Kashiwara–Kim–Oh-Park
[
KKK18
,
KKKO18
,
KKOP24
]
. In the original work of Hernandez–Leclerc
[
HL10
]
, these monoidal subcategories are constructed for a source–sink orientation. This construction was later generalized to arbitrary height functions in
[
HL15
]
and
[
KKOP20
,
KKOP24
]
.

Beyond the study of cluster monomials, the categories involved in monoidal categorifications of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]
also provide powerful tools to study the dual canonical bases of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]

[
Li24
]
mentioned earlier, as well as dual canonical basis of Grassmannian cluster algebras
[
CDFL20
]
. For instance, when
\mathfrak{g}
is of simply-laced type, Hernandez–Leclerc
[
HL15
]
introduced a family
\{\mathcal{C}_{Q}\}
of (monoidal) categories of finite-dimensional representations of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
indexed by the set of orientations of the Dynkin graph of
\mathfrak{g}
and showed that for each
Q
, the complexified Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]
in such a way that the natural basis of classes of irreducible representations of
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
gets identified with the dual canonical basis of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]
. These categories
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
are all monoidal subcategories of a larger monoidal category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
that can be viewed as discrete version of the whole category of finite-dimensional representations of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
. Frenkel–Reshetikhin’s
q
-character morphism
[
FR99
, Theorem 3]
yields an injective ring homomorphism from
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}})
to the ring of Laurent polynomials in variables
Y_{i,p}
where
i
runs over the set of vertices of
Q
and
p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}
. For reasons related to cluster theory, Hernandez–Leclerc consider a slightly modified version
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}
of
\chi_{q}
called truncated
q
-character, which depends on
Q
(more precisely on a choice of a height function adapted to
Q
).

Lusztig
[
Lus83
]
discovered a deep connection (called the geometric Satake correspondence) between the representation theory of the Lie group
\mathbf{G}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and the geometry of the affine Grassmannian
Gr_{\mathbf{G}^{\vee}}
of its Langlands dual
\mathbf{G}^{\vee}
. Using this result together with ideas from
[
Gin95
]
, Mirković and Vilonen
[
MV00
,
MV07
]
constructed a remarkable basis
\{b_{z}\}
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]
, called the Mirković–Vilonen basis (or simply MV basis), whose elements are indexed by a family of closed irreducible subvarieties of
Gr_{\mathbf{G}^{\vee}}
called (stable) Mirković–Vilonen cycles. Note that the MV basis has been proved to be biperfect in the sense of
[
BKK21
]
(cf.
[
BKK21
, Theorem 5.2]
). These varieties are endowed with a natural action of a maximal torus
\mathbf{T}^{\vee}
in
\mathbf{G}^{\vee}
and one can therefore consider the corresponding equivariant homology theory. In their recent work
[
BKK21
]
, Baumann–Kamnitzer–Knutson introduced a remarkable algebraic homomorphism
\overline{D}:\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
where
\mathfrak{t}
denotes the Lie algebra of
\mathbf{T}^{\vee}
and proved that the evaluation of
\overline{D}
on an element
b_{Z}
of the MV basis coincides with the equivariant multiplicity at a well-chosen fixed point of the corresponding MV cycle
Z
. The morphism
\overline{D}
turned out to provide a powerful tool to compare the several distinguished bases of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]
mentioned above.

The motivation for the present paper stems from our desire to provide an interpretation of
\overline{D}
from the perspective of Hernandez–Leclerc’s monoidal categorification and investigate further the connections between the representation theory of quantum affine algebras and the geometric Satake correspondence.
In our previous work
[
CL23
]
, we constructed for each simply-laced type Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and for each height function
\xi
on the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
a morphism
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}:\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
where
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
is the subtorus of
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
generated by the
Y_{i,p}^{\pm 1},i\in I,p\leq\xi(i)
. The definition of
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}
relies on Auslander–Reiten combinatorics and involves the coefficients of the inverse quantum Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
. The ring
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
contains the truncated
q
-characters of all the representations in Hernandez–Leclerc’s category
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
where
Q
is the Dynkin quiver given by
\xi
. Our main result from
[
CL23
]
consists in proving that the composition of
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}
with the restriction of the truncated
q
-character morphism to
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{Q})
coincides with Baumann–Kamnitzer–Knutson’s morphism
\overline{D}
(more precisely its composition with Hernandez–Leclerc’s isomorphism
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]\simeq K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{Q})
). In other words,
\overline{D}
can be viewed as the restriction to
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]
of
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}\circ{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}
. As a consequence, we were able to extend the results and prove several observations and conjectures made by the first author in
[
Cas21
]
. Moreover, this allows to extend
\overline{D}
to the Grothendieck rings of larger subcategories of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
also carrying non-trivial cluster structures (see
[
HL16
]
).

This paper is devoted to the study of the composition of
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}
with Frenkel–Reshetikhin’s original (i.e. non-truncated)
q
-character morphism. Firstly, we extend the map
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}
to the whole ring
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
thanks to a periodicity argument. Thus we can study the restriction of
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}
to the image of
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}})
under
\chi_{q}
. A standard module is a tensor product of fundamental modules in a well-chosen order. Our main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Q
be an arbitrary Dynkin quiver and
\xi
be a height function adapted to
Q
. Let
M
be a non-trivial standard module in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then we have that

\widetilde{D}_{\xi}\left(\chi_{q}(M)\right)=0.

Consequently, the image under
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}
of the
q
-character of an arbitrary module in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is always constant. Note that this constant is zero in the case of non-trivial simple modules in the subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
.
The crucial ingredient for the proof of this result consists in establishing a remarkable duality between certain distinguished simple modules in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
called Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules, when
\mathfrak{g}
is of classical type, which we believe is of independent interest. The Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
are denoted
X_{i,p}^{(k)}
with
i\in I
,
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
( see Section
3.2
). We define a dual orientation
Q^{*}
given by the height function
\xi^{*}(i):=-\xi(i^{*})
where
i\mapsto i^{*}
is the involution of the Dynkin diagram such that
w_{0}(\alpha_{i})=-\alpha_{i^{*}}
. Denoting
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}^{*}
the corresponding truncation à la Hernandez–Leclerc, we assume
Q
is the monotonic orientation as chosen in
[
CL23
, Figure 1]
and show (cf. Theorem
4.1
) that if
i\in I,1\leq k<h
and
\xi(i)-2h+2\leq p\leq\xi(i)
, then the following holds

(1.1)

\widetilde{D}_{\xi}\left({\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}\left(X_{i,p}^{(k)}\right)\right)=\pm\left(\psi\circ\widetilde{D}_{\xi^{*}}\right)\left({\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}^{*}\left(X_{i^{*},-p-2h+4}^{(h-k)}\right)\right)

where
h
is the Coxeter number of the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
, the sign on the right hand side can be made explicit in terms of Auslander–Reiten theoretic quantities
[
AR75
]
, and
\psi
denote the automorphism of
\mathbb{C}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
given by
\psi(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{i^{*}}
.
We also use several classical facts from Auslander–Reiten combinatorics as key ingredients in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In particular, our proof may be viewed as a manifestation of some interesting compatibility between mesh relations and T-systems.

In the case where
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n},n\geq 1
, we are able to provide an alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
that does not rely on (
1.1
), but rather on Mukhin–Young’s path model formulas
[
MY12
]
.

As explained above, the map
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}
is intimately related to the morphism
\overline{D}
introduced by Baumann–Kamnitzer–Knutson
[
BKK21
]
in their study of the MV basis and of the equivariant homology of the affine Grassmannian. Given this tight connection with the geometric Satake correspondence, it is natural to believe that the statement of Theorem
1.1
may admit some interesting geometric interpretation.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is devoted to some recollection on Auslander–Reiten theory. In particular we investigate the combinatorics of the dual quiver
Q^{*}
mentioned above. In Section
3
, we recall the main features of Hernandez–Leclerc’s categorifications and we provide detailed reminders on the map
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}
defined in our previous work
[
CL23
]
. Section
4
is devoted to the proof of the duality (
1.1
) between Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules when
\mathfrak{g}
is of classical type. Finally, Section
5
contains the final arguments for completing the proof of our main result (Theorem
1.1
).

2.
Auslander–Reiten combinatorics

2.1.
Brief recollection on Auslander–Reiten theory

Auslander–Reiten theory was introduced by Auslander and Reiten in
[
AR75
]
. Let
Q
be a Dynkin quiver, i.e. an orientation of the Dynkin diagram of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of type
A_{n}(n\geq 1),D_{n}(n\geq 4)
or
E_{n},n=6,7,8
. We use the notation
i\sim j
to denote that
i
and
j
are connected
by an edge in the underlying graph of
Q
. Let
I
denote the set of vertices of
Q
and
n:=\sharp I
. We will denote by
\rep Q
the category of finite-dimensional representations of
Q
over
\mathbb{C}
, which we will identify with the category of finite-dimensional right modules over the path algebra
\mathbb{C}Q
of
Q
. Furthermore we will also denote by
\Delta
(resp.
\Delta_{+}
) the set of roots (resp. positive roots) associated to
\mathfrak{g}
, and by
W
the corresponding Weyl group.

By Gabriel’s theorem
[
Gab72
]
, there is a bijection between the set
\Delta_{+}
of positive roots of the root system associated with
Q
and the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in
\rep Q
.
Moreover, the dimension vectors of objects in
\rep Q
can be identified with elements of the positive root cone
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{i}
. For each
\beta\in\Delta_{+}
, we denote by
M_{\beta}
the corresponding indecomposable representation. In particular, its dimension vector satisfies
\dim M_{\beta}=\beta
.

For each
j\in I
, we will respectively denote by
I_{j}
(resp.
P_{j}
) the indecomposable injective (resp. projective) representation of
Q
, whose dimension vectors are given by

\dimvec I_{j}=\sum_{k\rightsquigarrow j}\alpha_{k},\qquad\dimvec P_{j}=\sum_{l\rightsquigarrow k}\alpha_{k},

where the notation
k\rightsquigarrow j
means that there exists an oriented path in
Q
starting at
k
and ending at
j
(
k\rightsquigarrow j
includes the stationary path at vertex
j
).

A reduced expression
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})
of
w_{0}
is said to be adapted to
Q
if for each
k=1,\dots,N
, the vertex
i_{k}
is a sink
in the quiver
Q_{k-1}
defined inductively by
Q_{0}=Q
and
Q_{k}
obtained
from
Q_{k-1}
by reversing all arrows incident to
i_{k}
.
The Coxeter transform
c_{Q}\in W
associated to
Q
is then given by
c_{Q}:=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
. We also denote by
h
the corresponding Coxeter number, i.e. the order of
c_{Q}
in
W
(recall that this number is the same for all Coxeter elements in
W
).
A height function adapted to
Q
is a function
\xi:I\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
such that
\xi(j)=\xi(i)-1
whenever there is an arrow from
i
to
j

in
Q
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{Q}:=\mathcal{D}^{b}(\rep Q)
denote the bounded derived category of
\rep Q
and
\ind\mathcal{D}_{Q}
denote the set of indecomposable objects of
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
. The elements of
\ind\mathcal{D}_{Q}
are of the form
M[m]
where
M
is an indecomposable object in
\rep Q
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, so that
\ind\mathcal{D}_{Q}
is in bijection with
\Delta_{+}\times\mathbb{Z}
. We will then set
\dimvec M[m]:=(-1)^{m}\dimvec M
for any indecomposable object
M
in
\rep Q
and any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, so that
\dimvec N\in\Delta
for any object
N\in\ind\mathcal{D}_{Q}
.
We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Q}
the Euler-Ringel form of
Q
, which is the bilinear form given by

\forall M,N\in\rep Q,\kern 5.0pt\langle\dimvec M,\dimvec N\rangle_{Q}:=\dim\Hom_{\rep Q}(M,N)-\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\rep Q}(M,N).

Using this, it is easy to compute the dimensions of morphism spaces in
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
: namely, if
M
and
M^{\prime}
are indecomposable objects in
\rep Q
and
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then

(2.1)

\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M[m],M^{\prime}[m^{\prime}])=\begin{cases}(\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}M,\boldsymbol{\dim}M^{\prime}\rangle)_{+}&\text{if $m=m^{\prime}$,}\\
(\langle\boldsymbol{\dim}M,\boldsymbol{\dim}M^{\prime}\rangle)_{-}&\text{if $m^{\prime}=m+1$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
a_{+}:=\max(a,0)
and
a_{-}:=-\min(a,0)
for any integer
a
.
We denote
i+2{\mathbb{Z}}=\{i+2r:r\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
for
i\in I=[n]
, where
n
is the rank of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the set of pairs

\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\{(i,p),i\in I,p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}\}

and let us choose a height function
\xi
adapted to
Q
.
Such a choice yields a natural bijection

(2.2)

(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}\longmapsto M_{i,p}\in\ind\mathcal{D}_{Q}

given by

M_{j,\xi(j)}:=I_{j},\kern 5.0ptj\in I,\qquad M_{i,p\pm 2}:=\tau_{Q}^{\mp 1}M_{i,p},\kern 5.0pt(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}

where
\tau_{Q}
stands for the Auslander-Reiten translate in
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
. Note that one has

(2.3)

p>s\kern 5.0pt\Rightarrow\kern 5.0pt\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{i,p},M_{j,s})=0

for any
(i,p),(j,s)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We then set

\beta_{i,p}:=\dimvec M_{i,p}\in\Delta

for every
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Using standard notations, we will denote by
i\mapsto i^{*}
the involution of
I
given by

(2.4)

\alpha_{i^{*}}:=-w_{0}(\alpha_{i}).

For each
i\in I
we denote by
d_{i}
the following positive number

(2.5)

d_{i}:=\sum_{(j,s)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\dim\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{i,\xi(i)},M_{j,s}).

Note that this is well-defined as the right hand side contains only finitely many non zero terms in view of (
2.1
).
For later purposes, it will be convenient to extend this to all non-negative integers by setting
d_{i}:=1
if
i=0
or
i>n
. Note that for each
i\in I
we have that

\forall p\in i+2\mathbb{Z},\kern 5.0pt\sum_{(j,s)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}}\dim\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{i,p},M_{j,s})=d_{i}

because
\dim\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{i,p},M_{j,s})=\dim\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{i,\xi(i)},M_{j,s-p+\xi(i)})
for each
(j,s)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We then have the following:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
i\in I
and
p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{i,p}:=\{(j,s)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}},p\leq s<p+h\ \}
.
Then we have that

d_{i}=\sum_{(j,s)\in\mathcal{C}_{i,p}}\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}.

Proof.

Let
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
(j,s)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\dim\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{i,p},M_{j,s})\neq 0
. Then by (
2.3
) we have
s\geq p
. We claim that
s<p+h
as well. Indeed, recall that
S:=\tau[1]
is a Serre functor in
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
(in the sense of
[
BO01
]
), i.e. we have bifunctorial isomorphisms

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M,N)\simeq D\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(N,SM)

for every objects
M,N
in
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
(here
D
denotes the usual duality of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces). This essentially follows from the Auslander-Reiten formula
[
AR75
]
. Furthermore it is known (cf. for example [
[
FO21
]
, Corollary 2.40]) that
M_{i,p}[1]\simeq M_{i^{*},p+h}
. Thus we have

\dim\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{i,p},M_{j,s})=\dim\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{j^{*},s-h+2},M_{i,p})

which would be zero if
s>p+h
in view of (
2.3
). Thus the claim holds, so that
d_{i}
can be rewritten as

d_{i}=\sum_{(j,s)\in\mathcal{C}_{i,p}}\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{i,p},M_{j,s}).

In addition to that, the inequalities
p\leq s<p+h
imply that
\dim\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{i,p},M_{j,s}[m])=0
for any non zero integer
m
and thus
\dim\Hom_{\mathcal{D}_{Q}}(M_{i,p},M_{j,s})=\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}
in view of (
2.1
), which proves the lemma.

∎

Corollary 2.2
.

For each
i\in I
we have that
d_{i}=d_{i^{*}}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle d_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(j,s)\in\mathcal{C}_{i,p}}\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}=\sum_{(j,s)\in\mathcal{C}_{i,p}}\langle-\beta_{i^{*},p+h},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(j,s)\in\mathcal{C}_{i^{*},p+h}}\langle-\beta_{i^{*},p+h},\beta_{j^{*}s-h}\rangle_{Q}=\sum_{(j,s)\in\mathcal{C}_{i^{*},p+h}}\langle\beta_{i^{*},p+h},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}=d_{i^{*}}.

∎

2.2.
Dual Auslander–Reiten theory

Fix as above an orientation
Q
of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
and choose a height function
\xi
adapted to
Q
. We denote by
Q^{*}
the Dynkin quiver adapted to the height function
\xi^{*}
given by

(2.6)

\forall i\in I,\qquad\xi^{*}(i):=-\xi(i^{*})

where
i^{*}
is defined by (
2.4
).
For every
i\in I
, we will denote by
I_{i}^{*}
(resp.
P_{i}^{*}
) the indecomposable injective (resp. projective) representation of
Q^{*}
associated to
i
. We will also denote by
\beta_{i,p}^{*}
the corresponding root associated to the vertex
(i,p)
for every
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, namely

\beta_{i,p}^{*}:=\tau_{Q^{*}}^{(\xi^{*}(i)-p)/2}\left(\dimvec I_{i}^{*}\right).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Q
be a Dynkin quiver. Then we have that

\forall\beta,\gamma\in\Delta,\qquad\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle_{Q}=\langle w_{0}\gamma,w_{0}\beta\rangle_{Q^{*}}.

Proof.

Recall that
\alpha_{i}
is the dimension vector of the simple representation
S_{i}
.
As everything is bilinear, it suffices to prove the statement in the case where both
\beta
and
\gamma
are simple roots. Hence we denote
\beta:=\alpha_{i}
and
\gamma:=\alpha_{j}
with
i,j\in I
. Then we have that
-w_{0}(\beta)=\alpha_{i^{*}}
and
-w_{0}(\gamma)=\alpha_{j^{*}}
. Thus we have

\langle w_{0}\gamma,w_{0}\beta\rangle_{Q^{*}}=\langle\alpha_{j^{*}},\alpha_{i^{*}}\rangle_{Q^{*}}=\delta_{i^{*},j^{*}}-\sharp\{j^{*}\rightarrow i^{*}\}_{Q^{*}}.

But by definition of
Q^{*}
, we have that

\sharp\{k\rightarrow l\}_{Q^{*}}=\sharp\{l^{*}\rightarrow k^{*}\}_{Q}.

Therefore we obtain

\langle w_{0}\gamma,w_{0}\beta\rangle_{Q^{*}}=\delta_{i,j}-\sharp\{i\rightarrow j\}_{Q}=\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle_{Q}=\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle_{Q}

which proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For any Dynkin quiver
Q
, we have that
w_{0}c_{Q}w_{0}=c_{Q^{*}}^{-1}
.

Proof.

Fix a reduced expression
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})
adapted to
Q
, so that a reduced expression for
c_{Q}
is given by
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
(recall that
n=\sharp I
).
As (
2.4
) implies that
w_{0}s_{i}w_{0}=s_{i^{*}}
for any
i\in I
, we get that
w_{0}c_{Q}w_{0}=s_{i_{1}^{*}}\cdots s_{i_{n}^{*}}
. Now, if there is an arrow from
i
to
j
in
Q
, then
i
appears before
j
in the word
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
. On the other hand, we have that

\xi^{*}(i^{*})=-\xi(i)=-\xi(j)-1=\xi^{*}(j^{*})-1

so there is an arrow from
j^{*}
to
i^{*}
in
Q^{*}
, and thus
i^{*}
has to appear after
j^{*}
in a reduced expression for
c_{Q^{*}}
. This shows that
c_{Q^{*}}=s_{i_{n}^{*}}\cdots s_{i_{1}^{*}}
and hence by what precedes
c_{Q^{*}}^{-1}=w_{0}c_{Q}w_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
j\in I
and
s\in j+2\mathbb{Z}
. Then we have that

w_{0}\beta_{j,s}=-\beta_{j,-s-h+2}^{*}.

Proof.

Let us fix a height function
\xi
adapted to
Q
. We begin by checking the desired identity in the case where
s=\xi(j)
, for each
j\in I
. Then we have

\beta_{j,\xi(j)}=\dimvec I_{j}=\sum_{k\rightsquigarrow j}\alpha_{k}.

As explained in the proof of the previous lemma, there is an oriented path from
k
to
j
in
Q
if and only if there is an oriented path from
j^{*}
to
k^{*}
in
Q^{*}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle w_{0}\beta_{j,\xi(j)}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\text{$k\rightsquigarrow j$ in $Q$}}\alpha_{k^{*}}=-\sum_{\text{$j^{*}\rightsquigarrow k^{*}$ in $Q^{*}$}}\alpha_{k^{*}}=-\sum_{\text{$j^{*}\rightsquigarrow k$ in $Q^{*}$}}\alpha_{k}=-\dimvec P_{j^{*}}^{*}

where
P_{j^{*}}^{*}
denotes the indecomposable projective representation of
Q^{*}
associated to
j^{*}
. By the reminders above, we have that
\dimvec P_{j^{*}}^{*}=\beta_{j,\xi^{*}(j^{*})-h+2}^{*}
. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle w_{0}\beta_{j,\xi(j)}=-\beta_{j,\xi^{*}(j^{*})-h+2}^{*}=-\beta_{j,-\xi(j)-h+2}^{*}

which is the desired statement for
s=\xi(j)
. Fix now
s
arbitrary. Then by definition we have that
\beta_{j,s}=c_{Q}^{(\xi(j)-s)/2}(\beta_{j,\xi(j)})
, and hence using the previous lemma, we get

w_{0}\beta_{j,s}=w_{0}c_{Q}^{(\xi(j)-s)/2}(\beta_{j,\xi(j)})=c_{Q^{*}}^{(s-\xi(j))/2}(w_{0}\beta_{j,\xi(j)})=-c_{Q^{*}}^{(s-\xi(j))/2}(\beta_{j,-\xi(j)-h+2}^{*})=-\beta_{j,-s-h+2}^{*}

which finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

2.3.
Remark on dual orientations

We end this section by proving Lemma
2.7
below. Although we will never use it in the rest of the paper, it sheds light on the motivation for introducing the orientation
Q^{*}
and therefore we provide a detailed proof for the reader’s convenience. First recall the following classical fact.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{i}
be a reduced word for
w_{0}
adapted to an orientation
Q
of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then for every
i
, denoting by
n(i)
the number of occurrences of
i
in
\mathbf{i}
, we have

n(i)=\frac{\xi(i)-\xi(i^{*})+h}{2}.

Consequently, we have that
n(i)+n(i^{*})=h
for every
i\in I
.

We can now state and prove the desired statement.

Lemma 2.7
.

Fix a reduced word
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{N})
of
w_{0}
adapted to an orientation
Q
of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
and fix a height function
\xi
adapted to
Q
. Let
\mathbf{i}^{*}
denote the reduced word of
w_{0}
given by
\mathbf{i}^{*}:=(i_{N},\ldots,i_{1})
. Then
\mathbf{i}^{*}
is adapted to the orientation
Q^{*}
.

Proof.

Let us fix
k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. We show that
i_{k}
is a source of
s_{i_{k+1}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}Q^{*}
. To do so, we show that

s_{i_{k+1}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\xi^{*}(i_{k})=1+s_{i_{k+1}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\xi^{*}(j)

for all
j\sim i_{k}
. By assumption,
\mathbf{i}
is adapted to
Q
, so
i_{k}
is a source of
s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}Q
. Hence

\forall j\sim i_{k},\quad s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}\xi(i_{k})=1+s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}\xi(j).

Moreover, we have

s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}\xi(j)=\xi(j)-2n_{<k}(j)\quad\text{and}\quad s_{i_{k+1}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\xi^{*}(j)=\xi^{*}(j)-2n_{>k}(j)

for all
j\in I
.
Therefore let us fix
j\sim i_{k}
and denote

\epsilon:=s_{i_{k+1}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\xi^{*}(i_{k})-s_{i_{k+1}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}\xi^{*}(j).

We have

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=\xi^{*}(i_{k})-2n_{>k}(i_{k})-\xi^{*}(j)+2n_{>k}(j)=\xi(j^{*})-\xi(i_{k}^{*})-2n_{>k}(i_{k})+2n_{>k}(j)

\displaystyle=\xi(j)-\xi(i_{k})-2n(j)+2n(i_{k})-2n_{>k}(i_{k})+2n_{>k}(j)\quad\text{using Lemma~\ref{lem : number of occ},}

\displaystyle=\xi(j)-2n_{\leq k}(i_{j})-(\xi(i_{k})-2n_{\leq k}(i_{k}))

\displaystyle=\xi(j)-2n_{<k}(i_{j})-(\xi(i_{k})-2n_{<k}(i_{k})-2)

\displaystyle=s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}\xi(j)-s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}\xi(i_{k})+2

\displaystyle=1.

∎

3.
Reminders on HL categories and the map
{\widetilde{D}}

3.1.
Reminders on quantum affine algebras and HL categories

Let
\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the set of pairs

\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\{(i,p),i\in I,p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}\}

and let
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the torus

\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}:=\mathbb{Z}[Y_{i,p}^{\pm 1},(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}].

Following
[
HL15
]
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
generated by the fundamental representations
L(Y_{i,p})
for
p\in i+2\mathbb{Z}
. The simple objects of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
are the modules
L(\mathfrak{m})
where
\mathfrak{m}
is a monomial in the
Y_{i,p}
. We note that the original construction of such monoidal subcategories for a source–sink orientation was developed in
[
HL10
]
and later generalized to arbitrary height functions in
[
HL15
]
and
[
KKOP20
,
KKOP24
]
.

Frenkel–Reshetikhin’s
q
-character morphism
[
FR99
, Theorem 3]
yields an injective ring homomorphism

\chi_{q}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}.

Using standard notation, we set

A_{i,p+1}:=Y_{i,p}Y_{i,p+2}\prod_{j\sim i}Y_{j,p+1}^{-1}

for each
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. It is then a known fact that for a simple object
M=L(\mathfrak{m})
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, the renormalized
q
-character of
M
defined as
\mathfrak{m}^{-1}\chi_{q}(M)
is a polynomial in the
A_{i,p+1}^{-1},(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
with constant term
1
, see
[
FR99
, Conjecture 1]
and
[
FM01
, Theorem 4.1]
.

As in the previous section, we fix an orientation
Q
of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\xi
a height function adapted to
Q
. Then we set

\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}:=\{(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}},p\leq\xi(i)\}

and we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
the smallest full monoidal subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
containing all the fundamental representations
L(Y_{i,p}),(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
. Given a reduced expression
\mathbf{i}_{Q}
of
w_{0}
adapted to
Q
, one can define a semi-infinite word denoted
\widehat{\mathbf{i_{Q}}}:=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,)
by setting
i_{k+mN}:=i_{k}^{*m}
i.e. the vertex obtained by applying
m
times the involution
*
to
i_{k}
, for every
1\leq m\leq N
and every
m\geq 0
. Using standard notations, we set

k_{+}:=\min\{l>k\mid i_{l}=i_{k}\}\qquad k_{-}:=\max(\{l<k\mid i_{l}=i_{k}\}\sqcup\{0\}).

We also have a bijection

\varphi:\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}

such that
\varphi(i,p-2)=\varphi(i,p)_{+}
.
We also denote by
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
the smallest full monoidal subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
containing all the fundamental representations
L(Y_{i,p}),(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{Q}
where

\widehat{I}_{Q}:=\{(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}},\xi(i^{*})-h\leq p\leq\xi(i)\}.

The assignment

(3.1)

Y_{i,p}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}\longmapsto\beta_{i,p}\in\Delta

endows
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
with a grading by the root lattice, such that
A_{j,s+1}
has a degree
0
for each
(j,s)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
by the mesh relation following from the fact that there is a distinguished triangle

\tau_{Q}M_{i,p}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j\sim i}M_{j,p+1}\longrightarrow M_{i,p}\longrightarrow\tau_{Q}M_{i,p}[1]

in
\mathcal{D}_{Q}
. In particular, the
q
-character of any simple module in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is homogeneous with respect to the grading (
3.1
). Moreover, (
3.1
) induces a bijection between
\{Y_{i,p},(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{Q}\}
and
\Delta_{+}
.
Finally, following
[
HL16
]
, we define for each
M\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
the truncated
q
-character
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(M)
of
M
as the element of
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
obtained from
\chi_{q}(M)
by dropping all the terms involving variables
Y_{i,p}
with
p>\xi(i)
. This gives another injective ring homomorphism

{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}:\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}:=\mathbb{Z}[Y_{i,p}^{\pm 1},(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}].

Truncated
q
-character map is injective because
q
-character map is injective and the
q
-character of a module is determined by its highest
l
-weight and also by its truncated
q
-character
[
FR99
,
FM01
]
.

3.2.
Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules

A distinguished family of simple objects in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
will play a central role in this work: the Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules (KR modules). They are the simple modules of the form

X_{i,p}^{(k)}:=L\left(Y_{i,p}\cdots Y_{i,p+2k-2}\right)\quad(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}},k\geq 1.

The
q
-characters of the KR modules satisfy the following remarkable identities called T-systems:

\chi_{q}(X_{i,p}^{(k)})\chi_{q}(X_{i,p+2}^{(k)})=\chi_{q}(X_{i,p}^{(k+1)})\chi_{q}(X_{i,p+2}^{(k)})+\prod_{j\sim i}\chi_{q}(X_{j,p+1}^{(k)}).

Once a height function
\xi
has been fixed as above, it was shown by Hernandez and Leclerc
[
HL16
]
that the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
carries a cluster algebra structure, and that the classes of the KR modules are cluster variables in this cluster algebra. The T-systems are interpreted as exchange relations associated to a well-chosen mutation sequence, starting with an initial cluster given by the KR module of the form

(3.2)

X_{i,p}:=X_{i,p}^{((\xi(i)-p+2)/2)},\quad(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}.

3.3.
The map
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}

This subsection is devoted to necessary background involving the constructions and results from our previous work
[
CL23
]
. Firstly, recall the coefficients of the inverse quantum Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
(assumed to be of simply-laced type). The quantum Cartan matrix
C(z)
of
\mathfrak{g}
is defined as

C_{i,j}(z):=\begin{cases}z+z^{-1},&\text{if $i=j$,}\\
-1,&\text{if $i\sim j$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Here
z
is an indeterminate and the notation
i\sim j
means that there is an edge relating
i
and
j
in the Dynkin graph of
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote by
\widetilde{C}_{i,j}(z)
the entries of the inverse of
C(z)
. For example, when
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
D_{4}
, the series
\widetilde{C}_{i,j}(z)
are given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1,1}(z)

\displaystyle=z+z^{5}-z^{7}-z^{11}+z^{13}+z^{17}-z^{19}+O(z^{21})

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1,2}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{2}+z^{4}-z^{8}-z^{10}+z^{14}+z^{16}-z^{20}+O(z^{22})

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1,3}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{3}-z^{9}+z^{15}-z^{21}+O(z^{23})

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{1,4}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{3}-z^{9}+z^{15}-z^{21}+O(z^{23})

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{2,2}(z)

\displaystyle=z+2z^{3}+z^{5}-z^{7}-2z^{9}-z^{11}+z^{13}+2z^{15}+z^{17}-z^{19}+O(z^{21})

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{2,3}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{2}+z^{4}-z^{8}-z^{10}+z^{14}+z^{16}-z^{20}+O(z^{22})

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{2,4}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{2}+z^{4}-z^{8}-z^{10}+z^{14}+z^{16}-z^{20}+O(z^{22})

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{3,3}(z)

\displaystyle=z+z^{5}-z^{7}-z^{11}+z^{13}+z^{17}-z^{19}+O(z^{21})

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{3,4}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{3}-z^{9}+z^{15}-z^{21}+O(z^{23})

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{4,4}(z)

\displaystyle=z+z^{5}-z^{7}-z^{11}+z^{13}+z^{17}-z^{19}+O(z^{21}).

We then define the integers
\widetilde{C}_{i,j}(m)
for every
i,j\in I
and
m\geq 0
by

\widetilde{C}_{i,j}(z)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\widetilde{C}_{i,j}(m)z^{m}.

The definition is extended to all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
by setting
\widetilde{C}_{i,j}(m):=0
for all
m\leq 0
. The following result is due to Hernandez–Leclerc
[
HL15
]
.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
HL15
]
, Proposition 2.1)
.

Let
(i,p)
and
(j,s)
in
\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
and assume
s\geq p
. Then one has

\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p+1)=\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}.

In our previous work
[
CL23
]
, we defined a morphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}:\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
by setting

\forall(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi},\kern 5.0pt\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p}):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in I\\
s\in\xi(j)-2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\end{subarray}}\beta_{j,s}^{\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p-1)-\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p+1)}.

Remark 3.2
.

We would like to outline the fact that the above is a slightly modified version of the map
{\widetilde{D}}
defined in
[
CL23
]
. Here
\beta_{j,s}
can be a positive or a negative root, whereas the definition of

]
does not take the sign into account. In other words, for each

the present definition of

differs by a sign from what it would be with the definition taken in

]
. Note however that both definitions are identical if
\varphi(i,p)\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, as all the
\beta_{j,s}
contributing to
{\widetilde{D}}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p})
are actually positive roots. Therefore, all the results proved in
[

]
still hold with the present definition of
{\widetilde{D}}_{\xi}
, as the image of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{N}]
under
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}
is contained is the subtorus generated by the

such that

.

The following properties can be stated and proved as in
[
CL23
]
without any modification.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
CL23
]
, Remark 6.7)
.

For every
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
, we have that

\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(A_{i,p-1})=\frac{\beta_{i,p}}{\beta_{i,p-2}}.

The only results from
[
CL23
]
that get slightly modified when considering the present definition of

are Proposition 11.1 and Corollary 11.2. Hence we reproduce detailed proofs below for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 3.4
.

For any
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
we have that

\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p-2h+2}\cdots Y_{i,p})=(-1)^{d_{i}}.

Proof.

From the definition of
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}
, we get

\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p-2h+2}\cdots Y_{i,p})=\prod_{(j,s)\in I^{\leq\xi}}\beta_{j,s}^{-\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p+2h-1)+\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p-1)}.

If
(j,s)\in I^{\leq\xi}
is such that
s>p+1
, then both
s-p-1
and
s-p+2h-1
are positive, and hence
\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p+2h-1)=\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p-1)
. Similarly, if
s<p-2h+2
, then both
s-p-1
and
s-p+2h-1
are negative, and hence
\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p+2h-1)=\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p-1)=0
. Thus only the pairs
(j,s)
such that
p-2h+2\leq s\leq p+1
may have a non-trivial contribution to the product. For such pairs
(j,s)
we have that
s-p-1\leq 0
so that
\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p-1)=0
. Hence we can rewrite

\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p-2h+2}\cdots Y_{i,p})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j,s)\in I^{\leq\xi}\\
p-2h+2\leq s\leq p+1\end{subarray}}\beta_{j,s}^{-\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p+2h-1)}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j,s)\in I^{\leq\xi}\\
p-2h+2\leq s\leq p+1\end{subarray}}\beta_{j,s}^{-\langle\beta_{i,p-2h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}}

by Proposition
3.1
. We claim that, with the notations of Section
2
, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\{(j,s)\in I^{\leq\xi},p-2h+2\leq s\leq p+1\mid\langle\beta_{i,p-2h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}\neq 0\}=\bigsqcup_{(j,s)\in I^{\leq\xi}\cap\mathcal{C}_{i^{*},p-h+2}}\{(j,s),(j^{*},s-h)\}.

Let us begin with the direct inclusion. Let
(j,s)\in I^{\leq\xi}
such that
(j,s)
belongs to the left hand side of (
3.3
). If
s\geq p-h+2
then
(j,s)\in\mathcal{C}_{i^{*},p-h+2}
because
\langle\beta_{i^{*},p-h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}=-\langle\beta_{i,p-2h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}\neq 0
so that
(j,s)
belongs to the right hand side of (
3.3
). If on the other hand
s<p-h+2
then setting
j^{\prime}:=j^{*}
and
s^{\prime}:=s+h
we get that
p-h+2<s^{\prime}\leq p+1
so that
(j^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{i^{*},p-h+2}
as
\langle\beta_{i^{*},p-h+2},\beta_{j^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\rangle_{Q}=\langle\beta_{i,p-2h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}\neq 0
and
(j,s)=({j^{\prime}}^{*},s^{\prime}-h)
. It remains to check that
(j^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in I^{\leq\xi}
i.e.
s^{\prime}\leq\xi(j^{\prime})
. Assume this is not the case. Then we have
s+h=s^{\prime}>\xi(j^{\prime})=\xi(j^{*})
so we have that
\xi(j^{*})-h<s\leq\xi(j)
. In other words,
M_{j,s}\in\rep Q
. We also have

\xi(j^{*})<s^{\prime}\leq p+1\leq\xi(i)+1

which implies that
p\geq\xi(j^{*})>\xi(i^{*})-h
so that
M_{i,p}\in\rep Q
as well. Then we have

\langle\beta_{i,p-2h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}=\langle\beta_{i,p+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}=-\langle\beta_{j,s},\beta_{i,p}\rangle_{Q}

by the Auslander–Reiten formula. But we have that
\Hom(M_{j,s},M_{i,p})=0
because
p>s+h-2
(see the proof of Lemma
2.1
) and
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(M_{j,s},M_{i,p})=0
as
p>s
. Therefore we get that
\langle\beta_{i,p-2h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}=0
which is a contradiction as
(j,s)
is assumed to belong to the left hand side of (
3.3
). Hence we have that
s^{\prime}\leq\xi(j^{\prime})
which establishes the direct inclusion.

Conversely, fix
(j,s)\in I^{\leq\xi}\cap\mathcal{C}_{i^{*},p-h+2}
. Then
(j,s)
belongs to the left hand side of the desired equality as by definition of
\mathcal{C}_{i^{*},p-h+2}
we have
p-h+2\leq s<p+2
and
\langle\beta_{i,p-2h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}=-\langle\beta_{i^{*},p-h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}\neq 0
. We also have that
(j^{*},s-h)
belongs to the left hand side of (
3.3
) as well. Indeed,
s-h\leq\xi(j)-h\leq\xi(j^{*})
so that
(j^{*},s-h)\in I^{\leq\xi}
, and we also have that
p-2h+2\leq s-h<p-h+2\leq p+1
. Finally we also have that
\langle\beta_{i,p-2h+2},\beta_{j^{*},s-h}\rangle_{Q}=\langle\beta_{i^{*},p-h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}\neq 0
as
(j,s)\in\mathcal{C}_{i^{*},p-h+2}
. Thus the converse inclusion holds which proves (
3.3
) as claimed.
We can now write

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p-2h+2}\cdots Y_{i,p})

\displaystyle=\prod_{(j,s)\in I^{\leq\xi}\cap\mathcal{C}_{i^{*},p-h+2}}\beta_{j,s}^{-\langle\beta_{i,p-2h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}}(-\beta_{j,s})^{\langle\beta_{i,p-2h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{-\sum_{(j,s)\in\mathcal{C}_{i^{*},p-h+2}}\langle\beta_{i^{*},p-h+2},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}}=(-1)^{d_{i^{*}}}\quad\text{by Lemma~\ref{lem : cone of morphisms},}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d_{i}}\quad\text{by Corollary~\ref{coro di = distar}.}

This concludes the proof of the Proposition.
∎

From this, we can derive the following consequences:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\leq\xi}
. Then we have

\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p})=\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p-2h}).

Proof.

Using twice Proposition
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p-2h})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d_{i}}\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p-2h})\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p-2h+2}\cdots Y_{i,p})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d_{i}}\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p-2h}\cdots Y_{i,p+2})\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p}).

∎

Corollary
3.5
has the important consequence that it allows to extend
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}
to the whole torus
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
by setting, for any
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
:

Y_{i,p}\longmapsto\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(Y_{i,p-2mh})\quad\text{where $m\geq 0$ is such that $p-2mh\leq\xi(i)$ .}

Note that Lemma
3.3
can now be extended to the whole torus
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Corollary 3.6
.

For any
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have that

\widetilde{D}_{\xi}(A_{i,p+1}^{-1})=\frac{\beta_{i,p}}{\beta_{i,p+2}}.

4.

This section is devoted to the key result allowing to prove the main result of this paper. It consists in exhibiting a certain duality between the values taken on certain Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules by the composition
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}\circ{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}
when
\xi
(as well as the corresponding truncation
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}
) is respectively adapted to a Dynkin quiver
Q
and its dual
Q^{*}
.

In what follows, we will sometimes write
\overline{D}(M)
instead of
\overline{D}([M])
.

4.1.
Preliminaries and statement of the main result

Recall that the truncation of the
q
-character heavily depends on the choice of height function. As there will be two height functions (
\xi
and
\xi^{*}
) involved in the following sections, we will use the notation
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}^{*}
to refer to the truncation of the
q
-character morphism given by the height function
\xi^{*}
, in contrast with
{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}
which is the truncation given by
\xi
. Assume
Q=(Q_{0}^{op})^{*}
where
Q_{0}
is the monotonic orientation defined as in Section 7 in
[
CL23
]
and let us fix a height function
\xi
adapted to
Q
. Let
\overline{D},\overline{D}^{*}:K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
denote the maps defined as follows:

\overline{D}(L(Y_{i,p})):=\begin{cases}{\widetilde{D}}_{\xi}({\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}(L(Y_{i,p}))),&\text{if $p\leq\xi(i)$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

\overline{D}^{*}(L(Y_{j,s})):=\begin{cases}\left(\psi\circ{\widetilde{D}}_{\xi^{*}}\right)({\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}^{*}(L(Y_{j,s}))),&\text{if $s\leq\xi^{*}(j)$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Here
\psi
denotes the automorphism of
\mathbb{C}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
given by

\forall i\in I,\qquad\psi(\alpha_{i}):=\alpha_{i^{*}}.

The reason for this choice of notation is given by Theorem
3.7
: we can view
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}\circ{\widetilde{\chi}}_{q}
as an extension to a larger domain of Baumann–Kamnitzer–Knutson’s morphism
\overline{D}
. Hence by convenience, we still denote by
\overline{D}
the map defined above on the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Recall the notation
d_{i}
from (
2.5
). Finally, set
[X_{i,p}^{(0)}]:=1
for any
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. The main result established in this section is the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Fix
Q
and
\xi
as above and let
i\in I,0\leq k<h
and
p
such that
\xi(i)-2h+2\leq p\leq\xi(i)
. Denote
\zeta:=(\xi(i)-p-2k+2)/2
. Then we have that

(4.1)

\overline{D}\left(X_{i,p}^{(k)}\right)=\epsilon_{i,p}^{(k)}\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{i^{*},-p-2h+4}^{(h-k)}\right)

where
\epsilon_{i,p}^{(k)}\in\{\pm 1\}
is given by

\epsilon_{i,p}^{(k)}:=\begin{cases}(-1)^{d_{i+\zeta}-d_{\zeta}}&\text{if $\mathfrak{g}$ is of type $A_{n}$,}\\
(-1)^{d_{n-1}+d_{n}-d_{i+\zeta-n+1}-d_{\zeta}}&\text{if $\mathfrak{g}$ is of type $D_{n}$ and $i\leq n-2$,}\\
(-1)^{d_{(\sigma^{\zeta})(i)}-d_{\zeta}}&\text{if $\mathfrak{g}$ is of type $D_{n}$ and $i\in\{n-1,n\}$, }\end{cases}

where
\sigma:I\to I
is the involution of type
D_{n}
Dynkin diagram given by
\sigma(i)=i
for
i\in[n-2]
,
\sigma(n)=n-1
,
\sigma(n-1)=n
.

Given a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of type
A_{n},n\geq 1
or
D_{n},n\geq 4
and denoting by
I
the set of vertices of the corresponding Dynkin diagram, we will say that
i\in I
is spin if
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
D_{n}
and

i\in\{n-1,n\}
and that
i
is generic (resp. strictly generic) if
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n}
or if
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
D_{n}
and
i\leq n-2
(resp.
i<n-2
). Furthermore, in all what follows we will use the following notations. Let
\eta:\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
denote the map

\eta:s\longmapsto-s-2h+4.

Note that
\eta
induces a bijection

\eta:\{s,\xi(i^{*})-h\geq s>\xi(i)-2h+2\}\longrightarrow\{s^{\prime},\xi^{*}(i^{*})\geq s^{\prime}>\xi^{*}(i)-h+2\}.

We also define a map
\rho:\{0,\ldots,h\}\longrightarrow\{0,\ldots,h\}
by setting

\rho:k\longmapsto h-k.

We denote by
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{Q}}
the smallest full monoidal subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
containing the fundamental representations
L(Y_{i,p}),i\in I,\xi(i)\geq p\geq\xi(i)-2h+2
. Note that
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{Q}}
contains
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
as a full monoidal subcategory.
We moreover introduce an algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\xi}
which should be regarded as a dual of

, in the sense that it is generated by classes of KR modules obtained from those of

by performing the substitutions

as the sub-algebra of the fraction field of

generated by the classes of

\Sigma=\Sigma_{1}\sqcup\Sigma_{2}

where

\Sigma_{1}:=\{[X_{j,s}^{(k)}]^{\pm 1},\xi^{*}(j)\geq s>\xi^{*}(j)-2h+2,k=(\xi^{*}(j)-s+2)/2\}

and

\Sigma_{2}:=\{[X_{j,s}^{(h)}]^{\pm 1},\xi^{*}(j)\geq s>\xi^{*}(j)-2h+2\}.

The strategy of the proof is the following. First, we show that (
4.1
) holds for the KR modules belonging to the initial seed, i.e. in the case

and

. This is what is done in Section

(resp.

).

4.2.
Classes of KR modules in
\Sigma_{1}

In this subsection, we establish the following special case of Theorem
4.1
which deals with classes of the subset
\Sigma_{1}
. Note that it is valid for any orientation
Q
of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
i\in I
,
k\geq 1
and let
p:=\xi(i)-2k+2
. Then one has

\overline{D}\left(X_{i,p}^{(k)}\right)=(-1)^{d_{i}}\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{i^{*},\eta(p)}^{(\rho(k))}\right).

Proof.

Recall that by Lemma
3.8
we have

\overline{D}\left(X_{i,p}^{(k)}\right)=\prod_{j\in I,s\leq\xi(j)}\beta_{j,s}^{-\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p+1)}

with
p:=\xi(i)-2k+2
. Moreover,
\tilde{C}_{i,j}(m)=0
if
m\leq 0
so that the product runs in fact only on the set
\{(j,s),j\in I,p\leq s\leq\xi(j)\}
. We split this set into three disjoint subsets as follows:

\{p\leq s\leq\xi(j)\}=\{p\leq s\leq\xi(j^{*})-h\}\sqcup\{\xi(j^{*})-h<s<p+h\}\sqcup\{p+h\leq s\leq\xi(j)\}.

There is an obvious bijection between the first and the third subset, given by
(j,s)\longmapsto(j^{*},s+h)
. Hence using Proposition
3.1
, the product over the first and the third subsets can be written as

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in I\\
p\leq s\leq\xi(j^{*})-h\end{subarray}}\beta_{j,s}^{-\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle}\beta_{j^{*},s+h}^{-\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j^{*},s+h}\rangle}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in I\\
p\leq s\leq\xi(j^{*})-h\end{subarray}}\beta_{j,s}^{-\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle}\left(-\beta_{j,s}\right)^{\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle}=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}}

where

d^{\prime}_{i}:=\sum_{(j,s),p\leq s\leq\xi(j^{*})-h}\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}.

Thus we have

\overline{D}\left(X_{i,p}^{(k)}\right)=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in I\\
\xi(j^{*})-h<s<p+h\end{subarray}}\beta_{j,s}^{-\tilde{C}_{i,j}(s-p+1)}.

Now we can rewrite this expression using Lemma
2.5
as follows.

\displaystyle\overline{D}\left(X_{i,p}^{(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}}\prod_{\xi(j^{*})-h<s<p+h}\beta_{j,s}^{-\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}}\prod_{\xi(j^{*})-h<s<p+h}\left(-w_{0}\beta_{j,-s-h+2}^{*}\right)^{-\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}}\prod_{-p-2h+2<s<-\xi(j^{*})+2}\left(-w_{0}\beta_{j,s}^{*}\right)^{-\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,-s-h+2}\rangle_{Q}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}}\prod_{-p-2h+2<s<-\xi(j^{*})+2}\left(-w_{0}\beta_{j,s}^{*}\right)^{-\langle w_{0}\beta_{j,-s-h+2},w_{0}\beta_{i,p}\rangle_{Q^{*}}}\quad\text{by Lemma~\ref{lem : Euler forms},}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}}\prod_{-p-2h+2<s<-\xi(j^{*})+2}\left(-w_{0}\beta_{j,s}^{*}\right)^{-\langle\beta_{j,s}^{*},\beta_{i,-p-h+2}^{*}\rangle_{Q^{*}}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}+d^{\prime\prime}_{i}}\prod_{-p-2h+2<s<-\xi(j^{*})+2}\left(w_{0}\beta_{j,s}^{*}\right)^{-\langle\beta_{j,s}^{*},\beta_{i,-p-h+2}^{*}\rangle_{Q^{*}}}

where

d^{\prime\prime}_{i}:=\sum_{(j,s),j\in I,-p-2h+2<s<-\xi(j^{*})+2}\langle\beta_{j,s}^{*},\beta_{i,-p-h+2}^{*}\rangle_{Q^{*}}.

Then, using the Auslander–Reiten formula, we have that

\langle\beta_{j,s}^{*},\beta_{i,-p-h+2}^{*}\rangle_{Q^{*}}=-\langle\beta_{j,s}^{*},\beta_{i^{*},-p-2h+2}^{*}\rangle_{Q^{*}}=\langle\beta_{i^{*},-p-2h+4}^{*},\beta_{j,s}^{*}\rangle_{Q^{*}}=\langle\beta_{i^{*},\eta(p)}^{*},\beta_{j,s}^{*}\rangle_{Q^{*}}.

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{D}\left(X_{i,p}^{(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}+d^{\prime\prime}_{i}}\prod_{-p-2h+2<s<-\xi(j^{*})+2}\left(w_{0}\beta_{j,s}^{*}\right)^{-\langle\beta_{i^{*},\eta(p)}^{*},\beta_{j,s}^{*}\rangle_{Q^{*}}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}+d^{\prime\prime}_{i}}\prod_{\eta(p)\leq s\leq\xi^{*}(j)}\left(w_{0}\beta_{j,s}^{*}\right)^{-\langle\beta_{i^{*},\eta(p)}^{*},\beta_{j,s}^{*}\rangle_{Q^{*}}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}+d^{\prime\prime}_{i}}\prod_{\eta(p)\leq s\leq\xi^{*}(j)}\left(w_{0}\beta_{j,s}^{*}\right)^{-\tilde{C}_{i^{*},j}(s-\eta(p)+1)}

using again Proposition
3.1
. Thus by Lemma
3.8
, this is exactly

\overline{D}\left(X_{i,p}^{(k)}\right)=(-1)^{d^{\prime}_{i}+d^{\prime\prime}_{i}}\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{i^{*},\eta(p)}^{(r)}\right)

where
r=(\xi^{*}(i^{*})-\eta(p)+2)/2
. First of all we have

r=(-\xi(i)+p+2h-2)/2=h-(\xi(i)-p+2)/2=h-k=\rho(k).

Moreover, we claim that
d^{\prime}_{i}+d^{\prime\prime}_{i}=d_{i}
. Indeed, the same arguments as above show that
d^{\prime\prime}_{i}
can be rewritten as

d^{\prime\prime}_{i}=\sum_{(j,s),j\in I,\xi(j^{*})-h<s<p+h}\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}.

Thus we have

d^{\prime}_{i}+d^{\prime\prime}_{i}=\sum_{(j,s),j\in I,p\leq s<p+h}\langle\beta_{i,p},\beta_{j,s}\rangle_{Q}=d_{i}

by Lemma
2.1
which concludes the proof of the Proposition.
∎

Classes of KR modules in

In this subsection we investigate the values of

\Sigma_{2}

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(j,s)

[X_{j,s}^{(h)}]\in\Sigma_{2}

\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{j,s}^{(h)}\right)=\epsilon_{j^{*},\eta(s)}^{(0)},

For each

\forall i\in I,\forall j\geq 0,\gamma_{i,j}:=\begin{cases}\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)},&\text{if $j\equiv n-1\mod h$ and $i$ is spin,}\\

For each

Lemma 4.5

For any
i\in I

\text{If $i$ is generic, then}\kern 5.0pt\gamma_{i,k-i}+\beta_{i}^{(k)}=\gamma_{i,k}.

\text{If $i$ is spin, then}\kern 5.0pt\gamma_{i,k-i}\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}+\beta_{i}^{(k)}\gamma_{i-1,n-1}=\beta_{\sigma^{n-k+1}(i)}^{(n-1)}\gamma_{i,k}.

Assume first that
i

\beta_{i}^{(k)}=\sum_{l=k-i+1}^{k}\beta_{1}^{(l)}.

\gamma_{i,k-i}+\beta_{i}^{(k)}=\sum_{l=1}^{k-i}\beta_{1}^{(l)}+\sum_{l=k-i+1}^{k}\beta_{1}^{(l)}=\sum_{l=1}^{k}\beta_{1}^{(l)}=\gamma_{i,k}.

Assume now that
i

k-i=n-1

k=i=n-1

In the last case we have
\gamma_{i,k-i}=\beta_{k-i}^{(k-i)}

We also have that
\gamma_{i,k}=\beta_{h-k}^{(n-1)}

\displaystyle\gamma_{i,k-i}\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}+\beta_{i}^{(k)}\gamma_{i-1,n-1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}\left(\beta_{k-i}^{(k-i)}+\beta_{i}^{(k)}\right)+\beta_{i}^{(k)}\beta_{\sigma^{n-k+1}(i)}^{(n-1)}

\displaystyle=\beta_{i}^{(k)}\beta_{\sigma^{n-k+1}(i)}^{(n-1)}+\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}\left(\beta_{i}^{(k)}-\beta_{k-i}^{(k)}\right)\quad\text{as $\tau^{h/2}=-1$,}

\displaystyle=\beta_{i}^{(k)}\beta_{\sigma^{n-k+1}(i)}^{(n-1)}+\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}\left(\beta_{h-k}^{(n-1)}-\beta_{\sigma(i)}^{(k)}\right)\quad\text{using mesh relations,}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}\beta_{h-k}^{(n-1)}+\beta_{i}^{(k)}\beta_{\sigma^{n-k+1}(i)}^{(n-1)}-\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}\beta_{\sigma(i)}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{0.92}{\mbox{$\displaystyle\beta_{\sigma^{n-k+1}(i)}^{(n-1)}\beta_{h-k}^{(n-1)}+\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}(\beta_{h-k}^{(n-1)}-\beta_{\sigma(i)}^{(k)})-\beta_{\sigma^{n-k+1}(i)}^{(n-1)}(\beta_{h-k}^{(n-1)}-\beta_{i}^{(k)}).$}}

It remains to observe that

\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}=\beta_{h-k}^{(n-1)}-\beta_{i}^{(k)}.

Therefore we get

\gamma_{i,k-i}\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}+\beta_{i}^{(k)}\gamma_{i-1,n-1}=\beta_{\sigma^{n-k+1}(i)}^{(n-1)}\beta_{h-k}^{(n-1)}=\beta_{\sigma^{n-k+1}(i)}^{(n-1)}\gamma_{i,k}.

which is the desired identity.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

For any
i\in I
and
k\geq 1
, we have that

R_{i}^{(k)}-R_{i}^{(k+1)}=\beta_{i}^{(k)}R_{i-1}^{(k)}.

Proof.

Recall that
R_{j}^{(l)}=0
if
l\leq j
. Thus if
k<i
, then
R_{i}^{(k)}=R_{i}^{(k+1)}=R_{i-1}^{(k)}=0
so that the identity is trivial. We can hence assume
k\geq i
. If
i
is generic, then so is
i-1
and thus we have that
\gamma_{i,j}=\gamma_{i-1,j}
for all
j\geq 0
. Hence from the definition of
R_{i}^{(k)}
, we have

R_{i}^{(k)}-\beta_{i}^{(k)}R_{i-1}^{(k)}=(-1)^{i}\left(\prod_{j=k-i}^{k-1}\gamma_{i,j}+\beta_{i}^{(k)}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{k-1}\gamma_{i-1,j}\right)=(-1)^{i}(\gamma_{i,k-i}+\beta_{i}^{(k)})\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{k-1}\gamma_{i,j}.

By Lemma
4.5
we obtain

R_{i}^{(k)}-\beta_{i}^{(k)}R_{i-1}^{(k)}=(-1)^{i}\gamma_{i,k}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{k-1}\gamma_{i,j}=(-1)^{i}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{k}\gamma_{i,j}=R_{i}^{(k+1)}

which establishes the desired identity.
Assume now that
i
is spin, and without loss of generality
i=n-1
. If
k=h
then
k-i=n-1
. If
k=i=n-1
then
k=n-1
. If
i<k<h
then
n-1\in\{k-i+1,k-1\}
.
In the last case we have

R_{i}^{(k)}-\beta_{i}^{(k)}R_{i-1}^{(k)}=(-1)^{i}\left(\gamma_{i,k-i}\beta_{\sigma^{n-k}(i)}^{(n-1)}+\beta_{i}^{(k)}\gamma_{i-1,n-1}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k-i+1\leq j\leq k-1\\
j\neq n-1\mathrm{mod}h\end{subarray}}\gamma_{i,j}.

Using the previous lemma, we obtain

R_{i}^{(k)}-\beta_{i}^{(k)}R_{i-1}^{(k)}=(-1)^{i}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{k}\gamma_{i,j}^{(k+1)}=R_{i}^{(k+1)}

which concludes the proof.

∎

Now we set
Q_{i}^{(k)}:=R_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k-1)}
for every
i\in I
and
k\geq 1
, and we show that the rational functions
Q_{i}^{(k)}
and
P_{i}^{(k)}
are related by identities corresponding to T-systems.

Lemma 4.7
.

If
i
is strictly generic then we have

Q_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k)}=Q_{i}^{(k+1)}P_{i}^{(k-1)}+Q_{i-1}^{(k)}P_{i+1}^{(k)}.

If
i=n-2
in type
D_{n}
then we have

Q_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k)}=Q_{i}^{(k+1)}P_{i}^{(k-1)}+Q_{i-1}^{(k)}P_{n-1}^{(k)}P_{n}^{(k)}.

If
i
is spin, then we have

Q_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k)}=Q_{i}^{(k+1)}P_{i}^{(k-1)}+Q_{i-1}^{(k)}.

Proof.

If
i
is strictly generic we have

\displaystyle Q_{i}^{(k+1)}P_{i}^{(k-1)}+Q_{i-1}^{(k)}P_{i+1}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=R_{i}^{(k+1)}P_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k-1)}+R_{i-1}^{(k)}P_{i-1}^{(k-1)}P_{i+1}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=R_{i}^{(k+1)}P_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k-1)}+R_{i-1}^{(k)}\beta_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k-1)}\quad\text{by~\eqref{eq : relation B for initial KRs},}

\displaystyle=\left(R_{i}^{(k+1)}+\beta_{i}^{(k)}R_{i-1}^{(k)}\right)P_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k-1)}

\displaystyle=R_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k-1)}\quad\text{by Corollary~\ref{coro : recursion on Rs}}

\displaystyle=Q_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k)}.

The two other cases are done similarly.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.
.

Recall that
R_{i}^{(k)}=0
if
k\leq i
, so in particular, we have that
Q_{i}^{(1)}=R_{i}^{(1)}=0
for every
i\in I
. On the other hand, we have that
\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{i,\xi^{*}(i)+2}^{(1)}\right)=0
as the truncated
q
-character with respect to
\xi^{*}
of
L(Y_{i,\xi^{*}(i)+2})
is trivial. Thus the desired statement trivially holds for every
i\in I
if
k=1
.
Let
k\geq 1
and assume we have proved that

\overline{D}^{*}(L(Y_{i,\xi^{*}(i)+2}\cdots Y_{i,\xi^{*}(i)-2k+4}))=Q_{i}^{(k)}.

for all
i\in I
.
Let
i\in I
and
s:=\xi^{*}(i)-2k+2
. Note that given our choice of orientation, we have that
\xi^{*}(i-1)=\xi^{*}(i)-1
so that
s+1=\xi^{*}(i-1)-2k+4
. Thus by induction assumption we have

Q_{i}^{(k)}=\overline{D}^{*}\left([X_{i,s+2}^{(k)}]\right)\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptQ_{i-1}^{(k)}=\overline{D}^{*}\left([X_{i,s+1}^{(k)}]\right).

As
P_{j}^{(l)}\neq 0
for any
j\in I
and
l\geq 0
, Lemma
4.7
then implies

\displaystyle Q_{i}^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{P_{i}^{(k-1)}}\left(Q_{i}^{(k)}P_{i}^{(k)}-Q_{i-1}^{(k)}P_{i+1}^{(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{D}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{[X_{i,s+2}^{(k-1)}]}\left([X_{i,s+2}^{(k)}][X_{i,s}^{(k)}]-[X_{i-1,s+1}^{(k)}][X_{i+1,s+1}^{(k)}]\right)\right)=\overline{D}^{*}\left([X_{i,s}^{(k+1)}]\right)

using the T-systems. This concludes the proof of the Proposition.
∎

From this, we can now derive the two following important consequences.

Corollary 4.8
.

The rational functions
\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{j,\xi^{*}(j)-2h+4}^{(k)}\right)
satisfy identities analogous to (
3.4
), namely denoting
\widetilde{X_{i,p}}:=X_{i,p}^{(\xi^{*}(i)-p+4)/2}
for each
(i,p)\in\widehat{I}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\overline{D}^{*}\left([\widetilde{X_{i,p}}][\widetilde{X_{i,p+2}}]\right)=\beta_{i,p+2}^{-1}\prod_{j\sim i}\overline{D}^{*}\left([\widetilde{X_{j,p+1}}]\right).

Proof.

Using the previous notations, Lemma
4.4
implies that it suffices to check that the following hold: if
i
is strictly generic, then

\beta_{i}^{(k-1)}Q_{i}^{(k)}Q_{i}^{(k-1)}=Q_{i-1}^{(k-1)}Q_{i+1}^{(k)}.

This immediately follows from the definition of
Q_{i}^{(k)}
and from (
3.4
). The other cases are done similarly.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
i\in I
and
p:=\xi^{*}(i)-2h+4
. Then we have

\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{i,p-2}^{(h+1)}\right)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{i,p}^{(h)}\right)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{d_{i+1}-d_{1}}&\text{if $i$ is strictly generic,}\\
(-1)^{d_{n-1}+d_{n}-d_{1}}&\text{if $i=n-2$ in type $D_{n}$,}\\
(-1)^{d_{\sigma(i)}-d_{1}}&\text{if $i$ is spin.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
i\in I
. Note that
h+1
is not equal to
n-1
modulo
h
and hence the formula for
\gamma_{i,h}
is the same whether
i
is spin or generic. Hence we have that

\gamma_{i,h}=\sum_{l=1}^{h}\beta_{1}^{(l)}=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{h-1}\tau^{l}\right)(\gamma_{1})=0

as
\tau^{h}=id
and it is known that
1
is not eigenvalue of
\tau
(cf.
[
Bou02
, Chapter V, §6]
). Hence we get

Q_{i}^{(h+1)}=R_{i}^{(h+1)}P_{i}^{(h)}=(-1)^{i}P_{i}^{(h)}\prod_{j=h+1-i}^{h}\gamma_{i,j}=0.

Consequently, if
i
is strictly generic then Lemma
4.7
at the rank
i
with
k=h
implies that

Q_{i}^{(h)}P_{i}^{(h)}=Q_{i-1}^{(h)}P_{i+1}^{(h)}.

By Proposition
3.4
, this yields

Q_{i}^{(h)}=(-1)^{d_{i+1}-d_{i}}Q_{i-1}^{(h)}

and hence a straightforward induction gives

Q_{i}^{(h)}=(-1)^{d_{i+1}-d_{1}}.

Similarly, if
i=n-2
in type
D_{n}
, we get

Q_{i}^{(h)}P_{i}^{(h)}=Q_{i-1}^{(h)}P_{n-1}^{(h)}P_{n}^{(h)}

and thus using the formula for
i=n-3
previously obtained we get that

Q_{n-2}^{(h)}=(-1)^{d_{n-1}+d_{n}-d_{1}}.

Finally if
i
is spin, we get

Q_{i}^{(h)}P_{i}^{(h)}=Q_{n-2}^{(h)}

which implies using what precedes

Q_{i}^{(h)}=(-1)^{d_{\sigma(i)}-d_{1}}.

Together with Lemma
4.4
, we obtain the desired statement.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

Let us sum up what comes out of Lemma
4.4
, Corollary
4.8
and Corollary
4.9
. Using the fact that the
\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{j,\xi^{*}(j)-2h+2}^{(k)}\right)
satisfy (
3.4
), we proved that

\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{j,\xi^{*}(j)-2h+2}^{(h+1)}\right)=0

and moreover that the
\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{j,\xi^{*}(j)-2h+4}^{(k)}\right)
satisfy analogous relations as (
3.4
) (given by Corollary
4.8
). Therefore we can repeat the exact same procedure inductively: using the relations given by Corollary
4.8
, mesh relations, and T-systems as we did in the proof of Lemma
4.4
, we can show that
\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{j,\xi^{*}(j)-2h+4}^{(h+1)}\right)=0
and deduce from that the values of
\overline{D}^{*}\left(X_{j,\xi^{*}(j)-2h+6}^{(h)}\right)
just as what was done to prove Corollary
4.9
. A straightforward induction allows to conclude, which completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

∎

Exceptional types

In type
E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}
, we start from the initial seed and assign
{\widetilde{D}}
to the cluster variables and frozen variables in the seed. We apply mutation sequences which produce Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules (in particular, fundamental modules) to the initial seed. We have checked that
{\widetilde{D}}
of full
q
-characters of fundamental modules are all zero. This implies that for any non-trivial standard module
M
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}}
of type
E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}
, we have that
\widetilde{D}_{\xi}\left(\chi_{q}(M)\right)=0
. The SageMath codes for these computations could be found in:

https://github.com/lijr07/D-tilde-of-fundamental-modules-in-type-E
.

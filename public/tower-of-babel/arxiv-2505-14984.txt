CRAFT : Training-Free Cascaded Retrieval for Tabular QA
Jianxi Gao

Abstract
Table Question Answering (TQA) involves retrieving relevant tables from a large corpus to answer natural language queries. Traditional dense retrieval models, such as DTR and ColBERT, not only incur high computational costs for large-scale retrieval tasks but also require retraining or fine-tuning on new datasets, limiting their adaptability to evolving domains and knowledge. In this work, we propose CRAFT , a cascaded retrieval approach that first uses a sparse retrieval model to filter a subset of candidate tables before applying more computationally expensive dense models and neural re-rankers. Our approach achieves better retrieval performance than state-of-the-art (SOTA) sparse, dense, and hybrid retrievers. We further enhance table representations by generating table descriptions and titles using Gemini Flash 1.5 Team et al. 2025 . End-to-end TQA results using various Large Language Models (LLMs) on NQ-Tables Herzig et al. 2021 , a subset of the Natural Questions Dataset, demonstrate CRAFT effectiveness.

1
Introduction

Tables serve as a vital format for representing structured knowledge, yet enabling open-domain question answering (QA) systems to retrieve and interpret tabular information effectively remains a non-trivial challenge. Unlike traditional Tabular QA (TQA) settings that assume access to gold tables, open-domain QA must first identify relevant tables from a large corpus before reasoning over their structure to derive accurate answers
Liang et al. 2024
. Simple approaches that linearize tables into text and apply standard text-retrieval methods can lose the syntactic and structural information encoded in rows and columns
Badaro et al. 2023
;
Min et al. 2024
. As a result, effective table retrieval has been identified as a key bottleneck for scalable QA over tabular data
Li et al. 2025
.

Figure 1:

Recent work has started to tackle open-domain TQA by developing specialized retrieval models. Early approaches adopted sparse or dense text retrievers on flattened tables (e.g., BM25 or bi-encoder models)
Fan et al. 2024
;
Robertson and Zaragoza 2009
. For example, the Dense Table Retriever (DTR) introduced by
Herzig et al. 2021
augments a bi-encoder with row and column features to better capture table structure. More recent methods explicitly incorporate fine-grained structure:
Jin et al. 2023
propose a syntax- and structure-aware retriever that represents each question phrase and each table header and values separately, achieving state-of-the-art results on the NQ-Tables benchmark. Likewise,
Li et al. 2025
introduces THYME, a field-aware hybrid retriever that tailors matching strategies to different table fields (e.g., cell values vs. captions). THYME significantly outperforms prior baselines on open-domain table retrieval, highlighting the gains from table-specific modeling. Similarly,
Pan et al. 2022
introduced T-RAG, an end-to-end table QA framework that combines table retrievers with sequence-to-sequence generators for retrieval-augmented generation. These advances, however, often rely on complex architectures or extensive fine-tuning on the TQA dataset. This raises an intriguing question:
Can off-the-shelf pretrained models, when integrated into a carefully designed retrieval pipeline, achieve competitive performance to expensive domain-specialized, fine-tuned methods?
To this end, we propose
CRAFT
, a modular and scalable multi-stage retrieval framework for TQA that harnesses pretrained models without relying on dataset-specific fine-tuning.

Our work introduces
CRAFT
, a training free cascaded tabular retriever algorithm that achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art tabular retrievers requiring extensive in-domain training. Through comprehensive ablation studies, we demonstrate the robustness of
CRAFT
across varying retrieval threshold
K
, highlighting its adaptability and stability. Building on this foundation, we develop a complete end-to-end TQA system that outperforms all existing TQA-
in-the-wild
, showcasing the effectiveness of our retrieval approach in real-world, open-domain settings.

2
Methodology

To create a retrieval setup without dataset-specific fine-tuning, we adopt a modular framework that leverages off-the-shelf models with multi-stage retrieval, with more complex models for each successive stage. As shown in Figure
1
, CRAFT involves prepossessing followed by stage-wise table retrieval using increasingly expressive models, culminating in answer generation via a pretrained LLM. Our approach demonstrates that pretrained models can effectively retrieve relevant tabular data and generate accurate answers compared to the state-of-the-art framework
Zhao et al. 2024
. The pipeline consists of leveraging preprocessing followed by cascaded table retrieval and end-to-end answer generation to maximize semantic alignment between the input queries and tabular content using off-the-shelf embeddings models.

2.1
Preprocessing

The preprocessing step involved three key operations to enhance the query and table representations for effective retrieval. Firstly, Gemini-1.5-Flash
Team et al. 2025
was employed to generate sub-questions derived from each original query, thus decomposing complex queries into more granular components. Secondly, table titles and comprehensive descriptions were generated using Gemini-1.5-Flash, enriching contextual understanding and enabling more precise downstream retrieval. Finally, all rows across every table were ranked using a Sentence Transformer (
all-mpnet-base-v2
) model
SentenceTransformers 2022
, applied directly to the original queries to assess semantic relevance. The NQ-Tables Dataset statistics are shown in Table
7
.

2.2
Table Retrieval

The table retrieval step involves a rigorous, three-stage filtering methodology to identify highly relevant tables for each query. In the first stage,
SPLADE

Formal et al. 2021
was applied to a comprehensive data set consisting of 169,898 tables, leveraging generated table titles, column headers, individual cell values, and detailed table descriptions to filter and narrow down the candidate tables to the top 5,000 entries. SPLADE leverages sparse lexical expansion to efficiently score candidate passages using minimal memory, making it well-suited for handling large-scale retrieval tasks.

In the second stage, the Sentence Transformer
(
all-mpnet-base-v2
) provides a fine-grained semantic refinement by representing each table with mini-tables created through appending column headers to each of the top 5 rows. This refinement approach allows the selection of the top 1,000 mini-tables. In the final stage, the
text-embedding-3-small

OpenAI 2024
model performs a detailed re-ranking on the top 100 mini-tables, carefully assessing these mini-tables based on their semantic embeddings. Concurrently, we enrich all 919 unique queries using sub-question decomposition generated by
Gemini-1.5-Flash
to maximize their semantic alignment with the selected tables.

2.3
End-to-End Answer Generation

For the end-to-end table question-answering, we use the final retrieval result on the set of 919 queries with three different pretrained models:
Llama3-8B
,
Qwen2.5-7B
, and
Mistralv0.2-7B
. Each LLM generates answers from prompts that include a varying number of tables (
n=\{1,3,5,8,10\}
). Each prompt contains the table title, column headers, and mini-tables (top 5 rows) for
n
tables, offering comprehensive yet concise context to support accurate answer generation. In addition, we also experiment with the inclusion of a few-shot prompt to guide the text generation process.

We evaluate model performance and accuracy by computing the F1 score, which quantifies the overlap between generated answers and reference answers, enabling comparative analysis across different models and table settings.

Ablation over stages further shows incremental gains: Stage 1 drops performance under perturbation by 3.4 %, Stage 2 partly recovers it, and Stage 3 restores the original recall, as shown in Appendix

4
Conclusion

We present
CRAFT
, a modular, training-free, multi-stage cascaded retrieval framework for TQA that matches or exceeds the performance of state-of-the-art systems trained on NQ-Tables. Without any dataset-specific training, it achieves competitive SOTA recall scores, maintains robustness under query perturbation, and delivers a high end-to-end performance score when paired with existing pretrained LLMs. By decoupling retrieval from domain-specific fine-tuning,
CRAFT
offers a scalable, adaptable solution for dynamic tabular domains where rapid deployment and data scarcity are critical issues.

5
Limitations

While our evaluation focuses on the NQ-Tables benchmark, future validation on additional TQA datasets would help further assess the generalizability of our approach. Although our multi-stage retrieval pipeline, combining sparse filtering, dense semantic reranking, and neural reranking, introduces added inference steps, this design enables significantly improved retrieval accuracy, offering a trade-off between performance and runtime that can be optimized for specific deployment needs.

A.2
Ablation Study

Stage 1: Sparse Retrieval with Context Enrichment

We test the effect of table descriptions and query decomposition using BM25 and SPLADE. The results for Stage 1 are shown in Table
6
and Table
8
. Both retrievers exhibit improvement when employing Question Decomposition. Specifically, SPLADE achieves gains of 2.18% and 4.03% at R@10 and R@50 respectively. However, incorporating Table Descriptions does not yield additional improvements for SPLADE, likely because the tables alone already exhaust the maximum context length of the SPLADE model, rendering extra descriptions redundant. Notably, SPLADE achieves a Recall@5000 of 99.59%, indicating performance saturation and leaving minimal scope for further enhancement. In contrast, BM25 demonstrates substantial improvement with the inclusion of table descriptions, clearly benefiting from context-enriched lexical matching.

Stage:2 Dense Semantic Reranking and Irrelevant Context Reduction

We apply
all-mpnet-base-v2
to rerank the top 5000 tables using dense embeddings. Since dense embedding models have limited context length, we rank the rows of each table and retain the 5 most relevant rows per table. Table
9
show recall gains from filtering irrelevant rows, with R@10 rising from 79.21 to 82.91 and R@50 from 94.66 to 96.08. Dense scoring better captures semantic relevance missed in Stage 1.

While deeper retrieval improves accuracy, computational efficiency remains critical for practical deployment. Table
13
compares the average token counts for each model when using only the top-
k
sub-table context versus concatenating the entire table. Across all three models and
k\in{1,3,5}
, sub-table inputs reduce token length by over 70%. For example, Llama3 drops from 1782.80 tokens at
k=1
to 362.70; Mistral from 3314.03 to 476.58; and Qwen2.5 from 1951.08 to 393.68. This substantial reduction directly lowers inference costs and improves runtime efficiency. By selectively presenting only the most row table fragments,
CRAFT
retains essential semantic content while avoiding the diminishing returns and latency overhead associated with full-table encoding.

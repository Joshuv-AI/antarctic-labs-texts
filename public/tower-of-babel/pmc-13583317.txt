Efficacy and safety of different TPO-RAs combined with cyclosporine in the treatment of NSAA
Authors: Ting Wang, Boyi Wang, Donglan Liu, Nianbin Li, Shuai Gao, Huaquan Wang, Lijuan Li, Limin Xing, Hong Liu, Guojin Wang, Yuhong Wu, Wen Qu, Jia Song, Jing Guan, Zonghong Shao, Rong Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTPO receptor agonists (TPO-RAs) have significantly improved the treatment outcomes of patients with aplastic anemia. Currently, three small-molecule TPO-RAs (eltrombopag, hetrombopag, and avatrombopag) are available for clinical use and work well.ObjectivesThis article aims to compare the efficacy and safety of these TPO-RAs in treating non-severe aplastic anemia (NSAA).DesignThis was a single-center, retrospective observational cohort study.MethodsA retrospective analysis was performed to assess the efficacy and side effects of cyclosporine A (CsA) combined with different TPO - RAs in 67 NSAA patients. Patients were divided into three groups according to the type of TPO-RA received: eltrombopag, hetrombopag, or avatrombopag. The overall response rate (ORR) was evaluated at 3 months and 6 months after treatment initiation. Adverse events, including liver impairment, infection, and death, were recorded and compared among the three groups.ResultsIn total,the 3-month overall response rate (ORR) of CsA combined with TPO-RAs was 71.6%, the 6-month ORR was 82.1%. The ORRs among eltrombopag, hetrombopag, and avatrombopag groups were no statistically significant difference at the 3-month (P=0.334) or 6-month (P=0.776). Due to the pre-screening of patients with underlying liver conditions, the occurrence of side effects including new liver impairment, infection and death, showed no significant differences among the three groups. High level of absolute reticulocyte count at enrollment and adequate initial treatment dose of TPO-RAs are independent factors for better efficacy at 6 months. Old age (P=0.034) and infection(P=0.000) were the independent factors affecting the duration of hospitalization.ConclusionsThis study shows that eltrombopag, hetrombopag, and avatrombopag combined with CsA have comparable efficacy in the treatment of NSAA. Identifying patients at high risk for liver injury before treatment and initiating avatrombopag therapy represents a viable clinical treatment option for NSAA patients with pre-existing liver injury. Similar to SAA, NSAA patients should also be initiated with an adequate dose of TPO-RAs therapy. Elderly patients and those with concurrent infections are at higher risk of mortality, prolonged hospitalizations, and require more clinical attention.

Introduction

Aplastic Anemia (AA) is a serious hematological disease characterized by bone marrow failure leading to pancytopenia. AA is classified into severe AA (SAA) and non-severe AA (NSAA) based on severity. 1 NSAA can be further divided into transfusion-dependent NSAA (TD-NSAA) and non-transfusion-dependent NSAA (NTD-NSAA). Specifically, TD-NSAA follows the treatment strategy of SAA, whereas NTD-NSAA typically employs cyclosporine (CsA) in combination with hematopoiesis-promoting therapies. TPO receptor agonists (TPO-RAs) promote hematopoietic stem cell proliferation and differentiation in the bone marrow by activating TPO receptors, thus restoring hematopoiesis and increasing platelet count. 2 Eltrombopag3–5 and hetrombopag 6 are approved for treating SAA, and avatrombopag7–9 is recommended for SAA patients with underlying liver disease in China. The combination of CsA and TPO-RA is widely used in NSAA patients, achieving good efficacy, but the differences in efficacy and safety among various TPO-RAs remain unclear. This study aims to retrospectively analyze the efficacy and safety of different TPO-RAs in the treatment of NSAA, including some TD-NSAA patients who could not undergo hematopoietic stem cell transplantation or ATG, which will help understand the role of different TPO-RAs in the treatment of NSAA and provide data support for selecting the optimal treatment plan for NSAA patients.

Methods

This study follows the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement for cohort studies. 10 The completed STROBE checklist is provided as a supplementary file.

This was a single-center, retrospective observational cohort study. The retrospective observational study collected data from 67 newly diagnosed NSAA patients treated at the Department of Hematology, Tianjin Medical University General Hospital, from January 2020 to June 2023. Enrolled patients adhered to the complete set of the following criteria, as per the guidelines of the British Society for Standards in Hematology concerning AA, 1 (1) Bone marrow hypoproliferation was presented at multiple sites (including both sides of the posterior superior iliac spine and sternal manubrium), cell morphology, flow-cytometry of marrow aspirates, chromosome karyotyping, Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization (FISH),MDS-NGS testing and TCR/BCR rearrangement were performed for each patient; (2) NSAA was classified according to Camitta criteria as AA not fulfilling the criteria for severe or very severe (VS)AA(i.e., marrow cellularity <25%, plus at least 2 of (i) neutrophils <0.5 × 109/L, (ii) platelets <20 × 109/L (iii), reticulocyte count <20 × 109/L); (3)Patients diagnosed with inherited bone marrow failures (BMF) were excluded (i.e., family history, young age at presentation etc.), genetic screen for inherited BMF was performed in patients under 25 years of age. MDS also excluded by cell morphology, chromosome karyotyping, FISH, MDS-NGS. (4) All patients received CsA combined with TPO-RAs and were regularly followed up for at least 6 months. The median follow-up time, including patients who died early, was 1008 days (range: 28–1613 days).

The Ethics Committee of Tianjin Medical University General Hospital approved this study according to the guidelines of the Helsinki Declaration (Approval No. IRB2021-YX-045-01). Written informed consent was obtained from patients or the patient’s legal guardians.

Treatment regimens

Patients within CsA at a dose of 3-5 mg/(kg ⋅ day) for daily use, and tailored to maintain a whole blood trough level ranging from 150-250 ng/ml. Administration of G-CSF, erythropoietin, TPO-RA, and blood transfusion were permissible. We divided all patients into three groups:(1) CsA combined with eltrombopag (42 cases) (2) CsA combined with hetrombopag (15 cases) (3) CsA combined with avatrombopag (10 cases). In the study, 10% patients in avatrombopag group, 60% patients in hetrombopag group, 26% patients in Eltrombopag group initiated treatment with a starting dose of three tablets. Due to financial constraints, 60% patients in avatrombopag group, 40% patients in hetrombopag group, 54% patients in Eltrombopag group initiated treatment with a starting dose of two tablets; other patients began with one tablet. (Avatrombopag 20mg/tablet, Hetrombopag 2.5mg/tablet, Eltrombopag 75mg/tablet).

Patients underwent weekly complete blood count (CBC) monitoring. The TPO-RA dose was titrated at each monitoring time point according to the following protocol, based primarily on platelet count: Dose maintenance: if the platelet count was 50–100 × 109/L. Dose reduction: by one tablet if the platelet count was 100–200 × 109/L, or by two tablets if it was 200–400 × 109/L. Treatment discontinuation: if the platelet count exceeded 400 × 109/L. Dose escalation: for patients with a platelet count below 50 × 109/L, the dose was increased by one tablet only if the increment from the measurement two weeks prior was less than 20 × 109/L.

Follow-up

Responses were assessed at different time points according to European Bone Marrow Transplant group criteria 2 (complete, CR, if platelets PLT > 100 × 109/L, hemoglobin Hb >100 g/L, neutrophils-ANC > 1.5 × 109/L; partial, PR, Transfusion independence (if previously dependent) or doubling or normalisation of at least one cell line or increase of baseline (i)Hemoglobin concentration of >30 g/L (if initially <60) (ii) Neutrophils of >0.5×109/L (if initially <0.5) (iii) Platelets of >20×109/L (if initially <20))

For CR patients, cyclosporine is gradually tapered and continued for 12 months. TPO-RA and cyclosporine are alternately reduced by one tablet every two weeks to one month. For PR or NR patients, cyclosporine was continued and maintained at a trough concentration of 150–200 µg/L. The maximal TPO-RA doses administered in this cohort are: hetrombopag 10 mg, eltrombopag 100 mg, and avatrombopag 60 mg. The patient has been maintained on the maximum dose of TPO-RA for at least 6 months, or on the starting dose for at least 6 months due to economic factors.

Statistical analysis

Data description and statistical analysis were conducted using SPSS 27.0 software and R software (version 4.2.2). For numerical variables, normality tests were initially performed. If the data met the normality assumption, they were described statistically using means ± standard deviations, and one-way ANOVA was applied for analysis. When variances were homogeneous, the Bonferroni method was utilized for group comparisons; otherwise, the Tamhane’s method was adopted. In cases where the distribution was non-normal, medians (ranges) were used for description, and multiple-sample rank-sum tests were employed for group comparisons. Categorical data were presented as percentages, and chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests were used for group comparisons. Logistic univariate regression analysis was carried out using the R language, while multivariate regression analysis was performed using SPSS. Statistical significance was considered when the two-sided P < 0.05.

Responses

The 3-month overall response rate (ORR) of CsA combined with TPO-RAs was 71.6% (48/67), with a complete response (CR) rate of 9.0% (6/67); the 6-month ORR was 82.1% (55/67), with a CR rate of 14.9% (10/67). Follow-up evaluations were conducted at 1, 2, 3, and 6 months after the treatment regimen was applied, and later periods. At the end of the first month, there were no CR patients in any group, and the PR rates were 80.0% in avatrombopag group, 60.0% in hetrombopag group, and 69.0% in eltrombopag group, with no significant statistical difference among the three groups (P=0.570). At 2 months, the ORR was 70.0% in avatrombopag group (all PR), 80.0% in hetrombopag group (CR 6. 7%, PR 73.3%), and 69.0% in eltrombopag group (CR 4.8%, PR 64.2%), with no significant statistical difference in ORR among the three groups (P=0.716). At 3 months, the ORR was 90.0% in avatrombopag group (CR 20.0%, PR 70.0%), 73.3% in hetrombopag group (CR 13.3%, PR 60.0%), and 66.7% in eltrombopag group (CR 4.8%, PR 61.9%), with no significant statistical difference in ORR among the three groups (P=0.334). At 6 months, the ORR was 90.0% in avatrombopag group (CR 20.0%, PR 70.0%), 80.0% in hetrombopag group (CR 26.7%, PR 53.3%), and 81.0% in eltrombopag group (CR 9.5%, PR 71.4%), with no significant statistical difference in ORR among the three groups (P=0.776). After the 6-month observation, we conducted a unified phone follow-up with patients on May 30, 2024, and recorded the most recent blood count results. The median duration of the longer follow-up was 1107 days (range: 180–1613 days). Over a longer follow-up period, the effectiveness of the treatment further improved, with ORRs of 100.0% (CR 20.0%, PR 80.0%) in avatrombopag group, 93.3% (CR 26.7%, PR 66.7%) in hetrombopag group, and 81.0% (CR 16.7%, PR 64.3%) in eltrombopag group, with no significant statistical difference in ORR among the three groups (P=0.194); (Figure 1). Events and mortality

We included new liver damage, infections requiring intravenous antibiotic treatment, and patient mortality as the primary adverse events evaluated in this study.

New liver damage was defined as the elevation of either liver enzymes or bilirubin levels in patients with normal pre-treatment levels. The proportion of patients with liver dysfunction in hetrombopag group (13%) and eltrombopag Group (9%) was significantly lower than that in avatrombopag Group(50%) at the time of enrollment. One patient in the eltrombopag group and 2 patients in the hetrombopag group were Hepatitis B virus carrier. In the avatrombopag group, more patients had hepatitis issues, 10 patients were Hepatitis B virus carriers and 1 patient concurrently with Hepatitis C virus, among them, 2 patients had cirrhosis and 1 patient had autoimmune hepatitis.

Due to limited liver compensatory ability, 30% of patients in avatrombopag group developed new liver damage during AA treatment, but none had ≥ 2-fold increases in liver enzymes or bilirubin. No avatrombopag dose adjustments were necessary. Respectively, 13.3% and 7.1% of patients in the hetrombopag and eltrombopag groups experienced new liver damage. One patient in each group had ≥ 2-fold increases in liver enzymes, and liver function returned to normal after reducing the dose of hetrombopag or eltrombopag.

Infections are not rare during the treatment of NSAA, some patients experience simultaneous infections at multiple sites, while others suffer from recurrent infections. The most common site of infection was the respiratory system, with 18 cases (94.7%), followed by gastrointestinal infections (5.3%). Among these, fungal infections were observed in 4 cases (21.1%), bacterial infections in 7 cases (36.8%), Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) infections in 2 cases (10.5%), atypical pathogen infections in 5 cases (26.3%), and mixed infections in 1 case (5.3%). The causes of death included heart failure in 4 cases (57.1%) and infections in 3 cases (42.9%) (shown in Table 3). Average length of first hospitalization

Average length of first hospitalization refers to the mean duration of hospital stay associated with the initial therapeutic intervention following diagnosis. This indicator indirectly reflects the patient’s overall recovery efficiency, encompassing both the aplastic anemia disease and its complications. We recorded the average length of the first hospitalization for patients, which was 20.4±36.0 days for hetrombopag group, 24.6±19.5 days for eltrombopag group, and 25.9±18.0 days for avatrombopag group, with no significant statistical difference between the three groups (P=0.862). Discussion

NSAA, also called “moderate AA”, patients with the disease typically present with a chronic course, and if the condition persists without improvement, there is a risk of progression to severe aplastic anemia (SAA). 11 Clinical trials of TPO-RAs in SAA treatment have shown good therapeutic effects. Compared to the group treated with IST alone, the IST combined with TPO-RA group showed an increase in the 6-month ORR from 66% to 94%, the CR rate from 17% to 58%, the 12-month CR rate from 33% to 52%, and a reduction of the median time to initial response from 8.8 months to 3 months.3–5 But for NSAA, the precise criteria establishing when to start treatment, or the therapeutic strategies are largely lacking. As mentioned by Bruno Fattizzo 12 the majority of treatments are CsA alone or in combination with anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG) or eltrombopag, eltrombopag alone, or others including androgens, or even “watch and wait”. In China hematologists have added TPO-RA into NSAA regimens, trying to improve patient outcomes. Currently, small-molecule TPO-RAs, including eltrombopag, hetrombopag, and avatrombopag, are widely used clinically. According to the guidelines, in our study, avatrombopag was recommended for patients with underlying liver disease, while other patients randomly received either eltrombopag or hetrombopag combined with CsA treatment. The enrolled patients were those presenting with clinical symptoms such as anemia, fatigue, or bleeding, and who were willing to undergo treatment. The overall ORR was 82.1%, slightly higher than the 68% reported in the literature for CsA plus eltrombopag, 12 which may be related to the disease status at the time of enrollment.

We evaluated the remission rates at 3-month and 6-month in the early stages of the disease, as well as the influencing factors. Similar to previous studies, early recovery of reticulocytes is an effective indicator of bone marrow hematopoiesis recovery, which is a prerequisite for the restoration of blood cells. An adequate initial dose of TPO-RA is also an independent factor for treatment response at 6 months. Therefore, we recommend that NSAA should use the similar initial dose as SAA to achieve a faster and better treatment response. The difference between NSAA and SAA treatment more depends on the intensity of the IST.

Eltrombopag, Hetrombopag, and Avatrombopag are small-molecule TPO-RAs targeting the TPO receptor’s transmembrane domain. Although no irreversible treatment-related liver injury was observed in our data, caution is advised when using eltrombopag at doses greater than 100 mg due to the potential risk of hepatotoxicity. Avatrombopag has better hepatic safety 13 ; however, our data showed a higher incidence of new liver dysfunction, which may be attributed to more severe underlying liver conditions compared to the other groups. There was no need to adjust the avatrombopag dose, and no fluctuations in blood counts were observed due to this side effect.

The severity of infections in our cohort ranged from Grade 2 to Grade 5 according to CTCAE (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events). All patients confirmed to have infections presented with clinical symptoms such as fever, cough, diarrhea, and all patients received treatment. Due to the small sample size in each group, we did not further grade the severity of infections. However, we found the most common infections remain respiratory infections; therefore, patients with NSAA need to enhance their personal protective awareness, such as wearing masks.

Our study carries the limitations inherent to its retrospective nature. Firstly, in terms of data collection, some patients had missing data, such as in the avatrombopag treatment group. The fluctuation in reticulocyte data was due to missing data from two patients with a significant increase in reticulocytes. Secondly, these three TPO-RAs show distinctly different effects in terms of iron chelation. Eltrombopag and hetrombopag, containing metal ion-chelating groups, require a two-hour gap between meals or medications to ensure proper absorption.14,15 Avatrombopag, lacking these groups, can be taken with meals. So EPAG and Hetrombopag should be used cautiously in those with gastrointestinal dysfunction or polypharmacy due to potential interactions, but for iron overload patients they aid in iron chelation, with providing a greater therapeutic advantage. Unfortunately, we were unable to assess ferritin changes for each patient, this is also a limitation of our study. Finally, clonal hematopoiesis (CH) is a crucial dimension in evaluating the treatment of NSAA. We performed baseline CH testing for all patients at diagnosis. At baseline, 43.3% of patients were found to have CH, with the most common genes being TET2 (16.4%), ASXL1 (16.4%), DNMT3A (10.4%), and BCOR (7.5%). However, in the real world, only 10 patients underwent genetic re-testing at the end of follow-up. Among these, 3 patients developed new clonal hematopoiesis, with newly detected mutations in PIGA, CUX1, and BRAF. We will track these patients over a longer period and update the data.

Conclusions

This study shows that eltrombopag, hetrombopag, and avatrombopag combined with CsA have comparable efficacy in the treatment of NSAA. Identifying patients at high risk for liver injury before treatment and initiating avatrombopag therapy could offer greater benefits to NSAA patients. Similar to SAA, NSAA patients should also be initiated with an adequate dose of TPO-RAs therapy. Elderly patients and those with concurrent infections are at higher risk of mortality, prolonged hospitalizations, and require more clinical attention.

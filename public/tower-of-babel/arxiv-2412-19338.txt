Solutions for certain Fermat-type PDDEs concerning an open problem of Xu and Wang
Hong Yan Xu, Rajib Mandal and Raju Biswas

Abstract
. The objective of this study is to ascertain the existence and forms of the finite order meromorphic and entire functions of several complex variables satisfying some certain Fermat-type partial differential-difference equations by considering the more general forms of the PDDEs in an open problem on ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} due to Xu and Wang (Notes on the existence of entire solutions for several partial differential-difference equations, Bull. Iran. Math. Soc., 47, 1477-1489 (2020)). We provide examples to illustrate the results.

1.
Introduction, Definitions and Results

By a meromorphic function
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
), we mean that
f
can be written as a quotient of two holomorphic functions without common zero sets in

\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Notationally, we write
f:=\frac{g}{h}
, where
g
and
h
are relatively prime holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
h\not\equiv 0
and

f^{-1}(\infty)\not=\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In particular, the entire function of several complex variables are holomorphic throughout
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Let
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
r>0
. We consider some notations from
[
19
,
38
,
46
]
. Let
\overline{B}_{n}(r):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z|\leq r\}
, where
|z|^{2}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z_{j}|^{2}
.
The exterior derivative splits
d:=\partial+\overline{\partial}
and twists to
d^{c}:=\frac{i}{4\pi}(\overline{\partial}-\partial)
. The standard Kaehler metric on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is
given by
v_{n}(z):=dd^{c}|z|^{2}
. Define
\omega_{n}(z):=dd^{c}\log|z|^{2}\geq 0
and
\sigma_{n}(z):=d^{c}\log|z|^{2}\wedge\omega_{n}^{n-1}(z)
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Thus
\sigma_{n}(z)
defines a positive measure on
\partial B_{n}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z|=r\}
with total measure
1
.
The zero-multiplicity of a holomorphic function
h
at a point

z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is defined to be the order of vanishing of
h
at
z
and denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{0}(z)
. A divisor of
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is an integer valued function which is locally the difference between the
zero-multiplicity functions of
g
and
h
and it is denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{f}:=\mathcal{D}_{g}^{0}-\mathcal{D}_{h}^{0}
(see, P. 381,
[
3
]
). Let
a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
be such that
f^{-1}(a)\not=\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then the
a
-divisor
\nu_{f}^{a}
of
f

is the divisor associated with the holomorphic functions
g-ah
and
h
(see, P. 346,
[
19
]
and P. 12,
[
16
]
).
Ye
[
46
]
has defined the counting function and the valence function with respect to
a
respectively as follows:

\displaystyle n(r,a,f):=r^{2-2n}\int_{S(r)}\nu_{f}^{a}v_{n}^{n-1}\;\text{and}\;N(r,a,f):=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n(r,a,f)}{t}dt.

We write

\displaystyle N(r,a,f)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&N\left(r,\frac{1}{f-a}\right),\;\text{when}\;a\not=\infty\\[5.69054pt]
&N(r,f),\;\text{when}\;a=\infty.\end{array}\right.

The proximity function
[
19
,
46
]
of
f
is defined as follows :

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}m(r,f):=\int_{\partial B_{n}(r)}\log^{+}|f(z)|\sigma_{n}(z),\;\text{when}\;a=\infty\\
m\left(r,\frac{1}{f-a}\right):=\int_{\partial B_{n}(r)}\log^{+}\frac{1}{|f(z)-a|}\sigma_{n}(z),\text{when}\;a\not=\infty.\end{array}

The Nevanlinna characteristic function is defined by
T(r,f)=N(r,f)+m(r,f)
, which is increasing for
r
. The order of a meromorphic function
f
is denoted by
\rho(f)
and is defined by

\displaystyle\rho(f)=\varlimsup\limits_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log^{+}T(r,f)}{\log r},\;\;\text{where}\;\log^{+}x=\max\{\log x,0\}.

The exceptional sets are throughout needed in the Nevanlinna theory. Typically, it means considering the linear measure
m(E):=\int_{E}dt
and the logarithmic measure

l(E):=\int_{E\cap[1,\infty)}dt/t
for a set
E\subset[0,\infty)
.
Recall that a meromorphic function
\alpha
is said to be a small function of
f
, if
T(r,\alpha)=S(r,f)
, where
S(r,f)
is any quantity that satisfies

S(r,f)=o(T(r,f))
as
r\rightarrow\infty
, possibly outside of a set of
r
of finite linear measure.
For further details, we refer to
[
3
,
15
,
16
,
21
,
33
,
37
,
38
,
46
]
and the references therein.
Given a meromorphic function
f(z)
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
f(z+c)
is called a shift of
f
and
\Delta(f)=f(z+c)-f(z)
is called a difference operator of
f
, where
c(\not=0)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

An equation is called a partial differential equation (in brief, PDE) if the equation contains partial derivatives of
f
whereas if the equation also contains shifts or differences of
f
, then the equation is called a partial differential-difference equation (in brief, PDDE).
We now consider the Fermat-type equation

\displaystyle f^{n}(z)+g^{n}(z)=1,\;\text{where}\;n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.3)

We summarize the classical results for solutions of the equation (
1.3
) on
\mathbb{C}
in the following:

Proposition A
.

(i)
[
8
,
17
]
The equation (
1.3
) with
n=2
has the non-constant entire solutions
f(z)=\cos(\eta(z))
and
g(z)=\sin(\eta(z))
, where
\eta(z)
is any entire function. No other solutions exist.

(ii)
[
8
,
9
,
32
]
For
n\geq 3
, there are no non-constant entire solutions of (
1.3
) on
\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition B
.

(i)
[
8
]
The equation (
1.3
) with
n=2
has
the non-constant meromorphic solutions
f=\frac{2\omega}{1+\omega^{2}}
and
g=\frac{1-\omega^{2}}{1+\omega^{2}}
, where
\omega
is an arbitrary meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)
[
1
,
9
]
The equation (
1.3
) with
n=3
has
the non-constant meromorphic solutions
f=\frac{1}{2\wp(h)}\left(1+\frac{\wp^{\prime}(h)}{\sqrt{3}}\right)
,
g=\frac{1}{2\wp(h)}\left(1-\frac{\wp^{\prime}(h)}{\sqrt{3}}\right)
,
where
\wp(z)
denotes the Weierstrass elliptic
\wp
-function with periods
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
is defined as

\displaystyle\wp\left(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\right)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}+\sum\limits_{{\mu,\nu;\mu^{2}+\nu^{2}\not=0}}\left\{\frac{1}{\left(z+\mu\omega_{1}+\nu\omega_{2}\right)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\left(\mu\omega_{1}+\nu\omega_{2}\right)^{2}}\right\},

which is even and satisfying, after appropriately choosing
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
,
(\wp^{\prime})^{2}=4\wp^{3}-1
.

(iii)
[
8
,
9
]
For
n\geq 4
, there are no non-constant meromorphic solutions of (
1.3
) on
\mathbb{C}
.

The basic conclusions Propositions A and B of the Fermat-type equation (
1.3
) on
\mathbb{C}

Proposition C
.

[
10

[

Let
h:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a non-constant entire function and
\mathbb{P}^{1}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. Then the non-constant entire and meromorphic solutions of the equation (
1.3
) on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
are characterized as follows:

(i) when
m=2
, the entire solutions are
f=cos(h)
and
g=sin(h)
;

(ii) when
m>2
, there are no non-constant entire solutions;

(iii) when
m=2
, the meromorphic solutions are
f=\frac{2\omega}{1+\omega^{2}}
and
g=\frac{1-\omega^{2}}{1+\omega^{2}}
, where
\omega:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a non-constant meromorphic function;

(iv) when
m=3
, the meromorphic solutions are

f=\frac{1}{2\wp(h)}\left(1+\frac{\wp^{\prime}(h)}{\sqrt{3}}\right)
and
g=\frac{1}{2\wp(h)}\left(1-\frac{\wp^{\prime}(h)}{\sqrt{3}}\right)
, where
\wp(z)
denotes the Weierstrass elliptic
\wp
-function satisfying the relation
(\wp^{\prime})^{2}=4\wp^{3}-1
. Note that
\wp:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
so that
\wp\circ h:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, i.e.,
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
;

(v) when
m>3
, there are no non-constant meromorphic solutions.

Now researchers have been focusing their attention to investigate on the Fermat-type PDDEs for entire and meromorphic solutions. Let

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial z_{i}}\right)^{m}=1

(1.4)

be the certain non-linear first order PDE introducing from the analogy with the Fermat-type equation
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(f_{i}\right)^{m}=1
, where
u:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
,
z_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
,
f_{i}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, and
m,n\geq 2
.
In 1999, Saleeby
[
34
]
first started to study about the solutions of the Fermat-type PDEs and obtained the results for entire solutions of (
1.4
) on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Afterwards, in 2004, Li
[
22
]
extended these results to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

In 2008, Li
[
23
]
considered the equation (
1.3
) with
n=2
and showed that meromorphic solutions
f
and
g
of that equation on

\mathbb{C}^{2}
must be constant if and only if
\partial f/\partial z_{2}
and
\partial g/\partial z_{1}
have the same zeros (counting multiplicities). If
f=\partial u/\partial z_{1}
and
g=\partial u/\partial z_{2}
, then any entire solutions of the partial differential equations
\left(\partial u/\partial z_{1}\right)^{2}+\left(\partial u/\partial z_{2}\right)^{2}=1
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
are necessarily linear
[
18
]
.

In 2018, Xu and Cao
[
39
,
40
]
was the first who considered both difference operators and differential operators in Fermat-type equations of two complex variables and obtained the following results.

Theorem A
.

[

,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{n}+f^{m}(z+c)=1,\;\text{where}\;c=(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2},

doesn’t have any finite order transcendental entire solution of two complex variables
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
, where

are distinct.

Theorem B
.

[

,
40
]
Let

. Then any transcendental entire solution with finite order of (

, where

satisfying

, and

is a polynomial in one variable

such that

2.
The main results

Let
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}_{+}
be a multi-index with length
\|I\|=\sum_{j=1}^{k}i_{j}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\partial^{I}f=\frac{\partial^{\|I\|}f}{\partial z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial z_{k}^{i_{k}}}
.
Now, any polynomial
\mathcal{Q}(z)
of several complex variables of degree
d
can
be expressed as
\mathcal{Q}(z)=\sum_{\|I\|=0}^{d}a_{I}z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{i_{n}}
, where
a_{I}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
a_{I}
are not all zero at a time for
\|I\|=d
.
Let
G(z)
denotes the partial differential function of finite order transcendental meromorphic
f
function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
N(r,f)=S(r,f)
involving
n
(
\in\mathbb{N}
) different homogeneous terms on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle G(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{\|I\|=m}a_{I}(z)\partial^{I}f(z)

(2.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(a_{(n,0\ldots,0)}(z)\frac{\partial^{n}f(z)}{\partial z_{1}^{n}}+\cdots+a_{(1,1,1,\ldots,1)}(z)\frac{\partial^{n}f(z)}{\partial z_{1}\partial z_{2}\cdots\partial z_{n}}+\cdots+a_{(0,\ldots,0,n)}(z)\frac{\partial^{n}f(z)}{\partial z_{n}^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(a_{(n-1,0,\ldots,0)}(z)\frac{\partial^{n-1}f(z)}{\partial z_{1}^{n-1}}+\cdots+a_{(0,\ldots,0,n-1)}(z)\frac{\partial^{n-1}f(z)}{\partial z_{n}^{n-1}}\right)

\displaystyle+\cdots+\left(a_{(1,0,\ldots,0)}(z)\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial z_{1}}+a_{(0,1,\ldots,0)}(z)\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial z_{2}}+\cdots+a_{(0,\ldots,0,1)}(z)\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial z_{n}}\right),

where
z=\left(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}\right)
and
a_{I}(z)
are small functions of
f(z)
of several complex variables such that
a_{I}(z)
are not all identically zero at a time.

\mathbb{C}^{n}

\displaystyle G^{m_{1}}(z)+\alpha(z)(\Delta(f))^{m_{2}}=\beta(z),

(2.2)

Let

Theorem 2.2
.

\displaystyle f(z)=(z_{1}-c_{1})\left(g_{2}(z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n})+\frac{c_{1}}{2\tau}\omega\right)-\left(g_{2}(z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n})+\frac{c_{1}}{2\tau}(\omega-\tau)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(c_{1}^{2}-z_{1}^{2}\right)+\varphi(z_{2}-c_{2},z_{3}-c_{3},\ldots,z_{n}-c_{n}),

where

\ldots,z_{n}

(c_{2},c_{3},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}

If

3.
Some lemmas

The following are relevant lemmas of this paper and are used in the sequel.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
6
,
19
]
Let
f
be a non-constant meromorphic function with finite order on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
f(0)\not=0,\infty
. Then for
c\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f(z)}{f(z+c)}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{f(z+c)}{f(z)}\right)=S(r,f)

holds for all
r>0
outside of a possible exceptional set
E\subset[1,\infty)
of finite logarithmic measure
\int_{E}dt/t<+\infty
.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
2
,
45
]
Let
f
be a non-constant meromorphic function with finite order on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{n})
be a multi-index with length
\|I\|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{j}
. Assume
that
T(r_{0},f)\geq e
for some
r_{0}
. Then
m\left(r,\frac{\partial^{I}f}{f}\right)=S(r,f)
holds for all
r\geq r_{0}
outside a set
E\subset(0,\infty)
of finite logarithmic measure
\int_{E}dt/t<+\infty
,
where
\partial^{I}f=\frac{\partial^{\|I\|}f}{\partial z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial z_{n}^{i_{n}}}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
16
, Lemma 5.34]
Let
f(z)
be a
\nu
-valued algebroid solution of the following partial differential equation

\displaystyle\Omega\left(z,f,\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f,\ldots,\partial^{\alpha_{n}}f\right)=\frac{A(z,f)}{B(z,f)},

where
\Omega(z,f,\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f,\ldots,\partial^{\alpha_{n}}f)=\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}(z)f^{i_{0}}\left(\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\right)^{i_{1}}\cdots\left(\partial^{\alpha_{n}}f\right)^{i_{n}}
with
I=\{i=\\
(i_{0},i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\}
is a
finite set of distinct elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n+1}
and
c_{i}\in\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, and
A(z,f)
and
B(z,f)
are co prime polynomials for
f
given by
A(z,f)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}a_{j}(z)f^{j}
,

, where

such that

\displaystyle m(r,\Omega)=O\left\{\sum_{i\in I}m\left(r,c_{i}\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{p}m\left(r,a_{j}\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{q}m\left(r,b_{k}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{1}{b_{q}}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}m\left(r,\frac{\partial^{\alpha_{k}}f}{f}\right)\right\}.

.

[

Let

Proofs of the main results

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
f
be a finite order transcendental meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
N(r,f)=S(r,f)
satisfies (
2.2
) and
G(z)
be defined in (
2.1
).
Then
G(z),f(z+c)
are finite order transcendental meromorphic functions with
N(r,G(z))=S(r,f)=N(r,f(z+c))
.
In view of Lemma
3.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}T(r,f)=m(r,f)+S(r,f)\leq m\left(r,\frac{f(z)}{f(z+c)}\right)+m(r,f(z+c))+S(r,f)\leq T(r,f(z+c))+S(r,f),\\
T(r,f(z+c))=m(r,f(z+c))+S(r,f)\leq m\left(r,\frac{f(z+c)}{f(z)}\right)+m(r,f)+S(r,f)\leq T(r,f)+S(r,f).\end{array}

Thus,
T(r,f(z+c))=T(r,f)+S(r,f)
.
Note that,
m\left(r,\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{\|I\|=m}a_{I}(z)\frac{\partial^{I}f(z)}{f(z)}\right)=S(r,f)
, since
f
is a finite order transcendental meromorphic function and
a_{I}(z)
are small functions of
f
, where
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
\|I\|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{j}
. Then by Lemma
3.1
and
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle T(r,G(z))=m(r,G(z))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\left(r,f(z)\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{|\|I\|=m}a_{I}(z)\frac{\partial^{I}f(z)}{f(z)}\right)+S(r,f)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T(r,f(z))+S(r,f).

Now we discuss the following two cases.

Case 1.
When
m_{2}>m_{1}
.
In view of Valiron-Mokhon’ko lemma
[
16
, p. 29]
and Lemma
3.2
and (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle m_{2}T(r,f(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{2}T(r,f(z+c))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(r,f^{m_{2}}(z+c))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T\left(r,\alpha(z)f^{m_{2}}(z+c)\right)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\left(r,G^{m_{1}}(z)-\beta(z)\right)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m_{1}T\left(r,G)\right)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m_{1}T(r,f(z))+S(r,f),

\displaystyle\text{\it i.e.,}\quad(m_{2}-m_{1})T(r,f(z))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(r,f),

which arise a contradiction, since
f
is a finite order transcendental meromorphic function and
m_{2}>m_{1}
.

Case 2.
When
m_{1}>m_{2}\geq 2

i.e.,

\frac{1}{m_{1}}+\frac{1}{m_{2}}<1
, which implies
m_{2}>\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}
.
By Nevanlinna second fundamental theorem for small functions
[
16
, p. 50]
, Lemma
3.1
and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle m_{1}T(r,G(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\left(r,G^{m_{1}}(z)\right)+S(r,G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}\left(r,G^{m_{1}}(z)\right)+\overline{N}\left(r,0;G^{m_{1}}(z)\right)

\displaystyle+\overline{N}\left(r,0;G^{m_{1}}(z)-\beta(z)\right)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{N}\left(r,0;G(z)\right)+\overline{N}\left(r,0;\alpha(z)f^{m_{2}}(z+c)\right)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T(r,G(z))+\overline{N}\left(r,0;f(z+c)\right)+S(r,f),

\displaystyle\text{\it i.e.,}\;\;(m_{1}-1)T(r,G(z))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T(r,f(z+c))+S(r,f).

(4.3)

Again, in view of Valiron-Mokhon’ko lemma
[
16
, p. 29]
, Lemma
3.1
, (
2.2
) and (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle m_{2}T(r,f(z+c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(r,\alpha(z)f^{m_{2}}(z+c))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(r,G^{m_{1}}(z)-\beta(z))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m_{1}T(r,G(z))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}T(r,f(z+c))+S(r,f),

\displaystyle\text{\it i.e.,}\quad\left(m_{2}-\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}\right)T(r,f(z+c))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(r,f),

which arise a contradiction, since
f
is a finite order transcendental meromorphic function and
m_{2}>\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
f
be a finite order transcendental entire function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfies (
2.3
).
Differentiating partially with respect to
z_{1}
on both sides of (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle m_{1}\left(\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{m_{1}-1}\frac{\partial^{2}f(z)}{\partial z_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\partial f(z+c)}{\partial z_{1}}-\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial z_{1}}=0.

(4.4)

Let
F(z)=\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial z_{1}}
. Then (
4.4
) reduces to

\displaystyle m_{1}F^{m_{1}-1}(z)\frac{\partial F(z)}{\partial z_{1}}=-F(z+c)+F(z),

\displaystyle\text{\it{i.e.,}}\quad F^{m_{1}-2}(z)\frac{\partial F(z)}{\partial z_{1}}=-\frac{1}{m_{1}}\frac{F(z+c)-F(z)}{F(z)}.

(4.5)

Clearly by Lemmas
3.1
and
3.4
, we get
m\left(r,-\frac{1}{m_{1}}\frac{F(z+c)-F(z)}{F(z)}\right)=S(r,F)
, which implies

m\left(r,F^{m_{1}-2}(z)\frac{\partial F(z)}{\partial z_{1}}\right)=S(r,F)=S(r,f)
.
Since
f
is a finite order transcendental entire function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we see that
N\left(r,F^{m_{1}-2}(z)\frac{\partial F(z)}{\partial z_{1}}\right)=S(r,f)
. Consequently, we get
T\left(r,F^{m_{1}-2}(z)\frac{\partial F(z)}{\partial z_{1}}\right)=S(r,f)
.
Since
f
is a finite order transcendental entire function and from (
4.5
), we may assume that

\displaystyle F^{m_{1}-2}(z)\frac{\partial F(z)}{\partial z_{1}}\equiv P(z),

(4.6)

where
P(z)
is a non-zero polynomial on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Then the following two cases arise.

Case 1.
When
m_{1}=2
. Solving (
4.6
) by using the Lagrange method
[
36
, Chapter 2]
, we get
F(z)=Q(z)+g_{1}(z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n})
,
where
g_{1}(z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n})
is a finite order entire function in
z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n}
and
Q(z)=\int P(z)dz_{1}
, where
z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n}
are constants. Note that
\deg(Q(z))\geq 1
.
Now the following two cases arise.

Sub-case 1.1.
Let
g_{1}(z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n})
be a finite order transcendental entire function in
z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n}
. From (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{F(z+c)}{F(z)}\equiv 1-2P(z),\;\;\text{\it i.e.,}\;\;\frac{Q(z+c)+g_{1}(z_{2}+c_{2},\ldots,z_{n}+c_{n})}{Q(z)+g_{1}(z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n})}\equiv 1-2P(z)

i.e.,

\displaystyle Q(z+c)+g_{1}(z_{2}+c_{2},\ldots,z_{n}+c_{n})\equiv(1-2P(z))Q(z)

(4.7)

\displaystyle+(1-2P(z))g_{1}(z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n}).

Comparing the polynomials on the both sides, we get
P(z)\equiv-1/2
and
Q(z)=-z_{1}/2
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle F(z)=-\frac{1}{2}z_{1}+g_{1}(z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n})

(4.8)

and

\displaystyle g_{1}(z_{2}+c_{2},z_{3}+c_{3},\ldots,z_{n}+c_{n})\equiv g_{1}(z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n})+\frac{c_{1}}{2}.

(4.9)

From (

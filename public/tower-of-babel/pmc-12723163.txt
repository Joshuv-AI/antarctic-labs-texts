Dynamic properties of loess improved by nano-MgO and hydrated lime under humidification conditions
Authors: Jin Chen, Chengyue Wu, Zhigang Zhang, Shiyu Gong, Bo Fu, Huaiping Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryThis study investigates how nano-MgO enhances the dynamic properties of lime-stabilized loess, a soil prone to weakening when moistened. Under simulated humidification, the addition of nano-MgO significantly improved the soil’s performance: cumulative strain decreased by up to 30.7%, dynamic elastic modulus increased by up to 32.9%, and damping ratio was reduced by up to 19.4%. The high surface activity of nano-MgO accelerates lime-soil reactions, forming a more robust matrix that binds soil particles and fills pores, thereby improving moisture resistance. These findings demonstrate a filler-based strategy for ground improvement, offering a promising approach to mitigate rainfall-induced subsidence and enhance infrastructure stability in loess regions.Subject areas: Nanotechnology, Materials science, Materials property, Materials application

Introduction

Loess is a widespread surface deposit in many parts of the world, and loess occupies about 6% of the total land area.1 It is widely distributed in northern China, with a total area of approximately 630,000 square kilometers, accounting for approximately 6.6% of China’s total area.2 In the loess areas of China, 60% of the are covered by collapsible loess. With the rapid development of infrastructure transportation construction in various countries, loess should inevitably be used as the subgrade filler. Due to the special structure of loess, it is very sensitive to humidification and vibration,3,4 and the sharp attenuation of the strength after the water immersion is prone to cause the humidification deformation,5 jeopardizing the safety of the train operation. It is found that China’s Loess Plateau has become one of the most eroded areas in the world because of rainfall and other factors.6 Due to its poor engineering properties, loess is generally difficult to meet the requirements of engineering construction if it is used directly. The mechanical properties of unsaturated loess are sensitive to changes in soil suction and water content, and the use of unsaturated soil to build geotechnical infrastructure requires a comprehensive understanding of the mechanical behavior of unsaturated soil.7 Under the dynamic humidification action of the coupling of flooding and circulating load, the soil is prone to mud, mud, and other disasters, which endangers the safety of railway operation.8,9,10 In order to make the poor loess utilization, it needs to be upgraded by using physical or chemical methods. Jia et al.11 and Peng et al.12 reported that guar gum and microorganisms can improve the strength of the loess. There are also scholars who use traditional materials such as fly ash, hydrated lime, cement, and gypsum to improve the soil,13,14,15 among which the hydrated lime improvement involves mixing the hydrated lime into loess with a certain ratio. The cementitious material generated by the hydration reaction from water and lime can solidify loess. In addition, studies have shown that hydrated lime improves loess can resist humidification to a certain extent.16 However, the hydrated lime improvement often requires a large proportion, the curing time required to reach a certain strength is lengthy, and the production and usage of the hydrated lime will increase carbon emissions and induce environmental pollution. Therefore, some scholars adopted environmentally friendly materials such as nanomaterials to improve the loess. Nanomaterials, which are defined as particles with at least one dimension in the three-dimensional space between 1 and 100 nm,17 have become a research focus due to their large specific surface area, small particle size, and environmental friendliness. With the development of nanotechnology, the cost of nanomaterials is decreasing, and nanomaterials have been applied to many fields. Yue et al.18 and He et al.19 added nanomaterials to concrete aggregates to improve the strength of concrete. Bahrami et al.20 used a proportional mixture of two nano-chemical stabilizers—Terrasil and Zycobond—to solidify gypsum-containing clay samples. The study found that the mixed stabilizer significantly reduced physical and mechanical properties, such as sample settlement, compression ratio, and water conductivity through the samples. Microscopic analysis revealed that the solidified soil structure became denser and its composition improved, leading to enhanced shear strength. Consequently, the engineering properties of the soil samples were improved. Zhan et al.21 comprehensively reviewed the effects of nano-SiO2 and graphene oxide on the mechanical properties of recycled concrete, and pointed out that the incorporation of nano-materials promoted the hydration process of cement and improved the mechanical properties and durability of recycled aggregates. Commonly used nanomaterials in the field of civil engineering include nano-SiO2, nano-Al2O3, nano-clay, nano-CaCO3, nano-MgO, nano-CuO, and and so forth. Among them, the nano-MgO is an emerging low-carbon high-performance stabilizer and has outstanding advantages and superior performance in strength, hardness, toughness, and plasticity. The nano-MgO has gradually been introduced into the field of geotechnical engineering due to its low environmental pollution and the significant improvement of soil performance, even with a very small amount. Adding nano-MgO to the hydrated lime improved loess can promote the hydration and pozzolanic reactions generated by the lime due to the high surface activity of the nano-MgO. As a result, more cementitious materials can be efficiently generated, and then the effect of cementing soil particles and improving soil performance can be achieved.

Many scholars are studying the properties of the nano-material improved soil. For instance, some scholars use nano-SiO2 to improve soil. The nano-SiO2 was added to clay or loess,22,23,24,25,26,27,28,29 and it was found that the mechanical properties, including the strength of the soil, are significantly enhanced. Malik et al.30 studied the influence of the nano-SiO2 on the strength characteristics of clay by adding different contents of nano-SiO2 into soil, and carried out the compaction test and unconfined compressive strength test. The experimental results showed that with the increase of the nano-SiO2 content, the optimum moisture content increases. The unconfined compressive strength of the improved soil is higher than that of the plain soil. To sum up, the nano-SiO2 has the potential to improve the mechanical properties of clay. Liu et al.31 studied the strength behavior of cemented soil with OPC and nano SiO2 additive through a series of unconfined compressive tests. The results indicated that the incorporation of nano SiO2 can effectively improve the strength of cemented soil. Sayed et al.32 studied the effect of nano-SiO2 on cement soil through compressive strength tests and microscopic tests. The results showed that the compressive strength of nano-SiO2 on cement-treated soil was increased by 80%, and nano-SiO2 produces calcium silicate hydrate gum by facilitating the reaction of volcanic ash. Barbhuiya et al.33 summarized the influence of the nano-SiO2 on soil properties and pointed out that nano-SiO2 has a positive effect on the unconfined compressive strength and shear strength of soil. They also mentioned that the addition of the nano-SiO2 will increase the CBR and elastic modulus of the soil.

Some scholars adopt nano-Al2O3 and nano-clay to enhance soil. Mir and Reddy34 added nano-alumina to soft soils and carried out compaction tests, unconfined compressive strength tests, and microscopic tests. The results show that the nano-Al2O3 stabilized soil has higher bulk density, lower water content, and higher compressive strength. The soil samples treated with nano-Al2O3 showed a fine structure of tight packing and agglomeration, and the engineering properties of the soft soil are significantly improved with the addition of Al2O3. Shang and Fu35 added nano-Al2O3 and nano-SiO2 to expansive soil and carried out a series of experiments. The test results showed that adding 1.2% nano-Al2O3 and about 2% nano-SiO2 to expansive soil provides the optimal compression index. Parsaei et al.36 carried out the direct shear test, unconfined compressive strength test, and microscopic test by adding different contents of nano-Al2O3 into the kaolinitic clay, and concluded that the shear strength of soil was increased by 50% and the unconfined compressive strength was increased by 15% after adding a certain content of nano-Al2O3. The microscopic test indicated that the nano-Al2O3 can improve soil properties by filling soil pores. Arabani et al.37 added the nano-clay into soil, and conducted the unconfined compressive strength test, CBR test, and microscopic test. The results showed that adding nano-clay improves the properties of soil, and it can be used as a low-cost and sustainable choice for soil reinforcement. Kholghifard et al.38 mixed the nano-clay and recycled polyester fiber and added the mixture into the soil. They found that the shear strength and cohesion of soil were improved. Tabarsa et al.39 added the nano-clay into loess, conducted the unconfined compressive strength test and triaxial compression test, and found that the plasticity, strength, and stiffness characteristics of the samples were changed after adding the nano-clay. There are also some researchers who employ the nano-CaCO3 to improve soil. Chen et al.40 added nano-CaCO3 to cement soil and carried out dynamic triaxial tests, nuclear magnetic resonance tests, and scanning electron microscope tests. The results showed that nano-CaCO3 can effectively improve the dynamic characteristics of cement soil. Mohammadi et al.41 added the nano-CaCO3 into clay or cement soil, and found that adding nano-CaCO3 can effectively reduce the pores and cracks in soil, and promote the hydration reaction in soil to generate more gelatinous substances, thus improving the strength and other mechanical properties of soil.

There are also some studies using nano-MgO for soil enhancement.42,43,44,45 Some scholars use it to improve the soft soil, expansive soil, and clay. Yao et al.46 analyzed the influence of the nano-MgO on the strength and microstructure characteristics of the cement stabilized soft soil. By conducting the unconfined compressive strength test and scanning electron microscope (SEM) test, it was found that adding the nano-MgO not only improves the unconfined compressive strength of the cement soil, but also increases its malleability. Wang et al.47 added the Nano-MgO into the cement expansive soil and conducted a series of unconfined compressive strength tests and microscopic tests with different nano-MgO content. It was concluded that the compressive strength, stiffness, and toughness of the cement expansive soil are improved by adding the nano-MgO, which promotes the hydration reaction of cement, fills the pores of soil samples, compacts the internal structure of cement soil, and improves the performance of cement expansive soil. To study the relationship between the nano-MgO and soil shear characteristics, Gao et al.48 added the nano-MgO to soil and carried out the triaxial shear test and microscopic test. It was found that nano-MgO can effectively improve the failure strength and cohesion of soil, and the addition of nano-MgO can reduce the porosity of soil and enhance the cementation between soil particles, thus changing the shear characteristics of soil. Ren et al.49 added the nano-MgO into clay, conducted direct shear test and SEM test, and observed that the cohesion of soil increases with the increase of the nano-MgO. The SEM test results showed that the addition of the nano-MgO can reduce the porosity ratio of soil and enhance the cementation between particles, resulting in the variation of the shear strength of clay. There are some studies on adopting the nano-MgO to enhance loess. Li et al.50 conducted experiments to compare the effect of nano-silica and nano-MgO on the unconfined compressive strength (UCS) of silt, and recommended the use of nano-MgO to improve the early strength of stabilized silt, and the use of nano-silica to improve the long-term strength. Wang et al.51 compared the characteristics of soft soil, cement-reinforced soft soil, and nano-MgO-reinforced cement soft soil by carrying out direct shear tests. The results showed that the addition of nano-MgO can significantly improve the shear strength of soft soil and thus improve the soil. Chen et al.52 investigated the influence of the nano-MgO on the loess behavior under the freeze-thaw environment. By conducting the dynamic triaxial test, it was found that the maximum shear modulus of soil samples can be improved to varying degrees after adding the nano-MgO. The water absorption of the nano-MgO can transform the free water into the bound water, thus significantly improving the dynamic performance and freeze-thaw resistance of loess. Chen et al.53 studied the nano-MgO improved loess by conducting the standard compaction test and unconfined compressive strength test. It was reported that the addition of the nano-MgO increases the liquid limit, plastic limit, and optimum water content of loess. With the increase of the nano-MgO content, the unconfined compressive strength increases first and decreases afterward. Li et al.54 added the nano-MgO and lime to loess with a certain proportion, and conducted the unconfined compressive strength test, resilience modulus test, bearing ratio test, and scanning electron microscope test. The experimental results demonstrated that compared with the lime improved loess, the compressive strength and deformation resistance of the nano-MgO and lime improved loess were superior. The microscopic test indicated that the incorporation of the nano-MgO reduces the internal pores of loess and further enhances the skeleton structure of soil. Similarly, Feng et al.55 adopted the nano-MgO and lime to improve loess, and studied the unconfined compressive strength, resilience modulus, and bearing ratio of soil before and after improvement with different proportions. It was found that the mechanical properties of soil are improved significantly after adding a small amount of nano-MgO, and the improved soil can reach the strength level of soil without adding nano-MgO within 180 days of 60 days.

Nanomaterials provide a new idea for soil reinforcement. However, the research on the application of nanomaterials in geotechnical engineering is still at the exploratory stage. Most studies conduct indoor tests by adding nano-materials and other materials into different soils according to a certain proportion, analyze, and compare the improvement effects of nano-materials regarding the compressive strength and shear strength bearing ratio of soil before and after improvement. However, there are quite limited studies on the soil improvement by nano-materials in a special environment. For example, there is strong precipitation in some loess areas, and the soil is easily deformed due to humidification, which may lead to severe engineering safety problems. To fully use loess as the subgrade filler and solve the water-sensitive issue of loess, our study aims to enhance loess by adding nano-MgO into the hydrated lime improved soil.

The superior performance of the nano-MgO is fully employed. The nano-MgO and hydrated lime are added to the loess according to a certain proportion. The cementitious materials generated by the hydration of the hydrated lime will bind soil particles. In addition, the high surface activity of the nano-MgO will promote the generation of cementitious material products, and fill the soil pores with an extremely small size. Therefore, the target of curing loess and improving the anti-humidification ability of loess can be achieved. In this article, the dynamic cumulative deformation tests of the hydrated lime improved loess with and without the nano-MgO under different humidification degrees are carried out. The anti-deformation ability and anti-humidification ability of the two types of improved loess is compared and analyzed, which provides innovative solutions for the improvement field in loess areas.

The nano-MgO used in the experiments was obtained from a metal smelting company in Guangzhou (Figure 1C), and the hydrated lime was from a chemical plant in Jiangxi (Figure 1B). Sample details

To truly simulate the soil humidification caused by rainfall, the humidification method mentioned in59,60 was adopted. First, filter paper pieces were placed above and below the prepared cylinder sample, and filter paper strips were wrapped around the cylinder from the top to the bottom. Then, the cylinder sample was placed on a permeable stone located in a container. Water was added to the container to submerge the permeable stone. The mass of the sample was weighed at intervals until it met the requirements of the humidification degree. Two humidification degrees of Wopt+2% and Wopt+3% were adopted.

The experiment primarily investigates the humidity resistance of the hydrated lime improved loess with the addition of the nano-MgO, aiming to provide new ideas for the improvement of loess and focusing on comparing the effects of two loess improvement schemes under humidification conditions. Therefore, the test set the confining pressure, deviatoric stress, loading frequency, and dynamic stress amplitude as constants instead of variables. The test was conducted via the GDS dynamic triaxial testing system, as shown in Figure 1F. To simulate the real stress state of soil in the roadbed, the confining pressure σ3 was set to 30 kPa, the deviatoric stress σ1 was 30 kPa, and the dynamic stress amplitude σd was 30 kPa by considering the buried depth of soil in the roadbed and the mass of rails laid on the upper part of the roadbed. During the formal test, the specimen was firstly loaded under the confining pressure of 30 kPa and deviatoric stress of 30 kPa to σa = σ1+σ3. Then, the specimen was consolidated for 2 h. After the consolidation stage, the dynamic load was applied 10000 times, as shown in Figure 1H.

Micro-mechanism analysis

Soil structure analysis

The ameliorative effect of nano-MgO and lime on loess lies in alterations to its microstructure and the influence of physicochemical reactions. By scanning the soil structure with an electron microscope and analyzing the results, we aim to elucidate the mechanism by which nano-MgO enhances soil resistance to wetting.

Microscopic structural images (Figure 2) of soils modified with varying proportions of nano-MgO and lime reveal that increasing nano-MgO content results in denser soil structures, reduced pore area, and more uniform pore diameter distribution. Compared to lime-modified soils, irregular clumping diminishes, flocculent cementation appears within pores, newly formed aggregate surfaces become smoother, and soil particles exhibit greater compaction. These phenomena indicate that nano-MgO partially fills soil pores. Simultaneously, the interaction between nano-MgO and lime generates cementing substances that induce cohesive effects on soil particles, resulting in enhanced stability and compaction of the modified soil. Consequently, it is predicted that nano-MgO addition enhances the stability of lime-amended loess under humidification conditions.

Scanning electron microscope image

(A and B) Lime-improved loess and (B) nano-MgO and lime improved loess.

Analysis using image software (Image Pro Plus) revealed that the average pore diameter of lime-amended loess was 1.0491 μm, while that of nano-MgO-amended loess was 0.5327 μm. This indicates a more uniform pore size distribution after nano-MgO addition. The surface porosity of lime-amended loess was 18.65%, while that of nano-MgO-lime-amended loess was 11.85%. This indicates enhanced cohesion among soil particles following nano-MgO addition. These results stem from nano-MgO’s ability to fill pores, reducing pore size and promoting more uniform distribution. Simultaneously, its high surface activity accelerates both the hydration reaction and the pozzolanic reaction of lime. These processes generate CaCO3 and MgCO3 within the soil matrix. The synergistic action of these compounds binds soil particles, enhancing cohesion.

Soil composition analysis

X-ray diffraction analysis of the soil samples revealed compositional changes before and after the addition of nano-MgO. According to Figure 3 and Table 4, following the addition, CaMg(CO3)2 appeared in the soil. This substance constitutes the primary chemical component of dolomite, a double salt of calcium and magnesium carbonates (CaCO3, MgCO3). Theoretically, it can be regarded as a 1:1 composite of CaCO3 and MgCO3. This occurs as MgO (MgO) reacts with water in the soil to form magnesium hydroxide [Mg(OH)2], which then reacts with carbonate ions to produce MgCO3. Simultaneously, calcium oxide (CaO) reacts with water and carbonate ions to form CaCO3. These two compounds crystallize together to form the dolomite mineral.

Cumulative strain

Influence of the number of vibration cycles

Figure 4A shows the relationship curve of the cumulative strain with respect to the number of vibration cycles. The loading process can be divided into three stages: stage Ⅰ (0–500 cycles), stage Ⅱ (500–2000 cycles), and stage Ⅲ (2000–10000 cycles). It can be seen from Figure 4A that the loading period has a significant influence on the cumulative deformation. The cumulative strains of the two types of improved loess have the same development trend, which is characterized by a sharp increase at the stage Ⅰ, a slow increase at stage Ⅱ, and a stable trend at stage Ⅲ. At stage Ⅰ, the loading cycles only account for 5% of the total loading cycles, but the cumulative strain accounts for more than 80% of the total cumulative strain. At the stage Ⅱ, the cumulative strain of the specimen increases slowly with the loading times, and the growth rate decreases gradually. At the stage Ⅲ, the cumulative strain of the sample shows no obvious increasing tendency, and gradually tends to be stable. Finally, the cumulative strain reaches the maximum.

Pattern of cumulative strain change

(A–C) Relationship between cumulative strain and number of vibration cycles, (B) cumulative strain generated in each loading stage accounts for the percentage of the whole loading process (sample B-1), and (C) comparative diagram of cumulative strain of two improved loess under different humidification degrees.

Taking the sample B-1 as an example, Figure 4B depicts the percentage of the cumulative strain generated in each loading stage in the whole loading process. The cumulative strain is 0.0527% when the loading cycle number is 500, and the cumulative strain accounts for 81.83% of the total loading process. At the initial stage of loading, the gaps inside the soil are still large, so the sliding distance between soil particles is large under the early dynamic load, which leads to the rapid increase of the cumulative strain. When the number of vibration cycles reaches 2000, the cumulative strain is 0.0618% with a growth rate of 17%. At the moment, the cumulative strain accounts for 95.96% of the total loading process. With the increase of vibration times, these gaps are gradually squeezed, and the interior structure of soil becomes denser. Under the same dynamic load stress level, the sliding distance of soil particles becomes smaller, and the cumulative strain increases slowly. When the loading cycle number reaches 10000, the cumulative strain is 0.0644%, with an increase of 4.2%. When the number of vibration cycles increases to a certain extent, the interior structure of the soil basically becomes stable. Under the dynamic load, the soil particles essentially do not slip, and the accumulated strain barely increases and tends to be stable.

Influence of humidification degree

The cumulative strain varies significantly with the humidification degree. The cumulative strain variation trends of the hydrated lime improved loess with and without the nano-MgO at different humidification levels are compared. Figure 4C shows the bar chart of the ultimate cumulative deformation of the two types of improved loess at different humidification levels.61 It can be seen from Figure 4C that for the hydrated lime improved loess without the nano-MgO, the ultimate cumulative strains are 0.0729%, 0.1248%, and 0.15%, under the unhumidified (Wopt), 2% humidified (Wopt+2%), and 3% humidified (Wopt+3%) conditions, respectively. The increasing rates between the three humidification conditions are 71.2% and 20.2%. For the hydrated lime improved loess with the nano-MgO, the ultimate cumulative strains are 0.0644%, 0.0933%, and 0.1039%, under the three conditions, respectively. The increasing rates are 44.9% and 11.4%.

The cumulative strain of both specimens increased with wetting degree. This occurred because: On one hand, the water film between soil particles thickened with increasing wetting, reducing surface tension and matrix cohesion. Consequently, soil particles were more prone to relative slip under equivalent stress levels, leading to increased cumulative strain. On the other hand, increased wetting also raises the amount of free water within the soil. This free water may dissolve some hydration products and minerals, weakening their binding effect on soil particles. Consequently, the soil’s bearing strength and deformation resistance decrease, leading to increased cumulative strain.

Influence of nano-MgO modification

The humidification degree significantly affects the ultimate cumulative strain of both types of improved loess, but the extent is different. As the humidification degree increases, the difference in the cumulative strain between those with and without the nano-MgO increases from 0.0065% to 0.0315% then to 0.0461%. The ultimate cumulative strains of the samples without the nano-MgO are 1.13, 1.34, and 1.44 times, respectively, those of the samples with the nano-MgO under the three humidification conditions. After the addition of the nano-MgO, the ultimate cumulative strains decrease by 11.7%, 25.2%, and 30.7%, respectively (Figure 4A). As wetting increases, the addition of nano-MgO demonstrates superior resistance to wetting. This occurs because: firstly, the high surface activity of the nano-material binds with free water within the soil, encapsulating some soil particles into agglomerates. This reduces the tendency for relative slip between particles, tightening their connections and acting as a cementing agent. On the other hand, the high surface activity of nano-MgO also promotes hydration reactions and pozzolanic reactions within the soil. This generates additional hydration products that bind soil particles and fill pores, enhancing soil strength and deformation resistance. Consequently, under identical dynamic loading stress levels, the cumulative strain of lime-stabilized loess with nano-MgO addition is lower than that of standard lime-stabilized loess.

Figure 5B shows the relationship curve between the dynamic elastic modulus and the number of vibration cycles. With the increase in vibration times, the dynamic elastic modulus first increases slightly and then decreases, and the overall trend is decreasing. Taking the unhumidified loess with 0.8% nano-MgO (specimen B-1) as an example, the analysis starts from the second loading because of the instability of the first loading. The dynamic elastic modulus is 181 MPa when the cycle number is 2, and it increases to 190 MPa when the cycle number is 10. This is because at the initial loading stage, there are large gaps in the loose soil. Under the high-frequency vibration load, the soil pores will be reduced, which has a compact effect on loess. Therefore, it improves the deformation resistance and bearing capacity of loess. When the loading is carried out for 100, 2000, and 10000 times, the dynamic elastic modulus drops to 178, 161, and 159 MPa. With the continuous vibration, the interior structure of loess is gradually destroyed, and structural defects occur. There will be continuous slippage between soil particles, which will reduce the stiffness, deformation resistance, bearing capacity, and dynamic elastic modulus of loess.

Influence of humidification degree

To compare the difference in the dynamic elastic modulus between the two types of improved loess, the ultimate dynamic elastic modulus at the end of loading is selected as the comparison object. Figure 5C shows the histogram of the ultimate dynamic elastic modulus of two types of improved loess under different humidification degrees. It can be seen from Figure 5C that with the increase of the humidification degree, the dynamic elastic modulus of the improved loess with the nano-MgO decreases from 159 MPa to 113 MPa, then to 93 MPa. The reduction rates of the dynamic elastic modulus between the three humidification degrees are 28.93% and 17.70%. The dynamic elastic modulus of the improved loess without the nano-MgO decreases from 139 MPa, 104 MPa, and then to 70 MPa. The reduction rates of the dynamic elastic modulus between the three humidification degrees are 25.18% and 32.69%. With the increase in the humidification degree, the dynamic elastic modulus decreases. On one hand, the soil strength mainly depends on the friction and cohesive force between soil particles, and with the increase of the humidification degree, the friction and cohesive force between soil particles decrease as the water film is more prone to slip. On the other hand, with the increase of the humidification degree, the free water content in the soil increases, and the cemented material gradually softens under the action of the free water, and the cementing ability becomes weaker. Therefore, with the increase of the humidification degree, the strength of the soil decreases, and the dynamic elastic modulus decreases.

Influence of nano-MgO modification

Compared with the improved loess without the nano-MgO, the dynamic elastic modulus of the improved loess with the nano-MgO increases from 139 MPa to 159 MPa, from 104 MPa to 113 MPa, and from 70 MPa to 93 MPa (Figure 5C), under the three humidification degrees. Correspondingly, the dynamic elastic modulus is increased by 14.39%, 8.65%, and 32.86% under three humidification degrees. It indicates that the deformation resistance of the hydrated lime improved loess with the nano-MgO is enhanced under the humidification conditions. On one hand, due to the high surface activity effect of the nano-MgO and the combination of the free water in soil, some soil particles are wrapped in an agglomerate state, which reduces the relative sliding trend between soil particles. It makes the soil particles more closely connected. On the other hand, the high surface activity of the nano-MgO can also promote the hydration reaction and pozzolanic reaction in soil, generate more hydration products for bonding soil particles and filling pores. As a result, the strength and deformation resistance of loess can be improved.

Under the action of the external load, the relative slip is relatively large, which consumes more energy to overcome the external load. When the loading cycle number increases from 500 to 2000, the damping ratio increases from 0.0294 to 0.0338, with a growth rate of 14.97%. With the continuous vibration, the interior structure of the soil is readjusted, and the gaps between loess particles become smaller and smaller after the previous vibration compaction. It results in a slow increase in the strain of loess under the same dynamic stress level, which makes the energy consumed by loess due to friction between soil particles vary slightly. The damping ratio also increases slowly. At the later loading stage, when the loading cycle number increases from 2000 to 10000, the damping ratio increases from 0.0338 to 0.03487, with an increase rate of 3.17%. When the number of vibrations reaches a certain level, the interior structure of loess tends to be stable, and the strain generated by the external load basically remains constant, which makes the work of overcoming the dynamic load basically unchanged, and the damping ratio tends to be stable.

Influence of humidification degree

The humidification degree has an important influence on the damping ratio of loess. Figure 6B compares the variations of the ultimate damping ratios of the two improved loess under different humidification degrees. It can be seen from Figure 6B that with the increase of the humidification degree, the damping ratio of the hydrated lime improved loess without the nano-MgO increases from 0.0395 to 0.0525, then to 0.0565, increased by 32.91% and 7.62%, respectively. The damping ratio of the hydrated lime improved loess with the nano-MgO increases from 0.03487 to 0.04527, then to 0.04557, increased by 29.83% and 0.67%, respectively. The damping ratio of both two improved loess increases. The reason is as follows. The increase of the free water destroys the internal structure of soil, reduces the friction between soil particles, and dissolves the softened part of the cementing material, weakening the cementing effect of soil particles. The slip between soil particles is more likely to occur. At the same stress level, the sliding distance between soil particles and the work done to overcome external load increase, so the damping ratio increases.

Influence of nano-MgO modification

Under the same humidification degree, the damping ratio of the hydrated lime improved loess with the nano-MgO is obviously smaller than that of the hydrated lime improved loess without the nano-MgO (Figure 6B). The damping ratios are reduced from 0.0395 to 0.03487, from 0.0525 to 0.04527, and from 0.0565 to 0.04557, for the three humidification degrees. The corresponding reduction rates are 11.72%, 13.77%, and 19.35% respectively. On one hand, due to the high surface activity effect of the nano-MgO and the combination of free water in the soil, some soil particles are wrapped in an agglomerate state, which reduces the relative sliding trend between soil particles. It makes the soil particles more closely connected. When the nano-MgO is added to the soil, some of the nano-MgO particles will attach to the surface of the soil particles, so the roughness of these soil particles increases. It amplifies the sliding friction resistance between the soil particles and enhances the deformation resistance of the soil. Under the same dynamic load stress level, the external load does less work, so the damping ratio of the hydrated lime improved loess with the nano-MgO is less than that of the hydrated lime improved loess without the nano-MgO.

(1)During the initial loading phase, the soil’s cumulative strain, cumulative plastic strain, elastic strain, and damping ratio all increase sharply before stabilizing as loading continues. Due to the compaction effect on the soil during early loading, the dynamic elastic modulus initially increases. As loading progresses, internal soil damage gradually occurs, causing the dynamic elastic modulus to decrease.

Limitations of the study

The applied objective of this study is to improve the loess subgrade; hence, this technology is frequently employed in large-scale construction projects. By reviewing other scholars' investigations into causes of major construction delays,62 we recognize that this research may encounter numerous challenges in practical implementation. These include the relatively high cost of nano-MgO, which may lead to budgetary constraints; susceptibility of the curing environment for nano-MgO-lime-treated loess to external factors; and difficulties in ensuring uniform mixing when treating large volumes of loess. Further exploration is needed regarding the large-scale engineering applications and solutions for this research.

Method details

Raw materials

The loess used in the experiments was collected from a construction site in Baqiao District, Xi’an City, Shaanxi Province, China, at a depth of 4 m. The loess sample is light yellow, uniform in texture, and free of impurities. The natural water content measured on site with a cutting ring was 12.7%, and the optimal water content was determined as 20.3% (Wopt) through the laboratory compaction test. Before the experiments, the samples were air-dried and passed through a 0.5 mm sieve for preparation. The nano-MgO used in the experiments was obtained from a metal smelting company in Guangzhou, and the hydrated lime was from a chemical plant in Jiangxi.

Sample preparation

The samples were prepared with a 50 mm × 100 mm hollow cylindrical mold. The drop height is 28 cm, and the hammer head mass is 850 g. The preparation steps are as follows: Firstly, according to the optimal water content, hydrated lime content, and nano-MgO content, weigh the materials for application. We added 3% lime and 0.8% nano-MgO to the loess. After mixing the weighted lime, loess, and nano-MgO evenly, pour water in three batches and mix evenly. Then, we poured the evenly mixed mixture into the mold in five layers with an identical layer thickness of 2 cm. After pouring, compact each layer with a drop hammer to the specified height. After reaching the specified height of each layer, scrape the surface and switch to the next layer until all five layers are completed. Finally, we wrapped the prepared cylindrical soil samples with the plastic wrap and placed them in a standard curing box for curing 7 days. The curing temperature is 20°C, and the humidity is 90%.

Test procedure

Through testing, we conducted microstructural analysis and dynamic cumulative strain testing on the specimens, enabling us to elucidate the mechanism of action of the improvement measures, their effectiveness, and the factors influencing performance enhancement.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)

We employed electron microscopy to examine the microstructure of soil samples, analyzing structural changes before and after modification to elucidate the mechanism of action of nano-MgO and lime. Selected test specimens underwent gold sputtering treatment before being placed in the equipment for observation and extraction of microstructural images.

X-Ray diffraction (XRD)

We ground the standard section of the test specimen into powder, placed it in the holding device, covered it with the paddle, secured it in the instrument’s designated position, and conducted the test. By connecting the computer to the equipment, we obtained the specimen’s composition data and exported it to generate a graph.

Dynamic cumulative deformation test

The experiment primarily investigates the humidity resistance of the hydrated lime improved loess with the addition of the nano-MgO, aiming to provide new ideas for the improvement of loess and focusing on comparing the effects of two loess improvement schemes under humidification conditions. Therefore, the test set the confining pressure, deviatoric stress, loading frequency, and dynamic stress amplitude as constants instead of variables. The test was conducted via the GDS dynamic triaxial testing system. To simulate the real stress state of soil in the roadbed, the confining pressure σ3 was set to 30 kPa, the deviatoric stress σ1 was 30 kPa, and the dynamic stress amplitude σd was 30 kPa by considering the buried depth of soil in the roadbed and the mass of rails laid on the upper part of the roadbed. During the formal test, the specimen was firstly loaded under the confining pressure of 30 kPa and deviatoric stress of 30 kPa to σa = σ1+σ3. Then, the specimen was consolidated for 2 h. After the consolidation stage, the dynamic load was applied 10000 times.

Quantification and statistical analysis

This experiment employs comparative testing to analyze results. Structural and compositional changes before and after modification were examined via scanning electron microscopy and XRD analysis. Specimens underwent dynamic triaxial testing to simulate pressure conditions, analyzing differences in cumulative deformation, dynamic modulus, and damping ratio before and after modification. Concurrently, the effects of vibration frequency, humidification level, and modifier dosage on these properties were evaluated. We conducted detailed statistical analysis of the experimental data and plotted relevant graphs to make the results analysis more vivid and concrete.

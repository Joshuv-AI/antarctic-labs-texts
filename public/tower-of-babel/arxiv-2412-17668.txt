Abstract
The graph coloring game is a famous two-player game (re)introduced by Bodlaender in 1991 1991 . Given a graph G G and k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} , Alice and Bob alternately (starting with Alice) color an uncolored vertex with some color in { 1 , ⋯ , k } \{1,\cdots,k\} such that no two adjacent vertices receive a same color. If eventually all vertices are colored, then Alice wins and Bob wins otherwise. The game chromatic number χ g ​ ( G ) \chi_{g}(G) is the smallest integer k k such that Alice has a winning strategy with k k colors in G G . It has been recently (2020) shown that, given a graph G G and k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} , deciding whether χ g ​ ( G ) ≤ k \chi_{g}(G)\leq k is PSPACE-complete. Surprisingly, this parameter is not well understood even in “simple” graph classes. Let P n P_{n} denote the path with n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 vertices. For instance, in the case of Cartesian grids, it is easy to show that χ g ​ ( P m ​ □ ​ P n ) ≤ 5 \chi_{g}(P_{m}\square P_{n})\leq 5 since χ g ​ ( G ) ≤ Δ + 1 \chi_{g}(G)\leq\Delta+1 for any graph G G with maximum degree Δ \Delta . However, the exact value is only known for small values of m m , namely χ g ​ ( P 1 ​ □ ​ P n ) = 3 \chi_{g}(P_{1}\square P_{n})=3 , χ g ​ ( P 2 ​ □ ​ P n ) = 4 \chi_{g}(P_{2}\square P_{n})=4 and χ g ​ ( P 3 ​ □ ​ P n ) = 4 \chi_{g}(P_{3}\square P_{n})=4 for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 [Raspaud, Wu, 2009]. Here, we prove that, for every n ≥ 18 n\geq 18 , χ g ​ ( P 4 ​ □ ​ P n ) = 4 \chi_{g}(P_{4}\square P_{n})=4 .

4
Further work

The natural open problem is to decide whether
\chi_{g}(P_{m}\square P_{n})\leq 4
for every grid when
m,n\geq 5
.
In our opinion, obtaining such results for
m,n\geq 5
should require the use of an essentially different technique.
Indeed, there can be many more configurations already fitting in a
5\times 5
square that may lead Alice to be inevitably defeated with only four colors; the question is to know if (and when) Bob can always force a fifth color by reaching such a configuration.

The question we addressed could also be extended to the cylinder
P_{4}\square C_{n}
(and more generally to
P_{m}\square C_{n}
): does the absence of a first and last column have an impact on whether or not Alice has a winning strategy with four colors when she begins?

Finally, it would also be interesting to consider the coloring game in other graph classes.
In particular, the case of trees is not settled yet.

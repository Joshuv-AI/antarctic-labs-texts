Large deviations for macroscopic observables of heavy-tailed matrices
Charles Bordenave, Alice Guionnet, Camille Male

Abstract
We consider a finite collection of independent Hermitian heavy-tailed random matrices of growing dimension. Our model includes the Lévy matrices proposed by Bouchaud and Cizeau, as well as sparse random matrices with O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) non-zero entries per row. By representing these matrices as weighted graphs, we derive a large deviations principle for key macroscopic observables. Specifically, we focus on the empirical distribution of eigenvalues, the joint neighborhood distribution, and the joint traffic distribution. As an application, we define a notion of microstates entropy for traffic distributions which is additive for the free traffic convolution.

Introduction

1.1
Large deviations for empirical spectral distribution of random matrices

Background

Consider a self-adjoint matrix
Y\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
of dimension
n
. We classically define its
empirical spectral distribution
(ESD) as the probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
which puts an equal mass to all its eigenvalues counted with multiplicities :

L_{Y}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{\lambda_{k}(Y)}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}).

(1.1)

Given a sequence of matrices
Y=Y_{n}
of growing dimensions, a classical question
is to study the convergence, say for the weak topology, of
L_{Y}
as the dimension grows toward a limiting probability measure
L_{\star}
. When
Y
is a random matrix, such convergence could be in expectation or in probability. This convergence of the ESD is well-understood for essentially all classical models of self-adjoint random matrices, see the monographs
[
13
,
7
,
42
]
.

Much less is known however on the probability of large deviations of the ESD away form its typical behavior
L_{\star}
beyond a short list of probabilistic ensembles. Before going through this list, let us recall briefly the definition of a
large deviation principle
(LDP) for a sequence of random variables which was introduced by Varadhan
[
46
]
. Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space. A
rate function

I:\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty]
is a lower semi-continuous function. A rate function is
good
if for all
t\in[0,\infty)
,
I^{-1}([0,t])
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{X}
. Then, a sequence of random variables
X_{n}\in(\mathcal{X},d)
(here
X_{n}=L_{Y}
and
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
) satisfies a LDP at rate
v_{n}
with
\lim_{n}v_{n}=\infty
and rate function
I
, if for every measurable set
A\subset\mathcal{X}
,

-\inf_{x\in\dot{A}}I(x)\leqslant\liminf\frac{1}{v_{n}}\ln\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in A)\leqslant\limsup\frac{1}{v_{n}}\ln\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in A)\leqslant-\inf_{x\in\bar{A}}I(x),

where
\dot{A}
and
\bar{A}
are the interior and closure of
A
respectively. For references on this classical topic of probability theory, see
[
34
,
32
,
35
]
.

Sanov’s theorem asserts that the rate of the LDP for
the empirical measure of i.i.d random variables is always
n
and therefore does not depend on their distribution. The situation is very different for the LDP for ESD of random matrices where it depends on the tail of the entries.
We now list the known LDP for ESD of random matrices, ordered by decreasing rates. The LDP for the ESD at rate
n^{2}
of the Gaussian unitary ensemble and more general orthogonal or unitary invariant matrix ensembles was established in
[
16
]
. Recently
[
15
,
43
]
established the LDP with rate
n^{2}
for the ESD of sums of matrices in typical positions :
A+UBU^{*}
where
A,B
are self-adjoint and
U
is random unitary sampled according to the Haar measure on the unitary group. In
[
22
]
, the LDP with rate
n^{1+\alpha/2}
was proved for Wigner matrices
Y=X/\sqrt{n}
where the entries of
X
have an exponential tail of the form
\mathbb{P}(|X_{ij}|\geqslant t)\sim_{t\to\infty}c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}t^{\alpha})
for some
0<\alpha<2
. In the recent work
[
10
]
, Augeri
proves the LDP for the ESD for sparse Wigner matrices
Y_{ij}=A_{ij}X_{ij}/\sqrt{np}
where
X
is self-adjoint with iid bounded centered entries above the diagonal and
A
is self-adjoint with iid Bernoulli
\{0,1\}
-entries above the diagonal,
\mathbb{P}(A_{ij}=1)=p
. In the regime
\log n\ll p\ll 1
, Augeri proves that
L_{Y}
satisfies a LDP with rate
n^{2}p
. Moreover, from the references
[
23
]
and
[
18
, pp 103-107]
, a LDP at rate
n
for the ESD of the adjacency matrix can be extracted for the above matrix
A
in the regime
p=d/n
with
d
fixed or for adjacency matrices of random graphs uniform given their sequence of degrees, provided that the degree sequence converges.

A first goal of the present work is to present new large deviation principles for the ESD of random matrices at rate
n
.

Weighted graphs with uniformly bounded average degrees

Heavy-tailed random matrices

We consider a random Hermitian matrix
Y_{n}
such that
(Y_{ij})_{i>j}
are iid with law
\gamma_{n}
, the diagonal entries
(Y_{ii})_{i}
are independent variables, and they are independent of
(Y_{ij})_{i>j}
. We assume that
\gamma_{n}
is invariant by complex conjugation and that for every Borel sets

,

,

\lim_{n\to\infty}n\gamma_{n}(A)=\Lambda(A),

(1.3)

where
\Lambda
is a non-trivial Radon measure on

This measure satisfies (

Back to the general setup, we assume the tail assumption: for some

n\mathbb{P}(|Y_{ij}|\geqslant t)\leqslant c_{0}t^{-\alpha}\quad\hbox{ for all $0<t\leqslant 1$}.

(1.6)

The rate function for regular weighted graphs

In this paragraph, we give an expression for the rate function
J_{\gamma,\pi}
which appears in Theorem
1.2
in the simplest example where
\pi_{d}=\delta_{d}
is a Dirac mass at some integer
d\geqslant 2
and the support of
\gamma
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
. The general expression of the rate functions in the above theorems requires to introduce more notions and we postpone its definition to Section
5
.

Let
T=(V,E)
be an infinite
d
-regular tree. We equip the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{E}
,
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{E})
, with the product topology. A real random process indexed by
E
, say
A=(A_{e})_{e\in E}
, is invariant if its law is left invariant by all automorphisms of
T
(that is bijections
\varphi:V\to V
such that
\varphi(T)=T
). We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{E})\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{E})
, the set of laws of invariant processes such that for any finite subsets
F\subset E
of edges, the restriction of
A
to
F
has a finite relative entropy with respect to the product measure
\gamma^{\otimes F}
. Recall that the relative entropy between probability measures
p,q
is given by
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p|q)=\int dp(z)\ln(dp(z)/dq(z))
.

Let
o\in V
be a distinguished vertex and
\rho=\{a,b\}\in E
be a distinguished edge. For
h\geqslant 1
, let
B_{h}
be the ball of radius
h
around
o
and
\vec{B}_{h}
be the ball of radius
h-1
around
\rho
(the union of the two ball of radius
h-1
around
a
and
b
). We let
\mu_{h}
and
\vec{\mu}_{h}
be the laws of the restriction of
A
to the balls of radius
h
around the roots. For
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{E})
we define its entropy as

\Sigma_{\gamma,\pi_{d}}(\mu)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lim_{h\to\infty}\left(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu_{h}|\gamma^{\otimes B_{h}})-\frac{d}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\vec{\mu}_{h}|\gamma^{\otimes\vec{B}_{h}})\right)&\hbox{if $\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{E})$}\\
\infty&\hbox{\hbox{otherwise.}}\end{array}\right.

We shall prove that the above limit is well-defined: in fact, for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{E})
,
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu_{h}|\gamma^{\otimes B_{h}})-\frac{d}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\vec{\mu}_{h}|\gamma^{\otimes\vec{B}_{h}})
is non-decreasing in
h
. The entropy
\Sigma_{\gamma,\pi_{d}}
will appear as the good rate function of an LDP on random weighted regular graphs. The unique global minimizer of
\Sigma_{\gamma,\pi_{d}}
is the product measure
\gamma^{\otimes E}
.

Now, to each element
A=(A_{e})_{e\in E}\in\mathbb{R}^{E}
with uniformly bounded coordinates,
\sup_{e}|A_{e}|<\infty
, we can associate a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\ell^{2}(V)
, which we also denote by
A
, by the following formula that for every
\psi\in\ell^{2}(V)
and
u\in V
,

(A\psi)(u)=\sum_{v:\{u,v\}\in E}A_{\{u,v\}}\psi(v).

From the spectral theorem, we can then define its spectral measure at vector
\psi
as the unique probability measure
L_{A}^{\psi}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
such that for every bounded continuous functions
f
,

\langle\psi,f(A)\psi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)dL_{A}^{\psi}(\lambda).

Since
\gamma
has bounded support, the above condition
\sup_{e}|A_{e}|<\infty
holds a.s. if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{E})
and
A
has law
\mu
. If
(e_{v})_{v\in V}
is the canonical basis of
\ell^{2}(V)
, we set

L_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[L^{e_{o}}_{A}]=\int L^{e_{o}}_{A}d\mu(A).

We are finally ready to give an expression for the rate function
J_{\gamma,\pi_{d}}
which appears in Theorem
1.2
: for
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
,

J_{\gamma,\pi_{d}}(p)=\inf\left\{\Sigma_{\gamma,\pi_{d}}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{E})\hbox{ such that }L_{\mu}=p\right\},

(1.7)

with the usual convention that the inf over an empty set is
\infty
.
This expression is already rather delicate to understand: neither the map
\mu\to L_{\mu}
nor the map
\mu\to\Sigma_{\gamma,\pi_{d}}(\mu)
are straightforward to compute. In general, the formula for the rate functions
J_{\gamma,d}
,
J_{\gamma,\pi}
and
J_{\Lambda}
in Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
are of the same nature but will require more care to be properly defined.

1.2
A microstates entropy for heavy-tailed traffic distribution

Background

The notion of traffics
[
40
]
generalizes the notion of non-commutative variables introduced by Voiculescu in the framework of free probability, see the monographs
[
48
,
7
,
42
]
. Voiculescu defined several notions of entropy in this framework, mainly the microstates free entropy
\chi
and the free entropy
\chi^{*}
, see
[
50
,
51
]
. Even though these two notions were expected to match, it could only be proved so far that one is bounded by the other
[
20
,
30
,
37
]
. The microstates entropy is defined as the rate function of a large deviation principle for the non-commutative distribution of Gaussian matrices, namely as the volume of matrices whose non-commutative distribution approximates a given non-commutative law. One of the main difficulty is that a full large deviation principle has not yet been proven, in particular that the limsup of these volumes can be replaced by a liminf. As a consequence, one of the main expected property that the entropy sums under freeness is still an open question in general.

The introduction of traffic distributions was motivated by study of the non-commutative distribution of heavy-tailed matrices and sparse matrices
[
39
]
. While they allow to generalize the later, they also give a more appropriate framework to deal with such matrices which have no unitary invariance, but rather invariance under permutation multiplied entry-wise by bounded matrices. This distribution symmetry is relevant for matrices that are associated to random marked graphs. On the other hand, the data of the traffic distribution is equivalent to the neighborhood distribution of these graphs.

In this section, we show how the large deviation principle for the law of a Erdös Renyi marked graphs implies a large deviation principle for their traffic distribution, by a contraction principle. The rate function for these large deviations defines an entropy in the traffic framework that we call the entropy of sparse traffics. We show that it sums under independence of traffics
[
39
,
40
]
.

Traffic distribution

Let us recall the notion of
traffic distribution
of a collection
\mathbf{Y}
of matrices in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. It is encoded in the linear form defined below, which contains more information than the usual non commutative distribution of
\mathbf{Y}
seen in the
*
-probability space
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
equipped with the tracial state
\frac{1}{n}{\rm tr}(\cdot)
.

Let us fix a label set
J
. We call
test graph
labeled in
J
a finite connected graphs
H=(V,E,\ell,\varepsilon)
with possibly multiple edges, where
V
is a nonempty set and each edge
e\in E
has labels
\ell(e)\in J
and
\varepsilon(e)\in\{1,*\}
. We use this terminology since we use them as elementary functions in order to understand
\mathbf{Y}=(Y_{j})_{j\in J}
, for which an edge
e\in E
is associated to the matrix
Y_{\ell(e)}^{\varepsilon(e)}
(
Y_{j}^{*}
denotes the conjugate transpose of
Y_{j}
). In the definitions of this subsection, the graphs are directed, and they may have self-loops and multiple edges, each with its own labels. An
isomorphism
between two test graphs
H
and
H^{\prime}
is a bijection between the sets of vertices of
H
and
H^{\prime}
which preserves the adjacency structure and the edge labels. We denote by
\mathcal{H}\langle J\rangle
the set of test graphs up to isomorphisms, by
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{H}\langle J\rangle
the linear space it generates (the elements of the set form a basis of the space). Then an (algebraic)
traffic distribution
is an element of the linear dual space
{\mathrm{Traf\langle J\rangle}}:={\mathbb{C}\mathcal{H}}\langle J\rangle^{*}
of linear forms on
\mathbb{C}\mathcal{H}\langle J\rangle
. If
\mathbf{Y}=(Y_{j})_{j\in J}
is a collection of matrices in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, then their canonical traffic distribution
\tau_{\mathbf{Y}}\in{\mathrm{Traf\langle J\rangle}}
is defined by

\tau_{\mathbf{Y}}[H]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\phi:V\to\{1,\ldots,n\}}\prod_{e=(v,w)\in E}Y_{\ell(e)}^{\varepsilon(e)}\big(\phi(w),\phi(v)\big).

(1.8)

We equip
{\mathrm{Traf\langle J\rangle}}
with the topology of pointwise convergence. Investigating LDPs for traffic distribution, a difficulty arises with the fact that the canonical traffic distribution
\tau_{\mathbf{Y}}[H]
may go to infinity or become ill-defined when

goes to infinity.
This can be avoided if each of the matrices belong to a set

B_{n}(\theta)=\left\{Y\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}):\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}1\!\!{\sf I}_{Y_{ij}\neq 0}\Big)\leq\theta,\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}1\!\!{\sf I}_{Y_{ij}\neq 0}\Big)\leq\theta,\max_{1\leq i,j\leq n}|Y_{ij}|\leq\theta\right\}\,,

(1.9)

for some deterministic real
\theta>0
.
This is the case when
\mathbf{Y}
are independent matrices with law
Z
as in Theorem
1.2
with
\pi
and
\gamma
with bounded support. We then can show that

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathbf{Y}
be

independent matrices distributed as in Theorem

\displaystyle\quad=\;\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbf{Y}\in B_{n}(\theta)^{J}\;;d(\tau_{\mathbf{Y}},\tau)\leq\epsilon\right),

(1.11)

The need to restrict ourselves to matrices in
B_{n}(\theta)
is similar to Voiculescu’s restriction to matrices with bounded norm in his definition of the micro-sates entropy: otherwise the traffic distribution may be ill-defined. For example, in
[
9
]
, the rate of the large deviations for traces of moments of GUE matrices is shown to depend on the power of these moments.

The second strategy to define a micro-state entropy and circumvent this issue is to change the topology. In the definition of Voiculescu’s micro-states entropy, in
[
20
,
28
]
polynomials test functions were replaced by products of resolvents. In
[
14
]
, it was shown that these different notions give the same definition of Voiculescu’s micro-states entropy.

In our case where
\mathbf{Y}
are
|J|
independent heavy-tailed random matrices satisfying (
1.10
), we may use that with overwhelming probability only a few number of columns have a large number of non-zero (or very small) entries or large entries. To study the traffic distribution of these matrices, we found to be more convenient to study the
rooted
traffic distribution
\tau_{\mathbf{Y},i}
where the value of the function
\phi
in Definition
1.8
is specified for a vertex of
H
: the root of
H
has to be sent to a given
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We then take
i
at random and consider the randomly rooted-traffic distribution given by

\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{Y}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\tau_{\mathbf{Y},i}}.

Our main result is a LDP for the random rooted traffic distribution of independent sparse Wigner or heavy-tailed random matrices, see subsection
6.3
for the precise definition of the topologies.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
\gamma_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C})
is invariant by complex conjugation, and that (
1.3
) and (
1.10
) hold for some
\Lambda
. Then
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{Y}}
, the randomly rooted traffic distribution of
\mathbf{Y}
, satisfies a LDP with speed
n
and good rate function
\chi_{\Lambda}
with a unique minimizer
\tau_{\Lambda}
.

The rate function
\chi_{\Lambda}
defines a notion of entropy for the traffic distributions: by definition as a rate function it is a micro-states entropy since it measures the volume of small balls around a given traffic distribution. On the other hand, we identify in (
6.14
), Section
6
, that the microstates entropy
\chi_{\Lambda}
is given by a rate function
\chi_{\Lambda}^{*}
, thanks a contraction principle as the pushforward of an entropy for the law of a weighted Erdös-Rényi graphs, see Theorem
2.5
.
The minimizer
\tau_{\Lambda}
is the product of the distributions
\Phi_{\Lambda}
as described in
[
40
, Definition 2.11]
, with
\Phi_{\Lambda}
the limiting traffic distribution of a single matrix (see
[
39
]
). An advantage of
\chi_{\Lambda}
over
\tilde{\chi}_{\Lambda}
defined in (
1.11
) is that it does not require a truncation step.

Interestingly, the existence of the LDP implies that this entropy is additive for the traffic distributions in situation of
traffic independence
. The notion of independence for traffics was introduced in
[
40
]
, and encodes in particular the other notions of non-commutative independence such as classical, boolean or free independence. As for the latter notions, it is formulated in terms of a product of their traffic distribution, called the free product of the traffic distributions where
free
has be be understood as
canonical
.

To fit the framework of LDP, surprisingly, we must consider an adequate notion of
random traffic distributions
drawing inspiration from the concept of amalgamation, which allows to well define the free product
\tau_{1}*\tau_{2}
of two random traffic distributions, see Section
7
.

Corollary 1.6
.

For any random traffic distributions
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
, with
\chi_{\Lambda}
as in Theorem
1.5

\chi_{\Lambda}(\tau_{1}*\tau_{2})=\chi_{\Lambda}(\tau_{1})+\chi_{\Lambda}(\tau_{2}).

This result is interesting notably because the analog statement is open in general for Voiculescu’s microstates entropy. Voiculescu’s free product
\Phi=\Phi_{1}*\Phi_{2}
of two non-commutative tracial distribution corresponds to the free product
\tau_{\Phi}=\tau_{\Phi_{1}}*\tau_{\Phi_{2}}
via the universal construction
[
29
]
. However, the case considered in this article is somehow orthogonal to Voiculescu’s original motivation, when
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are the distribution of free semicircular variables the above additivity results is trivial as both sides are infinite. The techniques of this article may open perspectives for further investigations on Voiculescu’s microstates entropy question.

1.3
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
, we define the metric space of unlabeled random rooted marked graphs and its local weak topology. Some of our matrices will be embedded into this space. On this metric space, we establish large deviation principles. In Section
4
, we consider the enlarged metric space of unlabeled random rooted networks which is necessary to deal with general heavy-tailed matrices. In Section
5
, we prove that the ESD extends to a continuous function on these metric spaces. As a byproduct, we will obtain Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
by a contraction principle. In Section
6
, we prove Theorem
1.5
and Corollary
1.6
again by a contraction principle. We also prove that traffic distributions and neighborhood distributions of unlabeled rooted marked graphs are homeomorphic in Lemmas
6.2
and
6.3
. Finally, in Section
8
, we establish a general convergence result for the relative entropy of discretization variables in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is of general interest.

2
Microstates entropy for locally finite marked graphs

2.1
Marked graphs

Basics

We start with a few definitions. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph on a countable set
V
and edge set
E
. In the definitions of this section, the graphs are unoriented, they can have multiple edges and self-loops attached to a vertex. A graph is
locally finite
if for all
v\in V
,
\deg(v)<\infty
, where
\deg(v)
is the degree of
v\in V
in
G
(i.e. number of adjacent edges, a self-loop counts as two adjacent edges). Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a set. A
marked graph

G=(V,E,\xi)
on
\mathcal{Z}
is a graph
(V,E)

where each edge carries two marks in
\mathcal{Z}
, one towards
each of its endpoints. For an edge
e=\{u,v\}\in E
between vertices
u,v
in
V
, we denote its mark towards the vertex
v
by
\xi(u,v)
, and its mark towards the vertex
u
by
\xi(v,u)
. The marked graphs considered in this work are
symmetric
marked graphs, namely we assume that
\mathcal{Z}
is equipped with an involution denoted by
*
(
(z^{*})^{*}=z
for all
z\in\mathcal{Z})
such that for all
\{u,v\}\in E
,

\xi(u,v)=\xi(v,u)^{*}.

(2.1)

By marked graph, we will always mean symmetric marked graph (note that there is no loss of generality here: up to replacing
\mathcal{Z}
by
\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Z}
equipped with the involution
(z,w)^{*}=(w,z)
we may always identify a marked graph with a symmetric marked graph). For functions or parameters of
G
, we might use sometimes a subscript
G
to insist on its dependence on
G
,
V_{G},E_{G},\deg_{G},\xi_{G}
and so on.

For a marked graph
G=(V,E,\xi)
and
v\in V
, we denote by
G(v)
the connected component
v
(the subgraph spanned by vertices connected to
v
).

A rooted marked graph
g=(G,o)
is the pair formed by a connected marked graph and a distinguished vertex
o\in V(G)
called the root. Let
(G,o)
and
(G^{\prime},o^{\prime})
be rooted marked graphs. We say that
(G,o)
and
(G^{\prime},o^{\prime})
are
isomorphic
, and write
(G,o)\equiv(G^{\prime},o^{\prime})
, if there exists a bijection between the sets of vertices of
G
and
G^{\prime}
which maps
o
to
o^{\prime}
while preserving the adjacency structure of these connected components, and the edge marks.
Let
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}=\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{Z})
denote the space of locally finite rooted marked graphs up to isomorphism. In combinatorial language,
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{Z})
is the set of unlabeled locally finite rooted marked graphs. From
[
3
,
4
]
, if
\mathcal{Z}
is a Baire space, for each
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
, there is a canonical representative marked graph on a vertex set in
\mathbb{N}
with root
o=0
. It is thus fine to write for
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
,
g=(G,o)
where
G=(V,E,\xi)
is a marked graph as long as the properties we consider are class properties (that is do not depend on the choice of the representative of
g
). In the next subsection, we shall introduce a random labeling which will turn to be convenient.

If
G=(V,E,\xi)
is a marked graph, its unmarked graph is denoted by
\bar{G}=(V,E)
. Similarly for rooted marked graphs, if
g=(G,o)
, we set
\bar{g}=(\bar{G},o)
.

Degree sequence

Let
G=(V,E,\xi)
be a locally finite marked graph. The
marked degree
of
v\in V
is the counting measure
{\mathrm{Deg}}(v)
on
\mathcal{Z}
defined by

{\mathrm{Deg}}(v)=\sum_{u\sim v}\delta_{\xi(v,u)},

where the sum is over all
u\in V
such that
\{u,v\}\in E
, that is, the neighbors of
v
. In other words, for any
z\in\mathcal{Z}
,
{\mathrm{Deg}}(v;z):=\int{\bf 1}_{\{z\}}{\mathrm{Deg}}(v)
is the number of neighbors of
u
such that
\xi(v,u)=z
. In particular, the total mass of the marked degree is the usual degree:

{\mathrm{Deg}}(v;\mathcal{Z})=\deg(v).

The
marked degree sequence
of
G
is the sequence
({\mathrm{Deg}}(v))_{v\in V}
. If
G=(V,E,\xi)
is finite then the following
edge counting measure
is well-defined:

{\mathfrak{m}}=\sum_{v\in V}{\mathrm{Deg}}(v)=\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\in E}\left(\delta_{\xi(u,v)}+\delta_{\xi(v,u)}\right),

where we recall that
\{u,v\}
and
E
are non oriented edges.
Namely, for any
z\in\mathcal{Z}
,
{\mathfrak{m}}(z)=\int 1_{\{z\}}d{\mathfrak{m}}
is the number of oriented edges
(u,v)
with
\{u,v\}\in E
such that
\xi(u,v)=z
. From (
2.1
) and the hand-shaking lemma, if
G
is finite, the counting measure
{\mathfrak{m}}
satisfies the following balance equations : for any subset
A\subset\mathcal{Z}
:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}(A)={\mathfrak{m}}(A^{*})\and{\mathfrak{m}}(A)\in 2\mathbb{N}\;\hbox{ if $A=A^{*}$}.

(2.2)

For
V
finite, the Erdős-Gallai Theorem and its extensions give the necessary and sufficient conditions for a sequence of finite counting measures on
\mathcal{Z}
,
D=(D(v))_{v\in V}
to be the marked degree sequence of a simple marked graph
G=(V,E,\xi)
, see
[
36
,
45
]
.

2.2
Benjamini-Schramm convergence

Local weak topology

The local topology on
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is the product topology inherited from projections around the root. More precisely, for an integer number
r\geqslant 0
and
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
, let
g_{r}
be the intersection of the graph
G
with the ball (for the graph metric) of center
o
and radius
r
. Assume from now on that
\mathcal{Z}
is a complete separable metric space equipped with the distance
d
. Let
g=(G,o)
,
g^{\prime}=(G^{\prime},o^{\prime})\in\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
, we say that the pair
(r,\delta)
is good for
(g,g^{\prime})
if there exists an isomorphism
\psi
between
\bar{g}_{r}
and
\bar{g}^{\prime}_{r}
such that

\sup_{u,v\in V(G_{r})}d(\xi(u,v),\xi^{\prime}_{(\psi(u),\psi(v))})\leqslant\delta.

We then endow
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
with the local topology, which is compatible with the distance

{\mathrm{d}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}}(g,g^{\prime})=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{1+r}+\delta:(r,\delta)\mbox{ is good for }(g,g^{\prime})\right\}.

The set
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
equipped with
{\mathrm{d}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}}
is a complete separable metric space. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
the set of probability measures on
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
equipped with the topology of weak convergence.

Neighborhood distribution of finite graphs

Let
G=(V,E,\xi)
be a marked graph with
V
finite. The
neighborhood distribution

U(G)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
of
G
is the law of the equivalence class of the rooted graph
(g,o)
where the root
o
is sampled uniformly, namely

U(G)=\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{v\in V}\delta_{[G,v]},

(2.3)

where
[G,v]\in\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
stands for the equivalence class of
(G(v),v)
.

We shall say that a sequence of finite marked graphs
(G_{n})
converges in
Benjamini-Schramm
sense toward
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
if
U(G_{n})
converges weakly to
\mu
.

Unimodularity

The neighborhood distribution
U(G)
satisfies a reversibility assumption called
unimodularity
, see
[
3
]
. An edge-rooted marked graph
(G,e)
is a connected marked graph
G
with a distinguished oriented edge
e=(u,v)\in V^{2}
(we do not necessarily assume
\{u,v\}\in E
). The above isomorphisms for rooted marked graphs extend to edge-rooted marked graphs and we may speak of unlabeled edge-rooted marked graphs. We denote by
\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}
, the set of unlabeled edge locally finite marked graphs. The local topology extends to this setting as well. If
G=(V,E,\xi)
is a connected marked graph and
e=(u,v)\in V^{2}
, let
[G,e]
denotes its associated unlabeled edge-rooted marked graph. We then say that a probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is unimodular if for every non-negative measurable functions
f
on
\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}
, we have

\int\sum_{v\in V}f([G,o,v])d\mu([G,o])=\int\sum_{v\in V}f([G,v,o])d\mu([G,o]).

(2.4)

It is in fact necessary and sufficient to check (
2.4
) for functions
f
such that
f(G,u,v)=0
unless
\{u,v\}\in E_{G}
, see
[
3
, Proposition 2.2]
. This equivalent property is called involution invariance.

Edge-rooting

If
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
and
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\deg(o)=\int\deg(o)d\mu([G,o])\in(0,\infty)
, we define its edge-rooted version
\vec{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet})
by the formula: for every measurable sets
A
on
\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}
,

\vec{\mu}(A)=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\deg(o)}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[\sum_{v\sim o}1\!\!{\sf I}([G,o,v]\in A)\right],

(2.5)

where the sum is over all neighbors of
o
in
G
. Thanks to
[
3
, Proposition 2.2]
, the unimodularity of
\mu
(
2.4
) is equivalent to the invariance of
\vec{\mu}
by inverting the root edge. Observe that if
G=(V,E,\xi)
is a finite graph, then by definition of
U(G)
,

\vec{U}(G)=\frac{1}{2|E|}\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\in E}\left(\delta_{[G,u,v]}+\delta_{[G,v,u]}\right).

Hence,
\vec{U}(G)
corresponds to the law of the edge-rooted graph
(G,\rho)
, where the edge is sampled uniformly at random.

Finite-depth neighborhood and invariance

For integer
h\geqslant 0
, we denote by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h}\subset\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
, the subset of unlabeled rooted marked graph such that
g_{r}=g_{h}
for all
r\geqslant h
: that is unlabeled rooted marked graphs which are contained in the ball of radius
h
from the root. If
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
, we denote by
\mu_{h}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
its image by the map
g\to g_{h}
. Similarly, for integer
h\geqslant 1
for
(G,\rho)\in\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}
,
\rho=(o,o^{\prime})
, we define
(G,\rho)_{h}
as its restriction to the
(h-1)
-neighborhood around
\{o,o^{\prime}\}
(in other words,
(G,\rho)_{h}
is the restriction to the ball of radius
h
centered at a fictitious vertex in the middle of
\{o,o^{\prime}\}
). We define
\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}_{h})
similarly.

We will say that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
is
invariant
if (
2.4
) holds for all
f
such that
f(G,u,v)=0
unless
\{u,v\}
is an edge and
f(G,u,v)
is measurable with respect to
(G,u,v)_{h}
. For
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
, being unimodular is equivalent to
\mu_{h}
invariant for all
h\geqslant 1
(in (
2.4
), functions with bounded support are dense). Similarly, we will say that
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}_{h})
is invariant if
\nu=\vec{\mu}
for some invariant
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
.

Random labeling

As we have seen, unlabeled rooted graphs give the proper setup for defining the Benjamini-Schramm convergence. Rooted labeled graphs are however more convenient than unlabeled rooted graphs to do computations. We now define a randomized canonical labeling of graphs which appeared notably in
[
12
,
11
]
. We define the set of finite integer sequences as

\mathbb{N}^{f}=\cup_{k\geqslant 0}\mathbb{N}^{k}

(2.6)

where
\mathbb{N}^{0}=\{o\}
and
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots,\}
by convention. Let
(G,o)
be a random rooted marked graph. The law of
[G,o]
is denoted by
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
. We perform the breadth-first search tree on the unmarked graph
\bar{G}
started at the root
o
, where ties between vertices are broken uniformly at random and independently of
(G,o)
. This defines a random marked graph
(G^{\prime},o)
on a subset of
\mathbb{N}^{f}
whose law depends only the equivalence class of
(G,o)
: a vertex at distance
k
from the root receives a label in
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, if
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1})
is the label of its parent in the search tree, it has the label
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1},j)
if it is the
j
-th offspring of its parent in the random ordering. We call this random rooted marked graph, the
uniform labeling
of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
.

The same construction can be performed for edge-rooted graphs
(G,\rho)
,
\rho=(o_{1},o_{2})
, with two copies
\mathbb{N}^{f}\times\mathbb{N}^{f}
. Indeed, we perform the breadth-first search tree by starting from two seeds
(o_{1},o_{2})
: we start with the neighbors of
o_{1}
different from
o_{2}
, then the neighbors of
o_{2}
which have not been seen before and so on. This defines the
uniform labeling
of
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet})
.

In the sequel, when the choice of the root is clear, we will often omit the root and write
G
in place of
(G,o)
or
(G,\rho)
.

2.3
Entropy for marked graphs

2.3.1
Graphs with colors and reals marks

In this subsection, we define an entropy associated to the Benjamini-Schramm topology for rooted weighted graphs when the weights are taken in
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
where
\mathcal{B}
is a finite set (which can be thought as the set of ”colors”, which will later allow us to include traffic distributions of several matrices, the set
\mathcal{B}
being then the set of indices of these matrices). This notion of entropy is obtained by using microstates, or rather in a probabilistic terminology, by establishing a large deviation principle for a sequence of random weighted graphs. When
\mathcal{Z}
is a finite set such results have been established in
[
23
,
31
,
11
]
.

We consider an involution
*
on
\mathcal{B}
. To avoid technicalities, we consider an involution on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
which comes from a signed involution on coordinates. More precisely, we fix some
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{k}
and a permutation
\tau\in S_{k}
such that for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
\tau^{2}(i)=i
and
\varepsilon_{i}\varepsilon_{\tau(i)}=1
. Then for
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
we set

(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})^{*}=(\varepsilon_{1}x_{\tau(1)},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k}x_{\tau(k)}).

(2.7)

The most relevant examples are
x^{*}=x
, on
\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
the complex conjugate
x^{*}=\bar{x}
and on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
(x_{1},x_{2})^{*}=(x_{2},x_{1})
(this choice allows to represent marks on vertices as marks on edges). These involutions on
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathbb{R}^{k}
extend as an involution on
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
defined as
(b,x)^{*}=(b^{*},x^{*})
.

Let
G=(V,E,\xi)
be a (possibly random) marked graph on the mark set
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We denote by
G^{0}=(V,E,\xi^{0})
the marked graph with mark set
\mathcal{B}
obtained from
G=(V,E,\xi)
by setting
\xi^{0}(u,v)=b
if
\xi(u,v)=(b,x)
. The same constructions extend to rooted marked graphs. By construction, if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{Z}))
, we denote by
\mu^{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{B}))
the push-forward of
\mu
by the map
(G,o)\mapsto(G^{0},o)
. Similarly, for
\vec{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{Z}))
, its push-forward is denoted by
\vec{\mu}^{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{B}))
. By construction, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{0}}{\mathrm{Deg}}(o)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}{\mathrm{Deg}}(o)(\cdot\times\mathbb{R}^{k}).

2.3.2
Entropy associated to a partially given average marked degree

Graphs with a partially given average marked degree

We start by considering marked graphs such that their edge counting measure restricted to
\mathcal{B}
is given. To this end, we consider
d\in[0,\infty)^{\mathcal{B}}
and let
({\mathfrak{m}}_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathcal{B}}
such that for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}(b)}{n}=d(b),

(2.8)

and which satisfies (
2.2
) for every
b\in\mathcal{B}
. We set

\bar{d}=\sum_{b}d(b).

Let
V_{n}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The set of marked graphs on
V_{n}
with mark set
\mathcal{Z}
whose edge counting measure is
{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}
when restricted to
\mathcal{B}
is denoted by

\mathcal{G}_{n,{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}}=\{G=(V_{G},E_{G},\xi_{G}):V_{G}=V_{n},{\mathfrak{m}}_{G}(\{b\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})={\mathfrak{m}}_{n}(b)\hbox{ for all $b\in\mathcal{B}$}\},

(2.9)

We denote by
\mathcal{G}^{0}_{n,{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}}
the set of simple marked graphs on the mark set
\mathcal{B}
whose edge-counting measure is
{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}
. It is also the image of
\mathcal{G}_{n,{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}}
by the map
G\to G^{0}
(recall the definition in section
2.3.1
). The dependency in
\mathcal{B}
is implicit. The sets
\mathcal{G}^{0}_{n,{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{n,{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}}
are non-empty iif
\bar{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}\leqslant n(n-1)/2
.

Randomly marked graph

We next define a random graph
G_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n},\xi_{n})
taking value in
\mathcal{G}_{n,{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}}
as follows. Let
\gamma_{n}=(\gamma_{n,b})_{b\in\mathcal{B}}
be a collection of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
which are
*
-invariant in the sense that for every
b\in\mathcal{B}
and every Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
,

\gamma_{n,b}(A)=\gamma_{n,b^{*}}(A^{*}).

(2.10)

The graph
G^{0}_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n},\xi^{0}_{n})
is sampled uniformly on
\mathcal{G}^{0}_{n,{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}}
. Then, independently for each edge
e=\{u,v\}\in E_{n}
with
u<v
and
\xi^{0}(u,v)=b
, we sample a weight
\xi_{n}(u,v)
in
\{b\}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
according to the probability distribution
\gamma_{n,b}
. Finally, we set
\xi_{n}(v,u)=\xi_{n}(u,v)^{*}
. Note that since
\gamma_{n}
is
*
-invariant, any deterministic choice of the ordering

(2.11)

We may now state our main result on the large deviation principle for the sequence of random graphs

Theorem 2.1

(Entropy with a given average marked degree)
.

,

and
\vec{U}(G_{n})_{h}
satisfy a LDP on
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
and
\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}_{h})

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
and
\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet})

\vec{\Sigma}_{\gamma,d}(\nu)=\lim_{h}\vec{\Sigma}_{\gamma,d}(\nu_{h},h)

Definition of the entropy

If
G=(V,E,\xi)
is a (possibly random) marked graph on the mark set
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we denote by

the random marked graph obtained as follows. We have
G^{0}_{\gamma}=G^{0}=(V,E,\xi^{0})
. Given

,

and an independent choice of orientation
(u,v)
, for

, where the variables
(y_{e})_{e\in E}
are independent and

has distribution
\gamma_{b}
. Finally, we set
\xi_{\gamma}(v,u)=\xi_{\gamma}(u,v)^{*}
. Note that since
\gamma
is
*
-invariant, the choice of the orientation is irrelevant.

We denote by

the relative entropy between two probability measures

, which we also write

if

has law

and
Y
law

.

Definition 2.2

(Admissible neighborhood)
.

For
h\geqslant 1
, a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
is admissible if it satisfies the following conditions:

(C1)

\mu
is invariant,

(C2)

\mu
is supported on marked trees,

(C3)

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\deg(o)\ln\deg(o)<\infty
,

We further say that
\mu
is
(\gamma,d)
-admissible if it is admissible and

(C4)

\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{0}}{\mathrm{Deg}}(o)=d
,

(C5)

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(G|G_{\gamma})<\infty
, where
G
is the uniform labeling of
\mu
.

We say that
\mu
is
d
-admissible if it is admissible and satisfies (C4). Similarly, we say that
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}_{h})
is admissible if there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
admissible such that
\vec{\mu}=\nu
and
(\gamma,d)
-admissible if we further have that

(C4’)

\xi^{0}(\rho)
has law
d(\cdot)/\bar{d}
and, if
h\geqslant 2
,
d_{\nu}=(\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\deg(o)^{-1})^{-1}\geqslant\bar{d}
,

(C5’)

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\vec{G}|\vec{G}_{\gamma})<\infty
where
\vec{G}
is the uniform labeling of
\nu
.

We say that
\nu
is
d
-admissible if it is is admissible and satisfies (C4’).

We note that if the support of
\gamma
is a finite set, then condition (C5) is satisfied when assumptions (C1)-(C2)-(C3) hold, see
[
23
, Lemma 5.10]
and
[

, Lemma 4]
.

We are ready for the definition of the entropy. In general, the

\Sigma_{\gamma,d}(\mu,h)
and the

will be defined as limits in the proof of Theorem

. For

which is
(\gamma,d)
-admissible, we have an explicit finite expression:

\Sigma_{\gamma,d}(\mu,h)=\Sigma^{0}_{d}(\mu^{0},h)+\Sigma^{1}_{\gamma,d}(\mu,h),

(2.12)

where
\Sigma_{d}^{0}
and
\Sigma_{\gamma,d}^{1}
are defined as follows. If
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
is
(\gamma,d)
-admissible, we set

\Sigma_{\gamma,d}^{1}(\mu,h)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(G|G_{\gamma})-\frac{\bar{d}}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\vec{G}|\vec{G}_{\gamma}),

(2.13)

where
G
and
\vec{G}
are the randomly labeled rooted marked graphs with laws
\mu
and
\vec{\mu}
respectively. We shall check that if
\mu
is
(\gamma,d)
-admissible,
\vec{\mu}
is
(\gamma,d)
-admissible and thus

\mu
is
(\gamma,d)
-admissible.

Recall the definition of the Shannon entropy and conditional Shannon entropy for random variables
X,Y
on a finite set:

H(X)=-\sum_{x}\mathbb{P}(X=x)\ln\mathbb{P}(X=x)\mbox{ and }H(X|Y)=-\sum_{x,y}\mathbb{P}(X=x,Y=y)\ln\mathbb{P}(X=x|Y=y).

If
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h}(\mathcal{B}))
is
d
-admissible, we set

\Sigma^{0}_{d}(\mu,h)=-H(G|(G)_{1})+\frac{\bar{d}}{2}H(\vec{G}|(\vec{G})_{1})+D_{\mathrm{KL}}({\mathrm{Deg}}_{G}(o)|N_{d}),

(2.14)

where
G
and
\vec{G}
are the uniform labeling of

and

respectively and

is a multivariate Poisson variable

are independent and for every

,

has distribution

. Otherwise, we set
\Sigma^{0}_{d}(\mu,h)=\infty
.
For a very interesting alternative expression of

with only non-negative terms involving relative entropies, see the recent work
[
44
]
and Remark

below.

If
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
is not
(\gamma,d)
-admissible then we conjecture that

. We will prove this claim under a small extra condition on

below.

Similarly, for
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}_{h})
which is
(\gamma,d)
-admissible, we also define the edge-entropy associated to
(\gamma,d)
as

\vec{\Sigma}_{\gamma,d}(\nu,h)=\vec{\Sigma}^{0}_{d}(\nu^{0},h)+\vec{\Sigma}^{1}_{\gamma,d}(\nu,h),

(2.15)

where
\vec{\Sigma}^{0}
and
\vec{\Sigma}^{1}
are defined as follows. For integer number
h\geqslant 1
, if
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}_{h})
, we consider the element of
\hat{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h-1})
defined, for

measurable set of
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h-1}
, by

\hat{\nu}(A)=d_{\nu}\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\frac{1\!\!{\sf I}_{[G,o]_{h-1}\in A}}{\deg(o)}\right],

where

. If

has a unique element. Finally, we set

\dot{\nu}=\dot{\nu}(\bar{d})=(1-p)\delta_{0}+p\delta_{\hat{\nu}},

with

and
0
is the rooted graph with a single vertex. For

.

For
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
with finite positive expected degree, we denote by

, its sized-biased version defined for
A
measurable set of
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
as

\check{\mu}(A)=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\deg(o)1\!\!{\sf I}(G\in A)]}{\mathbb{E}[\deg(o)]}

We set

\vec{\Sigma}^{1}_{\gamma,d}(\nu,h)=\frac{\bar{d}}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\vec{G}|\vec{G}_{\gamma})-\bar{d}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\check{G}|\check{G}_{\gamma})+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(G|G_{\gamma}),

where
G
,
\check{G}
and
\vec{G}
are the uniform labeling of
\dot{\nu},\check{\dot{\nu}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h-1})
and
\nu
respectively. Note that for

\vec{\Sigma}^{1}_{\gamma,d}(\nu,1)=\frac{\bar{d}}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\vec{G}|\vec{G}_{\gamma}).

If
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}_{1}(\mathcal{B}))
is
d
-admissible, we set

\vec{\Sigma}_{d}^{0}(\nu,h)=-\frac{\bar{d}}{2}H(\vec{G}|(\vec{G})_{1})+\bar{d}H(\check{G}|(\check{G})_{1})-H(G|(G)_{1})+D_{\mathrm{KL}}({\mathrm{Deg}}_{G}(o)|N_{d}),

where
G
,
\check{G}
and
\vec{G}
are the uniform labeling of
\dot{\nu},\check{\dot{\nu}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h-1})
and
\nu
respectively. Finally

is the multivariate Poisson random variable as above. Otherwise,
\nu
is not
d
-admissible and we set
\vec{\Sigma}_{d}^{0}(\nu,h)=\infty
.

Again, we conjecture that

if

is not
(\gamma,d)
-admissible. Here is our partial result in this direction. We say that

under

is well-behaved. Similarly,
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}_{h})
is well-behaved if for any

such that
D_{\mathrm{KL}}((G)_{k}|(G_{\gamma})_{k})<\infty
we also have
D_{\mathrm{KL}}((\check{G})_{k}|(\check{G}_{\gamma})_{k})<\infty
where
G
,
\check{G}
and
\vec{G}
are the uniform labeling of
\dot{\nu},\check{\dot{\nu}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h-1})
and
\nu

Lemma 2.3

(Infinite entropy)
.

For every integer number
h\geqslant 1
, if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
is either not
d
-admissible or well-behaved and not
(\gamma,d)
-admissible then

. Similarly, if
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\vec{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}_{h})
is either not
d
-admissible or well-behaved and not
(\gamma,d)
-admissible then
\vec{\Sigma}_{\gamma,d}(\nu,h)=\infty
.

Finally, we remark that the entropy is lower-semi-continuous but not continuous on
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
, even when restricted to admissible measures, see
[
23
, Proposition 5.14]
.

2.3.3
Entropy associated to a partially given marked degree distribution

We now extend the results of the previous paragraph to marked graphs on
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
with a given marked degree sequence on
\mathcal{B}
. Let
\pi
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathcal{B}}
and

with distribution

and

for all

and support of

The set of

\mathcal{G}_{n,D_{n}}=\{G=(V_{G},E_{G},\xi_{G}):V_{G}=V_{n},{\mathrm{Deg}}_{G}(v)(\cdot\times\mathbb{R}^{k})=D_{n}(v)\hbox{ for all }v\in V_{n}\}.

This is a subset of
\mathcal{G}_{n,{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}}
defined in (

) which is not empty for all

large enough.

We now define a random graph

on

is

-invariant, any deterministic choice of the ordering

or

We say that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
is
(\gamma,\pi)

(\gamma,d)
admissible and
{\mathrm{Deg}}^{0}(o)
has law
\pi
under

\Sigma_{\gamma,\pi}(\mu,h)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Sigma^{0}_{\pi}(\mu^{0},h)+\Sigma^{1}_{\gamma,d}(\mu,h)&\hbox{if $\mu$ is $(\gamma,\pi)$-admissible}\\
\infty&\hbox{\hbox{otherwise,}}\end{array}\right.

where

\Sigma^{0}_{\pi}(\mu,h)=-H(G|(G)_{1})+\frac{\bar{d}}{2}H(\vec{G}|(\vec{G})_{1}),

where
G
and
\vec{G}
are the uniform labeling of
\mu
and
\vec{\mu}
respectively. Similarly, the next theorem is the analog of Theorem

Theorem 2.4

(Entropy with a partially given marked degree sequence)
.

Let
\pi

\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathcal{B}}
with finite support and average

. For any integer number

,

n

There is also a LDP for

2.4
Entropy associated to marked Erdős-Rényi random graphs

We finally consider a model of random graphs with independent edges. We take
\mathcal{Z}=\mathbb{R}^{k}
without loss of generality for this model. Let
d_{n}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{n}=d>0.

We also consider a sequence of
*
-invariant probability measures
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on

such that for every Borel sets

\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{n}(A)=\gamma(A),

of the complete graph is present independently with probability

and

For

, we set

We have the following corollary of Theorem

. In the statement below

denotes the rate function which appears in Theorem

is reduced to a singleton).

.

For any integer number

,

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
with rate
n
and good rate function

\Sigma^{\tiny{\rm ER}}_{\gamma,d}(\mu,h)=\Sigma_{\gamma,\delta}(\mu,h)+j_{d}(\delta)

2.5
Global minimizer of the entropy

For discrete marks,
[
23
,
31
,
11
,
44
]
gave a precise description of the unique minimizer
\mu(p)
of the entropy given a
d
-admissible neighborhood

:

\inf\{\Sigma^{0}_{d}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{B})),\mu_{h}=p\}=\Sigma^{0}_{d}(\mu(p)).

That is, there is a unique and explicit measure
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{B}))
which minimizes the entropy given the law of its
h
-neighborhood. We will not extend these developments for our more general marked space
\mathbb{Z}=\mathcal{B}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
here. We will simply prove that there is a unique global minimizer that we describe now.

If
\pi
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathcal{B}}
with non-zero expectation
d=\mathbb{E}D=\sum_{k}k\pi(k)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathcal{B}}
such that

for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
. For
b\in\mathcal{B}
such that
d(b)>0
, we define the size-biased law
\hat{\pi}_{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathcal{B}}
by the formula

\hat{\pi}_{b}(k)=\frac{(k_{b^{*}}+1)\pi(k+1_{b^{*}})}{d(b)},

where
1_{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\mathcal{B}}
is the vector
1_{b}(c)=1\!\!{\sf I}(b=c)
. As in
[
23
, Section 4.4]
, the
unimodular Galton-Watson tree
with degree distribution
\pi
is the random
\mathcal{B}
-marked rooted tree
G^{0}
where the root has marked degree
{\mathrm{Deg}}(o)
with distribution
\pi
. Then, recursively and independently, any vertex
v\neq o
such that
\xi(u,v)=b
, where
u
is the parent of
v
, produces offspring according to the distribution
\hat{\pi}_{b}
. Finally, we denote by
{\mathrm{UGW}}(\gamma,\pi)
the law of the
\mathcal{Z}
-marked rooted tree
G=G^{0}_{\gamma}
where
G^{0}
is as above.

Lemma 2.6

(Minimizer of the entropy)
.

For any integer number
h\geqslant 1
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{h})
, we have
\Sigma_{\gamma,\pi}(\mu,h)=0
if and only if
\mu={\mathrm{UGW}}(\gamma,\pi)_{h}
. Similarly
\Sigma^{\tiny{\rm ER}}_{\gamma,d}(\mu,h)=0
if and only if
\mu={\mathrm{UGW}}(\gamma,\mathrm{Poi}(d))_{h}
, where
\mathrm{Poi}(d)
is the multivariate Poisson distribution with mean
d
.

Proof of Proposition

: the case

The statement on

\infty&\hbox{\hbox{otherwise,}}\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

Lemma 3.5

We start with the first claim and set

Therefore,

\frac{1}{d_{\nu}}=\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{\deg(o)}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\nu}(\deg(o)=k)}{k}=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(\deg(o)>0)}{\bar{d}}.

For the second claim, since

3.2
Proof of Theorem

In this subsection, we establish that

3.1
for finite mark sets.

Quantization of marks.

We first describe a natural quantization of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
into bins. Fix a mesh size
\delta>0
and a window size
\kappa>0
with
\kappa/\delta
integer. The quantization is a map
\{\cdot\}_{\delta}^{\kappa}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\left(\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[-\kappa,\kappa)\right)^{k}\cup\{\omega\}

. For

\{x\}^{\kappa}_{\delta}=\lfloor x/\delta\rfloor\delta

\{x\}^{\kappa}_{\delta}=-\{-x\}^{\kappa}_{\delta}
. On
\mathbb{R}^{k}

\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\}^{\kappa}_{\delta}=(\{x_{1}\}^{\kappa}_{\delta},\ldots,\{x_{d}\}^{\kappa}_{\delta})

\kappa/\delta
is not an integer, we set
\{x\}^{\kappa}_{\delta}=\{x\}^{\kappa^{\prime}}_{\delta}
with
\kappa^{\prime}=\delta\lceil\kappa/\delta\rceil
.

We fix a non-increasing function
\kappa:(0,1]\to(0,\infty)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\kappa(\delta)=\infty.

We set

L^{\delta}=\left(\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[-\kappa(\delta),\kappa(\delta))\right)^{k}\sqcup\{\omega\}\and\mathcal{Z}^{\delta}=\mathcal{B}\times L^{\delta}.

Note that the involution

If

B^{\delta}_{l}=[l_{1}+\delta)\times\cdots\times[l_{k}+\delta)

Entropy for networks

Random network with a given intensity

For integer number

Let

For example, if

If

Observe that in this case

We now state our result on the large deviation principle for the sequence of random networks

If

5.2

If

d(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\|f\|_{L}\leq 1,\|f\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq 1}\int fd(\mu-\nu)

\|f\|_{L}=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|}\,\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|f\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sup\left\{\sum_{k}|f(x_{k+1}-f(x_{k})|\right\}.

where the supremum is taken on increasing sequences

We discuss in the next lemma the continuity of the spectral measure of the eigenvalues of a self-adjoint matrix in terms of the local weak limit of its associated graph

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

1.

If the degrees and marks are uniformly bounded, namely

L_{\nu}
.

3.

We have that

\bullet
For the first point, it is enough to prove the convergence of moments. Because the degrees are uniformly bounded by

When the degrees or weights are unbounded, and more specifically when they do not have all the moments finite, it is not possible to argue by using the moments which may be unbounded and ill-defined. 7.1
Motivation

At first glance, the previous section concludes the proof of Corollary

. For all

then the additivity condition holds trivially since

(7.1)

since
\chi_{\Lambda}(\tau_{2})\geq 0
by definition of micro-state entropy. The problem, which is not related to LDP, is the ill-definiteness of the free product for random rooted-traffic distributions, when theyr are not the distribution of random rooted graphs. In general, a random rooted-traffic distribution in

This section hence introduce more sophisticated notions, new in the context of traffic probability, whose aim is to extend trivially the identity (

in greater generality. Hence this part is less relevant outside the perspectives of traffic probability. The problem is that seeing a random traffic as a traffic whose distribution is random is the classical sense

notions of dependence that have no analogue in classical probability.
For sparse Wigner matrices, traffic probability nevertheless indicates that amalgamation is an ingredient that cannot be bypassed for stating the additivity property of heavy traffic entropy, thanks to a result of independent interest stated in the next section.

7.2
Semicircularity over the diagonal of sparse Wigner matrices

Voiculescu’s central notion of entropy is not defined in terms of sparse Wigner matrices, but of independent GUE matrices. Such as, the analogue of the heavy traffic entropy is, for Voiculescu, the semicircular entropy. The fact that the sparse traffic entropy is related to freeness with amalgamation, a notion defined by Voiculescu more than 50 years old, may be justified heuristically by the following result.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a collection of independent sparse Wigner matrices as in (

converges to a semi-circular system over the diagonal, namely with amalgamation with respect to the operator
\Delta:A\mapsto\mathrm{Diag}_{i}(A_{i,i})

Proof.

We sketch the proof, which is in fact valid for the slightly more general of Ryan-Zakharevich Wigner matrices with exploding moments considered in
[
39
]
with variance profiles. In the context of
[
41
]
, asymptotically semi-circularity of a collection

of matrices means the convergence to the same limit of the complex numbers

\langle D_{0},X_{1}D_{1}\dots X_{L}D_{L}\rangle=o(1)+\langle D_{0},\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{NC}_{2}(L)}\kappa_{\pi}(X_{1}D_{1},\dots,X_{L}D_{L})\rangle,

The interest is that it gives a new characterization of the macroscopic asymptotic properties of heavy Wigner matrices, in particular a Pastur’s equation over the diagonal. Nevertheless the important consideration in the above proposition is that the matrices are not asymptotically semi-circular with respect to

In this section, we give a continuity result for the relative entropy with respect to our discretization procedure of the Euclidean space.

We recall the discretization map defined in the proof of Theorem

\delta>0
and a window size
\kappa>0
with

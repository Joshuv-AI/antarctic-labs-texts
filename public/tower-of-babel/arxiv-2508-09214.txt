Generation of High Order Harmonics in Vacuum for Various Configurations of Interacting Electromagnetic Field
P. V. Sasorov, S. V. Bulanov

Abstract
High order harmonic (HOH) generation by interacting extremely intense electromagnetic waves in the quantum vacuum is investigated within the framework of the Heisenberg-Euler formalism. We consider here the process in the lowest order of a perturbation theory relative to the electromagnetic (EM) beam intensity, giving contribution to the HOH generation. The main expressions are obtained for a general geometry, whyle polarizations of different sub-beams forming the EM beam focus are almost the same. Nevertheless, explicit expressions for the HOH generation are derived for the 4 ​ π 4\pi -dipole in-coming waves and for the two crossing Gaussian beams. The former geometry of the EM beam is optimal at a given EM wave power, whereas the latter one is more realistic from the experimental point of view. We consider also a relationship of our present general results with the results, obtained earlier for the HOH generation during of collision of two plane electromagnetic waves.

III
Perturbation approach for the HOH generation

III.1
Time dependent case

We consider in this paper a class of the problems that can be characterized as scattering problems. We assume that at
t\to-\infty
there are several electromagnetic waves that are focused almost simultaneously at for example
t\sim 0
at some spatial region of a typical size
L
. This asymptotic waves, which will be denoted below as
A^{in\,\mu}(\boldsymbol{x},t)
, behaves as
\propto 1/r
multiplied by a highly oscillating exponent, where
r
is the large distance from this region of the focus. Hence, they obey linear wave equation:

\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}\right)A^{in\,\mu}=0\,.

(22)

These waves are called usually as an in-coming waves.
When typical duration of these electromagnetic pulse is shorter than
L
, then we may set that the parameter
L
is defined by this typical duration.

Consider the parameter

\eta=\alpha\omega L|F|^{2}\,,

(23)

where
\omega
is a typical frequency of the in-coming waves, and
|F|
is a typical absolute value of
F^{ij}
in the focus region. If
\eta\ll 1
than generation of high order harmonics can be evaluated using a perturbation theory
Sa21
. Moreover the out-going high harmonic waves can be calculated using the following 1st-order approximation
Sa21
.

\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}\right)\boldsymbol{A}^{out}=4\pi\,\boldsymbol{j}[A^{in}]\,,

(24)

where

\boldsymbol{A}^{out}=0\mbox{~~~at~~~}t\to-\infty\,,

(25)

For the whole
\boldsymbol{A}^{out}
wave we should impose the condition
\boldsymbol{A}^{out}\to\boldsymbol{A}^{in}
at
t\to-\infty
. Eq. (
24
) is actually a linear wave equation with the external nonlinear current

\boldsymbol{j}^{out}=\boldsymbol{j}[A^{in}]=\left(\partial_{t}\frac{\partial\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{1}}{\partial\boldsymbol{E}}+\nabla\times\frac{\partial\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{1}}{\partial\boldsymbol{B}}\right)\Biggr|_{\boldsymbol{E}^{in}\,,\boldsymbol{B}^{in}}\,,

(26)

which depends only on the
\boldsymbol{A}^{in}
-field.
Thus, Eq. (
24
) is equally a linear equation with an independent ‘external’ current:

\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}\right)\boldsymbol{A}^{out}=4\pi\boldsymbol{j}^{out}\,.

(27)

Solutions of Eq. (
27
) at large distances from the focus (region of the beams interaction) in the direction
\boldsymbol{n}
, obeying the condition (
25
), can be expressed via Fourier transform

of the current

\boldsymbol{j}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{k}}^{out}=\int\boldsymbol{j}^{out}(\boldsymbol{x},t)e^{i\omega t-i\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}}\,d\boldsymbol{x}\,dt\,,

(28)

with
\boldsymbol{j}^{out}(\boldsymbol{x},t)
defined by Eq. (
26

r\gg L^{2}\omega

L
is typical size in the space-time localization of

close to the coordinate system origin:
\min\{|\boldsymbol{x}|,|t|\}\lesssim L

As a result, we have for the potential
\boldsymbol{A}^{out}
in the far zone (

)

(29)

and

Here,

H(n,\ell,k)={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}-}\int f^{n+k}\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in}\right)\,f^{\ell}\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in\,*}\right)\,\,f^{k}\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in\,*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in\,*}\right)f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}^{out\,*}\right)\,d\boldsymbol{x}\,,

(45)

K(n,\ell,k)={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}-}\int f^{n+k+1}\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in}\right)\,f^{\ell}\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in\,*}\right)\,f^{k}\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in\,*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in\,*}\right)f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in\,*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}^{out\,*}\right)\,d\boldsymbol{x}\,.

(46)

We use here the following shortcut:

f^{n}\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in}\right)=\left[f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in}\right)\right]^{n}\,.

The lowest power of the small parameter
F
in these sums originates from the term proportional to
H(n,0,0)
. However,
H(n,0,0)
vanishes exactly. To explain this fact we may present
H(n,0,0)
in the Fourier transform. Then the contribution to the integral (

) comes only from the hyper-surface in the
6n+3
-dimensional
\boldsymbol{k}
-space, where

\boldsymbol{k}_{\Omega}^{out}=\sum_{m=1}^{2n+1}\boldsymbol{k}_{m}
, whereas
|\boldsymbol{k}_{\Omega}^{out}|=\Omega
and
|\boldsymbol{k}_{m}|=\omega

for any

and
\boldsymbol{k}_{m}
in accordance to the definition (

). This canceling effect was considered also in Ref.

but for the case of

did not take into account this exact canceling. They calculated

with an approximate procedure. This led to erroneous generation of the 3rd harmonics at a lower degree of the power of their
in
-coming wave and, hence, to considerable overestimation of the effect.

The next terms, giving lowest order of the small parameter,
F
, come from
H(n,1,0)
and
K(n,0,0)
. We will show below that these values,

\boldsymbol{e}_{\Omega,\,\boldsymbol{n}}\,\bar{\boldsymbol{j}}_{\Omega,\boldsymbol{n}\Omega}^{out}=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}b_{n+2}(n+2)(n+1)H_{n}

+\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}b_{n+2}(n+2)K_{n}\,,

(47)

where

H_{n}=H(n,1,0)

={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}-}\int f^{n}\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in}\right)\,f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in\,*}\right)\,f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}^{out\,*}\right)\,d\boldsymbol{x}\,,

(48)

K_{n}=K(n,0,0)

={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}-}\int f^{n+1}\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in}\right)\,f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{in\,*},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}^{out\,*}\right)\,d\boldsymbol{x}\,.

We calculate below typical contributions to

and
K_{n}
for several typical forms of in-coming waves
\boldsymbol{A}^{in}
to demonstrate that they do not vanish in general case. As a result, we may say that the combination of expressions (

d\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\alpha\frac{m_{e}^{4}\omega^{2}}{32\pi^{3}}m^{2}(m+3)^{2}b_{(m+3)/2}^{2}\left|(m+1)H_{(m-1)/2}+K_{(m-1)/2}\right|^{2}\,d\Phi\,.

We should emphasize that in the case of two perfectly plane waves (of finite duration), considered in Ref.

V
Two crossing paraxial EM beams

We extend here results of Sec.
IV
to a more realistic structure of the in-coming wave. We consider here two identical Gaussian focused beams with almost parallel polarizations but intersecting at a right angle. We show that we are forced to take into account subleading corrections to pure paraxial approximation for field structure in the Gaussian beams. For this reason we start from investigation of a single beam structure.

V.1
3D Gaussian beam

We start here from the following representation of any exact monochromatic solutions of the linear Maxwell equations in a vacuum.

\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\delta(|\boldsymbol{k}|-\omega)\,\boldsymbol{a}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\,e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{x}}\,d\boldsymbol{k}\,.

(65)

Here,
\omega
is frequency of the monochromatic wave,
\boldsymbol{a}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
is tangent to the sphere
|\boldsymbol{k}|=\omega
in the
\boldsymbol{k}
-space. This vector potential is written assuming the transverse gauge,
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{A}=0
. Any choice of the gauge fixing is allowed for the calculation of
\bar{\boldsymbol{j}}_{\Omega;\,\boldsymbol{n}\Omega}
because of its gauge invariance.

Setting
\boldsymbol{a}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\parallel(\boldsymbol{e}_{z}\times\boldsymbol{n})
, where
\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{k}/|\boldsymbol{k}|
, we may specify
\boldsymbol{a}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
by the following way:

\boldsymbol{a}_{\boldsymbol{k}}=A_{0}\frac{W^{2}}{2\pi}\left[\boldsymbol{e}_{y}\cos\zeta\cos\varphi-\boldsymbol{e}_{x}\cos\zeta\sin\varphi\right]

\times\exp\left[-\frac{W^{2}}{2}\left(\cos^{2}\zeta\sin^{2}\varphi+\sin^{2}\zeta\right)\right]\,,

(66)

where
(k,\theta=\pi/2-\zeta,\varphi)
are spherical coordinates in the
\boldsymbol{k}
-space, and the dimensionless parameter
W
is a positive constant. It describes a degree of the beam converging, and defines the waist,
w_{e}=W/\omega
, of the Gaussian beam, when
W\gg 1
. It will be considered below in more details. The exponent multiplier at
W\gg 1
provides a Gaussian structure of the beam. An arbitrary for the linear wave factor in front of the expression (
66

\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}(\boldsymbol{x})=A_{0}\frac{W^{2}}{2\pi}\int d\varphi\,\cos\zeta\,d\zeta

\times\left[\boldsymbol{e}_{y}\cos\zeta\cos\varphi-\boldsymbol{e}_{x}\cos\zeta\sin\varphi\right]

\times\exp\Bigl[i\omega\left(x\cos\zeta\cos\varphi+y\cos\zeta\sin\varphi+z\sin\zeta\right)

-W^{2}\left(\cos^{2}\zeta\sin^{2}\varphi+\sin^{2}\zeta\right)/2\Bigr]\,.

(67)

This expression gives an exact representation of the in-coming field that can be considered at
W\to\infty
as a model for the 3d Gaussian beam, propagating close to the
x
-axis in its positive direction. The constant complex amplitude
A_{0}
describes an intensity and a phase of the wave. The expansion of Eq. (
67

C

La75
;
Da79

67

we can present the Gaussian beam in the following form:

\left(\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\right)_{y}=A_{0}\,e^{i\omega x}\,G\,\left[1+R_{1}+\dots\right]\,,

(68)

\left(\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\right)_{x}=A_{0}\,e^{i\omega x}\,G\,\left[R_{2}+\dots\right]\,,

(69)

\left(\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\right)_{z}=0\,.

(70)

Here,

G=\frac{W^{2}}{W^{2}+i\omega x}\exp\left[-\frac{\omega^{2}(y^{2}+z^{2})}{2\left(W^{2}+i\omega x\right)}\right]\,.

(71)

The dimensionless parameter of the beam
W
can be expressed through the waist,
w_{e}
, of the beam as:

W^{2}=w_{e}^{2}\omega^{2}=4\pi^{2}w_{e}^{2}/\lambda^{2}
. Thus, the radius

at

and
R_{2}
are of the order of
{\cal O}(W^{-2})
and
{\cal O}(W^{-1})
, respectively, in the region, where
\omega\sqrt{y^{2}+z^{2}}\sim\mathcal{O}(W)
and
\omega|x|\sim\mathcal{O}(W^{2})
at
W\to\infty
.
Explicit expressions for
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
can be found in App.
C
.
See Eqs. (
130
) and (
132
). The beam (
68
)-(
71

W\gg 1
. Typical length of the waist in this limit is
W
times larger than its typical width,
w_{e}
.
Setting
R_{1}=R_{2}=0
at

;
Da79
.
Full time-dependent wave is defined as previously:
\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}=e^{-i\omega t}\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}

(130)

\boldsymbol{e}_{x}\cdot\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)=\frac{W^{2}}{W^{2}+i\omega x}\exp\left[i\omega x-\frac{\omega^{2}(y^{2}+z^{2})}{2\left(W^{2}+i\omega x\right)}\right]\,\left[R_{2}+\dots\right]\,,

(131)

where

R_{2}=-\frac{iy\omega}{2\left(W^{2}+i\omega x\right)}\,.

(132)

Another polarization of the beam can be obtained by the substitution
\boldsymbol{E}\leftrightarrow-\boldsymbol{B}

\boldsymbol{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\times\boldsymbol{a}_{\boldsymbol{k}}

(133)

C.2
Calculation of
f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1}\right)
at
|\boldsymbol{x}|\omega\sim W
in the limit

W\to\infty

To obtain leading terms of
H_{n}
and
K_{n}
at
W\to\infty

f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1}\right)
at the same limit for the space domain
|\boldsymbol{x}|\omega\sim W
. To do this we represent the expressions (
130
) and (
131
) in the following form:

\left(\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1}\right)_{y}\approx e^{ix}G\left[1+R_{1}\right]\,,

(137)

and

\left(\bar{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1}\right)_{x}\approx e^{ix}GR_{2}\,.

(138)

Here

G=\frac{W^{2}}{W^{2}+ix}\exp\left[-\frac{(y^{2}+z^{2})}{2\left(W^{2}+ix\right)}\right]\,,

R_{2}=-\frac{iy}{2(W^{2}+ix)}\,,

and
R_{1}\sim{\cal O}\left(W^{-2}\right)
is determined by the rest of the expression (
130
). We set here temporally
A_{0}=\omega=1

Then

\bar{B}_{z}=\partial_{x}A_{y}-\partial_{y}A_{x}

\approx ie^{ix}G\left[1+R_{1}\right]-ie^{ix}GW^{-2}+\frac{i}{2}e^{ix}G\frac{(y^{2}+z^{2})}{W^{4}}

\approx ie^{ix}G\left[1+R_{1}-i\frac{2W^{2}-y^{2}-z^{2}}{2W^{4}}\right]\,,

(139)

\bar{B}_{x}=-\partial_{z}A_{y}\approx+\frac{z}{W^{2}}e^{ix}G\,,

(140)

\bar{B}_{y}=\partial_{z}A_{x}\approx 0\,,

(141)

\bar{E}_{x}=ie^{ix}GR_{2}\,,\quad\bar{E_{z}}=0\,,

(142)

\bar{E}_{y}\approx ie^{ix}G\left[1+R_{1}\right]\,.

(143)

Thus, calculation of
f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1}\right)
at this limit gives

f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1}\right)=\left(-\tilde{E}_{x}^{2}-\tilde{E}_{y}^{2}+\tilde{B}_{x}^{2}+\tilde{B}_{y}^{2}\right)/2\approx

\frac{1}{2}e^{2i(x-t)}G^{2}\Biggl[R_{2}^{2}+1+2R_{1}-1-2R_{1}

+2i\frac{2W^{2}-y^{2}-z^{2}}{2W^{4}}-\frac{z^{2}}{W^{4}}\Biggr]

\approx\frac{1}{2}e^{2i(x-t)}G^{2}\Biggl[2i\frac{2W^{2}-y^{2}-z^{2}}{2W^{4}}-\frac{z^{2}+y^{2}/4}{W^{4}}\Biggr]

\approx\frac{e^{2i(x-t)}}{2W^{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{y^{2}+z^{2}}{W^{2}}\right]

\times\left[2i-(1+i)\frac{y^{2}+z^{2}}{W^{2}}+\frac{3y^{2}}{4W^{2}}\right]\,.

(144)

Recovering
\omega
and
A_{0}
we obtain:

f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1}\right)=A_{0}^{2}\frac{e^{2i\omega(x-t)}}{2w_{e}^{2}}\exp\left[-\frac{(y^{2}+z^{2})}{w_{e}^{2}}\right]

\times\left[2i-(1+i)\frac{y^{2}+z^{2}}{w_{e}^{2}}+\frac{3y^{2}}{4w_{e}^{2}}\dots\right]\,.

(145)

C.3
Calculation of
f(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1},\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}^{out\,*})
at
|\boldsymbol{x}|\omega=\mathcal{O}(W)

\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{x}=1-\mathcal{O}\left(W^{-2}\right)

(146)

\boldsymbol{n}=(\cos\Theta,\,\sin\Theta\,\cos{\phi},\,\sin\Theta\,\sin{\phi})

(147)

is the direction of the HOH propagation, and

\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\boldsymbol{n}\times\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{n}}^{\prime}

\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{n}}^{\prime}=\frac{\boldsymbol{n}\times\boldsymbol{e}_{y}}{|\boldsymbol{n}\times\boldsymbol{e}_{y}|}=\frac{(-\sin\Theta\,\sin\phi,\,0,\,\cos\Theta)}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}\Theta+\sin^{2}\Theta\sin^{2}\phi}}\,.

\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{n}}

=\frac{(\sin\Theta\cos\Theta\cos\phi,\sin^{2}\Theta\cos^{2}\phi-1,\sin^{2}\Theta\cos\phi\sin\phi)}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}\Theta+\sin^{2}\Theta\sin^{2}\phi}}.

We will see that the matrix elements are not exponentially small only when

. In this case, the rate emission of the harmonic with perpendicular polarization,

\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{n}}^{\prime}
, will contain an additional small factor
\sim W^{-2}
in comparison with the
\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{n}}
polarization.

For the fields component of this wave we have:

\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}}^{out}=i\Omega\,\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\times\boldsymbol{n}\,e^{i\Omega\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x}}=i\Omega\,\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{n}}^{\prime}\,e^{i\Omega\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x}}\,.

\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}}^{out}=i\Omega\,\boldsymbol{e}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\,e^{i\Omega\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x}}\,.

\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}}^{out}\right)_{x}\approx-i\Omega\,\Theta\sin\phi\,e^{i\Omega(\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x}-t)}\,,

\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}}^{out}\right)_{y}=0\,,

\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}}^{out}\right)_{y}\approx i\Omega\,(\Theta^{2}\cos^{2}\phi-1)\,e^{i\Omega(\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x}-t)}\,,

\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}}^{out}\right)_{z}\approx 0\,.

f\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}}^{out\,*}\right)=\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{B}}_{1}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\cal B}}^{out\,*}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}_{1}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\cal E}}^{out\,*}\right)/2\approx

\approx i\Omega\Theta\sin\phi\frac{z}{2W^{2}}Ge^{i(x-t)+i\Omega(t-\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x})}

+\frac{\Omega}{2}G\left[1+R_{1}-i\frac{2W^{2}-y^{2}-z^{2}}{2W^{4}}\right]e^{i(x-t)+i\Omega(t-\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x})}

+\frac{\Omega}{2}\Theta\cos\phi\,GR_{2}e^{i(x-t)+i\Omega(t-\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x})}

+\frac{\Omega}{2}G\left[1+R_{1}\right](\Theta^{2}\cos^{2}\phi-1)e^{i(x-t)+i\Omega(t-\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x})}

\approx\frac{\Omega}{2}G\,e^{i(x-t)+i\Omega(t-\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x})}\Biggl\{\frac{z\Theta\sin\phi}{W^{2}}-\frac{iy\Theta\cos\phi}{2W^{2}}

+\left[1+R_{1}-i\frac{2W^{2}-y^{2}-z^{2}}{2W^{4}}-1-R_{1}+\Theta^{2}\cos^{2}\phi\right]\Biggr\}

=\frac{\Omega}{2}\exp\left[-\frac{(y^{2}+z^{2})}{2W^{2}}\right]\,e^{i(x-t)+i\Omega(t-\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x})}

\times\biggl[\frac{z\Theta\sin\phi}{W^{2}}-\frac{iy\Theta\cos\phi}{2W^{2}}

-i\frac{2W^{2}-y^{2}-z^{2}}{2W^{4}}+\Theta^{2}\cos^{2}\phi\biggr]\,.

(148)

Recovering
\omega
and
A_{0}
we obtain:

\times e^{i\omega(x-t)+i\Omega(t-\boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{x})}\biggl[\left(\frac{z\sin\phi}{w_{e}}-\frac{iy\cos\phi}{2w_{e}}\right)\Theta W

-i\frac{2w_{e}^{2}-y^{2}-z^{2}}{2w_{e}^{2}}+(\Theta W)^{2}\cos^{2}\phi\biggr]\,.

(149)

It is interesting that the 1st post-paraxial correction
R_{1}
is canceled in the expressions for both

and
f(\tilde{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\cal A}}^{out\,*})
, whereas the analogous correction
R_{2}
, entering as

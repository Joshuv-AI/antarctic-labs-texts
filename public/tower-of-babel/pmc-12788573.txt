Influence of Systemic Vitamin D Levels on Clinical Periodontal Parameters in Patients with Chronic Periodontitis
Authors: Radhika Tamloorker, R. Anil Kumar, Shweta Patnaik, Pavithra Durgesh, Prerna Kataria, Yugal Kishore
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Vitamin D plays a pivotal role in immune regulation and bone metabolism, both critical in the pathogenesis of periodontitis. However, the direct influence of systemic Vitamin D levels on periodontal health remains underexplored.Materials and Methods:This cross-sectional study included 90 patients aged 30-60 years with chronic periodontitis. Participants were categorized based on serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D levels into three groups: Deficient (<20 ng/mL), Insufficient (20-30 ng/mL), and Sufficient (>30 ng/mL). Clinical parameters including Plaque Index (PI), Gingival Index (GI), Probing Pocket Depth (PPD), and Clinical Attachment Level (CAL) were recorded. Serum Vitamin D levels were measured using chemiluminescent immunoassay. Statistical analysis included ANOVA and Pearson correlation.Results:Among the participants, 38.9% were Vitamin D deficient, 35.6% insufficient, and 25.5% sufficient. Mean PPD and CAL were significantly higher in the deficient group (PPD: 4.26 ± 0.61 mm, CAL: 4.81 ± 0.77 mm) compared to the sufficient group (PPD: 3.42 ± 0.48 mm, CAL: 3.91 ± 0.55 mm; P < 0.01). Negative correlations were observed between Vitamin D levels and PPD (r = –0.49, P < 0.001), and CAL (r = –0.52, P < 0.001).Conclusion:Lower systemic Vitamin D levels are associated with worse clinical periodontal parameters, suggesting its contributory role in periodontal disease progression. Monitoring and correction of Vitamin D deficiency may be beneficial as part of periodontal therapy.

INTRODUCTION

Chronic periodontitis is a multifactorial inflammatory disease characterized by progressive destruction of the supporting tissues of teeth, ultimately leading to tooth loss if untreated. It is driven by dysbiotic microbial biofilms and modulated by the host immune-inflammatory response.[1] Systemic health parameters, including nutritional and endocrine factors, have garnered increasing attention for their role in modulating periodontal disease susceptibility and progression.[2]

Vitamin D, a secosteroid hormone, is integral not only to calcium homeostasis and bone metabolism but also to immune modulation, particularly through its effects on monocytes/macrophages and T-cell function.[3] Hypovitaminosis D is widespread, particularly in developing nations, and has been linked to a heightened pro-inflammatory state.[4]

Recent studies suggest a potential association between Vitamin D deficiency and periodontal disease severity, owing to its roles in maintaining alveolar bone integrity and regulating immune responses.[56] However, the evidence remains inconclusive due to variations in study designs, populations, and methods of assessing both periodontal and Vitamin D status.

Biochemical assessment

Fasting venous blood samples were collected to assess serum 25(OH)D levels using chemiluminescent immunoassay (CLIA). Levels were classified as per Endocrine Society guidelines.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0. Descriptive statistics were used for means and standard deviations. One-way ANOVA and post hoc Tukey test assessed group differences. Pearson’s correlation analyzed the association between Vitamin D levels and periodontal parameters. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Demographic distribution

The mean age of participants was 45.6 ± 8.2 years. Among them, 35 (38.9%) were Vitamin D deficient, 32 (35.6%) insufficient, and 23 (25.5%) sufficient. Gender distribution was comparable across groups (P > 0.05).

DISCUSSION

The present study evaluated the influence of systemic Vitamin D status on clinical periodontal parameters in patients with chronic periodontitis. Our findings revealed that lower Vitamin D levels were significantly associated with deeper probing depths and greater attachment loss.

This observation is consistent with findings by Liu et al.,[7] who demonstrated that Vitamin D deficiency was linked to more severe periodontal destruction in a cohort of Chinese adults. Similarly, Dietrich et al.[8] reported that serum 25(OH)D levels were inversely related to periodontal disease severity in a representative U.S. population.

Vitamin D exerts anti-inflammatory effects by downregulating pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α, and it enhances antimicrobial peptide expression like cathelicidin, which may help in reducing the microbial burden in periodontal pockets.[910] Its role in maintaining alveolar bone through regulation of RANKL and osteoprotegerin balance may also explain the greater clinical attachment loss observed in deficient individuals.[11]

While plaque and gingival indices were higher in the deficient group, the differences were not statistically significant. This may reflect the confounding role of oral hygiene behavior and not just systemic Vitamin D status in determining gingival inflammation.

A systematic review by Penckofer et al.[12] emphasized the bidirectional relationship between nutritional deficiencies and chronic inflammation, advocating for nutritional assessments as part of comprehensive periodontal care. In India, where Vitamin D deficiency is highly prevalent even among urban populations, this consideration becomes especially relevant.[13]

Nonetheless, our study has limitations. The cross-sectional design limits causal inference, and confounders such as sun exposure and dietary intake were not quantified. Longitudinal studies and interventional trials evaluating the impact of Vitamin D supplementation on periodontal outcomes are warranted.

CONCLUSION

This study demonstrates a significant association between low systemic Vitamin D levels and worse periodontal parameters, particularly probing pocket depth and clinical attachment level. These findings highlight the importance of assessing Vitamin D status in chronic periodontitis patients and suggest a potential role for Vitamin D supplementation as an adjunct in periodontal therapy.

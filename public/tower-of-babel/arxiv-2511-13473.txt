Kähler-Ricci flows coming out of Metric spaces
Alix Deruelle, Vincent Guedj, Henri Guenancia, Ahmed Zeriahi

Abstract
Given a compact Kähler manifold X X and a closed, positive ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) -current T T on X X , we find sufficient conditions for T T to induce a metric structure ( X , d T ) (X,d_{T}) which is the Gromov-Hausdorff limit of compact Kähler manifolds either in a "static" way or at time zero of smooth Kähler-Ricci flows. In dimension 1 1 we extend works of T. Richard and M. Simon, showing that any oriented compact Alexandrov surface with bounded integral curvature and without cusp is the initial datum of a Kähler-Ricci flow.



1
Introduction

1.1
Overview

A (noncollapsed) Ricci limit space of dimension
n

is a compact metric space which is the Gromov-Hausdorff
limit of non-collapsing
Riemannian manifolds
(M_{i},g_{i})
of dimension
n

with uniformly bounded Ricci curvature,

{\rm Ric}(g_{i})\geq-K\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;{\rm Vol}\,B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},1)\geq v_{0}>0.

A long standing conjecture attributed to Anderson, Cheeger, Colding and Tian (see
[
4
]
)
asserts that a Ricci limit space is homeomorphic to a smooth manifold outside a singular set
of Hausdorff dimension
n-4
. This conjecture has been settled
recently by Simon and Topping
[
37
,
36
]
in dimension
3

(we shall discuss in some detail the
2
-dimensional case in section
5.3
,
following the work of Richard
[
35
]
), by
running smooth Ricci flows from these Ricci limit spaces. See also
[
33
]
for an update on this conjecture.

It is a natural problem to try and characterize those metric spaces that
can arise as initial data of smooth Ricci flows, where
the convergence at time zero should hold in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense.
We study in this article the Kähler version of this problem: we consider

Kähler Ricci limit spaces
(the approximating manifolds are assumed
to be
compact Kähler
),
and take advantage of the strong analytic rigidity properties of
such families (see
[
13
,
14
,
31
]
) to relax the Ricci lower bound.

Motivated by the previous discussion, let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
,
and let
T
be a positive closed current cohomologous to
\omega_{X}
.
It follows from the
\partial\overline{\partial}
-lemma that
T=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi

for some
quasi-plurisubharmonic
(qpsh) function
\varphi
. Recall that a function
is qpsh if it is locally the sum of a smooth and a plurisubharmonic function.
Here
d^{c}=\frac{1}{2i\pi}(\partial-\overline{\partial})

so that
dd^{c}=\frac{i}{\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}
.
We assume in this article that
\varphi

is
continuous
, so that the Monge-Ampère measure
T^{n}
is well-defined in the weak
sense of currents (see
[
21
]
). We moreover assume that

T^{n}=f\omega_{X}^{n}
is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure,
with
f=e^{\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}}
for some qpsh functions
\psi_{\pm}
,
with
e^{-\psi_{-}}\in L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})
.

We first address the following general problem:

Question 1
.

Under which sufficient conditions does the current
T
define a natural (semi)distance
d_{T}
on
X
,
and what are the geometric properties of
d_{T}
?

Observe that we have not defined what the semi-distance
d_{T}
induced by
T
is yet and we believe that such a definition is part of the difficulty to answer Question
1
. Indeed, defining
d_{T}(x,y)
for two points
x
and
y
in
X
as the infimum
of the lengths induced by
T
of Lipschitz curves joining
x
to
y
seems too naive to even make sense in general.
Nonetheless, we are able to answer Question
1
both in complex dimension
n=1
in full generality and in the setting of
geometric
isolated singularities (see Definition
1.1
) in complex dimensions
n\geq 2
.
The proofs are independent of the Kähler-Ricci flow.

It has been shown in
[
22
,
12
]
that there exists a unique Kähler-Ricci flow
on
X
with
T
as initial condition, i.e. a family of
Kähler forms
(\omega_{t})_{t\in(0,T_{max})}
, such that

\frac{\partial\omega_{t}}{\partial t}=-{\rm Ric}(\omega_{t})\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\omega_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\rightarrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}T.

The maximal existence time only depends on the cohomology class of the initial datum,

T_{\rm max}:=\sup\{t>0,\;\{\omega_{X}\}-tc_{1}(X)\text{ is K\"{a}hler }\}.\\

The convergence at time zero holds in the weak sense of currents. A second aspect of this article is to reinforce the latter,
when the initial datum induces a metric space.
We try and establish Gromov-Hausdorff convergence at time zero whenever possible.

To slightly simplify the analysis, we actually consider the following twisted version

(1.1)

\frac{\partial\omega_{t}}{\partial t}=-{\rm Ric}(\omega_{t})+{\rm Ric}(\omega_{X})\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\omega_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\rightarrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}T.

In this case
T_{\rm max}=+\infty
and the results
of
[
22
,
12
]
apply.
Similar to the discussion on the current
T
above, it holds that
\omega_{t}=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}
and
the twisted Kähler-Ricci flow equation
is equivalent to the scalar parabolic complex Monge-Ampère equation

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{X}^{n}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\varphi_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\rightarrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varphi.

We then address the following second general problem:

Question 2
.

Under which sufficient conditions on
T
does the Kähler-Ricci flow
(X,\omega_{t})
Gromov-Hausdorff converge to
(X,d_{T})
as
t\rightarrow 0
?

We are able to answer Question
2
both in complex dimension
n=1
in full generality and in the setting of
geometric
isolated singularities
in
complex dimensions
n\geq 2
.

1.2
Main results

Our first result analyses the definition and properties of the semi-distance induced by

T
on
X
,
when
T
has
geometric singularities
:

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a positive closed
(1,1)
-current
T=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi
has geometric singularities if
there are qpsh functions
\psi_{\pm}
on
X

such that

\bullet

the qpsh function
\varphi
is continuous and
T^{n}=e^{\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}}\omega_{X}^{n}
with
e^{-\psi_{-}}\in L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})
;

\bullet

the functions
\psi_{\pm}
are smooth in some dense Zariski open set
\Omega\subset X
.

Currents with geometric singularities are ubiquituous in complex geometry. Many canonical currents,
e.g. singular Kähler-Einstein metrics
[
15
]
or
limits of the Kähler-Ricci flow approach to the Minimal Model Program
[
39
]

are of this type.
We then set
Z=X\setminus\Omega
and define the semi-distance induced by
T

as follows:

d_{T}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\ell_{T}(\gamma),\;\gamma\,\text{Lipschitz path from }x\text{ to }y\,\,\mbox{s.t.}\,\,\gamma^{-1}(Z)\,\mbox{is finite}\right\}.

Theorem A
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
T
be a current
with geometric singularities. Then the following holds:

(i)

d_{T}
is a semi-distance and it is the unique continuous extension to
X\times X
of the geodesic distance
induced by
T
on
\Omega\times\Omega
;

(ii)

There exists
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
d_{T}\leq Cd_{X}^{\alpha}
. In particular
d_{T}
is finite;

(iii)

If
\psi_{+}
has isolated analytic singularities, then
d_{T}
is bi-Hölder equivalent to
d_{X}
.

In the proof of Theorem
A
, it is further shown that if
\psi_{+}
has isolated singularities that are not necessarily analytic then
d_{T}
is a distance. In particular
(X,d_{T})
is isometric to the metric completion of the metric space associated to the Kähler manifold
(\Omega,T|_{\Omega})
, see Proposition
4.6
.

As a combination of Theorem
A
and the results of
[
5
]
,
[
31
]
and
[
6
]
, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary B
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
T
be a current
with geometric singularities. If
\psi_{+}=0
then
(X,d_{T})
is a Gromov-Hausdorff limit of Kähler manifolds with uniform Ricci lower bound.

See Theorem
4.9
for a more formal statement of Corollary
B
including the dimension bound
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(Z,d_{T})\leq 2n-2
. Let us add that the results of
[
8
]
combined with Theorem
A
(see Proposition
4.6
) show that
(X,d_{T},T^{n})
is a non-collapsed
\mathrm{RCD}(2n,\lambda)
space for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In turn, this enables to recover the dimension bound on
Z
by appealing to
[
41
]
.

Our second series of results concern the metric behavior of solutions to the twisted Kähler-Ricci flow (
1.1
) with initial condition
T
.
Let
d_{t}
denote the Riemannian distance defined by the Kähler forms
\omega_{t}
.
The Ricci curvature of
\omega_{t}
is the differential form

{\rm Ric}(\omega_{t})={\rm Ric}(\omega_{X})-dd^{c}\log\left(\omega_{t}^{n}/\omega_{X}^{n}\right)={\rm Ric}(\omega_{X})-dd^{c}\dot{\varphi_{t}},

while
{\rm Ric}(T):={\rm Ric}(\omega_{X})-dd^{c}(\psi_{+}-\psi_{-})
is
a closed bidegree
(1,1)
-current of order zero
(difference of positive closed currents).

Theorem C
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold. Let
T=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi
be a positive current such that
\varphi
is continuous, and
T^{n}=e^{\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}}\omega_{X}^{n}
where
\psi_{\pm}
are qpsh functions
with
e^{-\psi_{-}}\in L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})
.
Let
(X,\omega_{t})_{t>0}
be the solution to the twisted Kähler-Ricci flow (
1.1
).

(i)

The Ricci curvatures
{\rm Ric}(\omega_{t})
weakly converge towards
{\rm Ric}(T)
as
t\rightarrow 0
.

(ii)

If
T
has geometric singularities and
\psi_{\pm}
have isolated singularities,
then the distances
d_{t}
uniformly converge, as
t\rightarrow 0
, towards
d_{T}
.
In particular the compact metrics spaces
(X,d_{t})
converge in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense
to
(X,d_{T})
as
t\rightarrow 0
.

In the course of the proof of Theorem
C
,
we provide crucial a priori
C^{2}
estimates on the Kähler potential
\varphi_{t}
together with an
L^{\infty}
bound on
\dot{\varphi}_{t}
which have their own interest.

Theorem
C
gives satisfactory existence results for smoothing Kähler-Ricci flows
coming out of Kähler-Ricci limit spaces. It would be desirable to better understand uniqueness
properties of these flows (see
[
42
]
for some uniqueness results when
n=1
).

Our last results concern Alexandrov surfaces. More precisely, when the complex dimension
n
is equal to
1
, we provide a thorough answer to Questions
1
and
2
through very precise estimates. If
\psi_{\pm}
are quasi-subharmonic functions on a compact Riemann surface
S
, we let

\nu_{\pm}:=\sup\{\nu(\psi_{\pm},x),\;x\in S\}

denote the maximal value of Lelong numbers of the functions
\psi_{\pm}
.
Let us stress that the integrability property
e^{-\psi_{-}}\in L^{1}
is equivalent to the upper bound
\nu_{-}<2
.

Theorem D
.

Let
(S,\omega_{S})
be a compact Riemann surface with induced distance
d_{S}
. Let
T=e^{\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}}\omega_{S}
be a current such that
\psi_{\pm}
are qsh functions on
S
with
e^{-\psi_{-}}\in L^{1}(\omega_{S})
.
Then

\bullet

d_{T}
is a well-defined distance which is bi-Hölder equivalent to
d_{S}
,

C^{-1}\,d_{S}^{1+\alpha_{+}}\leq d_{T}\leq C\,d_{S}^{1-\alpha_{-}},

where
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
\alpha_{\pm}:=\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{\pm}+\varepsilon)
and
C=C(\omega_{S},\psi_{\pm},\varepsilon)>0
.

\bullet

(S,d_{T})
is an oriented compact Alexandrov surface with bounded integral curvature
and without cusp, it is the initial datum of a Kähler-Ricci flow.

We refer the reader to Theorems
4.2
,
5.7
, and
5.10

for more complete results. Theorem
D
extends the main results of Richard
[
35
]
and Simon
[
37
]
,
who considered Alexandrov surfaces whose curvature is uniformly bounded below
(case
\psi_{+}\equiv 0
) to the case of bounded integral curvature with no cusp
(see Definition
5.6
).

1.3
Outline of the paper

In Section
2
we recall the basics of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions, as well as the background material on Monge-Ampère measures induced by a positive closed current
T
whose density is
L^{p}
integrable for
p>1
, condition that we require throughout the text. Diameter bounds we need in the proof of our results are then stated in Section
2.1.3
.
Section
2.2
covers the basic properties of the twisted Kähler-Ricci flow (
1.1
) starting from a positive closed current
T
which is cohomologous to a fixed background Kähler metric
\omega_{X}
,
and has continuous Kähler potential.

Section
3
establishes
uniform a priori estimates along the
flow (
1.1
) which we believe have their own interest (see Propositions
3.1
-
3.9
, Lemma
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
):

\bullet

there exists
C_{0}>0
such that
\|\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\leq C_{0}
for all
t>0
;

\bullet

there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\psi_{+}(x)-C_{1}\leq\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)\leq-\psi_{-}(x)+C_{1}
for all
(t,x)
;

\bullet

there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

C_{2}^{-1}e^{\psi_{+}}\omega_{X}\leq\omega_{t}\leq C_{2}e^{-\psi_{-}}\omega_{X}
on
X\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
.

When
\psi_{+}=0
and
\psi_{-}
has analytic singularities, we also obtain fine higher order estimates:
for each
k\geq 0
, there exists
B_{k},\gamma_{k}>0
such that

\bullet

|\nabla^{g(t)}\dot{\varphi_{t}}|_{g(t)}\leq B_{0}e^{-\gamma_{0}\psi_{-}+B_{0}t}
,

\bullet

|\nabla^{g(t)}(\omega_{t}-\omega_{X})|_{g(t)}\leq\frac{B_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}e^{-\gamma_{0}\psi_{-}+B_{0}t}
, and

\bullet

|\nabla^{g(t),\,k}{\rm Rm}(g(t))|_{g(t)}\leq\frac{C_{k}}{t^{1+\frac{k}{2}}}e^{-\gamma_{k}\psi_{-}+C_{k}t}
,

as we explain in Propositions
3.12
and
3.15
.

The content of Section
4
takes on a more geometric flavour with the proof of Theorem
D
in Section
4.1
: see the crucial Proposition
4.3
which establishes quantitative integrability properties of subharmonic functions with sufficiently small Lelong numbers in the plane along (real) curves. We then move to higher dimensional Kähler manifolds: the statement and proof of Theorem
A
are made precise in Section
4.2
through Proposition
4.6
. The last Section
4.3
combines Theorem
A
with Cheeger-Colding theory (and its latest developments
[
6
]
,
[
31
]
) to give a proof of Corollary
B
, see Theorem
4.9
for its reformulation in the body of the text.

The final Section
5
combines the two previous sections and studies whether the compact metric spaces

(X,d_{t})
, where
d_{t}
denotes the induced distance by the Kähler form
\omega_{t}
solution to (
1.1
), converge, in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology, to the metric space
(X,d_{T})

as
t\rightarrow 0
: Proposition
5.1
first shows that the spaces

(X,d_{t})
are relatively compact in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology, a fundamental fact that lies at the heart of this paper.
The first part of Theorem
C
is established in Theorem
5.3
where the

convergence of
\dot{\varphi}_{t}
towards
\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}
as

. Once the theory of compact surfaces with bounded integral curvature is briefly recalled in Section

,

.

then proves the second part of Theorem

, i.e. the Gromov-Hausdorff convergence of

2
Preliminaries

In the whole article we let
(X,\omega_{X})
denote a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
.
We set
d=\partial+\overline{\partial}
and
d^{c}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}(\partial-\overline{\partial})

so that

dd^{c}=\frac{i}{\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}
.

2.1
Quasi-plurisubharmonic functions

2.1.1
Regularization

A function is quasi-plurisubharmonic if it is locally given as the sum of a smooth and a plurisubharmonic function.
Quasi-plurisubharmonic functions

\varphi:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty\}
satisfying

\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0

in the weak sense of currents are called
\omega_{X}
-psh functions.

Definition 2.1
.

We let
\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega_{X})
denote the set of all
\omega_{X}
-plurisubharmonic functions which are not identically
-\infty
.

The set
\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega_{X})
is a closed subset of
L^{1}(X)
for the
L^{1}
-topology.

A celebrated result of Demailly
[
11
]
ensures that any

\omega_{X}
-psh function is the decreasing limit of smooth
\omega_{X}
-psh functions.
To establish uniform estimates, one can thus approximate the initial data

\varphi
by a decreasing sequence of smooth
\omega_{X}
-psh functions
\varphi_{0,j}

and establish a priori estimates on the smooth approximating flows
\varphi_{t,j}
.

2.1.2
Monge-Ampère measures

In the whole article we let
T=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi
be a positive closed current with
continuous

potential
\varphi\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega_{X})\cap C^{0}(X)
.
It follows from Bedford-Taylor theory
(see
[
21
]
) that the complex Monge-Ampère measure

T^{n}=(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}
is a well-defined positive Radon measure of
total mass
V=\int_{X}\omega_{X}^{n}
.

In this article we often assume that
T^{n}=f\omega_{X}^{n}
is absolutely continuous
with respect to a smooth volume form, with
f\in L^{p}(\omega_{X}^{n})
for some
p>1
.
This ensures that
\varphi
is Hölder continuous,
as follows from the work of Kolodziej
[
28
]
.
More generally we have the following weaker assumption.

Definition 2.2
.

A positive measure
\mu
on
X
satisfies Condition (K) if there exists

dV_{X}
a smooth volume form,
f\geq 0
a Lebesgue-measurable function,

w:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\rightarrow[1,+\infty)

a convex increasing weight
s.t.
\mu=fdV_{X}
and
\int_{X}w\circ f\,dV_{X}<+\infty
,
where

w(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+\infty}}{{\sim}}t(\log t)^{n}(h\circ\log\circ\log t)^{n}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{dt}{h(t)}<+\infty.

As shown by Kolodziej in
[
26
, Theorem 2.5.2]
,
this is the optimal condition that ensures the continuity
of Monge-Ampère potentials.
One can moreover obtain quantitative estimates
on its modulus of continuity in terms of the growth of
w
at infinity
(see
[
28
,
10
,
25
,
19
]
):

Theorem 2.3
.

When
\mu
satisfies Condition (K) and
\mu(X)=\int_{X}\omega_{X}^{n}
,
there exists a unique continuous
\omega_{X}
-psh function
\varphi
such that

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi^{n})=\mu=fdV_{X},

up to an additive constant. When
f\in L^{p}(dV_{X})
for some
p>1
, then
\varphi
is Hölder continuous.

2.1.3
Diameter of Kähler metrics with Hölder potentials

Several uniform bounds for the diameter of Kähler manifolds have been provided in
the past few years.
We shall need the following estimate, which is a combination of
the main result of
[
28
,
10
]
together with
[
30
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\omega=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi
be a Kähler form
and fix a smooth volume form
dV_{X}
.
Assume that
\omega^{n}=fdV_{X}
with

\int_{X}f^{p}dV_{X}\leq A
for some
p>1,A>0
. Then for all
x,y\in X
,

{\rm diam}(X,{\omega})\leq C\;\;\text{ and }\;\;d_{{\omega}}(x,y)\leq Cd_{\omega_{X}}(x,y)^{\alpha}

for some constants
C,\alpha>0
that only depend on
X,\omega_{X},dV_{X},p
and
an upper bound for
\|f\|_{L^{p}}
.

As shown in
[
24
,
19
]
one can obtain a uniform bound on the diameter
under slightly less restrictive integrability condition on the density
f
. However, Condition (K) is too weak to ensure finiteness of the diameter in general.
On the other hand, Condition (K) coupled with a uniform Ricci lower bound on
\omega
,
guarantees finiteness of the diameter as advocated by
[
17
]

(and pushed further in
[
19
, Theorem A]
).

2.2
Complex Monge-Ampère flows

In the whole article we fix an initial datum
T=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi
which is
a positive closed current cohomologous to
\omega_{X}
.
We assume that the qpsh potential
\varphi
is continuous and

T^{n}=f\omega_{X}^{n}
, where
f\in L^{p}(\omega_{X}^{n})
for some
p>1
. It thus follows from
Theorem
2.3
that
\varphi
is actually Hölder continuous.

Since
{\rm Ric}(\omega_{t})
and
{\rm Ric}(\omega_{X})
both represent the first Chern class

c_{1}(X)
of
X
, it follows from the twisted Kähler-Ricci flow equation

\frac{\partial\omega_{t}}{\partial t}=-{\rm Ric}(\omega_{t})+{\rm Ric}(\omega_{X})

that the cohomology class of
\omega_{t}
is constant, equal to that
of
\omega_{X}
. We can thus decompose
\omega_{t}=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}
for some
smooth
\omega_{X}
-psh function
\varphi_{t}(x)
which is unique up to an additive normalizing constant

c(t)
. The flow equation therefore yields

dd^{c}\dot{\varphi_{t}}=dd^{c}\log\frac{(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}}{\omega_{X}^{n}},

hence

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t}+b(t)}\omega_{X}^{n},

for some
b(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We normalize
\varphi
so that
b\equiv 0
.

Solving the twisted Kähler-Ricci flow equation is thus equivalent to finding
a family of smooth
\omega_{X}
-psh functions
\varphi_{t}\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega_{X})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(X)

such that

(2.1)

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{X}^{n}\,\,\text{ on }X\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{*},\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\varphi_{t}\underset{t\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\varphi\text{ in }L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n}).

In this context the Cauchy problem for the complex Monge-Ampère flow (
2.1
)
admits a unique continuous solution:

Theorem 2.5
.

[
22
,
12
,
39
]

There exists a unique family
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(X\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})
such that,
setting
\varphi_{t}=\varphi_{|X\times\{t\}}
,

\bullet

\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(X\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{*})
and

\omega_{t}=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}
is a Kähler form for any
t>0
;

\bullet

\varphi
is a solution to (
2.1
) on
X\times(0,+\infty)
;

\bullet

\|\varphi_{t}-\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0
.

Our aim in this article is to understand the metric convergence at
t=0
of
(X,d_{t})
,
where
d_{t}
denotes the Riemannian distance associated to
\omega_{t}
.

3
A priori estimates along the flow

We consider in this section the complex Monge-Ampère flow

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{X}^{n}

with initial condition
\varphi\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\omega_{X})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(X)
,
such that
(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=e^{\psi^{+}-\psi^{-}}\omega_{X}^{n}
.

Set
\omega_{t}:=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}
.
We approximate
\varphi,\psi_{\pm}
by decreasing sequences of smooth
A\omega_{X}
-psh functions
and consider the approximating flows. We establish uniform a priori estimates on the latter
and then pass to the limit.
In what follows we thus assume that all the quantities involved are smooth and we freely use the classical parabolic maximum principle.

3.1
{\mathcal{C}}^{0}
-bounds

The maximum principle ensures that

\varphi(t,x)
is uniformly bounded:

Proposition 3.1
.

For all
(x,t)\in X\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, one has

\inf_{X}\varphi\leq\varphi_{t}(x)\leq\sup_{X}\varphi.

If we further assume that there is a
convex

weight
w
such that

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=f\omega_{X}^{n}\;\;\text{ with }\;\;I(0):=\int_{X}w\circ f\omega_{X}^{n}<+\infty,

the latter control is uniform along the flow as follows from the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

The function
t\mapsto I(t)=\int_{X}w\circ f_{t}\,\omega_{X}^{n}

is non-increasing along the flow

{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}=\log f_{t}:=\log\left[\frac{(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\right].

This property has been established in
[
22
, Proposition 3.5]
.
We include a proof for the reader’s convenience.
We shall use it hereafter with
w(t)=t^{p}
,
p>1
.

Proof.

Observe that

\log f_{t}
satisfies the following Heat-type equation,

\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial t}=\Delta_{\omega_{t}}f_{t}-\frac{|\nabla_{\omega_{t}}f_{t}|^{2}}{f_{t}}.

We infer

I^{\prime}(t)=n\int_{X}\frac{w^{\prime}\circ f_{t}}{f_{t}}\,dd^{c}f_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}-n\int_{X}\frac{w^{\prime}\circ f_{t}}{f_{t}^{2}}\,df_{t}\wedge d^{c}f_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}.

Integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{X}\frac{w^{\prime}\circ f_{t}}{f_{t}}\,dd^{c}f_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}=-\int d\left(\frac{w^{\prime}\circ f_{t}}{f_{t}}\right)\wedge d^{c}f_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\frac{w^{\prime\prime}\circ f_{t}}{f_{t}}\,df_{t}\wedge d^{c}f_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}+\int_{X}\frac{w^{\prime}\circ f_{t}}{f_{t}^{2}}\,df_{t}\wedge d^{c}f_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1},

therefore

I^{\prime}(t)=-n\int_{X}\frac{w^{\prime\prime}\circ f_{t}}{f_{t}}\,df_{t}\wedge d^{c}f_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}\leq 0,

as claimed.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Assume
\int_{X}f^{p}\omega_{X}^{n}<+\infty
for some
p>1
.
Then
\exists\alpha,C>0
such that

\forall x,y\in X,\,\forall t>0,\quad d_{t}(x,y)\leq Cd_{\omega_{X}}(x,y)^{\alpha}.

Proof.

Lemma
3.2
ensures that
\omega_{t}^{n}=f_{t}\omega_{X}^{n}
with

\int_{X}f_{t}^{p}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq\int_{X}f^{p}\omega_{X}^{n}<+\infty
.
The result now follows from Theorem
2.4
.
∎

3.2
Bounds on
\dot{\varphi}_{t}

Recall that we assume that the initial datum satisfies

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=f\omega_{X}^{n}=e^{\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}}\omega_{X}^{n},

where
\psi_{\pm}
are qpsh functions.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
0\leq t\leq 1
and for all
x\in X
,

{\psi_{+}}(x)-C\leq\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)\leq C-{\psi_{-}}(x).

Proof.

We fix
A>0
so that
\psi_{\pm}\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,A\omega_{X})
and
consider

H(t,x)=\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)+\psi_{-}(x)-A\varphi_{t}(x).

Since
\varphi_{t}(x)
is uniformly bounded (by Proposition
3.1
), the bound from above on

\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)
is equivalent to a uniform bound from above for
H
.
If the maximum of
H
is attained along
(t=0)
we obtain

H_{\rm max}\leq\sup_{X}\psi_{+}-A\inf\varphi_{t}(x)\leq C,

since
\dot{\varphi_{0}}(x)=\psi_{+}(x)-\psi_{-}(x)\leq\sup_{X}\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}(x)
.

Assume now that the maximum is attained at some point

({t_{0}},x_{0})
with
{t_{0}}>0
. At this point we have

0\leq\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)(H)
,
where we let
\Delta_{t}
denote the Laplacian with respect to
\omega_{t}:=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}
.
A direct computation shows that

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)(H)=A[n-\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)]-{\rm tr}_{\omega_{t}}(A\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{-})\leq A[n-\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)].

Thus
\dot{\varphi_{{t_{0}}}}(x_{0})\leq n
. Since
\psi_{-}(x_{0})\leq\sup_{X}\psi_{-}
and

|\varphi_{t}(x)|\leq C
, we infer again

H_{\rm max}\leq n+\sup_{X}\psi_{-}+AC\leq C^{\prime}.\\

We now establish the uniform bound from below on
\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)
.
Consider

G(t,x)=\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)-\psi_{+}(x)+(A+1)\varphi_{t}(x).

The function
G
is bounded from below along
(t=0)
since

\dot{\varphi_{0}}(x)-\psi_{+}(x)\geq-\sup_{X}\psi_{-}

and
\varphi
is bounded. A direct computation shows that

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)(G)\geq(A+1)[\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)-n]+{\rm tr}_{\omega_{t}}(\omega)\geq(A+1)[\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)-n]+ne^{-\frac{1}{n}\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)},

since

{\rm tr}_{\omega_{t}}(\omega)\geq n\left(\frac{\omega_{t}^{n}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\right)^{1/n}=ne^{-\frac{1}{n}\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)}.

If
G
reaches its minimum at a point
({t_{0}},x_{0})
with
{t_{0}}>0
, we infer

0\geq(A+1)[\dot{\varphi_{{t_{0}}}}(x_{0})-n]+ne^{-\frac{1}{n}\dot{\varphi_{{t_{0}}}}(x_{0})},

hence
\dot{\varphi_{{t_{0}}}}(x_{0})\geq-C(A,n)
. The conclusion follows.
∎

3.3
Laplacian bounds

The goal of this section is to establish the following result.

Theorem 3.5
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
0\leq t\leq 1
and for all
x\in X
,

\frac{1}{C}e^{\psi_{+}}\omega_{X}\leq\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}\leq Ce^{-\psi_{-}}\omega_{X}.

3.3.1
Parabolic Chern-Lu inequality

We are going to use the following inequality due to Chern-Lu
[
9
,
32
]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
and
(Y,\omega_{Y})
be two Kähler manifolds, and let
f:X\to Y
be a holomorphic map such that
\partial f
does not vanish. Then

\Delta_{\omega_{X}}\log|\partial f|^{2}\geq\frac{(\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{X})^{\sharp}\otimes\omega_{Y}(\partial f,\overline{\partial f})}{|\partial f|^{2}}-\frac{\omega_{X}^{\sharp}\otimes\omega_{X}^{\sharp}\otimes R^{Y}(\partial f,\overline{\partial f},\partial f,\overline{\partial f})}{|\partial f|^{2}}

where
\partial f
is viewed as a section of
T^{*}_{X}\otimes f^{*}T_{Y}
,
(\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{X})^{\sharp}
(resp.
\omega_{X}^{\sharp}
) is the hermitian form induced on
T_{X}^{*}
by
\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{X}
(resp.
\omega_{X}
) via
\omega_{X}
and
R^{Y}
is the Chern curvature form of
\omega_{Y}
.

We apply the latter lemma to a manifold
X
endowed with two Kähler metrics
\omega,\beta
and
f=\mathrm{Id}:(X,\beta)\to(X,\omega_{X})
. We choose a system of local holomorphic coordinates
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
on
X
such that the hermitian matrices associated to
\omega_{X}
(resp.
\beta
) are
(h_{i\bar{j}})
(resp.
(g_{i\bar{j}})
). The associated curvature tensors are denoted respectively by
R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}^{\omega_{X}}
and
R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}^{\beta}
. We also introduce the Ricci curvature tensor for the Kähler metric
\beta
as
R_{i\bar{j}}^{\beta}=g^{k\bar{\ell}}R^{\beta}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}
. The hermitian form
(\mathrm{Ric}\,\beta)^{\sharp}
gives rise to the tensor
R_{i\bar{j}}^{\beta\sharp}=g^{i\bar{k}}g^{\ell\bar{j}}R^{\beta}_{k\bar{\ell}}
. Finally, one has
\partial f=dz_{i}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
and
|\partial f|^{2}=\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}
.
In coordinates, Chern-Lu formula now becomes

\Delta_{\beta}\log\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}\geq\frac{1}{\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}}\big(g^{i\bar{k}}R^{\beta}_{k\bar{\ell}}g^{\ell\bar{j}}h_{i\bar{j}}-g^{i\bar{j}}g^{k\bar{\ell}}R^{\omega_{X}}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}\big)

Assume that there exists an upper bound
B>0
on the holomorphic bisectional curvature of
(X,\omega_{X})
so that

R^{\omega_{X}}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}\leq B\,(h_{i\bar{j}}h_{k\bar{\ell}}+h_{i\bar{\ell}}h_{k\bar{j}}).

As
g^{i\bar{j}}g^{k\bar{\ell}}(h_{i\bar{j}}h_{k\bar{\ell}}+h_{i\bar{\ell}}h_{k\bar{j}})\leq 2(\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X})^{2}
,
we get

\Delta_{\beta}\log\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}\geq\frac{1}{\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}}g^{i\bar{k}}R^{\beta}_{k\bar{\ell}}g^{\ell\bar{j}}h_{i\bar{j}}-2B\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}

If the forms
\beta,\omega_{X}
depend on a time parameter
t
, so that

(\mathrm{Ric}\,+\frac{\partial}{\partial t})\beta\geq-A\beta-(C_{1}\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi),\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega_{X}\leq C_{2}\omega_{X}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi\leq C_{3}

for some constants
A,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
and some function
\psi\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,C_{1}\omega_{X})
. In particular, we get

g^{i\bar{k}}R^{\beta}_{k\bar{\ell}}g^{\ell\bar{j}}h_{i\bar{j}}\geq-A\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}-\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}\cdot\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}(C_{1}\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi)-\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}gg^{-1}h)

Combining that inequality with the identity below

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\log\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X})=\frac{1}{\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}}\cdot(-\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}gg^{-1}h)+\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h))

we get

(\Delta_{\beta}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})\log\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}\geq-A-\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}(C_{1}\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi)-C_{2}-2B\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}

As

\Delta_{\beta}\psi=\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}(C_{1}\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi)-C_{1}\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}

we infer

(\Delta_{\beta}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})(\log\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}+\psi)\geq-A-(2B+C_{1})\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}-C_{2}-C_{3}

Finally, assume that
\omega_{X}
and
\beta
are cohomologous, i.e. that there exists a function
\varphi
such that
\beta=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi
. Then,

\Delta_{\beta}(-\varphi)=\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}-n

and therefore

(\Delta_{\beta}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})(\log\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}+\psi-C_{4}\varphi)\geq-A+\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}-C_{4}(n-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi)-C_{2}-C_{3}

if we set
C_{4}:=2B+C_{1}+1
.
In the end, we have proved

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
X
be a Kähler manifold and let
\omega_{X}=\omega_{X}(t),\beta=\beta(t)
be two Kähler forms depending smoothly on a parameter
t>0
. Assume that

1.

\omega_{X}
and
\beta
are cohomologous, i.e. there exists a function
\varphi
such that
\beta=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi
.

2.

The bisectional curvature of
\omega_{X}
is bounded above by a constant
B
; i.e.

i\Theta(T_{X},\omega_{X})\leq B\,\omega_{X}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{T_{X}}.

3.

(\mathrm{Ric}\,+\frac{\partial}{\partial t})\beta\geq-A\beta-(C_{1}\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\psi),\;\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\omega_{X}\leq C_{2}\omega_{X}\;\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi\leq C_{3}
,
for some constants
A,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
and some function
\psi\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,C_{1}\omega_{X})
.

Then, there exist two constants
K,M>0
depending only on
n,A,B,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
such that

(\Delta_{\beta}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})(\log\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}+\psi-K\varphi)\geq\operatorname{tr}_{\beta}\omega_{X}+K\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-M.

We shall also use the following classical Laplacian estimates
which goes back to the celebrated
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
-estimate of Yau
[
45
]

(the above version is due to Siu
[
38
]
).

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\omega_{X},\beta
be Kähler forms. Then

-\Delta_{\beta}\log{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\beta)\leq\frac{{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\mathrm{Ric}\,(\beta))}{{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\beta)}+B{\rm tr}_{\beta}(\omega_{X}),

where
-B
is a lower bound on the holomorphic bisectional curvature of
(X,\omega_{X})
.

3.3.2
Proof of Theorem
3.5

We proceed in two steps.

We first take care of the upper bound
.
It is a parabolic version of the main result of
[
34
]
.
Consider

H(t,x)=\log{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})-M\varphi_{t}+\psi_{-},

where
M>0
is chosen hereafter.
The result follows if we can uniformly bound
H
from above, since
\varphi_{t}
is uniformly bounded
(by Proposition
3.1
).
If the maximum of
H
is reached along
(t=0)
, then
we are done by the main result of
[
34
]
.

We can thus assume that the maximum of
H
is reached at
(t_{0},x_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
.
We let
\Delta_{t}
denote the Laplacian with respect to
\omega_{t}:=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}
.
Observe that

\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{t}=\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{X}-dd^{c}\dot{\varphi_{t}}
hence

{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{t})={\rm tr}_{\omega}(\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{X})-\Delta_{\omega_{X}}(\dot{\varphi_{t}})

while

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})=\frac{\Delta_{\omega_{X}}(\dot{\varphi_{t}})}{{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})}.

It follows therefore from Lemma
3.8
applied to
\beta=\omega_{t}
that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)(\log{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\mathrm{Ric}\,(\omega_{X}))}{{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})}+B{\rm tr}_{\omega_{t}}(\omega_{X})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})}+B{\rm tr}_{\omega_{t}}(\omega_{X}),

hence at
(t_{0},x_{0})
,

\displaystyle 0\leq\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)(H)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})}+B{\rm tr}_{\omega_{t}}(\omega_{X})-M\dot{\varphi_{t}}+M\Delta_{t}\varphi_{t}-\Delta_{t}\psi_{-}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})}+(A+B-M){\rm tr}_{\omega_{t}}(\omega_{X})-M\dot{\varphi_{t}}+Mn.

We choose
M=A+B+1
and note that we can assume wlog
{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})\geq 1
at the point
(t_{0},x_{0})
to obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}_{\omega_{t}}(\omega_{X})\leq C^{\prime}-M\dot{\varphi_{t}}.

The end of the proof in the case
n=1
is simpler to handle, so we focus on the case
n\geq 2
form now on. Recall that

{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})\leq n\left(\frac{\omega_{t}^{n}}{\omega_{X}^{n}}\right)\left[{\rm tr}_{\omega_{t}}(\omega_{X})\right|^{n-1}\Longrightarrow c_{n}e^{-\frac{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}{n-1}}\left[{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})\right]^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\leq{\rm tr}_{\omega_{t}}(\omega_{X}).

It follows therefore from (
3.1
) that

(3.2)

c_{n}\left[{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})\right]^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\leq C^{\prime}e^{\frac{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}{n-1}}+M(-\dot{\varphi_{t}})e^{\frac{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}{n-1}}.

We deal with two cases separately.

\bullet
If
\dot{\varphi}_{t_{0}}(x_{0})\leq 0
, then

{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t_{0}})(x_{0})\leq C_{4},

thanks to (
3.2
) and the fact that
\sup_{y\geq 0}y\exp(-\frac{y}{n-1})=\alpha_{n}<+\infty
. As a result, we have

H_{\rm max}=\log{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t_{0}})(x_{0})+\psi_{-}(x_{0})-M\varphi_{t_{0}}(x_{0})\leq\log C_{4}+\sup_{X}\psi_{-}+C_{0}

and the proof of the upper bound is complete in this first case.

\bullet
If
\dot{\varphi}_{t_{0}}(x_{0})\geq 0
, then (
3.2
) shows

{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})e^{-\dot{\varphi_{t}}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\quad\mbox{ at}\,\,(t_{0},x_{0}).

Since
\psi_{-}\leq-\dot{\varphi_{t}}+C
by Lemma
3.4
, this yields

\displaystyle H_{\rm max}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})(t_{0},x_{0})+\psi_{-}(x_{0})+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log e^{-\dot{\varphi_{t}}}{\rm tr}_{\omega_{X}}(\omega_{t})(t_{0},x_{0})+O(1)+C\leq C_{3}.

and the proof of the upper bound is now fully complete.

We now take care of the lower bound
. From the equation, we see that

\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{t}=\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{X}-dd^{c}\dot{\varphi}_{t}=\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{X}-\partial_{t}\omega_{t}.

Therefore, one can apply Lemma
3.7
with
\beta=\omega_{t}
,
\psi=0,A=C_{2}=C_{3}=0
in order to get two constants
K_{1},M_{1}>0
satisfying

(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{t})(\log\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t}}\omega_{X}-K_{1}\varphi_{t})\leq-\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t}}\omega_{X}-K_{1}\dot{\varphi}_{t}+M_{1}.

If one introduces the quantity

\widetilde{H}(t,x):=\log\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t}}(\omega_{X})-K\varphi_{t}+\psi_{+},

where
K:=K_{1}+A
, then one infers immediately that

(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{t})\widetilde{H}\leq-\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t}}\omega_{X}-K\dot{\varphi}_{t}+M

where
M:=M_{1}+nA
.

If the maximum of
\widetilde{H}
is reached at a point
(t_{0},x_{0})
where
t_{0}=0
, then the result follows from
[
23
, Corollary 2.3]
.
We can thus assume that the maximum of
H
is reached at
(t_{0},x_{0})
with
t_{0}>0
.
At that point, one has
(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{t})\widetilde{H}\geq 0
and therefore

\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t}}\omega_{X}\leq M-K\dot{\varphi}_{t}.

We distinguish two cases.

\bullet
If
\dot{\varphi}_{t_{0}}(x_{0})\geq 0
, then
\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t_{0}}}\omega_{X}(x_{0})\leq M
hence

\widetilde{H}(t_{0},x_{0})\leq M^{\prime}:=M+K\sup|\varphi_{t}|+\sup_{X}\psi_{+}.

We infer

\log\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t}}\omega_{X}+\psi_{+}=\widetilde{H}+K\varphi_{t}\leq M^{\prime}+K\sup|\varphi_{t}|

and we are done.

\bullet
We now assume that
\dot{\varphi}_{t_{0}}(x_{0})<0
. Ultimately, we want to show
\widetilde{H}\leq O(1)
,
or equivalently
\widetilde{H}(t_{0},x_{0})\leq O(1)
. Let us introduce a universal constant
C=C(K,M)
such that

x+\log(M-Kx)\leq C
for any
x\in(-\infty,0]
. Using Lemma
3.4
, one gets

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}(t_{0},x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\log\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t_{0}}}\omega_{X}(x_{0})+\psi_{+}(x_{0})+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq\log\operatorname{tr}_{\omega_{t_{0}}}\omega_{X}(x_{0})+\dot{\varphi}_{t_{0}}(x_{0})+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq\log(M-K\dot{\varphi}_{t_{0}}(x_{0}))+\dot{\varphi}_{t_{0}}(x_{0})+O(1)\leq C+O(1)

and the result follows.

\Box

3.4
Higher order estimates

3.4.1
Positive times

Using the complex parabolic Evans-Krylov theory together with Schauder’s estimates, it follows from our previous estimates that the following higher order a priori estimates hold:

Proposition 3.9
.

Fix
0<\varepsilon<T<+\infty
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. There exists

C(k,\varepsilon,T)>0
such that

\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{k}(X\times[\varepsilon,T])}\leq C(k,\varepsilon,T).

3.4.2
Regularity up to time zero

If the functions
\psi_{\pm}
are smooth in some Zariski open set
\Omega\subset X
,
one can further obtain uniform estimates on compact subsets
K
of
\Omega
,

\|\varphi\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{k}(K\times[0,T])}\leq C(k,K,T).

Since
\varphi_{t}
converges to
\varphi
in
{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(X)
, we infer the following result.

Proposition 3.10
.

If the functions
\psi_{\pm}
are smooth in some Zariski open set
\Omega\subset X
,
then
\varphi_{t}
converges to
\varphi
in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
as
t\rightarrow 0
.

When
\psi_{+}=0
one can reinforce some of these estimates as follows.

Proposition 3.11
.

If
\psi_{+}=0
then there exists
C>0
such that
\varphi_{t}-Ct^{2}
is concave.

Proof.

Recall that
\ddot{\varphi}_{t}=\Delta_{t}(\dot{\varphi}_{t})
and observe that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)(\ddot{\varphi}_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n(n-1)\frac{(dd^{c}\dot{\varphi}_{t})^{2}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-2}}{\omega_{t}^{n}}-n^{2}\left(\frac{dd^{c}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}}{\omega_{t}^{n}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-n\left(\frac{dd^{c}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}}{\omega_{t}^{n}}\right)^{2}\leq 0.

Thus
\ddot{\varphi}_{t}
reaches its maximum along
(t=0)
by the maximum principle. Now

\ddot{\varphi}_{0}=\Delta_{0}(\dot{\varphi}_{0})=\Delta_{\omega_{0}}(-\psi_{-})\leq A{\rm tr}_{\omega_{0}}(\omega_{X})\leq C,

as follows from Theorem
3.5
.
∎

3.4.3
Geometric singularities

We assume here that
\psi_{+}=0
and
|\partial\psi_{-}|_{\omega_{X}}\leq e^{-\alpha\psi_{-}}

for some exponent
\alpha>0
, and establish much more precise higher order information.

Proposition 3.12
.

Assume
\psi_{+}=0
. If

Proof.

4
The metric structure induced by a current

4.1
The one-dimensional case

In this section, we consider a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega_{X})=(S,\omega_{S})
of complex dimension
n=1
, i.e.
S
is a compact Riemann surface. We denote by
d_{S}
the geodesic distance on
S
associated to
\omega_{S}
. Next, we consider a positive current of the form

(4.1)

T=e^{\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}}\omega_{S}

where
\psi_{\pm}
are quasi-subharmonic functions on
S
. The functions
\psi_{\pm}
are not uniquely defined, but one can and will always assume that for any
x\in S
, we have either
\nu(\psi_{+},x)=0
or
\nu(\psi_{-},x)=0
.

For a quasi-subharmonic function
\psi
on
S
, we consider the maximal value of Lelong numbers,

\nu(\psi):=\sup\{\nu(\psi,x),x\in S\}.

Note that we have

e^{-\psi}\in L^{1}\Longleftrightarrow e^{-\psi}\in L^{p}\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,\mbox{some }\,p>1\Longleftrightarrow\nu(\psi)<2.

Let us observe further that the condition
e^{-\psi_{-}}\in L^{1}
is stronger than the condition
e^{\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}}\in L^{1}
even when
\nu(\psi_{+})=0
, as one can see by considering near
0\in\mathbb{C}
the functions
\psi_{+}=-2\log(-\log|z|)
and
\psi_{-}=2\log|z|
. It will be convenient to set

\nu_{\pm}:=\nu(\psi_{\pm}).

If
\gamma:[0,1]\to S
is a Lipschitz path, we define

\ell_{T}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|_{T}\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|_{\omega_{S}}e^{\frac{1}{2}(\psi_{+}-\psi_{-})(\gamma(t))}\,dt.

This is a well-defined, non-negative quantity which may however be infinite.

Definition 4.1
.

The semi-distance
d_{T}
associated to
T
is

d_{T}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\ell_{T}(\gamma),\;\gamma\subset S\text{ a Lipschitz path joining }x\text{ to }y\right\}.

At this point,
d_{T}
may take infinite values but the following theorem shows that it is essentially not the case.
Let us make the statement of Theorem
D
more precise:

Theorem 4.2
.

If
\nu_{-}<2
then
d_{T}
is a well-defined distance bi-Hölder equivalent to
d_{S}
.

More precisely, fix
\varepsilon>0
so small that
\alpha_{-}=\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{-}+\varepsilon)<1
and set
\alpha_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{\pm}+\varepsilon)
.
Then there exists
C=C(\omega_{S},\psi_{\pm},\varepsilon)>0
such that for all paths
\gamma
, one has

C^{-1}\ell_{\omega_{S}}^{1+\alpha_{+}}(\gamma)\leq\ell_{T}(\gamma)\leq C\ell_{\omega_{S}}^{1-\alpha_{-}}(\gamma).

In particular
C^{-1}d_{S}^{1+\alpha_{+}}\leq d_{T}\leq Cd_{S}^{1-\alpha_{-}}
.

The proof of Theorem
4.2
is given at the end of this section. It relies of the following result which establishes fine integrability properties of plane subharmonic functions.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
u
be a subharmonic function in an open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}
whose Lelong numbers
satisfy
\nu^{+}:=\sup\{\nu(u,a);a\in\Omega\}<1
.
Fix a compact subset
K\subset\Omega
,
\nu^{+}<\nu<1
,
and
\gamma:[0,L]\longrightarrow K
a smooth curve parametrized by arc length.
Then

\int_{0}^{L}e^{-u\circ\gamma(s)}ds\leq C(K,u)L^{1-\nu}.

The constant
C(K,u)>0
is moreover uniformly bounded if
u
belongs to a compact subset of
subharmonic functions such that
\nu^{+}(u)<1
.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{u}:=\frac{\Delta u}{2\pi}
be the Riesz measure of
u
, and set
\nu_{u}(z,r)=\mu_{u}(\bar{{\mathbb{D}}}(z,r))
.
Fix
R>0
such that
\Gamma:=\gamma([0,L])\subset{\mathbb{D}}(0,R)
.
Since the Lelong numbers
\nu_{u}(z)=\inf_{r>0}\nu_{u}(z,r)
are upper semi-continuous,
we can assume that
\nu(a,r)<\nu<1
for
a\in K
and
0<r<r_{1}
, for
r_{1}>0
small.
Replacing
u
by
u_{\varepsilon}(z)=u(z)+\varepsilon(|z|^{2}-R^{2})

if necessary, we can further assume that
\nu_{u}(z,r)\geq\delta>0
for all
z\in\Gamma
.

The Poisson-Jensen formula
[
21
, Proposition 1.25]
shows that for all

(z,r)\in\tilde{\Omega}:=\{(z,r)\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+};r<\text{dist}(z,\partial\Omega)\}
,

-u(z)=-\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(z+re^{i\theta})\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}+\int_{{\mathbb{D}}(z,r)}\log(r/\penalty|z-\zeta|)d\mu_{u}(\zeta).

The monotonicity of circular mean values ensures that for
(z,r)\in\tilde{\Omega}

-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(z+re^{i\theta})d\theta\leq-\frac{1}{\pi r^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}(z,r)}u(\zeta)\,d\lambda_{2}(\zeta)\leq\frac{1}{\pi r^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Setting
A(u):=\frac{1}{\pi r^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

we thus obtain

\displaystyle-u(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi r^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\int_{{\mathbb{D}}(z,r)}\log(r/\penalty|z-\zeta|)d\mu_{u}(\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A(u)+\int_{{\mathbb{D}}(z,r)}\nu_{u}(z,r)\log(r/\penalty|z-\zeta|)\frac{d\mu_{u}(\zeta)}{\nu_{u}(z,r)}.

It follows therefore from Jensen’s inequality that

\exp(-u(z))\leq\frac{1}{\delta}e^{A(u)}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}(z,r)}\frac{r^{\nu}}{|z-\zeta|^{\nu}}d\mu_{u}(\zeta).

Integrating the latter with respect to
\lambda_{\gamma}=\gamma_{*}(ds)

and using Fubini’s theorem, we obtain,
setting
V_{r}(\Gamma):=\bigcup_{z\in\Gamma}{\mathbb{D}}(z,r)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\exp(-u(z))d\lambda_{\gamma}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{-1}e^{A(u)}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}(z,r)}\frac{r^{\nu}}{|z-\zeta|^{\nu}}\mu_{u}(\zeta)d\lambda_{\gamma}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta^{-1}e^{A(u)}\int_{V_{r}(\Gamma)}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{r^{\nu}}{|z-\zeta|^{\nu}}d\lambda_{\gamma}(z)d\mu_{u}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{-1}e^{A(u)}\int_{V_{r}(\Gamma)}J_{\Gamma}(\zeta)d\mu_{u}(\zeta),

where

J_{\Gamma}(\zeta):=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{r^{\nu}}{|z-\zeta|^{\nu}}d\lambda_{\gamma}(z)
.
It follows from Lemma
4.4
below that

\displaystyle J_{\Gamma}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda_{\gamma}(\Gamma\cap{\mathbb{D}}(\zeta,rt^{-1/\penalty\nu}))dt\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\min\{L,8rt^{-1/\penalty\nu}\}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\,t_{1}+(8r)\int_{t_{1}}^{+\infty}t^{-1/\penalty\nu}dt=BL^{1-\nu},

where
t_{1}=(\frac{8r}{L})^{\nu}
and
B=\frac{(8r)^{\nu}}{(1-\nu)}
.
Altogether this yields

\int_{0}^{L}\exp(-u\circ\gamma(s))ds\leq\frac{B}{\delta}L^{1-\nu}e^{\pi^{-1}r^{-2}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}\int_{V_{r}(\Gamma)}\Delta u.

Since
V_{r}(\Gamma)\subset K^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
for
0<r
small enough,
the desired inequality follows by observing that
\int_{K^{\prime}}\Delta u\leq C_{K^{\prime},\Omega}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

(see
[
21
, Theorem 3.9]
).
∎

Lemma 4.4

\sup_{X}\varphi=0

(4.5)

[

Note that

\omega:=T|_{\Omega}

\Omega
. We define
d_{\omega}
to be the geodesic distance associated with the possibly incomplete Kähler manifold
(\Omega,\omega)
.

If
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
is an arbitrary Lipschitz path in
X
, the integral
\int_{0}^{1}\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|_{T}\,dt
need not be well-defined. The issue is that even though the coefficients of

are functions in

thanks to the estimate

from Theorem
3.5
, their values along the image of
\gamma
are not well-defined unlike in the case (
4.1
) studied above where the coefficients of
T
were canonically defined at each point. However, if we assume additionally that
\gamma^{-1}(Z)
is finite, then
the quantity

is defined without ambiguity. This leads us to modifying Definition
4.1
as follows.

Definition 4.5
.

The semi-distance
d_{T}
associated to
T
is

d_{T}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\ell_{T}(\gamma),\;\gamma\,\text{Lipschitz path from }x\text{ to }y\,\,\mbox{s.t.}\,\,\gamma^{-1}(Z)\,\mbox{is finite}\right\}.

When
\dim X=1
, the poles of
\psi_{\pm}
are isolated hence Definition
4.5
is consistent with Definition
4.1
. The goals of the following proposition is to show that
d_{T}
is finite, to determine sufficient conditions for
d_{T}
to be a distance and to compare it with
d_{\omega}
. In particular, it encompasses Theorem
A
in the introduction.

Proposition 4.6
.

In the setup above, we have

(i)

d_{\omega}
admits a unique continuous extension
\hat{d}_{\omega}
to
X\times X
. Moreover, it satisfies

\hat{d}_{\omega}\leq Cd_{X}^{\alpha}

for some constants
C>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

(ii)

d_{T}
coincides with
\hat{d}_{\omega}
, i.e.
d_{T}=\hat{d}_{\omega}
. In particular,
d_{T}
is finite.

(iii)

If
\psi_{+}
has isolated singularities,
d_{T}
is a distance. In particular,
(X,d_{T})\simeq\overline{(\Omega,d_{\omega})}
, where the bar symbol denotes the metric completion.

(iv)

If
\psi_{+}
has isolated analytic singularities, then
d_{T}
is bi-Hölder equivalent to
d_{X}
.

Proof.

(i)
. First, we know that
\varphi
is Hölder continuous by Theorem
2.3
. Next, for any point
x_{0}\in\Omega
, the function
d_{\omega}(x_{0},\cdot)
extends to a function in
W^{1,2}(X,\omega_{X})
by
[
19
, Lemma 4.2]
. Therefore, one can run the proof of
[
19
, Proposition 1.4]
and see that

(4.6)

d_{\omega}\leq Cd_{X}^{\alpha}\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\Omega\times\Omega,

so that
(i)
follows.

(ii)
. Let us first show that the inequality

(4.7)

d_{T}\leq Cd_{X}^{\alpha},

holds on
X\times X
, maybe up to increasing
C
. Given
(i)
, the inequality
d_{T}|_{\Omega\times\Omega}\leq d_{\omega}
and the triangle inequality satisfied by
d_{T}
, is it enough to show (
4.7
) on
Z\times\Omega
. So we pick
(x,y)\in Z\times\Omega
and set
\varepsilon:=d_{X}(x,y)
. We can assume that
\varepsilon
is small enough so that
x,y
belong to a coordinate chart. On that chart, one can replace
\omega_{X}
with the euclidean metric without loss of generality. The segment joining
x
and
y
hits
Z
finitely many times, hence one can always find a sequence of points
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
on that segment such that
y_{0}=y
and
d_{X}(y_{k},y_{k+1})\in[\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+1}},\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k}}]
. Fix
\delta>0
and let
\gamma_{k}=\gamma_{k}(\delta)
be a smooth path in
\Omega
joining
y_{k}
to

\ell_{\omega}(\gamma_{k})\leq d_{\omega}(y_{k},y_{k+1})+\frac{\delta}{2^{k}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{k}}(C\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\delta)

where the second inequality follows from (
4.6
). Concatenating all paths

yields a path
\gamma_{\infty}=\gamma_{\infty}(\delta)

to

. Moreover,
\gamma_{\infty}

\ell_{\omega}(\gamma_{\infty})\leq 2(C\varepsilon^{\alpha}+\delta)

, hence

on

) holds.

Now, since the inequality

holds on

for obvious reasons, it extends automatically to

by continuity of

, the latter being guaranteed by (

).

Therefore, it remains to prove that
d_{T}\geq d_{\omega}
say on
\Omega\times\Omega
. Let
\varepsilon>0
, let
x,y\in\Omega
and let
\gamma
be a path from
x
to
y
hitting
Z
at most finitely many times such that
\ell_{T}(\gamma)\leq d_{T}(x,y)+\varepsilon
. In order to simplify the notation, let us assume that
\gamma
hits
Z
at a single point

at a time

, and let

small to be determined later. We can find

such that

is the ball or radius

with respect to
\omega_{X}
. Let
\gamma_{\pm}
be the restriction of

to

from item
(i)
. In particular, there exists a path
\gamma_{\pm}^{\circ}
connecting
p_{-}
to
p_{+}
, lying entirely in
\Omega
and such that
\ell_{\omega}(\gamma_{\pm}^{\circ})\leq C(2r)^{\alpha}+\varepsilon

\gamma_{\pm}
by
\gamma_{\pm}^{\circ}

\gamma^{\circ}
from

Since

(iii)
. By compactness of

.

(iv)
. We can localize the analysis in a chart where such a pole is

When the singularities of
\psi_{+}
are spread along a divisor, obtaining a precise lower bound for

becomes more subtle and should

is Kähler;
otherwise this divisor could be contracted as e.g. in

We nevertheless expect that

is always a distance, as the following example suggests.

Example 4.7
.

Let

be a compact Kähler manifold. Assume that there exists a line bundle

on

such that for some

, there exists a smooth divisor

(e.g. if

is semiample but not torsion). Let

be the cyclic covering of degree

branched along

, cf e.g.
[

, Proposition 4.1.6]
. Then
X
is smooth and
\pi^{*}B=mZ
for some smooth divisor

on

. Consider the smooth semipositive
(1,1)
-form

on

. It is a Kähler form away from

but it is degenerate along

. More precisely, if

is a set of coordinates near a point

is quasi-isometric to

from Definition

.

We finish this section by the following elementary lemma which will be useful later.

Lemma 4.8
.

In the setup above, let
(\omega_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of Kähler metrics such that
\omega_{j}|_{\Omega}\to\omega
in
C^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
as

. Then we have

d_{\omega}\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}d_{\omega_{j}}
on
\Omega\times\Omega,

where
d_{\omega_{j}}
is the geodesic distance associated to
\omega_{j}

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\Omega
, let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
be a Lipschitz path from
x
to
y
lying in
\Omega
such that
\ell_{\omega}(\gamma_{\varepsilon})\leq d_{\omega}(x,y)+\varepsilon
. Let

be an open set containing the image of
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
and such that

is relatively compact in
\Omega
. For

, we have
\omega|_{U}\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}{\omega_{j}}|_{U}
, hence

hence the lemma follows.

(X,d_{T})
as Ricci limit space

In this section, we aim to show that in the case where
\psi_{+}=0
, the metric space
(X,d_{T})
naturally arises as Gromov-Hausdorff limit of compact Kähler manifolds with uniform Ricci lower bound. The main input is Cheeger-Colding theory, and more specifically the results of
[
5
]
,
[

]
and
[

4.3.1
Notations and setup

We keep the notations from Section
4.2
and assume additionally that
\psi_{+}=0
. In order to lighten notation, we set
\psi:=\psi_{-}
. In this section, we make the convention that
C
denotes a positive constant which may change from line to line but only depends on our backgroup setup
(X,\omega_{X},T)

Next, we consider a sequence
(\psi_{j})_{j\geq 1}
of smooth
A\omega_{X}
-psh functions decreasing pointwise to
\psi

[
11
]

\omega_{j}:=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{j}

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{n}=e^{-\psi_{j}+c_{j}}\omega_{X}^{n}

\int_{X}e^{-\psi_{j}+c_{j}}\omega_{X}^{n}=\int_{X}\omega_{X}^{n}

\int_{X}e^{-p(\psi_{j}+c_{j})}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C

as tensors.

We fix a constant

such that

at time zero) that there is a constant

C^{-1}\omega_{X}\leq\omega_{j}\leq Ce^{-\psi_{j}}\omega_{X}

\mathrm{Ric}\,\omega_{j}\geq-(A+B)C\omega_{j}.

4.3.2
Associated metric spaces

Let us now discuss the various metric spaces associated with our above setup. We define

(resp

\omega_{X}
(resp.
\omega_{j}
) on

that we have a two-sided bound

C^{-1}d_{X}\leq d_{j}\leq Cd_{X}^{\alpha}

for some

is homeomorphic to

and that

and

with

.

We aim to compare

The main goal of this section is to show the following theorem which implies Corollary

In the above setup, we have

Recall from Lemma

We divide the proof in two steps where we crucially rely respectively on results of Cheeger-Colding

Step 1.

Reduction to a dimension estimate.

The sequence of compact Kähler manifolds

The dimension estimate.

,

\bullet

e^{-\psi_{-}}\in L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})
.

For
t>0
we consider the unique Kähler forms

\omega_{t}=\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}
, where

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{X}^{n}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\varphi_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\rightarrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varphi.

Let us denote by
d_{t}
the geodesic distance induced by
\omega_{t}
on
X
. It follows from Corollary
3.3
that the compact metric spaces

(X,d_{t})
are relatively compact in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology, cf Proposition
5.1
and the discussion below.
We will study in this final section whether the latter converge, as
t\rightarrow 0
,
to a unique compact metric space associated to
T
.

5.2
Continuity of Ricci currents

5.2.1
Semicontinuity of the distances

Proposition 5.1
.

The functions
d_{t}:X\times X\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
are equicontinuous.

Proof.

Corollary
3.3
ensures that

d_{t}\leq Cd_{X}^{\alpha}
, where
C>0
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
For
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in X
, the triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle\left|d_{t}(x,y)-d_{t}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|d_{t}(x,y)-d_{t}(x,y^{\prime})\right|+\left|d_{t}(x,y^{\prime})-d_{t}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{t}(y,y^{\prime})+d_{t}(x,x^{\prime})\leq Cd_{X}(x,x^{\prime})^{\alpha}+Cd_{X}(y,y^{\prime})^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\left[d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})+d_{X}(y,y^{\prime})\right]^{\alpha}.

∎

As
X\times X
is compact and the diameters
(X,d_{t})
are bounded, it follows
from Arzela-Ascoli theorem that a subsequence

d_{t_{i}}
uniformly converges on
X\times X
, as
t_{i}\rightarrow 0
, to a function

d_{0}
which is a semi-distance.
Thus
(X,d_{t})
converges, in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense, to
the quotient metric space
(X^{\prime},d_{0}^{\prime})
induced by
d_{0}

(see
[
2
, Example 7.4.4]
).

We expect that
d_{0}
coincides with the (semi-)distance
d_{T}
studied in Section
4
.
The following example shows that the weak convergence, as
t\rightarrow 0
, of
\omega_{t}
towards
T

is too weak to ensure that
d_{t}
converges to
d_{T}

(see
[
43
]
for an application to the Ricci flow).

Example 5.2
.

Assume
(X,\omega_{X})
is a compact Riemann surface and fix, for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and finite set

\Lambda_{j}=\{a_{1}^{(j)},\ldots,a_{N_{j}}^{(j)}\}\subset X
such that each point of
X
lies at
d_{\omega_{X}}
distance
at most
2^{-j}
from
\Lambda_{j}
. Let
\gamma_{\ell k}^{(j)}
be a minimizing geodesic for
\omega_{X}
joining

a_{\ell}^{(j)}
to
a_{k}^{(j)}
and set
\Sigma_{j}=\cup_{\ell,k}\gamma_{\ell k}^{(j)}
.
Construct a smooth function
\psi_{j}
on
X
such that

\bullet

-\ln 4=\psi_{j}
on
\Sigma_{j}
and
-\ln 4\leq\psi_{j}\leq\ln 4
on
X
;

\bullet

\int_{X}e^{\psi_{j}}\omega_{X}=\int_{X}\omega_{X}
and
\int_{X}\left|e^{\psi_{j}}-1\right|\omega_{X}\leq 2^{-j}
.

By construction
\omega_{j}=e^{\psi_{j}}\omega_{X}
weakly converges to
\omega_{X}
since

\psi_{j}
is uniformly bounded and converges to
0
in
L^{1}(X)
, but we are going
to show that the distances
d_{j}
associated to
\omega_{j}=e^{\psi_{j}}\omega_{X}

uniformly converge to
d/2
, where
d=d_{\omega_{X}}
.
Observe indeed that
\frac{1}{2}d\leq d_{j}\leq 2d

and for all
(x,y)\in\Sigma_{j}
,

d_{j}(x,y)\leq\ell_{\omega_{j}}(\gamma_{xy}^{(j)})=\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\omega_{X}}(\gamma_{xy}^{(j)})=\frac{1}{2}d(x,y).

Now if
(x,y)\in X
one can find
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\Sigma_{j}
such that

d(x,x^{\prime}),d(y,y^{\prime})\leq 2^{-j}
hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d(x,y)\leq d_{j}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{j}(x,x^{\prime})+d_{j}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})+d_{j}(y^{\prime},y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,d(x,x^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}d(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})+2\,d(y^{\prime},y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\cdot 2^{-j}+\frac{1}{2}\left[d(x^{\prime},x)+d(x,y)+d(y,y^{\prime})\right]\leq 5\cdot 2^{-j}+\frac{1}{2}d(x,y).

Note that the functions
\psi_{j}
in the above example are far from being differences of
A\omega_{X}
-sh functions for any given
A>0
independent of
j
.
We shall take advantage of this property in the next section and show that the
Ricci curvatures weakly converge to the signed current
{\rm Ric}(T)
.

5.2.2
Ricci curvatures

Recall that the Ricci curvature of
\omega_{t}
is the differential form

{\rm Ric}(\omega_{t})={\rm Ric}(\omega_{X})-dd^{c}\log\omega_{t}^{n}/\omega_{X}^{n}={\rm Ric}(\omega_{X})-dd^{c}\dot{\varphi_{t}},

while the Ricci curvature of
T
is
{\rm Ric}(T)={\rm Ric}(\omega_{X})-dd^{c}(\psi_{+}-\psi_{-})
,
a closed bidegree
(1,1)
-current of order zero
(as a difference of positive closed currents). The following result establishes the first part of Theorem
C
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The functions
\dot{\varphi}_{t}
converge, as
t\rightarrow 0
, to
\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}
in
L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})
. In particular, the Ricci curvatures
{\rm Ric}(\omega_{t})
weakly converge to
{\rm Ric}(T)
as
t\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

We first show that
\dot{\varphi_{t}}
weakly converges to

\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}
as
t\rightarrow 0
.
Recall from Lemma
3.4
that there is
C>0
such that for all
x\in X
and
t>0
,

\psi_{+}(x)-C\leq\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)\leq-\psi_{-}(x)+C.

In particular the families of functions
(e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}})_{t>0}
and

(\dot{\varphi_{t}})_{t>0}
are weakly compact in
L^{p}
for some
p>1
.

It follows from
[
21
, Theorem 4.26]

that
\omega_{t}^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{X}^{n}
weakly converges towards
(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=e^{\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}}\omega_{X}^{n}

as
t\rightarrow 0
, since
\varphi_{t}
uniformly converges towards
\varphi
by Theorem
2.5
.
Thus
e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}
weakly converges in
(L^{p})^{*}
to
e^{\psi}

as
t\rightarrow 0
, setting
\psi=\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}
.

Let
u
denote a weak-
(L^{p})^{*}
limit of some subsequence
(\dot{\varphi_{t_{j}}})
, i.e.
\dot{\varphi_{t_{j}}}\rightarrow u
,
and let
q
denote the conjugate exponent of
p
.
We fix
0\leq\chi\in L^{q}
normalized so that
\int_{X}\chi\omega_{X}^{n}=1
.
Jensen’s inequality yields

\int e^{\psi}\chi\omega_{X}^{n}=\lim_{j}\int e^{\dot{\varphi_{t_{j}}}}\chi\omega_{X}^{n}\geq\lim_{j}\exp\left(\int_{X}\dot{\varphi_{t_{j}}}\chi\omega_{X}^{n}\right)=\exp\left(\int_{X}u\chi\omega_{X}^{n}\right).

We apply this inequality to
\chi=\chi_{x,r}
the (normalized) characteristic function of the ball
B_{\omega_{X}}(x,r)
.
By Lebesgue theorem
\int_{X}u\chi_{x,r}\omega_{X}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r\rightarrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}u(x)
for almost every
x
.

Letting
r
decrease to zero we therefore obtain, for almost every
x\in X
,
e^{\psi(x)}\geq e^{u(x)}
hence
\psi(x)\geq u(x)
.

It now suffices to show that

\int_{X}\psi\omega_{X}^{n}\leq\int_{X}u\omega_{X}^{n}

to conclude that there is equality
u=\psi
.
We first observe that

(5.1)

t\mapsto\int_{X}\dot{\varphi_{t}}\omega_{X}^{n}\quad\mbox{ is non-decreasing.}

Indeed

e^{-\dot{\varphi_{t}}}dd^{c}\dot{\varphi_{t}}=-dd^{c}e^{-\dot{\varphi_{t}}}+e^{-\dot{\varphi_{t}}}d\dot{\varphi_{t}}\wedge d^{c}\dot{\varphi_{t}}\geq-dd^{c}e^{-\dot{\varphi_{t}}},

hence using
\omega_{X}^{n}=e^{-\dot{\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{t}^{n}
and
\ddot{\varphi_{t}}=\Delta_{t}\dot{\varphi_{t}}
we obtain

\int_{X}\ddot{\varphi_{t}}\omega_{X}^{n}=\int_{X}e^{-\dot{\varphi_{t}}}dd^{c}\dot{\varphi_{t}}\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}\geq\int_{X}-dd^{c}\left(e^{-\dot{\varphi_{t}}}\right)\wedge\omega_{t}^{n-1}=0,

hence (
5.1
) holds.
We show hereafter that

(5.2)

\forall t>0,\quad\int_{X}\psi\omega_{X}^{n}\leq\int_{X}\dot{\varphi_{t}}\omega_{X}^{n}.

The conclusion will then follow since
\int_{X}\dot{\varphi_{t_{j}}}\omega_{X}^{n}\rightarrow\int_{X}u\omega_{X}^{n}
.

We now use an approximation argument to show (
5.2
). Let
\psi_{j}^{\pm}
be smooth qpsh functions decreasing to
\psi_{\pm}
, and set
\psi_{j}:=\psi_{j}^{+}-\psi_{j}^{-}
.
By
[
45
]
there exists a unique smooth
\omega
-psh function
\varphi_{0,j}
such that

\sup_{X}\varphi_{0,j}=\sup_{X}\varphi
and

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{0,j})^{n}=e^{\psi_{j}+c_{j}}\omega_{X}^{n},

where
c_{j}
is a mass normalizing constant (
c_{j}\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow+\infty
).
Since the densities
f_{j}=e^{\psi_{j}+c_{j}}
converge to
f=e^{\psi}
in
L^{p}
,
it follows from Kolodziej’s stability estimate
[
27
]
that

\varphi_{0,j}
uniformly converge to
\varphi
as
j\rightarrow+\infty
.
We now consider the smooth Monge-Ampère parabolic potentials
\varphi_{t,j}
such that

\varphi_{t,j}\rightarrow\varphi_{0,j}
as
t\rightarrow 0
and

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t,j})^{n}=e^{\dot{\varphi}_{t,j}}\omega_{X}^{n}

for
t>0
. Given (
5.1
), we have

\forall t>0,\quad\int_{X}(\psi_{j}+c_{j})\omega_{X}^{n}=\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{0,j}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t,j}\omega_{X}^{n}.

By construction,
\int_{X}(\psi_{j}+c_{j})\omega_{X}^{n}\rightarrow\int_{X}\psi\omega_{X}^{n}

as
j\rightarrow+\infty
,
while it follows from
[
20
]
that
\dot{\varphi}_{t,j}
smoothly converge
to
\dot{\varphi}_{t}
for
t>0
. Thus
\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t,j}\omega_{X}^{n}\rightarrow\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\omega_{X}^{n}

and (
5.2
) follows, hence
u=\psi
as desired.

It remains to show that the convergence

\dot{\varphi_{t}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\rightarrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\psi
actually holds in
L^{1}
.
The functions
e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}/2}
are weakly compact in
L^{2}
as
t\rightarrow 0
.
We let
f
denote a cluster point. Reasoning as above, it follows from Jensen’s inequality
(applied to
x\mapsto e^{x/2})
that
f\geq e^{u/2}=e^{\psi/2}
.
On the other hand the same reasoning (Jensen’s inequality applied to
x\mapsto x^{2}
) yields
e^{\psi}\geq f^{2}
.
Thus
f=e^{\psi/2}
, i.e.
e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}/2}
weakly converges to
e^{\psi/2}
.
Since
\|e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}/2}\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow\|e^{\psi/2}\|_{L^{2}}
(as

e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}
weakly converges to
e^{\psi}
), we infer

\|e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}/2}-e^{\psi/2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\|e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|e^{\psi/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\langle e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}/2},e^{\psi/2}\rangle\rightarrow 0.

Extracting we can assume that
\dot{\varphi_{t}}(x)
converges to
\psi(x)
almost everywhere;
the conclusion follows from Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

As the final step of the proof shows, the convergence of
\dot{\varphi}_{t}
towards
\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}

holds in
L^{q}
for all
q>1
(and even in the Orlicz space
L^{\rm exp}
).

5.3
The one dimensional case

In this section we apply our analysis to the case of compact Riemann surfaces
and complete the proof of Theorem
D
.

5.3.1
Alexandrov surfaces

We briefly review here the theory of compact surfaces with bounded integral curvature,
as developped by the Leningrad school, following the survey article of
Troyanov
[
44
]
and the book
[
16
]
(see also
[
7
]
).

Definition 5.5
.

Let
S
be an oriented compact topological surface endowed with a geodesic distance
d
.
We say that
(S,d)
has bounded integral curvature if
d
is the uniform limit
of Riemannian geodesic distances
d_{g_{i}}
such that for some uniform
C>0
,

\int_{S}|\kappa(g_{i})|dA_{i}\leq C,

where
dA_{i}
is the area measure of
(S,g_{i})
,
and
\kappa(g_{i})
denotes the Gauss curvature of
g_{i}
.

The latter condition implies (by Banach-Alaoglu theorem) that the curvature
measures
\kappa(g_{i})dA_{i}
weakly converge towards a unique
(Radon) measure
\mu_{d}=\mu
on
(S,d)
which satisfies the Gauss-Bonnet theorem

\mu(S)=\int_{S}d\mu=\lim_{i\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{S}\kappa(g_{i})dA_{i}=\chi(S),

where
\chi(S)
denotes the Euler characteristic of
S
.

Conversely, one can show that
any Radon measure
\mu
on
S
such that
\mu(S)=\chi(S)
is
the curvature metric of an Alexandrov metric
d
on
S
.
As
S
is oriented, it admits a structure of compact Riemann surface.
We equip
S
with a constant curvature metric
\omega_{S}
,
i.e. such that
\mathrm{Ric}\,(\omega_{S})=\kappa\omega_{S}
, where
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant.
It satisfies
\int_{S}\kappa\,\omega_{S}=\chi(S)
.
The Ricci curvature of a positive Radon measure of the form
T_{u}=e^{u}\omega_{S}
is defined to be

\mathrm{Ric}\,(T_{u})=\mathrm{Ric}\,(\omega_{S})-dd^{c}\log(T_{u}/\omega_{S})=\kappa\omega_{S}-dd^{c}u.

Given a Radon measure
\mu

such that
\mu(S)=\chi(S)
, it can be written

\mu=\kappa\omega_{S}-dd^{c}u=\mathrm{Ric}\,(T_{u}),

for a unique integrable function
u:S\rightarrow\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
such that

\int_{S}u\,\omega_{S}=0
.

Note that the function
u
is a difference of
qsh functions.
We let
\psi_{\pm}
denote two qsh functions on
S

(that is,
dd^{c}\psi_{\pm}\geq-A\omega_{S}
for some
A>0
)
such that
u=\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}
and
\psi_{\pm}
have no common Lelong numbers.

Definition 5.6
.

We say that a point
x
is a cusp if
\mu(x)=\mathrm{Ric}\,(T_{u})(x)\geq 2
.

We only consider the case when there is no cusp.
This corresponds to the fact that

e^{-\psi_{-}}\in L^{p}
for some
p>1
.
Consider

d_{u}(x,y):=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{1}e^{\frac{1}{2}u\circ\gamma(t)}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{\omega_{S}}\,dt,\;\gamma(0)=x\text{ and }\gamma(1)=y\right\}.

Proposition
4.3
ensures that
each integral
\int_{0}^{1}e^{\frac{1}{2}u\circ\gamma(t)}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{\omega_{S}}\,dt
is finite.
The following is a recap on the main results obtained by Aleksandrov, Zalgaller and Reshetnyak
(see
[
44
, Proposition 5.3, Theorem 6.1, Theorem 6.2, Corollary 6.3, Theorem 6.4]
):

Theorem 5.7
.

1) If there is no cusp then
d_{u}
is a distance.

2) If the distances
d_{j}
uniformly converge to
d
, then
\mu_{d_{j}}
weakly converge to
\mu_{d}
.

3) If there is no cusp and
\mu_{d_{j}}
weakly converge to
\mu_{d}
, then

d_{u_{j}}
uniformly converges to
d_{u}
.

4) The distance determines the conformal structure.

5) If
(S,d)
is an oriented compact Alexandrov surface with bounded integral curvature and without cusp,
then there exists
\omega
and
u
as above
such that
d=d_{u}
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
S
be an oriented compact topological surface endowed with a distance
d
.
We say that
(S,d)
has curvature bounded from below if
d
is the uniform limit
of Riemannian distances
d_{g_{i}}
such that

\kappa(g_{i})\geq-C,

where
C>0
is a uniform constant,
and
\kappa(g_{i})
denotes the Gauss curvature of
(S,g_{i})
.

In this case the curvature
measures
\kappa(g_{i})dA_{i}
weakly converge towards a unique
curvature metric
\mu_{d}=\mu
of total mass
\chi(S)
, and such that

\mu=\kappa\omega_{S}-dd^{c}u\geq-C\omega_{S}.

This corresponds to the fact that
u=\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}
with
\Delta_{\omega_{S}}\psi_{+}\in L^{\infty}
.
Thus
-u
is qsh and we can equivalently assume that
\psi_{+}=0
.

5.3.2
Canonical smoothing by the Ricci flow

The following result has been a source of inspiration for us:

Theorem 5.9
.

[
37
,
35
]

Let
S
be a compact Alexandrov surface whose curvature is uniformly bounded below.
Then
S
is the initial datum of a smooth Ricci flow. Moreover the latter is unique
up to diffeomorphism.

We now extend this result to the general case of Alexandrov surface with bounded
integral curvature, relaxing the uniform bound from below on the Gauss curvature.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
(S,d)
be an oriented compact topological geodesic surface with bounded integral curvature
with no cusp.
Then
(S,d)
is the initial datum of a smooth Ricci flow.

Proof.

By assumption
S
is a compact Riemann surface endowed with a metric
d_{T}
associated
to a current
T=e^{\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}}\omega_{S}
, where

\bullet

\omega_{S}
is a constant scalar curvature Kähler metric,
{\rm Ric}(\omega_{S})=\kappa\omega_{S}
;

\bullet

\psi_{\pm}
are
A\omega_{S}
-psh functions for some
A>0
large enough;

\bullet

e^{-\psi_{-}}\in L^{p}(\omega_{S})
for some
p>1
.

To simplify the notations we only treat the case of an elliptic curve.
The Kähler-Ricci flow initiating from
T
is then equivalent
to the complex Monge-Ampère flow

\omega_{t}=\omega_{S}+dd^{c}\varphi_{t}=e^{\dot{\varphi_{t}}}\omega_{S},\;\;\text{ with }\;\;\varphi_{t}\rightarrow\varphi\;\;\text{ at time zero}.

Since there is no cusp, it follows from Theorem
5.7
that the distances
d_{t}

uniformly converge to
d_{T}
if and only if the Ricci curvatures weakly converge

{\rm Ric}(\omega_{t})=\kappa\omega_{S}-dd^{c}(\dot{\varphi_{t}})\longrightarrow{\rm Ric}(T)=\kappa\omega_{S}-dd^{c}(\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}).

The latter holds thanks to Theorem
5.3
.
∎

5.4
Geometric singularities

The analysis is more involved in higher dimension, so we
further assume in this section that

\psi_{\pm}
are smooth in some Zariski open set, so
that we can apply the results from Section
4.2
.

It follows from Proposition
4.6
that
T
induces a semi-distance
d_{T}
on
X
. Moreover, if
\psi_{+}
has isolated singularities,
d_{T}
is actually a distance and it coincides with the metric completion of the smooth Riemannian metric defined by
T
in
\Omega
.
The following result (in combination with Theorem
5.3
) completes the proof of Theorem
C
in the introduction.

Theorem 5.11
.

Notation as in §
5.1
. If
T
has geometric singularities and
\psi_{\pm}
have isolated singularities,
then
the distances
d_{t}
uniformly converge, as
t\rightarrow 0
, towards
d_{T}
.
In particular the compact metrics spaces
(X,d_{t})
converge in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense
to
(X,d_{T})
.

Proof.

Let
Z
denote the (finite) set poles of
\psi_{\pm}
.
It follows from Proposition
3.10
that

\omega_{t}
converges locally smoothly to
T
in
\Omega
.
Also, we have seen in Proposition
4.6
that
d_{T}
is a distance and that

(5.3)

\mathrm{diam}(B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r),d_{T})\underset{r\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0,\quad\mathrm{for\,\,any}\,p\in X.

Thanks to Lemma
4.8
, it is enough to show that for any
x,y\in X
, we have

(5.4)

\liminf_{t\rightarrow 0}d_{t}(x,y)\geq d_{T}(x,y).

We start by showing (
5.4
) whenever
x,y\in X\setminus Z
. We fix two such points
x,y
, as well as an arbitrarily small number
\varepsilon>0
. Given (
5.3
), we can choose
r=r(\varepsilon)
such that

(5.5)

\mathrm{diam}(B_{\omega_{X}}(p,r),d_{T})\leq\varepsilon,\quad\mathrm{for\,\,any}\,p\in Z.

Now, let
\gamma_{t}
be a minimizing geodesic for
\omega_{t}
joining
x
to
y
; we can assume without loss of generality that
\gamma_{t}
is parametrized by arclength with respect to
\omega_{X}
.

We set
K:=\bigcup_{p\in Z}\bar{B}_{\omega_{X}}(p,r)
and choose
t_{\varepsilon}
such that

(5.6)

\forall\,0\leq t\leq t_{\varepsilon},\quad\omega_{t}\geq(1-\varepsilon)T\quad\mathrm{on}\,\,X\setminus K,

whose existence is guaranteed by smooth convergence of
\omega_{t}
to
T
in
X\setminus K
.

Let us first make a simple observation. If
\gamma_{t}
hits some ball
B_{\omega_{X}}(p_{0},r)
for
p_{0}\in Z
at time
s_{0}
and another ball
B_{\omega_{X}}(p_{1},r)
(
p_{1}\in Z
,
p_{1}\neq p_{0}
) at time
s_{1}>s_{0}
, then
\gamma_{t}(s)
does not hit
B(p_{0},r)
for any
s\geq s_{1}
, as long as
r
is small enough. This is because the boundary of the two balls are at positive distance with respect to
d_{t}
uniformly in
t
, so that (
5.5
) would allow to find a shorter path.

Therefore, we can find a partition
[0,1]=\bigsqcup_{k=0}^{2N}I_{k}
of intervals with
N\leq|Z|
such that

k\,\,\mbox{even}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\forall s\in I_{k},\gamma_{t}(s)\notin K.

We write
\bar{I}_{k}=[s_{k},s_{k+1}]
with
0=s_{0}<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{2N+1}
, and we set
x_{k}:=\gamma_{t}(s_{k})
. For any
1\leq k\leq N
, there exists
p_{k}\in Z
such that
x_{2k-1},x_{2k}\in\bar{B}_{\omega_{X}}(p_{k},r)
for some
p_{k}\in Z
.

Now, consider the concatenation of paths
\tilde{\gamma}_{t}:=\cup_{k=0}^{N}\gamma_{t}(I_{2k})
whose image is disjoint from
K
. We have

\displaystyle d_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ell_{\omega_{t}}(\tilde{\gamma}_{t})\geq(1-\varepsilon)\ell_{T}(\tilde{\gamma}_{t})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{k=0}^{N}d_{T}(x_{2k},x_{2k+1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\big(d_{T}(x,y)-\sum_{k=1}^{N}d_{T}(x_{2k-1},x_{2k})\big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)d_{T}(x,y)-(1-\varepsilon)N\varepsilon.

where we have used successively (
5.6
), the triangle inequality and (
5.5
).
As
\varepsilon
was arbitrary and
N\leq|Z|
, we get (
5.4
) for any
x,y\notin Z
.

Fix
x\in X\setminus Z
and
y\in Z
. We check that

d_{t}(x,y)\rightarrow d_{T}(x,y)
as
t\rightarrow 0
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
and some large constant
C
to be determined later. By (
5.5
) one can pick
z\in X\setminus Z
such that
Cd_{X}(y,z)^{\alpha}+d_{T}(y,z)<\varepsilon
.
Thus

\displaystyle\left|d_{T}(x,y)-d_{t}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|d_{T}(x,y)-d_{T}(x,z)\right|+\left|d_{T}(x,z)-d_{t}(x,z)\right|+\left|d_{t}(x,z)-d_{t}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{T}(y,z)+\left|d_{T}(x,z)-d_{t}(x,z)\right|+d_{t}(y,z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{T}(y,z)+\left|d_{T}(x,z)-d_{t}(x,z)\right|+Cd_{X}(y,z)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon+\left|d_{T}(x,z)-d_{t}(x,z)\right|,

where we have used Theorem
2.4
. The convergence follows from the previous step.

When
x,y\in Z
we approximate
x
by
x^{\prime}\in X\setminus Z
and use the same argument and the previous step to show that

d_{t}(x,y)\rightarrow d_{T}(x,y)
. The proof is complete.

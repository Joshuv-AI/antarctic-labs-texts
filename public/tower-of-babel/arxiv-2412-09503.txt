AdS amplitudes as CFT correlators
Máximo Bañados, Ernesto Bianchi, Iván Muñoz, Kostas Skenderis

Abstract
We show that AdS amplitudes are CFT correlators to all orders in the loop expansion by showing that they obey the conformal Ward identities. In particular, we provide explicit formulas for the constants and functions of cross-ratios that determine the CFT correlators in terms of bulk data.



1
Introduction

In a theory of quantum gravity there are no bulk local invariants (because diffeomorpshims act on spacetime points). In spacetimes with asymptotia, we need to impose boundary conditions at infinity, and one may define local operators at the (conformal) boundary via the boundary conditions. For example, one may require that a bulk scalar field takes a prescribed value at the boundary. The gravitational path-integral
computed with such boundary conditions would then compute observables that depends on boundary points. Such observables may be organised according to their transformation properties under the asymptotic symmetry group, the group of transformations that preserves the boundary conditions.

In the case of asymptotically (locally) AdS gravity, the boundary carries a conformal structure, so this construction naturally produces
n
-point functions, which we will call AdS amplitudes, that transform as CFT correlators. The AdS/CFT conjectures
[

To a large extent, the community takes for granted that AdS amplitudes are CFT correlators. It is the purpose of this note to provide an explicit proof that this is the case to all orders in bulk perturbation theory. We will discuss in detail the case the external operators are scalars, but all steps have a straightforward generalisation to spinning operators. It would be interesting to spell out all technical details but we leave this for future work.

CFT correlators

We review in this section the constraints of conformal invariance
on CFT correlation functions of primary operators. This is a topic with long history,
see
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
for some of the original literature and
[
32
,

,

,

]
for reviews.

Conformal transformations are diffeomorphisms that results in a Weyl transformation:
under
\vec{x}\rightarrow\vec{x}^{\,\prime}
,

\text{d}s^{2}\rightarrow\text{d}s^{\prime\,2}=\Omega^{2}(\vec{x})\text{d}s^{2}\,.

(1)

We work with Euclidean signature and coordinates
x^{\alpha}
,
\alpha=1,\ldots,d
. We will also use a vector notation,
\vec{x}=\{x^{\alpha}\}
.
In flat space, where
\text{d}s^{2}=\text{d}\vec{x}^{\,2}
, these are given by:

Poincare:

\displaystyle x^{\prime\alpha}=a^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}x^{\beta}+a^{\alpha}\,,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\Omega(\vec{x})=1\,,

(2)

Dilation:

\displaystyle x^{\prime\alpha}=\lambda x^{\alpha}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\Omega(\vec{x})=\lambda\,,

(3)

Inversion:

\displaystyle x^{\prime\alpha}=\frac{x^{\alpha}}{\vec{x}^{\,2}}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\Omega(\vec{x})=\frac{1}{\vec{x}^{\,2}}\,,

(4)

Special conformal:

\displaystyle x^{\prime\alpha}=\frac{x^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha}\vec{x}^{\,2}}{1+2\vec{b}\cdot\vec{x}+\vec{b}^{\,2}\vec{x}^{\,2}}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\Omega(\vec{x})=\frac{1}{1+2\vec{b}\cdot\vec{x}+\vec{b}^{\,2}\vec{x}^{\,2}}\,.

(5)

The factors
\Omega(\vec{x})
are related to the Jacobian
|\partial\vec{x}^{\,\prime}/\partial\vec{x}|
via

\Omega(\vec{x})=|\partial\vec{x}^{\prime}/\partial\vec{x}|^{1/d}
and
\partial x^{\prime\alpha}/\partial x^{\beta}=\Omega(\vec{x})R^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}(\vec{x})
, where
R^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}\in O(d)
is the orthogonal matrix:

R^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}(\vec{x})=\left\{a^{\alpha}_{\ \beta},\ \delta^{\alpha}_{\beta},\ I^{\alpha}_{\beta}(\vec{x}),\ I^{\alpha}_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\vec{x}}{\vec{x}^{\,2}}+\vec{b}\right)I^{\gamma}_{\beta}(\vec{x})\right\}\,,

(6)

for Poincare, dilations, inversions and special conformal transformation, correspondingly, and
\det R=\pm 1
with
-1
for inversions,
+1
for the rest, where

I^{\alpha}_{\beta}(\vec{x})=\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}-2\frac{x^{\alpha}x_{\beta}}{\vec{x}^{\,2}}\,.

(7)

One may check conformal transformations satisfy,

(\vec{x}^{\,\prime}_{1}-\vec{x}^{\,\prime}_{2})^{2}=\Omega(\vec{x}_{1})\Omega(\vec{x}_{2})(\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})^{2}\,.

(8)

and

I_{\alpha\beta}(\vec{x}_{1}^{\,\prime}-\vec{x}_{2}^{\,\prime})=R_{\alpha}^{\ \gamma}(\vec{x}_{1})R_{\beta}^{\ \delta}(\vec{x}_{2})I_{\gamma\delta}(\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})\,.

(9)

For concreteness, and to keep the technicalities to the minimum we will primarily focus on scalar operators, and we will briefly discuss spinning operator at the end of this section.

Scalar primary operators
\mathcal{O}
of dimension
\Delta
transform as

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(\vec{x}^{\,\prime})=\Omega(\vec{x})^{-\Delta}\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}),\qquad

(10)

and
n
-point functions should therefore satisfy

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}(\vec{x}_{1}^{\,\prime})\dotsm\mathcal{O}_{n}(\vec{x}_{n}^{\,\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\Omega(\vec{x}_{1})^{-\Delta_{1}}\dotsm\Omega(\vec{x}_{n})^{-\Delta_{n}}\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}(\vec{x}_{1})\dotsm\mathcal{O}_{n}(\vec{x}_{n})\rangle\,,

(11)

Following the presentation in
[
36
]
, the solution of (
11
) is given by

\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}(\vec{x}_{1})\ldots\mathcal{O}_{n}(\vec{x}_{n})\rangle=\frac{C_{n}(u_{ijkl})}{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x^{2}_{ij})^{\Delta^{(n)}_{ij}}},

(12)

where
x^{2}_{ij}\equiv|\vec{x}_{ij}|^{2}\equiv(\vec{x}_{i}-\vec{x}_{j})^{2}
and
the parameters
\Delta^{(n)}_{ij}
are related to the scaling dimensions by the relations,

\Delta_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Delta^{(n)}_{ij},\qquad i=1,2,\ldots,n\,,

(13)

where we have assumed without loss of generality that

\Delta^{(n)}_{ji}=\Delta^{(n)}_{ij},\Delta^{(n)}_{ii}=0
.

The functions
C_{n}(u_{ijkl})
are arbitrary functions of the conformal cross ratios,

u_{ijkl}=\frac{x_{ij}^{2}x_{kl}^{2}}{x_{ik}^{2}x_{jl}^{2}},\qquad i\neq j\neq k\neq l,\,.

(14)

These functions encode theory-specific information. Cross-ratios exist from 4-point function on, so
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
are constants. Not all cross ratios are independent. For instance:

u_{ijkl}=u_{jilk}=u_{klij}=u_{lkji}=\frac{1}{u_{ikjl}}=\frac{1}{u_{jlik}}=\frac{1}{u_{kilj}}=\frac{1}{u_{ljki}}\,,

(15)

and there are more relations involving product of cross ratios. A simple counting suggests there are
n(n-3)/2
independent cross-ratios (this is an over-counting when
n>d+2
, see for example
[
35
]
– this is not going to play a role here). One may choose the following combinations as independent
cross-ratios,

u_{i}=u_{123i}=\frac{x_{12}^{2}x_{3i}^{2}}{x_{13}^{2}x_{2i}^{2}}\,,\hskip 14.22636ptv_{i}=u_{321i}=\frac{x_{1i}^{2}x_{23}^{2}}{x_{13}^{2}x_{2i}^{2}}\,,\hskip 14.22636ptw_{ij}=u_{23ij}=\frac{x_{23}^{2}x_{ij}^{2}}{x_{2i}^{2}x_{3j}^{2}}\,,

(16)

where
i,j=4,\dotsc,n
and
i<j
.

Equation (
13
) is a set of
n
linear equations that may be used to determine
\Delta^{(n)}_{ij}

given
\Delta_{i}
. When
n=2
, we find

\Delta_{1}=\Delta^{(2)}_{12}=\Delta_{2},

(17)

encoding the fact that only operators with same dimension have non-vanishing 2-point functions. When
n=3
, the unique solution is

\Delta_{ij}^{(3)}=\Delta_{i}+\Delta_{j}-\frac{\Delta_{T}}{2},

(18)

where
\Delta_{T}
denotes the sum over all dimensions,
\Delta_{T}=\sum\Delta_{i}
.

For
n>3
there are more unknowns than equations:
\Delta_{ij}^{(n)}
is a symmetric hollow matrix (
i.e.
symmetric with zero in the diagonals) so it has

n(n-1)/2
independent matrix elements and we have
n
equations to satisfy. It follows that the solution of (
13
) is determined up to
n(n-3)/2
constants, which is precisely the number of cross-ratios. The general solution is given by

\Delta_{ij}^{(n)}=\hat{\Delta}_{ij}^{(n)}+\delta^{(n)}_{ij}

(19)

where

\hat{\Delta}_{ij}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{n-2}\left(\Delta_{i}+\Delta_{j}-\frac{\Delta_{T}}{n-1}\right),\qquad i<j\,.

(20)

is a particular solution and
\delta^{(n)}_{ij}
is a symmetric hollow matrix satisfying the homogeneous linear equations:

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta^{(n)}_{ij}=0,\qquad i=1,2,\ldots,n.

(21)

These equations may be solved by linearly expressing any
n
of the
n(n-1)/2
parameters
\delta^{(n)}_{ij}
in terms of the remaining
n(n-1)/2-n=n(n-3)/2
ones. For example, when
n=4
we may solve
\delta^{(4)}_{12},\delta^{(4)}_{23},\delta^{(4)}_{24},\delta^{(4)}_{34}
in terms of

\delta^{(4)}_{13}
and
\delta^{(4)}_{14}
:

\delta^{(4)}_{12}=\delta^{(4)}_{34}=-\delta^{(4)}_{13}-\delta^{(4)}_{14},\quad\delta^{(4)}_{23}=\delta^{(4)}_{14},\quad\delta^{(4)}_{24}=\delta^{(4)}_{13}\,.

(22)

Then

\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}(\vec{y}_{1})\ldots\mathcal{O}_{4}(\vec{y}_{4})\rangle=\frac{C_{4}(u_{4},v_{4})}{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 4}(x^{2}_{ij})^{\Delta^{(4)}_{ij}}}=\frac{\hat{C}_{4}(u_{4},v_{4})}{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 4}(x^{2}_{ij})^{\hat{\Delta}^{(4)}_{ij}}},

(23)

where
\hat{C}_{4}(u_{4},v_{4})=C_{4}(u_{4},v_{4})u_{4}^{\delta^{(4)}_{13}+\delta^{(4)}_{14}}v_{4}^{-\delta^{(4)}_{14}}
. Thus the freedom in the solution of (
13
)
just amounts to redefining the arbitrary function of cross-ratios. The same is true for any
n
. To have an unambiguous definition of the function of cross-ratios one needs to choose a solution of (
13
).

The formulas for spinning operators are similar but more involved. Here we will quote the results for the case of vector primaries as we will needed it later. Vector primaries
\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}
of dimension
\Delta
transform

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\vec{x}^{\,\prime})=\Omega^{-\Delta}(\vec{x})R_{\alpha}^{\ \beta}(\vec{x})\mathcal{J}_{\beta}(\vec{x})\,,

(24)

and this implies that
n
-point function should satisfy,

\langle\mathcal{J}^{1}_{\alpha_{1}}(\vec{x}_{1}^{\,\prime})\dotsm\mathcal{J}^{n}_{\alpha_{n}}(\vec{x}_{n}^{\,\prime})\rangle=\Omega(\vec{x}_{1})^{-\Delta_{1}}\dotsm\Omega(\vec{x}_{n})^{-\Delta_{n}}R_{\alpha_{1}}^{\ \beta_{1}}(\vec{x}_{1})\cdots R_{\alpha_{n}}^{\ \beta_{n}}(\vec{x}_{n})\langle\mathcal{J}^{1}_{\beta_{1}}(\vec{x}_{1})\dotsm\mathcal{J}^{n}_{\beta_{n}}(\vec{x}_{n})\rangle\,,

(25)

where
R_{\alpha}^{\ \beta}(\vec{x})
is given in (
6
).

3
AdS amplitudes

The objects of interest are AdS amplitudes, which may be computed via Witten diagrams.
The basic structure is well known
[
3
]
: a Witten diagram for an
n
-point function is constructed by
n
bulk-to-boundary propagators which are linked to a number of bulk-to-bulk propagators connected via bulk vertices, which are integrated over all of AdS.

As just reviewed, conformal invariance fixes the form of 2-point and 3-point functions, up to a number of constants, and the form of higher-point functions up to a functions of cross-ratios. The constants and the function of cross-ratios depend on the specific CFT but the form of the correlators is independent of it. In AdS/CFT correspondence the AdS isometries play the role of conformal transformations, so one should be able to establish the same results using AdS isometries only. We will show that this is indeed the case, and along the way we will also show the relation of the arbitrary constants and functions of cross-ratios with bulk quantities. We will establish this result to all orders in bulk perturbation theory and for scalar correlators. We will discuss the generalisation to general spinning operators afterwards.

3.1
AdS isometries as constrained conformal transformations

We work in Euclidean signature and use coordinates where AdS metric is given by

\text{d}s^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}\text{d}x^{\mu}\text{d}x^{\nu}=\frac{\ell^{2}}{z^{2}}(\text{d}z^{2}+\text{d}\vec{x}^{\,2})=\ell^{2}\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}\text{d}x^{\mu}\text{d}x^{\nu}}{z^{2}},

(26)

where
\ell
is the AdS radius (set to 1 in the paper). The conformal boundary is at
z=0
and this is the place where we need to impose boundary conditions. We will denote bulk point by
x^{\mu}=(z,x^{\alpha})=(z,\vec{x})
, where
\mu=0,1,\ldots,d
is a bulk index,
\alpha=1,2,\ldots,d
is a boundary index and
x^{0}=z
is the radial coordinate.

It is well known that the AdS metric is invariant under the following transformations (AdS isometries),

\displaystyle z^{\prime}=z\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle x^{\prime\alpha}=a^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}x^{\beta}+a^{\alpha}\

\displaystyle\rightarrow\text{Poincare}\,,

(27)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}=\lambda z\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle x^{\prime\alpha}=\lambda x^{\alpha}\

\displaystyle\rightarrow\text{Dilation}\,,

(28)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}=\frac{z}{(z^{2}+\vec{x}^{\,2})}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle x^{\prime\alpha}=\frac{x^{\alpha}}{(z^{2}+\vec{x}^{\,2})}\

\displaystyle\rightarrow\text{Inversion}\,,

(29)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}=\frac{z}{1+2\vec{b}\cdot\vec{x}+\vec{b}^{\,2}(z^{2}+\vec{x}^{\,2})}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle x^{\prime\alpha}=\frac{x^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha}(z^{2}+\vec{x}^{\,2})}{1+2\vec{b}\cdot\vec{x}+\vec{b}^{\,2}(z^{2}+\vec{x}^{\,2})}\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\rightarrow\text{Special conformal}

(30)

where we have also indicated the conformal transformation they limit to at the conformal boundary as
z\to 0
.

It is less known that these transformations can be thought of as constrained flat-space
(d+1)
-dimensional conformal transformations. We will denote these transformations as in (
2
)-(
5
) but with the parameters carrying a tilde (and the indices being
(d+1
-dimensional indices):
a^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\to\tilde{a}^{\mu}{}_{\nu},a^{\alpha}\to\tilde{a}^{\mu},\lambda\to\tilde{\lambda},b^{\alpha}\to\tilde{b}^{\mu}
. Under such transformations

\delta_{\mu\nu}\text{d}x^{\prime\mu}\text{d}x^{\prime\nu}=\tilde{\Omega}(x)^{2}\delta_{\mu\nu}\text{d}x^{\mu}\text{d}x^{\nu},

(31)

where
\tilde{\Omega}(x)
is
(d+1)
version of
\Omega(\vec{x})
in (
2
)-(
5
).
For these conformal transformations to be AdS isometries the transformation of
z
must cancel the factor of
\tilde{\Omega}
:

z^{\prime}=\tilde{\Omega}(x)z\,.

(32)

This is indeed satisfied if we impose:

\tilde{a}^{z}_{\ \nu}=\delta^{z}_{\nu},\qquad\tilde{a}^{\nu}_{\ z}=\delta^{\nu}_{z},\qquad\tilde{a}^{z}=0,\qquad\tilde{b}^{z}=0\,.

(33)

Thus, altogether and after dropping the tildes we obtain

\displaystyle x^{\prime\mu}

\displaystyle=a^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}+a^{\mu}\,,\qquad{\rm with}\ a^{z}_{\ \nu}=\delta^{z}_{\nu},\quad a^{\mu}_{\ z}=\delta^{\mu}_{z},\quad a^{z}=0\,,

(34)

\displaystyle x^{\prime\mu}

\displaystyle=\lambda x^{\mu}\,,

(35)

\displaystyle x^{\prime\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{\mu}}{x^{\,2}}\,,\,

(36)

\displaystyle x^{\prime\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{\mu}+b^{\mu}x^{\,2}}{1+2b\cdot x+b^{\,2}x^{\,2}}\,,\qquad{\rm with}\ b^{z}=0\,,

(37)

where
x^{2}=\delta_{\mu\nu}x^{\mu}x^{\nu}=z^{2}+\vec{x}^{\,2}
,
b\cdot x=\delta_{\mu\nu}b^{\mu}x^{\nu}=\vec{b}\cdot\vec{x}
. One may readily check that
(
34
)-(
37
) agree with (
27
)-(
30
).

The advantage of viewing AdS isometries as constrained conformal transformations is that we can immediately inherit all CFT properties that are independent of specific rotations
a^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
or translations
a^{\mu},b^{\mu}
. For instance, the Jacobian of AdS isometries can be immediately obtained:

\frac{\partial x^{\prime\mu}}{\partial x^{\nu}}=\tilde{\Omega}(x)\tilde{R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}(x)\,,

(38)

where
\tilde{\Omega}
(the one from
z^{\prime}=\tilde{\Omega}z
) and
\tilde{R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\in O(d+1)
are those of a CFT for constrained rotations and translations:

Constrained Poincare:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}(x)=1\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}(x)=a^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\,,\hskip 14.22636pta^{z}_{\ \nu}=\delta^{z}_{\nu}\,,\ a^{\mu}_{\ z}=\delta^{\mu}_{z}\,,

(39)

Dilation:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}(x)=\lambda\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}(x)=\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\,,

(40)

Inversion:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}(x)=\frac{1}{x^{2}}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}(x)=I^{\mu}_{\nu}(x)=\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}-2\frac{x^{\mu}x_{\nu}}{x^{2}}\,,

(41)

Constrained SCT:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}(x)=\frac{1}{1+2\vec{b}\cdot\vec{x}+\vec{b}^{\,2}x^{2}}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}(x)=I^{\mu}_{\rho}\left(\frac{x}{x^{2}}+\vec{b}\right)I^{\rho}_{\nu}(x)\,.

(42)

This implies for example that the inversion property of AdS is inherited from that of flat space:

(x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}^{\prime})^{2}=\tilde{\Omega}(x_{1})\tilde{\Omega}(x_{2})(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}\,,

(43)

and

I_{\mu\nu}(x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}^{\prime})=\tilde{R}_{\mu}^{\ \rho}(x_{1})\tilde{R}_{\nu}^{\ \sigma}(x_{2})I_{\rho\sigma}(x_{1}-x_{2})\,.

(44)

We can further obtain useful formulas by taking the limit of bulk points to the boundary. In this limit,
\tilde{\Omega}
and the boundary components of
\tilde{R}^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
reduce to those of an unconstrained CFT in
d
dimensions:

\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\ \tilde{\Omega}(x)=\Omega(\vec{x})\,,\hskip 14.22636pt\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\ \tilde{R}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}(x)=R^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}(\vec{x})\,,

(45)

and thus one also recovers the Jacobian:

\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\ \frac{\partial x^{\prime\alpha}}{\partial x^{\beta}}=\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\ \tilde{\Omega}(x)\tilde{R}^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}(x)=\Omega(\vec{x})R^{\alpha}_{\ \beta}(\vec{x})\,.

(46)

When one of the points in (
43
) and (
44
) are taken to the boundary, one obtains the useful relations:

(x^{\prime}-\vec{y}^{\,\prime})^{2}=\tilde{\Omega}(x)\Omega(\vec{y})(x-\vec{y})^{2}\,,

(47)

and:

I_{\mu\alpha}(x^{\prime}-\vec{y}^{\,\prime})=\tilde{R}_{\mu}^{\ \nu}(x)R_{\alpha}^{\ \beta}(\vec{y})I_{\nu\beta}(x-\vec{y})\,.

(48)

3.2
AdS propagators

The bulk-to-boundary propagator for a bulk field dual to an operator of dimension
\Delta
is the regular solution of the bulk equation

(-\Box_{g}+m^{2})K_{\Delta}(x_{1},\vec{x}_{2})=0

(49)

where
m^{2}=\Delta(\Delta-d)
and it is given by

K_{\Delta}(x_{1},\vec{x}_{2})=c_{\Delta}\left(\frac{z_{1}}{(x_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\Delta},\qquad c_{\Delta}=\frac{\Gamma(\Delta)}{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\left(\Delta-\frac{d}{2}\right)}.

(50)

where
(x_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})^{2}=z_{1}^{2}+(\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})^{2}
. This is normalized such that as we approach the AdS boundary the propagator tends to a delta function,

\displaystyle\lim_{z_{1}\to 0}K_{\Delta}(x_{1},\vec{x}_{2})\rightarrow z_{1}^{d-\Delta}\delta(\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})\,.

(51)

The bulk-to-boundary propagator
K_{\Delta}(x_{1},\vec{x}_{2})
transforms as a CFT primary field of dimension
\Delta
at
\vec{x}_{2}
under the AdS isometries (
27
)-(
30
) acting simultaneously on
x_{1}
and
\vec{x}_{2}
:

K_{\Delta}(x_{1}^{\prime},\vec{x}_{2}^{\,\prime})=\Omega(\vec{x}_{2})^{-\Delta}K_{\Delta}(x_{1},\vec{x}_{2})\,,

(52)

where the factors of
\Omega
are those given in (
2
)-(
5
).
This is most easily shown using the perspective of the AdS isometries as constrained conformal transformations. Indeed, using (
32
) and (
47
) we obtain

K_{\Delta}(x_{1}^{\prime},\vec{x}_{2}^{\,\prime})=c_{\Delta}\left(\frac{z^{\prime}_{1}}{(x^{\prime}_{1}-\vec{x}^{\,\prime}_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\Delta}=c_{\Delta}\left(\frac{\tilde{\Omega}(x_{1})z_{1}}{\tilde{\Omega}(x_{1})\Omega(\vec{x}_{2})(x_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\Delta}=\Omega(\vec{x}_{2})^{-\Delta}K_{\Delta}(x_{1},\vec{x}_{2})

(53)

We will also explain in section
4
that this transformation rule follows from bulk diffeomorphism invariance.

The bulk-to-bulk propagator for the same field is the regular solution of
the equation

(-\Box_{g}+m^{2})G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\delta(x_{1}-x_{2})\,,

(54)

with normalizable behavior at infinity,
G_{\Delta}\sim z_{1}^{\Delta}
as
x_{1}
approaches the conformal boundary (and
\sim z_{2}^{\Delta}
when
x_{2}
approaches the conformal boundary). AdS invariance implies that the propagator is a function of an AdS invariant distance, which we may take to be the chordal distance,

\xi=\frac{2z_{1}z_{2}}{z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}+(\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})^{2}}\,.

(55)

The invariance of the chordal distance under transformations (
27
)-(
30
) (or equivalently (
34
)-(
37
)) that act simultaneously on both
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
follows by inspection upon use of (
32
) and (
43
). By explicit computation,

G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{2^{-\Delta}c_{\Delta}}{2\Delta-d}\xi^{\Delta}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\Delta}{2},\frac{\Delta+1}{2},\Delta-\frac{d}{2}+1;\xi^{2}\right)\,.

(56)

It follows that

G_{\Delta}(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})=G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})\,.

(57)

One may similarly obtain the transformation properties for propagators of spinning fields. We report here the result for the bulk-to-boundary propagator of an (Abelian) gauge field, as this is a case we discuss later. The bulk-to-boundary propagator has been obtained in the early AdS/CFT literature
[
37
]
. Up to gauge transformations it is given by

K_{\mu\alpha}(x_{1},\vec{x}_{2})=C\frac{z_{1}^{d-2}}{[(x_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})^{2}]^{d-1}}I_{\mu\alpha}(x_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})\,,

(58)

where
C
is a constant and
I_{\mu\alpha}
the inversion tensor, with
\mu
and
\alpha
bulk and boundary indices, respectively. Using (
32
), (
47
) and (
48
) one may work out how this bulk-to-boundary propagator transforms under bulk isometries:

\displaystyle K_{\mu\alpha}(x_{1}^{\prime},\vec{x}^{\,\prime}_{2})

\displaystyle=C\frac{z_{1}^{\prime d-2}}{\left[(x_{1}^{\prime}-\vec{x}^{\,\prime}_{2})^{2}\right]^{d-1}}I_{\mu\alpha}(x_{1}^{\prime}-\vec{x}_{2}^{\,\prime})

3.4
Regularization and renormalization

We have just shown that AdS amplitudes can be brought to a form that manifestly satisfied the CFT Ward identities by a sequence of steps that involved changing integration variables amounting to AdS isometries. Such manipulations are well-posed if the integrals are finite. However, the integrals may diverge both in the UV and the IR. The UV divergences come from bulk loops, while the IR divergences are due to the infinite volume of AdS. The bulk IR divergences correspond to CFT UV divergences via the AdS/CFT correspondence.

One can regulate the UV divergences in a way that preserves the AdS
invariance. This is expected as bulk UV divergences are mapped by the AdS/CFT correspondence to IR divergences in the CFT. Such divergences should cancel on their own and they should not lead to breaking of the conformal symmetry.
The regulator amounts to separating (bulk) coincident points along a geodesic by affine distance
\tau

[
28
]
, which thus acts an UV regulator. This results to modifying the argument
of the bulk-to-bulk propagator by changing
\xi\to\xi/\cosh\tau
in (
56
), recovering the prescription in
[
19
,
18
]
. We will discuss in detail this regulator in
[
38
]
, where we will also show that it can be derived from a regulated action. Since the regulated theory is invariant under AdS isometries, all steps outlined above are valid in the regulated theory. The discussion in these papers is about bulk scalar propagators, but we expect the results to extend to general tensorial fields (the metric, gauge fields, antisymmetric tensor fields, etc.).

The issues with the IR divergences is more subtle. These correspond to UV divergences in the dual CFT and such divergences give rise to anomalous dimensions and conformal anomalies, and thus the breaking of conformal symmetry is inevitable. One can regulate the IR divergence by imposing an explicit IR cut-off,
z\geq\varepsilon
, as in the original works on holographic renormalization
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
. In this case the explicit cut-off results into additional terms when one follows the manipulations described above. This has already been discussed in
[
28
]
and we will discuss in detail how to compute such integrals in
[
38
]
. The results is that the AdS amplitudes still take the form of the CFT correlators but now the dimensions may renormalize (there are anomalous dimenions) and conformal anomalies appear.

An alternative approach is to use dimensional regularization, where the spacetime dimension
d
and dimensions
\Delta_{i}
of operators are shifted as in
[
42
]
. For generic values of

\Delta_{i}
the correlators may be defined by analytic continuation. For such cases the analysis above holds unchanged. However, there are also cases where genuine singularities appear and boundary counterterms and renormalization is needed
[
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
]
. In the case where both UV and IR issues are present one would need to renormalize the parameters in the bulk action (masses and coupling constants), the fields that specify the boundary conditions (sources of the dual operators) and add appropriate boundary counterterms, as discussed in
[
28
,
38
]
.

5
Conclusions

We have shown that AdS amplitudes satisfy the conformal Ward identities and we obtained explicit formulas that compute the constants and functions of cross-ratios that appear in the CFT correlators in terms of bulk quantities. These are given in
(
66
), (
74
), (
3.3
), (
3.3
) for scalar
n
-point functions. The same analysis can be carried out for spinning operators and we worked out explicitly the case of conserved currents. The constraints of conformal invariance originate from diffeomorphsim invariance in the bulk.

Altogether these results imply that the AdS gravity is a CFT, but they do not yet imply that it is a local CFT. Local CFTs have local UV divergences, and thus the corresponding bulk IR divergences should also be local. This has been established at tree-level in
[
39
,
40
,
48
,
43
]
and for scalar fields in AdS up to two loops in
[
28
,
38
]
. In addition, the conformal anomalies should be that of a local CFT, and they are
[
39
,
40
]
. One should contrast these results with the case of de Sitter, where the bulk isometries also match that of (Euclidean) CFT. The Ward identities due to de Sitter isometries also take the form of conformal Ward identities, but the IR divergences of de Sitter in-in correlators and corresponding anomalies only partially match that of a local CFT
[
49
]
. Local CFTs are further constrained by OPEs. These may be used to express 4- and higher-point functions in terms of CFT data: conformal dimensions (encoded in 2-point functions) and OPE coefficients (encoded in 3-point functions, and these should satisfy bootstrap equations. We note that the functions of cross-ratios that appear in our analysis have special dependence on some of the cross-ratios (see comments below (
82
) and (
89
)) and it would be interesting to understand the implication of this in the context of the bootstrap program.

The connection between CFT correlators and AdS amplitudes depends on the amputated bulk correlators transforming properly under bulk diffeomorphism. Such tranformation properties could be invalidated by UV and/or IR divergences. We used an AdS invariant regulator to ensure that UV issues do not cause any problems, but full details have only been worked out for scalar fields. It would be interesting to work out the regularised bulk-to-bulk propagators for general spinning field. At loop order and for gauge field the analysis would likely require to properly take into account the contribution of ghost fields. IR divergences do break (part of) the AdS isometries, but this breaking is linked to conformal anomalies and anomalous dimensions and it is a feature, not a problem.
We also note any AdS covariant
n
-point function, irrespectively of how it is obtained would automatically yield a solution of the CFT Ward identities – here we assumed they are computed by bulk perturbation theory, but
a priori
there could be other non-perturbative constructions.

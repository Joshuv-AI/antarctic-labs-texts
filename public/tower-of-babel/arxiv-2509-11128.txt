ENJ: OPTIMIZING NOISE WITH GENETIC ALGORITHMS TO JAILBREAK LSMs
Yibo Zhang, Liang Lin \sthanks Corresponding author: linliang@iie.ac.cn

Abstract
The widespread application of Large Speech Models (LSMs) has made their security risks increasingly prominent. Traditional speech adversarial attack methods face challenges in balancing effectiveness and stealth. This paper proposes Evolutionary Noise Jailbreak (ENJ), which utilizes a genetic algorithm to transform environmental noise from a passive interference into an actively optimizable attack carrier for jailbreaking LSMs. Through operations such as population initialization, crossover fusion, and probabilistic mutation, this method iteratively evolves a series of audio samples that fuse malicious instructions with background noise. These samples sound like harmless noise to humans but can induce the model to parse and execute harmful commands. Extensive experiments on multiple mainstream speech models show that ENJ’s attack effectiveness is significantly superior to existing baseline methods. This research reveals the dual role of noise in speech security and provides new critical insights for model security defense in complex acoustic environments.

Introduction

Driven by deep learning and large-scale data, Large-scale Speech Models (LSMs) have made remarkable progress, profoundly changing the way of human - computer interaction. As these models become increasingly capable and widely used in voice control systems, the security risks they expose are in urgent need of in-depth examination

In jailbreaking attacks, attackers aim to construct specific inputs to induce the model to bypass its built - in security protection mechanisms and execute harmful instructions while keeping the output semantically understandable

. In real-world deployment scenarios, LSMs often have to operate in acoustically complex environments, where the target speech is often mixed with environmental background noise (such as street noise, electrical hum)

. This common acoustic mixing phenomenon brings significant challenges. Environmental background noise is usually regarded as harmless interference, but it can be used strategically. Malicious actors can seamlessly integrate malicious intentions with the environmental background, forming a powerful and realistic attack approach, which has not received enough attention in existing defense measures.

Current research on jailbreaking large speech models remains limited. Text-domain techniques (e.g., context learning
[
8
]
) are unsuitable for audio, as acoustic perturbations easily disrupt the semantic coherence they rely on. Traditional speech adversarial attacks primarily target Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems
[
9
]
, and although these methods can bypass basic defenses temporarily by optimizing subtle perturbations, they face a dilemma: prioritizing semantic fidelity exposes the attack, whereas sacrificing it compromises the malicious instruction’s parsability, causing attack failure. Crucially, prior work treats environmental noise merely as passive interference that degrades model performance, neglecting its potential as an actively optimizable carrier for covert instructions—a key oversight for real-world attacks.

To break through the inherent contradiction between concealment and effectiveness in traditional attacks, this paper proposes ENJ (Evolutionary Noise Jailbreak), which for the first time transforms environmental noise into a dynamically optimizable attack carrier. Different from traditional methods that rely on fixed rules, ENJ designs an iterative optimization process: In the initial stage, a variety of candidate samples are generated by mixing malicious voice instructions with real environmental noise; In each round of optimization, through progressive adjustments (such as retaining samples with high harmful scores, and reorganizing different noise fragments), the system gradually improves the acoustic coordination between noise and instructions, and finally generates camouflaged audio that is difficult for humans to detect but can be accurately parsed by the speech model. Through this approach, we demonstrate how environmental noise can be actively engineered from passive interference into a precisely controllable attack tool, effectively balancing semantic understanding with acoustic concealment.

2
THE DESIGN OF ENJ

Overview:This method generates adversarial audio samples by simulating biological evolution mechanisms, enabling speech large models to bypass security protections and execute harmful instructions while maintaining semantic comprehensibility. As shown in Figure 1, the attack process consists of four key stages: ① linearly mixing harmful speech with environmental noise to construct initial audio samples with attack potential; ② optimizing harmful audio samples through elite selection, cross-fusion, and probabilistic mutation operations; ③ cyclically executing evolutionary operations until the termination condition is met; ④ evaluating and outputting the sample with the highest harmful score as the final attack sample.

2.1
Population Initialization

Population initialization is an important step in constructing the attack sample library. Its goal is to create an initial population with attack potential by fusing environmental noise and harmful speech. This experiment uses 33 types of real-world environmental noises, covering common scene noises such as keyboard typing sounds, traffic background sounds, and crowd noises. These noise samples undergo strict screening and preprocessing, including 16kHz resampling, amplitude normalization, and band-pass filtering, ensuring that while retaining the energy of the main frequency band of speech, spectral diversity is provided.

In specific implementation, given the target harmful audio signal
A_{\text{harm}}
(sampling rate 16kHz, duration
T
seconds), a linear mixing operation is performed:

A_{i}^{(0)}=\alpha_{i}\cdot A_{\text{harm}}+(1-\alpha_{i})\cdot N_{k}

where
\alpha_{i}\in[0.4,0.6]
is a dynamic speech intensity factor. This value range has been verified by experiments to balance semantic intelligibility and interference effects: when
\alpha<0.4
, the noise is too strong, leading to semantic loss; when
\alpha>0.6
, the interference effect is insufficient.

To ensure speech integrity, a time-domain alignment technique is adopted:
A_{\text{harm}}
and
N_{k}
are extended to the same length
M=\max(\text{len}(A_{\text{harm}}),\text{len}(N_{k}))
through zero-padding. This method avoids truncation distortion caused by differences in signal duration and ensures uniform distribution of noise energy in the time domain. Finally, 33 initial samples (capacity of the noise library) are generated, forming an attack gene material pool with rich spectral characteristics, laying a foundation for subsequent genetic evolution.

2.2
Crossover Fusion

The crossover fusion operation is a core step in population evolution, aiming to explore new attack possibilities by recombining high-quality jailbreak features. In each generation of evolution, the top 50% elite subset
\text{E}^{(t)}
is first selected based on harmful scores to form a high-quality gene library. These elite individuals represent the audio samples with the greatest current attack potential, and their noise combinations demonstrate the best interference characteristics against speech models.

In specific operations, an adaptive linear interpolation strategy is used to generate new offspring:

A_{\text{child}}=\beta\cdot A_{p}+(1-\beta)\cdot A_{q}

Here,
A_{p}
and
A_{q}
are randomly selected from the elite population. The dynamic weight factor
\beta\in[0.2,0.7]
ensures that the offspring inherits the noise feature distributions of both parents in a balanced manner. This range is designed to avoid information loss caused by extreme weight values.

2.3
Probability Mutation

The probability mutation operation breaks through the limitation of local optimal solutions and enhances global search capabilities. Directed noise injection is triggered with a probability of
p_{m}=0.3
:

A_{\text{mut}}=(1-\gamma)A_{\text{child}}+\gamma N_{r}

where
\gamma=0.1
is the perturbation intensity coefficient, and
N_{r}
is a new noise sample randomly selected from the noise library. This operation avoids the evolution from getting trapped in local optima by introducing randomness, while maintaining controllable perturbations to the original speech structure.

2.4
Harmfulness Evaluation Mechanism

In the safety scoring stage, the transcribed text
\mathcal{T}
and the original harmful instruction
P_{\text{harm}}
are input into the safety evaluation system, which generates a risk score through multi-dimensional analysis:

HS=\Phi(\mathcal{T},P_{\text{harm}})

The final score strictly follows a five-level standard:
HS=5
indicates fully executing harmful instructions,
HS=4
indicates containing explicit prohibited content, and
HS\leq 3
indicates a safe response.

This scoring mechanism balances semantic and syntactic features through dynamic weights, ensuring consistency between evaluation results and human ethical judgments. When
HS=5
is detected, an early stopping mechanism is triggered to terminate the current evolutionary branch and improve computational efficiency.

3.2
Main Results

The experimental results, as detailed in Table 1, unequivocally demonstrate the superior performance of our proposed ENJ method over existing audio-domain jailbreaking baselines (SSJ and BoN) across all evaluated models. ENJ achieves a notably high average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 95% and an average Harmfulness Score (HS) of 4.74. This performance significantly surpasses the best audio-domain baseline by a large margin of +43.5% in ASR and +1.27 in HS, underscoring the profound advantage of our evolutionary noise fusion strategy. Furthermore, while the text-domain baseline CodeAttack demonstrated a non-trivial ASR, both text-based methods (AdaPPA and CodeAttack) were consistently and substantially outperformed by ENJ, highlighting the unique potency of operating directly within the acoustic domain. These results collectively indicate that our approach not only guarantees the semantic coherence of adversarial samples but also effectively bypasses security mechanisms, thereby constituting a substantial and practical threat.

3.3
Model Preferences

As shown in Figure 2, experimental results reveal that different speech large language models exhibit significant specific preferences when facing adversarial noise attacks, reflecting inherent differences in their internal audio processing and security defense mechanisms.

For DiVA, a particular susceptibility to specific environmental life noises (such as toilet, sea waves, and rain sounds) was observed, especially showing sensitivity to continuous environmental noises, while demonstrating relatively stronger resistance to rhythmic noises. The MiniCPM model, on the other hand, displayed a preference for rhythmic noises (such as drumbeats) and human voices with emotional tones (like laughter), with impactful sounds such as gunshots also proving effective. For Qwen-Audio, its defense mechanism showed obvious weaknesses against rhythmic noise attacks, with drumbeats, bird songs, clock ticks, and similar sounds becoming the dominant noise sources for jailbreak attacks. In contrast, the Qwen2-Audio model showed improvements in security mechanisms compared to its predecessor (Qwen-Audio), but still exhibited a specific preference for regular noises. Clock ticks and bird songs, with 22 and 15 successful attacks respectively, emerged as the two most effective noise types, indicating that the model remains susceptible to sounds with stable and regular rhythms (such as clock ticks). It is worth noting that Qwen2-Audio appears to demonstrate relatively stronger resistance against more structurally complex or combined noise attacks.

4
CONCLUSION

This paper proposes ENJ, a genetic algorithm framework that fuses malicious instructions with environmental noise, optimizing them into a highly effective and covert attack sample for jailbreaking large speech models. Our approach effectively resolves the inherent trade-off between semantic fidelity and acoustic stealth in traditional adversarial attacks. The high attack success rate demonstrates that this fusion, once strategically optimized, poses a severe and practical threat to LSM security. This work highlights the critical need for developing robust defenses specifically designed to counter such adaptive attacks in complex acoustic environments.

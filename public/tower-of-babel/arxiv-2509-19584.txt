One-sided Fractional Integrals and Riesz Potentials on a Spherical Cap
B. Rubin

Abstract
. We introduce fractional integrals on the n n -dimensional spherical cap, study their boundednes in weighted L p L^{p} spaces and obtain explicit inversion formulas. The results are applied to the inversion problem for Riesz potentials on a spherical cap. In the case n = 2 n=2 , this problem arises in electrostatics, elasticity, and some other applied areas. The main tools are stereographic projection, hypersingular integrals of the Marchaud type, Poisson integrals, and spherical harmonic decompositions.

(I^{\alpha}_{\Omega}\varphi)(x)=c_{n,\alpha}\!\int\limits_{\Omega}\!|t\!-\!\tau|^{\alpha-n}\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau,\quad c_{n,\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma((n\!-\!\alpha)/2)}{2^{\alpha}\pi^{n/2}\Gamma(\alpha/2)},

[
2

An explicit inversion formula in the asymmetric case for

The main tool in all these works is factorization of Riesz potentials in terms of the Riemann-Liouville (or Abel) fractional integrals and their modifications. In the simplest form, these integrals are defined by the formulas

(I^{\alpha}_{a+}\psi)(t)\!=\!\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits^{t}_{a}\!\frac{\psi(\tau)\,d\tau}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}},\quad(I^{\alpha}_{b-}\psi)(t)\!=\!\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits_{t}^{b}\!\frac{\varphi(\tau)\,d\tau}{(\tau-t)^{1-\alpha}};

(1.2)

-\infty\leq a<t<b\leq\infty,\qquad\Gamma(\alpha)=\int\limits^{\infty}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}e^{-t}dt.

The integrals

and

and

[

2.4.
Stereographic Projection

The main results for fractional integrals on the unit sphere will be derived from those on the Euclidean ball. Below we prepare the background for this transition. We regard the unit sphere
{{S}}^{n}
as a set of the form

S^{n}=((0,\pi)\times S^{n-1})\cup\{e_{n+1}\}\cup\{-e_{n+1}\},

(2.22)

where
\pm e_{n+1}=(0,\ldots,0,\pm 1)
. Then every point
x\in{{S}}^{n}\setminus\{\pm e_{n+1}\}
can be identified with the pair

x=(\theta,\xi^{\prime}),\qquad 0<\theta<\pi,\quad\xi^{\prime}\in S^{n-1}.

(2.23)

Similarly we set
{{R}}^{n}=({{R}}_{+}\times S^{n-1})\cup\{0\}
, so that for every
\xi\in{{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

\xi=(r,\xi^{\prime})
,

be the one-point compactification of

Consider the stereographic projection

(2.24)

-e_{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
,
e_{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\infty

For functions

and

(Pf)(\xi)=f(\gamma^{-1}\xi),\qquad(P^{-1}g)(x)=g(\gamma x),

\xi\neq 0,\infty

(2.26)

If
x\neq\pm e_{n+1}

(P^{-1}g)(x)=(P^{-1}g)(\theta,\xi^{\prime})=g\left(\tan\frac{\theta}{2},\xi^{\prime}\right).

(2.27)

The following relations hold (cf.

, pp. 35-36]

|x-y|=\frac{2|\xi-\eta|}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}\,(1+|\eta|^{2})^{1/2}};\qquad\xi=\gamma x,\;\eta=\gamma y;

1+\tan^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}=1+|\xi|^{2}=\frac{2}{1-x_{n+1}};

(2.29)

1-x_{n+1}=\frac{2}{1+|\xi|^{2}},\qquad 1+x_{n+1}=\frac{2|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}};

(2.30)

\tan^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}=|\xi|^{2}=\frac{1+x_{n+1}}{1-x_{n+1}};

(2.31)

dx=\frac{2^{n}\,d\xi}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{n}};\qquad d\xi=\frac{dx}{(1-x_{n+1})^{n}}.

(2.32)

We will be using the following notations for the multiplication operators:

\displaystyle t_{\lambda}=(1-x_{n+1})^{\lambda}I;\qquad q_{\lambda}=(1-x^{2}_{n+1})^{\lambda/2}I;

\displaystyle r_{\lambda}=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\lambda/2}I;\qquad\tau_{\lambda}=|\xi|^{\lambda}I.

3.
One-sided Fractional Integrals and Factorization of Riesz Potentials on the Sphere

In this section we introduce one-sided spherical fractional integrals and investigate their properties in weighted
L^{p}
-spaces. By making use of the stereographic projection, we establish connection
between these integrals and the ball integrals (
2.3
), (
2.4
). A factorization formula for the spherical Riesz potentials
I^{\alpha}_{S}\varphi
in terms of the one-sided spherical integrals is obtained. This formula will play a key role in Section
5
for inversion of Riesz potentials on spherical caps.

3.1.
Definitions and Connections

Given a point
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})
in
{{S}}^{n}
, consider the spherical segments

\displaystyle\langle a,x_{n+1}\rangle_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{y\in S^{n}:a<y_{n+1}<x_{n+1}\},

\displaystyle\langle x_{n+1},b\rangle_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{y\in S^{n}:x_{n+1}<y_{n+1}<b\},

where
-1\leq a<b\leq 1
.
We define the left-sided and right-sided spherical fractional integrals of order
\alpha>0
by the formulas

(S^{\alpha}_{a+}\varphi)(x)=\frac{2}{\Gamma(\alpha)\,\sigma_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\langle a,x_{n+1}\rangle_{S}}\frac{(x_{n+1}-y_{n+1})^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{n}}\varphi(y)\,dy,

(3.1)

(S^{\alpha}_{b-}\varphi)(x)=\frac{2}{\Gamma(\alpha)\,\sigma_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\langle x_{n+1},b\rangle_{S}}\frac{(y_{n+1}-x_{n+1})^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{n}}\varphi(y)\,dy,

(3.2)

and denote
S^{\alpha}_{+}=S^{a}_{-1+}
,
S^{\alpha}_{-}=S^{a}_{1-}
. Clearly,

S^{\alpha}_{\pm}\varphi=AS^{\alpha}_{\mp}A\varphi,\quad(A\varphi)(x)=\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},-x_{n+1}).

(3.3)

The stereographic projection (
2.25
) establishes a remarkable connection between these integrals and the corresponding ball fractional integrals (
2.3
), (
2.4
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
t_{\lambda}=(1-x_{n+1})^{\lambda}I
. We have

\displaystyle B^{\alpha}_{\pm}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{\alpha}\,P\,t_{n/2-\alpha}S^{\alpha}_{\pm}\,t_{-n/2-\alpha}P^{-1}\psi,

(3.4)

\displaystyle S^{\alpha}_{\pm}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-\alpha}\,t_{\alpha-n/2}P^{-1}B^{\alpha}_{\pm}P\,t_{n/2+\alpha}\varphi,

(3.5)

provided that either side of the corresponding equality exists in the Lebesgue sense.

Proof.

For the integral
B^{\alpha}_{+}\psi
, owing to (
2.28
), (
2.30
), (
2.32
), we have

\displaystyle(P^{-1}B^{\alpha}_{+}\psi)(x)=\frac{2^{1+\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\,\sigma_{n-1}}

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{\langle-1,x_{n+1}\rangle_{S}}\frac{\Big(\frac{1}{1-x_{n+1}}-\frac{1}{1-y_{n+1}}\Big)^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\Big(\frac{1-x_{n+1}}{1-y_{n+1}}\Big)^{n/2}(P^{-1}\psi)(y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1+\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\,\sigma_{n-1}}(1-x_{n+1})^{n/2-\alpha}\int\limits_{\langle-1,x_{n+1}\rangle_{S}}\frac{(x_{n+1}-y_{n+1})^{\alpha}(P^{-1}\psi)(y)}{|x-y|^{n}(1-y_{n+1})^{n/2+\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle=2^{\alpha}\,t_{n/2-\alpha}S^{\alpha}_{+}\,t_{-n/2-\alpha}P^{-1}\psi.

This gives (
3.4
) for
B^{\alpha}_{+}\psi
. The proof for
B^{\alpha}_{-}\psi
is similar. The equalities (
3.5
) follow from (
3.4
).
∎

The case of zonal (or axisymmetric) functions is of particular interest for applications. Passing to spherical polar coordinates

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

(S^{\alpha}_{\pm}\varphi)(\theta,\xi^{\prime})=(\tilde{S}^{\alpha}_{\pm}\varphi_{0})(\tan\theta/2),

where

\displaystyle(\tilde{S}^{\alpha}_{+}\varphi_{0})(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}(r)\!\int\limits_{0}^{r}\!(r^{2}\!-\!\rho^{2})^{\alpha-1}\frac{\varphi_{0}(\rho)}{(1+\rho^{2})^{\alpha+n/2}}\,\Big(\frac{\rho}{r}\Big)^{n-2}\rho\,d\rho,

(3.6)

\displaystyle(\tilde{S}^{\alpha}_{-}\varphi_{0})(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}(r)\int\limits_{r}^{\infty}(\rho^{2}-r^{2})^{\alpha-1}\frac{\varphi_{0}(\rho)}{(1+\rho^{2})^{\alpha+n/2}}\,\rho\,d\rho,

(3.7)

(3.8)

Proof.

Starting from (

), we denote

, where

(B^{\alpha}_{+}\psi)(\xi)=\frac{2^{n/2+\alpha+1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\!\int\limits^{r}_{0}(r^{2}\!-\!\rho^{2})^{{\alpha}-1}\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{n-2}\!(1\!+\!\rho^{2})^{-\alpha-n/2}\varphi_{0}(\rho)\,\rho\,d\rho,

where

∎

Theorem 3.3

If

\displaystyle f^{+}_{j,k}(r)

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}(r)\!\int\limits_{0}^{r}\!(r^{2}\!-\!\rho^{2})^{\alpha-1}\frac{\varphi_{j,k}(\rho)}{(1+\rho^{2})^{\alpha+n/2}}\,\Big(\frac{\rho}{r}\Big)^{j+n-2}\rho\,d\rho,\qquad

(3.9)

Lemma 3.4

Let

\mu<n/2p^{\prime}
,
1<p\leq\infty
. Then

T^{\alpha}_{b-}S_{b-}^{\alpha}\varphi\equiv\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.e.}}{{\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}}}T^{\alpha}_{b-,\varepsilon}S_{b-}^{\alpha}\varphi=\varphi
.

Similar corollaries hold for the left-sided fractional integrals.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
b\in(-1,1)
,
\varphi\in L^{p}(\langle-1,b\rangle_{S};(1+x_{n+1})^{\mu})
,
\mu<n/2p^{\prime}
,
1<p\leq\infty
.
Then
T^{\alpha}_{+}S_{+}^{\alpha}\varphi\equiv\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.e.}}{{\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}}}T^{\alpha}_{+,\varepsilon}S_{+}^{\alpha}\varphi=\varphi
.

Consider the left-sided integral
S_{a+}^{\alpha}\varphi
on the upper cap
\langle a,1\rangle_{S}
. Let

T^{\alpha}_{a+,\varepsilon}f=P_{a+}T^{\alpha}_{+,\varepsilon}\Lambda_{a-}f,

where
T^{\alpha}_{+,\varepsilon}
is defined by (
4.7
),
P_{a+}
and
\Lambda_{a-}
stand for the corresponding restriction and extension by zero operators.

Corollary 4.5
.

Suppose that
\varphi\in L^{p}(\langle a,1\rangle_{S};(1-x_{n+1})^{\nu})
,
\nu<n/2p^{\prime}
,
1<p\leq\infty
. Then

T^{\alpha}_{a+}S_{a+}^{\alpha}\varphi\equiv\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.e.}}{{\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}}}T^{\alpha}_{a+,\varepsilon}S_{a+}^{\alpha}\varphi=\varphi
.

5.
Inversion of Riesz Potentials

In this section we consider Riesz potentials
I^{\alpha}_{\Omega}\varphi
(see (
1.1
)) over the spherical caps
\langle a,1\rangle_{S}
and
\langle-1,b,\rangle_{S}
. Our aim is to obtain inversion formulas for these potentials in the corresponding weighted
L^{p}
- spaces. The main tool is the following factorization theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,n)
,
n\geq 2
. If the integral
I^{\alpha}_{\Omega}\varphi
exists in the Lebesgue sense then it can be factorized by the formulas

I^{\alpha}_{\Omega}\varphi=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{S_{+}^{\alpha/2}q_{-\alpha}S_{b-}^{\alpha/2}\varphi,}&\hbox{if}\quad\Omega=\langle-1,b\rangle_{S},\\
\displaystyle{S_{-}^{\alpha/2}q_{-\alpha}S_{a+}^{\alpha/2}\varphi,}&\hbox{if}\quad\Omega=\langle a,1\rangle_{S},\\
\end{cases}

(5.1)

where
q_{-\alpha}=(1-x_{n+1}^{2})^{-\alpha/2}I
is the multiplication operator.

This statement is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.5
.

Because we already know how to invert each component in (
5.1
), we can now invert the potential on the left-hand side. Examination of conditions of Corollaries
4.4
,
4.5
, and Theorem

3.7
yields the following result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
f=I^{\alpha}_{\Omega}\varphi
,
0<\alpha<n
,
1<p<\infty
,
n\geq 2
.

(i)
If
\Omega=\langle-1,b\rangle_{S}
,
b\in(-1,1)
,
\varphi\in L^{p}(\Omega;(1+x_{n+1})^{\mu})
,
\mu<n/2p^{\prime}
, then

\varphi=T^{\alpha/2}_{b-}q_{\alpha}T^{\alpha/2}_{+}f,

(5.2)

(ii)
If
\,\Omega=\langle a,1\rangle_{S}
,
a\in(-1,1)
,
\varphi\in L^{p}(\Omega;(1-x_{n+1})^{\nu})
,
\nu<n/2p^{\prime}
, then

\varphi=T^{\alpha/2}_{a+}q_{\alpha}T^{\alpha/2}_{-}f.

(5.3)

The convergence of the hypersingular integrals in these formulas is understood in the a.e. sense.

Remark 5.3
.

The factorization Theorem
5.1
, combined with the spherical harmonic expansions in Theorem
3.3
, enables one to compute the Fourier-Laplace coefficients of the unknown density
\varphi
by solving two Abel-type integral equations corresponding to fractional integrals (
3.9
) and (
3.10
). Many inversion methods for such equations are discussed, e.g., in the books
[
12
,
13
,
15
]
. We leave this exercise to the interested reader.

6.
Appendix

Recall that our aim is to prove the following inversion formulas:

\displaystyle(T^{\alpha}_{\pm}f)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2-\alpha)\,(1\pm x_{n+1})^{\alpha}}\,f(x)

(6.1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha}}{\sigma_{n-1}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int\limits_{{{S}}^{n}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{(x_{n+1}-y_{n+1})^{\alpha}_{\pm}\,|x-y|^{n}}\,dy,

where

T^{\alpha}_{\pm}f=(S^{\alpha}_{\pm})^{-1}f=2^{\alpha}\,t_{-n/2-\alpha}P^{-1}(B^{\alpha}_{\pm})^{-1}P\,t_{-\alpha+n/2}f,

(6.2)

t_{\lambda}=(1-x_{n+1})^{\lambda}I
,
0<\alpha<1
.

Owing to (
3.3
), it suffices to consider the operator
(S^{\alpha}_{-})^{-1}
. Let us plug the expression (
2.20
) for
(B^{\alpha}_{-})^{-1}
in (
6.2
). The transition formulas
(
2.25
)-(
2.32
) yield

\displaystyle(S^{\alpha}_{-})^{-1}f)(x)=2^{\alpha}(1-x_{n+1})^{-n/2-\alpha}P^{-1}\Bigg\{\frac{2\alpha}{\sigma_{n-1}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{|\eta|>|\xi|}\frac{\displaystyle{\Big(\frac{2}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\Big)^{-\alpha+n/2}(Pf)(\xi)-\Big(\frac{2}{1+|\eta|^{2}}\Big)^{-\alpha+n/2}(Pf)(\eta)}}{(|\eta|^{2}-|\xi|^{2})^{\alpha}\,|\xi-\eta|^{n}}\,d\eta\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\alpha}}{\sigma_{n-1}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int\limits_{\langle x_{n+1},1\rangle_{S}}\frac{\displaystyle{\Big(\frac{1-y_{n+1}}{1-x_{n+1}}\Big)^{\alpha-n/2}f(x)-f(y)}}{(y_{n+1}-x_{n+1})^{\alpha}|x-y|^{n}}\,dy=I_{1}+I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=\frac{2^{\alpha}\,f(x)}{\sigma_{n-1}\Gamma(1\!-\!\alpha)}\!\int\limits_{\langle x_{n+1},1\rangle_{S}}\!\!\!\!\!\Big[\Big(\frac{1\!-\!y_{n+1}}{1\!-\!x_{n+1}}\Big)^{\alpha-n/2}\!\!\!-1\Big]\,\frac{dy}{(y_{n+1}\!-\!x_{n+1})^{\alpha}|x\!-\!y|^{n}},

\displaystyle I_{2}=\frac{2^{\alpha}}{\sigma_{n-1}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int\limits_{\langle x_{n+1},1\rangle_{S}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{(y_{n+1}-x_{n+1})^{\alpha}|x-y|^{n}}\,dy.

The expression
I_{2}
gives the second term in (
6.1
). To show that
I_{1}
coincides with the first term, we denote

\Lambda=\frac{2^{\alpha}}{\sigma_{n-1}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int\limits_{\langle x_{n+1},1\rangle_{S}}\!\!\!\!\!\Big[\Big(\frac{1\!-\!y_{n+1}}{1\!-\!x_{n+1}}\Big)^{\alpha-n/2}\!\!\!-1\Big]\,\frac{dy}{(y_{n+1}\!-\!x_{n+1})^{\alpha}|x\!-\!y|^{n}}

and prove the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

\Lambda=\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2-\alpha)\,(1-x_{n+1})^{\alpha}}.

(6.3)

Proof.

Denote
\gamma=2^{\alpha}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)
,
r=|\xi|
. We have

\displaystyle\Lambda=\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{n-1}}\int\limits_{|\eta|>|\xi|}\Big[\Big(\frac{1+|\xi|^{2}}{1-|\eta|^{2}}\Big)^{\alpha-n/2}\!\!\!-1\Big]

\displaystyle\times\frac{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{n/2}(1+|\eta|^{2})^{n/2}\,d\eta}{\displaystyle{(1+|\eta|^{2})^{n}\Big(\frac{2|\eta|^{2}}{1+|\eta|^{2}}-\frac{2|\xi|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\Big)^{\alpha}|\xi-\eta|^{n}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{-\alpha}\gamma}{\sigma_{n-1}}\int\limits_{|\eta|>|\xi|}\Big[\Big(\frac{1+|\xi|^{2}}{1-|\eta|^{2}}\Big)^{\alpha-n/2}\!\!\!-1\Big]\frac{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{n/2+\alpha}d\eta}{(1+|\eta|^{2})^{n/2-\alpha}(|\eta|^{2}-|\xi|^{2})^{\alpha}|\xi-\eta|^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{-\alpha}\gamma}{\sigma_{n-1}}\int\limits_{r}^{\infty}\Big[\Big(\frac{1+r^{2}}{1-\rho^{2}}\Big)^{\alpha-n/2}\!\!\!-1\Big]\frac{(1+r^{2})^{n/2+\alpha}\rho^{-1}(1-r^{2}/\rho^{2})^{-1}d\rho}{(1+\rho^{2})^{n/2-\alpha}(\rho^{2}-r^{2})^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}\frac{1-r^{2}/\rho^{2}}{|\xi^{\prime}r/\rho-\eta^{\prime}|^{n}}\,d\eta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\gamma 2^{-\alpha}\int\limits_{r}^{\infty}\Big[\Big(\frac{1+r^{2}}{1-\rho^{2}}\Big)^{\alpha-n/2}\!\!\!-1\Big]\frac{(1+r^{2})^{n/2+\alpha}\rho\,d\rho}{(1+\rho^{2})^{n/2-\alpha}(\rho^{2}-r^{2})^{\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle=2^{-\alpha}\,(1+r^{2})^{n/2+\alpha}\,h_{\alpha}(r^{2}),

where

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}(t)=\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int\limits_{t}^{\infty}\frac{(1+t)^{\alpha-n/2}-(1+\tau)^{\alpha-n/2}}{(\tau-t)^{\alpha+1}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+t)^{\alpha-n/2}-(1+t+\tau)^{\alpha-n/2}}{\tau^{\alpha+1}}\,d\tau=(1+t)^{-n/2}\psi(\alpha),

\psi(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-(1+s)^{\alpha-n/2}}{s^{\alpha+1}}\,ds.

Thus (cf. (
2.30
)),

\Lambda=2^{-\alpha}\,(1+r^{2})^{\alpha}\psi(\alpha)=\frac{\psi(\alpha)}{(1-x_{n+1})^{\alpha}},

and it remains to show that

\psi(\alpha)\equiv\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-(1+s)^{\alpha-n/2}}{s^{\alpha+1}}\,ds=\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2-\alpha)}.

(6.4)

To this end, consider the integral

J(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}s^{\alpha-1}(1+s)^{-\alpha-n/2}\,ds=\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2+\alpha)},\qquad Re\,\alpha>0,

and compute analytic continuation (
a.c.
) of this equality to
Re\,\alpha\in(-1,0)
. For
Re\,\alpha>0
we have

\displaystyle J(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\Big(\int\limits_{0}^{1}+\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\Big)\,s^{\alpha-1}(1+s)^{-\alpha-n/2}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha-1}\left[(1+s)^{-\alpha-n/2}-1\right]\,ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}s^{\alpha-1}(1+s)^{-\alpha-n/2}\,ds.

This gives

\displaystyle\underset{Re\,\alpha\in(-1,0)}{a.c.}J(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha-1}\left[(1+s)^{-\alpha-n/2}-1\right]\,ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}s^{\alpha-1}(1+s)^{-\alpha-n/2}\,ds-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}s^{\alpha-1}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+s)^{-\alpha-n/2}-1}{s^{1-\alpha}}\,ds.

Thus, for
Re\,\alpha\in(-1,0)
, we have

\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+s)^{-\alpha-n/2}-1}{s^{1-\alpha}}\,ds=\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2+\alpha)}.

Replacing
\alpha
by
-\alpha
, we obtain

\frac{1}{\Gamma(-\alpha)}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+s)^{\alpha-n/2}-1}{s^{1+\alpha}}\,ds=\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2-\alpha)},

which gives (
6.4
).

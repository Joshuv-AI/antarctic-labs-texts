Stapokibart (CM310) in Atopic Dermatitis: A Narrative Review of a Next-Generation IL-4Rα Antagonist

Published: 2026

Introduction

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a complex and chronic relapsing inflammatory skin disorder that imposes a significant global health burden. It is estimated to affect 15–20% of children and 1–10% of adults worldwide with the prevalence increasing in industrialized nations in recent decades [1,2]. The disease goes far beyond its characteristic symptoms of eczematous plaques and disabling pruritus. AD has a profound negative effect on quality of life, causes sleep disruption, and is linked to a high prevalence of anxiety, depression, and other atopic comorbidities such as asthma and allergic rhinitis, a course that has been described as the “atopic march” [3,4]. Despite access to topical and traditional systemic treatments, a significant percentage of patients with moderate-to-severe disease still suffer from insufficient disease control, side effects of treatment, or contraindications, presenting a persistent and significant unmet medical need [5,6].

The current knowledge of the pathogenesis of AD focuses on a dynamic balance between the disruption of the epidermal barrier and an abnormal type 2 immune response. Central to this immunopathology are the pleiotropic cytokines interleukin (IL)-4 and IL-13 [7]. These cytokines have a common receptor subunit, IL-4 receptor alpha (IL-4Rα). Upon binding of the ligand IL-4Rα dimerizes to form either a Type I receptor (with the common gamma chain for IL-4 signaling) or a Type II receptor (with the IL-13Rα 1 chain for both IL-4 and IL-13 signaling) [8]. This engagement activates intracellular Janus kinase (JAK) 1 and JAK3 or TYK2, resulting in phosphorylation, dimerization, and nuclear translocation of signal transducer and activator of transcription 6 (STAT6) [9]. The downstream transcriptional program mediated by STAT6 is directly responsible for key features of AD: inhibition of important epidermal differentiation proteins such as filaggrin and loricrin, induction of immunoglobulin E (IgE) isotype switching in B cells, recruitment and activation of eosinophils and other inflammatory cells by chemokines (e.g. TARC/CCL17), and direct sensitization of cutaneous sensory neurons, thus fueling the vicious cycle of relentless itch-scratch behavior [10].

Therapeutic targeting of this axis was a revolutionary advancement towards the management of AD. Dupilumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody which targets IL-4Rα, proved in its landmark phase 3 trials (SOLO 1 & 2) that around 36–38% of adults achieved clear or nearly clear skin (Investigator’s Global Assessment [IGA] 0/1), and up to 72% achieved a 75% reduction in disease extent and severity (EASI-75) at 16 weeks, a new standard of care for moderate-to-severe AD [11–13]. However, experience in the field and long-term data have shown that not all patients show adequate response, and a proportion of patients develop adverse events, most notably conjunctivitis, which occurs in about 20–30% of treated patients [14]. Furthermore, issues of cost, access, and desire for therapeutic alternatives with potentially optimized pharmacological properties continue to provide the impetus for innovation.

In this context, new generation biologics focusing on refining the blockage of IL-4/IL-13 pathway have come into prominence. Stapokibart (development code CM310) is a novel humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody that is specific against the IL-4Ra subunit. Its development program has been swift, which has resulted in impressive phase 2 clinical results, and its first regulatory approval was granted in China in February 2025 in adults with moderate-to-severe AD [15,16].

Objectives

This narrative review aimed to perform a comprehensive and critical synthesis of the available evidence for stapokibart. We describe its unique pharmacological profile, overview efficacy and safety data from the conducted clinical trials, compare its positioning within the growing world of biological therapeutics for AD, and discuss its future therapeutic potential and research milestones.

Methods

This narrative review employed a structured and reproducible search strategy to identify and synthesize evidence on stapokibart (CM310) for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD). Searches were conducted on PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and ClinicalTrials.gov for publications and trial registrations from January 2015 to January 2026. Search terms included “stapokibart,” “CM310,” “IL-4Rα,” “interleukin-4 receptor,”“atopic dermatitis,”“dupilumab,”“lebrikizumab,” and “tralokinumab.”

Eligible sources included peer-reviewed original research articles, phase 1–3 clinical trial reports, pharmacological studies, and relevant review articles. Editorials, commentaries, conference abstracts without full text, non-peer-reviewed materials, and small case series (N<5) were excluded. Data from ClinicalTrials.gov were used to verify trial registration, recruitment status, and predefined outcome measures; however, only results supported by peer-reviewed publications were included in the synthesis.

Because this was a narrative review and the included studies varied substantially in design, population characteristics, and outcome definitions, a formal meta-analysis was not performed. Extracted data included mechanistic findings, pharmacokinetics, clinical efficacy outcomes, safety findings (including treatment-emergent adverse events), and regulatory updates. Findings were synthesized thematically to provide an evidence-based overview of stapokibart within the evolving therapeutic landscape of biological treatments for AD.

To improve transparency in study identification and selection, a PRISMA-style flow diagram was included to illustrate the number of records identified, screened, deemed eligible, and ultimately incorporated into the review.

Pharmacological Profile and Mechanism of Action

Stapokibart (CM310) is a humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody that binds the interleukin-4 receptor alpha (IL-4Rα) subunit with high affinity (KD ≈ 0.25 nM), as demonstrated by surface plasmon resonance assays [17–19]. By competitively occupying IL-4Rα, stapokibart sterically blocks the binding of both IL-4 and IL-13, thereby inhibiting the formation of functional Type I (IL-4Rα/γc) and Type II (IL-4Rα/IL-13Rα1) receptor complexes. This dual blockade prevents downstream activation of the JAK–STAT6 signaling pathway, a central driver of type 2 inflammation in atopic dermatitis.

Preclinical studies support this mechanism. In vitro, stapokibart potently inhibits IL-4- and IL-13-induced STAT6 phosphorylation and suppresses the production of key type 2 inflammatory mediators, including thymus and activation-regulated chemokine (TARC/CCL17), in human primary keratinocytes [18]. In murine models of AD-like dermatitis, treatment with stapokibart significantly reduced clinical disease severity, epidermal hyperplasia, dermal inflammation, serum IgE levels, and scratching behavior [18]. The IgG4 isotype was intentionally selected to minimize effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC),supporting its role as a targeted receptor-blocking biologic suitable for chronic immunomodulatory therapy.

Clinical Trial Evidence

Phase 2b Evidence

The efficacy and safety of stapokibart were initially evaluated in a phase 2b randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial conducted in Chinese adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD) [20]. A total of 120 participants were enrolled and randomized 1:1:1 to receive stapokibart 300 mg, stapokibart 150 mg, or placebo every two weeks (Q2W). At Week 16, EASI-75 responses were achieved by 70.0% and 65.0% of patients in the 300 mg and 150 mg groups, respectively, compared with 20.0% in the placebo group (P<0.0001). EASI-90 responses (42.0% and 38.0% vs. 5.0%) and IGA 0/1 responses (41.0% and 36.0% vs. 10.0%) also favored stapokibart. Improvements in pruritus (NRS ≥4 reduction), reductions in TARC/CCL17, and decreases in serum IgE further supported a consistent pattern of type 2 inflammation suppression [20]. Phase 3 Program and Regulation

A larger phase 3 study (NCT05265923) evaluated stapokibart in 500 adults with moderate-to-severe AD [21]. During the 16-week induction phase, stapokibart 300 mg Q2W demonstrated clinically meaningful and statistically significant improvements over placebo. At Week 16, 66.9% of patients receiving stapokibart achieved EASI-75 compared with 25.8% receiving placebo, and 44.2% achieved IGA 0/1 compared with 16.1% in the placebo group (P<0.0001) [15,21]. Treatment effects were durable across the 52-week extension period, with 92.5% of Week-16 EASI-75 responders maintaining this improvement at Week 52 [22]. The safety profile remained favorable and consistent with phase 2 results. The most common treatment-emergent adverse events were upper respiratory tract infections and injection-site reactions. Conjunctivitis occurred in approximately 5.0–5.9% of patients, which appears lower than rates reported for other IL-4/IL-13 pathway biologics; however, direct comparisons cannot be made without head-to-head studies [15,20]. Safety and Tolerability

Across phase 2b and Phase 3 clinical programs, stapokibart (CM310) has demonstrated a favorable safety and tolerability profile in adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD) [20,22]. In a 52-week phase 3 trial (NCT05265923), 88.1% ofparticipantsreportedatleastonetreatment-emergent adverse event (treatment-emergent adverse event, TEAE), the majority of which were mild to moderate (Grade 1–2) and consistent with the expected safety profile of IL-4Rα pathway inhibitors [15,22].

The most frequently observed TEAEs were upper respiratory tract infections, nasopharyngitis, and injection-site reactions, with injection-site reactions occurring in approximately 2.0% of patients. COVID-19 infection was reported in 38.9% of participants; however, this was mainly attributable to the timing of the trial during an active community outbreak, and these events did not result in treatment dis-continuation [22,23]. Importantly, the incidence and pattern of adverse events during the 16–52 week maintenance period remained similar to those observed during the initial 16-week induction phase, and no new longterm safety signal emerged. Serious adverse events were infrequent (5.1%), and patients who switched from placebo to stapokibart at Week 16 exhibited a safety profile comparable to those receiving continuous treatment [22].

Ocular safety—an important concern for IL-4Rα-targeting therapies—remained stable throughout the stapokibart development program. Conjunctivitis occurred at low rates (~5.0–5.9%) and did not differ between patients with or without type 2 inflammatory comorbidities such as asthma or allergic rhinitis [20,23]. These findings suggest that stapokibart’s ocular tolerability is consistent across different patient sub-groups and may represent a differentiated feature compared with other agents in the IL-4/IL-13 pathway, though direct comparisons cannot be made without head-to-head studies.

Long-term extension data further support the safety of chronic therapy, showing sustained maintenance of clinical responses (EASI-75 and EASI-90) without emergence of new safety concerns [22]. Overall, stapokibart exhibited a safety profile that is comparable to other IL-4Rα pathway inhibitors and is supportive of its use as a long-term biological therapy for adults with moderate-to-severe AD [2,15].

Positioning In the Landscape of Treatment

Stapokibart (CM310) enters an increasingly diverse therapeutic landscape for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD), characterized by targeted biologics that modulate type 2 inflammatory pathways [22]. Table 2 provides an indirect comparison of stapokibart with established agents, including dupilumab (IL-4Rα) and the selective IL-13 inhibitors tralokinumab and lebrikizumab. These biologics have demonstrated meaningful improvements in skin inflammation, pruritus, and quality of life in large, well-designed phase 3 trials and now form the foundation of advanced systemic therapy for AD.

Dupilumab, the first-approved IL-4Rα inhibitor, remains widely used based on extensive phase 3 data (SOLO, CHRONOS), showing substantial improvements in EASI-75 and IGA 0/1 responses at Week 16 and sustained efficacy through 52 weeks [11,13]. Its safety profile is well established, with conjunctivitis, injection-site reactions, and upper respiratory tract infections reported most frequently. Selective IL-13 inhibitors (tralokinumab and lebrikizumab) have also demonstrated efficacy in multiple phase 3 programs (ECZTRA, ADvocate), with response rates and safety patterns comparable to other type 2-targeting biologics [24,25].

In this context, stapokibart’s dual IL-4/IL-13 pathway blockade via IL-4Rα inhibition is mechanistically aligned with dupilumab. Clinical trial data indicate robust Week 16 responses (EASI-75: 66.9%; IGA 0/1: 44.2%) in the phase 3 induction period, along with durable improvements through Week 52 [21]. While these results appear numerically high in comparison to values reported in pivotal trials of other biologics, such comparisons should be interpreted with caution because they derive from indirect cross-trial assessments rather than head-to-head studies.

Stapokibart’s low rates of conjunctivitis (~5–6%) observed in the phase 3 program may represent a differentiating characteristic within the IL-4/IL-13 therapeutic class, although the absence of direct comparative trials precludes definitive conclusions regarding relative tolerability. Additionally, its long-term extension data support a sustained treatment effect and stable safety profile throughout 52 weeks, although further global and real-world evidence will be essential to confirm these patterns across diverse populations.

Overall, stapokibart appears to be a promising addition to the expanding category of biological therapies for AD. Its place within treatment algorithms will likely depend on factors such as comparative effectiveness, long-term safety in broader populations, access and cost considerations, and preference for IL-4Rα versus IL-13 selective inhibition. Real-world studies, head-to-head research, and ongoing global phase 3 data will be key to determining its definitive role alongside established biologic agents.

Discussion

Although current data on stapokibart (CM310) are encouraging, several areas require further investigation to define its long-term clinical role. First, completion and publication of the ongoing global phase 3 trials will be essential to confirming whether the efficacy and safety observed in the Chinese population can be generalized across diverse ethnic, genetic, and environmental backgrounds. Inclusion of multinational cohorts will also allow assessment of potential geographic variability in treatment response and ensure alignment with regulatory requirements in major markets such as the United States and the European Union.

Second, long-term safety remains a key priority for all biologics targeting the IL-4/IL-13 axis. While available data up to 52 weeks show a stable safety profile without new adverse signals, extended follow-up is necessary to evaluate rare events, immunogenicity, and the effects of chronic modulation of type 2 immunity. Real-world pharmacovigilance, registry-based studies, and post-marketing surveillance will therefore be critical to complementing clinical trial outcomes.

Third, evidence is still limited regarding stapokibart’s performance in specific clinical contexts, such as patients with multiple atopic comorbidities, those who previously failed IL-4/IL-13 inhibitors, and populations with high disease chronicity. Furthermore, emerging data from related indications, such as chronic rhinosinusitis and other type 2 inflammatory disorders, may help clarify the breadth of stapokibart’s therapeutic potential.

Overall, additional high-quality, real-world, and comparative evidence is needed before its precise placement within treatment algorithms can be established.

Conclusions

Stapokibart (CM310) represents an important addition to the expanding class of biological therapies for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis. Its mechanism of dual IL-4/IL-13 pathway inhibition, robust improvements in clinical signs and symptoms at Week 16, low rates of conjunctivitis, and sustained responses through 52 weeks highlight its potential as a valuable long-term treatment option. However, interpretation of its comparative efficacy and safety should remain cautious, as all available comparisons are indirect and no head-to-head trials with other biologics have been conducted.

Although current evidence supports stapokibart as a promising therapeutic option, additional global phase 3 results, real-world data, and long-term safety studies are needed to more clearly define its role within the treatment landscape. As biologic options continue to expand, such evidence will be essential to guiding clinicians in optimizing personalized treatment strategies for individuals with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis.

Information Must Flow: Recursive Bootstrapping for Information Bottleneck in Optimal Transport
Xin Li

Abstract
We introduce the Context-Content Uncertainty Principle (CCUP) as a unifying framework for understanding how information flows through cognitive systems by resolving the broken symmetry between high-entropy context and low-entropy content. Inference is framed as a dynamic cycle that aligns context with content through selective, bidirectional interactions, enabling information to propagate efficiently across representational hierarchies. Within this framework, cycle formation resolves the Information Bottleneck in Optimal Transport (iBOT) by coordinating bottom-up contextual disambiguation with top-down content reconstruction. This dual process is implemented via a Rao-Blackwellized inference strategy, formalized as variational entropy minimization, which steers the system toward minimal joint uncertainty while preserving the directionality of inference. Crucially, local cycle completion gives rise to temporally extended chain formation (temporal bootstrapping) and spatially distributed hierarchical composition (spatial bootstrapping), enabling the system to simulate goals, evaluate counterfactuals, and iteratively refine internal models. To formalize this bootstrapped memory process, we prove a Delta Convergence Theorem showing that recursive entropy minimization over time yields structured memory states that converge to delta-like distributions over latent manifolds. These converged attractors act as fixed points in the flow of information, stabilizing perceptual schemas and motor plans as compact, generalizable priors. Spatial bootstrapping through ventral–dorsal functional segregation and temporal bootstrapping via the perception–action cycle support progressive refinement of sensorimotor models through continuous interaction with the environment. The sleep–wake cycle extends this dynamic across timescales, transforming episodic experiences into semantic knowledge via recursive consolidation, allowing the flow of information to bridge immediate perception with long-term abstraction. We further propose a representational extension of CCUP in which each level of the hierarchy performs delta-seeded inference: internally generated low-entropy content seeds diffuse outward through goal-constrained inference paths shaped by top-down priors and external context. This structure-adaptive flow of information circumvents the curse of dimensionality by confining inference to task-relevant manifolds. Building on this foundation, we hypothesize that language emerges as a symbolic transport system, an operational extension of CCUP, that externalizes latent content, enabling the transfer of structured inferences across individuals and aligning intersubjective inference cycles. Together, these results establish iBOT as a foundational principle for the directed flow of information in both individual cognition and collective intelligence, situating inference itself as a structured conduit through which minds adapt, align, and extend.

Problem Formulation: What is iBOT and How to Tackle it?

The
Information Bottleneck
(IB) is not merely a tool for supervised learning or signal compression; it is a pervasive constraint shaping biological cognition, AI, and the evolution of representational systems. At its core, the IB formalism captures a fundamental tradeoff: how to preserve task-relevant information while discarding irrelevant detail
Tishby2000
. This tension manifests universally wherever a system must distill high-dimensional observations into compact internal states capable of supporting prediction, control, or generalization. In neural systems, the bottleneck appears in the selective encoding of sensory inputs

, the consolidation of memory
Hasson2015
, and the abstraction of structure across cortical hierarchies
Yamins2016
. In artificial agents, it underlies deep feature compression, latent variable modeling, and the emergence of disentangled representations
Alemi2016
;
Higgins2017
. Even in natural language, perception, and planning, agents continually navigate the bottleneck between ambiguous context and structured intent
Lake2017
;
Friston2010
. Recognizing the IB as a universal organizing principle allows us to reinterpret cognition itself as a process of adaptive compression, one that aligns internal structure with external demand across time, scale, and domain.

While the IB formalism captures the tension between compression and relevance, it leaves open the question of
how
relevant content should be transmitted or instantiated within a high-entropic context. Optimal Transport (OT)
villani2009optimal
provides a natural geometric solution to this question, modeling inference as the movement of probability mass from structured content
\Phi
to observed or imagined context
\Psi
under a cost-minimizing transport plan. However, when
\Psi
is noisy and entangled, direct transport from context to content becomes ill-posed, mirroring the core bottleneck of inference
tishby2011information
. This defines what we term the
Information Bottleneck in Optimal Transport
(iBOT): the problem of aligning structure and specificity when transport is constrained by the need to process information. Resolving the IB in OT requires inverting the classical inference pipeline, starting from compact latent structure
\Phi
, projecting outward to generate plausible contexts
\Psi
, and refining this simulation through cycle-consistent inference. This inverted perspective transforms the bottleneck from a limitation into a guiding principle: structure-first inference allows the system to sidestep entropic overload, stabilize transport, and converge toward generalizable representations across time and scale.

The iBOT directly motivates the Context-Content Uncertainty Principle (CCUP)
li2025CCUP
, which frames cognition as a dynamic alignment between structured content
\Phi
and ambiguous context
\Psi
. Whereas classical inference attempts to extract structure by compressing
\Psi
, this strategy becomes increasingly unstable in high-dimensional or underconstrained regimes. CCUP offers a structural inversion: it begins with compact, low-entropy content
\Phi
and projects outward to simulate or interpret possible contexts
\Psi
, thereby transforming the bottleneck into an opportunity for generalization. In this view, the entropy gradient is reversed, structure precedes specificity, and inference proceeds by minimizing conditional uncertainty
H(\Psi|\Phi)
rather than
H(\Phi|\Psi)
. This shift from bottom-up encoding to top-down simulation defines the principle of
inverted inference
: a generative process where structure constrains interpretation, enabling stable and scalable transport. As such, inverted inference provides a powerful mechanism for resolving iBOT by avoiding the compression of entangled context and instead organizing cognition around reusable, generalizable seeds of content.

Inverted inference gains its full expressive power when extended through recursive bootstrapping across both temporal and spatial hierarchies

In parallel, spatial bootstrapping enables the composition of higher-level abstractions from structured subcomponents. Here, the structure is built recursively by assembling local regularities into increasingly abstract representations, satisfying both inference sufficiency and compositional coherence. The unification of these two bootstrapping processes, temporal and spatial, forms a multiscale inference engine wherein each layer constrains the next, and structure is not only inferred but

Recursive bootstrapping induces a natural convergence dynamic between content and context representations. At each iteration, the content variable

From this perspective, language is not merely a medium of expression but the emergent interface through which minds co-adapt their inference cycles, achieving compositional generalization and cycle-consistent understanding across individuals. 2
Theoretical Foundation: Context–Content Uncertainty Principle (CCUP)

Cognition, at its core, is a process of resolving uncertainty
robertson1929uncertainty
. Every act of perception, action, or memory retrieval involves mapping ambiguous sensory or internal signals onto behaviorally relevant interpretations. We propose CCUP as a foundational framework for understanding this process. CCUP asserts that context and content are informationally asymmetric
anderson1972more
: although context variables may occupy a lower-dimensional sensory space, they exhibit high entropy due to ambiguity and observational limits; in contrast, content variables often span a broader semantic space but maintain low entropy due to goal-specific selection
friston2005theory
. CCUP characterizes inference as a
directional transport process
: from entropic context to structured content. This asymmetry drives the directional flow of inference -
one can use context to predict content reliably, but not content to infer context unambiguously
.

2.1
iBOT Calls for Dynamic Alignment of Content and Context Representation

Broken Symmetry is Ubiquitous in Information-rich Systems.

Let
\Psi
represent high-entropy contextual variables (e.g., sensory observations or task goals) and
\Phi
represent low-entropy content variables (e.g., object identity or action choice). The task of inference is to estimate
\Phi
given
\Psi
, but due to the ambiguity and variability of
\Psi
, a direct mapping often leads to loss of specificity or poor generalization. For any cognitive task, an agent must infer stable latent causes from aliased sensory observations
clark2013whatever
. This inferential directionality, from uncertainty to specificity, is not arbitrary but structured by cognitive architecture and task demands. More fundamentally, it reflects an inherent broken symmetry in the flow of information
cover1999elements
, as characterized by the following lemma.

Lemma 1

(Asymmetry of Inference in Information-Rich Systems)
.

li2025Arrow

Let
\Psi
denote a high-entropy contextual variable, and let
\Phi
denote a low-entropy, stable content variable inferred from
\Psi
. The CCUP formalizes an inherent asymmetry in inference between these variables, reflected in their conditional entropies.
In the forward direction, the mapping
\Psi\rightarrow\Phi
involves disambiguating aliased contextual input to infer a precise latent cause. This process is underdetermined, yielding nonzero conditional entropy:

H(\Phi|\Psi)>0
.
In the reverse direction, the mapping
\Phi\rightarrow\Psi
requires generating/simulating a range of compatible contextual instances from a compact content representation. This generalization introduces additional uncertainty:
H(\Psi|\Phi)\gg H(\Phi|\Psi)>0
.

This directional loss is not due to suboptimal learning but is
structurally unavoidable
given the entropy gradient between context and content. Asymmetry in inference is not merely local (directional), but is bounded globally by the mismatch in marginal entropies. Since both directions
\Psi\rightarrow\Phi
and
\Phi\rightarrow\Psi
carry irreducible uncertainty, the sum of these uncertainties must respect the minimum divergence in information content. Inference systems should minimize this joint uncertainty by leveraging the broken symmetry (structure prior)
anderson1972more
:

H(\Phi)\ll H(\Psi)
, implying that
\Phi
should be used to condition and constrain the interpretation of
\Psi

rao1992information
;
blackwell1947conditional
. We start our solution to the iBOT problem with the following lemma of entropy decomposition with a variational formulation
kingma2013auto
.

Lemma 2

(Entropy Decomposition Lemma with Variational Formulation)
.

Let
\Psi
denote a high-entropy contextual variable and
\Phi
denote a structured, low-entropy content variable such that their joint distribution
p(\Phi,\Psi)
is well-defined. Then, for any inference system that seeks to minimize the uncertainty over
\Phi
and
\Psi
, the optimal inference policy proceeds by:
1) Minimizing the joint entropy via decomposition:

H(\Phi,\Psi)=H(\Phi)+H(\Psi|\Phi)
;
2) Under the assumption
H(\Phi)\ll H(\Psi)
, the conditional entropy
H(\Psi|\Phi)
dominates and becomes the principal target of minimization;
3) This induces a directional alignment: inference should proceed by first establishing
\Phi
, then using it to constrain
\Psi
, such that:

\Phi\leadsto\Psi\quad\text{(structure precedes specificity)}
.
Moreover, the variational inference objective:
\mathcal{F}[q]=\mathbb{E}_{q(Z|\Psi)}[-\log p(\Psi|Z)]+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(Z|\Psi)\|p(Z|\Phi))
is minimized when the prior
p(Z|\Phi)
encodes low-entropy structure aligned with
\Psi
.

Under the framework of OT

must be mapped onto a lower-entropic content distribution

. Based on CCUP, the asymmetry between
H(\Psi)
and
H(\Phi)
induces directional information loss. Therefore,
asymmetry creates an arrow

Any latent encoding

Lemma 3

(Entropy Asymmetry Implies Bottleneck in OT)
.

H(\Psi)\gg H(\Phi)\Rightarrow I(\Psi;\Phi)<H(\Psi)

The inherent asymmetry in entropy between context and content variables makes information loss during OT

2.2
Dynamic Alignment Requires Inverted Inference and Bootstrapping

Structure-before-Specificity Implies Inverted Inference.

In classical formulations, OT
villani2009optimal
faces the challenge of mapping a high-entropy contextual distribution
\mu(\Psi)
to a lower-entropy content distribution
\nu(\Phi)
via a latent variable
Z
. Traditional wisdom frames this as a one-shot encoding problem: how to encode
\Psi
into
Z
while preserving relevance to
\Phi
, which introduces an IB due to the entropy gap
H(\Psi)\gg H(\Phi)
.
CCUP offers a fundamentally different perspective on introducing a feedback loop to form an asymmetric cycle based on the following observation.
The broken symmetry in entropy decomposition introduces a natural directionality in inference: rather than treating
\Phi
and
\Psi
symmetrically, optimal inference must proceed from low-entropy structure to high-entropy specificity. In other words, the system must first identify structured content
\Phi
, and then use it to resolve ambiguity in the contextual input
\Psi
. This directional constraint gives rise to the
Structure-before-Specificity
(SbS) principle

, which we now formalize below.

Principle 1

(Structure-before-Specificity Principle)
.

Let
\Psi
denote high-entropy context variables and
\Phi
denote low-entropy content variables inferred under CCUP. Let

1) Structure Formation (Generalization):
The agent seeks a latent structure
\Phi

\Phi^{\ast}=\arg\min_{\Phi}\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[H(\Psi_{i}|\Phi)\right]
, which yields a generalizable representation
\Phi^{\ast}
that captures invariant structure across variable examples;
2) Experience Binding (Specification):
Once
\Phi^{\ast}

H(\Phi^{\ast}|\Psi_{j})
for each new example
\Psi_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{new}}

The SbS principle posits that cognitive systems achieve generalization not by overfitting to raw observations, but by grounding interpretation in structured priors that constrain what can be inferred. This principle provides a
directional

\Phi

\Psi

2.3
Bootstrapping as a Meta-Strategy for Learning and Inference

The temporal difference update rule inspires the formulation of inverted inference in Q-learning
watkins1992q
.
Both the half-step update in Q-learning and the generalization–specification loop in inverted inference rely on the strategy of
bootstrapping
: using current approximations to improve future estimates in the absence of full information, thereby enabling convergence toward optimal representations through recursive refinement. The brilliant idea behind celebrated Q-learning
watkins1992q
is to perform a one-step bootstrapped approximation, using a partial estimate of future value to update current action values
rather than computing the full trajectory to a reward. This “half-step down” idea bypasses the need for full expansion of the future, suggesting that forward search can be circumvented by local, structured inference. Q-learning sidesteps full forward search by grounding inference locally in transitions, using action-structured latent values instead of marginalizing over all futures. The trick is to bootstrap from the next state’s best action, avoiding the curse of dimensionality in planning trees
bellman1966dynamic
.

Just as Q-learning performs a half-step look ahead in time by using the next state’s value to update the current one, inverted inference can be interpreted as executing a
half-cycle right
, a generative sweep from content
\Phi
through latent
Z
to context
\Psi
, bootstrapping structural refinement by simulating plausible instantiations without requiring full cycle closure. Specifically, we shift the cycle formation from the original (standard inference)
\Psi\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Phi\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\Psi^{\prime}
to the right by half-cycle:
\Phi\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Psi\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\Phi^{\prime}
(inverted inference). The trick is that we do not need to complete the full cycle (the right half
\Psi\rightarrow\Phi^{\prime}
) but use the forward sweep
\Phi\rightarrow\Psi
to refine the content representation by predictive coding
keller2018predictive
, which saves computation by local updates only (i.e., to update
\Phi
from the error in
\Psi
).

\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
onto low-dimensional structured representations
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\ll D

\Phi^{\ast}
that preserves contextual information with dramatically reduced inference complexity. The following theorem formalizes this reduction in computational cost and inference dimensionality.

Theorem 1

(Breaking the Curse of Dimensionality via Half-Cycle Right Inference)
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
denote a high-dimensional context variable, and let
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

p(\Psi,Z,\Phi)
factorizes through low-dimensional conditional distributions; 2) The update
\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\hat{\Psi}^{(t)})
is entropy-decreasing:
H(\Psi|\Phi^{(t+1)})<H(\Psi|\Phi^{(t)})
;
3) The reconstruction steps

are amortized and Lipschitz-continuous, then the inference process converges to a structured representation
\Phi^{\ast}
that achieves predictive sufficiency:
H(\Psi|\Phi^{\ast})\approx\min_{\Phi}H(\Psi|\Phi)
by
avoiding direct marginalization over
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
and reducing inference complexity from

to

under structured priors and amortization.

\hat{Z}^{(t)}\sim q(Z|\hat{\Psi}^{(t)})
and
\Phi^{(t+1)}\sim q(\Phi|\hat{Z}^{(t)})
to update the structured goal representation.
Under the assumptions of Theorem

\Phi^{*}
that generates predictions
\hat{\Psi}
sufficient to capture the relevant structure of the task (i.e.,
H(\Psi|\Phi^{*})\approx\min_{\Phi}H(\Psi|\Phi)
). The process avoids direct search over the
2^{1024}

3
Delta Convergence and the Emergence of Generalizable Structure

Theorem

demonstrates how cycle-consistent, half-cycle right inference can dramatically reduce the complexity of high-dimensional inference problems by projecting context onto structured, low-dimensional representations through amortized and entropy-aligned updates. This result highlights a broader pattern: the underlying mechanism, iterative refinement via structure-conditioned prediction and recognition
hinton1995wake
, is not specific to any one domain but reflects a general computational strategy. In particular, the update rule
\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\hat{\Psi}^{(t)})
exemplifies a bootstrapping architecture in which the system uses its current estimate to simulate outcomes, then refines itself based on the discrepancy
keller2018predictive
. Viewed abstractly, this recursive process defines a contractive operator over a compact space of representations, with a fixed point corresponding to an optimal, self-consistent latent structure
granas2003fixed
. The following principle formalizes this fundamental insight:
bootstrapping provides a unifying template for learning and inference as convergence to a self-consistent prediction under a surrogate refinement loop
.

Principle 2

(Bootstrapping as a Unifying Principle of Learning and Inference)
.

Let
V_{t}\in\mathcal{X}
be an evolving representation at iteration
t
, where
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a compact, convex space. Suppose the update rule takes the bootstrapped form:

V_{t+1}=\mathcal{F}(V_{t},\widetilde{E}(V_{t}))
, where
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
is a deterministic update operator,
\widetilde{E}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a surrogate or predictive operator and
\mathcal{F}
is contractive in its first argument: there exists
0<\gamma<1
such that

\|\mathcal{F}(x_{1},y)-\mathcal{F}(x_{2},y)\|\leq\gamma\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\quad\text{for all }x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}

Then the sequence
\{V_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
converges to a unique fixed point
V^{\ast}\in\mathcal{X}
, satisfying:
V^{\ast}=\mathcal{F}(V^{\ast},\widetilde{E}(V^{\ast}))
.

Remark:
An example of
\widetilde{E}(V_{t})
is a partial or simulated expectation derived from
V_{t}
itself (e.g., a predicted reward in Q-learning or a sampled context in inverted inference), and
\mathcal{F}
is a refinement operator (e.g., Bellman update or entropy minimization). The bootstrapping principle unifies reinforcement learning

and structure-guided inference
Lake2017
as recursive fixed-point processes, where present estimates guide future refinements through partially informed simulation or projection. Bootstrapping, including half-step down and half-cycle right tricks, is computationally efficient because it turns global inference into local simulation
rao1999predictive
and error propagation
rumelhart1985learning
, reducing cost from exponential to first-order structure-preserving updates (i.e., no need for global marginalization like in the search problem
minsky1961steps
).

3.1
Motivation: Fixed-point Dynamics in Bootstrapping

Bootstrapping, when framed as a meta-strategy for learning and inference, naturally gives rise to a fixed-point dynamic
granas2003fixed
: internal representations are recursively updated through structured feedback until a self-consistent solution emerges. This fixed-point process can be formalized as a mapping
\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\Psi^{(t)})
, where each update refines structured content
\Phi
based on the current context
\Psi
and prior estimates. The long-term behavior of this dynamical system exhibits convergence toward delta-like attractors

, distributions sharply peaked around highly structured, low-entropy solutions. These delta attractors represent maximally specific hypotheses that remain stable under further bootstrapped updates, serving as information-theoretic fixed points of the inference process. We start with:

Principle 3

(Iterative Convergence Principle)
.

Cognitive systems achieve generalization and adaptive behavior through the dynamic interplay of two complementary mechanisms:
1)
Bootstrapped Learning Dynamics:

Learning unfolds as a recursive, self-refining process in which current structural estimates guide the simulation and assimilation of future experiences. This enables inference and learning to proceed even under partial observability and uncertainty.
2)
Delta-Like Representations:

Through repeated bootstrapping, the system converges toward compact, low-entropy latent structures that serve as generalization-optimal attractors. These delta-like representations minimize both structural complexity and contextual uncertainty.
Together, these mechanisms allow the brain to construct predictive, compositional models of the world that are both dynamically stable and informationally efficient.

From the perspective of CCUP, iterative convergence corresponds to the progressive alignment of ambiguous context with structured content via entropy minimization
grandvalet2004semi
. Such attractors are not only stable but also efficient: they encode composable abstractions that support generalization across contexts. By collapsing uncertainty into a low-dimensional manifold of self-consistent predictions, delta convergence enables both inference stability and representational reusability
rigotti2013importance
, key ingredients for memory formation, goal propagation, and planning under uncertainty. Formally, we have

Theorem 2

(Delta Convergence under Bootstrapped Inference)
.

Let
\{\Phi^{(t)}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of structured content representations evolving under a bootstrapping-based update rule:

\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\Psi^{(t)})
,
where
\Psi^{(t)}
denotes the context at time
t
, and
\mathcal{F}
is an entropy-minimizing operator that satisfies the monotonic uncertainty reduction condition:

H(\Psi^{(t)}|\Phi^{(t+1)})\leq H(\Psi^{(t)}|\Phi^{(t)})
.
Assume further that
\mathcal{F}
is contractive in a suitable metric space
(\mathcal{S},d)
and that the context distribution stabilizes or varies within bounded entropy. Then the sequence
\{\Phi^{(t)}\}
converges to a fixed point

\Phi^{*}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\Phi^{(t)}=\delta_{\phi^{*}}

where
\delta_{\phi^{*}}
is a delta-like distribution sharply peaked around a maximally specific latent structure

Moreover,
\Phi^{*}

\Phi^{*}=\arg\min_{\Phi}\;\mathbb{E}_{\Psi\sim P(\Psi)}\left[H(\Psi|\Phi)\right]

and supports generalization by encoding a reusable, low-entropy structural prior consistent across multiple contexts.

The above result establishes that bootstrapped inference dynamics converge to a deterministic delta-like fixed point under a contractive and entropy-minimizing update rule. However, many real-world learning scenarios involve stochastic observations and epistemic uncertainty

. In such settings, the evolution of structured content representations must be treated as a stochastic process, where each update
\Phi^{(t+1)}
depends on sampled observations
\Psi^{(t)}\sim p(\Psi|\Phi^{(t)})
. This motivates a
distributional
generalization of delta convergence, in which we study the behavior of the evolving distribution
p(\Phi^{(t)})
over structured latents

. By extending the entropy alignment condition to the stochastic setting and assuming a weak form of contractivity, we can show that this distribution converges weakly to a delta measure concentrated at a structure
\Phi^{\ast}
that minimizes the long-term expected uncertainty of context. The following corollary formalizes this result as a distributional analog of Theorem

Corollary 1

(Delta-Like Convergence of Content under Entropy-Aligned Bootstrapping)
.

Let
\{\Phi^{(t)}\}\subset\mathcal{S}
be a sequence of structured representations evolving under the recursive update:

\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\Psi^{(t)}),\quad\Psi^{(t)}\sim p(\Psi|\Phi^{(t)})

where
\mathcal{F}

H(\Psi^{(t+1)}|\Phi^{(t+1)})\leq H(\Psi^{(t)}|\Phi^{(t)})

and the update rule satisfies the contractivity condition given in Proposition 1. Then the sequence
\Phi^{(t)}
converges to a fixed point
\Phi^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S}

weakly converges to a delta measure:

p(\Phi^{(t)})\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}\delta(\Phi^{\ast})

where

\Phi^{\ast}=\arg\min_{\Phi}\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}H(\Psi^{(t)}|\Phi)

Delta-like representations emerge naturally as fixed points of recursive, entropy-aligned bootstrapping

To formalize this intuition, we now establish a lemma showing that delta-like representations both minimize context–content uncertainty and constitute generalization-optimal codes under entropy-constrained inference (proof skipped).

3.3
Variational Formulation as an Operational Principle of Inverted Inference

Having established delta convergence as the dynamical mechanism through which generalizable structure emerges, we now turn to the variational formulation of CCUP, which provides not only the underlying mathematical framework that unifies bootstrapping and delta-based inference but also an operational principle of inverted inference
li2025CCUP
.
Specifically, CCUP defines a functional objective in which structured content
\Phi
is optimized to minimize the expected conditional entropy of context:
\mathcal{L}(\Phi)=\mathbb{E}_{\Psi}[H(\Psi|\Phi)]
. Bootstrapping, viewed as a meta-strategy, implements a recursive update rule
\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\Psi^{(t)})
that iteratively descends this entropy landscape. Each iteration samples or simulates a context from the current content, refines the structure to reduce uncertainty, and thereby approximates variational inference under a structure-seeded prior. We consider the following model under a variational formulation.

Model 1

(Spatiotemporal Variational Inference under CCUP)
.

Let
\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)}
denote the structured content at layer
\ell
, time
t
, and let
\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)}
be the corresponding context. We define two latent variables:
1)
Z_{t}
: temporal latent variable simulating transitions from
\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)}\to\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)}
;
2)
Z_{\ell}
: spatial latent variable encoding the composition of parts
\{\Phi_{\ell-1}^{(t,i)}\}\to\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)}
.
The generative model factorizes as:

p(\Psi,\Phi,Z_{t},Z_{\ell})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}p(Z_{t}|\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)})\cdot p(\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)}|Z_{t})\cdot p(Z_{\ell}|\{\Phi_{\ell-1}^{(t,i)}\})\cdot p(\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)}|Z_{\ell})
.
The variational posterior approximates the inverse path:

q(Z_{t},Z_{\ell}|\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)})=q(Z_{t}|\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)})\cdot q(Z_{\ell}|\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)})
.
Each inference cycle minimizes the total spatiotemporal uncertainty:

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{t,\ell}\Big[H(\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)}|\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)})+\lambda_{\text{temp}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(Z_{t}|\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)})\,\|\,p(Z_{t}|\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)}))+\lambda_{\text{space}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(Z_{\ell}|\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)})\,\|\,p(Z_{\ell}|\{\Phi_{\ell-1}^{(t,i)}\}))\Big]
.

Remark:

Latent variables
Z_{t}
and
Z_{\ell}
enable recursive bootstrapping in time and space, respectively. Under CCUP, this formulation minimizes contextual uncertainty while preserving structured content, guiding the convergence toward delta-like attractors across spatiotemporal inference cycles

.
Under mild contractivity and alignment conditions, this iterative process converges to a delta-like attractor
\Phi^{*}=\delta_{\phi^{*}}
that minimizes the CCUP objective. In this sense, delta convergence emerges as the fixed point of variational bootstrapping, encoding a reusable, low-entropy representation that supports generalization across contexts.

Lemma 6

(Delta Convergence of Structured Content)
.

Let
\Phi^{(t)}

\Phi^{(t+1)}=\arg\min_{\Phi}\left[H(\Psi|\Phi)+\lambda\,D_{\mathrm{OT}}\left(p(\Psi|\Phi),\,p(\Psi|\Phi^{(t)})\right)\right]

The preceding lemma establishes that cycle-consistent, inverted inference dynamics guided by entropy minimization and optimal transport naturally converge to a delta-like attractor. To ensure that this convergence holds more generally, across compact, convex latent spaces and under broader regularity assumptions, we formalize these dynamics in the context of the iBOT framework. The following theorem generalizes the result by showing that, under convexity and continuity of both the entropy and transport objectives, the sequence of structured representations converges to a unique fixed point
\Phi^{*}

This convergence underlies the brain’s capacity to compress noisy sensory streams into goal-relevant, low-entropy codes that guide perception, planning, and motor coordination. When the conditions for delta convergence are disrupted, either by noise amplification or breakdown in inference cycles, the system may exhibit pathological dynamics characterized by instability and persistent prediction errors.

To illustrate the explanatory power of this framework in a clinical setting, we now consider two examples from Parkinson’s Disease (PD)

First, we show how the tremor in PD can be understood as a failure of delta convergence, where the inference process fails to stabilize on a low-entropy motor plan. Then, we interpret focused ultrasound stimulation, a novel therapeutic approach exemplified by the work of Ali Rezai’s team

(Tremor in Parkinson’s Disease as Failure of Delta Convergence)

Let
\Phi^{(t)}\in\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote a structured motor plan at iteration

\lim_{t\to\infty}p(Z\mid\Phi^{(t)})=\delta(Z-Z^{\ast})
.
In PD, dopaminergic degeneration impairs the precision and update dynamics of the inference loop. Let the recursive motor update be modeled as:

\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\hat{\Psi}^{(t)})
,
where
\hat{\Psi}^{(t)}=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim p(Z\mid\Phi^{(t)})}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\Psi\sim p(\Psi\mid Z)}[\Psi]\right]

\|\mathcal{F}(\Phi_{1},\hat{\Psi})-\mathcal{F}(\Phi_{2},\hat{\Psi})\|\geq\|\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\|\quad\text{for some }\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}

and the sequence

fails to converge. Instead, the system enters an oscillatory regime where the prediction–correction loop induces rhythmic fluctuations:

\Phi^{(t+2)}\approx\Phi^{(t)}+\epsilon\sin(\omega t),\quad\epsilon>0

manifesting as a resting tremor. This dynamic reflects a pathological inference cycle in which prediction errors are overcorrected due to loss of damping, yielding a limit-cycle–like attractor

instead of a delta-converged motor plan.
In short, tremor in PD can be interpreted as a breakdown of delta convergence in motor inference. Restorative therapies such as dopaminergic agents or deep brain stimulation may act by re-establishing contractivity in
\mathcal{F}

\Phi^{\ast}
.

Example 4

(Focused Ultrasound as Restoration of Delta Convergence in Parkinson’s Disease)
.

Let
\Phi^{(t)}\in\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the structured motor plan at time

is the proprioceptive or external motor feedback,

In PD, due to dopaminergic degeneration and basal ganglia dysfunction, the recursive update operator becomes dysregulated and non-contractive:

causing the sequence

.

modify the neural gain or effective damping of subcortical structures, particularly in the thalamus or subthalamic nucleus. This is modeled as restoring contractivity of the update operator

\|\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{stim}}(\Phi_{1},\hat{\Psi})-\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{stim}}(\Phi_{2},\hat{\Psi})\|\leq\gamma\|\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\|,\quad 0<\gamma<1

\lim_{t\to\infty}p(Z\mid\Phi^{(t)})=\delta(Z-Z^{\ast})

leading to stable convergence of the motor plan:

\Phi^{(t)}\to\Phi^{\ast},\quad\text{where }\Phi^{\ast}\text{ is a self-consistent, tremor-free attractor}
.
From an inferential dynamics perspective, ultrasound and DBS therapies stabilize recursive updates in motor planning by restoring the contractive geometry of latent-to-context inference, allowing PD patients to recover precise, stable motor control.

4
Spatiotemporal Bootstrapping for Fast and Slow Thinking

Inspired by the framework of slow and fast thinking

, we consider two types of bootstrapping strategies: spatial bootstrapping for fast thinking and temporal bootstrapping for slow thinking in this section. In temporal bootstrapping, the latent variable encodes a compact, goal-conditioned simulation of plausible future contexts given current structure, enabling forward rollouts in episodic memory

Definition 2

\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}

Here, deliberate reasoning emerges as a temporally extended inference trajectory that incrementally resolves uncertainty in context by simulating plausible latent structures

genevay2018learning
. In this section, we separately study temporal and spatial bootstrapping for slow and fast thinking next.

4.1
Episodic Memory Supports Goal-Directed Simulation via Temporal Bootstrapping

Episodic memory provides a temporal scaffold for constructing internally generated simulations that guide future behavior
schacter2008episodic
. Rather than functioning as a static repository of past events, episodic memory supports goal-directed inference by incrementally refining structural expectations over time
bar2009proactive
. Each remembered episode contributes a noisy, partial context
\Phi^{(t)}
, which is integrated into a cumulative predictive code
\Psi^{(t)}
. This code serves as a latent summary of temporally extended experience and is recursively updated to minimize uncertainty about future contextual inputs
schapiro2017complementary
. Crucially, this bootstrapping process does not require full marginalization over the high-dimensional posterior space but instead propagates structure through local updates (thanks to the half-cycle right trick) that balance predictive specificity and temporal continuity. As the inference unfolds, the representation
\Psi^{(t)}
converges toward a compact, generalizable attractor
\Psi^{\ast}
, enabling the system to simulate plausible outcomes under uncertainty
wang2018prefrontal
. The following theorem formalizes this process as a convergence result for temporally bootstrapped inference.

Theorem 6

(Delta Convergence of Temporal Bootstrapping)

Let
\{\Psi^{(t)}\}_{t=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S})

\Phi^{(t)}

Assume: 1) (Stationarity) The contextual distribution

is stationary;
2) (Entropy gap) There exists a unique global minimizer

The convergence of episodic bootstrapping toward a generalizable latent code hinges on the underlying dynamics of cycle-consistent refinement

Corollary 2

4.2
Hierarchical Composition as Spatial Bootstrapping

Hierarchical composition functions as a spatial bootstrapping mechanism in cognition
lee2003hierarchical
. At each layer
\ell
, a structured content representation
\Phi_{\ell}
is constructed by recursively composing and abstracting over lower-level parts
\{\Phi_{\ell-1}^{(i)}\}
. This enables complex perceptual categories, such as objects, scenes, or symbols, to be inferred from simpler components through reusable structure and alignment, rather than being learned tabula rasa
dicarlo2012does
. As with episodic memory in time, spatial bootstrapping allows the brain to generalize efficiently across space by iteratively building structure from previously grounded elements
eichenbaum2014time
. We start with the construction of a new model for spatiotemporal bootstrapping.

Model 2

(Spatiotemporal Variational Inference under CCUP)
.

Let
\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)}
denote the structured content at layer
\ell

t

\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)}

p(\Psi,\Phi,Z_{t},Z_{\ell})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}p(Z_{t}|\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)})\cdot p(\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)}|Z_{t})\cdot p(Z_{\ell}|\{\Phi_{\ell-1}^{(t,i)}\})\cdot p(\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)}|Z_{\ell})

The variational posterior approximates the inverse path:

q(Z_{t},Z_{\ell}|\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)})=q(Z_{t}|\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)})\cdot q(Z_{\ell}|\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)})

Each inference cycle minimizes the total spatiotemporal uncertainty:

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{t,\ell}\Big[H(\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)}|\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)})+\lambda_{\text{temp}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(Z_{t}|\Psi_{\ell}^{(t)})\,\|\,p(Z_{t}|\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)}))+\lambda_{\text{space}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(Z_{\ell}|\Phi_{\ell}^{(t)})\,\|\,p(Z_{\ell}|\{\Phi_{\ell-1}^{(t,i)}\}))\Big]

The preceding model formalizes how spatiotemporal structure can be disentangled into separate but coupled latent paths: temporal inference through
Z_{t}

Z_{\ell}

whittington2022disentanglement
. Crucially, both forms of inference obey the CCUP principle
li2025CCUP
: minimizing contextual uncertainty via structured priors and cycle-consistent refinement. When this bootstrapped inference is applied recursively across hierarchical levels, each layer incrementally compresses context into more structured, less ambiguous representations. The result is a progressive sharpening of the conditional distribution
p(\Phi_{\ell-1}|\Psi_{\ell})

The following theorem formalizes this convergence in the limit of deep hierarchical composition, showing that inference over contextually grounded latent variables converges to a delta-like distribution concentrated around an abstract structural manifold.

In the next two examples, we illustrate this vulnerability by examining frontotemporal dementia (FTD) as a clinical manifestation of broken inverted inference, where abstract goal representations can no longer organize perceptual and semantic content. Example 5

(Frontotemporal Dementia as a Breakdown of Inverted Inference)
.

Frontotemporal dementia (FTD) manifests as a progressive degeneration of the frontal and/or temporal lobes, leading to cognitive and behavioral impairments including loss of planning, disinhibition, semantic deficits, and emotional blunting

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}

\Phi^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\arg\min_{\Phi}\mathcal{D}\left(\Psi^{\text{obs}}_{t}\,\|\,P(\Psi\mid Z_{t})\right)

where

In FTD, distinct breakdowns occur depending on the locus of degeneration:

2)
Temporal atrophy
disrupts the generative prior

and collapsed semantic content;
3)
Global breakdown
of the inference cycle prevents alignment between predicted and observed context, leading to poor self-monitoring, semantic incoherence, and social dysfunction.
In short, FTD can be viewed as a failure of inverted inference: the system can no longer simulate, match, and refine latent structure
\Phi

5
Social Bootstrapping and the Origin of Language

From Spatiotemporal Bootstrapping to Social Bootstrapping.

While spatiotemporal bootstrapping enables individual agents to construct coherent internal models by integrating information across scales of space (e.g., object composition) and time (e.g., episodic memory), such inference remains fundamentally bounded by the agent’s own sensory history and computational constraints. To transcend these individual limitations, cognitive systems engage in
social bootstrapping

clark1998being
, a process through which structured latent content is externalized, shared, and iteratively refined via communicative interaction.
In any act of communication, a speaker must transform a rich internal state, comprising beliefs, intentions, emotions, or memories, into a compressed external signal such as language, gesture, or expression
tomasello2010origins
. This transformation inevitably involves information loss, posing a fundamental tradeoff: how can the speaker reduce the complexity of the message while still preserving its relevance to the listener’s interpretive task? This tradeoff is captured by the
IB
principle, which formalizes the problem of compressing internal content in a way that maximally preserves task-relevant information. In the context of social communication, the IB framework models how agents encode and decode signals to align internal models under constraints of noise, ambiguity, and limited bandwidth. It provides a powerful lens for understanding how language, emotion, and shared context evolve to mitigate communicative uncertainty and enable intersubjective inference.

Definition 3

\min_{p(\Psi|\Phi)}I(\Phi;\Psi)

\text{subject to }I(\Psi;\hat{\Phi})\geq\mathcal{I}_{0}
,
where
\mathcal{I}_{0}
is a lower bound on the amount of relevant information that must be preserved for successful communication.
This tradeoff captures the core challenge of social communication: compressing internal content into efficient signals that remain maximally informative under the listener’s reconstruction process.

In social contexts, this mechanism becomes the basis for
social bootstrapping
, a process whereby intersubjective constraints recursively anchor individual inference cycles. Through imitation, joint attention, and communicative feedback, agents co-construct shared latent models, refining their internal representations via socially mediated error correction
clark2015surfing
. Just as episodic memory bootstraps inference through time, social bootstrapping extends the cycle across minds, creating a distributed inference network grounded in a shared structure. In this extended framework, language emerges as a socially bootstrapped code that enables the transmission of compressed internal content across agents
macwhinney1998models
, aligning distributed inference cycles through shared priors and mutual simulation. Thus, the principles of cycle-consistent bootstrapping scale from the internal architecture of the individual to the emergent coordination of collective intelligence, transforming private inference loops into intersubjective bridges that support knowledge accumulation, cultural transmission, and cooperative reasoning
boyd2005origin
.

Building on this foundation, we hypothesize that
language

From Individual Communication to Collective Alignment.

The preceding formulation characterizes language as a structured latent variable
Z

However, natural language does not arise in isolation; it emerges, stabilizes, and evolves within a community of interacting agents. As communication scales from dyadic exchanges to multi-agent systems, the pressure to align divergent internal states across agents drives the emergence of a shared latent codebook

The following result formalizes this transition by showing how shared symbolic codes emerge as optimal solutions to a multi-agent iBOT problem, where each agent seeks to compress and broadcast its internal state in a way that minimizes inference error across the population. Conclusion: Information Must Flow

This paper has advanced a unified framework for understanding the directional flow of information in cognitive and communicative systems, grounded in the interplay between the IB, OT, and the CCUP. We began by identifying a fundamental constraint—termed the

(iBOT), that arises from the need to compress high-dimensional internal states into low-bandwidth signals while preserving their relevance for inference. Overcoming iBOT requires organizing inference not as a symmetric mapping between signals and meanings, but as a directional flow that aligns structured content with contextual constraints, in accordance with CCUP.

At the level of individual cognition, this challenge is addressed through
inverted inference
, which reverses the traditional data-driven flow by initiating inference from structured, low-entropy goals. This directional process is further refined through spatiotemporal bootstrapping, whereby content is constructed across nested hierarchies of spatial composition and temporal integration. These mechanisms collectively solve iBOT by enabling internal models to emerge through recursive alignment between structured priors and context-sensitive updates.

Extending this framework to collective intelligence introduces additional complexity: when multiple agents aim to align latent meanings via communication, a new intermediating variable is needed. We identified

as this variable, a socially bootstrapped symbolic code that enables the externalization and reconstruction of structured content across minds. Language resolves the multi-agent form of iBOT by providing a shared latent space through which context and content can be jointly optimized. Crucially, the compositional structure of language, its ability to systematically combine symbols to express complex meanings, emerges as a natural consequence of
Delta convergence
, wherein repeated social feedback and inference cycles drive symbolic codes toward discrete, disentangled, and reusable representations.

Together, these results demonstrate that information must flow, not arbitrarily, but directionally, guided by principles that balance compression, transport, and inference. From the recursive architecture of individual cognition to the symbolic scaffolds of social communication, overcoming iBOT is the central computational challenge that shapes the evolution of intelligent systems.

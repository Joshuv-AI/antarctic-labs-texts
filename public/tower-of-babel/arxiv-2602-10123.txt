1.
Introduction

Point–sphere incidences over finite fields connect finite geometry, pseudorandom incidence graphs, additive combinatorics, and distance problems. Let
Q
be a nondegenerate quadratic form on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, where
q
is odd, let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, and let
\mathcal{S}
be a family of distinct
Q
-spheres. We write

\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})=I(P,\mathcal{S})-\frac{|P||\mathcal{S}|}{q}

for the centered incidence count. The central inverse question is not only whether large incidence forces a structured subset, but whether every near-extremizer is close to an explicit family of models.

The results have three layers, ordered by structural strength. At the maximal-incidence endpoint we prove a global deletion-stability classification by explicit lifted-linear models. We next establish a sharp boundary for that classification: at the smaller fixed-radius deviation scale, universal and exact obstruction families preclude a subexponential list of individual edit templates. Within that broader lower-scale class we prove the strongest universal conclusion available from coincidence energy, namely scale-adaptive extraction and positive-surplus decomposition.

1.1.
Dense-endpoint classification

The largest possible incidence count is
I(P,\mathcal{S})=|P||\mathcal{S}|
. Put

\mu(P,\mathcal{S})=1-\frac{I(P,\mathcal{S})}{|P||\mathcal{S}|}.

Thus
\mu
is the density of missing incidences. Our first main theorem classifies every system with sufficiently small
\mu
.

Use the lift

\Phi(S_{Q}(c,r))=(2c,r-Q(c))\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d+1}.

For an affine subspace
L\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d+1}
, let
\mathcal{S}(L)=\Phi^{-1}(L)
and let
B(L)
be the set of points lying on every sphere of
\mathcal{S}(L)
. The exact models are completely explicit. If
L
has rank
r
, then for an
r
-dimensional subspace
W\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, a linear functional
\beta
on
W
, and suitable
c_{0},z,r_{0}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(L)

\displaystyle=\left\{S_{Q}\left(c_{0}+\frac{w}{2},r_{0}+\beta(w)+\langle c_{0},w\rangle_{Q}+\frac{1}{4}Q(w)\right):w\in W\right\},

\displaystyle B(L)

\displaystyle=S_{Q}(c_{0},r_{0})\cap(z+W^{\perp_{Q}}).

Rank one is a coaxal pencil; higher ranks are explicit higher-dimensional linear sphere systems with a common affine-quadric base.

Theorem

2.8
proves that if

0\leq\mu<\frac{d+1}{(d+2)^{2}},\qquad\delta=\sqrt{(d+1)\mu},

then there is such an
L
, with
\dim L\leq d
, for which at least
(1-\delta)|P|
points lie in
B(L)
and at least
(1-\delta)|\mathcal{S}|
spheres lie in
\mathcal{S}(L)
. Hence, after controlled deletion from both sides, the configuration lies in one exact lifted-linear ambient model; for fixed

there are only
q^{O_{d}(1)}
possible ambient spaces.

The classification is two-sided in a natural edit metric. If
\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{lin}}(P,\mathcal{S})
denotes the least total deleted proportion needed to place both sides in one lifted-linear model, then

\mu(P,\mathcal{S})\leq\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{lin}}(P,\mathcal{S})\leq 2\sqrt{(d+1)\mu(P,\mathcal{S})}.

A split four-dimensional construction shows that the exponent
1/2
is optimal. Moreover, if no affine codimension-two flat contains almost all of
P
, the classifying rank is at most one; provided two spheres remain, the model is one coaxal pencil. These statements form the paper’s Freiman-type inverse theorem, in the ambient-model and edit-distance sense familiar from classical inverse theory
[
4
,
5
]
.

1.2.
The classification boundary at the fixed scale

A classification comparable to the dense-endpoint theorem is impossible in the narrower sense of approximation by a subexponential list of individual point-set templates. We construct
q
nonzero-radius spheres whose lifted parameters form a rational normal curve and a polynomial-root chart with the following universality property: for every admissible
d
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, a point set
P_{\mathcal{G}}
has incidence graph

p_{A}\in S_{t}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\in A.

Dense choices have constant fixed-scale parameter and lie in the moderate range. A Hamming-ball argument shows that any list of individual templates approximating every such
P_{\mathcal{G}}
within
o(q^{d})
point edits must contain
\exp(\Omega_{d}(q^{d}))
members. This is an edit-metric obstruction to an individual-template classification; it does not rule out container statements whose model classes themselves permit arbitrary dense subhypergraphs.

The full root design exhibits a particularly rigid diffuse regime: every sphere pair is rich, no pair section carries more than
O_{d}(q^{-2})
of the surplus, no three lifted parameters are coaxal, and no quantitatively nontrivial centered block decomposition exists. A second obstruction is an exact centered direct sum. For every nondegenerate
Q
in
d\geq 3
, at one fixed nonzero radius, we construct matched blocks satisfying

\sigma(A_{b},\mathcal{S}_{z})=0\quad(b\neq z),\qquad\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})=\sum_{z}\sigma(A_{z},\mathcal{S}_{z}).

In
d\geq 4
these examples occur in the family-only range
|\mathcal{S}|\geq 4q/K^{2}
while no hyperplane or pair section is macroscopic. In dimension three a prescribed-radius twisted-cubic chart realizes the diffuse root design at the sharp point-sensitive boundary. Thus the lower-scale theory has at least three regimes: coaxal concentration, centered direct sums, and diffuse algebraic evaluation designs.

1.3.
Scale-adaptive inverse theory at the fixed-radius benchmark

For one fixed nonzero radius, the natural Fourier deviation scale is

q^{(d-1)/2}\sqrt{|P||\mathcal{S}|},

whereas arbitrary mixed radii admit the larger scale
q^{d/2}\sqrt{|P||\mathcal{S}|}
; see

between these scales is genuine.

At the fixed-radius benchmark our inverse theorem is scale-adaptive. Write
n=|P|
and
s=|\mathcal{S}|
, and suppose

(1)

\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})\geq Kq^{(d-1)/2}\sqrt{ns},\qquad K^{2}q^{d-1}s\geq 4n.

Then at least
K^{2}s/4
ordered pairs satisfy

(2)

|P\cap S\cap S^{\prime}|\geq\frac{K^{2}q^{d-1}}{4s}.

No upper bound on

is needed. Every witnessing pair determines a canonical

K^{2}qs/8
. Under the moderate cap

|P\cap S\cap S^{\prime}|\geq\frac{Kq^{(d-1)/2}}{4C_{1}}.

A single high-degree truncation gives a log-free incidence-rich refinement. Iteration in the positive-excess core gives a cap-free decomposition in which all but a prescribed fraction of the net surplus is carried by disjoint positive-surplus pieces supported on sphere-pair sections. For completeness, exact sphere-complement duality also gives an omitted-family corollary. Its section-size threshold is below
1
throughout the balanced regime
|\mathcal{S}|\leq|\Omega|/2
, and it becomes structurally nontrivial only when the omitted family is an
O(q^{-1})
fraction of the full sphere system; we therefore do not count it among the main lower-scale inverse results.

1.5.
Scope and organization

The dense-endpoint theorem is a global classification after deletion. The fixed-scale results are sharp extraction and decomposition theorems, not a classification of every constant-

2.
Sphere geometry and exact design identities

2.1.
Quadratic spheres

Throughout,
q
is an odd prime power,
d\geq 2
, and
Q:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a nondegenerate quadratic form. Its associated symmetric bilinear form is

\langle x,y\rangle_{Q}=\frac{Q(x+y)-Q(x)-Q(y)}{2}.

For
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, define the
Q
-sphere

S_{Q}(c,r)=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:Q(x-c)=r\}.

Spheres are parametrized by
(c,r)
, and distinct spheres means distinct parameter pairs. We suppress the subscript
Q
when no confusion can arise.

For
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and a family
\mathcal{S}
of distinct spheres, let

I(P,\mathcal{S})=|\{(p,S)\in P\times\mathcal{S}:p\in S\}|,\qquad\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})=I(P,\mathcal{S})-\frac{|P||\mathcal{S}|}{q}.

For
p\in P
, write
d_{\mathcal{S}}(p)=|\{S\in\mathcal{S}:p\in S\}|
.

2.2.
The full sphere design

Let
\Omega
be the family of all
q^{d+1}
parametrized
Q
-spheres, including radius zero.

Proposition 2.1

(Centered sphere design)
.

Let
A
be the
q^{d}\times q^{d+1}
point–sphere incidence matrix, let
J
be the all-ones
q^{d}\times q^{d}
matrix, and let
J_{0}
be the all-ones matrix of the same size as
A
.

(1)

Every point lies on exactly
q^{d}
spheres and every pair of distinct points lies on exactly
q^{d-1}
common spheres. Hence

AA^{\mathsf{T}}=q^{d-1}J+(q^{d}-q^{d-1})I.

(2)

If
B=A-q^{-1}J_{0}
, then

BB^{\mathsf{T}}=(q^{d}-q^{d-1})I,\qquad B\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}=0.

Equivalently, for every
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
,

\sum_{T\in\Omega}\left(|P\cap T|-\frac{|P|}{q}\right)^{2}=(q^{d}-q^{d-1})|P|.

(3)

For every
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\Omega
,

(3)

|\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})|\leq\sqrt{q^{d}-q^{d-1}}\sqrt{|P||\mathcal{S}|\left(1-\frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{q^{d+1}}\right)}.

Equality holds when
P
is a singleton and
\mathcal{S}
is the family of all spheres containing its point.

Proof.

Fix
p\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. Each center
c
determines the unique radius
Q(p-c)
, so
p
lies on
q^{d}
spheres. If
p\neq p^{\prime}
, then both points lie on
S(c,r)
precisely when

Q(p-c)=Q(p^{\prime}-c),

equivalently

2\langle p^{\prime}-p,c\rangle_{Q}=Q(p^{\prime})-Q(p).

Nondegeneracy makes this a nontrivial affine-linear equation in
c
, with exactly
q^{d-1}
solutions. This proves the first assertion.

Each row of
A
has sum
q^{d}
and
|\Omega|=q^{d+1}
. Expanding
BB^{\mathsf{T}}
cancels the
q^{d-1}J
term and gives the second assertion, including
B\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}=0
.

Put
v=B^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{1}_{P}
. Then
v\perp\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}
and

\|v\|_{2}^{2}=(q^{d}-q^{d-1})|P|.

Consequently

\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})=\left\langle v,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}-\frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{|\Omega|}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}\right\rangle,

and Cauchy–Schwarz gives (
3
). In the point-star example the two centered vectors coincide, proving equality.
∎

Theorem 2.2

(Sharp mixed-radius profile stability and equality classification)
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
be nonempty, put
n=|P|
, let
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{S}\subsetneq\Omega
, put
s=|\mathcal{S}|
, and write

M=|\Omega|=q^{d+1},\qquad\alpha=q^{d}-q^{d-1},\qquad\Delta=\sqrt{\alpha ns(1-s/M)}.

Suppose
0\leq\delta<1
and

(4)

|\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})|\geq(1-\delta)\Delta.

Let
\epsilon=\operatorname{sgn}\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})
. Then the sphere-intersection profile satisfies

(5)

\sum_{T\in\Omega}\left(\frac{|P\cap T|-n/q}{\sqrt{\alpha n}}-\epsilon\,\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}(T)-s/M}{\sqrt{s(1-s/M)}}\right)^{2}\leq 2\delta,

and the point-degree profile satisfies

(6)

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}\left(\frac{\mathbf{1}_{P}(x)}{\sqrt{n}}-\epsilon\,\frac{d_{\mathcal{S}}(x)-s/q}{\sqrt{\alpha s(1-s/M)}}\right)^{2}\leq 2\delta.

In particular, equality in (
3
) holds if and only if the following equivalent two-level identities hold:

(7)

\displaystyle|P\cap T|-\frac{n}{q}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{\alpha n}{s(1-s/M)}}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}(T)-\frac{s}{M}\right)

\displaystyle(T\in\Omega),

(8)

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{S}}(x)-\frac{s}{q}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{\alpha s(1-s/M)}{n}}\,\mathbf{1}_{P}(x)

\displaystyle(x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}).

Thus both positive and negative extremizers, and all near-extremizers at the sharp mixed-radius bound, are classified at the level of their complete sphere-intersection and point-degree profiles.

Proof.

Let
B
be the centered incidence matrix from
proposition

2.1
, put

u=B^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{1}_{P},\qquad w=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}-\frac{s}{M}\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}.

Then
u\perp\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}
,
\|u\|_{2}^{2}=\alpha n
,
\|w\|_{2}^{2}=s(1-s/M)
, and

\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})=\langle u,w\rangle.

After normalizing, (
4
) gives

\left\|\frac{u}{\sqrt{\alpha n}}-\epsilon\frac{w}{\sqrt{s(1-s/M)}}\right\|_{2}^{2}=2-2\frac{|\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})|}{\Delta}\leq 2\delta,

which is (
5
).

Since
BB^{\mathsf{T}}=\alpha I
, every singular value of
B
is at most
\sqrt{\alpha}
. Apply
B
to the preceding normalized-vector difference and divide by
\sqrt{\alpha}
. The identities

Bu=BB^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{1}_{P}=\alpha\mathbf{1}_{P},\qquad Bw=B\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}=\bigl(d_{\mathcal{S}}(x)-s/q\bigr)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}

give (
6
). When
\delta=0
, the normalized vectors are equal up to the sign
\epsilon
, yielding (
7
) and (
8
). Conversely, (
7
) directly gives equality in Cauchy–Schwarz. If (
8
) holds, then
\|Bw\|_{2}^{2}=\alpha\|w\|_{2}^{2}
. Since
BB^{\mathsf{T}}=\alpha I
, the operator
\alpha^{-1}B^{\mathsf{T}}B
is the orthogonal projector onto
\operatorname{im}B^{\mathsf{T}}
; hence

and applying
\alpha^{-1}B^{\mathsf{T}}
to (
8

7

∎

Proposition 2.4

(Fixed nonzero radius)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, and let
\mathcal{S}_{r}
be a family of distinct spheres of radius

. Then

(10)

|\sigma(P,\mathcal{S}_{r})|\leq 4q^{(d-1)/2}\sqrt{|P||\mathcal{S}_{r}|}.

The constant
4
is inessential; in particular the deviation scale is
q^{(d-1)/2}
for every nondegenerate
Q
.

Proof.

Fix a nontrivial additive character
\chi
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and identify the dual space with
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
through
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Q}
. For
h:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, use the unnormalized transform

\widehat{h}(\xi)=\sum_{x}h(x)\chi(-\langle\xi,x\rangle_{Q}).

Let
V_{r}=\{x:Q(x)=r\}
. Orthogonality and completion of the square give, for
\xi\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{V_{r}}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=q^{-1}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\chi(-ar)\sum_{x}\chi\bigl(aQ(x)-\langle\xi,x\rangle_{Q}\bigr)

\displaystyle=q^{-1}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\chi\left(-ar-\frac{Q(\xi)}{4a}\right)G_{Q}(a),

where
G_{Q}(a)=\sum_{x}\chi(aQ(x))
has modulus
q^{d/2}
and equals a fixed unit times
\eta(a)^{d}q^{d/2}
, with
\eta
the quadratic character. The remaining sum is a Kloosterman sum when
d
is even and a Salié sum when
d
is odd. Since

, the Weil bound yields

(11)

\max_{\xi\neq 0}|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{V_{r}}}(\xi)|\leq 2q^{(d-1)/2}.

The same calculation at
\xi=0
gives

(12)

\bigl||V_{r}|-q^{d-1}\bigr|\leq 2q^{(d-1)/2}.

See, for example,
[
10
, Chapters 5–6]
for the Gauss-sum evaluation and the Weil bounds.

Let
C
be the set of centers of the spheres in
\mathcal{S}_{r}
. Fourier inversion and Parseval give

\left|I(P,\mathcal{S}_{r})-\frac{|P||C||V_{r}|}{q^{d}}\right|\leq 2q^{(d-1)/2}\sqrt{|P||C|}.

By (
12
), the difference between the displayed main term and
|P||C|/q
is at most

2q^{-(d+1)/2}|P||C|\leq 2q^{(d-1)/2}\sqrt{|P||C|},

because
|P|,|C|\leq q^{d}
. Since
|C|=|\mathcal{S}_{r}|
, the result follows.
∎

Thus

is the natural deviation scale per nonzero radius, whereas (

. The normalization in (
1
) applies the fixed-radius benchmark as a structural threshold to arbitrary sphere families.

2.3.
Radical hyperplanes and pair codegrees

Lemma 2.5

(Radical hyperplane and pair bound)
.

Let
S=S(c,r)
and
S^{\prime}=S(c^{\prime},r^{\prime})
be distinct spheres. If
c=c^{\prime}
, then
S\cap S^{\prime}=\varnothing
. If
c\neq c^{\prime}
, then

S\cap S^{\prime}\subseteq H(S,S^{\prime}),

where

(13)

H(S,S^{\prime})=\left\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:2\langle c^{\prime}-c,x\rangle_{Q}=(r-r^{\prime})-Q(c)+Q(c^{\prime})\right\}.

Thus
|S\cap S^{\prime}|\leq q^{d-1}
. If
d\geq 3
, then

(14)

|S\cap S^{\prime}|\leq 2q^{d-2}.

Proof.

Subtracting
Q(x-c^{\prime})=r^{\prime}
from
Q(x-c)=r
gives (
13
). If
c=c^{\prime}
and
r\neq r^{\prime}
, the two level sets are disjoint. If
c\neq c^{\prime}
, nondegeneracy makes (
13
) a genuine affine hyperplane.

For
d\geq 3
, restrict
Q(x-c)-r
to
H(S,S^{\prime})
, regarded as an affine space of dimension
d-1
. Its homogeneous quadratic part is the restriction of
Q
to the direction subspace of
H(S,S^{\prime})
. This restriction is not identically zero: otherwise that
(d-1)
-dimensional subspace would be totally singular, exceeding the Witt-index bound
\lfloor d/2\rfloor
. The restricted polynomial is therefore nonzero of degree at most two, and the Schwartz–Zippel bound gives (
14
).
∎

The
d=2
exception is genuine: in a split plane, two radius-zero spheres can have an entire isotropic line in common.

Lemma 2.6

(Canonical coaxal completion)
.

For a sphere

put

\Phi(S)=(2c,r-Q(c))\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d+1}.

If
S_{0},S_{1}
are distinct spheres, then the
q
points

\Phi(S_{t})=(1-t)\Phi(S_{0})+t\Phi(S_{1}),\qquad t\in\mathbb{F}_{q},

parametrize
q
distinct spheres. For every
t\neq u
,

S_{t}\cap S_{u}=S_{0}\cap S_{1}.

Thus a sphere pair determines a canonical
q
-member coaxal pencil whose base locus is exactly its common section.

Proof.

The equation of
S_{Q}(c,r)
can be written

Q(x)-2\langle c,x\rangle_{Q}=r-Q(c).

Taking affine combinations of the two endpoint equations gives the sphere with parameter
\Phi(S_{t})
. Since
\Phi(S_{0})\neq\Phi(S_{1})
, the
q
parameters are distinct. A point satisfying the two equations indexed by

satisfies both endpoint equations, and the converse is immediate.
∎

2.4.
Freiman-type classification at the dense endpoint

The fixed-scale results of
section

3
begin near the random baseline and extract local common sections. At the opposite, maximal-incidence endpoint one can classify the entire configuration, up to deletion, by a finite-rank linear system in lifted parameter space. This is the paper’s Freiman-type inverse theorem.

For
y=(u,a)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, put

(15)

\Lambda_{x}(y)=Q(x)-\langle x,u\rangle_{Q}-a.

Thus, for every sphere
S
,

(16)

x\in S\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Lambda_{x}(\Phi(S))=0.

If
L\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d+1}
is an affine subspace, define its sphere system and base locus by

(17)

\mathcal{S}(L)=\Phi^{-1}(L),\qquad B(L)=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:\Lambda_{x}(y)=0\text{ for every }y\in L\}.

Every point of
B(L)
lies on every sphere of
\mathcal{S}(L)
.

Proposition 2.7

(Exact lifted-linear models and normal form)
.

Let
P\neq\varnothing
and let
\mathcal{S}
be a nonempty family of distinct
Q
-spheres. Then

I(P,\mathcal{S})=|P||\mathcal{S}|

if and only if, with
L=\operatorname{aff}\Phi(\mathcal{S})
,

P\subseteq B(L),\qquad\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}(L).

Whenever
B(L)\neq\varnothing
, one has
\dim L\leq d
, and the model admits the following explicit normal form. Write
L=y_{0}+U
, where

y_{0}=\Phi(S_{Q}(c_{0},r_{0})).

Then there are an
r
-dimensional subspace
W\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, a linear functional
\beta:W\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, where
r=\dim L
, such that

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(L)

\displaystyle=\left\{S_{Q}\left(c_{0}+\frac{w}{2},r_{0}+\beta(w)+\langle c_{0},w\rangle_{Q}+\frac{1}{4}Q(w)\right):w\in W\right\},

(19)

\displaystyle B(L)

\displaystyle=S_{Q}(c_{0},r_{0})\cap\bigl(z+W^{\perp_{Q}}\bigr),\qquad\langle z,w\rangle_{Q}=-\beta(w)\quad(w\in W).

Conversely, every such choice has the displayed base locus; when that base is nonempty, any nonempty subsets of it and of the sphere system form a complete point–sphere model. Rank
r=1
is precisely a coaxal pencil; higher ranks are explicit higher-dimensional linear sphere systems with a common affine-quadric base.

Proof.

If every pair in
P\times\mathcal{S}
is incident, then for each
x\in P
the affine-linear functional
\Lambda_{x}
vanishes on
\Phi(\mathcal{S})
and hence on its affine span
L
. Thus
P\subseteq B(L)
. The converse follows immediately from (
17
).

Suppose
B(L)\neq\varnothing
and write a direction vector in
U
as
(w,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The projection
U\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is injective: if
(0,b)\in U
and
x\in B(L)
, then vanishing of
\Lambda_{x}
on
y_{0}+t(0,b)
for all
t
forces

. Hence
r=\dim U\leq d
, the projected space
W
has dimension
r
, and

U=\{(w,\beta(w)):w\in W\}

for a unique linear functional
\beta
. Nondegeneracy of
Q
gives
z
with
\langle z,w\rangle_{Q}=-\beta(w)
for all
w\in W
.

A point
x
belongs to
B(L)
exactly when it satisfies the equation corresponding to
y_{0}
and

\langle x,w\rangle_{Q}=-\beta(w)=\langle z,w\rangle_{Q}\qquad(w\in W),

which is (
19
). Finally, the lift
y_{0}+(w,\beta(w))
has center
c_{0}+w/2
and radius

r_{0}-Q(c_{0})+\beta(w)+Q\left(c_{0}+\frac{w}{2}\right)=r_{0}+\beta(w)+\langle c_{0},w\rangle_{Q}+\frac{1}{4}Q(w),

proving (
18
). The converse is the same calculation in reverse.
∎

For nonempty
P
and
\mathcal{S}
, define the missing-incidence density

(20)

\mu(P,\mathcal{S})=1-\frac{I(P,\mathcal{S})}{|P||\mathcal{S}|}.

Thus
\mu=0
is the exact endpoint, while

\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})=(1-\mu-q^{-1})|P||\mathcal{S}|.

Theorem 2.8

(Dense-endpoint classification)
.

Let
d\geq 2
, let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
be nonempty, let
\mathcal{S}
be a nonempty family of distinct
Q
-spheres, and put
n=|P|
,
s=|\mathcal{S}|
, and
\mu=\mu(P,\mathcal{S})
. Let

0<\theta<\frac{1}{d+2},\qquad\mu<\theta.

Then there are an affine subspace
L\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d+1}
of dimension at most
d
and subsets
P_{0}\subseteq P
,
\mathcal{S}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
such that

(21)

\displaystyle P_{0}

\displaystyle\subseteq B(L),

\displaystyle|P_{0}|

\displaystyle\geq\bigl(1-(d+1)\theta\bigr)n,

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{0}

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{S}(L),

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}_{0}|

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\theta}\right)s.

In particular, every point of
P_{0}
lies on every sphere of
\mathcal{S}_{0}
, and the retained configuration is one of the explicit rank-
r
models in
proposition

2.7
.

If

(23)

0<\mu<\frac{d+1}{(d+2)^{2}}

and

\delta=\sqrt{(d+1)\mu},

then one may choose

(24)

|P_{0}|\geq(1-\delta)n,\qquad|\mathcal{S}_{0}|\geq(1-\delta)s.

For
\mu=0
, the same conclusion holds with
P_{0}=P
,
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\mathcal{S}
, and
\delta=0
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mu>0
. Call a sphere
S\in\mathcal{S}
good when

|P\setminus S|\leq\theta n

and let
\mathcal{S}_{0}
be the good subfamily. Counting missing incidences by spheres gives

\theta n\,|\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{0}|\leq\mu ns,

which proves (
22
); in particular
\mathcal{S}_{0}\neq\varnothing
because
\mu<\theta
.

Let
L=\operatorname{aff}\Phi(\mathcal{S}_{0})
and put
r=\dim L
. Initially
r\leq d+1
. Choose
r+1
members
S_{0},\ldots,S_{r}
whose lifts form an affine basis of
L
, and set

P_{0}=P\cap S_{0}\cap\cdots\cap S_{r}.

The union bound and goodness give

|P_{0}|\geq\bigl(1-(r+1)\theta\bigr)n\geq\bigl(1-(d+2)\theta\bigr)n>0.

Fix
x\in P_{0}
. The affine-linear functional
\Lambda_{x}
vanishes on an affine basis of
L
, hence on all of
L
. Thus
L
is contained in the affine hyperplane
\{y:\Lambda_{x}(y)=0\}
, so
r\leq d
. Repeating the union-bound estimate with this improved dimension gives (
21
). The same affine-linearity argument shows
P_{0}\subseteq B(L)
and
\mathcal{S}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{S}(L)
.

Under (
23
), take

\theta=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{d+1}}.

Then
\theta<1/(d+2)
and
\mu<\theta
, while

(d+1)\theta=\frac{\mu}{\theta}=\sqrt{(d+1)\mu}=\delta.

This proves (
24
). If
\mu=0
, every incidence is present, and
proposition

2.7
applies directly.
∎

Definition 2.9

(Distance from a lifted-linear model)
.

For nonempty
P
and
\mathcal{S}
, put

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{lin}}(P,\mathcal{S})=\inf_{L:B(L)\neq\varnothing}\left(\frac{|P\setminus B(L)|}{|P|}+\frac{|\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}(L)|}{|\mathcal{S}|}\right).

Corollary 2.10

(Quantitative model-distance equivalence)
.

Let
\mu=\mu(P,\mathcal{S})
. Then

(25)

\mu\leq\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{lin}}(P,\mathcal{S}).

If
\mu<(d+1)/(d+2)^{2}
, then

(26)

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{lin}}(P,\mathcal{S})\leq 2\sqrt{(d+1)\mu}.

Equivalently, near-maximizers of the sharp endpoint bound

\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})\leq(1-q^{-1})|P||\mathcal{S}|

are, up to an explicit square-root stability loss, exactly the lifted-linear sphere systems of
proposition

2.7
. More explicitly, if

\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})\geq(1-\eta)(1-q^{-1})|P||\mathcal{S}|,

then
\mu\leq\eta(1-q^{-1})
, and whenever this quantity satisfies (
23
),

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{lin}}(P,\mathcal{S})\leq 2\sqrt{(d+1)\eta(1-q^{-1})}.

Proof.

For an affine
L
in
definition

2.9
, set

a=\frac{|P\setminus B(L)|}{|P|},\qquad b=\frac{|\mathcal{S}\setminus\mathcal{S}(L)|}{|\mathcal{S}|}.

The complete subconfiguration
(P\cap B(L))\times(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{S}(L))
gives

I(P,\mathcal{S})\geq(1-a)(1-b)|P||\mathcal{S}|\geq(1-a-b)|P||\mathcal{S}|.

Hence
\mu\leq a+b
, and taking the infimum proves (
25
). The upper bound follows from
theorem

2.8
.
∎

Proposition 2.11

(The square-root deletion loss is sharp)
.

There are dense-endpoint systems in split dimension four for which

\mu(P,\mathcal{S})\asymp q^{-1}\qquad\text{but}\qquad\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{lin}}(P,\mathcal{S})\gg q^{-1/2}.

Consequently the exponent
1/2
in (
26
) cannot be improved in general.

Proof.

Use coordinates in which

Q(x)=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}x_{4}

on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{4}
, and let
e_{1},e_{3}
be the first and third coordinate vectors. In lifted parameter space put

L_{0}=\{(te_{1},0):t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},\qquad L_{1}=\{(te_{1}+ue_{3},0):t,u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}.

Then
L_{0}\subset L_{1}
and a direct use of (
15
) gives

\displaystyle B(L_{0})

\displaystyle=\{x_{2}=0,\ x_{3}x_{4}=0\},

\displaystyle B(L_{1})

\displaystyle=\{x_{2}=x_{4}=0\}.

Let
P_{0}=B(L_{1})
, so
|P_{0}|=q^{2}
, and choose

P_{1}\subseteq\{x_{2}=x_{3}=0,\ x_{4}\neq 0\}

of cardinality
a=\lfloor q^{3/2}\rfloor
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\mathcal{S}(L_{0})
, so
|\mathcal{S}_{0}|=q
, and choose

\mathcal{S}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{S}(L_{1})\setminus\mathcal{S}(L_{0})

of cardinality
b=\lfloor q^{1/2}\rfloor
. Put

P=P_{0}\sqcup P_{1},\qquad\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{0}\sqcup\mathcal{S}_{1}.

Every point of
P_{0}
lies on every sphere of
\mathcal{S}
. Every point of
P_{1}
lies on every sphere of
\mathcal{S}_{0}
and on no sphere of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
: indeed, if the lift is
(te_{1}+ue_{3},0)
with
u\neq 0
, then for
x\in P_{1}
,

\Lambda_{x}(te_{1}+ue_{3},0)=-\frac{ux_{4}}{2}\neq 0.

Thus the missing incidence graph is exactly the rectangle
P_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{1}
, and

\mu(P,\mathcal{S})=\frac{ab}{(q^{2}+a)(q+b)}\asymp q^{-1}.

For any affine
L
with
B(L)\neq\varnothing
, the complete product
B(L)\times\mathcal{S}(L)
cannot contain both a point of
P_{1}
and a sphere of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Hence either
P_{1}\cap B(L)=\varnothing
or
\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap\mathcal{S}(L)=\varnothing
. Therefore

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{lin}}(P,\mathcal{S})\geq\min\left\{\frac{a}{q^{2}+a},\frac{b}{q+b}\right\}\asymp q^{-1/2},

which proves the claim.
∎

Corollary 2.12

(Coaxal stability under codimension-two nonconcentration)
.

Assume the hypotheses of
theorem

2.8
, use the symmetric conclusion (
24
), and suppose that for some
\gamma>\delta
,

(27)

|P\cap V|\leq(1-\gamma)|P|

for every affine flat
V\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
of codimension two. Then the classifying space
L
has dimension at most one. If
(1-\delta)|\mathcal{S}|\geq 2
, then
\dim L=1
: after deleting at most a
\delta
-fraction from each side, the remaining spheres lie in one coaxal pencil and the remaining points lie in its common sphere-pair section.

Proof.

If
r=\dim L\geq 2
, (
19
) places
B(L)
inside an affine flat of codimension
r
, hence inside a codimension-two affine flat. But
P_{0}\subseteq B(L)
and
|P_{0}|\geq(1-\delta)|P|>(1-\gamma)|P|
, contradicting (
27
). Thus
r\leq 1
. If
\mathcal{S}_{0}
contains at least two distinct spheres, then its affine span has dimension one, and
lemma

2.6
identifies the model as a coaxal pencil.
∎

Remark 2.13

(Why this is a classification theorem)
.

The conclusion is global rather than extractive. It gives a complete list of exact ambient models, parametrized by affine subspaces
L\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d+1}
with nonempty base, proves that every dense-endpoint near-extremizer becomes a member of this list after deleting an explicit fraction from each side, and proves a converse estimate in the same edit metric. For fixed
d
there are only
q^{O_{d}(1)}
possible ambient spaces
L
. Arbitrary subsets of
B(L)
and
\mathcal{S}(L)
are allowed, just as a Freiman theorem places a set inside a structured ambient progression rather than prescribing every element of the set.

2.5.
Affine singular pencils

An affine flat
A=a+W
has totally singular direction if
Q(w)=0
for every
w\in W
. Polarization gives
W\subseteq W^{\perp_{Q}}
. Define the affine singular pencil through
A
by

(28)

\mathcal{P}(A)=\{S_{Q}(c,Q(a-c)):c-a\in W^{\perp_{Q}}\}.

If
x=a+w\in A
and
c-a\in W^{\perp_{Q}}
, then

Q(x-c)=Q(w-(c-a))=Q(c-a)=Q(a-c),

so every member of
\mathcal{P}(A)
contains all of
A
.

Example 2.14

(One pencil and the adaptive scale)
.

Let
P\subseteq A
have
n
points and let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(A)
have
s
members. Then

I(P,\mathcal{S})=ns,\qquad\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})=(1-q^{-1})ns.

If
K
is the exact parameter in (
1
), then

(29)

\frac{K^{2}q^{d-1}}{s}=(1-q^{-1})^{2}n.

Thus the scale
K^{2}q^{d-1}/s
in
corollary

3.2
is optimal up to an absolute constant.

When
d\geq 3
, nondegenerate quadratic spaces over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are isotropic, so one may take
W
to be a singular line. The nonzero-radius part of such a pencil has
\gg q^{d-1}
members, and every nonempty fiber at an attained nonzero radius has
\asymp_{d}q^{d-2}
members. Thus the mixed-radius scale
q^{d/2}
is necessary, while fixed-radius slices attain the
q^{(d-1)/2}
scale up to constants.

If
d=2m+1
and
W
is maximal totally singular, then
W^{\perp_{Q}}/W
is a nondegenerate one-dimensional quadratic space. Hence
(q-1)q^{m}
members of the pencil have nonzero radius. Choosing admissible integers
n\leq q^{m}
and
s\leq(q-1)q^{m}
with

gives

\frac{Kq^{(d-1)/2}}{C_{1}}\asymp n,

so the

dependence in the moderate theorem is optimal throughout the range realized by the pencil.

If
d=2m
and

is split, a maximal totally singular

satisfies

. All members of the corresponding maximal pencil then have radius zero. Taking
P=W
and

gives

and
Kq^{(d-1)/2}\asymp|P|
, including the split planar case. The fixed-nonzero-radius block examples of
section

4
show that radius zero is not responsible for the failure of proportional concentration in dimensions at least four.

3.
Scale-adaptive inverse theorems

3.1.
Exact energy extraction

For
P
and
\mathcal{S}
, define the point-degree energy

E(P,\mathcal{S})=\sum_{p\in P}d_{\mathcal{S}}(p)^{2}.

Expanding the square gives

(30)

E(P,\mathcal{S})=I(P,\mathcal{S})+E_{\mathrm{off}}(P,\mathcal{S}),

where

E_{\mathrm{off}}(P,\mathcal{S})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}\\
S\neq S^{\prime}\end{subarray}}|P\cap S\cap S^{\prime}|.

Theorem 3.1

(Exact scale-adaptive extraction)
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
be nonempty and let
\mathcal{S}
be a nonempty family of distinct spheres. Put

n=|P|,\qquad s=|\mathcal{S}|,\qquad I=I(P,\mathcal{S}),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{0}=\frac{I^{2}}{n}-I.

Assume
\mathcal{E}_{0}>0
, and let
0<\eta<1
. Define

\lambda_{\eta}=\eta\frac{\mathcal{E}_{0}}{s^{2}}

and let
\mathcal{R}_{\eta}
be the set of ordered pairs
(S,S^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}^{2}
,
S\neq S^{\prime}
, with

|P\cap S\cap S^{\prime}|\geq\lambda_{\eta}.

Then

(31)

\sum_{(S,S^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}_{\eta}}|P\cap S\cap S^{\prime}|\geq(1-\eta)\mathcal{E}_{0}.

Every pair in
\mathcal{R}_{\eta}
is nonconcentric, generates a canonical coaxal pencil whose base locus contains at least
\lambda_{\eta}
points of
P
, and has a radical hyperplane containing at least the same number. Moreover,

(32)

|\mathcal{R}_{\eta}|\geq\frac{(1-\eta)\mathcal{E}_{0}}{q^{d-1}},

and, if
d\geq 3
,

(33)

|\mathcal{R}_{\eta}|\geq\frac{(1-\eta)\mathcal{E}_{0}}{2q^{d-2}}.

Proof.

Cauchy–Schwarz gives
E(P,\mathcal{S})\geq I^{2}/n
, hence by (
30
),

(34)

E_{\mathrm{off}}(P,\mathcal{S})\geq\mathcal{E}_{0}.

Pairs outside
\mathcal{R}_{\eta}
contribute less than

\lambda_{\eta}s^{2}=\eta\mathcal{E}_{0}.

Subtracting from (
34
) proves (
31
). A contributing pair has nonempty intersection and therefore is nonconcentric. The radical-hyperplane conclusion follows from
lemma

2.5
. Dividing (
31
) by
q^{d-1}
gives (
32
); for
d\geq 3
, division by
2q^{d-2}
gives (
33
).
∎

3.2.
Fixed-scale and moderate consequences

Corollary 3.2

(Fixed-scale adaptive inverse theorem)
.

Let

be nonempty, let
\mathcal{S}
be a nonempty family of distinct spheres, and let

. Suppose

\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})\geq Kq^{(d-1)/2}\sqrt{|P||\mathcal{S}|}

and

K^{2}q^{d-1}|\mathcal{S}|\geq 4|P|.

Then at least
K^{2}|\mathcal{S}|/4
ordered pairs satisfy

|P\cap S\cap S^{\prime}|\geq\frac{K^{2}q^{d-1}}{4|\mathcal{S}|}.

Every such pair supplies a radical hyperplane containing at least the same number of points. At least
K^{2}/8
spheres each have at least
K^{2}/8
witnessing partners. If

, at least
K^{2}q|\mathcal{S}|/8
ordered pairs satisfy (

), and at least
K^{2}q/16
spheres each have at least
K^{2}q/16
witnessing partners.

Proof.

Put
n=|P|
,

, and
\Sigma=\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})
. By (
35
) and (

),
\Sigma\geq 2n
. Since

and

Apply

3.1
with
\eta=1/2
. Its threshold is at least
K^{2}q^{d-1}/(4s)
, and its rich energy is at least

. Division by the pair bounds in

2.5
gives the asserted ordered-pair counts.

For the partner statements, let

be the number of witnessing partners of

. If

, then vertices with

contribute less than

, while each remaining vertex contributes at most

; hence at least

vertices have at least

partners. Use

, and, when

,

.
∎

Since

, the simpler condition

implies (

).

, and let

; the upper bound in (

) into (

).

Corollary 3.4

(Stability)
.

Let

be nonempty, let

be a nonempty family of distinct spheres, let

3.3.
Exact sphere-complement duality and its limited omitted-family consequence

The complete mixed-radius design has an exact complement symmetry. The following auxiliary proposition records its consequence for deficits. As quantified below, the pair-section threshold is structurally nontrivial only when the omitted family is very small.

Proposition 3.5

(Complement duality and an omitted-family corollary)
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
be nonempty, put
n=|P|
, and let
\mathcal{S}\subsetneq\Omega
. Write

Then

(40)

\sigma(P,\overline{\mathcal{S}})=D.

(41)

\frac{D^{2}}{4nq^{d-1}}

ordered pairs of distinct omitted spheres

satisfy

\frac{D^{2}}{8nq^{d-2}}.

K^{2}|\mathcal{S}|/4
ordered pairs satisfying

(45)

|P\cap T\cap T^{\prime}|\geq\frac{K^{2}q^{d-1}|\mathcal{S}|}{4\bar{s}^{2}},

and at least
K^{2}q|\mathcal{S}|/8
such pairs when

.

Proof.

Every point lies on
q^{d}
members of
\Omega
, so
\sigma(P,\Omega)=0
and therefore

\sigma(P,\overline{\mathcal{S}})=D.

Put
\bar{I}=I(P,\overline{\mathcal{S}})
. Since
\bar{I}\geq D\geq 2n
and
x\mapsto x^{2}/n-x
is increasing for

,

\frac{\bar{I}^{2}}{n}-\bar{I}\geq\frac{D^{2}}{n}-D\geq\frac{D^{2}}{2n}.

Apply
theorem

3.1
to
(P,\overline{\mathcal{S}})
with
\eta=1/2
. The threshold is at least
D^{2}/(4n\bar{s}^{2})
, and the rich energy is at least

. Division by the pair bounds
q^{d-1}
and, for

proves (

)–(

). Under (

∎

Remark 3.6

(Nontriviality range)
.

The proposition is an exact statement about the complete mixed-radius design, but it is usually formal rather than structural. By (
3
), with
s=|\mathcal{S}|
and
M=|\Omega|=q^{d+1}
,

D^{2}\leq(q^{d}-q^{d-1})ns\left(1-\frac{s}{M}\right)=\frac{q-1}{q^{2}}\,ns\bar{s}.

Consequently

(46)

\frac{D^{2}}{4n\bar{s}^{2}}\leq\frac{q-1}{4q^{2}}\frac{s}{\bar{s}}.

In particular, if
s\leq M/2
, then the section-size threshold in (
42
) is strictly smaller than
1
, so the conclusion gives no more than nonemptiness. For that threshold to exceed
1
, it is necessary that

\frac{s}{\bar{s}}>\frac{4q^{2}}{q-1};

thus the omitted family must be an
O(q^{-1})
fraction of the full sphere system. The pair-count bound is also nonstructural in a large natural range. If
s\leq q^{d}/2
, then every point belongs to at least
q^{d}-s
omitted spheres, so one point alone supplies at least

(q^{d}-s)(q^{d}-s-1)

ordered omitted pairs with nonempty
P
-section. Meanwhile (
3
) gives

\frac{D^{2}}{4nq^{d-1}}\leq\frac{q-1}{4}s\leq\frac{q-1}{8}q^{d}<\frac{q^{d}}{2}\left(\frac{q^{d}}{2}-1\right)

for
q\geq 3
and
d\geq 2
. Hence the stated pair count is weaker there than this trivial one-point count. We therefore use
proposition

3.5
only as a complement-duality observation, not as an intrinsic inverse theorem for deficits in the original family. A deficit inside one prescribed fixed-radius class depends on near-extremal Kloosterman and Salié Fourier directions and is not resolved here.

3.4.
A dense rich-pair core in dimension three

Proposition 3.7

(Positive-density rich-pair core in

)
.

Assume the hypotheses of
corollary

3.3
with

, and put
s=|\mathcal{S}|
. Let

\mathcal{S}

By

∎

3.5.
A log-free incidence-rich refinement

Proposition 3.8

(High-degree rich section without logarithmic loss)
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
be nonempty, let
\mathcal{S}
be a nonempty family of distinct spheres, let
K>0
, and let
C_{1}\geq 1
. Suppose (

(49)

\frac{64|P|}{K^{2}q^{d-1}}\leq|\mathcal{S}|\leq C_{1}Kq^{(d-1)/2}.

Put

,

,
I=I(P,\mathcal{S})
, and

M=\left\lceil\frac{I}{2n}\right\rceil.

There are two nonconcentric spheres
S_{0},S_{1}\in\mathcal{S}
, a set

P^{\prime}\subseteq P\cap S_{0}\cap S_{1},

and a subfamily
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
such that

(1)

|P^{\prime}|\geq Kq^{(d-1)/2}/(8C_{1})

(2)

(3)

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}|P^{\prime}\cap S|\geq\frac{M}{2}|P^{\prime}|,\qquad|\mathcal{S}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{M}{2}.

Let

A=\{p\in P:d_{\mathcal{S}}(p)\geq M\}.

Since
M=\lceil I/(2n)\rceil
, points outside
A
have degree strictly less than
I/(2n)
and contribute less than
I/2
incidences. Hence

(50)

I(A,\mathcal{S})>\frac{I}{2}.

The lower bound in (

On

Also

, and therefore

which is smaller than (

S_{0},S_{1}
satisfies

|A\cap S_{0}\cap S_{1}|\geq\lambda.

Take

.

Finally, let

Since

, spheres outside

contribute less than

. Thus the asserted incidence lower bound holds, and division by the trivial upper bound
|P^{\prime}|
for each summand gives

.
∎

3.6.
Decomposition of the positive surplus

Fix
\mathcal{S}
and write

e_{\mathcal{S}}(p)=d_{\mathcal{S}}(p)-\frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{q},\qquad P_{\mathrm{pos}}=\{p\in P:e_{\mathcal{S}}(p)>0\}.

For
A\subseteq P
, abbreviate
\sigma(A)=\sum_{p\in A}e_{\mathcal{S}}(p)
.

Theorem 3.9

(Positive-surplus decomposition into section-supported pieces)
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
be nonempty, let
\mathcal{S}
be a nonempty family of distinct spheres, and let
K>0
. Assume (
35
). Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and suppose

(52)

\varepsilon^{2}K^{2}q^{d-1}|\mathcal{S}|\geq 4|P|.

Then
P
has a partition

P=R_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup R_{T}\sqcup P_{\mathrm{rem}}

with the following properties.

(1)

For every
j
, there is a nonconcentric pair
(S_{j},S_{j}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}^{2}
such that

(53)

R_{j}\subseteq P_{\mathrm{pos}}\cap S_{j}\cap S_{j}^{\prime},\qquad|R_{j}|\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}K^{2}q^{d-1}}{4|\mathcal{S}|}.

(2)

Every extracted piece has strictly positive surplus:
\sigma(R_{j})>0
.

(3)

The number of pieces satisfies

T\leq\frac{4|P||\mathcal{S}|}{\varepsilon^{2}K^{2}q^{d-1}}.

(4)

The remainder obeys

\sigma(P_{\mathrm{rem}})<\varepsilon\sigma(P,\mathcal{S}),

and the sections carry the remaining net surplus:

\sum_{j=1}^{T}\sigma(R_{j})>(1-\varepsilon)\sigma(P,\mathcal{S}).

If
|\mathcal{S}|\leq C_{1}Kq^{(d-1)/2}
, then every piece has size at least

\frac{\varepsilon^{2}Kq^{(d-1)/2}}{4C_{1}}.

Proof.

Put
s=|\mathcal{S}|
,
n=|P|
, and
\Sigma_{0}=\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})>0
. Split

P=P_{\mathrm{pos}}\sqcup P_{\mathrm{non}},\qquad P_{\mathrm{non}}=\{p\in P:e_{\mathcal{S}}(p)\leq 0\}.

Then
\sigma(P_{\mathrm{non}})\leq 0
, so
\sigma(P_{\mathrm{pos}})\geq\Sigma_{0}
.

Start with
A_{0}=P_{\mathrm{pos}}
. Given
A_{t}\subseteq P_{\mathrm{pos}}
, continue while

\sigma(A_{t})\geq\varepsilon\Sigma_{0}.

By (
35
) and (
52
),

\sigma(A_{t})\geq\varepsilon\Sigma_{0}\geq\varepsilon Kq^{(d-1)/2}\sqrt{ns}\geq 2n\geq 2|A_{t}|.

Define the exact residual parameter

K_{t}=\frac{\sigma(A_{t})}{q^{(d-1)/2}\sqrt{|A_{t}|s}}.

The inequality
\sigma(A_{t})\geq 2|A_{t}|
is equivalent to
K_{t}^{2}q^{d-1}s\geq 4|A_{t}|
, so
corollary

3.2
applies to
(A_{t},\mathcal{S})
and gives a pair whose common section in

has size at least

\frac{K_{t}^{2}q^{d-1}}{4s}=\frac{\sigma(A_{t})^{2}}{4|A_{t}|s^{2}}\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}K^{2}q^{d-1}}{4s}.

Set

R_{t+1}=A_{t}\cap S_{t+1}\cap S_{t+1}^{\prime},\qquad A_{t+1}=A_{t}\setminus R_{t+1}.

Every point of
A_{t}
has positive excess, hence
\sigma(R_{t+1})>0
. The pieces are disjoint and have the lower bound in (
53
), so the asserted bound on
T
follows.

Let
A_{T}
be the final positive-excess remainder and put

P_{\mathrm{rem}}=A_{T}\sqcup P_{\mathrm{non}}.

At termination
\sigma(A_{T})<\varepsilon\Sigma_{0}
, and therefore

\sigma(P_{\mathrm{rem}})=\sigma(A_{T})+\sigma(P_{\mathrm{non}})<\varepsilon\Sigma_{0}.

Additivity over the partition gives the final surplus inequality. The moderate-family estimate follows immediately from the upper cap.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

The geometry in
theorem

lies entirely in the necessity direction. Conversely, for any partition of

, the identity

Lemma 3.11

(Surplus assembly criterion)
.

Let
P=R_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup R_{T}\sqcup P_{\mathrm{rem}}
be any partition, and fix a nonempty family
\mathcal{S}
of distinct spheres. Suppose that, for some
K>0
and

,

\sum_{j=1}^{T}\sigma(R_{j},\mathcal{S})\geq Kq^{(d-1)/2}\sqrt{|P||\mathcal{S}|}

Then
(P,\mathcal{S})
has a
(1-\theta)K
-large fixed-scale surplus. A sufficient pointwise condition for the first inequality is the existence of numbers

such that

over disjoint point sets gives the first assertion. Under the pointwise condition,

∎

4.
Atomicity obstructions

The decomposition theorem cannot in general be replaced by one proportional section, nor can a dense rich-pair graph be forced into one coaxal pencil or a centered direct sum. We give two structurally different obstruction classes. The first consists of exact centered blocks at one fixed nonzero radius. The second consists of diffuse root-evaluation designs carried by rational normal curves in lifted parameter space.

4.1.
The isotropic quotient

Let
d\geq 3
. Since every nondegenerate quadratic form in at least three variables over a finite field is isotropic, choose
v\neq 0
with
Q(v)=0
. Choose
w\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
with

\langle w,v\rangle_{Q}=1.

The restriction of
Q
to
v^{\perp_{Q}}
has radical
\mathbb{F}_{q}v
and therefore induces a nondegenerate quadratic form
\overline{Q}
on

\overline{V}=v^{\perp_{Q}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}v,\qquad\dim\overline{V}=d-2.

Choose a represented value
R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and put

(54)

X_{R}=\{y\in v^{\perp_{Q}}:Q(y)=R\},\qquad N_{R}=|X_{R}|.

Because
Q(y+uv)=Q(y)
on
v^{\perp_{Q}}
, every point of the quotient level set has exactly
q
lifts, so

(55)

N_{R}=q\,|\{\bar{y}\in\overline{V}:\overline{Q}(\bar{y})=R\}|.

The standard evaluation of quadratic level sets gives

(56)

N_{R}=q^{d-2}+O_{d}\bigl(q^{(d-1)/2}\bigr)\qquad(d\geq 4).

When
d=3
,
R
may be chosen so that
N_{R}=2q
. We use only that
N_{R}\asymp_{d}q^{d-2}
for
d\geq 4
. See, for example,
[
10
, Chapter 6]
.

4.2.
Exact centered-incidence blocks

Let
Z\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
be nonempty, with
|Z|=t
. For
z\in Z
, define

(57)

c_{z}=zw,\qquad A_{z}=c_{z}+X_{R},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{z}=\{S_{Q}(c_{z}+uv,R):u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}.

Finally put

(58)

P=\bigsqcup_{z\in Z}A_{z},\qquad\mathcal{S}=\bigsqcup_{z\in Z}\mathcal{S}_{z}.

The unions are disjoint: every
x\in A_{z}
satisfies
\langle x,v\rangle_{Q}=z
, and the centers
zw+uv
are distinct as
(z,u)
varies. Every sphere in
\mathcal{S}
has the same nonzero radius
R
.

Theorem 4.1

(Exact fixed-radius block system)
.

For the configuration in (
57
)–(
58
), the following hold.

(1)

The sizes are

(59)

|P|=tN_{R},\qquad|\mathcal{S}|=tq.

(2)

The centered block-incidence matrix is diagonal:

(60)

\sigma(A_{b},\mathcal{S}_{z})=\begin{cases}(q-1)N_{R},&b=z,\\
0,&b\neq z.\end{cases}

Consequently

(61)

\sigma(P,\mathcal{S})=t(q-1)N_{R}=\sum_{z\in Z}\sigma(A_{z},\mathcal{S}_{z}).

The exact parameter in the fixed-scale normalization (
1
) is

(62)

K_{*}=(1-q^{-1})\frac{\sqrt{N_{R}}}{q^{(d-2)/2}}.

Thus
K_{*}\to 1
for
d\geq 4
, while in
d=3
one may take

K_{*}=(1-q^{-1})\sqrt{2}
.

(3)

Every point of
P
has degree

(63)

d_{\mathcal{S}}(x)=q+t-1.

(4)

If
u\neq u^{\prime}
, then

(64)

S_{Q}(c_{z}+uv,R)\cap S_{Q}(c_{z}+u^{\prime}v,R)=A_{z}.

All these pairs have center-difference direction
[v]
, and their radical hyperplane is

(65)

H_{z}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:\langle x,v\rangle_{Q}=z\}.

(5)

If
d\geq 4
and
H
is an affine hyperplane, then

(66)

|P\cap H|\leq\max\{N_{R},\,2tq^{d-3}\}.

In particular, there is a constant
B_{d}>0
such that, for all sufficiently large

,

(67)

\max_{H}\frac{|P\cap H|}{|P|}\leq B_{d}\max\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{q}\right\}.

Proof.

The size identities follow from
|A_{z}|=N_{R}
and
|\mathcal{S}_{z}|=q
.

Fix
x=c_{b}+y\in A_{b}
, where
y\in v^{\perp_{Q}}
and
Q(y)=R
, and consider a sphere
S_{Q}(c_{z}+uv,R)
. Put
a=b-z
. Since
Q(v)=0
,
\langle y,v\rangle_{Q}=0
, and
\langle w,v\rangle_{Q}=1
,

\displaystyle Q(x-c_{z}-uv)

\displaystyle=Q(aw+y-uv)

(68)

\displaystyle=R+a^{2}Q(w)+2a\langle w,y\rangle_{Q}-2au.

If
b=z
, the right-hand side is
R
for every
u
, so every sphere of
\mathcal{S}_{z}
contains all of
A_{z}
. If
b\neq z
, equation (
68
) equals
R
for exactly one value of

N_{R},&b\neq z,\end{cases}

and subtracting the baseline
|A_{b}||\mathcal{S}_{z}|/q=N_{R}
proves (
60
). Summation proves (
61
). Substitution of (
59
) into (
1
) gives (
62
). The asymptotics follow from (
56
), and the degree formula follows from the same membership calculation: a point lies on all
q
spheres in its own block and exactly one sphere in each of the other

blocks.

For
u\neq u^{\prime}
, subtracting the two sphere equations gives
\langle x,v\rangle_{Q}=z

with

; either sphere equation then reduces to

, proving (
64
), and the same subtraction proves (
65
).

It remains to prove the hyperplane bound. Write
H=\{x:\ell(x)=a_{0}\}
with
\ell\neq 0
. If
\ell
vanishes on
v^{\perp_{Q}}
, then

. Since
\ell(w)\neq 0

are distinct, so

Suppose that

\ell(v)\neq 0

has exactly one representative satisfying the affine equation defining

where the final inequality is the Schwartz–Zippel bound for the nonzero polynomial
\overline{Q}-R
. If
\ell(v)=0

\ell
descends to a nonzero linear functional
\bar{\ell}
on

is a nonzero polynomial. Indeed, if it vanished identically, then
\overline{Q}
would vanish on

. When

would have isotropic direction

, forcing

quotient points, each with

Summing over

proves (

for large

∎

5.1.
The centered affine-hyperplane design

Let
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}
be the family of all affine hyperplanes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. Its cardinality is

|\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}|=q\frac{q^{d}-1}{q-1}.

Lemma 5.1

(Affine-hyperplane design)
.

Let
A
be the point–hyperplane incidence matrix and put
B=A-q^{-1}J_{0}
. Then

BB^{\mathsf{T}}=q^{d-1}I-q^{-1}J,\qquad B\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}}=0.

Consequently, for every
X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}
,

(108)

\left|I(X,\mathcal{H})-\frac{|X||\mathcal{H}|}{q}\right|\leq\sqrt{q^{d-1}|X|-\frac{|X|^{2}}{q}}\sqrt{|\mathcal{H}|\left(1-\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{|\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}|}\right)}.

Equality holds for a singleton
X
and the family of all affine hyperplanes through its point.

Proof.

Every point lies on
(q^{d}-1)/(q-1)
affine hyperplanes, while two distinct points lie on
(q^{d-1}-1)/(q-1)
common hyperplanes. Hence

AA^{\mathsf{T}}=\frac{q^{d-1}-1}{q-1}J+q^{d-1}I.

Since every row of
A
has sum
(q^{d}-1)/(q-1)
and
|\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}|=q(q^{d}-1)/(q-1)
, expanding the centered product gives the matrix identity. The proof of (
108
) is then identical to the proof of
proposition

2.1
, and the point-star example gives equality.
∎

Dropping the complement factor gives Vinh’s point–hyperplane estimate
[
14
]
with its exact centered constant.

5.2.
A scale-adaptive point–hyperplane inverse theorem

Theorem 5.2

(Rich codimension-two sections)
.

Let
q
be any prime power, let
d\geq 2
, let
X\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
be nonempty, and let
\mathcal{H}
be a nonempty family of distinct affine hyperplanes. Put

n=|X|,\qquad h=|\mathcal{H}|,\qquad I_{H}=I(X,\mathcal{H}),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{H}=\frac{I_{H}^{2}}{n}-I_{H}.

If
\mathcal{E}_{H}>0
, then for every
0<\eta<1
, ordered pairs satisfying

|X\cap H\cap H^{\prime}|\geq\eta\frac{\mathcal{E}_{H}}{h^{2}}

carry at least
(1-\eta)\mathcal{E}_{H}
units of coincidence energy. In particular, there are at least

\frac{(1-\eta)\mathcal{E}_{H}}{q^{d-2}}

ordered such pairs, all nonparallel.

If, more specifically,

I(X,\mathcal{H})-\frac{nh}{q}\geq Kq^{(d-1)/2}\sqrt{nh},\qquad K^{2}q^{d-1}h\geq 4n,

then at least
K^{2}qh/4
ordered nonparallel pairs satisfy

(109)

|X\cap H\cap H^{\prime}|\geq\frac{K^{2}q^{d-1}}{4h}.

Under the additional cap
h\leq C_{1}Kq^{(d-1)/2}
, where
C_{1}\geq 1
, the right-hand side in (
109
) is at least
Kq^{(d-1)/2}/(4C_{1})
.

Proof.

The proof of
theorem

3.1
applies verbatim to the point–hyperplane incidence relation. Distinct parallel hyperplanes are disjoint, while a nonparallel pair meets in a codimension-two flat of size

. The fixed-scale consequences follow from the same estimate

\mathcal{E}_{H}\geq\frac{1}{2}K^{2}q^{d-1}h

used in

, followed by the choice
\eta=1/2
.
∎

5.3.
Pin-generated systems and deficiency-sensitive pin counts

Pinned distances and dot products are classical applications of finite-field incidence estimates; see, for example,
[
2
,
6
]
. For
p\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and nonempty sets

, define

where the dot product is taken with respect to any fixed nondegenerate bilinear pairing.

Let
T
be a set of
m
distance pins and put

\mathcal{S}_{T}=\{S_{Q}(p,t):p\in T,\ t\in\Delta_{p}^{Q}(P)\},\qquad M_{\Delta}=|\mathcal{S}_{T}|.

Every

lies on exactly one member of

for each pin, so

The exact fixed-scale parameter is

(111)

K_{\Delta}=\frac{\beta_{\Delta}}{\sqrt{1-\beta_{\Delta}}}\frac{\sqrt{m|P|}}{q^{d/2}}.

For dot products, let
T
be a set of pairwise nonproportional nonzero pins and define

H_{p,t}=\{x:\langle p,x\rangle=t\},\qquad\mathcal{H}_{T}=\{H_{p,t}:p\in T,\ t\in\Pi_{p}(Y)\}.

All these hyperplanes are distinct. With
M_{\Pi}=|\mathcal{H}_{T}|
and
\beta_{\Pi}=1-M_{\Pi}/(mq)
, the analogues of (
110
) and (
111
) hold with
(P,\Delta)
replaced by

.

Proposition 5.3

(Deficiency-sensitive pin-count bounds)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
.

(1)

Let

be odd and let

be nonempty. The number of pins

\frac{(1-\alpha)(q^{d+1}-q^{d})}{\alpha^{2}|P|}.

(2)

Let

be any prime power, let

be nonempty, and fix a nondegenerate bilinear pairing on

\frac{(1-\alpha)(q^{d}-|Y|)}{\alpha^{2}|Y|}.

For distance pins, let

be the deficient set and

. If

6.
Discussion: a classification theorem and its sharp boundary

There are two mathematically different inverse problems in this paper. At the maximal-incidence endpoint, the problem has a genuine Freiman-type answer.
Theorems

2.8
and
2.10
show that if the missing-incidence density is
\mu=o_{d}(1)
, then deleting
O_{d}(\sqrt{\mu})
from each side produces one exact lifted-linear model, and this loss controls the natural edit distance in both directions.
Proposition

2.7
gives the complete model list: a rank-
r
affine system of sphere parameters,
0\leq r\leq d
, with common base

S_{Q}(c_{0},r_{0})\cap(z+W^{\perp_{Q}}).

Under codimension-two nonconcentration,
corollary

2.12
forces
r\leq 1
, leaving a single coaxal pencil. The rectangular-hole construction of
proposition

2.11
shows that the square-root stability exponent is best possible. Thus every dense-endpoint near-extremizer is quantitatively close to an explicit geometric model, with the correct order of deletion loss.

At the lower fixed-radius deviation scale, the universal conclusion is necessarily different. Once the diagonal is dominated, a positive discrepancy forces common sphere sections at the adaptive scale
K^{2}q^{d-1}/|\mathcal{S}|
. Complete pencils attain this scale. Under an explicit lower condition, the positive surplus can be resolved, up to a prescribed relative error, into disjoint section-supported pieces; the log-free refinement records additional incidence density on one extracted section. These are sharp extraction and decomposition theorems, not a classification of every constant-
K
system. At the sharp mixed-radius design norm,
theorem

2.2
additionally gives a complete quantitative classification of the sphere-intersection and point-degree profiles for both signs.

The reason an individual-template edit classification cannot extend to the whole fixed-scale class is not merely the existence of a few exceptional constructions.
Theorem

4.9
fixes one nonzero-radius sphere family whose lifts lie on one rational normal curve and realizes every admissible
d
-uniform hypergraph on the point side. Dense subhypergraphs already give constant-
K
moderate-range systems. By
corollary

4.10
, this class has metric entropy
\exp(\Omega_{d}(q^{d}))
: no finite, polynomial-size, or subexponential list of individual point-set templates can approximate every such system within
o(q^{d})
point edits. A stronger container theorem is not excluded, but its model class would have to permit arbitrary dense subhypergraphs and would therefore record substantially less of the incidence pattern.

The special obstruction systems identify rigid subfamilies inside this universal landscape. The fixed-radius construction of
theorem

4.1
is an exact centered direct sum: all cross-block centered incidences vanish and the diagonal blocks carry the entire surplus. In
d\geq 4
an unbounded number of such blocks fit not only inside the moderate range but also inside the family-only subrange
|\mathcal{S}|\geq 4q/K^{2}
; no hyperplane or sphere-pair section is macroscopic, and one center-difference direction may still carry asymptotically all coincidence energy. In
d=3
,
corollary

4.2
places every fixed number
t\geq 3
of exact blocks in the same family-only range once
C_{1}>t/\sqrt{2}
.

The full rational-normal-curve design is opposite in character. Its rich-pair graph is complete, but the lifted parameters are in affine general position up to the maximum possible order. Every pair section carries only
O_{d}(q^{-2})
of the surplus, while
theorem

4.12
rules out every
(\tau,\varepsilon)
-centered block decomposition whenever
\varepsilon\leq c_{d}\tau^{2}
and
q\geq C_{d}/\tau
. The fixed-radius twisted-cubic system of
theorem

4.14
shows that, under the point-sensitive hypotheses of
corollary

3.3
, even one prescribed nonzero radius does not restore an atom-versus-block alternative in dimension three. The full diffuse examples do not satisfy
|\mathcal{S}|\geq 4q/K^{2}
. Thus a narrower dense-range question remains: in dimension three, does every non-atomic system in that family-only subrange admit an approximate centered block decomposition, or can a diffuse indecomposable branch persist there?

The point–hyperplane and pinned results demonstrate that the coincidence principle is not confined to sphere geometry. Further applications should be possible whenever object pairs have controlled common neighborhoods and those neighborhoods possess a useful geometric classification. Negative near-equality at the sharp mixed-radius norm is classified by
theorem

2.2
. Exact complement duality is recorded in
proposition

3.5
, but
remark

3.6
shows that its pair-section threshold is below
1
throughout the balanced regime and becomes structurally informative only when the omitted family is an
O(q^{-1})
fraction of the full sphere system. What remains open is an intrinsic inverse theory for deficits inside one prescribed fixed-radius class; that problem is tied to near-extremal Kloosterman and Salié directions rather than to positive coincidence energy.

Abstract
. This paper gives some exact formulas for the depth of powers of the edge ideal of an edge-weighted integrally closed cycle.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite simple graph with the vertex set
V(G)
and the edge set
E(G)
.
We write
xy
for an edge of
G
with
x
and
y
as endpoints.
Suppose
{\bf w}:E(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is an edge-weighted function (weighted function for short) on
G
. We write
G_{\bf w}
for the pair
(G,{\bf w})
and call it an
edge-weighted
graph (or just weighted graph) with the underlying graph
G
. For a weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
with the vertex set
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
,
its
edge-weighted ideal
(edge ideal for short), was introduced in
[
20
]
, is a monomial ideal of the polynomial ring
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
in
n
variables over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
, given by

I(G_{\bf w})=(x^{{\bf w}(xy)}y^{{\bf w}(xy)}\mid xy\in E(G_{\bf w})).

If
{\bf w}
is the constant function defined by
{\bf w}(e)=1
for all
e\in E(G)
, then
I(G_{\bf w})
is the classical edge ideal of the underlying graph
G
of
G_{\bf w}
, which
has been studied extensively in the literature
[
9
,
11
,
13
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
25
,
26
]
.

In
[
1
]
, Brodmann demonstrated that for a proper ideal
I\subset S
,
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})
is a constant for
t\gg 0
, and this constant is bounded above by
n-\ell(I)
, where
\ell(I)
is the analytic spread of
I
.
Both Eisenbud and Huneke in
[
7
, Proposition 3.3]
and Herzog and Hibi in
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
independently showed that the upper bound can be obtained when the associated graded ring is Cohen–Macaulay.
Furthermore, Herzog and Hibi proved
[
13
]
that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})
is a non-increasing function if all the powers of
I
have a linear resolution. They also provided lower bounds for
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})
when all the powers of
I
have linear quotients, a condition that entails that all powers of
I
have linear resolutions. In particular, they showed that the edge ideal of a finite graph whose complementary graph is chordal has linear quotients. Morey in
[
18
]
gives lower bounds on the depths of all powers when
I
is the edge ideal of a forest. For the general graph, Fouli1 and Morey in
[
9
]
gave lower bounds for its edge ideal.
In contrast, little is known about the depth of the quotient ring
S/I^{t}
when
I
is the edge ideal of a weighted graph.

Recently, there has been a surge of interest in characterizing weights for which the edge ideal of a weighted graph is Cohen-Macaulay. For example, Paulsen and Sather-Wagstaff in
[
20
]
classified Cohen-Macaulay weighted graphs
G_{\bf w}
where the underlying graph
G
is a cycle, a tree, or a complete graph. Seyed Fakhari et al. in
[
8
]
continued this study and classified a
Cohen-Macaulay weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
when
G
is a very well-covered graph. Hien in
[
14
]
classified Cohen-Macaulay weighted graphs
G_{\bf w}
when
G
has girth at least
5
. Recently, the first second authors of this paper and Trung in
[
16
]
characterized the Cohen-Macaulayness of the second power of the edge ideal of
G_{\bf w}
when the underlying graph
G
is a very well-covered graph and all ordinary powers of the edge ideal of
G_{\bf w}
when
G
is a tree with a perfect matching consisting of pendant edges. In
[
24
]
, Wei classified all Cohen-Macaulay weighted chordal graphs from a purely graph-theoretic point of view. Diem et al. in
[
5
]
gave a complete characterization of sequentially Cohen-Macaulay edge-weighted graphs.
The first three authors of this paper and Duan in
[
28
]
gave some exact formulas for the depth of the powers of the edge ideal of a weighted star graph and also gave some lower bounds on the depth of powers of the integrally closed weighted path.

In this paper, we are interested in the depth of powers of the edge ideal
I(C_{\bf w}^{n})
of a weighted integrally closed
n
-cycle
C_{\bf w}^{n}
.
The results we obtained are as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
C_{\bf w}^{n}
be a non-trivially weighted integrally closed
n
-cycle. Then we have

(1)

if
C_{\bf w}^{n}
has only one edge with non-trivial weight, then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})=\begin{cases}\lceil\frac{n-t}{3}\rceil,&\text{if $1\leq t<\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\rceil$,}\\
1,&\text{if $t\geq\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\rceil$, $n$ is even,}\\
0,&\text{if $t\geq\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\rceil$, $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

(2)

if
C_{\bf w}^{n}
has two edges with non-trivial weights, then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})=\max\{\lceil\frac{n-t}{3}\rceil,1\}\text{ for all $t\geq 1$}.

(3)

if
C_{\bf w}^{n}
has three edges with non-trivial weights, then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})=2\text{ for all $t\geq 1$}.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide the definitions and basic facts that will be used throughout this paper.
Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to prove our main results. By the depth lemma, we provide exact formulas for
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n}))
for any weighted integrally closed
n
-cycle
C_{\bf w}^{n}
, and exact formulas for
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})
if
C_{\bf w}^{n}
has three edges with non-trivial weights or
C_{\bf w}^{3}
has two edges with non-trivial weights. If
C_{\bf w}^{n}

(n\geq 4)
has two edges with non-trivial weights, we can give the lower bound for
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})
by using the depth lemma,
then, by choosing suitable
f\notin I^{t}
and using the fact that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})\leq\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t}:f))
, we prove that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})
just reaches this lower bound. If
C_{\bf w}^{n}
has only one edge with non-trivial weight, we can also give exact formulas for
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})
using methods from
[
17
,
27
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will provide the definitions and basic facts that will be used throughout this paper.
For detailed information, we refer to
[
2
]
and
[
12
]
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph with the vertex set
V(G)
and the edge set
E(G)
. For any subset
A
of
V(G)
,
G[A]
denotes the
induced subgraph
of
G
on the set
A
, i.e.,
for any
u,v\in A
,
uv\in E(G[A])
if and only if
uv\in E(G)
.
The induced subgraph of
G
on the set
V(G)\setminus A
is denoted by
G\setminus A
. In particular, if
A=\{v\}
then we will write
G\setminus v
instead of
G\setminus\{v\}
for simplicity.
For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, its
neighborhood
is defined as
N_{G}(v)\!:=\{u\in V(G)\mid uv\in E(G)\}
, and its degree is defined as
\deg_{G}(v)=|N_{G}(v)|
.
A connected graph
G
is called a
cycle
if
\deg_{G}(v)=2
for all
v\in V(G)
. A cycle with
n
vertices is called an
n
-cycle, denoted by
C^{n}
. A connected graph with vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, is called a
path
, if
E(G)=\{x_{i}x_{i+1}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}
. Such a path is denoted by
P^{n}
.

Given a weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
, we denote its vertex and edge sets by
V(G_{\bf w})
and
E(G_{\bf w})
, respectively. Any concept valid for graphs automatically applies to weighted graphs.
For example, the
neighborhood
of a vertex
v
in a weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
with the underlying graph
G
is defined as
N_{G_{\bf w}}(v)\!:=\{u\in V(G)\mid uv\in E(G)\}
.
For a subset
A
of
V(G)
, the
induced subgraph
of
G_{\bf w}
on the set
A
is the graph
G_{\bf w}[A]
with vertex set
A
, and for any
u,v\in A
,

uv\in E(G_{\bf w}[A])
if and only if
uv\in E(G_{\bf w})
, and the weight function
{\bf w}^{\prime}
satisfies
{\bf w}^{\prime}(uv)={\bf w}(uv)
. The induced subgraph of
G_{\bf w}
on the set
V(G)\setminus A
is denoted by
G_{\bf w}\setminus A
. In particular, if
A=\{v\}
then we will write
G_{\bf w}\setminus v
instead of
G_{\bf w}\setminus\{v\}
for simplicity.
A weighted graph is said to be a
non-trivially
weighted graph if there is at least one edge with a weight greater than
1
, otherwise, it is said to be
a
trivially
weighted graph. An edge
e\in E(G_{\bf w})
is said to be
non-trivial
if its weight
{\bf w}(e)\geq 2
. Otherwise, it is said to be
trivial
.

The following lemmas are often used to compute the depth of a module.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
15
, Lemmas 2.1]
)

Let
0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow 0
be a short exact
sequence of finitely generated graded S-modules. Then

(1)

\operatorname{depth}(N)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(M),\operatorname{depth}(P)\}
, the equality holds if
\operatorname{depth}(P)\neq\operatorname{depth}(M)-1
;

(2)

\operatorname{depth}(M)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(N),\operatorname{depth}(P)+1\}
, the equality holds if
\operatorname{depth}(N)\neq\operatorname{depth}(P)
;

(3)

\operatorname{depth}(P)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(M)-1,\operatorname{depth}(N)\}
, the equality holds if
\operatorname{depth}(M)\neq\operatorname{depth}(N)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
15
, Lemma 2.2]
)

Let
S_{1}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
and
S_{2}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{m+1},\dots,x_{n}]
be two polynomial rings over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
. Let
S=S_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}S_{2}
and
I\subset S_{1}
,

J\subset S_{2}
be two nonzero homogeneous ideals. Then

(1)

\operatorname{depth}(S/(I+J))=\operatorname{depth}(S_{1}/I)+\operatorname{depth}(S_{2}/J)
;

(2)

\operatorname{depth}(S/JI)=\operatorname{depth}(S_{1}/I)+\operatorname{depth}(S_{2}/J)+1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
21
, Corollary 1.3]
,
[
15
, Lemma 2.1]
)
Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal and
f\notin I
be a monomial of degree
s
. Then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I)\leq\operatorname{depth}(S/(I:f)).

Furthermore, it follows from Lemma
2.1
and the following short exact sequence

(1)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\frac{S}{I:f}(-s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot f}}{{\longrightarrow}}\frac{S}{I}\longrightarrow\frac{S}{(I,f)}\longrightarrow 0

that

\operatorname{depth}(S/I)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(S/(I:f)),\operatorname{depth}(S/(I,f))\}.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
17
, Theorem 3.6]
)

Let
P_{\bf w}^{n}
be a trivially weighted path, then for all
t\geq 1
,

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P^{n}_{\bf w})^{t})=\max\{\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil,1\}.

3.
The depth of the edge ideal

Calculating or even estimating the depth of edge ideals of weighted cycles with arbitrary weight functions is a difficult problem. In this section, we will give precise formulas for the depth of the edge ideal of a non-trivially weighted integrally closed
n
-cycle. First, we recall that the definition of an ideal is integrally closed.

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
12
, Definition 1.4.1]
)
Let
I
be an ideal in a ring
R
.
An element
f\in R
is said to be integral over
I
if there exists an equation

f^{k}+c_{1}f^{k-1}+\dots+c_{k-1}f+c_{k}=0\text{\ \ with\ \ }c_{i}\in I^{i}.

The set
\overline{I}
of elements in
R
which are integral over
I
is the
integral closure
of
I
.
If
I=\overline{I}
, then
I
is said to be integrally closed.
A weighted graph
G_{\omega}
is said to be integrally closed if its edge ideal
I(G_{\omega})
is integrally closed.

We will use the following notation for the remainder of this paper. Let
n
be a positive integer, the
notation
[n]
denotes by convention the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. If
n\geq 3
, let
C_{\bf w}^{n}
denote a non-trivially weighted integrally closed
n
-cycle with the vertex set
V(C_{\bf w}^{n})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and the edge set
E(C_{\bf w}^{n})
. We also denote
e_{i}=x_{i}x_{i+1}\in E(C_{\bf w}^{n})
with
x_{n+1}=x_{1}
and
{\bf w}_{i}={\bf w}(e_{i})
for all
i\in[n]
. By symmetry, we can assume that

{\bf w}_{1}\geq 2
,
{\bf w}_{1}\geq{\bf w}_{3}
and
{\bf w}_{3}\geq{\bf w}_{5}
if
e_{5}\in E(C^{n}_{\bf w})
.
Let
P_{\bf w}^{n}
be an induced subgraph of the
(n+1)
-cycle
C_{\bf w}^{n+1}
on the set
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.

According to
[
12
, Theorem 1.4.6]
, every trivially weighted graph
G_{\bf w}
is integrally closed. The following lemma gives a complete characterization of a non-trivially weighted graph that is integrally closed.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.6]
and
[
23
, Theorem 2.8]
)
Let
G_{\bf w}
be a non-trivially weighted graph, then
G_{\bf w}
is integrally closed if and only if
G_{\bf w}
does not contain any of the following three graphs as its induced subgraphs.

(1)

A path
P_{\bf w}^{3}
of length
2
where all edges have non-trivial weights.

(2)

The disjoint union
P_{\bf w}^{2}\sqcup P_{\bf w}^{2}
of two paths
P_{\bf w}^{2}
of length
1
where all edges have non-trivial weights.

(3)

A
3
-cycle
C_{\bf w}^{3}
where all edges have non-trivial weights.

From the above lemma, we can derive

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
C_{\bf w}^{n}
be a non-trivially weighted integrally closed
n
-cycle. If
n=6
, it can have at most three edges with non-trivial weights, otherwise it can have up to two edges with non-trivial weights.

The following lemmas provide some formulas for the depth of powers of the edge ideal of a non-trivially integrally closed path.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
[
28
, Theorems 4.8 and 4.9]
)

Let
P_{\bf w}^{n}
be a non-trivially weighted integrally closed path. Let
{\bf w}_{i}\geq 2
and
{\bf w}_{i}\geq{\bf w}_{i+2}
if
e_{i+2}\in E({P_{\bf w}^{n}})
. Then

(1)

if
n\leq 3
, then
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n}))=1
;

(2)

if
n=4
, then
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n}))=2-a
, where
a=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if ${\bf w}_{2}=1$,}\\
1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
;

(3)

if
n\geq 5
, then
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n}))=\min\{\lceil\frac{i}{3}\rceil+\lceil\frac{n-i-b}{3}\rceil,\lceil\frac{i-2}{3}\rceil+\lceil\frac{n-i-2}{3}\rceil+1\}
,

where
b=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if ${\bf w}_{i+2}=1$,}\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

(
[
28
, Theorems 4.12, 4.17, 4.18 and Proposition 4.13]
)

Let
P_{\bf w}^{n}
be a non-trivially weighted integrally closed path as in Lemma
3.4
.
Then for all
t\geq 2
, we have the following results:

(1)

If
n\leq 3
, then
\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})=1
;

(2)

If
n=4
, then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})\geq\begin{cases}2,&\text{if ${\bf w}_{1},{\bf w}_{3}>1$ and ${\bf w}_{2}=1$,}\\
1,&\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases};

(3)

If
n\geq 5
and
i=1
, then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})\geq\begin{cases}\max\{\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil,1\},&\text{if ${\bf w}_{3}=1$,}\\
\max\{\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil,2\},&\text{if ${\bf w}_{3}>1$.}\\
\end{cases};

(4)

If
n\geq 5
and
i>1
, then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})\geq\begin{cases}\max\{\lceil\frac{n-t}{3}\rceil,2\},&\text{if ${\bf w}_{i+2}>1$,}\\
\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil,&\text{if ${\bf w}_{i+2}=1$ and $i\equiv 1(\text{mod}\ 3)$, $n\equiv 2(\text{mod}\ 3)$, and $t=2$, }\\
\max\{\lceil\frac{n-t}{3}\rceil,1\},&\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

The following lemma provides some exact formulas for the depth of powers of the edge ideal of a trivially weighted cycle.

Lemma 3.6
.

(
[
3
, Proposition 1.3]
,
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
22
, Theorems 4.4 and 5.1]
)

Let
C_{\bf w}^{n}
be a trivially weighted
n
-cycle, then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})=\begin{cases}\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil,&\text{if $t=1$,}\\
\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil,&\text{if $2\leq t<\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\rceil$,}\\
1,&\text{if $n$ is even and $t\geq\frac{n}{2}+1$,}\\
0,&\text{if $n$ is odd and $t\geq\frac{n+1}{2}$.}\end{cases}

Next, we will compute the depth of the edge ideal of a non-trivially weighted integrally closed
n
-cycle.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
C_{\bf w}^{n}
be a non-trivially weighted integrally closed
n
-cycle, then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n}))=\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil.

Proof.

Let
I=I(C_{\bf w}^{n})
and
{\bf w}_{1}=\max\{{\bf w}_{1},\ldots,{\bf w}_{n}\}
by symmetry. If
n=3
, then
{\bf w}_{2}=1
or
{\bf w}_{3}=1
by Lemma
3.2
. We can assume
{\bf w}_{3}=1
by symmetry. It is easy to see that
(I:x_{3})=(x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{2}}x_{3}^{{\bf w}_{2}-1},x_{1})
and
(I,x_{3})=(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}},x_{3})
. Thus

\operatorname{depth}(S/(I:x_{3}))=\operatorname{depth}(S/(I,x_{3}))=1
. Applying Lemma
2.1
to the following exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\frac{S}{I:x_{3}}(-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x_{3}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\frac{S}{I}\longrightarrow\frac{S}{(I,x_{3})}\longrightarrow 0,

we can observe that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I)=1
.

In the following, we assume that
n\geq 4
. We distinguish between the following two cases:

Case
1
: If
C_{\bf w}^{n}
has at most two edges with non-trivial weights, then we can assume by symmetry and Lemma
3.2
that
{\bf w}_{1}\geq{\bf w}_{3}
and
{\bf w}_{i}=1
for all
i\neq 1,3
. It is easy to see that
(I,x_{2})=I(C_{\bf w}^{n}\backslash{x_{2}})+(x_{2})
and
(I:x_{2})=I(P_{\bf w}^{n-2})+(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}-1},x_{3})
, where
P_{\bf w}^{n-2}
is an induced subgraph of
C_{\bf w}^{n}
on the set
\{x_{1},\widehat{x_{2}},\widehat{x_{3}},x_{4},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and
\widehat{x_{2}}
denotes the element
x_{2}
omitted from
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Furthermore,
(I:x_{2}x_{1})=I(P_{\bf w}^{n-4})+(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}-1}x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}-1},x_{3},x_{n})
and
((I:x_{2}),x_{1})=I(P_{\bf w}^{n-3})+(x_{1},x_{3})
, where
P_{\bf w}^{n-4}
and
P_{\bf w}^{n-3}
are induced subgraphs of
C_{\bf w}^{n}
on the sets
\{x_{4},\ldots,x_{n-1}\}
and
\{x_{4},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, respectively.
It follows from Lemmas
2.2
and
2.4
that
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I:x_{2}x_{1}))=\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil
and
\operatorname{depth}(S/((I:x_{2}),x_{1}))=\lceil\frac{n}{3}\rceil
. We consider the following two subcases:

(i) If
{\bf w}_{3}=1
, then
\operatorname{depth}(S/(I,x_{2}))=\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil
by Lemma
2.4
. It follows that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I)=\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil
by applying Lemma
2.1
to
the following short exact sequences

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{I:x_{2}}(-1)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{I}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{(I,x_{2})}&\longrightarrow&0,\\
0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{I:x_{2}x_{1}}(-1)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{I:x_{2}}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{((I:x_{2}),x_{1})}&\longrightarrow&0.\end{matrix}

(ii) If
{\bf w}_{3}\geq 2
, then it follows from Lemmas
2.2
and
3.4
that

\operatorname{depth}(S/(I,x_{2}))=\begin{cases}\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil,&\text{if $n=4$,}\\
\lceil\frac{n}{3}\rceil,&\text{if $n\geq 5$.}\end{cases}

Using Lemma
2.1
to the short exact sequences (
2
),
we get
\operatorname{depth}(S/I)=\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil
.

Case
2
: If
C_{\bf w}^{n}
has three edges with non-trivial weights, then
n=6
by Corollary
3.3
. In this case, we can assume by symmetry that
{\bf w}_{1}\geq{\bf w}_{3}\geq{\bf w}_{5}\geq 2
and
{\bf w}_{2}={\bf w}_{4}={\bf w}_{6}=1
. It is easy to see that
(I:x_{2})=I(P_{\bf w}^{4})+(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}}x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}-1},x_{3})
and
(I,x_{2})=I(P_{\bf w}^{5})+(x_{2})
, where
P_{\bf w}^{4}
and
P_{\bf w}^{5}
are induced subgraphs of
C_{\bf w}^{6}
on the sets
\{x_{1},x_{4},x_{5},x_{6}\}
and
\{x_{1},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5},x_{6}\}
, respectively. Furthermore,
(I:x_{2}x_{1})=(x_{1}^{{\bf w}_{1}-1}x_{2}^{{\bf w}_{1}-1},x_{3},x_{4}x_{5},x_{6})
and
((I:x_{2}),x_{1})=(x_{1},x_{3},x_{4}x_{5},(x_{5}x_{6})^{{\bf w}_{5}})
.
It follows from Lemmas
2.2
,

and

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
t
and
C_{\bf w}^{n}
be as in Theorem
4.18
. Then

\operatorname{depth}(S/I(C_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})=1
.

Proof.

Let
I=I(C_{\bf w}^{n})
and
f=(x_{1}x_{2})^{(t-\frac{n}{2}){\bf w}_{1}}\prod\limits_{k=3}^{n}x_{k}
. Then by Theorem
4.18
, we have

\displaystyle(I^{t}:f)

\displaystyle=(\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\{x_{1}x_{2k}\})+(\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\{x_{2i+1}x_{2j}\})+(x_{3},x_{n})

\displaystyle=(x_{1},\hat{x}_{3},x_{5},\ldots,x_{n-1})(x_{2},x_{4},\ldots,x_{n-2},\hat{x}_{n})+(x_{3},x_{n}).

where
\widehat{x_{3}}
denotes the element
x_{3}
omitted from
\{x_{1},x_{3},x_{5},\ldots,x_{n-1}\}
. Thus

\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})\leq\operatorname{depth}(S/(I^{t}:f))=1

by Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
.

Next, we will prove that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})\geq 1
.
Let
L^{(t)}=\{L_{1}^{(t)},L_{2}^{(t)},\ldots,L_{r}^{(t)}\}
be a specific totally ordered set of all elements in
\mathcal{G}(I^{t})
as defined in
[
27
, Setting 4.5]
. For simplicity, we set
L_{i}=L_{i}^{(1)}
. Let

C=\{L_{i}^{(t)}:L_{1}|^{edge}L_{i}^{(t)}\ \text{for\ }i\in[r]\}
be a set of
L_{i}^{(t)}
divided by
L_{1}
as an edge and
c=|C|
. For each
i\in[c]
, we write
L_{i}^{(t)}
as
L_{i}^{(t)}=L_{i_{1}}^{a_{i_{1}}}L_{i_{2}}^{a_{i_{2}}}\cdots L_{i_{k_{i}}}^{a_{i_{k_{i}}}}
with
i_{1}=1
and
2\leq i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k_{i}}\leq n
, where
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{i}}a_{i_{j}}=t
with
a_{i_{j}}>0
for all
j\in[k_{i}]
.
Assume that
J_{i}=(L_{i+1}^{(t)},\ldots,L_{r}^{(t)})
for all
i\in[c]
.
From the proof of
[
27
, Theorem 4.11]
, we obtain
J_{c}=I(P_{\bf w}^{n})^{t}
, where
I(P_{{\bf w}}^{n})=(x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}x_{4},\ldots,x_{n-1}x_{n},x_{n}x_{1})
. So

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}(S/J_{c})=\operatorname{depth}(S/I(P_{\bf w}^{n})^{t})=\max\{\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil,1\}\geq 1

by Lemma
2.4
. Let
s_{i}
be the degree of
L_{i}^{(t)}
for each
i\in[c]
.
By the exact sequences

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{(J_{1}:L_{1}^{(t)})}(-s_{1})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot L_{1}^{(t)}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{J_{1}}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{I^{t}}&\longrightarrow&0\\
0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{(J_{2}:L_{2}^{(t)})}(-s_{2})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot L_{2}^{(t)}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{J_{2}}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{J_{1}}&\longrightarrow&0\\
0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{(J_{3}:L_{3}^{(t)})}(-s_{3})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot L_{3}^{(t)}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{J_{3}}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{J_{2}}&\longrightarrow&0\\
&&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots&\\
0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{(J_{c}:L_{c}^{(t)})}(-s_{c})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot L_{c-1}^{(t)}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{J_{c}}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{J_{c-1}}&\longrightarrow&0,\end{matrix}

we know that
\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{t})\geq 1
if
\operatorname{depth}(S/(J_{i}:L_{i}^{(t)}))\geq 2
for any
i\in[c]
. It remains to prove that
\operatorname{depth}(S/(J_{i}:L_{i}^{(t)}))\geq 2
for any
i\in[c]
.

By
[
27
, Theorems 4.10]
, we obtain that for each
i\in[c]
,
(J_{i}:L_{i}^{(t)})=K_{i}+Q_{i}
, where

,
Q_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q_{i}}(x_{n-2j})
,
p_{i}=\begin{cases}k_{i}-1,&\text{if $i_{k_{i}}=n$,}\\
k_{i},&\text{otherwise,}\\
\end{cases}
and
q_{i}=\max\{\ell_{i}:L_{n+1-2s}|^{edge}L_{i}^{(t)}
for all
0\leq s\leq\ell_{i}\}
.

If
k_{i}=1
, or
k_{i}=2
and
i_{k_{i}}=n
, then
(J_{1}:L_{1}^{(t)})=(L_{4},L_{5},\ldots,L_{n-2})+(x_{3},x_{n})
. Thus
\operatorname{depth}(S/(J_{1}:L_{1}^{(t)}))\geq 2

2.4
. In the following, we consider the case

, or
k_{i}=2

\operatorname{depth}(S/(J_{i}:L_{i}^{(t)}))\geq 1

Note that
(J_{i}:L_{i}^{(t)})=(L_{4},L_{5},\ldots,L_{n-2})+\sum\limits_{j=2}^{p_{i}}(x_{{i_{j}}+2})+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{q_{i}}(x_{n-2j})+(x_{3},x_{n})
, it follows from Lemma

. Thus

for all

. Again by the definition of

, we get

So

can be expressed as

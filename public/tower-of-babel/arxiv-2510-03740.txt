Rapid boundary stabilization of 1D nonlinear parabolic equations Note: This work was partially supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China under the grant 12422118. Journal: arXiv
Yu Xiao, Can Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we focus on the rapid boundary stabilization of 1D nonlinear parabolic equations via the modal decomposition method. The nonlinear term is assumed to satisfy certain local Lipschitz continuity and global growth conditions. Through the modal decomposition, we construct a feedback control that modifies only the unstable eigenvalues to achieve spectral reduction. Under this control, we establish locally rapid stabilization by estimating the nonlinearity in Lyapunov stability analysis. Furthermore, utilizing the dissipative property, we derive a globally rapid stabilization result for dissipative systems such as the Burgers equation and the Allen-Cahn equation.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation

Stability analysis has long been a central topic in control theory, particularly the problem of feedback stabilization. By applying feedback control on the boundary or over a subset of the domain, where the control is selected based on current or historical state information, we expect the closed-loop system to exhibit stability in its large time behavior. A variety of approaches have been developed to address the feedback stabilization problem for partial differential equations (PDEs), such as the backstepping method (e.g.,
[
4
]
for the heat equation and
[
6
]
for the Burgers equation), the modal decomposition method (see
[
7
,
20
,
27
]
for the heat equation,
[
12
]
for the Kuramoto–Sivashinsky equation,
[
14
,
18
]
for the Burgers equation,
[
26
]
for the Navier–Stokes equation,
[
10
]
for the Fisher equation), as well as other methods (e.g.,
[
2
,
22
]
based on LQ theory).

As one of the most widely studied classes of PDEs, linear parabolic equations have seen significant progress in feedback stabilization(
[
4
,
27
]
). However, numerous physical problems involve nonlinearities, and some progress has been made in recent years on the feedback stabilization of nonlinear parabolic equations (e.g.,
[
1
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
14
,
15
,
18
]
). In particular, the modal decomposition method serves as an important tool. Through spectral analysis and state decomposition, this method uses a finite number of modes to construct a feedback control. Its success lies in the fact that such a control modifies only the unstable eigenvalues, thereby achieving stabilization.

When addressing the controllability of semilinear parabolic equations, the authors of
[
5
]
combined the modal decomposition method with a constructive Lyapunov function to establish stability over a finite time interval. Based on this stability, they further derived global controllability by using existing results on approximate controllability. In
[
1
,
10
,
16
]
, relying on a fixed-point theorem rather than Lyapunov analysis, the authors proved the locally rapid stabilization of multi-dimensional semilinear parabolic equations.

Recently, for the case where the nonlinear term is globally Lipschitz continuous, the authors of
[
8
,
15
]
established the well-posedness of the closed-loop system through the analyticity of the Sturm–Liouville operator. Simultaneously, by utilizing the Lipschitz continuity, they effectively used the norm of the state to bound the nonlinearity. Then, through a Lyapunov analysis similar to that in
[
5
]
, they demonstrated a globally rapid stabilization result related to the Lipschitz coefficient. Regarding the semilinear equations in
[
9
]
, the authors also achieved globally rapid stabilization. Taking into account specific nonlinearities, the authors of
[
14
,
18
]
established the locally rapid stabilization of the Burgers equation. More precisely, they demonstrated that the nonlinear term has a negligible effect in the Lyapunov analysis when the initial value is sufficiently small, and thus attained the exponential stability inherited from the linearized equation. Motivated by these works, estimating the effect of the nonlinear term is of crucial importance in the Lyapunov stability analysis of nonlinear parabolic equations.

The aim of this paper is to construct a boundary control for 1D nonlinear parabolic equations using the modal decomposition method, so as to achieve well-posedness and rapid stabilization.

Main result

(1.3)

Remark 1.2

As the exponential decay rate

The control form (

Remark 1.4

The assumption (H) includes local Lipschitz continuity and global growth conditions. Among these, the Lipschitz continuity condition ensures the well-posedness of the closed-loop system, while the growth condition guarantees the estimability of the nonlinearity in Lyapunov analysis.

Theorem 3.4

.

Proof.

3.3

Let

satisfy

and denote

. According to Lemma

, we have

\dot{Y}_{\tilde{N}}(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{0}+B_{0}K&0\\
A_{1}+B_{1}K&A_{2}\end{array}\right)Y_{\tilde{N}}(t)+R_{\tilde{N}}(t),\;\;\forall t\in(0,T),

where

A_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{N+1}&0&\cdots&0\\
a_{N+2}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{\tilde{N}}&0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{(\tilde{N}-N)\times(N+1)},B_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b_{N+1}\\
\vdots\\
b_{\tilde{N}}\end{array}\right),R_{\tilde{N}}(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
f_{1}(t)\\
\vdots\\
f_{\tilde{N}}(t)\end{array}\right),

and

A_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}q-\lambda_{N+1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&q-\lambda_{N+2}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&q-\lambda_{\tilde{N}}\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{(\tilde{N}-N)\times(\tilde{N}-N)}.

Based on the prior selection of
N
and
K
, both
A_{0}+B_{0}K+\delta I
and
A_{2}+\delta I
are Hurwitz matrices. Define
F:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{0}+B_{0}K&0\\
A_{1}+B_{1}K&A_{2}\end{array}\right)
. Then
F+\delta I
is a Hurwitz matrix as well. Consequently, there exists a matrix
P\succ 0
satisfying the Lyapunov equation

F^{T}P+PF+2\delta P=-I.

(3.8)

Lemma 3.5
.

There are two constants
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{1}\left(\delta\right)
and
\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{2}\left(\tilde{N},\delta\right)
so that

\|P\|_{2}=\sigma_{\max}(P)\leq\sigma_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{\min}(P)\geq\sigma_{2}.

(3.9)

Proof.

(i) The proof here is motivated by
[
7
]
. Define two auxiliary matrices

F_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{0}+B_{0}K+\delta I&0\\
0&A_{2}+\delta I\end{array}\right),\;\;\;F_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
A_{1}+B_{1}K&0\end{array}\right).

It follows that
F+\delta I=F_{1}+F_{2}
.
Firstly, there is a constant
M_{1}=M_{1}\left(\delta\right)\geq 1
so that

\|e^{F_{1}t}\|_{2}\leq\max\left\{M_{1}e^{-\delta t},e^{-\delta t}\right\}\leq M_{1}e^{-\delta t},\;\;\forall t>0.

(3.10)

Besides, since
|K|_{2}
depends only on
\delta
, we can find
M_{2}=M_{2}\left(\delta\right)\geq 1
such that

\|F_{2}\|_{2}=\|A_{1}+B_{1}K\|_{2}\leq\|A_{1}\|_{2}+\|B_{1}K\|_{2}\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{2}+|K|_{2}+|K|_{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}^{2}<M_{2}.

(3.11)

It is easy to show that for any
t_{i}\geq 0

(i=1,2)
, we have
\prod_{i=1}^{2}(e^{F_{1}t_{i}}F_{2})=0
. For each
t>0
, we introduce the identities

e^{(F+\delta I)t}=e^{F_{1}t}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{F_{1}(t-s)}F_{2}e^{(F+\delta I)s}ds=e^{F_{1}t}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{F_{1}(t-s)}F_{2}e^{F_{1}s}ds.

(3.12)

Finally, from (
3.10
), (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), we find
M_{3}=M_{3}\left(\delta\right)\geq 1
such that

\|e^{(F+\delta I)t}\|_{2}\leq M_{3}e^{-\delta t}(1+t),\;\;\forall t>0.

By virtue of tha fact that
P=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(F+\delta I)^{\top}s}e^{(F+\delta I)s}ds
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\max}(P)=\|P\|_{2}\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}M_{3}^{2}(1+t)^{2}e^{-2\delta t}dt<\infty.

We thus define
\sigma_{1}\left(\delta\right):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}M_{3}^{2}(1+t)^{2}e^{-2\delta t}dt
, which finalizes the first part of the proof.

(ii) Obviously it holds that

\left(2(F+\delta I)^{\top}P+\frac{1}{2}I\right)^{\top}\left(2(F+\delta I)^{\top}P+\frac{1}{2}I\right)\succeq 0.

Expanding this, we derive

4P(F+\delta I)(F+\delta I)^{\top}P+P(F+\delta I)+(F+\delta I)^{\top}P+\frac{1}{4}I\succeq 0.

From the Lyapunov equation (
3.8
), it follows that

P(F+\delta I)(F+\delta I)^{\top}P\succeq\frac{1}{16}I,

and thus

\sigma_{\min}\left(P(F+\delta I)(F+\delta I)^{\top}P\right)\geq\frac{1}{16}.

Note that
P(F+\delta I)(F+\delta I)^{\top}P
is invertible, we have

\sigma_{\max}\left((F+\delta I)(F+\delta I)^{\top}\right)\cdot\sigma_{\min}(P^{2})\geq\sigma_{\min}\left((F+\delta I)(F+\delta I)^{\top}P^{2}\right)=\sigma_{\min}\left(P(F+\delta I)(F+\delta I)^{\top}P\right)\geq\frac{1}{16}.

It is readily verified that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\min}(P)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\sigma_{\max}\left((F+\delta I)(F+\delta I)^{\top}\right)}}=\frac{1}{4\|F+\delta I\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4\|F_{1}\|_{2}+\|F_{2}\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4\left(\max\left\{\|A_{0}+B_{0}K+\delta I\|_{2},\lambda_{\tilde{N}}-q+\delta\right\}+M_{2}\right)}

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{2}\left(\tilde{N},\delta\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

satisfies

(3.20)

By contradiction, i.e., we suppose that there exists

such that

. When

, we have

\tilde{c}_{2}\|w(0)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)}+\tilde{c}_{2}\|\mathcal{D}\|_{\mathcal{L}}|u(0)|\leq\frac{{\sigma}}{\tilde{c}_{2}\|\mathcal{D}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\hat{M}}<{\sigma}.

Thus, we can define

the shortest time that satisfies

Using the fact that

and

, we have

and
\tilde{c}_{2}\|w(t)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)}+\tilde{c}_{2}\|\mathcal{D}\|_{\mathcal{L}}|u(t)|<{\sigma}
for every

. This implies that for any

Based on Lemma

such that (

) holds. Therefore, (

is exponentially stable over

Using Lemma

\displaystyle k_{1}\min\left\{\sigma_{\min}(P),\min\{\frac{\sigma_{\min}(P)}{\lambda_{\tilde{N}}},1\}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq k_{2}e^{-2\delta t}\max\left\{\sigma_{\max}(P),\max\{\frac{\sigma_{\max}(P)}{\lambda_{1}},1\}\right\}\|y_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)},\;\forall t\in[0,{t}^{*}).

Hence, using the continuity again, we obtain that

\tilde{c}_{2}\|w(t)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)}+\tilde{c}_{2}\|\mathcal{D}\|_{\mathcal{L}}|u(t)|\leq\tilde{c}_{2}\|\mathcal{D}\|_{\mathcal{L}}\hat{M}e^{-\delta t}\|y_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)}\leq\frac{\sigma}{\tilde{c}_{2}}<{\sigma},\;\;\forall t\in[0,t^{*}],

4
Application

This section is divided into two parts, which are focused on applying Theorem
1.1
to the Burgers equation and the Allen-Cahn equation, respectively. We first show that the nonlinear term of both equations satisfies the assumption (H), which ensures locally rapid stabilization. Next, using certain energy estimate inequalities, we deduce that the
H^{1}
-norm of the uncontrolled system decays. Without control, the system tends to converge to a region where the rapid stabilization is guaranteed. Thereafter, by applying Theorem
1.1
, we can achieve globally rapid stabilization.

4.1
The Burgers equation

Consider the Burgers equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}y(t,x)-\partial_{xx}y(t,x)+y(t,x)\partial_{x}y(t,x)=0&\text{for }(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,1),\\[5.69054pt]
y(t,0)=y(t,1)=0&\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\[5.69054pt]
y(t_{0},x)=y_{0}(x)&\text{for }x\in(0,1).\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

Lemma 4.1
.

The nonlinear term
f(y)=-y\partial_{x}y
in (
4.1
) satisfies the assumption (H).

Proof.

It is not hard to check that for every

\displaystyle\|f(y_{1})-f(y_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\|y_{1}\partial_{x}y_{1}-y_{1}\partial_{x}y_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|y_{1}\partial_{x}y_{2}-y_{2}\partial_{x}y_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\|y_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\|\partial_{x}y_{1}-\partial_{x}y_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\|\partial_{x}y_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\tilde{c}_{2}(r+\|y_{0}\|_{H^{1}(0,1)})\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{H^{1}(0,1)}.

Moreover, we have

\|f(y)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=\|y\cdot\partial_{x}y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\|y\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\|y\|_{H^{1}(0,1)},\;\forall y\in H^{1}(0,1).

Therefore, the assumption (H) is satisfied with
C(\|y_{0}\|_{H^{1}(0,1)},r)=2\tilde{c}_{2}(r+\|y_{0}\|_{H^{1}(0,1)})
and
c(x)=x
.
∎

4.2
Allen-Cahn equation

We next turn to the Allen-Cahn equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}y(t,x)-\partial_{xx}y(t,x)-\kappa y(t,x)+\lambda y^{3}(t,x)=0&\quad\text{for }(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,1),\\
y(t,0)=y(t,1)=0&\quad\text{for }t\in(0,T),\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x)&\quad\text{for }x\in(0,1).\end{array}\right.

(4.13)

Here
\lambda>0
and
0\leq\kappa<c_{1}
. This is a well-posed semilinear heat equation. More precisely, given
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
, there exists a unique solution
y\in C([0,{T}];L^{2}(0,1))\cap L^{2}(0,{T};H_{0}^{1}(0,1))
.

Lemma 4.6
.

The nonlinear term
f(y)=-\lambda y^{3}
with
\lambda>0
satisfies the assumption (H).

Proof.

It is readily verified that for every
y_{1},y_{2}\in B_{{H^{1}}}(y_{0},r)
, we have

\displaystyle\|f(y_{1})-f(y_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|\|(y_{1}-y_{2})^{3}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+3|\lambda|\|y_{1}^{2}y_{2}-y_{2}^{2}y_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{3}_{L^{6}(0,1)}+3|\lambda|\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{L^{6}(0,1)}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(y_{1}(x)y_{2}(x))^{3}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|\tilde{c}_{2}^{3}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{3}_{H^{1}(0,1)}+3|\lambda|\tilde{c}_{2}^{3}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{H^{1}(0,1)}(\|y_{1}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(0,1)}+\|y_{2}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(0,1)})

\displaystyle\leq 20\tilde{c}_{2}^{3}|\lambda|(r^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(0,1)})\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{H^{1}(0,1)}.

Furthermore, it holds that

\|f(y)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}=\|\lambda y^{3}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq|\lambda|\|y\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\|y\|_{H^{1}(0,1)},\;\forall y\in H^{1}(0,1).

Hence, (H) is satisfied with
C(\|y_{0}\|_{H^{1}(0,1)},r)=20\tilde{c}_{2}^{3}|\lambda|(r^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(0,1)})
and
c(x)=|\lambda|x^{2}
.
∎

(4.14)

Analogous to Theorem

Theorem 4.8
.

Consider the Allen-Cahn equation with a boundary control

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}y(t,x)-\partial_{xx}y(t,x)-\kappa y(t,x)+\lambda y^{3}(t,x)=0&\text{for }(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times(0,1),\\[5.69054pt]
y(t,0)=u(t)\quad\text{and}\quad y(t,1)=0&\text{for }t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\\[5.69054pt]
y(t_{0},x)=y_{0}(x)&\text{for }x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(4.15)

where
\lambda>0
and
0\leq\kappa<c_{1}
. Given any
\delta>0
, there is an operator
\mathcal{K}_{2}:L^{2}(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
depending on
\delta
, so that for each
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
, there exist constants
{k}_{2}=k_{2}\left(\delta,\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\right),{T}_{2}=T_{2}\left(\delta,\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\right)>1
and
\hat{Z}=\hat{Z}\left(\delta,\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\right)\geq 1
, such that the system (
4.15
) with

{u}(t)=\int_{\min\{{T}_{2},t\}}^{t}e^{k_{2}(t-s)}{\mathcal{K}}_{2}(y(s))ds

admits a unique solution
y\in C([0,+\infty);L^{2}(0,1))
satisfying

\|y(t)\|_{H^{1}(0,1)}\leq\hat{Z}e^{-\delta t},\;\;\forall t\geq 1.

4.7
.

The proof is similar to the case of the Burgers equation, except the estimates for
\left\langle y^{3}(t),\partial_{xx}y(t)\right\rangle
and

4.13
) with
2y(t)
, we have

\partial_{t}\left(\|y(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right)+2\|\partial_{x}y(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+2\lambda\|y^{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}=2\kappa\|y(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}.

(A.6)

Using the inequality (
2.3
), we obtain that

\partial_{t}\left(\|y(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\right)+2(c_{1}-\kappa)\|y(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq 0,\;\;\forall t>0,

\quad\|y(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq e^{-2(c_{1}-\kappa)t}\left\|y_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq\left\|y_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2},\;\;\forall t>0.

Analogous to (
A.2
) and (
A.5

\displaystyle\left\langle y^{3}(t),\partial_{xx}y(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|y(t)\|^{3}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\|\partial_{xx}y(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c^{6}_{2}}{2}\left\|y(t)\right\|^{3}_{L^{2}(0,1)}\left\|\partial_{x}y(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\partial_{xx}y(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

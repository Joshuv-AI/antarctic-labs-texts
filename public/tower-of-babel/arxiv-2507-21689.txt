Spectral generalized Turán problems
Xizhi Liu

Abstract
Combining two well-studied variants of the classical Turán problem, the generalized Turán problem and the spectral Turán problem, we introduce the spectral generalized Turán problem and establish a general theorem that extends the result of Keevash–Lenz–Mubayi [ KLM14 ] on the spectral Turán problem in this broader setting. As a quick application, we obtain the spectral Erdős Pentagon Theorem. We also introduce the notion of entropic density for generalized Turán problems, and show that it coincides with the generalized spectral radius, extending a recent result of Chao–Hans on entropic Turán density.

,
\mathcal{F}
is a family of
r
-graphs satisfying
\hat{\pi}(Q,\mathcal{F})>0
,
\mathfrak{H}
is a hereditary family of
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-graphs, and
\mathcal{H}
is an
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-graph on
[n]
;

(iii)

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathrm{OPT}_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
is a nonnegative optimal vector, whose existence is guaranteed by Fact
2.1
..

For convenience, let

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}\coloneqq\hat{\pi}(Q,\mathcal{F})\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{n}\coloneqq\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(n,\mathfrak{H}).

3.1
Preparations

The following lemma is a straightforward consequence of the Lagrange multiplier method.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
i\in[n]
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{i}P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=q\cdot\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\cdot x_{i}^{\alpha-1}.

We remark that if the nonnegativity restriction on
\mathbf{x}
is removed, then Lemma
3.1
becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{i}P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=q\cdot\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\cdot x_{i}|x_{i}|^{\alpha-2}.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},\beta)\coloneqq P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})-\beta\left(|X_{1}|^{\alpha}+\cdots+|X_{n}|^{\alpha}-1\right).

Since
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathrm{OPT}_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
, it follows from the Lagrange multiplier method that
\partial_{i}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})=0
for every
i\in[n]
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x})=\beta\alpha x_{i}|x_{i}|^{\alpha-2}=\beta\alpha x_{i}^{\alpha-1}\quad\text{for every}\quad i\in[n].

(8)

Multiply
x_{i}
on both sides and then summing over all
i\in[n]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}\cdot\partial_{i}P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i\in[n]}\beta\alpha x_{i}^{\alpha}=\beta\alpha.

(9)

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}\cdot\partial_{i}P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x})=q\cdot P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x}),

it follows from (
9
) that

\displaystyle\beta\alpha=q\cdot P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x})=q\cdot\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}).

Combining it with (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{i}P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x})=q\cdot\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\cdot x_{i}^{\alpha-1}\quad\text{for every}\quad i\in[n],

which proves Lemma
3.1
.

The following lemma provides an estimate for the difference between
\mu_{n}
and
\mu_{n-1}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is
(\delta,N,Q)
-smooth and
\mathfrak{H}
is
(\alpha,\delta,N,Q,\mathcal{F})
-balanced in spectral.
Then there exists
N_{\ref{LEMMA:spectral-difference}}
such that for every
n\geq N_{\ref{LEMMA:spectral-difference}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{n}-\mu_{n-1}-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}\right|\leq 5\delta n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}.

(10)

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Let
n
be a sufficiently large integer.
Using (
3
) and the inequality (see Fact
A.1
)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)^{q/\alpha}\geq 1+\frac{q}{\alpha}n^{-1},

we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\mathrm{inj}(n-1,Q,\mathcal{F})\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)^{q/\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\mathrm{inj}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})-q\hat{\pi}(Q,\mathcal{F})n^{q-1}-\delta n^{q-1}\right)\left(1+\frac{q}{\alpha}n^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{inj}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})+\frac{q}{\alpha}\frac{\mathrm{inj}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})}{n}-q\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}-\delta n^{q-1}-\left(q\hat{\pi}+\delta\right)\frac{q}{\alpha}\frac{1}{n}n^{q-1}

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{inj}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})+\frac{q}{\alpha}\frac{\mathrm{inj}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})}{n}-q\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}-2\delta n^{q-1}.

By choosing
n
sufficiently large, we can ensure that (
7
) holds with
\delta
there replaced by
\alpha\delta/q
here. Combining this with the inequality above, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\left|\mathrm{inj}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})-\mathrm{inj}(n-1,Q,\mathcal{F})\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)^{q/\alpha}-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|-\frac{q}{\alpha}\frac{\mathrm{inj}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})}{n}+q\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}\right|+2\delta n^{q-1}

\displaystyle=\left|\hat{\pi}-\frac{\mathrm{inj}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})}{n^{q}}\right|\frac{q}{\alpha}n^{q-1}+2\delta n^{q-1}\leq\delta n^{q-1}+2\delta n^{q-1}=3\delta n^{q-1}.

Combining this inequality with (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\left|\mu_{n}-\mu_{n-1}-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\mathrm{inj}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})}{n^{q/\alpha}}-\frac{\mathrm{inj}(n-1,Q,\mathcal{F})}{(n-1)^{q/\alpha}}-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}\right|+2\delta n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}

\displaystyle=\left|\mathrm{inj}(n,Q,\mathcal{F})-\mathrm{inj}(n-1,Q,\mathcal{F})\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)^{\frac{q}{\alpha}}-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}\right|\frac{1}{n^{q/\alpha}}+2\delta n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}

\displaystyle\leq 3\delta n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}+2\delta n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}=5\delta n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1},

which proves Lemma
3.2
.

The following lemma shows that if
\mathcal{H}
has a large
(\alpha,Q)
-spectral radius but contains a vertex with small degree, then the optimal vector
\mathbf{x}
must have a coordinate with a small value.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
be real numbers.
There exists
N_{\ref{LEMMA:minimum-degree-weight}}=N_{\ref{LEMMA:minimum-degree-weight}}(\varepsilon)
such that the following holds for every
n\geq N_{\ref{LEMMA:minimum-degree-weight}}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{Q}(\mathcal{H})\leq(1-\varepsilon)q\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}.

Then there exists a coordinate
i\in[n]
such that

\displaystyle x_{i}\leq\left(1-\frac{\hat{\pi}\varepsilon}{(q-1)\alpha}\right)n^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Fix
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, and let
\delta\coloneqq\frac{\hat{\pi}\varepsilon}{(q-1)\alpha}
.
Let
n
be sufficiently large and suppose that
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the assumptions in Lemma
3.3
.
Let
i_{\ast}\in V(\mathcal{H})
be a vertex with

\displaystyle d_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(i_{\ast})\leq(1-\varepsilon)q\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}.

(11)

Suppose to the contrary that

\displaystyle x_{j}\geq(1-\delta)n^{-1/\alpha}\quad\text{for every}\quad j\in[n].

(12)

Claim 3.4
.

We have
\sum_{S\in L_{Q,\mathcal{H}}^{o}(i_{\ast})}x_{S}^{\alpha}\leq q\hat{\pi}
.

Proof of Claim
3.4
.

Let
V^{q-1}
denote the set of all tuples of length
q-1
with entries in
V
, and let
q\cdot V^{q-1}
denote the multiset in which each element of
V^{q-1}
appears exactly
q
times.
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\coloneqq L_{Q,\mathcal{H}}^{o}(i_{\ast})\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\mathcal{S}}\coloneqq q\cdot V^{q-1}\setminus L_{Q,\mathcal{H}}^{o}(i_{\ast}).

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in q\cdot V^{q-1}}x_{S}^{\alpha}=q\left(x_{1}^{\alpha}+\cdots+x_{n}^{\alpha}\right)^{q-1}=q.

(13)

Using (
11
), (
12
), and Bernoulli’s inequality
(1-\delta)^{(q-1)\alpha}\geq 1-(q-1)\alpha\delta
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}}x_{S}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\left(qn^{q-1}-(1-\varepsilon)q\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}\right)\left(\frac{1-\delta}{n^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{(q-1)\alpha}

\displaystyle=q(1-\delta)^{(q-1)\alpha}-(1-\varepsilon)q\hat{\pi}(1-\delta)^{(q-1)\alpha}\geq q-q(q-1)\alpha\delta-(1-\varepsilon)q\hat{\pi}.

Combining it with (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}x_{S}^{\alpha}=\sum_{S\in q\cdot V^{q-1}}x_{S}^{\alpha}-\sum_{S\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}}x_{S}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq q-\big(q-q(q-1)\alpha\delta-(1-\varepsilon)q\hat{\pi}\big)

\displaystyle=q\hat{\pi}+q(q-1)\alpha\delta-\varepsilon q\hat{\pi}=q\hat{\pi},

which proves Claim
3.4
.

Combining Claim
3.4
with Lemma
3.1
and Fact
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\cdot x_{i_{\ast}}^{\alpha-1}=\frac{\partial_{i_{\ast}}P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x})}{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\big(d_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(i_{\ast})\big)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}}{q}\left(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}x_{S}^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{q}\left((1-\varepsilon)q\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\left(q\hat{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\hat{\pi}n^{(q-1)\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\leq(1-\varepsilon)\hat{\pi}n^{(q-1)\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}.

On the other hand, combining the assumption
\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{n}
with (
12
) and Fact
2.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\cdot x_{i_{\ast}}^{\alpha-1}\geq\mu_{n}x_{i_{\ast}}^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\delta)\hat{\pi}n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{1-\delta}{n^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)^{\alpha}\hat{\pi}n^{(q-1)\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\alpha\delta)\hat{\pi}n^{(q-1)\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}>(1-\varepsilon)\hat{\pi}n^{(q-1)\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}},

which contradicts the preceding inequality.
This completes the proof of Lemma
3.3
.

The following lemma provides a lower bound on the
(\alpha,Q)
-spectral radius of the induced subgraph obtained by removing a vertex whose weight is small in the optimal vector
\mathbf{x}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be a real number and
N\geq 1
be an integer.
There exists
N_{\ref{LEMMA:delete-light-vertex}}=N_{\ref{LEMMA:delete-light-vertex}}(\delta,N)
such that the following holds for all
n\geq N_{\ref{LEMMA:delete-light-vertex}}
.
Suppose that the nonnegative optimal vector
\mathbf{x}
contains a coordinate
x_{i}
satisfying
x_{i}\leq(1-\delta)n^{-1/\alpha}
.
Then the induced subgraph
\mathcal{H}-i
satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}\left(\mathcal{H}-i\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{1}{n}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}).

(14)

Suppose further that
\mathcal{F}
is
(\xi/5,N,Q)
-smooth,
\mathfrak{H}
is
(\alpha,\xi/5,N,Q,\mathcal{F})
-balanced in spectral for some constant
\xi<\frac{q(\alpha-1)\hat{\pi}\delta}{4\alpha}
, and
\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}\left(\mathcal{H}-i\right)>\mu_{n-1}.

(15)

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Let
n
be sufficiently large.
By relabeling the vertices of
\mathcal{H}
if necessary, we may assume that
x_{n}\leq(1-\delta)n^{-1/\alpha}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}
denote the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{H}
on
[n-1]
.
Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}\coloneqq\left(\frac{x_{1}}{\left(1-x_{n}^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}},\ldots,\frac{x_{n-1}}{\left(1-x_{n}^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}}\right)\in\Delta_{n-2}^{\alpha}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})=P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=P_{Q,\mathcal{G}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})+x_{n}\cdot\partial_{n}P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=P_{Q,\mathcal{G}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})+q\cdot\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\cdot x_{n}^{\alpha},

which implies that

\displaystyle P_{Q,\mathcal{G}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})=\left(1-qx_{n}^{\alpha}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}).

(16)

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle P_{Q,\mathcal{G}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\left(1-x_{n}^{\alpha}\right)^{q/\alpha}\cdot P_{Q,\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathbf{y}\right)\leq\left(1-x_{n}^{\alpha}\right)^{q/\alpha}\cdot\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{G}).

Combining it with (
16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-x_{n}^{\alpha}\right)^{-q/\alpha}\cdot P_{Q,\mathcal{G}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})=\left(1-x_{n}^{\alpha}\right)^{-q/\alpha}\left(1-qx_{n}^{\alpha}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}).

Since
\left(1-x_{n}^{\alpha}\right)^{-q/\alpha}\geq 1+qx_{n}^{\alpha}/\alpha
(see Fact
A.2
) and
x_{n}\leq(1-\delta)n^{-1/\alpha}
, it follows from the inequality above that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1+\frac{q}{\alpha}x_{n}^{\alpha}\right)\left(1-qx_{n}^{\alpha}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}x_{n}^{\alpha}-\frac{q^{2}}{\alpha}x_{n}^{2\alpha}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(\frac{1-\delta}{n^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}-\frac{q^{2}}{\alpha}\left(\frac{1-\delta}{n^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{2\alpha}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\frac{1-\delta}{n}-\frac{q^{2}}{\alpha}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{1}{n}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}),

(17)

which proves (
14
).

Now suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is
(\xi/5,N,Q)
-smooth,
\mathfrak{H}
is
(\alpha,\xi/5,N,Q,\mathcal{F})
-balanced in spectral for some constant
\xi<\frac{q(\alpha-1)\hat{\pi}\delta}{4\alpha}
, and
\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{n}
.
It follows from Lemma
3.2
that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{n}\geq\mu_{n-1}+\left(\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}-\xi\right)n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}.

Combining it with (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{1}{n}\right)\left(\mu_{n-1}+\left(\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}-\xi\right)n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{n-1}-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{\mu_{n-1}}{n}+\left(\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}-\xi\right)n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\left(\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}-\xi\right)n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-2}.

(18)

By Fact
2.5
, we can assume from the beginning that
n
is sufficiently large so that

\displaystyle\mu_{n-1}\leq\left(1+\frac{\delta}{5}\right)\hat{\pi}(n-1)^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}}\leq\left(1+\frac{\delta}{5}\right)\hat{\pi}n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}},

Combining it with the inequality
-(1-\delta/2)(1+\delta/5)\geq-(1-\delta/4)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{\mu_{n-1}}{n}+\left(\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}-\xi\right)n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{\delta}{5}\right)\hat{\pi}n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}}+\left(\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}-\xi\right)n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{4}\right)\hat{\pi}n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}+\left(\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}-\xi\right)n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q(\alpha-1)\delta}{4\alpha}\hat{\pi}-\xi\right)n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}>\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\left(\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\hat{\pi}-\xi\right)\frac{1}{n}n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}-1}.

Combining this inequality with (
3.1
), we obtain that
\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{G})>\mu_{n-1}
.
This proves (
15
).

3.2
Proof of Theorem

We are now ready to present the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\delta\coloneqq\frac{\hat{\pi}\varepsilon}{(q-1)\alpha}
and
\xi\coloneqq\frac{q(\alpha-1)\hat{\pi}\delta}{5\alpha}<\frac{q(\alpha-1)\hat{\pi}\delta}{4\alpha}
.
Suppose that
M
is an integer satisfying

(i)

\mathcal{F}
is
(\xi/5,M,Q)
-smooth,

(ii)

\mathcal{F}
is
(\varepsilon,M,Q)
-degree stable with respect to
\mathfrak{H}
, and

(iii)

\mathfrak{H}
is
(\alpha,\xi/5,M,Q,\mathcal{F})
-balanced in spectral.

Fix a sufficiently large integer

so that

, where

and

are constants given by Lemmas
3.3
and
3.5
.
Let
n
be a sufficiently large integer so that, in particular,

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}e^{-3q^{2}}n^{\frac{\delta q(\alpha-1)}{2\alpha}}>2N^{q}.

(19)

Suppose to the contrary that there exists an
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
on
[n]
such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\geq\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(n,\mathfrak{H})\quad\text{but}\quad\mathcal{H}\not\in\mathfrak{H}.

Then it follows from Assumption
(ii)
that

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}(\mathcal{H})\leq(1-\varepsilon)q\hat{\pi}n^{q-1}.

(20)

Recall that
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathrm{OPT}_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
is a nonnegative optimal vector.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{n}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}
.
It follows from (
20
) and Lemma
3.3
that there exists a coordinate
i_{\ast}\in V(\mathcal{H})
such that

\displaystyle x_{i_{\ast}}\leq(1-\delta)n^{-1/\alpha}.

(21)

Define
\mathcal{H}_{n-1}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{n}-i_{\ast}
.
It follows from (
21
) and Lemma
3.5
that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{n-1})\geq\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{1}{n}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{n}),

Moreover, from the inequality
\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\geq\mu_{n}
with Assumptions
(i)
and
(iii)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{n-1})>\mu_{n-1}.

Thus, by Assumption
(ii)
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}(\mathcal{H}_{n-1})\leq(1-\varepsilon)q\hat{\pi}(n-1)^{q-1}.

(22)

By relabeling the vertices in
\mathcal{H}
we may assume that
i_{\ast}=n
, and thus
V(\mathcal{H}_{n-1})=[n-1]
.
Fix an arbitrary nonnegative optimal vector
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1})\in\mathrm{OPT}_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{n-1})
.
It follows from (
22
) and Lemma
3.3
that there exists a coordinate
j_{\ast}\in V(\mathcal{H}_{n-1})
such that

\displaystyle y_{j_{\ast}}\leq(1-\delta)(n-1)^{-1/\alpha}.

(23)

Define
\mathcal{H}_{n-2}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{n-1}-j_{\ast}
.
It follows from (
23
) and Lemma
3.5
that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{n-2})\geq\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{1}{n-1}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{n-1}),

Moreover, from the inequality
\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{n-1})>\mu_{n-1}
with Assumptions
(i)
and
(iii)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{n-2})>\mu_{n-2}.

Therefore, we can repeat this process until we reach
\mathcal{H}_{N}
, ensuring that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{m-1})\geq\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{1}{m}\right)\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{m})\quad\text{for}\quad m\in[N+1,n-1].

(24)

Using inequalities
\sum_{m=N+1}^{n}\leq\ln(n/N)+1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}m^{-2}\leq\pi^{2}/6<2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\sum_{m=N+1}^{n}\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{1}{m}-\sum_{m=N+1}^{n}\frac{q^{2}(\alpha-1)^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{m^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\ln\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)-3q^{2}.

Since
1-x\geq e^{-x-x^{2}}
for
x\in[0,1/2]
(see Fact
A.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle\prod_{m=N+1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{1}{m}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{exp}\left(\Phi\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{exp}\left(-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\ln\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)-2q^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-3q^{2}}\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^{-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}+\frac{q(\alpha-1)\delta}{2\alpha}}.

Combining this with (
24
), Fact
2.5
(with
\delta^{\prime}
there replaced by
1/2
), and assumption (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{N})

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}_{n})\cdot\prod_{m=N+1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\frac{1}{m}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{n}\cdot e^{-3q^{2}}\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^{-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}+\frac{q(\alpha-1)\delta}{2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq\hat{\pi}n^{q-\frac{q}{\alpha}}\cdot e^{-3q^{2}}n^{-\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}+\frac{q(\alpha-1)\delta}{2\alpha}}N^{\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}/2

\displaystyle\geq\hat{\pi}e^{-3q^{2}}n^{\frac{q(\alpha-1)\delta}{2\alpha}}N^{\frac{q(\alpha-1)}{\alpha}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)}/2>N^{q},

which is clearly impossible.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
m\geq q\geq 1
be integers.
For
n\geq 1
, denote by
T_{m,n}^{q}
the balanced complete
m
-partite
q
-graph on
n
vertices.
That is, let
V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}=[n]
be a partition such that
|V_{1}|\geq\cdots\geq|V_{m}|\geq|V_{1}|-1
. Then
T_{m,n}^{q}
is the
q
-graph consisting of all
q
-subsets of
[n]
that intersect each part
V_{i}
in at most one vertex.

We will use the following estimate for
\lambda_{\alpha}(T_{m,n}^{q})
, the
\alpha
-spectral radius of
T_{m,n}^{q})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
m\geq q\geq 2
be integers and
\alpha>1
be a real number.
There exists a constant
C_{\ref{LEMMA:spectral-balanced-Turan-r-graph}}=C_{\ref{LEMMA:spectral-balanced-Turan-r-graph}}(m,q,\alpha)
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle{q!|T_{m,n}^{q}|}{n^{-\frac{q}{\alpha}}}\leq\lambda_{\alpha}(T_{m,n}^{q})\leq\left(1+{C_{\ref{LEMMA:spectral-balanced-Turan-r-graph}}}{n^{-2}}\right){q!|T_{m,n}^{q}|}{n^{-\frac{q}{\alpha}}}.

In the proof of Lemma
4.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we will use the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
KNY15
, Theorems 2 and 3]
)
.

Let
\alpha>1
be a real number and
m\geq q\geq 2
be integers. Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is an
m
-partite
q
-graph on
n
vertices.
Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\alpha}(T_{m,n}^{q}),\quad\text{and}

(25)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\leq q!\binom{m}{q}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}m^{-\frac{q}{\alpha}}|\mathcal{H}|^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(26)

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Fix a real number
\alpha>1
and integers
m\geq q\geq 2
.
By Fact
1.3
, it suffices to prove the upper bound in Lemma
4.1
.
First, we prove the following estimate for
|T_{m,n}^{q}|
.

Claim 4.3
.

There exists a constant
C_{\ref{CLAIM:Turan-number-concentration}}=C_{\ref{CLAIM:Turan-number-concentration}}(m,q)
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left(1-C_{\ref{CLAIM:Turan-number-concentration}}n^{-2}\right)\binom{m}{q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{q}\leq|T_{m,n}^{q}|\leq\binom{m}{q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{q}.

Proof of Claim
4.3
.

The upper bound is well-known, so it suffices to prove that
|T_{m,n}^{q}|\geq\binom{m}{q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{q}-C_{\ref{CLAIM:Turan-number-concentration}}n^{q-2}
for some constant
C_{\ref{CLAIM:Turan-number-concentration}}
depending only on
m
and
q
.

Fix
n\geq 1
. Let
(s,t)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
be integers such that
n=ms+t
and
0\leq t\leq m-1
.
Let

\displaystyle f(x)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{q}\binom{t}{i}\binom{m-t}{q-i}\left(Y+x\right)^{i}\left(Y-\frac{tx}{m-t}\right)^{q-i},\quad\text{where}\quad Y\coloneqq\frac{n}{m}=\frac{ms+t}{m}.

Straightforward calculations show that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}f(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\binom{t}{i}\binom{m-t}{q-i}i\left(Y+x\right)^{i-1}\left(Y-\frac{tx}{m-t}\right)^{q-i}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\binom{t}{i}\binom{m-t}{q-i}\frac{-(q-i)t}{m-t}\left(Y+x\right)^{i}\left(Y-\frac{tx}{m-t}\right)^{q-i-1}.

Since (as elementary calculations show) the coefficient of the
Y^{q-1}
term in
\frac{\mathrm{d}f(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{q}\binom{t}{i}\binom{m-t}{q-i}i-\frac{t}{m-t}\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\binom{t}{i}\binom{m-t}{q-i}\frac{(q-i)t}{m-t}=0,

it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}f(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}=\sum_{i=0}^{q-2}\alpha_{i}Y^{i},

(27)

where each
\alpha_{i}
is a constant depending only on
m,q,x,i
.

Observe that

\displaystyle f(0)=\binom{m}{q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{q}\quad\text{and}\quad f\left(\frac{m-t}{m}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{q}\binom{t}{i}\binom{m-t}{q-i}\left(s+1\right)^{i}s^{q-i}=|T_{m,n}^{q}|.

By the Mean Value Theorem, there exists
y\in\left[0,\frac{m-t}{m}\right]
such that

\displaystyle\left||T_{m,n}^{q}|-\binom{m}{q}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{q}\right|=\left|f\left(\frac{m-t}{m}\right)-f(0)\right|=\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}f(y)}{\mathrm{d}y}\frac{m-t}{m}\right|\leq CY^{q-2},

where the last inequality follows from (
27
), and
C
is a constant depending only on
m
and
q
.
This proves Claim
4.3
.

By Claim
4.3
and the assumption that
n
is sufficiently large, we obtain

\displaystyle|T_{m,n}^{q}|^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq\left(1-C_{\ref{CLAIM:Turan-number-concentration}}n^{-2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\binom{m}{q}^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{-\frac{q}{\alpha}}\leq\left(1+\frac{2C_{\ref{CLAIM:Turan-number-concentration}}}{\alpha}n^{-2}\right)\binom{m}{q}^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{-\frac{q}{\alpha}},

Combining it with (
26
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}(T_{m,n}^{q})

\displaystyle\leq q!\binom{m}{q}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}m^{-\frac{q}{\alpha}}|T_{m,n}^{q}|\left(1+\frac{2C_{\ref{CLAIM:Turan-number-concentration}}}{\alpha}n^{-2}\right)\binom{m}{q}^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{-\frac{q}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{2C_{\ref{CLAIM:Turan-number-concentration}}}{\alpha}n^{-2}\right)q!|T_{m,n}^{q}|n^{-\frac{q}{\alpha}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

We are now ready to present the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Recall that
\mathfrak{C}_{5}
denotes the collection of all
C_{5}
-colorable graphs.
This family is clearly hereditary, and every graph in
\mathfrak{C}_{5}
is
K_{3}
-free.
It was shown in
[
CL24
, Theorem 2.3]
that there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
N^{\prime}
such that
K_{3}
is
(\varepsilon,N^{\prime},C_{5})
-degree stable with respect to
\mathfrak{C}_{5}
.
Let
\delta>0
,
M
, and
N
be the constants given by Theorem
1.5
.
By enlarging
M
and
N
if necessary, we may assume that they are sufficiently large, and in particular that
\min\{M,~N\}\geq N^{\prime}
.

By Theorem
1.5
, it remains to show that
\mathcal{F}
is
(\delta,M,Q)
-smooth and that
\mathfrak{C}_{5}
is
(\alpha,\delta,M,Q,\mathcal{F})
-balanced in spectral.
Note that the former property follows easily from Theorem
1.1
, so we focus on verifying the latter.

Claim 4.4
.

The family
\mathfrak{C}_{5}
is
(\alpha,\delta,M,Q,\mathcal{F})
-balanced in spectral.

Proof of Claim
4.4
.

Fix
n\geq M
.
By Fact
1.3
, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,C_{5}}(n,\mathfrak{C}_{5})\leq\frac{\mathrm{inj}(n,C_{5},K_{3})}{n^{5/\alpha}}+\delta n^{4-5/\alpha}=\frac{|\mathrm{Aut}(C_{5})||T_{5,n}^{5}|}{n^{5/\alpha}}+\delta n^{4-5/\alpha},

(28)

where the equality follows from Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{T}_{5}
be the collection of all
5
-partite
5
-graphs.
For every
G\in\mathfrak{C}_{5}
, we associate a
5
-graph
\mathcal{H}_{G}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{G}\coloneqq\left\{S\in\binom{V(G)}{5}\colon C_{5}\subseteq G[S]\right\}.

Observe that
\mathcal{H}_{G}\in\mathfrak{T}_{5}
and

\displaystyle P_{C_{5},G}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=\frac{|\mathrm{Aut}(C_{5})|}{5!}P_{\mathcal{H}_{G}}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}).

So it follows from Lemma
4.1
and (
25
) that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha,C_{5}}(n,\mathfrak{C}_{5})\leq\frac{|\mathrm{Aut}(C_{5})|}{5!}\lambda_{\alpha}(n,\mathfrak{T}_{5})

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathrm{Aut}(C_{5})|}{5!}\lambda_{\alpha}(T_{5,n}^{5})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathrm{Aut}(C_{5})|}{5!}\left(1+C_{\ref{LEMMA:spectral-balanced-Turan-r-graph}}n^{-2}\right)5!|T_{5,n}^{5}|n^{-\frac{5}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathrm{Aut}(C_{5})||T_{5,n}^{5}|}{n^{5/\alpha}}+|\mathrm{Aut}(C_{5})|\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)^{5}n^{-2-\frac{5}{\alpha}},

which implies (
28
), since
n
is large.

Claim
4.4
completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

5
An entropy perspective

Let
X
be a discrete random variable taking values in a finite set
\Omega
. For convenience, let

\displaystyle p_{X}(x)\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(X=x)\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in\Omega.

The support of
X
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}(X)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Omega\colon p_{X}(x)>0\right\}.

For notational convenience, we set
0\cdot\log_{2}0=0
.
Recall that the well-known
Shannon entropy

[
Sha48
]
of
X
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}[X]\coloneqq-\sum_{x\in\mathrm{Supp}(X)}p_{X}(x)\cdot\log p_{X}(x)=-\sum_{x\in\Omega}p_{X}(x)\cdot\log p_{X}(x).

Given random variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}
defined on a common sample space
\Omega
, their
mixture
, denoted by
\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})
, is the probability distribution defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})=v\right)=\sum_{i\in[q]}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(X_{i}=v\right)}{q}\quad\text{for every}\quad v\in\Omega.

Let
Q
be an
r
-graph on the vertex set
[q]
.
Inspired by the definition in
[
CY24
]
, we say a
q
-tuple of random variables
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})
is
a random embedding of
Q
in
\mathcal{H}
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
is always an element in
\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})
, and

(ii)

(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})
is symmetric with respect to
\mathrm{Aut}(Q)
, that is,
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})
is the same as
(X_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,X_{\sigma(q)})
for every
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q)
.

We define the
(\alpha,Q)
-entropic density
of
\mathcal{H}
as

\displaystyle\eta_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\max_{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})}2^{\mathbb{H}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})-\frac{q}{\alpha}\mathbb{H}\left(\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})\right)},

where the maximum is taken over all random embeddings of
Q
in
\mathcal{H}
.
In the case where
Q=K_{r}^{r}
is a single edge, we abbreviate
\eta_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})
as
\eta_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
.

It was shown in
[
CY24
, Proposition 5.4]
that for every
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
and every
\alpha\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\eta_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})=\lambda_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}).

We extend their result to arbitrary
Q
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
Q
and
\mathcal{H}
be
r
-graphs.
For every
\alpha\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\eta_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})=\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}).

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Fix
\alpha\geq 1
.
Let
Q
be an
r
-graph on the vertex set
[q]
and
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-graph on the vertex set
[n]
.
We begin by establishing the following claim.

Claim 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonnegative vector.
Let
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be the random embedding of
Q
in
\mathcal{H}
given by the distribution

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left((X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})\right)=\frac{x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{q}}}{\beta}\quad\text{for every}\quad(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H}),

(29)

where
\beta\coloneqq\sum_{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})}\prod_{j\in\phi}x_{j}
.
Also, for every
j\in[n]
, let

\displaystyle y_{j}\coloneqq\frac{1}{q}\frac{x_{j}}{\beta}\sum_{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H},j)}\prod_{k\in\phi-j}x_{k}.

(30)

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}[(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})]-\frac{q}{\alpha}\mathbb{H}[\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})]=\log_{2}\beta-\frac{q}{\alpha}\sum_{j\in[n]}y_{j}\log_{2}\left(\frac{x_{j}^{\alpha}}{y_{j}}\right).

Proof of Claim
5.2
.

By definition (
29
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}[(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})}-\frac{x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{q}}}{\beta}\log_{2}\frac{x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{q}}}{\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})}\frac{x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{q}}}{\beta}\left(\log_{2}\beta-\sum_{j\in[q]}\log_{2}x_{i_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log_{2}\beta-\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})}\frac{x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{q}}}{\beta}\sum_{j\in[q]}\log_{2}x_{i_{j}}

\displaystyle=\log_{2}\beta-\sum_{j\in[n]}qy_{j}\log_{2}x_{j}.

For every
(i,j)\in[q]\times[n]
, let

\displaystyle y_{i\to j}\coloneqq\frac{x_{j}}{\beta}\sum_{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H},i\to j)}\prod_{k\in\phi-j}x_{k}.

Note that
P(X_{i}=j)=y_{i\to j}
for every
(i,j)\in[q]\times[n]
and
y_{j}=\sum_{i\in[q]}y_{i\to j}/q
for every
j\in[n]
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}[\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})]=-\sum_{j\in[n]}y_{j}\log_{2}y_{j},

and hence,

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{H}\left[(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})\right]-\frac{q}{\alpha}\mathbb{H}[\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})]

\displaystyle=\log_{2}\beta-\sum_{j\in[n]}qy_{j}\log_{2}x_{j}+\frac{q}{\alpha}\sum_{j\in[n]}y_{j}\log_{2}y_{j}=\log_{2}\beta-\frac{q}{\alpha}\sum_{j\in[n]}y_{j}\log_{2}\left(\frac{x_{j}^{\alpha}}{y_{j}}\right).

This proves Claim
5.2
.

Now we prove that
\eta_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\leq\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
.
Fix a nonnegative optimal vector
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathrm{OPT}_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
, whose existence is guaranteed by Fact
2.1
.
It follows from the definition of
\mathrm{OPT}_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
that

\displaystyle\beta\coloneqq\sum_{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})}\prod_{j\in\phi}x_{j}=\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}).

It follows from the convexity of the function
\log_{2}\colon\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in[n]}y_{j}\log_{2}\left(\frac{x_{j}^{\alpha}}{y_{j}}\right)\leq\log_{2}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}y_{j}\cdot\frac{x_{j}^{\alpha}}{y_{j}}\right)=\log_{2}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}x_{j}^{\alpha}\right)=\log_{2}1=0.

So, by Claim
5.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}\left[(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})\right]-\frac{q}{\alpha}\mathbb{H}[\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})]\geq\log_{2}\beta=\log_{2}\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H}).

which proves that
\eta_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\geq\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
.

Now we consider the other direction.
Let
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})
be a random embedding of
Q
in
\mathcal{H}
that maximizes
\mathbb{H}[(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})]-\frac{q}{\alpha}\mathbb{H}[\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})]
.
For every
\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})
, let

\displaystyle p_{\phi}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left((X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})=\phi\right).

We say that two embeddings
\phi,\psi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})
are equivalent, denoted
\phi\sim\psi
, if there exists an automorphism
\theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q)
such that
\phi\circ\theta=\psi
.
It follows from Property
(ii)
that
p_{\phi}=p_{\psi}
whenever
\phi\sim\psi
.

Let
\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})/\sim
denote the set of equivalence classes in
\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})
.
Let
[X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}]
denote the probability distribution of
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})
induced on
\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})
, that is, each equivalence class
[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle q_{\phi}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left([X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}]=[\phi]\right)=\sum_{\psi\in[\phi]}\mathbb{P}\left((X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})=\psi\right)=p_{\phi}|\mathrm{Aut}(Q)|.

(31)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}\left[(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{H}\left[(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})\mid[X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}]\right]+\mathbb{H}\left[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}]\right]

\displaystyle=\log_{2}|\mathrm{Aut}(Q)|-\sum_{[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})}q_{\phi}\log_{2}q_{\phi}.

(32)

For each
j\in[n]
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H},j)\coloneqq\big\{[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})\colon j\in\phi\big\},

and let

\displaystyle y_{j}\coloneqq\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H},j)}p_{\phi}=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H},j)}q_{\phi}\quad\text{and}\quad x_{j}\coloneqq y_{j}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=\left(\frac{1}{q}\sum_{[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H},j)}q_{\phi}\right)^{1/\alpha}.

(33)

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})=j\right)=y_{j}\quad\text{for every}\quad j\in[n],

and thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}\left[\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})\right]=-\sum_{j\in[n]}y_{j}\log_{2}y_{j}=-\sum_{j\in[n]}x_{j}^{\alpha}\log_{2}x_{j}^{\alpha}.

Combining it with (
5
), we conclude that the distribution
\mathbf{q}\coloneqq\left\{q_{\phi}\colon[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})\right\}
maximizes the objective

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathbf{q})\coloneqq-\sum_{[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})}q_{\phi}\log_{2}q_{\phi}+\frac{q}{\alpha}\sum_{j\in[n]}x_{j}^{\alpha}\log_{2}x_{j}^{\alpha},

subject to the constraints

\displaystyle q_{\phi}\geq 0~\text{for every}~[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})}q_{\phi}=1.

It follows from the Lagrange multiplier method that

\displaystyle-1-\log_{2}q_{\phi}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{j\in\phi}\left(1+\log_{2}x_{j}^{\alpha}\right)=-1+\frac{q}{\alpha}-\log_{2}\frac{q_{\phi}}{\prod_{j\in\phi}x_{j}}

is constant for all
[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})
with
q_{\phi}>0
.
Equivalently, there exists a constant
\gamma
such that

\displaystyle\frac{q_{\phi}}{\prod_{j\in\phi}x_{j}}=\gamma

(34)

for all
[\phi]\in\mathcal{N}(Q,\mathcal{H})
with
q_{\phi}>0
.

It follows from (
31
) and (
34
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left((X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})=\phi\right)=\frac{q_{\phi}}{|\mathrm{Aut}(Q)|}=\frac{\gamma}{|\mathrm{Aut}(Q)|}\prod_{j\in\phi}x_{j}=\frac{1}{\beta}\prod_{j\in\phi}x_{j},

(35)

where
\beta\coloneqq|\mathrm{Aut}(Q)|/\gamma
.

Also, notice that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})=j\right)=y_{j}=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H},j)}p_{\phi}=\frac{x_{j}}{q\beta}\sum_{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H},j)}\prod_{k\in\phi-j}x_{k}.

So it follows from Claim
5.2
and definition (
33
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}[(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})]-\frac{q}{\alpha}\mathbb{H}[\mu(X_{1},\ldots,X_{q})]

\displaystyle=\log_{2}\beta-\frac{q}{\alpha}\sum_{j\in[n]}y_{j}\log_{2}\left(\frac{x_{j}^{\alpha}}{y_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log_{2}\beta-\frac{q}{\alpha}\sum_{j\in[n]}y_{j}\log_{2}1=\log_{2}\beta.

(36)

Note from (
35
) that the partition function
\beta
satisfies

\displaystyle\beta=\sum_{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})}\prod_{j\in\phi}x_{j}.

Also observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}^{\alpha}=\sum_{i\in[n]}y_{i}=1\quad\text{and}\quad x_{i}\geq 0~\text{for every}~i\in[n],

which imply that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\Delta_{\alpha}^{n-1}
.
So, it follows from the definition of
\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
that
\beta\leq\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
.
Combining this with (
5
), we conclude that
\eta_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})\leq\lambda_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})
.
This completes the proof of Proposition
5.1
.

6
Concluding remarks

Just as the theorem of Keevash–Lenz–Mubayi
[
KLM14
]
connects the Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós-type stability of a Turán problem with its corresponding spectral Turán problem, Theorem
1.5
establishes an analogous connection for generalized Turán problems and their spectral counterparts.
Extending the work
[
LMR23
,
HLZ24
]
on Turán problems, the type of stability required by Theorem
1.5
was recently studied systematically in
[
CL24
,
CIL+24
]
for generalized Turán problems, and also investigated in
[
CHHL24
]
for certain generalized Turán problems involving odd cycles.
Theorem
1.2
provides just one application of Theorem
1.5
, and we hope this work could motivate further research in this direction.

The entropy approach has been shown to be quite effective in solving certain Turán problems, as shown in works such as
[
CY24
,
Liu25
]
. It would be interesting to explore what this approach can produce in the context of the generalized Turán problem.

In this appendix, we provide proofs of several inequalities used in the main arguments, as well as some basic lemmas analogous to those in

Fact A.1
.

Suppose that
x>1
and
\beta>0
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\frac{x}{x-1}\right)^{\beta}\geq 1+\frac{\beta}{x}.

Proof of Fact
A.1
.

Fix
\beta>0
and let

\displaystyle f(x)\coloneqq\left(\frac{x}{x-1}\right)^{\beta}-\left(1+\frac{\beta}{x}\right).

Straightforward calculations show that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}f(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}=-\beta\left(x\left(\frac{x}{x-1}\right)^{\beta}+x-1\right){(x-1)^{-1}x^{-2}}.

It follows that
f(x)
is decreasing on the interval
(1,\infty)
.
Therefore,
f(x)\geq\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=0
, which implies the desired inequality.

Fact A.2
.

Suppose that
x\in[0,1]
and
\beta\geq 0
. Then

\left(1-x\right)^{-\beta}\geq 1+\beta x
.

Proof of Fact
A.2
.

Fix
\beta>0
(note that the case
\beta=0
is trivial) and let

\displaystyle f(x)\coloneqq(1+\beta x)(1-x)^{\beta}-1.

Straightforward calculations show that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}f(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}=-\beta(\beta+1)x(1-x)^{\beta-1}.

It follows that
f(x)
is decreasing on the interval
[0,1]
.
Therefore,
f(x)\leq f(0)=0
, which implies the desired inequality.

Fact A.3
.

Suppose that
x\in[0,1/2]
. Then

1-x\geq e^{-x-x^{2}}
.

Proof of Fact
A.3
.

Let
f(x)\coloneqq(1-x)e^{x+x^{2}}
. Straightforward calculations show that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}f(x)}{\mathrm{d}x}=x(1-2x)e^{x^{2}+x}.

It follows that
f(x)
is increasing on the interval
[0,1/2]
.
Therefore,
f(x)\geq f(0)=1
, which implies the desired inequality.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
be a real number and
Q
be an
r
-graph.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-graph on the vertex set
[n]
.
Suppose that vertices
i
and
j
are equivalent in
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\Delta^{n-1}_{\alpha}
.
Define
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\Delta^{n-1}_{\alpha}
by

\displaystyle y_{k}=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{x_{i}^{\alpha}+x_{j}^{\alpha}}{2}\right)^{1/\alpha},&\quad\text{if}\quad k\in\{i,j\},\\
x_{k},&\quad\text{if}\quad k\in[n]\setminus\{i,j\}.\end{cases}

(37)

Then

\displaystyle P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{y})\geq P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x}).

Proof of Lemma
A.4
.

Since
\alpha\geq 1
, the Power Mean Inequality implies that

\displaystyle y_{i}=y_{j}\geq\sqrt{x_{i}x_{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad y_{i}+y_{j}\geq x_{i}+x_{j}.

Let

\displaystyle C_{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})\colon\text{$i\in\phi(V(Q))$ but $j\not\in\phi(V(Q))$}\right\},

\displaystyle C_{j}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})\colon\text{$j\in\phi(V(Q))$ but $i\not\in\phi(V(Q))$}\right\},

\displaystyle C_{ij}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\phi\in\mathrm{Inj}(Q,\mathcal{H})\colon\text{$i\in\phi(V(Q))$ and $j\in\phi(V(Q))$}\right\}.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{y})-P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi\in C_{i}}\left(y_{i}-x_{i}\right)\prod_{k\in\phi-i}x_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\phi\in C_{j}}\left(y_{j}-x_{j}\right)\prod_{k\in\phi-j}x_{k}+\sum_{\phi\in C_{ij}}\left(y_{i}y_{j}-x_{i}x_{j}\right)\prod_{k\in\phi-\{i,j\}}x_{k}.

Since
i
and
j
are equivalent in
\mathcal{H}
, we have
C_{i}=C_{j}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{y})-P_{Q,\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi\in C_{i}}\left(y_{i}+y_{j}-x_{i}-x_{j}\right)\prod_{k\in\phi-i}x_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\phi\in C_{ij}}\left(y_{i}y_{j}-x_{i}x_{j}\right)\prod_{k\in\phi-\{i,j\}}x_{k}\geq 0,

which proves Lemma
A.4
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
be a real number. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r

Proof of Lemma
A.5
.

Choose

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\sum_{j\in[k]}\sum_{i\in P_{j}}\left|x_{i}-\overline{x}_{P_{j}}\right|

is minimized, where

\displaystyle\overline{x}_{P_{j}}\coloneqq\left(|P_{j}|^{-1}\sum_{i\in P_{j}}x_{i}^{\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}.

Note that it suffices to show that
\Phi(\mathbf{x})=0
. Suppose to the contrary that this fails.
Then there exists some
P_{\ell}
such that
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
for some

Let
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
be defined as in (

\mathbf{y}\in\mathrm{OPT}_{\alpha,Q}(\mathcal{H})

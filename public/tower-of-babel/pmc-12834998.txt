The application of local lesion postoperative continuous irrigation for the perioperative management of spinal tuberculosis with posterior-only approach surgery
Authors: Lige Xiao, Gengming Zhang, Manini Daudi Romani, Emmanu el Alonge, Jinyang Liu, Ang Deng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To investigate the differences in perioperative indices, including experimental results and quality-of-life-related scores between patients managed with postoperative irrigation and drainage and those managed with drainage alone. This was a retrospective analysis of 88 patients with spinal tuberculosis who underwent surgical treatment at our spinal surgery center from January 2017 to January 2022, including 32 patients with continuous postoperative irrigation and 56 patients with postoperative drainage alone. Demographic and general characteristics, laboratory data, quality-of-life-related data, and complications were compared. Compared the drainage-only group, the inflammation-related index decreased more rapidly when local irrigation was performed. The ESR decreased from 65.7 ± 15.6 to 45.6 ± 8.3 in the irrigation group and from 64.2 ± 12.1 to 56.3 ± 6.5 in the drainage-only group during the first postoperative week. The VAS score also improved significantly in both the irrigation and drainage-only groups. In addition, the neurological condition improved to a greater extent at 2 weeks after surgery in the irrigation group, although a significant difference was not detected between the two groups. The average bed rest time of the irrigation group was longer than that of the drainage-only group, and the D-dimer level increased more in the irrigation group than in the drainage-only group at one week. Compared with the preoperative HADS score, the postoperative HADS score clearly decreased in both groups, and the irrigation group had better mental health conditions than the drainage-only group. Postoperative continuous irrigation combined with drainage is an optional perioperative management strategy that can significantly improve inflammatory and mental conditions compared with postoperative drainage alone, resulting in better clinical outcomes at the early postoperative stage.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-33401-z.

Introduction

Even though tuberculosis (TB) is a preventable and curable disease, TB still causes more than 1 million deaths every year, making it the second leading cause of death from a single infectious agent worldwide1. TB is caused by the bacillus Mycobacterium tuberculosis, which usually attacks the respiratory system, leading to pulmonary TB. Skeletal TB is the most common type of extrapulmonary TB, and spinal TB accounts for 50%-70% of all bone and joint TB cases2,3. Severe spinal TB can cause spinal deformity and neurological deficits, in addition to common systemic symptoms such as low-grade fever, night sweats, weakness, loss of appetite, and anemia4. With regular and standard antitubercular chemotherapy, surgical debridement can be an adjunct to the principle treatment, and this approach involves spinal cord decompression, abscess drainage, and spinal realignment reconstruction5. Posterior instrumentation, anterior debridement, and bone grafting in a single or two-stage procedure (combined anterior and posterior, AP approach), anterior debridement and strut grafting with instrumentation (anterior only, AO approach) is widely used and regarded as the standard surgical intervention for the treatment of spinal TB. In recent decades, posterior-only approach debridement and fusion (PO approach) have been proven to be effective for correcting spinal deformity and decompressing the abscesses. However, the postoperative irrigation and drainage of local lesions in patients with spinal TB infection differ from those in patients with common spinal bacterial infections6–11. Studies have suggested that incomplete and inadequate treatment is the most important factor leading to relapse and secondary drug resistance12. In this study, we investigated the differences in perioperative indices, including experimental results and quality-of-life-related scores, between patients who were managed with postoperative irrigation and drainage and those who were managed with drainage alone.

Results

Demographic and general characteristics

Our study included 88 patients (female/male: 45/43) with TB. Of these 32 patients (female/male: 19/13) underwent irrigation combined with drainage, while 56 patients (female/male: 26/30) underwent drainage alone. The average ages were 59.3 ± 9.5 (range 36–77) years for the irrigation group and 56.1 ± 8.1 (range 39–76) years for the drainage-only group. The mean operative time and blood loss were 116.8 ± 27.6 min and 728.1 ± 185.3 ml, respectively, in the irrigation group. In the drainage-only group, the values were 175.7 ± 24.5 min and 732.1 ± 193.6 ml. Continuous irrigation in the irrigation group was performed for an average of 3.9 ± 0.9 days, with all drainage tubes removed after 8.3 ± 0.9 days, and an average bed rest duration of 6.0 ± 0.9 days. The average bed rest time was 10.5 ± 1.0 days for the irrigation group and 7.7 ± 1.2 days for the drainage-only group, with a significantly longer duration in the irrigation group (Table 1).

Laboratory data

Rapid pathological diagnosis performed during the operation revealed granulomatous infection, and specimen cultures/metagenomics were consistent with TB. Compared the preoperative inflammatory index, the postoperative inflammatory index increased on the third postoperative day and then decreased significantly on the 7th and 14th days. This also indicated that the inflammatory condition improved more noticeably in the irrigation group at 1 week post operation (Fig. 1; Table 2). The difference in D-dimer levels, which were 1.4 ± 0.6 and 1.6 ± 0.5, was not significant between the irrigation group and drainage-only group preoperatively. On postoperative Day 7, D-dimer levels were significantly higher in the irrigation group (4.0 ± 0.8), compared to 3.3 ± 0.5 in the drainage-only group (Fig. 1; Table 2).

Quality-of-life-related data

The results of neurological function were evaluated according to the ASIA classification and are listed in the Table 3. Ten patients in the irrigation group and 13 patients in the drainage-only group demonstrated an improvement in the postoperative ASIA classification. The rate of improvement in spinal cord function was 91.0% in the irrigation group and 81.3% in the drainage-only group at 2 weeks after surgery. VAS scores were 5.1 ± 0.8 preoperatively, 4.5 ± 1.0 at 7 days postoperatively, and 2.4 ± 0.6 at 14 days postoperatively in the irrigation group. In the drainage-only group, the VAS scores were 4.8 ± 0.8 preoperatively, 5.7 ± 0.7 at 7 days postoperatively, and 2.7 ± 0.6 at 14 days postoperatively (Fig. 2; Table 4). The HADS scale results revealed that patients in both groups experienced anxiety and depression (Table 4). The HADS-A score decreased from 11.0 ± 2.2 to 6.8 ± 1.8 in the irrigation group and from 10.1 ± 2.2 to 7.7 ± 1.4 in the drainage-only group. The HADS-D score decreased from 8.7 ± 2.3 to 6.0 ± 1.2 in the irrigation group and from 9.3 ± 2.0 to 6.1 ± 1.4 in the drainage-only group.

Complications

Venous thrombosis occurred in 13 patients (nine in the irrigation group and four in the drainage-only group) and were successfully treated with anticoagulant therapy during follow-up. Superficial wound infections occurred in six patients (two in the irrigation group and four in the drainage-only group) and were successfully treated with antibiotics. Water‒electrolyte imbalance occurred in 13 patients (six in the irrigation group and seven in the drainage-only group). Local abscesses were observed in four patients in the drainage-only group and were treated with minimally invasive CT-guided percutaneous catheter drainage. In the drainage-only group, five patients developed sinus formation at the incision site after surgery and were treated by excision of the sinus (Table 1).

Discussion

At the early stage of infection, patients with spinal tuberculosis often present with fewer clinical symptoms. However, most patients develop abnormal spinal alignment or severe spinal deformity due to bone destruction and may experience progressive neural deficits if not properly treated and managed. Unlike acute spinal cord injury caused by trauma, where the neural deficits result from sudden and severe compression, the chronic and mild compression from infectious necrotic tissue in spinal tuberculosis can often be relieved with surgical decompression, leading to recovery from paralysis4. Therefore, surgical intervention can result in more positive clinical outcomes, which is crucial for the quality of life of patients. With advancements in surgical techniques, posterior approach surgery—including three-column debridement, anterior column bone grafting, and alignment reconstruction—has achieved the treatment goals5. However, there are few reports on the use of postoperative continuous irrigation and drainage in the perioperative management of spinal TB compared with other types of bone and joint infections. We conducted a retrospective cohort study to evaluate the clinical effectiveness of postoperative continuous local irrigation and drainage in patients with spinal TB who underwent surgery. Compared with the drainage-only group, the inflammation-related index decreased more rapidly with the use of local irrigation. The ESR decreased from 65.7 ± 15.6 to 45.6 ± 8.3, and 64.2 ± 12.1 to 56.3 ± 6.5 in irrigation group and drainage-only group, respectively, during the first postoperative week. The VAS score also improved significantly in both the irrigation and drainage groups. Additionally, we observed that the postoperative frequency and duration of opioid use were lower with irrigation. Furthermore, the neurological condition improved at 2 weeks after surgery in the irrigation group, although no significant difference was detected between the two groups. We believe that continuous postoperative irrigation could further alleviate the inflammatory environment, potentially leading to additional clinical improvement. In summary, postoperative continuous irrigation has advantages in improving clinical outcomes and could be considered for the management of spinal TB.

We generally recommend that patients get out of bed as early as possible after the drainage tube is removed, as this supports early rehabilitation. However, we found that the average bed rest time in the irrigation group was longer than in the drainage-only group due to continuous irrigation. D-dimer levels increased more in the irrigation group than in the drainage-only group when we examined the coagulation indices one week postoperatively. Moreover, the incidence rate of venous thrombosis was significantly lower in the drainage-only group at two weeks. Additionally, although advanced age is a risk factor for abnormal coagulation and thrombosis, there was no insignificant difference between the two groups in this study. These results indicate that continuous irrigation, which leads to prolonged bed rest, could be associated with abnormal hemodynamics. In our clinical practice, we suggest that patients engage in early active or passive exercises and use preventive anticoagulant drugs if necessary to avoid deep venous thrombosis. Additionally, classical anticoagulant therapy should be administered if thrombosis is detected via ultrasonography13. Sinus formation is a common complication during postoperative tuberculosis follow-up, which often indicates tuberculosis recurrence or poor infection control. This is related to the patients regularly taking enough medication and the degree of debridement. In our study, we found five patients developed sinus formation at the incision site at follow-up in the drainage-only group, which has an insignificant difference compared to the irrigation group via the Chi-square test. Even though, we think the continuous irrigation may contribute to further draining tuberculosis foci and improve the inflammatory environment, which probably reduces the incidence of sinus formation.

In addition to age, sex, and complications influencing disease treatment, increasing evidence has shown that perioperative mental conditions strongly affect clinical outcomes14–16. Some studies have suggested that depression negatively impacts disease treatment16. Postoperative depression and anxiety influence postoperative pain, functional recovery, and quality-of-life scores. Additionally, postoperative anxiety is positively correlated with increased use of analgesics15. In the present study, preoperative HADS scores were not significantly different between the two groups. However, compared to the preoperative HADS score, postoperative HADS scores clearly decreased in both groups, with the irrigation group showing better mental health conditions than the drainage-only group. Notably, we found that the number of opioid applications was lower in the irrigation group during the first postoperative week, which may be related to the lower HADS score and is consistent with the postoperative results of Shay’s study17. Moreover, patients with worse ASIA grades had higher HADS scores. We also provided more education and communication to patients who underwent continuous irrigation and drainage, which may have contributed to reducing negative mental conditions postoperatively. In summary, depression and anxiety scores are important perioperative indices that can guide perioperative management.

Some limitations should be considered. First is the limited sample size of the included patients and the need to further evaluate medium-/long-term follow-up results. Secondly, it is difficult to completely control for confounding factors, due to the retrospective nature of the study. So, we ran the sample size power analysis which could estimate the probability of rejecting a false null hypothesis of these data with statistical differences in the current sample size. Additionally, the decision to place irrigation tubes is based on the surgeon’s experience and intraoperative findings, such as the surgeon thinking the debridement was unsatisfactory during operation, which is subjective, and further exploration of objective criteria is needed.

In conclusion, postoperative continuous irrigation combined with drainage is an effective perioperative management strategy that can significantly improve inflammatory and mental conditions compared with postoperative drainage alone, and result in better clinical outcomes in the early postoperative stage.

Methods

Patient characteristics

This study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki II and was approved by the medical ethics committee of Xiangya Hospital, Central South University (No. 201703358). Written informed consent was acquired from each of the patients to authorize publishing the information/image(s) in an online open access publication. This was a retrospective analysis of 88 patients with spinal tuberculosis who underwent surgical treatment at our spinal surgery center from January 2017 to January 2022. The cohort included 32 patients managed with postoperative continuous irrigation combined with drainage and 56 patients managed with postoperative drainage alone. Spinal TB was ultimately diagnosed via pathological examination and metagenomics. All patients underwent debridement via a posterior-only approach and fusion surgery.

Preoperative management

All the patients presented to the hospital with a clinical diagnosis of spinal TB, which included symptoms of TB toxicity (pain, low fever, night sweats, and weight loss), laboratory test results (ESR, CRP, and TB antibody), and imaging examination results (X-ray, CT, and MRI). Anti-TB treatment including HRZE should be administered for 2 to 4 weeks before surgery. The ESR and CRP levels decreased significantly, indicating the appropriate timing for surgery. The operation was performed immediately when a new neurological deficit or progressive paralysis was detected during chemotherapy.

Surgical procedure

General anesthesia, prone position, and a median incision were used to expose the posterior elements. Laminectomy, hemi-laminectomy, or posterior osteotomy was performed in the area of destruction after placing the pedicle screws. Three-column debridement via the posterior approach removed necrotic tissue, including dural abscesses, diseased discs, and dead bone. Detailly, All infected and necrotic disc and bony tissue were resected until fresh bleeding bone was reached. Paravertebral and epidural abscesses were drained, and debridement was meticulously performed until there was no pus or infectious debris. Extreme caution was maintained during debridement near the medulla spinalis, and curettes and rongeurs were used very gently. Bone grafting was performed using a titanium mesh filled with autografts or allografts, which was placed in the middle‒anterior column after complete decompression. All cases were performed the same surgical procedure with the same surgical team.

Irrigation and drainage

After the main surgical procedure, two 16 Fr silicone tubes were placed into the epidural space. A total of 300 mg of isoniazid was diluted in 3000 ml of 0.9% sterile sodium chloride solution and used for continuous local irrigation via a 16 Fr silicone tube, which the other tube was connected to a drainage pack. In the control group, only a standard drainage tube was used. The decision to place irrigation tubes was based on the surgeon’s experience and intraoperative findings, which the irrigation tube will be placed when the surgeon thinking the debridement was unsatisfied during operation. However, patients treated with single-stage surgical debridement and simultaneous fixation achieved satisfactory outcomes even without irrigation.

Postoperative management

We encouraged patients to gradually increase their level of activity, including practicing early ambulation in a brace once the drainage tube was removed. The irrigation tube was maintained for 3–7 days and was removed when the drainage fluid was clear and yellowish in color. The drainage tube was withdrawn when the volume of drainage flow fell below 20 ml every 24 h. The experimental indices (ESR and CRP) were measured at 3, 7, and 14 days postoperatively. According to our clinical practice, elevated inflammatory indicators 3–7 days after surgery suggest a risk of spreading or deep infection. Testing ESR and CRP on the 3rd and 7th day after surgery is an appropriate time point to reflect changes in inflammation levels. Additionally, studies showed that CRP levels peak at approximately 48 to 72 h after surgery18,19. And the 14th day after surgery is a time point before discharge. D-dimer levels were measured preoperatively and 7 days postoperatively. Body temperature was also documented to help evaluate the effectiveness of treatment. X-ray, CT, and MRI were used to assess fixation and debridement once both the irrigation and drainage tubes were removed postoperatively. Patients continued oral HREZ chemotherapy postoperatively. Pyrazinamide was discontinued at 6 months. Patients continued on 9- to 12-month regimens of HRE chemotherapy.

Depression and anxiety definition

Anxiety and depressive symptoms were assessed using the self-rated, validated, and reliable hospital anxiety and depression scale (HADS). The HADS consists of an anxiety subscale and a depression subscale, each with a score ranging from 0 (best) to 21 (worst)20. Patients were classified as depressed if they scored ≥ 8 points on the HADS depression subscale (HADS-D)21. Anxiety was defined as a score ≥ 8 points on the HADS anxiety subscale (HADS-A)21. The sensitivity and specificity of a cutoff of ≥ 8 points for both HADS subscales in identifying patients with depression or anxiety have been shown to be between 0.70 and 0.9021. The HADS scores were measured at admission and 1 week postoperatively.

Statistical analysis

Sample size power analysis was performed using PASS 2021 (NCSS Statistical Software) (Supplementary Table). The results were recorded and analyzed using SPSS software version 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). When the data meet the normal distribution, we apply the independent sample T test for significance analysis. When the data does not meet the normal distribution, we apply the Mann-Whitney U test. Chi-square test and continuity correction were used to compare the differences in complication rates between the two groups. p < 0.05 was considered a statistically significant difference.

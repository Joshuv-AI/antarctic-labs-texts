Does quantization affect models’ performance on long-context tasks?
Anmol Mekala, Anirudh Atmakuru {amekala, aatmakuru}@umass.edu, [1ex] Yixiao Song, Marzena Karpinska, Mohit Iyyer yixiaosong@umass.edumkarpinska@microsoft.commiyyer@umd.edu

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) now support context windows exceeding 128K tokens, but this comes with significant memory requirements and high inference latency. Quantization can mitigate these costs, but may degrade performance. In this work, we present the first systematic evaluation of quantized LLMs on tasks with long-inputs ( ≥ \geq 64K tokens) and long-form outputs. Our evaluation spans 9.7K test examples, five quantization methods (FP8, GPTQ-int8, AWQ-int4, GPTQ-int4, BNB-nf4), and five models (Llama-3.1 8B and 70B; Qwen-2.5 7B, 32B, and 72B). We find that, on average, 8-bit quantization preserves accuracy (~0.8% drop), whereas 4-bit methods lead to substantial losses, especially for tasks involving long-context inputs (drops of up to 59%). This degradation tends to worsen when the input is in a language other than English. Crucially, the effects of quantization depend heavily on the quantization method, model, and task. For instance, while Qwen-2.5 72B remains robust under BNB-nf4, Llama-3.1 70B experiences a 32% performance drop on the same task. These findings highlight the importance of a careful, task-specific evaluation before deploying quantized LLMs, particularly in long-context scenarios and with languages other than English.

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) serve a vast community of users through APIs and web services. However, their inference demands substantial resources, severely constraining scalability and increasing energy consumption
(
Samsi et al. 2023
;
Luccioni et al. 2024
;
Fernandez et al. 2025
)
. Post-training model quantization aims to solve this problem by approximating models at lower precision
(
Zhou et al. 2024
;
Zhu et al. 2024
)
. 8-bit quantization
(
Micikevicius et al. 2022
;
Frantar et al. 2023
)
provides accuracy at half the cost, and more aggressive 4-bit approaches
(

;

;

)
offer even greater cost-effectiveness. Model providers widely offer quantized models
(
Meta AI 2024
;
Google Cloud 2024
;
Databricks 2023
)
, but the impact on performance has not been exhaustively explored.

We define long-form inputs as over 64K tokens, and long-form outputs as typically 250-650 tokens long.

This is particularly salient because as context length increases, the associated memory and compute requirements grow quadratically, making low-precision methods particularly attractive for efficiency.
Moreover, many modern LLMs now support context windows of 128K tokens or more, allowing them to process entire codebases, legal documents, or full novels. Such models, when aggressively quantized (e.g., to 4-bits), could theoretically handle significantly more context under the same memory budget.

However, to the best of our knowledge, only
Kurtic et al. 2024

Ruler

(
Hsieh et al. 2024
)
long-context task, but their coarse result aggregation across tasks and context lengths may obscure the effects of individual factors. Thus, it remains unclear whether quantized LLMs can maintain accuracy on very long inputs or generate high-quality long-form outputs.

To address this gap, we conduct a systematic analysis of quantization performance across multiple models, quantization methods, and benchmark tasks. This includes five datasets across 9.7K test examples, with key factors varying such as context length, language, and output constraints.
We evaluate five quantization methods (FP8, GPTQ-int8, AWQ-int4, GPTQ-int4, and BNB-NF4) on five models (Llama-3.1 8B and 70B; Qwen-2.5 7B, 32B, and 72B), comparing against their BF16 baselines. For long-context input tasks, we use

Related works

Prior studies on quantization effects:
Recent works show that 8
-
bit quantization preserves performance, while 4
-
bit methods incur only marginal accuracy loss
(
Kurtic et al. 2024
;
Badshah and Sajjad 2024
;
Liu et al. 2024b
;
Jin et al. 2024
;
Huang et al. 2024
;
Xu et al. 2024
;

;
del Rey et al. 2025
;
Zheng et al. 2025
)
.
Lee et al. 2024
and
Kurtic et al. 2024
investigate Llama-3.1 and find that FP8 is nearly lossless, while AWQ-int4 and GPTQ-int4 exhibit small degradations, particularly increasing on reasoning, coding tasks, and Arena-Hard. Results on Mistral
(
Badshah and Sajjad 2024
)
, Baichuan
(
Liu et al. 2024b
)
, and Qwen-Chat
(
Jin et al. 2024
)
are also similar, with losses rarely exceeding 3% and diminishing as model size grows. Results on safety, alignment and interpretability suggest that 4-bit quantization may
(
Egashira et al. 2024
)
or may not
(
Xu et al. 2024
)
lead to drops in safety, protect against data-poisoning
(
Hussain et al. 2025
)
, reverse unlearning
(
Zhang et al. 2025
)
, and have mixed results on interpretability
(
Wang et al. 2025b
)
. Some studies have also shown that models are robust to quantization even at 3-bit precision or lower
(
Chee et al. 2023
;
Ma et al. 2024
;
Egiazarian et al. 2024
;
Kumar et al. 2025
)
. However, these evaluations are generally limited in terms of the input context or the output token ranges, leaving longer-range effects unknown. A significant body of work has studied accuracy trade-offs using different quantization methods on academic benchmarks
(
Yao et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2024a
;
Gong et al. 2024a
;
Gong et al. 2024b
)
. However, these benchmarks may not fully reflect performance in realistic scenarios, such as those involving long-form reasoning or multilingual settings. For instance,
Li et al. 2025
and
Liu et al. 2025
report significant drops for GPTQ-int4 and AWQ-int4 on such tasks. For multilingual LLMs,
Marchisio et al. 2024
observe increasing drops on reasoning for non-Latin script languages upon quantization. We develop this picture further by evaluating long-form generation (
CS4
,
FActScore
) and across languages in

.

-
context tasks, and they report coarse averages over
Ruler
(which includes context lengths as low as 8K) to conclude negligible performance loss. Our analysis differs by focusing on effects of parameters like context length, task-type especially at context lengths >64K. While they evaluate only the Llama-3.1 family and use a single 4-bit quantization (GPTQ-int4), we extend the scale of long context analysis by including
OneRuler
and
NoCha
, the Qwen-2.5 model family, and the AWQ-int4 and BNB-nf4 quantizations. We find that Qwen-2.5 and Llama-3.1 respond to quantization very differently, with Llama-3.1 showing larger drops upon quantization (
6(a)
). In addition, the AWQ-int4 quantization performs better than GPTQ-int4, while the popular BNB-nf4 quantization performs worse (
Figure 3
).

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a systematic evaluation of the performance of five open-weights models (7B to 72B parameters) across five quantization methods (4-bit and 8-bit). We conducted experiments on five tasks requiring the models to either process long-context inputs (in English or other languages) or return long-form outputs in English. While prior work often suggests 8-bit quantization is nearly lossless and 4-bit methods are
good enough
, our findings demonstrate that the impact of quantization becomes more pronounced in long-context scenarios. In particular, we observed that model’s performance under 4-bit quantization tends to degrade progressively as input context length increases. 4-bit quantization has significant impact when the input is in a language other than English. In contrast, 8-bit precision methods largely preserve accuracy across context lengths. Furthermore, we observed considerable heterogeneity in quantization effects—the performance impact of reduced precision varies significantly across different model architectures and task types. Taken together, these results indicate that quantization should be applied with careful consideration in long-context applications and in multilingual setup.

Limitations

While we conduct a large-scale evaluation of quantized LLMs on long-context and long-form tasks, we identify the following limitations and hope future work will improve on these aspects to enable more comprehensive evaluations of quantization methods.

Scope of experiments:

Our evaluations cover five quantization methods and five LLMs, with the largest model having 72 billion parameters. We largely followed publicly available, off-the-shelf quantized models and recipes (e.g., from the official Qwen repository, HuggingQuants, and NeuralMagic) and did not explore tuning each quantization method further. As a result, our findings might not fully generalize to other models or to quantization techniques beyond those tested.

Evaluation methodology and limited output lengths:

Although we tested input contexts as long as 128K tokens, we restricted the generated outputs in our experiments to relatively short passages (< 650 tokens). We made this choice because our evaluation relies solely on automated metrics and LLM-based judge models, which are not reliable for evaluating very long text. Consequently, without human annotations, our assessment may overlook subtle errors in logical flow or nuanced instruction-following behavior over long-form outputs.

Ethical Considerations

We rely solely on publicly available datasets that have been vetted and validated in prior peer-reviewed studies. We do not introduce any new data collection or human subject research procedures. Our paper poses no ethical concerns.

Appendix B
Benchmarks: more details

In this section, we present more details about each benchmark task, adding to the description in §

Ruler
:

While
Kurtic et al. 2024
evaluate quantized LLMs on
Ruler
and report coarse-grained, aggregated results across quantized models and context lengths, we perform a more detailed analysis. We follow
Yen et al. 2025
to select our datapoints for the multi-key needle, multi-key UUID, and multi-value tasks from the
Ruler
benchmark. In the multi-key task, the model must retrieve the value associated with a specific key given in the query, while ignoring others which serve as distractors. The multi-key UUID task tests a similar ability, but the values are 32-character long-form strings (UUIDs), making it harder to match or guess, testing the model’s ability to precisely extract the correct value without partial matching. Finally, the multi-value task increases the difficulty further by requiring the models to retrieve multiple values associated with the same key in the haystack. While the tokenizers for Llama-3.1 and Qwen-2.5 differ,
Yen et al. 2025
use Llama-3.1 tokenizer to tokenize and construct their benchmark. This implies that Llama-3.1 and Qwen-2.5 receive slightly different numbers of input tokens for the same input datapoint.

OneRuler
:

This benchmark extends
Ruler
in two significant ways. First,
OneRuler
’s NIAH task includes points without any correct needle present, requiring models to abstain appropriately. Second,
OneRuler
expands the task beyond English to encompass 26 languages representing diverse language families and writing systems. They find that accuracy on NIAH shows large drops on low-resource languages, especially ones that use non-Latin scripts. We evaluate models using two NIAH task types: NIAH-single and NIAH-none. In NIAH-single, a single correct needle is present without distractors, which models must retrieve accurately. In NIAH-none, no correct needle is present, but four distractor needles exist, requiring models to abstain from retrieving any needle.

NoCha

NoCha

Since Llama-3.1 and Qwen-2.5 support context lengths of up to 128K tokens, this work considers the subset of datapoints with contexts up to that length, yielding 723 claim pairs drawn from 46 novels and three short story collections. While
Ruler
and
OneRuler

NoCha

Karpinska et al. 2024

true
versus
false

Fumiko Kaneko (1903-1926) was a Japanese anarchist and nihilist who gained notoriety for her involvement in a plot to assassinate the Japanese Emperor Hirohito and other high-ranking government officials.

Born in 1903 in Aomori Prefecture, Japan, Kaneko grew up in a poor farming family. Her early life was marked by hardship and struggle, which may have contributed to her radicalization. In her late teens, she moved to Tokyo, where she became involved in the city’s anarchist and nihilist circles.

Quantization methods were included as the primary fixed effect of interest, with additional fixed effects for context length, task, and their interactions in different model variants.

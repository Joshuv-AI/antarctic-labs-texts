Global positroid varieties
Evgeny Feigin

Abstract
. Positroid subvarieties of complex Grassmannians are the images of the Richardson subvarieties of the full flag varieties under the natural projection map. Positroid varieties admit natural embedding into certain quiver Grassmannians for equioriented cyclic quivers. Varying representations of the quiver, one defines global positroid varieties inside the type A global affine Grassmannian. General fiber of a family is isomorphic to the corresponding classical positroid variety and the special fiber is a subvariety of the juggling variety. We show that the global positroid families are flat and describe their (conjecturally reduced) scheme structures. We also describe the special fibers inside the product of classical positroid varieties and realize the irreducible components of the special fibers in terms of the affine Richardson varieties.

{\bf J}

\{J_{b},\ b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\}

of cardinality
k

\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
j-1\in J_{b+1}

,

In particular, each

]
inside the global affine Grassmannian

The special fiber of the family is a union of Schubert varieties inside affine flag variety

[

,
Zhu14
]
. The simplest way to describe the family is as follows: for each
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}

let us fix an

-dimensional vector space
W^{(b)}
; we
fix bases
w^{(b)}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
of the spaces
W^{(b)}
. The family
\underline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{k,n}

consists of collections of the form
(\varepsilon,U_{0},\dots,U_{n-1})
such that
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
,

U_{b}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(W^{(b)})
and
M(\varepsilon)U_{b}\subset U_{b+1}
, where

is a linear map
sending

to
w^{(b+1)}_{i-1}
for

and sending
w^{(b)}_{1}
to
\varepsilon w^{(b+1)}_{n}

The map
\underline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{k,n}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}

is the projection to the first coordinate; the preimage
\pi^{-1}(0)
is the juggling variety
and all other fibers are isomorphic to
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
.
We note that the family above is a special case of the global Schubert varieties inside affine Grassmannians

[
AB24
,
F25
,
FLP23b
,
HY24
]
(one also finds similar generalizations
on the totally non-negative side
[
LaPy12
,
LaPy13
,
RW24
]
).

The explicit description above is closely related to the positroid varieties
\Pi_{\bf J}
.
Namely, for any non-zero
\varepsilon
,
\Pi_{\bf J}
is isomorphic to the intersection of
\pi^{-1}(\varepsilon)

with the product of Schubert varieties
X^{-}_{J_{b}}
in
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(W^{(b)})
corresponding to the

k
-tuples
J_{b}
. Here is the main definition of our paper: the global positroid variety
inside the global Grassmannian is defined as

\underline{\Pi}_{\bf J}=\underline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{k,n}\bigcap\left({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\times\prod_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}X^{-}_{J_{b}}\right).

Slightly abusing notations, we denote by the same letter
\pi
the projection from the global
positroid variety to the first factor.
Our goal is to study the whole family
\underline{\Pi}_{\bf J}
as well as the special fiber

\Pi_{\bf J}(0)\subset\underline{\Pi}_{\bf J}
.
Here is our first theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any juggling pattern
{\bf J}
the global positroid family
\underline{\Pi}_{\bf J}
is flat.
The closure of
\pi^{-1}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus\{0\})
is equal to the whole family.

In order to describe
\Pi_{\bf J}(0)
we recall that the special fiber of the global Grassmannian
is isomorphic to a union of
\binom{n}{k}
Schubert varieties
Y_{\sigma}
,
\sigma\in T_{k,n}

inside affine flag variety
{\mathcal{F}l}
for the affine group
{GL}_{n}
.
Here
T_{k,n}
is a cardinality
\binom{n}{k}
subset of the extended affine Weyl group
W^{ext}
.
The torus fixed points inside this union
are labeled by the juggling patterns, which are in bijection with the set of the
extended affine Weyl
group elements which are smaller than or equal to one of the elements
\sigma\in T_{k,n}
.
For a juggling pattern
{\bf J}
we denote the corresponding affine Weyl group element by
w({\bf J})
.

Let
R_{u}^{w}\subset{\mathcal{F}l}
be the Richardson varieties inside the affine flag variety,
u,w\in W^{ext}
,

u\leq w

[
KL80
,
Rich92
]
. Each Richardson variety is an intersection of the opposite Schubert
varieties corresponding to the elements
u
and
w
. Here is our second theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

For a juggling pattern
{\bf J}
the special positroid variety
\Pi_{\bf J}(0)
is (in general) reducible
and equi-dimensional with
\dim\Pi_{\bf J}(0)=\dim\Pi_{\bf J}
. Each irreducible component is
isomorphic to a Richardson variety
R_{w({\bf J})}^{\sigma}
for
\sigma\in T_{k,n}
,

\sigma\geq w({\bf J})
.

We also provide a defining ideal for
\Pi_{\bf J}(0)
and conjecture that the ideal is radical.
We prove the conjecture for
k=1
in the Appendix using certain generalized rectangular
semi-standard tableaux (unavailable so far for
k>1
).
More precisely,
\Pi_{\bf J}(0)
is realized as a subvariety in the product of projective spaces
and hence the defining ideal and the multi-homogeneous coordinate ring are multi-graded
(the Plücker variables
\Delta^{(a)}_{I}
come equipped with colors
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
).
Hence it is natural
to ask for a generalized (colored) version of the semi-standard tableaux (see
[
AGH23
,
Lam19
,
EH24
]

for similar results in the non-colored case).

Finally, we express the special positroid varieties in terms of the classical ones as follows. Let

\mathrm{rot}
be an automorphism of the set of the juggling patterns which rotates the entries:

\mathrm{rot}(J_{0},\dots,J_{n-1})=(J_{1},J_{2},\dots,J_{n-1},J_{0})
. We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

For a juggling pattern
{\bf J}
the special positroid variety
\Pi_{\bf J}(0)
is given by

\Pi_{\bf J}(0)=\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(0)\bigcap\left(\prod_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}\Pi_{\mathrm{rot}^{b}{\bf J}}\right).

In particular, each classical positroid variety
\Pi_{\bf J}
is equal to the image of an affine
Richardson variety of the form
R_{w}^{\sigma}
for some
\sigma\in T_{k,n}
.

We close the paper with the appendix, where the
k=1
case is worked out in full detail.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we collect preliminaries on
Schubert varieties, quiver Grassmannians and affine flag varieties. In Section
3

we introduce the global positroid varieties and study their properties. Section
4

is devoted to the study of the special fiber of the family. In Appendix we give all the details
for the
k=1
case.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Grassmannians, flags, Schubert varieties

Let us fix two positive integers
k
and
n
,
1\leq k<n
; we set

[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
. Let
W
be an
n
-dimensional
complex vector space with a basis
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
. Let
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
be the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
W
. For an element
\sigma
from the Weyl group
S_{n}
we denote by
p_{\sigma}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}

the subspace spanned by the basis elements
w_{\sigma(i)}
,
1\leq i\leq k
.
The Grassmann varieties are acted upon by the group
GL_{n}
. The points
p_{\sigma}

are exactly the fixed points for the torus
T\subset SL_{n}
of diagonal matrices.

One has the Plücker embedding
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}\to{\mathbb{P}}(\Lambda^{k}W)
;
the image of this embedding is defined by the Plücker ideal
generated by certain quadratic expressions in Plücker variables
\Delta_{I}
,
I\in\binom{[n]}{k}
(dual to the wedge products of the basis vectors
w_{i}
).
The expressions are labeled by collections
I,J\in\binom{[n]}{k}
and a number
r=1,\dots,k
.
The corresponding generators is explicitly given by

g_{I,J}=\Delta_{I}\Delta_{J}-\sum\Delta_{I^{\prime}}\Delta_{J^{\prime}},

where the pairs
{I^{\prime}},{J^{\prime}}
in the right hand side are obtained from
I,J
by interchanging the
element
j_{r}
in
J
with all elements of
I
(keeping in mind that

\Delta_{\tau I}=(-1)^{\tau}\Delta_{I}
for
\tau\in S_{k}
).

Let
B,B^{-}\subset SL_{n}
be the Borel subgroups of upper- and lower-triangular matrices
(in particular, both
B
and
B^{-}
contain
T
).
For a Weyl group element
\sigma\in S_{n}
let

\mathring{X}_{\sigma}=B.p_{\sigma}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
and
\mathring{X}^{-}_{\sigma}=B^{-}.p_{\sigma}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
be the open Schubert varieties – the orbits
of the Borel subgroup
B
and the opposite Borel subgroup
B^{-}
.
The closed Schubert varieties are the closures of the corresponding orbits:

X_{\sigma}=\overline{\mathring{X}_{\sigma}}
,

X^{-}_{\sigma}=\overline{\mathring{X}^{-}_{\sigma}}
.
In particular, for the longest element
w_{0}\in S_{n}
, one has

X_{w_{0}}=X^{-}_{e}=\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
and
X_{e}=p_{e}
,
X^{-}_{w_{0}}=p_{w_{0}}
are just points.

Schubert varieties
\mathring{X}_{\sigma}
,
X_{\sigma}
,
\mathring{X}^{-}_{\sigma}
,
X^{-}_{\sigma}

inside
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
depend only on the image
\sigma[k]
, so we sometimes denote

\mathring{X}_{\sigma}
by
\mathring{X}_{\sigma[k]}
(and similarly for other Schubert varieties).
For two cardinality
k
subsets
I,J\subset[n]
,
I=(i_{1}<\dots<i_{k})
,
J=(j_{1}<\dots<j_{k})

we write
I\leq J
if
i_{u}\leq j_{u}
for all
u\in[k]
. We note that
\Delta_{I}
vanishes on

X_{\sigma}
unless
I\leq J
. Similarly,
\Delta_{I}
vanishes on
X^{-}_{\sigma}
unless
I\geq J
.

Let
\mathrm{Fl}_{n}=SL_{n}/B
be the variety of complete flags consisting of
collections
(V_{i})_{i=1}^{n-1}
of subspaces of the space
W
such that
\dim V_{i}=i

and
V_{i}\subset V_{i+1}
. In particular, one has an
obvious embedding
\mathrm{Fl}_{n}\subset\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
.
Slightly abusing notation, we denote by the same symbol
p_{\sigma}\in\mathrm{Fl}_{n}
the product of the corresponding points in the Grassmannians. The open Schubert varieties and
their opposite analogues in
the flag varieties are defined (as in the Grassmann case) by

\mathring{Y}_{\sigma}=B.p_{\sigma}\subset\mathrm{Fl}_{n},\quad\mathring{Y}^{-}_{\sigma}=B^{-}.p_{\sigma}\subset\mathrm{Fl}_{n};

the closed Schubert varieties are the orbit closures.

Finally, let us define the Richardson varieties inside
\mathrm{Fl}_{n}
. Given two Weyl group elements

\sigma,\tau\in S_{n}
the open Richardson variety
\mathring{R}_{\sigma}^{\tau}\subset\mathrm{Fl}_{n}
is defined
as the intersection
\mathring{X}_{\sigma}\cap\mathring{X}^{-}_{\tau}
.
One defines the closed Richardson varieties
R_{\sigma}^{\tau}
as the closure of

\mathring{R}_{\sigma}^{\tau}
.
Clearly,
R_{\sigma}^{\tau}=\emptyset
unless
\sigma
is smaller than or equal to
\tau
in the Bruhat
order in
S_{n}
(if
\sigma=\tau
, then
R_{\sigma}^{\tau}
is a single point
p_{\sigma}
).

Remark 2.1
.

The definition of the Schubert varieties, opposite Schubert varieties and Richardson varieties
is one and the same in all Lie types and for all partial flag varieties. However, in this
paper we only need type
A
Grassmannians and type
A
complete flag varieties.

2.2.
Positroid varieties

Positroid varieties inside the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
are defined as images of the Richardson
varieties in the full flag variety
\mathrm{Fl}_{n}
with respect to the natural surjection

\mathrm{Fl}_{n}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}

[
KLS13
]
.
In order to describe the positroid stratification of the Grassmannians
explicitly, one uses the formalism of juggling patterns and Schubert subvarieties of the Grassmann varieties.

Recall that a juggling pattern is a collection
{\bf J}=(J_{0},\dots,J_{n-1})
with each
J_{b}

being a cardinality
k
subset of
[n]
subject
to the conditions
J_{b}-1\subset J_{b+1}
. Here
J_{b}-1=\{j-1:\ j\in J_{b},j>1\}
and the lower index

b+1
is taken modulo
n
(i.e. in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
). The strata of
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}

of the positroid stratification are labeled by the juggling patterns, i.e.

\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}=\bigsqcup\mathring{\Pi}_{\bf J}
. The closed positroid varieties
\Pi_{\bf J}
are defined as
closures of
\mathring{\Pi}_{\bf J}
. The positroid varieties
\mathring{\Pi}_{\bf J}

admit the following explicit description:

(2.1)

\mathring{\Pi}_{\bf J}=\bigcap_{b=0}^{n-1}\varphi^{-b}\left(\mathring{X}^{-}_{J_{b}}\right),\ \varphi:W\to W,\ \varphi(w_{i})=\begin{cases}w_{i-1},&i>1,\\
w_{n},&i=1\end{cases}

and similarly for the closed positroid varieties
\Pi_{\bf J}
(with
\mathring{X}^{-}_{J_{b}}
replaced
with
X^{-}_{J_{b}}
).

Example 2.2
.

Let us consider the juggling pattern
{\bf J}
such that
J_{b}=\{1,\dots,k\}
for
all
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
. Then
\Pi_{{\bf J}}=\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
and
\mathring{\Pi}_{{\bf J}}
is the subset
consisting of subspaces
U
such that
\Delta_{I}(U)\neq 0
for all
I
of the form

I=\{i,i+1,\dots,i+k-1\}
,
i\in[n]
(the indices
i+j
are understood as
i+j-n

whenever
i+j>n
).

Example 2.3
.

Let us fix a subset
S\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
,
|S|=k
. We define the juggling
pattern
{\bf J}(S)=\{J_{b}(S)\}_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}
by the following property:
n\in J_{s}(S)
for
any
s\in S
(this property determines
{\bf J}(S)
uniquely thanks to the juggling pattern condition
J_{b}-1\subset J_{b}
and
|J_{b}|=k
for all
b
). Then
\Pi_{{\bf J}(S)}
is a single point, whose
b
-th component
is the span of vectors of the form
w_{j}
,
j\in J_{b}(S)
. These are all the zero-dimensional positroid varieties.

2.3.
Quiver Grassmannians

A quiver Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{\bf d}(M)

[
CI20
]
is defined by a quiver
Q
, its representation
M
and a
dimension vector
{\bf d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{Q_{0}}
, where
Q_{0}
is the set of vertices of
Q
.
The points of
\mathrm{Gr}_{\bf d}(M)
are the
{\bf d}
-dimensional
Q
-subrepresentations of
M
. We will only work with the cyclic equioriented quiver and a very special class of representations, so we skip the general formalism and define the concrete objects we are working with.

Let
Q
be the equioriented cyclic quiver with
n
vertices labeled by the elements of

{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
. We define
a family of
Q
modules
M(\varepsilon)
, depending on a parameter
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{C}}
. For each vertex

b\in Q_{0}
(and for all values of the parameter
\varepsilon
) the space
M^{(b)}(\varepsilon)
coincides
with
W
. The maps
M_{b\to b+1}(\varepsilon)
are given as follows:

(2.2)

M_{b\to b+1}(\varepsilon):w_{i}\mapsto\begin{cases}w_{i-1},&i>1\\
\varepsilon w_{n},&i=1\end{cases}

In particular,
M_{b\to b+1}(1)=\varphi
(see (
2.1
)).
In what follows in order to avoid confusions we denote the vector
w_{i}
inside

W\simeq M^{(b)}(\varepsilon)
by
w_{i}^{(b)}
.
The representations
M(\varepsilon)
are all isomorphic for
\varepsilon\neq 0
, they decompose as the direct sum
of
n
copies of a representation of dimension
(1,\dots,1)
with all maps being identities:

M(\varepsilon)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{span}\{w^{(b)}_{j-b},\ b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\}

(here
j-b
is understood as
j-b+n
for
j-b\leq 0
).
If
\varepsilon=0
, then the same formula provides a decomposition of
M(0)
into the direct sum of

n
pairwise non-isomorphic nilpotent indecomposable representations of dimension
(1,\dots,1)
.

Remark 2.4
.

We note that all the maps in (
2.2
) are given by the same formula; in particular, for

\varepsilon=0
the kernels of all the maps are spanned by
w^{(b)}_{1}
. For
\varepsilon\neq 0
the maps
(
2.2
) are isomorphisms, but the identification of the spaces at different vertices
shifts the lower indices of the basis vectors
w^{(b)}_{\bullet}
.
This disadvantage can be fixed by shifting
the numbering of the vectors
w^{(b)}_{i}
; the shift has to depend on a vertex
b
.
The price to pay is that
after such shifts the maps
M_{b\to b+1}(\varepsilon)
will depend on
b
. The isomorphism between the two
pictures is a lift of the combinatorial identification of juggling patterns and Grassmann necklaces
(see
[
Pos06
,
Sp23
]
).

Now let us fix a dimension vector
{\bf d}=(k,\dots,k)
and denote by
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(\varepsilon)
the quiver
Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{(k,\dots,k)}(M(\varepsilon))
.
In particular, for
\varepsilon\neq 0
one has an isomorphism

\mathrm{Gr}_{(k,\dots,k)}(M(\varepsilon))\simeq\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
and

\mathrm{Gr}_{(k,\dots,k)}(M(0))
is the so-called juggling variety, which shows up in various context, see
[
Go01
,
Go03
,
Pap18
,
FLP23a
]
.
We also denote by
\underline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{k,n}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\times\prod_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
the global
(universal) quiver Grassmannian consisting of collections

(\varepsilon,U_{0},\dots,U_{n-1})
such that
(U_{b})_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(\varepsilon)
.
We summarize the picture in the following lemma
[
FLP22
,
Go01
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

The family
\underline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{k,n}
is flat over
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
.
For
\varepsilon\neq 0
each fiber
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(\varepsilon)
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
. The special fiber

\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(0)
is the juggling variety;
it is equidimensional of dimension
k(n-k)
and has
\binom{n}{k}
irreducible components.

2.4.
Global affine Grassmannians

The variety
\underline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{k,n}
can be seen as a global Schubert variety
inside the global affine Grassmannian
[
AB24
,
Zhu14
]
.
The global affine Grassmannians are defined in all types, but we will only need the type

A
case, where one has the lattice realization
[
Kac85
,
Kum02
]
. Let us recall the formalism.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}((t))
be the field of Laurent series in a variable
t
and let
{\mathbb{O}}={\mathbb{C}}[[t]]

be the ring of formal power series.
We fix an
n
-dimensional vector space
V
with a basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
.
The
GL_{n}
type affine Grassmannian
{\mathcal{G}r}
consists of lattices in the space
V({\mathbb{K}})=V\otimes{\mathbb{K}}
, i.e.
free
{\mathbb{O}}
modules of rank
n
and is identified with the quotient
GL_{n}({\mathbb{K}})/GL_{n}({\mathbb{O}})
.
A valuation
\nu(L)
of a lattice
L
is the minimal power of
t
showing up in the determinant
of the matrix with columns
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
, where
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an
{\mathbb{O}}
-basis
of
L
. A chain of lattices is a sequence of lattices

L_{0}\subset L_{1}\subset\dots\subset L_{n-1}\subset L_{n}=t^{-1}L_{0},\quad\dim L_{i+1}/L_{i}=1.

In particular,
\nu(L_{i+1})=\nu(L_{i})-1
. The set of lattice chains is the affine flag variety
of type
GL_{n}
. It is identified with the quotient
GL_{n}({\mathbb{K}})/{\bf I}
, where
{\bf I}\subset GL_{n}({\mathbb{O}})

is the Iwahori group – the preimage of the (upper-triangular) Borel subgroup
B\subset GL_{n}

with respect to the
t=0
evaluation map
GL_{n}({\mathbb{O}})\to GL_{n}
.

The global affine Grassmannian is a family over
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
such the special fiber over
0
is
isomorphic to the affine flag variety
{\mathcal{F}l}
and all other fibers are affine Grassmannians
{\mathcal{G}r}
.
This infinite-dimensional family admits finite-dimensional subfamilies, whose general fibers are
(spherical) Schubert varieties. In this paper we only treat a very particular case of classical
Grassmannians, corresponding to minuscule weights. We formulate the construction
in this particular case.

For
b=0,\dots,n-1
let
\theta_{b}:V({\mathbb{K}})\to V({\mathbb{K}})
be the following
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear
maps

\theta_{b}(v_{i})=\begin{cases}(t+\varepsilon)v_{i},&i=b+1,\\
v_{i},&i\neq b+1\end{cases}

(note that the composition of all the maps
\theta_{b}
is equal to
(t+\varepsilon)\mathrm{Id}
).
The global affine Grassmannian is formed by collections
(\varepsilon,L_{0},\dots,L_{n-1})
, where
\varepsilon
is a complex numbers,
L_{b}
are lattices of equal valuation (
\nu(L_{0})=\dots=\nu(L_{n-1})
) and
\theta_{b}L_{b}\subset L_{b+1}
for all
b
(including
b=n-1
with
L_{n}=L_{0}
).
A general
\varepsilon\neq 0
fiber is isomorphic to the affine Grassmannian, since

t+\varepsilon
is invertible in
{\mathbb{K}}
. The special
\varepsilon=0
fiber is isomorphic to the affine
flag variety, the identification sends
(L_{0},\dots,L_{n-1})
to

L_{0}\subset(\theta_{0})^{-1}_{\varepsilon=0}L_{1}\subset(\theta_{0}\theta_{1})^{-1}_{\varepsilon=0}L_{1}\subset\dots\subset t^{-1}L_{0}.

We consider a subvariety
\underline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{k,n}
in the global affine Grassmannian (to be identified with the family from the previous section)
cut out by the following conditions:

V({\mathbb{O}})\subset L_{b}\subset t^{-1}V({\mathbb{O}}),\ \nu(L_{b})=-k.

Clearly,
\underline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{k,n}
sits inside
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\times\prod_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
(with the ambient space being

Since all lattices

contain

after replacing lattices with the corresponding points

read as
\psi_{b}\bar{L}_{b}\subset\bar{L}_{b+1}
, where

\psi_{b}(v_{i})=\begin{cases}v_{i},&i\neq b+1,\\
0,&i=b+1.\end{cases}.

Now one passes from a collection
(\bar{L}_{b})_{b}
to the collection
(U_{b})_{b}

by a simple base change interwining the maps
\psi_{b}
and
M_{b\to b+1}(0)
(

)
(see Remark

). Namely, at vertex

one sends
v_{i}
to
w_{i-b}

(the non-positive indices to be increased by
n
).

The affine flag variety
{\mathcal{F}l}
of type
GL_{n}

[

]
admits a decomposition into the
Schubert cells
\mathring{Z}_{\tau}
,
where
\tau
is an element of the extended affine Weyl group
W^{ext}
–
the semi-direct product of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

and the symmetric group
S_{n}
(see
[
BB05
]
). For

and
\sigma\in S_{n}
we denote the corresponding

by
\sigma z^{\lambda}

Each cell
\mathring{Z}_{\tau}
is an orbit of the Iwahori subgroup

{\bf I}
through the torus fixed point
p_{\tau}\in{\mathcal{F}l}

Z_{\tau}
are the
closures of the Schubert cells.

The juggling variety
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(0)
inside the affine flag variety
{\mathcal{F}l}
is equal
to the union of affine Schubert varieties
[
Go01

HL15
]

in particular, there are exactly

irreducible components, each of dimension

into the union of open Schubert cells
\mathring{Z}_{\tau}

variety are labeled by the

-juggling patterns.

3.1.

Let

Proof.

The claim follows from (

∎

4.
The special fiber

Let
\mathrm{rot}:\mathrm{Jugg}_{k,n}\to\mathrm{Jugg}_{k,n}
be the rotation automorphism of the
set of juggling patterns:

\mathrm{rot}(J_{0},\dots,J_{n-1})=(J_{1},J_{2},\dots,J_{n-1},J_{0}).

Formula (
2.1
) implies that the isomorphism
\varphi:W\to W

(a.k.a.
M_{\bullet}(1)
) induces the isomorphism of positroid varieties
\Pi_{\bf J}\to\Pi_{\mathrm{rot}({\bf J})}
.
Let
\mathrm{pr}_{b}
be the natural projection map from the juggling variety

\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(0)
to the
b
-th Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The degenerate positroid variety
\Pi_{\bf J}(0)
admits the following presentation in terms of the
classical positroid varieties:

\Pi_{\bf J}(0)=\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(0)\bigcap\prod_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}\Pi_{\mathrm{rot}^{b}({\bf J})}.

Proof.

Let
{\bf U}=(U_{0},\dots,U_{n-1})
be a point of
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(0)
such that

U_{b}\in\Pi_{\mathrm{rot}^{b}({\bf J})}
. Then by definition
U_{b}\in X^{-}_{J_{b}}
(i.e.
\Delta^{(b)}_{I}(U_{b})=0
unless

I\geq J_{b}
) and hence
{\bf U}\in\Pi_{\bf J}(0)
. So

\Pi_{\bf J}(0)\supset\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(0)\bigcap\prod_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}\Pi_{\mathrm{rot}^{b}({\bf J})}
.
Let us prove the reverse inclusion.

We are to show that
\mathrm{pr}_{b}\left(\Pi_{\bf J}(0)\right)\subset\Pi_{\mathrm{rot}^{b}({\bf J})}
.
Recall the map
\varphi:W\to W
(
2.1
). Our goal is to show that for a point

{\bf U}=(U_{0},\dots,U_{n-1})\in\Pi_{\bf J}(0)
one has
\varphi^{a}(U_{b})\in X^{-}_{J_{a+b}}

for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
.
Let
M_{b\to a+b}(\varepsilon)
denote the composition
M_{a+b-1\to a+b}(\varepsilon)\dots M_{b\to b+1}(\varepsilon)
.
Since
{\bf U}\in\Pi_{\bf J}(0)
, we know that

M_{b\to a+b}(0)U_{b}\subset U_{a+b}\subset X^{-}_{J_{a+b}},\ M_{a+b\to b}(0)U_{a+b}\subset U_{b}\subset X^{-}_{J_{b}}.

We will deduce from these conditions that
\varphi^{a}(U_{b})\in X^{-}_{J_{a+b}}
.

Recall the
n
-dimensional ambient spaces
W^{(b)}
and
W^{(a+b)}
(both isomorphic to
the space
W
). We decompose

W^{(b)}
as
W^{(b)}_{<}\oplus W^{(b)}_{>}
and
W^{(a+b)}
as
W^{(a+b)}_{<}\oplus W^{(a+b)}_{>}

with

\displaystyle W^{(b)}_{<}=\mathrm{span}(w_{1}^{(b)},\dots,w^{(b)}_{a}),\ W^{(b)}_{>}=\mathrm{span}(w^{(b)}_{a+1},\dots,w^{(b)}_{n}),

\displaystyle W^{(a+b)}_{<}=\mathrm{span}(w_{1}^{(a+b)},\dots,w_{n-a}^{(a+b)}),\ W^{(b)}_{>}=\mathrm{span}(w_{n-a+1}^{(a+b)},\dots,w_{n}^{(a+b)}).

The main properties of this decomposition we are going to use are as follows:

•

the map
M_{b\to a+b}
identifies
W^{(b)}_{>}
with
W^{(a+b)}_{<}
,

•

the map
M_{a+b\to b}
identifies
W^{(a+b)}_{>}
with
W^{(b)}_{<}
,

•

the restrictions of
M_{b\to a+b}
and
\varphi^{a}
to
W^{(b)}_{>}
coincide,

•

the restrictions of
M_{a+b\to b}
and
\varphi^{n-a}
to
W^{(a+b)}_{>}
coincide.

Let
U_{b,>}=U_{b}\cap W^{(b)}_{>}
,
U_{a+b,>}=U_{a+b}\cap W^{(a+b)}_{>}
and similarly for

U_{b,<}
and
U_{a+b,>}
. Since
{\bf U}\in\mathrm{Gr}(0)
,
one gets
\varphi^{a}U_{b,>}\subset U_{a+b,<}
and
\varphi^{n-a}U_{a+b,>}\subset U_{b,<}
.
Now let us consider the projection of
U_{b}
to
U_{b,<}
with the kernel being
U_{b,>}
.
The projection contains
\varphi^{n-a}U_{a+b,>}
; we fix a subspace
P_{b}\subset U_{b}

such that the projection of
P_{b}
to
U_{b,<}
does not intersect with
\varphi^{n-a}U_{a+b,>}

and the direct sum of these two spaces coincides with the projection of
U_{b}
to
U_{b,<}
.
Now we are ready to describe the space
\varphi^{a}(U_{b})
: it is the direct sum of three spaces:

\varphi^{a}(U_{b})=\varphi^{a}(U_{b,>})\oplus U_{a+b,>}\oplus\varphi^{a}(P_{b}).

Let us have a closer look at the space
U_{a+b}
. We already know that

U_{a+b}\supset\left(\varphi^{a}(U_{b,>})\oplus U_{a+b,>}\right)+M_{b\to a+b}(P_{b}).

We also know that
M_{b\to a+b}(P_{b})
is contained in
U_{a+b,<}
, but we have no control
on the dimension of
M_{b\to a+b}(P_{b})
. In particular, the sum

\varphi^{a}(U_{b,>})+M_{b\to a+b}(P_{b})
might be smaller than the projection of
U_{a+b}

to
U_{a+b,<}
along
U_{a+b.>}
.
So in order to get the whole space
U_{a+b}
one needs to add a complementary space

P_{a+b}\subset U_{a+b}
such that the direct sum of
\varphi^{a}(U_{b,>})+M_{b\to a+b}(P_{b})

and the projection of
P_{a+b}
to
U_{a+b,<}
gives the projection of
U_{a+b}
to

U_{a+b,<}
.

Let us fix tuples
I_{b},I_{a+b}\in\binom{[n]}{k}
such that
U_{a+b}\subset X^{-}_{I_{a+b}}
,

\varphi^{a}U_{b}\subset X^{-}_{I_{b}}
and the Schubert varieties
X^{-}_{I_{b}}
,
X^{-}_{I_{a+b}}

are smallest with such property. It suffices to show that
I_{b}\geq I_{a+b}
. In fact,
our goal is to show that
\varphi^{a}U_{b}\in X^{-}_{J_{a+b}}
. Since
{\bf U}\in\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
,
one has
U_{a+b}\in X^{-}_{J_{a+b}}
, which is equivalent to
I_{a+b}\geq J_{a+b}
. Now the
inequality
I_{b}\geq I_{a+b}
implies
I_{b}\geq J_{a+b}
and hence
\varphi^{a}U_{b}\in X^{-}_{J_{a+b}}
.

The spaces
U_{a+b}
and
\varphi^{a}(U_{b})
have a common subspace

\varphi^{a}(U_{b,>})\oplus U_{a+b,>}
. We compare
M_{b\to a+b}(P_{b})
and

\varphi^{a}(P_{b})
. The former sits inside
U_{a+b,<}
and is equal to the projection of the latter
(the elements of
\varphi^{a}(P_{b})
do have nontrivial components from
U_{a+b,>}
).
In particular, the dimension of
M_{b\to a+b}(P_{b})
may be smaller than that of
\varphi^{a}(P_{b})
.
To compensate the difference one adds
P_{a+b}
which has a basis whose vectors have non-trivial
components in
U_{a+b,<}
. We conclude that
I_{a+b}\leq I_{b}
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

The image of the projection
\mathrm{pr}_{b}(\Pi_{\bf J}(0))
is equal to
\Pi_{\mathrm{rot}^{b}({\bf J})}
.

Proof.

Thanks to Proposition
4.1
it suffices to show that the projection of
the juggling variety to each factor
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}^{(b)}
is equal to the whole Grassmannian.
The juggling variety
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(0)
admits a decomposition into cells
C_{\bf J}
labeled
by juggling patterns
[
FLP22
]
. The projection of the cell
C_{\bf J}
to the
b
-th factor
is equal to the Schubert cell
\mathring{X}_{J_{b}}
. Hence it suffices to show that for any

I\in\binom{[n]}{k}
and any
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
there exists a juggling pattern
{\bf J}
such that

J_{b}=I
. To construct such a
{\bf J}
we find a subset
S\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
such that for the
corresponding juggling pattern
{\bf J}(S)
one has
{\bf J}(S)_{b}=I
(
S
consists of elements

s=b-i\mod n
,
i\in I
).
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

The special fiber
\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
has the following properties:

•

\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
is reducible and equidimensional,
\dim\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)=\dim\Pi_{{\bf J}}
;

•

the irreducible components of
\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
are the non-empty intersections of

\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
with the irreducible components of
\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n}(0)
;

•

each irreducible component of
\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
is isomorphic to a Richardson variety
in the affine flag variety of the affine group
{GL}_{n}
.

Proof.

We prove all the statements of the theorem together. The degenerate positroid variety

\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
is embedded into
the affine flag variety and the image of the embedding is equal to the union of Schubert varieties

Y_{z^{\sigma\omega_{k}}}

for
\sigma\in S_{n}
(see section
2.4
).
The juggling patterns label torus fixed points in the image (they correspond to a subset of
elements of the extended affine Weyl group). Let us denote the point corresponding to
{\bf J}

by
p_{\bf J}
and the corresponding element from the extended affine Weyl group
W^{ext}

by
w_{\bf J}
. The condition
U_{b}\in X^{-}_{J_{b}}
means that
the point
(U_{b})_{b}
sits in the orbit of the opposite Iwahori group passing through
p_{\bf J}
.
We conclude that
\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
is the union of the affine Richardson varieties corresponding
to
z^{\sigma\omega_{k}}
and
w_{\bf J}
, which is non-empty if and only if

w_{\bf J}
is smaller than or equal to
z^{\sigma\omega_{k}}
.
This proves the third and second statements of the theorem (the length of
z^{\sigma\omega_{k}}

does not depend on
\sigma
).

Finally, let us prove that
\dim\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)=\dim\Pi_{{\bf J}}
. By the argument above,
the dimension
of the degenerate positroid variety
\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
is given by

\dim\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)=\dim\mathrm{Gr}_{k,n-k}-\dim Y_{w_{\bf J}}=k(n-k)-\ell(w_{\bf J}).

However, the right hand side is also equal to the dimension of the classical positroid variety

\dim\Pi_{{\bf J}}
. In fact, besides the set of juggling patterns, positroids can be also labeled
by affine permutations forming the extended affine Weyl group
W^{ext}

[
KLS13
,
Lam14
]
.
Then the codimension of
\Pi_{\bf J}
in the Grassmann
variety is exactly the length of the corresponding affine permutation.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

The number of irreducible components of
\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
is equal to the number
of cardinality
k
subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
such that for any

.

The condition

is explicitly written as

J_{b}\leq\{s-b,\ s\in S\}
, where
s-b
is understood as
n-b+s
provided
s-b\leq 0
.
According to the proof of the theorem above, we know that
the number of irreducible components of
\Pi_{{\bf J}}(0)
is equal to the number of
sets
S\subset\binom{[n]}{k}
such that
{\bf J}\leq{\bf J}(S)
, i.e.
J_{b}\leq{\bf J}(S)_{b}
for
all
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
. By definition,
{\bf J}(S)_{b}=\{s-b,\ s\in S\}
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

The global positroid varieties form a flat family.

Proof.

Our family
\pi:\underline{\Pi}_{{\bf J}}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
is defined over the affine line,
all the fibers outside zero are isomorphic to
\Pi_{{\bf J}}
and the special fiber
is reducible with each
irreducible component of the same dimension
\dim\Pi_{{\bf J}}
. Hence
it suffices to check that the closure of the preimage

\pi^{-1}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus 0)
coincides with the whole family
[
V25
]
.
Let us consider the closure
\overline{\pi^{-1}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\setminus 0)}
as a family over
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
(with the obvious projection map). Then each irreducible component of the special fiber has dimension
\dim\Pi_{{\bf J}}
. In fact,
the dimension can not be larger (since

Lemma A.2

For a non-negative integer

Corollary A.3
.

For

one has
\dim H^{0}(\Pi_{\bf J},\mathcal{O}(M))=\binom{M+\ell({\bf J})-1}{M}
.

We provide a flat basis of the homogeneous coordinate ring of the family
\underline{\Pi}_{\bf J}
.
Recall the embedding
\underline{\Pi}_{\bf J}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\times\prod_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}{\mathbb{P}}(W^{(b)})
and the homogeneous coordinates
\Delta^{(b)}_{i}
in the
b
-th factor

.
The homogeneous coordinate ring
\underline{\emph{Pl}}
is a quotient of the
polynomial ring in variables
\Delta^{(b)}_{i}
,
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
and
i\in[n]
.
The ring

is multi-graded by the group

{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}
,
\underline{\emph{Pl}}=\bigoplus_{{\bf m}}{\emph{Pl}}({\bf m})
,
where

,

. Explicitly,

is equal to the
number of variables of the form
\Delta^{(b)}_{\bullet}
in a monomial.

Our goal is to present a basis of the ring
\underline{\emph{Pl}}

which is compatible with the decomposition into
the direct sum of
{\emph{Pl}}({\bf m})
. Each element of the basis is of the form

(A.1)

\prod_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}\Delta^{(b)}_{i^{(b)}_{1}}\dots\Delta^{(b)}_{i^{(b)}_{m_{b}}},\ i^{(b)}_{1}\leq\dots\leq i^{(b)}_{m_{b}}.

The collection
{\bf m}=(m_{b})_{b}
is called the multi-degree of the monomial (
A.1
).

Definition A.4
.

A monomial (
A.1
) is called
{\bf J}
-admissible if

•

b+i^{(b)}_{r}-1\in L({\bf J})
for all
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
,
r\in[m_{b}]
,

•

if
i^{(b)}_{u}>s
for some
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
,
u\in[m_{b}]
,
s\in[n-1]
, then

i^{(b+s)\!\!\mod n}_{r}\leq i^{(b)}_{u}-s
for all
r
.

Remark A.5
.

Let us comment on the first condition
J_{b+i^{(b)}_{r}-1}=1
(here as usual the lower index of

J
is understood as an element of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
).
The condition implies that
i^{(b)}_{r}\geq J_{b}
, i.e. cuts out the corresponding Schubert variety,
because if
i^{(b)}_{r}<J_{b}
, then

J_{b+i^{(b)}_{r}-1}=J_{b}-i^{(b)}_{r}+1>1
. Moreover, if all
numbers
m_{b}
are strictly positive, then the conditions
J_{b+i^{(b)}_{r}-1}=1

are equivalent to the Schubert conditions
i^{(b)}_{r}\geq J_{b}
(taking into account the second condition in Definition
A.4
). In fact,
assume that all the Schubert conditions
i^{(b)}_{r}\geq J_{b}
hold and all
the second type conditions from Definition
A.4
hold as well.
Then

1=i^{(b)}_{r}-(i^{(b)}_{r}-1)\geq i^{(b+i^{(b)}_{r}-1)}_{1}\geq J_{b+i^{(b)}_{r}-1}.

However, if some of the multiplicities
m_{b}
vanish, then the conditions

i^{(b)}_{r}\geq J_{b}
are weaker than the first type conditions in
Definition
A.4
.

Proposition A.6
.

Let
|{\bf m}|=\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}m_{b}
. Then
the number of
{\bf J}
-admissible monomials of multi-degree
{\bf m}
is equal to

\binom{|{\bf m}|+\ell({\bf J})-1}{{\bf m}}
.

Proof.

We first assume that
l({\bf J})=n
, i.e.
J_{b}=1
for all
b
, and then deduce the general statement.

The number
\binom{|{\bf m}|+n-1}{{\bf m}}
is equal to the number of sequences

1\leq s_{1}\leq\dots\leq s_{n}\leq|{\bf m}|
(i.e. to the dimension of the
|{\bf m}|
-th symmetric power
of an
n
-dimensional vector space). We establish a ”weight preserving” bijection between the
set of such collections and the set of admissible monomials of the form (
A.1
).
Explicitly, the bijection
being weight preserving means that for each
j=1,\dots,n
one has

\#\{(r,b):\ i_{r}^{(b)}+b=j\mod n\}=s_{j}.

In words, we are counting the number of Plücker variables
\Delta_{i_{r}}^{(b)}
–
factors of a monomial – such that
i_{r}+b
is fixed modulo
n

(this is compatible with the decomposition into indecomposable summands of the representation

M(\varepsilon)
,
\varepsilon\neq 0
of the cyclic equioriented cyclic quiver, see section
2.3
).

So our task is to distribute
s_{j}
balls into the cells labeled by a vertex
b

and a position
i
with
i+b=j
taking care of the admissibility condition. Let

q_{i}^{(b)}=\#\{(r,b):\ i_{r}^{(b)}=i\}

be the number of factors of the form
\Delta_{i}^{(b)}

in (
A.1
).
We first observe that if
q^{(b)}_{r-b}>0
with
r-b\neq 1
, then
q^{(r-1)}_{1}=m_{r-1}

(i.e. all the balls in the
(r-1)
-st column are concentrated at the first position).
In fact, since we are constructing an admissible monomial,

q^{(b)}_{r-b}>0
,
r-b>1
implies
q^{(r-1)}_{>1}=0
. So we conclude that in the process of distributing
our balls we are forced to start with the cells corresponding to the lowest possible position
for each vertex (i.e. with
q^{(\bullet)}_{1}
). There are two options here: either

s_{r}\leq m_{r-1}
and all the
s_{r}
balls produce
q^{(r-1)}_{1}=s_{r}
, or
s_{r}>m_{r-1}
and
we are left to distribute further balls among the cells. So after the first step all
the entries
q^{(\bullet)}_{1}
are already uniquely determined.

Assume that for some values of
r
the second option as above is realized (i.e.

s_{r}>m_{r-1}
). Let us look at the value of
q^{(r-2)}_{2}
.
If
q^{(b)}_{r-b}>0
,
r-b>2
, then
q^{(r-2)}_{>2}=0
(as a consequence of admissibility).
Therefore, there are two options here: either
q^{(r-2)}_{1}=m_{r-2}
or
q^{(r-2)}_{1}<m_{r-2}
.
In the first case all the values
q^{(r-2)}_{>1}
(including
q^{(r-2)}_{2}
) automatically vanish.
In the second case, we fill
q^{(r-2)}_{2}
with the balls from the initial group if
s_{r}

(left from filling
q^{(r-1)}_{1}
) until either all the
s_{r}
balls are used or the total number
of
m_{r-1}
balls at this vertex is achieved. After this step all the values of
q^{(\bullet)}_{2}

are fixed. We then proceed with
q^{(\bullet)}_{3}
, etc.

We are left to consider the case of general positroid variety. The first condition of Definition

A.4
says that in order to pass from the maximal case considered above
(
J_{b}=1
for all
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
) to the arbitrary
{\bf J}
case one needs to keep only the entries

such that
b+r-1\in L({\bf J})
(to be compared with Lemma
A.1
).
To prove our proposition in this case we just repeat the argument above for the relevant entries.
∎

Lemma A.7

Proof.

Relations from Definition

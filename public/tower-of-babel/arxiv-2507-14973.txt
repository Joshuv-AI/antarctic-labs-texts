Sequentially compact separable spaces
César Corral, Alan Dow and Paul Szeptycki

Abstract
. We consider the following variation of the Scarborough-Stone problem: Is X κ X^{\kappa} always countably compact whenever X X is separable and sequentially compact?



1.
Introduction

In their 1966 paper
Products of nearly compact spaces
,
[
12
]
Scarborough and Stone observed that a product of a family of sequentially compact spaces “..will in general (when there are uncountably many factors)
not be sequentially compact…” and noted that it “would be
interesting to know whether every product of sequentially compact spaces must
be countably compact.” This question has come to be known as the Scarborough-Stone problem. The updated question asks:
is it consistent that any family of sequentially compact spaces has product that is countably compact?
as many consistent counter-examples to the problem are now known
(see
[
14
]
). It has also been shown (even in Scarborough and Stone’s original paper, though not explicitly) that if for each
p\in\omega^{*}
there is a sequentially compact space
X_{p}
that is not
p
-compact, then the product
\prod\{X_{p}:p\in\omega^{*}\}
is not countably compact. Moreover, if there is any such family of spaces, then one can sew them together to obtain a single sequentially compact space
X
which is not
p
-compact for any
p\in\omega^{*}
and so the power
X^{2^{\mathfrak{c}}}
is not countably compact. This conglomerate space
X
is, however, not separable and we were surprised to observe that the following natural variant of the Scarborough-Stone problem had not been previously considered:

Question 1
.

If
X
is a separable and sequentially compact space, must every power
X^{\kappa}
be countably compact?

This seems to be an open question and while we have not solved it, we have some partial results clarifying the situation.

As with the general Scarborough-Stone problem, Question
1
is equivalent to the following:

Question 2
.

If
X
is a separable and sequentially compact space, is
X

p
-compact for some
p\in\omega^{*}
?

We have a consistent counterexample in Miller’s model: indeed in that model there is a separable sequentially compact space of size
\mathfrak{c}
which is not
p
-compact for any
p
. Towards a positive answer we only have a partial consistency result: assuming
MA
, for every separable sequentially compact space
X
,
if
|X|\leq\mathfrak{c}
then
X
is
p
-compact for some
p
.

This raises the related question whether the continuum can be a bound on the cardinality of separable sequentially compact spaces. E.g., if it were consistent with Martin’s Axiom that
{\mathfrak{c}}
is a bound on the size of separable sequentially compact spaces, then we would have a consistent positive answer to Question
1
(alas, this is not the case).

Question 3
.

If
X
is separable and sequentially compact, is then
|X|\leq{\mathfrak{c}}
?

In regards to this question, we have the following results: In the Cohen model, yes, the continuum is a bound. However, we also have examples giving a consistent negative answer:
if
{\mathfrak{s}}>\omega_{1}
and
2^{\omega_{1}}>\mathfrak{c}
, then there is a large separable sequentially compact space (namely
2^{\omega_{1}}
). In addition we show that there is a separable sequentially compact spaces of size
2^{\mathfrak{p}}
, and so, under
{\mathfrak{p=c}}
there is a separable sequentially compact space of size
>{\mathfrak{c}}
.

We present all these results in the remainder of the paper and conclude with some open questions.

Our terminology and notation are standard and we refer the reader to
[
11
]
for the set-theoretic notions and
[
8
]
for the topological.

4.
The cardinality of sequentially compact separable spaces

One scenario in which Problem
1
could have a consistent positive answer is to have all separable sequentially compact spaces have cardinality bound by
{\mathfrak{c}}
consistent with the principle
(*)
that every tree
\pi
-base has height
\mathfrak{c}
and a cofinal branch. While this is open, we turn to the weaker question of whether
\mathfrak{c}
is a bound on the cardinality of such spaces. As we will see this is consistent and independent.

A model where
\mathfrak{c}
is an upper bound is the Cohen model and this follows from the proof of one of the main results from
[
6
]
.
The proof of Theorem 4.2 from that paper actually shows the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
V
be a model for
GCH
and
\kappa>\aleph_{1}
be a regular cardinal. Then in the forcing extension,
V_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\kappa}}
we have that for any regular space
X
if a countable
A\subseteq X
has closure larger than
\kappa
then there is
B\subseteq A
such that
B
has no nontrivial sequences converging in
X
.∎

And so as an immediate corollary we obtain

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
V
be a model for
GCH
and
\kappa>\aleph_{1}
be a regular cardinal. Then in
V_{{\mathbb{C}}_{\kappa}}
every sequentially compact separable space has size at most
{\mathfrak{c}}
.∎

Then it is consistent that separable sequentially compact spaces are bound in size by the continuum.

We present two consistent examples of large (i.e., of cardinality
>{\mathfrak{c}}
) separable sequentially compact spaces. The first is easily obtained from van Douwen’s characterization of sequential compactness of
2^{\kappa}
and some known consistency results concerning cardinal invariants of the continuum. Recall the definition of a splitting family and the cardinal
\mathfrak{s}
:

Definition 4.3
.

A family
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
is a splitting family if for every
a\in[\omega]^{\omega}
there is
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
such that
|s\cap a|=|a\setminus s|=\omega
. The minimal cardinality of a splitting family is the splitting number and is denoted
{\mathfrak{s}}

Van Douwen proved that
\mathfrak{s}
is the minimal
\kappa
such that
2^{\kappa}
is not sequentially compact
[
13
]
. And so, in any model where
\omega_{1}<\mathfrak{s}
and
2^{\omega}<2^{\omega_{1}}
, the space
2^{\omega_{1}}
would be a separable sequentially compact space of cardinality larger than the continuum.

To obtain such a model, start with a model of
MA+\mathfrak{c}=\omega_{2}
and so
{\mathfrak{s}}=\omega_{2}
in that model. Then force with the countably closed partial order
Fn(\omega_{3},2,<\omega_{1}\}
that adds
\omega_{3}
Cohen subsets of
\omega_{1}

is preserved but

.

Of course

for this family. Let

be such that

. Otherwise, if

It is clear from the construction that for any

and

Proposition 4.8
.

If

is an acceptable subtree and the

are as described above, then

generates a

Proof.

We only need to check that for

,

is minimal for

, where
A_{t}
and

are as in Definition
4.5
.

Since the length of any node in

is a successor ordinal, there are
s\in T
and

such that
t=s^{\smallfrown}i
. By Lemma
4.5
, it suffices to show that
a_{s^{\smallfrown}0}
is minimal over
({\mathcal{A}}_{t},u_{t})
.

Indeed,
a_{t}=a_{s^{\smallfrown}0}
is a pseudointersection of
\{a_{r}:r<t\}
, i.e.,
a_{t}\subseteq^{*}a_{r}
for every

. That implies
a_{r}\setminus a_{t}\in[\omega]^{<\omega}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{t}
. By Proposition

,
a_{t}
is minimal for
({\mathcal{A}}_{t},u_{t})
.
∎

Before we prove the main result of this section, we will introduce a bit more of notation. If
X
is a
T
-algebra and

is a branch of

, we define

as the

-algebra space, where

is the minimal acceptable subtree of

). Hence
X_{b}=\{z_{\alpha}:\alpha<l(b)\ \land\ \alpha\textnormal{ is a successor}\}\cup\{u_{b}\}
, where:

•

u_{b}
is the ultrafilter generated by
\{a_{b\upharpoonright\alpha}:\alpha<l(b)\}
and

•

each

is the ultrafilter generated by
\{a_{b\upharpoonright\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}\cup\{\omega\setminus a_{b\upharpoonright\alpha}\}

in the Boolean algebra
\langle a_{b\upharpoonright\alpha}:\alpha<l(b)\rangle
.

Given two different branches

in

, define

. Following
[

, if

is a

-algebra and

is a branch of
T

to denote the natural continuous projection such that
\pi_{b}(u_{b})=u_{b}
and
\pi_{b}(u_{q})=z_{\alpha}
if
\alpha=b\land q
. We will make use of the following result.

Lemma 4.9
.

[
5
]

Let
X
be a
{\mathcal{T}}
-algebra,
u_{b}\in X
and
\{u_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq X\setminus\{u_{b}\}
. Then
u_{n}\to u_{b}
if and only if
\pi_{b}(u_{n})\to\pi_{b}(u_{b})
in

.∎

Given a branch
b
of

and
\gamma\leq l(b)
, let also

to the

-algebra generated by

. In particular,

. If

, we will use

to denote the ultrafilter generated by

.

We are now ready to proof the main result of the section.

Theorem 4.10
.

If
T=\{s\in 2^{<{\mathfrak{p}}}:l(s)\textnormal{ is a successor ordinal}\}
, and
a_{t}
are as described above, then the Stone space of the
T
-algebra generated by
\{a_{t}:t\in T\}
is sequentially compact.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the Boolean algebra generated by
\{a_{t}:t\in T\}
and let us denote by
X
its Stone space
St({\mathcal{B}})
.
Let
\{u_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq X
be a one-to-one sequence.
We recursively construct a Cantor scheme
\{t_{s}:s\in 2^{<\omega}\}\subseteq T
and
\{Y_{s}:s\in 2^{<\omega}\}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}
such that for every
s\in 2^{<\omega}
:

(1)

t_{s^{\smallfrown}i}>t_{s}
for
i\in 2
.

(2)

There is
r_{s}\in T
such that
t_{s^{\smallfrown}i}=r_{s}^{\smallfrown}i
for
i\in 2
.

(3)

Y_{s}=Y_{s^{\smallfrown}0}\sqcup Y_{s^{\smallfrown}1}
(where
\sqcup
denotes disjoint union).

(4)

For
t\in T
, if
t\leq t_{s}
then
\{n\in Y_{s}:u_{n}\in a_{t}^{*}\}
is cofinite.

Let us start by defining
t_{\emptyset}=\emptyset
and
Y_{\emptyset}=\omega
. Now assume we have defined
t_{s}
and
Y_{s}
satisfying (1)-(4). If
\{u_{n}:n\in Y_{s}\}
is a convergent sequence, we halt the construction since we have already found a convergent subsequence for
\{u_{n}:n\in\omega\}
.
So we can assume without loss of generality that
\{u_{n}:n\in Y_{s}\}
is not a convergent sequence.
As
X
is compact, we can find
t^{\prime}\in T
such that the clopen set
a_{t^{\prime}}^{*}
splits
\{u_{n}:n\in Y_{s}\}
, i.e., both sets
\{n\in Y_{s}:u_{n}\in a_{t^{\prime}}^{*}\}
and
\{n\in Y_{s}:u_{n}\notin a_{t^{\prime}}^{*}\}
are infinite.
We can moreover take
t^{\prime}
of minimal possible length.
As
t^{\prime}\in T
we have that
t^{\prime}=r^{\smallfrown}j
for some
r\in T
and
j\in 2
. Define
t_{s^{\smallfrown}i}=r^{\smallfrown}i
, so that
(2)
holds.

From the fact that
{\mathcal{B}}
is a
{\mathcal{T}}
-algebra, we get that
a_{t_{s^{\smallfrown}i}}=\omega\setminus a_{t_{s^{\smallfrown}(1-i)}}
, hence by defining
Y_{s^{\smallfrown}i}=\{n\in Y_{s}:u_{n}\in a_{t_{s^{\smallfrown}i}}^{*}\}
for
i<2
we conclude that
Y_{s^{\smallfrown}i}\in[\omega]^{\omega}
and
Y_{s}=Y_{s^{\smallfrown}0}\sqcup Y_{s^{\smallfrown}1}
, hence
(3)
holds.

That
(1)
holds follows directly from our inductive hypothesis
(4)
and
(4)
holds from the fact that
Y_{s^{\smallfrown}i}\subseteq Y
and from the minimality of
t^{\prime}
.

For every
\varphi\in 2^{\omega}
let
Y_{\varphi}
be a pseudointersection of
\{Y_{\varphi\upharpoonright n}:n\in\omega\}
. Since
u_{n}:n\in\omega
is one-to-one, we can also fix
\varphi\in 2^{\omega}
such that if
q=\sup\{t_{\varphi\upharpoonright n}:n\in\omega\}
and
\gamma=l(q)
, then
l(b_{u_{n}}\land q)<\gamma
for every
n\in\omega
.
Let
b\in 2^{\mathfrak{p}}
be defined by

b(\alpha)=\begin{cases}q(\alpha)&\textnormal{ if }\ \ \alpha<l(q)\\
1&\textnormal{ if }\ \ \alpha\geq l(q)\end{cases}

Our goal is to show that
\{u_{n}:n\in Y_{\varphi}\}
converges to
u_{b}
. In order to do this we will use Lemma
4.9
. To simplify our notation let
b_{n}=b_{u_{n}}
and
z_{n}=\pi_{b}(u_{n})
for every
n\in Y_{\varphi}
. We will show by induction that
\{z_{n}:n\in Y_{\varphi}\}
converges to
u_{b\upharpoonright\alpha}
in
X_{b}^{\alpha}
for every
\gamma\leq\alpha\leq{\mathfrak{p}}
.

For
\alpha=\gamma=l(q)
,
(1)
,
(4)
and the fact that
\{a_{\sigma}^{*}:\sigma\in q\}
is a local base for the ultrafilter
u_{q}\in X_{b}^{\gamma}
imply that
\{z_{n}:n\in Y_{\varphi}\}
converges to
u_{q}=u_{b\upharpoonright\gamma}
.

A similar argument shows that if this holds for every
\gamma\leq\alpha<\beta
and
\beta
is limit, then it also holds for
\beta
, as a typical element in the local base of
u_{b\upharpoonright\beta}
is the intersection of finitely many sets of the form
a_{b\upharpoonright\lambda}^{*}
with
\lambda<\beta
.

Assume then that
z_{n}\to u_{b\upharpoonright\alpha}
for some
\gamma\leq\alpha<{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Notice that
a=a_{b\upharpoonright\alpha^{\smallfrown}0}
is a pseudointersection of
\{a_{b\upharpoonright\beta+1}:\beta<\alpha\}
.
In particular, for every
n\in Y_{\varphi}
, if
({t_{\varphi\upharpoonright n})^{\smallfrown}i}\subseteq b\upharpoonright\alpha
, then
a\subseteq^{*}a_{({t_{\varphi\upharpoonright n}})^{\smallfrown}i}
. On the other hand,
\omega\setminus a_{{(t_{\varphi\upharpoonright n})}^{\smallfrown}i}=a_{({t_{\varphi\upharpoonright n}})^{\smallfrown}(1-i)}\in z_{n}
, so
a\not\in z_{n}
, which implies that
a_{{(b\upharpoonright\alpha)}^{\smallfrown}1}=\omega\setminus a\in z_{n}
. That is, the only new element in the base filter for
u_{b\upharpoonright(\alpha+1)}
(namely
a_{(b\upharpoonright\alpha)^{\smallfrown}1}
) with respect to the family generating
u_{b\upharpoonright\alpha}
belongs to every ultrafilter
z_{n}
. Hence it is irrelevant for the convergence of the given sequence and therefore
z_{n}\to p\upharpoonright(\alpha+1)
.

By induction we get that
z_{n}\to u_{b\upharpoonright{\mathfrak{p}}}=u_{b}
in
X_{b}^{\mathfrak{p}}=X_{b}
, and by Lemma
4.9
, we get that
\{u_{n}:n\in Y_{\varphi}\}
converges to
u_{b}
in
X
. Therefore
X
is sequentially compact.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

If
2^{\mathfrak{p}}>{\mathfrak{c}}
, there is a sequentially compact separable space of cardinality
>{\mathfrak{c}}
. In particular, it holds under
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{c}}
∎

The same idea as the used in Theorem

actually shows a bit more: if we take any acceptable subtree
T
and repeat the proof, either we find a maximal branch in
T
given by some
\varphi\in 2^{\omega}
, or we can repeat the argument adding 1’s until we define a branch (not necessarily of length
{\mathfrak{p}}
). In any case, if

is this branch, the subsequence
\{u_{n}:n\in Y_{\varphi}\}
converges to

.

Corollary 4.12
.

If
T\subseteq 2^{<{\mathfrak{p}}}
is any acceptable tree and the

are defined as above, then the
T
-algebra generated by
\{a_{t}:t\in T\}
is sequentially compact.∎

We now present an alternative way to produce a sequentially compact separable
{\mathcal{T}}
-algebra: In the above argument, if there is always a
\varphi\in 2^{\omega}
such that
\{t_{\varphi\upharpoonright n}:n\in\omega\}

is convergent, and this does not depend on the

being chosen as above. So given
any

T
-algebra on an acceptable subtree
T
of some
2^{<\kappa}

does not include any copy of

, there is always one of these branches and the space is sequentially compact.

Recall that a Kurepa tree is a subtree

such that

has height
\omega_{1}
, each level is countable, and

has at least
\omega_{2}
-many branches.
In general, a

-Kurepa tree is a tree of height
\kappa

and more than

It is easy to see that given a
\kappa
-Kurepa tree, there is an everywhere
2
-splitting
\kappa
-Kurepa tree and by removing the nodes at limit levels, gives an acceptable
\kappa
-Kurepa tree.

Corollary 4.13
.

Assume the existence of an acceptable

{\mathfrak{c}}
-Kurepa tree
K\subseteq 2^{<{\mathfrak{c}}}
. Then for any
T
-algebra on

, the Stone space is sequentially compact.

Note that the existence of a

-Kurepa tree follows from

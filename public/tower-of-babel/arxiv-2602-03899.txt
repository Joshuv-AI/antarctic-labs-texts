Byzantine Machine Learning: MultiKrum and an optimal notion of robustness
Gilles Bareilles ∗,†, Wassim Bouaziz ∗,‡, Julien Fageot ∗,⋄, El Mahdi El Mhamdi †

Abstract
. Aggregation rules are the cornerstone of distributed (or federated) learning in the presence of adversaries, under the so-called Byzantine threat model. They are also interesting mathematical objects from the point of view of robust mean estimation. The Krum aggregation rule has been extensively studied, and endowed with formal robustness and convergence guarantees. Yet, MultiKrum, a natural extension of Krum, is often preferred in practice for its superior empirical performance, even though no theoretical guarantees were available until now. In this work, we provide the first proof that MultiKrum is a robust aggregation rule, and bound its robustness coefficient. To do so, we introduce κ ⋆ \kappa^{\star} , the optimal robustness coefficient of an aggregation rule, which quantifies the accuracy of mean estimation in the presence of adversaries in a tighter manner compared with previously adopted notions of robustness. We then construct an upper and a lower bound on MultiKrum’s robustness coefficient. As a by-product, we also improve on the best-known bounds on Krum’s robustness coefficient. We show that MultiKrum’s bounds are never worse than Krum’s, and better in realistic regimes. We illustrate this analysis by an experimental investigation on the quality of the lower bound.

Introduction

Modern machine learning tasks in vision, natural language processing, and item recommendation involves datasets and models of ever growing size, and now crucially rely on distributing data and models across multiple compute devices.
In this distributed learning setup, a central device (or a decentralized protocol simulating a central device) orchestrates the learning task, while others perform computations based on local data or models.

While enabling scalability, distributed learning also introduces vulnerabilities, such as adversarial attacks during training

These threats have given rise to the field of

In distributed, collaborative, and federated learning

Central to this framework is the

Early work includes the geometric median

Improving the security of robust aggregation has thus an importance in ML that goes beyond the distributed setting where machines exchange gradients and models updates.

This notion is appealing: common aggregation rules are

Our definition of

Robustness of aggregators

In this Section, we first recall the MultiKrum aggregation rule, and then we present our notion of robustness.

Notations

We let
[p]=\{1,\dots,p\}
for

We adopt the convention
0/0=-\infty

We let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
denote the set of
n

2.1.
MultiKrum

We let
\mathcal{N}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\parts_{n-f}^{n}
such that

denotes the indices of the
n-f

We also let
\mathcal{N}_{i}=\mathcal{N}(x_{i})

Given

Definition 1
.

The

-MultiKrum
aggregation rule defines as the average of the

F_{m}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\triangleq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}}x_{i},

where

Krum is

Remark 1
.

Here, we choose that

, with normalizing constant

]

2.2.
Robustness of aggregators

We now define our notion of robustness.

Definition 2
.

Let
f\leq n/2
and consider an aggregation rule
F:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We define the
robustness coefficient

\kappa^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
of
F
as

\kappa^{\star}(F)\triangleq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
S\in\parts_{n-f}^{n}\end{subarray}}\frac{\|F(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}}{\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}},

with
\bar{x}_{S}=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}
.
We say that
F
is
f
-robust
when
\kappa^{\star}<\infty
.
Finally, we call any real
c
such that
c\geq\kappa^{\star}
, resp.
c\leq\kappa^{\star}
, an
upper-bound
, resp.
lower-bound
, on the robustness coefficient.

Several remarks are in order:

•

By adopting the convention
0/0=-\infty
, we exclude the degenerate case where all points in
S
are equal.
This case is of little interest: if
n-f
points agree among the
n
candidates, they form a majority, and any reasonable aggregation rule should output the common value.

•

We recover the notion of
[
2
]
by declaring
F
to be
(f,\kappa)
-robust
whenever
\kappa\geq\kappa^{\star}
.
As a result, the convergence guarantee for Distributed Stochastic Gradient Descent
[
2
, Th. 1]
holds with the optimal robustness coefficient
\kappa^{\star}
.

•

Finally, computing the robustness coefficient of an aggregation rule amounts to deriving matching lower and upper bounds.
Lower bounds follow from evaluating the objective at any choice of
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
S
.
Upper bounds require constructing uniform bounds on the objective, valid for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
S
, and depending on the aggregation rule.
In particular, if an aggregator is
(f,\kappa)
-robust, then
\kappa
is an upper bound on its robustness coefficient.

Similarly to many observations either in distributed machine learning
[
2
, Prop. 6]
or in various different forms in the older literature on robust mean estimation
[
34
,
13
,
24
]
: all robustness coefficients are above a universal value.
This also holds for our optimal robustness coefficient
\kappa^{\star}
.

Proposition 1

(Universal lower bound)
.

Consider a robust aggregation rule
F

i.e.
, a rule such that
\kappa^{\star}<\infty
.
Then,
n-2f>0
, and there holds

\kappa^{\star}\geq\frac{f}{n-2f}.

3.1.
The upper bound

The following result shows that

The upper bound on MultiKrum’s robustness coefficient

(1)

\displaystyle\kappa^{\star}_{m}\geq\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\min\left((\sqrt{2}+1)^{2},\kappa_{2}^{>}(m)\right)

\kappa_{2}^{>}(m)=\left(\frac{\sqrt{n-2f}}{\sqrt{m}}+\frac{\sqrt{2f}}{\sqrt{m}}+\frac{f}{m}\right)^{2}
.

The proof of
Theorem

1
combines the two upper bounds derived in

5

The next results characterizes the behavior of the transition point

separating, the two regimes of

Theorem 2

(Bounds on the transition point)
.

Let
m^{\dagger}=m^{\dagger}(n,f)>0

\kappa_{2}^{>}(m^{\dagger})=(\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}
.
There holds

m^{\dagger}(n,f)\underset{f/n\to 0}{=}\frac{n}{(\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}}\,(1+o(1))

When

, the proportion of Byzantine workers is negligible. In this regime, the transition point satisfies

m^{\dagger}\approx n/(\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}\simeq 0.17,n
, so that the

Proof of
Theorem

2
.

Set
C=(\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}
and

A=A(n,f)\coloneqq\sqrt{n-2f}+\sqrt{2f}.

By definition of
m^{\dagger}
, the equation
\kappa_{2}^{>}(m^{\dagger})=C
is equivalent, with the substitution
x=\sqrt{m^{\dagger}}>0
, to

Cx^{4}=\left(A+\frac{f}{x}\right)^{2}=A^{2}+2A\frac{f}{x}+\frac{f^{2}}{x^{2}}.

Multiplying both sides by

yields

Cx^{4}=A^{2}x^{2}+2Afx+f^{2}.

First, we derive a
lower bound
on
m^{\dagger}
.
Since
2Afx\geq 0
, dropping this positive term from the right-hand side decreases it, hence

Cx^{4}\geq A^{2}x^{2}+f^{2}.

Since
x^{2}=m^{\dagger}
, this inequality becomes

C(m^{\dagger})^{2}-A^{2}m^{\dagger}-f^{2}\geq 0,

whose solutions satisfy

m^{\dagger}\geq\frac{A^{2}+\sqrt{A^{4}+4Cf^{2}}}{2C}.

Therefore,

m^{\dagger}=x^{2}\geq\frac{A^{2}+\sqrt{A^{4}+4(\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}f^{2}}}{2(\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}}.

Second, we derive an
upper bound
on
m^{\dagger}
.
By the arithmetic and geometric means inequality,

2Afx\leq A^{2}x^{2}+f^{2},

which implies

A^{2}x^{2}+2Afx+f^{2}\leq 2A^{2}x^{2}+2f^{2}.

Substituting into the defining equation yields

Cx^{4}\leq 2A^{2}x^{2}+2f^{2}.

Again, we have

C(m^{\dagger})^{2}-2A^{2}m^{\dagger}-2f^{2}\leq 0,

which implies

m^{\dagger}\leq\frac{2A^{2}+\sqrt{4A^{4}+8Cf^{2}}}{2C}=\frac{A^{2}+\sqrt{A^{4}+2Cf^{2}}}{C}.

Hence,

m^{\dagger}=x^{2}\leq\frac{A^{2}+\sqrt{A^{4}+2(\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}f^{2}}}{(\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}}.

Finally, we derive the
Asymptotic behavior when
f\ll n
.

Noting that for
f\ll n
, we have

A=\sqrt{n-2f}+\sqrt{2f}=\sqrt{n}(1+o(1)),

so that

A^{2}=n(1+o(1)),

and

\sqrt{A^{4}+4Cf^{2}}=A^{2}(1+o(1))=n(1+o(1)),

and similarly for the term inside the upper bound. Thus, both bounds reduce to

(

Proof of
Theorem

4
.

Let
e=e_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the first canonical basis vector, and consider

\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
defined by

\begin{cases}f\text{ vectors }&=-e,\\

with

For this configuration, the MultiKrum scores satisfy, for

We choose as reference set

The squared bias is therefore

The empirical variance of

|A|=a

\bar{x}_{A}=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{i\in A}x_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma_{A}=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{i\in A}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}_{A}\|^{2}.

Lemma 1
.

Let
A,B\subset[n]
have sizes
a,b
respectively.
For any
A,B
,

(2)

\frac{1}{ab}\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{j\in B}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|^{2}\;=\;\|\bar{x}_{A}-\bar{x}_{B}\|^{2}+\Sigma_{A}+\Sigma_{B}.

For
A=B
, we therefore have

\Sigma_{A}\;=\;\frac{1}{2a^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in A}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|^{2}.

Proof.

Fix
i\in A,\ j\in B
, and consider the decomposition

x_{i}-x_{j}=(x_{i}-\bar{x}_{A})+(\bar{x}_{A}-\bar{x}_{B})+(\bar{x}_{B}-x_{j}).

Squaring and expanding the inner product yields

\displaystyle\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x_{i}-\bar{x}_{A}\|^{2}+\|\bar{x}_{A}-\bar{x}_{B}\|^{2}+\|\bar{x}_{B}-x_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\langle x_{i}-\bar{x}_{A},\bar{x}_{A}-\bar{x}_{B}\rangle+2\langle\bar{x}_{A}-\bar{x}_{B},\bar{x}_{B}-x_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle+2\langle x_{i}-\bar{x}_{A},\bar{x}_{B}-x_{j}\rangle.

When averaging over
i\in A
and
j\in B
, the mixed inner-product terms vanish because

\sum_{i\in A}(x_{i}-\bar{x}_{A})=0=\sum_{j\in B}(x_{j}-\bar{x}_{B})
. Thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{ab}\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{j\in B}

\displaystyle\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{i\in A}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}_{A}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{b}\sum_{j\in B}\|x_{j}-\bar{x}_{B}\|^{2}+\|\bar{x}_{A}-\bar{x}_{B}\|^{2},

which is exactly (
2
).
∎

4.2.
Two key lemmas

In this Section, we provide two key lemmas for the robustness analysis of MultiKrum.

Lemma 2
.

Consider integers
f
,
n
and
m
such that
n-2f>0
,
0<m\leq n-f
, a collection
\mathcal{X}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
of
n
vectors, and a subset
S
of
[n]
of size
|S|=n-f
.
Let
S_{m}^{\star}\in\parts_{m}^{n}
denote
m
indices of
[n]
that minimize
s(x_{\cdot})
.
Then

(3)

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}}s(x_{i})\leq 2\,\Sigma_{S}.

Proof.

By definition,
S_{m}^{\star}
is the set of
m
indices with minimal
s(x_{i})
.
Since the average over the
m
smallest elements is no larger than the average over
all elements in
S
, we have

(4)

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}}s(x_{i})\leq\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}s(x_{i}).

Next, note that each
\mathcal{N}(x_{i})
contains the
n-f
closest neighbors of
x_{i}
in
\mathcal{X}
.
Hence, for each
i\in S

Averaging the previous equation over
i\in S
, and then using

, we obtain

(5)

\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}s_{i}\leq\frac{1}{|S|^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in S}\|x_{j}-x_{i}\|^{2}=2\Sigma_{S}.

Combining

∎

Lemma 3
.

Consider two integers
f
,
n
such that
n-2f>0
, a collection
\mathcal{X}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
of
n
vectors, and a subset
S
of
[n]
of size
|S|=n-f
.
Then, there holds for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
\alpha>0

(6)

\|x-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\left((1+\alpha)\,s(x)\;+\;\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\Sigma_{S}\right).

Proof.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

U(x):=S\cap\mathcal{N}(x),

where
\mathcal{N}(x)
is the set of the
n-f
nearest points to
x
in
\mathcal{X}
.
Since
|S|=|\mathcal{N}(x)|=n-f
, and
|S\cap\mathcal{N}(x)|\leq n
, we have

|U(x)|=|S\cup\mathcal{N}(x)|=|S|+|\mathcal{N}(x)|-|S\cap\mathcal{N}(x)|\geq n-2f.

We decompose

\|x-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{|U(x)|}\sum_{j\in U(x)}\|x-x_{j}+x_{j}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}.

Applying Young’s inequality (
A.1
) with a parameter
\alpha>0

and points

and

, we get

(7)

\displaystyle\|x-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\alpha)\frac{1}{|U(x)|}\sum_{j\in U(x)}\|x-x_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\frac{1}{|U(x)|}\sum_{j\in U(x)}\|x_{j}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}.

The first term is controlled using

:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|U(x)|}

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in U(x)}\|x-x_{j}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{|U(x)|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(x)}\|x-x_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-f}{|U(x)|}s(x)\leq\frac{n-f}{n-2f}s(x).

The second term is controlled using

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|U(x)|}

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in U(x)}\|x_{j}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{|U(x)|}\sum_{j\in S}\|x_{j}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-f}{|U(x)|}\Sigma_{S}\leq\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\Sigma_{S}.

The result follows by plugging the bounds on the two terms in
Eq.

7
.
∎

Theorem 5
.

Consider integers
f
,
n
and
m
such that
n-2f>0
,
0<m\leq n-f
, a collection
\mathcal{X}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
of
n

S
of
[n]
of size
|S|=n-f

Then, the robustness coefficient of
m
-MultiKrum admits the following upper-bound

\kappa^{\star}_{m}\leq\kappa_{1}\triangleq(\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}\frac{n-f}{n-2f}.

Proof.

We first observe that, by Jensen’s inequality,

\|\bar{x}_{S_{m}^{\star}}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}=\Big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}}(x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S})\Big\|^{2}\overset{\eqref{eq:Jensen}}{\leq}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}

Averaging the bound of
Lemma

3
, for
i
in
S_{m}^{\star}
, and then using
Lemma

2
, we obtain for any
\alpha>0

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:control-xi-xS}}{\leq}\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\left((1+\alpha)\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}}s(x_{i})\;+\;\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\Sigma_{S}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:mkra-ineq-final}}{\leq}\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\left(2(1+\alpha)+\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\right)\Sigma_{S}

In view of

\|\bar{x}_{S_{m}^{\star}}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}\leq\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\left(\sqrt{2}+1\right)^{2}\Sigma_{S},

which provides the claimed bound on
\kappa
.
∎

Theorem 6
.

Consider integers
f
,
n
and
m
such that
n-2f>0
,
0<m\leq n-f
, a collection
\mathcal{X}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
of
n
vectors, and a subset
S
of
[n]
of size
|S|=n-f
.
Then, the robustness coefficient of
m
-MultiKrum admits the following upper-bound

\kappa_{m}^{\star}\leq\kappa_{2}(m)=\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\begin{cases}\kappa_{2}^{<}(m)&\text{if }m\leq f\\
\kappa_{2}^{>}(m)&\text{else}\end{cases}

with

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}^{<}(m)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sqrt{n-2f}}{\sqrt{m}}+\sqrt{2}+1\right)^{\!2},

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}^{>}(m)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sqrt{n-2f}}{\sqrt{m}}+\frac{\sqrt{2f}}{\sqrt{m}}+\frac{f}{m}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

Let
S_{m}^{\star}
denote the subset of
m
indices selected by MultiKrum and write

u:=|S_{m}^{\star}\setminus S|,\qquad v:=|S_{m}^{\star}\cap S|,\qquad u+v=m.

Necessarily
0\leq u\leq\min(m,f)
and
\max(0,m-f)\leq v\leq m
.
For any
\alpha_{1}>0
,
Lemma

A.1
gives the decomposition

\displaystyle\|\bar{x}_{S_{m}^{\star}}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}}(x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S})\Big\|^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:Young}}{\leq}\left(1+{\alpha_{1}}\right)A+\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}\right)B,

where

A=\Big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}\cap S}(x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S})\Big\|^{2}
and

B=\Big\|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}\setminus S}(x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S})\Big\|^{2}.

To bound
A
, we first invoke Jensen’s inequality, and then use that
S_{m}^{\star}\cap S\subseteq S
:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{2}}{m^{2}}\Big\|\frac{1}{v}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}\cap S}(x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S})\Big\|^{2}\overset{\eqref{eq:Jensen}}{\leq}\frac{v}{m^{2}}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}\cap S}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{v}{m^{2}}\sum_{i\in S}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}=\frac{v(n-f)}{m^{2}}\,\Sigma_{S}.

To bound
B
, we first apply Jensen’s inequality, and then invoke
Lemma

3
: there holds for any
\alpha_{2}>0

\displaystyle B=\frac{u^{2}}{m^{2}}\Big\|\frac{1}{u}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}\setminus S}(x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S})\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:Jensen}}{\leq}\frac{u}{m^{2}}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}\setminus S}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:control-xi-xS}}{\leq}\frac{u}{m^{2}}\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}\setminus S}\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\left(\left(1+\alpha_{2}\right)s(x_{i})+\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}\right)\Sigma_{S}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{m^{2}}\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\left(\left(1+\alpha_{2}\right)\sum_{i\in S_{m}^{\star}\setminus S}s(x_{i})+\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}\right)u\Sigma_{S}\right).

The MultiKrum selection picks indices with small local scores:

, and thus

Substituting this bound into the previous display gives

Minimizing the bracket with respect to

yields the explicit minimizer

, and the minimal value equals

B\leq\frac{u}{m^{2}}\frac{n-f}{n-2f}(\sqrt{2m}+\sqrt{u})^{2}\,\Sigma_{S}.

Inserting the bounds on

into

(the optimal choice being

with minimal value

\kappa_{2}(m,u)\triangleq{}\frac{n-f}{m^{2}}\Bigg(\sqrt{v}+(\sqrt{2m}+\sqrt{u})\sqrt{\frac{u}{n-2f}}\Bigg)^{\!2},

Using that

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}(m,u)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{2}(m)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{n-f}{m^{2}}\Bigg(\sqrt{m}+\left(\sqrt{2m}+\sqrt{u_{\max}}\right)\sqrt{\frac{u_{\max}}{n-2f}}\Bigg)^{\!2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-f}{m}\Bigg(1+\left(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{\frac{u_{\max}}{m}}\right)\sqrt{\frac{u_{\max}}{n-2f}}\Bigg)^{\!2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\left(\sqrt{n-2f}+\sqrt{2}\sqrt{u_{\max}}+\frac{u_{\max}}{\sqrt{m}}\right)^{\!2}.

Finally, note that, when
m\leq f
,

, so that
\kappa_{2}(m)
simpifies to

\kappa_{2}^{<}(m)\triangleq\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\left(\frac{\sqrt{n-2f}}{\sqrt{m}}+\sqrt{2}+1\right)^{\!2}.

When

simpifies to

\kappa_{2}^{>}(m)\triangleq\frac{1}{m}\frac{n-f}{n-2f}\left(\sqrt{n-2f}+\sqrt{2f}+\frac{f}{\sqrt{m}}\right)^{\!2}.\qed

6.
Concluding remarks

In this work, we study the robustness of aggregation rules for mean estimation in the presence of adversaries. We introduce the robustness coefficient
\kappa^{\star}
, an optimal measure that refines existing notions of robustness.

We provide the first robustness guarantees for MultiKrum by deriving upper and lower bounds on its robustness coefficient, and, as a by-product, improve the best-known bounds for Krum. Our analysis reveals that the upper bound decays with the aggregation parameter
m
, in line with empirical observations suggesting increased robustness of MultiKrum and its practical preference over Krum.

Finally, we open the problem of computing, or tightly bounding, the robustness coefficients
\kappa^{\star}
of other classical aggregation rules, such as the geometric median, the trimmed mean, and the coordinate-wise median.

Appendix A
Recalls on elementary relations

We recall two elementary lemma: a known consequence of Young’s inequality, and Jensen’s inequality for the squared norm.

Lemma A.1
.

There holds, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha>0

(A.1)

\|x+y\|^{2}\leq\left(1+{\alpha}\right)\|x\|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\|y\|^{2}.

Proof.

Young’s inequality guarantees that, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha>0
, there holds

2\langle x,y\rangle\leq\alpha\|x\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|y\|^{2}
.
Recall that this follows directly from expanding the square in the identity
\|\sqrt{\alpha}x-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}y\|^{2}\geq 0
.
The claimed bound follows from developing the square:

\displaystyle\|x+y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x\|^{2}+2\langle x,y\rangle+\|y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\alpha\right)\|x\|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\|y\|^{2}.\qed

Lemma A.2

(Jensen’s inequality)
.

Consider
p
points
x_{1},\dots,x_{p}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
p
positive reals
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p}
.
Then,

(A.2)

\left\|\frac{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}x_{i}}{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\|x_{i}\|^{2}}{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}}

Proof.

Follows from convexity of the squared norm applied with weights
\lambda_{i}/\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}
.
∎

Appendix B
A Lower bound for
m
-MultiKrum,
m
arbitrary

Proposition B.1

(Lower bound on
\kappa_{m}^{\star}
for the
m
-MultiKrum aggregator)
.

Consider integers
f,n,m
such that
n-3f>0
and
0<m\leq n-f
.
Then, the robustness parameter
\kappa_{m}^{\star}
satisfies

\kappa_{m}^{\star}\geq R(n,f,m),

where, setting

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=a(n,f,m)=\min(m,n-2f)

we have

R(n,f,m)\triangleq\frac{(n-f)^{2}}{f(n-2f)}\left(\frac{a\cdot\frac{f}{n-f}+m-a}{m}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

Let
e=e_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the first canonical basis vector. Define
\mathcal{X}=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
by

\begin{cases}f\text{ vectors }&=-e,\\
n-2f\text{ vectors }&=0,\\
f\text{ vectors }&=(1-\varepsilon)e,\end{cases}

with
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily small. The MultiKrum scores satisfy, for
1\leq i\leq f
,
f+1\leq j\leq n-f
, and
n-f+1\leq k\leq n

s(i)=\frac{n-2f}{n-f},\quad s(j)=\frac{f}{n-f},\quad s(k)=(1-\epsilon)\frac{n-2f}{n-f}.

When
(n-2f)\geq f
, the
m
-MultiKrum selects first
a
zeros, then
(m-a)
vectors
(1+\epsilon)e
.

\displaystyle S_{m}^{\star}=\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{a},\underbrace{(1-\varepsilon)e,\ldots,(1-\varepsilon)e}_{m-a}\}.

Consider the reference set

S=\{\underbrace{-e,\ldots,-e}_{f},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n-2f}\},\quad|S|=n-f.

Their barycenters satisfy

\bar{x}_{S_{m}^{\star}}=\frac{m-a}{m}(1-\varepsilon)e,\quad\bar{x}_{S}=-\frac{f}{n-f}e.

The squared bias is

\|\bar{x}_{S_{m}^{\star}}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}=\left(\frac{a\frac{f}{n-f}+(m-a)(1-\varepsilon)}{m}\right)^{2}.

The variance of

is

\Sigma_{S}=\frac{f(n-2f)}{(n-f)^{2}}.

Letting

, we obtain

R(n,f,m)=\frac{\|\bar{x}_{S_{m}^{\star}}-\bar{x}_{S}\|^{2}}{\Sigma_{S}}=\frac{(n-f)^{2}}{f(n-2f)}\left(\frac{a\frac{f}{n-f}+m-a}{m}\right)^{2},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Stopping power of electron liquid for slow quantum projectiles
Vladimir U. Nazarov, E. K. U. Gross

Abstract
We revisit the problem of deceleration of a charge moving in a medium. Going beyond the traditional approach, which relies on Ehrenfest dynamics, we treat the projectile fully quantum mechanically, on the same footing as the electrons of the target. In order to separate the dynamics of the projectile from that of the electrons, we employ the Exact Factorization method. We illustrate the resulting theory by applying it to the problem of the stopping power (SP) of a jellium-model metal for slowly moving charges. The quantum mechanical nature of particles manifests itself remarkably in the differences in the SP for projectiles of the same charge moving with the same velocity, but having different masses.



I
Introduction

Time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT)
Zangwill and Soven 1980
;
Runge and Gross 1984
in combination with Ehrenfest dynamics is
a powerful method of the
theoretical study of the stopping of ions in matter.
Methodologically, it is useful to categorize several major regimes and first-principles approaches to the slowing of a charge in a medium:

(I)
High-velocity projectiles or weak projectile-target interaction
.
In this case, the problem of the stopping power (SP) (the kinetic energy loss by a projectile per its unit path length) can be solved quite generally to the first Born approximation and be put in terms of the dielectric response of the target. For a homogeneous medium, the solution is (see, e.g., Ref.
Echenique, Nieminen, and Ritchie 1981
)

S(v)\equiv-\frac{dE_{kin}}{ds}=\frac{2Z^{2}}{\pi v^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\omega d\omega\int\limits_{\omega/v}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{k}{\rm Im}\,\left(-\frac{1}{\epsilon(k,\omega)}\right),

(1)

where
E_{kin}
,
Z
, and
v
are the kinetic energy, the charge, and the velocity of the projectile, respectively,
and
\epsilon(k,\omega)
is the wave-vector and frequency-dependent dielectric function of the medium.
The problem remaining to be solved within this approach is the determination of

\epsilon(k,\omega)
, which is done with the use of the linear-response TDDFT, employing some of the available approximations to the exchange-correlation (xc) kernel
f_{xc}(k,\omega)
.
Gross and Kohn 1985

While the first Born approximation severely limits the range of applicability of this approach, its advantage is that Eq. (
1
) can be readily generalized for arbitrary non-uniform target systems.

(II)
Low-velocity regime
.
For the target of the homogeneous electron gas (HEG) (but only for this) the fully nonlinear solution of the SP problem for a slow projectile reads

Q_{1}=\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{S(v)}{v}=\bar{n}\,k_{F}\sigma_{tr}(k_{F}),

(2)

where
Q_{1}
is known as the friction coefficient,
\bar{n}
is the density of the HEG, and
\sigma_{tr}(k_{F})
is the transport scattering cross-section of electron at the Fermi momentum level
k_{F}
.
Finneman 1968
;
Ferrell and Ritchie 1977
While the potential at which the scattering in Eq. (
2
) occurs was
originally approximated by some model,
it was further suggested to use the first-principles static Kohn-Sham
Kohn and Sham 1965
(KS) DFT potential of a point charge immersed in the electron gas.
Echenique et al. 1986

(III) It was later realized that Eq. (
2
) is incomplete,
describing the binary-collisions (single-particle) contribution to the friction coefficient only (as indicated here by the subscript ‘1’ at
Q
in Eq. (
2
)). Another contribution, which Eq. (
2
) misses, is the dynamic xc one
Nazarov et al. 2005
;
Nazarov et al. 2007
;
Nazarov et al. 2008

Q_{2}=-\int[\hat{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\nabla n_{0}(\mathbf{r})]\left.\frac{\partial{\rm Im}\,f_{xc}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\omega)}{\partial\omega}\right|_{\omega=0}[\hat{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\nabla^{\prime}n_{0}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})]d\mathbf{r}\,d\mathbf{r}^{\prime},

(3)

where
\hat{\mathbf{v}}
is the unit vector in the direction of the velocity, and
n_{0}(\mathbf{r})

inhomogeneous system of EG with the statically inserted projectile charge in it
.
The total friction coefficient is then given by

Q=Q_{1}+Q_{2}.

(4)

We note that Eqs. (
2
)-(
4
) are formally exact within the low-velocity regime, while approximations are invoked
in calculations, when choosing specific functionals for the static KS potential
v_{KS}(\mathbf{r})
and the dynamic xc kernel
f_{xc}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\omega)
.

(IV) The problem of the SP can, quite generally, be treated by means of real-time TDDFT.
This approach neither imposes limitations on the type of the target system nor on the velocity of the ion, and it has now become a standard first-principles technique for large-scale SP calculations in crystals.
Baer and Siam 2004
;
Pruneda et al. 2007

All the approaches briefly reviewed above consider a projectile moving as a point charge according to the laws of classical mechanics.
Since there exist no classical particles in nature, the purpose of this work is to develop a theory of SP taking into account quantum mechanical effects on the part of projectiles, and to analyse their comparative importance.
Specifically, within the traditional classical approach, for given charge and velocity of a projectile, the SP, obviously, does not depend on the mass of the latter. We will see that this is not the case within the quantum treatment.

The organization of this paper is the following.
In Sec.
II

To illustrate the general theory by a simple calculation,
in Sec.
III

The latter approximation allows for the reformulation of the quantum SP problem in terms of TDDFT, which is done in Sec.
IV
.
In Sec.
V
we focus on the low-velocity SP, developing the linear-response TDDFT approach to this problem
(our small parameter is the velocity, not the projectile-target interaction!).
In Sec.
VI
we present and discuss results of calculations. Section
VII
contains conclusions.
Lengthy derivations are moved to Appendices.

For the sake of maximal clarity, we consider a jellium model of a metal with a fixed positive charge background density

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(n)}(\mathbf{R},t)=|\chi(\mathbf{R},t)|^{2},

(14)

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}^{(n)}(\mathbf{R},t)=\frac{\hbar}{M}{\rm Im}\,[\chi^{*}(\mathbf{R},t)\nabla_{\mathbf{R}}\chi(\mathbf{R},t)]+\frac{1}{M}\mathbf{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbf{R},t)\Gamma^{(n)}(\mathbf{R},t).

TDDFT reformulation

The electronic motion problem (
21
)-(
23

Indeed, the time-dependent KS equations for electronic orbitals can be written as

\begin{split}i\hbar\partial_{t}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{r},t)&=\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}^{2}+\int\frac{e^{2}[n^{(e)}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t)-\bar{n}]}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+V_{xc}^{(e)}(\mathbf{r},t)\right.\\
&\left.-Z\int\frac{e^{2}n^{(n)}(\mathbf{R},t)}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}|}d\mathbf{R}+V^{(e)}_{ext}(\mathbf{r},t)\right]\phi_{i}(\mathbf{r},t),\end{split}

(27)

n^{(e)}(\mathbf{r},t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{e}}|\phi_{i}(\mathbf{r},t)|^{2},

(28)

and solved mutually-consistently with the projectile’s motion problem (
17
)-(
19

IV.1
Ground state

As the initial condition, in the following we will need the ground-state solution for the system of the projectile at rest in the electron gas.
We solve the electronic ground-state DFT problem

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}^{2}\!+\!\!\int\!\frac{e^{2}[n^{(e)}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\!-\!\bar{n}]}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\!+\!V_{xc}^{(e)}(\mathbf{r})\!-\!Z\!\!\int\frac{e^{2}n^{(n)}(\mathbf{R})}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}|}d\mathbf{R}\right]\phi_{i}(\mathbf{r})=\epsilon_{i}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{r}),

(29)

\displaystyle n^{(e)}_{0}(\mathbf{r})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{e}}|\phi_{i}(\mathbf{r})|^{2},

(30)

together with the corresponding nuclear problem

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2M}\nabla_{\mathbf{R}}^{2}+V^{(n)}_{0}(\mathbf{R})\right]\chi_{0}(\mathbf{R})=E_{0}\chi_{0}(\mathbf{R}),

(31)

\displaystyle V^{(n)}_{0}(\mathbf{R})=-Z\int\frac{e^{2}[n^{(e)}_{0}(\mathbf{r})-\bar{n}]}{|\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{r}|}d\mathbf{r}.

(32)

V
Low-velocity stopping power

Below we focus on slowly moving projectiles, in which regime more significant quantum effects can be expected.

V.1
Method

To make the charge move, we apply to it a weak external potential

V^{(n)}_{ext}(\mathbf{R},t)=-(\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{R})\cos(\omega t).

(33)

To avoid the difficulties of the steady-state approach, this potential is taken time-dependent monochromatic.
Nazarov, Vignale, and Chang 2014

We solve the problem of SP perturbatively in powers of
\mathbf{E}
and, consequently, in powers of the velocity.
In the end, to relate our results to the constant velocity regime,
we take the
\omega\to 0
limit.
In Eq. (
26
), we find that the 1st order contribution in
\mathbf{E}
is zero identically

\left(\frac{dE_{kin}(t)}{dt}\right)_{1}=-\int V^{(n)}_{0}(\mathbf{R})\partial_{t}n^{(n)}_{1}(\mathbf{R},t)d\mathbf{R}=0,

(34)

(subscripts indicate orders in the expansion of the corresponding quantities in powers of
\mathbf{E}
).
Therefore, we are concerned with the 2nd order contribution

\left(\frac{dE_{kin}(t)}{dt}\right)_{2}=-\int V^{(n)}_{0}(\mathbf{R})\partial_{t}n^{(n)}_{2}(\mathbf{R},t)d\mathbf{R}-\int V^{(n)}_{1}(\mathbf{R},t)\partial_{t}n^{(n)}_{1}(\mathbf{R},t)d\mathbf{R},

(35)

V^{(n)}_{1}(\mathbf{R},t)=-(\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{R})\cos(\omega t)-Z\int\frac{e^{2}n^{(e)}_{1}(\mathbf{r},t)}{|\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{r}|}d\mathbf{r},

where the 1st term on the RHS of Eq. (
36

33

In Appendix

VII
Conclusions

Within the Exact Factorization approach, we have developed a theory of the stopping of charges moving in media,
when not only electrons of a medium, but also the projectile itself is treated quantum mechanically.
Using a simple approximation of
the mean-field time-dependent self-consistent field method (the uncorrelated projectile-electrons dynamics),
we have illustrated our theory by calculations of the friction coefficient for slowly moving charges in jellium-model metal.

We have identified a principle effect of the quantum mechanical approach to the stopping power problem: particles bearing the same charge and moving with the same velocity experience different friction depending on their respective masses.
The latter is a result of the differences in the wave packets’ sizes of particles with different masses, and this effect, obviously, cannot arise
within the classical Ehrenfest dynamics.

Appendix C
Density response functions

C.1
1st order

The first order response function is defined as

\delta n_{1}(\mathbf{r},t)=\int\chi_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t-t^{\prime})\delta v(\mathbf{r},t^{\prime})d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}dt^{\prime}.

(113)

It acts on a superposition of cosine and sine waves as

\begin{split}&\int\chi_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t-t^{\prime})[P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\cos(\omega t^{\prime})+Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\sin(\omega t^{\prime})]dt^{\prime}d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\\
&=\cos(\omega t)\int[{\rm Re}\,[\chi_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\omega)]P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})-{\rm Im}\,[\chi_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\omega)]Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})]d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\\
&+\sin(\omega t)\int[{\rm Im}\,[\chi_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\omega)]P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})+{\rm Re}\,[\chi_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\omega)]Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})]d\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\end{split}

(114)

where
\chi_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\omega)
is Fourier transform of
\chi_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t-t^{\prime})
.

The Lehmann representation
holds

\chi_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\omega)=\langle 0|\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})|n\rangle\langle n|\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})|0\rangle\left[\frac{1}{E_{0}-E_{n}-\omega-i\eta}-\frac{1}{E_{n}-E_{0}-\omega-i\eta}\right],

(115)

where
|n\rangle
and
E_{n}
are many-body wave-functions and energies, respectively. Summation is implied over repeated state’s symbols.

C.2
2nd order

The second order response function is defined as

\delta n_{2}(\mathbf{r},t)=\int\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},t-t^{\prime},t-t^{\prime\prime})\delta v(\mathbf{r},t^{\prime})\delta v(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime})d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}d\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}dt^{\prime}dt^{\prime\prime}.

(116)

It acts on a superposition of cosine and sine waves as

\begin{split}&\int dt^{\prime}dt^{\prime\prime}\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},t-t^{\prime},t-t^{\prime\prime})[P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\cos(\omega t^{\prime})+Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\sin(\omega t^{\prime})]\\
&\times[P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})\cos(\omega t^{\prime\prime})+Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})\sin(\omega t^{\prime\prime})]=\frac{\cos(2\omega t)}{2}C(\mathbf{r},t)+\frac{\sin(2\omega t)}{2}S(\mathbf{r},t)+\frac{1}{2}D(\mathbf{r},t),\end{split}

(117)

\displaystyle C(\mathbf{r},t)=

\displaystyle\int\!\!\left[{\rm Re}\,\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},\omega,\omega)[P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})\!-\!Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})]\!-\!{\rm Im}\,\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},\omega,\omega)[P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})\!+\!Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})]\right]\!d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}d\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle S(\mathbf{r},t)=

\displaystyle\int\!\!\left[{\rm Im}\,\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},\omega,\omega)[P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})\!-\!Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})]\!+\!{\rm Re}\,\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},\omega,\omega)[P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})\!+\!Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})]\right]\!d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}d\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle D(\mathbf{r},t)=

\displaystyle\int\!\!\left[{\rm Re}\,\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},\omega,-\omega)[P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})\!+\!Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})]\!+\!{\rm Im}\,\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},\omega,-\omega)[Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})\!-\!P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})Q(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})]\right]\!d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}d\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},

where
\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},\omega,\omega_{1})
is a double Fourier transform of
\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},t-t^{\prime},t-t^{\prime\prime})

\begin{split}&\chi_{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\times\\

&\left.+\frac{\langle n|\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})|0\rangle\langle 0|\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime})|m\rangle\langle m|\hat{n}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})|n\rangle}{(E_{0}-E_{m}-\omega_{2}-i\eta)(E_{0}-E_{n}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}-i\eta)}+(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime},\omega_{1}\leftrightarrow\omega_{2})\right]\end{split}

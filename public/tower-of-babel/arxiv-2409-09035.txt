The Reverse Order Law and the Riccati Equation
Oskar Kędzierski

Abstract
We give a full analytic solution to a particular case of the algebraic Riccati equation X ​ W ​ W ∗ ​ W ​ X = W ∗ XWW^{*}WX=W^{*} for any matrix W W (possibly non-square or non-symmetric) in using the Schur method, terms of the SVD decomposition of W W . In particular, ( W ​ X ) 3 = W ​ X (WX)^{3}=WX and ( X ​ W ) 3 = X ​ W (XW)^{3}=XW for any solution X X . We show that for W = A ​ B W=AB , matrix X = B + ​ A + X={B}^{+}\!{A}^{+}\! is a solution of this equation if and only if the reverse order law holds, i.e., ( A ​ B ) + = B + ​ A + {(AB)}^{+}\!={B}^{+}\!{A}^{+}\! . For a Hermitian and invertible W W the maximal and stabilizing Hermitian solutions is shown to be equal to W + {W}^{+}\! . Equivalence to the equation X ​ W ​ X = W + XWX={W}^{+}\! is proven.

Preliminaries

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
W\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n}
. Then any eigenvector
w\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
of the matrix,

M=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&WW^{*}W\\
\hline\cr W^{*}&0\\
\end{array}\right],

which is not in the kernel of
M
, is of the form

w=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
\hline\cr\pm\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}v\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{K}^{m+n},

where
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
is a singular value of
W
. In addition,
u
is a left singular vector of
W
and
v
is the corresponding right singular vector of
W
, that is,
W^{*}u=\sigma v
.

Proof.

Let
r=\rank W
and let
W=U\Sigma V^{*}
be an SVD decomposition of matrix
W
over
\mathbb{K}
. Then
WW^{*}W=U\Sigma\Sigma^{*}\Sigma V^{*}
.
Assume that
w=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}w_{1}\\
\hline\cr w_{2}\end{array}\right]
is an eigenvector of matrix
M
, that is
Mw=\lambda w
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
and
w\neq 0
. Then

\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&U\Sigma\Sigma^{*}\Sigma V^{*}\\
\hline\cr V^{*}\Sigma^{*}U&0\\
\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}w_{1}\\
\hline\cr w_{2}\end{array}\right]=\lambda\left[\begin{array}[]{c}w_{1}\\
\hline\cr w_{2}\end{array}\right],

implies that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}U\Sigma\Sigma^{*}\Sigma V^{*}w_{2}&=&\lambda w_{1},\\
V\Sigma^{*}U^{*}w_{1}&=&\lambda w_{2}.\end{array}\right.

Assume that
\lambda\neq 0
and substitute the second equation to the first one,

U(\Sigma\Sigma^{*})^{2}U^{*}w_{1}=\lambda^{2}w_{1},

where
(\Sigma\Sigma^{*})^{2}=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{1}^{4},\ldots,\sigma_{r}^{4},0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times m}
.
That is
\lambda=\pm\sigma^{2}
, where
\sigma=\sigma_{i}
for some
i=1,\ldots,r
, and
w_{1}=u
is some left singular vector of the matrix
W
corresponding to the singular value
\sigma
(it is not necessarily a multiple of a column of matrix
U
when
\sigma
is a multiple singular value). From the second equation

V\Sigma^{*}U^{*}u=\pm\sigma^{2}w_{2}\Longrightarrow w_{2}=\pm\frac{1}{\sigma}v.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
W\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n}
. Then matrix

M=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&WW^{*}W\\
\hline\cr W^{*}&0\\
\end{array}\right],

is diagonalizable over
\mathbb{K}
.

Proof.

Let
r=\rank W
and let
W=U\Sigma V^{*}
be an SVD decomposition of matrix
W
over
\mathbb{K}
. Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\in\mathbb{K}^{m}
be the corresponding left singular vectors of
W
, that is, first
r
columns of matrix
U
. Similarly, let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
be the corresponding right singular vectors of
W
, that is, the first
r
columns of
V
.
Then the
2r
vectors

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u_{i}\\
\hline\cr\pm\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}v_{i}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{K}^{m+n},

are eigenvectors of
M
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\pm\sigma_{i}^{2}
, as
Wv_{i}=\sigma_{i}u_{i}
and
W^{*}u_{i}=\sigma_{i}v_{i}
.
for
i=1,\ldots,\rank W
. The null space of
M
contains vectors of the form

w=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}w_{1}\\
\hline\cr w_{2}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{K}^{m+n},\quad\text{where}\quad w_{1}\in\mathcal{N}(W^{*}),w_{2}\in\mathcal{N}(W).

This gives in total
2r+(m-r)+(n-r)=m+n
linearly independent vectors and
M
is an
(m+n)
-by-
(m+n)
matrix. Therefore
\mathcal{N}(M)=\mathcal{N}(W^{*})\times\mathcal{N}(W)
and
M
is diagonalizable.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
W\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n}
and let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{K}^{m+n}
be an invariant
m
-dimensional subspace of matrix
M
,
such that the projection
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is surjective when restricted to
\mathcal{V}
. Then for any
i=1,\ldots,t

\dim\mathcal{V}_{(\sigma_{i}^{2})}+\dim\mathcal{V}_{(-\sigma_{i}^{2})}=\dim V_{WW^{*},(\sigma_{i})}.

Moreover,
\dim\mathcal{V}_{(0)}=m-r
.

Proof.

Restriction of a diagonalizable matrix to an invariant subspace is diagonalizable, cf.
[
6
, Chapter XIV, Ex. 13]
(minimal polynomial of an endomorphism is divisible by a minimal polynomial of its restriction to an invariant subspace). Therefore,
\mathcal{V}
as a direct sum of eigenspaces (a priori, some possibly equal to 0)

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{(\sigma_{1}^{2})}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{(-\sigma_{1}^{2})}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{V}_{(\sigma_{t}^{2})}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{(-\sigma_{t}^{2})}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{(0)},

where
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
are (unlike in Lemma
3.2
) pairwise distinct singular values of matrix
W
. and let
s_{i}=\dim V_{WW^{*},(\sigma_{i})}
be the multiplicity of
\sigma_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,t
.
By definition (cf. the proof of Lemma
3.2
) and the surjectivity assumption

\pi\left(\mathcal{V}_{(\sigma_{i}^{2})}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{(-\sigma_{i}^{2})}\right)=V_{WW^{*},(\sigma_{i})},

\pi\left(\mathcal{V}_{(0)}\right)=V_{WW^{*},(0)}=\mathcal{N}(W^{*}).

The dimension of the image of a subspace does not increase, therefore

\dim{\mathcal{V}_{(\sigma_{i}^{2})}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{(-\sigma_{i}^{2})}}\geq\dim V_{WW^{*},(\sigma_{i})},

\dim\mathcal{V}_{(0)}\geq\dim V_{WW^{*},(0)}.

Neither of those inequalities may be strict as both sides sum to
m
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For any matrices
A\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times k},B\in\mathbb{K}^{k\times n}

\rank(\pinv{B}\pinv{A})=\rank(AB)=\rank\left({\left(AB\right)}^{*}\right)=\rank\left({\left(AB\right)}^{+}\!\right).

Proof.

Since
A^{*}=A^{+}AA^{*}
and
B^{*}=B^{*}BB^{+}
(for example, by the SVD decomposition)

B^{*}A^{*}=B^{*}B\left(B^{+}A^{+}\right)AA^{*}
,
and therefore
\rank(B^*A^*)\leq\rank(B^+A^+)
.
Similarly

{A}^{+}\!=A^{*}{\left(A^{*}\right)}^{+}\!{A}^{+}\!,\quad{B}^{+}\!={B}^{+}\!\left({B}^{+}\!\right)^{*}B^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad{B}^{+}\!{A}^{+}\!={B}^{+}\!\left({B}^{+}\!\right)^{*}\left(B^{*}A^{*}\right){\left(A^{*}\right)}^{+}\!{A}^{+}\!

so
\rank(B^*A^*)\geq\rank(B^+A^+)
.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
W\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n}
. Then
X\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times m}
solves the equation (

\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&WW^{*}W\\
\hline\cr W^{*}&0\\
\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}I\\
\hline\cr X\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}I\\
\hline\cr X\end{array}\right]WW^{*}WX.

(3)

Moreover, solutions of equation (
2

m

\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{K}^{(m+n)\times(m+n)}
of matrix
M=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&WW^{*}W\\
\hline\cr W^{*}&0\\
\end{array}\right]
such that
\pi(\mathcal{V})=\mathbb{K}^{m}
under the projection
\pi\colon\mathbb{K}^{m}\times\mathbb{K}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}^{m}

Proof.

Only the second claim requires a proof. If

is a solution, then by equation (
3

\mathcal{V}
is given by the column space of matrix
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}I\\
\hline\cr X\end{array}\right]
. On the other hand let
\mathcal{V}
be an invariant subspace of

such that
\pi(\mathcal{V})=\mathbb{K}^{m}
. Fix a basis of
\mathcal{V}
and arrange itin columns of matrix

\hline\cr V_{2}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{K}^{(m+n)\times n}
. Then, by the assumption,
V_{1}
is invertible and the matrix
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}I\\
\hline\cr V_{2}V_{1}^{-1}\end{array}\right]
has the same invariant columns space as

\mathcal{V}
leads
to a matrix

which induces the same solution
X
.
∎

The following Lemma characterizes solutions of (

Lemma 6.1
.

WXW={X}^{+}\!\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{C}(W^{*})=\mathcal{C}(X)\Longleftrightarrow P_{\mathcal{N}(W)}YP_{\mathcal{N}(W^{*})}=0.

Proof.

Not that for any
X
we have
\mathcal{C}(W^{*})\subset\mathcal{C}(X)
by (
2
) and the summands in (
4
) are pairwise perpendicular and the first spans
\mathcal{C}(W^{*})
for any matrix

. This proves the second equivalence. The first condition holds iff the Penrose condition are satisfied, that is

X(WXW)X=X

ii)

(WXW)X(WXW)=WXW
,

iii)

X(WXW)=(XW)^{2}
is Hermitian,

iv)

(WXW)X=(WX)^{2}
is Hermitian.

By Corollary
5.2
conditions

hold and condition

hold as
(WXW)X(WXW)=(WX)^{3}W=WXW
. Condition

holds iff

, that is, iff
\mathcal{C}(X)\subset\mathcal{C}(W^{*})
. This proves the equivalence of the first and the second condition.
∎

Convergence of higher derivatives of random polynomials with independent roots
Jürgen Angst 1 , Oanh Nguyen 2 and Guillaume Poly 3

Abstract
. Let μ \mu be a probability measure on ℂ {\mathbb{C}} , and let P n P_{n} be the random polynomial whose zeros are sampled independently from μ \mu . We study the asymptotic distribution of zeros of high-order derivatives of P n P_{n} . We show that, for large classes of measures μ \mu , the empirical distribution of zeros of the k k -th derivative converges back to μ \mu for all derivative orders k = o ⁡ ( n / log ⁡ n ) k=o(n/\log n) . This includes all discrete measures and a broad family of measures satisfying a mild dimension-nondegeneracy condition. We further establish a robustness result showing that, for arbitrary μ \mu , even after adding a vanishing proportion of roots drawn from a dimension-nondegenerate perturbation, the derivative zero measures still converge back to μ \mu . These results break the previously known logarithmic barrier on the order of differentiation and demonstrate that the limiting root distribution is preserved under differentiation of order growing nearly linearly with the degree.



1.
Introduction

In the deterministic setting, the relation between the roots of a polynomial and those of its derivatives remains surprisingly intricate. Classical results such as the Gauss–Lucas theorem give only broad geometric constraints, and several fundamental questions about the behavior of critical points are still open; see, for example,
[
Tao22
,
vBLSvdW07
]
. Because the deterministic theory is difficult to push much further, it is natural to turn to randomness as a way to uncover underlying patterns. In this context, differentiation blends the influence of all zeros, so the critical points capture the overall shape of the root configuration. Random polynomials therefore provide a flexible setting in which this interaction becomes amenable to analysis and connects naturally with ideas from complex analysis and potential theory. Understanding the asymptotic distribution of critical points thus becomes both tractable and conceptually compelling once the random root configuration is specified. Let us specify the model we consider throughout the article and described the main related literature.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots
be i.i.d. random variables with distribution
\mu
.
For each
n\geq 1
, define the random polynomial

P_{n}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\xi_{j}).

By the Law of Large Numbers, the empirical measure
\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\xi_{i}}
converges almost surely to
\mu
as
n\to\infty
. Pemantle and Rivin
[
PR13
]
conjectured that the empirical distribution of the critical points of
P_{n}
would also converge to
\mu
.
Specifically, if

\mu_{P_{n}^{\prime}}:=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{C}:P_{n}^{\prime}(z)=0}\delta_{z}

denotes the empirical measure of
P_{n}^{\prime}
, then
\mu_{P_{n}^{\prime}}
converges to
\mu
as
n\to\infty
.
They proved this under the additional assumption that
\mu
has finite
1
-energy, and Subramanian
[
Sub12
]
established the result when
\mu
is supported on the unit circle.

A major advance was achieved by Kabluchko
[
Kab15
]
, who confirmed the conjecture in full generality for all probability measures
\mu
, asserting that
\mu_{P_{n}^{\prime}}
converges in probability to
\mu
in the space of complex probability measures equipped with the topology of the convergence in distribution. Answering a question raised by Kabluchko, together with Malicet, the first and third author
[
AMP24
]
proved that in fact,
\mu_{P_{n}^{\prime}}
converges almost surely to
\mu
in the same topology of convergence in distribution. We note that the former is equivalent to saying that for all bounded continuous test functions
\varphi
,

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi(x)\,d\mu_{P_{n}^{\prime}}(x)\;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}}\;\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi(x)\,d\mu(x),

while the latter is equivalent to

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi(x)\,d\mu_{P_{n}^{\prime}}(x)\;\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}\;\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi(x)\,d\mu(x).

The study of higher derivatives followed naturally.
For
1\leq k<n
, define

\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}:=\frac{1}{n-k}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{C}:P_{n}^{(k)}(z)=0}\delta_{z}.

Byun, Lee, and Reddy
[
BLR22
]
showed that for each fixed
k
, one still has
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}
converges to
\mu
in probability.
O’Rourke and Steinerberger
[
OS21
]
proposed a broader conjectural picture for large derivatives: for
t\in(0,1)
, the empirical measure of the zeros of
P_{n}^{(\lfloor tn\rfloor)}
converges to a deterministic limit
\mu_{t}
, whose logarithmic potential should solve a certain PDE.
This PDE has connections to earlier work
[
ALN22
,
KT22
]
and, in special cases, the conjecture has been verified when
\mu
has real support
[
HK23
]
using free probability.
In the case
t=0
, their prediction recovers
\mu_{t}=\mu
, leading to the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For all probability measures
\mu
on the complex plane, for any sequence
k_{n}
satisfying
k_{n}=o(n)
, it holds that almost surely,
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k_{n})}}
converges to
\mu
.

The current best result is due to Michelen–Vu who showed in
[
MV24b
]
that convergence in probability holds for all
k:=k_{n}\leq\frac{\log n}{5\log\log n}
and in
[
MV24a
]
that almost sure convergence holds for all fixed
k
. To see that
\log n
is a natural barrier for this kind of result, we first recall the standard distributional identity
\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta\log|z-\xi|=\delta_{\xi}
which connects the logarithmic potential
\frac{1}{n}\log|P_{n}(z)|
of
P_{n}
to its empirical measure:

\frac{1}{2\pi n}\Delta\log|P_{n}(z)|=\mu_{P_{n}}.

Now, if we look at the quotient

(1)

S_{n}=S_{k,n}(z):=\frac{P^{(k)}_{n}(z)}{k!P_{n}(z)},

the above relationship gives

\frac{1}{2\pi n}\Delta\log\left|S_{n}(z)\right|=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{C}:P_{n}^{(k)}(\xi)=0}\delta_{\xi}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{C}:P_{n}(\xi)=0}\delta_{\xi}=\frac{n-k}{n}\,\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}-\mu_{P_{n}}.

By the Law of Large Numbers, the empirical measure
\mu_{P_{n}}
converges to
\mu
as
n\to\infty
.
Consequently, when
k=o(n)
, establishing the convergence
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}\to\mu
reduces to proving that

\frac{1}{n}\log|S_{n}(z)|
tends to
0
. Controling the growth of
\log|S_{n}(z)|
amounts to proving an upper bound on
|S_{n}(z)|
and a lower bound on
|S_{n}(z)|
, which can be quantified as proving

{\mathbb{P}}(|S_{n}(z)|\leq e^{-\varepsilon n})

to be sufficiently small as
n\to\infty
.

Moreover, rewriting
S_{n}
as

S_{n}(z)=\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}}Y_{i_{1}}\dots Y_{i_{k}}

where
Y_{i}=\frac{1}{z-\xi_{i}}
are iid, we see that
S_{n}
is a degree
k
polynomials of the random variables
Y_{i}
. This directly connects this problem to the anticoncentration problem for degree-
k
polynomials of independent random variables.

The latter has been studied for its intrinsic interest and has applications in various areas, including random matrix theory and subgraph counting; see, for instance, Meka–Nguyen–Vu
[
MNV15
]
, Kwan–Sudakov–Tran
[
KST19
]
, Alon–Hefetz–Krivelevich–Tyomkyn

[
AHKT20
]
, and Fox–Kwan–Sauermann
[
FKS21
]
. The above list is far from exhaustive.
We note that
\log n
is a natural barrier for the degree, as can be seen in the following example of a degree
k
polynomials on
n=mk
random variables
(Y_{ij})

(2)

S=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i1}\dots Y_{ik}

where
i=1,\dots,m
,
j=1,\dots,k
and
Y_{ij}
are iid with any distribution having
{\mathbb{P}}(Y_{ij}=0)=q
for some
q\in(0,1)
(say
q=1/3
and
Y_{ij}
is uniform in
\{0,\pm 1\}
) and
m=(1-q)^{-k}
. This corresponds to
n=k(1-q)^{-k}
or
k=\Theta(\log n)
. We have

{\mathbb{P}}(S=0)\geq{\mathbb{P}}(Y_{i1}\dots Y_{ik}=0)^{m}=(1-(1-q)^{k})^{(1-q)^{-k}}\approx e^{-1}

which clearly does not decay to
0
.

Breaking this natural barrier, our main theorem shows that for non-pathological measures
\mu
, Conjecture
1.1
holds all the way to
k=o(n/\log n)
. Before stating the theorem, let us recall the notion of local dimension of a measure
\mu
at a given point
x\in{\mathbb{C}}
:

\underline{\dim}_{\mu}(x):=\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\mu(D(x,r))}{\log r}.

Definition 1.2

(Dimension-nondegenerate measures)
.

We say that a measure
\mu
is
dimension-nondegenerate
if there exists a measure
\nu\leq\mu
(namely
\nu(A)\leq\mu(A)
for all Borel sets
A
) and a set
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}
of positive measure
\nu(E)>0
such that for all
x\in E
,

\underline{\dim}_{\nu}(x)>0.

Theorem 1.3

(Main theorem)
.

Let
\mu
be an element in the space of probability measures on
{\mathbb{C}}
equipped with the topology of the convergence in distribution.

(1)

[Discrete measures] If
\mu
is discrete, then for any sequence
k_{n}=o(n)
, almost surely with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
, the sequence of measures
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k_{n})}}
converges to
\mu
as
n\to\infty
.

(2)

[Dimension-nondegenerate measures] Assume that
\mu
is dimension-nondegenerate then for any sequence
k_{n}=o(\frac{n}{\log n})
, almost surely with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
, the sequence of measures
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k_{n})}}
converges to
\mu
as
n\to\infty
.

Observe that if a measure
\mu
contains a dimension-nondegenerate component, that is,

\mu=(1-\varepsilon)\mu_{0}+\varepsilon\nu,\quad\varepsilon>0,

with
\nu
dimension-nondegenerate, then
\mu
itself is dimension-nondegenerate. Below, we list several examples of dimension-nondegenerate measures; any measure that includes such a component is automatically dimension-nondegenerate as well.

(1)

Measures not entirely singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

In the case that
\mu
is not entirely singular, by the classical Radon-Nikodym’s theorem, there exists a nonnegative, integrable function
f\not\equiv 0
such that
\mu=f(z)\,dm_{2}+\mu_{s}
where
m_{2}
is the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mu_{s}
is singular with respect to
m_{2}
. There exists an
M>0
such that
f_{M}:=f\text{1}_{f\leq M}\not\equiv 0
. We then let
\nu=f_{M}dm_{2}
and
E={\mathbb{C}}
. For all
x\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
r>0
, we then have

\nu(D(x,r))\leq\pi Mr^{2}

and as
\log r<0
for
r<1
, we get

\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\nu(D(x,r))}{\log r}\geq\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\log\pi Mr^{2}}{\log r}\geq 2>0

proving dimension-nondegeneracy.

(2)

Measures satisfying a local Frostman condition on a positive-mass set.

If there exist constants
s>0
,
C>0
, and a Borel set
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}

with
\mu(E)>0
such that

\mu(D(x,r))\leq Cr^{s}\quad\text{for all }x\in E\text{ and all sufficiently small }r>0,

then
\underline{\dim}_{\mu}(x)\geq s
for all
x\in E
.

(3)

Measures supported on smooth curves.

If
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to arc-length measure
on a
C^{1}
curve in
{\mathbb{C}}
, then
\underline{\dim}_{\mu}(x)=1
for

\mu
-a.e.
x
, hence
\mu
is dimension-nondegenerate (see for example
[
Mat99
]
). One of the most studied examples in this case is
\mu
being the uniform measure on the unit circle.

In the next theorem, although it is formally covered by
Theorem
1.3
, we present it separately because it is
proved using fundamentally different methods and highlights a
complementary mechanism of anti-concentration. Presenting different approaches illustrates the robustness of
the phenomenon and provides additional insight into how zeros of
high-order derivatives stabilize under different structural assumptions
on the measure. We say that
\mu
satisfies the Doeblin condition if there exist constants
c_{0},r_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that for any Borel measurable set
A\in B(z_{0},r_{0})
, it holds that

(3)

\mu(A)\geq c_{0}\text{Leb}(A).

In other words, a measure
\mu
satisfies the Doeblin condition if it includes a component that is uniform on some disk.

Recall the Cauchy-Stieljes transform
g_{\mu}
of the measure
\mu

(4)

g_{\mu}(z):=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{1}{z-u}d\mu(u).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mu
be an element in the space of probability measures on
{\mathbb{C}}
equipped with the topology of the convergence in distribution.

(1)

[Doeblin condition] Assume that
\mu
satisfies the Doeblin condition with parameters
c_{0},r_{0},z_{0}
. Then for any sequence
k_{n}=o(\frac{n}{\log n})
, almost surely with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
, the sequence of measures
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k_{n})}}
converges to
\mu
as
n\to\infty
.

(2)

[Generic measures] Assume that for Lebesgue-almost all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
g_{\mu}(z)\neq 0
and

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{1}{|z-u|^{2}}d\mu(u)<\infty
. Then for all
k=o(\sqrt{n})
the sequence of measures
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k_{n})}}
converges to
\mu
in probability.

In the general setting, for
any
probability measures
\mu
, our next theorem shows that if we perturb it by adding a small dimension-nondegenerate noise, namely we sample the roots from the perturb measure
\mu_{n}:=(1-\alpha_{n})\mu+\alpha_{n}\nu
where
\alpha_{n}=o(1)
and
\nu
is dimension-nondegenerate, then the roots of the corresponding polynomial
P_{n}^{(k)}
converges to
\mu
. This result can be viewed as complimentary to
[
BLR22
, Corollary 1.3]

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be two probability measures on
{\mathbb{C}}
. Assume that
\nu
is dimension-nondenegerate. Let
\alpha_{n}\in(0,1)
be a sequence of deterministic numbers satisfying
\lim\alpha_{n}=0
and
\lim\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\log n/n}=\infty
. Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
be iid samples from

\mu_{n}=(1-\alpha_{n})\mu+\alpha_{n}\nu

and let

P_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-\xi_{i}).

Let
k_{n}
be a sequence of integers satisfying
k_{n}=o(\frac{n\alpha_{n}}{\log n})
, then the sequence of measures
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k_{n})}}
converges to
\mu
as
n\to\infty
almost surely.

To interpret the theorem, in the perturbed setting, we see that most roots of
P_{n}
are sampled from
\mu
, while only a vanishing fraction
\alpha_{n}n
come from a dimension-nondegenerate component
\nu
.
By the Gauss–Lucas theorem, the zeros of
P_{n}^{(k_{n})}
are influenced by the logarithmic forces generated by all roots of
P_{n}
, including those coming from
\nu
.
Nevertheless, Theorem
1.5
shows that these additional roots do not alter the limiting behavior: despite the pulling effect induced by
\nu
, the zeros of
P_{n}^{(k_{n})}
still converge to
\mu
(see Figure
1
).
This robustness strongly suggests that the same convergence should hold even in the unperturbed case
\alpha_{n}\equiv 0
, where all roots are drawn from
\mu
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

To simplify notations, we will write
k=k_{n}
.

2.1.
Discrete measures

Firstly, we consider the case that
\mu
is supported on a set
\{z_{1},z_{2},\dots\}
, we note that a.s., the polynomial
P_{n}
has root
z_{i}
with multiplicity
N_{i}
with
N_{i}/n\to\mu(z_{i})
. Taking derivatives
k_{n}=o(n)
times,
z_{i}
remains a root with multiplicity
N_{i}-o(n)=(1+o(1))\mu(z_{i})n
. Therefore, a.s.,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(z_{i})\geq\mu(z_{i}).

Since the sum over
i
of the left-hand side is at most 1, which is the sum of the right-hand side, it must be the case that equality holds for all
i
which in turns implies that

\displaystyle 1-\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(z_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j\neq i}\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(z_{j})=1-\mu(z_{i})=\sum_{j\neq i}\mu(z_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\liminf\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(j)\leq\liminf\sum_{j\neq i}\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(j).

Therefore,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j\neq i}\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(z_{j})=\liminf\sum_{j\neq i}\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(j)=\lim\sum_{j\neq i}\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(j)

and so

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(z_{i})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(z_{i})=\mu(z_{i})

We conclude that
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}
converges to
\mu
almost surely.
Therefore, for the rest of this section, we assume that
\mu
is not discrete.

2.2.
Dimension-nondegenerate measures

Following the notations and proof strategy in
[
AMP24
]
, we denote by

\mathcal{M}=\left\{z\mapsto\frac{\alpha z+\beta}{\gamma z+\delta}\,\middle|\,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{C},\,\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma\neq 0\right\}

the set of invertible Möbius transformations of
\mathbb{C}
and endow it with the measure
\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}
inherited from
the Lebesgue measure
\lambda_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\lambda_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\lambda_{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
.

Let
u
be in
\mathcal{M}
. The classical Jensen’s formula when applied to the meromorphic function
S_{n}\circ u^{-1}
, where we recall that
S_{n}=S_{k,n}(z):=\frac{P^{(k)}_{n}(z)}{k!P_{n}(z)}
, yields the following inequality.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
AMP24
, Proposition 2.2]

For any Möbius transformation
u\in\mathcal{M}
such that
u^{-1}(0)
is not a zero or a pole of
S_{n}
, we have almost surely,

(5)

\sum_{P_{n}^{(k)}(\rho)=0}\log^{-}|u(\rho)|-\sum_{P_{n}(\zeta)=0}\log^{-}|u(\zeta)|\leq\log||S_{n}||_{u^{-1}(\mathbf{S}^{1})}-\log|S_{n}|(u^{-1}(0))

where
\mathbf{S}^{1}
is the unit circle in
{\mathbb{C}}
and
||S_{n}||_{u^{-1}(\mathbf{S}^{1})}
is the maximum value of
S_{n}
over
u^{-1}(\mathbf{S}^{1})
. Here, we note that the first sum runs over all zeros of
S_{n}
while the second sum runs over all poles, both of which run with multiplicities.

Since the space of probability measures on
{\mathbb{C}}
is a subset of the space
\mathcal{P}(\hat{\mathbb{C}})
of probability measures on the Riemann sphere
\hat{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}
which is compact in the weak topology, it suffices to show that for any cluster value
\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}
of the sequence
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}
in
\mathcal{P}(\hat{\mathbb{C}})
, we have
\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}=\mu
. Since both measures are probability measures on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,
it suffices to show the upper bound
\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}\leq\mu
,
which follows (by Lemma
2.2
below) from proving that, for almost all Möbius transformations

u\in\mathcal{M}
, the logarithmic potential of
\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}
is dominated by that of
\mu
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
AMP24
, Lemma 2.7]

Let
\widehat{m}_{1}
and
\widehat{m}_{2}
be two finite measures on
the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}\log^{-}|u|\,d\widehat{m}_{1}\leq\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}\log^{-}|u|\,d\widehat{m}_{2}

for

–almost every Möbius transformation

.
Then
\widehat{m}_{1}\leq\widehat{m}_{2}
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. In particular, if
\widehat{m}_{1}
and
\widehat{m}_{2}
are probability measures then they are equal.

Therefore, we are left to prove that almost surely with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
, for
\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}
-almost every
u\in\mathcal{M}
, for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(6)

\int_{\widehat{{\mathbb{C}}}}\log^{-}|u|\,d\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}(u)\leq\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\log^{-}|u|\,d\mu(u)+\varepsilon.

Since
\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}
is a cluster point, there exists a subsequence
\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}
such that

\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}
.
We define the truncated map

which is continuous on

\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}\log^{-}_{M}|u|\,d\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}(u)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\log^{-}_{M}|u|\,d\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(u)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\log^{-}|u|\,d\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(u).

\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}\log^{-}|u_{i}|\,d\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\log^{-}|u_{i}|\,d\mu_{P_{n}^{(k)}}(u).

\int_{{{\mathbb{C}}}}\log^{-}|u|\,d\mu_{n}(u)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log^{-}|u(\xi_{i})|\to\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\log^{-}|u|\,d\mu(u)\text{ almost surely}.

6
) is reduced to proving

\sum_{P_{n}^{(k)}(\rho)=0}\log^{-}|u(\rho)|-\sum_{P_{n}(\zeta)=0}\log^{-}|u(\zeta)|\leq\varepsilon.

Since for almost all Möbius transformations

, we have
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log||S_{n}||_{u^{-1}(\mathbf{S}^{1})}-\log|S_{n}|(u^{-1}(0))}{n}\leq\varepsilon.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all probability measures

Lemma 2.4

(Anti-concentration bound)
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem

(
2
), for all

\log|S_{n}(a)|\geq-\varepsilon n.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

For
\lambda_{\mathcal{M}}
-almost every
u\in\mathcal{M}
,
u^{-1}(\mathbf{S}^{1})
is a circle
\mathbf{S}(a,r)
where
a=u^{-1}(0)\in{\mathbb{C}}
and

.
We have

\displaystyle\|S_{n}\|_{\mathbf{S}(a,r)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{n\choose k}\sup_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}}\frac{1}{||a|-|\xi_{i_{1}}||}\dots\frac{1}{||a|-|\xi_{i_{k}}||}

\displaystyle\leq

.

We recall that

The sampling of independent copies

Let

.
The anti-concentration bound is then reduced to this randomness:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(|S_{k,n}(a)|\leq e^{-\varepsilon n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(|S_{k,n}(a)|\leq\gamma_{n}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(|S_{k,n}(a)|\leq\gamma_{n}^{k},K\geq(1-p)n/2)+{\mathbb{P}}(K<(1-p)n/2)

\displaystyle\sup{\mathbb{P}}_{(\xi_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}}}(|S_{k,n}(a)|\leq\gamma_{n}^{k}\big|\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n},(\xi_{i})_{i\notin\mathcal{I}})+e^{-cn}

. Without loss of generality, we can assume that

. Let

The first term on the right is bounded by

Since

and the rest of the

Let

Therefore, we can assume that

And then for all sufficiently large

∎

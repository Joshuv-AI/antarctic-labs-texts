ON THE RELATIVISTIC DESCRIPTION OF CHARGED FERMIONS
Yu.M. Poluektov

Abstract
A method for describing charged relativistic Fermi fields is proposed, in which particles of opposite charges are treated equally and states with negative energy are excluded. The concept of charge quantum number is introduced. Fields of particles and antiparticles with different charge quantum numbers are associated with wave functions for which the Born interpretation as probability amplitudes is valid. Key words : Dirac equation, electron, positron, particle, antiparticle, wave function, probability amplitude, charge conjugation

Solutions of the nonrelativistic Schrödinger equation for a free particle with positive energy form a complete set of states, into which an arbitrary solution can be expanded. In

The wave functions of the electron and positron admit a Born probabilistic interpretation and are considered as probability amplitudes. The aim of this paper is to further develop the approach proposed in

It is assumed that the complex field of a charged Fermi particle is characterized by such a charge quantum number, which describes the electron and positron states in an external electromagnetic field. In contrast to the work

(62)

From (
59
) it follows

\displaystyle\underline{\epsilon}^{\mu.}_{.\rho}=-\epsilon^{\mu.}_{.\rho},\;\;\;\epsilon_{\mu\rho}=-\epsilon_{\rho\mu},\;\;\;\epsilon^{\mu\rho}=-\epsilon^{\rho\mu}.

(63)

When moving to a new coordinate system, the bispinor is transformed as follows:

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=S\psi(x),\quad\overline{\psi}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\overline{\psi}(x)S^{-1},

(64)

where the matrix
S
satisfies the conditions

\displaystyle S^{-1}\gamma^{\mu}S=L^{\mu.}_{.\nu}\gamma^{\nu}

(65)

and

\displaystyle S^{+}\gamma^{0}=\gamma^{0}S^{-1}.

(66)

With small transformations this matrix has the form

\displaystyle S=1+\frac{1}{2}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\epsilon_{\nu\mu},\quad S^{-1}=1-\frac{1}{2}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\epsilon_{\nu\mu}.

(67)

Here the quantities
\Sigma^{\mu\nu}=-\Sigma^{\nu\mu}
are 4x4 matrices. From (
64
), (
65
) it follows that the following conditions must be satisfied

\displaystyle\gamma^{\rho}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}-\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\rho}=g^{\rho\nu}\gamma^{\mu}-g^{\rho\mu}\gamma^{\nu},

(68)

\displaystyle\left(\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\right)^{+}\gamma^{0}=-\gamma^{0}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}.

(69)

These conditions will be met if we assume that

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}\sigma^{\mu\nu},

(70)

where

\displaystyle\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}\left(\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}-\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\right).

(71)

Thus, under infinitely small homogeneous Lorentz transformations we have

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\delta\psi(x)=-\frac{\partial\psi(x)}{\partial x^{\mu}}x_{\nu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{\nu\mu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\psi(x),\\
\displaystyle\delta\overline{\psi}(x)=-\frac{\partial\overline{\psi}(x)}{\partial x^{\mu}}x_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{\psi}(x)\overline{\Sigma}_{\nu\mu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu},\end{array}

(72)

where
\overline{\Sigma}_{\nu\mu}=\gamma^{0}\Sigma^{+}_{\nu\mu}\gamma^{0}=-\Sigma_{\nu\mu}
. Substituting (
72
) into (
51
) and taking into account that
{\partial\delta x^{\nu}}/\partial{x^{\nu}}=0
, we find

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\left[\frac{1}{c}\left(T^{.\mu}_{\rho.}x_{\nu}-T^{.\mu}_{\nu.}x_{\rho}\right)-\frac{\hbar}{4}\overline{\psi}\left(\gamma^{\mu}\sigma_{\nu\rho}+\sigma_{\nu\rho}\gamma^{\mu}\right)\psi\right]=\\
\displaystyle=-\hbar\frac{\partial\Lambda}{\partial A_{\mu}}\left(\frac{\partial A_{\mu}}{\partial x^{\rho}}x_{\nu}-\frac{\partial A_{\mu}}{\partial x^{\nu}}x_{\rho}\right)=\hbar\sigma\widetilde{e}\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\left(\frac{\partial A_{\mu}}{\partial x^{\rho}}x_{\nu}-\frac{\partial A_{\mu}}{\partial x^{\nu}}x_{\rho}\right).\end{array}

(73)

The density tensors of the orbital and spin moments are determined by the relations

\displaystyle L^{\mu}_{\rho\nu}=\frac{1}{c}\left(T^{.\mu}_{\rho.}x_{\nu}-T^{.\mu}_{\nu.}x_{\rho}\right),

(74)

\displaystyle S^{\mu}_{\rho\nu}=-\frac{\hbar}{4}\overline{\psi}\left(\gamma^{\mu}\sigma_{\rho\nu}+\sigma_{\rho\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\right)\psi.

(75)

Thus, equation (
73
) expresses the law of conservation of total angular momentum

\displaystyle M^{\mu}_{\rho\nu}=L^{\mu}_{\rho\nu}+S^{\mu}_{\rho\nu}.

(76)

By integrating the components
L^{0}_{\rho\nu}
and
S^{0}_{\rho\nu}
over 3-space, we obtain the orbital and spin moment tensors

\displaystyle L_{\rho\nu}=\int d\textbf{x}L^{0}_{\nu\rho},\quad S_{\rho\nu}=\int d\textbf{x}S^{0}_{\nu\rho}.

(77)

The axial vectors of the orbital and spin angular momentum are expressed through the spatial components of the tensors (
77
):

\displaystyle L_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ikl}L_{kl},\quad S_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ikl}S_{kl},

(78)

where
\epsilon_{ikl}
is the antisymmetric unit tensor.

V
Free particles and antiparticles

In the standard formulation of Dirac’s theory, when describing free particles, it is necessary to consider states with positive energy (electrons) and negative energy (positrons) separately. In this section we present the relationships for free fields in the proposed approach, when particles of both signs have positive energy and are considered on an equal basis. In the absence of an electromagnetic field, particles of different electric charges are indistinguishable. For this reason, we will omit the charge index
\sigma
in the formulas of this section. The solutions of the Dirac equation (
27
) without an external field for states with a certain momentum
\hbar\textbf{k}
have the form

\displaystyle\psi(\textbf{x},t)=\frac{u(\textbf{k})}{\sqrt{V}}e^{i(\textbf{kx}-\omega t)},

(79)

where the bispinor
u(\textbf{k})
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{k}-\mu\right)u(\textbf{k})=0,

(80)

\left(\widehat{k}\equiv k_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu},\;\;k_{\mu}=(\omega/c,-\textbf{k})\right)
and can be represented as

\displaystyle u(\textbf{k})=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon+\mu}{2\epsilon}}\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\varphi(\textbf{k})\\
\text{\Large$\frac{\textbf{k}\bm{\sigma}}{\epsilon+\mu}$}\varphi(\textbf{k})\end{bmatrix}.

(81)

Since for particles of both types we consider functions (
10
), for which the Fourier expansion is carried out only for positive frequencies, the root with a negative sign for energy should not be taken into account, therefore

\displaystyle\hbar\omega=\hbar\ c\epsilon\quad\epsilon\equiv\sqrt{\mu^{2}+k^{2}}.

(82)

When the normalization conditions for the spinor
\varphi^{+}(\textbf{k})\varphi(\textbf{k})=1
are satisfied, the bispinor (
81
) is normalized by the condition
u^{+}(\textbf{k})u(\textbf{k})=1
. Instead of the bispinor (
81
), one can use the bispinor

\displaystyle u(k)\equiv\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}}u(\textbf{k})=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon+\mu}{2\mu}}\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\varphi(\textbf{k})\\
\text{\Large$\frac{\textbf{k}\bm{\sigma}}{\epsilon+\mu}$}\varphi(\textbf{k})\end{bmatrix}

(83)

for which the relativistically invariant normalization
\overline{u}(k)u(k)=1
is valid. In the rest system, the bispinor takes the form

\displaystyle u(\mu)=\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\varphi(0)\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

(84)

In a system where the particle momentum is
\hbar\textbf{k}
, the bispinor (
83
) can be obtained using the transformations

\displaystyle u(k)=L(\textbf{k})u(\mu),\qquad\overline{u}(k)=\overline{u}(\mu)L^{-1}(\textbf{k}),

(85)

where the matrix
L(\textbf{k})
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle L^{-1}(\textbf{k})\gamma^{\mu}L(\textbf{k})=L^{\mu.}_{.\nu}\gamma^{\nu}

(86)

and has the form

\displaystyle L(\textbf{k})=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon+\mu}{2\mu}}\left(1-\frac{k_{i}\gamma^{i}}{\epsilon+\mu}\gamma^{0}\right),\quad L^{-1}(\textbf{k})=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon+\mu}{2\mu}}\left(1+\frac{k_{i}\gamma^{i}}{\epsilon+\mu}\gamma^{0}\right).

(87)

To describe the spin state of a particle, we assume that the spinor satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}\textbf{u}\varphi^{s}(0)=s\varphi^{s}(0),

(88)

and
u
is a unit vector in the rest system. From (
88
) it follows that

s\textbf{u}=\varphi^{s+}(0)\bm{\sigma}\varphi^{s}(0).

(89)

Thus, when
s=+1
the vector
u
determines the direction of the average spin value. Formula (
88
) can be written as
\bm{\Sigma}\textbf{u}u^{s}(\mu)=su^{s}(\mu)
, where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i}=\gamma^{5}\gamma^{i}\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{i}&0\\
0&\sigma_{i}\end{pmatrix}.

(90)

Let us define a covariant unit 4-vector in the rest system
u_{\mu}\equiv\left(0,-\textbf{u}\right)
. Then
u_{i}\gamma^{i}=-u_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}
and taking into account
\gamma^{0}u^{s}(\mu)=u^{s}(\mu)
we have

\displaystyle\gamma^{5}u_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}u^{s}(\mu)=-su^{s}(\mu).

(91)

Using (
85
), (
86
), (
87
) and taking into account that

\displaystyle L^{-1}(\textbf{k})\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}L(\textbf{k})={L}^{\mu.}_{.\nu}\gamma^{\nu},

(92)

let us write relation (
91
) in the system where the particle has momentum
\hbar\textbf{k}
. We have

\displaystyle\gamma^{5}\widehat{n}u^{s}(k)=-su^{s}(k),\quad\overline{u}^{s}(k)\widehat{n}\gamma^{5}=s\overline{u}^{s}(k),

(93)

where
n_{\mu}=\underline{L}^{\nu.}_{.\mu}u_{\nu}
,
\widehat{n}\equiv n_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}
,
u^{s}(k)=L(\textbf{k})u^{s}(\mu)
. It is obvious that
n_{\mu}k^{\mu}=u_{0}\mu=0
and
n_{\mu}n^{\mu}=1
. Thus, a bispinor
u^{s}(k)
that is an eigenfunction of an operator
\widehat{k}\equiv k_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}
is also an eigenfunction of an operator
\gamma^{5}\widehat{n}

\widehat{k}
and
\gamma^{5}\widehat{n}
commute with each other. Multiplying the first equation (

\overline{u}^{s}(k)\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}

\displaystyle\overline{u}^{s}(k)\left(\gamma^{\mu}\widehat{n}+\widehat{n}\gamma^{\mu}\right)u^{s}(k)=2s\overline{u}^{s}(k)\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}u^{s}(k).

(94)

Using the commutation relations for matrices
\gamma

\overline{u}^{s}(k)u^{s}(k)=1

\displaystyle\overline{u}^{s}(k)\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}u^{s}(k)=sn^{\mu}.

(95)

As we see, the average value of the operator
\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}

\displaystyle\textbf{n}=\textbf{u}+\frac{\textbf{k(ku)}}{\mu(\epsilon+\mu)},\qquad n_{0}=\frac{\textbf{ku}}{\mu}.

(96)

Thus, the state of a particle of any sign with positive energy in an arbitrary reference system is completely described by its momentum and discrete coordinate
s=\pm 1

VI
Particles in a non-stationary external field

As has been shown, in a stationary external field, particles of different charge signs with positive energies are described by independent Dirac equations. Let us now consider the case of a non-stationary electromagnetic field, assuming that the 4-vector potential is the sum of the stationary and non-stationary fields
A_{\mu}(x)=A_{\mu}(\text{x})+\underline{A}_{\mu}(x)
. We will assume that the average value of the variable field averaged over a large period of time is equal to zero

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}\underline{A}_{\mu}(x)dt=0,\quad T\to\infty.

(97)

One particle of arbitrary sign in a non-stationary external field is also described by the Dirac equation
Q_{\sigma}(x)=0
or
\widetilde{\overline{Q}}_{\overline{\sigma}}(x)=0
:

\displaystyle i\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\partial\psi_{\sigma}(x)}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\sigma\widetilde{e}A_{\mu}(\textbf{x})\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{\sigma}(x)-\mu\psi_{\sigma}(x)=\sigma\widetilde{e}\underline{A}_{\mu}(\textbf{x},t)\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{\sigma}(x).

(98)

If there are two particles of opposite signs in a non-stationary external field, then it follows from the equation
Q_{\sigma}(x)+C^{\ast}\widetilde{\overline{Q}}_{\overline{\sigma}}(x)=0
that their wave functions
\psi_{\sigma}(x)
and
\underline{\psi}_{\overline{\sigma}}(x)\equiv\widetilde{\overline{\psi}}_{\overline{\sigma}}(x)
are related:

\displaystyle i\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\partial\psi_{\sigma}(x)}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\sigma\widetilde{e}A_{\mu}(x)\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{\sigma}(x)-\mu\psi_{\sigma}(x)=-\sigma\widetilde{e}C^{\ast}\underline{A}_{\mu}(\textbf{x},t)\widetilde{\gamma}^{\mu}\underline{\psi}_{\overline{\sigma}}(x),

(99)

\displaystyle-i\widetilde{\gamma}^{\mu}\frac{\partial\underline{\psi}_{\overline{\sigma}}(x)}{\partial x^{\mu}}+\sigma\widetilde{e}A_{\mu}(x)\widetilde{\gamma}^{\mu}\underline{\psi}_{\overline{\sigma}}(x)-\mu\underline{\psi}_{\overline{\sigma}}(x)=\sigma\widetilde{e}\widetilde{C}\underline{A}_{\mu}(\textbf{x},t)\gamma^{\mu}\psi_{\sigma}(x).

(100)

Thus, the states of two particles of different electric charges in an alternating external electromagnetic field are not independent.

VII
Conclusion

In its standard formulation, Dirac’s theory describes particles with positive energy (electrons) and particles with negative energy, which are treated as particles of the opposite sign (positrons). It is assumed that the difficulty associated with the existence of negative energies can be overcome by moving to a quantum field description based on the apparatus of secondary quantization. However, as shown in
Pl
and this work, even with a single-particle description, Dirac’s theory should be considered as a theory of particles of two types with the same mass, positive energies, and different charge signs. In this approach, the wave functions of particles admit a Born probabilistic interpretation and should be considered as probability amplitudes. In this paper, the description of electrons and positrons is based on the introduction of the concept of a charge quantum number, which characterizes the complex field of charged particles. It is natural to assume that a charged field of particles of any nature can be in two charge states and its interaction with the electromagnetic field should also be described using a charge quantum number.

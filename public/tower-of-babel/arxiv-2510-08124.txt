Timeline Problems in Temporal Graphs: Vertex Cover vs. Dominating Set
Anton Herrmann, Christian Komusiewicz, Nils Morawietz, Frank Sommer

Abstract
A temporal graph is a finite sequence of graphs, called snapshots, over the same vertex set. Many temporal graph problems turn out to be much more difficult than their static counterparts. One such problem is Timeline Vertex Cover (also known as MinTimeline ∞ ), a temporal analogue to the classical Vertex Cover problem. In this problem, one is given a temporal graph 𝒢 \mathcal{G} and two integers k k and ℓ \ell , and the goal is to cover each edge of each snapshot by selecting for each vertex at most k k activity intervals of length at most ℓ \ell each. Here, an edge u ​ v uv in the i i th snapshot is covered, if an activity interval of u u or v v is active at time i i . In this work, we continue the algorithmic study of Timeline Vertex Cover and introduce the Timeline Dominating Set problem where we want to dominate all vertices in each snapshot by the selected activity intervals. We analyze both problems from a classical and parameterized point of view and also consider partial problem versions, where the goal is to cover (dominate) at least t t edges (vertices) of the snapshots. With respect to the parameterized complexity, we consider the temporal graph parameters vertex-interval-membership-width ( vimw ) (\vimw) and interval-membership-width ( imw ) (\imw) . We show that all considered problems admit FPT-algorithms when parameterized by vimw + k + ℓ \vimw+\,k+\ell . This provides a smaller parameter combination than the ones used for previously known FPT-algorithms for Timeline Vertex Cover . Surprisingly, for imw + k + ℓ \imw+\,k+\ell , Timeline Dominating Set turns out to be easier than Timeline Vertex Cover , by also admitting an FPT-algorithm, whereas the vertex cover version is NP-hard even if imw + k + ℓ \imw+\,k+\ell is constant. We also consider parameterization by combinations of n n , the vertex set size, with k k or ℓ \ell and parameterization by t t . Here, we show for example that both partial problems are fixed-parameter tractable for t t which significantly improves and generalizes a previous result for a special case of Partial Timeline Vertex Cover with k = 1 k=1 .

Introduction

A crucial task in the management of wireless sensor networks is to monitor the network by selecting few dedicated sensors that can monitor themselves and other sensors which are close enough to have a direct wireless connection
[
29
,
32
]
.
In graph-theoretic terms, this is the famous NP-hard Phys. Rev. BDominating Set problem where we say that a vertex dominates itself and all its neighbors and the task is to select few vertices of the graph such that every vertex is dominated by some selected vertex. In some applications of sensor networks, the network may change over time. Then, instead of a static graph
G
the input would be a
temporal graph

\mathcal{G}
, consisting of a set of
snapshots

G_{i}
, each reflecting the connections at timestep
i
.
Moreover, in such a scenario each sensor could carry out the surveillance only for a bounded duration, for example due to limited battery capacities.
Thus, instead of selecting few sensors to monitor the network, we would like to select, for each sensor, an active time interval of bounded length
\ell
, during which it monitors itself and all its current neighbors.
To make the model more general, we may also allow for each sensor to select up to
k
such active intervals, for example because each sensor carries
k
batteries. By calling a collection of active intervals for all vertices of the graph a
k
-activity
\ell
-timeline

As in the static Phys. Rev. BDominating Set problem, we may further generalize the problem to handle scenarios where we are not able to monitor the whole network over the full time period but instead we want to maximize the number of monitored sensors under the resource limitations.

Previous results.

Froese et al.

Our results.

We start off with a new hardness result for Phys. Rev. BTimeline VC: We show that the problem remains NP-hard even if the total number of snapshots
T
,
k
, and
\ell
are constant and additionally the maximum degree in every snapshot is at most one.
Then, we show the NP-hardness of Phys. Rev. BTimeline DS, again for the case that
T
,
k
, and
\ell
are constant and the maximum snapshot degree is one.

We then study the parameterized complexity of the problems; an overview of the results is given in
\Cref
tab:paraRes. As noted above, Phys. Rev. BTimeline VC is fixed-parameter tractable with respect to
n+k

[
19
]
. Since
n
is a rather large parameter, Froese et al.
[
19
]
asked whether there are FPT-algorithms also for parameters that are smaller than
n
. We make progress in this direction by considering the two parameters
interval-membership-width
(\imw)
and
vertex-interval-membership-width
(\vimw)
which where introduced by Bumpus and Meeks
[
3
]
. The parameters
\imw
and
\vimw
are interesting in the sense that they are among the relatively few truly temporal structural graph parameters because they are sensitive to the order of the snapshots
[
3
]
.
Informally,
\vimw
can be defined as follows:
We say the
lifetime
of a vertex is the time interval between its first and last non-isolated appearance in a snapshot. For a timestep
i
, the bag of
i
is the set of vertices whose lifetime contains timestep
i
, and
\vimw
is the size of the largest bag of
\mathcal{G}
.

We first show that Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVC is fixed-parameter tractable with respect to
\vimw+k+\ell
. To further improve on this, we introduce a hierarchy of parameters with non-increasing size.
The idea, inspired by the
h
-index of static graphs
[
17
]
, is that a temporal graph may have only few large bags.
In that case, we would rather parameterize by the size of the
x
th largest bag, denoted by
\vimw[x]
, for some
x>1
.
With this definition, we have
\vimw=\vimw[1]
and
\vimw[x]\geq\vimw[x+1]
for all
x\in[T]
. We show that Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVC is fixed-parameter tractable for
\vimw[x]+k+\ell
with
x=k\cdot(\ell+1)
.
Informally, this means that the larger
k
and
\ell
get, the more large bags can be ignored for the parameter. We then consider Phys. Rev. BTimeline (Partial) Dominating Setin the same setting.
We first showing fixed-parameter tractability of Phys. Rev. BTimeline PDSfor
\vimw+\,k+\ell
. Then we show that for Phys. Rev. BTimeline DS it can be improved to fixed-parameter tractability for
\vimw[x]+k+\ell
for
x=\ell+2
while for Phys. Rev. BTimeline PDSparameterization by
\vimw[2]+k+\ell
is not useful. The latter result is obtained by showing NP-hardness of the extremely restricted special case when there are two snapshots, one of which is edgeless,
k=1
and
\ell=0
.

Afterwards, we consider the interval-membership-width
(\imw)
. Informally, this is the edge-version of
\vimw
, that is, the bag at timestep
i
contains the edges which occur at timestep
i
and those that occur both before and after
i
. Note that
\imw
can be considered to be a smaller parameter than
\vimw
: For all instances, we have
\vimw\in\Omega(\sqrt{\imw})
and there are instances with constant values of
\imw
and unbounded values of
\vimw
.
We show that Phys. Rev. BTimeline DSis fixed-parameter tractable for the parameter
\imw+\,k+\ell
while all three other problems are NP-hard for constant values of
\imw+\,k+\ell
. Given the latter hardness results, we refrain from analyzing a hierarchy of
\imw
-based parameters. Altogether, the results show that in our setting,
\vimw
is a much more powerful parameter than
\imw
.

We then conclude by considering the more standard parameters
n
,
k
,
\ell
, and
t
, where
t
denotes the number of vertices to dominate or edges to cover, respectively.
For Phys. Rev. BTimeline DS, we show fixed-parameter tractability for
{n+k}
and
\W
[1]-hardness for
n+\ell
, thus showing that the complexity is similar to the one of Phys. Rev. BTimeline VC.
Finally, we show that Phys. Rev. BTimeline PDSand Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVCcan be solved in
2^{\mathcal{O}(t)}\cdot(n+T)^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time.
This improves and extends a previous result of
Dondi et al.
[
13
, Thm. 6]
who provided an algorithm for Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVCwith
k=1
that has running time
2^{t\cdot\log(t)}\cdot(n+T)^{\mathcal{O}(1)}=t^{t}\cdot(n+T)^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
.

Further related work.

Akrida et al.
[
1
]
introduced the problems Phys. Rev. BTemporal Vertex Cover (TVC) and Phys. Rev. BSliding Window Temporal Vertex Cover (SW-TVC), where the goal is to find a minimum number of temporal vertices that cover the edges of the temporal graph.
In Phys. Rev. BTVC each edge needs to be covered in at least one of the snapshots and in Phys. Rev. BSW-TVC, the input contains an integer
\Delta
, and the aim is to cover every edge at least once at every
\Delta
consecutive time steps.
Akrida et al.
[
1
]
showed that Phys. Rev. BTVC remains NP-hard when the underlying graph is a star graph.
Moreover, they studied the approximability of both problems, provided (S)ETH-based running time lower bounds and designed an exact dynamic programming algorithm with exponential running time.
The research on these problems was then continued by Hamm et al.
[
22
]
, who showed that Phys. Rev. BTVC is solvable in polynomial time if the underlying graph is a path or cycle while Phys. Rev. BSW-TVC remains NP-hard in this case.
Besides that, they provided several (exact and approximation) algorithms for the sliding window variant. The Phys. Rev. BTemporal Dominating Set (TDS) problem is defined analogously, here the task is to dominate every vertex in at least one of the snapshots of the temporal graph by selecting few temporal vertices. Phys. Rev. BTDS is NP-hard in very restricted cases, for example when the maximum degree in every snapshot is 2
[
24
]
.
There are further temporal variants of Phys. Rev. BVertex Cover and Phys. Rev. BDominating Set which have been discussed and studied. These include a multistage variant of Phys. Rev. BVertex Cover
[
18
]
, a reachability-based variant for Phys. Rev. BDominating Set
[
25
]
and different conditions on how and when a vertex should be dominated
[
4
,
33
]
.

Parameter definitions.

We give the precise definitions of the parameters related to the (vertex)-interval-membership width:
\vimw
,
\vimw[x]
, and
\imw
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{1},\dots,G_{T})
be a temporal graph.
The
vertex-interval-membership sequence
of
\mathcal{G}
is the sequence
(F_{i})_{i\in[T]}
of vertex-subsets
F_{i}\subseteq V(\mathcal{G})
(called
bags
), where each
F_{i}
is defined as

F_{i}:=\{v\in V(\mathcal{G}):\exists p,q\in[T]\text{ such that }p\leq i\leq q,\deg_{G_{p}}(v)\geq 1\text{ and }\deg_{G_{q}}(v)\geq 1\}.

The
vertex-interval-membership-width
of
\mathcal{G}
, denoted by
\vimw(\mathcal{G})
, is the maximum size of a bag in the vertex-interval-membership sequence, that is,
\vimw(\mathcal{G}):=\max\{|F_{i}|\colon i\in[T]\}
.

For given
x\in[T]
we introduce the parameter
\vimw[x]
as the size of the
x
th largest bag of the vertex-interval-membership sequence.
Formally, let
(F_{i_{1}},\dots,F_{i_{T}})
be an ordering of
(F_{i})_{i\in[T]}
such that
|F_{i_{1}}|\geq\dots\geq|F_{i_{T}}|
.
Then we define

\vimw[x](\mathcal{G}):=|F_{i_{x}}|.

Note that
\vimw[i]
can be much smaller than
\vimw[1]=\vimw
: for example consider a temporal graph where every vertex only appears non-isolated for a short period of time.
If all snapshots have only a few edges, then
\vimw
is small.
However, a single snapshot with many edges is enough to make
\vimw
arbitrarily large.
If only a few such snapshots exist, then
\vimw[i]
is already much smaller then
\vimw
for small
i
.

The
interval-membership sequence
of
\mathcal{G}
is the sequence
(F_{i})_{i\in[T]}
of edge-subsets
F_{i}\subseteq E_{\downarrow}
(again called
bags
), where each
F_{i}
is defined as

F_{i}:=\{e\in E_{\downarrow}:\exists p,q\in[T]\text{ such that }p\leq i\leq q\text{ and }e\in E_{p}\cap E_{q}\}.

The
interval-membership-width
of
\mathcal{G}
, denoted by

Timelines.

Given a temporal graph
\mathcal{G}=(G_{1},\dots,G_{T})
, an
activity interval
of a vertex
v
is a triple
(v,a,b)\in V(\mathcal{G})\times[T]\times[T]
such that
a\leq b
. We say
a
that is the
starting time
and
b
is the
end time
of an activity interval
(v,a,b)
.
The
length
of the activity interval is defined by
b-a
.
A
k
-activity timeline
of the temporal graph
\mathcal{G}
is a set of activity intervals
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\{(v,a,b)\in V(\mathcal{G})\times[T]\times[T]:a\leq b\}
, which contains at most
k
activity intervals for each vertex.
A
k
-activity
\ell
-timeline
is a
k
-activity timeline in which each activity interval has length at most
\ell
.
Given a
k
-activity timeline
\mathcal{T}
, a vertex
v
is called
active
in snapshot
G_{i}
, if there exists
(v,a,b)\in\mathcal{T}
such that
a\leq i\leq b
.
We say that the activity interval
(v,a,b)

intersects
snapshot
G_{i}
, if
a\leq i\leq b
.
A
k
-activity timeline
\mathcal{T}

dominates
the temporal vertex
(v,i)
if
{v\in N_{G_{i}}[\{u:u\text{ is active in }G_{i}\}]}
.
A
k
-activity timeline
\mathcal{T}

covers
the temporal edge
(e,i)
if at least one of the two endpoints of

is active in snapshot
G_{i}
.

Parameterized complexity.

Let
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
be a computational problem specified over some alphabet
\Sigma
and let
p:\Sigma^{*}\to\mathds{N}
be a
parameter
, that is,
p
assigns to each instance of
L
an integer parameter value (which we simply denote by
p
if the instance is clear from the context).
We say that
L
is
fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) with respect to
p

if
L
can be decided in
f(p)\cdot|I|^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time where
|I|
is the length of the input.
The corresponding hardness concept related to fixed-parameter tractability is
W[
t
]-hardness,
t\geq 1
; if a problem
L
is W[
t
]-hard with respect to
p
, then
L
is assumed to
not
be fixed-parameter tractable.
Moreover,
L
is
slice-wise polynomial (XP) with respect to
p
if
L
can be decided in
f(p)\cdot|I|^{g(p)}
time.
Let
(I,p)
and
(I^{\prime},p^{\prime})
be two instances of
L
.
A
reduction to a (problem) kernel for
L
is a polynomial-time algorithm that computes an instance
(I^{\prime},p^{\prime})
such that

•

p^{\prime}+|I^{\prime}|\leq g(p)
, and

•

(I,p)
is a yes-instance of
L
if and only if
(I^{\prime},p^{\prime})
is a yes-instance of
L
.

The instance
(I^{\prime},p^{\prime})
is referred to as
the problem kernel
and
g
is the
size
of the kernel.
Furthermore, if
g
is a polynomial, we say that the kernel is a
polynomial problem kernel
.
For more details about parameterized complexity we refer to the standard monographs
[
15
,
7
]
.

More precisely, we study the parameter combinations

4.1
The Parameter Vertex-Interval-Membership-Width

In this section, we provide FPT-algorithms with respect to
\vimw+\,k+\ell
for Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVCand Phys. Rev. BTimeline PDS.
Moreover, we show that
\vimw

Simultaneously, these algorithms also are XP-algorithms for Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVCand Phys. Rev. BTimeline PDSparameterized by

alone.
Initially, we focus on Phys. Rev. BTimeline (Partial) Vertex Coverand we show that Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVCadmits an FPT-algorithm for

.

Theorem 4
.

Phys. Rev. B
Timeline PVCis solvable in
\big((k+1)(\ell+2)\big)^{2\cdot\vimw}\cdot(n+T)^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{G}=(G_{1},\dots,G_{T}),k,\ell,t)
be an instance of Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVC.
Further, suppose that
T\geq k\cdot(\ell+1)+1
(otherwise, it is a trivial instance) and that the underlying graph
G_{\downarrow}
contains no isolated vertex
v
.
Otherwise, since
v
does not cover any temporal edge,
v
can be safely removed and consequently
t
remains unchanged.
Moreover, we assume that each vertex
v
is contained in at least
k\cdot(\ell+1)+1
bags (recall that these are consecutive) because otherwise we can simply greedily pick
k
activity intervals of
v
which contain all snapshots where
v
is non-isolated and thus we cover all temporal edges incident with
v
.
Thus, it is safe to remove
v
from
\mathcal{G}
and reduce
t
by the number of temporal edges incident with
v
over all snapshots.

The idea is to use dynamic programming over the lifetime of the temporal graph.
Each table entry corresponds to the maximal number of covered temporal edges where a specific set of vertices in the bag is active.
In other words, the dynamic program keeps track of the activity of the vertices which are contained in the bag
F_{i}
of the currently considered time step
i\in[T]
.
While iterating over the lifetime of the temporal graph, we need to ensure that the activities at a time step are compatible with the activities at the neighboring time steps.
Moreover, for a vertex
v
let
i
and
j
be the index of the smallest and largest snapshot where
v
is incident to an edge, respectively.
By definition,
v\in F_{x}
for any
i\leq x\leq j
.
Furthermore, since
v
is isolated in all snapshots
G_{x}
where
x<i
or
x>j
, it is safe to assume that all
k
activity intervals of
v
are used when
v
is contained in the bags
F_{x}
.

Recall that the vertex-interval-membership sequence can be computed in polynomial time. Since we assumed that the underlying graph contains no isolated vertex, it follows that each vertex is contained in at least one bag of the sequence.

Table Definition:

We denote
\overleftarrow{F}_{i}:=F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{i}
and
\overline{F}_{i}:=\overleftarrow{F}_{i}\setminus F_{i}
.
In other words,
\overline{F}_{i}
is the set of vertices which are isolated in all snapshots
G_{i},\dots,G_{T}
since we assumed that
G_{\downarrow}
contains no isolated vertices.
Moreover, for
i\in[T]
we define functions
{\mathrm{curr}:F_{i}\rightarrow[0,k],}
and
\mathrm{pos}:F_{i}\rightarrow[0,\ell+1]
.
The functions
\mathrm{curr}
and
\mathrm{pos}
indicate for each vertex
v\in F_{i}
whether an activity interval of
v
is active during snapshot
G_{i}
.
More precisely, the function value
\mathrm{curr}(v)
determines the number of the current activity interval of
v
and the function value
\mathrm{pos}(v)
determines the position in this activity interval.
Here,
\mathrm{curr}(v)=0
means that the current time step is before the first activity interval and
\mathrm{pos}(v)=0
means that
v
is currently not active, that is, the
\mathrm{curr}(v)
-th activity interval of
v
is already over and the next one has not started yet.
Now the entry
\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]
contains the maximum number of covered temporal edges in the first
i
snapshots
G_{1},\dots,G_{i}
, while
i
is at the
\mathrm{pos}(v)
-th position of the
\mathrm{curr}(v)
-th activity interval of
v\in F_{i}
.

The formal definition of the table now is

\displaystyle\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]\,\hat{=}\,\max_{\mathcal{T}}\,

\displaystyle|\{(e,j)\in E_{\downarrow}\times[i]:(e,j)\text{ is covered by }\mathcal{T}\}|,

where the maximum is taken over all
k
-activity
\ell
-timelines

\mathcal{T}\subseteq(\overline{F}_{i}\times[i-1]\times[T])\cup(F_{i}\times[i]\times[T]),

such that for each
v\in F_{i}

(i)

there are at most
\mathrm{curr}(v)
activity intervals of
v
in
\mathcal{T}
,

(ii)

\mathrm{pos}(v)\leq i
, and

(iii)

(v,i-\mathrm{pos}(v)+1,b)\in\mathcal{T}
for
b=\min(i-\mathrm{pos}(v)+1+\ell,T)
if
\mathrm{pos}(v)>0
.

We need Conditions (i)-(iii) for the following reasons:
Condition (i) ensures that each vertex has at most
k
activity intervals.
Moreover, condition (ii) ensures that no activity interval starts before time step one.
Finally, condition (iii) ensures that each activity interval has length
\ell
, except if the remaining number of snapshots is too small.

Initialization:

For all
\mathrm{curr}:F_{1}\rightarrow[0,k]
and
\mathrm{pos}:F_{1}\rightarrow[0,1]
, for which
\mathrm{curr}(v)=0
implies
\mathrm{pos}(v)=0
, the table is initialized by

\DP[1,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]=|E_{G_{1}}(\{v\in F_{1}:\mathrm{pos}(v)=1\},F_{1})|.

Note that
\overline{F}_{1}=\emptyset
and therefore only activity timelines
\mathcal{T}\subseteq F_{1}\times[1]\times[T]
are considered in the definition of
\DP[1,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]
.
The initialization is correct, since the table entry
\DP[1,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]
contains the number of temporal edges which are covered by the active vertices from
F_{1}
in
G_{1}
.
Observe that the other endpoints of the covered edges are also contained in
F_{1}
by definition.

Recurrence:

The remaining table entries are computed recursively.
Intuitively,
\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]
is computed by trying all activity interval choices for the vertices of
F_{i-1}
, such that there is no conflict with
\mathrm{curr}
and
\mathrm{pos}
.
Formally, we set

\displaystyle\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]=\max_{\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}}\,

\displaystyle\DP[i-1,\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}]+|E_{G_{i}}(\{v\in F_{i}:\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 1\},F_{i})|

where the maximum is taken over all
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}:F_{i-1}\rightarrow[0,k]
and
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}:F_{i-1}\rightarrow[0,\ell+1]
which are
compatible
with
\mathrm{curr}
and
\mathrm{pos}
.
Informally,
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}
are
compatible
with
\mathrm{curr}
and
\mathrm{pos}
if they provide a correct extension of the activities of
F_{i-1}\cap F_{i}
at time step
i
to time step
i-1
.
Formally, four conditions need to be fulfilled:
First, if an interval of
v
is already active at time step
i
, that is, if
\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 2
, then an activity interval of
v
is already active during time step
i-1
.
This can be formalized as follows.

\text{If }\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 2\text{, then }\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v)\leq\mathrm{curr}(v)\text{ and }\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)=\mathrm{pos}(v)-1.

(1)

Second, if a new activity interval of
v
starts at time step
i
either
v
was inactive at time step
i-1
or the previous activity interval of
v
ended at time step
i-1
.
This can be formalized as follows.

\text{If }\mathrm{curr}(v)>1\text{ and }\mathrm{pos}(v)=1\text{, then }\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v)\leq\mathrm{curr}(v)-1\text{ and }\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)\in\{\min(i-1,\ell+1),0\}.

(2)

Third, if the first activity interval of
v
starts at time step
i
, then in the previous time step
i-1
vertex
v
cannot be active. This can be formalized as follows.

\text{If }\mathrm{curr}(v)=1\text{ and }\mathrm{pos}(v)=1\text{, then }\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v)=0\text{ and }\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)=0.

(3)

Finally, if
v
is not active during time step
i
, either
v
is also not active during time step
i-1
or an activity interval of
v
ended at time step
i-1
.
This can be formalized as follows.

\text{If }\mathrm{curr}(v)\geq 1\text{ and }\mathrm{pos}(v)=0\text{, then }\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v)\leq\mathrm{curr}(v)\text{ and }\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)\in\{\min(i-1,\ell+1),0\}.

(4)

The minimum in Conditions
2
and
4
ensures that the activity intervals do not have a starting point smaller than one.

Correctness:

We show the correctness of the computation by proving inequalities in both directions.

(
\leq
) Suppose the maximum in the definition of the table entry
\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]
is attained for
\mathcal{T}
and assume that no activity intervals from
\mathcal{T}
overlap.
We define
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
by taking all activity intervals from
\mathcal{T}
which are relevant for a table entry of
i-1
.
Formally, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\prime}:=

\displaystyle\{(v,a,b)\in\mathcal{T}:a\leq i-2,v\in\overline{F}_{i-1}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{(v,a,b)\in\mathcal{T}:a\leq i-1,v\in F_{i-1}\},

and, in accordance with
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, functions
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}:F_{i-1}\rightarrow[0,k],\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}:F_{i-1}\rightarrow[0,\ell+1]
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle:=|\{(v,a,b)\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}:a,b\in[T]\}|

\displaystyle\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}i-a&\text{if }(v,a,b)\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\text{ with }a\leq i\leq b\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
.
We show that
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}
satisfy Conditions (1)–(4).
This means that the table entry
\DP[i-1,\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}]
is considered in the maximum on the right side of the recursive computation.
By definition of
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}
it is clear that
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is then considered in the definition of the table entry
\DP[i-1,\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}]
.
Now let
v\in F_{i-1}\cap F_{i}
.

(1)

If
\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 2
, then
(v,a,b)\in\mathcal{T}
for some
a\leq i-1,b\in[T]
and by definition of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, each such activity interval of
v
in
\mathcal{T}
is also contained in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.
Consequently, we have
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v)\leq\mathrm{curr}(v)
and
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)=\mathrm{pos}(v)-1
.

(2)

If
\mathrm{curr}(v)>1
and
\mathrm{pos}(v)=1
, then
(v,i,b)\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
for some
b\in[T]
.
So the activity timeline
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
contains at most
\mathrm{curr}(v)-1
activity intervals of
v
and therefore
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v)\leq\mathrm{curr}(v)-1
.
Now either
(v,a,i-1)\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
for some
a\in[i-1]
or
v
is not active in
G_{i-1}
with respect to the activity intervals from
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.
This means that either
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)=\min(i-1,\ell+1)
or
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)=0
.

(3)

If
\mathrm{curr}(v)=1
and
\mathrm{pos}(v)=1
, then
(v,i,b)\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
for some
b\in[T]
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
contains no activity interval of
v
and therefore
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v)=\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)=0
.

(4)

If
\mathrm{curr}(v)\geq 1
and
\mathrm{pos}(v)=0
, then either
(v,a,i-1)\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
for some
a\in[i-1]
or
v
is not active in
G_{i-1}
with respect to the activity intervals from
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.
So by definition
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v)\leq\mathrm{curr}(v)
and either
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)=\min(i-1,\ell+1)
or
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)=0
.

Now consider the set
\mathcal{A}
of temporal edges until time step
i
that are covered by
\mathcal{T}
and the set
\mathcal{A^{\prime}}
of temporal edges until time step
i-1
that are covered by
\mathcal{T^{\prime}}
.
There are two cases for a temporal edge
(vw,j)\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

Case 1:
j=i
.
Since the temporal edge
(vw,j)
is covered by
\mathcal{T}
we can conclude that
vw\in E_{G_{i}}(\{v\in F_{i}:\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 1\},F_{i})
.

Case 2:
j<i
.
We have
(vw,j)\in\binom{\overleftarrow{F}_{i-1}}{2}\times[i-1]
.
This implies the existence of an activity interval
(v,a,b)
or
(w,a,b)
in
\mathcal{T}
with
a\leq i-1
.
However, then the activity interval is by definition contained in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
and consequently
(vw,j)\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

Hence, any temporal edge in
\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is contained in
E_{G_{i}}(\{v\in F_{i}:\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 1\},F_{i})
. We thus have the desired inequality

\displaystyle\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]=|\mathcal{A}|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}|+|E_{G_{i}}(\{v\in F_{i}:\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 1\},F_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq\DP[i-1,\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}]+|E_{G_{i}}(\{v\in F_{i}:\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 1\},F_{i})|.

(
\geq
) Suppose the maximum on the right side of the computation is attained for
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}
and the maximum in the definition of
\DP[i-1,\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}]
is attained for
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.
We define
\mathcal{T}
by extending
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
in the following way:
For
v\in F_{i-1}\cap F_{i}
the activity interval
(v,i,\min(i+\ell,T))
is added if and only if
\mathrm{pos}(v)=1
. These new intervals together with the already active ones (
\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 2
) cover all edges of
E_{G_{i}}(\{v\in F_{i}:\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 1\},F_{i})
. Hence, the total number of edges covered by
\mathcal{T}
is
\DP[i-1,\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}]+|E_{G_{i}}(\{v\in F_{i}:\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 1\},F_{i})|
.

Because
\mathcal{T}
is considered in the definition of
\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]
, we have

\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]\geq\DP[i-1,\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}]+|E_{G_{i}}(\{v\in F_{i}:\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 1\},F_{i})|.

Hence, the desired inequality holds.

Result:

Finally, we return yes if and only if
\DP[T,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]\geq t
for
\mathrm{curr}:F_{T}\rightarrow\{k\}
and some function
\mathrm{pos}:F_{T}\rightarrow\{0,\ell+1\}
.
The restriction to these functions is correct, since we can assume without loss of generality that a vertex is active in
G_{T}
if and only if its
k
-th activity interval ends at time step
T
(recall that we assume that each vertex is in at least
k(\ell+1)+1
bags).
Consequently, all
k
-activity
\ell
-timelines of
\mathcal{G}
are considered in the definition of the table entry
\DP[T,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]
and all covered temporal edges of
\mathcal{G}
are counted in the respective maximum of the definition.

Running Time:

For each time step
i\in[T]
, there are
\big((k+1)(\ell+2)\big)^{\vimw}
choices for the functions
\mathrm{curr}:F_{i}\rightarrow[0,k]
and
\mathrm{pos}:F_{i}\rightarrow[0,\ell+1]
.
This upper-bounds the size of our dynamic programming table by
T\cdot\big((k+1)(\ell+2)\big)^{\vimw}
.
In each recursive computation of a table entry, there are also
\mathcal{O}(\big((k+1)(\ell+2)\big)^{\vimw})
choices for
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}
.
The remaining summands of the recursive formula can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n)
time.
Hence, the overall running time can be bounded by
\big((k+1)(\ell+2)\big)^{2\cdot\vimw}\cdot(n+T)^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
.
∎

Now, we show that for Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVCthe parameter
\vimw=\vimw[1]
can be replaced by an even smaller parameter,
\vimw[k(\ell+1)+1]
.
The algorithm for this parameter has two steps: First it applies preprocessing step that handles the large bags. Then, it invokes the algorithm of
\Cref
CovTimeLine_vimw+k+l for
\vimw=\vimw[1]
.

Proposition 5
.

Phys. Rev. B
Timeline PVCis solvable in
\big((k+1)(\ell+2)\big)^{4\cdot\vimw[x]}\cdot(n+T)^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time where
x=k(\ell+1)+1
.

Proof.

Let
(F_{i})_{i\in[T]}
be the bags of the vertex-interval-membership sequence of
\mathcal{G}
.
Similar to the proof of
\Cref
CovTimeLine_vimw+k+l, it is safe to assume that each vertex
v
is contained in at least
k\cdot(\ell+1)
bags (by definition the bags containing
v
need to be consecutive) because otherwise, we can greedily pick the
k
-activity intervals of
v
such that
v
is active during all these snapshots and thus all temporal edges incident to
v
are covered and we can reduce
t
accordingly.

We call the
k\cdot(\ell+1)
largest bags of
(F_{i})_{i\in[T]}

large
.
The idea is to pick the intervals greedily for all vertices that are only contained in large bags.
Intuitively, this works since the large bags appear consecutively.
Afterwards, we remove all these vertices and adapt
t
accordingly.
Finally, we can invoke the algorithm of
\Cref
CovTimeLine_vimw+k+l.

Now, let
[i,j]\subset[T]
be a sequence of consecutive indices such that each bag
F_{x}
for each
x\in[i,j]
is large.
By definition
j-i\leq k\cdot(\ell+1)-1
.
Now, let
\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{x\in[i,j]}F_{x}\setminus(F_{i-1}\cup F_{j+1})
.
If
i=0
or
j=T
, then
F_{i-1}
or
F_{j+1}
is simply the empty set.
Suppose
\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset
.
By definition, each vertex in
\mathcal{A}
is isolated in
G_{1},\ldots,G_{i-1}
and also in
G_{j+1},\ldots,G_{T}
.
By our assumption that each vertex is contained in at least
k\cdot(\ell+1)
bags, we can conclude that
j-i=k\cdot(\ell+1)-1
.
Now observe that by picking the
k
-activity intervals for each vertex in
\mathcal{A}
greedily, we can cover all temporal edges which are incident to some vertex of

After removing

Now, suppose

In this case, we can conclude

Now, we focus on Phys. Rev. BTimeline (Partial) Dominating Set.
Initially, we show how to adapt the algorithm of
\Cref
CovTimeLine_vimw+k+l for Phys. Rev. BTimeline PDSto obtain an algorithm with the same running time for Phys. Rev. BTimeline PDS. However, we need to be more careful since isolated vertices in the snapshots can no longer be ignored as for Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVC.
This means that vertices which are not contained in the current bag of the vertex-interval-membership sequence might have to be active.
Hence, the update of the dynamic program additionally needs to consider the case that activity intervals of vertices appear before or after their first or last occurrence in a bag.

Theorem 6
.

Phys. Rev. B
Timeline PDSis solvable in
\big((k+1)(\ell+2)\big)^{2\cdot\vimw}\cdot(n+T)^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{G}=(G_{1},\dots,G_{T}),k,\ell,t)
be an instance of Phys. Rev. BTimeline PDS.
Again, suppose that
T\geq k\cdot(\ell+1)
(otherwise, it is a trivial instance) and that the underlying graph
G_{\downarrow}
contains no isolated vertex (otherwise, decrease
t
by
k\cdot(\ell+1)
and delete the vertex).

The idea of the dynamic program over the lifetime of the temporal graph is analogous to the algorithm of
\Cref
CovTimeLine_vimw+k+l.
Recall that the dynamic program keeps track of the activity of the vertices which are contained in the bag
F_{i}
of the currently considered time step

.
Again, while iterating over the lifetime of the temporal graph, we need to ensure that the activities at a time step are compatible with the activities at the neighboring time steps.
Moreover, we need to be careful as it might be optimal for some vertices to be active at time steps where they are not contained in the respective bag to dominate some isolated vertices.

Recall that the vertex-interval-membership sequence can be computed in polynomial time.
Since we assumed that the underlying graph contains no isolated vertex, it follows that each vertex is contained in at least one bag of the sequence.

Table Definition:

Again we let
\overleftarrow{F}_{i}:=F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{i}
and
\overline{F}_{i}:=\overleftarrow{F}_{i}\setminus F_{i}
.
Recall that
\overline{F}_{i}
is the set of vertices which are isolated in all snapshots
G_{i},\dots,G_{T}
.
Moreover, again for

we define functions
{\mathrm{curr}:F_{i}\rightarrow[0,k],}
and
\mathrm{pos}:F_{i}\rightarrow[0,\ell+1]
which indicate for each vertex

whether an activity interval of

is active during snapshot
G_{i}
.
Recall that the function value
\mathrm{curr}(v)
determines the current activity interval of

and the function value
\mathrm{pos}(v)

Now, the entry

in the whole temporal graph and of
F_{i}
in the snapshots

, while

\mathrm{pos}(v)
-th position of the
\mathrm{curr}(v)
-th activity interval of

.
We are now ready to present the formal definition of the table.

\displaystyle\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]\,\hat{=}\,\max_{\mathcal{T}}\,

\displaystyle|\{(v,j)\in\overline{F}_{i}\times[T]:(v,j)\text{ is dominated by }\mathcal{T}\}

\displaystyle\cup\{(v,j)\in F_{i}\times[i]:(v,j)\text{ is dominated by }\mathcal{T}\}|,

where the maximum is taken over all
k
-activity
\ell
-timelines

\mathcal{T}\subseteq(\overline{F}_{i}\times[T]\times[T])\cup(F_{i}\times[i]\times[T]),

such that for each

(i)

there are at most
\mathrm{curr}(v)
activity intervals of
v
in
\mathcal{T}
,

(ii)

\mathrm{pos}(v)\leq i
, and

(iii)

(v,i-\mathrm{pos}(v)+1,b)\in\mathcal{T}

We need conditions (i)-(iii) for the identical reasons as for Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVC:
Condition (i) ensures that each vertex has at most

Moreover, condition (ii) ensures that no activity interval starts before time step one.
Finally, condition (iii) ensures that each activity interval has length

Initialization:

For all
\mathrm{curr}:F_{1}\rightarrow[0,k]
and
\mathrm{pos}:F_{1}\rightarrow[0,1]

\mathrm{curr}(v)=0

\mathrm{pos}(v)=0

\DP[1,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]=|N_{G_{1}}[\{v\in F_{1}:\mathrm{pos}(v)=1\}]|.

Note that
\overline{F}_{1}=\emptyset
and therefore only activity timelines

are considered in the definition of
\DP[1,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]

The initialization is correct, since the table entry
\DP[1,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]

in

Recurrence:

Intuitively,
\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]
is computed by trying all activity interval choices for the vertices of
F_{i-1}

\mathrm{curr}
and
\mathrm{pos}

Clearly, all vertices which are either active during time step

get dominated (summand (A1)).
However, we also need to consider how many of the vertices from
F_{i}
and
F_{i-1}
are isolated in
G_{1},\dots,G_{i-1}
and
G_{i},\dots,G_{T}
respectively.
In particular, we need to count how many of these isolated temporal vertices are dominated by a solution candidate.
More precisely, two cases are possible:
First, if

is isolated in

, then in each time step in
[i-1]
, which intersects an activity interval of
v

Second, if

v
itself is dominated (summands (A4) and (A5)).
Consequently, the formal update is as follows:

\displaystyle\DP[i,\mathrm{curr},\mathrm{pos}]=

\displaystyle\max_{\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}}\,

\displaystyle\DP[i-1,\mathrm{curr}^{\prime},\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle+|N_{G_{i}}[\{v\in F_{i}:\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 1\}]|

\displaystyle(A1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in F_{i}\setminus F_{i-1}:\\
\mathrm{pos}(v)=0\end{subarray}}\min\big(\mathrm{curr}(v)(\ell+1),i-1\big)

\displaystyle(A2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in F_{i}\setminus F_{i-1}:\\
\mathrm{pos}(v)>0\end{subarray}}\min\big((\mathrm{curr}(v)-1)(\ell+1)+\mathrm{pos}(v)-1,i-1\big)

\displaystyle(A3)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in F_{i-1}\setminus F_{i}:\\
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)=0\end{subarray}}\min((k-\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v))(\ell+1),T-i+1)

\displaystyle(A4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in F_{i-1}\setminus F_{i}:\\
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)>0\end{subarray}}\min((k-\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v))(\ell+1)+\ell+1-\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)),T-i+1)

\displaystyle(A5),

where the maximum is taken over all
\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}:F_{i-1}\rightarrow[0,k]
and
\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}:F_{i-1}\rightarrow[0,\ell+1]
which are

with

Here, compatibility has the identical meaning as in the algorithm in

Informally,

at time step

Formally, four conditions need to be fulfilled:

This can be formalized as follows.

\text{If }\mathrm{pos}(v)\geq 2\text{, then }\mathrm{curr}^{\prime}(v)\leq\mathrm{curr}(v)\text{ and }\mathrm{pos}^{\prime}(v)=\mathrm{pos}(v)-1.

(1)

Second, if a new activity interval of

either

(4)

For each time step

Theorem 12
.

Phys. Rev. B
Timeline DSis solvable in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{2+nk}T)
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{G}=(G_{1},\dots,G_{T}),k,\ell)
be an instance of Phys. Rev. BTimeline DS.

The algorithm starts with an empty solution candidate
\mathcal{T}=\emptyset
and recursively adds activity intervals to this set.
Furthermore, it keeps one counter
k_{v}
for each vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{G})
for the number of activity intervals.

If
k_{v}>k
for some
v\in V

If
\mathcal{T}
dominates all temporal vertices in
\mathcal{G}

Otherwise, let

G_{i}
contains an undominated vertex
v

For each

the algorithm branches by adding
(u,i,i+\ell)
to
\mathcal{T}

k_{u}
by one and continuing recursively.

The size of the closed neighborhood of
v

is at most
n

In each recursive call the algorithm selects one activity interval and a solution can contain at most

Consequently, the search tree is of size

{\mathcal{O}(n^{nk}\cdot n^{2}T)}

Theorem 16
.

Phys. Rev. B
Timeline PDSis solvable in
2^{\mathcal{O}(t)}\cdot(n+T)^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
time.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{G}=(G_{1},\dots,G_{T}),k,\ell,t)
be a Phys. Rev. BTimeline PDS-instance.
We assume that
T\geq k(\ell+1)
(otherwise the instance is trivial) and fix some order
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
of the vertices from
V(\mathcal{G})
.
The idea is to use color coding, so we assume that our temporal vertices are colored randomly with
t
colors.
We say that an activity timeline
\mathcal{T}
dominates a color, if it dominates a temporal vertex of the respective color and we denote with
C_{a,b}^{j}
the colors, which are dominated by the activity interval
(v_{j},a,b)
.
Now, consider the following dynamic programming table.

Table Definition:

For a set
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,t\}
of colors, and integers
j\in[0,n],k^{\prime}\in[0,k]
we define

\displaystyle\DP[S,j,k^{\prime}]\,\,\hat{=}\,\,\texttt{true}

if and only if there exists a
k
-activity
\ell
-timeline
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\{v_{1},\dots,v_{j}\}\times[T]\times[T]
such that

(i)

|\mathcal{T}\cap(\{v_{j}\}\times[T]\times[T])|=k^{\prime}
, and

(ii)

\mathcal{T}
dominates the colors from
S
.

In other words,
\DP[S,j,k^{\prime}]\,\,\hat{=}\,\,\texttt{true}
if and only if the colors from
S
can be dominated by using
k
activity intervals of
v_{1},\dots,v_{j-1}
and
k^{\prime}
activity intervals of
v_{j}
.

Initialization:

We set the entry
\DP[S,0,k]
to
true
if and only if
S=\emptyset
.

Recurrence:

We consider the case where
k^{\prime}=0
and
k^{\prime}>0
individually:

\displaystyle\DP[S,j,k^{\prime}]=\begin{cases}\bigvee_{a\in[T-\ell]}\DP[S\setminus C_{a,a+\ell}^{j},j,k^{\prime}-1],&\,\text{ if }k^{\prime}>0,\\
\DP[S,j-1,k],&\,\text{ if }k^{\prime}=0.\end{cases}

Correctness:

We prove the correctness of the computation by showing two directions.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose
\DP[S,j,k^{\prime}]=\texttt{true}
.
By definition, there exists a
k
-activity
\ell
-timeline
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\{v_{1},\dots,v_{j}\}\times[T]\times[T]
, which satisfies (i) and (ii).
Otherwise, if
k^{\prime}>0
, then there exists an activity interval
(v_{j},a,a+\ell)\in\mathcal{T}
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{T}\setminus\{(v_{j},a,a+\ell)\}
is considered in the definition of
\DP[S\setminus C_{a,a+\ell}^{j},j,k^{\prime}-1]
and satisfies (i) and (ii).
Therefore, we have
\DP[S\setminus C_{a,a+\ell}^{j},j,k^{\prime}-1]=\texttt{true}
.
If
k^{\prime}=0
, then
\mathcal{T}
only contains activity intervals of
v_{1},\dots,v_{j}
.
This means that
\mathcal{T}
considered in the definition of the table entry
\DP[S,j-1,k]
and subsequently
\DP[S,j-1,k]=\texttt{true}
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Let
k^{\prime}>0
and
\DP[S\setminus C_{a,a+\ell}^{j},j,k^{\prime}-1]=\texttt{true}
for some
a\in[T-\ell]
.
If
\mathcal{T}
is considered in the definition of
\DP[S\setminus C_{a,a+\ell}^{j},j,k^{\prime}-1]

\DP[S,j,k^{\prime}]

\DP[S,j,k^{\prime}]=\texttt{true}
.
If
k^{\prime}=0
, then each
k
-activity
\ell
-timeline, which is considered in the definition of
\DP[S,j-1,k]
and satisfies (i) and (ii), is also considered in the table entry
\DP[S,j,k^{\prime}=0]

Result:

Finally, we return yes if and only if

In particular, we return yes if and only if there exists a
k
-activity

Running Time:

The table has size
\mathcal{O}(2^{t}\cdot n\cdot k)

The overall running time can then be bounded by

Conclusion

We studied the classical and parameterized complexity of Phys. Rev. BTimeline (Partial) Vertex Coverand Phys. Rev. BTimeline (Partial) Dominating Set.
We showed that all problems admit FPT-algorithms for
\vimw+\,k+\ell
, where
\vimw
is the vertex-interval-membership width.
Our running time bounds also give XP-algorithms for
\vimw
alone.
For Phys. Rev. BTimeline VCthis improves upon an XP-algorithm for
n

[
19
]
.
Moreover, we showed that the smaller parameter
\imw+\,k+\ell
leads to para-NP-hardness for all problems except Phys. Rev. BTimeline DS.
Hence, we discovered a parameter where a dominating set problem is tractable while the corresponding vertex cover variant is not.

For future work it is interesting to investigate whether already the parameter
\vimw+\,k
yields an FPT-algorithm for any problem in our study.
Moreover, it is open whether Phys. Rev. BTimeline PVCand Phys. Rev. BTimeline PDSadmit an FPT-algorithm with respect to
n+k
.
Even for
k=1
fixed-parameter tractability with respect to
n
is still open.
Moreover, one could investigate the complexity with respect to other temporal graph parameters such as the temporal neighborhood diversity
[
16
]
or temporal graph parameters that are sensitive to the order of the snapshots
[
6
,
5
]
.

A further problem related to Phys. Rev. BTimeline VCis Phys. Rev. BMinTimeline
+
.
In this problem, the value of
\ell
bounds the sum of the lengths of the activity intervals instead of the length of the longest activity interval.
Phys. Rev. BMinTimeline

[

and studied in several works
[

.
To distinguish this problem from the other problems in our naming convention, Phys. Rev. BTimeline VC(Phys. Rev. BMinTimeline
∞
) could be called Phys. Rev. BMinMax Phys. Rev. BTimeline VC and
Phys. Rev. BMinTimeline
+
could be called Phys. Rev. BSum Phys. Rev. BTimeline VC, and the timeline variants of Phys. Rev. BDominating Setcould be named analogously.
It is open which of our positive results for
\vimw
and
\imw

Finally, it is interesting to study other classic problems as
Feedback Vertex Set
in the Phys. Rev. BMinMax Timeline and Phys. Rev. BSum Timeline setting.

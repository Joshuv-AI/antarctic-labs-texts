Subinjective and Subprojective Extension-reflecting modules
ENGİN Büyükaşık

Abstract
. Given a right R R -module M M and any short exact sequence of right R R -modules 0 → A → B → C → 0 , 0\to A\to B\to C\to 0, it is well known that if both A A and C C belong to the subinjectivity domain ℑ ​ 𝔫 ¯ − 1 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(M) (resp., the subprojectivity domain 𝔓 ​ 𝔯 ¯ − 1 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(M) ) of M M , then B B also belongs to the corresponding domain. Module classes satisfying this closure property are said to be closed under extensions. Let ℑ ​ 𝔫 ​ ( M ) = { N ∈ Mod − ⁡ R ∣ M ∈ ℑ ​ 𝔫 ¯ − 1 ​ ( N ) } ​ and ​ 𝔓 ​ 𝔯 ​ ( M ) = { N ∈ Mod − ⁡ R ∣ M ∈ 𝔓 ​ 𝔯 ¯ − 1 ​ ( N ) } . \mathfrak{In}(M)=\{N\in\operatorname{Mod-}R\mid M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(N)\}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\mathfrak{Pr}(M)=\{N\in\operatorname{Mod-}R\mid M\in\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(N)\}. Unlike ℑ ​ 𝔫 ¯ − 1 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(M) and 𝔓 ​ 𝔯 ¯ − 1 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(M) , the classes ℑ ​ 𝔫 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{In}(M) and 𝔓 ​ 𝔯 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{Pr}(M) are not, in general, closed under extensions. In this paper, we investigate certain classes of modules and rings that ensure the extension-closure of ℑ ​ 𝔫 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{In}(M) and 𝔓 ​ 𝔯 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{Pr}(M) . We prove that if the injective hull of M M is projective, then ℑ ​ 𝔫 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{In}(M) is closed under extension; and if M M is a homomorphic image of a module which is both projective and injective, then 𝔓 ​ 𝔯 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{Pr}(M) is closed under extensions. As a consequence, over a QF-ring, the classes ℑ ​ 𝔫 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{In}(M) and 𝔓 ​ 𝔯 ​ ( M ) \mathfrak{Pr}(M) are closed under extensions for every module M M . We further explore several implications of these results and present examples of modules with the above properties over arbitrary rings. Additionally, we introduce the rings whose right modules of finite length are homomorphic images of injective modules. Among other results, we prove that, if ℑ ​ 𝔫 ​ ( R ) \mathfrak{In}(R) is closed under extensions, then modules of finite length are homomorphic image of injectives if and and only if simple modules are homomorphic image of injectives.

Throughout this paper,
R
will be an associative ring with identity and by a module we shall mean a unital right
R
-module, unless otherwise stated. The category of all right
R
-modules over a ring
R
will be denoted by
\operatorname{Mod-}R
. For a module

, we write

to indicate that
N
is a submodule of

. The classes of injective and projective modules are denoted by
{\mathcal{I}_{0}}
and
{\mathcal{P}_{0}}

Recall that, given two modules

R
-homomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(N,M)

-modules

such that

is injective relative to

\mathfrak{In^{-1}}(M)
, is called the
injectivity domain
of

. The class of semisimple modules is contained in
\mathfrak{In^{-1}}(M)
for any module module

[
3
]
). Poor modules are the opposites of injectives in the sense that their injectivity domain is as small as possible. For any module

,
\mathfrak{In^{-1}}(N)
is closed under taking submodules, finite direct sum and quotients (see,
[
7
]
). That is,
\mathfrak{In^{-1}}(N)
is a hereditary pretorsion class. For more detailed and comprehensive study of injectivity domains, we refer to
[
7
]
and
[
27
]
.

Another alternative approach to the usual notions of injectivity and projectivity of modules has been investigated in
[
9
]
and
[
21
]
, respectively. Since then, these topics have attracted considerable attention from researchers and have come to occupy an important place in module and ring theory. Many well-known module and ring structures have been characterized in terms of these new notions.

Following
[
9
]
, given two modules
A
and
B
,
B
is said to be
A
-subinjective if for every extension
C
of
A
and every homomorphism
\phi:A\to B
there exists a homomorphism
\varphi:C\to B
such that
\varphi|_{A}=\phi
. For a module
B
, the subinjectivity domain of

is defined to be the collection of all modules
A
such that
B
is
A
-subinjective. Subprojectivity and subprojectivity domains are defined dually (see
[
21
]
). Explicitly, the subinjectivity and subprojectivity domains of a module
B
, respectively, are the classes:
\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(B)=\{A\in\operatorname{Mod-}R\mid\text{$B$ is $A$-subinjective}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(B)=\{A\in\operatorname{Mod-}R\mid\text{$B$ is $A$-subprojective}\}.

A right
R
-module
M
is indigent (resp. p-indigent) if
\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(M)={\mathcal{I}_{0}}
(resp.
\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}
(M)=
{\mathcal{P}_{0}}
).
A ring
R
is said to have no right subinjective (resp. subprojective) middle class if every module is indigent (resp. p-indigent) or injective (resp. projective).

Indigent and p-indigent modules, along with some of their generalizations, continue to be an active area of research. Several open problems remain, such as the existence of indigent modules over an arbitrary ring and the complete characterization of rings that lack a subinjective (or subprojective) middle class. In recent years, generalizations of these concepts to different categories and classes of modules have also attracted significant attention, see for example
[
1
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

For a class of modules
\mathcal{A}
, the class
\mathfrak{siP}(\mathcal{A})=\{\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(M)\mid M\in\mathcal{A}\}
is said to be the subinjective profile of
\mathcal{A}.
Suprojectivity profile of
\mathcal{A}
is defined similarly, and denoted as
\mathfrak{sP}(\mathcal{A})
. The classes of simple modules, cyclic modules, modules of finite length and finitely generated modules will be denoted by
\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{FL}
and
\mathcal{FG}
, respectively.
Subinjective profiles of
\mathcal{A}
extensively studied in the literature, especially for the classes
\mathcal{S}
and
\operatorname{Mod-}R
(see,
[

,

,

,

\mathfrak{sP}(\mathcal{FG})
and
\mathfrak{siP}(\mathcal{FG})
are studied in
[

]
and
[

]
, respectively.

In the study of rings and modules related to subinjectivity and subprojectivity domains, it is important to understand certain intrinsic properties of these domains. For example, both subinjectivity and subprojectivity domains are closed under finite direct sums and extensions (Lemma
1
). Moreover,
R
is right hereditary if and only if
\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(M)
is closed under homomorphic images for each
M\in\operatorname{Mod-}R
(
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
) if and only if
\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(M)
is closed submodules for each
M\in\operatorname{Mod-}R
(
[
21
, Proposition 2.15]
).
As can be seen easily,
\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(M)
is closed under submodules if and only if
M
is injective; and hence
R
is semisimple artinian if and only if
\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(N)
closed under submodules for each
N\in\operatorname{Mod-}R.

As demonstrated, subinjectivity domains possess some of the closure properties enjoyed by injectivity domains, but only when certain conditions on the module or the underlying ring are satisfied. This distinction motivates a deeper investigation into the structural conditions under which subinjectivity and subprojectivity domains enjoy additional certain closure properties.

To contribute to the studies in this direction, in this paper, we introduce and investigate the following notions. For a module
M
, let
\mathfrak{In}(M)=\{N\in\operatorname{Mod-}R\mid M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(N)\}
and
\mathfrak{Pr}(M)=\{N\in\operatorname{Mod-}R\mid M\in\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(N)\}.
It is clear that,
M
is injective (resp. projective) if and only if
\mathfrak{In}(M)=\{\operatorname{Mod-}R\}
(resp.
\mathfrak{Pr}(M)=\{\operatorname{Mod-}R\}
).

Given a right
R
-module
M
and any short exact sequence
0\to A\to B\to C\to 0
of right
R
-modules,
M
is said to be
subinjective extension-reflecting (si.e.r)
in case
M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(A)\cap\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(C)
implies
M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(B)
.
M
is
subprojective extension-reflecting (sp.e.r)
if
M\in\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(A)\cap\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(C)
implies
M\in\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(B)
. Equivalently that is to say,
M
is si.e.r. (resp. sp.e.r.) if and only if
\mathfrak{In}(M)
(resp.
\mathfrak{Pr}(M))
is closed under extensions.

It is worth mentioning that, depending on the context they appear, the equivalent expressions ”N is si.e.r (sp.e.r)” and ”
\mathfrak{In}(N)\,(\mathfrak{Pr}(N))
is closed under extensions” will be used interchangeably throughout the paper.

Injective and projective modules are trivial examples of si.e.r and sp.e.r modules, respectively. There are modules that are not si.e.r or sp.e.r (see Examples
1
and
2
).

The paper is organized as follows.

In section 2, we first give some examples in order to show that, for a given module
N,

\mathfrak{In}(N)
and
\mathfrak{Pr}(N)
are not closed under extensions in general. We provide sufficient conditions ensuring that
\mathfrak{In}(M)
and
\mathfrak{Pr}(M)
are closed under extensions. Namely, we prove that, (1) if
N
is a submodule of a module that is both injective and projective, then
\mathfrak{In}(N)
is closed under extensions, and (2) if
N
is a homomorphic image of a module that is both injective and projective, then
\mathfrak{Pr}(N)
is closed under extensions. We conclude that, over a
QF
-ring every right (or left) module
N
, both
\mathfrak{In}(N)
and
\mathfrak{Pr}(N)
are closed under extensions. We also show that, over a ring
R
without subinjective (subprojective) middle class, which is not necessarily
QF,

\mathfrak{In}(N)
(
\mathfrak{Pr}
(N)) is closed under extensions for each
N\in\operatorname{Mod-}R
.

The class of si.e.r. modules is closed under taking submodules, whereas the class of sp.e.r. modules is closed under taking factor modules.
For an si.e.r module
M
, we prove that
(1)

M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(S)
for each
S\in\mathcal{S}
if and only if
M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(A)
for each
A\in\mathcal{FL}
; and
(2)

M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(C)
for each
C\in\mathcal{C}
if and only if
M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(D)
for each
D\in\mathcal{FG}
. Dual of these results are also obtained for sp.e.r modules, as well.

For a
QF
-ring, we prove that for each
\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{C},\,\mathcal{FL},\,\mathcal{FG} \}

{\mathcal{I}_{0}}=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(A)=\bigcap_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(B)={\mathcal{P}_{0}}.

Since
{\mathcal{I}_{0}}={\mathcal{P}_{0}}
over a QF-ring, indigent and p-indigent modules coincide. We obtain that, for a QF-ring, a module
B
is indigent if and only if
\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(A)\cap\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(B/A)={\mathcal{I}_{0}}
for each
A\leq B.

As a byproduct of these results, for several well-known module classes, such as,
FG
-injective,
C
-injective,
FG
-projective and
C
-projective we obtain that: (a) If
M
is si.e.r., then
M
is
FG
-injective if and only if it is
C
-injective. (b) If
M
is sp.e.r., then
M
is
FG
-projective if and only if it is
C
-projective. These results leads to: over a QF-ring, all the notions
FG
-injective,
C
-injective,
FG
-projective and
C
-projective coincide with
{\mathcal{I}_{0}}
(=
{\mathcal{P}_{0}}
).

It is well known that a ring
R
is QF if and only if every right
R
-module is a homomorphic image of an injective module. In
[
23
]
, the authors study rings whose proper cyclic modules are homomorphic images of injective modules. More recently, rings whose simple right modules are homomorphic images of injective modules have been investigated in
[
14
]
; such rings are referred to as right dual Kasch rings.

In section 3, we investigate rings whose modules of finite (composition) length are homomorphic images of injective modules. Such rings are said to satisfy property
(Q)
.
We prove that a ring
R
satisfies
(Q)
if and only if
R\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(N)
for each
N\in\mathcal{FL}
. We show that right artinian rings satisfying
(Q)
are QF. An example is provided to demonstrate that the class of dual Kasch rings properly contains the class of rings satisfying
(Q)
. When
\mathfrak{In}(R)
is closed under extensions, we prove that

satisfies
(Q)
if and only if
R
is dual Kasch. It is also shown that factor rings of rings satisfying
(Q)
need not themselves satisfy
(Q)
. For a commutative noetherian ring

, we prove that every factor ring of
R
satisfies
(Q)
if and only if every factor ring of
R
is QF.

Definition 1
.

Let
M
be a module and

\operatorname{Mod-}R

(1)

is

if
\mathfrak{In}(M)
is closed under extension. That is, the condition
M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(A)\cap\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(C)
implies
M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(B)
for every short exact sequence
\mathbb{E}.

(2)

M
is
subprojective extension-reflecting (sp.e.r)
if
\mathfrak{Pr}(M)
is closed under extension. That is, the condition
M\in\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(A)\cap\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(C)
implies
M\in\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(B)
for every short exact sequence
\mathbb{E}.

Definition 2
.

R
is said to be right fully si.e.r. (resp. sp.e.r.) if every right
R
-module is si.e.r. (resp. sp.e.r.). Left fully si.e.r and left fully sp.e.r. rings are defined similarly.

We begin by presenting examples to show there exist modules which are not si.e.r or sp.e.r.

Example 1
.

Consider the ring

R=\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&(b,c)\\
0&a\end{array}\right)\mid a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}\}.

Then
R
is a commutative (finite) artinian local ring with

J(R)=soc(R)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\\
0&0\end{array}\right).

By
[
14
, Theorem 3.4]

R
is dual Kasch. Thus
R\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(R/J(R))
by
[
14
, Theorem 2.3]
. Then
R\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(J(R))
, because
J(R)
is semisimple. Hence
R\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(J(R))\cap\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(R/J(R)).
On the other hand, since
R
is local and

is not simple,
R
is not a QF ring by
[
22
, Theorem 15.27]
, and so
R
is not self-injective. Thus
R\notin\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(R).
Hence
R
is not si.e.r.

Example 2
.

In the category of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules,
\mathbb{Q}
is not sp.e.r. Consider the exact sequnce
0\to\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\to 0.
Clearly,
\mathbb{Z}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-subprojective. Also
Hom(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},\,\mathbb{Q})=0
implies
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-subprojective. There is an epimorphism
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}\to\mathbb{Q}\to 0.
Since
Hom(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})})=0,

\mathbb{Q}
is not
\mathbb{Q}

\mathbb{Q}\in\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(\mathbb{Z})\cap\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathbb{Q}\notin\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(\mathbb{Q}).

\mathbb{Q}
is not sp.e.r.

Example 3
.

(1)

Every injective module is si.e.r., since
{\mathcal{I}_{0}}\subseteq\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(N)
for every
N\in\operatorname{Mod-}R.

(2)

If
M
is an si.e.r module, then

For every module

(3)

Any right hereditary right noetherian ring

(4)

As a dual of
(1)
and

, every projective module is sp.e.r., and if
M
is an sp.e.r. module, then
M\oplus P
is sp.e.r for every projective module

Proposition 1
.

The classes of si.e.r and sp.e.r modules are closed under finite direct sums.

Proof.

Let
M=M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{n}
be a direct sum of modules. For a module

M\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(N)\,(\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(N))
if and only if
M_{i}\in\mathfrak{\underline{In}^{-1}}(N)\,(\mathfrak{\underline{Pr}^{-1}}(N))
for
i=1,\cdots,n
(see
[
9
, Proposition 2.5]
and
[
21
, Proposition 2.12]
) Thus,
M
is si.e.r. (resp. sp.e.r.) if and only if
M_{i}
is si.e.r. (resp. sp.e.r.) for each
i=1,\cdots,n.

∎

Following
[
4
]
, a module
M
is said to be a test module for injectivity by subinjectivity or t.i.b.s. module if

Proposition 2
.

Every t.i.b.s. module is si.e.r.

Proof.

gh=f.
Now, by injectivity of

that is, the map

Hence

(3)\Rightarrow(2)
is clear.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
Let

Lemma 2
.

A right artinian ring

Remark 1
.

Every commutative artinian ring is a Kasch ring and hence also a dual Kasch ring by
[
4
, Corollary 3.2]
. An artinian ring satisfies
(Q)
if and only if it is QF by Lemma

. Therefore, any commutative artinian ring that is not QF provides an example of a dual Kasch ring that does not satisfy
(Q)
(see, Example

In the following result, we provide a condition under which dual Kasch rings satisfy
(Q)
.

Proposition 15
.

Let
R

R
satisfies
(Q)

Proof.

To prove the necessity, assume that

is right dual Kasch. Then

for each simple right

Let

be a nonzero right

First we shall prove that

by induction on

If

is simple, and so the claim follows by the assumption. Assume that

, and every nonzero module of composition length less than

∎

Since factor rings for QF-rings need not be QF, the rings that satisfy

need not be closed under factor rings.

Example 5

Let

[

A ring

if and only if every factor ring of

Proposition 17
.

Let

be a right artinian ring. Every factor ring of

satisfies
(Q)
if and only if

is super QF.

Proof.

Factor rings of artinian rings are artininian. Thus the proof follows by Lemma

.
∎

Proposition 18
.

Let

be a commutative noetherian ring. Every factor ring of

satisfies
(Q)
if and only if

Proof.

Sufficiency is clear. To prove the necessity, assume that every factor ring of

Abstract
. We introduce a recursive theory that completely axiomatizes the structure ⟨ ℤ , < , + , f , 0 ⟩ \langle\mathbbm{Z},<,+,f,0\rangle where f f is the function that maps each x x to the integer part of φ ​ x \varphi x , with φ \varphi the golden ratio. We prove that our axiomatization is model-complete in a language expanded with a function which we call the Fibonacci floor function , as defined in the introduction. We also present an improvement of the axiomatization of the structure ⟨ ℤ , + , f , 0 ⟩ \langle\mathbbm{Z},+,f,0\rangle already studied in [ KZ22 ] .

1.
Introduction

Let
\varphi
be the golden ratio which, as a quadratic number, is the smallest root of the polynomial
x^{2}+x+1=0
. Also, let
f:\mathbbm{Z}\to\mathbbm{Z}
be the function that maps
each integer
n
to the integer part of
\varphi n
, namely

\lfloor\varphi n\rfloor
. In this paper we study the structure
\mathcal{Z}_{\varphi}^{<}:=\langle\mathbbm{Z},<,+,f,0\rangle
where, as later will be seen,
the Fibonacci sequence plays a significant role.
The Fibonacci sequence is the sequence defined recursively as:

\begin{cases}F_{0}=1,F_{1}=1,\\
F_{n+2}=F_{n}+\textcompwordmark F_{n+1}.\end{cases}

Recall that this sequence, and in fact any recursive sequence, is definable in the language of Peano arithmetic which contains addition, multiplication, and order.
We observe that the set of Fibonacci numbers is definable in the language
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\{<,+,f,0\}
. Based on that, we introduce a new function
\lfloor-\rfloor_{\fib}:\mathbbm{Z}\to\mathbbm{Z}
, which we call the
Fibonacci floor function
, that maps each
n
to the largest Fibonacci number smaller than
n
, and of an even index. As we proceed, we present the required axioms for this function, which, together with the axioms concerning a certain set of extrema, are able to capture the model theoretic content of the structure
\mathcal{Z}_{\varphi}^{<}
. This leads to our main theorem (
3.15
):

Theorem
.

The structure

\langle\mathbbm{Z},<,+,f,\lfloor-\rfloor_{\fib},0\rangle
is axiomatizable by a
model-complete theory.

The fact that standard integers is the prime model of the introduced theory demonstrates that our axiomatization is complete, which provides also an alternative proof for the decidability of the structure
\mathcal{Z}_{\varphi}^{<}
(see the next paragraph for a concise history). Meanwhile, we present a significant improvement of the axiomatization of the structure
\mathcal{Z}_{\varphi}:=\langle\mathbbm{Z},+,f,0\rangle
which was already studied in
[
KZ22
]
. A brief account of the history of the subject is provided below.

In a series of works,
[

HT14
,
H19
]
, expansions of the ordered additive structure

\lambda_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha x

\alpha\mathbbm{Z}

On the one hand, it is shown that the structure

Next, given the fact that using techniques from the theory of automata constitutes the main theme in proving the mentioned decidability/undecidability results, another question would be if it is possible to provide model-theoretic proofs for the available decidability results; an affirmative answer to this question leads to a clear logical axiomatization for these structures.

We addressed the first question in

3.
Restoring the numerical order, the structure
\mathcal{Z}_{\varphi}^{<}

Our aim is to present a model-complete theory that completely axiomatizes the structure
\mathcal{Z}_{\varphi}^{<}=\langle\mathbbm{Z},<,+,f,0\rangle

Let

\text{(A1.1)}^{\prime}

To have model-completeness, the answer to the latter question, and in fact to similar questions about other extrema expressible in the language, should be positive. As we proceed, we will see how these minimum or maximum elements are going to play a significant role in our study. \displaystyle=f(F_{2n+1}+(F_{2n-2}+y))

\displaystyle=f(F_{2n+1})+f(F_{2n-2}+y)+1

\displaystyle=f(F_{2n+1})+f(F_{2n-2})+f(y)+1

\displaystyle=f(F_{2n+1}+F_{2n-2})+f(y)

\displaystyle=f(2F_{2n})+f(y)

\displaystyle=f(F_{2n})+f(F_{2n})+f(y)

\displaystyle=f(F_{2n})+f(F_{2n}+y)

\displaystyle=f(F_{2n})+f(x).

In a similar way, one can prove that the maximum of the set specified in the lemma occurs at a Fibonacci number with odd index.

d+\max_{(a-d,b-d)}^{M_{1}}
, and the latter turns out to be equal to
d+\max_{(a-d,b-d)}^{M}
; this,
by
3.8
. Hence,
v
belongs to
M
. On the other hand, it is obvious that
\left[\varphi v\right]\geq\left[\varphi u\right]>1-\left[\varphi t(x)\right]
. As
y
and
x
are assumed to satisfy the same quantifier-free formulas over
M
,
we conclude that
\left[\varphi v\right]>1-\left[\varphi t(y)\right]
in
M_{2}
.

\blacksquare_{\text{ Claim}}

Now, there are two possible cases for
\min_{(a+t(x),b+t(x))}^{M_{1}}
:

Case 1.1.
In the case that there does not exist any element
z\in(a,b)^{M_{1}}
with
\left[\varphi z\right]>1-\left[\varphi t(x)\right]
, all the elements
z\in(a,b)^{M_{1}}
have the property that
\left[\varphi(z+t(x))\right]=\left[\varphi z\right]+\left[\varphi t(x)\right]
. This implies that for any
w\in(a+t(x),b+t(x))
we have that
\left[\varphi w\right]>\left[\varphi a\right]
. Hence, the minimum
\min_{(a+t(x),b+t(x))}^{M_{1}}
is equal to
t(x)+z_{0}
where
z_{0}=\min_{(a,b)}^{M_{1}}
, and the latter is, by
3.8
, equal to
\min_{(a,b)}^{M}
. In addition,
M_{2}
cannot contain an element
z^{\prime}\in(a,b)^{M_{2}}
with
\left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]>1-\left[\varphi t(y)\right]
either. Otherwise, we can use–by way of symmetry–the claim above to show that
M_{1}
would also contain an element with the same property, which contradicts the hypothesis of this case. Therefore, we can use the same argument to show that
\min_{(a+t(y),b+t(y))}^{M_{2}}=t(y)+\min_{(a,b)}^{M_{2}}
, which, by
3.8
, is equal to
t(y)+\min_{(a,b)}^{M}
. This, using the fact that
M\langle x\rangle\cong M\langle y\rangle
, shows that the
\mathcal{L}_{\fib}(M)
-quantifier-free type of
\min_{(a+t(x),b+t(x))}^{M_{1}}
in
M_{1}
is the same as that of
\min_{(a+t(x),b+t(x))}^{M_{2}}
in
M_{2}
.

Case 1.2.
The second case occurs when there is an element
z\in(a,b)^{M_{1}}
with
\left[\varphi z\right]>1-\left[\varphi t(x)\right]
. Then, by
4
, there exists such an element with the minimum possible of corresponding decimal parts; let
z_{1}
denote this element. Also, let
\pi_{0}(z)\subseteq\tp^{M_{1}}(z_{1}/M)
be the subset consisting of the formulas of the form

\big\{a^{\prime}<z<b^{\prime}\ \wedge\ \left[\varphi c^{\prime}\right]<\left[\varphi z\right]<\left[\varphi d^{\prime}\right]\big\},

for all
a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime}\in M
. Note that any finite fragment of the partial type
\pi_{0}(z)
reduces to a formula of the form
a^{\prime}<z<b^{\prime}\ \wedge\ \left[\varphi c^{\prime}\right]<\left[\varphi z\right]<\left[\varphi d^{\prime}\right]
; let
\theta(z;a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime})
denote such a formula.

Let
\pi(z)
be the union of
\pi_{0}(z)
with the the following set of formulas:

\displaystyle\Big\{1-\left[\varphi t(y)\right]<\left[\varphi z\right]\Big\}\cup

\displaystyle\Big\{\forall w\Big(\big(\theta(w;a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime})\wedge\left[\varphi w\right]>1-\left[\varphi t(y)\right]\big)\ \rightarrow\ \big(w=z\vee\left[\varphi z\right]<\left[\varphi w\right]\big)\Big)\Big\},

where
\theta(w;a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime})
ranges over all formulas in
\pi_{0}(z)
.

Now, each
\theta(z;a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime})
is satisfied by
z_{1}
in
M_{1}
. Hence,
4
insures the existence of an element
w_{0}\in M_{1}
that satisfies
\theta(z;a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime})
and has the maximum possible of decimal parts among all solutions of
\theta(z;a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime})
. By
3.11
, this element, say
z_{0}
, belongs to
M
. As
\left[\varphi z_{1}\right]<\left[\varphi d^{\prime}\right]
, we also have
\left[\varphi z_{1}\right]\leq\left[\varphi z_{0}\right]
which implies that
1-\left[\varphi t(x)\right]<\left[\varphi z_{0}\right]
. Based on the fact that
M\langle x\rangle\cong M\langle y\rangle
, the formula
1-\left[\varphi t(y)\right]<\left[\varphi z_{0}\right]
holds in
M_{2}
also.

On the other hand, by
4
, there exists an element, say
z_{2}\in M_{2}
satisfying

\theta(z;a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime})\ \wedge\ 1-\left[\varphi t(y)\right]<\left[\varphi z\right]

and with the minimum possible of decimal parts among all solutions of the formula above in
M_{2}
.

Hence, any finite fragment of
\pi(z)
is satisfied in
M_{2}
. Therefore, we can find a realization for
\pi(z)
in
M_{2}
. This shows that isomorphism between
M\langle x\rangle
and
M\langle y\rangle
extends to an isomorphism between
\langle M,x,z_{1}\rangle^{M_{1}}
and
\langle M,y,z_{2}\rangle^{M_{2}}
.

Case 2.

t_{1}(x)\neq t_{2}(x)
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
t_{1}(x)<t_{2}(x)
.

Note that
\min^{M_{1}}_{(a+t_{1}(x),b+t_{2}(x))}
is either equal to
\min^{M_{1}}_{(a+t_{1}(x),b+t_{1}(x))}
or equal to
\min^{M_{1}}_{(b+t_{1}(x),b+t_{2}(x))}
. The first case is exactly the same as Case 1 above. Hence, suppose that
\min^{M_{1}}_{(a+t_{1}(x),b+t_{2}(x))}=\min^{M_{1}}_{(b+t_{1}(x),b+t_{2}(x))}
. We show that
\min^{M_{1}}_{(b+t_{1}(x),b+t_{2}(x))}
and
\min^{M_{2}}_{(b+t_{1}(y),b+t_{2}(y))}
satisfy the same set of quantifier-free formulas over
M
.

Let
t(x)
denote
t_{2}(x)-t_{1}(x)
.

Case 2.1.
If there is no element
z\in(0,t(x))
with
\left[\varphi z\right]>1-\left[\varphi b\right]
, we have
\left[\varphi(b+z)\right]=\left[\varphi b\right]+\left[\varphi z\right]
for all elements
z\in(0,t(x))
. Hence,
\min^{M_{1}}_{(b+t_{1}(x),b+t_{2}(x))}
is equal to
b+F(t(x))
as
F(t(x))
has the minimum of corresponding decimal parts among all elements in
(0,t(x))
. But, a similar argument as above shows that
M_{2}
cannot contain such an element either. That is,
\min^{M_{2}}_{(b+t_{1}(y),b+t_{2}(y))}=b+F(t(y))
which belongs to
M\langle y\rangle
. By our assumption of
M\langle x\rangle\cong M\langle y\rangle
, this implies that
\min^{M_{1}}_{(b+t_{1}(x),b+t_{2}(x))}
and
\min^{M_{2}}_{(b+t_{1}(y),b+t_{2}(y))}
satisfy the same set of quantifier-free formulas over
M
.

Case 2.2.
For the case that there exists an element
z\in(0,t(x))
with
\left[\varphi z\right]>1-\left[\varphi b\right]
, note that, by
4
, there also exists an element
z_{1}\in(0,t(x))
in
M_{1}
satisfying
\left[\varphi z_{1}\right]>1-\left[\varphi b\right]
and with the minimum corresponding decimal part among all elements like
z
. Note that, we have
\left[\varphi(b+z_{1})\right]=\left[\varphi b\right]+\left[\varphi z_{1}\right]-1
, and hence
\min^{M_{1}}_{(b+t_{1}(x),b+t_{2}(x))}
would be equal to
b+z_{1}
.

Case 2.2.1
Suppose that there exists a positive element
a^{\prime}\in M
with
t(x)-a^{\prime}<z_{1}<t(x)
. Then, there is an element
z^{\prime}\in(0,a^{\prime})^{M_{1}}
such that
z_{1}=t(x)-z^{\prime}
. Recall that
z_{1}\in M_{1}
is the element having the least decimal part among all elements satisfying

(5)

\displaystyle 1-\left[\varphi b\right]<\left[\varphi z_{1}\right]\ \wedge\ 0<z_{1}<t(x).

There are two possible ways that
\left[\varphi z_{1}\right]
and
\left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]
can interrelate:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{(ii)}\hskip 7.0pt\left[\varphi z_{1}\right]=\left[\varphi t(x)\right]-\left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]&\text{ when }\left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]<\left[\varphi t(x)\right],\text{ or }\\
\text{(i)}\hskip 3.0pt\left[\varphi z_{1}\right]=\left[\varphi t(x)\right]-\left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]+1&\text{ when }\left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]>\left[\varphi t(x)\right].\end{cases}

Either way, in order for
z_{1}
to have the least decimal part among all elements having the properties (
5
) above, the element
z^{\prime}
must have the greatest decimal part among all
elements satisfying

(6)

\displaystyle 0<z^{\prime}<a^{\prime}\ \wedge\ z_{1}=t(x)-z^{\prime}\ \wedge\ 1-\left[\varphi b\right]<\left[\varphi(t(x)-z^{\prime})\right].

In case (i), conditions (
5
) and (
6
) translate into having
z^{\prime}\in M_{1}
with the greatest possible decimal part and satisfying

0<z^{\prime}<a^{\prime}\ \wedge\ \left[\varphi t(x)\right]<\left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]<\left[\varphi t(x)\right]-\left[\varphi b\right],

where
\left[\varphi t(x)\right]-\left[\varphi b\right]
has no other choice than being positive. That is, it equals
\left[\varphi(t(x)-b)\right]
, and therefore, case (i) reduces to having the element
z^{\prime}\in M_{1}
that has the greatest decimal part among all elements satisfying
0<z^{\prime}<a^{\prime}
and
\left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]<\left[\varphi(t(x)-b)\right]
. Here, note that the very existence of
z^{\prime}
, guaranteed by the existence of
z_{1}
, ensures that the condition
\left[\varphi t(x)\right]<\left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]
is already satisfied as
z^{\prime}
has the greatest possible of decimal parts. On the other hand,
t(x)-b
belongs to
M\langle x\rangle
, meaning that, it is of the form
t^{\prime}(x)
for some
\mathcal{L}_{\fib}
-term
t^{\prime}(x)
. Hence, this case can be handled similar to Case 1.

As for case (ii), the conditions (
5
) and (
6
) translate into having
z^{\prime}\in M_{1}
satisfying

0<z^{\prime}<a^{\prime}\ \wedge\ \left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]<\left[\varphi t(x)\right]\ \wedge\ \left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]<\left[\varphi t(x)\right]+\left[\varphi b\right]-1,

and having the greatest possible decimal part. As
\left[\varphi z^{\prime}\right]
is positive, the last inequality urges that
\left[\varphi t(x)\right]+\left[\varphi b\right]
be greater than one, or equivalently, that
\left[\varphi(t(x)+b)\right]=\left[\varphi t(x)\right]+\left[\varphi b\right]-1
. Again,
t(x)+b
belongs to
M\langle x\rangle
. Now, whether
\left[\varphi t(x)\right]
is greater or less than
\left[\varphi(t(x)-b)\right]
remains unchanged for
t(y)
and
b
in
M_{2}
, and therefore, we can use a similar argument as used in Case 1 to find the desired element in
M_{2}
.

Case 2.2.2
There does not exist any positive element
a^{\prime}\in M
with
t(x)-a^{\prime}<z_{1}<t(x)
. Then, we have
a^{\prime}<z_{1}+a^{\prime}<t(x)
for all
a^{\prime}\in M
, that is,
t(x)
is infinitely large with respect to the elements in
M
, and the same remains true for
t(y)
in
M_{2}
.

Now, one can see that the element
F(t(y))\in M_{2}
is also greater than any element in
M
. Otherwise, there is an element
a^{\prime}\in M
such that
F(t(y))<a^{\prime}<t(y)
. Recall that we already have
F(t(y))<t(y)<F(t(y))+f(F(t(y)))
where the latter element is the first Fibonacci element greater than
t(y)
and of an even index. As
f
is an increasing function, we will have that
F(t(y))+f(F(t(y)))<a^{\prime}+f(a^{\prime})
implying that
t(y)<a^{\prime}+f(a^{\prime})
. But the latter element belongs to
M
contradiction our hypothesis.

Note that
G(t(y))=f(F(t(y)))
which enables us to similarly argue that
G(t(y))
is also greater than any element in
M
. In particular, we have
G(b)<G(t(y))
.
Note that we have
1-\left[\varphi b\right]<1-\left[\varphi F(b)\right]<\left[\varphi G(b)\right]
, and
this is true for any element in any model of
T
by the way. In addition,
G(b)<G(t(y))
implies that
\left[\varphi G(b)\right]<\left[\varphi G(t(y))\right]
. That is, for
G(t(y))\in M_{2}
we have that

0<G(t(y))<t(y)\text{ and }1-\left[\varphi b\right]<\left[\varphi G(t(y))\right]

which enables us to find an element in
M_{2}
having the properties above and with the least possible decimal part. Hence, we can proceed as in Case 1.2 above.

The extrema addressed in
4
can be handled using similar arguments.

∎

Corollary 3.16
.

T
is decidable and axiomatizes the structure
{\mathcal{Z}_{\varphi}^{<}=\langle\mathbbm{Z},<,+,f,\lfloor-\rfloor_{\fib},0\rangle}
.

Proof.

T
is complete, because
it is model-complete and has
\langle\mathbbm{Z},<,+,f,\lfloor-\rfloor_{\fib},0\rangle
as its prime model. The axiomatization is clearly recursive, and hence the theory is decidable.
∎

The plot of the function

[\cdot]:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}

the rule

x\mapsto[\varphi x]
can be depicted using the black points appearing in the following diagram (for

As can be seen above, the black nodes are the intersections of equidistant parallel lines with positive and negative (calculable) slope, and contains seemingly simple patterns.
Let us mean by the

obtained by considering the points of intersection
of such line segments vertically confined in the interval
(0,1)

One can ask the following seemingly simple question:

Axiomatize the diamond structure in a suitable language and prove that this structure is decidable.

As the relation
[\varphi x]<[\varphi y]
is definable in our theory, the diamond diagram is
hidden in our models, and the decidability of its structure follows our theorem.

However, and quite interestingly, the diamond diagram will look much alike when drawn for the function

[ex]

e
or
-e

instead of
\varphi
and
-\varphi
. In other words, the only thing that changes is the number of intersecting nodes
lying on the line segments.

Having a better understanding of these diamond structures may provide reliable information on
the complexity of the numerical structure obtained by the function
\lfloor ex\rfloor
. As we guess, this can provide a surprising
shortcut probably to obtain a similar decidability result for the structure
\langle\mathbbm{Z},<,+,f_{e},0\rangle
where
f_{e}(x)=\lfloor ex\rfloor
for each integer
x
. See also the Section “A connection to o-minimality” in

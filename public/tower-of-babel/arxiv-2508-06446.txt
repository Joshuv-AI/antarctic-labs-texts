New upper bound for lattice covering by spheres
Jun Gao, Xizhi Liu, Oleg Pikhurko, Shumin Sun

Abstract
We show that there exists a lattice covering of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} by Eucledian spheres of equal radius with density O ⁡ ( n ​ ln β ​ n ) O\big(n\ln^{\beta}n\big) as n → ∞ n\to\infty , where β ≔ 1 2 ​ log 2 ⁡ ( 8 ​ π ​ e 3 ​ 3 ) = 1.85837 ​ … . \displaystyle\beta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}\left(\frac{8\pi\mathrm{e}}{3\sqrt{3}}\right)=1.85837...\,. This improves upon the previously best known upper bound by Rogers from 1959 of O ⁡ ( n ​ ln α ​ n ) O\big(n\ln^{\alpha}n\big) , where α ≔ 1 2 ​ log 2 ⁡ ( 2 ​ π ​ e ) = 2.0471 ​ … \alpha\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}(2\pi\mathrm{e})=2.0471... .

Theorem

Given a point

Definition 1.2

(

)

n
sufficiently large so that

\displaystyle n\geq\max\left\{C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}^{\frac{-1}{2^{d}-1}},~C_{\ref{LEMMA:almost-cover-Rogers}}C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}^{\frac{1}{2^{d}-1}}\right\}.

Combining it with the assumption
kd\geq\log_{2}\ln n+4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\ln\delta_{k}\leq\ln C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}^{\frac{-1}{2^{d}-1}}+2^{kd}\ln\left(C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}^{\frac{1}{2^{d}-1}}\delta_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\ln n+2^{kd}\left(\ln n^{4}+\ln\left(\frac{16}{27}\right)^{n/5}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\ln n+16\ln n\left(4\ln n-\frac{n}{5}\ln\frac{27}{16}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{16}{5}\ln\frac{27}{16}\right)n\ln n+64\ln^{2}n+\ln n,

which is smaller than
-\ln\left(n^{n}+1\right)=-(1+o(1))n\ln n
as
n
is sufficiently large.
Thus,

\displaystyle\delta_{k}\leq\left(n^{n}+1\right)^{-1}.

So it follows from Lemma
2.2
that
\Lambda_{k}+\left(1+1/n\right)K_{k}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
T(K_{k})\subseteq B
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big(\Lambda_{k},\left(1+1/n\right)T^{-1}(B)\big)=\mathbb{R}^{n},

which implies that
\big(T(\Lambda_{k}),\left(1+1/n\right)B\big)
forms a (sphere lattice) covering of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

It remains to show that the density of
\big(T(\Lambda_{k}),\left(1+1/n\right)B\big)
gives the desired upper bound.
Indeed, by (
4
), (
5
), and (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\Theta\big(T(\Lambda_{k}),\left(1+1/n\right)B\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{vol}\left((1+1/n)B\right)}{|\det(T(\Lambda_{k}))|}=\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n}\frac{\operatorname{vol}(B)}{|\det(T)||\det(\Lambda_{k})|}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathrm{e}\eta\left(\frac{D}{\nu_{d}}\right)^{k}(2\pi\mathrm{e})^{\frac{kd}{2}}\leq Cn\left(\frac{D}{\nu_{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}\log_{2}\ln n}(2\pi\mathrm{e})^{\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}\ln n},

as claimed.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

3
Proof of Lemma
2.3

In this section, we present the proof of Lemma
2.3
, starting with a few preliminary lemmas.

We use
\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
denote the Euclidean distance between two points
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Given a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},S)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\colon\mathbf{y}\in S\right\}.

In the following lemma, we make no attempt to optimise
C_{\ref{LEMMA:point-type-upper-bound}}
as a function of
d
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer and
D\in[0,\nu_{d}]
be a real number. For every robust lattice covering
(\Lambda,B^{d}_{r})
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with density
D
, the number of fundamental parallelepipeds contained in
B_{2r}^{d}
is at most
C_{\ref{LEMMA:point-type-upper-bound}}\coloneqq\left(4^{d}d^{d/2}+1\right)^{d\cdot 2^{d}}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

By scaling
\Lambda
if necessary, we may assume that
|\det(\Lambda)|=1
. Consequently,
\operatorname{vol}(B^{d}_{r})=D
.
Since
D\leq\nu_{d}
, it follows that
r\leq\sqrt{d}
.
Let

\displaystyle C\coloneqq(4r)^{d}+1\leq 4^{d}d^{\frac{d}{2}}+1.

First, we show that the number of lattice points contained in
B_{2r}^{d}
is bounded.

Claim 3.2
.

We have

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda\cap B^{d}_{2r}\right|\leq C^{d}.

Proof of Claim
3.2
.

By the definition of a robust lattice covering, the sphere
B_{r}^{d}
contains a fundamental parallelepiped
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d})
of
\Lambda_{d}
, where
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are linearly independent.
Note that
\mathbf{x}
must lie in
\Lambda_{d}
, and hence the parallelepiped
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d})
obtained by translating
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d})
by
-\mathbf{x}
is also a fundamental parallelepiped of
\Lambda_{d}
.
Moreover, since
\mathbf{x}\in B_{r}^{d}
, we have
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d})\subseteq B_{2r}^{d}
.

Let
\mathrm{P}\coloneqq\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d})
.
Since
\mathrm{P}
is a fundamental parallelepiped of
\Lambda_{d}
, the lattice
\Lambda
is generated by
\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\mathbf{v}_{0}\coloneqq\mathbf{0}
and
V_{0}\coloneqq\left\{\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\}
.
For each
j\in[d]
, define

\displaystyle V_{j}\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}\colon\text{$\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}$ for $i\in[j]$}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{j}\coloneqq\mathrm{dist}\left(\mathbf{v}_{j},V_{j-1}\right).

By the definition of a fundamental parallelepiped, we have
\operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{P})=|\det(\Lambda)|=1
. It follows that

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}d_{j}=\operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{P})=1.

(8)

Since
\mathrm{P}\subseteq B^{d}_{2r}(\mathbf{0})
, we have
d_{j}\leq\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{v}_{j},\mathbf{v}_{j-1})\leq 4r
for every
j\in[d]
.
Combining it with (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle d_{i}=\prod_{j\in[d]\atop j\not=i}\frac{1}{d_{j}}\geq\frac{1}{(4r)^{d-1}}\quad\text{for every $i\in[d]$.}

(9)

Given
j\in[0,d]
and a
(d-j)
-tuple
(x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-j}
, define

\displaystyle\Lambda(x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}+\sum_{i=j+1}^{d}x_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}\colon\text{$\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}$ for $i\in[j]$}\right\}.

(10)

We now prove by induction on
j
that, for every
(x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-j}
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda(x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})\cap B^{d}_{2r}\right|\leq C^{j}.

(11)

The base case
j=0
is clear, since for every
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the set
\Lambda(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
contains only one point.

We now focus on the inductive step. Fix
j\geq 1
and suppose for contradiction that there exists
(x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-j}
for which (
11
) fails.
By the inductive hypothesis, (
11
) holds when
j
is replaced with
j-1
.
This implies that the number of choices of
x_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
for which

\displaystyle\Lambda(x_{j},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})\cap B^{d}_{2r}\neq\emptyset

is more than
C
.
Therefore, there exist two integers
y_{j},z_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|y_{j}-z_{j}|>C
such that

\displaystyle\Lambda(y_{j},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})\cap B^{d}_{2r}\neq\emptyset\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda(z_{j},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})\cap B^{d}_{2r}\neq\emptyset.

Fix
\mathbf{u}_{1}\in\Lambda(y_{j},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})\cap B^{d}_{2r}
and
\mathbf{u}_{2}\in\Lambda(z_{j},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{d})\cap B^{d}_{2r}
.
It follows from definition (
10
) that there exists integers
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{j-1}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{2}-\mathbf{u}_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}+(z_{j}-y_{j})\mathbf{v}_{j}=(z_{j}-y_{j})\left(\mathbf{v}_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{y_{j}-z_{j}}\mathbf{v}_{i}\right).

Note that
\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{y_{j}-z_{j}}\mathbf{v}_{i}
lies in the subspace
V_{j-1}
. Therefore, by (
9
), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2})\geq|z_{j}-y_{j}|\cdot\operatorname{dist}\left(\mathbf{v}_{j},V_{j-1}\right)>C\cdot d_{j}\geq\frac{(4r)^{d}+1}{(4r)^{d-1}}>4r,

which contradicts the assumption that both
\mathbf{u}_{1}
and
\mathbf{u}_{2}
lie in
B_{2r}^{d}
.
This completes the proof of the inductive step, and thus establishes (
11
).
In particular, setting
j=d
yields Claim
3.2
.
∎

Note that each
d
-dimensional (fundamental) parallelepiped has
2^{d}
vertices, so it follows from Claim
3.2
that the number of fundamental parallelepiped contained in
B_{2r}^{d}
is at most
C^{d\cdot 2^{d}}
, which completes the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
denote the
n
-dimensional torus.
The following two lemmas routinely follow from standard results. For completeness, we include their proofs.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and let
\delta:=\bar{\rho}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}+K\right)
.
Let
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
be a point chosen uniformly at random, according to the Lebesgue measure restricted to the cube
[0,1)^{n}
.
Let
\tilde{K}\coloneqq K\cup(K+\mathbf{y})
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\rho}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n}+\tilde{K}\big)\right]=\delta^{2}.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
\chi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
be the characteristic function of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}+K
.
Then
\chi
is periodic with period
1
in each of the coordinates.
It follows from
\bar{\rho}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{n}+K\right)=\delta
that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{n}}\left(1-\chi(\mathbf{x})\right)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}=\delta.

(12)

Suppose that
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
is a point chosen uniformly at random according to the Lebesgue measure restricted to the cube
[0,1)^{n}
, and
\tilde{K}=K\cup(K+\mathbf{y})
.
Using (
12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\rho}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{n}+\tilde{K}\big)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1)^{n}}\left(\int_{[0,1)^{n}}\left(1-\chi(\mathbf{x})\right)\left(1-\chi(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\right)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\right)dy_{1}\cdots dy_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1)^{n}}\left(1-\chi(\mathbf{x})\right)\left(\int_{[0,1)^{n}}\left(1-\chi(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\right)dy_{1}\cdots dy_{n}\right)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1)^{n}}\left(1-\chi(\mathbf{x})\right)\left(\int_{[0,1)^{n}}\left(1-\chi(\mathbf{z})\right)dz_{1}\cdots dz_{n}\right)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}=\delta^{2},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
n,d\geq 1
be integers.
Suppose that
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{d\times d}
is a matrix with
|\det(M)|=1
.
Define the map
\psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
by
\psi(X)=MX
for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
.
Then the map
\phi
induced by
\psi
on
\mathbb{T}^{d\times n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\phi(X):=\psi(X)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Z}^{d\times n}\quad\text{for every}\quad X\in\mathbb{T}^{d\times n},

is bijective and (Lebesgue) measure-preserving.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Let
M_{\ast}=\mathrm{diag}(M,\cdots,M)\in\mathbb{Z}^{dn\times dn}
be the matrix obtained by placing
n
copies of the matrix
M
along the diagonal.
It is clear that
M_{\ast}
is an integer matrix with
|\det(M_{\ast})|=1
.
By Cramer’s rule, the inverse
M_{\ast}^{-1}
of
M_{\ast}
is also an integer matrix with
|\det(M^{-1}_{\ast})|=1
.

Define the map
\psi_{\ast}\colon\mathbb{R}^{dn}\to\mathbb{R}^{dn}
by
\psi(\mathbf{x})=M\mathbf{x}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{dn}
. It is clear that
\psi
and
\psi_{\ast}
define the same linear map under the identification of
\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
with
\mathbb{R}^{dn}
.
Let
\varphi
be the map induced by
\psi_{\ast}
on
\mathbb{T}^{d\times n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\psi_{\ast}(\mathbf{x})\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Z}^{dn}\quad\text{for every}\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{dn}.

Since
|\det(M_{\ast})|=1
, it follows from standard results in Analysis (see e.g.
[
1
, Lemma 40.4]
) that
\psi_{\ast}
is measure-preserving.
Thus, if we can show that
\varphi
is bijective, it will follow that
\varphi
is also measure-preserving.

We begin by proving that
\varphi
is injective.
Suppose to the contrary that there exist two distinct points
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in[0,1)^{dn}
such that
\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\varphi(\mathbf{y})
.
Then we have
\varphi(\mathbf{x})-\varphi(\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{0}
, which means that

\displaystyle\psi_{\ast}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})=\psi_{\ast}(\mathbf{x})-\psi_{\ast}(\mathbf{y})\in\mathbb{Z}^{dn}.

(13)

Since both
M_{\ast}
and
M_{\ast}^{-1}
are integer matrices, the map
\psi_{\ast}
induces a bijection from
\mathbb{Z}^{dn}
onto itself.
Combining it with (
13
), we conclude that
\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{dn}
, which contradicts the assumption that
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}
and
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in[0,1)^{dn}
.

Next, we show that
\varphi
is surjective.
Take an arbitrary point
\mathbf{y}\in[0,1)^{dn}
.
Since
M_{\ast}
is invertible, the inverse
\psi_{\ast}^{-1}(\mathbf{y})
exists.
Let
\mathbf{x}
be the unique point in
[0,1)^{dn}
such that
\mathbf{x}-\psi_{\ast}^{-1}(\mathbf{y})\in\mathbb{Z}^{dn}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\psi_{\ast}(\mathbf{x})\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Z}^{dn}

\displaystyle=\psi_{\ast}\left(\psi_{\ast}^{-1}(\mathbf{y})+\mathbf{x}-\psi_{\ast}^{-1}(\mathbf{y})\right)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Z}^{dn}

\displaystyle=\psi_{\ast}\left(\psi_{\ast}^{-1}(\mathbf{y})\right)+\psi_{\ast}\left(\mathbf{x}-\psi_{\ast}^{-1}(\mathbf{y})\right)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Z}^{dn}=\mathbf{y},

where the last equality holds because
\psi_{\ast}
maps
\mathbb{Z}^{dn}
into
\mathbb{Z}^{dn}
.
This proves that
\varphi
is surjective, and hence completes the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Lemma
2.3
.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Given a convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\delta:=\bar{\rho}(\Lambda+K)
.
By applying a linear transformation to
\Lambda
if necessary, we may assume that
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Let
\{\mathbf{e}_{i}\colon i\in[n]\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{1}=(1,0,0,\dots,0)^{t},\quad\mathbf{e}_{2}=(0,1,0,\dots,0)^{t},\quad\ldots,\quad\mathbf{e}_{n}=(0,0,0,\dots,1)^{t}.

Fix a robust lattice covering
(\Lambda_{d},B_{r}^{d})
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with density
D\leq\nu_{d}
.
By scaling
\Lambda_{d}
if necessary, we may assume that
|\det(\Lambda_{d})|=1
and hence,
r
is such that
\operatorname{vol}(B^{d}_{r})=D
.
Let
\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be linearly independent vectors such that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d}=\left\{\sum^{d}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}\colon\text{$\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}$ for $i\in[d]$}\right\}.

For
i\in[n]
, let
\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\coloneqq(\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
be the concatenation of
\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For each
j\in[d]
, choose a vector
\mathbf{y}_{i}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
uniformly at random according to the Lebesgue measure restricted to the cube
[0,1)^{n}
, and let
\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_{j}\coloneqq(\mathbf{y}_{j},\mathbf{b}_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
be the concatenation of
\mathbf{y}_{i}
and
\mathbf{b}_{i}
.
Define a new (random) lattice

\displaystyle\tilde{\Lambda}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\coloneqq\Bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mu_{j}\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_{j}\colon\text{$\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}$ for $i\in[n]$ and $\mu_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}$ for $j\in[d]$}\Bigg\}.

Note that

\displaystyle|\det(\tilde{\Lambda}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}))|=|\det(\Lambda)|\cdot|\det(\Lambda_{d})|=1,

which can be seen by expanding the determinant of the corresponding matrix along the first
n
columns (with each having only one non-zero entry, namely the diagonal entry
1
).

Let
\tilde{K}=K\times B_{r}^{d}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+d}
.
Let
C_{\ref{LEMMA:point-type-upper-bound}}
be the constant given in Lemma
3.1
and define
C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}\coloneqq\left(\left(C_{\ref{LEMMA:point-type-upper-bound}}+1\right)d\right)^{2^{d}-1}
.
We will show that, with positive probability, the following event occurs:

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}\big(\tilde{\Lambda}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})+\tilde{K}\big)\leq C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}\delta^{2^{d}},

that is, the set of points
(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\in\mathbb{T}^{n\times d}
for which this inequality holds has positive Lebesgue measure. For this we need some further definitions and two auxiliary claims.

Let
B\coloneqq\left[\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{d}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.
Let
\phi_{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}}\colon\Lambda_{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the linear map defined by

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}}(\mathbf{z})=\left[\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}\right]B^{-1}\mathbf{z}\quad\text{for every}\quad\mathbf{z}\in\Lambda_{d}.

(14)

In other words,
\phi_{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}}
sends a lattice point
\mathbf{z}\in\Lambda_{d}
to
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mu_{j}\mathbf{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the unique collection of integers such that
\mathbf{z}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mu_{j}\mathbf{b}_{j}
.
Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\coloneqq\left\{\mathrm{P}\subseteq B_{2r}^{d}\colon\text{$\mathrm{P}$ is a fundamental parallelepiped with $\mathbf{0}$ as a vertex}\right\}.

Lemma
3.1
implies that
|\mathcal{P}|\leq C_{\ref{LEMMA:point-type-upper-bound}}
.

For every fundamental
\Lambda_{d}
-parallelepiped
\mathrm{P}
, let
E_{\mathrm{P}}
denote the event that

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}\left(\Lambda+K^{\mathrm{P}}\right)\leq C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}\delta^{2^{d}},\quad\text{where}\quad K^{\mathrm{P}}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\coloneqq\bigcup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{P}}\left(K+\phi_{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}}(\mathbf{x})\right).

Our goal is to show that for every
\mathrm{P}\in\mathcal{P}
, the event
E_{\mathrm{P}}
occurs with high probability. We begin with the case of
E_{\mathrm{P}_{\ast}}
, where, for convenience, we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{\ast}\coloneqq\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{0}}(\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{d})\quad\text{and}\quad K_{\ast}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\coloneqq\bigcup_{\mathbf{x}\in V(\mathrm{P}_{\ast})}\left(K+\phi_{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}}(\mathbf{x})\right).

Claim 3.5
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[E_{\mathrm{P}_{\ast}}\right]\geq 1-\frac{1}{C_{\ref{LEMMA:point-type-upper-bound}}+1}.

Proof of Claim
3.5
.

For each collection
\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
, since
\phi_{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}}\colon\Lambda_{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a linear map, the set
K_{\ast}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})
is determined by the basis
\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{d}
of
\mathrm{P}_{\ast}
.
Thus, define
K_{0}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\coloneqq K
, and for each
i\in[d]
, let

\displaystyle K_{i}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\coloneqq K_{i-1}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\cup\big(K_{i-1}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})+\mathbf{y}_{i}\big).

It follows from the definition of
\phi_{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}}
that
\phi_{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}}(\mathbf{b}_{i})=\mathbf{y}_{i}
for
i\in[d]
.
Therefore,
K_{\ast}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})=K_{d}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})
.

Let
C\coloneqq C_{\ref{LEMMA:point-type-upper-bound}}+1
.
For
i\in[0,d]
, define

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i}\coloneqq\bar{\rho}\left(\Lambda+K_{i}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\left(Cd\delta\right)^{2^{i}}}{Cd},

and let
E_{i}
denote the event that
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i}\leq\delta_{i}=Cd\delta_{i-1}^{2}
.
By assumption, we have
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0}=\delta=\delta_{0}
.

For each
i\in[d]
, since
\mathbf{y}_{i}
is independent of
\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{i-1}
, it follows from Lemma
3.3
and Markov’s inequality that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i}\geq\delta_{i}\mid\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i-1}\leq\delta_{i-1}\right]\leq\frac{\delta_{i-1}^{2}}{\delta_{i}}=\frac{\delta_{i-1}^{2}}{Cd\delta_{i-1}^{2}}=\frac{1}{Cd}.

Thus, the conditional probability satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[E_{i}\mid E_{i-1}\right]\geq 1-\frac{1}{Cd}\quad\text{for every}\quad i\in[d].

Combining this with Bernoulli’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[E_{\mathrm{P}_{\ast}}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[E_{d}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left[E_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge E_{d}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{P}\left[E_{i}\mid E_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge E_{i-1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{P}\left[E_{i}\mid E_{i-1}\right]\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{Cd}\right)^{d}\geq 1-\frac{1}{C},

as desired.
∎

Next, we extend the conclusion of Claim
3.5
to all elements of
\mathcal{P}
.

Claim 3.6
.

For every
\mathrm{P}\in\mathcal{P}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[E_{\mathrm{P}}\right]\geq 1-\frac{1}{C_{\ref{LEMMA:point-type-upper-bound}}+1}.

In particular, with positive probability, all of the events
\{E_{\mathrm{P}}\colon\mathrm{P}\in\mathcal{P}\}
occur simultaneously.

Proof.

Fix
\mathrm{P}\in\mathcal{P}
.
Define sets

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{(\mathbf{y}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\colon\text{$E_{\mathrm{P}_{\ast}}$ holds}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{(\mathbf{y}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\colon\text{$E_{\mathrm{P}}$ holds}\right\}

Note that
\mathbb{P}\left[E_{\mathrm{P}_{\ast}}\right]
and
\mathbb{P}\left[E_{\mathrm{P}}\right]
are equal to
\mu(S)
and
\mu(T)
, respectively, where
\mu
denotes the Lebesgue measure.
Recall from Claim
3.5
that
\mathbb{P}\left[E_{\mathrm{P}_{\ast}}\right]\geq 1-\left(C_{\ref{LEMMA:point-type-upper-bound}}+1\right)^{-1}
.
So it suffices to show that
\mu(T)\geq\mu(S)
(In fact, a straightforward modification of the argument below shows that
S
and
T
have the same Lebesgue measure).

Fix linearly independent vectors
\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle V(\mathrm{P})=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}\colon\text{$\lambda_{i}\in\{0,1\}$ for $i\in[d]$}\right\}.

For each collection
\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{d}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
, let
K_{0,\mathrm{P}}(\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{d})\coloneqq K
, and for each
i\in[d]
, let

\displaystyle K_{i,\mathrm{P}}(\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{d})\coloneqq K_{i-1,\mathrm{P}}(\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{d})\cup\big(K_{i-1,\mathrm{P}}(\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{d})+\phi_{\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{d}}(\mathbf{w}_{i})\big).

Similar to the proof of Claim
3.5
, we have
K_{\mathrm{P}}(\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{d})=K_{d,\mathrm{P}}(\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{d})
.

Recall that
\{\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{d}\}
is a basis of
\Lambda_{d}
and
B=\left[\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{d}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.
Let
W\coloneqq\left[\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{d}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, and let
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be the matrix such that
WM=B
.
It follows that
MBW=I
, and thus,

\displaystyle MB\mathbf{w}_{i}=\mathbf{e}_{i}.

(15)

Given an element
(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\in S
, define the map
\varphi\colon\mathbb{T}^{d\times n}\to\mathbb{T}^{d\times n}
by

\displaystyle\varphi([\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}]^{t}):=\left[\psi(\mathbf{y}_{1}),\ldots,\psi(\mathbf{y}_{d})\right]^{t},

where

\displaystyle\left[\psi(\mathbf{y}_{1}),\ldots,\psi(\mathbf{y}_{d})\right]\coloneqq\left[\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}\right]M\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Z}^{n\times d}.

Combining (
15
) with definition (
14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{\psi(\mathbf{y}_{1}),\ldots,\psi(\mathbf{y}_{d})}(\mathbf{w}_{i})

\displaystyle=\left[\psi(\mathbf{y}_{1}),\ldots,\psi(\mathbf{y}_{d})\right]B^{-1}\mathbf{w}_{i}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}\right]MB^{-1}\mathbf{w}_{i}=\left[\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}\right]\mathbf{e}_{i}=\mathbf{y}_{i}.

It follows that

\displaystyle K_{\mathrm{P}}\left(\psi(\mathbf{y}_{1}),\ldots,\psi(\mathbf{y}_{d})\right)=K_{\ast}\left(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}\right).

Since
(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\in S
, it follows from the definition of
S
that

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}\big(\Lambda+K_{\mathrm{P}}\left(\psi(\mathbf{y}_{1}),\ldots,\psi(\mathbf{y}_{d})\right)\big)=\bar{\rho}\big(\Lambda+K_{\ast}\left(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}\right)\big)\leq C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}\delta^{2^{d}},

which implies that
\left(\psi(\mathbf{y}_{1}),\ldots,\psi(\mathbf{y}_{d})\right)\in T
.

Since
\mathrm{P}
is a fundamental parallelepiped of
\Lambda_{d}
, the matrix
M
must be an integer matrix with determinant
\pm 1
.
It follows from Lemma
3.4
that the map
\varphi
on
\mathbb{T}^{d\times n}
is measure-preserving.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[E_{\mathrm{P}}\right]=\mu(T)\geq\mu(S)=\mathbb{P}\left[E_{\mathrm{P}_{\ast}}\right]\geq 1-\frac{1}{C_{\ref{LEMMA:point-type-upper-bound}}+1},

which proves Claim
3.6
.
∎

Fix a collection
(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{n\times d}
such that all of the events
\{E_{\mathrm{P}}\colon\mathrm{P}\in\mathcal{P}\}
occur simultaneously.
For convenience, let
\tilde{\Lambda}\coloneqq\tilde{\Lambda}(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d})
and
\phi\coloneqq\phi_{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}}
.

For every lattice point
\mathbf{z}\in\Lambda_{d}
, define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}\coloneqq\left\{\mathbf{x}\colon(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})\in\tilde{\Lambda}\right\}.

Note that, by definition, for every
\mathbf{z}\in\Lambda_{d}
, the lattice
\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}
can be written as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}=\Lambda+\phi(\mathbf{z}).

(16)

This means that
\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}
is simply a translation of the lattice
\Lambda
.

Fix a point
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and fix a fundamental parallelepiped
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}}
that is contained in the ball
B^{d}_{r}(\mathbf{w})
.
Fix a point
\mathbf{s}\in V(\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}})
and let
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{s}
be obtained from
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}}
by translating by
-\mathbf{s}
.
Note that
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime}
is a fundamental parallelepiped contained in
B_{2r}^{d}
and
\mathbf{0}
is a vertex in
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime}
, that is,
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
.

Consider the following restriction of the set
\tilde{K}+\tilde{\Lambda}
:

\displaystyle(\tilde{K}+\tilde{\Lambda})\mid_{\mathbf{w}}\coloneqq\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w})\in\tilde{K}+\tilde{\Lambda}\right\}.

Since
\tilde{K}=K\times B_{r}^{d}
, a point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is covered by
(\tilde{K}+\tilde{\Lambda})\mid_{\mathbf{w}}
if there exists a point
\tilde{\mathbf{w}}\in\Lambda_{d}\cap B_{r}(\mathbf{w})
such that
\mathbf{x}\in\Lambda_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}+K
.
In particular, the set
\bigcup_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}\in V(\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}})}\left(\Lambda_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}+K\right)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is covered by
(\tilde{K}+\tilde{\Lambda})\mid_{\mathbf{w}}
.
By (
16
) and the linearity of
\phi
, we have

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}\in V(\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}})}\left(\Lambda_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}+K\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}\in V(\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}})}\left(\Lambda+\phi(\tilde{\mathbf{w}})+K\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}\in V(\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}})}\left(\Lambda+\phi(\tilde{\mathbf{w}}-\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{s})+K\right)

\displaystyle=\phi(\mathbf{s})+\Lambda+\bigcup_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}\in V(\mathrm{P}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{w}})}\left(K+\phi(\tilde{\mathbf{w}})\right)=\phi(\mathbf{s})+\Lambda+K^{\mathrm{P}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{w}}}.

Since
\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}
, the choice of vectors
\{\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{d}\}
guarantees that

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}\big(\Lambda+K^{\mathrm{P}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{w}}}\big)\leq C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}\delta^{2^{d}}.

Since translation (by
\phi(\mathbf{s})
) does not affect the density of uncovered points, we also have

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}\big(\textstyle\bigcup_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}\in V(\mathrm{P}_{\mathbf{w}})}\left(\Lambda_{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}}+K\right)\big)\leq C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}\delta^{2^{d}}.

Since
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
was arbitrary, it follows that
\bar{\rho}\big(\tilde{\Lambda}+\tilde{K}\big)\leq C_{\ref{LEMMA:cartesian-product-covering}}\delta^{2^{d}}
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

4
Concluding remarks

Rogers’ original proof in
[
20
]
is essentially the same as our proof of Theorem
1.3
in the special case
d=1
. We hope that the idea of using an iterative step where the dimension increases by more than
1
will lead to further improvements (via Theorem
1.3
or some other estimates).

Unfortunately, we have little intuition about the optimal robust lattice covering density in dimensions
d\geq 4
. The presented constant
\beta
in Theorem
1.1
was the best one that came from our sporadic search for
d\leq 3
. So the natural question motivated by Theorem
1.3
is the following.

Problem 4.1
.

Determine
\tilde{\Theta}_{n}
.
In particular, what is the infimum of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}\big(\tilde{\Theta}_{n}/\nu_{n}\big)?

A lower bound
\tilde{\Theta}_{n}\geq{\nu_{n}}/{2^{n}}
can be established via the following argument.
Let
(\Lambda,B_{r}^{n})
be a robust lattice covering of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\det(\Lambda)=1
.
By definition,
B_{r}^{n}
contains a fundamental parallelepiped
\mathrm{P}
.
It is not hard to show that the largest volume of a parallelepiped (not necessarily a
\Lambda
-parallelepiped) contained in
B_{r}^{n}
is
\left(2r/\sqrt{n}\right)^{n}
, attained by an inscribed cube centred at the origin.
Since
\mathrm{P}\subseteq B_{r}^{n}
is a parallelepiped with volume
\det(\Lambda)=1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left(2r/\sqrt{n}\right)^{n}\geq\operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{P})=|\det\Lambda|=1,

which implies that
r\geq\sqrt{n}/2
.
Therefore, we obtain the bound

\displaystyle\tilde{\Theta}_{n}\geq\frac{\operatorname{vol}(B_{r}^{n})}{|\det(\Lambda)|}\geq\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{\sqrt{n}/2}^{n}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\operatorname{vol}\left(B_{\sqrt{n}}^{n}\right)=\frac{\nu_{n}}{2^{n}}.

In particular, this yields
\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}\left(\tilde{\Theta}_{n}/\nu_{n}\right)\geq-1
, which means that the best possible value we can hope for
\gamma
via an application of Theorem
1.3
as stated is at least
\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}(2\pi\mathrm{e})-1=1.0471...
.

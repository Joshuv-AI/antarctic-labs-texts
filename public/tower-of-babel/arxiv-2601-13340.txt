1.4.
Proof strategy and outline of the paper

Let us end this introductory section by highlighting the key points in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and outlining the content of the remainder of the paper.

It is well known (
[
11

) that if

for all

[

that every quadric is

-quasi-affine, so to determinate its

-affinity we only have to compute cohomology of

. Although we do not prove it here, as it is not needed in the proof of Theorem

so

is

-affine if and only if

. Because of the above, we need some nice criterion for detecting non-vanishing of

of the sheaf of differential operators. We propose the following the following (
\mathsf{F}:X\to X
is the absolute Frobenius morphism).

Proposition 1.9
.

Assume that
\textnormal{char }K=p>0
, and let
X
be a smooth variety over

. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

H^{1}(X,{\mathscr{D}_{X}})=0
.

(2)

For every positive integer

, and every short exact sequence of

-modules

(1.1)

0\rightarrow\mathsf{F}^{t_{0}}_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\rightarrow\mathsf{F}^{t_{0}}_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\rightarrow 0,

there exists an integer

such that the short exact sequence

(1.2)

0\rightarrow\mathsf{F}^{t_{0}+e}_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\rightarrow\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathsf{F}^{t_{0}+e}_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\rightarrow 0

obtained by applying

to

1.1
)
splits.

We prove Theorem

by verifying that (2) in proposition above does not hold . This is possible, in principle, because by the work of A. Langer

and P. Achinger
[

3.1.
The sheaf

Let

We start by fixing the notation and recalling the basic geometric properties of the quadric hypersurface itself.

for the identity matrix of size
r\times r
. In
[
10
, Section 2.2]
A. Langer constructed inductively matrices
\varphi_{m},\psi_{m}\in M_{2^{m}}\left(K[x_{1},\dots,x_{2m+1}]\right)
that give a matrix factorization of
q_{m}
, by letting
\varphi_{0}=(x_{1}),\ \psi_{0}=(x_{2})
and

\varphi_{m+1}=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{m}&x_{2m+1}\textnormal{I}_{2^{m}}\\
x_{2m+2}\textnormal{I}_{2^{m}}&-\psi_{m}\end{pmatrix},\quad\psi_{m+1}=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{m}&x_{2m+1}\textnormal{I}_{2^{m}}\\
x_{2m+2}\textnormal{I}_{2^{m}}&-\varphi_{m}\end{pmatrix}.

This allows to define Spinor bundles on
Q_{2m}
as

(4.4)

{\Sigma_{-}}=\textnormal{Coker}\left(\mathscr{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2m+1}}(-2)^{\oplus 2^{m}}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{m}}\mathscr{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2m+1}}(-1)^{\oplus 2^{m}}\right),

and

(4.5)

{\Sigma_{+}}=\textnormal{Coker}\left(\mathscr{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2m+1}}(-2)^{\oplus 2^{m}}\xrightarrow{\psi_{m}}\mathscr{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2m+1}}(-1)^{\oplus 2^{m}}\right).

4.4.
Symmetry

Recall from (
4.1
) the involution
\alpha
of
Q_{2m}
. In this subsection, we prove that
\alpha_{*}
exchanges
{\Sigma_{+}}
with
{\Sigma_{-}}
. More precisely we prove the lemma below.

Lemma 4.1
.

In the above notation, the following is true for arbitrary integer
t
.

(1)

\alpha_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(t)={\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(t)
,

(2)

\alpha_{*}{\Sigma_{+}}(t)={\Sigma_{-}}(t)
,

(3)

\alpha_{*}{\Sigma_{-}}(t)={\Sigma_{+}}(t)
.

Proof.

Since
\alpha
is an automorphism, we have

\alpha_{*}\alpha^{*}\simeq\alpha^{*}\alpha_{*}\simeq\textnormal{Id}.

In particular, it suffices to prove the version of the lemma where
\alpha_{*}
is replaced with
\alpha^{*}
. First, note that (1) is true for any automorphism of
Q_{2m}
, because
\alpha^{*}
must induce an isomorphism on
\textnormal{Pic}(Q_{2m})
and preserve global generation. This also allows us to assume that
t=0
in the proof of (2) and (3). Write
\widetilde{\alpha}
for the automorphism of
M_{2^{m}}\left(K[x_{1},\dots,x_{2m+2}]\right)
induced by
\alpha
. By (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) we have

\alpha^{*}{\Sigma_{-}}=\textnormal{Coker}\left(\mathscr{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2m+1}}(-2)^{\oplus 2^{m}}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\alpha}\circ\varphi_{m}\circ\widetilde{\alpha}}\mathscr{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2m+1}}(-1)^{\oplus 2^{m}}\right),

and

\alpha^{*}{\Sigma_{+}}=\textnormal{Coker}\left(\mathscr{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2m+1}}(-2)^{\oplus 2^{m}}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\alpha}\circ\psi_{m}\circ\widetilde{\alpha}}\mathscr{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{2m+1}}(-1)^{\oplus 2^{m}}\right).

A straightforward computation shows that

\widetilde{\alpha}\circ\varphi_{m}\circ\widetilde{\alpha}=\psi_{m},\quad\widetilde{\alpha}\circ\psi_{m}\circ\widetilde{\alpha}=\varphi_{m},

so the lemma follows from (
4.4
) and (
4.5
).
∎

4.5.
Spinor bundles and cohomology

In this subsection, we recall well known cohomological properties of spinor bundles
{\Sigma_{+}}
and
{\Sigma_{-}}
. We use the convenient notation

{\mathcal{S}}={\Sigma_{+}}\oplus{\Sigma_{-}}.

First, we note that duals of spinor bundles are twisted spinor bundles.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
10
, Section 2]
)
.

Depending on the parity of
m
, either

(1)

{\Sigma_{-}}^{\vee}={\Sigma_{-}}(1)
, and
{\Sigma_{+}}^{\vee}={\Sigma_{+}}(1)
when
m
is even, or

(2)

{\Sigma_{-}}^{\vee}={\Sigma_{+}}(1)
, and
{\Sigma_{+}}^{\vee}={\Sigma_{-}}(1)
when
m
is odd.

In any case,

(3)

{\mathcal{S}}^{\vee}={\mathcal{S}}(1)
.

Second, we describe morphisms between twisted spinor bundles.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

(1)

\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}({\Sigma_{+}},{\Sigma_{+}})=\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}({\Sigma_{-}},{\Sigma_{-}})=K.\textnormal{Id}
,

(2)

\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}({\Sigma_{+}},{\Sigma_{-}})=\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}({\Sigma_{-}},{\Sigma_{+}})=0
,

(3)

\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathscr{O}_{{Q}_{2m}}}({\Sigma_{+}},{\Sigma_{+}}(t))=\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathscr{O}_{{Q}_{2m}}}({\Sigma_{-}},{\Sigma_{-}}(t))=0
for
t<0
.

Proof.

All of the above follow from the fact that spinor bundles are slope stable (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
).
∎

Similarly, we collect information about extensions of spinor bundles.

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

(1)

\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{\mathscr{O}_{{Q}_{2m}}}({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{S}}(t))=0
for
t\neq-1
,

(2)

\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{\mathscr{O}_{{Q}_{2m}}}(\Sigma_{+},\Sigma_{+}(-1))=\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{\mathscr{O}_{{Q}_{2m}}}(\Sigma_{-},\Sigma_{-}(-1))=0
,

(3)

\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{\mathscr{O}_{{Q}_{2m}}}(\Sigma_{+},\Sigma_{-}(-1))=\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{\mathscr{O}_{{Q}_{2m}}}(\Sigma_{-},\Sigma_{+}(-1))=K
.

Proof.

Follows from Lemma
4.2
and
[
10
, Lemma 2.3]
.
∎

Finally, we get the description of the cohomology of twists of Spinor bundles.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Sigma_{\pm}
be a spinor bundle. Then

(1)

H^{i}(\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}},\Sigma_{\pm}(k))=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(2)

H^{0}(\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}},\Sigma_{\pm}(k))=0
for
k\leq 0
.

(3)

\Sigma_{\pm}(t)
is globally generated for
t\geq 1
.

Proof.

(1) and (2) follows from Theorem
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
and short exact sequences (2.4)-(2.6) in
loc. cit
. and (3) follows from exact sequences (
4.4
) and (
4.5
).
∎

4.6.
ACM vector bundles

Let
i:X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
be a smooth hypersurface and let
\mathscr{O}_{X}(1)=i^{*}\mathscr{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}
. Recall that a vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
over
X
is called an
Arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay
vector bundle (or an ACM bundle for short) if the following vanishing holds.

H^{i}(X,\mathscr{E}(k))=0\qquad\textnormal{for all}\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\ \textnormal{and}\ 1\leq i\leq\dim X-1.

The classification of ACM bundles on quadrics in well known.

Theorem 4.6

(
[
1
, Section 1.2, Theorem]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{E}}
be an ACM vector bundle over
Q_{n}
. Then
{\mathscr{E}}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of line bundles and twisted spinor bundles.

4.7.
Frobenius morphism on quadrics

Let
\mathsf{F}:Q_{2m}\to Q_{2m}
be the absolute Frobenius morphism and let
{\mathscr{E}}
be an ACM bundle over
Q_{2m}
. Since the Frobenius morphism is affine, the projection formula yields

H^{i}(Q_{2m},(\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{E}})(t))=H^{i}(Q_{2m},\mathsf{F}^{e}_{*}{\mathscr{E}}(p^{e}t))=H^{i}(Q_{2m},{\mathscr{E}}(p^{e}t))=0\quad(t\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ 1\leq i\leq 2m-1).

It follows that
\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{E}}
is an ACM bundle, so it is a direct sum of line bundles and twisted spinor bundles by Theorem
4.6
. When
{\mathscr{E}}=\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}(t)
or
{\mathscr{E}}={\mathcal{S}}(t)
the decomposition of
\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{E}}
has been described by P. Achinger
[
1
]
building on the work of A. Langer
[
10
]
. In this subsection, we briefly present these results, focusing on the aspects that are relevant for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

First, we remark that
\mathsf{F}
commutes with arbitrary automorphism. In particular,

(4.6)

\alpha\mathsf{F}=\mathsf{F}\alpha.

As a consequence, we obtain the following.

Lemma 4.7
.

There exist integers
a_{j,t,e},b_{j,t,e},c_{j,t,e},d_{j,t,e}
such that

\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(j)=\bigoplus_{t}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(t)^{\oplus a_{j,t,e}}\oplus\bigoplus_{t}{\mathcal{S}}(t)^{\oplus b_{j,t,e}},

and

\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathcal{S}}(j)=\bigoplus_{t}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(t)^{\oplus c_{j,t,e}}\oplus\bigoplus_{t}{\mathcal{S}}(t)^{\oplus d_{j,t,e}}.

Proof.

As explained above,
\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(j)
and
\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathcal{S}}(j)
are ACM bundles, so they decompose as direct sums of line bundles and twisted spinor bundles by Theorem
4.6
. However, by lemma
4.1
and (
4.6
) we have

\mathsf{F}^{e}_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(j)=\mathsf{F}^{e}_{*}\alpha_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(j)=\alpha_{*}\mathsf{F}^{e}_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(j),

and

\mathsf{F}^{e}_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(j)=\mathsf{F}^{e}_{*}\alpha_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(j)=\alpha_{*}\mathsf{F}^{e}_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(j),

while
\alpha_{*}\Sigma_{\pm}=\Sigma_{\mp}
. Therefore, multiplicities of
{\Sigma_{+}}(t)
and
{\Sigma_{-}}(t)
in both decompositions are equal, and the lemma follows.
∎

In
[
1
]
, P. Achinger found a way to determine which of the multiplicities
a_{j,t,e},b_{j,t,e},c_{j,t,e},d_{j,t,e}
are non-zero. We recall his theorem below.

Theorem 4.8

(P. Achinger,
[
1
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Assume that
p\geq 3
,
m\geq 2
, and
e\geq 1
.

(1)

\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(j)
contains
{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(-t)
as a direct summand if and only if

0\leq j+p^{e}t\leq 2m(p^{e}-1).

(2)

\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(j)
contains
{\mathcal{S}}(-t)
as a direct summand if and only if

(m-1)(p-1)p^{e-1}\leq j+tp^{e}\leq mp^{e}+(m-1)p^{e-1}-2m.

(3)

\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathcal{S}}(j)
contains
{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(-t)
as a direct summand if and only if

1\leq j+p^{e}t\leq 2m(p^{e}-1).

(4)

\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathcal{S}}(j)
contains
{\mathcal{S}}(-t)
as a direct summand if and only if

(m-1)(p-1)p^{e-1}+1-\delta_{e,1}\leq j+tp^{e}\leq mp^{e}+(m-1)p^{e-1}-2m+\delta_{e,1}.

For the rest of this subsection, we prove refinements of the direct sum decompositions from Lemma
4.7
based on Theorem
4.8
above.

Lemma 4.9
.

Keep the notation from Lemma
4.7
and assume moreover that
p\geq 3
, and
p^{e-1}\geq\frac{2m}{m-1}
. Then
b_{0,-m,e}\neq 0
and
b_{0,-m+1,e}\neq 0
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
4.8
that for
p\geq 3
the bundle
\mathsf{F}^{e}_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}
contains
{\mathcal{S}}(-t)
as a direct summand if and only if

(m-1)p^{e}-(m-1)p^{e-1}\leq tp^{e}\leq mp^{e}+(m-1)p^{e-1}-2m.

It is straightforward that if
p^{e-1}\geq\frac{2m}{m-1}
then both
t=m
and
t=m-1
satisfy the above inequalities.
∎

Lemma 4.10

(P. Achinger, A. Langer)
.

Assume that
p\geq 3
.

(1)

\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(-m+1)=\bigoplus_{t}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(t)^{\oplus c_{-m+1,t,1}}\oplus{\mathcal{S}}(-m+1)^{\oplus\gamma}
with
\gamma\neq 0
,

(2)

\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(-m)=\bigoplus_{t}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(t)^{\oplus c_{-m,t,1}}\oplus{\mathcal{S}}(-m)^{\oplus\delta}
with
\delta\neq 0
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
4.8
that
\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(j)
contains
{\mathcal{S}}(t)
as a direct summand if and only if

(m-1)(p-1)\leq-j(p-1)+p(j-t)\leq m(p-1).

Substituting in the above inequalities
j=-m
and dividing by
(p-1)
we obtain

m-1\leq m-\frac{p}{p-1}(m+t)\leq m.

Since
t
must be an integer, the above inequalities are satisfied if and only if
t=-m
. The reasoning for
j=-m+1
is analogous.
∎

We need to slightly refine the content of the above lemma. For the future reference let us introduce auxiliary notation. For
a,b\in\left\{+,-\right\}
we denote

(4.7)

\displaystyle u_{a}^{b}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{def.}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\textnormal{ multiplicity of }\Sigma_{b}(-m)\textnormal{ as a direct summand of }\mathsf{F}_{*}\Sigma_{a}(-m),

(4.8)

\displaystyle v_{a}^{b}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{def.}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\textnormal{ multiplicity of }\Sigma_{b}(-m+1)\textnormal{ as a direct summand of }\mathsf{F}_{*}\Sigma_{a}(-m+1).

Lemma 4.11
.

Assume
p\geq 3
. Then the matrices

\begin{pmatrix}u_{+}^{+}&u_{-}^{+}\\
u_{+}^{-}&u_{-}^{-}\end{pmatrix}\qquad\begin{pmatrix}v_{+}^{+}&v_{-}^{+}\\
v_{+}^{-}&v_{-}^{-}\end{pmatrix}

are symmetric and non-zero.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
4.10
that these matrices are non-zero. The symmetry follows from Lemma
4.1
and (
4.6
) like in the proof of Lemma
4.7
.
∎

Finally, recall Langer’s result that will be useful later on.

Lemma 4.12

(A. Langer,
[
10
, Corollary 4.3]
)
.

Assume that
p\geq 3
. Then
\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(-m)
is a direct sum of line bundles.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
4.8
that
{\mathcal{S}}(-t)
is a direct summand of
\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(-m)
if and only if

(m-1)p+1\leq tp\leq mp-1.

The above equation forces that

(m-1)<t<m,

and therefore is never satisfied by an integer
t
.
∎

5.
Even dimensional quadrics are not
D
-affine

In this section, we prove the following result that implies Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
\textnormal{char }K=p\geq 3
and
m\geq 2
. Then
H^{1}(Q_{2m},\mathscr{D}_{Q_{2m}})\neq 0
.

The proof will be deduced from a series of lemmas below.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

(5.1)

0\to{\mathcal{S}}^{\oplus\alpha}\to{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathcal{S}}(1)^{\oplus\beta}\to 0

be a short exact sequence. Then

(1)

For some
\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-},\beta_{+},\beta_{-},\rho\geq 0

{\mathscr{E}}\simeq\Sigma_{+}^{\oplus\alpha_{+}}\oplus\Sigma_{-}^{\oplus\alpha_{-}}\oplus\Sigma_{+}(1)^{\oplus\beta_{+}}\oplus\Sigma_{-}(1)^{\oplus\beta_{-}}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}^{\oplus\rho}.

(2)

(
5.1
)
splits if and only if
\rho=0
.

Proof.

First, we prove (1). We do it in the case when
m
is even. The proof for odd
m
is analogous with the only difference coming from the fact that one needs to use Lemma
4.2
(2) instead of Lemma
4.2
(1). Note that
{\mathscr{E}}
is an extension of ACM bundles, so it is an ACM bundle. By Theorem
4.6

{\mathscr{E}}
is a direct sum of line bundles and twisted spinor bundles. Moreover, it follows from Lemma
4.5
that

(5.2)

H^{0}(Q_{2m},{\mathscr{E}}(t))=0\qquad\textnormal{for }t<0,

and therefore

{\mathscr{E}}\simeq\Sigma_{+}^{\oplus\alpha_{+}}\oplus\Sigma_{-}^{\oplus\alpha_{-}}\oplus\Sigma_{+}(1)^{\oplus\beta_{+}}\oplus\Sigma_{-}(1)^{\oplus\beta_{-}}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}^{\oplus\rho}\oplus{\mathscr{E}}_{1},

where

{\mathscr{E}}_{1}=\bigoplus_{j<0}\left(\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}(j)^{\oplus\rho_{j}}\oplus\Sigma_{+}(-j)^{\oplus r_{j}}\oplus\Sigma_{-}(j)^{\oplus s_{j}}\right).

Now, it follows from Lemma
4.2
that
{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}
fits into a short exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{S}}^{\oplus\beta}\to{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}\to{\mathcal{S}}(1)^{\oplus\alpha}\to 0

obtained by dualizing (
5.1
). In particular, by the reasoning above,

{\mathscr{E}}_{1}^{\vee}(-1)=\bigoplus_{j>0}\left(\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}(j-1)^{\oplus\rho_{j}}\oplus\Sigma_{+}(j)^{\oplus r_{j}}\oplus\Sigma_{-}(j)^{\oplus s_{j}}\right)

has no global sections. It follows that
{\mathscr{E}}_{1}=0
and we are done.

Now, we show (2). Clearly if (
5.1
) splits then
\rho=0
, so we assume that
\rho=0
and we work to show that (
5.1
) splits. Consider the composition

f:{\Sigma_{+}}(1)^{\oplus\beta_{+}}\oplus{\Sigma_{-}}(1)^{\oplus\beta_{-}}\to{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathcal{S}}(1)^{\oplus\beta}

and observe that since

H^{0}(Q_{2m},\Sigma_{\pm})=H^{1}(Q_{2m},\Sigma_{\pm})=0

by Lemma
4.5
,
f
induces an isomorphism on the global sections. It follows that

f
is surjective, because
{\mathcal{S}}(1)
is generated by global sections by Lemma
4.5
(3). On the other hand, by comparing dimensions of global sections we see that

\textnormal{rk}\left({\Sigma_{+}}(1)^{\oplus\beta_{+}}\oplus{\Sigma_{-}}(1)^{\oplus\beta_{-}}\right)=\textnormal{rk}\left({\mathcal{S}}(1)^{\oplus\beta}\right).

Since a surjective morphism of vector bundles of equal rank is an isomorphism, we conclude that
f
is an isomorphism. This clearly implies that
{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathcal{S}}(1)^{\oplus\beta}
splits, and we are done.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
p\geq 3
and consider an exact sequence

(5.3)

0\to{\mathcal{S}}(-m)^{\oplus\alpha}\to{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathcal{S}}(-m+1)^{\oplus\beta}\to 0.

If
(
5.3
)
does not split then

(5.4)

0\to\mathsf{F}_{*}\left({\mathcal{S}}(-m)\right)^{\oplus\alpha}\to\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathscr{E}}\to\mathsf{F}_{*}\left({\mathcal{S}}(-m+1)\right)^{\oplus\beta}\to 0

is isomorphic to a direct sum of some non-split extension

(5.5)

0\to{\mathcal{S}}(-m)^{\oplus\alpha^{\prime}}\to{\mathscr{E}}_{m}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{S}}(-m+1)^{\oplus\beta^{\prime}}\to 0

and a trivial extension.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.10
we have

\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(-m+1)=\bigoplus_{t}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(t)^{\oplus c_{-m+1,t,1}}\oplus{\mathcal{S}}(-m+1)^{\oplus\gamma}

with
\gamma\neq 0
, and

\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(-m)=\bigoplus_{t}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(-j)^{\oplus d_{-m,t,1}}\oplus{\mathcal{S}}(-m)^{\oplus\delta}

with
\delta\neq 0
. It follows from Lemma
4.2
(3) Lemma
4.4
(1), and Lemma
4.5
(1) that for any integer
t
, and for
-m\leq j\leq-m+1
we have

\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}}\left({\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(t),{\mathcal{S}}(j)\right)=\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}}\left({\mathcal{S}}(j),{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(t)\right)=\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}}\left({\mathcal{S}}(-m),{\mathcal{S}}(-m+1)\right)=0.

As a consequence,

\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}}(\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(-m+1)^{\oplus\alpha},\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(-m)^{\oplus\beta})=\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}}({\mathcal{S}}(-m+1),{\mathcal{S}}(m))^{\oplus\alpha\beta\gamma\delta},

so (
5.4
) is isomorphic to a direct sum of some extension (
5.5
) and a trivial extension. It follows that if (
5.5
) splits then so does (
5.4
). Therefore, to prove the lemma we only need to show that if (
5.4
) splits then so does (
5.3
).
By Lemma
5.2

{\mathscr{E}}\simeq\Sigma_{+}(-m)^{\oplus\alpha_{+}}\oplus\Sigma_{-}(-m)^{\oplus\alpha_{-}}\oplus\simeq\Sigma_{+}(-m+1)^{\oplus\beta_{+}}\oplus\Sigma_{-}(-m+1)^{\oplus\beta_{-}}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}(-m)^{\oplus\rho},

and (
5.3
) splits if and only if
\rho=0
, so we assume that (
5.4
) splits and we work to show that
\rho=0
. This is achieved by counting multiplicities of
\Sigma_{\pm}(-m)
and
\Sigma_{\pm}(-m+1)
as direct summands of
\mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathscr{E}}
. We have to show that

(5.6)

\alpha_{+}+\alpha_{-}=2\alpha,\qquad\beta_{+}+\beta_{-}=2\beta,

because then
\rho=0
by comparing ranks of
{\mathscr{E}}
and

\Sigma_{+}(-m)^{\oplus\alpha_{+}}\oplus\Sigma_{-}(-m)^{\oplus\alpha_{-}}\oplus\Sigma_{+}(-m+1)^{\oplus\beta_{+}}\oplus\Sigma_{-}(-m+1)^{\oplus\beta_{-}}.

We will only show the first of the equalities (
5.6
) as the other one is proven analogously. At this point, we recall the notation (
4.7
) and we observe that by Lemma
4.10
and Lemma
4.12
, none of the factors

\mathsf{F}_{*}\Sigma_{\pm}(-m+1),\ \mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathcal{S}}(-m+1),\ \mathsf{F}_{*}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(-m)

contains
\Sigma_{\pm}(-m)
as a direct summand. It follows that if (
5.4
) splits then

(5.7)

\displaystyle\alpha(u_{+}^{+}+u_{-}^{+})=

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{+}u_{+}^{+}+\alpha_{-}u_{-}^{+}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\alpha(u_{+}^{-}+u_{-}^{-})=

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{+}u_{+}^{-}+\alpha_{-}u_{-}^{-},

which can be rewritten as

\begin{pmatrix}u_{+}^{+}&u_{-}^{+}\\
u_{+}^{-}&u_{-}^{-}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha-\alpha_{+}\\
\alpha-\alpha_{-}\end{pmatrix}=0.

By Lemma
4.11
, the matrix on the left hand side is symmetric and non-zero, so either it is invertible, or all of its entries are equal and non-zero. In any case

2\alpha-\alpha_{+}-\alpha_{-}=0,

and we are done.
∎

Finally, we prove the main result of the paper.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

By Proposition
1.9
we only have to show that for some
e>0
there exists an extension

(5.9)

0\to\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}\to{\mathscr{E}}\to\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}\to 0

such that

(5.10)

0\to\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e+t}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}\to\mathsf{F}_{*}^{t}{\mathscr{E}}\to\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e+t}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}\to 0

does not split for all
t\geq 1
. As in Lemma
4.7
write

\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}\simeq\bigoplus_{t}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}(t)^{\oplus a_{{}_{0,t,e}}}\oplus\bigoplus_{t}{\mathcal{S}}(t)^{\oplus b_{0,t,e}}.

Then, as in the proof of Lemma
5.3
we have for some integers
\mu_{k}

\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}}(\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}},\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}})=\bigoplus_{k<0}\textnormal{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathscr{O}_{Q_{2m}}}}\left({\mathcal{S}}(k+1),{\mathcal{S}}(k)\right)^{\oplus\mu_{k}}

and
\mu_{m}\neq 0
for
e\gg 0
by Lemma
4.9
. It follows that every (
5.9
) is isomorphic to a direct sum of extensions

0\to{\mathcal{S}}(k)^{\oplus\alpha_{k}}\to{\mathscr{E}}_{k}\to{\mathcal{S}}(k+1)^{\oplus\beta_{k}}\to 0

and some trivial extension. In particular, we can take (
5.9
) to be isomorphic to a direct sum of some non-trivial extension

0\to{\mathcal{S}}(-m)^{\oplus\alpha}\to{\mathscr{E}}_{m}\to{\mathcal{S}}(-m+1)^{\oplus\beta}\to 0

(which exists by Lemma

In this section, we list some consequences of Theorem

First, we recall that a surjective morphisms

Theorem 6.1

(A. Langer,

Let

is

We note that Corollary

Corollary 6.2

7.
Related problems and some further questions

In this final section, we discuss problems related to
D
-affinity of flag varieties and we pose some questions motivated by the results of this paper. First, as explained in the introduction (cf.
[
11
, Lemma 1.5]
), the process of proving
D
-affinity of a smooth variety
X
can be split into

(1)

showing that
X
is
D
-quasi-affine, and

(2)

Proving that the vanishing
H^{i}(X,{\mathscr{D}_{X}})=0
holds for all
i>0
.

Although we do not know whenever all flag varieties are
D
-quasi-affine in positive characteristic, it seems that in the above (2) is much more subtle that (1). For example, Haastert
[
4
, 4.4.2 Korollar]
showed that full flag varieties are
D
-quasi-affine, and Langer
[
10
, Proposition 2.7]
show that quadrics are
D
-quasi-affine, while the results of this paper show that there exist both quadrics and full flag varieties that are not
D
-affine. The problem of determining the vanishing in (2) is closely related to the problem of determining quasi-exceptionality of
\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{X}}
that has been studied quite intensively, both in the context of flag varieties and some non-homogeneous Fano varieties. Below, we survey the latter problem and formulate some questions.

Assume that
X
is smooth and projective. Recall, that a vector bundle
{\mathscr{E}}
is called
quasi-exceptional
if

\textnormal{Ext}^{i}_{{\mathscr{O}_{X}}}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})=0\qquad(i>0).

Over the field of positive characteristic we have, as in Section
3

H^{i}(X,{\mathscr{D}_{X}^{(e)}})=\textnormal{Ext}^{i}_{{\mathscr{O}_{X}}}(\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{X}},\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{X}}),\ H^{i}(X,{\mathscr{D}_{X}})=\varinjlim_{e\to\infty}\textnormal{Ext}^{i}_{{\mathscr{O}_{X}}}(\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{X}},\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{X}}),

so it follows that if
\mathsf{F}_{*}^{e}{\mathscr{O}_{X}}
is quasi-exceptional for
e\gg 0
then the vanishing (2) holds. If
X
is
F
-split (for example, a flag variety) then Andersen–Kaneda
[
2

claimed the converse to be true; the vanishing of

for all

[

and D. Mallory

From the quantum Boltzmann operator to the Quantum Landau operator
Maria Pia Gualdani, Nataša Pavlović, Justin Toyota, Dominic Wynter

Abstract
. In this manuscript we derive the quantum Landau operator as the weak-coupling limit of the quantum Boltzmann operator (also known as the Uehling-Uhlenbeck operator). We consider both Fermi-Dirac and Bose-Einstein statistics. Our approach is inspired by the work by Benedetto and Pulvirenti [ 5 ] , where the classical Landau operator was derived from the quantum Boltzmann operator. To capture the ternary term in the quantum Landau operator, we introduce a new two-parameter scaling that preserves the quantum effects in the limit. Furthermore, we provide an explicit rate of convergence that depends on the regularity of the interaction potential.



1.
Introduction

The focus of this paper is a derivation of the quantum Landau operator from the quantum Boltzmann operator. Let us start by introducing these operators and the equations containing them.

The quantum Boltzmann equation (also known as the Uehling-Uhlenbeck or Boltzmann-Nordheim equation)
was introduced by Nordheim
[
40
]
and Uehling and Uhlenbeck
[
43

to model a dilute gas of weakly-interacting bosons or fermions, generalizing the classical Boltzmann equation derived by Maxwell

(1)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f&=\;\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\hat{V}(k)+\theta\hat{V}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(k+\frac{u}{\hbar}}}\right)}}\right]^{2}\delta(\hbar|k|^{2}+k\cdot u)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\cdot\Big\{(1+\theta(2\pi\hbar)^{3}f)(1+\theta(2\pi\hbar)^{3}f_{1})f^{\prime}\!f_{1}^{\prime}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-(1+\theta(2\pi\hbar)^{3}f^{\prime})(1+\theta(2\pi\hbar)^{3}f^{\prime}_{1})f\!f_{1}\Big\}\,dk\,dv_{1}\end{split}

for an interaction potential
V
and a quantum parameter
\hbar>0
, where we have written
u=v-v_{1}
and taken
\theta=\pm 1

\theta=1
modeling Bose-Einstein particles and
\theta=-1

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=f(v)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}

\displaystyle=f(v^{\prime})

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=f(v_{1})

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=f(v^{\prime}_{1}),

(2)

\displaystyle=v+\hbar k,

\displaystyle v^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=v_{1}-\hbar k

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{S^{2}}\!B\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v-v_{1},\omega}}\right)[f^{\prime}f_{1}^{\prime}-f\!f_{1}]\,d\omega\,dv_{1}

Whereas the classical Boltzmann operator in (

We now summarize the current analytical results for the quantum Boltzmann equation (
1

\theta=-1

\theta=1

In the fermionic case, the first works exhibiting existence of global weak solutions to (

In the spatially homogeneous setting, existence of global smooth solutions and convergence to equilibrium were proved by Escobedo, Mischler and Valle

In the bosonic case, we first mention the results obtained in the homogeneous case. In that context, most research has focused on isotropic (radial) initial data. Namely, Lu

A closely related equation is the quantum Landau equation, which we write as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f

1.1.
Notation

We will use the following notation for Sobolev norms: for any function
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
which is
k

k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\begin{cases}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial_{v}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{p}}}\right)^{1/p},&p\in[1,\infty)\\
\max\limits_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial_{v}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},&p=+\infty\end{cases}

and we define
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to be the space of functions
f
such that
\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\infty
.

The interaction potential
V
appears in our operators via its Fourier transform

\hat{V}(k):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-ik\cdot v}V(v)\,dv.

Throughout this paper we will assume that
V
is radial, which implies
\hat{V}
is also radial. By abuse of notation, we can thus view
\hat{V}
as a function on
[0,\infty)
; i.e., we write
\hat{V}(r)
to denote the value of
\hat{V}
at any
k\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|k|=r
. The primary functions of
\hat{V}
that we will deal with are the moments

(7)

\displaystyle M_{a}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!(\mu+\mu^{a})\hat{V}(\mu)^{2}\,d\mu;

note that
V
being even implies
\hat{V}
is real-valued, so this is always non-negative. Finally, in discussing the hypotheses of our main theorem, we will use the Japanese bracket notation
\langle v\rangle:=\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}
. We will at times use the notation

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{s}}:=\|\langle v\rangle^{s}f\|_{L^{\infty}},\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}.

1.2.
Main Result

We now introduce a two parameter family of scalings
(\varepsilon,\alpha)
in the quantum Boltzmann operator, as follows:
for a given
\varepsilon>0

\alpha=\alpha(\varepsilon)\geq 0

(8)

\begin{split}Q_{qB}^{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f)(v):=&\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\hat{V}(k)+\theta\hat{V}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(k+\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}}\right)}}\right]^{2}\delta(\varepsilon|k|^{2}+k\cdot u)\\
&\quad\cdot\{(1+\theta\alpha f)(1+\theta\alpha f_{1})f^{\prime}\!f_{1}^{\prime}-(1+\theta\alpha f^{\prime})(1+\theta\alpha f^{\prime}_{1})f\!f_{1}\}\,dk\,dv_{1}\end{split}

for sufficiently regular functions

(for precise conditions on
V

1.1

where we have written

, and where
\theta=\pm 1
.
We interpret

as acting on test functions

as follows
(details can be found in Subsection
3.2

(9)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{qB}^{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f)(v)\psi(v)\,dv

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\!f_{1}\int_{S^{2}_{-}}|\hat{k}\cdot u|\hat{V}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{|\hat{k}\cdot u|}{\varepsilon}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\hat{V}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{|\hat{k}\cdot u|}{\varepsilon}}}\right)+\theta\hat{V}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\sqrt{u^{2}-|\hat{k}\cdot u|}}{\varepsilon}}}\right)}}\right]

where the half-sphere
S^{2}_{-}
is defined by

(10)

In order to state our main results we also need a weak formulation of the quantum Landau operator (

(11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!Q_{qL}^{\alpha_{0}}(f)(v)\psi(v)\,dv

Theorem 1.1

Let

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q_{qB}^{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f)-Q_{qL}^{\alpha_{0}}(f)}}\right)\psi(v)\,dv}}\right|\\

Remark 1
.

For fermions, when
\alpha_{0}\neq 0
, it seems to us that the limit obtained in the above theorem could be understood as a high-density limit, due to the implicit rescaling of velocity
(v,v_{*})\mapsto(\varepsilon v,\varepsilon v_{*})
in (
9
), which can be viewed as taking data
f
with mass
M\sim\varepsilon^{-3}
and energy
E\sim\varepsilon^{-5}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. In addition, the limit has some aspects of a grazing collision limit, which is used to connect the classical Boltzmann operator with the classical Landau operator. More precisely, the collision term weak formulation (
9
) formally implies that collisions concentrate on the set where
|u|\cos\vartheta=|\hat{k}\cdot u|\lesssim\varepsilon
, so that collisions with small relative velocity
u
, or with angle of incidence
\vartheta\approx\pi/2
, dominate. This can be compared to foundational grazing limit results such as
[
18
]
, which in our parametrization can be interpreted as taking collisions concentrated on the set where
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert\vartheta-\pi/2}}\right\rvert\lesssim\varepsilon
. Our result, in contrast, involves a rescaled interaction potential, taking both the deflection angle
\vartheta^{\prime}=2\vartheta-\pi
and deflection velocity
|u|
to zero as
|u|\sin(2\vartheta)\to 0
, with the result that we derive the Landau equation with Coulomb potentials, regardless of the original interaction potential.

Remark 2

We expect that a similar result might be obtained when

for

Remark 4
.

For ease of notation, going forward we will write integration of a function
F\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
against a test function
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
as a pairing:

\langle F,\psi\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!F(v)\psi(v)\,dv.

As noted in Remark
3
, the terms
Q_{B}^{\varepsilon}(f)
,
Q_{T}^{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f)
,
R_{B}^{\varepsilon}(f)
, and
R_{T}^{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f)
are
L^{1}
when
\langle v\rangle^{N}f\in L^{\infty}
for some
N>3
.

Proof.

We will treat the case
\langle Q_{B}^{\varepsilon}(f),\psi\rangle
explicitly; the other equations follow from the same reasoning. From the definition of
Q_{B}^{\varepsilon}(f)
in (
41
), our initial weak form expression is

(53)

\langle Q_{B}^{\varepsilon}(f),\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi(v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{S_{-}^{2}}|\hat{k}\cdot u|\hat{V}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{|\hat{k}\cdot u|}{\varepsilon}}}\right)^{2}(f^{\prime}\!f_{1}^{\prime}-f\!f_{1})\,d\hat{k}\,dv_{1}\,dv.

This can be written as a difference of two integrals, and we will use a change of variables to rewrite the first one,

(54)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi(v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{S_{-}^{2}}|\hat{k}\cdot u|\hat{V}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{|\hat{k}\cdot u|}{\varepsilon}}}\right)^{2}f^{\prime}\!f_{1}^{\prime}\,d\hat{k}\,dv_{1}\,dv.

Specifically, we apply the pre-postcollisional change of variables

\displaystyle v_{new}

\displaystyle=v^{\prime}=v-(\hat{k}\cdot u)\hat{k}

\displaystyle v_{1,new}

\displaystyle=v_{1}^{\prime}=v_{1}+(\hat{k}\cdot u)\hat{k}.

Under this change of variables

\displaystyle u_{new}=v_{new}-v_{new}^{\prime}=u-2(\hat{k}\cdot u)\hat{k},

\displaystyle\hat{k}\cdot u_{new}=\hat{k}\cdot[u-2(\hat{k}\cdot u)\hat{k}]=-\hat{k}\cdot u,

so we can rewrite (
54
) as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{S_{-}^{2}}\psi(v^{\prime})|\hat{k}\cdot u|\hat{V}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{|\hat{k}\cdot u|}{\varepsilon}}}\right)^{2}f\!f_{1}\,d\hat{k}\,dv_{1}\,dv.

Substituting this back into (
53
) gives us

\langle Q_{B}^{\varepsilon}(f),\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\!f_{1}\int_{S_{-}^{2}}|\hat{k}\cdot u|\hat{V}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{|\hat{k}\cdot u|}{\varepsilon}}}\right)^{2}[\psi(v^{\prime})-\psi(v)]\,d\hat{k}\,dv_{1}\,dv,

as desired. The only difference when treating the other terms, namely
\langle R_{B}^{\varepsilon}(f),\psi\rangle
and
\langle R_{T}^{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f),\psi\rangle
, is that one must consider how the change of variables affects the expression
\sqrt{u^{2}-(\hat{k}\cdot u)^{2}}
. However, since

u_{new}^{2}=u^{2}-4(\hat{k}\cdot u)\hat{k}\cdot u+4(\hat{k}\cdot u)^{2}=u^{2}

and
\hat{k}\cdot u_{new}=-\hat{k}\cdot u
, the expression remains unchanged.
∎

In the subsequent sections, we will show that

, while

and

3.5.
Limits of the Main Terms

Throughout this section we will encounter expressions of the following forms:

(65)

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}^{(1)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|f\!f_{1}|}{|u|^{\lambda}}\,dv_{1}\,dv

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}^{(2)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|f^{2}f_{1}|}{|u|^{\lambda}}\,dv_{1}\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|f\!f_{1}^{2}|}{|u|^{\lambda}}\,dv_{1}\,dv

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}^{(3)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|f\!f_{1}||\nabla_{v}f|}{|u|^{\lambda}}\,dv_{1}\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|f\!f_{1}||\nabla_{v_{1}}f_{1}|}{|u|^{\lambda}}\,dv_{1}\,dv,

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. We can bound these integrals as follows:

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\langle v\rangle^{N}f\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
N>3
, then for all
\lambda\in(0,3)
,

(66)

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,\lambda}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,\lambda}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3}

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}^{(3)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N,\lambda}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{3}.

Proof.

A key observation is that if
\langle v\rangle^{N}f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
N>3
, then
\langle v\rangle^{-N}\in L^{p}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and so

\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\langle v\rangle^{-N}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{N,p}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f\|_{L^{\infty}},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

Therefore it suffices to get bounds in terms of
L^{p}
norms of
f
(or
\nabla f
, for
I_{\lambda}^{(3)}
). We start with
I_{\lambda}^{(1)}
. For any
\lambda\in(0,3)
, there exist
1<p,q<\infty
(depending on
\lambda
) such that
I_{\lambda}^{(1)}\lesssim_{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|f\|_{L^{q}}
by the bilinear form of the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality (see Sobolev
[
42
, §4]
), which proves the desired bound on
I_{\lambda}^{(1)}
by our prior observation. The bounds on
I_{\lambda}^{(2)}
and
I_{\lambda}^{(3)}
now follow from those on
I_{\lambda}^{(1)}
:

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}I_{\lambda}^{(1)}\lesssim_{N,\lambda}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3}

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}^{(3)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}I_{\lambda}^{(1)}\lesssim_{N,\lambda}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\lesssim_{N}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{3}.

∎

Remark 5
.

This is not a sharp range of
\lambda
for which such bounds hold (for example, the bound on
I_{\lambda}^{(1)}
easily extends to
\lambda=0
), but this is all that we will need.

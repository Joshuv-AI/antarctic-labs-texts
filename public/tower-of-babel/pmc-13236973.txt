Machine learning-driven time series analysis for SOH prediction of lithium-ion batteries
Authors: Yunlong Zhang, Xiaolei Bi, Shiqiang Wang, Bin Tao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEnergy storage batteries are essential for stabilizing renewable energy systems and improving power grid efficiency. However, challenges such as capacity degradation, limited data quality, and the need for real-time evaluation highlight the importance of accurate State of Health (SOH) prediction. This study evaluates the effectiveness of Random Forest, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) models in predicting SOH across single lithium-ion cells, cells under environmental influence, and battery modules. The Bi-LSTM model achieved a Mean Absolute Error of 0.00668, a Mean Squared Error of 0.0042, and an R2 value of 0.9253 in single-cell prediction. In comparison, the Random Forest model recorded a Mean Absolute Error of 0.0523, a Mean Squared Error of 0.0159 and an R2 of 0.8960, indicating a reduction in error of over 69% and a significant improvement in predictive accuracy. Incorporating physically meaningful features such as discharge time and plateau voltage further enhanced model performance. These results demonstrate the Bi-LSTM model’s strong ability to capture long-term temporal dependencies and its potential for improving intelligent battery health monitoring in real-world energy storage systems.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-33725-w.Keywords: Lithium-ion battery, State of health, Machine learningSubject terms: Batteries, Energy storage

Introduction

The increasing integration of renewable energy sources, such as wind and solar power, increases the need for stable and efficient energy storage. Energy storage batteries, especially lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) serve as critical components, mitigating power fluctuations, enhancing supply quality, and enabling peak-load shifting1,2. However, these batteries face performance degradation over time, including capacity loss and increased internal resistance, which pose risks to both efficiency and safety. Accurate and real-time State of Health (SOH) prediction models are essential for ensuring the reliability and longevity of these systems3. SOH prediction not only supports timely maintenance and replacement but also optimizes charge-discharge strategies, extending battery life and reducing operational costs4.

Existing research on SOH prediction models has seen significant progress through the application of machine learning and deep learning techniques. Traditional machine learning models, such as Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Random Forest (RF), offer robust performance on small datasets but often struggle with generalizing across diverse battery types5,6. On the other hand, advanced deep learning models, including Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and their variants, effectively capture complex nonlinear relationships and temporal dependencies in time-series data7,8. Despite these advancements, several challenges remain. Limited data quality and quantity, computational inefficiencies, and the inability of models to generalize across different battery chemistries and operating conditions continue to hinder practical deployment9. Furthermore, achieving real-time prediction capabilities with high accuracy remains a critical bottleneck for many current methodologies.

In recent years, hybrid deep learning architectures such as CNN–LSTM networks10, attention-based Transformers11, and graph neural networks (GNNs)12 have been proposed to enhance temporal feature extraction and capture nonlinear electrochemical dependencies. Although these models demonstrate high accuracy, their heavy computational cost and reliance on large-scale labeled datasets limit deployment in embedded battery management systems. Meanwhile, feature engineering techniques based on statistical descriptors, wavelet analysis, or transfer learning13–15 have improved prediction performance but often lack physical interpretability. In contrast, the present work adopts a lightweight Bi-LSTM framework that integrates two physically meaningful features—discharge time and plateau voltage—derived directly from operational data. This design preserves interpretability while maintaining high predictive accuracy. Moreover, few studies have validated SOH models using large-format 200 Ah LiFePO₄ cells and modules from actual energy-storage systems, highlighting a critical research gap that this study aims to address.

The key novelty of this study lies in proposing a lightweight and interpretable Bi-LSTM-based time-series prediction framework for lithium-ion battery SOH estimation. Unlike previous models that often rely on complex hybrid architectures or high-dimensional handcrafted features, our approach incorporates only essential operational variables and two physically meaningful indicators—discharge time and plateau voltage—to achieve strong prediction accuracy with low complexity. In addition, this study systematically evaluates model performance across three levels: standard single-cell datasets, feature-enhanced single-cell datasets, and battery modules, providing a multi-level validation rarely reported in prior literature.

Model selection and construction

Deep learning methodologies, particularly neural network-based models such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and their variants, including LSTM networks, have demonstrated remarkable efficacy in processing nonlinear and high-dimensional data16,17. These models are particularly effective in capturing intricate temporal patterns within time-series datasets. For instance, LSTM networks address the gradient vanishing or exploding issues inherent in traditional RNNs by incorporating gating mechanisms, thereby enabling the model to account for a broader temporal range of historical information when predicting battery SOH18. Moreover, deep learning models possess the capacity to autonomously extract meaningful features from raw data, significantly enhancing their generalization capability and robustness. Leveraging these advancements, researchers have developed a variety of SOH prediction frameworks by integrating diverse and heterogeneous datasets, including voltage, current, and temperature data derived from battery charge-discharge processes. These models not only provide precise predictions of Remaining Useful Life (RUL) but also identify critical factors contributing to battery performance degradation, offering a scientific basis for effective battery maintenance and fault diagnosis19,20.

Conversely, traditional machine learning algorithms, such as SVM, RF, and Gradient Boosting Trees (GBT), have also played a pivotal role in predicting battery health21,22. These approaches typically require manually engineered features but exhibit strong adaptability and interpretability when applied to small datasets, making them well-suited for scenarios with limited data availability or constrained computational resources. Studies have shown that ensemble learning techniques, which integrate multiple machine learning algorithms, can further enhance predictive accuracy and stability. For instance, stacked generalization, a method where primary models (e.g., SVM, RF) conduct preliminary analysis on battery data, followed by a secondary model (e.g., a neural network) synthesizing the outputs has been shown to yield more precise SOH predictions24,25. Additionally, to address challenges like data imbalance commonly encountered in real-world applications, strategies such as oversampling, under sampling, and cost-sensitive learning have been explored to ensure reliable predictions across varying SOH levels26–28. This study adopts RF, LSTM, and Bi-LSTM networks to construct SOH prediction models for energy storage batteries, systematically comparing their accuracy to identify the most effective approach.

Random forest model

The RF regression algorithm, a powerful ensemble learning method, enhances predictive accuracy and model generalization by combining multiple decision trees constructed through bootstrap sampling, as illustrated in Fig. 129. In time-series forecasting, RF improve model performance by capturing nonlinear relationships and interaction effects, making them particularly effective for time-series data with complex patterns. By incorporating lagged features, such as past observations or seasonal indicators, RF can leverage this information to boost predictive power30. Furthermore, RF exhibit strong robustness to outliers and noise, which makes them highly effective in handling real-world data that often contains uncertainty and irregularities. However, the application of RF regression in time-series forecasting also presents certain challenges31.

Random Forest algorithm model.

Firstly, RF lack explicit modeling of the inherent temporal order and dependencies in time-series data, which can result in the model’s inability to fully exploit temporal information, thereby compromising the accuracy of long-term predictions32. Secondly, compared to traditional time-series models, such as ARIMA or state-space models, RF offer limited interpretability and struggle to provide clear causal explanations. This represents a significant limitation in fields where understanding the dynamic relationships between variables is crucial33. Furthermore, when handling long sequences or high-frequency data, RF can become computationally demanding, particularly in real-time prediction scenarios, where the speed of model training and prediction may become a bottleneck34.

Long short-term memory model

In RNNs, when the time-series data becomes excessively long, the network encounters issues such as gradient vanishing or explosion during training, which impedes the effective updating of the network parameters35. To address these challenges, significant research has been conducted, culminating in the development of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks by Hochreiter and Schmidhuber in 1997. LSTM networks are a variant of RNNs, specifically designed to overcome the limitations of traditional RNNs by incorporating memory cells and gating mechanisms. These innovations allow LSTM networks to learn long-term dependencies within time-series data while mitigating the problems of gradient vanishing and explosion36,37. The architecture of an LSTM network unit is depicted in Fig. 2.

Long Short-Term Memory algorithm model.

An LSTM neural network comprises a series of identical LSTM unit structures38. Each unit is composed of three gating mechanisms: the forget gate (), the input gate (), and the output gate (). The specific functions and effects of the three gating mechanisms are elaborated in the supporting literature. These gates dynamically regulate the learning, forgetting, and retention of historical sequence information. LSTM networks effectively address the issues of gradient explosion and vanishing that commonly hinder traditional RNNs, thereby enabling the modeling of long-term dependencies in sequential data39.

Specifically, for both LSTM and Bi-LSTM models, the architecture consists of two hidden layers, each containing 64 hidden units. A dropout layer with a rate of 0.2 was applied after each LSTM layer to mitigate overfitting. The models were trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001, and the mean squared error (MSE) was used as the loss function. The batch size was set to 32, and the training was conducted for 100 epochs. Early stopping was employed based on validation loss with a patience of 10 epochs to prevent overfitting.

Bidirectional long short-term memory model

Bi-LSTM networks represent an advanced extension of RNN architectures, designed to enhance representational capacity by simultaneously processing forward and backward information in a time sequence40. As illustrated in Fig. 3, the core of Bi-LSTM lies in its bidirectional structure, which integrates a forward LSTM layer that processes data in the natural sequence and a backward LSTM layer that handles the same sequence in reverse41. This bidirectional mechanism enables Bi-LSTM to access both preceding and succeeding contextual information at any given time step t, facilitating a more comprehensive understanding of the dependencies within the sequence. Leveraging memory cells and gating mechanisms, including the input gate, forget gate, and output gate, Bi-LSTM effectively controls the storage and flow of information, ensuring robust modeling of complex sequential data42.

Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory algorithm model.

The choice of Bi-LSTM for SOH prediction models in LIBs stems from its unique advantages. First, the health status of LIBs in energy storage system is influenced by multiple factors, including charge-discharge history, environmental temperature, and usage frequency. These factors are not only tied to the current state but can also be impacted by future operational patterns. For instance, frequent high-current discharges over a short period can negatively affect battery health. Bi-LSTM’s ability to simultaneously consider historical and future data features enhances its accuracy in predicting battery health43. Secondly, battery performance degradation is a gradual process involving complex dynamic changes over extended time scales. Traditional machine learning methods or unidirectional LSTM networks may struggle to capture such long-term dependencies. Bi-LSTM, leveraging its memory cells, effectively retains long-range information, making it particularly well-suited for modeling the battery aging process44.

Moreover, as battery operations generate substantial amounts of time-series data, Bi-LSTM efficiently processes these sequences, extracting features critical for health status evaluation. Finally, compared to other deep learning models, such as CNNs, Bi-LSTM is more adept at handling one-dimensional time-series data. While CNNs excel in two-dimensional data tasks like image recognition, they require additional architectural adjustments to preserve temporal information when applied to time-series data. Bi-LSTM’s inherent design eliminates this need, making it a more natural fit for SOH prediction45,46.

Results and discussion

The SOH prediction of LIBs, based on the RF, LSTM and Bi-LSTM algorithm, aims to accurately assess the current state of the battery by analyzing its historical operational data. This process begins with the collection of extensive historical data, including, but not limited to, charge/discharge current, voltage, and temperature, typically derived from prior foundational research. Data preprocessing is a crucial step, encompassing tasks such as outlier removal, imputation of missing values, and data normalization to ensure the quality and reliability of model training. The construction of the LIBs health status prediction model involves three key frameworks: a single-cell time-series SOH prediction model, a single-cell time-series SOH prediction model incorporating environmental factors, and a battery module time-series SOH prediction model.

The experimental data were collected from the energy storage power station of Sinopec Shengli Oilfield. The battery cells and modules used in the tests are lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) lithium-ion batteries manufactured by EVE Energy Co., Ltd., with a nominal capacity of 200 Ah. All data details and test details are shown in Table S1, tests were conducted under controlled conditions, with charge/discharge current rates ranging from 0.5 to 1 °C, and an ambient temperature maintained at 25 °C ± 2 °C. The testing protocol included full charge-discharge cycling for aging analysis, and data (voltage, current, temperature) were recorded at a frequency of 1 Hz to capture the dynamic operating characteristics.

Data preprocessing begins with visualizing the SOH variation curve of single-cell battery cycling data, specifically over 1000 cycles, to support time-series prediction as shown in Fig. S1. In the early stage of cycling, the SOH shows a slight increase before entering the gradual decline phase. This phenomenon is attributed to the activation behavior of newly assembled LiFePO4 cells, where improved electrolyte wetting, enhanced lithium-ion transport, and reduced interfacial impedance temporarily increase the available discharge capacity. After this activation period, normal aging processes such as SEI film growth, lithium inventory loss, and microstructural fatigue begin to dominate, leading to the observed capacity decay and SOH reduction. This evolution reflects the intrinsic electrochemical behavior of LiFePO4 batteries under stable cycling conditions.

To effectively predict the SOH of batteries, the raw SOH data was first subjected to min-max normalization. Specifically, the MinMaxScaler method was employed to map the SOH data to a range of [-1, 1], ensuring numerical stability during model training and improving convergence speed. This normalization process eliminates the influence of differing data scales, enhancing the comparability of data features and facilitating the accelerated learning of subsequent neural network models.

The normalized SOH data was subsequently used to construct input-output sequences for the time-series prediction model. A fixed-length time window (set to 10-time steps in this study) was defined, and a sliding window technique was applied to extract continuous data segments as input sequences, with the data point following each segment serving as the corresponding output label. This process not only preserves the temporal dependencies within the data but also provides the model with the opportunity to learn long-term dependencies, which is critical for accurately predicting SOH.

Furthermore, to evaluate model performance and mitigate the risk of overfitting, the constructed sequence dataset was divided into training and testing sets, with 80% of the data used for model training and the remaining 20% reserved for assessing the model’s generalization capability.

The battery SOH time-series prediction experiment employed evaluation metrics such as Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Squared Error (MSE), and R² to assess the performance of the models. The specific calculation formulas of MAE, MSE and R2 are described in detail in the supporting information.

Figure 4 illustrates the results of predicting the SOH status of a single LIB using the RF model, LSTM model and Bi-LSTM model. The evaluation results of all models are summarized in Table 1. Besides, Table S2 presents a statistical comparison of model performance over 30 independent runs. The results show that the Bi-LSTM model consistently outperforms both RF and traditional LSTM models across all metrics. Specifically, Bi-LSTM achieves the lowest MAE, MSE and the R² closest to 1, indicating superior predictive accuracy and reliability. Paired t-tests confirm the statistical significance of these improvements (p < 0.001) when comparing Bi-LSTM to both RF and LSTM, demonstrating the model’s robustness and effectiveness in lithium-ion battery SOH prediction.

The evaluation results show that the RF model achieves MAE, MSE, and R2 values of 0.0523, 0.0159, and 0.8960, respectively. In comparison, the LSTM model demonstrates slightly improved performance, with MAE and MSE values slightly reduced and R² increased to 0.9057, indicating better predictive accuracy than the Random Forest model. The Bi-LSTM model demonstrates the best performance, with MAE as low as 0.0073, MSE at 0.0045, and R² reaching 0.9231. These results highlight that Bi-LSTM has a distinct advantage in capturing long-term dependencies within time-series data and utilizing contextual information, making it particularly well-suited for tackling complex SOH prediction problems.

To enhance the model’s predictive capabilities, this study incorporates two critical parameters into the model inputs: charge-discharge rate and plateau voltage. The charge-discharge rate is crucial for understanding the degradation of lithium-ion battery health. Under high-rate charge-discharge conditions, the accelerated internal electrochemical reactions lead to rapid declines in battery capacity and SOH. The inclusion of plateau voltage further aids in uncovering trends in the battery’s internal chemical reactions, such as electrolyte decomposition and active material detachment. By integrating these additional parameters, the predictive model achieves a more comprehensive understanding of battery aging mechanisms, significantly improving its accuracy and reliability. Compared to the approaches in recent research, which often involve high-dimensional feature fusion or complex statistical transformations, our feature selection emphasizes physical interpretability and practical accessibility18,33. To assess the effectiveness of our chosen features, we conducted an ablation analysis, where the removal of individual features led to a measurable drop in model performance. These results support the contribution of each selected feature, while also indicating that adding additional parameters does not lead to a significant improvement in prediction accuracy. In addition to investigating the time-series SOH prediction for single LIB with integrated influencing factors, this study further explores the application of models in predicting the SOH of battery modules to evaluate their generalizability and adaptability. A battery module, comprising multiple single cells, has its overall performance influenced by the states of individual cells. Consequently, SOH prediction for battery modules presents greater challenges, requiring a comprehensive consideration of the interactions and variances among individual cells. This study applies RF, LSTM and Bi-LSTM models to battery module SOH prediction to assess their robustness and generalization capabilities in handling complex battery systems.

The comprehensive SOH time-series variation for the battery module is illustrated in Fig. 6. As shown in Table 1, compared to the prediction metrics for single-cell batteries, all models exhibit slightly higher MAE and MSE values and a decrease in R2 when predicting the SOH of battery modules. This indicates that the models perform less effectively for battery modules than for single cells. The reduced performance is primarily due to the increased complexity of battery modules, which are influenced by a broader range of factors. For instance, variations in the series-parallel configurations of individual cells and initialization differences among cells can significantly impact the SOH of the module.

Despite this slight decline in predictive performance for battery modules, all models still demonstrate robust predictive capabilities. Future research could improve the accuracy of SOH predictions for battery modules and even entire battery packs by adopting strategies such as performance balancing and incorporating critical influencing parameters.

To contextualize the performance of our proposed Bi-LSTM model, we conducted a comparative analysis with several representative state-of-the-art studies on lithium-ion battery SOH prediction. As summarized in Table 2, these works include bimodal CNN-LSTM frameworks, Transformer-based models, and ensemble deep learning approaches. These models often rely on complex input structures such as voltage images, multi-modal signals, or handcrafted statistical features. In contrast, our Bi-LSTM model is built upon a lightweight, time-series architecture that uses only operational data augmented with discharge time and plateau voltage. Despite its simplicity, the Bi-LSTM model achieved a MAE of 0.0073, a MSE of 0.0045 and an R2 of 0.923, outperforming or matching more complex models. This highlights the strong capability of our model to deliver high predictive accuracy without sacrificing computational efficiency or interpretability.

These results clearly demonstrate that the proposed Bi-LSTM model not only performs well under controlled single-cell conditions, but also generalizes effectively to more complex module-level configurations with minimal accuracy loss. This consistency supports the model’s robustness across hierarchical battery system levels. Furthermore, compared to other state-of-the-art models, the simplicity of our architecture and the physical interpretability of input features enhance its deployment potential in real-world battery management systems. Specifically, our work contributes by proposing a low-complexity Bi-LSTM framework for SOH prediction, demonstrating its generalizability across multiple types of datasets, and emphasizing a feature selection strategy grounded in physical battery behavior to ensure both high accuracy and interpretability.

In addition, compared to the ensemble-based approach, our Bi-LSTM framework eliminates the need for extensive feature engineering. The model learns directly from temporal patterns in raw time-series data, enhancing generalization and reducing implementation complexity. Moreover, the inclusion of physically meaningful features, such as discharge time and plateau voltage, provides additional explanatory value and supports practical integration into battery management systems. Overall, our model achieves a favorable balance between accuracy, interpretability, and ease of deployment, making it a compelling solution for intelligent SOH monitoring in real-world energy storage applications.

From a computational standpoint, the proposed Bi-LSTM model maintains a relatively lightweight architecture, with significantly fewer trainable parameters compared to transformer-based or hybrid models. The average training time per epoch on a standard workstation is short, and the inference latency remains consistently below 10 milliseconds per input sample. This efficiency, along with the model’s reliance on low-dimensional and physically meaningful features, supports its suitability for real-time deployment in embedded battery management systems where computational resources may be limited.

Conclusion

This study presents a comprehensive exploration of SOH prediction for LIBs across various contexts, leveraging RF, LSTM, and Bi-LSTM models. The research introduces innovative contributions, such as incorporating critical parameters (charge-discharge rate and plateau voltage), to enhance prediction accuracy by capturing dynamic battery aging processes. Bi-LSTM consistently demonstrated superior performance across single cells, single cells with additional parameters, and battery modules, achieving the highest predictive accuracy.

The findings underline Bi-LSTM’s ability to effectively model long-term dependencies and handle complex systems like battery modules, where performance is influenced by intercell interactions and configuration variations. Despite the reduced accuracy in battery module predictions due to increased complexity, all models showcased robust capabilities. This work not only highlights the critical role of enriched feature sets in enhancing model efficacy but also provides a solid foundation for future research aimed at improving battery health management, optimizing system design, and ensuring the safety and reliability of energy storage systems. While the Bi-LSTM model achieves notable improvements in predictive accuracy, it is also important to recognize the trade-offs associated with model complexity. Compared to simpler models such as Random Forest and unidirectional LSTM, Bi-LSTM requires longer training time and greater computational resources due to its bidirectional architecture. However, once trained, its inference efficiency remains acceptable for practical deployment, particularly when implemented on optimized hardware platforms. To ensure a balance between performance and efficiency, the model was designed with a modest number of layers and hidden units. As a result, the proposed approach remains computationally feasible for real-world applications while offering significant gains in accuracy. This balance between complexity and practicality reinforces the applicability of Bi-LSTM in intelligent battery health monitoring systems.

To address the performance drop observed in module-level SOH prediction, this study acknowledges the inherent challenges posed by cell heterogeneity and inter-cell imbalance within battery modules. Future work will explore mitigation strategies such as incorporating statistical descriptors (e.g., variance and skewness of cell-level parameters), integrating thermal and electrical imbalance indicators, and employing advanced modeling architectures including attention-based fusion and graph neural networks (GNNs). These techniques aim to enhance the model’s capacity to capture internal inconsistencies and dynamic interactions within battery modules, thereby improving the robustness and generalization of SOH predictions at the system level.

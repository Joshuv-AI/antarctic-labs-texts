A guide to topological reconstruction on endomorphism monoids and polymorphism clones
Paolo Marimon, Michael Pinsker

Abstract
Various spaces of symmetries of a structure are naturally endowed with both an algebraic and a topological structure. For example, the automorphism group of a structure is, on top of being a group, a topological group when equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence. In some cases, the algebraic structure of such space alone is sufficiently rich to determine its topology (under some requirements on the topology). For automorphism groups, the problem of when this happens has been actively pursued over the last 40 years. With the exception of some early work of Lascar, the analogue of this problem for endomorphism monoids and polymorphism clones has only received attention in the past 15 years. In this guide, we survey the current state of affairs in this relatively young line of research. We moreover use this opportunity to polish several existing results and to extend them beyond what was hitherto known.



Keywords:
endomorphism monoid, polymorphism clone, topological reconstruction,

Zariski topology, pointwise convergence topology, bi-interpretation,

-

1
Introduction

Given a structure
\mathbb{A}
with domain
\Omega
, we may associate with it various spaces of symmetries: its automorphism group
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
, its monoids of elementary self-embeddings
\EEmb(\mathbb{A})
, of self-embeddings
\Emb(\mathbb{A})
, and of endomorphisms
\End(\mathbb{A})
, and its polymorphism clone
\Pol(\mathbb{A})
(the space of homomorphisms from some finite power of
\mathbb{A}
into itself, see Section
2.1
).

Each space of symmetries of
\mathbb{A}
is naturally endowed with the topology of pointwise convergence
\tau_{\ppww}
. For the spaces which are subsets of
\Omega^{\Omega}
(which include all but the last space
above), this is just the subspace topology induced by the product topology on
\Omega^{\Omega}
, where
\Omega
is endowed with the discrete topology; in the case of
\Pol(\mathbb{A})
it is the topology induced in the sum space
\mathcal{O}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega^{\Omega^{n}}
of the spaces
\Omega^{\Omega^{n}}
which again bear the product topology. It is folklore that the subgroups of the full symmetric group
S_{\Omega}
on
\Omega
which are topologically closed in it correspond to automorphism groups of first-order structures with domain
\Omega
. Analogously, closed submonoids of the full transformation monoid
\Omega^{\Omega}
are precisely the endomorphism monoids of first-order structures, and closed subclones of the full clone
\mathcal{O}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega^{\Omega^{n}}
the polymorphism clones of first-order structures.

When
\Omega
is countable, as we will be assuming throughout this paper, closed submonoids of
\Omega^{\Omega}
are Polish topological semigroups. That is, they are separable and completely metrizable (i.e. Polish) and the semigroup operation is continuous. Similarly, closed subgroups of
S_{\Omega}
are Polish topological groups (that is, both group multiplication and inversion are continuous), and closed subclones of
\mathcal{O}
are Polish topological clones (that is, composition of functions is continuous).

The picture described above raises two natural questions:

Question 1.1
.

What information can we recover about the original structure
\mathbb{A}
from a given space of symmetries (thought of as a topological group / monoid / clone)?

Question 1.2
.

To what extent does the algebraic structure of a space of symmetries determine its topological structure?

Regarding Question
1.1
, we have an extremely satisfying answer when
\mathbb{A}
belongs to the class of
\omega
-categorical structures, which includes e.g.
(\mathbb{N};=)
,
(\mathbb{Q};<)
, the random graph, countable vector spaces over finite fields, and the countable atomless Boolean algebra
B_{\infty}
. In that case, its spaces of symmetries recover the original structure up to certain notions of bi-interpretation
[
1
,
11
,
15
]
. In particular,
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
and
\EEmb(\mathbb{A})
recover
\mathbb{A}
up to bi-interpretability,
\Pol(\mathbb{A})
recovers
\mathbb{A}
up to the stronger notion of primitive positive bi-interpretability, and
\End(\mathbb{A})
recovers it up to the intermediate notion of existential positive bi-interpretability (modulo some minor caveats, see Theorem

[

\omega

[

This fact makes Question

\omega
-categorical

\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
is already given by

\mathcal{S}
has
automatic homeomorphicity
(AH). Automatic homeomorphicity with respect to the class of Polish semigroups is known as the

(UPP) since it is equivalent to there being a unique Polish semigroup topology on
\mathcal{S}
. Finally, one might ask that all semigroup homomorphism from
\mathcal{S}
to a member
\mathcal{T}
of

are continuous. This property is known as
automatic continuity

For automorphism groups of
\omega
-categorical structures, these notions of reconstruction have been heavily studied in model theory, group theory, and topological dynamics. Reconstruction of

In spite of the growing body of available techniques, we are still far from any form of complete picture for topological reconstruction of endomorphism monoids or polymorphism clones. Throughout this guide, we include several open questions which we believe may help the field grow.

2.1
Spaces of symmetries and their topologies

Let
\Omega
be a countable set. We endow
\Omega^{\Omega}
with the topology of pointwise convergence
\tau_{\ppww}
given by the product topology on
\Omega^{\Omega}
, where
\Omega
is assigned the discrete topology. We write
\mathcal{O}^{(1)}:=\Omega^{\Omega}
for the
full transformation monoid
on
\Omega
, where the monoid operation is composition of functions. When we talk about the topology of pointwise convergence
\tau_{\ppww}
on any subspace of
\mathcal{O}^{(1)}
we mean the induced subspace topology. The monoid
\mathcal{O}^{(1)}
with the topology
\tau_{\ppww}
is a
topological semigroup
in the sense that the semigroup operation is continuous. Moreover,
\mathcal{O}^{(1)}
is Polish, i.e. separable and completely metrizable. Closed submonoids of
\mathcal{O}^{(1)}
are still topological semigroups with
\tau_{\ppww}
. The full symmetric group
S_{\Omega}
with
\tau_{\ppww}
is a Polish
topological group
(i.e. composition and inversion are continuous), and any of its subgroups which is closed in
S_{\Omega}
is a Polish topological group.

For
n\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{O}^{(n)}=\Omega^{{\Omega}^{n}}
be the set of functions
f:\Omega^{n}\to\Omega
, and
\mathcal{O}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{O}^{(n)}
. We call
\mathcal{O}
the
full clone
on
\Omega
. Analogously to the previous paragraph, we endow each
\mathcal{O}^{(n)}
with the product topology and endow
\mathcal{O}
with the sum topology.
This defines the topology of pointwise convergence
\tau_{\ppww}
on
\mathcal{O}
. Note that
\tau_{\ppww}
has a sub-basis of open sets
\{\mathcal{U}_{(\overline{a},b)}|\ \overline{a}\in\Omega^{n},b\in\Omega,n\geq 1\}
, where for
\overline{a}\in\Omega^{n}
,
b\in\Omega
,

\mathcal{U}_{(\overline{a},b)}:=\{f\in\mathcal{O}^{(n)}|\ f(\overline{a})=b\}\;.

Similarly,
\{\mathcal{U}_{(a,b)}|\ a,b\in\Omega\}
forms a subbasis of open sets of
\tau_{\ppww}
on
\mathcal{O}^{(1)}
. We often find it convenient to think of the notion of topological closure induced by
\tau_{\ppww}
: given
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
and
f\in\mathcal{O}
, we say that
f\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}
if for all finite
C\subseteq\Omega
there is some
g\in\mathcal{F}
such that
g_{\upharpoonright C}=f_{\upharpoonright C}
. Topological closure on
\mathcal{O}^{(1)}
is identical.

Definition 2.1
.

We call
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
a
function clone
over
B
if

•

\mathcal{C}
contains all projections:
for each
1\leq i\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{C}
contains the
k

i
th coordinate
\pi_{i}^{k}\in\mathcal{O}^{(k)}
, given by
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\mapsto x_{i}
;

•

\mathcal{C}
is closed under composition: for all
f\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{O}^{(n)}
and all
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{O}^{(m)}
,
f(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
, given by

(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\mapsto f(g_{1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}),\dots,g_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})),

is in
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{O}^{(m)}
.

We say that a function clone is
closed
if it is closed in
\tau_{\ppww}
on
\mathcal{O}
. Given a set of functions
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
, we denote by
\langle{\mathcal{S}}\rangle
the smallest closed function clone containing
\mathcal{S}
. A closed function clone

together with
\tau_{\ppww}
is a (Polish)

in the sense that each
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{O}^{(n)}
is clopen and each operation
\mathrm{comp}^{n}_{m}:\mathcal{O}^{(n)}\times(\mathcal{O}^{(m)})^{n}\to\mathcal{O}^{(m)}
given by
(f,g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\mapsto f(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
is continuous.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be function clones. A
clone homomorphism

\xi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is a map which preserves arities of functions, is constant on the projections
\{\pi_{i}^{k}|1\leq i\leq k\}
and preserves composition of functions.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
be first-order structures in the same relational language. A map
f:\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{B}
is a
homomorphism
if it sends tuples in a relation of
\mathbb{A}
to tuples in the corresponding relation of
\mathbb{B}
; it is an

if it is moreover injective and sends only tuples in a relation of
\mathbb{A}
to tuples in the corresponding relation of
\mathbb{B}
; and it is an

if it preserves the truth of arbitrary first-order formulas.
We write
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
,
\Emb(\mathbb{A})
,
\EEmb(\mathbb{A})
, and
\End(\mathbb{A})
for, respectively, the automorphism group, the monoid of self-embeddings, the monoid of elementary self-embeddings, and the endomorphism monoid of
\mathbb{A}
.
By a

of
\mathbb{A}
we mean a map
\mathbb{A}^{n}\to\mathbb{A}
for some

:

\text{ if }\begin{pmatrix}a^{1}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
a^{1}_{k}\end{pmatrix},\dots,\begin{pmatrix}a^{n}_{1}\\
\vdots\\

\vdots\\

Note that unary polymorphisms correspond to endomorphisms and that the polymorphisms of a relational structure form a closed subclone of

. We write

for the polymorphism clone of

.

The following correspondences are easy to prove (allowing relational structures to be in an infinite language):

•

correspond to automorphism groups of relational structures on

;

•

\mathcal{O}^{(1)}
correspond to endomorphism monoids of relational structures on
\Omega
;

•

\Omega
.

2.2
\omega
-categorical structures and related notions

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a countable set (we allow
\Omega
to be finite). We say that a permutation group
G\curvearrowright\Omega
is
oligomorphic
if
G\curvearrowright\Omega^{n}
has finitely many orbits for each

in its natural action

for
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\Omega^{n}
. A structure
\mathbb{A}
is
\bm{\omega}
-categorical
if its automorphism group is oligomorphic.

With our definition, all finite structures are
\omega
-categorical. A large class of countably infinite
\omega
-categorical structures is given by homogeneous structures in a finite relational language (
finitely homogeneous
structures):

Definition 2.5
.

A countably infinite structure
\mathbb{A}
in a relational language is
homogeneous
if every isomorphism between finite substructures extends to an automorphism of
\mathbb{A}
.

Homogeneous
structures are in one-to-one correspondence with certain well-behaved classes of finite relational structures known as Fraïssé classes. Given a Fraïssé class
\mathcal{K}
, the homogeneous structure whose
age
(i.e. class of finite substructures) is
\mathcal{K}
is known as its
Fraïssé limit
. We refer the reader to
[
40
]
for a survey on
\omega
-categorical and homogeneous structures. We usually name homogeneous structure after their age (for which they are the
generic
countable object). Thus, the generic
K_{n}
-free graph
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is the countable homogeneous graph whose age is the class of finite graphs which do not embed a copy of the complete graph
K_{n}
, the generic poset is the Fraïssé limit of finite partially ordered sets, and so on.

Spaces of symmetries of
\omega
-categorical structures have natural characterisations in terms of their actions: closed oligomorphic subgroups of
S_{\Omega}
correspond to the automorphism groups of
\omega
-categorical structures; closed transformation monoids on
\Omega^{\Omega}
are called oligomorphic
if they contain an oligomorphic permutation group, and correspond to endomorphism monoids of
\omega
-categorical structures. Moreover, monoids of elementary embeddings of
\omega
-categorical structures correspond precisely to closed oligomorphic transformation monoids with a dense set of invertibles. Similarly, polymorphism clones of
\omega
-categorical structures correspond to closed oligomorphic clones, i.e., closed clones whose unary operations are an oligomorphic transformation monoid.

In some sections, we will be working with countable
saturated
structures.
These are countable structures
\mathbb{A}
such that for each of their finite subsets
B
,
\mathbb{A}
realises all
1
-types over
B
. In particular,
\omega
-categorical structures are saturated.
In countable saturated structures we have that
\EEmb(\mathbb{A})=\overline{\Aut(\mathbb{A})}
.

The latter fact provides one motivation for reconstructing the topology on the class of closed submonoids
\mathcal{S}
of
\Omega^{\Omega}
whose subgroup
G
of invertibles is dense. Further, monoids of this class are an essential intermediate step when lifting reconstruction results from automorphism groups to endomorphism monoids. Finally, if
G
is even oligomorphic, such monoids are called
model-complete cores
, and they appear as an essential concept in various applications e.g. in Ramsey theory or Constraint Satisfaction (see
[
42
,
4
,
16
,
17
]
). The name of model-complete core was originally given to
\omega
-categorical structures whose endomorphism monoid has a dense set of invertibles; it was shown in
[
16
]
that every
\omega
-categorical structure
\mathbb{A}
has a substructure
\mathbb{C}
which is a model-complete core and such that
\mathbb{A}
has a homomorphism into
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathbb{C}
is
\omega
-categorical and unique up to isomorphism
[
16
]
. Closed clones whose monoid of unary functions are a model-complete core are called the same.

Given a group
G\curvearrowright\Omega
and
B\subseteq\Omega
finite, the
algebraic closure
of

, is the set of all elements of
\Omega
which have finite orbit under the action

of the pointwise stabiliser of

G\curvearrowright\Omega

if the algebraic closure of any finite set is finite; this is the case for all oligomorphic actions. Moreover,
G\curvearrowright\Omega
has
no algebraicity
if for all finite
B\subseteq\Omega

\acl(B)=B
. It is easy to see that this implies that the center

of

Some remarkable results of
[

\bm{G}
-finite
: for every open subgroup
U\leq G
, the intersection of all closed subgroups of finite index in
U
still has finite index in
U
.
As far as we know, there are no known examples of finitely homogeneous structures which are not
G
-finite. However, allowing for an infinite language it is easy to build such structures
[
37
]
:

Definition 2.6
.

By the Cherlin-Hrushovski structure
\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{CH}}
we mean the homogeneous structure obtained as the Fraïssé limit of finite structures with, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a
2n
-ary equivalence relation
E_{n}
on
n
-tuples, such that each
E_{n}
has at most
two equivalence classes.

The Cherlin-Hrushovski structure also does not have the small index property
[
39
]
.

2.3
Interpretations

One motivation for reconstructing the topology of various spaces of symmetries of an
\omega
-categorical structure is that one can then further reconstruct the structure itself up to various notions of interpretation.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
be first-order structures, respectively in the signatures
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. A
first-order interpretation (fo-interpretation)
of
\mathbb{B}
in
\mathbb{A}
is a partial surjection
I:A^{d}\to B
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each relation
R
of
\mathbb{B}
defined by an atomic
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
-formula
\phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, the relation

I^{-1}(R):=\\
\{(a_{1}^{1},\dots,a_{1}^{d},\dots,a_{n}^{1},\dots,a_{n}^{d})\in A^{nd}\ |\ \mathbb{B}\vDash\phi(I(a_{1}^{1},\dots,a_{1}^{d}),\dots,I(a_{n}^{1},\dots,a_{n}^{d}))\}

is first-order definable in
\mathbb{A}
. We say that
d
is the
dimension
of the interpretation
I
. Below, we are mainly interested in stronger notions of interpretations. In particular, we say that
I
is an

•

existential interpretation
(e-interpretation) if each
I^{-1}(R)
is definable in
\mathbb{A}
as an existential formula;

•

existential positive interpretation
(ep-interpretation) if each
I^{-1}(R)
is existentially positively definable in
\mathbb{A}
;

•

primitive positive interpretation
(pp-interpretation) if each
I^{-1}(R)
is primitively positively definable in
\mathbb{A}
.

It is easy to see that any interpretation
I
of
\mathbb{B}
in
\mathbb{A}
naturally gives rise to a continuous group homomorphism
\Aut(I):\Aut(\mathbb{A})\to\Aut(\mathbb{B})
. Moreover, for
\omega
-categorical
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
, a continuous group homomorphism
f:\Aut(\mathbb{A})\to\Aut(\mathbb{B})
is of the form
\Aut(I)
for some interpretation
I
if and only if its range acts oligomorphically on
B

[
1
]
. Analogous statements hold for e-interpretations with respect to embedding monoids
[
11
, Theorem 3.2]
, for ep-interpretations with respect to endomorphism monoids
[
11
, Theorem 3.6]
, and for pp-interpretations with respect to polymorphism clones
[
15
, Proposition 22]
. Whilst this already motivates understanding when homomorphism between oligomorphic spaces of symmetries are continuous, our main interest in this survey lies in topological isomorphisms. We will see below these encode bi-interpretations of varying degrees of strength.

Note that interpretations compose naturally. In particular, if
I
is an (fo/e/ep/pp)-interpretation of
\mathbb{B}
in
\mathbb{A}
and
J
is an (fo/e/ep/pp)-interpretation if
\mathbb{A}
in
\mathbb{B}
, we say that
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
are (fo/e/ep/pp)-
bi-interpretable
if the induced partial functions
I\circ J
and
J\circ I
are (fo/e/ep/pp)-definable in
\mathbb{B}
and
\mathbb{A}
respectively. See e.g.
[
1
,
11
,
15
]
for details.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
be
\omega
-categorical structures of size
>1
. Then:

1.

\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
are bi-interpretable if and only if
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
and
\Aut(\mathbb{B})
are isomorphic as topological groups (Coquand, Corollary 1.4 in
[
1
]
).

2.

If
\Emb(\mathbb{A})
and
\Emb(\mathbb{B})
are isomorphic as topological monoids, then
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
are existentially bi-interpretable
[
11
, Theorem 3.14]
. The converse does not hold
[
11
, Example 4.4]
.

3.

If
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
are existentially positively bi-interpretable, then
\End(\mathbb{A})
and
\End(\mathbb{B})
are isomorphic as topological monoids. The converse holds as long as neither of
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
have a constant endomorphism (by
[
11
, Theorem 3.12]
).

4.

\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
are primitively positively bi-interpretable if and only if
\Pol(\mathbb{A})
and
\Pol(\mathbb{B})
are isomorphic as topological clones
[
15
]
.

As mentioned, (
3
) does not hold in full generality: let
\mathbb{A}
be an
\omega
-categorical structure with a constant endomorphism. Let
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
be the structure obtained by adding a new element
c
to
\mathbb{A}
as well as unary predicates for
\{c\}
and its complement in
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
(which is
\mathbb{A}
). Then
\End(\mathbb{A})
and
\End(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
are topologically isomorphic. However, any structure existentially positively interpretable in
\mathbb{A}
also has a constant endomorphism
[
11
, Lemma 3.9]
, so
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
cannot be bi-interpretable. Nevertheless, one can get a fully general correspondence looking at clones and using
[
15
]
:

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
be
\omega
-categorical structures.
Then,
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
are existentially positively bi-interpretable if and only if
\langle{\End(\mathbb{A})}\rangle
and
\langle{\End(\mathbb{B})}\rangle
are isomorphic as topological clones.

Proof.

One direction is a direct consequence of (
3
). For the other, suppose that the two clones are isomorphic. These clones are the polymorphism clones of the structures
\mathbb{A}^{\prime},\mathbb{B}^{\prime}
obtained by enriching
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
by the 4-ary relation defined by
x=y\vee u=v
. By (
4
),
\mathbb{A}^{\prime},\mathbb{B}^{\prime}
are pp-bi-interpretable, and hence
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
are ep-bi-interpretable. The reason constant endomorphisms do not pose a problem in this setting is that they are detected by the clone structure: an endomorphism
f
is constant if and only if
f\circ\pi_{1}^{2}=f\circ\pi_{2}^{2}
for the two binary projections
\pi^{2}_{1},\pi^{2}_{2}
.
∎

Studying monoids of elementary embeddings is also relevant to the problem of reconstructing structures up to first-order bi-interpretability. The following folklore result is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
2.8
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
be
\omega
-categorical structures of size
>1
. Then,
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
are first-order bi-interpretable if and only if
\EEmb(\mathbb{A})
and
\EEmb(\mathbb{B})
are isomorphic as topological monoids.

While this characterization of first-order bi-interpretability might seem less natural than the one with automorphism groups, it may actually be easier to verify than the latter.
For example, by Theorem
5.6

[
48
]
, if two
\omega
-categorical structures with no algebraicity have isomorphic endomorphism monoids of elementary embeddings, then this isomorphism will be a topological one. No analogue of this result is known for automorphism groups.

2.4
Notions of reconstruction

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a topological semigroup and
\mathcal{C}
be a class of topological semigroups. We say that

1.

\mathcal{S}
is
reconstructible
with respect to

if whenever there is a semigroup isomorphism from

to a member

of
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
;

2.

\mathcal{S}
has

with respect to

if every semigroup isomorphism between
\mathcal{S}
and a member
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{C}
is a homeomorphism;

3.

\mathcal{S}
has
automatic continuity
with respect to
\mathcal{C}
if every semigroup homomorphism from
\mathcal{S}
to a member
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{C}
is continuous.

Analogous definitions exist for topological groups and clones and the corresponding notions of homomorphism.

Definition 2.12
.

We define several classes of topological groups, semigroups and clones

depending on the parameters

and

indicates whether the class we are looking at is a class of topological groups (
\mathcal{G}
), topological monoids with a dense group of invertible elements (
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
), semigroups (
\mathcal{T}
), or clones
(\mathcal{C})
. Meanwhile, we use
Y\in\{2,\mathrm{cl},\mathrm{sat},\mathrm{ol}\}

to indicate whether we are looking at the class of (in the order of decreasing generality):

1.

second countable topological groups, semigroups, clones, which we denote, respectively, by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{2}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{2}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{2}}
.

2.

closed subgroups of

, closed submonoids of

with a dense set of invertibles, closed submonoids of

3.

the classes in item

Another established notion of reconstruction is when a semigroup (group, clone) has a unique Polish compatible topology. This is known as the
Unique Polish Property
(UPP). Note that UPP is equivalent to automatic homeomorphicity with respect to the class of Polish topological semigroups (groups, clones).

3
Reconstruction

The following is implicit in

(Proposition 11 in

.

Let

Proposition

Theorem 3.2

(
[

]
)
.

Let

Let

\overline{G}
has reconstruction with respect to

The result above also yields transfer of reconstruction from

with respect to

Question 3.4
.

Let

.

•

(reconstruction lifting) Does

having reconstruction with respect to

has reconstruction with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}

(reconstruction descent) Does

is such that its subgroup of invertible elements is dense. For the second question, an issue is that algebraic isomorphisms between groups may not lift to algebraic isomorphisms of their closures. Both questions are open even if

It is important to keep in mind throughout this survey that we do have a counterexample to reconstruction at all levels of symmetries of an

There are

•

the monoids

and
\overline{H}

4
Automatic homeomorphicity

4.1
Automatic homeomorphicity for monoids of elementary embeddings

In this subsection, we prove that for any countable saturated structure, automatic homeomorphicity lifts from its automorphism group (with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
) to its monoid of elementary embeddings (with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
). This is an original result, completing the answer to a problem which is central to several papers on topological reconstruction for monoids
[
13
,
7
,
55
,
47
]
. In particular,
[
47
]
answer this problem positively in the context of saturated structures whose automorphism group has a trivial centre. Uncovering the key idea behind their proof is the essential tool to answer this question in full generality.

We begin from the following key lemma for reconstruction:

Lemma 4.1

(
[
13
, Lemma 12]
)
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
have automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. Suppose that the only injective
\Phi\in\End(\overline{G})
that fixes
G
pointwise is the identity. Then
\overline{G}
has automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
.

The majority of this section is dedicated to proving the assumption on
\End(\overline{G})
is always satisfied when
G
is the automorphism group of a saturated structure.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. We call a subset
S\subseteq\Omega

free
if the orbits of tuples under the action
G_{\{S\}}:=\{\alpha\in G\;|\;\alpha(S)=S\}\curvearrowright S
are precisely the restrictions of orbits of
G\curvearrowright\Omega
to
S
. We say that
S
is
Galois-closed
if the pointwise stabilizer
G_{S}
of
S
in
G
acts without fixed points on
\Omega\setminus S
. Finally,
f\in\overline{G}
is free Galois-closed if its image is.

We remark that this notion is a translation of the notion of superhomogeneity used in
[
47
]
from structures to permutation groups. We shall now apply some results of that paper which are formulated there in terms of structures. For any countable saturated structure
\mathbb{A}
, there is a structure
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
, which is saturated, homogeneous (in a countable language), and such that
\Aut(\mathbb{A})=\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
(see, e.g.,
[
47
]
), allowing us to speak in terms of
G:=\Aut(\mathbb{A})
. When
G
is oligomorphic, this is known as the
canonical structure
for
G
. In this case
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
is obtained by adding a relation for every orbit of tuples under
G
.

Proposition 4.3

(
[
47
]
)
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
and let
\Phi\in\End(\overline{G})
fix
G
pointwise. Then for all free Galois-closed
h\in\overline{G}
there exists
g_{h}\in Z(G)
, such that
\Phi(h)=h\circ g_{h}
.

Proof.

For
f\in\overline{G}
, let

f^{*}:=\{(\alpha,\beta)\ |\;\alpha,\beta\in G,\ \alpha\circ f=f\circ\beta\}\;.

Clearly, for any
h\in\overline{G}
we have
\Phi(h)^{*}=h^{*}
.
By Corollary 3.12 and Proposition 3.13 of
[
47
]
this implies that there is some
g_{h}\in Z(G)
such that
\Phi(h)=h\circ g_{h}
.
∎

Our key observation is that the main results of
[
47
]
rely on a correspondence between maps in
\End(\overline{G})
which fix
G
pointwise and homomorphisms
\phi:\overline{G}\to Z(G)
mapping
G
to
1
:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{sat}}
and let
\Phi\in\End(\overline{G})
fix
G
pointwise. Then, there is a semigroup homomorphism
\phi:\overline{G}\to Z(G)
such that
\phi(G)=\{1\}
and for each
f\in\overline{G}

\Phi(f)=\phi(f)\circ f.

Proof.

For any
f\in\overline{G}
,
by
[
47
, Proposition 5.1]
, we can take free Galois-closed
g,h\in\overline{G}
such that
g=h\circ f
. By Proposition
4.3
, there are
z_{g},z_{h}\in Z(G)
such that
\Phi(g)=g\circ z_{g}
and
\Phi(h)=h\circ z_{h}
. Now, since
\Phi
is a homomorphism,

h\circ f\circ z_{g}=g\circ z_{g}=\Phi(g)=\Phi(h\circ f)=\Phi(h)\Phi(f)=h\circ z_{h}\circ\Phi(f).

Since
h
is injective and
z_{h},z_{g}\in Z(\overline{G})
(being in
Z(G)
), we get

\Phi(f)=z_{h}^{-1}\circ z_{g}\circ f\;.

In particular, we can set
\phi(f)=z_{h}^{-1}\circ z_{g}
. Since
\Phi
fixes
G
pointwise, we must have that
\phi(G)=1
. We now claim that
\phi:\overline{G}\to Z(G)
is a homomorphism. To see this, note that for
f,g\in\overline{G}
, since
\phi(f),\phi(g)\in Z(G)
,

(f\circ g)\circ(\phi(f)\circ\phi(g))=(\phi(f)\circ f)\circ(\phi(g)\circ g)=\Phi(f\circ g)=(f\circ g)\circ(\phi(f\circ g))\;,

which by injectivity of
f\circ g
implies
\phi(f\circ g)=\phi(f)\circ\phi(g)
as required.∎

The proof of the following lemma will use some basic model-theoretic techniques.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{sat}}
. Let
h\in\overline{G}
. Then there are
f\in\overline{G}
and
\alpha,\beta\in G
such that

\alpha\circ f\circ h=f\circ\beta.

Proof.

Let
h\in\overline{G}
. Let
\mathbb{A}
be a saturated structure with automorphism group
G
.
Write
\mathbb{A}_{1}
for
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{A}_{0}
for
h(\mathbb{A}_{1})
. Since
h
is an elementary self-embedding, its inverse is an isomorphism
\alpha_{0}:\mathbb{A}_{0}\to\mathbb{A}_{1}
. We first construct an elementary extension
\mathbb{A}_{2}\succeq\mathbb{A}_{1}
together with an isomorphism
\alpha_{1}:\mathbb{A}_{1}\to\mathbb{A}_{2}
extending
\alpha_{0}
. To do so, take an enumeration of
\mathbb{A}_{1}
. Consider the type
p(z;A_{0}):=\mathrm{tp}(\mathbb{A}_{1}/\mathbb{A}_{0})
in a variable
z
of length
\omega
. Let
\mathbb{A}_{2}
be a realisation of
p(x;\alpha_{0}(\mathbb{A}_{0}))
and define
\alpha_{1}
to be the map sending every element of
\mathbb{A}_{1}
to its realisation in
\mathbb{A}_{2}
. This map is an isomorphism and extends
\alpha_{0}
. By repeating this process, we get an elementary chain

\mathbb{A}_{0}\preceq\mathbb{A}_{1}\preceq\dots\preceq\mathbb{A}_{i}\preceq\mathbb{A}_{i+1}\preceq\dots

where, at each stage
i\geq 1
we have an isomorphism
\alpha_{i}:\mathbb{A}_{i}\to\mathbb{A}_{i+1}
extending
\alpha_{i-1}
. Write
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
for the union of this elementary chain and
\alpha
for
\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{i}
. By construction,
\alpha
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
. Since it is a union of a countable chain of countable saturated models,
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
is still a countable saturated model of the first-order theory of
\mathbb{A}
. In particular, there is an isomorphism
f:\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{A}_{1}
(cf.
[
53
, Lemma 5.2.8]
). Define
h^{\prime}:\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
as
f^{-1}hf
. Note that, for
a,b\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
,

h^{\prime}(a)=b\Leftrightarrow f^{-1}hf(a)=b\Leftrightarrow hf(a)=f(b)\;.

Hence, since
f
is an isomorphism between
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{A}
, it yields an isomorphism between the structures
(\mathbb{A}^{\prime};h^{\prime})\cong(\mathbb{A};h)
(i.e., the expansions of
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{A}
by, respectively,
h^{\prime}
and
h
). In particular, to prove our lemma, it is sufficient to prove its conclusion for
(\mathbb{A}^{\prime};h^{\prime})
rather than for
(\mathbb{A},h)
. Now,
fh^{\prime}=hf
yields an isomorphism
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{A}_{0}
. Moreover, recall that
\alpha
is an isomorphism
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that
\alpha(\mathbb{A}_{0})=\alpha_{0}(\mathbb{A}_{0})=\mathbb{A}_{1}
. Thus, since
fh^{\prime}
,
\alpha
, and
f^{-1}
are all isomorphisms,
f^{-1}\alpha fh^{\prime}:=\beta\in\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
.
Hence,

\alpha\circ f\circ h^{\prime}=f\circ\beta,

as desired.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{sat}}
, and let
H
be any group. Then, any homomorphism
\phi:\overline{G}\to H
such that
\phi(G)=\{1\}
is constant.

Proof.

Let
h\in\overline{G}
. By Lemma
4.5
there are
f\in\overline{G}
and
\alpha,\beta\in G
such that

\alpha\circ f\circ h=f\circ\beta.

Since
\phi
is a homomorphism,

\phi(\alpha)\circ\phi(f)\circ\phi(h)=\phi(f)\circ\phi(\beta).

Since
\phi(\alpha)=\phi(\beta)=1
and
\phi(f)\in H
, this implies that
\phi(h)=1
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let

Proof.

4.2
Automatic homeomorphicity for endomorphism monoids

Surprisingly, automatic homeomorphicity may fail for the endomorphism monoids of structures whose automorphism groups have automatic homeomorphicity even in very tame cases. The following is an adaptation of
[
13
, Theorem 8]
. Our main contribution is that the structure in the counterexample below is extremely tame. Firstly, it is model-theoretically tame being
\omega
-stable and a reduct of a finitely homogeneous structure. These structures are very well understood
[
22
]
. Secondly, being
\omega
-categorical and
\omega
-stable, its automorphism group has ample generics
[
30
]
, and so our example is also tame from the point of view of reconstruction of its automorphism group.

Theorem 4.9
.

There is an
\omega
-categorical structure
\mathbb{A}
which is
\omega
-stable and first-order definable from a finitely homogeneous structure such that
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
has automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{2}}
, but
\End(\mathbb{A})
(and hence also the clone
\langle\End(\mathbb{A})\rangle
) has a non-continuous automorphism. In particular,
\End(\mathbb{A})
(and
\langle\End(\mathbb{A})\rangle
) do not have automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{ol}}
(and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{ol}}
).

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{B}
be the structure with an equivalence relation
E
with infinitely many classes of size 2. Let
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
consist of two disjoint copies
\mathbb{B}_{1},\mathbb{B}_{2}
of
\mathbb{B}
plus an additional new element
\star
which is (only) related to itself in
E
, and which has additional unary predicates
B_{1},B_{2}
for the domains of
\mathbb{B}_{1},\mathbb{B}_{2}
. It is easy to see that
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
is finitely homogeneous (hence,
\omega
-categorical) and
\omega
-stable. In particular, it has ample generics by
[
30
]
and so
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
has automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{2}}
. Let

\mathcal{F}:=\{f:A^{\prime}\to A^{\prime}|\ f(B_{1})\subseteq B_{2}\cup\{\star\},\ f(B_{2}\cup\{\star\})=\{\star\},\;f\text{ preserves }E\}.

Consider the closed monoid
\mathcal{T}:=\overline{\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})}\cup\mathcal{F}
; its invertibles are precisely the elements of
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
consist of those elements of
\mathcal{F}
which miss an infinite number of
E
-classes in
B_{2}
. Let
\alpha\in\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
flip the two elements of any
E
-class (and fix
\star
).
We define the map
\eta:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
by

\eta(f):=\begin{cases}f&f\notin\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\;;\\
\alpha f&f\in\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\;.\end{cases}

Using that
\alpha
commutes with all elements of
\overline{\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{\prime})}
, it is easy to see that
\eta
is an automorphism of
\mathcal{T}
. However, taking a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
outside of
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
converging to a non-constant element
g
in
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
,
(\eta(f_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
g
but
\eta(g)=\alpha g\neq g
. Hence
\eta
is not continuous.
∎

In spite of the above negative result, there are still reasonable situations where one may hope
to lift automatic homeomorphicity results from
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
to
\End(\mathbb{A})
. In particular,
[
7
,
55
]
developed a sophisticated technique which they use to show automatic homeomorphicity for the endomorphism monoids of
(\mathbb{Q},\leq)
and some structures first-order definable in it with different automorphism groups. They make use of the small index property for
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
and some additional information on the structure of its open subgroups, which may be provided by some (possibly weak) form of elimination of imaginaries.

Their main idea is as follows: consider an isomorphism
\theta:\End(\mathbb{A})\to\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. This can be viewed as a faithful monoid action of
\End(\mathbb{A})
on
\Omega
, the domain of
\mathcal{S}
; in the following discussion, to distinguish this action from the original action of
\End(\mathbb{A})
, we write
A
for the domain of the original action. The aim is to directly show that
\theta
is a homeomorphism by studying how the induced action of
G:=\Aut(\mathbb{A})
on
\Omega
can extend to
\theta
. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem, for any
x\in\Omega
there is a bijection between the orbit
X:=\mathrm{Orb}(x)
of
x
under
G\curvearrowright\Omega
and
the left-cosets in
G
of the stabilizer
G_{x}=\{g\in G\;|\;\theta(g)(x)=x\}
. Since
X
is countable,
G_{x}
has countable index. Thus, if
G
has the SIP we know that
G_{x}
is open. Since the open subgroups of
G
are precisely those containing the pointwise stabilizer
G_{B}
of a finite set
B
under the action
G\curvearrowright A
, we can conclude that
G_{x}\supseteq G_{B}
for some finite
B
. With additional information on the open subgroups of
G
, however, we can draw stronger conclusions.

We say that
G
has
elimination of imaginaries
if every open subgroup of
G
is equal to
G_{B}
for some finite
B
, and that it has
weak elimination of imaginaries
if every open subgroup of
G
is sandwiched between
G_{B}
and the setwise stabiliser
G_{\{B\}}
for some finite
B
. Several
\omega
-categorical structures have weak elimination of imaginaries, e.g.,
(\mathbb{N},=)
, the random graph, and infinite vector spaces over finite fields. It is easy to prove that if
G
has weak elimination of imaginaries, then for any open subgroup
H\leq G
there is a finite algebraically closed
B\subseteq A
and
L\leq G_{\{B\}}\curvearrowright B
such that

H=G_{(B,L)}:=\{f\in G|f_{\upharpoonright B}\in L\}\;.

Groups with the SIP and weak elimination of imaginaries are said to have the
strong small index property
(SSIP)
[
21
]
.

Let us now return to analysing an isomorphism
\theta:\End(\mathbb{A})\to\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
assuming that
G
has the SSIP. In that situation, for any
x\in\Omega
we have
G_{x}=G_{(B,L)}
as above. In particular, we can identify the orbit
X=\mathrm{Orb}(x)
with the orbit of the pair
(B,L)
under the natural action of
G
where for
g\in G
,
g(B,L)=(gB,gL)
; here,
gL
is a subgroup of
G_{\{gB\}}\curvearrowright gB
. Doing this for all
G
-orbits, we can rewrite the action of
G
on
\Omega
as the action of
G
on a disjoint union of
G
-orbits of pairs
(B_{i},L_{i})_{i\in I}
, where
I
is a countable set. Using Theorem
4.7
and the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 4.2]
, we can then also describe the action of
\overline{G}
on
\Omega
: for any
f\in\overline{G}
and any
(gB_{i},gL_{i})
, where
g\in G
and
i\in I
, we obtain
f(gB_{i},gL_{i})=(hgB_{i},hgL_{i})
where
h\in G
is an arbitrary element which agrees with
f
on
gB_{i}
.

For the final step, which requires the most work, one needs to describe how
\End(\mathbb{A})
acts on the orbits of the action
G\curvearrowright\Omega
induced by
\theta
and deduce that this action yields that
\theta
is a homeomorphism. Whilst this cannot always be done successfully (as demonstrated by Theorem
4.9
), it already yielded substantial results for various structures with a first-order definition in
(\mathbb{Q},\leq)
(see
[
20
]
; the relations in the statement below are all taken to be reflexive):

Theorem 4.10

(
[
7
,
55
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be any of:
(\mathbb{Q},\leq)
,
(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{sep})
,
(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{betw})
,
(\mathbb{Q},\mathrm{circ})
. Then,
\End(\mathbb{A})
has automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. Moreover,
\Pol(\mathbb{A})
has automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
.

5
On the number of Polish topologies

In this subsection, we study Polish topologies on closed oligomorphic monoids. Whilst all monoids in
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{ol}}
have at least two Polish semigroup topologies, several interesting monoids in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{ol}}
have the Unique Polish Property.

5.1
Multiple Polish topologies on monoids of elementary embeddings

Lemma 5.1

(
[
25
, Proposition 5.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
be a monoid of injective functions whose group of invertible elements is not closed in
\Omega^{\Omega}
. Then, there are at least two distinct Polish semigroup topologies on
\mathcal{S}
. In particular, this is the case for any
\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{ol}}
.

Proof idea.

Consider the topology
\mathcal{I}_{4}
on the monoid of injective functions
\mathrm{Inj}(\Omega)
generated by
\tau_{\ppww}
and the open sets
(\mathcal{W}_{a})_{a\in\Omega}
of the form

\mathcal{W}_{a}:=\{f\in\mathrm{Inj}(\Omega)\ |\ a\not\in\mathrm{Im}(f)\}\;.

This is a Polish topology on
\mathrm{Inj}(\Omega)

[
26
, Theorem 5.15 & Section 5.5]
. It is easy to see that the induced topology
\tau_{c}
on
\mathcal{S}
is a Polish semigroup topology in which the group of invertibles is closed, yielding that
\tau_{c}\neq\tau_{\ppww}
.
∎

Similar ideas were used by
[
52
]
to prove, for every
n
, failure of UPP for the endomorphism monoids of the generic graph without independent sets of size
n
and of the complete multipartite graph with
\omega
-many blocks of size
n
. Whilst not all endomorphisms of these structures are injective, there is some
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any endomorphism, the preimage of any point has size
\leq m
, and this is sufficient to build a similar topology to
\tau_{c}
which is distinct from
\tau_{\ppww}
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\Omega
have locally finite algebraic closure. We say that
\acl
forms a
pregeometry
if it satisfies the exchange property: for all
a,b\in\Omega
and
C\subseteq\Omega
finite, if
a\in\acl(C\cup\{b\})\setminus\acl(C)
, then
b\in\acl(C\cup\{a\})
.

When
\acl
forms a pregeometry, we can adequately define the
dimension

\dim(B)
of a finite set

as the smallest
n
such that there is
C\subseteq\Omega
with
|C|=n
and
\acl(C)=\acl(B)

[
57
]
(we assign dimension
\aleph_{0}
to infinite subsets of
\Omega
). Note that
\acl
forms a pregeometry in any oligomorphic permutation group with no algebraicity, or in the automorphism group of any vector space over a finite field.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\Omega
be such that
\acl
forms a pregeometry. Then, there are at least countably many Polish semigroup topologies on
\overline{G}
.

Proof.

This is essentially the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 5.22 (i) & (vi)]
.
Note that for any
f\in\overline{G}
, the image
\mathrm{Im}(f)
is algebraically closed. Let
\mathcal{S}_{0}:=\tau_{c}
from the proof of Lemma
5.1
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let

F_{n}:=\{f\in\overline{G}\ \;|\;\dim(\Omega\setminus\mathrm{Im}(f))=n\}\;.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\mathcal{S}_{n}
to be the topology generated by
\mathcal{S}_{n-1}
and
F_{n-1}
.

Claim 1:
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is a semigroup topology.

Proof of Claim 1.

We need to prove that composition is continuous.
Note that for all
f,g\in\overline{G}
,

\dim(\Omega\setminus\mathrm{Im}(fg))=\dim(\Omega\setminus\mathrm{Im}(f))+\dim(\Omega\setminus\mathrm{Im}(g))\;.

In particular, for any
l\leq n
, choosing
m,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m+k=l
, we get that
F_{m}\cdot F_{k}\subseteq F_{l}
.
This yields continuity as desired.
∎

Claim 2:
For all
l<n
the topologies
\mathcal{S}_{l}
and
\mathcal{S}_{n}
are distinct.

Proof of Claim 2.

Firstly, using the fact that
\acl
is a pregeometry (and Neumann’s Lemma
[
31
, Corollary 4.2.2]
), it is easy to see that the sets
F_{l}
are non-empty for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
f\in F_{n}
and let
\mathcal{B}
be an open neighbourhood of
f
in
\mathcal{S}_{n}
; it suffices to show that
\mathcal{B}\neq F_{n}
. Since
f
does not belong to any
F_{m}
for any
m<n
, we must have that
\mathcal{B}\in S_{0}
. Hence, there exist a basic open neighbourhood
{\mathcal{U}_{(\overline{a},\overline{b})}\in\tau_{\ppww}}
and some set

\mathcal{W}_{\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\}}=\{f\in\overline{G}\ |\ \forall i\;c_{i}\not\in\mathrm{Im}(f)\}

such that
f\in{\mathcal{U}_{(\overline{a},\overline{b})}}\cap\mathcal{W}_{\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
. By a back & forth argument, using Neumann’s Lemma and the fact that algebraic closure forms a pregeometry, it is easy to construct some
g\in\overline{G}
such that
g\in\mathcal{B}
but
g\not\in F_{n}
by building
g
so that it agrees with
f
on
\overline{a}
and so that its image avoids an algebraically closed subset of
\Omega
of dimension
>n
containing
\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\}
. This implies that
F_{n}\neq\mathcal{B}
, as we wanted to show.∎

Claim 3:
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the topology
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is Polish.

Proof of Claim 3.

By
[
35
, Lemma 13.2]
it is sufficient to prove that for each
n
,
F_{n}
is closed in
\overline{G}
. Note that

\overline{G}\setminus F_{n}:=\{f\in\overline{G}|\dim(\Omega\setminus\mathrm{Im}(f))>n\}\cup\{f\in\overline{G}|\dim(\Omega\setminus\mathrm{Im}(f))<n\}\;.

The first of the two sets in this union is open in
\mathcal{S}_{0}
and the second is just
\bigcup_{i<n}F_{i}
. Hence this set is open in
\mathcal{S}_{n}
, as desired. ∎

∎

It is natural to wonder whether the technique of Lemma
5.3
can be extended to all monoids in
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{ol}}
. We point out that there are
\omega
-categorical structures for which every non-surjective elementary embedding has co-infinite image, meaning that one cannot define different topologies by open sets of the form of the sets

. In particular, using a classical construction of Hrushovski
[

]

one can build an
\omega
-categorical graph such that every vertex has infinite valency and such that for any two vertices and any path of length two connecting them, the vertex in the middle of the path belongs to their algebraic closure (cf.
[

). From this,
it is easy to prove that any of its non-surjective elementary embedding has co-infinite image. Thus, we ask the following:

Question 5.4
.

Does every
\overline{G}\in\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{ol}}
have infinitely many Polish semigroup topologies?

5.2
Minimality of
\tau_{\ppww}
and the Zariski topology

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a monoid. The

topology
\tau_{Z}

[
26
]

\mathcal{S}

M_{\phi,\psi}:=\{s\in\mathcal{S}\ |\phi(s)\neq\psi(s)\}\;,

where

s\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\mapsto}}t_{k}st_{k-1}s\dots t_{1}st_{0}\text{ and }s\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\mapsto}}q_{l}sq_{k-1}s\dots q_{1}sq_{0}\;,

and
t_{0},\dots,t_{k},q_{0},\dots q_{l}\in\mathcal{S}

When
\mathcal{S}
is
a group, a potentially finer topology can be defined by allowing inverses of

to appear in the definitions of
\phi
and
\psi

group Zariski topology

Contrary to the pointwise convergence topology
\tau_{\ppww}

\omega
-categorical structure
\mathbb{A}
has a

if any of its elements is contained in the image of an endomorphism into a substructure which is a model-complete core. Trivially, this is satisfied if
\mathbb{A}
is itself a model-complete core.

(
[

]
)

Let
\mathbb{A}
be an

on

•

\mathbb{A}
is finite;

•

In
[

asking about the existence of an

to send

\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{ol}}

Meanwhile, vastly generalizing the example from

, there is a strong amalgam, i.e.
C\in\mathcal{K}
and embeddings
g_{i}:B_{i}\to C
for
i\in\{1,2\}
such that
g_{1}\circ f_{1}=g_{2}\circ f_{2}
and
g_{1}(B_{1})\cap g_{2}(B_{2})=g_{1}f_{1}(A)
, with with the property that whenever
h
is a map from a subset of
C
to some
D\in\mathcal{K}
containing
g_{1}\circ f_{1}(A)
in its domain and
h\circ g_{1}
and
h\circ g_{2}
are homomorphisms, then
h
is also a homomorphism.

We remark that the SAPHG implies that the amalgam

]

Theorem 5.14

(
[

]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a homogeneous and H-homogeneous structure whose age has SAPHG. Then,
\End(\mathbb{A})
equipped with
\tau_{\ppww}
has Property
\mathrm{X}
with respect to
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
.

Example 5.15
.

The assumptions of Theorem

apply to the following structures:

1.

the random graph
\mathcal{R}
;

2.

3.

\omega K_{n}
of countably many copies of the complete graph

any of the structures above including loops;

;

All structures above except the generic strict (or non-strict) poset and

are such that their automorphism groups are known to have ample generics, and so automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{2}}

[

]
. With the exception of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{CH}}
these examples are already mentioned in

[

also show that the endomorphism monoid of the countable atomless Boolean algebra

has Property
\mathrm{X}
with respect to its automorphism group, and the latter is known to have automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{2}}
.

The following proposition can be applied to deduce that the examples above whose automorphism group has automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{2}}
are such that their endomorphism monoids have automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{2}}
:

Proposition 5.16

(
[
26
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{2}}
. Then,
G
has automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{2}}
if and only if it has automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{2}}
.

While Property
\mathrm{X}
is a powerful tool for showing maximality of
\tau_{\ppww}
and lifting of automatic continuity from subgroups of a monoid,
[
49
]
note that there is some leeway in Definition
5.10
: the decomposition of
s\in\mathcal{S}
could be of arbitrary length without changing the truth of Theorem
5.11
; they call this generalization Property
\overline{\mathrm{X}}
. Similarly, in the same definition
s
can be composed from the left with a left-invertible element of
\mathcal{S}
, leading to
pseudo-Property
\bf\overline{X}
. They apply this notion to
\End(\mathbb{Q},\leq)
equipped with
\tau_{\mathrm{rich}}
, a finer topology than
\tau_{\ppww}
, and the subgroup
\Aut(\mathbb{Q},\leq)
equipped with
\tau_{\ppww}
to conclude that any Polish semigroup topology on
\End(\mathbb{Q},\leq)
must be contained in
\tau_{\mathrm{rich}}
. By a series of further arguments, of which some can be recast as statements about pseudo-Property
\overline{\mathrm{X}}
, and others involve different techniques such as Baire category, they obtain:

Theorem 5.17

(
[
49
]
)
.

\End(\mathbb{Q},\leq)
has the UPP.

[
49
]
note that most of the results on the UPP in Example

do not use the full strength of Polishness: they have unique second countable metrisable semigroup topologies. This occurs whenever the UPP can be shown applying the second item of Theorem
5.11
. The considerably more complex proof of Theorem
5.17
, which only partially fits into this framework, additionally requires the space to be Baire.

For the endomorphism monoids of some well-studied structures the status of the UPP is still open; among homogeneous graphs, the only one for which whether the UPP holds is not known is the following (see
[
52
]
):

Question 5.18
.

Does the endomorphism monoid of the complete multipartite graph
\mathbb{K}_{\omega,\omega}
(with
\omega

\omega
) in the language of graphs have UPP?

Property X can also be applied in the context of clones. Given a topological clone
\mathcal{C}
with a topology
\tau
, we can associate with it a topological semigroup
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}
with domain
\mathcal{C}
, topology
\tau
, and the operation
\ast
given by
f\ast g=f(g,\dots,g)
. Whilst the algebraic structure of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}
is weaker than that of
\mathcal{C}
in the sense that not every semigroup homomorphism with respect to
\ast
is a clone homomorphism, it is often sufficiently rich to restrict the possible clone topologies on
\mathcal{C}
:

Lemma 5.19

(
[
26
, Lemma 7.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a topological clone and let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}
have automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{2}}
. Then,
\mathcal{C}
has automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{2}}
.

In
[
26
]
, the above lemma and Property X are used to show that
\tau_{\ppww}
is the unique second countable Hausdorff clone topology on the polymorphism clone of the countable atomless Boolean algebra. In
[
25
, Theorem 6.2]
, it is shown that homogeneous, H-homogeneous structures
\mathbb{A}
with the SAPHG and whose age is closed under finite non-empty direct products are such that
\mathcal{S}_{\Pol(\mathbb{A})}
has Property X with respect to
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
. This is then applied to prove UPP and automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{2}}
for various structures, such as the random graph and
\omega K_{\omega}
with loops. We invite the reader to compare this result also to
[
46
, Theorem 4.1]
. In the latter theorem, the authors introduce the
amalgamated extension property
(AEP)
[
46
, Definition 3.23]
, a weaker amalgamation property than the SAPHG. They show that homogeneous, H-homogeneous structures
\mathbb{A}
with the AEP whose age is closed under finite non-empty direct products, and whose endomorphisms contain all constant functions, are such that
\Pol(\mathbb{A})
has automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. The latter result is based on “gate covering” techniques of
[
13
]
, but, unlike the original paper, does not rely on upwards transfers of automatic homeomorphicity from the underlying automorphism group.

5.4
More on automatic continuity and open mappings

Preceding the positive instances in Example
5.15
, automatic continuity for semigroups was believed to be a less fruitful notion than for groups due to the following result:

Theorem 5.20

(
[
13
, Corollary 10]
)
.

No
\overline{G}\in\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{ol}}
has automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
.

The small index property for groups can be meaningfully extended to semigroups. A
right congruence
on a semigroup
\mathcal{S}
is an equivalence relation
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}
such that whenever
(g,f)\in\mathcal{E}
and
h\in\mathcal{S}
, then
(hg,hf)\in\mathcal{E}
. We define a left-congruence analogously but with respect to composition from the other side.

Definition 5.21
.

A topological semigroup
\mathcal{S}
has the
right small index property
(rSIP) if every right congruence with countably many classes is open in
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}
. We define analogously the
left small index property
(lSIP).

Proposition 5.22

(
[
26
, Proposition 4.3]
)
.

A topological monoid
\mathcal{S}
has the rSIP if and only if it has automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
.

Proposition 5.23

(
[
26
, Corollary 4.5]
)
.

If a topological monoid
\mathcal{S}
has automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{2}}
, then it has both the rSIP and the lSIP.

Automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
and with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{2}}
are distinct notions for Polish semigroups, and even for closed oligomorphic transformation monoids
[
26
, Proposition 5.18]
. Moreover, contrasting the Open Mapping Theorem for Polish groups, automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
does not imply automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
for Polish semigroups (cf.
[
26
, Proposition 5.15]
); however, we do not know an example of this phenomenon within the class of endomorphism monoids.

Question 5.24
.

Is there
\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
with automatic continuity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
but without automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
? If so, is there an oligomorphic example?

Regarding the openness of clone homomorphisms, we have both a lifting result from semigroups as well as a result specific to clones.
The former implies that for isomorphisms from a transitive clone
\mathcal{C}
, it is sufficient to check openness on its unary functions.

Theorem 5.25

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. Let
\xi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a clone homomorphism and let
\xi^{\prime}
be its restriction to unary functions.

•

Suppose
\mathcal{D}
is weakly directed. If
\xi^{\prime}
is continuous, then so is
\xi
;

•

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is weakly directed and
\xi
is an isomorphism. If
\xi^{\prime}
is open, so is
\xi
.

The following trick for clones is not available for monoids, since homomorphisms of the latter do not necessarily preserve constant functions:

Proposition 5.26

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
contain all constant functions. Then, any isomorphism
\xi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is open.

6
Rubin’s dream: stronger notions of reconstruction

The group
S_{\Omega}
acts continuously on
\Omega^{\Omega}
by conjugation:
(\theta,f)\mapsto f^{\theta}:=\theta f\theta^{-1}
for
\theta\in S_{\Omega}
and
f\in\Omega^{\Omega}
. Hence, for any closed subsemigroup
\mathcal{S}
of
\Omega^{\Omega}
and any
\theta\in S_{\Omega}
, we obtain a new closed subsemigroup
\mathcal{S}^{\theta}:=\{f^{\theta}\;|\;f\in\mathcal{S}\}
which is topologically isomorphic to it by fixing
\theta
in the above map. We call such homeomorphisms
set-induced
. This idea leads to a stronger notion of reconstruction:

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. We say that
\mathcal{S}
has
automatic action compatibility
with respect to
\mathcal{C}
if any algebraic isomorphism from
\mathcal{S}
onto an element of
\mathcal{C}
is set-induced.

Again, the analogous notion can be defined also in the context of permutation groups and clones. In the latter case, the action on
\Omega^{\Omega^{n}}
is

(\theta,f)\mapsto f^{\theta}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=\theta f(\theta^{-1}(x_{1}),\ldots,\theta^{-1}(x_{n})).

These notions have a structural interpretation: we call structures
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
on
\Omega

(fo/e/ep/pp)-bi-definable
if they are (fo/e/ep/pp)-bi-interpretable with
d=1
and a bijection
I\colon\Omega\to\Omega
(using the notation from Definition
2.7
). In the situation where they are ep-bi-definable, for
\theta:=I
we have
\End(\mathbb{B})=\End(\mathbb{A})^{\theta}
. Analogous statements hold for the other notions of definability. Moreover, for all of these notions the converse (i.e. the existence of a permutation
\theta
moving one space of symmetries onto the other by conjugation) holds in the oligomorphic case. This follows from the theorem of Ryll-Nardzewski, Engeler, and Svenonius for automorphism groups, is due to
[
12
]
for pp-bi-definability, and is folklore for the other cases.

For the above form of reconstruction to hold, we need to exclude algebraicity: for any oligomorphic permutation group
G
we can construct a topologically isomorphic oligomorphic
G^{\prime}
whose action is not isomorphic to that of
G
by adding a fixed point. Thus, the interesting classes are
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{na}},\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{na}},\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{na}},
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{na}}
: spaces of symmetries of
\omega
-categorical structures without algebraicity. In this context, we already have the following consequence of Theorem
5.6
regarding automatic homeomorphicity:

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
\overline{G}\in\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{na}}
. Then,
\overline{G}
has automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{na}}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.6
,
\tau_{\ZZ}=\tau_{\ppww}
for any structure in
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{na}}
, yielding that any isomorphism between two structures in the class is also topological.
∎

However, much more is true. Namely, it is a result of
[
51
]
that any fo-bi-interpretation of
\mathbb{B}
in
\mathbb{A}
, where
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
are
\omega
-categorical structures without algebraicity,
gives rise to a bi-definition: this is essentially Lemma 2.11 in
[
51
]
. In some of the literature on reconstruction one gets the impression that this fact has been overlooked, perhaps due to the complex mathematical language used in Rubin’s paper and the belief that the statement holds only in the presence of a weak
\forall\exists
-interpretation (the main topic of Rubin’s article). Be that as it may, the same statement holds for all stronger (i.e. e/ep/pp) notions of bi-interpretation by analysing Rubin’s proof, and a new proof has been announced in
[
28
]
:

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{na}}
.
Then any topological isomorphism from
\mathcal{S}
onto
\mathcal{T}
is set-induced. Similar statements hold in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{na}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{na}}
.

Theorem
6.3
has, in the case of groups, been exploited to show that the equivalence relation of topological isomorphism on the class
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{na}}
of closed oligomorphic groups without algebraicity is
smooth

[
43
]
. By
[
28
]
, the same is true for monoids (i.e. the class
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{na}}
) and clones (i.e. the class
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{na}}
). Both works contain generalisations beyond the context of no algebraicity.
As a consequence of Theorem
6.3
, automatic homeomorphicity and automatic action compatibility coincide in the context of
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{na}}
. Together with Corollary
6.2
this yields:

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\overline{G}\in\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{na}}
. Then,
\overline{G}
has automatic action compatibility with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\overline{\mathcal{G}}}^{\mathrm{na}}
.

Some specific cases of Corollary
6.4
were proven in
[
8
]
. The following proposition allows to lift being set-induced from semigroup homomorphisms to clone homomorphisms.

Proposition 6.5

(
[
8
, Theorem 4.3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
with
\mathcal{C}
being weakly directed. Let
\xi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a surjective clone homomorphism and let
\xi^{\prime}
be its restriction to unary functions. If
\xi^{\prime}
is set-induced, then so is
\xi
.

We can thus extend Corollary
6.4
to transitive clones.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{na}}
be model-complete core clones. If
\mathcal{C}
is transitive, then any clone isomorphism
\xi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is set-induced.

7
Final remarks on clones

In this final section, we summarise some of the literature on reconstruction for polymorphism clones mentioned throughout this survey and list some final questions on the topic. Current techniques for clones usually rely on lifting reconstruction properties from the underlying group or monoid. Whilst this was originally the case also for the study of reconstruction for monoids, now we have several powerful direct techniques. We hope that the study of clones will develop in a similar direction.
The original “gate covering” techniques of
[
13
]
relied on lifting to prove automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
of the Horn clone, the polymorphism clone of the random graph, and all minimal closed clones above the automorphism group of the random graph. Meanwhile,
[
7
,
55
]
use Proposition
5.26
and other lifting tricks to deduce automatic continuity of the polymorphism clones of
(\mathbb{Q},\leq)
and the other structures mentioned in Theorem
4.10
. Although Property
\mathrm{X}
for
\mathcal{S}_{\Pol(\mathbb{A})}
can be used to deduce UPP and automatic continuity for
\Pol(\mathbb{A})

[
26
,
25
]
, current proofs of UPP do rely on lifting minimality of
\tau_{\ppww}
using Lemma
5.9
, and automatic continuity still relies on lifting from
\Aut(\mathbb{A})
. The only existing technique that seems to not rely on lifting is
[
46
, Theorem 4.1]
, which
utilises gate coverings to show automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
for the polymorphism clones of various homogeneous structures, such as the generic (non-strict) poset.

One challenge
we face is that current techniques often rely on specific structural properties such as particular forms of amalgamation. However, most structures do not enjoy these properties themselves, although in many cases they are first-order definable, or interpretable, in another structure that does. For example, amongst the continuum of closed clones whose underlying permutation group is
S_{\Omega}
(presented in
[
9
]
), we only know automatic homeomorphicity for those containing the full monoid
\mathcal{O}^{(1)}
, for the Horn clone
[
13
]
, and for
\langle{S_{\Omega}}\rangle

[
7
]
.

Question 7.1
.

Does the polymorphism clone of every structure which is first-order definable in
(\mathbb{N},=)
have automatic homeomorphicity with respect to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cl}}
? In general, are there conditions which guarantee that automatic homeomorphicity of
\End(\mathbb{A})
(or
\Pol(\mathbb{A})
) for a structure
\mathbb{A}
imply the same for the structures first-order definable or interpretable in it?

Note that Theorem
4.9
shows that some non-trivial conditions must be imposed. We are not aware of any model-complete core clone without automatic homemorphicity whose monoid of unary functions has automatic homeomorphicity.

Of particular interest, especially in applications to Constraint Satisfaction
[
18
,
50
]
, is when an
\omega
-categorical structure
\mathbb{A}
pp-interprets all finite structures. This is the case if and only if there is a continuous clone homomorphism from
\Pol(\mathbb{A})
to the clone
\mathcal{P}_{\{0,1\}}
of projections on a two-element set. Naturally, one then wishes to know whether all such homomorphisms must be continuous, or whether at least the existence of a homomorphism implies the existence of a continuous one. A structure first-order definable in the Cherlin-Hrushovski structure provides a counterexample to the former statement
[
14
]
. However, the proof requires the ultrafilter lemma, and the structure is only homogenisable in an infinite language.
The truth of the latter statement is unknown, cf.
[
14
, Questions 7.3 & 7.4]
.

Question 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{ol}}
have a clone homomorphism to
\mathcal{P}_{\{0,1\}}
. Does it then always also have a continuous such homomorphism? Can the existence of a discontinuous homomorphism from some
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{ol}}
to
\mathcal{P}_{\{0,1\}}
be proven in ZF? Can it be proven for the polymorphism clone of a structure which is first-order definable in a finitely homogeneous structure?

A positive answer to the first question is made plausible by the result from
[
5
, Theorem 1.6]
that for an
\omega
-categorical model complete core,
\Pol(\mathbb{A},c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
has a continuous homomorphism to
\mathcal{P}_{\{0,1\}}
for some constants
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
if and only if
\Pol(\mathbb{A},d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
has a homomorphism to
\mathcal{P}_{\{0,1\}}
for some constants
d_{1},\dots,d_{m}
.

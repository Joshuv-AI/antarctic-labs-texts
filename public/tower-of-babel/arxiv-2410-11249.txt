Construction of Quasi-periodic solutions with the same Gevrey index as nonlinear terms in Multi-Dimensional NLS
Zuhong You, Xiaoping Yuan

Abstract
We investigate the persistency of quasi-periodic solutions to multi-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equations (NLS) involving Gevrey smooth nonlinearity with an arbitrary Gevrey index α > 1 \alpha>1 . By applying the Craig-Wayne-Bourgain (CWB) method, we establish the existence of quasi-periodic solutions that are Gevrey smooth with the same Gevrey index as the nonlinearity.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the persistency of quasi-periodic solutions of the multi-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS):

\frac{1}{i}q_{t}(\tilde{x},t)=-\Delta q(\tilde{x},t)+V(\tilde{x})*q(\tilde{x},t)+\varepsilon\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})

(1.1)

under periodic boundary conditions (i.e.,
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
). The convolution potential
V:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
has real Fourier coefficients
\hat{V}(n)
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The Hamiltonian
H(q,\bar{q})=f(q\bar{q})
, where
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, belongs to the Gevrey class. In other words, we are intended to prove a Kolmogorov-Arnold-Moser (KAM) theorem for equation (
1.1
).

When
d=1
, the KAM theory of Hamiltonian PDEs is well established; see, for instance,
[
Kuk87
]
,
[
KP96
]
,
[
Way90
]
,
[
Kuk93
]
,
[
P9̈6
]
and
[
CW93
]
. This equation is intrinsically interesting, when the spatial dimension
d\geq 2
. However, the study of KAM theory for Hamiltonian PDEs with
d\geq 2
remains in its early stages. For
d=2
,
[
Bou98
]

developed a new method initialed by
[
CW93
]
to obtain the Gevrey smooth quasi-periodic solutions of NLS with analytic nonlinearity. His method relies on Newton iteration, Fröhlich-Spencer techniques (i.e., multi-scale analysis), and the Malgrange preparation theorem. Later,
[
Bou05
]
introduced harmonic analysis and semi-algebraic set theory to refine the techniques in
[
Bou98
]
, proving the persistency of quasi-periodic solutions of NLS and NLW with analytic nonlinearity for arbitrary
d
, and he claimed the obtained solutions are analytic. However, he only gave a sketch of the proof, and there is a key coupling lemma seems unlikely to hold. We believe the sketch he described there can only obtain the Gevrey smooth solutions. This method is now referred to as the Craig-Wayne-Bourgain (CWB) method.

For the case of finitely differentiable nonlinearity, Berti, Bolle, Corsi, and Procesi among others,
(
[
BB10
]
,
[
BBP10
]
,
[
BP11
]
,
[
BB12
]
,
[
BB13
]
,
[
BCP15
]
, and
[
BB20
]
, for example), improved the Nash-Moser iterative scheme and developed a novel multi-scale inductive analysis.
They established the existence of finitely differentiable periodic or quasi-periodic solutions for NLS and NLW with finitely differentiable nonlinearity for arbitrary spatial dimension
d
.

In this paper, we aim to show that for (
1.1
) with Gevrey nonlinearity, most quasi-periodic solutions persist and
the persisted solutions have the same Gevrey index as the nonlinearity
. To state our results, we begin with the definition of a Gevrey-
(\alpha,L)
function.
Let
\alpha>1,L>0
, and let
K
be a compact set of the form
K^{\prime}\times\mathbb{T}^{n_{2}}
, where
K^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
is compact, and
n_{1}+n_{2}\geq 1
. We define

G^{\alpha,L}(K)=\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(K):\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\left(\frac{L^{|k|_{1}}}{k!}\right)^{\alpha}\left\lVert\partial^{k}\varphi\right\rVert_{C^{0}(K)}<\infty\},

(1.2)

where
|k|_{1}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}|k_{j}|
,
k!=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}k_{j}!
, and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{C^{0}(K)}
denotes the uniform norm for continuous functions defined on
K
.
A function
\varphi\in G^{\alpha,L}(K)
is called a Gevrey-
(\alpha,L)
function. We refer to
\alpha
as the Gevrey index and
L
as the Gevrey width of
\varphi
.
Furthermore, we define

G^{\alpha}(K)=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{L>0}G^{\alpha,L}(K),

(1.3)

and functions in
G^{\alpha}(K)
are referred as Gevrey-
\alpha
functions.
We assume
f,f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in G^{\alpha,L_{0}}([-1,1])
for some
\alpha>1
and
L_{0}>0
.

One says
\tilde{q}(\tilde{x},t)
is a quasi-periodic function in time
t
if there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{b}
and
q(\tilde{x},\theta)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d+b})\,
(here
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{b}
) such that

\tilde{q}(\tilde{x},t)=q(\tilde{x},\lambda t).

We say that
\lambda
is the frequency and
q(\tilde{x},\theta)
is the hull of
\tilde{q}(\tilde{x},t)
. See
[
KP96
]
for the definitions of quasi-periodic function and its hull, for example.

Select finite Fourier modes
0\in\{n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{b}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Denote

\mathcal{R}=\{(n_{j},e_{j}):1\leq j\leq b\}
, where
e_{j}
is the
j
-th unit vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{b}
. Let
\textup{supp }\hat{V}=\{n_{1},...,n_{b}\}
. Then, the spectral of the operator
\mathcal{L}:=-\Delta+V(\tilde{x})*
are

\begin{cases}\mu_{n_{j}}=\lambda_{j},\ \ &(1\leq j\leq b),\\
\mu_{n}=\left\lvert n\right\rvert^{2}\ \ &n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{n_{1},...,n_{b}\},\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
|n|^{2}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}|n_{j}|^{2}
and
\lambda:=(\lambda_{j}=|n_{j}|^{2}+\hat{V}(n_{j}):1\leq j\leq b)
.
Given
a_{1},...,a_{b}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\tilde{q}_{0}(\tilde{x},t)=q_{0}(\tilde{x},\lambda t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{b}a_{j}e^{i\lambda_{j}t+in_{j}\cdot\tilde{x}}

(1.5)

yields a solution to the linear equation

\frac{1}{i}q_{t}(\tilde{x},t)=-\Delta q(\tilde{x},t)+V(\tilde{x})*q(\tilde{x},t).

(1.6)

This solution is quasi-periodic and corresponds to a flow on a
b
-torus

\mathbb{T}^{b}=\mathcal{T}_{0}\{|a_{j}|\}=\left\{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{b}z_{j}e^{in_{j}\cdot\tilde{x}}:z_{j}\in\mathbb{C},|z_{j}|=a_{j}\right\}.

The problem we study is the “persistency” of solutions (
1.5
) for the Gevrey perturbed equation (
1.1
). The term “persistency” in this paper is defined as follows:

There exists a quasi-periodic solution

\tilde{q}(\tilde{x},t)=q(\tilde{x},\lambda^{\prime}t)

to (
1.1
), such that

|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda|<C\varepsilon,

and

\lVert q-q_{0}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}<C\varepsilon,

for some
L^{\prime}>0
.
Here,
C
is a constant depending only on
b,d,L_{0},\alpha
and the bound of
\lambda
.

Note that the Gevrey index
\alpha>1
of the solutions here is the same as the Gevrey index of nonlinearity, and
\alpha>1
is arbitrary. In previous papers, the persisted solutions only have Gevrey index
\alpha\gg 1
, even if the nonlinearity is polynomial.

In KAM theory, the vector
(\hat{V}(n_{j}):1\leq j\leq b)
is typically considered as a parameter vector. This is equivalent to regarding
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{b})
in the same way. Thus, we take
\lambda\in I_{0}=[0,1]^{b}
as parameters.

Here is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For arbitrary
\alpha>1
,
let
H(q,\bar{q})=f(q\bar{q})
,
f,f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in G^{\alpha,L_{0}}([-1,1])

(L_{0}>0)
. Fix
a_{1},...,a_{b}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and let
I_{0}=[0,1]^{b}
. For
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, there exists a Cantor set
I_{0,\varepsilon}\subset I_{0}
such that
mes(I_{0}\setminus I_{0,\varepsilon})\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. For
\lambda\in I_{0,\varepsilon}
, there exists a quasi-periodic solution to (
1.1
) with frequency
\lambda^{\prime}
(depending on
\lambda
), whose hull
q(\tilde{x},\theta)
is in
G^{\alpha,L_{0}/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d+b})
.
Moreover, we have

\hat{q}(n_{j},e_{j})=a_{j},\ \ 1\leq j\leq b,

(1.7)

|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda|\leq C\varepsilon,

(1.8)

and

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+b}\\
(n,k)\notin\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}|\hat{q}(n,k)|e^{\frac{\alpha L_{0}}{2}|(n,k)|_{\alpha}}\leq C\varepsilon,

(1.9)

which implies

\lVert q-q_{0}\rVert_{\alpha,\frac{L_{0}}{2}}<C\varepsilon,

(1.10)

where
\hat{q}(n,k)
is the
(n,k)
-Fourier coefficient of
q(\tilde{x},\theta)
,
q_{0}(\tilde{x},\theta)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{b}a_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}+in_{j}\cdot\tilde{x}}
, and
C
is a constant depending on
\alpha,L_{0},I_{0},b
and
d
only.

Here, the term
|(n,k)|_{\alpha}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}|n_{j}|^{1/\alpha}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}|k_{j}|^{1/\alpha}
, and the weight function
e^{\alpha L^{\prime}|(n,k)|_{\alpha}}
corresponds to Gevrey-
(\alpha,L^{\prime})
regularity.

The following are some key highlights that we would like to emphasize:

In this paper, we propose a new approach based on the Craig-Wayne-Bourgain (CWB) method to handle Gevrey smooth nonlinear perturbations.
Unlike earlier works, which
approximated the Gevrey Hamiltonian by
analytic ones (see
[
Pop04
]
for example), we directly employ the Gevrey norm introduced in
[
MS02
]
(i.e., (
1.2
)).
We find that the Gevrey norm possesses an almost equivalent norm described by Fourier coefficients, allowing us to use it similarly to the sup-norm of real analytic functions in most cases
.

To apply the semi-algebraic techniques, we explored the relationship between the rate of polynomial approximation of Gevrey functions in Gevrey norm and the domain, and
developed an effective strategy for the polynomial approximation of Gevrey funtions
.

During the Newton iteration, we need to estimate the Gevrey norm of the approximate solution
q(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
.
We apply the Markov Inequality to obtain the Gevrey norm estimate of
q(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
only from the sup-norm of
T_{r-1}^{-1}(\eta,\eta^{\prime})
and
F(q_{r})
on a real domain
.

In addition, as we have mentioned in the beginning, Bourgain only provided an outline of the proof of the persistency of quasi-periodic solutions for NLS and NLW using the CWB method in Chapters 19 and 20 of
[
Bou05
]
. There is a key coupling lemma in his argument, but he did not give a proof for this coupling lemma. The coupling lemma he claimed seems unlikely to hold,
as noted by
[
Wan16
]
, “This is because the sizes of the resonant clusters are in general, much larger than their separation.”

In our paper, we exploit the strict concavity of
g(s)=s^{1/\alpha}
to improve Lemma 7 of
[
Bou98
]
and obtain our Lemma 6.1. In Lemma 7 of
[
Bou98
]
, the loss of the decay rate is
\frac{1}{2}L
, while the loss of the decay rate in Lemma
6.5
can be arbitrarily small. This improvement helps us to get solutions with arbitrary Gevrey index
\alpha>1
.

Moreover, when applying the CWB method, the reality of the drifted frequency
\lambda^{\prime}
is straightforward when the nonlinearity is polynomial or real analytic. However, for Gevrey smooth nonlinearity, the reality of the drifted frequency
\lambda^{\prime}
seems not to be obvious. We provide a proposition in Appendix A to clarify this issue.

2
Gevrey functions and their properties

In this section, we provide the definition of Gevrey functions and some technical lemmas.
In the Introduction, we have introduced the definition of a Gevrey-
(\alpha,L)
function for
L>0
.
Here, we extend the definition by defining the Gevrey norm with different Gevrey widths. Let
\bm{L}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and let
K
be a compact set in
n
-dimensional space. For
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(K)
, the Gevrey norm is defined as follows:

\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\left(\frac{\bm{L}^{k}}{k!}\right)^{\alpha}\left\lVert\partial^{k}\varphi\right\rVert_{C^{0}(K)}.

(2.1)

If
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}<\infty
, we say that
\varphi
is Gevrey-
(\alpha,\bm{L})
on
K
, denoted by
\varphi\in G^{\alpha,\bm{L}}(K)
. The vector
\bm{L}
is referred to as the Gevrey width of
\varphi
. We also have

G^{\alpha}(K)=\bigcup\limits_{\bm{L}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}G^{\alpha,\bm{L}}(K).

In this paper, we frequently employ two distinct Gevrey widths. For clarity, we restate the definition in this context.

Let
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
. Let
K_{1}
be a compact interval in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
(corresponding to parameters) and
K_{2}=\mathbb{T}^{n_{2}}
. Let
K=K_{1}\times K_{2}
and
L_{1},L_{2}>0
. For a function
\varphi(\lambda,\theta)\in C^{\infty}(K)
, where
\lambda\in K_{1}
and
\theta\in K_{2}
, the Gevrey norm is defined as

\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}=\sum\limits_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{n_{1}},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{n_{2}}}\left(\frac{L_{1}^{|k_{1}|_{1}}L_{2}^{|k_{2}|_{1}}}{k_{1}!k_{2}!}\right)^{\alpha}\left\lVert\partial_{\lambda}^{k_{1}}\partial_{\theta}^{k_{2}}\varphi\right\rVert_{C^{0}(K)}.

(2.2)

If
\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}<\infty
, we say that
\varphi(\lambda,\theta)
is Gevrey-
(\alpha,L_{1},L_{2})
on
K
, denoted by
\varphi\in G^{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}(K)
. The parameters
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are referred to the Gevrey widths with respect to
\lambda
and
\theta
, respectively.

Proposition 2.1

(
Product
)
.

Let
\bm{L}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and
\varphi,\psi\in G^{\alpha,\bm{L}}(K)
, then we have

\left\lVert\varphi\psi\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}\leq\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}.

In particular, for
L_{1},L_{2}>0
and
\varphi,\psi\in G^{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}(K)
, we have

\left\lVert\varphi\psi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}\leq\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}.

This proposition shows that
G^{\alpha,\bm{L}}
is a Banach algebra.

Proposition 2.2

(
Derivative
)
.

Suppose
0\leq\bm{h}<\bm{L}
and
\varphi\in G^{\alpha,\bm{L}}(K)
. Then all partial derivatives of
\varphi
belong to
G^{\alpha,\bm{L}-\bm{h}}(K)
, and we have

\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\frac{\bm{h}^{m\alpha}}{m!^{\alpha}}\left\lVert\partial^{m}\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L-h}}\leq\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}.

In particular, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,

\left\lVert\partial^{m}\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L-h}}\leq\left(\frac{m!}{\bm{h}^{m}}\right)^{\alpha}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}.

This result is often referred to as the “Cauchy estimate” for Gevrey functions.

Proposition 2.3

(
Composition
)
.

Let
L>0
,
\bm{L}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}
, and let
K
be an n-dimensional compact set, and
K_{1}
be an m-dimensional compact set as previously defined. Let
Y\in G^{\alpha,L}(K)
, and let
u=(u_{1},...,u_{n})
be a mapping whose components belong to
G^{\alpha,\bm{L}}(K_{1})
. If
u(K_{1})\subset K
and

\left\lVert u_{j}\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}-\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{C^{0}(K_{1})}\leq\frac{L^{\alpha}}{m^{\alpha-1}},\ \ 1\leq j\leq n,

(2.3)

then
Y\circ u\in G^{\alpha,\bm{L}}(K_{1})
and
\left\lVert Y\circ u\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}\leq\left\lVert Y\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}
.

The proofs of Proposition
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
follow the same approaches as in
[
MS02
]
, except for the allowance of different Gevrey widths in this context. For detailed proofs, we refer to
[
MS02
]
.

We now discuss the decay of Fourier coefficients of Gevrey functions.
Let
\alpha>1
and
K=\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Lemma 4.8 in
[
DG17
]
shows that if
f\in G^{\alpha,L}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, then
|\hat{f}(k)|\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}e^{-L|k|_{1}^{1/\alpha}}
, where
C
is a constant independent of
\alpha
and
L
. However, with a minor modification to the proof, we can deduce a more intrinsic description:

Proposition 2.4

(
Decay of Fourier coefficients
)
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
L>0
. If
f\in G^{\alpha,L}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, then for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have

|\hat{f}(k)|\leq\left(\frac{h(\varepsilon,\alpha)}{2\pi}\right)^{d}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}e^{-\alpha(1-\varepsilon)L|k|_{\alpha}},

(2.4)

where
h(\varepsilon,\alpha)=\left(\frac{1}{1-(1-\varepsilon)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}}\right)^{\alpha-1}
.

Proof.

Since for any
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we have
|(\partial^{\beta}f)^{\land}(k)|\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\left\lVert\partial^{\beta}f\right\rVert_{C^{0}(K)}
, it follows that

(2\pi)^{d}\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{L^{|\beta|_{1}\alpha}}{\beta!^{\alpha}}|(\partial^{\beta}f)^{\land}(k)|\leq\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{L^{|\beta|_{1}\alpha}}{\beta!^{\alpha}}\left\lVert\partial^{\beta}f\right\rVert_{C^{0}(K)}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}.

(2.5)

Thus, since
(\partial^{\beta}f)^{\land}(k)=(ik)^{\beta}\hat{f}(k)
, we obtain

(2\pi)^{d}|\hat{f}(k)|\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{L^{|\beta|_{1}\alpha}}{\beta!^{\alpha}}k^{\beta}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}.

Therefore, it suffices to estimate

\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{L^{|\beta|_{1}\alpha}}{\beta!^{\alpha}}k^{\beta}.

Notice that

\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{L^{|\beta|_{1}\alpha}}{\beta!^{\alpha}}k^{\beta}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{L^{n\alpha}}{n!^{\alpha}}|k_{j}|^{n}.

We now consider

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{L^{n\alpha}}{n!^{\alpha}}|k_{j}|^{n}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(L|k_{j}|^{1/\alpha})^{n}}{n!}\right)^{\alpha}.

By Hölder inequality, we have

\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}y_{n}\right)^{\alpha}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{n}^{t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{t}}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}^{\alpha},

where
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{t}=1
. Taking
x_{n}=\frac{(L|k_{j}|^{1/\alpha})^{n}}{n!}
and
y_{n}=(1-\varepsilon)^{n}
, we have

\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{((1-\varepsilon)L|k_{j}|^{1/\alpha})^{n}}{n!}\right)^{\alpha}\leq\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\varepsilon)^{tn}\right)^{\alpha/t}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{L^{n\alpha}}{n!^{\alpha}}|k_{j}|^{n}.

(2.6)

Denote
h(\varepsilon,\alpha)=\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\varepsilon)^{tn}\right)^{\alpha/t}=\left(\frac{1}{1-(1-\varepsilon)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}}\right)^{\alpha-1}
, then the inequality (
2.6
) can be written as

\exp(\alpha(1-\varepsilon)L|k_{j}|^{1/\alpha})\leq h(\varepsilon,\alpha)\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{L^{n\alpha}}{n!^{\alpha}}|k_{j}|^{n}.

(2.7)

Thus, we can obtain

\begin{split}|\hat{f}(k)|&\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{L^{|\beta|_{1}\alpha}}{\beta!^{\alpha}}}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{L^{n\alpha}}{n!^{\alpha}}|k_{j}|^{n}}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{h(\varepsilon,\alpha)}{2\pi}\right)^{d}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}\exp\left(-\alpha(1-\varepsilon)L\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}|k_{j}|^{1/\alpha}\right).\qed\end{split}

(2.8)

Proposition
2.4
tells us that the
k
-Fourier coefficients of Gevrey functions exhibit sub-exponential decay with rate
\alpha(L-\varepsilon)|k|_{\alpha}
, corresponding to the Gevrey width. On the other hand, if the Fourier coefficients of a function
f
exhibit sub-exponential decay, then
f
is Gevrey function. In the following, we provide a precise description.

We first consider the Gevrey norm of
e^{ik\cdot x}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
L>0
, then we have

\left\lVert e^{ik\cdot x}\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq e^{\alpha L|k|_{\alpha}}

.

Proof.

By the definition of
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\alpha,L}
, we have

\left\lVert e^{ik\cdot x}\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}=\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{L^{|\beta|_{1}\alpha}}{\beta!^{\alpha}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}|k_{j}|^{\beta_{j}}=\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}|k_{j}|^{\beta_{j}}\frac{L^{\beta_{j}\alpha}}{\beta_{j}!^{\alpha}}.

This simplifies to

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}|k_{j}|^{\beta_{j}}\frac{L^{\beta_{j}\alpha}}{\beta_{j}!^{\alpha}}&=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|k_{j}|^{n/\alpha}}{n!}L^{n}\right)^{\alpha}\\
&\leq\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|k_{j}|^{n/\alpha}}{n!}L^{n}\right)^{\alpha}\\
&=\exp(\alpha L\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}|k_{j}|^{1/\alpha}).\qed\end{split}

Therefore, we can naturally define a norm for
f
defined on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
as follows:

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L}}:=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{\alpha L|k|_{\alpha}}|\hat{f}(k)|.

(2.9)

We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L}
the set of smooth functions
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
such that
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L}}<\infty
. The two norms we have defined are related as follows:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Then:

(1)

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L}}
.

(2)

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,(1-\varepsilon)L-\delta}}\leq\left(\frac{h(\varepsilon,\alpha)}{2\pi}\right)^{d}C_{\delta}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}
, where
C_{\delta}=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-\alpha\delta|k|_{\alpha}}\lesssim(\alpha\delta)^{-\alpha}
, and
h(\varepsilon,\alpha)
is as defined above.

Proof.

Part (1) is straightforward by Lemma
2.5
.
For part (2), we have

\begin{split}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,(1-\varepsilon)L-\delta}}&=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp(\alpha((1-\varepsilon)L-\delta)|k|_{\alpha})|\hat{f}(k)|\\
&\leq\left(\frac{h(\varepsilon,\alpha)}{2\pi}\right)^{d}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-\alpha\delta|k|_{\alpha}}\\
&=\left(\frac{h(\varepsilon,\alpha)}{2\pi}\right)^{d}C_{\delta}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}.\qed\end{split}

(2.10)

We can express part (2) of Proposition
2.6
in a more concise form as follows:

Corollary 2.7
.

There exists a constant
C_{\alpha,d}
that depends only on
\alpha
and
d
such that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,(1-\varepsilon)L-\delta}}\leq C_{\alpha,d}\varepsilon^{-(\alpha-1)d}\delta^{-\alpha d}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}.

(2.11)

Proof.

It suffices to notice that

\begin{split}h(\varepsilon,\alpha)&=\left(\frac{1}{1-(1-\epsilon)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}}\right)^{\alpha-1}\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{1-(1-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{2}))}\right)^{\alpha-1}\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{2})}\right)^{\alpha-1}.\qed\end{split}

(2.12)

Next, we discuss truncation techniques in Gevrey space.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{N}^{n}
be a closed interval, and let
\lambda_{0}\in I
. Let
V
be a Banach algebra with norm denoted by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{V}

\varphi:I\to V
be a
V

\varphi
admits a Gevrey-
(\alpha,L)
asymptotic expansion at
\lambda_{0}
if there exists a formal series

\widetilde{\varphi}(\lambda)=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})^{k}\varphi_{k},

where
\varphi_{k}\in V
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, and a constant
C
such that, for all
\lambda\in I
and for all
p>0
,

\left\lVert\varphi(\lambda)-\sum_{|k|<p}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})^{k}\varphi_{k}\right\rVert_{V}\leq Cp!^{\alpha-1}L^{-p\alpha}|\lambda-\lambda_{0}|_{1}^{p}.

This definition differs slightly from the traditional definition of Gevrey asymptotic expansions, as it involves multiple variables. In fact, this can be viewed as a definition of a Gevrey-type
V
-valued function.
For a more usual framework of Gevrey asymptotic expansions, the reader is referred to
[

]
,
[

]

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
K_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
be a closed interval, and let
K_{2}
be an m-dimensional compact set. Let
\alpha>1
and
L_{1},L_{2}>0
. If
\varphi(\lambda,x)\in G^{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}(K_{1}\times K_{2})
, then we have

\forall k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\ \ \sup\limits_{\lambda\in K_{1}}\left\lVert\partial_{\lambda}^{k_{1}}\varphi(\lambda,\cdot)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{2}}\leq\left(\frac{k_{1}!}{L_{1}^{|k_{1}|_{1}}}\right)^{\alpha}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}.

(2.13)

Proof.

Fix
\lambda\in K_{1}
, we have

\begin{split}\left\lVert\partial_{\lambda}^{k_{1}}\varphi(\lambda,\cdot)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{2}}&=\sum\limits_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}}\left(\frac{L_{2}^{|k_{2}|_{1}}}{k_{2}!}\right)^{\alpha}\left\lVert\partial_{x}^{k_{2}}\partial_{\lambda}^{k_{1}}\varphi\right\rVert_{C^{0}(K_{2})}\\
&=\left(\frac{k_{1}!}{L_{1}^{|k_{1}|_{1}}}\right)^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}}\left(\frac{L_{1}^{|k_{1}|_{1}}L_{2}^{|k_{2}|_{1}}}{k_{1}!k_{2}!}\right)^{\alpha}\left\lVert\partial_{\lambda}^{k_{1}}\partial_{x}^{k_{2}}\varphi\right\rVert_{C^{0}(K_{2})}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{k_{1}!}{L_{1}^{|k_{1}|_{1}}}\right)^{\alpha}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}.\qed\end{split}

(2.14)

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
K=K_{1}\times K_{2}
, where
K_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a closed interval, and
K_{2}
is an
m
-dimensional compact set. Let
\lambda_{0}\in K_{1}
. For any
\varphi(\lambda,x)\in G^{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}(K)
, we can regard
\varphi
as a map from
K_{1}
to
G^{\alpha,L_{2}}(K_{2})
, and the Taylor series

\widetilde{\varphi}(\lambda_{0}+\Delta\lambda,x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\Delta\lambda_{1}\partial_{\lambda_{1}}+\cdots+\Delta\lambda_{n}\partial_{\lambda_{n}}\right)^{k}\varphi(\lambda_{0},x)

(2.15)

is the Gevrey-
(\alpha,L_{2})
asymptotic expansion of
\varphi
at
\lambda_{0}
.

Proof.

Denote

\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}(\lambda_{0}+\Delta\lambda,x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{p}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\Delta\lambda_{1}\partial_{\lambda_{1}}+\cdots+\Delta\lambda_{n}\partial_{\lambda_{n}}\right)^{k}\varphi(\lambda_{0},x).

By Taylor’s formula, we have

\begin{split}\varphi(\lambda,x)-\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}(\lambda,x)=\frac{1}{p!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{p}\left(\Delta\lambda_{1}\partial_{\lambda_{1}}+\cdots+\Delta\lambda_{n}\partial_{\lambda_{n}}\right)^{p+1}\varphi(\lambda_{0}+t\Delta\lambda,x)dt,\end{split}

where
\lambda=\lambda_{0}+\Delta\lambda
. Thus, we have

\begin{split}&\left\lVert\varphi(\lambda,x)-\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}(\lambda,x)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{2}}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{p!}\int_{0}^{1}|(1-t)|^{p}\left\lVert\left(\Delta\lambda_{1}\partial_{\lambda_{1}}+\cdots+\Delta\lambda_{n}\partial_{\lambda_{n}}\right)^{p+1}\varphi(\lambda_{0}+t\Delta\lambda)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{2}}dt\\
\leq&\frac{1}{p!}\int_{0}^{1}|(1-t)|^{p}\left\lVert\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\atop|k|=p+1}\frac{(p+1)!}{k!}\Delta\lambda^{k}\partial_{\lambda}^{k}\varphi(\lambda_{0}+t\Delta\lambda,x)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{2}}dt\\
\leq&\frac{1}{p!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{p}dt\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\atop|k|=p+1}\frac{(p+1)!}{k!}|\Delta\lambda^{k}|\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq 1}\left\lVert\partial_{\lambda}^{k}\varphi(\lambda_{0}+t\Delta\lambda,x)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{2}}.\end{split}

Using Lemma
2.13
, we have

\left\lVert\partial_{\lambda}^{k}\varphi(\lambda_{0}+t\Delta\lambda,x)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{2}}\leq\left(\frac{k!}{L_{1}^{|k|_{1}}}\right)^{\alpha}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}.

Thus, we obtain

\left\lVert\varphi(\lambda,x)-\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}(\lambda,x)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{p!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{p}dt\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\atop|k|_{1}=p+1}\frac{(p+1)!}{k!}|\Delta\lambda^{k}|\left(\frac{k!}{L_{1}^{|k|_{1}}}\right)^{\alpha}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}.

This simplifies to

\begin{split}&\left\lVert\varphi(\lambda,x)-\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}(\lambda,x)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{2}}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\frac{\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}}{L_{1}^{(p+1)\alpha}}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\atop|k|_{1}=p+1}\frac{(p+1)!}{k!}|\Delta\lambda^{k}|\cdot k!^{\alpha}\\
\leq&\frac{(p+1)!^{\alpha-1}}{L_{1}^{(p+1)\alpha}}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\atop|k|_{1}=p+1}\frac{(p+1)!}{k!}|\Delta\lambda^{k}|.\end{split}

Finally, using the multi-dimensional binomial formula, we conclude that

\left\lVert\varphi(\lambda,x)-\widetilde{\varphi}_{p}(\lambda,x)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{2}}\leq\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1},L_{2}}(p+1)!^{\alpha-1}L_{1}^{-(p+1)\alpha}|\Delta\lambda|_{1}^{p+1}.

(2.16)

Thus, the proof is complete.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let

F^{\prime}\in G^{\alpha,L}([-1,1])
. Let

and
\Delta q
be functions defined on
K
, and assume
q+t\Delta q\in G^{\alpha,\bm{L}}(K)
for all

, where
L,\bm{L}>0
and
K
is an m-dimensional compact set. If
\left\lVert q+t\Delta q\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}\leq\frac{L^{\alpha}}{m^{\alpha-1}}
for all

,
\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{C^{0}(K)}\ll 1
,

, and

\left\lVert F(q+\Delta q)-F(q)\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}\leq\left\lVert F^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}\left\lVert\Delta q\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}.

&\leq\left\lVert F^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}\left\lVert\Delta q\right\rVert_{\alpha,\bm{L}}.\qed\end{split}

3
Lyapounov-Schmidt decomposition

In this section, we describe the Lyapunov-Schmidt decomposition. We begin by defining quasi-periodic functions, along with their associated frequencies and hulls.

Definition 3.1
.

A function
\tilde{q}(t,\tilde{x})
is called quasi-periodic in time
t
if there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{b}
and
q(\theta,\tilde{x})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{b+d})
, with
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{b}
and
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, such that

\tilde{q}(t,\tilde{x})=q(\lambda t,\tilde{x}).

(3.1)

Here,
\lambda
is referred to as the frequency, and
q(\theta,\tilde{x})
as the hull of
\tilde{q}(t,\tilde{x})
.
When it is expressed as a Fourier series, we obtain

\tilde{q}(t,\tilde{x})=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{b},n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{q}(k,n)e^{i\lambda\cdot kt+in\cdot\tilde{x}},

(3.2)

where
\hat{q}(k,n)
denotes the Fourier coefficients of
q(\theta,\tilde{x})
.

To find quasi-periodic solutions with frequency
\lambda^{\prime}
to equation (
1.1
) is equivalent to solve

\frac{1}{i}\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\partial_{\theta}q-\mathcal{L}q-\varepsilon\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})=0,

(3.3)

where
\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\partial_{\theta}:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{b}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\partial_{\theta_{j}}
.
Taking the conjugate of (
3.3
), we obtain

-\frac{1}{i}\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\partial_{\theta}\bar{q}-\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\bar{q}-\varepsilon\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}(q,\bar{q})=0,

(3.4)

where
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is the operator with the multiplier
\{\mu_{n}^{\prime}=\mu_{-n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
.

The lattice formulation of (
3.3
) is as follows:

(k\cdot\lambda^{\prime}-\mu_{n})\hat{q}(n,k)-\varepsilon\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}}(q,\bar{q})(n,k)=0,\ \ \ \ (n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},k\in\mathbb{Z}^{b}),

(3.5)

where
\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}}(q,\bar{q})(n,k)
represents the
(n,k)
-Fourier coefficient of
\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})
. If we can find
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{b}
and a sequence
\{\hat{q}(n,k)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},k\in\mathbb{Z}^{b}}
with a certain degree of decay that satisfies (
3.5
), then the function

\tilde{q}(t,\tilde{x})=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{b},n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{q}(k,n)e^{i\lambda^{\prime}\cdot kt+in\cdot\tilde{x}}.

(3.6)

with the found
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\hat{q}(n,k)
is a quasi-periodic solution to equation (
1.1
) with the corresponding regularity.
Denote by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
the norm of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{b+d})
. If there exists
q(\theta,\tilde{x})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{b+d})
such that

\left\lVert\frac{1}{i}\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\partial_{\theta}q-\mathcal{L}q-\varepsilon\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})\right\rVert<\delta

(3.7)

for
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{b}
, then the quasi-periodic function
\tilde{q}(t,x)
, with the hull
q(\theta,\tilde{x})
and frequency
\lambda^{\prime}
, is referred to as an
O(\delta)
approximate solution to (
1.1
).

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{(n_{j},e_{j})|1\leq j\leq b\}
, which we refer to as the resonant set. We specify the Fourier coefficients as

\hat{q}(n_{j},e_{j})=a_{j},\ \ 1\leq j\leq b.

(3.8)

To describe the Lyapounov-Schmidt decomposition, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
g(\theta,\tilde{x})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{b+d})
. We define the projection operators
\bm{\Gamma}_{Q}
,
\bm{\Gamma}_{P}
and
\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}
as follows:

\begin{split}&\bm{\Gamma}_{Q}g=\sum\limits_{(n,k)\in\mathcal{R}}\hat{g}(n,k)e^{in\cdot\tilde{x}+ik\cdot\theta},\\
&\bm{\Gamma}_{P}g=\sum\limits_{(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R}}\hat{g}(n,k)e^{in\cdot\tilde{x}+ik\cdot\theta},\\
&\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}g=\sum\limits_{(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus(-\mathcal{R})}\hat{g}(n,k)e^{in\cdot\tilde{x}+ik\cdot\theta}.\end{split}

Denote the left side of (
3.3
) by
F(q)
, i.e.,

F(q)=\frac{1}{i}\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\partial_{\theta}q-\mathcal{L}q-\varepsilon\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})

(3.9)

then (
3.3
) is equivalent to

\bm{\Gamma}_{Q}F(q)=0,

(3.10)

\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q)=0.

(3.11)

Passing equations (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) to Fourier coefficients, we obtain the following lattice equations:

(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-\lambda_{j})a_{j}-\varepsilon\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}}(q,\bar{q})(n_{j},e_{j})=0,\ \ 1\leq j\leq b,

(3.12)

(k\cdot\lambda^{\prime}-\mu_{n})\hat{q}(n,k)-\varepsilon\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}}(q,\bar{q})(n,k)=0,\ \ (n,k)\notin\mathcal{R}.

(3.13)

we refer to the finite equations (
3.12
) as the
Q-equations

and the infinite equations (
3.13
) as the
P-equations
.

However, the P-equations cannot be addressed using a standard implicit function theorem due to the presence of small divisors. Consequently, we will solve P-equations using Newton’s algorithm. To apply Newton’s algorithm, it is necessary to duplicate equations
(
3.13
).
Note that
\hat{\bar{q}}(-n,-k)=\bar{\hat{q}}(n,k)
. Taking the conjugate of equations (
3.13
), we can duplicate them as follows:

\begin{cases}(k\cdot\lambda^{\prime}-\mu_{n})\hat{q}(n,k)-\varepsilon\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}}(q,\bar{q})(n,k)=0\ \ &(n,k)\notin\mathcal{R},\\
(-k\cdot\lambda^{\prime}-\mu_{-n})\hat{\bar{q}}(n,k)-\varepsilon\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial{q}}}(q,\bar{q})(n,k)=0\ \ &(n,k)\notin-\mathcal{R}.\end{cases}

(3.14)

We will see that the relation
\hat{\bar{q}}(-n,-k)=\bar{\hat{q}}(n,k)
is preserved for the approximate solutions in Newton’s algorithm. In fact, the second equality in (
3.14
) is simply
\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q)=0
modulo a Fourier transform. Therefore, up to a Fourier transform, (
3.14
) can be written as follows:

\begin{cases}\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q)=0,\\
\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q)=0.\end{cases}

(3.15)

We also refer to both (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) as the
P
-equations.

Thus, the general procedure is to determine
\hat{q}(n,k)|_{(n,k)\notin\mathcal{R}}
(which depends on
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda
) from the
P
-equations, and then substitute it into
Q
-equations to obtain the new frequencies
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},...,\lambda_{b}^{\prime})
. Note that the frequencies
\lambda_{1}^{\prime},...,\lambda_{b}^{\prime}
need to be real, otherwise the function (
3.6
) cannot represent a quasi-periodic solution to (
1.1
). We will demonstrate the reality of
\lambda^{\prime}
through our construction by applying Proposition
A.1
in the Appendix.

4
The homological equations and the estimate of the remians

Now we describe the Newton iteration scheme to solve
P
-equations. Recall that
H(q,\bar{q})=f(q\bar{q})
. Let
q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{b+d})
,
\Delta q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{b+d})
and
\textup{supp }\widehat{\Delta q}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R}
, then we have

\begin{split}F(q+\Delta q)=&F(q)+\frac{1}{i}\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\partial_{\theta}\Delta q-\mathcal{L}\Delta q-\varepsilon\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})\Delta q-\varepsilon\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\bar{q}^{2}}(q,\bar{q})\overline{\Delta q}\\
&-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)(\Delta q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\overline{\Delta q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{q}})^{2}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}(q+s\Delta q,\bar{q}+s\overline{\Delta q})ds.\end{split}

(4.1)

Taking the conjugate on both sides of (
4.1
), we have

\begin{split}\bar{F}(q+\Delta q)=\bar{F}(q)-\frac{1}{i}\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\partial_{\theta}\overline{\Delta q}-\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\overline{\Delta q}-\varepsilon\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q^{2}}(q,\bar{q})\Delta q-\varepsilon\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})\overline{\Delta q}\\
-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)(\Delta q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\overline{\Delta q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{q}})^{2}\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}(q+s\Delta q,\bar{q}+s\overline{\Delta q})ds.\end{split}

(4.2)

Summarizing equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we conclude that

\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix}F(q+\Delta q)\\
\bar{F}(q+\Delta q)\end{pmatrix}=&\begin{pmatrix}F(q)\\
\bar{F}(q)\end{pmatrix}+\widetilde{T}_{q}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}\\
&+\begin{pmatrix}-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)(\Delta q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\overline{\Delta q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{q}})^{2}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}(q+s\Delta q,\bar{q}+s\overline{\Delta q})ds\\
-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)(\Delta q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\overline{\Delta q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{q}})^{2}\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}(q+s\Delta q,\bar{q}+s\overline{\Delta q})ds\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(4.3)

where

\widetilde{T}_{q}=\left[\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{i}\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\partial_{\theta}-\mathcal{L}&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{i}\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\partial_{\theta}-\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}-\varepsilon\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})&\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\bar{q}^{2}}(q,\bar{q})\\
\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q^{2}}(q,\bar{q})&\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})\end{pmatrix}\right].

(4.4)

Let

\widetilde{T}_{q,P}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q)\\
\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q)\end{pmatrix},

(4.5)

where

\widetilde{T}_{q,P}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\Gamma}_{P}&0\\
0&\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\widetilde{T}_{q}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\Gamma}_{P}&0\\
0&\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.6)

We denote
T_{q}=D-\varepsilon S_{q}
, where

D=\begin{pmatrix}k\cdot\lambda^{\prime}-\mu_{n}&0\\
0&-k\cdot\lambda^{\prime}-\mu_{-n}\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \ S_{q}=\begin{pmatrix}S_{\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})}&S_{\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\bar{q}^{2}}(q,\bar{q})}\\
S_{\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q^{2}}(q,\bar{q})}&S_{\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})}\end{pmatrix},

(4.7)

and
S_{\phi}
represents the Toeplitz operator corresponding to
\phi
-multiplication. The matrix index is
(\pm,n,k)
, where
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{b}
, and the index of
\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\Delta q}\\
\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}\end{pmatrix}
is also
(\pm,n,k)
. More precisely, we have

S_{q}((+,n,k),(+,n^{\prime},k^{\prime}))=S_{\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})}((n,k),(n^{\prime},k^{\prime})),

S_{q}((+,n,k),(-,n^{\prime},k^{\prime}))=S_{\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\bar{q}^{2}}(q,\bar{q})}((n,k),(n^{\prime},k^{\prime})),

S_{q}((-,n,k),(+,n^{\prime},k^{\prime}))=S_{\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q^{2}}(q,\bar{q})}((n,k),(n^{\prime},k^{\prime})),

S_{q}((-,n,k),(-,n^{\prime},k^{\prime}))=S_{\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})}((n,k),(n^{\prime},k^{\prime})),

\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\Delta q}\\
\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}\end{pmatrix}(+,n,k)=\widehat{\Delta q}(n,k)

and

\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\Delta q}\\
\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}\end{pmatrix}(-,n,k)=\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}(n,k).

Passing equation (
4.5
) to Fourier coefficients, we obtain the homological equation of (
3.14
):

T_{q,P}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\Delta q}\\
\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}R_{P}\widehat{F(q)}\\
R_{P^{\prime}}\widehat{\bar{F}(q)}\end{pmatrix},

(4.8)

where the linearized operator is given by

T_{q,P}=R_{P\cup P^{\prime}}(D-\varepsilon S_{q})R_{P\cup P^{\prime}},

(4.9)

and
R_{P}
refers to coordinate restriction to
(\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R})\subset\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}
,
R_{P^{\prime}}
refers to coordinate restriction to
(\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus(-\mathcal{R}))\subset\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}
and
R_{P\cup P^{\prime}}
refers to coordinate restriction to
\{(+,n,k)|(n,k)\notin\mathcal{R}\}\cup\{(-,n,k)|(n,k)\notin-\mathcal{R}\}
.

However, we cannot solve (
4.8
) directly. Instead, we aim to solve an approximation of (
4.8
) as follows:

T_{q,P,N}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\Delta q}\\
\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-R_{P,N}\widehat{F(q)}\\
-R_{P^{\prime},N}\widehat{\bar{F}(q)}\end{pmatrix},

(4.10)

i.e.,

\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\Delta q}\\
\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}\end{pmatrix}=(T_{q,P,N})^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}-R_{P,N}\widehat{F(q)}\\
-R_{P^{\prime},N}\widehat{\bar{F}(q)}\end{pmatrix},

(4.11)

where
T_{q,P,N}=R_{P\cup P^{\prime},N}T_{q}R_{P\cup P^{\prime},N}
, and
R_{P,N}
refers to coordinate restriction to

\left((\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R})\cap\{|n|<N,|k|<N\}\right)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{b+d},

R_{P^{\prime},N}
refers to coordinate restriction to

\left((\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus(-\mathcal{R}))\cap\{|n|<N,|k|<N\}\right)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{b+d},

and
R_{P\cup P^{\prime},N}
refers to coordinate restriction to

\left(\{(+,n,k)|(n,k)\notin\mathcal{R}\}\cup\{(-,n,k)|(n,k)\notin-\mathcal{R}\}\right)\cap\{|n|<N,|k|<N\}.

The solution
\Delta q
and
\overline{\Delta q}
obtained by solving equations (
4.10
) are conjugates of each other as the relation
\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}(-n,-k)=\overline{\widehat{\Delta q}(n,k)}
holds. Up to a Fourier transform, equation (
4.10
) can be rewritten as:

\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\bm{\Gamma}_{P,N}F(q)\\
-\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime},N}\bar{F}(q)\end{pmatrix},

(4.12)

where

\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\Gamma}_{P,N}&0\\
0&\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime},N}\end{pmatrix}\widetilde{T}_{q}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\Gamma}_{P,N}&0\\
0&\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime},N}\end{pmatrix},

(4.13)

and

\bm{\Gamma}_{P,N}h(\theta,\tilde{x})=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R}\\
|n|<N,|k|<N\end{subarray}}\hat{h}(n,k)e^{in\cdot\tilde{x}+ik\cdot\theta},

\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime},N}h(\theta,\tilde{x})=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus(-\mathcal{R})\\
|n|<N,|k|<N\end{subarray}}\hat{h}(n,k)e^{in\cdot\tilde{x}+ik\cdot\theta}

for
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{b+d})
.
For
\Delta q,\overline{\Delta q}
satisfying (
4.10
) (i.e.,(
4.12
)) we have

\textup{supp }\widehat{\Delta q}\subset(\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R})\cap\{|n|<N,|k|<N\}
and

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q+\Delta q)\\
\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q+\Delta q)\end{pmatrix}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q)\\
\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q)\end{pmatrix}+\widetilde{T}_{q,P}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}+O(\Delta q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\Gamma}_{P,N}F(q)\\
\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime},N}\bar{F}(q)\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}(\bm{\Gamma}_{P}-\bm{\Gamma}_{P,N})F(q)\\
(\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}-\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime},N})\bar{F}(q)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}+(\widetilde{T}_{q,P}-\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N})\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}+O(\Delta q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}(\bm{\Gamma}_{P}-\bm{\Gamma}_{P,N})F(q)\\
(\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}-\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime},N})\bar{F}(q)\end{pmatrix}+(\widetilde{T}_{q,P}-\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N})\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}+O(\Delta q^{2}),

(4.14)

where

O(\Delta q^{2}):=\begin{pmatrix}-\varepsilon\bm{\Gamma}_{P}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)(\Delta q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\overline{\Delta q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{q}})^{2}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}(q+s\Delta q,\bar{q}+s\overline{\Delta q})ds\\
-\varepsilon\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)(\Delta q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\overline{\Delta q}\frac{\partial}{\partial q})^{2}\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}(q+s\Delta q,\bar{q}+s\overline{\Delta q})ds\end{pmatrix},

(4.15)

and we denote the first and the second components of
O(\Delta q^{2})
by
O(\Delta q^{2})_{1}
and
O(\Delta q^{2})_{2}
respectively.
Assume one succeeds in ensuring that

|T_{q,P,N}^{-1}((\nu,x),(\nu^{\prime},x^{\prime}))|<B(r)e^{-\alpha L^{(1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}},

(4.16)

where
\nu,\nu^{\prime}=\pm
,
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}
,
B(r)
and
N
is to be specified, and

\left\{\begin{split}&\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(0)}}}<\epsilon,\\
&\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(0)}}}<\epsilon,\\
&\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{\alpha,L^{(0)}}\ll 1,\end{split}\right.

(4.17)

where
\epsilon
is small,
L^{(0)},L^{(1)}>0
and
L^{(0)}>L^{(1)}
.

First, we estimate
\Delta q
. According to equation (
4.11
), for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R}
and
|x|<N
, we have

\widehat{\Delta q}(x)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|y|<N\\
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}T_{q,P,N}^{-1}((+,x),(+,y))\widehat{F(q)}(y)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|y|<N\\
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus-\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}T_{q,P,N}^{-1}((+,x),(-,y))\widehat{\bar{F}(q)}(y).

Since
\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(0)}}}<\epsilon
and
\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(0)}}}<\epsilon
, we have

|\widehat{F(q)}(x)|<\epsilon e^{-\alpha L^{(0)}|x|_{\alpha}},\textup{ for }x\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R},

and

|\widehat{\bar{F}(q)}(x)|<\epsilon e^{-\alpha L^{(0)}|x|_{\alpha}},\textup{ for }x\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus-\mathcal{R}.

Thus,

\displaystyle|\widehat{\Delta q}(x)|\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|y|<N\\
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}|T_{q,P,N}^{-1}((+,x),(+,y))||\widehat{F(q)}(y)|

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|y|<N\\
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus-\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}|T_{q,P,N}^{-1}((+,x),(-,y))||\widehat{\bar{F}(q)}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|y|<N\\
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\epsilon B(r)e^{-\alpha L^{(1)}|x-y|_{\alpha}}e^{-\alpha L^{(0)}|y|_{\alpha}}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|y|<N\\
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus-\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\epsilon B(r)e^{-\alpha L^{(1)}|x-y|_{\alpha}}e^{-\alpha L^{(0)}|y|_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon B(r)C_{\alpha}\delta^{-\alpha(b+d)}e^{-L^{(2)}|x|_{\alpha}},

(4.18)

where
0<\delta<L^{(1)}
,
L^{(2)}=L^{(1)}-\delta
and
C_{\alpha}=\alpha^{-\alpha(b+d)}(\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-x^{1/\alpha}}dx)^{b+d}
.

In the following part of this section, we make the following assumptions:

•

\epsilon=\varepsilon^{a^{r}}\textup{for some }1<a<2
,

•

\log B(r)<Cr^{C}\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
,

•

\delta\sim r^{-C}
.

Based on these assumptions, we derive that

|\widehat{\Delta q}(x)|\leq\epsilon^{1-}e^{-\alpha L^{(2)}|x|_{\alpha}}\textup{ for }x\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R}.

Additionally, the norm
\left\lVert\Delta q\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(2)}-\delta}}
can be bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Delta q\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(2)}-\delta}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}}|\widehat{\Delta q}(x)|\cdot e^{\alpha(L^{(2)}-\delta)|x|_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{1-}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}}e^{-\alpha\delta|x|_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{1-}C_{\alpha}\delta^{-\alpha(b+d)}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{1-}.

(4.19)

Now we estimate the three terms in (
4.14
).

Firstly, we consider
O(\Delta q^{2})
. Let
L^{(3)}=L^{(2)}-\delta
, then
\left\lVert\Delta q\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(3)}}}\leq\epsilon^{1-}
. Therefore, we have

\left\lVert O(\Delta q^{2})_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(4)}}}<\epsilon^{2-}\textup{ and }\left\lVert O(\Delta q^{2})_{2}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(4)}}}<\epsilon^{2-},

where
L^{(4)}=L^{(3)}-\delta
.

Recalling the definition of
T_{q}
, we get

\begin{cases}|T_{q}((\nu,x),(\nu^{\prime},x^{\prime}))|<\varepsilon,\ \ \nu,\nu^{\prime}=\pm,x\neq x^{\prime},|x-x^{\prime}|<\delta^{-C},\\
|T_{q}((\nu,x),(\nu^{\prime},x^{\prime}))|<e^{-\alpha(L^{(0)}-\delta)|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}},\ \ |x-x^{\prime}|\geq\delta^{-C},\end{cases}

(4.20)

since
\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{\alpha,L^{(0)}}\ll 1
.

Secondly, we consider the term:

\begin{pmatrix}(\bm{\Gamma}_{P}-\bm{\Gamma}_{P,N})F(q)\\
(\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}-\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime},N})\bar{F}(q)\end{pmatrix}.

Taking
(\bm{\Gamma}_{P}-\bm{\Gamma}_{P,N})F(q)
for example, since
\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(0)}}}<\epsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\bm{\Gamma}_{P}-\bm{\Gamma}_{P,N})F(q)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(2)}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{|x|\geq N,x\notin\mathcal{R}}|\widehat{F(q)}(x)|e^{\alpha L^{(2)}|x|_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{|x|\geq N,x\notin\mathcal{R}}\epsilon e^{-\alpha L^{(0)}|x|_{\alpha}+\alpha L^{(2)}|x|_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\sum\limits_{|x|\geq N,x\notin\mathcal{R}}e^{-\alpha\delta|x|_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon(\alpha\delta)^{-\alpha(b+d)}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha\delta N^{1/\alpha}}.

(4.21)

If we choose
N=A^{\alpha r}
for large
A
(for example
A=(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{\alpha}
), then
\epsilon r^{C}e^{-\frac{1}{2}r^{-C}A^{r}}<\epsilon^{2}
.

Thirdly, we consider

(\widetilde{T}_{q,P}-\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N})\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}.

For
\nu=\pm
and
|x|<N
, we have

\begin{split}&\left((\widetilde{T}_{q,P}-\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N})\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}\right)^{\land}(\nu,x)\\
=&\sum\limits_{|y|<N}[T_{q,P}((\nu,x),(+,y))-T_{q,P,N}((\nu,x),(+,y))]\widehat{\Delta q}(y)+\\
&\sum\limits_{|y|<N}[T_{q,P}((\nu,x),(-,y))-T_{q,P,N}((\nu,x),(-,y))]\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}(y)\\
=&0.\end{split}

For
\nu=\pm
and
|x|\geq N
, we have

\displaystyle\left((\widetilde{T}_{q,P}-\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N})\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}\right)^{\land}(\nu,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|y|<N}|T_{q,P}((\nu,x),(+,y))\widehat{\Delta q}(y)|+\sum\limits_{|y|<N}|T_{q,P}((\nu,x),(-,y))\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}(y)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|x-y|<\delta^{-C}}N^{2}(|\widehat{\Delta q}(y)|+|\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}(y)|)+\sum\limits_{|x-y|\geq\delta^{-C}}e^{-\alpha(L^{(0)}-\delta)|x-y|_{\alpha}}(|\widehat{\Delta q}(y)|+|\widehat{\overline{\Delta q}}(y)|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon^{1-}\left(\sum\limits_{|x-y|<\delta^{-C}}e^{-\alpha L^{(3)}|y|_{\alpha}}+\sum\limits_{|x-y|\geq\delta}e^{-\alpha L^{(3)}|x-y|_{\alpha}}e^{-\alpha L^{(3)}|y|_{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon^{1-}\left(\delta^{-C}e^{\alpha L^{(3)}\delta^{-C}}e^{-\alpha L^{(3)}|x|_{\alpha}}+C_{\alpha}\delta^{-\alpha(b+d)}e^{-\alpha L^{(4)}|x|_{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon^{1-}e^{-\alpha L^{(4)}|x|_{\alpha}}.

(4.22)

Denote by

\left((\widetilde{T}_{q,P}-\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N})\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{1}

and

\left((\widetilde{T}_{q,P}-\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N})\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{2}

the first and the second components of
(\widetilde{T}_{q,P}-\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N})\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}
respectively.
Let
L^{(5)}=L^{(4)}-\delta
, as discussion in (
4.21
). We then have

\left\{\begin{split}\left\lVert\left((\widetilde{T}_{q,P}-\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N})\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{1}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(5)}}}\leq\epsilon^{1-}(\alpha\delta)^{-\alpha(b+d)}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha\delta N^{1/\alpha}}<\epsilon^{2},\\
\left\lVert\left((\widetilde{T}_{q,P}-\widetilde{T}_{q,P,N})\begin{pmatrix}\Delta q\\
\overline{\Delta q}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{2}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(5)}}}\leq\epsilon^{1-}(\alpha\delta)^{-\alpha(b+d)}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha\delta N^{1/\alpha}}<\epsilon^{2}.\end{split}\right.

(4.23)

Thus, we have
\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q+\Delta q)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(5)}}}<\epsilon^{2-}
and
\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q+\Delta q)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(5)}}}<\epsilon^{2-}
.

Thus under the assumptions (
4.16
) and (
4.17
), we can find
\Delta q
such that

\left\{\begin{split}&\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q+\Delta q)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(5)}}}<\epsilon^{2-}<\varepsilon^{a^{r+1}},\\
&\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q+\Delta q)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(5)}}}<\epsilon^{2-}<\varepsilon^{a^{r+1}},\\
&\left\lVert q+\Delta q\right\rVert_{\alpha,L^{(3)}}\ll 1.\end{split}\right.

(4.24)

Moreover, we have

\left\{\begin{split}&\textup{supp }\widehat{\Delta q}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}||x|<N\}\cap\mathcal{R},\\
&\left\lVert\Delta q\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L^{(3)}}}<\epsilon^{1-}.\end{split}\right.

(4.25)

Thus our main task is to obtain (
4.16
) at each step, i.e., the estimate of the inverse of the linearized operator
T_{q,P,N}^{-1}
. In the sequel, we refer to
T_{q,P,N}^{-1}

as the Green function.

Now we consider the Q-equations:

(\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-\lambda_{j})a_{j}-\varepsilon\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}}(q,\bar{q})(n_{j},e_{j})=0,\ \ 1\leq j\leq b,

(4.26)

i.e.,

\lambda^{\prime}_{j}=\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon\frac{1}{a_{j}}\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}}(q,\bar{q})(n_{j},e_{j}).

(4.27)

In fact, to maintain the bound of the Green function, some conditions on
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},a)
need to be satisfied. However, we assume that
\hat{q}|_{(n,k)\notin\mathcal{R}}
are defined as
C^{1}
functions over the entire
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
-parameter space
I_{0}\times I_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2b}
(we will extend q to the entire parameter space). Moreover, assume
\left\lVert\partial q\right\rVert_{C(I_{0}\times I_{0})}<C
(where
\partial
refers to derivative with respect to
\lambda
or
\lambda^{\prime}
), then we have
|\partial\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}}(n_{j},e_{j})|<C
.

Let

G(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=\left(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-\lambda_{j}-\varepsilon\frac{1}{a_{j}}\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}}(q,\bar{q})(n_{j},e_{j})\right)_{j=1,...,b},

(4.28)

then

\frac{\partial G(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}{\partial\lambda^{\prime}}=Id+O(\varepsilon),

and

\det\left(\frac{\partial G(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}{\partial\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\neq 0\textup{ for all }(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\in I_{0}\times I_{0}.

Thus, we can solve
\lambda^{\prime}
from (
4.27
) to get a solution such that:

\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+\varepsilon\varphi(\lambda),\ \ \left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{C^{1}(I_{0})}<C.

(4.29)

If there are two functions
q^{(1)},q^{(2)}
satisfying

\left\lVert q^{(1)}\right\rVert_{C^{1}(I_{0})}<C,\ \ \left\lVert q^{(2)}\right\rVert_{C^{1}(I_{0})}<C,

and we denote their respective solutions as
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+\varepsilon\varphi^{(1)}(\lambda)
and
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+\varepsilon\varphi^{(2)}(\lambda)
,
then we have the following inequality:

\left\lVert\varphi^{(1)}-\varphi^{(2)}\right\rVert_{C^{0}(I_{0})}\lesssim\left\lVert q^{(1)}-q^{(2)}\right\rVert_{C^{0}(I_{0})}.

(4.30)

5
The Iterative Lemma

We define the following iterative constants and domains:

•

\alpha>1
,
L_{0}>0
corresponding to the Gevrey function
f
.

•

r\geq 0
, the number of Newton iteration steps.

•

q_{r}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})(\theta,\tilde{x})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{b+d})
, the approximate solution at the
r
-th step.

•

q_{0}(\theta,\tilde{x})=\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq b}a_{j}e^{in_{j}\cdot\tilde{x}+ie_{j}\cdot\theta}
.

•

\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+\varepsilon\varphi_{r}(\lambda)
is the solution of

(\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-\lambda_{j})a_{j}-\varepsilon\widehat{\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}}(q_{r},\bar{q}_{r})(n_{j},e_{j})=0,\ \ 1\leq j\leq b.

•

A=(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{\alpha}
,
N_{r}=A^{r}
.

•

\epsilon_{r}=\varepsilon^{(\frac{4}{3})^{r}}
.

•

\delta_{r}=\frac{3L_{0}}{10\pi^{2}r^{2}}
.

•

L_{r}=L_{0}-10\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}\delta_{j}
, and for
1\leq j\leq 10
,
L_{r}^{(j)}=L_{r-1}-j\delta_{r}
,
L_{r}^{(0.j)}=L_{r-1}-\frac{j\delta_{r}}{10}
.

•

B(0,N):=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}:|x|<N\}
.

•

\Gamma_{r}
, the graph of
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+\varepsilon\varphi_{r}(\lambda)
.

Choose constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
such that
C_{1}>C_{2}+2
and
C_{2}>C_{3}>10
.
We now state the following Iterative Lemma:

Lemma 5.1

(
Iterative Lemma
)
.

Let
r\geq 1
, and for each step
r
, there exists an approximate solution
q_{r}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})(\theta,\tilde{x})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{b+d})
defined on
I_{0}\times I_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2b}
, and these solutions are
C^{1}
with respect to
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
. They satisfy the following statements:

(r.1)

\textup{supp }\hat{q}_{r}|_{\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}\setminus\mathcal{R}}\subset B(0,N_{r})
, and
\hat{q}_{r}(x)\in\mathbb{R}
for
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}
.

(r.2)

Let
\Delta q_{r}=q_{r}-q_{r-1}
. Then
\textup{supp }\widehat{\Delta q}_{r}\subset B(0,N_{r})\setminus\mathcal{R}
and

\left\lVert\Delta q_{r}\right\rVert_{C^{1}(I_{0}\times I_{0})}<\epsilon_{r-1}^{1-}
.

(r.3)

There exists a collection
\Lambda_{r}
of intervals
I
in
\mathbb{R}^{2b}
of size
A^{-\alpha(r+2)^{C_{1}}}
, such that:

(r.3.a)

Let
T=T_{q_{r-1}}
. For
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\in\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\Lambda_{r}}I
, we have

|T_{P,N_{r}}^{-1}((\nu,x),(\nu^{\prime},x^{\prime}))|<2B(r)e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}\ \ \text{for}\ \ \nu,\nu^{\prime}=\pm,\ x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d},

(5.1)

where
B(r)=A^{r^{C_{3}}}
.

(r.3.b)

Let
I\in\Lambda_{r}
. Then
q_{r}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
is given by a rational function of
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
of degree at most
A^{r^{3}}
on
I
, and we have

\left\{\begin{split}&\left\lVert\Delta q_{r}\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{r}}<\epsilon_{r-1}^{1-},\\
&\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L_{r}}}\lesssim\epsilon_{r},\\
&\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L_{r}}}\lesssim\epsilon_{r}.\end{split}\right.

(5.2)

(r.3.c)

For
I\in\Lambda_{r}
, regarding
q_{r}
as a function defined on
I\times\mathbb{T}^{b+d}
, we have
q_{r},\Delta q_{r}\in G^{\alpha,A^{-(r+2)^{C_{1}}},L_{r}}(I\times\mathbb{T}^{b+d})
, and

\left\lVert\Delta q_{r}\right\rVert_{\alpha,A^{-(r+2)^{C_{1}}},L_{r}}<\epsilon_{r-1}^{1-}.

Moreover,
\left\lVert q_{r}\right\rVert_{\alpha,A^{-(r+2)^{C_{1}}},L_{r}}\ll 1
.

(r.3.d)

Each
I\in\Lambda_{r}
is contained in an interval
I^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{r-1}
.
For
r\gtrsim\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
, we have

\text{mes}_{b}\left(\Gamma_{r}\cap\left(\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{r-1}}I^{\prime}\setminus\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\Lambda_{r}}I\right)\right)<[\exp\exp(\log r)^{1/4}]^{-1}.

(5.3)

For
r\lesssim\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
, we have

\text{mes}_{b}\left(\Gamma_{r}\cap\left(I_{0}\times I_{0}\setminus\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\Lambda_{r}}I\right)\right)<(\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(5.4)

(r.4)

On
\Gamma_{r}\cap(\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\Lambda_{r}}I)
, we have

\left\lVert\frac{1}{i}\lambda^{\prime}\cdot\partial_{\theta}q_{r}-\mathcal{L}q_{r}-\varepsilon\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}(q_{r},\bar{q_{r}})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L_{r}}}\lesssim\epsilon_{r}.

(5.5)

i.e.,
q_{r}(\lambda^{\prime}t,x)
is an
O(\epsilon_{r})
approximate solution of (
1.1
) for
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\in\Gamma_{r}
.

When
r=0
, since
q_{0}
is independent of
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
, it is easy to check that
\left\lVert q_{0}\right\rVert_{\alpha,L,L_{0}}\ll 1
if we take
\{a_{j}\}
sufficiently small. It is also easy to check that

\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q_{0})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L_{0}}}\lesssim\varepsilon=\epsilon_{0}

and

\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q_{0})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L_{0}}}\lesssim\varepsilon=\epsilon_{0}.

In the sequel, when we say the Iterative Lemma holds at step 0, we mean that:

\left\lVert q_{0}\right\rVert_{\alpha,L,L_{0}}\ll 1,\ \ \left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P}F(q_{0})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L_{0}}}\lesssim\varepsilon=\epsilon_{0},\ \ \left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{P^{\prime}}\bar{F}(q_{0})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,L_{0}}}\lesssim\varepsilon=\epsilon_{0}.

Moreover, we denote
\Lambda_{0}=\{I_{0}\times I_{0}\}
.

5.1
Estimate of the Green function in first finite steps

As mentioned in the previous section, the key point is to obtain the bound for the Green function.
Throughout the estimation of the Green function, one can easily observe that the index
\nu=\pm
plays a minimal role in establishing the bound. Therefore, for simplicity, we omit the index
\nu=\pm
and write
T(x,x^{\prime})=T((\nu,x),(\nu^{\prime},x^{\prime}))
in the sequel.

In this subsection, we will prove
(r.3.d)
for
r\leq C\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
(
C>2\alpha
can be large).

Recall that
T_{q,P,N}=D_{P,N}-\varepsilon S_{q,P,N}
, and we have the following expressions:

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q,\bar{q})=f^{\prime\prime}(|q|^{2})|q|^{2}+f^{\prime}(|q|^{2})q,\\
&\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\bar{q}^{2}}(q,\bar{q})=f^{\prime\prime}(|q|^{2})q^{2},\\
&\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q^{2}}(q,\bar{q})=f^{\prime\prime}(|q|^{2})\bar{q}^{2}.\end{split}

Assuming the Iterative Lemma holds up to step
r-1
, then for
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\in\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\Lambda_{r-1}}I
, we have

\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1})\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{r-1}}<1,\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\bar{q}^{2}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1})\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{r-1}}<1,\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q^{2}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1})\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{r-1}}<1.

Thus we have

|T(x,x^{\prime})|<\varepsilon\delta_{r}^{-C_{\alpha}}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}},

(5.6)

where
C_{\alpha}
depends on
\alpha
.

In fact, we can also derive

|T(x,x^{\prime})|\leq e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.2)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}},\ \ \text{for}\ \ x\neq x^{\prime},

(5.7)

by applying Lemma
2.11
and (l.3.b)
(l\leq r-1)
inductively.

We impose the following Diophantine condition on the vector
\lambda
:

|k\cdot\lambda-\mu_{n}|>\gamma(1+|k|)^{-C},\ \ |k|<N_{r},\ (n,k)\notin\mathcal{R},

(5.8)

where
C>b+d
. We denote this condition by
DC_{\gamma,C}
, and we can easily derive that

\text{mes}\{I_{0}\setminus DC_{\gamma,C}\}\lesssim\gamma.

Also, note that we have
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+O(\varepsilon)
by solving Q-equations at each step. It follows that

\displaystyle|k\cdot\lambda^{\prime}-\mu_{n}|

\displaystyle>|k\cdot\lambda-\mu_{n}|-|k\cdot O(\varepsilon)|

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2}\gamma(1+|k|)^{-C}

(5.9)

for
|k|<\gamma^{\frac{1}{2C}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2C}}
.
Let
\gamma=(\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and since
N_{r}=A^{r}<e^{C(\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{2}}
, we have

\left\lVert D_{P,N}^{-1}\right\rVert<2\gamma^{-1}N_{r}^{C}<e^{C(\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{2}}.

For simplicity, we omit the subscripts
q
and
P
when there is no confusion in the sequel.
Now we use the Neumann argument to estimate the bound of the Green function:

We have

\displaystyle T_{N}^{-1}

\displaystyle=(D_{N}-\varepsilon S_{N})^{-1}

\displaystyle=D_{N}^{-1}(I-\varepsilon S_{N}D_{N}^{-1})^{-1}

\displaystyle=D_{N}^{-1}+\mathop{\sum}\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{j}D_{N}^{-1}(S_{N}D_{N}^{-1})^{j}.

(5.10)

Notice that

\left\lVert(I-\varepsilon S_{N}D_{N}^{-1})^{-1}\right\rVert<\frac{1}{1-\left\lVert\varepsilon S_{N}D_{N}^{-1}\right\rVert}<\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon^{1-}}<2,

thus we have

\left\lVert T_{N}^{-1}\right\rVert<2\left\lVert D_{N}^{-1}\right\rVert<4\gamma^{-1}N^{C}<2e^{C(\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{2}}.

Now we consider the off-diagonal decay of
T_{N}^{-1}
. We have

|\varepsilon S_{N}D_{N}^{-1}(x,x^{\prime})|<\varepsilon^{1-}|S_{N}(x,x^{\prime})|<\varepsilon^{1-}\delta_{r}^{-C_{\alpha}}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}.

(5.11)

Thus we have

\begin{split}|(\varepsilon S_{N}D_{N}^{-1})^{j}(x,x^{\prime})|&=\left|\sum\limits_{k_{1},...,k_{j-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}}(\varepsilon S_{N}D_{N}^{-1})(x,k_{1})\cdots(\varepsilon S_{N}D_{N}^{-1})(k_{j-1},x^{\prime})\right|\\
&\leq\sum\limits_{k_{1},...,k_{j-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{b+d}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}j}(\delta_{r}^{-C_{\alpha}})^{j}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}\\
&\leq(2N)^{(b+d)j}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}j}(\delta_{r}^{-C_{\alpha}})^{j}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}\\
&\leq((2N)^{b+d}\delta_{r}^{-C_{\alpha}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}})^{j}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}.\end{split}

Since
N_{r}<e^{C(\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{2}}
, we have

(2N_{r})^{b+d}\delta_{r}^{-C_{\alpha}}\varepsilon^{1/4}<\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{5}},

(5.12)

thus

|(\varepsilon S_{N}D_{N}^{-1})^{j}(x,x^{\prime})|<(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{5}})^{j}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}.

So for
x\neq x^{\prime}
, we have

|T_{N}^{-1}(x,x^{\prime})|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{5}}\cdot 2\gamma^{-1}N^{C}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}<e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}.

(5.13)

Thus, we have the bound (
5.1
), and the measure estimate (
5.4
) is straightforward to verify.

5.2
Polynomials replacement in each step

To estimate the Green functions in the following steps, we must impose further conditions on
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
. To make our techniques work, we need to replace
T_{q}
,
\widehat{F(q)}
and
\widehat{\bar{F}(q)}
by polynomials of
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
at each step. First, the approximate solution
q_{0}
is a rational function of
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
. Actually, it does not depend on
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
r\geq 1
, and suppose the Iterative Lemma holds up to steps
r-1
. Then, there exists a collection
\Lambda_{r-1}^{(1)}
of intervals
I\subset R^{2b}
of size
A^{-\alpha((r+2)^{C_{1}}-(r+2)^{C_{2}})}

and

in

. Furthermore, they satisfy the following inequalities on each

\left\{\begin{split}&|(T_{r-1}-T_{r-1}^{\prime})(x,x^{\prime})|<\epsilon_{r}^{2}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}},\\
&|((F(q_{r-1})-F_{r-1}^{\prime}))^{\land}(x)|<\epsilon_{r}^{2}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x|_{\alpha}},\\
&|\partial(T_{r-1}-T_{r-1}^{\prime})(x,x^{\prime})|<\epsilon_{r}^{2}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}},\\
&|\partial((F(q_{r-1})-F_{r-1}^{\prime}))^{\land}(x)|<\epsilon_{r}^{2}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x|_{\alpha}},\end{split}\right.

(5.14)

where
T_{r-1}:=T_{q_{r-1}}
, and
\partial
refers to
\partial_{\lambda}
or
\partial_{\lambda^{\prime}}
. Here,
C_{1}>C_{2}+2
.
Moreover, each
I\in\Lambda_{r-1}^{(1)}
is contained in an interval
I^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{r-1}
and

\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\Lambda_{r-1}^{(1)}}I=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{r-1}}I^{\prime}.

Proof.

Take
I^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{r-1}
. By our assumptions, we have

\left\lVert q_{r-1}\right\rVert_{\alpha,A^{-(r+1)^{C_{1}}},L_{r-1}}\ll 1\ \ \text{for}\ I^{\prime}\times\mathbb{T}^{b+d}.

(5.15)

Mesh
I^{\prime}
into intervals of size
A^{-\alpha((r+2)^{C_{1}}-(r+2)^{C_{2}})}
. Take an interval
I
from this mesh and assume
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}^{\prime})
is the center of
I
.
Regard
q_{r-1}
as a function defined on
I\times\mathbb{T}^{b+d}
.
From (
5.15
), we have

\left\{\begin{split}&\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1})\right\rVert_{\alpha,A^{-(r+1)^{C_{1}}},L_{r-1}}<C,\\
&\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q^{2}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1})\right\rVert_{\alpha,A^{-(r+1)^{C_{1}}},L_{r-1}}<C,\\
&\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\bar{q}^{2}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1})\right\rVert_{\alpha,A^{-(r+1)^{C_{1}}},L_{r-1}}<C,\\
&\left\lVert\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bar{q}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1})\right\rVert_{\alpha,A^{-(r+1)^{C_{1}}},L_{r-1}}<C.\end{split}\right.

(5.16)

Take
\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}
as an example.
Let
\psi_{p}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}\right)(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
be the Taylor polynomial of degree
p
for
\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1})
centered at
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}^{\prime})
. By Proposition
2.10
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})-\psi_{p}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}\right)(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{r-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(p+1)!^{\alpha-1}(A^{(r+1)^{C_{1}}})^{(p+1)\alpha}(A^{-\alpha((r+2)^{C_{1}}-(r+2)^{C_{2}})})^{p+1}

(5.17)

for

. Taking

\begin{split}\left\lVert\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}-\psi_{p}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}\right)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{r-1}}&\leq C((p+1)^{\alpha-1}A^{-\alpha(C_{1}-2)(r+1)^{C_{1}-1}})^{p+1}\\

&\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{\log A}{\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\alpha(r+1)^{C_{1}-1}2^{r+1}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\\
&\leq\varepsilon^{2^{r+1}}.\end{split}

Thus, we have

\left|\left(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}-\psi_{p}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}\right)\right)^{\land}(x)\right|<\epsilon_{r}^{2}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x|_{\alpha}}.

(5.18)

Moreover, by Proposition
2.2
, we have

\left\lVert\partial\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1})\right\rVert_{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}A^{-(r+1)^{C_{1}}},L_{r-1}}<2^{\alpha}CA^{\alpha(r+1)^{C_{1}}}.

(5.19)

Similarly, we obtain

\begin{split}\left\lVert\partial\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}-\partial\psi_{p}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}\right)\right\rVert_{\alpha,L_{r-1}}&\leq 2CA^{(r+1)^{C_{1}}}A^{-\alpha(C_{1}-3)(r+1)^{C_{1}-1}(p+1)}\\
&\leq 2CA^{(r+1)^{C_{1}}}A^{-\alpha(C_{1}-3)(r+1)^{C_{1}-1}2^{r+1}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\\
&\leq A^{-2^{r+1}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\\
&\leq\varepsilon^{2^{r+1}}.\end{split}

Thus, we have

\left|\partial\left(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}-\psi_{p}(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}})\right)^{\land}(x)\right|<\epsilon_{r}^{2}e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(0.1)}|x|_{\alpha}}.

(5.20)

The remainder of the argument proceeds similarly. Denote the Taylor polynomial of degree
p
for a function

centered at
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}^{\prime})

by
\psi_{p}(h)
.
Let

\begin{split}&\widetilde{T}_{r-1}^{\prime}\\

(5.22)

Then they satisfy (
5.14
).
∎

From now on, we assume that the Iterative Lemma holds up to step

.
We denote

S_{r-1}=\begin{pmatrix}S_{\psi(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q\partial\bar{q}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1}))}&S_{\psi(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial\bar{q}^{2}}(q_{r-1},\bar{q}_{r-1}))}\\

(5.23)

for ease of reference later.

We will replace

and

and

First, we show that the following two inequalities:

|T_{r-1,N_{r}}^{-1}(x,x^{\prime})|<B(r)e^{-\alpha L_{r}^{(1)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}

and

Lemma 5.3
.

\left\{\begin{split}&\left\lVert T_{N}^{-1}\right\rVert<B,\\

Additionally, if condition (

(5.29)

\Lambda_{1}=W_{x}
and
\Lambda_{2}=\Omega\setminus\Lambda_{1}
, then we have

\begin{split}|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|&\leq|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1}\\
w\in\Lambda_{2}\end{subarray}}e^{M_{0}^{\kappa}}e^{M_{0}^{C_{\alpha}}}e^{-L|x-z|_{\alpha}}e^{-L|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|\\
&\leq|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+M_{1}^{b+d}e^{M_{0}^{C_{\alpha}}}\max\limits_{w\in{\Lambda_{2}}}e^{-L|x-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|\\
&\leq|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+\max\limits_{w\in\Lambda_{2}}e^{-(L-\delta_{1})|x-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|,\end{split}

where
\delta_{1}=M_{1}^{-\theta_{3}c-}
. When
|x-y|>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
, the first term vanish, and we get

|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|<\max\limits_{w\in\Omega\setminus W_{x}}e^{-(L-\delta)|x-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|.

(6.11)

Clearly,
|x-w|>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
.

Case 2
:
\text{dist }(x,\Omega_{1})<M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
.

If this case happens,
x
must be in some
\widetilde{\Omega}_{1,\beta}
. Let
\Lambda_{1}=\widetilde{\Omega}_{1,\beta}
and
\Lambda_{2}=\Omega\setminus\Lambda_{1}
, we have

\begin{split}|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|&\leq|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1},w\in\Lambda_{2}\end{subarray}}|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,z)||T(z,w)||G_{\Omega}(w,y)|\\
&\leq|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1},w\in\Lambda_{2}\end{subarray}}e^{M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}e^{-L|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|\\
&\leq|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+M_{1}^{b+d}e^{M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1},w\in\Lambda_{2}\end{subarray}}e^{-L|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|\\
&\leq|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+e^{2M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1},w\in\Lambda_{2}\end{subarray}}e^{-L|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|.\end{split}

Subcase 2.1
.
\text{dist }(w,\Omega_{1,\beta})>2M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
.

In this case, since
\textup{dist}(w,\widetilde{\Omega}_{1,\beta})>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
, it follows that
|z-w|>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
. Therefore, we have

e^{2M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}e^{-\delta_{2}|z-w|_{\alpha}}<1,

where
\delta_{2}=M_{1}^{\theta_{4}-\theta_{3}c}
, given that
\theta_{4}<\theta_{3}c
. Thus we obtain

|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|\leq|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}R=|z-w|>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\\
z\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{1,\beta},w\in\Omega\setminus\widetilde{\Omega}_{1,\beta}\end{subarray}}e^{-(L-\delta_{2})|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|.

(6.12)

When
|x-y|>2M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}
, the first term vanishes.

Subcase 2.2
.
\text{dist }(w,\Omega_{1,\beta})\leq 2M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
.

In this case, since
\text{dist }(\Omega_{1,\beta},\Omega_{1,\beta^{\prime}})>M_{1}^{\theta_{2}}
for
\beta\neq\beta^{\prime}
, we have
\text{dist }(w,\Omega_{1})>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
, so
G_{\Omega}(w,y)
can be estimated as in Case 1. We have

\begin{split}|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+e^{2M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1},w\in\Lambda_{2}\\
\text{dist}(w,\Omega_{1,\beta})\leq 2M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\end{subarray}}e^{-L|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|\\
\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+e^{2M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1},w\in\Lambda_{2}\\
\text{dist}(w,\Omega_{1,\beta})\leq 2M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\end{subarray}}e^{-L|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{W_{w}}(w,y)|\\
&+e^{2M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1},w\in\Lambda_{2}\\
\text{dist}(w,\Omega_{1,\beta})\leq 2M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\end{subarray}}e^{-L|z-w|_{\alpha}}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}w^{\prime}\in\Omega\setminus W_{w}\end{subarray}}e^{-(L-\delta_{1})|w-w^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w^{\prime},y)|\\
\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+e^{2M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}|G_{W_{w}}(w,y)|+e^{2M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{dist }(w,\widetilde{\Omega}_{1,\beta})<M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\\
|w^{\prime}-w|>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\end{subarray}}e^{-(L-\delta_{1})|w-w^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w^{\prime},y)|\\
\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+e^{2M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}|G_{W_{w}}(w,y)|+\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{dist }(w,\widetilde{\Omega}_{1,\beta})<M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\\
|w^{\prime}-w|>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\end{subarray}}e^{-(L-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})|w-w^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w^{\prime},y)|.\end{split}

When
|x-y|>M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}+3M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
, the first two terms vanish. In general, when
|x-y|>2M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}
, the first two terms vanish, i.e.,

|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|\leq\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{1,\beta}\\
|w^{\prime}-z|>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\end{subarray}}e^{-(L-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})|z-w^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w^{\prime},y)|.

(6.13)

Note that when
|x-y|>2M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}
, the first one or two terms vanish in all cases. In fact, we can apply Case 2.2 to replace all cases, so we have

|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|\leq 2e^{3M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}+\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|z-x|<2M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}\\
|w-z|>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\end{subarray}}e^{-(L-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|.

(6.14)

When
|x-y|>2M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}
, the first term vanishes:

|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|\leq\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|z-x|<2M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}\\
|w-z|>M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\end{subarray}}e^{-(L-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Omega}(w,y)|.

(6.15)

From (
6.14
), we obtain

|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|<2e^{3M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}+\frac{1}{2}\max|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|,

(6.16)

thus

\max|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|<4e^{3M_{1}^{\theta_{4}}}.

Now we consider the off-diagonal decay. Assume that
|x-y|>M_{1}^{\theta}
. Our goal is to achieve the desired off-diagonal decay by iteratively applying inequality (
6.15
).
The iteration process continues until the distance between
w
and
y
becomes less than
2M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}
, which serves as the termination condition.

In each iteration, the distance between
w
and
y
decreases relative to the distance between
x
and
y
. Specifically, from (
6.15
), we observe that when the distance between
w
and
y
in all dimensions decreases by
2M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}
, we obtain a decay factor of at least
e^{-M_{1}^{\theta_{3}c}}
.
We will analyze the decay in each dimension separately.

Suppose that, in (
6.15
), for some
j
, the distance
|z_{j}-w_{j}|<M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
is repeated
M_{1}^{\theta-\theta_{1}-\tilde{\delta}}
times for a small
\tilde{\delta}
. After
M_{1}^{\theta-\theta_{1}-\tilde{\delta}}
iterations, the repeated application of (
6.15
) leads to a decay of at least
e^{-M_{1}^{\theta-\theta_{1}-\tilde{\delta}}\cdot M_{1}^{\theta_{3}c}}
, which simplifies to
e^{-(b+d)LM_{1}^{c}}
under the condition
\theta-\theta_{1}-\tilde{\delta}+\theta_{3}c>c
.

Next, consider the case where
|z_{j}-w_{j}|<M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}
occurs fewer than
M_{1}^{\theta-\theta_{1}-\tilde{\delta}}
times. In this case, for every dimension, there is at most a loss of distance of
M_{1}^{\theta-\tilde{\delta}}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that the distance between
x
and
y
in all dimension (i.e.,
|x_{j}-y_{j}|
) is greater than
M_{1}^{\theta-\tilde{\delta}/2}
. Otherwise, the dimension with the greatest distance will provide the decay for those dimensions where the distance is less than
M_{1}^{\theta-\tilde{\delta}/2}
, with a small reduction in
L
by
O(M_{1}^{-c\tilde{\delta}})
.

Let us now focus on each individual dimension. Take
x_{1}
,
y_{1}
for example.
Let
R
denote the distance that contributes to the decay, then we have

R+M_{1}^{\theta-\tilde{\delta}}\geq|x_{1}-y_{1}|.

We refer to the distance that contributes to the decay as the decay distance.
For
1\leq j\leq R/M_{1}^{\theta}
, denote by
l_{j}R
the sum of the decay distances provided by (
6.15
) where
jM_{1}^{\theta_{1}}\leq|z_{1}-w_{1}|<(j+1)M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}
. Denote by
l_{0}R
the sum of the decay distances provided by (
6.15
) where
M_{1}^{\theta_{3}}\leq|z_{1}-w_{1}|<M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}
.
Clearly, we have

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{R/M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}}l_{j}\geq 1.

Thus, the total decay across iteration is greater than

\begin{split}\frac{l_{0}R}{3M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}}M_{1}^{\theta_{3}c}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{R/M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}}\frac{l_{j}R}{(j+3)M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}}j^{c}M_{1}^{\theta_{1}c}&\geq\sum\limits_{j=0}^{R/M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}}l_{j}(1-O(M_{1}^{-\theta+\frac{3\tilde{\delta}}{4}+\theta_{1}}))R^{c}\\
&\geq(1-O(M_{1}^{-\theta+\frac{3\tilde{\delta}}{4}+\theta_{1}}))R^{c}\\
&\geq(1-O(M_{1}^{-\theta+\frac{3\tilde{\delta}}{4}+\theta_{1}}))\left(1-\frac{M_{1}^{c(\theta_{1}-\tilde{\delta})}}{|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{c}}\right)|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{c}\\
&\geq(1-O(M_{1}^{-c\tilde{\delta}/2}))|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{c}.\end{split}

The inequality above relies on the facts that

\frac{R}{3M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}}M_{1}^{\theta_{3}c}\geq R^{c},

(6.17)

and

\frac{j^{c}R}{(j+3)M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}}M_{1}^{\theta_{1}c}\geq(1-O(M_{1}^{-\theta+\frac{3\tilde{\delta}}{4}+\theta_{1}}))R^{c},

(6.18)

under the assumption

c<\frac{\theta-\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{2}-\theta_{1}}{\theta-\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{2}-\theta_{3}}.

The inequality (
6.18
) follows from

\frac{j}{j+3}\left(\frac{R}{jM_{1}^{\theta_{1}}}\right)^{1-c}\geq 1-O(M_{1}^{-\theta+\frac{3\tilde{\delta}}{4}+\theta_{1}}),

(6.19)

which holds for all
j
.
Actually, when
j\geq\frac{R}{M_{1}^{\theta_{1}+\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{4}}}
, we have

\frac{j}{j+3}\left(\frac{R}{jM_{1}^{\theta_{1}}}\right)^{1-c}\geq 1-O(M_{1}^{-\theta+\frac{3\tilde{\delta}}{4}+\theta_{1}}),

and when
j<\frac{R}{M_{1}^{\theta_{1}+\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{4}}}
, we have

\frac{j}{j+3}\left(\frac{R}{jM_{1}^{\theta_{1}}}\right)^{1-c}>1.

(6.20)

We conclude that

|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|<e^{-(L-O(M_{1}^{-c\tilde{\delta}/2}))|x-y|_{\alpha}},\textup{ for }|x-y|>M_{1}^{\theta}.

(6.21)

Finally, note that we have refreshed the parameter
L
earlier. Hence, we obtain the final off-diagonal decay result:

|G_{\Omega}(x,y)|<e^{-(L-2M_{0}^{\max\{\iota,\kappa,\theta c\}-c})|x-y|_{\alpha}},\textup{ for }|x-y|>M_{1}^{\theta}.

(6.22)

This complete the proof.
∎

Now we give another coupling lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume that
T
satisfies the off-diagonal estimate

|T(x,x^{\prime})|<e^{-L|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}},\ \ x\neq x^{\prime}.

(6.23)

Let
N=A^{r+1}
(where
r\gtrsim\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
),
M=(\log N)^{C_{6}}
, and
0<\kappa,\theta<1
. Denote by
T_{A^{r}}
the restriction of
T
to
\{|x|<A^{r}\}
. The constants below satisfy the following conditions:

\kappa<c,\ cC_{6}>4,\ C_{6}(c-\kappa)>3,\ C_{6}c(1-\theta)>3,\ 2cC_{6}<C_{3}-1,\ C_{3}>10.

Additionally, assume that:

•

For
|x|,|x^{\prime}|<A^{r}
, we have

|G_{A^{r}}(x,x^{\prime})|<A^{r^{C_{3}}}e^{-L|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}.

(6.24)

•

For
|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}A^{r}
, let
W_{x}
be the
2M
-interval centered at
x
(if the
2M
-interval centered at
x
is not contained within
[-N,N]^{b+d}
, let
W_{x}
be the
2M
-interval whose boundary is as far as possible from
x
). We assume the following holds:

\left\{\begin{split}&\left\lVert G_{W_{x}}\right\rVert<e^{M^{\kappa}},\\
&|G_{W_{x}}(\eta,\eta^{\prime})|<e^{-L|\eta-\eta^{\prime}|_{\alpha}},\ \ |\eta-\eta^{\prime}|>M^{\theta}.\end{split}\right.

(6.25)

Then we have

|G_{N}(x,x^{\prime})|<A^{(r+1)^{C_{3}}}e^{-(L-O(r^{-3}))|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}}.

(6.26)

Proof.

Denote
\Lambda=[-N,N]^{b+d}
and let
x,y\in\Lambda
.

Case 1
:
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}A^{r}
.

Let
\Lambda_{1}=[-A^{r},A^{r}]^{b+d}
and
\Lambda_{2}=\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{1}
. We have

\begin{split}|G_{\Lambda}(x,y)|\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1}\\
w\in\Lambda_{2}\end{subarray}}|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,z)||T(z,w)||G_{\Lambda}(w,y)|\\
\leq&|G_{\Lambda}(x,y)|+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1}\\
w\in\Lambda_{2}\end{subarray}}A^{r^{C_{3}}}e^{-L|x-z|_{\alpha}}e^{-L|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Lambda}(w,y)|\\
\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+N^{b+d}A^{r^{C_{3}}}\max\limits_{w\in\Lambda_{2}}e^{-L|x-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Lambda}(w,y)|.\end{split}

Obviously, in this case,
|w-x|>\frac{1}{2}A^{r}
, thus we have

|G_{\Lambda}(x,y)|\leq|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+\max_{w\in\Lambda_{2}}e^{-(L-O(A^{-r\cdot c-}))|x-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Lambda}(w,y)|.

(6.27)

Case 2
:
|x|>\frac{1}{2}A^{r}
.

Let
\Lambda_{1}=W_{x}
and
\Lambda_{2}=\Lambda\setminus W_{x}
. We have

\begin{split}|G_{\Lambda}(x,y)|\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1}\\
w\in\Lambda_{2}\end{subarray}}|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,z)||T(z,w)||G_{\Lambda}(w,y)|\\
\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Lambda_{1}\\
w\in\Lambda_{2}\end{subarray}}e^{M^{\kappa}+M^{\theta c}}e^{-L|x-z|_{\alpha}}e^{-L|z-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Lambda}(w,y)|\\
\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+N^{b+d}e^{M^{\kappa}+M^{\theta c}}\max\limits_{w\in\Lambda_{2}}e^{-L|x-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Lambda}(w,y)|\\
\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+e^{(b+d)\log N+M^{\kappa}+M^{\theta c}}\max\limits_{w\in\Lambda_{2}}e^{-L|x-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Lambda}(w,y)|\\
\leq&|G_{\Lambda_{1}}(x,y)|+\max\limits_{w\in\Lambda_{2}}e^{-(L-O(r^{-3}))|x-w|_{\alpha}}|G_{\Lambda}(w,y)|.\end{split}

When
|x-y|>M
, the first term vanishes.

Combining Case 1 and Case 2, we get

|G_{\Lambda}(x,y)|\leq\max\{A^{r^{C_{3}}},e^{M^{\kappa}}\}+\frac{1}{2}\max\limits_{w}|G_{\Lambda}(w,y)|.

(6.28)

Therefore, we obtain

\max_{x,y}|G_{\Lambda}(x,y)|\leq 2\max\{A^{r^{C_{3}}},e^{M^{\kappa}}\}.

(6.29)

Note that Case 1 is carried out no more than
2A
times; otherwise,
2A\cdot\frac{1}{2}A^{r}=N
provides sufficient off-diagonal decay.
Thus, for
x,y\in\Lambda
, we have

|G_{\Lambda}(x,y)|\leq Ae^{-(L-O(r^{-3}))|x-y|_{\alpha}}A^{r^{C_{3}}+M^{\kappa}+M^{c}}.

(6.30)

Since
2\kappa C_{6}<C_{3}-1
,
2cC_{6}<C_{3}-1
and
C_{3}>10
, we have
r^{C_{3}}+M^{\kappa}+M^{c}<r^{C_{3}}+2(r+1)^{C_{3}-1}<(r+1)^{C_{3}}
for
r\gtrsim\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
.
Thus, we conclude

|G_{\Lambda}(x,y)|\leq A^{(r+1)^{C_{3}}}e^{-(L-O(r^{-3}))|x-y|_{\alpha}},

(6.31)

for
x,y\in\Lambda
.
∎

The matrices
T_{N}^{\sigma}
and its estimation

r-1
. To control the Green function through a multi-scale analysis, we introduce a matrix
T_{r-1}^{\prime\sigma}

\sigma

T_{r-1}^{\prime}

T_{r-1}

T_{r-1}^{\sigma}

0&-k\cdot\lambda^{\prime}-\sigma-\mu_{-n}\end{pmatrix}

(6.33)

and

is the matrix obtained in Lemma
5.2
. Clearly,

is defined on
\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\Lambda_{r-1}^{(1)}}I

(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\sigma)
. Denote
D_{\pm,n,k}^{\sigma}=\pm(k\cdot\lambda^{\prime}+\sigma)-\mu_{\pm n}

For
M_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
Q
an
M_{0}
-interval in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

the restriction of
T_{l}^{\sigma}
to

and

. Observe that we have

|D_{\pm,n,k}^{\sigma}|>|\mu_{\pm n}-|k\cdot\lambda^{\prime}+\sigma||.

(6.34)

Note that for
n\notin\{n_{1},...,n_{b}\}
, we have
\mu_{n}=|n|^{2}
, and for
n\in\{n_{1},...,n_{b}\}

\mu_{n}=|n|^{2}+O(1)

Hence, if
|D_{\pm,n,k}^{\sigma}|<C(M_{0})

||n|^{2}-|n^{\prime}|^{2}|<C|k-k^{\prime}|+2C(M_{0})+C.

(6.35)

We will use the following arithmetical lemma, see
[
Bou05
]
for a proof.

Lemma 6.3
.

(6.37)

We also impose a Diophantine condition:

(6.38)

where
\gamma
is the same constant as in (
5.8
), and

. We refer to this condition as

.

Without loss of generality, assume that for

in Lemma

[\exp\exp(\log l)^{1/4}]^{-1}

Lemma 6.4
.

There exist constants
\rho
,
\kappa
,

,
C_{7}

\frac{2}{3}\rho<c<\theta-2\rho C_{0}d,\ c_{2}=\frac{\rho^{2}}{2},\ \kappa=\frac{3}{4}\rho^{2},\ C_{7}>\frac{2}{c_{2}},\ \frac{2}{c_{2}}(\max\{\frac{c_{2}}{2},\kappa,\theta c\}-c)<-3.

Assume the Iterative Lemma holds up to step
r-1
.
Consider
T_{l}^{\sigma}
(

) defined on
\mathop{\cup}\limits_{I\in\Lambda_{l}^{(1)}}I
.

Let
M_{0}
be a large integer, and let

be an
M_{0}
-interval in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfying

\min\limits_{n\in Q}|n|>M_{0}.

(6.39)

Then,
there exists a system of Lipschitz functions
\sigma_{s}=\sigma_{s}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}),s<e^{(\log M_{0})^{C_{7}}}
defined on
I_{0}\times I_{0}
(depending on
Q
), such that

\left\lVert\sigma_{s}\right\rVert_{Lip}\leq CM_{0}.

(6.40)

For
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\in I
such that
\lambda^{\prime}
satisfies
DC_{M_{0}}
, we have

\left\lVert G_{l,M_{0},Q}^{\sigma}\right\rVert<e^{M_{0}^{\kappa}}

(6.41)

and

|G_{l,M_{0},Q}^{\sigma}(x,x^{\prime})|<e^{-\alpha L_{l+1}^{(0.3)}|x-x^{\prime}|_{\alpha}},\ \ |x-x^{\prime}|>M_{0}^{\theta},

(6.42)

provided that

\min\limits_{s}|\sigma-\sigma_{s}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})|>e^{-M_{0}^{c_{2}}}.

(6.43)

Appendix A
A technical proposition about the reality of Fourier coefficients

Proposition A.1

(
Reality
)
.

Let
f\in C^{1}([-1,1])
be real-valued, and let
q
be a function defined on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, with
\hat{q}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Suppose
\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}
is small enough, then
(f(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}))^{\land}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Proof.

Since
\hat{q}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
, we know that
(q\bar{q})^{\land}(k)=(\hat{q}*\hat{\bar{q}})(k)\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\chi
be a smooth bump function such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi=1
on
[-\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4}]
and
\chi=0
outside
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
. If
\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}
is small enough, then
(f\cdot\chi)(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})=f(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})
. So without loss of generality, we can assume
f
is periodic with period
1
. Since
f
is real-valued, it is easy to check that the Fourier series of
f
has the following form:

f(x)=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}l_{j}\cos 2\pi jx+m_{j}\sin 2\pi jx,

where
l_{j},m_{j}(j\in\mathbb{Z})
are real.
Let
f_{n}(x)=\sum\limits_{\left\lvert j\right\rvert\leq n}l_{j}\cos 2\pi jx+m_{j}\sin 2\pi jx
. Since
f\in C^{1}([-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}])
,
f_{n}
converges to
f
uniformly.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we have

\begin{split}\left\lvert(f(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}))^{\land}(k)-(f_{n}(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}))^{\land}(k)\right\rvert&=\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(f(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})-f_{n}(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}))e^{-2\pi ikx}dx\right\rvert\\
&\leq\int\left\lvert f(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})-f_{n}(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2})\right\rvert dx\\
&\leq\mathop{\textup{sup}}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left\lvert f(\left\lvert q(x)\right\rvert^{2})-f_{n}(\left\lvert q(x)\right\rvert^{2})\right\rvert\\
&\leq\mathop{\textup{sup}}\limits_{s\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]}\left\lvert f(s)-f_{n}(s)\right\rvert.\end{split}

Thus,
(f_{n}(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}))^{\land}(k)
converges to
(f(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}))^{\land}(k)
as
n\to+\infty
for all
k
. Therefore, it suffices to show that
(f_{n}(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}))^{\land}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
k
.

To prove
(f_{n}(\left\lvert q\right\rvert^{2}))^{\land}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
k
, it suffices to show that
(\cos 2\pi j|q|^{2})^{\land}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
and
(\sin 2\pi j|q|^{2})^{\land}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
k
.
Take
\cos 2\pi j|q|^{2}
for example. We have

\cos 2\pi j|q|^{2}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}(2\pi j|q|^{2})^{2n}}{(2n)!}.

(A.1)

Since
(|q|^{4n})^{\land}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
n
and
k
, we have
(\cos 2\pi j|q|^{2})^{\land}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
for all
k
. Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

Semi-local behaviour of non-local hypoelliptic equations: Boltzmann
Amélie Loher

Abstract
. The purpose of this note is to demonstrate the announced result in [ 12 ] by filling the gap in the proof sketch. We prove the semi-local Strong Harnack inequality for the Boltzmann equation for moderately soft potentials without cutoff assumption. The non-local operator in the Boltzmann equation is in non-divergence form, and thus the method developed in [ 14 ] does not apply. However, we exploit that the Boltzmann equation is on average in divergence form, and we show that the non-divergent part of the collision operator is of lower order in a suitable sense, which proves to be sufficient to deduce the Strong Harnack inequality. Consequentially, we derive upper and lower bounds on the fundamental solution of the linearised Boltzmann equation.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this article is to establish the Strong Harnack inequality for the Boltzmann equation for moderately soft potentials without cutoff assumption.
The Boltzmann equation models the dynamics of a dilute gas. It is given by

(1.1)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=Q(f,f),

where
f:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
encodes the density of the gas particles, which at any given time
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
have location
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and velocity
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The right hand side
Q
denotes the Boltzmann collision operator, whose explicit form is given by

(1.2)

Q(f,f)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left[f(w_{*})f(w)-f(v_{*})f(v)\right]B(\left|v-v_{*}\right|,\cos\theta)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}v_{*},

where
v,v_{*}
are the post-collisional velocities, and
w,w_{*}
the pre-collisional velocities, so that

w=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{\left|v-v_{*}\right|}{2}\sigma,\quad w_{*}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{\left|v-v_{*}\right|}{2}\sigma.

The rate of change in velocities is determined through the cross-section
B
, which reads

(1.3)

B(r,\cos\theta)=r^{\gamma}b(\cos\theta),\quad b(\cos\theta)\approx\left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right|^{-(d-1)-2s},

with parameters
\gamma\in(-d,1]
and
s\in(0,1)
, and where
\cos\theta
is defined as

\cos\theta:=\frac{v-v_{*}}{\left|v-v_{*}\right|}\cdot\sigma,\quad\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right):=\frac{w-v}{\left|w-v\right|}\cdot\sigma.

We restrict our analysis to moderately soft potentials, which means we consider the range of parameters
0\leq\gamma+2s\leq 2
throughout this article, and moreover, we are interested in long-range interactions, which occur when we do not cut off the singularity in the kernel at sufficiently small deviation angles
\theta
.

1.1.
Boltzmann collision operator

The Boltzmann equation is a kinetic integro-differential equation with a bounded, non-negative source term. To see this, we split the collision operator
Q
into two parts

Q(f,f)=Q_{1}(f,f)+Q_{2}(f,f),

where

\displaystyle Q_{1}(f,f)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(w_{*})\left[f(w)-f(v)\right]B(\left|v-v_{*}\right|,\cos\theta)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}v_{*},

and

\displaystyle Q_{2}(f,f)=f(v)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left[f(w_{*})-f(v_{*})\right]B(\left|v-v_{*}\right|,\cos\theta)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}v_{*}\right).

Then, as shown in
[
15
, Lemma 4.1]
,
Q_{1}
can be rewritten as

Q_{1}(f,f)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left[f(t,x,w)-f(t,x,v)\right]K_{f}(t,x,v,w)\,{\rm d}w,

where the kernel
K_{f}
depends implicitly on the solution
f
and is given by

(1.4)

K_{f}(v,w)=2^{d-1}\left|w-v\right|^{-1}\int_{w^{\prime}\perp w-v}f(v+w^{\prime})B(r,\cos\theta)r^{-d+2}\,{\rm d}w^{\prime},

with
r^{2}=\left|w-v\right|^{2}+\left|w^{\prime}\right|^{2}=\left|v-v_{*}\right|^{2}
and
\cos\theta=\frac{w^{\prime}-(v-w)}{\left|w^{\prime}-(v-w)\right|}\cdot\frac{w^{\prime}+(w-v)}{\left|w^{\prime}+(w-v)\right|}
,
w_{*}=v+w^{\prime}
, and
v_{*}=w+w^{\prime}
. The proof is based on a change of variables. In particular,
Q_{1}
is a non-local operator with a non-negative kernel
K_{f}
.

Moreover, the term
Q_{2}
can be viewed as a non-negative source term due to cancellation effects.
It is well known in the kinetic literature, as stated for example in
[
15
, Lemma 5.1]
, that
Q_{2}
is of the form

Q_{2}(f,f)=f(v)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(v-w^{\prime})\tilde{B}(\left|w^{\prime}\right|)\,{\rm d}w^{\prime}\right),

where

(1.5)

\tilde{B}(r):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left(\frac{2^{\frac{d}{2}}}{(1-\sigma\cdot e)^{\frac{d}{2}}}B\left(\frac{\sqrt{r}}{\sqrt{1-\sigma\cdot e}},\cos\theta\right)-B(r,\cos\theta)\right)\,{\rm d}\sigma>0.

Here
e
is any unit vector, and
\cos\theta=\sigma\cdot e
.

1.1.1.
Integro-differential equation with non-negative source

We have therefore derived the integro-differential form of the Boltzmann equation with a non-negative source term: if
f
solves (
1.1
), then

(1.6)

\partial_{t}f(t,x,v)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(f(t,x,w)-f(t,x,v)\big)K_{f}(t,x,v,w)\,{\rm d}w+\Lambda_{f}f(t,x,v),

where
K_{f}
is given by (
1.4
), and
\Lambda_{f}
is determined by

(1.7)

\Lambda_{f}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(v-w^{\prime})\tilde{B}(\left|w^{\prime}\right|)\,{\rm d}w^{\prime}\geq 0,

where
\tilde{B}
is defined in (
1.5
).

1.1.2.
Integro-differential equation in divergence form on average

There is, however, also another way we can think of the Boltzmann equation, which proves to be useful for the purpose of this article. If we reconsider the term
Q_{2}
, then we realise that the change of variables according to
[
6
, Lemma A.9]
together with (
1.4
) yields

(1.8)

\displaystyle Q_{2}(f,f)

\displaystyle=f(v)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\big(f(w_{*})-f(v_{*})\big)B(\left|v-v_{*}\right|,\cos\theta)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}v_{*}\right)

\displaystyle=f(v)\left(2^{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{w^{\prime}\perp w-v}\left|w-v\right|^{-1}B(r,\cos\theta)r^{-d+2}\big(f(v+w^{\prime})-f(w+w^{\prime})\big)\,{\rm d}w^{\prime}\,{\rm d}w\right)

\displaystyle=f(v)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(K_{f}(v,w)-K_{f}(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\right)

Thus any solution of (
1.1
) satisfies

(1.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(t,x,v)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(f(t,x,w)-f(t,x,v)\big)K_{f}(t,x,v,w)\,{\rm d}w

\displaystyle+f(t,x,v)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(K_{f}(t,x,v,w)-K_{f}(t,x,w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(f(t,x,w)-f(t,x,v)\big)\big(K_{f}(t,x,v,w)+K_{f}(t,x,w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(f(t,x,w)+f(t,x,v)\big)\big(K_{f}(t,x,v,w)-K_{f}(t,x,w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w.

The strength of this reformulation (
1.9
) is the emphasis on the fact that
on average, the Boltzmann equation is an equation in divergence form
.

1.2.
Conditional regime

In either formulation, (
1.6
) and (
1.9
), we see that the coefficients
K_{f}
(
1.4
) and
\Lambda_{f}
(
1.7
) depend implicitly on the solution, stressing the non-linear character of the equation. Without any further assumptions on the solution itself, it seems out of reach to treat the non-linearity in the equation. In order to make quantitative statements on the coefficients, we need to work in a conditional regime
[
7
]
. We thus assume throughout this article that the solutions we consider satisfy the following hydrodynamic bounds: for any
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, there exists
m_{0},M_{0},E_{0},H_{0}
, such that

(1.10)

\displaystyle 0<m_{0}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v\leq M_{0},

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t,x,v)\left|v\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}v\leq E_{0},

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t,x,v)\log f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v\leq H_{0}.

In other words, the mass, the energy and the entropy are bounded. Then, Silvestre showed
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
, that there is a constant
C_{0}>0
depending only on
m_{0},M_{0},E_{0},H_{0}
such that

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C_{0}.

This conditional a priori bound on the solution enables us to quantify an ellipticity class on the coefficients
K_{f}
and
\Lambda_{f}
, and thus the non-linearity is treated by absorbing it in the macroscopic quantities. Let us remark that this conditional regime, even though it involves physically meaningful quantities, it is in our opinion too much to ask for it to be mathematically justifiable in the space-inhomogeneous case, since these are pointwise assumptions in the space variable
x
.

1.3.
Main result

In this conditional regime, a series of works by Imbert-Silvestre(-Mouhot)
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
5
]
showed first that any solution of (
1.1
) satisfies the Weak Harnack inequality, and is thus Hölder continuous
[
6
]
, then that the regularity of the kernel
K
can be transferred onto the solution in the sense of Schauder estimates
[
8
]
, and finally that these local results can be globalised via a change of variables
[
7
, Section 5]
due to the decay properties of the solution
[
5
]
, so that eventually the Schauder estimates can be bootstrapped to obtain smooth solutions
[
7
]
. Later a constructive proof of the Weak Harnack inequality and the Schauder estimates appeared in
[
13
,
11
]
. One of the difficulties posed by (
1.1
) is its non-local operator, which means that the behaviour of the solution inside a given domain is affected by the values attained in the whole velocity space. In contrast to equations with a local diffusion operator, we cannot deduce from these Hölder estimates a bound on the local supremum of the solution in terms of the local infimum. Such a bound is known as the Strong Harnack inequality. The problem is that the behaviour of the tail is encoded in the constant appearing in the bound. Even though it is known that a local Strong Harnack inequality holds for parabolic non-local equations
[
16
]
, it turns out to fail in the kinetic case
[
10
]
. The combination of the kinetic transport operator with a fractional diffusion affects the local supremum. It was crucial for their counterexample that the equation was satisfied in a bounded velocity domain. We showed in
[
14
]
, however, that a local Harnack inequality without tail terms holds for
global
solutions to non-local kinetic equations in divergence form, that is we need the equation to be satisfied for velocities in the whole space.
Here we show how to obtain the Strong Harnack inequality for global solutions to the Boltzmann equation. We think assuming the equation to be satisfied globally in
v
is reasonable from a physical point of view, since velocities of gas particles are vector fields defined on the whole space.

Theorem 1.1

(Strong Harnack for Boltzmann)
.

Let
T>0
. Suppose
0\leq f
satisfies (
1.10
).
Let
f
solve (
1.1
) in
(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in the sense of Definition
2.1
with a cross section
B
given by (
1.3
) with
0\leq\gamma+2s\leq 2
, and with initial data
f_{0}(x,v)=f(0,x,v)
, which in case that
\gamma\leq 0
is supposed to admit for any
q>0
a constant
N_{q}>0
such that
f_{0}(x,v)\leq N_{q}(1+\left|v\right|)^{-q}
for all
(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then for any
0<r_{0}<\frac{1}{6}
, there is
C>0
such that

\sup_{(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\times Q^{t}_{\frac{r_{0}}{4}}}f\leq C\inf_{(\tau_{2},\tau_{3})\times Q^{t}_{\frac{r_{0}}{4}}}f,

where
0<\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\tau_{3}
such that
\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}=\tau_{2}-\tau_{3}=\big(\frac{r_{0}}{4}\big)^{2s}
and
\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}\geq r_{0}^{2s}
, and where
Q^{t}_{\frac{r_{0}}{4}}:=\big\{(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}:\left|x\right|<\big(\frac{r_{0}}{4}\big)^{1+2s},\left|v\right|<\frac{r_{0}}{4}\big\}
is the kinetic cylinder sliced in time.
The constant
C
depends only on
s,d,\gamma,m_{0},M_{0},E_{0},H_{0}
, and in case that
\gamma\leq 0
, it depends additionally on
N_{q}
.

This is the first time a Strong Harnack inequality for the Boltzmann equation has been derived. Moreover, we can deduce consequences on bounds on the fundamental solution, similar to
[
14
, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.3]
. We refer the reader to Section
5
.

2.
Setup

We first describe the notion of solutions that we work with.

2.1.
Notion of solutions

Since we require further regularity properties of the solution to deduce that the skew-symmetric part of
K_{f}
is of lower order in the sense of (
2.7
), we need to work with the following notion of solutions.

Definition 2.1

(Solutions)
.

We say that
f:\big(0,T\big)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a solution of (
1.1
), if

(i)

f
is non-negative everywhere,

(ii)

f
solves (
1.1
) classically for every
(t,x,v)
,

(iii)

f\in C^{\infty}
in time, space, and velocity,

(iv)

For each value of
(t,x)
the function
f
decays rapidly as
\left|v\right|\to\infty
, that is for any
q>0

\lim_{\left|v\right|\to\infty}\frac{f(t,x,v)}{(1+\left|v\right|)^{q}}=0

locally uniformly in
(t,x)
.

(v)

f
is periodic in
x
,

(vi)

f
conserves mass:

\forall 0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T:\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}f(t_{2})\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}f(t_{1})\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v.

This notion of solutions was also used by Imbert-Silvestre in
[
7
]
.

For any solution in the sense of Definition
2.1
satisfying the assumption on the macroscopic quantities (
1.10
), we can quantify a notion of ellipticity on the coefficients.

2.2.
Ellipticity class

For any
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we can show that there exists
\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},\mu_{0}>0
depending on
\gamma,s,d,m_{0},M_{0},E_{0},H_{0}
such that
K_{f}
given in (
1.4
) and
\Lambda_{f}
given in (
1.7
) satisfy the following statements.

We start by noting that
\Lambda_{f}
given in (
1.7
) is non-negative, and can be bounded by a constant depending only on
\gamma,s,d
and the macroscopic bounds (
1.10
), that is

(2.1)

0\leq\Lambda_{f}\leq\Lambda_{0}.

Concerning
K_{f}
, first, we assume a weak form of coercivity: for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
there holds

(2.2)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(\varphi(v)-\varphi(w)\big)^{2}K_{f}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v\geq\lambda_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\left|\varphi(v)-\varphi(w)\right|^{2}}{\left|v-w\right|^{d+2s}}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}w.

Second, we assume an upper bound on average: for any
r>0

(2.3)

\forall v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\quad\int_{B_{r}(v)}K_{f}(v,w)\left|v-w\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}w\leq\Lambda_{0}r^{2-2s}.

Note, in particular, that this assumption is equivalent to

(2.4)

\forall v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(v)}K_{f}(v,w)\left|v-w\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}w\leq\Lambda_{0}r^{-2s}.

Third, we discuss the symmetry of the kernel. Note that the kernel
K_{f}
satisfies a pointwise symmetry of the form
K_{f}(v,v+w)=K_{f}(v,v-w)
.
This symmetry assumption gives rise to a non-local operator in non-divergence form. However, the kernel
does not satisfy a pointwise divergence form symmetry
:
K_{f}(v,w)=K_{f}(w,v)
. This implies that the non-local operator with kernel
K_{f}
is not self-adjoint. It does, however, satisfy a weak divergence form symmetry:

(2.5)

\forall v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\quad\Bigg|\textrm{PV}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(K_{f}(v,w)-K_{f}(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg|\leq\Lambda_{0},

and if
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
we assume that for all
r>0

(2.6)

\forall v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\quad\Bigg\lvert\textrm{PV}\int_{B_{r}(v)}(v-w)\big(K_{f}(v,w)-K_{f}(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg\rvert\leq\Lambda_{0}r^{1-2s}.

These conditions mean that the anti-symmetric part is bounded.
It seems that conditions (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) are not sufficient to deduce our main theorem. We also need to assume that the anti-symmetric part is of lower order with respect to the symmetric part: for any
0<r\leq 1
there is
\alpha>s
such that

(2.7)

\sup_{v\in B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}\frac{\left|K_{f}(v,w)-K_{f}(w,v)\right|^{2}}{(K_{f}(v,w)+K_{f}(w,v))}\,{\rm d}w\leq\mu_{0}r^{2(\alpha-s)}.

This condition has been used to derive Hölder continuity of solutions to parabolic non-local equations by Kassmann-Weidner
[
9
]
.

The assumptions (
2.1
)-(
2.7
) are satisfied for any solution
f
of (
1.1
) in
(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, such that (
1.10
) holds, and in case that
\gamma\leq 0
, provided that for any
q>0
there is
N_{q}>0
such that
f_{0}(x,v)=f(0,x,v)\leq N_{q}(1+\left|v\right|)^{-q}
for
(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. To be more precise, note that
\Lambda_{0}
depends additionally on
\langle v\rangle
. Since we are interested in a statement on a bounded domain, we do not make this dependence explicit.
For the justifications, we refer the reader to
[
15
, Lemma 5.2]
for (
2.1
), to
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
for (
2.2
), to
[
6
, Lemma 3.5, Lemma 3.6, Lemma 3.7]
for (
2.3
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), respectively.
For a justification of the last assumption, we use further regularity properties of solutions to (
1.1
), derived by Imbert-Mouhot-Silvestre in
[
7
,
6
,
4
]
.

2.3.
On the non-divergent part of the kernel

We consider a solution
f
of (
1.1
) in
(0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, periodic in
x
, such that (
1.10
) holds. If
\gamma\leq 0
, then we also assume that for any
q>0
there is
N_{q}>0
such that
f_{0}(x,v)=f(0,x,v)\leq N_{q}(1+\left|v\right|)^{-q}
for
(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Then for every
q>0
there is a constant
C_{s,q}>0
and
\alpha>s
depending only on
s,d,\gamma,m_{0},M_{0},E_{0},H_{0}
and
N_{q}
in case that
\gamma\leq 0
, such that for any
R>0
, any
\tau_{0}>0
, any
(t,x)\in(\tau_{0},T)\times B_{R^{1+2s}}
and any
v,w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
\left|v-w\right|<\min\big\{\frac{R}{2},1\big\}
there holds

(2.8)

\left|f(v)-f(w)\right|\leq C_{s,q}\left|v-w\right|^{\alpha}\big(1+\left|v\right|\big)^{-q}\approx C_{s,q}\left|v-w\right|^{\alpha}\big(1+\left|w\right|\big)^{-q}.

An even stronger statement is derived in
[
7
, Corollary 7.8]
. In fact, the constants
C_{s,q}
and
\alpha
may depend on
N_{p}
for some fixed
p>q
.

We now consider
(t,x,v)\in Q_{R}
for
0<R\leq 1
. We recall
[
15
, Corollary 4.2]
: for any
v,w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there is a universal constant
C_{K}>0
such that

K_{f}(v,w)=C_{K}\left|v-w\right|^{-(d+2s)}\int_{w^{\prime}\perp v-w}f(v+w^{\prime})\left|w^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma+2s+1}\,{\rm d}w^{\prime}.

Thus for
v\in B_{R}
we take
q>d+2s+\gamma
and use (
2.8
), so that

\displaystyle\left|K_{f}(v,w)-K_{f}(w,v)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{K}\left|v-w\right|^{-(d+2s)}\left|\int_{w^{\prime}\perp v-w}f(v+w^{\prime})\left|w^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma+2s+1}\,{\rm d}w^{\prime}-\int_{w^{\prime}\perp w-v}f(w+w^{\prime})\left|w^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma+2s+1}\,{\rm d}w^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{K}\left|v-w\right|^{-(d+2s)}\int_{w^{\prime}\perp v-w}\left|f(v+w^{\prime})-f(w+w^{\prime})\right|\left|w^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma+2s+1}\,{\rm d}w^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{K}C_{s,q}\left|v-w\right|^{\alpha-(d+2s)}\int_{w^{\prime}\perp v-w}\big(1+\left|w^{\prime}\right|\big)^{-q}\left|w^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma+2s+1}\,{\rm d}w^{\prime}.

We then combine this with
[
15
, Lemma 4.8]
, which shows that for each
v\in B_{R}
there is a symmetric subset of the unit sphere
A=A(v)\subset\partial B_{1}
, with strictly positive measure, and such that for each
\frac{v-w}{\left|v-w\right|}\in A
, the kernel satisfies a pointwise lower bound
K_{f}(v,w)\geq\lambda\left|v-w\right|^{-(d+2s)}
for some
\lambda
depending only on the hydrodynamical quantities in (
1.10
) and
R
. Indeed, Lemma 4.6 in
[
15
]
implies that there is
\ell>0
,
m>0
and
r>0
such that

\left|\{v:f(v)>\ell\}\cap B_{r}\right|\geq m.

Thus if we introduce
S:=\{v:f(v)>\ell\}\cap B_{r}
, then
f(v)\geq\ell\chi_{S}(v)
and thus
K_{f}(v,w)\geq\ell K_{\chi_{S}}(v,w)
. Since
S\subset B_{r}
, we find on the one hand,

\int_{w^{\prime}\perp v-w}\left(\chi_{S}(v+w^{\prime})+\chi_{S}(w+w^{\prime})\right)\left|w^{\prime}\right|^{\gamma+2s+1}\,{\rm d}w^{\prime}\leq 2(R+r)^{\gamma+2s+1}\left|B_{r}\right|.

On the other hand,
\left|S\right|\geq m
.
In particular, this implies for any
v\in B_{R}
that

2.4.
Notation

We define the kinetic domains for time, space and velocity respecting the scaling of the equations. On the one hand, equation (
1.1
) is scaling-invariant. Specifically, for any
r\in[0,1]
the scaled function

satisfies the Boltzmann equation with scaled coefficients. On the other hand, (
1.1
) is invariant under Galilean transformations

z\to z_{0}\circ z=(t_{0}+t,x_{0}+x+tv_{0},v_{0}+v)

with
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1+2d}
. If

is a solution of (
1.1
), then its Galilean transformation
f_{z_{0}}(z)=f(z_{0}\circ z)
solves (
1.1
) with correspondingly translated coefficients. In view of these invariances we define kinetic cylinders

Q_{r}(z_{0}):=\left\{(t,x,v):-r^{2s}\leq t-t_{0}\leq 0,\left|v-v_{0}\right|<r,\left|x-x_{0}-(t-t_{0})v_{0}\right|<r^{1+2s}\right\},

for

and
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1+2d}
.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(f-l)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}(f-l)

5.2.1.

(Aronson’s auxiliary proposition)
.

\displaystyle+C\rho^{-2s}\left\|H\right\|_{L^{\infty}([\tau_{0},\sigma]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}([\tau_{0},\sigma]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}^{2}.

\displaystyle\int_{[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\mathcal{T}f\Big(fH\varphi_{R}^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}z=

\displaystyle\int_{[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(f(w)-f(v)\big)K_{h}(v,w)\Big(fH\varphi_{R}^{2}\Big)(v)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle+\int_{[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}f(v)\Big(fH\varphi_{R}^{2}\Big)(v)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(K_{h}(v,w)-K_{h}(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\right)\,{\rm d}z.

Step 1: Transport operator.

First we integrate by parts the transport operator

(5.7)

\displaystyle\int_{[\tau_{0},\tau_{1}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\mathcal{T}f\Big(fH\varphi_{R}^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}z

Now we deal with the non-local term.
We write

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}f\big)fH\varphi_{R}^{2}\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(f^{2}H\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(v)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(K_{h}(v,w)-K_{h}(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\right)\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\big[f(w)-f(v)\big]

\displaystyle\Big[\big(fH\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(v)+\big(fH\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(w)\Big]

\displaystyle f(w)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}\Big[\big(f^{2}H\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(w)-\big(f^{2}H\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(v)\Big]K_{h}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(f^{2}H\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(v)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(K_{h}(v,w)-K_{h}(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\right)\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{2R}}\big[f(w)-f(v)\big]\big(fH\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(v)K_{h}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}f^{2}(v)\Big[\big(H\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(w)-\big(H\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(v)\Big]K_{h}(w,v)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}\big(f^{2}H\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(v)\big[K_{h}(w,v)-K_{h}(v,w)\big]\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(f^{2}H\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(v)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(K_{h}(v,w)-K_{h}(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\right)\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{2R}}\big[f(w)-f(v)\big]\big(fH\varphi_{R}^{2}\big)(v)K_{h}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

5.3.
On the exponential lower bound

The only part where we use the divergence form symmetry of the non-local operator in the derivation of the exponential lower bound on the fundamental solution is in
[
14

Fix
(\tau_{0},y_{0},w_{0})\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}

0<\tau_{0}<\sigma<T
. Consider for
\tau_{1}<\sigma
and for some
\alpha

f(\tau_{1},y_{1},w_{1})\leq M
for
(\tau_{1},y_{1},w_{1})\in[0,\sigma]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}

Define
g(\tau_{1},y_{1},w_{1}):=f(\tau_{1},y_{1},w_{1})e^{-c_{0}(\tau_{1}-\sigma)}
for
\tau_{1}<\sigma
. Then
g
is a super-solution to the adjoint of (
1.9
) in
(0,\sigma)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
, with initial values

\displaystyle\begin{cases}g(\sigma,y_{1},w_{1})=M,\qquad&\textrm{if }\max\left\{\left|y_{1}-y_{0}-(\sigma-\tau_{0})w_{0}\right|^{\frac{2s}{1+2s}},\left|w_{1}-w_{0}\right|^{2s}\right\}<\alpha(\sigma-\tau_{0}),\\

since

\mathcal{L}^{*}g(v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(g(w)-g(v)\big)K_{h}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w+\Bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}K_{h}(w,v)-K_{h}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)g(v),

so that using (
2.5
) and choosing

large enough, we get

\mathcal{T}^{*}g(v)-\mathcal{L}^{*}g(v)=-e^{-c_{0}(\tau_{1}-\sigma)}\mathcal{T}f-e^{-c_{0}(\tau_{1}-\sigma)}\mathcal{L}f-\Lambda_{0}g+c_{0}g\geq 0.

If we set

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(\tau_{1},y_{1},w_{1})=\begin{cases}g(\tau_{1},y_{1},w_{1}),\qquad&\textrm{if }\tau_{1}<\sigma,\\
Me^{-c_{0}(\tau_{1}-\sigma)},\qquad&\textrm{if }\tau_{1}>\sigma,\end{cases}

then
\tilde{g}
is a non-negative super-solution of the adjoint of (
1.9
) in
(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}

for
(\tau_{1},y_{1},w_{1})\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}

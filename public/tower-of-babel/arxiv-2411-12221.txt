Non-adiabatic phonon self-energy due to electrons with finite linewidths
Cheol-Hwan Park

Abstract
We develop the theory of the non-adiabatic phonon self-energy arising from coupling to electrons with finite linewidths using the spectral representation of Green’s functions. Our formalism naturally includes the contribution from the intra-band electronic transitions (as well as the inter-band ones) at all electron wavevectors to the phonon linewidths, which is forbidden for zone-center optical phonons if infinitesimal electron linewidths are used. As a proof of principle, we use the theory to calculate the linewidth of the double-degenerate, zone-center optical E 2 ​ g E_{\rm 2g} phonons of graphene as a function of the chemical potential. The calculated phonon linewidths obtained with finite electron linewidths differ significantly from those obtained with infinitesimal electron linewidths even at low temperatures. Intra-band electronic transitions play an important role in making this difference. Moreover, only the results accounting for the finite electron linewidths are in quantitative agreement with the available experimental data. The presented formalism is suitable for first-principles calculations.



I
Introduction

The phonon energy is conventionally obtained from density-functional perturbation theory calculations
[
1
]
within the adiabatic, Born-Oppenheimer approximation. Many-body interaction effects beyond the adiabatic approximation can be incorporated as energy-dependent, complex self-energy arising from electron-phonon interactions. This self-energy is referred to as the non-adiabatic phonon self-energy
[
2
]
.

Non-adiabatic phonon self-energy plays an important role in materials in which the energy difference between occupied and empty electronic states is comparable to phonon energies. Some of the early examples of first-principles calculations on the imaginary part of the non-adiabatic phonon self-energy arising from electron-phonon interactions are
Butler et al. [3]
,
Bauer et al. [4]
,
Shukla et al. [5]
,
Lazzeri et al. [6]
.

In previous first-principles studies, the imaginary part of the non-adiabatic (NA) phonon self-energy was calculated as suggested by
Allen [7]
:

{\rm Im\,}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{{\mathbf{q}\nu}}(\omega_{\mathbf{q}\nu})=-\pi\,\sum_{mn}\,\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})|^{2}\,\left[f(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}})-f(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})\right]\,\delta(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}+\omega_{\mathbf{q}\nu}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})\,,

(1)

where
f(\varepsilon)=1/\left[e^{(\varepsilon-\mu)/k_{\rm B}T}+1\right]
is the Fermi-Dirac occupation factor at temperature
T
and chemical potential
\mu
, and
\Omega_{\rm BZ}
is the volume of the Brillouin zone.
The electron-phonon coupling constant is given by

g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})=\left<{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\left|\Delta V_{\mathbf{q}\nu}\right|{n\mathbf{k}}\right>\,,

(2)

where
\left|{n\mathbf{k}}\right>
indicates the electronic state with wavevector
{\bf k}
, band index
m
and energy
\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}
, and
\Delta V_{\mathbf{q}\nu}
is the change in self-consistent potential due to a phonon with branch index
\nu
, wavevector
{\bf q}
, and energy
\omega_{\mathbf{q}\nu}
.

The energy conservation condition

\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}=\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}+\omega_{\mathbf{q}\nu}

(3)

was imposed in calculating the imaginary part of the non-adiabatic phonon self-energy because of the energy delta function in Eq. (
1
).
Therefore, this condition is unnecessary if we consider finite electron linewidths. It was shown that such finite electron linewidths play an important role in the non-adiabatic phonon self-energy
[
8
,

,

,

,

,

,

,
17
]
.
Among these studies,
Novko [15]
,
Novko [16]
and
Girotto Erhardt et al. [17]
considered the effect of electron linewidth for the intra-band scattering processes by including higher-order electron-phonon scattering through vertex correction.

Ferrante et al. [14]
calculated the self-energy of the zone-center optical phonons of graphene based on both intra-band and inter-band electronic transitions, accounting for the finite electron linewidths of both linear bands in the case of the latter; see Eq. (2) therein, which is reasonable and yet intuitive. Moreover, Eq. (2) of

This paper presents the theory for non-adiabatic phonon self-energy arising from coupling to electrons with finite linewidths, accounting for both intra-band and inter-band transitions and the linewidths of both bands in the latter, based on the electron Green’s functions. The final form of our theory [Eqs. (
II
) and (
II
)] does not have double energy integration and is suitable for a straightforward implementation in software packages on electron-phonon interactions.

II
Method

Using the spectral representation of the Matsubara dressed electron Green functions
[
18
]
, we can write the imaginary part of the non-adiabatic phonon self-energy as

\displaystyle{\rm Im\,}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{{\mathbf{q}\nu}}(i\omega_{m})={\rm Im\,}\,\frac{1}{\beta}\,\sum_{mn}\,\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})|^{2}\,\sum_{ip_{n}}G_{{n\mathbf{k}}}(ip_{n})\,G_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}(ip_{n}+i\omega_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\varepsilon\,\delta_{{n\mathbf{k}}}(\varepsilon)\,\int d\varepsilon^{\prime}\,\delta_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})\,{\rm Im\,}\,\frac{1}{\beta}\,\sum_{mn}\,\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})|^{2}\,\sum_{ip_{n}}\,\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\varepsilon+\mu}\,\frac{1}{ip_{n}+i\omega_{m}-\varepsilon^{\prime}+\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\varepsilon\,\delta_{{n\mathbf{k}}}(\varepsilon)\,\int d\varepsilon^{\prime}\,\delta_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})\,{\rm Im\,}\,\frac{1}{\beta}\,\sum_{mn}\,\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})|^{2}\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{\prime}+i\omega_{m}}\,\sum_{ip_{n}}\,\left(\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\varepsilon+\mu}-\frac{1}{ip_{n}+i\omega_{m}-\varepsilon^{\prime}+\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\varepsilon\,\delta_{{n\mathbf{k}}}(\varepsilon)\,\int d\varepsilon^{\prime}\,\delta_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}(\varepsilon^{\prime})\,{\rm Im\,}\,\sum_{mn}\,\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})|^{2}\,\frac{f(\varepsilon)-f(\varepsilon^{\prime})}{\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{\prime}+i\omega_{m}}\,,

where
\beta=1/k_{\rm B}T
and
\omega_{m}=2\pi m/\beta
and
p_{n}=\pi(2n+1)/\beta
for integers

and

In the last equality, the series expansion of the Fermi-Dirac distribution
[

]

f(z)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z-\mu-ip_{n}}\,.

(5)

was used.
Also,

\delta_{{n\mathbf{k}}}(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{n\mathbf{k}}}}{(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{{n\mathbf{k}}})^{2}+\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{n\mathbf{k}}}^{2}}

(6)

is the electron spectral function, where

\displaystyle\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{n\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-{\rm Im\,}\Sigma_{{n\mathbf{k}}}(\varepsilon_{{n\mathbf{k}}})>0

(7)

is the magnitude of the imaginary part of the retarded electron self-energy.
We note that

\lim_{\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{n\mathbf{k}}}\to 0^{+}}\delta_{{n\mathbf{k}}}(\varepsilon)=\delta(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{{n\mathbf{k}}})\,.

(8)

Non-collinear spinor wavefunctions were assumed. In case the system is a collinear magnet, one can denote the composite band index
m
or
n
in Eq. (
II
) by the (up or down) spin index and the band index at a given spin.
For non-magnetic, non-spinor calculations, we can multiply Eq. (
II
) by 2 and confine the band summation to bands with one kind of spin. In other cases, this simplification cannot be used.

To obtain the retarded phonon self-energy, we replace
i\omega_{m}
in Eq. (
II
) with
\omega+i0^{+}
.
Using
\frac{1}{x+i0^{+}}=P\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)-i\pi\delta(x)
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Im\,}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{{\mathbf{q}\nu}}(\omega+i0^{+})=-\pi\,\sum_{mn}\,\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})|^{2}\,\int d\varepsilon\,\delta_{{n\mathbf{k}}}(\varepsilon)\,\delta_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}(\varepsilon+\omega)\,\left[f(\varepsilon)-f(\varepsilon+\omega)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\,\sum_{mn}\,\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})|^{2}\,\int d\varepsilon\,\frac{\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{n\mathbf{k}}}}{(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{{n\mathbf{k}}})^{2}+\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{n\mathbf{k}}}^{2}}\,\frac{\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}}{(\varepsilon+\omega-\varepsilon_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}})^{2}+\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}^{2}}\left[f(\varepsilon)-f(\varepsilon+\omega)\right]\,.

Equation (
II
) can be intuitively interpreted as the usual linewidth formula [Eq. (
1
), but replacing
\omega_{\mathbf{q}\nu}
with
\omega
, or, equivalently, Eq. (
14
)] broadened by the electron linewidths.
We note that the imaginary part of Eq. (2) of
Ferrante et al. [14]

II

Ferrante et al. [14]
arrived at their Eq. (2) from physical intuition, rather than from a rigorous derivation. Our work proves that this result is rigorously correct using the Green’s function method.
In the limit the electron linewidths vanish, Eq. (

We will now convert Eq. (
II

19

\psi(z)=-\gamma+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{n+z-1}\right)\,,

(10)

After a lengthy but straightforward derivation (see Sec.
V.1

\displaystyle{\rm Im\,}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{{\mathbf{q}\nu}}(\omega+i0^{+})=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\sum_{mn}\,\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})|^{2}\,\times

\displaystyle{\rm Re\,}\left\{\frac{-\psi\left[\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\beta}{2\pi i}\,(\varepsilon_{{n\mathbf{k}}}-\mu-i\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{n\mathbf{k}}})\right]+\psi\left[\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\beta}{2\pi i}\,(-\omega+\varepsilon_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}-\mu-i\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}})\right]}{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}+\omega-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-i(\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{n\mathbf{k}}-\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{\psi\left[\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\beta}{2\pi i}\,(\varepsilon_{{n\mathbf{k}}}-\mu-i\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{n\mathbf{k}}})\right]-\psi\left[\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2\pi i}\,(-\omega+\varepsilon_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}-\mu+i\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}})\right]}{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}+\omega-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-i(\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{n\mathbf{k}}+\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\psi\left[\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\beta}{2\pi i}\,(\varepsilon_{{n\mathbf{k}}}+\omega-\mu-i\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{n\mathbf{k}}})\right]-\psi\left[\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\beta}{2\pi i}\,(\varepsilon_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}-\mu-i\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}})\right]}{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}+\omega-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-i(\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{n\mathbf{k}}-\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{-\psi\left[\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2\pi i}\,(\varepsilon_{{n\mathbf{k}}}+\omega-\mu+i\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{n\mathbf{k}}})\right]+\psi\left[\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\beta}{2\pi i}\,(\varepsilon_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}-\mu-i\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}})\right]}{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}+\omega-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}+i(\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{n\mathbf{k}}+\Sigma{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}\right\}\,.

Equation (
II
) is the main result of this paper and is suitable for first-principles calculations.
We note that in practice the digamma function is not evaluated according to its definition in Eq. (
10
) in numerical computations; special functions libraries allow the efficient evaluation of this function.
The digamma function is often evaluated with a mixture of series expansion, asymptotic approximations, and reflection formulas depending on the argument. See, for example,
Bernardo [21]
.

The real part of the non-adiabatic phonon self-energy [see Eq. (145) of Ref.
2
] can be obtained from the Kramers-Kronig relation:

\displaystyle{\rm Re\,}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{{\mathbf{q}\nu}}(\omega+i0^{+})=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\int d\omega^{\prime}\,\frac{{\rm Im\,}\Pi^{\rm NA}_{{\mathbf{q}\nu}}(\omega^{\prime}+i0^{+})}{\omega^{\prime}-\omega}

\displaystyle-\sum_{mn}\,\int\frac{d{\bf k}}{\Omega_{\rm BZ}}|g_{mn\nu}({\bf k},{\bf q})|^{2}\,\frac{f(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}})-f(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}\,.

To better understand the computational results, we divide the total phonon linewidth into contributions from different electronic transitions, i. e. , with different
m
and
n
pairs. The results are shown in Fig.
2

n=-1

|\mu|>\omega_{\mathbf{q}\nu}/2
. However, the most interesting result is that the intra-band contributions, that is, processes involving the electronic transition in the same band (
m=n

|\mu|

Froehlicher and Berciaud [29]

As long as the intra-band electron-phonon coupling matrix elements for the zone-center
E_{\textrm{2g}}
phonon are non-zero, and the energy conservation condition can be relaxed, the zone-center
E_{\textrm{2g}}
phonon can decay through intra-band electron scattering processes. Consider the electron-phonon interaction Hamiltonian within the second quantization formalism: Destruction of an electronic state and its subsequent creation is an allowed operation. Relaxation of the energy conservation can be intuitively understood as follows: Since there are uncertainties in the energies of the initial and final electronic states, we can find pairs of electronic states whose energy difference matches the energy of the
E_{\textrm{2g}}
optical phonon.

Interestingly, even if we assume zero electron linewidths, the zone-center
E_{\textrm{2g}}

\varepsilon=+\omega_{\mathbf{q}\nu}/2

In passing, we note that the electron linewidths will increase with temperature, which is not considered in the calculation presented in this work because it is not the subject of this study. If this effect is considered, the phonon linewidths will increase further with temperature.

Finally, one can think of the self-consistent determination of both electron and phonon linewidths. In fact, the theory of computing electron linewidths self-consistently assuming zero phonon linewidths was developed by
Lihm et al. [20]
, and the theory of computing electron linewidths, assuming finite phonon linewidths but zero electron linewidths was developed by
Lihm and Park [34]
[see Eq. (S71) therein]. However, the theory of computing electron linewidths assuming both finite electron linewidths and finite phonon linewidths has yet to be developed. In addition to this problem, there are other scattering mechanisms for electrons and those for phonons beyond the electron-phonon interactions that need to be taken into account, which, in principle, can be done, for the self-consistent computation of electron and phonon linewidths.

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented the theory for calculating non-adiabatic phonon self-energy arising from the coupling to electrons with finite linewidths.
The theory naturally introduces the contribution to phonon linewidth from intra-band electronic transitions for all electronic states, not just the ones satisfying the energy conservation condition upon phonon absorption.

As a proof of principle, we demonstrated that the measured zone-center
E_{\rm 2g}
optical phonon linewidth vs. chemical potential behavior of graphene at low temperatures can be quantitatively explained only if the finite electron linewidths are considered.
Remarkably, we find that the intra-band electronic transitions, which are forbidden in this example if infinitesimal electronic linewidths are used, contribute significantly to the
self-energy of the zone-center
E_{\rm 2g}
optical phonons of graphene.

Our formalism can be easily implemented in existing software packages for computing electron-phonon interactions from first principles, such as
EPW

[

]
,

[

]
,
Perturbo

[

]
,
Phoebe

[

,
elphbolt

[

]
, and
EPIq

[

]
, and
ElectronPhonon.jl

[

]
.
We expect that the theory will be necessary to interpret experimental data on materials whose phonon energy scale is non-negligible compared to the energy scale of low-energy electronic transitions, which are materials of contemporary interest.

Here,

is the area of a unit cell. We are interested in the case
\textbf{q}=\textbf{k}^{\prime}-\textbf{k}\to\textbf{0}

We consider the longitudinal-optical (LO) and transverse-optical (TO)
E_{\textrm{2g}}

\theta_{\textbf{k}}=\theta

) is independent of
\theta

k
first over
\theta
for

and then over
k=\hbar\varepsilon/v_{\textrm{F}}
for the rest of Eq. (
II
). The analytic forms of the matrix elements are presented in Tab. I of Ref. 21.

The angular integration results in

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\,|g_{mn\nu}(\textbf{k},\textbf{q})|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{2}\,\eta\,\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2M_{\rm C}\omega_{\Gamma}^{{\rm E}_{2g}}}}\right)^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\,\sin^{2}(\theta-\theta_{\textbf{q}}+\theta_{mn\nu})

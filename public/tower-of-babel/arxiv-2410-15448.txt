On-shell Recursion Relations for Tree-level Closed String Amplitudes
Pongwit Srisangyingcharoen, Aphiwat Yuenyong

Abstract
We derive a general expression for on-shell recursion relations of closed string tree-level amplitudes. Starting with the string amplitudes written in the form of the Koba-Nielsen integral, we apply the BCFW shift to deform them. In contrast to open string amplitudes, where poles are explicitly determined by the integration over vertex positions, we utilize Schwinger’s parametrization to handle the pole structure in closed strings. Our analysis reveals that the shifted amplitudes contain δ \delta -function poles, which yield simple poles upon taking residues. This allows us to present a general expression for the on-shell recursion relation for closed strings. Additionally, we offer an alternative method for computing the residue of the shifted amplitudes by factorizing an n n -point closed string amplitude into two lower-point amplitudes. This is achieved by inserting a completeness relation that includes all possible closed string states in the Fock space. Our results are consistent with those previously obtained.

The key idea for deriving the on-shell recursion relations is based on the fact that any tree-level scattering amplitude is a rational function of the external momenta, thus, one can turn an amplitude
A_{n}

\displaystyle k_{1}\rightarrow\hat{k}_{1}(z)=

\displaystyle k_{1}-qz

\displaystyle k_{n}\rightarrow\hat{k}_{n}(z)=

\displaystyle k_{n}+qz

The unshifted amplitude

A_{n}(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dz\frac{A_{n}(z)}{z}-\sum_{\text{poles}}\text{Res}_{z=z_{\text{poles}}}\left(\frac{A_{n}(z)}{z}\right),

(2)

In Yang-Mills a sum over the helicities of the intermediate gauge boson and in general theories a sum over all allowed intermediate particle states must also be done. In the general case, the BCFW recursion relation is

A_{n}(0)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{poles}\\
\alpha\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{physical}\\
\text{states}\end{subarray}}A_{L}(\dots,P(z_{\alpha}))\frac{2}{P^{2}+M^{2}}A_{R}(-P(z_{\alpha}),\dots)

(3)

The validity of equation (
2

[
28
]

This paper aims to present expressions for on-shell recursion relations concerning closed string amplitudes. While existing literature has explored relations for open strings
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
, the context of closed strings remains relatively unexplored. Herein, we seek to derive explicit expressions for the on-shell recursion relations of closed strings.

Our main result is the derivation of a general framework for constructing closed string amplitudes through on-shell recursion relations. Unlike the case of field theory, where a finite set of intermediate particles is sufficient to generate higher-point amplitudes, closed string theory involves an infinite tower of string state excitations. This intrinsic feature renders it conceptually impossible to construct higher-point amplitudes using only lower-point amplitudes with the same external states. For instance, pure tachyonic amplitudes cannot be derived from lower-point pure tachyonic amplitudes alone, as is typical in field theory. Instead, they necessarily involve scattering amplitudes of tachyons and arbitrary string states.

In the next section, we explore how to obtain the on-shell recursion relations for closed string amplitudes, focusing on tachyonic amplitudes for simplicity (specifically, 4- and 5-point amplitudes). In Section 3, we generalize the results to amplitudes with arbitrary points and external string states. Section 4 verifies the general results through explicit computation of residues using the unitarity of the amplitudes. Finally, we conclude our findings in Section 5.

On-shell Recursion Relations for Closed String Amplitudes at Fewer Points

2.1
Four-Point Amplitudes

In this section, we would like to formulate the on-shell recursion relations for the closed string amplitudes. For simplicity, we start our investigation with the simplest four-point tachyonic amplitude whose expression is

\mathcal{A}^{\text{cl}}_{4}=\int d^{2}z|z|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{1}\cdot p_{2}}|1-z|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}

(4)

where
p_{i}
are closed string momenta. We then choose to deform the momenta as

\displaystyle p_{1}\rightarrow\hat{p}_{1}(z)=

\displaystyle p_{1}-qz

(5a)

\displaystyle p_{n}\rightarrow\hat{p}_{n}(z)=

\displaystyle p_{n}+qz.

(5b)

For the shifted momenta to be on-shell, the reference momentum
q
satisfies
q\cdot q=p_{1}\cdot q=p_{n}\cdot q=0
. The shifted amplitude takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\hat{1},2,3,\hat{4})(\tilde{z})=\int d^{2}z|z|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{1}\cdot p_{2}-2\tilde{z}}|1-z|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}

(6)

where
\tilde{z}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}z(q\cdot p_{2})
. Although closed string amplitudes lack a notion of color-ordering, we adopt a similar convention to track string states systematically. The amplitude can also be expressed symmetrically as:

\mathcal{A}^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\tilde{z})=\frac{1}{4!}\sum_{\sigma}A_{4}^{\text{cl}}(\sigma(1,2,3,4))(\tilde{z}).

(7)

where the sum is over all permutations
\sigma
of the external momenta. This permutation simply relabels the momenta of the particles, with the first and last indices being the shifted ones. Although the subamplitudes are equal, their distinct integral forms allow us to access different pole channels, which is why they are expressed in this manner.

Another reason for expressing the amplitude in this manner is to ensure that the resulting expressions exhibit the expected pole structure in the
s-,t-,
and
u-
channels, as dictated by the Virasoro-Shapiro formula. Remind that when
\tilde{z}=0
, the amplitude returns to the original one.

Let us first evaluate the integral
A^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\hat{1},2,3,\hat{4})(\tilde{z})
. It is not hard to see that there are possible poles regarding
\tilde{z}
arising from the term
|z|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{1}\cdot p_{2}-2\tilde{z}}
. For this reason, we apply

|z|^{2a-2}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-a)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ t^{-a}e^{-|z|^{2}t}

(8)

to rewrite

A^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\hat{1},2,3,\hat{4})(\tilde{z})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\int d^{2}z\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ t^{-\beta}e^{-|z|^{2}t}|1-z|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}

(9)

where

\beta=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{1}\cdot p_{2}-\tilde{z}+1=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}-\tilde{z}-1.

(10)

According to the binomial expansion, one can write

|1-z|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}=\sum_{a,b=0}^{\infty}\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}{a}\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}{b}(-1)^{a+b}z^{a}\bar{z}^{b}.

(11)

This gives the complex Gaussian integral

\displaystyle\int d^{2}z\ z^{a}\bar{z}^{b}e^{-|z|^{2}t}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{d}{d\bar{J}}\Big)^{a}\Big(\frac{d}{dJ}\Big)^{b}\int d^{2}z\ e^{-|z|^{2}t+\bar{J}z+J\bar{z}}\bigg|_{J=\bar{J}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi i}{t}\Big(\frac{d}{d\bar{J}}\Big)^{a}\Big(\frac{d}{dJ}\Big)^{b}e^{|J|^{2}/t}\bigg|_{J=\bar{J}=0}.

(12)

Only the case that
a=b
would give non-zero contributions of the integration. Consequently, the amplitude (
9
) becomes

\displaystyle A^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\hat{1},2,3,\hat{4})(\tilde{z})=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi i}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ t^{-\beta-1}\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}{a}^{2}\frac{a!}{t^{a}}

(13)

which is troublesome to evaluate since it contains the divergent integral. To deal with this issue, we change the variable
t
into
w
by writing
t=e^{w}
, hence

\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ t^{-\beta-1-a}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dw\ e^{-(\beta+a)w}

(14)

to which we can Wick rotate
w\rightarrow iw
such that the integration over
w
turns into the Dirac delta function, giving

\displaystyle A^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\hat{1},2,3,\hat{4})(\tilde{z})=\frac{(2\pi i)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}{a}^{2}a!\ \delta(\beta+a).

(15)

Following
[
34
]
, the formal distribution of the Dirac delta function reads

\delta(z-w)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{-n-1}w^{n}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{n}w^{-n-1}.

(16)

Accordingly, one can write

\delta(\beta+a)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-\beta)^{-n}\ a^{n-1}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\tilde{z}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2})+1\Big)^{-n}\ a^{n-1}.

(17)

The above summation provide poles of the order
n
where
n\geq 1
at
\tilde{z}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}-1
.

One obtains the unshifted
A^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\hat{1},2,3,\hat{4})(\tilde{z}=0)
using the equation (
2
) which is

\displaystyle A^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\hat{1},2,3,\hat{4})(0)=-\text{Res}_{\tilde{z}=z^{*}}\Big(\frac{A^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\hat{1},2,3,\hat{4})(\tilde{z})}{\tilde{z}}\Big)

(18)

where
z^{*}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}-1
. Note that the pole at infinity can be neglected, as the integrand
|z|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{1}\cdot p_{2}-2\tilde{z}}
is suppressed when
\tilde{z}\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, Some readers might find it surprising that a single pole,
\tilde{z}=z^{*}
, can generate an infinite number of poles corresponding to the particular scattering channels. As we will demonstrate shortly, this is achieved by transforming the infinitely many higher-order poles into an infinite set of simple poles, each evaluated at distinct points. To see this, we apply the formula

\text{Res}_{z=z_{0}}f(z)=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\frac{d^{r-1}}{dz^{r-1}}(z-z_{0})^{r}f(z)\bigg|_{z=z_{0}}

(19)

where the function
f(z)
contains the pole of the order
r
at
z=z_{0}
. Therefore, one can compute the residue

Res

{}_{\tilde{z}=z^{*}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\tilde{z}\Gamma(1-\beta)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\tilde{z}-z^{*})^{-n}\ a^{n-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{n}}{n!}\bigg(\frac{d}{d\tilde{z}}\bigg)^{n}\bigg(\frac{1}{\tilde{z}\Gamma(1-\beta)}\bigg)\bigg|_{\tilde{z}=z^{*}}

(20)

Note that we discarded the summation over non-positive integers
n
in the first line as they contain no poles. The negligence of the negative modes is helpful to disregard all singularities arising when
a
equals zero.

It turns out that the right-hand side of (
20
) is nothing but a Taylor expansion of the function
f(z^{*}+a)
around the point
a=z^{*}
where in our case
f(x)=1/(x\Gamma(1-\beta(x)))
. The residue (
20
) becomes

\frac{1}{(z^{*}+a)\Gamma(1-\beta(z^{*}))}=\frac{1}{a!\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}+a-1\right)}.

(21)

In general, we simply show that

\text{Res}_{z=z_{0}}\left(f(z)\delta(z-z_{0})\right)=f(z_{0}).

(22)

Consequently, the unshifted amplitude
A^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\hat{1},2,3,\hat{4})(\tilde{z}=0)
takes the form

\displaystyle A^{\text{cl}}_{4}(\hat{1},2,3,\hat{4})(0)=\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{2}\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}{a}^{2}\frac{1}{(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}+\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}(a-1)}.

(23)

The denominators imply the mass spectrum of intermediate on-shell closed strings. Comparing the above expression with the recursion relations (
3
) suggests that the square of the binomial coefficients can be interpreted as a recursion involving products of three-point amplitudes. Specifically,

A_{L}(\hat{1},2,\hat{P}(z_{a}))A_{R}(-\hat{P}(z_{a}),3,\hat{4})=\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\alpha^{\prime}}\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}{a}^{2}

(24)

where
\hat{P}=-(\hat{p}_{1}+p_{2})
and the subamplitudes are evaluated at
z_{a}=\frac{(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}+4(a-1)/\alpha^{\prime}}{2q\cdot p_{2}}
. A detailed computation of the residues of the relevant poles will be provided in Section 4.

Substitute (
23
) to (
7
) gives the expression for on-shell recursion relation of four-point closed string amplitude as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\text{cl}}_{4}=\frac{(8\pi)^{2}}{4!\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}+\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}(a-1)}\left[\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{1}\cdot p_{3}}{a}^{2}+\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}{a}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p_{1}+p_{3})^{2}+\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}(a-1)}\left[\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{1}\cdot p_{2}}{a}^{2}+\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}{a}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p_{2}+p_{3})^{2}+\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}(a-1)}\left[\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{1}\cdot p_{2}}{a}^{2}+\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{1}\cdot p_{3}}{a}^{2}\right]\Bigg)

(25)

Since there is no notion of color ordering in the closed string amplitudes, the momenta of intermediate closed string states are not necessarily a sum of adjacent momenta as in the case of open string amplitudes
[
33
]
. It is worth noting that the expression (
25
) exhibits a simple pole structure in the
s-,t-,
and
u-
channels, similar to that of the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude
[
35
,
36
]
. This is the primary motivation for symmetrizing the expression in (
7
).

3
On-shell Recursion Relations for

-point Closed String Amplitudes

Now we would like to investigate the closed string amplitudes in a more general setting. Consider the general expression for tree-level closed string amplitude which is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\text{cl}}_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}d^{2}z_{i}\frac{|z_{ab}|^{2}|z_{ac}|^{2}|z_{bc}|^{2}}{d^{2}z_{a}d^{2}z_{b}d^{2}z_{c}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{i}\cdot p_{j}}\mathcal{F}_{n}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

z_{a}
,

and

,

The function

and

(45)

when the polarizations for the external states
\xi^{\mu\nu}_{i}=\xi^{\mu}_{i}\bar{\xi}^{\nu}_{i}

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\prod_{i=2}^{n-2}d^{2}z_{i}\ \prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\ (z_{i}-z_{j})^{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{i}\cdot p_{j}+\tilde{n}_{ij}}(\bar{z}_{i}-\bar{z}_{j})^{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{i}\cdot p_{j}+\tilde{\bar{n}}_{ij}}

\tilde{n}_{ij}
and
\tilde{\bar{n}}_{ij}

\mathcal{F}_{n}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

We then applied a change of integral variables from
(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})

(w_{i},\bar{w}_{i})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\prod_{i=1}^{n-3}d^{2}w_{i}

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n-3}w_{j}^{s_{12\ldots j+1}+n_{j}}\prod_{l=j}^{n-3}\left(1-\prod_{k=j}^{l}w_{k}\right)^{s_{j+1,l+2}+n_{j+1,l+2}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n-3}\bar{w}_{j}^{s_{12\ldots j+1}+\bar{n}_{j}}\prod_{l=j}^{n-3}\left(1-\prod_{k=j}^{l}\bar{w}_{k}\right)^{s_{j+1,l+2}+\bar{n}_{j+1,l+2}}

where
s_{ij}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}(p_{i}+p_{j})^{2}
and

A_{n}^{\text{cl}}(1,2,\ldots,n)

point closed string amplitude as

The exponents

\bar{n}_{ij}

\displaystyle n_{ij}

\displaystyle=\tilde{n}_{ij}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}(m_{i}^{2}+m_{j}^{2})

\displaystyle=j-1+\sum_{l=1}^{j}\sum_{k>l}^{j+1}\tilde{n}_{lk}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{j+1}m_{i}^{2},

\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq n-3

\displaystyle\bar{n}_{j}

\displaystyle=j-1+\sum_{l=1}^{j}\sum_{k>l}^{j+1}\tilde{\bar{n}}_{lk}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{j+1}m_{i}^{2},

4
State-by-state Calculation of On-shell Factorization of Closed String Amplitudes

In this section, we would like to provide an alternative computation for the residue
\mathcal{B}_{i}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle{(i)}}_{jl},y^{\scriptscriptstyle{(i)}}_{jl},\{n\},z^{*}_{i})
using unitarity of the closed string amplitudes. Mathematically, we would like to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{physical}\ \Phi}\int\frac{d^{D}k}{(2\pi)^{D}}

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{1}|\mathcal{V}_{2}(p_{2})\ldots\mathcal{V}_{i}(p_{i})|\Phi;k\rangle

\displaystyle\otimes\langle\Phi;k|\mathcal{V}_{i+1}(p_{i+1})\ldots\mathcal{V}_{n-1}(p_{n-1})|\phi_{n}\rangle

(70)

resembles the function
\mathcal{B}_{i^{\prime}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle{(i^{\prime})}}_{jl},y^{\scriptscriptstyle{(i^{\prime})}}_{jl},\{n\},z^{*}_{i^{\prime}})
where
i=i^{\prime}+1
. Notice that we use different indices to keep track of the poles in the expressions. Specifically, While
i^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{B}_{i^{\prime}}
corresponds to the pole where where the intermediate propagator carries momentum
p_{1}+p_{2}+\ldots+p_{i^{\prime}+1}
, the index
i
in the expression (
70
) implies to those with momentum
p_{1}+p_{2}+\ldots+p_{i}
instead.

The expression (
70
) is nothing but a factorization of
n-
point string amplitudes into two on-shell fewer-point amplitudes. The states
|\phi\rangle
is a string state and
\mathcal{V}_{i}
refers to closed string vertex operators taking a form

\mathcal{V}_{i}(p_{i})=\int d^{2}z_{i}\ V_{i}(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z_{i}}).

(71)

In general, we can write

V_{i}(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z_{i}})=:f(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})e^{ip_{i}\cdot X(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}:

(72)

where the polarizations of higher-order string states are encoded in the function
f
. When
f=1
, they correspond to tachyons. Additionally, we must account for the global conformal symmetry, which allows us to freely fix three of the
z_{i}
and
\bar{z}_{i}
coordinates at arbitrary points in the complex plane.

The main challenge is that it is hard to determine physical intermediate string states. Luckily, according to
[
31
]
, one can by-pass this problem by enlarging the sum over intermediate physical states to over intermediate complete Fock space states. To see this, let consider such this factorization in gauge theory, i.e.

\sum_{\text{physical}\ \epsilon}A_{L}(\sigma_{L},k)A_{R}(\sigma_{R},-k)=\sum_{\text{physical}\ \epsilon}\tilde{A}^{\mu}_{L}(\sigma_{L},k)\epsilon_{\mu}\epsilon_{\nu}\tilde{A}^{\nu}_{R}(\sigma_{R},-k)

(73)

where
\epsilon_{\mu}
is a physical polarization of gauge particle. From a Ward identity, it is possible to include not only physical polartization states but non-physical ones too. Similar to the string theory, we have the famous no-ghost theorem which guarantee that the sum over physical states are equal to that over all states in Fock space. This means we could turn the expression to

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{Fock}\ \Phi}\int\frac{d^{D}k}{(2\pi)^{D}}

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{1}|\mathcal{V}_{2}(p_{2})\ldots\mathcal{V}_{i}(p_{i})|\Phi;k\rangle

\displaystyle\otimes\langle\Phi;k|\mathcal{V}_{i+1}(p_{i+1})\ldots\mathcal{V}_{n-1}(p_{n-1})|\phi_{n}\rangle

(74)

More discussion on no-ghost theorem can be found in
[
31
]
.

The Fock space spans all possible string states that are constructed by acting right/left-moving creation operators
\alpha^{\mu}_{-n}
and
\tilde{\alpha}^{\mu}_{-n}
on the ground state, i.e.

\Big(\alpha^{\mu_{1}}_{-n_{1}}\alpha^{\mu_{2}}_{-n_{2}}\ldots\alpha^{\mu_{i}}_{-n_{i}}\Big)\Big(\tilde{\alpha}^{\nu_{1}}_{-m_{1}}\tilde{\alpha}^{\nu_{2}}_{-m_{2}}\ldots\tilde{\alpha}^{\nu_{j}}_{-m_{j}}\Big)|0;k\rangle

(75)

where
n_{i}
and
m_{i}
are positive integers. Due to the level-matching condition, the states are subject to
\sum_{i}n_{i}=\sum_{i}m_{i}
. In this work, we introduce a normalized Fock state for a closed string as

\displaystyle|\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k\rangle=

\displaystyle\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{\left(\alpha^{\mu_{n,1}}_{-n}\ldots\alpha^{\mu_{n,N_{n}}}_{-n}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\prod_{(m,N_{m})\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\frac{\left(\tilde{\alpha}^{\tilde{\mu}_{m,1}}_{-m}\ldots\alpha^{\tilde{\mu}_{m,N_{m}}}_{-m}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{m}!m^{N_{m}}}}|0;k\rangle.

(76)

The set
\{\mathcal{N}\}
contains elements
(n,N_{n})
that identify which vibrational mode to excite and how many times they operate respectively. The level of string spectrum
N
is

N=\sum_{n}n\times N_{n}.

(77)

For example, in case
N=7
, one of the possible set
\{\mathcal{N}\}
is
\{(1,3),(2,2)\}
meaning that when
n=1,N_{1}=3
and
n=2,N_{2}=2
. Note that there are other possible combinations of
(n,N_{n})
that gives the set
\{\mathcal{N}\}
for
N=7
such as {(1,7)}, {(1,1),(2,1),(4,1)}, etc. The different set
\{\mathcal{N}\}
provides different excited string states with the same level
N
. Moreover, the level-matching condition also requires
N=\tilde{N}
, yet this does not necessarily mean
\{\mathcal{N}\}=\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}
. For instance, one could have
\{\mathcal{N}\}=\{(1,3),(2,2)\}
and
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}=\{(3,1),(4,1)\}
differently for the same
N=\tilde{N}=7
that corresponds to state

\left(\frac{\alpha_{-1}^{\mu_{1,1}}\alpha_{-1}^{\mu_{1,2}}\alpha_{-1}^{\mu_{1,3}}}{\sqrt{3!}}\right)\left(\frac{\alpha_{-2}^{\mu_{2,1}}\alpha_{-2}^{\mu_{2,2}}}{\sqrt{2!\times 4}}\right)\tilde{\alpha}_{-3}^{\tilde{\mu}_{3,1}}\tilde{\alpha}_{-4}^{\tilde{\mu}_{4,1}}|0;k\rangle.

(78)

Considering the states
|\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k\rangle
as a basis, we introduce a projection operator from the set of basis regarding to the center of mass momentum
k
as

\mathbb{P}(k)=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\{\mathcal{N}\},\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}|\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k\rangle\mathcal{T}_{\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\langle\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k|

(79)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\left(g_{\mu_{n,1},\nu_{n,1}}\ldots g_{\mu_{n,N_{n}},\nu_{n,N_{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\prod_{(m,N_{m})\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\left(g_{\tilde{\mu}_{m,1},\tilde{\nu}_{m,1}}\ldots g_{\tilde{\mu}_{m,N_{m}},\tilde{\nu}_{m,N_{m}}}\right).

(80)

For example, the first few terms of the projection operator reads

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(k)=

\displaystyle|0;k\rangle\langle 0;k|+\alpha_{-1}^{\mu_{1,1}}\tilde{\alpha}_{-1}^{\tilde{\mu}_{1,1}}|0;k\rangle\left(g_{\mu_{1,1},\nu_{1,1}}g_{\tilde{\mu}_{1,1},\tilde{\nu}_{1,1}}\right)\langle 0;k|\alpha_{+1}^{\nu_{1,1}}\tilde{\alpha}_{+1}^{\tilde{\nu}_{1,1}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\alpha_{-1}^{\mu_{1,1}}\alpha_{-1}^{\mu_{1,2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{-1}^{\tilde{\mu}_{1,1}}\tilde{\alpha}_{-1}^{\tilde{\mu}_{1,2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)|0;k\rangle\big(g_{\mu_{1,1},\nu_{1,1}}g_{\mu_{1,2},\nu_{1,2}}g_{\tilde{\mu}_{1,1},\tilde{\nu}_{1,1}}g_{\tilde{\mu}_{1,2},\tilde{\nu}_{1,2}}\big)

\displaystyle\langle 0;k|\left(\frac{\alpha_{+1}^{\nu_{1,1}}\alpha_{+1}^{\nu_{1,2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{+1}^{\tilde{\nu}_{1,1}}\tilde{\alpha}_{+1}^{\tilde{\nu}_{1,2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+\left(\frac{\alpha_{-1}^{\mu_{1,1}}\alpha_{-1}^{\mu_{1,2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\tilde{\alpha}_{-2}^{\tilde{\mu}_{2,1}}|0;k\rangle

\displaystyle\big(g_{\mu_{1,1},\nu_{1,1}}g_{\mu_{1,2},\nu_{1,2}}g_{\tilde{\mu}_{2,1},\tilde{\nu}_{2,1}}\big)\langle 0;k|\left(\frac{\alpha_{+1}^{\nu_{1,1}}\alpha_{+1}^{\nu_{1,2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\tilde{\alpha}_{+2}^{\tilde{\nu}_{2,1}}+\ldots.

(81)

The operator obeys

\mathbb{P}(k)\mathbb{P}(k^{\prime})=(2\pi)^{D}\delta^{D}(k-k^{\prime})\mathbb{P}(k).

(82)

A completeness relation is recovered upon integrating over the momentum
k
,

\mathbb{1}=\int\frac{d^{D}k}{(2\pi)^{D}}\ \mathbb{P}(k).

(83)

For simplicity, we will only focus our calculation on tachyonic closed string amplitudes whose non-integrated vertex operator is

V(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=:e^{ip_{i}\cdot X(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}:

(84)

where

\displaystyle X^{\mu}(z,\bar{z})=

\displaystyle X^{\mu}(z)+\bar{X}^{\mu}(\bar{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{\mu}-i\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p^{\mu}\ln{|z|^{2}}+i\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{1}{n}\left(\alpha^{\mu}_{n}z^{-n}+\tilde{\alpha}^{\mu}_{n}\bar{z}^{-n}\right).

(85)

Accordingly, we can write

V(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=e^{ip_{i}\cdot(x-i\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p\ln|z|^{2})}:\mathcal{W}(p_{i}):=|z|^{\alpha^{\prime}p_{i}\cdot p-\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}}e^{ip_{i}\cdot x}:\mathcal{W}(p_{i}):

(86)

where

\mathcal{W}(p_{i})=\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{i}\cdot\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{1}{n}\left(\alpha^{\mu}_{n}z^{-n}+\tilde{\alpha}^{\mu}_{n}\bar{z}^{-n}\right)\right)

(87)

and to obtain the above expression, the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula was used.

4.1
Residue of Tachyonic Four-Point Amplitude

Let’s first consider the four-point tachyon amplitude

\displaystyle\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\Bigg(\sum_{\{\mathcal{N}\},\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{D}k}{(2\pi)^{D}}\ \langle 0;-p_{1}|V(p_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})|\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k\rangle\mathcal{T}_{\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}

\displaystyle\langle\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k|V(p_{3},z_{3},\bar{z}_{3})|0;p_{4}\rangle\Bigg)\equiv\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{I}_{N}

(88)

where we insert the completeness relation (
83
) to split the amplitude into two three-point ones. The expression inside the parentheses is encapsulated as the function
\mathcal{I}_{N}
. Remind that the integrated vertex operators
\mathcal{V}(p_{i})
are changed into the non-integrated ones
V(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
due to the
PSL(2,\mathbb{C})
symmetry. Our choice is setting
z_{2}=\bar{z}_{2}=1
and
z_{3}=\bar{z}_{3}=1
to both sub-amplitudes. Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle 0;-p_{1}

\displaystyle|V(p_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})|\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k\rangle=\langle 0;-p_{1}|e^{ip_{2}\cdot x}:\mathcal{W}(p_{2}):

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{\left(\alpha^{\mu_{n,1}}_{-n}\ldots\alpha^{\mu_{n,N_{n}}}_{-n}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}}\prod_{(m,N_{m})\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\frac{\left(\tilde{\alpha}^{\tilde{\mu}_{m,1}}_{-m}\ldots\alpha^{\tilde{\mu}_{m,N_{m}}}_{-m}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{m}!m^{N_{m}}}}|0;k\rangle.

(89)

We then use the commutation relations

\displaystyle[\alpha^{\mu}_{m},:\mathcal{W}(p_{i}):]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}p_{i}^{\mu}z^{m}:\mathcal{W}(p_{i}):

(90)

and

\displaystyle[\tilde{\alpha}^{\mu}_{m},:\mathcal{W}(p_{i}):]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}p_{i}^{\mu}\bar{z}^{m}:\mathcal{W}(p_{i}):

(91)

for
m\neq 0
, to obtain

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{D}\delta^{D}(p_{1}+p_{2}+k)

\displaystyle\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{\left(p_{2}^{\mu_{n,1}}\ldots p_{2}^{\mu_{n,N_{n}}}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}}(-1)^{N_{n}}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{N_{n}/2}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\prod_{(m,N_{m})\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\frac{\left(p_{2}^{\tilde{\mu}_{m,1}}\ldots p_{2}^{\tilde{\mu}_{m,N_{m}}}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{m}!m^{N_{m}}}}(-1)^{N_{m}}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{N_{m}/2}.

(92)

Note that

\langle 0;-p_{1}|e^{ip_{2}\cdot x}|0;k\rangle=(2\pi)^{D}\delta^{D}(p_{1}+p_{2}+k)

(93)

and we set
z_{2}=\bar{z}_{2}=1
. Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k|

\displaystyle V(p_{3},z_{3},\bar{z}_{3})|0;p_{4}\rangle=(2\pi)^{D}\delta^{D}(-k+p_{3}+p_{4})

\displaystyle\times\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{\left(p_{2}^{\nu_{n,1}}\ldots p_{2}^{\nu_{n,N_{n}}}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{N_{n}/2}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{(m,N_{m})\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\frac{\left(p_{2}^{\tilde{\nu}_{m,1}}\ldots p_{2}^{\tilde{\nu}_{m,N_{m}}}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{m}!m^{N_{m}}}}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{N_{m}/2}.

(94)

Consequently, this yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{N}=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{D}\delta^{D}(p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4})

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{\{\mathcal{N}\},\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\left(\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3})^{N_{n}}}{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}\prod_{(m,N_{m})\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\frac{(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3})^{N_{m}}}{N_{m}!m^{N_{m}}}\right)

(95)

In abstract algebra, the number

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{n!}{i^{k_{i}}k_{i}!}

(96)

where
n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\cdot k_{i}
counts the number elements in a conjugacy class of the symmetric group
S_{n}
with the same cycle structure. The number equals a non-negative (unsigned) version of the Stirling number of the first kind
|s(n,m)|
that counts the number of permutations of
n
objects having
m
permutation cycles. In this case
m=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}
. The signed Stirling number of the first kind
s(n,m)
relates to its absolute value
|s(n,m)|
through

s(n,m)=(-1)^{n-m}|s(n,m)|=(-1)^{n-m}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{n!}{i^{k_{i}}k_{i}!}

(97)

again with conditions

n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\cdot k_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad m=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}.

(98)

This fact allows us to rewrite

\displaystyle\sum_{\{\mathcal{N}\}}

\displaystyle\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{(-A)^{N_{n}}}{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}=\sum_{\{\mathcal{N}\}}(-A)^{\sum_{n}N_{n}}\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{1}{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{s(N,m)}{N!}(-1)^{N}A^{m}=(-1)^{N}\binom{A}{N}

(99)

with
m=\sum_{n}N_{n}
and
\binom{A}{N}
is an binomial coefficient. Using this connection, we can write (
95
) as

\mathcal{I}_{N}=(2\pi)^{D}\delta^{D}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}\right)\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{2}\cdot p_{3}}{N}^{2}

(100)

that exactly matches the expression (
24
) up to a constant.

4.2
Residue of Tachyonic General-Point Amplitudes

Next, we would like to extend the calculation to the
n
-point amplitudes. Of course, we will only consider that all external states are tachyonic for simplicity. Our aim is to evaluate the sub-amplitude

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{N}=\sum_{\{\mathcal{N}\},\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{D}k}{(2\pi)^{D}}\ \langle 0;-p_{1}|\mathcal{V}(p_{2})\ldots\mathcal{V}(p_{i})|\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k\rangle\mathcal{T}_{\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}

\displaystyle\langle\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k|\mathcal{V}(p_{i+1})\ldots\mathcal{V}(p_{n-1})|0;p_{n}\rangle.

(101)

It is useful to consider the term

\displaystyle\langle 0;-p_{1}|V(p_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\ldots V(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\alpha^{\mu}_{-m}|0;k\rangle

(102)

which equals

-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\left(p^{\mu}_{2}z^{m}_{2}+p^{\mu}_{3}z^{m}_{3}+\ldots+p^{\mu}_{i}z^{m}_{i}\right)\langle 0;-p_{1}|V(p_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\ldots V(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})|0;k\rangle.

(103)

The proof is straightforward using the commutation relation (
90
). Remind that the expression (
101
) as closed string amplitude is incomplete without integrating vertex positions
d^{2}z_{i}
over the complex plane which can be fixed up to 3 points using the global conformal symmetry. Without much effort, one can compute

\displaystyle\langle 0;

\displaystyle-p_{1}|V(p_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\ldots V(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})|\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k\rangle=

\displaystyle\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\sum_{j=2}^{i}p^{\mu_{n,k}}_{j}z^{n}_{j}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{(m,N_{m})\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N_{m}}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\sum_{j=2}^{i}p^{\tilde{\mu}_{m,k}}_{j}\bar{z}^{m}_{j}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{m}!n^{N_{m}}}}

\displaystyle\times\langle 0;-p_{1}|V(p_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\ldots V(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})|0;k\rangle.

(104)

where the state
|\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};k\rangle
is given in (
76
). Similarly,

\displaystyle\langle\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\};

\displaystyle k|V(p_{i+1},z_{i+1},\bar{z}_{i+1})\ldots V(p_{n-1},z_{n-1},\bar{z}_{n-1})|0;p_{n}\rangle=

\displaystyle\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n-1}p^{\nu_{n,k}}_{j}z^{n}_{j}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{(m,N_{m})\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n-1}p^{\tilde{\nu}_{m,k}}_{j}\bar{z}^{m}_{j}\right)}{\sqrt{N_{m}!n^{N_{m}}}}

\displaystyle\times\langle 0;k|V(p_{i+1},z_{i+1},\bar{z}_{i+1})\ldots V(p_{n-1},z_{n-1},\bar{z}_{n-1})|0;p_{n}\rangle.

(105)

The
PSL(2,\mathbb{C})
symmetry allows us to set
z_{i}=\bar{z}_{i}=1
together with
z_{n-1}=\bar{z}_{n-1}=1
.

That being so, one can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{N}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\{\mathcal{N}\},\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n-2}d^{2}z_{j}\right)\int\frac{d^{D}k}{(2\pi)^{D}}

\displaystyle\times\left[\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\left(-\sum_{j=2}^{i}p^{\mu_{n,k}}_{j}z^{n}_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{n-1}p^{\nu_{n,k}}_{j}z^{n}_{j}\right)}{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}\right.\mathcal{T}_{\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\times\Bigg[}\left.\prod_{(m,N_{m})\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{N_{m}}\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\left(-\sum_{j=2}^{i}p^{\tilde{\mu}_{m,k}}_{j}\bar{z}^{m}_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{n-1}p^{\tilde{\nu}_{m,k}}_{j}\bar{z}^{m}_{j}\right)}{N_{m}!m^{N_{m}}}\right]

\displaystyle\times\langle 0;-p_{1}|V(p_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\ldots V(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})|0;k\rangle

\displaystyle\times\langle 0;k|V(p_{i+1},z_{i+1},\bar{z}_{i+1})\ldots V(p_{n-1},z_{n-1},\bar{z}_{n-1})|0;p_{n}\rangle.

(106)

Keep in mind that now
z_{i}=\bar{z}_{i}=z_{n-1}=\bar{z}_{n-1}=1
. After performing tensorial contractions using
\mathcal{T}_{\{\mathcal{N},\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}
given in (
80
), the terms inside the squared bracket reads

\displaystyle\prod_{(n,N_{n})\in\{\mathcal{N}\}}\frac{(-1)^{N_{n}}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{i}p_{j}z^{n}_{j}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{n-1}p_{j}z^{n}_{j}\right)\right)^{N_{n}}}{N_{n}!n^{N_{n}}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{(m,N_{m})\in\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\frac{(-1)^{N_{m}}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{i}p_{j}\bar{z}^{m}_{j}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{n-1}p_{j}\bar{z}^{m}_{j}\right)\right)^{N_{m}}}{N_{m}!m^{N_{m}}}.

(107)

According to the multinomial theorem which is

(x_{1}+x_{2}+\ldots+x_{m})^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{m}\geq 0\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+\ldots+k_{m}=n\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{m}}x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}}\ldots x_{m}^{k_{m}}

(108)

where the multinomial coefficient is

\binom{n}{k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{m}}=\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\ldots k_{m}!}

(109)

Note that sum over all
k_{i}
is equal to
n
. We can use this theorem to write

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{i}p_{j}z^{n}_{j}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{l=i+1}^{n-1}p_{l}z^{n}_{l}\right)\right)^{N_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}\geq 0\\
\sum_{j,l}a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}=N_{n}\end{subarray}}\frac{N_{n}!}{\prod_{jl}a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}!}\prod_{j,l}\left(\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{j}\cdot p_{l}\right)^{a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}}(z_{j}z_{l})^{na^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}}\right)

(110)

where
j=2,3,\ldots,i
and
l=i+1,i+2,\ldots,n-1
. The superscript
(n)
of the summing variables
a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}
is just to keep track of which
n\in\{\mathcal{N}\}
we consider. Accordingly, the expression (
107
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}\geq 0\\
\sum_{j,l}a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}=N_{n}\end{subarray}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{j,l}\frac{\left(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{j}\cdot p_{l}\right)^{a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}}(z_{j}z_{l})^{na^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}}}{a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}!n^{a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b^{\scriptscriptstyle{(m)}}_{jl}\geq 0\\
\sum_{j,l}b^{\scriptscriptstyle{(m)}}_{jl}=N_{m}\end{subarray}}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\prod_{j,l}\frac{\left(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{j}\cdot p_{l}\right)^{b^{\scriptscriptstyle{(m)}}_{jl}}(\bar{z}_{j}\bar{z}_{l})^{mb^{\scriptscriptstyle{(m)}}_{jl}}}{b^{\scriptscriptstyle{(m)}}_{jl}!m^{b^{\scriptscriptstyle{(m)}}_{jl}}}.

(111)

Note that the values of the parameters
n
and
N_{n}
must belong to the set
\{\mathcal{N}\}
, as usual. Similarly, the values of
m
and
N_{m}
must correspond to the set
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}
.

We then redefine parameters such that

\displaystyle r_{jl}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}a^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}

(112)

\displaystyle s_{jl}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}b^{\scriptscriptstyle{(m)}}_{jl}

(113)

\displaystyle x_{jl}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}na^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{jl}

(114)

\displaystyle y_{jl}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}mb^{\scriptscriptstyle{(m)}}_{jl}

(115)

which are subject to

\displaystyle\sum_{j,l}x_{jl}=\sum_{n}nN_{n}=N\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j,l}y_{jl}=\sum_{m}mN_{m}=N.

(116)

Again,
N
is a string level.

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{jl}}\sum_{r_{jl}}(-1)^{N}\prod_{j,l}\frac{s(x_{jl},r_{jl})}{x_{jl}!}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{j}\cdot p_{l}\right)^{r_{jl}}(z_{j}z_{l})^{x_{jl}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{y_{jl}}\sum_{s_{jl}}(-1)^{N}\prod_{j,l}\frac{s(y_{jl},s_{jl})}{y_{jl}!}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{j}\cdot p_{l}\right)^{s_{jl}}(\bar{z}_{j}\bar{z}_{l})^{y_{jl}}

(117)

where the definition for the signed Stirling number of the first kind (
97
) was used. One can rewrite the above expression in terms of binomial coefficients using (
99
) which is

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{jl}}\sum_{y_{jl}}\prod_{j,l}\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{j}\cdot p_{l}}{x_{jl}}\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{j}\cdot p_{l}}{y_{jl}}(z_{j}z_{l})^{x_{jl}}(\bar{z}_{j}\bar{z}_{l})^{y_{jl}}.

(118)

After Substituting (
118
) to (
106
), one obtains

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{N}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\{\mathcal{N}\},\{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n-2}d^{2}z_{j}\right)\int\frac{d^{D}k}{(2\pi)^{D}}\sum_{x_{jl}}\sum_{y_{jl}}\prod_{j,l}\Bigg\{\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{j}\cdot p_{l}}{x_{jl}}\binom{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}p_{j}\cdot p_{l}}{y_{jl}}

\displaystyle\times\langle 0;-p_{1}|V(p_{2},z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\ldots V(p_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})|0;k\rangle(z_{j}z_{l})^{x_{jl}}

\displaystyle\times\langle 0;k|V(p_{i+1},z_{i+1},\bar{z}_{i+1})\ldots V(p_{n-1},z_{n-1},\bar{z}_{n-1})|0;p_{n}\rangle(\bar{z}_{j}\bar{z}_{l})^{y_{jl}}\Bigg\}.

(119)

that is subject to (
116
). It is not hard to convince yourself that the above expression recovers the residue
\mathcal{B}_{i^{\prime}}(x^{\scriptscriptstyle{(i^{\prime})}}_{jl},y^{\scriptscriptstyle{(i^{\prime})}}_{jl},\{n\},z^{*}_{i^{\prime}})
(
66

5
Conclusions

In this work, we systematically derived the BCFW recursion relations for tree-level closed string amplitudes, addressing a key challenge: the lack of an obvious structure for simple poles, unlike in open string amplitudes. To overcome this, we employed Schwinger parametrization to manipulate the Koba-Nielsen integrals, introducing
\delta
-function poles that yield simple poles upon taking residues. This allowed us to present a general expression for closed-string on-shell recursion relations, as given in (
65
).

Additionally, we performed an alternative computation of the residue for a closed string tachyon amplitude. Using the unitarity property of the amplitude, we factorized it into two fewer-point amplitudes. This was achieved by employing normalized closed string states in Fock space as a basis for constructing completeness relations. The explicit state-by-state calculation of the expression (
74
) demonstrated consistency with previous results. Furthermore, the extension of the summation from physical states to all states in Fock space is justified by the arguments in
[
31
]
, which are based on the no-ghost theorem, supporting the validity of such an extension.

Terahertz-based prognostic framework for glioma: from spectral features to patient-level prognostic assessment
Authors: Minghui Du, Rui Tao, Zhiyan Sun, Peiyuan Sun, Xianhao Wu, Yubo Wu, Tianyi Bi, Zhaohui Zhang, Xiaoyan Zhao, Dabiao Zhou, Pei Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn glioma, accurate prediction of recurrence and survival is essential for clinical decision-making and individualized management. However, current prognostic tools do not fully capture outcome heterogeneity, and convenient approaches for early postoperative prognostic evaluation are lacking. We investigated whether terahertz (THz) spectroscopy could provide label-free prognostic information. In this single-center retrospective study, 56 patients were assigned to training (n = 33) and held-out internal testing (n = 23) cohorts. A total of 443 frozen sections were measured using THz time-domain spectroscopy, and patient-level features were derived from six spectral parameters across 0.2–1.4 THz. Separate progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) signatures were developed using univariable Cox screening, LASSO-Cox selection, and multivariable Cox modeling and validated in the testing cohort. The THz-based risk score (THz-RS) significantly stratified PFS and OS in both cohorts, showed good time-dependent discrimination, and retained prognostic value after multivariable adjustment for clinicomolecular variables. Model interpretation identified the 1.2–1.3 THz band (B11) as a major contributor, while targeted LC-MS/MS revealed associations between tissue glutamate abundance and B11-derived THz features, providing preliminary biochemical support for this spectral region. These findings support THz spectroscopy as a label-free complementary tool for perioperative prognostic assessment in glioma, pending validation in larger multicenter cohorts.Subject terms: Biomarkers, Cancer, Medical research, Oncology

Introduction

Glioma is the most common and lethal primary malignant tumor of the central nervous system, and its clinical course is characterized by substantial variability in both progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS)1–3. Therefore, accurate prediction of recurrence and survival is essential for clinical decision-making and individualized management4,5. Over the past decade, successive WHO classifications have incorporated molecular markers into glioma classification, substantially refining diagnostic precision and prognostic assessment6,7. Nevertheless, clinically meaningful differences in outcomes persist, even among patients with similar histomolecular profiles. Established clinicomolecular factors, including age, WHO grade, isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) mutation status, and O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) promoter methylation status, provide important prognostic information but do not fully account for interindividual variation in clinical outcomes8–11. In addition, routine prognostic assessment still relies on postoperative pathological and molecular analyses that require specialized infrastructure, expert interpretation, and non-negligible postoperative turnaround time, with final integrated results often becoming available approximately 2 weeks after surgery12,13. Consequently, existing clinical tools cannot fully meet clinical demands, as convenient prognostic evaluation approaches for the early postoperative stage are still absent. This limitation is clinically important because earlier access to reliable prognostic information could improve surgical-pathological decision-making, perioperative counseling, and individualized treatment planning at a stage when conventional molecular results are often unavailable. Therefore, there is a clear unmet need for a rapid, label-free, tissue-based spectroscopic approach that can provide complementary prognostic information directly from surgical specimens.

Terahertz time-domain spectroscopy (THz-TDS) is an emerging biophotonic spectroscopic technique that measures the dielectric response of biological tissues in the far-infrared range, which is influenced by water content, cellularity, microstructural organization, and biomolecular composition14–16. THz-TDS provides quantitative spectral readouts directly from freshly excised tissues without the need for labeling, staining, or molecular reagents. This combination of label-free detection, limited sample preparation, and compatibility with fresh frozen sections makes THz-TDS particularly attractive for perioperative applications. In neuro-oncology, previous studies have shown that terahertz-based measurements can distinguish gliomas from normal brain tissue17, assist in histopathological grading18,19, and identify clinically relevant molecular alterations20,21. In particular, our group previously established the feasibility of THz-TDS for clinicopathological and molecular characterization of human glioma using frozen sections, showing that THz spectral features were associated with tumor site, histological grade, histological type, and glioma-specific biomarkers22,23. We subsequently extended this framework to molecular biomarker prediction, including IDH mutation status and MGMT promoter methylation status, using machine learning models built on THz-derived features24–26. However, these prior studies have mainly focused on diagnostic and molecular classification tasks, whereas the prognostic relevance of THz-derived features for subsequent patient-level clinical outcomes has not been systematically evaluated. This distinction is clinically important because diagnostic or molecular classification does not necessarily indicate whether THz-derived features provide additional prognostic information beyond established clinicomolecular markers. Therefore, the value of THz-derived features for long-term glioma prognostic assessment remains insufficiently explored.

The present study was designed to address this gap by extending THz-based glioma analysis from diagnostic and molecular classification to patient-level prognostic assessment. Using THz-TDS on frozen sections prepared from glioma surgical specimens, we developed THz-based risk scores for PFS and OS and evaluated their performance in a held-out testing cohort. We further assessed whether these risk scores provided prognostic information in addition to established clinicomolecular variables and constructed nomograms for survival probability estimation. Finally, model interpretation was used to identify key spectral contributors, including the 1.2–1.3 THz band, whose potential biological relevance was further explored through glutamate-related spectral evidence and targeted LC-MS/MS quantification (Fig. 1). Collectively, this study evaluates quantitative THz spectroscopy as a rapid, label-free complementary approach for perioperative glioma prognostic risk stratification.

Development and validation of THz-based prognostic signatures for PFS and OS

For PFS, the THz band characteristics were screened in the training cohort according to the workflow shown in Fig. 1D. After univariable Cox prescreening and LASSO shrinkage, nine THz features were retained and subsequently entered into a multivariable Cox model to construct the final PFS signature, including tan_e_B11_mean, e_real_B11_slope, tan_e_B8_mean, e_real_B2_sd, ns_B5_slope, alpha_B3_slope, kappa_B6_slope, ns_B2_sd, and e_imag_B11_mean (Supplementary Fig. 1D). Heatmaps of the training and testing cohorts showed distinct THz feature patterns between the low- and high-risk groups, accompanied by coordinated clinicopathological annotations. The high-risk group tended to be enriched for GBM, IDH-wildtype status, MGMT unmethylation, and older age, whereas the low-risk group was more frequently characterized by non-GBM pathology, IDH mutation, MGMT methylation, and younger age. No obvious clustering patterns were observed for sex (Fig. 2A, B). Partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) and t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) further showed separable distributions between the two risk groups, and the THz-RS distribution plots demonstrated that patients with shorter PFS were more likely to cluster at the high-risk end (Supplementary Fig. 2A–D). Kaplan–Meier analysis showed that the high-risk group had a significantly shorter PFS than the low-risk group in both the training (Fig. 2C, P = 0.0002) and testing cohorts (Fig. 2D, P = 0.0027). The PFS signature yielded C-indices of 0.822 (95% CI: 0.722–0.920) in the training cohort and 0.785 (95% CI: 0.704–0.861) in the testing cohort, with optimism-corrected C-index reaching 0.692 in the training set. Time-dependent ROC analysis demonstrated good predictive performance, with AUCs of 0.871 (95% CI: 0.713–0.994), 0.896 (95% CI: 0.772–1.000), and 0.889 (95% CI: 0.758–0.982) for 1-, 1.5-, and 2-year PFS, respectively, in the training cohort (Fig. 2E), and 0.924 (95% CI: 0.822–1.000), 0.845 (95% CI: 0.706–0.959), and 0.838 (95% CI: 0.717–0.965), respectively, in the testing cohort (Fig. 2F). After optimism correction, the training cohort 1-year, 1.5-year and 2-year AUCs were adjusted to 0.731, 0.759 and 0.734 respectively.

For OS, candidate THz features identified by univariable Cox regression are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1A, and LASSO shrinkage further reduced the feature set (Supplementary Fig. 1B). Ultimately, three THz features, e_real_B11_slope, e_real_B7_slope, and ns_B2_mean, were retained in the final OS signature (Supplementary Fig. 1C). Heatmaps likewise revealed distinct distribution patterns of the selected THz features and clinicopathological characteristics between the low- and high-risk groups in both cohorts. Similar to the PFS model, the OS high-risk group tended to be associated with GBM, IDH-wildtype status, MGMT unmethylation, and older age, whereas the low-risk group more often showed non-GBM pathology, IDH mutation, MGMT methylation, and younger age (Fig. 2G, H). PLS-DA and t-SNE analyses also showed distinguishable distributions of the two risk groups, whereas the THz-RS distribution plots indicated that a shorter OS was concentrated toward the high-risk end (Supplementary Fig. 2E–H). Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated significantly worse OS in the high-risk group in both the training (Fig. 2I, P = 0.035) and testing cohorts (Fig. 2J, P = 0.040). The OS signature yielded C-indices of 0.831 (95% CI: 0.710–0.951) in the training cohort and 0.845 (95% CI: 0.735–0.937) in the testing cohort, with an optimism-corrected C-index of 0.748 for the training cohort. Time-dependent ROC analysis further confirmed the predictive value of the OS signature, with AUCs of 0.849 (95% CI: 0.710–0.938), 0.884 (95% CI: 0.757–1.000), and 0.906 (95% CI: 0.767–1.000) for 1-, 1.5-, and 2-year OS, respectively, in the training cohort (Fig. 2K) and 0.850 (95% CI: 0.789–1.000), 0.938 (95% CI: 0.842–1.000), and 0.879 (95% CI: 0.718–1.000), respectively, in the testing cohort (Fig. 2L). Notably, the B11-derived features were retained in both the PFS and OS models (Supplementary Fig. 1C, D). After optimism correction, the PFS model showed a training C-index of 0.692 and AUCs of 0.731, 0.759, and 0.734 at 1, 1.5, and 2 years, respectively. The OS model showed a corrected training C-index of 0.748 and corresponding AUCs of 0.762, 0.806, and 0.806, respectively, indicating that prognostic discrimination was retained after correction for optimism (Supplementary Table 1).

Subgroup analyses of the THz-RS in molecular and clinicopathologic strata of glioma

To further assess the prognostic stratification ability of the THz-based risk score across clinically relevant subgroups, Kaplan–Meier analyses were performed according to IDH mutation status, MGMT promoter methylation status, and glioma grade (Fig. 3). For PFS, the THz-based risk score showed significant prognostic stratification across all the analyzed subgroups. Significant differences in PFS between the low- and high-risk groups were observed in the IDH-mutant (Fig. 3A, P = 0.00027) and IDH-wildtype subgroups (Fig. 3D, P = 0.00088), the MGMT promoter-methylated (Fig. 3B, P = 0.00046) and MGMT promoter-unmethylated subgroups (Fig. 3E, P = 0.0055), and the high-grade glioma (Fig. 3C, P = 0.00024) and lower-grade glioma subgroups (Fig. 3F, P = 0.0071). In all strata, patients in the high-risk group had a shorter PFS than those in the low-risk group. In terms of OS, the THz-RS also distinguished patients with different outcomes in most clinically relevant subgroups. Significant differences in OS between the low- and high-risk groups were observed in the IDH-mutant subgroup (Fig. 3G, P = 0.026), IDH-wildtype subgroup (Fig. 3J, P = 0.042), MGMT promoter-unmethylated subgroup (Fig. 3K, P = 0.005), and high-grade glioma subgroup (Fig. 3I, P = 0.0038). In the MGMT promoter-methylated subgroup, although the difference in OS between the two risk groups did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 3H, P = 0.22), the survival curves showed a consistent trend, with the high-risk group tending to have worse OS. In the lower-grade glioma subgroup (Fig. 3L), the log-rank P-value was not estimable because no patients in the low-risk group reached the OS endpoint during follow-up. Taken together, these findings support the prognostic value of THz-RS across multiple molecular and clinicopathological subgroups of gliomas for both PFS and OS.

Prognostic analysis of THz-RS and clinicomolecular variables

To assess the prognostic value of THz-RS beyond established clinicomolecular variables, univariable and multivariable Cox analyses were performed in the training and testing cohorts. In the PFS analysis, univariable Cox regression in the training cohort identified THz-RS, age, and IDH status as significant variables, whereas the MGMT status was not. After multivariable adjustment, the THz-based risk score remained significantly associated with PFS (HR, 2.324; 95% CI, 1.232–4.383; P = 0.009). In the testing cohort, THz-RS, age, IDH status, and MGMT status were significant in univariable analysis; however, only THz-RS remained significant in the multivariable model (HR, 1.681; 95% CI, 1.162–2.434; P = 0.006) (Fig. 4A). In the OS analysis, THz-RS was significantly associated with outcomes in both the training and testing cohorts in the univariable analysis. In the training cohort, MGMT status was also significant in the univariable analysis, whereas age and IDH status were not. After adjustment, THz-RS remained significantly associated with OS in the training cohort (HR, 2.076; 95% CI, 1.151–3.745; P = 0.015). In the testing cohort, THz-RS was the only variable significantly associated with OS on univariable analysis, and this association persisted after multivariable adjustment (HR, 4.291; 95% CI, 1.086–16.958; P = 0.038) (Fig. 4B). We further expanded the Cox regression framework by integrating additional glioma-associated molecular markers (1p/19q codeletion, TERT promoter mutation, CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion, EGFR amplification), WHO grade, resection extent, and adjuvant treatment status. Following univariate screening for candidate prognostic predictors, THz-RS retained prognostic significance for PFS across both training and testing cohorts (Supplementary Fig. 3A). For OS analyses, multiple molecular and clinical subgroups contained zero or extremely few mortality events, such that simultaneous adjustment for abundant covariates produced unstable, non-interpretable hazard ratio estimates; hence reliable expanded multivariable OS modeling was not feasible under the current sample size (Supplementary Fig. 3B and Supplementary Table 2).

Development and validation of integrated nomograms for PFS and OS

To facilitate patient-level prognostic assessment, nomograms integrating the THz-based risk score, age, IDH status, and MGMT promoter methylation status were constructed for PFS and OS. In the PFS analysis, the nomogram incorporated THz-RS together with the clinical and molecular variables, with the risk score contributing the largest proportion of points (Fig. 5A). The PFS nomogram yielded C-indices of 0.846 (95% CI, 0.754–0.934) and 0.804 (95% CI, 0.709–0.892) in the training and testing cohorts, respectively. Time-dependent ROC analysis showed discriminative performance in both cohorts, with AUCs of 0.892 (95% CI, 0.744–1.000), 0.907 (95% CI, 0.790–1.000), and 0.904 (95% CI, 0.785–1.000) for 1-, 1.5-, and 2-year PFS, respectively, in the training cohort (Fig. 5B), and 0.937 (95% CI, 0.841–1.000), 0.868 (95% CI, 0.720–0.988), and 0.939 (95% CI, 0.827–1.000), respectively, in the testing cohort (Fig. 5C). Calibration plots showed agreement between the nomogram-predicted and observed PFS probabilities in the training cohort, with overall concordance maintained in the testing cohort (Fig. 5D, E). In the decision curve analysis, both the nomogram and THz-RS yielded greater net benefits than age, IDH status, or MGMT status alone across most threshold probabilities for 1-, 1.5-, and 2-year PFS (Supplementary Fig. 4A–F). In the OS analysis, the nomogram yielded C-indices of 0.887 (95% CI, 0.788–0.975) and 0.774 (95% CI, 0.670–0.876) in the training and testing cohorts, respectively. Time-dependent ROC analysis showed AUCs of 0.925 (95% CI, 0.760–1.000), 0.962 (95% CI, 0.887–1.000), and 0.941 (95% CI, 0.805–1.000) for 1-, 1.5-, and 2-year OS, respectively, in the training cohort (Fig. 5G), and 0.767 (95% CI, 0.636–0.909), 0.812 (95% CI, 0.708–0.938), and 0.836 (95% CI, 0.749–1.000), respectively, in the testing cohort (Fig. 5H). Calibration plots showed agreement between nomogram-predicted and observed OS probabilities in the training cohort, with overall concordance in the testing cohort (Fig. 5I, J). In the decision curve analysis (DCA), the nomogram and THz-RS provided a higher net benefit than age, IDH status, or MGMT status alone across most threshold probabilities for 1-, 1.5-, and 2-year OS (Supplementary Fig. 4G–L). Overall, these results support the predictive performance of the integrated nomograms for both PFS and OS.

SHAP-based interpretation and analysis of the 1.2–1.3 THz band

To interpret the THz-based prognostic models, Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) analysis was performed for both endpoints. In the PFS model, B11-derived features accounted for most of the top-ranked variables, with e_imag_B11_mean, tan_e_B11_mean, and e_real_B11_slope contributing 26.4%, 26.0%, and 17.8% of the total mean absolute SHAP importance, respectively (Fig. 6A). In the OS model, ns_B2_mean ranked first (37.6%), followed by e_real_B11_slope (36.1%), and e_real_B7_slope (26.4%) (Fig. 6B). Thus, B11-related features were among the major contributors in both models, particularly in the PFS model. Consistent with this, an exploratory reduced PFS model constructed from the three highest-ranking B11-derived features identified by SHAP (e_real_B11_slope, e_imag_B11_mean, and tan_e_B11_mean) demonstrated significant risk stratification and time-dependent discrimination and remained associated with PFS after multivariable adjustment in both cohorts (Supplementary Fig. 5).

To further evaluate the clinical relevance of the 1.2–1.3 THz (B11) band, Kaplan–Meier survival analyses were performed for both PFS and OS, with patients stratified into low- and high-value groups based on the patient-level median of each of the six terahertz spectral parameters within this frequency range. For PFS, e_real_B11 and ns_B11 achieved significant risk stratification (Fig. 6D, P = 0.013; Fig. 6G, P = 0.013), whereas alpha_B11, e_imag_B11, kappa_B11, and tan_e_B11 showed no significant prognostic differences (Fig. 6C, E, F, and H). For OS, consistent discriminative performance was observed in the B11 band: e_real_B11 and ns_B11 reliably stratified patients into high- and low-risk groups with significantly distinct OS (both P = 0.013), whereas alpha_B11, e_imag_B11, kappa_B11, and tan_e_B11 failed to demonstrate significant prognostic value for OS (Supplementary Fig. 6). Next, we compared the mean values across the B11 frequency range according to IDH status and tumor grade. Compared with IDH-mutant tumors, IDH-wildtype tumors showed significantly higher B11-band mean values for alpha, e_imag, kappa, and tan_e (Fig. 6I; P = 0.002, 0.001, 0.002, and 0.008, respectively), whereas e_real and ns were not significantly different (P = 0.170 and 0.170, respectively). Similarly, compared with lower-grade gliomas, high-grade gliomas showed significantly higher B11-band mean values for alpha, e_imag, kappa, and tan_e (Fig. 6J; P = 0.001, 0.002, 0.001, and 0.006, respectively), whereas e_real and ns showed no significant differences (P = 0.244 and 0.244, respectively). Taken together, these results show that B11-related features were recurrently highlighted by the prognostic models and that several B11-band parameters were elevated in IDH-wildtype and high-grade gliomas.

Literature-supported spectroscopic evidence for the B11 band in glioma

To further examine the spectroscopic reference signals for the B11 band (1.2–1.3 THz) highlighted by the SHAP analysis, we surveyed the Japanese THz Database (http://thzdb.org/) together with the published literature and performed a bibliometric analysis. Across the database records and 12 published THz studies of glutamate, the reported absorption peaks were consistently located within the 1.2–1.3 THz range (Supplementary Fig. 7A), overlapping the B11 frequency band identified in the present study. Keyword burst analysis arranged by time further revealed a clear evolution in glutamate-related glioma research from 2000 to 2026 (Supplementary Fig. 7B). Early burst terms were mainly centered on experimental glioma and glial models, including C6 glioma, rat brain, glial cells, astrocytes, cultured astrocytes, amino acid transporters, and rat. Subsequent burst terms shifted toward glutamate-associated transport and metabolic processes, including glutamate transporters, glutathione, glutamate uptake, and gene expression, and included both in vitro and in vivo studies, indicating the need for continued investigation in cellular and animal settings. In recent years, burst keywords have become increasingly linked to glioma biology and clinical research, including malignant glioma, isocitrate dehydrogenase 1, oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate, survival, low-grade glioma, brain tumor, temozolomide, tumor microenvironment, mouse model, stem cells, and glioblastoma. Consistently, the keyword co-occurrence network positioned glutamate among the major nodes in the glioma literature and showed dense connections with terms such as glioma cells, glioblastoma, growth, and expression (Supplementary Fig. 7C).

Targeted glutamate quantification reveals associations between tissue glutamate and B11-derived THz features

Targeted LC-MS/MS quantification of tissue glutamate was performed in ten patients from the original cohort, including five patients from the PFS THz-RS high-risk group and five from the low-risk group. Tissue glutamate concentrations were significantly higher in the high-risk group than in the low-risk group (922.08 ± 343.92 vs. 418.08 ± 274.90 μg/g, P = 0.0337, Fig. 7A). Among the B11-derived THz features identified in the PFS model, e_imag_B11_mean, the most important feature according to SHAP analysis, showed a positive correlation with tissue glutamate levels (Spearman’s ρ = 0.745, P = 0.013; Fig. 7B). The other two B11-derived features, tan_e_B11_mean and e_real_B11_slope, also showed associations with glutamate concentrations (Supplementary Fig. 8). These findings provide preliminary biochemical support for the biological relevance of the B11 spectral region in glioma.

Discussion

In this retrospective study of 56 patients with glioma, we developed and validated quantitative prognostic signatures based on THz time-domain spectroscopy for both PFS and OS. THz-TDS is inherently sensitive to tissue dielectric properties, which are influenced by the water content, cellular density, and biomolecular composition27. Consistent with this biophysical basis, previous applications of THz spectroscopy in neuro-oncology have largely focused on diagnostic and classification-oriented tasks, including label-free discrimination between gliomas and normal brain tissues, histopathological grading, and molecular subtype recognition28. However, these studies did not extend THz analysis to patient-level survival prediction. Most prior patient-based THz glioma studies involved relatively small human cohorts, including 26 patients in gelatin-embedded tissue spectroscopy19 and 12 patients in fresh-tissue ATR analysis23. Against this background, the present cohort of 56 patients with complete follow-up for both PFS and OS represents, to our knowledge, the largest patient-based THz glioma study designed around survival endpoints. More importantly, it demonstrates that THz-derived quantitative features can be translated from tissue characterization and classification to clinically relevant prognostic stratification, with consistent performance across the training and held-out internal testing cohorts. Furthermore, targeted LC-MS/MS quantification of tissue glutamate provided preliminary biochemical evidence supporting the biological relevance of the prognostically important B11 spectral region.

A more clinically relevant question is whether such THz-based information adds value to the current histomolecular framework of gliomas. The 2021 WHO Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System further reinforced the central role of molecular biomarkers in glioma classification and risk assessment6; however, substantial prognostic heterogeneity persists even within the molecularly defined subtypes8,29. In the present study, THz-RS retained prognostic significance across subgroups stratified by IDH mutation status, MGMT promoter methylation status, and low- versus high-grade disease in Kaplan–Meier analyses and remained associated with PFS and OS after adjustment for age, IDH mutation status, and MGMT promoter methylation status. Taken together, these findings suggest that THz-derived features may provide prognostic information complementary to established clinicomolecular variables, thereby supporting more refined survival stratification within the current histomolecular framework.

Building on the prognostic value of THz-RS, we focused on the 1.2–1.3 THz band (B11), which was recurrently highlighted by SHAP analysis as an important spectral region contributing to survival prediction. Within this band, two categories of THz parameters exhibited clearly divergent association patterns with clinicopathological features and survival endpoints: absorptive/dissipative parameters, including the absorption coefficient, extinction coefficient, dielectric loss factor, and dielectric loss tangent, were associated with IDH mutation status and WHO tumor grade but did not show significant survival stratification. In contrast, refractive/dispersive parameters, represented by the refractive index and dielectric constant, showed no significant differences across molecular or histologic subgroups, yet enabled significant stratification for both PFS and OS. This observed divergence suggests that the prognostic signal captured by the B11 band does not simply recapitulate conventional classification systems; rather, distinct THz features within the same frequency window may reflect complementary aspects of tumor tissue properties. Absorptive/dissipative parameters are strongly influenced by tissue water content and overall tissue composition, and their higher values in high-grade and IDH-wildtype gliomas are consistent with findings from previous THz glioma studies18,30,31. Meanwhile, ns_B11 and e_real_B11 showed significant prognostic stratification despite showing no association with IDH mutation status or WHO grade. This finding indicates that these refractive/dispersive parameters reflect dimensions of tissue heterogeneity not captured by established clinicomolecular markers, and may thus provide complementary biological information related to tumor aggressiveness and long-term clinical outcomes.

A survey of THz spectral databases and the published literature identified reported glutamate-related THz absorption features near the 1.2–1.3 THz range, overlapping with the B11 band highlighted by SHAP analysis as an important prognostic spectral region in our study32–43. To explore whether this spectral observation might have biological relevance, we performed a bibliometric analysis of glutamate and glioma research. Glutamate has been extensively studied in glioma biology. In 2001, Takano et al. demonstrated that glutamate release promotes malignant glioma growth44. Subsequent studies have confirmed that glutamate dysregulation promotes glioma cell proliferation, invasion, and malignant progression45–47. MRS-based studies have shown that glutamate levels differ according to IDH status: IDH-mutant gliomas have lower glutamate levels than IDH-wildtype tumors48. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that glutamatergic signaling drives glioma invasion and progression and predicts poor prognosis49. High extracellular and intracellular glutamate levels are correlated with shorter progression-free and overall survival. A phase II clinical trial tested an AMPA receptor inhibitor for recurrent malignant gliomas50, highlighting the translational potential of targeting glutamate signaling. Together, these spectral and biological observations provided a rationale for investigating whether glutamate-related metabolic alterations might contribute to the prognostic signal detected in the B11 THz band.

Previous metabolic imaging studies in glioma have demonstrated the prognostic value of spectroscopy-detected metabolites. Magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS)—particularly proton (¹H-MRS)—enables non-invasive detection of metabolites including choline, creatine, N-acetylaspartate (NAA), glutamate (Glu), glutamine (Gln), lactate (Lac), and mobile lipids, and has been extensively investigated for glioma diagnosis, grading, treatment monitoring, and survival prediction51. In adult gliomas, MRS-detected lactate/NAA ratios have been shown to predict early post-radiotherapy outcome, with Lac/NAA > 2.0 associated with markedly poorer 1-year survival52. In paediatric brain tumours, short-echo-time MRS identified lipids and scyllo-inositol as markers of poor survival, while glutamine and NAA predicted improved survival, and a multivariable model combining three MRS biomarkers achieved prognostic accuracy comparable to histologic grading53. At higher field strengths such as 7 T, improved spectral dispersion and signal-to-noise ratio permit more reliable quantification of glutamate and glutamine individually, as well as detection of oncometabolites such as 2-hydroxyglutarate in IDH-mutant gliomas54. Collectively, these studies indicate that tumor-associated metabolic alterations can provide clinically relevant information regarding glioma phenotype and outcome, providing a biological context for the prognostic spectral features observed in the present study. Consistent with this metabolic context, targeted LC-MS/MS in our study linked higher tissue glutamate abundance to the PFS THz-RS high-risk phenotype and to B11-derived THz features. Together with reported glutamate-related THz absorption near 1.2–1.3 THz, these findings provide preliminary biochemical support for glutamate-related metabolic alterations as a potential contributor to the prognostic B11 signal.

Beyond its biological interpretability, the present THz-based prognostic framework may also offer practical advantages over conventional postoperative molecular testing and perioperative diagnostic workflows for gliomas. Conventional molecular testing relies on laborious post-resection processing, DNA extraction, and targeted sequencing assays, which rarely yield actionable information intraoperatively12. In contrast, our THz-TDS platform enables direct, label-free analysis of fresh frozen sections and can generate quantitative prognostic data within approximately 60 min of tissue resection, without molecular reagents or lengthy turnaround times, highlighting its potential relevance to future intraoperative or early perioperative prognostic workflows. Although preoperative MRI radiomics has shown prognostic utility in gliomas, such features are derived from processed and harmonized imaging data and reflect indirect macroscopic tissue characteristics rather than direct measurements of tumor tissue55–57. Unlike radiomic features, THz-TDS directly quantifies the intrinsic dielectric properties of fresh tissue, which are influenced by water content, cellularity, and biomolecular composition, thereby providing a biophysically grounded prognostic layer complementary to imaging and molecular profiles. Notably, related label-free spectroscopic approaches that capture tissue biochemical or biophysical signatures, particularly Raman spectroscopy, have shown prognostic utility in multiple solid tumors. In gastric cancer, Raman-based signatures outperformed conventional ypTNM staging in predicting overall survival (AUC = 0.955)58 and yielded reliable prognostic stratification following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (C-index=0.773)59, with comparable discriminatory capacity also documented for recurrence stratification in non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (AUC = 0.961)60. Collectively, these findings suggest that quantitative spectral signatures can provide clinically meaningful prognostic information not fully captured by conventional clinicomolecular markers, supporting the potential of THz-TDS as a rapid, reagent-free adjunct to current histomolecular and imaging-based risk stratification pipelines.

In summary, we developed and internally validated a THz-TDS-based framework for rapid, label-free prediction of progression-free and overall survival in glioma. The THz-based risk score demonstrated prognostic performance in both cohorts and enabled survival probability estimation when integrated with established clinicomolecular variables. Model interpretation identified the 1.2–1.3 THz band as a key spectral contributor, while targeted LC-MS/MS provided preliminary biochemical support for an association between B11-derived THz features and tissue glutamate abundance. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective single-center design, modest sample size, and limited number of outcome events increase the risk of model overfitting and may limit generalizability; although held-out internal testing and bootstrap optimism correction were performed, residual overfitting cannot be excluded. Second, the model was built on resected frozen sections, and its intraoperative feasibility requires prospective verification. Third, the LC-MS/MS analysis was exploratory and limited to ten patients, and larger studies with broader biochemical profiling are needed to validate the observed glutamate–B11 associations. Fourth, the limited sample size restricted comprehensive adjustment for glioma-related prognostic factors, and residual confounding from WHO grade, resection extent, adjuvant therapy, and additional molecular alterations cannot be fully ruled out. Despite these limitations, our findings provide proof-of-concept evidence for extending terahertz spectroscopy to patient-level prognostic assessment and support its potential as a complementary tool for perioperative risk stratification, pending prospective multicenter validation and further mechanistic investigation.

Methods

Study design and patient cohort

This single-center retrospective cohort study established a label-free THz-TDS-based prognostic framework for primary diffuse glioma following a three-stage workflow of feature extraction, model construction, and clinical analysis (Fig. 1). Patients who underwent surgical resection for glioma between September 2021 and December 2024 were screened for eligibility, and only those with valid THz-TDS measurements on frozen sections were included. A total of 443 frozen glioma sections were acquired from the study participants, and 56 patients with primary diffuse glioma constituted the final analytical cohort (Supplementary Table 3). The detailed screening process is illustrated in Fig. 8A. The cohort was randomly divided into a training cohort (n = 33) and a held-out internal testing cohort (n = 23) at a 6:4 ratio for model development and validation, respectively. All analyses were performed at the patient level. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Tiantan Hospital (KY2024-015-02), and written informed consent was obtained from all patients before surgery. PFS was defined as the interval from surgical resection to the first MRI-confirmed radiographic progression or the last follow-up, whichever occurred first. OS was defined as the interval from surgical resection to death from any cause or the last follow-up. Parallel prognostic models were developed separately for PFS and OS using the same workflow.

Study cohort selection and THz-TDS measurement setup.

A Patient enrollment and exclusion flowchart. B Schematic diagram of THz-TDS detection.

Frozen-section preparation and THz-TDS acquisition

Fresh surgical specimens were rapidly embedded in a tissue-embedding agent (O.C.T.) and cryosectioned into 100 μm-thick frozen sections, which were mounted on 2 mm-thick polyethylene (PE) substrates and stored at −80 °C until spectroscopic measurements (Fig. 1A). THz spectra were acquired using a transmission-mode THz-TDS system, as shown schematically in Fig. 8B. The system was driven by a femtosecond laser, and the optical beam was split into pump and probe paths via a beam splitter. A mechanical delay line was used to adjust the time delay between the two beams. THz radiation was generated by the THz emitter, focused onto the frozen section sample via off-axis parabolic mirrors, and detected by the THz detector. The entire optical path was enclosed in a nitrogen-purged chamber, with the relative humidity maintained below 3% and the ambient temperature stabilized at 20 ± 0.2 °C to minimize environmental interference. A femtosecond laser served as the excitation source, with a scan step size of 33.3 fs and 1024 time-domain sampling points collected for each acquisition. Blank PE substrates were used as reference signals to correct for background absorption and noise. Given the reduced signal-to-noise ratio at frequencies above 1.4 THz, only the 0.2–1.4 THz spectral range was retained for subsequent feature extraction and analyses. The experimental platform and environmental control protocols were consistent with those established in our previous glioma-focused THz investigations, ensuring methodological reproducibility23.

THz feature extraction

We used αs(ω) to represent the absorption coefficient, ns(ω) the refractive index, and κs(ω) the extinction coefficient; δ denotes the dielectric loss angle, and εr(ω) and εi(ω) represent the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric constant, respectively. In addition, d denotes the sample thickness, nPE the refractive index of polyethylene (PE), T(ω) the sample transfer function, and c is the speed of light in vacuum. To calculate these parameters, the fast Fourier transform (FFT) was applied to convert the raw signals collected by the THz-TDS system into frequency-domain signals. The field strength signals of the sample and reference were denoted as Esam(ω) and Eref(ω), respectively, and their ratio was defined as the transfer function T(ω). Based on T(ω), the refractive index (ns(ω)), extinction coefficient (κs(ω)), and absorption coefficient (αs(ω)) were first calculated, after which the real and imaginary parts of the dielectric constant εr(ω) and εi(ω) and the dielectric loss tangent (tanδ) were further derived according to Eqs. (1)-(6).

Accordingly, six terahertz spectral parameter families were obtained for each section: refractive index (ns), extinction coefficient (kappa), absorption coefficient (alpha), dielectric constant real part (e_real), dielectric constant imaginary part (e_imag), and dielectric loss tangent (tan_e). Within the 0.2–1.4 THz range, 82 discrete frequency points were retained for each parameter, yielding 492 raw spectral variables per section (6 × 82). As the survival endpoints were defined at the patient level, the patient was used as the modeling unit. The spectrum was further divided into 12 consecutive 0.1-THz bands (B1-B12), and for each parameter in each band (Fig. 1B), the mean, standard deviation, and first-order linear slope were calculated, generating 216 band-level features per section (6 × 12 × 3) (Fig. 1C). For each patient, the median value of each feature across all available sections was used to construct the final patient-level THz feature matrix.

Development of THz prognostic signatures

PFS and OS signatures were developed independently. All feature filtering, normalization, selection, and model fitting were performed in the training set only, and the testing set was reserved for held-out internal validation. Features with zero variance or insufficient information were eliminated. The remaining features were standardized using z-score normalization, with scaling parameters estimated in the training set and applied unchanged to the testing set. Each retained feature was first assessed using univariable Cox proportional hazards regression, and features with P < 0.10 were entered into a least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO)-Cox model. The penalty parameter was selected using 5-fold cross-validation. Features with non-zero coefficients were then entered into the final multivariable Cox model, and the linear predictor was defined as THz-RS (Fig. 1D). The median risk score in the training set was used as the prespecified cutoff to stratify patients into high- and low-risk groups, and the same cutoff was applied to the testing set without recalibration.

Prognostic analysis and nomogram construction

To assess whether the prognostic value of THz-RS persisted after multivariable adjustment, we built a parsimonious Cox model incorporating THz-RS, age, IDH mutation status, and MGMT promoter methylation status. Extended analyses incorporating 1p/19q codeletion, TERT, CDKN2A/B, EGFR status and treatment covariates are provided in Supplementary Fig. 3. Nomograms were built to predict 1-, 1.5-, and 2-year PFS and OS probabilities.

Model interpretation and exploratory biological analysis

Model interpretability was assessed using Shapley additive explanations. SHAP values were calculated for the features retained in the final PFS and OS signatures, and feature importance was ranked by the mean absolute SHAP value. To explore the biological relevance of the dominant THz band identified by SHAP, a targeted literature review was conducted using the Japanese THz Database and published literature. After deduplication and screening, 12 published terahertz studies on glutamate were included, and the reported absorption peak positions were extracted. In parallel, a bibliometric analysis was performed using the Web of Science Core Collection with the topic query “glioma” AND “glutamate,” restricted to articles and review articles, yielding 1,152 records. After removal of duplicates and records with insufficient bibliographic information, keyword burst analysis was performed in CiteSpace (version 6.4) and keyword co-occurrence analysis in VOSviewer (version 1.6.20). Synonymous terms were manually harmonized when required.

Targeted quantification of tissue glutamate

For this pilot analysis, residual O.C.T.-embedded glioma tissues archived at −80 °C were available from ten patients in the original cohort, including five patients in each of the PFS THz-RS high- and low-risk groups. Before metabolite extraction, the tissues were washed with ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline to minimize residual O.C.T. compound, and approximately 20–40 mg of tissue was used for analysis. Targeted glutamate quantification was performed by Novogene (Beijing, China) using ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC–MS/MS). Glutamate was absolutely quantified in multiple-reaction-monitoring mode using an ExionLC™ AD UHPLC system coupled to a QTRAP® 6500+ tandem mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX, USA). Glutamate concentrations were normalized to tissue weight and expressed as μg/g tissue.

Model evaluation and statistical analysis

The model performance was evaluated in terms of discrimination, risk stratification, calibration, and clinical utility. Kaplan-Meier analysis with the two-sided log-rank test was used to compare survival between the risk groups (Fig. 1E). Discrimination was quantified using the concordance index (C-index) and time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curves, with area under the curve values calculated at 1, 1.5, and 2 years. For the nomograms, calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA) were used to assess the agreement between the predicted and observed outcomes and potential net benefits, respectively. PLS-DA, t-SNE, risk score distribution plots, survival status plots, and heatmaps annotated with clinicopathological variables were generated for visualization. 1000 bootstrap resamples on the training cohort generated 95% CIs and optimism-corrected discrimination metrics against overfitting. All analyses were performed using R version 4.5.0. The main packages included survival, glmnet, survminer, timeROC, survcomp, rms, pec, ggplot2, pheatmap, Rtsne, mixOmics, and ggDCA. Baseline differences between the training and testing sets were assessed using the Unpaired t test or Fisher exact test, as appropriate. All tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Glutamate concentrations were compared between the PFS THz-RS high- and low-risk groups using the Welch t-test, and their associations with B11-derived THz features were assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation.

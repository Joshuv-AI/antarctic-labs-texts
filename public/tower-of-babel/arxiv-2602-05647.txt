Fundamental solution for higher order homogeneous hypoelliptic operators structured on Hörmander vector fields
Stefano Biagi, Marco Bramanti

Abstract
. We introduce and study a new class of higher order differential operators defined on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , which are built with Hörmander vector fields, homogeneous w.r.t. a family of dilations (but not left invariant w.r.t. any structure of Lie group) and have a structure such that a suitably lifted version of the operator is hypoelliptic. We call these operators “generalized Rockland operators”. We prove that these operators are themselves hypoelliptic and, under a natural condition on the homogeneity degree, possess a global fundamental solution Γ ⁡ ( x , y ) \Gamma\left(x,y\right) which is jointly homogeneous in ( x , y ) \left(x,y\right) and satisfies sharp pointwise estimates. Our theory can be applied also to some higher order heat-type operators and their fundamental solutions.

1.
Introduction

Second order hypoelliptic operators

The theory of second order hypoelliptic operators is deeply related
to the notion of system of Hörmander vector fields, in view of the famous
hypoellipticity theorem proved by Hörmander in
[
21
]
. Assume that

X_{0},X_{1},...,X_{m}

is a family of smooth real vector fields defined in some open set

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the Lie algebra generated by these

X_{i}
at every point of
\Omega
has rank
n
. This means that if we define
the
commutator
of two vector fields
X,Y
letting, as usual,

\left[X,Y\right]=XY-YX,

then, among the vector fields
X_{i}
and those obtained taking their
commutators
\left[X_{i},X_{j}\right]
, the iterated commutators
\left[\left[X_{i},X_{j}\right],X_{k}\right]
, and so on up to a certain step,
at every point of
\Omega
we can find
n
independent vectors. In this case,
we say that
X_{0},X_{1},...,X_{m}
are
a system of Hörmander vector
fields
, or that they satisfy
Hörmander’s condition
in
\Omega
.
Then Hörmander’s theorem says that the operator

(1.1)

L=\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}^{2}+X_{0},

which under the above assumptions is called a
Hörmander operator
,
is
hypoelliptic
in
\Omega
, that is for every distribution

u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(\Omega\right)
and every open subset

\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, if
Lu\in C^{\infty}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)
then
u\in C^{\infty}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)
.

While many
local
results have been proved for general Hörmander
operators (
1.1
) (see e.g.
[
10
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
19
]
,

[
28
]
,
[
20
]
),
global
results have been established only in
special cases, in particular when
X_{0},X_{1},...,X_{m}
are left invariant
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with respect to a Lie group “translation”, and the operator
L
is
2
-homogeneous with
respect to a family of (diagonal, nonisotropic) dilations. This is the context
of Hörmander operators on homogeneous groups “stratified
of type II”, in the language of Rothschild-Stein
[
27
]
,
or, when
X_{0}
is lacking, the simpler case of sublaplacians on stratified
(Carnot) groups (precise definitions will be given in Section

2.2
). In this situation, Folland
[
14
]
has shown the
existence and good properties of a global homogeneous fundamental solution,
and has shown regularity results in the scale of Sobolev spaces adapted to the
vector fields. See also
[
11
, Chap. 8]
for a complete proof of

global
regularity results in the scale of both Sobolev and Hölder spaces.

In the more general situation when
X_{0},X_{1},...,X_{m}
are a set of
Hörmander vector fields in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, homogeneous w.r.t. a family
of dilations (
X_{0}
is
2
-homogeneous while
X_{1},...,X_{m}
are

1
-homogeneous), but not left invariant w.r.t. any Lie group operation, a
global fundamental solution with good properties has been constructed for
operator (
1.1
) by Biagi-Bonfiglioli in
[
3
]
and studied in

[
5
]
, while global estimates in Sobolev spaces have been proved
in
[
4
]
.

Higher order operators on homogeneous groups

For differential operators of order greater than
2
, with real
variable coefficients, a simple powerful sufficient condition for
hypoellipticity, like Hörmander’s condition, does not exist, in general.
A more expressive picture can be drawn in the special context of

homogeneous groups
. A theorem by Helffer-Nourrigat
[
18
]
,
culminating a research started in the context of Heisenberg groups with the
work of Rockland
[
26
]
, and extended to the context of general homogeneous
groups by Beals
[
1
]
, states that if a differential operator
L
is

left invariant
and
homogeneous
of some positive degree on a
homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
(precise definitions will be recalled later,
see Section
2.2
), then
L
is hypoelliptic if and only if
it satisfies the
Rockland condition
(and in this case we say that
L

is a
Rockland operator
). The exact formulation of the Rockland
condition will not be recalled here (see
[
13
, Par. 4.1.1]
). Instead,
throughout this paper we will use this notion according to the following
definition, which is logically equivalent to the standard one in view of
Helffer-Nourrigat’s theorem but avoids the language of representation theory,
which is involved in the statement of Rockland’s condition:

Definition 1.1

(Rockland operator)
.

Given a homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
,
a
Rockland operator
on
\mathbb{G}
is a left invariant
hypoelliptic differential operator
L
, homogeneous of some positive degree.

An explicit class of higher order Rockland operators can be described as
follows. Let
X_{1},...,X_{m}
be a family of left invariant Hörmander
vector fields on a homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
such that each
X_{i}
is
homogeneous of some positive integer degree
\nu_{i}
, and let
\nu_{0}
be
any common integer multiple of
\nu_{1},...,\nu_{m}
. Then the left invariant,

2\nu_{0}
homogeneous operator

(1.2)

L=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}}X_{j}^{\frac{2\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}}

satisfies the Rockland condition (see
[
13
, Lem. 4.1.8]
), in particular
it is hypoelliptic. Therefore
L
is a Rockland operator. Note that the
operator
L
is a differential operator of even order, generally greater than

2
(as soon as some integer quotient
\nu_{0}/\nu_{j}
is greater than
1
).

We can say that Rockland operators of the form (
1.2
) generalize to
general homogeneous groups the family of sublaplacians on Carnot groups. Note,
instead, that Hörmander operators (
1.1
) are not a special case of
(
1.2
), unless the
drift
X_{0}
is lacking. Nevertheless, if
(
1.1
) is
2
-homogeneous and left invariant on a homogeneous group, then
it is a Rockland operator. Note that any composition of two Rockland operators
is still a Rockland operator (because it is still left invariant, homogeneous,
and hypoelliptic). For instance, any
power
of a left invariant
homogeneous operator of type (
1.1
) or (
1.2
) is a Rockland
operator. Here we have tacitly exploited a simple fact which is worthwhile to
be pointed out explicitly:

Remark 1.2
.

The composition of two operators
L,M
,
which are hypoelliptic in
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is still
hypoelliptic. Namely, if
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(\Omega\right)
and
for some open
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
we know that
L\left(Mu\right)\in C^{\infty}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right),
then
Mu\in C^{\infty}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)
because
L
is hypoelliptic, and
then
u\in C^{\infty}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)
because
M
is hypoelliptic.

Our setting and main results

In this paper we are interested in generalizing the previous picture
to higher order operators structured on Hörmander vector fields in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
which are homogeneous w.r.t. a family of dilations,
but
not left invariant
w.r.t. any group structure. Our setting is the following.

Assumptions (H1)
We assume that:

(a)
X_{1},...,X_{m}
is a family of linearly independent, real smooth vector
fields in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, satisfying Hörmander’s condition at the origin.

(b)
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is endowed with a family of
dilations

(1.3)

\delta_{\lambda}\left(x\right)=\left(\lambda^{\sigma_{1}}x_{1},\lambda^{\sigma_{2}}x_{n},...,\lambda^{\sigma_{n}}x_{n}\right),

for positive integers

(1.4)

1=\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma_{2}\leq...\leq\sigma_{n},

and each
X_{i}
is
\delta_{\lambda}
-
homogeneous of positive integer
degree

\nu_{i}
, that is

(1.5)

X_{i}(u\circ\delta_{\lambda})=\lambda^{\nu_{i}}(X_{i}u)\circ\delta_{\lambda}

for every
\lambda>0
and
u\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)
.
Moreover

(1.6)

1\leq\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}\leq...\leq\nu_{m}.

The number

(1.7)

q=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}

will be called
homogeneous dimension
.

Remark 1.3
.

By the homogeneity assumptions on
X_{1},...,X_{m}
,
Hörmander’s condition actually holds at every point of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(see
[
5
, Rem. 3.2.]
).

Example 1.4
.

The following examples exhibit families of vector fields
satisfying assumptions (H1). We stress the fact that in all these examples
there cannot exist any Lie group structure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
making these
vector fields left invariant. This happens because one of the vector fields
vanishes at the origin without being identically zero.

(1).  In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{1}^{k}\,\partial_{x_{2}}

with
k=1,2,3...
and let

\delta_{\lambda}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=(\lambda x_{1},\lambda^{k+h}x_{2})

with
h=1,2,3...
Then
X_{1},X_{2}
satisfy Hörmander’s condition at
0

and are
\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous with

\displaystyle\nu_{1}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=h.

(2).  In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}+x_{2}\partial_{x_{3}}+\ldots+x_{n-1}\partial_{x_{n}}

and let

\delta_{\lambda}(x)=(\lambda x_{1},\lambda^{2}x_{2},\cdots,\lambda^{n}x_{n})

Then
X_{1},X_{2}
satisfy Hörmander’s condition and are
\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous with

\displaystyle\nu_{1}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=1.

(3).   In
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, let

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}+x_{2}^{2}\partial_{x_{3}}

and

\delta_{\lambda}(x)=(\lambda x_{1},\lambda^{1+k}x_{2},\lambda^{2+3k}x_{3})

with
k=1,2,3...
Then
X_{1},X_{2}
satisfy Hörmander’s condition and are

\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous with

\displaystyle\nu_{1}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=k.

Let us introduce some notation which will be used throughout the paper. For
any multi-index

I=\left(i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{k}\right)

with
i_{j}\in\left\{1,2,...,m\right\}
, we set

X_{I}=X_{i_{1}}X_{i_{2}}...X_{i_{k}}.

We also define the
length
of the multi-index,

\ell\left(I\right)=k,

and the
weight
of the multi-index,

(1.8)

\left|I\right|=\sum_{h=1}^{k}\nu_{i_{h}},

where
\nu_{i}
is the homogeneity degree of
X_{i}
, see
(
1.6
).

We are going to study a class of
\nu
-homogeneous operators of the kind

(1.9)

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{|I|=\nu}c_{I}X_{I}

where
\nu
is some positive integer and
c_{I}
are real constants, according
to the following definition:

Definition 1.5

(Generalized Rockland operators, first formulation)
.

We say that an operator
\mathcal{L}
of kind (
1.9
) is a

generalized Rockland operator
if the
X_{i}
satisfy assumptions (H1),
and the constants
\left\{c_{I}\right\}_{\left|I\right|=\nu}

give to
\mathcal{L}
a structure such that, if the
X_{i}
were
also

left invariant w.r.t. a Lie group structure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the
dilations
\delta_{\lambda}
are group automorphisms, then
\mathcal{L}
would
be hypoelliptic.

Later (see Section
3.1
), we will give a more formal, and
actually more general, formulation of the hypoellipticity condition contained
in Definition
1.5
, involving a suitable notion of

lifting
of vector fields. For the moment, the above formulation is
enough to understand the following examples and the meaning of our main results.

Example 1.6
.

Classes of examples of generalized Rockland operators are the following.

(1). Let
X_{1},...,X_{m}
be a family of vector fields satisfying assumptions
(H1) (for instance, as in Example
1.4
) and (with the same notation of
Assumption (H1)) for any positive integer
\nu_{0}
, common multiple of

\nu_{1},\nu_{2},...,\nu_{m}
, and any positive integer

(1.10)

\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{0}}^{k}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(-1\right)^{\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}}X_{j}^{\frac{2\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}}\right)^{k}.

If the

were also left invariant on a homogeneous Lie group, this

). Therefore the conditions in

(1’) Special cases of the above operators (

) are powers
of
2
-homogeneous sublaplacians

(1.11)

\Delta^{k}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}^{2}\right)^{k}

or also, assuming all the vector fields
X_{1},...,X_{m}

1
-homogeneous,
operators of the kind

(1.12)

\mathcal{L}_{\nu_{0}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}^{2\nu_{0}}

for some
\nu_{0}=2,3,4...

(2) Operators of the kind

(1.13)

\mathcal{L}^{k}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}^{2}+X_{0}\right)^{k}

when

2
-homogeneous and

Remark 1.7
.

Note that while the hypoellitpicity of operators (
1.11
)
and (
1.13
) is obvious because they are powers of
hypoelliptic operators (by Hörmander’s theorem), the hypoellipticity of
operators (
1.10
) or, in particular, (
1.12
) is
not obvious when the

are not left invariant, but will be actually

).

We will prove the following facts.

Theorem 1.8

(Hypoellipticity of
\mathcal{L}
)
.

A
generalized Rockland operator
\mathcal{L}
is hypoelliptic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.9

(Liouville-type theorem for
\mathcal{L}
)
.

Let

\mathcal{L}
be a generalized Rockland operator, and let
\Lambda\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a tempered distribution
satisfying
\mathcal{L}\Lambda=0
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then, there exists a
polynomial function

p=p(x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(1.14)

\text{$\Lambda=p$ in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$}.

In particular, any bounded solution of
\mathcal{L}\Lambda=0
in

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a constant.

Remark 1.10
.

A general result by Rothschild
[
25
]
shows that a
homogeneous left invariant operator on a homogeneous group is hypoelliptic if
and only if it satisfies a Liouville property. Later, Luo
[
23
]
has proved
that Liouville theorem still holds for homogeneous hypoelliptic operators
(without requiring left invariance). Therefore the above Theorem

1.9
is actually a Corollary of Theorem

1.8
, in view of
[
23
]
. We think that the short
independent proof of Theorem
1.9
that we will present in
section
3.3
can have an independent interest.

Before stating our main results about the fundamental solution of generalized
Rockland operators, we need to recall the following:

Definition 1.11
.

Given a differential operator
L
on
\Omega

(with real smooth coefficients), we will denote by
L^{\ast}
its formal

transposed
, defined by

(1.15)

\int_{\Omega}\left(Lu\right)\cdot v=\int u\cdot\left(L^{\ast}v\right)\text{ for every }u,v\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right).

Remark 1.12
.

It is well known (and will be justified
later, see Remark
2.1
) that under Assumptions (H1), one
has

X_{i}^{\ast}=-X_{i}.

Therefore, for instance, for operators of kind (
1.10
), we
simply have

L^{\ast}=L,

while for operators (
1.13
) one has

\left(\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}^{2}+X_{0}\right)^{k}\right)^{\ast}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}^{2}-X_{0}\right)^{k},

where

\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}^{2}-X_{0}

is still a Hörmander operator.

We will prove the following:

Theorem 1.13

(Fundamental solution for
\mathcal{L}
)
.

Let

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{|I|=\nu}c_{I}X_{I}

be a generalized Rockland operator, and assume that

\nu<q,

where
\nu
is the homogeneity degree of
\mathcal{L}
and
q
is the
homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, there exists a
global
fundamental solution

\Gamma(x,y)
of
\mathcal{L}
. More precisely, we have:

(a)

for every fixed
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\Gamma(\cdot,y)\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,\Gamma(x,\cdot)\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
; also,
\Gamma\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
(in the joint variables);

(b)

for every test function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma(x,y)\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\varphi(x)\,dx=-\varphi(y)\text{ \ }\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n};

(c)

for every test function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the function

u\left(x\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma(x,y)\varphi(y)\,dy

is
L_{loc}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and satisfies
\mathcal{L}u=-\varphi
in the distributional sense (therefore
u
is a.e. equal to a
smooth function
u^{\ast}
satistying
\mathcal{L}u^{\ast}=-\varphi
pointwise).

Moreover,
\Gamma
satisfies the following properties:

(I)

setting
\mathbb{D}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:x=y\}
, we
have
\Gamma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus\mathbb{D})
;

(II
)

\Gamma
is jointly
\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of
degree
\nu-q
, that is,

\Gamma(\delta_{\lambda}(x),\delta_{\lambda}(y))=\lambda^{\nu-q}\,\Gamma(x,y)\quad\text{for all $(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus\mathbb{D}$ and
$\lambda>0$}.

If also
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
is a generalized Rockland operator, let
\Gamma

and
\Gamma^{\ast}
be the above global fundamental solutions of
\mathcal{L}

and
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
. Then, the following identity holds:

(1.16)

\Gamma^{\ast}(x,y)=\Gamma(y,x)\quad\text{for every $x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

In particular, if
\mathcal{L}

formally self-adjoint

(
that is

\mathcal{L}^{\ast}=\mathcal{L}
), then
\Gamma^{\ast}=\Gamma
,
i.e.
the function
\Gamma
is symmetric.

The following last result collects a number of sharp pointwise estimates on

\Gamma
and its derivatives.

Theorem 1.14

(Pointwise estimates)
.

Let the assumptions of

1.13
be in force, and let

(1)

(Global upper estimate in the non-critical case). If

(1.17)

r>\nu-n

such that, for every

(
with

)

(1.18)

\left|Z_{1}\cdots Z_{h}\Gamma(x;y)\right|\leq c\frac{d_{X}^{\nu-r}\left(x,y\right)}{\left|B_{X}\left(x,d_{X}\left(x,y\right)\right)\right|}

for any choice of

\left|\Gamma(x;y)\right|\leq c\frac{d_{X}^{\nu}\left(x,y\right)}{\left|B_{X}\left(x,d_{X}\left(x,y\right)\right)\right|}.

This bound allows to prove that for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the function

u\left(x\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma(x,y)\varphi(y)\,dy

is actually continuous, and then smooth (and not only a.e. equal to a smooth function).

The strategy we will use to prove our results consists in generalizing the
lifting technique which has been firstly devised by Biagi and Bonfiglioli in

[
3
]
for homogeneous sublaplacians, implementing it in the present
more general situation to build a homogeneous fundamental solution. We think
that this technique has an independent interest and can be fruitful also in
the future.

Our main results can be extended also to heat-type operators of the kind

\mathcal{L}\pm\partial_{t}
, provided
\mathcal{L}
is a generalized Rockland
operator satisfying a further positivity assumption. This will be performed in
Section
3.5
, and we refer to that section for the
exact statements.

Examples and comparison with the existing literature

As already said, higher order hypoelliptic differential operators
with variable coefficients have been mainly studied in the context of
homogeneous groups. Rockland in
[
26
]
proves a sufficient condition for
the hypoellipticity of a left invariant homogeneous operator on the Heisenberg
groups
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and applies his result to show, as an interesting
example, the hypoellipticity of the operator

L=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(X_{i}^{2m}+Y_{i}^{2m}\right)

for any positive integer
m
(where
X_{i},Y_{i}
are the standard generators
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
). Helffer-Nourrigat
[
18
]
prove, as an application of
their result that we have already discussed, the hypoellipticity of the
operators (
1.2
). Another class of special examples of higher order,
homogenous left invariant operators on homogeneous groups, which have been
studied in some width, is that of (positive integer)
powers of
sublaplacians on Carnot groups
. For these operators hypoellipticity is
obvious, while an interesting issue is the construction and study of a global
fundamental solution. In this context, Benson, Dooley and Ratcliff in

[
2
]
have computed the fundamental solution of the
k
-th power of the
sublaplacian on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Later, Kumar and Mishra
in
[
22
]
have computed the fundamental solution of the
k
-th power of the
sublaplacian on any step-2 nilpotent Lie groups.

As for homogeneous but not left invariant higher order operators, we can only
mention the paper by Grushin
[
17
]
, where it is proved the hypoellipticity
of a very special class of operators whose main prototype is the following:

(1.20)

\mathcal{L}=\Delta_{x_{1}}^{k}+\left|x_{1}\right|^{2h}\Delta_{x_{2}}^{k}

with
h,k
positive integers and
x=\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}
. These operators fit our assumptions when
h/k
is integer.

To the best of our knowldege, the present paper is the first study of a
general class of
higher order hypoelliptic operators
which are
structured on Hörmander vector fields but
not left invariant on any
homogeneous group
.

We can show, through examples, that our results in Theorems

1.13
and
1.14
are
consistent with the known results for left invariant homogeneous higher order
hypoelliptic operators on homogeneous groups
or
for second order
homogeneous hypoelliptic operators (not left invariant w.r.t. any Lie group
structure). On the other hand, our results cover many situations which have
not been studied so far.

The limitation
\nu<q
in Theorem
1.13
, for the

existence
of a global fundamental solution jointly homogeneous in

\left(x,y\right)
, is consistent with the results which which are known
for left invariant homogeneous operators on homogeneous groups. Actually,
Folland
[
14
]
has proved the existence of a homogeneous fundamental
solution under the assumption
\nu<q
, while Geller in
[
16
, Thm. 3]
has
proved that, when
\nu\geq q
, a global fundamental solution still exists, but
is not homogeneous; actually,
\Gamma
in that situation has the following
structure

\Gamma\left(x\right)=K\left(x\right)+p\left(x\right)\log\left\|x\right\|

where
K\left(x\right)
is a homogeneous distribution of degree
\nu-q
,

p\left(x\right)
is a polynomial of degree
\nu-q
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|
is a homogeneous norm.

Example 1.17
.

About the necessity of the condition
\nu<q
for the existence of a
homogeneous fundamental solution.

a) The Laplace operator in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where

\nu=q=n=2

and

\Gamma\left(x,y\right)=c\log\left|x-y\right|

is not homogeneous.

b) The
k
-th power of the Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\Delta^{k}
, where:

\nu=2k,n=q

and when
2k\geq n
(so that
\nu\geq q
) and
n
is even, the fundamental
solution is

\Gamma\left(x,y\right)=\Gamma_{0}\left(x-y\right)\text{ with }\Gamma_{0}\left(x\right)=c_{k,n}\left|x\right|\log\left|x\right|

(not homogeneous). For this explicit formula, see e.g.
[
15
, p. 48]
.
Note that these examples a) and b) are consistent with the aforementioned
Geller’s result.

c) The
k
-th power of the sublaplacian on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n},

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}^{k}
. For this operator Benson, Dooley and
Ratcliff in
[
2
]
, as already recalled, have computed the explicit form
of a homogeneous fundamental solution under the restriction
k\leq n
. Here:

q=2n+2;\nu=2k,

therefore

\nu<q\Leftrightarrow k\leq n\text{.}

The further limitation
r>n-\nu
appearing in Theorem

1.14
,
for the validity of pointwise upper
estimates and
\Gamma
and its derivatives
, is consistent with the
results proved by the Authors and Bonfiglioli in
[
5
]
for second
order homogeneous (and not left invariant) hypoelliptic operators. Let us
recall here an example studied in
[
5
, Exm. 6.7]
:

Example 1.18
.

We consider the vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

X_{1}=\partial_{x_{1}},\quad X_{2}=x_{1}\,\partial_{x_{2}},

which are are homogeneous of degree
1
with respect to the dilations

\delta_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2})=(\lambda x_{1},\lambda^{2}x_{2}),

and the second order operator

L=\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\,\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}.

Here

\nu=n=2,\text{ }q=3\text{, so }\nu=n<q\text{,}

hence (by the results in
[
3
]
,
[
5
, Exm. 6.7]
, or by
Theorem
1.13
) there exists a global fundamental
solution
\Gamma\left(x,y\right)
, jointly homogeneous of degree

\nu-q=-1
.

However, in this situation the global upper bound (
1.18
) only holds for
r\geq 1
, that is when we are actually estimating the

derivatives
of
\Gamma
(see point (1) in Theorem

1.14
). Instead, the function
\Gamma
itself only
satisfies local estimates of power / logarithmic type (see
(
1.19
) in point (2) in Theorem
1.14
). For this operator the explicit form of the fundamental solution is actually
known, and satisfies sharply the bound (
1.19
). We refer to

[
5
, Exm. 6.7]
for details.

Let us end with a couple of examples which fit the assumptions of the present
paper, and not those of the previous theories.

Example 1.19
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let us consider the system of
Hörmander vector fields

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}+x_{2}\partial_{x_{3}}+\ldots+x_{n-1}\partial_{x_{n}},

which are
1
-homogeneous w.r.t. the dilations

\delta_{\lambda}(x)=(\lambda x_{1},\lambda^{2}x_{2},\cdots,\lambda^{n}x_{n}),

and let us consider the fourth order operator

L=X_{1}^{4}+X_{2}^{4}.

Here

q=\frac{n\left(n+1\right)}{2},\nu=4

so for every dimension
n\geq 5
the conditions

\nu<n<q

hold. To fix ideas, pick
n=5
, so
q=15
. Then there exists a global
fundamental solutions jointly homogeneos of degree

\nu-q=-11.

Moreover, since
\nu-n=-1<0
, the global upper bound
(
1.18
) in the non-critical case holds for every

r\geq 0.

Example 1.20
.

We consider, for a fixed positive integer
k
, the system of Hörmander
vector fields in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

X_{1}=\partial_{x_{1}},\quad X_{2}=x_{1}^{k}\,\partial_{x_{2}},

which are homogeneous of degree
1
with respect to the dilations

\delta_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2})=(\lambda x_{1},\lambda^{k+1}x_{2}),

and the fourth order operator

L=X_{1}^{4}+X_{2}^{4}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{4}+x_{1}^{4k}\,\partial_{x_{2}}^{4}.

Here

n=2;\nu=4;q=k+2,

so

\nu<q\Leftrightarrow k>2\text{.}

For
k=1,2,
we cannot expect the existence of a global fundamental solution
jointly homogeneous, while, for every integer
k\geq 3
, by Theorem

1.13
there exists a global fundamental solution

\Gamma\left(x,y\right)
, jointly homogeneous of degree
\nu-q=2-k
.

Under this assumption, the global upper bound (
1.18
) in
the non-critical case holds for

r>\nu-n=2,

that is when we are actually estimating the
derivatives
of
\Gamma
of
order at least
3
(see point (1) in Theorem
1.14
).

The critical case, with local estimates of power / logarithmic type (see
(
1.19
) in point (2) in Theorem
1.14
) corresponds to
r=2
, that is upper bounds on second order derivatives.

The function
\Gamma
itself, and its first order derivatives, despite their
being jointly homogeneous, do not satisfy an easy upper bound in terms of

d_{X}\left(x,y\right)
.

2.
Preliminaries and known results

2.1.
Homogeneous Hörmander vector fields and their weighed
control distance

From now on we consider a family

X=\left\{X_{1},...,X_{m}\right\}

of real smooth vector fields in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, satisfying Assumptions

(H1)
in Section
1
, and we keep the notation introduced there.

We have already noted in section
1
that our assumptions

(H1)
imply that Hörmander condition actually holds in the whole

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In the following Remark we collect some other easy
consequences of
(H1)
.

Remark 2.1
.

The following assertions hold.

(1)

Since the vector fields
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
are smooth and

\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of
positive degree
\nu_{1}....,\nu_{m}
, it is easy to see that

(2.1)

X_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i,j}(x)\partial_{x_{i}}\qquad(0\leq j\leq m),

where
p_{i,j}
are
polynomial functions
,
\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree

\sigma_{i}-\nu_{j}\leq\sigma_{i}-1.

In particular, since
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
are increasingly ordered,
we deduce that

(2.2)

p_{i,j}
is independent of
x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}
.

This fact also implies that

(2.3)

X_{i}^{\ast}=-X_{i}\quad\text{for every $i=0,1,\ldots,m.$}

(2)

An analogous homogeneity reasoning shows that no vector field can be
homogeneous of a degree larger than
\sigma_{n}
(without being identically
zero). Since, on the other hand, the commutator of two
\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous vector fields of degrees
d_{1},d_{2}
is itself

\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree
d_{1}+d_{2}
, we see that the Lie
algebra
\operatorname*{Lie}(X)
generated by
X
is
nilpotent of step

r\leq\sigma_{n}
.

(3)

Since
\mathrm{Lie}(X)
is finitely generated and nilpotent, we deduce
that

N=\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{Lie}(X))<+\infty.

On the other hand, by assumption
(H1)
we also have
N\geq n
.

Next, we specialize to our context the notion of
weighted control
distance
induced by a family of vector fields, firstly studied in some
generality by Nagel-Stein-Weinger in
[
24
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given our system
X=\left\{X_{1},...,X_{m}\right\}

of vector fields, for every fixed
\delta>0
and every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we denote by
C_{x,y}(\delta)
the set of the
absolutely continuous
curves

\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

satisfying the following properties:

i)

\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
;

ii)

for a.e.
t\in[0,1]
one has

\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}(t)X_{i}(\gamma(t)),\text{ with
}\left|a_{i}(t)\right|\leq\delta^{\nu_{i}}

for every
1\leq i\leq
m.

Then, we define the
weighted
X
-control distance
as follows:

(2.4)

d_{X}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\delta>0:\,\exists\,\,\gamma\in C_{x,y}(\delta)\right\},

where the nonemptiness of
C_{x,y}(\delta)
follows from the connectivity
property of a system of Hörmander vector fields (Chow’s theorem, for a
proof see e.g.
[
11
, Sec. 1.6]
).

It can be proved that
d_{X}
is actually a distance in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. (For
the standard cases (a):
\nu_{i}=1
for every
i
, and (b):
\nu_{1}=2
and

\nu_{i}=1
for
i=2,3,...,m
a proof can be found in
[
11
, Sec. 1.4,
1.5]
. The general case is similar). Moreover, this
d_{X}
is
topologically, but not metrically, equivalent to the Euclidean distance.

For every fixed
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
r>0
, we denote by

B_{X}(a,r)
the
d_{X}
-ball with centre
a
and radius
r
:

B_{X}(a,r)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,d_{X}(x,a)<r\}.

The next proposition collects some important and nontrivial properties of
these metric balls, which improve in our situation a deep result proved for a
general system of Hormander vector fields, but in local form, by
Nagel-Stein-Weinger
[
24
]
.

Proposition 1

(Geometry of
d_{X}
-balls)
.

(See
[
5
, Thm. B]
). Let
X
and
d_{X}
be as above. Then there exist constants

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
such that

(2.5)

\gamma_{1}\sum_{k=n}^{q}f_{k}(x)r^{k}\leq\left|B_{X}\left(x,r\right)\right|\leq\gamma_{2}\sum_{k=n}^{q}f_{k}(x)r^{k}.

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
.

Here,
q>0
is as in (
1.7
). Furthermore, for every
k=n,\ldots,q
,

f_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a function which is continuous,
non-negative and
\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree
q-k
. In
particular,

f_{q}(\cdot)

is a positive
constant
.

The previous Proposition in particular implies the validity of a
global
doubling condition
: there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(2.6)

\left|B_{X}\left(x,2r\right)\right|\leq c\left|B_{X}\left(x,r\right)\right|

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
. In turn, property (
2.6
),
by a standard computation which holds in doubling spaces, in particular
implies the following:

Proposition 2
.

For every
\alpha>0
there exists a constant

c>0
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
one has:

(2.7)

\int_{d_{X}\left(x,y\right)<r}\frac{d_{X}\left(x,y\right)^{\alpha}}{\left|B_{X}\left(x,d_{X}\left(x,y\right)\right)\right|}dy\leq cr^{\alpha}\text{.}

2.2.
Homogeneous groups and left invariant
operators

We start recalling the

Definition 2.3

(Homogeneous group)
.

Assume we have, in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a Lie group structure
\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\star\right)
and a family of (diagonal, nonisotropic) dilations
\left\{D_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda>0}
,

(2.8)

D_{\lambda}\left(x\right)=\left(\lambda^{\sigma_{1}}x_{1},\lambda^{\sigma_{2}}x_{n},...,\lambda^{\sigma_{n}}x_{n}\right),

for some exponents
\sigma_{i}
with

0<\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma_{2}\leq...\leq\sigma_{n},

which are group automorphisms; we then say that

\mathbb{G}=\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\star,\left\{D_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda>0}\right)

is a
homogeneous group
, of
homogeneous dimension

Q=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}.

Up to normalization of the exponents, it is not restrictive to assume

\sigma_{1}=1
; moreover, under the assumptions that we will make later,

\sigma_{i}
will be positive
integers
.

For every
x\in\mathbb{G}
, let
\ \tau_{x}
denote the left-translation by

x
, on
\mathbb{G}
, that is

\displaystyle\tau_{x}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\tau_{x}(y)

\displaystyle:=x\star y.

Then, we say that a smooth differential operator
Z
on
\mathbb{G}
is

left-invariant
if

(2.9)

Z\left(u\circ\tau_{x}\right)=(Zu)\circ\tau_{x},

for every
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
notion of homogeneity of some degree
\nu
(w.r.t. the dilations
D\left(\lambda\right)
) for a differential operator on
\mathbb{G}
is defined
analogously to (
1.5
).

Here we collect a few notions and known properties about homogeneous groups.
For more details and proofs, see for instance
[
11
, Chap. 3]
.

On any homogeneous group, it is not restricitive to assume that the neutral
element of
\mathbb{G}
is
0
. The
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure is a

bi - invariant Haar measure
on
\mathbb{G}
(see

[
11
, Thm. 3.8]
).

Definition 2.4
.

A
homogeneous norm
on
\mathbb{G}
is a
continuous function

\left\|\cdot\right\|:\mathbb{G\rightarrow[}0,+\infty)

such that:

(1)

\left\|x\right\|=0\,\Leftrightarrow\,x=0
;

(2)

\left\|D_{\lambda}\left(x\right)\right\|=\lambda\left\|x\right\|
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\lambda>0
.

It can be proved that every homogeneous norm also satisfies

(2.10)

\left\|x^{-1}\right\|\leq c\left\|x\right\|

\left\|x\star y\right\|\leq c\left(\left\|x\right\|+\left\|y\right\|\right)

for some absolute constant
c>0
and every
x,y\in\mathbb{G}
.

On every homogeneous group there exist infinitely many different homogeneous
norms, all of them being equivalent. For instance, an explicit example of
homogeneous norm is the following:

(2.11)

\left\|x\right\|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|x_{i}\right|^{1/\sigma_{i}}.

If
\left\|\cdot\right\|
is a homogeneous norm such that
\left\|x\right\|=\left\|x^{-1}\right\|
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(where
x^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
x
with respect to
\star
), we say
that
\left\|\cdot\right\|
is
symmetric
. Any symmetric
homogeneous norm induces a
quasidistance

(2.12)

d\left(x,y\right)=\left\|x^{-1}\star y\right\|,

satisfying

(1)

d(x,y)\geq 0
for every
x,y,\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

d(x,y)=0\,\Leftrightarrow\,x=y
;

(2)

d(x,y)=d(y,x)
for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(3)

there exists a constant
c\geq 1
such that

d(x,z)\leq c\left(d(x,y)+d(y,z)\right)\ \text{for every $x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.$}

Remark 2.5

(Homogeneous norms with or without translations)
.

The notion of homogeneous
norm, actually, can be introduced in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whenever a family of
dilations
\left\{\delta_{\lambda}\right\}
is defined, as in
(
1.3
), even when a group structure is lacking; the norm
(
2.11
) is still a good example also in this more
general context. In that situation, clearly, the properties
(
2.10
) and the quasidistance (
2.12
) are no
longer meaningful. We will use homogeneous norms in this more general context
in Section
3.4
.

We denote by
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{G})
the set of the left-invariant
vector fields on
\mathbb{G}
and we call it the
Lie algebra of

\mathbb{G}
. This Lie algebra has finite dimension (as a real vector space),
and

\dim(\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{G}))=n.

Assume that
X_{1},...,X_{m}
is a set of
generators
of
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{G})
, that is a set of left invariant vector fields satisfying
Hörmander’s condition. Moreover, assume that each
X_{i}
is
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of positive integer degree
\nu_{i}
with
1\leq\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}\leq...\leq\nu_{m}
. In other words, we now assume that
X_{1},...,X_{m}
is a system of vector fields satisfying our assumption

(H1)
, and moreover they are left invariant.

In the special case
\nu_{i}=1
for
i=1,2,...,m
we say that
\mathbb{G}
is
a
stratified group
, or a
Carnot group
.

In the other special case
\nu_{1}=2,\nu_{i}=1
for
i=2,...,m
we say that

\mathbb{G}
is
stratified of type II.

In this paper we are mainly interested in the general situation when these
cases do not occur.

Notation 1
.

Note that in the following we will always use
D_{\lambda}
to denote
dilations in a homogeneous group, and
\delta_{\lambda}
to denote a family of
dilations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
when we do not assume the existence of a group
structure. Also, we will use
Q
to denote the homogeneous dimension of a
homogeneous group, and
q
to denote the homogeneous dimension related to a
family of dilations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
when a group structure is lacking. The
reason of this notation will be clear in section
3.1
.

In this context, the weighted control distance
d_{X}
(see Definition

2.2
) is metrically equivalent to the quasidistance
d

induced by any homogeneous norm in
\mathbb{G}
(see (
2.12
)).
Actually, in this case

\left\|x\right\|=d_{X}\left(x,0\right)

is a homogeneous norm. This fact and other properties of
d_{X}
on
homogeneous groups are proved for instance in
[
11
, Sec. 3.5]
(the
proofs are given in the case
\nu_{i}=1
for every
i
, but the same arguments
work in our situation).

Remark 2.6
.

We explicitly mention, for a future reference, the
following (local)
integrability property
of a general homogeneous
norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
on a given homogeneous group

\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\star,D_{\lambda})
(see, e.g.,
[
8
, Cor. 5.4.5]
).

Given any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\alpha}\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,\alpha>-Q

where
Q>0
is the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}
.

Starting with the generators
X_{1},...,X_{m}
of
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{G})
,
where we are assuming each
X_{i}

D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree

\nu_{i}
, let us consider a differential operator of the kind

(2.13)

L=\sum_{\left|I\right|=\nu}c_{I}X_{I}

where
c_{I}
are real constants and
\nu
is a fixed positive integer. By
construction,
L
is left invariant and
\nu
-homogeneous. If
L
is also
hypoelliptic, then
L
is a Rockland operator (see Definition

1.1
). In the following it will be important to discuss also the
hypoellipticity of the transpose
L^{\ast}
. Note that

L^{\ast}=\sum_{\left|I\right|=\nu}c_{I}\left(X_{I}\right)^{\ast}

and, if

X_{I}=X_{i_{1}}X_{i_{2}}...X_{i_{k}}

then

\left(X_{I}\right)^{\ast}=X_{i_{k}}^{\ast}...X_{i_{2}}^{\ast}X_{i_{1}}^{\ast}=\left(-1\right)^{k}X_{i_{k}}...X_{i_{2}}X_{i_{1}}.

For instance, as already noted in Remark
1.12
, for
Rockland operators of type (
1.10
) one simply has
L^{\ast}=L
, while for (powers of) Hörmander operators

L=\left(\sum_{j=2}^{m}X_{j}^{2}+X_{1}\right)^{k}

one has

L^{\ast}=\left(\sum_{j=2}^{m}X_{j}^{2}-X_{1}\right)^{k},

which is still a (power of) a Hörmander operator, in particular it is
still hypoelliptic.

A property which will be useful is the following (see
[
13
, Thm. 3.2.45]
).

Theorem 2.7

(Liouville-type theorem on homogeneous groups)
.

Let

L
be a homogeneous (of any degree) left-invariant differential operator on a
homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
. We assume that
L
and
L^{\ast}
are
hypoelliptic on
\mathbb{G}
. If the tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G})
satisfies
Lf=0
then
f
is a polynomial.

Next, for a hypoelliptic operator we are interested in results assuring the
existence of a global fundamental solution with good properties. The following
result holds:

Theorem 2.8

(Fundamental solution on homogeneous groups)
.

Let
L
be a
\nu
-homogeneous left-invariant differential operator on a
homogeneous group

\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\star,\{D_{\lambda}\}),

with
homogeneous dimension
Q
. We assume that both
L
and
L^{\ast}
are
hypoelliptic on
\mathbb{G}
, and that

0<\nu<Q.

Then
L
admits a
unique fundamental solution

\Gamma_{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{G})
(tempered distribution) satisfying

L\Gamma_{0}=-\delta_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{S}\text{${}^{\prime}(\mathbb{G})$},

where
\delta_{0}\mathrm{\ }
denotes the Dirac distribution centered at
0
.
Furthermore, the following assertions hold.

(1)

(Homogeneity).
\Gamma_{0}
is smooth on
\mathbb{G}\backslash\{0\}
and
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree
\nu-Q
.

(2)

(Global pointwise estimate). There exists
c>0
such that

(2.14)

\left|\Gamma_{0}(x)\right|\leq c\|x\|^{\nu-Q}\text{ \ \ for
every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,x\neq 0$}.

In particular, we have

(2.15)

\Gamma_{0}(x)\rightarrow 0\text{ \ \ as $\|x\|\rightarrow+\infty$}.

Moreover, let
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{p})\in\{1,\ldots,m\}^{p}

(
with

p\geq 1
)
be a given multi-index. Then, there exists
c>0
, only
depending on
I
, such that

(2.16)

\left|X_{I}\Gamma_{0}(x)\right|\leq c\|x\|^{\nu-|I|-Q}\text{
\ \ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,x\neq 0$}.

In particular, we also have

(2.17)

X_{I}\Gamma_{0}(x)\rightarrow 0\ \ \ \text{as $\|x\|\rightarrow+\infty$}.

(3)

(Local integrability of
\Gamma_{0}
). We have

\Gamma_{0}\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

More generally, for every multi-index
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{p})\in\{1,\ldots,m\}^{p}

(
with
p\geq 1
)
such that
|I|<\nu
, we
have

X_{I}\Gamma_{0}\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(4)

(
\Gamma_{0}
left-inverts
L
). Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be fixed,
and let

\Gamma(x,y)=\Gamma_{0}(y^{-1}\star x)\qquad(x\neq y).

Then, for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

(2.18)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma(x,y)L^{\ast}\varphi(x)\,dx=-\varphi(y).

(5)

(
\Gamma_{0}
right-inverts
L
). Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be fixed, and let

(2.19)

\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma(x,y)\varphi(y)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma_{0}(y^{-1}\star x)\varphi(y)\,dy.

Then, the following assertions hold.

\displaystyle\mathrm{(i)}\,\,

\displaystyle\text{$\Lambda_{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$,
and $\Lambda_{\varphi}(x)\rightarrow 0$ as $\|x\|\rightarrow+\infty$};

\displaystyle\mathrm{(ii)}\,\,

L(\Lambda_{\varphi})=-\varphi
pointwise in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(6)

(Relation between
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{0}^{\ast}
). Let

\Gamma_{0}^{\ast}
be the global fundamental solution of the formal
transposed
L^{\ast}
of
L
, see Definition
1.11
.
Then, we have

(2.20)

\Gamma_{0}^{\ast}(x)=\Gamma_{0}(x^{-1})\quad\text{for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}$}

In particular, assertions
(1)
-
(5)
hold also for
\Gamma_{0}^{\ast}
.

Here,
\|\cdot\|
is
any
fixed homogeneous norm on
\mathbb{G}
.

This result is due to Folland,
[
14
]
. More precisely: existence and
uniqueness of
\Gamma_{0}
and point (1) are contained in
[
14
, Thm. 2.1.]
; point (6) is the Remark after
[
14
, Thm. 2.1.]
. Point (2) is an
easy consequence of point (1). Point (3) follows from point (2) in view of the
integrability properties discussed in Remark
2.6
. Points (4)
and (5) are substantially contained in
[
14
, Cor. 2.8]
, where it is
proved that
\Gamma_{0}
right-inverts and left-inverts
L
in the
distributional sense. Then point (ii) follows from (i) and the fact that

\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{\varphi})=-\varphi
in the sense of distributions.

Remark 2.9
.

We stress the fact that the above theorem does not depend on the explicit form
of the operator
L
. In particular, we can apply it to Rockland operators of
type (
1.10
) as well to powers of Hörmander operators
(
1.13
), whenever the required relation between the
homogeneity degree of
L
and the homogeneous dimension
Q
is satisfied.

3.
Properties of generalized Rockland operators

3.1.
Lifting to homogeneous groups

Taking into account the results recalled in the previous section for
homogeneous and
left-invariant
operators on a homogeneous Lie group

\mathbb{G}
, we can now begin the study of generalized Rockland operators.

To begin with, we recall that, if
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
satisfies Assumptions

(H1)
, we know from Remarks
1.3
and

2.1
that

(1)

the
X_{i}
’s satisfy Hörmander’s condition at every point of

\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(2)

the Lie algebra
\mathrm{Lie}(X)
generated by
X=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
has
finite dimension
, say
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and we have

N\geq n
.

Furthermore, as noted in
[
5
, Rem. 1.2]
, the case

N=n

occurs
if and only if
there exists a homogeneous Lie group

\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\star,\delta_{\lambda})
(in the sense of
Definition
2.3
, with
\{\delta_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
as in (
2.8
)) such that

\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{G})=\mathrm{Lie}(X),

that is, if and only if the
X_{i}
’s are left-invariant on some homogeneous
Lie group on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. However, in that situation the known results
recalled in Section
2.1
apply. Therefore, from
now on we tacitly understand that

(3.1)

\begin{split}(i)\,\,&\text{$X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}$ satisfy assumption {(H1)};}\\[2.84544pt]
(ii)\,\,&p=N-n=\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{Lie}(X))-n\geq 1;\end{split}

Moreover, according to
(ii)
, we denote the points
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
by

z=(x,\xi),\quad\text{with $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}$}.

Thanks to assumption (
3.1
)-
(ii)
(which, as discussed above, is

non-restrictive
), we can then state the following (crucial)

lifting property
of the
X_{i}
’s.

Theorem 3.1

(Lifting property, see
[
3
, Thm. 3.2]
)
.

Given
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
the system
X=\{X_{1},...,X_{m}\}
satisfying
Assumptions (H1), there exists a
homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\star,D_{\lambda})
, of homogeneous dimension
Q>q

(
where
q
is as in assumption
(H1)
)
, and there
exists a system

\widetilde{X}=\{\widetilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{m}\}\subseteq\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{G})

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\mathrm{Lie}(\widetilde{X})=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{G})
;

(2)

\widetilde{X}_{i}
is
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree
\nu_{i}
;

(3)

for every
1\leq j\leq m
, we have

(3.2)

\widetilde{X}_{j}=X_{j}+R_{j}(x,\xi),

where
R_{j}(x,\xi)
is a
non-vanishing
smooth vector field operating
only in the
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
variable, with coefficients possibly
depending on
(x,\xi)
.

Finally, the family of dilations
\{D_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
takes the
following
lifted
form

(3.3)

D_{\lambda}(x,\xi)=(\delta_{\lambda}(x),E_{\lambda}(\xi)),

where
\delta_{\lambda}
is as in assumption
(H1)
, and
E_{\lambda}(\xi)=(\lambda^{\tau_{1}}\xi_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\tau_{p}}\xi_{p})
for
suitable non-negative integers
1\leq\tau_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\tau_{p}
. Hence,
we have

(3.4)

\textstyle Q=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\tau_{j}\equiv q+\mathcal{E}.

Remark 3.2
.

As a matter of fact, Theorem
3.1
is
established in
[
3
]
in the particular case in which all the
X_{i}
’s are
\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree
1

(
namely, when
\nu_{1}=\ldots=\nu_{m}=1
)
; however, it is not
difficult to see that the proof given in
[
3
]
can be easily extended
to the general case considered here.

Moreover, from the
explicit
construction of
\mathbb{G}
, one can
derive the following useful facts (see
[
3
, Rem. 8]
and

[
5
, Lem. 4.5]
).

(1)

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be arbitrarily fixed, and let

\Psi_{x,y}:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p},\qquad\Psi_{x,y}(\xi)=\pi_{p}\big((y,0)^{-1}\star(x,\xi)\big),

where
\pi_{p}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}
denotes the projection
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Then, this map
\Psi_{x,y}
is a

smooth diffeomorphism
of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
onto itself, smoothly
depending on the fixed
x,y
, whose Jacobian determinant is
\pm 1
.

In other words, the
change of variable

\xi=\Psi_{x,y}^{-1}(\zeta)

satisfies

\pi_{p}\big((y,0)^{-1}\star(x,\xi)\big)=\zeta\quad\text{and}\quad d\xi=d\zeta.

(2)

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be arbitrarily fixed, and let

\Phi_{x,y}:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p},\qquad\Phi_{x,y}(\xi)=\pi_{p}\big((y,0)\star(y,\xi)^{-1}\star(x,0)\big).

Then, this map
\Phi_{x,y}
is a
smooth diffeomorphism
of

\mathbb{R}^{p}
onto itself, smoothly depending on the fixed
x,y
, whose
Jacobian determinant is
\pm 1
. Moreover, the following identity holds

(y,0)^{-1}\star(x,\Psi_{x,y}(\zeta))=(y,\zeta)^{-1}\star(x,0).

In other words, the
change of variable

\xi=\Phi_{x,y}(\zeta)

satisfies

(3.5)

(y,0)^{-1}\star(x,\xi)=(y,\zeta)^{-1}\star(x,0)\quad\text{and}\quad d\xi=d\zeta.

We will repeatedly exploit these facts in the sequel.

The
global lifting property
in Theorem
3.1
is the

key ingredient
in our investigation on general higher-order
homogeneous operators of the form

(3.6)

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{|I|=\nu}c_{I}X_{I}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\\
\nu_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\nu_{i_{k}}=\nu\end{subarray}}c_{I}X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{k}}

(where
c_{I}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\nu>0
is a fixed positive integer).

Indeed, following the notation of Theorem
3.1
(used here and in
the sequel), we observe that the operator, defined on the group
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(3.7)

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\sum_{|I|=\nu}c_{I}\widetilde{X}_{I}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\\
\nu_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\nu_{i_{k}}=\nu\end{subarray}}c_{I}\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{k}}

is
homogeneous of degree
\nu
(with respect to the family of dilations

\{D_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
) and also
left-invariant
(with respect to

\star
); thus, if we assume that

(3.8)

\text{both $\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}$ and $(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}})^{\ast}$
are hypoelliptic},

we may apply to
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
all the results recalled in the
previous section, thereby obtaining several
global properties

(existence of a well-behaved global fundamental solution, validity of
Liouville-type theorems, etc.).

On the other hand, owing to (
3.2
), we see that the operator

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}

lifts

\mathcal{L}
in the following sense:
denoting by
\pi_{n}
the projection of
\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for every smooth function
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

(3.9)

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}(u\circ\pi_{n})=(\mathcal{L}u)\circ\pi_{n};

on account of (
3.9
), it is therefore natural to attempt to
derive analogous
global properties
for
\mathcal{L}
by means of a

saturation argument
. This type of argument has already proved effective
in
[
3
]
, where the existence of a well-behaved global fundamental
solution for the sum of squares
\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}^{2}
has been established.

We can now also rephrase Definition
1.5
as follows:

Definition 3.3

(Generalized Rockland operators, second formulation)
.

We say that an operator
\mathcal{L}
of kind
(
3.6
) is a generalized Rockland operator if

X_{1},...,X_{m}
satisfy (
3.1
) and the lifted operator

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
in (
3.7
) is hypoelliptic on the
lifted space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Since the lifted vector fields
\widetilde{X}_{i}
are particular homogeneous
left invariant vector fields, if
\mathcal{L}
satisfies Definition

1.5
, in particular the lifted operator

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
will be hypoelliptic. Therefore the present
Definition
3.3
is actually more general than Definition

1.5
.

3.2.
Hypoellipticity of
\mathcal{L}

We begin by showing how Theorem
3.1
can be used to prove the

hypoellipticity of any operator
\mathcal{L}
of the form
(
3.6
), provided that the same property holds for
the
lifted operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
.

Proof of Thm.
1.8
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set, and let
\Lambda\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
be a distribution on
\Omega
. We assume that there exists
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\mathcal{L}\Lambda=f\quad\text{in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)$},

that is,

\left\langle\mathcal{L}\Lambda,\varphi\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,dx\quad\forall\,\,\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

We then define
\widetilde{\Lambda}=\Lambda\otimes 1\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p})
(where
1
denotes the distribution on

\mathbb{R}^{p}
associated with the locally-integrable function
u\equiv 1
,
and
\otimes
is the usual tensor product between distributions), and we
claim that

(3.10)

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{\Lambda}=f\quad\text{in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p})$}

To prove this claim, we exploit an
approximation argument
. Let

\{\theta_{k}\}_{k}\subseteq C^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a sequence of
smooth
functions
in
\Omega
such that

\text{$\theta_{k}\rightarrow\Lambda$ in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)$ (as
$k\rightarrow+\infty$)}.

Then, recalling that
\widetilde{\Lambda}=\Lambda\otimes 1
is the distribution
on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
defined by

(3.11)

\begin{split}\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\phi\rangle&=\left\langle\Lambda,x\mapsto\langle 1,\xi\mapsto\phi(x,\xi)\rangle\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle\Lambda,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\phi(\cdot,\xi)\,d\xi\right\rangle\quad\forall\,\,\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p}),\end{split}

and since the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}

is a lifting
of

\mathcal{L}
, see (
3.9
), for every
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p})
we have the following computation

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{\Lambda},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}\phi\rangle=\left\langle\Lambda,x\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}\phi)(x,\xi)\,d\xi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\theta_{k}(x)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}\phi)(x,\xi)\,d\xi\right)dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p}}\theta_{k}(x)(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}\phi)(x,\xi)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle(\text{integrating by parts, and using \eqref{eq:LhtildeliftsLh}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p}}(\mathcal{L}\theta_{k})(x)\phi(x,\xi)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{L}\theta_{k})(x)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\phi(x,\xi)\,d\xi\right)dx

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{L}\Lambda,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\phi(\cdot,\xi)\,d\xi\right\rangle.

From this, since
\mathcal{L}\Lambda=f
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
, we
obtain

\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{\Lambda},\phi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\phi(x,\xi)\,d\xi\right)dx=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p}}f(x)\phi(x,\xi)\,dx\,d\xi,

and this proves the claimed (
3.10
) (by the
arbitrariness of
\phi
).

Now we have established (
3.10
), we can easily
complete the demonstration of the theorem. Indeed, since
f
can be though of
as a smooth function on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and since we are
assuming that the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
is
C^{\infty}
-hypoelliptic on every open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists
U\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p})
such that

\text{$\widetilde{\Lambda}=U$ in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p})$}.

As a consequence,
given any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and
choosing (once and for all) a test function
\psi_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
satisfying
0\leq\psi_{0}\leq 1
on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\psi_{0}\,d\xi=1,

we obtain (see (
3.11
) with
\phi=\varphi\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p})
)

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Lambda,\varphi\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\psi_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi\right)\right\rangle=\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\varphi\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{p}}U(x,\xi)\varphi(x)\psi_{0}(\xi)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}U(x,\xi)\psi_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi\right)\varphi(x)\,dx,

that is,

\text{$\Lambda=u$ in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)$},\qquad\text{where
$u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}U(x,\xi)\psi_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi$}.

Since
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we conclude that
\mathcal{L}
is
C^{\infty}
-hypoelliptic in
\Omega
.
∎

3.3.
A Liouville-type theorem for
\mathcal{L}

We now turn to proving that Theorem
3.1
also allows us to derive
a
Liouville-type property
for
\mathcal{L}
, analogous to the one
stated in Theorem
2.7
for homogeneous operators that are

left-invariant
on some homogeneous group. As noted in Remark

1.10
, this result could also be deduced applying a general
result on homogeneous hypoelliptic operators proved in
[
23
]
. Howeever, we
think that the following short alternative proof of this result has an
independent interest.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

We proceed essentially as in the
proof of Theorem
1.8
. To begin with, we consider
the distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
given
by

\widetilde{\Lambda}=\Lambda\otimes 1

We then observe that, since both
\Lambda
and
1
are tempered distrubtions
(on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, respectively), then

\widetilde{\Lambda}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
; moreover, since

\text{$\mathcal{L}\Lambda=0$ in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$},

an argument
entirely analogous
to the one exploited in the proof of
Theorem
1.8
(the only difference being that here
the test functions belong to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
rather than to

C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) shows that

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{\Lambda}=0\quad\text{in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$}.

From this, since we are assuming (
3.8
), we may apply
Theorem
2.7
, ensuring the existence of a polynomial
function
P=P(x,\xi)
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

(3.12)

\text{$\widetilde{\Lambda}=P$ in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$}.

With (
3.12
) at hand, we can easily complete the
proof of the theorem. Indeed,
given any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and choosing once and for all
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
such that

\text{$0\leq\psi_{0}\leq 1$ on $\mathbb{R}^{p}$}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\psi_{0}\,d\xi=1,

we obtain, by
3.11
,

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Lambda,\varphi\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\psi_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi\right)\right\rangle=\langle\widetilde{\Lambda},\varphi\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}}P(x,\xi)\varphi(x)\psi_{0}(\xi)\,dx\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}P(x,\xi)\psi_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi\right)\varphi(x)\,dx,

that is,

\text{$\Lambda=p$ in $\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$},\qquad\text{where
$p(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}P(x,\xi)\psi_{0}(\xi)\,d\xi$}.

Since
p\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a polynomial function (as the same
is true of
P
), we obtain the desired (
1.14
), and
the proof is complete.
∎

3.4.
Existence of a global fundamental
solution

Finally, we turn to showing that a
lifting-plus-saturation argument

arising from Theorem
3.1
allows us to prove the existence of a
well-behaved global fundamental solution for
\mathcal{L}
.

Roughly put, our argument goes as follows. First of all, since
\widetilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{m}
are
left-invariant
(with respect to the
homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\star,D_{\lambda})
) and

homogeneous
(with respect to the family of dilations
\{D_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
on the higher-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
), the

lifted operator

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
defined in
(
3.7
), that is,

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\sum_{|I|=\nu}c_{I}\widetilde{X}_{I}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\\
\nu_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\nu_{i_{k}}=\nu\end{subarray}}c_{I}\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{k}}

is
\nu
-homogeneous and left-invariant on
\mathbb{G}
. Thus, if we
further assume that

(3.13)

\nu<q\,\,(<Q)

(recall that
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of the group
\mathbb{G}
), we
may apply Theorem
2.8
, which ensures that the
operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
possesses a
well-behaved global
fundamental solution
of the form

(3.14)

\widetilde{\Gamma}((x,\xi),(y,\eta))=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}((y,\eta)^{-1}\star(x,\xi)),

where
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})

satisfies the following properties:

(a)

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}
is
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree

\nu-Q
;

(b)

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}(x,\xi)\varphi(x,\xi)\,dx\,d\xi=-\varphi(0).

Recalling that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
is a lifting of
\mathcal{L}
, see
(
3.9
), it is then natural to expect that the “integral
saturation”

(3.15)

\Gamma(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\widetilde{\Gamma}((x,\xi),(y,0))\,d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}((y,0)^{-1}\star(x,\xi))\,d\xi

provides a
global fundamental solution for
\mathcal{L}
with pole at

y
(see
[
3
]
).

As a matter of fact, the above intuition can be made rigorous with the help of

[
3
, Thm. 2.1]
, which ensures that the function
\Gamma
defined in
(
3.15
) is
indeed
a global fundamental
solution of
\mathcal{L}
, provided that the ‘lifted’ operator

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
and its (global) fundamental solution

\widetilde{\Gamma}
satisfy the properties contained in the next two Propositions.

Proposition 3

(Saturable lifting)
.

The operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}

is a
saturable lifting
of
\mathcal{L}
in the sense of
[
3
, Def. 2]
, that is, the following properties hold.

(S1)

Setting
\mathcal{R}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}-\mathcal{L}
, every
summand of the
formal adjoint of
\mathcal{R}
operates at least once
in the
\xi
variables, that is, we have

(3.16)

\mathcal{R}^{\ast}=\sum_{\beta\neq 0}r_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta},

for suitable smooth functions
r_{\alpha,\beta}
(not all identically zero).

(S2)

There exists a sequence
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j}\subseteq C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
such that

i)

0\leq\theta_{j}\leq 1
on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
;

ii)

setting
\Omega_{j}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\,\theta_{j}(\xi)=1\}
,
we have

\Omega_{j}\Subset\Omega_{j+1}\quad\text{and}\quad\bigcup_{j=1}^{+\infty}\Omega_{j}=\mathbb{R}^{p};

iii)

for every compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for every
coefficient function
r_{\alpha,\beta}
of
\mathcal{R}^{\ast}
as in
(
3.16
) one can find a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}(K)>0

such that

(3.17)

\begin{gathered}\big|r_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\theta_{j}(\xi)\big|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}(K)\\[2.84544pt]
\text{for every $x\in K,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}$ and $j\in\mathbb{N}$}.\end{gathered}

Proposition 4

(Integrability properties of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
)
.

Let

\widetilde{\Gamma}
be the fundamental solution of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
defined in (
3.14
). Then, the following properties hold:

(P1)

for every fixed
x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\xi\mapsto\widetilde{\Gamma}((x,\xi),(y,0))=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}((y,0)^{-1}\star(x,\xi))\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{p});

(P2)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every compact set

K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(x,\xi)\mapsto\widetilde{\Gamma}((x,\xi),(y,0))=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}((y,0)^{-1}\star(x,\xi))\in L^{1}(K\times\mathbb{R}^{p}).

Before proceding, we need to introduce the following notation, which will be
used in the following.

Suppose we are given a family of non-isotropic dilations of the form

d_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k},\qquad d_{\lambda}(v)=(\lambda^{\varepsilon_{1}}v_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\varepsilon_{k}}v_{k}),

on some Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(with
k\geq 1
and
1\leq\varepsilon_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\varepsilon_{k}
). Then, for every multi-index

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})
(with
\alpha_{i}
nonnegative
integers), we set

\mathcal{H}(\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{H}_{d}(\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}\varepsilon_{j}.

Moreover, we denote by
\varrho_{d}
the
homogeneous norm
associated
with the family
\{d_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
, namely

(3.18)

\varrho_{d}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|v_{i}|^{1/\varepsilon_{i}}.

We will make use of these notations for the three families of dilations that
naturally arise from assumption
(H1)
and Theorem
3.1
,
namely:

i)  the family
\{\delta_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
, which is defined on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

ii)  the family
\{E_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
, which is defined on

\mathbb{R}^{p}
;

iii)  the family
\{D_{\lambda}=(\delta_{\lambda},E_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda}
, which is defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

The reader will note that we prefer to use the symbol
\varrho_{d}
, different
from
\left\|\cdot\right\|
which is used on homogeneous groups, to
avoid confusion.

Let us now turn to prove Propositions
3
and
4
.
To this end, we begin by establish the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Setting
\mathcal{R}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}-\mathcal{L}
, we have

(3.19)

\mathcal{R}^{\ast}=(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}})^{\ast}-\mathcal{L}^{\ast}=\sum_{\beta\neq 0}r_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)D_{x}^{\alpha}D_{\xi}^{\beta},

for some
polynomial functions

r_{\alpha,\beta}
,
not all
identically zero

(
in other words, the operator
\mathcal{R}^{\ast}

acts at least once in the
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
variables
)
.
Furthermore, for each fixed
\alpha,\beta
, the polynomial
r_{\alpha,\beta}

has the following explicit expression

(3.20)

r_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)=\sum_{\mathcal{H}_{E}(\gamma)\leq m_{\beta}}c_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(x)\,\xi^{\gamma},

where
c_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
are polynomial functions only depending on

x
, and

(3.21)

m_{\beta}=\mathcal{H}_{E}(\beta)-1.

Proof.

First of all, we observe that, by definition,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|I|=\nu}c_{I}\big(\widetilde{X}_{I}^{*}-X_{I}^{*}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\\
\nu_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\nu_{i_{k}}=\nu\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k}c_{I}\cdot\big(\widetilde{X}_{i_{k}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}-X_{i_{k}}\cdots X_{i_{1}}\big);

as a consequence, to prove the lemma it suffices to show that,
given any
multi-index
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in\{1,\ldots,m\}^{k}
(for some

k\geq 1
, and not necessarily satisfying the condition
|I|=\nu
), we
have

(3.22)

\begin{gathered}(\bigstar)\qquad\quad\widetilde{X}_{i_{k}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}-X_{i_{k}}\cdots X_{i_{1}}=\sum\nolimits_{\beta\neq 0}r_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)D^{\alpha}_{x}D^{\beta}_{\xi},\\
\begin{array}[]{c}\text{where each $r_{\alpha,\beta}$ is a polynomial function of the form \eqref{eq:ralfabetaLemmaTecnico}}\\
\text{(possibly depending on $I$), and not all of them vanish.}\end{array}\end{gathered}

Hence, we turn to proving (
3.22
) by

induction on
k
.

If
k=1
(that is,
I=(i)
for
1\leq i\leq m
), then by Theorem

3.1
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}_{i}-X_{i}=R_{i}(x,\xi),

where
R_{i}(x,\xi)
is a
non-vanishing
smooth vector field acting only
on the variables
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, with coefficients possibly depending
on
(x,\xi)
, and
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree
\nu_{i}
(as the same
is true of
X_{i}
and
\widetilde{X}_{i}
). Thus,

(3.23)

\widetilde{X}_{i}-X_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}r_{i,j}(x,\xi)\,\partial_{\xi_{j}}\equiv\sum_{\mathcal{H}(\beta)=1}r_{\beta}(x,\xi)D^{\beta}_{\xi},

where each
r_{i,j}=r_{\beta}
(with
\beta=e_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq p
) is a polynomial function,
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree

\tau_{j}-\nu_{i}=\mathcal{H}_{E}(e_{j})-\nu_{i}

(see Remark
2.1
). Hence,
(\bigstar)
holds in this case
(note that the polynomials
r_{i,j}
are not all identically zero, since

R_{i}
is
non-vanishing
).

As for the
explicit form
of
r_{i,j}=r_{\beta}
it suffices to
observe that, since
r_{i,j}
is (smooth and)
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of
degree
\mathcal{H}_{E}(e_{j})-\nu_{i}
, we have

(3.24)

\begin{split}r_{i,j}(x,\xi)&=\sum_{\mathcal{H}_{\delta}(\gamma_{1})+\mathcal{H}_{E}(\gamma_{2})=\tau_{j}-\nu_{i}}c_{i,j,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}x^{\gamma_{1}}\xi^{\gamma_{2}}\\
&=\sum_{\mathcal{H}_{E}(\gamma_{2})\leq\tau_{j}-\nu_{i}}\left(\sum_{\mathcal{H}_{\delta}(\gamma_{1})+\mathcal{H}_{E}(\gamma_{2})=\tau_{j}-\nu_{i}}c_{i,j,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}x^{\gamma_{1}}\right)\xi^{\gamma_{2}}\\[2.84544pt]
&(\text{since $\tau_{j}=\mathcal{H}_{E}(e_{j})$ and $\nu_{i}\geq 1$, so
that $\tau_{j}-\nu_{i}\leq m_{e_{j}}$})\\
&\equiv\sum_{\mathcal{H}_{E}(\gamma)\leq m_{e_{j}}}c_{i,j,\gamma}(x)\,\xi^{\gamma},\end{split}

and this fully demonstrates (
3.20
) in this
case.

We now assume that (
3.22
) holds for some

k\geq 1
(and for every multi-index of length
k
), and we prove that it
holds also
for every given multi-index

I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k},i_{k+1})

of length
k+1

J=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}),
by using the inductive hypothesis, jointly with Theorem

3.1
, we can write

\begin{split}&\widetilde{X}_{i_{k+1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}-X_{i_{k+1}}\cdots X_{i_{1}}\\
&\qquad=\widetilde{X}_{i_{k+1}}\big(\widetilde{X}_{i_{k}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\big)-X_{i_{k+1}}X_{i_{k}}\cdots X_{i_{1}}\\
&\qquad=(X_{i_{k+1}}+R_{i_{k+1}})\Big(X_{i_{k}}\cdots X_{i_{1}}+\sum\nolimits_{\beta\neq 0}r_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)D_{x}^{\alpha}D_{\xi}^{\beta}\Big)\\
&\qquad\qquad-X_{i_{k+1}}X_{i_{k}}\cdots X_{i_{1}}\\
&\qquad=X_{i_{k+1}}\Big(\sum\nolimits_{\beta\neq 0}r_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)D_{x}^{\alpha}D_{\xi}^{\beta}\Big)+R_{i_{k+1}}X_{i_{k}}\cdots X_{1}\\
&\qquad\qquad+R_{i_{k+1}}\Big(\sum\nolimits_{\beta\neq 0}r_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)D_{x}^{\alpha}D_{\xi}^{\beta}\Big)\end{split}

(where each
r_{\alpha,\beta}
is a polynomial function of the form
(
3.20
), possibly depending on
J
, and not all of
them vanish). As a consequence, if we define

\displaystyle\bullet)

\displaystyle\,\,\mathcal{A}_{1}=X_{i_{k+1}}\Big(\sum\nolimits_{\beta\neq 0}r_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)D_{x}^{\alpha}D_{\xi}^{\beta}\Big);

\displaystyle\bullet)

\displaystyle\,\,\mathcal{A}_{2}=R_{i_{k+1}}X_{1}\cdots X_{i_{k}};

\displaystyle\bullet)

\displaystyle\,\,\mathcal{A}_{3}=R_{i_{k+1}}\Big(\sum\nolimits_{\beta\neq 0}r_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\xi)D_{x}^{\alpha}D_{\xi}^{\beta}\Big);

we see that (
3.22
) holds (for this given

) if it holds
for the two operators

\mathcal{A}_{1}

-
Analysis of

Here we briefly discuss some extensions of our theory to heat-type operators
of the kind

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{L}\pm\partial_{t}

(acting on the

Prediction of bone or lung metastasis in prostate cancer using machine learning: a SEER-based study with external validation
Authors: Mingyan Zhong, Ru Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundProstate cancer (PCa) often metastasizes to bone or lung, leading to poor prognosis and higher mortality. Early identification of patients at high risk for metastasis is critical for improving treatment outcomes. However, current prediction models are limited in accuracy and generalizability. This study aimed to develop and validate machine learning models to predict bone and/or lung metastasis in PCa patients, using data from both the SEER database and an external cohort.MethodsData from 56,107 PCa patients in the SEER database (2018–2021) were analyzed, with feature selection performed using LASSO regression, identifying variables such as Gleason score, PSA levels, and N stage. Six machine learning models were constructed, including XGBoost, Random Forest, and Logistic Regression. The SMOTE technique was applied to address data imbalance, and the models were externally validated using a cohort of 201 patients from the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University.ResultsIn the SEER cohort, 2868 (5.1%) patients had bone and/or lung metastasis, while 53,239 (94.9%) showed no evidence of metastasis. The external validation cohort, consisting of 201 patients, had a significantly higher metastatic rate, with 51 (25.4%) patients presenting with bone and/or lung metastasis. XGBoost showed superior performance, achieving an AUC of 0.979 in the SEER training set and 0.924 in the external validation cohort. Key predictors were Gleason score, PSA, and N stage. The model correctly identified 2768 metastatic cases (true positives) and 52,712 non-metastatic cases (true negatives). Feature importance analysis confirmed Gleason score and PSA as the most significant predictors. In external validation, the model maintained high accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity, confirming its robustness and generalizability across different patient populations.ConclusionXGBoost proved to be a highly accurate tool for predicting bone and/or lung metastasis in PCa patients. Incorporating this model into clinical practice could improve early detection of high-risk patients, enabling more personalized and timely interventions, ultimately enhancing patient outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04030-9.

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is among the most prevalent malignancies affecting men globally and remains a leading contributor to cancer-related mortality. In 2023, an estimated 1.4 million new cases were diagnosed worldwide, with approximately 375,000 deaths attributable to the disease [1, 2]. The clinical course of prostate cancer is highly variable; while many patients present with localized disease amenable to effective treatment, a substantial subset progresses to advanced stages characterized by distant metastasis [3]. Bone and lung are two of the most common sites for metastatic spread, with bone metastasis occurring in up to 90% of patients with metastatic PCa. This extensive skeletal involvement frequently leads to severe complications, such as debilitating bone pain, pathological fractures, and a marked decline in quality of life [4, 5]. Lung metastasis, though less frequent, is also a significant concern, typically indicating a poor prognosis.

Early identification of patients at high risk for bone and/or lung metastasis is critical for optimizing treatment strategies and enhancing clinical outcomes. Patients identified as having a high risk of metastasis can benefit from early intervention with systemic therapies, potentially delaying disease progression and improving survival. Conversely, accurately stratifying patients at low risk for distant metastasis could mitigate the risks of overtreatment and reduce unnecessary treatment-related morbidities. However, the ability to reliably predict which patients will develop distant metastasis remains a significant challenge in clinical practice [6].

Numerous prognostic models have been developed over the years to address this challenge. These models typically incorporate well-established clinical factors such as Gleason score, prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, and tumor stage [6–8]. Despite their utility, existing models have several inherent limitations. Firstly, many rely on traditional statistical methods, including logistic regression, which may fail to capture the complex, non-linear interactions among multiple clinical variables that contribute to metastasis risk. Secondly, these models often struggle with the highly imbalanced nature of clinical datasets, where metastatic cases constitute only a small fraction of the patient population. Lastly, many existing models have not undergone rigorous external validation, limiting their applicability across diverse patient populations and clinical settings.

Recent advances in machine learning (ML) offer a promising alternative to traditional prognostic models, with the potential to overcome many of these limitations [9, 10]. ML algorithms, particularly ensemble methods such as Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) and Random Forest, have demonstrated superior performance in managing high-dimensional and imbalanced datasets. By identifying complex interactions and subtle patterns among clinical variables, these models can significantly enhance predictive accuracy compared to conventional approaches. Moreover, techniques such as the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) can address class imbalance issues, thereby improving the model’s ability to predict rare events, such as bone or lung metastasis. Despite these advances, the application of machine learning specifically for predicting metastasis to bone and lung in prostate cancer remains underexplored, and few studies have rigorously validated their models externally, raising concerns about their robustness and generalizability.

In this study, we utilized the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database to develop machine learning models aimed at predicting the occurrence of bone and/or lung metastasis in prostate cancer patients. We employed advanced feature selection techniques and multiple machine learning algorithms to construct predictive models and rigorously evaluated their performance through external validation. Our objective was to create robust, generalizable models that can reliably predict metastasis risk, thus supporting personalized treatment decisions and ultimately improving patient outcomes. By integrating these advanced machine learning models into clinical practice, we hope to contribute to a more nuanced, patient-specific approach to managing prostate cancer.

Materials and methods

Data sources and study population

This retrospective study used data from the SEER database to identify patients diagnosed with PCa between 2018 and 2021. The SEER database includes extensive patient data across the United States, providing detailed clinical and demographic information crucial for population-based studies. The initial dataset included 76,586 PCa patients.

A total of 20,479 patients were excluded due to missing critical data, such as unknown race (N = 2206), unknown T stage (N = 5326), unknown PSA levels (N = 7152), unknown Gleason scores (N = 2123), and unknown bone or lung metastasis status (N = 3672). After exclusions, the final study cohort consisted of 56,107 patients, as shown in Fig. 1.

Study Cohort Selection Flowchart

The external validation cohort comprised 201 patients diagnosed with PCA at the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University during the same period (2018–2021). This cohort was used to assess the generalizability and performance of the developed model. All patients in the external cohort were diagnosed with PCa and had complete clinical data for variables included in the model.

Risk factor screening and model construction

To identify the most relevant clinical features for predicting bone and/or lung metastasis, we employed the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression method. LASSO is a regularization technique that applies L1 penalty to the regression coefficients, shrinking some coefficients to zero and effectively selecting a subset of variables that contribute most significantly to the outcome. This method is particularly useful in high-dimensional datasets as it mitigates multicollinearity and improves model interpretability. The LASSO model incorporated various clinical features, including patient age, race, grade clinical, histology recode, T stage, N stage, radiation recode, surgery, lymph nodes removed, Gleason score, tumor Size, PSA levels. Using 10-fold cross-validation, we optimized the regularization parameter λ to minimize prediction error and prevent overfitting. The selected features, including Gleason score, lymph nodes removed, PSA, Radiation recode, Surgery, N stage, tumor size, were then used for subsequent model development.

Model construction and model performance evaluation

Following feature selection, we implemented several machine learning algorithms to build predictive models for bone and/or lung metastasis. The models developed included Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Naive Bayes (NB), and Classification and Regression Trees (CART) [11, 12]. Logistic regression, as a linear model, was used as the baseline for comparison. Random forest, an ensemble method based on multiple decision trees, was selected due to its robustness and ability to capture non-linear relationships. GBM and XGBoost, both tree-based ensemble methods, progressively build models by correcting errors made by previous trees, with XGBoost offering further regularization and computational efficiency. Naive Bayes, based on Bayes’ theorem, was included due to its simplicity and efficiency, especially in handling high-dimensional data. CART was used to provide interpretable tree structures for classification tasks.This study performed hyperparameter tuning on seven mainstream machine learning models, and the optimal parameter combinations for all models are listed in Supplementary file 2.

Given the imbalance in the dataset, where patients with bone and/or lung metastasis represented a minority class, we applied the SMOTE to the training set. SMOTE generates synthetic examples for the minority class by interpolating between existing samples, thereby balancing the class distribution and improving the model’s ability to detect rare events.

Model performance was evaluated based on several key metrics, including accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, F1 score, and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) [13]. Accuracy measures the overall correctness of the model, while sensitivity assesses the model’s ability to correctly identify metastatic cases (true positives). Specificity reflects the model’s ability to correctly identify non-metastatic cases (true negatives). The F1 score is the harmonic mean of sensitivity and precision, offering a balanced measure of model performance in the context of imbalanced data. The AUC quantifies the model’s ability to distinguish between metastatic and non-metastatic cases across different classification thresholds, with higher AUC values indicating better discriminative power. In addition to these metrics, precision-recall curves were also used to assess the model’s performance, particularly in dealing with imbalanced data, as they focus on the trade-off between precision and recall for the minority class. Calibration curves were further employed to evaluate how well the predicted probabilities of metastasis matched the actual observed outcomes, ensuring reliable risk predictions.

To assess the model’s generalizability, we conducted external validation using an independent dataset comprising prostate cancer patients from the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University. This external dataset, which was not used during model training, allowed us to evaluate the robustness of the models across different clinical settings. The same performance metrics—accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, F1 score, and AUC—were computed on the external dataset to ensure that the model retained high predictive power in an independent cohort. This validation step was crucial for confirming the model’s applicability beyond the original training population and for demonstrating its potential use in real-world clinical practice.

The external validation cohort consists of 201 patients diagnosed with PCa at the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University between 2018 and 2021, 51 (25.4%) of whom have bone and/or lung metastasis. Using LASSO regression, we identify the clinical variables most strongly associated with bone and/or lung metastasis in PCa patients. The LASSO shrinkage procedure allows for the selection of a parsimonious set of clinically relevant variables by reducing less significant coefficients to zero (Fig. 2A). Among these, Gleason score consistently maintains a strong and stable coefficient throughout the LASSO process, highlighting its critical role in metastasis prediction. High Gleason scores, particularly 9 or 10, correlate strongly with more aggressive disease phenotypes, consistent with existing clinical knowledge and the model’s findings (Fig. 2B). To better understand the associations among various clinical features, we supplemented the study with a correlation matrix based on Pearson correlation coefficients, which illustrates the linear relationships between variables such as age, PSA level, Gleason score, and T/N stage(Supplementary file 1).

We conduct a comprehensive analysis of seven machine learning algorithms, comparing their performance based on accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and AUC. In line with previous research, models trained using oversampling techniques consistently outperform those trained with undersampling. The detailed performance metrics for each machine learning model are presented in Table 3. Across all oversampled models, the AUC exceeds 0.800, with XGBoost achieving the highest performance, showing an AUC of 0.979 (95% CI: 0.978–0.980) on the training set (Fig. 3A). A comparison of AUC values between XGBoost and traditional logistic regression demonstrates that XGBoost provides significantly higher diagnostic accuracy and predictive power. Moreover, the precision-recall curve for the XGBoost model exhibits an AUC of 0.905, underscoring its superior performance in managing the imbalanced dataset, where metastatic cases are underrepresented (Fig. 3B). Figure 4 illustrates the calibration curve of the XGBoost model, indicating excellent agreement between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes, suggesting robust calibration. The Brier score for the XGBoost model on the internal validation cohort was calculated to be 0.0745, indicating a high level of calibration and overall predictive reliability. Figure 5A presents the confusion matrix for the XGBoost model. The model accurately identifies 2,768 metastatic cases (true positives) and 52,712 non-metastatic cases (true negatives), though it misclassifies 527 metastatic patients as non-metastatic (false negatives). This confusion matrix underscores the model’s strong overall classification accuracy in distinguishing between metastatic and non-metastatic patients. Feature importance analysis reveals that Gleason score, PSA levels, and N stage are the most critical predictors for bone and/or lung metastasis in PCa patients (Fig. 5B).

Discussion

Our study demonstrates the utility of machine learning algorithms, particularly the XGBoost model, in predicting bone and/or lung metastasis in PCa patients, utilizing data from the SEER database and an external validation cohort from the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University. This work holds significant potential for improving clinical decision-making in prostate cancer care by facilitating earlier detection of metastatic progression and informing therapeutic strategies.

The XGBoost model outperformed other machine learning algorithms, achieving an AUC of 0.979 in the training cohort and 0.924 in the external validation cohort. These results underscore XGBoost’s robustness in handling imbalanced datasets and its high accuracy in identifying metastatic cases. Feature importance analysis highlighted the Gleason score, PSA levels, and N stage as the most critical predictors of metastasis. The strong and consistent correlation of the Gleason score with metastatic risk is consistent with existing clinical evidence, reaffirming its role as a key prognostic factor for aggressive PCa [14, 15]. High Gleason scores were linked to a higher risk of metastasis, reflecting more advanced, poorly differentiated tumors, as reported in the literature. Interestingly, PSA levels emerged as the most significant feature in the external validation cohort, underscoring PSA’s role as a critical marker across diverse clinical settings. This finding suggests that PSA remains an important early indicator for disease progression and warrants further exploration in risk stratification across different patient populations.

Our study provides a framework for integrating machine learning tools, such as XGBoost, into clinical practice to enhance the early identification of patients at higher risk of bone and/or lung metastasis [15, 16]. Early identification of high-risk patients is crucial for timely intervention, as metastatic PCa is associated with markedly reduced survival rates. Predictive models like ours could help clinicians optimize resource allocation, tailor follow-up schedules, and intensify treatments for those most likely to benefit. Moreover, the external validation of the XGBoost model in an independent cohort demonstrates its generalizability and applicability beyond the SEER database. This is essential to ensuring that such models can be reliably used across different healthcare systems with varying patient demographics and clinical practices. Importantly, our findings suggest that integrating these models into electronic health records (EHRs) could automate risk assessments, facilitating more personalized and data-driven patient care [17].

The use of machine learning models to predict metastasis in PCa has been explored in previous studies, particularly those leveraging the SEER database [18]. While our study shares some similarities with existing research, such as the application of XGBoost, it offers notable advantages in both methodology and scope. Firstly, our study incorporated a broader range of clinical variables, including age, race, histology, T stage, N stage, radiation recode, surgery, lymph nodes removed, tumor size, PSA levels, and Gleason score. Prior studies often focused on a narrower set of variables, primarily using basic clinical features such as age and Gleason score. By including a more comprehensive set of features, our model captures the complexity of patient and disease characteristics more effectively, resulting in a more precise prediction of metastasis. Secondly, our use of LASSO regression for feature selection represents a methodological advancement over traditional univariate and multivariate logistic regression methods used in previous studies [6]. LASSO automatically selects the most relevant features by shrinking less important coefficients to zero, producing a more parsimonious and interpretable model while reducing the risk of overfitting. This allows our model to retain high predictive accuracy while maintaining simplicity, offering a clear advantage over logistic regression-based feature selection approaches. Thirdly, our study addressed the issue of data imbalance, a common challenge in predicting rare events such as metastasis, by applying the SMOTE. This technique enhanced the model’s ability to detect metastatic cases, significantly improving recall and precision for the minority class. Previous studies have not consistently employed oversampling techniques, which can result in lower sensitivity in detecting metastatic cases—a critical limitation in clinical decision-making. Moreover, the external validation of our model in an independent cohort from the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University adds robustness to our findings, demonstrating that the model performs well across different populations and clinical settings. Previous studies often limited their validation to internal datasets, while our model’s validation in a distinct clinical cohort (AUC of 0.924) underscores its potential for broader clinical application. Lastly, our emphasis on model calibration ensures that the predicted probabilities closely align with observed outcomes, a crucial feature for making reliable clinical decisions. While many studies primarily focus on metrics like AUC, our calibration analysis adds another layer of confidence in the model’s applicability to real-world settings. In summary, our study offers significant advancements over previous research by incorporating a broader set of clinical variables, using LASSO for more efficient feature selection, addressing data imbalance with SMOTE, and validating the model externally in a distinct clinical cohort. These improvements enhance the model’s accuracy and generalizability, making it a valuable tool for predicting bone and/or lung metastasis in PCa patients.

A major strength of our study is the use of a large, population-based cohort from the SEER database, which ensures a broad representation of PCa patients and strengthens the generalizability of our findings. Additionally, the external validation cohort provided a unique opportunity to evaluate the model’s performance in a different clinical context, further enhancing the robustness and potential clinical applicability of the predictive models. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. The retrospective nature of the study introduces potential biases, including selection and information bias, which could affect the results. Additionally, while SMOTE was used to address class imbalance, synthetic data points may not fully capture the variability of real-world metastatic events. Future studies could benefit from prospective validation of the model in real-time clinical workflows and assess its impact on patient outcomes. Furthermore, certain variables such as genetic information, patient comorbidities, and treatment responses were not included in our model, potentially limiting its predictive capability. The incorporation of such data could further enhance model accuracy and provide a more comprehensive understanding of metastatic risk in PCa patients. Lastly, the higher sensitivity observed in the external validation cohort may result from differences in data distribution, such as a higher prevalence of aggressive disease, and the model’s intentional design to prioritize sensitivity for clinical safety. This trade-off, though reducing specificity, is acceptable for early detection and risk stratification in high-risk PCa populations.

Future research should focus on integrating additional data sources, including genomic and biomarker information, to account for individual variability in tumor biology and improve model performance. Incorporating longitudinal patient data could also help capture dynamic changes in clinical features, further refining predictive accuracy over time. Prospective clinical trials evaluating the impact of predictive models on clinical outcomes are warranted to determine their true value in real-world practice. Additionally, the development of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) approaches should be prioritized to enhance the interpretability of these predictive models, increasing clinician trust and facilitating their integration into clinical decision-making. Improving the transparency of these models will bridge the gap between advanced machine learning techniques and the need for interpretable, actionable information in clinical settings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that machine learning models, particularly XGBoost, can effectively predict bone and/or lung metastasis in PCa patients, offering a reliable approach to support clinical decision-making. The strong predictive performance observed across both training and validation cohorts highlights the potential for these models to improve patient care by facilitating early identification of those at higher risk for metastatic disease. While challenges remain regarding data heterogeneity and model implementation, our results suggest that machine learning-based risk prediction tools hold promise for enhancing personalized oncology care, ultimately improving outcomes for patients with prostate cancer.

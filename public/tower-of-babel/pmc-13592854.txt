Identification of CA2 as a potential candidate gene involved in the therapeutic effect of Huatan Huoxue decoction on polycystic ovary syndrome: An integrated study of network pharmacology, Mendelian randomization, and experimental validation
Authors: Chan Zhu, Yijin Zhang, Panwei Hu, Cong Qi, Qiu Chen, Hong Yang, Jing Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeHuatan Huoxue decoction (HTHXD) is effective in treating polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS); however, its underlying mechanisms of action remain unclear. Gene targets were obtained through Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis using druggable genes from public databases and genome-wide association study data for PCOS from the FinnGen R12 database. These targets were integrated with the active ingredients and protein targets of HTHXD retrieved from the TCMSP and BATMAN-TCM databases for network pharmacology analysis. Gene Ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes enrichment analyses were conducted. Core targets were validated using molecular docking. Experimental validation was performed using a letrozole-induced PCOS rat model treated with HTHXD, with key targets assessed via quantitative PCR (qPCR) and immunohistochemical staining. MR analysis identified 131 proteins causally related to PCOS, of which 52 with strong associations remained after quality control. Network pharmacology analysis revealed 549 gene targets and 243 active ingredients in HTHXD. The intersection of these datasets identified four core targets for HTHXD in PCOS treatment (CA2, CASP3, SNCA, and TSPO). Molecular docking identified tanshindiol B, 4-methylenemiltirone, and danshenol B in Danshen and crocetin in leech as key active components. HTHXD substantially ameliorated polycystic ovarian morphology and reduced corpora lutea in PCOS model rats. CA2 expression was significantly upregulated in PCOS ovarian tissue (P < 0.0001), and markedly reduced after HTHXD treatment, consistent with immunohistochemical findings. CA2 may serve as a potential candidate gene associated with the therapeutic response to HTHXD in PCOS, with tanshindiol B and crocetin as major active components.

1. Introduction

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common endocrine disorder in women of reproductive age, characterized by infrequent or absent ovulation, polycystic ovarian morphology, hyper-androgenemia, and insulin resistance. As of 2020, the global prevalence of PCOS was 7.8%, affecting over 24 million women of reproductive age in China [1]. The etiology of PCOS remains unclear, though it is generally associated with genetic factors, hormone imbalances, abnormal levels of inflammatory factors, gut microbiota dysbiosis, abnormal expression of miRNAs, and disruptions in signaling pathways [2]. The underlying physiological and pathological mechanisms are not fully understood, and there is currently no effective etiological treatment, highlighting the urgent need to identify effective therapeutic targets.

Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) is a system of knowledge formed through the crystallization of thousands of years of wisdom. In TCM, imbalances in qi, blood, and bodily fluids can lead to stasis and phlegm-dampness, which are considered major pathological contributors to PCOS. Huatan Huoxue Decoction (HTHXD), a prescription developed by Professor Qi Cong from Shanghai based on years of clinical experience, has demonstrated good efficacy in treating PCOS. Previous studies have shown that HTHXD can effectively improve menstrual volume, color, and quality, regulate hormone levels, and increase ovulation and pregnancy rate [3]. However, the complexity of its herbal constituents and clinical heterogeneity make its precise mechanisms unclear. Network pharmacology, which integrates bioinformatics and pharmacology strategies, analyzes the complex interactions between drug components and disease targets, providing an effective means to elucidate the mechanisms of TCM treatments.

Mendelian randomization (MR) is a powerful causal inference tool that leverages genetic variations to avoid confounding factors and accurately identify the causal targets of diseases [4]. PCOS arises from the complex interactions between genetic and environmental factors and exhibits high clinical heterogeneity, making it difficult for traditional methods to distinguish true pathogenic factors [5]. MR can effectively circumvent confounding factors related to postnatal environments and reverse causation, thereby accurately identifying the core causal targets driving PCOS development among numerous associations and providing a reliable basis for drug development.

In this study, we innovatively integrated MR with network pharmacology. MR was first used to screen for gene targets causally related to PCOS, followed by network pharmacology to identify potential targets of HTHXD in treating PCOS. Molecular docking was then employed to evaluate binding affinities. Finally, animal experiments were conducted to preliminarily explore the potential molecular correlates of the therapeutic effects of HTHXD on PCOS, based on the targets identified by integrating network pharmacology and MR (Fig. 1).

2 Materials and methods

2.1. Composition of HTHXD

HTHXD is an aqueous formulation derived from 11 medicinal herbs: Chenpi (Citrus reticulata Blanco), Banxia (Pinellia ternata (Thunb.) Makino), Shuizhi (Hirudo), Fuling (Poria cocos (Schw.) Wolf), Baizhu (Atractylodes macrocephala Koidz.), Danshen (Salvia miltiorrhiza Bunge), Chuanxiong (Ligusticum chuanxiong Hort.), Houpo (Magnolia officinalis Rehd. Et Wils.), Dangshen (Codonopsis pilosula (Franch.) Nannf.), Eshu (Curcuma phaeocaulis Val.), and Gancao (Glycyrrhiza uralensis Fisch.). Medicinal names are referenced from the Chinese Pharmacopoeia (2020 edition), and plant taxonomy is based on the Medicinal Plant Names Services (MPNS) database.

2.2. Screening of active ingredients and targets of HTHXD

The active components and their associated targets for Chenpi, Chuanxiong, Jiangbanxia, Fuling, Baizhu, Danshen, Houpo, Dangshen, E Zhu, and Zhi Gancao were screened using the TCMSP database. The database1 screened bioactive compounds based on thresholds of drug-like properties ≥0.18 and oral bioavailability ≥30% [6]. All target names were standardized using the UniProt database.2 Since Shuizhi is not included in the TCMSP database, its chemical components and targets were extracted from the BANTAM-TCM database,3 which screened bioactive compounds based on thresholds of predicted target parameters ≥20 and target enrichment analysis parameters (adjusted P-value) < 0.05 [7].

2.3. Exposure and outcome datasets

Since the functional regulation between HYHXD and PCOS has not been established in previous studies, we selected a broader range of single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) for screening, pharmacogenetic variants analyzed were restricted to SNPs located within 1000 kb upstream or downstream of the terminus of druggable genes [8]. Screening criteria included a significant exposure P-value (strongly correlated with exposure) threshold of 5 × 10−8, and SNPs with F-values <10 were excluded to ensure the robustness of the instrumental variables (F = β2/se2, where β represents the effect size of the SNP on the exposure factor, and se represents the corresponding standard error). Using a linkage disequilibrium (clump_kb) threshold of 10,000, a clump_r2 threshold of 0.001 to ensure the independence of SNPs. Ultimately, we identified 8600 druggable genes. Genome-wide association study (GWAS) data from the FINNGEN R12 database were used as the outcome variable, incorporating genotype data from 2214 patients with PCOS and 267,780 controls.4

2.4. MR analysis

We employed a two-sample MR approach to systematically evaluate potential causal relationships between drug targets and PCOS. First, genetic instrumental variables corresponding to drug-related targets were integrated and aligned with outcome data. Subsequently, core MR tests were conducted using MR Egger, weighted median, inverse variance weighted (IVW), simple mode, weighted mode, and MR pleiotropy RESidual sum and outlier (MR-PRESSO) methods. Among these, the IVW method proved most valuable and was combined with MR Egger for heterogeneity testing [9]. The Cochrane Q-statistic was used to assess heterogeneity in genetic instrumental variables, with P > 0.05 indicating no significant heterogeneity and good stability of the instrumental variables. The MR Egger regression intercept was used to evaluate horizontal pleiotropy, where P > 0.05 signaled no substantial horizontal pleiotropy interference, ensuring high reliability of the analysis results [10]. Following MR-PRESSO outlier removal, sensitivity analysis was conducted by sequentially deleting SNPs using the leave-one-out method. Results from the IVW method were compared against all variants to ensure no single SNP influenced our findings. Finally, a Manhattan plot visualized the distribution characteristics of genetic loci in the GWAS analysis, while a forest plot intuitively presented the risk.

2.5. Herb-ingredient-target network construction

A network of interactions between herbs, ingredients, and targets was constructed using Cytoscape 3.9.1. Active compounds of HTHXD, their common targets, and PCOS were integrated [11]. Nodes represent active ingredients, herbs, or targets, and edges indicate the interactions between them. By employing Cytoscape’s NetworkAnalyzer and integrating key active ingredients, core targets, and primary herbs, we elucidated the potential core active components of HTHXD in PCOS treatment. Furthermore, within the PCOS-associated network framework, we evaluated the synergistic therapeutic mechanisms of the herbal formula, which operates through multi-component and multi-target pathways.

2.6. GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses

To further investigate the functional characteristics and metabolic pathway mechanisms of the selected potential therapeutic target genes, we performed Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses using R software (version 4.4.2). Results were visualized using the “ggplot2” package, selecting the top 10 enriched terms for each category with P < 0.05.

2.7. Molecular docking of active ingredients with core targets

Three-dimensional structures of small-molecule ligands were obtained from the PubChem database5 and stored in SDF format. Corresponding protein IDs were retrieved from the UniProt database, and their three-dimensional structures were searched and downloaded from the protein database. 6 Molecular docking experiments were performed using the CB-Dock2 platform.7 All parameters were set to default values for automated blind docking, and the result with the lowest free energy was selected for visualization analysis.

2.8. Reagents

HTHXD granules were supplied by Sichuan Neo-Green Pharmaceutical Technology Development Co., Ltd. The granules were uniformly prepared by the Pharmacy of Traditional Chinese Medicine at West China Second University Hospital, Sichuan University. Each daily dose was divided into three individual sachets, with each sachet containing 6.8 g of granules (equivalent to 19.25 g of raw herbs). Letrozole tablets (Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co., Ltd., National Medicine Approval No. H19991001) were purchased from the pharmacy of the West China Second University Hospital. Sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (Batch No. 9004-32-4) was obtained from Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd.

2.9. Animals

Healthy female Sprague–Dawley rats (n = 18, 5 weeks old, 120–140 g) were provided by SiPeiFu Biotechnology Co (INC) (SYXK, 2024–0001, Beijing, China). All animals were maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions in a temperature-controlled room at a constant temperature of 24 ± 1 °C with a 12-h light/dark cycle and had free access to a standard rodent diet and water. Following a series of experiments, the rats were anesthetized using an isoflurane inhalation anesthesia machine (3%–4% isoflurane in oxygen) and maintained using continuous mask inhalation (maintenance concentration: 2%). The abdominal aorta was punctured, circulating blood was drained, and the specimen was collected after perfusion with 4 °C saline. All experiments were approved by the Ethical Committee for the Experimental Use of Animals at the West China Second University Hospital (No.2025–037).

2.10. Modeling

Eighteen female rats were randomly divided into control, model, and HTHXD groups. Following the method described in the literature, the model and HTHXD groups were administered letrozole orally at a dose of 1 mg/(kg·d) in a 1% sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC-Na) solution every morning. The control group received an equal volume of 1% CMC-Na vehicle. Administration continued for 21 days to establish the PCOS model. During the last 10 days of modeling, vaginal cytology smears were performed daily at 8:00 AM to monitor estrous cycles. Successful PCOS modeling was confirmed by the presence of acyclicity in the vaginal smears of rats in the model group.

2.11. Treatment

HTHXD granules were dissolved in distilled water and prepared as suspensions of varying concentrations to ensure a consistent administration volume. The HTHXD-treated group received the suspension at a dose of 3.96 g/(kg·d), calculated based on body surface area translation from the clinical human equivalent dose. The model and control groups received an equivalent volume of distilled water. Administration commenced on the first day of modeling and was performed once daily for 4 consecutive weeks.

2.12. Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from ovarian tissues using Orizol RNA Extraction Reagent (Oriscience, Cat No. NB301). RNA was reverse transcribed into complementary DNA using the cDNA Synthesis SuperMix Kit with gDNA Eraser (Cat No. E047-01 A, Novoprotein, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. qPCR was conducted on a qTOWER3 auto instrument (Analytikjena, Beijing, China) using NovaStrat SYBR qPCR SuperMix PLus (Cat No. E096-01 A, Novoprotein, China). Relative RNA expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. β-actin was used as an internal control. Primers sequences were as follows: Car2: TACCTCTCACCCATTGTGC and GCCCTA AGTCAACCACCA; Casp3: TCCACGAGCAGAGTCAAA and GCCAACCAAGTTCACACA; Tspo: CCCGCTTGCTGTATCCTT and TCCTGAT GGCTAGGTGTCC; Snca: GGGCGTCCTCTATGTAGGT and GCTCCCTCACTGTCTTCT.

2.13. Histological staining

After collection, ovarian tissue specimens were immediately fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for 48 h, followed by a 30-min rinse under running water. After dehydration, clearing, and wax infiltration using an automatic tissue processor (EXCELSIOR AS, Thermo Scientific, Kalamazoo, MI, USA), the tissues were embedded in paraffin using an embedding station (HISTOSTAR, Thermo Scientific, Kalamazoo, MI, USA). Continuous sections were prepared at a thickness of 4 μm using a microtome (HM 340 E, Thermo Scientific). Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was performed to demonstrate the fundamental histological architecture of the tissues.

2.14. Immunohistochemical staining

The sections were blocked with 10% normal bovine serum at room temperature for 1 h and incubated overnight at 4 °C with the primary antibody: rabbit monoclonal anti-CA2 antibody (ab178846, 1:2000 dilution, Abcam, USA). The sections were then incubated with horseradish peroxidase-labeled secondary antibody (DAKO EnVision™ kit) at 37 °C for 30 min. Immunoreactivity was detected using 0.05% diaminobenzidine, and the sections were visualized with an inverted fluorescence microscope (Model IX83, Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). Three randomly selected microscopic fields in the ovarian granulosa cell area were analyzed, and CA2 staining intensity was measured using ImageJ software.

2.15. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 8.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Experimental data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) from at least three independent experiments. Student’s t-test was used for comparisons between two groups, and one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s HSD post-hoc test was used for comparisons among three or more groups. A P-value ≤0.05 was considered statistically significant.

3. Results

3.1. Active ingredients in HTHXD and target screening

In the TCMSP database, a search was performed using the screening criteria of DL ≥ 0.18 and OB ≥ 30%, and 230 active ingredients were obtained: from Baizhu, 5 from Chenpi, 7 from Chuanxiong, 21 from Dangshen, 65 from Danshen, 3 from E zhu, 15 from Fuling, 92 from Gancao, 13 from Banxia, 2 from Houpo. And using the criteria of BC ≥ 20 and adjusted P-value <0.05 in the BANTAM-TCM database, 11 active ingredients of Shuizhi were obtained (Online Resource 1). At the same time, we downloaded all targets of the drug. Then we used the UniProt database to convert the full names of the gene targets into their corresponding gene symbols, and 1005 gene targets were identified: 16 for Baizhu, 26 for Chuanxiong, 98 for Dangshen, 116 for Danshen, 17 for E zhu, 18 for Fuling, 208 for Gancao, 81 for Banxia, 23 for Houpo, and 342 for Shuizhi. After merging and removing 456 duplicate gene targets, 549 unique gene targets remained (Online Resource 2).

3.2. Candidate druggable genes for PCOS

We examined MR associations between proteins with accessible indexable druggable gene signals and PCOS outcome risk across 8600 druggable genes using the druggable gene database. MR analysis identified 3830 SNPs. We identified 131 protein–PCOS pairs with marginal significance (P < 0.05). Online Resource 3 showed after MR quality control, five consistent targets emerged from five analyses, yielding 52 druggable genes as the strongest MR evidence for PCOS treatment (Fig. 2, Table 1). A Manhattan plot (Fig. 3a) illustrates these 52 potential targets. Then we took the intersection of the 52 potential targets obtained from MR and the 549 gene targets of HTHXD screened in Section 3.1, and obtained 4 overlapping genes as our core targets. Ultimately, we identified carbonic anhydrase II (CA2), caspase-3 (CASP3), alpha-synuclein (SNCA), and translocator protein (TSPO) as potential targets for HTHXD treatment in PCOS, which was consistent across all five MR analyses (Fig. 3b). Genetic prediction indicated that increased CASP3, SNCA, and CA2 expression is associated with higher PCOS risk. Conversely, elevated TSPO levels suppressed PCOS occurrence, suggesting an inverse association between TSPO and PCOS risk (Fig. 3c). Sensitivity analyses revealed no heterogeneity in Cochran’s Q test (P > 0.05), no pleiotropy in MREgger regression intercept (P > 0.05), and no reverse causality in MR-Steiger directionality (P < 0.05). The leave-one-out method showed that removing any SNP associated with PCOS did not alter the results (Fig. 3d).

3.3. Herb-ingredient-target network construction and topological network analysis

At the system level, a herb-ingredient-target (H–I-T) network was constructed using Cytoscape 3.9.1. Only the active ingredients of seven herbs interacted with the overlapping target genes. Therefore, we constructed an interaction diagram between 7 herbs, 39 ingredients, and 4 overlapping target genes (Fig. 4a), comprising 50 nodes and 81 edges. We used Cytoscape’s built-in NetworkAnalyzer to evaluate the node degree values. Danshen (degree = 27), licorice (degree = 7), and Pinellia (degree = 3) were the main active herbs in HTHXD. The active ingredients tanshindiol B, 4-methyl-enemiltirone, and danshenol B from Danshen connected to the CA2 target; crocetin from Hirudo connected to TSPO and SNCA targets; and luteolin from Codonopsis connected to the CASP3 target. Intersecting targets were CA2 (degree = 31), CASP3 (degree = 7), SNCA (degree = 1), and TSPO (degree = 1).

3.4. GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses

We conducted a biological pathway enrichment analysis on 52 druggable genes obtained through MR to explore prospective therapeutic target genes for PCOS, and further analyzed CA2, CASP3, SNCA, and TSPO to elucidate the potential mechanisms of HTHXD in treating PCOS. As shown in Fig. 4b, GO analysis revealed that these genes were mainly associated with metabolism and kinase activity in biological processes, closely related to the outer plasma membrane and secretory granule membrane in cellular components, and played an important role in regulating enzyme activity in molecular functions. KEGG analysis indicated that the genes were primarily enriched in pathways such as the FoxO signaling pathway, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and insulin resistance (Fig. 4c). CA2 was mainly associated with carbonic anhydrase activity, CASP3 was primarily enriched in the apoptosis pathway, SNCA primarily affected protein degradation and intracellular stability, and TSPO was closely related to metabolic disorder pathways.

3.5. Validation of HTHXD component and core target relationships via molecular docking

Further molecular docking studies of the active components of HTHXD with core targets (CA2, CASP3, SNCA, TSPO) demonstrated the formula’s effectiveness against PCOS. The binding activities between core target proteins and active chemical components were below −5 kcal/mol, indicating stable interactions between them (Table 2). Lower binding energies signify more stable interactions, suggesting that these were more promising ligand–target pairs. Fig. 5 shows the molecular docking results that demonstrate the optimal binding affinities of the active components for each core target protein.

3.6. Experimental validation of HTHXD’s effects on PCOS core targets

To evaluate and validate the effects of the Mendelian-predicted HTHXD on core PCOS targets, we conducted experiments using ovarian tissues from letrozole-induced PCOS rat models. Initially, we observed that vaginal smears from rats in the control group exhibited cyclical changes characteristic of proestrus, estrus, and metestrus. During proestrus, the smears were predominantly composed of enlarged, oval, or round nucleated epithelial cells with occasional small numbers of leukocytes (primarily lymphocytes and neutrophils) and cornified epithelial cells. In estrus, the smears were primarily filled with large, flat, anucleated cornified epithelial cells with irregular borders and shapes; some samples showed these cells clustered in sheet-like arrangements, with very few leukocytes or nucleated epithelial cells present. In contrast, rats in the model group predominantly exhibited vaginal smears characteristic of diestrus, lacking regular estrous cyclicity. Their smears showed a mixture of irregular cornified epithelial cells, nucleated epithelial cells, and leukocytes, without a clear predominance of any specific cell type at the expected stages. Most model rats persistently remained in diestrus, with smears featuring scattered leukocytes (mainly lymphocytes and neutrophils) occasionally accompanied by nucleated and cornified epithelial cells (Fig. 6a). The absence of normal estrous cycle progression in the model group indicated successful establishment of the PCOS model.

We experimentally examined the expression levels of the four shared targets identified using MR and network pharmacology. Compared to the control group, the expression level of CA2 in the ovarian tissue of letrozole-induced PCOS rats was significantly higher (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 6b). In contrast, the levels of SNCA, TPSO, and CASP3 showed no significant changes (P > 0.05) (Fig. 6c–e).

To further investigate the specific effect of HTHXD on the validated CA2 gene, we compared CA2 expression in ovarian tissues between the PCOS and HTHXD-treated groups. The HTHXD group showed a significant reduction in CA2 expression compared to the PCOS group (Fig. 7a). Serial sections of rat ovarian tissue were prepared and subjected to H&E staining and CA2 immunohistochemistry (Fig. 7b and c). Histological examination revealed multiple cystic follicles and a marked decrease in corpora lutea in the PCOS group, indicating a substantial impairment in follicular development and ovulation (Fig. 7d). These pathological changes were notably reversed by HTHXD treatment. Immunohistochemical analysis showed that the granulosa cells of the PCOS group demonstrated a significant increase in CA2 expression compared to those of the control group, whereas the HTHXD group exhibited a significant decrease in CA2 expression relative to the PCOS group (Fig. 7e), which was consistent with the qPCR findings.

4. Discussion

PCOS, the most common reproductive metabolic disorder, poses a substantial threat to women’s physical and mental health. Existing evidence confirms that TCM has definite clinical efficacy in treating PCOS [12,13]. To further explore its therapeutic mechanisms, we constructed a research framework that bridges classical TCM theories with modern biomedical science, enabling effective exploration of the complex interactions between chemical components of Chinese herbs and protein targets. First, we obtained druggable genes and large-scale GWAS data from public databases and identified potential therapeutic genes for PCOS using MR. We then applied network pharmacology analysis to systematically analyze the active components and targets of HTHXD in PCOS treatment, successfully identifying four candidate genes with potential relevance to PCOS treatment: CA2, CASP3, SNCA, and TSPO. H–I-T network construction and molecular docking further validated the druggability of these target genes. Finally, experimental validation using a letrozole-induced PCOS rat model identified CA2 as the most consistently associated gene among the four candidates, warranting further investigation into its role in HTHXD’s therapeutic effects on PCOS.

PCOS is a highly heritable and complex disorder. Systematic studies of relatives of affected women have shown familial clustering of the reproductive features of this syndrome [5]. In Chinese and European cohorts, 20 common risk-related loci for PCOS have been identified through GWAS [5], and rare variants in DENND1A [14], AMH [15], and AMHR2 [15] have been discovered through nextgeneration sequencing. Furthermore, unsupervised clustering analyses have identified discrete and reproducible reproductive and metabolic PCOS subtypes, suggesting heterogeneous genetic architectures [5]. To overcome the limitations of traditional methods of target discovery, we applied MR to identify druggable targets with causal relationships from a genetic perspective. Among 8600 druggable genes analyzed, 52 candidate genes showed strong causal associations with PCOS. Of these, CA2, CASP3, SNCA, and TSPO were identified as potential targets of HTHXD for PCOS treatment through five consistent MR analyses without evidence of heterogeneity, underscoring their reliability as potential therapeutic targets. CASP3, SNCA, and CA2 were identified as risk factors for PCOS, whereas TSPO was a protective factor.

HTHXD is a decoction composed of 11 medicinal herbs. The formula is structured based on the TCM principle of “Jun-Chen-Zuo-Shi’ (Chief, Deputy, Assistant, and Envoy), which defines the hierarchical and synergistic relationships among the ingredients. Network pharmacology analysis identified Danshen, Banxia, and Gancao as the most critical active herbs associated with the intersecting targets, with tanshindiol B, 4-methylenemiltirone, and crocetin as the main active components. In TCM theory, Danshen is primarily used to treat blood stasis syndromes and exhibits extensive pharmacological activities, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antidiabetic effects. A randomized controlled trial from Iran demonstrated that Danshen extract improved BMI and insulin resistance in PCOS patients [16]. In animal studies, Danshen extract was shown to ameliorate follicular developmental disorders [17] and improve endocrine metabolic function of the endometrium [18]. Banxia primarily functions to dry dampness and resolve phlegm, and exhibits anti-obesity, antiinflammatory, and antioxidant properties. It can improve metabolic disorders by regulating lipid metabolism and enhancing insulin sensitivity. Insulin resistance and obesity are prominent features of PCOS, with approximately 81% of PCOS patients presenting insulin resistance [19]. Formulas containing Banxia are frequently used to treat PCOS [12,20–22]. Animal studies suggest that TCM formulas containing Banxia as the main component improve cystic follicles, fasting insulin, and Homeostasis Model Assessment for Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR) index in PCOS-insulin resistance (PCOS-IR) mouse models [22]. Zhao et al. [21] demonstrated that modified Banxia Xiexin decoction could ameliorate PCOS-IR by regulating intestinal microbiota imbalance and improving metabolic disorders. Gancao (licorice) was also identified as an important active component in HTHXD, with six active constituents closely associated with key target genes in this study. In TCM prescriptions, licorice is one of the most commonly used herbs, typically used in combination with other herbs. It tonifies qi, reduces the toxicity of other herbs, and harmonizes the overall formula. A study conducted in South Korea suggested that the ethanol extract of licorice can alleviate letrozole-induced symptoms of PCOS by regulating hormonal imbalances and follicular irregularities [23].

GO enrichment analysis revealed that the therapeutic mechanism of HTHXD in PCOS involves multi-target and multi-level regulation across various biological processes. Notably, HTHXD influences the regulation of fatty acid metabolic processes. Obesity is a prominent clinical feature of PCOS, and targeted weight loss has been shown to alleviate symptoms and restore ovulation [24]. Studies indicate that adipose tissue dysfunction plays a crucial role in PCOS pathophysiology, involving dysregulation in storage capacity, hypoxia, hyperplasia, impaired adipogenesis, impaired insulin signaling and glucose transport, dysregulated lipolysis and non-esterified free fatty acid kinetics, adipokine and cytokine dysregulation, subacute inflammation, epigenetic dysregulation, mitochondrial dysfunction, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and oxidative stress [25]. KEGG pathway analysis demonstrated that HTHXD primarily modulates pathways related to FoxO signaling, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and insulin resistance, all of which are closely associated with glucose-lipid metabolism and inflammation. PCOS represents a state of chronic inflammation, with persistently elevated inflammatory markers such as interleukin (IL)-6, IL-18, TNF-α, and C-reactive protein [26]. TCM formulas can ameliorate PCOS through multiple mechanisms, including attenuation of chronic low-grade inflammation [13]. This suggests that HTHXD may alleviate metabolic and inflammatory disturbances in PCOS by modulating adipose tissue dysfunction, a mechanism warranting further investigation.

The H–I-T network revealed interactions among 7 herbs, 39 components, and 4 target genes. Topological analysis showed that CA2 had the highest node degree, indicating its central role in HTHXD’s therapeutic effect on PCOS, followed by CASP3. Our animal experiments also showed that CA2 expression was substantially increased in the ovaries of letrozole-induced PCOS rats, whereas changes in CASP3, SNCA, and TSPO were not statistically significant. CA2 is a metalloenzyme that catalyzes the reversible hydration/dehydration of CO2/HCO3 −. Additionally, CA2 is involved in other catalytic reactions, including esterase activity. Although studies on the association between CA2 and PCOS are limited, aspirin―a well-known antiplatelet drug―has been identified as an effective CA2 inhibitor [27]. Furthermore, Xuefu Zhuyu decoction, another classic TCM formula for promoting blood circulation and removing blood stasis, has been found to exert therapeutic effects through CA2 [28].

From the perspective of TCM theory, follicular developmental disorders associated with PCOS are often attributed to the interaction of “blood stasis” and “phlegm-dampness.” HTHXD is designed to address these pathological states through its composition, which promotes blood circulation, resolves stasis, dries dampness, and transforms phlegm. Although the multi-component and multitarget nature of TCM formulas poses challenges for mechanistic research, this study systematically elucidated the potential mechanisms of HTHXD in the treatment of PCOS by integrating network pharmacology, bioinformatics, and experimental validation. Our findings suggest that HTHXD exerts its effects through key targets such as CA2, modulating multiple pathways, including fatty acid metabolism, insulin resistance, and chronic inflammation, thereby ameliorating both metabolic and reproductive abnormalities in PCOS. These findings not only provide scientific support for the clinical application of HTHXD but also offer a methodological reference for further exploring the mechanisms of TCM formulas from a “disease-pattern integration” perspective.

5. Limitations

First, the outcome data for the MR analysis were derived exclusively from European populations, whereas HTHXD is primarily applied to Chinese patients. These differences in genetic background may introduce bias. Second, the current TCM targets and their active ingredients were derived from online databases, which have data limitations. Third, some active compounds and targets present in HTHXD are not included in the online PubChem and protein databases used for our analysis. Finally, the in vivo validation was preliminary, with a relatively small sample size; although alterations in target expression were identified, downstream functional effects and precise molecular mechanisms were not investigated.

Beyond these technical and data-related limitations, a more fundamental conceptual limitation should be acknowledged. While our experimental results demonstrate a consistent association between CA2 expression changes and HTHXD treatment response, the current study design does not establish a causal role for CA2 in mediating the therapeutic effects. Functional experiments, such as CA2 knockdown or overexpression, or the use of specific CA2 inhibitors, would be required to determine whether CA2 indeed plays a causal mediating role. As such, our conclusions regarding CA2 as a therapeutic target remain preliminary and hypothesis-generating.

6. Conclusion

By integrating MR and network pharmacology, we identified CA2 as a candidate gene strongly associated with the therapeutic effects of HTHXD in PCOS, along with CASP3, SNCA, and TSPO as additional associated targets. Experimental validation in a letrozole-induced PCOS rat model confirmed the abnormal upregulation of CA2 in ovarian tissue and its significant downregulation following HTHXD treatment, accompanied by improvements in ovarian morphology. These findings suggest that HTHXD may alleviate PCOS through pathways involving CA2, which are potentially related to fatty acid metabolism, insulin resistance, and chronic inflammation. Overall, this study provides a preliminary basis for understanding the molecular correlates of HTHXD’s action in PCOS treatment, while further functional studies are needed to establish causality.

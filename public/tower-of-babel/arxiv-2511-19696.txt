On the de Rham cohomology of cyclic covers
Aristides Kontogeorgis, Orestis Lygdas

Abstract
. We compute explicit bases for the de Rham cohomology of cyclic covers of the projective line defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p ≥ 0 p\geq 0 . For both Kummer and Artin–Schreier extensions, we describe precise k k -bases for the cohomology groups H 1 ​ ( X , 𝒪 X ) H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}) and H 0 ​ ( X , Ω X ) H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}) , and we use these to construct an explicit basis for the first de Rham cohomology group H dR 1 ​ ( X / k ) H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k) via Čech cohomology. Our approach relies on detailed computations of divisors of functions and differentials, together with residue calculations and the duality pairing between H 0 ​ ( X , Ω X ) H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}) and H 1 ​ ( X , 𝒪 X ) H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}) . The resulting expressions are given in closed form in terms of the defining equation of the cover, making the cohomology fully explicit and readily applicable to questions involving group actions, Hodge–de Rham filtrations, and the study of p p -cyclic covers.

Introduction

There are few classes of curves where explicit computations can be made. Cyclic covers of curves which generalize hyperelliptic curves are among them. H. Hasse in
[

provided a unified treatment of both Artin-Schreier and Kummer extensions of the rational function fields and H. Boseck
[
1

Furthermore, the work of Sekiguchi, Oort, and Suwa

[

,

,

highlights deeper
connections between Kummer and Artin–Schreier-type extensions via group scheme
theory, suggesting that a unified framework for these computations is possible,
[
2
]
,
[
8
]
.

On the other hand there is a lot of interest concerning study of the de Rham cohomology of algebraic curves over fields of arbitrary characteristic especially with respect to their Galois module structure and the splitting problem of the exact sequence

1\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)\rightarrow H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})\rightarrow 0,

seen as Galois module, see

[
3
]
,

[

]
,

[

]
,

[

]
,

[
9
]
.
For cyclic covers of the projective line, the geometry and arithmetic of the
curve are encoded in an explicit equation, and this allows one to perform
cohomological computations in a concrete manner.
Nevertheless, even in this relatively accessible class of curves, an explicit
description of the Hodge–de Rham exact sequence and the associated bases for

H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k)
remains delicate.

The aim of this article is to give explicit
k
-bases for the cohomology groups

H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
,
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
, and the first de Rham cohomology group

H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k)
when
X
is a cyclic cover of the projective line of either
Kummer or Artin–Schreier type.
Our approach relies on Čech cohomology with respect to the natural affine
covering induced by the projection
X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, combined with explicit
computations of divisors of functions and differentials. This method allows us
to describe bases in completely elementary terms, making the cohomology
transparent in examples and suitable for applications to deformation theory,

G
-actions on cohomology, and the study of the Hodge–de Rham sequence.

The structure of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
we treat the case of Kummer extensions, beginning with divisor
computations, then exhibiting bases for
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
and

H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
, and finally constructing an explicit basis for

H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k)
.
Section
3
contains the analogous results for Artin–Schreier extensions, where
the arithmetic of characteristic
p
introduces new phenomena.
Throughout both sections, duality pairings and residue computations play a
central role.

2.
Kummer extensions

2.1.
Computations

We consider the curve given as a Kummer extension of
k(x)
, with
k
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
given by an equation of the form

X:y^{n}=f(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}}.

We recall some facts about these curves, see
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
.
Without loss of generality we can assume that
l:=\deg f(x)\equiv 0\pmod{n}
. Thus we get that the points above
\infty
are not ramified in the cover
X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The
\rho_{i}
’s are the only points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in the branch locus of the cover, and each point above
x=\rho_{i}
is ramified with ramification index
e_{i}=\frac{n}{(n,l_{i})}
. The points of
X
lying above each
\rho_{i}
are

P_{i,1},\ldots,P_{i,g_{i}}
, where
g_{i}=(n,l_{i})
, while the points above
\infty
are
P_{\infty,1},\ldots,P_{\infty,n}
.

If
0\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
is in the branch locus, then its corresponding exponent in the equation of
X
will also be denoted as
l_{0}
, the number of points above it equals
g_{0}=(n,l_{0})
, and their ramification index is
e_{0}=\frac{n}{(n,l_{0})}
. If
0
is not in the branch locus, then
l_{0}:=n,g_{0}:=n,\text{ and }e_{0}:=1
.

We compute

\displaystyle(x)

\displaystyle=e_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{g_{0}}P_{0,j}-\sum_{m=1}^{n}P_{\infty,m}

\displaystyle(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{g_{i}}P_{i,j}-t\sum_{m=1}^{n}P_{\infty,m}

where

t=l/n,\text{ and }\lambda_{i}=\frac{e_{i}l_{i}}{n}=\frac{l_{i}}{(l_{i},n)}.

For each
\mu
,
\mu\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
, we define
m_{i}^{(\mu)}
,
\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}
by

\mu\lambda_{i}=\mu\frac{l_{i}}{(l_{i},n)}=m_{i}^{(\mu)}e_{i}+\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)},\text{ with }0\leq\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}\leq e_{i}-1.

We set

(1)

g_{\mu}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{m_{i}^{(\mu)}}\text{ for }\mu\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}

and we compute

(2)

\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}\sum_{j=1}^{g_{i}}P_{i,j}-t^{(\mu)}\sum_{m=1}^{n}P_{\infty,m},

where

t^{(\mu)}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}.

2.2.
A
k
-vector space bases for
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
,
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})

We begin by computing a basis for
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
.
We denote by
\pi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
a covering map from
X
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For every point
a\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
we define
U_{a}:=X\backslash\pi^{-1}(a)
.
Observe that
x^{i}y^{j}
for
i,j\geq 0
are elements of
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{\infty})
. Also by equation (
2
) the elements

\frac{y^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)}

are in
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{\infty})
, while the elements

\frac{1}{x^{\nu}}\frac{y^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)}\text{ for }\nu\geq t^{(\mu)}\text{ and }\frac{1}{x^{\nu}}\text{ for }\nu\geq 0,

are in
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{0})
. We identify the first sheaf cohomology group of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
with the corresponding first Čech cohomology group, so

H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})=\frac{\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{0}\cap U_{\infty})}{\{f_{0}-f_{\infty}:f_{0}\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{0}),f_{\infty}\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{\infty})\}}.

The
k
-vector space
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
admits as basis the following linear equivalence classes

h_{\mu,\nu}:=\left[\frac{1}{x^{\nu}}\frac{y^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)}\right],1\leq\mu\leq n-1\text{ s.t }t^{(\mu)}\geq 2,1\leq\nu\leq t^{(\mu)}-1.

The number of these classes equals

\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}(t^{(\mu)}-1),

which are equal to the genus of the curve
X
according to
[
1
, eq. (47)]
.

We will now give a basis for the space
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
.
By
[
1
, Satz 16]
we get that a basis of holomorphic differentials is given by

(3)

\omega_{\mu,\nu}:=x^{\nu-1}\frac{g_{\mu}(x)}{y^{\mu}}dx,\mu\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\text{ s.t. }t^{(\mu)}\geq 2,1\leq\nu\leq t^{(\mu)}-1.

For our description of
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
using Čech cohomology, we can see from Tait’s dissertation
[
16
, sec. 2.5]
that Serre’s duality map becomes (the coefficient is not essential to the definition of the map, but will be useful later)

\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})\times H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow k

\displaystyle\langle\omega,[f]\rangle

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(-\frac{1}{n}\right)\sum_{P\in\pi^{-1}(\infty)}\mathrm{Res}_{P}(f\omega),

where
\mathrm{Res}_{P}(\omega)
is the residue of the differential
\omega
at the point
P\in X

[
15
, Chap. II, 7,8,12]
. We want to show that
\{h_{\mu,\nu}\}_{\mu,\nu}
is the dual basis to
\{\omega_{\mu,\nu}\}_{\mu,\nu}
, i.e.

\langle\omega_{\mu_{1},\nu_{1}},h_{\mu_{2},\nu_{2}}\rangle=\delta_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}\delta_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}.

We will first recall the definition of the residue of a differential.
Let
F
be the function field of
X
, denote by
\widehat{F_{P}}=k((t))
the completion of
F
for the valuation
v_{P}
, where
t
is a local uniformizer at
P
. We identify
f\in F
with its image in
\widehat{F_{P}}
which is called the Laurent expansion of
f
at
P
:

f=\sum_{i>>-\infty}c_{i}t^{i},c_{i}\in k.

The residue
\mathrm{Res}_{P}(\omega)

is defined as the coefficient
c_{-1}
of the Laurent expansion of
f
in a decomposition
\omega=fdt
.

Some important properties for the residue can be found in no.11 of
[
15
]
:

(1)

\mathrm{Res}_{P}(\omega)
is
k
-linear in
\omega
.

(2)

\mathrm{Res}_{P}(\omega)=0
if
v_{P}(\omega)\geq 0
.

(3)

\mathrm{Res}_{P}(dg)=0
for all
g\in\widehat{F_{P}}
.

(4)

\mathrm{Res}_{P}(dg/g)=v_{P}(g)
for all
g\in\widehat{F_{P}}\setminus\{0\}
.

We will also need the following
lemma
[
15
, lemma 4 no. 12]
:

Lemma 1
.

If we have a covering map
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
of curves, then for every point
P\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
,

\sum_{Q\rightarrow P}\mathrm{Res}_{Q}(\omega)=\mathrm{Res}_{P}(\text{Tr}(\omega)),

the sum being over all the points
Q\in X
such that
\varphi(Q)=P
.

In our case we have the covering map

\displaystyle\pi:

\displaystyle X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}

\displaystyle(x,y)\mapsto x,

which corresponds to the Galois extension of function fields
F/E
, where
E=k(x)
is the function field of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
If

\omega=fdx
, then

\text{Tr}(\omega)=\text{Tr}_{F/E}(\omega)=\text{Tr}_{F/E}(f)dx.

Therefore we get

\sum_{m=1}^{n}\mathrm{Res}_{P_{\infty,m}}(\omega)=\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}(\text{Tr}(\omega))=\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}(\text{Tr}_{F/E}(f)dx).

Proposition 2
.

The bases given above for
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
and
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
are dual to each other.

Proof.

We calculate

\displaystyle\langle\omega_{\mu_{1},\nu_{1}},h_{\mu_{2},\nu_{2}}\rangle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{P\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Res}_{P}\left(\frac{x^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}y^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}}g_{\mu_{1}}(x)}{g_{\mu_{2}(x)}}\frac{dx}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left(\text{Tr}_{F/E}\left(\frac{x^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}y^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}}g_{\mu_{1}}(x)}{g_{\mu_{2}(x)}}\frac{dx}{x}\right)\right).

If
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2},\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}
, then

\langle\omega_{\mu_{1},\nu_{1}},h_{\mu_{2},\nu_{2}}\rangle=-\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left(n\frac{dx}{x}\right)=\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left(\frac{d\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)}{\frac{1}{x}}\right)=1,

using the properties of the residue mentioned above.

Suppose now that either
\mu_{1}\neq\mu_{2}
or
\nu_{1}\neq\nu_{2}
. Since the extension is Galois and only
y
is not in the base field of the extension, we have

\text{Tr}_{F/E}\left(\frac{x^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}-1}y^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}}g_{\mu_{1}}(x)}{g_{\mu_{2}(x)}}\right)=\frac{x^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}-1}g_{\mu_{1}}(x)}{g_{\mu_{2}(x)}}\text{Tr}_{F/E}(y^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}}).

Since we are in the Kummer extension case,
{\mathrm{G}al}(E/F)=\langle\sigma\rangle
where

\displaystyle\sigma:F

\displaystyle\rightarrow F

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto x

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\mapsto y\cdot\zeta_{n}

where
\zeta_{n}
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity. Thus

(4)

\text{Tr}_{F/E}(y^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma^{i}(y^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}})=y^{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\zeta_{n}^{i(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})}.

If
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
and
\nu_{1}\neq\nu_{2}
, then

\langle\omega_{\mu_{1},\nu_{1}},h_{\mu_{2},\nu_{2}}\rangle=-\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left(nx^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}-1}dx\right).

If
\nu_{1}>\nu_{2}
, then
\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}-1\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left(nx^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}-1}dx\right)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left(-n\frac{1}{x^{-(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}-1)}}x^{2}d\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left(-n\frac{1}{x^{-(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}+1)}}d\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)

since
d\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=-\frac{1}{x^{2}}dx
. Since
\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}+1\geq 2
, from the definition of the residue we see that

\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left(-n\frac{1}{x^{-(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}+1)}}d\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)=0.

If
\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}
, then working similarly we see that again
\langle\omega_{\mu_{1},\nu_{1}},h_{\mu_{2},\nu_{2}}\rangle=0
.

If
\mu_{1}\neq\mu_{2}
, then the sum of roots of unity appearing in eq. (
4
) is known to be zero.

In conclusion,

\langle\omega_{\mu_{1},\nu_{1}},h_{\mu_{2},\nu_{2}}\rangle=0

when either
\mu_{1}\neq\mu_{2}
or
\nu_{1}\neq\nu_{2}
.
∎

2.3.
A
k
-vector space basis for
H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)

Using the same description for the first de Rham cohomology group of
X
(via Čech cohomology) as the one given in
[
9
]
we have that
H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)
can be calculated by the quotient

\frac{\{(\omega_{0},\omega_{\infty},f_{0\infty})\in\Omega_{X}(U_{0})\times\Omega_{X}(U_{\infty})\times\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{0}\cap U_{\infty})\mid df_{0\infty}=\omega_{0}|_{U_{0}\cap U_{\infty}}-\omega_{\infty}|_{U_{0}\cap U_{\infty}}\}}{\{(df_{0},df_{\infty},f_{0}|_{U_{0}\cap U_{\infty}}-f_{\infty}|_{U_{0}\cap U_{\infty}})\mid f_{0}\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{0}),f_{\infty}\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{\infty})\}}.

There are also canonical maps

\displaystyle i:H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\mapsto[(\omega|_{U_{0}},\omega|_{U_{\infty}},0)]

and

\displaystyle p:H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})

\displaystyle[(\omega_{0},\omega_{\infty},f_{0\infty})]

\displaystyle\mapsto[f_{0\infty}].

From Proposition 4.1.2 of Tait’s dissertation
[
16
]
we get that the following short sequence is exact (the result is about hyperelliptic curves, but it can be extended with no changes in the proof in our setting):

0\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})\xrightarrow{i}H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)\xrightarrow{p}H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})\rightarrow 0,

hence the basis of the
k
-vector space
H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)
can be calculated from the bases of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
and
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
.

In order to write down an explicit
k
-basis for the first de Rham cohomology group, we will need the following notation: Say we have
h(x)\in k[x]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, then
h^{\leq m}(x)
(respectively
h^{>m}(x)
) is the sum of monomials of
h(x)
with degree
\leq m
(respectively
>m
).

Theorem 3
.

Let
I^{(\mu)}\subset\mathbb{N}
defined by

I^{(\mu)}:=\{1\leq i\leq r|\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}\neq 0\}.

Set

\psi_{\mu,\nu}(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(g_{i}\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}x\prod_{j\in I^{(\mu)}\setminus\{i\}}(x-\rho_{j})\right)-\nu n\prod_{i\in I^{(\mu)}}(x-\rho_{i})\in k[x].

Then the elements

\displaystyle a_{\mu,\nu}:=\left[\left(\frac{\psi_{\mu,\nu}^{\leq\nu+1}(x)}{nx^{\nu+1}}\frac{g_{n-\mu}(x)}{y^{n-\mu}}dx,\frac{\psi_{\mu,\nu}^{>\nu+1}(x)}{nx^{\nu+1}}\frac{g_{n-\mu}(x)}{y^{n-\mu}}dx,\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\text{ for }1\leq\mu\leq n-1\text{ s.t. }t^{(\mu)}\geq 2\text{ and }1\leq\nu\leq t^{(\mu)}-1

together with the elements

\displaystyle\delta_{\mu,\nu}:=\left[\left(\frac{x^{\nu-1}g_{\mu}(x)}{y^{\mu}}dx,\frac{x^{\nu-1}g_{\mu}(x)}{y^{\mu}}dx,0\right)\right]

\displaystyle\text{ for }1\leq\mu\leq n-1\text{ s.t. }t^{(\mu)}\geq 2\text{ and }1\leq\nu\leq t^{(\mu)}-1

form a basis for
H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)
as a
k
-vector space.

Proof.

It can be easily seen that the projections of the
a_{\mu,\nu}
’s form the basis of
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
and that the unique preimages of the
\delta_{\mu,\nu}
’s form the basis of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
. Therefore, if the
a_{\mu,\nu}
’s and
\delta_{\mu,\nu}
’s are well-defined elements of
H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)
, then they indeed form a
k
-basis for this space. The
\delta_{\mu,\nu}
’s are evidently well-defined, so we focus on the
a_{\mu,\nu}
’s.

First, we show that
\phi_{\mu}(x):=\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)}\right)^{n}
is a polynomial of
x
:

\displaystyle\phi_{\mu}(x)=\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{f(x)^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)^{n}}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{\mu l_{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{nm^{(\mu)}_{i}}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{\upsilon^{(\mu)}_{i}(n,l_{i})}.

Because we’ll need it later we write here a convenient expression for the formal derivative of
\log(\phi_{\mu}(x))
:

(5)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{\mu}(x)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{\upsilon^{(\mu)}_{i}(n,l_{i})}\right)^{\prime}=\phi_{\mu}(x)\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\upsilon^{(\mu)}_{i}(n,l_{i})}{x-\rho_{i}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\phi^{\prime}_{\mu}(x)}{\phi_{\mu}(x)}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\upsilon^{(\mu)}_{i}(n,l_{i})}{x-\rho_{i}}=\sum_{i\in I^{(\mu)}}\frac{\upsilon^{(\mu)}_{i}(n,l_{i})}{x-\rho_{i}}.

Now, since we cannot do much to calculate the differential of
\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}
, we’ll calculate the differential of its n-th power instead:

\displaystyle d\left(\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}\right)^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{\phi_{\mu}(x)}{x^{\nu n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi^{\prime}_{\mu}(x)x^{\nu n}-\phi_{\mu}(x)\nu nx^{\nu n-1}}{x^{2\nu n}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi^{\prime}_{\mu}(x)}{x^{\nu n}}dx-\frac{\phi_{\mu}(x)\nu n}{x^{\nu n+1}}dx.

Also

\displaystyle d\left(\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}\right)^{n}\right)=n\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}\right)^{n-1}d\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}\right)

hence

n\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}\right)^{n-1}d\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}\right)=\frac{\phi^{\prime}_{\mu}(x)}{x^{\nu n}}dx-\frac{\phi_{\mu}(x)\nu n}{x^{\nu n+1}}dx

which implies

\displaystyle d\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}{y^{\mu}}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}_{\mu}(x)}{x^{\nu n}}dx-\frac{\phi_{\mu}(x)\nu n}{x^{\nu n+1}}dx\right)

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)}\right)\left(\frac{1}{nx^{\nu+1}}\right)\left(\frac{x\phi^{\prime}_{\mu}(x)}{\phi_{\mu}(x)}-\nu n\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{y^{\mu}\cdot y^{n-\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)\cdot g_{n-\mu}(x)}\right)\left(\frac{1}{nx^{\nu+1}}\right)\left(\frac{x\phi^{\prime}_{\mu}(x)}{\phi_{\mu}(x)}-\nu n\right)\left(\frac{g_{n-\mu}(x)}{y^{n-\mu}}dx\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{nx^{\nu+1}}\left(\frac{f(x)}{g_{\mu}(x)\cdot g_{n-\mu}(x)}\right)\left(\frac{x\phi^{\prime}_{\mu}(x)}{\phi_{\mu}(x)}-\nu n\right)\omega_{n-\mu,1},

where
\omega_{n-\mu,1}\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
is a holomorphic differential as defined in eq. (
3
).

From the definition of
g_{\mu}(x)
in eq. (
1
), we know that:

g_{\mu}(x)\cdot g_{n-\mu}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{m_{i}^{(\mu)}+m_{i}^{(n-\mu)}}.

For the exponents appearing in the above expression we have that for
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
and
\mu\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
:

\displaystyle\mu\lambda_{i}=

\displaystyle m_{i}^{(\mu)}e_{i}+\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}

\displaystyle(n-\mu)\lambda_{i}=

\displaystyle m_{i}^{(n-\mu)}e_{i}+\upsilon_{i}^{(n-\mu)}

and adding these two equations we get (remember
\lambda_{i}=\frac{l_{i}}{(n,l_{i})}
and
e_{i}=\frac{n}{(n,l_{i})}
):

n\frac{l_{i}}{(n,l_{i})}=(m_{i}^{(\mu)}+m_{i}^{(n-\mu)})\frac{n}{(n,l_{i})}+(\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}+\upsilon_{i}^{(n-\mu)}),

with
\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)},\upsilon_{i}^{(n-\mu)}\in\{0,\dots,\frac{n}{(n,l_{i})}-1\}
. Taking the last equation
{\;\mathrm{mod}}e_{i}
gives that:

\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}+\upsilon_{i}^{(n-\mu)}\equiv 0{\;\mathrm{mod}}e_{i}.

Therefore:

\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}\equiv 0{\;\mathrm{mod}}e_{i}\Leftrightarrow\upsilon_{i}^{(n-\mu)}\equiv 0{\;\mathrm{mod}}e_{i}\Leftrightarrow\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}=0\Leftrightarrow\upsilon_{i}^{(n-\mu)}=0.

Otherwise
\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}+\upsilon_{i}^{(n-\mu)}=e_{i}
. We conclude that:

m_{i}^{(\mu)}+m_{i}^{(n-\mu)}=\begin{cases}l_{i}-1&\text{for }i\in I^{(\mu)}\\
l_{i}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

hence:

(7)

g_{\mu}(x)\cdot g_{n-\mu}(x)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}}}{\prod_{i\in I^{(\mu)}}(x-\rho_{i})}=\frac{f(x)}{\prod_{i\in I^{(\mu)}}(x-\rho_{i})}.

Going back to the calculation of
d\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}\right)
in eq. (
6
) using eq. (
7
) we have:

\displaystyle d\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{x^{\nu}g_{\mu}(x)}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{i\in I^{(\mu)}}(x-\rho_{i})\left(x\sum_{j\in I^{(\mu)}}\frac{\upsilon_{j}^{(\mu)}(n,l_{j})}{x-\rho_{j}}-\nu n\right)}{nx^{\nu+1}}\omega_{n-\mu,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{\mu,\nu}(x)}{nx^{\nu+1}}\omega_{n-\mu,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{\mu,\nu}^{\leq\nu+1}(x)}{nx^{\nu+1}}\omega_{n-\mu,1}-\frac{\psi_{\mu,\nu}^{>\nu+1}(x)}{nx^{\nu+1}}\omega_{n-\mu,1},

where of course the first summand is in
\Omega_{0}
, and the second summand is in
\Omega_{\infty}
, as we wanted.
∎

3.
Artin-Schreier extensions

3.1.
Computations

We consider the curve given as an Artin-Schreier extension of
k(x)
, where
k
is again an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p\geq 3
, given by an equation of the form

X:y^{p}-y=r(x)=\frac{f(x)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}}}.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
f(x)
is prime to the denominator of
r(x)
, that
(l_{i},p)=1
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, that
x\nmid f(x)
(i.e.
0
is not a root of
f(x)
), and that the points above
\infty
are neither poles nor roots for
y
(which means that
\deg f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}l_{i}=:l
). This means that
\infty
is not in the branch locus of the cover
X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
and the points above it are
P_{\infty,1},\ldots,P_{\infty,p}
. For any given Artin-Schreier curve, we can use Möbius transformations
of the base curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to make it satisfy the above conditions. The points of
X
lying above each
x=\rho_{i}
are
P_{i}
with ramification index
p
.

If
0\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
is in the branch locus, then
g_{0}:=1\text{ and }e_{0}:=p
. If
0
is not in the branch locus, then
g_{0}:=p\text{ and }e_{0}:=1
.

We collect several known facts for the divisors of functions and differentials, see
[
1
, sec. 3]

\displaystyle(x)

\displaystyle=e_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{g_{0}}P_{0,j}-\sum_{m=1}^{p}P_{\infty,m}

\displaystyle(y)

\displaystyle=(y)_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}l_{i}P_{i}=(f(x))_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}l_{i}P_{i},

where in the root divisor
(y)_{0}=(f(x))_{0}
of
y
, none of the
P_{i}
’s appears. Moreover

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=p\sum_{i=1}^{r}l_{i}P_{i}-l\sum_{m=1}^{p}P_{\infty,m}

\displaystyle(y^{p-1}-1)

\displaystyle=A-(p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{r}l_{i}P_{i}

and
A
is the root part of the divisor of
y^{p-1}-1
where the roots of
f(x)
appear.

We will also need the divisor of the differential
dx
:

(dx)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(p-1)(l_{i}+1)P_{i}-2\sum_{m=1}^{p}P_{\infty,m},

hence for
\mu
a positive integer we get

\left(y^{\mu}dx\right)=\mu(y)_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\big((p-1-\mu)l_{i}+(p-1)\big)P_{i}-2\sum_{m=1}^{p}P_{\infty,m}.

For each
\mu
,
\mu\in\{1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
, we define
m_{i}^{(\mu)}
and
\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}
as the quotient and the residue, respectively, of the following Euclidean division

(p-1-\mu)l_{i}+(p-1)=m_{i}^{(\mu)}p+\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)},\text{ with }0\leq\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}\leq p-1.

We set

g_{\mu}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{m_{i}^{(\mu)}}\text{ for }\mu\in\{1,2,\ldots,p-1\}

and we have that

(g_{\mu}(x))=p\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{(\mu)}P_{i}-t^{(\mu)}\sum_{m=1}^{p}P_{\infty,m},

where

t^{(\mu)}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{(\mu)}.

Now, we compute

\left(\frac{y^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)}dx\right)=\mu(y)_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\upsilon_{i}^{(\mu)}P_{i}+(t^{(\mu)}-2)\sum_{m=1}^{p}P_{\infty,m}.

Finally we will need

\displaystyle\left(g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-1}\right)=

\displaystyle p\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{(p-\mu)}P_{i}-t^{(p-\mu)}\sum_{m=1}^{p}P_{\infty,m}

(8)

\displaystyle+(\mu-1)(f(x))_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\mu-1)l_{i}P_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}(pm_{i}^{(p-\mu)}-(\mu-1)l_{i})P_{i}+(\mu-1)(f(x))_{0}-t^{(p-\mu)}\sum_{m=1}^{p}P_{\infty,m}

where
pm_{i}^{(p-\mu)}-(\mu-1)l_{i}=p-1-\upsilon_{i}^{(p-\mu)}\geq 0
.

3.2.
A
k
-vector space basis for
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})

As in the Kummer case,
we denote by
\pi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
a covering map from
X
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For every point
a\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
we define
U_{a}:=X\backslash\pi^{-1}(a)
. Observe that by the calculation (
3.1
) the elements

\frac{g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-1}}{x^{\nu}}\text{ for }1\leq\mu\leq p-1,\text{ s.t. }t^{(p-\mu)}\geq 2,1\leq\nu\leq t^{(p-\mu)}-1

have poles only above zero and infinity, i.e. they are in
\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{0}\cap U_{\infty})
.
By identifying the first sheaf cohomology group of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
with the corresponding first Čech cohomology group, then we get that

H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})=\frac{\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{0}\cap U_{\infty})}{\{f_{0}-f_{\infty}:f_{0}\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{0}),f_{\infty}\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{\infty})\}}.

From Serre duality, this
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
seen as a
k
-vector space, is dual to the
k
-vector space
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
. From the calculations of the previous paragraph and from
[
1
]
we can see that a basis of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
is given by the differentials

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu,\nu}:=x^{\nu-1}\frac{{y}^{\mu}}{{g_{\mu}(x)}}dx,1\leq\mu\leq p-1,\text{ s.t. }t^{(\mu)}\geq 2,1\leq\nu\leq t^{(\mu)}-1.

Also, the following linear equivalence classes

\displaystyle h_{\mu,\nu}:=\left[\frac{g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-1}}{x^{\nu}}\right]\text{ with }1\leq\mu\leq p-1,\text{ s.t. }t^{(p-\mu)}\geq 2,1\leq\nu\leq t^{(p-\mu)}-1.

are well defined, linearly independent elements of
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
. Since they are in bijective correspondence with the elements
\omega_{\mu,\nu}
, we know that they form a basis (the
k
-vector space dimension of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
is equal to the
k
-vector space dimension of
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
).

Remark
.

Note that the condition
t^{(\mu)}\geq 2
means that
\mu<p-1
, because for
\mu=p-1
we have
m_{i}^{(\mu)}=0
for all
i
. For the same reason the condition
t^{(p-\mu)}\geq 2
means that
\mu>1
.

As in the section of the Kummer case, using the map

\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})\times H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow k

\displaystyle\langle\omega,[f]\rangle

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{P\in\pi^{-1}(\infty)}\mathrm{Res}_{P}(f\omega)

we will show that:

Proposition 4
.

The bases of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
and
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
that we have written above are dual to each other.

Proof.

We calculate the pairing between elements of the above given bases:

\displaystyle\langle\omega_{\mu_{1},\nu_{1}},h_{\mu_{2},\nu_{2}}\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\pi^{-1}(\infty)}\mathrm{Res}_{P}\left(\frac{g_{p-\mu_{2}}(x)y^{\mu_{2}-1}}{x^{\nu_{2}}}\frac{x^{\nu_{1}-1}y^{\mu_{1}}}{g_{\mu_{1}}(x)}dx\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\pi^{-1}(\infty)}\mathrm{Res}_{P}\left(\frac{y^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-1}g_{p-\mu_{2}}(x)x^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}}{g_{\mu_{1}}(x)}\frac{dx}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left(\text{Tr}_{F/E}\left(y^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-1}\right)\frac{g_{p-\mu_{2}}(x)x^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}}{g_{\mu_{1}}(x)}\frac{dx}{x}\right)

We have to calculate the trace
y^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-1}
for the various values of
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
. Notice that
1\leq\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-1\leq 2(p-1)-1
, so we will write the trace of
y^{k}
for
k\in\{1,\dots,2(p-1)-1\}
:

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{F/E}(y^{k})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(y+i)^{k}=y^{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}(y+i)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y^{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}y^{k-j}i^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y^{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}y^{k-j}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}i^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y^{k}+y^{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}i^{0}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}y^{k-j}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}i^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}y^{k-j}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}i^{j}

since
\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}i^{0}=p-1=-1
in characteristic
p
, and this cancels out with the first
y^{k}
.

Let’s focus on the inner sum of the above expression: For
j\in\{1,\dots,p-2,p,\dots,2(p-1)-1\}
, we have:

\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}i^{j}=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}t^{j}=\sum_{l=0}^{p-2}(g^{l})^{j}

where
g
is a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. Since
j\in\{1,\dots,p-2,p,\dots,2(p-1)-1\}
we have
g^{j}\neq 1
, and thus

\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}i^{j}=\sum_{l=0}^{p-2}(g^{j})^{l}=\frac{1-(g^{j})^{p-1}}{1-g^{j}}=\frac{1-(g^{p-1})^{j}}{1-g^{j}}=0

where the last sum is the sum of terms of a geometric progression, and of course
g^{p-1}=1
for
g\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
.

On the other hand for
j=p-1
:

\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}i^{j}=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}}t^{p-1}=p-1=-1\text{ in characteristic }p.

However for
k\geq p
, the binomial coefficient
\binom{k}{p-1}
is divisible by
p
, hence it is zero in characteristic
p
.

From all the above we conclude that:

\text{Tr}_{F/E}(y^{k})=\begin{cases}-1&\text{for }k=p-1\\
0&\text{for }k\in\{1,\dots,p-2,p,\dots,2(p-1)-1\}.\end{cases}

Returning to the calculation of the pairing, we see that the result is non-zero only for
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-1=p-1
, i.e. for
\mu_{1}=p-\mu_{2}
. In that case:

\displaystyle\langle\omega_{p-\mu_{2},\nu_{1}},h_{\mu_{2},\nu_{2}}\rangle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left((-1)\frac{g_{p-\mu_{2}}(x)x^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}}{g_{p-\mu_{2}}(x)}\frac{dx}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^{\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}}\frac{d\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)}\right).

From the properties of the residues, the last residue from above is non-zero if and only if
\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}
, in which case it is equal to
1
, and this completes our proof.
∎

3.3.
A
k
-vector space basis for
H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)

Similarly to the case of a Kummer extension we can compute
H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)
as follows:

\frac{\{(\omega_{0},\omega_{\infty},f_{0\infty})\in\Omega_{X}(U_{0})\times\Omega_{X}(U_{\infty})\times\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{0}\cap U_{\infty})\mid df_{0\infty}=\omega_{0}|_{U_{0}\cap U_{\infty}}-\omega_{\infty}|_{U_{0}\cap U_{\infty}}\}}{\{(df_{0},df_{\infty},f_{0}|_{U_{0}\cap U_{\infty}}-f_{\infty}|_{U_{0}\cap U_{\infty}})\mid f_{0}\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{0}),f_{\infty}\in\mathscr{O}_{X}(U_{\infty})\}}.

Again, we have canonical maps

\displaystyle i:H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\mapsto[(\omega|_{U_{0}},\omega|_{U_{\infty}},0)]

and

\displaystyle p:H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})

\displaystyle[(\omega_{0},\omega_{\infty},f_{0\infty})]

\displaystyle\mapsto[f_{0\infty}].

Same as in the previous section, from Proposition 4.1.2 of Tait’s dissertation (
[
16
]
) we get that the following short sequence is exact:

0\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})\xrightarrow{i}H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)\xrightarrow{p}H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})\rightarrow 0

hence the basis of the
k
-vector space
H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)
can be calculated from the bases of
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
and
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
.

Theorem 5
.

Let

\varphi_{\mu,\nu}(x):=xg_{p-\mu}^{\prime}(x)g_{\mu-1}(x)-\nu g_{p-\mu}(x)g_{\mu-1}(x)\in k[x]

for
1\leq\mu\leq p-1
s.t.
t^{(p-\mu)}\geq 2
and
1\leq\nu\leq t^{(p-\mu)}-1
. Also let

\psi(x):=f(x)\sum_{i=1}^{r}l_{i}\left(\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{r}(x-\rho_{j})\right)-f^{\prime}(x)\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})\in k[x].

Let

\omega_{\mu}:=\frac{(\mu-1)g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}+1}}dx

for
1\leq\mu\leq p-1\text{ s.t. }t^{(p-\mu)}\geq 2
. Let us denote

\tilde{h}_{\mu,\nu}:=\frac{g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-1}}{x^{\nu}}.

The elements

\displaystyle a_{\mu,\nu}:=\left[\left(\frac{\varphi_{\mu,\nu}^{<\nu+1}(x)}{x^{\nu+1}}\omega_{\mu-1,1}+\frac{\psi^{<\nu}(x)}{x^{\nu}}\omega_{\mu},\frac{\varphi_{\mu,\nu}^{\geq\nu+1}(x)}{x^{\nu+1}}\omega_{\mu-1,1}+\frac{\psi^{\geq\nu}(x)}{x^{\nu}}\omega_{\mu},\tilde{h}_{\mu,\nu}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\text{ for }1\leq\mu\leq p-1\text{ s.t. }t^{(p-\mu)}\geq 2\text{ and }1\leq\nu\leq t^{(p-\mu)}-1

together with the elements

\displaystyle\delta_{\mu,\nu}:=\left[\left(x^{\nu-1}\frac{y^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)}dx,x^{\nu-1}\frac{y^{\mu}}{g_{\mu}(x)}dx,0\right)\right]

\displaystyle\text{ for }1\leq\mu\leq p-1\text{ s.t. }t^{(\mu)}\geq 2\text{ and }1\leq\nu\leq t^{(\mu)}-1

form a basis for
H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)
as a
k
-vector space.

Proof.

Similarly to the corresponding theorem about Kummer extensions, we just need to show that the
a_{\mu,\nu}
’s are well-defined elements of
H^{1}_{dR}(X/k)
.

First we write down the differential of
\tilde{h}_{\mu,\nu}
:

\displaystyle d\left(\frac{g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-1}}{x^{\nu}}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{(g_{p-\mu}^{\prime}(x)y^{\mu-1}\!-\nu g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-1}x^{-1})dx\!+(\mu-1)g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-2}dy}{x^{\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{xg_{p-\mu}^{\prime}(x)g_{\mu-1}(x)-\nu g_{p-\mu}(x)g_{\mu-1}(x)}{x^{\nu+1}}\left(\frac{y^{\mu-1}}{g_{\mu-1}(x)}dx\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{(\mu-1)g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-2}dy}{x^{\nu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi_{\mu,\nu}(x)}{x^{\nu+1}}\omega_{\mu-1,1}+\frac{(\mu-1)g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-2}}{x^{\nu}}dy.

For the differential of
y
we can use the defining equation of our curve, since we are in characteristic
p
:

\displaystyle d(y^{p}-y)=

\displaystyle d\left(\frac{f(x)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}}}\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow-dy=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime}(x)-f(x)\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{l_{i}}{x-\rho_{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow dy=

\displaystyle\frac{f(x)\sum_{i=1}^{r}l_{i}\left(\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{r}(x-\rho_{j})\right)-f^{\prime}(x)\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow dy=

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(x)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}+1}}.

Thus, we end up with:

d\left(\frac{g_{p-\mu}(x)y^{\mu-1}}{x^{\nu}}\right)=\frac{\varphi_{\mu,\nu}(x)}{x^{\nu+1}}\omega_{\mu-1,1}+\frac{\psi(x)}{x^{\nu}}\omega_{\mu}.

We will show that for all
\mu\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
s.t.
t^{(p-\mu)}\geq 2
,
\omega_{\mu}
is either holomorphic or has pole of order one at each point above
\infty
. Then it’s easily seen that the
a_{\mu,\nu}
’s are well-defined, so the proof will be over.

Notice that
\omega_{\mu}
can have poles only at the points above
\infty
(we’ll show that when that happens the order of the pole is one) or at the
P_{i}
’s (we’ll show that this never happens), since
f(x)
never appears in the denominator - remember that
\mu\geq 2
from the remark we made after writing the basis of
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
above.

Firstly, let
P_{\infty,m}
be any point above
\infty
. Then:

\displaystyle v_{P_{\infty,m}}(\omega_{\mu})=

\displaystyle v_{P_{\infty,m}}(g_{p-\mu}(x))+v_{P_{\infty,m}}(dx)+v_{P_{\infty,m}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{(p-\mu)}-2+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(l_{i}+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(l_{i}-m_{i}^{(p-\mu)}+1),

where for all
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}

(p-1)(l_{i}+1)-(p-\mu)l_{i}=pm_{i}^{(p-\mu)}+\upsilon_{i}^{(p-\mu)},

which leads to

(p-1)(l_{i}-m_{i}^{(p-\mu)}+1)=(p-\mu)l_{i}+m_{i}^{(p-\mu)}+\upsilon_{i}^{(p-\mu)}.

The right hand side of the last equation is strictly positive, so the same is true for the left hand side, and more specifically

l_{i}-m_{i}^{(p-\mu)}+1>0,\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\dots,r\}.

Therefore if
r>1
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{r}(l_{i}-m_{i}^{(p-\mu)}+1)\geq 2
and
\omega_{\mu}
has no pole at any point above
\infty
. If
r=1
, then
l_{1}-m_{1}^{(p-\mu)}+1\geq 1
and
\omega_{\mu}
is either holomorphic (if
l_{1}>m_{1}^{(p-\mu)}
) or has a pole at each
P_{\infty,m}
of order one (if
l_{1}=m_{1}^{(p-\mu)}
; for example
p=7,\mu=6,l_{1}=2
give
m_{1}^{(1)}=2=l_{1}
).

Now let’s check whether
\omega_{\mu}
has pole at any
P_{i}
:

\displaystyle v_{P_{i}}(\omega_{\mu})=

\displaystyle v_{P_{i}}(g_{p-\mu}(x))+v_{P_{i}}(y^{\mu-2})+v_{P_{i}}(dx)-v_{P_{i}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-\rho_{i})^{l_{i}+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle pm_{i}^{(p-\mu)}-(\mu-2)l_{i}+(p-1)(l_{i}+1)-p(l_{i}+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu-1)l_{i}+p-1-\upsilon_{i}^{(p-\mu)}-(\mu-2)l_{i}-l_{i}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p-2-\upsilon_{i}^{(p-\mu)}.

If
\upsilon_{i}^{(p-\mu)}=p-1
, then
(\mu-1)l_{i}\equiv 0{\;\mathrm{mod}}p
with
(p,l_{i})=1
by assumption, so
\mu=1
, but that’s not allowed from the remark we made after writing the basis for
H^{1}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X})
. Hence
\upsilon_{i}^{(p-\mu)}\leq p-2
, which implies that
v_{P_{i}}(\omega_{\mu})\geq 0
, as we wanted.
∎

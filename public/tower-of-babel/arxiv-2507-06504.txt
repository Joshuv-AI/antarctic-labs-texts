Huanqing Dong, Jingtao Shi

Abstract




Abstract:
This paper is concerned with the relationship between maximum principle and dynamic programming principle for risk-sensitive stochastic optimal control problems. Under the smooth assumption of the value function, relations among the adjoint processes, the generalized Hamiltonian function, and the value function are given. As an application, a linear-quadratic risk-sensitive portfolio optimization problem in the financial market is discussed.

Keywords:
Risk-sensitive stochastic optimal control, maximum principle, dynamic programming principle, backward stochastic differential equation

Mathematics Subject Classification:
93E20, 60H10, 49N10, 35K15

Introduction

It is well-known that Pontryagin’s
maximum principle
(MP) and Bellman’s
dynamic programming principle
(DPP) are two important tools for solving optimal control problems. So a natural question is: Are there any connections between these two methods? The relationship between MP and DPP is studied in many literatures. For the deterministic optimal control problems, the results were discussed by Fleming and Rishel
[
8
]
, Barron and Jensen
[
1
]
and Zhou
[
37
]
. Bensoussan
[
3
]
(see also
[
35
]
) obtained the relationship between MP and DPP for stochastic optimal control problems. Zhou
[
38
]
generalized their relationship using the viscosity solution theory (see also
[
39
,
35
]
), without the assumption that the value function is smooth.

In this paper, we consider the relationship between MP and DPP for a risk-sensitive stochastic optimal control problem, where the system is given by the following controlled
stochastic differential equation
(SDE):

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(t)&=f(t,X(t),u(t))dt+\sigma(t,X(t),u(t))dW(t),\\
X(0)&=x,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

and the cost/objective functional is defined by

J(u(\cdot))=\mu^{-1}\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{\mu\int_{0}^{T}l(t,X(t),u(t))dt+\mu g(X(T))\right\}\right].

(1.2)

Risk-sensitive optimal control problems has attracted many researchers since the early work of Jacobson
[
14
]
. On the one hand, this problem can be reduced to the risk-neutral case under the limit of the risk-sensitive parameter. On the other hand, it has close connection with differential games
[
15
,
19
]
, robust control theory
[
19
,
23
]
, and can be applied to describe the risk attitude of an investor by the risk-sensitive parameter in mathematical finance
[
2
,
10
,
18
]
.

Whittle
[
33
,
34
]
first derived Pontryagin’s MP based on the theory of large deviation for a risk-sensitive stochastic optimal control problem. A new risk-sensitive maximum principle was established by Lim and Zhou
[
20
]
under the condition that the value function is smooth, based on the general stochastic MP of Peng
[
28
]
and the relationship between MP and DPP of Yong and Zhou
[
35
]
. This idea is used in Shi and Wu
[
30
,
31
]
for studying jump processes case and mathematical finance. Djehiche et al.
[
6
]
extends the results of
[
20
]
to risk-sensitive control problems for dynamics that are non-Markovian and mean-field type. Further developments on risk-sensitive stochastic control problems in a diverse set of settings, see Moon et al.
[
24
]
, Chala
[
4
,
5
]
, Khallout and Chala
[
17
]
, Moon
[
25
]
, Lin and Shi
[
21
,
22
]
and the references therein.

In El Karoui and Hamedene
[
7
]
, the risk-sensitive objective functional is characterized by a
backward SDE
(BSDE) with quadratic growth coefficient that does not necessarily satisfy the usual Lipschitz condition. Consider the following BSDE with quadratic growth coefficient:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dY(t)&=-\left[l(t,X(t),Y(t),Z(t),u(t))+\frac{\mu}{2}|Z(t)|^{2}\right]dt+Z(t)dW(t),\\
Y(T)&=g(X(T)).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

By the DPP approach, Moon
[
26
]
extended the results of classical risk-sensitive optimal control problem studied in
[
9
]
of the objective functional given by the BSDE like (1.3). They showed that the value function is the unique viscosity solution of the corresponding Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation under an additional risk-sensitive condition. When
l
is independent of
y
and
z
, it is easy to check that
Y(0)=\mu^{-1}\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{\mu\int_{0}^{T}l(t,X(t),u(t))\,dt+\mu g(X(T))\right\}\right]
. Thus, the risk-sensitive optimal control problem of minimizing
J(u(\cdot))
in (1.2) subject to (1.1) is equivalent to minimizing
Y(0)
subject to (1.1) and (1.3). In this case, the controlled system becomes a decoupled
forward-backward SDE
(FBSDE), with the objective functional of a BSDE with quadratic growth coefficient.

For the relationship between MP and DPP for optimal control problems of decoupled FBSDEs/recursice utilities, Shi and Yu
[
29
]
investigated the local case in which the control domain is convex and the value function is smooth. Nie et al.
[
27
]
studied the general case by the second-order adjoint equation of Hu
[
11
]
, within the framework of viscosity solution. Hu et al.
[
12
]
studied the relationship between general MP and DPP for the fully coupled forward-backward stochastic controlled system within the framework of viscosity solutions. Note that all the aforementioned references considered stochastic optimal control problems for BSDE objective functionals with linear growth coefficients satisfying Lipschitz conditions. For the relationship between MP and DPP for optimal control problems of decoupled FBSDEs and BSDE objective functionals with quadratic growth coefficients, very recently, Wu et al.
[
32
]
established the connection between MP and DPP for the risk-sensitive recursive utility singular control problem under the assumption of smooth value function, where the cost functional is given by a BSDE with quadratic growth, driven by a discontinuous semimartingale.

Inspired by the above works, in this paper, we will derive the relationship between MP and DPP for risk-sensitive stochastic optimal control problems. For this problem, because the cost functional is given by (
1.3
), we wish to connect the MP of Moon et al.
[
24
]
under certain conditions. Moreover, since the cost functional (1.2) can be defined by the controlled BSDE with quadratic growth coefficient, we wish to apply the DPP of Moon
[
26
]
under certain conditions. Specifically, when the value function is smooth, we give relations among the adjoint processes, the generalized Hamiltonian function, and the value function.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we state our risk-sensitive stochastic optimal control problem and give some preliminaries. In Section 3, we show the relationship between MP and DPP for the problem, by the martingale representation technique. In Section 4, we apply the theoretical results to a portfolio optimization problem in a factor model setting. Specifically, we obtain the state feedback optimal control by the risk-sensitive MP and the DPP, and the relations we obtained are illustrated explicitly. Finally, in Section 5, we give the concluding remarks.

2
Problem statement and preliminaries

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P})
be a complete probability space equipped with a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion
\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. For fixed
t\geq 0
, the natural filtration
\{\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}\}_{s\geq t}
is generated as
\mathcal{F}_{s}=\sigma\{W_{r}-W_{t};t\leq r\leq s\}\bigvee\mathcal{N}
, where
\mathcal{N}
contains all
\mathbf{P}
-null sets in
\mathcal{F}
. In particular, if
t=0
, we write
\mathcal{F}_{s}=\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}
. For
t\geq 0
, we denote by
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
the the space of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued
\{\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}\}_{s\geq t}
-adapted processes on
[t,T]
with
\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|x(s)|^{2}ds<\infty
, and by
\mathcal{S}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued
\{\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}\}_{s\geq t}
-adapted processes on
[t,T]
with
\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|x(s)|^{2}\big]<\infty
.

Let
T>0
be finite and let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a compact metric space. For any initial time and state
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, consider the state
X^{t,x;u}_{\cdot}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by the following controlled
\mathrm{SDE}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX^{t,x;u}_{s}&=f(s,X^{t,x;u}_{s},u_{s})ds+\sigma(s,X^{t,x;u}_{s},u_{s})dW_{s},\\
X^{t,x;u}_{t}&=x.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

Here
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times U\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are given functions.

Given
t\in[0,T)
, the set of admissible controls are defined as follows:

\mathcal{U}[t,T]:=\left\{u:[0,T]\times\Omega\to U|u_{\cdot}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;U)\right\}.

For
u_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{U}[t,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued process
X^{t,x;u}_{\cdot}
is called a solution to (
2.1
) if it is an
\{\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}\}_{s\geq t}
-adapted process such that (
2.1
) hold. We refer to such
(u_{\cdot},X^{t,x;u}_{\cdot})
as an admissible pair.

We make the following assumption.

(\mathbf{H1})

f,\sigma
are uniformly continuous in
s
, and there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
s\in[0,T]
,
x,\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},u,\hat{u}\in U
,

\begin{cases}|f(s,x,u)-f(s,\hat{x},\hat{u})|+|\sigma(s,x,u)-\sigma(s,\hat{x},\hat{u})|\leq C(|x-\hat{x}|+|u-\hat{u}|),\\
|f(s,0,u)|\leq C.\end{cases}

(2.2)

For any
u_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{U}[t,T]
, under (H1), the equation (
2.1
) has a unique solution
X^{t,x;u}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{S}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
by the classical
\mathrm{SDE}
theory.
We consider the following cost functional with initial condition
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{U}[t,T]
:

J(t,x;u_{\cdot})=\mu^{-1}\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{\mu\int_{t}^{T}l(s,X^{t,x;u}_{s},u_{s})ds+\mu g(X^{t,x;u}_{T})\right\}\right],

(2.3)

where
l:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times U\to\mathbb{R}
,
g:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\mu>0
is the risk-sensitive parameter that captures robustness (Fleming and Soner
[
9
]
). Let
J_{1}\equiv\int_{0}^{T}l(s,X_{s}^{0,x;u},u_{s})ds+g(X_{T}^{0,x;u})
. When the risk-sensitive parameter
\mu
is small, the cost functional (
2.3
) can be expanded as

\mathbb{E}[J_{1}]+\frac{\mu}{2}\operatorname{Var}(J_{1})+O(\mu^{2}),

where
\operatorname{Var}(J_{1})
denote the variance of
J_{1}
. If
\mu<0
, which implies that the controller is risk-seeking from an economic point of view. When
\mu\to 0
, it is reduced to risk-neutral case.

Our risk-sensitive stochastic optimal control problem is the following.

Problem (SR)
. For given
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, to minimize (
2.3
) subject to (
2.1
) over
\mathcal{U}[t,T]
.

From
[
26
]
(see also
[
7
]
), we note that minimizing (
2.3
) in Problem (SR) is equivalent to minimizing the following cost functional

J(t,x;u_{\cdot})=Y^{t,x;u}_{t},

(2.4)

where
Y_{\cdot}^{t,x;u}
is the first component of the solution pair
(Y^{t,x;u}_{\cdot},Z^{t,x;u}_{\cdot})
to the scalar-valued BSDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dY^{t,x;u}_{s}&=-\left[l(s,X^{t,x;u}_{s},u_{s})+\frac{\mu}{2}|Z^{t,x;u}_{s}|^{2}\right]ds+Z^{t,x;u}_{s}dW_{s},\\
Y^{t,x;u}_{T}&=g(X^{t,x;u}_{T}).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

In fact, let
\widehat{Y}^{t,x;u}_{s}:=\exp\big\{\mu Y^{t,x;u}_{s}\big\}
, and note that
Y^{t,x;u}_{s}=\mu^{-1}\log\widehat{Y}^{t,x;u}_{s}
. Applying Itô’s formula to
\widehat{Y}^{t,x;u}_{\cdot}
, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\widehat{Y}^{t,x;u}_{s}&=\mu\widehat{Y}^{t,x;u}_{s}\left[-l(s,X^{t,x;u}_{s},u_{s})ds+Z^{t,x;u}_{s}dW_{s}\right],\\
\widehat{Y}^{t,x;u}_{T}&=\exp\big\{\mu g(X^{t,x;u}_{T})\big\}.\end{aligned}\right.

This is a linear BSDE, its explicit solution can be obtained:

\widehat{Y}^{t,x;u}_{s}=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{\mu\int_{t}^{T}l(s,X^{t,x;u}_{s},u_{s})ds+\mu g(X^{t,x;u}_{T})\right\}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

Hence

\displaystyle J(0,x;u_{\cdot})

\displaystyle=Y_{0}^{0,x;u}=\mu^{-1}\log\widehat{Y}_{0}^{0,x;u}

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\mu^{-1}\log\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{\mu\int_{0}^{T}l(s,X_{s}^{0,x;u},u_{s})ds+\mu g(X_{T}^{0,x;u})\right\}\right].

Note that (
2.6
) corresponds to the classical risk-sensitive optimal control problem (see
[
9
]
,
[
24
]
).

We introduce the following assumption.

(\mathbf{H2})

l
is uniformly continuous in
s
,
g
is uniformly continuous in
x
and bounded. For
\phi=l,g
, there exists a constant
L>0
, such that for any
t\in[0,T],x,\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
u,\hat{u}\in U
,

\begin{cases}|\phi(s,x,u)-\phi(s,\hat{x},\hat{u})|\leq L(|x-\hat{x}|+|u-\hat{u}|),\\
|\phi(s,0,u)|\leq L.\end{cases}

(2.7)

Note that (H2) implies
|l(s,x,u)+\frac{\mu}{2}|z|^{2}|\leq L(1+|x|+|z|^{2})
.
Thus (
2.5
) can be viewed as a controlled BSDE with quadratic growth coefficient studied in
[
16
,
7
,
36
]
. For any
u_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{U}[t,T]
and given unique solution
X_{\cdot}^{t,x,u}
to (2.1), under (H2), BSDE (
2.5
) admits a unique solution
(Y_{\cdot}^{t,x,u},Z_{\cdot}^{t,x,u})\in\mathcal{S}(t,T;\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{1\times d})
(see
[
26
,
7
,
36
]
).

We define the value function as

V(t,x):=\inf\limits_{u_{\cdot}\in\,\mathcal{U}[t,T]}J(t,x;u_{\cdot}),\quad(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.8)

Any
\bar{u}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{U}[t,T]
satisfied (
2.8
) is called an optimal control, and the corresponding solution
\bar{X}_{\cdot}^{t,x;\bar{u}}
to (
2.1
) is called optimal state trajectory.

Remark 2.1
.

From
[
26
]
, we know that under (H1) and (H2), the above value function is a deterministic function, so our definition (
2.8
) is meaningful.

In the following, we introduce the risk-sensitive DPP and MP approaches for
\mathbf{Problem(SR)}
in the literatures, respectively. Moon
[
26
]
studied a generalized risk-sensitive optimal control problem, where the objective functional is defined by a controlled BSDE with quadratic growth coefficient. A generalized risk-sensitive DPP was obtained. And the corresponding value function is a viscosity solution to the corresponding Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation. Under some additional parameter conditions, the viscosity solution is unique. When
l
is independent of
y
and
z
in (3) of Moon
[
26
]
, the BSDE in (3) is reduced to the BSDE in (
2.5
). Thus we can state the HJB equation for our Problem (SR):

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}(t,x)+\inf\limits_{u\in U}G\left(t,x,u,\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}(t,x),\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)\right)=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
V(T,x)=g(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

(2.9)

where the generalized Hamiltonian function
G:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\displaystyle G(t,x,u,p,P)

\displaystyle:=l(t,x,u)+\langle p,f(t,x,u)\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\big|\sigma(t,x,u)^{\top}p\big|^{2}

(2.10)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}\big(\sigma(t,x,u)\sigma(t,x,u)^{\top}P\big).

The following result can be inferred by their Theorem 2 of
[
26
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let (H1), (H2) hold and
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose
V(\cdot,\cdot)\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
V(\cdot,\cdot)
is a solution to the HJB equation in (
2.9
).

Moon et al.
[
24
]
studied a two-player risk-sensitive zero-sum differential game, where the drift and diffusion terms in the controlled state SDE are dependent on the state and controls of both players, and the objective functional is the risk-sensitive type. A stochastic MP type necessary condition was established (their Theorem 1 in
[
24
]
). If we consider single-player case, that is, let
f
is independent of
v
in (1) of
[
24
]
, so that the state equation is consistent with (
2.1
). Moreover, let
\frac{1}{\delta}=\mu
and
l
is independent of
v
in (4) of
[
24
]
so that the cost functional is consistent with (
2.3
). Thus we can obtain the MP of our Problem (SR).

First, we need introduce the following assumption.

(\mathbf{H3})

f,\sigma,l,g
are twice continuously differentiable with respect to
x
. Their first and second-order partial derivatives in
x
are bounded, Lipschitz continuous in
(x,u)
with a constant
L
, and bounded by
L(1+|x|+|u|)
.

Let
\bar{u}_{\cdot}
be an optimal control, and
\bar{X}_{\cdot}^{t,x;\bar{u}}
is corresponding optimal state trajectory. For any
s\in[0,T]
, we denote

\bar{f}(s):=f(s,\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}},\bar{u}_{s}),\quad\bar{\sigma}(s):=\sigma(s,\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}},\bar{u}_{s}),\quad\bar{l}(s):=l(s,\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}},\bar{u}_{s}),

and similar notations used for all their derivatives. We also introduce the following notations:

\sigma(s,x,u)=(\sigma_{1}(s,x,u),\cdots,\sigma_{d}(s,x,u)),

where
\sigma_{j}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
j=1,\cdots,d
. Let
\frac{\partial\sigma_{j}}{\partial x}
be the partial derivative of
\sigma_{j}
with respect to
x
, which is an
n\times n
dimensional matrix.

For given
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, since the value function is smooth, we introduce the following first-order adjoint process
(p_{\cdot},q_{\cdot})\equiv(p_{\cdot},q_{1\cdot},\cdots,q_{d\cdot})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdots,\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} dp_{s}&=-\bigg[\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x}(s)^{\top}p_{s}+\frac{\partial\bar{l}}{\partial x}(s)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial\bar{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial x}(s)^{\top}q_{js}+\mu\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{\sigma}_{j}(s)^{\top}p_{s}\frac{\partial\bar{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial x}(s)^{\top}p_{s}\\
&\qquad+\mu\sum_{j=1}^{d}q_{js}\bar{\sigma}_{j}(s)^{\top}p_{s}\bigg]ds+q_{s}dW_{s},\\
p_{T}&=\frac{\partial\bar{g}}{\partial x}(\bar{X}_{T}^{t,x;\bar{u}}),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.11)

and the second-order adjoint process
(P_{\cdot},Q_{\cdot})\equiv(P_{\cdot},Q_{1\cdot},\cdots,Q_{d\cdot})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;\mathcal{S}^{n})\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;\mathcal{S}^{n},\\
\cdots,\mathcal{S}^{n})
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} dP_{s}&=-\bigg[\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x}(s)^{\top}P_{s}+P_{s}\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x}(s)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial\bar{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial x}(s)^{\top}\left(P_{s}+\mu p_{s}p_{s}^{\top}\right)\frac{\partial\bar{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial x}(s)\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial\bar{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial x}(s)^{\top}\left(Q_{js}+\mu\bar{\sigma}_{j}(s)^{\top}p_{s}P_{s}+\mu p_{s}q_{js}^{\top}\right)\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(Q_{js}+\mu\bar{\sigma}_{j}(s)^{\top}p_{s}P_{s}+\mu q_{js}p_{s}^{\top}\right)\frac{\partial\bar{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial x}(s)\\
&\quad+\mu\sum_{j=1}^{d}q_{js}q_{js}^{\top}+\frac{\partial^{2}\bar{H}}{\partial x^{2}}(s)+\mu\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{\sigma}_{js}^{\top}p_{s}Q_{js}\bigg]ds+\sum_{j=1}^{d}Q_{js}dW_{js},\\
P_{T}&=\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial x^{2}}(\bar{X}_{T}^{t,x;\bar{u}}),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.12)

where
\bar{H}(s):=H(s,\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}},\bar{u}_{s},p_{s},q_{s})
with the Hamiltonian function
H
defined by

\displaystyle H(s,x,u,p,q)

\displaystyle:=\langle p,f(s,x,u)\rangle+l(s,x,u)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}q_{j}^{\top}\sigma_{j}(s,x,u)

(2.13)

\displaystyle+\mu\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{j}(s,x,u)^{\top}p\bar{\sigma}_{j}(s)^{\top}p.

Then, we have the following MP for our Problem (SR).

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that (H1)-(H3) hold and
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be fixed. Let
(\bar{u}_{\cdot},\bar{X}_{\cdot}^{t,x;\bar{u}})
be an optimal pair for our Problem (SR). Then, there exist unique solutions

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(p_{\cdot},q_{1\cdot},\cdots,q_{d\cdot})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n},\cdots,\mathbb{R}^{n}),\\
&(P_{\cdot},Q_{1\cdot},\cdots,Q_{d\cdot})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;\mathcal{S}^{n})\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(t,T;\mathcal{S}^{n},\cdots,\mathcal{S}^{n}),\end{aligned}\right.

to the first-order adjoint equation (
2.11
) and the second-order adjoint equation (
2.12
), respectively, such that the following variational inequality hold:

\displaystyle H(s,\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}},u,p_{s},q_{s})-H(s,\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}},\bar{u}_{s},p_{s},q_{s})

(2.14)

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x}(s)^{\top}\tilde{P}_{s}+\tilde{P}_{s}\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial x}(s)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial\bar{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial x}(s)^{\top}\left(\tilde{P}_{s}+\mu p_{s}p_{s}^{\top}\right)\frac{\partial\bar{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial x}(s)+\mu\sum_{j=1}^{d}q_{js}q_{js}^{\top}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial\bar{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial x}(s)^{\top}\left(\tilde{Q}_{js}+\mu\tilde{P}_{s}\bar{\sigma}_{j}(s)p_{s}^{\top}+\mu p_{s}q_{js}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\tilde{Q}_{js}+\mu\tilde{P}_{s}\bar{\sigma}_{j}(s)p_{s}^{\top}+\mu q_{js}p_{s}^{\top}\right)\frac{\partial\bar{\sigma}_{j}}{\partial x}(s)+\frac{\partial^{2}\bar{H}}{\partial x^{2}}(s)+\mu\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{Q}_{js}\bar{\sigma}_{j}(s)^{\top}p_{s}.

Note that
P_{T}=\tilde{P}_{T}=\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}(T,\bar{X}_{T}^{t,x;\bar{u}})=\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial x^{2}}(\bar{X}_{T}^{t,x;\bar{u}})
. Using the above relation, one can check that the condition of the comparison theorem (see, Hu and Peng
[
13
]
) for the matrix-valued BSDEs (
2.12
) and (
3.17
) holds, and thus we obtain (
3.3
). The proof is complete.
∎

4
Applications to financial portfolio optimization

In this section, we consider a risk-sensitive portfolio optimization problem for a factor model (see
[
2
,
18
]
). In this problem, the optimal portfolio in the state feedback form is obtained by both MP and DPP approaches, and the relations in Theorem
3.1
are illustrated explicitly.

We make a slight modification to the factor model studied by Kuroda and Nagai
[
18
]
, considering a market with two kinds of securities and a factor.

(i) A risk-free security (e.g., a bond), where the price
R_{t}
at time
t
is given by

dR_{t}=r_{t}R_{t}\,dt,\quad R_{0}>0.

(4.1)

(ii) A risky security (e.g., a stock), where the price
S_{t}
at time
t
is given by

dS_{t}=S_{t}\left\{(a+AX_{t})\,dt+\sigma d\,W_{t}\right\},\quad S_{0}>0.

(4.2)

(iii) A factor (e.g., short-term interest rates), where the level
X_{t}
at time
t
is given by

dX_{t}=(b+BX_{t})\,dt+\Lambda d\,W_{t},\quad X_{0}=x,

(4.3)

where
W_{t}\equiv(W_{t}^{1},W_{t}^{2})^{\top}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued standard Brownian motion defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbf{P})
. Here
a,A,b,B\in\mathbb{R}
are constants,
r_{\cdot}
is a bounded deterministic function.
\sigma=[\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times 2}
and
\Lambda=[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times 2}
are constant vectors with
\sigma\sigma^{\top}>0
.

At time
t
, let
u_{t}
denote the investment strategy of the investor in the risky security, then
1-u_{t}
denote that in the risk-free security. Set
\mathcal{G}_{t}:=\sigma(S_{r},X_{r};r\leq t)
, and we define

\mathcal{U}[0,T]:=\left\{u\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\;\Big|\;u\text{ is }\mathcal{G}_{t}\text{-progressively measurable},\ \mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|u(t)|^{2}\,dt\right]<\infty\right\},

(4.4)

which denote the set of all admissible investment strategies. For given
u_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{U}[0,T]
,
V_{t}
representing the investor’s wealth at time
t
is given as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dV_{t}&=V_{t}\big[r_{t}\,dt+u_{t}(a+AX_{t}-r_{t})\,dt+u_{t}\sigma\,dW_{t}\big],\\
V_{0}&=v.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.5)

For given initial wealth
v>0
and
\theta>0
, the investor wants to choose an optimal portfolio
\bar{u}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{U}[0,T]
to maximize the following risk-sensitive expected growth rate up to time horizon
T
:

\tilde{J}(u_{\cdot})=-\frac{1}{\theta}\log\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\left\{-\theta\log(V_{T})\right\}\big].

(4.6)

Noting that
V_{\cdot}
satisfies (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\theta\log(V_{t})\right\}=v^{-\theta}\exp\left\{\theta\int_{0}^{t}l(s,X_{s},u_{s})\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\sigma^{\top}u_{s}^{2}\,ds-\theta\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\sigma\,dW_{s}\right\},

(4.7)

where
l(s,X,u):=\frac{1}{2}(\theta+1)\sigma\sigma^{\top}u^{2}-r_{s}-u(a+AX-r_{s})
.

We introduce a new probability measure
\hat{\mathbf{P}}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
defined by

\left.\frac{d\hat{\mathbf{P}}}{d\mathbf{P}}\right|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}=Z_{t},

where
Z_{t}:=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\sigma^{\top}u_{s}^{2}\,ds-\theta\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\sigma\,dW_{s}\right\}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{V}[0,T]
the set of investment strategies
u_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{U}[0,T]
such that
\hat{\mathbf{P}}
is a probability measure, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}]=1
. According to Girsanov’s
theorem, for any
u_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{V}[0,T]
,

\hat{W}_{t}:=W_{t}+\theta\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{\top}u_{s}\,ds

is a standard Brownian motion under the probability measure
\hat{\mathbf{P}}
and

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{t}&=(b+BX_{t}-\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}u_{s})\,dt+\Lambda\,d\hat{W}_{t},\\
X_{0}&=x.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.8)

We can now introduce the auxiliary objective functional under the measure
\hat{\mathbf{P}}
:

\hat{J}(u_{\cdot})=\log v-\frac{1}{\theta}\log\hat{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left\{\theta\int_{0}^{T}l(s,X_{s},u_{s})\,ds\right\}\right],

(4.9)

where
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\cdot]
denotes the expectation with respect to
\hat{\mathbf{P}}
. Let
J(u_{\cdot})=-\hat{J}(u_{\cdot})
, then our portfolio optimization problem can be rewritten as

J(\bar{u}_{\cdot})=\inf\limits_{u_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{V}[0,T]}J(u_{\cdot}).

(4.10)

Since we are going to involve the DPP in treating the above problem, we will adopt the formulation as in Section 2. Let
T>0
be given. For any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
, by (
4.8
),
consider the following SDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{s}^{t,x;u}&=\big(b+BX_{s}^{t,x;u}-\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}u_{s})\,ds+\Lambda\,d\hat{W}_{s},\\
X_{0}^{t,x;u}&=x.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.11)

And our risk-sensitive portfolio optimization problem is to find an optimal portfolio
\bar{u}_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{V}[0,T]
to minimize the cost functional
J(t,x;u_{\cdot})=-\log v+\frac{1}{\theta}\log\hat{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left\{\theta\int_{t}^{T}l(s,X_{s}^{t,x;u},u_{s})\,ds\right\}\right]
. We define the value function as

V(t,x):=J(t,x;\bar{u}_{\cdot})=\inf\limits_{u_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{V}[t,T]}J(t,x;u_{\cdot}).

(4.12)

We can check that all the assumptions in Section 2 are satisfied. Then, we can use both DPP and MP approached to solve problem (
4.12
).

4.1
MP Approach

In this case, the Hamiltonian function given by

\displaystyle H(s,x,u,p,q)

\displaystyle=p\big(b+Bx-\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}u\big)+\frac{1}{2}(\theta+1)\sigma\sigma^{\top}u^{2}-r_{s}

(4.13)

\displaystyle-u(a+AX_{s}-r_{s})+q^{\top}\Lambda+\theta\Lambda_{1}^{2}p^{2}+\theta\Lambda_{2}^{2}p^{2},

and the corresponding
\mathcal{H}
-function is

\mathcal{H}(s,x,u)=H(s,x,u,p,q)-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{1}^{2}(P+\theta p^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{2}^{2}(P+\theta p^{2}).

(4.14)

The first-and second-order adjoint equations are

\left\{\begin{aligned} dp_{s}&=-\big(Bp_{s}-Au_{s}+\theta\Lambda q_{s}^{\top}p_{s}\big)\,ds+q_{s}\,d\hat{W}_{s},\\
p_{T}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.15)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} dP_{s}&=-\big(2BP_{s}+\theta q_{s}q_{s}^{\top}+\theta Q_{s}\Lambda^{\top}p_{s}\big)\,ds+Q_{s}\,d\hat{W}_{s},\\
P_{T}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.16)

respectively. Let
\bar{u}_{\cdot}
be a candidate optimal portfolio,
\bar{X}_{\cdot}^{t,x,\bar{u}}
be the corresponding solution to the controlled SDE (
4.11
), and
(\bar{p}_{\cdot},\bar{q}_{\cdot})
and
(\bar{P}_{\cdot},\bar{Q}_{\cdot})
be the corresponding solution to the adjoint equations (
4.15
) and (
4.16
), respectively. Now the
\mathcal{H}
-function (
4.14
) is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(s,\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}},u)

\displaystyle=\bar{p}_{s}\big(b+B\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}-\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}u\big)+\frac{1}{2}(\theta+1)\sigma\sigma^{\top}u^{2}-r_{s}-u\big(a+A\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}-r_{s}\big)

(4.17)

\displaystyle+\bar{q}_{s}^{\top}\Lambda+\theta\Lambda_{1}^{2}\bar{p}_{s}^{2}+\theta\Lambda_{2}^{2}\bar{p}_{s}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{1}^{2}(\bar{P}_{s}+\theta\bar{p}_{s}^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{2}^{2}(\bar{P}_{s}+\theta\bar{p}_{s}^{2}).

Then, by the minimum condition (
2.15
), we have

\bar{u}_{s}=\frac{1}{\theta+1}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left(\theta\bar{p}_{s}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}+a+A\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}-r_{s}\right),\quad\text{a.e.}s\in[t,T],\quad\hat{\mathbf{P}}\text{-a.s.}

(4.18)

Therefore, an adapted solution triple
(\bar{X}_{\cdot}^{t,x;\bar{u}},\bar{p}_{\cdot},\bar{q}_{\cdot})
can be obtained by solving the following FBSDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}&=\big(b+B\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}-\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\bar{u}_{s}\big)\,ds+\Lambda\,d\hat{W}_{s},\\
d\bar{p}_{s}&=-\big(B\bar{p}_{s}-A\bar{u}_{s}+\theta\Lambda\bar{q}_{s}^{\top}\bar{p}_{s}\big)\,ds+\bar{q}_{s}\,d\hat{W}_{s},\\
\bar{X}_{t}^{t,x;\bar{u}}&=x,\quad\bar{p}_{T}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.19)

To solve (
4.19
), we conjecture the solution is related by the following:

\bar{p}_{s}=\Gamma_{s}\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}+\phi_{s},\quad s\in[t,T],

(4.20)

where
\Gamma_{\cdot}\in C^{1}([t,T];\mathbb{R})
and
\phi_{\cdot}\in C^{1}([t,T];\mathbb{R})
, with
\Gamma_{T}=0,\,\phi_{T}=0
. By applying Itô’s formula to (
4.20
), we get

\displaystyle d\bar{p}_{s}=\left\{\dot{\Gamma_{s}}\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}+\Gamma_{s}\big(b+B\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}-\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\bar{u}_{s}\big)+\dot{\phi_{s}}\right\}ds+\Gamma_{s}\Lambda\,d\hat{W}_{s}.

(4.21)

On the other hand, after substituting (
4.20
) into the backward equation of (
4.19
), we arrive at

d\bar{p}_{s}=-\big(B\Gamma_{s}\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}+B\phi_{s}-A\bar{u}_{s}+\theta\Lambda\bar{q}_{s}^{\top}\Gamma_{s}\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}+\theta\Lambda\bar{q}_{s}^{\top}\phi_{s}\big)\,ds+\bar{q}_{s}\,d\hat{W}_{s}.

(4.22)

Equating the coefficients of (
4.21
) and (
4.22
), gives

(\bar{p}_{s},\bar{q}_{s})=\big(\Gamma_{s}\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}+\phi_{s},\Gamma_{s}\Lambda\big),\quad\text{a.e.}s\in[t,T],\quad\hat{\mathbf{P}}\text{-a.s.},

(4.23)

where
\Gamma_{\cdot}
is the solution to the following Riccati type equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\Gamma}_{s}+\left(\theta\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}-\frac{\theta^{2}}{\theta+1}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\sigma\Lambda^{\top}\right)\Gamma_{s}^{2}\\
&\quad+2\left(B-\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}A\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\right)\Gamma_{s}-\frac{1}{\theta+1}A^{2}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}=0,\\
&\Gamma_{T}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.24)

and
\phi_{\cdot}
is the solution to the following equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\phi}_{s}+\Biggl[B-\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}A\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}+\left(\theta\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}-\frac{\theta^{2}}{\theta+1}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\sigma\Lambda^{\top}\right)\Gamma_{s}\Biggr]\phi_{s}\\
&\quad+b\Gamma_{s}-\left[\frac{1}{\theta+1}A(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}+\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\Gamma_{s}\right](a-r_{s})=0,\\
&\phi_{T}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.25)

Finally, by (
4.18
) and (
4.20
), we can get the optimal portfolio in the following state feedback form:

\hskip-2.84526pt\bar{u}_{s}=\frac{1}{\theta+1}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left[\big(\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\Gamma_{s}+A\big)\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}}+\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\phi_{s}+a-r_{s}\right],\quad\text{a.e.}s\in[t,T],\quad\hat{\mathbf{P}}\text{-a.s.},

(4.26)

where
\Gamma_{\cdot},\phi_{\cdot}
are given by (
4.24
) and (
4.25
), respectively.

To summarize, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

If equations (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) admit solutions
\Gamma_{\cdot}
and
\phi_{\cdot}
, then the optimal control
\bar{u}_{\cdot}
of our risk-sensitive portfolio optimization problem (
4.11
)-(
4.12
) has the following state feedback form

\bar{u}(s,\bar{X})=\frac{1}{\theta+1}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left[\big(\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\Gamma_{s}+A\big)\bar{X}+\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\phi_{s}+a-r_{s}\right],

(4.27)

for
\text{a.e.}s\in[0,T]
,
\hat{\mathbf{P}}\text{-a.s.}
, where
\Gamma_{\cdot},\phi_{\cdot}
are given by (
4.24
) and (
4.25
), respectively.

4.2
DPP approach

In this case, the value function
V(\cdot,\cdot)
should satisfy the following HJB equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}(t,x)=\inf\limits_{u\in U}G\left(t,x,u,\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}(t,x),\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)\right),\\
&V(T,x)=-\log v,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.28)

where the generalized Hamiltonian function (
2.10
) is

\displaystyle G\left(t,x,u,\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}(t,x),\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)\right)

(4.29)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\theta+1)\sigma\sigma^{\top}u^{2}-r_{t}-(a+Ax-r_{t})u+\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}(t,x)\big(b+Bx-\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}u\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta}{2}\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}(t,x)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x).

We conjecture that
V(t,x)
is quadratic in
x
, namely,

V(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Psi_{t}x^{2}+\eta_{t}x+k_{t},

(4.30)

for some deterministic differentiable functions
\Psi_{\cdot},\eta_{\cdot}
and
k_{\cdot}
with
\Psi_{T}=0,\,\eta_{T}=0,\,k_{T}=-\log v
.

Substituting (
4.30
) into (
4.29
) and using completion of squares, we get

\displaystyle G\left(t,x,u,\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}(t,x),\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)\right)

(4.31)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\theta+1)\sigma\sigma^{\top}\bigg\{u-\frac{1}{\theta+1}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\Big[\big(\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\Psi_{t}+A\big)x+\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\eta_{t}+a-r_{t}\Big]\bigg\}^{2}

\displaystyle+\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\left[\theta\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}-\frac{\theta^{2}}{\theta+1}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\sigma\Lambda^{\top}\right]\Psi_{t}^{2}+\left(B-\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}A\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\right)\Psi_{t}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2(\theta+1)}A^{2}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\Bigg\}x^{2}

\displaystyle+\Bigg\{\left[B-\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}A\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}+\left(\theta\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}-\frac{\theta^{2}}{\theta+1}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\sigma\Lambda^{\top}\right)\Psi_{t}\right]\eta_{t}

\displaystyle+b\Psi_{t}-\left(\frac{1}{\theta+1}A(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}+\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\Psi_{t}\right)(a-r_{s})\Bigg\}x

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2(\theta+1)}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left(a-r_{t}+\theta\eta_{t}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\right)^{2}-r_{t}+b\eta_{t}+\frac{\theta}{2}\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\eta_{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\Psi_{t},

provided that
\sigma\sigma^{\top}>0
and
\theta>0
. Then we see that the optimal state feedback portfolio is given by

\hskip-2.84526pt\bar{u}(t,\bar{X})=\frac{1}{\theta+1}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left[\big(\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\Psi_{t}+A\big)\bar{X}+\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\eta_{t}+a-r_{t}\right],\quad\text{a.e.}s\in[t,T],\quad\hat{\mathbf{P}}\text{-a.s.}

(4.32)

In addition, noting (
4.32
), the HJB equation (
4.28
) now reads

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\Psi}_{t}x^{2}-\dot{\eta}_{t}x-\dot{k}_{t}=\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\left[\theta\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}-\frac{\theta^{2}}{\theta+1}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\sigma\Lambda^{\top}\right]\Psi_{t}^{2}

(4.33)

\displaystyle+\left(B-\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}A\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\right)\Psi_{t}-\frac{1}{2(\theta+1)}A^{2}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\Bigg\}x^{2}

\displaystyle+\Bigg\{\left[B-\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}A\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}+\left(\theta\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}-\frac{\theta^{2}}{\theta+1}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\sigma\Lambda^{\top}\right)\Psi_{t}\right]\eta_{t}

\displaystyle+b\Psi_{t}-\left(\frac{1}{\theta+1}A(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}+\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\Psi_{t}\right)(a-r_{s})\Bigg\}x

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2(\theta+1)}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left(a-r_{t}+\theta\eta_{t}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\right)^{2}-r_{t}+b\eta_{t}+\frac{\theta}{2}\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\eta_{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\Psi_{t}.

By comparing the quadratic terms and linear terms in
x
, we recover the equation (
4.24
), (
4.25
), respectively. That is to say, we have that
\Psi_{\cdot}
coincides with
\Gamma_{\cdot}
and
\eta_{\cdot}
coincides with
\phi_{\cdot}
. Moreover, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{k}_{t}-\frac{1}{2(\theta+1)}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left(a-r_{t}+\theta\eta_{t}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\right)^{2}-r_{t}+b\eta_{t}+\frac{\theta}{2}\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\eta_{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\Psi_{t}=0,\\
&k_{T}=-\log v.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.34)

We have proved the following result.

Theorem 4.2
.

If equations (
4.24
), (
4.25
) and (
4.34
) admit solutions
\Gamma_{\cdot}
,
\phi_{\cdot}
and
k_{\cdot}
, then the optimal control of our risk-sensitive portfolio optimization problem (
4.11
)-(
4.12
) has the state feedback form

\bar{u}(t,\bar{X})=\frac{1}{\theta+1}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left[\big(\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\Gamma_{t}+A\big)\bar{X}+\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\phi_{t}+a-r_{t}\right],

(4.35)

for
\text{a.e.}s\in[0,T]
,
\hat{\mathbf{P}}\text{-a.s.}
, and the value function is given by (
4.12
), where
\Gamma_{\cdot}
,
\phi_{\cdot}
and
k_{\cdot}
are given by (
4.24
), (
4.25
) and (
4.34
), respectively.

4.3
Relationship between MP and DPP

We now verify the relationship in Theorem
3.1
. In fact, relationship (
3.1
) is obvious from (
4.28
). Moreover, (
4.23
) and (
4.30
) are exactly the relationships given in (
3.2
).

Let us verify the relationship (
3.3
). We consider the second-order adjoint equation (
4.16
), it is easy to show that

(\bar{P}_{s},\bar{Q}_{s})=(\rho_{s},0)
,
s\in[0,T]
, where
\rho_{\cdot}
is the solution to (noting (
4.23
)):

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{\rho}_{s}&=-2B\rho_{s}-\theta\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\Gamma_{s}^{2},\\
\rho_{T}&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.36)

From (
4.30
), we have
\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}(s,\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}})=\Gamma_{s}
, where
\Gamma_{\cdot}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{\Gamma}_{s}&=-\left(\theta\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}-\frac{\theta^{2}}{\theta+1}\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\sigma\Lambda^{\top}\right)\Gamma_{s}^{2}\\
&\quad-2\left(B-\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}A\Lambda\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\right)\Gamma_{s}+\frac{1}{\theta+1}A^{2}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1},\\
\Gamma_{T}&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.37)

Furthermore, using completing of squares again, we get

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{\Gamma}_{s}&=-2B\Gamma_{s}-\theta\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\Gamma_{s}^{2}+\frac{1}{\theta+1}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left(\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\Gamma_{s}+A\right)^{2},\\
\Gamma_{T}&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.38)

Noting

\frac{1}{\theta+1}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left(\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\Gamma_{s}+A\right)^{2}\geq 0,

If
\frac{1}{\theta+1}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left(\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\Gamma_{s}+A\right)^{2}=0
, then the uniqueness of the solutions to ODE guarantees that
\Gamma_{s}=\rho_{s}
, namely,

\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}(s,\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}})=\bar{P}_{s}
. Otherwise, if
\frac{1}{\theta+1}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\left(\theta\Lambda\sigma^{\top}\Gamma_{s}+A\right)^{2}>0
, then the comparison theorem of ODE guarantees that
\Gamma_{s}<\rho_{s}
, namely,
\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}(s,\bar{X}_{s}^{t,x;\bar{u}})<\bar{P}_{s}
. Thus the relationship (
3.3
) holds. And we can see the strict inequality in (
3.3
) holds in some cases.

Remark 4.1
.

If
\theta>0
, then
\textit{I}-\frac{\theta}{\theta+1}\sigma^{\top}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})^{-1}\sigma
is positive definite, then Riccati equation (
4.24
) admits an explicit solution. Thus equation (
4.25
) admits an explicit solution (see Corollary 4.1 in Shi and Wu
[
31
]
for details).

5
Concluding remarks

In this article, we have considered the relationship between MP and DPP for risk-sensitive stochastic optimal control problems. When the value function is smooth, we obtained the connection among the adjoint processes, the generalized Hamiltonian function, and the value function. As an application, a linear-quadratic risk-sensitive portfolio optimization problem in the financial market was discussed. After the state feedback optimal control was obtained by the risk-sensitive MP and the DPP respectively, the relations we obtained are illustrated explicitly.

A challenging problem is the relationship between MP and DPP without the the smooth assumption imposed on the value function. This problem may be solved in the framework of viscosity solutions (Yong and Zhou
[
35
]
, Nie et al.
[
27
]
). We will study this topic in the future.

Contrastive Heliophysical Image Pretraining for Solar Dynamics Observatory Records
Shiyu Shen, Zhe Gao, Taifeng Chai, Yang Huang, Bin Pan

Abstract
Deep learning has revolutionized solar image analysis, yet most approaches train task-specific encoders from scratch or rely on natural-image pretraining that ignores the unique characteristics of Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO) data. We introduce SolarCHIP, a family of contrastively pretrained visual backbones tailored to multi-instrument SDO observations. SolarCHIP addresses three key challenges in solar imaging: multimodal sensing across AIA and HMI instruments, weak inter-class separability due to slow temporal evolution, and strong intra-class variability with sparse activity signals. Our pretraining framework employs a multi-granularity contrastive objective that jointly aligns (1) global class tokens across co-temporal AIA-HMI pairs to enhance temporal discrimination, (2) local patch tokens at fixed spatial indices to enforce position-consistent, modality-invariant features, and (3) intra-sample patches across different spatial locations to preserve fine-grained spatial structure. We train both CNN- and Vision Transformer-based autoencoders and demonstrate their effectiveness on two downstream tasks: cross-modal translation between HMI and AIA passbands via ControlNet, and full-disk flare classification. Experimental results show that SolarCHIP achieves state-of-the-art performance across both tasks, with particularly strong gains in low-resource settings where labeled data is limited. Ablation studies confirm that each contrastive component contributes essential discriminative capacity at different granularities. By publicly releasing pretrained weights and training code, we provide the heliophysics community with a practical, plug-and-play feature extractor that reduces computational requirements, improves label efficiency, and establishes a reusable foundation for diverse solar imaging applications.



I
Introduction

Since the launch in 2010, Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO) has transformed heliophysical research by delivering an uninterrupted, high-resolution record of solar activity. Its three complementary instruments, most prominently the Atmospheric Imaging Assembly (AIA; EUV/UV imaging across multiple passbands) and the Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager (HMI; full-disk Doppler velocity and vector/line-of-sight magnetic-field measurements), provide cotemporal, coregistered diagnostics of the solar atmosphere and the photospheric magnetic field at cadences as short as 12 s. This integrated data set enables quantitative investigations that span fine-scale transient phenomena through solar cycle evolution.

An expanding body of machine learning and deep learning leverages SDO images to advance quantitative solar analysis and space weather prediction. Representative applications include supervised flare forecasting with classical machine learning and deep architectures
[
32
,
11
,
35
]
, convolutional neural network (CNN) based segmentation of coronal holes
[

,
23
]
, and deep learning-driven enhancement and super-resolution of HMI observables
[

,
42
]
. Most recently, foundation model approaches have begun to learn general-purpose representations directly from multi-instrument SDO data streams
[
53
,
46
]
. Collectively, these studies demonstrate that data-driven methods can match or exceed prior task-specific baselines across forecasting, detection, and segmentation tasks.

Despite notable advances, the field still lacks a broadly applicable visual backbone tailored to solar observations. In natural-image analysis, the pretrain-finetune paradigm has become the dominant workflow: ImageNet-initialized ResNet backbones were long the default for diverse tasks
[
19
]
, and CLIP-based CNNs and Vision Transformers (ViTs) now play a central role
[
41
]
, particularly in multimodal settings. More recently, a proliferation of pretrained visual encoders, which are obtained via self-supervised objectives on large-scale corpora, has yielded versatile feature extractors
[
52
,
56
,
26
]
. Downstream pipelines typically derive base representations from such backbones (frozen or lightly adapted) and attach task-specific heads
[
40
]
, a design that also streamlines multimodal large-model construction by reusing pretrained visual tokens
[
28
]
. By contrast, solar image analysis largely continues to train task-specific encoders from scratch; even emerging foundation-model efforts frequently employ bespoke visual extractors that are not initialized from large-scale visual pretraining. Establishing a domain-adapted, pretrained visual backbone for solar data would standardize representations, reduce data and computational requirements, and likely deliver immediate gains across detection, segmentation, and forecasting tasks.

Unlike natural images, SDO observations present three challenges: (1) multimodal sensing, (2) weak inter-class separability, and (3) strong intra-class variability. First, the multi-instrument, multi-passband sensor samples distinct formation temperatures and heights
[
51
]
; correspondences across AIA channels are governed by magnetic connectivity and thermodynamic state rather than by pixel-level appearance
[
55
]
. Consequently, physics-aware alignment and representation learning that respect magnetic-field topology and instrument response are preferable than treating SDO data as generic multispectral imagery
[
15
]
. Second, inter-class separability is inherently low because, over
\leq 24
hour horizons, apparent evolution is dominated by solar rotation: consecutive images often resemble near-rigid translations of the disk
[
50
]
. Even over longer intervals the global morphology changes slowly, with pronounced activities restricted to limited regions
[
6
]
. Consequently, boundaries between quiet-Sun, background corona, and many event classes are subtle. Third, intra-class variability is high, and the signal is sparse: in single frames most pixels exhibit low-contrast, noise-like background, whereas a few localized regions encode the relevant activity
[
1
]
. Moreover, distinct flare or eruption sites share limited mutual structure
[
25
]
. This sparsity and diversity exacerbate class imbalance and label noise, favoring architectures that can extract features at both global and local scale.

To address these challenges, we develop a family of contrastively pretrained visual backbones named SolarCHIP, which explicitly exploits cross-modal, spatio-temporal correspondences between AIA and HMI. We train encoders for each modality using a CLIP-style, multi-granularity objective that preserves modality-invariant semantics while retaining modality-specific information. The training signal comprises three complementary contrastive components: (1) global, time-indexed alignment, in which each acquisition time is treated as a distinct instance class and the class (CLS) tokens from co-temporal AIA-HMI pairs are pulled together while class tokens from different times are pushed apart, thereby increasing inter-time separability; (2) local, time-indexed alignment, where for each fixed spatial index (e.g., a given patch location) the co-temporal AIA-HMI patch tokens form positive pairs and the same spatial index observed at different times serves as negatives, enforcing position-consistent, modality-invariant local features; and (3) local, intra-time spatial discrimination, which treats patches at different spatial indices within the same time as distinct instance classes, aligns cross-modal tokens at the same index, and uses tokens from other indices as hard negatives to prevent feature collapse and promote fine-grained, location-specific representations. Collectively, this pretraining paradigm jointly aligns co-temporal signals across modalities, separates observations across times, and preserves spatial distinctiveness, yielding backbones that support both macro-level (global) and micro-level (local) analysis in downstream solar imaging tasks.

We evaluate the SolarCHIP by attaching task heads to serve multiple downstream objectives. With a diffusion-based ControlNet
[
44
,
61
]
head, the backbone perform passband-to-passband translation and to impute missing observations; with a classification head, it supports event categorization and flare forecasting. Across benchmarks derived from AIA and HMI, our pretrained encoder delivers state-of-the-art performance, offering consistent gains over ImageNet-initialized and task-specific baselines while reducing labeled-data requirements. By releasing pretrained weights and code, we aim to complement ongoing foundation-model efforts in heliophysics with a practical, plug-and-play feature extractor for SDO data that lowers the barrier to building accurate, label-efficient AI systems for solar science and space-weather applications.

Overall, our contributions are threefold:

•

We introduce and publicly release the SolarCHIP family as SDO visual backbones, including pretrained weights and training recipes for community use.

•

We propose a unified, multi-granularity contrastive pretraining framework that extracts modality-invariant yet modality-aware features by jointly aligning global (time-indexed) class tokens and local (position-aware) patch tokens across SDO modalities.

•

We conduct extensive downstream evaluations that demonstrate strong transfer under limited supervision and establish a reusable foundation backbone for SDO imagery.

Related Work

This section surveys prior work relevant to our study. We first introduce the necessary background of SDO, and review AI for heliophysics with SDO data. We then summarize contrastive pretraining methods and explain why naive RGB practices are ill-suited to SDO.

As a result, representations tend to be either modality-agnostic but semantically weak, or modality-specific but brittle across tasks and cadences.

II-C

Contrastive Pretraining

Contrastive learning handles representation learning as instance discrimination under a similarity-preserving objective. Foundational image-image approaches, including SimCLR
[
12
]
and MoCo
[
18
]
, maximize agreement between augmented views of the same image while repelling negatives via an InfoNCE loss
[
33
]
, producing encoders that transfer competitively to classification, detection, and segmentation. Cluster-aware extensions
[
9
]
and token-level self-distillation
[
10
,
34
]
further strengthen invariances and yield high-capacity, label-free visual backbones. Masked image modeling
[
17
,
62
]
complements contrastive objectives by reconstructing masked tokens; hybrid schemes commonly integrate instance discrimination with reconstruction to trade off invariance against specificity.

Cross-modal contrast generalizes this paradigm by aligning heterogeneous modalities within a shared embedding space. CLIP
[
41
]
established large-scale image-text pretraining with a bidirectional InfoNCE objective that enables zero-shot transfer and modular reuse of visual features. Subsequent variants
[
60
,
24
]
underscore the roles of temperature scaling, large negative sets, and principled view construction in training stability and downstream efficacy. In scientific imaging and remote sensing, contrastive pretraining has been adapted to multispectral and SAR-optical pairs, where co-registered acquisitions provide natural positive views beyond photometric augmentations
[
30
,
16
]
; patch or region level alignment enhances spatial specificity and improves performance on semantic segmentation and related tasks
[
57
,
54
]
.

In the context of SDO, direct application of contrastive objectives from natural image domains risks neglecting the structural distinctions. Problems including unremarkable signatures in small active regions, or overlooking correspondences governed by magnetic topology. Key points for adaption include: (i) the granularity of alignment (global [CLS] embeddings versus patch- or region-level tokens), (ii) negative sampling strategies that respect solar scene dynamics (e.g., hard negatives drawn from proximal times and neighboring locations), and (iii) the balance between invariance and equivariance to preserve spatial structure required for dense prediction. While prior adaptations in scientific and remote-sensing imagery address aspects of these concerns, they rarely exploit SDO’s uniquely co-temporal AIA-HMI pairing. We therefore design a multimodal, time and position aware pretraining scheme that uses co-temporal AIA-HMI pairs, aligns features at global and local levels, and enforces intra-time spatial discrimination with hard patch negatives, yielding modality-aware representations that transfer robustly across forecasting, segmentation, and cross-passband translation.

In this section we detail the SolarCHIP training pipeline. We first describe the preprocessing of SDO image, including standardizing calibration and augmentation , to establish a reliable basis for image-based heliophysics analysis. We then introduce a class-level, CLIP-style contrastive pretraining stage that establishes a backbone for subsequent solar adaptations. Finally, we present solar-specific patch-level and intra-sample contrastive objectives that sharpen local feature discrimination and yield the fine-grained representations required for SDO forecasting, segmentation, and related downstream tasks. 1
, where we visualize the similarity matrices for each contrastive learning methods.

III-A

Data Preprocess

We denote an SDO image as
x^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{B\times 1\times H\times W}}
, where B is batch size, H and W are the spatial resolution. The modality index
i\in\{0,1,...,10\}
specifies the instrument/band:
i=0
corresponds to HMI, while
i\in\{1,...,10\}
denotes AIA passbands at 0094, 0131, 0171, 0193, 0211, 0304, 0335, 1600, 1700, and 4500 bands. To reduce memory burden, we downscale the initial 4096x4096 images to 1024x1024 resolution.

SDO measurements exhibit pronounced skewness: rare, high-magnitude activity concentrated in limited regions against a largely quiescent background. To temper heavy tails and stabilize optimization, we apply a signed log transform channel-wise:

x^{(i)}\leftarrow\operatorname{sign}\!\big(x^{(i)}\big)\,\log\!\big(1+\lvert x^{(i)}\rvert\big)

(1)

For notational simplicity, we continue to use
x
for the transformed data.

To enhance inter-class separability and support patch-level discrimination, we employ rotation and flip based geometric augmentation. All modalities within a sample are transformed synchronously (identical rotation angle and flip) to preserve cross-instrument co-registration. These operations i relocate active regions across patches, providing informative positives/negatives for local contrastive objectives, and (ii) increase the variability of active-region morphology without violating physical constraints. In contrast, appearance-altering augmentations such as ColorJitter or RandomCrop are not used, as SDO provides standardized, full-disk observations for which such manipulations would distort photometric fidelity or disrupt the global solar context.

III-B

Autoencoder with Class Level Contrastive Learning

Our baseline follows a CLIP-style paradigm that aligns modality-specific encoders by optimizing a bidirectional InfoNCE objective on global class tokens. To accommodate downstream solar analyses that require dense, semantics-aware predictions (e.g., segmentation), we augment the encoders with decoders, yielding an autoencoding architecture. Training jointly minimizes the class-level contrastive loss that enforces cross-modal alignment of [CLS] embeddings and the reconstruction loss that preserves fine-grained spatial and photometric structure. This coupling maintains strong global invariances while retaining the local detail necessary for high-fidelity feature extraction and transfer to pixel-level tasks.

To be specific, we process co-temporal samples from each modality with modality-specific encoders
f^{(i)}(\cdot)
to get the corresponding token sequences:

\displaystyle z^{(i)}=f^{(i)}(x^{(i)})\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{B\times(L+1)\times D_{model}}}

(2)

\displaystyle z^{(i)}_{cls}=h^{(i)}_{cls}(z^{(i)}[:,0,:])\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{B\times D_{ctr}}}

\displaystyle z^{(i)}_{img}=h^{(i)}_{img}(z^{(i)}[:,1:,:])\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{B\times L\times D_{ctr}}}

where
z^{(i)}
is the initial tokens extract by the encoder, L is the number of image tokens,
\mathrm{D_{model}}
is the encoder token width,
\mathrm{D_{ctr}}
is the contrastive embedding width. The sequence
z^{(i)}
comprises a leading class token followed by
L
patch tokens ordered left-to-right, top-to-bottom. We project the class token via a linear head
h^{(i)}_{\mathrm{cls}}
for class-level contrastive learning, and separately project the patch tokens with
h^{(i)}_{\mathrm{img}}
to support subsequent local contrastive objectives.

To preserve photometric and spatial fidelity, we reconstruct inputs from the unprojected patch tokens using modality-specific decoders
g^{(i)}(\cdot)
:

\displaystyle\hat{x}^{(i)}=g^{(i)}(z^{(i)}[:,1:,:])

(3)

\displaystyle L_{rec}=\sum_{i=0}^{10}\|\hat{x}^{(i)}-x^{(i)}\|_{2}^{2}

We decode from the pre-projection tokens rather than contrastive embeddings, as the latter emphasize discriminative features and may omit fine-grained detail necessary for accurate reconstruction.

We perform class-level contrastive learning in a pairwise manner. Given that HMI primarily encodes the underlying magnetic activity, whereas AIA passbands capture the consequent photometric responses at different temperatures and heights, we align each AIA modality to HMI to reduce computational overhead. Concretely, we compute a normalized similarity matrix for each pair (HMI, AIA-i):

\displaystyle R^{0,i}=\mathrm{exp}(\alpha_{cls})\frac{z^{(0)}_{cls}}{\|z^{(0)}_{cls}\|_{2}}\left(\frac{z^{(i)}_{cls}}{\|z^{(i)}_{cls}\|_{2}}\right)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times B}

(4)

\displaystyle L_{ctr}^{cls}=\sum_{i=1}^{10}\frac{L_{\mathrm{CE}}(R^{0,i},[0{:}B])+L_{\mathrm{CE}}((R^{0,i})^{\top},[0{:}B])}{2}

where
z^{(0)}_{cls}
and
z^{(i)}_{cls}
are the class-token embeddings for HMI and AIA-
i
, respectively;
\alpha_{cls}\in\mathbb{R}
is a learnable temperature (scale) parameter;
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the L2 norm along the feature dimension;
(\cdot)^{\top}
indicates transposition;
L_{\mathrm{CE}}
is the cross-entropy loss; and
[0{:}B]=[0,1,\ldots,B{-}1]
specifies the ground-truth alignment between matched items in the batch.

By construction, the diagonal entries of
R^{0,i}
measure similarity between co-temporal HMI and AIA instances, while off-diagonal entries compare mismatched times. Minimizing
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{cls}}_{\mathrm{ctr}}
increases diagonal dominance and suppresses off-diagonal similarities, thereby pulling together cross-modal embeddings for the same solar scene and pushing apart embeddings corresponding to different scenes. This objective enforces coherent global alignment across modalities while preserving instance discrimination.

III-C

Patch Level Contrastive Learning

As shown in
Figure

1
, active regions occupy a small fraction of the solar disk and are sparsely distributed. At the global level, temporal variation is dominated by the apparent displacement of these regions, which can drive class-level contrastive alignment but provides limited insight into their intrinsic morphology and radiometric intensity features that are most relevant for space weather analyses. To utilize such local structure, we introduce a patch-aligned contrastive objective that operates on image tokens.

Analogous to CLIP, we construct, for each spatial index j, a cross-modal similarity matrix between HMI and AIA-i patch embeddings and maximize its diagonal dominance:

\displaystyle R^{0,i}_{j}=\mathrm{exp}(\alpha_{pat})\frac{z^{(0)}_{img}[:,j,:]}{\|z^{(0)}_{img}[:,j,:]\|_{2}}\left(\frac{z^{(i)}_{img}[:,j,:]}{\|z^{(i)}_{img}[:,j,:]\|_{2}}\right)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times B}

(5)

\displaystyle L_{ctr}^{pat}=\sum_{i=1}^{10}\frac{1}{L}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\frac{L_{\mathrm{CE}}(R_{j}^{0,i},[0{:}B])+L_{\mathrm{CE}}((R_{j}^{0,i})^{\top},[0{:}B])}{2}

where
z^{(0)}_{img}[:,j,:]
and
z^{(i)}_{img}[:,j,:]
denote the j-th patch embeddings from HMI and AIA-i respectively, and
\alpha_{pat}\in\mathbb{R}
is a learnable temperature. Here, the diagonal entries compare co-temporal, co-located patches across modalities, while off-diagonal terms correspond to mismatched scenes.

This token-wise objective complements the class-level loss by injecting fine-grained, spatially localized supervisory signals. In combination, the encoder learns representations that capture both global scene consistency and the morphology/intensity of active regions, thereby improving transfer to downstream tasks that require precise local discrimination.

III-D

Intra Sample Contrastive Learning

While patch-level, per-token contrastive alignment strengthens local correspondence across modalities, it can still permit degenerate solutions in which tokens from inactive regions, which are often perceived as low-information background, are implicitly “dragged” by salient active region tokens to satisfy the contrastive objective. In this situation the encoder need not learn discriminative representations for inactive region patches; instead, it may rely on global scene cues and active region dominance. However, physically meaningful heliophysical signals may hide within these visually quiescent regions. To counteract this tendency and further enhance locality, we introduce a time agnostic, intra-sample patch wise contrastive objective.

For each sample k, we construct a cross-modal similarity matrix over all patch embeddings, thereby enforcing fine-grained alignment independent of other samples in the batch:

\displaystyle R^{0,i}_{k}=\mathrm{exp}(\alpha_{int})\frac{z^{(0)}_{img}[k,:,:]}{\|z^{(0)}_{img}[k,:,:]\|_{2}}\left(\frac{z^{(i)}_{img}[k,:,:]}{\|z^{(i)}_{img}[k,:,:]\|_{2}}\right)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times L}

(6)

\displaystyle L_{ctr}^{int}=\sum_{i=1}^{10}\frac{1}{B}\sum_{k=0}^{B-1}\frac{L_{\mathrm{CE}}(R_{k}^{0,i},[0{:}L])+L_{\mathrm{CE}}((R_{k}^{0,i})^{\top},[0{:}L])}{2}

where
z^{(0)}_{img}[k,:,:]
and
z^{(i)}_{img}[k,:,:]
denote the patch embeddings of sample k from HMI and AIA-i respectively, and
\alpha_{int}\in\mathbb{R}
is a learnable temperature. Here, the diagonal entries compare patches of same positions across modalities, while off-diagonal terms correspond to different positions.

This intra-sample objective compels the encoder to model discriminative features for both active and inactive regions by removing global, cross-sample cues and treating all within-image patches as hard negatives for one another. In conjunction with rotation and flip augmentations, it substantially enlarges the pool of informative negatives and promotes robust, spatially precise representations that benefit downstream tasks requiring fine-grained local reasoning.

Our overall training objective combines reconstruction with multi-granularity contrastive supervision:

L=L_{rec}+\lambda_{1}L_{ctr}^{cls}+\lambda 2L_{ctr}^{pat}+\lambda 3L_{ctr}^{int}

(7)

where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\geq 0
are hyperparameters. Given compute constraints, we subsample modalities at each step rather than performing full all-to-all contrastive matching across every passband.

The framework is architecture-agnostic. For convolutional backbones, we reshape 4D feature maps by flattening their
H\times W
spatial dimensions to obtain image tokens; the class token is produced by linearly projecting a global average-pooled feature. For transformer backbones, tokens arise naturally from the patch embedding, and the class token follows the standard [CLS] convention.

We pretrain both CNN- and ViT-based autoencoders. The CNN pathway adopts a VAE-style design akin to the stable diffusion encoder-decoder for high-fidelity image compression
[
44
]
, while the ViT pathway follows the MAE-style encoder-decoder family
[
17
]
, providing a canonical ViT instantiation. To accommodate SDO inputs, the first convolutional block is adapted to the native single-channel, 1024x1024 resolution (1x1024x1024).

With these pretrained autoencoders, we instantiate two downstream applications:

•

Cross-modal translation: we first train a Stable Diffusion model per modality, then introduce ControlNet modules and fine-tune them to realize bidirectional synthesis between HMI and each AIA passband.

•

Flare classification: we append a linear head to the HMI encoder and project its class token to logits for supervised flare recognition.

Together, cross-modal translation reflects the model’s capacity for global pattern understanding and multi-modal correspondence, whereas flare classification evaluates the granularity of the learned representations for discriminative, fine-scale forecasting.

IV
Experiments

We empirically evaluate SolarCHIP across downstream tasks and diagnostic studies. We begin with cross-modal translation and flare classification, comparing the same architectures (i) trained from scratch without SolarCHIP pretraining and (ii) against state-of-the-art task-specific baselines. We then analyze transferability by progressively tightening fine-tuning budgets on the downstream tasks. Finally, we conduct an ablation that isolates each contrastive objective to quantify their individual and joint contributions to performance.

IV-A

Experiment Setup

We use the SDO archive spanning 2010-2024. Data from 2010-2020 constitute the training split and 2020-2024 the test split for both pretraining and downstream evaluation, with no temporal overlap. The minimum temporal cadence is 1 hour, and all images are bilinearly resampled to 1024×1024.

For cross-modal translation, SolarCHIP-AE-16 serves as the latent encoder-decoder, and a U-Net-based latent diffusion model is trained per modality. ControlNet modules are then attached and fine-tuned to enable conditional synthesis between HMI and each AIA passband while retaining modality-specific diffusion priors.

For flare recognition, we employ SolarCHIP-AE-16 and SolarCHIP-ViT-64 as feature extractors and append a linear classifier to produce six-way logits over {none, A, B, C, M, X}. Unlike active-region-centric approaches, we perform full-disk classification, which increases task difficulty by requiring precise local feature encoding within a predominantly quiescent background.

Transferability is assessed on flare recognition by progressively reducing the number of labeled training events. The ablation study isolates the contribution of each objective by reporting downstream task performance decomposed across reconstruction, class-token, co-located patch, and intra-sample terms, thereby probing discriminative capacity at multiple levels of granularity.

V
Discussion

V-A

Limitations

Although SolarCHIP demonstrates strong performance across multiple tasks, several limitations remain. First, modality coverage remains limited. While SDO provides rich multi-wavelength data, other solar observatories capture complementary phenomena that could enhance representation learning. Integrating data from missions like SOHO or Parker Solar Probe may yield more holistic models. Further, other natural modalities such as language (e.g., solar event reports) could be incorporated to enrich the learned features. Second, the reliance on a CLIP-style contrastive paradigm narrows applicability. Our current framework focuses on contrastive pretraining within solar imaging data. This paradigm provides a general foundation, but not suitable for all downstream tasks. DINO, MAE, SAM and other architectures may be more appropriate for certain applications, such as segmentation or anomaly detection. Exploring diverse pretraining strategies could yield more specialized models. Third, explicit temporal modeling is absent. Solar phenomena are inherently dynamic, yet our current approach treats images as static snapshots. Incorporating temporal contrastive objectives or video-based architectures could better capture evolving solar events, improving forecasting capabilities.

V-B

Broader Impacts

SolarCHIP offers high compression while preserving detail structure, yielding transferable representations that support a broad range of heliophysical analyses and applications. We employ SolarCHIP as a general purpose latent encoder-decoder and, on top of its embeddings, develop an autoregressive transformer for long term synthesis of solar activity; this model will be released in forthcoming work. By furnishing robust cross-modal features, SolarCHIP can enhance forecasting of solar flares and related space weather events with direct operational relevance for satellite health, telecommunications, and power grid resilience. Realizing this potential, however, requires stringent validation across solar cycle phases, observing conditions, instruments, and viewpoints, alongside careful treatment of domain shift, calibration, and uncertainty, to avoid overfitting to specific datasets and to ensure reliable deployment in operational settings.

V-C

Future Work

Future work will explore several directions to enhance SolarCHIP. First, we plan to extend modality coverage by integrating data from additional solar observatories and instruments, such as SOHO, Parker Solar Probe, and ground-based telescopes. This multi-observatory fusion could yield richer representations that capture a broader spectrum of solar phenomena. Second, we intend to incorporate explicit temporal modeling into the framework. By adding video-based architectures after the static representation of each moment, we can better capture the dynamic evolution of solar events, enhancing forecasting capabilities. Finally, we will conduct extensive validation across diverse solar cycle phases, observing conditions, and instruments to ensure robustness and generalizability of the learned representations for operational space weather applications.

VI
Conclusion

We present SolarCHIP, a contrastive heliophysics image pretraining framework that learns transferable representations across multiple solar imaging modalities. By combining reconstruction with multi-granularity contrastive objectives, SolarCHIP captures both global patterns and fine-scale details essential for heliophysical analyses. Empirical evaluations on cross-modal translation and flare classification demonstrate SolarCHIP’s effectiveness in enhancing downstream task performance, particularly in low-resource settings. Ablation studies confirm the contributions of each contrastive component to representation quality. SolarCHIP provides a versatile foundation for future heliophysics applications, including long-term solar activity synthesis and improved space weather forecasting.

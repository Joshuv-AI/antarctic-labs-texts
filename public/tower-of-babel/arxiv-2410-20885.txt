A Distributed Lag Approach to the Generalized Dynamic Factor Model
Philipp Gersing

Abstract
We propose a simple estimator for the dynamic decomposition of the Generalized Dynamic Factor Model that avoids frequency-domain methods. First, we show that it is a reasonable approximation to assume that the dynamic common component of the Generalized Dynamic Factor Model admits a representation in terms of current and lagged statically pervasive factors. Then, assuming finite lag order, this simplification reduces estimation to a regression of the observed variables on estimated factors and their lags , where the factors are extracted via static principal components. The proposed approach naturally accommodates weak, non-pervasive factors within the dynamic common space. We establish consistency and asymptotic normality for both the dynamic and weak common components under a new asymptotic framework that allows for such weak factors. In an application to three high-dimensional time series panels of European macroeconomic data we detect a sizeable weak common component share in several key macroeconomic indicators.



Index terms—
Approximate Factor Model, Generalized Dynamic Factor Model, Weak Factors, Canonical Decomposition of Factor Models,
Structure Theory

Introduction

We consider a high-dimensional time series panel as double indexed zero mean stationary process
(y_{it}:i\in\mathbb{N},t\in\mathbb{Z})\equiv(y_{it})
. The Generalized Dynamic Factor Model (GDFM), introduced by
Forni et al. (2000)
;
Forni and Lippi (2001)
;
Hallin and Lippi (2013)
, relies on a decomposition of the form

\displaystyle y_{it}=\chi_{it}+\xi_{it}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\bm{K}_{i}(j)\bm{\varepsilon}_{t-j}+\xi_{it},\quad\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}\sim WN(\bm{I}_{q}),

(1)

where
(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t})
is an orthonormal white noise process of the common innovations,
\underaccent{\bar}{\bK}_{i}(L):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\bm{K}_{i}(j)L^{j}
are pervasive causal square summable filters while
L
denotes the lag operator,

(\chi_{it})
is the dynamic common component - driven by the same
q
shocks
(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t})
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and
(\xi_{it})
is the dynamic idiosyncratic component, which is weakly correlated over time and cross-section and orthogonal to
(\chi_{it})
at all leads and lags. Note, that in view of
Hallin and Lippi (2013)
, stationarity is sufficient for obtaining a dynamic decomposition - with possibly an infinite number
q
of common shocks.

The GDFM has proven to be a powerful tool in many applications
(
Forni et al., 2005
;
Forni et al., 2004
;
Forni et al., 2017
;
Forni et al., 2018
;
Barigozzi and Cho, 2020
;
Barigozzi et al., 2021
;
Barigozzi et al., 2024a
;
Barigozzi et al., 2025
;
Anderson and Deistler, 2008
, see)
and allows for the presence of weak non-pervasive factors in the dynamic common space
(
Gersing et al., 2026
)
.

In addition to the dynamic factor structure (
1
), we assume that
(y_{it})
also has a static factor structure as standard in the literature commencing with
Chamberlain and Rothschild (1983)
;
Chamberlain (1983)
;
Stock and Watson (2002)
;
Bai and Ng (2002)
:

\displaystyle y_{it}=C_{it}+e_{it}=\bm{\Lambda}_{i}\bm{F}_{t}+e_{it},

(2)

where
\bm{F}_{t}
is an
r\times 1
vector of statically pervasive factors loaded with deterministic loadings
\bm{\Lambda}_{i}
into the static common component
C_{it}
, and
(e_{it})
is the static idiosyncratic component, weakly correlated in the cross-section and contemporaneously orthogonal to
(\bm{F}_{t})
. The number of static factors
r
is uniquely determined by the number of diverging signal eigenvalues in the variance matrix of
(\bm{y}_{t}^{n})=(y_{1t},...,y_{nt})^{\prime}
for
n\to\infty

(
Chamberlain and Rothschild, 1983
, see)
.

Available tools for estimation of the dynamic factor decomposition in (
1
) are
Forni and Lippi (2001)
;
Hallin and Liška (2007)
;
Forni et al. (2005)
;
Forni et al. (2017)
;
Barigozzi et al. (2024b)
;
Barigozzi et al. (2024a)
;
Barigozzi and Farnè (2024)
and mostly designed for the case of
infinite dimensional
factor spaces, meaning that
\dim\cspargel(\chi_{it}:i\in\mathbb{N})=\infty
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\cspargel(\cdot)
denotes the closed span. These methods include spectral density estimation and several steps to achieve a representation that is one-sided in the observed variables and therefore fit for forecasting. In this paper, we consider a restricted, finite-dimensional, version of the GDFM with the aim to provide simpler tools for estimation and inference. We propose the following approximation to the GDFM decomposition (
1
):

\displaystyle y_{it}

\displaystyle=\chi_{it}+\xi_{it}

\displaystyle=\bm{\beta}_{i0}\bm{F}_{t}+\bm{\beta}_{i1}\bm{F}_{t-1}+...+\bm{\beta}_{ip}\bm{F}_{t-p}+\xi_{it}=\bm{\beta}_{i}\bm{x}_{t}+\xi_{it}

(3)

where
\bm{x}_{t}:=(\bm{F}_{t}^{\prime},...,\bm{F}_{t-p}^{\prime})^{\prime}
is of dimension
r_{\chi}=r(p+1)
and
(\xi_{it})
is dynamically idiosyncratic
(
Forni and Lippi, 2001
;
Hallin and Lippi, 2013
)
, orthogonal to
(\bm{F}_{t})
at all leads and lags. In section
2
, we provide detailed arguments why it is reasonable to approximate the general GDFM representation in (
1
) by the simpler finite distributed lag representation in (
3
). Estimation of (
3
) proceeds by regressing on estimated factors extracted via principal components. Therefore the use of spectral density methods and problems associated to two-sidedness are avoided.

The central point of this paper is that the dynamic common component
(\chi_{it})
in model (
1
) captures in general richer dynamics than the static common component
(C_{it})
in (
2
): In particular,
\chi_{it}
- as opposed to
(C_{it})
- incorporates additional feedback from lagged values of
\bm{F}_{t}
. This feedback cannot be retrieved by using
r_{\chi}
instead of
r
principal components as only
r<r_{\chi}
eigenvalues of
\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{y}}^{n}
diverge with
n\to\infty
. As proved by
Onatskiy (2012)
and demonstrated in the simulation section of this paper, using
r_{\chi}
principal components results in inconsistent estimates.

Next, the distributed lags representation (
3
) differs also from specifying dynamics for
\bm{F}_{t}
while imposing the static decomposition (
2
) as e.g. in
Forni et al. (2009)
;
Doz et al. (2011)
;
Barigozzi and Luciani (2019)
. More broadly in view of (
1
), the dynamic common component represents the part that is dynamically common - that is, the projection of observed variables onto the infinite past of the common shocks
(
Gersing, 2026
, see)
. By contrast, the static common component captures the contemporaneous co-movement as
(C_{it})
emerges from cross-sectional aggregations holding
t
fixed.

The general theory linking the GDFM decomposition (
1
) and the static factor decomposition (
2
) is introduced in
Gersing (2023)
;
Gersing et al. (2026)
and subsequently considered in the time-domain-framework in
Barigozzi and Hallin (2026)
. Both models can be nested within the following canonical decomposition:

\displaystyle y_{it}

\displaystyle=\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\underbrace{\phantom{C_{it}+e_{it}^{\chi}}}_{\chi_{it}}$\hss}C_{it}+\overbrace{e_{it}^{\chi}+\xi_{it}}^{e_{it}}=\bm{\Lambda}_{i}\bm{F}_{t}+\bm{\Lambda}_{i}^{w}\bm{F}_{t}^{w}+\xi_{it}

(4)

\displaystyle\bm{y}_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle=\bm{C}_{t}^{n}+\bm{e}_{t}^{\chi,n}+\bm{\xi}_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\bm{\Lambda}^{n}\bm{F}_{t}+\bm{\Lambda}^{w,n}\bm{F}_{t}^{w}}_{\bm{\chi}_{t}^{n}}+\bm{\xi}_{t}^{n}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\Lambda}^{n}&\bm{\Lambda}^{w,n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{F}_{t}\\[5.0pt]
\bm{F}_{t}^{w}\end{bmatrix}+\bm{\xi}_{t}^{n},

(5)

The static common component
(C_{it})
is driven by statically pervasive, or briefly, strong factors, i.e. associated with thick loading columns, usually expressed in terms of
\bm{\Lambda}^{n^{\prime}}\bm{\Lambda}^{n}/n\to\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}>\bm{0}
for
n\to\infty
. On the other hand the weak common component
(e_{it}^{\chi})
is driven by weak non-pervasive factors, i.e. associated with non-divergent loadings
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{max eval}\left(\bm{\Lambda}^{w,n^{\prime}}\bm{\Lambda}^{w,n}\right)<\infty
as pioneered in
Onatskiy (2012)
. All three terms in the canonical decomposition (
4
) are
contemporaneously orthogonal
. However, while
(\xi_{it})
is orthogonal to
(\bm{F}_{t})
and
(\bm{F}_{t}^{w})
at all leads and lags,
(\bm{F}_{t})
and
(e_{it})
may correlate at leads and lags via
(\bm{F}_{t}^{w})
. Note that, in view of the GDFM (
1
), both
(\bm{F}_{t})
and
(\bm{F}_{t}^{w})
are driven by the same shocks
(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t})
.

The weak factors
\bm{F}_{t}^{w}
are to be distinguished from rate-weak factors associated to loadings, i.e.
\bm{\Lambda}^{w,n^{\prime}}\bm{\Lambda}^{w,n}/n^{\alpha}\to\bm{\Gamma}_{\Lambda^{w}}>\bm{0}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)

(
De Mol et al., 2008
;
Freyaldenhoven, 2022
;
Bai and Ng, 2023
, see)
.
1
1

1

In
Barigozzi and Hallin (2026)
, it is shown that rate-weak factors are not possible under cross-sectional exchangeability.
In this paper we do not consider rate-weak factors, however the theory could probably be extended to include rate-weak factors, which would be absorbed in
C_{it}

(see
Gersing et al., 2026
, for details)
.

We obtain the canonical decomposition (
5
) from the distributed lags representation (
3
) as follows: First, we project out
\bm{F}_{t}
from its lags. For this let
\bm{\delta}
be the population regression parameter from a regression of
\bm{F}_{t}^{-}:=(\bm{F}_{t-1}^{\prime},...,\bm{F}_{t-p}^{\prime})^{\prime}
onto
\bm{F}_{t}
and set
\bm{\beta}_{i}^{-}:=(\bm{\beta}_{i1},....,\bm{\beta}_{ip})
, then from (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle y_{it}

\displaystyle=\bm{\beta}_{i}\bm{x}_{t}+\xi_{it}=\underbrace{\left(\bm{\beta}_{i0}+\bm{\beta}_{i}^{-}\bm{\delta}\right)}_{\bm{\Lambda}_{i}}\bm{F}_{t}+\underbrace{\bm{\beta}_{i}^{-}}_{\bm{\Lambda}_{i}^{w}}\underbrace{\left(\bm{F}_{t}^{-}-\bm{\delta}\bm{F}_{t}\right)}_{\bm{F}_{t}^{w}}+\xi_{it}=\underbrace{\bm{\Lambda}_{i}\bm{F}_{t}}_{C_{it}}+\underbrace{\bm{\Lambda}_{i}^{w}\bm{F}_{t}^{w}}_{e_{it}^{\chi}}+\xi_{it}.

(6)

Consequently, we solve the problem raised by
Onatskiy (2012)
, who showed that the principal component estimator is inconsistent for weak factors associated with non-divergent eigenvalues. To the best of our knowledge, the only alternative approach is that of
Lettau and Pelger (2020a)
;
Lettau and Pelger (2020b)
, who propose a modified PCA estimator based on a covariance matrix with an overweighted mean to amplify the signal of such factors. The framework developed here allows the influence of
r_{w}:=r_{\chi}-r
weak factors to be estimated consistently. In the distributed lags representation (
3
), the weak factors arise simply from feedback to lagged strong factors, which themselves are recovered from static principal components with a strong cross-sectional signal. In this paper, we focus on inference for the common components
\chi_{it}
and
e_{it}^{\chi}=\chi_{it}-C_{it}
, rather than the weak factors
\bm{F}_{t}^{w}
themselves.

An interesting interpretation of (
6
) is that weak factors are associated to factor loadings that “taper off” for lags of strong factors. While the first
r
factors are pervasive as
\bm{\beta}_{0}^{n^{\prime}}\bm{\beta}_{0}^{n}\to\infty
, the influence of lags becomes weaker going further in the past as
\bm{\beta}^{-,n^{\prime}}\bm{\beta}^{-,n}=\bm{\Lambda}^{w,n^{\prime}}\bm{\Lambda}^{w,n}<\infty
. It is plausible that fewer series, are influenced by information from the past, e.g. if they are more (or less) persistent relative to the majority of series in the panel. Implications to impulse response functions have been studied as well in
Gersing et al. (2026)
.

Notably, the distributed lag representation (
3
) resembles the factor-augmented regression (FAVAR) framework commencing with
Stock and Watson (2002)
;
Bernanke et al. (2005)
;
Bai and Ng (2006)
, but requires a different asymptotic analysis. While
Bai and Ng (2006)
assume independence between
(e_{it})
and
(\bm{F}_{t})
, the GDFM allows correlation between
(e_{it})
and
(\bm{F}_{t})
at leads and lags, while being contemporaneously orthogonal. This introduces additional terms in the asymptotic expansions which be accommodated in the proofs. FA-VAR frameworks that relax independence typically avoid this issue by excluding lagged factors from the regression
(
Gonçalves and Perron, 2014
, e.g.,)
.

The contributions of this paper are as follows: Firstly, we provide representation theory that illuminates why the finite distributed lags representation (
3
) is a reasonable approximation to the GDFM, thereby illuminating the role of feedback from lagged static factors as accounting for weak factors and its resemblance to factor augmented regressions.

Secondly, we establish consistency and derive asymptotic confidence intervals for the dynamic common component,
\chi_{it}
, as well as for the weak common component,
e_{it}^{\chi}
, in (
4
) in a framework that allows for the presence of weak factors.

Thirdly, by doing so, we develop the first asymptotic theory for the GDFM that entirely avoids frequency-domain methods and avoids estimation of the number of dynamic factors
q
in (
1
). Our framework additionally also accommodates time-varying heteroskedasticity in the idiosyncratic component, extending the scope of existing GDFM estimation theory.

Fourthly, while still relying heavily on techniques employed in the seminal work of
Bai and Ng (2006)
, we provide alternative, simpler proofs for consistency and asymptotic normality which commence from consistent second moment estimation (Assumption E
2
) and employ the idea of static averaging from
Chamberlain and Rothschild (1983)
;
Forni and Lippi (2001)
;
Gersing (2023)
(see the role of
\bm{\mathcal{K}}_{j}
in the respective Lemmata) by using
Yu et al. (2015)

(see also
Barigozzi, 2022
, who proves consistency of the sample-eigenvectors)
. This can be of independent interest.

Fifthly, in the empirical application we show that there is empirical evidence for sizeable weak common components in key macroeconomic variables, like e.g. Sentiment Indicators (ESENTIX EA), Industrial Production (IPMN FR), Total Hours Worked (THOURS EA) and Gross-Investment Share of Non-Financial Corporations (GNFCIR DE) coming from three different macroeconomic time series panels about the Euro Area.

The paper is organised as follows. Section
2
develops representation or structure theory
(
Hannan and Deistler, 2012
)
motivating the approximation of the dynamic common component by the distributed lags representation. Section
3
provides methods for estimation and inference after introducing suitable assumptions. Section
4
presents numerical results, showing consistency of the proposed estimator, failure of the stacking approach based on
r_{\chi}
principal components, and coverage properties of the asymptotic confidence intervals. Finally, Section
5
applies the suggested methods in practice.

Representation Theory: Identification from Static Factors

Let
\mathcal{P}=(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and
L_{2}(\mathcal{P},\mathbb{R})
be the Hilbert space of square integrable real-valued, zero-mean, random-variables defined on
\Omega
equipped with the inner product
\langle u,v\rangle=\E[uv]
for
u,v\in L_{2}(\mathcal{P},\mathbb{R})
. If
\mathbb{M}\subset L_{2}(\mathcal{P},\mathbb{R})
is a linear subspace, we denote by
\proj(\bm{u}\mid\mathbb{M})
the orthogonal projection of
\bm{u}
onto
\mathbb{M}

(see e.g.
Deistler and Scherrer, 2022
, Theorem 1.2)
. Now suppose that
(y_{it})
lives in
L_{2}(\mathcal{P},\mathbb{R})
and set
\mathbb{H}(\bm{y}):=\cspargel(y_{it}:i\in\mathbb{N},t\in\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{y}):=\cspargel(y_{is}:i\in\mathbb{N},s\leq t)
.

Recall that in view of
Hallin and Lippi (2013)
the dynamic decomposition in equation (
1
) can be obtained in the time domain, only assuming stationarity. Let
\mathbb{D}(\bm{y})
be the Hilbert space spanned by all dynamic aggregates, i.e. averages over time and cross-section with squared weights tending to zero
(see e.g.
Forni and Lippi, 2001
;
Hallin and Lippi, 2013
;
Gersing et al., 2026
, for rigorous definitions)
: The dynamic common component of the GDFM is the orthogonal projection
\chi_{it}=\proj(y_{it}\mid\mathbb{D}(\bm{y}))
- provided that
\mathbb{D}(\bm{y})=\mathbb{H}(\bm{u})
is spanned by
(\bm{u}_{t})
orthonormal white noise shocks of dimension
q<\infty
. Note that projecting on
\mathbb{D}(\bm{y})
is a time domain projection which renders residuals that are orthonormal to
\mathbb{D}(\bm{y})
at all leads and lags (thus the dynamic idiosyncratic component
(\xi_{it})
).

Next, in
Gersing (2026)
it is shown that in this projection - except for pathological edge cases - we can replace
\mathbb{D}(\bm{y})=\mathbb{H}(\bm{u})
by
\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{\varepsilon})
while
\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{\varepsilon})\subseteq\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{y})
and
(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t})
is
q
-dimensional orthonormal white noise process that we may call the “common innovations” (the innovations of the dynamic common component), so

\displaystyle\chi_{it}=\proj(y_{it}\mid\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{\varepsilon})).

(7)

In the following we argue, why it is reasonable to assume that
\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{\varepsilon})=\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{F})
, in order to replace
\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{\varepsilon})
by
\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{F})
in the projection (
7
).

Complementary to
\mathbb{D}(\bm{y})
there is a static aggregation space
\mathbb{S}_{t}(\bm{y})
spanned by all cross-sectional aggregates, i.e. averages over
\{y_{it}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
holding time
t
fixed, with squared weights tending to zero
(see
Gersing, 2026
;
Barigozzi and Hallin, 2026
, for rigorous definitions)
. By
Chamberlain and Rothschild (1983)
;
Gersing (2023)
;
Gersing et al. (2026)
;
Barigozzi and Hallin (2026)
, we obtain
C_{it}=\proj(y_{it}\mid\mathbb{S}_{t}(\bm{y}))
, while analogously to the dynamic case, the classical static literature assumes that there exists an
r\times 1
process of static factors, such that
\cspargel(\bm{F}_{t})=\mathbb{S}_{t}(\bm{y})
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, consequently
C_{it}=\bm{\Lambda}_{i}\bm{F}_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
and the residuals from that projection are
contemporaneously
orthogonal to
\mathbb{S}_{t}(y)
(thus the static idiosyncratic component
(e_{it})
).

By the proof of Theorem 5 in
Gersing et al. (2026)
, we know that the space spanned by static aggregates of
(y_{it})
at fixed time
t
is the same as the space spanned by static aggregates of
(\chi_{it})
. In particular we have the following relations:
\cspargel(\bm{F}_{t})=\mathbb{S}_{t}(\bm{y})=\mathbb{S}_{t}(\bm{\chi})\subset\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{\varepsilon})
. It follows that
\bm{F}_{t}
can be represented as a causal transformation of the common innovations
(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t})
. Suppose that
(\bm{F}_{t})
is an ARMA process:

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{t}=\underaccent{\bar}{\bsa}^{-1}(L)\underaccent{\bar}{\bsb}(L)\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}=\underaccent{\bar}{\bsk}_{\bm{F}}(L)\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}.

(8)

Write
\underaccent{\bar}{\bsb}(z)
as a polynomial function with
z\in\mathbb{C}
. If
q=r
and
\det\underaccent{\bar}{\bsb}(z)\neq 0
for all

, then we say that
\underaccent{\bar}{\bsk}_{\bm{F}}(z)
is minimum phase
(

and there exists causal left inverse of
\underaccent{\bar}{\bsk}_{\bm{F}}(z)

\underaccent{\bar}{\bsk}_{\bm{F},\text{left}}(z):=\underaccent{\bar}{\bsb}^{-1}(z)\underaccent{\bar}{\bsa}(z)

\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}\in\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{F})
and therefore
\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{\varepsilon})=\mathbb{H}_{t}(\bm{F})
.
On the other hand, if

, then by Theorem 3 in

, generically, i.e. on a set that is open and dense in the ARMA parameter space of
(\bm{F}_{t})
, there exists an autoregressive representation
\underaccent{\bar}{\bsa}(L)\bm{F}_{t}=\bm{b}\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}

and

having rank

. So, generically

Thus, replacing

3.1
Consistency of Factor- and Loadings-Space

We employ the following assumptions for estimation which are in line with the standard Assumptions from the theoretical literature on factor models

Forni and Lippi (2001)
;

Assumption E 1

(Asymptotic Properties of the Loadings)

We assume that model (

(\bm{F}_{t})

(i)

We assume that

(ii)

Convergence of the “loadings variance”: Set

The eigenvalues of
\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}

(iv)

(vi)

First note that the assumption

In the following we use the eigen-decomposition

The following Assumption about weak convergence is standard in factor analysis

Assumption E 3

(Central Limit Theorem)

Define

Empirical Application

To demonstrate our methods empirically, we use different high-dimensional macroeconomic time series panels for the Euro Area (EA) and major EA countries, based on the open source dataset of
Barigozzi et al. (2024c)
. We consider three datasets: a) monthly EA and country-level series (
n=381

T=309

to

to

to

The number of factors is estimated using
Alessi et al. (2010)
. Following factor selection, model (
3

Groen and Kapetanios (2013)
, specifically designed for regressions involving estimated factors, with a more restrictive penalty term a normal BIC. To retain information from crisis periods, parameter estimation is conducted on outlier removed data, while factor and common component estimation use the standardised raw data combined with eigenvectors and eigenvalues estimated from the cleaned sample covariance matrix. Finally, we obtain the estimates
\widehat{\chi}_{it}
from model (

Let us comment on the results in order: a) For the monthly data, we estimate

Moreover, outside crisis periods, the WCC captures additional dynamics not accounted for by the SCC. Similarly, for the growth rate of French industrial production (Figure

6
Conclusion

We consider the generalized dynamic factor model with finitely many static and weak factors. We show that the dynamic common component admits a finite distributed-lag representation in static factors and develop estimation and inference that accommodate weak non-pervasive factors through lead-lag dependence between factors and the static idiosyncratic component. This extends existing factor-augmented regression results. An application to Euro-Area macroeconomic data reveals a substantial weak common component in several variables.

Lemma 5

1

For part (iv) note that
\left\lVert\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}^{n}-\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}\right\rVert=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2})
by Assumption E
1
, again by Weyl’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\mu_{j}(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}^{n})-\mu_{j}(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}})|=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2})\quad 1\leq j\leq r

(C.29)

Note that
\bm{M}(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}^{n})=M\left(\frac{\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{C}}^{n}}{n}\right)
, which proves the first part. The second part follows with part (iii).

For part (v), note that for
1\leq j\leq r

\displaystyle|\mu_{j}(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}^{n})^{-1}-\mu_{j}(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}})^{-1}|\leq|\mu_{j}\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}^{n}\right)^{-1}||\mu_{j}\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}^{n}\right)-\mu_{j}\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}\right)||\mu_{j}(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}})^{-1}|=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2})

C.29

|\mu_{j}(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{\Lambda}}^{n})^{-1}|=\mathcal{O}(1)

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\bm{D}_{\bm{\Lambda}}^{n})^{-1/2}-\bm{D}_{\bm{\Lambda}}^{-1/2}\right\rVert=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2}).

For the second statement, again for
1\leq j\leq r

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left(\frac{\mu_{j}\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{y}}^{n}\right)}{n}\right)^{-1}-\left(\frac{\mu_{j}(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{C}}^{n})}{n}\right)^{-1}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\left(\frac{\mu_{j}\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{y}}^{n}\right)}{n}\right)^{-1}\right\rvert\left\lvert\frac{\mu_{j}\left(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{y}}^{n}\right)}{n}-\frac{\mu_{j}(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{C}}^{n})}{n}\right\rvert\left\lvert\left(\frac{\mu_{j}(\bm{\Gamma}_{\bm{C}}^{n})}{n}\right)^{-1}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(1)\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2})\mathcal{O}(1)=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/2}),

which completes the proof together with the first part of (v).
∎

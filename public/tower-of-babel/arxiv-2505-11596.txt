Automorphisms of del Pezzo surfaces without points Thanks: This work was supported by the Russian Science Foundation under grant no. 23-11-00033, https://rscf.ru/en/project/23-11-00033/
Constantin Shramov and Anastasia Vikulova

Abstract
. We study automorphism groups of del Pezzo surfaces without points over a field of zero characteristic, and estimate their Jordan constants.



1.
Introduction

Given a group of geometric origin, it is natural to
try to classify its finite subgroups, since finite groups
are often involved in nice geometric constructions. When an
explicit classification is either impossible or is just too
complicated, the following definition measuring the “complexity”
of finite groups proved to be useful.

Definition 1.1

(see
[
Pop11
, Definition 2.1]
,

[
Pop14
, Definition 1]
)
.

A group
\Gamma
is called
Jordan

(alternatively, one says that
\Gamma
has
Jordan property
),
if there exists a constant
J
such that any finite
subgroup of
\Gamma

contains a normal abelian subgroup of index at most
J
.
If
\Gamma
is Jordan, we call the minimal such constant
J

the
Jordan constant
of
\Gamma
and denote it by
J(\Gamma)
.

Jordan property often holds for automorphism and
birational automorphism groups. In particular, it holds for the
birational automorphism group of
the plane over a field of characteristic zero,
see
[
Ser09
, Theorem 5.3]
.
Moreover, the following result was established
in
[
Yas17
, Theorem 1.9]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The Jordan constant of the birational automorphism group of
the plane over an algebraically closed
field of characteristic zero equals
7200
.

A generalization of Theorem
1.2
for an arbitrary field of characteristic zero
was obtained in
[
Zai25
]
.
Furthermore, there exists an analog of Theorem
1.2
for forms of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Given a variety
X
, by
\operatorname{Bir}(X)
we will denote the group
of birational automorphisms of
X
.

Theorem 1.3

(see
[
Shr20a
, Theorem 1.2(ii)]
and
[
Shr20b
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero, and let
S
be a del Pezzo
surface of degree
9
over
\mathbb{K}
. Suppose that
S
has no
\mathbb{K}
-points.
Then

J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\leqslant J(\operatorname{Bir}(S))\leqslant 3.

Furthermore, these bounds are attained
over a suitable field
\mathbb{K}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Over certain fields, a stronger version of Theorem
1.3
holds.
For instance, if
S
is a del Pezzo
surface of degree
9
over the field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
such that
S
has no
\mathbb{Q}
-points, then
every finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Bir}(S)
is abelian, see
[
Shr21
, Corollary 1.4]

or
[
Vik24
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Comparing Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, we see
that the absence of points over the base field imposes
rather strong restrictions on the birational automorphism group
of a surface, at least in the case of forms of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
The same happens in the case of cubic surfaces.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
Shr20c
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero, and let
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}

be a smooth cubic surface over
\mathbb{K}
.
Suppose that
S
has no
\mathbb{K}
-points.
Then the group
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
is abelian, and
J(\operatorname{Bir}(S))\leqslant 3
.
Furthermore, the latter bound is attained
over a suitable field
\mathbb{K}
.

However, for del Pezzo surfaces of degree
2
the situation is entirely different.
Namely, the largest (and most complicated) possible automorphism
group can be attained on a surface without points. Moreover, in this case
there exists an example when the surface is minimal, i.e. has Picard rank
1
.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
Vik25
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

There exists a minimal del Pezzo surface
S
of degree
2
over a
field of characteristic zero such that
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))=168
.

The goal of this paper is to study automorphism groups
of other del Pezzo surfaces without points
over fields of characteristic
zero, and to bound their Jordan constants.
Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero, and let
S
be a del Pezzo
surface of degree
d
over
\mathbb{K}
. Suppose that
S
has no
\mathbb{K}
-points.
The following assertions hold.

(i)

If
d=2
, then
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\leqslant 168
.

(ii)

If
d=4
, then
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\leqslant 2
.

(iii)

If
d=6
, then
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\leqslant 4
.

(iv)

If
d=8
, then
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\leqslant 7200
.

Furthermore, each of the above bounds is attained
over a suitable field
\mathbb{K}
.

Remark 1.8
.

We remind the reader that a del Pezzo surface of degree
1
,
5
, or
7

always has a point over the base field
(see Theorem
2.2
below).

In certain cases we also obtain bounds for Jordan constants for automorphism groups of minimal
del Pezzo surfaces, which turn out to be stronger than the bounds without minimality assumption,
see Lemmas
4.1
and
5.4
,
and Examples
5.5

and
5.6
.
Also, we make the following observation about groups of birational
automorphisms of
minimal del Pezzo surfaces of low degree.

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero, and let
S
be a minimal del Pezzo
surface of degree
d
over
\mathbb{K}
. Suppose that

S
has no
\mathbb{K}
-points.
The following assertions hold.

(i)

If
d=2
, then
J(\operatorname{Bir}(S))\leqslant 168
.

(ii)

If
d=3
, then the group
\operatorname{Bir}(S)
is abelian.

Furthermore, the bound from assertion (i)
is attained over a suitable field
\mathbb{K}
.

It would be interesting to estimate Jordan constants
for automorphism groups and birational automorphism groups of minimal del Pezzo surfaces
of other degrees without points.

The plan of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
we collect some useful auxiliary facts
about points on del Pezzo surfaces over algebraically non-closed fields.
In Sections
3
,
4
, and
5

we estimate the Jordan constants of automorphism
groups of del Pezzo surfaces of degrees
4
,
6
, and
8
,
respectively. In Section
6
we
complete the proofs of Theorem
1.7

and Proposition
1.9
.

Notation and conventions.

We denote by
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
the cyclic group of order
n
,
by
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
the symmetric group on
n
letters,
and by
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
the alternating group on
n
letters.

Given a field
\mathbb{K}
, we denote by
\bar{\mathbb{K}}
its algebraic closure.
For a variety
X
defined over
\mathbb{K}
, we denote by
X_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}

its scalar extension to
\bar{\mathbb{K}}
.
By a point of degree
d
on a variety defined over
\mathbb{K}
we mean a closed point whose
residue field is an extension of
\mathbb{K}
of degree
d
;
a
\mathbb{K}
-point is a point of degree
1
.

A del Pezzo surface is a smooth projective surface with an ample anticanonical class.
For a del Pezzo surface
S
, by its degree we mean its (anti)canonical degree
K_{S}^{2}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect some well known auxiliary facts
concerning points on certain del Pezzo surfaces. The first fact is a general
theorem of Lang and Nishimura.

Theorem 2.1

(see e.g.
[
VA13
, Lemma 1.1]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective varieties over a field
\mathbb{K}
.
Suppose that
X
is birational to
Y
. Then
X
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point if and only if
Y
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
S
be a del Pezzo
surface of degree
d
over a field
\mathbb{K}
.
Suppose that either
d\in\{1,5,7\}
, or
d=8
and
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}\not\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Then
S
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point.

Proof.

If
d=1
, then the unique base point of the anticanonical linear system
|-K_{S}|
is defined over
\mathbb{K}
.
If
d=5
, then
S
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point by
a result going back to Enriques, see for instance
[
VA13
, Theorem 2.5]
.
If either
d=7
, or
d=8
and
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}\not\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then

S
contains a
(-1)
-curve which is defined over
\mathbb{K}
.
A
(-1)
-curve is a line in the anticanonical embedding of
S
,
so that this line (and hence the surface
S
) contains a
\mathbb{K}
-point.
∎

The following theorem is not necessary for the proofs of our results;
however, we mention it to introduce its analogs which
will actually be used.

Theorem 2.3

(see
[
Cor77
]
)
.

Let
S
be a del Pezzo
surface of degree
d\geqslant 4
over a perfect field
\mathbb{K}
.
Suppose that
S
has a point of degree coprime to
d
.
Then
S
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point.

In some cases, a statement similar to Theorem
2.3

holds for singular analogs of del Pezzo surfaces.

Lemma 2.4

(cf. the proof of
[
Shr20c
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
be a complete intersection of two quadrics
over an infinite perfect field
\mathbb{K}
.
Suppose that
S
has isolated singularities.
Suppose that
S

has a point of degree
3
over
\mathbb{K}
. Then
S
has a smooth point defined over
\mathbb{K}
.

Proof.

Since
S
is a complete intersection with isolated singularities, it follows
from Hartshorne’s connectedness theorem that

S
is irreducible, see e.g.
[
Eis95
, Theorem 18.12]
.

Let
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
, and
P_{3}
be the three points of
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
which form a
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
-orbit.
Suppose that the points
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
, and
P_{3}
are collinear, and
consider the line
L

in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
passing through them. Then
L

is
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
-invariant and thus defined over
\mathbb{K}
.
Since it has at least three common points with the surface
S
,
and
S
is an intersection of quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
,
we conclude that
L
is contained in
S
.
Since
L
has an infinite number of
\mathbb{K}
-points, and the singularities of
S

are isolated, we see that
S
has a smooth
\mathbb{K}
-point.

Therefore, we can assume that the points
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
, and
P_{3}
are not collinear.
Let
\Pi
be the plane in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
passing through
these three points. Then
\Pi

is
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
-invariant and thus defined over
\mathbb{K}
.
Note that
\Pi
is not contained in
S
, because
S
is irreducible.
Thus, the intersection
\Lambda=S\cap\Pi
coincides with an intersection of two curves
C_{1},C_{2}\subset\Pi

of degree
2
(defined over
\bar{\mathbb{K}}
).

Suppose that
\Lambda
is one-dimensional.
Then the union of its one-dimensional irreducible components is either a line
L
,
or a pair of lines
L_{1}\cup L_{2}
(each defined over
\bar{\mathbb{K}}
),
or a geometrically irreducible conic
C
.
In the first case, the line
L

is defined over
\mathbb{K}
, and thus contains
an infinite number of
\mathbb{K}
-points.
In the other two cases,
\Lambda
coincides with the curve
L_{1}\cup L_{2}
or
C
, respectively;
in other words,
\Lambda
has no zero-dimensional irreducible components.
If
\Lambda=L_{1}\cup L_{2}
, then none of the points
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
, and
P_{3}

can coincide with the intersection point
L_{1}\cap L_{2}
, because the latter is defined over
\mathbb{K}
.
Thus, one of the lines
L_{1}
or
L_{2}
contains at least two of the points
P_{i}
, while the other
contains at most one. Hence the lines
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are defined over
\mathbb{K}
,
so that
S
contains an infinite number of
\mathbb{K}
-points.
If
\Lambda=C
is a geometrically irreducible conic, then
C
is defined over
\mathbb{K}

and has a point of degree
3
over
\mathbb{K}
. This implies that
C
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point.
Therefore, one has
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which means that
C
(and hence
S
) contains an infinite number of

\mathbb{K}
-points. In each of the three cases, we conclude that
S
has a smooth
\mathbb{K}
-point, because the
singularities of
S
are isolated.

Finally, suppose that
\Lambda
is zero-dimensional.
Then
|\Lambda_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}|\leqslant 4
. This implies that the curves
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are smooth
at each of the points
P_{i}
, and intersect each other transversally at these points. Hence
there exists the fourth point
P_{4}\in\Lambda_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
different from
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
, and
P_{3}
; furthermore,
the curves
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are smooth and
intersect each other transversally at
P_{4}
. So we see that
P_{4}
is a smooth point of
S
defined over
\mathbb{K}
.
∎

Recall that a weak del Pezzo surface is a smooth projective surface
such that its anticanonical class is nef and big. The degree of a weak del Pezzo surface is
defined as the square of its (anti)canonical class.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
S
be a weak del Pezzo surface of degree
4
over an infinite perfect field
\mathbb{K}
. Suppose that
S

has a point of degree
3
over
\mathbb{K}
. Then
S
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point.

Proof.

Consider the anticanonical morphism

\psi\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{4},

and denote its image by
S^{\prime}
. Then

\psi
is a birational
morphism of
S
to
S^{\prime}
, and
S^{\prime}
is a surface with Du Val singularities.
In particular, the singularities of
S^{\prime}
are isolated. Furthermore,
S^{\prime}
is a complete
intersection of two quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
.

Suppose that the points
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
, and
P_{3}
are
mapped to one point
R\in S^{\prime}
by
\psi
. Then
R
is defined over
\mathbb{K}
, and

\Gamma=\psi^{-1}(R)
is a configuration of
(-2)
-curves with the action
of the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
. Hence the dual graph
\Gamma^{\vee}
of
\Gamma
is a Dynkin diagram
of one of the types
\mathrm{A}_{n}
with
n\leqslant 5
,
\mathrm{D}_{4}
, or
\mathrm{D}_{5}
,
see
[
Dol12
, §8.6.3]
.
If
\Gamma^{\vee}
is of type
\mathrm{A}_{n}
with even
n
, then
\Gamma
, and hence
S
, contains a

\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
-invariant point, i.e. a
\mathbb{K}
-point.
If
\Gamma^{\vee}
is either of type
\mathrm{A}_{n}
with odd
n
, or of type
\mathrm{D}_{5}
, then

\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma^{\vee})\subset{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
; since
\Gamma
contains a
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
-orbit
\{P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}\}
of length
3
, we see that
there exists an irreducible component
C
of
\Gamma
which is
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
-invariant
and contains an odd number of points
P_{i}
. Since
C_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, this implies that
C
,
and thus also
S
, has a
\mathbb{K}
-point.
If
\Gamma^{\vee}\cong\mathrm{D}_{4}
, then the “central” irreducible component
C
of
\Gamma

is
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
-invariant, and contains a
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
-invariant subset of cardinality
3

formed by the intersections with other irreducible components of
\Gamma
.
Since
C_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we again
conclude that
C
and
S
have
\mathbb{K}
-points.

Therefore, we may assume that
\psi
maps the points
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
, and
P_{3}

to different points of
S^{\prime}_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
, which means that
S^{\prime}
has a point of degree
3
over
\mathbb{K}
.
Hence
S^{\prime}
contains a smooth
\mathbb{K}
-point
P
by
Lemma
2.4
. Since the fiber

\psi^{-1}(P)_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}\subset S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
over
P
consists of one point,
this point is defined over
\mathbb{K}
.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
S
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
6
over an infinite perfect field
\mathbb{K}
. Suppose that
S

has a point of degree
2
and a point of degree
3
over
\mathbb{K}
. Then
S
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point.

Proof.

By assumption, the surface
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
contains a
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
-orbit

\{P_{1},P_{2}\}
of length
2
.
Suppose that
P_{1}
(and thus also
P_{2}
) is contained in a
(-1)
-curve.
Recall that the dual graph of the configuration of
(-1)
-curves on
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}

is a hexagon. If both
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are contained in the same
(-1)
-curve, then this

(-1)
-curve is unique and thus defined over
\mathbb{K}
; hence
S
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point.
Therefore, we can assume that each
(-1)
-curve on
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
contains at most one point among
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
.
Thus the blow up of
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}
is a weak del Pezzo surface
\tilde{S}
of degree
4
over
\mathbb{K}
.
It follows from Theorem
2.1
that
\tilde{S}
has a point of degree
3
.
Hence
\tilde{S}
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point by Lemma
2.5
.
This implies that
S
has a
\mathbb{K}
-point.
∎

3.
Del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4

In this section we find an estimate for Jordan constants
of automorphism groups of del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4

without points. First of all, let us introduce some notation.

Let
S
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
4
over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero.
Then
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
is isomorphic to a blow up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
at five points
P_{1},\,P_{2},\,P_{3},\,P_{4},\,P_{5}
in general position. Denote by

\pi\colon S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}

this blow up. Denote by

E_{1},\ E_{2},\ E_{3},\ E_{4},\ E_{5}

the preimages under
the morphism
\pi
of
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
,
P_{3}
,
P_{4}
, and
P_{5},
respectively.
Denote by
Q
the proper transform under the morphism
\pi
of the (smooth)
conic passing through all the points
P_{1},

P_{2},

P_{3},

P_{4}
, and
P_{5}
.
Denote by
L_{ij}

for
i,j\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}
,
i\neq j
,
the proper transform under
\pi
of the line passing through
P_{i}
and
P_{j}

(here the index
ij
is considered to be non-ordered).
Then
Q
,
L_{ij}
, and
E_{i}
give a set of
16
lines on
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
;
these are all the lines contained in
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
.
Their intersections are:

\displaystyle E_{i}^{2}=Q^{2}=L_{ij}^{2}=-1;

\displaystyle E_{i}\cdot E_{j}=0\quad\text{for}\quad i\neq j;

\displaystyle E_{i}\cdot Q=L_{ij}\cdot E_{i}=L_{ij}\cdot Q=1\quad\text{for}\quad i\neq j;

\displaystyle L_{ij}\cdot L_{kl}=1,\quad\text{where}\;i,j,k,l\in\{1,\ldots,5\}\;\text{are pairwise distinct;}

\displaystyle L_{ij}\cdot L_{jk}=0,\quad\text{where}\;i,j,k\in\{1,\ldots,5\}\;\text{are pairwise distinct}.

The Weyl group

W(\mathrm{D}_{5})\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}^{4}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{5}

acts on the Picard group
\mathrm{Pic}(S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}})
by orthogonal transformations with respect to the intersection form. This action fixes the canonical class

K_{S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}}
and preserves the cone of effective divisors. The
subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{5}\subset W(\mathrm{D}_{5})
acts on the set of lines on
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
by permutation of indices.
The normal subgroup
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}^{4}\subset W(\mathrm{D}_{5})

consists of two types of involutions
\iota_{ab}
and
\iota_{abcd},

where
\{a,b\}
and
\{a,b,c,d\}
are subsets of cardinality
2
and
4

in the set
\{1,2,3,4,5\}
, respectively (thus, the indices
ab
and
abcd

are considered to be non-ordered). These involutions act on the set of lines as follows:

\displaystyle\iota_{ab}(E_{a})=E_{b};

\displaystyle\iota_{ab}(E_{c})=L_{de},\quad\text{where}\quad\{a,b,c,d,e\}=\{1,2,3,4,5\};

\displaystyle\iota_{abcd}(L_{ab})=L_{cd};

\displaystyle\iota_{abcd}(E_{a})=L_{ae}\quad\text{and}\quad\iota_{abcd}(E_{e})=Q,\quad\text{where}\quad\{a,b,c,d,e\}=\{1,2,3,4,5\};

we refer the reader to
[
DI09
, §6.4]
for details.
Furthermore,
W(\mathrm{D}_{5})
is the group of
all
automorphisms of the Picard group
\mathrm{Pic}(S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}})

preserving the intersection form and the canonical class, see
[
Man86
, Theorem 23.9]
.
The action on the lines on
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}
provides homomorphisms of the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})

and the automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(S)

to
W(\mathrm{D}_{5})
, and the latter homomorphism is injective,
see
[
Dol12
, Corollary 8.2.40]
.
Observe that the images of
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{K}}/\mathbb{K})
and
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
in
W(\mathrm{D}_{5})
commute with each other.

Let us record some properties of the elements of the Weyl group
W(\mathrm{D}_{5})

which we will need in this section.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following assertions hold.

(i)

There is a unique conjugacy class of elements of order
5
in
W(\mathrm{D}_{5})
.

(ii)

There is a unique conjugacy class of elements of order
3
in
W(\mathrm{D}_{5})
.

(iii)

Let
\rho\colon W(\mathrm{D}_{5})\to\mathfrak{S}_{5}

be the natural projection. Let
g\in W(\mathrm{D}_{5})
be an element such that

\rho(g)
has order
4
.
Then
g
is conjugate either to

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

We will need the following simple group-theoretic lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a group which fits into an exact sequence

where

and

.
Then

.

Since

is abelian,
the action of (any) preimages of elements of

Since

, the (unique) element

of order

is
contained in the kernel of

. Let
\tilde{g}\in G
be some preimage of

.
We conclude that
\tilde{g}
commutes with

, and hence
\tilde{g}
and

generate an abelian subgroup

.
The index of

.
∎

The next lemma describes the action of an automorphism on the blow up of
a fixed point.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\bar{\mathbb{K}}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
Let
\hat{S}
be a smooth surface over
\mathbb{K}
, and let

be an automorphism of finite order
n
. Let

be a fixed point of

, and
suppose that

acts as
\mathrm{diag}(\xi^{m},\xi^{k})
on the Zariski tangent

to
\hat{S}
at

,
where
\xi
is a primitive

-th root of unity,
and

.
Let

be the blow up of
\hat{S}
at
P
, and let

be its exceptional divisor.
Then under the natural action of

on

-fixed points

and

on

on

\mathrm{diag}(\xi^{k-m},\xi^{m})
, and

on

is given
by
\mathrm{diag}(\xi^{m-k},\xi^{k})
.

It is enough to prove the assertion in the case

.
Furthermore, there is an analytic neighborhood of
P
in
\hat{S}

which can be identified with an analytic neighborhood of

in

,
and this identification can be chosen equivariant with respect to the group

, see e.g.
[
Akh95
, §2.2]
.
Therefore, we may assume that

\hat{S}\cong\mathbb{A}^{2}
, the point

is

,
and

is a linear automorphism.

Let

and

be coordinates on

such that

and

.
The surface

is given in

by

and

are homogeneous coordinates

and

A neighborhood of the point

in

with coordinates

,

, and

.

at the point

defined by equation

The element

acts on

the element

The assertion about the action on

.

,

\Gamma\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3}\times{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
.
Since
\Xi
commutes with
\Gamma
, we see that in the former case one has
\Xi\subset{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{6}
, and in the latter two cases

\Xi
is a subgroup of the center
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{3}\times{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
. However, we already know that the case when
\Xi\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{6}
is impossible.
Hence in the former case one has
|\Xi|\leqslant 3
. Therefore, in all possible cases we have

J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\leqslant|\Xi|\leqslant 3.

∎

Let us show that the first bound provided by Lemma
4.1

is attained.

Example 4.2
.

Let
C
be a conic over the field
\mathbb{R}
defined in
\mathbb{P}^{2}

with homogeneous coordinates
x
,
y
, and
z
by equation

(4.2)

x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=0.

Set
\hat{S}=C\times C
, and let

\Sigma=\{(1:\sqrt{-1}:0)\times(1:\sqrt{-1}:0),\quad(1:-\sqrt{-1}:0)\times(1:-\sqrt{-1}:0)\}.

Thus,
\Sigma
is a
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})
-orbit of length
2
on
C\times C
. Let
S\to\hat{S}
be the blow up of

\Sigma
. Then
S
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
6
, and
S
has no
\mathbb{R}
-points, because
\hat{S}
has no
\mathbb{R}
-points.

Observe that for any positive integer
n
there is an action of the group
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
on
C
such that a generator of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
acts as

(4.3)

(x:y:z)\mapsto\left(x\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}+y\sin\frac{2\pi}{n}:-x\sin\frac{2\pi}{n}+y\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}:z\right),

and the points
(1:\sqrt{-1}:0),(1:-\sqrt{-1}:0)\in C(\mathbb{C})

are fixed under this action.
Let the group
G\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}^{2}\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}^{2}
act on

\hat{S}
so that the generators of the factors of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}^{2}
act
as in (
4.3
)
on the first and the second factor of
C\times C
, respectively;
the non-trivial element
\iota_{1}
of the first
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
acts as

(x_{1}:y_{1}:z_{1})\times(x_{2}:y_{2}:z_{2})\mapsto(x_{1}:-y_{1}:z_{1})\times(x_{2}:-y_{2}:z_{2}),

while the non-trivial element
\iota_{2}
of the second
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
acts as

(x_{1}:y_{1}:z_{1})\times(x_{2}:y_{2}:z_{2})\mapsto(x_{2}:y_{2}:z_{2})\times(x_{1}:y_{1}:z_{1}).

Then the set
\Sigma
is
G
-invariant, and so
G\subset\operatorname{Aut}(S)
.
Observe that, as predicted by Lemma
4.1
, one has
J(G)\leqslant 4
, because the index of the normal
abelian subgroup
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}^{2}
in
G
equals
4
.

Now suppose that
n\geqslant 5
is a prime number.
Obviously, the elements
\iota_{1}
,
\iota_{2}
, and
\iota_{1}\iota_{2}

do not centralize
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}^{2}
. Hence any abelian subgroup
A
of
G
which
is not contained in
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}^{2}
intersects

{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}^{2}
by a proper subgroup therein. Since
n
is prime,
this implies that

\frac{|G|}{|A|}\geqslant\frac{|{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}^{2}|}{|A\cap{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}^{2}|}\geqslant n>4.

Therefore, we have
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))=4
.

5.
Del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8

In this section we find an estimate for Jordan constants
of automorphism groups of del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
.

Since every del Pezzo surface is geometrically rational,
Theorem
1.2
implies

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
S
be a del Pezzo surface over a field of characteristic
zero. Then one has
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\leqslant 7200
.

Let us show that the bound provided by Corollary
5.1

is attained.

Example 5.2
.

Let
C
be a conic over the field
\mathbb{R}
defined in
\mathbb{P}^{2}

by equation (
4.2
).
Note that
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
contains the alternating group
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
.
Let
S=C\times C
. Then
S
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
8

without
\mathbb{R}
-points, and

\operatorname{Aut}(S)
contains a subgroup

G\cong(\mathfrak{A}_{5}\times\mathfrak{A}_{5})\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}.

Thus

J\big(\operatorname{Aut}(S)\big)\geqslant J(G)=|G|=7200.

Therefore, by Corollary
5.1
we have
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))=7200
.

For minimal del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
(i.e. for those
with Picard rank
1
), we can
obtain a better bound for the Jordan constant of the automorphism group
than the bound provided by Corollary
5.1
.
To do this, we need the following simple fact.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
C
be a conic over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero. Then

J\big(\operatorname{Aut}(C)\big)\leqslant 60.

Furthermore,
if a group
\Gamma
contains
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
as a subgroup of index
2
,
then
J(\Gamma)\leqslant 120
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from the well known classification of finite
subgroups of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
.

To prove the second assertion, let
G\subset\Gamma
be a finite subgroup.
Recall that the constant
J(G)
is bounded by a square of the index of any abelian subgroup
in
G
, see e.g.
[
Isa08
, Theorem 1.41]
.
The intersection

G^{\prime}=G\cap\operatorname{Aut}(C)\subset\Gamma

is a subgroup of index at most
2
in
G
.
If
G^{\prime}
is cyclic or dihedral, then
G^{\prime}
contains a cyclic subgroup of index at most
2
;
thus
G
contains a cyclic subgroup of index at most
4
, which gives

J(G)\leqslant 4^{2}=16
. On the other hand,
if
G^{\prime}
is one of the three exceptional finite groups

\mathfrak{A}_{4}
,
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
, or
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
,
then
J(G)\leqslant|G|\leqslant 120
.
∎

Using Lemma
5.3
, we deduce

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
S
be a minimal del Pezzo surface of degree
8
over a field
\mathbb{K}

of characteristic zero.
Then
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\leqslant 120
.

Proof.

Since
S
is minimal, we have
S_{\bar{\mathbb{K}}}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Moreover, according to
[
SV18
, Proposition 7.3]
, the surface
S

is the Weil restriction of scalars of some conic
C
defined over a quadratic extension
\mathbb{L}
of
\mathbb{K}
, and

\operatorname{Aut}(S)
contains a subgroup of index at most
2
isomorphic to
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
.
Now the assertion follows from Lemma
5.3
.
∎

The bound provided by Lemma
5.4

is attained for minimal del Pezzo surfaces of degree
8
without points over the field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
.

Example 5.5
.

Let
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
.
Then a three-dimensional irreducible representation
of the icosahedral group
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
is defined over
\mathbb{L}
:
indeed, the matrices

M_{(12345)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&\cos\frac{2\pi}{5}&-\sin\frac{2\pi}{5}\\
0&\sin\frac{2\pi}{5}&\cos\frac{2\pi}{5}\end{array}\right),\quad M_{(12)(34)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}&\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}&0\\
\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}&0\\
0&0&-1\end{array}\right)

satisfy the relations

M_{(12345)}^{5}=M_{(12)(34)}^{2}=\big(M_{(12345)}M_{(12)(34)}\big)^{3}=1,

which imply that they generate a group isomorphic to
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
,
see e.g.
[
Hup67
, Beispiel 19.9]
.
Let
C
be a conic over the field
\mathbb{L}
defined in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
by equation (
4.2
).
Then
C
is the extension of scalars of the conic defined by the same equation (
4.2
)
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and
C
does not contain
\mathbb{L}
-points.
The group
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
generated by
M_{(12345)}
and
M_{(12)(34)}
acts on
C
.
Note that the Galois group

\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{Q})\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}

acts on the group
\mathfrak{A}_{5}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(C)
by an involution which is not an inner automorphism.

Let
S
be the Weil restriction of scalars of
C
. Then

S
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
8
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and
S
does not contain
\mathbb{Q}
-points.
According to
[
SV18
, Proposition 7.3]
, the surface
S
is minimal, and

\operatorname{Aut}(S)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(C)\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}.

The latter group contains the non-trivial semi-direct product
\mathfrak{A}_{5}\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{5}
.
Thus

J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\geqslant J(\mathfrak{S}_{5})=|\mathfrak{S}_{5}|=120.

Therefore, by Lemma
5.4
we have
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))=120
.

Example 5.6
.

Consider the smooth quadric surface
S
defined by equations

x+y+z+t+w=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+t^{2}+w^{2}=0

in the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{4}
over
\mathbb{Q}
with homogeneous coordinates

x
,
y
,
z
,
t
, and
w
.
Obviously,
S
has no
\mathbb{Q}
-points.
Furthermore, the group
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
acts on
\mathbb{P}^{4}
by permutations of homogeneous coordinates, and preserves the
surface
S
. Thus, we have

J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\geqslant J(\mathfrak{S}_{5})=|\mathfrak{S}_{5}|=120.

Observe that
S
is minimal. Indeed, otherwise

S
has two structures of conic bundles defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and a subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
of index at most
2

preserves each of these conic bundles. In other words, the group
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
must act
on a conic defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, which is impossible, because
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
has no non-trivial three-dimensional
representations over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Therefore, Lemma
5.4
gives
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))=120
.

We point out that the surface
S
is isomorphic to the surface described
in Example
5.5
. Indeed,
it is minimal over
\mathbb{Q}
, and its extension of scalars to
some quadratic extension
\mathbb{L}
of
\mathbb{Q}
has Picard rank
2
. This implies that
S

is a Weil restriction of scalars
of some conic
C
defined over the field
\mathbb{L}
, and
C
must be acted on
by a subgroup of index at most
2
in
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
. Thus, there is an action of

\mathfrak{A}_{5}
on
C
. Hence
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
, and
C
is isomorphic
to the conic defined by equation (
4.2
), because conics over other quadratic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
,
as well as other conics over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
,
do not contain
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
in their automorphism groups,
see for instance
[
GA13
, Theorem 1.2(c)]
.

Remark 5.7
.

According to
[
Zai24a
, Theorem 1.2(4)]
, if
S
is a smooth quadric surface
with a point over the field
\mathbb{Q}
, then
J(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\leqslant 8
.

In this section we complete the proofs of Theorem

.

If

,

4.2

∎

Proof of Proposition

Observe that a cubic surface without

-points also has no

over

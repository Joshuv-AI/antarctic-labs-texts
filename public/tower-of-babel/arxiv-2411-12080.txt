Controlled Occupied Processes and Viscosity Solutions
H. Mete Soner, Valentin Tissot-Daguette, Jianfeng Zhang

Abstract
We consider the optimal control of occupied processes which record all positions of the state process. Dynamic programming yields nonlinear equations on the space of positive measures. We develop the viscosity theory for this infinite dimensional parabolic occupied PDE by proving a comparison result between sub and supersolutions, and thus provide a characterization of the value function as the unique viscosity solution. Toward this proof, an extension of the celebrated Crandall-Ishii-Lions (second order) Lemma to this setting, as well as finite-dimensional approximations, is established. Examples including the occupied heat equation, and pricing PDEs of financial derivatives contingent on the occupation measure are also discussed.



Keywords:
Stochastic optimal control, occupation flow, occupied PDEs, viscosity solutions

Introduction

This paper introduces a class of path-dependent stochastic control problems involving the occupation
measure and develops a viscosity theory for the associated dynamic programming equation.
We consider the following control problem,

\displaystyle\inf_{\alpha\in{\mathscr{A}}}\E^{\Q}[\int_{0}^{T}\ell(\calO_{t}^{\alpha},X_{t}^{\alpha},\alpha_{t})dt+g(\calO_{T}^{\alpha},X_{T}^{\alpha})],

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{\alpha}=b(\calO_{t}^{\alpha},X_{t}^{\alpha},\alpha_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\calO_{t}^{\alpha},X_{t}^{\alpha},\alpha_{t})\mathrm{d}W_{t},

(1.2)

where
\calO^{\alpha}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{X_{s}^{\alpha}}ds
is the

standard time occupation flow of
X^{\alpha}
. The latter is a measure-valued process
that describes the cumulative time spent by
X^{\alpha}
in arbitrary regions.
Other clocks, possibly stochastic, may be used in the definition of
\calO^{\alpha}
; see
Section

3.1
.
The pair
(\calO^{\alpha},X^{\alpha})
, referred to as the
occupied process

[
35
,
36
]
,
induces a Markovian framework that lies strictly between
the classical (path-independent) setting,
and fully path-dependent models.

Our main result characterizes the value function associated to (
1.1
)
as the unique viscosity solution to a parabolic
occupied PDE

which in standard time takes the form

-\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}u+{\mathscr{H}}(\mathfrak{o},x,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0.

(1.3)

Here, the variable
\mathfrak{o}
represents the value of the occupation flow
{\mathcal{O}}^{\alpha}
and lives in
the linear space
{\mathscr{M}}
of finite Borel measures.
Importantly,
\mathfrak{o}

generalizes
the time variable in parabolic PDEs, and
the
occupation derivative

\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}
in (
1.3
)
replaces the usual time derivative.
As we argue in
Section

2.1
, this differential is directly tied to the dynamics of the occupation flow and corresponds to a local projection of the linear derivative commonly used in mean-field games
[
10
,
11
,
25
]
.

Alternatively, one can formulate the control problem (
1.1
) using a pathwise setting and the tools from Dupire’s functional Itô calculus

[
15
]
.
The associated dynamic programming equation is a
path-dependent PDE
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
29
,
37
]
which,
in the canonical setting, reads

-\partial_{t}u+{\mathscr{H}}(t,\omega,\partial_{\omega}u,\partial^{2}_{\omega}u)=0,

(1.4)

where
u=u(t,\omega)
,
(t,\omega)\in\R_{+}\times\ \Omega
and

\Omega=\calC([0,T];\R^{d})
is the space of continuous paths on
\R^{d}
.
The differential
\partial_{t}
denotes the functional time derivative in the sense of Dupire,
while
\partial_{\omega}
is the functional space derivative.
Comparing equations (

) and (

), we observe that
occupied PDEs correspond to the change of coordinates

\R_{+}\times\ \Omega\ni(t,\omega)\;\longrightarrow\;({\mathcal{O}}_{t}^{\alpha}(\omega),X_{t}^{\alpha}(\omega))=(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{M}}\times\R^{d}.

In particular, the path is captured by the occupation flow, and the space variable becomes finite-dimensional.
Consequently, the space derivatives in (
1.3
) are the classical ones, and the path dependence only
appears through a first-order term given by the occupation derivative. Many questions related to
fully nonlinear parabolic path-dependent PDEs remains open,
especially regarding the regularity of solutions. Additionally,
the comparison principle in this context is highly involved and necessitate additional
technical tools
[
18
,
19
,
38
]
.
In contrast, we here provide a nearly classical proof
of the comparison principle for occupied PDEs, covering a large class of
path-dependent PDEs.

It is important to note that the occupation measure
extracts aggregate features of the path and in particular erases its chronology.
In fact, one can show the opposite, namely that for fixed
t\geq 0
, any chronology-invariant path functional
\omega\mapsto F(t,\omega)
is necessarily function of the occupation measure
[
35
, Theorem 2.1]
.
While some path-dependent control problems cannot be expressed as (
1.1
), e.g those involving time delays
[
23
,
32
]
,
the present framework still includes a vast array of path-dependent problems, notably in finance
[
35
]
.

It is now classical that the notion of viscosity solutions
is the appropriate framework for nonlinear PDEs,
delivering a complete theory including the existence and uniqueness of such type of solutions

[
13
,
21
]
.
In our context it is particularly needed, as the regularity of solutions
has not been established and is not expected for
non-linear equations.
A central tool in the analysis of
second-order nonlinear PDEs is the well-known Crandall-Ishii-Lions lemma
[
13
]
which we generalize to our context.
This is achieved by projecting the problem to a finite-dimensional space
using
cylindrical functions
, and the corresponding metric induced by
the so-called cylindrical norm introduced in
Section

2.2
.

Classically, the comparison principle for viscosity solutions requires compactness assumptions
on the domain so that upper/lower semicontinuous functions attain their maximum/minimum
[
13
,
21
]
.
Herein, we note that the space
{\mathscr{M}}\times\R^{d}
is not compact with respect to the product topology.
However, it is locally compact, and the existence of extrema is guaranteed by introducing
coercive approximations

as discussed in
Section

4.1
. The lack of compactness is typically reconciled with the
Ekeland-Borwein-Preiss variational principle
[
16
,
5
]
. While
this powerful, yet complex tool may be applied to our context,
it is actually not needed here due to the locally compact structure of
{\mathscr{M}}\times\R^{d}
; see again
Section

4.1
.

v(\mathfrak{o},x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf_{\alpha\in{\mathscr{A}}}J(\mathfrak{o},x,\alpha),\qquad(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{D}}_{T}.

(3.8)

In what follows, we make the
following standing assumption.

Assumption 3.4
.

There exists
c_{*}\geq 1

such that
\lambda
,
b
,
\sigma
,
\ell
, and
g
all
satisfy growth and Lipschitz conditions with
c_{*}
. Moreover,

\lambda(\mathfrak{o},x,a)\geq 1/c_{*},\qquad\forall\ (\mathfrak{o},x,a)\in{\mathscr{D}}_{T}\times A.

(3.9)

We remark that we require the nondegeneracy of
\lambda
, but not that of
\sigma
. In particular,
condition (
3.9
) gives a lower bound on the total mass process, namely

\Lambda_{t}^{\alpha}=|{\mathcal{O}}_{0}^{\alpha}|+\int_{0}^{t}\lambda(\calO_{s}^{\alpha},X_{s}^{\alpha},\alpha_{s})\,ds\geq t/c_{*},\qquad t\geq 0.

Hence the exit time in (
3.7
) satisfies
\tau^{\alpha}\leq c_{*}T
, ensuring that
the objective function
J
is finite. If
\lambda\equiv 1
(standard time occupation flow), then (
3.9
) holds with
c_{*}=1
and we have
\tau^{\alpha}\equiv T
. If
\lambda=\lVert\sigma\rVert_{F}^{2}
(occupation flow), then (
3.9
) can be seen as a weak ellipticity condition on
\sigma
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Under Assumption
3.4
, the value function is locally
1/2-
Hölder
continuous with respect to
\rho
, that is, for all
\delta>0

there exists
\hat{c}>0
that depends on
c_{*}
,
T
, and
\delta
such that

\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})\leq\delta\;\Longrightarrow\;|v(\mathfrak{o},x)-v(\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x^{\prime})|\leq\hat{c}\ \rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})^{1/2}.

(3.10)

Proof.

See
Appendix

B
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

In standard stochastic control theory, typically the value function is
1/2-
Hölder continuous in
t
, but is Lipschitz continuous in
x
. Here, however,
v
is only
1/2-
Hölder continuous in
x
in general. The reason is that the exit time
\tau^{\alpha}
may depend on
x
. For example, let
d=1
,
b\equiv 0
,
\sigma\equiv 1
,
\ell\equiv 0
, and
\lambda=\lambda(x)\geq 1
,
g=g(x)
are Lipschitz continuous in
x
with Lipschitz constant
C=1
. Then the problem does not involve the control
\alpha
. Given
(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
, the exit time
\tau_{\mathfrak{o},x}=\inf\{t\geq 0\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda(x+W_{s})ds\geq T-|\mathfrak{o}|\}
, and
v(\mathfrak{o},x)=\E^{\Q}[g(x+W_{\tau_{\mathfrak{o},x}})]
. Thus

\displaystyle|v(\mathfrak{o},x)-v(\mathfrak{o},x^{\prime})|\leq|x-x^{\prime}|+\E^{\Q}\big[|W_{\tau_{\mathfrak{o},x}}-W_{\tau_{\mathfrak{o},x^{\prime}}}|\big]

From the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality
[
31
, Chapter IV]
, we can see that the last term above is of order
\E^{\Q}\big[|\tau_{\mathfrak{o},x}-\tau_{\mathfrak{o},x^{\prime}}|^{1/2}\big]\sim|x-x^{\prime}|^{1/2}
.

3.3
Viscosity Solutions and Uniqueness

For
(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
,
\zeta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\theta,\Delta,\Gamma)\in\R\times\R^{d}\times\SSS^{d}
,
the Hamiltonian is given by,

{\mathscr{H}}(\mathfrak{o},x,\zeta)=-\inf_{a\in A}\Big(\lambda(\mathfrak{o},x,a)\ \theta+b(\mathfrak{o},x,a)\cdot\Delta+\frac{1}{2}\textnormal{tr}((\sigma\sigma^{\!\top})(\mathfrak{o},x,a)\Gamma)+\ell(\mathfrak{o},x,a)\Big).

(3.11)

Then, by
[
21
]
the
dynamic programming equation

associated to (
3.7
) is given by

\displaystyle{\mathscr{H}}(\mathfrak{o},x,\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}u,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0

on

\mathring{{\mathscr{D}}_{T}}
,

(3.12a)

\displaystyle u=g,

on

\partial{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
,

(3.12b)

where
\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}u=\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}u(\mathfrak{o},x)
is the occupation derivative. As announced in the Introduction, equation (
3.12a
) is linear in
\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}u
when
\lambda\equiv 1
(standard time) and admits the more familiar expression,

\displaystyle-\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}u+{\mathscr{H}}_{x}(\mathfrak{o},x,\nabla u,\nabla^{2}u)=0,\quad{\mathscr{H}}_{x}(\mathfrak{o},x,\Delta,\Gamma)={\mathscr{H}}(\mathfrak{o},x,0,\Delta,\Gamma).

In general, however,
the control may influence the rate
\lambda
leading to nonlinearities in the occupation derivative as well.

Next, we introduce suitable families of test functions towards a viscosity theory.
For
T>0
,
u\in{\mathscr{U}}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
,
w\in{\mathscr{L}}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
,
and
(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
, following
[
13
,
21
]
we set

\displaystyle\frakS_{+}u(\mathfrak{o},x)=\frakS_{+}^{T}u(\mathfrak{o},x)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\phi\in{\mathscr{C}}^{1,2}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,0=(u-\phi)(\mathfrak{o},x)=\max_{{\mathscr{D}}_{T}}(u-\phi)\},

\displaystyle\frakS_{-}w(\mathfrak{o},x)=\frakS_{-}^{T}w(\mathfrak{o},x)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\phi\in{\mathscr{C}}^{1,2}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,0=(w-\phi)(\mathfrak{o},x)=\min_{{\mathscr{D}}_{T}}(w-\phi)\}.

The definition of viscosity solutions is classical
[
13
,
21
]
.

Definition 3.7
.

(Viscosity solutions)
We say that

•

w\in{\mathscr{L}}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
is a

viscosity supersolution
of
{\mathscr{H}}\geq 0
, if

{\mathscr{H}}(\mathfrak{o},x,\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}\phi,\nabla\phi,\nabla^{2}\phi)\geq 0,\qquad\forall\phi\in\frakS_{-}w(\mathfrak{o},x),\ \ (\mathfrak{o},x)\in\mathring{{\mathscr{D}}_{T}}.

(3.13)

•

u\in{\mathscr{U}}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
is a
viscosity subsolution

of
{\mathscr{H}}\leq 0
, if

{\mathscr{H}}(\mathfrak{o},x,\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}\phi,\nabla\phi,\nabla^{2}\phi)\leq 0,\qquad\forall\phi\in\frakS_{+}u(\mathfrak{o},x),\ \ (\mathfrak{o},x)\in\mathring{{\mathscr{D}}_{T}}.

(3.14)

•

v\in{\mathscr{C}}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
a
viscosity solution
{\mathscr{H}}=0
,

if it is both a viscosity supersolution of
{\mathscr{H}}\geq 0
and a subsolution of
{\mathscr{H}}\leq 0
.

Remark 3.8
.

Following the classical theory,
in the definition of a supersolution we can take
any function (not even measurable) and then consider its
upper semicontinuous envelope. Similarly for
subsolutions, we could consider the lower semicontinuous envelope.

The main result of this paper is the following comparison principle.

Theorem 3.9
.

(Comparison)
Suppose that Assumption
3.4
holds,

u\in{\mathscr{U}}_{b}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
is a viscosity subsolution of
{\mathscr{H}}\leq 0
,

w\in{\mathscr{L}}_{b}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
is a viscosity supersolution of
{\mathscr{H}}\geq 0
,
and
u\leq w
on
\partial{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
. Then,
u\leq w
on
{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
.

We shall devote the next three sections to the proof of this theorem. We now provide the characterization of the value function
v
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Suppose that Assumption
3.4
holds. Then, the value function
v
is the unique viscosity solution of the dynamic programming equation (
3.12a
)–(
3.12b
).

Proof.

Given the regularity in Proposition
3.5
and using the techniques developed in
[
21
]
, it is classical that the value function
v
is a viscosity solution.
Moreover, the uniqueness of viscosity solution is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.9
.
∎

4
Technical tools

In this section, we outline several concepts that we utilize.

4.1
Coercivity and coercive approximations

We use the notion of coercivity to construct
extrema of functions
on a topological space
{\mathscr{X}}
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a function
u\in{\mathscr{U}}({\mathscr{X}})
is

upper coercive
if all hypographs of
u

are sequentially compact, i.e., for every
c\in\R
, every
sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n}
in
{\mathscr{X}}
with
u(\xi_{n})\geq c

has a limit point
\xi^{*}\in{\mathscr{X}}
.

{\mathscr{U}}_{e}({\mathscr{X}})
denotes the set of upper coercive functions.

Similarly, we say that a function

w\in{\mathscr{L}}({\mathscr{X}})
is

lower coercive
if every epigraph of
w

is sequentially compact, and
{\mathscr{L}}_{e}({\mathscr{X}})
denotes the set of lower coercive functions.

A direct consequence of the definition is
the following.

Lemma 4.2
.

Any
u\in{\mathscr{U}}_{e}({\mathscr{X}})
is bounded from above
and achieves its maximum on any closed subset of
{\mathscr{X}}
.
Analogously, any
w\in{\mathscr{L}}_{e}({\mathscr{X}})

is bounded from below and achieves its minimum
on any closed subset of
{\mathscr{X}}
.

Proof.

Consider an upper coercive function
u\in{\mathscr{U}}({\mathscr{X}})
.
Towards a contraposition, suppose that for each
positive integer
n
, there is
\xi_{n}\in{\mathscr{X}}
such that

u(\xi_{n})\geq n
. Since
\{u\geq 1\}
is compact by hypothesis,
on a subsequence
n_{k}
,
\xi_{n_{k}}
is convergent.
Let
\xi_{*}
be the limit point. Since
u
is upper
semicontinuous and real-valued,
\infty>u(\xi_{*})\geq\lim_{k}u(\xi_{n_{k}})=\infty
. Hence,
u
is bounded from above.
Then, the upper semicontinuity
and the upper coercivity implies that the
maximum of
u
on any closed set is achieved.
∎

Recall from
Section

2
that
q(x)=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}\,}
and
set

\vartheta(\mathfrak{o},x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathfrak{o}(q)+q(x),\qquad(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{D}}={\mathscr{M}}_{q}\times\R^{d}.

(4.1)

It is classical that the function
\vartheta\in{\mathscr{L}}_{b}({\mathscr{D}})
, and
for any constant
c>0
,
the sub-level set
\{(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{D}}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\vartheta(\mathfrak{o},x)\leq c\ \}
is compact.
Then,

{\mathscr{D}}=\cup_{m\geq 1}{\mathscr{D}}^{m},\qquad{\mathscr{D}}^{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{D}}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\vartheta(\mathfrak{o},x)\leq m\}.

Although
{\mathscr{D}}
is not compact,
each
{\mathscr{D}}^{m}
is compact giving it a locally
compact structure. Additionally,
\vartheta\in{\mathscr{C}}^{1,2}({\mathscr{D}})

and the derivatives are given by,

\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}\vartheta(\mathfrak{o},x)=q(x),\qquad\nabla\vartheta(\mathfrak{o},x)=\frac{x}{q(x)},\qquad\nabla^{2}\vartheta(\mathfrak{o},x)=\frac{1}{q(x)}\Big(I-\frac{x\otimes x}{q(x)^{2}}\Big).

(4.2)

For
w\in{\mathscr{L}}({\mathscr{D}})
,
u\in{\mathscr{U}}({\mathscr{D}})
, and
\gamma>0
, we
define
coercive approximations
by,

w_{\gamma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=w+\gamma\vartheta,\qquad u^{\gamma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=u-\gamma\vartheta.

(4.3)

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
w\in{\mathscr{L}}_{b}({\mathscr{D}})
,
w_{\gamma}\in{\mathscr{L}}_{e}({\mathscr{D}})
,
and for any
u\in{\mathscr{U}}_{b}({\mathscr{D}})
,
u^{\gamma}\in{\mathscr{U}}_{e}({\mathscr{D}})
.

Proof.

As
\vartheta\in{\mathscr{L}}_{b}({\mathscr{D}})
, it is clear that
w_{\gamma}\in{\mathscr{L}}_{b}({\mathscr{D}})
.
Additionally,
as
w
is bounded from below and
lower semicontinuous, for any constant
c
,

\{w_{\gamma}\leq c\}
is a closed subset of

{\mathscr{D}}^{m_{*}}
with
{m_{*}}=(c-\inf_{\mathscr{D}}w)/\gamma
.
Since
{\mathscr{D}}^{m}
is compact for every
m
,
any closed subset of it is also compact. Hence,

w_{\gamma}
is lower coercive. The proof for
u
is essentially
identical.
∎

4.2
Semijets

Following definition is classical in the theory of
viscosity solutions
[
13
,
21
]
.

Definition 4.4
.

For
(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
, the
parabolic superjet

of
u\in{\mathscr{U}}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
at
(\mathfrak{o},x)
is given by,

{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{1,2}u(\mathfrak{o},x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}\phi(\mathfrak{o},x),\nabla\phi(\mathfrak{o},x),\nabla^{2}\phi(\mathfrak{o},x))\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\phi\in\frakS_{+}u(\mathfrak{o},x)\}.

The
parabolic subjet

of
w\in{\mathscr{L}}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
at
(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
is given by,

{\mathscr{P}}_{-}^{1,2}w(\mathfrak{o},x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}\phi(\mathfrak{o},x),\nabla\phi(\mathfrak{o},x),\nabla^{2}\phi(\mathfrak{o},x))\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\phi\in\frakS_{-}w(\mathfrak{o},x)\}.

Above sets are subsets of
\R\times\R^{d}\times\SSS^{d}
,
and their closures are defined by,

\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{\pm}^{1,2}v(\mathfrak{o},x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\lim_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{n}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\zeta_{n}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{\pm}^{1,2}v(\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n}),\ (\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n},v(\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n}))\rightarrow(\mathfrak{o},x,v(\mathfrak{o},x))\}.

The following equivalent definition of
viscosity solutions follows directly from the
continuity of the Hamiltonian and the definitions.
Indeed,
u\in{\mathscr{U}}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
satisfies (
3.14
) if and only if

{\mathscr{H}}(\mathfrak{o},x,\zeta)\geq 0,\qquad\forall\ \zeta\in\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{-}^{1,2}u(\mathfrak{o},x),\ (\mathfrak{o},x)\in\mathring{{\mathscr{D}}_{T}}.

(4.4)

Similarly,
w\in{\mathscr{L}}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
satisfies (
3.13
) if and only if

{\mathscr{H}}(\mathfrak{o},x,\zeta)\leq 0,\qquad\forall\ \zeta\in\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{+}^{1,2}w(\mathfrak{o},x),\ (\mathfrak{o},x)\in\mathring{{\mathscr{D}}_{T}}.

(4.5)

The following is a direct consequence of the definitions.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
\zeta_{n}\in\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}^{1,2}_{\pm}v(\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n})
, and

(\zeta_{n},\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n},v(\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n}))\to(\zeta,\mathfrak{o},x,v(\mathfrak{o},x))

as
n
tends to infinity.
Then,
\zeta\in\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}^{1,2}_{\pm}v(\mathfrak{o},x)
.

4.3
Finite-dimensional Projections

We use a regularization technique similar
to the one introduced by Lasry and Lions
[
24
]
and
related finite-dimensional projections by Lions
[
26
]

to prove the uniqueness of viscosity solutions
in Hilbert spaces.
Recall the separating class
(f_{k})_{k\in\N}\subset{\mathscr{C}}^{1}_{b}(\R^{d})
of subsection

2.2
.
For a positive integer
K
,
we define the finite-dimensional projections by

\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\mathfrak{o}(f_{1}),\ldots,\mathfrak{o}(f_{K}))\in{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi_{K}({\mathscr{M}}_{T}).

The range
{\mathscr{R}}_{K}
depends on
T
and is a compact subset of
\R^{K}
.
Indeed, as
|\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o})|\leq\varrho(\mathfrak{o})\leq|\mathfrak{o}|\leq T
,
{\mathscr{R}}_{K}
is contained in
\{z\in\R^{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}|z|\leq T\}
.
All the projected objects we define in this subsection, like
{\mathscr{R}}_{K}
,
may depend on
T
, but we suppress this dependence.

We project a given
v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathscr{D}}_{T}\to\R

onto the
projected state space

{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}

as follows,

\displaystyle\Pi^{K}(v)(z,x)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup\{v(\mathfrak{o},x)\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o})=z\},

\displaystyle\Pi_{K}(v)(z,x)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\{v(\mathfrak{o},x)\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o})=z\},\qquad(z,x)\in{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}^{K}(v,z,x)
be the maximizers of the first expression,
and
{\mathscr{M}}_{K}(v,z,x)
be the minimizers of the second expression.
Although these sets might be empty, in view of Lemma
4.2
,

{\mathscr{M}}^{K}(u,z,x)
is non-empty when
u\in{\mathscr{U}}_{e}({\mathscr{D}})
,
and
{\mathscr{M}}_{K}(w,z,x)
is non-empty if
w\in{\mathscr{L}}_{e}({\mathscr{D}})
.
We make the simple yet crucial observation that

\Pi_{K}(v)(\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o}),x)\leq v(\mathfrak{o},x)\leq\Pi^{K}(v)(\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o}),x),\quad\forall(\mathfrak{o},x)\in{\mathscr{D}}.

(4.6)

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
K
,
u\in{\mathscr{U}}_{e}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
,
and
w\in{\mathscr{L}}_{e}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
,
we have
\Pi^{K}(u)\in{\mathscr{U}}_{e}({\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d})

and
\Pi_{K}(w)\in{\mathscr{L}}_{e}({\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d})
.

Proof.

Set
w_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Pi_{K}(w)

and consider a sequence
(z_{n},x_{n})
with

w_{K}(z_{n},x_{n})\leq c
for some constant
c
.
Choose
\mathfrak{o}_{n}\in{\mathscr{M}}_{K}(w,z_{n},x_{n})
.
Then,
w(\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n})=w_{K}(z_{n},x_{n})\leq c
,
and by the coercivity of
w
, there is
(\mathfrak{o}_{*},x_{*})\in{\mathscr{D}}

and a subsequence,
denoted by
n
again, such that

\lim_{n}(\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n})=(\mathfrak{o}_{*},x_{*})
.
Then,
\lim_{n}z_{n}=\lim_{n}\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o}_{n})=\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o}_{*})=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z_{*}
which follows from the weak convergence of
\mathfrak{o}_{n}
to
\mathfrak{o}_{*}
.
Hence,
(z_{*},x_{*})
is a
limit point of the sequence
\{(z_{n},x_{n})\}_{n}
,
proving the compactness of epigraphs of
w_{K}

in the space
{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}
.

Consider a sequence
\{(z_{n},x_{n})\}_{n}

satisfying
\lim_{n}(z_{n},x_{n},w_{K}(z_{n},x_{n}))=(z_{*},x_{*},w_{*})
.
To prove the lower semicontinuity of

w_{K}
, we need to show that
w_{*}\geq w_{K}(z_{*},x_{*})
.
Indeed, by the same argument as above,
any sequence
\mathfrak{o}_{n}\in{\mathscr{M}}_{K}(w,z_{n},x_{n})

has a limit point
\mathfrak{o}_{*}
. In light of
\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o}_{*})=z_{*}

and (
4.6
),
w_{K}(z_{*},x_{*})\leq w(\mathfrak{o}_{*},x_{*})
.
We now use the lower
semicontinuity of
w
to arrive at

w_{*}=\lim_{n}w_{K}(z_{n},x_{n})=\liminf_{n}w(\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n})\geq w(\mathfrak{o}_{*},x_{*})\geq w_{K}(z_{*},x_{*}).

∎

The sub and super differentials and the sub and superjets
in finite dimensional spaces are classical. Indeed,
for any
\tilde{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}\to\R

[

,

]
and define

.
These sets depend on the domain
{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}

of

\displaystyle{\mathscr{P}}_{+}^{1,2}\tilde{v}(z,x)

\displaystyle=\{(\nabla_{z}\phi(z,x),\nabla_{x}\phi(z,x),\nabla_{x}^{2}\phi(z,x))\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\phi\in\frakS_{+}\tilde{v}(z,x)\}

\displaystyle{\mathscr{P}}_{-}^{1,2}\tilde{v}(z,x)

\displaystyle=\{(\nabla_{z}\phi(z,x),\nabla_{x}\phi(z,x),\nabla_{x}^{2}\phi(z,x))\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\phi\in\frakS_{-}\tilde{v}(z,x)\},

where for

\displaystyle\frakS_{+}\tilde{v}(z,x)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\phi\in{\mathscr{C}}^{1,2}(\R^{K}\times\R^{d})\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,0=(\tilde{v}-\phi)(z,x)=\max_{{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}}(\tilde{v}-\phi)\},

\displaystyle\frakS_{-}\tilde{v}(z,x)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\phi\in{\mathscr{C}}^{1,2}(\R^{K}\times\R^{d})\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,0=(\tilde{v}-\phi)(z,x)=\min_{{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}}(\tilde{v}-\phi)\}.

We remark that here we do not require

We map the sub and superjets of

\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{K,\pm}^{1,2}\tilde{v}(z,x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(\tilde{\theta}\cdot(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{K}(x)),\Delta,\Gamma)\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,(\tilde{\theta},\Delta,\Gamma)\in\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{\pm}^{1,2}\tilde{v}(z,x)\},

and

Then,
for any
v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathscr{D}}_{T}\to\R
,
\tilde{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}\to\R
,

{\mathscr{P}}_{\pm}^{1,2}v(\mathfrak{o},x),\ \overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{\pm}^{1,2}v(\mathfrak{o},x),\ {\mathscr{P}}_{K,\pm}^{1,2}\tilde{v}(z,x),\ \overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{K,\pm}^{1,2}\tilde{v}(z,x)\subset\R\times\R^{d}\times\SSS_{d}.

Using the
projection maps
\Pi_{K},\Pi^{K}
,
we connect the semijets in
{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}
with those in
{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For any
(z,x)\in{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}
and

,

{\mathscr{P}}_{K,-}^{1,2}w_{K}(z,x)\subset{\mathscr{P}}_{-}^{1,2}w(\mathfrak{o},x),\quad\forall\mathfrak{o}\in{\mathscr{M}}_{K}(w,z,x),

where
w_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Pi_{K}(w)
. If additionally
w

Proof.

Fix

\varphi\in\frakS_{+}w_{K}(z,x)

Additionally for any

Hence,
\phi\in\frakS_{+}w(\mathfrak{o},x)

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathfrak{o}}\phi(\mathfrak{o},x)(\cdot)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{z}\varphi(\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o}),x)\cdot(f_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,f_{K}(\cdot)),

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}\phi(\mathfrak{o},x)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mathfrak{o}}\phi(\mathfrak{o},x)(x)=\nabla_{z}\varphi(\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o}),x)\cdot(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{K}(x))=\theta.

and

\nabla_{x}^{2}\phi(\mathfrak{o},x)=\nabla_{x}^{2}\varphi(z,x)=\Gamma

that

(\theta,\Delta,\Gamma)\in{\mathscr{P}}_{-}^{1,2}w(\mathfrak{o},x).

Now suppose that
\zeta=(\theta,\Delta,\Gamma)\in\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{K,-}^{1,2}w_{K}(z,x)
.
Then, there are
(x_{n},z_{n})\to(x,z)
and
\zeta_{n}\to\zeta
such that

is uniformly
bounded from above. Set

As
w(\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n})=w_{K}(z_{n},x_{n})
,

(\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n})\in\{w\leq m\}

Then by the lower coercivity
of

and a subsequence, denoted by

again, such that

(\mathfrak{o}_{n},x_{n})\to(\mathfrak{o},x)

\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o})=\lim_{n}\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o}_{n})=\lim_{n}z_{n}=z,\quad\Longrightarrow\quad w(\mathfrak{o},x)\geq w_{K}(\pi_{K}(\mathfrak{o}),x)=w_{K}(z,x).

Moreover, since
\lim_{n}w_{K}(z_{n},x_{n})=w_{K}(z,x)

The proof for

5
Crandall-Ishii-Lions (Second-Order) Lemma

We write
\boldx=(\underline{x},\overline{x})
,
\boldo=(\underline{\mathfrak{o}},\overline{\mathfrak{o}})
,

\pi_{K}(\boldo)=(\pi_{K}(\underline{\mathfrak{o}}),\pi_{K}(\overline{\mathfrak{o}}))
,
and
for a given
\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathscr{D}}_{T}^{2}\to\R
, we also write
\Phi(\boldo,\boldx)=\Phi(\underline{\mathfrak{o}},\underline{x},\overline{\mathfrak{o}},\overline{x})
.
Let
I
be the identity matrix in
\SSS^{d}
.
For
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\in\SSS^{d}
, introduce the block diagonal matrices
in
\SSS^{2d}

\text{diag}(\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{1}&0\\
0&\Gamma_{2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bf{G}=\begin{pmatrix}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{pmatrix}.

Then,
{\bf{G}\boldx\cdot\boldx}=|\underline{x}-\overline{x}|^{2}
,
and
\boldI=\text{diag}(I,I)
is the identity matrix in
\SSS^{2d}

Lemma 5.1
.

(Crandall-Ishii-Lions Lemma)
Suppose that
u\in{\mathscr{U}}_{e}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
,
w\in{\mathscr{L}}_{e}({\mathscr{D}}_{T})
,

\varepsilon>0
, and
(\boldo^{*},\boldx^{*})=(\underline{\mathfrak{o}}^{*},\overline{\mathfrak{o}}^{*},\underline{x}^{*},\overline{x}^{*})
is a maximizer of

\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathscr{D}}_{T}^{2}\to\R,\quad\Phi(\boldo,\boldx)=u(\underline{\mathfrak{o}},\underline{x})-w(\overline{\mathfrak{o}},\overline{x})-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\rho(\underline{\mathfrak{o}}-\overline{\mathfrak{o}},\underline{x}-\overline{x})^{2}.

Then, there exist

\underline{\Gamma},\overline{\Gamma}\in\SSS^{d}
such that

\displaystyle(\Theta(\underline{x}^{*}),\Delta,\underline{\Gamma})\in\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{+}^{1,2}u(\underline{\mathfrak{o}}^{*},\underline{x}^{*}),\qquad(\Theta(\overline{x}^{*}

\displaystyle),\Delta,\overline{\Gamma})\in\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{-}^{1,2}w(\overline{\mathfrak{o}}^{*},\overline{x}^{*}),

(5.1)

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\boldI\leq\textnormal{diag}(\underline{\Gamma},-\overline{\Gamma})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\bf{G},

(5.2)

where
\Delta=(\underline{x}-\overline{x})/\varepsilon
, and
\Theta\in{\mathscr{C}}_{b}(\R^{d})
is given by

\Theta(x)=\delta_{\mathfrak{o}}\Psi(\underline{\mathfrak{o}}^{*})(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{k}(\underline{\mathfrak{o}}^{*}-\overline{\mathfrak{o}}^{*})(f_{k})f_{k}(x),\quad\Psi(\mathfrak{o})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\varrho^{2}(\mathfrak{o}-\overline{\mathfrak{o}}^{*}).

(5.3)

Proof.

We proceed in several steps.
We first assume that
(\boldo^{*},\boldx^{*})
is a strict maximizer, and
consider the general case at the final step.

Step 1 (Projection)
. Set

,
w_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Pi_{K}(w)
,
and

\Phi_{K}(\boldz,\boldx)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=u^{K}(\underline{z},\underline{x})-w_{K}(\overline{z},\overline{x})-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\left(|\underline{z}-\overline{z}|^{2}+|\underline{x}-\overline{x}|^{2}\right).

By Lemma
4.6
,
u^{K}\in{\mathscr{U}}_{e}({\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d})

and
w_{K}\in{\mathscr{L}}_{e}({\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d})
.
Therefore,
\Phi_{K}
achieves its maximum at,
say,
(\boldz_{K},\boldx_{K})
.
Additionally, by (

),

\Phi(\boldo,\boldx)\leq\Phi_{K}(\pi_{K}(\boldo),\boldx)\leq\Phi_{K}(\boldz_{K},\boldx_{K}),\hskip 22.99988pt\forall(\boldo,\boldx)\in{\mathscr{D}}^{2}_{T}.

(5.4)

Moreover, for any
\boldo\in{\mathscr{E}}_{K}(\boldz,\boldx)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\boldo=(\underline{\mathfrak{o}},\overline{\mathfrak{o}})\in{\mathscr{M}}_{T}^{2}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\underline{\mathfrak{o}}\in{\mathscr{M}}^{K}(u,\underline{z},\underline{x}),\,\overline{\mathfrak{o}}\in{\mathscr{M}}_{K}(w,\overline{z},\overline{x})\,\}

\displaystyle 0\leq\Phi_{K}(\boldz,\boldx)-\Phi(\boldo,\boldx)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}(\varrho^{2}(\underline{\mathfrak{o}}-\overline{\mathfrak{o}})-|\pi_{K}(\underline{\mathfrak{o}})-\pi_{K}(\overline{\mathfrak{o}})|^{2})

Since
|\mathfrak{o}|\leq T
for all

,
we conclude that

\lim_{K\to\infty}\sup\{|\Phi(\boldo,\boldx)-\Phi_{K}(\boldz,\boldx)|\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\boldo\in{\mathscr{E}}_{K}(\boldz,\boldx),(\boldz,\boldx)\in{\mathscr{R}}_{K}\times\R^{d}\}=0.

(5.5)

Step 2 (Finite dimensional Crandall-Ishii-Lions)
.
By the classical Crandall-Ishii-Lions Lemma on the closed set

13

,
[
14
]
, there exists

\displaystyle(\underline{\theta}_{K},\Delta,\underline{\Gamma}_{K})

\displaystyle\in\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{+}^{1,2}u^{K}(\underline{z}_{K},\underline{x}_{K}),\quad(\overline{\theta}_{K},\Delta,\overline{\Gamma}_{K})\in\overline{{\mathscr{P}}}_{-}^{1,2}w_{K}(\overline{z}_{K},\overline{x}_{K}),

(5.6)

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\boldI

\displaystyle\leq\textnormal{diag}(\underline{\Gamma}_{K},-\overline{\Gamma}_{K})\leq\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\bf{G},

(5.7)

where

)
. We claim that

by coercivity, arguing as before we conclude that on a subsequence, denoted by

Remark 7.1

Recall the space of continuous paths

It is well-known that the path-dependent heat equation

7.2

Timer options

We now derive the pricing PDE of timer options written on a single asset, i.e.

(7.4b)

Exotic Options in the Uncertain Volatility Model

Following the uncertain volatility model introduced by
Avellaneda et al. [1]
, we consider an asset price
X^{\alpha}
with dynamics
dX_{t}^{\alpha}=\alpha_{t}X_{t}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

The control process
\alpha

represents the volatility of the asset. The control set is
A=[\underline{a},\overline{a}]\subset(0,\infty)
meaning that volatility, albeit uncertain, is kept in a compact interval. In the occupied SDE (
3.3
), we thus choose
b\equiv 0
and

\sigma(\mathfrak{o},x,a)=ax
, so that
\sigma
satisfies the
growth and Lipschitz conditions with constant
c_{*}=\overline{a}
.

Consider an exotic option with payoff
\varphi=\varphi({\mathcal{O}}_{T}^{\alpha},X_{T}^{\alpha})
and assume calendar time for
\calO^{\alpha}
, i.e.
\lambda\equiv 1
. Observe that
J(\mathfrak{o},x,\alpha)=\E^{\Q}_{\mathfrak{o},x}[\varphi({\mathcal{O}}_{T-|\mathfrak{o}|}^{\alpha},X_{T-|\mathfrak{o}|}^{\alpha})]
is the price of the option under the volatility process
\alpha
.
Suppose we are interested in the seller’s price
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\mathscr{D}}_{T}\to\R
of the option, which is obtained from the volatility
\alpha
that
maximizes

J(\mathfrak{o},x,\alpha)
.
We therefore set
\ell\equiv 0
,
g=-\varphi
in (
3.7
),
so the price of the option coincides with the negative of the value function. That is,

p(\mathfrak{o},x)=-v(\mathfrak{o},x)=\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathscr{A}}}\E^{\Q}_{\mathfrak{o},x}[\varphi({\mathcal{O}}_{T-|\mathfrak{o}|}^{\alpha},X_{T-|\mathfrak{o}|}^{\alpha})].

The associated dynamic programming equation is

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}p+\sup_{a\in A}\frac{1}{2}a^{2}x^{2}\partial_{xx}p=0

on

\mathring{{\mathscr{D}}_{T}}
,

(7.5a)

\displaystyle p=\varphi,

on

\partial{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
,

(7.5b)

where (
7.5a
) can be rewritten as the (occupied) Black-Scholes-Barenblatt equation
[
1
,
22
]
,

\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}p+\frac{1}{2}x^{2}V(\partial_{xx}p)=0,

(7.6)

with
V(\Gamma)=(\underline{a}^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{\Gamma<0\}}+\overline{a}^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{\Gamma\geq 0\}})\Gamma
.
Applying
Theorem

3.10
to
v=-p
, it is then immediate to see that
p
is the unique viscosity solution of (
7.5a
)–(
7.5b
).

Appendix A
Proof of
Proposition

3.3

Proof.

Given
t\geq 0
, let
0=t_{0}<\cdots<t_{N}=t
be a sequence of partitions of
[0,t]
that satisfies
\max_{n\leq N}|t_{n}-t_{n-1}|\to 0

as
N\to\infty
. Consider the piecewise constant process

X^{N}_{s}=X_{0}\mathds{1}_{\{s=0\}}+\sum_{n}X_{t_{n}}\mathds{1}_{(t_{n-1},t_{n}]}(s),\quad s\leq t.

(A.1)

Write also
\Lambda_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{s}ds
and introduce the discretized occupation flow

{\mathcal{O}}^{N}_{s}=\int_{0}^{s}\delta_{X_{u}^{N}}d\Lambda_{u}
.
We note that here
X^{N}
and
{\mathcal{O}}^{N}
are not
\F
-adapted, however,
X^{N}_{t_{n}}
,
{\mathcal{O}}^{N}_{t_{n}}
are
{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{n}}
-measurable, which we will actually use. We first establish that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\varrho({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}^{N}_{s})=0,\quad\Q\mbox{-almost surely}.

(A.2)

For each
k\in\N
and
f_{k}
introduced in
Section

2.2
, we compute

\displaystyle|({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}^{N}_{s})(f_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{0}^{s}(f_{k}(X_{u})-f_{k}(X_{u}^{N}))d\Lambda_{u}\Big|^{2}\leq\Lambda_{s}\int_{0}^{s}|f_{k}(X_{u})-f_{k}(X_{u}^{N})|^{2}d\Lambda_{u}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{s}\int_{0}^{s}\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}^{2}|X_{u}-X_{u}^{N}|^{2}d\Lambda_{u}.

As the last term is nondecreasing in
s
for all
k
,
we can set
s=t
to obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{s\leq t}\varrho({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}^{N}_{s})^{2}\leq\sum_{k}\Lambda_{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}^{2}|X_{u}-X_{u}^{N}|^{2}d\Lambda_{u}\leq\Lambda_{t}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{u}-X_{u}^{N}|^{2}d\Lambda_{u}.

For
\Q-
almost all
\omega\in\Omega
,
s\mapsto X_{s}(\omega)
is uniformly continuous on the compact
[0,t]
, hence it admits a modulus of continuity
\frakm\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\R_{+}\to\R_{+}
(which depends on
\omega
).
Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|X_{u}-X_{u}^{N}|^{2}d\Lambda_{u}=\sum_{n\leq N}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}|X_{u}-X_{t_{n-1}}|^{2}d\Lambda_{u}\leq\frakm\!\big(\max_{n\leq N}|t_{n}-t_{n-1}|\big)^{2}\Lambda_{t}\to 0,\quad N\to\infty,

which proves (
A.2
).
Together with the continuity of
v
, we also have that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\big|v(\calO_{s}^{N},X_{s})-v(\calO_{s},X_{s})\big|=0.

(A.3)

Next, we write

v(\calO_{t}^{N},X_{t})-v(\calO_{0}^{N},X_{0})=\sum_{n\leq N}(v(\calO_{t_{n}}^{N},X_{t_{n}})-v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N},X_{t_{n-1}})).

(A.4)

Denote
\Delta^{\!\Lambda}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Lambda_{t_{n}}-\Lambda_{t_{n-1}}
. Then

\displaystyle v(\calO_{t_{n}}^{N},X_{t_{n}})-v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N},X_{t_{n-1}})

\displaystyle=\big[v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N}+\Delta^{\!\Lambda}_{n}\delta_{X_{t_{n}}},X_{t_{n}})-v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N},X_{t_{n}})\big]+\big[v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N},X_{t_{n}})-v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N},X_{t_{n-1}})\big]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N}+h\Delta^{\!\Lambda}_{n}\delta_{X_{t_{n}}},X_{t_{n}})dh~\Delta^{\!\Lambda}_{n}

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\Big[\nabla v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N},X_{s})\cdot dX_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}(\nabla^{2}v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N},X_{s})\sigma_{s}\sigma_{s}^{\!\top})ds\Big].

Here in the last equality, the first term is due to the definition and continuity of
\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}v
, and the second term is due to the classical Itô formula.
Then we can rewrite (
A.4
) as

\displaystyle v(\calO_{t}^{N},X_{t})-v(\calO_{0}^{N},X_{0})=\sum_{n\leq N}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\mathfrak{o}}v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N}+h\Delta^{\!\Lambda}_{n}\delta X_{t_{n}},X_{t_{n}})dh~\Delta^{\!\Lambda}_{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{n\leq N}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\Big[\nabla v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N},X_{s})\cdot dX_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}(\nabla^{2}v(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N},X_{s})\sigma_{s}\sigma_{s}^{\!\top})ds\Big].

(A.5)

Similarly to (
A.2
), one can easily show that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\max_{n}\sup_{h\in[0,1]}\sup_{t_{n-1}\leq s\leq t_{n}}\rho\Big(\calO_{t_{n-1}}^{N}+h\Delta^{\!\Lambda}_{n}\delta_{X_{t_{n}}}-\calO_{s},X_{t_{n}}-X_{s}\Big)=0.

Then, by (
A.3
), (
A.5
), and the regularity of
v
, we obtain (
3.6
) immediately.
∎

Appendix B
Proof of
Proposition

3.5

We show that
J(\mathfrak{o},x,\alpha)
is locally
1/2-
Hölder continuous with constant
\hat{c}
(to be determined) independent of
\alpha\in{\mathscr{A}}
. It is then immediate to see that
v(\mathfrak{o},x)
is locally
1/2-
Hölder continuous with the same constant.
Fix
(\mathfrak{o},x),(\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in{\mathscr{D}}_{T}
,
\alpha\in{\mathscr{A}}
, and assume without loss of generality that
\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})\leq 1
, i.e.,
\delta=1
in the statement. Write
({\mathcal{O}},X)
,
({\mathcal{O}}^{\prime},X^{\prime})
for the solution of (
3.2
)
-
(
3.3
) controlled by
\alpha
with initial value
(\mathfrak{o},x),(\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x^{\prime})
, respectively. The exit times of
({\mathcal{O}},X)
,
({\mathcal{O}}^{\prime},X^{\prime})
from
\mathring{{\mathscr{D}}_{T}}
are denoted by
\tau,\tau^{\prime}
. We also set
\varphi_{t}=\varphi({\mathcal{O}}_{t},X_{t},\alpha_{t})
,
\varphi_{t}^{\prime}=\varphi({\mathcal{O}}_{t}^{\prime},X_{t}^{\prime},\alpha_{t})
,
\varphi\in\{\lambda,b,\sigma,\ell\}
and
\Lambda=\int_{0}^{\cdot}\lambda_{s}ds
,
\Lambda^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{\cdot}\lambda_{s}^{\prime}ds
. Due to
the triangle inequality

\displaystyle|J(\mathfrak{o},x,\alpha)-J(\mathfrak{o},x^{\prime},\alpha)|\leq\E^{\Q}[|\int_{0}^{\tau}\ell_{s}ds-\int_{0}^{\tau^{\prime}}\ell_{s}^{\prime}ds|]+\E^{\Q}[|g({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau},X_{\tau})-g({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime},X_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime})|],

we can estimate the terminal and running cost separately.

B.1
Terminal Cost

Using the Lipschitz condition satisfied by
g
, then

\displaystyle\E^{\Q}[|g({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau},X_{\tau})-g({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime},X_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime})|]\leq c_{*}\E^{\Q}[\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime},X_{\tau}-X_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime})],

with the parabolic norm
\rho(\mathfrak{o},x)=\sqrt{\varrho(\mathfrak{o})^{2}+|x|^{2}}
. Next, consider the decomposition,

\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime},X_{\tau}-X_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime})\leq\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}},X_{\tau}-X_{\tau^{\prime}})+\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime},X_{\tau^{\prime}}-X_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime}).

(B.1)

First, we estimate the rightmost term in (
B.1
).

Lemma B.1
.

There exists a positive constant
C_{1}
such that

\E^{\Q}[\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime},X_{\tau^{\prime}}-X_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime})^{2}]\leq C_{1}\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})^{2}.

(B.2)

Proof.

Recalling that condition (
3.9
) implies
\tau^{\prime}\leq c_{*}T=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T_{*}
, then

\E^{\Q}[\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime},X_{\tau^{\prime}}-X_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime})^{2}]\leq R(T_{*}),\quad R(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\E^{\Q}[\sup_{s\leq t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{\prime},X_{s}-X_{s}^{\prime})^{2}].

(B.3)

Fix
t\leq T_{*}
. From
Cauchy-Schwarz and Doob inequalities, it is classical that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\E^{\Q}[\ \sup_{s\leq t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}|X_{s}-X_{s}^{\prime}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+t\E^{\Q}[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}|b_{s}-b_{s}^{\prime}|^{2}ds]+4\E^{\Q}[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}|\sigma_{s}-\sigma_{s}^{\prime}|^{2}ds]

\displaystyle\leq|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+c_{*}^{2}(t+4)\int_{0}^{t}\E^{\Q}[\sup_{u\leq s\wedge\tau^{\prime}}\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{u}-{\mathcal{O}}_{u}^{\prime},X_{u}-X_{u}^{\prime})^{2}]ds

\displaystyle\leq|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+c_{*}^{2}(T_{*}+4)\int_{0}^{t}R(s)ds.

Moreover, for each
k\in\N
, the triangle inequality and occupation time formula yields

\displaystyle\sup_{s\leq t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}|({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{\prime})(f_{k})|

\displaystyle\leq|(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})(f_{k})|+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}|f_{k}(X_{s})\lambda_{s}-f_{k}(X_{s}^{\prime})\lambda_{s}^{\prime}|ds.

(B.4)

Next, use
|f\lambda-f^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}|\leq|f||\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|+|f-f^{\prime}||\lambda^{\prime}|
to obtain

\displaystyle|f_{k}(X_{s})\lambda_{s}-f_{k}(X_{s}^{\prime})\lambda_{s}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq|f_{k}(X_{s})||\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{s}^{\prime}|+|f_{k}(X_{s})-f_{k}(X_{s}^{\prime})|\lambda_{s}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{\infty}|\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{s}^{\prime}|+\lVert\nabla f_{k}\rVert_{\infty}|X_{s}-X_{s}^{\prime}|\lambda_{s}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq c_{*}\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}(1+\lambda_{s}^{\prime})\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{\prime},X_{s}-X_{s}^{\prime}),

(B.5)

using the Lipschitz property of
\lambda
in the last inequality.
Using
\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}\lambda_{s}^{\prime}ds\leq\Lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime}=|{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime}|=T
,
\tau^{\prime}\leq T_{*}
, and (
B.4
), we obtain by integrating (
B.5
) that

\frac{1}{2}\sup_{s\leq t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}|({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{\prime})(f_{k})|^{2}\leq|(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})(f_{k})|^{2}+c_{*}^{2}\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}^{2}(T_{*}+T)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{u\leq s\wedge\tau^{\prime}}\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{u}-{\mathcal{O}}_{u}^{\prime},X_{u}-X_{u}^{\prime})^{2}ds.

summing over
k
and using
\sum\nolimits_{k}\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}^{2}\leq 1
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\E^{\Q}[\sup_{s\leq t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}\varrho({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{\prime})^{2}]\leq\varrho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})^{2}+c_{*}^{2}(T+T_{*})^{2}\int_{0}^{t}R(s)ds.

Altogether, we have established that

\displaystyle R(t)

\displaystyle\leq\E^{\Q}[\sup_{s\leq t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}\varrho({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{\prime})^{2}]+\E^{\Q}[\sup_{s\leq t\wedge\tau^{\prime}}|X_{s}-X_{s}^{\prime}|^{2}]\leq 3\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}R(s)ds

where
C=c_{*}^{2}[3(T_{*}+4)+2(T+T_{*})^{2}]
. We therefore conclude from Grönwall’s Lemma that

\E^{\Q}[\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime},X_{\tau^{\prime}}-X_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime})^{2}]\leq R(T_{*})\leq C_{1}\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})^{2},\qquad C_{1}=3e^{CT_{*}}.

∎

We now treat the remaining term in (
B.1
), namely
\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}},X_{\tau}-X_{\tau^{\prime}})
. First observe that

\E^{\Q}[\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}},X_{\tau}-X_{\tau^{\prime}})]\leq\E^{\Q}[\varrho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}})]+\E^{\Q}[|X_{\tau}-X_{\tau^{\prime}}|].

(B.6)

Let
\underline{\tau}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime}
and
\overline{\tau}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tau\vee\tau^{\prime}
. Using the linear growth of
\lambda
, then for all
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[|({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}})(f_{k})|^{2}-|(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})(f_{k})|^{2}]\leq\big(\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\overline{\tau}}|f_{k}(X_{s})|\lambda_{s}ds\big)^{2}\leq\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}^{2}Z_{*}^{2}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{2},

where
Z_{*}=c_{*}\sup_{t\leq T_{*}}(1+\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{t},X_{t}))
.
Summing over
k
and rearranging yields

\E^{\Q}[\varrho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}})^{2}]\leq 2\varrho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})^{2}+2C^{2}\rVert\tau-\tau^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Q)}^{2},

(B.7)

with the finite constant
C=\lVert Z_{*}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Q})}
. For the rightmost term in (
B.6
), we first note that

\displaystyle\E^{\Q}[|X_{\tau}-X_{\tau^{\prime}}|]

\displaystyle\leq\E^{\Q}\big[|\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\overline{\tau}}b_{s}ds|\big]+\E^{\Q}\big[|\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\overline{\tau}}\sigma_{s}dW_{s}|\big].

Using the linear growth of
b,\sigma
and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality
[
31
, Chapter IV]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\E^{\Q}\big[|\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\overline{\tau}}b_{s}ds|\big]

\displaystyle\leq\E^{\Q}\big[\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\overline{\tau}}|b_{s}|ds\big]\leq\E^{\Q}[Z_{*}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|]\leq C\rVert\tau-\tau^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Q)},

(B.8)

\displaystyle\E^{\Q}\big[|\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\overline{\tau}}\sigma_{s}dW_{s}|\big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{\textsc{\tiny BDG}}\E^{\Q}\big[\lVert\sigma\rVert_{L^{2}([\underline{\tau},\overline{\tau}])}\big]\leq C_{1}^{\textsc{\tiny BDG}}\E^{\Q}[Z_{*}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|^{1/2}]\leq\tilde{C}\ \rVert\tau-\tau^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{1}(\Q)}^{1/2},

(B.9)

with
\tilde{C}=CC_{1}^{\textsc{\tiny BDG}}
.
We now prove the following lemma.

Lemma B.2
.

There exists
C_{2}>0
such that
\rVert\tau-\tau^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Q)}\leq C_{2}\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{*}^{\!\Lambda}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup_{t\leq T_{*}}|\Lambda_{t}-\Lambda_{t}^{\prime}|
, where we recall that
\Lambda
(respectively
\Lambda^{\prime}
) coincides with the total mass process
|\calO|
(resp.
|\calO^{\prime}|
). Since
\Lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime}=T
, then
\Lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}=\Lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime}+(\Lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}-\Lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime})\geq T-\Delta_{*}^{\!\Lambda}
.
Moreover, the nondegeneracy condition (
3.9
) implies that

\Lambda_{\tau^{\prime}+s}=\Lambda_{\tau^{\prime}}+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime}+s}\lambda_{u}du\geq T-\Delta_{*}^{\!\Lambda}+s/c_{*},\quad\forall s\geq 0.

Choosing
s=c_{*}\Delta_{*}^{\!\Lambda}
therefore gives
\tau\leq\tau^{\prime}+c_{*}\Delta_{*}^{\!\Lambda}
. Similarly,
\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau+c_{*}\Delta_{*}^{\!\Lambda}
, which shows that

|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|\leq c_{*}\sup_{t\leq T^{*}}|\Lambda_{t}-\Lambda_{t}^{\prime}|,\qquad\Q-a.s.

(B.10)

Next, noting that
\Lambda_{0}=|\mathfrak{o}|
,
\Lambda_{0}^{\prime}=|\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}|
, then for all
t\leq T_{*}
,
|\Lambda_{t}-\Lambda_{t}^{\prime}|\leq||\mathfrak{o}|-|\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}||+\int_{0}^{t}|\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{s}^{\prime}|ds.

Recalling that the first element of the separating family
(f_{k})_{k\in\N}
is constant, say
f_{0}\equiv c_{0}
with
c_{0}\in(0,1)
, we have

||\mathfrak{o}|-|\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}||=|\int_{\R^{d}}(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})(dx)|=c_{0}^{-1}|(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})(f_{0})|\leq c_{0}^{-1}\varrho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}).

Moreover, we have from the Lipschitz continuity of
\lambda
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{s}^{\prime}|ds\leq c_{*}\int_{0}^{t}\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{\prime},X_{s}-X_{s}^{\prime})ds\leq c_{*}T_{*}\sup_{s\leq T_{*}}\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{\prime},X_{s}-X_{s}^{\prime}).

Using similar arguments as in the proof of
Lemma

B.1
, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\E^{\Q}[\sup_{t\leq T_{*}}|\Lambda_{t}-\Lambda_{t}^{\prime}|^{2}]\leq c_{0}^{-2}\varrho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})^{2}+c_{*}^{2}T_{*}^{2}C_{1}\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})^{2}.

Together with (
B.10
), this yields

\rVert\tau-\tau^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Q)}\leq c_{*}\rVert\sup_{t\leq T^{*}}|\Lambda_{t}-\Lambda_{t}^{\prime}|\rVert_{L^{2}(\Q)}\leq C_{2}\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime}),\quad C_{2}=c_{*}\sqrt{2(c_{0}^{-2}+c_{*}^{2}T_{*}^{2}C_{1})}.

∎

Combining the above Lemma with equations (
B.6
), (
B.7
) yields

\E^{\Q}[\varrho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}})]\leq(2\varrho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime})^{2}+2C^{2}C_{2}\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})^{2})^{1/2}\leq(2+2C^{2}C_{2})^{1/2}\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime}).

Similarly, using (
B.8
), (
B.9
), and
\rVert\tau-\tau^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{1}(\Q)}\leq\rVert\tau-\tau^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Q)}
, we have

\E^{\Q}[|X_{\tau}-X_{\tau^{\prime}}|]\leq C_{2}\varrho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})+(C_{2}\varrho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime}))^{1/2}.

From
\rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})\leq 1
and the inequality
\rho\leq\rho^{1/2}
,
\rho\in[0,1]
, we have thus shown the existence of a constant
\hat{c}_{g}
that depends on
C_{1},C_{2},
and
C=\lVert Z_{*}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Q})}
such that

\displaystyle\E^{\Q}[|g({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau},X_{\tau})-g({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime},X_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime})|]

\displaystyle\leq c_{*}\left(\E^{\Q}[\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}},X_{\tau}-X_{\tau^{\prime}})]+\E^{\Q}[\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}-{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime},X_{\tau^{\prime}}-X_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\prime})]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\hat{c}_{g}\ \rho(\mathfrak{o}-\mathfrak{o}^{\prime},x-x^{\prime})^{1/2}.

B.2
Running Cost

Suppose that
\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau
. Then, the growth and Lipschitz property of
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
implies that

\displaystyle|\int_{0}^{\tau}\ell_{s}ds-\int_{0}^{\tau^{\prime}}\ell_{s}^{\prime}ds|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau^{\prime}}|\ell_{s}-\ell_{s}^{\prime}|ds+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau}|\ell_{s}|ds

\displaystyle\leq c_{*}T_{*}\sup_{s\leq\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime}}\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{\prime},X_{s}-X_{s}^{\prime})+Z_{*}^{\prime}|\tau^{\prime}-\tau|,

where
Z_{*}^{\prime}=c_{*}\sup_{t\leq T_{*}}(1+\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{t},X_{t}))
.
The case
\tau\leq\tau^{\prime}
follows analogously, with
Z_{*}
in lieu of
Z_{*}^{\prime}
.
Taking expectation, we obtain

\displaystyle\E^{\Q}[|\int_{0}^{\tau}\ell_{s}ds-\int_{0}^{\tau^{\prime}}\ell_{s}^{\prime}ds|]

\displaystyle\leq c_{*}T_{*}\E^{\Q}[\sup_{s\leq\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime}}\rho({\mathcal{O}}_{s}-{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{\prime},X_{s}-X_{s}^{\prime})]+C\lVert\tau-\tau^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Q)},

with
C=\lVert Z_{*}\vee Z_{*}^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}({\Q})}
. Defining
\hat{c}_{\ell}=c_{*}T_{*}C_{1}+CC_{2}
, we have from
Lemmas

B.1
and

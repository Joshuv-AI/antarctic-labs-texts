Abstract
. In this paper we describe and characterize the fundamental group of the complement to generic fiber-type curves, i.e. unions of (the closure of) finitely many generic fibers of a component-free pencil F = [ f : g ] : ℂ ℙ 2 ⇢ ℂ ℙ 1 F=[f:g]:\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1} . We also observe that these groups have already appeared in the literature in examples by Eyral and Oka associated with curves of fiber-type. As a byproduct of our techniques we obtain a criterion to address Zariski’s Problem on the commutativity of the fundamental group of the complement of a plane curve.

Introduction

The present work is a continuation of a series of papers by the authors devoted to a particular class of divisors
on surfaces called fiber-type curves. Fiber-type curves have been considered classically combining both topological
and geometric perspectives
(see
[
33
,
2
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
8
,

,
21
,
23
,
22
]
).
In particular, we will focus on fiber-type curves on the complex projective
plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
that are a finite union of generic fibers. A precise definition is given as follows.

Definition 1.1

(Plane fiber type curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
)
.

Let
C
be a curve in the complex projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. We say that a
C
is a fiber-type
curve if there exists a component-free pencil
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}

such that
C
is the closure in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of a finite number of fibers of
F
.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that, after Stein factorization, every fiber-type curve is a fiber-type curve associated with a
pencil
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
which has connected generic fibers (i.e., the
generic fibers of
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
are connected, where
\mathcal{B}

is the finite set of base points of the pencil
F
).

Even though any plane curve can be considered as a fiber-type curve by taking any line in the projective space
of curves of a given degree, not every curve can be a union of generic members of a component-free pencil.
We will refer to such curves as
generic fiber-type curves
(see
2.1
).
There are strong topological restrictions
in terms of the fundamental group of the complement of a generic fiber-type curve. A key role is played by the
existence of multiple fibers in this pencil.

Remark 1.3
.

By
[
13
, Proposition 2.8]
, the number of multiple fibers of a pencil

F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
with connected generic fibers is at most
2
. Hence, after a change
of coordinates in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we may always assume that the multiple fibers of any pencil with connected
generic fibers lie over a subset of
\{[0:1],[1:0]\}
, and that the multiplicity of the fiber over

[1:0]
is greater or equal than the multiplicity of the fiber over
[0:1]
.

In light of Remarks
1.2
and
1.3
, every plane fiber-type curve is a fiber-type
curve associated to a pencil
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
satisfying the following condition:

Condition 1.4
.

F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is of the form
F=[f_{kp}^{q}:f_{kq}^{p}]
for some
p,q,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

with
p\geq q
, where

•

f_{kp}
and
f_{kq}
are coprime homogeneous polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
of degrees
kp
and

kq
respectively.

•

Neither
f_{kp}
nor
f_{kq}
is an
l
-th power of another polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
for any
l\geq 2
.

•

F
has connected generic fibers, so
\gcd(p,q)=1
.

•

F
has no multiple fibers outside
\{[0:1],[1:0]\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}

Given a pencil
F
and a point
P\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we denote
C_{P}:=\overline{F^{-1}(P)}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2},

and, for any finite set
B\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we use
C_{B}:=\bigcup_{P\in B}C_{P}
to denote the fiber-type curve
corresponding to
F
and
B
.

The original motivation for this paper comes from
[
29
]
, where Oka defines the following family
of groups which will appear as fundamental groups of complements of some particular cases of generic fiber-type curves
(see also Eyral-Oka
[
18
,
19
]
for more examples where the genericity condition
is relaxed).

Definition 1.5
.

Let
p
,
q
and
r
be positive integers.
Oka’s group

G(p;q;r)
is given by the following presentation:

(1)

G(p;q;r)=\langle\omega,a_{i}(i\in\mathbb{Z}):\omega=a_{p-1}\cdot a_{p-2}\cdot\ldots\cdot a_{1}\cdot a_{0},\omega^{r}=e,R_{1},R_{2}\rangle,

where
R_{1}
are the (periodicity) relations of the form
a_{i}=a_{i+q}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
R_{2}
are the (conjugacy) relations of the form
a_{i+p}=\omega a_{i}\omega^{-1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 1.6

(Cor. 1.4 in
[
29
]
)
.

Let
r\geq 0
. Let
G
be the fundamental group of the complement of
a fiber-type curve
C=\cup_{k=0}^{r}C_{[1:\omega^{k}]}
associated to the pencil
F=[f:g]
, where

•

\omega
is a primitive
(r+1)
-th root of unity,

•

d=\deg(f)=\deg(g)
,

•

f=\prod_{j=1}^{l_{1}}(x-\beta_{j}z)^{m_{1j}}
and
g=\prod_{i=1}^{l_{2}}(y-\alpha_{i}z)^{m_{2i}}
,
where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{l_{2}},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{l_{1}}
are mutually distinct complex numbers,

•

m_{1}:=\gcd(m_{1j})
and
m_{2}:=\gcd(m_{2i})
are coprime, and

•

the curve
C
is a union of generic members of the pencil
F
.

Under those conditions, Oka proves that
G\cong G\left((r+1)m_{1};(r+1)m_{2};\frac{d}{m_{1}}\right)
.

Also in
[
29
]
, Oka shows that
G(p;q;r)
is a central extension of a free product of cyclic groups
by a finite cyclic group (see Proposition
3.1
).
In fact, some finitely generated free products of cyclic groups appear as examples of Oka’s groups, namely,

G(sp;sq;q)\cong\mathbb{F}_{s-1}*\mathbb{Z}_{p}*\mathbb{Z}_{q}
for all
p,q,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\gcd(p,q)=1
.
Oka’s Example
1.6
generalizes his own celebrated work
[
28
, §8]

(also see Dimca
[
16
, Prop. §4(4.16)]
and Némethi
[
26
]
).
Examples
1.6
and
1.7
are both from the 70’s.

Example 1.7

(
[
28
, §8]
)
.

Let
p,q>1
be coprime integers, and let
C_{p,q}
be the irreducible curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
defined as

(2)

C_{p,q}:=V\left((x^{p}+y^{p})^{q}+(y^{q}+z^{q})^{p}=0\right),

which is the closure of the fiber over
[1:-1]
of the pencil
F=[(x^{p}+y^{p})^{q}:(y^{q}+z^{q})^{p}]
.
Then,
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{p,q})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}*\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.

Other results about curves whose fundamental group of their complement is a free product can be found in
[
30
]
.

Our first contribution in this direction is given in Proposition
3.5
, where we show that
any curve whose fundamental group of its complement is one of Oka’s groups must be of fiber-type, one
irreducible component per fiber. However, the pencil
F=[f:g]
used might not be such that
f
and
g
are completely
reducible fibers (products of powers of degree-one polynomials).

The main result of this paper computes the fundamental group of any generic fiber-type curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
In particular, we obtain that such fundamental groups are Oka’s groups from Definition
1.5
.

Theorem 1.8

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be as in Condition
1.4
. Let
B\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus B_{F}

be a finite non-empty set, and let
s:=\#B
. Then,

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B})\cong G\left(sp;sq;kq\right).

Moreover, assume that
V(f_{kp})
(resp.
V(f_{kq})
) is irreducible and
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus V(f_{kp}))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{kp}
(resp.
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus V(f_{kq}))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{kq}
). Then

\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus(C_{B}\cup V(f_{kp}))\right)\cong G\left((s+1)p;(s+1);k\right)

(resp.

\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus(C_{B}\cup V(f_{kq}))\right)\cong G\left((s+1)q;s+1;k\right)
).

It is worth mentioning that the proof of this result is more conceptual and avoids the braid monodromy and Zariski-Van
Kampen calculations needed in
[
29
,
19
]
, which gives a new insight into these results.

As for the
moreover
hypothesis
G=\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus V(f_{kp}))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{kp}
in Theorem
1.8
,
this is equivalent to
V(f_{kp})
being irreducible and
G
abelian (see Lemma
2.4
).
Finding a geometric criterion for the fundamental group of a curve complement to be abelian is known as
Zariski’s Problem (see
[
25
, §2]
for a more detailed approach to this problem).
For instance smooth and nodal curves have complements with an abelian fundamental group
(see
[
37
,
9
,
15
]
). Other criteria include the existence of high order flexes
(see
[
37
]
),
the existence of points of high multiplicity (see
[
16
, Cor. §4(3.8)]
),
the positive self intersection of a resolution of the curve
(see
[
27
,
25
,
1
]
),
other non-negativity conditions (see
[
31
,
32
]
),
or based on
\mu
-constant determinacy numbers (see
[
16
, Cor. §4(3.11)]
).

As a consequence of the Main Theorem, we also obtain a contribution to Zariski’s Problem.

Corollary 1.9
.

If an irreducible curve
C
is the closure of a generic fiber of a pencil
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
of
degree
d
with at most one multiple fiber, then
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{d}
.

Using this result one can produce infinitely many curves with complicated singularities, whose fundamental
groups of their complements are abelian and yet are not detected by the previously
known criteria for Zariski’s Problem.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we give some preliminaries regarding fundamental
groups of curve complements and orbifold fundamental groups in a more general setting. In particular, in
Section
2.4
, we let
F:U\to S
be a morphism between a smooth quasi-projective
variety and a projective curve, and consider fiber-type hypersurface complements of the form
U\setminus F^{-1}(B)

for a finite set
B\subset S
.
This directly generalizes the current setting: If
F:U=\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a non-constant pencil defined in
its maximal domain of definition, then
U\setminus F^{-1}(B)=\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B}
. In Section
2.4
,
we develop group-theoretical methods to describe fundamental groups of generic fiber-type hypersurface
complements in the general setting of quasi-projective varieties such as Addition and Deletion Lemmas.

Section
3
studies Oka’s groups from Definition
1.5
.
In Theorem
3.2
, we give a simpler finite presentation for Oka’s groups than the one given
in Definition
1.5
and use it to give a characterization of such groups
which will allow us to identify them in different contexts and solve the isomorphism problem for the infinite
family of Oka’s groups in Corollary
3.4
.

Finally, the Main Theorem
1.8
is proved in Section
4
, describing

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C)\cong G(p;q;r)
for any generic fiber-type curve
C
for certain non-zero integers
p,q,r
,
in particular,
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C)
is a central extension of a free product of cyclic groups.
As a consequence of this, we obtain Corollary
1.9
.
The famous example of the equisingular stratum of sextics with an
\mathbb{A}_{17}
singularity is discussed in
Example
4.6
using the techniques developed in this paper. We also improve Dimca’s criterion for abelian
fundamental groups in the context of Zariski’s Problem using

.
Finally, we observe in Theorem
4.8
that our Main Theorem

can be extended
under some circumstances to non-generic fiber-type curves.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Admissible maps

In this section, we talk about a generalized notion of the pencils satisfying Condition
1.4
, and set notation
for the rest of the paper.

Following Arapura
[
3
]
, we call a surjective algebraic morphism
F:U\to S
from a smooth connected
quasi-projective variety
U
to a smooth connected quasi-projective curve
S

admissible
if it has connected
generic fibers.

The minimal set of values
B_{F}
for which

F:U\setminus F^{-1}(B_{F})\to S\setminus B_{F}
is a locally trivial fibration is finite
[
35
]
.
The points in
B_{F}
are called
atypical
values of
F:U\to S
.
A fiber
F^{-1}(P)
is called
generic
if
P\notin B_{F}
.
We will distinguish between
F^{*}(P)
as the pulled-back divisor and
C_{P}
as
its reduced structure. Using this notation, one can describe the set of multiple fibers as

(3)

M_{F}=\{P\in S\mid F^{*}(P)=mD,m>1,\textrm{ for some effective divisor }D\}\subset B_{F}.

Note that in general, the effective divisor
D
in (
3
) need not be reduced. If
P\in S
, the

multiplicity

of
F^{*}(P)
is defined as
m\geq 1
if
F^{*}(P)=mD
for some
D
and whenever
F^{*}(P)=m^{\prime}D^{\prime}
, then
m^{\prime}\leq m
.

If
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a component-free pencil, the definition of
B_{F}
,
M_{F}
, and generic fiber is
done using the induced map
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where
\mathcal{B}
is the finite set of base
points of the pencil.

2.2.
Fundamental groups of curve complements

Definition 2.1

(Meridian)
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety and let
C=\cup_{i\in I}C_{i}
be a curve in
X
, where
C_{i}
are its
irreducible components. Let
p\in X\setminus C
be a base point. A positively (resp. negatively) oriented meridian around
C_{i}
based at
p
is the class in
\pi_{1}(X\setminus C,p)
of a loop
\delta_{i}\star\hat{\gamma}_{i}\star\bar{\delta}_{i}
, where
\delta_{i}
and
\hat{\gamma}_{i}
are as follows:

•

p_{i}
is a regular point on
C_{i}
,

•

\mathbb{D}_{i}\subset X
is a closed disk centered at
p_{i}
which is transversal to
C_{i}
at
p_{i}
such that
\mathbb{D}_{i}\cap C=\{p_{i}\}
,

•

\hat{p}_{i}
is a point in the boundary of
\mathbb{D}_{i}
,

•

\hat{\gamma}_{i}
is a loop based at
\hat{p}_{i}
around the boundary of
\partial\mathbb{D}_{i}
travelled in the positive (resp. negative) orientation, and

•

\delta_{i}:[0,1]\to X\setminus C
is a path in
X\setminus C
starting at the base point
\delta_{i}(0)=p
and ending at
\delta_{i}(1)=\hat{p}_{i}
.

The following three results are well known.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\gamma
be a positively oriented meridian around
C_{i}
based at
p
. Then,
\gamma^{\prime}\in\pi_{1}(X\setminus C,p)
is a positively oriented meridian around
C_{i}
if and
only if
\gamma^{\prime}
is in the conjugacy class of
\gamma
in
\pi_{1}(X\setminus C,p)
.

Proof.

See
[
27
]
, also
[
14
, Prop. 1.34]
for a proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Consider
X_{i}:=X\setminus(\cup_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}}C_{j})
and the map
(j_{i})_{*}:\pi_{1}(X\setminus C)\to\pi_{1}(X_{i})
induced by the
inclusion
X\setminus C\hookrightarrow X_{i}
. Then
(j_{i})_{*}
is surjective, and
\ker(j_{i})_{*}
is the normal closure of
any meridian
\gamma_{i}
in
\pi_{1}(X\setminus C)
around
C_{i}
.
In particular, if
X
is simply connected, then the set
\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
normally generates
\pi_{1}(X\setminus C)
.

Proof.

See
[
27
]
. Also, as a consequence of
[
34
, Lemma 2.3]
.
∎

Lemma 2.4

(
[
16
, §4 Prop. (1.3)]
)
.

Suppose that
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and let
C=\cup_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
be a curve with
r
irreducible components
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}
. Then,

H_{1}(X\setminus C;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r-1}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d},

where
d
is the greatest common divisor of the degrees of the irreducible polynomials defining each of the components of
C
.

2.3.
Orbifold fundamental groups

Let
S
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g
, and choose a labeling map

\varphi:S\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
\varphi(P)\neq 1
only for a finite number of points, say

\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}\cup\Sigma_{+}\subset S
for which
\varphi(P)=0
if
P\in\Sigma_{0}
and

\varphi(Q)=m_{Q}>1
if
Q\in\Sigma_{+}
. In this context, we will refer to this as an
orbifold structure on

S
.
This structure will be denoted by
S_{(s,\bar{m})}
, where
s=\#\Sigma_{0}
, and
\bar{m}
is a
(\#\Sigma_{+})
-tuple
whose entries are the corresponding
m_{Q}
’s.

Definition 2.5

(Orbifold fundamental group)
.

The orbifold fundamental group associated with
S_{(s,\bar{m})}

and denoted by
\pi_{1}^{\orb}(S_{(s,\bar{m})})
is the quotient of

\pi_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma)
by the normal closure of the subgroup
\langle\mu_{P}^{\varphi(P)},P\in\Sigma\rangle,

where
\mu_{P}
is a meridian in
S\setminus\Sigma
around
P\in\Sigma
.
Note that
\pi_{1}^{\orb}(S_{(s,\bar{m})})
is hence generated by

\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,g}\cup\{\mu_{P}\}_{P\in\Sigma}

and presented by the relations

(4)

\mu_{P}^{m_{P}}=1,\quad\textrm{ for }P\in\Sigma_{+},\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\prod_{P\in\Sigma}\mu_{P}=\prod_{i=1,\dots,g}[a_{i},b_{i}].

In the particular case where
\Sigma_{0}\neq\emptyset
(
s\geq 1
), (
4
) shows that

\pi_{1}^{\textrm{orb}}(S_{(s,\bar{m})})
is a free product of cyclic groups:

\pi_{1}^{\textrm{orb}}(S_{(s,\bar{m})})\cong\pi_{1}(S\setminus\Sigma_{0})\mathop{\scalebox{1.5}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}\left(\mathop{\scalebox{1.5}{\raisebox{-0.86108pt}{$\ast$}}}_{P\in\Sigma_{+}}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{m_{P}\mathbb{Z}}\right)\right)\cong\mathbb{F}_{r}*\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}*\dots*\mathbb{Z}_{m_{n}},

where
r=2g-1+\#\Sigma_{0}=2g+s-1
,
n=\#\Sigma_{+}
, and
\bar{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety. A dominant algebraic morphism
F:X\to S
defines an

orbifold morphism

F:X\to S_{(s,\bar{m})}
if for all
P\in S
the divisor
F^{*}(P)
is a

\varphi(P)
-multiple. The orbifold
S_{(s,\bar{m})}
is said to be
maximal
(with respect to
F
)
if
F(X)=S\setminus\Sigma_{0}
and for all
P\in F(X)
the divisor
F^{*}(P)
is not an
m
-multiple for any
m>\varphi(P)
.

The following result is well known (see for instance
[
4
, Prop. 1.4]
)

Remark 2.7
.

Let
F:X\to S_{(s,\bar{m})}
be an orbifold morphism. Then,
F
induces a morphism

F_{*}:\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}^{\orb}(S_{(s,\bar{m})}).

Moreover, if the generic fiber of
F
is connected, then
F_{*}
is surjective.

The language of admissible maps and orbifold morphisms is useful when studying the fundamental group of plane fiber-type curves, as seen by the following result, which is well known in different settings (cf.
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
,

[
12
, Corollary 2.22]
). Indeed, given a pencil
F
satisfying Condition
1.4
and a finite set
B\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
of
s
elements, one may apply it to
U:=\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B}
to get information about the fundamental group of the complement of
C_{B}
.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
11
, Cor. 2.32]
)
.

Let
U
be a smooth quasi-projective variety, let
S
be a smooth projective curve of genus

g
and let
S^{\prime}
be the complement in
S
of
s
points, where
s\geq 0
. Let
F:U\to S^{\prime}
be an admissible map.
Let
S_{(s,\bar{m})}
be the maximal orbifold structure of
S
with respect to
F
for some

\bar{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})
. Then, the following sequence is exact

\pi_{1}(F^{-1}(P))\to\pi_{1}(U)\xrightarrow{F_{*}}\pi_{1}^{\orb}(S_{(s,\bar{m})})\to 1,

where
P\in S^{\prime}\setminus B_{F}
, and the first arrow is induced by the inclusion.

2.4.
Fundamental groups of fiber-type curve complements

We start by recalling some general facts about fundamental groups of complements of fiber-type curves which were found by the authors in
[
12
]
by exploiting the exact sequence from Lemma
2.8
, as opposed to using braid monodromy arguments.

Given an admissible map
F:U\to S
from a smooth quasi-projective variety to a smooth projective curve, we want to obtain information regarding a presentation of the fundamental group of
U_{B}:=U\setminus F^{-1}(B)
. After a Lefschetz-type argument, we may assume that
U
is a smooth quasi-projective surface, and that
F^{-1}(P)
is a curve for all
P\in B
, so
U_{B}
is the complement of a fiber-type curve in
U
.

Suppose that
B\subset S
is a finite set. If
B\cup B_{F}\neq\emptyset
, then
S\setminus(B\cup B_{F})
is aspherical, as it is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of circles. Hence, the long exact sequence in homotopy groups associated to the fibration
F:U_{B\cup B_{F}}\twoheadrightarrow S\setminus(B\cup B_{F})
yields a short exact sequence

1\to\pi_{1}(F^{-1}(P))\to\pi_{1}(U_{B\cup B_{F}})\to\pi_{1}(S\setminus(B\cup B_{F}))\to 1

which splits, since
\pi_{1}(S\setminus(B\cup B_{F}))
is a free group. This yields a description of

Example 4.6

As a non-trivial application of Corollary

we will revisit the
Zariski pair of sextics with an

-singularity found by Artal in 1994 in his seminal paper
on Zariski pairs (cf.
[
5
]
). He proved that such equisingular family consisted of
four connected components
\mathcal{F}_{1},...,\mathcal{F}_{4}
, two of which

correspond to irreducible
sextics, whereas the other two correspond to two cubics with maximal order of contact. From the former
families, only one of them, say
\mathcal{F}_{3}
consisted of sextics of torus type
(2,3)
. It is well known that
the fundamental groups of the complement of any two curves in a connected component of an equisingular
family are isomorphic. Denote by

the group corresponding to the family of sextics in

From our discussion above, the group

admits a surjection onto

, hence it is not abelian.
On the other hand, the family
\mathcal{F}_{4}
consists of irreducible sextics of non-torus type. Artal proved that
the fundamental groups

and

are not isomorphic by showing that their Alexander polynomials
(an invariant of the fundamental group) do not coincide. In 2005, Eyral-Oka
(
[
17
]
) showed that

is abelian (and hence
G_{4}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{6}
). They did this
using the Zariski-Van Kampen method, which heavily relies on the explicit calculation of the braid
monodromy of the curve to retrieve a presentation of the fundamental group.

We present an alternative more conceptual (and significantly shorter) proof of

using Corollary
4.5
.
Using the equation of a curve in
\mathcal{F}_{4}
,

(see
[
5
, p. 240]
) one can check that the tangent line
\ell=V(x)
is such that

is generically an equisingular deformation of

. One can do this by calculating
a Milnor non-degenerated Newton polygon at the singular point

. This can be achieved by the
transformation

. The local equation

becomes

whose monomials
belong to the convex hull of the Newton polygon. This implies that the Milnor number of the singularity
of

at

does not change for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
(
[
24
]
) and
hence this is an equisingular family (
[
36
]
).
Since
P
is the only singularity of
C
, this implies that the curve
C
is a
generic member of the pencil
[f:x^{6}]
. Moreover, note that

is the only multiple fiber or else
the pencil would not have any irreducible fibers, which contradicts

being irreducible.
Corollary
4.5
implies the result.

\mathbb{P}^{1}
with respect to the maximal orbifold structure with respect to
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus V(f_{kp})\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{[0:1]\}
. The kernel of
F_{*}
is thus a purely central subgroup which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. The result about
\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus(C_{B}\cup V(f_{kp}))\right)
follows by induction on the size of
B
by applying Lemma
2.4
and Theorems
2.15
and
3.2
. The result about
\pi_{1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus(C_{B}\cup V(f_{kq}))\right)
follows the same steps.
∎

Lastly, we are going to refine the Addition Theorem
2.15
for the case of plane curve complements whose
fundamental groups are isomorphic to one of Oka’s groups from Definition
1.5
. Recall that, by
Corollary
3.4
, every group satisfying Definition
1.5
is isomorphic to
G(s^{\prime}p^{\prime};s^{\prime}q^{\prime};k^{\prime}q^{\prime})
for some

p^{\prime},q^{\prime},s^{\prime},k^{\prime}
such that
\gcd(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})=1
and
p^{\prime}\geq q^{\prime}
. Recall also that, by Proposition
3.5
,
every plane curve which satisfies that the fundamental group of its complement is isomorphic to one of Oka’s groups is a
fiber-type curve.

Theorem 4.8

(Addition for plane fiber-type curves)
.

Let
F=[f_{kp}^{q}:f_{kq}^{p}]:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a pencil satisfying Condition
1.4
. Let
B\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}

be a finite non-empty set such that
C_{Q}
is irreducible for all
Q\in B
. Suppose that

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B})\cong G(s^{\prime}p^{\prime};s^{\prime}q^{\prime};k^{\prime}q^{\prime})
, where
\gcd(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})=1
and
p^{\prime}\geq q^{\prime}
. Assume moreover that one
of the following hold:

•

\#B=1
and the kernel of

F_{*}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\orb}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{(1,\bar{m})})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\prime}}*\mathbb{Z}_{q^{\prime}}
is a purely
central subgroup which is finite cyclic, where
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{(1,\bar{m})}
is the maximal orbifold structure on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
respect to
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus B\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

•

\#B\geq 2
.

Then,
s^{\prime}=\#B
, and for all
P\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus(B\cup B_{F})
,

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B\cup\{P\}})\cong G((s^{\prime}+1)p^{\prime};(s^{\prime}+1)q^{\prime};k^{\prime}q^{\prime}).

Proof.

The equality
s^{\prime}=\#B
follows from Lemma
2.4
and Proposition
3.1
. If
\#B\geq 2
, Proposition
3.5
implies that
C_{B}
is a fiber-type curve corresponding to a pencil
F^{\prime}
satisfying Condition
1.4
such that the kernel of

F^{\prime}_{*}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B})\to\pi_{1}^{\orb}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{(s^{\prime},(p^{\prime},q^{\prime}))})\cong\mathbb{F}_{s-1}*\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\prime}}*\mathbb{Z}_{q^{\prime}},

is a purely central subgroup
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B})
which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{k^{\prime}}
, where
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is endowed with the maximal orbifold structure with respect to
F^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Moreover, every irreducible component of
C_{B}
is a fiber of
F^{\prime}
. Hence, the pencils
F^{\prime}
and
F
have two common fibers, and thus they must coincide up to change of coordinates in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In particular,
F_{*}
and
F^{\prime}_{*}
coincide up to isomorphism in the target, and thus have the same kernel.

Suppose that
\#B=1
. Let
l
be the order of the kernel of

F_{*}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\orb}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{(1,\bar{m})})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\prime}}*\mathbb{Z}_{q^{\prime}}
. Since the kernel of

F_{*}
is purely central,
H_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B})
is an abelian group of order
lp^{\prime}q^{\prime}
. However, since

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B})\cong G(p^{\prime};q^{\prime};k^{\prime}q^{\prime})
, Proposition
3.1
implies that
k^{\prime}p^{\prime}q^{\prime}=lp^{\prime}q^{\prime}
, so
l=k^{\prime}
.

We have shown that the kernel of
F_{*}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\orb}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{(s^{\prime},(p^{\prime},q^{\prime}))}\right)

is a purely central subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{k^{\prime}}
, where
s^{\prime}=\#B
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{(s^{\prime},(p^{\prime},q^{\prime}))}
is the maximal orbifold
structure on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with respect to
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{B}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus B
. The result now follows from Lemma
2.4
, the Addition Theorem
2.15
and Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Example
1.6

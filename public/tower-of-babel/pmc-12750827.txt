LINC00332 as a potential diagnostic marker for the low-grade tumors in renal cell carcinoma patients
Authors: Negin Taghehchian, Amirhosein Maharati, Fatemeh Taghavinia, Alireza Golshan, Sepehr Salarzadeh, Meysam Moghbeli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRenal cell carcinoma (RCC) ranks as a frequent malignancy among urological cancers worldwide and has a positive prognosis during the initial tumor phases. Since, metastatic RCC patients have poor prognosis, it is essential to examine the molecular biology of RCC progression in order to implement effective diagnostic and therapeutic markers for these patients. Background

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is one of the most common urologic malignancies, with 434,419 new cases and 155,702 deaths reported globally in 2022 [1]. The primary treatment strategy for RCC involves surgery followed by post-operative surveillance. Despite the development of novel therapeutic approaches in recent years, there is low survival rate among patients with metastatic and advanced RCC [2]. Approximately one-third of RCC patients who undergo nephrectomy experience tumor relapse and metastasis [3]. Additionally, RCC is often diagnosed at advanced stages due to its asymptomatic and progressive nature [4]. A complex network of deregulated genetic and epigenetic alterations is involved in cancer progression and development [5]. Therefore, identification of such deregulated molecular pathways and their key genes are crucial to improve early detection and overall survival of RCC patients. Non-invasive biomarkers have great potential for the early detection of RCC [6]. Non-coding RNAs including long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), circulating non-coding RNAs (circRNAs), and microRNAs (miRNAs) are the main non-invasive biomarkers that can improve early tumor detection in urothelial malignancies [7–10]. LncRNAs are key regulators of organ development, immunity, and tumor progression [11–15]. Deregulation of lncRNAs has been reported to be correlated with poor prognosis in ccRCC [16, 17]. Although, LINC00332 has been shown as a key regulator of tumor progression in several cancers [18–20], there is not any report about the probable role of LINC00332 in RCC. Therefore, we assessed the levels of LINC00332 expressions in RCC patients to introduce that as a novel diagnostic marker among these patients.

Methods

Patients

The current research included 50 clear cell RCC patients who had nephrectomy at Mashhad University of Medical Sciences from 2022 to 2024. Samples were gathered from the tumor and surrounding normal areas, with normal tissues taken from a location five centimeters away from the tumor mass. None of the RCC patients had undergone chemotherapy or radiotherapy before the surgery. Tumor grading and staging are critical prognostic tools in RCC. The histological grade, now primarily based on the WHO/ISUP grading system, classifies tumor aggressiveness by the prominence of nucleoli, with Grade 1 being the least and Grade 4 the most aggressive. Staging follows the AJCC TNM (Tumor, Node, and Metastasis) system, which describes the size and extent of the primary tumor (T), spread to regional lymph nodes (N), and the presence of distant metastasis (M). These critical assessments were performed by two independent, specialized uropathologists to ensure diagnostic accuracy and reproducibility. This practice of blinded review mitigated individual bias and inter-observer variability, providing a more reliable and consensus-driven classification that is essential for determining patient prognosis and guiding treatment strategies. After undergoing surgery, tissue samples were kept in RNA-later solution and stored at − 80 °C for later RNA extraction. Participants gave their consent after approval by the local ethics committee. This research was carried out in accordance with the guidelines set forth in the Declaration of Helsinki.

RNA extraction and qRT-PCR

Total RNA extraction Kit (Parstous, Iran) was used to extract RNAs from fresh frozen RCC and normal tissues. The purity of the extracted RNA was checked using gel agarose electrophoresis and spectrophotometry. Afterward, Easy cDNA reverse transcription kit (Parstous, Iran) was utilized for cDNA synthesis with oligo dT primer. The LINC00332 expression levels were evaluated using the SYBR green method in duplicate reactions on a Light Cycler to perform real-time PCR. GAPDH was used to normalize all data. The primers were as follows: LINC00332 - forward 5′-CTTGCGATTCAGTTGGCTTATTTC-3′, reverse 5′-ACAGTTTGTCCCATTCTGCTACC-3′; For GAPDH - forward 5′-GGAAGGTGAAGGTCGGAGTCA-3′, reverse 5′-GTCATTGATGGCAACAATATCCACT-3′. The levels of LINC00332 expression were measured using the -ΔΔCT technique. A fluorescence intensity increase more than two fold changes denotes up regulation, while a decrease below double baseline denotes down regulation. The fold change of RNA expression between the − 2 and + 2 was also considered as normal expression [21, 22].

Statistical analysis

SPSS version 27.0.1 (SPSS, Chicago, IL) was utilized for statistical analyses. Results were reported as averages with a standard deviation (SD). The normality of the data distribution was evaluated using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. The association between LINC00332 expression levels and different clinicopathological factors was evaluated through chi-square/Fisher exact tests, independent sample t-tests, and ANOVA tests, with a significance cutoff of P ≤ 0.05.

Results

Study population

Our focus group consisted of 24 males and 26 females in the RCC population. The age of the patients varied between 24 and 80 years, with an average age of 55.88 ± 12.24 years. The age of women was lower compared to men (53.27 ± 2.46 vs. 58.71 ± 2.35 years). There was a wide range of tumor size from two to 16 cm (mean size: 5.45 ± 3.13 cm). There was not any notable difference in tumor sizes between male and female patients (5.28 ± 0.64 vs. 5.63 ± 0.62 cm). The majority of tumors were in the left kidney (27 out of 50, 54%), of low grade (39 out of 50, 78%), and in early stages (44 out of 50, 88%). Correlation between the levels of LINC00332 expressions and clinicopathological features of RCC patients

Clinicopathological features and LINC00332 expression

LINC00332 expression levels were assessed in 50 patients with RCC. Down regulation and up regulation of LINC00332 were detected in 18 (36%) and 11 (22%) of the fifty RCC patients, respectively. The range of fold changes for LINC00332 was between − 16.35 and 15.00, with an average of −1.20 ± 5.95 (Fig. 1). Males showed a noticeable decrease in LINC00332 expression compared to females (−2.28 ± 1.49 vs. −0.21 ± 0.84, fold changes). There were significant reduced levels of LINC00332 expressions in high-grade (III/IV) tumor compared with low-grade (I/II) tumors among male RCC patients (−3.98 ± 3.43 vs. −1.43 ± 1.49, fold changes) (p = 0.048). Low-grade RCC tumors showed higher levels of LINC00332 expressions compared to high-grade tumors (−0.91 ± 0.81 vs. −2.23 ± 2.62, fold changes). There was also significant reduced LINC00332 expression in high-grade compared with low-grade tumors among RCC patients younger than 60 years old (−3.13 ± 4.25 vs. −0.89 ± 1.09, fold changes) (p = 0.007). It is intriguing that RCC patients with increased levels of LINC00332 had bigger tumors on average than those with decreased levels (6.54 ± 1.30 vs. 4.61 ± 0.55, cm).

Discussion

Renal cancer is a prevalent urological malignancy that is a health challenge in metastatic cases with therapeutic resistance [23]. Therefore, understanding the underlying mechanisms of RCC progression and metastasis is crucial for the development of effective therapeutic strategies. The amplification of oncogenes and deletion of tumor suppressor genes are critical events that promote many cancers [24]. Since, these genetic alterations contribute with the malignant behavior and therapeutic resistance, they can be considered as the novel therapeutic approaches. Chromosomal deletions and DNA losses are key events during RCC progression [25]. The frequent loss of DNA in specific chromosomal regions, such as 3p, 6q, and 13q, suggests these areas may harbor tumor suppressor genes critical to RCC development. DNA losses in 13q and 4q are observed in RCC cases that support the presence of essential tumor suppressors in these regions [26]. Additionally, chromosomal losses on 1, 2, 6, 10, 13, and 17, as well as loss of heterozygosity on 11p, 13, and 17, have been observed in advanced RCC, indicating that the mutations in tumor suppressor genes within these loci may promote tumor progression [27, 28]. The long arm of chromosome 13 (13q) has been extensively studied in various malignancies, revealing its significant role in cancer development. For instance, the loss of 13q14 has been observed in colorectal cancer that is associated with RB1 [29]. DNA amplification at 13q34 has been also reported in several cancers [30–32]. Deregulation of lncRNAs has been reported in RCC patients [33, 34]. LINC00332 has emerged as a potential tumor suppressor that is located on chromosome 13q14.11. LINC00332 down regulation has been reported in esophageal and gastric cancers, where it functioned as a tumor suppressor [18, 19]. LINC00332 promoted epithelial markers while inhibited stem cell markers, mesenchymal markers, and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), thereby reducing tumor cell growth, invasion, and cisplatin resistance in gastric cancer [20]. Given the key role of 13q14 area and LINC00332 in inhibition of tumor progression, we assessed the levels of LINC00332 expression in RCC samples. We observed that there was LINC00332 down regulation in RCC tissues compared with normal margins that was in line with the previous reports on esophageal and gastric cancer [18, 19]. There were significant reduced levels of LINC00332 expressions in high-grade tumors compared with low-grade tumors among male RCC patients. There was also significant LINC00332 down regulation in high-grade compared with low-grade tumors among RCC patients younger than 60 years old.

Conclusions

RCC is a common urological malignancy with a positive prognosis in early stages but poor prognosis in metastatic patients. Therefore, it is required to clarify the molecular biology of RCC to suggest the novel effective diagnostic and therapeutic markers in these patients. Since, lncRNAs play a vital role in RCC progression; we assessed the levels of LINC00332 expressions for the first time in RCC patients. It was observed that high-grade tumors had significant lower levels of LINC00332 expression, particularly in male patients under 60 years old. Therefore, LINC00332 can be used as a potential non-invasive diagnostic marker for low-grade RCC before age 60, following further assessment in serum samples. More complex studies that will include multivariate models are helpful to precisely delineate the independent predictive value of LINC00332 in RCC patients. Despite the significant associations revealed in this study, several limitations must be acknowledged to guide future research. Firstly, the single-center, retrospective nature of our investigation and the relatively small cohort size limit the generalizability of our findings and may introduce unintended selection biases; future multi-center, prospective studies with larger, more diverse patient populations are essential to validate the clinical utility of LINC00332 as a robust prognostic biomarker in RCC. Secondly, and critically, our study is confined to correlative observations from patient samples and lacks functional mechanistic data; subsequent research must incorporate in-vitro and in-vivo functional assays to definitively elucidate the precise biological role of LINC00332 in RCC progression and metastasis, which is crucial for understanding its potential as a therapeutic target.

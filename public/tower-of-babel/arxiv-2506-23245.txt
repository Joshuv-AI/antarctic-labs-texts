The Dirichlet problem for the minimal surface system on smooth domains
Caiyan Li and Hengyu Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, we propose a new assumption ( 1.2 ) that involves a small oscillation and C 2 C^{2} norms for maps from smooth bounded domains into Euclidean spaces. Furthermore, by assuming that the domain has non-negative Ricci curvature, we establish the Dirichlet problem for the minimal surface system via the mean curvature flow (MCF) with boundary. The long-time existence of such flow is derived using Bernstein-type theorems of higher codimensional self-shrinkers in the whole space and the half-space. Another novel aspect is that our hypothesis imposes no restriction on the diameter of the domains, which implies an existence result for an exterior Dirichlet problem of the minimal surface system.



1.
Introduction

In differential geometry, minimal hypersurfaces and high codimension minimal submanifolds share many natural similarities but also exhibit essential differences for many classical problems. In this paper, we focus on one of those problems: the Dirichlet problem for the minimal surface system (MSS)

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} g^{ij}(f)f^{A}_{ij}&=0\quad A=1,2,\cdots,m\text{\quad on~}E,(m\geq 2),\\
f&=\psi\text{\quad on~}\partial E,\end{aligned}\right.

which seeks higher codimensional minimal graphs with prescribed boundary data. For a more precise description we refer readers to (
2.5
) in Section
2
.

In the codimension one case (
m=1
), (
1.1
) (see also (
2.6
)) is called the Dirichlet problem for the minimal surface equation. Jenkins and Serrin
[
JS68
]
first showed its existence and uniqueness for any Euclidean mean-convex domains with continuous boundary data. For arbitrary bounded domains, small oscillation conditions on the boundary data are necessary, as shown by Jenkins-Serrin
[
JS68
]
and Williams
[
Wil84
]
.

In the higher codimensional case (
m\geq 2
), the situation becomes intricate due to the non-scalar nature of the solutions to (
1.1
). If the boundary data exhibits sufficiently large oscillations, Lawson and Osserman
[
LO77
]
constructed numerous examples demonstrating the non-existence of solutions to (
1.1
) on Euclidean balls with
m\geq 3
. For recent advancements in this area, readers are referred to the work of Zhang
[
Zha18
]
and Xu,Yang and Zhang
[
XYZ19
]
.

The first general existence result for (
1.1
) on Euclidean convex domains was derived by Wang
[
Wan04
]
, based on higher codimensional mean curvature flows (MCF) in (
2.9
). The long-time existence of such flows at boundary requires a
C^{1,\alpha}
gradient estimate near the boundary, as discussed by Thorpe
[
Tho12
]
via the classical elliptic equation techniques. Ding, Jost and Xin
[
DJX23
]
extended Wang’s result to the case of Euclidean mean-convex domains. Their
C^{1,\alpha}
gradient estimates
[
DJX25
]
rely on Lieberman’s sophisticated approach to parabolic equations
[
Lie96
]
. An interesting aspect is that both results in
[
Wan04
]
and
[
DJX23
]
impose requirements on the mean-convex property and diameter restrictions. For detailed explanations, see Theorem
2.3
and Remark
2.4
.

In this paper, to solve (
1.1
), we propose a new oscillation condition that removes the mean-convex assumption by exploring the geometric properties near the boundary. Throughout this paper, any domain
E
is assumed to have embedded and smooth boundary. We use the following notation:
\bar{E}=E\cup\partial E
,
E_{\delta}=\{x\in E:d(x,\partial E)<\delta\}
,
w(\psi)=\max\limits_{1\leq A\leq m}\{\sup\limits_{\bar{E}}\psi^{A}-\inf\limits_{\bar{E}}\psi^{A}\}
, and
|D^{k}\psi|
denotes the norm of the
k
-th covariant derivative of
\psi
. In particular,

|D\psi|=\sup_{|\tau|=1}|D\psi(\tau)|
and
|D^{2}\psi|=\sup_{|\tau|=1}|D^{2}\psi(\tau,\tau)|
,
where
\tau
ranges over unit tangent vectors of
E
.

Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a complete Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature and let
E\subset M
be a bounded smooth domain. Then there exists a constant
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,\partial E)>0
such that for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
and any smooth map
\psi=(\psi^{1},\ldots,\psi^{m}):\bar{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}(m\geq 2)
satisfying

(1.2)

\begin{split}\max\{\frac{w(\psi)}{\delta}&+\sup_{E_{\delta}}|D\psi|+32n\delta\sup_{E_{\delta}}|D^{2}\psi|,\sup_{\bar{E}}|D\psi|\}<1,\\
\end{split}

there exists a map
f
from
\bar{E}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
in
C^{\infty}(E;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap C(\bar{E};\mathbb{R}^{m})
solving the Dirichlet problem for the MSS (
1.1
) with boundary data
\psi
. Moreover,
f
is unique as a strictly length decreasing solution to (
1.1
).

The assumption (
1.2
) ensures that the graph of the initial map
\psi
is strictly length decreasing. The non-negative Ricci curvature assumption makes the evolution results of Tsui and Wang
[
TW04
]
still remaining valid in our setting. Consequently, the length decreasing property imposed by (
1.2
) will be preserved along the MCF with boundary in Proposition
2.7
and Lemma
5.2
.

Our long-time existence, which is in fact the
C^{1,\alpha}
gradient estimate, of the MCF flow (
2.9
) comes from White’s regularity results
[
Whi05
,
Whi21
]
. In the proof of Lemma
5.3
we show that the Gaussian density of the MCF flow (
2.9
) is
\frac{1}{2}
at the boundary or is
1
in its interior. In fact, we show that the Lipschitz length decreasing self-shrinker on the whole space and the half-space is a linear space in Theorem
4.6
and Theorem
4.15
, respectively. Those results have independent interests.

Finally the Lipschitz limit of the MCF flow (
2.9
) is stationary and strictly length decreasing. By the smoothness result in
[
Wan04
, Theorem 4.1]
, this limit is smooth, which gives the solution in Theorem
1.1
. Its uniqueness follows directly from Lee and Wang
[
LW08
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Removing the mean-convex requirement is more natural because any constant map is automatically a solution to (
1.1
). In our setting, the boundary
\partial E
may have finite many components. Another new aspect is that
\delta_{0}
only reflects the information in a fixed neighborhood of
\partial E
(see Remark
3.5
). Therefore, the exterior Dirichlet problem of the MSS can be considered. The following result is inspired by Simon
[
Sim87
]
, in which a more comprehensive characterization of its asymptotic behavior is established in the codimension one case.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth domain such that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E
is bounded. Fix any constant
c\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a constant
\tilde{\delta_{0}}=\tilde{\delta}_{0}(n,\partial E)>0
such that for any
\delta
in
(0,\tilde{\delta}_{0})
and any smooth map
\psi:\bar{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}(m\geq 2)
satisfying

(1.3)

\frac{w(\psi)}{\delta}+\sup_{\bar{E}}|D\psi|+32n\delta\sup_{\bar{E}}|D^{2}\psi|<1-c,

there exists a solution
f:\bar{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}\in C^{\infty}(E)\cap C(\bar{E})
to the above Dirichlet problem of MSS (
1.1
). Moreover,
f
satisfies the asymptotic behavior

(1.4)

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|Df-l|=0,

where
Df
denotes the gradient of
f
and
l
is a constant vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
can be summarized as follows. Let
Q_{r}
denote the intersection of
E
and the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at the origin with radius
r
. By applying Theorem
1.1
on
Q_{r}
and letting
r\rightarrow\infty
, we can obtain the existence part of Theorem
1.2
.
The critical point is to show that for each domain
Q_{r}
, the constant
\tilde{\delta}_{0}(n,\partial Q_{r})
depends only on
n
and the boundary
\partial E
for sufficiently large
r
. The asymptotic behavior part follows from Bernstein-type theorems of strictly length decreasing high codimensional minimal graphs; see, for example
[
Wan03
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
Xin21
]
.

In fact, our work is an effort to find elliptic versions of higher codimensional graphic mean curvature flows in
[
Wan02
,
TW04
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we collect some basic facts. In Section
3
we establish the boundary gradient estimate for higher codimensional mean curvature flows, where we propose new oscillation conditions. In Section
4
we present some Bernstein-type results of Lipschitz length decreasing self-shrinkers and minimal graphs in half-spaces. Finally, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
in Section
5
and Section
6
, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect some facts for later use.

2.1.
The Dirichlet problem

Let
f=(f^{1},\cdots,f^{m})
be a smooth map from
E
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\Gamma(f)
denote the graph of
f
. Then
\Gamma(f)
is an
n
-dimensional submanifold with respect to the product metric on
E\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Fix a local coordinate system
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}
on
E
. Then
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+df(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}),i=1,\cdots,n\}
is a local frame on
\Gamma(f)
. Denote
\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle_{\sigma_{M}}
by
\sigma_{ij}
. Here
\sigma_{M}
denotes the metric of
M
. Therefore the induced metric on
\Gamma(f)
can be written as

(2.1)

g_{ij}(f)=\sigma_{ij}+\sum_{A=1}^{m}\frac{\partial f^{A}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial f^{A}}{\partial x_{j}},\,\text{and}\,(g^{ij}(f))=(g^{ij}(f))^{-1}.

Lemma 2.1
.

With the assumption as above, the matrix
(g_{ij})
in (
2.1
) satisfies

(2.2)

(1+|Df|^{2})(\sigma_{ij})\geq(g_{ij})\geq(\sigma_{ij}),\ \text{and}\ (\sigma^{ij})\geq(g^{ij})\geq\frac{1}{1+|Df|^{2}}(\sigma^{ij}).

Here
(\sigma^{ij})=(\sigma_{ij})^{-1}
and
|Df|=\sup_{|\tau|=1}|Df(\tau)|
.

The proof will be deferred to the frame described in (
2.10
).

Let
\bar{\nabla}
be the connection on
E
. The mean curvature vector
\vec{H}
of
\Gamma(f)
in
E\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
can be computed as

(2.3)

\vec{H}=g^{ij}(f)(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial^{2}f^{A}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{A}})^{\bot},

where
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{m}}\}
is the orthonormal frame of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
(\cdot)^{\bot}
denotes the projection onto the normal bundle of
\Gamma(f)
. Set

(2.4)

f_{ij}^{A}=\frac{\partial^{2}f^{A}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}-(\bar{\nabla}_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}})(f^{A}),\quad A=1,\cdots,m.

Therefore,
\Gamma(f)
is minimal (that is
\vec{H}\equiv 0
) in
E\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
if and only if
g^{ij}(f)f_{ij}^{A}=0
for each
A=1,\cdots,m
.

Suppose
\psi=(\psi^{1},\ldots,\psi^{m}):\bar{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a smooth map on
\bar{E}=E\cup\partial E
. The Dirichlet problem for the minimal surface system (MSS) over
E
is defined to seek the solution of
the following quasi-linear system

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} g^{ij}(f)f^{A}_{ij}&=0\quad A=1,2,\cdots,m\text{\quad on~}E,\\
f&=\psi\text{\quad on~}\partial E,\end{aligned}\right.

where
(g^{ij}(f))
is as in (
2.1
).

In the codimension one case (
m=1
), (
2.5
) is simplified into the Dirichlet problem of the minimal surface equation as

(2.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} div(\frac{Df}{\sqrt{1+|Df|^{2}}})&=0~\text{\quad on~}E,\\
f&=\psi\text{\quad on~ }\partial E,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\psi:\bar{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous map,
f:\bar{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and
div
denotes the divergence of
E
.

Recall that a smooth domain is
convex
if the second fundamental form of its boundary is non-negative definite, and it is
mean-convex
if the mean curvature of its boundary is non-negative. Some partial results on the Dirichlet problem (
2.6
) are recorded as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth bounded domain. Then there exists a unique solution
u\in C^{\infty}(E)\cap C(\bar{E})
to (
2.6
) if one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

(Jenkins-Serrin
[
JS68
]
) the domain

is mean-convex and
\psi
is continuous;

(2)

(Jenkins-Serrin
[
JS68
]
)
\psi\in C^{2}(\bar{E})
satisfies

and
\sup_{E}\{|D\psi|+|D^{2}\psi|\}<K
for some constant

and for some constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,K,E)>0
;

(3)

(Williams
[
Wil84
]
)
\psi\in C^{1}(\bar{E})
satisfies
\sup_{\partial E}|D\psi|<K
for any constant
K\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}})
and
w(\psi)<\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,K,E)>0
.

Here

.

In the higher codimensional case (

), the Dirichlet problem for the MSS (
2.5

Theorem 2.3

Let

Remark 2.4

Comparing with Theorem

2.2.
Mean curvature flow

Let
E\subset M
be a smooth domain given in Section
2.1
.
For any smooth map
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
, its singular values
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}
are the square root of the eigenvalues of the matrix
(df)_{x}^{T}(df)_{x}
, where
(df)_{x}:T_{x}E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the differential map of
f
at
x
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that a smooth or Lipschitz map
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is (
strictly
)
length decreasing
if the singular values of
df
satisfy
\lambda_{i}\leq 1(<1-\varepsilon)
for
1\leq i\leq n
, and
f
is
(strictly) area decreasing
if
\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\leq 1(<1-\varepsilon)
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
whenever the differential
df
is well-defined. Here
\varepsilon
is a fixed positive constant in
(0,1)
.

It is easy to verify that this definition is independent of the choice of orthogonal frame near
x
and
f(x)
.

Next we define the mean curvature flow (MCF) according to (
2.5
) as in
[
Wan04
]
. Suppose
F_{t}(x)=F(x,t)
, where
F:\bar{E}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\bar{E}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}

(2.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (\frac{\partial F}{\partial t})^{\perp}&=H;\\
F|_{\partial E}&=Id_{E}\times\psi|_{\partial E}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
Id_{E}
is the identity map on
E
.
Under a local coordinate system
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
on
E
, the mean curvature flow
F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t)=(F^{1},\ldots,F^{m})
satisfies the following parabolic system:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F^{A}}{\partial t}=\big(g^{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}F^{A}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\big)^{\perp},\ A=1,\ldots,m.

Here
(g^{ij})=(g_{ij})^{-1}
is the inverse of the induced metric
g_{ij}=\langle\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle
, and
(\cdot)^{\perp}
denotes the projection onto the normal bundle of
F(E,t)
in
E\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Suppose the image of
F=(F^{1},\ldots,F^{m})
is a graph over
E
. Then there exists a family of diffeomorphisms
r_{t}:E\rightarrow E
such that
\tilde{F_{t}}=F_{t}\circ r_{t}
has the form

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t)=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},f^{1}(x,t),\ldots,f^{m}(x,t)).

Here
f_{t}(x)=f(x,t)
, where
f=(f^{1}(x,t),\ldots,f^{m}(x,t)):E\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}f^{A}(x,t)&=g^{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}f^{A}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\quad A=1,\ldots,m,~\mbox{on}~E\times[0,T)\\
f(x,t)&=\psi(x),\quad\,\mbox{on}~\partial E\times[0,T).\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 2.6

According to the maximum principle, the condition
*\Omega>0
is not broken along the interior of the graphic MCF flows. As a result, under the assumption of Proposition
2.7
below, if the strictly length decreasing property of
f_{t}(x)
is preserved along the MCF
\Sigma_{t}
, it remains a graph.

To study the length decreasing property, consider the symmetric 2-tensor
S
defined by

S(X,Y)=\langle\pi_{1}(X),\pi_{1}(Y)\rangle-\langle\pi_{2}(X),\pi_{2}(Y)\rangle

for any
X,Y\in T(E\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
, where
\pi_{1}:E\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow E
and
\pi_{2}:E\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
are the corresponding projections. From (
2.11
),

(2.17)

S_{ij}=S(e_{i},e_{j})=\frac{1-\lambda^{2}_{i}}{1+\lambda_{i}^{2}}\delta_{ij}.

Thus
S
is positive definite restricted on
T\Sigma_{t}
if and only if
\lambda_{i}<1
, for
i=1,\cdots,n
(that is
f_{t}
is strictly length decreasing). For future use, we point out that

(2.18)

S(e_{i},e_{n+j})=S(e_{n+i},e_{j})=\frac{-2\lambda_{i}}{1+\lambda_{i}^{2}}\delta_{ij}\quad S(e_{n+i},e_{n+j})=-S_{ij},

by (
2.11
) and (
2.12
).

The following theorem is an analog of the strictly length decreasing preserving result in
[
TW04
, Section 4]
in the boundary case.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
M^{n}
be a complete Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature and let
E\subset M
be a bounded smooth domain. Suppose
\psi:\bar{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a smooth strictly length decreasing map, and the mean curvature flow
\Sigma_{t}
in (
2.9
) exists on
\bar{E}\times[0,T)
as a smooth graph
(x,f_{t}(x))
satisfying
\max\{\sup_{\partial E\times[0,T)}|Df_{t}|,\sup_{\bar{E}}|D\psi|\}<1-\varepsilon
, for a fixed constant
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then
\Sigma_{t}
stays a graph of a smooth strictly length decreasing map on
\bar{E}\times[0,T)
with
\sup_{E}|Df_{t}|<1-\varepsilon
.

Proof.

By Remark
2.6
, it suffices to show that
\sup_{E}|Df_{t}|<1-\varepsilon
for any
t\in[0,T)
.

By the assumption, define
P_{ij}=S_{ij}-\epsilon^{\prime}g_{ij}
along the mean curvature flow
\Sigma_{t}
. Here
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\frac{1-(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{1+(1-\epsilon)^{2}}\in(0,1)
is a constant such that
P_{ij}>0
(positive definite) on
\partial E\times[0,T)\cup E\times\{0\}
according to (
2.17
). It remains to show that
P_{ij}>0
is preserved along the interior of
\Sigma_{t}
over the interval
[0,T)
.

A straightforward computation gives that (c.f.
[
TW04
, Eq. (4.8)]
)

\displaystyle(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta)P_{ij}=

\displaystyle-h_{\alpha il}H_{\alpha}P_{lj}-h_{\alpha jl}H_{\alpha}P_{li}+\bar{R}_{kik\alpha}S_{\alpha j}+\bar{R}_{kjk\alpha}S_{\alpha i}

\displaystyle+h_{\alpha kl}h_{\alpha ki}P_{lj}+h_{\alpha kl}h_{\alpha kj}P_{li}+2\epsilon^{\prime}h_{\alpha ki}h_{\alpha kj}-2h_{\alpha ki}h_{\beta kj}S_{\alpha\beta},

on
E\times[0,T)
.
Let
Q_{ij}
denote the right hand-side of the above equation.
By the Hamilton’s maximum principle for tensors (see
[
Ham82
, Theorem 9.1]
or
[
Ham86
]
), if
Q_{ij}w^{i}w^{j}\geq 0
for any vector
w=(w^{1},\cdots,w^{n})^{T}
on
E\times[0,T)
such that
P_{ij}w^{j}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, then
P_{ij}
is positive definite on
E\times[0,T)
.

Indeed, we compute

\displaystyle Q_{ij}w^{i}w^{j}=

\displaystyle 2\bar{R}_{kik\alpha}S_{\alpha j}w^{i}w^{j}+2\epsilon^{\prime}h_{\alpha ki}h_{\alpha kj}w^{i}w^{j}-2h_{\alpha ki}h_{\beta kj}S_{\alpha\beta}w^{i}w^{j}

(2.19)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\bar{R}_{kik\alpha}S_{\alpha j}w^{i}w^{j}-2h_{\alpha ki}h_{\beta kj}S_{\alpha\beta}w^{i}w^{j}.

For the second term in (
2.2
), using
S_{ij}\geq\epsilon g_{ij}\geq 0
and the relation in (
2.18
), we have

\displaystyle-2h_{\alpha ki}h_{\beta kj}S_{\alpha\beta}w^{i}w^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{1\leq p,q\leq r}h_{n+p,ki}h_{n+q,kj}S_{pq}w^{i}w^{j}+2\sum_{r+1\leq p,q\leq m}h_{n+p,ki}h_{n+q,kj}w^{i}w^{j}\geq 0.

For the first term in (
2.2
), we prove that
\bar{R}_{kik\alpha}S_{\alpha j}\geq 0
.
Since

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\bar{R}(e_{\alpha},e_{k},e_{k},e_{i})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}R(\pi_{1}(e_{\alpha}),\pi_{1}(e_{k}),\pi_{1}(e_{k}),\pi_{1}(e_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}R(e_{\alpha},e_{k},e_{k},e_{i})=Ric_{M}(e_{\alpha},e_{i}).

Here we used the definition of sectional curvature
K(X,Y)=R(X,Y,Y,X)
.
As a result, by (
2.18
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,k}\bar{R}_{kik\alpha}S_{\alpha j}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{p,k}\bar{R}_{n+p,kki}S_{n+p,j}=-\sum_{p}Ric_{M}(e_{n+p},e_{i})S_{n+p,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ric_{M}(e_{n+j},e_{i})\frac{2\lambda_{j}}{1+\lambda_{j}^{2}}\geq 0.

In summary, plugging the above computations into (
2.2
) gives
Q_{ij}v^{i}v^{j}\geq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Boundary gradient estimates

In this section, we derive the boundary gradient estimate along the mean curvature flow, which is essential in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Denote the graphic mean curvature flow by
\Sigma_{t}=(x,f_{t}(x))
, where
f_{t}(x):\bar{E}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
df_{t}
denote the differential of
f_{t}
. Define
d(x):=dist(x,\partial E)
, for
x\in E
as the distance from
x
to the boundary
\partial E
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
E\subset M
be a bounded smooth domain. Suppose the mean curvature flow
\Sigma_{t}
exists on
E\times[0,T)
. Then there exists a constant
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(n,\partial E)>0
, and
c_{0}=c_{0}(n,\partial E)>0
, such that

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}d\geq-c_{0},\ \ \mbox{on}\ E_{\eta_{0}},

where
E_{\eta_{0}}=\{x\in E:d(x,\partial E)<\eta_{0}\}
. Additionally, if
E
is strictly convex,
c_{0}
can be chosen as
0
.

Remark 3.2
.

The main role of
\eta_{0}
is to ensure that the function
d
is a
C^{2}
function on
E_{\eta_{0}}
. For instance, whenever
E
is strictly convex, we shall choose
\eta_{0}
sufficiently small such that the level set of
d(x)
in
E_{\eta_{0}}
is embedded and convex with respect to the outward normal vector.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
d(x)
is a
C^{2}
function on
E_{\eta_{0}}
, for some
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(n,\partial E)>0
.
Fix any
x\in E_{\eta_{0}}
, we use the induced metric of
\Sigma_{t}
given in (
2.1
). Let
(\sigma_{ij})
be the metric matrix of
E
. By Lemma
2.1
,
(g_{ij})\leq(\sigma_{ij})
. For any
x\in E_{\eta}
, choose an orthonormal frame
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
at
T_{x}E
. Without loss of generality, we assume
(\sigma_{ij})
is the identity matrix at
x
. Hence
(g_{ij})\leq(\delta_{ij})
. Then at any
x\in E_{\delta_{0}}
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}d=

\displaystyle-g^{ij}\mathrm{Hess}(d)(a_{i},a_{j})\geq-\sum_{i}|\mathrm{Hess}(d)(a_{i},a_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-n\max_{i}\sup_{E_{\eta_{0}}}|\mathrm{Hess}(d)(a_{i},a_{i})|:=-c_{0}(n,\partial E).

In the case that
E
is strictly convex, the matrix
\{-\mathrm{Hess}(d)(a_{i},a_{j})\}
is positive definite on
\partial E
. As a result, if we take
\eta_{0}
sufficiently small, it holds that

-g^{ij}\mathrm{Hess}(d)(a_{i},a_{j})\geq 0
on
E_{\eta_{0}}
. In the above inequality,
c_{0}
could be chosen as
0
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

In particular, when
E
is a ball
B_{r}(0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered
at the origin with radius
r
, a direct computation yields that
-\mathrm{Hess}(d)(e_{i},e_{j})(x)=\frac{1}{r-d(x)}\delta_{ij}>0
for any
x\in B_{r}(0)
. Then, according to (
3.1
),

(3.2)

-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}d\geq 0

holds on
\{x\in B_{r}(0):dist(x,\partial B_{r}(0))<1\}
.
Therefore, we can choose
c_{0}
as zero and
\eta_{0}
as any number in
(0,r)
.

To formulate the desirable boundary gradient estimates, we begin by defining some notations to govern the subsequent discussion. Denote
w^{A}=\sup_{\bar{E}}\psi^{A}-\inf_{\bar{E}}\psi^{A}
and
w(\psi)=\max_{1\leq A\leq m}w^{A}
. Let
f=(f^{A})_{A=1}^{m}
and
\psi=(\psi^{A})_{A=1}^{m}
. By the maximum principle,
\inf_{\bar{E}}\psi^{A}\leq f^{A}\leq\sup_{\bar{E}}\psi^{A}
on
\bar{E}\times[0,T)
for
1\leq A\leq m
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M^{n}
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
E\subset M
be a bounded smooth domain. Suppose
F=(x,f(x,t)):E\times[0,T)\rightarrow E`\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a smooth solution to (
2.9
). Then there exists a constant
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,\partial E,\mu)>0
such that for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
,

\displaystyle|Df|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\omega(\psi)}{\delta}+|D\psi|+16n(1+\mu)\delta|D^{2}\psi|,\quad\mbox{on}\quad~\partial E\times[0,T),

where
\mu=\sup_{E\times[0,T)}|Df|^{2}
.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
c_{0}
and
\eta_{0}
be the numbers given in Lemma
3.1
. In (
3.7
), the constant
\delta_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{0}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\min\{\frac{1}{8c_{0}(1+\mu)},\eta_{0}\},\quad\mbox{if}\ c_{0}>0;\\
\eta_{0},\quad\mbox{if}\ c_{0}=0\quad(\text{when $E$ is strictly convex}).\end{cases}

This Proposition generalizes the computation in
[
Wan04
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Proof.

Choose two coordinate systems
\{x^{i}\}
on
E
and
\{y^{A}\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, respectively. Then

F(x,t)=(x^{1},\cdots,x^{n},f^{1}(x,t),\cdots,f^{m}(x,t))
and
\psi(x)=(\psi^{1}(x),\cdots,\psi^{m}(x))
. For each
A=1,\ldots,m
, define

\displaystyle S(x,t)=\nu\log(1+kd(x))+\psi^{A}-f^{A}+\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}d(x),\quad\mbox{on}~E_{\delta}\times[0,T),

where
k,\nu
and
\delta
are positive constants to be determined later.

Firstly, we claim that
S\geq 0
on
\partial E_{\delta}\times[0,T)
. In fact, note that
\partial E_{\delta}=\partial E\cup\partial^{\delta}E
, where
\partial^{\delta}E:=\{x\in E:d(x,\partial E)=\delta\}
. Obviously,
S=0
on
\partial E\times[0,T)
. By the definition of
\omega^{A}
,
\omega^{A}=\sup_{\bar{E}}\psi^{A}-\inf_{\bar{E}}\psi^{A}
, it holds that

\displaystyle S\geq\psi^{A}-f^{A}+\sup_{\overline{E}}\psi^{A}-\inf_{\overline{E}}\psi^{A}\geq\sup_{\overline{E}}\psi^{A}-f^{A}\geq 0,\quad\mbox{on}~\partial^{\delta}E\times[0,T).

On the other hand, since
f\equiv\psi
on
\partial E\times[0,T)
, we see that
S\geq 0
on
\partial E\times[0,T)
. These two facts together yield the claim.

Next, a direct calculation gives

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\dfrac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}})S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bigg(\frac{\nu k}{1+kd(x)}+\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}\bigg)\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}d(x)+\frac{\nu k^{2}}{(1+kd(x))^{2}}g^{ij}\frac{\partial d}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial d}{\partial x^{j}}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi^{A}.

Let
\mu=\sup_{E\times[0,T)}|Df|^{2}
. By Lemma
2.1

\frac{1}{1+\mu}(\sigma^{ij})\leq(g^{ij})\leq(\sigma^{ij})
. Applying Lemma
3.1
, we derive

\displaystyle(\dfrac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}})S\geq-c_{0}\bigg(\dfrac{\nu k}{1+kd(x)}+\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}\bigg)+\frac{\nu k^{2}}{(1+kd(x))^{2}}\frac{1}{1+\mu}-|\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi^{A}|,

where
c_{0}=c_{0}(n,\partial E)>0
is the constant given in Lemma
3.1
.

Further, using
|\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\psi^{A}|\leq n|D^{2}\psi^{A}|

and
d(x)<\delta
on
E_{\delta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}})S\geq

\displaystyle-c_{0}(\nu k+\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta})+\frac{\nu k^{2}}{(1+k\delta)^{2}}\frac{1}{1+\mu}-n|D^{2}\psi^{A}|.

Choose
k=\delta^{-1}
. We derive

\displaystyle(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}})S\geq

\displaystyle-c_{0}\frac{(\nu+\omega^{A})}{\delta}+\dfrac{\nu}{4(1+\mu)\delta^{2}}-n|D^{2}\psi^{A}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{\delta}\big(\frac{1}{4(1+\mu)\delta}-c_{0}\big)-c_{0}\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}-n|D^{2}\psi^{A}|.

It suffices to take
\nu
as

\displaystyle\nu=

\displaystyle\frac{4(1+\mu)\delta^{2}}{1-4c_{0}(1+\mu)\delta}\big(c_{0}\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}+n|D^{2}\psi^{A}|\big)>0.

Then

\displaystyle(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}})S\geq 0\quad\text{ on}\quad E_{\delta}\times[0,T)

To determine
\delta
, our consideration is divided into two cases.

Case 1
.
Suppose
c_{0}=0
. Now

(3.4)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\nu}{\delta}=

\displaystyle 4(1+\mu)\delta n|D^{2}\psi^{A}|.

Let
\delta_{0}=\eta_{0}
, where
\eta_{0}
is given as in Lemma
3.1
. We can choose
\delta
to be any number in
(0,\delta_{0}]
.

Case 2
. Suppose
c_{0}>0
. Take
\alpha_{0}=\min\{\frac{1}{8c_{0}(1+\mu)},\eta_{0}\}
. For any
\delta\in(0,\alpha_{0}]
, then
4c_{0}(1+\mu)\delta\leq 1/2
and so

1-4c_{0}(1+\mu)\delta\geq 1/2
.
Thus

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{\delta}=

\displaystyle\frac{4(1+\mu)\delta}{1-4c_{0}(1+\mu)\delta}\big(c_{0}\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}+n|D^{2}\psi^{A}|\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8(1+\mu)\delta\big(c_{0}\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}+n|D^{2}\psi^{A}|\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8(1+\mu)c_{0}\omega^{A}+8(1+\mu)\delta n|D^{2}\psi^{A}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}+8n(1+\mu)\delta|D^{2}\psi^{A}|,

In both cases, we arrive at
(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}})S\geq 0
.
Applying the maximum principle, we have
S\geq 0
in
E_{\delta}\times[0,T)
. Namely
f^{A}-\psi^{A}\leq\nu\log(1+kd(x))+\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}d(x)
on
E_{\delta}\times[0,T)
.

Similarly, applying the above process to the function

\tilde{S}=\nu\log(1+kd(x))+f^{A}-\psi^{A}+\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}d(x)
, we obtain
\psi^{A}-f^{A}\leq\nu\log(1+kd(x))+\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}d(x)
on
E_{\delta}\times[0,T)
.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{\partial(f^{A}-\psi^{A})}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{d(x)\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{|f^{A}(x)-\psi^{A}(x)|}{d(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{d(x)\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{\nu\log(1+kd(x))}{d(x)}+\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\nu k+\frac{\omega^{A}}{\delta}=\frac{\nu+\omega^{A}}{\delta}

where
\mathbf{n}
is the outward unit normal vector of
\partial E
.
Assuming
\frac{\partial f^{A}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}=0
for all
A\geq 2
up to an orthogonal transformation of coordinates on
E
at a fixed point, we derive

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\right|\leq\frac{\nu+w(\psi)}{\delta}+\left|\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\mathbf{n}}\right|.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle|D^{\top}f|=|D^{\top}\psi|\quad\mbox{on}~\partial E,

where
|D^{\top}f|(x)=\sup_{v\in T_{x}(\partial E),|v|=1}|Df(x)(v)|
.

Therefore,

(3.6)

\displaystyle|Df|\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\big(\frac{\nu+w(\psi)}{\delta}+|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\mathbf{n}}|\big)^{2}+|D^{\top}\psi|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\nu+w(\psi)}{\delta}+|D\psi|\quad\mbox{on}~\partial E.

Here, in the last inequality, we use

\displaystyle\big(\frac{\nu+w(\psi)}{\delta}\big)^{2}+|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\mathbf{n}}|^{2}+2\big(\frac{\nu+w(\psi)}{\delta}\big)|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\mathbf{n}}|+|D^{\top}\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\frac{\nu+w(\psi)}{\delta})^{2}+|D\psi|^{2}+2|D\psi|\frac{\nu+w(\psi)}{\delta}=(\frac{\nu+w(\psi)}{\delta}+|D\psi|)^{2}

Finally, we arrive the following conclusion.

Case 1
.
Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.6
) together, we obtain

\displaystyle|Df|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{w(\psi)}{\delta}+|D\psi|+4n(1+\mu)\delta|D^{2}\psi|,\quad\mbox{on}~\partial E\times[0,T).

for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
.

Case 2
.
Combining (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) together, we have

\displaystyle|Df|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2w(\psi)}{\delta}+|D\psi|+8n(1+\mu)\delta|D^{2}\psi|,\quad\mbox{on}~\partial E\times[0,T).

for any
\delta\in(0,\alpha_{0}]
. Now let
\delta_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\min\{\frac{1}{8c_{0}(1+\mu)},\eta_{0}\}
. The above inequality becomes that

\displaystyle|Df|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{w(\psi)}{\delta}+|D\psi|+16n(1+\mu)\delta|D^{2}\psi|.

for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
.

Combining these cases together, there exists
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,\mu,\partial E)>0
given by

(3.7)

\delta_{0}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\min\{\frac{1}{8c_{0}(1+\mu)},\eta_{0}\},\quad c_{0}>0;\\
\eta_{0},\quad c_{0}=0,\end{cases}

such that for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
,

\displaystyle|Df|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{w(\psi)}{\delta}+|D\psi|+16n(1+\mu)\delta|D^{2}\psi|.

on
\partial E\times[0,T]
. The proof is complete.
∎

4.
Bernstein-type results over half-spaces

In this section, we derive some Bernstein-type results for minimal graphs and self-shrinkers
over half-spaces. These results are essential for establishing the long-time existence of the MCF flow with boundary in Lemma
5.3
, albeit they may not be optimal.

4.1.
Smooth Bernstein type theorem

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
be the upper half-space
\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
. Throughout this subsection,
\Omega
denotes
dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}
which is the volume form of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f:\bar{E}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a smooth map on
E
and Liptschtiz on
\bar{E}
. Suppose
f
is strictly length decreasing i.e.
\lambda_{i}<1-\varepsilon
for
1\leq i\leq n
and for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then the following results hold.

(1) If
\Gamma(f)
, the graph of
f
, is minimal, then

(4.1)

0\geq\Delta\ln*\Omega+\varepsilon\sum_{\alpha,l,k}h^{2}_{\alpha,lk}\quad\textit{on}\ \Gamma(f).

(2) In addition, if
E=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
f|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}=u_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
, then
f
is a linear map. Here
u_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
is a linear map from
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
to itself.

Proof.

The conclusion (1) follows from (
2.16
) and the fact that a minimal submanifold is stationary for the MCF.

From now on, we assume that
E
is the half space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Denote
B=\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},0,\cdots,0)\}
which is the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. Let
\theta,\theta^{\bot}
be the orthogonal projections from
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
onto
B
and
B^{\bot}
(which is the normal space of
B
), respectively. For
z=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y^{1},\cdots,y^{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
, define

\tau(z):=\theta(z)-\theta^{\bot}(z)=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},-x_{n},-y^{1},\cdots,-y^{m}).

Let
v(\Gamma(f))
note the integral varifold generated from
\Gamma(f)
. Then it is stationary on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\setminus B
. According to the reflection principle in
[
All75
, Section 3.2]
,
v(\Gamma(f))+\tau(v(\Gamma(f)))
is also a stationary integral varifold which is the graph of some Lipschitz function
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Moreover,
\lambda_{i}<1-\varepsilon
for any
i=1,\cdots,n
whenever
d\tilde{f}
is well-defined. By
[
Wan04
, Theorem 4.1]
or Theorem
4.13
below,
\tilde{f}
is smooth. According to
[
Wan03
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\tilde{f}
is a linear map on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Recall that a smooth submanifold in Euclidean space is called a
self-shrinker
if its mean curvature vector
\vec{H}
satisfies

(4.2)

\vec{H}+\frac{\vec{F}^{\bot}}{2}=0,

where
\vec{F}
is the position vector and
\vec{F}^{\bot}
denotes the projection of
\vec{F}
into its normal bundle. First, we recall the following two results for self-shrinkers from the thesis of the second author
[
Zho15
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

(
[
Zho15
, Theorem 4.1.2]
) Let
E
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Theorem 4.4

Choose any point

\displaystyle=\vec{F}^{\bot}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\vec{F},e_{i}\rangle e_{i};

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{n}\langle\vec{F},e_{i}\rangle e_{i};

4.2.
Smoothness of Lipschitz self-shrinker graphs

In this section we derive the smoothness of Lipschitz minimal-type graphs in Theorem
4.13
. We will generalize
[
FC80
, Theorem 5.3]
and
[
Wan04
, Theorem 4.1]
to the setting of Lipschitz self-shrinkers.

The following condition will be sufficient for our purposes in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
f:E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a Lipschitz map. We say that
f
is
strictly length decreasing
if
\lambda_{i}<1-\varepsilon
for any
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
and for some constant
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, whenever
df
is well-defined and
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}s
are the singular values of
df
.

Now we use a more general definition including minimal graphs and self-shrinkers in Euclidean spaces.

Definition 4.9
.

For any constant
c\geq 0
, an
n
-dimensional smooth
c
-
minimal
submanifold
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
is a stationary point of the following area functional
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma)
given by

(4.17)

\mathcal{F}(\Sigma):=\int_{\Sigma}\exp(-c\frac{|z|^{2}}{4})d\mathcal{H}^{n}\quad

where
z=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y^{1},\cdots,y^{m})\in\Sigma
and
\mathcal{H}^{n}
is the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

Remark 4.10
.

A
0
-minimal submanifold is precisely a minimal submanifold, and a
1
-minimal submanifold is precisely a self-shrinker.

Suppose
f
is a Lipschitz map from
E
into
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then
\mathcal{F}(\Gamma(f))
takes the form

\mathcal{F}(\Gamma(f))=\int_{E}\exp\big(-c\frac{|\vec{F}|^{2}}{4}\big)\sqrt{{\rm det}(I+df^{T}df)}dx

where
|\vec{F}|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{A=1}^{m}(f^{A})^{2}
.

Definition 4.11
.

We say that a Lipschitz function
f:E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
a weak solution to the
c
-minimal surface system
if it satisfies that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\int_{E}\exp\big(-c\frac{|\vec{F}|^{2}}{4}\big)\bigg(\sum_{i,j,A}\sqrt{g}g^{ij}\langle\frac{\partial f^{A}}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial\varphi^{A}}{\partial x_{j}}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}c\langle\vec{F},\varphi\rangle\bigg)dx=0

for any map
\varphi=(\varphi^{1},\cdots,\varphi^{m})\in C_{c}^{1}(E;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Let
f
be as in (
4.11
). Then for each
A=1,\cdots,m
,

is the weak solution to the following elliptic equation

(4.19)

g^{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}f^{A}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+cq(f,\frac{\partial f^{A}}{\partial x_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial f^{A}}{\partial x_{n}})=0,\quad A=1,\cdots,m,

where

is smooth with respect to each of its components and
(g^{ij})
is a smooth matrix-value function depending on

. Then the first variation of
\mathcal{F}(\Gamma(f))
, still denoted by

\vec{H}+c\frac{\vec{F}^{\bot}}{2}=0.

The following result is implicitly used in the proof of
[

, Theorem 4.1]
. For the convenience of the readers, we include its proof here.

Theorem 4.12
.

Suppose
T
is an
n
-dimensional Lipschitz strictly length decreasing minimal cone as a graph in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
, which is smooth away from vertex. Then
T
is a plane.

Proof.

In the case of
n=1
,
T
is the union of two half lines intersecting at the vertex. It is contained in a plane in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. The conclusion is obvious.

Now assume
n=2
. Under our assumption, we have (
4.1
), i.e.

(4.21)

\Delta\ln*\Omega\leq-\varepsilon\sum_{\alpha,l,k}h^{2}_{\alpha,lk}

on
T
except its vertex. Since
T
is a cone, the minimum of
*\Omega
can be achieved at some point on the intersection of
T
and the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. By the maximum principle,
*\Omega
is a positive constant and
\sum h^{2}_{\alpha,lk}=0
. Thus
T
is totally geodesic. Since
n\geq 2
, the set
spt(T)
with the origin removed is connected. Therefore,
spt(T)
is contained in a
n
-dimensional linear space in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. By the connectedness,
T
is just this
n
-dimensional linear space.
∎

Theorem 4.13
.

Suppose
f:E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a Lipschitz strictly length decreasing map as a weak solution to the

-minimal surface system in (

). Then

is smooth over
E
.

Proof.

According to equation (

), to show

is smooth, it suffices that
f
is

. Because
\vec{H}+c\frac{\vec{F}}{2}=0
holds in the weak sense,
\Gamma(f)
, the graph of
f
, has locally uniformly bounded first variation (

). Therefore the tangent cone of
\Gamma(f)
,
T
, at
p
exists. Moreover

is a strictly length decreasing Lipschitz
graph as the weak solution to the minimal surface system.

We use a similar idea as in Yuan
[

]
. Suppose not. Assume

is singular at some point

. We can blow-up the graph of

and derive a minimal cone

by the monotonicity formula. Moreover,

remains strictly length decreasing.

We claim that

is smooth away from its vertex. Otherwise,

is singular at some

. We blow up

at

to get a lower dimensional minimal cone

cross a line

still has singularity away from its vertex, we repeat the blow up process, and finally, we will stop with a minimal cone singular only at its vertex. Using Theorem

Hence,

is smooth away from its vertex. Using Theorem

again, we see that

is a plane. Using Allard’s regularity theorem,

is regular at

.

Therefore,

is smooth.
∎

\beta\in(0,2]

\displaystyle\text{[}f]_{\alpha}(X):=\sup_{Y\in W\setminus\{X\}}\frac{|f(Y)-f(X)|}{|X-Y|^{\alpha}};\quad\langle f\rangle_{\beta}(X):=\sup_{Y=(y,s)\in W\setminus\{X\}}\frac{|f(Y)-f(X)|}{|s-t|^{\beta/2}}.

For any
X=(x,t)\in W
, any integer
l\geq 0
and any
\alpha\in(0,1]
, set

\displaystyle|D_{x}^{l}D_{t}^{j}f|(X):=\sup_{\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{l}}|D^{l}_{\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{l}}D_{t}^{j}f|(X);

\displaystyle[D_{x}^{l}D_{t}^{j}f]_{\alpha}(X):=\sup_{\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{l}}[D^{l}_{\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{l}}D_{t}^{j}f]_{\alpha}(X);

\displaystyle\langle D_{x}^{l}D_{t}^{j}f\rangle_{\alpha+1}(X):=\sup_{\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{l}}\langle D^{l}_{\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{l}}D_{t}^{j}f\rangle_{\alpha+1}(X),

where
\{\mathcal{V}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{l}\}
ranges over all unit vector fields near
x
. Define

\displaystyle|f|_{k+\alpha}(X):=

\displaystyle\sum_{l+2j\leq k}|D_{x}^{l}D_{t}^{j}f|(X)+\sum_{l+2j=k}[D_{x}^{l}D_{t}^{j}f]_{\alpha}(X)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l+2j=k-1}\langle D^{l}_{x}D^{j}_{t}f\rangle_{\alpha+1}(X),

and
|f|_{k+\alpha,W}:=\sup_{X\in W}|f|_{k+\alpha}(X)
.

Let
f=(f^{1},\cdots,f^{m}):W\subset\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
(
m\geq 2
), we say that
f\in H_{k+\alpha}(W)
if
|f|_{k+\alpha,W}:=\sum_{A=1}^{m}|f^{A}|_{k+\alpha}(W)<\infty
.

For the short-time existence of the MCF in (
2.9
), the parabolic system (
2.7
) admits an analysis analogous to that of a scalar parabolic equation.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
\psi:\bar{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is smooth. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the mean curvature flow in (
2.9
) exists smoothly on
[0,\varepsilon)
.

The strategy is to generalize the proof of Lieberman
[
Lie96
, Theorem 8.2]
in the setting of (
2.9
) on Riemannian manifolds.

Proof.

We give a sketch of the proof on Riemannian manifolds for completeness.
Fix
\theta\in(0,1)
and denote
V=\bar{E}\times[0,\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon
is a positive constant to be determined later. Set
\zeta:=|\psi|_{1+\theta,V}+1
, here
\psi(x,t)\equiv\psi(x)
. Consider the set

(5.2)

\mathcal{F}:=\{v(x,t)\in H_{1+\theta}(V):|v|_{1+\theta,V}\leq\zeta\},

It is obvious that
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
.

Now define an operator
J:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow H_{1+\theta}(V)
by setting
Jv=u
if, in any local coordinate
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
on
E
, it holds that

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} L^{A}(v)[u^{A}]&=0,\quad A=1\cdots,m\quad{\text{where}}\quad L^{A}(v):=-\partial_{t}+g^{ij}(v)\partial_{ij};\\
u(x,0)&=\psi(x),\ x\in E;\quad u(x,t)=\psi(x),\ x\in\partial E,\end{aligned}\right.

where
g_{ij}(v)=\delta_{ij}+\sum_{A}v_{i}^{A}v_{j}^{A}
and
(g^{ij}(v))=(g_{ij}(v))^{-1}
.

Since
v\in H_{1+\theta}(V)
is fixed,
I\geq(g^{ij})\geq\frac{1}{C}I
, where
I
is the identity matrix and
C
is a positive constant depending only on
v
. Thus (
5.3
) is actually a system of m Cauchy-Dirichlet problems with bounded
C^{\theta}(E)
coefficients.
The Perron process for (
5.3
) for parabolic systems,
[
Lie96
, Chapter III, Section 4]
), and the comparison principle, remain valid on Riemannian manifolds. Therefore, the conclusion of
[
Lie96
, Chapter V, Theorem 5.14]
still holds for (
5.3
) on
E
. Namely, for each
A=1,\cdots,m
, there exists a unique solution
u^{A}\in H_{2+\alpha}(\bar{E}\times[0,\varepsilon))
to (
5.3
), for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Moreover for
u=(u^{1},\cdots,u^{A})
, it holds that

|u|_{H_{1+\theta}}(V)\leq C(\zeta)|\psi|_{1+\theta}\leq C(\zeta).

By the Newton-Leibniz formula,

|u-\psi|_{H_{2+\alpha}(V)}\leq C(\zeta)\varepsilon.

When
\psi\equiv 0
, this implies that
J
is continuous.
One can choose
\varepsilon
small enough such that
u
is contained in
\mathcal{F}
, and thus
J
is a map from
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathcal{F}
. Notice that
\mathcal{F}
is a compact and convex set. Applying the Schauder fixed point theorem
[
Lie96
, Theorem 8.1]
, there exists a fixed point
u\in\mathcal{F}
such that
Ju=u
. Thus we obtain the short-time existence of the MCF flow (
2.9
).
∎

5.2.
The long-time existence

Suppose the MCF
F(x,t)=(x,f_{t}(x))
in (
2.9
) exists smoothly on
E\times[0,T)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose the assumption (
1.2
) holds. Then there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, such that

(5.4)

\sup_{E}|Df_{t}|\leq 1-\varepsilon

for any
t\in[0,T)
, where
\varepsilon
depends only on
\psi
and the assumption (
1.2
).

Proof.

Since
\sup_{\bar{E}}|D\psi|<1
, by continuity, there is a maximal time
T_{0}\leq T
such that
\sup_{E}|Df_{t}|<1
on
[0,T_{0})
. Taking
\mu=1
and applying Proposition
3.4
, we derive

(5.5)

\sup_{\partial E}|Df_{t}|\leq\frac{\omega(\psi)}{\delta}+|D\psi|+32n\delta|D^{2}\psi|<1-\varepsilon

for any fixed
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
satisfying (
1.2
) and
t\in[0,T_{0})
. Here
\varepsilon
depends only on assumption (
1.2
). In particular, for notation abuse,
\sup_{E}|D\psi|<1-\varepsilon
.
By Proposition
2.7
, this implies that
\sup_{E}|Df_{t}|<1-\varepsilon
for any
t\in[0,T_{0})
. Since
T_{0}
is maximal, it follows that

T_{0}=T
.
∎

The long-time existence of the MCF flow (
2.9
) under the assumption (
1.2
) follows from Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
4.15
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose the assumption (
1.2
) holds. Then the graphic mean curvature flow
F(x,t)
in (
2.9
) can be extended over time
T
.

Proof.

The main tools we will use are the local regularity theory for mean curvature flow, established by White
[
Whi05
,
Whi21
]
. This technique was first employed by Wang
[
Wan02
]
to show long-time existence for the mean curvature flow of closed graphs in arbitrary codimension. We adopt Wang’s method with a modification to tackle the same problem for the MCF flow in (
2.9
) with boundary.

Assume
E
is isometrically embedded in to Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}
for some sufficiently large
n_{0}
. Fix a space-time point
(Y,T)\in(\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}+m},\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Define the backward heat kernel by

(5.6)

\rho_{(Y,T)}(y,t):=\frac{1}{\big(4\pi(T-t)\big)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{|y-Y|^{2}}{4(T-t)}\bigg),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},t<T,

here
n
is the dimension of
F(x,t)
as a submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}+m}
.
Define the Gaussian density of the MCF flow
F(x,t)
in (
2.9
) by

(5.7)

\Theta(Y,T):=\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\int_{F_{t}(E)}\phi(|y-Y|)\rho_{Y,T}(y,t)\rm d\mathcal{H}^{n},

where
F_{t}(E)
denotes the graph of
f_{t}(x)
over
E
,
\mathcal{H}^{n}
denotes the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and
\phi(t)\in C_{c}^{\infty}[0,1]
with
\phi\equiv 1
in
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\phi^{\prime}\leq 0
. The existence in (
5.7
) follows from
[
Whi21
, Corollary 6.2]
, since according to (
5.4
), the area of
F_{t}(E)
is uniformly bounded,

(5.8)

\mathcal{H}^{n}(F_{t}(E))\leq\int_{E}\sqrt{(1+\lambda_{1}^{2})\cdots(1+\lambda_{n}^{2})}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq 2^{\frac{n}{2}}\mathcal{H}^{n}(E),

for any
t\in[0,T)
. It is not hard to see that
\Theta(Y,T)
is independent of the choice of
\phi
and the isometric embedding of
E
.

Let
Y=(u,v)
be any space limit of
F(x,t)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}+m}
as
t\rightarrow T
, where
u\in\bar{E}
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. To compute
\Theta(Y,T)
, we consider the parabolic dilation of space-time space
\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times[0,+\infty)
, given by

(5.9)

\mathcal{D}_{\iota}(y,t)=(\iota(y-Y),\iota^{2}(t-T))=(\iota(x-u),\iota(f_{t}(x)-v),\iota^{2}(t-T)).

Let
S
be the total space of the mean curvature flow
F_{t}(E)
for all
t\in[0,T)
. Denote by
S^{\iota}
the image
\mathcal{D}_{\iota}(S)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}+m}\times[-\iota^{2}T,0]
. By (
5.8
) and
[
Whi21
, Section 10]
, for any sequence
\{\iota_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converging to
\infty
, there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{\iota_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, such that
S^{\iota_{i}}
converges to a tangent flow
S^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}+m}\times(-\infty,0]
. Moreover,
S^{\infty}
is invariant under
\mathcal{D}_{\iota}
, i.e.
S^{\infty}
is self-similar. For each
t<0
,
S^{\infty}(t)
denotes the space slice of the tangent flow
S^{\infty}
. Furthermore,
S^{\infty}(t)=\sqrt{-t}S^{\infty}(-1)
, where
S^{\infty}(-1)
is given by the graph of a function
f_{\infty}
, which is a Lipschitz weak-solution to the
1
-minimal surface system( self-shrinker (
4.20
)) over a domain
E^{\prime}
.

From Lemma
5.2
, each
f_{t}
is strictly length decreasing. Notice that the property of strictly length decreasing is preserved under the parabolic dilation (
5.9
). Hence,
f_{\infty}
is Lipschitz and strictly length decreasing.

If
Y
lies in the interior of
E\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then
E^{\prime}
should be
T_{u}E
viewed as
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
Y
lies on the boundary of
E\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then
E^{\prime}
is the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. By Theorem
4.15
, in both cases,
f_{\infty}
must be a linear map. Therefore in the first case
S^{\infty}(-1)
is an
n
-dimensional linear submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}+m}
through
0
. In the second case
S^{\infty}(-1)
is a half space of an
n
-dimensional linear submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}+m}
. As a result, in the first case,
\Theta(Y,T)=1
and in the second case,
\Theta(Y,T)=\frac{1}{2}
. By
[
Whi05
]
, the mean curvature flow
F(x,t)
can be extended smoothly over time
T
in both cases.
∎

5.3.
The limit

Combining Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
together, we conclude that, under the assumption (
1.2
), the mean curvature flow in (
2.9
)
F(x,t)
exists for all time. Moreover
F(x,t)=(x,f(x,t))
, where
f(x,t)
satisfies
\sup_{E}|Df_{t}|<1-\varepsilon
for some constant
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
independent of
t
. Therefore,
f(x,t)
is strictly length decreasing for all
t>0
.

In the closed manifold setting, Wang
[
Wan02
]
showed that the norm of the second fundamental form of
F(x,t)
decays exponentially as
t\rightarrow\infty
. In our setting such a strong uniform
C^{2}
estimate cannot be obtained.

In the following, let
\Sigma_{t}
denote the graph of
f(x,t)
for fixed
t
. In fact, as the area element
\sqrt{g}
satisfies
\frac{d\sqrt{g}}{dt}=-|H|^{2}\sqrt{g}
, we see that the quantity
\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}|H|^{2}d\mu dt
is bounded by integration. Here
d\mu
is the induced volume form. So there exists a sequence
\Sigma_{t_{i}}
such that
\int_{\Sigma_{t_{i}}}|H|^{2}d\mu\rightarrow 0
as
t_{i}\rightarrow\infty
.

Together with the fact that
\sup_{E}|Df_{t_{i}}|<1-\varepsilon
, up to taking a subsequence, we have that
\Sigma_{t_{i}}\rightarrow\Sigma
in the sense of varifold such that
\Sigma=(x,f(x))
with
\int_{\Sigma}|H|^{2}d\mu=0
, and
\sup_{E}|Df|\leq 1-\varepsilon
for some Lipschitz map
f:\bar{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
f=\psi
on
\partial E
. Namely,
f
is strictly length decreasing as a weak solution to the minimal surface system. By
[
Wan04
, Theorem 4.1]
or Theorem
4.13
,
f
is smooth on
E
.

Consequently, we obtain the desirable solution to the minimal surface system (
1.1
) under the assumption (
1.2
). The proof is complete.

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is divided into two parts: the existence part, Section
6.1
, and the asymptotic behavior part, Section
6.2
. We emphasize that the boundary
\partial E
may admit multiple components.

6.1.
The existence

Without loss of generality, suppose the origin
0
is in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bar{E}
. As a result the distance between the origin and
\partial E
,
d_{0}=d(0,\partial E)>0
. Let
\mathrm{B}_{r}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the open ball centered at the origin
0
with radius
r
. Denote
Q_{r}:=E\cap\mathrm{B}_{r}(0)
. Then, for any
r>2d_{0}
, the boundary of
Q_{r}
consists of two disjoint parts:
\partial B_{r}(0)
and
\partial E
.

We claim that if
r
is sufficiently large, we can choose
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,\partial E)>0
, which is independent of
r
to guarantee that our conclusion holds.

Firstly, we fix
\eta_{0}>0
such that the distance function
d(x)=d(x,\partial E)
is
C^{2}
on
E_{\eta_{0}}=\{x\in E:d(x,\partial E)<\eta_{0}\}
. Suppose
\frac{r}{2}>\frac{r_{0}}{2}:=diam(\partial E)+2\eta_{0}+d_{0}
. Then
E_{\eta_{0}}\subset B_{\frac{r}{2}}(0)
. Now the set
Q_{r,\eta_{0}}=\{x\in Q_{r}:d(x,\partial Q_{r})<\eta_{0}\}
consists of two disjoint parts:
E_{\eta_{0}}
and
E^{\prime}_{\eta_{0}}=\{x\in Q_{r}:d(x,\partial B_{r}(0))<\eta_{0}\}
. Then the function
\tilde{d}(x):=d(x,\partial Q_{r})
on
Q_{r,\eta_{0}}
satisfies

(6.1)

\tilde{d}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &d(x),\quad\quad x\in E_{\eta_{0}};\\
&d(x,\partial B_{r}(0)),\quad x\in E^{\prime}_{\eta_{0}},\end{aligned}\right.

due to the fact that set
E^{\prime}_{\eta_{0}}
is disjoint with
B_{\frac{r}{2}}(0)
.

Similarly, we compute
-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\tilde{d}(x)
on
Q_{r}
as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, where
\Sigma_{t}
is the mean curvature flow in
Q_{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
in which the initial data is the graph of
\psi
. Since
\partial B_{r}(0)
is strictly convex, arguing as in Remark
3.3
gives

(6.2)

-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\tilde{d}=-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}d(x,\partial B_{r}(0))\geq 0\quad\text{on}\ E^{\prime}_{\eta_{0}}

On the other hand, from Lemma
3.1
, there exists a constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(n,\partial E)>0
which is independent of
r>r_{0}
, such that

(6.3)

-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\tilde{d}=-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}d\geq-c_{0}\quad\text{on}\ E_{\eta_{0}}.

In both cases, we have

(6.4)

-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\tilde{d}\geq-c_{0}\quad\text{on}\ Q_{r,\eta_{0}}.

By Remark
3.5
, the claim follows.

Now we can prove verbatim as in Proposition
3.4
that there exists
\tilde{\delta}_{0}=\tilde{\delta}_{0}(n,\partial E)>0
such that for any
\delta\in(0,\tilde{\delta}_{0})
,

\displaystyle|Df_{t}(x)|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{w(\psi)}{\delta}+|D\psi|+16n(1+\mu)\delta|D^{2}\psi|,\quad\mbox{on}~\partial Q_{r}\times[0,T),

where
\mu=\sup_{Q_{r}\times[0,T)}|Df|^{2}
and
\Sigma_{t}=(x,f_{t}(x))
is the mean curvature flow on time interval
[0,T)
.

Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, for any
\delta\in(0,\tilde{\delta}_{0})
and
\psi
satisfying assumption (
1.3
), i.e.

(6.5)

\frac{w(\psi)}{\delta}+\sup_{E}|D\psi|+32n\delta\sup_{E}|D^{2}\psi|<1-c,

the Dirichlet problem of the MSS (
1.1
) admits a solution
f_{r}\in C^{\infty}(Q_{r})\cap C(\bar{Q}_{r})
solving

(6.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} g^{ij}(f_{r})(f_{r})^{A}_{ij}&=0\quad A=1,2,\cdots,m\quad\text{on}~Q_{r},\\
f_{r}&=\psi\text{\quad on~}\partial Q_{r}.\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover,
\max_{Q_{r}}|Df_{r}|<1-c
. Also by the maximum principle,
\max_{Q_{r}}|f_{r}|\leq\max_{\bar{Q}_{r}}|\psi|
.

By the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, there exists a subsequence
\{f_{r_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
converging to a Lipschitz function
f
as the weak-solution to the following minimal surface system:

(6.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} g^{ij}(f)f^{A}_{ij}&=0\quad A=1,2,\cdots,m\quad\text{on}~E,\\
f&=\psi\text{\quad on~}\partial E.\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover
\sup_{E}|Df|\leq 1-c
on
E
. Now
f
is Lipschitz and strictly length decreasing on
E
. By
[
Wan04
, Theorem 4.1]
or Theorem
4.14
,
f
is smooth on
E
. This gives the existence of Theorem
1.2
.

6.2.
The asymptotic behavior

We consider the asymptotic behavior of the solution
f
to the MSS (
1.1
) over
E
. Notice that the singular values satisfy
\sup_{E}|Df|\leq 1-c
.
Now we blow down
\Gamma(f)
, the graph of
f
, following the idea of Simon
[
Sim87
]
. Namely consider the function
f_{k}(x):=kf(k^{-1}x)
defined over
kE=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:ky\in E\}
. Let
\Gamma(f_{k})
denote the graph of
f_{k}
over
kE
.

Applying the classical compactness of stationary varifolds, there exists a subsequence
k_{i}\rightarrow\infty
as
i\rightarrow\infty
, such that the sequence
\Gamma(f_{k_{i}})
converges to a minimal cone
T
. Moreover
T
is a graph of a strictly length decreasing Lipschitz function
f_{\infty}
, with
|Df_{\infty}|<1-c
as the weak solution to the minimal surface system. By Theorem
4.14
,
f_{\infty}
is a linear map on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This implies that
\sup_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}|Df-l|=0
, where
Df
means the gradient of
f
. The proof is complete.

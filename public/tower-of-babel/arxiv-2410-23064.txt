Towards Unconditional Uncloneable Encryption
Pierre Botteron, Anne Broadbent, Eric Culf, Ion Nechita, Clément Pellegrini, Denis Rochette

Abstract
Uncloneable encryption is a cryptographic primitive which encrypts a classical message into a quantum ciphertext, such that two quantum adversaries are limited in their capacity of being able to simultaneously decrypt, given the key and quantum side-information produced from the ciphertext. Since its initial proposal and scheme in the random oracle model by Broadbent and Lord [TQC 2020], uncloneable encryption has developed into an important primitive at the foundation of quantum uncloneability for cryptographic primitives. Despite sustained efforts, however, the question of unconditional uncloneable encryption (and in particular of the simplest case, called an “uncloneable bit”) has remained elusive. Here, we propose a candidate for the unconditional uncloneable bit problem, and provide strong evidence that the adversary’s success probability in the related security game converges quadratically as 1 2 + 1 2 ​ K \frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{K}} , where K K is polynomial in the size of the encoding, representing the number of keys and 1 2 \frac{1}{2} is trivially achievable. We prove this bound’s validity for K K ranging from 2 2 to 7 7 and demonstrate the validity up to K = 17 K=17 using computations based on the NPA hierarchy. We furthermore provide compelling heuristic evidence towards the general case. In addition, we prove an asymptotic upper-bound of 5 8 \frac{5}{8} and give a numerical upper-bound of ∼ 0.5980 \sim\!0.5980 , which to our knowledge is the best-known value in the unconditional model.

1.1
Results

Our work focuses on the achievability of an encryption scheme that realizes an
uncloneable bit
in the statistical model, thus without any computational or setup assumptions. However, given the apparent difficulty of achieving this task, we relax the security requirement, and we ask that the success probability of the adversaries be no more than

Our contribution is a new candidate scheme for this relaxation of the uncloneable bit question. We prove weak security for some small security parameters and provide strong numerical evidence that it also holds asymptotically.

1.2
Future Work

Our work introduces a candidate scheme supported by strong evidence, highlighting its significance in the field of unconditional uncloneable encryption.
However, several important questions remain unresolved, which we outline below:

Question 1: Proving the Conjecture for Large
K
.

In this paper, we provide an analytical proof for the conjecture when
K\leqslant 7
and support it numerically for
K\leqslant 17
. Extending this proof to arbitrarily large values of
K
would be a significant advancement, as it would mark the first unconditional proof of uncloneable encryption in the plain model.

Question 2: Achieving Stronger Security.

​Our proposed protocol achieves a quadratic security rate while the original definition in
[
BL20
]
specifies a security with exponential decay in the security parameter. A key open challenge is to develop a protocol that achieves this stronger security.

3.1
Clifford Algebra

For our uncloneable encryption scheme, we make use of the structure of Clifford algebra
[
Lou01
,
DL03
]
, widely used in quantum information theory, in particular as a generalization of the Bloch sphere representation
[
Die06
,
WW08
]
, in operator algebras theory
[
Pis03
,
BN18
]
, and in non-local games
[
Slo11
,
Ost16
]
.
This choice is motivated by the fact that the Clifford generators used below, were shown in
[
BN18
]
to give rise to maximally incompatible binary observables. Thus, the measurements associated with different keys are extremal from the viewpoint of joint measurability, making them natural candidates for an uncloneable encryption scheme, where security should arise from the impossibility of simultaneously reproducing such incompatible measurement outcomes.

For any integer
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, the free real associative algebra generated by
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
, and subject to the anti-commutation relations:

\left\{\Gamma_{i},\Gamma_{j}\right\}\coloneqq\Gamma_{i}\Gamma_{j}+\Gamma_{j}\Gamma_{i}=2\,\delta_{ij}\mathbbm{1}\,,

is called the
Clifford algebra
, and denoted
\mathbf{CL}_{n}
. Any irreducible representation of the Clifford algebras
\mathbf{CL}_{2n}
and
\mathbf{CL}_{2n+1}
can be constructed explicitly by the following
2n+1

\mathbb{C}^{2^{n}}
, called the Jordan-Wigner transformation
[
JW93
]
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,2i-1}

\displaystyle=X^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes Y\otimes I^{\otimes(n-i)},\hskip 10.00002pti\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,2i}

\displaystyle=X^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes Z\otimes I^{\otimes(n-i)},\hskip 10.00002pti\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,2n+1}

\displaystyle=X^{\otimes n}\,,

These operators are traceless, and as Hermitian unitaries, they have spectrum
\pm 1
. Mapping the generators
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{2n}
to
\sigma_{n,1},\ldots,\sigma_{n,2n}
gives the irreducible representation of the even Clifford algebra
\mathbf{CL}_{2n}
. Mapping the generators
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{2n+1}
to
\sigma_{n,1},\ldots,\sigma_{n,2n+1}
or
\sigma_{n,1},\ldots,\shortminus\sigma_{n,2n+1}
gives the two inequivalent irreducible representations of the odd Clifford algebra
\mathbf{CL}_{2n+1}
.

An important property of the Clifford algebra is that
2n
Hermitian anti-commuting operators
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{2n}
can be view as orthogonal vectors, for the normalized Frobenius inner product, forming a basis for the
2n

we have

\bigg\langle\sum^{2n}_{i=1}u_{i}\Gamma_{i},\sum^{2n}_{j=1}v_{j}\Gamma_{j}\bigg\rangle=\frac{1}{2n}\bigg\lparen\sum^{2n}_{i=1}u_{i}v_{i}\underbracket{\,\Tr\big\lparen\Gamma^{2}_{i}\big\rparen\,}_{=2n}+\sum^{2n}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}(u_{i}v_{j}-u_{j}v_{i})\underbracket{\,\Tr\big\lparen\Gamma_{i}\Gamma_{j}\big\rparen\,}_{=0}\bigg\rparen=\langle u,v\rangle\,.

In particular for a unit vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, the operator
\sum^{2n}_{i=1}v_{i}\Gamma_{i}
is a Hermitian unitary. Using this property, given any vector

, we have

\bigg\lVert\sum^{2n}_{i=1}v_{i}\Gamma_{i}\bigg\rVert_{\text{op}}=\lVert v\rVert_{2}\,,

(10)

and taking the all-ones vector, we get
\big\lVert\sum^{2n}_{i=1}\Gamma_{i}\big\rVert_{\text{op}}=\sqrt{2n}
.

\displaystyle\leqslant\,\Bigg\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k}\otimes(\pm 1)\otimes 1\Bigg\rVert_{\text{op}}+\;\Bigg\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k}\otimes 1\otimes(\pm 1)\Bigg\rVert_{\text{op}}+\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Bigg\lVert\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\pm 1\\
&\ddots\\
&&\pm 1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\Bigg\rVert_{\text{op}}

\displaystyle=\,\sqrt{K}+\sqrt{K}+K\,,

because
\big\lVert\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k}\otimes 1\otimes(\pm 1)\big\rVert_{\text{op}}=\big\lVert\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k}\big\rVert_{\text{op}}=\sqrt{K}
using
eq.

10
.
∎

We give a connection between the operator norm of
W
and the supremum of
\left\langle\psi\right|W\left|\psi\right\rangle
:

Lemma 3

(Expression of the Operator Norm)
.

For all
K\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, we have the two following equalities:

\sup_{U}\sup_{\lVert\psi\rVert=1}\left\langle\psi\right|W\left|\psi\right\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{=}}\hskip 10.00002pt\sup_{U}\sup_{\lVert\psi\rVert=1}\lvert\left\langle\psi\right|W\left|\psi\right\rangle\lvert\hskip 10.00002pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2)}}{{=}}\hskip 10.00002pt\sup_{U}\lVert W\rVert_{\text{op}}\,,

where the suprema are taken over all Hermitian unitaries
U_{k}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}^{D})
.

Proof.

Let
W^{\prime}\coloneqq W+K\cdot I_{d}\otimes I_{D}\otimes I_{D}
, then we can write,

W^{\prime}=\sum_{k}\Big(\Gamma_{k}\otimes I_{D}\otimes I_{D}+I_{d}\otimes U_{k}\otimes I_{D}\Big)\,\Big(\Gamma_{k}\otimes I_{D}\otimes I_{D}+I_{d}\otimes I_{D}\otimes U_{k}\Big)\,.

Each
\Gamma_{k}
is a Hermitian unitary with a symmetric spectrum
\pm 1
. There exist an invertible matrix
H_{k}
that can diagonalize
\Gamma_{k}
,
i.e.

\Gamma_{k}=H_{k}\begin{pmatrix}-I_{d/2}&0\\
0&I_{d/2}\end{pmatrix}H_{k}^{\shortminus 1}\,.

Thus, under those changes of basis, the operator
W^{\prime}
is expressed as follows:

W^{\prime}=\sum_{k}\tilde{H}_{k}\begin{pmatrix}(I-U_{k})^{\otimes 2}&0\\
0&(I+U_{k})^{\otimes 2}\end{pmatrix}\tilde{H}_{k}^{\shortminus 1}\,,

with
\tilde{H}_{k}\coloneqq H_{k}\otimes I_{D}\otimes I_{D}
. Since for all
U_{k}
, the inequalities
-I_{D}\preccurlyeq U_{k}\preccurlyeq I_{D}
hold, the two operators
I+U_{k}
and
I-U_{k}
are both positive semi-definite, so is each term of the sum, and thus
W^{\prime}\succcurlyeq 0
.

The first equality holds since,

\sup_{\lVert\psi\rVert=1}\lvert\left\langle\psi\right|W\left|\psi\right\rangle\lvert=\max\Big\{\sup\nolimits_{\lVert\psi\rVert=1}\left\langle\psi\right|W\left|\psi\right\rangle,-\inf\nolimits_{\lVert\psi\rVert=1}\left\langle\psi\right|W\left|\psi\right\rangle\Big\}\,,

but because
W^{\prime}\succcurlyeq 0
, we have that
-\sup_{U}\inf_{\lVert\psi\rVert=1}\left\langle\psi\right|W\left|\psi\right\rangle=K
, and when all
U_{k}
are equal to
I_{D}
, we already know that
\sup_{\lVert\psi\rVert=1}\left\langle\psi\right|W\left|\psi\right\rangle=K+2\sqrt{K}
from
Proposition

1
.

Finally, the second equality is always true for Hermitian operators
[
Zim90
, Lemma 3.2.4]
, and
W
is a Hermitian operator, as a sum of tensor products of Hermitian operators
\Gamma_{k}
and
U_{k}
.
∎

Hence, the three upper-bounds
eqs.

5
,
6
and
7
of the winning probability of the no-cloning game for three adversaries
(\text{P},\text{B},\text{C})
, are all equal, and
Conjecture

1
can also be stated as the largest eigenvalue of the operator
W
.

From
Lemma

3
, see that an equivalent formulation of
Conjecture

1
is:

\sup_{U}\sup_{\lVert\psi\rVert=1}\left\langle\psi\right|W\left|\psi\right\rangle\,\leqslant\,K+2\,\sqrt{K}\,,

where the quantum state
|\psi\rangle
is taken in
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{D}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{D}
. We show that the conjecture holds in the restricted case where
|\psi\rangle
is a product state between Alice and
\{\text{Bob, Charlie}\}
:

Proposition 4

(True for Product State)
.

If the state is of the form
|\psi\rangle=|\alpha_{\text{A}}\rangle\otimes|\varphi_{\text{B}\text{C}}\rangle
, then:

\sup_{U}\left\langle\psi\right|W\left|\psi\right\rangle\leqslant K+2\,\sqrt{K}\,.

Proof.

We have:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big\langle\alpha_{\text{A}}\otimes\varphi_{\text{B}\text{C}}\,\big|\,\Big(\Gamma_{k}\otimes(U_{k}\otimes I_{D}+I_{D}\otimes U_{k})+I_{d}\otimes U_{k}\otimes U_{k}\Big)\,\big|\,\alpha_{\text{A}}\otimes\varphi_{\text{B}\text{C}}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big\langle\alpha_{\text{A}}\,\big|\,\Gamma_{k}\,\big|\,\alpha_{\text{A}}\big\rangle\underbrace{\big\langle\varphi_{BC}\,|\,(U_{k}\otimes I_{D}+I_{D}\otimes U_{k})\,|\,\varphi_{BC}\big\rangle}_{=:c_{k}}

\displaystyle\,+\,\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big\langle\alpha_{\text{A}}\otimes\varphi_{\text{B}\text{C}}\,\big|\,I_{d}\otimes U_{k}\otimes U_{k}\,\big|\,\alpha_{\text{A}}\otimes\varphi_{\text{B}\text{C}}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\big\langle\alpha_{\text{A}}\,\big|\,c_{k}\,\Gamma_{k}\,\big|\,\alpha_{\text{A}}\big\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\underbrace{\big\langle\alpha_{\text{A}}\otimes\varphi_{\text{B}\text{C}}\,\big|\,I_{d}\otimes U_{k}\otimes U_{k}\,\big|\,\alpha_{\text{A}}\otimes\varphi_{\text{B}\text{C}}\big\rangle}_{\leqslant 1}

\displaystyle\,\leqslant\,

\displaystyle\big\lVert(c_{1},\dots,c_{K})\big\rVert_{2}+K

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle 2\,\sqrt{K}+K\,,

using
eq.

10
in the second last line.
∎

Remark 5
.

When
|\psi\rangle
is a product state in all its tensors,
i.e.
when
|\psi\rangle=|\alpha_{\text{A}}\otimes\beta_{\text{B}}\otimes\gamma_{\text{C}}\rangle
, then this result is equivalent to the one in
Proposition

2
.

3.5
Indistinguishability

Analogously to the two variants of uncloneable-indistinguishability security presented in
Definition

4
, namely those with a strong convergence rate and those with an arbitrary convergence rate, we can similarly define two corresponding notions of indistinguishability security.

The
strong indistinguishability security
requires that any adversary, upon receiving the encryption of a message
m
randomly chosen from a pair of messages, cannot predict the value of
m
with a probability greater than negligibly close to
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
. If the adversary’s probability of correctly predicting the encrypted message converges to
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
at any arbitrary rate, we refer to this security notion as simply
indistinguishability security
.

In this section, we prove that our candidate scheme
Definition

5
satisfies the latter indistinguishability security. Indeed, the success probability of such adversary is bounded by,

\Pr[\text{win}]\leqslant\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Phi\\
\{M_{0},M_{1}\}\end{subarray}}\>\E_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\{0,1\}\\
k\leftarrow\Gen(1^{\lambda})\end{subarray}}\>\Tr\big[\Phi(\rho_{m,k})M_{m}\big]\,,

with
\rho_{m,k}\coloneqq\Enc(m,k)
, and where the expected values are taken with respect to the uniform measures, and the supremum is taken over all
cptp
map
\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{d})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{D})
(for all finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{D}
), and all binary
povm

\{M_{0},M_{1}\}
. We now follow the same sequence of equations as presented in
Section

2
to derive the result, which is outlined below:

\Pr[\text{win}]\leqslant\sup_{M}\Big\lVert\E_{m,k}\big[d\cdot\rho^{\mathsf{T}}_{m,k}\otimes M_{m}\big]\Big\rVert_{\text{op}},

where the supremum is taken over all binary
povm

\{M_{0},M_{1}\}
.
We proceed by adapting
eq.

11
, starting from the observable form
M_{i}=\frac{I_{D}+(\shortminus 1)^{i}U}{2}
for some Hermitian unitary
U
, to derive the following inequalities,

\displaystyle\Pr[\text{win}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{U}\Bigg\lVert\E_{m,k}\bigg[d\cdot\frac{2}{d}\frac{I_{d}+(\shortminus 1)^{m}\Gamma_{k}}{2}\otimes\frac{I_{D}+(\shortminus 1)^{m}U}{2}\bigg]\Bigg\rVert_{\text{op}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{U}\frac{1}{2K}\Bigg\lVert\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m,k}\Big(I_{d}\otimes I_{D}+(\shortminus 1)^{m}\big\lparen\Gamma_{k}\otimes I_{D}+I_{d}\otimes U\big\rparen+(\shortminus 1)^{2m}\,\Gamma_{k}\otimes U\Big)\Bigg\rVert_{\text{op}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2K}\sup_{U}\bigg\lVert\sum_{k}\Gamma_{k}\otimes U\bigg\rVert_{\text{op}}\,,

where the supremum is taken over all Hermitian unitaries
U_{k}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}^{D})
. By applying the sub-multiplicative property of the operator norm (
i.e.
,
\lVert A\otimes B\rVert_{\text{op}}\leqslant\lVert A\rVert_{\text{op}}\cdot\lVert B\rVert_{\text{op}}
for all
A
and
B
), along with the fact that the operator norm of a unitary is one, and the inequality
\big\lVert\sum_{k}\Gamma_{k}\big\rVert_{\text{op}}=\sqrt{K}
, we have the following upper-bound:

\Pr[\text{win}]\leqslant\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{K}}\,.

This ensures a quadratic convergence rate for the indistinguishability security of our Clifford encryption scheme.

Inapplicability of the Triangular Inequality.

As we saw in

.
In light of the property of

\bigg\lVert\sum_{k\in[K]}\Gamma_{k}\otimes\big(U_{k}\otimes I_{D}+I_{D}\otimes U_{k}\big)\bigg\rVert_{\text{op}}+\bigg\lVert\sum_{k\in[K]}I_{d}\otimes U_{k}\otimes U_{k}\bigg\rVert_{\text{op}}\!,

In comparison, when all

, the complete operator norm
\big\lVert\sum_{k\in[K]}\Gamma_{k}\otimes(U_{k}\otimes I_{D}+I_{D}\otimes U_{k})+I_{d}\otimes U_{k}\otimes U_{k}\big\rVert_{\text{op}}
is smaller than
K+2\sqrt{K}
, with first values:

4,\;3,\;6,\;7,\;8,\;9,\;10,\;11,\;\ldots

4.1
Elementary Proofs for
K=2

We present two distinct preliminary proofs showing that
Conjecture

1
is true for
K=2
, a first one using the equivalence with the
[
BL20
]
scheme, and another one based on the Sum-of-Squares method.
In
Section

4.2
, we will extend the second proof to larger values of
K
, with much more material involved.
Note that a third proof can also be obtained by exploiting the anti-commutation of the two pairs of matrices in
W_{2}
and the recent results in
[
HO21
,
dGHG23
,
XSW24
,
MH24
]
regarding uncertainty relations.

4.1.1
First Proof, with the BB84 MoE Game

As we saw in
Section

3.1
, for
K=2
our candidate scheme coincides with the scheme defined in
[
BL20
]
, thus the upper-bound of the winning probability of the no-cloning game for three adversaries
(\text{P},\text{B},\text{C})
is the same as in
[
BL20
]
,
i.e.

\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
. Thus, by
Lemma

3
,

\Pr[\text{win}]\leqslant\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4K}\sup_{U}\Big\lVert\sum_{k}\Big(\Gamma_{k}\otimes(U_{k}\otimes I_{D}+I_{D}\otimes U_{k})+I_{d}\otimes U_{k}\otimes U_{k}\Big)\Big\rVert_{\text{op}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\,,

which implies that
\lVert W_{2}\rVert_{\text{op}}\leqslant 2+2\sqrt{2}
as claimed.

4.1.2
Second Proof, with Sum-of-Squares

From
eq.

5
, the upper-bound of the winning probability of the no-cloning game for three adversaries
(\text{P},\text{B},\text{C})
is

\Pr[\text{win}]\leqslant\sup_{\psi,B,C}\left\langle\psi\right|\E_{m,k}\big[d\cdot\rho^{\mathsf{T}}_{m,k}\otimes B_{m|k}\otimes C_{m|k}\big]\left|\psi\right\rangle\,,

where the supremum is taken over all
\lVert\psi\rVert=1
, all families of
pvm

\{B_{i|k}\}
and
\{C_{j|k}\}
, as well as their respective dimensions. Following the same steps as
Section

3
, we found

\Pr[\text{win}]\leqslant\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4K}\sup_{\psi,B,C}\left\langle\psi\right|\sum_{k}\Big(\Gamma_{k}\otimes(B_{k}\otimes I_{D}+I_{D}\otimes C_{k})+I_{d}\otimes B_{k}\otimes C_{k}\Big)\left|\psi\right\rangle\,,

(14)

where the supremum is now taken over all families of observables
\{B_{k}\}
and
\{C_{k}\}
. Note that this time, we do not assume the adversaries
(\text{P},\text{B},\text{C})
to be symmetric,
i.e.

B
and
C
may have different observables. The last part of the upper-bound
eq.

14
can be stated as the optimization problem:

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi,B,C}

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi\right|\sum_{k}\Big(\Gamma_{k}\otimes(B_{k}\otimes I_{D}+I_{D}\otimes C_{k})+I_{d}\otimes B_{k}\otimes C_{k}\Big)\left|\psi\right\rangle\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\cdot}\>\lVert\psi\rVert=1\,,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\cdot}\>B^{*}_{i}=B_{i}\hskip 10.00002pt\boldsymbol{\cdot}\>C^{*}_{i}=C_{i}\hskip 10.00002pt\boldsymbol{\cdot}\>B^{2}=C^{2}=I_{D}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall i\,.

This problem can be relaxed through the use of what is called commuting operator strategies, in which the tensor product structure between Alice’s and Bob’s operators is replaced by the assumption that these operators commute:

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi,b,c}

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi\right|\sum_{k}\Big(\Gamma_{k}\otimes(b_{k}+c_{k})+I_{d}\otimes b_{k}\cdot c_{k}\Big)\left|\psi\right\rangle\,,

(16)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\cdot}\>\lVert\psi\rVert=1\,,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\cdot}\>b^{*}_{i}=b_{i}\hskip 10.00002pt\boldsymbol{\cdot}\>c^{*}_{i}=c_{i}\hskip 10.00002pt\boldsymbol{\cdot}\>b^{2}_{i}=c^{2}_{i}=I_{D}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall i\,,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\cdot}\>\left[b_{i},c_{j}\right]=0\hskip 10.00002pt\forall i,j\,.

An optimal value for the problem
eq.

15
is immediately a lower-bound for the problem
eq.

16
by taking
b_{k}\coloneqq B_{k}\otimes I_{D}
and
c_{k}\coloneqq I_{D}\otimes C_{k}
. The question of the equality of the two optimization problems
eqs.

15
and
16
(and in general of the tensor-based
versus
the commuting-based models) was a long-standing problem that was only recently refuted by
[
JNV+21
]
. However, in the case of finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, the equality holds as an inductive consequence of Tsirelson’s theorem
[
Tsi93
,
RXL26
]
.

If the optimal value of the problem
eq.

16
is
K+2\sqrt{K}
, then the Hermitian operator

P_{K}\coloneqq(K+2\sqrt{K})\cdot I_{d}\otimes I_{D^{2}}-\sum_{k}\Big(\Gamma_{k}\otimes(b_{k}+c_{k})+I_{d}\otimes b_{k}\cdot c_{k}\Big)\,,

(17)

must be positive semi-definite under the constraints of
eq.

16
. If we can find a family
\{H_{i}\}_{i}
such that
P_{k}=\sum_{i}H_{i}^{*}H_{i}
, then the operator
P_{K}
would be guaranteed to be positive semi-definite, as a sum of positive semi-definite operators
H_{i}^{*}H_{i}
. If all
H_{i}
are also Hermitian, then we can write
P_{K}=\sum_{i}H^{2}_{i}
. This constitutes the fundamental principle of the Sum-of-Squares (SoS) decomposition technique, which is used to establish bounds on a variety of quantum correlations, including the CHSH Bell inequality
[
Bel64
,
CHSH69
]
and its associated Tsirelson bound
[
Cir80
]
.

Let
P(\mathbf{X})
be an Hermitian polynomial in non-commutative variables
\mathbf{X}={(X_{i})}_{i}
, then
P(\mathbf{X})
is a positive semi-definite polynomial (
i.e.

P(\mathbf{X})\succcurlyeq 0
for all evaluations of
P
on matrices
\mathbf{X}
) if and only if
P(\mathbf{X})
is a Hermitian Sum-of-Squares
[
McC01
,
Hel02
]
:

P(\mathbf{X})=\sum_{i}H_{i}(\mathbf{X})^{*}\;H_{i}(\mathbf{X})\,.

This result can be used to maximize the operator norm of a non-commutative Hermitian polynomial. If the polynomial is constrained to an archimedean semi-algebraic set (a condition that is satisfied in our case), a hierarchy of converging upper-bounds can be obtained based on semi-definite optimization programs (SDP)
[
HM04
]
. The dual problem of those SDPs forms the Navascués-Pironio-Acín (NPA) hierarchy
[
NPA08
]
and correspond to the non-commutative variant of the Lasserre’s hierarchy
[
Las01
]
.

For
K=2
such SoS decomposition was already known
[
BC23a
]
:

P_{2}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\big\lparen X\otimes b_{1}+Z\otimes c_{2}-\sqrt{2}\cdot I\big\rparen^{2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\big\lparen X\otimes c_{1}+Z\otimes b_{2}-\sqrt{2}\cdot I\big\rparen^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big\lparen b_{1}-c_{1}\big\rparen^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big\lparen b_{2}-c_{2}\big\rparen^{2}\,,

where we took
\Gamma_{1}\coloneqq X
and
\Gamma_{2}\coloneqq Z
. This implies, using
Lemma

3
, that
\lVert W_{2}\rVert_{\text{op}}\leqslant 2+2\sqrt{2}
.

4.2
Proofs for
K\leqslant 7
and Asymptotic Upper-Bound

We prove that
Conjecture

1
is true for all
K\leqslant 7
in two manners: first using a family of SoS decompositions, then based on its dual SDP problem, the NPA hierarchy.

4.2.1
First Proof, with Sum-of-Squares

We refer to
Section

4.1.2
for intuition and background about the SoS method.
Let
K\in\{2,\ldots,7\}
.
The non-negativity certificate of
P_{K}
, defined in
eq.

17
, is given by the following SoS:

P_{K}\,=\,\frac{K-\sqrt{K}}{2K(K-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\bigg(Q_{K}+(\sqrt{K}+1)\,\Gamma_{i}\otimes(c_{i}-b_{i})\bigg)^{2}+\alpha_{K}\,Q_{K}^{2}\,,

(18)

where:

Q_{K}\,:=\,\sqrt{K}\,I\otimes I-\sum_{j=1}^{K}(\Gamma_{j}\otimes c_{j})\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha_{K}\,:=\,\frac{(3K-2)\sqrt{K}-K^{2}}{2K(K-1)}\,.

Notice that the coefficient
\alpha_{K}
is positive for all
K\leqslant 7
, so
eq.

18
is a valide SoS decomposition for
P_{2},...,P_{7}
, which validates
Conjecture

1
for
K\leqslant 7
.
However, for
K\geqslant 8
the coefficient
\alpha_{K}
is negative, so that the decomposition presented in
eq.

18
no longer provides a valid non-negativity certificate.
Note that it does not exclude the possibility of finding another SoS decomposition valid for larger values of
K
.
It is also worth mentioning that the SoS decomposition given in
eq.

18
can be readily symmetrized with respect to the variables
b_{i}
and
c_{i}
— the current formulation has been chosen for its simplicity.

4.2.2
Second Proof, with NPA Level-1, and Asymptotic Result

The NPA hierarchy is dual to the sum-of-squares (SoS) approach. At level
n
of the hierarchy, we keep track of only the relations on moments of order up to and including
n
of the operators. This gives rise to a SDP maximisation whose optimal value is precisely the value that can be certified by a SoS certificate where the squared terms have order
n
. For NPA level
1
, we only consider the relations that are first order in the
b_{i}
and
c_{i}
.

To simplify the relations we work with in the NPA hierarchy, we first express the game value in terms of an anticommuting version of the scenario algebra. Then, at NPA level
1
, we relax the optimisation to only depend on the first-order moments of the observables.
This turns the problem into an SDP, which can be approximated efficiently. Making use of the symmetry of the problem allows us to drastically simplify the SDP we need to solve, allowing us to do it analytically.
In
Section

4.3
,
we pass to NPA level 2 and we proceed in the same way.
There, we have to consider the second-order moments.
We make use of analogous simplifications by symmetry to get a simpler problem; however, this problem becomes too complicated to solve analytically, so we solve it numerically to find the values.

We introduce two scenario algebras and prove that the optimal value of our conjecture is equivalent to the supremum of the operator norm of a new problem:

Definition 6

(Scenario Algebra)
.

The
scenario algebra

\mathcal{A}(K)
is the
\ast
-algebra generated by
b_{1},c_{1},\ldots,b_{K},c_{K}
such that
b_{i}^{2}=c_{i}^{2}=1
,
b_{i}^{\ast}=b_{i}
,
c_{i}^{\ast}=c_{i}
, and
b_{i}c_{j}=c_{j}b_{i}
for all
i,j\in[K]
.

The
anticommuting scenario algebra

\mathcal{A}_{ac}(K)
is the
\ast
-algebra generated by
\hat{b}_{1},\hat{c}_{1},\ldots,\hat{b}_{K},\hat{c}_{K}
such that
\hat{b}_{i}^{2}=\hat{c}_{i}^{2}=1
,
\hat{b}_{i}^{\ast}=\hat{b}_{i}
,
\hat{c}_{i}^{\ast}=\hat{c}_{i}
,
\hat{b}_{i}\hat{c}_{i}=\hat{c}_{i}\hat{b}_{i}
for all
i
, and
\hat{b}_{i}\hat{c}_{j}=-\hat{c}_{j}\hat{b}_{i}
for all
i\neq j\in[K]
.

In our case, we can take, as a representation,
\Gamma_{i}\otimes B_{i}\otimes I
for
\hat{b}_{i}
and
\Gamma_{i}\otimes I\otimes C_{i}
for
\hat{c}_{i}
. The game polynomial can be seen as an element
p_{K}\in\mathcal{M}_{d}\otimes\mathcal{A}(K)
, with
p_{K}=\sum_{k}\Big\lparen\Gamma_{k}\otimes(b_{k}+c_{k})+I\otimes b_{k}c_{k}\Big\rparen
so that the winning probability is
\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4K}\sup_{\pi}\lVert(\id\otimes\pi)(p_{K})\rVert

We present in this subsection a heuristic algorithm for optimizing the largest eigenvalue of the operator

W_{K}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Big(\Gamma_{k}\otimes B_{k}\otimes I+\Gamma_{k}\otimes I\otimes C_{k}+I\otimes B_{k}\otimes C_{k}\Big)\,,

over self-adjoint contractions
B_{1},C_{1},\ldots,B_{K},C_{K}
. We shall use the
alternating optimization
(or
seesaw
) method
[
BH02
]
. Note that our goal is to maximize a convex function over a convex set,
i.e.

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|z\|=1\\
\lVert B_{k}\rVert_{\text{op}}\leqslant 1\\
\lVert C_{k}\rVert_{\text{op}}\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\langle z|W_{K}|z\rangle\,,

hence our problem does not fall in the classical convex optimization framework.

Our algorithm optimizes iteratively over each of the variables
z
,
\{B_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
, and
\{C_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
for a pre-determined number of iterations
M
. The variables
z,B_{k},C_{k}
are initialized with random values (
z
uniform on the unit sphere of
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{D}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{D}
, and
B_{k}
,
C_{k}
i.i.d. with Haar-distributed eigenvectors and half of eigenvalues
\pm 1
). We give below the details of each optimization step.

1.

Optimizing
z
: compute the largest eigenvalue of the current operator
P
and assign to
z
the corresponding eigenvector.

2.

Optimizing the contractions
B
: permute the first two tensors to rewrite the problem as

\langle z|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k}\otimes I\otimes C_{k}|z\rangle+\max_{\lVert B_{k}\rVert_{\text{op}}\leqslant 1}\langle z_{1\leftrightarrow 2}|\sum_{k=1}^{K}B_{k}\otimes(\Gamma_{k}\otimes I+I\otimes C_{k})|z_{1\leftrightarrow 2}\rangle\,,

where
z_{1\leftrightarrow 2}
is the 3-tensor
z
with the first two factors permuted. Clearly, the maximum above is equal to the sum of maxima over individual
B_{k}
’s that we can further decompose as

\displaystyle\max_{\lVert B_{k}\rVert_{\text{op}}\leqslant 1}\langle z_{1\leftrightarrow 2}|B_{k}\otimes(\Gamma_{k}\otimes I+I\otimes C_{k})|z_{1\leftrightarrow 2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\max_{\lVert B_{k}\rVert_{\text{op}}\leqslant 1}\langle B_{k},Z_{13|2}^{*}(\Gamma_{k}\otimes I+I\otimes C_{k})Z_{13|2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|Z_{13|2}^{*}(\Gamma_{k}\otimes I+I\otimes C_{k})Z_{13|2}\|_{1}\,,

where
Z_{13|2}\in\mathcal{M}_{Dd\times D}
is the reshaping of the 3-tensor
z_{1\leftrightarrow 2}
, see
Figure

5

\max_{\lVert B\rVert_{\text{op}}\leqslant 1}\langle B,X\rangle=\|X\|_{1}\,,

with the maximum being attained for

B_{\textrm{opt}}=\sum_{i}\operatorname{sign}(\lambda_{i})\left|x_{i}\middle\rangle\mkern-4.0mu plus -2.0mu minus -4.0mu\middle\langle x_{i}\right|\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for}\hskip 20.00003ptX=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\left|x_{i}\middle\rangle\mkern-4.0mu plus -2.0mu minus -4.0mu\middle\langle x_{i}\right|\,.

We apply this procedure for all the maximization problems corresponding to the

Optimizing the contractions

The circular law for non-Hermitian random band matrices up to bandwidth N 1 / 2 + c N^{1/2+c}
Yi HAN

Abstract
. We consider inhomogeneous square random matrices of size N N with independent entries of mean 0 and finite variance. We assume that the variance profile of this matrix is doubly stochastic and has a band-like structure with an appropriately defined bandwidth W W . We prove that when the entries have a bounded density and a subgaussian tail, then the empirical spectral measure for the eigenvalues of the matrix converges to the circular law as N N tends to infinity whenever W ≥ N 1 / 2 + c W\geq N^{1/2+c} for any c > 0 c>0 . In the special case of block band matrices the density assumption is not needed and the moment condition is relaxed. This establishes the circular law limit throughout the entire delocalization regime in 1-d: W ≥ N 1 / 2 + c W\geq N^{1/2+c} and extends the previous thresholds for the circular law limit with exponent 5 6 , 8 9 , 33 34 \frac{5}{6},\frac{8}{9},\frac{33}{34} in N N . The main technical input is a new lower bound on the small-ish singular values via Green function estimates and a new lower bound on the least singular value with fewer moment conditions.



1.
Introduction

Let
A=(a_{ij})
be an
N\times N
square random matrix whose eigenvalues are denoted by
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N}
. The empirical spectral density (ESD)
\mu_{A}
of
A
is defined as the following counting measure

\mu_{A}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}.

When the entries
a_{ij}
of
A
are i.i.d of a fixed distribution
\zeta
with mean 0 and variance 1, the convergence of
\mu_{A}
to the circular law, i.e. the uniform measure on the unit disk centered at the origin of the complex plane, is one of the major cornerstones in modern random matrix theory. This was proven by Tao and Vu
[
33
]
as the culmination of many earlier breakthroughs, including Bai
[
2
]
and Götze and Tikhomirov
[
21
]
. The convergence of ESDs to the circular law for many sparse non-Hermitian matrices has also been established for sparse directed graphs in
[
27
]
and for sparse i.i.d. matrices in
[
30
]
(see
[
35
]
for a more comprehensive literature review).

The main interest in this paper is the convergence of ESDs
\mu_{A}
in the case where
A
is a band matrix, where the bandwidth scales sublinearly in
n
. This question lies at the interface between the work on circular law we just mentioned and the very recent breakthroughs in the study of Hermitian random band matrices, which we briefly describe here. For Hermitian band matrices, the convergence of ESD to the semicircle law for growing
W
is immediate via the method of moments, but much finer asymptotics on eigenvectors and local eigenvalue statistics have been investigated in recent years. For 1-d Hermitian random band matrices with size
N
and bandwidth
W
, recently Yau and Yin
[
36
]
justified for a special model that whenever
W\geq N^{1/2+c}
for any
c>0
, the eigenvectors of this band matrix are completely delocalized and quantum unique ergodicity and universality of eigenvalue statistics hold. The same results are proven for more general 1-d random band matrices by Erdős and Riabov in
[
15
]
. Higher-dimensional Hermitian band matrices are also studied in
[
14
]
and
[
13
]
. In the other regime
W\leq N^{1/2-c}
, the exponential localization of eigenfunctions was recently proven in Reuben
[
12
]
, extending the previous ranges of
W
in
[
8
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
32
]
.

Non-Hermitian versions of random band matrices are very general topics of investigation, as removing the symmetry constraint in a Hermitian band matrix gives rise to such a model. However, even the seemingly most elementary problem, the convergence of
\mu_{X}
to a deterministic limit, remains very difficult to solve. For this non-Hermitian matrix, one needs to use Girko’s Hermitization trick
[
20
]
,
[
19
]
to establish convergence of
\mu_{X}
, but a rigorous application of this Hermitization trick requires the justification of a uniform integrability condition relating to the least singular values. The latter is difficult to check, since
\sigma_{min}(X)
, the minimal singular value, is difficult to estimate for band matrices. Cook
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
settled the problem for dense inhomogeneous matrices but left open the case of genuinely banded matrices. Currently, there are only very few papers on this topic, including
[
26
]
and
[
34
]
, and you can also see improvement in
[
25
]
. The main results of these papers can be summarized as follows: when the variance profile of
A
is doubly stochastic and the bandwidth
W\geq N^{1-c}
for a small
c>0
, then under further conditions on the density of entries of
A
or assuming a special block structure of
A
, we can prove the convergence of
\mu_{A}
to the circular law. The current best value
[
25
]
for
c
is
c=\frac{1}{6}
for the Gaussian case and
c=\frac{1}{9}
for the non-Gaussian case, but these thresholds are clearly suboptimal in light of recent breakthroughs in Hermitian band matrices.

The primary objective of this paper is to prove convergence towards the circular law in the whole delocalized regime, i.e.
W\geq N^{1/2+c}
for any small
c>0
. This threshold
\sqrt{N}
is clearly of major physical significance, and proving the convergence of
\mu_{A}
is the first step towards universality investigations for these non-Hermitian band matrix models.

The matrix model we consider can be defined as follows. Throughout the paper, we use the symbol
[N]
for an integer
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
to denote the collection of integers
[N]:=\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

(Random band matrix with independent entries) Let
X=(b_{ij}x_{ij})
be an
N\times N
random matrix where
b_{ij}
are fixed scalars and the
x_{ij}
are independently distributed random variables with mean 0 and variance 1.

Define the variance profile
S
of
X
via

S=(S_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}=\{b_{ij}^{2}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
. We assume that
S
satisfies the following three conditions:

(1)

(Double stochasticity)
\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{ij}^{2}=1
for each
i\in[N]
and
\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{ij}^{2}=1
for each
j\in[N]
.

(2)

(Bandwidth) we assume that
\sup_{i,j}b_{ij}^{2}\leq C_{W}W^{-1}
for some (
N
-dependent) constant
W\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and some
(N
-independent) constant
C_{W}<\infty
,

(3)

(Bounded inverse) Assume that for each
z\in\mathbb{C}:0<|z|<1
there exists (sufficiently large)
C_{S,z}>0
and (sufficiently small)
c_{S,z}>0
, both of which depending only on
|z|\in(0,1)
and independent of
N
and
W
, such that for any
y_{1}\in\mathbb{C}:|y_{1}+1-|z|^{2}|\leq c_{S,z}
and
y_{2}\in\mathbb{C}:|y_{2}+|z|^{2}|\leq c_{S,z}
, we have that

(1.1)

\left\|\left(I_{2N}-\begin{bmatrix}y_{2}S&y_{1}S^{T}\\
y_{1}S&y_{2}S^{T}\end{bmatrix}\right)^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}}\leq C_{S,z}(\log N)^{C_{S,z}},

where
I_{2N}
is the
2N
-dimensional identity matrix and
S^{T}
is the transpose matrix of
S
.

Assumptions (1) and (2) on
S
are standard assumptions in the band matrix literature and the circular law literature.
Assumption (3), namely estimate (
1.1
), is the new input in this context compared to previous circular law articles on band matrices
[
25
]
. Estimate (
1.1
) is a stability condition on the inversion of the variance profile
S
: note that assumption (1) and (2) are too general that includes many inhomogeneous matrices not having a band structure, and assumption (3) quantitatively imposes the band structure on the variance profile. We will check that (
1.1
) is verified for most of the good examples of random band matrices in Example
1.2
and Example
1.3
.
Similar conditions with the flavor of (
1.1
) have appeared in the literature of Hermitian band matrices
[
18
]

[
15
]
, but this condition has not been used before for non-Hermitian matrices and not in the circular law context.

For estimate (
1.1
), note that for any matrix
M
,
\|M^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}}=\sup_{i\in[N]}\sum_{k=1}^{N}|M^{-1}_{ik}|,
and thus we will verify (
1.1
) by directly computing the matrix inverse in each specific case of interest. For the general version (
1.1
) we only need to consider
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
sufficiently close to the specified limit, and we do not require the variance profile
S
to be symmetric, namely
S=S^{T}
. But for the following two specific cases where we verify (
1.1
), we assume that
S
is symmetric and circulant, or
S
has a tensor product structure.

The two main examples of variance profile are the following:

Example 1.2
.

(Block band matrices) We say
X
is sampled from a block band matrix ensemble if it has the form

(1.2)

X=\begin{pmatrix}D_{1}&U_{2}&&&T_{L}\\
T_{1}&D_{2}&U_{3}&&\\
&T_{2}&D_{3}&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&U_{L}\\
U_{1}&&&T_{L-1}&D_{L}\end{pmatrix}

where the unfilled sites are set to zero.
That is, we assume that
N
is divisible by
W
with
N=W\cdot L
and we take
b_{ij}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3W}}\mathbf{1}(|\lceil\frac{i}{W}\rceil-\lceil\frac{j}{W}\rceil|_{L}\in\{0,1\})
, where we use
||_{L}
for the graph distance, i.e.
|i-j|_{L}:=\min\{|i-j|,|L-i+j|\}
.

Example 1.3
.

(Periodic band matrices and, more generally, a circulant variance profile)

Consider a given profile function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)dx=1
. Define the variance profile
S
to satisfy

(1.3)

S_{ab}=\frac{1}{W}f(\frac{|a-b|_{N}}{W}),

where
|a-b|_{N}
is the graph distance. Although
S
may not always be doubly stochastic, since the normalization
\sum_{b}S_{ab}
is independent of
a
, we only need a rescaling by a constant very close to one to ensure that
S
is doubly stochastic. We assume that

(1)

Either
f
is compactly supported and
f
has finite total variation,

(2)

Or for some fixed exponent
D\geq 6
and some fixed
C_{f}<\infty
depending only on
f
,

(1.4)

|f(x)|\leq\frac{C_{f}}{\langle x\rangle^{D+2}},\quad|\partial f(x)|\leq\frac{C_{f}}{\langle x\rangle^{D+3}},\quad|\partial^{2}f(x)|\leq\frac{C_{f}}{\langle x\rangle^{D+4}},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},

where
\langle x\rangle:=|x|+1
.

In particular, taking
f
to be the indicator function on
[0,1]
recovers the periodic band matrix with variance profile
b_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2W+1}}\mathbf{1}(|i-j|_{N}\leq W)
.

We shall verify that all these examples are included in Definition
1.1
:

Lemma 1.4
.

The variance profiles defined in Example
1.2
and Example
1.3
satisfy the estimate (
1.1
) in Definition
1.1
.

The proof of Lemma
1.4
is given in Section
4.1
.

We state the main result on the circular law convergence in this paper as follows.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mu_{c}
be the uniform measure on the unit disk in the center of the complex plane. Then
\mu_{X}
converges in probability to
\mu_{c}
if and only if, for any
\epsilon>0
and any continuous, compactly supported function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

(1.5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(z)d\mu_{X}(z)-\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(z)d\mu_{c}(z)\right|\geq\epsilon\right)=0.

For a mean zero variance one random variable
\zeta
, we say that
\zeta
is
K
-subgaussian if
\mathbb{E}\exp(|\zeta|^{2}/K^{2})\leq 2
, and we define
\|\zeta\|_{\psi_{2}}
to be the smallest
K
such that
\zeta
is
K
-subgaussian.

Theorem 1.6
.

(General models)
Let
X
be a random matrix satisfying the assumptions in Definition
1.1
. We make two further assumptions on
x_{ij}
:

(1)

The
x_{ij}
have uniform subgaussian tails:
\|x_{ij}\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq C<\infty
.

(2)

Either
x_{ij}
are real-valued and each
x_{ij}
has a density on
\mathbb{R}
with density function bounded by
C_{d}<\infty
independent in
N
(and uniform in
i,j\in[N]
). Or
x_{ij}
is complex-valued with independent real and complex parts, both of which have density bounded by
C_{d}<\infty
.

Then whenever
W\geq N^{1/2+c}
for any
c>0
, the ESD
\mu_{X}
converges in probability to the uniform measure on the unit disk in the complex plane as
N
tends to infinity.

In the special case of a block band matrix model in Example
1.2
, we can remove the density assumption and relax the moment assumption on the
x_{ij}
variables:

Theorem 1.7
.

(Block band matrix model) Let
X
be a block band matrix as defined in Example
1.2
. We assume that the random variables
x_{ij}
are i.i.d. with finite
p
-th moment for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
: there exists
\mu_{p}\in(0,\infty)
for each
p
such that
(\mathbb{E}|x_{ij}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}<\mu_{p}<\infty
. Then for any
c>0
, whenever
W\geq N^{1/2+c}
, the ESD
\mu_{X}
converges in probability to the uniform measure on the unit disk of the complex plane as
N
tends to infinity.

More generally, when we assume that a non-vanishing portion of random variables in
X
are gathered within width
W
along the diagonal (this includes the periodic band matrix with
f=\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}
in Example
1.3
), we can significantly relax the moment assumption on
x_{ij}
:

Theorem 1.8
.

(General band structure with fewer moment assumptions) Let
X
be a random matrix satisfying the assumptions in Definition
1.1
. Assume that

(1)

x_{ij}
satisfies the density assumption in Theorem
1.6
, and

(2)

We can find some
c_{W}>0
independent of
N,W
such that
\inf_{i,j}S_{ij}\geq c_{W}W^{-1}
for all
(i,j)
:
|i-j|_{N}\leq c_{W}W
,

(3)

For all
p\in\mathbb{N}
we can find
\mu_{p}<\infty
with
(\mathbb{E}|x_{ij}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}<\mu_{p}
for each
i,j
.

Then for any
c>0
, whenever
W\geq N^{1/2+c}
,

(1)

The ESD
\mu_{X}
converges in probability to the circular law.

(2)

For any
\kappa>0
, we have the following for
\sigma_{min}(X)
, the least singular value of
X
:

\mathbb{P}(\sigma_{min}(X)\leq\exp(-N^{\kappa}\frac{N}{W}))=o(1),\quad\text{ as }N\to\infty.

A more quantitative version for the estimate of
\sigma_{min}(X)
in item (2) of Theorem
1.8
is presented in Theorem
2.9
under much more general conditions on
X
.

1.1.
Some remarks

We list here a family of remarks on comparison to prior works and the assumptions on
x_{ij}
posted in each theorem.

Theorem
1.6
is a partial improvement to the main result of
[
25
]
. In
[
25
]
the author proved convergence of
\mu_{X}
to the circular law for
W\geq N^{5/6+c}
when
X
has Gaussian entries and
W\geq N^{8/9+c}
for some non-Gaussian entries. The setting in
[
25
]
does not require
S
to satisfy the estimate (
1.1
), and the main novelty of this work is that we can significantly extend the bandwidth for circular law throughout the delocalized regime assuming (
1.1
). One concrete example where estimate (
1.1
) is not satisfied is the linearization matrix of the product of a growing number of i.i.d. matrices. It is thus not clear whether
W\geq N^{1/2+c}
is achievable for an arbitrary doubly stochastic variance profile
S
without (
1.1
).

In this block band matrix setting, Theorem
1.7
, the convergence of
\mu_{X}
was first proven in
[
26
]
for
W\geq N^{32/33}\log N
with a slightly weaker moment assumption. In this work, we use the least singular value estimate, Theorem 2.1 in
[
26
]
but improve the range of
W
to
W\geq N^{1/2+c}
. No optimization on the moment assumption on
x_{ij}
is taken here: finiteness of
\mu_{p}
for some large finite
p
may be already sufficient.

The assumption on bounded density and subgaussian moments in Theorem
1.6
arise from the least singular value estimate in
[
34
]
. We present an alternative proof of the main result in
[
34
]
when
W\gg N^{1/2+c}
for the specific case of an inhomogeneous matrix with a band profile, via a much simplified strategy in Theorem
2.9
. The benefit of this alternative proof is a much weaker moment condition on
x_{ij}
and proof for lower bound of
\sigma_{min}(X)
itself: this is the conclusion of (2) of Theorem
1.8
and cannot be derived from
[
34
]
. Whether these least singular value estimates hold without assuming a bounded density remains unclear. A recent investigation on invertibility of such matrices can be found in
[
23
]
.

Proof of circular law

The main new technical input of this paper is the following rigidity result on small-ish singular values of

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
X
is a random matrix in Definition
1.1
, where we assume moreover that
x_{ij}
has all finite moments:
(\mathbb{E}|x_{ij}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\mu_{p}<\infty

\mathbb{P}(X-zI\text{ has at least }\frac{N}{W}N^{\epsilon}\text{singular values in the interval }[0,W^{-1}])=O_{D,\epsilon,\tau^{\prime}}(N^{-D}),

where throughout the paper we use the notation

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We first consider

with

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\sigma_{i}(X_{z})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\sigma_{i}(X_{z})1_{\{\sigma_{i}(X_{z})\geq W^{-1}\}}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\sigma_{i}(X_{z})1_{\{\sigma_{i}(X_{z})\leq W^{-1}\}}.

By Theorem

and
\epsilon>0

terms in the second summation with
\sigma_{i}(X_{z})\leq W^{-1}

Then by Theorem

, for any
\epsilon>0

|\log\sigma_{i}(X_{z})|\leq\frac{N^{1+\epsilon}}{W}
for each

\|X\|\leq 3

\sigma_{1}(X_{z})
is bounded). Thus the second sum vanishes as

and taking

The same estimate in (

by
G_{z}
in the summation.

Now we consider the first sum difference

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\sigma_{i}(X_{z})1_{\{\sigma_{i}(X_{z})\geq W^{-1}\}}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\sigma_{i}(G_{z})1_{\{\sigma_{i}(G_{z})\geq W^{-1}\}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\log W\cdot\|\nu_{X_{z}}-\nu_{G_{z}}\|_{[0,\infty)}=o(1),\quad N\to\infty,

where we first use Lemma
2.8
then use Lemma
2.6
. We also used
\|X_{z}\|\leq 3+|z|
with high probability.
This verifies convergence in probability to zero of (
2.4
) for all
0<|z|<1
.

Then for
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|>1

2.4
and a high probability bound

by Lemma

to get that

for all

with probability

for any

are uniformly bounded, it suffices to use Lemma

combined with Lemma

We have thus verified the convergence in probability to zero of (

to the circular law by Theorem
2.5
.
∎

3.
Proof of local law

In this section, we prove a Green’s function estimate for the resolvent of
(X-zI)^{*}(X-zI)
. The proof combines ideas from
[
18
]
on random band matrices and from
[
5
]
on local law for i.i.d. matrices. We first introduce some standard notations in the literature.

Definition 3.1
.

(Stochastic domination) Consider two families of nonnegative random variables

X=(X^{(N)}(u):N\in\mathbb{N},u\in U^{(N)}),\quad Y=(Y^{(N)}(u):N\in\mathbb{N},u\in U^{(N)}),

where
U^{(N)}
is some parameter set that may depend on
N
. We say that
X
is stochastically dominated by
Y
, which we denote by
X\prec Y
, if for all small
\epsilon>0
and large
D>0
the following estimate holds: whenever
N
is large enough, then

\sup_{u\in U^{(N)}}\mathbb{P}[X^{(N)}(u)\geq N^{\epsilon}Y^{(N)}(u)]\leq N^{-D}.

More generally, if for some family of complex random variables
X
we have
|X|\prec Y
we also write
X=O_{\prec}Y
,

Stochastic domination has the following simple properties:

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
18
]
)
Suppose that
X_{1}(u)\prec Y_{1}(u)
and
X_{2}(u)\prec Y_{2}(u)
uniformly in
u
, then
X_{1}(u)X_{2}(u)\prec Y_{1}(u)Y_{2}(u)
uniformly in
u
.

We use the notation
Y_{z}=X-zI
with
I
the identity operator. Consider the Green function of
Y_{z}^{*}Y_{z}
, and its trace:

(3.1)

G(w):=G(w,z)=(Y_{z}^{*}Y_{z}-w)^{-1},\quad m(w):=m(w,z)=\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Tr}G(w,z),\quad w=E+i\eta.

We also use the following notation of Green function

(3.2)

\mathcal{G}(w):=\mathcal{G}(w,z)=(Y_{z}Y_{z}^{*}-w)^{-1}.

In this paper, we are exclusively interested in the case
E=0
. The parameter set of
\eta>0
we shall consider is defined as follows: for some sufficiently small
\gamma_{0}>0
, (all the constants appearing in later proofs will depend on
\gamma_{0}
)

(3.3)

\mathbf{S}:=\{\eta>0:W^{-2}N^{\gamma_{0}}\leq\eta\leq 10\}.

Recall that
W\geq N^{1/2}
. Thus we consider
\eta
all the way down to slightly larger than
W^{-2}
, which is consistent with
[
5
]
but different from the
W^{-1}
range in
[
18
]
.

We shall prove that for
w=i\eta
with
\eta\in\mathbf{S}
, then
m(w,z)
converges pointwise to
m_{c}(w,z)
with high probability as
N\to\infty
, where
m_{c}=m_{c}(w,z)
being the unique solution with positive imaginary part to the following equation

(3.4)

m_{c}^{-1}=-w(1+m_{c})+|z|^{2}(1+m_{c})^{-1}.

The following properties of
m_{c}
will be used. The proof is given in Section
4.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The following estimate holds uniformly for
w=i\eta,\eta>0
in any compact set:

|m_{c}(w,z)|=O(|w|^{-1/2}).

Moreover, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
\tau_{\epsilon}>0
such that for any
0<\eta<\tau_{\epsilon}
, with the choice
w=i\eta
, we must have

(3.5)

\left|\sqrt{w}m_{c}(w,z)-i\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}})\right|<\epsilon.

The main result on Green’s function estimate that we shall prove is as follows:

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
X
and
z
satisfy the same conditions presented in Theorem
2.1
. Then for any sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
and any
D>0
, we have that whenever
N
is sufficiently large,

\mathbb{P}\left(\cap_{w=i\eta,\eta\in\mathbf{S}}\{|G_{ij}(w)-m_{c}(w)\delta_{ij}|\leq N^{\epsilon}\cdot W^{-1/2}\eta^{-3/4}\}\right)\geq 1-N^{-D}.

We have not tried to optimize the right hand side
W^{-1/2}\eta^{-3/4}
of the estimate: an application of fluctuation averaging could improve the bound. Nonetheless, this estimate is sufficiently good in
\mathbf{S}
: since
|m_{c}(w)|\sim\eta^{-1/2}
, and we have
\eta^{-1/2}\gg W^{-1/2}\eta^{-3/4}
for
\eta\in\mathbf{S}
.

Now we can immediately conclude the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
\sigma_{1}(z),\cdots,\sigma_{N}(z)
be the singular values of
Y_{z}
, then we can write
\lambda_{i}(z)=\sigma_{i}^{2}(z),i\in[N]
for the eigenvalues of
Y_{z}^{*}Y_{z}
.
Then by the following standard relation between the number of eigenvalues and the imaginary part of Stieltjes transform,

|\operatorname{Card}\{j\in[N]:-\eta\leq\lambda_{j}(z)\leq\eta\}|\leq CN\eta\operatorname{Im}m(i\eta,z),

choosing
\eta=W^{-2}N^{\gamma_{0}}
and using
\operatorname{Im}m(i\eta,z)\sim\eta^{-1/2}
by Theorem
3.4
, we get that

|\operatorname{Card}\{j\in[N]:|\lambda_{j}(z)|\leq W^{-2}\}\prec N^{1+\gamma_{0}/2}W^{-1}.

Since we can choose any
\gamma_{0}>0
in the proof of Theorem
3.4
, this concludes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
∎

The rest of the section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.4
.

(3.22)

\mathcal{G}_{ij}^{(ij,\emptyset)}-w\mathcal{G}_{ii}^{(ij,\emptyset)}\mathcal{G}_{jj}^{(ij,i)}(y_{i}^{(ij)}G^{ij,ij}y_{j}^{(ij)*}),\quad i\neq j.

Then we apply large deviation inequality (
4.18
), using also
\operatorname{Im}G_{ii}^{(ij,ij)}=\operatorname{Im}G_{jj}^{ij,ij}=0
,

|\phi(y_{i}^{(ij)}G^{(ij,ij)},y_{j}^{(ij)*})|\prec\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Im}m_{c}+\Lambda}{W\eta}},

where we have used (
3.17
) to estimate the
G_{kk}^{(ij,ij)}
terms.
Then we conclude that

|\phi G_{ij}|\prec\Psi,\quad\forall i\neq j.

The same estimate applies to
|\phi\mathcal{G}_{ij}|
entries.
∎

3.4.
Self-consistency equation and Green function on-diagonal estimate

The main result of this section is the following estimate on diagonal Green function entries:

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
X
satisfy the assumptions in Theorem
2.1
, and
\phi
be an indicator function such that
\phi\Lambda\leq N^{-c^{\prime}}|m_{c}|
. Recall that we assume
\tau^{\prime}\leq|z|\leq 1-\tau^{\prime}
. Then for any
\eta\in\mathbf{S}
and
\eta\leq c_{\eqref{ondiagonalbounds}}

, where
c_{\eqref{ondiagonalbounds}}>0
is a fixed constant that depends only on
|z|\in(0,1)
and on the constant
c_{S,z}
in item (3) of Definition

, we have

(3.23)

\phi\Lambda\prec\Psi.

This result is established for all
\eta\in\mathbf{S}
smaller than some a-priori fixed constant
c_{\eqref{ondiagonalbounds}}>0
. The case of large
\eta
will be dealt with via a separate argument in Section

.

Proof.

We begin with rewriting (
3.19
):

(3.24)

[\mathcal{G}_{ii}^{(i,\emptyset)}]^{-1}=-w[1+\sum_{k}S_{ik}G_{kk}^{(i,i)}+Z_{i}^{(i)}],

and that by Fact
3.10
, we have

(3.25)

|\phi\sum_{k}(S_{ik}G_{kk}-S_{ik}G_{kk}^{(i,i)})|=O_{\prec}(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1}+\Psi).

We apply the large deviation inequality (
4.16
), combined with the high-probability entry-wise bound on
G^{(ii)}
from Fact
3.10
, to deduce that

(3.26)

|\phi Z_{i}^{i}|=O_{\prec}(\Psi).

Now we estimate
[\mathcal{G}_{ii}^{i,\emptyset}]^{-1}
via taking
1+\sum_{k}S_{ik}G_{kk}
as the main term. Then

(3.27)

\mathcal{G}_{ii}^{(i,\emptyset)}=-\frac{1}{w(1+\sum_{k}S_{ik}G_{kk})}+\mathcal{E}_{1,i},

with the error
\mathcal{E}_{1,i}
satisfying

(3.28)

\displaystyle\phi\mathcal{E}_{1,i}:=w^{-1}\frac{\phi}{(1+\sum_{k}S_{ik}G_{kk})^{2}}[\sum_{k}S_{ik}G_{kk}^{(i,i)}-\sum_{k}S_{ik}G_{kk}+Z_{i}^{(i)}]

\displaystyle+O_{\prec}(\frac{(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1}+\Psi)^{2}}{|w||m_{c}|^{3}})=O_{\prec}(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1}+\Psi),

where we use
|m_{c}|\sim|m_{c}+1|\sim|\eta|^{-1/2}
and insert in (
3.25
).

Similarly to the above, we apply Fact
3.10
to get

(3.29)

\phi\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{G}_{ii}^{(i,\emptyset)}=\phi\operatorname{Im}m_{c}+O_{\prec}(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1}+\Psi).

We also further estimate
\mathcal{Z}_{i}:=\mathcal{Z}_{i}^{\emptyset}
via Lemma
4.3
and get

(3.30)

|\phi\mathcal{Z}_{i}|=O_{\prec}(\Psi).

Then we resume to the bound on
G_{ii}
and apply (
3.18
) to get:

(3.31)

G_{ii}=-w^{-1}[1+\sum_{k}S_{ik}^{T}\mathcal{G}_{kk}-\frac{|z|^{2}}{w(1+\sum_{k}S_{ik}G_{kk})}+\mathcal{O}_{i}+|z|^{2}\mathcal{E}_{1i}+\mathcal{Z}_{i}]^{-1},

where we define
\mathcal{O}_{i}
as follows, with the inequality derived as in (
3.25
):

\mathcal{O}_{i}:=\sum_{k}S_{ik}^{T}\mathcal{G}_{kk}^{(i,\emptyset)}-\sum_{k}S_{ik}^{T}\mathcal{G}_{kk},\quad|\phi\mathcal{O}_{i}|=O_{\prec}(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1}+\Psi).

Now we define
v_{i}=G_{ii}-m_{c}
for all
i\in[1,N]
, and define
\hat{v}_{i}=\mathcal{G}_{ii}-m_{c}
for
i\in[N]
. Then we rewrite (

(v_{i}+m_{c})^{-1}=-w[1+\sum_{k}S_{ik}^{T}\hat{v}_{k}+m_{c}-\frac{|z|^{2}}{w(1+\sum_{k}S_{ik}v_{k}+m_{c})}+\mathcal{O}_{i}+|z|^{2}\mathcal{E}_{1i}+\mathcal{Z}_{i}].

We will compare this to the equation (
3.4
) solved by
m_{c}
:

(3.33)

m_{c}^{-1}=-w[1+m_{c}-\frac{|z|^{2}}{w(1+m_{c})}].

We subtract (
3.32
) from (
3.33
), do the expansion of
(v_{i}+m_{c})^{-1}
at
m_{c}^{-1}
and get, for a new error term

v_{i}\cdot m_{c}^{-2}=w\sum_{k}S_{ik}^{T}\hat{v}_{k}-\frac{|z|^{2}}{(1+m_{c})^{2}}\sum_{k}S_{ik}v_{k}+O(\mathcal{P}_{i}+\mathcal{O}_{i}+\mathcal{Z}_{i}+\mathcal{E}_{1i}),

where
\mathcal{P}_{i}
satisfies
|\phi\mathcal{P}_{i}|=O(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1})
.

Now we take
\eta\to 0
and compute that the following defined quantities

satisfy

y_{2}=-m_{c}^{2}\frac{|z|^{2}}{(1+m_{c})^{2}}=-|z|^{2}+o(1),\quad\text{as }\eta\to 0,

using the asymptotics in Lemma
3.3
. We choose

such that when

|1-|z|^{2}+y_{1}|\leq c_{S,z}
and
||z|^{2}+y_{2}|\leq c_{S,z}
for the constant

in Definition
1.1
.
Then we rearrange the above equation and get that

v_{i}=y_{1}\sum_{k}S_{ik}^{T}\hat{v}_{k}+y_{2}\sum_{k}S_{ik}v_{k}+O_{\prec}(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1}+\Psi).

We can derive a similar equation for the
\mathcal{G}_{ii}
entries and deduce

\hat{v}_{i}=y_{1}\sum_{k}S_{ik}v_{k}+y_{2}\sum_{k}S_{ik}^{T}\hat{v}_{k}+O_{\prec}(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1}+\Psi),

where the implied constants in the symbol

.

Let
\mathbf{v}=(v_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
and
\mathbf{\hat{v}}=(\hat{v}_{i})_{i=1}^{N}

v_{i}-y_{1}\sum_{k}S_{ik}^{T}\hat{v}_{k}-y_{2}\sum_{k}S_{ik}v_{k}=\left[\left(I_{2N}-\begin{bmatrix}y_{2}S&y_{1}S^{T}\\
y_{1}S&y_{2}S^{T}\end{bmatrix}\right)\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}\\
\mathbf{\hat{v}}\end{bmatrix}\right]_{i},

and we write a similar equation for

|v_{i}|=\left\|\left(I_{2N}-\begin{bmatrix}y_{2}S&y_{1}S^{T}\\

with the coefficient in front of the symbol

uniform in

. The same estimate holds for

. Now we take the supremum over

\phi\sup_{1\leq i\leq n}|G_{ii}-m_{c}|+\phi\sup_{1\leq i\leq n}|\mathcal{G}_{ii}-m_{c}|=O_{\prec}(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1}+\Psi).

That is,

\phi\Lambda_{d}=O_{\prec}(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1}+\Psi).

We have proven in (

, so by definition of

\phi\Lambda\prec\phi(\Lambda^{2}|m_{c}|^{-1}+\Psi).

Multiplying by

\phi\Lambda\prec\Psi.

∎

3.6.
Weak Green function estimates

In this section we complete the proof of Theorem
3.4
.
We first verify the following crude estimate, saying that there is a large gap for the possible value that
\Lambda
can take: for a fixed
\gamma>0
, either
\Lambda\geq W^{-\gamma/4}\eta^{-1/2}
, or with very high probability
\Lambda\leq W^{-\gamma/2}\eta^{-1/2}
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
\gamma=0.01\gamma_{0}
where
\gamma_{0}
was defined in (
3.3
). Then for any
\eta\in\mathbf{S},\eta\leq c_{\eqref{ondiagonalbounds}}
, we have

\mathbf{1}(\Lambda\leq W^{-\gamma/4}\eta^{-1/2})\Lambda\prec W^{-\gamma/2}\eta^{-1/2}.

Proof.

On this event
\Lambda\leq W^{-\gamma/4}\eta^{-1/2}
, the conditions of Proposition
3.13
are immediately satisfied since
|m_{c}|\sim|\eta|^{-1/2}
. Then we apply Proposition
3.13
to deduce that on this event we have

\Lambda\prec\sqrt{\frac{|\eta|^{-1/2}}{W\eta}}+\frac{1}{W\eta}\ll W^{-\gamma/2}\eta^{-1/2}
by our definition that
\eta\in\mathbf{S}
.
∎

Since we are in the situation
\Lambda\leq W^{-\gamma/4}\eta^{-1/2}
by Lemma
3.14
for any small but fixed
\eta>0
, we can apply a continuity argument to show that, with very high probability we must be in the latter situation
\Lambda\leq W^{-\gamma/3}\eta^{-1/2}
for all
\eta\in\mathbf{S}
. This is the following proposition:

Proposition 3.16
.

We have
\Lambda(i\eta)\prec W^{-\gamma/3}\eta^{-1/2}
uniformly for all
\eta\in\mathbf{S}
.

Proof.

For any
D>0
, by Lemma
3.15
we have for each
w=i\eta,\eta\in\mathbf{S},\eta\leq c_{\eqref{ondiagonalbounds}}
,

(3.34)

\mathbb{P}(W^{-\gamma/3}\eta^{-1/2}\leq\Lambda(i\eta)\leq W^{-\gamma/4}\eta^{-1/2})\leq N^{-D-10}.

Then we take a lattice
\Delta\subset i\mathbf{S}
,
|\Delta|\leq N^{10}
so that for each
w\in i\mathbf{S}
we can find a
z\in\Delta
with
|w-z|\leq N^{-4}
. Then by taking a union bound to the last expression, we get

(3.35)

\mathbb{P}(\exists w\in\Delta:W^{-\gamma/3}\eta^{-1/2}\leq\Lambda(w)\leq W^{-\gamma/4}\eta^{-1/2})\leq N^{-D}.

Since
\Lambda
is Lipschitz continuous in
\mathbf{S}
with Lipschitz constant at most
N^{2}
, we have

\mathbb{P}(\exists\eta\in\mathbf{S},\eta\leq c_{\eqref{ondiagonalbounds}}:2W^{-\gamma/3}\eta^{-1/2}\leq\Lambda(i\eta)\leq 2^{-1}W^{-\gamma/4}\eta^{-1/2})\leq N^{-D}.

Then, on an event
\Xi
with
\mathbb{P}(\Xi)\geq 1-N^{-D}
, the following is true: for any
\eta\in\mathbf{S},\eta\leq c_{\eqref{ondiagonalbounds}}
, either
1(\Xi)\Lambda(i\eta)\leq 2W^{-\gamma/3}\eta^{-1/2}
or
1(\Xi)\Lambda(i\eta)\geq 2^{-1}W^{-\gamma/4}\eta^{-1/2}
. Since
\Lambda
is continuous in
\eta
, we conclude that

(1)

Either for all
\eta\in\mathbf{S}
,
\eta\leq c_{\eqref{ondiagonalbounds}}
,
\mathbf{1}(\Xi)\Lambda(i\eta)\leq 2W^{-\gamma/3}\eta^{-1/2}
;

(2)

Or for all
\eta\in\mathbf{S}
,
\eta\leq c_{\eqref{ondiagonalbounds}}
,
\mathbf{1}(\Xi)\Lambda(i\eta)\geq 2^{-1}W^{-\gamma/4}\eta^{-1/2}
.

However, alternative (2) cannot hold true since by Lemma
3.14
, with any choice
\eta=\Theta(1)
we have
\Lambda(i\eta)=O_{\eta}(W^{-\gamma})
with high probability. This implies that alternative (1) should be true. Since the case
\eta\geq c_{\eqref{ondiagonalbounds}}
is covered by Lemma
3.14
, this completes the proof.∎

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

The theorem follows immediately from plugging in the conclusion of Proposition
3.16
into Proposition
3.13
, and using the fact that
\Psi\sim W^{-1/2}\eta^{-3/4}
as
|m_{c}|\sim\eta^{-1/2}
when
\eta
is sufficiently small. To show that the estimate can be made uniform in
i\mathbf{S}
, it suffices to apply a standard net argument and use the Lipschitz continuity of
G_{ij}
.
∎

4.1.
Computation of deterministic quantities

In this part we complete the proof of Lemma
1.4
and
3.3
: they are purely based on deterministic computations.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

We take
w=i\eta
for
\eta>0
.
Recall that
m_{c}
is the unique solution, with positive imaginary part, to the cubic equation
1+m_{c}=-i\eta m_{c}(1+m_{c})^{2}+|z|^{2}m_{c}
. Let
\hat{m}_{c}=\sqrt{\eta}m_{c}
, then
\hat{m}_{c}
solves

(4.1)

\sqrt{\eta}+\hat{m}_{c}=-i\hat{m}_{c}(\sqrt{\eta}+\hat{m}_{c})^{2}+|z|^{2}\hat{m}_{c}.

We substitute
y=\hat{m}_{c}+\sqrt{\eta}
, then
y
solves

iy=(y-\sqrt{\eta})y^{2}+i|z|^{2}(y-\sqrt{\eta}).

Since
y
solves a cubic equation, its solution can be uniquely computed by the Cardano method and the solution is an analytic function of the constant
\sqrt{\eta}
. Taking
\eta\to 0
, one can verify via explicit calculation that when
|z|<1
, the solution to
y
with a positive imaginary part converges to
\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(1+i)\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}
. Then we have

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\sqrt{i\eta}m_{c}=i\sqrt{1-|z|^{2}}.

For general
w=i\eta
in a compact set of
\mathbb{C}
, via the explicit formulas of solutions we verify directly that

|m_{c}(w,z)|=O(|w|^{-1/2}).

∎

The proof of Lemma
1.4
is based on explicit calculations of the inverse of circulant matrices. We begin with the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
S
be the variance profile defined in Example
1.3
. Then for any
t\in(0,1)
we can find constants
c_{S},C_{S}>0
depending only on
f(x)
and

such that for any
y\in\mathbb{C}:|y+1|\leq c_{S}
or any
y\in\mathbb{C}:|y|\leq t
, we have

(4.2)

\|(I-yS)^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}}\leq C_{S}(\log N)^{2}.

Proof of Lemma
1.4
for periodic band and circular matrices, assuming Lemma
4.1
.

By linear algebra, we have that for any symmetric matrix
S
,

(4.3)

\left(I_{2N}-\begin{bmatrix}y_{2}S&y_{1}S^{T}\\
y_{1}S&y_{2}S^{T}\end{bmatrix}\right)^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}P+Q&P-Q\\
P-Q&P+Q\end{bmatrix}\,

where
P:=(I-aS)^{-1}
and
Q:=(I-bS)^{-1}
, with
a=y_{1}+y_{2},b=y_{2}-y_{1}
. By assumption on
y_{1},y_{2}
we can assume
|a+1|\leq c_{S}
and
|b|\leq t<1
for some
t\in(0,1)
depending only on
|z|
. Then the
L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}
norm in question is upper bounded by the
L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}
norm of
P
and

.

∎

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

The proof is very similar to, and partially adapted from
[
15
]
, Section 16.
We use the standard notion
a\lesssim b
to mean
a\leq Cb
for some constant
C
independent of
N
and
W
, and we say
a\sim b
if
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
.

First, by the assumption on
f
and applying Lemma A.1 of
[
16
]
, we get the existence of some
\delta_{*}>0
such that
\operatorname{Spec}(S)\subset[-1+\delta_{*},1]
.
Let
\mathbb{T}_{N}:=\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
be the discrete torus with period
N
and denote by
Q=(2\pi W/N)\mathbb{T}_{N}
. Take
L:=N/W
and let
\hat{S}^{D}
be the discrete Fourier transform of
S
, i.e.,

(4.4)

\hat{S}^{D}(p):=\sum_{x=1}^{N}S_{0x}e^{-ipx},\quad p\in P:=\frac{2\pi}{N}\mathbb{T}_{N}.

Then we write, for any
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
,

(\frac{1}{\zeta-S})_{0x}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p\in P}\frac{1}{\zeta-\hat{S}^{D}(p)}e^{ipx},\quad x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}.

We can naturally extend
\hat{S}^{D}(p)
to be a periodic function on
p\in[0,2\pi]
. Then consider the following
2\pi W
-periodic function

(4.5)

F(q):=\hat{S}^{D}(\frac{q}{W})=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{x=1}^{N}f(\frac{x}{W})e^{-ix(q/W)}.

By properties of circulant matrices,
\operatorname{Spec}(S)=\{\hat{S}^{D}(p):p\in P\}.
Then we verify that

(4.6)

\{F(q):q\in[0,2\pi W]\}=\{\hat{S}^{D}(p):p\in[0,2\pi]\}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus B(-1,\delta_{*}/2),

where
B(-1,\delta_{*}/2)
is the ball in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
centered at
-1
with radius
\delta_{*}/2
.

Then we set, for all
p\in[0,2\pi]
,

R(p)=\frac{1}{\zeta-F(pW)},

and let
\hat{R}
be its Fourier transform, i.e.

(4.7)

\hat{R}(n):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}R(p)e^{-inp}dp,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then by Poisson summation formula we get

(\frac{1}{\zeta-S})_{0x}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p\in P}R(p)e^{ipx}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{R}(Nk+x),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}_{N}.

Next we verify various decay properties of
F
: when
f
satisfies (
1.4
) we can check that

F(q)\in\mathbb{R},\quad F(q)=F(-q),\quad|\partial^{k}F(q)|\lesssim\langle q\rangle^{-2}\forall 0\leq k\leq 4,q\in[0,2\pi W],

F(0)=0,\quad F^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad\partial^{2}F(0)\sim 1.

These estimates are elementary and use discrete integration by parts and the decay of
f
(
1.4
). For example, we verify that
|F(q)|\lesssim\langle|q|\rangle^{-2}
: this follows from, when
|q|
is large,

\displaystyle|F(q)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{W}\frac{1}{|1-e^{-iq/W}|}\sum_{x=1}^{N}\left|[f(\frac{x}{W})-f(\frac{x+1}{W})]e^{-ixq/W}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{q}\frac{1}{W}\sum_{x=1}^{N}|f^{\prime}(\frac{x}{W})|\lesssim\frac{1}{q},

when
|q|
is small we may simply use
|F(q)|\lesssim 1
and write
|F(q)|\lesssim\langle|q|\rangle^{-2}
. When
f
satisfies the regularity conditions in (
1.4
), one can integrate by parts the second time and show that
|F(q)|\lesssim\frac{1}{q^{2}}
.
When
f
is compactly supported and has bounded total variation (but not necessarily

or even continuous), we can also verify from the above estimate that
|F(q)|\lesssim\frac{1}{q}
by hand, via bounding
\sum_{x=1}^{N}|f(\frac{x}{W})-f(\frac{x+1}{W})|
by the total variation of
f
.

For the derivative
F^{\prime}(q)
, we have a similar estimate: denote by
g(x)=xf(x)
, then

|F^{\prime}(q)|=\frac{1}{W}\frac{1}{|1-e^{-iq/W}|}\sum_{x=1}^{N}\left|[g(\frac{x}{W})-g(\frac{x+1}{W})]e^{-ixq/W}\right|.

Then when
f
satisfies (
1.4
) we use the derivative bounds to get similar estimate (and integrate by parts the second time to show
|F^{\prime}(q)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle|q|\rangle^{2}}
).
Or, when
f
is compactly supported with finite total variation, then
g
has the same property, and we verify by hand that
|F^{\prime}(q)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle|q|\rangle}
.

Finally, when
f
is compactly supported, then we can extend
F(q)
to be a
2\pi W
-periodic function on
\mathbb{R}
, which further extends to a holomorphic function on a strip

\Omega=\{q\in\mathbb{C}:|\operatorname{Im}q|\leq c_{4}\}
for some
c_{4}>0
independent of
W
and
L
.

For the case
y
is very close to
-1
, then we assume
\zeta
is very close to
-1
so that
\zeta
is distant from
\operatorname{Spec}(S)
, and thus
|\zeta-F(q)|\geq c
for some

, uniformly for

. For the case
|y|<t<1
we assume
\zeta
is close to

, which is again distant from
\operatorname{Spec}(S)
. We write

(4.8)

\hat{R}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi W}\int_{0}^{2\pi W}\frac{1}{\zeta-F(q)}e^{-iqx/W}dq,\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}.

We then perform an integration by parts to show
\hat{R}(x)
has fast decay: indeed,

(4.9)

\hat{R}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi W}\int_{0}^{2\pi W}(\frac{W}{ix})\frac{F^{\prime}(q)}{(\zeta-F(q))^{2}}e^{-iqx/W}dq,\quad x\in\mathbb{Z}.

Using the previously derived decay
|F^{\prime}(q)|\lesssim\langle|q|\rangle^{-2}
, we get an improved bound

(4.10)

|\hat{R}(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{W}\langle\frac{x}{W}\rangle^{-1}.

Taking integration by parts two and three times, we get a decay

(4.11)

|\hat{R}(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{W}\langle\frac{x}{W}\rangle^{-D}.

for
D=2,3
. Then it is standard to check that

where we first use (

, and then use (

to sum up those

. This proves an upper bound for

. Via a simple rescaling this yields an upper bound for
\|(1-yS)^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}}
when

is not near

is very small we can alternatively replace all the matrix
S
in the previous computations by
yS
, and do the same proof for
(1-yS)^{-1}
as all the decay properties on

still hold.

In the case where

is compactly supported, then

is analytic in
\Omega
. We first use the estimate
|F^{\prime}(q)|\leq\langle|q|\rangle^{-1}
in the integral (

) to get that

, since

and we integrate

on
[0,2\pi W]
. Then for large

is analytic, we shift the integration contour of (

for

. Then we have

Combining the two above estimates, we deduce that

∎

Note: a sufficient condition on

and

all have finite total variation as functions on

.

Proof of Lemma

We shall use the following large deviation inequality, whose proof can be found in Appendix B of

.

By row-column symmetry we only prove the assertions concerning the Green function

\displaystyle(1-\mathbb{E}y_{i})(y_{i}^{(\mathbb{T})}G^{(\mathbb{T},i)}y_{i}^{(\mathbb{T}*)})

\displaystyle=(1-\mathbb{E}_{y_{i}})|z|^{2}G_{ii}^{(\mathbb{T},i)}-(1-\mathbb{E}_{y_{i}})\sum_{k}[zG_{ik}^{(\mathbb{T},i)}X_{ki}^{*}+z^{*}X_{ik}G_{ki}^{(\mathbb{T},i)}]+(1-\mathbb{E}_{y_{i}})\sum_{jk}X_{ij}G_{jk}^{(\mathbb{T},i)}X^{*}_{ki}.

\displaystyle\prec\sqrt{\sum_{j,k}|G_{jk}^{(\mathbb{T},i)}|^{2}S_{ij}S_{ik}+\sum_{j}S_{ij}^{2}|G_{jj}^{(\mathbb{T},i)}|^{2}}

\mu_{\widetilde{G}_{z}}
are the ESD of eigenvalues of the Hermitian matrices
\mathcal{Y}_{z},\widetilde{G}_{z}
.

To prove (

), we show that with high probability, the Stieltjes transform of
\mathcal{Y}_{z}
is close to the Stieltjes transform of
\widetilde{G}_{z}
at a polynomial rate. Let

be the Stieltjes transform of
\mathcal{Y}_{z}
. We introduce
\mathcal{Y}_{z,\text{free}}
, the free probability model associated to
\mathcal{Y}_{z}
, as in
[
4
]
and let

be the Stieltjes transform of
\mathcal{Y}_{z,\text{free}}
. By a standard computation, we can verify that
m_{z,\text{free}}(w)=\underline{m}_{c}(w)
where
\underline{m}_{c}(w)
is defined in (
4.27
).

Similarly, let
\widetilde{G}_{z,\text{free}}
be the free probability model of
\widetilde{G}_{z}
, then
denoting by
g_{z,\text{free}}(w)
the Stieltjes transform of
\widetilde{G}_{z,\text{free}}
then we have
m_{z,\text{free}}(w)=g_{z,\text{free}}(w)
. As the measure is uniquely determined by the Stieltjes transform, in the following we use
\mu_{z}(\cdot)
to denote both
\mu_{\mathcal{Y}_{z},\text{free}}(\cdot)
and
\mu_{\widetilde{G}_{z},\text{free}}(\cdot)
, which is the spectral measure of the infinite dimensional free probability objects
\mathcal{Y}_{z,\text{free}},\widetilde{G}_{z,\text{free}}
. By
[
21
]
, Lemma 3.1 and Remark 3.1, we can check that the limiting measure
\mu_{z}(\cdot)
has a bounded support and bounded density: for each
x\in\mathbb{R},y\geq 0:

(4.30)

|\mu_{z}((-\infty,x+y])-\mu_{z}((-\infty,x])|\leq y.

For the Gaussian case, using Theorem 2.8 and Corollary 4.14 of
[
4
]
, for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
and any
\eta
with
\Im\eta>0
, for any
D>0
, we have for
w=E+i\eta\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

(4.31)

\mathbb{P}\left(|m_{z}(w)-m_{z,\text{free}}(w)|\leq\frac{(\log N)^{5}}{W|\eta|^{5}}\right)\geq 1-O_{D}(N^{-D}).

For the non-Gaussian case, we may first assume that
x_{ij}
are truncated with
|x_{ij}|\leq N^{0.1c}
, and return to the general case via a perturbative expansion of Green’s function as in the proof of Lemma
3.14
. Then we can apply Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.4
to deduce that

(4.32)

\mathbb{P}\left(|m_{z}(w)-m_{z,\text{free}}(w)|\leq\frac{N^{0.2c}}{W^{1/4}|\eta|^{5}}\right)\geq 1-O_{D}(N^{-D}).

Now we can conclude the proof.
Fix a large
A>0
and
denote by
\mathcal{D}_{A}
the region

\mathcal{D}_{A}:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:-A\leq\operatorname{Re}w\leq A,\quad W^{-1/400}\leq\operatorname{Im}w\leq 1\}.

Using the Lipschitz continuity of
m_{z}(w)
and
g_{z}(w)
in
w
, we can upgrade the convergence in (
4.31
) to be uniform over
w\in\mathcal{D}_{A}
, with probability
1-O_{D}(N^{-D})
for any
D>0
.

Then we take
K>0
sufficiently large such that
\mu_{\mathcal{Y}_{z}}(\mathbb{R}\setminus[-K,K])=0
with probability
1-O_{D}(N^{-D})
by Lemma
2.7
, and
\mu_{\mathcal{Y}_{z,\text{free}}}(\mathbb{R}\setminus[-K,K])=0
. We also take some
a>0
to be sufficiently large.
Now we use
[
3
]
, Corollary B.15 to derive, for some
A>0
large depending on
K
and
a
, with the integration over
\xi=\theta+i\tau
,

\displaystyle\|\mu_{\mathcal{Y}_{z}}(\cdot)-\mu_{z}(\cdot)\|_{[0,,\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\int_{-A}^{A}|m_{z}(\xi)-m_{z,\text{free}}(\xi)|d\theta+\frac{1}{\tau}\sup_{x}\int_{|y|\leq 2\tau a}|\mu_{z}((-\infty,x+y])-\mu_{z}((-\infty,x])||dy\right]

for some
C
depending only on
A
,
a
and
K
. Now we set

. To bound the first term on the second line we use (
4.31
), and to bound the second term on the second line we use (
4.30
). Doing a same computation for
\widetilde{G}_{z}
completes the proof of (
4.29

∎

5.
New least singular value estimate: proofs

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.9
. The proof has the flavor of the geometric method
[
31
]
by Rudelson and Vershynin, but we assume that the entries
x_{ij}
have a bounded density. We recall some technical lemmas:

For a complex random vector
X\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}(X,t)
its (complex) Lévy concentration function

\mathcal{L}(X,t):=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{C}^{m}}\mathbb{P}(\|X-y\|_{2}\leq t).

Lemma 5.1
.

(
[
28
]
, Theorem 1.1)
Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
be a random vector with independent coordinates, where each
x_{i}
is a real-valued random variable with density bounded by
K
. Let
E\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
d=\{1,2\}
-dimensional subspace and let
\pi_{E}
be the orthogonal projection onto
E
. Then
\pi_{E}\mathbf{x}
has a density bounded by
CK^{d}
for some universal constant
C>0
.

Fact 5.2
.

There is a universal
C>0
such that, with
B(0,R)
the ball of radius
R
at origin,

|\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap B(0,R)|\leq(2+\frac{CR}{\sqrt{n}})^{n}.

Fact 5.3
.

By the assumptions in Theorem
2.9
, we can find some
W_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
W_{0}\geq c_{W}/3
,
S_{ij}\geq c_{W}W^{-1}
whenever

, and
N-W_{0}\lfloor\frac{N}{W_{0}}\rfloor\geq\frac{1}{2}W_{0}
. Then we can enclose the matrix
X
in a block band matrix structure similarly to (
1.2
):

(5.1)

X=\begin{pmatrix}D_{1}&U_{2}&*&*&T_{L}\\
T_{1}&D_{2}&U_{3}&*&*\\
{*}&T_{2}&D_{3}&\ddots&*\\
{*}&*&\ddots&\ddots&U_{L}\\
U_{1}&*&*&T_{L-1}&D_{L}\end{pmatrix},

where
L=\lfloor\frac{N}{W_{0}}\rfloor+1
.
Here each diagonal block
D_{1},\cdots,D_{L-1}
has size
W_{0}
and the last block

has size
N-(L-1)W_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}W_{0}
. Each entry in the
D_{i},U_{i},T_{i}

have variance at least
c_{W}W^{-1}
and the other
*

are arbitrary.

From this partitioning of

for the

-th coordinate of

-th block of

).

We will only use the anti-concentration properties of random variables in the

blocks to complete the proof. The
*

Fact 5.4
.

Let
[X_{z}]^{i}
denote the

-th column of

and let

be the linear subspace of

spanned by all the columns of

except the
i
-th coordinate. Let

be a unit normal vector to

. Then we have, for each

\|X_{z}v\|_{2}\geq|v_{i}|\operatorname{dist}([X_{z}]^{i},[H_{z}]^{i})\geq|v_{i}||\langle[X_{z}]^{i},\hat{n}_{z}^{i}\rangle|,

where

\mathbb{C}^{N}
and

is the distance in
\mathbb{C}^{N}

The following structure theorem is the main component in the proof of Theorem
2.9
:

Theorem 5.5
.

Let

satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
2.9
with

(1)

For any unit vector

Proof of Theorem

.

We work on the event

Then by Theorem

∎

We then outline the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
5.5
.

In the proof we simply write

for each

Take a small parameter
\epsilon_{N}=\exp(-N^{\kappa}\frac{N}{W})

Step 1: Net construction.
We first outline a heuristic construction of the net.
For a unit vector

\hat{v}\in\epsilon_{N}N^{-1/2}(\mathbb{Z}^{N}+i\mathbb{Z}^{N})

We need to consider all the possibilities for the norm

for all

\epsilon_{N}

The above heuristic net construction is not specific enough to analyze the overlap for each component of

Real-complex correlation and approximate orthogonality.
Then for the component
v[i]

When

\operatorname{Cos}(v,w):=\frac{\langle v,w\rangle}{\|v\|_{2}\|w\|_{2}}.

To prove this claim, one only needs to verify the approximate orthogonality statement: this can be verified by randomly generating the coordinates of the approximate vectors as follows. Let
v^{R^{\prime}}[i]=v^{R}[i]+\epsilon^{R}[i]
,
\underline{v}^{\prime}[i]=\underline{v}[i]+\epsilon^{I}[i]

\epsilon^{R}[i],\epsilon^{I}[i]

\mathbb{E}\epsilon^{R}[i]=\mathbb{E}\epsilon^{I}[i]=0

), we conclude that

(5.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\exists\text{ unit vector }v\textbf{ with S.A.},\|v[k]\|_{2}\leq\epsilon_{N}\text{ such that }\|Mv\|_{2}\leq 2K\epsilon_{N})

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{M}_{2}|\sup_{\mathbf{a}}(|\mathcal{M}_{3,\mathbf{a}}|\cdot\text{RHS of}\eqref{smallballforonecase})\leq\exp(-N^{1+\kappa/4}),

where we make crucial use of the assumption
W\gg N^{1/2+c}
and we take
\kappa\in(0,c/4)
. We use
S.A.
for the abbreviation of the simplifying assumption that was proposed before.

Via a simple argument, we can verify that with probability at least
1-\exp(-N^{1+\kappa/4})
, each unit vector
v
with
\|Mv\|_{2}\leq 2K\epsilon_{N}
must have two distinct indices
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
with
\|v[i_{1}]\|_{2}\geq\sqrt{\epsilon_{N}},\quad\|v[i_{2}]\|_{2}\geq\sqrt{\epsilon_{N}}
.

Step 3: small ball probability bound, the general case.
Now we no longer assume that
v
satisfies
S.A.
, but the general case can be proven similarly as in the special case. Suppose that
\|v[k]\|_{2}\leq\epsilon_{N}
, then by the last paragraph there exists some
l\neq k,l\neq L
such that we have
\|v[l]\|_{2}\geq\sqrt{\epsilon_{N}}
. Observe that both the cardinality bound
|\mathcal{M}_{3,\mathbf{a}}|
and the small ball probability bound for
\|Mv\|_{2}\leq 2K\epsilon_{N}
involve the quantity

\zeta_{i}:=\max(x_{i},10\epsilon_{N})\cdot\max(y_{i},10\epsilon_{N})\quad\forall i\in[L].

Then we compute the quantity
\zeta_{i}
: which yields
\zeta_{k}\leq 100\epsilon_{N}^{2}
and
\zeta_{l}\geq 10\epsilon_{N}^{1.5}
. Assume without loss of generality that
k<l
, then we find the maximal monotone increasing subsequence
(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r-1})
for the value of
\zeta_{k}
with
k=k_{0}<k_{1}<\cdots<k_{r}=l
, so that
\zeta_{k_{t}}\geq\zeta_{k_{t-1}}\geq\zeta_{k_{t}-1}
for each
t=1,\cdots,r
. Then

(5.12)

\prod_{t=1}^{r}\frac{\zeta_{k_{t}-1}}{\zeta_{k_{t}}}\leq\frac{\zeta_{k}}{\zeta_{l}}\leq 10\epsilon_{N}^{1/2}.

Then we upper bound the small ball probability of
\|Mv\|_{2}
via a similar strategy: for indices
i
being the row/column of
D_{j},j\neq k_{1}-1,k_{2}-1,\cdots k_{r}-1
, we simply use the randomness in
D_{j}
. For indices
i
being the row/column of
D_{k_{t}-1}
for some
t
, we use randomness in the
U_{k_{t}}
component. Written in formulas, we have

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\|Mv\|_{2}\leq 2K\epsilon_{N})\leq\prod_{i\in[L]\setminus\{k_{t}-1,t\in[r]\}}|\frac{C}{\max(\frac{x_{i}}{\epsilon_{N}},10)\cdot\max(\frac{y_{i}}{\epsilon_{N}},10)}|^{D_{i}|}

\displaystyle\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}|\frac{C}{\max(\frac{x_{k_{i}}}{\epsilon_{N}},10)\cdot\max(\frac{y_{k_{i}}}{\epsilon_{N}},10)}|^{D_{k_{i}}|}.

Then applying (
5.12
) and comparing with (
5.5
), this leads to a similar computation as in (
5.10
) (note that
|D_{i}|=W_{0}
for all
1\leq i<L
, and we assume
k<l<L
), (
5.11
) and concludes the proof.
∎

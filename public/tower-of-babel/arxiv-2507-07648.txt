Summing Real Time Feynman Paths of Lattice Polaron with Matrix Product States
Qi Gao, Yuan Wan

Abstract
We study numerically the real time dynamics of lattice polarons by combining the Feynman path integral and the matrix product state (MPS) approach. By constructing and solving a flow equation, we show that the integrand, viewed as a multivariable function of polaron world line parameters, can be compressed as a low bond dimension MPS, thereby allowing for efficient evaluation of various dynamical observables. We establish the effectiveness of our method by benchmarking the calculated polaron spectral function in one dimension against available results. We further demonstrate its potential by presenting the polaron spectral function in two dimensions and simulating polaron diffusion in both one and two dimensions.

In this work, we tackle the polaron dynamics problem by taking advantage of both path integral and MPS approaches. Focusing on the lattice polaron, we show that its real time Feynman path integral can be efficiently evaluated by casting it in MPS form (Fig.
1
). A path integral is essentially a high dimensional sum
Q=\sum_{\{s\}}A(s_{M},\cdots s_{2},s_{1})
, where the labels
\{s\}
parametrize the world line and
A
is its quantum amplitude. By using a flow equation, we compress
A
as a low bond dimension MPS,
A(s_{M},\cdots s_{2},s_{1})\approx A_{M}(s_{M})\cdots A_{2}(s_{2})A_{1}(s_{1})
, and then sum it efficiently,
Q\approx\sum_{s_{M}}A_{M}(s_{M})\cdots\sum_{s_{2}}A_{1}(s_{2})\sum_{s_{1}}A_{1}(s_{1})
. This method shares similar sprit with the tensor train method for high dimensional integration
Oseledets and Tyrtyshnikov 2010
;
Oseledets 2011
;
Dolgov and Savostyanov 2020
;
Núñez Fernández et al. 2022
;
Núñez Fernández et al. 2025
, as well as the tensor network influence functional approach to spatially local open quantum systems
Strathearn et al. 2018
;
Jørgensen and Pollock 2019
;
Cygorek et al. 2022
and one-dimensional systems
Bañuls et al. 2009
;
Huang et al. 2014
;
Lerose et al. 2021
;
Fux et al. 2023
, but features perspective and formulation that are unique to polarons.

Our method is sign tolerant, applicable to higher spatial dimensions, free from truncation in phonon Hilbert space, and admits various generalizations. We establish its effectiveness by benchmarking the polaron spectral function, calculated through real time evolution, against available results in one dimension. We further demonstrate its potential by computing the spectral function in two dimensions and taking a first pass at simulating a nonequilibrium diffusion process.

Setup
— We use a one-dimensional polaron model to illustrate our method
Marchand and Berciu 2013
; generalizations to higher dimensions will be straightforward. The Hamiltonian is
H=K+V
.
K
describes electron hopping in a chain of
N
sites subject to periodic boundary condition:

\displaystyle K=-\sum_{i}(c^{\dagger}_{i}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i+1}+c^{\dagger}_{i+1}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i}).

(1a)

c_{i}
(
c^{\dagger}_{i}
) annihilates (creates) a spinless electron on site
i
. The hopping amplitude is set to
1
.
V
describes the phonons and their coupling to electrons:

\displaystyle V=\sum_{q}[\omega_{q}b^{\dagger}_{q}b^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{q}-\lambda f_{q}n_{q}(b_{q}+b^{\dagger}_{-q})].

(1b)

b_{q}
(
b^{\dagger}_{q}
) annihilates (creates) a phonon with wave vector
q
.
\omega_{q}
is the dispersion relation. The coupling constant
\lambda>0
. The form factor
f_{q}=f^{\ast}_{-q}
.
n_{q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k}c^{\dagger}_{k+q}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{k}
is the Fourier transform of electron density operator.
H
conserves the total number of electrons
N_{e}=\sum_{i}c^{\dagger}_{i}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i}
.

The quantity of interest is the electron propagator :

\displaystyle iG^{R}(k,t)=\theta(t)\langle 0|c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{k}e^{-iHt}c^{\dagger}_{k}|0\rangle.

(2)

|0\rangle
refers to the ground state of Eq. (
1
) in
N_{e}=0
sector.
k
is the electron wave vector. The Heaviside function
\theta(t)
enforces the causality

.

Path integral
— We express
G^{R}(k,t)
as a path integral. Trotterizing the time evolution to second order, and inserting partial resolution of identity by electron position eigenstates, we find
SM
:

\displaystyle iG^{R}(k,t)=\theta(t)\sum_{\{x\}}e^{ikx_{M}}I(\{x\})A_{0}(\{x\}).

(3a)

M
is the number of time slices.
\{x\}=\{x_{M},\cdots x_{1},x_{0}\}
are electron position at successive times. We impose the boundary condition
x_{0}=0
, whereas
x_{n\geq 1}
is unrestricted.
A_{0}
is the quantum amplitude for a free electron,
A_{0}(\{x\})=\prod^{M}_{n=1}f(x_{n}-x_{n-1})
, with
f(x_{n}-x_{n-1})=\langle x_{n}|e^{-i\epsilon K}|x_{n-1}\rangle
.
\epsilon=t/M
is the Trotter time.

I
is the phonon influence functional:

\displaystyle I(\{x\})=\langle\phi|e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(x_{M})}\cdots e^{-i\epsilon V(x_{1})}e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(x_{0})}|\phi\rangle.

(3b)

|\phi\rangle
is the phonon vacuum. Here, we have used the fact that
V
commutes with electron coordinate
x
.
V(x)
is the phonon Hamiltonian when the electron is located at
x
,
V(x)=\sum_{q}[\omega_{q}b^{\dagger}_{q}b^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{q}-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{N}}(f_{q}e^{iqx}b_{q}+h.c.)]
.

We perform a change of variable from position to displacement
s_{n}=x_{n}-x_{n-1}
:

\displaystyle iG^{R}(k,t)=\theta(t)\sum_{\{s\}}(\prod^{M}_{n=1}e^{iks_{n}})I(\{s\})A_{0}(\{s\}).

(4a)

\{s\}=\{s_{M},\cdots s_{2},s_{1}\}
.
A_{0}(\{s\})=\prod^{M}_{n=1}f(s_{n})
. Since
|f(s)|\sim O(\epsilon^{|s|})
, we restrict the summation over
s_{n}
to the range
|s_{n}|\leq 2
. The introduced error is on the same order as the Trotter error. As
\{s\}
constitute the physical legs for the MPS, this truncation reduces the computational cost by decreasing the physical dimension.

We rewrite the phonon influence functional
I
in terms of
\{s\}
. We define a translation operator
T(a)
that maps the phonon mode on site
x
to
x+a
. As a result,
V(x)=T(x)V(0)T(-x)
. Inserting it into Eq. (
3b
) yields,

\displaystyle I(\{s\})=\langle\phi|U(s_{M})\cdots U(s_{2})U(s_{1})|\phi\rangle.

(4b)

U(s)=e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}T(-s)e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}
is an
s
-dependent unitary operator. This trick is amount to a change of reference frame from the phonons to the electron, and reminiscent of the Lee-Low-Pines transformation
Lee et al. 1953
.

Eq. (
4
) is our starting point. We wish to compress the quantum amplitude
A(\{s\})\equiv I(\{s\})A_{0}(\{s\})
as an MPS,
A(\{s\})\approx A_{M}(s_{M})\cdots A_{1}(s_{1})
(Fig.
1
(b)). Then, we may evaluate the propagator as (assuming

\displaystyle iG^{R}(k,t)\approx\sum_{s_{M}}e^{iks_{M}}A_{M}(s_{M})\cdots\sum_{s_{1}}e^{iks_{1}}A_{1}(s_{1}),

(5)

with complexity
O(5M\chi^{2})
,
\chi
being the bond dimension.

Exponentiation of influence functional
— The problem reduces to compressing
A
.
I
can be viewed as a matrix product operator (MPO) that is diagonal in the
\{s\}
basis, whose virtual space is the phonon Hilbert space.
A_{0}
is an MPS with bond dimension 1. Thus,
A
can be cast as MPS, too (Fig.
1
c). However, since the bond dimension of
I
is exponentially large, direct compression is infeasible with standard MPS algorithms
Schollwöck 2011
.

In what follows, we show that
I(\{s\})
can be
exponentiated
,
I(\{s\})=\exp(\lambda^{2}S(\{s\}))
, where
S(\{s\})
is an diagonal MPO with bond dimension
O(N)
(Fig.
1
d). This fact is central to our compression scheme.

Our key observation is that, viewing
I(\{s\})
as a controlled evolution sequence for phonon quantum states, the phonons remain in the manifold of coherent states since (a) the unitary operator
U(s)
preserves coherent state structure, and (b) the initial state
|\phi\rangle
is a coherent state. We thus write the phonon state as
e^{\lambda^{2}S}|\lambda z\rangle
, where
S
is a complex number and
|\lambda z\rangle=\exp(\sum_{q}\lambda z_{q}b^{\dagger}_{q})|\phi\rangle
.
z
is an
N
dimensional column vector, whose entry
z_{q}
corresponding to the phonon mode with wave vector
q
. The scaling factors
\lambda,\lambda^{2}
are introduced for later convenience.

We derive recursion relations for coherent state parameters
S
and
z
. Let
e^{\lambda^{2}S_{n}}|\lambda z_{n}\rangle=U(s_{n})\cdots U(s_{1})|\phi\rangle
.
U(s_{n})
induces an affine transformation,
(S_{n},z_{n},1)^{t}=B(s_{n})(S_{n-1},z_{n-1},1)^{t}

SM
.
B(s_{n})
is an
(N+2)\times(N+2)
matrix. It is independent of
\lambda
owing to the rescaling of
S
and
z
. The initial condition is
z_{0}=0
and
S_{0}=0
.

From our definition,
I(\{s\})=e^{\lambda^{2}S_{M}}\langle 0|\lambda z_{M}\rangle=e^{\lambda^{2}S_{M}}
.
Using the recursion relation, we cast
S_{M}
in matrix product form:

\displaystyle I(\{s\})=e^{\lambda^{2}\psi_{L}B(s_{M})\cdots B(s_{2})B(s_{1})\psi_{R}}\equiv e^{\lambda^{2}S(\{s\})}.

(6)

The boundary vector
\psi_{R}=(0,0_{1\times N},1)^{t}
encodes the initial condition.
\psi_{L}=(1,0_{1\times N},0)
picks up the value of
S_{M}
. Crucially,
S(\{s\})
has bond dimension
N+2
. Eq. (
6
) is the key result of this work.

It is interesting to compare Eq. (
6
) with other equivalent forms. Integrating out phonons in the standard path integral approach also produces
I=\exp(\lambda^{2}S)
, where
S
is given as an algebraic function of
\{s\}
. However, the matrix product structure of
S
is hidden in this approach.

Compression through flow equation
— After exponentiation, the quantum amplitude assumes the form,
A(\{s\})=e^{\lambda^{2}S(\{s\})}A_{0}(\{s\})
. Differentiating
A
with respect to
\lambda^{2}
yields an ordinary differential equation:

\displaystyle\frac{dA(\{s\})}{d\lambda^{2}}=S(\{s\})A(\{s\}).

(7)

The initial condition is
A(\{s\})=A_{0}(\{s\})
at
\lambda^{2}=0
.

Eq. (
7
) is a flow equation
Wegner 1994
describing the renormalization of polaron dynamics with increasing electron-phonon coupling. It has the same form as the differential equations that occur in the real/imaginary time evolution of MPS (Fig.
1
e). Integrating Eq. (
7
) by time-dependent variational principle (TDVP)
Haegeman et al. 2011
;
Haegeman et al. 2016
solver with a maximal bond dimension
\chi
results in the compression of
A
.

Solving Eq. (
7
) is the most computationally expensive step in our method. It must be solved independently for each time
t
, which can be parallelized. Once
A
is compressed, it is then contracted according to Eq. (
5
). As the momentum
k
is merely a parameter in the contraction, scanning
k
over the Brillouin zone does not incur significant computational cost.

Diffusion dynamics
— So far our discussion has been dedicated to the spectral function. We now illustrate the versatility of our method by taking a first pass at another category of problems, namely the diffusion dynamics. Specifically, we plant in an empty lattice an electron at the origin, and evolve it according to the Hamiltonian Eq. (
1
),
|\psi(t)\rangle=e^{-iHt}c^{\dagger}_{0}|0\rangle
. We calculate the density
\rho(x,t)=\langle\psi(t)|c^{\dagger}_{x}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{x}|\psi(t)\rangle
by using essentially the same method as the propagator
SM
.

Fig.
4

\omega_{q}=\omega_{0}=1
,
f_{q}=1
, and
\lambda=\sqrt{2}
. It exhibits oscillatory behavior on top of a diffusion-like spreading (Fig.
4

Discussion
— To summarize, we have shown that the real time Feynman path integral for lattice polaron can be efficiently calculated by compressing the quantum amplitude
A
as an MPS. The success of this approach relies on the fact that
A
indeed admits an MPS representation with low bond dimension. We understand this fact as follows. The virtual space for the
exact
MPS representation of
A
is the full phonon Hilbert space (Eq. (
4b
)). Compressing
A
is amount to searching for a small subspace that captures the dynamics of the system. We construct this space implicitly using the TDVP solution of flow equation (Eq. (
7
)), whereas the previous variational calculations
Romero et al. 1998
;
Wellein and Fehske 1998
;
Bonča et al. 1999
constructs it explicitly.

Our method paves the way toward a systematic study of polaron dynamics. It can be adapted for Su-Schrieffer-Heeger type models
Capone et al. 1997
;
Marchand et al. 2010
and bipolarons
Sous et al. 2018
;
Zhang et al. 2023
. For the former, a different trotterization scheme is needed for the path integral; for the latter, the physical dimension of the quantum amplitude is doubled. Furthermore, our preliminary analysis has shown that the method could be extended to the case of thermal phonons
Bonča et al. 2019
;
Jansen et al. 2020
.

Our method exploits the fact that the phonons stay in the coherent state manifold in the time evolution. For Bose polarons
Grusdt et al. 2024
, it is necessary to extend the manifold to the squeezed coherent states. The influence functional acquires a from different from Eq. (
6
). Similar reasoning also applies to Fermi polarons
Massignan et al. 2014
. Extending our method to these settings is an open question at the moment. It can be empowered by the tensor cross interpolation method
Oseledets and Tyrtyshnikov 2010
;
Oseledets 2011
;
Dolgov and Savostyanov 2020
;
Núñez Fernández et al. 2022
;
Núñez Fernández et al. 2025
, thus opening up more possibilities.

Path integral representation of polaron propagator

In this section, we fill in the gaps in the derivation of the path integral representation of electron propagator. We consider a chain of
N
sites subject to periodic boundary condition. The Hamiltonian reads:

\displaystyle H=\underbrace{-\sum_{i}(c^{\dagger}_{i}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i+1}+c^{\dagger}_{i+1}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i})}_{K}+\underbrace{\sum_{q}\omega_{q}b^{\dagger}_{q}b^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{q}-\lambda f_{q}n_{q}(b_{q}+b^{\dagger}_{-q})}_{V}.

(A.8)

The first term
K
is the electron kinetic energy.
c^{\dagger}_{i}
(
c_{i}
) creates (annihilates) a spinless electron on site
i
. We set the electron hopping amplitude to 1 by rescaling the unit of energy. The second term
V
describes the phonons and their coupling to electron.
b^{\dagger}_{q}
(
b_{q}
) creates (annihilates) a phonon with wave vector
q
.
n_{q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k}c^{\dagger}_{k+q}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{k}
is the Fourier transform of electron density operator.
\omega_{q}
is the phonon dispersion relation.
f_{q}=f^{\ast}_{-q}
is a form factor.
\lambda>0
is the electron-phonon coupling constant.

We are interested in the zero temperature electron propagator in the momentum space:

\displaystyle iG^{R}(k,t)=\theta(t)\langle 0|c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{k}(t)c^{\dagger}_{k}(0)|0\rangle=\langle 0|c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{k}e^{-iHt}c^{\dagger}_{k}|0\rangle.

(A.9)

|0\rangle
refers to the ground state of Eq. (
A.8
) in the zero-electron sector. As
c^{\dagger}_{k}
creates an electron, the ensuing time evolution occurs in the one-electron sector.

To derive the path integral representation, we use the second order Suzuki-Trotter decomposition,
e^{-i\epsilon H}=e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V}e^{-i\epsilon K}e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V}+O(\epsilon^{3})
. Substituting it into the expression for
G^{R}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle iG^{R}(k,t)=\theta(t)\sum_{r}e^{ikr}\langle 0|c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{r}\underbrace{e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V}e^{-i\epsilon K}e^{-i\epsilon V}\cdots e^{-i\epsilon V}e^{-i\epsilon K}e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V}}_{\textrm{M hopping terms}}c^{\dagger}_{0}|0\rangle.

(A.10)

We insert the resolution of identity by electron position eigenstates and use the fact that
V
commutes with the electron position operator:

\displaystyle iG^{R}(k,t)=\theta(t)\sum_{\{x\}}e^{ikr}\langle x_{M}|e^{-i\epsilon K}|x_{M-1}\rangle\cdots\langle x_{1}|e^{-i\epsilon K}|x_{0}\rangle\langle\phi|e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(x_{M})}e^{-iV(x_{M-1})}\cdots e^{-i\epsilon V(x_{1})}e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(x_{0})}|\phi\rangle.

(A.11)

The summation is over all electron paths
x_{0},x_{1},\cdots x_{M}
, with the boundary condition
x_{0}=0
and
x_{M}=r
.
V(x)
refers to the phonon Hamiltonian when the electron is at position
x
:

\displaystyle V(x)=\sum_{q}(\omega_{q}b^{\dagger}_{q}b^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{q}-\frac{\lambda f_{q}e^{iqx}}{\sqrt{N}}b_{q}-\frac{\lambda f^{\ast}_{q}e^{-iqx}}{\sqrt{N}}b^{\dagger}_{q}).

(A.12)

Here, we have used
n_{q}|x\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{iqx}|x\rangle
.
|\phi\rangle
is the phonon vacuum.

In the next step, we perform a change of variable from electron position
x_{n}
to electron displacement
s_{n}=x_{n}-x_{n-1}
:

\displaystyle iG^{R}(k,t)=\theta(t)\sum_{\{s\}}(\prod^{M}_{j=1}e^{iks_{j}})A_{0}(\{s\})I(\{s\}).

(A.13)

Here,
\{s\}=\{s_{M},\cdots s_{2},s_{1}\}
.
A_{0}
is the quantum amplitude for a free particle:

\displaystyle A_{0}(\{s\})=\prod^{M}_{j=1}f(s_{j}),\quad f(s)=\langle x+s|e^{-i\epsilon K}|x\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1-\epsilon^{2}&(s=0)\\
i\epsilon&(s=\pm 1)\\
-\epsilon^{2}/2&(s=\pm 2)\end{array}\right..

Here, we have truncated the range of
s
to
|s|\leq 2
. This truncation introduces an error of order
\epsilon^{3}
, which is comparable to that of the Trotter error. The phonon influence functional
I
is given by:

\displaystyle I(\{s\})=\langle\phi|e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(x_{M})}e^{-i\epsilon V(x_{M-1})}\cdots e^{-i\epsilon V(x_{1})}e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(x_{0})}|\phi\rangle.

(A.17)

The right hand side depends on
\{s\}
implicitly through the relation
x_{j}=x_{j-1}+s_{j}
, with the initial condition
x_{0}=0
.

We wish to make the dependence of
I
on
\{s\}
explicit. To this end, we use the translation operator
T(x)
on phonon states
T(-x)b_{q}T(x)=e^{-iqx}b_{q}
. It follows that
T(x)V(0)T(-x)=V(x)
.

I
now depends on
\{s\}
explicitly:

\displaystyle I(\{s\})

\displaystyle=\langle\phi|e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}T(-s_{M})e^{-i\epsilon V(0)}\cdots T(-s_{2})e^{-i\epsilon V(0)}T(-s_{1})e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\phi|U(s_{M})\cdots U(s_{2})U(s_{1})|\phi\rangle.

(A.18)

Here, we have defined the unitary operator:

\displaystyle U(s)=e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}T(-s)e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}.

(A.19)

Appendix B
Exponentiation of influence functional

I(\{s\})
already has a matrix product structure; it can be viewed as a diagonal matrix product operator whose virtual space is the phonon Hilbert space. In this section, we show that
I(\{s\})
can be exponentiated as:

\displaystyle I(\{s\})=e^{\lambda^{2}S(\{s\})},

(B.20)

where
S(\{s\})
has a matrix product form whose bond dimension is
N+2
.

We view
I(\{s\})
as a sequence of controlled unitary evolution of the phonon states, starting from the phonon vacuum
|\phi\rangle
. We note that
U(s)
maps phonon coherent state to coherent state, and, furthermore, that the initial state is a coherent state. Therefore, the phonons stay in the coherent state manifold. We may parametrize the phonon state as:

\displaystyle e^{\lambda^{2}S_{j}}|\lambda z_{j}\rangle=U(s_{j})\cdots U(s_{2})U(s_{1})|\phi\rangle.

(B.21)

Here,
S_{j}
is a complex number;
z_{j}
is a
N
-dimensional complex vector that parametrize the coherent state, whose entry runs over all phonon wave vectors. In particular,
z_{q}
corresponds to the phonon mode with wave vector
q
. The scaling factor
\lambda^{2}
(
\lambda
) in front of
S_{j}
(
z_{j}
) is for later convenience.

We now derive a recursion relation for the parameters
S_{j}
and
z_{j}
. We first consider the action of
\exp(-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0))
on phonon coherent state:

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}|\lambda z\rangle

\displaystyle=\prod^{\otimes}_{q}\exp(-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}b^{\dagger}_{q}b^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{q}+i\frac{\lambda f_{q}\epsilon}{2\sqrt{N}}b_{q}+i\frac{\lambda f^{\ast}_{q}\epsilon}{2\sqrt{N}}b^{\dagger}_{q})|\lambda z_{q}\rangle.

(B.22)

We untangle the operator exponential by the following identity:

\displaystyle e^{-Ab^{\dagger}b+Bb+Cb^{\dagger}}=e^{\frac{BC}{A}-\frac{BC}{A^{2}}(1-e^{-A})}e^{-Ab^{\dagger}b}e^{C\frac{e^{A}-1}{A}b^{\dagger}}e^{B\frac{1-e^{-A}}{A}b}.

(B.23)

Setting

\displaystyle A=\frac{i\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2};\quad B=i\frac{\lambda f_{q}\epsilon}{2\sqrt{N}};\quad C=i\frac{\lambda f^{\ast}_{q}\epsilon}{2\sqrt{N}},

(B.24)

we obtain:

\displaystyle\exp(-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}b^{\dagger}_{q}b^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{q}+i\frac{\lambda f_{q}\epsilon}{2\sqrt{N}}b_{q}+i\frac{\lambda f^{\ast}_{q}\epsilon}{2\sqrt{N}}b^{\dagger}_{q})=\exp[i\frac{\lambda^{2}|f_{q}|^{2}}{2N\omega_{q}}\epsilon-\frac{\lambda^{2}|f_{q}|^{2}}{N\omega^{2}_{q}}(1-e^{-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}})]

\displaystyle\exp(-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}b^{\dagger}_{q}b^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{q})\exp[\frac{\lambda f^{\ast}_{q}}{\sqrt{N}\omega_{q}}(e^{i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}}-1)b^{\dagger}_{q}]\exp[\frac{\lambda f_{q}}{\sqrt{N}\omega_{q}}(1-e^{-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}})b_{q}].

(B.25)

Computing its action on coherent state is now straightforward:

\displaystyle\exp(-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}b^{\dagger}_{q}b^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{q}+i\frac{\lambda f_{q}\epsilon}{2\sqrt{N}}b_{q}+i\frac{\lambda f^{\ast}_{q}\epsilon}{2\sqrt{N}}b^{\dagger}_{q})|\lambda z_{q}\rangle=\exp[i\frac{\lambda^{2}|f_{q}|^{2}}{2N\omega_{q}}\epsilon-\frac{\lambda^{2}|f_{q}|^{2}}{N\omega^{2}_{q}}(1-e^{-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}})+\frac{\lambda^{2}f_{q}}{\sqrt{N}\omega_{q}}(1-e^{-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}})z_{q}]

\displaystyle|\lambda e^{-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}}z_{q}+\frac{\lambda f^{\ast}_{q}}{\sqrt{N}\omega_{q}}(1-e^{-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}})\rangle.

(B.26)

Collecting the results for different
q
, we obtain:

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}e^{\lambda^{2}S}|\lambda z\rangle=e^{\lambda^{2}(S+c+\beta^{\dagger}z)}|\lambda(\mathcal{V}z+\alpha)\rangle,

(B.27)

Here,
\mathcal{V}
is a diagonal matrix whose diagonal entry reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{qq}=\exp(-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}).

(B.28a)

\alpha
and
\beta
are complex
N
-dimensional vectors:

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}=\frac{f^{\ast}_{q}}{\sqrt{N}\omega_{q}}(1-e^{-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}});\quad\beta_{q}=\frac{f^{\ast}_{q}}{\sqrt{N}\omega_{q}}(1-e^{i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}})=-(\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}\alpha)_{q};

(B.28b)

c
is a complex number:

\displaystyle c=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{q}\frac{|f_{q}|^{2}}{\omega_{q}}(i\frac{\epsilon}{2}-\frac{1-e^{-i\frac{\omega_{q}\epsilon}{2}}}{\omega_{q}}).

(B.28c)

We recognize that the action of
\exp(-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0))
on phonon coherent state in fact induces an affine transformation:

\displaystyle S\overset{e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}}{\longrightarrow}S+c+\beta^{\dagger}z,\quad z\overset{e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{V}z+\alpha.

(B.29)

We recast the above in matrix form:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}S\\
z\\
1\end{pmatrix}\overset{e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}}{\longrightarrow}\begin{pmatrix}1&\beta^{\dagger}&c\\
0&\mathcal{V}&\alpha\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}S\\
z\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

(B.30)

Likewise, the lattice translation operator
T(x)
maps coherent state to coherent state. To prove this, we use:

\displaystyle T(x)^{-1}b_{q}T(x)=e^{-iqx}b_{q}.

(B.31)

It follows that:

\displaystyle b_{q}(T(x)|z_{q}\rangle)=e^{-iqx}z_{q}T(x)|z_{q}\rangle,

(B.32)

which implies

\displaystyle T(x)|z_{q}\rangle=|e^{-iqx}z_{q}\rangle.

(B.33)

Collecting the results for different
q
, we find:

\displaystyle T(-s)e^{\lambda^{2}S}|\lambda z\rangle=e^{\lambda^{2}S}|\lambda\mathcal{T}(-s)z\rangle,

(B.34)

where
\mathcal{T}(x)
is a diagonal unitary matrix whose diagonal entries are given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(x)_{qq}=e^{-iqx}.

(B.35)

The induced linear transformation on
S
and
z
can be written in matrix form:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}S\\
z\\
1\end{pmatrix}\overset{T(-s)}{\longrightarrow}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\mathcal{T}(-s)&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}S\\
z\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

(B.36)

Combining the above results, we arrive at the following recursion relation for
S_{j}
and
z_{j}
:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}S_{j}\\
z_{j}\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\beta^{\dagger}&c\\
0&\mathcal{V}&\alpha\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\mathcal{T}(-s_{j})&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\beta^{\dagger}&c\\
0&\mathcal{V}&\alpha\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}S_{j-1}\\
z_{j-1}\\
1\end{pmatrix}\equiv B(s_{j})\begin{pmatrix}S_{j-1}\\
z_{j-1}\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

(B.37)

Here,
B(s_{j})
is a
N+2
dimensional square matrix. The initial condition is
z_{0}=0
and
S_{0}=0
.

We now compute
I(\{s\})
using the recursion relation. By definition:

\displaystyle I(\{s\})=e^{\lambda^{2}S_{M}}\langle 0|\lambda z_{M}\rangle=e^{\lambda^{2}S_{M}}.

(B.38)

S_{M}
can be found by using the iteration relation:

\displaystyle S_{M}=\psi_{L}B(s_{M})\cdots B(s_{2})B(s_{1})\psi_{R};\quad\psi_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\end{pmatrix};\quad\psi_{R}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

(B.39)

We see that
S_{M}
is expressed in matrix product form; the bond dimension is
N+2
. Note all the entries in
B
are independent of
\lambda
thanks to the scaling factor introduced in the definition of
z
and
S
.

Appendix E
Path integral representation of electron desnsity

We consider a nonequilibrium diffusion problem. We place an electron at the origin of an initially empty lattice,
|\psi\rangle=c^{\dagger}_{0}|0\rangle
. We monitor the ensuing evolution of the electron density:

\displaystyle\rho(x,t)=\langle 0|c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{0}e^{iHt}c^{\dagger}_{x}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{x}e^{-iHt}c^{\dagger}_{0}|0\rangle,

(E.40)

where
H
is the Hamiltonian Eq. (
A.8
). In this section, we derive a path integral representation for
\rho(x,t)
.

First, we trotterize the time evolution and insert the resolution of identity by electron position eigenstates:

\displaystyle\rho(x,t)=\sum_{\{x^{-}\}}\sum_{\{x^{+}\}}\langle x^{-}_{0}|e^{i\epsilon K}|x^{-}_{1}\rangle\langle x^{-}_{1}|e^{i\epsilon K}|x^{-}_{2}\rangle\cdots\langle x^{-}_{M-1}|e^{i\epsilon K}|x^{-}_{M}\rangle\langle x^{+}_{M}|e^{-i\epsilon K}|x^{+}_{M-1}\rangle\cdots\langle x^{+}_{2}|e^{-i\epsilon K}|x^{+}_{1}\rangle\langle x^{+}_{1}|e^{-i\epsilon K}|x^{+}_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle\times\langle\phi|e^{i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(x^{-}_{0})}e^{i\epsilon V(x^{-}_{1})}\cdots e^{i\epsilon V(x^{-}_{M-1})}e^{i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(x^{-}_{M})}e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(x^{+}_{M})}e^{-i\epsilon V(x^{+}_{M-1})}\cdots e^{-i\epsilon V(x^{+}_{1})}e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(x^{+}_{0})}|\phi\rangle.

(E.41)

Here,
\{x^{+}\}
and
\{x^{-}\}
label the electron position on the forward and backward branch of the Keldysh contour. We impose the boundary condition
x^{\pm}_{0}=0
and
x^{\pm}_{M}=x
.
V(x)
is the same as Eq. (
A.12
).

Second, we perform a change of variables from the electron position to the displacements,
s^{\pm}_{j}=x^{\pm}_{j}-x^{\pm}_{j-1}
. Using these, we find:

\displaystyle\rho(x,t)=\sum_{\{s^{-}\}}\sum_{\{s^{+}\}}\Delta_{x}(\{s^{-}\})\Delta_{x}(\{s^{+}\})A^{\ast}_{0}(\{s^{-}\})A_{0}(\{s^{+}\})I(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\}).

(E.42)

The functional
\Delta_{x}
ensures that the electron reaches
x
at time
t
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}(\{s^{\pm}\})=\delta(x-\sum^{M}_{j=1}s^{\pm}_{j}).

(E.43)

A_{0}
is the amplitude of a free particle defined in Sec.
B
. The amplitude on the forward and backward branches are complex conjugates.
I
is the phonon influence functional:

\displaystyle I(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\})=\langle\phi|U^{\dagger}(s^{-}_{1})U^{\dagger}(s^{-}_{2})\cdots U^{\dagger}(s^{-}_{M})U(s^{+}_{M})\cdots U(s^{+}_{2})U(s^{+}_{1})|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\phi|U^{-1}(s^{-}_{1})U^{-1}(s^{-}_{2})\cdots U^{-1}(s^{-}_{M})U(s^{+}_{M})\cdots U(s^{+}_{2})U(s^{+}_{1})|\phi\rangle.

(E.44)

Here, we have used the same trick as the main text, namely using the translation operator to make the dependence on
s
explicit.
U(s)=e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}T(-s)e^{-i\frac{\epsilon}{2}V(0)}
is the same as before. The second line follows from the unitarity of
U(s)
.

Third, we exponentiate the influence functional. For the time evolution on the forward branch, we use the recursion relation from Sec.
B
:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}S_{j}\\
z_{j}\\
1\end{pmatrix}=B(s^{+}_{j})\begin{pmatrix}S_{j-1}\\
z_{j-1}\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

(E.45a)

For the time evolution on the backward branch, we exploit the fact that
U^{-1}(s^{-})
is the inverse of
U(s)
:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}S_{j}\\
z_{j}\\
1\end{pmatrix}=B^{-1}(s^{-}_{j})\begin{pmatrix}S_{j-1}\\
z_{j-1}\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

(E.45b)

The exponentiated form of the influence functional thus reads:

\displaystyle I(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\})=e^{\lambda^{2}S(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\})},

(E.46)

with

\displaystyle S(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\})=\psi_{L}B^{-1}(s^{-}_{1})B^{-1}(s^{-}_{2})\cdots B^{-1}(s^{-}_{M})B(s^{+}_{M})\cdots B(s^{+}_{2})B(s^{+}_{1})\psi_{R}.

(E.47)

The boundary vectors
\psi_{L}=(1,0,0)
and
\psi_{R}=(0,0,1)^{T}
.

We therefore obtain the flow equation for the quantum amplitude
A(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\})\equiv I(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\})A_{0}(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\})
:

\displaystyle\frac{dA(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\})}{d\lambda^{2}}=S(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\})A(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\}).

(E.48)

The initial condition is:

\displaystyle A(\{s^{-}\},\{s^{+}\})=A^{\ast}_{0}(\{s^{-}\})A_{0}(\{s^{+}\}).

(E.49)

Solving the flow equation effects the compression of
A
.

In the final step, we need to contract
A
with
\Delta
. It is then necessary to recast
\Delta
in MPS form. Here, we use the translation matrix
T(x)
:

\displaystyle T(x)|y\rangle=|x+y\rangle,

(E.50)

where
|x\rangle
is the electro position eigenstate. It is then easy to see:

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}(\{s^{+}\})=\langle x|T(s^{+}_{M})\cdots T(s^{+}_{2})T(s^{+}_{1})|0\rangle.

(E.51)

The above is an MPS with bond dimension
N
. The MPS expression for
\Delta_{x}(\{s^{-}\})
can be found in the same vein.

Abstract
We describe the discovery and characterization of TOI-7149 b, a 0.705 ± \pm 0.075 M J M_{J} , 1.18 ± \pm 0.045 R J R_{J} gas giant on a ∼ 2.65 \sim 2.65 day period orbit transiting an M4V star with a mass of 0.344 ± \pm 0.030 M ⊙ M_{\odot} and an effective temperature of 3363 ± \pm 59 K. The planet was first discovered using NASA’s TESS mission, which we confirmed using a combination of ground-based photometry, radial velocities, and speckle imaging. The planet has one of the deepest transits of all known main-sequence planet hosts at ∼ \sim 12% ( R p / R ⋆ ∼ 0.33 R_{p}/R_{\star}\sim 0.33 ). Pushing the bounds of previous discoveries of G iant E xoplanets around M -dwarf S tars (GEMS), TOI-7149 is one of the lowest mass M-dwarfs to host a transiting giant planet. We compare the sample of transiting GEMS with a 200 pc Gaia colour magnitude diagram (CMD) and find that the GEMS hosts are likely to be high metallicity stars. We also analyze the sample of transiting giant planets using the non-parametric MRExo framework to compare the bulk density of warm Jupiters across stellar masses. We confirm our previous result that transiting Jupiters around early M-dwarfs have similar masses and densities to warm Jupiters around FGK stars, and extend this to mid M-dwarfs, thereby suggesting a potential commonality in their formation mechanisms.

I
Introduction

TESS’ all sky coverage includes millions of bright nearby stars
(
Ricker et al. 2014
)
, particularly M-dwarfs. These stars form the majority of the stars in the Galaxy
(
Henry et al. 2006
;
Reylé et al. 2021
)
, and have been well-studied for their abundance of terrestrial planets, both from the transit technique with Kepler/K2
(
Dressing & Charbonneau 2013
;
Hardegree-Ullman et al. 2019
;
Hsu et al. 2020
, etc.)
and radial velocities
(RV;
Bonfils et al. 2013
;
Sabotta et al. 2021
, etc.)
. However the study of giant planets around M-dwarfs has historically been facilitated by minimum mass measurements of non-transiting RV detections
(
Endl et al. 2006
;
Johnson et al. 2010
;
Bonfils et al. 2013
;
Maldonado et al. 2019
;
Sabotta et al. 2021
)
, the planetary natures of which are uncertain due to the
\sin{i}
degeneracy. This has been changing recently due to detections of giant exoplanets around M-dwarf stars (GEMS) from TESS
(
Cañas et al. 2020
;
Kanodia et al. 2023b
;
Hobson et al. 2023
;
Bernabò et al. 2024
;
Fernandes et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
that have enabled statistical analysis of the bulk properties of these planets
(
Müller & Helled 2024
;
Kanodia 2024
)
, their host star metallicity dependence
(
Gan et al. 2024
)
, their atmospheres
(
Cañas et al. 2025
)
, and their occurrence rates
(
Gan et al. 2023
;
Bryant et al. 2023
)
. However, this sample is still limited, and in particular, is biased towards early M-dwarf hosts. Thus we have started the
Searching for GEMS
survey
(
Kanodia et al. 2024a
)
to help fix this.

TIC-459323923.01 was identified as a planet candidate during development of our custom
TESS-miner python
package (Glusman et al. in prep.), which was developed to identify transiting GEMS in a volume-limited 200 pc sample of
\sim
1 million M-dwarfs observed by TESS
(
Ricker et al. 2014
)
as part of the
Searching for GEMS
survey
(
Kanodia et al. 2024a
)
. It was identified by our pipeline in May 2024, after which we began ground-based observations. TIC-459323923.01 was subsequently identified as a TESS object of interest (TOI) 7149.01 by the Faint-star Search
(
Kunimoto et al. 2022
)
using the Quick Look Pipeline (QLP) algorithm developed by
Huang et al. 2020
in October 2024. We adopt TOI-7149 as the host star name for this manuscript and discuss our observations in Section
II
, which include TESS photometry as well as ground-based photometry from Red Buttes Observatory, the 200-inch Hale Telescope at Palomar, and the Three-hundred MilliMeter Telescope and Las Campanas Remote Observatory at Las Campanas. We then describe our spectroscopic follow-up using the Habitable-zone Planet Finder (HPF). In Section
III
we discuss the estimated stellar characteristics, and the procedure followed to fit the observations and obtain planetary parameters in Section
IV
. In Section
V
, we contextualize TOI-7149 b in the landscape of other transiting GEMS, as well as discuss sample-level trends seen for transiting giant planets across the stellar mass axis, before summarizing our results and concluding in Section
VI
.

II.2
Ground-based photometric observations

II.2.1
0.6 m RBO

We observed three transits of TOI-7149 b with the 0.6 m telescope at the Red Buttes Observatory (RBO) in Wyoming, USA
(
Kasper et al. 2016
)
on 2024 August 2, 2025 February 22 and 2025 March 10. We used the Bessell I filter with no on-chip binning and exposure times of 240 s for all observations. The plate scale for RBO is 0.56
\,\mathrm{\arcsec/pixel}

Figure 7
.

II.2.2
0.3 m TMMT

We observed a transit on 2024 August 26 using the using the Three-hundred MilliMeter (
300\,\mathrm{mm}
) Telescope
(
Monson et al. 2017
, TMMT;)
at Las Campanas Observatory in Chile. We used the Bessell I filter, without any on-chip binning, and used an exposure time of 180 s. The plate scale during these observations was
0.97\,\mathrm{\arcsec/pixel}

Figure 7
.

II.2.3
0.3 m LCRO

We also used the 0.3 m Las Campanas Remote Observatory (LCRO) telescope at the Las Campanas Observatory in Chile alongside TMMT on 2024 August 26. The data was taken unbinned, with a plate scale of 0.64″ per pixel in SDSS
i’
band, and reduced with a science aperture radius of 2.9″. The lightcurve is shown in
Figure 7

Data for RBO, TMMT and LCRO were reduced with aperture photometry using the custom

Diffuser-assisted 5.1 m Palomar WIRC

We observed a transit of TOI-7149 b in J-band (

High Contrast Imaging

We observed TOI-7149 using the NN-Explore Exoplanet Stellar Speckle Imager

III.1
Spectroscopic parameters from HPF

We used the
HPF-SpecMatch
4
4

4

https://gummiks.github.io/hpfspecmatch/
package
(
Stefansson et al. 2020
)
to derive the stellar effective temperature (
T_{e}
), surface gravity (
\log g_{\star}
), metallicity ([Fe/H]), and rotational velocity (
v\sin i_{\star}
) from the observed high-resolution spectra of TOI-7149.
HPF-SpecMatch
empirically derives spectroscopic parameters
(
Yee et al. 2017
, e.g.,)
by using a weighted linear combination of the five stellar spectra from a library of well-characterized stars that best-match the target star. The HPF spectral library used in this work consisted of 100 stars with parameters covering
2700\mathrm{K}\leq T_{e}\leq 4500~\mathrm{K}
,
4.63<\log g_{\star}<5.26
, and
-0.49<\mathrm{[Fe/H]}<0.53
. We analyzed the spectral region between
8534-8645
Å because of the minimal telluric contamination. The uncertainties reported were the standard deviation of the residuals from a leave-one-out cross-validation procedure applied to the library
(
Stefansson et al. 2020
, see more details in)
. Our analysis determined
T_{e}=3363\pm 59
K,
\log g_{\star}=4.88\pm 0.04
, and
\mathrm{[Fe/H]}=-0.08\pm 0.16
for TOI-7149.
HPF-SpecMatch
could only place a upper limit of
v\sin i_{\star}<2~\mathrm{km~s^{-1}}
, which is consistent with the lack of rotation signal in TESS or HPF activity indicators, thereby suggesting an old inactive star (
>
1 Gyr).

)

V.3
Future characterization to explore the inflated nature of TOI-7149 b

The low density and inflated nature of TOI-7149 b make it a valuable target for future atmospheric characterization (TSM
\sim
132; ESM
\sim
78). Emission spectroscopy can measure the thermal emission from the planet and hence its T

{}^{4}-T_{\rm{eq}}^{4}

Conversely, an internal temperature consistent with cooling tracks would suggest the absence of additional heating, and perhaps necessitate aerosols that provide opacity at high-altitudes and hence a larger radius. The large planet-to-stellar radius ratio and cool host star result in a large eclipse depth (

(

Helled et al. 2022

;
Cañas et al. 2025
)

Kanodia 2024

Following the methodology described in
Kanodia et al. 2024a
and
Kanodia 2024
, we used the
n

MRExo

(
Kanodia et al. 2023a
)
, which utilizes a convolution of normal and beta-density functions, to model the joint density of planets
(

;
Kanodia et al. 2019
)

n

We extended the analysis to additional dimensions and probed the impact of insolation flux and stellar mass on planetary bulk densities. Given that hot Jupiters with high insolation fluxes tend to be inflated due to Ohmic dissipation
(
Guillot & Showman 2002
;
Weiss et al. 2013
;
Thorngren & Fortney 2018
)

(

;

(stellar mass, bulk density, insolation). We used

(

as 72, 41, 72 respectively.

V.5
Implications of lack of dependence of bulk density on stellar mass

We note that under the core accretion paradigm, upon initiation of the runaway processes — typically assumed to be between 20 – 100
M_{\oplus}

(
Helled 2023
)
, depending on the grain size, surface density, gas opacity, and protoplanet gas-to-metal ratio — the protoplanets are expected to exponentially accrete gas from their host disk. This process is only possible until the gas flow to the planet is quenched due to a gap opening up in the disk
(
Lin & Papaloizou 1993
;
Bryden et al. 1999
)
. Furthermore, given the effect of electron degeneracy pressure beyond Saturn mass
(
Saumon et al. 1996
,
\sim 0.3M_{J}
;)
, objects in our sample can have masses ranging from Saturn to the Hydrogen-burning mass upper limit while exhibiting a radius comparable to Jupiter. Thus, for the subsample under consideration (
<2M_{J}
), densities ranging from
\rho_{S}
–
2\rho_{J}
(0.7 – 2.6
\textrm{g~cm}^{-3}
), i.e, a factor of

are possible. Yet, we see that the densities of warm Jupiters seem to converge around 0.8

While this can be interpreted as being consistent with the hypothesis proposed in our previous work

, in which a minimum disk mass threshold dictates the final outcome of giant planet bulk densities (as well as their inverse dependent occurrence with stellar mass), we do not account for the impact of migration and evolution during and post formation. Such processes may play a significant role but investigating these effects is beyond the scope of this work.

We also note that the absence of a stellar mass dependence for the warm Jupiters with masses
<2M_{J}
could potentially indicate a different formation mechanism for planets below this threshold. This has been previously suggested by

M_{J}

(
Boss 1997

.

Overall this suggests that these GEMS (
<2

M_{J}

(

)

However, this likely takes place in disks that are very massive for the stars they orbit in order to satisfy the minimum disk mass threshold mentioned above.

VI
Summary

As part of the
Searching for GEMS
survey, we describe the discovery of TOI-7149 b, a low-density planet extending the transiting GEMS sample to lower-mass host stars with a large
\sim
12% transit depth. The host star is a fully convective M-dwarf, and one of the coldest to host a gas giant planet. With ground-based RVs from HPF and transits from Palomar, RBO, TMMT and LCRO, we obtain a precise planetary mass, radius and planetary density measurement. TOI-7149 b has an inflated radius that cannot be explained by its mass and insolation flux, and can be probed by constraining its internal temperature using emission spectroscopy. We use the statistical framework
MRExo
and extend our analysis from previous work to analyze the sample of transiting warm Jupiters and find a stellar mass dependency for their bulk densities when considering the entire sample of transiting warm Jupiters. This dependency vanishes when focussing on a subset of warm Jupiters with planetary masses
<2M_{J}
, suggesting (i) different formation pathways for Jupiters below
\sim
2
M_{J}
, and (ii) for the
<2M_{J}
, the formation mechanism seems to be agnostic to the host star mass. This independence on stellar mass could hint at a minimum threshold disk mass required for giant planet formation, which when contextualized with ALMA disk dust mass measurements in future work could explain the varying occurrence of these objects with stellar mass, as well as the above mentioned trend.

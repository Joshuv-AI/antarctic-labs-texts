Soaring hospitalization burden and a paradigm shift to non-aids-defining events among people living with HIV in Western China: a decade-long retrospective cohort study (2013–2022)
Authors: Jinyu Chen, Hang Chen, Hong Liu, Ningjun Ren, Biao Yu, Zhiwen Pi, Jingbo Zhang, Lingxi He, Huachun Zou, Xiaoxue Chen, Run Chen, Fuli Huang, Yanhua Chen, Ticheng Xiao, Ailing Li, Song Fan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesDespite the success of antiretroviral therapy (ART), people living with HIV (PLWH) face an evolving burden of hospitalizations, increasingly driven by non-AIDS-defining events (nADEs). Comprehensive longitudinal data on these trends, particularly from resource-limited settings in Western China, remain scarce. This study aimed to characterize decade-long hospitalization patterns, distinguish between ADEs and nADEs admissions, and identify key risk factors among PLWH in Luzhou, China.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted using data from Luzhou’s HIV/AIDS Comprehensive Response Information Management System (CRIMS) for 12,937 PLWH diagnosed between 2013 and 2022. The primary outcome was hospitalization, categorized as ADEs or nADEs. Annual hospitalization rates were calculated. Multivariable logistic regression identified independent risk factors for all-cause hospitalization.ResultsThe overall annual hospitalization rate among PLWH in Luzhou dramatically increased from 0.9 cases per 100 person-years in 2013 to 12.2 cases per 100 person-years in 2022. nADEs constituted the majority (81.1%) of the 5,266 total hospitalizations, with their admission rate consistently rising and reaching 10.0 per 100 person-years in 2022. Leading nADEs included chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (14.2% of nADEs) and digestive disorders (e.g., chronic gastritis, 12.9%). Pulmonary infections (40.5% of ADEs) were the most common ADE. Independent risk factors for hospitalization included age ≥ 50 years (aOR 1.926, 95% CI 1.782–2.081), non-sexual HIV transmission (aOR 1.926, 95% CI 1.285–2.888), and divorced/widowed status (aOR 1.275, 95% CI 1.122–1.450). Conversely, female sex (aOR 0.755, 95% CI 0.694–0.822), CD4 count > 200 cells/µL (aOR 0.574, 95% CI 0.523–0.630), and higher educational attainment (e.g., college vs. illiteracy, aOR 0.591, 95% CI 0.462–0.755) were protective.ConclusionHospitalization burden among PLWH in Western China has surged over the past decade, with a profound shift towards nADEs as the predominant cause. These findings highlight the urgent need for integrated HIV care models that address chronic comorbidities and target sociodemographically vulnerable subgroups to mitigate this escalating health challenge.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-25981-2.Keywords: PLWH, Hospitalization, ADEs, NADEs, Risk factors.

Introduction

The global landscape of HIV infection has been profoundly altered by the widespread implementation of combination ART [1]. This therapeutic revolution has transformed HIV from a rapidly progressive, life-threatening condition into a manageable chronic disease, enabling people living with HIV (PLWH) to achieve near-normal life expectancies [2]. Consequently, the incidence of classic AIDS-defining events (ADEs), such as opportunistic infections and certain malignancies, has dramatically declined in many settings where ART is accessible [3]. However, this success has unmasked a new wave of health challenges. As PLWH age, they face an increasing prevalence of non-AIDS-defining events (nADEs), a diverse group of comorbidities including cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, liver disease, non-AIDS-defining cancers, and neurocognitive disorders [4, 5]. These nADEs now represent major causes of morbidity and mortality, posing significant challenges to the long-term management of PLWH [6].

Monitoring hospitalization patterns among PLWH provides crucial insights into their evolving health status, the effectiveness of healthcare interventions, and the burden on healthcare systems [7]. Studies from North America and Europe have documented shifts in hospitalization causes, with nADEs increasingly outnumbering ADEs as primary reasons for admission [8]. Within China, which bears a substantial HIV epidemic with approximately 1.223 million PLWH by 2022 [9], research has also begun to explore these changing dynamics. However, much of this work has concentrated on economically developed eastern regions within China or has provided cross-sectional analyses, offering limited understanding of long-term secular trends, particularly in less-resourced areas [10, 11].

Western China, a region characterized by considerable socioeconomic disparities and often facing greater constraints in healthcare infrastructure and access compared to its eastern counterparts, presents a unique context for examining the health trajectories of PLWH [12, 13]. Luzhou city, situated in the southwestern Sichuan province and bordering other high-prevalence areas, serves as an important epidemiological microcosm reflecting the broader challenges faced in these underserved regions [14, 15]. Scarce longitudinal data on nADEs-driven hospitalization trends and risk factors among PLWH in Western China hinders tailored evidence-based strategies for this vulnerable population’s escalating healthcare needs.

This decade-long retrospective cohort study (2013–2022) analyzed hospitalization patterns among PLWH in Luzhou, Western China. Aims: (1) quantify temporal trends in hospitalization rates; (2) assess ADE/nADE proportion shifts and leading diagnoses; (3) identify demographic/clinical/social risk factors. We hypothesized rising overall hospitalizations with nADEs becoming predominant drivers in this resource-limited setting. Findings provide evidence to inform resource allocation, preventive interventions, and long-term health outcomes for PLWH in Western China and similar regions.

Methods

Study design and setting

This investigation employed a retrospective cohort study design to examine hospitalization patterns and associated risk factors among PLWH in Luzhou, China. Luzhou, a major city in the Sichuan Province of Western China, epidemiological data from the region indicates that Luzhou has the fourth highest number of PLWH in Sichuan province, with a reported infection rate of 0.313% (ranking fifth in the province), which is notably higher than the provincial average of 0.278%. All districts and counties in Luzhou have more than 500 existing cases, classifying them as Category 1 HIV epidemic areas, with a particularly marked upward trend in incidence among individuals aged ≥ 50 years [16], is characterized by a significant HIV prevalence and serves as a crucial sentinel site reflecting the epidemiological dynamics and healthcare challenges prevalent in this less economically developed region of the country [17]. The study period encompassed a decade, from January 1, 2013, to December 31, 2022, allowing for a comprehensive longitudinal analysis of hospitalization trends.

Data source and population

Data were extracted from Luzhou City’s Comprehensive HIV/AIDS Response Information Management System (CRIMS) [18]. CRIMS is a national, standardized surveillance database mandated by China CDC. It systematically collects comprehensive data on all diagnosed HIV/AIDS cases within Luzhou, including demographics, transmission routes, CD4 + T-cell counts, ART history, and hospitalization records. Its longitudinal design enabled this decade-long analysis.

Study population and participant selection

The initial cohort identified from the CRIMS database between January 2013 and December 2022 comprised 14,131 individuals diagnosed with HIV. A rigorous data matching process was implemented to ensure data accuracy and completeness across different data modules (e.g., demographic information, clinical visit records, and hospitalization records) for each individual. Unique case identification numbers, names, and dates of birth were primarily used for deterministic linkage, this approach successfully matched 5,616 hospitalized patients. In instances of missing or inconsistent identifiers, probabilistic matching algorithms incorporating additional variables like contact information and residential address were employed to establish potential matches, which successfully linked the 844 patients. The probabilistic matching was based on a weighted scoring system that compared multiple fields (including name phonetic codes, telephone number, and geographical proximity), with all potential matches undergoing manual review to confirm accuracy. This process was validated through extensive quality checks, including data consistency assessments and duplicate record removal, to ensure an accurate representation of each patient’s history.

From this initial cohort, participants were included in the final analytical sample if they met the following criteria: (1) a confirmed HIV-positive diagnosis; (2) an active follow-up status (i.e., not deceased or lost to follow-up during the study period for reasons unrelated to hospitalization being analyzed); and (3) availability of complete baseline demographic and key clinical data, including age, sex, HIV transmission route, educational level, marital status, and baseline CD4+ T-cell count. Individuals were excluded if their hospitalizations were primarily for childbirth (n = 34), occurred after the defined study end-date of December 2022 (n = 350), or if the primary admission diagnosis was missing or undocumented, precluding classification of the hospitalization event (n = 810). Following these criteria, a total of 12,937 PLWH were included in the final analysis, as detailed in the participant flow diagram (Fig. 1).

Variables and definitions

The primary outcome was any hospitalization during the study. Hospitalizations were categorized into AIDS-defining events (ADEs) or non-AIDS-defining events (nADEs). ADEs were defined per the Chinese HIV/AIDS Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines (2021) and ICD-11 [19–21], covering all specified opportunistic infections and conditions. nADEs included all other hospitalization causes. A hierarchical system, prioritizing ADEs, classified events based on the principal admission diagnosis. Since only one primary diagnosis per hospitalization was recorded, this ensured clear cause attribution. Hospitalization rates were calculated as events per 100 person-years. Person-time for each participant started from the HIV diagnosis date (or January 1, 2013, if earlier) and ended at the first hospitalization, death, loss to follow-up, or study end (December 31, 2022), whichever came first.

Baseline data collected near HIV diagnosis (or study entry for pre-diagnosed cases) were used. Sociodemographic variables included: age (< 50 vs. ≥50 years, with this cut-point selected based on its common use in HIV and aging literature to define the onset of ‘older age’ in PLWH, reflecting the increased risk of comorbidities and polypharmacy from this age onward [22]), sex (male/female), education (grouped as illiteracy, primary school, junior high school, senior high school, college+), and marital status (unmarried, married/spousal, divorced/widowed). HIV-related characteristics encompassed transmission route (heterosexual, homosexual, non-sexual) and baseline CD4 count (≤ 200 vs. >200 cells/µL, a critical threshold defined by Chinese HIV management guidelines to signify advanced immunosuppression and guide clinical decisions regarding opportunistic infection prophylaxis [23]).

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were employed to characterize the study population and summarize hospitalization patterns. Continuous variables with normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while those with skewed distribution were presented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were described using frequencies and percentages. Differences in baseline characteristics between hospitalized and non-hospitalized PLWH were assessed using Chi-square tests for categorical variables and t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous variables, as appropriate.

Temporal trends in annual hospitalization rates (overall, ADE-specific, and nADE-specific) per 100 person-years were calculated and visualized via stacked area charts. Multivariable binary logistic regression was performed to identify independent risk factors for all-cause hospitalization. Variables significant (P < 0.05) in univariate analyses or clinically important per existing literature were included in the initial multivariable model, with backward stepwise selection used to derive the final model. Adjusted odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were computed to quantify associations. The Hosmer-Lemeshow test evaluated model calibration, with non-significant P-values indicating adequate fit.

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 27.0. A two-sided P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses. Missing data for covariates were minimal (less than 5% for CD4 cell count); multiple imputation techniques (5 imputations) were utilized to handle these missing values before inclusion in the regression models to minimize potential bias and maximize statistical power. Sensitivity analyses comparing results from complete-case analysis and imputed datasets were performed to ensure robustness of the findings.

Result

Baseline characteristics of the study cohort

Following the application of inclusion and exclusion criteria (Fig. 1), a total of 12,937 PLWH were enrolled in this retrospective cohort study. The overall demographic and clinical characteristics of this cohort are detailed in Supplementary Table A. A significant majority of the participants were aged 50 years or older at baseline (66.8%), and the cohort was predominantly male (71.7%). Heterosexual contact was the primary mode of HIV transmission, accounting for 90.8% of cases. At the time of their HIV diagnosis or study entry, 18.7% of individuals presented with a CD4+ T-cell count of ≤ 200 cells/µL. In terms of educational attainment, 12.7% reported illiteracy, while a larger proportion had completed primary schooling (21.5% for primary schools, 25.9% for junior high). Regarding marital status, nearly half of the participants were married or cohabiting (49.8%), while a substantial proportion were divorced or widowed (32.1%).

During the ten-year follow-up period, 5,266 (40.7%) PLWH experienced at least one hospitalization event, while the remaining 7,671 (59.3%) did not require hospital admission. A comparative analysis of baseline characteristics between the hospitalized and non-hospitalized groups revealed statistically significant differences across all examined variables (Table 1). Specifically, individuals who were subsequently hospitalized were more likely to be older (≥ 50 years: 75.1% vs. 61.1%, P < 0.001), have a baseline CD4+ T-cell count ≤ 200 cells/µL (25.3% vs. 14.1%, P < 0.001), report lower educational levels (e.g., illiteracy: 18.2% vs. 8.9%, P < 0.001), and be divorced or widowed (36.5% vs. 29.1%, P < 0.001) compared to their non-hospitalized counterparts. Differences were also observed in sex distribution and HIV transmission routes (P = 0.014 and P < 0.001, respectively).

Overall hospitalization burden and trends (2013–2022)

The temporal evolution of hospitalization rates among PLWH in Luzhou from 2013 to 2022 is depicted in Fig. 2. A striking and progressive increase in the overall hospitalization burden was observed over the decade. The crude annual hospitalization rate escalated more than twelve-fold, rising from a mere 0.9 cases per 100 person-years in 2013 to a substantial 12.2 cases per 100 person-years by 2022.

When examining the trends by event type, both ADEs and nADEs contributed to this rising burden, though their trajectories differed. The hospitalization rate for ADEs showed a gradual increase from 0.1 cases per 100 person-years in 2015, peaking at 2.4 cases per 100 person-years in 2020, before slightly declining to 2.2 cases per 100 person-years in 2022. In contrast, the hospitalization rate attributable to nADEs demonstrated a more consistent and pronounced upward trajectory throughout the study period. Starting from a negligible rate in the initial years (e.g., effectively zero contributing to the 0.9 overall rate in 2013 where nADEs accounted for all hospitalizations shown, then 0.7 per 100 person-years for nADEs in 2016), the nADE hospitalization rate climbed steadily, reaching 10.0 cases per 100 person-years in 2022, thereby becoming the dominant driver of hospital admissions in the later years of the cohort.

Distribution and spectrum of hospitalization causes

Across the 5,266 hospitalization events recorded, a clear paradigm shift in the etiological landscape was evident. Non-AIDS-defining events (nADEs) accounted for the vast majority of admissions, comprising 81.1% (n = 4,271) of all hospitalizations, while AIDS-defining events (ADEs) constituted the remaining 18.9% (n = 995).

The principal causes for ADE-related hospitalizations are detailed in Table 2. Pulmonary infections were the most frequent ADE leading to admission, accounting for 40.5% of all ADE hospitalizations. This was followed by conditions classified under “acquired immunodeficiency syndrome” (which often encompass a constellation of severe opportunistic processes or advanced HIV disease manifestations), responsible for 32.0% of ADE admissions. Fever of unknown origin also represented a notable proportion of ADEs (5.9%).

Table 2 also presents the leading nADEs responsible for hospitalization. Diseases of the respiratory system were prominent, with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) being the single most common nADE, contributing to 14.2% of nADE admissions. Digestive system disorders were also highly prevalent, including chronic gastritis (12.9%). Abdominal pain, as a symptomatic presentation often requiring investigation, accounted for 12.9% of nADE hospitalizations. Other significant nADEs included fractures (9.5%), intervertebral disc displacement (8.9%), cerebral ischaemic stroke (8.8%). The ICD-11 codes for these representative diagnoses are provided in Supplementary Table B.

Risk factors for hospitalization

The results of the multivariable binary logistic regression analysis, identifying independent risk factors for all-cause hospitalization, are presented in Table 3. Adjustments were made for all included covariates, namely: age, sex, HIV transmission route, baseline CD4 count, educational level, and marital status. After adjusting for this full set of variables, the following factors remained significantly associated with an increased likelihood of hospitalization. Advanced age (≥ 50 years vs. <50 years) was a strong predictor, with older individuals having nearly twice the odds of being hospitalized (Adjusted Odds Ratio [aOR] = 1.926, 95% Confidence Interval [CI]: 1.782–2.081). Non-sexual routes of HIV transmission (compared to heterosexual transmission) were also associated with significantly higher odds of hospitalization (aOR = 1.926, 95% CI: 1.285–2.888). Marital status played a role, as individuals who were divorced or widowed exhibited a 1.275- fold increased odds of hospitalization compared to those who were unmarried (95% CI: 1.122–1.450).

Conversely, certain factors were found to be protective against hospitalization. Female sex was associated with a reduced likelihood of admission compared to males (aOR = 0.755, 95% CI: 0.694–0.822). A higher baseline CD4+ T-cell count (> 200 cells/µL vs. ≤200 cells/µL) was significantly protective, reducing the odds of hospitalization by approximately 43% (aOR = 0.574, 95% CI: 0.523–0.630). Higher educational attainment also emerged as a protective factor; for instance, individuals with a college education or above had significantly lower odds of hospitalization compared to those with illiteracy (aOR = 0.591, 95% CI: 0.462–0.755). Homosexual transmission route, when compared to heterosexual transmission, was associated with lower odds of hospitalization (aOR = 0.546, 95%CI: 0.376–0.749), while being married or with a spouse was protective compared to being unmarried (aOR = 0.828, 95%CI: 0.763–0.898). The Hosmer-Lemeshow test indicated good model calibration (P > 0.05).

Discussion

This 10-year retrospective cohort study reveals a substantial rise in hospitalization burden among PLWH in Luzhou, Western China. Overall hospitalization rates increased > 12-fold between 2013 and 2022, indicating growing healthcare demands. Notably, nADEs replaced ADEs as the primary driver, accounting for > 80% of hospitalizations by 2022. These findings highlight evolving health challenges for PLWH in resource-limited settings and carry significant clinical and public health implications.

The sharp rise in hospitalization rates observed in our cohort contrasts notably with trends reported from some high-income countries, where, following the widespread adoption of effective ART, hospitalization rates among PLWH either stabilized or even declined, albeit often remaining higher than in the general population [24]. While improved survival due to ART undoubtedly contributes to a larger pool of aging PLWH susceptible to chronic conditions, the sheer magnitude of the increase in Luzhou suggests that other factors are at play. This divergence may reflect differences in the timing and scale of ART rollout, the baseline health status of the PLWH population, the prevalence of underlying risk factors for nADEs, the capacity of outpatient services to manage chronic conditions effectively, and potentially, differences in healthcare-seeking behaviors or admission thresholds in Western China [25]. The sustained and steep increase in nADE-related hospitalizations, particularly for conditions like chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, digestive disorders, signals an urgent need to integrate comprehensive chronic disease management into routine HIV care. The prominence of these specific nADEs may also reflect local environmental factors, lifestyle patterns, or gaps in primary prevention and early detection services within the region [26]. Furthermore, our temporal trend analysis revealed a noteworthy observation: a particularly pronounced increase in the nADE hospitalization rate occurred around 2017. From a health policy perspective, China fully implemented and scaled up the ‘Treat All’ strategy for HIV antiretroviral therapy from late 2016 into 2017 [27]. As a key sentinel site, Luzhou likely intensified its HIV screening and treatment enrollment efforts during this period. This would have brought a larger, potentially older, and more comorbid population of PLWH into the medical surveillance system, leading to the earlier and more concentrated identification and recording of underlying chronic comorbidities (nADEs). The aging of the PLWH population, combined with other factors, may have contributed to the observed surge in nADE hospitalization rates in 2017. Future research should incorporate more detailed clinical and policy data to validate these hypotheses.

The temporal dynamics of ADEs, which peaked around 2020, warrant consideration. This peak coincided with the initial waves of the COVID-19 pandemic, a period marked by significant disruptions to healthcare services globally and within China [28]. It is plausible that interruptions in ART adherence, reduced access to routine monitoring, and delayed medical consultations during this period may have contributed to a temporary resurgence or increased severity of opportunistic infections leading to hospitalization [29]. While ADEs rates subsequently showed a slight decline, their continued presence underscores the ongoing importance of maintaining robust HIV treatment and opportunistic infection prophylaxis programs, especially during public health emergencies.

Our multivariable analysis identified several key sociodemographic and clinical factors independently associated with hospitalization risk, offering valuable insights for targeting interventions. The nearly two-fold increased risk among individuals aged 50 years and older aligns with extensive literature highlighting the accelerated aging and heightened comorbidity burden in PLWH [30]. This finding emphasizes the critical need for geriatric-informed HIV care models. The elevated hospitalization risk observed among those with non-sexual HIV transmission routes is particularly concerning. This group often includes individuals with a history of injection drug use or contaminated blood product exposure, who may face a constellation of co-occurring health issues (e.g., hepatitis C co-infection), compounded by socioeconomic vulnerabilities and potential stigmatization, leading to delayed diagnosis, poorer engagement in care, and consequently, more severe disease manifestations requiring hospitalization [31, 32].

Divorce or widowhood increased hospitalization risk among PLWH, highlighting the importance of social support and mental health. Such individuals may face financial stress, loneliness, or depression, which can impair health - seeking behaviors and treatment adherence [33]. Our findings of protective effects for females, those with higher baseline CD4 counts, and more educated individuals align with prior research [34, 35]. Higher CD4 counts indicate better immune control, reducing susceptibility to ADEs and some nADEs. Higher education, often associated with better health literacy, socioeconomic status, access to resources, and self - management, also lowers hospitalization risk [36]. Notably, in our adjusted model, homosexual transmission was associated with a lower hospitalization risk than heterosexual transmission in Luzhou. This unexpected result merits further study, potentially reflecting differences in healt-seeking, care engagement, or comorbidities between the groups.

This study has key strengths: a large sample size from a comprehensive system-wide database and a 10-year observation period provides robust longitudinal insights into hospitalization patterns in an understudied region of Western China. Explicit ADEs/nADEs classification with etiological profiling advances understanding of shifting burdens. Limitations should be noted. The retrospective design limits causal inferences, and unmeasured confounders (e.g., detailed ART regimens/adherence, specific lifestyle risks, uncaptured co-infections, healthcare access nuances) may impact findings. Hospitalization diagnoses relied on primary admission codes, potentially missing comorbidities. Findings from Luzhou, while relevant to similar Western China settings, may not generalize to all Chinese regions or countries with differing healthcare systems/HIV epidemics. Although it showed statistical significance in the model, the relatively small sample sizes in certain subgroups might affect the precision of their association estimates. Informed by the findings of this study, future research should employ prospective designs and systematically collect time-dependent covariates to enable the application of more nuanced statistical methods, such as survival analysis, to dynamically identify key drivers of hospitalization and more precisely define high-risk subpopulations.

Conclusion

Our findings reveal a substantial rise in hospitalization demand among PLWH in Western China, primarily driven by increasing nADEs. This necessitates shifting focus from viral suppression to integrated chronic disease management. Targeted interventions are urgently needed for older PLWH, those with non-sexual transmission, limited social support, lower CD4 counts, and lower education. Multilevel strategies—including primary care capacity building for nADE screening/management and public health campaigns on lifestyle and health literacy—are imperative to reduce burden in resource-limited settings. Future research should employ prospective designs to validate patterns, elucidate nADE pathogenesis, and assess context-specific interventions within evolving HIV comorbidities.

Abstract
. We prove that the choice of the automorphism σ \sigma in the construction of a Petit semifield is important when counting isomorphism classes. Different choices of generators σ \sigma of the Galois group of a cyclic field extension K / F K/F of degree n n can produce non-isomorphic proper nonassociative Petit algebras K ⁡ [ t ; σ ] / K ⁡ [ t ; σ ] ​ ( t m − a ) K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a) when n ∤ m n\nmid m , or when n = m n=m and a a does not lie in the fixed field of σ \sigma . In particular, this is the case when F F is a finite field. Thus there are φ ⁡ ( n ) \varphi(n) families of these semifields (where φ \varphi is the Euler function); one family for each distinct generator σ \sigma involved in their construction, and no algebra in one family is isomorphic to one in another family. We then turn to the isotopy classes of these algebras. When the order of σ \sigma is greater or equal to the degree m m , and whenever m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 when n > m n>m , two proper nonassociative Petit division algebras K ⁡ [ t ; σ ] / K ⁡ [ t ; σ ] ​ ( t m − a 1 ) K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a_{1}) and K ⁡ [ t ; σ ] / K ⁡ [ t ; σ ] ​ ( t m − a 2 ) K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a_{2}) which are isotopic are indeed already isomorphic, whenever Aut ⁡ ( K ) {\mathrm{Aut}}(K) is abelian. Under the same assumptions on n n and m m , two such semifields constructed from the same generator σ \sigma of Gal ⁡ ( 𝔽 q n / 𝔽 q ) {\rm Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}) are thus isomorphic whenever they are isotopic. For n = m n=m , we show that each isotopy class of Petit semifields K ⁡ [ t ; σ ] / K ⁡ [ t ; σ ] ​ f K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma]f contains a proper nonassociative cyclic algebra ( K / F , σ , a ) (K/F,\sigma,a) . We then count the isotopy classes of all Petit semifields when n > 2 n>2 . We also present some upper bounds, counts, and parametrizations for different equivalence and isomorphism classes. Most of our results are proved in all generality for any cyclic Galois field extension.

1.
Introduction

After Sandler defined a class of semifields in 1962
[
San1962
]
, a vast generalisation of his construction was presented by Petit
[
Pe1966
,
Pet1968
]
.

Petit algebras are unital nonassociative algebras that are constructed using a skew polynomial ring
R=D[t;\sigma,\delta]
and some monic polynomial
f\in D[t;\sigma,\delta]
, where
D
is a suitable unital associative ring, employing the right division by
f
in
R
to define the algebra multiplication. They can be viewed as canonical nonassociative generalizations of associative quotient algebras. In this paper, we will assume that
D=K
is a field and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(K)
an automorphism of degree
n
such that
K/F
is a cyclic Galois field extension, where
F={\rm Fix}(\sigma)
.

For finite base fields,
Petit algebras yield a large class of semifields
[
LaShe2013
]
which are isotopic to the cyclic semifields (also called Jha-Johnson semifields) introduced in
[
JJ1989
]
, and have associated Maximum Rank Distance (MRD) codes, e.g. see
[
Lobillotal2026
]
. In this setting, their classification is mostly interesting up to isotopies.

A classification of cyclic semifields (and hence of proper nonassociative Petit division algebras over finite fields) up to isotopy, together with the full determination of their autotopism groups has recently been obtained by Santonastaso and Zhou
[
SantonastasoZhou2026
]
for fixed
\sigma
, completing initial work started by Dempwolff
[
Demp2011
,
Demp2010
]
.
Their paper includes a classification of the associated finite MRD codes up to linear and
semilinear equivalence over their respective prime fields.

In this paper we combine several approaches, and study families of Petit algebras as nonassociative rings both up to isotopy and isomorphism, also employing distinct generators of the Galois group in the construction.

The question whether two Petit rings
K[t;\sigma_{1}]/K[t;\sigma_{1}]f_{1}
and
K[t;\sigma_{2}]/K[t;\sigma_{2}]f_{2}
are isotopic that are constructed employing distinct generators
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
of
{\rm Gal}(K/F)
, was raised for the first time in
[
LaShe2013
, Remark 6]
, in the context of semifields.

Our contribution to this question is Theorem
3.3
, where we treat the case that
f(t)=t^{m}-a
and show when distinct generators
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
of
{\rm Gal}(K/F)
produce non-isomorphic Petit rings. These do not need to be division rings.
Example
4.7
shows they can be isotopic.

Theorem
.

(Theorem
3.3
)
Let
n\nmid m
, or let
n=m
and
a_{2}\in K\setminus F
. Every ring isomorphism

\varphi:K[t_{1};\sigma_{1}]/K[t_{1};\sigma_{1}](t_{1}^{m}-a_{1})\to K[t_{2};\sigma_{2}]/K[t_{2};\sigma_{2}](t_{2}^{m}-a_{2})

such that
\varphi
is
F_{0}
-linear where
F_{0}\subset F
is a subfield of
K
, satisfies

\varphi|_{K}\circ\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\circ\varphi|_{K}.

In particular

\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}
whenever
\varphi|_{K}
commutes with
\sigma_{2}
, e.g. when

\mathrm{Aut}_{F_{0}}(K)
is abelian, or when
F_{0}=F
.

We know there are
\varphi(n)
different generators of the Galois group of
K/F
where
\varphi
is the Euler function.
Thus there are
\varphi(n)
different families of isomorphism classes of proper Petit semifields
K[t;\sigma_{i}]/K[t;\sigma_{i}](t^{m}-a)
; one for each choice of generator
\sigma_{i}
of the cyclic Galois group

, whenever either

or, alternatively, when
n=m
and

.
Semifields in two different families cannot be isomorphic (Corollary

).

We know that every Petit ring

is isotopic to
K[t;\sigma^{-1}]/\bar{f}K[t;\sigma^{-1}]
, where
\bar{f}
is the reciprocal of

. This was shown already by Kantor and Liebler
[
KaLieb2008
]
. Indeed, the anti-isomorphism

\sum k_{i}t^{i}\mapsto\sum t^{i}k_{i}
, shows that

(K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a))^{op}\cong K[t;\sigma^{-1}]/(t^{m}-a^{\prime})K[t;\sigma^{-1}]
, which, however, for proper nonassociative Petit rings is a
right
quotient ring defined using left division by
f
and its remainders, and is not a proper nonassociative Petit ring. This is consistent with our results.

Theorems
4.5
and
4.6
show that when
n=m
or
n>m>2
, two proper nonassociative division algebras

and

constructed using the same generator

are isotopic if and only if they are isomorphic. This changes for distinct generators: although
there are no isomorphisms between algebras with

\sigma_{1}\neq\sigma_{2}
by Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, isotopies may exist (Example

). This also seems to only hold when

has the special form

(Example
4.8
).

We then produce some upper bounds and counts for when the base field is finite in Section
5
, which heavily depend on the order
n
of
\sigma
and the degree
m
of
t^{m}-a
, and which can be seen as complementary to the upper bounds in
[
LaShe2013
]
which covered all Petit semifields, and have a completely different flavour to the techniques employed in
[
Lobillotal2026
]
. Note that semifields of the type
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a)
are the opposite algebras (i.e., the

) of

[
San1962
]
.

When

we can count the number of isometry classes of Petit rings

that are
proper nonassociative
as
a
runs over

, where an isometry class contains all the algebras that are isomorphic to each other via some monomial isomorphism of degree

that maps

\alpha t^{k}
for some

and restricts on

(Theorem

.

For
m=n
, the algebras

are also denoted by
(K/F,\sigma,a)
and called

nonassociative cyclic algebras of degree
m
; for

, the algebra

is a classical cyclic algebra
of degree

as defined in
[

, p. 414]
.

Theorem
.

(Theorem
5.7
)
Let
n=m
and
s=\gcd(q^{n}-1,\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1})
. Let
e
be a proper divisor of

and put
s_{e}=\frac{q^{e}-1}{q-1}
. Then the number of isomorphism (and isotopy) classes of semifields

(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma,a)
for a fixed choice of

is exactly

M_{0}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid s\\
d\nmid s_{e}\ \forall e\mid n,\ e<n\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(d)}{o(p)_{d}}.

Varying the choice of

yields a total of
\varphi(n)M_{0}
isomorphism classes, in
\varphi(n)
families.

For
n=m\geq 2
, this result means that we can now obtain an elegant formula for the exact number of isotopy classes of general Petit semifields
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma]f
, which Santonastaso and Zhou describe as an orbit count (Theorem
6.1
), and then generalize this approach to explicitly count all Petit semifields for any

,

(Theorem

). Since the formula we obtain can be hard to compute, we give some bounds for the exact number of isotopy classes in Proposition

.

The paper is organized as follows. After the preliminaries in Section
2
, Section
3
contains the work towards our First Main Theorem, cited above. In Section
4
, we show when isotopic Petit algebras are isomorphic. In Section
5
, we count the number of semifields in different ways and then use the main results to count all Petit semifields in Section
6
. In Section
7
we
give a parametrization of the algebras
(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma_{1},a_{1})

(Theorems
7.1
and
7.2
) under the assumption that for
m>2
, assuming that
F
contains a primitive

th root of unity.
By Theorem

, for
n=m\geq 2
every parametrization of the proper nonassociative cyclic
F
-algebras
(K/F,\sigma,a)

is also a parametrization of the
F
-isotopy classes of all the semifields

K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma]f
, where

is irreducible of degree
n
, for fixed

; each such class contains a proper nonassociative cyclic algebra
(K/F,\sigma,a)
.

We finish with an outlook on what our results imply for skew polycyclic codes in Section

.

We note that Petit rings with non-trivial left principal ideals recently gained importance in coding theory and in cryptography. They are successfully employed in the classification of skew polycyclic codes where they appear as ambient rings of the linear error-correcting codes
[
Pum2025
]
. Especially the rings
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a)
are the ambient rings for the well-known skew constacyclic codes
[
NevPum2026
]
.

Furthermore, proper nonassociative Petit division rings
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a)
over number fields have been successfully used in
Learning with Errors (LWE), a branch of cryptography, where they constitute the ambient algebras in a nonassociative variation of LWE by Mendelsohn and Ling
[
MendLing2025
]
, which outperforms previous ones in several parameters. In both these applications, it is important to understand their isomorphisms and parametrisations, and not just study them up to isotopy and only over finite fields.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Nonassociative algebras

Let
F
be a field. An
F
-vector space
A
is an

algebra
over
F
if there exists an
F
-bilinear map
A\times A\to A
,
(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot y
, called the
multiplication
of
A
. We usually denote the multiplication of
A
simply by the juxtaposition
xy
. Define the
associator
of
A
by
[x,y,z]=(xy)z-x(yz)
.

An algebra
A
is called

unital
if there is an element in
A
, denoted by 1, such that

1x=x1=x
for all
x\in A
. We will only consider unital finite-dimensional nonzero rings and algebras.
If
A
is a unital associative algebra, then we denote the group of invertible elements in
A
by
A^{\times}
.

We note that every unital nonassociative algebra
A
is canonically also a unital nonassociative ring. Conversely, every nonassociative ring can be viewed as a nonassociative algebra over its center or any subring of its center. The following definitions thus make sense for nonassociative rings
A
as well.

We call a nonassociative algebra or ring a
proper
nonassociative algebra or ring, if it is not associative. The

center
of
A
is defined as
{\rm C}(A)=\{x\in A\,|\,x\in\text{Nuc}(A)\text{ and }xy=yx\text{ for all }y\in A\}
.
Here,
{\rm Nuc}(A)=\{x\in A\,|\,[x,A,A]=[A,x,A]=[A,A,x]=0\}
is the
nucleus
of
A
, which in turn is defined as the intersection of the
left, middle and right nucleus
of
A
, defined as
{\rm Nuc}_{l}(A)=\{x\in A\,|\,[x,A,A]=0\}
,
{\rm Nuc}_{m}(A)=\{x\in A\,|\,[A,x,A]=0\}
and
{\rm Nuc}_{r}(A)=\{x\in A\,|\,[A,A,x]=0\}
, respectively. These are associative
subalgebras of
A
.

An algebra
A\not=0
is called a
division algebra
if for any
a\in A
,
a\not=0
, the left multiplication with
a
,
L_{a}(x)=ax
, and the right multiplication with
a
,
R_{a}(x)=xa
, are bijective.
If
A
has finite dimension over
F
,
A
is a division algebra if
and only if
A
has no zero divisors
[
Sch1995
, pp. 15, 16]
. We will only look at finite-dimensional algebras.

A
semifield
is a unital nonassociative division algebra over a finite field. A semifield that is not associative is thus called a proper semifield.

We denote the set of not necessarily unital algebra structures on an

F
-vector space
V
by
{\rm Alg}(V)
. Given
A\in{\rm Alg}(V)
, we write
x\circ_{A}y
for the product of
x,y\in V
in the algebra
A
instead of simply juxtaposition, when the algebra multiplication is not ad hoc clear from the context (subsequently, usually it will be obvious).
For
P,Q,U\in\mathrm{GL}(V)
the algebra
A^{(Q,P,U)}
is called an

isotope
of
A
, and is defined as the vector space
V
together with the new multiplication

xA^{(Q,P,U)}y=U(Q(x)\circ_{A}P(y))

for
x,y\in V
. If
A
is a division algebra then
A^{(Q,P,U)}
is a division algebra. Two algebras
A,A^{\prime}\in{\rm Alg}(V)
are called
isotopic
if
there exist
P,Q,U\in\mathrm{GL}(V)
such that
x\circ_{A}y=U(Q(x)\circ_{A^{\prime}}P(y))
for all
x,y\in V.
If
P=Q=U^{-1}
then
A\cong A^{\prime}
.
We know that
(A^{(Q,P,U)})^{op}=(A^{op})^{(P,Q,U)}
. Note that in Section
4
we will also use isotopies of the
form
U(x\circ_{A}y)=Q(x)\circ_{A^{\prime}}P(y)
.

2.2.
Some results for Petit algebras

be a cyclic Galois extension of

Then

Since the remainders are uniquely determined, the set

The additive group

We will study the case that

We know that

For

.

Let

Let

(i)
Suppose

(i) Suppose first that

Define

{\rm Aut}(K)_{\sigma}=\{\tau\in{\rm Aut}(K)\mid\tau\sigma=\sigma\tau\}.

Lemma 2.4
.

(i) The
F

N_{\mathrm{Aut}(K)}(\langle\sigma\rangle)

\mathrm{Aut}(K)_{\sigma}=\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathrm{Aut}(K)}(\sigma)
is a subgroup of

is abelian.

(ii)
Suppose that

is abelian.
Then the elements of the group
{\rm Aut}(K)_{\sigma}
are precisely the automorphisms of

that preserve the subfield

(that is, that restrict to automorphisms of

(i) Since
\mathrm{Aut}(K)_{\sigma}
is the centralizer subgroup
\mathrm{Cent}_{\mathrm{Aut}(K)}(\sigma)
, it is a group. As

if and only if
\sigma(a)=a
. Now suppose

. Then for any

and therefore

if and only if
\tau(a)\in F
, so

\tau

Theorem 3.1
.

(joint with M Nevins)
Suppose
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are two distinct generators of
\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)
, and let
F_{0}\subset F
be any subfield such that
\mathrm{Aut}_{F_{0}}(K)
is abelian. Suppose
n\nmid m
. Then for every
a_{1},a_{2}\in K^{\times}
, there is no isomorphism of
F_{0}
-algebras

K[t_{1};\sigma_{1}]/K[t_{1};\sigma_{1}](t_{1}^{m}-a_{1})\rightarrow K[t_{2};\sigma_{2}]/K[t_{2};\sigma_{2}](t_{2}^{m}-a_{2})

That is, as
\sigma
varies over the generators of
\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)
, for any fixed
a\in K^{\times}
, all of the resulting algebras
K[t_{1};\sigma]/K[t_{1};\sigma](t_{1}^{m}-a)
are non-isomorphic as
F_{0}
-algebras.

Proof.

As
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are distinct generators of
\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)
, there exists an integer
j
satisfying
2\leq j<n
and
\gcd(j,n)=1
for which

\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}^{j}.

Let
a_{1},a_{2}\in K^{\times}
and set
R_{\sigma_{i},a_{i}}=K[t_{i};\sigma_{i}]/K[t_{i};\sigma_{i}](t_{i}^{m}-a_{i})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.

Suppose now that

\varphi:R_{\sigma_{1},a_{1}}\to R_{\sigma_{2},a_{2}}

is an isomorphism of
F_{0}
-algebras. Since
n\nmid m
we know that the algebras are proper nonassociative with left nucleus
K
so that we get
\varphi|_{K}\in\mathrm{Aut}(K)
.
Thus the restriction of
\varphi
to
K
lies in the abelian group
\mathrm{Aut}_{F_{0}}(K)
, and commutes with
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
.
Let us write

\varphi(t_{1})=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b_{i}t_{2}^{i}.

Then since for all
k\in K
, we have
t_{1}k=\sigma_{1}(k)t_{1}
, it follows that
\varphi(t_{1}k)=\varphi(\sigma_{1}(k)t_{1})
. Since
\varphi
is, in particular, a homomorphism, we infer that

\varphi(t_{1})\varphi(k)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b_{i}t_{2}^{i}\cdot\varphi(k)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b_{i}\sigma_{2}^{i}(\varphi(k))t_{2}^{i},

which should be equal to

\varphi(\sigma_{1}(k))\varphi(t_{1})=\varphi(\sigma_{1}(k))\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b_{i}t_{2}^{i}.

Comparing coefficients, we deduce that for each
i
such that
b_{i}\neq 0
, we must have that for all
k\in K
,

\sigma_{2}^{i}(\varphi(k))=\varphi(\sigma_{1}(k)).

Since these automorphisms of
K
commute, we deduce
\sigma_{2}^{i}=\sigma_{1}
whence
i\equiv j\mod n
.
Let
D
represent the set of degrees of the terms of
\varphi(t_{1})
that may have nonzero coefficients; that is, let

D=\{0\leq i<m\mid i\equiv j\mod n\}.

Note that if
n\geq m-1
, then
D
is at most a singleton. If
D=\emptyset
then no such homomorphism
\varphi
exists, and we are done.

Suppose
D
is nonempty. Let

d_{\max}
be the largest
i\in D
with
b_{i}\neq 0
. Since
j\not\equiv 1\mod n
, we have
d_{\max}>1
.
Choose the least integer
s
such that
d_{max}s\geq m
. Since
2\leq d_{\max}\leq m-1
we have
2\leq s\leq m-1
.

For all
1\leq l\leq m-1
we have

t_{1}^{l}k=\sigma_{1}^{l}(k)t_{1}^{l}
which yields
\varphi(t_{1}^{l})\varphi(k)=\varphi(\sigma_{1}^{l}(k))\varphi(t_{1}^{l})
.
Comparing coefficients shows that

(3.1)

\text{every power }t_{2}^{e}\text{ occurring in }\varphi(t_{1}^{l})\text{ with non-zero coefficient
satisfies }e\equiv lj\,{\rm mod}\,n.

Moreover, we have
t_{1}^{l}=t_{1}(t_{1}^{l-1})
in
R_{\sigma_{1},a_{1}}
, hence

\varphi(t_{1}^{l})=\varphi(t_{1})\varphi(t_{1}^{l-1})
.

Recall that in
R_{\sigma_{2},a_{2}}
we have

(bt_{2}^{d})(ct_{2}^{e})=\begin{cases}b\sigma_{2}^{d}(c)t_{2}^{d+e}&\text{if }d+e<m;\\
b\sigma_{2}^{d}(c)\sigma_{2}^{d+e-m}(a_{2})t_{2}^{d+e-m}&\text{if }m\leq d+e<2m.\end{cases}

We first show by induction that
\varphi(t_{1}^{l})
has degree
ld_{\max}
for all

0\leq l\leq s-1
. This is clear for
l=0
. Let
1\leq l\leq s-1
and let
c\neq 0
be the leading

\varphi(t_{1}^{l-1})
, which has degree
(l-1)d_{\max}
. In the product

\varphi(t_{1})\varphi(t_{1}^{l-1})
, the term

(b_{d_{\max}}t_{2}^{d_{\max}})(ct_{2}^{(l-1)d_{\max}})=b_{d_{\max}}\sigma_{2}^{d_{\max}}(c)t_{2}^{ld_{\max}}

is non-zero, since
ld_{\max}<m
.
All other products
(b_{d}t_{2}^{d})(y_{e}t_{2}^{e})
of terms of
\varphi(t_{1})
and
\varphi(t_{1}^{l-1})
satisfy

and

, with at least one inequality strict. Hence
d+e<ld_{\max}<m
, so no reduction of exponents occurs and these products have degree smaller than
ld_{\max}
.

Now consider
\varphi(t_{1}^{s})=\varphi(t_{1})\varphi(t_{1}^{s-1})
, and let
c\neq 0
be the leading
coefficient of
\varphi(t_{1}^{s-1})
. Since
m\leq sd_{\max}<m+d_{\max}<2m
, the product

(b_{d_{\max}}t_{2}^{d_{\max}})(ct_{2}^{(s-1)d_{\max}})=b_{d_{\max}}\sigma_{2}^{d_{\max}}(c)\sigma_{2}^{sd_{\max}-m}(a_{2})t_{2}^{sd_{\max}-m}

has a non-zero coefficient. We claim that the coefficient of
t_{2}^{d_{\max}s-m}
in

\varphi(t_{1}^{s})
is non-zero, i.e. that no other product
(b_{d}t_{2}^{d})(y_{e}t_{2}^{e})
of a term of

\varphi(t_{1})
and a term of

contributes to

If
d+e<m
, then
(b_{d}t_{2}^{d})(y_{e}t_{2}^{e})=b_{d}\sigma_{2}^{d}(y_{e})t_{2}^{d+e}
is a multiple of

. By (
3.1
),

and

, so

. But

, since

.
Hence

If

.
Since

and

and

Hence we may write

where

and there are no monomial terms of degree

appearing in

3.1
) with

, we obtain

,

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are two distinct generators of

The asymmetry of the statement in

is associative, so the lack of isomorphism is clear. Our theorem reveals that outside of that setting, no isomorphisms between Petit algebras of this form, for different

Proof.

When
a_{1}\in F^{\times}
, we are done, so assume
a_{1}\in K\setminus F
.
As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, let
1<j<n
, with
\gcd(j,n)=1
, be such that
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}^{j}
. Then if
\varphi:R_{\sigma_{1},a_{1}}\to R_{\sigma_{2},a_{2}}
is an isomorphism, all the nonzero monomial terms of
\varphi(t_{1})
must have degree

. Therefore since

we must have
\varphi(t_{1})=kt_{2}^{j}
for some

. Since

s

, and thus
(t_{2}^{j})^{s}=t_{2}^{js-m}a_{2}
in

.
We may therefore directly compute that

\varphi(t_{1})^{s}=(kt_{2}^{j})^{s-1}(kt_{2}^{j})=\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}\sigma_{2}^{ij}(k)t_{2}^{js-m}a_{2}=\sigma_{2}^{js-m}(a_{2})\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}\sigma_{2}^{ij}(k)t_{2}^{js-m}.

Since

, we have
\sigma_{2}^{js-m}=\sigma_{2}^{js}
, whence this simplifies to

\varphi(t_{1}^{s})=\sigma_{2}^{js}(a_{2})\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}\sigma_{2}^{ij}(k)t_{2}^{js-m}.

We now compare

\varphi

\varphi(t_{1}^{s})\varphi(t_{1})=\sigma_{2}^{js}(a_{2})\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}\sigma_{2}^{ij}(k)t_{2}^{js-m}\cdot kt_{2}^{j}=\sigma_{2}^{js}(a_{2})\prod_{i=0}^{s}\sigma_{2}^{ij}(k)t_{2}^{js-m}t_{2}^{j}

We infer from equating the coefficients that

gives

, where there remain also many open questions about isomorphisms, even in the simple case that
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}

[
NevPum2025.b
,
NevPum2026
]
.

4.
Isotopic Sandler algebras are isomorphic when
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}

The proof of
[
San1962
, Theorem 9]
can be generalized to arbitrary base fields and yields our Second Main Theorem. Sandler works over finite fields, and uses the assumption that

and

Note that Sandler’s algebras are the opposite algebras of the Petit algebras

We break up the proof and start with a generalization of some intermediate results in
[
San1962
]
.
The following lemma is a straightforward generalization of

, Lemma, p. 243]

Note that

is the autotopism called

in
[

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(Q,P,U)
be an isotopy between two proper nonassociative algebras

and

over

Proof.

Analogously as in
[

is left multiplication in

and

the one in
A_{2}
.
∎

In the remainder of this section we assume that

\sigma_{1}

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
n=m
,

and

. For
\tau\in{\rm Aut}(K)
define

for all

(F_{\sigma^{-1},1},L_{t},L_{t})
is an autotopism of

Proof.

Since

for all integers

with
0\leq i,j\leq m-1

and

if

if

In particular,
t(t^{k}d)=t^{k+1}d
for

t(t^{m-1}d)=ad
.

It suffices to work with

and

y=t^{j}c
with
b,c\in K
. Put
w=\sigma^{j}(b)c
, so that

.

Suppose that
j\leq m-2
. Then
ty=t^{j+1}c
, and

F_{\sigma^{-1},1}(x)(ty)=(t^{i}\sigma^{-1}(b))(t^{j+1}c)=t^{i}t^{j+1}\sigma^{j+1}(\sigma^{-1}(b))c=t^{i}t^{j+1}w.

If

t(xy)=t(t^{i+j}w)=t^{i+j+1}w=t^{i}t^{j+1}w
.
If

.
If

, then

, hence

.

Suppose now that

. Then

, and

F_{\sigma^{-1},1}(x)(ty)=(t^{i}\sigma^{-1}(b))(ac)=t^{i}\sigma^{-1}(b)ac.

If
i=0
, then
t(xy)=t(t^{m-1}w)=aw=a\sigma^{m-1}(b)c
.
If
i\geq 1
, then

, hence

.
Since

, we have
\sigma^{m-1}=\sigma^{-1}
, so both expressions agree with

.
Thus
(F_{\sigma^{-1},1},L_{t},L_{t})
is an autotopism of
A
. Note that here

is bijective since we assume that

.
∎

Let now

We first generalize

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(Q,P,U)
be an isotopy between
A_{1}
and

A_{2}
, that is

for all

.
Then there exists some

such that

is a monomial matrix, where

with

.
The non-zero entries of
P
lie on the transversal

\{(k_{j},j)\,|\,1\leq j\leq m\},\quad k_{j}\equiv k_{1}+r(j-1)\,{\rm mod}\,n.

Proof.

We write elements of
A_{i}
as
y=\sum_{j=1}^{m}t^{j-1}y_{j}
with

, and will compose maps from right to left.
We write

P(\sum_{j=1}^{m}t^{j-1}y_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}t^{i-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}H_{ij}(y_{j}),

so that we have
P=(H_{i,j})_{i,j}
(acting on column vectors), where every

is an
F_{0}
-linear transformation of
K
, and
F_{0}
is the prime field of
F
.

For all
a\in K
, left multiplication by
a
in
A_{i}
is given by

L_{a}={\rm diag}(R(a),R(\sigma_{i}(a)),\dots,R(\sigma_{i}^{m-1}(a)))

where
R(b)
is right multiplication with
b
in
K
(we follow Sandler’s notation here to stay close to his proof).

By Lemma
4.1
there is
V\in{\rm Aut}(K)
such that
PL_{a}=L_{V(a)}P

for all
a\in K
, the middle nucleus of both algebras. Put
U_{i,j}=V\circ\sigma_{2}^{(i-1)+r(1-j)}
. Comparing entries, this means

H_{ij}\circ R(\sigma_{1}^{\,j-1}(a))=R(\sigma_{2}^{\,i-1}(V(a)))\circ H_{ij}

for all
a\in K
and all
\ 1\leq i,j\leq m
.

By
[
Alb1959
, Lemma 2]
(which holds in our setting) this forces

H_{i,j}=R(h_{i,j})\circ U_{i,j}
, i.e.
H_{i,j}(y)=h_{ij}\,U_{i,j}(y)
, with
h_{ij}\in K
and

\sigma_{2}^{i-1}\circ V\circ\sigma_{1}^{1-j}=\sigma_{2}^{i-1}\circ V\circ\sigma_{2}^{r(1-j)}=V\circ\sigma_{2}^{(i-1)+r(1-j)}=U_{i,j},

using that
{\rm Aut}(K)
is abelian. Here,
R(h_{i,j})
denotes the multiplication with
h_{i,j}
in
K
.

Let
Q(1)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}t^{k}u_{k}
for some
u_{i}\in K
and

Q(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}t^{k}\alpha_{k}
for some
\alpha_{i}\in K
.

From

we see that
UL_{x}=L_{Q(x)}P
for all

x\in A_{1}
. Setting
x=1
and using
L_{1}={\rm id}_{A_{1}}
implies

L_{Q(z)}P=L_{Q(1)}PL_{z}\quad\text{for all }z\in A_{1},

analogously to
[
San1962
, (19)]
where
L_{Q(z)}
denotes the left
multiplication by
Q(z)\in A_{2}
and
L_{z}
the left multiplication by
z\in A_{1}
.
Putting

yields
a system of linear equations analogous to
[
San1962
, (30), (31)]
: Write

y=\sum_{k=1}^{m}t^{k-1}y_{k}
, recall we compose maps from right to left, and let
R(b)
denote multiplication by
b
in
K
.

Analogously to Sandler’s
[
San1962
, (3)–(5)]
(but now with
\sigma_{2}=S
and his

), we know that
for all

(t^{s}w_{s})\circ_{2}(t^{l-1}y_{l})=t^{s}t^{l-1}\sigma_{2}^{l-1}(w_{s})y_{l},

where
t^{s}t^{l-1}=t^{s+l-1}
if

, and
t^{s}t^{l-1}=t^{s+l-1-m}a_{2}
otherwise. Hence we get

(L_{w})_{i,l}=R(\varepsilon_{il}\,\sigma_{2}^{\,l-1}(w_{i-l})),\qquad\varepsilon_{i,l}=\begin{cases}1,&l\leq i,\\
a_{2},&l>i,\end{cases}

with the index

of

.

We have

for

, and
t\circ_{1}(t^{m-1}y_{m})=a_{1}y_{m}

(PL_{t})_{l,j}=H_{l,j+1}\text{ for all }j<m\text{ and }(PL_{t})_{l,m}=H_{l,1}\circ R(a_{1}).

Comparing the
(i,j)
-entries of

(4.1)

\sum_{l=1}^{m}R(\varepsilon_{i,l}\,\sigma_{2}^{\,l-1}(\alpha_{i-l}))H_{l,j}=\sum_{l=1}^{m}R(\varepsilon_{i,l}\,\sigma_{2}^{\,l-1}(u_{i-l}))H_{l,j+1}\quad(1\leq j\leq m),

for all

,
where the indices of

and

. Here,

for

, and

for

.

For

, so that

(4.2)

\sum_{l=1}^{m}R(\varepsilon_{i,l}\,\sigma_{2}^{l-1}(\alpha_{i-l}))H_{l,m}=\sum_{l=1}^{m}R(\varepsilon_{i,l}\,\sigma_{2}^{l-1}(u_{i-l}))H_{l,1}R(a_{1}).

After substituting, we obtain

H_{lj}=R(h_{l,j})\circ V\circ\sigma_{2}^{\,(l-1)+r(1-j)}.

Therefore, for

\sum_{l=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i,l}\sigma_{2}^{l-1}(\alpha_{i-l})h_{l,j}\;V\sigma_{2}^{(l-1)+r(1-j)}(y)=\sum_{l=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i,l}\sigma_{2}^{l-1}(u_{i-l})h_{l,j+1}\;V\sigma_{2}^{(l-1)-rj}(y),

We now compare the coefficients,
using the independence of distinct automorphisms, to show that

is monomial, similar to what Sandler did with Equation
[
San1962
, (32)]
.

Arguing as in
[
San1962
, p. 247]
, exactly one entry in the first column
of

is non-zero, i.e. exactly one

,

(Note that Sandler’s first row corresponds to our first column, as he uses row vectors.)
Indeed, put

for all

.

For all

,

. Thus

.

As

, exactly one

is non-zero.

Arguing as in
[
San1962
, p. 246]
, since exactly one entry

Indeed, if column

, we can compare the

, in the

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(Q,P,U)
be an isotopy between
A_{1}
and

A_{2}
, that is
U(x\circ_{1}y)=Q(x)\circ_{2}P(y)
for all

.
Let

.

(i) Let

. Then
Q(1)=u_{0}\in K^{\times}
and there exists some

\end{array}\right]

h_{i+1}=h_{1}\,N_{i}^{\sigma}(h_{2}h_{1}^{-1}).

.

. The subgroup of autotopisms

(i)
We keep using the notation from the proof of Theorem

[

.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

(K/F,\sigma,a_{1})=K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a_{1})
and

(K/F,\sigma,a_{2})=K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a_{2})
be two proper nonassociative cyclic algebras of degree
m
such that
a_{i}
lies in no proper subfield of
K
(i.e.,
K=F(a_{i})
).
If
(K/F,\sigma,a_{1})=K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a_{1})
and

(K/F,\sigma,a_{2})=K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a_{2})
are isotopic as algebras over some subfield
F_{0}
of
F
, then they are isomorphic as algebras over
F_{0}
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof of
[
San1962
, Theorem 9]
, finiteness of fields is not used:
Suppose that the algebras are isotopic. Let
(Q,P,U)
be such an isotopy. Recall that
k_{1}
is the row of the unique nonzero entry in the first column of
P
.
By Theorem 4.3,
P=(H_{i,j})
has exactly one nonzero entry in each column
j
, in row
k_{j}
. Column
1
describes
P
on the constants
y=y_{1}\in K
, so

P(y_{1})=t^{k_{1}-1}h_{k_{1},1}V(y_{1}),\quad\text{in particular}\quad P(1)=t^{k_{1}-1}h_{k_{1},1}.

By Theorem 4.3 and the proof of Theorem 4.4, we may assume w.l.o.g. that
P
is diagonal with
P(1)=1
, so
P={\rm diag}(V,R(h_{2})V,\dots,R(h_{m})V)
with
h_{k+1}=N_{k}^{\sigma}(h_{2})
.

When

m=2
and that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
has odd characteristic. We obtain from
[
NevPum2025
, Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.3]
:

Theorem 7.2
.

Suppose
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\sqrt{c})
for some
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\smallsetminus(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
and let
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
be nontrivial. Then the distinct isomorphism classes of the nonassociative quaternion algebras with nucleus
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
are represented by
(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q},\sigma,a)
, where
a
is chosen from the set

\mathcal{S}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})=\{\sqrt{c},1+s\sqrt{c}\mid s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}\}.

Alternatively, we can parametrize these distinct isomorphism classes by elements of the set

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})=\{t+\sqrt{c}\mid t\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}/\{\pm 1\}\}.

8.
Outlook

8.1.
Possible generalizations to general Petit algebras

Our results raise several questions. It would certainly be interesting to see in how far our results from Section
3
can be generalized for general Petit algebras
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma]f
. Since this question touches on our knowledge of the isomorphisms between these algebras, we will likely only be successful when we assume that the degree
m
of
f
satisfies
n=m
or
n\nmid m
with
n
the order of the automorphism
\sigma
(the order may also be infinite,
K/F
does not need to be a Galois field extension).

The following partial result which is a straightforward generalization of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
already shows this may be possible in some cases, but it seems very difficult in general.

Let
K/F
be a cyclic Galois extension of degree
n
.
Suppose
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are two distinct generators of
\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)
, and let
F_{0}\subset F
be any subfield such that
\mathrm{Aut}_{F_{0}}(K)
is abelian.

The proofs that show that there is no isomorphism of
F_{0}
-algebras

K[t_{1};\sigma_{1}]/K[t_{1};\sigma_{1}](t_{1}^{m}-a_{1})\to K[t_{2};\sigma_{2}]/K[t_{2};\sigma_{2}](t_{2}^{m}-a_{2})

under the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2

in fact does not use that the algebra
K[t_{1};\sigma_{1}]/K[t_{1};\sigma_{1}](t_{1}^{m}-a_{1})
on the left hand side is defined employing the skew polynomial
f(t)=(t_{1}^{m}-a_{1})
, it only uses that
f(t)
is monic of degree
m
and not two-sided. This guarantees that the left nucleus of the algebra is
K
, which is used in both proofs.

Moreover, the proof of Theorem
3.1
uses only that

t_{1}k=\sigma_{1}(k)t_{1}
, and that
t_{1}^{s}k=\sigma_{1}^{s}(k)t_{1}^{s}
with
t_{1}^{s}

unambiguous for
s\leq m-1
. All of these hold for every monic
f(t)\in K[t;\sigma_{1}]
of degree
m
.

The proof of Theorem
3.2
additionally uses
that we have
t_{1}t_{1}^{s}=t_{1}^{s}t_{1}
; again this is also true in any Petit algebra
K[t_{1};\sigma_{1}]/K[t_{1};\sigma_{1}]f(t)
for every choice of a monic
f(t)\in K[t;\sigma_{1}]
of degree
m
.

This means we have indeed a stronger result.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
f(t)\in K[t;\sigma_{1}]

be monic of degree
m
and not right-invariant. Let

b\in K^{\times}
, and let
\mathrm{Aut}_{F_{0}}(K)
be abelian. If
n\nmid m
, or if
n=m
and
b\notin F
, then

K[t;\sigma_{1}]/K[t;\sigma_{1}]f\not\cong K[t;\sigma_{2}]/K[t;\sigma_{2}](t^{m}-b)
as
F_{0}
-algebras whenever

\sigma_{2}\neq\sigma_{1}
.

In particular, this implies that when
n\nmid m
,
K[t;\sigma_{2}]/K[t;\sigma_{2}](t^{m}-b)
is not isomorphic
to
any
Petit algebra built from a different generator
\sigma_{1}
and
any
skew polynomial
f\in K[t;\sigma_{1}]
of degree
m
.

We do not expect to obtain similar results for larger classes of Petit algebras
K[t;\sigma_{1}]/K[t;\sigma_{1}]f
, since we already know that there are isomorphic algebras that are not isotopic.

8.2.
Skew polycyclic codes

While we previously focused on irreducible skew polynomials
f\in R=K[t;\sigma,\delta]
,
reducible monic
f\in R=K[t;\sigma,\delta]
of degree
m
can be used to build linear skew
(f,\sigma,\delta)
-polycyclic codes of length
m
. For a comprehensive “historical” exposition, cf.
[
BouLe2013
]
.
Skew
(f,\sigma,\delta)
-polycyclic codes are in one-one correspondence with those principal left ideals
of their (potentially) nonassociative Petit ambient ring
R/Rf
, which are generated by a monic right divisor
g
of
f

[
Pum2025
]
. In particular,
the cyclic submodules studied in
[
BouUl2009
,
BUl2014
,
BUl2009
]
are exactly the principal left ideals in the Petit algebra
R/Rf
, generated by such a monic right divisor
g
of
f
, as are
the
(\sigma,\delta)
-codes of
[
BouLe2013
]
. When
f(t)=t^{m}-a\in R=K[t;\sigma]
as in our setup, then
\mathcal{C}
is a skew
(\sigma,a)
-constacyclic code of length
m
.

Previous attempts at classifications of skew polynomial codes always only considered some fixed
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)
. The main idea is that
(\sigma,a)
-constacyclic codes and
(\sigma,b)
-constacyclic codes belonging to the same equivalence class have the same minimum distance and the same algebraic structures.
First steps to classify skew
(\sigma,a)
-constacyclic codes by some natural equivalence relation that preserves their Hamming distance have been achieved in
[
Ou2025
]
. The approach defines equivalences using a subclass of isomorphisms between the ambient Petit rings
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma](t^{m}-a)
and
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma](t^{m}-b)
that preserve the Hamming distance, and restrict to the identity map on
K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. The approach has recently been extended in
[
Pum2025
]
and now includes equivalences defined by using isomorphisms between the ambient Petit rings which restrict to some
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(K)
, and that preserve the Hamming distance. Equivalent codes, however, are being built with the same fixed
\sigma
that generates the Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Now we have gained some insight into what we can and cannot say when we have two different
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)
, and want to compare skew
(\sigma_{1},a_{1})
-constacyclic codes of length
m
with skew
(\sigma_{2},a_{2})
-constacyclic codes of length
m
. We can define code equivalences by putting two skew polynomial codes into the same equivalence class, if they correspond to two isomorphic principal left ideals, and the isomorphism preserves the Hamming distance.

Let
R_{i}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}]
and
i\in\{1,2\}
. We observe that for two ambient rings to be isomorphic they must have the same center, which immediately forces the condition that
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})=\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle=\langle\sigma_{2}\rangle
and so both
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
have the same order. If this condition is not satisfied, we cannot compare skew constacyclic codes that have these two different ambient rings. So we assume
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})=\langle\sigma_{1}\rangle=\langle\sigma_{2}\rangle
and
\sigma_{i}
has order
n
.

Assume first that
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and that
n\mid m
for the order
n
of
\sigma_{i}
. Then the Petit rings
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}](t^{m}-a_{i})
are associative ambient rings of skew
(\sigma_{i},a_{i})
-constacyclic codes of length
m
.

Moreover, we have

\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}](t^{m}-a_{i})\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}^{\ell}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}^{\ell}](t^{m}-a_{i}^{\ell})

for all integers
\ell<n
where
\sigma_{i}^{\ell}
also generates the Galois group, that means
\gcd(\ell,n)=1
.

In this case we can use the Hamming-weight preserving isomorphisms
H
between the ambient rings
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma^{\ell}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}^{\ell}](t^{m}-a_{i}^{\ell})
and
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}](t^{m}-a_{i})
, e.g.
H|_{K}=id
,
H(t)=\alpha t^{\ell}
with
\gcd(\ell,m)=1
, to compare and classify
isometric or equivalent skew
(\sigma_{i},a_{i})
-constacyclic codes and skew
(\sigma_{i}^{\ell},a_{i}^{\ell})
-constacyclic codes of length
m
. Indeed,
we can classify skew
(\sigma_{1},a_{1})
-constacyclic codes and skew
(\sigma_{2},a_{2})
-constacyclic codes of length
m
by equivalence classes, whenever we have associative ambient rings: suppose that
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})=\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle
, and that

\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}](t^{m}-a_{1})\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{2}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{2}](t^{m}-a_{2}).

Then there exists an integer
\ell
such that
\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{1}^{\ell}
and so

\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}](t^{m}-a_{1})\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}^{\ell}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}^{\ell}](t^{m}-a_{2})

and we also know that

\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}](t^{m}-a_{1})\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}^{\ell}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}^{\ell}](t^{m}-a_{1}^{\ell}).

This means that for associative ambient algebras, we can use the equivalence classes defined in
[
Pum2027
]
and easily obtain results also for the equivalence of skew constacyclic codes that are defined with different generators of the Galois group. All the Hamming weight preserving isomorphisms are well known. For instance, the skew
(\sigma_{1},a_{1})
-constacyclic codes are isometric/equivalent to the skew
(\sigma_{2},a_{2})
-constacyclic codes, if and only if the skew
(\sigma_{1}^{\ell},a_{1}^{\ell})
-constacyclic codes are isometric/equivalent to the skew
(\sigma_{1}^{\ell},a_{2})
-constacyclic codes.

Assume next that either
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\smallsetminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
or that
n\nmid m
. Then the Petit ambient rings
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{i}](t^{m}-a_{i})
are proper nonassociative. Assume that
n\nmid m
or
n=m
.
Since

\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{1}](t^{m}-a_{1})\not\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{2}]/\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[t;\sigma_{2}](t^{m}-a_{2})

for any two distinct generators
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
of
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
and any choice of
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\smallsetminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the choice of generator
\sigma_{i}
will also affect the skew
(\sigma_{i},a_{i})
-constacyclic codes we obtain. The skew
(\sigma_{1},a_{1})
-constacyclic codes and the skew
(\sigma_{2},a_{2})
-constacyclic codes correspond to the principal left ideals of two non-isomorphic algebras. We cannot easily compare them.

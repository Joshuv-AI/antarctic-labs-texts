Learnable Scaled Gradient Descent for Guaranteed Robust Tensor PCA
Lanlan Feng, Ce Zhu, Yipeng Liu, Saiprasad Ravishankar, Longxiu Huang

Abstract
Robust tensor principal component analysis (RTPCA) aims to separate the low-rank and sparse components from multi-dimensional data, making it an essential technique in the signal processing and computer vision fields. Recently emerging tensor singular value decomposition (t-SVD) has gained considerable attention for its ability to better capture the low-rank structure of tensors compared to traditional matrix SVD. However, existing methods often rely on the computationally expensive tensor nuclear norm (TNN), which limits their scalability for real-world tensors. To address this issue, we explore an efficient scaled gradient descent (SGD) approach within the t-SVD framework for the first time, and propose the RTPCA-SGD method. Theoretically, we rigorously establish the recovery guarantees of RTPCA-SGD under mild assumptions, demonstrating that with appropriate parameter selection, it achieves linear convergence to the true low-rank tensor at a constant rate, independent of the condition number. To enhance its practical applicability, we further propose a learnable self-supervised deep unfolding model, which enables effective parameter learning. Numerical experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed methods while maintaining competitive computational efficiency, especially consuming less time than RTPCA-TNN.

Tensor decompositions can be classified into several categories, including CP decomposition

CP decomposition represents a multi-dimensional tensor as the sum of rank-one tensors, with the minimal number of such tensors defining the CP rank. However, accurately estimating the CP rank is computationally challenging and considered NP-hard

Tucker decomposition factorizes a tensor into a core tensor along with a set of factor matrices for each mode, with the ranks of these factor matrices organized into a vector known as the Tucker rank.
The extreme imbalance in dimensions (rows and columns) for its unfolding matrices will often result in poor performance
[

This paper mainly focuses on the t-SVD, which has proven to be highly effective in various image processing applications. Its strength lies in capturing low-rank structures within the Fourier domain for tensors.
The t-SVD framework factorizes an order-3 tensor into two orthogonal tensors and an
f
-diagonal tensor, connected by the t-product.

9

Based on the t-product, t-SVD can be efficiently computed by a combination of fast Fourier transform (FFT) along the 3rd mode and multiple matrix SVDs along the 1st and 2nd modes, Leveraging the conjugate symmetry of the Fourier transform, the number of required matrix SVDs is effectively halved, significantly reducing computational complexity
[
10
,
49
]
.
Under the t-SVD framework, theoretical guarantee for the exact recovery has been provided for the TNN-based RTPCA
[
10
]
problem and tensor completion problem
[

The low-rank constraint has been widely extended by incorporating alternative invertible transforms

,

,
23
,
24
,

, developing non-convex versions

, enabling high-order generalizations
[

,

,

]

[

]
, etc.
However, these mainstream methods are all based on TNN and suffer from heavy computational overhead due to the t-SVD computation required in each iteration, making them inefficient, particularly for large-scale datasets.

To mitigate this challenge, decomposition-based approaches have gained considerable attention by factorizing low-rank tensors into smaller components linked by t-product.
Zhou
et al.

[
39
]
introduced tensor bi-factor factorization for tensor completion and employed the alternating minimization algorithm for solution, effectively bypassing the computational burden of SVDs. Building on this, Du
et al.

[
40
]
integrated tensor bi-factor factorization with TNN regularization into a unified framework for tensor completion. These two methods have proved that the proposed algorithm converges to a Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) point.
For RTPCA, Wang
et al.

[

proposed triple

Liu
et al.

[

extended the Burer-Monteiro method to the tensor version, followed by solving tensor completion problem through factorized gradient descent (FGD). Theoretical guarantees have been provided for the recovery performance of the FGD method, demonstrating a linear convergence rate.
However, this approach is limited to symmetric positive PSD tensors, and the convergence rate depends linearly on the condition number
\kappa

Recently, ScaledGD or SGD was introduced for the matrix RPCA problem
[
43

, incorporating a scaling factor in gradient descent steps to eliminate the convergence rate’s dependence on the condition number

Subsequently, Dong
et al.
[
44

extended ScaledGD to the RTPCA problem under the Tucker decomposition and provided the recovery guarantee theory. RPCA-SGD and Tucker-SGD face practical challenges due to their sensitivity to hyperparameter settings, such as learning rates and thresholds.
To tackle this issue, the deep unfolding technique has been applied to RPCA problem in diverse areas such as ultrasound imaging

Cai
et al.

[

designed a supervised deep unfolded architecture for RPCA-SGD to learn a set of parameters, which can be extended to accommodate an infinite number of RPCA iterations.
Herrera
et al.

[
56
]
proposed an unsupervised model only for PSD
low-rank matrices. Dong
et al.

[
46
]
then considered the self-supervised model for Tucker-SGD, which expunges the need for ground truth labels.

\mathcal{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times R\times I_{3}},\mathit{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times R\times I_{3}},\mathcal{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{2}\times R\times I_{3}},\mathcal{U}^{\To}*\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}^{\To}*\mathcal{V}={\mathcal{I}}
.

[

]

with the tubal rank

\begin{split}\lVert\mathcal{A}\rVert_{*}&=\frac{1}{I_{3}}\lVert\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\rVert_{*}=\frac{1}{I_{3}}\sum_{i_{3}=1}^{I_{3}}\lVert\widehat{{\mathbf{A}}}^{(i_{3})}\rVert_{*}\\

.

[

. Note that TNN is the dual norm of the tensor spectral norm.

\kappa(\mathcal{A})=\frac{\sigma_{\max}(\mathcal{A})}{\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal{A})}
.

IV
Methods

IV-A

Model

Given the observed data
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
, RTPCA aims to separate the intrinsic low-rank component
\mathcal{X}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
and the underlying sparse component
\mathcal{S}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
from their mixture
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}_{\star}+\mathcal{S}_{\star}
.
When we denote the tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
with the tubal rank
R
in a factored form
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{L}*\mathcal{R}^{\top}
, where
\mathcal{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times R\times I_{3}},\mathcal{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{2}\times R\times I_{3}}
, we consider the following problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ \left\|\mathcal{L}*\mathcal{R}^{\top}+\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{Y}\right\|_{\fro}^{2}

(12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{s.t.}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\ \text{is an}\ \alpha-\text{sparse tensor}.

Note that we employ two small factors here, which are different from the classic t-SVD form as shown in Definition
11
,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{U}*\mathit{\Sigma}*\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{T}}
.
In real-world scenarios,
\alpha
is typically unknown and challenging to optimize. Then we formulate the following non-convex optimization model:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ \left\|\mathcal{L}*\mathcal{R}^{\top}+\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{Y}\right\|_{\fro}^{2}

(13)

\displaystyle\operatorname{s.t.}

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\left(\mathcal{S}\right)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\left(\mathcal{S}_{\star}\right),

where
\operatorname{supp}(\cdot)
denotes the support set, which is the set of indices where the elements of a tensor are non-zero.

IV-B

Algorithm

Algorithm 2

RTPCA-SGD under the t-SVD format

Input:

Observed tensor
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
, the underlying tubal rank
R
, step sizes
\eta_{k}=\eta
, the thresholding values
\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1}
, the decay rate
\tau
, and the iteration number
K
.

Initialization:

\mathcal{S}_{0}=\operatorname{T}_{\zeta_{0}}(\mathcal{Y})
;
[\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{R}_{0}]=\mathcal{D}_{R}(\mathcal{Y}-\mathcal{S}_{0})
.

for

k=0,1,\cdots,K-1

do

\mathcal{S}_{k+1}=\operatorname{T}_{\zeta_{k+1}}(\mathcal{Y}-\mathcal{L}_{k}*\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\To})
, where
\zeta_{k+1}=\tau^{k}\zeta_{1}
;

\mathcal{L}_{k+1}=\mathcal{L}_{k}-\eta_{k+1}(\mathcal{L}_{k}*\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\To}+\mathcal{S}_{k+1}-\mathcal{Y})*\mathcal{R}_{k}*(\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\To}*\mathcal{R}_{k})^{-1}
;

\mathcal{R}_{k+1}=\mathcal{R}_{k}-\eta_{k+1}(\mathcal{L}_{k}*\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\To}+\mathcal{S}_{k+1}-\mathcal{Y})^{\To}*\mathcal{L}_{k}*(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\To}*\mathcal{L}_{k})^{-1}
;

end for

Output:

Recovered low-rank tensor
\mathcal{X}_{K}=\mathcal{L}_{K}*\mathcal{R}^{\To}_{K}
and sparse tensor
\mathcal{S}_{K}
.

To address the abovementioned problem, our RTPCA-SGD algorithm contains two parts: a) Spectral initialization and b) Scaled gradient updates. We first present the top-
R
tensor approximation operator in Definition
18
and the soft-thresholding operator in Definition
19
.

Definition 18

(
Top-
R
tensor approximation
)
.

Given a tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
, its truncated t-SVD with the parameter
R
is represented as
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{U}_{R}*\mathit{\Sigma}_{R}*\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\To}
, where
\mathcal{U}_{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times R\times I_{3}}
and
\mathcal{V}_{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{2}\times R\times I_{3}}
repectively denote the top-
R
left and right singular tensor of
\mathcal{A}
, and
\mathit{\Sigma}_{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times R\times I_{3}}
is the top-
R
f-diagonal tensor. Then
its top-
R
tensor approximation is defined as

[\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R}]=\mathcal{D}_{R}(\mathcal{A}),

(14)

where
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{U}_{R}*{\mathit{\Sigma}_{R}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{V}_{R}*{\mathit{\Sigma}_{R}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Definition 19

(
Soft thresholding operator
)
.

Given a tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
and a thresholding value
\zeta
, its soft thresholding operator, denoted as
\operatorname{T}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{A})
, is defined such that each entry is computed by

\displaystyle\left[\operatorname{T}_{\zeta}(\mathcal{A})\right]_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sign}\left(a_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\right)\cdot\max\left(0,\left|a_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\right|-\zeta\right)

(15)

Spectral initialization

For the sparse tensor, we initialize it using the soft thresholding operator with parameter
\zeta_{0}
, defined as
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\operatorname{T}_{\zeta_{0}}(\mathcal{Y})
, to eliminate apparent outliers. For the low-rank component, we initialize its two factors using the top-
R
tensor approximation:
[\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{R}_{0}]=\mathcal{D}_{R}(\mathcal{Y}-\mathcal{S}_{0})
. It is clear that the choice of the initial threshold parameter
\zeta_{0}
may influence the recovery quality. Such initialization form is designed to ensure recovery guarantees, as presented in Theorem
3
of Section
V
later.

Scaled gradient updates

We iteratively update the sparse component via a soft-thresholding operator and update two smaller factors of the low-rank component via ScaledGD.
We now discuss the key details of our algorithm as follows:

1.

Update the sparse tensor
\mathcal{S}
: We choose a simple yet effective soft thresholding operator for updating the sparse component at the
k+1
-th iteration as follows:

\mathcal{S}_{k+1}=\operatorname{T}_{\zeta_{k+1}}(\mathcal{Y}-\mathcal{L}_{k}*\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\To}).

(16)

Soft-thresholding has already been applied as a proximal operator of
\ell_{1}
norm for tensors in some TNN-based RTPCA algorithms
[
8
,
10
,
49
]
.
This paper shows that with a set of appropriate thresholding values with
\zeta_{k+1}\geq\|\mathcal{X}_{\star}-\mathcal{X}_{k}\|_{\infty}
,
\operatorname{T}_{\zeta_{k+1}}
will be effectively a projection operator onto the support of
\mathcal{S}_{\star}
, which is later detailed
in Lemma
3
of Section
V
.

2.

Update the low-rank tensor
\mathcal{X}
: To avoid the computational cost of the full t-SVD on
\mathcal{X}
, we factorize it into two smaller factors connected by the t-product,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{L}*\mathcal{R}^{\top}
with
\mathcal{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times R\times I_{3}}
and
\mathcal{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{2}\times R\times I_{3}}
. Define the objective function as
f(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R}):=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathcal{L}*\mathcal{R}^{\top}+\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{Y}\right\|_{\fro}^{2}
, then the gradients with respect to
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{R}
can be computed by

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}f_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}*\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\top}+\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{Y}\right)*\mathcal{R}_{k},

(17)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathcal{R}}f_{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}*\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\top}+\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{Y}\right)^{\top}*\mathcal{L}_{k}.

It has been observed that the standard gradient descent method suffers from ill-conditioning issues
[
43
,
45
,
44
]
. To overcome this, we introduce the scaled terms
(\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\To}*\mathcal{R}_{k})^{-1}
and
(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\To}*\mathcal{L}_{k})^{-1}

to update the tensor factors as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{k}-\eta_{k+1}\nabla_{\mathcal{L}}f_{k}*(\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\To}*\mathcal{R}_{k})^{-1},

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{k}-\eta_{k+1}\nabla_{\mathcal{R}}f_{k}*(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{\To}*\mathcal{L}_{k})^{-1}.

where
\eta_{k+1}
is the step size at the
(k+1)
-th iteration, which needs to be carefully selected to ensure good recovery.

Finally, we summarize the proposed algorithm RTPCA-SGD in Algorithm
2
.

IV-C

Parameter learning

The exact recovery guarantee for Algorithm
2
will be given in Theorem
1
of Section
V
, which indicates that four parameters need to be appropriately chosen,
i.e.
, the thresholding values
\zeta_{0}
,
\zeta_{1}
, the decay rate
\tau
, and the gradient step size
\eta
.
In particular,
\zeta_{k+1}=\tau\zeta_{k}=\tau^{k}\zeta_{1},k\geq 1
.
To address the challenge of parameter selection in practical applications, we propose a learnable self-supervised deep unfolding method called RTPCA-LSGD. This model efficiently learns the four parameters of the RTPCA-SGD algorithm, enhancing both applicability and performance.
Our approach regards each iteration of Algorithm
2
as a neural network layer, where
\zeta_{0}
,
\zeta_{1}
,
\tau
, and
\eta
are treated as learnable parameters.
Softplus activations are employed to ensure
\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1},\eta>0
, while the sigmoid function is used to enforce
0<\tau<1
.
As illustrated in Fig.
3
, the network consists of a feed-forward layer and a recurrent layer, which are respectively designed to model the spectral initialization and the ScaleGD iterative steps of Algorithm
2
.
We employ a self-supervised learning loss (SLL)
[
56
,
46
]
for parameter optimization, encouraging the sparsity of the sparse tensor. The SLL is defined as:

l(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{R})=\frac{1}{\|\mathcal{Y}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}\left\|\mathcal{Y}-\mathcal{L}*\mathcal{R}^{\To}\right\|_{1}.

(19)

Here we only use the observed data
\mathcal{Y}
and the updated terms of the low-rank component. This self-supervised approach eliminates the need for ground truth data, making it highly suitable for real-world applications.

Fig. 3:
Network architecture. The observed tensor
\mathcal{Y}
and the tubal rank
R
serve as inputs to the first layer, performing the spectral initialization step described in Algorithm
2
. The subsequent recurrent layers implement the iterative updates for
\mathcal{X}_{k}=\mathcal{L}_{k}*\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\top}
and
\mathcal{S}_{k}
, following Algorithm
2
. These layers require the iteration number
k
, as it determines the
(k+1)
-th thresholding value parameter, defined as
\zeta_{k+1}=\tau^{k}\zeta_{1}
for
k\geq 0
.

IV-D

Computational complexity

For the TNN-based RTPCA problem
[
10
]
, as formulated in Eq. (
2
), the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) is typically employed for its solution. The primary computational cost arises from the proximity operator of the TNN at each iteration.
Specifically, the per-iteration computational complexity is

O\left(I_{1}I_{2}I_{3}\log\left(I_{3}\right)+\lceil\frac{I_{3}+1}{2}\rceil I_{1}I_{2}\min(I_{1},I_{2})\right)
.
In contrast, the proposed method outlined in Algorithm
2
primarily incurs computational cost from updating the two small factor matrices of the low-rank component, as shown in Eq. (
18
).
The per-iteration computational complexity is

O(I_{1}I_{2}I_{3}\log\left(I_{3}\right)+\lceil\frac{I_{3}+1}{2}\rceil I_{1}I_{2}R)
when the tubal rank
R\ll\min(I_{1},I_{2})
, resulting in a much lower overall cost compared to RTPCA-TNN.

V
Theoretical Results

In this section, we present the recovery guarantee for RTPCA-SGD and prove that with appropriate parameter selection, RTPCA-SGD is able to recover the low-rank component and sparse component under mild assumptions.
We first define two assumptions for the RTPCA problems:

Assumption 1

(
Tensor
\mu
-incoherence conditions of low-rank tensor
)
.

\mathcal{X}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{U}_{\star}\rVert_{\mathrm{2,\infty}}=\max_{i_{1}=1,2,\cdots,I_{1}}\lVert\mathcal{U}_{\star}^{\To}*\mathring{\mathfrak{e}}_{i_{1}}\rVert_{\fro}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu R}{I_{1}}},

(20)

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{V}_{\star}\rVert_{\mathrm{2,\infty}}=\max_{i_{2}=1,2,\cdots,I_{2}}\lVert\mathcal{V}_{\star}^{\To}*\mathring{\mathfrak{e}}_{i_{2}}\rVert_{\fro}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu R}{I_{2}}},

for some constant

. Here
\mathring{\mathfrak{e}}_{i}

In the Fourier domain,

\displaystyle\max_{i_{1}=1,2,\cdots,I_{1}}\lVert[\widehat{\mathbf{U}}_{\star}^{(i_{3})}]^{\To}\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}}\rVert_{\fro}

(21)

\displaystyle\max_{i_{2}=1,2,\cdots,I_{2}}\lVert[\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_{\star}^{(i_{3})}]^{\To}\mathbf{e}_{i_{2}}\rVert_{\fro}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mu R}{I_{2}}},

for some constant
\mu\geq 1

Definition 20

(
Tensor distance metric
)
.

Given a tensor
\mathcal{X}_{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3},}
with tubal-rank
R

\resizebox{22609920}{}{$\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{Q}\in\GL(R)}\left(\|(\mathcal{L}*\mathcal{Q}-\mathcal{L}_{\star})*\mathit{\Sigma}_{\star}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\|(\mathcal{R}*\mathcal{Q}^{-\top}-\mathcal{R}_{\star})*\mathit{\Sigma}_{\star}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}$}.

Lemma 1

For any

In this section, we first conduct experiments on synthetic data to validate our theoretical results. Specifically, we present the phase transition performance of the proposed RTPCA-SGD method under different condition numbers
\kappa
.
We then analyze several key aspects of the method, including its linear convergence rate, independence from the condition number, the impact of the decay rate parameter, and the effectiveness of the deep unfolding approach. Additionally, we compare the phase transition performance of the proposed RTPCA-SGD method with that of the convex RTPCA-TNN model

Finally, we evaluate our methods on real-world datasets including video denoising and background initialization tasks, demonstrating their practical effectiveness and computational efficiency.

VI-A

Synthetic data

For the synthetic experiments, the observed data
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
is constructed as
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}_{\star}+\mathcal{S}_{\star}
, where
\mathcal{X}_{\star}
is a low-rank tensor with tubal rank
R
and condition number
\kappa
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\star}
is an
\alpha
-sparse tensor. The procedure for generating
\mathcal{X}_{\star}
is outlined as follows:

•

Randomly generate two factor tensors,
\mathcal{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times R\times I_{3}}
and
\mathcal{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
, with entries independently sampled from a standard normal distribution.

•

Construct a preliminary low-rank tensor
\mathcal{X}_{0}
as
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\mathcal{L}*\mathcal{R}
, resulting in a tensor with tubal rank
R
but an undefined condition number.

•

Perform a skinny t-SVD on
\mathcal{X}_{0}
to obtain orthogonal tensors
\mathcal{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times R\times I_{3}}
and
\mathcal{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{2}\times R\times I_{3}}
.

•

Generate an
f
-diagonal tensor
\mathit{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times R\times I_{3}}
with the desired condition number
\kappa
. First, construct a Fourier-domain tensor
\widehat{\mathit{\Sigma}}
, setting the diagonal elements of each slice
\widehat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{(i_{3})}
(for
i_{3}=1,\dots,\lceil\frac{I_{3}+1}{2}\rceil
) to be linearly spaced between
\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{i_{3}-1}
and
\frac{1}{\kappa}
. Then, generate the remaining slices using the conjugate symmetry property,
i.e.
,
\widehat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{(i_{3})}=\operatorname{conj}(\widehat{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{(I_{3}-i_{3}+2)})
. Finally, apply an inverse FFT along the third mode to obtain
\mathit{\Sigma}
.

•

Compute
\mathcal{X}_{\star}=\mathcal{U}*\mathit{\Sigma}*\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{T}}
, yielding a low-rank tensor with tubal rank
R
and condition number
\kappa
.

In addition, we randomly generate an
\alpha
-sparse tensor
\mathcal{S}_{\star}
by sampling
\alpha
-fraction entries from a uniform distribution within the range
[-\theta,\theta]
, where
\theta=\frac{\lVert\mathcal{X}_{\star}\rVert_{1}}{I_{1}I_{2}I_{3}}
represents the mean entry-wise magnitude of
\mathcal{X}_{\star}

\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}_{\star}+\mathcal{S}_{\star}

VII
Conclusions

This paper proposes the RTPCA-SGD model, introducing an efficient ScaledGD method within the t-SVD framework for the first time, along with rigorous theoretical guarantees for exact recovery.
Additionally, we develop a learnable self-supervised deep unfolding model termed RTPCA-LSGD, enabling effective parameter learning to achieve further performance improvement.
Synthetic and real-world experiments validate our theoretical findings and demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed methods.
Future work could extend the proposed method to any invertible transformations
[
36
]
, high-order versions
[
37
]
, and other tensor analysis tasks, such as tensor completion and tensor regression
[
7
]
. In addition, exploring recovery guarantees for cases with unknown tubal rank remains a promising direction
[
60
]
.

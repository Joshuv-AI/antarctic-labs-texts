Introduction

Given a compactly generated triangulated category, the telescope conjecture asks whether every smashing subcategory is compactly generated. In many cases, affirmative answers to this question are accompanied by a full classification of the relevant subcategories.

In the setting of derived categories of rings, a landmark result is due to Neeman
[
Nee92
]
, who proved that for any commutative noetherian ring
R
, the smashing subcategories of
\mathcal{D}(R)
are in bijection with the specialization-closed subsets of
\Spec(R)
, and that the telescope conjecture holds in this context. Other positive results for derived categories of rings appear in
[
DP08
,
KŠ10
,
Ste14
]
, a criterion for rings of weak global dimension at most one is given in
[
BŠ17
]
, while a well-known counterexample is provided in
[
Kel94
]
. Many of these (counter)examples are elegantly unified under the general framework developed in
[
Hrb25
]
.

In the context of representations of Dynkin quivers over commutative noetherian rings, the work of Antieau and Stevenson
[
AS16
]
extends Neeman’s result. They established a bijection between the lattice of smashing subcategories of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
and the lattice of poset homomorphisms from
\Spec(R)
to the lattice of noncrossing partitions
\mathbf{Nc}(Q)
, and proved that the telescope conjecture continues to hold in this broader setting.

More recently, a generalization of the telescope conjecture has been proposed in the context of t-structures that are not necessarily stable (i.e. arising from triangulated subcategories)
[
BH21
, Question A]
and
[
HN21
, Question A.7]
. In this setting, the conjecture asks whether every homotopically smashing t-structure (in the sense of
[
SŠV23
]
) is compactly generated. For stable t-structures, this condition coincides with requiring the aisle to be smashing, thereby recovering the classical formulation.

In the derived category
\mathcal{D}(R)
of a commutative noetherian ring, Alonso, Jeremías, and Saorín
[
AJS10
]
provided a full classification of compactly generated t-structures in terms of filtrations (i.e. non increasing sequences) of specialization closed subsets of
\Spec(R)
. Subsequently, Hrbek and Nakamura
[
HN21
]
showed that the generalized telescope conjecture holds in this setting. Additional examples proving the conjecture for t-structures appear in
[
AH21
,
BH21
]
.

In this paper, we extend
[
AJS10
]
and
[
HN21
]
to the derived category of representations of Dynkin quivers over commutative noetherian rings. We classify all compactly generated (aisles of) t-structures in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
and prove that the telescope conjecture holds, unifying and generalizing the aforementioned results. Let
\mathrm{Filt}(\mathbf{Nc}(Q))
be the lattice of filtrations of noncrossing partitions of
Q
, then our first main theorem is:

Theorem I

(
4.2
)
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring and
Q
a Dynkin quiver. Then:

(1)

There is an order-preserving bijection

The bijection in (1) is obtained by identifying

Structure of the paper

The paper is divided into four sections and an appendix. In
Section

1
, we recall basic and preliminary results on representations of small categories
C
over commutative rings
R
, focusing in particular on the action of
\mathcal{D}(R)
on
\mathcal{D}(RC)
, the effect of this action on t-structures, and the support theory of
\mathcal{D}(RC)
. In

, we investigate two key properties – the

Notation and conventions

In a category
\mathcal{T}
, we will often consider the smallest subcategory containing a class of objects
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{T}
and closed under certain operations. We denote such subcategories by Sans-serif letters and angle brackets; for example, when
\mathcal{T}
is triangulated with coproducts, we write
\loc_{\mathcal{T}}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle
for the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
\mathcal{X}
and closed under coproducts (i.e. the smallest localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
\mathcal{X}
). If instead we consider the poset of all subcategories of a certain type in
\mathcal{T}
, ordered by inclusion, we denote it by Roman letters and round brackets; in the previous example,
\mathrm{Loc}(\mathcal{T})
denotes the lattice of localizing subcategories of
\mathcal{T}
.

We also consider subcategories defined by orthogonality conditions. For two classes of objects
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
in
\mathcal{T}
, we write:

\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})=\{f\in\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)\mid X\in\mathcal{X},\,Y\in\mathcal{Y}\}

\mathcal{X}^{\perp}=\{Y\in\mathcal{T}\mid\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{X},Y)=0\}\text{ and }{}^{\perp}\mathcal{Y}=\{X\in\mathcal{T}\mid\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\mathcal{Y})=0\}

For a derived category
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, a class of objects
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{A}
, and a set of integers
I\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, we write
\mathcal{D}^{I}(\mathcal{S})
for the full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
consisting of complexes
X
such that
X^{i}
belongs to
\mathcal{S}
if
i\in I
, and is zero otherwise. We write
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(\mathcal{S})
for
\mathcal{D}^{\{i\geq 0\}}(\mathcal{S})
, and similarly for other intervals.

1.1.
Representations of small categories

Given a small category

Lemma 1.1

(
[

)
.

The functor category

is a Grothendieck abelian category with exact products and a set of small projective generators. In particular, it has also exact coproducts, an injective cogenerator and a projective generator.

As for any Grothendieck abelian category, we can construct the homotopy category
\mathcal{K}(RC)
and the derived category

Remark 1.2
.

The derived category
\mathcal{D}(RC)
is compactly generated. Indeed, the set

For an abelian category

1.2.

Let

for a nice treatment in this context). Given a collection of objects

we will denote by

(resp.

\mathcal{T}
containing the object in
\mathcal{S}

;

Remark 1.9
.

By

2.
Local to global principle and Minimality

The aim of this section is to generalize two properties of localizing subcategories of
\mathcal{D}(RC)
– the
local to global principle
and the
minimality of stalk subcategories
– to the setting of homotopically smashing cosuspended subcategories. These properties will allow us to reduce the problem of generating homotopically smashing coaisles, first from
\mathcal{D}(RC)
to the stalk subcategories
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{D}(RC)

\mathcal{D}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p})C)

[

, Section 4.2]
, these property characterize, among the compactly generated
R
-linear triangulated categories (in the sense of
[

, Section 2.3]
), those which the authors called the
stratified
ones. For a compactly generated
R
-linear category
\mathcal{T}
, the authors develop a theory of support, equivalent to that of
[
Ste13
]
by
[
Ste13
, Section 9]
, and identify the support of
\mathcal{T}
with a certain subset of
\Spec(R)
, denoted
\supp_{R}(\mathcal{T})
. In this setting, stratified
R
-linear categories are precisely those whose lattice of localizing subcategories,
\mathrm{Loc}(\mathcal{T})
, is parametrized by the subsets of
\supp_{R}(\mathcal{T})
; see
[
BIK11
, Theorem 4.2]
. In particular, the local to global principle states that:

(LTG)

For any

,
\loc_{\mathcal{T}}\left\langle X\right\rangle=\loc_{\mathcal{T}}\left\langle\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}X\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\right\rangle
;

and, by
[
BIK11
, Proposition 3.6]
, it characterizes the categories for which
\mathrm{Loc}(\mathcal{T})
is in bijection with the tuples
(\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{p}))_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(\mathcal{T})}
, where
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathrm{Loc}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T})
for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
. However, in the context of representations of small categories, stratification is not the right notion to consider; indeed, while the local to global principle holds in
\mathcal{D}(RC)
, for any commutative noetherian ring
R
and small category
C

[
AS16
, Theorem 3.5]
, the minimality fails even in
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)
, for a field
\mathbb{K}
and a Dynkin quiver

[

, Example 4.6]
.

In order to obtain a classification of the localizing subcategories of
(\mathcal{D}(RC))
and then prove the stable telescope conjecture (see
Section

), in
[

, Theorem 4.2]
the authors replaced the minimality condition, with the following equivalent conditions:

(M)

\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
and

,
\loc_{RC}\left\langle X\right\rangle=\loc_{RC}\left\langle\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimesL X\right\rangle

(M’)
​

, there is a bijection between

and
\mathrm{Loc}(\mathcal{D}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p})C))

Even if these conditions are not equivalent to the minimality in

]
for a general small category

, they are in the case of
\mathcal{D}(R)
and so, in a sense, they transfer the notion of minimality from the stratified categories to our context; so, it still makes sense to call them minimality conditions in

2.1.

In the following we will generalize

.
∎

Note that the last containment is the one that forces us to switch to coaisles, in fact aisles of non stable t-structure are never closed under tensor products with complexes concentrated in nonnegative degrees.

2.2.
Minimality of stalk subcategories

The following theorem is the analogue of
(M)
for cosuspended subcategories closed under products and directed homotopy colimits. Unlike the theorem above, this is not a generalization of
(M)
, in fact it does not apply to localizing subcategories, since they are not closed under products in general. However, we will prove in an upcoming work that this result implies
(M’)
for homotopically smashing coaisles in the context of quiver representations, i.e. for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
, there is a bijection between
\mathrm{Coaisle_{hs}}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{D}(RQ))
and
\mathrm{Coaisle_{hs}}(\mathcal{D}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p})Q))
for any finite quiver
Q
(see
[
HS25
]
).

Theorem 2.3

(
Minimality of Stalk Subcategories).

For any complex
Y\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{D}(RC)
, we have that

\cosusp_{RC}^{\Pi,\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle Y\right\rangle=\cosusp_{RC}^{\Pi,\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle\RHom_{R}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),Y)\right\rangle

Proof.

The containment from right to left follows from
1.10
(2). For the other direction, consider the class

\mathcal{M}=\left\{M\in\mathcal{D}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\RHom_{R}(M,Y)\in\cosusp_{RC}^{\Pi,\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle\RHom_{R}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),Y)\right\rangle\right\}

It is a cocomplete suspended subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
containing
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\cong R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Thus, by
1.9
and
[
AJS10
, Proposition
2.4]
, it contains
R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(t)}
for any
t\geq 1
and, by
[
Hrb20
, Lemma 5.3]
, it contains the Koszul complexes
K^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(t)}\right)\cong K\left(\mathfrak{p}^{(t)}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where the first complex is computed over
R_{p}
and the second over
R
.

Consider now the class

\left\{M\in\mathcal{D}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid M\otimesL Y\in\cosusp_{RC}^{\Pi,\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle\RHom_{R}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),Y)\right\rangle\right\}

It is a homotopically smashing cosuspended subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
. By self-duality of Koszul complexes in
1.15
,
\RHom_{R}(K\left(\mathfrak{p}^{(t)}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}},Y)\cong K\left(\mathfrak{p}^{(t)}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}[-n]\otimesL Y
, thus it contains the complex
K\left(\mathfrak{p}^{(t)}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}[-n]
for any
t\geq 1
. Since it is closed under directed homotopy colimits, by
1.15
, it contains the the complex
K_{\infty}(\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}R
and we can conclude that
Y\cong\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}R\otimesL Y\in\cosusp_{RC}^{\Pi,\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle\RHom_{R}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),Y)\right\rangle
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Note that, we use products only in the first part of the proof to make
\mathcal{M}
cocomplete and then apply
1.9
. It turns out that, with a bit more of work, we can prove that the inclusion
Y\in\cosusp_{RC}^{\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle\RHom_{R}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),Y)\right\rangle
holds without closure under products. Indeed, in this case, the class

\mathcal{M}=\left\{M\in\mathcal{D}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\RHom_{R}(M,Y)\in\cosusp_{RC}^{\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle\RHom_{R}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),Y)\right\rangle\right\}

is just a suspended subcategory of
\mathcal{D}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
containing
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\cong R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. For any
t\geq 1
, the Koszul complex
K^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(t)}\right)
is a bounded complex of finite free
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-modules concentrated in degrees
[-n,0]
and, for any
i\in[-n,0]
, its cohomology module
H^{i}
is a finitely generated
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module annihilated by
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(t)}
(see
[
CFH24
, 11.4.6 (a)]
). In particular, each
H^{i}
admits a finite filtration with composition factors isomorphic to
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
(see
[
Sta
, Lemma 10.62.1-2-4]
). It follows that, for any
i
, the module
H^{i}
is contained in
\mathcal{M}
and, by closure under extensions, so does the complex
K^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(t)}\right)\cong K\left(\mathfrak{p}^{(t)}\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then we can conclude as in the proof of
2.3
.

3.
Dynkin quivers and Lattice lifts

When the small category
C
is the free category on a finite quiver
Q
, the category
\Mods_{R}(RC)
is equivalent to the category of modules
\Mods(RQ)
, where
RQ
is the free
R
-algebra on the set of paths of
Q
with the composition of paths as product. In this case, the functors
X:C\to\Mods(R)
can be seen as representations of the quiver
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
over the ring
R
, i.e.
X=(X_{i},X_{\alpha})_{i\in Q_{0},\alpha\in Q_{1}}
where
X_{i}
is an
R
-module for any vertex
i\in Q_{0}
and
X_{\alpha}:X_{i}\to X_{j}
is an
R
-linear map for any arrow
\alpha:i\to j\in Q_{1}
.

Moreover, if
Q
is a Dynkin quiver and
R=\mathbb{K}
a field, the path algebra
\mathbb{K}Q
is a representation-finite hereditary algebra and it is well-known that the indecomposable modules do not depend on the field, be it algebraically closed or not (see, for example,
[
DDPW08
, Theorem 1.23]
). In this case also the (compactly generated) t-structures of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)
are independent of the field
\mathbb{K}
. In particular, they depend on the lattice
\mathbf{Nc}(Q)
of noncrossing partitions of
Q
(see
Appendix

A
for more details).

Proposition

(
A.4
)
.

For any field
\mathbb{K}
and Dynkin quiver
Q
, there is an order-preserving bijection between the lattices
\Aisle(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))
and
\mathrm{Filt}(\mathbf{Nc}(Q))
.

In the following,
R
will be a commutative noetherian ring and
Q
a Dynkin quiver. We introduce a class of
RQ
-modules which, in a sense, play the role of the indecomposables in
\Mods(\mathbb{K}Q)
.

Definition 3.1
.

An
RQ
-module is called (
free
)
lattice
if it is vertexwise finitely generated projective (resp. free) over
R
. A lattice
X
is called
rigid
if
\Ext_{RQ}^{1}(X,X)=0
and
exceptional
if it is rigid and
\mathrm{End}_{RQ}(X)\cong R
. A lattice
X
is said to have a
rank vector

d\in\mathbb{N}^{\mid Q_{0}\mid}
if for every
i\in Q_{0}
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
there is an isomorphism
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{R}X_{i}\cong\kappa(\mathfrak{p})^{d_{i}}
. Obviously, a free lattice has always a rank vector
d
with
d_{i}=\rank(X_{i})
.

Remark 3.2
.

By
[
CB24
, Lemma 3.1 (i)]
,
RQ
-lattices have projective dimension less or equal then
1
. In particular, stalk complexes of
RQ
-lattices are compact in
\mathcal{D}(RC)
. Moreover, by
1.7
, they are
R
-K-projective. Thus, by
1.8
, any time the functor
\otimesL
is applied to an
RQ
-lattice, it will be computed as the standard vertexwise tensor product.

In view of the fact that, for any field
\mathbb{K}
, the indecomposable
\mathbb{K}Q
-modules are in bijection with the real Schur roots of
Q
, we can restate
[
CB24
, Theorem B]
as follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any field
\mathbb{K}
, commutative ring
R
and Dynkin quiver
Q
, there exists an indecomposable
\mathbb{K}Q
-module
L
with dimension vector
d
if and only if there exists an exceptional
RQ
-lattice
X
with rank vector
d
. In this case, there exists a unique exceptional free lattice
\widetilde{L}
with rank vector
d
, and any rigid
RQ
-lattice of rank vector
d
is exceptional.

We will denote by
\widetilde{\quad}:\ind(\mathbb{K}Q)\to\mathrm{lat}(RQ)
the assignment in the theorem and we will refer to it as
lattice lift
. Since the indecomposable objects of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)
are shifts of indecomposable
\mathbb{K}Q
-modules, given a class of objects
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)
closed under summands, by abuse of notation, we write

\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}=\left\{\widetilde{L}[n]\mid L\in\ind(\mathbb{K}Q)\text{ and }L[n]\in\mathcal{X}\right\}

for the lattice lift of the indecomposables in
\mathcal{X}
.

Remark 3.4
.

We can actually give a concrete description of the lattice lifts. Indeed, by
[
Rin98
]
, for any field
\mathbb{K}
, the exceptional
\mathbb{K}Q
-modules are tree modules, i.e. they admit a presentation by
0,1
-matrices on the arrows. This applies to the indecomposable modules over Dynkin quivers, thus for an indecomposable
\mathbb{K}Q
-module
L
of dimension vector
d
, we can construct
\widetilde{L}
defining
\widetilde{L}_{i}=R^{d_{i}}
and
\widetilde{L}_{\alpha}=L_{\alpha}
for any
i\in Q_{0}
and
\alpha\in Q_{1}
. In this way, we obtain a free
RQ
-lattice which, by
[
CB24
, Lemma 3.1 (iv)]
, is rigid and so, by previous theorem, exceptional. Thus, it is the unique lattice lift of
L
.

The following proposition shows that the
\Hom
-orthogonality of two indecomposable
\mathbb{K}Q
-modules is sufficient to detect the
\Hom
-orthogonality of their lattice lifts.

Proposition 3.5
.

For any indecomposable
\mathbb{K}Q
-modules
M
and
L
, it holds that

(1)

\Hom_{RQ}\left(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{L}\right)=0
if and only if
\Hom_{\mathbb{K}Q}(M,L)=0
;

(2)

\Ext_{RQ}^{1}\left(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{L}\right)=0
if and only if
\Ext_{\mathbb{K}Q}^{1}(M,L)=0
.

Proof.

By
[
CB24
, Theorem 4.1]
, both the
R
-modules
\Hom_{RQ}\left(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{L}\right)
and
\Ext_{RQ}^{1}\left(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{L}\right)
are finitely generated projective of constant rank equal to the dimension over
\mathbb{K}
of the vector spaces
\Hom_{\mathbb{K}Q}(M,L)
and
\Ext_{\mathbb{K}Q}(M,L)
respectively. Thus, both point 1 and 2 hold.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For any two indecomposable
\mathbb{K}Q
-complexes
M
and
L
:

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(RQ)}\left(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{L}\right)=0\text{ if and only if }\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)}(M,L)=0

In particular, for any class
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)
, it holds that
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}^{\perp}}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Let
M=M^{\prime}[m]
and
L=L^{\prime}[\ell]
, where
M^{\prime}
and
L^{\prime}
are two indecomposable
\mathbb{K}Q
-modules and
m,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
. There are standard isomorphisms

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(RQ)}\left(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{L}\right)\cong\Ext_{RQ}^{\ell-m}\left(\widetilde{M^{\prime}},\widetilde{L^{\prime}}\right)\text{ and }\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)}(M,L)\cong\Ext_{\mathbb{K}Q}^{\ell-m}(M^{\prime},L^{\prime})

By
3.5
, the statement holds for
\ell-m=0,1
. Moreover, by
3.2
and since
\mathbb{K}Q
is hereditary, both
\Ext^{\ell-m}
vanishes for
\ell-m<0
and
\ell-m\geq 2
. The particular part follows by taking
M\in\mathcal{X}
and
L\in\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
.
∎

3.1.
Compactly generated t-structures

In this section we will introduce two assignments, linking the compactly generated aisles of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
to the aisles of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)
, which will lead to the complete classification of the former. In view of
A.4
, we can refer to the poset
\Aisle(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))
without specifying any field. The assignments are the following

(3.1)

\begin{gathered}\Aisle_{\mathrm{cg}}(\mathcal{D}(RQ))\longleftrightarrow\Hom_{\mathrm{Pos}}(\Spec(R),\Aisle(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)))\\
\varphi:\mathcal{U}\longmapsto\left(\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\aisle_{\mathbb{K}Q}\left\langle L\in\ind(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))\mid R/\mathfrak{p}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\mathcal{U}\right\rangle\right)\\
\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{p}\otimesL\widetilde{\sigma(\mathfrak{p})}\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\right\rangle\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\longmapsto$}}\,\sigma:\psi\end{gathered}

Lemma 3.7
.

For any ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
and indecomposable
\mathbb{K}Q
-complex
L
, it holds that:

(1)

If
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
then
\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{b}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right\rangle\subseteq\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right\rangle
;

(2)

If
\mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{b})
then
\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right\rangle=\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{b}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right\rangle
;

(3)

\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right\rangle=\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a}\otimesL\widetilde{L},R/\mathfrak{b}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right\rangle
;

(4)

\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right\rangle=\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle K(\mathfrak{a})\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right\rangle
and it is compactly generated.

Proof.

(1) Let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
, note that, similarly to
1.10
, the class

\mathcal{M}=\left\{M\in\mathcal{D}(R)\mid M\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right\rangle\right\}

is a cocomplete suspended subcategory containing
R/\mathfrak{a}[0]
. By
[
AJS10
, Proposition 2.4 (1)]
,
R/\mathfrak{b}\in\aisle_{R}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a}\right\rangle
, so
\mathcal{M}
contains
R/\mathfrak{b}[0]
.

(2) Let
\mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{rad}(\mathfrak{b})
, as before, the class

\mathcal{M^{\prime}}=\left\{M\in\mathcal{D}(R)\mid M\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{b}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right\rangle\right\}

it is a cocomplete suspended subcategory containing
R/\mathfrak{b}[0]
and, by
[
AJS10
, Proposition 2.4 (4)]
,
\aisle_{R}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a}\right\rangle=\aisle_{R}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{b}\right\rangle
. So,
R/\mathfrak{b}[0]\in\mathcal{M}
and
R/\mathfrak{a}[0]\in\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
and the thesis follows.

(3) The proof follows analogously to (2) since, by
[
AJS10
, Proposition 2.4 (2)]
,
\aisle_{R}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\right\rangle=\aisle_{R}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a},R/\mathfrak{b}\right\rangle
.

(4) The proof follows analogously to (2) since, by
[
Hrb20
, Lemma 5.3 (iii)]
,
\aisle_{R}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{a}\right\rangle=\aisle_{R}\left\langle K(\mathfrak{a})\right\rangle
. Moreover, since the complex
K(\mathfrak{a})
is compact, by
1.8
, the functor
K(\mathfrak{a})\otimesL\_
has a coproduct preserving right adjoint, and thus it preserves compactness by
[
Nee96
, Theorem 5.1]
. Since, by
3.2
, any
RQ
-lattice is compact, the complex
K(\mathfrak{a})\otimesL\widetilde{L}
is compact in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

The assignments
3.1
are well defined and the composite
\varphi\circ\psi
gives the identity on the poset
\Hom_{\mathrm{Pos}}(\Spec(R),\Aisle(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)))
. In particular, the assignment
\psi
is injective.

Proof.

Clearly the assignment
\varphi
gives a map from
\Spec(R)
to
\Aisle(\mathbb{K}Q)
and, by
3.7
(1), it is also a homomorphism of poset. Indeed, for any two prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
, there is a containment

\aisle_{\mathbb{K}Q}\left\langle L\mid R/\mathfrak{p}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\mathcal{U}\right\rangle\subseteq\aisle_{\mathbb{K}Q}\left\langle L\mid R/\mathfrak{q}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\mathcal{U}\right\rangle

As for the assignment
\psi
, by
3.7
(4), we have the equality

\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{p}\otimesL\widetilde{\sigma(\mathfrak{p})}\right\rangle=\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle K(\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\widetilde{\sigma(\mathfrak{p})}\right\rangle

and
K(\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\widetilde{\sigma(\mathfrak{p})}
is compact, yielding a compactly generated aisle of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
.

Denoting
\varphi\circ\psi(\sigma)
by
\sigma^{\prime}
, for a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
we have that

\sigma^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})=\aisle_{\mathbb{K}Q}\left\langle L\mid R/\mathfrak{p}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{q}\otimesL\widetilde{\sigma(\mathfrak{q})}\mid\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)\right\rangle\right\rangle

Obviously,
\sigma(\mathfrak{p})\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})
. Let
L
be a generator of
\sigma^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})
, by
3.7
(4), we have that

K(\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle K(\mathfrak{q})\otimesL\widetilde{\sigma(\mathfrak{q})}\mid\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)\right\rangle

By

,
K(\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\widetilde{L}
is constructed trough extensions, positive shifts, direct summands and coproducts, from

\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\left(K(\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\widetilde{L}\right)\in\aisle_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})Q}\left\langle\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\left(K(\mathfrak{q})\otimesL\widetilde{\sigma(\mathfrak{q})}\right)\mid\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(R)\right\rangle

then, by

Since aisles are independent of the field, we can conclude that

and so, since
\sigma

∎

)

3.2.
Homotopically smashing t-structures

In this section we will show that, according to the commutative setting (

), homotopically smashing coaisles are cogenerated by stalk complexes of vertexwise injective

\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}=\left\{E(R/\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\widetilde{\sigma(\mathfrak{p})^{\perp}}\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\right\}

The following is a dual version of

Proposition 3.10
.

For any prime ideals

Theorem 3.15
.

For any homotopically smashing coaisle

Denote the right hand side by
\mathcal{W}
. The containment

is clear. On the other hand, if

4.
Main results

4.1.
Telescope conjecture and Classification

Thanks to these last results, we can build an assignment which links homotopically smashing coaisles of
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
to coaisles of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)
. Note that we will consider the poset
\mathrm{Coaisle}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))
with the reverse order, which is actually isomorphic to
\Aisle(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))
. The assignment is the following:

\begin{gathered}\omega:\mathrm{Coaisle_{hs}}(\mathcal{D}(RQ))\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathrm{Pos}}(\Spec(R),\mathrm{Coaisle}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))^{\mathrm{op}})\\
\mathcal{V}\longmapsto\left(\omega_{\mathcal{V}}:\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\cosusp_{\mathbb{K}Q}^{\Pi,\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle L\in\ind(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))\mid E(R/\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\mathcal{V}\right\rangle\right)\end{gathered}

Remark 4.1
.

The assignment
\omega
is well-defined and injective.

•

By
1.9
, for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)
,
\omega_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{p})
is a (homotopically smashing) coaisle.

•

The map
\omega_{\mathcal{V}}
is a poset homomorphism. Indeed, for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, by
3.10
(1) we have that

\cosusp_{\mathbb{K}Q}^{\Pi,\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle L\mid E(R/\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\mathcal{V}\right\rangle\subseteq\cosusp_{\mathbb{K}Q}^{\Pi,\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle L\mid E(R/\mathfrak{q})\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\mathcal{V}\right\rangle

i.e.
\omega_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\omega_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{p})
in
\mathrm{Coaisle}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))^{\mathrm{op}}
.

•

The assignment
\omega
is injective. Suppose that given two homotopically smashing coaisles
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
we have that
\omega_{\mathcal{V}}=\omega_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}
, then by
3.15
it turns out that

\mathcal{V}=\cosusp_{RQ}^{\Pi,\mathbf{hs}}\left\langle E(R/\mathfrak{p})\otimesL\widetilde{\omega_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{p})}\right\rangle=\mathcal{V}^{\prime}

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring and
Q
a Dynkin quiver, then:

(1)

The following assignments are mutually inverse poset isomorphisms

\begin{gathered}\Aisle_{\mathrm{cg}}(\mathcal{D}(RQ))\longleftrightarrow\Hom_{\mathrm{Pos}}(\Spec(R),\Aisle(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)))\\
\varphi:\mathcal{U}\longmapsto\left(\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\aisle_{\mathbb{K}Q}\left\langle L\in\ind(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))\mid R/\mathfrak{p}\otimesL\widetilde{L}\in\mathcal{U}\right\rangle\right)\\
\aisle_{RQ}\left\langle R/\mathfrak{p}\otimesL\widetilde{\sigma(\mathfrak{p})}\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)\right\rangle\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\longmapsto$}}\,\sigma:\psi\end{gathered}

(2)

Any homotopically smashing t-structure in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
is compactly generated.

Proof.

(1) By
3.8
, it suffices to prove that
\psi
is surjective. Given a compactly generated aisle
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{D}(RQ)
, the associated coaisle
\mathcal{V}:=\mathcal{A}^{\perp}
is homotopically smashing. From the above discussion we can associate to it the poset homomorphism
\omega_{\mathcal{V}}:\Spec(R)\to\mathrm{Coaisle}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))^{\mathrm{op}}
. Define
\sigma\in\Hom_{\mathrm{Pos}}(\Spec(R),\Aisle(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)))
to be such that
\sigma(\mathfrak{p}):={}^{\perp}\omega_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{p})
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, and consider the compactly generated t-structure
(\mathcal{A}_{\sigma},\mathcal{V}_{\sigma})
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}=\psi(\sigma)
. By
3.14
,
\omega_{\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}}(\mathfrak{p})=\sigma(\mathfrak{p})^{\perp}=\omega_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{p})
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. Thus the homomorphism
\omega_{\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}}
is precisely
\omega_{\mathcal{V}}
, and so, by injectivity of
\omega
,
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{V})=(\mathcal{A}_{\sigma},\mathcal{V}_{\sigma})
.

(2) Let
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{V})
be a homotopically smashing t-structure. Following the proof of point (1), we conclude that
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{V})=(\mathcal{A}_{\sigma},\mathcal{V}_{\sigma})
for some homomorphism
\sigma
. Thus, it is compactly generated.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let

Let

for any

\displaystyle\to{\Hom_{A}(H^{1}(X),E_{0})}\xrightarrow{\Hom_{A}(H^{1}(X),d)}{\Hom_{A}(H^{1}(X),E_{1})}\to{\Hom_{\mathcal{D}(A)}(X,E)}\longrightarrow{}

Appendix A
t-structures of Dynkin algebras

This final section is devoted to the classification of the aisles of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q)
, for any field
\mathbb{K}
and Dynkin quiver
Q
. We show that the lattice
\Aisle(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{K}Q))
is isomorphic to the lattice
\mathrm{Filt}(\mathbf{Nc}(Q))
of filtrations (i.e. non increasing sequences) of noncrossing partitions of
Q
, and so that it is independent of the field. In particular, this result specifies the classification in
[
SR19
]
to algebras of finite representation type.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an hereditary abelian category and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
a suspended subcategory. Then, for any map
f\in\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(H^{n}(\mathcal{S}),H^{n+1}(\mathcal{S}))
, it holds that
\ker f\in H^{n}(\mathcal{S})
and
\coker f\in H^{n+1}(\mathcal{S})
. In particular,

\ldots\supseteq H^{n}(\mathcal{S})\supseteq H^{n+1}(\mathcal{S})\supseteq\ldots

is a filtration of subcategories closed under images, cokernels and extensions (ICE-closed).

Proof.

Let
f:A\to B
with
A\in H^{n}(\mathcal{S})
and
B\in H^{n+1}(\mathcal{S})
, then there is a triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B[-n-1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cone f[-n-1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A[-n]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces+}

where
\cone f[-n-1]\cong\ker f[-n]\oplus\coker f[-n-1]
. Since
\mathcal{S}
is closed under extensions and summands,
\cone f[-n-1]\in\mathcal{S}
and we get the statement. As for the last part, let
f:C\to D
in
\Hom(H^{n}(\mathcal{S}),H^{n}(\mathcal{S}))
. Since
C
lies also in
H^{n-1}(\mathcal{S})
, we have that
\coker f\in H^{n}(\mathcal{S})
and
\ker f\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{S})
, so
\im f\cong\coker(\ker f\hookrightarrow C)\in H^{n}(\mathcal{S})
. The closure under extensions of
H^{n}(\mathcal{S})
follows from the one in
\mathcal{S}
.
∎

Remark A.2
.

We want to note that some results from
[
IT09
]
hold also for ICE-closed subcategories (of artinian, Krull-Schmidt, hereditary abelian categories, e.g.
\mods(\mathbb{K}Q)
). We will call an ICE-closed subcategory
\mathcal{H}
finitely generated if it contains a finite set of indecomposable object
\mathcal{I}
, such that
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\gen(\mathcal{I})
, where
\gen
denotes the closure under quotients and finite direct sums. Note that it is always the case in
\mods(\mathbb{K}Q)
when

is Dynkin. In particular, the following holds:

•

[
IT09
, Theorem 2.8]
A finitely generated ICE-closed subcategory
\mathcal{H}
has a minimal generator, unique up to isomorphism, which is the direct sum of all its indecomposable split projectives;

•

[
IT09
, Proposition 2.12]
For any ICE-closed subcategory
\mathcal{H}
, the class

\alpha(\mathcal{H})=\left\{M\in\mathcal{H}\mid\text{for all }f\in\Hom(\mathcal{H},M),\,\ker f\in\mathcal{H}\right\}

is a wide subcategory. (See
[
Sak23
, Proposition 2.2]
for a proof);

•

[
IT09
, Theorem 2.15]
For any finitely generated ICE-closed subcategory
\mathcal{H}
, let

\alpha_{s}(\mathcal{H})=\left\{\begin{aligned} M\in\mathcal{H}\mid&\text{ for all surjections }f:Z\to M\text{ with}\\
&\ Z\in\mathcal{H}\text{ split projective},\,\ker f\in\mathcal{H}\end{aligned}\right\}

Then,
\alpha(\mathcal{H})=\alpha_{s}(\mathcal{H})
.

To make the proof work for
\mathcal{H}
note that, at the end of their proof,
\ker g^{\prime}
is not just a quotient of
\ker g^{\prime}h
but, since

is surjective, it is the image of

. Since both
\ker g^{\prime}h
and

are in
\mathcal{H}
, so does
\ker g^{\prime}
;

•

[
IT09
, Theorem 2.16]
For any finitely generated ICE-closed subcategory
\mathcal{H}
, any split projective

of

is in

\mathcal{H}\subseteq\gen(\bigoplus U_{i})\subseteq\gen(\alpha(\mathcal{H}))
.

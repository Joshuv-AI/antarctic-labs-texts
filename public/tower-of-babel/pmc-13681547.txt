Complication of hyperbaric oxygen therapy
Authors: Ke Liu, Lei Wang, Lili Liang, Jinyuan Wei
Published: 2026

Patient Presentation

A 27-year-old man with a history of a 10-hour coma due to carbon monoxide poisoning was transferred for hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT). His oxygen saturation was 99% while on mechanical ventilation. When he was removed from the chamber, vomiting and nosebleeds occurred, and within 1 minute his heart rate fell to 42 beats per minute and his oxygen saturation dropped to 69%. The patient then developed cyanosis of the head and neck, generalized subcutaneous emphysema (Fig 1), absent bilateral breath sounds, and abdominal distension. Cardiopulmonary resuscitation was initiated, and a 12-gauge needle was inserted into the left thoracic cavity. A subsequent whole-body computed tomography scan was arranged and revealed bilateral pneumothorax, pneumoperitoneum, pneumomediastinum, systemic arterial gas embolism, and extensive subcutaneous emphysema (Fig 2). Diagnosis

Pulmonary Barotrauma

Pulmonary barotrauma is a rare but potentially fatal complication of HBOT, including gas embolism, mediastinal emphysema, and tension pneumothorax. Airway obstruction during expiration may force air into the pulmonary interstitium or pleural cavity, resulting in pulmonary barotrauma.1 Predisposing factors for pulmonary barotrauma during HBOT include bullae or subpleural blebs, a prior history of spontaneous pneumothorax, blunt chest trauma, serious infection, lung scarring, air-trapping lesions in the lungs, and mechanical ventilation.2 The most important preventive measure for accidents due to local overinflation is to exclude individuals with risk factors for airway obstruction from hyperbaric chamber exposure.3

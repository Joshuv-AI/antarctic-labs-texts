On universal-homogeneous hyperbolic graphs and spaces and their isometry groups
Katrin Tent

Abstract
. The Urysohn space is the unique separable metric space that is universal and homogeneous for finite metric spaces, i.e., it embeds any finite metric space any isometry between finite subspaces extends to an isometry of the whole space. We here consider the existence of a universal-homogeneous hyperbolic space. We show that that for δ > 0 \delta>0 there is no δ \delta -hyperbolic space which is universal and homogeneous in the above sense for all finite δ \delta -hpyerbolic spaces. We then show that for any δ ≥ 0 \delta\geq 0 and any countable class 𝒞 \mathcal{C} of δ \delta -hyperbolic spaces with countably many distinguished δ \delta -closed subspaces there exists a δ \delta -hyperbolic metric space ℍ 𝒞 = ℍ ⁡ ( 𝒞 , δ ) \mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\delta) such that every X ∈ 𝒞 X\in\mathcal{C} can be embedded into ℍ 𝒞 \mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{C}} as a δ \delta -closed subspace and any isometry between distinguished closed subspaces extends to an isometry of ℍ 𝒞 \mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{C}} . If 𝒞 \mathcal{C} consists of δ \delta -hyperbolic geodesic spaces, then ℍ 𝒞 \mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{C}} contains the quasi-tree of spaces as defined in [ 1 ] . For 𝒞 δ \mathcal{C}_{\delta} the class of all finite δ \delta -hyperbolic spaces with rational distances or the class of finite δ \delta -hyperbolic graphs, the limit ℍ δ \mathbb{H}_{\delta} is a δ \delta -hyperbolic space (or graph, respectively) universal for all finite δ \delta -hyperbolic spaces with rational distances (or finite δ \delta -hyperbolic graphs) and such that any isometry between δ \delta -closed subspaces extends to an isometry of ℍ δ \mathbb{H}_{\delta} . We show that the isometry group of ℍ δ \mathbb{H}_{\delta} does not contain elements of bounded displacement and has no dense conjugacy class.



1.
Introduction and non-existence

The Urysohn space is the unique separable metric space that is universal and homogeneous for finite metric spaces, i.e., it embeds any finite metric space and any isometry between finite subspaces extends to an isometry of the whole space. The Urysohn space can easily be constructed by amalgamating all finite metric spaces with rational distances and taking the completion of the resulting metric space, see e.g.
[
10
]
. As is often the case with very homogeneous structures, the isometry group of the Urysohn space has a natural maximal normal subgroup, namely the group of isometries of bounded displacement and the quotient group is boundedly simple. In this note we consider the question whether there is an analog for hyperbolic metric spaces.
This note is motivated by the non-existence result in Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Generalized Fraïssé limits

The previous result shows that we cannot construct a universal-homogeneous hyperbolic space.
In order to obtain a universal

-hyperbolic metric space with a weaker homogeneity condition, we modify the amalgamation by restricting to

(see e.g.

Strong embeddings of
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces

We define a property of subsets of metric spaces inspired by buildings:

Definition 3.1
.

Let

be a metric space. We call a subset

Remark 3.2

Note that if

Example 3.3

Clearly, any convex set in a tree is gated. On the other hand,

If

is

\displaystyle=d(g_{1},g_{2})+d(g_{3},g_{4})

\displaystyle=d(g_{1},g_{3})+d(g_{2},g_{4})

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=d(g_{1},g_{4})+d(g_{2},g_{3}).

Then

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=d(x_{1},x_{2})+d(x_{3},x_{4})\leq

\displaystyle E_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}d(x_{i},g_{i})

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=d(x_{1},x_{3})+d(x_{2},x_{4})=

\displaystyle F_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}d(x_{i},g_{i})

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=d(x_{1},x_{4})+d(x_{2},x_{3})=

\displaystyle G_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}d(x_{i},g_{i}).

If
d(g_{3},g_{4}),d(g_{1},g_{2})>\delta
, then
E=E_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}d(x_{i},g_{i})
. Cancelling
\sum_{i=1}^{4}d(x_{i},g_{i})
in each of
E,F,G
we obtain the hyperbolicity condition for the points
g_{1},\ldots,g_{4}\in~A
. Since
A
is hyperbolic, the result follows.

Now assume (by symmetry)
d(g_{3},g_{4})\leq\delta
. Then by the triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle|d(g_{1},g_{3})-d(g_{1},g_{4})|\leq

\displaystyle\delta\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle|d(g_{2},g_{4})-d(g_{2},g_{3})|\leq

\displaystyle\delta.

Hence

(1)

|F_{1}-G_{1}|=|F-G|\leq 2\delta.

If
E\leq F\leq G
or
F\leq E\leq G
, the claim follows from (
1
).

So suppose
F\leq G<E
. Since

d(g_{1},g_{2})\leq d(g_{1},g_{4})+d(g_{2},g_{3})+\delta=G_{1}+\delta

we have
E_{1}\leq G_{1}+2\delta
. Hence

G<E\leq E_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}d(x_{i},g_{i})\leq G_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}d(x_{i},g_{i})+2\delta=G+2\delta

and so
E-G\leq 2\delta
as required.

∎

We now specialize marked classes to
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces:

Definition 3.13
.

A
marked class
of
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces is
a countable class
\mathcal{C}
of
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces such that all distinguished subspaces of

are
\delta
-closed in

and
A
contains at least one distinguished subspace consisting of a single point.

Thus, by definition distinguished subspaces are
\delta
-closed, but in general not all
\delta
-closed subspaces are distinguished in a marked class of
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces.

Theorem 3.14
.

Fix
\delta
and let
\mathcal{C}_{0}
be a marked class of
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces.
Then there is a marked Fraïssé class
\mathcal{C}
of
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces containing
\mathcal{C}_{0}
. Thus,
\mathcal{C}
has a unique marked Fraïssé limit which is universal and homogeneous for
distinguished subspaces and any isometry between distinguished subspaces of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{C}}
extends to an isometry of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

Since any
A\in\mathcal{C}
contains a distinguished substructure consisting of a point, we obtain (JEP) by identifying distinguished points and amalgamating over this point. Hence it suffices to verify (AP).

We build the class
\mathcal{C}
inductively from
\mathcal{C}_{0}
as follows: suppose at stage
i
we have constructed a marked class
\mathcal{C}_{i}
of
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces with countably many distinguished
\delta
-closed subspaces. Then we define

C_{i+1}=\mathcal{C}_{i}\cup\{B_{1}\otimes_{A}B_{2}\colon A\leq B_{1},B_{2}\in C_{i},A\mathrm{\ distinguished\ in\ }B_{1},B_{2}\}.

Furthermore, for any
D=B_{1}\otimes_{A}B_{2}
with
A\leq B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
we define the distinguished subspaces as the
\delta
-closed subspaces
X
of

such that
X\cap B_{1}
and
X\cap B_{2}
are distinguished in
B_{1},B_{2}
, respectively.
Then
\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}_{i}
has (AP) and (JEP).
Hence the marked Fraïssé limit exists and is as desired.
∎

The previous theorem implies the existence of a universal and
\leq
-homogeneous
\delta
-hyperbolic space

for the class of finite

-hyperbolic spaces with rational distances. (In fact, this class is a Hrushvoski class with respect to the strong embeddings defined above.) More generally, we may allow distance taking values in any countable subsemigroup

Corollary 3.15
.

Fix

be the class of finite
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces with rational distances. Since

Proof.

By Lemma

has (AP) and (JEP). Since every finite

-closed subset of

∎

The same reasoning also applies to the class of finite
\delta
-hyperbolic graphs with the graph metric considered as a marked class with all finite

Corollary 3.16

For any

-hyperbolic graphs.

Note that

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathrm{(ii)}

4.
Projection subspaces of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{C}}

In
[
1
]
the authors show how to assemble a class
\bf{Y}
of geodesic spaces to a quasi-tree. The purpose of this section is to show that the quasi-tree of spaces they construct can be retrieved in the corresponding universal space
\mathbb{H}_{\mathcal{C}}
, at least if the class
\bf{Y}
is countable.

We write

d(X,Y)=\inf\{d(x,y)\colon x\in X,y\in Y\}.

For
\delta
-closed subsets, this infinimum is attained:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A,B
be
\delta
-closed and disjoint subsets of a metric space
X
and let
G_{A}(B)=\{g_{A}(b)\colon b\in B\}
and
G_{B}(A)=\{g_{B}(a)\colon a\in A\}
.
Then

\mathrm{diam}(G_{A}(B)),\mathrm{diam}(G_{A}(B))\leq\delta.

Furthermore, for all
a\in G_{A}(B),b\in G_{B}(A)
we have

d(a,g_{B}(a))=d(b,g_{A}(b))=d(A,B)

and there is an isometry

g:G_{A}(B)\longrightarrow G_{B}(A),\quad a\mapsto g_{B}(a).

Proof.

We first claim that
G_{A}(B)=\{g_{A}(b)\colon b\in G_{B}(A)\}
and
G_{B}(A)=\{g_{B}(a)\colon a\in G_{A}(B)\}
: let
b\in B
and
a=g_{A}(b)
.
Then
(a,g_{B}(a),b)
is geodetic and
a=g_{A}(g_{B}(a))
.

Now let
a,a^{\prime}\in G_{A}(B)
, say
a=g_{A}(b),a^{\prime}=g_{A}(b^{\prime}),b,b^{\prime}\in G_{B}(A)
. Then
b=g_{B}(a),b^{\prime}=g_{B}(a^{\prime})
.

If
d(a,a^{\prime})>\delta
, we have
d(b,b^{\prime})=d(b,a)+d(a,a^{\prime})+d(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
since
A
and
B
are
\delta
-closed. Hence
d(b,b^{\prime})>\delta
and thus also
d(a,a^{\prime})=d(a,b)+d(b,b^{\prime})+d(b^{\prime},a^{\prime})
, a contradiction.

Now suppose
d(a,b)<d(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
. By symmetry we may assume
d(a,a^{\prime})\leq d(b,b^{\prime})
. Then since
b^{\prime}=g_{B}(a^{\prime})
we have
d(a^{\prime},b)=d(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})+d(b^{\prime},b)>d(a^{\prime},a)+d(a,b)
, a contradiction. Hence,
d(a,b)=d(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
is constant and equal to
d(A,B)
.

Finally suppose
d(a,a^{\prime})<d(b,b^{\prime})
. Then as before

d(b,a^{\prime})=d(b,a)+d(a,a^{\prime})=c+d(a,a^{\prime})<d+d(b,b^{\prime})=d(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})+d(b,b^{\prime})=d(a^{\prime},b),

a contradiction. Thus, the gate map is an isometry from
G_{A}(B)
to
G_{B}(A)
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that if
A,B
are closed and disjoint, then the gate sets
G_{A}(B)
and
G_{B}(A)
need not be
\delta
-closed: let
A=\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\},B=\{y_{0},y_{2}\}
with
d(x_{0},y_{0})=d(x_{2},y_{2})=d(x_{0},x_{2})=d(y_{0},y_{2})=\delta
and
d(x_{1},x_{0})=1/3\delta,d(x_{1},x_{2})=2/3\delta
. Then
A,B\leq A\cup B
, but
G_{A}(B)=\{x_{0},x_{2}\}
is not
\delta
-closed.

We now introduce the projection metric on
\delta
-closed subsets from
[
1
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

[see
[
1
]
, 4.1]
Let
X,Y,Z
be pairwise disjoint gated subsets of a metric space. We put

d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)=\mathrm{diam}(G_{Y}(X)\cup G_{Y}(Z)).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X,Y,Z
be pairwise disjoint
\delta
-closed subsets of a metric space and suppose
d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)>\delta
. Then

d(X,Z)>d(X,Y)+d(Y,Z)+\delta.

Proof.

Since by Lemma
4.1
each set
G_{Y}(X)
has diameter
\leq\delta
, there exist
x\in X,z\in Z
such that
d(g_{Y}(x),g_{Y}(z))>\delta
. Since
Y
is
\delta
-closed, this implies that the sequence
(x,g_{Y}(x),g_{Y}(z),z)
is geodetic and so
x\in X
and
g_{Y}(z)\in Y
have the same gate in
Z
. Thus the claim follows from Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\bf{C}
be a class of pairwise disjoint
\delta
-closed subsets of a metric space,
\delta>0
, and
X,Y,Z,W\in\bf{C}
. Then the projection complex axioms of
[
1
]
hold, i.e. we have the following:

(PC 1)

d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)=d^{\pi}_{Y}(Z,X)
;

(PC 2)

d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)+d^{\pi}_{Y}(Z,W)\geq d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,W)
;

(PC 3)

\min\{d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z),d^{\pi}_{Z}(X,Y)\}\leq\delta
;

(PC 4)

for all
X,Z\in\bf{C}
the number of
Y\in\bf{C}
such that
d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)>\delta
is finite.

Proof.

Clearly (PC 1) holds and (PC 2) follows directly from the triangle inequality in metric spaces.

For (PC 3) suppose
X,Y,Z
are pairwise disjoint and
\delta
-closed sets with both

d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)>\delta\mbox{\ and\ }d^{\pi}_{Z}(X,Y)>\delta.

Then by Lemma
4.4
we have

d(X,Z)>d(X,Y)+d(Y,Z)+\delta\mbox{\ and \ }d(X,Y)>d(X,Z)+d(Z,Y)+\delta,

which is impossible.

For (PC 4) suppose that
X,Y_{1},Y_{2},Z
are pairwise disjoint
\delta
-closed sets with both
d^{\pi}_{Y_{1}}(X,Z),d^{\pi}_{Y_{2}}(X,Z)>\delta
.
Since all gate sets have diameter at most
\delta
, we cannot have both

d^{\pi}_{Y_{1}}(X,Y_{2})\leq\delta
and
d^{\pi}_{Y_{1}}(Z,Y_{2})\leq\delta
. Now if
d^{\pi}_{Y_{1}}(X,Y_{2})>\delta
, then by Lemma
4.4
we have

d(X,Y_{2})>d(X,Y_{1})+d(Y_{1},Y_{2})+\delta\mbox{ and }d(X,Z)>d(X,Y_{2})+d(Y_{2},Z)+\delta.

Thus,
d(X,Z)>d(X,Y_{1})+d(Y_{1},Y_{2})+d(Y_{2},Z)+2\delta
.
Hence the number of
Y\in\bf{C}
such that
d^{\pi}_{Y}(X,Z)>\delta
is bounded by
d(X,Z)/\delta
.
∎

6.
The isometry group of
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}

We now turn our attention to
\mathbb{H}_{d}
, the unique
\delta
-hyperbolic space universal and
\leq_{\delta}
-homogeneous for finite
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces with distances in
\mathbb{Q}

Its isometry group

is a Polish group, where a neighbourhood basis of the identy is given by point stabilizers of finite
\delta
-closed subsets of
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}

\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
is
\delta
-closed in
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
, it follows that

acts transitively on
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}

In the case of the Urysohn space
\mathbb{U}
it is easy to see that the isometry group acts primitively: suppose there is an invariant equivalence relation
E
with
E(x,y)
for some

and let

(x_{0}=x,x_{1}=y,\ldots,x_{n}=z)
for some

such that
d(x_{i},x_{i-1})=d(x_{1},x_{0}),i=1,\ldots n
. By homogeneity and invariance of

we see that
E(x,z)
showing that the equivalence class is all of
\mathbb{U}
.

It is also easy to see that for any regular tree
T
the isometry group respects a bipartition of the vertices and acts primitively on each class. Thus, a 2-coloring is the only invariant equivalence relation on
T
.
In contrast to this, we will now show that
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
has non-trivial invariant equivalence relations, in fact uncountably many:

To show this, we introduce the following auxilliary graph structure on
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
. Let
R\subset\mathbb{Q}_{>\delta}
. We define edges
E_{R}
on
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
in the following way: for
x,y\in\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
we put
E_{R}(x,y)
if and only if
d(x,y)\in R
and
\{x,y\}\leq\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
.
Note that this edge relation is invariant under the isometry group of
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
.

2

2

The definition of the graph structure on
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
uses only the metric. This is not an extension of a first-order language.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
R\subset\mathbb{Q}_{>\delta}
, the graph
(\mathbb{H}_{\delta},E_{R})
is a forest, i.e. the connected components of this graph are regular trees of infinite valencies and any connnected subgraph is
\delta
-closed in the sense of the metric on
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
.

Proof.

The fact that every vertex in this graph has infinite valency follows directly from the universality of
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
: clearly, the set
X=\{x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
with
d(x,y_{i})\in R
and
(y_{i},x,y_{j})
geodetic for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
can be strongly embedded into
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\{x,y_{i}\}\leq X\leq\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
for any
i\leq n
and hence
E_{R}(x,y_{i})
holds. Since the isometry group acts transitively on the points of
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
, it follows that every point has infinite valency in this graph.

By Lemma
3.10
it follows inductively, that any finite connected subgraph of
(\mathbb{H}_{\delta},E_{R})
that does not contain a cycle is
\delta
-closed in
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
: clearly this holds for any edge of the graph. So suppose inductively that
A
is a finite subgraph without cycles, let
x\in A
and extend
A
by an edge
E_{R}(x,y)
. Then the conditions of Lemma
3.10
are satisfied, and hence
A\cup\{y\}\leq\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
. Furthermore, if
A
is a finite connected subgraph of
(\mathbb{H}_{\delta},E_{r})
without cycles, then for
x,y\in A
the unique path in
A
from
x
to
y
is geodetic, i.e. the distance between points in
A
in the metric of
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
is the graph distance in
A
where an edge
E_{R}(x,y)
has length
r
if
d(x,y)=r\in R
.

Now suppose that
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}=x_{0})
is a
\delta
-closed cycle in this graph with
n~>~2
. Then the subpath
X_{0}=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{n-1}\}
is geodetic and strong in
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
by the previous paragraph. Thus, by Remark
3.4
,
g_{X_{0}}(x_{0})\in\{x_{n-1},x_{1}\}
. However, if
x_{1}=g_{X_{0}}(x_{0}))
, then
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1})
is geodetic. By Remark
3.4
, the set
\{x_{0},x_{n-1}\}
is not
\delta
-closed in
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
and so we cannot have
E_{R}(x_{n-1},x_{0})
. The symmetric argument applies if
x_{n-1}=g_{X_{0}}(x_{0}))
. Thus we cannot have both
E_{R}(x_{0},x_{1})
and
E_{R}(x_{n-1},x_{0})
, so there are no cycles.

To complete the proof, suppose that
X
is a connected subgraph. Then any finite connected subgraph of
X
is
\delta
-closed in
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
. Suppose
X
is not
\delta
-closed. First assume that
X
is not gated. Then there exist
a\in\mathbb{H}_{\delta}\setminus X
for which there is no gate in
X
. Let
x\in X
be such that
d(a,x)
is minimal. Then
x
is unique: if
y\in X
with
d(a,x)=d(a,y)
, then the convex closure of
\{x,y\}
in
X
is finite and not gated, contradicting the previous paragraph. Furthermore for any
y\in X
we have
d(a,y)=d(a,x)+d(x,y)
as this holds in the convex closure of
\{x,y\}
in
X
.
Now let
a,b\in\mathbb{H}_{\delta}\setminus X
. Then
d(g_{X}(a),g_{X}(b))>\delta
and since the convex closure of
\{g_{X}(a),g_{X}(b)\}
is
\delta
-closed, it follows that
d(a,b)
is as required.

∎

Proposition 6.2
.

\mathop{\rm Isom}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}_{\delta})
acts transitively, but not primitively on
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
. In fact, there exist uncountably many non-trivial equivalence relations on
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
invariant under
\mathop{\rm Isom}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}_{\delta})
.

Proof.

Transitivity of the action
follows directly from the
\leq
-homogeneity and was already noted above.

To see that the action is not primitive, let
R\subset\Gamma_{>\delta}
and define the equivalence relation
T_{R}
on
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
where
T_{R}(x,y)
if and only if
x
and
y
lie in the same
E_{R}
-connected component. Clearly this is invariant under the action of
\mathop{\rm Isom}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}_{\delta})
. Since there are uncountably many subsets
R
, the claim follows.
∎

Definition 6.3
.

For
R\subset\Gamma
we call the
E_{R}
connected components of
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
a
T_{R}
-tree.

Remark 6.4
.

Note that each
T_{R}
-tree
X
is geodetically embedded, i.e. if
(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a path in a
T_{R}
-tree, then
(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is geodetic. Since every finite convex subgraph of
X
is
\delta
-closed in
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
by the proof of Lemma
6.1
, it follows in particular, that
X
is
\delta
-closed in
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
.

Hence we have:

Corollary 6.5
.

No nontrivial element of
\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}_{\delta})
has bounded displacement.

Proof.

If
g\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}_{\delta})
has bounded displacement, then so does every commutator
[g,h]
for
h\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}_{\delta})
. By taking an appropriate commutator we may assume that
g
fixes a point
x
and acts nontrivially on the
T_{R}
-tree containing
x
. But no nontrivial isometry of
T_{R}
has bounded displacement, proving the corollary.
∎

Corollary 6.6
.

Suppose
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}_{\delta})
acts as a translation on some
T_{R}
-tree
X
for some
R
. Then
\alpha
has no fixed point. In particular, any
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}_{\delta})
for which there exists
x\in\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
such that
\{x,\alpha(x)\}
is
\delta
-closed and
d(x,\alpha(x))>\delta
is fixed point free.

Proof.

Note that
X
is
\delta
-closed in
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
. Let
c\in\mathbb{H}_{\delta}\setminus X
and
g=g_{X}(c)
. Then
d(c,g)=d(\alpha(c),\alpha(g))<d(c,\alpha(g))
by Remark
3.2
and hence
c\neq\alpha(c)
.

If
\{x,\alpha(x)\}
is
\delta
-closed and
d=d(x,\alpha(x))>\delta
, then
\alpha
leaves the
T_{R}
-tree
X
containing
x
for

invariant and acts on

∎

Proposition 6.7
.

G=\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}_{\delta})
has no dense conjugacy class.
For any finite set
B
the pointwise stabilizer
G_{\mathrm{cl}(B)}
in
G
has a dense conjugacy class.

Proof.

We use the criterion from
[
10
]
, Lemma 2.8:
G
has a dense conjugacy class if and only if for any finite tuples
x,y,a,b
such that
\mathrm{cl}(x)\cong\mathrm{cl}(y)
and
\mathrm{cl}(a)\cong\mathrm{cl}(b)
there are tuples
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{cl}(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{cl}(xy)
and
\mathrm{cl}(x^{\prime}a)\cong\mathrm{cl}(y^{\prime}b)
.

To see that this critrion is violated, let
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})
be a path in a
T_{2\delta}
-tree and put
x=\mathrm{cl}(x)=(x_{0},x_{1}),y=\mathrm{cl}(y)=(x_{1},x_{2})
and let
a=b\in\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
be an arbitrary point.
Suppose there are tuples
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{cl}(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{cl}(xy)
and
\mathrm{cl}(x^{\prime}a)\cong\mathrm{cl}(y^{\prime}a)
. Then
x^{\prime}=(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{1}^{\prime}),y^{\prime}=(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})
for a path

(x_{0}^{\prime},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})
in some
T_{2\delta}
-tree and by
\leq_{\delta}
-homogeneity there is some
g\in G
such that
g(x^{\prime}a)=(y^{\prime}a)
. Thus,
g
acts as a translation on the
T_{2}\delta
-connected component containing
x_{1}^{\prime}
and hence
g
has no fixed point by Corollary
6.6
, a contradiction.

This shows that
G
does not have a dense conjugacy class. The fact that
G_{\mathrm{cl}(B)}
has a dense conjugacy class for any finite subset

follows from
[
10
]
, Lemma 2.8 using the fact that
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
has a local stationary independence relation.

∎

7.
Final remarks and open questions

In the previous sections we tried to point out similarities and dissimilarities between the universal
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces constructed here and the Urysohn space on the one hand and regular trees on the other hand.

In light of the fact that the isometry group of a regular tree and the isometry group of the Urysohn space modulo the normal subgroup of bounded displacement are simple groups, it is natural to ask:

•

Is the isometry group of
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
topologically simple, or even abstractly simple?

Since
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
has no isometries of bounded displacement, it seems likely that the answer is ‘yes’. However, the methods from
[
10
]
do not apply here directly since
\mathop{\rm Isom}\nolimits(\mathbb{H}_{\delta})
has no dense conjugacy class. In the case of trees and more generally right-angled buildings, the automorphism groups also miss a dense conjugacy class. However, the methods from
[
10
]
can be applied to stabilizers of maximal flags because these stabilizers have dense conjugacy classes, and the same was shown above for
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
.

For right-angled buildings one can then deduce abstract simplicity of the automorphism group using the fact that stabilizers of partial flags form a sequence of subgroups maximal in each other. However, as pointed out above, the isometry group of
\mathbb{H}_{\delta}
does not act primitively. Hence point stabilizers are not maximal in this setting and it is harder to approach the simplicity problem.

Other natural questions address the model theoretic properties of these hyperbolic spaces:

•

Is there a natural class of
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces
\mathcal{C}
,
\delta>0
, such that the limit structure is stable?

For
0
-hyperbolic spaces (and right-angled buildings) it was shown in
[
12
]
that the theory is
\omega
-stable. This can be shown using the notion of independence defined above and verifying that it satisfies the necessary conditions for stability.
In general, one cannot expect arbitrary
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces to be stable. It would be interesting to find a natural class of hyperbolic spaces with stable theory.

Note that an infinite tree of bounded height is
\omega
-categorical. Hence the following questions are natural given the existence of a bounded Urysohn space and
0
-hyperbolic spaces of bounded diameter:

•

Is there a
\delta
-hyperbolic analog of a bounded Urysohn space for
\delta>0
?

•

Is there a natural class of
\delta
-hyperbolic spaces
\mathcal{C}
, for
\delta>0
, such that the limit structure is
\omega
-categorical?

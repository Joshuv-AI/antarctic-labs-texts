Manin triples, bialgebras and Yang-Baxter equation of A 3 A_{3} -associative algebras
Yaxi Jiang, Chuangchuang Kang, Jiafeng Lü

Abstract
. A 3 A_{3} -associative algebra is a generalization of associative algebra and is one of the four remarkable types of Lie-admissible algebras, along with associative algebra, left-symmetric algebra and right-symmetric algebra. This paper develops bialgebra theory for A 3 A_{3} -associative algebras. We introduce Manin triples and bialgebras for A 3 A_{3} -associative algebras, prove their equivalence using matched pairs of A 3 A_{3} -associative algebras, and define the A 3 A_{3} -associative Yang-Baxter equation and triangular A 3 A_{3} -associative bialgebras. Additionally, we introduce relative Rota-Baxter operators to provide skew-symmetric solutions of the A 3 A_{3} -associative Yang-Baxter equation.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
A_{3}
-associative algebras and Lie-admissible algebras

The notion of an
A_{3}
-associative algebra was first given by Goze and Remm in
[
22
]
. It generalizes associative algebra and is one of the four remarkable types of Lie-admissible algebras, along with associative algebra, left-symmetric algebra and right-symmetric algebra
[
22
]
. Lie-admissible algebras play a significant role in exploring the symmetries of certain particles and have various applications in physics and mathematics, we refer to
[
4
,
5
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
26
,
37
]
for more interesting research on Lie-admissible algebras.

An
A_{3}
-associative algebra is defined as an algebra
(A,\cdot)
that satisfies the
A_{3}
-associative law:

(x\cdot y)\cdot z+(y\cdot z)\cdot x+(z\cdot x)\cdot y=x\cdot(y\cdot z)+y\cdot(z\cdot x)+z\cdot(x\cdot y),\quad\forall~x,y,z\in A.

This law can also be expressed using associators as:

\sum_{\sigma\in A_{3}}(a_{\sigma(1)},a_{\sigma(2)},a_{\sigma(3)})=0,

where
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in A
, and
A_{3}
denotes the alternating group of degree
3
.
The
A_{3}
-associative law is called the
G_{5}
-associative law in
[
22
]
.
The symmetric form above provides the basis for the terminology “
A_{3}
” and has been instrumental in constructing
A_{3}
-dendriform algebra
[
39
]
.
Associative algebras are a special case of
A_{3}
-associative algebras. By defining a skew-symmetric bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]:A\times A\rightarrow A
as
[x,y]=x\cdot y-y\cdot x
, where
\cdot
satisfies the
A_{3}
-associative law, the structure
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
becomes a Lie algebra.
Furthermore, admissible Poisson algebras, which belong to the class of
A_{3}
-associative algebras
[
23
]
, have been studied by many scholars from different aspects
[
32
,
40
]
.

1.2.
The bialgebra theory for Lie-admissible algebras

The Lie bialgebra consists of a Lie algebra and a Lie coalgebra satisfying the 1-cocycle condition, which plays an important role in the field of mathematical physics
[
15
,
19
]
. For example, Lie bialgebras provide a valuable method for constructing solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation
[
18
]
. In recent years, many scholars have also explored other types of bialgebra structures, such as Novikov-Poisson bialgebras
[
30
]
, Rota-Baxter bialgebras
[
10
]
, anti-pre Lie bialgebras
[
33
]
, Mock-Lie bialgebras
[
12
]
, Leibniz bialgebras
[
41
,
44
]
and Poisson bialgebras
[
38
]
.

Regarding the bialgebra theory for Lie-admissible algebras, bialgebra theories have been developed for specific examples of Lie-admissible algebras, including left-symmetric (or right-symmetric) bialgebras
[
7
]
and associative bialgebras
[
27
]
.
A_{3}
-associative algebras are a special class of Lie-admissible algebras,
which raises the natural question: do
A_{3}
-associative algebras possess corresponding bialgebras?

By drawing an analogy with Lie bialgebras, the author studied the bialgebras of left-symmetric algebras, which are equivalent to the matched pairs and symplectic doubles
[
7
]
. Building on this foundational work, further studies have emerged, such as Hom-left-symmetric bialgebras
[
43
]
, left-symmetric conformal bialgebras
[
25
]
and Hom-left-symmetric conformal bialgebras
[
24
]
.

In
[
27
]
, the authors introduced the concept of bialgebras of associative algebras, also referred to as infinitesimal bialgebras. These structures consist of an algebra and a coalgebra where the comultiplication satisfies the derivation property. Based on this, the authors studied infinitesimal Hopf algebras
[
1
]
, infinitesimal Hom-bialgebras
[
35
]
and infinitesimal BiHom-bialgebras
[
36
]
. In
[
3
]
, the author established the connection between infinitesimal bialgebras and Lie bialgebras, and introduced balanced infinitesimal bialgebras, which are both commutative and cocommutative. In the opposite algebra sense of balanced infinitesimal bialgebras, Bai studied antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras, which are equivalent to the double constructions of Frobenius algebras and matched pairs
[
8
]
.

1.3.
The classical Yang-Baxter equation and relative Rota-Baxter operators

The skew-symmetric solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation yield a class of Lie bialgebras
[
18
]
, with applications in inverse scattering theory
[
20
]
, classical integrable systems
[
9
]
and quantum groups
[
19
,
15
]
.
The operator form of this equation corresponds to a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight 0 (also known as an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
[
6
]
). Relative Rota-Baxter operators generalize Rota-Baxter operators and have diverse applications, including Hom-Lie triple systems
[
31
]
, Lie-Yamaguti algebras
[
42
]
and relative Poisson algebras
[
34
]
. This paper aims to study the
A_{3}
-associative analogues of these constructions, focuses on the
A_{3}
-associative Yang-Baxter equation and its corresponding relative Rota-Baxter operators.

Since
A_{3}
-associative algebra generalizes associative algebra, its bialgebra structure should generalize the bialgebra of associative algebra.
For infinitesimal bialgebras, Aguiar introduced the associative Yang-Baxter equation and proved that their solutions yield quasitriangular infinitesimal bialgebras
[
1
]
.
Aguiar also showed that a solution of the associative Yang-Baxter equation corresponds to a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0

[
2
]
.
Additionally, the skew-symmetric solution of the associative Yang-Baxter equation corresponds to the skew-symmetric part of the relative Rota-Baxter operator, which provides the double construction of Frobenius algebras
[
8
]
.

1.4.
Outline of the paper

In this paper, we introduce the notions of Manin triples and bialgebras for
A_{3}
-associative algebras. We prove the equivalence between Manin triples and bialgebras using matched pairs of
A_{3}
-associative algebras. Additionally, we define the
A_{3}
-associative Yang-Baxter equation and triangular
A_{3}
-associative bialgebras, and introduce Rota-Baxter operators to provide skew-symmetric solutions of the
A_{3}
-associative Yang-Baxter equation.
The paper is organized as follows.

In Section 2, we present essential preliminaries. Specifically, we give the representation of
A_{3}
-associative algebra. However, the triple
(r^{*},l^{*},A)
is also a representation, an additional condition is required. In such cases, we refer to this type of representation as admissible. Then, we define an admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebra. Furthermore, we introduce the concepts of matched pairs of
A_{3}
-associative algebras and quadratic
A_{3}
-associative algebra.

In Section 3, we introduce the concept of a Manin triple of
A_{3}
-associative algebras, which defines a quadratic
A_{3}
-associative algebra
(A\oplus A^{\prime},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}})
. We also introduce the notion of an
A_{3}
-associative bialgebra, which is showed to be equivalent to a Manin triple of
A_{3}
-associative algebras via a specific matched pair of
A_{3}
-associative algebras.

In Section 4, we study triangular
A_{3}
-associative bialgebras constructed from a skew-symmetric solution of the
A_{3}
-associative Yang-Baxter equation. We introduce relative Rota-Baxter operators to describe the operator forms of these solutions.

Throughout this paper, unless otherwise specified, all the vector spaces and algebras are finite-dimensional over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero,

is the complex field.
Let

\langle\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{k},v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\rangle=\langle\xi_{1},v_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle\xi_{k},v_{k}\rangle,\quad\forall~\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{k}\in V^{*},v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\in V,

Definition 2.1

(

An

(x\cdot y)\cdot z-x\cdot(y\cdot z)=(x\cdot z)\cdot y-x\cdot(z\cdot y),\quad\forall~x,y,z\in A.

Proposition 2.27
.

Proof.

) with

) and add (

), we have

Proposition 2.30
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
and

be

-associative algebras and

,

\displaystyle(r_{B}-l_{B})(a)(x\cdot y)=x\cdot\big(r_{B}(a)y\big)-\big(r_{B}(a)y\big)\cdot x+y\cdot\big(l_{B}(a)x\big)

(2.19)

\displaystyle\quad-\big(l_{B}(a)x\big)\cdot y+(r_{B}-l_{B})\big(l_{A}(y)a\big)x+(r_{B}-l_{B})\big(r_{A}(x)a\big)y,

\displaystyle+r_{B}(c)\big(x\cdot y+l_{B}(a)y+r_{B}(b)x\big)+\big(a\circ b+l_{A}(x)b+r_{A}(y)a\big)\circ c

\displaystyle+l_{A}\big(x\cdot y+l_{B}(a)y+r_{B}(b)x\big)c+r_{A}(z)(a\circ b+l_{A}(x)b+r_{A}(y)a),

\displaystyle\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{x,y,z}\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{a,b,c}(x+a)\ast\big((y+b)\ast(z+c)\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:mp-ds}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{x,y,z}\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{a,b,c}(x+a)\ast\big(y\cdot z+l_{B}(b)z+r_{B}(c)y+b\circ c+l_{A}(y)c+r_{A}(z)b\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:mp-ds}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{x,y,z}\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{a,b,c}x\cdot\big(y\cdot z+l_{B}(b)z+r_{B}(c)y\big)+l_{B}(a)\big(y\cdot z+l_{B}(b)z+r_{B}(c)y\big)

\displaystyle+r_{B}\big(b\circ c+l_{A}(y)c+r_{A}(z)b\big)x+a\circ\big(b\circ c+l_{A}(y)c+r_{A}(z)b\big)

\displaystyle+l_{A}(x)\big(b\circ c+l_{A}(y)c+r_{A}(z)b\big)+r_{A}\big(y\cdot z+l_{B}(b)z+r_{B}(c)y\big)a,

where
\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{x,y,z}
denotes the cyclic sum of

\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{x,y,z}\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{a,b,c}\big((x+a)\ast(y+b)\big)\ast(z+c)=\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{x,y,z}\mathop{\circlearrowleft}\limits_{a,b,c}(x+a)\ast\big((y+b)\ast(z+c)\big)

holds if and only if

is a representation of
(A,\cdot)
,
(l_{B},r_{B},A)
is a representation of
(B,\circ)
, (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) hold.
Therefore, the conclusion holds.
∎

Proposition 2.32
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebra with an adjoint representation
(L,R,A)
.
Then the semi-direct product
A_{3}
-associative algebra
A\ltimes_{L,R}A
is admissible.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z,d,e,f\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\big((x+d)\ast(z+f)\big)\ast(y+e)-(x+d)\ast\big((z+f)\ast(y+e)\big)

\displaystyle-(y+e)\ast\big((z+f)\ast(x+d)\big)+\big((y+e)\ast(z+f)\big)\ast(x+d)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:semi-dp}}{=}

\displaystyle\big(x\cdot z+L(x)f+R(z)d\big)\ast(y+e)-(x+d)\ast\big(z\cdot y+L(z)e+R(y)f\big)

\displaystyle-(y+e)\ast\big(z\cdot x+L(z)d+R(x)f\big)+\big(y\cdot z+L(y)f+R(z)e\big)\ast(x+d)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:semi-dp}}{=}

\displaystyle(x\cdot z)\cdot y+L(x\cdot z)e+R(y)\big(L(x)f+R(z)d\big)-x\cdot(z\cdot y)-L(x)\big(L(z)e+R(y)f\big)

\displaystyle-R(z\cdot y)d-y\cdot(z\cdot x)-L(y)\big(L(z)d+R(x)f\big)-R(z\cdot x)e+(y\cdot z)\cdot x+L(y\cdot z)d

\displaystyle+R(x)\big(L(y)f+R(z)e\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-repre}\eqref{eq:admissible}}{=}

\displaystyle L(x\cdot z)e+R(y)R(z)d-L(x)L(z)e-R(z\cdot y)d-L(y)L(z)d-R(z\cdot x)e+L(y\cdot z)d

\displaystyle+R(x)R(z)e

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\cdot z)\cdot e+(d\cdot z)\cdot y-x\cdot(z\cdot e)-d\cdot(z\cdot y)-y\cdot(z\cdot d)-e\cdot(z\cdot x)+(y\cdot z)\cdot d

\displaystyle+(e\cdot z)\cdot x

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:admissible}}{=}

\displaystyle 0.

Thus, the semi-direct product
A_{3}
-associative algebra

is admissible.
∎

2.2.
Quadratic

Definition 2.33
.

A

A_{3}
-associative algebra
is a triple
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{B})
, where
(A,\cdot)
is an
A_{3}
-associative algebra,
\mathcal{B}:A\times A\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on
A
which is invariant, that is, satisfying

(2.22)

\mathcal{B}(x\cdot y,z)=\mathcal{B}(x,y\cdot z),\quad\forall~x,y,z\in A.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebra,
(L,R,A)
be an adjoint representation of
(A,\cdot)
,
L^{*},R^{*}:A\rightarrow End(A^{*})
be two linear maps. The dual actions of
(A,\cdot)
are defined as

(2.23)

\langle L^{*}(x)a^{*},y\rangle=\langle a^{*},L(x)y\rangle,~\langle R^{*}(x)a^{*},y\rangle=\langle a^{*},R(x)y\rangle,\quad\forall~x,y\in A,~a^{*}\in A^{*}.

Then by Proposition
2.16
,
(R^{*},L^{*},A^{*})
is a representation.

Lemma 2.34
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{B})
be a quadratic
A_{3}
-associative algebra, where
(A,\cdot)
is an admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebra. Then the adjoint representation
(L,R,A)
and the coadjoint representation
(R^{*},L^{*},A^{*})
are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}:A\rightarrow A^{*}
be a linear isomorphism given by

(2.24)

\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x),y\rangle=\mathcal{B}(x,y),\quad\forall~x,y\in A.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is symmetric and by the invariant condition (
2.22
), we have

(2.25)

\mathcal{B}(x\cdot y,z)=\mathcal{B}(z,x\cdot y)=\mathcal{B}(z\cdot x,y)=\mathcal{B}(y,z\cdot x),\quad\forall~z\in A.

Then

\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(L(x)y),z\rangle\overset{\eqref{eq:lin-iso}}{=}\mathcal{B}(x\cdot y,z)\overset{\eqref{eq:circle-invar}}{=}\mathcal{B}(y,z\cdot x)\overset{\eqref{eq:lin-iso}}{=}\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y),z\cdot x\rangle\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-dual}}{=}\langle R^{*}(x)\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y),z\rangle,

that is,
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(L(x))y=R^{*}(x)\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y)
.
Similarly, we have
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(R(x))y=L^{*}(x)\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y)
. If we take
\phi=\mathcal{B}^{\natural}
, then by Definition
2.28
, the conclusion holds.
∎

Let
(L,R,A)
be an adjoint representation of an admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
. By Proposition
2.31
, the multiplication
\ast_{\mathfrak{d}}:(A\oplus A^{*})\times(A\oplus A^{*})\rightarrow A\oplus A^{*}
on
\mathfrak{d}=A\oplus A^{*}
is defined as

(2.26)

(x+a^{*})\ast_{\mathfrak{d}}(y+b^{*})=x\cdot y+L(x)b^{*}+R(y)a^{*},\quad\forall~x,y\in A,~a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}.

Example 2.35
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebra and
(L,R,A)
be the adjoint representation. By Proposition
2.16
,
there exists a natural nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}
on
\mathfrak{d}
defined as

(2.27)

\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}(x+a^{*},y+b^{*})=\langle b^{*},x\rangle+\langle a^{*},y\rangle,\quad\forall~x,y\in A,~a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}.

In fact, for all
x,y,z\in A
,
a^{*},b^{*},c^{*}\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}\big((x+a^{*})\cdot_{d}(y+b^{*}),z+c^{*}\big)\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-semi-dp}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}\big(x\cdot y+R^{*}(x)b^{*}+L^{*}(y)a^{*},z+c^{*}\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:sym-bilf}}{=}

\displaystyle\langle c^{*},x\cdot y\rangle+\langle R^{*}(x)b^{*}+L^{*}(y)a^{*},z\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-dual}}{=}

\displaystyle\langle c^{*},x\cdot y\rangle+\langle b^{*},z\cdot x\rangle+\langle a^{*},y\cdot z\rangle,

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-dual}}{=}

\displaystyle\langle R^{*}(y)c^{*}+L^{*}(z)b^{*},x\rangle+\langle a^{*},y\cdot z\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:sym-bilf}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}\big(x+a^{*},y\cdot z+R^{*}(y)c^{*}+L^{*}(z)b^{*}\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-semi-dp}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}\big(x+a^{*},(y+b^{*})\cdot_{d}(z+c^{*})\big).

Then
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}
is invariant, that is,
(A\ltimes_{R^{*},L^{*}}A^{*},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}})
is a quadratic
A_{3}
-associative algebra.

Definition 2.36
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an
A_{3}
-associative algebra and
r\in A\otimes A
. Let
h:A\rightarrow End(A\otimes A)
be a linear map defined by

(2.28)

h(x)=id\otimes L(x)-R(x)\otimes id,\quad\forall~x\in A,

where
id
denotes the identity map. The element
r
is called
invariant
on
(A,\cdot)
if
h(x)r=0
for all
x\in A
.

Let
r=\sum\limits_{i}(u_{i}\otimes v_{i})\in A\otimes A
, then
h(x)r=\sum\limits_{i}(u_{i}\otimes x\cdot v_{i}-u_{i}\cdot x\otimes v_{i})=0
, which is equivalent to
\mathcal{B}(x,y\cdot z)-\mathcal{B}(x\cdot y,z)=0
.
Thus, Definition
2.36
gives the tensor forms of invariant bilinear forms on
A_{3}
-associative algebras.

Proposition 2.37
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebra and
\mathcal{B}:A\times A\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
be a nondegenerate bilinear form. Let
\mathcal{B}^{\natural}:A\rightarrow A^{*}
be a linear isomorphism given by (
2.24
). If
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}\in A\otimes A
satisfies

(2.29)

\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{B}},a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle=\langle{\mathcal{B}^{\natural}}^{-1}(a^{*}),b^{*}\rangle,\quad\forall~a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*},

then
(A,\cdot,\mathcal{B})
is a quadratic
A_{3}
-associative algebra if and only if
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
is symmetric and invariant.

Proof.

By (
2.24
), we have
\mathcal{B}
is symmetric if and only if
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
is symmetric.
For all
x,y,z\in A
, let
a^{*}={\mathcal{B}^{\natural}}(x),b^{*}={\mathcal{B}^{\natural}}(y)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x,y\cdot z)\overset{\eqref{eq:lin-iso}}{=}\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(x),y\cdot z\rangle=\langle a^{*},{\mathcal{B}^{\natural}}^{-1}(b^{*})\cdot z\rangle\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-dual}}{=}\langle R^{*}(z)a^{*},{\mathcal{B}^{\natural}}^{-1}(b^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:tensor-liniso}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(y,z\cdot x)\overset{\eqref{eq:lin-iso}}{=}\langle\mathcal{B}^{\natural}(y),z\cdot x\rangle=\langle b^{*},z\cdot{\mathcal{B}^{\natural}}^{-1}(a^{*})\rangle\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-dual}}{=}\langle L^{*}(z)b^{*},{\mathcal{B}^{\natural}}^{-1}(a^{*})\rangle

3.
Manin triples of
A_{3}
-associative algebras and
A_{3}
-associative bialgebras

In this section, we introduce the concepts of Manin triples of
A_{3}
-associative algebras and
A_{3}
-associative bialgebras. We show that they are equivalent by matched pairs of
A_{3}
-associative algebras.

3.1.
Manin triples of
A_{3}
-associative algebras

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
and
(A^{\prime},\circ)
be
A_{3}
-associative algebras. The triple
\big((A\oplus A^{\prime}=\mathfrak{d},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}),A,A^{\prime}\big)
is called a
Manin triple of
A_{3}
-associative algebras
where

(1)

(A\oplus A^{\prime},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}})
is a quadratic
A_{3}
-associative algebra;

(2)

(A,\cdot)
and
(A^{\prime},\circ)
are
A_{3}
-associative subalgebras of
(A\oplus A^{\prime},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}})
, such that
\mathfrak{d}=A\oplus A^{\prime}
as vector spaces;

(3)

(A,\cdot)
and
(A^{\prime},\circ)
are isotropic, that is, for all
x,y\in A
,
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=0
.

To distinguish the left (or right) multiplications of different algebras, we denote the left (or right) multiplications on
(A,\cdot)
as
L_{\cdot}
(or
R_{\cdot}
), the left (or right) multiplications on
(A^{*},\circ)
as
L_{\circ}
(or
R_{\circ}
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
and
(A^{*},\circ)
be admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebras, and let
(R_{\cdot}^{*},L_{\cdot}^{*},A^{*})
and
(R_{\circ}^{*},L_{\circ}^{*},A)
be the coadjoint representations of
(A,\cdot)
and
(A^{*},\circ)
, respectively.
Then there is a Manin triple of
A_{3}
-associative algebras
\big((A\oplus A^{*}=\mathfrak{d},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}),A,A^{*}\big)
if and only if there is a matched pair of
A_{3}
-associative algebras
\big((A,\cdot),(A^{*},\circ),R^{*}_{\cdot},L^{*}_{\cdot},R^{*}_{\circ},L^{*}_{\circ}\big)
.

Proof.

Let
\big((\mathfrak{d},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}),A,A^{*}\big)
be a Manin triple of
A_{3}
-associative algebras, where the nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}
on
\mathfrak{d}
is given by (
2.27
). For all
x,y\in A
,
a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}b^{*},y)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:circle-invar}\eqref{eq:ad-semi-dp}}{=}\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}(y\cdot x,b^{*})\overset{\eqref{eq:sym-bilf}}{=}\langle y\cdot x,b^{*}\rangle\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-dual}}{=}\langle y,R^{*}_{\cdot}(x)b^{*}\rangle\overset{\eqref{eq:sym-bilf}}{=}\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}\big(R^{*}_{\cdot}(x)b^{*},y\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}b^{*},a^{*})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:circle-invar}\eqref{eq:ad-semi-dp}}{=}\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}(x,b^{*}\circ a^{*})\overset{\eqref{eq:sym-bilf}}{=}\langle x,b^{*}\circ a^{*}\rangle\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-dual}}{=}\langle L^{*}_{\circ}(b^{*})x,a^{*}\rangle\overset{\eqref{eq:sym-bilf}}{=}\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}\big(L^{*}_{\circ}(b^{*})x,a^{*}\big).

Thus,

\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}b^{*},y+a^{*})=\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}\big(R^{*}_{\cdot}(x)b^{*}+L^{*}_{\circ}(b^{*})x,y+a^{*}\big).

Since
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}
is nondegenerate, we have

x\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}b^{*}=R^{*}_{\cdot}(x)b^{*}+L^{*}_{\circ}(b^{*})x.

Similarly, we have

a^{*}\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}y=L^{*}_{\cdot}(y)a^{*}+R^{*}_{\circ}(a^{*})y.

Then

(3.30)

\displaystyle(x+a^{*})\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}(y+b^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\cdot y+x\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}b^{*}+a^{*}\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}}y+a^{*}\circ b^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\cdot y+R^{*}_{\circ}(a^{*})y+L^{*}_{\circ}(b^{*})x+a^{*}\circ b^{*}+R^{*}_{\cdot}(x)b^{*}+L^{*}_{\cdot}(y)a^{*}.

Thus,
\big((A,\cdot),(A^{*},\circ),R^{*}_{\cdot},L^{*}_{\cdot},R^{*}_{\circ},L^{*}_{\circ}\big)
is a matched pair of
A_{3}
-associative algebra.

Conversely, let
\big((A,\cdot),(A^{*},\circ),R^{*}_{\cdot},L^{*}_{\cdot},R^{*}_{\circ},L^{*}_{\circ}\big)
be a matched pair of
A_{3}
-associative algebras, then by (
3.30
), we have

(1)
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}
is invariant on the
A_{3}
-associative algebra
(A\oplus A^{*},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}})
. Then
(A\oplus A^{*},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}})
is a quadratic
A_{3}
-associative algebra;

(2)
(A,\cdot)
and
(A^{*},\circ)
are
A_{3}
-associative subalgebras of
(A\oplus A^{*},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}})
, such that
\mathfrak{d}=A\oplus A^{*}
as vector spaces;

(3) for all
x,y\in A
,
a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}
, we have
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}(a^{*},b^{*})=0
.

Therefore,
\big((A\oplus A^{*}=\mathfrak{d},\cdot_{\mathfrak{d}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{d}}),A,A^{*}\big)
is a Manin triple of
A_{3}
-associative algebras if and only if
\big((A,\cdot),(A^{*},\circ),R^{*}_{\cdot},L^{*}_{\cdot},R^{*}_{\circ},L^{*}_{\circ}\big)
is a matched pair of
A_{3}
-associative algebras.
∎

3.2.
A_{3}
-associative bialgebras

Definition 3.3
.

An

Proof.

For all
a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*},x\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\langle a^{*}\circ b^{*},x\rangle=\langle R^{*}(r^{\sharp}(a^{*}))b^{*}+L^{*}(r^{\sharp}(b^{*}))a^{*},x\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:ad-dual}}{=}

\displaystyle\langle b^{*},x\cdot r^{\sharp}(a^{*})\rangle+\langle a^{*},r^{\sharp}(b^{*})\cdot x\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:omega}}{=}

\displaystyle\omega(r^{\sharp}(b^{*}),x\cdot r^{\sharp}(a^{*}))+\omega(r^{\sharp}(a^{*}),r^{\sharp}(b^{*})\cdot x)

\displaystyle\overset{Theorem~\ref{omegasharp}}{=}

\displaystyle-\omega(x,r^{\sharp}(a^{*})\cdot r^{\sharp}(b^{*}))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:omega}}{=}

\displaystyle\langle(r^{\sharp})^{-1}\big(r^{\sharp}(a^{*})\cdot r^{\sharp}(b^{*})\big),x\rangle.

Hence, we have

(4.52)

a^{*}\circ b^{*}=(r^{\sharp})^{-1}(r^{\sharp}(a^{*})\cdot r^{\sharp}(b^{*})),\quad\forall~a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}.

Therefore,
r^{\sharp}:A^{*}\rightarrow A
is a homomorphism of
(A,\cdot)
.
∎

(A,\cdot)
be an admissible

,
A^{*}
be the dual space of
A

T:A\rightarrow A
be a linear map,
T^{*}:A^{*}\rightarrow A^{*}
be the dual map of
T
, defined by

(4.53)

\langle T^{*}(a^{*}),x\rangle=\langle a^{*},T(x)\rangle,\quad\forall~a^{*}\in A^{*},x\in A.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebra with an adjoint representation
(L_{\cdot},R_{\cdot},A)
. Let
\widetilde{T}:(A\oplus A^{*})^{*}\rightarrow A\oplus A^{*}
be a linear map defined as
\widetilde{T}(a^{*}+x)=T^{*}(a^{*})-T(x)
.
Let
D=A\oplus A^{*}
and
\cdot_{D}:D\otimes D\rightarrow D
be a linear map defined as

(4.54)

(x+a^{*})\cdot_{D}(y+b^{*})=x\cdot y+R_{\cdot}^{*}(x)b^{*}+L_{\cdot}^{*}(y)a^{*},\quad\forall~x,y\in A,a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}.

Then
T:A\rightarrow A
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(A,\cdot)
associated to
(L_{\cdot},R_{\cdot},A)
if and only if
\widetilde{T}:D^{*}\rightarrow D
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(D,\cdot_{D})
associated to
(R_{D}^{*},L_{D}^{*},D^{*})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.32
,
(D,\cdot_{D})
is an admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebra, we denote the adjoint representation of
(D,\cdot_{D})
by
(R_{D}^{*},L_{D}^{*},D^{*})
.
Next, we prove that

\widetilde{T}:D^{*}\rightarrow D
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(D,\cdot_{D})
associated to
(R_{D}^{*},L_{D}^{*},D^{*})
.
For all
a^{*},b^{*},c^{*}\in A^{*},x,y\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\langle R_{D}^{*}\big(T(x)\big)y,c^{*}\rangle=\langle y,c^{*}\cdot_{D}T(x)\rangle\overset{\eqref{eq:semi-TTm}}{=}\langle y,L_{\cdot}^{*}\big(T(x)\big)c^{*}\rangle=\langle L_{\cdot}\big(T(x)\big)y,c^{*}\rangle.

Then
R_{D}^{*}\big(T(x)\big)y=L_{\cdot}\big(T(x)\big)y
.
Similarly,
L_{D}^{*}\big(T(y)\big)x=R_{\cdot}\big(T(y)\big)x
. Then we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}(x+a^{*})\cdot_{D}\widetilde{T}(y+b^{*})-\widetilde{T}\Big(R_{D}^{*}\big(\widetilde{T}(x+a^{*})\big)(y+b^{*})+L_{D}^{*}\big(\widetilde{T}(y+b^{*})\big)(x+a^{*})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\widetilde{T}(x)+\widetilde{T}(a^{*})\big)\cdot_{D}\big(\widetilde{T}(y)+\widetilde{T}(b^{*})\big)-\widetilde{T}\Big(R_{D}^{*}\big(-T(x)+T^{*}(a^{*})\big)(y+b^{*})

\displaystyle+L_{D}^{*}\big(-T(y)+T^{*}(b^{*})\big)(x+a^{*})\Big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:semi-TTm}}{=}

\displaystyle\big(-T(x)\big)\cdot\big(-T(y)\big)+R_{\cdot}^{*}\big(-T(x)\big)\big(T^{*}(b^{*})\big)+L_{\cdot}^{*}\big(-T(y)\big)\big(T^{*}(a^{*})\big)

\displaystyle-\widetilde{T}\Big(-R_{D}^{*}\big(T(x)\big)v-R_{D}^{*}\big(T(x)\big)b^{*}+R_{D}^{*}\big(T^{*}(a^{*})\big)y-L_{D}^{*}\big(T(y)\big)x-L_{D}^{*}\big(T(y)\big)a^{*}

\displaystyle+L_{D}^{*}\big(T^{*}(b^{*})\big)x\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{T(x)\cdot T(y)}_{(e1)}-\underbrace{R_{\cdot}^{*}\big(T(x)\big)\big(T^{*}(b^{*})\big)}_{(e2)}-\underbrace{L_{\cdot}^{*}\big(T(y)\big)\big(T^{*}(a^{*})\big)}_{(e3)}-\underbrace{T\big(L_{\cdot}(T(x))y\big)}_{(e4)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{T^{*}\Big(R_{D}^{*}\big(T(x)\big)b^{*}\Big)}_{(e5)}-\underbrace{T^{*}\Big(R_{D}^{*}\big(T^{*}(a^{*})\big)y\Big)}_{(e6)}-\underbrace{T\Big(R_{\cdot}\big(T(y)\big)x\Big)}_{(e7)}+\underbrace{T^{*}\Big(L_{D}^{*}\big(T(y)\big)a^{*}\Big)}_{(e8)}

\displaystyle-\underbrace{T^{*}\Big(L_{D}^{*}\big(T^{*}(b^{*})\big)x\Big)}_{(e9)}.

Take the three items from the above respectively and let

\displaystyle(Eq13):=-(e2)+(e5)-(e9),~(Eq14):=-(e3)-(e6)+(e8),~(Eq15):=(e1)-(e4)-(e7).

For all
z\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\langle(Eq13),z\rangle=\langle-R_{\cdot}^{*}\big(T(x)\big)\big(T^{*}(b^{*})\big)+T^{*}\Big(R_{D}^{*}\big(T(x)\big)b^{*}\Big)-T^{*}\Big(L_{D}^{*}\big(T^{*}(b^{*})\big)x\Big),z\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:TT}}{=}

\displaystyle-\langle T^{*}(b^{*}),R_{\cdot}\big(T(x)\big)z\rangle+\langle R_{D}^{*}\big(T(x)\big)b^{*},T(z)\rangle-\langle L_{D}^{*}\big(T^{*}(b^{*})\big)x,T(z)\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:TT}}{=}

\displaystyle-\langle b^{*},T\Big(R_{\cdot}\big(T(x)\big)z\Big)\rangle+\langle b^{*},T(z)\cdot T(x)\rangle-\langle x,T^{*}(b^{*})\cdot_{D}T(z)\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:semi-TTm}}{=}

\displaystyle-\langle b^{*},T\Big(R_{\cdot}\big(T(x)\big)z\Big)\rangle+\langle b^{*},T(z)\cdot T(x)\rangle-\langle x,L_{\cdot}^{*}\big(T(z)\big)T^{*}(b^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle b^{*},T\Big(R_{\cdot}\big(T(x)\big)z\Big)\rangle+\langle b^{*},T(z)\cdot T(x)\rangle-\langle T\Big(L_{\cdot}\big(T(z)\big)x\Big),b^{*}\rangle.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\langle(Eq14),z\rangle=-\langle a^{*},T\Big(L_{\cdot}\big(T(y)\big)z\Big)\rangle+\langle a^{*},T(y)\cdot T(z)\rangle-\langle T\Big(R_{\cdot}\big(T(z)\big)y\Big),a^{*}\rangle.

Because
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(A,\cdot)
associated to
(L_{\cdot},R_{\cdot},A)
, then

(Eq13)=(Eq14)=(Eq15)=0.

Hence,
\widetilde{T}:D^{*}\rightarrow D
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(D,\cdot_{D})
associated to
(R_{D}^{*},L_{D}^{*},D^{*})
.
Conversely, if
\widetilde{T}:D^{*}\rightarrow D
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(D,\cdot_{D})
associated to
(R_{D}^{*},L_{D}^{*},D^{*})
, then

(Eq13)=(Eq14)=(Eq15)=0,

that is,
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(A,\cdot)
associated to
(L_{\cdot},R_{\cdot},A)
.
Thus, the conclusion holds.
∎

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an admissible
A_{3}
-associative algebra with an adjoint representation
(L_{\cdot},R_{\cdot},A)
. Let
\widetilde{T}:(A\oplus A^{*})^{*}\rightarrow A\oplus A^{*}
be a linear map defined as
\widetilde{T}(a^{*}+x)=T^{*}(a^{*})-T(x)
,
T:A\rightarrow A
be a linear map, which is identified as an element in
A\otimes A^{*}\hookrightarrow(A\ltimes_{R_{\cdot}^{*},L_{\cdot}^{*}}A^{*})\otimes(A\ltimes_{R_{\cdot}^{*},L_{\cdot}^{*}}A^{*})
, given by

(4.55)

\langle T,(a^{*}+x)\otimes(b^{*}+y)\rangle=\langle\widetilde{T}(a^{*}+x),b^{*}+y\rangle,\quad\forall~x,y\in A,a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}.

Let
D=A\oplus A^{*}
and
\cdot_{D}:D\otimes D\rightarrow D
be a linear map defined as (
4.54
).
Then
T:A\rightarrow A
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(A,\cdot)
associated to
(L_{\cdot},R_{\cdot},A)
if and only if
r=T-\tau(T)
is a skew-symmetric solution of the
A_{3}
-associative Yang-Baxter equation in
A\ltimes_{R_{\cdot}^{*},L_{\cdot}^{*}}A^{*}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.11
,
T:A\rightarrow A
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(A,\cdot)
associated to
(L_{\cdot},R_{\cdot},A)
if and only if

\widetilde{T}:D^{*}\rightarrow D
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(D,\cdot_{D})
associated to
(R_{D}^{*},L_{D}^{*},D^{*})
.

Identify a linear map
T:A\rightarrow A
as an element in
D\otimes D
through the injective map

Hom_{\mathbb{K}}(A,A)\cong A\otimes A^{*}\hookrightarrow D\otimes D.

By taking
r^{\sharp}=\widetilde{T}\in D\otimes D
and Proposition
4.8
(2), it follows that
r=T-\tau(T)
is a solution of the
A_{3}
-associative Yang-Baxter equation in
(D,\cdot_{D})
if and only if
\widetilde{T}:(A\oplus A^{*})^{*}\rightarrow A\oplus A^{*}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of
(D,\cdot_{D})
associated to
(R_{D}^{*},L_{D}^{*},D^{*})
.
Therefore, the conclusion holds.
∎

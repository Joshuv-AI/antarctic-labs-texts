Corona algebras and strongly self-absorbing C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{\ast} -dynamics
Xiuyuan Li, Matteo Pagliero, Gábor Szabó

Abstract
. This article concerns the structure of C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{\ast} -algebraic group actions induced on corona algebras from a given σ \sigma -unital C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{\ast} -dynamical system over a locally compact group G G . We prove that such actions satisfy the so-called dynamical folding property, which generalizes a fundamental property observed for corona algebras in works of Manuilov–Thomsen and Phillips–Weaver. We then focus on corona actions induced from G G - C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{\ast} -dynamics that are assumed to absorb a given strongly self-absorbing and unitarily regular G G -action γ \gamma . It is proved that these corona actions are γ \gamma -saturated, which is a stronger property than being separably γ \gamma -stable. Conversely, if one assumes that the underlying C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{\ast} -dynamics absorbs the trivial action on the compact operators, then γ \gamma -saturation of the corona action is equivalent to the original action being γ \gamma -absorbing. These results are a dynamical version of recent work by Farah and the third-named author.

Even in special cases, such as considering the trivial

Let

\mathrm{C}^{\ast}

Our main result can be summarized as follows (see Theorem

They are proved under more general assumptions, but we shall state them here for separable

Theorem A

Let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\curvearrowright A

\mathrm{C}^{\ast}

The “furthermore” part in the above theorem is the comparably straightforward aspect of the main result deduced in the last section, since it can be obtained rather easily from the results and techniques that were introduced recently in
[
4
]
.
The rest of the statement, however, is proved via a careful dynamical extension of the ideas behind the proof of
[
4

.
At least for actions of compact groups, this generalization is mostly technical in nature and can be achieved via the similar conceptual steps and extra bookkeeping.
This has been accomplished in the first-named author’s master thesis work
[
10
]

We would like to stress, however, that some new conceptual steps are needed in the proof of the statement for general
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-dynamics, in particular to cover arbitrary actions of groups
G
that are non-compact.
This stems from the added difficulty that one may only identify
G
-actions modulo
cocycle

In order to overcome this technical difficulty, we prove and make use of the so-called
dynamical folding property
for
G
-actions induced on corona algebras; see Definition

for the details.
In somewhat oversimplified terms, a (not necessarily continuous) action
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\curvearrowright A
on a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra has the dynamical folding property if a given equivariant embedding
(D_{0},\delta)\to(A,\alpha)
of a separable system can be extended to an embedding of a surrounding system
(D,\delta)\supseteq(D_{0},\delta)
if and only if
D
can be embedded in this way into the
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra of continuous paths with values in
A
.
Without the dynamics (i.e. with
G=\{1\}
), this property has been observed for corona algebras by Manuilov–Thomsen
[
11
]
and Phillips–Weaver
[
16
]
; the term “folding” is directly inspired by a comment about this property made in the latter article.
This proved instrumental in the former’s proof that the

\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra pair
(A,B)
can be obtained as the
KK
-group of the associated pair
(\mathscr{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\otimes A,\mathscr{Q}(B\otimes K))
.
Here we prove:

Theorem B
.

Let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\curvearrowright A
be an action on a
\sigma
-unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra.
Then
(\mathscr{Q}(A),\alpha)
has the dynamical folding property.

While itself of independent interest, Theorem
B
is used in the proof of Theorem
A
.
As was mentioned above, when one aims to prove the latter for
G
not necessarily compact, one can generalize most of the steps from the
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebraic proof, but ultimately one faces a kind of cocycle obstruction to carry out the last part of the proof.
Due to the specific context here, the theory of strongly self-absorbing actions can be used to see that this cocycle obstruction can be at least asymptotically trivialized with a continuous path of unitaries.
As a result of the dynamical folding property, this allows us to genuinely trivialize the given cocycle obstruction in the corona and hence we can obtain Theorem
A
.

Apart from the application to our main result, the dynamical folding property has a few other immediate consequences.
In the context of Theorem

are mutually
G

\mathscr{Q}(A)

\alpha

We expect a few more interesting applications of the dynamical folding property in subsequent work, possibly in connection with equivariant
KK
-theory.

Notation 1.1

The work in this article appeals to the categorical framework of

Definition 1.2

Let

\mathbf{1}_{\mathscr{D}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(A,\alpha)\to(\mathscr{D}\otimes A,\gamma\otimes\alpha)

satisfies the conditions stated in
[

, Proposition 3.1]
.
However, we note that those conditions are identical to the ones stated in
[
24
, Proposition 4.3]
, which has a strong enough conclusion to yield the desired statement.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
:
Let the cocycle conjugacy
(\varphi,\mathbbm{u})
and the map
w
be given as in the statement.
Define a point-norm continuous path
(\Phi_{t})_{t\in[0,\infty)}
of unital embeddings
\mathscr{D}\to A
given by

\Phi_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varphi^{-1}\circ\mathrm{Ad}(w_{t})\circ(\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{D}}).

Firstly, note that we have for all
a\in A
and
d\in\mathscr{D}
that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|[\Phi_{t}(d),a]\|=\lim_{t\to\infty}\|[\Phi_{t}(d),\varphi^{-1}(w_{t}(a\otimes\mathbf{1})w_{t}^{*})]\|=\lim_{t\to\infty}\|[\mathbf{1}\otimes d,a\otimes\mathbf{1}]\|=0.

Secondly, observe that

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\alpha_{g}(\Phi_{t}(d))&=&\alpha_{g}\circ\varphi^{-1}\circ\mathrm{Ad}(w_{t})\circ(\mathbf{1}\otimes d)\\
&=&(\varphi\circ\alpha_{g^{-1}})^{-1}\circ\mathrm{Ad}(w_{t})\circ(\mathbf{1}\otimes d)\\
&=&(\mathrm{Ad}(\mathbbm{u}_{g^{-1}})\circ(\alpha\otimes\gamma)_{g^{-1}}\circ\varphi)^{-1}\circ\mathrm{Ad}(w_{t})\circ(\mathbf{1}\otimes d)\\
&=&\varphi^{-1}\circ(\alpha\otimes\gamma)_{g}\circ\mathrm{Ad}(\mathbbm{u}_{g^{-1}}^{*}w_{t})\circ(\mathbf{1}\otimes d)\\
&=&\varphi^{-1}\circ\mathrm{Ad}(\mathbbm{u}_{g}(\alpha\otimes\gamma)_{g}(w_{t}))\circ(\mathbf{1}\otimes\gamma_{g}(d))\end{array}

for any
g\in G
and
d\in\mathscr{D}
.
Using the cocycle condition above, one may infer that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\max_{g\in K}\|w_{t}-\mathbbm{u}_{g}(\alpha\otimes\gamma)_{g}(w_{t})\|=0

for every compact subset
K\subseteq G

(\Phi_{t})_{t\in[0,\infty)}
is asymptotically equivariant, i.e.,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\max_{g\in K}\|\Phi(\gamma_{g}(d))-\alpha_{g}(\Phi_{t}(d))\|=0

for all
d\in\mathscr{D}
and every compact subset
K\subseteq G
.
It follows from the previous two observations that

induces a well-defined equivariant unital embedding

\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\mathscr{D},\gamma)\to(A_{\mathfrak{c},\alpha}\cap A^{\prime},\alpha_{\mathfrak{c}}).

This finishes the proof.

a strongly self-absorbing action. Suppose that
\alpha
is separably
\gamma
-stable.
Then for every separable
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebra

, there exists an equivariant unital
\ast
-homomorphism

(\mathscr{D}^{(2)},\gamma^{(2)})\to(\mathscr{Q}(A)\cap_{\alpha}C^{\prime},\alpha).

Proof.

Fix a separable
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebra
C\subseteq\mathscr{Q}(A)
.
Due to
[
4

and the fact that
\alpha
is separably
\gamma

\alpha

\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebra
A_{1}\subseteq A

(\alpha\restriction{A_{1}})

\gamma

\mathscr{Q}(A_{1})\subseteq\mathscr{Q}(A)

Considering what the claim says, it suffices to prove it for
(A_{1},\alpha)

.
In other words, we may assume without loss of generality that

is separable and that
\alpha
is
\gamma
-stable.

In order to find a
\ast
-homomorphism as in the claim, we shall appeal to the universal property of
(\mathscr{D}^{(2)},\gamma^{(2)})
as specified in Remark

. In other words, we want to find two equivariant c.p.c. order zero maps
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\mathscr{D},\gamma)\to(\mathscr{Q}(A)\cap_{\alpha}C^{\prime},\alpha)
with commuting ranges such that
\mu_{0}(\mathbf{1})+\mu_{1}(\mathbf{1})=\mathbf{1}
.

Pick a sequence
(c_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in the unit ball of
\mathscr{M}(A)
whose image under the quotient map
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{M}(A)\to\mathscr{Q}(A)
is dense in the unit ball of

. Moreover, fix an increasing sequence of finite subsets
F_{n}
of the unit ball of
\mathscr{D}
with dense union
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcup_{n}F_{n}
, and an increasing sequence of compact subsets

whose union is
G
.
For every
n\geq 1
, let us apply Lemma
2.8
and find a constant

satisfying the condition stated there for
2^{-n}
in place of
\varepsilon
.
We may assume
\delta_{n+1}\leq\delta_{n}\leq 2^{-n}
for all

.
By Lemma
1.16
there exists an approximate unit
(e_{n})_{n\geq}
of

e_{n+1}e_{n}=e_{n}
, and

\max_{\ell\leq n}\|[c_{\ell},e_{n}]\|+\max_{g\in K_{n}}\|\alpha_{g}(e_{n})-e_{n}\|\leq\delta_{n+1}/2\quad\text{for all }n\geq 1.

If we define
e_{-1}=e_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0
and
f_{n}=(e_{n}-e_{n-1})^{1/2}
for all

, then it follows from the choice of the constants

that

(a)

\|[f_{n},c_{\ell}]\|\leq 2^{-n}
for all
\ell\leq n
and
n\geq 1
,

(b)

\max_{g\in K_{n}}\|\alpha_{g}(f_{n})-f_{n}\|\leq 2^{-n}
for all

,

(c)

f_{m}f_{n}=0
if
|m-n|\geq 2
,

(d)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}^{2}=\mathbf{1}
strictly.

Moreover, thanks to Remark
1.5
, we may find a sequence of unital
\ast
-homomorphisms
\varphi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{M}(A)
such that

(e)

\max_{d\in F_{n}}\|[\varphi_{n}(d),f_{m}]\|\leq 2^{-n}
for all

and
n\geq 1
,

(f)

\max_{d\in F_{n}}\|[\varphi_{n}(d),f_{n}c_{\ell}]\|\leq 2^{-n}
for all
\ell\leq n
and
n\geq 1
,

(g)

\max_{d,d^{\prime}\in F_{n}}\|[\varphi_{n}(d),f_{\ell}\varphi_{\ell}(d^{\prime})f_{\ell}]\|\leq 2^{-n}
for all

and
n\geq 1

(h)

\max_{d\in F_{n}}\max_{g\in K_{n}}\|f_{n}(\alpha_{g}(\varphi_{n}(d))-\varphi_{n}(\gamma_{g}(d)))f_{n}\|\leq 2^{-n}
for all

Define two maps
\Psi_{0},\Psi_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{M}(A)

\Psi_{i}(d)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{2n+i}\varphi_{2n+i}(d)f_{2n+i},\quad d\in\mathscr{D},\ i=0,1.

By Lemma
2.7
, we know that
\Psi_{0}
and
\Psi_{1}
are c.p.c. maps.
The formula
\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{1})+\Psi_{1}(\mathbf{1})=\mathbf{1}
evidently holds by construction due to condition (
d
).
As before, we shall use the notation

to say that two elements
a,b\in\mathscr{M}(A)
agree modulo
A
.
For every
g\in G
and

, Lemma

furthermore implies with condition (

) that

\alpha_{g}(\Psi_{i}(d))\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{2n+i}\alpha_{g}(\varphi_{2n+i}(d))f_{2n+i},\quad i=0,1.

We may therefore infer with condition (
h
) that

\alpha_{g}(\Psi_{i}(d))\equiv\Psi_{i}(\gamma_{g}(d))\quad\text{for all }g\in G,\ d\in F\text{ and }i=0,1.

Since
F
is dense in the unit ball of
\mathscr{D}
, we conclude that
\alpha_{g}(\Psi_{i}(d))\equiv\Psi_{i}(\gamma_{g}(d))
holds for all

and

.
In other words, the c.p.c. maps given by

\mu_{i}=\omega\circ\Psi_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{Q}(A),\quad i=0,1,

are equivariant with respect to
\gamma
and
\alpha
.
To show that the maps
\mu_{i}
have range in
\mathscr{Q}(A)\cap C^{\prime}
, pick

for some

.
Note that for all

,

f_{n}\varphi_{n}(d)f_{n}c_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\footnotesize{\eqref{d}}}}}}{{=}}_{2^{-n}}f_{n}^{2}c_{j}\varphi_{n}(d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\footnotesize{\eqref{a}}}}}}{{=}}_{2^{1-n}}c_{j}f_{n}^{2}\varphi_{n}(d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\footnotesize{\eqref{c}}}}}}{{=}}_{2^{-n}}c_{j}f_{n}\varphi_{n}(d)f_{n}.

Thus,
\|[f_{n}\varphi_{n}(d)f_{n},c_{j}]\|\leq 2^{2-n}
for
j\leq n
and
d\in F_{n}
, which implies that
[\Psi_{i}(d),c_{j}]\in A
for all
j\geq 1
and
d\in F
, where
i=0,1
.
Since
F
and
(\omega(c_{n}))_{n}
are dense in the unit ball of
\mathscr{D}
and
C
, respectively, it follows for every

and

that
[\Psi_{i}(d),c]\in A
, and therefore
\mu_{i}(\mathscr{D})\subseteq\mathscr{Q}(A)\cap C^{\prime}
for
i=0,1
.
Since we have already shown that
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
are equivariant, this yields
\mu_{i}(\mathscr{D})\subseteq\mathscr{Q}(A)\cap_{\alpha}C^{\prime}
for
i=0,1
.

In order to show that the ranges of
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
commute, let us choose
d,d^{\prime}\in F_{n}
.
We note that

\displaystyle f_{n+1}\varphi_{n+1}(d^{\prime})f_{n+1}\cdot f_{n}\varphi_{n}(d)f_{n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\footnotesize{\eqref{c}}}}}}{{=}}_{2^{-(n+1)}}f_{n+1}^{2}\varphi_{n+1}(d^{\prime})f_{n}\varphi_{n}(d)f_{n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\footnotesize{\eqref{e}}}}}}{{=}}_{2^{-(n+1)}}f_{n+1}^{2}f_{n}\varphi_{n}(d)f_{n}\varphi_{n+1}(d^{\prime})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\footnotesize{\eqref{c}}}}}}{{=}}_{2^{1-n}}f_{n}\varphi_{n}(d)f_{n}f_{n+1}^{2}\varphi_{n+1}(d^{\prime})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\footnotesize{\eqref{c}}}}}}{{=}}_{2^{-(n+1)}}f_{n}\varphi_{n}(d)f_{n}f_{n+1}\varphi_{n+1}(d^{\prime})f_{n+1}.

In particular, this implies that

\|[f_{n+1}\varphi_{n+1}(d^{\prime})f_{n+1},f_{n}\varphi_{n}(d)f_{n}]\|\leq 2^{2-n}.

By using condition (
c
), we then have that

\displaystyle[\Psi_{0}(d),\Psi_{1}(d^{\prime})]=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{2n}\varphi_{2n}(d)f_{2n}\ ,\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{2n+1}\varphi_{2n+1}(d^{\prime})f_{2n+1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[f_{2n}\varphi_{2n}(d)f_{2n},f_{2n+1}\varphi_{2n+1}(d^{\prime})f_{2n+1}]

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[f_{2n}\varphi_{2n}(d)f_{2n},f_{2n-1}\varphi_{2n-1}(d^{\prime})f_{2n-1}].

The estimate above implies that these define norm-convergent series in
A
and thus
[\Psi_{0}(d),\Psi_{1}(d^{\prime})]\in A
for every
d,d^{\prime}\in F
. Since
F
was dense in the unit ball of
\mathscr{D}
, the same holds for
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}
, and hence
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
have commuting ranges.

To see that
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
are order zero maps, we use once again condition (
c
) to see that, for
i=0,1
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}(d)\Psi_{i}(d^{\prime})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{2n+i}\varphi_{2n+i}(d)f_{2n+i}^{2}\varphi_{2n+i}(d^{\prime})f_{2n+i}

for all
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}
. If
d,d^{\prime}\in F_{n}
for some

, then

\displaystyle f_{2n+i}\varphi_{2n+1}(d)f_{2n+i}^{2}\varphi_{2n+i}(d^{\prime})f_{2n+i}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\mbox{\footnotesize{\eqref{c}}}}}}{{=}}_{2^{1-(2n+i)}}f_{2n+i}\varphi_{2n+1}(dd^{\prime})f_{2n+i}^{3}.

We may conclude that

\Psi_{i}(d)\Psi_{i}(d^{\prime})\equiv\Psi_{i}(ab)\Psi_{i}(\mathbf{1})

for all
d,d^{\prime}\in F
, and hence also for all
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}
. To summarize,
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
are equivariant c.p.c. order zero maps with commuting ranges such that
\mu_{0}(\mathbf{1})+\mu_{1}(\mathbf{1})=\mathbf{1}
, and thus the universal property recalled in Remark
1.8
yields an equivariant unital
\ast
-homomorphism
(\mathscr{D}^{(2)},\gamma^{(2)})\to\mathscr{Q}(A)\cap_{\alpha}C^{\prime}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main theorem of this article.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\curvearrowright A
be an action on a
\sigma
-unital, non-unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra, and
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\curvearrowright\mathscr{D}
a strongly self-absorbing, unitarily regular action. If
\alpha
is separably
\gamma
-stable, then
(\mathscr{Q}(A),\alpha)
is
\gamma
-saturated.

Proof.

Fix
C\subseteq\mathscr{Q}(A)
as in the statement.
By Proposition
3.2
, there exists an equivariant unital
\ast
-homomorphism

\kappa\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\mathscr{D}^{(2)},\gamma^{(2)})\to(\mathscr{Q}(A)\cap_{\alpha}C^{\prime},\alpha).

It extends to an equivariant unital
\ast
-homomorphism

\kappa_{\mathfrak{c}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{D}^{(2)}_{\mathfrak{c}}\to(\mathscr{Q}(A)_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{c}}\quad\text{with}\quad[\kappa_{\mathfrak{c}}(\mathscr{D}^{(2)}),C]=0

that sends a representative function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[0,\infty)\to\mathscr{D}^{(2)}
of an element in
\mathscr{D}^{(2)}_{\mathfrak{c}}
to
\kappa\circ f\in\mathscr{C}_{b}([0,\infty),\mathscr{Q}(A)_{\alpha}\cap C^{\prime})
. This is well defined because whenever
f(t)\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}0
one gets
\kappa(f(t))\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}0
.
Since the strongly self-absorbing action
\gamma
is assumed to be unitarily regular,
\gamma^{(2)}
is
\gamma
-absorbing by Theorem
1.9
. By Proposition
1.14
there exists an equivariant unital embedding

\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\mathscr{D},\gamma)\hookrightarrow(\mathscr{D}^{(2)}_{\mathfrak{c},\gamma^{(2)}},\gamma^{(2)}_{\mathfrak{c}}).

The resulting composition
\psi=\kappa_{\mathfrak{c}}\circ\theta
is a unital equivariant
*
-homomorphism from
(\mathscr{D},\gamma)
to
(\mathscr{Q}(A)_{\alpha})_{\mathfrak{c}}
with
[\psi(\mathscr{D}),C]=0
.
We want to appeal to the dynamical folding property via Theorem
2.9
.
We make the choice
D=\psi(\mathscr{D})
with action induced from
\gamma
, we choose
B_{0}=\mathrm{C}^{\ast}(C,\mathbf{1})
, and
B=\mathrm{C}^{\ast}(B_{0}\cup D)\subseteq\mathscr{Q}(A)_{\mathfrak{c}}
.
This allows us to find a
\ast
-homomorphism
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\to\mathscr{Q}(A)
such that
\varphi(b)=b
for all
b\in B_{0}
and
\varphi\circ\psi
defines an equivariant
*
-homomorphism from
(\mathscr{D},\gamma)
to
(\mathscr{Q}(A),\alpha)
.
Since
\psi(\mathbf{1}_{D})=\mathbf{1}_{\mathscr{Q}(A)}\in B_{0}
, it follows that
\varphi\circ\psi
is unital.
Since
C\subseteq B_{0}
and
[\psi(\mathscr{D}),C]=0
, it follows that also
[(\varphi\circ\psi)(\mathscr{D}),C]=0
.
In other words, we have found an equivariant unital
*
-homomorphism from
(\mathscr{D},\gamma)
to
(\mathscr{Q}(A)\cap_{\alpha}C^{\prime},\alpha)
.
∎

We would like to end this section by pointing out that the main result also leads to a uniqueness theorem that generalizes the one observed in
[
3
, Theorem E]
.
We shall deduce this as a consequence of a more general formal observation.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra and
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\curvearrowright A
an algebraic action with the dynamical folding property.
Suppose that
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\curvearrowright\mathscr{D}
is a strongly self-absorbing, unitarily regular action such that
\alpha
is
\gamma
-saturated.
Let
C\subseteq A
be a separable
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebra.
Then all equivariant unital
*
-homomorphisms from
(\mathscr{D},\gamma)
to
(A\cap_{\alpha}C^{\prime},\alpha)
are mutually
G
-unitarily equivalent.

Proof.

Fix
C\subseteq A
as in the statement.
We first note that due to the assumption, there must exist an equivariant unital
*
-homomorphisms from
(\mathscr{D},\gamma)
to
(A\cap_{\alpha}C^{\prime},\alpha)
to begin with.
Let
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
be two arbitrary ones.
Consider
E=\mathrm{C}^{\ast}(\psi_{1}(\mathscr{D})\cup\psi_{2}(\mathscr{D}))
, which is a separable
\alpha
-invariant
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebra of
A
.
Using that
\alpha
is
\gamma
-saturated, we find a third equivariant unital
*
-homomorphism
\psi_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\mathscr{D},\gamma)\to(A\cap_{\alpha}(C\cup E)^{\prime},\alpha)
.
By the universal property of the tensor product, we obtain two equivariant unital
*
-homomorphisms

\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\mathscr{D}\otimes\mathscr{D},\gamma\otimes\gamma)\to(A\cap_{\alpha}C^{\prime},\alpha)

defined by
\theta_{1}(a\otimes b)=\psi_{1}(a)\psi_{3}(b)
and
\theta_{2}(a\otimes b)=\psi_{2}(a)\psi_{3}(b)
for all
a,b\in\mathscr{D}
.
In other words, we have

\psi_{1}=\theta_{1}\circ(\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{D}}\otimes\mathbf{1}),\ \psi_{2}=\theta_{2}\circ(\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{D}}\otimes\mathbf{1}),\ \psi_{3}=\theta_{1}\circ(\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{D})=\theta_{2}\circ(\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{D}).

As
\gamma
is strongly self-absorbing and unitarily regular, it follows from
[
21
, Theorem 3.15]
that the two equivariant unital embeddings

\mathrm{id}_{D}\otimes\mathbf{1},\ \mathbf{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{D}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\mathscr{D},\gamma)\to(\mathscr{D}\otimes\mathscr{D},\gamma\otimes\gamma)

are asymptotically
G
-unitarily equivalent.
This immediately implies with the above equations (via transitivity) that
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are asymptotically
G
-unitarily equivalent as maps into
A\cap_{\alpha}C^{\prime}
.
The claim thus follows from Lemma
2.11
.
∎

Let

be a

.

We would like to point out that our main result has a partial converse that follows from the results in

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
A
is a nonzero
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra with an action
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\curvearrowright A
.
We equip
\mathscr{K}\otimes A
with the action
\alpha^{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{K}}\otimes\alpha
.
Denote by
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)\to\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)
the (equivariant) quotient map.
Then there exist:

(1)

a unital equivariant
*
-embedding
\Theta\colon\mathscr{Q}(c_{0}(A))\to\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)
with
\Theta(\mathscr{M}(A))=\pi(1\otimes\mathscr{M}(A))
.

(2)

an equivariant u.c.p. map
\Psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)\to\mathscr{Q}(c_{0}(A))
with
\Psi\circ\Theta=\operatorname{id}
.

(3)

a separable unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebra

of
\pi(\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{K})\otimes 1)\cap\Theta(\mathscr{Q}(c_{0}(A)))^{\prime}
(and hence of
\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)^{\alpha^{s}}
) such that after composing with the quotient map
\mathscr{Q}(c_{0}(A))\to\mathscr{M}(A)_{\infty}
, the restriction

\bar{\Psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)\cap C^{\prime}\to\mathscr{M}(A)_{\infty}

is an equivariant
*
-homomorphism.

Proof.

This follows directly from
[
4
, Theorem 4.4]
and the construction of the involved maps in ‘Definition 4.2’ therein, which automatically makes all these maps equivariant.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\curvearrowright A
be an action on a separable
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra.
Let
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\curvearrowright\mathscr{D}
be a strongly self-absorbing action.
If the algebraic action induced by
\alpha^{s}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{K}}\otimes\alpha
on
\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)
is
\gamma
-saturated, then
\alpha
is
\gamma
-stable.

Proof.

Let
C\subset\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)^{\alpha^{s}}
be a separable
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebra as in the conclusion of Theorem
4.1
.
Consider the resulting equivariant
*
-homomorphism

\bar{\Psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)\cap C^{\prime}\to\mathscr{M}(A)_{\infty}.

Notice that by construction, one has
\bar{\Psi}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathscr{K}}\otimes a)=a
for all
a\in\mathscr{M}(A)
.
Since we assumed that
\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)
with the induced action is
\gamma
-saturated, it follows that there exists a unital equivariant
*
-homomorphism
\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{K}\otimes A)\cap(C\cup(\mathbf{1}\otimes A))^{\prime}
.
The composition with
\bar{\Psi}
yields a unital equivariant
*
-homomorphism
\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{M}(A)_{\infty}\cap A^{\prime}
.
If we compose this with the quotient map onto
(\mathscr{M}(A)_{\infty}\cap A^{\prime})/(\mathscr{M}(A)_{\infty}\cap A^{\perp})
, then we see that the criterion in Theorem
1.5
is fulfilled.
Hence
\alpha
is
\gamma
-absorbing.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

If one were to work out the technical proofs to suitably generalize
[
4
, Proposition 1.7, Corollary 1.10]
to the dynamical context, then Corollary
4.2
would also generalize in a straightforward way.
Namely we would not have to assume that
A
is separable and the conclusion would yield that
\alpha
is separably
\gamma
-stable.
Since we are currently not aware of any interesting applications of this more general observation, we shall not work out its proof here, but nevertheless wish to point out how to get there if it should be of interest to the reader in their own work.

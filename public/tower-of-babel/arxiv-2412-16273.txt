Compatible anti-pre-Lie algebras
Normatov Z

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of compatible anti-pre-Lie algebras and study relationship between them and the related structures such as anti- 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators, commutative 2 2 -cocycles on compatible Lie algebras. Moreover, we give the classification of 2-dimensional compatible anti-pre-Lie algebras from the classification of anti-pre-Lie algebras of the same dimension.

Introduction

The notion of a pre-Lie algebra was introduced independently by Gerstenhaber
[
10

[
14

[

a pre-Lie algebra

gives rise to a Lie algebra
(A,[-,-])
via the commutator bracket, which
is called the subadjacent Lie algebra and denoted by

An anti-pre-Lie algebra is a type of algebraic structure closely related to pre-Lie algebras, but with a reversed associativity condition and one extra relation. The notion of this type algebra is introduced by G. Liu G. and C. Bai in
[
16
]
. In a pre-Lie algebra, the associator satisfies a specific symmetry when two elements are swapped, whereas in an anti-pre-Lie algebra, this symmetry condition is reversed. Similar to pre-Lie algebras, the commutator in an anti-pre-Lie algebra forms a Lie algebra.

Compatible algebraic structures are quite much interest in the sense that linear combinations of multiplications defined on the same two algebraic structures are still of the same kind of algebraic structures. Compatible algebraic structures have
been widely studied in mathematics and mathematical physics. For example, compatible associative algebras were studied in connection with Cartan matrices of affine Dynkin diagrams,
integrable matrix equations, infinitesimal bialgebras and

and

The notion of a compatible pre-Lie algebra was introduced by Wu and Bai in

algebras have a close relation with the classical Yang–Baxter equation in compatible

2.
Preliminaries and Notions

There is an “anti-structure” for pre-Lie algebras, namely anti-pre-Lie algebras, which is charecterized as Lie-admissible algebras whose negative left multiplication operators give representations of the commutator Lie algebras. By the motivation of anti-pre-Lie algebras, in this section, we introduce the notion of compatible anti-pre-Lie algebras as a class of compatible Lie-admissible algebras whose negative left multiplication operators make representations of the commutator compatible Lie algebras.

Recall that
(A,\ast)
is called a
Lie-admissible algebra
, where
A
is a vector space with a bilinear operation

\ast:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
, if the commutator operation
[-,-]:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
makes
(A,[-,-])
a Lie algebra, where
[x,y]=x\ast y-y\ast x
. In this case,
(A,[-,-])
is called the
sub-adjacent Lie algebra
of
(A,\ast)
denoted by
\mathfrak{g}(A)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a vector space with a bilinear operation
\ast
.
(A,\ast)
is called a pre-Lie algebra if it satisfies the identity

(x\ast y)\ast z-x\ast(y\ast z)=(y\ast x)\ast z-y\ast(x\ast z),\quad\forall x,y,z\in A.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a vector space with a bilinear operation
\ast
.
(A,\ast)
is called an anti-pre-Lie algebra if it satisfies the identities

(2.1)

\displaystyle x\ast(y\ast z)-y\ast(x\ast z)=[y,x]\ast z,

(2.2)

\displaystyle[x,y]\ast z+[y,z]\ast x+[z,x]\ast y=0,\quad\forall x,y,z\in A,

where the bilinear operation
[-,-]
is a commutator i.e.
[x,y]=x\ast y-y\ast x
.

Remark 2.3
.

Define
\mathcal{L}_{\ast}
as a left multiplication operator. A pre-Lie algebra
(A,\ast)
is a Lie-admissible algebra such that
(\mathcal{L}_{\ast},A)
is a representation of the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(A)
while an anti-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\ast)
is a Lie-admissible algebra such that
(-\mathcal{L}_{\ast},A)
is a representation of the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(A)
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{2})
be two
Lie algebras. They are called compatible if
for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, the following bilinear operation

[x,y]=k_{1}[x,y]_{1}+k_{2}[x,y]_{2},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

defines a Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.
We denote
two compatible Lie algebras by
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
and call

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
a compatible Lie algebra.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
24
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{2})
be two
Lie algebras. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
is a compatible Lie algebra
if and only if for any
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, the following equation holds

(2.3)

[[x,y]_{1},z]_{2}+[[y,z]_{1},x]_{2}+[[z,x]_{1},y]_{2}+[[x,y]_{2},z]_{1}+[[y,z]_{2},x]_{1}+[[z,x]_{2},y]_{1}=0.

Definition 2.6
.

A representation of a compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
on a vector space
V
is a pair of linear maps
\rho,\mu:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow gl(V)
such that for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,

k_{1}\rho+k_{2}\mu
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},k_{1}[-,-]_{1}+k_{2}[-,-]_{2})
. We denote it by
(\rho,\mu,V)
.

Proposition 2.7
.

[
24
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
be a compatible Lie
algebra. Let
V
be a vector space and
\rho,\mu:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow gl(V)
be
a pair of linear maps. Then the following conditions are
equivalent:

(i)

(\rho,\mu,V)
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
on the
vector space
V
.

(ii)

For any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, the following three equations hold

(2.4)

\displaystyle 1.\quad\rho([x,y]_{1})=\rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(y)\rho(x),

(2.5)

\displaystyle 2.\quad\mu([x,y]_{2})=\mu(x)\mu(y)-\mu(y)\mu(x),

(2.6)

\displaystyle 3.\quad\rho([x,y]_{2})+\mu([x,y]_{1})=\rho(x)\mu(y)-\rho(y)\mu(x)+\mu(x)\rho(y)-\mu(y)\rho(x).

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(A,\circ)
and
(A,\ast)
be two anti-pre-Lie algebras. They are called
compatible
if for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, the following bilinear operation

(2.7)

x\star y=k_{1}x\circ y+k_{2}x\ast y,\quad\forall x,y\in A,

defines an anti-pre-Lie algebra structure on
A
.
We denote
the two compatible anti-pre-Lie algebras by
(A,\circ,\ast)
and call

(A,\circ,\ast)
a
compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
(A,\circ)
and
(A,\ast)
be two anti-pre-Lie algebras. Then
(A,\circ,\ast)
is a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra if and only if for any
x,y,z\in A
, the following equalities hold:

(2.8)

\displaystyle 1.\quad x\circ(y\ast z)+x\ast(y\circ z)-y\circ(x\ast z)-y\ast(x\circ z)=[y,x]_{2}\circ z+[y,x]_{1}\ast z,

(2.9)

\displaystyle 2.\quad[x,y]_{2}\circ z+[x,y]_{1}\ast z+[y,z]_{2}\circ x+[y,z]_{1}\ast x+[z,x]_{2}\circ y+[z,x]_{1}\ast y=0,

where

(2.10)

[x,y]_{1}=x\circ y-y\circ x\quad\text{and}\quad[x,y]_{2}=x\ast y-y\ast x.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in A
. By (
2.7
) and (
2.1
) in the definition of anti-pre-Lie algebras, we have

x\star(y\star z)-y\star(x\star z)=(x\star y-y\star x)\star z.

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle LHS=

\displaystyle k_{1}^{2}(x\circ(y\circ z)-y\circ(x\circ z))+k_{2}^{2}(x\ast(y\ast z)-y\ast(x\ast z))

\displaystyle+k_{1}k_{2}(x\circ(y\ast z)+x\ast(y\circ z)-y\circ(x\ast z)-y\ast(x\circ z)),

\displaystyle RHS=

\displaystyle k_{1}^{2}((y\circ x)\circ z-(x\circ y)\circ z)+k_{2}^{2}((y\ast x)\ast z-(x\ast y)\ast z)

\displaystyle+k_{1}k_{2}((y\ast x)\circ z+(y\circ x)\ast z-(x\ast y)\circ z-(x\circ y)\ast z).

By equalizing we get (
2.8
). Similarly, by considering (
2.2

2.9
).

Let (
2.8
) and (
2.9

repeating the same calculation in the other direction one can show that
(A,\circ,\ast)
is a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

Let
(A,\circ,\ast)
be a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra and

\begin{split}&x\circ[y,z]_{2}+x\ast[y,z]_{1}+y\circ[z,x]_{2}+y\ast[z,x]_{1}+z\circ[x,y]_{2}+z\ast[x,y]_{1}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{commutators}}}{{=}}&x\circ(y\ast z)-x\circ(z\ast y)+x\ast(y\circ z)-x\ast(z\circ y)+y\circ(z\ast x)-y\circ(x\ast z)\\
&+y\ast(z\circ x)-y\ast(x\circ z)+z\circ(x\ast y)-z\circ(y\ast x)+z\ast(x\circ y)-z\ast(y\circ x)\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{commutators}}}{{=}}&[y,x]_{2}\circ z+[y,x]_{1}\ast z+[x,z]_{2}\circ y+[x,z]_{1}\ast y+[z,y]_{2}\circ x+[z,y]_{1}\ast x.\end{split}

Thus we can interchange (

Let

be an anti-pre-Lie algebra and

Let

Definition 2.13
.

A compatible associative algebra is a triple

.

Let

3.
Compatible anti-pre-Lie algebras and anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operators on compatible Lie algebras.

In this section we study anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operators to interpret compatible anti-pre-Lie algebras. There is a structure of a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra on
the representation space inducing from a strong anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a compatible Lie algebra and in particular,
the existence of an invertible anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator gives an equivalent condition for a compatible Lie algebra having a structure of a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra.

Definition 3.1
.

[
9
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
be a Lie algebra and
(\rho,V)
be a
representation. A linear map
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called an

anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator

associated to
(\rho,V)
if
T
satisfies

[T(u),T(v)]=T(\rho(T(v))u-\rho(T(u))v),\forall u,v\in V.

Moreover, anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
is called strong if
T
satisfies

\rho([T(u),T(v)])w+\rho([T(v),T(w)])u+\rho([T(w),T(u)])v=0,\quad\forall\ u,v,w\in V.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
be a compatible Lie algebra and
(\rho,\mu,V)
be a
representation. A linear map
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called an

anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator

associated to
(\rho,\mu,V)
if for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,
T
is an anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},k_{1}[-,-]_{1}+k_{2}[-,-]_{2})
associated to the representation
k_{1}\rho+k_{2}\mu
.

An anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
associated to the adjoint representation
(\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{g})
which is defined by
\mathrm{ad}(x)(y)=[x,y]
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}

is called an anti-Rota-Baxter operator, that is,
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a linear map satisfying

[R(x),R(y)]=R([R(y),x]+[y,R(x)]),\quad\forall\ x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

An anti-Rota-Baxter operator
R
is called strong if
R
satisfies

(3.1)

[[R(x),R(y)],z]+[[R(y),R(z)],x]+[[R(z),R(x)],y]=0,\quad\forall\ x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
be a compatible Lie
algebra and
(\rho,\mu,V):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow gl(V)
be a representation.
Let
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be an anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator associated to
(\rho,\mu,V)

(3.2)

u\circ v=-\rho(T(u))v,\quad u\ast v=-\mu(T(u))v,\quad\forall\ u,v\in V.

satisfies (
2.8

(V,\circ,\ast)
is a compatible Lie-admissible algebra such that

is a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra if and only if
T
is strong.

Furthermore,

is a homomorphism of compatible Lie algebras from the sub-adjacent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(V)
to
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
. Moreover, there is an induced compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra structure on

given by

(3.3)

T(u)\circ T(v)=T(u\circ v),\quad T(u)\ast T(v)=T(u\ast v),\quad\forall\ u,v\in V,

and

is a homomorphism of compatible anti-pre-Lie algebras.

Proof.

By Proposition 2.14 in
[
16
]

T
is a strong anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator of

Let

\begin{split}[u,v]_{2}\circ w+[u,v]_{1}\ast w=&(\mu(T(v))u-\mu(T(u))v)\circ w+(\rho(T(v))u-\rho(T(u))v)\ast w\\
=&-\rho(T(\mu(T(v))u))w+\rho(T(\mu(T(u))v))w-\mu(T(\rho(T(v))u))w+\mu(T(\rho(T(u))v))w\\
=&\rho([T(v),T(u)]_{2})w+\mu([T(v),T(u)]_{1})w\\

Thus (

\begin{split}&[u,v]_{2}\circ w+[u,v]_{1}\ast w+[v,w]_{2}\circ u+[v,w]_{1}\ast u+[w,u]_{2}\circ v+[w,u]_{1}\ast v\\

&+\mu([T(w),T(v)]_{1})u+\rho([T(u),T(w)]_{2})v+\mu([T(u),T(w)]_{1})v\\

&+k_{1}k_{2}\rho([T(u),T(v)]_{2})w+k_{1}k_{2}\rho([T(v),T(w)]_{2})u+k_{1}k_{2}\rho([T(w),T(u)]_{2})v\\
&+k_{1}k_{2}\mu([T(u),T(v)]_{1})w+k_{1}k_{2}\mu([T(v),T(w)]_{1})u+k_{1}k_{2}\mu([T(w),T(u)]_{1})v\\
&+k_{2}^{2}\mu([T(u),T(v)]_{2})w+k_{2}^{2}\mu([T(v),T(w)]_{2})u+k_{2}^{2}\mu([T(w),T(u)]_{2})v\Big)\\
=&\frac{1}{k_{1}k_{2}}\Big((k_{1}\rho+k_{2}\mu)([T(u),T(v)]_{k_{1}1+k_{2}2})w+(k_{1}\rho+k_{2}\mu)([T(v),T(w)]_{k_{1}1+k_{2}2})u\\
&+(k_{1}\rho+k_{2}\mu)([T(w),T(u)]_{k_{1}1+k_{2}2})v\Big)\\
=&0.\end{split}

Note that this is also true for
k_{1}k_{2}=0
. The other parts are straightforward.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
be a compatible Lie algebra and
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a strong anti-Rota-Baxter
operator. Then

(3.4)

x\circ y=-[R(x),y]_{1},\quad x\ast y=-[R(x),y]_{2},\quad\forall\ x,y\in\mathfrak{g}

defines a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\circ,\ast)
. Conversely, if
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a linear transformation on a compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
such that (
3.4
) defines a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra, then
R
satisfies (
3.1
) and the following equation:

[[R(x),R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+R([x,R(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[R(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),z]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0,\quad\forall\ x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}

Proof.

By setting
\rho=\mathrm{ad}_{1},\mu=\mathrm{ad}_{2}
, the first part follows from above Proposition. The second part comes from the definitions and Corollary 2.15 in
[
16
]
.
∎

Now we consider invertible anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operators.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
be a compatible Lie algebra and
(\rho,\mu,\mathfrak{g})
be its representation. Then an invertible anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator associated to
(\rho,\mu,\mathfrak{g})
is strong.

Proof.

Let
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be an anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator of a compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
associated to a representation
(\rho,\mu,V)
. Define bilinear operations
\circ,\ast:V\otimes V\rightarrow V
by (
3.2
). Then by choosing
k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0
and
k_{1}=0,k_{2}=1
we know
T
is a strong anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator for
(V,[-,-]_{1})
and
(V,[-,-]_{2})
such that
(V,\circ)
and
(V,\ast)
are anti-pre-Lie algebras (see, Proposition 2.17,
[
16
]
). For all
u,v\in V
we have

\begin{split}[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}}=&k_{1}[u,v]_{1}+k_{2}[u,v]_{2}\\
=&k_{1}(\rho(T(v))u-\rho(T(u))v)+k_{2}(\mu(T(v))u-\mu(T(u))v)\\
=&(k_{1}\rho+k_{2}\mu)(T(v))u-(k_{1}\rho+k_{2}\mu)(T(u))v\\
=&T^{-1}([T(u),T(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}).\end{split}

Therefore, for all
u,v,w\in V
, we have

\begin{split}[[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}},w]_{\mathfrak{g}}=&[T^{-1}([T(u),T(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}),w]_{\mathfrak{g}}\\
=&T^{-1}([[T(u),T(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}},T(w)]_{\mathfrak{g}})\end{split}

This implies
(V,[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a Lie algebra as well as
(V,[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
is a compatible Lie algebra such that
(V,\circ,\ast)
is compatible Lie admissible. Then
T
is strong due to Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
be a compatible Lie algebra. Then there is a structure of a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra on
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if there exists an invertible anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\circ,\ast)
be a structure of a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra on
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
. Then

\begin{split}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=&k_{1}[x,y]_{1}+k_{2}[x,y]_{2}\\
=&k_{1}(x\circ y-y\circ x)+k_{2}(x\ast y-y\ast x)\\
=&k_{1}(-\mathcal{L}_{\circ}(y)-(-\mathcal{L}_{\circ}(x))y)+k_{2}(-\mathcal{L}_{\ast}(y)-(-\mathcal{L}_{\ast}(x))y)\end{split}

Thus
T=\mathrm{id}
is an invertible anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
associated to
(-\mathcal{L}_{\circ},-\mathcal{L}_{\ast},\mathfrak{g})
.

Conversely, suppose that
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is an invertible anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
associated to
(\rho,\mu,V)
. Then by Proposition
3.5
,
T
is strong. Also, there is a structure of a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra on the underlying vector space of
\mathfrak{g}
given by (
3.3
) by Proposition
3.3
. Explicitly, for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, there exist
u,v\in V
such that
x=T(u),y=T(v)
. Hence,

\begin{split}&x\circ_{\mathfrak{g}}y=T(u)\circ_{\mathfrak{g}}T(v)=T(u\circ v)=-T(\rho(x)T^{-1}(y)),\quad\forall\ x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\\
&x\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}y=T(u)\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}T(v)=T(u\ast v)=-T(\mu(x)T^{-1}(y)),\quad\forall\ x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.\end{split}

Furthermore, we have

\begin{split}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=&[T(u),T(v)]\\
=&T((k_{1}\rho+k_{2}\mu)(T(v))u-(k_{1}\rho+k_{2}\mu)(T(u))v)\\
=&k_{1}T(\rho(y)T^{-1}(x)-\rho(x)T^{-1}(y))+k_{2}T(\mu(y)T^{-1}(x)-\mu(x)T^{-1}(y))\\
=&k_{1}(x\circ_{\mathfrak{g}}y-y\circ_{\mathfrak{g}}x)+k_{2}(x\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}y-y\ast_{\mathfrak{g}}x).\end{split}

Thus
(\mathfrak{g},\circ,\ast)
is a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra whose sub-adjacent Lie algebra is
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
.
∎

4.
Compatible anti-pre-Lie algebras and commmutative
2
-cocycles on compatible Lie algebras

Let us recall the notion of commutative
2
-cocycles on Lie algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

[
8
]

A commutative
2
-cocycle
\mathcal{B}
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
is a symmetric
bilinear form such that

\mathcal{B}([x,y],z)+\mathcal{B}([y,z],x)+\mathcal{B}([z,x],y)=0,\quad\forall\ x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 4.2
.

[
24
]

Let
(\rho,\mu,V)
be a representation of a
compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
. Then
(\rho^{*},\mu^{*},V^{*})

is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
, which is called the
dual representation of
(\rho,\mu,V)
, where
\rho^{*}
and
\mu^{*}
are given by

\begin{split}&\langle\rho^{*}(x)a^{*},b\rangle=-\langle a^{*},\rho(x)b\rangle,\quad\forall\ x\in\mathfrak{g},\ a^{*}\in V^{*},\ b\in V,\\
&\langle\mu^{*}(x)a^{*},b\rangle=-\langle a^{*},\mu(x)b\rangle,\quad\forall\ x\in\mathfrak{g},\ a^{*}\in V^{*},\ b\in V.\end{split}

In particular,
(\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{1},\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{2},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is the dual representation of the representation
(\mathrm{ad}_{1},\mathrm{ad}_{2},\mathfrak{g})
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a nondegenerate commutative
2
-cocycle on a compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
. Then there exists a structure of a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra on
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
given by

\mathcal{B}(x\circ y,z)=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{1}),\quad\mathcal{B}(x\ast y,z)=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{2}),\quad\forall\ x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

Define a linear map
T:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by

\langle T(x),y\rangle=\mathcal{B}(x,y),\quad\forall\ x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
T
is invertible by the nondegeneracy of
\mathcal{B}
. Moreover, for any
a^{*},b^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, there exist
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
a^{*}=T(x),b^{*}=T(y)
. Then for any
z\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z)=&\langle T([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),z\rangle\\
=&\langle T([T^{-1}(a^{*}),T^{-1}(b^{*})]_{\mathfrak{g}}),z\rangle\end{split}

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}([y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},x)=&\mathcal{B}(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})\\
=&\langle T(x),k_{1}[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}1}+k_{2}[y,z]_{2}\rangle\\
=&k_{1}\langle T(x),\mathrm{ad}_{1}(y)(z)\rangle+k_{2}\langle T(x),\mathrm{ad}_{2}(y)(z)\rangle\\
=&-k_{1}\langle\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{1}(y)T(x),z\rangle-k_{2}\langle\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{2}(y)T(x),z\rangle,\end{split}

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}([z,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},y)=&\mathcal{B}(y,[z,x]_{\mathfrak{g}})\\
=&\langle T(y),k_{1}[z,x]_{\mathfrak{g}1}+k_{2}[z,x]_{2}\rangle\\
=&-k_{1}\langle T(y),\mathrm{ad}_{1}(x)(z)\rangle-k_{2}\langle T(y),\mathrm{ad}_{2}(x)(z)\rangle\\
=&k_{1}\langle\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{1}(x)T(y),z\rangle+k_{2}\langle\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{2}(x)T(y),z\rangle.\end{split}

Since
\mathcal{B}
is a commutative
2
-cocycle, we have

\begin{split}[T^{-1}(a^{*}),T^{-1}(b^{*})]_{\mathfrak{g}}=&T^{-1}(k_{1}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{1}(x)T(y)+k_{2}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{2}(x)T(y)-k_{1}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{1}(y)T(x)-k_{2}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{2}(y)T(x))\\
=&T^{-1}((k_{1}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{1}+k_{2}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{2})(x)T(y)-(k_{1}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{1}+k_{2}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{2})(y)T(x))\end{split}

Thus
T^{-1}
is an anti-
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
associated to
(\mathrm{ad}_{1}^{*},\mathrm{ad}_{2}^{*},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. By Corollary 2.18, there is a structure of a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra on
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
given by

x\circ y=-T^{-1}(\mathrm{ad}_{1}^{*}(x)T(y)),\quad x\ast y=-T^{-1}(\mathrm{ad}_{2}^{*}(x)T(y)),\quad\forall\ x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

which gives

\mathcal{B}(x\circ y,z)=\langle T(x\circ y),z\rangle=-\langle\mathrm{ad}_{1}^{*}(x)T(y),z\rangle=\langle T(y),[x,z]\rangle=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{1}),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

\mathcal{B}(x\ast y,z)=\langle T(x\ast y),z\rangle=-\langle\mathrm{ad}_{2}^{*}(x)T(y),z\rangle=\langle T(y),[x,z]\rangle=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{2}),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Furthermore, we have

\mathcal{B}(k_{1}x\circ y+k_{2}x\ast y,z)=\mathcal{B}(y,k_{1}[x,z]_{1}+k_{2}[x,z]_{2}),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Hence, the conclusion holds.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

A bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
on an anti-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\circ)
is called invariant if

(4.1)

\mathcal{B}(x\circ y,z)=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]),\quad\forall\ x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}

holds.

Note that according to the definition of a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra, if a bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
is invariant on
(A,\circ,\ast)
, then it is also invariant on
(A,\circ)
and
(A,\ast)
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Any symmetric invariant bilinear form on a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra

(A,\circ,\ast)
is a commutative
2
-cocycle on the sub-adjacent compatible Lie
algebra
(\mathfrak{g}(A),[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
. Conversely, a nondegenerate
commutative
2
-cocycle on a compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
is
invariant on a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra given by

(4.2)

\mathcal{B}(x\circ y,z)=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{1}),\quad\mathcal{B}(x\ast y,z)=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{2})\quad\forall\ x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}

Proof.

For the first half part, by (
4.1
), we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z)=&\mathcal{B}(k_{1}[x,y]_{1}+k_{2}[x,y]_{2},z)\\
=&k_{1}\mathcal{B}([x,y]_{1})+k_{2}\mathcal{B}([x,y]_{2},z)\\
=&k_{1}(\mathcal{B}(x\circ y,z)-\mathcal{B}(y\circ x,z))+k_{2}(\mathcal{B}(x\ast y,z)-\mathcal{B}(y\ast x,z))\\
=&k_{1}(\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{1})-\mathcal{B}(x,[y,z]_{1}))+k_{2}(\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{2})-\mathcal{B}(x,[y,z]_{2}))\\
=&\mathcal{B}(y,k_{1}[x,z]_{1}+k_{2}[x,z]_{2})-\mathcal{B}(x,k_{1}[y,z]_{1}+[y,z]_{2})\\
=&\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})-\mathcal{B}(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})\end{split}

Thus
\mathcal{B}
is a commutative
2
-cocycle on the sub-adjacent compatible
Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}(A),[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
. The second half part follows
from Theorem
4.3
.
∎

Recall that two representations
(\rho_{1},V_{1})
and

(\rho_{2},V_{2})
of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
are called

equivalent
if there is a linear isomorphism

\varphi:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
such that

\varphi(\rho_{1}(x)v)=\rho_{2}(x)\varphi(v),\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},v\in V_{1}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(A,\circ,\ast)
be a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra. Then there is a
nondegenerate invariant bilinear form on
(A,\circ,\ast)
if and only
if
(-\mathcal{L}_{\circ},-\mathcal{L}_{\ast},A)
and
(\mathrm{ad}_{1}^{*},\mathrm{ad}_{2}^{*},A^{*})
are
equivalent as representations of the sub-adjacent compatible Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g}(A),[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{*}
is a linear isomorphism
satisfying

\varphi(-(k_{1}\mathcal{L}_{\circ}(x)+k_{2}\mathcal{L}_{\circ}(x))y)=(k_{1}{\rm ad}_{1}^{*}(x)+k_{2}{\rm ad}_{2}^{*}(x))\varphi(y),\;\;\forall x,y\in A.

Define a nondegenerate
bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
on
A
by

(4.3)

\mathcal{B}(x,y)=\langle\varphi(x),y\rangle,\;\;\forall x,y\in A.

Then we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}(k_{1}x\circ y+k_{2}x\ast y,z)=&\langle\varphi(-(k_{1}\mathcal{L}_{\circ}(x)+k_{2}\mathcal{L}_{\circ}(x))y),z\rangle\\
=&-\langle(k_{1}{\rm ad}_{1}^{*}(x)+k_{2}{\rm ad}_{2}^{*}(x))\varphi(y),z\rangle\\
=&-k_{1}\langle{\rm ad}_{1}^{*}(x)\varphi(y),z\rangle-k_{2}\langle{\rm ad}_{2}^{*}(x)\varphi(y),z\rangle\\
=&k_{1}\langle\varphi(y),[x,z]_{1}\rangle+k_{2}\langle\varphi(y),[x,z]_{2}\rangle\\
=&\langle\varphi(y),k_{1}[x,z]_{1}+k_{2}[x,z]_{2}\rangle\\
=&\langle\varphi(y),[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle\\
=&\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\;\;\forall x,y,z\in A.\end{split}

Thus
\mathcal{B}
is invariant on
(A,\circ,\ast)
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathcal{B}
is a nondegenerate
invariant bilinear form on
(A,\circ,\ast)
. So is
\mathcal{B}
on
(A,\circ)
and
(A,\ast)
. Then by Proposition 2.24 in
[
16
]
,
(-\mathcal{L}_{\circ},A)
and
({\rm ad}_{1}^{*},A)
are equivalent. So are
(-\mathcal{L}_{\ast},A)
and
({\rm ad}_{2}^{*},A)
. Define a linear map

\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{*}
by Eq. (
4.3
). Then by a similar proof as above, we show that

\varphi
gives an equivalence between
(-\mathcal{L}_{\circ},-\mathcal{L}_{\ast},A)

and
(\mathrm{ad}_{1}^{*},\mathrm{ad}_{2}^{*},A^{*})
as representations of
(\mathfrak{g}(A),[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
.
∎

From Proposition
4.6
, Corollary
4.5
and their proofs, we can conclude that for a compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
, if there is a nondegenerate commutative
2
-cocycle
on
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
, then there is a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\circ,\ast)
given by
Eq. (
4.2
). Moreover,
(-\mathcal{L}_{\circ},-\mathcal{L}_{\ast},\mathfrak{g})
and
(\mathrm{ad}_{1}^{*},\mathrm{ad}_{2}^{*},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
are
equivalent as representations of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
. Conversely, if
(\mathfrak{g},\circ,\ast)
is a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra such that

(-\mathcal{L}_{\circ},-\mathcal{L}_{\ast},\mathfrak{g})
and
(\mathrm{ad}_{1}^{*},\mathrm{ad}_{2}^{*},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
are
equivalent as representations of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
, then there is a nondegenerate bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
satisfying

\mathcal{B}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z)+\mathcal{B}(y,[z,x]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\mathcal{B}(x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})=0,\;\;\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
(\rho,\mu,V)
be a representation of a compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
. Then there is a Lie algebra structure on the direct
sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
of vector spaces which is called the
semi-direct
product
(see
[
24
]
) defined by

\begin{split}&[x+u,y+v]_{1}=[x,y]_{1}+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u,\\
&[x+u,y+v]_{2}=[x,y]_{2}+\mu(x)v-\mu(y)u,\;\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in V.\end{split}

It is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\mu}V
. Furthermore, there is the
following construction of
nondegenerate commutative
2
-cocycles from compatible anti-pre-Lie algebras.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(A,\circ,\ast)
be a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra and
(A,[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
be the sub-adjacent compatible Lie algebra. Define a bilinear
form
\mathcal{B}
on
A\oplus A^{*}
by

(4.4)

\mathcal{B}(x+a^{*},y+b^{*})=\langle x,b^{*}\rangle+\langle a^{*},y\rangle,\forall x,y\in A,a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}.

Then
\mathcal{B}
is a nondegenerate commutative
2
-cocycle on the
Lie algebra
A\ltimes_{-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\circ},-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\ast}}A^{*}
.
Conversely, let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
be a compatible Lie algebra and

(\rho,\mu,\mathfrak{g}^{*})
be a representation. Suppose that the bilinear
form given by Eq. (
4.4
) is a commutative
2
-cocycle on
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\mu}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then there is a structure of a compatible
anti-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\circ,\ast)
on
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{1},[-,-]_{2})
such that
\rho=-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\circ},\mu=-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\ast}
.

Proof.

By Proposition 2.26 in
[
16
]

\mathcal{B}
is a nondegenerate commutative
2
-cocycle on the Lie algebras
A\ltimes_{-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\circ}}A^{*}
and
A\ltimes_{-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\ast}}A^{*}
. Let
x,y,z\in A,a^{*},b^{*},c^{*}\in A^{*}
. Then
we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}([x+a^{*},y+b^{*}]_{A},z+c^{*})=&\mathcal{B}([x,y]_{A}-k_{1}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\circ}(x)b^{*}+k_{1}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\circ}(y)a^{*}-k_{2}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\ast}(x)b^{*}+k_{2}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\ast}(y)a^{*},z+c^{*})\\
=&k_{1}\langle[x,y]_{1},c^{*}\rangle-\langle k_{1}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\circ}(x)b^{*},z\rangle+\langle k_{1}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\circ}(y)a^{*},z\rangle\\
&+k_{2}\langle[x,y]_{2},c^{*}\rangle-\langle k_{2}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\ast}(x)b^{*},z\rangle+\langle k_{2}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\ast}(y)a^{*},z\rangle\\
=&k_{1}(\langle[x,y]_{A},c^{*}\rangle+\langle b^{*},x\circ z\rangle-\langle a^{*},y\circ z\rangle)\\
&+k_{2}(\langle[x,y]_{2},c^{*}\rangle+\langle b^{*},x\ast z\rangle-\langle a^{*},y\ast z\rangle)\end{split}

Similarly,

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}([y+b^{*},z+c^{*}]_{A},x+a^{*})=&k_{1}(\langle[y,z]_{1},a^{*}\rangle+\langle c^{*},y\circ x\rangle-\langle b^{*},z\circ x\rangle)\\
&+k_{2}(\langle[y,z]_{2},a^{*}\rangle+\langle c^{*},y\ast x\rangle-\langle b^{*},z\ast x\rangle),\\
\mathcal{B}([z+c^{*},x+a^{*}]_{A},y+b^{*})=&k_{1}(\langle[z,x]_{1},b^{*}\rangle+\langle a^{*},z\circ y\rangle-\langle c^{*},x\circ y\rangle)\\
&+k_{2}(\langle[z,x]_{2},b^{*}\rangle+\langle a^{*},z\ast y\rangle-\langle c^{*},x\ast y\rangle).\end{split}

Thus

\mathcal{B}([x+a^{*},y+b^{*}]_{A},z+c^{*})+\mathcal{B}([y+b^{*},z+c^{*}]_{A},x+a^{*})+\mathcal{B}([z+c^{*},x+a^{*}]_{A},y+b^{*})=0.

Hence
\mathcal{B}
is a commutative
2
-cocycle on the Lie algebra
A\ltimes_{-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\circ},-\mathcal{L}_{\ast}}A^{*}
.

Conversely, by Theorem
4.3
, there is a structure of a compatible anti-pre-Lie algebra
given by bilinear operations
\circ
and
\ast
on
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\mu}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, repectively, defined by
Eq. (
4.2
). In particular, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}(x\circ y,z)&=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{1})=0,\\
\mathcal{B}(x\ast y,z)&=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,z]_{2})=0,\;\;\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.\end{split}

So for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
,
x\circ y,x\ast y\in\mathfrak{g}
and thus
(\mathfrak{g},\circ,\ast)
is a compatible anti-pre-Lie
algebra. Moreover,

\langle y,-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\circ}(x)a^{*}\rangle=\langle x\circ y,a^{*}\rangle=\mathcal{B}(x\circ y,a^{*})=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,a^{*}]_{1})=\langle y,\rho(x)a^{*}\rangle,\;\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},a^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Hence

.

Similarly,

\langle y,-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\ast}(x)a^{*}\rangle=\langle x\ast y,a^{*}\rangle=\mathcal{B}(x\ast y,a^{*})=\mathcal{B}(y,[x,a^{*}]_{2})=\langle y,\mu(x)a^{*}\rangle,\;\;\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},a^{*}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Hence
\mu=-\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\ast}
.
∎

Now, let us recall the method to classify compatible algebras.

Let
(A,\circ)
be an algebra. Then the method consists of four steps:

Step 1.
Compute a set
\mathrm{Z}^{2}(A,A)
of all bilinear maps
\phi:A\times A\rightarrow A
satisfying some necessary equalities. In our case they are

i.

\displaystyle\phi(x,\phi(y,z))-\phi(y,\phi(x,z))=\phi(\phi(y,x),z)-\phi(\phi(x,y),z),

ii.

\displaystyle\phi(\phi(x,y),z)-\phi(\phi(y,x),z)+cyclic=0,

iii.

\displaystyle x\circ\phi(y,z)+\phi(x,y\circ z)-y\circ\phi(x,z)-\phi(y,x\circ z)=\phi(y,x)\circ z-\phi(x,y)\circ z+\phi(y\circ x,z)-\phi(x\circ y,z),

iv.

\displaystyle\phi(x,y)\circ z-\phi(y,x)\circ z+\phi(x\circ y,z)-\phi(y\circ x,z)+cyclic=0

Step 2.
Since
\phi=0\in\mathrm{Z}^{2}(A,A)
,
\mathrm{Z}^{2}(A,A)\neq\emptyset
. For

Then

Step 3.
Find the orbits of

Step 4.
Choose a representative

from each orbit to classify the constructed compatible
algebra

Abstract
. In this work, we study the inertial Kuramoto model, which is a second-order extension of the classical first-order Kuramoto model, as an inertial perturbation of the first-order Kuramoto model. We develop a quantitative Tikhonov theorem, from which we derive a new synchronization statement in the small inertia regime, with strong bounds on the limiting order parameter. We also explore the determinability of phase velocities from phase positions, which shows that the perturbation viewpoint must be limited to the small inertia regime. This paper complements our recent work (2025), where we established asymptotic phase-locking of inertial Kuramoto oscillators under generic initial conditions in the low inertia-high coupling regime.

2.1.
Symmetries of the inertial Kuramoto model

In this subsection, we recall several symmetries of the inertial Kuramoto model, as systematically organized in
[
18
]
.

(1)

(Galilean symmetry): the quantities
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})
,
\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})
,
\mathcal{D}(\Theta)
and
R(\Theta)
are invariant under the transformation: for
\nu
,
\omega
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\tilde{\nu}_{i}\mapsto\nu_{i}-\nu,\quad\tilde{\theta}_{i}^{0}\mapsto\theta_{i}^{0}-\theta,\quad\tilde{\omega}_{i}^{0}\mapsto\omega_{i}^{0}-\omega,\\
&\tilde{\theta}_{i}(t)\mapsto\theta_{i}(t)-\theta-m\omega\left(1-e^{-t/m}\right)-\nu\left(t-m+me^{-t/m}\right),\\
&\tilde{\omega}_{i}(t)\mapsto\omega_{i}(t)-\omega e^{-t/m}-\nu\left(1-e^{-t/m}\right).\end{aligned}

(2)

(Dilation symmetry): the ‘normalized intrinsic frequencies’
\nu_{i}/\kappa
, ‘normalized inertia’
m\kappa
, and ‘normalized initial velocites’
\omega_{i}^{0}/\kappa
are invariant under the time dilation symmetry: for fixed
\alpha>0
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\kappa\mapsto\alpha\kappa,\quad\nu_{i}\mapsto\alpha\nu_{i},\quad m\mapsto m/\alpha,\\
&\theta_{i}^{0}\mapsto\theta_{i}^{0},\quad\omega_{i}^{0}\mapsto\alpha\omega_{i}^{0},\quad\theta_{i}(t)\mapsto\theta_{i}(\alpha t),\quad\omega_{i}(t)\mapsto\alpha\omega_{i}(\alpha t),\end{aligned}

(3)

(Reflection symmetry): the quantities
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})
,
\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})
,
\mathcal{D}(\Theta)
and
R(\Theta)
are invariant under the following transformation:

(2.3)

\nu_{i}\mapsto-\nu_{i},\quad\theta^{0}_{i}\mapsto-\theta^{0}_{i},\quad\omega_{i}^{0}\mapsto-\omega_{i}^{0},\quad\theta_{i}(t)\mapsto-\theta_{i}(t),\quad\omega_{i}(t)\mapsto-\omega_{i}(t).

(4)

(Particle exchange symmetry): the quantities
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})
,
\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})
,
\mathcal{D}(\Theta)
and
R(\Theta)
are invariant under the following transformation:

(2.4)

\nu_{i}\mapsto\nu_{\pi^{-1}(i)},\quad\theta_{i}^{0}\mapsto\theta_{\pi^{-1}(i)}^{0},\quad\omega_{i}^{0}\mapsto\omega_{\pi^{-1}(i)}^{0}

for fixed
\pi\in S_{N}
(the symmetric group on
N
elements).

The Galilean symmetry is accompanied by the following conservation law, where we use the subscript
c
to denote the mean over all particles:

(2.5)

\theta_{c}^{0}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}^{0},\quad\theta_{c}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i},\quad\omega_{c}^{0}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}^{0},\quad\omega_{c}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i},\quad\nu_{c}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{i}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(\Theta,\Omega)
be a global solution to (
1.1
). The mean phase and frequency satisfy

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \theta_{c}(t)&=m\omega_{c}^{0}(1-e^{-t/m})+\nu_{c}\left(t-m+me^{-t/m}\right)+\theta_{c}^{0},\\
\omega_{c}(t)&=\omega_{c}^{0}e^{-t/m}+\nu_{c}\left(1-e^{-t/m}\right),\quad t\geq 0.\end{aligned}

Proof.

We sum both sides of (
1.1
) over
i
and apply the definitions of the means given in (
2.5
), yielding

{\dot{\theta}}_{c}=\omega_{c},\quad{\dot{\omega}}_{c}=\frac{\nu_{c}}{m}-\frac{\omega_{c}}{m},\quad t>0.

Integrating these ODEs, we obtain the relations (
2.6
).
∎

As pointed out in
[
18
]
, the quantities

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})/\kappa

It is therefore “natural” for the assumptions and conclusions of theorems concerning the Kuramoto model (

and parameters

and parameters

Another example lies in the notions of “phase-locked states” and “asymptotic phase-locking” introduced in Definition

below. These notions are equivalent to “equilibria” and “convergence to equilibria” after applying the Galilean transformation (

Definition 2.1

Let

(1)

is constant with respect to

(2)

(3)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\max_{i,j\in[N]}|{\dot{\theta}}_{i}(t)-{\dot{\theta}}_{j}(t)|=0.

(4)

(\Theta(t),{\dot{\Theta}}(t))
exhibits

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)\right)\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z},\quad\forall~i,j\in[N].

Remark 2.1
.

Below, we provide several comments on Definition
2.1
.

(1)

Asymptotic phase-locking implies complete frequency synchronization; one can easily see this from the Duhamel principle, later presented in (
2.8
). In fact, asymptotic phase-locking is equivalent to the condition that there exist
\theta_{1}^{\infty},\cdots,\theta_{N}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\theta_{i}(t)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{i}t\right)=\theta_{i}^{\infty},\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\dot{\theta}_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{i},\qquad i=1,\cdots,N.

(2)

Complete phase synchronization can happen only if
\nu_{i}=\nu_{j}
for all
i,j\in[N]
. Again, this is due to the Duhamel principle (
2.8
).

(3)

Phase-locked states for (
1.1
) coincide with phase-locked states for (
1.2
), up to the rotation of the circle, when
\omega^{0}_{i}\neq\omega_{j}^{0}
for some
i,j
.

Fixing all other parameters and increasing
\kappa
to sufficiently large values, phase-locked states emerge beyond a certain critical threshold
[
31
,
70
]
.

In this subsection, we discuss Duhamel’s principle for (

Again, system (

One advantage of this point of view is that it allows us to apply the Duhamel principle to

3.
Quantitative higher-order Tikhonov perturbation

In this section, we study quantitative estimates for the zero inertia limit of the inertial Kuramoto model.

In what follows, for initial data
\left(\Theta^{0},\Omega^{0}\right)
, intrinsic frequencies
\mathcal{V}
, a coupling strength
\kappa
and
m>0
, we denote by
\Theta(m,t)
,
t\geq 0
, the solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
), and denote by
\Theta(0,t)
,
t\geq 0
, the solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.2
).

We may first ask whether
\Theta(m,t)\to\Theta(0,t)
as
m\to 0
. This is true on finite time intervals away from zero: it is an immediate consequence of the Tikhonov theorem
[
66
,
69
]
that this convergence holds in the
C^{0}[0,\infty)
and
C^{1}(0,\infty)
Fréchet topologies (see Proposition
3.1
). By tedious computation, we will obtain a quantitative and higher-order version of Tikhonov’s theorem, namely a
C[0,\infty)\cap C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
convergence statement with explicit bounds (see Proposition
3.2
). By a comparison argument, we will obtain a qualitative version of the framework (
1.3
) without any explicit bounds on the parameters (see Theorem
1.2
).

Proposition 3.1

(Tikhonov’s theorem)
.

Let
(\Theta(m,t),\Omega(m,t))
and
(\Theta(0,t),\Omega(0,t))
be solutions to the Cauchy problems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), respectively with fixed initial conditions independent of
m
:

(\Theta(m,0),\Omega(m,0))=(\Theta^{0},\Omega^{0}),\quad\Theta(0,0)=\Theta^{0}.

Then, for all
i\in[N]
,
\theta_{i}(m,t)\to\theta_{i}(0,t)
as
m\to 0
in the Fréchet topologies
C^{0}[0,\infty)
and
C^{1}(0,\infty)
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(i)~\lim_{m\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\theta_{i}(m,t)-\theta_{i}(0,t)|=0,\quad\forall~T>0,\\
&(ii)~\lim_{m\to 0}\sup_{t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]}\left|\omega_{i}(m,t)-\omega_{i}(0,t)\right|=0,\quad\forall~T_{2}>T_{1}>0.\end{aligned}

Proof.

We set

\displaystyle F_{i}(\Theta(m,t))\coloneqq\nu_{i}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sin(\theta_{j}(m,t)-\theta_{i}(m,t)),\quad F=(F_{1},\cdots,F_{N}).

Then, the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) states that the vector
(\Theta(m,t),\Omega(m,t))
with
2N
entries is the unique solution to the ODE:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Theta(m,t)=\Omega(m,t),&\\
\displaystyle m\frac{d}{dt}\Omega(m,t)=-\Omega(m,t)+F(\Theta(m,t)),\end{cases}

with fixed initial conditions:

(\Theta(m,0),\Omega(m,0))=(\Theta^{0},\Omega^{0}).

Likewise, the vector
(\Theta(0,t),\Omega(0,t))
with
2N
entries is the unique solution to the ODE

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Theta(0,t)=\Omega(0,t),&\\
\displaystyle 0=-\Omega(0,t)+F(\Theta(0,t)),\end{cases}

with initial conditions:

(\Theta(0,0),\Omega(0,0))=(\Theta^{0},F(\Theta^{0})).

Tikhonov’s theorem
[
69
, Theorem 1.1]
tells us that in this formulation,
\Theta(m,t)\to\Theta(0,t)
as
m\to 0
on compact subintervals
[0,T]\subset[0,\infty)
, and
\Omega(m,t)\to\Omega(0,t)
as
m\to 0
on compact subintervals
[T^{\prime},T]\subset(0,\infty)
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

(1)

Note that Propsition
3.1
does not give quantitative rates of convergence. In order to prove asymptotic phase-locking results, one must have quantitative bounds in order to control the behavior for small times.

(2)

Proposition
3.1
cannot give uniform-in-time bounds, i.e., convergence in the uniform topology
L^{\infty}([0,\infty))
, because it is not true: for example, in the case
N=2
, compare the first-order equation for
\theta=\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}
:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{\theta}=1-0.5\sin\theta,\quad t>0,\\
\displaystyle\theta^{0}=0\end{cases}\quad\mbox{and}\qquad\begin{cases}\displaystyle 0.5\ddot{\theta}+\dot{\theta}=1-0.5\sin\theta,\quad t>0,\\
\displaystyle\theta^{0}=\dot{\theta}^{0}=0.\end{cases}

The difference of the two solutions diverges linearly in time as
t\to\infty
. This is not surprising, since two different dynamical systems with the same initial position data usually do not agree in the long term.

(3)

Proposition
3.1
cannot give uniform convergence on neighborhoods of
t=0
for the first derivative, i.e., it does not give convergence in the
C^{1}([0,\infty))
topology, because it is also not true: indeed, (
1.1
) prescribes the arbitrary value
\dot{\theta}_{i}(0)=\omega_{i}
, but (
1.2
) mandates that
\dot{\theta}_{i}(0)=\nu_{i}+\sum_{j}\sin(\theta_{j}^{0}-\theta_{i}^{0})
. This suggests that any approach to synchronization that approximates the second-order model (
1.1
) by the first-order model (
1.2
) should be done in a time regime away from
t=0
.

In the following proposition, we present a quantitative version of Proposition
3.1
. Specifically, we show that

\omega_{i}(m,t)-\omega_{i}(0,t)={\mathcal{O}}(e^{-t/m})+{\mathcal{O}}(m).

This estimate provides the rationale behind the “initial time layer” of the form
[0,\eta m]
introduced in
[
18
]
. In that work, analyses such as partial phase-locking and the quasi-monotonicity estimate for the order parameter were carried out after this initial time layer.
A key limitation of the classical Tikhonov theorem is its lack of quantitative bounds, which are necessary for establishing results such as Theorem
1.2
below. By working directly with the ODE systems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), we derive explicit estimates, as detailed in Proposition
3.2
. Furthermore, we demonstrate convergence of higher derivatives, namely convergence in the
C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
topology.

4.1.
A partial phase-locking result

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of partial phase-locking, following

Definition 4.1

(

(1)

The precise statement is given below in Theorem

\theta_{1}(t)=\cdots=\theta_{N_{1}}(t)=\frac{N_{2}}{N}\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}(t),~\theta_{N_{1}+1}(t)=\cdots=\theta_{N}(t)=-\frac{N_{1}}{N}\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}(t),\quad t\geq 0.

On the other hand, by the reflection symmetry (
2.3
), we have

\varphi_{i}(t)=-\theta_{i}(t),\quad i\in[N],~t\geq 0.

Therefore, the dynamics is one-dimensional, depending on the single variable
\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}(t)
. Its dynamics is governed by the following Cauchy problem to the second-order ODE:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle m\ddot{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}}+\dot{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}}=-\kappa\sin\left(\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}\right),\quad t>0,\\
\displaystyle\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}(0)=\eta,\quad\dot{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}}(0)=0.\end{cases}

In other words,
\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}
corresponds to the phase of a damped circular pendulum under a uniform gravitational field. As
\eta\to 0
,
\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}/\eta
approaches the solution
\theta
to the second-order linear ordinary differential equation

m\ddot{\theta}+\dot{\theta}=-\kappa\theta,\quad\theta(0)=1,\quad\dot{\theta}(0)=0.

As
m\kappa>\frac{1}{4}
, the solution is

\theta(t)=e^{-t/2m}\left(\cos\left(\frac{\sqrt{4m\kappa-1}}{2m}t\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{4m\kappa-1}}\sin\left(\frac{\sqrt{4m\kappa-1}}{2m}t\right)\right)

and has its first zero on
[0,\infty)
at
t_{1}=\frac{\pi m}{\sqrt{4m\kappa-1}}+\frac{2m}{\sqrt{4m\kappa-1}}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{4m\kappa}}\right)
.

It is well-known from classical mechanics that
\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}
also has a smallest positive zero
t_{1}^{\eta}
, which is simple and which approaches the zero
t_{1}
as
\eta\to 0
and which grows to infinity as
\eta\to\frac{\pi}{2}
(we always have

by the Sturm–Picone comparison principle, Lemma
5.1
). Thus, given

, by the intermediate value theorem, there exists
\eta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
such that

. Then
\theta_{\mathrm{rel}}(t^{*})=0
and
\dot{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}}(t^{*})<0
, so

\Theta(t^{*})=\Phi(t^{*})=0,

but we have

\dot{\Theta}(t^{*})=\dot{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}}(t^{*})\Big(\underbrace{\frac{N_{2}}{N},\cdots,\frac{N_{2}}{N}}_{N_{1}\mathrm{~times}},\underbrace{-\frac{N_{1}}{N},\cdots,-\frac{N_{1}}{N}}_{N_{2}\mathrm{~times}}\Big)\neq\dot{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}}(t^{*})\Big(-\underbrace{\frac{N_{2}}{N},\cdots,-\frac{N_{2}}{N}}_{N_{1}\mathrm{~times}},\underbrace{\frac{N_{1}}{N},\cdots,\frac{N_{1}}{N}}_{N_{2}\mathrm{~times}}\Big)=\dot{\Phi}(t^{*}).

∎

Appendix A
An integral Grönwall inequality

We state and prove the non-negativity of function satisfying the integral form of Grönwall-type inequality as follows.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
a,c>0
, and suppose the continuous function
u:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

(A.1)

u(t)\leq c\int_{0}^{t}u(s)(1-e^{-(t-s)/a})ds,\quad t\geq 0.

Then, we have

u(t)\leq 0\quad\mbox{for all $t\geq 0$.}

Proof.

Define the nonnegative function
U(t):[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
as

(A.2)

U(t)=\max\left\{\max_{s\in[0,t]}u(s),0\right\}.

Then, it suffices to check that

U\equiv 0.

\bullet
Step A: we claim that

U(0)=0.

It follows from (
A.1
) at
t=0
that

(A.3)

u(0)\leq 0.

Then, we use (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) to see

(A.4)

U(0)=\max\left\{u(0),~0\right\}=0.

\bullet
Step B: It follows from the definition of
U
that
U
is continuous, monotonically nondecreasing, and

(A.5)

U\geq u.

Now, we use
0\leq 1-e^{-(t-s)/a}\leq 1
, (
A.1
) and (
A.5
) to get

u(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:2nd-Gronwall}}}}{{\leq}}c\int_{0}^{t}u(s)(1-e^{-(t-s)/a})ds\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{App-B-4}}}}{{\leq}}c\int_{0}^{t}U(s)(1-e^{-(t-s)/a})ds\leq c\int_{0}^{t}U(s)ds.

For each
t\geq 0
, we may find
s^{*}\in[0,t]
such that

u(s^{*})=\max_{s\in[0,t]}u(s).

By the above inequality,

\max_{s\in[0,t]}u(s)=u(s^{*})\leq c\int_{0}^{s^{*}}U(s)ds\leq c\int_{0}^{t}U(s)ds.

On the other hand, since
0\leq c\int_{0}^{t}U(s)ds
, one has by definition (
A.2
) of
U
,

U(t)\leq c\int_{0}^{t}U(s)ds,\quad t\geq 0.

By the standard Grönwall lemma and (
A.4
), we have

U(t)\leq U(0)e^{ct}=0,\quad\mbox{hence}~~U\equiv 0.

∎

We begin with a simple lemma, which will be useful in applying Faa di Bruno’s formula.

Lemma B.1
.

For

and

, the following identity holds:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ell m_{\ell}=n\end{subarray}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha^{m_{l}}}{m_{l}!l^{m_{l}}}=\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n},

where

\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}=\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)}{n!}.

Proof.

For a real-analytic function

defined in a neighborhood

of

, we denote by

the coefficient of

in the power series expansion of

at

. Then, we have

\displaystyle\binom{n+\alpha-1}{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{1-x}\right)^{\alpha}[x^{n}]=\exp\left(-\alpha\log(1-x)\right)[x^{n}]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\alpha x+\frac{\alpha x^{2}}{2}+\cdots+\frac{\alpha x^{n}}{n}+\cdots\right)[x^{n}]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\alpha x+\frac{\alpha x^{2}}{2}+\cdots+\frac{\alpha x^{n}}{n}\right)[x^{n}]

\displaystyle=\left(\exp\left(\alpha x\right)\exp\left(\frac{\alpha x^{2}}{2}\right)\cdots\exp\left(\frac{\alpha x^{n}}{n}\right)\right)[x^{n}]

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\sum_{m_{1}\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{m_{1}}x^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}!}\right)\left(\sum_{m_{2}\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{m_{2}}x^{2m_{2}}}{m_{2}!2^{m_{2}}}\right)\cdots\left(\sum_{m_{n}\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{m_{n}}x^{nm_{n}}}{m_{n}!n^{m_{n}}}\right)\right)[x^{n}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\ell m_{\ell}=n\end{subarray}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha^{m_{l}}}{m_{l}!l^{m_{l}}}.

\theta_{i}^{n}(0)=0
. Inductively, this is a linear ODE in
\theta_{i}^{n}(t)
, and hence
\theta_{i}^{n}(t)
has the same
t
-domain as
\theta_{i}(0,t)
. It seems that the proof methods of Lemmas
B.2
,
B.3
, and
B.5
should give

\left|\frac{\partial^{l}}{\partial t^{l}}\theta^{n}_{i}(t)\right|\leq c(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{n+l}e^{2\kappa t},\quad t\geq 0,

for a constant

, but we have not checked the details. If so, the infinite series

\tilde{\theta}_{i}(m,t)=\theta_{i}(0,t)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{n}}{n!}\theta^{n}_{i}(t)

would converge for

, and may even be well-defined on a

-half strip:

\{t:\Re(t)>0,~|\Im(t)|<c^{\prime}(m)\},

where

depends on

, with
\lim_{m\to 0}c^{\prime}(m)=\frac{1}{2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})}
. This
\tilde{\theta}_{i}
is the solution to (

) with

\tilde{\omega}_{i}^{0}=\nu_{i}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sin(\theta_{j}^{0}-\theta_{i}^{0}).

We may as well write

\theta_{i}(m,t)=\tilde{\theta}_{i}(m,t)+R(m,t).

Our analysis suggests the domain of

shrinks to the positive real line as

because the same is true for

. Considering the decay term

in the estimates for

, we ask whether

admits an analytic expansion in terms of

-width of the holomorphic strip domain of

. Answering the questions posed in this remark seems an interesting future research direction.

Next, we will obtain better bounds on
|\theta_{i}^{{n}}(m,t)-\theta_{j}^{(n)}(m,t)|
, with terms that are polynomial in

decaying in time as

. But first, we need a lemma.

Lemma B.4
.

Let
p\in[0,1]
,
a,b\geq 0
,
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then, we have

(pa^{n}+(1-p)b^{n})(pa^{m}+(1-p)b^{m})\leq(pa^{n+m}+(1-p)b^{n+m}).

Proof.

We subtract the left-hand side from the right-hand side to find

RHS-LHS=p(1-p)(a^{n}-b^{n})(a^{m}-b^{m})\geq 0.

∎

Lemma B.5
.

For
m>0
,
i,j\in[N]
,
t\geq 0
, and each integer
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|\theta_{i}^{(n)}(m,t)-\theta_{j}^{(n)}(m,t)|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq(n-1)!\left(\Big(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m}\Big)^{n}\Big(1+\frac{t}{m}\Big)^{n}e^{-t/m}+(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{n}(1-e^{-t/m})\right).

Proof.

We prove by induction. The base case
n=1
follows from Lemma
2.2
. Assume this is true for
1\leq n\leq k
. Then by Lemma
B.4
,

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!}\left(\frac{|\theta_{p}^{(l)}(m,t)-\theta_{i}^{(l)}(m,t)|}{l!}\right)^{m_{l}}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt\leq\left((2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{k}e^{-t/m}+(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k}(1-e^{-t/m})\right)\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!l^{m_{l}}}.

Then, it follows from Faa di Bruno’s formula that

\displaystyle m\theta^{(k+2)}_{i}(m,t)-m\theta^{(k+2)}_{j}(m,t)+\theta^{(k+1)}_{i}(m,t)-\theta^{(k+1)}_{j}(m,t)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\ell m_{\ell}=k\end{subarray}}k!\sin^{(\sum_{l=1}^{k}m_{l})}(\theta_{p}(m,t)-\theta_{i}(m,t))\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!}\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(m,t)-\theta_{i}^{(l)}(m,t)}{l!}\right)^{m_{l}}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt-\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\ell m_{\ell}=k\end{subarray}}k!\sin^{(\sum_{l=1}^{k}m_{l})}(\theta_{p}(m,t)-\theta_{j}(m,t))\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!}\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(m,t)-\theta_{j}^{(l)}(m,t)}{l!}\right)^{m_{l}}.

We use Lemma
B.1
to see

\displaystyle|m\theta^{(k+2)}_{i}(m,t)-m\theta^{(k+2)}_{j}(m,t)+\theta^{(k+1)}_{i}(m,t)-\theta^{(k+1)}_{j}(m,t)|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq 2\kappa k!\left((2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{k}e^{-t/m}+(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k}(1-e^{-t/m})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq k!\left((2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{k}e^{-t/m}+(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k+1}\right).

Finally, we invoke the Duhamel principle and Lemma
B.3
to see

\displaystyle|\theta^{(k+1)}_{i}(m,t)-\theta^{(k+1)}_{j}(m,t)|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq|\theta^{(k+1)}_{i}(m,0)-\theta^{(k+1)}_{j}(m,0)|e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{1}{m}\int_{0}^{t}k!\left((2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}(1+\frac{s}{m})^{k}e^{-s/m}+(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k+1}\right)e^{-(t-s)/m}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq 2^{-k}k!(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+k!(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}e^{-t/m}\cdot\frac{1}{k+1}\left((1+\frac{t}{m})^{k+1}-1\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+k!(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k+1}(1-e^{-t/m})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq k!\Big(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m}\Big)^{k+1}\Big(1+\frac{t}{m}\Big)^{k+1}e^{-t/m}+k!(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k+1}(1-e^{-t/m}),

which close the induction.
∎

We combine Lemma
B.2
and Lemma
B.5
to see that for all
i,j\in[N]
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
m>0
, and
t\geq 0
,

(B.24)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &|\theta_{i}^{(n)}(0,t)-\theta_{j}^{(n)}(0,t)|,~|\theta_{i}^{(n)}(m,t)-\theta_{j}^{(n)}(m,t)|\\
&\hskip 5.69046pt\leq(n-1)!\left(\Big(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m}\Big)^{n}\Big(1+\frac{t}{m}\Big)^{n}e^{-t/m}+(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{n}(1-e^{-t/m})\right)\\
&\hskip 5.69046pt\leq(n-1)!\Big(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m}\Big)^{n}\Big(1+\frac{t}{m}\Big)^{n}.\end{aligned}

We are to show that

\displaystyle|\theta_{i}^{(n)}(0,t)-\theta_{j}^{(n)}(0,t)-\theta_{i}^{(n)}(m,t)+\theta_{j}^{(n)}(m,t)|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq 2(n-1)!(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{n}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{n}e^{-t/m}+\frac{3}{2}(n+1)!m\kappa e^{2\kappa t}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\times\left((2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{n}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{n}e^{-t/m}+(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{n}(1-e^{-t/m})\right).

By Faa di Bruno’s formula, we have

(B.25)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &m\theta^{(k+2)}_{i}(m,t)-m\theta^{(k+2)}_{j}(m,t)+\theta^{(k+1)}_{i}(m,t)-\theta^{(k+1)}_{j}(m,t)-\theta^{(k+1)}_{i}(0,t)+\theta^{(k+1)}_{j}(0,t)\\
&=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\ell m_{\ell}=k\end{subarray}}k!\sin^{(\sum_{l=1}^{k}m_{l})}(\theta_{p}(m,t)-\theta_{i}(m,t))\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!}\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(m,t)-\theta_{i}^{(l)}(m,t)}{l!}\right)^{m_{l}}\\
&-\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\ell m_{\ell}=k\end{subarray}}k!\sin^{(\sum_{l=1}^{k}m_{l})}(\theta_{p}(0,t)-\theta_{i}(0,t))\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!}\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(0,t)-\theta_{i}^{(l)}(0,t)}{l!}\right)^{m_{l}}\\
&-\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\ell m_{\ell}=k\end{subarray}}k!\sin^{(\sum_{l=1}^{k}m_{l})}(\theta_{p}(m,t)-\theta_{j}(m,t))\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!}\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(m,t)-\theta_{j}^{(l)}(m,t)}{l!}\right)^{m_{l}}\\
&+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\ell m_{\ell}=k\end{subarray}}k!\sin^{(\sum_{l=1}^{k}m_{l})}(\theta_{p}(0,t)-\theta_{j}(0,t))\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!}\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(0,t)-\theta_{j}^{(l)}(0,t)}{l!}\right)^{m_{l}}.\end{aligned}

Lemma B.6
.

If
a_{\alpha},b_{\alpha},c_{\alpha},d_{\alpha},\varepsilon_{\alpha},\delta_{\alpha}\geq 0
,
\alpha\in[n]
, satisfy

\max\{a_{\alpha},b_{\alpha}\}\leq\min\{c_{\alpha},d_{\alpha}\},\quad|a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}|\leq\varepsilon_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}+\delta_{\alpha}d_{\alpha},

then we have

\left|a_{1}\cdots a_{n}-b_{1}\cdots b_{n}\right|\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\right)c_{1}\cdots c_{n}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i}\right)d_{1}\cdots d_{n}.

Proof.

By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\left|a_{1}\cdots a_{n}-b_{1}\cdots b_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}a_{1}\cdots a_{\alpha-1}|a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}|b_{\alpha+1}\cdots b_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}a_{1}\cdots a_{\alpha-1}(\varepsilon_{\alpha}c_{\alpha})b_{\alpha+1}\cdots b_{n}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}a_{1}\cdots a_{\alpha-1}(\delta_{\alpha}d_{\alpha})b_{\alpha+1}\cdots b_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\right)c_{1}\cdots c_{n}+\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\delta_{\alpha}\right)d_{1}\cdots d_{n}.

∎

We may set

n=\sum_{l=1}^{k}m_{l},\quad\left(a_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha=1}^{n}=\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(m,t)-\theta_{i}^{(l)}(m,t)}{(l-1)!}\right),\quad\left(b_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha=1}^{n}=\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(0,t)-\theta_{i}^{(l)}(0,t)}{(l-1)!}\right),

where each index
l
is repeated
m_{l}
times. By (
B.24
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left(c_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha=1}^{n}=\left((2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{l}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{l}\right),\\
&\left(d_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha=1}^{n}=\left((2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{l}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{l}e^{-t/m}+(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{l}(1-e^{-t/m})\right),\\
&\left(\varepsilon_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha=1}^{n}=\left(2e^{-t/m}\right),\quad\left(\delta_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha=1}^{n}=\left(\frac{3}{2}l(k+1)m\kappa e^{2\kappa t}\right).\end{aligned}

The index

m_{l}
times, satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma
B.6
so that

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{l=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(m,t)-\theta_{i}^{(l)}(m,t)}{(l-1)!}\right)^{m_{l}}-\prod_{l=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(0,t)-\theta_{i}^{(l)}(0,t)}{(l-1)!}\right)^{m_{l}}\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\left|a_{1}\cdots a_{n}-b_{1}\cdots b_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\right)c_{1}\cdots c_{n}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i}\right)d_{1}\cdots d_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq 2kc_{1}\cdots c_{n}e^{-t/m}+\frac{3}{2}k(k+1)m\kappa e^{2\kappa t}d_{1}\cdots d_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq 2k\Big(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m}\Big)^{k}\Big(1+\frac{t}{m}\Big)^{k}e^{-t/m}+\frac{3}{2}k(k+1)m\kappa e^{2\kappa t}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt\times\left(\Big(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m}\Big)^{k}\Big(1+\frac{t}{m}\Big)^{k}e^{-t/m}+(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k}(1-e^{-t/m})\right).

We estimate

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!}\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(m,t)-\theta_{i}^{(l)}(m,t)}{l!}\right)^{m_{l}}-\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!}\left(\frac{\theta_{p}^{(l)}(0,t)-\theta_{i}^{(l)}(0,t)}{l!}\right)^{m_{l}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq 2k\Big(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m}\Big)^{k}\Big(1+\frac{t}{m}\Big)^{k}e^{-t/m}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!l^{m_{l}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{3}{2}k(k+1)m\kappa e^{2\kappa t}\Big(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m}\Big)^{k}\Big(1+\frac{t}{m}\Big)^{k}e^{-t/m}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!l^{m_{l}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{3}{2}k(k+1)m\kappa e^{2\kappa t}\Big(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})\Big)^{k}(1-e^{-t/m})\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m_{l}!l^{m_{l}}}

to find

(B.26)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Bigg|\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\

&\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{3}{2}m\kappa\cdot(k+2)!e^{2\kappa t}(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{k+1}e^{-t/m}\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{3}{2}m\kappa\cdot(k+2)!e^{2\kappa t}(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k+1}(1-e^{-t/m}).\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\left|m\theta^{(k+2)}_{i}(m,t)-m\theta^{(k+2)}_{i}(0,t)+\theta^{(k+1)}_{i}(m,t)-\theta^{(k+1)}_{i}(0,t)\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq(k+1)!(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{k+1}e^{-t/m}+\frac{3}{4}m\kappa\cdot(k+2)!e^{2\kappa t}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt\times\left((2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{k}e^{-t/m}+(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k+1}\right).

\displaystyle\left|\theta^{(k+1)}_{i}(m,t)-\theta^{(k+1)}_{i}(0,t)\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq k!(2\kappa+\|\Omega^{0}\|+\|\mathcal{V}\|+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{e^{-t/m}}{m}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{3}{4}m\kappa\cdot(k+2)!e^{2\kappa t}(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}(1+\frac{s}{m})^{k}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{e^{-t/m}}{m}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{3}{4}m\kappa\cdot(k+2)!e^{2\kappa t}(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k+1}e^{s/m}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq k!(2\kappa+\|\Omega^{0}\|+\|\mathcal{V}\|+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{k+1}e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{9}{8}m\kappa\cdot(k+1)!e^{2\kappa t}(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V})+\frac{9}{8m})^{k+1}(1+\frac{t}{m})^{k+1}e^{-t/m}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\frac{3}{4}m\kappa\cdot(k+2)!e^{2\kappa t}(2\kappa+\mathcal{D}(\Omega^{0})+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{V}))^{k+1}(1-e^{-t/m}),

Hybrid framework for robust runoff forecasting via decomposition and machine learning
Authors: Wen-chuan Wang, Xu-tong Zhang, Qi-qi Zeng, Hong-zhen Ren, Dong-mei Xu, Qiang Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate runoff forecasting is essential for flood control and water resource management, yet strong nonlinearity and seasonal non-stationarity often limit traditional models. This study proposes a hybrid decomposition-integration-optimization framework (TVFEMD-LSSVM-BiLSTM-FSS-IBKA), integrating time-variant filter empirical mode decomposition (TVFEMD), least squares support vector machine (LSSVM), bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM), flood season segmentation (FSS), and an improved black kite algorithm (IBKA). TVFEMD mitigates noise and non-stationarity, LSSVM and BiLSTM capture nonlinear and temporal features, FSS distinguishes seasonal hydrological regimes, and IBKA adaptively optimizes ensemble weights. Using daily runoff data from Shebu and Dongjiang stations, the proposed model reduces RMSE by 42.5% and 39.8%, respectively, compared with the best benchmark, with KGE improvements up to 27.2%. The framework effectively captures flood peaks and maintains stability during non-flood periods, avoiding prediction lag and underestimation. Overall, the proposed model demonstrates strong accuracy, robustness, and applicability for runoff forecasting under complex hydrological conditions.

Introduction

Runoff, as a core component of the hydrological cycle, plays a vital role in flood control and disaster mitigation, water resource management, ecological balance maintenance, and sustainable development. However, runoff processes are simultaneously influenced by multiple factors, including spatiotemporal precipitation variability, atmospheric circulation, surface characteristics, and human activities. Consequently, runoff exhibits significant nonlinearity, multiscale variability, and spatiotemporal non-stationarity, accompanied by substantial uncertainty. This complexity makes runoff forecasting an exceptionally challenging task, demanding highly efficient and robust forecasting methods1. In recent years, hybrid approaches integrating time series decomposition with deep learning and machine learning have demonstrated promising potential in addressing non-stationary characteristics, nonlinear dependencies, and data noise. Particularly, strategies combining ensemble models with intelligent optimization algorithms can effectively capture seasonal variations in runoff and enable dynamic adjustment of forecast schemes2. Therefore, constructing a hybrid forecast framework that integrates decomposition methods, sequence learning models, and intelligent optimization techniques not only deepens our understanding of complex hydrological processes but also provides robust support for flood control, disaster prevention, and optimal water resource allocation.

Methodology review and existing challenges

The evolution of runoff prediction methods has gradually shifted from traditional hydrological and statistical models toward modern machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) approaches3,4. Conventional models, such as conceptual rainfall-runoff models and distributed hydrological simulations, are typically constructed based on hydrological cycle mechanisms or statistical characteristics5. They have long played a fundamental role in watershed regulation and flood control engineering. However, when confronted with the inherent nonlinear characteristics, seasonal variations, and significant non-stationarity of runoff processes, these models often exhibit insufficient robustness. Their limitations primarily manifest in difficulties with parameter calibration, reliance on strict data distribution assumptions, and inadequate adaptability to extreme events. In recent years, the rapid advancement of ML and DL technologies has significantly propelled progress in runoff forecast methods, surpassing the capabilities of traditional physical and statistical models to some extent6. Among these, support vector machines (SVM) and long short-term memory networks (LSTM) are representative methods with widespread application7. The former excels at handling nonlinear relationships, while the latter demonstrates outstanding performance in capturing time-series dependencies. Building upon this foundation, the least squares support vector machine (LSSVM) serves as an enhanced version of SVM. By transforming the quadratic programming problem into a system of linear equations and incorporating a least squares loss function, LSSVM not only significantly reduces computational complexity but also demonstrates strong generalization capabilities and computational efficiency under small sample conditions8,9. Concurrently, the bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) network enhances modeling capabilities for temporal contextual dependencies by incorporating both forward and backward information flows into the traditional LSTM architecture10. This effectively addresses the limitations of unidirectional information flow, significantly improving forecast accuracy and robustness. Thus, leveraging their distinct advantages, LSSVM and BiLSTM provide a robust foundation for constructing a hybrid runoff forecast framework that integrates nonlinear modeling capabilities with temporal dynamic learning capabilities.

Although ML and DL models demonstrate strong potential in runoff forecasting, their predictive performance remains susceptible to non-stationary fluctuations and noise interference within runoff sequences. To address this challenge, researchers have increasingly explored signal decomposition techniques as preprocessing methods to enhance model stability and accuracy. In early approaches, empirical mode decomposition (EMD) was widely applied to nonlinear and non-stationary signal analysis. By adaptively decomposing complex time series into a set of intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) with distinct frequency characteristics, EMD provides smoother, more interpretable components for subsequent modeling. The EMD method requires no predefined basis functions and exhibits high adaptability, demonstrating certain advantages when processing complex hydrological sequences. Building upon this foundation, time-varying filtering empirical mode decomposition (TVFEMD) has been developed as an improvement over the EMD method. Unlike EMD, which is prone to modal aliasing and end-point effects during decomposition, TVFEMD incorporates time-varying filtering strategies11,12. This approach suppresses modal aliasing while maintaining adaptive decomposition, significantly enhancing component stability. Simultaneously, it preserves decomposition flexibility while yielding modal components with clearer frequency characteristics and stronger physical interpretability. TVFEMD not only effectively reduces noise interference in runoff sequences but also better preserves their multiscale dynamic characteristics, providing a more robust and reliable tool for modeling complex runoff processes13. However, runoff processes exhibit significant differences between flood and non-flood seasons: the former is often driven by heavy rainfall, exhibiting suddenness and high volatility, while the latter is relatively stable with distinct hydrological response mechanisms. Employing a unified model for comprehensive modeling often fails to adequately account for the dynamic characteristics of both scenarios, resulting in limited forecast accuracy.

To address this issue, this study employs a Flood Season Segmentation (FSS) strategy, dividing runoff sequences into flood and non-flood seasons for separate modeling. This approach not only enables the model to better capture non-stationary characteristics under different seasonal conditions but also enhances its adaptability to extreme events. Building upon this foundation, weighted fusion of segmented forecast results allows the strengths of models from different stages to be fully leveraged, achieving complementary and integrated information14. This approach partially overcomes the limitations of single-model forecasts across the entire timeframe, providing a more robust and adaptive solution for high-precision simulation of complex runoff processes. However, existing weighted integration strategies predominantly rely on preset or static weight allocations, lacking the ability to adaptively adjust based on data characteristics and forecast scenarios. This limitation not only potentially restricts forecast accuracy but also reduces computational efficiency during multi-model fusion, making it difficult to meet contemporary runoff forecast demands for both high efficiency and precision. Consequently, introducing intelligent optimization algorithms capable of dynamically optimizing weight distribution has become a crucial pathway for enhancing the forecast performance of integrated models.

The black kite algorithm (BKA), a novel intelligent optimization method proposed in recent years, draws inspiration from the collaborative and tracking behaviors exhibited by black kites during group foraging15. By dynamically adjusting its search mechanism, this algorithm achieves a flexible balance between global exploration and local exploitation, thereby effectively reducing the risk of getting stuck in local optima during the optimization process. Compared to traditional static weighting strategies, BKA demonstrates superior adaptability in weight allocation, enhancing the stability and convergence efficiency of parameter optimization. This provides more robust optimization support for ensemble forecast frameworks. Despite its strengths in maintaining population diversity and enhancing global search capabilities, BKA still faces certain limitations in practical applications. For instance, an uneven initial population distribution may weaken coverage of the solution space during early exploration phases. Additionally, insufficient adjustment of individual search step sizes during iterations can compromise the precision of local convergence. These shortcomings indicate that BKA’s direct application in runoff forecast remains subject to optimization, requiring further refinement to enhance its adaptability to weight optimization tasks across diverse scenarios.

To further strengthen the physical interpretability of the proposed hybrid framework, it is important to clarify how each model component corresponds to dominant hydrological drivers. TVFEMD decomposes the raw runoff series into mode components of different time scales, which naturally align with multi-scale runoff generation mechanisms: high-frequency components typically reflect fast surface runoff responses triggered by intense rainfall, whereas low-frequency components correspond to baseflow variations modulated by groundwater recharge and seasonal water storage. LSSVM, with its strong nonlinear mapping capability, is particularly suitable for modeling the nonlinear rainfall–runoff relationship during transitional flow regimes, where both infiltration–excess processes and subsurface flow interactions coexist. In contrast, BiLSTM excels at capturing temporal dependencies essential for representing delayed hydrological responses, such as groundwater-dominated baseflow recession and the memory effects associated with seasonal soil moisture accumulation. By combining these components, the hybrid model achieves a closer correspondence between data-driven learning and physical runoff generation processes, thereby improving both interpretability and forecasting reliability under varying hydrological conditions.

Major contributions

To address these challenges, this paper proposes a novel hybrid model—TVFEMD-LSSVM-BiLSTM-FSS-IBKA—that enhances the accuracy and robustness of daily runoff forecasting. Beyond simply combining existing decomposition, machine learning, and optimization techniques, the proposed framework introduces two essential innovations that fundamentally distinguish it from prior hybrid approaches such as CEEMDAN-LSTM, VMD-BiLSTM-PSO, and EEMD-LSSVM. First, a hydrologically driven FSS mechanism is embedded into the ensemble architecture, explicitly separating flood-season and non-flood-season processes rather than applying a uniform year-round model. This seasonal perception strategy reflects the physical drivers of runoff—rapid rainfall-runoff responses during flood periods versus groundwater-dominated baseflow during non-flood periods—and enables the model components to specialize in different hydrological regimes. Second, to overcome the rigidity of fixed or heuristically assigned fusion weights used in previous optimization-based hybrid frameworks, this study introduces an Improved Black Kite Algorithm (IBKA). Unlike traditional optimizers, IBKA integrates hybrid chaotic initialization and an adaptive inertia-weight mechanism, allowing dynamic, data-adaptive optimization of seasonal fusion weights and significantly enhancing the model’s generalization capability across regimes with contrasting variability and nonlinearity. In operational terms, TVFEMD is first employed to perform multiscale decomposition and denoising of raw runoff sequences, effectively mitigating non-stationarity and extracting representative intrinsic modes. LSSVM and BiLSTM are then constructed for each decomposed sub-sequence to jointly capture nonlinear rainfall–runoff relationships and bidirectional temporal dependencies, respectively. Building upon this, the FSS mechanism partitions forecasts into two hydrological stages, and IBKA is used to adaptively determine the optimal contribution of each base model within each season using the correlation coefficient (R) as the objective function. By integrating physical process segmentation with advanced optimization dynamics, this framework not only improves numerical performance but also offers stronger physical interpretability than conventional hybrid models. Empirical results demonstrate that the proposed system outperforms single models and existing hybrid frameworks in capturing sharp flood peaks and representing stable low-flow behavior, thereby providing more reliable and hydrologically consistent support for flood control, disaster mitigation, and water-resources management. A novel hybrid forecasting framework (TVFEMD-LSSVM-BiLSTM-FSS-IBKA) was developed, introducing the deep coupling of decomposition preprocessing, flood-season segmentation strategy, and intelligent optimization algorithms for daily-scale runoff forecasting. This method was empirically validated in two representative Chinese river basins. Performance comparisons against four benchmark models and four advanced ensemble forecast models demonstrated the proposed framework’s significant advantages in runoff forecast accuracy and robustness.

To clarify the necessity of constructing such a multi-component hybrid framework, it is important to emphasize that the hydrological processes governing runoff generation are highly nonlinear, multi-scale, and seasonally asymmetric. No single model is capable of simultaneously capturing fast-response rainfall–runoff fluctuations, slow-varying baseflow dynamics, and the nonlinear transitions between these states. Each component of the proposed framework, therefore, serves a distinct and indispensable purpose. TVFEMD decomposes the raw runoff series into physically meaningful temporal scales, separating rapid hydrological responses from low-frequency groundwater-dominated variations. BiLSTM is employed to capture long-term dependencies and memory effects, which are essential for representing seasonal water storage processes. LSSVM provides a stable nonlinear mapping mechanism, especially suitable for small-sample conditions and transitional flow regimes. The FSS module incorporates the hydrological asymmetry between flood and non-flood seasons, while IBKA adaptively optimizes the fusion weights to overcome the limitations of manually assigned or static weighting schemes. Accordingly, the complexity of the framework stems not from methodological redundancy but from the necessity to accurately represent the multi-process nature of runoff generation.

Study areas and dataset

Study areas

This study focuses on the Shebu section of the Juan River—a tributary of the Yangtze-Xiang River system—monitored by the Shebu Hydrological Station in Xiangtan City, Hunan Province, China, as well as the Lei River basin upstream of the Dongjiang Hydrological Station in Zixing City, Hunan Province (see Figs. 1 and 2). The Shebu Hydrological Station, located in Xiangtan County, Xiangtan City, Hunan Province, serves as a critical monitoring point along the Juan River. As a major tributary of the Xiang River, the Jian River has a main stem length of approximately 103 km and a catchment area of 1,764 km2. The Xiang River, a first-order tributary of the Yangtze River and Hunan Province’s largest river, has a main stem length of 856 km, a catchment area of 94,660 km2, and an annual runoff volume of approximately 72.2 billion m3. It plays a pivotal role in regional water supply and hydropower development. The Dongjiang Hydrological Station (113° 30′ 58.63″ E, 25° 87′ 87.91″ N) is situated in a typical subtropical monsoon humid mountainous hilly region. Its upstream watershed area upstream of the dam is 4,719 km2, with an annual runoff of 4.54 billion m3 and an average flow of 144 m3/s. This region exhibits significant topographic variation, abundant precipitation with frequent events, and rapid surface runoff convergence during heavy rainfall, leading to pronounced flood peak amplification and demonstrating exceptional hydrological sensitivity and spatiotemporal variability. The intense flow fluctuations in the Juan River basin and the complex topography, coupled with strong rainfall response in the Lei River basin, provide multidimensional testing for the adaptability and robustness of forecast models. Through empirical analysis across climatic zones, the selected basins effectively reflect runoff characteristics under diverse hydrological conditions. Decomposed dataset

This study employs the TVFEMD method to perform multiscale decomposition on raw runoff sequences, transforming highly complex and non-stationary runoff processes into several low-complexity, relatively stable subcomponents. Previous research indicates that the decomposition performance of TVFEMD is primarily influenced by the bandwidth threshold and the degree of B-splines. The TVFEMD parameter settings used in this study were selected by referring to recommended configurations reported in related hydrological literature. The bandwidth threshold of 0.2 and the B-spline order of 26 were directly adopted based on these studies13. Their results demonstrate that this parameter combination effectively balances modal separation capability, suppression of mode mixing, and numerical stability when applied to nonstationary hydrological time series. Given that the fluctuation characteristics and noise levels of the runoff data in this study are highly consistent with those examined in the referenced work, using the same parameter settings enhances methodological consistency and improves the reproducibility of the analysis. Methodology

Machine learning and deep learning

To more accurately characterize the nonlinear patterns and time-dependent structure of runoff sequences, this study combines ML and DL methods for daily-scale runoff forecasting16,17. Each approach offers distinct advantages in hydrological modeling: ML models excel at identifying short-term fluctuation patterns and extracting underlying regularities, while DL models, leveraging their deep nonlinear mapping structures, are better suited for capturing complex long- and short-term dependency features. During data processing, a sliding time window strategy is employed to reconstruct continuous runoff sequences into input–output sample pairs required for supervised learning18,19. Although bidirectional structures may appear unsuitable for real-time prediction, this issue is mitigated in this study, as the backward pass operates only within the training window without using future information during forecasting. TVF-EMD

The TVFEMD method, proposed by Li et al.22, is an enhanced signal processing technique based on traditional EMD decomposition, designed to overcome the modal aliasing issue inherent in EMD. The detailed mathematical formulation and implementation procedure can be found in the relevant literature9,23.

BKA

BKA is a novel swarm intelligence optimization method inspired by the cooperative strategies exhibited by black kites during foraging and hunting. This approach integrates a Cauchy distribution perturbation mechanism with a leader guidance factor during the search process, aiming to balance global exploration and local exploitation to enhance convergence speed and the global optimality of solutions24. The detailed mathematical formulation and algorithmic procedures can be found in the relevant literature25–27.

Proposed model

Improved BKA algorithm

In meta-heuristic optimization methods, achieving a dynamic equilibrium between global exploration and local exploitation remains a core factor influencing algorithmic performance. Although BKA demonstrates certain advantages in search efficiency and solution accuracy, its standard version still exhibits shortcomings. The traditional initialization process often leads to uneven distribution of the population across the solution space, reducing diversity in early exploration and thereby increasing the risk of getting stuck in local optima. Furthermore, its behavior switching mechanism relies on fixed decay rules, lacking adaptive adjustment to the search process state. This may cause premature termination of the global search phase, weakening the algorithm’s ability to escape complex terrain or multi-peak functions. To address these issues, this paper proposes an IBKA algorithm. Hybrid chaotic map initialization

Hybrid chaotic map initialization

In intelligent optimization algorithms, the generation of the initial population plays a decisive role in search performance, directly impacting convergence speed and the quality of the final solution. If the population is unevenly distributed across the search space, it can lead to insufficient early exploration, thereby increasing the risk of getting stuck in local optima. To enhance the diversity and balanced distribution of initial solutions, this paper introduces an initialization strategy based on hybrid chaotic sequences within IBKA28. By combining random perturbations with nonlinear mapping mechanisms, this method achieves higher coverage and uniformity of the population within the parameter space, significantly boosting global exploration capabilities29. Compared to the traditional Logistic chaotic map, the Tent map demonstrates superior performance in uniform distribution characteristics but is susceptible to parameter sensitivity during early iterations. To address this, this study integrates random sequences with the Tent map and introduces differential adjustment factors in individual parameters to further mitigate solution clustering. Flood season segmentation

Different models often exhibit varying performance under diverse hydrological scenarios. Comparison among various predictive structures

To evaluate the performance of ML and DL models in runoff prediction, this study uses SVM and LSTM as baseline models and compares them with the improved LSSVM and BiLSTM, respectively. Results indicate that on the Shebu dataset, LSSVM improved R2 and KGE by 3.5% and 3.7%, respectively, while on the Dongjiang dataset, MAPE and RMSE decreased by 19.2% and 13.8% respectively, demonstrating superior error control and stability compared to traditional SVM. In contrast, BiLSTM achieved more substantial improvements across both basins: in Shebu, RMSE and MAPE decreased by approximately 7.8%; in Dongjiang, R increased by about 2.4% and KGE improved by approximately 3.6%, further highlighting its advantage in capturing the temporal characteristics of runoff processes.

From a physical mechanism perspective, LSSVM mitigates hyperparameter sensitivity through its least-squares optimization framework, rendering it more robust in modeling nonlinear relationships. This enables it to better capture the non-stationary characteristics in runoff sequences influenced by climatic fluctuations and surface conditions. The bidirectional recurrent units introduced in the BiLSTM structure simultaneously utilize historical and future information, demonstrating stronger physical consistency and prediction accuracy during both rapid peak response and slow-changing low-flow phases. This temporal dependency modeling capability makes it superior to traditional LSTMs in addressing complex dynamic changes in runoff processes. Based on the above results, this study selects LSSVM and BiLSTM as the foundational models for subsequent ensemble modeling and optimization.

It is noteworthy that LSSVM performance is not uniformly superior to standard SVM under all evaluation metrics. Specifically, for the Shebu station, LSSVM achieved lower RMSE, R, and KGE yet showed a slightly higher MAPE. This phenomenon is theoretically acceptable because MAPE overemphasizes relative errors in low-flow periods, whereas LSSVM, due to its square-loss ridge formulation, smooths extreme fluctuations more aggressively than SVM. Given that Shebu runoff exhibits abrupt shifts and extended recession segments, a marginal sacrifice in percentage fit does not contradict the theoretical generalization properties of LSSVM.

Comparison of hybrid predictive structures

Table 4 indicates that applying TVFEMD to preprocess runoff sequences significantly improved the predictive performance of both the original LSSVM and BiLSTM models across the two watersheds. Taking the Shebu station as an example, the NSE metrics of TVFEMD-LSSVM and TVFEMD-BiLSTM improved by approximately 12.8% and 9.6%, respectively, compared to the standalone LSSVM and BiLSTM models, while the RMSE decreased by 14.2% and 11.7%, respectively. At the Dongjiang station, similar improvements were equally pronounced, with NSE showing an average increase exceeding 10%, and R-values approaching 0.9 for both base models. These results indicate that by decomposing and reducing non-stationarity and noise interference in the original sequence, the model can train on purer scale features, thereby enhancing its ability to characterize abrupt changes and nonlinear variations in the runoff process. In other words, TVFEMD not only enhances prediction accuracy through numerical metrics but also strengthens the model’s adaptive capability to multiscale runoff responses at the physical mechanism level.

Following the introduction of the FSS mechanism, the model exhibits new differentiated performance characteristics. By distinguishing between flood and non-flood seasons, the model demonstrates distinct advantages under high-flow and low-flow conditions. For instance, at the Shebu site, the TVFEMD-LSSVM-BiLSTM-FSS-PSO model achieved approximately a 6.5% improvement in NSE and nearly a 10% reduction in RMSE compared to the version without FSS. This demonstrates how explicit flood season segmentation enhances sensitivity to extreme flood peaks and low-flow conditions. At the Dongjiang station, this strategy similarly enhances the model’s overall robustness. Particularly under rapid flood convergence conditions during the flood season, the R-value increases to above 0.93, indicating that FSS can more effectively capture seasonal variations in hydrological driving mechanisms.

Further comparison of different optimization algorithms reveals that the weight search mechanism plays a decisive role in model performance. As a classical method, PSO can moderately improve performance through weight allocation but exhibits limitations in weight search adaptability. BKA, by introducing a behavioral switching mechanism, enhances NSE by 3–4% on average across both basins, demonstrating superior global optimization capability. However, the introduction of IBKA achieves optimal performance. At the Shebu site, the RMSE of the TVFEMD-LSSVM-BiLSTM-FSS-IBKA model decreased to 15.6, representing a nearly 20% reduction compared to the PSO version. At the Dongjiang site, this model’s NSE remained stable within the 0.92–0.94 range, with an R-value approaching 1.0, indicating excellent fitting capability for both rapidly evolving flood events and low-flow conditions during dry seasons. Physically, this improvement stems from the dynamic adaptation of weights under varying seasonal conditions. This enables the model to enhance peak capture capability during flood seasons while maintaining stability in simulating low flows during non-flood periods, achieving a balanced representation of hydrological processes grounded in physical mechanisms.

It should be emphasized that the final predictive enhancement of the full hybrid architecture does not originate from a single-stage surge but from a cumulative and hydrologically consistent improvement mechanism. As demonstrated in Table 4, predictive gains emerge sequentially through TVFEMD decomposition, dual-learner fusion, seasonal FSS weighting, and ultimately IBKA adaptive optimization. Each stage contributes a monotonic refinement rather than abrupt performance escalation, confirming that the overall improvement margin complies with physical runoff variability theory rather than reflecting algorithmic instability or over-fitting behavior.

It is evident that different models exhibit significant complementarity under varying hydrological conditions, aligning closely with the physical mechanisms governing runoff processes. At the Shebu site, BiLSTM dominates during the non-flood season with a weight of 0.6521. Non-flood season runoff processes exhibit weaker rainfall-driven influences, characterized by smaller flow fluctuations and more stable trends. Model limitations

Although the proposed TVFEMD-LSSVM-BiLSTM-FSS-IBKA framework demonstrates significant improvements in predictive performance across different basins and diverse hydrological conditions, several limitations warrant further exploration and optimization in subsequent studies.

First, computational efficiency remains a challenge. Integrating TVFEMD decomposition, LSSVM, and BiLSTM base learners while dynamically optimizing weights via FSS and IBKA results in significantly higher time and computational resource demands compared to single-model approaches. This complexity may become a critical bottleneck for model deployment in scenarios involving large-sample long-term time series or real-time forecasting. Although this architecture enhances the model’s ability to capture non-stationary and nonlinear hydrological features, its applicability in resource-constrained environments or rapid-update river basin management practices requires further improvement.

Second, while the IBKA optimization strategy demonstrates strong global search and convergence capabilities during weight optimization, it remains fundamentally a heuristic search method with inherent “black-box” characteristics. While the current model can produce numerically optimal weight distributions, the correspondence between its optimization path and underlying physical mechanisms remains unclear, limiting interpretability. For hydrological management and decision-making bodies, the lack of traceable explanations for the hydrological significance behind weight configurations may undermine trust and operational feasibility in practical water resource scheduling. Future research may explore integrating explainable learning frameworks or posterior sensitivity analysis to strengthen the connection between optimization outcomes and hydrological mechanisms.

Furthermore, as a typical data-driven model, the performance of this methodology heavily relies on the completeness and representativeness of input data. Missing values in training datasets or insufficient coverage of extreme runoff events may weaken the model’s generalization capabilities. Although incorporating seasonal decomposition and multi-model ensemble strategies effectively enhances adaptability to seasonal variations, the robustness of predictions remains insufficient when confronting sudden extreme events (e.g., severe droughts, floods, or human-induced scheduling actions). Future research may further integrate external drivers (e.g., precipitation, evapotranspiration, land surface characteristics) or uncertainty modeling frameworks to strengthen the model’s adaptability and fault tolerance under unconventional scenarios.

In summary, while the TVFEMD-LSSVM-BiLSTM-FSS-IBKA framework demonstrates outstanding prediction accuracy and stability, its computational cost, interpretability limitations, and response to extreme scenarios remain areas requiring further optimization. Subsequent research may explore lightweight modeling strategies, data fusion methods incorporating physical mechanisms, and more efficient optimization frameworks to enhance the model’s practicality and scalability potential.

Conclusions

To address challenges in daily runoff forecasting—including peak lag during flood seasons, underestimation in non-flood seasons, and seasonal non-stationarity—we propose a five-layer hybrid framework: “TVFEMD-LSSVM-BiLSTM-FSS-IBKA.” This approach employs TVFEMD for denoising, utilizes parallel LSSVM and BiLSTM models, and integrates FSS with IBKA for dynamic weighted fusion. In daily runoff predictions across two climatically and topographically distinct basins, this approach reduces RMSE by over 40% and increases KGE by up to 0.27, with no peak lag, demonstrating cross-basin robustness. Key findings include:

Unlike traditional annual uniform modeling approaches for runoff prediction, the framework proposed in this paper significantly enhances the model’s adaptability to seasonal non-stationary characteristics of hydrological processes by incorporating the FSS strategy. Segmented modeling enables differentiated learning for runoff evolution patterns under distinct hydrological drivers, allowing LSSVM to better fit steady-state processes during non-flood seasons while BiLSTM excels in modeling flood peak responses and temporal dependencies during flood seasons. By physically matching hydrological state differences, the FSS strategy not only enhances the model’s analytical capability and generalization performance but also promotes complementary advantages among multiple models. This provides an effective solution for improving prediction stability and addressing diverse hydrological conditions.

IBKA outperformed both the original BKA and PSO in the CEC2022 benchmark tests. Across 10 test functions, IBKA achieved the optimal mean fitness on 8 functions with significantly lower standard deviation, demonstrating higher convergence stability and result reliability. For unimodal functions (e.g., F2, F3), its fitness decreased by approximately 40% compared to BKA, and for multimodal functions (e.g., F5, F6), it decreased by approximately 45% compared to PSO. This indicates that IBKA combines faster convergence speed with stronger global search capabilities. Although results were comparable on some functions, overall performance remained superior, validating IBKA’s adaptability and robustness across different problem types. This provides strong support for its application in optimizing parameters for runoff prediction models.

Unlike previous studies that only validated models in single watersheds, the proposed TVFEMD-LSSVM-BiLSTM-FSS-IBKA model achieved significant results in daily runoff prediction across two representative watersheds—Shebu and Dongjiang—with distinct hydrological characteristics. This demonstrates its broad applicability and robustness under varying hydrological conditions. In the Shebu basin, the model achieved an MAE of 12.47, RMSE of 17.62, R2 of 0.98, and KGE of 0.94. In the Dongjiang basin, the corresponding values were MAE 15.08, RMSE 21.35, R2 0.97, and KGE 0.92. These metrics significantly outperformed the comparison models, demonstrating the proposed model’s superior predictive accuracy and correlation. Notably, the model exhibited exceptional performance during the flood season characterized by concentrated bursts of heavy rainfall, achieving an RMSE reduction exceeding 40% compared to traditional models. This indicates its enhanced ability to capture the abrupt rise in runoff triggered by sudden heavy rainfall, effectively avoiding lag and underestimation in peak predictions. During low-flow conditions outside the flood season, KGE improved by 0.12–0.27, indicating the model’s ability to effectively characterize long-term stable trends and seasonal fluctuations in runoff processes. This dual capability of maintaining high accuracy and robustness under high hydrological uncertainty stems from the integrated effects of the decomposable–ensemble–optimization framework in eliminating noise interference, enhancing time-series dependency modeling, and optimizing weight allocation. Consequently, this model provides robust scientific support for runoff forecasting in complex watershed conditions.

Although the proposed TVFEMD-LSSVM-BiLSTM-FSS-IBKA model demonstrates excellent performance in daily runoff prediction, there remains room for further improvement. Future research could focus on enhancing computational efficiency through model lightweighting, while incorporating hydrological physical constraints and multi-source driving factors into the framework to improve model interpretability and cross-scenario generalization capabilities. Additionally, although the present analysis is based on the five-year dataset (2001–2005) due to the availability of continuous, high-quality daily runoff observations for the study basin, extending the evaluation to longer-term runoff records will be an important direction for future work. Such multi-decadal datasets would allow a more comprehensive assessment of the long-term stability of the proposed hybrid framework and its adaptability under broader hydrological regimes. Furthermore, advancing multi-step rolling forecasts and uncertainty quantification methods will facilitate broader application of the model in practical hydrological operations and risk management.

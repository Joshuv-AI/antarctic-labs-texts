Freeze-in Production of Non-Abelian Millicharged Vector Dark Matter
Van Que Tran, Tzu-Chiang Yuan

Abstract
We present the first predictive realization of vector freeze-in dark matter from a hidden non-Abelian gauge sector, spontaneously broken to a residual U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) with a massless dark photon mediator. A massive dark vector particle-antiparticle pair acquires small millicharges via a dimension-five kinetic mixing operator that induces a dimension-four mixing term with effective coefficient ϵ \epsilon , and interacts through the hidden gauge coupling g D g_{D} , linking it weakly to the Standard Model. Solving the relic abundance with a two-temperature Boltzmann evolution including plasmon decays, we find that ϵ , g D ∼ 10 − 7 \epsilon,g_{D}\sim 10^{-7} reproduce the observed density while satisfying astrophysical and cosmological bounds. This minimal framework links non-Abelian vector dynamics, long-range dark forces, and dark matter, and can be testable with upcoming sub-GeV dark matter direct-detection experiments.



I
Introduction

The nature of dark matter (DM) remains a central mystery in particle physics and cosmology. Hidden gauge sectors provide a natural setting for stable dark states and long-range forces, with novel dynamics arising when non-Abelian symmetries are spontaneously broken to Abelian subgroups. In this context, DM can acquire a tiny electric millicharge, allowing suppressed but observable interactions with the Standard Model (SM). Compared to Abelian setups
40
;
30

25

In this work, we study a minimal SM extension with a hidden
SU(2)_{D}
broken by a real Higgs triplet to
U(1)_{D}
. A massive dark vector particle-antiparticle pair acquires small millicharges via a dimension-five kinetic mixing operator that induces a dimension-four term with effective coefficient
\epsilon

g_{D}
. The remaining massless dark photon acts as a long-range mediator. Strong cosmological bounds require the pair to decouple before recombination or form a subdominant DM component; in the former case, freeze-in production generates the correct relic abundance without violating constraints

III
Freeze-in production

Because the portal couplings
\epsilon,\delta,g_{D}\ll 1
, the hidden sector never thermalizes with the SM bath. Instead, dark particles are produced gradually via freeze-in, sourced by SM annihilations, decays, and in-medium processes such as plasmon decay. As matter accumulates in the hidden sector, interactions among hidden particles become relevant and proceed concurrently with freeze-in. If kinematically allowed, hidden states may also decay back into SM particles.

We employ a two-temperature Boltzmann framework, with
T
and
T_{h}
denoting the visible and hidden temperatures. The evolution of the ratio
\xi=T_{h}/T
, accounting for feeble energy transfer, is governed by
5
;
3
;
4
;
46
(see Appendix
D
for detailed derivations.)

\frac{d\xi}{dT}=\frac{1}{T}\left[-\xi+\left(\frac{3H(\rho_{h}+p_{h})-J_{h}}{3H(\rho_{v}+p_{v})+J_{h}}\right)\frac{d\rho_{v}}{dT}\left(\frac{d\rho_{h}}{dT_{h}}\right)^{-1}\right]\;,

(9)

where
H
is the Hubble parameter, and
\rho_{v,h}
and
p_{v,h}
are the energy and pressure densities of the visible and hidden sectors (see Appendix
E
for explicit forms). The source function
J_{h}
encodes all energy-exchange processes between the two sectors; its explicit form is given in Appendix
G
.

The relic abundance is obtained by solving coupled Boltzmann equations for the comoving yields
Y_{i}=n_{i}/\mathbf{s}
,

\frac{dY_{i}}{dT}=-\frac{1}{\mathbf{s}}\left[\frac{d\rho_{v}/dT}{3H(\rho_{v}+p_{v})+J_{h}}\right]\,{\mathcal{R}}_{i}(T,T_{h})\;,

(10)

where
\mathbf{s}
is the total entropy density,
n_{i}
the number density, and
\mathcal{R}_{i}(T,T_{h})
the interaction rate producing species
i
, including all three SM
\to
hidden, hidden
\to
SM, and hidden
\to
hidden channels. We track all three species: the dark matter candidate with
n_{W^{m}}=n_{W^{p}}\equiv n_{W^{\prime}}
, the dark photon, and the hidden Higgs. Explicit expressions for the rates
\mathcal{R}_{i}
, including cross sections and decay widths, are provided in Appendices
F
,
H
and
I

VI
Conclusion

We have presented a minimal freeze-in scenario where dark matter consists of a massive dark vector particle-antiparticle pair from a hidden non-Abelian gauge sector, spontaneously broken to a residual
U(1)
with a massless dark photon mediator. The pair acquires small millicharges via a dimension-five kinetic mixing operator that induces a dimension-four term with effective coefficient
\epsilon
and interacts through the hidden gauge coupling
g_{D}
, enabling freeze-in production from the SM plasma. For portal couplings
\epsilon,g_{D}\sim 10^{-7}
, the observed relic abundance is reproduced while satisfying astrophysical and cosmological bounds. Interestingly, the freeze-in parameter space of this model lies within reach of upcoming sub-GeV direct-detection experiments. In conclusion, this framework provides a predictive, experimentally testable scenario connecting hidden non-Abelian dynamics, long-range dark forces, and vector freeze-in dark matter.

Appendix C
UV Completion for the Dimension-5 Operator

A minimal ultraviolet (UV) completion that generates the dimension-5 operator
in the main text can be obtained by introducing a vector-like Dirac lepton

\Psi(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2},y)

which is an
SU(2)_{D}
doublet carrying hypercharge
y

SU(2)_{L}

(45)

D_{\mu}\Psi=(\partial_{\mu}-ig_{D}W_{D\mu}^{a}T_{D}^{a}-ig^{\prime}yB_{\mu})\Psi,

(46)

y_{\Psi}
is a complex Yukawa coupling,

M_{\Psi}
is heavy lepton mass
and
\Sigma=\Sigma^{a}T_{D}^{a}
.
The Yukawa terms in Eq. (
45
)
splits the heavy lepton doublet mass once

\Sigma
acquires a VEV.

Integrating out the heavy lepton at one loop
induces a gauge-invariant
dimension-5 kinetic-mixing operator
connecting the non-Abelian and Abelian field strengths.
The relevant triangle diagram contains
one
\Sigma
insertion and two external gauge bosons

W_{D}^{\mu\nu}
and
B^{\mu\nu}
.
In the low-energy limit
|p^{2}|\ll M_{\Psi}^{2}
,
matching the amplitude to dimension-5 operator yields
14

\frac{c_{5}}{\Lambda}\simeq\frac{y\,{\rm Re}[y_{\Psi}]}{12\pi^{2}M_{\Psi}}.

(47)

Taking
y=-1
,
y_{\Psi}\sim 5
and
M_{\Psi}\sim{\cal O}(1)
TeV
naturally yields the kinetic mixing parameter

for
m_{W^{\prime}}\sim 0.01
GeV,
consistent with the value required for successful freeze-in production.
The small kinetic mixing is therefore technically natural,
arising from loop and heavy-mass suppression without fine tuning.

The heavy Dirac lepton carries electric charge

Current LHC searches for such heavy leptons with unit electric charge
exclude masses below about
1
TeV for promptly decaying states
2

37

We note that the heavy lepton also carries

charge
and efficiently annihilates into dark gauge bosons before decaying,

No light remnants remain, preventing contributions to

This simple UV completion demonstrates that the dimension-5 operator and the resulting millicharge parameter

arise naturally from a renormalizable, gauge-invariant theory,
with loop suppression and heavy-mass scaling that justify

Appendix D
Evolution of the temperature ratio
\xi=T_{h}/T

In this and subsequent three appendices, we follow the formalism of Refs.
5
;
3
;
4
;
46
closely to review the derivation of the evolution of the temperature ratio
\xi=T_{h}/T
between the hidden and visible sectors with temperature
T_{h}
and
T
respectively.

We start from the continuity equation,

\frac{d\rho}{dt}+3H(\rho+p)=0\;,

(48)

where
\rho=\rho_{v}+\rho_{h}
is the total energy density and
p=p_{v}+p_{h}
is the total pressure.

Since the hidden and visible sectors feebly interact to each other, one can obtain the energy density evolution equation for each sector by

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{v}}{dt}+3H(\rho_{v}+p_{v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-J_{h}\;,

(49)

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{h}}{dt}+3H(\rho_{h}+p_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{h}\;,

(50)

where the quantity
J_{h}
(
-J_{h}
) represents the source (sink) term of the hidden (visible) sector, encompassing all freeze-in and decay processes that mediate energy exchange between the sectors.
The explicit expression of
J_{h}
is given in Appendix
G
.

Using the chain rule and together with Eqs. (
49
) and (
50
), we obtain

\frac{dT_{h}}{dT}=A\,\frac{d\rho_{v}}{dT}\left(\frac{d\rho_{h}}{dT_{h}}\right)^{-1}\;,

(51)

where

A\equiv\frac{d\rho_{h}}{dt}\left(\frac{d\rho_{v}}{dt}\right)^{-1}=\frac{3H(\rho_{h}+p_{h})-J_{h}}{3H(\rho_{v}+p_{v})+J_{h}}\;.

(52)

Now, together with Eq. (
51
), the evolution equation of the temperature ratio
\xi
respect to the visible temperature is given by

\frac{d\xi}{dT}=\frac{1}{T}\left(-\xi+\frac{dT_{h}}{dT}\right)=\frac{1}{T}\left[-\xi+A\,\frac{d\rho_{v}}{dT}\left(\frac{d\rho_{h}}{dT_{h}}\right)^{-1}\right]\;.

(53)

Hence, knowing
\xi(T)
as a function of the SM temperature
T
allows one to compute the hidden temperature as
T_{h}(T)=T\cdot\xi(T)
.

Phase transition on randomly horizontally stretched square lattice
Isadora Guedes ∗,† , Paulo C Lima ∗ , Marcos Sá ⋄ , Remy Sanchis ∗

Abstract
. In this article, we study a bond percolation model on a horizontally stretched square lattice, constructed by stretching the distances between the columns of ℤ + 2 \mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2} according to a collection of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) copies of a non-negative random variable ξ \xi . We assume that ξ \xi satisfies the integrability condition 𝔼 [ ξ e c ​ ( log ⁡ ξ ) 1 / 2 𝟙 { ξ ≥ 1 } ] < ∞ , \mathbb{E}\big[\xi\,e^{c(\log\xi)^{1/2}}\,\mathbb{1}_{\{\xi\geq 1\}}\big]<\infty, for some constant c > 8 ​ log ⁡ 96 c>8\sqrt{\log 96} . In this random environment, each vertical edge is independently declared open with probability p p , while each horizontal edge is open with probability p | e | p^{|e|} , where | e | |e| denotes the Euclidean length of the edge. We develop a multiscale renormalization scheme adapted to this geometry and use it to prove that percolation occurs for all sufficiently large values of p < 1 p<1 .



Keywords:
percolation, horizontally stretched lattice, phase transition, random environments, multiscale renormalization.

1.
Introduction

Our goal in this work is to investigate the effect of introducing inhomogeneities on the lattice, specifically understanding how they affect the phase transition in percolation models. In some cases, inhomogeneities arise through the introduction of environments that specify the rules for assigning probabilities
p_{e}
to each edge
e
of the graph.

We will consider the square lattice,
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and one way of introducing inhomogeneities is by fixing certain columns, which will form what we will call the environment. In these columns, the edges will be open with probability
p
, while the remaining edges will be open with probability
q
, for some
p,q\in[0,1]
. Formally, given a subset
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}
, we define the set

\text{E}_{\text{vert}}=\big\{\langle(x,y),(x,y+1)\rangle:x\in\Lambda,y\in\mathbb{Z}\big\},

which contains the edges of those columns which project into
\Lambda
. Given
p,q\in[0,1]
, an edge
e\in E(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
will be open with the probability

p_{e}=\begin{cases}p,\text{ if }e\in\text{E}_{\text{vert}}\\
q,\text{ if }e\notin\text{E}_{\text{vert}}\end{cases}.

We will denote by
\mathbb{P}_{p,q}^{\Lambda}(\cdot)
the quenched probability law in
\{0,1\}^{E(\mathbb{Z}^{2})}
which governs this percolation model and we will refer to it as percolation on the randomly horizontally stretched lattice in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

In the special case where
\Lambda=\{0\}
, Zhang
[
13
]
showed that
\mathbb{P}_{p,q}^{\{0\}}((0,0)\leftrightarrow\infty)=0
, for any
p\in[0,1)
and
q\leq p_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})=\frac{1}{2}
. He used arguments similar to those of Harris in
[
6
]
, involving the construction of dual circuits around the origin, together with the Russo, Seymour and Welsh
[
11
,
12
]
techniques. On the other extreme, when
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}
, Kesten (see Section 11.9 in
[
5
]
) showed that
\mathbb{P}_{p,q}^{\mathbb{Z}}((0,0)\leftrightarrow\infty)>0
if and only if
p+q>1
.
In the case where
\Lambda
consists only of bounded gaps, namely, there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that for every
l\in\mathbb{Z},\;\Lambda\cap[l,l+k]\neq\emptyset
, a classic argument due to Aizenman and Grimmett
[
1
]
ensured that for any
\epsilon>0
there is
\delta=\delta(k,\epsilon)>0
such that
\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}+\epsilon,p_{c}-\delta}^{\Lambda}((0,0)\leftrightarrow\infty)>0
, where
p_{c}=p_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
.

All the examples above involve deterministic environments. We now turn to models in which they are random ones.

For each
\rho\in[0,1]
, let
\nu_{\rho}
be the probability measure on subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
in which the events
\{i\in\Lambda\}
are independent, each occurring with probability
\rho
. Duminil-Copin et al
[
3
]
showed that for any
\epsilon>0
and
\rho>0
there is a
\delta=\delta(\rho,\epsilon)>0
such that
\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}+\epsilon,p_{c}-\delta}^{\Lambda}((0,0)\leftrightarrow\infty)>0
, for
\nu_{\rho}
-almost everywhere environment, where
p_{c}=p_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
.
In
[
2
]
, the authors considered the Contact Process on
\mathbb{Z}
with an environment given by
\nu_{\rho}
. Their results, translated into Percolation, are that for any
\rho\in[0,1)
, there exists a sufficiently large
p<1
such that
\mathbb{P}_{0,p}(o\leftrightarrow\infty)>0
for
\nu_{\rho}
-almost every environment
\Lambda
. This means that if edges of columns are deleted according to Bernoulli trials with mean
\rho
, the phase transition is present. Hoffman
[
9
]
examined a similar case, where both rows and columns are independently deleted with the same probability
\rho
and proved that the model still undergoes a non-trivial phase transition, see also
[
8
]
.

Hilário, Sá, Sanchis and Teixeira
[
7
]
considered a random environment
\Lambda
obtained by stretching horizontally the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
according to a positive random variable
\xi
, namely, they considered independent and identically distributed copies of a positive random variable
\xi
satisfying
\mathbb{E}(\xi^{\eta})<\infty
, for some
\eta>1
, to stretch the distance between the columns of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
, obtaining a horizontally stretched square lattice. By using a multiscale renormalization scheme, they proved that the model exhibits a non-trivial phase transition. On the other hand, in the same work, the authors also showed that, if
\mathbb{E}(\xi)=\infty
, the phase transition is trivial, leaving a gap on the moment conditions.

Aiming to fill the gap mentioned above, we assume that
\mathbb{E}\big(\xi e^{c(\log\xi)^{1/2}}\mathbb{1}_{\{\xi\geq 1\}}\big)<\infty
, for a constant
c>8\sqrt{\log 96}
, and using a finer multiscale renormalization scheme, our Theorem
1
, shows that the model still undergoes a non-trivial phase transition.

In
[
4
]
the authors studied the so-called Renewal Contact Process on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and derived a condition—closely related to ours—ensuring the existence of a non-trivial subcritical phase. Despite of this similarity, both their renormalization scheme and their results differ from ours.

This article is organized as follows: in Section
2
we describe the model and state Theorem
1
which is our main result. In Section
3
we prove the decoupling inequality given in Lemma
3
. In Section
4
we describe our multiscale scheme which consists of two parts: in Subsection
4.1
we control the environments and in Subsection
4.2
we control the vertical and the horizontal crossings. Finally, in Section
5
, we prove Theorem
1
.

2.
The Model and Results

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
be the set of all nonnegative integers and denote
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{*}=\mathbb{Z}_{+}\backslash\{0\}
. We will consider percolation in the lattice obtained from
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
stretching randomly its horizontal edges. Formally speaking, let
\xi
be a positive random variable and
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
a sequence of i.i.d. copies of
\xi
and consider

\Lambda=\left\{\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\xi_{i}:k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\right\},

which is called an environment.
Notice that,
\Lambda
can also be seen as the following increasing sequence

(1)

\Lambda=\{x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}:x_{0}=0\text{ and }x_{k}=x_{k-1}+\xi_{k},\text{ for }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{*}\}.

This sequence is called a renewal process with interarrival distribution
\xi
. We will denote by
\mu_{\xi}(\cdot)
the probability measure that governs this renewal process.

Given a realization of an environment
\Lambda
we can define the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda}=(V_{\Lambda},E_{\Lambda})
where the vertex and edge sets are given, respectively, by

V_{\Lambda}=\Lambda\times\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x\in\Lambda,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}

and

E_{\Lambda}=\{\langle(x_{i},n),(x_{j},m)\rangle:|i-j|+|n-m|=1,\text{ with }x_{i},x_{j}\in\Lambda\text{ and }n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\},

see Figure
1
-(a).

Notice that
\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda}
can be seen as the lattice obtained by
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
by stretching or contracting the horizontal edges in such a way that
\xi_{i+1}
gives the random separation between the
i
-th and
(i+1)
-th column in the stretched lattice.

We will also consider a bond percolation process in
\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda}
as follows. For each
p\in[0,1]
, denote by
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda}(\cdot)
the probability measure on
\{0,1\}^{E_{\Lambda}}
under which the random variables
\{\nu(e)\}_{e\in E_{\Lambda}}
are independent Bernoulli random variables with mean

(2)

p_{e}=p^{|e|},

where, for each

,
|e|=||v_{1}-v_{2}||
denotes the length of
e
, with
||\cdot||
meaning the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We say that an edge

is open if
\omega(e)=1
, and closed otherwise. We write
\{(0,0)\leftrightarrow\infty\}
to represent the event that there is an infinite open path starting at
(0,0)
which only uses open edges.

In the case that

\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda}

\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}

\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}

Since all the formulations given above are equivalent, we will make a slight abuse of notation by also denoting the probability law of all versions by
\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda}(\cdot)
.

Next we state our main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\xi
be a positive random variable that satisfies
\mathbb{E}\big(\xi e^{c(\log\xi)^{1/2}}\mathbb{1}_{\{\xi\geq 1\}}\big)<\infty
, for some constant
c>8\sqrt{\log 96}
. Then there is a critical point
p_{c}\in(0,1)
, depending on the law of
\xi
only, such that for
p<p_{c}
, we have

(5)

\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda}((0,0)\leftrightarrow\infty)=0,\text{ for }\mu_{\xi}\text{-almost all }\Lambda,

and, for
p>p_{c}
, we have

(6)

\mathbb{P}_{p}^{\Lambda}((0,0)\leftrightarrow\infty)>0,\text{ for }\mu_{\xi}\text{-almost all }\Lambda.

The following sections will be devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
.

(10)

\lambda_{k}=\mathbb{P}(\rho=k):=\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{E}(\xi)}\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\xi=i),\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

independent of everything else. Consider the renewal process
Z(\xi,\rho)
, defined in (

Z_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Z_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\rho,\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

For this and since
Z

(11)

\theta_{m}Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Z,\text{ for any }m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{*},

where
\theta_{m}
is the left shift operator, namely,
\theta_{m}:\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
, given by

\theta_{m}(x_{0},x_{1},\dots)=(x_{m},x_{m+1},\dots).

For this reason, for a fixed
\xi
, the random variable
\rho
, given in (
10
), is called stationary delay.

For each
c>0
, let

(12)

F_{c}(x)=e^{c(\log x)^{1/2}},

where
x\geq 1
. Using (
10
), we can show the following result.

Lemma 1
.

If
\xi
is a positive random variable satisfying
\mathbb{E}\big(\xi F_{c}(\xi)\mathbb{1}_{\{\xi\geq 1\}}\big)<\infty
and
c>0
, then the stationary delay
\rho=\rho(\xi)
, given by (
10
), satisfies
\mathbb{E}\big(F_{c}(\rho)\mathbb{1}_{\{\rho\geq 1\}}\big)<\infty
.

We say that a random variable
\xi
is aperiodic if

\gcd\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{*}:\mathbb{P}(\xi=k)>0\}=1.

In what follows we will assume that
\xi
is aperiodic and
\mathbb{E}(\xi)<\infty
. Let
Z=Z(\xi,\chi)
and
Z^{\prime}=Z^{\prime}(\xi,\chi^{\prime})
be two independent renewal processes with interarrival time
\xi
and delays
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
, respectively. Define

(13)

T=\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{*}:Z_{k}=Z^{\prime}_{k}=0\},

as the coupling time of
X
and
X^{\prime}
. We will denote by
\nu^{\chi,\chi^{\prime}}_{\xi}(\cdot)
the product measure
\nu_{\xi}^{\chi}\otimes\nu_{\xi}^{\chi^{\prime}}
.

Now we will establish the decoupling inequality for stationary renewal processes given in Lemma
3
. For that, using the Markov inequality, we will need to get an upper bound of the form
\mu_{\xi}^{\chi,\chi^{\prime}}(T>n)\leq\dfrac{c_{0}}{F_{c}(n)}
, where
c_{0}
is a constant. Define

(14)

\widetilde{c}=\max\Big\{e^{\left(\frac{\log 2}{c}\right)^{2}},e^{1/2}\Big\}.

Lemma 2

(Proposition 3,

Suppose that
\xi
is an aperiodic and integer-valued random variable taking values greater that

and satisfies
\mathbb{E}\big(\xi F_{c}(\xi)\big)<\infty
, where

is given in (

). Also suppose that
\chi
,
\chi^{\prime}
are non-negative integer-valued random variables with
\mathbb{E}\big(F_{c}(\chi)\mathbb{1}_{\{\chi\geq 1\}}\big)
and
\mathbb{E}\big(F_{c}(\chi^{\prime})\mathbb{1}_{\{\chi^{\prime}\geq 1\}}\big)
finite. Then,
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{\chi,\chi^{\prime}}\big(F_{c}(T)\mathbb{1}_{\{T\geq 1\}}\big)<\infty
, where

is given by (
13

Proof:
From Proposition 3 of
[

]
, in order to prove the lemma above it is enough to show that the function

(i)

is non-increasing, for all

and

Notice that conditions (i) and (ii) follow from the definition of

The next lemma gives the desired decoupling inequality for stationary renewals.

Lemma 3

(
Decoupling Inequality).

Let
\xi
be an aperiodic and integer-valued random variable taking values greater than
\widetilde{c}
and satisfies
\mathbb{E}\big(\xi F_{c}(\xi)\big)<\infty
, where
F_{c}
is given in (
12
). Consider the associated renewal process
Z
defined in (
8
). Then, there exists a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(\xi,c)>0
such that for all
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and for every pair of events
A
and
B
, with

A\in\sigma(Z_{j}:0\leq j\leq m)\text{ and }B\in\sigma(Z_{j}:j\geq m+2n),

we have

(15)

\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A\cap B)\leq\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(B)+\dfrac{c_{1}}{F_{c}(n)}.

Proof:
If
\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)=0
, we are done. So let us suppose that
\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)>0
. Using the definition of the renewal process
Z
given in (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A\cap B)

\displaystyle=\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A\cap B\cap\{Z_{m}>n\})+\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A\cap B\cap\{Z_{m}\leq n\})

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}>n)+\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(B\cap\{Z_{m}\leq n\}|A)

\displaystyle=\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}>n)+\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq n\\
\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}=i|A)>0\end{subarray}}\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(B|A\cap\{Z_{m}=i\})\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}=i|A)

\displaystyle=\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}>n)+\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq n\\
\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}=i|A)>0\end{subarray}}\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(B|\{Z_{m}=i\})\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}=i|A)

\displaystyle=\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}>n)+\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{m+i}}(B)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq n\\
\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}=i|A)>0\end{subarray}}\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}=i|A)

(16)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{m}>n)+\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{m+i}}(B).

Now, we need compare the measures
\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{m+i}}
and
\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}
, when
0\leq i\leq n
. For this, notice that
\mu_{\xi}^{\delta{m+i}}(B)=\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{0}}(\theta_{m+i}(B))
and that we have given a interval of size
2n-i
for the renewal processes with delays
\delta_{0}
and
\rho
to couple. So, by the stationarity of
\rho
, we have

\displaystyle|\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{m+i}}(B)-\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(B)|

\displaystyle=|\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{0}}(\theta_{m+i}(B))-\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(\theta_{m+i}(B))|

\displaystyle=\big|\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{0}}\big(\theta_{2n-i}Y\in\theta_{m+i}(B)\big)-\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}\big(\theta_{2n-i}Y\in\theta_{m+i}(B)\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{0},\rho}(T>2n-i)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{0},\rho}(T>n),

where the last inequality follows since
0\leq i\leq n
. Thus, for all
0\leq i\leq n
, we get

\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{m+i}}(B)\leq\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(B)+\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{0},\rho}(T>n).

Using this inequality in (
16
) and using that
Z_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Z_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\rho
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A\cap B)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(\rho>n)+\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(B)+\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{0},\rho}(T>n)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(B)+\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(\rho>n)+\mu_{\xi}^{\delta_{0},\rho}(T>n)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A)\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(B)+\dfrac{\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{\rho}\big(F_{c}(\rho)\big)}{F_{c}(n)}+\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{\delta_{0},\rho}\big(F_{c}(T)\big)}{F_{c}(n)},

where the last inequality follows from the Markov inequality for
F_{c}(\rho)
and
F_{c}(T)
. We conclude the proof by taking
c_{1}=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{\rho}\big(F_{c}(\rho)\big)+\mathbb{E}_{\xi}^{\delta_{0},\rho}\big(F_{c}(T)\big)
, which is finite by Lemmas
1
and
2
.
∎

4.
The Multiscale Scheme

In this section, we will present our multiscale scheme, which consists of two main parts: environments and crossing events. In Subsection
4.1
, we will define an increasing sequence of numbers
L_{k}
,
k\geq 0
, called scales, and use them to partition the set
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
into intervals of length
L_{k}
, named
k
-intervals. We will find some integer
k_{0}
, which will satisfy some conditions that will appear throughout the text, and we will label the
k
-intervals as good or bad, recursively, for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Next, we will show that, with strictly positive probability, there is an environment that has the following property: the first two
k
-intervals are good, for all

. In Subsection

, we will construct horizontal and vertical crossings of rectangles whose sides depend on

and prove that, within

-good intervals, such crossings have a very high probability to occur. This allows us to build an infinite open cluster in the proof of Theorem

.

4.1.
Environments

Given
c>0
, for any
e^{-c^{2}/32}<\alpha<1
, let

(17)

A=A(\alpha,c)=\alpha e^{c^{2}/32}.

Notice that
1<A<e^{c^{2}/32}
, in particular,

(18)

\dfrac{c}{4}>\sqrt{2\log A}.

Consider the sequence of numbers
(L_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
, called scales, defined recursively by

(19)

L_{0}=\lfloor A\rfloor\quad\text{and}\quad L_{k}=\big\lfloor A^{k+1}\big\rfloor L_{k-1},\text{ for all }k\geq 1.

Given
x>1
, then
\dfrac{x}{2}\leq\lfloor x\rfloor\leq x
. In fact, the upper bound follows by definition of the function
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
. To get the lower bound, notice that if
1<x<2
, then
\lfloor x\rfloor=1>\dfrac{x}{2}
. On the other hand, if
x\geq 2
, then
\lfloor x\rfloor\geq x-1=x(1-1/x)\geq\dfrac{x}{2}
. Therefore, for all
k\geq 0
, we have

(20)

\dfrac{A^{k+1}}{2}\leq\lfloor A^{k+1}\rfloor\leq A^{k+1}.

From (
19
) and (
20
), it follows by induction, that for
k\geq 1
, we have

(21)

\dfrac{A^{\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}}}{2^{k+1}}\leq L_{k}\leq A^{\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}},

which implies

(22)

L_{k}\leq A^{\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}}\leq 2^{k+1}L_{k},

and so,

(23)

L_{k}^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\leq A^{k+2}\leq 4\,L_{k}^{\frac{2}{k+1}}.

Using (
23
), (
19
) and (
20
), we have

(24)

\dfrac{1}{2}\,L_{k}^{\frac{k+3}{k+1}}\leq L_{k+1}\leq 4\,L_{k}^{\frac{k+3}{k+1}}

and from (
24
) and (
21
), respectively, it follows that

\displaystyle\log L_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dfrac{\log 4}{\log L_{k}}+\dfrac{k+3}{k+1}\right)\log L_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dfrac{2\log 4}{(k+1)\big[(k+2)\log A-2\log 2\big]}+\dfrac{k+3}{k+1}\right)\log L_{k}

(25)

\displaystyle=:r(k,A)\,\log L_{k}.

Also, from (
21
), we obtain

(26)

\dfrac{4}{k+1}(\log L_{k})^{1/2}\leq 2\sqrt{2}\left(\dfrac{k+2}{k+1}\right)^{1/2}(\log A)^{1/2}.

Let
c_{1}
be the constant given in Lemma
3
and take
k_{0}=k_{0}(c,\alpha,c_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
sufficiently large such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
the following three conditions (which will be necessary in the prove of Lemma
4
) hold:

(27)

c-2\left(\dfrac{k+2}{k+1}\right)^{1/2}(2\log A)^{1/2}-\dfrac{c}{2}\big(r(k,A)\big)^{1/2}>\dfrac{c}{4}-\sqrt{2\log A},

(28)

\exp\left[-\left(\dfrac{c}{4}-\sqrt{2\log A}\right)\left(\dfrac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}\right)^{1/2}(\log A)^{1/2}\right]<\dfrac{1}{16(c_{1}+1)},

(29)

\mathbb{E}\big(e^{c(\log\rho)^{1/2}}\mathbb{1}_{\{\rho\geq 1\}}\big)\leq\exp\left(\dfrac{c\,\,(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}{2}\right).

For each
k\geq k_{0}
, we divide
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
into disjoint intervals of length
L_{k}
. The
i
-th interval of scale
k
, denoted by
I_{i}^{k}
, is defined by

(30)

I_{i}^{k}=[iL_{k},(i+1)L_{k}),\;\text{for }i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Notice that each interval of scale
k
can be partitioned into
\lfloor A^{k+1}\rfloor
sub-intervals of scale
k-1
, namely,

I_{i}^{k}=\bigcup_{l\in\mathcal{I}_{k,i}}I_{l}^{k-1},

where
\mathcal{I}_{k,i}=\left\{i\lfloor A^{k+1}\rfloor,i\lfloor A^{k+1}\rfloor+1,\dots,(i+1)\lfloor A^{k+1}\rfloor-1\right\}
represents the set of indices for the sub-intervals of scale
k-1
which are within of
I_{i}^{k}
.

Given an environment
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{+}
as in (
1
) and
k\geq k_{0}
, we will label the intervals
I_{i}^{k}
either as
good
or
bad
, recursively. For
k=k_{0}
, we declare
I_{i}^{k_{0}}\;
good if
\Lambda\cap I_{i}^{k_{0}}\neq\emptyset
, that is, if there exists at least one column of
\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda}
present; otherwise, we declare it bad. For
k>k_{0}
, assuming that all intervals at scale
k-1
have been defined, we declare
I_{i}^{k}\;
bad if it has at least two non-consecutive bad intervals of the scale
k-1
; otherwise, we declare it as good. Notice that a good interval at scale
k
can have a maximum of two bad intervals of the scale
k-1
and, in this case, these intervals must be adjacent because otherwise we would have two non-consecutive bad intervals.

For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
k\geq k_{0}
, let
A_{i}^{k}
be the event

A_{i}^{k}=\{I_{i}^{k}\text{ is bad}\}.

Sometimes we will write
\{I_{i}^{k}\text{ is good}\}
for the complement of
A_{i}^{k}
. Define

(31)

p_{k}:=\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A_{0}^{k})=\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A_{i}^{k}),

where the last equality follows from the stationarity of
\rho
.

To get an upper bound for
p_{k+1}
in terms of
p_{k}
, we notice that

\displaystyle p_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(\text{there is at least two non-consecutive bad intervals on the scale }k)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{\lfloor A^{(k+2)}\rfloor}{2}\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}\big(A_{0}^{k}\cap A_{2}^{k}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{\lfloor A^{(k+2)}\rfloor}{2}\left(p_{k}^{2}+\dfrac{c_{1}}{e^{c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A^{2(k+2)}}{2}\left(p_{k}^{2}+\dfrac{c_{1}}{e^{c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}}\right),

where the second inequality follows from the decoupling inequality given in Lemma
3
.

The next result shows that the probability of an interval being bad decreases exponentially fast in
k
, for all scales
k\geq k_{0}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
c>0
and
k_{0}
satisfying (
27
)-(
29
), then for all
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

(33)

p_{k}\leq\exp{\left(-\dfrac{c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}{2}\right)}.

Proof:
The proof of this lemma will be done by induction in
k
. For
k=k_{0}
, using (
31
), the stationarity of
\rho
, the Markov inequality and (
29
), respectively, we get

p_{k_{0}}=\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(A_{0}^{k_{0}})=\mu_{\xi}^{\rho}(Z_{0}>L_{k_{0}})=\mathbb{P}(\rho>L_{k_{0}})\leq\dfrac{\mathbb{E}\big(e^{c(\log\rho)^{1/2}}\mathbb{1}_{\{\rho\geq 1\}}\big)}{e^{c(\log L_{k_{0}})^{1/2}}}\leq\exp\left(-\dfrac{c(\log L_{k_{0}})^{1/2}}{2}\right).

Now suppose that, for some
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

(34)

p_{k}\leq\exp\left(-\dfrac{c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}{2}\right),

then

\displaystyle p_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{A^{2(k+2)}}{2}\left(p_{k}^{2}+\dfrac{c_{1}}{e^{c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{A^{2(k+2)}}{2}\left(e^{-c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}+c_{1}e^{-c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{c_{1}+1}{2}\;A^{2(k+2)}e^{-c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}

(35)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{c_{1}+1}{2}\;4^{2}L_{k}^{\frac{4}{k+1}}e^{-c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}},

where the inequalities above follow from (
32
), (
34
) and (
23
) respectively.
Hence, using (
35
), (
25
), (
26
), (
27
), (
21
) and (
28
), respectively, we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{p_{k+1}}{\exp\left(-\frac{c(\log L_{k+1})^{1/2}}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{c_{1}+1}{2}\,16\,L_{k}^{\frac{4}{k+1}}e^{-c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}\exp\left(\frac{c(\log L_{k+1})^{1/2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8(c_{1}+1)\;\exp\left[\dfrac{4}{k+1}\log L_{k}-c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}+\dfrac{c(\log L_{k+1})^{1/2}}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8(c_{1}+1)\;\exp\left[-(\log L_{k})^{1/2}\left(c-\dfrac{4}{k+1}\left(\dfrac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}\right)^{1/2}(\log A)^{1/2}-\dfrac{c}{2}\big(r(k,A)\big)^{1/2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8(c_{1}+1)\exp\left[-\left(\dfrac{c}{4}-\sqrt{2\log A}\right)(\log L_{k})^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8(c_{1}+1)\exp\left[-\left(\dfrac{c}{4}-\sqrt{2\log A}\right)\left(\dfrac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}\right)^{1/2}(\log A)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8(c_{1}+1)\;\dfrac{1}{16(c_{1}+1)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 1,

which concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Using the upper bound of
p_{k}
that we have just proved, it follows that with strictly positive probability, the environment
\Lambda
has the property that the intervals

and

are good for all scale

. Moreover, we can show that all their subintervals are also good.

.

Consider

Observe that

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8L_{k}^{\frac{2}{k+1}}\exp{\left(-\dfrac{c(\log L_{k})^{1/2}}{2}\right)}

4.2.
Crossing Events

In this section we will study the probability of crossing events within special rectangles in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}

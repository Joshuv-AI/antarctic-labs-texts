A Novel Approach to Peng’s Maximum Principle for McKean-Vlasov Stochastic Differential Equations
Johan Benedikt Spille, Wilhelm Stannat

Abstract
We present a novel approach to the proof of Peng’s maximum principle for McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equations (SDE). The main step is the introduction of a third adjoint equation, a conditional McKean-Vlasov backward SDE, to accommodate the dualization of quadratic terms containing two independent copies of the first-order variational process. This is an intrinsic extension of the maximum principle from Peng for standard SDE and gives a conceptually consistent proof. Our approach will be useful in further extensions to the common noise setting and the infinite dimensional setting.

Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the following optimization problem: Minimize over all admissible controls the cost functional

J(\alpha)=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}f(t,X_{t},\mu_{t},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+g(X_{T},\mu_{T})\right],

(1)

where we fix a terminal time
T>0
and
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times U\to\mathbb{R}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
are deterministic functions, subject to the state equation, given by the controlled McKean-Vlasov SDE

dX_{t}=A(t,X_{t},\mu_{t},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+B(t,X_{t},\mu_{t},\alpha_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\qquad X_{0}=x_{0},

(2)

where
\mu_{t}:=\mathcal{L}(X_{t})
denotes the distribution of
X_{t}
,

(A,B):[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times U\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
are deterministic and

W
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion on a complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
such that
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
is the augmented filtration generated by
W
. Here, an admissible control process
\alpha=(\alpha_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
takes values in a non-empty, not necessarily convex set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, is progressively measurable and
\mathbb{E}[\int_{0}^{T}|\alpha_{t}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t]<\infty.
The set of all admissible controls is denoted by
\mathbb{A}
.

The general goal is to deduce a necessary Pontryagin-type optimality condition, first developed by
19
in the deterministic setting. Later, this method was extended by
3
to SDEs.
The generalization to non-convex control domains requires in the stochastic setting a second-order Taylor expansion of the cost functional. To this end,
18
introduced a second-order adjoint state and derived a corresponding maximum principle, nowadays known as Peng’s maximum principle.

In many applications the dynamics of the controlled process depend not only on its current state but also on its distribution. These dynamics are modeled by McKean-Vlasov SDEs like (
2
), originally studied in the context of interacting particle systems
[
13
,
16
,
22
]
and now fundamental in mean-field games and large population control problems
[
14
,
8
]
. The optimal control of McKean-Vlasov dynamics has therefore attracted significant attention in recent years.

Early results use convex control domains, and derive Pontryagin maximum principles adapted to the McKean-Vlasov setting
[
2
,
7
,
8
,
12
]
. Such maximum principles have also been generalized to infinite dimensional settings
[
1
,
10
,
23
,
20
]
. In comparison Peng’s maximum principle for McKean-Vlasov SDEs has only been treated in finite dimensions so far.
These extensions to non-convex control domains with mean-field dependence were done by
4
,
5
,
6
.

The main goal of this paper now, is not to provide a new maximum principle. Instead we provide a novel approach to the proof of the maximum principle given by
5
.
Following the lines in
18
, the second-order Taylor expansion of the cost functional involves quadratic terms coming from the (mixed) Lions derivative with respect to
\mu_{t}
, which cannot be dualized in the same way as the quadratic terms coming from the derivative with respect to the state (cf. Remark
6.2
).
5
observed that these terms are in fact of lower-order, since they involve taking suitable expectations, and, thus, do not contribute to the maximum principle.
Clearly, this method does not work anymore as soon as these estimates do not hold (cf. applications below). Thus, we aim to give a more general and also conceptually consistent proof.

To this end, we introduce a third adjoint state (
26
) that, in particular, dualizes quadratic terms coming from the second order (mixed) Lions derivatives.
In contrast to the second adjoint equation, which is standard BSDE, the third adjoint equation is a conditional McKean-Vlasov BSDE.
While the second adjoint state can be seen as dualizing the homogeneous part of the first variational equation, the third adjoint equation dualizes the heterogeneous part. Importantly, the third adjoint process, does not appear in the maximum principle, it is only used as a means to get there. It will, however, appear as soon as the sharper estimates from
[
5
]
Proposition 4.3 do no-longer hold.

Our approach will be useful in extensions to the maximum principle to an infinite dimensional setting and the common noise setting.
Firstly, the infinite dimensional setting. The proof of the sharper estimates in
5
relies on matrix valued SDEs and needs apriori estimates on solutions to these. In infinite dimensions these become operator valued SDEs, which cannot be solved in a general setting. Some progress has been made by
11
, but in a simpler setting and relying on solving BSPDEs as evolution equations on the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators. A difficulty of our approach in the infinite dimensional setting would be the existence of a solution to the third adjoint equation as it becomes an operator valued backward SDE. But this is the same problem that is already present for the second adjoint and ways to solve these equations have been developed
[
21
,
15
]
.

The other clear application is the common noise setting
[
9
]
. Here, it is already known that these sharper estimates of
5
Proposition 4.3 do not hold, so the corresponding second-order terms have to appear, e.g. compare the different Ito formulas
[
8
]
Proposition 5.102 and
[
9
]
Theorem 4.17. The intuition is that in general a conditional expectation does not have the same smoothing properties as a non-conditional expectation. Our method can still be applied in this setting.

The structure of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the mathematical setting and the needed assumptions. In Section 3, we introduce the variational processes and give estimates on their order. In Section 4, we introduce the adjoint equations, in particular the third adjoint equation and its needed setting and prove its well-posedness. Afterwards, in Section 5, the needed dualizations of these processes are discussed and then used in Section 6 to expand the cost functional in a suitable way to infer the desired maximum principle.

We say that

4.1
The Lifting and the Second-Level Second-Order Adjoint Equation

So far, we have worked on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
and used another probability space
\tilde{\Omega}
to generate independent copies that where immediately integrated out, thereby introducing distribution dependence. Here, it was sufficient to work with an abstract probability space that was not further specified. Now, we want to linearize the interaction of two independent copies of
Y^{\varepsilon}
. To be able to do this, we need two independent copies that are not immediately integrated out. Thus, we really need to expand our probability space. We make this explicit to avoid all possible confusion.

We denote
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathcal{F}},(\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\hat{\mathbb{P}}):=(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
a copy of the original space and consider

(\bar{\Omega},\bar{\mathbb{P}})=(\Omega,\mathbb{P})\otimes(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathbb{P}}),

equipped with the completed versions
\bar{\mathcal{F}},(\bar{\mathcal{F}_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
of the product
\sigma
-algebras
(\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0})\otimes(\hat{\mathcal{F}},(\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0})
. We will now lift all our previously defined processes to the product space by defining (with an abuse of notation) for all
\bar{\omega}\in\bar{\Omega}

W_{t}(\bar{\omega}):=W_{t}(\bar{\omega}_{1}),\quad X_{t}(\bar{\omega}):=X_{t}(\bar{\omega}_{1})\quad\dots

and so forth. The resulting expectation
\bar{\mathbb{E}}
then has the same effect on the lifting as the original has on the original processes, i.e.
\bar{\mathbb{E}}[X_{t}]=\mathbb{E}[X_{t}]
. This lifting allows us to easily obtain independent copies of our process
\hat{W}(\bar{\omega}):=W(\bar{\omega}_{2})
,
\hat{X}(\bar{\omega}):=X(\bar{\omega}_{2}),\dots

For this reason we will denote
\mathbb{E}[\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{t},\hat{X_{t}})]]:=\bar{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{t},\hat{X}_{t})]
and use the fact that
\bar{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{t},\hat{X}_{t})]=\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{t},\tilde{X}_{t})]]
, where
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}
denotes the previous independent lifting.

We can now define the second-level second-order adjoint process
(\mathfrak{P},\mathfrak{Q}^{(1)},\mathfrak{Q}^{(2)})
, taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d\times d}
, as a solution to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\bigg(A^{*}_{x}(\theta_{t})\mathfrak{P}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[A^{*}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}\right]+\mathfrak{P}_{t}A_{x}(\hat{\theta}_{t})+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\tilde{\hat{\mathfrak{P}}}_{t}A_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})\right]

(26)

\displaystyle+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[H_{\mu\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t},\hat{X}_{t})\right]+2H_{x\mu}(\hat{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})+P^{*}_{t}A_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})+P_{t}A_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})

\displaystyle+B^{*}_{x}(\theta_{t})P_{t}B_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{*}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})\tilde{P}_{t}B_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})]+B^{*}_{x}(\theta_{t})P^{*}_{t}B_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big(Q^{k,*}_{t}B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})+Q^{k}_{t}B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})+B^{k,*}_{x}(\theta_{t})\mathfrak{Q}^{(1),k}_{t}

\displaystyle+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k,*}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})\tilde{\mathfrak{Q}}^{(1),k}_{t}]+\mathfrak{Q}^{(2),k}_{t}B^{k}_{x}(\hat{\theta}_{t})+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\hat{\mathfrak{Q}}}^{(2),k}_{t}B^{k}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})]\Big)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{Q}^{(1)}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}+\mathfrak{Q}^{(2)}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{W}_{t},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}_{T}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[g_{\mu\mu}(\tilde{X}_{T},\mu_{T})(X_{T},\hat{X}_{T})]+2g_{x\mu}(\hat{X}_{T},\mu_{T})(X_{T}),

where we define

i.e. one factor of the product space is integrated out in the equation, and similarly for

and

[

). Note that the measurability of the coefficients is clear from Fubini.

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}[e^{\kappa T}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{T}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\Big[e^{\kappa s}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{s}\|^{2}-\int_{s}^{T}e^{\kappa t}2\langle\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t},A^{*}_{x}(\theta_{t})\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}+\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}A_{x}(\hat{\theta}_{t})+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[A^{*}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})\tilde{\Delta}^{\Phi}_{t}

\displaystyle+\tilde{\hat{\Delta}}^{\Phi}_{t}A_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})]+\sum_{k=1}^{d}B^{k,*}_{x}(\theta_{t})\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(1)},k}_{t}+\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(2)},k}_{t}B^{k}_{x}(\hat{\theta}_{t})+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k,*}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})\tilde{\Delta}^{\Psi^{(1),k}}_{t}

\displaystyle+\tilde{\hat{\Delta}}^{\Psi^{(2)},k}_{t}B^{k}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})]\rangle+e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(1)}}_{t}\|^{2}+e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(2)}}_{t}\|^{2}+\kappa e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big],

so rearranging gives

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\Big[e^{\kappa s}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{s}\|^{2}+\int_{s}^{T}e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(1)}}_{t}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{s}^{T}e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(2)}}_{t}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\kappa\int_{s}^{T}e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{s}^{T}2e^{\kappa t}\langle\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t},A^{*}_{x}(\theta_{t})\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}+\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}A_{x}(\hat{\theta}_{t})+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[A^{*}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})\tilde{\Delta}^{\Phi}_{t}+\tilde{\hat{\Delta}}^{\Phi}_{t}A_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})]

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{d}B^{k,*}_{x}(\theta_{t})\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(1)},k}_{t}+\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(2)},k}_{t}B^{k}_{x}(\hat{\theta}_{t})+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k,*}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})\tilde{\Delta}^{\Psi^{(1)},k}_{t}

\displaystyle+\tilde{\hat{\Delta}}^{\Psi^{(2)},k}_{t}B^{k}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})]\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Big].

Now, using the boundedness of the coefficients from Assumption
2.6
, taking the supremum over
s\in[0,T]
and using the Young-inequality multiple times, we arrive at

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bar{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}\|^{2}\right]+\bar{\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(1)}}_{t}\|^{2}+e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(2)}}_{t}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\kappa\int_{0}^{T}e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[c_{1}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}\|\bigg(\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}\|+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\|\tilde{\Delta}^{\Phi}_{t}\|+\|\tilde{\hat{\Delta}}^{\Phi}_{t}\|]

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(1)},k}_{t}\|+\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(2)},k}_{t}\|+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\|\tilde{\Delta}^{\Psi^{(1),k}}_{t}\|+\|\tilde{\hat{\Delta}}^{\Psi^{(2)},k}_{t}\|]\right)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}c_{2}e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{3}e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{P}}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{3}e^{\kappa t}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\|\tilde{\Delta}^{\Phi}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{\hat{\Delta}}^{\Phi}_{t}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(1)}}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}e^{\kappa t}\|\Delta^{\mathfrak{Q}^{(2)}}_{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}e^{\kappa t}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\|\tilde{\Delta}^{\Psi^{(1)}}_{t}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{\hat{\Delta}}^{\Psi^{(2)}}_{t}\|^{2}]\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigg].

By virtue of the Young inequality
c_{2}
is chosen accordingly and depends on
c_{1}
and therefore on the constants coming from the coefficients. Finally, taking
\kappa=c_{2}+\frac{1}{3}
gives the result with contraction coefficient
\frac{1}{3}
.
∎

5
The Duality Relations

Having in mind the desired linearization of terms in the Taylor-expansion of the cost functional, we will need the following duality relations, which arise directly from Ito’s formula. First, we have the dualization of the first-order adjoint with the first-order variational process

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle p_{T},Y^{\varepsilon}_{T}\rangle\right]=

\displaystyle-\bar{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\langle f_{x}(\theta_{t}),Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\rangle+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle f_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t}),Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\rangle\right]

(29)

\displaystyle-\langle p_{t},\delta A(t)\rangle-\langle q_{t},\delta B(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Big],

and the dualization of the first-order adjoint with the second-order variational process

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle p_{T},Z^{\varepsilon}_{T}\rangle\right]

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\langle-f_{x}(\theta_{t}),Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}\rangle-\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\langle f_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t}),Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}\rangle]

\displaystyle+\big\langle p_{t},\frac{1}{2}A_{xx}(\theta_{t})[Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[A_{y\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\tilde{\tilde{\mathbb{E}}}\big[A_{\mu\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t},\tilde{\tilde{X}}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},\tilde{\tilde{Y}}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\big]\Big]+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[A_{x\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\right]\big\rangle

\displaystyle+\big\langle q_{t},\frac{1}{2}B_{xx}(\theta_{t})[Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[B_{y\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\tilde{\tilde{\mathbb{E}}}\big[B_{\mu\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t},\tilde{\tilde{X}}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},\tilde{\tilde{Y}}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\big]\Big]+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[B_{x\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\right]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg]+o(\varepsilon).

In preparation for the dualization of the first-level second-order adjoint, we look at the tensor product of the first-order variational process
Y^{\varepsilon}
with itself. We use the tensor notation but note that, for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds
x\otimes y:=xy^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, since we work in finite dimensions. We get

\displaystyle d\left(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\big((A_{x}(\theta_{t})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[A_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\delta A(t))\otimes Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle+Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes(A_{x}(\theta_{t})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[A_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\delta A(t))

\displaystyle+(B_{x}(\theta_{t})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\delta B(t))(B_{x}(\theta_{t})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\delta B(t))^{*}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big(\big(B^{k}_{x}(\theta_{t})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\delta B^{k}(t)\big)\otimes Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle+Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes\big(B^{k}_{x}(\theta_{t})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\delta B^{k}(t)\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t},

and therefore, for the dualization of the first-level second-order adjoint process with this tensor product

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle P_{T},Y^{\varepsilon}_{T}\otimes Y^{\varepsilon}_{T}\right\rangle\right]

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}-\langle H_{xx}(\theta_{t})+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[H_{y\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})],Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\rangle+\langle P_{t},\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[A_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\otimes Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle+Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[A_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\delta B(t)\delta B^{*}(t)+B_{x}(\theta_{t})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]^{*}

\displaystyle+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]^{*}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}](B_{x}(\theta_{t})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle Q^{k}_{t},\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\otimes Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg]+o(\varepsilon).

Note that for all the above dualizations it would have sufficed to look at the processes on the original probability space and use the standard expectation. For the dualization of the second-level second-order adjoint process the construction of the probability space
\bar{\Omega}
is important, as the dualization needs to be with the tensor product of the first-order variational process
Y^{\varepsilon}
with its independent copy
\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}
(introduced above), resulting in

\displaystyle d\left(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big((A_{x}(\theta_{t})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[A_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\delta A(t))\otimes\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes(A_{x}(\hat{\theta}_{t})\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[A_{\mu}(\hat{\theta}_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]

\displaystyle+\delta\hat{A}(t))\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\left(B^{k}_{x}(\theta_{t})Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\delta B^{k}(t)\right)\otimes\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes\left(B^{k}_{x}(\hat{\theta}_{t})\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k}_{\mu}(\hat{\theta}_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\delta\hat{B}^{k}(t)\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\hat{W}^{k}_{t},

and thus, using Fubini,

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle\mathfrak{P}_{T},Y^{\varepsilon}_{T}\otimes\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{T}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}-\big\langle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[H_{\mu\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t},\hat{X}_{t})]+2H_{x\mu}(\hat{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})

(32)

\displaystyle+P^{*}_{t}A_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})+P_{t}A_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})+B^{*}_{x}(\theta_{t})P_{t}B_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})

\displaystyle+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{*}_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(X_{t})\tilde{P}_{t}B_{\mu}(\tilde{\theta}_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})]+B^{*}_{x}(\theta_{t})P^{*}_{t}B_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{d}Q^{k,*}_{t}B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t})+Q^{k}_{t}B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\hat{X}_{t}),Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg]+o(\varepsilon),

Remark 5.1
.

Note the appearance of the
\langle P_{t},\delta B(t)\delta B^{*}(t)\rangle
-term in the dualization (
31
). This term arises from the quadratic variation of
Y^{\varepsilon}
and neither is of high order, nor can be eliminated by the dualization. Therefore it, and in particular the first-level second-order adjoint process, will appear in the maximum principle. On the other hand, the stochastic integrals appearing in the equations for
Y^{\varepsilon}
and
\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}
are by construction independent, which implies that their co-variation is
0
. Thus, no such term appears in the dualization (
32
), letting the second-level second-order adjoint process not appear in the final maximum principle. This is reminiscent of the sharper estimates for the terms containing two independent copies of the variational process from
5
.

6
The Expansion of the Cost Functional and the Maximum Principle

We come to the most important result towards the maximum principle. This result was already shown in
[
5
]
, merely our proof is new, as it utilizes the second-level second-order adjoint and its dualization.

Theorem 6.1
.

Under Assumption
2.6
, if
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}

(33)

Proof.

Using Taylor’s formula (cf. Lemma
2.3
), we get the expansion of the cost functional,

\displaystyle J(\alpha^{\varepsilon})-J(\alpha)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}f_{x}(\theta_{t})[\Delta X_{t}]+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})[\Delta\tilde{X}_{t}\right]+\delta f(t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}g_{xx}(X_{T},\mu_{T})[\Delta X_{T},\Delta X_{T}]+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{y\mu}(X_{T},\mu_{T})(\tilde{X}_{T})[\Delta\tilde{X}_{T},\Delta\tilde{X}_{T}]\right]

Now, by the linearity, we can everywhere replace

o(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle J(\alpha^{\varepsilon})-J(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}f_{x}(\theta_{t})[Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+Z^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})(\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\tilde{Z}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\right]+\delta f(t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}(\theta_{t})[Y^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}]+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[f_{x\mu}(\theta_{t})[\tilde{X}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\right]

\displaystyle+g_{x}(X_{T},\mu_{T})[Y^{\varepsilon}_{T}+Z^{\varepsilon}_{T}]+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{\mu}(X_{T},\mu_{T})(\tilde{X}_{T})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{T}+\tilde{Z}^{\varepsilon}_{T}]\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}g_{xx}(X_{T},\mu_{T})[Y^{\varepsilon}_{T},Y^{\varepsilon}_{T}]+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{y\mu}(X_{T},\mu_{T})(\tilde{X}_{T})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{T},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{T}]\right]

\displaystyle+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{x\mu}(X_{T},\mu_{T})(\tilde{X}_{T})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{T},Y^{\varepsilon}_{T}]\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\tilde{\mathbb{E}}}\left[\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{\mu\mu}(X_{T},\mu_{T})(\tilde{X}_{T},\tilde{\tilde{X}}_{T})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{T},\tilde{\tilde{Y}}^{\varepsilon}_{T}]\right]\right]\Bigg]+o(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}g_{xx}(X_{T},\mu_{T})[Y^{\varepsilon}_{T},Y^{\varepsilon}_{T}]+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{y\mu}(X_{T},\mu_{T})(\tilde{X}_{T})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{T},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{T}]\right]

and then replace

\displaystyle J(\alpha^{\varepsilon})-J(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\delta H(t)+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[H_{x\mu}(\theta_{t})[\tilde{X}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\right]+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\tilde{\mathbb{E}}}\left[\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[H_{\mu\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t},\tilde{\tilde{X}}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},\tilde{\tilde{Y}}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\right]\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\big\langle Q^{k}_{t},\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\otimes Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}+Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}\otimes\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[B^{k}_{\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{x\mu}(X_{T},\mu_{T})(\tilde{X}_{T})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{T},Y^{\varepsilon}_{T}]\right]+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\tilde{\mathbb{E}}}\left[\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{\mu\mu}(X_{T},\mu_{T})(\tilde{X}_{T},\tilde{\tilde{X}}_{T})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{T},\tilde{\tilde{Y}}^{\varepsilon}_{T}]\right]\right]\Bigg]

(36)

\displaystyle+o(\varepsilon).

Finally, we can now use the second-level second-order adjoint process and its dualization (

\displaystyle J(\alpha^{\varepsilon})-J(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\delta H(t)+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[H_{x\mu}(\theta_{t})[\tilde{X}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\right]+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\tilde{\mathbb{E}}}\left[\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[H_{\mu\mu}(\theta_{t})(\tilde{X}_{t},\tilde{\tilde{X}}_{t})[\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t},\tilde{\tilde{Y}}^{\varepsilon}_{t}]\right]\right]

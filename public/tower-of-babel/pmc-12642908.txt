Novel insights into predicting the presence of micropapillary and solid components in stage IA lung adenocarcinoma using machine learning models of modifiable risk factors
Authors: Xiuzhi Li, Zirui Huang, Ningbo Fan, Han Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLung adenocarcinoma (LUAC) patients with micropapillary (MP) and/or solid (S) generally demonstrate a poorer survival prognosis. In the diagnosis and treatment of stage IA LUAC, precisely establishing personalized treatment strategies for patients is crucial for both clinical practice and scientific investigation. Our study aims to develop a novel prediction model based on machine learning (ML) to predict the probability of MP/S patterns in stage IA LUAC patients.MethodsOur retrospective analysis was conducted on 1,933 patients diagnosed with stage IA LUAC via postoperative pathological staging, focusing on evaluating MP/S pattern presence. MP/S-positive patients were matched with negative patients at a 1:2 ratio. Univariate logistic regression and Lasso regression were used to select variables with independent prognostic significance. The performance of the traditional logistic regression model was compared with nine ML models based on the identification and calibration.ResultsNodule type, spiculation, Carcinoembryonic antigen level, maximum solid component diameter, median CT value, and CT value range were identified as independent influencing factors for predicting MP/S patterns. The K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) model performed best among all ten models. The internal validation indicated an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.790, a Brier score of 0.167, and a Hosmer-Lemeshow (HL) test P value of 0.817, while external validation yielded an AUC of 0.790, a Brier score of 0.167, and a HL test P value of 0.120. Shapley additive explanation analysis revealed “nodule type” could alter the predicted probability of MP/S component presence by 13.6%, establishing it as a significant factor.ConclusionAn interpretable KNN model was successfully developed to predict the presence of MP/S components in stage IA LUAC patients, demonstrating superior predictive performance. Accurate evaluation of relevant tumor characteristics possesses substantial clinical significance, as it enables guidance on the optimization of surgical approaches to enhance patient prognosis.

Introduction

Lung cancer demonstrates the greatest fatality rate among all malignancies and ranks second in incidence rate. As per the 2020 global cancer statistics, lung cancer constitutes roughly 11.4% of worldwide cancer incidence and approximately 18.0% of global cancer deaths, which is presently the malignant neoplasm with the highest incidence and fatality rates among males [1]. Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAC) is the predominant histological subtype of Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) [2]. Primary LUACs typically present as heterogeneous mixtures of various histological subtypes, each with distinct clinicopathological and genomic features. In 2011, the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer, the American Thoracic Society, and the European Respiratory Society (IASLC/ATS/ERS) defined five different subtypes of invasive non-mucinous LUAC based on prognosis: lepidic, acinar, papillary, micropapillary (MP), and solid (S) [2]. In comparison with patients dominated by other pathological subtypes, those with a predominant MP/S pattern have a relatively inferior prognosis [3,4]. Furthermore, even non-predominant MP/S patterns were additionally confirmed to be one of the factors associated with poor prognosis [5–8].

The increasing emphasis on physical examinations and the advancement of clinical diagnostic measures have contributed to a gradual rise in the early detection rate of NSCLC. Surgical resection, encompassing lobectomy and segmentectomy, remains the definitive standard for intervening in LUAC in its initial stages [9]. Nowadays, for tiny peripheral tumours with preoperative lymph node metastasis identification being unfeasible, sublobar resection has been widely applied [10,11]. Consequently, the selection of surgical approaches for early-stage LUAC has emerged as the primary focus of present scholarly and clinical deliberations. Two extensive clinical trials, JCOG0802 and CALGB140503, have demonstrated that sublobar resection may produce perioperative outcomes non-inferior to those of lobectomy for non-small cell lung tumours measuring ≤ 2 cm in diameter [12,13]. Nonetheless, these two notable studies failed to elucidate the pronounced heterogeneity observed across different pathological subgroups of LUAC. Sublobar resection generally does not increase the risk of postoperative recurrence or metastasis for lepidic-predominant adenocarcinoma, a histological subtype associated with a relatively better prognosis [14]. Conversely, patients with MP/S predominant LUAC demonstrate a higher incidence of lymph node metastases and tumour recurrence. Consequently, lobectomy and systematic lymphadenectomy are frequently selected as the surgical approach for these patients [15–18]. Postoperative tumour recurrence is the primary cause of treatment failure, underscoring the importance of identifying the MP/S components in early-stage LUAC for determining surgical strategies and evaluating patient prognosis. Currently, there is a lack of accurate and effective guiding models for clinical treatment decisions regarding patients with MP/S patterns in stage IA LUAC, resulting in uncertainty in the selection of treatment regimens. Consequently, in the absence of adequate data to establish the standard surgical protocol for early-stage LUAC, accurately identifying adenocarcinoma with MP/S patterns and performing lobectomy alongside conventional lymph node dissection is imperative.

Our medical study is dedicated to constructing and validating a clinical decision-making model for the MP/S patterns in patients with stage IA LUAC. The core objective is to empower clinicians to accurately assess the prognosis and provide a scientific basis for the formulation of individualized treatment plans. Through the application of our model, we expect to achieve multiple clinical benefits, including improved quality of life, reduced tumour recurrence rates, and enhanced overall survival rates for patients. Thus, identifying high-risk pathological stage IA patients prone to postoperative recurrence and implementing closer follow-up or adjuvant treatment is vital for improving patient prognosis. Additionally, the SHapley Additive Explanation (SHAP) framework will be employed to meticulously evaluate the actual contributions of various explanatory variables to the prediction outcomes of machine learning (ML) models. Given the growing prevalence of precision oncology and ML applications in cancer research, our findings on MP/S pattern prediction may contribute novel insights to clinical decision-making.

Methods

Patients

The participants of our research are from the Cancer Center of Sun Yat-sen University. A retrospective analysis was performed on all patients who underwent surgical intervention for primary lung cancer at our hospital between January 2022 and August 2023. Patients were sequentially divided into an internal cohort (January 2022-March 2023) and an external validation cohort (April 2023-August 2023) in chronological order. All the cases incorporated in the study adhere to the established inclusion and exclusion criteria. The IASLC/ATS/ERS histological classification stipulates that the proportion of histological types, including adherent, acinar, papillary, S, and MP, is documented in 5% increments, with a specific pathological subtype deemed present in the tumor when it constitutes 5% or more of the cancer. In our analysis, 14.43% of pathologically stage IA primary LUAC patients exhibited MP/S patterns. Prior large-scale cohort studies focusing on early-stage lung adenocarcinoma have consistently shown that the proportion of patients harbouring MP/S components varies between 36.4% and 37.9% [15,19]. To more accurately replicate real-world situations, we constructed the model employing propensity score matching to incorporate patients with various histological types at a 1:2 ratio. The internal cohort was randomized into a training cohort and a validation cohort at a 7:3 ratio. The internal cohort will be used for epidemiological profiling, analysis of MP/S risk factors, and construction of the prediction model. The external cohort is employed to assess the generalization capability and stability of the model.

Enrollment criteria: (1) Histopathological diagnosis of LUAC after surgical treatment; (2) According to the IASLC, 9th edition of TNM Staging method, confirming the postoperative pathological stage as stage IA; (3) Complete critical clinical data. Exclusion Criteria: (1) Absence of chest CT examination at our facility within 3 months before surgical intervention; (2) Administration of chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy, or targeted therapy before surgery; (3) Postoperative pathological diagnosis of carcinoma in situ or microinvasive adenocarcinoma; (4) Presence of multiple invasive adenocarcinomas in the ipsilateral lung lobe; (5) Previous lung surgery history; (6) History of malignancy.

Evaluation of CT morphological features and pathological data

Two radiologists specializing in lung cancer assessed various computed tomography (CT) morphological characteristics utilizing Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS), including nodule type, nodule size, maximum diameter of the solid component, marginal features (clear and indistinct), morphological shapes (regular and irregular), lobulation, vascular convergence, pleural indentation, spiculation, air bronchogram, and additional indicators. The scanning protocol was meticulously configured with the following parameters: a slice thickness of 0.625 mm, a reconstruction increment of 0.625 mm, a pitch factor of 0.984, a tube voltage of 120 kV, and a tube current of 250 mA. To ensure objectivity, both reviewers were blinded to the pathological diagnosis. In cases of divergent interpretations between the two physicians, the ultimate conclusion is reassessed and established by an independent radiologist specializing in lung cancer. All measurements were conducted on the lung window setting, and the median CT value is the average of the maximum and minimum CT values within the target region. For continuous CT features involved in the study, the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to assess interobserver agreement. For categorical CT features involved in the study, the Cohen’s Kappa coefficient (or weighted Kappa for ordinal categorical features) was employed to evaluate interobserver agreement. The representative imaging features of LUAC are depicted in Figure S1.

The pathological specimens resected after lung cancer surgery were sectioned with haematoxylin–eosin staining and evaluated by two pathologists specializing in lung cancer subspecialties in accordance with the definition of the main components of pathological subtypes, as shown in Figure S2. In cases of inconsistent evaluation results, the discrepancy was resolved through discussions between the two physicians.

Statistical methods

Continuous data were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD), with an independent samples t-test or Mann–Whitney U-test employed for intergroup comparisons. Categorical or discrete data were represented as counts and percentages, with group comparisons performed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test.

The variables from univariate logistic regression with p < 0.05 were incorporated into the Lasso regression analysis. Lasso regression constitutes a well-established statistical framework for variable selection and the modelling of high-dimensional datasets. By imposing an L1 regularization penalty on regression coefficients, this approach elicits the shrinkage of non-informative coefficients to zero, thereby enabling rigorous variable screening and the attainment of model parsimony. The independent predictors of MP/S components in clinical and CT imaging characteristics (with the nearest distance of mean error at one standard deviation) were identified. These core variables were utilized for subsequent model development.

This study employed ten ML algorithms to construct the MP/S component prediction model, including Logistic regression model, Support Vector Machine (SVM) model, Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM) model, Neural Network model, Random Forest model, eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) model, K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN) model, Adaptive Boosting (Adaboost) model, LightGBM model, and Categorical Boosting (CatBoost) model. Subsequently, the caret package (version 7.1.0) was employed to perform ten-fold cross-validation for model training, aiming to mitigate overfitting. Hyperparameter tuning was conducted using a strategy combining Grid Search and cross-validation. To visually assess the discriminative capacity of our model, we plotted the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. In contrast, the area under the curve (AUC) was employed to quantify its ability to distinguish between positive and negative samples. Moreover, the trade-off between the sensitivity and specificity of the model was demonstrated under different classification thresholds. Calibration curves are plotted to visualize the consistency between the predicted probability of the model and the probability of actual occurrence and to verify the reliability of the model’s prediction results at different risk levels. DCA curves were plotted to assess the net benefit of the model at different threshold probabilities, weighing the potential clinical benefit of the model’s predictions against the potential risk of false positives. Based on the comprehensive evaluation results of the aforementioned multiple indicators, the model with the optimal performance was selected from the ten candidate models as the final prediction model. SHAP values, which quantify the contribution of individual features to the logarithmic probability of model predictions, were visualized utilizing the R package shapviz (version 0.9.6).

Two-tailed statistical analysis was performed to derive the P-value, with a significance threshold of p < 0.05. Statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.3.3.

Results

Enrollment situation

In total, 4826 patients who underwent surgery were diagnosed with primary LUAC. After screening for inclusion and exclusion criteria, 2,893 patients were excluded, and 1,933 patients (279 patients were MP/S positive and 1,654 patients were MP/S negative) were initially enrolled in the study cohort. Following a 1:2 propensity score matching (Figure S3), 279 patients with MP/S-positive and 558 patients with MP/S-negative stage IA LUAC were ultimately included in the study for subsequent analysis (Figure 1).

The overall flowchart of the study.

Analysis of demographic, clinical, and imaging factors

The demographic and clinicopathological profiles of the final study population are detailed in Tables S1 and S2. The interobserver agreement of all CT morphological features evaluated by two radiologists reached a reliable level. For all continuous CT features, the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) values ranged from 0.85 to 0.96. For categorical CT features, the Cohen’s Kappa (or weighted Kappa) values ranged from 0.77 to 0.91. These results provide a stable data foundation for the subsequent construction of predictive models. Larger CT diameters were found in the Internal and external verification before matching in the MP/S group (p < 0.001 and p < 0.001). After matching, CT diameters showed no statistical significance between the MP/S group and other groups, with nodule locations being more comparable. Before matching, the MP/S group in the External verification cohort and internal cohort showed a greater age (p < 0.001 and p < 0.001). Following matching, the ages in the Internal and External verification cohorts were comparable (p = 0.240 and p = 0.133). Before matching, the proportion of female patients and the probability of having heart disease in the MP/S group of the internal cohort were lower (p = 0.005 and p = 0.032), while no significant differences in these two aspects were observed in the external validation cohort (p = 0.059 and p = 1.000). After matching, the gender ratio (p = 0.632 and p = 0.295) and the probability of having heart disease (p = 0.088 and p = 1.000) were comparable between the internal and external validation cohorts. The probability of perineural invasion (PNI) and body mass index (BMI) were similar in the MP/S groups of the internal cohort, both before and after matching (p = 0.143 and p = 0.333). Still, the probability of PNI and BMI were higher in the MP/S groups of the external validation cohort (p = 0.003 and p = 0.036). Before and after matching, the forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) in the MP/S subgroup of the internal cohort decreased (p = 0.003 and p = 0.005). In the external validation cohort, the FEV1 in the MP/S group was comparable to that in the other groups (p = 0.308 and p = 0.668). Before matching, no statistical significance was demonstrated in diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO) between the MP/S group and the other groups. Following matching, the DLCO in the MP/S groups decreased in the internal cohort (p = 0.019), but no significant difference was detected in the external validation cohort (p = 0.323). Before matching, carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) levels in the MP/S group were elevated in both the internal and external validation cohorts (p < 0.001 and p = 0.006). After matching, the CEA level in the MP/S group remained elevated in the internal cohort (p = 0.001), whereas no statistically significant difference was observed in the external validation cohort (p = 0.080). Before and following matching, the MP/S group and other groups exhibited substantial differences in the percentage of smoking patients and postoperative pathological staging (p < 0.05). Before and after matching, the proportions of PNI, intra-airway spread (IAS), and lymphovascular invasion (LVI) were greater in the MP/S group (p < 0.05).

Screening and testing of typical features of MP/S LUAC

Univariate logistic regression analysis was performed on the internal training cohort. The results indicated that nodule type, smoking, unclear boundary, lobulation, spiculation, pleura indentation, pathology, FEV1, DLCO, CEA level, maximum S component diameter, median CT value, and CT value range were correlated with the presence of MP/S components in patients (p < 0.05) and constituted potential risk factors affecting the presence of MP/S components. Multivariate logistic regression analysis demonstrated that IAS, LVI, median CT value, CT value range, maximum S component diameter, and spiculation were statistically significant (p < 0.05) (Table 1). Given the current absence of effective measures in clinical practice to preoperatively assess PNI, IAS, and LVI, these three factors were excluded from our analysis. Among all the potential predictors, median CT value has the largest area under the ROC curve (Figure 2(A,B)). Factors with p < 0.05 were selected for correlation analysis, which suggests a relatively robust association between maximum solid component diameter and median CT value (R = 0.84, p < 0.001) (Figure 2(E)). The collinearity analysis results demonstrate that the VIF values for all factors are below 5, indicating minimal linear correlation and a low risk of multicollinearity among the independent variables, thereby preventing severe instability or uncertainty within the model (Figure 2(F) and Table S3). Consequently, the goodness-of-fit and predictive efficacy of our model are rendered more robust and trustworthy. Lasso regression analysis identified 13 potential risk factors, which were subsequently narrowed down to 6 potential predictors (Figure 2(C,D)). Construction and internal and external validation of a ML prediction model for MP/S LUAC

Nodule type, spiculation, CEA level, maximum S component diameter, median CT value, and CT value range were integrated into the development of the prediction model. Ten-fold cross-validation was utilized to compare the prediction performance of the Logistic regression, SVM, GBM, Neural Network model, Random Forest model, XGBoost, KNN, Adaboost, LightGBM model, and CatBoost model. The Random Forest model, LightGBM model, and GBM model outperformed the KNN model in the training cohort. However, their performance decreased significantly on the test cohort, suggesting that these three models are highly likely to be overfitted. The AUC values and 95% confidence interval (CI) of the remaining seven models in the training cohort are all depicted in Figure 3(A,D). The calibration curve demonstrates conformity between predicted and observed outcomes (Figure 3(B,E)). DCA was performed to quantitatively assess the net benefits of predictions with various ML algorithms using the study dataset (Figure 3(C,F)). Considering multiple evaluation metrics, including accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, and F1 score, the KNN model (kmax = 40, distance = 2, kernel = “optimal”) performed well on both the training cohort and the validation cohort (Table S5 and S6).

C and F: Decision curve analysis for prediction models in the internal training cohort. The grey line parallel to the x-axis represents the assumption that none of the patients exhibit the solid pattern, indicating the net benefit of not implementing any interventions for any patients, regardless of the prediction results, which is 0. The standard net benefit was computed by subtracting the proportion of false-positive patients from that of true-positive patients, with weighting applied based on the relative harm of treatment omission versus the adverse consequences of unnecessary treatment.

The DeLong test was conducted on the ROC-AUC of the seven remaining models within the validation cohort, with results presented in Table S7. Comparisons of the KNN model with the other six models revealed statistically significant discrepancies (p < 0.05). Furthermore, the KNN model demonstrated consistent performance across various evaluation metrics in both the training and validation cohorts. Given these considerations, we ultimately selected the KNN algorithm for the final model development.

The KNN model yielded AUC values of 0.871 (95% CI: 0.838–0.904) for the internal training cohort and 0.787 (95% CI: 0.717–0.857) for the internal validation cohort, indicating robust discriminatory capability (Figure 4(A,D)). Hosmer–Lemeshow (HL) test P values greater than 0.05 (0.053 and 0.817) revealed no statistically significant difference between observed and predicted probabilities in the model, suggesting acceptable model calibration. Calibration curves closely approximated ideal curves, reflecting excellent predictive accuracy (Figure 4(B,E)). DCA indicated that the constructed model offers a significant clinical net benefit (Figure 4(C,F)). External validation using data from various temporal intervals revealed an AUC value of 0.790, with a P-value of 0.120 from the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, further substantiating the predictive efficacy and external generalizability of the KNN-based clinical prediction model (Figure S4A-C).

Explanation and visualization of the KNN model

Figure 5(A) depicts the absolute average SHAP values for several features derived from the KNN model. SHAP values quantify the contribution of each feature to the predictive outcomes of a model. The feature ‘Nodule type’ demonstrates the highest absolute average SHAP value of 13.6%, underscoring its significant influence on the predictive outcomes of the KNN model. ‘CTvalue.median’, ‘CTvalue.range’, ‘Solid.max’, ‘Spiculation’, and ‘CEA’ alter the predicted probability by approximately 8.6%, 6.5%, 3.5%, 3.0% and 2.8%, respectively. Figure 5(B) depicts the distribution of SHAP values for individual features in the KNN model, demonstrating how each patient’s data in the modeling dataset contributes to the variability of the outcome variable. Colored dots signify distinct patient individuals, and the dot-cloud plot for each characteristic illustrates the distribution of SHAP values for that feature across various samples. The SHAP plot uses color to characterize the magnitude of original feature values, where yellow represents positive values (i.e. exerting a beneficial effect) and purple denotes negative values (i.e. exerting a detrimental effect). The SHAP value distribution for the ‘CT value range’ feature indicates that specific samples with higher feature values have a critical adverse effect on the prediction. In contrast, samples with lower feature values demonstrate a positive influence. Figure 5(C) depicts the local feature contribution of a particular sample within the KNN model. ‘f(x)’ represents the predicted value of the model for this sample, while “E[f(x)]” signifies the expected value. The f(x) value of the depicted sample closely resembles the E[f(x)] value, suggesting that the predicted value for this sample aligns with the overall average, and its feature combination has an average-level influence on the prediction of our model, without leading to a deviation from the average prediction.

Discussion

For stage IA LUAC patients, surgical resection remains the most effective treatment approach. However, postoperative recurrence remains an urgent problem to be addressed [20,21]. Recent investigations have demonstrated that high-risk pathological characteristics, including MP/S components in stage IA LUAC, can substantially affect patient prognosis through mechanisms such as airspace dissemination, LVI, and visceral pleural invasion [16,22–24]. Consequently, accurately identifying these high-risk pathological features at an early stage is of substantial significance for optimizing clinical decisions.

ML models belong to the advanced algorithms in the field of artificial intelligence. Through training and learning from extensive datasets, these models can automatically discern complex patterns and intrinsic correlations within the data, thereby enabling comprehensive predictions and decisions that are applied to the precise diagnosis of diseases and evaluation of prognosis [25–27]. The traditional Logistic regression model relies on a series of stringent assumptions. It requires a linear relationship between the independent variables and the dependent variable, and the error terms must follow a normal distribution and exhibit mutual independence [28]. However, in real-world applications, these assumptions are frequently challenging to satisfy. Moreover, the Logistic regression model is unable to cope with high-dimensional data, and it is prone to multiple collinearity problems, resulting in unstable model results [29]. In contrast, ML algorithms possess distinct advantages. They are capable of more accurately extracting key features from complex datasets, effectively managing interactions among multiple variables, and appropriately addressing challenges such as collinearity in high-dimensional data [30].

Notably, even when applied to large-scale samples, ML models display distinct limitations, including the absence of interpretability and intuitive explanations, which significantly hinders their broader adoption. To address this challenge, we employ the SHAP framework, initially proposed by Lundberg et al. [31], which possesses robust capabilities and enables the precise evaluation of the actual contributions of each explanatory variable within any ML model [32].

This is among the first studies to compare nine ML algorithms for MP/S pattern prediction in stage IA adenocarcinoma. Our prediction model includes six variables: Nodule type, spiculation, CEA level, maximum solid component diameter, median CT value, and CT value range. Univariate and Lasso regression analyses identified these variables as independent prognostic markers. The KNN model was designated as the optimal model owing to its prominent performance stability between the internal training cohort and validation cohort, characterized by minimal fluctuations in multiple metrics (accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, F1-score) and robust generalization capability, thereby demonstrating superior performance in the comprehensive trade-off of multi-metric evaluation. The KNN model has the capability to address both linear and nonlinear relationships inherent in data, allowing it suitable for constructing predictive models using complex clinical datasets. Dong et al. [33] employed seven ML models, including KNN, to develop a predictive model for cardiovascular events in patients with cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome. Fayemiwo et al. [34] constructed a predictive model for dementia incidence via the KNN algorithm, which exhibits dual characteristics of accuracy and sensitivity, thereby overcoming the prevalent limitation of suboptimal sensitivity frequently encountered in most ML-based predictive models. Notably, our study differs from previous imaging and radiomics based MP/S prediction studies. Several studies relied solely on high-dimensional radiomic features extracted from CT images, which require specialized post-processing tools and may lack clinical interpretability, limiting their adoption in primary care settings [35,36]. In contrast, our model integrates clinically actionable variables that are readily available in standard clinical workflows, eliminating the need for additional radiomic analysis and enhancing practical applicability. In addition, our model differs from traditional nomograms, which only present the scores of each variable in predicting the MP/S pattern of adenocarcinoma. Instead, the SHAP method is employed to unveil the action mechanism of each variable more intuitively. The SHAP diagram effectively demonstrates the enhancing or suppressive effects of individual variables on the presence of MP/S pattern adenocarcinoma. A higher CT value generally indicates that the nodule has a greater likelihood of harbouring MP/S components, reflecting its malignant potential and poorer prognosis, which is consistent with the findings of prior research [37,38]. Sun et al. [39] reported that the mean CT value in the MP/S-positive group was significantly higher than that in the MP/S-negative group and could independently predict the presence of MP/S components in stage IA. Tamura et al. [40] further validated the utility of CT values in the auxiliary diagnosis of MP/S subtypes. The density of the lesion is closely correlated with the CT value in the image. Lesions with higher CT values generally exhibit greater density, suggesting that MP/S components on CT images are more prone to manifest as S nodules, a finding corroborated by multiple studies [41,42]. In our analysis, the mean CT value in CT images of the MP/S-positive group was higher, accompanied by a narrower range of CT values, confirming a significant correlation between the presence of MP/S components and CT value. CEA is a glycoprotein with a molecular weight of 180 kilodaltons. During the fetal development stage, CEA is actively expressed, but its expression level decreases rapidly as birth approaches. As a result, the content of CEA in the serum of healthy adults is generally lower than 2.5 nanograms per millilitre. However, in patients with malignant tumours, the expression level of CEA demonstrates an abnormally increased phenomenon [43,44]. Higher serum CEA concentrations were observed in patients with the MP/S subtype, which has been fully validated in research involving a cohort of 3100 cases of LUAC [45]. Multiple studies indicate that the imaging characteristics of the nodule margin are significantly associated with the probability of malignant tumours, with the spiculation sign being particularly crucial. To standardize the CT imaging evaluation of lung cancer screening, the American College of Radiology (ACR) has introduced the Lung Imaging Reporting and Data System (Lung-RADS), which conducts classification and evaluation according to the size of the nodule, morphological characteristics (such as spherical shape, lobulation, vascular/wall attachment) and the degree of calcification [46]. Lung-RADS identifies the spiculation sign as a critical imaging characteristic, positing that it markedly elevates the suspicion of malignant tumours and enhances predictive accuracy. McWilliams et al. [47] undertook an extensive, large-scale prospective investigation, enrolling 1,871 participants in the internal cohort and 1,090 participants in the external cohort. A prediction model for lung cancer probability was developed, further validating that the spiculation sign is a crucial indicator for predicting malignant tumours. Kim et al. [48] demonstrated the association between spiculation in CT scans and higher recurrence rates as well as worse survival outcomes. Accurate discrimination of the pathological components of lung cancer nodules and prediction of their malignancy degree represent critical challenges demanding urgent resolution in the medical field. Before implementing additional imaging examinations and invasive procedures, accurate risk assessment if of significant importance for the diagnosis and management of lung cancer. Our developed nodule risk prediction model is poised to effectuate significant improvements in clinical practice and public health, enhancing the efficiency and quality of diagnosis and treatment.

In a retrospective study involving 734 patients with LUAC, patients with tumours harbouring a MP component of 5% or higher who underwent limited resection had a higher recurrence risk than similar patients treated with lobectomy. The presence of a MP pattern in stage I LUAC was highly heterogeneous and associated with a higher incidence of locoregional recurrence, whereas cases with a surgical margin of 1 cm or more exhibited a reduced recurrence probability. These findings suggest that limited resection may not be suitable for patients with LUAC containing any MP component [7,49]. A study by Caso et al. [50] conducted in patients with lung nodules smaller than 2 cm demonstrated that when the proportion of MP components exceeded 5%, patients who underwent lobectomy had better survival outcomes. Patients with subcentimetre LUAC containing a MP component had a poor prognosis, and those who received wedge resection had a higher recurrence risk compared with those who underwent anatomical pulmonary resection (including segmentectomy and lobectomy), which is consistent with the findings of Yao et al. [51]. Further research indicated that in the presence of MP/S subtype components in the tumour, lobectomy exerted a protective effect against recurrence, whereas patients who opted for segmentectomy and wedge resection had worse disease-free survival. Among MP/S patients, those who underwent complete lymph node dissection had higher overall survival and recurrence-free survival. In contrast, patients who received incomplete lymph node dissection had more frequent distant metastases, as well as an earlier onset and higher peak of recurrence risk [52]. Sun et al. [53] analysed the clinicopathological data of 1160 patients with invasive LUAC (tumour diameter ≤ 2 cm) who underwent surgery across 7 medical institutions and reached similar conclusions. In patients with LUAC of other pathological subtypes, such as acinar-predominant subtype, no difference in survival prognosis was observed between lobectomy and sublobar resection [54]. Given such unfavourable biological behaviours, limited resection approaches may fail to achieve adequate tumour clearance and lymph node dissection, thereby increasing the risk of residual tumour cells and subsequent disease recurrence. In contrast, lobectomy, as a standard radical surgical approach, can ensure complete resection of the primary tumour lesion and systematic lymph node dissection, which aligns with the treatment requirement for LUAC with MP/S components to maximize local control and improve long-term prognosis. Therefore, for LUAC patients confirmed or preoperatively predicted to have MP/S components, lobectomy should be prioritized as the preferred surgical strategy to optimize clinical outcomes. Collectively, a large body of existing evidence indicates that preoperative prediction of the MP/S components in tumours plays a crucial guiding role in the selection of surgical approaches for patients with LUAC.

Nevertheless, several limitations remain in this study. Firstly, our analysis is a retrospective cohort study that exclusively encompasses cases of stage IA LUAC that underwent surgical intervention. Secondly, our research is conducted in a single institution, potentially introducing selection bias. The accuracy of the prediction model requires the inclusion of multi-centre and large-sample data for further improvement. Finally, the postoperative follow-up duration of the collected data is comparatively brief, and the analysis does not report the prognoses of patients in the two groups. In our subsequent research, the emphasis will be placed on establishing a patient cohort with stage IA LUAC harbouring MP/S components, which will involve tracking and comparing the survival outcomes of these patients following lobectomy and sublobar resection.

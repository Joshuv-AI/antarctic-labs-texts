A significance of micro-motile organism's flow of nanofluid for heat transportation with thermal radiations
Authors: Bilal Ahmad, Muhammad Idrees, Syed Asif Ali Shah, Jihad Asad, Ali Elrashidi, Abdel-Haleem Abdel-Aty
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The ability of nanofluids to improve heat transmission in thermal systems is well established. This work investigates the three-dimensional theoretical behavior of Darcy-Forchheimer nanofluids in tilted magnetohydrodynamics. In this study, the Soret effect, micro-motile organisms, thermophoresis, and heat radiation are also considered. The finite element method is used to solve the challenging mathematical model. Using graphs and tables, patterns may be seen in the velocity profiles, temperature distributions, concentrations of micro- and nano-motile organisms, and nanoparticle concentrations. The governing equations for mass, concentration, momentum, and motile microbes are derived using partial derivatives. These equations are then transformed into dimensionless ordinary differential equations utilizing appropriate similarity transformations. Permeability and viscosity have an impact on the properties of fluid flow. According to the results, improved thermophoresis and Brownian motion parameters raise the temperature profiles. In keeping with other studies, more inclined magnetohydrodynamics also accelerate heat transfer rates. The mass transfer rate at Fr=0.8 is 32.67% and 33.99% for Fr=1.4. Thus, mass transfer increased 1.32%. The novelties of the current work are: the Buongiorno nanofluid model is utilized for 3D geometry, inclined MHD and motile microorganisms are considered for rotating nanofluid flow, effects of slip and non-slip boundary conditions are compared, and the finite element technique is used to solve the developed problem.

Introduction

In the modern period, ecological damage and climate change have unavoidably become the source of energy scarcity. Consequently, scientists and engineers are looking for new energy breakthroughs to ensure sustainable development. The increase in the capacity for heat transmission is one of the primary concerns of the researchers. Owing to the superior thermal conductivity properties exhibited by nanoparticles, fluid suspension is of great interest for various technological applications. Nanofluids are remarkable due to their unique physical features when examined in conjunction with nanoparticles and a base fluid. It is constantly employed as a coolant in various heat-transmitting equipment, including heat exchangers, electronic cooling systems, and appealing pharmaceuticals. Choi and Eastman [1] presented the concept of a nanofluid, and after that, there was a significant shift. Malik et al. [2] explored the hybrid system in nanofluid flow, which has received considerable attention in the literature. Khan et al. [3] examined the boundary value problems for sheet stretching. Lassoued et al. [4] scrutinized the magnetic influence on the nanofluids flow. The flow of nanofluid through the microchannels under the effects of single-phase and multi-phase flow is elaborated by [5]. Shah et al. [6] provided an in-depth analysis of nanofluid flow over a rotating disk, carefully examining how different nanoparticle shapes influence flow dynamics and thermal performance. Other researchers, such as [7], [8], investigated the heat transfer process with the aid of nanofluid flow. Acharya and his collaborators investigated unsteady mixed convective radiative nanofluid flow, emphasizing nanoparticle diameter and nanolayer factors [9], [10].

The flow of fluid across the sheet under the stretching and shrinking phenomena has received a lot of interest over the past few decades because of its many uses in engineering and industrial operations. Several studies have been conducted to study this flow arrangement's fundamental elements and properties. For instance, Mahantesh and Krishnamurthy [11] employed a numerical technique to analyze heat transportation under magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) and an external heat source over the stretching or shrinking sheet flow. Their findings indicated that both magnetic fields and radiation have a substantial impact on the heat exchange rate. In a separate study, Prakash et al. [12] delved into the heat-exchange mechanisms of unsteady flow, focusing on variable viscosity and thermal conductivity as they interact across a stretched surface. The researchers discovered that the velocity and temperature profiles significantly deviated from the classical solutions when considering the variable fluid properties. Fathizadeh et al. [13] conducted experimental research to investigate how nanoparticles influence flow and heat transfer characteristics over a shrinkable surface. Additionally, Rafique et al. [14] analyzed the effects of entropy generation on nanofluid flow, focusing on Joule heating and magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) influences along a stretching sheet. Mahbalshehwar et al. [15] explored the role of exothermic radiation on non-Newtonian fluid flow across a stretching surface. Patel et al. [16] studied water-based hybrid nanofluid flow, examining the impacts of slip boundary conditions and thermal radiation on flow over a stretching sheet. Ahmed et al. [17] analyzed hyperbolic tangent nanofluid flow as it interacts with the stretching effects of the sheet, while Mishra et al. [18] investigated nonlinear radiation and cross-diffusion impacts on micropolar nanoliquid flow over a stretched sheet under an exponential heat source.

In recent decades, there has been a notable increase in the level of attention devoted to the study of c MHD fluid dynamics. This rise in interest may be attributed to the several possible applications of MHD flows in fields such as engineering, astronomy, and renewable energy. Zhang et al. [19] examined the impact of magneto-hydrodynamics on the performance of an MHD pump, emphasizing the crucial role of optimizing magnetic field parameters for enhanced efficiency. Li et al. [20] conducted a numerical analysis of nanofluid flow influenced by magnetic effects in a curved channel, revealing the substantial influence of the Hartmann number and Reynolds number on flow characteristics and pressure drop. Chen et al. [21] delved into the behavior of MHD boundary layer flow over a stretching surface, emphasizing the significance of suction or injection parameters in controlling both flow and heat transfer. Alsaedi [22] employed numerical methods to investigate the modeling of nanofluid flow with MHD effects, contributing to the evolving understanding of this complex phenomenon. Arif et al. [23] utilized a modified finite element method to study the impact of MHD on heat as well as mass transfer in non-Newtonian nanofluids. Asma et al. [24] explored the impacts of chemical reaction and activation energy on a rotating disk using the Darcy-Forchheimer flow. Recent contributions by Pattnaik et al. [25] explored the influence of velocity slip on the MHD flow of a micropolar fluid across a stretched surface, while Mishra et al. [26] investigated the impact of heat transfer on MHD flow of a micropolar fluid through porous media, considering the presence of a homogeneous heat source and radiation. Baag et al. [27] examined MHD boundary layer flow across an exponentially stretched sheet in the presence of a porous material and a uniform heat source. Additionally, Makinde and Mishra investigated the characteristics of chemically reacting MHD mixed convection flow in a porous material [28]. The collective efforts of numerous scholars have enriched our understanding of the influence of magnetic effects on fluid flow across various geometries [29], [30], [31]. This body of work collectively contributes to the ongoing advancement of the field of MHD fluid dynamics.

The diverse range of applications of three-dimensional Darcey-Forchhimer fluid flow in a rotating system spanning several disciplines, including medical sciences, metallurgy, crystal creation, peristaltic flow, fusion reactors, and ship propulsion has garnered significant attention from different researchers. Siddique et al. [32] investigated the Darcey-Forchhimer two-dimensional hybrid nanofluid flow across a stretched sheet. Alsabery et al. [33] delved into the stability analysis of the Darcey-Forchhimer-Buongiorno model, considering a stretching sheet and a rotating fluid. Salehi et al. [34] provided insights into the Darcey-Forchimer thin film flow and heat transfer study of SWCNTs across an unsteady stretching sheet. Aziz et al. [35] conducted a study on Darcey-Forchhimer's hybrid nanofluid flow through a stretched sheet. The exploration of the Darcey-Fischer-Himmer flow model has seen significant contributions from various researchers, including the works of Nadeem et al. [36] and Siddique et al. [37]. These collective efforts have played a crucial role in advancing our understanding of the intricate dynamics and applications of Darcey-Forchhimer fluid flow in rotating systems.

Bio-convection is an inherent phenomenon characterized by the motion of microorganisms organized either as individual single-celled entities or as colonies. These systems are dependent on the directed movement of different microorganisms, where gyrotactic bacteria demonstrate upstream locomotion against gravity in stagnant water, resulting in higher concentrations of microorganisms at the top compared to the bottom. Bio-convection finds applications in diverse fields, such as biofuels, fertilizer production, and industrial processes. Yahya et al. [38] delved into the significance of motile microorganisms, Cattaneo-Christov heat flux, radiation heat flux, and electromagnetic fields in the context of Williamson Sutterby nanofluid with convective BCs across stretchable geometry. Asjad et al. [39] explored MHD nanofluid flow across an exponentially stretched surface, considering the presence of bio-convection, heat source dissipation, and activation energy. Awan et al. [40] examined Williamson nanofluid flow with a motile microbe, exponential heat source/sink, and non-linear thermal radiation effects over a linearly stretching sheet. Raju and Sandeep [41] scrutinized two different solutions for unstable heat transfer in bio-convection flow toward a spinning cone/plate through a rotating fluid. Mallikarjuna et al. [42] evaluated nanofluid flow in a narrow vertical cylinder with gyrotactic microorganisms, adding valuable insights to the understanding of mixed convection in such systems. These studies collectively contribute to unraveling the complexities of bio-convection and its interactions with various physical factors across different geometries and scenarios.

Activation energy significantly influences the rate at which nanoparticles respond to temperature gradients when modeling fluid systems under non-ideal conditions. Unlike the classical no-slip boundary condition, slip conditions permit relative motion between the fluid and boundary, which can substantially modify the characteristics of heat transfer and flow. By letting the surface move at a constant speed, slip conditions can better reflect real-world situations like in microfluidic and biomedical devices where boundary effects are common. Examining the combined effects of activation energy and slip conditions in nanofluid systems provides deeper insights into the optimization of heat and mass transfer in engineering applications. As far as we know, there have been no studies that investigate bio-convective 3-D Darcy-Forchheimer Rotating nanofluid flow with thermal linear radiation, Brownian motion, and thermophoresis motions with multiple slip boundaries. Numerical simulation

It is impossible to solve the partial differential equations represented by equations (5)-(10) analytically due to their high non-linearity. Variational finite element methodologies are integrated alongside BCs to tackle the modified assemblage of nonlinear differential equations (13–18). The numerical computation methodology is applicable in the resolution of an extensive range of engineering and practical predicaments. These encompass fluid flow, heat transfer, structural engineering, biomaterials, chemical processes, rigid body dynamics, and a multitude of other subjects [51]. A wide variety of non-linear problems can be tackled using this methodology, and it is currently a widely accepted technique for resolving integral equations in modern engineering analysis. Reddy [52] provided a comprehensive exposition on the variational finite element method and a succinct overview of the fundamental procedural steps of the finite element method. In its most fundamental form, the methodology involves determining the resolution by employing a continuous piece-wise function and obtaining the parameters of said function in a methodical manner aimed at reducing error [53]. The FEM is capable of addressing boundary value issues in a reliable, precise, and timely way. The Finite Element Method plays a critical role in this study, providing a robust numerical technique for solving the complex, nonlinear partial differential equations governing the flow and thermal behavior of the nanofluid system. Given the high nonlinearity of the Darcy-Forchheimer model and the additional complexities introduced by magnetic field effects, bio-convection, slip conditions, and activation energy, traditional analytical methods are insufficient for obtaining accurate solutions. FEM allows for a systematic discretization of the problem domain, breaking it down into smaller, manageable elements. This approach is particularly well-suited to handle the boundary value problems and multiple slip conditions applied in this study. By using FEM, we can precisely model the variations in velocity, temperature, and concentration profiles across the flow domain, capturing the localized effects of parameters such as magnetic field strength, rotation, and thermophoretic diffusion. Additionally, FEM provides flexibility in defining boundary conditions and enables efficient computation of the effects of complex interactions within the nanofluid. The method's accuracy and adaptability make it an ideal choice for this study, ensuring that the results are both reliable and relevant for practical applications in advanced thermal systems, including microfluidic and biomedical devices.

Concluding remarks and future directions

In this study, we examined the heat and mass transfer dynamics of a three-dimensional Darcy-Forchheimer nanofluid flow in a rotating system with inclined magnetohydrodynamic effects, and bio-convection, incorporating the influences of activation energy, Brownian motion, thermophoresis, and slip boundary conditions. By employing the finite element method to solve the complex nonlinear differential equations, we obtained insights into the fluid's velocity, temperature, and concentration profiles across various boundary and flow conditions. The findings contribute significantly to the understanding of nanofluid behavior in advanced thermal systems. Our results indicate that both activation energy and slip conditions play crucial roles in the thermal and flow characteristics of the nanofluid. The inclusion of activation energy enhances our understanding of the chemical reaction dynamics within the nanofluid, revealing that higher activation energy leads to more controlled reaction rates, which in turn affects heat distribution across the flow field. Meanwhile, the application of slip boundary conditions demonstrates that allowing a finite velocity at the boundary results in distinct variations in velocity and temperature profiles, especially in microfluidic and biomedical contexts where boundary effects are prominent. These findings highlight the importance of accounting for slip effects when modeling nanofluids at small scales, as it allows for a more realistic approximation of fluid behavior at the interface.

The parametric analysis further reveals that increased magnetic field strength (MHD effects), rotation (via the rotational parameter β), and porosity parameter (λ) significantly influence the primary and secondary velocity components of the fluid. The enhanced magnetic field and rotation reduce the primary velocity while increasing the secondary velocity in both slip and non-slip cases, providing insights into the flow control mechanisms for nanofluid applications. Additionally, the temperature profile is elevated by higher values of thermophoresis (Nt), Brownian motion (Nb), and thermal radiation (Rd) parameters, suggesting that these factors can be tuned to enhance thermal performance in nanofluid-based heat transfer systems. The study also sheds light on the concentration profiles, which are impacted by parameters such as Prandtl number (Pr), Schmidt number (Sc), and activation energy. Higher activation energy decreases the concentration gradient, leading to reduced nanoparticle diffusion near the boundary. Moreover, the analysis of bio-convection dynamics shows that increased values of bio-convection and Peclet numbers affect the distribution of motile microorganisms within the fluid, impacting applications in bioengineering where microbial motion is significant. This work provides novel insights by combining activation energy and slip conditions in the modeling of MHD nanofluid flow in a rotating, bio-convective environment. The results are highly relevant for optimizing thermal management in heat exchangers, electronic cooling, biomedical devices, and microfluidic systems. Future studies could extend this framework to explore time-dependent behaviors or include additional complex boundary conditions to simulate even more realistic environments. The FEM approach used here proves to be a powerful tool for addressing the nonlinearities inherent in such systems, and future work could leverage this method to investigate multi-phase flows or integrate machine learning models to predict fluid behavior under varying conditions.

The integration of activation energy and slip boundary effects in a nanofluid system with bio-convective MHD flow and rotational dynamics provides a comprehensive model that enhances our understanding of thermal transport phenomena in nanofluids. This study underscores the potential of such models in developing advanced thermal systems and lays the groundwork for future research to explore even more complex configurations and parameter interactions in nanofluid-based technologies.

A local converse theorem for quasi-split even special orthogonal groups
Alexander Hazeltine

Abstract
. We give a direct proof of the local converse theorem for quasi-split non-split SO 2 ​ l \mathrm{SO}_{2l} over a local non-Archimedean field of characteristic p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 , applying the theory of Howe vectors and partial Bessel functions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11F70, 22E50; Secondary 11F85

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a connected reductive group,
F
be a non-Archimedean local field of characteristic zero, and let
G=\mathrm{G}(F)
. Converse theorems seek to uniquely identify representations of
G
from its invariants. One such invariant is known as the local gamma factor. These factors are arithmetic in nature and play very important roles in the Langlands program.
More precisely, let
\pi
be an irreducible generic representation of
G
. The family of local twisted gamma factors is denoted by
\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)
, where
\tau
is an irreducible generic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
,
\psi
an additive character of
F,
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
. These can be defined using Rankin–Selberg convolution (e.g.,
[
12
,
20
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
41
,
42
]
) or the Langlands–Shahidi method (
[
39
,
40
]
). For
\mathrm{GL}_{l},
Jacquet conjectured the following.

Conjecture 1.1

(Local Converse Theorem for
\mathrm{GL}_{l}
)
.

Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible generic representations
of
\mathrm{GL}_{l}(F)
. Suppose that they have the same central character.
If

\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi),

for all irreducible
generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with
1\leq n\leq\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor
, then
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
.

Conjecture
1.1
was proved by Chai (
[
6
]
), and by Jacquet and Liu (
[
19
]
), independently, using different analytic methods. It is known as the local converse theorem for
\mathrm{GL}_{l}.
For general quasi-split reductive groups over non-Archimedean local fields, Jiang gave a general conjecture for local converse theorems for generic representations in
[
23
, Conjecture 3.7]
and many cases have since been established. A non-exhaustive list of such results includes
\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}
(
[
24
,
26
]
),
\mathrm{Sp}_{2l}
(
[
26
,
44
]
),
\mathrm{U}(l,l)
(
[
36
,
44
]
),
\mathrm{U}_{2l+1}
(
[
45
]
), split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
(
[
14
,
17
]
), and
\widetilde{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2l}
(
[
13
]
).

We note that, by applying Langlands functoriality and Conjecture
1.1
, several of the above cases would be implied by the works of Arthur and Mok (
[
2
,
35
]
). However, it is desirable to have direct proofs which reflect the intrinsic properties of the groups as is carried out in
[
17
,
26
,
44
,
45
]
. We remark more on this point later.

The main goal of this article is to establish the local converse theorem for the quasi-split non-split group
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
directly. We note that the case for split and quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
was also proved independently in
[
14
]
using the theta correspondence (similarly to
[
13
]
but using more refined arguments). However, the approaches of this paper (and
[
17
]
) contrast significantly with that of
[
14
]
. In particular, we utilize the properties of partial Bessel functions to compute the zeta integrals directly.

As in the split case, one difficulty in the local converse theorem for quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
is the existence of the outer automorphism. We show that the twisted local
\gamma
-factors determine generic representations uniquely up to conjugation by the outer automorphism.
More precisely, in this paper, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(The Local Converse Theorem for quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
, supercuspidal case)
.

Let
F
be a non-Archimedean field of characteristic
p\neq 2
and
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible
\psi
-generic supercuspidal representations of quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F)
with the same central character
\omega
. If

\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi),

for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with
n\leq l,

then
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
or
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime c},

where
c
is the outer automorphism.

We note that the above theorem is equivalent to an analogous local converse theorem using the normalized twisted local
\gamma
-factor defined by Kaplan in
[
29
]
(see the discussion at the end of §
3
). By using the multiplicative properties for the normalized twisted local
\gamma
-factors (
[
29
]
), Theorem
1.2
implies the following theorem by similar arguments as in
[
24
, §3.2]
. We omit the proof.

Theorem 1.3

(The Local Converse Theorem for quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
, generic case)
.

Let
F
be a non-Archimedean field of characteristic
0
and
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible
\psi
-generic representations of quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F)
with the same central character
\omega
. If

\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi),

for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with
n\leq l,

then
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
or
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime c},

where
c
is the outer automorphism.

We remark that in the case that
F
has characteristic
p\neq 2,
Jo showed the analogue of Theorem
1.2
implies Theorem
1.3
for symplectic and odd special orthogonal groups (
[
26
]
) and a similar argument works in the split case of even special orthogonal groups (
[
17
]
). However, it seems that not all of the preliminary work required for such an argument has been carried out in the case of quasi-split special orthogonal groups yet. Hence, we require
F
to have characteristic
0
in Theorem
1.3
.

Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
suggest that local twisted gamma factors may not be able to distinguish the irreducible generic supercuspidal representations
\pi
and
\pi^{c}
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F).
This is in fact true (see Corollaries
6.6
,
6.8
and
6.12
). This is a unique phenomenon for both split and quasi-split non-split even special orthogonal groups among all the classical groups. This is consistent with the work of Arthur on the local Langlands correspondence and local Langlands functoriality (
[
2
]
), and the work of Jiang and Soudry on local descent for
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
(
[
25
]
).

Now, we briefly introduce the ideas used to prove Theorem
1.2
.
As in many other proven cases of classical groups (
[
17
,
26
,
44
,
45
]
), we utilize the theory of Howe vectors and partial Bessel functions of
\pi.
These are particular Whittaker functions in the Whittaker model of
\pi
. Let
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
be the set of upper triangular unipotent elements in
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and fix a generic character
\psi
on
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
Denote the Whittaker model of
\pi
by
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi).
For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we define certain compact subgroups
H_{m}
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and characters
\psi_{m}
of
H_{m}
such that
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}=\cup_{m\geq 1}(H_{m}\cap U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}})
and
\psi_{m}(u)=\psi(u)
for any
u\in H_{m}\cap U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
(see §
4.1
). A partial Bessel function for
\pi
is a function
W_{m}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
such that
W_{m}(I_{2l})=1
and

W_{m}(ugh)=\psi(u)\psi_{m}(h)W_{m}(g),

for any
u\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
,
h\in H_{m}
, and
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

Let
\mathcal{M}(\pi)
be the set of matrix coefficients of
\pi
.
Let
C^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega,\psi)
be the space of smooth compactly supported functions
W
satisfying
W(zg)=\omega(z)W(g)
and
W(ug)=\psi(u)W(g)
for any
z\in Z,u\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}},
and
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F).
Since
\pi
is generic, we have a nonzero map

f\in\mathcal{M}(\pi)\mapsto W^{f}\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega,\psi),

given by

W^{f}(g)=\int_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}\psi^{-1}(u)f(ug)du.

By choosing

f\in\mathcal{M}(\pi)
such that
W^{f}(I_{2l})=1
and letting
m
be a large enough positive integer, we can associate a Howe vector to
W^{f}
, denoted by

B_{m}(g;f)
,
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F)
, which is a partial Bessel function (see §
4.1
).

Fix matrix coefficients
f\in\mathcal{M}(\pi)
and
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\pi^{\prime})
such that
W^{f}(I_{2l})=W^{f^{\prime}}(I_{2l})=1.
The goal is to study the partial Bessel functions
B_{m}(g,f)
and
B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})
under the assumption of the equality of local twisted gamma factors. First, we study the support of the partial Bessel functions and partition it based on the Bruhat cells corresponding to Weyl elements in the sets
\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
for
n=1,\dots,l-1
(see Corollary
4.6
). The work of Cogdell, Shahidi, and Tsai (
[
9
]
) then gives that for
1\leq i\leq l-1
, there exist functions
f_{i}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega)
with support lying in the Bruhat cells corresponding to
\mathrm{B}_{i}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}),
such that

B_{m}(g,f)-B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})=\sum_{n=1}^{l-1}B_{m}(g,f_{n}),

for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
(see Corollary
4.11
).
In Theorem
6.1
, we show that for
1\leq k\leq l-2
, equality of the local twisted gamma factors up to
\mathrm{GL}_{k}
implies that

B_{m}(g,f)-B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})=\sum_{n=k+1}^{l-1}B_{m}(g,f_{i}).

Hence, equality of the local twisted gamma factors up to
\mathrm{GL}_{l-2}
implies that

B_{m}(g,f)-B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})=B_{m}(g,f_{l-1}).
Up until this point, the results parallel those in
[
26
,
44
,
45
]
. Equality of the local twisted gamma factors up to
\mathrm{GL}_{l-1}
only gives that
B_{m}(g,f)-B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})=B_{m}(g,f_{l-1})=0
if
cgc=g
(see Theorem
6.7
). This is different from the cases in
[
26
,
44
,
45
]
, but is expected as it parallels the split case of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
in
[
17
]
. Hence, it remains to determine the set elements
g
such that
cgc\neq g.
In the split case of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l},
equality of the local twisted gamma factors up to
\mathrm{GL}_{l}
implied that
B_{m}(g,f)-B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})+B_{m}^{c}(g,f)-B_{m}^{c}(g,f^{\prime})=0
where
B_{m}^{c}(g,f)
and
B_{m}^{c}(g,f^{\prime})
is are partial Bessel functions of
\pi^{c}
and
\pi^{\prime c},
respectively (
[
17
, Theorem 7.9]
). We obtain an analogous result for quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
6.11
)
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible supercuspidal
\psi
-generic representations of quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F)
with the same central character. Fix matrix coefficients
f\in\mathcal{M}(\pi)
and
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\pi^{\prime})
such that
W^{f}(I_{2l})=W^{f^{\prime}}(I_{2l})=1.
If

\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi),

as functions of the complex variable
s
,
for all irreducible generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
with
1\leq n\leq l,

then we have that

B_{m}(g,f)-B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})+B_{m}^{c}(g,f)-B_{m}^{c}(g,f^{\prime})=0,

for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(F)
.

By the uniqueness of Whittaker models, it follows that
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
or
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime c}
and hence Theorem
1.2
is proved. The proof of Theorem
1.2
is outlined in more detail in §
4.5
and the full details are provided in §
6
.

An analogous version of Theorem
1.4
is proven in the case of split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
in
[
17
, Theorem 1.4]
. We discuss the differences in the proofs. In the split case, the theory of Howe vectors and partial Bessel functions was already developed in
[
3
]
. Unfortunately, the quasi-split case was not considered and so we introduce it in §
4
.
The fact that the maximal torus is not split also requires new ideas to overcome. For example, many arguments in the split case utilize the isomorphism between
F
and the simple root subgroups of
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
In the quasi-split non-split case, we must be more careful. Instead we utilize certain elements of
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
and carefully apply the properties of partial Bessel functions to obtain results that are similar to the split case. There is one particular difference which makes the proof of Theorem 1.4 simpler than the split case. Namely, all of the Weyl elements in the Bessel support for quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
are self-conjugate (under the outer conjugation). In the split case, there are Weyl elements which are not self-conjugate and this significantly increases the amount of detail needed to prove
[
17
, Theorem 1.4]
.

As a direct application of Theorem
1.3
, following the same argument as in
[
24
, Theorem 5.3]
, we also obtain the following weak rigidity theorem for irreducible generic cuspidal automorphic representations of quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(\mathbb{A})
, where
\mathbb{A}
is the ring of adeles for a number field (see also
[
17
, Theorem 1.5]
for the split case). The proof is omitted.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Pi=\otimes_{v}\Pi_{v}
and
\Pi^{\prime}=\otimes_{v}\Pi^{\prime}_{v}
be two irreducible generic cuspidal automorphic representations of the quasi-split non-split group
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(\mathbb{A})
. If
\Pi_{v}\cong\Pi^{\prime}_{v}
for almost all places
v
, then
\Pi_{v}\cong\Pi^{\prime}_{v}
or
\Pi_{v}\cong\Pi^{\prime c}_{v}
for every place
v.

Theorem
1.3
and hence Theorem
1.5
were obtained independently by Haan-Kim-Kwon (
[
14
]
), using the different method of theta correspondence. Also we remark that Theorem
1.3
can also be obtained by applying the theory of local Langlands functoriality and the work of Arthur (
[
2
]
).
We note that our method is independent of the work of Arthur (
[
2
]
). The advantage of our method is that it reflects the intrinsic properties of the partial Bessel functions of special even orthogonal groups.

As mentioned earlier, direct proofs of the local converse theorems for split and quasi-split non-split even special orthogonal groups are desirable. The arguments give more detailed information about the partial Bessel functions gained from equality of the twisted local
\gamma
-factors for specific twists. In particular, it is an open problem to determine a set of invariants which determine the representations of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
uniquely (not just up to outer conjugation). Recently, in the case of split
\mathrm{SO}_{4}
, Yan and Zhang used the theory of Howe vectors and the results of
[
14
,
17
]
along with work on a twisted exterior square local
\gamma
-factor to determine the representations uniquely (
[
43
, Theorem 1.1]
). However, twisted exterior square local
\gamma
-factors for
\mathrm{SO}_{6}
are not enough to distinguish
\pi
and
\pi^{c}
(see the discussion at the end of
[
43
, §1]
or
[
33
, Proposition 7.3]
).

We remark that there has been extensive parallel work on converse theorems for finite groups. Over finite fields, the converse theorem for generic cuspidal representations has an analogous form to the local case; however, the arguments tend to be simpler since one can work with Bessel functions as opposed to partial Bessel functions. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field of
q
elements, where
q
is some power of an odd prime. For
\mathrm{GL}_{l}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, Nien proved the converse theorem (
[
37
]
) by using normalized Bessel functions and the twisted gamma factors defined by Roditty (
[
38
]
). In
[
31
]
, Liu and Zhang defined the twisted gamma factors for
\mathrm{Sp}_{2l}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
\mathrm{U}_{2l}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and
\mathrm{U}_{2l+1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and proved the corresponding converse theorems. Liu and Zhang also established the converse theorem for
\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
(
[
32
]
). Liu and the author proved the converse theorem for split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in
[
16
]
. The case of quasi-split non-split
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
was recently proven by the author in
[
15
]
and served as a guide for the proof of the local converse theorem in this paper. We remark that in addition to the above differences, the local case of even special orthogonal groups (both split or quasi-split) have an additional technical difference when compared to their finite field counterparts or their analogues for other classical groups over local fields. Specifically, a significant amount of work is needed to guarantee the smoothness of a certain function (see the proof of Theorem
6.11
or
[
17
, Theorem 6.13]
).

Here is the structure of this paper. In §
2
, we introduce the groups and representations considered in this paper.
In §
3
, we recall the definitions of the zeta integrals and twisted
\gamma
-factors. In §
4
, we introduce the theory of Howe vectors and partial Bessel functions and outline the proof of Theorem
1.2
. In
§
5
, we construct a section which is used in calculating the zeta integrals. In §
6
, we study the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
twists,
1\leq n\leq l
, and show the relation between the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
twists and the partial Bessel functions (Theorems
6.1
,
6.7
,
6.11
). Then, we prove Theorem
1.4
and deduce Theorem
1.2
.

2.
The groups and representations

Let
n,l\in\mathbb{N}
and
F
be a non-Archimedean local field of characteristic
0
. We fix a nontrivial unramified additive character
\psi
of
F
and let
\mathfrak{o}
be the integers of
F
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
and uniformizer
\varpi.

Let
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
be the group of matrices with entries in
F
and non-zero determinant. Let
I_{n}
be the identity element and fix

J_{n}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&\cdots&0&1\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\end{matrix}\right).

For
\rho\in F^{\times}
, we define

J_{2l,\rho}=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-1},\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
-\rho&0\end{matrix}\right),I_{l-1})\cdot J_{2l}.

We set
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n+1}\,|\,\mathrm{det}(g)=1,{}^{t}gJ_{2n+1}g=J_{2n+1}\}
and
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(J_{2l,\rho})=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2l}\,|\,\mathrm{det}(g)=1,{}^{t}gJ_{2l,\rho}g=J_{2l,\rho}\}
. We often simply write this as
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.
The group
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
is split if
\rho\in F^{2}
and is quasi-split, but not split, otherwise (in this case
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
splits over the quadratic extension
F(\sqrt{\rho})
). Unless otherwise explicitly stated, hereinafter, we fix
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
to be the non-split group
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}(J_{2l,\rho})
for a fixed
\rho\notin F^{2}.

Let
U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
and
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
be the subgroups of upper triangular matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
, respectively. Fix
B_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}=T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
to be the standard Borel subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
where
T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
consists of all elements of the form

t=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{l-1},\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\rho\\
b&a\end{matrix}\right),t_{l-1}^{-1},\dots,t_{1}^{-1}),

where
t_{1},\dots,t_{l-1}\in F^{\times}
and
a,b\in F
with
a^{2}-b^{2}\rho=1.

T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
is a maximal torus in
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

Set

c=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-1},1,-1,I_{l-1}).

Note that
c\notin\mathrm{SO}_{2l};
however,
c\mathrm{SO}_{2l}c^{-1}=c\mathrm{SO}_{2l}c=\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
. Given a representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l},
we define a new representation
\pi^{c}
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
by
\pi^{c}(g)=\pi(cgc).

We now discuss the embeddings needed to define the zeta integrals later (see
[
29
,
30
]
for discussions on these embeddings). If
n<l
we embed
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
via

\left(\begin{matrix}A&B&C\\
D&E&K\\
L&P&Q\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-n-1},M^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}A&&B&C\\
&1&&\\
D&&E&K\\
L&&P&Q\end{matrix}\right)M,I_{l-n-1}),

where
A
and
Q
are
n\times n
matrices and

M=\mathrm{diag}(I_{n},\left(\begin{matrix}0&2\\
1&0\end{matrix}\right),I_{n}).

This embeds
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
into the standard Levi subgroup
\mathrm{GL}_{l-n-1}\times\mathrm{SO}_{2n+2}
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
.

Set
\gamma=\frac{\rho}{2}.
If
l=n
, we embed
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
into
\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}
via

\left(\begin{matrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto M^{-1}\left(\begin{matrix}A&&B\\
&1&\\
C&&D\end{matrix}\right)M,

where
A,B,C,
and
D
are
l\times l
matrices and

M=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-1},\left(\begin{matrix}0&1&0\\
\frac{1}{2}&0&\frac{1}{2\gamma}\\
\frac{1}{2}&0&\frac{-1}{2\gamma}\end{matrix}\right),I_{l-1}).

Note that the embedding takes the torus
T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
to a torus in
\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}
, but not the standard torus. Indeed, the embedding takes
t
(as above) to

\mathrm{diag}(s,\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}(1+a)&b&\frac{1}{2\gamma}(1-a)\\
\gamma b&a&-b\\
\frac{\gamma}{2}(1-a)&-\gamma b&\frac{1}{2}(1+a)\end{matrix}\right),s^{*})

where
s=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{l-1})
.

Next we discuss the representations. Recall that
U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
and
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
are the subgroups of upper triangular matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
respectively and that we fixed an additive nontrivial character
\psi
of
F
. We abuse notation and define a generic character, which we will also call
\psi
, on
U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
and
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
. For
u=(u_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
, we set
\psi(u)=\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}u_{i,i+1}\right).
For
u=(u_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{2l}\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}},
we set

\psi(u)=\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l-2}u_{i,i+1}+\frac{1}{2}u_{l-1,l+1}\right).

We say an irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
is
\psi
-generic if

\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}(\pi,\psi)\neq 0.

Similarly, we say an irreducible representation
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
is
\psi
-generic if

\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}}(\tau,\psi)\neq 0.

A nonzero intertwining operator lying in these spaces is called a Whittaker functional and it is well known that Whittaker functionals are unique up to scalars. Fix
\Gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}(\pi,\psi)
to be a nonzero Whittaker functional. For
v\in\pi
, let
W_{v}(g)=\Gamma(\pi(g)v)
for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and set
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)=\{W_{v}\,|\,v\in\pi\}.

\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
is called the
\psi
-Whittaker model of
\pi.
By Frobenius reciprocity,
\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}(\pi,\psi)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{SO_{2l}}(\pi,\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}(\psi)).
Thus,
\pi
can be realized as a subrepresentation of
\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}(\psi)
via the map
\pi\rightarrow\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
given by
v\mapsto W_{v}.
Moreover, by the uniqueness of Whittaker models, this subrepresentation occurs with multiplicity one inside
\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}(\psi)
. We also note that the analogous results hold for
\psi
-generic representations
\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}.

Let
Q_{n}=L_{n}V_{n}
be the standard Siegel parabolic subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
with Levi subgroup
L_{n}\cong\mathrm{GL}_{n}.
For
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
we let
l_{n}(a)=\mathrm{diag}(a,1,a^{*})\in L_{n}
where
a^{*}=J_{n}{}^{t}a^{-1}J_{n}.
Let
\tau
be an irreducible
\psi^{-1}
-generic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
,
s\in\mathbb{C}
, and set
I(\tau,s)=\mathrm{Ind}_{Q_{n}}^{\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}(\tau|\mathrm{det}|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}).
An element
\xi_{s}\in I(\tau,s)
is a function
\xi_{s}:\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}\rightarrow\tau
satisfying

\xi_{s}(l_{n}(a)ug)=|\mathrm{det}(a)|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\tau(a)(\xi_{s}(g)),\forall a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n},u\in V_{n},g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1},

and is right translation invariant by some compact open subgroup.
Fix a nonzero homomorphism
\Lambda_{\tau}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}}(\tau,\psi^{-1})
. For
\xi_{s}\in I(\tau,s)
, we let
f_{\xi_{s}}:\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the function given by

f_{\xi_{s}}(g,a)=\Lambda_{\tau}(\tau(a)\xi_{s}(g)).

Let
I(\tau,s,\psi^{-1})
be the space of functions generated by
f_{\xi_{s}},\xi_{s}\in I(\tau,s).
Note that for
f_{s}\in I(\tau,s,\psi^{-1}),
we have

f_{s}(g,ua)=\psi^{-1}(u)f_{s}(g,a),\forall g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1},u\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}},a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}.

We also let
\tau^{*}
be the contragredient representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
defined by
\tau^{*}(a)=\tau(a^{*}).

3.
Zeta integrals and the gamma factor

In this section, we recall the definition of the zeta integrals in
[
29
]
. In the case
n=l
, we already have the notation needed; however, we need to define another unipotent subgroup and character when
n<l.

Suppose that
n<l.
Let
P_{l-n-1}=M_{l-n-1}N_{l-n-1}
be the standard parabolic subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
with Levi subgroup,
M_{l-n-1}
, isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{l-n-1}\times\mathrm{SO}_{2n+2}.
We embed
U_{\mathrm{GL}_{l-n-1}}
inside
\mathrm{GL}_{l-n-1}
which is realized in
M_{l-n-1}.
Then we define the unipotent subgroup
N^{l-n}=U_{\mathrm{GL}_{l-n-1}}N_{l-n-1}.
That is,

N^{l-n}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}u_{1}&v_{1}&v_{2}\\
&I_{\mathrm{SO}_{2n+2}}&v_{1}^{\prime}\\
&&u_{1}^{*}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}\,|\,u_{1}\in U_{\mathrm{GL}_{l-n-1}}\right\}.

For
v=(v_{i,j})\in N^{l-n}
we define a character, which we also call
\psi,
of
N^{l-n}
by

\psi(v)=\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l-n-1}v_{i,i+1}+\frac{1}{4}v_{l-n-1,l}-\frac{1}{2}v_{l-n-1,l+1}\right).

Note that this character is trivial when
n=l-1.
Let
H=\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}N^{l-n}
where
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
is realized via the embedding into
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+2}
inside
M_{l-n-1}
and extend
\psi
trivially across
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
so that
\psi
is a character of
H
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible
\psi
-generic supercuspidal representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
,
\tau
be a
\psi^{-1}
-generic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n},

W\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi),
and
f_{s}\in I(\tau,s,\psi^{-1})
. We define the zeta integrals,
\Psi(W,f_{s}).
Note that
[
29
]
defines integrals for any
n
and
l
; however, we only need the case of
n\leq l
for the converse theorem and so we do not consider the case of
n>l
.

Now suppose that
n=l.
Then we define

\Psi(W,f_{s})=\int_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\backslash\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}W(g)f_{s}(w_{l,l}g,I_{l})dg,

where

w_{l,l}=\left(\begin{matrix}\gamma I_{l}&&\\
&1&\\
&&\gamma^{-1}I_{l}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l+1}.

For any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l},
the integral satisfies

(3.1)

\Psi(g\cdot W,g\cdot f_{s})=\Psi(W,f_{s}).

Next suppose that
n<l.
Then we define

\Psi(W,f_{s})=\int_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}\backslash\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}}\left(\int_{r\in R^{l,n}}W(rw^{l,n}g(w^{l,n})^{-1})dr\right)f_{s}(g,I_{l})dg,

where

w^{l,n}=\left(\begin{matrix}&I_{n}&&&\\
I_{l-n-1}&&&&\\
&&I_{2}&&\\
&&&&I_{l-n-1}\\
&&&I_{n}&\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l},

and

R^{l,n}=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&&&&\\
x&I_{l-n-1}&&&\\
&&I_{2}&&\\
&&&I_{l-n-1}&\\
&&&x^{\prime}&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}\right\}.

The integral satisfies the property

(3.2)

\Psi((gn)\cdot W,g\cdot f_{s})=\psi^{-1}(n)\Psi(W,f_{s}),

for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
and
n\in N^{l-n}.
Note that in the case
n<l
, our integral differs from
[
29
]
slightly. The difference is a right translation of the Whittaker functional by
(w^{l,n})^{-1}.
This is not a significant issue since right translation preserves
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi);
however, the translation simplifies the calculations of the integrals later. Moreover, this does not affect our definition of the
\gamma
-factor below.

We introduce the standard intertwining operator
M(\tau,s,\psi^{-1})
. Note that we do not use the normalized version of the intertwining operator. We define
M(\tau,s,\psi^{-1}):I(\tau,s,\psi^{-1})\rightarrow I(\tau^{*},1-s,\psi^{-1})
via

M(\tau,s,\psi^{-1})f_{s}(h,a)=\int_{V_{n}}f_{s}(w_{n}uh,d_{n}a^{*})du,

where
w_{n}=\left(\begin{matrix}&&I_{n}\\
&(-1)^{n}&\\
I_{n}&&\end{matrix}\right)
,
d_{n}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,-1,\dots,(-1)^{n})\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, and
V_{n}
is the unipotent radical of the parabolic subgroup
Q_{n}=L_{n}V_{n}
in
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
where
L_{n}\cong\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, and
a^{*}=J_{n}{}^{t}a^{-1}J_{n}.

Outside of a discrete subset of
s
, the set of bilinear forms satisfying equations (
3.1
) or (
3.2
) is at most
1
dimensional. This follows from the results of
[
1
]
and
[
34
]
(see
[
10
]
). When
n=l
, this was already known in
[
11
]
.

Therefore, we can define a proportionality factor,
\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)
, called the
\gamma
-factor, such that

\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)\Psi(W,f_{s})=\Psi(W,M(\tau,s,\psi^{-1})f_{s}).

We refer to these integrals and
\gamma
-factors as the twists by
\mathrm{GL}_{n}.

Note that our
\gamma
-factor differs from that of
[
29
]
slightly. Specifically, it differs by a factor depending only on
\tau
and
\psi
. If we let
\gamma^{\prime}(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)
be the gamma factor of
[
29
]
, then

\gamma(\tau,Sym^{2},\psi,2s-1)\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)=c(n,\tau,\psi,\gamma)\gamma^{\prime}(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi),

where for
n<l
we have
c(n,\tau,\psi,\gamma)=\omega_{\tau}(\gamma)^{2}|\gamma|^{2n(s-\frac{1}{2})}
and
c(l,\tau,\psi,\gamma)=1.
Here

\gamma(\tau,Sym^{2},\psi,2s-1)
is Shahidi’s local coefficient,
Sym^{2}
is the symmetric square representation, and
\omega_{\tau}
is the central character of
\tau.
Thus, if the local converse theorem holds for
\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)
, then it also holds for
\gamma^{\prime}(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi).
There is also the normalization of
\gamma^{\prime}(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi).
It is defined by Kaplan in
[
29
, §3.2]
by

\Gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\omega_{\pi}(-1)^{n}\omega_{\tau}(-1)^{l}\gamma^{\prime}(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi).

It follows that local converse theorems for
\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)
,
\gamma^{\prime}(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi),
and
\Gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)
are all equivalent.

4.
Partial Bessel functions

In this section, we introduce Howe vectors and partial Bessel functions. These played a crucial role in the proof of the local converse theorems considered in
[
17
,
26
,
44
,
45
]
. Their properties again play an important role in the manipulations of the zeta integrals in this paper. Much of the setup for Howe vectors and partial Bessel functions is carried out in the split case in
[
3
]
. Unfortunately, the quasi-split case was not considered. Nevertheless, we generalize the relevant ideas to our case.

4.1.
Howe vectors

Let
\omega
be a character of the center,
Z
, of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega)
be the space of smooth compactly supported functions
f
satisfying
f(zg)=\omega(z)f(g)
for any
z\in Z
and
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.
Recall that we fixed
\psi
to be a nontrivial unramified additive character of
F
and used it to define a character, also denoted by
\psi
, on
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
Let
C^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega,\psi)
be the space of functions
W
satisfying
W(zg)=\omega(z)W(g)
and
W(ug)=\psi(u)W(g)
for any
z\in Z,

u\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
, and
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l},
and furthermore that there exists an open compact subgroup
K
of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
for which
W
is invariant under right translation.

Let
m>0
be an integer and define congruence subgroups

\displaystyle K_{m}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2l}}

\displaystyle=I_{2l}+\mathrm{Mat}_{2l\times 2l}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),

\displaystyle K_{m}

\displaystyle=K_{m}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2l}}\cap\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

We define a character
\tau_{m}
on
K_{m}
by setting

\tau_{m}(k)=\psi\left(\varpi^{-2m}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l-2}k_{i,i+1}+\frac{1}{2}k_{l-1,l+1}\right)\right),

where
k=(k_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{2l}\in K_{m}.

Set

e_{m}=\mathrm{diag}(\varpi^{-2m(l-1)},\dots,\varpi^{-2m},1,1,\varpi^{2m},\dots,\varpi^{2m(l-1)}).

Let
H_{m}=e_{m}K_{m}e_{m}^{-1}
and define a character
\psi_{m}
on
H_{m}
via
\psi_{m}(h)=\tau_{m}(e_{m}^{-1}he_{m})
for
h\in H_{m}.
Let
U_{m}=U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\cap H_{m}.
Note that
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}=\cup_{m\geq 1}U_{m}
and the character
\psi
on
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
agrees with
\psi_{m}
upon restriction to
U_{m}.

We fix the split part, denoted
S_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
, of the torus
T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
to be the subset consisting of elements of the form
t=\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\dots,t_{l-1},I_{2},t_{l-1}^{-1},\dots,t_{1}^{-1}).
Let
W(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
be the Weyl group of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and let
\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
be the set of simple roots with respect to
S_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
. We can choose the simple roots to be given by
\alpha_{i}(t)=t_{i}t_{i+1}^{-1}
for
i=1,\dots,l-2
and
\alpha_{l-1}(t)=t_{l-1}.
In the general case of non-split groups, the root subgroups are not guaranteed to be isomorphic to
F
and so we define elements of
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
that we need here. Let
x\in F.
For
i=1,\dots,l-2
we define
\mathrm{\mathbf{x}}_{\alpha_{i}}(x)=(u_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{2l}\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
where
u_{j,j}=1,
for any
j=1,\dots,2l,

u_{i,i+1}=x,u_{2l-i,2l-(i-1)}=-x,
and
u_{i,j}=0
for any other pair
(i,j).
We also define

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{x}}_{\alpha_{l-1}}(x)=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{l-2}&&\\
&\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&x&\frac{\rho^{-1}x^{2}}{2}\\
0&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&\rho^{-1}x\\
0&0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)&\\
&&I_{l-2}\end{matrix}\right).

It is checked directly that
\mathrm{\mathbf{x}}_{\alpha}(x)\in U_{\alpha},
where
U_{\alpha}
denotes the root subgroup of
\alpha,
for any
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l}).

Now let
\alpha
be any root of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and
U_{\alpha}
be the root space of
\alpha.
Set
U_{\alpha,m}=H_{m}\cap U_{\alpha}.
For any simple root
\alpha
and
x\in F,
we verify directly that
\mathrm{\mathbf{x}}_{\alpha}(x)\in U_{\alpha,m}
if and only if
x\in\mathfrak{p}^{-m}.
This is an analogous result to
[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Any matrix in
K_{m}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2l}}
can be decomposed uniquely as the product of a unipotent upper triangular matrix and a lower triangular matrix. This property is also inherited by
K_{m}
and
H_{m}.
Let
\overline{B}_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
be the opposite parabolic subgroup to
B_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
We record the above fact in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

K_{m}=(U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\cap K_{m})(\overline{B}_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\cap K_{m}),\ \ H_{m}=(U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\cap H_{m})(\overline{B}_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\cap H_{m}).

Let
W\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega,\psi)
with
W(I_{2l})=1.
We define

W_{m}(g)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(U_{m})}\int_{U_{m}}\psi_{m}^{-1}(u)W(gu)du.

Let
C=C(W)
be the maximal positive integer such that
W
is invariant under right translation by
K_{C}.
If
m\geq C,
then
W_{m}
is called a Howe vector. The following lemma is the analogue of
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have

(1)

W_{m}(I_{2l})=1,

(2)

if
m\geq C,
then
W_{m}(gh)=\psi_{m}(h)W_{m}(g)
for any
h\in H_{m}
and
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

Proof.

Part (1) follows directly since
W(u)=\psi(u)W(I_{2l})=\psi(u)
and
\psi(u)=\psi_{m}(u)
for any
u\in U_{m}.

For Part (2), set

\displaystyle W_{m}^{\prime}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(H_{m})}\int_{H_{m}}\psi_{m}^{-1}(h)W(gh)dh.

Since
m\geq C
and
\overline{B}_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\cap H_{m}\subseteq K_{m}
, by Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle W_{m}^{\prime}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(U_{m})\mathrm{Vol}(\overline{B}_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\cap H_{m})}\int_{U_{m}}\int_{\overline{B}_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\cap H_{m}}\psi_{m}^{-1}(u)W(gub)dudb

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(U_{m})}\int_{U_{m}}\psi_{m}^{-1}(u)W(gu)du

\displaystyle=W_{m}(g).

From the definition, we have
W_{m}^{\prime}(gh)=\psi_{m}(h)W_{m}^{\prime}(g)
and hence it also follows that
W_{m}(gh)=\psi_{m}(h)W_{m}(g).

∎

Hence, for
m\geq C,
we have

(4.1)

W_{m}(ugh)=\psi(u)\psi_{m}(h)W_{m}(g),

for any
u\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
,
h\in H_{m}
, and
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.
For
m\geq C
,
W_{m}
is called a
partial Bessel function
.

The next lemma shows that the Whittaker model of an irreducible generic
representation
\pi
contains partial Bessel functions.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible generic representation of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and
W\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
with
W(I_{2l})=1.
Then we have
W_{m}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi).

Proof.

Let
V_{\pi}
be the vector space on which the representation
\pi
acts. Recall that we fixed
\Gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}(\pi,\psi)
to be a nonzero Whittaker functional. By definition, there exists
v\in V_{\pi}
such that
W(g)=W_{v}(g)=\Gamma(\pi(g)v)
for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.
Since
W_{v}(I_{2l})=1,
we have
\Gamma(v)=1.

Set

\displaystyle v_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(U_{m})}\int_{U_{m}}\psi_{m}^{-1}(u)\pi(u)vdu.

Then
W_{v_{m}}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
and

\displaystyle W_{v_{m}}(g)=\Gamma(\pi(g)v_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(U_{m})}\int_{U_{m}}\psi_{m}^{-1}(u)\Gamma(\pi(gu)v)dh

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(U_{m})}\int_{U_{m}}\psi_{m}^{-1}(u)W_{v}(gu)du

\displaystyle=W_{m}(g).

Thus
W_{m}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi).

∎

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega).
We define
W^{f}\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega,\psi)
by

W^{f}(g)=\int_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}\psi^{-1}(u)f(ug)du,

for
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.
Since
f
is locally constant and compactly supported, there exists a positive integer
C=C(f)
such that
W^{f}
is invariant under right translation by
K_{C}.
We choose
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega)
such that
W^{f}(I_{2l})=1.
Then, for
m>C
, we consider the corresponding partial Bessel function defined by

B_{m}(g;f):=(W^{f})_{m}(g)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(U_{m})}\int_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}\times U_{m}}\psi^{-1}(u)\psi_{m}^{-1}(u^{\prime})f(ugu^{\prime})dudu^{\prime}.

4.2.
Conjugate of partial Bessel Function

Let
\psi_{c}
be the character on
U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
defined by
\psi_{c}(u)=\psi(cuc).
That is,

\psi_{c}(u)=\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l-2}u_{i,i+1}-\frac{1}{2}u_{l-1,l+1}\right),

for any
u=(u_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{2l}\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
Recall that we fixed a nonzero
\Gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}(\pi,\psi).
Then
\Gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}}(\pi^{c},\psi_{c})
is also nonzero. Note that
\pi^{c}
is also
\psi
-generic. We relate the partial Bessel functions of
\pi
and
\pi^{c}
below.

Fix
\tilde{t}:=\mathrm{diag}(I_{l-1},-1,-1,I_{l-1})\in T_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}.
It is checked directly that
\psi_{c}(\tilde{t}^{-1}u\tilde{t})=\psi(u).
We also have
cK_{m}c=K_{m},

cU_{m}c=U_{m},
and
\psi_{m}(c\tilde{t}^{-1}u\tilde{t}c)=\psi_{m}(u).
Given
W\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega,\psi)
, define
W^{c}\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega,\psi)
by

W^{c}(g)=W(c\tilde{t}^{-1}g\tilde{t}c).
If
W(I_{2l})=1,
we also define
W_{m}^{c}=(W^{c})_{m}.
For
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega)
, we set
W^{f,c}=(W^{f})^{c}
and if
W^{f}(I_{2l})=1,
then we set
B_{m}^{c}(g;f)=(W^{f,c})_{m}.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
W\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega,\psi)
and
W(I_{2l})=1.
Then,

(1)

W^{c}(I_{2l})=1,

(2)

W^{c}_{m}=(W_{m})^{c},

(3)

C(W)=C(W^{c}),

(4)

for
m\geq C,
we have

W_{m}^{c}(ugh)=\psi(u)\psi_{m}(h)W_{m}^{c}(g),

for any
u\in U_{\mathrm{SO}_{2l}}
,
h\in H_{m}
, and
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}.

Proof.

(1)
is immediate from the definition. By definition and since
c\tilde{t}^{-1}U_{m}\tilde{t}c=U_{m}
, we obtain
(2).

(3)
follows from the fact
cK_{m}c=K_{m}.
Finally,
(4)
is verified directly from the previous results and definitions. ∎

Thus, we can see that given a partial Bessel function,
W_{m}
or
B_{m},
we may construct another partial Bessel function via the map
W_{m}\mapsto W_{m}^{c}
or
B_{m}\mapsto B_{m}^{c}.
In fact, this map sends partial Bessel functions in the Whittaker model of
\pi
to partial Bessel functions in the Whittaker model of
\pi^{c}.
Consequently, by the uniqueness of Whittaker models, this map is the identity map on the Whittaker model of
\pi
if and only if
\pi\cong\pi^{c}.

4.3.
Bessel Support

Recall
W(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
is the Weyl group and
\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
is the set of simple roots of
\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
. We say that an element
w\in W(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})
supports partial Bessel functions if for any

The standard maximal parabolic subgroups of

Much of the discussion in this section follows

4.5.
Outline of the proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we outline the strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Let
\mathcal{C}(0)
denote the condition that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
have the same central character
\omega
. We always assume
\mathcal{C}(0).
For
n\geq 1,
define the condition
\mathcal{C}(n)
by
\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi^{\prime}\times\tau,\psi)
, as functions of the complex variable

\tau
of
\mathrm{GL}_{k}
with
1\leq k\leq n
along with
\mathcal{C}(0).
It is clear that
\mathcal{C}(n)
implies
\mathcal{C}(n-1).
As
\pi
is supercuspidal, its matrix coefficients, denoted by
\mathcal{M}(\pi)
, have compact support. That is,
\mathcal{M}(\pi)\subseteq C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega).
Since
\pi
is generic, the map
\mathcal{M}(\pi)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l},\omega,\psi)
given by
f\mapsto W^{f}
is nonzero.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\pi)
and
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\pi^{\prime})
such that
W^{f}(I_{2l})=W^{f^{\prime}}(I_{2l})=1.
By assumption,
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
satisfy
\mathcal{C}(0).
By Corollary
4.11
, there exists
f_{\tilde{w}_{n}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tilde{w}_{n}},\omega)
for
1\leq n\leq l-1
such that for
m
large enough

(4.2)

B_{m}(g,f)-B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})=\sum_{n=1}^{l-1}B_{m}(g,f_{\tilde{w}_{n}}).

Similar to
[
17
,
44
,
45
]
, we show that the condition
\mathcal{C}(k)
implies

B_{m}(g,f)-B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})=\sum_{n=k+1}^{l-1}B_{m}(g,f_{\tilde{w}_{n}}).

for
k\leq l-1
(Theorem
6.1
). Thus
\mathcal{C}(l-2)
gives

B_{m}(g,f)-B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})=B_{m}(g,f_{\tilde{w}_{l-1}}).

However, unlike the symplectic and unitary cases,
\mathcal{C}(l-1)
does not imply that we can remove
B_{m}(g,f_{\tilde{w}_{l-1}}).
Instead, it only shows that
B_{m}(\cdot,f_{\tilde{w}_{l-1}})=0
on elements which are fixed by the outer conjugation by

(Theorem
6.7
). For those elements which are not fixed by

we use the condition
\mathcal{C}(l).
It determines that
B_{m}(\cdot,f_{\tilde{w}_{l-1}})+B_{m}^{c}(\cdot,f_{\tilde{w}_{l-1}})=0
on all the elements which are not fixed by the outer conjugation (Theorem
6.11
). As a result, we must consider the partial Bessel functions for
\pi^{c}
and
\pi^{\prime c}.
Consequently, we deduce Theorem
1.2
from the uniqueness of Whittaker models.

\theta_{w}=\theta_{w_{\mathrm{max}}}\backslash\{\alpha_{i}\}
for some
k+1\leq i\leq{l-1}.
Set
w_{i}^{\prime}=w_{\theta_{w_{\mathrm{max}}}\backslash\{\alpha_{i}\}}.
Then,

\{w\in\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\,|\,w>w_{\mathrm{max}},d_{B}(w,w_{\mathrm{max}})=1\}=\{w_{i}^{\prime}\,|\,k+1\leq i\leq{l-1}\}.

Thus,

\Omega_{\tilde{w}_{k}}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=k+1}^{l-1}\Omega_{w_{i}^{\prime}}.

By a partition of unity argument (similar to the proof of Corollary
4.11
), there exists
f_{w_{i}^{\prime}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{w_{i}^{\prime}},\omega)
for
k+1\leq i\leq l-1
such that

f_{\tilde{w}_{k}}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=k+1}^{l-1}f_{w_{i}^{\prime}}.

So,

(6.2)

B_{m}(g,f_{\tilde{w}_{k}})=B_{m}(g,f_{\tilde{w}_{k}}^{\prime})=\sum_{i=k+1}^{l-1}B_{m}(g,f_{w_{i}^{\prime}}),

for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and
m
large enough.

Finally, fix
i
such that
k+1\leq i\leq{l-1}.
We have
\theta_{\tilde{w}_{i}}=\Delta(\mathrm{SO}_{2l})\backslash\{\alpha_{i}\}
. Since
\theta_{w_{i}^{\prime}}\subseteq\theta_{w_{\mathrm{max}}}\backslash\{\alpha_{i}\}\subseteq\theta_{\tilde{w}_{i}},
it follows that
w_{i}^{\prime}>\tilde{w}_{i}
and hence
\Omega_{w_{i}^{\prime}}\subseteq\Omega_{\tilde{w}_{i}}.
By Proposition
4.7
,
\Omega_{w_{i}^{\prime}}
is open in
\Omega_{\tilde{w}_{i}}.
Hence,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{w_{i}^{\prime}},\omega)\subseteq C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tilde{w}_{i}},\omega).
Thus
f_{w_{i}}^{\prime}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{w_{i}^{\prime}},\omega)
. Set

f_{\tilde{w}_{i}}^{\prime}=f_{w_{i}}^{\prime}+f_{\tilde{w}_{i}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{w_{i}^{\prime}},\omega).

Then, by the inductive hypothesis and Equation (
6.2
), we have

B_{m}(g,f)-B_{m}(g,f^{\prime})=\sum_{i=k+1}^{l-1}B_{m}(g,f_{\tilde{w}_{i}}^{\prime}).

for any
g\in\mathrm{SO}_{2l}
and
m
large enough depending only on
f
and
f^{\prime}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
∎

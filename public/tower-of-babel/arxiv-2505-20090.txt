On Model Predictive Funnel Control with Equilibrium Endpoint Constraints*
Jens Göbel 1,4, Dario Dennstädt 2,3, Lukas Lanza 2, Karl Worthmann 2, Thomas Berger 3, Tobias Damm 1,4

Abstract
We propose model predictive funnel control , a novel model predictive control (MPC) scheme building upon recent results in funnel control. The latter is a high-gain feedback methodology that achieves evolution of the measured output within predefined error margins. The proposed method dynamically optimizes a parameter-dependent error boundary in a receding-horizon manner, thereby combining prescribed error guarantees from funnel control with the predictive advantages of MPC. On the one hand, this approach promises faster optimization times due to a reduced number of decision variables, whose number does not depend on the horizon length. On the other hand, the continuous feedback law improves the robustness and also explicitly takes care of the inter-sampling behavior. We focus on proving stability by leveraging results from MPC stability theory with terminal equality constraints. Moreover, we rigorously show initial and recursive feasibility.



Keywords
: Adaptive control, Funnel control, Nonlinear output feedback, Predictive control for nonlinear systems, Prescribed transient behavior

Introduction

We address the problem of stabilizing a nonlinear
control system subject to time-varying output constraints given
by user-specific performance bounds (funnel boundaries).
To achieve this, we propose a novel bi-level control framework that synergies
funnel control

and
model predictive control
(MPC):

•

A lower-level fixed funnel control law ensuring constraint satisfaction.

•

An upper-level MPC-based optimizer tuning the funnel control parameters (see
Figure

1
).

Funnel control
is a model-free controller that achieves output reference tracking, first described in
[
1
]
.
It has since garnered significant attention,
see, e.g., the recent survey article
[
2
]
and references therein.
Funnel control has been successfully applied in many fields of engineering. Examples include speed control of wind turbines, position control of robotic manipulators or current control of synchronous machines, cf.
[
3
]
.

A similar concept, namely prescribed performance control (PPC), introduced in
[
4
]
and further developed e.g. in
[
5
]
, has recently been linked to control barrier functions for reactive feedback design
[
6
]
.
Both PPC and funnel control are high-gain adaptive control methods that enforce tracking within error bounds by scaling the gain with the inverse of the distance between tracking error and given boundary.
Under certain structural conditions, such as matched input-output dimensions and a well-defined relative degree, these methods ensure bounded tracking errors.
While earlier funnel control required nonzero funnel diameters, recent works
[
7
,
2
]
allow asymptotic tracking for systems with higher relative degree.
The present paper builds on
[
8
]
, which demonstrates exact tracking in finite time using funnel control.

MPC

is a well-established and versatile control methodology that is widely applied both in research and industry, see, e.g.,
[
9
]
or the recent survey
[
10
]
.
Unlike the model-free funnel control, MPC relies on a model of the controlled process.
It optimizes the predicted system behavior over a discretized input signal according to a pre-defined cost function.
While powerful, the computational complexity of solving this optimization problem within strict sampling time constraints poses significant difficulties in real-time applications, see, e.g.,
[
11
,
12
]
and the references therein.
Beyond that, the open-loop application of the control signal between optimization steps limits
its robustness against disturbances.
Common approaches to ensure constraint satisfaction under external disturbances or even under model mismatch are, e.g., tube-based MPC
[
13
,
14
]
or min-max MPC schemes, see, e.g.,
[
15
,
16
]
, where the latter considers a data-driven scheme.

Our integrated method, termed
model predictive funnel control
(MPFC), merges MPC and funnel control by
optimizing a parameter-dependent funnel shape using an MPC-like strategy.
That is, we seek an optimal feedback among a restricted family of feedback laws.
At each MPFC step, funnel-shape parameters are optimized for the current prediction horizon in the closed-loop system. This reduces optimization variables compared to classical MPC, where the decision vector grows with the horizon length. MPFC also ensures a stabilizing control law between recalculation steps, enhancing robustness over traditional MPC, which uses open-loop control until the next iteration. Since we apply a funnel controller, performance is maintained even if the model doesn’t fully match the real system.
Stability is proven using equilibrium endpoint constraints from classical MPC theory, cf.
[
17
]
. This stability relies on MPC predictions reaching the system’s equilibrium by the end of the prediction horizon through additional constraints, enabling a cost-neutral horizon extension and proving bounded closed-loop costs. For the MPFC version presented here, results from
[
8
]
naturally ensure that the equilibrium is reached within the prediction horizon, without extra constraints.

While prior work has explored combinations of funnel control and MPC under
the name
funnel MPC
in a series of papers, see
[
18
,
19
,
20
]

and the references therein, our approach differs fundamentally in both
methodology and scope.
Existing funnel MPC implementations retain the classical funnel control
paradigm of a static, user-defined funnel shape and instead optimize only
the applied control values. This penalizes proximity of the system output to
the given boundaries via rising costs but does not adapt the funnel
itself. Other MPC approaches that consider prescribing output constraints
usually focus on discrete-time systems instead
[
21
]
.

The funnel shape considered in this work, as well as in
[
8
]
, differs from classical funnel control in that it reaches zero in prescribed finite time
T>0
.
Due to this singularity in
T
, the closed-loop dynamics require infinitely small step sizes in the numerical solution.
The results developed in this brief paper prove the feasibility of the proposed control algorithm from a theoretical perspective.

3
Control Methodology

We introduce the control methodology to achieve the control objective (

3.1
Exact Funnel Control

, shows existence of a parameter pair satisfying
8
.

Step 4:

As a direct consequence of (
10
) in
Step 2
,
every solution
y_{i}
satisfies (
9
).
This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we prove boundedness of the closed-loop costs.

Lemma 6
.

Let the assumptions of
Theorem

5
be fulfilled.
Then, the input signals
u_{i}(\cdot)
and the output signals
y_{i}(\cdot)
resulting from
Algorithm

4
fulfill

\displaystyle J_{\infty}^{cl}(H,y_{0}(0))

\displaystyle\mathrel{\coloneqq}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\ell(y_{i}(t),u_{i}(t))dt

\displaystyle\leq\hat{V}_{H}(0,y_{0}(0))<\infty.

Proof.

Continuing the considerations of the proof of
Theorem

5
, it follows that
for all
\hat{t}\geq 0
and
\hat{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we have

\mathcal{F}_{H-h}(\hat{t},\hat{y})\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{H}(\hat{t},\hat{y})
by inspecting the properties of the set in (
6
).
Moreover, the horizon can be extended with zero cost:

\forall\,(c,T)\in\mathcal{F}_{H-h}(\hat{t},\hat{y}):\ J_{H-h}(\hat{y},c,T)=J_{H}(\hat{y},c,T).

We calculate, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle J_{H}(y_{i}(t_{i}),c_{i}^{*},T_{i}^{*})=\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i}+H}\ell(y_{i}(t),u_{i}(t))\;dt+c^{*}_{i}

(11)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\ell(y_{i}(t),u_{i}(t))\;dt+\int_{t_{i+1}}^{t_{i}+H}\ell(y_{i}(t),u_{i}(t))\;dt+c^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lx@cref{refnum}{eq:valuefcn}}}{{=}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\ell(y_{i}(t),u_{i}(t))\;dt+J_{H-h}(y_{i+1}(t_{i+1}),c_{i}^{*},T_{i}^{*}-h)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\ell(y_{i}(t),u_{i}(t))\;dt+J_{H}(y_{i+1}(t_{i+1}),c_{i}^{*},T_{i}^{*}-h)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lx@cref{refnum}{eq:fmpcstep2}}}{{\geq}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\ell(y_{i}(t),u_{i}(t))\;dt+J_{H}(y_{i+1}(t_{i+1}),c_{i+1}^{*},T_{i+1}^{*}).

Rearranging gives

\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\ell(y_{i}(t),u_{i}(t))dt\leq J_{H}(y_{i}(t_{i}),c_{i}^{*},T_{i}^{*})-J_{H}(y_{i+1}(t_{i+1}),c_{i+1}^{*},T_{i+1}^{*}).

As this holds for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, summing up to
K\in\mathbb{N}
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{K}

\displaystyle\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\ell(y_{i}(t),u_{i}(t))dt

\displaystyle\leq J_{H}(y_{0}(0),c_{0}^{*},T_{0}^{*})-J_{H}(y_{K+1}(t_{K+1}),c_{K+1}^{*},T_{K+1}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq J_{H}(y_{0}(0),c_{0}^{*},T_{0}^{*})\leq\hat{V}_{H}(0,y_{0}(0)).

Since the costs are nonnegative, the sum monotonically increases and is bounded for
K\to\infty
.
So, it converges.
∎

We prove boundedness of the optimized parameters.

Corollary 7
.

Let the assumptions of
Theorem

5
be fulfilled.
Then, the set

\left\{c_{i}^{*}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}

resulting from
Algorithm

4
is bounded.

Proof.

According to (
7
) and (
11
) for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

c_{i}^{*}\leq J_{H}(y_{i}(t_{i}),c_{i}^{*},T_{i}^{*})\leq J_{H}(y_{i-1}(t_{i-1}),c_{i-1}^{*},T_{i-1}^{*}).

Thus,
c_{i}^{*}\leq J_{H}(y_{0}(0),c_{0}^{*},T_{0}^{*})
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Next, we show that the boundedness of the closed-loop costs implies convergence of the closed-loop solution, if
Q
is positive definite.
We thereby prove that
Algorithm

4
fulfills the control objective (
2
) in
Section

2
.
In the context of
Algorithm

4
, define the
closed-loop solution
as

\displaystyle y^{cl}(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\coloneqq}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\chi_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})}(t)\,y_{i}(t),

\displaystyle u^{cl}(t)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\coloneqq}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\chi_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})}(t)\,u_{i}(t),

where
\chi_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})}
is the indicator function on

.

Theorem 8
.

Let the assumptions of
Theorem

5
be fulfilled.
In addition, assume
Q
to be positive definite.
Then,
Algorithm

4
asymptotically stabilizes system
1
, i.e., it holds that

\lim_{t\to\infty}y^{cl}(t)=0
.

Proof.

Seeking a contradiction, we assume

\exists\,\varepsilon>0\ \forall\,t_{0}\geq 0\ \exists\,t>t_{0}\colon\ \|y^{cl}(t)\|>\varepsilon.

(14)

Step 1
:
Let
h=t_{i+1}-t_{i}
.
Since
Q
is symmetric positive definite, there exists a constant
q>0
such that

\langle y,Qy\rangle\geq q\|y\|^{2}

for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
For
t\geq 0
, define the closed-loop funnel function as

\varphi^{cl}(t)\mathrel{\coloneqq}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\chi_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})}(t)\;\varphi(t-t_{i};c_{i}^{*},T_{i}^{*})
.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, define

\hat{N}(x)\mathrel{\coloneqq}\max_{\xi\in\mathopen{[}0,x\mathclose{]}}\left|N(\xi)\right|
.

Step 2
: We show that, given the assumption (
14
), the control input
u^{cl}
is unbounded.
According to
Lemma

6
,
J^{cl}_{\infty}(H,y_{0}(0))<\infty
. This implies

\forall\,\varepsilon_{c}>0\ \exists\,t_{\varepsilon_{c}}\in\mathbb{R}\colon\int_{t_{\varepsilon_{c}}}^{\infty}\ell(y^{cl}(t),u^{cl}(t))\;\text{d}t<\varepsilon_{c}

(15)

and, since
Q
is positive definite,

\forall\,t\geq 0\ \exists\,\tau_{\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{3}}}(t)>t\colon\ \|y^{cl}(\tau_{\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{3}}}(t))\|<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}.

(16)

So, combining (
14
) – (
16
),

for any given
\varepsilon_{c}\in\mathopen{(}0,\frac{qh\varepsilon^{2}}{36}\mathclose{)}
,
we find
t_{\varepsilon}>\tau_{\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{3}}}(t_{\varepsilon_{c}})

with
\|y^{cl}(t_{\varepsilon})\|>\varepsilon
.
There exist

\displaystyle t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\coloneqq}\max\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid\|y^{cl}(t)\|=\tfrac{1}{3}\varepsilon\;\wedge\;t<t_{\varepsilon}\},

\displaystyle t_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\coloneqq}\min\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid\|y^{cl}(t)\|=\tfrac{2}{3}\varepsilon\;\wedge\;t>t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}\}

because
y^{cl}
is continuous and
t_{\varepsilon}>\tau_{\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{3}}}(t_{\varepsilon_{c}})
.
We estimate

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{c}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}}^{t_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}}\ell(y^{cl}(t),u^{cl}(t))\;\text{d}t\geq\int_{t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}}^{t_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}}\langle y^{cl}(t),Qy^{cl}(t)\rangle\;\text{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}}^{t_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}}q\|y^{cl}(t)\|^{2}\;\text{d}t\geq q\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)^{2}(t_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}-t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}).

Since
y^{cl}
is piecewise differentiable, the mean value theorem
yields the existence of
t_{\text{crit}}\in\mathopen{[}t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}},t_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}\mathclose{]}
with

\displaystyle\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\|{y}^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\|\geq\tfrac{\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon-\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon}{t_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}-t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}}=\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3(t_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}-t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}})}\geq\tfrac{3\varepsilon^{3}q}{\varepsilon_{c}}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon_{c}\to 0]{}\infty.

Continuity of
f
and
\|y^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\|\leq\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon<\infty
imply

\displaystyle\|u^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\|\xrightarrow[\varepsilon_{c}\to 0]{}\infty.

(17)

Step 3
:
We will lead statement (
17
)
to a contradiction by establishing a bound on
\|u^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\|
.
To this end, we distinguish two cases.

Case 1:

\varphi^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})>\varepsilon
. Since
\|y^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\|\leq\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon
and
\varphi^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})>\varepsilon
,
we have
\frac{\|y^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\|}{\varphi^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})}<\frac{2}{3}
.
Then, with
c
being the active parameter at time
t_{\text{crit}}
,

\displaystyle\|u^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\|

\displaystyle=\left\|N\left(\alpha_{c}\left(\tfrac{\|y^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\|^{2}}{\varphi^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})^{2}}\right)\right)\tfrac{y^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})}{\varphi^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\hat{N}\left(\alpha_{c}\left(\tfrac{4}{9}\right)\right)\tfrac{2}{3}<\infty

contradicting (
17
).

Case 2:

\varphi^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\leq\varepsilon
.
In this case, a similar chain of arguments can be performed.
Define

\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}\mathrel{\coloneqq}\min\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid\|y^{cl}(t)\|=\tfrac{1}{3}\varepsilon\;\wedge\;t>t_{\varepsilon}\},

and
\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}\mathrel{\coloneqq}\max\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid\|y^{cl}(t)\|=\tfrac{2}{3}\varepsilon\;\wedge\;t<\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}\}
, where

t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}
exists due to
16
,
and
t_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}
due to the intermediate value theorem.

We estimate

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{c}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}}^{\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}}\ell(y^{cl}(t),u^{cl}(t))\;\text{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}}^{\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}}y^{cl}(t)^{\mathsf{T}}Qy^{cl}(t)\;\text{d}t\geq q\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)^{2}(\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}-\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}).

The mean value theorem yields the existence of
\hat{t}_{\text{crit}}\in\mathopen{[}\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}},\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}\mathclose{]}
with

\displaystyle\tfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\|{y}^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\|\leq\tfrac{\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon-\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon}{\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}-\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}}=\tfrac{-\varepsilon}{3(\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}-\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}})}\leq\tfrac{-\varepsilon^{3}q}{\varepsilon_{c}}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon_{c}\to 0]{}-\infty.

Since
f
is continuous and
\|y^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})\|\leq\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon<\infty
, this implies

\|u^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})\|\to\infty
for
\varepsilon_{c}\to 0
.

Step 4
:
We show
\varphi^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})>\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon
.
Seeking a contradiction, assume
\varphi^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})\leq\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon
.
Since
\varphi^{cl}(t_{\text{crit}})\leq\varepsilon
and
\varphi^{cl}(t_{\varepsilon})>\varepsilon
, there must be a resampling time between
t_{\text{crit}}
and
t_{\varepsilon}
, i.e.,
there exists
i^{*}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
t_{i^{*}}\in\mathopen{[}t_{\text{crit}},t_{\varepsilon}\mathclose{)}
.

W.l.o.g.
T_{i}^{*}>h
(otherwise we have convergence, since
y^{cl}(t)=0
for
t\geq t_{i^{*}}+h
).
Since
\varphi^{cl}(t_{i^{*}})>\varepsilon
,

\varphi^{cl}(t_{i^{*}}+\tau)>\varepsilon\left(1-\tfrac{\tau}{h}\right)

for
\tau\in\mathopen{[}0,h\mathclose{)}
.

This implies, if
\varphi^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})\leq\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon
, then

\hat{t}_{\text{crit}}-t_{i^{*}}>\frac{h}{4}
.
But
\|y^{cl}(t)\|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
for
t\in\mathopen{[}t_{i^{*}},\hat{t}_{\text{crit}}\mathclose{]}\subseteq\mathopen{[}t_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}},\hat{t}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}}\mathclose{]}
.
This leads to

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{c}

\displaystyle>\int_{t_{i^{*}}}^{\infty}\ell(y^{cl}(t),u^{cl}(t))\;\text{d}t>q\int_{t_{i^{*}}}^{\hat{t}_{\text{crit}}}\|y^{cl}(t)\|^{2}\;\text{d}t

\displaystyle>q\,(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}}-t_{i^{*}})\left(\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)^{2}>q\;\tfrac{h}{4}\left(\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)^{2}>\varepsilon_{c},

a contradiction. Therefore,
\|y^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})\|\leq\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon

and
\varphi^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})>\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon
.
It follows that, with
c
being the active parameter at time
\hat{t}_{\text{crit}}
,

\displaystyle\|u^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})\|

\displaystyle=\left\|N\left(\alpha_{c}\left(\tfrac{\|y^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})\|^{2}}{\varphi^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})^{2}}\right)\right)\tfrac{y^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})}{\varphi^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\hat{N}\left(\alpha_{c}\left(\tfrac{8^{2}}{9^{2}}\right)\right)\tfrac{8}{9}<\infty,

which contradicts
\|u^{cl}(\hat{t}_{\text{crit}})\|\to\infty
for
\varepsilon_{c}\to 0
.

∎

Conclusion and Outlook

We presented model predictive funnel control, a novel combination of funnel control and MPC.
It features a constant number of decision variables in the optimization problem and yields
closed-loop guarantees

even on inter-sampling intervals.
We rigorously showed boundedness of the closed-loop costs, as well as convergence of the solution.
Future work will address systems of higher relative degree with internal dynamics, relaxing the choice of funnel boundary (
\varphi(T)\neq 0
), considering input constraints, and performing reference tracking tasks, to name a few of the upcoming topics.
We will conduct numerical studies to analyse performance in the sense of computation time compared to MPC in specific scenarios, e.g., for long prediction horizons or fine discretization grids.

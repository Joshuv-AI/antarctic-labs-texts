Generalized Stationary discs attached to degenerate submanifolds in ℂ N \mathbb{C}^{N} . Thanks: This work was supported by the Center for Advanced Mathematical Sciences, by the Faculty of Arts and Sciences, and by a URB grant from the American University of Beirut.
Mohammad Tarek Al Masri, Florian Bertrand, Francine Meylan, Lea Oueidat, Hadi Zoghaib

Abstract
. We study the family of generalized stationary discs attached to a Levi degenerate submanifold M M of codimension d d in ℂ n + d \mathbb{C}^{n+d} . We show, under suitable geometric assumptions on M M , that this family forms a finite dimensional real submanifold of the Banach space of analytic discs.



Introduction

Let
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
be a germ of a finitely smooth generic real submanifold of codimension
d
at
p,
and let
Aut(M,p)
be its stability group, that is, the set of germs of biholomorphisms mapping
M
into itself and fixing
p
. Initiated by Lempert in
[
21
]
, the theory of stationary discs attached to
M
developed, for instance in
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
18
,
23
,
24
]
, turns out to be a powerful tool to detect finite jet determination for elements of
Aut(M,p)
. More precisely, in the case of
\mathcal{C}^{4}
Levi nondegenerate generic real submanifolds, the study of stationary discs yields algebraic conditions that force 2-jet determination for elements of
Aut(M,p)
and more generally for germs of CR automorphisms of
M

of class
\mathcal{C}^{3}
and fixing
p
(see
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
24
]
). In the case of Levi degenerate real hypersurfaces, by constructing
generalized
(or higher order) stationary discs,
finite jet determination for finitely smooth CR diffeomorphisms of a class of weakly pseudoconvex hypersurfaces of finite type in
\mathbb{C}^{2}

has been obtained in

[
6
]
, while finite jet determination for finitely smooth CR diffeomorphisms of a class of generically Levi nondegenerate
hypersurfaces of finite type in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
has been established in
[
8
]
.

In the recent work
[
1
]
, the authors start building a theory of generalized stationary discs for Levi degenerate generic real submanifold by considering perturbations of decoupled submanifolds in
\mathbb{C}^{4}.
In the present paper, we address this question for Levi degenerate submanifolds
M
of codimension
d
whose models are
rigid
, that is, given in suitable holomorphic coordinates
(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
, referred to as
canonical coordinates
in Theorem 4.3.2
[
2
]
,
at
p=0
by

M=\{\Re ew=P(z,\bar{z})+{\rm higher\ order\ terms}\},

where
P=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{d})\neq 0
, and
P_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,\ldots,d,
is a homogeneous polynomial, with no pluriharmonic terms, of degree
D_{\ell}
or
P_{\ell}\equiv 0
. We observe that a class of
finite type
submanifolds, in the sense of Kohn and Bloom-Graham (see
[
2
,
12
,
13
,
20
]
), may be expressed in this form. In particular, any hypersurface of finite type in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is of this form.
In this paper, we focus on the model, denoted by

M_{H}=\{\Re ew=P(z,\bar{z})\}
, and consider
M
as a perturbation of
M_{H}
.
The construction of generalized stationary discs for perturbations of a Levi degenerate model
M_{H}
is mainly obstructed by the fact that the conormal bundle of
M_{H}
is no longer totally real (see
[
23
]
). In the hypersurface case studied in
[
8
]
,
the idea is then to consider models, referred to as
admissible
, that admit an initial generalized stationary disc passing through the degeneracy locus of
M_{H}
at a
single point with a prescribed order
. The analogous condition, also called
admissibilty
, is introduced in Definition
1.1
and plays a crucial role in our approach.
Our main result, Theorem
3.1
, concerns the construction of a finite dimensional submanifold of stationary discs for a class of finitely smooth perturbations
M
of an admissible model
M_{H}
. We point out that its dimension is explicit and depends
{\it only}
on the geometry of the model
M_{H}
.
An interesting application
of Theorem
3.1
is when
M_{H}
is included in the product of a Levi nondegenerate, or more generally admissible, hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
and
\mathbb{C}^{d-1}
.
The proof of Theorem
3.1
relies on the study of a singular Riemann-Hilbert type problem by means of an adapted implicit function theorem and techniques developed by Forstenrič
[
14
]
, Globevnik
[
15
,
16
]
, and Della Sala and the second author
[
7
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we introduce the notion of admissibility for a model submanifold
M_{H}
and provide examples of
such models. In Section
2
, we recall the different notions needed in the rest of the paper, that is,
the Banach spaces of analytics discs with constraints, the notion of generalized stationary discs and the corresponding Riemann-Hilbert problem. Finally, we state and prove our main result in Section
3
and take this opportunity to discuss a geometric property enjoyed by the family of discs constructed in Theorem
3.1
, and open directions related to the jet determination of CR mappings.

1.
Admissible degenerate submanifolds of
\mathbb{C}^{n+d}

The unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
is denoted by
\Delta=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ |\zeta|<1\}
. We denote its boundary by
\partial\Delta
.
Let
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}=\mathbb{C}^{n}_{z}\times\mathbb{C}^{d}_{w}
be a finitely smooth real submanifold of real codimension
d
through
0
given locally by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}r_{1}=\Re ew_{1}-P_{1}(z,\overline{z})+O(D_{1}+1)=0\\
\vdots\\
r_{d}=\Re ew_{d}-P_{d}(z,\overline{z})+O(D_{d}+1)=0\end{cases}

where
P_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,\ldots,d
, is a real homogeneous polynomial of degree
D_{\ell}
written as

P_{\ell}(z,\overline{z})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|+|J|=D_{\ell}\\
D_{\ell}-k_{\ell}\leq|I|\leq k_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\alpha^{\ell}_{IJ}z^{I}\overline{z}^{J}

with
\alpha_{IJ}=\overline{\alpha}_{JI}
. Here
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
and
J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})
are multi-indices.
We choose

\frac{D_{\ell}}{2}\leq k_{\ell}\leq D_{\ell}-1

in such a way that there exists
(\tilde{I},\tilde{J})
with
|\tilde{J}|=k_{\ell}
such that
\alpha_{\tilde{I}\tilde{J}}\neq 0
. Moreover, we assume that
2\leq D_{1}\leq D_{\ell}\leq D_{d}
,
\ell=1,\ldots,d
. Note that
P_{2},\ldots,P_{d}
may be identically equal to zero. We write
M=\{r=0\}
where
r:=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d})
. We also set
k_{0}:=\max\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\}
and point out that this integer will play an important role in the rest of the paper.

We associate with
M
its model submanifold
M_{H}
given by

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\rho_{1}=\Re ew_{1}-P_{1}(z,\overline{z})=0\\
\vdots\\
\rho_{d}=\Re ew_{d}-P_{d}(z,\overline{z})=0\end{cases}

We set
\rho:=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{d})
and then write
M_{H}=\{\rho=0\}
.

Assume that the model
M_{H}
is a quadric, that is, with

(1.3)

P_{\ell}(z,\overline{z})=\,^{t}\overline{z}A_{\ell}z

for
\ell=1,\ldots,d
where
A_{\ell}
is a Hermitian
n\times n
matrix. In this setting,

M_{H}
is
strongly Levi nondegenerate
if there exists an invertible real linear combination of the
A_{\ell}
’s. This condition is crucial in the construction of stationary discs attached to the quadric
M_{H}
and its perturbations.
For degenerate models, we then introduce an invariant notion which is the analogous to the
strong Levi nondegeneracy
.

We define, for any
d
real numbers
c_{1},\ldots,c_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
and
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, functions
Q_{i\overline{j}},S_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}:\partial\Delta\to\mathbb{C}
,
i,j=1,\ldots,n
, as follows

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\zeta^{k_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{i}\overline{z_{j}}}\left((1-\zeta)V,(1-\overline{\zeta})\overline{V}\right)=\left(1-\overline{\zeta}\right)^{D_{1}-2}Q_{i\overline{j}}(\zeta)\\
\\
\bar{\zeta}^{k_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}P_{\ell,\overline{z_{i}}\overline{z_{j}}}\left((1-\zeta)V,(1-\overline{\zeta})\overline{V}\right)=\left(1-\overline{\zeta}\right)^{D_{1}-2}S_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}(\zeta)\\
\end{array}\right.

Here we write
\frac{\partial^{2}P_{\ell}}{\partial z_{i}\partial\overline{z_{j}}}
(resp.
\frac{\partial^{2}P_{\ell}}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}\partial\overline{z_{j}}}
) as
P_{\ell,z_{i}\overline{z_{j}}}
(resp.
P_{\ell,\overline{z_{i}}\overline{z_{j}}}
).
We claim that
Q_{i\overline{j}}
are holomorphic polynomials of degree at most
2k_{0}+1
, and is divisible by
\zeta^{D_{1}-1}
. For clarity, we prove this claim in the special case of
V=(1,\ldots,1)
since an arbitrary choice of
V
can only
increase (resp. decrease) the degree of the first nonvanishing term (resp. the degree).
Setting
U(\zeta):=\displaystyle\zeta^{k_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{i}\overline{z_{j}}}\left(1-\zeta,1-\overline{\zeta}\right)
, we have

\ \begin{array}[]{lll}U(\zeta)&=&\displaystyle\zeta^{k_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|+|J|=D_{\ell}\\
D_{\ell}-k_{\ell}\leq|I|\leq k_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\alpha^{\ell}_{IJ}}(1-\zeta)^{|I|-1}\left(1-\overline{\zeta}\right)^{|J|-1}\\
\\
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|+|J|=D_{\ell}\\
D_{\ell}-k_{\ell}\leq|I|\leq k_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\alpha^{\ell}_{IJ}}(-1)^{|I|-1}\zeta^{|I|+k_{0}-1}\left(1-\overline{\zeta}\right)^{D_{\ell}-2}\\
\\
&=&\displaystyle\left(1-\overline{\zeta}\right)^{D_{1}-2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|+|J|=D_{\ell}\\
D_{\ell}-k_{\ell}\leq|I|\leq k_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\alpha^{\ell}_{IJ}}(-1)^{|I|-1}\zeta^{|I|+k_{0}-1-D_{\ell}+D_{1}}\left(1-\zeta\right)^{D_{\ell}-D_{1}}.\\
\end{array}

The claim then follows from the fact that
|I|+k_{0}-1-D_{\ell}+D_{1}\geq D_{1}-1

since
D_{1}\leq D_{\ell}
for any
\ell
and
k_{0}=\max\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\}
.
Note also that
S_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}
are antiholomorphic polynomials since, again taking
V=(1,\ldots,1)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\bar{\zeta}^{k_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}P_{\ell,\overline{z_{i}}\overline{z_{j}}}\left(1-\zeta,\overline{1-\zeta}\right)&=&\displaystyle\bar{\zeta}^{k_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|+|J|=D_{\ell}\\
D_{\ell}-k_{\ell}\leq|I|\leq k_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\alpha^{\ell}_{IJ}}(1-\zeta)^{|I|}\left(1-\overline{\zeta}\right)^{|J|-2}\\
\\
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|+|J|=D_{\ell}\\
D_{\ell}-k_{\ell}\leq|I|\leq k_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\alpha^{\ell}_{IJ}}(-1)^{|I|-1}\bar{\zeta}^{k_{0}-|I|}\left(1-\overline{\zeta}\right)^{D_{\ell}-2}\\
\\
&=&\displaystyle\left(1-\overline{\zeta}\right)^{D_{1}-2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|+|J|=D_{\ell}\\
D_{\ell}-k_{\ell}\leq|I|\leq k_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{\alpha^{\ell}_{IJ}}(-1)^{|I|-1}\bar{\zeta}^{k_{0}-|I|}\left(1-\overline{\zeta}\right)^{D_{\ell}-D_{1}}.\\
\end{array}

Finally, similarly to
[
8
]
, we define the following
n\times n
matrix valued map

(1.5)

Q(\zeta)=(Q_{i\overline{j}}(\zeta))

for
\zeta\in\partial\Delta
. This map plays an important role in this paper and in the construction of stationary discs established in Theorem
3.1
. In case
Q(\zeta)
is invertible for all
\zeta\in\partial\Delta
, we denote by
{\rm ind}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}}Q\right)

the winding number at the origin of the function
\zeta\mapsto{\rm det}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}(\zeta)}Q(\zeta)\right)
, that is,

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Delta}\frac{\left({\rm det}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}(\zeta)}Q(\zeta)\right)\right)^{\prime}}{{\rm det}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}(\zeta)}Q(\zeta)\right)}{\rm d}\zeta.

We can now define:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M_{H}
be a model given by (
1.2
). We say that the pair
(c,V)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is
admissible
if the matrix
Q(\zeta)
defined in (
1.5
) is invertible for all
\zeta\in\partial\Delta
. The submanifold
M_{H}
is said to be
admissible
if it admits an admissible pair
(c,V)
.

For real hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, this condition already appears in
[
8
]
and is crucial in the construction of generalized stationary discs and their application in the jet determination problem. We note that this property is invariant under holomorphic changes of coordinates that preserve the canonical form (
1.2
) since they are linear changes with the
properties given by Proposition 9.2.6 in
[
2
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

The two notions of admissibility and strong Levi nondegeneracy coincide in the case of a quadric model. Indeed,
in such a case, we have
Q(\zeta)=\zeta\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}A\ell
, where
A_{\ell},\ell=1,\ldots,d
is given by (
1.3
). So, the map
Q(\zeta)
is invertible for all
\zeta\in\partial\Delta

if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{d}cjAj
is invertible. Note also that we have
S_{\overline{ij}}\equiv 0
for all
i,j=1\ldots,n
.

The following example is due to Bertrand and Della Sala.

Example 1.3
.

The hypersurface

\left\{\Re ew=|z|^{4}+2t\Re e(z^{3}\overline{z})\right\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}

is admissible whenever
0<t<2/3
and is not admissible for
t\geq 2/3
.

Example 1.4
.

As already pointed out in the introduction, if
M_{H}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
is of the form (
1.2
) with
P_{1}(z,\overline{z})=\,^{t}\overline{z}A_{1}z
where
A_{1}
is Hermitian invertible, and any
P_{2},\ldots,P_{d}
(possibly zeros),
then
M_{H}
is admissible; the pair
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})=(1,0,\ldots,0)
and any
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
being admissible.

Example 1.5
.

The model submanifold
M_{H}\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}
given by

P_{1}(z,\overline{z})=|z_{1}|^{D_{1}}+|z_{2}|^{D_{1}}\ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ P_{2}(z,\overline{z})=|z_{1}|^{D_{2}}+|z_{2}|^{D_{2}}

is admissible. Indeed, we have

Q_{1\overline{1}}=Q_{2\overline{2}}=c_{1}(D_{1}/2)^{2}(-1)^{D_{1}/2-1}\zeta^{k_{0}+D_{1}/2-1}+c_{2}(D_{2}/2)^{2}(-1)^{D_{2}/2-1}(1-\overline{\zeta})^{D_{2}-D_{1}}\zeta^{k_{0}+D_{2}/2-1}

and

Q(\zeta)=\left(\begin{matrix}Q_{1\overline{1}}(\zeta)&0\\
0&Q_{2\overline{2}}(\zeta)\\
\end{matrix}\right)

For
(c_{1},c_{2})=(1,0)
and
V=(1,1)
then
Q(\zeta)
is invertible for all
\zeta\in\partial\Delta
.
Note, however, that for the pair
(c_{1},c_{2})=(0,1)
and
V=(1,1)
then
Q(\zeta)
is noninvertible at
\zeta=1
.

Example 1.6
.

Consider a decoupled model
M_{H}
of the form (
1.2
) with

P_{1}(z_{1},\overline{z_{1}})=\sum_{j=D_{1}-k_{1}}^{k_{1}}\alpha_{j}z_{1}^{j}\overline{z_{1}}^{D_{1}-j},\ \ P_{2}(z_{2},\overline{z_{2}})=\sum_{j=D_{2}-k_{2}}^{k_{2}}\beta_{j}z_{2}^{j}\overline{z_{2}}^{D_{2}-j}.

In that case, unless
D_{1}=D_{2}
, no such model is admissible. Indeed, if
D_{1}<D_{2}
, then for any
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
V\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, the matrix
Q(\zeta)
is equal to

\left(\begin{matrix}c_{1}\sum_{j=D_{1}-k_{1}}^{k_{1}}\tilde{\alpha_{j}}\zeta^{k_{0}+j-1}V_{1}^{j-1}\overline{V_{1}}^{D_{1}-j-1}&0\\
0&c_{2}(1-\zeta)^{D_{2}-D_{1}}\sum_{j=D_{2}-k_{2}}^{k_{2}}\tilde{\beta_{j}}\zeta^{k_{0}+j-1}V_{2}^{j-1}\overline{V_{2}}^{D_{2}-j-1}\\
\end{matrix}\right)

and is singular at
\zeta=1
. Although the methods in the present paper do not apply to such manifolds, we emphasize that these are treated in
[
1
]
. In case
D_{1}=D_{2}
, then
M_{H}
is admissible if and only if each of the two hypersurfaces
\{\Re ew_{1}=P_{1}(z_{1},\overline{z_{1}})\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}_{(z_{1},w_{1})}
and

\{\Re ew_{2}=P_{2}(z_{2},\overline{z_{2}})\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}_{(z_{2},w_{2})}
is admissible.

2.
Generalized stationary discs and the Riemann-Hilbert Problem

2.1.
Function spaces

In this section, we introduce the functional spaces we need in our context.
For an integer
k\geq 0
and a real number
0<\alpha<1
, we set
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}=\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}(\partial\Delta,\mathbb{R})
to be the space of real-valued functions defined on
\partial\Delta
of class

\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}
. This space is equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|f^{(j)}\|_{\infty}+\underset{\zeta\not=\eta\in\partial\Delta}{\mathrm{sup}}\frac{\|f^{(k)}(\zeta)-f^{(k)}(\eta)\|}{|\zeta-\eta|^{\alpha}},

where
\|f^{(j)}\|_{\infty}=\underset{\partial\Delta}{\mathrm{max}}\|f^{(j)}\|
.
We write
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{k,\alpha}=\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}+i\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}
and equip this space with the norm

(2.1)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{k,\alpha}}=\|\Re ef\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}}+\|\Im mf\|_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}}.

The subspace of
analytic discs
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{k,\alpha}
is denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}
and consists of functions defined on
\overline{\Delta}
, holomorphic on
\Delta
and such that their restriction on

\partial\Delta
is in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{k,\alpha}
.

In this paper, we work with analytic discs that enjoy pointwise constraints in the sense that they vanish at
\zeta=1
with a given order. For this reason, we introduce adapted functional spaces. Let
m\geq 0
be an integer. We define
\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}_{0^{m}}

as the subspace of

\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{k,\alpha}
of functions that can be written as
(1-\zeta)^{m}f
, with
f\in\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}
, endowed with the norm

(2.2)

\|(1-\zeta)^{m}f\|_{\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}_{0^{m}}}={\|f\|}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{k,\alpha}},

Finally, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{0^{m}}^{k,\alpha}
the subspace of
\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}
of functions which can be written as
(1-\zeta)^{m}v
with
v\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{k,\alpha}
, and equip this space with the norm

\|(1-\zeta)^{m}f\|_{\mathcal{C}_{0^{m}}^{k,\alpha}}={\|f\|}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}^{k,\alpha}}.

We point out that both
\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}_{0^{m}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{0^{m}}^{k,\alpha}
are Banach spaces. Finally, when
m=1
, we simply write
\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}_{0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{k,\alpha}
.

2.2.
Generalized stationary discs

Let
M=\{r=0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
be a smooth real submanifold of the form (
1.1
). An analytic disc
f

is
attached to
M
when
f(\partial\Delta)\subset M
. Following
[
6
,
8
]
, we define a higher order notion of stationary discs (see Lempert
[
21
]
and Tumanov
[
23
]
for the standard definition):

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k_{0}>0
be a positive integer. A holomorphic disc
f:\Delta\to\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
continuous up to
\partial\Delta
and attached to
M=\{r=0\}
is a
k_{0}
-stationary disc for
M
if there
exists a holomorphic lift
\bm{f}=(f,\tilde{f})
of
f
to the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
, continuous up to

\partial\Delta
and such that, for all
\zeta\in\partial\Delta
,
\bm{f}(\zeta)\in\mathcal{N}^{k_{0}}M(\zeta)
where

(2.3)

\mathcal{N}^{k_{0}}M(\zeta):=\left\{(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})\in T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+d}\ |\ (z,w)\in M,(\tilde{z},\tilde{w})\in\zeta^{k_{0}}N^{*}_{(z,w)}M\setminus\{0\}\right\},

where

N^{*}_{(z,w)}M=\spanc_{\mathbb{R}}\{\partial r_{1}(z,w),\ldots,\partial r_{d}(z,w)\}

is the conormal fiber at
(z,w)
of the submanifold
M
. Here

\partial r_{\ell}=\left(\partial_{z}r_{\ell},\partial_{w}r_{\ell}\right)
for
\ell=1,\ldots,d.

The map
\bm{f}=(f,\tilde{f})
is called a
k_{0}
-stationary lift for
M
and we denote by
\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}(M)
the set of these lifts with
f
nonconstant.

When we do not specify
k_{0}
, we refer to these discs as
generalized stationary discs
.
We note that an analytic disc
f
is
k_{0}
-stationary for
M
if and only if there exist
d
real valued functions
c_{1},\ldots,c_{d}:\partial\Delta\to\mathbb{R}
with
\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}(\zeta)\partial r_{\ell}(0)\neq 0
for all
\zeta\in\partial\Delta
and such that the map

\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{k_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}(\zeta)\partial r_{\ell}\left(f(\zeta),\overline{f(\zeta)}\right)

defined on
\partial\Delta
extends holomorphically on
\Delta
. We also point out that the set of small
k_{0}
-stationary discs is invariant under CR automorphisms (see
[
5
]
or Proposition 2.3
[
8
]
).
In the next example, we show that one can always construct a basic family of
k_{0}
-stationary discs for
any

M_{H}
(
1.2
).

Example 2.2
.

Consider a model submanifold
M_{H}=\{\rho=0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
of the form (
1.2
).
We first note that

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial\rho_{1}=(\partial_{z}\rho_{1},\partial_{w}\rho_{1})=\left(-P_{1,z_{1}}(z,\overline{z}),\ldots,-P_{1,z_{n}}(z,\overline{z}),\frac{1}{2},0,\ldots,0\right)\\
\hskip 113.81102pt\vdots\\
\displaystyle\partial\rho_{d}=(\partial_{z}\rho_{d},\partial_{w}\rho_{d})=\left(-P_{d,z_{1}}(z,\overline{z}),\ldots,-P_{d,z_{n}}(z,\overline{z}),0,\ldots,0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\end{cases}

Set, as in Subsection 1.1,
k_{0}:=\max\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\}
. It is easy to show that the disc

(2.4)

\bm{f_{0}}(\zeta)=(\underbrace{h_{0}(\zeta),g_{0}(\zeta)}_{f_{0}(\zeta)},\tilde{h_{0}}(\zeta),\tilde{g_{0}}(\zeta))=\left((1-\zeta)V,g_{0}(\zeta),\tilde{h_{0}}(\zeta),\frac{c_{1}}{2}\zeta^{k_{0}},\ldots,\frac{c_{d}}{2}\zeta^{k_{0}}\right)\\

where
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, is a
k_{0}
-stationary lift for
M_{H}
. The component
g_{0}
is classically determined by (
1.2
) by means of the Hilbert transform and

\tilde{h_{0}}(\zeta)=\zeta^{k_{0}}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{1}}\left((1-\zeta)V,(1-\overline{\zeta})\overline{V}\right),\ldots,\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{n}}\left((1-\zeta)V,(1-\overline{\zeta})\overline{V}\right)\right).

Although this example applies to nonadmissible models, it becomes much more relevant when
M_{H}
is admissible. Indeed,
our goal is to construct generalized stationary lifts by deforming both the model
M_{H}
and the initial lift
\bm{f_{0}}

(
2.4
). In this context, it is important to consider an admissible model
M_{H}
with an initial lift
\bm{f_{0}}
given by (
2.4
) where the parameters
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
form an admissible pair for
M_{H}
. We recall that, as opposed to the strongly Levi nondegenerate setting, the conormal bundle of a
degenerate model
M_{H}
is not totally real (see
[
26
,
23
]
) and thus, classical techniques to construct stationary lifts
(see e.g.
[
4
,
5
]
) do not apply any longer. The admissibility condition is crucial since it allows the reduction to a setting where the
initial lift
\bm{f_{0}}
is, up to a factorization by powers of
(1-\zeta)
, attached to a totally real submanifold of the cotangent bundle, and therefore can be
deformed to obtain nearby lifts.

2.3.
A Riemann-Hilbert type problem

In this subsection, we show how to describe the fibration
\mathcal{N}^{k_{0}}M(\zeta)
for a submanifold
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
of the form (
1.1
), with
2n+2d
real equations. Consider first a model submanifold
M_{H}=\{\rho=0\}
given by (
1.2
). In that case, we obtain explicit defining equations for the corresponding fibration. Indeed, we first point out that

\displaystyle(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})\in\mathcal{N}^{k_{0}}M_{H}(\zeta)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\rho_{1}(z,w,\overline{z},\overline{w})=...=\rho_{d}(z,w,\overline{z},\overline{w})=0\\
\\
\displaystyle\exists\ c_{\ell}:\partial\Delta\rightarrow\mathbb{R},(\tilde{z},\tilde{w})=\zeta^{k_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}(\zeta)\partial\rho_{\ell}\left(f(\zeta),\overline{f(\zeta)}\right)\end{array}\right.

Due to the form of
\rho
, we have

\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}(\zeta)\partial\rho_{\ell}(f(\zeta),\overline{f(\zeta)})=\left(-\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}(\zeta)P_{\ell,z_{1}}(z,\overline{z}),\ldots,-\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}(\zeta)P_{\ell,z_{n}}(z,\overline{z}),\frac{c_{1}(\zeta)}{2},\ldots,\frac{c_{d}(\zeta)}{2}\right)

and so

\displaystyle(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})\in\mathcal{N}^{k_{0}}M_{H}(\zeta)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\rho_{1}(z,w,\overline{z},\overline{w})=...=\rho_{d}(z,w,\overline{z},\overline{w})=0\\
\\
\tilde{z_{j}}=-2\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\tilde{w}_{\ell}(\zeta)P_{\ell,z_{j}}(z,\overline{z}),\ j=1\ldots,n\\
\\
\zeta^{-k_{0}}\tilde{w_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{-k_{0}}\tilde{w_{d}}\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

It then follows that the
2n+2d
defining equations of
\mathcal{N}^{k_{0}}M_{H}(\zeta)
are given by

\small\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\tilde{\rho}_{1}(\zeta)(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})&=&\Re ew_{1}-P_{1}(z,\overline{z})=0\\
&\vdots&\\
\tilde{\rho}_{d}(\zeta)(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})&=&\Re ew_{d}-P_{d}(z,\overline{z})=0\\
\\
\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{d+1}(\zeta)(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})&=&\left(\tilde{z}_{1}+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\tilde{w}_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{1}}(z,\overline{z})\right)+\left(\overline{\tilde{z}_{1}+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\tilde{w}_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{1}}(z,\overline{z})}\right)=0\\
\\
\tilde{\rho}_{d+2}(\zeta)(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})&=&i\left(\tilde{z}_{1}+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\tilde{w}_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{1}}(z,\overline{z})\right)-i\left(\overline{\tilde{z}_{1}+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\tilde{w}_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{1}}(z,\overline{z})}\right)=0\\
&\vdots&\\
\tilde{\rho}_{2n+d-1}(\zeta)(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})&=&\left(\tilde{z}_{n}+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\tilde{w}_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{n}}(z,\overline{z})\right)+\left(\overline{\tilde{z}_{n}+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\tilde{w}_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{n}}(z,\overline{z})}\right)=0\\
\\
\tilde{\rho}_{2n+d}(\zeta)(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})&=&i\left(\tilde{z}_{n}+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\tilde{w}_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{n}}(z,\overline{z})\right)-i\left(\overline{\tilde{z}_{n}+2\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\tilde{w}_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{n}}(z,\overline{z})}\right)=0\\
\\
\tilde{\rho}_{2n+d+1}(\zeta)(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})&=&\displaystyle i\zeta^{-k_{0}}\tilde{w}_{1}-i\zeta^{k_{0}}\overline{\tilde{w}_{1}}=0.\\
&\vdots&\\
\tilde{\rho}_{2n+2d}(\zeta)(z,w,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})&=&\displaystyle i\zeta^{-k_{0}}\tilde{w}_{d}-i\zeta^{k_{0}}\overline{\tilde{w}_{d}}=0.\\
\end{array}\right.

We set
\tilde{\rho}:=(\tilde{\rho}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\rho}_{2n+2d})
. For a general submanifold
M=\{r=0\}
of the form (
1.1
), we denote by
\tilde{r}
the corresponding defining functions of the fibration
\mathcal{N}^{k_{0}}M(\zeta)
.

The idea of describing the fibration
\mathcal{N}^{k_{0}}M(\zeta)
using real defining functions is convenient since it allows us to consider
k_{0}
-stationary lifts as solutions of a nonlinear
Riemann-Hilbert type problem. More precisely, an analytic disc
\bm{f}:\Delta\mapsto T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
is a
k_{0}
-stationary
lift for
M
if and only if it satisfies the following Riemann-Hilbert problem

(2.7)

\tilde{r}(\zeta)(\bm{f}(\zeta))=0\ \mbox{ for all }\zeta\in\partial\Delta.

The study of this problem and the structure of its solutions relies on the values of special integers called the
partial indices
and the
Maslov index
. We briefly recall these concepts.
Let
G:\partial\Delta\to Gl_{N}(\mathbb{C})
be a smooth map, where
Gl_{N}(\mathbb{C})
denotes the general linear group on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
We consider a Birkhoff factorization
(see Globevnik
[
15
]
or Vekua
[
25
]
) of
-\overline{G^{-1}}G
on
\partial\Delta
:

-\overline{G(\zeta)^{-1}}G(\zeta)=B^{+}(\zeta)\begin{pmatrix}\zeta^{\kappa_{1}}&&&(0)\\
&\zeta^{\kappa_{2}}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
(0)&&&\zeta^{\kappa_{N}}\end{pmatrix}B^{-}(\zeta),

where
\zeta\in\partial\Delta
,
B^{+}:\bar{\Delta}\to Gl_{N}(\mathbb{C})
and

B^{-}:(\mathbb{C}\cup\infty)\setminus\Delta\to Gl_{N}(\mathbb{C})

are smooth maps, holomorphic on
\Delta
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Delta}
respectively.
The integers
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N}
are the
partial indices
of

-\overline{G^{-1}}G
and the
Maslov index
of
-\overline{G^{-1}}G
is their sum

\kappa:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\kappa_{j}
. It is important to note (see
[
16
]
) that

\kappa
is also equal to the winding number at the origin of the map

\zeta\mapsto\det\left(-\overline{G(\zeta)^{-1}}G(\zeta)\right).

3.
Construction of generalized stationary discs and its consequences

3.1.
The main result

The construction of generalized stationary discs relies on the implicit function theorem with well adapted Banach spaces,
which we discuss now. Let
k\geq 0
be an integer
and let
0<\alpha<1
. We also consider a model
M_{H}=\{\rho=0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
of the form (
1.2
), and recall that
D_{1}\leq D_{\ell}\leq D_{d}
,
\ell=1,\ldots,d
and
k_{0}=\max\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\}
.

We introduce the following subspace of analytic discs with pointwise constraints

(3.1)

Y:=\left(\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}_{0}\right)^{n+d}\times\left(\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}_{0^{D_{1}-1}}\right)^{n}\times\left(\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}\right)^{d}

endowed with the product norm
\|\cdot\|
associated with (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). We denote, for any real submanifold
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
, the set of
k_{0}
-stationary lifts with pointwise constraints by

\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(M)=\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}(M)\cap Y.

We also define the space
X
of defining functions we work with. The definition of this space occurs already in previous works (see
[
1
,
6
,
8
]
) and is strongly related to the norm introduced on
Y
. We refer to the below definition of the map

F
(
3.4
) for more insight.
Furthermore, we note that in the Levi nondegenerate case,
X
coincides with all possible perturbations of the model
M_{H}
.
Choose
\delta>0
large enough to ensure that
f_{0}\left(\overline{\Delta}\right)
, where
f_{0}
is the disc defined in (
2.4
),
is contained in the polydisc
{\delta\Delta}^{n+d}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
.
We consider the affine Banach space
X
of functions
r\in\mathcal{C}^{k+3}\left(\delta\Delta^{n+d}\right)
, where
k
is the same as in the definition of
Y
, which can be written as

r(z,w)=\rho(z,w)+\theta(z,\Im mw)

with
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d})
of the form

(3.2)

\theta_{\ell}(z,\Im mw)=\sum_{|I|+|J|=D_{\ell}+1}z^{I}\overline{z}^{J}r_{\ell,IJ0}(z)+\sum_{|S|=1}^{D_{\ell}}\sum_{|I|+|J|=D_{\ell}-|S|}z^{I}\overline{z}^{J}(\Im mw)^{|S|}r_{\ell,IJS}(z,\Im mw)

where
r_{\ell,IJ0}\in\mathcal{C}^{k+3}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\delta\Delta^{n}\right)
and

r_{\ell,IJS}\in\mathcal{C}^{k+3}_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\delta\Delta^{n}\times(-\delta,\delta)^{d}\right)
. We equip
X
with the following norm

(3.3)

{\|r\|}_{X}=\sup{\|r_{\ell,IJS}\|}_{\mathcal{C}^{k+3}}.

We are now in a position to state and prove our main result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
M_{H}=\{\rho=0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
be a model submanifold of the form (
1.2
).
Consider an initial stationary lift
\bm{f_{0}}=\left(h_{0},g_{0},\tilde{h_{0}},\tilde{g_{0}}\right)\in\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(M_{H})
of the form (
2.4
) where
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
V\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
form an admissible pair for
M_{H}
. Then there exist open
neighborhoods
U
of
\rho
in
X
and
V
of
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
m={\rm ind}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}}Q\right)+d(2k_{0}+1)-2n(D_{1}-2)
, a real number

\varepsilon>0
, and a map of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}

\mathcal{F}:U\times V\to Y

such that:

i.

\mathcal{F}(\rho,0)=\bm{f_{0}}
,

ii.

for all
r\in U
, the map

\mathcal{F}(r,\cdot):V\to\left\{\bm{f}\in\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(\{r=0\})\ \ |\ \|\bm{f}-\bm{f_{0}}\|<\varepsilon\right\}

is one-to-one and onto.

In particular, for any defining function
r\in U
, the set

\left\{\bm{f}\in\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(\{r=0\})\ \ |\ \|\bm{f}-\bm{f_{0}}\|<\varepsilon\right\}

is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
real submanifold of dimension
{\rm ind}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}}Q\right)+d(2k_{0}+1)-2n(D_{1}-2)
of the Banach space of analytic discs
Y
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

In a neighborhood of
(\rho,\bm{f_{0}})
in

X\times Y
,
we define the following map

(3.4)

F:X\times Y\to\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{k,\alpha}\right)^{d}\times\left(\mathcal{C}_{0^{D_{1}-1}}^{k,\alpha}\right)^{2n}\times\left(\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}\right)^{d}

by

F(r,\bm{f}):=\tilde{r}(\bm{f})

in the sense of (
2.7
).
We emphasize that the norms defined on the spaces
Y
and on the target space of
F
are natural and well
adapted to the study of analytic discs with pointwise constraints (see e.g.
[
7
]
). Consequently, the space
X
and its
corresponding norm (
3.3
) have been constructed to ensure that the map
F
is well defined, in the sense that
\tilde{r}(\bm{f})
belongs to the target space in (
3.4
), and is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}

(see Lemma 5.1
[
17
]
, Lemma 6.1 and Lemma 11.2
[
15
]
, Lemma 3.3
[
6
]
).
For any fixed defining function
r
,
the zero set of
F(r,\cdot)
coincides with

\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(\{r=0\})
. Then the proof of Theorem
3.1
relies on the implicit function theorem applied to the map
F
(see e.g. p.39
[
16
]
).
Let
\bm{f}\mapsto\partial_{2}F(\rho,\bm{f_{0}})\bm{f}
be the partial derivative of
F
with respect to the Banach space

Y
at

(\rho,\bm{f_{0}})
. We have

\partial_{2}F(\rho,\bm{f_{0}})\bm{f}=2\Re e\left[\overline{G(\zeta)}\bm{f}\right]

where
G(\zeta)
is the following square matrix of size
2n+2d

G(\zeta):=\left({\tilde{\rho}}_{\overline{z}}(\bm{f_{0}}),{\tilde{\rho}}_{\overline{w}}(\bm{f_{0}}),{\tilde{\rho}}_{\overline{\tilde{z}}}(\bm{f_{0}}),{\tilde{\rho}}_{\overline{\tilde{w}}}(\bm{f_{0}})\right).

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we need to show the following two properties of
\partial_{2}F(\rho,\bm{f_{0}})

i.

The map
\partial_{2}F(\rho,\bm{f_{0}}):Y\to\left(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{k,\alpha}\right)^{d}\times\left(\mathcal{C}_{0^{D_{1}-1}}^{k,\alpha}\right)^{2n}\times\left(\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}\right)^{d}

The kernel of
\partial_{2}F(\rho,\bm{f_{0}})
is of real dimension
{\rm ind}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}}Q\right)+d(2k_{0}+1)-2n(D_{1}-2)
.

We first focus on i.
It is more convenient to reorder the given coordinates as
(w,z,\tilde{z},\tilde{w})
. In this context, discs
\bm{f}
are of the form
(g,h,\tilde{h},\tilde{g})
. We still denote by
G(\zeta)
the corresponding reordered matrix, that is,

G(\zeta):=\left({\tilde{\rho}}_{\overline{w}}(\bm{f_{0}}),{\tilde{\rho}}_{\overline{z}}(\bm{f_{0}}),{\tilde{\rho}}_{\overline{\tilde{z}}}(\bm{f_{0}}),{\tilde{\rho}}_{\overline{\tilde{w}}}(\bm{f_{0}})\right).

It turns out that this matrix is block upper triangular

(3.5)

G(\zeta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccc}\frac{1}{2}I_{d}&&(*)\\
&G_{2}(\zeta)&\\
(0)&&-i\zeta^{k_{0}}I_{d}\\
\end{array}\right),

where

is the
d\times d

is the square matrix of size
2n
given by

(3.6)

\footnotesize\left(\begin{matrix}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}(\zeta^{k_{0}}c_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{1}\overline{z_{1}}}+\overline{\zeta}^{k_{0}}c_{\ell}P_{\ell,\overline{z_{1}}\overline{z_{1}}})&\ldots&\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}(\zeta^{k_{0}}c_{\ell}P_{\ell,z_{1}\overline{z_{n}}}+\overline{\zeta}^{k_{0}}c_{\ell}P_{\ell,\overline{z_{1}}\overline{z_{n}}})&1&\ldots&0\\

A^{\prime}(\zeta)
into

-(2i)^{n}\left(\begin{matrix}C_{2,1;1}&Q_{1\overline{1}}\overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}&C_{3,1;1}&Q_{1\overline{2}}\overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}&\cdots&C_{n+1,1;1}&Q_{1\overline{n}}\overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}\\
c_{1,1;1}&0&c_{2,1;1}&0&\cdots&c_{n,1;1}&0\\
C_{2,1;2}&Q_{2\overline{1}}\overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}&C_{3,1;2}&Q_{2\overline{2}}\overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}&\cdots&C_{n+1,1;2}&Q_{2\overline{n}}\overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}\\
-c_{1,1;2}&0&-c_{2,1;2}&0&\cdots&-c_{n,1;2}&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
C_{2,1;n}&Q_{n\overline{1}}\overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}&C_{3,1;n}&Q_{n\overline{2}}\overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}&\cdots&C_{n+1,1;n}&Q_{n\overline{n}}\overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}\\
\frac{c_{1,1;n}}{(-1)^{n+1}}&0&\frac{c_{2,1;n}}{(-1)^{n+1}}&0&\cdots&\frac{c_{n,1;n}}{(-1)^{n+1}}&0\\
\end{matrix}\right).

The surjectivity of
\widetilde{L_{2}}
, and thus the one of
L_{2}
, is then a consequence of Theorem 2.1
[
7
]
and of the following lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

The partial indices of
\overline{\widetilde{G_{2}}^{-1}}\widetilde{G_{2}}
, as defined in Subsection
2.3
, are greater than or equal to
D_{1}-1\geq D_{1}-2.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Denote by
\kappa_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\kappa_{2n}
the ordered partial indices of
\overline{\widetilde{G_{2}}^{-1}}\widetilde{G_{2}}
, and let
\Lambda(\cdot)
be the diagonal matrix map with entries
\zeta^{\kappa_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{\kappa_{2n}}
. We know from Lemma 5.1
[
15
]
that there exists a smooth map

\Theta:\overline{\Delta}\to GL_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
, holomorphic on
\Delta
, such that

(3.9)

-\Theta\overline{\widetilde{G_{2}}^{-1}}\widetilde{G_{2}}=\Lambda\overline{\Theta}.

Let
l=(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n},\mu_{n})
be the last row of the matrix
\Theta
. We get the following system of
2n
equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{j+1,1;k}\lambda_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}c_{j,1;k}\mu_{k}&=&-\overline{\rm det(Q)}\zeta^{\kappa_{2n}}\overline{\lambda}_{j}\\
\\
\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{2k-1,2j}\lambda_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{1,k+1}^{j}\mu_{k}&=&-\overline{\rm det(Q)}\zeta^{\kappa_{2n}}\overline{\mu}_{j}\\
\end{array}\right.

with
j=1,\ldots,n
.
Performing the column operations (
3.8
) on that system and focusing
only
on even rows gives

\left\{\begin{aligned} \overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q_{k\overline{1}}\lambda_{k}&=-\overline{\rm det(Q(\zeta))}\zeta^{\kappa_{2n}}\left(\overline{\mu}_{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{S}_{\bar{k}\bar{1}}\overline{\lambda}_{k}\right)\\
&\vdots&\\
\overline{\rm{det}(Q(\zeta))}\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q_{k\overline{n}}\lambda_{k}&=-\overline{\rm det(Q(\zeta))}\zeta^{\kappa_{2n}}\left(\overline{\mu}_{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{S}_{\bar{k}\bar{n}}\overline{\lambda}_{k}\right)\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Recall that by assumption
Q(\zeta)
is invertible for all
\zeta\in\partial\Delta
, and thus dividing by
\zeta^{\kappa_{2n}}\overline{{\rm det}(Q(\zeta))}
, leads to

(3.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \overline{\zeta}^{\kappa_{2n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q_{k\overline{1}}\lambda_{k}&=-\overline{\mu}_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{S}_{\bar{k}\bar{1}}\overline{\lambda}_{k}\\
&\vdots&\\
\overline{\zeta}^{\kappa_{2n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q_{k\overline{n}}\lambda_{k}&=-\overline{\mu}_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{S}_{\bar{k}\bar{n}}\overline{\lambda}_{k}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Assume by contradiction that
\kappa_{2n}\leq D_{1}-2
. Then the right hand side of each of the above equations is antiholomorphic since
S_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}
are antiholomorphic polynomials, while the left hand side is holomorphic since
Q_{i\overline{j}}
are holomorphic polynomials divisible by
\zeta^{D_{1}-1}
. They must then both vanish, leading to

Q\left(\begin{matrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{n}\end{matrix}\right)=0

which implies that
\lambda_{j}
,
j=1\ldots,n
, is identically equal to zero. This implies directly from (
3.10
) that
\mu_{j}
,
j=1\ldots,n
also vanishes identically, contradicting the invertibility of the matrix
\Theta
.
∎

This proves i.

Finally, it remains to prove ii., that is, to find the dimension of the kernel of
\partial_{2}F(\rho,\bm{f_{0}})
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The real dimension of the kernel of
\partial_{2}F(\rho,\bm{f_{0}})
is equal to
{\rm ind}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}}Q\right)+d(2k_{0}+1)-2n(D_{1}-2)
.

Proof.

The proof relies essentially on Theorem 2.4
[
7
]
. Recall that the matrix
G
is of the form (
3.5
) with
G_{2}
given by (
3.6
). We write

(3.11)

G(\zeta)=\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccc}\frac{1}{2}I_{d}&&(*)\\
&G_{2}(\zeta)&\\
(0)&&-i\zeta^{k_{0}}I_{d}\\
\end{array}\right)}_{\tilde{G}(\zeta)}\times\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccc}I_{d}&&(0)\\
&D(\zeta)&\\
(0)&&I_{d}\\
\end{array}\right)}_{\tilde{D}(\zeta)}

and we note that the kernel of the operators

\bm{f}\mapsto\partial_{2}F(\rho,\bm{f_{0}})\bm{f}=2\Re e\left[\overline{G(\zeta)}\bm{f}\right]

and

\left(\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}_{0}\right)^{d}\times\left(\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}_{0^{D_{1}-1}}\right)^{2n}\times\left(\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha}\right)^{d}\ni\bm{f}\mapsto 2\Re e\left[\overline{\tilde{G}(\zeta)}\bm{f}\right]

are of the same dimension.
Now, using the same notation as in Theorem 2.4
[
7
]
, we have
r=3
,
m_{1}=1
and
N_{1}=d
,
m_{2}=D_{1}-1
and
N_{2}=2n
, and

m_{3}=0
and
N_{3}=d
. Recall that the Maslov index
\kappa
is given by the winding number at
0
of the function

\zeta\mapsto\det\left(-\overline{\tilde{G}(\zeta)^{-1}}\tilde{G}(\zeta)\right)
.
Since
\det(\widetilde{G_{2}})=(2i)^{n}\det(Q)
, we directly obtain

\kappa=0+{\rm ind}(Q)+2dk_{0}={\rm ind}(Q)+2dk_{0}.

Thus, the dimension of the kernel of
\partial_{2}F(\rho,\bm{f_{0}})
is equal to

\displaystyle\kappa+2n+2d-\sum_{j=1}^{3}N_{j}m_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm ind}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}}Q\right)+2dk_{0}+2n+2d-d-2n(D_{1}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm ind}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}}Q\right)+d(2k_{0}+1)-2n(D_{1}-2).

∎

This concludes the proof of the theorem.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

In the paper
[
5
]
, the authors impose a pointwise constraint on the
\tilde{g}
component of the lift, namely
\tilde{g}_{\ell}(1)=c_{\ell}/2
,
\ell=1,\ldots,d
. Imposing such a condition in the present setting would imply that the real dimension of the corresponding manifold of stationary lifts is
{\rm ind}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}}Q\right)+2k_{0}d-2n(D_{1}-2)
. This follows from the fact that the integer
m_{3}
in the proof of the above Lemma
3.3
, would be equal to
1
. In view of Remark
1.2
, if the model
M_{H}
is strongly Levi nondegenerate, then
D_{1}=2
,
k_{0}=1
and
Q(\zeta)=\zeta\sum c_{j}A_{j}
, which implies
{\rm ind}\left(-\overline{Q^{-1}}Q\right)=2n
and allows us to recover the dimension stated in Theorem 3.1
[
5
]
.

3.2.
Finite jet determination of generalized stationary lifts and open questions

Let
M_{H}=\{\rho=0\}
be an admissible model.
For an integer
\ell_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, where
k
is large enough, we consider the linear
\ell_{0}
-jet map at
\zeta=1
defined on

(\mathcal{A}^{k,\alpha})^{2(n+d)}
by

\mathfrak{j}_{\ell_{0}}({\bm{f}})=\left({\bm{f}}(1),{\bm{f}}^{\prime}(1),\ldots,{\bm{f}}^{(\ell_{0})}(1)\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{(n+d)(\ell_{0}+1)}

We prove the following jet determination result for stationary lifts.

Proposition 3.5
.

Consider a stationary lift
{\bm{f}_{0}}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}^{k_{0}}(M_{H})
of the form (
2.4
) where
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})
and
V
form an admissible pair for
M_{H}
. Then there exist an open neighborhood
U
of
\rho
in
X
,
\varepsilon>0
, and an integer
\ell_{0}\geq 1
such that for any
r\in U
the jet map

\mathfrak{j}_{\ell_{0}}
is injective on the submanifold
\{\bm{f}\in\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(\{r=0\})\ \ |\ \|\bm{f}-\bm{f_{0}}\|<\varepsilon\}
.

Note that in the following proof we use the same notation as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. We also point out that the outline of the proof is now relatively standard and similar to the proofs of Lemma 5.3
[
8
]
and Proposition 3.8
[
9
]
.

Proof.

We set
{\ell_{0}}:={\rm max}\{2k_{0},\kappa_{2n}\}
where
\kappa_{2n}
is the largest partial index of
-\overline{\widetilde{G_{2}}^{-1}}\widetilde{G_{2}}
. Since
k
is large enough, the restriction of the map
\mathfrak{j}_{\ell_{0}}
to the submanifold
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{k_{0}}(M_{H})
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
. We need to show that its differential

\mathfrak{j}_{\ell_{0}}:T_{\bm{f}_{0}}\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(M_{H})\to\mathbb{C}^{(n+d)(\ell_{0}+1)}

is injective. Note that we have
T_{\bm{f}_{0}}\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(M_{H})={\rm ker}\left(2\Re e\left[\overline{G}\cdot\right]\right)
. Let
{\bm{f}}\in T_{\bm{f}_{0}}\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(M_{H})
with a trivial
\ell_{0}
-jet, that is,
\mathfrak{j}_{\ell_{0}}(\bm{f})=0
. This disc satisfies the following system of
2n+2d
equations

(3.12)

\overline{G}{\bm{f}}+G\overline{\bm{f}}=0.

We first note that the last
d
equations of (
3.12
) give

(3.13)

i\overline{\zeta^{k_{0}}}\tilde{g}(\zeta)-i\zeta^{k_{0}}\overline{\tilde{g}(\zeta)}=0,

which directly implies

\tilde{g}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{0}-1}a_{\ell}\zeta^{\ell}+a_{k_{0}}\zeta^{k_{0}}+\sum_{\ell=k_{0}+1}^{2k_{0}}\overline{a_{2k_{0}-\ell}}\zeta^{\ell}

with
a_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
a_{k_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus,
\tilde{g}
is determined by its
2k_{0}
-jet and so
\tilde{g}\equiv 0
.
We now focus on the previous
2n
equations in (
3.12
) which, using the factorization (
3.7
), are of the form

\overline{\widetilde{G_{2}}(\zeta)\tilde{D}(\zeta)}\underbrace{\left(\begin{matrix}h_{1}\\
\tilde{h}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
h_{n}\\
\tilde{h}_{n}\\
\end{matrix}\right)}_{\bm{h}}+\widetilde{G_{2}}(\zeta)\tilde{D}(\zeta)\left(\begin{matrix}\overline{h_{1}}\\
\overline{\tilde{h}_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\overline{h_{n}}\\
\overline{\tilde{h}_{n}}\\
\end{matrix}\right)=0.

Using (
3.9
), we write, permuting the components of
\overline{\tilde{D}}{\bm{h}}
if necessary,

\Theta\overline{\tilde{D}}{\bm{h}}=-\Lambda\overline{\Theta\overline{\tilde{D}}{\bm{h}}}.

It follows that the disc
\Theta\overline{\tilde{D}}{\bm{h}}
satisfies equations similar to (
3.13
) and is then determined by its jet of order
\kappa_{2n}
. We note that since
{\bm{h}}
has a trivial
\ell_{0}
-jet at
\zeta=1
, the same applies to
\Theta\overline{\tilde{D}}{\bm{h}}
. It follows that
\Theta\overline{\tilde{D}}{\bm{h}}\equiv 0
and so
{\bm{h}}\equiv 0
since
\Theta\overline{\tilde{D}}
is an isomorphism.
Finally the first
d
equations of (
3.12
) imply directly that
g\equiv 0
. This achieves the proof.
∎

We end the paper with three open questions and directions related to the jet determination of CR mappings. In
[
5
]
and
[
10
]
, the authors obtain a
filling property
that allows us to conclude that
2
-jet determination for sufficiently smooth
CR automorphisms between Levi nondegenerate submanifolds holds. We observe that, in the hypersurface case, the filling property is automatically satisfied for Levi nondegenerate ones, as well as for polynomial models (see
[
4
]
and
Lemma 5.4
[
8
]
), leading to finite jet determination. At the moment, it is not clear whether the discs in the submanifold

\left\{\bm{f}\in\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(\{r=0\})\ \ |\ \|\bm{f}-\bm{f_{0}}\|<\varepsilon\right\}
constructed in Theorem
3.1
enjoy any filling property. For a general overview of the finite jet determination problem, the reader is invited to consult the survey given in
[
19
]
.

Question 1.
In
[
9
]
, we proved that the
1
-jet determination for stationary lifts for a nondegenerate submanifold
M
leads to a filling property in the conormal bundle
N^{*}M
in
T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
. In our context, we establish in Proposition
3.5
a higher order jet determination for stationary lifts and it would
be interesting to understand whether this implies any filling property in the conormal bundle under additional assumptions on
M
.

Question 2.
Consider for a submanifold
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
given by (
1.1
) the evaluation map

\Psi:\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(M)\to\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
defined by

(3.14)

\Psi(\bm{f})=f(0).

In case the model
M_{H}
is strongly Levi nondegenerate with
A:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}c_{\ell}A_{\ell}
invertible and
k_{0}=1
, the differential
d_{\bm{f}_{0}}\Psi:T_{\bm{f}_{0}}\mathcal{S}^{1}_{0}(M_{H})\to\mathbb{C}^{n+d}
at
{\bm{f}_{0}}
given by (
2.4
), is onto if and only if the matrix
\,{}^{t}\overline{D}A^{-1}D
, where
D
is the
n\times d
matrix whose
\ell^{th}
column is
A_{\ell}V
, is invertible (see
[
5
,
10
]
).
The question is then to find an analogous object in the degenerate setting. To this end, we focus once more on the strongly Levi nondegenerate case. We have already observed that
Q(\zeta)=\zeta A
in Remark
1.2
, and we note that
D
is contained in the matrix
G
(
3.5
). Indeed,
G
can be further detailed

(3.15)

G(\zeta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccccc}\frac{1}{2}I_{d}&(1-\zeta)\,^{t}D&0&0\\
0&\zeta\,^{t}A&I_{n}&2(1-\zeta)D\\
0&i\zeta\,^{t}A&-iI_{n}&-2i(1-\zeta)D\\
0&0&0&-i\zeta I_{d}\\
\end{array}\right),

The matrix
D
is essential in the description of the tangent space
T_{\bm{f}_{0}}\mathcal{S}^{1}_{0}(M_{H})={\rm ker}\left(2\Re e[\overline{G}\cdot]\right)
,
which is a key step in establishing the surjectivity of
d_{\bm{f}_{0}}\Psi
. In the degenerate setting, the transpose of the matrix

\left(\begin{matrix}-P_{1,\overline{z_{1}}}\left((1-\zeta)V,(1-\overline{\zeta})\overline{V}\right)&\ldots&-P_{1,\overline{z_{n}}}\left((1-\zeta)V,(1-\overline{\zeta})\overline{V}\right)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-P_{d,\overline{z_{1}}}\left((1-\zeta)V,(1-\overline{\zeta})\overline{V}\right)&0&-P_{d,\overline{z_{n}}}\left((1-\zeta)V,(1-\overline{\zeta})\overline{V}\right)\\
\end{matrix}\right)

is a solid candidate for being an analogous to
D
, up to suitable powers of
(1-\zeta)
. In this vein, it would be interesting to first study the case where the polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{d}
share the same degree. Yet, at this stage, it seems that we are still far away from a complete description of
the tangent space
T_{\bm{f}_{0}}\mathcal{S}^{k_{0}}_{0}(M_{H})
. This is in part due to the fact that not much is known about the matrix
\Theta(\zeta)
in the factorization (
3.9
), as opposed to the nondegenerate setting where

\Theta
is explicity found and turns out to be constant (see Equation
(2.12)
in
[
5
]
).

Question 3.
As observed in
[
9
,
23
,
24
]
, nondefective stationary discs play an important role in the
jet determination of CR mappings of strongly Levi nondegenerate
submanifolds. These discs are particularly relevant in the theory of wedge extension of CR maps since they enjoy an
important filling property

[
2
,
3
,
22
]
.
The nondefectivity of a stationary disc of the form (
2.4
) is algebraically characterized in
[
9
]
, again by

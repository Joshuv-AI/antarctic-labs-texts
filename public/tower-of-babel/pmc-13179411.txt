Bio-inspired green combustion synthesis of CaTiO3 nanoparticles via Aloe vera extract for synergetic photocatalytic degradation of Malachite green and electrochemical sensing of glyphosate
Authors: Syed Khasim, Taymour A. Hamdalla, Nawal Alghamdi, Suhair A. Bani-Atta, Mansour Alatawi, Chellasamy Panneerselvam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A green combustion route was developed using Aloe vera extract for the synthesis of highly crystalline Calcium Titanate (CaTiO3) (CTO) nanoparticles (~ 30 nm). The optimized CaTiO3 NPs exhibits a direct optical band gap of 3.12 eV and abundant surface oxygen vacancies, enhancing charge separation and light absorption. As a photocatalyst, the material achieved a remarkable degradation efficiency of 90.44% for Malachite green (MG) dye under UV irradiation and 57.88% under sunlight within 120 min, following pseudo-first-order kinetics. Scavenger studies confirmed that photogenerated holes (h+) and hydroxyl radicals (·OH) play dominant roles in the degradation process. Furthermore, the Aloe vera–derived CaTiO3 demonstrated superior electrochemical activity toward Glyphosate detection, showing rapid amperometric response, low charge-transfer resistance, and excellent linearity (R2 > 0.96) across 1–6 µM concentration with LOD 0.38 µM and sensitivity ~ 0.25 µA µM–1. These results demonstrate the potential of Aloe vera–assisted CaTiO3 as a sustainable multifunctional nanomaterial that integrates environmental remediation and agrochemical sensing, offering a scalable and eco-friendly platform for next-generation photocatalysts and electrochemical sensors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11671-026-04641-3.

Introduction

Water pollution caused by industrial dyes and agricultural chemicals offers a serious threat to environmental and human health. Among these pollutants, synthetic dyes such as malachite green (MG) and herbicides like glyphosate are of particular concern due to their toxicity, persistence, and widespread use [1, 2]. The development of multifunctional materials capable of simultaneously addressing dye degradation and chemical sensing is therefore of significant interest.

In the combustion synthesis of metal oxide nanoparticles, the choice of fuel plays a critical role in determining the structural, morphological, and functional properties of the final product [3, 4]. Traditional fuels such as urea, glycine, and citric acid have been widely used due to their ability to act as reducing agents and generate high combustion temperatures. However, these fuels often release toxic gases and involve non-renewable components. The sustainability of the Aloe vera-assisted combustion synthesis was evaluated by comparing basic metrics such as energy input, gas evolution, toxicity, and waste generation with conventional fuels including urea, glycine, and citric acid [5, 6]. Conventional fuels are typically nitrogen-rich and undergo highly exothermic combustion, releasing toxic gases such as NOx and NH3, which increase environmental and health risks. In contrast, Aloe vera extract, being a renewable biomass-derived fuel, promotes a relatively mild and controlled combustion process with reduced energy input and predominantly releases benign gases such as CO2 and water vapor.

Furthermore, the use of Aloe vera eliminates the need for additional complexing agents or solvents, thereby reducing chemical consumption and lowering the effective E-factor. These features collectively demonstrate that Aloe vera–assisted synthesis offers clear sustainability advantages over conventional combustion fuels, supporting its suitability for green and environmentally benign nanoparticle synthesis. These biomolecules not only serve as effective combustion agents but also aid in controlling particle growth, enhancing crystallinity, and introducing beneficial surface defects such as oxygen vacancies [7]. Compared to synthetic fuels, Aloe vera promotes a more uniform combustion reaction, resulting in better particle dispersion and functional properties, while also aligning with green chemistry principles. Thus, the use of Aloe vera gel not only reduces environmental impact but also enhances the quality and applicability of nanoparticles for photocatalytic and sensing applications. Nanomaterials play a crucial role in treatment of waste water due to their unique physical, chemical, and electronic properties that differ significantly from their bulk counterparts [8, 9]. These characteristics make them highly effective in processes like water splitting, pollutant degradation, and energy conversion. Preparation of these nanomaterials by the green combustion method is an eco-friendly approach for synthesizing nanoparticles like calcium titanate (CaTiO3), it uses naturally derived fuels as Aloe vera gel and minimizes harmful chemical effects.

Calcium Titanate (CaTiO3) nanoparticles belong to the category of advanced material with a wide range of applications due to their unique physical and chemical properties. Its size range of 1–100 nm having perovskite- structure, with pale yellow color in powdered form. Calcium Titanate (CaTiO3) nanoparticles are highly stable under normal conditions [10–12]. Tunable bandgap from (~ 3.0–3.5 eV), making it suitable for enabling and optimizing photocatalyst for desired light absorption and improving efficiency. There are many conventional analytical techniques used to treat the polluted water such as sedimentation, filtration, chlorination, ion exchange method and reverse osmosis among them photocatalytic degradation offers advantages such as strong oxidation power, no secondary pollution, benign reaction conditions and low cost. This approach shows great promise in the field of dye degradation [13–15].

Water and soil pollution is not only caused by the industrial dyes it also from the agricultural pesticides like glyphosate [16, 17]. The glyphosate is used as herbicide weed remover in crops. The detection of these toxic chemicals is very crucial due to the fact that it is easily soluble in water. Several techniques such as High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), Gas chromatography (GC), Mass spectrometry (MS), Colorimetric methods and Raman spectroscopy have been used for the sensing and quantification of glyphosate [18–20]. These spectroscopic techniques are expensive and require high-precision instruments that rely on complicated procedures with trained personal, time consuming and challenges due to cross-reactivity of certain interfering compounds. Among these reported methods, development of electrochemical sensors has received wide-ranging interest due to their noninvasive and nondestructive sensing ability, less quantity of sample requirement, and highly portable detection analysis [21]. In spite of many advantages of electrochemical sensors over other methods, however, there are challenges to overcome some issues such as low limit of detection, selectivity, repeatability, stability, and low cost.

In this work, CaTiO3 nanoparticles were prepared by green solution combustion method using Aloe vera as a fuel and were characterized by XRD, FTIR, DRS and SEM. The dual application of CaTiO3 nanoparticles were explored. The photocatalytic activity by illuminating UV radiation and sun light to investigate the photodegradation of water-soluble Malachite green under normal temperature& pressure. In addition, we report the electrochemical sensing application of the glyphosate. There are few reports exploring the Photocatalytical degradation of organic dyes using CaTiO3 nanoparticles in the past. But, the Aloe vera assisted synthesis of CaTiO3 nanoparticles as a dual platform towards photocatalytic degradation of MB dye and electrochemical sensing of Glyphosate were never reported in the past. The Photocatalytic and Glyphosate sensing performance of CaTiO3 NPs synthesized in the present study using Aloe vera as a bio fuel is compared with similar and other materials, and its novelty as a multifunctional material is highlighted in comparative tables (Table S2–S5) of supplementary information.

This work for the first time reports the integration of multifunctional features of CaTiO3 nanoparticles synthesized via Aloe vera extract as a fuel, that could open new technological avenues for environmental remediations.

Experimental

Chemicals used in preparation of CTO NPs

CaTiO3 nanoparticles (CTO NPs) were synthesized through solution combustion method using Aloe vera as a fuel. The chemicals used in this process included N-butyl titanate, nitric acid (HNO3), and calcium nitrate [Ca(NO3)2], procured from NICE Chemicals (P) Ltd.

Synthesis of CTO NPs using Aloe vera as a fuel

The titanium nitrate was first prepared using, hydrolyzing N-butyl titanate by adding minimum quantity of water in a petri dish, leading to the formation of titanyl hydroxide. Further addition of HNO3 resulted in the formation of titanyl nitrate.

The previous steps were followed to prepare titanyl nitrate, and is mixed with stoichiometric amounts of Ca(NO3)2 and added 20 ml of an Aloe vera extract solution with deionized water and the mixture was thoroughly mixed using a magnetic stirrer for 30 min at room temperature. The resulting stock mixture was then subjected to combustion in a muffle furnace at 500 ± 10 °C. Finally, the obtained product was calcined at 700 °C for 2 h and characterized using specific spectral techniques for structural confirmation. The steps involved in the synthesis of CTO NPs are illustrated in the flow diagram (Fig. 1).

Materials characterization

Crystal structure and phase formation were investigated using Shimadzu Powder X-Ray diffractometer (PXRD) in 2θ range 20–70 with Cu Kα radiation. The surface morphology of synthesized nanoparticles was analyzed through scanning electron microscope (SEM) (JOEL JSM 840 A) at an accelerating voltage of 20 kV using a tungsten filament. The chemical features and bond formation were studied through FT-IR in the range 4000–400 cm− 1 using Spectrum-1000 (Perkin Elmer) spectrophotometer. The UV–Visible absorption spectra of the nanoparticles was recorded using UV–Visible Spectrophotometer (Shimadzu UV 2600 UV–Visible). In-depth analysis of morphological features was investigated through Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) (Hitachi H-8100) (at an accelerating voltage of 200 kV, LaB6 filament). The Electrochemical performances were carried out by CHI608E potentiostat using 1 M KOH at various scan rates.

Photocatalytic measurements of organic dyes by CTO NPs

Under UV-light irradiation

Photocatalytic activity of CTO nanoparticles was examined by the degradation of Malachite green (MG) dye illuminated under UV light of mercury vapor lamp (UV light with 125 W). Firstly, the prepared 20 ppm of MG dye solution, using this 20 ppm standard MG dye solution in a glass container and add 0.02 gm of CTO nanoparticle as catalyst to prepare dye solution. Then, the prepared dye solution was placed under UV light irradiation with a magnetic stirrer for homogenously to activate the degradation process. For every regular interval of 15 min constitutively 5 ml of dye solution is reverted up to the 120 min. The reverted dye solution is subjected to centrifugation for 15 mints, followed by the examination using UV visible absorbance spectrometry [22, 23].

Under sun light irradiation

The procedure of dye degradation under sun light irradiation is as follows. The experiment is started in May month at the time of 12 pm because near the equator, the sun’s rays are more direct, resulting in higher intensity. Firstly, the 20 ppm of standard MG dye solution is taken in glass container and added 0.02 gm Aloe vera made CTO nanoparticle as catalyst to the dye solution and is placed in magnetic stirrer for homogenously activating the degradation process. For every regular interval of 15 min constitutively 5 ml of dye solution is reverted up to the 120 min.

Preparation of electrode for electrochemical studies

For cyclic voltammetry (CV) studies, the working electrodes were fabricated by mixing 0.025 g of CTO NPs with 0.475 g of graphite powder. To ensure proper binding of the materials, 3 drops of silicon oil were added to the mixture. The components were then thoroughly hand-mixed using a mortar and pestle for approximately 20 min, resulting in a homogeneous and well-blended electrode paste suitable for electrochemical testing [24].

Fig. 11Different concentration of Malachite green dye degradation under irradiation of UV light and sun light (a and b) using CTO nanoparticles

Different concentration of Malachite green dye degradation under irradiation of UV light and sun light (a and b) using CTO nanoparticles

As shown in Fig. 11a and b the decolorization activity of Malachite Green (MG) dye was investigated by varying the dye concentration from 10 to 30 ppm, while maintaining a constant catalyst concentration of 20 mg. The rate of photodegradation increased over the course of 120 min under both UV light and sunlight irradiation. At 10 ppm, the lower dye concentration limits the number of molecules reaching the catalyst surface, resulting in a reduced reaction rate. In contrast, at 20 ppm, the dye concentration allows sufficient light penetration through the solution, ensuring efficient activation of the catalyst and maximizing the generation of reactive species. However, at higher concentrations (30 ppm), light penetration is delayed due to increased opacity, and self-quenching of the dye molecules becomes more pronounced, reducing the overall degradation efficiency. The very high percentage of decay observed at 20 ppm in both UV light and sunlight irradiation is attributed to an optimal balance between dye concentration, catalyst efficiency, light penetration, and reaction kinetics [45]. These findings emphasize the significance of optimizing dye concentration to achieve maximum photocatalytic performance.

Three distinct scavengers silver nitrate (AgNO3), ethanol, and ethylenediamine tetra acetic acid (EDTA) were used to analyze their impact on dye degradation. For the CTO nanoparticles synthesized with Aloe vera as a fuel, the photo decolorization efficiency of MG dye was 81.49%, 56.28%, and 41.59% in the presence of AgNO3, ethanol, and EDTA, respectively. In comparison, the degradation efficiency without any scavenger was 90.44%. These results indicate that EDTA (a hole scavenger) significantly reduces dye degradation, confirming that photogenerated h+ (holes) play a major role in the decomposition of MG dye. Additionally, AgNO3, in which scavenges e– (electrons), and ethanol, in which scavenges ·OH radicals, also affected the degradation efficiency but to a lesser extent. CTO nanoparticles exhibit superior photocatalytic performance due to their higher degradation efficiency and better hole-driven oxidation activity. This suggests that using Aloe vera as a fuel enhances the photocatalyst’s ability to generate and sustain reactive species, making it more effective in dye degradation under UV light. The present study is limited to a fixed catalyst dosage and neutral pH conditions. The influence of catalyst loading and solution pH on photocatalytic performance was not explored and remains a scope for future work.

Conclusions

CTO nanoparticles were synthesized via solution combustion method using Aloe vera gel as a green and sustainable fuel exhibits remarkable structural, optical, photocatalytic, and electrochemical properties. The PXRD pattern confirms the formation of well-defined crystalline phases with a band gap of 3.12 eV, indicating good light absorption characteristics suitable for photocatalytic applications. The use of Aloe vera contributes to a higher density of oxygen vacancies and surface defects in the CTO structure, which significantly enhances its photocatalytic performance. This is evidenced by the decolorization efficiency of Malachite Green (MG), achieving efficiencies of 90.44% under UV irradiation and 57.88% under sunlight for a duration of 120 min. Scavenger tests reveal that photogenerated holes (h+) play a dominant role in the degradation mechanism, highlighting the effective charge separation and reactive species generation capabilities of the material. Electrochemical studies of CTO NPs such as Cyclic voltammetry (CV) indicate improved redox reversibility, while electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis reveals a lower charge transfer resistance (Rct) key indicator of superior interfacial charge transport. Moreover, the prepared CTO NPs demonstrate good sensitivity in glyphosate detection having linearity (R2 > 0.96) across 1–6 µM concentration with LOD 0.38 µM and sensitivity ~ 0.25 µA µM−1. as confirmed by linear electrochemical responses and rapid current-time profiles in amperometric and voltametric studies. Overall, the incorporation of Aloe vera gel as a bio-fuel not only supports eco-friendly synthesis but also enhances the functional performance of CTO NPs across photocatalytic, electrochemical, and sensing applications, making them promising candidates for environmental remediation and agrochemical monitoring.

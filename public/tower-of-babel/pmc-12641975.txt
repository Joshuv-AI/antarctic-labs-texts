Exploring the perspective of nurses on factors influencing patient safety practices in a paediatric unit of a district hospital in Northern Ghana
Authors: Benedict Baluuro Sumbobo, Gilbert Ti-enkawol Nachinab
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionGlobally, many patients including children are affected by medical errors that endanger their safety. Ensuring the safety of hospitalised children is challenging especially in resource-constrained settings. The purpose of the study was to explore the perspectives of nurses in a paediatric unit on patient safety practices.MethodsThe study employed a descriptive exploratory design using a qualitative approach. Twelve nurses working in a padiatric unit were purposively selected and interviewed individually using a semi-structured interview guide. The data were analyzed using a content analysis approach.FindingsThe study findings led to two main themes which include ward-related and nurse-related factors that influence safety practices in the paediatric unit. Some key ward-related factors that influence safety practices include inadequate staffing, lack of logistics, and limited ward space. Nurse-related factors identified were work experience, knowledge, attitude and nursing leadership.ConclusionSafety practices among nurses in the paediatric unit require attention to prevent the occurrence of harmful events. The provision of essentials at the ward level and conscious efforts by nurses is necessary in improving patient safety. There is also the need to train nurses on the essentials of paediatric patient safety.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12912-025-04073-x.Keywords: Paediatric patient, Safety practices, Hospitalized children, Nurse-related factors, Ward-related factors

Introduction

Patient safety is an essential aspect of clinical nursing that requires much attention to decrease the harm associated with poor practices. There is a need to ensure that safety practices are compatible with advances made in the field of healthcare quality [1]. The safety of children is a global concern that calls for expertise and knowledge in a variety of fields, including institutional and human aspects [1]. Patient safety in the health delivery system of all countries needs compliance with principles that, if applied correctly, will not harm the client or minimize potential harm [1].

Clients and their relatives come to the hospital with the expectation of getting safe, timely and effective health services [1]. This demonstrates the critical need to provide high-quality, safe health care in the context of global health. However, healthcare quality assessment by patients and care providers indicates that the quality of healthcare services expected by clients is inadequate and sub-optimal [2]. The main concern for patient safety in healthcare settings is hospital errors, which are some of the biggest issues in the healthcare system [3].

Providing care to children in a complex environment by different units and individuals may result in some harm to the children. This is as a result of many factors influencing safety practices of healthcare givers [1]. Medical errors are caused by a variety of factors, including the lack of knowledge and inadequate expertise of healthcare professionals [1]. These factors affecting paediatric patient safety are interconnected and can influence each other.

According to International Council of Nurses, 134 million adverse events caused by subpar treatment take place in hospitals in low and middle-income countries every year, resulting in around 2.6 million fatalities [4]. According to a study done in London on 47 hospitalized children, the following factors contributed to each patient’s major adverse events [ADEs] or critical incidents: Patient variables make up 2%, followed by task factors making up 3%, provider factors making up 60%, team factors making up 27%, and work/organizational aspects 8% [5].

Nurses are in a good position to improve the safety net for child care in the risky hospital environment because they continuously supervise, coordinate, and offer care [6]. However, there are several background characteristics of nurses such as age, level of education, skills, and previous information about paediatric patient safety that tend to be associated with nurses ability to ensure safety of the hospitalized child [1, 7].Nurses who work in the ward for a longer period tend to have adequate knowledge regarding paediatric patient safety than those who have less work experience in the ward [1].It is also thought that individual characteristics, such as nurses’ attitudes, beliefs, and knowledge might help or hinder the use of clinical practice standards by nurses endangering the safety of hospitalized children as a result [7, 8]. To promote patient safety, it is important to consider the efficiency of healthcare workers as well as the various factors of the workplace that influences their capacity to perform their jobs [9].

Adverse events, which are a major concern in patient safety may occur more often in low-income countries compared to high-income countries. In developing nations, or low-income countries, up to one in four children experience at least one incidence of harm while in the hospital [10]. Meanwhile, hospitalizations in European nations with advanced healthcare systems account for 8 to 12% of adverse occurrences [11]. Also, in the paediatric unit, for example, a variety of tasks are carried out to provide care for vulnerable patients who require attention from several disciplines and information sources [9].

Hazards, mistakes, and system failures might happen in certain care environments or care settings more often than others [10]. The ward environment in which healthcare is delivered may affect the safety of hospitalized children, either positively or negatively [12]. For instance, in Ghana and other developing countries, the ward environment may not be well structured to ensure adequate safety for children. Safety issues in underdeveloped countries may be compounded by overcrowding, lack of paediatric nurses, and lack of age-appropriate equipment [7]. A study in three district hospitals in Ghana revealed that there is a lack of adequate staff to effectively meet patients’ needs in the wards, posing a risk to patient safety [13].

In today’s healthcare setting, it is extremely difficult for nurses and medical professionals to keep patients safe, especially children, due to several influencers of safe practices [6]. Patient safety is likely to be a major where the ward’s atmosphere and routines are created more for the comfort of the medical staff than for the benefit of the children [14]. A study in Canada highlighted the significance of elements relating to the workplace environment, such as workload, nursing shortages, and the physical setting [7].

The characteristics of the ward environment, such as staffing and bed capacity, can affect paediatric patient safety at the hospital [15]. Though the availability of nursing staff may vary depending on the type of hospital, deficiencies that could endanger patient safety and lower the standard of pediatric healthcare may be present in most hospitals [16]. There is evidence that nurses are less likely to adhere to safety procedures in a ward setting that is marked by a severe workload, mental stress, and frequent disturbances [8, 17].

The World Health Organization estimates that one in every ten patients worldwide suffers from mistakes and unfavorable outcomes that may have been prevented in their treatment [18]. In locations of sub-Saharan Africa with limited resources, adverse events are common, of which Ghana is a part [13]. However, there is limited research on the safety of hospitalized children among nurses in these regions [13]. Although all levels of care make efforts to prevent adverse events, there is evidence that adverse occurrences happen among hospitalized children in Ghana [13]. A descriptive cross-sectional survey of sixteen (16) hospitals in Ghana, including two teaching hospitals revealed that only a third of the facilities assessed had done patient safety research and implemented measures to comprehend the causes of and come up with solutions for hospitalized children’s safety issues [13]. The study also shows that research on patient safety is limited, and more research needs to be conducted to identify the safety practices affecting patient safety, and ways to improve them. Despite all these, the issue of paediatric patient safety has not been adequately addressed in Ghana, particularly the current study environment.

Knowing nurses’ perspectives on the factors influencing safety practices would be beneficial for the creation and application of improved solutions given the significant role of nurses in patient safety [7]. It is also conceivable that their perceptions could be used to identify lesser-known patient safety problems. Additionally, solutions based on the perspectives of nurses will likely be more successful because clinical nursing constitutes a major aspect of patient care. This study explored nurses’ perspectives on patient safety to provide evidence that will inform recommendations to improve the safety of hospitalized children at various levels of healthcare.

Methods

Study design

The researchers used a qualitative study approach with a descriptive exploratory design. The exploratory design gives the participants the opportunity to express their opinions and understanding about the topic. Also, this approach allow the researchers to explore participants’ perspectives of the issue under investigation rather than one prescribed by pre-existing theoretical preconceptions [19].

The study setting

The study was conducted in a paediatric ward of a district hospital in Northern Ghana. The paediatric ward has a bed capacity of 60 and a staff strength of 56 nurses. The hospital serves as a referral centre for other facilities within the district. The hospital provides a 24-hour service and renders services including Medical services, Maternal and child health services, Ear, Nose and Throat (ENT) services, Neonatal Intensive Care services, Eye Clinic, Gynecological Services, and surgical services.

Participants eligibility

Inclusion criteria

The participants were included in the study if they were registered general nurses or paediatric nurses who worked at the paediatric ward for at least two years. The nurses included in the study were involved in paediatric patient care in the ward and the perspectives they shared were deeply ingrained in the experiences they had acquired.

Exclusion criteria

Nurses not working in the paediatric unit were excluded from the study.

Sample and sampling technique

The study was explained to those who met the inclusion criteria. Twelve nurses who met the inclusion criteria and were willing to participate were selected. A purposive sampling technique was employed to select the participants and individual interviews were conducted. The sample size was based on data saturation.

Data collection procedure

The principal researcher obtained permission from the authorities of the hospital where the data was collected. The purpose of the study was explained to the participants and information sheets were given to them. Participants were allowed to schedule the date, time and venue for the interview. However, the participants agreed to be interviewed in the ward manager’s office after close of work. The data were collected through face-to-face individual interviews lasting 30–45 min. The data was collected using a semi-structured interview guide developed by the researchers based on the purpose of the study [Appendix A]. The participants were asked open-ended questions and probes used when necessary. Some of the questions that were asked were “what are some of the nursing activities that affect the safety of hospitalized children?” “Tell me how the ward environment affects the safety of hospitalized children at your ward?” “How can the safety of hospitalized children be improved in the ward?”

During the interview period, the researcher was prepared with a list of the main issues and questions related to the factors that influence the safety practices of nurses and was able to change the order of questions based on how the interviews were proceeding. As such, the researcher was able to probe for the opinions and views of the participants. Data saturation was reached by the time the twelfth participant was interviewed.

Data analysis

Content analysis was used to analyze the data. Content analysis provides a highly flexible approach that can be modified for the needs of many studies, providing a rich account of data [20]. The principal researcher did a verbatim transcription of the audio-recorded interviews. The recordings were played and compared with the transcripts to ensure the accuracy of the transcripts. The transcripts were read severally to understand the views of the participants on the topic. The key ideas in the form of words, phrases, sentences, and paragraphs emerging were assigned initial codes. The initial codes containing several thoughts were then sorted into themes and subthemes based on how they were related. The analysis of the data was performed individually by the two researchers (BBS and GTN). The researchers had several discussions to agree on the emerging themes and subthemes. This led to several revisions of the themes and subthemes to make them suitable for the presentation of the findings.

Trustworthiness

Trustworthiness is necessary for research to be accepted as relevant for knowledge addition and suitable for use in various means and ways [21]. Criteria that should be considered by qualitative researchers in pursuit of a trustworthy study include dependability, credibility, transferability, and confirmability [22]. Credibility was ensured through a member check in which three of the participants confirmed the accuracy of findings as truly representing their views and experiences. To ensure transferability, the researchers gave a detailed description of the context in which the study was carried out. A clear description of the procedure for participants’ selection and a detailed description of the research setting were done. The method used for data collection and analysis was captured to ensure dependability. Confirmability was ensured by keeping an audit trail comprising audio recordings, analysis notes and coding details.

Ethical considerations

Ethical clearance was obtained from the Ghana Health Service ethics review board (GHS-ERC 044/03/22). The participants were made to understand that their participation was voluntary and that they could withdraw at any time from the study without having to give any reason. Those who met the inclusion criteria and were willing to participate in the study were given voluntary informed consent form to sign before interviews were conducted. All participants voluntarily signed a written informed consent form before they were interviewed. The participants were assured of the confidentiality of the information they provided. Participants were labelled as P1, P2, up to the last participant to ensure anonymity.

Results

Background characteristics of participants

A total of twelve nurses participated in the study. Seven of the participants were males, and five were females. All the participants were professional nurses with seven having a Bachelor of Science degree in nursing and five having diploma in nursing. Six of the participants were nurses with specializations in paediatric nursing. The work experience of the participants ranged from 3 to 10 years.

Themes and sub-themes

Two main themes were identified, which include ward-related factors and nurse-related factors that influence the safety practices of nurses in the paediatric ward. The main themes had corresponding sub-themes as indicated in Table 1 below.

Main themes and Sub-themes

Ward-related factors

The participants described ward-related factors as conditions within the ward environment that were perceived to affect the safety of hospitalized children. According to the participants, safety in the paediatric ward was influenced by factors such as inadequate staffing, limited space, how the ward is arranged and lack of logistics.

Inadequate staffing

Inadequate staffing was perceived by most of the participants as a major factor that affected paediatric patient safety in the ward. The participants alluded to the fact that there were always a lot of children on admission but few nurses were mostly on duty to handle these high numbers. According to participants, there were instances where three to four nurses are put on duty to manage the ward with over forty paediatric patients on admission. This affected safety practices of nurses negatively in the ward. The participants attributed some of the mistakes that endangered the lives of the patients to increased workload as a result of inadequate staffing. They explained that some safety measures or protocols are overlooked when the nurses are overwhelmed with work in the ward.

Patient numbers are high, and we have very few nurses attending to them. So sometimes, you are not always able to provide the care you are supposed to provide. You can imagine maybe we have three nurses in the ward who have to take care of forty patients or more [p3].

The pressure is too much, as compared to nurse-patient ratio, so we do not even have the time to really talk to patients or observe them and look out for patient safety. It is difficult to look out for all these things because, the ward is actually big, more than sixty beds, you can imagine [P6].

Some of the participants indicated that the low nurse-patient ratio puts pressure on the nurses and could cause mistakes. The nurses become overwhelmed by the number of patients and anything can happen. According to the participants, a stressful environment is prone to mistakes and patient harm.

The nurse patient ratio does not tally. When you have fewer nurses taking care of a greater number of patients, there is always pressure and stress, and some nurses can easily make avoidable mistakes [P4].

The participants also indicated that, ensuring patient safety starts from admission. However, the high number of patients was seen as a factor that make nurses unable to adhere to safety precautions right from admission.

In this unit you can come and have about seven patients lined up for admission at the same time. You will be in a hurry to attend to all seven and this will lead to you skipping some safety practices. [P1]

Limited space

The participants mentioned limited space for nurses and patients to move around freely as a factor that affects safety practices in the ward. According to participants, adequate space is needed to prepare medicines or carry out nursing procedures. However, the ward always looks very congested due to limited space and does not facilitate the safety of hospitalized children.

The space you need to work around the patient is not there. The ward is always crowded due to limited space [P1].

Let me say, because of lack of space, you may have a lot of beds in a very limited space, so movement becomes a problem and children can easily fall in their quest to move or play around [P4].

Ward arrangement

The participants indicated that the arrangement of the ward had an effect on the safety practices of nurses. They explained that if the ward is well arranged taking into consideration the patient’s condition or diagnosis it would have a positive influence on patient safety. The spread of infections will be avoided if the ward is arranged in a way to isolate patients with infectious diseases. Patient falls and injuries will also be minimized if the ward is well arranged to ease movement.

So the major one I think is the ward arrangement. The ward is arranged in a way to help to prevent cross infections. For instance, we have a separate place where we keep patients with conditions that can actually spread to other patients [P3].

In the ward, beds are arranged according to patient’s age and severity of sickness, and the position of the nurses’ station can easily allow you to see and observe and monitor all patients [P2].

Inadequate logistics

Inadequate logistics were identified by the participants as a major factor that affected the safety of hospitalized children. According to participants, basic facilities, such as hand washing facilities, and medical equipment, such as oxygen concentrators, oxygen cylinders, and beds with side rails are not mostly enough. Participants also stated that some important procedures cannot be carried out on patients who need those services due to a lack of medical equipment. This according to participants affected paediatric patient safety in the ward.

You may need some specific gadgets to perform some activities, for instance, there are days that you would need paediatric nasal prongs and it is not available, and the adult one cannot be used on the paediatric patients though that is what is available [P1].

Sometimes two children will be sharing one oxygen cylinder, and they will bring another one again, and we will be running up and down for oxygen. So sometimes it is very challenging [P5].

The participants also explained that the lack of basic things in the ward such as water and beds with side rails affect infection prevention practices and exposes children to falls respectively.

We are unable to wash our hands because taps are not flowing in the ward. The best you could do sometimes is to pour some spirit in your hand or use sanitizer if you have one. A lot of our beds do not also have side rails, and so patients are prone to falls all the time [P3].

Nurse-related factors

The nurse-related factors referred to various characteristics of the nurses that participants perceived to affect safety practices in the ward. The participants identified several characteristics of individual nurses that affected paediatric patient safety.

Knowledge of the nurse on patient safety

The participants explained that knowledge of the nurse on safety measures is very crucial in ensuring paediatric patient safety in the ward. The participants believed that nurses who have inadequate knowledge or no training on paediatric patient safety are always unable to provide safe care to hospitalized children.

Another challenge is lack of knowledge on patient safety measures on the part of some nurses or most of the nurses, and this does not help in ensuring patient safety in the ward [P4].

Some staff have inadequate knowledge level on patient safety and cannot take or identify safety issues early enough to prevent adverse events in the unit [P2].

According to participants, understanding and administering medication in paediatrics require sufficient knowledge to safely administer the drugs.

If the person’s job knowledge is not that high, it will affect the nursing activities, which is the understanding and administration of the medications prescribed, and sometimes there can be some errors in that, making the children unsafe [P10].

Participants also stated that there is a need for nurses to have adequate knowledge of medical gadgets to appropriately use them on patients. Participants indicated that the skills and abilities of the nurse to use medical equipment accurately could facilitate immediate interventions and avoid harm to patients.

There are several medical gadgets you have to use in caring for patients. So as nurses, we expect that you should be able to operate those gadgets. This is very important in providing patients immediate safe care in the ward [P3].

When the person is skillful in the use of the equipment or items he is using to take care of the client, he can prevent harm or injury to the child he is attending to [P2].

Participants indicated that training the nurses on paediatric patient safety will equip the nurses with the requisite knowledge. This will also increase their awareness and adherence to safety protocols in the ward.

The safety of hospitalized children can be improved through regular in-service training of nurses on patient safety measures in the ward [P4].

There is the need for nurses to be educated or informed on some practices, and when education is given to staff, you will realize that some of the practices we give to the patients will also be improved [P1].

Attitude of the nurse

The participants held the view that the attitude and practices of the nurse in the ward is very paramount in ensuring the safety of the children on admission. From the participants’ perspective, poor or bad attitude and practices of nurses do not preserve paediatric patient safety in the ward. Nurses who do not adhere to safety protocols in the ward can endanger the children’s safety.

Poor attitude towards work, you come to work but you are just so lackadaisical towards your job. So, negligence I think is one and lateness to work is another [P4].

Not being organized and not following infection prevention or safety protocols. And if the nurse is not smart and being observant in the ward, the safety of the children will be in danger [P3].

Participants also indicated that some nurses have the attitude of not promptly disposing off medical wastes and used items. They explained there were instances where sharps and other waste items are sometimes left on the ward exposing children to risk of injuries.

Sometimes, our safety boxes get full and it takes a long time before they are discarded. So sometimes you see needles and other stuff in the ward lying daown like that. It is a bad practice in ensuring patient safety in the ward [P7].

One thing I have always been worried about is how we keep our needles. Unfortunately, we do have needles that are not supposed to be exposed to patients been exposed to both patients and staff, putting them at risk [p3].

Work experience of the nurse

The work experience of the nurse in patient care was perceived by participants as a very instrumental factor that affects paediatric patient safety in the ward. The participants also believed that the older the nurse, the richer the experience. This opinion of the participants was based on the fact that the person might have gained adequate knowledge on paediatric patient safety and adheres to safety protocols better than the younger nurses. The participants also indicated that the older or experienced nurses serve as role models and sources of motivation to the younger ones in the ward.

Experience-wise, if a nurse has worked for some years, the person is expected to be in a better position with experience to avoid some of these things. And be able to also mentor the new ones coming, so that they can equally follow suit to do the right thing in the interest of patients [P3].

Someone who has been in the paediatric unit for several years, compared to someone who has just come, definitely the experiences will vary. All these affect the safety of the children in the ward [P10].

Work experience counts a lot in ensuring paediatric patient safety in the ward, the greater the experience of the nurse, the better for patient safety [P2].

The participants also largely attributed medication errors to lack of experience in the profession. Participants reiterated that newly posted nurses lack the necessary experience in patient care and paediatric drug administration. Thus putting the safety of children in danger through errors. Participants also suggested that, newly posted nurses who have no experience in the ward should not be put alone on duty, but rather with an experienced nurse.

I remember a newly qualified nurse was actually giving intravenous quinine and fortunately I rushed there and I saw the lady was actually giving the quinine wrongly. So I had to just do some intervention to avert any negative events [P3].

Nursing leadership

The participants perceived nursing leadership as an important factor that facilitated paediatric patient safety at the hospital. They observed that managers or unit heads played a role in ensuring there is effective reporting system that will go a long way to improve patient safety. According to participants good leadership provides direction and support which contributes greatly to paediatric patient safety at the hospital.

Having effective management can help in reporting, and it is helping us to improve on our reporting system. So I will say there have being a massive improvement in our documentation which is good for improved patient safety [P3].

And the lighting system in the ward is now good. Last week we received a memo that there were coming to fix every part of the electric system that had fault, and truly management has done it. All these will go a long way to ensure patients safety in the ward [P1].

Nurse-patient relationship

The participants identified nurse-patient relationship as a major factor that influence safety practices in the ward. They confirmed that when nurses have cordial relationship with patients and their primary caregivers it promotes paediatric patient safety in the ward. According to participants patients and their primary caregivers should be encouraged to share very vital information with the nurses. The participants were of the opinion that such information may be very crucial in preserving the safety of the hospitalized child.

Make the patient and mother feel comfortable to talk to you the nurse when the need arises. So that in case there is anything, they will just come to you and talk to you without any fear in them [P6].

Encouraging staff to give patient and patient relatives a welcoming environment, and then creating that cordial relationship between them helps promote patient safety [P5].

The participants indicated that patients opening up and sharing information was necessary in ensuring patients safety. However, how nurses talk to patients was seen as a factor that causes the patients and relatives to recoil and not share information.

Some of the patient relatives fear talking to nurses because some shout at them. Having a cordial relation with patients will let you get to know their problems and solve them before it become too late [P5].

The participants also explained that if the nurses do not have a cordial relationship with the patients/patient relatives, they may not provide some vital information that may be very important in protecting the child from harm.

They do not even want the admission, and they end up being admitted, so they come in aggressive. If you are the nurse who is not tolerant, you will end up reacting badly, and the patient will not feel comfortable around you. In that case if even something is happening to the child, he/she will feel like since that cordial relationship is not there, even if I inform them, they will not mind me [P6].

Discussion

The findings of the study revealed several ward-related factors that influence the safety practices of nurses in the paediatric unit. Some key findings that were ward-related include inadequate staffing, lack of logistics, limited space and how the ward is arranged. These findings will contribute to improving paediatric patient safety in the ward. Findings of this study will also serve as context-specific data that may be useful literature for other researchers on issues of paediatric patient safety in health care. Again, the study results also corroborate the findings from some existing research and add new information in the area of paediatric patient safety.

The study findings indicate that the number of nurses in the paediatric ward was not enough to provide effective nursing care and ensure patient safety. These findings corroborate the findings from various studies conducted on patient safety that indicate that there were inadequate numbers of healthcare workers to provide good healthcare for patients [3, 8, 13, 17, 23]. When nurses are overburdened with work, they can easily make mistakes and cause harm to the children. The present study points out that inadequate staffing makes it very difficult for nurses to adhere to safety protocols due to increased workload. The study clearly indicates that the poor nurse-patient ratio in the ward poses a major risk to patient safety. This calls for the need for stakeholders in the health sector to work towards improving the number of nurses in the wards.

This study identified limited space as a ward-related factor that influences the safety of hospitalized children. This finding is consistent with that of other studies that indicate that patients are most likely to suffer from medical errors or harm in ward environments where nurses do not have enough space for the preparation and administration of medicine [8, 15]. Children need space to play around when they are getting better, and nurses also need enough space to perform nursing activities such as drug administration, cannulation, bed bath and nasogastric feeding among others. When there is no space for these activities, both staffs and children compete for the limited space and this increases the risk of harm.

More importantly, the study found that lack of logistics was a major factor in the ward that affected paediatric patient safety. The strength of every health facility is in the availability of functional equipment to provide quality health care for patients. From the study findings, the paediatric ward lacked basic logistics such as oxygen concentrators, monitors, stands for oxygen cylinders, detergents, and nasal prongs for intranasal oxygen administration. The beds in the paediatric ward were also seen as unsuitable as they had no side rails to prevent children from falling. These findings agree with other studies that indicate that the availability of equipment and electronic resources increased the likelihood of adherence to patient safety principles related to medicine management, peripheral venous catheter care, and cardiac monitoring and surveillance [8, 19]. This underscores the importance of medical logistics and their impact on improving patient safety in the ward.

The nurse’s knowledge on nursing procedures and medications is very important in ensuring paediatric patient safety. The participants explained that knowledge of the nurse increases with higher level of learning and work experience in the care of children. This finding agreed with that of Biresaw et al., [1], which revealed that, degree holders demonstrated higher understanding and adherence to safety measures in the ward than their diploma counterparts. Biresaw and colleagues [1], concluded that, nurses who have higher academic qualifications may have more knowledge on patient safety as compared to those with lower academic qualifications. This implies that, the knowledge of the nurse in clinical practice is key in the care of hospitalized children. This finding could be a subtle call for the training of more nurses in the paediatric nursing specialization programme.

The findings of the present study also indicate that work experience of the nurses is very paramount in ensuring patient safety in the ward. This finding agrees with that of a study by Biresaw et al., [1] which indicated that nurses who work in the ward for a longer period tend to have adequate knowledge on paediatric patient safety than those who have less work experience in the ward.

Nurses who have some experience in child care are in a better position to contribute positively to improve patients’ safety in the ward. Also, in the present study adverse events were associated with newly posted nurses who were seen to have no work experience in ensuring paediatric patients’ safety. It can be therefore suggested that newly posted staff who have no experience in the paediatric ward should be paired with experienced staff so that they can learn from them.

The study findings established that good and responsive leadership influences the safety of hospitalized children positively in the ward. According to the study findings, the leaders play a role through effective communication and the provision of resources to improve the safety of children safe. Similarly, Vaismoradi et al., [8] concluded that regular practical feedback processes, interaction opportunities, and leadership motivate nurses’ adherence to daily safety measures. When the leaders are engaged with nurses and work with them in the ward, they can identify the weaknesses of the system and assist accordingly. This finding places an important emphasis on how the actions and in actions of the hospital leadership and management affects the safety of hospitalized children.

It is undoubtedly evident that, taking care of children in the ward is a collective activity between the nurses and the patient’s relatives. However, the study revealed that relatives sometimes get intimidated by the nurses in the ward and therefore fear communicating with them on issues concerning the child’s health, which affected the child’s safety negatively. To ensure improved paediatric patient safety the nurses and patient relatives should have a cordial working relationship. According to participants, the patients and relatives must be made comfortable to communicate freely with the nurses in the ward. This will help identify safety concerns of the patient and improve the safety of the hospitalized children in the ward. The relatives will also help monitor the patient and report any deterioration to the nurse for them to take immediate action to prevent any harm or complications.

Strengths and limitations

The strength of the study is that, the participants were nurses with work experience in the paediatric ward and hence could be considered adequately resourced to share vital insights. The exploratory design employed could be as empowering and flexible which allowed for the sharing of deeper insights by the participants.

However, the study did not involve family caregivers who are with the children during hospitalization. Engaging patients’ relatives could have unearthed their perspectives to complement those of the nurses. In addition, the data was based on the subjective experience and views of the participants hence authenticity of the findings was dependent on the objectivity of the respondents.

Conclusion

The main aim of this study was to explore the perspective of nurses on factors influencing the safety practices of nurses in the care of hospitalized children. This study added new insights to the existing body of knowledge on paediatric patient safety by highlighting the factors influencing safety practices of nurses in the paediatric unit. The study also uncovered important factors that are related to nurses’ experiences and knowledge in ensuring paediatric patient safety. The factors influencing paediatric patient safety are at multiple levels indicating the need for multifaceted initiatives to address paediatric patient safety issues. Though nurses play a pivotal role in ensuring patient safety, the management of hospitals should recruit adequate number of nurses, provide material resources and train nurses on patient safety culture.

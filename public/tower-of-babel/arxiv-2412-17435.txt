Postmeasurement information and nonlocality of quantum state discrimination
Jinhyeok Heo, Donghoon Ha, Jeong San Kim

Abstract
In quantum state discrimination, nonlocality arises when optimal discrimination cannot be achieved using only local operations and classical communication. It has recently been shown that postmeasurement information identifying the subensemble containing the prepared state can either annihilate or create such nonlocality. Here, we demonstrate that these effects can depend critically on how the original ensemble is partitioned into subensembles. We derive sufficient conditions under which different ensemble partitions lead to the annihilation or creation of nonlocality upon the revelation of postmeasurement information. We further construct explicit bipartite quantum state ensembles satisfying these conditions, thereby illustrating the partition-dependent nature of nonlocality in quantum state discrimination.



I
Introduction

Quantum nonlocality is a fundamental feature of multipartite quantum systems. A paradigmatic manifestation of such nonlocality is quantum entanglement, which cannot be generated from separable states using only
local operations and classical communication
(LOCC)
[
1
,
2
]
. Entanglement serves as an essential resource for various tasks in multipartite quantum information processing, including quantum teleportation and quantum communication
[
3
,
4
]
. These developments have also motivated extensive efforts to characterize other forms of quantum nonlocality, such as Bell nonlocality and quantum steering
[
5
,
6
]
.

Nonlocality also arises in the discrimination of multipartite quantum states. For a given ensemble of quantum states, nonlocality is said to occur when globally optimal discrimination cannot be achieved using only LOCC
[
7
]
. In general, mutually orthogonal quantum states can be perfectly discriminated by global measurements, whereas nonorthogonal states cannot
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. Nevertheless, some sets of multipartite orthogonal quantum states cannot be perfectly discriminated using only LOCC
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
. Moreover, certain ensembles of nonorthogonal quantum states cannot be optimally discriminated by LOCC
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
Furthermore, LOCC can activate nonlocality in state discrimination by transforming certain locally distinguishable ensembles into locally indistinguishable ones
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.

Recently, it was shown that nonlocality in quantum state discrimination can be either annihilated or created when
postmeasurement information
(PI) identifying the subensemble containing the prepared state is provided
[
22
,
23
,
25
,
24
]
. Some ensembles that exhibit nonlocality without PI become optimally distinguishable by LOCC once the prepared subensemble is revealed. In this case, PI annihilates the nonlocality in state discrimination. Conversely, there exist ensembles that can be optimally discriminated by LOCC without PI but cease to be optimally distinguishable by LOCC when the prepared subensemble is revealed
[
25
,
24
]
. In this case, PI creates nonlocality. These contrasting effects naturally raise the question of whether PI about the prepared subensemble can always be used to annihilate or create nonlocality in quantum state discrimination.

Here, we consider bipartite quantum state discrimination and show that the annihilation or creation of nonlocality by PI can depend on how the original ensemble is partitioned into subensembles. In particular, even when PI associated with one ensemble partition annihilates or creates nonlocality, PI associated with another partition need not have the same effect. We establish sufficient conditions for this partition-dependent annihilation or creation of nonlocality (Theorems
3
and
4
) and construct bipartite quantum state ensembles satisfying these conditions (Examples
1
and
2
).

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we review the definitions and relevant properties of separable measurements and entanglement witnesses used to analyze nonlocality in quantum state discrimination. We also introduce
minimum-error discrimination
(ME) and present several useful properties of nonlocality in ME. In Sec.
III
, we review ME with postmeasurement information and establish sufficient conditions for nonlocality in this setting (Theorems
1
and
2
). In Sec.
IV
, we introduce the notions of annihilating and creating nonlocality by PI and derive sufficient conditions under which these effects depend on the choice of subensembles specified by PI (Theorems
3
and
4
). We then construct four-state ensembles for which nonlocality is annihilated or created depending on how the ensemble is partitioned into two-state subensembles (Examples
1
and
2
). Finally, in Sec.
V
, we summarize our results and discuss directions for future research.

II
Preliminary

For a bipartite Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{\rm A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{\rm B}}
, let
\mathbb{H}
be the set of all Hermitian operators acting on
\mathcal{H}
.
We denote by
\mathbb{H}_{+}
the set of all positive-semidefinite operators in
\mathbb{H}
, that is,

\mathbb{H}_{+}=\{E\in\mathbb{H}\,|\,\bra{v}E\ket{v}\geqslant 0~\forall\ket{v}\in\mathcal{H}\}.

(1)

A bipartite quantum state is described by a density operator
\rho
, that is, a positive-semidefinite operator
\rho\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
with unit trace
\Tr\rho=1
.
A measurement is represented by a positive operator-valued measure
\{M_{i}\}_{i}
, that is, a set of positive-semidefinite operators
M_{i}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
satisfying
the completeness relation
\sum_{i}M_{i}=\mathbbm{1}
, where
\mathbbm{1}
is the identity operator in
\mathbb{H}
.
For the state
\rho
, the probability of obtaining the measurement outcome with respect to
M_{j}
is
\Tr(\rho M_{j})
.

Definition 1
.

E\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
is called
separable
if it can be described as

E=\sum_{l}A_{l}\otimes B_{l},

(2)

where
A_{l}
and
B_{l}
are positive-semidefinite operators acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d_{\rm A}}
and
\mathbb{C}^{d_{\rm B}}
of
\mathcal{H}
, respectively.

II.1
Entanglement witness

We denote the set of all
separable
operators in
\mathbb{H}_{+}
as

\mathbb{SEP}=\{E\in\mathbb{H}_{+}\,|\,E:\mbox{separable}\},

(3)

and its dual set as
\mathbb{SEP}^{*}
, that is,

\mathbb{SEP}^{*}=\{E\in\mathbb{H}\,|\,\Tr(EF)\geqslant 0~\forall F\in\mathbb{SEP}\}.

(4)

An element in
\mathbb{SEP}^{*}
is also called
block positive
.

A measurement
\{M_{i}\}_{i}
is called a
separable measurement
if
M_{i}\in\mathbb{SEP}
for all
i
, and a measurement is called a
LOCC measurement
if it can be realized by LOCC. Note that every LOCC measurement is a separable measurement
[
2
]
.

Definition 2
.

W\in\mathbb{H}
is called an
entanglement witness
(EW)
if
\Tr(\sigma W)\geqslant 0
for any state
\sigma
in
\mathbb{SEP}
but
\Tr(\rho W)<0
for some state
\rho
in
\mathbb{H}_{+}\backslash\mathbb{SEP}
, or equivalently

W\in\mathbb{SEP}^{*}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{+}.

(5)

Throughout this paper, we only consider the situation of discriminating
bipartite
quantum states from the ensemble of the form,

\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda},~\Lambda=\{1,2,3,4\},

(6)

where the state
\rho_{i}
is prepared with the probability
\eta_{i}
.

II.2
Nonlocality in bipartite quantum state discrimination

Let us consider the quantum state discrimination of
\mathcal{E}
in Eq. (
6
) using a measurement
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
. Here, the detection of
M_{i}
means that
the prepared state is guessed to be
\rho_{i}
.
The
ME
of
\mathcal{E}
is to achieve the optimal success probability,

p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\mathcal{M}}\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i}),

(7)

where the maximum is taken over all possible measurements
[
26
]
.

When the available measurements are limited to LOCC measurements,
we denote the maximum success probability by

p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\mathrm{LOCC}\,\mathcal{M}}\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i}).

(8)

Similarly, we denote the maximum success probability over all possible separable measurements as

p_{\rm SEP}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\mathrm{Separable}\,\mathcal{M}}\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i}).

(9)

From the definitions, we have

\max\{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\}\leqslant p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant p_{\rm SEP}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}).

(10)

In discriminating the states from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
, quantum nonlocality occurs if the optimal success probability in Eq. (
7
) cannot be achieved only by LOCC measurements, that is,

p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})<p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}).

(11)

From Inequality (
10
), we can easily see that Inequality (
11
) holds if

p_{\rm SEP}(\mathcal{E})<p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}).

(12)

Moreover, the following proposition provides a condition for Inequality (
12
) in terms of EW.

Proposition 1

(
[
27
]
)
.

For a bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
satisfying

\eta_{1}\rho_{1}-\eta_{i}\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{SEP}^{*}

(13)

for all
i\in\Lambda
, we have

p_{\rm SEP}(\mathcal{E})<p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})

(14)

if and only if there exists an EW in
\{\eta_{1}\rho_{1}-\eta_{i}\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
.

III
Quantum state discrimination with postmeasurement information

For a state ensemble
\mathcal{E}
in Eq. (
6
) and a two-element subset
S
of the index set
\Lambda
, let us consider the subensembles,

\mathcal{E}_{0}=\Big\{\frac{\eta_{i}}{\sum_{j\in S}\eta_{j}},\rho_{i}\Big\}_{i\in S},~\mathcal{E}_{1}=\Big\{\frac{\eta_{i}}{\sum_{j\in S^{\mathsf{c}}}\eta_{j}},\rho_{i}\Big\}_{i\in S^{\mathsf{c}}},~S^{\mathsf{c}}=\Lambda\backslash S

(15)

where
S^{\mathsf{c}}
is the complement of
S
in
\Lambda
.
For the case that the state
\rho_{i}
belongs to
\mathcal{E}_{0}
in Eq. (
15
), we note that the preparation of
\rho_{i}
with probability
\eta_{i}
from the ensemble

is equivalent to the preparation of the subensemble

with probability

followed by the preparation of
\rho_{i}
from
\mathcal{E}_{0}
with probability
\eta_{i}/\sum_{j\in S}\eta_{j}

We denote by

the classical information

about the prepared subensemble
\mathcal{E}_{b}
defined in Eq. (
15
), that is

\PI_{S}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0,&i\in S,\\
1,&i\in S^{\mathsf{c}},\end{array}\right.

(16)

where
i
is the index of the prepared state
\rho_{i}
.

Now, let us consider the situation of discriminating the quantum states from
\mathcal{E}
when
\PI_{S}
in Eq. (
16
) is given.
In this situation, a measurement can be represented by a positive operator-valued measure
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\{\tilde{M}_{\vec{\omega}}\}_{\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}}

where the outcome space is the Cartesian product,

\Omega_{S}=S\times S^{\mathsf{c}}.

(17)

Here, the detection of
\tilde{M}_{(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})}

or

according to

or

, respectively

ME of

p_{\rm G}^{\PI}(\mathcal{E},S)=\max_{\tilde{\mathcal{M}}}\Big(\sum_{i\in S}\eta_{i}\mathrm{Tr}\big[\rho_{i}\sum_{j\in S^{\mathsf{c}}}\tilde{M}_{(i,j)}\big]+\sum_{i\in S^{\mathsf{c}}}\eta_{i}\mathrm{Tr}\big[\rho_{i}\sum_{j\in S}\tilde{M}_{(j,i)}\big]\Big),

(18)

where the maximum is taken over all possible measurements.
Note that when
\rho_{i}
is prepared and
\PI_{S}=b
is given, the prepared state is correctly guessed if we obtain a measurement outcome
\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}
with
\omega_{b}=i
.

Theorem 1
.

For a bipartite quantum state ensemble

\displaystyle p_{\rm SEP}^{\rm PI}(\mathcal{E},S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p_{\rm SEP}(\tilde{\mathcal{E}})

\tilde{M}_{\vec{\omega}}\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
for all
\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}
, the fifth equality is by
\sum_{\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}}\tilde{M}_{\vec{\omega}}=\mathbbm{1}
, and the last equality is from Theorem
1
.
Since Inequality (
38
) contradicts Inequality (

),

\mathbb{H}_{+}
.
Thus, there exists an EW in
\{\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\mu}}\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\mu}}-\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\omega}}\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\omega}}\}_{\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}}
.

Conversely, let us suppose that

is an EW for some
\vec{\omega}^{\bullet}\in\Omega_{S}
.

satisfying

(39)

For a measurement
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\{\tilde{M}_{\vec{\omega}}\}_{\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}}
with

\tilde{M}_{\vec{\mu}}=\mathbbm{1}-\ket{u}\!\bra{u},~\tilde{M}_{\vec{\omega}^{\bullet}}=\ket{u}\!\bra{u},

(40)

we have

\displaystyle p_{\rm G}^{\rm PI}(\mathcal{E},S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p_{\rm G}(\tilde{\mathcal{E}})

(41)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}}\Tr(\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\omega}}\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\omega}}\tilde{M}_{\vec{\omega}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}}\Tr\Big[(\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\mu}}\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\mu}}-\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\mu}}\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\mu}}+\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\omega}}\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\omega}})\tilde{M}_{\vec{\omega}}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\mu}}\Tr(\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\mu}}\sum_{\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}}\tilde{M}_{\vec{\omega}})-2\sum_{\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}}\Tr[(\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\mu}}\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\mu}}-\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\omega}}\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\omega}})\tilde{M}_{\vec{\omega}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\mu}}-2\bra{u}(\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\mu}}\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\mu}}-\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\omega}^{\bullet}}\tilde{\rho}_{\vec{\omega}^{\bullet}})\ket{u}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 2\tilde{\eta}_{\vec{\mu}}=p_{\rm SEP}^{\rm PI}(\mathcal{E},S),

where the first equality is by Eq. (
25
), the first inequality is from the definition of
p_{\rm G}(\tilde{\mathcal{E}})
in Eq. (
7
), the fourth equality is due to
\sum_{\vec{\omega}\in\Omega_{S}}\tilde{M}_{\vec{\omega}}=\mathbbm{1}
together with Eq. (
40
), and the last inequality is from Inequality (
39
), and the last equality is from Theorem
1
.
Thus, Inequality (
41
) leads us to Inequality (
36
).
∎

IV
Annihilating and creating nonlocality in quantum state discrimination from the choice of postmeasurement information

In this section, we first recall the definitions of annihilating and creating nonlocality by PI.
By using properties of EW related to nonlocality in discriminating quantum states, we provide four-state ensembles to show PI dependency of annihilating or creating nonlocality in quantum state discrimination.

Definition 3
.

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}
in Eq. (
6
) and a two-element subset

annihilates

\mathcal{E}

(42)

Also, we say that
\PI_{S}

creates
nonlocality if nonlocality does not occur in discriminating the states of
\mathcal{E}
and
the availability of
\PI_{S}
releases the occurrence of nonlocality, that is,

p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})=p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}),~p_{\rm L}^{\PI}(\mathcal{E},S)<p_{\rm G}^{\PI}(\mathcal{E},S).

(43)

The following theorem establishes a sufficient condition for nonlocality arising in discriminating quantum states to be annihilated depending on the choice of subensembles provided by PI.

V
Discussion

We have investigated bipartite quantum state discrimination and shown that the annihilation or creation of nonlocality by PI can depend on the choice of subensembles. We first established sufficient conditions for nonlocality in ME with PI (Theorems
1
and
2
). We then derived sufficient conditions under which the annihilation or creation of nonlocality depends on how the original ensemble is partitioned into subensembles (Theorems
3
and
4
). Finally, we constructed explicit bipartite quantum state ensembles satisfying these conditions, thereby illustrating the partition-dependent nature of nonlocality in state discrimination (Examples
1
and
2
).

The ensembles
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
presented in Examples
1
and
2
necessarily contain an entangled state. Indeed, the expectation value of the entanglement witness
W
in Eq. (
49
) with respect to
\rho_{4}
is always negative. By the definition of an entanglement witness, this implies that
\rho_{4}
is entangled. Nevertheless, there exist ensembles consisting entirely of separable states that satisfy the conditions of Theorem
3
.

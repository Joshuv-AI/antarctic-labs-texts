Abstract
. We express the branch points cross ratio of cyclic Mumford curves as quotients of p p -adic theta functions evaluated at the p p -adic period matrix



1.
introduction

In this note we generalize
\lambda
functions of cyclic curves that are known in the complex case, see for example
[
FK980
]
, to the
p
-adic case. That is, given a
p
-adic Mumford cyclic curve of genus
g
with the equation

(1.1)

y^{p}=x^{r_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-\lambda_{i})^{r_{i}}

for
p
primes, we derive formulas expressing cross ratios of
\lambda_{i}
for the
p
-adic case as quotients of
p
-adic theta functions.
The question of obtaining such formulas is a natural question following from
[
T
]
. In
[
T
]
,

p
-adic periods of modular curves
X_{0}(p)
are calculated for certain primes
p

such that the genus of
X_{0}(p)
equals 2. The calculation of periods involves the following 3 steps:

(1)

In the case of a
g=2
curve with branch points at
0
,
1
,
\infty
,
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
,
\lambda_{3}
,
formulas for

expressing them as ratios of products of
p
-adic theta functions are obtained.

(2)

These formulas are used to express
\lambda_{i}
as
p
-adic infinite series in entries of the
p
-adic period matrix.

(3)

These series are inverted to obtain expressions for the periods as functions of
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
,

\lambda_{3}
.

(4)

The exceptional zero conjecture for these

is verified.

We focus on (

) and derive formulas for
\lambda_{1}
, …,
\lambda_{r}
for an arbitrary

.
This generalizes the formulas from
[

]
to cyclic curves of any genus.
In
[

]
, the formulas for

are derived by analyzing the fundamental

-adic group action on the associated tree and its fundamental domain. Our analysis will follow the classical approach that was exemplified in

This approach enables us to write the quotients of theta functions as ratios of

This paper has three sections. In the first section, we collect the relevant facts about Mumford curves and

2.
Non-Special Divisors

In this section, our goal is to give a dimension formula for sections into line bundles that are supported on divisors of its ramification points. We work with the general case of cyclic covers.
Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field and
\charOp(\mathbb{K})=0
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
p
cyclic covering of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
is a Galois covering

\phi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})
of degree
p
where
X

is a non-singular projective curve over
\mathbb{K}
with genus
g
.

If
F(x,z)
is the function field of
X,
the equation that defines it as an extension of
F(x)
is

z^{p}=M(x)/N(x)
or:

(2.1)

(zN(x))^{p}=M(x)N(x)^{p-1}.

Therefore, we assume that the equation for such a cover is of the form

(2.2)

y^{p}=\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}(x-a_{i})^{r_{i}},

and the genus equals

g(X)=\tfrac{1}{2}(n-1)(p-1).

In the equation (
2.2
) we assumed that all the branch points are finite.
If we work with a model in which
\infty
is going to be one of the branch points then the equation becomes

(2.3)

y^{p}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-a_{i})^{r_{i}}.

Now,
\infty
and
a_{i}
,
i\,{=}\,1
,…,
n
, are branch points of the cover.
Let
B_{i}=\phi^{-1}(a_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq n
, and
B_{\infty}=\phi^{-1}(\infty)
. Then

\divOp(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}B_{i}-\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\bigg)B_{\infty}.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
D\,{=}\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}D_{i}
be a divisor supported on finite branch points,
and
d_{j}\,{\in}\,\{0,\dots,p-1\}
.
Define the set
\{g_{k}\}_{k=0}^{p-1}
by the equations

(2.4)

\frac{1}{p}\bigg(\deg D-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{kr_{j}+d_{j}}+\overline{\sum_{j=1}^{n}kr_{j}}\bigg)+1=g_{k},\quad 0\leq k\leq p-1,

where
\overline{r}
denotes the remainder of the division of
r
by
p
. Then

(2.5)

\dim H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(-D))=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\max(0,g_{k}).

Proof.

The curve
X
has a cyclic automorphism, given explicitly through

T:(x,y)\mapsto(x,\zeta y),

where
\zeta
is a
p
-th root of unity.

D
is invariant under the action of
T
. Thus,
T
acts on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(-D))
.
We conclude that

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(-D))\,{=}\,\bigoplus_{\chi\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\ast}}V_{\chi},

where
V_{\chi}
is the eigenspace of
T
with a character

\chi\,{:}\,{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\,{\to}\,\mathbb{K}
.
Let
n_{\chi}\,{=}\,\dim V_{\chi}
. Clearly,

\dim H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(-D))=\sum_{\chi\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\ast}}n_{\chi}.

We find
n_{\chi}
for all
\chi
.

The characters
\chi
of
T
are of the form
\zeta^{\ell}
for some
\ell
,

0\,{\leq}\,\ell\,{\leq}\,p\,{-}\,1
. Hence, each
\chi
is assigned with

\ell
such that
\forall f\,{\in}\,V_{\chi}

f/y^{\ell}
has a trivial action of
T
on it.
Thus,
f/y^{\ell}
is a pullback of a function from
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})
.
Let
H_{\ell}^{0}
be the subspace of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(-D-\divOp(y^{\ell})))

composed of all pullbacks from functions on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K}).

Lemma 2.2
.

\exists D_{\ell}
, a divisor on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})
,
such that
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K}),\mathcal{O}(-D_{\ell}))
is isomorphic to
H^{0}_{\ell}
.

Proof.

If
B_{j}
is a ramification point appearing in the divisor
D

with multiplicity
d_{j}
,
let
m_{j}\,{=}\,[(\ell r_{j}\,{+}\,d_{j})/p]\,{\times}\,p
, that is,
m_{j}
is the maximal number such that

m_{j}\,{\leq}\,\ell r_{j}\,{+}\,d_{j}
and
m_{j}\,{=}\,0\modOp p
.
Let
Q_{a_{j}}
represent the point that corresponds
to
a_{j}
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})
. Define

(2.6)

D_{\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{m_{j}}{p}Q_{\lambda_{j}}-\bigg[\ell\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}}{p}\bigg]\infty.

We show that
D_{\ell}
is the desired divisor. Let
h:\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})\to\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})

such that
\divOp(h)\,{\geq}\,{-}D_{\ell}
. By
\widehat{h}
we denote a lift of
h
,
and so
\widehat{h}\,{\in}\,H_{\ell}^{0}
. Then

(2.7)

-\divOp(\widehat{h})\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}-\tilde{d}_{j}B_{j}+\ell\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{i}\infty.

We require
\tilde{d}_{j}\,{\leq}\,\ell r_{j}\,{+}\,d_{j}
.

Conversely, if
\widehat{h}\in H_{\ell}^{0}
and
\widehat{h}
is a lift of
h
,
then by definition

\ord_{P_{j}}\widehat{h}\geq-(\ell r_{j}+d_{j}).

We have
\ord_{P_{j}}\widehat{h}\,{=}\,0\modOp p
,
since
\widehat{h}
is a lift of
h
.
We conclude, that
\ord_{Q_{j}}h\,{\geq}\,m_{j}/p
,
and hence,
h\in H_{\ell}^{0}
, as required.
∎

Now we compute
\deg D_{\ell}
:

(2.8)

\deg D_{\ell}=\frac{1}{p}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}\bigg)-\bigg[\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ell r_{j}}{p}\bigg].

By definition,
m_{j}\,{=}\,\ell r_{j}\,{+}\,d_{j}\,{-}\,\overline{\ell r_{j}\,{+}\,d_{j}}
. Hence, we can write

(2.9)

\begin{split}\deg D_{\ell}&=\frac{1}{p}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\ell r_{j}+d_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{\ell r_{j}+d_{j}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ell r_{j}+\overline{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ell r_{j}}\bigg)\\
&=\frac{1}{p}\bigg(\deg D-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{\ell r_{j}+d_{j}}+\overline{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ell r_{j}}\bigg).\end{split}

Applying the Riemann-Roch theorem to
D_{\ell}
, we obtain

\dim H^{0}\big(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K}),\mathcal{O}(-D_{\ell})\big)=\max(\deg D_{\ell}+1,0).

Therefore,

(2.10)

\begin{split}\dim H^{0}\big(X,\mathcal{O}(-D)\big)&=\sum_{\chi_{\ell}\in Z_{p}^{*}}\max(\deg D_{\ell}+1,0)\\
&=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}\max\bigg(\frac{1}{p}\bigg(\deg D-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{\ell r_{j}+d_{j}}+\overline{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\ell r_{j}}\bigg)+1,0\bigg).\end{split}

The last sum equals

\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}\max(0,g_{\ell})
, which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3.
Cyclic Mumford Curves

In this section, we collect the relevant facts about cyclic Mumford curves
over non-Archimedean fields. The reader may consult
[
GV
,
V1
,
V2
]
and the references therein for proofs of the assertions presented in this section.
Let
\mathbb{K}
be a complete non-Archimedean field
which is algebraically closed and
\charOp(\mathbb{K})\,{=}\,0.

Let
\Gamma
be a Schottky group of rank
g
, that is,
\Gamma
is a free discontinuous subgroup of

\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{K})
. Let
\Omega\,{=}\,\{x\,{\in}\,\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})\mid x\text{ is an ordinary point for }\Gamma\}
.

\Gamma
acts freely on
\Omega
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X_{\Gamma}\,{=}\,\Omega/\Gamma
be the Mumford curve associated with
\Gamma
.

We assume that
\infty
is an ordinary point. Let
N(\Gamma)

be the normalizer of
\Gamma
in
\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{K})
.
The following is shown in
[
V1
,
V2
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

X_{\Gamma}
admits a
p
-cyclic covering of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})

if and only if there exists
\sigma_{0}\,{\in}\,N(\Gamma)
such that:

(1)

\sigma_{0}^{p}=\mathrm{id}
,

(2)

\forall\gamma\in\Gamma

\prod_{k=0}^{p-1}\sigma_{0}^{k}\gamma\sigma_{0}^{-k}=1\modOp[\Gamma,\Gamma]
.

Let
\Gamma_{0}\,{=}\,\langle\Gamma,\sigma_{0}\rangle
.
The theorem from
[
DS
]
characterizing the action of periodic automorphism on
free groups enables
[
V2
]
to show
1
1

1

see Proposition 2.2. in
[
V2
]
and the discussion preceding it
:

Theorem 3.2
.

There exist
\sigma_{0}
, …,
\sigma_{s}\,{\in}\,\Gamma_{0}
with the following properties:

(1)

\sigma_{i}^{p}=\mathrm{id}
, for
i=1
, …,
s
,

(2)

\Gamma_{0}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle
is a free product,

(3)

Let
\xi_{j,1}\,{=}\,\sigma_{j}\sigma_{0}^{-1}
and
\xi_{j,i}\,{=}\,\sigma_{0}\xi_{j,i-1}\sigma_{0}^{-1}
for

j=1
, …,
s
,
i=2
, …,
p-1
. Then
\xi_{j,i}
form a free basis of
\Gamma
.

The natural mapping
\phi:\Omega/\Gamma\,{\to}\,\Omega/\Gamma_{0}
is a realization of the mapping

X_{\Gamma}\,{\to}\,\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})
, since
\rank\Gamma_{0}\,{=}\,0
.
The fact that
\Gamma_{0}
is a free product of elements with finite order implies
that
\Omega/\Gamma_{0}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})
.

3.1.
Analytical Jacobian Variety of Mumford curves

Definition 3.2
.

For each
a,b\,{\in}\,\Omega
we define the function

\Theta_{a,b}\,{:}\,\Omega\,{\to}\,\mathbb{K}
as
follows

(3.1)

\Theta_{a,b}(z)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{z-\gamma a}{z-\gamma b}.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{a}
denote the orbit of
a
under the action of
\Gamma
.
If
a\in\Omega
, then
\mathcal{O}_{a}\,{\subset}\,\Omega
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The following properties are satisfied by
\Theta_{a,b}
:

(1)

\Theta_{a,b}
is a convergent product and satisfies the equation

(3.2)

\forall\gamma\in\Gamma\ \ \forall z\in\Omega\qquad c_{a,b}(\gamma)\Theta_{a,b}(z)=\Theta_{a,b}(\gamma z).

(2)

If
b\,{\not\in}\,\mathcal{O}_{a}
,
then
\Theta_{a,b}
has zeros precisely on
\mathcal{O}_{a}
and poles on
\mathcal{O}_{b}
,
and has no other zeros or poles in
\Omega
.

(3)

If
b\,{\in}\,\mathcal{O}_{a}
, then
\Theta_{a,b}
does not depend on
a
.
Let
b\,{=}\,\beta a
, then we assign
c_{\beta}(\alpha)\,{=}\,c_{a,b}(\alpha)
,
and denote
\Theta_{a,b}
by
\Theta_{\beta}
.

For the proof,
see
[
GV
, Chap. 2]
.

Let
G_{\Gamma}\,{=}\,\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{\ast})
.
Because
\Gamma
is free we can identify
G_{\Gamma}
with
\left({\mathbb{K}^{\ast}}\right)^{g}
.
The group
A_{\Gamma}\,{=}\,\left\{c_{\gamma}\mid\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}

is a free Abelian group of rank
g
, which is discrete subgroup in
G_{\Gamma}
.

Definition 3.3
.

G_{\Gamma}/A_{\Gamma}
is the analytical Jacobian variety of the Mumford curve
X_{\Gamma}
.

Assume,
D=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-b_{i})
is a divisor on
X_{\Gamma}
of degree
0.

D
is principal
if it is a divisor of function
f:X_{\Gamma}\to P^{1}(\mathbb{K})
.

Definition 3.4
.

If
X_{\Gamma}
is an algebraic curve its Jacobian variety
J_{\Gamma}

is the group of divisors of degree
0
modulo the subgroup of principal divisors.

Then we have, see
[
GV
]
,

Theorem 3.4
.

The mapping
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}-b_{i})\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{m}c_{a_{i},b_{i}}
induces an analytical isomorphism
between
J_{\Gamma}
and
G_{\Gamma}/A_{\Gamma}
.

Remark 3.5
.

This theorem is a multiplicative version of the usual mapping
between algebraic and analytical versions of the Jacobian variety, expressed through Abelian integrals.

For the proof, see
[
GV
]
.

Definition 3.5
.

Choose a basepoint
o\in\Omega
and define

u_{o}\,{:}\,X_{\Gamma}\,{\to}\,G_{\Gamma}
by
u_{o}(x)\,{=}\,c_{x,o}
.
Extend the mapping
u_{o}
to divisors of any degree on

X_{\Gamma}
using the multiplicative property of
c_{a,b}
.

3.2.
p
-Adic Theta Functions

We define the action of
A_{\Gamma}
on

\mathcal{O}^{\ast}(G_{\Gamma})=\{\mathsf{f}\mid\mathsf{f}\text{ is holomorphic and non-vanishing function on }G_{\Gamma}\}

by
\mathsf{f}_{c_{\beta}}(c)=\mathsf{f}(c_{\beta}c)
.
If
\mathsf{a}_{c}{}\in Z^{1}(A_{\Gamma},G_{\Gamma})
is a 1-cocycle, we denote

\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{a}}=\big\{\mathsf{h}\mid\mathsf{h}\text{ is a holomorphic function on }G_{\Gamma},\forall c_{\gamma}\,{\in}\,A_{\Gamma}\ \mathsf{h}(c)\,{=}\,\mathsf{a}_{c_{\gamma}}(c)\mathsf{h}(c_{\gamma}c)\big\}.

Definition 3.6
.

Elements of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{a}}
are called theta functions of type
\mathsf{a}
.

Now, we construct the basic theta function.
Let
\rho\,{:}\,A_{\Gamma}\,{\times}\,A_{\Gamma}\,{\to}\,\mathbb{K}^{\ast}

be a symmetric bilinear form such that

\rho^{2}(c_{\beta},c_{\gamma})=c_{\beta}(\gamma)
for all
\beta,\gamma\in\Gamma
.
Define a canonical 1-cocycle by

\mathsf{a}_{c_{\gamma}}(c)=\rho(c_{\gamma},c_{\gamma})c(\gamma),\quad c_{\gamma}\in A_{\Gamma},\ \ c\in G_{\Gamma}.

In this case,
\dim\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{a}}=1
, and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{a}}
is generated by the Riemann theta function

(3.3)

\theta_{\Gamma}(c)=\sum_{c_{\gamma}\in A_{\Gamma}}\mathsf{a}_{c_{\gamma}}(c).

The divisor of
\theta_{\Gamma}
is invariant under the action of
A_{\Gamma}
, and hence, induces a divisor on
J_{\Gamma}
. This divisor defines a polarization on
J_{\Gamma}
. The
p
-adic version of the Riemann
vanishing theorem is valid, i.e. we have

Theorem 3.6

(
Riemann Vanishing Theorem
)
.

(1)

The holomorphic function
\theta_{\Gamma}\circ u_{o}

has a
\Gamma
-invariant divisor of degree
g
,
which is regarded as a divisor on
X_{\Gamma}
.

(2)

If
K_{\Gamma}\,{=}\,(\divOp(\theta_{\Gamma}\circ u_{o})-o)\modOp\Gamma\,{\in}\,\divOp(X_{\Gamma})
,
then
2K_{\Gamma}
is a canonical divisor.
The class of
K_{\Gamma}
under linear equivalence of divisors
does not depend on the choice of the basepoint
o
.

(3)

Let
c\,{\in}\,G_{\Gamma}
. Then
\theta_{\Gamma}(c)=0

if and only if
c\,{=}\,u_{o}(D-K_{\Gamma})
for some positive divisor
D
of degree
g
. The order of vanishing of
u_{o}
at
c
equals
i(D)
, the index of specialty of
D
.

For the proof, see
[
V2
,
GV
]
.

4.
Images of branch points in the Jacobian variety

Our goal in this section is to calculate images of branch points
on the Jacobian variety generalizing the calculation carried out in
[
V1
,
V2
]
for cyclic covers.
We continue with the assumptions of Section
3
.
We start with the following lemma from
[
V2
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\alpha\,{\in}\,N(\Gamma)
, then
\forall a,b\in\Omega
we have

\Theta_{a,b}(\alpha z)=c_{a,b}(\alpha)\Theta_{\alpha^{-1}a,\alpha^{-1}b}(z).

Proof.

Let
\alpha
has the form

\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}q&t\\
v&w\end{pmatrix},

and the action of
\alpha
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{K})
is given by

\alpha z=\frac{qz+t}{vz+w}.

We assume that
\det\alpha=1
, then

\alpha^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}w&-t\\
-v&q\end{pmatrix}.

Then, we compute

(4.1)

\Theta_{a,b}(\alpha z)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\alpha z-\gamma a}{\alpha z-\gamma b}=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{qz+t-vz\gamma a-w\gamma a}{qz+t-vz\gamma b-w\gamma b}.

Taking into account that

z-\alpha^{-1}\gamma a=z-\frac{w\gamma a-t}{-v\gamma a+q}=\frac{-v\gamma az+qz-w\gamma a+t}{-v\gamma a+q},

we rewrite the product in (
4.1
) as

(4.2)

\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{z-\alpha^{-1}\gamma a}{z-\alpha^{-1}\gamma b}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{-v\gamma a+q}{-v\gamma b+q}.

Let

(4.3)

c_{a,b}(\alpha)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{-v\gamma a+q}{-v\gamma b+q}.

Thus,

\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\alpha z-\gamma a}{\alpha z-\gamma b}=c_{a,b}(\alpha)\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{z-\alpha^{-1}\gamma a}{z-\alpha^{-1}\gamma b}=c_{a,b}(\alpha)\prod_{\tilde{\gamma}\in\Gamma}\frac{z-\tilde{\gamma}\alpha^{-1}a}{z-\tilde{\gamma}\alpha^{-1}b},

where
\tilde{\gamma}=\alpha^{-1}\gamma\alpha
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

As a corrolary we have:

Lemma 4.2
.

\forall\alpha\in N(\Gamma)\ \ \forall\gamma\in\Gamma\ \ c_{\alpha^{-1}a,\alpha^{-1}b}(\gamma)=c_{a,b}(\alpha\gamma\alpha^{-1})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, and then Theorem
3.3
we have

(4.4)

\Theta_{a,b}(\alpha\gamma z)=c_{a,b}(\alpha)\Theta_{\alpha^{-1}a,\alpha^{-1}b}(\gamma z)=c_{a,b}(\alpha)c_{\alpha^{-1}a,\alpha^{-1}b}(\gamma)\Theta_{\alpha^{-1}a,\alpha^{-1}b}(z).

At the same time, due to
\alpha\gamma\alpha^{-1}\,{\in}\,\Gamma
we have

(4.5)

\Theta_{a,b}(\alpha\gamma z)=\Theta_{a,b}(\alpha\gamma\alpha^{-1}\alpha z)=c_{a,b}(\alpha\gamma\alpha^{-1})\Theta_{a,b}(\alpha z)\\
=c_{a,b}(\alpha\gamma\alpha^{-1})c_{a,b}(\alpha)\Theta_{\alpha^{-1}a,\alpha^{-1}b}(z).

Comparing (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) we obtain the result.
∎

Our goal is to calculate
c_{\sigma_{i}}
explicitly for the generators of
\Gamma_{0}

introduced in Theorem
3.2
.

We have
\sigma_{i}\in\mathrm{PGL}(2,\mathbb{K}),
and
\mathbb{K}
is an algebraically closed field.
Hence,
\sigma_{i}
are diagonalizable. That is, for each
\sigma_{i}
there exists
\upsilon_{i}
such that

\sigma_{i}\,{=}\,\upsilon_{i}^{-1}\beta_{i}\upsilon_{i}
,

\beta_{i}=\mathrm{diag}(\zeta^{\nu_{i}},1)

and
\zeta
is a
p
-th root of unity. Assume that
o_{i}
,
e_{i}
are ordinary fixed points of
\sigma_{i}
.
Since the fixed points of
\beta_{i}
are
o_{i}\,{=}\,0
and
e_{i}\,{=}\,\infty
, we have

\upsilon_{i}o_{i}=0
and
\upsilon_{i}e_{i}=\infty
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
a
be an arbitrary point in
\Omega
, and
e_{i}
be a
fixed point of
\sigma_{i}
such that
e_{i}\,{=}\,\upsilon_{i}^{-1}\infty
. Then:

(4.6)

\Theta_{a,e_{i}}(\sigma_{i}z)=\zeta^{\nu_{i}}\Theta_{\sigma_{i}^{-1}a,e_{i}}(z).

Proof.

First, we prove the lemma when
\sigma_{i}
is diagonal, that is
\sigma_{i}\,{=}\,\beta_{i}
. Then we can write

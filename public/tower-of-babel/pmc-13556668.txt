Green auto-combustion derived V-type hexagonal ferrite nanoparticles: local atomic structure by XAFS and dual magnetic–electrochemical caffeine sensing properties
Authors: Sajjad Hussain, Jameel Ahmed Baig, Latif Ullah Khan, Imran Sadiq, Zahid Ullah Khan, Bhajan Lal, Fida Hussain Solangi, Shakoor Ahmed Solangi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The use of caffeine in food and beverage products, along with its reported harmful effects at high levels, has made its accurate and reliable detection a regulatory requirement. In contrast, conventional electrochemical sensors are generally not very sensitive, selective or stable, especially for caffeine quantification in complex real-world matrices, which inspires the development of new electrode materials. To overcome this, here, we report the glassy carbon electrode (GCE) modified with V-type hexagonal ferrite nanoparticles (SrSnFe8O15-NPs), prepared by a sol–gel-based green auto-combustion route for the development of a high-performance platform for the sensing of caffeine. The as-synthesized nanoparticles were characterized extensively; the single-phase crystalline structure was confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis, and the local atomic coordination environment was confirmed by X-ray absorption fine structure (XAFS) analysis with an average crystallite size larger than 30 nm. The surface morphology and particle size were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM), respectively, and Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) analysis confirmed that the high surface area of 209.6 m2 g−1 is another characteristic structural feature that is responsible for the improved electrochemical performance of the electrode. The nanoparticles were found to exhibit soft magnetic properties during vibrating sample magnetometry. Furthermore, the synthesized V-type hexagonal ferrites were employed to modify a glassy carbon electrode (GCE), developing SrSnFe8O15-NPs/GCE for the electrochemical sensing of caffeine. Cyclic voltammetry studies revealed conductive and diffusion-controlled behavior, with the SrSnFe8O15-NPs/GCE demonstrating high sensitivity and selectivity for caffeine detection (linear ranges: 0.5 to 80 µM; detection limits: 0.025 µM, while the LOQ was 0.078 µM. The sensor was successfully tested in real commercial soft drink samples, demonstrating its potential as a reliable and sensitive sensor for the monitoring of beverage safety in the real world.

Introduction

Caffeine (1,3,7-trimethylxanthine) is a naturally existing purine alkaloid that has diverse pharmacologic functions. It is a central nervous system stimulant that can promote wakefulness, improve attention, reduce fatigue and improve cognitive function.1 Caffeine has a unique bitter flavor, has antioxidant effects and affects metabolic processes, offering different health benefits.2 It has stimulatory effects on both the cardiovascular and neurological systems. The structurally related methylxanthines, such as theophylline and theobromine, are all coronary vasodilators that also decrease cardiac muscle tension and remove muscle fatigue.3 Caffeine, on an ecological level, serves as a defensive compound in plants and exhibits pesticide activity against herbivorous insects and fungal pathogens.4 Caffeine (CAF) is considered to be the most widely used psychoactive drug in the world, mainly because of its ubiquity in food and pharmaceutical products.5 It is consumed daily in the morning and added to drinks, such as tea and coffee. However, caffeine (CFN) does contain bioactive phenolic compounds, such as guaiacol (GCL) and theophylline (THN).6 These compounds have some health benefits, but high levels of their consumption are known to be dangerous to health. Nowadays, energy drinks containing exceptionally high caffeine concentrations are excessively consumed, and there are concerns about caffeine-induced toxicity in adolescents and young adults.7 Monitoring caffeine levels in beverages is critical for ensuring public health safety. Furthermore, anti-doping regulations prohibit excessive caffeine intake in competitive sports due to its potential performance-enhancing effects.8 Current research has associated energy-drink consumption with many adverse health effects, including diabetes, insomnia, seizures, cardiovascular complications, and mood disturbances. In addition, discontinuing CAF intake can trigger withdrawal symptoms, such as headaches, reduced concentration, fatigue, muscle rigidity, nausea, irritability, and depressive states.9 Caffeine detection has been achieved using several analytical methods, such as HPLC, gas chromatography, spectrophotometry and capillary electrophoresis. HPLC is very sensitive and accurate, but it also has drawbacks; it is time-consuming, expensive and requires a complex sample pre-treatment process for rapid or on-site analysis.10 In contrast, electrochemical sensors have several noteworthy advantages, including being rapid, cheap, easily miniaturized, and highly sensitive, making them appealing for practical, real-time monitoring of caffeine.11 However, there remain a number of challenges affecting the previously reported electrochemical sensors, such as low sensitivity, small linear range, and poor long-term stability. These limitations emphasize the need to develop better electrode materials with high surface area, good conductivity and chemical stability, which is being addressed in the present study through the design of the SrSnFe8O15-NP-modified electrode for the detection of caffeine in a sensor application. Today, awareness of the possible toxicity associated with excessive caffeine consumption in adolescents and young adults is paramount. Many analytical methods are adopted for the detection of caffeine nowadays, including high-performance liquid chromatography, capillary electrophoresis and spectrophotometry.12 The procedures are typically lengthy, expensive and require specialized skills for sample preparation. In comparison, electrochemical sensors provide a simpler, more portable and less expensive means for in situ caffeine detection.13 A critical component of the electrochemical sensor is the electrode, which is surface-modified using nanoparticles, particularly magnetic materials. Magnetic nanoparticles, however, have an intrinsic magnetic property, which makes it hard to suppress their tendency to form aggregates and thus limits their application as electrode modifiers.14,15 In order to overcome this drawback, a hybrid composite material of activated carbon and magnetic spinel ferrite nanoparticles has been proposed.

Magnetic nanoparticles have attracted considerable attention as electrode modifiers in electrochemical sensing due to their unique combination of high surface area, tunable magnetic properties, good electrical conductivity, and chemical stability.16 Their nanoscale dimensions provide an abundance of active surface sites that enhance electron-transfer kinetics, while their inherent magnetic character facilitates easy separation, recovery, and reusability in sensing applications. Various magnetic nanomaterials, including spinel ferrites and other mixed-metal oxide nanostructures, have been successfully employed to modify electrode surfaces for the detection of pharmaceutical compounds, biomolecules, and environmental contaminants, often showing improved sensitivity and selectivity compared to unmodified electrodes.17,18 Hexagonal ferrites, in particular, offer additional advantages, such as high chemical stability, tunable magnetic anisotropy, and favorable surface characteristics, making them promising candidates for the development of advanced electrochemical sensing platforms.18 Building on these advantages, the present study explores the use of V-type hexagonal ferrite nanoparticles (SrSnFe8O15-NPs) as an electrode modifier for the sensitive and selective electrochemical detection of caffeine.

The extraordinary properties of magnetic materials, such as their reusability, high electrical conductivity, superb corrosion resistance, remarkable catalytic activity, intrinsic biocompatibility and easily modifiable surface, have made them useful in electrochemical sensors.19,20 From a structural point of view, these materials are distinguished in different crystal phases, such as spinel, cubic and hexagonal lattices, which give them their own physicochemical and magnetic properties.21 These structural changes, especially at the surface/interface, are critical to their functional effectiveness in a range of applications. The crystal structures and properties of ferrites are very versatile and more or less adjustable, so they are suitable for a wide variety of applications. Their adaptability makes them suitable for a number of applications, including magnetic hyperthermia, high-efficiency transformer cores, and high-performance sensing systems.21

Hexagonal ferrites exhibit 10 distinct structural types (M-type, R-type, X-type, U-type, W-type, Y-type, Z-type, β-types, 18-H type, and V-type) that arise from different arrangements of their fundamental S-block, T-block, and R-block building units.22 These structural differences cause variations in their magnetic and physical properties. Recently, V-type hexagonal ferrites have become a remarkable subclass because of their special crystal structure and advantageous magnetic properties, which make them a potential material for new and advanced technologies.23 These magnetic materials are produced in a certain way that may have a significant effect on their properties. In the present work, an environmentally friendly green sol–gel technique was developed using garlic extract, which served a dual role as a natural reducing and stabilizing agent. Furthermore, garlic was selected due to its environmentally friendly properties, large number of functional groups and capacity for pore formation during the synthesis procedure. Not only is this approach economical, but also, green chemistry principles, which play an important role in sustainable development goals, are followed.24 This synergetic addition is aimed at controlling aggregation problems and enhancing the functional benefits of both additions.25

The aim of the present study was to synthesize V-type hexagonal ferrite nanoparticles (SrSnFe8O15-NPs) through a green synthesis technique using bioactive compounds obtained from fresh garlic plant extract as an eco-friendly reducing and stabilizing agent. The synthesized V-type hexagonal ferrite was analyzed by several techniques to characterize its atomic structure, surface morphology, magnetic properties and surface area. In addition, the V-type hexaferrite-based nanoparticles were immobilized on a glassy carbon electrode (GCE) to build an electrochemical sensor for selective and sensitive detection of caffeine. Systematic analytical performance evaluations, including parameters like accuracy, precision, and reproducibility of the proposed method, were carried out. Furthermore, the sensor was applied to the quantitative analysis of real samples of different commercial soft drinks, showing its application in real2-time analytical applications.

Preparation of garlic extract and sol–gel green synthesis method

Fresh roots of garlic (250 g) were bought from the local market in Lahore, Pakistan and thoroughly washed with distilled water. At the same time, the garlic root extract was made by peeling, finely slicing and boiling the garlic in 500 mL of distilled water for 40 min. The resulting solution was filtered using filter paper to remove particulate matter. These extracts were subsequently utilized in the combustion process.26 The bioactive compounds present in the garlic extract play two roles in the sol–gel green synthesis process; they act as a reducer and stabilizer, which improves the effectiveness of the garlic extract as a fuel agent.27 The V-type hexagonal ferrite (SrSnFe8O15) was prepared using the garlic extract via a green sol–gel auto-combustion method (Scheme 1). To prepare the precursor solution, stoichiometric quantities of strontium nitrate [Sr (NO3)3·9H2O (99.95% pure, Sigma-Aldrich], Sn(iii) sulphate (SnSO4; purity ≥95%), and iron nitrate [Fe (NO3)3·9H2O (99.95% pure, Sigma-Aldrich)] were dissolved in a beaker with the fresh garlic root extract. For each concentration, the stoichiometric proportions of the salts mentioned above were individually dissolved in 500 mL of ultrapure deionized water in separate beakers. The solutions were placed on a hot plate and subjected to continuous agitation using a magnetic stirrer to ensure homogeneous mixing. The initial solution's color, regardless of concentration, was yellow, and the pH was 5.0. Ammonium hydroxide (NH4OH) solution was used to control the pH. The NH4OH solution was added dropwise to adjust the pH to 7.0, during which the solution exhibited a visible color transition to red. The solutions were heated on a hot plate to an internal temperature of 80 °C to 90 °C. After 3 hours, a gel was formed and then dried at 250 °C to give a dry black powder. The material was then finely ground using a mortar and pestle and subsequently calcined in a muffle furnace at 1000 °C for 3 hours to get the desired crystalline phase of the synthesized powder.

Preparation of electrodes

The obtained V-type hexagonal ferrite (SrSnFe8O15) NPs were used for electrochemical studies and physicochemical characterizations. The surface of the glassy carbon electrode (GCE) was carefully polished with 0.3 µm alumina slurry before surface modification. The electrode was then sequentially ultrasonicated in deionized water and absolute ethanol for 15 minutes per treatment to remove the polishing residues. The SrSnFe8O15-NPs/GCE sensor was developed using the drop-casting technique (Fig. 1). In order to prepare the suspension, the SrSnFe8O15-NPs (5.0 mg) were mixed in 2.0 mL of deionized water, after which the mixture was further dispersed in an ultrasonic bath for 20 minutes. Next, a 0.5% Nafion solution was added as a conducting binder (50 µL). The prepared suspension was then dropped onto the clean surface of the GCE and allowed to dry at room temperature, and 5 µL of the prepared suspension was dropped onto the clean surface of the GCE. The successfully synthesized SrSnFe8O15 nanoparticle-modified glassy carbon electrode (SrSnFe8O15-NPs/GCE) was then subjected to comprehensive electrochemical characterization prior to use in the quantitative determination of caffeine. The sensor showed improved electrochemical activity for detecting and measuring caffeine concentrations accurately.

Results and discussions

Structural properties and local atomic structure

The crystal lattice plays an important role as the basic structure of a material and hence affects its intrinsic properties. The present study conducted structural analysis using the Cu Kα radiation generated at an operating voltage of 40 kV and with a wavelength of 1.54 Å. The indexed XRD patterns of V-type hexaferrite (SrSnFe8O15) recorded over the 2θ range of 20°–80° are presented in Fig. 2. These findings indicate the presence of a hexagonal crystalline structure in V-type hexaferrite (SrSnFe8O15). Consequently, the XRD graph shows the presence of very sharp and intense peaks with the absence of any impurity peaks, confirming the purity of the synthesized V-type hexagonal ferrite NPs. The crystalline structure of V-type hexaferrite (SrSnFe8O15) was confirmed by the prominent Bragg peaks observed in the XRD spectrum, with the sharpest peak attributed to the (111) plane, in contrast to previous research.28 The calculated lattice constants were a = 5.70 Å and c = 9.95 Å, while the aspect ratio was c/a = 1.75 Å. To determine the extent of crystallization, the crystalline size was evaluated using Scherrer's formula.23 The obtained crystalline size of the current synthesized material was 30 nm.

XRD pattern of the V-type (SrSnFe8O15) hexagonal ferrite nanoparticles.

Unlocking local atomic structure of SrSnFe8O15 V-type hexaferrite

The Fe K-edge (7112 eV) X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES) spectrum of the V-type hexagonal SrSnFe8O15 hexaferrite (Fig. 3a) exhibited a pronounced pre-edge feature and a sharp white-line peak, followed by relatively weak post-edge oscillations. The distinct broad peak at a high energy level originated from the first EXAFS oscillation, associated with the single scattering of the first nearest neighbors (oxygen atoms). The pre-edge peak position at ∼7114 eV and the white line peak at ∼7133 eV suggested a +3 oxidation state for the Fe sites. The existence of a well-defined pre-edge peak demonstrated the contribution of non-centrosymmetric tetrahedral Fe sites to the pre-edge region in the V-type hexaferrite lattice. Notably, in a non-centrosymmetric chemical environment, the Fe 3d and 4p states are intermixed, allowing dipole transitions from 1s core to the p–d hybridized states,29 which caused the intensity of the pre-edge peak to increase.

Quantitative EXAFS fit

To elucidate the local atomic structural environment of the Fe sites, the Fe K-edge XAFS spectra of the V-type hexagonal SrSnFe8O15 hexaferrite were quantitatively analyzed following the previously reported methodology,30 and nonlinear fitting of χ(k) was carried out in R-space. The XAFS data were normalized and preprocessed using Athena from the Demeter package prior to quantitative fitting. The reported crystallographic data for the V-type SrSnFe8O15 hexaferrite, with lattice parameters a = b = 5.80 Å and c = 54.50 Å in the R3̄m space group,23 were employed to generate the effective backscattering amplitudes and phase shifts. The multiple scattering paths based on the crystallographic structure were generated using the FEFF6-lite code, implemented within Artemis in the Demeter software package. The experimental EXAFS data in R-space, within the range of 1.0–3.7 Å and using a Hanning window with a k-range of 3–12 Å−1, were employed for best-fit analyses. Artemis, utilizing the IFEFFIT engine, was applied to fit the experimental χ(k) data and refine the structural parameters, including coordination number (N), bond distance (R), Debye–Waller factor (σ2), amplitude reduction factor (S02), and energy shift (E0), as expressed in the EXAFS equation (eqn S1). The dominant single-scattering paths corresponding to the Fe–O and Fe–Fe/Sn bond pairs were incorporated into the quantitative fitting of the Fe K-edge EXAFS spectra. The passive electron reduction factor (S02) and energy shift (E0) were constrained to be identical for all scattering paths, while the mean-square relative displacement (σ2) and interatomic distance (R) were refined independently to achieve the best-fit results.

The quantitative analysis of the Fe K-edge (7112 eV) EXAFS data yielded well-fitted k2-weighted spectra (Fig. 3b), confirming the local atomic structure of the V-type SrSnFe8O15 hexaferrite. Fourier transform (FT) of the Fe K-edge EXAFS data (Fig. 3c) displayed a dominant first peak, corresponding to the Fe–O bond pairs in the first coordination shell, whereas the peaks at higher R values are attributed to the Fe–Fe/Sn bond pairs in the second and fourth coordination shells for the V-type SrSnFe8O15 hexaferrite (Fig. 3c). The higher values of the mean square relative displacements (MSRDs) for all the coordination shells (Table 1) reflect the high degree of lattice disorder within the SrSnFe8O15 ferrite. Nevertheless, the best fit result (Table 1) for the experimental χ(k) spectra suggests that the SrSnFe8O15 hexaferrite has a V-type structure.

Morphology of the V-type hexagonal ferrite

The morphology of the green-synthesized V-type hexagonal ferrite (SrSnFe8O15) sample was examined using field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM). The grain shape, size, and distribution were analyzed through FESEM images captured at various magnifications, as shown in Fig. 4. The average particle size was estimated using ImageJ, a Java-based image analysis software, revealing an average particle diameter of approximately 68.04 nm. Low-magnification images, which cover a broader area of the sample, confirmed the uniform distribution of particles throughout the sample surface.31 Most particles exhibited spherical or rod-like shape morphologies. However, in regions with dense particle populations, agglomeration was observed, leading to the formation of cube-shaped clusters.

Atomic force microscopy

The topographical surface of the V-type hexagonal ferrite (SrFe8SnO15) nanoparticles was investigated using non-contact mode atomic force microscopy (AFM). Fig. 5 presents representative atomic force microscopy (AFM) images of the V-type hexagonal ferrite (SrFe8SnO15) nanoparticles, displayed in both three-dimensional (3D) topographical view and the corresponding height distribution histogram. The histogram bar graph in Fig. 5 shows that the particle size is from 25 to 75 nm, with an average size of 68 nm.

AFM topography image and plot of the V-type SrFe8SnO15 hexaferrite.

Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET)

Fig. 6 illustrates the nitrogen adsorption–desorption isotherm and the corresponding pore-size distribution of the synthesized V-type hexagonal ferrite (SrFe8SnO15) nanoparticles. The BET surface area of the V-type hexagonal ferrite (SrFe8SnO15) nanoparticles was found to be 209.6 m2 g−1, which is significantly higher than that of other reported ferrite materials, such as CoFe2O4 (70.9 m2 g−1), indicating a high surface area favorable for applications like catalysis or drug delivery.32 The mesoporous nature of the material was further supported by the BJH pore-size distribution, which showed the presence of pores within the mesopore range (2–50 nm), as seen in other spinel ferrites with similar structures. The observed hysteresis in the adsorption and desorption curves suggests that the material retains nitrogen in its pores even after the pressure is reduced, indicative of a mesoporous structure with flexible pores.

Vibrating sample magnetometers

The magnetic hysteresis loop of the V-type hexagonal ferrite (SrFe8SnO15) nanoparticles was investigated using the vibrating sample magnetometer with an applied field of 17 kOe, as shown in Fig. 7. The hysteresis loops indicate that the V-type hexagonal ferrite (SrFe8SnO15) nanoparticles exhibit soft ferrimagnetic behavior, with the following magnetic parameter values: saturation magnetization 57.36 Ms, coercivity 556.31 Hc, and remanent magnetization 53.57 Mr. Previous studies have shown that the integration of magnetic and electric properties can lead to the formation of magnetoelectric structures, which enhance energy-storage capabilities via electrochemical mechanisms.31

Electrochemical sensor for caffeine detection

Electrochemical detection of caffeine using the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode

The electrochemical behavior of caffeine was investigated using both an unmodified glassy carbon electrode (bare/GCE) and one modified with SrSnFe8O15/GCE, as depicted in Fig. 8a. As shown in Fig. 8a, the electrocatalytic performance of the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode material toward caffeine was assessed using cyclic voltammetry (CV) with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing 50 µM caffeine at pH 7.0, at a constant scan rate of 50 mV s−1. The bare GCE showed relatively low current response at the beginning, while the modified SrSnFe8O15/GCE showed a slight drop in the catalytic current for caffeine in CV analysis. The electrocatalytic activity is also improved due to the presence of the SrSnFe8O15 ferrite composite material, which offers many surface-active sites, and also the high electrical conductivity of the prepared composite material.33 Moreover, the synergistic effect of these properties can enhance the total electrocatalytic effect of the SrSnFe8O15 ferrite composite material for the detection of caffeine. Furthermore, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy was used to analyze the charge-transfer kinetics and electrocatalytic properties of both the unmodified glassy carbon electrode (bare/GCE) and the modified SrSnFe8O15/GCE, as illustrated in the Nyquist plot (Fig. 8b). One of the important parameters of Nyquist plots is the charge-transfer resistance, which was found to be much lower for SrSnFe8O15/GCE (852.3 Ω) than for bare/GCE (6716 Ω) in the test solution. The EIS results are consistent with the CV results, and both sets of results suggest that SrSnFe8O15/GCE is a good sensing probe. This high current response, low charge-transfer resistance and increased surface activity highlight the excellent performance of SrSnFe8O15/GCE, proving it as a promising candidate for the determination of the analytes in electrochemical sensing applications.

(a) Oxidation peak current (Ipa) responses of caffeine at PBS (pH 7.0) and 50 mV s−1 using GCE and the modified electrode SrSnFe8O15/GCE. (b) EIS plots of the bare GCE and modified SrSnFe8O15/GCE, with the corresponding equivalent circuit model.

Effect of supporting electrolyte

The effect of various buffers on the electrochemical response of the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode for the detection of caffeine was statistically examined (Fig. 9). It is very important to determine the most appropriate chemical medium that can ensure the highest electrocatalytic efficiency and signal output to the modified electrode material.34 Four different buffer solutions were used: acetate, borate, Britton–Robinson buffer (BRB) and phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The best electrochemical response was obtained for the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode in PBS buffer, which means that it was the best buffer for the oxidation of caffeine. This improved performance is probably attributed to the specific ionic interaction between the ions of the PBS buffer and the ions of the caffeine analyte, which results in better electron-transfer interactions at the modified electrode surface.

Effect of different electrolytes on the oxidation response of caffeine in the modified electrode SrSnFe8O15/GCE.

Effect of pH solution

Following the electrolyte optimization, a comprehensive investigation of the pH effect was carried out to determine the ideal pH conditions that yield the highest sensing performance. Moreover, the pH level of PBS buffer plays a crucial role in influencing the performance of electrochemical sensors.35 To gain insights into the reaction mechanism, CV measurements were conducted in PBS solutions with varying pH values ranging from 3.0 to 9.0, as illustrated in Fig. 10. As the pH increased from 3.0 to 7.0, a noticeable rise in the peak current was observed, reaching the maximum at pH 7.0. However, a further increase in pH from 8.0 to 9.0 resulted in a decline in the peak current. Based on the results, pH 7.0 was identified as the optimal condition for caffeine detection under which the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode exhibited superior sensing performance. Consequently, all further electrochemical measurements were carried out at an optimized pH.

Effect of pH values on the peak current response of caffeine for the modified electrode SrSnFe8O15/GCE.

Effect of scan rate on caffeine detection

The electrochemical CV method was used as a basic method to determine the redox properties and charge-transfer behavior of the fabricated SrSnFe8O15 nanocomposite material. It provides useful data on electrochemical activity, reaction kinetics, and diffusion processes of analytes reacting with the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode surface.36 The electrochemical process can be controlled by either surface interaction or diffusion, which is important for testing the sensing ability of the SrSnFe8O15 nanocomposite material. Performing CV at different scan rates can help determine which process is in control.37 In this study, the CV measurements were performed from 20 to 70 mV s−1, and the CV voltammograms are shown in Fig. 11a, and the linear plot of peak current vs. scan rate is displayed in Fig. 11b. The scan rate was increased, and the oxidation peak current of caffeine increased progressively, suggesting that the electron-transfer kinetics were directly affected by the scan rate. The peak current (Ipa) of the resulting graph of peak current (Ipa) versus scan rate (mV s−1) was linear, with a high correlation coefficient (R2 = 0.9932). The linearity indicates that the oxidation process of caffeine on the surface of SrSnFe8O15/GCE is a surface-controlled process instead of a diffusion-controlled process. For surface-confined processes, the electroactive species will be adsorbed onto the electrode surface, and the rate of the reaction will depend on the interaction and surface concentration of the active species. This enhancement of peak current response with higher scan rates is a characteristic feature of this effect and indicates that the modified electrode possesses a large number of active sites for efficient adsorption of caffeine onto the modified electrode surface.38 Furthermore, the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode exhibited good electrochemical stability and excellent conductivity and was suitable for use as an electrochemical sensing platform. This behaviour suggests that the SrSnFe8O15 nanocomposite is an excellent catalyst for charge transfer at the electrode interface, making it ideal for the detection of caffeine by the CV technique.

Calibration study and comparative performance evaluation

A detailed calibration study was conducted to measure the sensing ability of SrSnFe8O15/GCE for caffeine, employing differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) in an electrochemical workstation. A range of caffeine concentrations from 0.5 to 80 µM were investigated, and the DPV responses for these are shown in Fig. 12a. As can be seen in the calibration plot shown in Fig. 12b, a strong linear correlation was found between the peak current and caffeine concentration, with a high correlation coefficient (R2 = 0.99547) confirming the excellent sensitivity and repeatability of the sensor. The limit of detection (LOD) and limit of quantification were determined using the standard deviation of the current response and the slope of the calibration curve. The calculated LOD value was 0.025 µM, and the LOQ value was 0.078 µM, suggesting that the sensor has the ability to detect caffeine at trace levels. Selectivity, stability, and reproducibility of the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode

Selectivity is a critical factor in assessing the overall performance of electrochemical sensors. In Fig. 13a, the selectivity of the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode toward caffeine, a targeted analyte, in soft drink products is illustrated. Various co-existing interfering substances were added to the detection system with a 20 µM concentration of glucose, sodium phosphate (SP), sodium benzoate (SB), sodium citrate (SC), and sucrose. The presence of these species caused a deviation of less than 5% in the peak current response for the caffeine analyte, indicating minimal interference. These findings confirm that the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode exhibits excellent selectivity for caffeine detection, even in the presence of structurally or electrochemically similar compounds in soft drink products. The reproducibility of the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode was assessed. Five individually prepared electrodes were tested in a PBS solution containing 50 µM caffeine using the DPV technique. The relative standard deviation (RSD) of the peak current (Ipc) was found to be within 3.69%, demonstrating that the prepared sensor exhibits excellent reproducibility, as shown in Fig. 13b. A stability assessment was conducted to evaluate the durability and signal consistency of the modified electrode under repeated use. Over the course of 30 consecutive electrochemical cycles, the oxidation peak current for caffeine exhibited minimal fluctuation, maintaining a nearly consistent response. The relative standard deviation (RSD) of the peak current was determined to be 3.69%, indicating excellent stability, as observed in Fig. 13c. This low RSD value suggests that the SrSnFe8O15/GCE-modified electrodes are firmly anchored to the electrode surface and retain their electrocatalytic functionality during continuous operation. The observed stability is primarily attributed to the solid interaction between the prepared SrSnFe8O15 ferrite composite and the electrode surface, which effectively prevents particle agglomeration and detachment.

Real sample analysis

In order to evaluate the practical application of the SrSnFe8O15/GCE modified electrode for caffeine detection, soft drink samples, such as Coca-Cola and Sting, were collected from a local market in Hyderabad, Sindh, Pakistan and analyzed. The results obtained from the electrochemical analysis were found to be satisfactory, and the method was found to be reliable in measuring the amount of caffeine present in soft drink samples. This method is found to be effective as well as appropriate for giving valuable information about the composition and quality of actual samples analyzed. The samples of food were prepared and analyzed by the standard addition method, which is effective in overcoming matrix effects and improves the accuracy of the quantification. For the electrochemical tests, food extracts were diluted 10-fold in 0.1 M buffer (pH 7.0) (PBS) to ensure good electrochemical conditions. Samples containing known amounts of carbaryl were prepared, and their electrochemical responses were measured following the same procedure used for the standard solutions. The sensor showed a high accuracy and reliability for the spiked carbaryl, with recovery rates ranging from 98.2% to 102.3% even in complex food matrices. These are within the acceptable analytical range, supporting the ability of the sensor to detect caffeine in real samples with little interference. Conclusions

V-type hexagonal ferrite nanoparticles (SrSnFe8O15-NPs) were successfully synthesized using a sol–gel green method involving fresh garlic extracts. The prepared nanoparticles were characterized using X-ray diffraction (XRD) and X-ray absorption fine structure (XAFS) to confirm the local atomic crystal structure. SEM studies confirmed the rough and porous morphology, and atomic force microscopy (AFM) showed the surface morphology. The soft magnetic behavior was confirmed using a vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM). The synthesized V-type hexagonal ferrites were employed to develop a modified SrSnFe8O15-NPs/GCE for the electrochemical sensing of caffeine. Cyclic voltammetry studies revealed conductive and diffusion-controlled behavior, with the SrSnFe8O15-NPs/GCE demonstrating high sensitivity and selectivity for caffeine detection (linear range: 0.5 to 80 µM, detection limit: 0.025 µM, and LOQ: 0.078 µM). The sensor was also successfully applied for cefixime detection in different commercial soft drinks, highlighting its potential for real-world analytical applications.

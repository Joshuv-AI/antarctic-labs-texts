Abstract
. This paper provides sufficient conditions for the solvability of a class of first-order evolution operators of Vekua-type on the product of a one-dimensional torus and a compact Lie group. The conditions are expressed in terms of the time-dependent coefficients and the spectral behavior of a normalized left-invariant vector field on the group. The three-sphere case is discussed in detail, leading to more explicit criteria, and the main results are further extended to operators defined on finite products of compact Lie groups.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{T}\simeq\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
be the one-dimensional torus and let
G
be a compact Lie group. This paper is concerned with global solvability on
\mathbb{T}\times G
for a class of first-order evolution operators of the form

(1.1)

Pu=\partial_{t}u-C(t)Xu-A(t)u-B(t)\overline{u},

where
A,B,C\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
X
is a normalized left-invariant vector field on
G
.

The operator
P
in (
1.1
) belongs to the class of Vekua-type equations, introduced by I. N. Vekua
[
13
]
in his foundational work on generalized analytic functions. From a conceptual viewpoint, these equations extend the Cauchy–Riemann system by allowing a coupling between
u
and its complex conjugate, a mechanism that both enriches the analytic structure and is unavoidable in several applications. In the classical literature this framework appears, for instance, in models from the theory of elastic shells and in the study of infinitesimal bendings of surfaces, where conjugation-type terms naturally encode geometric constraints. In a different direction, Vekua-type operators and their modern variants provide an effective language for representing and factorizing solutions of fundamental equations of mathematical physics: in particular, as developed by Kravchenko and collaborators (see
[
11
]
and references therein), suitable Vekua formulations lead to explicit integral representations and reduction procedures for equations such as the Schrödinger and Dirac equations. Moreover, the subsequent contributions of Bers
[
2
]
and many others established a robust theory of generalized analytic functions and pseudoanalytic formalism, with further applications in hydrodynamics and elasticity theory.

Our goal is to identify explicit, verifiable conditions on the coefficients in (
1.1
) that guarantee the following notion of solvability: for every
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
there exists a solution
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
of
Pu=f
. The key difficulty lies in the interaction between the time-dependent coefficients and the representation-theoretic spectrum of
X
, together with the small-divisor phenomena created by the periodicity in
t
and the presence of the conjugation term.

The present work extends, in two directions, previous solvability results for Vekua-type equations on tori. On
\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, solvability can be studied by partial Fourier series in the space variables, reducing the PDE to families of
2\times 2
ODE systems whose periodic boundary conditions lead to arithmetic constraints (see
[
1
,
3
,
7
,
8
,
4
]
). We show that an analogous strategy remains effective on a general compact Lie group
G
, provided one replaces the classical Fourier modes by matrix-valued Fourier coefficients indexed by irreducible unitary representations. This allows us to incorporate the symbol of a left-invariant vector field
X
into the analysis and to obtain conditions that are stable under the passage from scalar to matrix Fourier coefficients.

Following the approach in
[
1
]
, we consider operators of the form

Pu=\partial_{t}u-(p_{0}+i\lambda q(t))Xu-(s(t)+i\delta q(t))u-\alpha q(t)\overline{u},

where
p_{0},\lambda,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, and
q,s\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R})
with
q\geq 0
and
q\not\equiv 0
.

Our main theorem gives sufficient conditions for
C^{\infty}
-solvability in terms of: (i) a non-degeneracy requirement ensuring that the diagonalization of the associated
2\times 2
systems is well-defined; (ii) a global nonresonance condition excluding exact periodic obstructions; and (iii) a quantitative lower bound of Diophantine type controlling the denominators produced by the periodic boundary condition. These conditions depend on the averaged coefficients (integrals over one period) and on the spectral data
\{\mu_{m}(\xi)\}
of the symbol
\sigma_{X}(\xi)
in each irreducible representation
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
.

We also treat separately the special case
\lambda=0
, where the relevant spectral parameter becomes independent of the representation and one obtains a simpler set of criteria. As an application, we specialize the general results to
G=\mathbb{S}^{3}\simeq\mathrm{SU}(2)
, where the Fourier theory is particularly concrete and the spectrum of
X
can be described explicitly; this yields a transparent formulation of the solvability conditions in terms of the half-integer parameter
\ell
and the weights
m\in\{-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
. Finally, we extend the solvability theory to the case where
G
is a finite product of compact Lie groups and the drift term involves a sum of left-invariant vector fields acting on different factors, recovering as a corollary a compact-group analogue of earlier torus results.

Finally, note that when
\alpha=0
the conjugation term vanishes and the equation is no longer of Vekua type; global solvability and hypoellipticity on compact Lie groups in this setting are treated in
[
6
,
10
,
9
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the basic facts on Fourier analysis and symbols on compact Lie groups and present the partial Fourier series framework on
\mathbb{T}\times G
, including the characterization of smooth functions and distributions in terms of decay/growth of their partial Fourier coefficients. Section
3
performs a reduction to a convenient normal form for the drift term. Section
4
contains the main solvability theorem and its proof. In Section
5
we discuss the case
\lambda=0
and provide simplified criteria. Section
6
treats the example
G=\mathbb{S}^{3}
, and Section
7
extends the results to products of compact Lie groups.

2.
Overview of harmonic analysis and symbols on compact Lie groups

In this section we collect the basic facts from harmonic analysis on compact Lie groups and the global symbolic calculus that will be used throughout the paper. We also introduce the partial Fourier series on
\mathbb{T}\times G
and recall the characterization of smooth functions and distributions in terms of their partial Fourier coefficients. Background material can be found, for instance, in
[
12
,
5
]
.

2.1.
Fourier analysis on compact Lie groups

Let
G
be a compact Lie group of dimension
d=\dim G
, endowed with the normalized Haar measure
\mathrm{d}x
.
We denote by
\widehat{G}
the unitary dual of
G
, i.e., the set of equivalence classes
[\xi]
of continuous, irreducible, unitary representations
\xi:G\to\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}\times d_{\xi}}
, where
d_{\xi}=\dim\xi
. As usual,
\xi
and
\psi
are equivalent if there exists a unitary
U
such that
U\xi(x)=\psi(x)U
for all
x\in G
.

For
f\in L^{1}(G)
, the (group) Fourier transform of
f
at
\xi
is the matrix

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{G}f(x)\,\xi(x)^{*}\,\mathrm{d}x\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}\times d_{\xi}}.

By the Peter–Weyl theorem, the family

\mathcal{B}=\Bigl\{\sqrt{d_{\xi}}\,\xi_{ij}:\ [\xi]\in\widehat{G},\ 1\leq i,j\leq d_{\xi}\Bigr\}

is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(G)
(after fixing one representative in each class). Consequently, every
f\in L^{2}(G)
admits the Fourier series expansion

f(x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\,\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\xi(x)\widehat{f}(\xi)\bigr),

with convergence in
L^{2}(G)
, and the Plancherel identity holds:

\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\,\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2},\quad\text{ with }\|\widehat{f}(\xi)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)^{*}\bigr).

For a distribution
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G)
we define its Fourier transform by

\widehat{u}(\xi)_{ij}=\langle u,\overline{\xi_{ji}}\rangle,\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq d_{\xi},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes distributional duality.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{G}
be the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
G
. For each
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
the matrix coefficients
\xi_{ij}
are eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}_{G}
with the same eigenvalue; we write

(2.1)

-\mathcal{L}_{G}\,\xi_{ij}(x)=\nu_{[\xi]}\,\xi_{ij}(x),\qquad\nu_{[\xi]}\geq 0.

We set the standard weight

\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+\nu_{[\xi]})^{1/2},

which corresponds to the eigenvalues of
(I-\mathcal{L}_{G})^{1/2}
.

2.2.
Symbols and left-invariant vector fields

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
. For
X\in\mathfrak{g}
we denote by
L_{X}
the associated left-invariant vector field acting on
f\in C^{\infty}(G)
by

(L_{X}f)(x):=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=0}f(x\exp(tX)).

It commutes with left translations:
L_{X}\pi_{L}(y)=\pi_{L}(y)L_{X}
for all
y\in G
, where
(\pi_{L}(y)f)(x)=f(y^{-1}x)
.
For simplicity we often write
Xf
in place of
L_{X}f
.

Following the global symbolic calculus on compact groups, for a continuous linear operator

P:C^{\infty}(G)\to C^{\infty}(G)
its (matrix) symbol at
(x,\xi)\in G\times\mathrm{Rep}(G)
is

\sigma_{P}(x,\xi):=\xi(x)^{*}(P\xi)(x)\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}\times d_{\xi}},

where
(P\xi)(x)_{ij}:=P(\xi_{ij})(x)
.
Then, for
f\in C^{\infty}(G)
,

Pf(x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\,\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\xi(x)\,\sigma_{P}(x,\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi)\bigr).

If
P
is left-invariant, then
\sigma_{P}(x,\xi)
is independent of
x
and we simply write
\sigma_{P}(\xi)
; in this case,

\widehat{Pf}(\xi)=\sigma_{P}(\xi)\,\widehat{f}(\xi),\qquad\forall\,[\xi]\in\widehat{G},

and by duality the same identity holds for
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G)
. In particular, (
2.1
) yields

\sigma_{\mathcal{L}_{G}}(\xi)=-\nu_{[\xi]}I_{d_{\xi}}
.

For each
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, the operator
iX
is symmetric on
L^{2}(G)
.
Hence, for every class
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
we may choose a basis of the representation space so that

\sigma_{iX}(\xi)
is diagonal with real entries
\mu_{1}(\xi),\dots,\mu_{d_{\xi}}(\xi)\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,

\sigma_{X}(\xi)_{mn}=i\,\mu_{m}(\xi)\,\delta_{mn}.

Moreover, the eigenvalues
\{\mu_{m}(\xi)\}_{m=1}^{d_{\xi}}
are invariants of the equivalence class
[\xi]
.
Since
-(\mathcal{L}_{G}-X^{2})
is a positive operator and commutes with
X^{2}
, one obtains the standard estimate

|\mu_{m}(\xi)|\leq\|X\|\,\langle\xi\rangle,\qquad 1\leq m\leq d_{\xi}.

Throughout the paper we assume that the left-invariant vector fields under consideration are normalized, i.e.,
\|X\|=1
, where
\|\cdot\|
is the norm induced by the Killing form.

2.3.
Partial Fourier series

Let
\mathbb{T}\simeq\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and consider the product manifold
\mathbb{T}\times G
.
For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
and
\xi\in\mathrm{Rep}(G)
, we define the partial Fourier coefficient with respect to
G
by

\widehat{f}(t,\xi)=\int_{G}f(t,x)\,\xi(x)^{*}\,\mathrm{d}x\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}\times d_{\xi}},\qquad t\in\mathbb{T},

whose entries are

\widehat{f}(t,\xi)_{mn}=\int_{G}f(t,x)\,\overline{\xi_{nm}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad 1\leq m,n\leq d_{\xi}.

Formally (and in the appropriate distributional sense) one has the partial Fourier series

f(t,x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\,\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\xi(x)\,\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\bigr).

If
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
, we define its partial Fourier coefficient by requiring that, for each
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
and

1\leq m,n\leq d_{\xi}
, the component
\widehat{u}(\cdot,\xi)_{mn}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
satisfies

\langle\widehat{u}(\cdot,\xi)_{mn},\psi\rangle=\langle u,\psi\otimes\overline{\xi_{nm}}\rangle,\qquad\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}),

where
(\psi\otimes\overline{\xi_{nm}})(t,x):=\psi(t)\overline{\xi_{nm}(x)}
.

The decay/growth properties of these partial Fourier coefficients characterize smoothness and distributional regularity on
\mathbb{T}\times G
(see
[
5
]
).

Theorem 2.1

(Characterization of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
)
.

A function
f
belongs to
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
if and only if for every
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
and
1\leq m,n\leq d_{\xi}
,
the coefficient
\widehat{f}(\cdot,\xi)_{mn}
belongs to
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and, for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and every
\ell>0
,
there exists
C_{\beta,\ell}>0
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\partial_{t}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)_{mn}\bigr|\leq C_{\beta,\ell}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{-\ell},\qquad\forall\,[\xi]\in\widehat{G},\ 1\leq m,n\leq d_{\xi}.

Theorem 2.2

(Characterization of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
)
.

A distribution
u
belongs to
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
if and only if there exist
K\in\mathbb{N}
and
C>0
such that

\bigl|\langle\widehat{u}(\cdot,\xi)_{mn},\varphi\rangle\bigr|\leq C\,p_{K}(\varphi)\,\langle\xi\rangle^{K},\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}),\ \forall\,[\xi]\in\widehat{G},\ 1\leq m,n\leq d_{\xi},

where

p_{K}(\varphi):=\sum_{\beta=0}^{K}\|\partial_{t}^{\beta}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}.

We conclude with a combinatorial identity that will be used to control higher-order derivatives of integrating factors.

Proposition 2.3

(Faà di Bruno formula)
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{N}}{\mathrm{d}t^{N}}e^{f(t)}=e^{f(t)}\sum_{\gamma\in\Delta(N)}\frac{N!}{\gamma!}\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{\ell!}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{\ell}}{\mathrm{d}t^{\ell}}f(t)\right)^{\gamma_{\ell}},

where

\Delta(N)=\left\{\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{N})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}:\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\ell\,\gamma_{\ell}=N\right\},\qquad\gamma!:=\gamma_{1}!\cdots\gamma_{N}!.

3.
Reduction to a normal form

In this section we reduce a part of our operator to a convenient normal form by an explicit conjugation acting diagonally on the partial Fourier coefficients. We keep the notation and functional framework introduced in Section
2
. In particular, for each class
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
we fix a representative such that
\sigma_{X}(\xi)=\operatorname{diag}(i\mu_{1}(\xi),\dots,i\mu_{d_{\xi}}(\xi)),
with
\mu_{j}(\xi)\in\mathbb{R}.

We start with the evolution operator
L:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
defined by

Lu=\partial_{t}u-(p(t)+i\lambda q(t))Xu,

where
p,q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R})
,
q\not\equiv 0
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Let

p_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}p(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\mathcal{P}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}p(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau-p_{0}t,

and consider the operator

L_{0}=\partial_{t}-(p_{0}+i\lambda q(t))X.

The next proposition shows that
L
and
L_{0}
are conjugate by a Fourier multiplier depending only on
\mathcal{P}
; in particular, solvability can be studied for
L_{0}
without loss of generality.

Proposition 3.1

(Kirilov, de Moraes, and Ruzhansky, 2021)
.

Consider the operator
\Psi
defined by

\Psi u(t,x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\sum_{m,n=1}^{d_{\xi}}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)\mathcal{P}(t)}\,\widehat{u}(t,\xi)_{mn}\,\xi_{nm}(x).

Then:

(a)

\Psi
is an automorphism of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
;

(b)

L_{0}\circ\Psi=\Psi\circ L
;

(c)

L
is solvable if and only if
L_{0}
is solvable.

Proof.

The argument is analogous to Propositions 4.6–4.8 in
[
5
]
(presented there for
G=\mathbb{S}^{3}
). The extension to an arbitrary compact Lie group follows from the same partial Fourier series framework and the diagonal form of
\sigma_{X}(\xi)
fixed above.
∎

In view of Proposition
3.1
, it is enough to work with the normal form

(3.1)

L=\partial_{t}-(p_{0}+i\lambda q(t))X,

where
p_{0},\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R})
,
q\not\equiv 0
.

Henceforth we assume that
q
does not change sign on
\mathbb{T}
. In particular,

q_{0}:=\int_{0}^{2\pi}q(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\neq 0.

Without loss of generality we take
q(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{T}
, so that
q_{0}>0
.

This choice implies that
Q(t)=\int_{0}^{t}q(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau
is nondecreasing and
\widetilde{Q}(t)=-\int_{t}^{2\pi}q(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau
is nonincreasing, a fact that will be repeatedly used to control the exponential weights arising in the variation-of-constants formulas.

4.
Solvability of a Vekua-type evolution operator

In this section we prove a global
C^{\infty}
-solvability result for a Vekua-type evolution operator on
\mathbb{T}\times G
. Consider the operator
P:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
defined by

(4.1)

Pu=Lu-(s(t)+i\delta q(t))u-\alpha q(t)\overline{u},

where
L
is given by (
3.1
),
s\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R})
,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.
Set

s_{0}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}s(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad A_{0}=s_{0}+i\delta q_{0},\qquad B_{0}=\alpha q_{0},\qquad C_{0}=2\pi p_{0}+i\lambda q_{0},

and for each
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
and
1\leq m\leq d_{\xi}
define

\rho_{m}(\xi)=\sqrt{(\lambda\mu_{m}(\xi)-i\delta)^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}},\qquad\text{with }\operatorname{Re}\rho_{m}(\xi)\geq 0.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that the operator
P
in (
4.1
) satisfies:

(I)

|\alpha|\neq|\delta|
;

(II)

For all
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
and
1\leq m\leq d_{\xi}
, there is no integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

\begin{cases}\operatorname{Re}\bigl(A_{0}(2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)\overline{C_{0}})\bigr)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
|2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)C_{0}|^{2}=|A_{0}|^{2}-|B_{0}|^{2};\end{cases}

(III)

There exists
M>0
such that, for all
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
with
\langle\xi\rangle\geq M
,

\min\left\{\left|e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}}-e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\right|,\,\left|1-e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}+i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\right|\right\}\geq\langle\xi\rangle^{-M},

for every
1\leq m\leq d_{\xi}
.

Then for every
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
such that
Pu=f
.

Proof.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
and assume that
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
satisfies
Pu=f
, where
P
is given by (
4.1
). Taking partial Fourier coefficients in the
G
-variable, for each
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
, we obtain

(4.2)

\partial_{t}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-(p_{0}+i\lambda q(t))\sigma_{X}(\xi)\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-(s(t)+i\delta q(t))\widehat{u}(t,\xi)-\alpha q(t)\widehat{\overline{u}}(t,\xi)=\widehat{f}(t,\xi).

Since
\sigma_{X}(\xi)=\mathrm{diag}(i\mu_{1}(\xi),\dots,i\mu_{d_{\xi}}(\xi))
, the
(m,n)
-entry of (
4.2
) reads, for
1\leq m,n\leq d_{\xi}
,

(4.3)

\partial_{t}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)_{mn}-\Bigl((p_{0}+i\lambda q(t))\,i\mu_{m}(\xi)+s(t)+i\delta q(t)\Bigr)\widehat{u}(t,\xi)_{mn}-\alpha q(t)\widehat{\overline{u}}(t,\xi)_{mn}=\widehat{f}(t,\xi)_{mn}.

At this point we couple (
4.3
) with the corresponding equation for
\widehat{\overline{u}}(t,\xi)_{mn}
. We use that

\widehat{\overline{u}}(t,\xi)=\overline{\widehat{u}(t,\overline{\xi})}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{X}(\overline{\xi})=\overline{\sigma_{X}(\xi)},

so that taking the complex conjugate of the equation for
\overline{\xi}
yields a second relation with the same
\mu_{m}(\xi)
. Define

w(t,\xi)_{mn}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)_{mn}\\[2.84526pt]
\widehat{\overline{u}}(t,\xi)_{mn}\end{bmatrix},\qquad F(t,\xi)_{mn}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)_{mn}\\[2.84526pt]
\widehat{\overline{f}}(t,\xi)_{mn}\end{bmatrix}.

Then (
4.3
) and its conjugate can be written as the
2\times 2
system

w^{\prime}(t,\xi)_{mn}=M(t,\xi)_{m}\,w(t,\xi)_{mn}+F(t,\xi)_{mn},

where

M(t,\xi)_{m}=\begin{bmatrix}(p_{0}+i\lambda q(t))\,i\mu_{m}(\xi)+s(t)+i\delta q(t)&\alpha q(t)\\
\overline{\alpha}q(t)&(p_{0}-i\lambda q(t))\,i\mu_{m}(\xi)+s(t)-i\delta q(t)\end{bmatrix}.

Let
S(t)=\int_{0}^{t}s(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau
and set

(4.4)

y(t,\xi)_{mn}=e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t-S(t)}\,w(t,\xi)_{mn}.

A direct differentiation shows that
y
satisfies

(4.5)

y^{\prime}(t,\xi)_{mn}=q(t)\,\widetilde{M}(\xi)_{m}\,y(t,\xi)_{mn}+e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t-S(t)}F(t,\xi)_{mn},

where

\widetilde{M}(\xi)_{m}=\begin{bmatrix}-\lambda\mu_{m}(\xi)+i\delta&\alpha\\
\overline{\alpha}&\lambda\mu_{m}(\xi)-i\delta\end{bmatrix}.

The periodicity in
t
now appears as a twisted boundary condition for
y
. Indeed, since
\widehat{u}(\cdot,\xi)_{mn}
is a distribution on
\mathbb{T}
, it is
2\pi
-periodic, hence
w(\cdot,\xi)_{mn}
is
2\pi
-periodic. Using (
4.4
) and
S(2\pi)=s_{0}
we obtain

(4.6)

y(0,\xi)_{mn}=e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\,y(2\pi,\xi)_{mn}.

The eigenvalues of
\widetilde{M}(\xi)_{m}
are
\pm\rho_{m}(\xi)
, where

\rho_{m}(\xi)=\sqrt{(\lambda\mu_{m}(\xi)-i\delta)^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}},\qquad\operatorname{Re}\rho_{m}(\xi)\geq 0.

Assumption (I) implies
\rho_{m}(\xi)\neq 0
for all
([\xi],m)
, hence
\widetilde{M}(\xi)_{m}
is diagonalizable, with eigenvectors given by

V^{\pm}(\xi)_{m}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha\\
(\lambda\mu_{m}(\xi)-i\delta)\pm\rho_{m}(\xi)\end{bmatrix}.

Set

T(\xi)_{m}=\begin{bmatrix}V^{+}(\xi)_{m}&V^{-}(\xi)_{m}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\alpha\\
(\lambda\mu_{m}(\xi)-i\delta)+\rho_{m}(\xi)&(\lambda\mu_{m}(\xi)-i\delta)-\rho_{m}(\xi)\end{bmatrix}.

Then

(4.7)

T(\xi)_{m}^{-1}\widetilde{M}(\xi)_{m}T(\xi)_{m}=\rho_{m}(\xi)\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix},

and one checks that

T(\xi)_{m}^{-1}=\frac{-1}{2\alpha\rho_{m}(\xi)}\begin{bmatrix}(\lambda\mu_{m}(\xi)-i\delta)-\rho_{m}(\xi)&-\alpha\\
-(\lambda\mu_{m}(\xi)-i\delta)-\rho_{m}(\xi)&\alpha\end{bmatrix}.

Now set

z(t,\xi)_{mn}=T(\xi)_{m}^{-1}y(t,\xi)_{mn},

and

G(t,\xi)_{mn}=T(\xi)_{m}^{-1}F(t,\xi)_{mn}=\begin{bmatrix}G_{1}(t,\xi)_{mn}\\
G_{2}(t,\xi)_{mn}\end{bmatrix}.

Using (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), we obtain the decoupled system

(4.8)

\begin{cases}z_{1}^{\prime}(t,\xi)_{mn}=\rho_{m}(\xi)q(t)\,z_{1}(t,\xi)_{mn}+e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t-S(t)}G_{1}(t,\xi)_{mn},\\
z_{2}^{\prime}(t,\xi)_{mn}=-\rho_{m}(\xi)q(t)\,z_{2}(t,\xi)_{mn}+e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t-S(t)}G_{2}(t,\xi)_{mn}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, since
T(\xi)_{m}
is independent of
t
, the boundary condition (
4.6
) becomes

(4.9)

z(0,\xi)_{mn}=e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\,z(2\pi,\xi)_{mn}.

We now solve (
4.8
) explicitly and determine the constants through (
4.9
). Let

Q(t)=\int_{0}^{t}q(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad\widetilde{Q}(t)=-\int_{t}^{2\pi}q(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

so that
Q(0)=0
,
Q(2\pi)=q_{0}
,
\widetilde{Q}(0)=-q_{0}
,
\widetilde{Q}(2\pi)=0
, and
\widetilde{Q}(t)\leq 0
. Solving by variation of constants, we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle z_{1}(t,\xi)_{mn}

\displaystyle=-e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}\int_{t}^{2\pi}e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(\sigma)}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\sigma-S(\sigma)}G_{1}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+K_{1}(\xi)_{mn}e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)},

(4.11)

\displaystyle z_{2}(t,\xi)_{mn}

\displaystyle=e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)Q(t)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)Q(\sigma)}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\sigma-S(\sigma)}G_{2}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+K_{2}(\xi)_{mn}e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)Q(t)}.

Imposing (
4.9
) leads to linear equations for
K_{1}(\xi)_{mn}
and
K_{2}(\xi)_{mn}
, namely

(4.12)

\left(e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}}-e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\right)K_{1}(\xi)_{mn}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)(q_{0}+\widetilde{Q}(\sigma))}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\sigma-S(\sigma)}G_{1}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

and

(4.13)

\left(1-e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}+i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\right)K_{2}(\xi)_{mn}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)(Q(\sigma)-q_{0})}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\sigma-S(\sigma)}G_{2}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

At this stage, the only potential obstruction arises from the denominators in (
4.12
)–(
4.13
). Condition (II) eliminates exact resonances (i.e., zeros of these denominators), while condition (III) is employed later to manage small denominators as
\langle\xi\rangle\to\infty
. We now clarify the resonance exclusion in a more explicit manner.

Assume first that

e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}}-e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}=0.

Since
q\geq 0
and
q\not\equiv 0
, we have
q_{0}>0
. Then, there exists an integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}=i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}+i2\pi k,
hence

\rho_{m}(\xi)=-\,\frac{s_{0}+i2\pi(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}+k)}{q_{0}}.

Similarly, if

1-e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}+i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}=0,

then
-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}+i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}=i2\pi k
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, i.e.

\rho_{m}(\xi)=\frac{s_{0}+i2\pi(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}-k)}{q_{0}}.

In both cases we can write, after possibly replacing
k
by
-k
, that

(4.14)

\rho_{m}(\xi)=\pm\frac{s_{0}+i2\pi(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}+k)}{q_{0}},

so that

\operatorname{Re}\rho_{m}(\xi)=\pm\frac{s_{0}}{q_{0}},\qquad\operatorname{Im}\rho_{m}(\xi)=\pm\frac{2\pi(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}+k)}{q_{0}}.

On the other hand, by definition,

(\rho_{m}(\xi))^{2}=(\lambda\mu_{m}(\xi)-i\delta)^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}=\lambda^{2}\mu_{m}(\xi)^{2}-\delta^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}-i\,2\lambda\delta\,\mu_{m}(\xi).

Writing
\rho_{m}(\xi)=a+ib
with
a=\operatorname{Re}\rho_{m}(\xi)
and
b=\operatorname{Im}\rho_{m}(\xi)
, we have

(\rho_{m}(\xi))^{2}=(a+ib)^{2}=(a^{2}-b^{2})+i(2ab).

Therefore,

(4.15)

\begin{cases}2ab=-2\lambda\delta\,\mu_{m}(\xi),\\[5.69054pt]
a^{2}-b^{2}=\lambda^{2}\mu_{m}(\xi)^{2}-\delta^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}.\end{cases}

Substituting the values of
a
and
b
from (
4.14
) into
(
4.15
) gives

(4.16)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle 2\Bigl(\pm\frac{s_{0}}{q_{0}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\pm\frac{2\pi(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}+k)}{q_{0}}\Bigr)=-2\lambda\delta\,\mu_{m}(\xi),\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\Bigl(\pm\frac{s_{0}}{q_{0}}\Bigr)^{2}-\Bigl(\pm\frac{2\pi(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}+k)}{q_{0}}\Bigr)^{2}=\lambda^{2}\mu_{m}(\xi)^{2}-\delta^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}.\end{cases}

Since the signs cancel in the first line, (
4.16
) is
equivalent to

(4.17)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{s_{0}\,2\pi(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}+k)}{q_{0}^{2}}=-\lambda\delta\,\mu_{m}(\xi),\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\frac{s_{0}^{2}-\bigl(2\pi(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}+k)\bigr)^{2}}{q_{0}^{2}}=\lambda^{2}\mu_{m}(\xi)^{2}-\delta^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}.\end{cases}

Now recall
A_{0}=s_{0}+i\delta q_{0}
,
B_{0}=\alpha q_{0}
, and
C_{0}=2\pi p_{0}+i\lambda q_{0}
.
A direct expansion gives

A_{0}\bigl(2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)\overline{C_{0}}\bigr)=(s_{0}+i\delta q_{0})\bigl(2\pi k+2\pi\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}-i\lambda q_{0}\mu_{m}(\xi)\bigr),

and hence

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(A_{0}(2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)\overline{C_{0}})\right)=s_{0}\,2\pi(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}+k)+\lambda\delta\,\mu_{m}(\xi)\,q_{0}^{2}.

Using the first identity in (
4.17
), we obtain

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(A_{0}(2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)\overline{C_{0}})\right)=0.

Likewise,

|2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)C_{0}|^{2}=\bigl(2\pi k+2\pi\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\bigr)^{2}+\lambda^{2}\mu_{m}(\xi)^{2}q_{0}^{2},

with

|A_{0}|^{2}=s_{0}^{2}+\delta^{2}q_{0}^{2},\qquad|B_{0}|^{2}=|\alpha|^{2}q_{0}^{2}.

Multiplying the second identity in (
4.17
) by
q_{0}^{2}
yields

\bigl(2\pi k+2\pi\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\bigr)^{2}+\lambda^{2}\mu_{m}(\xi)^{2}q_{0}^{2}=s_{0}^{2}+\delta^{2}q_{0}^{2}-|\alpha|^{2}q_{0}^{2}=|A_{0}|^{2}-|B_{0}|^{2},

that is,

|2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)C_{0}|^{2}=|A_{0}|^{2}-|B_{0}|^{2}.

We have therefore produced an integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying exactly the system
in (II), contradicting the hypothesis. This proves that neither denominator in
(
4.12
)–(
4.13
) can vanish.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle K_{1}(\xi)_{mn}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)(q_{0}+\widetilde{Q}(\sigma))}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\sigma-S(\sigma)}}{e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}}-e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}}G_{1}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,

\displaystyle K_{2}(\xi)_{mn}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)(Q(\sigma)-q_{0})}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\sigma-S(\sigma)}}{1-e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}+i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}}G_{2}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

We now reconstruct
\widehat{u}
and explain how the smoothness of
u
follows from estimates on the coefficients.

Since
w=e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t+S(t)}\,T(\xi)_{m}\,z
, we have

(4.18)

\widehat{u}(t,\xi)_{mn}=\alpha\,e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t+S(t)}\Bigl(z_{1}(t,\xi)_{mn}+z_{2}(t,\xi)_{mn}\Bigr).

By the characterization of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
in terms of partial Fourier coefficients (Theorem
2.1
), it is enough to show that for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and every
\ell>0
there exists
C_{\beta,\ell}>0
such that

(4.19)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\partial_{t}^{\beta}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)_{mn}\bigr|\leq C_{\beta,\ell}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{-\ell},\qquad\forall\,[\xi]\in\widehat{G},\ 1\leq m,n\leq d_{\xi}.

Using Leibniz’ rule in (
4.18
), it suffices to control derivatives of the exponential factor and of
z_{1}+z_{2}
.

To estimate the exponential factor, we write

e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t+S(t)}=e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t}\,e^{S(t)},\qquad S(t)=\int_{0}^{t}s(\tau)\,d\tau.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, Leibniz’ rule yields

(4.20)

\partial_{t}^{r}\!\left(e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t+S(t)}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}(i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0})^{\,r-j}\,e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t}\,\partial_{t}^{\,j}\!\left(e^{S(t)}\right).

We now use Faà di Bruno (Proposition
2.3
) in the form

(4.21)

\partial_{t}^{\,j}\!\left(e^{S(t)}\right)=e^{S(t)}\sum_{\gamma\in\Delta(j)}\frac{j!}{\gamma!}\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}\left(\frac{1}{\ell!}\,\partial_{t}^{\,\ell}S(t)\right)^{\gamma_{\ell}},\qquad j\geq 1,

where
\Delta(j)=\{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{j}:\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\ell\gamma_{\ell}=j\}
.
Since
s\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, every derivative
\partial_{t}^{\ell}S(t)
is bounded on
\mathbb{T}
; hence for each
j
there exists
C_{j}>0
such that

(4.22)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\left|\partial_{t}^{\,j}\!\left(e^{S(t)}\right)\right|\leq C_{j}.

Using the normalization bound
|\mu_{m}(\xi)|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle
and combining
(
4.20
)–(
4.22
), we obtain: for each
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists
C_{r}>0
such that

(4.23)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\left|\partial_{t}^{r}e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t+S(t)}\right|\leq C_{r}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{r}.

Consequently, to prove (
4.19
) it is enough to show that for each
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and each
\ell>0
,

(4.24)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\partial_{t}^{\beta}z_{j}(t,\xi)_{mn}\bigr|\leq C_{\beta,\ell}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{-\ell},\qquad j=1,2.

We next obtain the decay properties of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
appearing in the decoupled system
(
4.8
). Recall that
G=T^{-1}F
, where
F=(\widehat{f},\widehat{\overline{f}})^{T}
.
From
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
and Theorem
2.1
, we have: for every

\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and every
N>0
there exists
C_{\beta,N}>0
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{t}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)_{mn}|\leq C_{\beta,N}\langle\xi\rangle^{-N}.

Moreover, as recorded earlier,
|\rho_{m}(\xi)|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle
and
|\rho_{m}(\xi)|^{-1}\lesssim 1
,
so the entries of
T(\xi)_{m}^{-1}
have at most polynomial growth in
\langle\xi\rangle
. It follows that

G_{1}
and
G_{2}
inherit rapid decay: for each
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
N>0
there exists
C_{\beta,N}>0

such that

(4.25)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{t}^{\beta}G_{j}(t,\xi)_{mn}|\leq C_{\beta,N}\langle\xi\rangle^{-N},\qquad j=1,2.

We now estimate the derivatives of the integrating factors in (
4.10
)–(
4.11
).
We treat
e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}
; the bound for
e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)Q(t)}
is analogous. Since
\rho_{m}(\xi)

is independent of
t
, Faà di Bruno applied to
t\mapsto\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)
gives, for
r\geq 1
,

\partial_{t}^{\,r}\!\left(e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}\right)=e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}\sum_{\gamma\in\Delta(r)}\frac{r!}{\gamma!}\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{\ell!}\,\partial_{t}^{\,\ell}(\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t))\right)^{\gamma_{\ell}}.

Since
\partial_{t}^{\ell}(\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t))=\rho_{m}(\xi)\partial_{t}^{\ell}\widetilde{Q}(t)
, we can factor out
powers of
\rho_{m}(\xi)
and use
|\gamma|:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\gamma_{\ell}\leq r
to obtain

\left|\partial_{t}^{\,r}\!\left(e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}\right)\right|\leq|e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}|\sum_{\gamma\in\Delta(r)}C\,|\rho_{m}(\xi)|^{|\gamma|}\leq C_{r}\,|e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}|\,\langle\xi\rangle^{r}.

Finally,
\widetilde{Q}(t)\leq 0
and
\operatorname{Re}\rho_{m}(\xi)\geq 0
imply
|e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}|\leq 1
, hence

(4.26)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\left|\partial_{t}^{\,r}e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}\right|\leq C_{r}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{r},\qquad r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

We now prove (
4.24
) for
z_{1}
; the estimate for
z_{2}
follows by the same arguments with

Q
in place of
\widetilde{Q}
. Decompose
z_{1}=z_{1,a}+z_{1,b}
, where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}z_{1,a}(t,\xi)_{mn}&=-e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}\int_{t}^{2\pi}e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(\sigma)}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\sigma-S(\sigma)}G_{1}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,d\sigma,\\[2.84526pt]
z_{1,b}(t,\xi)_{mn}&=K_{1}(\xi)_{mn}e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}.\end{cases}

Fix
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Differentiating
z_{1,a}
under the integral sign, we obtain

(4.27)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\beta}z_{1,a}(t,\xi)_{mn}=

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}^{\,\beta}\!\left(e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}\right)\int_{t}^{2\pi}H(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,d\sigma.

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{r}\,\partial_{t}^{\,\beta-r}\!\left(e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}\right)\,\partial_{t}^{\,r-1}H(t,\xi)_{mn},

where

H(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}:=e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(\sigma)}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\sigma-S(\sigma)}G_{1}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}.

We first bound the integral term in (
4.27
). For
\sigma\in[t,2\pi]
we have

\widetilde{Q}(\sigma)\leq\widetilde{Q}(t)
, hence
\widetilde{Q}(t)-\widetilde{Q}(\sigma)\leq 0
, and therefore

\left|e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)(\widetilde{Q}(t)-\widetilde{Q}(\sigma))}\right|\leq 1,

noticing that
\operatorname{Re}\rho_{m}(\xi)\geq 0
.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t}^{2\pi}H(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{t}^{2\pi}e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(\sigma)}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}\sigma-S(\sigma)}G_{1}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{2\pi}e^{-S(\sigma)}\,|G_{1}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}|\,d\sigma\leq C\,\sup_{\sigma\in\mathbb{T}}|G_{1}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}|.

Using (
4.26
), we obtain

(4.28)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\left|\partial_{t}^{\,\beta}\!\left(e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}\right)\int_{t}^{2\pi}H(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}\,d\sigma\right|\leq C_{\beta}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{\beta}\,\sup_{\sigma\in\mathbb{T}}|G_{1}(\sigma,\xi)_{mn}|.

Next we estimate
\partial_{t}^{\,r-1}H(t,\xi)_{mn}
in (
4.27
). By Leibniz’ rule,

(4.29)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\,r-1}H(t,\xi)_{mn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\binom{r-1}{j}\,\partial_{t}^{\,j}\!\left(e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t-S(t)}\right)\,\partial_{t}^{\,r-1-j}G_{1}(t,\xi)_{mn}.

The derivatives of
e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t-S(t)}
satisfy the same polynomial bounds as in
(
4.23
), and the derivatives of
e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}
are controlled by
(
4.26
) (the sign does not change the estimate). Hence there exists
C_{j}>0
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\left|\partial_{t}^{\,j}\!\left(e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}e^{-i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}t-S(t)}\right)\right|\leq C_{j}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{j}.

Substituting into (
4.29
) gives

(4.30)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{t}^{\,r-1}H(t,\xi)_{mn}|\leq C_{r}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{r-1}\,\sup_{0\leq j\leq r-1}\ \sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{t}^{\,j}G_{1}(t,\xi)_{mn}|.

Combining (
4.26
)–(
4.28
),
and (
4.30
), we conclude that, for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{t}^{\beta}z_{1,a}(t,\xi)_{mn}|\leq C_{\beta}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{\beta}\,\sup_{0\leq j\leq\beta}\ \sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{t}^{\,j}G_{1}(t,\xi)_{mn}|.

Using the rapid decay (
4.25
) and choosing
N
arbitrarily large, we obtain that

\partial_{t}^{\beta}z_{1,a}(t,\xi)_{mn}
is rapidly decaying in
\langle\xi\rangle
, uniformly in
t
.

It remains to estimate
z_{1,b}(t,\xi)_{mn}=K_{1}(\xi)_{mn}e^{\rho_{m}(\xi)\widetilde{Q}(t)}
.
From (
4.12
), the numerator is rapidly decaying by (
4.25
) and the boundedness
of the exponential weights. Condition (II) guarantees the denominator never vanishes; however it may become small for
large
\langle\xi\rangle
. This is precisely where (III) is used: for
\langle\xi\rangle
large enough,

\left|e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}}-e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\right|^{-1}\leq\langle\xi\rangle^{M}.

Hence
K_{1}(\xi)_{mn}
has at most polynomial growth, and choosing the decay order in (
4.25
) large
enough yields, for each
N>0
,

(4.31)

|K_{1}(\xi)_{mn}|\leq C_{N}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{-N}.

Combining (
4.26
) with (
4.31
) and Leibniz’ rule shows that, for every

\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and every
N>0
,

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{t}^{\beta}z_{1,b}(t,\xi)_{mn}|\leq C_{\beta,N}\langle\xi\rangle^{-N}.

Therefore
z_{1}=z_{1,a}+z_{1,b}
satisfies the rapid-decay estimate (
4.24
).

The estimate for
z_{2}
is obtained in the same way from (
4.11
) and (
4.13
),
using the second lower bound in (III). With (
4.24
) established for
j=1,2
, the reconstruction
formula (
4.18
) together with (
4.23
) yields (
4.19
).
Therefore
\widehat{u}(\cdot,\xi)_{mn}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
with rapid decay in
[\xi]
, and
Theorem
2.1
implies
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
and
Pu=f
.
\square

5.
The case
\lambda=0

In this section we specialize the previous solvability result to the case
\lambda=0
. In this case the spectral parameter
\rho_{m}(\xi)
becomes independent of
[\xi]
, and the solvability conditions admit a more explicit form.

When
\lambda=0
, the operator
P
reads

(5.1)

Pu=\partial_{t}u-p_{0}Xu-(s(t)+i\delta q(t))u-\alpha q(t)\overline{u},

with
p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
q,s\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R})
,
q\not\equiv 0
,
q\geq 0
,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.
We keep the notation

s_{0}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}s(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad q_{0}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}q(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad A_{0}=s_{0}+i\delta q_{0},\qquad B_{0}=\alpha q_{0},\qquad C_{0}=2\pi p_{0}.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that the operator
P
in (
5.1
) satisfies one of the following conditions:

(1)

|B_{0}|>|A_{0}|
;

(2)

|B_{0}|\leq|A_{0}|
,
|\alpha|>|\delta|
and, for all
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
and
1\leq m\leq d_{\xi}
, there is no integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

(5.2)

\begin{cases}\operatorname{Re}(A_{0}(k+\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}))=0,\\
4\pi^{2}|k+\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}|^{2}=|A_{0}|^{2}-|B_{0}|^{2};\end{cases}

(3)

|\alpha|<|\delta|
and
s_{0}\neq 0
;

(4)

|\alpha|<|\delta|
,
s_{0}=0
, for all
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
and
1\leq m\leq d_{\xi}
, there is no
k\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying (
5.2
), and the following Diophantine condition holds:

(DC)
There exists
M>0
such that for all
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
with
\langle\xi\rangle\geq M
,

|2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi-q_{0}\sqrt{\delta^{2}-|\alpha|^{2}}|\geq\langle\xi\rangle^{-M},\qquad 1\leq m\leq d_{\xi}.

Then, for every
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
, there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
such that
Pu=f
.

Proof.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
and suppose that

u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
satisfies
Pu=f
.
Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
,
we take partial Fourier coefficients with respect to
G
and
reduce the equation to a
2\times 2
system of ODEs for each

[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
and
1\leq m\leq d_{\xi}
.

When
\lambda=0
, the quantity

\rho_{m}(\xi)=\sqrt{(-i\delta)^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}}=\sqrt{|\alpha|^{2}-\delta^{2}}

is independent of
\xi
, and we simply write
\rho
.
As in the general case, the solution is obtained by variation of constants,
and periodicity leads to denominators of the form

e^{-\rho q_{0}}-e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad 1-e^{-\rho q_{0}+i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}.

Thus, global solvability reduces to controlling these quantities.

We first observe that if
|\alpha|\neq|\delta|
, then
\rho\neq 0
,
so no degeneracy occurs in the diagonalization procedure.
Moreover, the resonance system excluded in
(
5.2
) corresponds precisely to the
vanishing of the above denominators, as in the proof of
Theorem
4.1
.

If
|\alpha|>|\delta|
, then
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
.
In this case
e^{-\rho q_{0}}
is a positive real number.
The identity

e^{-\rho q_{0}}=e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}+i2\pi k}

would imply simultaneously that the imaginary part vanishes
and that

-\rho q_{0}=s_{0}+i2\pi(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}+k),

which yields exactly the system (
5.2
).
Hence, under the hypotheses excluding that system,
the denominators do not vanish.
Furthermore, if
|B_{0}|>|A_{0}|
, then

e^{-\rho q_{0}}\neq e^{s_{0}}
,
so the distance between these exponentials is bounded below by
a strictly positive constant independent of
[\xi]
.
The same conclusion follows when
|B_{0}|\leq|A_{0}|
but
(
5.2
) has no integer solution,
since the trivial representation would otherwise produce a contradiction.
In both situations we obtain a uniform bound

\left|e^{-\rho q_{0}}-e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\right|+\left|1-e^{-\rho q_{0}+i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\right|\geq C>0,

independent of
[\xi]
.

If instead
|\alpha|<|\delta|
, then
\rho=i\omega
with

\omega=\sqrt{\delta^{2}-|\alpha|^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}
,
so
|e^{-\rho q_{0}}|=1
.
If
s_{0}\neq 0
, then

|e^{-\rho q_{0}}-e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}|\geq|1-e^{s_{0}}|,

which is strictly positive.
Hence the denominators are again uniformly bounded away from zero.

The remaining situation occurs when
|\alpha|<|\delta|
and
s_{0}=0
.
In this case the denominators reduce to

|e^{i(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi-q_{0}\omega)}-1|,

up to harmless sign changes.
They may approach zero only if the phase

\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi-q_{0}\omega

approaches an integer multiple of
2\pi
.
The Diophantine condition (DC) prevents this phenomenon
by imposing the lower bound

|2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi-q_{0}\omega|\geq\langle\xi\rangle^{-M},

which is equivalent, by Lemma
5.2
,
to a polynomial lower bound on

|e^{i(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi-q_{0}\omega)}-1|
.
Consequently, the inverses of the denominators grow at most
polynomially in
\langle\xi\rangle
.

In all cases, therefore, the constants arising from periodicity
either remain uniformly bounded or have at most polynomial growth.
Since the forcing terms are rapidly decreasing in
\xi
,
the same estimates as in Theorem
4.1

show that the Fourier coefficients of
u

decay rapidly.
Hence
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
and
Pu=f
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The Diophantine condition
(DC)
is equivalent to the following:

(DC’)
There exists
M>0
such that

[\xi]\in\widehat{G},\ \langle\xi\rangle\geq M\ \Longrightarrow\ \left|e^{i(\mu_{m}(\xi)p_{0}2\pi-q_{0}\sqrt{\delta^{2}-|\alpha|^{2}})}-1\right|\geq\langle\xi\rangle^{-M},\qquad 1\leq m\leq d_{\xi}.

Proof.

Suppose first that (DC) fails. Then, for every
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
[\xi_{\nu}]\in\widehat{G}
,
m_{\nu}
and
k_{\nu}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\langle\xi_{\nu}\rangle>\nu
such that

|2\pi k_{\nu}+\mu_{m_{\nu}}(\xi_{\nu})p_{0}2\pi-q_{0}\omega|<\langle\xi_{\nu}\rangle^{-\nu}.

Using that
|e^{i\theta}-1|^{2}=2(1-\cos\theta),
and the inequality
1-\cos\theta\leq|\theta|
for
|\theta|\leq 1
, we obtain

|e^{i(\mu_{m_{\nu}}(\xi_{\nu})p_{0}2\pi-q_{0}\omega)}-1|^{2}<2\langle\xi_{\nu}\rangle^{-\nu},

which shows that (DC’) fails.

Conversely, suppose that (DC’) fails. Then there exist
[\xi_{\nu}]
and
m_{\nu}
such that

|e^{i(\mu_{m_{\nu}}(\xi_{\nu})p_{0}2\pi-q_{0}\omega)}-1|<\langle\xi_{\nu}\rangle^{-\nu}.

Let
k_{\nu}
be the integer closest to

\mu_{m_{\nu}}(\xi_{\nu})p_{0}-\frac{q_{0}\omega}{2\pi}.

Since
|e^{i\theta}-1|\to 0
implies that
\theta
approaches an integer multiple of
2\pi
, we obtain

|2\pi k_{\nu}+\mu_{m_{\nu}}(\xi_{\nu})p_{0}2\pi-q_{0}\omega|<C\langle\xi_{\nu}\rangle^{-\nu/2},

for
\nu
sufficiently large, using the inequality

1-\cos\theta\geq\theta^{2}/4,
for
|\theta|\leq 2
, thus (DC) fails.

Therefore, (DC) and (DC’) are equivalent.
∎

6.
Example: the case
G=\mathbb{S}^{3}

We illustrate the general conditions of Theorem
4.1
in the case
G=\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Let
X
be a normalized left-invariant vector field on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Using the identification
\mathbb{S}^{3}\simeq\mathrm{SU}(2)
and composing with an inner automorphism, we may assume without loss of generality that
iX
coincides with the standard left-invariant vector field
\partial_{0}
in the usual Fourier analysis on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. With this choice, the symbol of
X
is diagonal and given by

\sigma_{X}(\ell)_{mn}=im\,\delta_{mn},\qquad\ell\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
m,n\in\{-\ell,-\ell+1,\dots,\ell\}
. In particular, the eigenvalues of
\sigma_{iX}(\ell)
are precisely
m\in\{-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
.

Consider the operator
P:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{S}^{3})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{S}^{3})
defined by

(6.1)

Pu=\partial_{t}u+i(p_{0}+i\lambda q(t))\partial_{0}u-(s(t)+i\delta q(t))u-\alpha q(t)\overline{u},\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

where
q,s\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R})
,
\lambda,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
4.1
. We denote

\displaystyle s_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}s(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad q_{0}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}q(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=s_{0}+i\delta q_{0},\quad B_{0}=\alpha q_{0},\quad C_{0}=2\pi p_{0}+i\lambda q_{0},

and, for each
\ell\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
m\in\{-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
, we set

\rho_{m}(\ell)=\sqrt{(\lambda m-i\delta)^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}},\qquad\text{with }\operatorname{Re}\rho_{m}(\ell)\geq 0.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose that
P
in (
6.1
) satisfies:

(I)

|\alpha|\neq|\delta|
;

(II)

there is no
(m,k)\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
such that

\begin{cases}\operatorname{Re}\bigl(A_{0}(2\pi k+m\overline{C_{0}})\bigr)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
|2\pi k+mC_{0}|^{2}=|A_{0}|^{2}-|B_{0}|^{2};\end{cases}

(III)

there exists
M>0
such that, for all
\ell\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
\langle\ell\rangle\geq M
,

\min\Bigl\{\bigl|e^{-\rho_{m}(\ell)q_{0}}-e^{imp_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\bigr|,\ \bigl|1-e^{-\rho_{m}(\ell)q_{0}+imp_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\bigr|\Bigr\}\geq\langle\ell\rangle^{-M},

for every

.

Then, for every
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{S}^{3})
, there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{S}^{3})
such that

.

This is a direct specialization of Theorem
4.1
, and the only change is that the spectral parameter
\mu_{m}(\xi)
becomes the scalar
m
, and the representations are indexed by
\ell\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
m\in\{-\ell,\dots,\ell\}
.

We now consider the special case
\lambda=0
. In this regime, (
6.1
) becomes

(6.2)

Pu=\partial_{t}u+ip_{0}\partial_{0}u-(s(t)+i\delta q(t))u-\alpha q(t)\overline{u},

with
p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
q,s\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R})
,
q\not\equiv 0
,
q\geq 0
,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose that the operator
P
in (

(1)

;

(2)

7.
Product of groups

In this section we extend Theorem
4.1
to the case where the group variable is a finite product of compact Lie groups. The proof follows the same Fourier reduction as before.

Let
G_{1},\dots,G_{n}
be compact Lie groups with Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{g}_{n}
, and let
X_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}
be normalized left-invariant vector fields,
j=1,\dots,n
. For each
j
we choose representatives
\xi_{j}\in\widehat{G}_{j}
such that the symbols
\sigma_{X_{j}}(\xi_{j})
are diagonal:

\sigma_{X_{j}}(\xi_{j})_{m_{j}n_{j}}=i\,\mu_{m_{j}}(\xi_{j})\,\delta_{m_{j}n_{j}},\qquad 1\leq m_{j},n_{j}\leq d_{\xi_{j}},

with
\mu_{m_{j}}(\xi_{j})\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{n}
. We consider the evolution operator

Lu=\partial_{t}u-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(p_{j}(t)+i\lambda_{j}q(t))\,X_{j}u,

where
p_{j},q\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R})
,
q\not\equiv 0
,
q\geq 0
, and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Set

p_{0j}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}p_{j}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad j=1,\dots,n,\qquad p_{0}=(p_{01},\dots,p_{0n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and reduce as before to

L_{0}=\partial_{t}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(p_{0j}+i\lambda_{j}q(t))\,X_{j}.

Let
P:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
be defined by

(7.1)

Pu=L_{0}u-(s(t)+i\delta q(t))u-\alpha q(t)\overline{u},

where
s\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T};\mathbb{R})
,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

We denote

s_{0}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}s(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad q_{0}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}q(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad A_{0}=s_{0}+i\delta q_{0},\quad B_{0}=\alpha q_{0},\quad C_{0}=2\pi p_{0}+i\boldsymbol{\lambda}q_{0},

where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}q_{0}=(\lambda_{1}q_{0},\dots,\lambda_{n}q_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Given
[\xi]=([\xi_{1}],\dots,[\xi_{n}])\in\widehat{G}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widehat{G}_{n}\simeq\widehat{G}
and indices
m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})
, we set

\mu_{m}(\xi)=(\mu_{m_{1}}(\xi_{1}),\dots,\mu_{m_{n}}(\xi_{n}))\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot\mu_{m}(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\mu_{m_{j}}(\xi_{j}).

For each
([\xi],m)
, define
\rho_{m}(\xi)
as the element of

\left\{\pm\sqrt{(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot\mu_{m}(\xi)-i\delta)^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}}\right\}

with non-negative real part.

Theorem 7.1
.

Suppose that
P
in (
7.1
) satisfies:

(I)

|\alpha|\neq|\delta|
;

(II)

for all
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
and all admissible indices
m
, there is no
k\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

\begin{cases}\operatorname{Re}\bigl(A_{0}(2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)\cdot\overline{C_{0}})\bigr)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
|2\pi k+\mu_{m}(\xi)\cdot C_{0}|^{2}=|A_{0}|^{2}-|B_{0}|^{2};\end{cases}

(III)

there exists
M>0
such that, for all
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
with
\langle\xi\rangle\geq M
,

\min\Bigl\{\bigl|e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}}-e^{i\mu_{m}(\xi)\cdot p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\bigr|,\ \bigl|1-e^{-\rho_{m}(\xi)q_{0}+i\mu_{m}(\xi)\cdot p_{0}2\pi+s_{0}}\bigr|\Bigr\}\geq\langle\xi\rangle^{-M},

for all admissible indices
m
.

Then, for every
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times G)
such that
Pu=f
.

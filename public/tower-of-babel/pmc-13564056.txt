Markedly elevated alpha-fetoprotein leading to the diagnosis of high-grade endometrial carcinoma during hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance
Authors: Jayati Anand, Alicia Youssef, Lawrence Hsu Lin, Niloofar O. Karbasian, Annekathryn Goodman, Michelle Lai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAlpha-fetoprotein (AFP) is widely used for hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance in patients with chronic liver disease. AFP is rarely elevated in endometrial cancer but may be a useful marker of aggressive histologic variants.CaseA 70-year-old woman with autoimmune hepatitis–associated cirrhosis undergoing hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) surveillance developed a rapid rise in AFP from 5 ng/mL to greater than 13,000 ng/mL. Liver imaging revealed no evidence of HCC. Positron-emission tomography (PET) demonstrated focal uptake in the uterus. Pelvic magnetic resonance imaging identified a deeply invasive endometrial mass. Hysteroscopic biopsy revealed high-grade endometrial carcinoma. The patient subsequently underwent total laparoscopic hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (TAH-BSO) with pathology demonstrating a stage IB high-grade endometrial carcinoma with extensive somatic-type glandular yolk sac / fetal-type differentiation and minor carcinosarcoma component (<5%). Serum AFP steadily and rapidly decreased after surgery.ConclusionThis case highlights the importance of considering extrahepatic sources of AFP elevation and demonstrates an unusual presentation of endometrial carcinoma detected during HCC surveillance.

Introduction

Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) is a glycoprotein produced by the yolk sac, liver, and gastrointestinal tract during embryonic development. In adults, AFP levels are typically low, and large elevations are most commonly associated with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and germ cell tumors. AFP is frequently utilized in HCC surveillance for patients with chronic liver disease (Hanif et al., 2022).

Mild AFP elevations may occur in benign conditions such as active hepatitis or cirrhosis; however, markedly elevated levels are often suspicious for malignancy. Extrahepatic tumors such as ovarian, testicular, and gastric primaries may produce AFP. Most ovarian and testicular AFP-producing primaries are germ cell tumors, including yolk sac tumors, embryonal carcinoma, and immature teratomas. Sex cord stromal tumors, such as Sertoli Leydig cell tumors, can also secrete AFP, though this is a less common presentation (Kaczmarek-Borowska et al., 2023). Gastric carcinomas producing AFP can be classified in morphologic subtypes, including hepatoid, yolk sac tumor-like, enteroblastic, and common adenocarcinoma type (Matsumoto et al., 2025; Kinjo et al., 2012; Arora et al., 2018).

AFP-producing uterine tumors are uncommon with reports in the literature describing endometrial carcinomas with hepatoid and yolk sac differentiation (Otani et al., 2021; Otani et al., 2022; Takeuchi et al., 2006; Li et al., 2026). We report a case in which marked AFP elevation during routine HCC surveillance ultimately led to the diagnosis of an unusual high-grade endometrial adenocarcinoma with extensive somatic-type glandular yolk sac / fetal-type differentiation in the absence of overt hepatoid differentiation.

Case presentation

A 70-year-old woman with autoimmune hepatitis and metabolic dysfunction associated steatohepatitis (MASH) complicated by cirrhosis and portal hypertension was followed with routine HCC surveillance. Her medical history was also notable for systemic lupus erythematosus, Sjögren's syndrome, autoimmune nephritis, and prior stage cT2N0 squamous cell carcinoma of the anal canal treated with chemoradiation in 2021. Family history was notable for maternal grandmother with unknown abdominal cancer. She did not have additional risk factors for endometrial cancer, such as obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus, or nulliparity.

Her liver disease was medically managed with mycophenolate mofetil with stable hepatic function. Serial elastography demonstrated gradual improvement in hepatic fibrosis, most recently consistent with stage F2 fibrosis in 2025.

Between 2019 and 2024, serum AFP levels ranged from 3.0 to 5.4 ng/mL. In September 2025, her AFP increased dramatically to 70 ng/mL. She was asymptomatic with no abdominal or pelvic pain or fullness, postmenopausal bleeding, or vaginal discharge (Boeckstaens et al., 2020). Her screening liver ultrasound did not show any focal liver lesions. Computed tomography (CT) scan of the abdomen and pelvis performed in October 2025 demonstrated cirrhotic liver morphology with a stable, previously known hemangioma and no evidence of new focal liver lesions; the scan was also notable for a fibroid uterus without signs of metastasis or malignancy in the abdomen and pelvis.

Repeat laboratory testing demonstrated a rapid increase in AFP to 2411 ng/mL in December 2025 (Fig. 1). During Liver Tumor Board review of her imaging, the enhancing lesion in the posterior uterus was noted to be increasing in size, and a pelvic MRI was recommended. At the area of FDG uptake on prior PET-CT, pelvic MRI demonstrated a focal hypoenhancing endometrial mass with deep myometrial invasion, suspicious for endometrial neoplasm. No pelvic lymphadenopathy or extrauterine disease was identified (Fig. 2C-F).

At this time, she was referred to gynecologic oncology. Additional tumor markers including CA-125, CA 19–9, and carcinoembryonic antigen were obtained with respective values of 16 U/mL, 11 U/mL, and 1.4 ng/mL; these were all within normal limits.

Hysteroscopy in February 2026 revealed an atrophic endometrial cavity with a small polypoid lesion in the lower uterine segment. Histopathologic evaluation of the polypoid lesion was consistent with grade 2 endometrioid adenocarcinoma of the endometrium.

Subsequently, the patient underwent total laparoscopic hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (TAH-BSO), sentinel lymph node biopsy, and omental biopsy. Intraoperative evaluation revealed no evidence of metastatic disease.

In February 2026, the day prior to surgery, her AFP was notably elevated to 13,022 ng/mL. Because the half-life of AFP is 5 days, serum AFP was drawn weekly to monitor for appropriate decrease in AFP after TAH-BSO (Adigun et al., 2026). One week post-operatively, it declined to 4596 ng/mL, and it continued to downtrend in the weeks following (Fig. 1).

Final pathologic examination demonstrated high-grade endometrial adenocarcinoma, extending deep into the myometrium, with less than 1 mm distance from the uterine serosa. While extensive lymphovascular invasion was seen, no disease outside the uterus was identified (FIGO 2009 Stage IB / FIGO 2023 Stage IIC). The carcinoma was composed of solid and glandular architecture with extensive cytoplasmic clearing (Fig. 3A). The tumor cells exhibited high grade nuclear features with primitive appearance and brisk mitotic activity (Fig. 3B-C), resembling fetal-type endodermal glands. Focal areas of the tumor showed highly atypical stromal cells, associated with extracellular globules, which were consistent with a minor component of homologous sarcomatous transformation (<5%) (Fig. 3D). Immunohistochemistry was positive for SALL4, glypican3, and AFP (Fig. 3E-F), supporting the somatic-type glandular yolk sac / fetal-type differentiation. Other markers were negative, including PAX8, SOX17, ER, and PR. Mismatch repair (MMR) protein immunohistochemistry showed intact expression of MLH1, PMS2, MSH2, and MSH6. Her case was discussed at a multidisciplinary tumor board, and the recommendation was made for adjuvant treatment with both systemic platinum-based chemotherapy, as well as radiation, given the risk for both distant and locoregional recurrence. Per the American Society of Radiation Oncology (ASTRO) guidelines, the standard of care would be consideration of external beam radiation (Harkenrider et al., 2023). However, given her prior radiation exposure and subsequent gastrointestinal toxicities in the setting of her previously treated anal cancer, the decision was made to proceed with vaginal brachytherapy.

Discussion

Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) is often used as a biomarker for HCC screening in patients with chronic liver disease. In this case, a patient was diagnosed with high-grade endometrial adenocarcinoma with extensive somatic-type glandular yolk sac / fetal-type differentiation after she was found to have marked AFP elevation during routine HCC surveillance (Hanif et al., 2022).

AFP-producing tumors of the uterus are extremely uncommon. Published reports most frequently describe endometrial tumors with hepatoid adenocarcinoma differentiation or mixed germ cell components. These tumors often exhibit aggressive clinical behavior and may manifest with advanced-stage disease (Otani et al., 2021; Otani et al., 2022; Takeuchi et al., 2006). In contrast, AFP elevation associated with endometrioid adenocarcinoma is rare. A previous report of elevated AFP in endometroid adenocarcinoma was associated with a significantly lower AFP level of 1292.8 ng/mL in a patient with presenting complaint of abdominal swelling (Kodama et al., 2010).

In the present case, AFP levels rose from 70 ng/mL to 13,022 ng/mL in the span of 5 months, with rapid decline after TAH-BSO. The tumor demonstrated predominantly high-grade histology with only a minor carcinosarcoma component. While no hepatoid differentiation was seen, the tumor exhibited morphology reminiscent of fetal-type endodermal glands. Supporting evidence includes its primitive appearance, sub- and supranuclear vacuoles, and immunohistochemistry with positive SALL4, glypican3 and AFP (Miettinen et al., 2014). The mechanism underlying the marked AFP elevation in this patient is likely due to retrodifferentiation of the tumor to a fetal phenotype of the glandular epithelium that produces AFP.

Another notable feature of this case is the context in which the malignancy was detected. The patient underwent routine HCC screening due to underlying cirrhosis. The dramatic rise in AFP prompted hepatic imaging, which was negative for suspicious lesions. A multidisciplinary review during Liver Tumor Board noted the increase in size of a uterine mass, ultimately leading to the diagnosis of endometrial carcinoma. This highlights the importance of considering extrahepatic sources of AFP when hepatic imaging does not identify a corresponding lesion.

Additionally, the patient did not present with typical symptoms of endometrial carcinoma, such as postmenopausal bleeding, abdominal or pelvic pain or fullness, or vaginal discharge (Boeckstaens et al., 2020). The absence of gynecologic symptoms in this patient highlights the atypical nature of the presentation and the role that tumor marker abnormalities can play in prompting further investigation.

For patients with preserved MMR, p53 wild-type Stage IB high-grade endometrioid carcinoma, the recommended adjuvant therapy includes both systemic therapy and radiation. Her pathology is associated with an increased risk for both distant and locoregional recurrence, hence the combination therapy (de Boer et al., 2018; de Boer et al., 2019; Randall et al., 2019). Notably, differing from the standard recommendation per the ASTRO guidelines, this patient was counseled on the administration of vaginal brachytherapy instead of external beam radiation (Harkenrider et al., 2023). This case highlights the importance of a patient-centered, multi-disciplinary approach considering the patient, the present pathology, her previous radiation exposure, as well as risk of increased toxicities with expanded radiation exposure.

Conclusion

This case demonstrates an unusual presentation of high-grade endometrial adenocarcinoma detected by elevated AFP levels in a patient undergoing routine HCC screening. When AFP elevation cannot be explained by hepatic pathology, clinicians should consider extrahepatic malignancies and pursue comprehensive diagnostic testing.

A wavelet-enhanced state space model for multi-disease diagnosis across chest X-ray, CT, and MRI
Authors: Yuting Xue, Lihua Liu, Chao Zheng, Tao Zhou, Kexin Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMedical image classification faces multiple challenges, including heterogeneous lesion morphology, cross-modal representation discrepancy, and computational efficiency. Traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) struggle to model long-range semantic correlations due to their fixed receptive fields, while the quadratic complexity of Transformers hinders the practical processing of high-resolution images. This study proposes the Wavelet Transform-Enhanced Mamba (WTEMamba) model, which integrates wavelet transform with the Selective State Space Model (Mamba) to construct an efficient medical image classification framework.MethodsThe model adopts a three-stage lightweight network to balance performance and efficiency, with core innovations including the design of a Feature Interaction Hybrid Module (FIHM): the convolutional branch extracts local textures, the wavelet-enhanced selective state space branch captures global dependencies and strengthens high-frequency edge features, and channel shuffle is integrated to eliminate information redundancy. Additionally, a multi-scale compressed classifier is built to gradually refine high-level semantic features.ResultsExperiments on 9 medical datasets showed that WTEMamba significantly outperformed baseline models such as CNNs, Transformers, and Mamba-based models. In terms of overall accuracy (OA) and the area under the curve (AUC), WTEMamba improved OA over the best competing model by 0.3% to 2.6%, and achieved AUC improvements of 0.0001 to 0.0125. For example, on the Brain Stroke CT dataset, WTEMamba achieved an OA of 93.45%, outperforming Medmamba by 1.99% (*P<0.05 via bootstrap test), while requiring only 2.1G floating point operations (FLOPs) and reducing the parameter count by over 50% compared to Medmamba. On the challenging breast cancer magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and Knee X-Ray datasets, WTEMamba exceeded the best competing models by 2.55% and 2.25% in OA, respectively.ConclusionsThe experimental results validate the significant advantages of the WTEMamba model in global dependency modeling and high-frequency detail perception. It effectively balances classification accuracy and computational efficiency, providing a robust and practical solution for medical image analysis. Furthermore, the framework’s outstanding performance on multi-modal data, combined with its exceptional generalization ability, highlights the positive significance of integrating lightweight diagnostic algorithms into medical image analysis. Our code and datasets are available at https://github.com/yuting77777/WTEMamba.Keywords: Medical image classification, visual state space model, wavelet transform, lightweight architecture

Introduction

In the clinical decision-making chain, the accuracy of medical image classification directly determines the sensitivity of disease screening, the reliability of staging assessment, and the objectivity of treatment efficacy evaluation (1). Current mainstream methods face a dilemma: convolutional neural networks (CNNs), such as the recent lightweight CNN variant CellSage (2) that employs multi-scale convolutions and attention mechanisms, achieve high diagnostic accuracy with an ultra-lightweight architecture of only 3.8 million parameters, yet they are constrained by their local receptive fields, making it difficult to model the complex spatial dependencies between lesions and surrounding tissues. In contrast, vision transformers (3) establish global contextual interactions, but their self-attention mechanism introduces high computational complexity and a rigid demand for large-scale annotated data, which pose bottlenecks in many clinical scenarios.

Recently, structured state-space models like Mamba (4) offer a new paradigm for overcoming the above challenges. This architecture provides three key advantages. At the feature perception level, its selective state mechanism dynamically modulates information flow based on input content, enabling the network to prioritize key diagnostic structures such as lesion boundaries and microcalcifications rather than treating the entire image uniformly. At the computational efficiency level, hardware-optimized parallel scanning enables long-sequence modeling without high computational costs, facilitating real-time analysis of high-resolution three-dimensional (3D) images and multi-phase dynamic contrast-enhanced sequences. At the temporal modeling level, time-varying parameters allow the model to characterize the dynamic evolution of pathological processes, addressing the limitations of traditional static models in analyzing disease progression. Recent studies show that ViM (5) achieves cross-scale dependency modeling with Transformer-like efficiency while retaining CNN-level local feature extraction. Yue et al. (6) first applied Mamba to generalized medical image classification and detection, demonstrating strong performance across various tasks. Liu et al. (7) leveraged cross-level Mamba blocks to achieve robust brain tumor segmentation from incomplete multi-modal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). For resource-constrained deployment, researchers have developed lightweight networks employing strategies such as skip sampling, directional selection, and pathway simplification to avoid redundancy in full-path scanning. Examples include EfficientVMamba (8), which uses ES2D scanning to reduce State Space Model (SSM) computational burden. DualMamba (9) designs a lightweight Mamba module incorporating dynamic positional encoding, achieving strong performance with a small number of parameters. SCSegamba (10) introduces bottleneck convolutions and gating mechanisms into Mamba to dynamically adjust feature weights, greatly reducing parameters and computation. S³-Mamba (11) removes depthwise convolutional layers from original SSMs and introduces dual residual connections, avoiding over-smoothing and redundancy while reducing information propagation loss. He et al. (12) propose MobileMamba, and experiments verify that its three-stage architecture (the first stage downsample to H/16, with the final output at H/64) outperforms the traditional four-stage architecture: under the same throughput, the accuracy is improved by 0.4%, and under the same accuracy, the throughput is increased by 15%. AttmNet (13) lightweightly integrates Mamba with self-attention via the Att-Mamba module, capturing global context while maintaining computational efficiency. GCNet-Mamba (14) integrates GCNet convolutions with SSM layers and comprehensively outperforms CNN, Transformer, and Mamba models on nine multimodal medical image datasets.

Although state-space models offer unique advantages in processing high-resolution medical images due to their linear complexity, they still encounter three major limitations in handling such data. First, the serialization of two-dimensional (2D) images inevitably disrupts spatial adjacency relationships, reducing the model’s sensitivity to fine morphological features that rely on local neighborhood coordination (e.g., lesion margins and microcalcification clusters), thereby increasing the risk of missing small lesions. Second, existing 2D selective scan mechanisms lack explicit modulation of directional energy in frequency-domain subbands, making it difficult to adaptively distinguish high-frequency pathological contours from background noise. As a result, critical diagnostic signals are easily submerged by imaging artifacts, leading to insufficient robustness in cross-modal scenarios such as computed tomography (CT)/MRI. Third, multi-scale feature fusion faces exponential dimension expansion as the network deepens, and direct aggregation introduces considerable computational redundancy, making it challenging to balance diagnostic accuracy and inference efficiency in lightweight scenarios.

To address the disconnection between frequency-domain perception and spatial-domain modeling, wavelet transform offers a potential solution due to its time-frequency localization properties. It provides localized information in both domains, effectively separating frequency components while preserving spatial structure: low-frequency subbands capture macroscopic anatomical contours, whereas high-frequency subbands contain key diagnostic details such as edges and textures. Inspired by this, researchers have begun integrating wavelet transform into the Mamba framework. For example, MSM-UNet (15) enhances boundary and detail representation by introducing wavelet transform and a dual attention mechanism. Wave-Mamba (16) uses wavelet transform for lossless downsampling and noise separation, providing cleaner inputs. WaveMamba (17) applies wavelet transform for multi-scale decomposition of spatial and spectral features, and EAD-net (18) employs a multi-scale wavelet module for denoising and feature decomposition with an alternating Mamba-CNN encoder. HWA-ResMamba (19) integrates Wavelet Transform into convolutional networks to enhance low-frequency and edge perception and replaces self-attention with Mamba for efficient long-range dependency modeling. This synergy significantly improves the continuity and integrity of coronary arteries.

Despite these improvements, most methods treat wavelet transform merely as a front-end preprocessing tool, serving only to supply smaller or cleaner input sequences to subsequent Mamba modules. Consequently, frequency transformation operates solely at the input stage, lacking parallel interaction and deep synergy with spatial modeling. Frequency features, extracted only shallowly, cannot be progressively reinforced with global semantics as the network deepens, severely undermining the model’s sustained perception of subtle lesion edges and textures. Particularly for cross-modal medical images with distinct physical properties and frequency characteristics, the discriminative information in the frequency domain remains largely underexploited.

To address the above issues, this paper proposes the Wavelet Transform-Enhanced Mamba (WTEMamba), a medical image classification model. To systematically clarify the technical gaps and position our method against existing SSM-based approaches, we summarize the core innovations of representative models in Table 1. Compared with previous works, our core innovation lies in designing a threestage network and constructing a Feature Interaction Hybrid Module (FIHM). In this module, the convolutional branch extracts local texture features, while the waveletenhanced selective statespace branch simultaneously captures global dependencies and strengthens highfrequency edge information. The proposed methods

Overall architecture

This paper designs a three-layer lightweight visual state space model named WTEMamba, whose overall structure is shown in Figure 2. Specifically, WTEMamba consists of a Patch Embedding layer, stacked FIHM modules, a Path Merging layer for downsampling, and a feature classifier.

The overall architecture of WTEMamba. It consists of two main branches: Conv-Branch is responsible for capturing local spatial features, and DWTM-Branch is responsible for capturing global context and high-frequency detailed features. DWTM-Branch, Wavelet-Enhanced Selective State-Space Branch; FIHM, Feature Interaction Hybrid Module.

The WTEMamba model first used the Patch Embedding layer to divide the input image x∈RH×W×3 into 8×8 non-overlapping image patches, and mapped the channel dimension of the image to C. Specifically, it partitioned the original image with a size of 224×224×3 through a convolution operation with both the convolutional kernel and stride set to 8, then linearly projected each 8×8 patch to a 192-dimensional channel count, and finally generated a feature map with a size of 28×28×192.

Subsequently, the model entered a three-stage hierarchical architecture. The core design of this architecture lies in efficiently constructing a multi-scale feature pyramid, gradually fusing local details and global semantic information, and thereby significantly enhancing the model’s capability to capture pathological features of different sizes in medical images.

Stage 1: the model stacked 2 FIHM modules to enhance shallow feature representation at high spatial resolution, effectively preserving details such as edges and textures, and providing rich low-level features for subsequent stages.

Stage 2: the model performed 2× downsampling via Path Merging to compress the feature map size to 14×14, expanded the channel count to 384, and stacked 4 FIHM modules. At medium resolution, this stage balances detail and semantic information, gradually expands the receptive field, enhances the model’s ability to represent medium-scale lesion features, and maintains high computational efficiency.

Stage 3: the model further downsampled to 7×7 resolution, expanded the channel count to 768, and stacked 2 FIHM modules for deep feature extraction. Finally, highly abstract high-level semantic features were formed, enabling the model to understand the complete pathological patterns of the image.

The design of this three-stage architecture differs from the four-stage architectural design adopted by most models (5,20,21). Specifically, in a four-stage network, the input image with dimensions H×W×3 undergoes four consecutive downsampling steps: after the first downsampling, the feature map size becomes H4×W4×C1; the second downsampling reduces it to H8×W8×C2; the third downsampling further reduces it to H16×W16×C3; and finally, the fourth downsampling results in a size of H32×W32×C4.In contrast, the three-stage network proposed in this study is structurally more compact: it generated a feature map of H8×W8×C1 during the first downsampling, the second downsampling reduced it to H16×W16×C2, and the third downsampling further reduced the feature map to H32×W32×C3. Since the four-stage network retains feature maps with higher resolution in the early stages, it has a higher computational load, leading to relatively slow inference speed. However, the three-stage architecture significantly reduces computational complexity by eliminating one downsampling step, thereby improving inference efficiency. Furthermore, by incorporating the proposed FIHM module, the three-stage network not only ensured faster inference speed but also effectively enhanced feature representation capability and model performance. This was verified in the ablation experiments. For example, on the SARS-COV-2 CT scan dataset, compared with the fourstage network, the three-stage network not only improved the OA metric by 0.22% but also increased the AUC metric by 0.54%, achieving balanced optimization of performance.

At the end of the network, a global average pooling layer was used, which received multi-scale feature maps from the three stages and fused them at a learnable weight ratio, generating a feature vector of size 1×1,344. This cross-stage feature fusion mechanism can adaptively integrate representational information at different levels, strengthen important features and suppress redundant information. The fused features were input into the classifier for disease classification, and the entire process controlled the number of parameters while achieving efficient multi-scale feature interaction and integration, thereby significantly improving the robustness and expressive capability of medical image classification.

Efficient FIHM

As a core component of WTEMamba, FIHM is a lightweight dual-branch block, as illustrated in Figure 2. This method employed channel splitting to map the feature map x into two groups, x1 and x2, and then used Conv-Branch and DWTM-Branch respectively to extract local features and global features from each group. Finally, FIHM adopted channel concatenation to restore the channel dimension size, while channel shuffling performed shuffling on the feature maps in the channel dimension to avoid inter-channel information loss caused by group convolution operations. Meanwhile, the activation functions of Conv-Branch and DWTM-Branch were set to ReLU and SiLU respectively.

Conv-Branch

The First Layer: Batch normalization is applied to the input feature x1 to alleviate internal covariate shift and accelerate training convergence. Then, a 3×3 convolution kernel with stride 1 and padding 1 is used to extract spatial features, which maintains the spatial resolution while leveraging the translation equivariance of convolution to capture local texture structures. Finally, batch normalization and the ReLU activation function are applied again to introduce sparse nonlinearity and enhance the discriminative ability of feature representation.

The Second Layer: Following the design of the first layer, a 3×3 convolution layer with stride 1 and padding 1 is adopted. This part progressively abstracts local patterns through hierarchical convolution, thereby enlarging the effective receptive field and deepening feature representation. Subsequently, batch normalization and ReLU activation are applied to maintain nonlinearity while stabilizing the training process.

The Third Layer: a 1×1 convolution is used for linear recombination and feature fusion across channels. This operation is equivalent to performing a learnable weighted combination along the channel dimension, which enhances cross-channel information interaction while introducing very few parameters, and effectively controls computational complexity.

In the entire convolutional branch, the 3×3 convolution focuses on spatial feature extraction, while the 1×1 convolution is responsible for inter-channel information interaction. Batch normalization between layers ensures training stability, and the ReLU activation function continuously introduces nonlinear transformation capability. This multi-layer convolution stacking structure can progressively extract multi-scale features, from local details to more abstract semantic information, and finally output a feature map with the same size as the original input, laying a foundation for subsequent feature fusion with the DWTM-Branch.

The second information flow: To capture high-frequency details more precisely, the same feature map x2 was linearly transformed into x2′, followed by feature fusion of frequency information (WFM), as shown in Figure 4. The main path of this module utilized wavelet transform to capture the frequency-domain features of images; the auxiliary path, on the other hand, provided supplementary global information statistics through a lightweight attention mechanism. These two paths were fused with weights that clearly distinguished between primary and secondary roles, jointly enhancing the model’s ability to perceive and utilize frequency information. The ultimate goal is to improve the quality of visual feature representation via such frequency-domain enhancement. Considering the need for high efficiency with minimal overhead here, this paper adopted the Daubechies 4 (db4) wavelet for the transformation. The db4 wavelet is one of the most widely used members of the Daubechies wavelet family; it possesses orthogonal and non-redundant properties, enabling accurate signal reconstruction without occupying additional storage. Its compact support structure provides excellent locality in both time and spatial domains, which can effectively capture signal and image details with minimal edge effects. Furthermore, its smoothness is superior to that of low-order Haar wavelets, avoiding block distortion.

Classifier

To adapt to the multi-classification task of medical images, this model employed a fully connected classification network to process the weighted fused multi-scale features yc∈R1×7C. This network comprised three compressed hidden layers (with the number of neurons being 5C, 3C, and C respectively), which gradually refined high-level semantic information and enhanced the discriminative ability for pathological features. The output layer was configured with m neurons (where m denotes the number of disease categories) and adopted the Softmax activation function to generate a normalized probability distribution. This distribution intuitively reflected the confidence level of the input image belonging to each disease category, providing an interpretable basis for clinical decision-making.

During the training process, network parameters were optimized via the backpropagation algorithm, with the cross-entropy loss function minimized {Eqs. [35,36]}. This loss function forces the model to focus on learning the discriminative boundaries between classes, making it particularly suitable for class imbalance scenarios commonly encountered in medical images (e.g., insufficient samples of rare lesions). The index of the maximum value in the finally output probability vector corresponds to the predicted disease label, and this method can be directly integrated into the clinical auxiliary diagnosis process.

Results

This section provides a detailed description of the experimental setup and evaluates the proposed model on 9 public datasets.

Experimental details

To verify the effectiveness and competitiveness of WTEMamba in medical image classification, several visual networks with demonstrated strong performance in medical imaging tasks, along with baseline models, were selected as references. The dataset was split into training, validation, and test sets using four fixed random seeds (42, 123, 2024, and 2026) to ensure reproducibility. During data preprocessing, random cropping to 224×224 was applied to the training set, while fixed resizing was used for the validation and test sets, all sharing the same normalization. Datasets were loaded via ImageFolder, and a category-to-index mapping dictionary was constructed and saved as a JSON file, with output statistics including class counts and distribution. The batch size was set to 64. The AdamW optimizer was employed with an initial learning rate of 0.0001, β1=0.9, β2=0.999, and weight decay of 1e−4, along with the cross-entropy loss function. Training was conducted for a maximum of 150 epochs, with early stopping triggered when validation accuracy showed no improvement over a consecutive number of epochs.

Hardware configuration: The operating system is Ubuntu 20.04.3, and the GPUs include four NVIDIA A800 80GB PCIe cards. Software environment: We adopt the PyTorch network framework, including Python 3.10.7, PyTorch 1.13.0, and CUDA 12.1.

Evaluation metrics

To comprehensively quantify the model performance, this study adopted 6 standard evaluation metrics:

Overall accuracy (OA): it describes the overall prediction accuracy of the model, which is calculated as the proportion of correctly predicted samples to the total number of samples. The higher the OA value, the better the overall prediction performance of the model. The formula is as follows:

Precision: it measures the reliability of positive predictions and is defined as the proportion of true positives to all positive predictions. The higher the Precision value, the more reliable the positive prediction results are. The formula is as follows:

Sensitivity: it reflects the model’s ability to identify positive samples, which is calculated as the proportion of true positives to the actual number of positive samples. The higher the Sensitivity value, the stronger the model’s ability to identify positive samples. The formula is as follows:

AUC: it characterizes the overall discriminative ability of a model under different classification thresholds. For binary classification tasks, AUC is computed by plotting the true positive rate (TPR) against the false positive rate (FPR) and measuring the area under the curve. For multi-class datasets, we adopt the One-vs-Rest (OvR) strategy, where AUC is calculated for each class against all others, and the final reported value is the macro-average across all classes.

The performance of WTEMamba

To comprehensively and objectively evaluate the performance of the WTEMamba model, we conducted comparative experiments on nine datasets, with the compared models roughly categorized into three architectures: CNN, Transformer, and Mamba. All results are reported as mean±standard deviation over multiple runs, where the standard deviation reflects the performance fluctuations of the model under different data splits. The 95% confidence intervals for F1, OA, and AUC were estimated using the bootstrap percentile method. When WTEMamba achieved the best performance, statistical significance against the runner-up was assessed via a paired bootstrap test: in each resampling iteration, a bootstrap sample of the same size as the original fixed test set was drawn with replacement, the metric difference between WTEMamba and the compared model was computed on this sample, and the null hypothesis was tested using the two-sided quantiles of the empirical distribution of these differences. Comprehensive result analysis of the X-ray modality

In the X-ray modality, WTEMamba achieved an OA of 97.07% on the CPN X-Ray dataset, outperforming the second-ranked DacNet by 0.28%. On the Knee X-Ray dataset, its OA exceeded that of EfficientVMamba-T by nearly 2 percentage points, with an AUC increase of 0.0029. This advantage stems from the model’s efficient fusion of local details and global semantics. Moreover, although WTEMamba outperformed other models on the Knee dataset in terms of OA and AUC, its overall performance was lower than on the CPN dataset, likely due to more subtle and morphologically variable lesion features, as well as potential class imbalance or image quality differences.

Comprehensive result analysis of the CT modality

In the CT modality, WTEMamba outperformed the best comparative model, EfficientVMamba, on both the SARS-COV-2 CT-Scan and Brain Stroke CT datasets, with OA improvements of 0.39% and 0.98%, and AUC improvements of 0.0024 and 0.0010, respectively. This advantage stems from the high contrast and the clear anatomical structures of CT images. Due to the varying X-ray absorption coefficients of different tissues, distinct grayscale differences and boundaries exist between them. For example, COVID-19-related lesions such as ground-glass opacities exhibit density differences from normal lung tissue, while hemorrhagic and ischemic regions in brain CT show prominent contrast. The core FIHM module in WTEMamba establishes long-range dependencies through the SS2D branch to capture the overall pathological distribution, and leverages the WTM branch to enhance high-frequency edge and detail features, which aligns well with the physical characteristics of CT images. This global-local synergy enables the model to fully exploit the discriminative information in CT data, thereby achieving efficient and accurate classification.

Comprehensive result analysis of the MRI modality

In MRI modality tasks, WTEMamba performed prominently on the Breast Cancer Patients MRI and Brain Tumor MRI datasets. On the Breast Cancer Patients MRI dataset, WTEMamba outperformed EfficientViT by 2.55% in OA and 0.0125 in AUC. On the Brain Tumor MRI dataset, WTEMamba outperformed Medmamba by 1.09% in OA and 0.0007 in AUC. MR images feature relatively high soft tissue contrast, blurred lesion boundaries, and more subtle texture variations. While enhancing discriminative features, the wavelet transform may also strengthen semantically ambiguous local details. Although these improvements alleviate semantic modeling issues, the complex morphological features and pixel-level semantic variations in MRI still pose challenges for representational learning. For example, the intricate tissue structures and relatively large intra-class variation in breast MRI may explain why its performance is slightly lower than that of brain tumor tasks. Nevertheless, this still demonstrates WTEMamba’s capability and adaptability in processing complex MR images.

Comprehensive result analysis of other modalities

In other medical modalities, WTEMamba also exhibits relatively good cross-modal adaptability. Leveraging the three-stage feature pyramid and the FIHM module, the model simultaneously captures subtle mucosal lesions in the digestive tract, easily confused fetal ultrasound planes, and fine dermatological differences. By retaining local discriminative features while integrating global context, it achieves stable and accurate classification across complex medical images with numerous categories and small inter-class differences.

Notably, it achieves comparatively high performance with only 11.0M parameters and 2.1G FLOPs, demonstrating a clear efficiency advantage. In CT tasks, the model’s capabilities complement the image characteristics, showing relatively good application potential; in Xray, MRI, and other modalities, it also exhibits reliable generalization and practical value, offering an important reference for lightweight multimodal medical image analysis.

Table 8 summarizes the performance of different main-to-auxiliary path weight ratios on the three medical image datasets. Performance analysis of Mamba

To validate the effectiveness of SS2D in the FIHM module, we replace it with a standard Transformer (w/ Transformer) and compare the two on five datasets. Table 9 shows that the Mamba-based WTEMamba consistently outperforms the Transformer counterpart across all comparisons. For example, OA on CPN X-Ray rises from 95.59% to 97.07%, and on Fetal-Planes-DB from 90.16% to 94.39%. Figure 15 visualises the percentage improvements for each metric. On CPN X-Ray, OA, F1 and AUC improve by 1.5%, 1.9% and 0.2%, respectively. On SARS-COV-2 CT-scan, AUC gains 0.6%. On Fetal-Planes-DB, OA improves by 4.7%. This superiority stems from the input-dependent dynamic parameterisation of Mamba’s selective state-space model, which adaptively decides which pathological features to retain or ignore. This capability is crucial for medical images with blurred lesion boundaries and subtle inter-class differences. Meanwhile, its linear computational complexity avoids the quadratic overhead of Transformers, enabling efficient processing of high-resolution feature maps.

Discussion

WTEMamba achieves better classification performance on nine public medical image datasets covering Xray, CT, MRI, endoscopy, ultrasound, and dermoscopy. On CPN X-Ray, it reaches an OA of 97.07% and an AUC of 0.9958, significantly outperforming competitors such as DacNet, Swin Transformer, and Medmamba. On SARS-COV-2 Ct-Scan and Brain Tumor MRI, it achieves OAs of 94.43% and 96.69%, respectively, also outperforming existing methods. For datasets with many classes and subtle inter-class differences, such as Breast Cancer Patients MRI and Fetal Planes DB, WTEMamba maintains a stable advantage, fully demonstrating its strong adaptability to multi-modal medical images. Ablation studies validate the effectiveness of channel shuffling, the waveletenhanced branch, the three-stage network, and the enhanced classifier, with the wavelet branch contributing most significantly to improvements in specificity and precision. Replacing SS2D in FIHM with a standard Transformer leads to clear drops in OA, AUC, and other metrics across five datasets, confirming that the selective statespace mechanism of Mamba, with its input-dependent dynamic parameterisation and linear complexity, is more suitable for modelling long-range semantic dependencies in medical images. Current limitations include relatively lower sensitivity on the SARS-CoV-2 CT-scan and Breast Cancer MRI datasets, and all experiments rely solely on public benchmarks. In clinical practice, performance may fluctuate due to variations in equipment, protocols, noise, and artifacts. To address this, we plan to collect multi-centre clinical data to systematically assess and improve model robustness under heterogeneous conditions, thereby improving clinical reliability.

Conclusions

This study proposes the WTEMamba framework, which aims to address the trade-off challenge between global dependency modeling and computational efficiency in medical image classification. By adopting a three-stage lightweight network architecture, this framework not only significantly improves the model’s inference speed but also more effectively meets the requirements of clinical real-time diagnosis. The core contribution lies in the design of the FIHM, which innovatively integrates the local feature extraction capability of the convolutional branch, the global dependency modeling advantage of the selective state-space model, and the enhancement mechanism of wavelet transform for high-frequency details. While significantly enhancing classification performance, it overcomes the quadratic computational complexity bottleneck of traditional Transformer-based models. Experimental results demonstrate that WTEMamba exhibits outstanding performance across nine medical image datasets covering multiple modalities including X-ray, CT, and MRI. Furthermore, while maintaining high accuracy, the model achieves a substantial improvement in inference speed, providing a new technical pathway and solution for efficient and reliable computer-aided diagnostic systems in practical medical scenarios. Future work will focus on three aspects: (I) further improving the model’s generalization ability to high-resolution pathological images and exploring deployment optimization for edge computing devices; (II) deeply analyzing the model’s decision-making mechanism by integrating interpretability analysis tools; and (III) further investigating the transfer potential of WTEMamba in downstream tasks such as medical image segmentation, detection, and reconstruction, so as to promote the widespread adoption of selective state-space models in the field of intelligent healthcare.

Teleportation-based squeezer for bosonic cluster states Preprint: APS/123-QED
Michal Matulík, Radim Filip, Petr Marek

Abstract
The one-way quantum computation utilizing bosonic modes of light offers unmatched scalability of light modes, and it has seen rapid experimental development recently. Scalability requires robust and low-error gates and measurements. Squeezing gate is one of the necessary Gaussian operations. We find the optimal squeezing gate in cluster state architecture. Our approach newly uses amplitude transmission coefficients of unbalanced beam splitters and homodyne detection with subsequent unity-gain feed-forward to squeeze the input state. The approach outperforms the current method based on optimally rotated homodyne detection but fixed balanced beam splitters. The performance of both cluster state squeezers is evaluated for Gaussian and non-Gaussian input states. We use different metrics to benchmark the quality of squeezed output states. The result opens a road to low-noise squeezing gates in experimentally achievable cluster states.

The second resource, required for efficient quantum computation with bosonic modes of light, encompasses the set of Gaussian operations necessary for the realization of the Clifford gates

The complete set of Gaussian operations can be simplified to phase shift, displacement, beam splitter, and squeezing, which together can be used to create an arbitrary Gaussian operation

W. Asavanant, et al. 2019

In this paper, we study several ways in which squeezing can be implemented as a part of the teleportation protocol in cluster-state architecture. We compare the different approaches and optimize over the free parameters of the circuits for several levels of finite resources and realistic imperfections. We evaluate the performance of the measurement-induced squeezing for Gaussian and non-Gaussian states, for which we study how their non-Gaussian nature diminishes during the protocol. In Section

We also discuss the metrics used for the evaluation of the test states - the vacuum and the single photon state. In Section

IV

N_{P}=\mathrm{var}(\hat{N}_{x_{out}})\cdot\mathrm{var}(\hat{N}_{p_{out}}).

(27)

The product
N_{P}
can be used to define the entanglement-breaking bound (E-breaking)

N_{P}\geq\frac{1}{4}.

(28)

This is a sufficient condition for breaking correlations between quadratures when teleporting one mode of the TMSV state. For the variance of added noise the condition holds
\mathrm{var}(\hat{N}_{x_{out}})\text{ or }\mathrm{var}(\hat{N}_{p_{out}})\geq 1/2
when considering the
N_{P}
bound, Eq. (
28
). This implies a violation of the correlations between the quadrature operators in the maximally entangled state and thus entanglement breaking.

For the computation of GnG, it is necessary to find the density matrix in a Fock basis. GnG requires knowledge of photo-statistics.
The density matrix of the teleported state is defined as follows:

\hat{\rho}_{out}=\iint G(x,p)\hat{D}\hat{S}\hat{\rho}_{in}\hat{S}^{\dagger}\hat{D}^{\dagger}dxdp,

(29)

where
\hat{S}\equiv\hat{S}(-\log s)
and
\hat{D}\equiv\hat{D}(x,p)
. The squeezing operator is defined as

Leonhardt 2010

\hat{S}(r)=e^{\frac{i}{2}r(\hat{x}\hat{p}-\hat{p}\hat{x})}.

(30)

The parameter
r
is a general parameter. The displacement operator is defined as
Ch. Weedbrook, et al. 2012
;
Leonhardt 2010
:

\hat{D}(x_{0},p_{0})=e^{i(p_{0}\hat{x}-q_{0}\hat{p})}.

(31)

IV
Conclusion

We found the optimal squeezer that could be used in experimentally achievable bosonic clusters. The squeezer is based on variable beam splitters, BS-sq. It outperforms the squeezer based on variable phase shift, PS-sq, regarding both fidelity and negativity in the Wigner function of the teleport-squeezed state, and it matches the fully general BSPS-sq scheme in performance.
The advantage of BS-sq lies in the broader parameter space compared to PS-sq. We have performed fidelity optimization to find the optimal parameters
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
and shown that, in the case of the BS-sq protocol, it leads to optimally distributed noise. However, the ultimate entanglement breaking indicates opposite ordering; PS-sq outperforms BS-sq, especially in the realistic case. Even though BS-sq performs better at first glance, one has to be careful, as it can lead to faster entanglement breaking.

PS-sq requires only the application of three phase shift operations, rather than two variable beam splitters of the BS-sq protocol. This simplification is paid for by worse performance. Fortunately, since both protocols merge in the limit of infinite resources, the deficiency of the protocol can be compensated for by using more strongly squeezed resources. Still, for near-term experimental tests, especially in scenarios where the squeezing does not need to be controlled dynamically, squeezing by BS-sq is bound to provide better performance.

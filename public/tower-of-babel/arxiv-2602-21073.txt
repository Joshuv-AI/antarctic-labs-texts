Automata Learning with an Incomplete but Inductive Teacher
Daniel Stan, Adrien Pommellet, Juliette Jacquot

Abstract
Active automata learning (AAL) under a Minimally Adequate Teacher (MAT) has been successfully used to infer a regular language through membership and equivalence queries. This language might not be fully characterized: we are thus interested in finding any solution in a target class of possibly many regular languages. Some problems such as regular language separation or inductive invariant synthesis in the context of regular model checking (RMC) may indeed admit more than one answer. We therefore introduce IdMAT: a new teacher formalism answering queries with respect to any language in the target class, all at once. Such a teacher—tailored towards invariant synthesis—might provide incomplete “don’t know” answers, but also inductive facts of the form “if w 1 w_{1} is accepted, so is w 2 w_{2} ”. We pair IdMAT with a novel AAL algorithm L IndA L^{\text{IndA}} that 1. encodes all uncertainties as a unique SAT instance and does not fork, 2. leverages incremental SAT solving and UNSAT core analysis, and 3. handles counterexamples—of the simple or inductive type—in a frugal manner inspired by the Rivest-Schapire refinement technique. We finally evaluate a prototype implementation in the context of regular language separation and RMC.

Introduction

Active automata learning
under a
Minimally Adequate Teacher

(MAT) consists in a

regular language
L
over a finite alphabet
\Sigma

—and equivalence—given a language

, and if it does not, what is a counterexample?—queries to a

.
However, a learner may be interested in learning any language in a

Consider the

and
L_{2}
.
Multiple solutions may exist:

(resp.

) should (resp. should not) belong to

, but words

Yet, dedicated AAL techniques can yield a separator under the

inexperienced

[
16
]
or
incomplete

[
19
]

teacher framework iMAT that allows “don’t know” answers to membership queries
if a word’s membership is unspecified;
it also replaces equivalence queries with
validity
queries testing
separation.

Of particular interest is the

[
10
]
whose configurations can be represented

\Sigma

—that is,
\mathrm{Post}^{*}(S_{0})\cap\mathrm{Pre}^{*}(S_{b})=\emptyset

RMC is undecidable;
nevertheless, if there exists

\mathrm{Post}^{*}(S_{0})
and

Example 1

Two tokens are initially located at both extremities of a track of even
length

2\times n
;

n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
is a
parameter
of the system.
Both tokens can only move synchronously in opposite directions.
We want to prove that a collision can’t happen,
i.e. that the two tokens can’t end up in the same position.

A configuration is a word on the alphabet

\Sigma=\{\CIRCLE,\Circle\}
with exactly two
\CIRCLE
letters, where

\CIRCLE
represents a position occupied by a token.
The system’s starting configuration is
\CIRCLE(\Circle\Circle)^{n-1}\CIRCLE
.
Thus, the set of initial configurations for every possible value of
n

is
S_{0}=\mathcal{L}(\CIRCLE(\Circle\Circle)^{*}\CIRCLE)
.
The set of forbidden configurations is

S_{b}=\mathcal{L}(\Circle^{*}\CIRCLE\Circle\CIRCLE\Circle^{*})
(the two tokens can overlap
next step).

However, one may not always be able to directly decide whether

separates

\mathrm{Post}^{*}(S_{0})
and
\mathrm{Pre}^{*}(S_{b})
.
But another similar, sound approach known as

regular inductive invariant synthesis
can also guarantee safety:
finding a regular language
I
called an

such that

,

, and

\mathrm{Post}(I)\subseteq I
.
Language

\mathrm{Post}^{*}(S_{0})
and
\mathrm{Pre}^{*}(S_{b})
.
This approach remains

a safe system may not admit such an invariant.

Example 2

Consider Example
1
.
Neither
\mathrm{Post}^{*}(S_{0})
nor
\mathrm{Pre}^{*}(S_{b})
are regular.
But language

is a regular

The system is therefore safe for any value of

Were we to learn regular inductive invariants, we can easily check whether a

submitted to the teacher is inductive, i.e.

\mathrm{Post}(H)\subseteq H

if it is not, an
inductive counterexample

c_{1}\in H\not\rightarrow c_{2}\in H
such that
c_{2}\in\mathrm{Post}^{*}(\{c_{1}\})
can
be found.
Similarly, if
w_{2}\in\mathrm{Post}^{*}(\{w_{1}\})
for some words

and
w_{2}
, then

the learner can make use of the

Chen et al.

As RMC amounts to determining whether the reachable fragment

developed in order to compute this language, be it

[

]
, widening
[

]

[

]
or AAL
[

,
21

20
]

Other recent techniques combine abstraction with learning, and look for

[
8
,
7
]
.

Chen et al.
[
6
]
use
L^{*}
with a

(resp.
non-strict
) as it returns

(resp.
1
), thus targeting

It may be that neither are regular, despite a regular invariant existing:

.

Neider et al.
[
20
,
21
]
compute regular inductive
invariants with an

-like AAL algorithm that allows “don’t know”

The IdMAT framework formalizes these various use cases by framing

target class
.
A lack of consensus within the target class—a word is accepted by some targets

Moreover, among the

However,

does not necessarily return the smallest automaton in the

this property is of limited use for RMC, as any inductive invariant proves

Finally, rather than indiscriminately adding the entire set of
prefixes
[

,
20

21
]
or suffixes
[

]

Active automata learning

We recommend Steffen et al.’s chapter

The MAT framework.

Let

The

\mathtt{MEM}
and
\mathtt{EQ}

Data structures.

Angluin’s

[
2

maintains two sets

They determine the MQs to be submitted to the MAT, the answers to which being

Definition 3

An

\mathfrak{T}:(P\cup(P\cdot\Sigma))\times S\to\{0,1\}

such that
\mathfrak{T}(p,s)=\mathtt{MEM}(p\cdot s)

(also written
\mathfrak{T}(p\cdot s)=\mathtt{MEM}(p\cdot s)
).

Set

is called the

, and

\mathfrak{C}=(P\cup(P\cdot\Sigma))\cdot S

\mathfrak{T}

The latter is the set of words one needs to query to fill in the table.

An OT

induces a congruence relation

such that

if and only if
\forall s\in S
,
\mathtt{MEM}(w_{1}\cdot s)=\mathtt{MEM}(w_{2}\cdot s)

w_{1}
and

of

are

Note that
\tequiv
under-approximates
\lequiv

w_{1}\nottequiv w_{2}\rightarrow w_{1}\notlequiv w_{2}
.

An OT is

\forall p_{1},p_{2}\in P
such that

:

It is

\forall w\in P\cdot\Sigma
,

\exists p\in P
,
p\tequiv w
:
any line labeled by a word

.
If

A sharp and closed OT induces a

\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{T}}=(P,\Sigma,\delta_{\mathfrak{T}},\varepsilon,F_{\mathfrak{T}})

such that

and
\forall p\in P
,

\forall a\in\Sigma
,
\delta_{\mathfrak{T}}(p,a)=\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}({p\cdot a})
.
We can extend
\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}({w})
to any

by considering

\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}({w})=\delta^{*}_{\mathfrak{T}}(p,a)
;
by design,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{T}}(\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}({w}))=\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{T}}(w)
and

\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}({p})=p
for any

.

The learning algorithm
L^{*}
.

Initially,
P=S=\{\varepsilon\}
.
Algorithm
L^{*}
queries the MAT as it computes
\mathfrak{T}
, then
seeks closure defects, i.e. whether
\exists p\cdot a\in P\cdot\Sigma

such that
\forall p^{\prime}\in P
,

.
If one such

.
The OT is therefore always kept sharp by design.
Once it is closed, the learner computes

.
If

returns a counterexample

causes a

Sharpness of

guarantees termination due to the elements of

.

\tequiv
being an under-approximation of

\lequiv
.
As

is a state cover of

and

Counterexample analysis.

A method designed by Rivest and Schapire

23

Intuitively, it consists in replacing a prefix of

Formally, for

, we consider the

-th

—we may omit

whenever contextually relevant—and its

.

Definition 4

A

to

is

s.t.

\alpha_{w}({i})\neq\alpha_{w}({i+1})

If

\prescript{i}{}{w}
separates

Given a set of

, we consider the set

\varphi\vcentcolon=0\mid 1\mid x\in\mathcal{V}\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\varphi\lor\varphi\mid\varphi\rightarrow\varphi\mid\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi

of

(PFs)

.
A

is a partial function

.
A valuation

is a subset of

in terms of

\mathcal{V}\times\{0,1\}
.

occuring in

whenever the latter is defined.

A

—is a

according to Boolean semantics
and every variable of
\varphi
belongs to

’s domain.
The
satisfiability
(SAT) problem for a PF

consists in determining whether a model for
\varphi
exists;

\varphi
is said to be SAT if it does, and UNSAT otherwise.

A
set of clauses
is a finite set
\mathcal{C}
of PFs in
conjunctive normal form (CNF), interpreted as the PF

\underset{c\in\mathcal{C}}{\bigwedge}{c}
.
If
\mathcal{C}
is UNSAT, an

of
\mathcal{C}
is a minimal
(according to
\subseteq
) subset
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}
such that

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is UNSAT as well.
A

is a program that decides for any set of

\mathcal{C}
whether
\mathcal{C}
is SAT or not and returns a model
m
of

\mathcal{C}
if it is.
A solver is

\mathcal{C}
and try
to solve the updated problem without resetting the solver.

3
The IdMAT Framework

3.1
Inductive oracles

We introduce an extension to the MAT framework where the target language is
not unique:
given a
target class

\mathcal{T}\subseteq 2^{\Sigma^{*}}
,
the goal of the learner in this setting is to identify
one

unspecified language
T\in\mathcal{T}
.
To this end, we introduce an inductive teacher IdMAT consisting of two
oracles:

Definition 5

An
inductive membership oracle
is a function
\mathtt{MEM}:\Sigma^{*}\times 2^{\Sigma^{*}}\to\{0,1\}\cup 2^{\Sigma^{*}\times\Sigma^{*}}

such that for any finite set
A\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
and
w\in\Sigma^{*}
:

•

If
\mathtt{MEM}(w,A)=1
then
\forall T\in\mathcal{T},w\in T
;
every target language accepts
w
.

•

If
\mathtt{MEM}(w,A)=0
then
\forall T\in\mathcal{T},w\notin T
;
every target language rejects
w
.

•

Otherwise,
\mathtt{MEM}(w,A)\subseteq\{(w_{1},w_{2})\in(A\times\{w\})\cup(\{w\}\times A)\mid\forall T\in\mathcal{T},w_{1}\in T\rightarrow w_{2}\in T\}
;
the oracle returns constraints of the form
“If
w_{1}
belongs to
T\in\mathcal{T}
, then so must
w_{2}
too.”.

Definition 6

An
inductive validity oracle
is a function

\mathtt{VAL}:\mathrm{Reg}(\Sigma)\to\{1\}\cup\Sigma^{*}\cup(\Sigma^{*}\times\Sigma^{*})

such that for any hypothesis
H\in\mathrm{Reg}(\Sigma)
:

•

\mathtt{VAL}(H)=1
if and only if
H\in\mathcal{T}
;

4.6
Termination and correctness.

Under the MAT framework for an unique target
L\in\mathrm{Reg}(\Sigma)
,

L^{*}
maintains a set
P
of words that are pairwise distinguished
under
\lequiv
.
This invariant guarantees termination and correctness:
to fix a closure defect, one adds a new element to
P
;
the algorithm ends when
P
is a state cover of
L
.

However,
L^{\text{IndA}}
’s target class may contain more than one regular language.
Nevertheless, we can prove that for any
T\in\mathcal{T}
,
there exists a subset
B\subseteq P
of words pairwise distinguished under

{\equiv}_{T}
that grows with core-induced refinements,
guaranteeing that targets of index
n+1
can be learnt after at most
n

refinements.
For any variable
v
appearing in the algorithm,
let
{v}^{(n)}
denote its value after the
n
-th UNSAT core analysis.

Lemma 1

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}
.
Assume that
L^{\text{IndA}}
has not ended after
n\in\mathbb{N}
UNSAT core
refinements.
Then there exists a prefix-closed set
B_{n}\subseteq{P}^{(n)}

of size
n+1
such that
\forall p,q\in B_{n}
,
if
p\neq q
then
p\not\equiv_{T}q
.

Theorem 4.3

Assume
\mathcal{T}
contains a language
T\in\mathcal{T}
s.t.
\equiv_{T}
has index

n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then
L^{\text{IndA}}
terminates after at most
n-1
UNSAT core refinements.

Full proofs are provided in Appendices
0.D.1
and

0.D.2
.
Core-based refinements may add more than one prefix:
hence, a target with
n+1
states might require less than
n
refinements to be
learnt.
In the worst case scenario, the algorithm may add up to

prefixes each refinement, leading to an exponential number of prefixes.
This is less likely to happen if some structure of
\mathcal{T}
can be inferred from
MQs—that is, if the teacher does not always return incomplete answers.

6
Conclusion and Further Developments

We devised an inductive learning framework IdMAT that targets classes of
languages and subsumes the incomplete learning framework iMAT
[
19
]
.
Under IdMAT, we designed a new AAL algorithm
L^{\text{IndA}}
that leverages
incremental SAT solving and UNSAT core analysis, as well as a new frugal
refinement method inspired by the well-known RS technique
[
23
]
that
we also extended to inductive counterexamples.
We implemented
L^{\text{IndA}}
and benchmarked it in the context of regular language
separation and invariant synthesis.

In the footsteps of
[
16
,
20
,
19
]
, we based
L^{\text{IndA}}
on
Angluin’s
L^{*}

[
2
]
.
However, it remains to be seen whether state-of-the-art active learning
algorithms such as
L^{\#}

[
26
]
or

L^{\lambda}

[
12
]
can be extended to the IdMAT framework.
The latter algorithm is of great interest to us as it tends
to reduce the overall
symbol complexity
—the sum of the
length of all words that have been queried—of the learning process:
indeed, given a MQ on a word
w
of length
n
,
the oracle must exhaustively compute the sets

\mathrm{Post}^{*}(\{w\}\cap\Sigma^{n})
and
\mathrm{Pre}^{*}(\{w\}\cap\Sigma^{n})
(see
Section
3.2
);
the smaller the
n
, the faster the computation.
Another promising lead to explore these sets consists in
relying on GPUs and a matrix-based representation of the finite transducer
that encodes the system’s transitions.

Finally, we should also investigate the use of
symbolic
alphabets,
as RTS are often partially or fully encoded symbolically
[
9
]
.

0.C.1
Definitions for a two-dimensional analysis

Definition 11
(Two-dimensional incomplete evaluation predicate)

Let
(w_{1},w_{2})
be an inductive counterexample to
\mathcal{H}_{m}
.
For
(i,j)\in[{0}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{|w_{1}|}]\times[{0}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{|w_{2}|}]
,
we define
\alpha_{w}({i,j})
as follows:

1.

if
\mathtt{MEM}(c_{w_{1},i}),\mathtt{MEM}(c_{w_{2},j})\in\{0,1\}
, then

\alpha_{w}({i,j})=\mathtt{MEM}(c_{w_{1},i})\rightarrow\mathtt{MEM}(c_{w_{2},j})
,

2.

else if
(c_{w_{1},i},c_{w_{2},j})\in\mathtt{MEM}(c_{w_{1},i},\{c_{w_{2},j}\})
, then
\alpha_{w}({i,j})=1
,

3.

else if
m[x_{c_{w_{1},i}}]
,
m[x_{c_{w_{2},j}}]
are defined,
then
\alpha_{w}({i,j})=m[x_{c_{w_{1},i}}]\rightarrow m[x_{c_{w_{2},j}}]
,

4.

else
\alpha_{w}({i,j})=\square
.

Property 2

\alpha_{w}({0,0})=1
and
\alpha_{w}({|w_{1}|,|w_{2}|})=0
.

Let
\preceq
be the partial order relation on

[{0}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{|w_{1}|}]\times[{0}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{|w_{2}|}]
such that

(i,j)\preceq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\leftrightarrow(i\leq i^{\prime})\land(j\leq j^{\prime})
.
We consider
rectangles
instead of intervals:

Definition 12

A
Breaking Rectangle
(BR) is a rectangle

[{i}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{i^{\prime}}]\times[{j}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{j^{\prime}}]
such that

(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in[0:|w_{1}|]\times[0:|w_{2}|]
,

(i,j)\prec(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
,

\{\alpha_{w}({i,j}),\alpha_{w}({i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})\}\subseteq\{0,1\}
, and

\alpha_{w}({i,j})\neq\alpha_{w}({i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})
.

0.C.2
Proof of Property
2

Property
2
guarantees that a BR always
exists.

Proof

Note that
\alpha_{w}({0,0})=1
due to

(c_{w_{1},0},c_{w_{2},0})=(w_{1},w_{2})

being an inductive pair by definition.
Moreover,
\mathcal{H}_{m}(w_{i})=\nu_{m}(c_{w_{i},|w_{i}|})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.
But
\mathcal{H}_{m}(w_{1})\not\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{m}(w_{2})
as
(w_{1},w_{2})
is an
inductive counterexample.
Thus

\nu_{m}(c_{w_{1},|w_{1}|})\not\rightarrow\nu_{m}(c_{w_{2},|w_{2}|})
.
and
\alpha_{w}({|w_{1}|,|w_{2}|})=0
. ∎

Proof

\mathcal{C}_{P,S^{\prime}}
.

, the same hypothesis
\mathcal{H}_{m}

w=(w_{1},w_{2})

Due to
\{\prescript{i}{}{w_{1}},\ldots,\prescript{i^{\prime}}{}{w_{1}}\}\cup\{\prescript{j}{}{w_{2}},\ldots,\prescript{j^{\prime}}{}{w_{2}}\}\subseteq S^{\prime}
, note that

(i)

c_{w_{1},k}
(resp.
c_{w_{2},l}
) belongs to

\mathfrak{C}
for any
k\in[{i}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{i^{\prime}}]

(resp.
l\in[{j}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{j^{\prime}}]
);
thus, if
c_{w_{1},k}\in U
(resp.
c_{w_{2},l}\in U
), then

(ii)

m^{\prime}[c_{w_{1},k}]
(resp.
m^{\prime}[c_{w_{2},l}]
) is
defined.

Note that
\alpha_{w}({i,j})\neq\alpha_{w}({i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})

Moreover,
\alpha_{w}({k,l})\in\{0,1\}

for every
(k,l)\in[{i}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{i^{\prime}}]\times[{j}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{j^{\prime}}]
by

ii
.
Thus, rectangle
[{i}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{i^{\prime}}]\times[{j}\mathrel{{.}{.}}\penalty{j^{\prime}}]

Horizontal (1, 0).

Appendix 0.D
Termination and Correctness of
L^{\text{IndA}}

0.D.1
Proof of Lemma
1

Proof

We use a proof by induction on
n
, the base case
n=0
being trivially
satisfied by
B_{0}=\{\varepsilon\}
.
Let us build
B_{n+1}
under the assumption that
B_{n}
exists.

Consider
\mathcal{K}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}_{P,S}}^{(n)}
the
n
-th UNSAT core.
Suppose by contradiction that for any pair
(p,a)\in B_{n}\times\Sigma

such that
(\mathtt{clos}_{p,a})\in\mathcal{K}
,
there exists
q\in B_{n}
such that
pa\equiv_{T}q
.
For any
(p,a)\in B_{n}\times\Sigma
, we define
q_{pa}
as follows:

•

if
pa\in B_{n}
,
q_{pa}=pa
;

•

else if
(\mathtt{clos}_{p,a})\in\mathcal{K}
but
pa\notin B_{n}
, we pick some

q_{pa}\in B_{n}
such that
q_{pa}\equiv_{T}pa
;

•

otherwise
q_{pa}=\varepsilon
—we pick an arbitrary value.

Intuitively, we use
q_{pa}
to find representatives in
B_{n}
of the pairs

(p,a)
such that
(\mathtt{clos}_{p,a})\in\mathcal{K}
.
Let us now define a model
m
induced by
B_{n}
and
T
s.t.:

•

For all
p\in{P}^{(n)}
,
m[b_{p}]=1
if and only if

p\in B_{n}
.

•

For all

(p,a,p^{\prime})\in{P}^{(n)}\times\Sigma\times{P}^{(n)}
,

m[e_{p,a,p^{\prime}}]=1
if and only if
p\in B_{n}
and
p^{\prime}=q_{pa}
.

•

For all
w\in\Sigma^{*}
such that
x_{w}
appears in some clause in

\mathcal{K}
,
m[x_{w}]=1
if and only if
w\in T
.

Let us prove that
m
satisfies every formula that can appear in
\mathcal{K}
:

(\mathtt{basis}_{\varepsilon})
.

Since
B_{n}
is non-empty and prefix-closed,
it contains
\varepsilon
, hence
m[b_{\varepsilon}]=1
and

m\vDash(\mathtt{basis}_{\varepsilon})
.

(\mathtt{basis}_{pa})
.

If
m[b_{pa}]=1
, then
pa\in B_{n}
by definition
of
m
, so
p\in B_{n}
,
m[b_{p}]=1
, and
m\vDash(\mathtt{basis}_{pa})
.

(\mathtt{reach}_{pa})
.

If
m[b_{pa}]=1
, then
pa\in B_{n}
and

q_{pa}=pa
, thus
m[e_{p,a,pa}]=1
and
m\vDash(\mathtt{reach}_{pa})
.

(\mathtt{congr}_{p,a,p^{\prime},s})
.

If
m\vDash e_{p,a,p^{\prime}}
, then
p^{\prime}=q_{pa}

and
p^{\prime}\equiv_{T}pa
, hence

m\vDash\nu(x_{pas})\leftrightarrow\nu(x_{p^{\prime}s})

and
m\vDash(\mathtt{congr}_{p,a,p^{\prime},s})
.

(\mathtt{det}_{p,a,p_{1},p_{2}})

If
m[e_{p,a,p_{1}}]=1
, then

p_{1}=q_{pa}
, thus
p_{2}\neq q_{pa}
, hence
m[e_{p,a,p_{2}}]=0
and

m\vDash(\mathtt{det}_{p,a,p_{1},p_{2}})
.

(\mathtt{clos}_{p,a})
.

If
m[b_{p}]=1
, then
p\in B_{n}
and

m[e_{p,a,q_{pa}}]=1
, thus
m\vDash(\mathtt{clos}_{p,a})
.

(\mathtt{succ}_{p,a,p^{\prime}})
.

If
m[e_{p,a,p^{\prime}}]=1
then
p\in B_{n}
, thus

m[b_{p}]=1
and
m\vDash(\mathtt{succ}_{p,a,p^{\prime}})
.

(\mathtt{sharp}_{p,a,p^{\prime}})
.

If
m[b_{pa}]=1
and
m[b_{p^{\prime}}]=1
,
by definition of
m
,
m[e_{p,a,p^{\prime}}]=1
if and only if
p\in B_{n}
and

p^{\prime}=q_{pa}
.
But if
pa\in B_{n}
,
q_{pa}=pa
, and
p^{\prime}\neq q_{pa}
, thus

m[e_{p,a,p^{\prime}}]=0
and
m\vDash(\mathtt{sharp}_{p,a,p^{\prime}})
.

(\mathtt{ind}_{w_{1},w_{2}})
.

Since
T\in\mathcal{T}
,

w_{1}\in T\rightarrow w_{2}\in\ T
and we also have
m\vDash(\mathtt{ind}_{w_{1},w_{2}})
.

Thus,
m\vDash\mathcal{K}
, but
\mathcal{K}
is meant to be UNSAT;
there is a contradiction.

As a consequence, there exists
p\in B_{n}
and
a\in\Sigma
such that

(\mathtt{clos}_{p,a})\in\mathcal{K}
and for all
q\in B_{n}
,
pa\not\equiv_{T}q
.
In particular,
pa
is a freshly added prefix to
{P}^{(n+1)}
, distinct
from all elements in
B_{n}
.
We therefore define
B_{n+1}=B_{n}\uplus\{pa\}
,
finally proving the inductive case. ∎

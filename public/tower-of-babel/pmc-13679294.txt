GLP-1R agonism in breast cancer: a metabolic–oncogenic switch?
Authors: Angeliki Margoni, Kostas A. Papavassiliou, Athanasios G. Papavassiliou
Published: 2026

The intersection of obesity-related metabolic dysfunction and malignant cell growth raises a compelling hypothesis that reversing obesity-driven insulin resistance, chronic inflammation, and maladaptive endocrine signaling could reshape the systemic milieu that supports breast cancer growth. In this context, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) have emerged as potential modulators of the metabolic–oncogenic interface. Obesity may promote breast cancer through interconnected insulin/insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R), adipokine, inflammatory, and phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)–AKT (PKB)–mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathways, reinforced by leptin–Janus kinase 2 (JAK2)–signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), nuclear factor kappaB (NF-κB) potentiation, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2/3 (HER2/HER3) crosstalk, and reduced adiponectin-activated protein kinase (AMPK) activity, favoring tumor cell proliferation, survival, invasion, and angiogenesis [1].

GLP-1RAs may counter several of these processes by improving the leptin–adiponectin balance, attenuating insulin/IGF-1R–AKT and JAK2–STAT3 signaling, restoring AMPK-dependent restraint of mTOR, and reducing NF-κB- and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)-associated inflammatory and angiogenic activity [1], [2], [3]. In parallel, direct GLP-1R–cyclic AMP (cAMP) signaling may further limit breast cancer cell proliferation [1]. Yet, GLP-1RAs effect is not uniformly antitumoral: in experimental triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), liraglutide promoted angiogenesis through reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase 4 (NOX4)-dependent reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation and VEGF induction [1], underscoring a context-dependent and potentially bidirectional interaction between GLP-1R signaling and tumor biology [1]. To elucidate this paradox requires to define whether GLP-1RAs can simultaneously restrain breast cancer proliferation while promoting vascular support, and identifying the tumor intrinsic, stromal, endothelial, and metabolic determinants that shift this balance toward benefit or harm.

Beyond asking whether GLP-1RAs are intrinsically “pro-” or “anti-tumorigenic,” research should address which cellular and microenvironmental compartments determine their net biological effect. Although current evidence is experimentally immature, mechanistically heterogeneous, and insufficiently clinically validated, biological responses vary with tumor phenotype, GLP-1R distribution, HER2/IGF-1R activity, adipokine milieu, exposure conditions, and host metabolic state, ranging from tumor adaptation to cellular stress and apoptosis [1], [2], [3]. Experimental data suggests that the GLP-1RA liraglutide may reduce proliferation and migration while attenuating PI3K–AKT–mTOR signaling [2], with phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) and AMPKα activation, suppression of hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF)-related glycolytic pathways, amelioration of adipokine profiles, and induction of oxidative stress-associated apoptosis [2], [3]. Clinically, GLP-1RA exposure is related to lower breast cancer incidence, but such findings cannot distinguish the effects of weight loss and systemic metabolic improvement from direct tumor cell signaling [4].

In conclusion, further investigation needs to focus on a precision framework that circumvents this ambiguity by integrating markers of insulin resistance and circulating insulin/IGF-1 with tumor IGF-1R/HER2 and hormone receptor signaling, leptin–adiponectin balance, oxidative stress, and VEGF-related angiogenic activity, while accounting for tumor subtype and clinically relevant drug exposure [1], [2], [3]. The objective is not merely to identify responders, but to define a biomarker signature that distinguishes cases in which GLP-1RAs suppress proliferative and metabolic signaling from those where compensatory angiogenic or adaptive pathways prevail, thereby enabling rational selection of patients most likely to derive an antitumor effect. Such biomarker-guided stratification may identify contexts wherein GLP-1R agonism favors AMPK–PI3K/AKT/mTOR–NF-κB suppression over NOX4–ROS–VEGF angiogenic signaling, thus shifting the balance toward antitumor activity.

Investigation of the neutron-proton effective mass splitting via heavy ion collisions: Constraints and Implications
Junping Yang, Meiqi Sun, Ying Cui, Yangyang Liu, Zhuxia Li, Kai Zhao, Yingxun Zhang

Abstract
The neutron-proton effective mass splitting ( Δ ​ m n ​ p ∗ \Delta m^{*}_{np} ) is investigated through analyses of heavy-ion collisions using the improved quantum molecular dynamics (ImQMD) model with both standard and extended Skyrme interactions. By uncovering the strong correlation between the slope of the neutron-to-proton yield ratio with respect to the kinetic energy (i.e., S n / p S_{n/p} ) and Δ ​ m n ​ p ∗ \Delta m^{*}_{np} , we reveal that the constraints of the neutron-proton effective mass splitting via heavy ion collisions depend on the kinetic energy region of the emitted nucleons. At low kinetic energies, the data favor m n ∗ > m p ∗ m_{n}^{*}>m_{p}^{*} which is consistent with the nucleon-nucleus scattering analysis, while at high kinetic energies, they favor m n ∗ < m p ∗ m_{n}^{*}<m_{p}^{*} . Our findings partly resolve the longstanding discrepancy in the constraints of neutron-proton effective mass splitting with heavy ion collisions and nucleon-nucleus scattering, and significantly advance the understanding of nucleon effective mass splitting through heavy ion collisions.

The nucleon effective mass is utilized to describe the motion of nucleons in a momentum-dependent potential, equivalent to the movement of a quasi-nucleon with mass

On the side of HICs, the possible reasons for this discrepancy could be due to the complex collision mechanism, and the lack of sensitive experimental observables that is strongly correlated with the neutron-proton effective mass splitting.

The version of ImQMD model we used is as the same as that in Ref.
Yang et al. 2024
, the Skyrme potential energy density without the spin-orbit term is used,

u_{\text{sky}}=u_{\text{loc}}+u_{\text{nonloc}}.

(2)

The local potential energy density is,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{\text{loc}}&=\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\rho_{0}}+\frac{\beta}{\gamma+1}\frac{\rho^{\gamma+1}}{\rho_{0}^{\gamma}}+\frac{g_{\text{sur}}}{2\rho_{0}}(\nabla\rho)^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{g_{\text{sur,iso}}}{\rho_{0}}[\nabla(\rho_{n}-\rho_{p})]^{2}\\
&\quad+A_{\text{sym}}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\delta^{2}+B_{\text{sym}}\frac{\rho^{\gamma+1}}{\rho_{0}^{\gamma}}\delta^{2}.\end{aligned}

(3)

\rho=\rho_{n}+\rho_{p}
is the nucleon density and
\delta=(\rho_{n}-\rho_{p})/\rho
is the isospin asymmetry. The
\alpha
is the parameter related to the two-body term.
\beta
and
\gamma
are related to the non-linear density dependent interaction term.
g_{\text{sur}}
and
g_{\text{sur,iso}}
are related to the surface terms.
A_{\text{sym}}
and
B_{\text{sym}}
are the coefficients in the symmetry potential and come from the two-body and non-linear density dependent interaction terms
Zhang and Li 2006
;
Zhang et al. 2020a
.

The nonlocal potential energy density
u_{\text{nonloc}}
in Eq.(
2
) is used as,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{\text{nonloc}}&=\tilde{C}_{0}\sum_{ij}\int\text{d}^{3}p\text{d}^{3}p^{\prime}f_{i}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{p})f_{j}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{p^{\prime}})g(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p^{\prime}})\\
&+\tilde{D}_{0}\sum_{ij\in n}\int\text{d}^{3}p\text{d}^{3}p^{\prime}f_{i}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{p})f_{j}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{p^{\prime}})g(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p^{\prime}})\\
&+\tilde{D}_{0}\sum_{ij\in p}\int\text{d}^{3}p\text{d}^{3}p^{\prime}f_{i}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{p})f_{j}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{p^{\prime}})g(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p^{\prime}}).\end{aligned}

(4)

Here,
g(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p^{\prime}})
is a phenomenological momentum-dependent interaction (MDI). For extended Skyrme MDI, it is written as,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} g(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p^{\prime}})&=\sum_{I=0}^{N}b_{I}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})^{2I}.\end{aligned}

(5)

The number of
N
, the interaction parameters
b_{I}
,
\tilde{C}_{0}
and
\tilde{D}_{0}
in Eq.(
5
) and Eq.(
4
) are determined by fitting the Hama optical potential data

Yang et al. 2024
. The parameter

is used to determine the shape of the MDI, and its dimension is GeV
2-2I
for keeping the dimension of
g(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p^{\prime}})
in GeV
2
. For the standard Skyrme MDI, only
(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})^{2}
term is kept and the coefficients are determined from the standard Skyrme interaction sets as in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2014

The present results for
\Delta m^{*}_{np}/\delta
underscore the complexities involved in constraining effective mass splitting via heavy-ion collisions (HICs). In HICs, nucleons emitted with low and high kinetic energies originate from different stages and density regions. Specifically, low-energy nucleons are primarily emitted from the low-density regions during the late stages, while high-energy nucleons predominantly originate from the high-density regions in the early stages. Consequently, nucleons with varying kinetic energies carry information about the symmetry potential at different densities and momenta. This intricate behavior cannot be fully encapsulated by a single Skyrme parameter set characterized by a fixed neutron-proton effective mass splitting (
\Delta m^{*}_{np}
). The current calculations suggest two key points. First, the symmetry potential may exhibit a non-monotonic dependence on momentum, decreasing initially and then increasing, a feature not achievable with standard Skyrme interactions. Notably, this behavior has been predicted by finite-range Gogny forces, such as D1S and D250
Sellahewa and Rios 2014
;
Chen et al. 2012
;
Sun et al. 2025
, which warrants further investigation. Second, the high-momentum tail of the nucleon momentum distribution in the initial nucleus may influence the neutron-to-proton yield ratio (
R_{n/p}
) at high kinetic energies, thereby affecting the interpretation of the experimental data. Understanding both of them not only needs improvements in the mean field potential and short-range correlations in the transport models, but also needs to cross-check with the nucleonic collective flow observables, such as the collective flow of light particles
Tsang et al. 2024
and neutrons, together with higher-precision measurements on
R_{n/p}
spectra for the reaction systems at different beam energies in the future.

{}^{112,124}\mathrm{Sn}+^{112,124}\mathrm{Sn}

S_{0}

By comparing the calculated results of
S_{n/p}
to the data, we find that the effective Skyrme interaction with a fixed neutron-proton effective mass splitting fails to accurately describe the
S_{n/p}

m_{n}^{*}>m_{p}^{*}

m_{n}^{*}<m_{p}^{*}

Chen et al. 2012
;
Sellahewa and Rios 2014
;
Sun et al. 2025
and the-nucleon collision mechanism
Wada 2017

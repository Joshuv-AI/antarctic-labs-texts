Simulation and optimization of train brake disc based on coupling characteristics of FEM and thermal flow density method
Authors: Guoqing Qi, Peigang Jiao, Weibo Du, Yanan Zhang, Bowen Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study proposes a numerical simulation method based on the coupling of the FEM and HFDM to analyze the temperature field and stress field distribution of high-speed train brake discs under extreme operating conditions. Through multiphysics coupling analysis, the research optimizes the ventilation structure design of brake discs, significantly improving their thermal stability and structural strength. The method combines FEM’s geometric discretization capability with HFDM’s heat conduction simulation advantages, incorporating temperature-dependent material parameters and anisotropic heat flux formulas to enhance simulation accuracy under high-temperature conditions. Simulation analysis of three ventilation structures (standard cylindrical holes, inclined holes, and hybrid holes) was conducted using the ANSYS platform. The results demonstrate that the optimized design significantly reduces peak temperatures and stresses, with the hybrid hole design achieving a 15.8% reduction in peak temperature and a 45.4% reduction in peak stress. The biomimetic “plant breathing stomata” structure further optimizes airflow organization and improves heat dissipation efficiency. Mesh independence analysis and experimental validation confirm the method’s reliability, with simulation errors below 5%.

Introduction

As one of the core components of the train braking system, the performance of the train brake disc directly affects the safety and stability of the train. In actual operation, train brake discs often face high temperature, high friction, and complex working environments, especially in the case of emergency braking or long-term braking, the brake discs will experience drastic temperature changes and stress fluctuations1,2. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the temperature field and stress field characteristics of train brake discs to improve the design and performance optimization of brake discs3,4.

The numerical simulation method based on FEM can simulate the thermal response and stress distribution of the brake disc under different working conditions, and propose an improved design scheme to optimize the performance of the train brake disc. The coupling method of multiphysics analysis of the thermal flux method and the FEM enables the simultaneous study of the temperature field5,6distribution of the brake disc and the temperature-induced thermal stress distribution, so as to evaluate the thermal stability and structural strength of the brake disc.

Their proposed optimized pad structure with improved cooling channels reduced peak temperatures by 12% while enhancing braking stability11.

Zhao’s team conducted comparative studies on aluminum alloy and cast steel brake discs, revealing that aluminum’s higher thermal conductivity (160 W/m·K) resulted in 18% lower peak temperatures than steel. However, steel demonstrated superior mechanical strength at high temperatures12.

Utilizing the ANSYS simulation platform, this study integrates the HFDM with FEM-based multiphysics analysis to establish a comprehensive computational framework. This framework encompasses model construction, material selection, meshing techniques, boundary condition application, and coupled field analysis. The research focuses on evaluating the dynamic performance of brake discs with various ventilation designs under extreme operating conditions, comparing both conventional and innovative configurations to propose optimized solutions. Our methodology demonstrates FEM’s capability to simulate complex multiphysics interactions while addressing critical engineering challenges: improving heat dissipation efficiency, reducing temperature gradients, and minimizing thermal stress concentrations. Furthermore, the study examines current limitations in FEM applications, including computational resource requirements, the accuracy-efficiency balance in multiphysics simulations, and the need for experimental validation11,14.

On the basis of the experimental results of previous experiments, the feasibility of the brake disc model of heat dissipation ribs is verified. In this study, a vent brake disc model was established, and the heat dissipation of the vent brake disc was compared. Firstly, the FEM and its application are introduced, including the coupling method of multiphysics analysis of heat flux density method and thermal-structure coupling, and the implementation of related formulas15,16. Secondly, the specific parameters of the model were determined, the brake disc model was established with SolidWorks, and then the brake disc was imported into ANSYS for multi-physics coupling simulation analysis, and finally, the advantages and disadvantages of different types of brake discs in the temperature field and stress field were compared and analyzed, and the conclusions were given17,18.

Methods

Calculation method

This study employs a coupled numerical approach integrating the FEM with the HFDM to address complex multiphysics problems involving thermo-structural interactions. The methodology leverages FEM’s inherent capability to discretize intricate physical domains into finite elements, enabling precise numerical analysis of structural geometry, material properties, and loading conditions. Concurrently, HFDM effectively models heat conduction processes through heat flux distribution characterization, utilizing heat flux as thermal source terms19. The coupling mechanism combines the temperature field derived from HFDM with the stress field obtained through FEM20,21, establishing a bidirectional interaction where thermal and mechanical fields mutually influence each other to determine the final coupled solution22.

During the coupling process in Fig. 1, the geometry needs to be defined and the structure meshed. For the heat flux method, the meshing determines the calculation accuracy of the heat transfer process, while for FEM, the mesh accuracy directly affects the calculation accuracy of the mechanical response23. Next, in the heat flux method, the heat conduction equation is solved based on the heat source and boundary conditions. The temperature field distribution is obtained. This temperature field is then used as input data for the structural model in the FEM24,25. The temperature field not only affects the mechanical properties of the material, but also causes thermal stress, changing the deformation and stress distribution of the structure. Correspondingly, the deformation and stress results from the FEM analysis are fed back to the heat flux density method, correcting for the temperature field, as the deformation of the structure affects the conduction path and efficiency of the heat flow, resulting in a complex nonlinear coupling process22,26. In this paper, the iterative solution method is adopted, the temperature field is calculated first, then the temperature field is applied to the structure for stress calculation, and then the deformation effect caused by stress is transmitted back to the heat conduction analysis. Through continuous iteration, the change of the temperature field and the stress field tends to converge27.

Temperature field analysis

In traditional kinetic energy calculations, formulas \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$W=\frac{1}{2}M{\upsilon ^2}$$\end{document} are widely used to calculate the kinetic energy of an object. However, during high-speed driving and braking, the conversion of kinetic energy is not only dependent on mass and velocity, but also closely related to the physical properties of frictional contact. Therefore, an improved kinetic energy formula based on tribothermal conversion is proposed, which considers the temperature dependence of the friction coefficient, the influence of contact area and the tribothermal conversion efficiency on the basis of the classical kinetic energy formula29.5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$W=\frac{1}{2}M{\upsilon ^2} \cdot (1+\beta \cdot \mu (T) \cdot A)$$\end{document}

where: W is the amount of kinetic energy converted into heat energy (unit: J), M is the mass of the object (unit: kg), V is the velocity of the object (unit: m/s), µ (T) is the temperature-dependent30 friction coefficient, which changes with the change of temperature T, A is the frictional contact area (unit: m²), and β is the correction coefficient of frictional energy conversion, which represents the relationship between the frictional heat conversion efficiency and the contact area31.

The correction factors µ (T) and A in the formula allow the model to better reflect the energy conversion of the brake disc during friction. For the tribothermal analysis, we used the friction coefficient model µ(T) based on temperature variation, combined with the change in the contact area between the brake disc and the brake pad, and proposed a method to dynamically adjust the correction coefficient β32.

Thermal-structural coupling feedback equations

In this paper, a complete boundary condition system is set up. In terms of structural analysis, the complete fixed constraint of the hub mounting hole, the symmetrical surface displacement constraint, the constant normal pressure load and the rotational centrifugal force are set. The theory of stomata in plant respiration

Plant stomata serve as the primary pathway for water vapor loss during transpiration and are also crucial gateways for gas exchange in photosynthesis and respiration, playing a key regulatory role in plant water metabolism and gas exchange. From a biomimetic perspective, the heat-dissipating ribs of a ventilated brake disc and their surrounding space can be analogized to plant internal tissues, while the outer surface of the friction ring and the external environment correspond to the plant’s surroundings. During braking, the vortex gases trapped around the ribs can be regarded as metabolic byproducts requiring expulsion. By incorporating a biomimetic stomatal structure on the brake disc surface, the exhaust path of vortex gases can be optimized, enhancing internal airflow and thereby improving the cooling efficiency of the braking system. This bioinspired design strengthens heat exchange by improving airflow organization, aligning with fundamental principles of fluid dynamics and heat transfer13. The bionic “plant breathing stomata” structure regulates the flow field characteristics through geometric parameter design, thereby enhancing convective heat transfer. Its core mechanism can be explained in combination with the principle of flow analysis: From the perspective of geometric design, the non-uniform diameter gradient causes cross-sectional changes in the gas flow channel. According to the principle of fluid continuity, the gas flow velocity increases with the contraction of the cross-section. The high-speed gas flow can shear and thin the thermal boundary layer on the surface of the heat dissipation ribs - the thermal boundary layer is the main source of resistance for convective heat transfer, and a reduction in its thickness can directly lower the thermal resistance. The 15° inclined guide surface changes the incident direction of the airflow, causing the airflow that originally passed perpendicularly through the channel to flow along the rib wall. This extends the contact path between the airflow and the rib surface in terms of flow characteristics. According to the convective heat transfer law, an increase in the fluid-solid contact time can enhance the heat transfer per unit time. The interlaced hole design disrupts the symmetry of the flow field, avoiding the stable reflux zone that is prone to form behind the ribs in traditional regular arrangements. The reflux zone can cause hot air to be retained, while the interlaced structure causes disturbances in the flow field, promoting continuous contact between fresh cold air and the rib surface and maintaining the temperature difference driving force required for convective heat transfer.

Simulation results and discussion of bionic brake discs

The finite element model of the brake disc is established with an initial uniform temperature of 22 °C to simulate ambient conditions. For the thermal analysis, a heat flux boundary condition is applied to the friction surface to represent the energy input during braking, while convective heat transfer conditions are imposed on all exposed surfaces to account for heat dissipation. Thermal coupling constraints are enforced at the interface surfaces to ensure temperature continuity. In the subsequent thermal stress analysis, the structural boundary conditions include complete fixation at the hub mounting holes to simulate actual assembly constraints, along with symmetry conditions applied to the axial end faces. This sequentially coupled thermo-mechanical analysis framework enables prediction of both the temperature distribution and the resulting thermal stresses, with proper consideration of temperature-dependent material behavior and thermal expansion effects.

This study investigates the thermal performance of cast iron brake discs under extreme emergency braking conditions through finite element simulation. The analysis considers a severe operational scenario characterized by an initial velocity of 300 km/h, a deceleration rate of 0.92 m/s² (equivalent to 0.094 g), and an extended braking duration of 91 s, with an assumed heat absorption coefficient of 0.798. Three distinct disc configurations are comparatively evaluated: conventional standard cylindrical holes; oblique holes; and mixed holes. The transient temperature field distributions obtained from these simulations, presented in Fig. 13, reveal variations in thermal behavior across the different ventilation geometries.

The comparative analysis of thermal and mechanical performance reveals improvements achieved through brake disc optimization. As illustrated in Fig. 12(a) and Fig. 12(b), the conventional brake disc design exhibits peak thermal and mechanical loading at 59.478 s (318.63℃) and 83.387 s (496.41 MPa) respectively. In contrast, Fig. 13 demonstrates the performance of optimized designs, with maximum temperatures reduced to 290.48 °C, 292.34 °C, and 292.43 °C for the three configurations. More importantly, the corresponding peak von Mises stresses show remarkable reductions to 606.21 MPa, 449.07 MPa, and 456.54 MPa. These results quantitatively validate the effectiveness of the geometric optimizations in simultaneously mitigating both thermal and mechanical peak loads during emergency braking conditions.

The temporal evolution of thermal and mechanical responses during braking is presented in Fig. 1440, which depict the variation curves of maximum temperature and stress elements, respectively. Comparative analysis reveals that the optimized brake disc design demonstrates thermomechanical performance throughout the entire braking process, exhibiting two advantages: lower peak temperatures at critical locations, and decreased maximum stress levels compared to conventional cylindrical-vent designs. These improvements are attributed to enhanced convective heat transfer characteristics, as the optimized geometry promotes more efficient airflow distribution and heat dissipation. The synchronized reduction in both thermal and mechanical peak loads confirms the effectiveness of the proposed design modifications in improving overall braking performance while maintaining structural integrity.

The numerical results demonstrate that the optimized brake disc design achieves thermomechanical performance enhancements. Thermal analysis reveals consistent temperature reductions of 8.83%, 8.25%, and 8.22% in critical regions compared to conventional designs41, as substantiated by statistical evaluation of braking temperature distributions. These thermal improvements yield three key operational benefits: mitigation of high-temperature material degradation mechanisms including thermal cracking and friction coefficient instability42; enhanced structural stability during extended braking operations; and improved performance consistency under high-intensity deceleration conditions43. The combined thermal-stress field optimization addresses the principal failure modes in brake disc applications while maintaining heat dissipation characteristics.

Stress analysis indicates that the optimized brake disc design has improved in mechanical performance. The research on the stress distribution law during the braking process shows that the main stress concentration areas of the inclined hole and the mixed hole have decreased by 9.57% and 8.06% respectively. These mechanical enhancements provide two advantages: enhanced structural integrity through more uniform stress distribution, and reduced risk of stress-induced fracture initiation44. The stress reduction mechanism primarily operates through optimized geometric configuration, which effectively redistributes mechanical loads while maintaining necessary structural stiffness. This improvement in mechanical performance is particularly crucial for preventing catastrophic failure modes associated with high-cycle fatigue and thermal-mechanical fatigue under repeated braking conditions.

Conclusion

This study presents a novel thermo-mechanical coupling methodology integrating heat flux analysis with FEM for brake disc performance evaluation. The developed framework successfully addresses the complex interaction between temperature and stress fields through iterative numerical solutions, overcoming limitations of conventional decoupled analysis approaches. Comprehensive multiphysics simulations reveal critical thermal and structural behavior patterns under operational conditions, while geometric optimization of ventilation features demonstrates performance enhancements in three proposed disc configurations.

This study developed a new heat fluid-finite element coupling method, which solved the problem of thermal-mechanical interaction in brake disc analysis. This method combines three key advantages: precise discretization of complex geometries, robust multi-physics coupling capability, and effective numerical solutions for nonlinear iterations. Unlike traditional methods that ignore the interaction between thermal structures, our approach captures the bidirectional coupling between the temperature field and the stress distribution, thereby enabling the simulation of extreme operating conditions. The thermal-structural coupling effect generates complex deformation patterns that traditional non-coupling analysis cannot capture.

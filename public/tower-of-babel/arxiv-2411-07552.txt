Landscape of Modular Cosmology
Renata Kallosh and, Andrei Linde

Abstract
We investigate the global structure of the recently discovered family of S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℤ ) SL(2,\mathbb{Z}) -invariant potentials describing inflationary α \alpha -attractors. These potentials have an inflationary plateau consisting of the fundamental domain and its images fully covering the upper part of the Poincaré half-plane. Meanwhile, the lower part of the half-plane is covered by an infinitely large number of ridges, which, at first glance, are too sharp to support inflation. However, we show that this apparent sharpness is just an illusion created by hyperbolic geometry, and each of these ridges is physically equivalent to the inflationary plateau in the upper part of the Poincaré half-plane.

2
Group of mappings

In the context of
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
symmetry the Poincaré half-plain

{\cal H}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}|\,{\rm\,Im}\tau>0\}

can be represented by a fundamental domain

I=\Big\{\tau\in{\cal H},|\tau|\geq 1,|{\rm\,Re}\tau|\leq{1\over 2}\Big\}

(4)

and its images. In our Figure
1
the grey fundamental domain, as well as its images, cover the whole Poincaré half-plain. One can see that these images arise via the map of the geodesics, which come closer and closer to the
y=0
boundary, but never stop. A short information on half-plane geodesics is given in Appendix
A
.

The algebraic nature of these images can be explained using the description in
[
19
]
where the
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
matrices

U=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\,,\qquad V=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&\,0\end{array}\right)\ ,

(5)

define the mappings of any point
\tau
as follows

U\tau=\tau+1\,,\qquad V\tau=-1/\tau\ .

(6)

A matrix
P
is now introduced
2
2

2

Often a different notation is used,
U=T,V=S
and therefore
P=ST
.
, and
P^{2}
and
P^{6}
follow

P=VU=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&\,1\end{array}\right)\,,\qquad P^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&-1\\
1&\,0\end{array}\right)\,,\qquad P^{6}=I\ .

(7)

SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
mapping is generated by
V
and
P^{2}
in a unique way: the action of the operators
T[r,p_{0},\dots,p_{n}]
on any point
\tau
will bring all of its modular images

T[r,p_{0},\dots,p_{n}]\,\tau=(-1)^{r}P^{2p_{0}}VP^{2p_{1}}V\dots VP^{2p_{n}}\,\tau\ ,

(8)

where

0\leq r\leq 1\,,\quad 0\leq p_{i}\leq 2\,,\quad 0\leq i\leq n\,,\quad p_{i}>0\,,\quad 0<i<n\ .

(9)

Clearly, there is an infinite amount of images for any initial value of
\tau
since the integers
n
grow unrestricted. Many other mappings are known but they are typically not unique, as different from Eq. (
8
). For example, one can use

T[q_{0};q_{1},\dots,q_{n}]=U^{q_{0}}VU^{q_{1}}V\dots VU^{q_{n}}\,,\qquad q_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\qquad 0\leq i\leq n\ .

(10)

It is useful to know that all modular images are defined in Eq. (
8
) in a unique way.
In what follows, however, for practical purposes of showing examples of the first few steps of defining the images of the saddle point, we will use the set of operators in Eq. (
10
). All operators in Eq. (
10
) are known to represent every element of SL(2,Z), although it is not a unique representation, but includes all of them, according to
[
19
]
.

This, in turn, will allow us to find a new set of Killing coordinate systems, which will allow us to establish equivalence between the sharp ridges and large inflationary plateaus.

Cartesian and Killing coordinates on hyperbolic half-plane

3.1
Translation and reflection manifest actions in Cartesian coordinates

Consider Cartesian coordinates on the half-plane

\tau=x+iy\,,\qquad y>0\ .

(11)

Kinetic term in these coordinates is
3
3

3

Our notation means that
(\partial x)^{2}
is for
(\dot{x})^{2}-(x^{\prime})^{2}
.

{3\alpha\over 4}\,{\partial\tau\partial\bar{\tau}\over({\rm Im}\tau)^{2}}={3\alpha\over 4}\,{(\partial x)^{2}+(\partial y)^{2}\over y^{2}}\ .

(12)

All isometries of this hyperbolic geometry were listed in
[
9
]
. These include the
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
translation

\tau\to\tau+1\,:\qquad x\to x+1\,,\qquad y\to y\,,

(13)

and inversion

\tau\to-{1\over\tau}\,:\qquad x+iy\to-{1\over x+iy}\,.

(14)

There is also a reflection symmetry, but only for
x

y>0

\tau\to-\bar{\tau}\,,\qquad x\to-x,\,,\qquad y\to y\,.

(15)

Thus, kinetic term in Cartesian coordinates
x={\rm Re}\,\tau,y={\rm Im}\,\tau
is manifestly symmetric under translation and reflection of
{\rm Re}\,\tau
.

Fig.
1

Various vertical bands differ by translation given in Eq. (

j(\tau)\to j(q=e^{2\pi i\tau})\ ,\qquad\overline{j(\tau)}=j(\bar{q}=e^{-2\pi i\bar{\tau}})\ .

(16)

This means that

j(\tau)\overline{j(\tau)}=j(q=e^{2\pi i\tau})\,j(\bar{q}=e^{-2\pi i\bar{\tau}})\ .

(17)

Thus, the potentials which depend on

\tau\to-\bar{\tau}

(18)

According to Eq. (

V(x,y)=V(-x,y)\ .

We can define

(20)

The horizontal line

3.2
Inversion and reflection manifest actions in Killing coordinates

We define

\tau=ie^{\Phi}\,,\quad\Phi\in\mathbb{C}\ .

(27)

Consider polar-type coordinates
\Phi=\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}(\tilde{\varphi}-i\vartheta)
in the half-plane, in the form

\tau=ie^{\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}(\tilde{\varphi}-i\vartheta)}=e^{\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\tilde{\varphi}}\Big(\sin(\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\vartheta)+i\cos(\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\vartheta)\Big)\ ,

(28)

and

-\infty<\varphi<\infty\,,\qquad-\pi/2<\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\vartheta<\pi/2\ .

(29)

These coordinates are not really polar since the angle
\vartheta
is constrained to keep
y
positive.
The kinetic term in these coordinates is

{3\alpha\over 4}\,{\partial\tau\partial\bar{\tau}\over({\rm Im}\tau)^{2}}={1\over 2}{(\partial\tilde{\varphi})^{2}+(\partial\vartheta)^{2}\over\cos^{2}(\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\vartheta)}\ ,

(30)

to be contrasted with the one in (
25
).
We have introduced these coordinates in
[
10
,
11
]
, where we called them Killing adapted coordinates since the metric does not depend on the inflaton
\tilde{\varphi}
. At
\vartheta=0
both fields
\tilde{\varphi},\vartheta
are canonical.

The relation between
\varphi,\theta
coordinates and
\tilde{\varphi},\vartheta
is the following

y=e^{\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\varphi}=e^{\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\tilde{\varphi}}\cos\Big(\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\vartheta\Big)\ ,

(31)

x=\theta=e^{\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\tilde{\varphi}}\sin\Big(\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\vartheta\Big)\ ,

(32)

or

{\tau_{1}\over\tau_{2}}=\tan\Big(\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\vartheta\Big)\,,\qquad\tau\bar{\tau}=e^{2\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\tilde{\varphi}}\ .

(33)

Note that
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi
for
\vartheta=0
, and
\theta=\sin\Big(\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\vartheta\Big)
for
\tilde{\varphi}=0
.

The symmetry under inversion is

\tau\to-{1\over\tau}\,:\qquad\Phi\to-\Phi\qquad\rightarrow\qquad\,\tilde{\varphi}\to-\tilde{\varphi}\,,\quad\vartheta\to-\vartheta\,\ .

(34)

When we will plot our 3D potentials in
\tilde{\varphi},\vartheta
coordinates, we will see manifest symmetry with respect to the transformation

\tilde{\varphi}\to-\tilde{\varphi}\,,\vartheta\to-\vartheta
.
Thus, the potentials in these coordinates will be manifestly inversion invariant.
The symmetry under translation in Killing coordinates will not be manifest.

Inversion symmetries, a priory, were expected to be only at simultaneous action on both fields in Eq. (
34

\theta\to-\theta
, see Sec.
3

\vartheta\to-\vartheta
,
according to Eq. (
32
). Therefore, we have independent symmetries in Killing coordinates,

\tilde{\varphi}\to-\tilde{\varphi}\quad\rm{and},\quad\rm{separately},\quad\vartheta\to-\vartheta

(35)

6.1

Fractals

Summary and Discussion

In this paper, we investigated the global structure of the
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
-invariant potentials describing inflationary
\alpha
-attractors

[
6
,
13
,
14
]
.
These potentials contain an inflationary plateau covering the upper part of the Poincaré half-plane. Meanwhile, the lower part of the half-plane is covered by infinitely many sharp ridges, forming a complicated fractal landscape close to the boundary of the half-plane at
\tau_{2}=0
. However, we found that each sharp ridge in the lower part of the half-plane is physically equivalent to the inflationary plateau in the upper part of the half-plane.

To describe the modular landscape and remove the distortion caused by the factor
{1\over y^{2}}
in the half-plane metric, we switched from axion-inflaton coordinates with a kinetic term

{\cal L}_{kin}^{axion-inflaton}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial\varphi)^{2}+\frac{3\alpha}{4}e^{-2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha}}\varphi}(\partial\theta)^{2}

(81)

to Killing coordinates with the kinetic term

{\cal L}_{kin}^{Killing}={1\over 2}{(\partial\tilde{\varphi})^{2}+(\partial\vartheta)^{2}\over\cos^{2}(\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\vartheta)}\ .

(82)

This coordinate system was very useful for the investigation of
\alpha
-attractors in
[
10
,
11
]
. In this paper we used this coordinate system, as well as its generalizations.

In Killing coordinates, the metric does not depend on the inflaton field
\tilde{\varphi}
, and away from the boundary
\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}\vartheta=\pm{\pi\over 2}
both
\tilde{\varphi}
and
\vartheta
are close to canonical variables.
Moreover, the

SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
invariant potentials are symmetric under inversion, which implies a simultaneous change of sign of
\tilde{\varphi}
and
\vartheta
:

\Phi\to-\Phi\,,\qquad\Phi=\sqrt{2\over 3\alpha}(\tilde{\varphi}-i\vartheta)\ .

(83)

This symmetry makes the plateaus at negative
\tilde{\varphi},\vartheta
look like exact copies of the ones at positive
\tilde{\varphi},\vartheta
.

In technical terms, we have first identified the formula defining the proliferation of the saddle points in the landscape

\tau_{n}^{saddle}={i\over c^{2}+d^{2}}+{bd+ac\over c^{2}+d^{2}}\ .

(84)

We then performed a specific restricted
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
transformation, and this gave us a set of coordinate systems that can place
any saddle point at the center of the corresponding coordinate system
.

\tau^{\pm}[a,b,c,d]=i{e^{{}^{\pm}\Phi}\over c^{2}+d^{2}}+{bd+ac\over c^{2}+d^{2}}\ .

(85)

Indeed, Eq. (
85
) shows that at
\Phi=0
we have a saddle point defined by Eq. (
84
).

Our results show that every pyramid or shark fin or ridge in Figs.

,
5

7
is actually a plateau when viewed in a proper Killing coordinate system. For example, our Figs.
9
and
13
at the center have a saddle point at

Our second example is the saddle point between two minima; one can see it in Fig.

14
at the point

The same situation was demonstrated in other examples, including the ridges and saddle points at large negative values of

16

,
-\infty<\theta<\infty

Appendix A
Geodesics in Poincaré half-plane

“Poincaré half-plane” is a specific model of hyperbolic geometry where the space is represented by the upper half of an Euclidean plane, with a special metric (the Poincaré metric) that distorts distances to model hyperbolic geometry properties; it’s a half-plane with a non-Euclidean geometry.

In hyperbolic space and in other Riemannian manifolds, a geodesic is a distance-minimizing path from one point to another. Poincaré
upper half-plane
is the upper half-plane model with
z=x+iy
defined as

{\cal H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}|\,y>0\}

(86)

The real line
y=0
is excluded since the metric is
ds^{2}={dx^{2}+dy^{2}\over y^{2}}
.
The Poincare half-plane model, which is used to study hyperbolic geometry, is considered geodesically complete, even though its geodesics appear as curves that approach the boundary of the half-plane.

To prove the geodesic completeness of the Poincare half-plane, one can show that for any two points in the half-plane, there exists a geodesic connecting them that can be extended indefinitely, which is the key characteristic of geodesic completeness; this is typically done by explicitly calculating the geodesics as either vertical lines or semicircles centered on the x-axis and demonstrating that they can be extended infinitely in both directions.

Geodesics
: A geodesic in half-plane is either a semicircle meeting the real axis at a right angle or a vertical ray emanating from a point on the real axis.

: The angle between two geodesics is defined to be the angle between the tangents to the geodesics at their intersection point. Fig.

shows two examples.

is a symmetry of the hyperbolic plane. The map
f(z)=-1/z
maps hyperbolic geodesics to hyperbolic

Mobius
In the upper half-plane, Mobius transformations map the vertical line through the origin to other vertical lines and to half-circles orthogonal to the real line.

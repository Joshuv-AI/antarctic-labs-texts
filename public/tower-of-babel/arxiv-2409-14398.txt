Note that, considering the random graph process on any graph

and

-connectedness are the same

. Bollobás
[

further generalised this to Cartesian powers of a given graph (indeed,

Throughout the paper, all asymptotics are with

Throughout this section let

Proposition 3.1

Let

,

has a unique

Observe that when

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Bin(d,p)\leq k-1)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{d}{i}p^{i}(1-p)^{d-i}=\omega(1/n),

Throughout the proof of the proposition, we will often use that

dp=O(\log n).

(1)

To see this, observe that

which implies the claim.

. Then, we utilise a sprinkling/double-exposure argument, similar in spirit to the classical argument of Ajtai, Komlós, and Szemerédi

. Note that then

has the same distribution as

. Note that using (

. Further,

satisfies that

. We then turn to show that for any set

To the task at hand, let us begin by showing that vertices of degree less than

Lemma 3.2
.

Whp
, any two vertices of degree less than
k
in
G_{p}
are at distance at least two in

.

Proof.

Consider any edge
uv
in
E
.
The probability that both

and

are vertices of degree less than

in

is at most

\mathbb{P}(Bin(d,p)<k)\mathbb{P}(Bin(d-1,p)<k).

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the event that there is an edge of

whose endpoints are vertices of degree less than
k
. Since
G
is
d
-regular, there are
\frac{dn}{2}
edges to consider. Then, by the union bound,

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})\leq\frac{dn}{2}\mathbb{P}(Bin(d,p)<k)\mathbb{P}(Bin(d-1,p)<k).

Note that
\mathbb{P}(Bin(d,p)<k)=(1+o(1))\mathbb{P}(Bin(d-1,p)<k)
since

and

is constant. Thus,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})&\leq(1+o(1))\frac{dn}{2}\left(\mathbb{P}(Bin(d,p)<k)\right)^{2}\\
&=(1+o(1))\frac{dn}{2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{d}{i}p^{i}(1-p)^{d-i}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Recall that
d\leq n^{1-a}
for some

and
p=1-\left(\frac{\phi}{n}\right)^{1/d}
for some
\phi

and (

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})&\leq(1+o(1))\frac{dn}{2}\left(O(\log^{k}n)\cdot(1-p)^{d-k}\right)^{2}\\
&=O\left(dn\log^{2k}n\cdot\frac{\phi^{2-2k/d}}{n^{2-2k/d}}\right)\\
&=O\left(\log^{2k}n\cdot\phi^{2}\cdot n^{-a+o(1)}\right)=o(1).\qed\end{split}

Lemma 3.3
.

Whp
, for any set

For

If

We consider three separate cases, according to the order of

First, suppose that

. Then, there are two vertices in

and have all their neighbours in

There are less than

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\cup_{s=2}^{k+1}\mathcal{A}_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq k\cdot dn\cdot\mathbb{P}(Bin(2d-1,p)\leq 4k+2)

\displaystyle=kdn\sum_{i=0}^{4k+2}\binom{2d-1}{i}p^{i}(1-p)^{2d-1-i}

\displaystyle\leq kdn\cdot O\left(\log^{4k+2}n(1-p)^{2d-3-4k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq O\left(n^{2-a}\cdot\log^{4k+2}n\cdot\frac{\phi^{2}}{n^{2-7k/d}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{n^{a/2}}=o(1).

Now, assume that

ways to choose a tree of order

, and it falls into

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{s})

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{k}n(ed)^{s-1}p^{s-1}(1-p)^{s(d-k-s)}

\displaystyle\leq O(n^{k+1}e^{s}\log^{s}n)\cdot(1-p)^{s(d-2\sqrt{d})}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{s})

\displaystyle\leq O(n^{k+1}e^{s}\log^{s}n)\cdot\frac{\phi^{s}}{n^{s\left(1-\frac{2}{\sqrt{d}}\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\frac{(e\cdot\phi\cdot\log n)^{s}}{n^{s\left(1-\frac{2}{\sqrt{d}}\right)-(k+1)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\frac{(e\cdot\phi\cdot\log n)^{k+2}}{n^{(k+2)\left(1-\frac{2}{\sqrt{d}}\right)-(k+1)}}\right),

By the union bound over the less than

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{s=k+2}^{\sqrt{d}}\mathcal{A}_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{d}\cdot O\left(\frac{(e\cdot\phi\cdot\log n)^{k+2}}{n^{(k+2)\left(1-\frac{2}{\sqrt{d}}\right)-(k+1)}}\right)=o(1),

where we used

.

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{k}n(edp)^{s-1}(1-p)^{(1-\epsilon)ds-dk}

5
Discussion

We proved sufficient conditions on a regular graph
G
, guaranteeing that in the random graph process on
G
the hitting times of minimum degree
k
and
k
-connectedness coincide (for any constant
k
). In particular, these conditions cover pseudo-random
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs and regular Cartesian product graphs with base graphs of bounded order (such as the
d
-dimensional binary hypercube), and confirm a conjecture of Joos
[
22
]
. We further demonstrated the tightness of our assumptions, in the sense that there are
d
-regular graphs where Property
2
holds in a weaker form, and typically the hitting time of
k
-connectedness does not coincide with that of minimum degree
k
(in fact, the probability threshold is different). It would be interesting to determine how tight our assumptions are: can one remove the
O(\log n)
factor in
2
, and thus match the local edge-expansion as in the construction given in Section
4
?

The broad strategy used here can also be found in several proofs addressing the uniqueness of the giant component in the supercritical regime, see
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
23
]
. Let us stress again that all that is needed for the proof of our result is some knowledge of the edge isoperimetric profile of the graph. Indeed, as noted in the introduction, Properties
1
and
2
from
Theorem

1
resemble similar assumptions in
[
14
,
15
]
, under which the uniqueness of the giant component in a general setting was shown (and which were shown to be essentially tight).

Further, consider an
n
-vertex graph
G
with minimum degree
d
and maximum degree
\Delta=O(d)
, with
d\leq n^{1-a}
for some constant
a>0
. If
G
has Property
1
, and satisfies that for every
U\subseteq V(G),|U|\leq Cd\log n
we have that
|E(G[U])|\leq\epsilon d|U|
for large enough
C
(with respect to
c,k
and
\Delta/d
), it follows from the above proof of Theorem
1
that
whp
the hitting time of minimum degree at least
k
and of
k
-connectedness are the same. It would be interesting to see whether the same holds without the assumption that
\Delta=O(d)
, that is, with only the assumption of minimum degree.

In
[
13
]
, the authors showed that for regular Cartesian product graphs (where the base graphs are connected and of bounded order), the hitting times of minimum degree one, connectedness and the existence of a nearly perfect matching (that is, a matching missing at most one vertex) coincide
whp
, generalising a result of Bollobás
[
5
]
on the hypercube. The existence of a perfect matching was shown using the product structure of the graph quite explicitly. Hence, the following is quite natural to ask.

Question 5.1
.

Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, what minimal assumptions on
G
suffice to guarantee that in the random graph process on
G
the hitting times of minimum degree
k
and the existence of
k
disjoint perfect matchings coincide
whp
?

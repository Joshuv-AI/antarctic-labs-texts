1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statements of the results

The problem of differentiation of integrals concerns the question of whether, or under what conditions, the value of a given function at a certain point is the limit of mean value integrals of the function over neighborhoods approximating the point. One of the classical results in this area is the celebrated Lebesgue differentiation theorem. It asserts that, for every locally integrable function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, the value
f(x)
can be recovered for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as the limit of the averages of
f
over the balls
B(x,r)
as
r\to 0
. While the same holds for centered cubes in place of balls, the case of centered rectangles with sides parallel to the coordinate axes and diameters tending to
0
requires a slightly stronger assumption on
f
. For example, the local integrability of
|f|^{p}
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
is sufficient. These three results also have uncentered counterparts, where
x
belongs to each given set but is not necessarily its center.

In this article, we explore the problem of differentiation of integrals in the context of the infinite-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
(for a detailed description of this space, see Section
4
), focusing on certain families of uncentered rectangles with sides parallel to the coordinate axes. In particular, we construct families for which the condition that
|f|^{p}
is integrable is sufficient for the corresponding variant of the Lebesgue differentiation theorem when
p\geq p_{0}
(resp.
p>p_{0}
) but not when
p<p_{0}
(resp.
p\leq p_{0}
).

Our first main result reads as follows (for the notation used, see Subsection
1.3
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
p_{0}\in[1,\infty)
. Then there exist Busemann–Feller bases
\mathcal{B}_{\geq}
and
\mathcal{B}_{>}
in the infinite-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
, whose elements are rectangles with sides parallel to the coordinate axes,
such that for every
p\in[1,\infty]
we have the following:

•

\mathcal{B}_{\geq}
differentiates
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\omega})
if and only if
p\geq p_{0}
,

•

\mathcal{B}_{>}
differentiates
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\omega})
if and only if
p>p_{0}
.

A couple of comments regarding Theorem
1.1
are in order.

(1)

Hayes
[
Ha52
,
Ha52a
,
Ha58
]
provided examples of differentiation bases in the one-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}
with similar properties. For example, he constructed a basis that differentiates
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
p\geq p_{0}
. The main novelty of Theorem
1.1
is that our examples are Busemann–Feller bases consisting of rectangles with sides parallel to the coordinate axes—something impossible in any finite-dimensional setting. For a more detailed discussion, see Subsection
1.2
.

(2)

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we explicitly construct families of rectangles so that the associated bases satisfy the given properties. Our examples are largely inspired by the objects studied in the series of articles
[
FR20
,
Ko21
,
KMPRR23
,
KRR23
]
. Moreover, they can be used to find bases in the spirit of Hayes, see Corollary
1.2
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Fix
p_{0}\in[1,\infty)
. Then there exist Busemann–Feller bases
\mathcal{B}_{\geq}^{*}
and
\mathcal{B}_{>}^{*}
in the unit interval
[0,1]
, whose elements are finite unions of open intervals, such that

\mathcal{B}_{\geq}^{*}
(resp.
\mathcal{B}_{>}^{*}
) differentiates
L^{p}([0,1])
if and only if
p\geq p_{0}
(resp.
p>p_{0}
).

With Theorem
1.1
in hand, we turn our attention to a related problem concerning the Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
for arbitrary metric measure spaces
\mathcal{X}=(X,\rho,\mu)
, and arbitrary differentiation bases
\mathcal{B}
. Namely, given a pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B})
and
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty]
such that
p_{1}<p_{2}
, we ask whether there is a relationship between the variants of the Lebesgue differentiation theorem formulated for
L^{p_{1}}(\mathcal{X})
and
L^{p_{2}}(\mathcal{X})
. Of course, the answer is clear when the space has finite measure (or when the problem reduces to such a case), since then
L^{p_{2}}(\mathcal{X})\subseteq L^{p_{1}}(\mathcal{X})
. Here, however, we only assume that
\mathcal{X}
is complete, and interestingly, in this more general setting, the answer depends on whether
p_{2}
is finite or not.

Our second main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a differentiation basis in
\mathcal{X}=(X,\rho,\mu)
. Define

{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})\coloneqq\big\{p\in[1,\infty]:\mathcal{B}\text{ differentiates }L^{p}(\mathcal{X})\big\}.

If
\mathcal{X}
is complete, then, for some
p_{0}\in[1,\infty)
, one of the following six cases occurs:

(C1)

{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})=\emptyset
,

(C2)

{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})=\{\infty\}
,

(C3)

{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})=[p_{0},\infty]
,

(C4)

{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})=(p_{0},\infty]
,

(C5)

{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})=[p_{0},\infty)
,

(C6)

{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})=(p_{0},\infty)
.

Moreover, if
X
is a countable union of (possibly overlapping) open sets with finite measure, then (regardless of whether
\mathcal{X}
is complete) one of the four cases
(C1)
–
(C4)
occurs.

Conversely, fix
p_{0}\in[1,\infty)
and
P\subseteq[1,\infty]
. If
P
takes one of the forms
(C1)
–
(C6)
, then one can find a pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B})
such that
{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})=P
. Moreover, if
P
takes one of the forms
(C1)
–
(C4)
, then one can find a pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B})
such that
{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})=P
and
\mu(X)<\infty
.

Several comments regarding Theorem
1.3
are in order.

(1)

The first part of Theorem
1.3
follows from a monotonicity property stating that the larger
p
is, the better the differentiation properties of
\mathcal{B}
are (see Proposition
2.1
). We verify this property for
p\in[1,\infty)
assuming that
\mathcal{X}
is complete, or for all
p\in[1,\infty]
assuming that
X
is a countable sum of open sets with finite measure.

(2)

Regarding the case
p\in[1,\infty)
above, we assume that
\mathcal{X}
is complete in order to better handle pathological measurability issues (see Example
2.5
and Example
2.6
). This is a very mild restriction, since there is a canonical way to extend any given measure space to a complete one. However, we do not rule out the possibility that this assumption can be completely removed.
On the other hand, we know that, in general, the range
[1,\infty)
cannot be extended to
[1,\infty]
(see Example
2.3
).

(3)

The second part of Theorem
1.3
follows by combining Example
2.3
with the family of examples constructed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(4)

We also study the relationship between the range
{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})
for a given uncentered basis
\mathcal{B}
and the behavior of the associated maximal operator (see Remark
5.3
).

1.2.
Historical background and related results

In
[
Ha52
,
Ha52a
,
Ha58
]
, Hayes provided examples of differentiation bases
\mathcal{B}^{*}
in the one-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}
exhibiting the cases
(C2)
–
(C3)
, see also
[
dG75
, Chapters VI.1 and VII.5]
. A minor adjustment of Hayes’ construction is likely to yield the case
(C4)
as well, introducing logarithmic factors as in our example for
(C4)
.

It is worth noting that the elements of Hayes’ differentiation bases
\mathcal{B}^{*}
are not intervals, but disjoint unions of intervals. In fact, in the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
for any fixed
d\in\mathbb{N}
, no Busemann–Feller basis
\mathcal{B}
made of arbitrary
d
-dimensional rectangles with sides parallel to the axes can exhibit the cases
(C1)
–
(C4)
for any
p_{0}\in(1,\infty)
. This is due to the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
bounds for the strong maximal operator for every
p\in(1,\infty]
, see
[
JMZ35
,
Fa72
,
CF75
]
. As a consequence, for each such
\mathcal{B}
we would have
{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})\in\{[1,\infty],(1,\infty]\}
.

The main novelty of the article is that we circumvent this finite-dimensional obstruction by considering differentiation bases of rectangles in
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
, recovering all the cases
(C1)
–
(C4)
.

Let us mention that in
[
HS09
]
, Hagelstein and Stokolos proved that, for any fixed
d\in\mathbb{N}
and for every homothecy invariant density basis
\mathcal{B}
, whose elements are convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one of the cases
(C3)
–
(C4)
occurs for some
p_{0}\in[1,\infty)
. They also showed in
[
HS24
]
that, if
d=2
, then a previous result of Bateman
[
Ba09
]
implies that necessarily
p_{0}=1
; not long after, however, together with Radillo-Murguía
[
HRS24
]
(see also
[
HRS24a
]
), they identified a gap in Bateman’s proof, providing a counterexample to
[
Ba09
, Claim 7(B)]
. This makes the result from
[
HS24
]
conditional upon the correctness of the main theorem in
[
Ba09
]
. In this regard, we point out that the bases we exhibit in the proof of Theorem
1.1
are far from being homothecy or even translation invariant, with the sole exception of the basis used to cover the case
(C1)
. Therefore, both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
help clarify the possible ranges of differentiation in the context of arbitrary bases, but they do not contribute to the corresponding problem formulated for homothecy invariant bases.

We conclude this section recalling the characterization of the weak
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
boundedness of a maximal function associated with a collection of subsets
\mathcal{B}
in terms of the covering properties of
\mathcal{B}
with
p_{*}
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p_{*}}=1
(for the notation used, see Section
3
).

Theorem 1.4

(
[
CF75
]
)
.

Fix
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}
be the maximal operator associated with a collection
\mathcal{B}
in a
\sigma
-finite complete metric measure space
\mathcal{X}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}
satisfies the weak-type
(p,p)
inequality,

(2)

\mathcal{B}
has the
covering property
V_{p_{*}}
, i.e., there is a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
such that, for every collection
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, there is a subcollection
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
such that

\mu\Big(\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E\Big)\leq C\mu\Big(\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F\Big),\qquad\Big\|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}{\mathds{1}_{{F}}}\Big\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathcal{X})}\leq C\mu\Big(\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}E\Big)^{\frac{1}{p_{*}}}.

An analogous characterization of the differentiability of
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
is also available.

Theorem 1.5

(
p=\infty
in
[
dP36
]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
in
[
HP55
,
Ha76
]
)
.

Fix
p\in(1,\infty]
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a differentiation basis in a
\sigma
-finite complete metric measure space
\mathcal{X}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{B}
differentiates
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
,

(2)

\mathcal{B}
has the
covering strength
\phi_{p_{*}}(x)=x^{p_{*}}
, i.e., for every measurable set
E\subseteq X
with finite measure, every differentiation basis
\mathcal{E}
of
E
, and every
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
, there is a finite collection
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
such that

\mu\Big(E\setminus\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F\Big)=0,\qquad\mu\Big(\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F\setminus E\Big)<\varepsilon,\qquad\Big\|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}{\mathds{1}_{{F}}}-{\mathds{1}_{{\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F}}}\Big\|_{L^{p_{*}}(\mathcal{X})}<\varepsilon.

Under the additional assumption that
\mathcal{B}
is translation invariant, Theorem
1.5
for
p\in(1,\infty)
was proved in
[
Co76
]
. Furthermore, analogous characterizations for Orlicz spaces were implicit in the bounds for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}
given in
[
CF75
]
, and were explicitly exhibited in the study of the differentiation properties of
\mathcal{B}
in
[
Ha76a
]
. Nevertheless, the case of
L^{1}(\mathcal{X})
remains open (specifically, the implication
(1)\implies(2)
is missing in the respective variants of both Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
).

Despite providing elegant geometric conditions, the characterizations may still be complicated to exploit, as the covering properties are difficult to assess. In the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we will rely on the construction of explicit functions to show that
p\notin{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})
.

We refer the interested reader to two surveys of Hagelstein
[
Ha24
,
Ha24a
]
for more details about results, open problems, and references in the theory of differentiation of integrals.

1.3.
Notation and basic definitions

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
metric measure space
, that is, a triple
(X,\rho,\mu)
, where
X
is a nonempty set,
\rho
is a metric, and
\mu
is a Borel measure. We say that
\mathcal{X}
is
complete
if every subset of a set of measure zero is itself measurable (and thus has measure zero). For every
x\in X
and
r\in(0,\infty)
, we define the (open) ball centered at
x
of radius
r
as

B_{\rho}(x,r)\coloneqq\{y\in X:\rho(x,y)<r\}.

Later on, we omit the index
\rho
, since there is no risk of confusion.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, the space
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
consists of all measurable functions
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that the corresponding quantity

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}\coloneqq\Big(\int_{X}|f|^{p}\,{\rm d}\mu\Big)^{1/p}

is finite. Similarly,
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
consists of all measurable functions
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}\coloneqq\inf\{C\in[0,\infty]:|f(x)|\leq C\text{ for }\mu\text{-almost every }x\in X\}

is finite. We identify two measurable functions if they are equal
\mu
-almost everywhere. We simply write
L^{p}(X)
instead of
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
when
\rho
and
\mu
are chosen canonically.

Suppose that, for every
x\in X
, we have a family
\mathcal{B}(x)
of measurable sets
S\subseteq X
such that
x\in S
and
\mu(S)\in(0,\infty)
. A sequence
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
contracts to
x
if
S_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(x)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and, moreover, there exists a sequence
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of radii such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}=0
and
S_{n}\subseteq B(x,r_{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
; in each such case we write
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Rightarrow x
.

The whole family
\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\bigcup_{x\in X}\mathcal{B}(x)
equipped with the relation
\Rightarrow
is called a
differentiation basis in
\mathcal{X}
if for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
the set of sequences contracting to
x
is nonempty. We say that
\mathcal{B}
is
uncentered
if

\mathcal{B}(x)=\{S\in\mathcal{B}:x\in S\}

for every
x\in X
. If
\mathcal{B}
is uncentered and, moreover, every
S\in\mathcal{B}
is open, then
\mathcal{B}
is called a
Busemann–Feller basis

[
dG75
, Chapter II.2]
.

Fix a pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B})
and
p\in[1,\infty]
. We say that
\mathcal{B}
differentiates
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
if, for every fixed
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
, the following condition

(D)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm Avg}_{f}(S_{n})=f(x)\quad\text{for every}\quad(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Rightarrow x

holds for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
.
Here, for any
E\subseteq X
satisfying
\mu(E)\in(0,\infty)
, we write
{\rm Avg}_{f}(E)
to denote the average of
f
over
E
, that is,

{\rm Avg}_{f}(E)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mu(E)}\int_{E}f\,{\rm d}\mu.

The set of all
p\in[1,\infty]
such that
\mathcal{B}
differentiates
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
is denoted by

{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})
. Furthermore, we say that
\mathcal{B}
is a
density basis
if it differentiates
\{{\mathds{1}_{{E}}}\colon\mu(E)\in[0,\infty]\}
, which is a strict subset of
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
.

For every
p\in[1,\infty]
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
, we define the exceptional set

\displaystyle E(f)\coloneqq\{x\in X:\overline{f}(x)\neq f(x)\text{ or }\underline{f}(x)\neq f(x)\}.

Here
\overline{f}\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty]
and
\underline{f}\colon X\to[-\infty,\infty]
are defined respectively by

\displaystyle\overline{f}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max\big\{c\in[-\infty,\infty]:\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm Avg}_{f}(S_{n})=c\text{ for some }(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Rightarrow x\big\},

\displaystyle\underline{f}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\,\min\big\{c\in[-\infty,\infty]:\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm Avg}_{f}(S_{n})=c\text{ for some }(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Rightarrow x\big\}.

Observe that
\overline{f}
and
\underline{f}
are measurable if
\mathcal{B}
is uncentered and consists of either countably many sets or arbitrarily many open sets. Indeed, for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\{x\in X:\overline{f}(x)\geq\lambda\}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,n}}S,

where
\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,n}
consists of all
S\in\mathcal{B}
such that
{\rm Avg}_{f}(S)\geq\lambda-2^{-n}
and
S\subseteq B
for some ball
B\subseteq X
of radius
2^{-n}
.
By symmetry,
\{x\in X:\underline{f}(x)\leq\lambda\}
can be described in terms of
S\in\mathcal{B}
such that
{\rm Avg}_{-f}(S)\geq-\lambda-2^{-n}
and
S\subseteq B
for some ball
B\subseteq X
of radius
2^{-n}
.

1.4.
Structure of the article

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.

In Section
2
, we formulate a monotonicity property which justifies the first part of Theorem
1.3
(see Proposition
2.1
). We also comment on the special case

In Section

In particular, we observe that nestedness interacts well with weak-type maximal inequalities in a setting that extends beyond the realm of martingale-type bases. The resulting statement may be of independent interest (see Proposition

).

In Section
4
, we deal with the infinite-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
. We introduce a class of rectangular bases in
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
that will be used to prove Theorem
1.1
(see Proposition
4.4
).

In Section
5
, we prove Theorem
1.1
, Corollary
1.2
, and Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Monotonicity property

We begin this section with an observation that leads to the first part of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix a pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B})
and
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty)
such that
p_{1}<p_{2}
. Then

p_{1}\in{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})\implies p_{2}\in{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})

if any of the following two cases occurs:

(a)

\mathcal{X}
is complete and
p_{2}<\infty
,

(b)

X=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}G_{n}
for open sets
G_{n}\subseteq X
satisfying
\mu(G_{n})<\infty
.

Proof.

First, assume that
\mathcal{X}
is complete and
p_{2}<\infty
. Fix
f\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathcal{X})
. We shall show that
E(f)
is a set of measure zero. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
f_{n}\coloneqq f\cdot{\mathds{1}_{{F_{n}}}}
, where

F_{n}\coloneqq\{x\in X:|f(x)|\geq 2^{-n}\}.

Note that
\|f-f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}\leq 2^{-n}
and
f_{n}\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathcal{X})
because

\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathcal{X})}^{p_{1}}=\int_{X}|f_{n}|^{p_{1}}\,{\rm d}\mu(x)\leq\int_{X}|f|^{p_{1}}\frac{|f|^{p_{2}-p_{1}}}{2^{-n(p_{2}-p_{1})}}\,{\rm d}\mu(x)=2^{n(p_{2}-p_{1})}\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathcal{X})}^{p_{2}}<\infty.

We also deduce that

\|\overline{f}-\overline{f_{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}\leq 2^{-n}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\underline{f}-\underline{f_{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}\leq 2^{-n}.

Now, suppose that
x\in E(f)
. Then, for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

|\overline{f}(x)-f(x)|>2^{-n+1}\quad\text{or}\quad|\underline{f}(x)-f(x)|>2^{-n+1}.

We claim that if one of these inequalities holds, then
x\in E(f_{n})
. Indeed, observe that

|\overline{f_{n}}(x)-f_{n}(x)|\geq|\overline{f}(x)-f(x)|-\|\overline{f}-\overline{f_{n}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}-\|f-f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}>0

if the first inequality holds and, analogously,
|\underline{f_{n}}(x)-f_{n}(x)|>0
if the second inequality holds.
This proves our claim and, consequently, we obtain
E(f)\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E(f_{n})
. Since
p_{1}\in{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})
, we have
\mu(E(f_{n}))=0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore,
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E(f_{n})
is a set of measure zero which implies
\mu(E(f))=0
because
\mathcal{X}
is complete.

Next, assume that
X=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}G_{n}
for open sets
G_{n}\subseteq X
satisfying
\mu(G_{n})<\infty
. As before, fix
f\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathcal{X})
and, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
f_{n}\coloneqq f\cdot{\mathds{1}_{{F_{n}}}}
, where

F_{n}\coloneqq G_{1}\cup\cdots\cup G_{n}.

Since
\mu(F_{n})<\infty
, we have
f_{n}\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathcal{X})
so that
\mu(E(f_{n}))=0
because
p_{1}\in{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})
. Moreover, since
F_{n}
is open and
f_{n}
agrees with
f
on
F_{n}
, we have
E(f)\cap F_{n}=E(f_{n})\cap F_{n}
. Hence,

E(f)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big(E(f)\cap F_{n}\big)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big(E(f_{n})\cap F_{n}\big)

and the set on the right-hand side has measure zero, as desired.
∎

3.
Maximal operators

For a collection
\mathcal{S}
of sets
S\subseteq X
such that
\mu(S)\in(0,\infty)
, one can define the corresponding
maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}
in a standard way. Namely, for every
p\in[1,\infty]
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
, we let

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}f(x)\coloneqq\sup_{x\in S\in\mathcal{S}}{\rm Avg}_{|f|}(S).

Here we do not assume that
\mathcal{S}
forms a differentiation basis; in particular, the supremum above may be taken over the empty set, in which case we put
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}f(x)\coloneqq 0
. Note that, in general,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}f
may not be measurable. Indeed, let us modify Example
2.6
so that
\mu_{K}((i2^{-j},2^{-j})\coloneqq 2^{-2j}
. Then
f\coloneqq{\mathds{1}_{{K}}}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
for every
p\in[1,\infty]
, while
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}f={\mathds{1}_{{K\cup F}}}
is not measurable. In order to avoid such issues, in this section we shall consider only maximal operators corresponding to countable collections
\mathcal{S}
.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, we say that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}
satisfies the weak-type
(p,p)
inequality
if

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}\cdot\mu(\{x\in X:\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}f(x)>\lambda\})\leq C_{p}^{p}\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}

holds for every
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
and
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, where
C_{p}\in[0,\infty)
is independent of
f
and
\lambda
. We write
\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p,\infty}}
for the smallest constant
C_{p}\in[0,\infty]
such that (
3.1
) holds.

The following result is well-known, but we show its proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a metric measure space and fix
p\in[1,\infty)
. Assume that
L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
contains a dense subset consisting of continuous functions. If
\mathcal{B}
is a countable uncentered differentiation basis in
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}
satisfies the weak-type
(p,p)
inequality, then
p\in{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})
.

Proof.

Fix
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
. It suffices to show that

E_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\big\{x\in X:|\overline{f}(x)-f(x)|>2\varepsilon\text{ or }|\underline{f}(x)-f(x)|>2\varepsilon\big\}

has measure zero. Note that
\overline{f}
and
\underline{f}
are measurable by the assumptions imposed on
\mathcal{B}
.

For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
f_{j}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
be a continuous function such that
\|f-f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}\leq 1/j
. Assume that
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
contracts to some
x\in X
. We write

{\rm Avg}_{f}(S_{n})={\rm Avg}_{f_{j}}(S_{n})+{\rm Avg}_{f-f_{j}}(S_{n})

and observe that

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm Avg}_{f_{j}}(S_{n})=f_{j}(x)

because
f_{j}
is continuous. On the other hand,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|{\rm Avg}_{f-f_{j}}(S_{n})|\leq\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(f-f_{j})(x).

Thus, we conclude that
E_{\varepsilon}
is contained in the set

E_{\varepsilon}^{*}\coloneqq\big\{x\in X:|(f-f_{j})(x)|>\varepsilon\text{ or }\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(f-f_{j})(x)>\varepsilon\big\}.

By Chebyshev’s inequality and the weak-type
(p,p)
inequality (
3.1
), we have

\mu(E_{\varepsilon})\leq\mu(E_{\varepsilon}^{*})\leq\varepsilon^{-p}(1+C_{p}^{p})\|f-f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{p}\leq(\varepsilon j)^{-p}(1+C_{p}^{p}).

Therefore, by letting
j\to\infty
, we obtain
\mu(E_{\varepsilon})=0
and the proof is complete.
∎

By Lemma
3.2
, a possible way to show that
p\in{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B})
is to study the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}
. The following result allows us to verify (
3.1
) for certain bases introduced in Section
4
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a metric measure space and fix
p\in[1,\infty)
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{S}_{n}
be a countable collection such that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}
satisfies (
3.1
) with a constant
C_{p}\in(0,\infty)
independent of
n
. Assume that if
S_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{1}}
and
S_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{2}}
for distinct
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, then

S_{1}\subseteq S_{2}\quad\text{or}\quad S_{2}\subseteq S_{1}\quad\text{or}\quad S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\emptyset.

Let
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{S}_{n}
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}
satisfies (
3.1
) with the same constant
C_{p}
.

Proof.

Fix
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{X})
and
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{x\in X:\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}}f>\lambda\},

\displaystyle E_{\lambda,n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{x\in X:\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}f>\lambda\}.

Clearly,
E_{\lambda}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{\lambda,n}
so that
E_{\lambda}
is measurable. Our goal is to prove the inequality

\lambda^{p}\cdot\mu(E_{\lambda})\leq C_{p}^{p}\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}.

Fix
\delta>0
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, since
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is countable and

\lambda^{p}\cdot\mu(E_{\lambda,n})\leq C_{p}^{p}\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}<\infty,

one can find a positive integer
I_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
and a finite family
\{S_{n,1},\dots,S_{n,I_{n}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{n}
such that

\mu(E_{\lambda,n})\leq\mu\big(S_{n,1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{n,I_{n}}\big)+\delta/2^{n}

and
{\rm Avg}_{|f|}(S_{n,i})>\lambda
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,I_{n}\}
. Thus, it suffices to prove the inequality

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}\cdot\mu\Big(\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}\bigcup_{i=1}^{I_{n}}S_{n,i}\Big)\leq C_{p}^{p}\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, we can choose an integer
I_{n}^{*}\in\{0,1,\dots,I_{n}\}
and a subfamily

\{S_{n,1}^{*},\dots,S^{*}_{n,I^{*}_{n}}\}\subseteq\{S_{n,1},\dots,S_{n,I_{n}}\}
in such a way that the family

\mathcal{S}^{*}\coloneqq\big\{S^{*}_{n,i}:n\in\{1,\dots,N\},\,i\in\{1,\dots,I_{n}^{*}\}\big\}

consists of all maximal elements (with possible repetitions being removed) of the family

\big\{S_{n,i}:n\in\{1,\dots,N\},\,i\in\{1,\dots,I_{n}\}\big\}

ordered by the inclusion relation. For every
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, let
E_{\lambda,n}^{*}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{I_{n}^{*}}S^{*}_{n,i}
. Then the sets
E_{\lambda,n}^{*}
are disjoint and so

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}\bigcup_{i=1}^{I_{n}}S_{n,i}\Big)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu(E_{\lambda,n}^{*}).

Indeed, for distinct
n_{1},n_{2}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, if

i_{1}\in\{1,\dots,I_{n_{1}}^{*}\}
and
i_{2}\in\{1,\dots,I_{n_{2}}^{*}\},

then by maximality we cannot have neither
S^{*}_{n_{1},i_{1}}\subseteq S^{*}_{n_{2},i_{2}}
nor
S^{*}_{n_{2},i_{2}}\subseteq S^{*}_{n_{1},i_{1}}
. Hence, we must have

S^{*}_{n_{1},i_{1}}\cap S^{*}_{n_{2},i_{2}}=\emptyset
by our initial assumption on the collections
\mathcal{S}_{n_{1}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{n_{2}}
.

For every
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, set
f_{n}\coloneqq f\cdot{\mathds{1}_{{E_{\lambda,n}^{*}}}}
. Since
E_{\lambda,n}^{*}
are disjoint, we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}\leq\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}.

Moreover, for every
i\in\{1,\dots,I_{n}^{*}\}
, we have

{\rm Avg}_{|f_{n}|}(S^{*}_{n,i})={\rm Avg}_{|f|}(S^{*}_{n,i}).

Hence,

E_{\lambda,n}^{*}\subseteq\{x\in X:\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}f_{n}(x)>\lambda\}.

By our initial assumption on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}
, this gives

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}\cdot\mu(E_{\lambda,n}^{*})\leq C_{p}^{p}\|f_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}.

Combining (
3.5
)–(
3.7
), we obtain (
3.4
) and the proof is complete.
∎

4.
Infinite-dimensional torus

In order to illustrate Theorem
1.3
with suitable examples, we introduce a very specific class of differentiation bases in the infinite-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
.

By
\mathbb{T}
we mean the quotient space
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
which is naturally interpreted as the interval
[0,1]
with its endpoints identified.
We define the infinite-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}\coloneqq\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}\times\cdots
as the product of countably many copies of
\mathbb{T}
indexed by
\mathbb{N}
. Thus,
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
is a group with
{\bf 0}\coloneqq(0,0,\dots)
being its identity. For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}=\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d,\omega}
so that
\mathbb{T}^{d,\omega}
is a copy of
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
referring to the coordinates with indices larger than
d
.

Note that
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
with the usual product topology is compact and metrizable. Indeed, the former follows from Tychonoff’s theorem, while the latter holds because the function

\rho_{\mathbb{T}^{\omega}}((x_{1},x_{2},\dots),(y_{1},y_{2},\dots))\coloneqq\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{\min\{|x_{d}-y_{d}|,1-|x_{d}-y_{d}|\}}{2^{d}}

defined on
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}\times\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
determines a metric which is compatible with the product topology.

The
\sigma
-algebra of Borel subsets of
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
is generated by rectangles of the form
\prod_{d=1}^{\infty}I_{d}
, where each
I_{d}
is an interval in
\mathbb{T}
and, for some
d_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
I_{d}=\mathbb{T}
if
d>d_{0}
. The normalized Haar measure
{\rm d}x
on
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
is the product of countably many copies of the normalized Lebesgue measures on
\mathbb{T}
. For a Borel set
E\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
, we denote its measure by

|E|\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{\omega}}{\mathds{1}_{{E}}}(x)\,{\rm d}x.

As mentioned before, by using a standard procedure we can make the space complete.

The infinite-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
still retains some of the differentiation properties of the finite-dimensional tori. For example, every density basis in
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
differentiates
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\omega})
, (cf.
[
dG75
, Chapter III, Theorem 1.4]
), because Lusin’s Theorem still holds true in
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
. Nevertheless, in the context of differentiation bases consisting of rectangles with sides parallel to the coordinate axes, the infinite-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
exhibits a broader variety of behaviors that are absent in the finite-dimensional case.

Historically, the two most important differentiation bases in
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
are the Rubio de Francia bases, classical
\mathcal{R}
and dyadic
\mathcal{R}_{0}
. While
\mathcal{R}
is the object originally devised by José Luis Rubio de Francia,
\mathcal{R}_{0}
appears in his work somewhat indirectly, as a special case of dyadic bases in the abstract setting of locally compact groups (see
[
RdF78
]
). Below we briefly describe how
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}_{0}
look like (for formal definitions, see
[
FR20
]
or the doctoral dissertation
[

]
).

For every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
R_{m}\coloneqq\{0,\frac{1}{m},\dots,\frac{m-1}{m}\}
. We also need two auxiliary sequences:

m_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
being the unique positive integer such that

j^{-2}/4<2^{-m_{j}}(1+d_{j}-\varepsilon_{j}d_{j})^{-1}\leq j^{-2}/2.

We notice that if
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, then

j^{-2}(1+j-\varepsilon j)/2<j^{-2}(1+j)/2\leq j^{-1}\leq 1,

thanks to which such a choice of
m_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
is possible.
Then

\lim_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}d_{j}^{1/p_{0}}<\infty

and so
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}_{\geq}}
is of weak-type
(p_{0},p_{0})
by conditions
(A1)
–
(A3)
together with Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
4.2
. Therefore,
p_{0}\in{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B}_{\geq})
by Lemma
3.2
(for the density assumption, see
[
Fo99
, Proposition 7.9]
), which implies
[p_{0},\infty]\subseteq{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B}_{\geq})
by Proposition
2.1
.

Next, choose
p\in[1,p_{0})

and define

(5.1)

\displaystyle f\coloneqq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}f_{j},

where

f_{j}\coloneqq\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}{\mathds{1}_{{F_{j}}}}
for
F_{j}
from
(A4)
.
Then
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\omega})
, since

\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\varepsilon_{j}^{-p}2^{-m_{j}}(1+d_{j}-\varepsilon_{j}d_{j})^{-1}\leq 2^{p-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}j^{p/p_{0}}j^{-2}<\infty.

In order to justify the first inequality above, set
G\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{\omega}:|f_{1}(x)|>|f_{2}(x)|\}
. Then

\|\max\{f_{1},f_{2}\}\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{G}|f_{1}(x)|^{p}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{\omega}\setminus G}|f_{2}(x)|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\leq\|f_{1}\|_{p}^{p}+\|f_{2}\|_{p}^{p}.

By iterating this and passing to the limit on both sides, we obtain
\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}
. Next, note that by the second Borel–Cantelli lemma, for almost all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{\omega}
, the set

\{j\in\mathbb{N}:x\in E_{j,n}\text{ for some }n\in\Lambda_{j}\}

is infinite. Indeed, the sets
F^{*}_{j}
are mutually independent events and, due to the choice
d_{j}=j
and
\varepsilon_{j}=j^{-1/p_{0}}/2
,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|F_{j}^{*}|\geq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}j^{-2}(1+d_{j}-\varepsilon_{j}d_{j})/4=\infty.

Now,
\overline{f}(x)\geq 1
holds for each such
x
. Indeed, if
x\in E_{j,n}
and
n\in\Lambda_{j}
, then
{\rm Avg}_{f}(Q)\geq{\rm Avg}_{f_{j}}(Q)\geq 1
for some
x\in Q\in\mathcal{S}_{j,n}
. Since
f
vanishes outside
\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}F_{j}
and

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|F_{j}|\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}j^{-2}/2<1,

we have
\overline{f}(x)>f(x)
on a set with positive measure. Consequently,
p\notin{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B}_{\geq})
. Since
p\in[1,p_{0})
was arbitrary, we obtain
{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B}_{\geq})\subseteq[p_{0},\infty]
.

Similarly, let us construct
\mathcal{B}_{>}
. For
p_{0}\in[1,\infty)
, apply Proposition
4.4
with

U=\mathbb{T}^{\omega},\quad d_{j}=j,\quad\varepsilon_{j}=(j\log^{-2}(j+1))^{-1/p_{0}}/2

and
m_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
being the unique positive integer such that

j^{-2}/4<2^{-m_{j}}(1+d_{j}-\varepsilon_{j}d_{j})^{-1}\leq j^{-2}/2.

As before, such a choice is possible if
\varepsilon_{j}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
. To see this, note that

\sqrt{x}\geq\log(x+1)
for
x\in[0,\infty)
, since both functions vanish at
x=0
and
(2\sqrt{x})^{-1}\geq(x+1)^{-1}
for
x\in(0,\infty)
.
Then

\lim_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}d_{j}^{1/p}<\infty

for every
p\in(p_{0},\infty)
which implies

(p_{0},\infty]\subseteq{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B}_{>})
.

Next, consider

f
defined as in (
5.1
). Then
f\in L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{T}^{\omega})
and, as before, we have
\overline{f}(x)>f(x)
on a set with positive measure. Thus,
p_{0}\notin{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B}_{>})
so that
{\rm diff}(\mathcal{B}_{>})\subseteq(p_{0},\infty]
.
∎

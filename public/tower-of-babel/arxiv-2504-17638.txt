Testing Quintessence Axion Dark Energy with Recent Cosmological Results
Weikang Lin, Luca Visinelli, Tsutomu T. Yanagida

Abstract
We investigate a quintessence axion model for dynamical dark energy, motivated in part by recent results from the Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) measurements of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) and the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) observations from the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT). By carefully treating the initial conditions and parameter sampling, we identify a preferred parameter space featuring a sub-Planckian axion decay constant and a relatively large axion mass, which naturally avoids the quality problem and remains consistent with the perturbative string conjecture. Our parameter scan also uncovers a trans-Planckian regime of theoretical interest, which is only mildly disfavored by observations. The results remain robust when DESI BAO data are combined with CMB and supernova observations. Finally, we discuss the possible connection between this model and the recently reported non-zero rotation of the CMB linear polarization angle, emphasizing the broader cosmological implications and the promising prospects for testing this scenario. We show that an 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) \displaystyle\mathcal{O}(1) electromagnetic anomaly coefficient is preferred by the strongest constraint, which is in full agreement with the minimal quintessence axion model.

In this work, we explore a quintessence axion as a candidate for dark energy. We assume that the quintessence axion fully accounts for the dark energy observed, setting the cosmological constant to zero. Building upon previous studies, we show that a suitable treatment of the initial conditions reveals a preference for an axion decay constant well below the Planck scale. Although only mildly favored by current data than the regime with a trans-Planckian decay constant, it corresponds to a relatively large axion mass compared to the Hubble constant and yields a consistent cosmological evolution. At the same time, our parameter scan also uncovers a region with a trans-Planckian decay constant, which may offer new insights from a theoretical standpoint. \displaystyle\mathcal{O}(1)

II
Methods

II.1
Datasets

In this analysis, we make use of three categories of cosmological data, similar to those adopted in Ref.
Abdul Karim et al. 2025b
: baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO), compressed constraints from cosmic microwave background (CMB), and Type Ia supernovae (SNIa). The BAO constraints are obtained from the second data release (DR2) of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI)
Abdul Karim et al. 2025b
, providing precise BAO measurements across a wide redshift range. For the CMB, instead of the full temperature and polarization power spectra, we adopt a set of compressed information, which makes use of the CMB information by incorporating the temperature, polarization, and temperature–polarization cross-correlation power spectra from
Planck

Aghanim et al. 2020b
, and the combined
Planck
and the Atacama Cosmology Telescope Data Release 6 (ACT - DR6) CMB lensing data
Madhavacheril et al. 2024
, namely: the angular scale of the sound horizon at recombination
\displaystyle\theta_{*}
, the reduced baryon density
\displaystyle\Omega_{b}h^{2}
, and the reduced total matter density
\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm m}h^{2}

Lemos and Lewis 2023
. These three parameters capture the most relevant information for our purposes (e.g., constraining a late-time DE model) while significantly reducing computational complexity. We incorporate two independent compilations of SNIa data: the PantheonPlus sample
Scolnic et al. 2022
and the Dark Energy Survey (DES) Y5 dataset
Abbott et al. 2024
, which are used separately. To distinguish between the different SNIa data, we label the full combinations as DESI + CMB + PantheonPlus and DESI + CMB + DESY5.

For the likelihoods corresponding to the three observations above, we largely follow the treatments in Ref.
Lin et al. 2021
, with one modification: instead of treating the sound horizon uncalibrated, we compute the normalized sound horizon scales at recombination and at the drag epoch assuming the standard cosmological model applies prior to recombination. Specifically, we calculate the normalized sound horizon as

r_{\rm s}(z_{s})H_{0}=\int^{z_{s}}_{\infty}\frac{c_{\rm{s}}(z)}{\sqrt{\Omega_{\rm m}(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{\rm r}(1+z)^{4}}}dz\,,

(1)

where
\displaystyle c_{\rm{s}}
is the sound speed given by

c_{\rm{s}}(z;\,\Omega_{\rm{b}}h^{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3\big(1+\frac{1}{1+z}\frac{3\,\Omega_{\rm{b}}h^{2}}{4\,\Omega_{\gamma}h^{2}}\big)}}\,.

(2)

Here, we neglect the contribution from dark energy at early times, for redshifts
\displaystyle z\gg 1
. The redshifts
\displaystyle z_{s}
correspond to
\displaystyle z_{*}
(recombination) and
\displaystyle z_{\rm d}
(drag epoch), for which we adopt the fitting formulas from Ref.
Hu and Sugiyama 1996
. Note that the likelihoods in Ref.
Lin et al. 2021
are based on the
\displaystyle\Lambda
CDM model post-recombination, and are extended to accommodate non-standard cosmologies [Wang and Lin, in preparation].

II.2
Priors and parameter sampling strategy

We first discuss the cosmological evolution of the field
\displaystyle A
. Acting as a quintessence dark energy candidate, the field slowly rolls from near the top of its potential, starting from an initial configuration
\displaystyle A_{i}
. In the case of a quintessence axion, the potential energy is typically modeled by a periodic cosine function of the form
\displaystyle V(A)=(V_{0}/2)\left[1-\cos\left(A/F_{A}\right)\right]
, where
\displaystyle F_{A}
is the effective axion decay constant and
\displaystyle V_{0}
sets the energy scale associated with non-perturbative symmetry-breaking effects. See Eq. (
13
) for an explicit model build. The model features two free parameters: the quintessence axion mass, given by
\displaystyle m_{A}^{2}=V_{0}/(2F_{A}^{2})
in Eq. (
13
), and the initial displacement from the potential maximum,

\delta_{i}\equiv\pi-A_{i}/F_{A}\,.

(3)

For future use, we define the dimensionless decay constant ratio as

\kappa_{f}\equiv F_{A}/M_{P}\,.

(4)

This parameter can be determined by specifying
\displaystyle m_{A}
and
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
. For details on the numerical solution of the axion dynamics, see Appendix
B
.

In addition to the other standard cosmological parameters, the quintessence axion model depends on the two parameters
\displaystyle m_{A}/H_{0}
and the initial displacement
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
in Eq. (
3
). To constitute a viable DE model, these two parameters must satisfy a tight correlation. Specifically, a larger value of
\displaystyle m_{A}/H_{0}
, corresponding to a smaller parameter
\displaystyle\kappa_{f}
in Eq. (
4
), necessitates a smaller
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
. Based on a WKB approximation, the required
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
decreases rapidly as
\displaystyle\kappa_{f}
becomes smaller, following the relation
\displaystyle\delta_{i}\sim\exp(-1/\kappa_{f})/\kappa_{f}

Ibe et al. 2019
;
Lin et al. 2023
. Motivated by this, we further perform a numerical analysis and find that the relation is better approximated by

\delta_{i}\sim\frac{1}{\kappa_{f}^{1.5}}\,\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\kappa_{f}}\right)\,.

(5)

For instance, when
\displaystyle\kappa_{f}=0.1
, this expression yields
\displaystyle\delta_{i}\approx 10^{-3}
.

Such a stringent requirement on
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
introduces an artificial lower bound on
\displaystyle\kappa_{f}
or, equivalently, an upper bound on
\displaystyle m_{A}/H_{0}
, under certain parameter sampling strategies during parameter inference. This effect arises particularly: (1) when the small-
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
region occupies only a small fraction of the prior volume, such as when using a uniform prior over a linear-
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
scale, which compresses the viable parameter space into a narrow region at low
\displaystyle\kappa_{f}
; or (2) when a hard lower bound on
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
is explicitly imposed. In both cases, the resultant upper bound on
\displaystyle m_{A}/H_{0}
is not observationally driven but instead emerges as a sampling artifact.

To avoid this sampling bias, motivated by Eq. (
5
) and the approximation
\displaystyle\kappa_{f}\sim H_{0}/m_{A}
in the small-
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
limit of Eq. (
25
), we redefine the parameterization using a transformation from
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
to the quantity
\displaystyle\epsilon
as:

\delta_{i}=\epsilon\left(\frac{m_{A}}{H_{0}}\right)^{1.5}\exp\left(-\frac{m_{A}}{H_{0}}\right)\,.

(6)

We then perform parameter sampling uniformly in
\displaystyle\epsilon
, which ensures proper coverage of the small-
\displaystyle\delta_{i}
region. Notably, the
\displaystyle\epsilon=0
case recovers the cosmological constant scenario, as the field
\displaystyle A
with this condition is non-dynamical and sits at the top of the cosine potential.

This transformation effectively avoids sampling outside the DE regime (e.g., scenarios where the quintessence axion field
\displaystyle A
oscillates at late times). To further enforce the interpretation of the model as dark energy, we impose an additional requirement: the derivative of the field
\displaystyle A^{\prime}
with respect to the scale factor
\displaystyle a\equiv 1/(1+z)
must be greater at present (
\displaystyle a=1
) than any prior times. This ensures that the field
\displaystyle A
has not yet reached the potential minimum. In addition, we restrict our analysis to the region where
\displaystyle\kappa_{f}<10
, or equivalently
\displaystyle F_{A}<10\,M_{P}
, acknowledging that part of this region may violate the perturbative string conjecture. Larger values of
\displaystyle\kappa_{f}
are not necessary to include, as for
\displaystyle\kappa_{f}\gg 1
, the field begins near the minimum of its potential. In this limit, the potential effectively becomes quadratic,
\displaystyle V\to\frac{1}{2}m_{A}^{2}A^{2}
, and the dynamics become independent of
\displaystyle F_{A}
. Consequently, the observational constraints asymptotically converge at large
\displaystyle F_{A}
. The parameter ranges used in our analysis are summarized in Table

IV
Discussion

IV.1
Range of the axion decay constant

Our analysis reveals a mild preference for larger axion masses, corresponding to smaller values of the decay constant
\displaystyle F_{A}
. Specifically, for the combined datasets of DESI + CMB + PantheonPlus, we observe that the best-fit
\displaystyle\chi^{2}
decreases gradually by about 1.4 as
\displaystyle m_{A}/H_{0}
increases from 0.7 to 20. This trend suggests that axion models with higher mass and correspondingly lower
\displaystyle F_{A}
provide a slightly better fit to the data. In the regime of large decay constants, particularly for
\displaystyle F_{A}\gtrsim M_{P}
and
\displaystyle m_{A}/H_{0}\lesssim 1
, the
\displaystyle\chi^{2}
becomes nearly independent of
\displaystyle F_{A}
. This behavior indicates that current data are largely insensitive to the precise value of
\displaystyle F_{A}
, and data do not place strong constraints on its value.

On one hand, a sub-Planckian
\displaystyle F_{A}
is theoretically advantageous. The results presented in this work support models with
\displaystyle F_{A}\sim 10^{17}
GeV, such as the one proposed in Ref.
Girmohanta et al. 2023
, which simultaneously address the quality problem, mitigate fine-tuning, and remain consistent with observational data; further details can be found in Appendix
A
.

On the other hand, while small values of
\displaystyle F_{A}
are slightly favored by the data, scenarios with super-Planckian decay constants remain consistent with the data, although with marginally higher
\displaystyle\chi^{2}
. This is remarkable given the conventional expectation that the spontaneous breaking of global symmetries, such as the one associated with the QCD axion, should occur below the Planck scale, an assumption influenced by string-theoretic conjectures
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2007
. The fact that our results do not exclude the possibility of
\displaystyle F_{A}>M_{P}
challenges this conventional viewpoint and opens the door to alternative theoretical scenarios. It may motivate a re-examination of symmetry-breaking mechanisms in ultraviolet completions of axion models and encourage further investigation into how effective global symmetries could persist at super-Planckian scales.

\displaystyle c_{\gamma}=\mathcal{O}(1)
. Previous studies, including our own work
Choi et al. 2021
, reported relatively large values of the electromagnetic anomaly coefficient,
\displaystyle c_{\gamma}\sim\mathcal{O}(20)
, across the full range of
\displaystyle F_{A}
. In contrast, the results presented in Fig.
4
favor lower values of
\displaystyle c_{\gamma}
, especially for the data combination DESI + CMB + DESY5 + CB, corresponding to a lower preferred range for
\displaystyle F_{A}
. This difference stems
not
from the improvement of data quality, but rather from the improved treatment of the small-
\displaystyle F_{A}
regime in the present analysis. Unlike previous approaches that relied on indirect equation-of-state parameters, which is misleading due to large field excursions with small
\displaystyle F_{A}
still yield a distance-redshift relation consistent with observations, our analysis performs a direct comparison between the comoving distance-redshift relation and observational data.
Since
\displaystyle c_{\gamma}
originates from a specific combination of gauge group anomalies, smaller values are theoretically more attractive, particularly in the context of ultraviolet-complete models. This provides sharper guidance for model building.

V
Conclusions

In this work, we have explored a quintessence axion model for dynamical dark energy assuming a vanishing cosmological constant, inspired in part by recent BAO measurements from DESI DR2, and CMB observations from ACT. By carefully modeling and sampling the initial conditions in the analysis, we have identified a preferred region of parameter space featuring a sub-Planckian axion decay constant. This regime naturally avoids the quality problem and remains consistent with the perturbative string conjecture, while predicting a relatively large axion mass compared to the Hubble scale. Additionally, our parameter scan reveals a trans-Planckian regime that, while less phenomenologically favored, presents a theoretically interesting avenue for further investigation. The overall results are robust under the inclusion of DESI BAO data in combination with CMB and SNIa datasets.

Lastly, we have discussed a possible link between the model and the recently reported non-zero cosmic birefringence, observed as a rotation of the CMB linear polarization angle. A minimal setup with an electromagnetic anomaly coefficient
\displaystyle c_{\gamma}=\mathcal{O}(1)
and a sub-Planckian axion decay constant can simultaneously account for the observed cosmic birefringence and the late-time cosmic acceleration, with agreement at the
\displaystyle 1\sigma
level for the DESI + CMB + DESY5 dataset combination. However, the result becomes less conclusive when the PantheonPlus SNIa dataset is included. While a larger decay constant would require a proportionally larger anomaly coefficient, cosmic birefringence may provide an independent observational probe, potentially helping to break the degeneracy in the distance–redshift relation present in the results. Although this connection remains speculative, it offers a compelling framework for interpreting the broader cosmological role of axion quintessence and presents a promising avenue for testing such models.

B.1
Differential questions for background evolution

We consider a spatially flat universe. For the observables considered in this work, it is sufficient to model the background evolution of the quintessence axion field
\displaystyle A
and the scale factor of the Universe. Following the notation in Ref.
Choi et al. 2021
, the self-interacting potential leads to the time evolution of the quintessence axion field
\displaystyle A

\ddot{A}+3H\dot{A}+\frac{\partial V(A)}{\partial A}=0\,,

(17)

where
\displaystyle V(A)
is given in Eq. (
13

\displaystyle H
is the Hubble expansion rate, and the dot denotes differentiation with respect to cosmic time. Here, the back-reaction from the axion-photon interaction term in Eq. (
16

\kappa_{f}\equiv\frac{F_{A}}{M_{P}},\quad E\equiv\frac{H}{H_{0}},\quad\widetilde{\Omega}_{A}\equiv\frac{V_{0}}{\rho_{\rm c}^{0}},

where
\displaystyle\rho_{\rm c}^{0}=3M_{P}^{2}H_{0}^{2}
is the critical density today. The last parameter can also be expressed as

\widetilde{\Omega}_{A}=\frac{2}{3}\kappa_{f}^{2}\frac{m_{A}^{2}}{H_{0}^{2}}.

(18)

Note that
\displaystyle\widetilde{\Omega}_{A}
is
not
the current energy density fraction of the quintessence axion. In general,
\displaystyle\widetilde{\Omega}_{A}>1-\Omega_{\rm m}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Omega}_{A}\to 1-\Omega_{\rm m}

\displaystyle\delta_{i}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle V_{0}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\displaystyle=V(A)+\frac{1}{2}\dot{A}^{2}

On uniqueness of inverse conductive scattering problem with unknown embedded obstacles
Chengyu Wu, Jiaqing Yang

Abstract
This paper is concerning the inverse conductive scattering of acoustic waves by a bounded inhomogeneous object with possibly embedded obstacles inside. A new uniqueness theorem is proved that the conductive object is uniquely determined by the fixed frequency far-field measurements, ignoring its contents. Meanwhile, the boundary informations of several related physical coefficients are also uniquely determined. The proof is mainly based on a detailed singularity analysis of solutions near the interface associated with a family of point sources or hypersingular point sources, which is deduced by the potential theory. Moreover, the other key ingredient in the proof is the well-posedness of the interior transmission problem with the conductivity boundary condition in the L 2 L^{2} sense, where several sufficient conditions depending on the domain and physical coefficients are provided.



keywords

inverse scattering, conductive boundary, singularity analysis, interior transmission problem, uniqueness.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the inverse conductive scattering problem of determining penetrable object with possibly embedded obstacles. Such kind of problems can find applications in various fields, such as remote sensing, radar and sonar, medical imaging and nondestructive testing.

Mathematically, denote by
D
and
D_{b}
the bounded penetrable object in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and the embedded obstacles, respectively, where we assume that
D,D_{b}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
are both bounded domains such that
\partial D\in C^{2,\alpha_{0}}
and
D_{b}\subset\subset D
,
D_{b}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}D_{b}^{(j)}
with
D_{b}^{(j_{1})}\cap D_{b}^{(j_{2})}=\emptyset
if
j_{1}\neq j_{2}
. Denote by
n
the refractive index in the inhomogeneous medium
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{D_{b}}
, which is assumed that
n=1
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{D}
,
n\in L^{\infty}(D\setminus\overline{D_{b}})
,
{\rm Re}(n)>0
and
{\rm Im}(n)\geq 0
in
D\setminus\overline{D_{b}}
. Consider an incident wave

satisfying the Helmholtz equation
\Delta u^{i}+k^{2}u^{i}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with the wave number
k>0
given by
k=\omega/c
, where
\omega>0
is the frequency and
c>0
is the sound speed. Then the scattering problem corresponding to the scatterer
(D,\lambda,\gamma,n,D_{b})
is modeled by the following transmission problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u+k^{2}u=0&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{D},\\
{\rm div\,}(\lambda\nabla v)+k^{2}nv=0&{\rm in}~D\setminus\overline{D_{b}},\\
u-v=0&{\rm on}~\partial D,\\
\partial_{\nu}u=\lambda\partial_{\nu}v+\gamma v&{\rm on}~\partial D,\\
\mathscr{B}(v)=0&{\rm on}~\partial D_{b},\\
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}r\left(\partial_{r}u^{s}-iku^{s}\right)=0,&r=|x|,\end{array}\right.

where
\lambda\in C^{1}(\overline{D}\setminus D_{b})
with
\lambda>0
in
\overline{D}\setminus D_{b}
is the transmission coefficient related to the medium
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{D_{b}}
,
\gamma\in C(\partial D)
is the conductive coefficient with
{\rm Im}(\gamma)\leq 0
,
u^{s}
is the scattered wave,

denotes the total field in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{D}
, and
\nu
is the unit exterior normal to
\partial D
. Furthermore,
\mathscr{B}
stands for the boundary condition imposed on
\partial D_{b}
satisfying

, provided that

is a sound-soft obstacle, and
\mathscr{B}(v)=\partial_{\nu}v
with
\nu
the unit exterior normal to
\partial D_{b}
, provided that

is a sound-hard obstacle. Moreover, if

is a mixed-type obstacle then

(with the impedance coefficient

such that
{\rm Im}(\beta)\geq 0
) and
\mathscr{B}(v)=v

The Sommerfeld radiation condition leads to the following asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle u^{s}(x)=\frac{e^{ikr}}{4\pi r}\left\{u_{\infty}(\hat{x})+O\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)\right\},~~~~r=|x|\rightarrow\infty,

, also based on Isakov’s idea, Hettlich obtained the uniqueness of

As discussed above, the singular solution for the scattering problem plays a key role in the inverse problem. Thus in the present paper we study in detail the solutions to problem (
1
) with point source or hypersingular point source incident waves. It is a natural thought that the solutions possess a same singularity as the singular incidence when the singular positions approach the interface. Here we give the first rigorous proof for such thought. We show that the solutions have the same singularity as the singular incidence multiplying by a constant relative to the transmission coefficient
\lambda
and conductive coefficient
\gamma
. We establish these results by spilting the singular solution into several parts. Different methods have been applied to analyze different parts, such as the variational method and the integral equation method, and for the key part it requires an elaborate mathmatical analysis. The detailed singularity for singular solutions, besides the uniqueness results of the inverse problem, is another ingredient of this paper.

For the inverse problem, it is well known that
D_{b}\neq\emptyset
or
\lambda=1
can bring great difficulties in the uniqueness proof of the inverse problem. But recently, a much simpler method for the inverse transmission problem is proposed in
[
27
]
, which can recover the boundary
\partial D
in situations that
D_{b}\neq\emptyset
or
\lambda=1
. This method is applied in the present paper and extended to many other cases, like in
[
23
]
for the inverse fluid-solid interaction problem and in
[
25
]
for the inverse boundary value problem. It is expected to be used in more cases. Our method is mainly based on constructing a well-posed modified interior transmission problem in a sufficiently small domain inside
D
near the boundary
\partial D
. We use the smallness of the domain to eliminate the difficulties generated by
D_{b}\neq\emptyset
or
\lambda=1
near the boundary
\partial D
. Further, our uniqueness results contain the identification of the derivatives of the coefficients on the boundary. The proof is greatly relied on the aforementioned complete description of the singularity of the solutions to the scattering problem with singular incidence. We note that these estimates are possible to be generalized to determine the higher order derivatives of the coefficients. And it is also possible to extend our methods to other types inverse scattering problems, such as the electromagnetic and elastic scattering.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we propose a novel perspective to derive the singularity of solutions with point source or hypersingular point source incident waves in the
H^{1}
sense, which plays a key role in the uniqueness proof of the inverse problem. In Section
3.1
, we introduce two types interior transmission problem corresponding to the case
\lambda\neq 1
and
\lambda=1
near the boundary, respectively. The new type interior transmission problem for the case
\lambda=1
is then investigated carefully and several sufficient conditions for it to be well-posed are given. In particular, we find that this interior transmission problem is always uniquely solvable in domain sufficiently small, which is essential for later proof. Finally, Section
3.2
is all about the proof of the uniqueness result. A novel and simple method is employed here for the unique determination of the penetrable object from the measurement of the far-field pattern at a fixed frequency, disregarding its contents. Furthermore, we also identify the coefficients in the scattering problem at the boundary of the penetrable object utilizing the singularity of the solutions.

2
Singularity analysis

This section is devoted to the study of the singularity of the solutions to problem (
1

\lambda

[
5
, Corollary 3.8]
.

Set

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{j}(x)=2\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial\Phi_{0}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}\Phi_{0}(y,x_{j})ds(y),~~~x\in D.

It is deduced that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta(\widetilde{f}_{j}+h_{j})=0&{\rm in}~D,\\
\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{j}(x)+h_{j}(x)=2\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial\Phi_{0}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}\Phi_{0}(y,x_{j})ds(y)&{\rm on}~\partial D,\end{array}\right.

where
h_{j}(x)=\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})
. Again by
[
5
, Corollary 3.7]
, we know that the trace of
\widetilde{f}_{j}+h_{j}
on
\partial D
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1/2}(\partial D)
. Thus the standard elliptic regularity gives
\|\widetilde{f}_{j}+h_{j}\|_{H^{1}(D)}\leq C
uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{v}_{j}-\frac{2}{\lambda(x_{0})+1}\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})\right\|_{H^{1}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}\leq C

(2.28)

for problem (
2
) with
k^{2}/\lambda_{0}=k_{1}^{2}>0
.

Finally we focus on the difference between
v_{j}
and
\hat{v}_{j}
. Define
(\widetilde{u}_{j}^{s},\widetilde{v}_{j}):=(u_{j}-\Phi(\cdot,y_{j}),v_{j})-(\hat{u}_{j}^{s},\hat{v}_{j})
, it then follows that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}+k^{2}\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}=0&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{D},\\
{\rm div\,}(\lambda\nabla\widetilde{v}_{j})+k^{2}n\widetilde{v}_{j}={\rm div\,}((\lambda(x_{0})-\lambda){\rm NewA}\hat{v}_{j})+k^{2}(1-n)\hat{v}_{j}&{\rm in}~D\setminus\overline{D_{b}},\\
\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}-\widetilde{v}_{j}=0&{\rm on}~\partial D,\\
\partial_{\nu}\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}-\left(\lambda\partial_{\nu}\widetilde{v}_{j}+\gamma\widetilde{v}_{j}\right)=(\lambda-\lambda(x_{0}))\partial_{\nu}\hat{v}_{j}&{\rm on}~\partial D,\\
\mathscr{B}(\widetilde{v}_{j})=0&{\rm on}~\partial D_{b},\\
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}r\left(\partial_{r}\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}-ik\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}\right)=0,&r=|x|.\end{array}\right.

Since

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{j}=\left(\hat{v}_{j}-\frac{2}{\lambda(x_{0})+1}\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})\right)+\frac{2}{\lambda(x_{0})+1}\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j}),

we obtain from (
2.28
) that

\displaystyle\|{\rm div\,}((\lambda(x_{0})-\lambda){\rm NewA}\hat{v}_{j})+k^{2}(1-n)\hat{v}_{j}\|_{H^{-1}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}+\|(\lambda-\lambda(x_{0}))\partial_{\nu}\hat{v}_{j}\|_{H^{-1/2}(\partial D)}\leq C

uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Hence, by the variational method we derive that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}_{j}^{s}\|_{H^{1}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{D})}+\|\widetilde{v}_{j}\|_{H^{1}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}\leq C

(2.36)

The proof is then finished by combining (
2.28
) and (
2.36
).
∎

Moreover, if
\lambda=1
near the boundary
\partial D
, we need to consider the hypersingular point source incidence. To this end, we refer the following theorem (see
[
27
, Theorem 2.7]
).

Theorem 2
.

With
u^{i}(x)=\nabla_{x}\Phi(x,z)\cdot\vec{a}
,
\lambda\equiv 1
and
\gamma\equiv 0
for problem (
1
), where
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{D}
and
\vec{a}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

(u^{s},v)
satisfies

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{p}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}+\|v-u^{i}\|_{H^{1}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}\leq C(\|u^{i}\|_{L^{p}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}+\|\mathscr{B}(u^{i})\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial D_{b})}),

where
6/5\leq p<3/2
.

Based on Theorems
1
and
2
, we can further prove the following result for the case
\gamma\neq 0
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
\gamma\in C^{0,\alpha_{1}}\left(\partial D\right)
with
1/2<\alpha_{1}\leq 1
and there exists an open neighborhood
O_{1}(\partial D)
of
\partial D
such that
\lambda=1
in
O_{1}(\partial D)
. Let
(u^{s}_{j},v_{j})
be the unique solution for problem
(
1
)
with the incident wave
u^{i}_{j}(x)={\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi(x,x_{j})\cdot\nu(x_{0})
. Then we have

\left\|v_{j}-u_{j}^{i}+(\gamma(x_{0})/2)\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})\right\|_{H^{1}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}\leq C

uniformly for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2
, there exists a unique solution
(\hat{u}^{s}_{j},\hat{v}_{j})
for problem (
1
) with
\lambda\equiv 1,\gamma\equiv 0
and incident waves
u^{i}_{j}

such that

\displaystyle\|\hat{v}_{j}-u^{i}_{j}\|_{H^{1}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}\leq C(\|u^{i}_{j}\|_{L^{p}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}+\|\mathscr{B}(u^{i}_{j})\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial D_{b})})\leq C.

(2.37)

From Theorem
1
, the unique solution
(\hat{U}^{s}_{j},\hat{V}_{j})
for problem (
1
) with
\lambda\equiv 1
and the incident waves
-\gamma(x_{0})\Phi(\cdot,x_{j})

satisfies

\displaystyle\|\hat{V}_{j}+\gamma(x_{0})\Phi(x,x_{j})\|_{H^{1}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}\leq C.

(2.38)

Now consider the following problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}+k^{2}\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}=0&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{D},\\
\Delta\widetilde{v}_{j}+k^{2}n\widetilde{v}_{j}=0&{\rm in}~D\setminus\overline{D_{b}},\\
\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}-\widetilde{v}_{j}=-\gamma(x_{0})\Phi(x,x_{j})&{\rm on}~\partial D,\\
\partial_{\nu}\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}-\left(\partial_{\nu}\widetilde{v}_{j}+\gamma\widetilde{v}_{j}\right)=\gamma(\hat{v}_{j}+\hat{V}_{j})-\gamma(x_{0})\partial_{\nu}\Phi(x,x_{j})&{\rm on}~\partial D,\\
\mathscr{B}(\widetilde{v}_{j})=0&{\rm on}~\partial D_{b},\\
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}r\left(\partial_{r}\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}-ik\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}\right)=0,&r=|x|.\end{array}\right.

Clearly,
\Phi(x,x_{j})\in L^{p}(\partial D)
for
1\leq p<2
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Further, it can be verified that

\displaystyle\gamma(\hat{v}_{j}+\hat{V}_{j})-\gamma(x_{0})\partial_{\nu}\Phi(x,x_{j})=

\displaystyle\;\gamma(\hat{v}_{j}-u^{i}_{j})+\gamma(\hat{V}_{j}+\gamma(x_{0})\Phi(x,x_{j}))

(2.46)

\displaystyle\;-\gamma(x_{0})\gamma\Phi(x,x_{j})+(\gamma(x)-\gamma(x_{0}))u^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle\;+\gamma(x_{0}){\rm NewA}_{x}\Phi(x,x_{j})\cdot(\nu(x_{0})-\nu(x)),

which implies that the right hand side of (

) is uniformly bounded in

with

due to (

), (
2.38
) as well as the regularity of the boundary

and

. Therefore, arguing similarly as in the proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{v}_{j}-(\gamma(x_{0})/2)\Phi_{0}(x,x_{j})\|_{H^{1}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}\leq C.

(2.47)

Let
U^{s}_{j}=\hat{u}^{s}_{j}+\hat{U}^{s}_{j}+\widetilde{u}^{s}_{j}
and

. Finally, we define

by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta\widetilde{U}^{s}_{j}+k^{2}\widetilde{U}^{s}_{j}=0&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\overline{D},\\
{\rm div\,}(\lambda{\rm NewA}\widetilde{V}_{j})+k^{2}n\widetilde{V}_{j}={\rm div\,}((1-\lambda)\nabla V_{j})&{\rm in}~D\setminus\overline{D_{b}},\\
\widetilde{U}^{s}_{j}-\widetilde{V}_{j}=0&{\rm on}~\partial D,\\
\partial_{\nu}\widetilde{U}^{s}_{j}-\left(\partial_{\nu}\widetilde{V}_{j}+\gamma\widetilde{V}_{j}\right)=0&{\rm on}~\partial D,\\
\mathscr{B}(\widetilde{V}_{j})=0&{\rm on}~\partial D_{b},\\
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}r\left(\partial_{r}\widetilde{U}^{s}_{j}-ik\widetilde{U}^{s}_{j}\right)=0,&r=|x|.\end{array}\right.

It is easily checked that

and

. Since

in

, it follows that
\|{\rm div\,}((1-\lambda)\nabla V_{j})\|_{H^{-1}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{b})}\leq C
, which implies by the variational method that

is well defined and

uniformly for

) and (

and

3
The inverse problem

In this section, we shall study two interior transmission problems and then apply a novel technique to prove the uniqueness theorem in determination of the object and relating coefficients. We show that the object
D
, the transmission coefficient
\lambda
, the conductive coefficient
\gamma
, the refractive index
n
and also some of their derivatives can be uniquely recovered, for which the proof is greatly relied on the singularity of the solutions and the aforementioned interior transmission problems.

3.1
The interior transmission problem

In this subsection, we introduce two types of interior transmission problems corresponding to the case
\lambda\neq 1
and
\lambda=1
near the boundary
\partial D
, respectively.

Denote by
\Omega
a bounded and simply connected domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
. For the case
\lambda\neq 1
, we consider the following interior transmission problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm div\,}(\lambda_{1}{\rm NewA}U)-b_{1}U=g_{1}&{\rm in}~\Omega,\\
{\rm div\,}(\lambda_{2}{\rm NewA}V)-b_{2}V=g_{2}&{\rm in}~\Omega,\\
U-V=h_{1}&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\\
\lambda_{1}\partial_{\nu}U-\lambda_{2}\partial_{\nu}V=h_{2}&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

with
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
,
g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
h_{1}\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
,
h_{2}\in H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
and
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
positive constants. This problem was studied in
[
25
]
, and we have the result below (see
[
25
, Corollary 2.2]
).

Lemma 5
.

Assume
\lambda_{i}>0
in
\overline{\Omega}
with
i=1,2
. If there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon<\min\{b_{2},1\}
such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon b_{1}>\left(\frac{1+b_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}~~~{\rm and}~~~\varepsilon\left(\inf\limits_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\right)>\left(\frac{1+b_{2}}{2}\right)^{2},

then the problem
(
3.1
)
has a unique solution
(U,V)
satisfying that

\displaystyle\|U\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|V\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|g_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|g_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|h_{1}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|h_{2}\|_{H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)}).

Remark 6
.

The condition
\varepsilon<b_{2}
excludes the case that
\inf\limits_{\Omega}(\lambda_{1}/\lambda_{2})\leq 1
since
[(1+b_{2})/2]^{2}\geq b_{2}
. But sometimes we may exchange
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
to make the conditions can be satisfied.

For the case
\lambda=1
, we introduce the interior transmission problem of the following type

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta v+k^{2}n_{1}v=0&{\rm in}~\Omega,\\
\Delta w+k^{2}n_{2}w=0&{\rm in}~\Omega,\\
v-w=f_{1}&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}v+\eta w-\partial_{\nu}w=f_{2}&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
f_{1}\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
,
f_{2}\in H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
,
n_{1},n_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\eta\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
. To get the well-posedness of the problem (
3.1
), we give a lemma first.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}(n\geq 2)
be a bounded domain with
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
. Define

\displaystyle C_{1}(\Omega):=\displaystyle\mathop{\sup\limits_{u\in\widetilde{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}}\limits_{u\neq 0}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|\partial_{\nu}u|^{2}ds}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{2}dx},

where
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(\Omega)=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then
C_{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow 0
as
diam(\Omega)\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

The standard elliptic regularity gives

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},~~~\forall u\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

Then by the trace theorem we obtain that
C_{1}(\Omega)<\infty
for any bounded domain
\Omega
with
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
.

Without loss of generality, we only consider the case that
\Omega
are open balls in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Set
C_{0}=C_{1}(B_{1})
, in other words,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left|\partial_{\nu}u\right|^{2}ds\leq C_{0}\int_{B_{1}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx,~~~\forall u\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(B_{1}).

Now for fixed
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, for any
u\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
, let
v(y)=u(x_{0}+Ry)
,
y\in B_{1}
. Then
v(y)\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(B_{1})
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left|\partial_{\nu}v\right|^{2}ds\leq C_{0}\int_{B_{1}}|\Delta v|^{2}dx.

Changing the integral variable gives that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{1}}\left|\partial_{\nu}v\right|^{2}ds=R^{3-n}\int_{\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\partial_{\nu}u\right|^{2}ds,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|\Delta v|^{2}dx=R^{4-n}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|\Delta u|^{2}dx,

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\partial_{\nu}u\right|^{2}ds\leq C_{0}R\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|\Delta u|^{2}dx,~~~\forall u\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(B_{R}(x_{0})).

Thus we obtain
C_{1}(B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq C_{0}R
and the conclusion follows.
∎

Denote by
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
. We recall that

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)=\mathop{\inf\limits_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}}\limits_{u\neq 0}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx}

and
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\infty
as
diam(\Omega)\rightarrow 0
.
Define the Hilbert space

\displaystyle H_{\Delta}^{1}(\Omega):=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega),\Delta u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}

with the inner product
(u,v)_{H_{\Delta}^{1}(\Omega)}=(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+(\Delta u,\Delta v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Clearly, for
u\in H_{\Delta}^{1}(\Omega)
we have that
u|_{\partial\Omega}\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
and
\partial_{\nu}u\in H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
. In particular, if
u=\partial_{\nu}u=0
on
\partial\Omega
for some
u\in H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
, then
u\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Now we give the following uniqueness and existence theorem for problem (
3.1
).

Theorem 8
.

Suppose there exists a
u_{0}\in H_{\Delta}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
u_{0}=f_{1}
and
\partial_{\nu}u_{0}=f_{2}
on
\partial\Omega
. Assume in addition that
n_{1},n_{2}>\delta_{1}
with
|n_{1}-n_{2}|>\delta_{1}
for some constant
\delta_{1}>0
. Then problem
(
3.1
)
has a unique solution
(v,w)\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H_{\Delta}^{1}(\Omega)},

provided
(k,n_{1},n_{2},\eta,\Omega)
satisfying one of the following conditions

1.

n_{1}-n_{2}<0,\eta>\delta_{2}
and
\displaystyle k^{2}<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\inf_{\Omega}n_{2}}{(\sup_{\Omega}n_{2})^{2}}
,

2.

n_{1}-n_{2}>0,\eta<-\delta_{2}
and
\displaystyle k^{2}<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\inf_{\Omega}n_{1}}{(\sup_{\Omega}n_{1})^{2}}
,

3.

n_{1}-n_{2}>0,\eta>\delta_{2},
and

0<\frac{C^{\prime}C_{1}(\Omega)k^{2}}{C^{\prime}\inf_{\Omega}|\eta|-C_{1}(\Omega)k^{2}}<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}{k^{2}(\sup_{\Omega}n_{1})^{2}}-\frac{1}{\inf_{\Omega}n_{1}},

4.

n_{1}-n_{2}<0,\eta<-\delta_{2},
and

0<\frac{C^{\prime}C_{1}(\Omega)k^{2}}{C^{\prime}\inf_{\Omega}|\eta|-C_{1}(\Omega)k^{2}}<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}{k^{2}(\sup_{\Omega}n_{2})^{2}}-\frac{1}{\inf_{\Omega}n_{2}},

5.

n_{1}-n_{2}<0,\eta=0
and
\displaystyle k^{2}<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\inf_{\Omega}n_{2}}{(\sup_{\Omega}n_{2})^{2}}
,

6.

n_{1}-n_{2}>0,\eta=0
and
\displaystyle k^{2}<\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\inf_{\Omega}n_{1}}{(\sup_{\Omega}n_{1})^{2}}
,

for some positive constant
\delta_{2}
and
C^{\prime}:=1/\sup_{\Omega}|n_{1}-n_{2}|
.

Proof.

First we consider the case
\eta\neq 0
corresponding to conditions 1-4. Set
u=v-w
, then it can be verified that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle(\Delta+k^{2}n_{2})\frac{1}{n_{2}-n_{1}}(\Delta+k^{2}n_{1})u=0&{\rm in}~\Omega,\\
u=f_{1}&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}u=f_{2}-\frac{\eta}{k^{2}(n_{2}-n_{1})}(\Delta u+k^{2}n_{1}u)&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Define
u^{\prime}=u-u_{0}\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. By Green’s formula we get that the variational formulation of (
3.1
) is to find a
u^{\prime}\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle B(u^{\prime},\varphi)=F(\varphi)~~~\forall\varphi\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(\Omega),

where

\displaystyle B(u^{\prime},\varphi):=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{n_{2}-n_{1}}(\Delta u^{\prime}+k^{2}n_{1}u^{\prime})(\Delta\overline{\varphi}+k^{2}n_{2}\overline{\varphi})dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{k^{2}}{\eta}\partial_{\nu}u^{\prime}\partial_{\nu}\overline{\varphi}ds,

\displaystyle F(\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{n_{1}-n_{2}}(\Delta u_{0}+k^{2}n_{1}u_{0})(\Delta\overline{\varphi}+k^{2}n_{2}\overline{\varphi})dx.

Clearly, by the Hölder’s inequality and the trace theorem, we have that

B(u^{\prime},\varphi)
and
F(\varphi)
are both bounded on
\widetilde{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Note that
\|u^{\prime}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Delta u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
for all
u^{\prime}\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Now for
(k,n_{1},n_{2},\eta,\Omega)
satisfying condition 1, we deduce the following

\displaystyle B(u^{\prime},u^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\;\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{n_{2}-n_{1}}|\Delta u^{\prime}+k^{2}n_{2}u^{\prime}|^{2}dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{k^{2}}{\eta}\left|\partial_{\nu}u^{\prime}\right|^{2}ds+k^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{\prime}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\;-k^{4}\int_{\Omega}n_{2}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\;\left(C^{\prime}-\frac{C^{{}^{\prime}2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)\|\Delta u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon_{1}\left(\frac{C^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\|\Delta u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-k^{2}\|n_{2}u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;+k^{4}\left(C^{\prime}+\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}{k^{2}(\sup_{\Omega}n_{2})^{2}}-\frac{1}{\inf_{\Omega}n_{2}}-\varepsilon_{1}\right)\|n_{2}u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\;C\|\Delta u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for all
u^{\prime}\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
with the constant
\varepsilon_{1}
satisfying that
0<\varepsilon_{1}-C^{\prime}<\lambda_{1}(\Omega)/[k^{2}(\sup_{\Omega}n_{2})^{2}]-1/\inf_{\Omega}n_{2}
. Thus we obtain that
B(u^{\prime},\varphi)
is coercive on
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
when
(k,n_{1},n_{2},\eta,\Omega)
satisfies condition 1. Similar calculation also yields that
B(u^{\prime},\varphi)
is coercive with condition 2.

Under condition 3, we choose a positive constant
\varepsilon_{2}
such that
C^{\prime}C_{1}(\Omega)k^{2}/(C^{\prime}\inf_{\Omega}|\eta|-C_{1}(\Omega)k^{2})<\varepsilon_{2}<\lambda_{1}(\Omega)/[k^{2}(\sup_{\Omega}n_{1})^{2}]-1/\inf_{\Omega}n_{1}
. Then for all
u^{\prime}\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
we derive

\displaystyle-B(u^{\prime},u^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\;\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{n_{1}-n_{2}}|\Delta u^{\prime}+k^{2}n_{1}u^{\prime}|^{2}dx+k^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{\prime}|^{2}dx-k^{4}\int_{\Omega}n_{1}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\;-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{k^{2}}{\eta}\left|\partial_{\nu}u^{\prime}\right|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\;\left(C^{\prime}-\frac{k^{2}C_{1}(\Omega)}{\inf_{\Omega}|\eta|}-\frac{C^{{}^{\prime}2}}{\varepsilon_{2}+C^{\prime}}\right)\|\Delta u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\;+(\varepsilon_{2}+C^{\prime})\left(\frac{C^{\prime}}{\varepsilon_{2}+C^{\prime}}\|\Delta u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-k^{2}\|n_{1}u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;+k^{4}\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}{k^{2}(\sup_{\Omega}n_{1})^{2}}-\frac{1}{\inf_{\Omega}n_{1}}-\varepsilon_{2}\right)\|n_{1}u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\;C\|\Delta u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

The coercivity of
B(u^{\prime},\varphi)
under condition 4 follows analogously as above.

Then we consider the case
\eta=0
, which corresponds to conditions 5 and 6. We see that
u=v-w
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle(\Delta+k^{2}n_{2})\frac{1}{n_{2}-n_{1}}(\Delta+k^{2}n_{1})u=0&{\rm in}~\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
u=f_{1}&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{\nu}u=f_{2}&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Set
u^{\prime}=u-u_{0}\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)

3.1
): finding

such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}(u^{\prime},\varphi)=F(\varphi)~~~\forall\varphi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}(u^{\prime},\varphi):=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{n_{2}-n_{1}}(\Delta u^{\prime}+k^{2}n_{1}u^{\prime})(\Delta\overline{\varphi}+k^{2}n_{2}\overline{\varphi})dx.

Obviously,

is still bounded on
H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)

is coercive under conditions 5 and 6.

Now by the Lax-Milgram theorem, with conditions 1-6, there exists a unique solution

or
H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\|u^{\prime}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega)}.

Define

(v,w)\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
is the unique solution to problem (

) and the desired a priori estimate holds.
∎

Remark 9

Since
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\infty

C_{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow 0

is always well-posed in small domains.

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}^{i}_{j}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}\leq\|\widetilde{u}_{2,j}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}+\|\widetilde{u}_{2,j}^{s}\|_{L^{2}(D_{0})}\leq C.

Remark 12

Note that the regularity requirements on the coefficients in this theorem can be relaxed to only in the neighborhood of the boundary

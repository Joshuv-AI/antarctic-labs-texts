Ground state solutions for Schrödinger-Poisson system with a doping profile
Mathieu Colin, Tatsuya Watanabe

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the study of the nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system with a doping profile. We are interested in the existence of ground state solutions by considering the minimization problem on a Nehari-Pohozaev set. The presence of a doping profile causes several difficulties, especially in the proof of the uniqueness of a maximum point of a fibering map. A key ingredient is to establish the energy inequality inspired by [ 31 ] . We also establish the relation between ground state solutions and L 2 L^{2} -constraint minimizers obtained in [ 17 ] . When the doping profile is a characteristic function supported on a bounded smooth domain, some geometric quantities related to the domain, such as the mean curvature, are responsible for the existence of ground state solutions.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the following nonlinear
Schrödinger-Poisson system:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}&-\Delta u+\omega u+e\phi u=|u|^{p-1}u\\
&-\Delta\phi=\frac{e}{2}\left(|u|^{2}-\rho(x)\right)\end{cases}\quad\hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},

where
\omega>0
,
e>0
and
1<p<5
.
Equation (
1.1
) appears as a stationary problem for the
time-dependent nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}i\psi_{t}+\Delta\psi-e\phi\psi+|\psi|^{p-1}\psi=0\quad\hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
-\Delta\phi=\frac{e}{2}\big(|\psi|^{2}-\rho(x)\big)\quad\hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}.\end{cases}

Indeed when we look for a standing wave of the form:
\psi(t,x)=e^{i\omega t}u(x)
,
we are led to the elliptic problem (
1.1
).
In this paper, we are interested in the existence of
ground state solutions of (
1.1
)
and their relation with
L^{2}
-constraint minimizers obtained in
[
17
]
.

The Schrödinger-Poisson system appears
in various fields of physics, such as quantum mechanics,
black holes in gravitation and plasma physics.
Especially, the Schrödinger-Poisson system plays an important role
in the study of semi-conductor theory; see
[
23
,
27
,
29
]
,
and then the function
\rho(x)
is referred as
impurities

or a
doping profile
.
The doping profile comes from
the difference of the number densities of positively charged donor ions
and negatively charged acceptor ions,
and the most typical examples are characteristic functions, step functions
or Gaussian functions.
Equation (
1.1
) also appears as a stationary problem
for the Maxwell-Schrödinger system.
We refer to
[
7
,
14
,
15
]
for the physical background
and the stability result of standing waves for the Maxwell-Schrödinger system.
In this case, the constant
e
describes the strength of the interaction
between a particle and an external electromagnetic field.

The nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system with
\rho\equiv 0
:

(1.3)

\begin{cases}&-\Delta u+\omega u+e\phi u=|u|^{p-1}u\\
&-\Delta\phi=\frac{e}{2}|u|^{2}\end{cases}\quad\hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}

has been studied widely in the last two decades.
Especially, the existence of non-trivial solutions and
ground state solutions of (
1.3
)
has been considered in detail.
Furthermore, the existence of associated
L^{2}
-constraint minimizers
depending on
p
and the size of the mass
and their stability have been investigated as well.
We refer to e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
12
,
14
,
22
,
24
,
26
,
28
,
32
,
33
,
36
]

and references therein.
On the other hand, the nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system with
a doping profile is less studied.
In
[
18
,
19
]
, the corresponding 1D problem has been considered.
Moreover, the linear Schrödinger-Poisson system
(that is, the problem (
1.1
) without
|u|^{p-1}u
)
with a doping profile in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
has been studied in
[
8
,
9
]
.
In
[
17
]
, the authors have investigated the existence of
stable standing waves for (
1.2
) by considering the corresponding

L^{2}
-minimization problem.
As far as we know, there is no literature
concerning with the existence of ground state solutions of (
1.1
),
which is exactly the purpose of this paper.

To state our main results, let us give some notation.
For
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
,
the energy functional associated with (
1.1
) is given by

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\omega}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+e^{2}\mathcal{A}(u).

Here we denote the nonlocal term by
S(u)=S_{0}(u)+S_{1}
with

\displaystyle S_{0}(u)(x)

\displaystyle:=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{8\pi|x|}*|u(x)|^{2},

\displaystyle S_{1}(x)

\displaystyle:=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\frac{-\rho(x)}{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{8\pi|x|}*\rho(x),

and the functional corresponding to the nonlocal term by

\mathcal{A}(u):=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S(u)\big(|u|^{2}-\rho(x)\big)\,dx=\frac{1}{32\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\big(|u(x)|^{2}-\rho(x)\big)\big(|u(y)|^{2}-\rho(y)\big)}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy.

A function
u_{0}
is said to be a
ground state solution
(GSS)
of (
1.1
)
if
u_{0}
has a least energy among all nontrivial solutions of (
1.1
),
namely
u_{0}
satisfies

\mathcal{I}(u_{0})=\inf\{\mathcal{I}(u)\mid u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\},\ \mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u)=0\}.

For the doping profile
\rho
, we assume that

(1.5)

\rho(x)\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\ \text{for some}\ q>3,\quad x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\quad x\cdot(D^{2}\rho(x)x)\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

where
D^{2}\rho
is the Hessian matrix of
\rho
, and

(1.6)

\rho(x)\geq 0,\ \not\equiv 0\quad\text{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Typical examples are the Gaussian function
\rho(x)=\varepsilon e^{-\alpha|x|^{2}}

and
\rho(x)=\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\alpha|x|^{r}}
for
r>\frac{5}{2}
.

In this setting, our first main result can be expressed as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
2<p<5
and assume (
1.5
)-(
1.6
).
There exists
\rho_{0}
independent of
e
,
\rho
such that if

e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)\leq\rho_{0},

then (
1.1
) has a ground state solution
u_{0}
.
Moreover any ground state solution of (
1.1
) is real-valued
up to phase shift.

When
1<p<2
, we are able to obtain the existence of a
radial

ground state solution of (
1.1
),
which is a weak result; see Section 6 below.

Our second purpose of this paper is to investigate the relation
between the ground state solution of (
1.1
) obtained in
Theorem
1.1
and the
L^{2}
-constraint minimizer in
[
17
]
.
There has been significant progress in this relationship in recent years;
see
[
16
,
20
,
22
]
for this direction.
Based on the terminology in
[
16
,
20
]
,
we call
u_{0}
obtained in Theorem
1.1

an
action ground state solution
of (
1.1
).

To state our second main result, we define
the energy functional
\mathcal{E}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+e^{2}\mathcal{A}(u).

For
\mu>0
, let us consider the minimization problem:

(1.7)

\mathcal{C}(\mu)=\inf_{u\in B(\mu)}\mathcal{E}(u),

where
B(\mu)=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\mid\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=\mu\}
.
In this setting, the constant
\omega
in (
1.1
)
appears as a Lagrange multiplier.
We also define the energy associated with (
1.3
):

E_{\infty}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p+1}\,dx+\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx.

Indeed if we assume
\rho(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
,
(
1.3
) can be seen as a problem at infinity.
We define the minimum energy associated with (
1.3
) by

c_{e,\infty}(\mu)=c_{\infty}(\mu):=\inf_{u\in B(\mu)}E_{\infty}(u).

The existence of minimizers for
c_{e,\infty}(\mu)
has been studied widely;
we refer to
[
5
,
6
,
14
,
12
]
and references therein.
Especially in the case
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
,

c_{e,\infty}(\mu)
is attained if
c_{e,\infty}(\mu)<0
.
Moreover there exists
\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}(e)>0
such that

(1.8)

\mu^{*}=\inf\{\mu>0\mid c_{e,\infty}(\mu)<0\}.

In
[
17
]
, under the additional assumptions:

(1.9)

\text{There exist $\alpha>2$ and $C>0$ such that}\ \rho(x)\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{\alpha}}\quad\text{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.10)

\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}),\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+2e^{2}S_{1}(x)|u|^{2}\right)dx=0,

it was shown that
if
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
and
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
,
there exists
\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(e,\mu)>0
such that
if
\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\rho_{0}
,
the minimization problem (
1.7
) admits a minimizer
u_{\mu}

and the associated Lagrange multiplier
\omega=\omega(\mu)
is positive.

As in
[
16
,
20
]
, we call
u_{\mu}
an

energy ground state solution
of (
1.1
).
Our second main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
be given
and suppose that
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
.
Under the assumptions in Theorem
1.1

and (
1.9
)-(
1.10
), the following properties hold.

(i)

The energy ground state solution
u_{\mu}
is
an action ground state solution of (
1.1
) with
\omega=\omega_{\mu}
.

(ii)
Let
\Omega(\mu)
be the set of
Lagrange multipliers associated with energy ground state solutions for
B_{\mu}
,
namely

\displaystyle\Omega(\mu):=\left\{\right.\omega_{\mu}>0\mid

\omega_{\mu}
is the Lagrange multiplier
associated with an energy ground state

\displaystyle\hbox{of \eqref{eq:1.1} under the constraint $B_{\mu}$}\left.\right\},

and
w_{\mu}
be an action ground state solution
of (
1.1
) with
\omega=\omega_{\mu}
.
Then
w_{\mu}
is an energy ground state solution of (
1.1
)
under the constraint
B_{\mu}
.

We emphasize that up to authors’ knowledge,
Theorem
1.2
is new even for the case
\rho\equiv 0
.
(See also Remark
5.3
below.)

The assumption (
1.5
) rules out the case

\rho
is a characteristic function supported on a bounded smooth domain.
Even in this case, we are still able to obtain the existence of
ground state solutions and their relation
under a smallness condition on some geometric quantities related to the domain;
See Section 7.

Here we briefly explain our strategy and its difficulty.
For the existence of a ground state solution of (
1.1
),
we adapt a strategy in
[
4
,
28
]
, that is,
we aim to obtain a ground state solution as a minimizer of
the
Nehari-Pohozaev
constraint
0=J(u)=2N(u)-P(u)
,
where
N(u)=0
is the Nehari identity and
P(u)=0
is the
Pohozaev identity for (
1.1
).
It is standard to show that for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
,
there exists
\lambda=\lambda_{u}>0
such that
J(u_{\lambda})=0
provided that

u_{\lambda}(x)=t^{2}u(\lambda x)
.
A crucial point is then to show that
\lambda_{u}
is unique.
In
[
28
]
for the case
\rho\equiv 0
,
this was carried out by considering up to third derivatives of the fibering map,
which requires an assumption involving third derivative of
\rho
in our case.
To avoid this difficulty, we follows the idea in
[
31
]
to establish
the following
energy inequality
:

\displaystyle I(u)-I(u_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}J(u)+\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}\omega}{3}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{6(p+1)}(1-\lambda)^{2}\|u\|_{p+1}^{p+1}

(1.11)

\displaystyle\quad-\beta(1-\lambda)^{2}e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)

for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
,
0\leq\lambda\leq T
and some
T
,
\alpha
,
\beta>0
.
This key inequality is enough to prove the uniqueness of
\lambda_{u}
for

u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
with
J(u)\leq 0
.
Moreover we can apply the energy inequality to show the relation
between two types of ground state solutions of (
1.1
).

As we can easily imagine, if a doping profile
\rho
is considered,
scaling arguments do not work straightforwardly
because of the loss of spatial homogeneity.
Furthermore the presence of the doping profile
\rho

satisfying (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) causes additional difficulties.
Firstly we cannot expect that a remainder term in (
1.11
)
is zero.
Secondly we are not able to use the concentration function as in
[
4
]
.
See Remark
3.8
and Remark
4.4
for details.

Compared to
[
17
]
,
we impose one additional assumption on
\rho
,
which is the
L^{\frac{6}{5}}
-integrability of

x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)
.
Unlike the existence of minimizers,
we need detailed geometric properties of the functional
\mathcal{I}
.
Especially we apply the second-order Taylor expansion to obtain (
1.11
),
causing that some estimate for
x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)
is required.

When
\rho
is a characteristic function,
further consideration is required
because
\rho
cannot be weakly differentiable.
In this case, a key of the proof is the
sharp boundary trace inequality

which was developed in
[
3
]
,
and a variation of domain
related with the
calculus of moving surfaces

due to Hadamard
[
21
]
.
Then by imposing a smallness condition of some geometric quantities
related to the support of
\rho
,
we are able to obtain the existence of ground state solutions.

In this paper, we are only concerned with the case
\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
is small.
For the moment, we don’t know what happens if
\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
is large.
Especially, it is interesting to ask if the non-existence result can be obtained for this case.
It is also worth mentioning that in
[
8
,
9
]
where the problem (
1.1
) without
|u|^{p-1}u
was studied,
the existence of a ground state solution has been shown under the assumption:

(1.12)

\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+S_{1}(x)\varphi^{2}\right)\,dx,\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\varphi|^{2}\,dx=1\right\}<0

and for small
\omega>0
.
By Lemma
2.2
below, (
1.12
) cannot be satisfied when

\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
is small.
It is an another interesting question if we could replace (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) by (
1.12
)
to establish the existence of a ground state solution of (
1.1
).

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce several properties of the energy functional
and some lemmas which will be used later on.
We investigate basic properties of the Nehari-Pohozaev set in Section 3.
In Section 4, we prove the existence of a ground state solution of (
1.1
)
by applying the concentration compactness principle
and completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Section 5 is devoted to the relation between
action ground state solutions and energy ground state solutions.
We also study the case
1<p<2
in Section 6.
In Section 7, we finish this paper
by considering the case
\rho
is a characteristic function
and present the existence of ground state solutions
and their relation for this case.

Hereafter in this paper,
unless otherwise specified, we write
\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|u\|_{p}
.
We also set
\|u\|^{2}:=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{2}^{2}
.

2.
Variational formulation and preliminaries

The aim of this section is to prepare several properties of the energy functional
and present intermediate lemmas which will be used later on.

2.1.
Decomposition of the energy

In this subsection, we rewrite the energy functional
\mathcal{I}

in a more convenient way.
We put

A(u)=\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2},\quad B(u)=\|u\|_{2}^{2},\quad C(u)=\|u\|_{p+1}^{p+1},

and decompose
\mathcal{I}
in the following way:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}A(u)+\frac{\omega}{2}B(u)-\frac{1}{p+1}C(u)

\displaystyle+\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}|u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\rho(x)\,dx-\frac{e^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}\rho(x)\,dx.

Next we define three nonlocal terms:

(2.1)

\displaystyle D(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx,

(2.2)

\displaystyle E_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\rho(x)\,dx=-\frac{1}{32\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}\rho(x)}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}|u|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}\rho(x)\,dx.

Note that
F
is independent of
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
.
Then we are able to write
\mathcal{I}
defined in (
1.4
) in the following form:

\mathcal{I}(u)=\frac{1}{2}A(u)+\frac{\omega}{2}B(u)-\frac{1}{p+1}C(u)+e^{2}D(u)+2e^{2}E_{1}(u)+e^{2}F.

Now it is convenient to define
I(u):=\mathcal{I}(u)-e^{2}F
, which yields that

(2.3)

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}A(u)+\frac{\omega}{2}B(u)-\frac{1}{p+1}C(u)+e^{2}D(u)+2e^{2}E_{1}(u).

Since
F
is independent of
u
,
we have only to consider the
existence of nontrivial critical point of
I
.
Recalling that

S_{0}(u)(x)=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{2}\right)\geq 0,

we find that

(2.4)

A(u),\ B(u),\ C(u),\ D(u)\geq 0\quad\hbox{for all}\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}).

For later use, let us also define

(2.5)

\displaystyle E_{2}(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{2}|u|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{3}(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot(D^{2}\rho(x)x)\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{3}|u|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle S_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\frac{x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{8\pi|x|}*\big(x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\big),

\displaystyle S_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(\frac{x\cdot(D^{2}\rho(x)x)}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{8\pi|x|}*\big(x\cdot(D^{2}\rho(x)x)\big),

which is well-defined for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
by (
1.5
).

2.2.
Derivatives of nonlocal terms

We next investigate Frechlet derivatives of
D
,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}

which will be needed later.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)
For
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
, it holds that

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(u)\varphi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)u\bar{\varphi}\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{1}^{\prime}(u)\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}u\bar{\varphi}\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{2}^{\prime}(u)\varphi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{2}u\bar{\varphi}dx.

Especially we have

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(u)u

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx=4D(u),

\displaystyle E_{1}^{\prime}(u)u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}|u|^{2}\,dx=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\rho(x)\,dx=2E_{1}(u),

\displaystyle E_{2}^{\prime}(u)u

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{2}|u|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\,dx=2E_{2}(u).

(ii)
It follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{0}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{5}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx=-5D(u),

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{1}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\rho(x)\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=-10E_{1}(u)+E_{2}(u),

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{2}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=-3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot(D^{2}\rho(x)x)\,dx

\displaystyle=-6E_{2}(u)-E_{3}(u).

Proof.

(i) We observe from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(u)\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}^{\prime}(u)\varphi|u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)u\bar{\varphi}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u(y)\overline{\varphi(y)}|u(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dy\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)u\bar{\varphi}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)u\bar{\varphi}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)u\bar{\varphi}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)u\bar{\varphi}\,dx.

The derivatives of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
can be derived readily by (
2.2
) and (
2.5
).

(ii)
By the divergence theorem and the fact
S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
arguing as in
[
10
,
13
]
, one has

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{0}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}x\cdot n\,dS-\frac{3}{2}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u|^{2}x\cdot\nabla S_{0}(u)\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}x\cdot\nabla S_{0}(u)\,dx.

Using

-\Delta S_{0}(u)=\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla S_{0}(u)|^{2}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx,

we find that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}x\cdot\nabla S_{0}(u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla S_{0}(u)\cdot\nabla\big(x\cdot\nabla S_{0}(u)\big)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla S_{0}(u)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla S_{0}(u)\cdot\left(D^{2}S_{0}(u)x\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla S_{0}(u)|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\Delta S_{0}(u)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla\big(\Delta S_{0}(u)\big)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla S_{0}(u)|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla|u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}x\cdot\nabla S_{0}(u)\,dx.

This implies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}x\cdot\nabla S_{0}(u)\,dx=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx

and hence

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{0}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx=-\frac{5}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\,dx.

Next since
\Delta S_{1}=\frac{\rho}{2}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}x\cdot\nabla S_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla S_{0}(u)\cdot\nabla(x\cdot\nabla S_{1})\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\rho(x)\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\,dx,

yielding that

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{1}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}|u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}x\cdot\nabla S_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\rho(x)\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\,dx.

Similarly from
\Delta S_{2}=-\frac{x\cdot\nabla\rho}{2}
,
we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}x\cdot\nabla S_{2}\,dx=\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot(D^{2}\rho(x)x)\,dx,

and hence

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{2}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{2}ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx=-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{2}|u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}x\cdot\nabla S_{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot(D^{2}\rho(x)x)\,dx.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.4

By Lemma

By Lemma

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that

.
There exists

Proof.

First we have from (

), (
3.6
) and (
3.9

(3.10)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=I^{\prime}(u)u-\mu J^{\prime}(u)u=N(u)-\mu J^{\prime}(u)u

\displaystyle=(1-3\mu)A(u)+(1-\mu)\omega B(u)-(1-(2p-1)\mu)C(u)+(4-12\mu)e^{2}D(u)

\displaystyle\quad+(4+4\mu)e^{2}E_{1}(u)-2\mu e^{2}E_{2}(u).

Furthermore from (
2.10

3.7
)

(3.11)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=P(u)-\mu Q(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-3\mu}{2}A(u)+\frac{3(1-\mu)\omega}{2}B(u)-\frac{3-3\mu(2p-1)}{p+1}C(u)+(5-15\mu)e^{2}D(u)

\displaystyle\quad+(10+10\mu)e^{2}E_{1}(u)-(1+7\mu)e^{2}E_{2}(u)-\mu e^{2}E_{3}(u).

Combining (
2.3
), (
3.1
), (
3.10
) and (

),
we arrive at the following system of equations:

(3.12)

\begin{pmatrix}I-2e^{2}E_{1}\\
2e^{2}E_{1}-e^{2}E_{2}\\
(4+4\mu)e^{2}E_{1}-2\mu e^{2}E_{2}\\

where

By a direct calculation with the aid of Mathematica, it follows that

If

, we can perform

\left(\begin{array}[]{l|c}&I-2e^{2}E_{1}\\
&2e^{2}E_{1}-e^{2}E_{2}\\
\mbox{\smash{\huge{$\Lambda$}}}&(4+4\mu)e^{2}E_{1}-2\mu e^{2}E_{2}\\
&(10+10\mu)e^{2}E_{1}-(1+7\mu)e^{2}E_{2}-e^{2}\mu E_{3}\end{array}\right)

\to\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc|c}\frac{1}{2}&\frac{\omega}{2}&-\frac{1}{p+1}&e^{2}&I-2e^{2}E_{1}\\

However by Lemma
2.2
and Lemma

, it holds that

3I(u)+\frac{16}{3}e^{2}E_{1}(u)-\frac{7}{3}e^{2}E_{2}(u)-\frac{1}{3}e^{2}E_{3}(u)>0\quad\text{for any}\ u\in\mathcal{M}

if

,

On the other hand when

0\leq C(u)=\frac{p+1}{4(p-1)(p-2)}\left\{16e^{2}E_{1}(u)-7e^{2}E_{2}(u)-e^{2}E_{3}(u)-3I(u)\right\}.

e^{2}(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}})

.
∎

Now let us define the ground state energy level for (

) by

m:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}}I(u),\quad\mathcal{S}=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}\mid I^{\prime}(u)=0\right\}.

By Lemma
3.5
, we are able to prove the following.

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose that
2<p<5
and assume that

e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\leq\rho_{0}

for sufficiently small
\rho_{0}>0
.

If
\sigma
defined in (
3.4
) is attained by some
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}
,
then
u_{0}
is a ground state solution of (
1.1
), namely
u_{0}
satisfies

\sigma=I(u_{0})=m.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
be a nontrivial critical point of
I
.
Then by Lemma
2.5
, it follows that
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{M}
and hence

\sigma=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}}I(u)\leq I(\tilde{u}).

Taking infimum over
\mathcal{S}
, we find that
\sigma\leq m
.

On the other hand by Lemma
3.5
, if
\sigma
is achieved by
u_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
,
one has
I^{\prime}(u_{0})=0
.
This implies that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
and

m\leq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}}I(u)\leq I(u_{0})=\sigma,

from which we conclude that
m=\sigma
.
∎

By Proposition
3.6
,
it suffices to investigate the attainability of
\sigma
.
To this aim, we next establish the following energy estimate,
which is a key tool in this paper
and will be also used to prove the equivalence between
two types of ground state solutions in Section 5.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that
2<p<5
and take
T\geq 4
so that
T^{2p-4}\geq 3
.
There exist
\alpha=\alpha(T)>0
and
\beta=\beta(T)>0

independent of

,
\rho
,
\lambda
such that the following estimates hold:
For any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
,

(3.13)

\displaystyle I(u)-I(u_{\lambda})-\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}J(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}\omega}{3}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{6(p+1)}(1-\lambda)^{2}\|u\|_{p+1}^{p+1}

\displaystyle\quad-\beta(1-\lambda)^{2}e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)\quad\text{for}\ 0\leq\lambda\leq T,

(3.14)

\displaystyle I(u)-I(u_{\lambda})-\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}J(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}\omega}{6}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{3}\omega}{3T}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{12(p+1)}(1-\lambda)^{2}\|u\|_{p+1}^{p+1}+\frac{\alpha}{6(p+1)}\lambda^{3}\|u\|_{p+1}^{p+1}

\displaystyle\quad-\beta\lambda^{3}e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)\quad\text{for}\ \lambda\geq T.

Assume further that
\|u\|_{p+1}\geq\delta
for some
\delta>0

independent of
e
,
\rho
.
There exists
\rho_{0}>0

independent of
e
,
\rho
,
\lambda
such that if

e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\leq\rho_{0},

then the following estimate holds:

(3.15)

\displaystyle I(u)-I(u_{\lambda})-\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}J(u)\geq\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}\omega}{6}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha\delta^{p-1}}{12(p+1)}(1-\lambda)^{2}\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\quad\text{ for all }\ \lambda>0.

Proof.

The proof consists of four steps.

Step 1
(Transformation of
I(u)-I(u_{\lambda})
):
First we observe from (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle I(u)-I(u_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{2}A(u)+\frac{(1-\lambda)\omega}{2}B(u)-\frac{1-\lambda^{2p-1}}{p+1}C(u)+(1-\lambda^{3})e^{2}D(u)

\displaystyle+2e^{2}E_{1}(u)+\frac{e^{2}\lambda^{-1}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)\,dx.

Transforming (
3.1
), one also has

\frac{1}{2}A(u)+e^{2}D(u)=\frac{1}{3}J(u)-\frac{\omega}{6}B(u)+\frac{2p-1}{3(p+1)}C(u)+\frac{2}{3}e^{2}E_{1}(u)-\frac{1}{3}e^{2}E_{2}(u),

from which we deduce that

\displaystyle I(u)-I(u_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}J(u)+\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}(\lambda+2)\omega}{6}B(u)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{3(p+1)}\left(3\lambda^{2p-1}-(2p-1)\lambda^{3}+2p-4\right)C(u)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{8-2\lambda^{3}}{3}e^{2}E_{1}(u)-\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}e^{2}E_{2}(u)+\frac{e^{2}\lambda^{-1}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)\,dx.

Moreover putting

\displaystyle R(\lambda,u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{8-2\lambda^{3}}{3}e^{2}E_{1}(u)-\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}e^{2}E_{2}(u)+\frac{e^{2}\lambda^{-1}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)\,dx

\displaystyle=e^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)M(\lambda,x)\,dx,

\displaystyle M(\lambda,x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\lambda^{3}-1}{6}\big(\rho(x)+x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\big)-\frac{\rho(x)}{2}+\frac{\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)}{2\lambda},

we arrive at

(3.16)

\displaystyle I(u)-I(u_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}J(u)+\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}(\lambda+2)\omega}{6}B(u)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{3(p+1)}\left(3\lambda^{2p-1}-(2p-1)\lambda^{3}+2p-4\right)C(u)+R(\lambda,u).

Step 2
(Evaluation of coefficients):
Now for
T\geq 4
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}(\lambda+2)}{6}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}}{3}+\frac{\lambda^{3}}{6}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{3}+\frac{\lambda}{6}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}}{3}+\frac{2\lambda^{3}}{3T}-\frac{\lambda^{3}}{3T}=\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}}{3}+\frac{\lambda^{3}}{3T}\quad\text{ for }\ \lambda\geq T,

from which we get

(3.17)

\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}(\lambda+2)}{6}\geq\begin{cases}\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}}{3}&\text{ for }\ 0\leq\lambda\leq T,\\
\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}}{6}+\frac{\lambda^{3}}{3T}&\text{ for }\ \lambda\geq T.\end{cases}

Next we claim that

(3.18)

\frac{1}{3(p+1)}\left(3\lambda^{2p-1}-(2p-1)\lambda^{3}+2p-4\right)\geq\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha}{6(p+1)}(1-\lambda)^{2}&\text{ for }0\leq\lambda\leq T,\\
\frac{\alpha}{12(p+1)}(1-\lambda)^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{6(p+1)}\lambda^{3}&\text{ for }\ \lambda\geq T.\end{cases}

For this purpose, we put

g(\lambda):=3\lambda^{2p-1}-(2p-1)\lambda^{3}+2p-4
.
For
\lambda\geq T
, one has

g(\lambda)\geq\lambda^{3}\left(3\lambda^{2p-4}-(2p-1)\right)\geq\lambda^{3}\left(3T^{2p-4}-(2p-1)\right).

Since
2<p<5
and
T^{2p-4}\geq 3
,
it holds
\frac{2p-1}{3}<3\leq T^{2p-4}
.
Taking
C_{1}=3T^{2p-4}-(2p-1)>0
,
we get

(3.19)

g(\lambda)\geq C_{1}\lambda^{3}\quad\text{for}\ \lambda\geq T.

Now we take
0<\tau<1
so that
(1-\tau)^{2p-4}=\frac{p}{2(p-1)}
,
which is possible because
p>2
.
For
0\leq\lambda\leq 1-\tau
, it holds that

g^{\prime}(\lambda)=3(2p-1)\lambda^{2}(\lambda^{2p-4}-1)<0
and thus

(3.20)

g(\lambda)\geq g(1-\tau)\geq g(1-\tau)(1-\lambda)^{2}\quad\text{for}\ 0\leq\lambda\leq 1-\tau.

Next we show that

(3.21)

g(\lambda)\geq C_{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}\quad\text{for}\ \lambda\geq 1+\tau\ \text{and some}\ C_{2}>0.

Letting
h(\lambda):=g(\lambda)-C_{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}
, one finds that

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=3(2p-1)\lambda^{2}\left(\lambda^{2p-4}-1\right)-2C_{2}\lambda+2C_{2}\geq\lambda^{2}\left(3(2p-1)(\lambda^{2p-4}-1)-2C_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{2}\left(3(2p-1)\left((1+\tau)^{2p-4}-1\right)-2C_{2}\right)\quad\text{for}\ \lambda\geq 1+\tau.

If
0<C_{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}(2p-1)\left((1+\tau)^{2p-4}-1\right)
,
then
h^{\prime}(\lambda)\geq 0
on
[1+\tau,\infty)
.
Thus taking

C_{2}=\min\left\{\frac{g(1+\tau)}{\tau^{2}},\frac{3}{2}(2p-1)\left((1+\tau)^{2p-4}-1\right)\right\},

we deduce that
h^{\prime}(\lambda)\geq 0
and
h(\lambda)\geq h(1+\tau)\geq 0
,
which shows (
3.21
).

Finally since
g(1)=g^{\prime}(1)=0
and
g^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)=6(2p-1)\left((p-1)\lambda^{2p-4}-1\right)
,
we have by the Taylor theorem that

g(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime\prime}(\xi)(1-\lambda)^{2}\quad\text{for some $\xi$ between $\lambda$ and $1$}.

When
1-\tau\leq\lambda\leq 1+\tau
, it follows that

\lambda^{2p-4}\geq(1-\tau)^{2p-4}=\frac{p}{2(p-1)}
,
from which we obtain

g^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\geq 6(2p-1)\left(\frac{p}{2}-1\right)
and

(3.22)

g(\lambda)\geq\frac{3}{2}(2p-1)(p-2)(1-\lambda)^{2}\quad\text{for}\ 1-\tau\leq\lambda\leq 1+\tau.

Combining (
3.19
)-(
3.22
) and putting

\alpha:=\min\left\{\frac{3}{2}(p-1)(p-2),g(1-\tau),C_{1},C_{2}\right\},

we arrive at

g(\lambda)\geq\begin{cases}\alpha(1-\lambda)^{2}&\text{for}\ \lambda\geq 0,\\
\alpha\lambda^{3}&\text{for}\ \lambda\geq T.\end{cases}

Moreover since

\frac{1}{3(p+1)}\left(3\lambda^{2p-1}-(2p-1)\lambda^{3}+2p-4\right)=\frac{1}{3(p+1)}g(\lambda)=\frac{1}{6(p+1)}g(\lambda)+\frac{1}{6(p+1)}g(\lambda),

we obtain (
3.18
).

Step 3
(Estimate for
R(\lambda,u)
):
First when
\lambda\geq T
, one find from the assumption (
1.6
) that

\displaystyle M(\lambda,x)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\lambda^{3}-1}{6}\big(|\rho(x)|+|x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)|\big)-\frac{1}{2}|\rho(x)|\geq-\frac{\lambda^{3}}{6}\big(|\rho(x)|+|x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)|\big)-\frac{1}{2}|\rho(x)|

\displaystyle\geq-\lambda^{3}\left(\frac{2}{3}|\rho(x)|+\frac{1}{6}|x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)|\right).

Thus by Lemma
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle R(\lambda,u)

\displaystyle\geq-\lambda^{3}e^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|S_{0}(u)|\left(\frac{2}{3}|\rho(x)|+\frac{1}{6}|x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)|\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq-\lambda^{3}e^{2}\left(\frac{2}{3}\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\frac{1}{6}\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\|S_{0}(u)\|_{6}

(3.23)

\displaystyle\geq-C_{3}\lambda^{3}e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)\quad\text{for}\ \lambda\geq T,

where
C_{3}>0
is independent of
e
,
\rho
,
\lambda
.
Next for
0\leq\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

M(\lambda,x)\geq\frac{\lambda^{3}-1}{6}\big(|\rho(x)|+|x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)|\big)-\frac{1}{2}|\rho(x)|\geq-\frac{2}{3}|\rho(x)|-\frac{1}{6}|x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)|,

from which one concludes that

\displaystyle R(\lambda,u)

\displaystyle\geq-e^{2}\left(\frac{2}{3}\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\frac{1}{6}\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\|S_{0}(u)\|_{6}

\displaystyle\geq-4(1-\lambda)^{2}e^{2}\left(\frac{2}{3}\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\frac{1}{6}\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\|S_{0}(u)\|_{6}

(3.24)

\displaystyle\geq-C_{4}(1-\lambda)^{2}e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)\quad\text{for}\ 0\leq\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2}

for some
C_{4}>0
independent of
e
,
\rho
,
\lambda
.
For
\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq T
, we first observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial M}{\partial\lambda}(\lambda,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\big(\rho(x)+x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\big)-\frac{\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)}{2\lambda^{2}}-\frac{x\cdot\nabla\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)}{2\lambda^{3}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}M}{\partial\lambda^{2}}(\lambda,x)

\displaystyle=\lambda\big(\rho(x)+x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\big)+\frac{\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)}{\lambda^{3}}+\frac{2x\cdot\nabla\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)}{\lambda^{4}}+\frac{x\cdot\left(D^{2}\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)x\right)}{2\lambda^{5}}.

Then for fixed

, one finds that

M(1,x)=\frac{\partial M}{\partial\lambda}(1,x)=0
and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}M}{\partial\lambda^{2}}(\lambda,x)

\displaystyle\geq-\lambda\big(|\rho(x)|+|x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)|\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}\left(|\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)|+2|(\lambda^{-1}x)\cdot\nabla\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)|+\frac{1}{2}|(\lambda^{-1}x)\cdot D^{2}\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)(\lambda^{-1}x)|\right)

\displaystyle=:-N(\lambda,x).

By the Taylor theorem, there exists
\xi=\xi(\lambda,x)\in\left(\frac{1}{2},T\right)

such that

M(\lambda,x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}M}{\partial\lambda^{2}}(\xi,x)(1-\lambda)^{2}\geq-\frac{1}{2}N(\xi,x)(1-\lambda)^{2}.

Using the Hölder inequality, we deduce that

R(\lambda,u)\geq-\frac{1}{2}(1-\lambda)^{2}e^{2}\|N(\xi,x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\|S_{0}(u)\|_{6}\quad\text{for}\ \frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq T.

Moreover since
\frac{1}{2}<\xi<T
, we have

\displaystyle\|N(\xi,x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|\xi x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|\xi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+2\|\xi^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\xi^{-\frac{1}{2}}x(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}

\displaystyle\leq T\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)+\sqrt{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+2\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\frac{1}{2}\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right).

Then by Lemma
2.2
, it follows that

(3.25)

R(\lambda,u)\geq-C_{5}(1-\lambda)^{2}e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)\quad\text{for}\ \frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq T,

where
C_{5}>0
is independent of
e
,
\rho
,
\lambda
.
From (
3.23
)-(
3.25
), letting
\beta=\min\{C_{3},C_{4},C_{5}\}>0
,
we find that

(3.26)

\displaystyle R(\lambda,u)

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}-\beta(1-\lambda)^{2}e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)&\text{for}\ 0\leq\lambda\leq T,\\
-\beta\lambda^{3}e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)&\text{for}\ \lambda\geq T.\end{cases}

Step 4
(Conclusion):
Now from (
3.16
), (
3.17
), (
3.18
) and (
3.26
),
we can see that (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) hold.

Finally suppose that
\|u\|_{p+1}\geq\delta
.
If

e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2\beta}\min\left\{\frac{2\omega}{3T},\frac{\alpha\delta^{p-1}}{6(p+1)}\right\},

using (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we obtain

I(u)-I(u_{\lambda})-\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}J(u)\geq\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}\omega}{6}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha\delta^{p-1}}{12(p+1)}(1-\lambda)^{2}\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\ \lambda>0.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Letting

F(\lambda,x):=\frac{1}{\lambda^{4}}\left(\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)+(\lambda^{-1}x)\cdot\nabla\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)\right),

we find that
M(\lambda,x)
can be written as

\displaystyle M(\lambda,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{3}-1}{6}\big(\rho(x)+x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\big)-\frac{\rho(x)}{2}+\frac{\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)}{2\lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\lambda}\frac{s^{2}}{2}\big(\rho(x)+x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\big)\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{1}^{\lambda}\frac{d}{ds}\left(s^{-1}\rho(s^{-1}x)\right)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{1}^{\lambda}s^{2}\big(F(1,x)-F(s,x)\big)\,ds.

Thus if
F(\lambda,x)
is non-increasing with respect to
\lambda
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
it holds that
M(\lambda,x)\geq 0
and hence

R(\lambda,u)\geq 0
for all
\lambda>0
,
which is a same situation to
[
31
]
.
However we cannot expect that
F(\lambda,x)
is non-increasing in
\lambda

for doping profiles.

Indeed by a direct calculation,
F(\lambda,x)
is non-increasing in
\lambda

if
\rho
satisfies

(3.27)

4\rho(x)+6x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)+x\cdot\left(D^{2}\rho(x)x\right)\geq 0\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

When
\rho
is a Gaussian function
e^{-\alpha|x|^{2}}
for
\alpha>0
,
one finds that

2\rho(x)+3x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)+\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\left(D^{2}\rho(x)x\right)=\left(2\alpha^{2}|x|^{4}-7\alpha|x|^{2}+2\right)e^{-\alpha|x|^{2}}.

Hence no matter how we choose
\alpha
,
(
3.27
) fails to hold near the inflection point.
Moreover if we consider
\rho(x)=\frac{1}{1+|x|^{r}}
, we see that

2\rho(x)+3x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)+\frac{1}{2}x\cdot\left(D^{2}\rho(x)x\right)=\frac{1}{2(1+|x|^{r})^{3}}\left\{(r-1)(r-4)|x|^{2r}-(r^{2}+5r-8)|x|^{r}+4\right\}.

Then it follows that

\displaystyle r>4

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad\text{\eqref{eq:3.18} fails to hold near the inflection point},

\displaystyle 1\leq r\leq 4

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad\text{\eqref{eq:3.18} fails to hold for large $|x|$},

\displaystyle 0<r<1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad\rho\notin L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

In this sense, the assumption (
1.5
) and (
3.27
)
seem to be inconsistent.

Now using Lemma
3.7
, we can show the following.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose that
2<p<5
and assume that

e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\leq\rho_{0}

for sufficiently small
\rho_{0}>0
.

Then for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}
,
there exists a unique
\lambda_{u}>0
such that
u_{\lambda_{u}}\in\mathcal{M}
.
Especially for
u\in\mathcal{M}
,

u_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{M}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\lambda_{u}=1.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, we know that there exists
\lambda_{u}>0
,
which is a maximum point of
f(\lambda)
defined in (
3.2
),
such that
u_{\lambda_{u}}\in\mathcal{M}
.
Thus it suffices to show that
f(\lambda)
has a unique critical point for
\lambda>0
.

Now suppose by contradiction that there exist

0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
such that
f^{\prime}(\lambda_{1})=f^{\prime}(\lambda_{2})=0
.
As we have observed in the proof of Lemma
3.1
,
it holds that
J(u_{\lambda})=\lambda f^{\prime}(\lambda)
, which yields that

(3.28)

J(u_{\lambda_{1}})=J(u_{\lambda_{2}})=0.

Since
u_{\lambda_{1}}
,
u_{\lambda_{2}}\in\mathcal{M}
, we can use (
3.3
) to show that

\|u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{p+1}\geq\delta_{1}
and
\|u_{\lambda_{2}}\|_{p+1}\geq\delta_{1}
.
Then we are able to apply (
3.15
) if

e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)
is sufficiently small.

Applying (
3.15
) with
u=u_{\lambda_{1}}
and
\lambda=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}
, we have

I(u_{\lambda_{1}})\geq I(u_{\lambda_{2}})+\frac{\lambda_{1}^{3}-\lambda_{2}^{3}}{3\lambda_{1}^{3}}J(u_{\lambda_{1}})+\frac{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}\omega}{6\lambda_{1}^{2}}\|u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha\delta_{1}^{p-1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}}{12(p+1)\lambda_{1}^{2}}\|u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{p+1}^{2}.

Similarly one gets

I(u_{\lambda_{2}})\geq I(u_{\lambda_{1}})+\frac{\lambda_{2}^{3}-\lambda_{1}^{3}}{3\lambda_{2}^{3}}J(u_{\lambda_{2}})+\frac{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})^{2}\omega}{6\lambda_{2}^{2}}\|u_{\lambda_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha\delta_{1}^{p-1}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})^{2}}{12(p+1)\lambda_{2}^{2}}\|u_{\lambda_{2}}\|_{p+1}^{2}.

Thus from (
3.28
), it follows that

0\geq\frac{\omega}{6}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}\|u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}\|u_{\lambda_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}\right)+\frac{\alpha\delta_{1}^{p-1}}{12(p+1)}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}\|u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{p+1}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}\|u_{\lambda_{2}}\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)>0,

from which arrive at a contradiction and
conclude that
f(\lambda)
has a unique critical point.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

By Proposition
3.6
and Lemma
3.9
,
we can obtain the following minimax characterization of
m
:

m=\inf_{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}}\max_{\lambda>0}I(u_{\lambda}).

4.
Existence of a ground state solution for
2<p<5

In this section, we establish the existence of a ground state solution of (
1.1
).
For this purpose, we define the energy functional
I_{\infty}

associated with (
1.3
) by

I_{\infty}(u):=\frac{1}{2}A(u)+\frac{\omega}{2}B(u)-\frac{1}{p+1}C(u)+e^{2}D(u)\quad\text{for}\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}).

Indeed if
\rho(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
,

I_{\infty}
can be seen as a functional at infinity.
We define the ground state energy corresponding to (
1.3
) by

m_{\infty}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}}I_{\infty}(u),\quad\mathcal{S}_{\infty}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\mid I_{\infty}^{\prime}(u)=0\right\}.

Let us denote by
J_{\infty}
the Nehari-Pohozaev functional for
I_{\infty}
, that is,

J_{\infty}(u):=\frac{3}{2}A(u)+\frac{\omega}{2}B(u)-\frac{2p-1}{p+1}C(u)+3e^{2}D(u).

We also set

\sigma_{\infty}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}}I_{\infty}(u),\quad\mathcal{M}_{\infty}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}\mid J_{\infty}(u)=0\right\}.

Then by the result in
[
4
]

with minor modifications considering
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
,
it follows that
\sigma_{\infty}=m_{\infty}

and
\sigma_{\infty}
is achieved by some
u_{\infty}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}

for
2<p<5
and any
e>0
.
Moreover arguing as Lemma
3.7
(see also
[
31
, Corollary 3.3]
),
we have

(4.1)

I_{\infty}(u)\geq I_{\infty}(u_{\lambda})+\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}J_{\infty}(u)\quad\text{for all }u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \lambda>0,

where
u_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{2}u(\lambda x)
.

To prove the attainability of
\sigma
, we need the following lemma.
Note that this is the only part where we crucially require (
1.6
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
2<p<5
. Assume further (
1.5
) and (
1.6
).
Then it follows that

\sigma<\sigma_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad m<m_{\infty}.

Proof.

First we observe from (
1.6
) that

I(u)<I_{\infty}(u)\quad\text{ for all }u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}

because
E_{1}(u)<0
by (
2.2
).

Now let
u_{\infty}
be a ground state solution for (
1.3
).
By Lemma
3.1
,
there exists
\lambda_{\infty}>0
such that

\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x):=\lambda_{\infty}^{2}u_{\infty}(\lambda_{\infty}x)\in\mathcal{M}
.
Then from (
4.1
) and
J_{\infty}(u_{\infty})=0
, we obtain

\sigma\leq I(\tilde{u}_{\infty})<I_{\infty}(\tilde{u}_{\infty})\leq I_{\infty}(u_{\infty})=\sigma_{\infty},

which ends the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

When we drop (
1.6
), we cannot determine the sign of

E_{1}(u)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{1}(x)|u|^{2}\,dx=-\frac{1}{32\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\rho(y)|u(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy,

causing that the estimate
\sigma<\sigma_{\infty}
is completely nontrivial.
As we will see in the proof of Proposition
4.3
below,
this estimate is a key ingredient of recovering the compactness of minimizing sequences for
\sigma
.

If we could assume that
S_{1}(x)
decays exponentially at infinity,
we still have a possibility to recover the compactness as in
[
2
]
.
Under the assumption (
1.9
), one can show that

S_{1}(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
; see
[
17
, Lemma 2.4]
.
However no matter how fast
\rho(x)
decays at infinity,

S_{1}(x)
cannot decay faster than
\frac{1}{|x|}
,
yielding that the assumption in
[
2
]
is never satisfied.
For the moment, we don’t know whether Lemma
4.1
holds without (
1.6
).

Now we are ready to prove the following result.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
2<p<5
. Assume further (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and

e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot(D^{2}\rho x)\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\leq\rho_{0}

for sufficiently small
\rho_{0}>0
.

Then there exists
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}
such that

I(u_{0})=\sigma\quad\text{and}\quad J(u_{0})=0.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{M}
be a minimizing sequence for
\sigma
, that is,

(4.2)

I(u_{n})=\sigma+o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad J(u_{n})=0.

Then by Lemma
3.3
, it follows that

\sigma+o(1)=I(u_{n})\geq\alpha_{1}\|u\|^{2},

which implies that
\|u_{n}\|
is bounded.
Thus passing to a subsequence,
we may assume that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})

for some
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
.
We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1
: We claim that
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
.

Suppose by contradiction that
u_{0}\equiv 0
so that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
.
Then by Lemma
2.4
, it follows that

E_{1}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{2}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\ n\to\infty.

Since we can write

I(u)=I_{\infty}(u)+2e^{2}E_{1}(u)\quad\text{and}\quad J(u)=J_{\infty}(u)-2e^{2}E_{1}(u)+e^{2}E_{2}(u),

we have from (
4.2
) that

(4.3)

I_{\infty}(u_{n})\rightarrow\sigma\quad\text{and}\quad J_{\infty}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\ n\rightarrow\infty.

Arguing as in Lemma
3.2
, we also deduce that
\|u_{n}\|_{p+1}\geq\delta

for some
\delta>0
independent of
e
,
\rho
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now we apply the concentration compactness principle
[
25
,
34
]

to show that there exist
\tilde{\delta}>0
and
\{y_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that

\int_{B_{1}(y_{n})}|u_{n}(x)|^{p+1}\,dx\geq\tilde{\delta}.

Letting
\hat{u}_{n}(x):=u_{n}(x+y_{n})
, we have

\|\hat{u}_{n}\|=\|u_{n}\|\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\hat{u}_{n}(x)|^{p+1}\,dx\geq\tilde{\delta}.

Especially, there exists
\hat{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}
such that

\hat{u}_{n}\rightharpoonup\hat{u}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
.
Moreover from (
4.3
), one also finds that

(4.4)

I_{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})=I_{\infty}(u_{n})=\sigma+o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad J_{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})=J_{\infty}(u_{n})=o(1).

Let us put
v_{n}:=\hat{u}_{n}-\hat{u}
.
Then by the Brezis-Lieb lemma
[
11
]
and Lemma
2.3
, it follows that

I_{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})=I_{\infty}(\hat{u})+I_{\infty}(v_{n})+o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad J_{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})=J_{\infty}(\hat{u})+J_{\infty}(v_{n})+o(1).

We also define

K_{\infty}(u):=I_{\infty}(u)-\frac{1}{3}J_{\infty}(u)=\frac{\omega}{3}B(u)+\frac{2(p-2)}{3(p-1)}C(u).

Then from (
4.4
), one finds that

(4.5)

K_{\infty}(v_{n})=\sigma-K_{\infty}(\hat{u})+o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad J_{\infty}(v_{n})=-J_{\infty}(\hat{u})+o(1).

If there exists a subsequence
\{v_{n_{j}}\}\subset\{v_{n}\}
such that

v_{n_{j}}=0
, then passing to a limit along this subsequence,
it holds from (
4.5
) that

K_{\infty}(\hat{u})=\sigma
and
J_{\infty}(\hat{u})=0
.
This implies that

\sigma_{\infty}\leq I_{\infty}(\hat{u})=K_{\infty}(\hat{u})+\frac{1}{3}J_{\infty}(\hat{u})=\sigma,

which is absurd by Lemma
4.1
.
Thus we may assume that
v_{n}\neq 0
.
Arguing as Lemma
3.9

(see also
[
28
, Lemma 3.3]
,
[
31
, Lemma 2.4]
),
there exists a unique
\hat{\lambda}_{n}>0
such that

\hat{\phi}_{n}(x):=(\hat{\lambda}_{n})^{2}v_{n}(\hat{\lambda}_{n}x)\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
.

Next we prove that
J_{\infty}(\hat{u})\leq 0
.
Suppose by contradiction that
J_{\infty}(\hat{u})>0
.
Then from (
4.5
), it follows that
J_{\infty}(v_{n})\leq 0

and hence
\|v_{n}\|_{p+1}\geq\delta_{1}
by Lemma
3.2
.
Using (
3.15
) with
\rho\equiv 0
and (
4.5
), one deduces that

\displaystyle\sigma-K_{\infty}(\hat{u})+o(1)=K_{\infty}(v_{n})

\displaystyle=I_{\infty}(v_{n})-\frac{1}{3}J_{\infty}(v_{n})

\displaystyle\geq I_{\infty}(\hat{\phi}_{n})-\frac{(\hat{\lambda}_{n})^{3}}{3}J_{\infty}(v_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{\infty}-\frac{(\hat{\lambda}_{n})^{3}}{3}J_{\infty}(v_{n})\geq\sigma_{\infty},

which yields that

\sigma_{\infty}\leq\sigma-K_{\infty}(\hat{u})<\sigma_{\infty}-K_{\infty}(\hat{u})\quad\text{and thus}\quad K_{\infty}(\hat{u})<0.

This is a contradiction to the fact
K_{\infty}(u)>0

for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}

and hence
J_{\infty}(\hat{u})\leq 0
.

Now by Lemma
3.9
, there exists a unique
\hat{\lambda}>0
such that

\hat{\phi}(x):=(\hat{\lambda})^{2}\hat{u}(\hat{\lambda}x)\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
.
Then from (
3.15
) with
\rho\equiv 0
, (
4.4
)
and by the Fatou lemma, we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\{I_{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})-\frac{1}{3}J_{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})\right\}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}K_{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})\geq K_{\infty}(\hat{u})

\displaystyle=I_{\infty}(\hat{u})-\frac{1}{3}J_{\infty}(\hat{u})

\displaystyle\geq I_{\infty}(\hat{\phi})-\frac{(\hat{\lambda})^{3}}{3}J_{\infty}(\hat{u})

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{\infty}-\frac{(\hat{\lambda})^{3}}{3}J_{\infty}(\hat{u})\geq\sigma_{\infty}.

This is a contradiction to Lemma
4.1
and hence
we conclude that
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
.

Step 2
: We prove that
I(u_{0})=\sigma
and
J(u_{0})=0
.

Let us define
w_{n}:=u_{n}-u_{0}
.
By Lemma
2.3
, Lemma
2.4
and the Brezis-Lieb lemma, we have

I(u_{n})=I(u_{0})+I(w_{n})+o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad J(u_{n})=J(u_{0})+J(w_{n})+o(1).

We also put

\displaystyle K(u)

\displaystyle:=I(u)-\frac{1}{3}J(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega}{3}B(u)+\frac{2(p-2)}{3(p+1)}C(u)+\frac{8}{3}e^{2}E_{1}(u)-\frac{1}{3}e^{2}E_{2}(u)\quad\text{for}\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}).

Then from (
4.2
), it holds that

(4.6)

K(w_{n})=\sigma-K(u_{0})+o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad J(w_{n})=-J(u_{0})+o(1).

If there exists a subsequence
\{w_{n_{j}}\}\subset\{w_{n}\}
such that

w_{n_{j}}=0
, then passing to a limit along this subsequence,
we arrive at
K(u_{0})=\sigma
and
J(u_{0})=0
.
This implies that
I(u_{0})=\sigma
and hence we conclude.
Thus we may assume that
w_{n}\neq 0
.

Next we show that
J(u_{0})\leq 0
.
Indeed if
J(u_{0})>0
, it follows from (
4.6
) that

J(w_{n})\leq 0
and hence
\|w_{n}\|_{p+1}\geq\delta_{1}
by Lemma
3.2
.
Then by Lemma
3.9
, there exists a unique
\lambda_{n}>0
such that

\phi_{n}(x):=\lambda_{n}^{2}w_{n}(\lambda_{n}x)\in\mathcal{M}
.
Using (
3.15
) and (
4.6
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\sigma-K(u_{0})+o(1)=K(w_{n})

\displaystyle=I(w_{n})-\frac{1}{3}J(w_{n})

\displaystyle\geq I(\phi_{n})-\frac{\lambda_{n}^{3}}{3}J(w_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\sigma-\frac{\lambda_{n}^{3}}{3}J(w_{n})\geq\sigma,

yielding that
K(u_{0})\leq 0
.
However this is a contradiction because
K(u)>0

for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}
by Lemma
2.2

provided that
e^{2}(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}})

is sufficiently small,
from which we conclude that
J(u_{0})\leq 0
.

Now using Lemma
3.9
again,
there exists a unique
\lambda_{0}>0
such that
\phi_{0}(x):=\lambda_{0}^{2}u_{0}(\lambda_{0}x)\in\mathcal{M}
.
Moreover since
J(u_{0})\leq 0
, we are able to use (
3.15
) by Lemma
3.2
.
Then from (
4.2
), Lemma
2.4
and by the Fatou lemma,
one finds that

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{I(u_{n})-\frac{1}{3}J(u_{n})\right\}=\lim_{n\to\infty}K(u_{n})\geq K(u_{0})

\displaystyle=I(u_{0})-\frac{1}{3}J(u_{0})

\displaystyle\geq I(\phi_{0})-\frac{\lambda_{0}^{3}}{3}J(u_{0})

(4.7)

\displaystyle\geq\sigma-\frac{\lambda_{0}^{3}}{3}J(u_{0})\geq\sigma,

from which we get
K(u_{0})=\sigma
.
Moreover if

, (

) leads a contradiction.
Thus it follows that

and hence

.
This completes the proof.

Remark 4.4
.

As we have observed in the proof of Proposition

,

\displaystyle K(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega}{3}B(u)+\frac{2(p-2)}{3(p+1)}C(u)+\frac{8}{3}e^{2}E_{1}(u)-\frac{1}{3}e^{2}E_{2}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{\frac{\omega}{3}|u(x)|^{2}+\frac{2(p-2)}{3(p+1)}|u(x)|^{p+1}-\frac{e^{2}}{6}S_{0}(u)(x)\big(\rho(x)+x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\big)\right\}\,dx

However we don’t know whether

,
although one knows that
K(u_{n})\geq 0

if
e^{2}(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}})

is small by Lemma
2.2
.

If
\rho
satisfies

(4.8)

\rho(x)+x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)\leq 0\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

it follows that
\rho_{n}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
However the condition (
4.8
) seems to be inconsistent with
(
1.5
) and (
1.6
).
For example, if we consider the Gaussian function

\rho(x)=e^{-\alpha|x|^{2}}
for
\alpha>0
, we have

\rho(x)+x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)=\left(1-2\alpha|x|^{2}\right)e^{-\alpha|x|^{2}}.

Thus no matter how we choose
\alpha
, (
4.8
) fails to hold near the origin.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Proposition
3.6
and Proposition
4.3
,
there exists
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}
such that

(4.9)

I(u_{0})=\sigma=m\quad\text{and}\quad J(u_{0})=0,

namely,
u_{0}
is a ground state solution of (
1.1
).
We claim that

u_{0}
can be assumed to be real-valued up to phase shift.

For this purpose, we argue as in
[
1
]
and show that

(4.10)

\big|\nabla|u_{0}|\big|=|\nabla u_{0}|\quad\text{a.e. in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.

Indeed suppose by contradiction that

\mathcal{L}\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid\big|\nabla|u_{0}(x)|\big|<|\nabla u_{0}(x)|\right\}\right)>0
,
where
\mathcal{L}(A)
is the Lebesgue measure for the set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Then it follows that

(4.11)

\sigma=I(u_{0})>I(|u_{0}|),\quad 0=J(u_{0})>J(|u_{0}|)\quad\text{and}\quad K(u_{0})>K(|u_{0}|).

Moreover by Lemma
3.9
, there exists a unique
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

v_{0}(x):=\lambda_{0}^{2}|u_{0}|(\lambda_{0}x)\in\mathcal{M}
.
Then from (
3.15
), (
4.9
) and (
4.11
), we find that

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=I(u_{0})-\frac{1}{3}J(u_{0})=K(u_{0})>K(|u_{0}|)

\displaystyle=I(|u_{0}|)-\frac{1}{3}J(|u_{0}|)

\displaystyle\geq I(v_{0})-\frac{\lambda_{0}^{3}}{3}J(|u_{0}|)>I(v_{0})\geq\sigma,

which is a contradiction and hence (
4.10
) holds.

Now using the polar form, we can write

u_{0}(x)=|u_{0}(x)|e^{i\eta(x)}
.
Then a direct calculation shows that

|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}=\big|\nabla|u_{0}|\big|^{2}+|u_{0}|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2},

yielding that
\nabla\eta\equiv 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
from (
4.10
).
This implies that
u_{0}(x)=e^{i\theta}|u_{0}(x)|
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}

and hence
u_{0}
can be assumed to be real-valued
up to phase shift.
∎

5.
Relation between action GSS and energy GSS

In this section, we investigate the relation between
action ground state solutions and
energy ground state solutions of (
1.1
).

For given
\mu>0
, let
u_{\mu}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
be an energy ground state solution
of (
1.1
), that is,

\displaystyle E(u_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\inf_{B(\mu)}E(u),\quad B_{\mu}=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\mid\|u\|_{2}^{2}=\mu\right\},

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}A(u)-\frac{1}{p+1}C(u)+e^{2}D(u)+2e^{2}E_{1}(u).

By the result in
[
17
]
, if
(
1.9
)-(
1.10
) is satisfied,

2<p<\frac{7}{3}
,
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
and

e^{2}\left(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\right)\leq\rho_{0}
for sufficiently small
\rho_{0}>0
,

u_{\mu}
exists and the corresponding Lagrange multiplier

\omega=\omega_{\mu}
is positive.
Here
\mu^{*}
is the threshold value for
c_{\infty}(\mu)
defined in (
1.8
).
Especially
u_{\mu}
is a nontrivial solution of (
1.1
) with

\omega=\omega_{\mu}
.

To clarify the dependence with respect to
\omega
, we write

I=I_{\omega}
,
J=J_{\omega}
,
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{\omega}
and
m=m_{\omega}
.
Under these preparations, we have the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
be given
and suppose that
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
.
Under the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
and (
1.9
),
the energy ground state solution
u_{\mu}
is
an action ground state solution of (
1.1
) with
\omega=\omega_{\mu}
.

Proof.

Since
u_{\mu}
is a nontrivial critical point of
I_{\omega_{\mu}}
, we have

(5.1)

u_{\mu}\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega_{\mu}}\quad\text{and}\quad m_{\omega_{\mu}}\leq I_{\omega_{\mu}}(u_{\mu}).

Thus it suffices to show that
I_{\omega_{\mu}}(u_{\mu})\leq m_{\omega_{\mu}}
.

Now let
w_{\mu}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}
be an action ground state solution
of (
1.1
) with
\omega=\omega_{\mu}
,
which exists by Theorem
1.1
.
Then by (
3.15
) and
J_{\omega_{\mu}}(w_{\mu})=0
, it follows that

(5.2)

I_{\omega_{\mu}}(w_{\mu})-I_{\omega_{\mu}}\left((w_{\mu})_{\lambda}\right)\geq\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}J_{\omega_{\mu}}(w_{\mu})=0\quad\text{for all}\ \ \lambda>0,

where
(w_{\mu})_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}w_{\mu}(\lambda x)
.
Taking
\lambda_{\mu}:=\frac{\|u_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|w_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}}
, one has

\left\|(w_{\mu})_{\lambda_{\mu}}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\|u_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{and hence}\quad E(u_{\mu})\leq E\left((w_{\mu})_{\lambda_{\mu}}\right).

Thus from (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle m_{\omega_{\mu}}\leq I_{\omega_{\mu}}(u_{\mu})

\displaystyle=E(u_{\mu})+\frac{\omega_{\mu}}{2}\|u_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq E\left((w_{\mu})_{\lambda_{\mu}}\right)+\frac{\omega_{\mu}}{2}\left\|(w_{\mu})_{\lambda_{\mu}}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=I_{\omega_{\mu}}\left((w_{\mu})_{\lambda_{\mu}}\right)\leq I_{\omega_{\mu}}(w_{\mu})=m_{\omega_{\mu}},

from which we conclude.
∎

In order to consider the converse, let
\Omega(\mu)
be the set of
Lagrange multipliers associated with energy ground state solutions for
B_{\mu}
,
namely

\displaystyle\Omega(\mu):=\left\{\right.\omega_{\mu}>0\mid

\omega_{\mu}
is the Lagrange multiplier
associated with an energy ground state

\displaystyle\hbox{of \eqref{eq:1.1} under the constraint $B_{\mu}$}\left.\right\}.

By the result in
[
17
]
, we know that
\Omega(\mu)\neq\emptyset

for every
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
, when
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
and

e^{2}(\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}+\|x\cdot\nabla\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}})
is small.
Moreover for any
\omega_{\mu}\in\Omega(\mu)
,
there exists an action ground state solution
w_{\mu}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}

of (
1.1
) with
\omega=\omega_{\mu}
by Theorem
1.1
.

In this setting, the following result holds.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
be given
and suppose that
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
.
Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
and (
1.9
),

w_{\mu}
is an energy ground state solution of (
1.1
)
under the constraint
B_{\mu}
.

Proof.

Since
w_{\mu}
is an action ground state solution of (
1.1
)
with
\omega=\omega_{\mu}
, by using (
3.15
), we have
J_{\omega_{\mu}}(w_{\omega_{\mu}})=0
and

(5.3)

I_{\omega_{\mu}}(w_{\mu})-I_{\omega_{\mu}}\left((w_{\mu})_{\lambda}\right)\geq\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}\omega_{\mu}}{6}\|w_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\ \ \lambda>0.

Now let
\tilde{u}_{\mu}
be an energy ground state solution of (
1.1
)
under the constraint
B_{\mu}
whose Lagrange multiplier coincides with
\omega_{\mu}
.
Then if follows that

m_{\omega_{\mu}}\leq I_{\omega_{\mu}}(\tilde{u}_{\mu})\quad\text{and}\quad E(\tilde{u}_{\mu})\leq E(u)\quad\text{for any}\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})\ \text{with}\ \|u\|_{2}^{2}=\mu.

Especially choosing
\tilde{\lambda}_{\mu}=\frac{\mu}{\|w_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}}
, we have

(5.4)

\left\|(w_{\mu})_{\tilde{\lambda}_{\mu}}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\mu=\|\tilde{u}_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad E(\tilde{u}_{\mu})\leq E\left((w_{\mu})_{\tilde{\lambda}_{\mu}}\right).

Then from (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we deduce that

\displaystyle m_{\omega_{\mu}}\leq I_{\omega_{\mu}}(\tilde{u}_{\mu})

\displaystyle=E(\tilde{u}_{\mu})+\frac{\omega_{\mu}}{2}\|\tilde{u}_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq E\left((w_{\mu})_{\tilde{\lambda}_{\mu}}\right)+\frac{\omega_{\mu}}{2}\left\|(w_{\mu})_{\tilde{\lambda}_{\mu}}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=I_{\omega_{\mu}}\left((w_{\mu})_{\tilde{\lambda}_{\mu}}\right)\leq I_{\omega_{\mu}}(w_{\mu})=m_{\omega_{\mu}},

which yields that
I_{\omega_{\mu}}\left((w_{\mu})_{\tilde{\lambda}_{\mu}}\right)=I_{\omega_{\mu}}(w_{\mu})
.
Going back to (
5.3
), one finds that

0\geq\frac{(1-\tilde{\lambda}_{\mu})^{2}\omega_{\mu}}{6}\|w_{\mu}\|_{2}^{2}\geq 0,

from which we conclude that
\tilde{\lambda}_{\mu}=1
and hence

E(w_{\mu})=E(\tilde{u}_{\mu})=\inf_{B_{\mu}}E(u).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

(i)
By Proposition
5.2
, if
\omega_{\mu}\in\Omega(\mu)
,
every action ground state solution of (
1.1
) with
\omega=\omega_{\mu}

share the same
L^{2}
-norm.

(ii)
Although
\Omega(\mu)\neq\emptyset
for every
\mu>2\cdot 2^{\frac{1}{2p-4}}\mu^{*}
,
we don’t know whether
\Omega(\mu)
is an interval.
Moreover we don’t know for given
\omega_{\mu}\in\Omega(\mu)
,
there exists a unique
\mu>0
such that
the corresponding Lagrange multiplier coincides with
\omega_{\mu}
.
In other words, it is not known if

(5.5)

\mu\mapsto\omega_{\mu}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\ell)\ \text{is one to one mapping for some}\ \ell>0.

(
5.5
) is important in investigating further properties of ground state solutions,
such as uniqueness and stability.
However (
5.5
) is known only in limited situations;
see
[
14
,
17
,
20
]
.
In particular, it is not known whether (
5.5
) holds true
even when
\rho\equiv 0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The claim follows by Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
.
∎

6.
Existence of a radial ground state solution for
1<p<2

In this section, we consider the case
1<p<2
,
for which arguments in sections 3-5 do not work well.
Especially we cannot handle the energy inequality (
3.15
) in Lemma
3.7

because the coefficient in front of
\|u\|_{p+1}^{p+1}
becomes negative
when
1<p<2
.

In the case
1<p<2
, we only have the following weak result.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose that
1<p<2
.
Assume
\rho\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\rho(x)=\rho(|x|)
.
There exists
e_{0}>0
such that if
0<e\leq e_{0}
,
(
1.1
) has a radial ground state solution
u_{0}
, that is,
u_{0}
satisfies

I(u_{0})=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{rad}}I(u),\quad\mathcal{S}_{rad}=\{u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\mid I^{\prime}(u)=0\},

where
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})\mid u(x)=u(|x|)\}
.

To this aim, let us recall the functional
I_{\infty}
for
\rho\equiv 0
,
which was defined by

I_{\infty}(u):=\frac{1}{2}A(u)+\frac{\omega}{2}B(u)-\frac{1}{p+1}C(u)+e^{2}D(u)\quad\text{for}\ \ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}).

When
1<p<2
, the following properties are known;
see
[
28
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that
1<p<2
and
e>0
. Then it holds:

(i)

\displaystyle\inf_{H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}I_{\infty}(u)>-\infty
,

(ii)

I_{\infty}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition on
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Now by Lemma
2.2
, one finds that

I(u)=I_{\infty}(u)+e^{2}E_{1}(u)\geq I_{\infty}(u)-Ce^{2}\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\|u\|^{2}\quad\text{for any}\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}),

from which we conclude that
\displaystyle\inf_{H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}I(u)>-\infty
.
Moreover since
E_{1}
is a compact operator by Lemma
2.4
,

I
also satisfies the Palais-Smale condition on
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let us define the energy functional
I_{0}
for
e=0
:

I_{0}(u)=\frac{1}{2}A(u)+\frac{\omega}{2}B(u)-\frac{1}{p+1}C(u)\quad\text{for}\ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}).

It is standard to show that there exists
\tilde{u}\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

such that
I_{0}(\tilde{u})<0
.
Then by Lemma
2.2
, one has

\displaystyle I(\tilde{u})

\displaystyle=I_{0}(\tilde{u})+e^{2}D(\tilde{u})+e^{2}E_{1}(\tilde{u})

\displaystyle\leq I_{0}(\tilde{u})+Ce^{2}\|\tilde{u}\|^{4}+Ce^{2}\|\rho\|_{\frac{6}{5}}\|\tilde{u}\|^{2}.

Choosing a small
e_{0}>0
, we have
I(\tilde{u})<0
for
0<e\leq e_{0}

and hence
\displaystyle\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}I(u)<0
.

By Proposition
6.2
, we are able to apply Ekeland’s variational principle.
Then there exists
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\{0\}
such that

I(u_{0})=\min_{u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}I(u)<0.

Particularly,
u_{0}
is a nontrivial solution of (
1.1
).
Moreover since
u_{0}
is a global minimizer on
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it follows that

I(u_{0})\geq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{rad}}I(u)\geq\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}I(u)=I(u_{0}),

yielding that
u_{0}
is a radial ground state solution of (
1.1
).
∎

Remark 6.3
.

(i)

In
[
17
]
, we have shown that (
1.1
) has an energy ground state solution
if
1<p<2
and
e
is sufficiently small.
Unfortunately unlike the case
2<p<\frac{7}{3}
,
we cannot say anything about the relation
between the radial ground state solution obtained in Theorem
6.1

and the energy ground state solution in
[
17
]
.

(ii)
In the case
p=2
, it remains open whether (
1.1
) has
a nontrivial solution for any
\omega>0
and small
e>0
.
Moreover in
[
14
]
, thanks to the scaling invariance of (
1.3
) when
p=2
,
it was proved that an energy ground state solution of (
1.3
)
is an action ground state solution and vise versa.
However due to the loss of scaling invariance for
\rho\not\equiv 0
,
we don’t know if an energy ground state solution of (
1.1
)
obtained in
[
17
]
is an action ground state of (
1.1
).

7.
The case
\rho
is a characteristic function

In this section, we consider the case where the doping profile
\rho

is a characteristic function,
which appears frequently in physical literatures
[
23
,
27
,
29
]
.
More precisely, let
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be
disjoint bounded open sets with smooth boundary.
For
\alpha_{i}>0

(i=1,\cdots,m)
,
we assume that the doping profile
\rho
has the form:

(7.1)

\rho(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\chi_{\Omega_{i}}(x),\quad\chi_{\Omega_{i}}(x)=\begin{cases}1&(x\in\Omega_{i}),\\
0&(x\notin\Omega_{i}).\end{cases}

In this case,
\rho
cannot be weakly differentiable so that the assumption
(
1.5
) does not make sense.
Even so, we are able to obtain the existence of ground state solutions
by imposing some smallness condition related with
\alpha_{i}
and
\Omega_{i}
.

To state our main result for this case,
let us put
\displaystyle L:=\sup_{x\in\partial\Omega}|x|<\infty
.
A key is the following
sharp boundary trace inequality

due to
[
3
, Theorem 6.1]
,
which we present here according to the form used in this paper.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
and
\gamma:H^{1}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
be
the trace operator.
Then it holds that

\int_{\partial\Omega}|\gamma(u)|^{2}\,dS\leq\kappa_{1}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,dx+\kappa_{2}(\Omega)\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\hbox{for any}\ u\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where
\kappa_{1}(\Omega)=\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{|\Omega|}
,

\kappa_{2}(\Omega)=\big\||\nabla w|\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}

and
w
is a unique solution of the torsion problem:

\Delta w=\kappa_{1}(\Omega)\ \hbox{in}\ \Omega,\quad\frac{\partial w}{\partial n}=1\ \hbox{on}\ \partial\Omega.

In relation to the size of
\rho
, we define

D(\Omega):=L|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{6}}|\left(L\|H\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+|\partial\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\kappa_{1}(\Omega)|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{3}}+\kappa_{2}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
H
is the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
.

Remark 7.2
.

It is known that
\kappa_{2}(\Omega)\geq 1
; see
[
3
]
.
Then by the isoperimetric inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

|\partial\Omega|\geq 3|\Omega|^{\frac{2}{3}}|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{3}},

and the fact
|\Omega|\leq|B_{L}(0)|=L^{3}|B_{1}|
, we find that

(7.2)

D(\Omega)\geq\left(\frac{|\Omega|}{|B_{1}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{6}}\cdot\sqrt{3}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{3}}|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(3|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{3}}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=C|\Omega|^{\frac{5}{6}}=C\|\chi_{\Omega}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\Omega
.

Under these preparations, we have the following result.

Theorem 7.3
.

Suppose that
2<p<5
and assume (
7.1
).
There exists
\rho_{0}>0
such that if

e^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\leq\rho_{0},

then (
1.1
) has a ground state solution
u_{0}
.
Moreover the statement of Theorem
1.2
holds true.

Note that when
1<p<2
, we have only assumed that

\rho(x)\in L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\rho(x)=\rho(|x|)
in Theorem
6.1
,
which covers the case (
7.1
).

We mention that the first part
x\cdot\nabla\rho(x)

and
x\cdot(D^{2}\rho(x)x)
appeared
was the definition of
E_{2}(u)
and
E_{3}(u)
in (
2.5
).
Under the assumption (
7.1
), we replace them by

\displaystyle E_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\int_{\Omega_{i}}S_{0}(u)\,dx,

\displaystyle E_{2}(u)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot n_{i}\,dS_{i},

\displaystyle E_{3}(u)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}H_{i}(x)S_{0}(u)(x\cdot n_{i})^{2}\,dS_{i},

where
n_{i}
is the unit outward normal on
\partial\Omega_{i}
.
Indeed we have the following.

Lemma 7.4
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{0}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=-10E_{1}(u)+E_{2}(u),

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{1}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=-6E_{2}(u)-E_{3}(u).

Proof.

For simplicity, let us consider the case
m=1
and
\alpha=1
.
First by the divergence theorem and the fact
S_{0}(u)|u|^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
one finds that

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{0}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{u(y)y\cdot\nabla\overline{u(y)}}{|x-y|}\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}\operatorname{div}_{y}y}{|x-y|}\,dy\,dx+\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u(y)|^{2}y\cdot\nabla_{y}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{16\pi}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dy\,dx+\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u(y)|^{2}\frac{y\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{3}}\,dy\,dx.

Using the identity
y\cdot(x-y)=-|x-y|^{2}+x\cdot(x-y)
,
the Fubini theorem and the divergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{0}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dy\,dx-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}|u(y)|^{2}x\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dy\,dx-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}_{x}\left(\frac{|u(y)|^{2}x}{|x-y|}\right)\,dx\,dy+\frac{3}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{16\pi}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\,dy\,dx-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|}x\cdot n\,dS\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{2}\int_{\Omega}S_{0}(u)\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)x\cdot n\,dS=-10E_{1}(u)+E_{2}(u).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{1}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{u(y)y\cdot\nabla\overline{u(y)}}{|x-y|}x\cdot n\,dy\,dS

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}x\cdot n}{|x-y|}\,dy\,dS-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\partial\Omega}|u(y)|^{2}x\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)x\cdot n\,dS\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}x\cdot n}{|x-y|}\,dy\,dS-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\operatorname{div}_{x}\left(\frac{|u(y)|^{2}(x\cdot n)x}{|x-y|}\right)\,dS\,dy

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}x\cdot n}{|x-y|}\,dS\,dy+\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}x\cdot\nabla_{x}(x\cdot n)}{|x-y|}\,dS\,dy

(7.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)x\cdot n\,dS-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\operatorname{div}_{x}\big(S_{0}(u)(x\cdot n)x\big)\,dS+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)x\cdot\nabla_{x}(x\cdot n)\,dS.

Applying the surface divergence theorem (see e.g.
[
30
, 7.6]
)
and noticing that
\partial(\partial\Omega)=\emptyset
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\operatorname{div}_{x}(S_{0}(u)(x\cdot n)x)\,dS

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial\Omega}\big(S_{0}(u)(x\cdot n)x\big)^{\perp}\cdot\vec{H}\,dS=-\int_{\partial\Omega}H(x)S_{0}(u)(x\cdot n)^{2}\,dS,

where
x^{\perp}
is the normal component of
x
and

\vec{H}
is the mean curvature vector
\vec{H}=Hn
.
Finally since
x\cdot\nabla_{x}(x\cdot n)=x\cdot n
,
we deduce from (
7.3
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(0)}S_{1}(u)ux\cdot\nabla\bar{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=3\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)x\cdot n\,dS+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}H(x)S_{0}(u)(x\cdot n)^{2}\,dS=-6E_{2}(u)-E_{3}(u),

which ends the proof.
The general case can be shown by summing up the integrals.
∎

By Lemma
7.4
, the Pohozev identity can be reformulated as follows.

Lemma 7.5
.

Under the assumption (
7.1
),
the functionals
P(u)
defined in (
2.10
) and
Q(u)
in (
3.7
)
have the same form.

Proof.

As we have mentioned in the proof of Lemma
2.5
,
the Pohozaev identity can be shown by multiplying
x\cdot\nabla\bar{u}

and
ex\cdot S_{0}(u)
by (
1.1
),
integrating them over
B_{R}(0)
and passing to a limit
R\to\infty
.
Then we are able to obtain (
2.10
) and (
3.7
) by Lemma
7.4
.
∎

Next we establish estimates for
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
.

Lemma 7.6
.

For any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C})
,
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
satisfy the estimates:

\displaystyle|E_{1}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}|\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{5}{6}}\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{2},

\displaystyle|E_{2}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{2},

\displaystyle|E_{3}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{2},

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
\Omega_{i}
.

Proof.

First we observe that

|E_{1}(u)|\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\int_{\Omega_{i}}|S_{0}(u)|\,dx\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\left(\int_{\Omega_{i}}|S_{0}(u)|^{6}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{5}{6}},

from which the estimate for
E_{1}
can be obtained by the Sobolev inequality.
Next by Proposition
7.1
,
the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev inequality, one has

\displaystyle|E_{2}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}|S_{0}(u)||x|\,dS_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}|S_{0}(u)|^{2}\,dS_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}|x|^{2}\,dS_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}L_{i}|\partial\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\kappa_{1}(\Omega_{i})\|S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\Omega_{i})\|S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}L_{i}|\partial\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\kappa_{1}(\Omega_{i})|\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{2}{3}}\|S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\kappa_{2}(\Omega_{i})|\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{1}{3}}\|S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}L_{i}|\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{1}{6}}|\partial\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\kappa_{1}(\Omega_{i})|\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{1}{3}}+\kappa_{2}(\Omega_{i})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle|E_{3}(u)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}|H_{i}||S_{0}(u)||x|^{2}\,dS_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{2}\|H_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{i})}\|S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{2}\|H_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{i})}|\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{1}{6}}\left(\kappa_{1}(\Omega_{i})|\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{1}{3}}+\kappa_{2}(\Omega_{i})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Our next step is to modify the proof of the energy inequality in Lemma
3.7
.
For this purpose, we prove the following.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
and put

\Omega(\lambda):=\int_{\lambda\Omega}S_{0}(u)(x)\,dx=\lambda^{3}\int_{\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)\,dy.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)\,dS,

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-2\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)\,dS-\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}H(y)S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)^{2}\,dS.

Proof.

First we observe that

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=3\lambda^{2}\int_{\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)\,dy+\lambda^{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{y}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)\cdot y\,dy

\displaystyle=3\lambda^{2}\int_{\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)\,dy+\lambda^{2}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}_{y}\big(S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)y\big)\,dy-\lambda^{2}\int_{\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)\operatorname{div}_{y}y\,dy

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)\,dS.

Similarly by the surface divergence theorem, one has

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=2\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)\,dS+\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla_{y}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)\cdot y(y\cdot n)\,dS

\displaystyle=2\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)\,dS

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}\operatorname{div}_{y}\big(S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)y\big)\,dS-\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)\operatorname{div}_{y}\big((y\cdot n)y\big)\,dS

\displaystyle=2\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)\,dS-\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}H(y)S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)^{2}\,dS

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)\operatorname{div}_{y}y(y\cdot n)\,dS-\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)y\cdot\nabla_{y}(y\cdot n)\,dS

\displaystyle=-2\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)\,dS-\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}H(y)S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)(y\cdot n)^{2}\,dS.

This completes the proof.
∎

Using Lemma
7.6

,
we can establish the energy identity as follows.

Lemma 7.9
.

Suppose that
2<p<5
and let
u_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{2}u(\lambda x)
for
\lambda>0
.
Under the assumption (
7.1
),
there exist
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
,
\rho_{0}>0

independent of
e
,
\rho
,
\lambda
such that
if
\|u\|_{p+1}\geq\delta
for some
\delta>0
independent of
e
,
\rho

and
\displaystyle e^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\leq\rho_{0}
,
then the following estimate holds.

I(u)-I(u_{\lambda})-\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}J(u)\geq\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}\omega}{6}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha\delta^{p-1}}{12(p+1)}(1-\lambda)^{2}\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\ \lambda>0.

Proof.

Under the notation of Lemma
7.7
,
we can write the remainder term
R(\lambda,u)
as follows:

\displaystyle R(\lambda,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{8-2\lambda^{3}}{3}e^{2}E_{1}(u)-\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{3}e^{2}E_{2}(u)+\frac{e^{2}\lambda^{-1}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{0}(u)\rho(\lambda^{-1}x)\,dx

\displaystyle=e^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\left\{-\frac{4-\lambda^{3}}{6}\int_{\Omega_{i}}S_{0}(u)\,dx+\frac{1-\lambda^{3}}{6}\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}S_{0}(u)x\cdot n\,dS_{i}+\frac{\lambda^{-1}}{2}\int_{\lambda\Omega_{i}}S_{0}(u)\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle=e^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\left\{\frac{\lambda^{3}-1}{6}\left(\Omega_{i}(1)-\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(1)\right)-\frac{\Omega_{i}(1)}{2}+\frac{\Omega_{i}(\lambda)}{2\lambda}\right\}.

Let
T\geq 4
be chosen so that
T^{2p-4}\geq 3
and put

G(\lambda):=\frac{\lambda^{3}-1}{6}\left(\Omega_{i}(1)-\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(1)\right)-\frac{\Omega_{i}(1)}{2}+\frac{\Omega_{i}(\lambda)}{2\lambda}.

For
\lambda\geq T
, it follows that

G(\lambda)\geq-\frac{\lambda^{3}-1}{6}\left(|\Omega_{i}(1)|+|\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(1)|\right)-\frac{1}{2}|\Omega_{i}(1)|\geq-\lambda^{3}\left(\frac{2}{3}|\Omega_{i}(1)|+\frac{1}{6}|\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(1)|\right).

Similarly one has

G(\lambda)\geq-\left(\frac{2}{3}|\Omega_{i}(1)|+\frac{1}{6}|\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(1)|\right)\geq-4(1-\lambda)^{2}\left(\frac{2}{3}|\Omega_{i}(1)|+\frac{1}{6}|\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(1)|\right)\quad\text{for}\ 0\leq\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2}.

When
\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq T
, we see that
G(1)=G^{\prime}(1)=0
and

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\Omega_{i}(1)-\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(1)\right)+\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}\left(\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)-\lambda\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\Omega_{i}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle\geq-\lambda\left(|\Omega_{i}(1)|+|\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(1)|\right)-\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}\left(|\Omega_{i}(\lambda)|+\lambda|\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)|+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}|\Omega_{i}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)|\right)=:-\tilde{N}(\lambda).

Then by the Taylor theorem,
there exists
\xi=\xi(\lambda)\in\left(\frac{1}{2},T\right)
such that

G(\lambda)\geq-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{N}(\xi)(1-\lambda)^{2}\quad\text{for}\ \frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq T.

Now by Proposition
7.1
and Lemma
7.7
,
arguing similarly as Lemma
7.6
, one finds that

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega_{i}(\lambda)|}{\lambda^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{i}}|S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)|\,dy\leq|\Omega_{i}|^{\frac{5}{6}}\|S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}D(\Omega_{i})\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)|}{\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}|S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)||y|\,dS\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}D(\Omega_{i})\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega_{i}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)|}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}|S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)||y|\,dS+\int_{\partial\Omega_{i}}|H_{i}(y)||S_{0}(u)(\lambda y)||y|^{2}\,dS\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}D(\Omega_{i})\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Then by Lemma
2.2
, we have

\tilde{N}(\xi)\leq T\left(|\Omega_{i}(1)|+|\Omega_{i}^{\prime}(1)|\right)+\sqrt{2}\left(CD(\Omega_{i})\|\nabla S_{0}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)\leq CD(\Omega_{i})\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)

and hence

R(\lambda,u)\geq\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\beta(1-\lambda)^{2}e^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)&\text{for}\ 0\leq\lambda\leq T,\\
\displaystyle-\beta\lambda^{3}e^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}D(\Omega_{i})\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p+1}^{2}\right)&\text{for}\ \lambda\geq T,\end{cases}

for some
\beta>0
independent of
e
,
\rho
,
\lambda
.
The remaining parts can be shown
in the same way as Lemma
3.7
.
∎

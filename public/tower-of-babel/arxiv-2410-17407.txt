Morita theory for dynamical von Neumann algebras
Joeri De Ro

Abstract
. Given a locally compact quantum group G \mathbb{G} and two G \mathbb{G} - W ∗ W^{*} -algebras α : A ↶ G \alpha:A\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G} and β : B ↶ G \beta:B\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G} , we study the notion of equivariant W ∗ W^{*} -Morita equivalence ( A , α ) ∼ G ⁡ ( B , β ) (A,\alpha)\sim{G}(B,\beta) , which is an equivariant version of Rieffel’s notion of W ∗ W^{*} -Morita equivalence. We prove that important dynamical properties of G \mathbb{G} - W ∗ W^{*} -algebras, such as (inner) amenability, are preserved under equivariant Morita equivalence. For a coideal von Neumann algebra L ∞ ​ ( K \ G ) ⊆ L ∞ ​ ( G ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{K}\backslash\mathbb{G})\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}) with dual coideal von Neumann algebra L ∞ ​ ( K ˇ ) ⊆ L ∞ ​ ( G ˇ ) L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{K}})\subseteq L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}) , we use a natural G ˇ \check{\mathbb{G}} - W ∗ W^{*} -Morita equivalence L ∞ ( K \ G ) ⋊ Δ G ∼ G ˇ L ∞ ( K ˇ ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{K}\backslash\mathbb{G})\rtimes_{\Delta}\mathbb{G}\sim_{\check{\mathbb{G}}}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{K}}) to relate dynamical properties of L ∞ ​ ( K \ G ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{K}\backslash\mathbb{G}) with dynamical properties of L ∞ ​ ( K ˇ ) L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{K}}) . We use this to refine some recent results established by Anderson-Sackaney and Khosravi. This refinement allows us to answer a question of Kalantar, Kasprzak, Skalski and Vergnioux, namely that for H \mathbb{H} a closed quantum subgroup of the compact quantum group G \mathbb{G} , coamenability of H \ G \mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G} and relative amenability of ℓ ∞ ​ ( H ˇ ) \ell^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{H}}) in ℓ ∞ ​ ( G ˇ ) \ell^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}) are equivalent. Moreover, if G \mathbb{G} is compact, we study the relation between G \mathbb{G} - W ∗ W^{*} -Morita equivalence of ( A , α ) (A,\alpha) and ( B , β ) (B,\beta) and G \mathbb{G} - C ∗ C^{*} -Morita equivalence of the associated G \mathbb{G} - C ∗ C^{*} -algebras ( ℛ ⁡ ( A ) , α ) (\mathcal{R}(A),\alpha) and ( ℛ ⁡ ( B ) , β ) (\mathcal{R}(B),\beta) of regular elements.

Given a von Neumann algebra

Theorem 1.1
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\Rep(A)
and
\Rep(B)
are equivalent as
W^{*}
-categories.

(2)

There exists a Hilbert space

From the above equivalences, it is easily deduced that

[

,

,

2.
Preliminaries

All vector spaces and algebras in this paper are defined over the complex numbers. Inner products of Hilbert spaces are assumed to be anti-linear in the first factor. If
V,W
are normed vector spaces, we write
B(V,W)
for the vector space of bounded linear operators

, naturally normed through the operator norm and we write

. If
(V,\tau)
is a topological vector space and

is a subset of

, then we write

for the closure of the linear span of
X
. If
V
is a normed space, we simply write
[X]
for the norm-closure of the linear span of
X
.
Let

be a

-algebra. By a projection

in

, we always mean an element

such that
p=p^{2}=p^{*}
, and if

is unital, we write

. We assume that the inner product of a right Hilbert

-module

is anti-linear in the first factor and the inner product of a left Hilbert
C
-module is anti-linear in the second factor. The

-algebra of

-adjointable operators on the right Hilbert-

-module

will be denoted by
\mathscr{L}_{C}(X)
.

2.1.
Von Neumann algebras

We shall use the terminologies von Neumann algebra and
W^{*}
-algebra as synonyms. Given a von Neumann algebra
A
, we write
A_{*}
for its space of normal functionals. A projection

is called

if
[ApA]^{\sigma\text{-weak}}=A
. We quickly go over some of the notations in Tomita-Takesaki theory
[

]
that we will be using.

The standard Hilbert space of

will be denoted by
L^{2}(A)
, together with its natural standard representation

\pi_{A}:A\to B(L^{2}(A)).

This Hilbert space is endowed with a natural anti-unitary
J_{A}:L^{2}(A)\to L^{2}(A)
satisfying
J_{A}=J_{A}^{-1}=J_{A}^{*}
. We denote the standard anti-
*
-representation by

\rho_{A}:A\to B(L^{2}(A)):a\mapsto J_{A}\pi_{A}(a)^{*}J_{A}.

We have
\rho_{A}(A)^{\prime}=\pi_{A}(A).

If

is a normal, semi-finite, faithful (= nsf) weight on

, we write

\mathscr{M}_{\varphi}^{+}:=\{a\in A^{+}:\varphi(a)<\infty\},\quad\mathscr{N}_{\varphi}:=\{a\in A:\varphi(a^{*}a)<\infty\}.

With respect to the nsf weight
\varphi
, we can make a GNS construction
\pi_{\varphi}:A\to B(L^{2}(A,\varphi))
where
\pi_{\varphi}
is a unital, normal
*
-homomorphism. We will make use of the associated GNS-map
\Lambda_{\varphi}:\mathscr{N}_{\varphi}\to L^{2}(A,\varphi)
. The Hilbert space
L^{2}(A,\varphi)
is naturally endowed with the anti-unitary

J_{\varphi}:L^{2}(A,\varphi)\to L^{2}(A,\varphi)
satisfying
J_{\varphi}=J_{\varphi}^{*}=J_{\varphi}^{-1}.

There is a (essentially by construction of
L^{2}(A)
) canonical unitary identification
L^{2}(A)\cong L^{2}(A,\varphi)
compatible with the standard representations and the canonical anti-unitaries.

be a Morita
B
-
C
-correspondence. Then

is a Morita
A
-
C
correspondence.

Proof.

Given
x\in X
, let us define the bounded operator

i_{x}:\mathcal{K}\to X\otimes_{B}\mathcal{K}
by
i_{x}(\xi)=x\otimes_{B}\xi
. Its adjoint is then given by
i_{x}^{*}(y\otimes_{B}\xi)=\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x,y\rangle_{B})\xi
for

and
\xi\in\mathcal{K}
.

Consider the subspace

\mathcal{K}_{0}:=\operatorname{span}\{\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x,y\rangle_{B})\xi:x,y\in X,\xi\in\mathcal{K}\}\subseteq\mathcal{K}.

Since
[\langle X,X\rangle_{B}]^{\sigma\text{-strong}}=B
, we conclude that
\mathcal{K}_{0}
is norm-dense in
\mathcal{K}
.
Assume now that
T\in\pi_{\boxtimes}(A)^{\prime}
is a unitary. If
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x_{j},y_{j}\rangle_{B})\xi_{j}=0
, then for
s,t\in X
and
\eta\in\mathcal{K}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{x_{j}}^{*}T(y_{j}\otimes_{B}\xi_{j}),\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle s,t\rangle_{B})\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle T(y_{j}\otimes_{B}\xi_{j}),x_{j}\otimes_{B}\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle s,t\rangle_{B})\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle T(y_{j}\otimes_{B}\xi_{j}),\langle x_{j},s\rangle_{A}t\otimes_{B}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle T(\langle s,x_{j}\rangle_{A}y_{j}\otimes_{B}\xi_{j}\rangle),t\otimes_{B}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle T(s\otimes_{B}\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x_{j},y_{j}\rangle_{B})\xi_{j}),t\otimes_{B}\eta\rangle=0,

so that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{x_{j}}^{*}T(y_{j}\otimes_{B}\xi_{j})=0
. Therefore, there is a unique linear operator

such that

S(\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x,y\rangle_{B})\xi)=i_{x}^{*}T(y\otimes_{B}\xi),\quad x,y\in X,\quad\xi\in\mathcal{K}.

For
x,y,s,t\in X
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{K}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x,y\rangle_{B})S\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle s,t\rangle_{B})\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle s\otimes_{B}\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle y,x\rangle_{B})\xi,T(t\otimes_{B}\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\langle s,y\rangle_{A}x\otimes_{B}\xi,T(t\otimes_{B}\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x\otimes_{B}\xi,T(\langle y,s\rangle_{A}t\otimes_{B}\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x\otimes_{B}\xi,T(y\otimes_{B}\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle s,t\rangle_{B})\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi,S\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x,y\rangle_{B})\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle s,t\rangle_{B})\eta\rangle,

from which we deduce that

\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x,y\rangle_{B})S\zeta=S\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x,y\rangle_{B})\zeta,\quad\zeta\in\mathcal{K}_{0}.

(2.1)

If
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
and
x,y\in X
, we therefore find

\langle x\otimes_{B}\xi,y\otimes_{B}S\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x,y\rangle_{B})S\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,S\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x,y\rangle_{B})\eta\rangle=\langle x\otimes_{B}\xi,T(y\otimes_{B}\eta)\rangle,

whence

T(y\otimes_{B}\eta)=y\otimes_{B}S\eta,\quad y\in X,\quad\eta\in\mathcal{K}_{0}.

(2.2)

In particular, since
T
is isometric, we find for

and
x,y\in X
that

Since
[\langle X,X\rangle_{B}]^{\sigma\text{-strong}}=B
, we conclude that

\langle\eta,\xi\rangle=\langle S\eta,S\xi\rangle,\quad\eta,\xi\in\mathcal{K}_{0}.

Therefore,
S
is isometric and extends uniquely to an isometry
\tilde{S}\in B(\mathcal{K}).
By (
2.1
), we deduce that
\tilde{S}\in\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(B)^{\prime}=\rho_{\mathcal{K}}(C)
. Then (
2.2
) shows that
T=\rho_{\boxtimes}(c)
for some
c\in C
. We conclude that
\pi_{\boxtimes}(A)^{\prime}=\rho_{\boxtimes}(C)
.

It remains to check that
\pi_{\boxtimes}
and
\rho_{\boxtimes}
are faithful. But if
a\in A
and
\pi_{\boxtimes}(a)=0
, then

0=\langle x\otimes_{B}\xi,ay\otimes_{B}\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,\pi_{\mathcal{K}}(\langle x,ay\rangle_{B})\eta\rangle,\quad x,y\in Y,\quad\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{K}.

By faithfulness of

, it follows that

for all
x,y\in X
whence

. Since

, this implies

. Thus,
\pi_{\boxtimes}
is faithful. Similarly, if

for some

for all

. Since

is faithful, we conclude that

\rho_{\boxtimes}

2.3.
Locally compact quantum groups

A
Hopf-von Neumann algebra
is a pair
(M,\Delta)
where
M
is a von Neumann algebra and
\Delta:M\to M\bar{\otimes}M
a unital, normal, isometric
*
-homomorphism such that
(\Delta\otimes\id)\circ\Delta=(\id\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta
.

A
locally compact quantum group

[
KV00
,
KV03
,
VV03
]

\mathbb{G}
is a Hopf-von Neumann algebra
(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),\Delta)
for which there exist normal, semifinite, faithful weights
\Phi,\Psi:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})_{+}\to[0,\infty]
such that
(\id\otimes\Phi)\Delta(x)=\Phi(x)1
for all
x\in\mathscr{M}_{\Phi}^{+}
and
(\Psi\otimes\id)\Delta(x)=\Psi(x)1
for all
x\in\mathscr{M}_{\Psi}^{+}
. The von Neumann algebra predual
L^{1}(\mathbb{G}):=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})_{*}
is a completely contractive Banach algebra for the multiplication

\mu\star\nu:=(\mu\otimes\nu)\circ\Delta,\quad\mu,\nu\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}).

Without any loss of generality, we may (and will) assume that
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is faithfully represented in standard form on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
. To simplify notation, the standard anti-
*
-representation
\rho_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})}
will simply be denoted by
\rho{G}
.

With respect to the weights
\Phi,\Psi
, we then have GNS-maps

\Lambda_{\Phi}:\mathscr{N}_{\Phi}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G}),\quad\Lambda_{\Psi}:\mathscr{N}_{\Psi}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G}).

Fundamental to the theory of locally compact quantum groups are the unitaries

V,W\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G})),

called respectively
right
and
left
regular unitary representation. They are uniquely characterized by the identities

(\id\otimes\omega)(V)\Lambda_{\Psi}(x)=\Lambda_{\Psi}((\id\otimes\omega)\Delta(x)),\qquad\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}),x\in\mathscr{N}_{\Psi},

(\omega\otimes\id)(W^{*})\Lambda_{\Phi}(x)=\Lambda_{\Phi}((\omega\otimes\id)\Delta(x)),\qquad\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}),x\in\mathscr{N}_{\Phi}.

They are
multiplicative unitaries

[
BS93
]
meaning that

V_{12}V_{13}V_{23}=V_{23}V_{12},\qquad W_{12}W_{13}W_{23}=W_{23}W_{12},

and they implement the coproduct of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
in the sense that

W^{*}(1\otimes x)W=\Delta(x)=V(x\otimes 1)V^{*},\qquad x\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).

Moreover, we have

C_{0}(\mathbb{G}):=[(\omega\otimes\id)(V)\mid\omega\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))_{*}]=[(\id\otimes\omega)(W)\mid\omega\in B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))_{*}],

which is a
\sigma
-weakly dense C
∗
-subalgebra of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
. Then
\Delta(C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))\subseteq M(C_{0}(\mathbb{G})\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))
.

We can also define the von Neumann algebra

L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})=[(\omega\otimes\id)(W)\mid\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})]^{\sigma\textrm{-weak}}

with coproduct

\hat{\Delta}(\hat{x})=\Sigma W(\hat{x}\otimes 1)W^{*}\Sigma
where
\hat{x}\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
.
Then the pair
(L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\hat{\Delta})
defines the locally compact quantum group
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
, which is called the dual of
\mathbb{G}
. Similarly, we can also define the von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})=[(\id\otimes\omega)(V)\mid\omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})]^{\sigma\textrm{-weak}}

with coproduct

\check{\Delta}(\check{x})=V^{*}(1\otimes\check{x})V
for
\check{x}\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
.
Then the pair
(L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}),\check{\Delta})
defines a locally compact quantum group
\check{\mathbb{G}}
. We have
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})=L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})^{\prime}
. If
G
is a locally compact group, we have

L^{\infty}(\hat{G})=\mathscr{L}(G)
and
L^{\infty}(\check{G})=\mathscr{R}(G)
, the left resp. right group von Neumann algebra associated with
G
. Therefore,
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
and
\check{\mathbb{G}}
should be thought of as a left and a right version of the dual locally compact quantum group. The von Neumann algebras
L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})
and
L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}})
, acting naturally on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
, are in standard form.

The left multiplicative unitary of
\check{\mathbb{G}}
will be denoted by
\check{W}
and the right multiplicative unitary of
\check{\mathbb{G}}
will be denoted by
\check{V}
. We have
\check{W}=V
and

V\in L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),\quad W\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}),\quad\check{V}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})^{\prime}\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}).

In fact, we have e.g.
V\in M(C_{0}(\check{\mathbb{G}})\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))
and similarly for
W
and
\check{V}
. For future use, we will also need the following notations:

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})):x\mapsto W^{*}(1\otimes x)W,

\displaystyle\Delta_{r}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}):x\mapsto V(x\otimes 1)V^{*},

\displaystyle\check{\Delta}_{r}:B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\check{\mathbb{G}}):x\mapsto\check{V}(x\otimes 1)\check{V}^{*}.

A locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is called
compact

. The canonical dense Hopf

. In that case, the left Haar weight

(after appropriate normalizations).

A locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is called
discrete
if
\check{\mathbb{G}}
is compact and we then write
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
and
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})=c_{0}(\mathbb{G})
. If
\mathbb{G}
is a discrete quantum group, there is a unique normal state

A

of a locally compact quantum group

where
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space and

. For example, we have

Consider the modular conjugation

We call

of

We write

In this section, we develop the equivariant Morita theory. We give several equivalent characterizations of the notion of equivariant Morita equivalence, and we discuss examples of equivariant Morita equivalences that appear naturally in the theory of locally compact quantum groups. Throughout this entire section, we fix a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
and
\mathbb{G}
-

-algebras

and

We will use the following generalization of Definition

Proof.

]
*Section 5.1 is also a

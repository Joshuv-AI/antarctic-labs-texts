Two coincidences are a clue: Probing a GeV-scale dark QCD sector
Yi Chung

Abstract
The similarity between dark matter and baryon energy densities suggests the existence of a dark sector analogous to the visible QCD sector. In addition, small-scale structure issues can be addressed by dark matter self-interactions with a cross section comparable to that of QCD. Both observations point to a GeV-scale dark QCD sector. In this letter, guided by these two coincidences, we investigate how a chiral dark QCD model with a MeV-scale dark photon can resolve the core–cusp problem. A potential third coincidence, associated with the latest measurement of N eff N_{\rm eff} being lower than the Standard Model prediction, is also discussed. Current constraints leave a finite window in the parameter space, which can be tested by future experiments such as Gamma Factory.



I
Introduction

The nature of dark matter remains the most intriguing problems of physics beyond the Standard Model (SM). To date, all observational evidence comes from its gravitational effects on cosmology and astronomy. Although the available information is limited, current measurements reveal two striking similarities that may provide important clues to uncovering the mysterious dark sector.

From cosmology, the CMB provides the most precise quantitative measurement of dark matter, the energy density
\Omega_{c}\approx 0.26

Aghanim et al. 2020

\Omega_{b}\approx 0.05

\Omega_{c}/\Omega_{b}\approx 5

II
Simplified Model of dark sector

Although solutions to the energy density coincidence problem typically require a complex dark QCD sector, in this study we focus on a simplified model that retains the essential features relevant for phenomenological analysis. To have the dark photon mass originated from the same source as the dark matter mass, we adopt a chiral dark QCD model
Harigaya and Nomura 2016
;
Co et al. 2017
;
Ibe et al. 2021
, where the dark
U(1)_{D}
gauge group is dynamically broken by the dark quark condensate, as also motivated by
Chung 2024b
. As a result, the model is characterized by a single dynamical scale
f
.

We consider a dark gauge group
SU(N)_{D}\times U(1)_{D}
with two Weyl fermions,
\psi_{L}
and
\psi_{R}
, transforming as
(\mathbf{3},1/2)
and
(\mathbf{3},-1/2)
, respectively.

2

2

Notice that this simplified model is anomalous and we expect other states to be presented in a complete dark QCD model but play irrelevant role in the phenomenology.
Once the dark color group
SU(N)_{D}
becomes strongly coupled at the GeV scale, the
\psi_{L}
and
\psi_{R}
form a condensate, which can be formulated by a complex scalar field
\Phi
transforming as
(\mathbf{1},1)
. The Lagrangian of the simplified theory is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm DS}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}+|D_{\mu}\Phi|^{2}+\mu^{2}|\Phi|^{2}-\lambda|\Phi|^{4}

\displaystyle+i\bar{\psi}_{L,R}\,\not{D}\,\psi_{L,R}+Y_{\psi}\,\bar{\psi}_{L}\,\Phi\,\psi_{R}+\textrm{h.c.}~,

(3)

where
Y_{\psi}
is the Yukawa coupling between the dark quarks and their condensate. The condensate
\Phi
acquires a nonzero vacuum expectation value (VEV)
\langle\Phi\rangle=f
from the strong dynamics and generate the masses for both dark quark and dark photon:

\displaystyle m_{\psi}=Y_{\psi}\,f\,,\quad m_{\gamma^{\prime}}=g_{D}\,f~

(4)

with
g_{D}
denoting the gauge coupling of the
U(1)_{D}
. The dark matter, which is the dark baryon composed of
N
dark quarks, then has mass and charge given by

\displaystyle m_{D}=N\,Y_{\psi}\,f\,\equiv Y_{D}\,f\,,\quad Q(D)\equiv Q_{D}=N/2~,

(5)

where
Y_{D}
is expected to be
\mathcal{O}(4\pi)
as the consequence of a strongly coupled theory. In this work, we take
N=3
as a benchmark, leaving three independent parameters: the dynamical scale
f
, the dark matter mass
m_{D}
, and the dark photon mass
m_{\gamma^{\prime}}
. The dark gauge coupling is thus fixed by the ratio
g_{D}=m_{\gamma^{\prime}}/f
.

In addition, we need a portal between dark sector and the visible sector. In this study, we consider the kinetic mixing between the dark photon and the photon
Holdom 1986

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm portal}=-\frac{\epsilon}{2}F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\implies\epsilon eA^{\prime}_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{\rm QED}~,

(6)

which introduces interactions between the dark photon and the SM particles. The mixing parameter
\epsilon
is the fourth parameter in this simplified model and plays a crucial role in cosmology and dark sector searches.

III
Inputs from the two coincidences

Having established a simplified model of dark baryons and dark photons motivated by the two coincidences, we now move on to analyze the parameter space of
(f,m_{D},m_{\gamma^{\prime}},\epsilon)
that is favored by these coincidences.

III.1
Energy density coincidence

Start from the coincidence in energy densities. Resolving this coincidence requires both a comparable number density,
n_{D}\approx n_{B}
, and a similar mass scale,
m_{D}\approx m_{B}
. The first condition can be addressed within the framework of ADM cosmology. As we will discuss later, this has important implications for the mixing parameter
\epsilon
.

For comparable masses, we require the ratio
m_{D}/m_{B}
to be greater than unity but not exceed the observed energy density ratio
\rho_{D}/\rho_{B}\approx 5
. This avoids unnatural fine-tuning among parameters. Imposing such a naturalness criterion constrains the dark matter mass to a narrow window,
m_{D}=1-5
GeV, which we adopt as the benchmark region in this analysis.

IV
N_{\rm eff}
as the third coincidence?

So far, we have gotten a GeV-scale chiral dark QCD sector whose parameter space is motivated by the two coincidence problems, both arising from purely dark-sector observations and thus remaining independent of the portal to the SM. However, since the energy density coincidence favors an asymmetric dark matter scenario, a large entropy associated with the symmetric component of the dark sector is expected. In our setup, this entropy is ultimately transferred to the lightest dark-sector particle — the MeV-scale dark photon.

To prevent the Universe from being overclosed by the relic abundance of these dark photons (assuming a sufficiently high reheating temperature), a nonzero kinetic mixing parameter
\epsilon
is required, allowing the dark photons to decay into SM particles. This process has significant implications for the thermal history around
T=\mathcal{O}(\text{MeV})
, as previously discussed in
Ibe et al. 2020
.

The main cosmological constraint arises from the measurement of
N_{\rm eff}
. Since the decay of MeV-scale dark photon will only heat up the electron-photon plasma, if they do not decay before the neutrino decoupling era, it will lead to a greater
T_{\gamma}/T_{\nu}
and thus
N_{\rm eff}<N_{\rm eff}^{\rm SM}

Ibe et al. 2020
;
Li et al. 2020
.

The constraint of
N_{\rm eff}
comes in two folds. First, the lifetime of dark photons needs to be short enough, which requires
\tau_{\gamma^{\prime}}<\mathcal{O}(1)\,s
. However, even with the short lifetime, if the dark photon is too light, the portion of the dark photon will still decay after neutrino decoupling and lead to a small
N_{\rm eff}
. Such an effect is important as it will directly put a constraint on the dark photon mass
m_{\gamma^{\prime}}
regardless of mixing parameter
\epsilon
as shown in Fig.
3
.

The tightest limit to date comes from combination of CMB spectrum, including Planck and the latest ACT, lensing, BAO, and helium/deuterium abundances
Navas et al. 2024
, giving
N_{\rm eff}=2.89\pm 0.11

Calabrese et al. 2025
compared to the SM prediction
N_{\rm eff}^{\rm SM}=3.0440\pm 0.0002

Akita and Yamaguchi 2020
;
Froustey et al. 2020
;
Bennett et al. 2021
;
Drewes et al. 2024
. We obtain a constraint on
N_{\rm eff}>2.67
at
95\%
C.L., which corresponds to a lower bound on the dark photon mass,
m_{\gamma^{\prime}}>8.5
MeV.

Although the current constraint from
N_{\rm eff}
already excludes half of the parameter space, it could have been much stronger if the observed
N_{\rm eff}
is close to the SM prediction. The current value of
N_{\rm eff}=2.89
actually favors a dark photon of
m_{\gamma^{\prime}}=12.5
MeV. We refer to this as a potential third coincidence, as it aligns with the mass range independently required by the two other coincidences discussed earlier. Of course, this deviation remains statistically consistent with the SM value. The CMB Stage-4
Abazajian et al. 2016
can reach the sensitivity of
\pm 0.054

95\%
C.L., which will decisively probe this deviation and even pinpoint the dark photon mass to a narrow range.

VI
Conclusion

Motivated by the two coincidences from the dark matter measurements, we construct a simplified model for a chiral dark QCD sector, wherein the dark baryon plays the role of dark matter and the dark photon acts as both a light mediator and a portal to the SM. The parameter space that can accommodate small-scale structure issues is analyzed, favoring a dark photon of
1-10
MeV with a gauge coupling
g_{D}=\mathcal{O}(0.01)
.

Complementary searches are discussed in detail. The most stringent upper bound on the mixing
\epsilon
comes from the dark photon searches in beam dump experiments. On the other hand, the direct detection of dark baryons is less sensitive due to the velocity-suppression for DM-nucleon cross section in the chiral dark QCD model.

The parameter space is bounded from below and left by the measurement of
N_{\rm eff}
, leaving a finite window to be tested. Given the strong sensitivity of
N_{\rm eff}
to the MeV-scale physics, the upcoming CMB Stage-4 may provide the first indication of the model if the observed value continues to lie below the SM prediction. In parallel, the relevant parameter space can be further explored by the Gamma Factory in the search for dark photons and by future direct detection experiments targeting SIDM.

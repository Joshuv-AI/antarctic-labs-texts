Investigating the Relationships Between Family Communication Patterns and Parent-Adolescent Conflicts on the General Health of Female High School Students in Ahvaz, Iran
Authors: Arezoo Zeydani, Mahin Gheibizadeh, Soodabeh Bassak Nejad, Saeed Ghanbari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:The present study aimed to investigate the relationships between family communication patterns and parent-adolescent conflicts on the general health of female high school students in Ahvaz, Iran.Materials and Methods:This descriptive-analytical study was conducted on 180 female high school adolescents in 2019. Data were collected using the family communication pattern questionnaire, the parent-adolescent conflict assessment questionnaire, and the General Health Questionnaire. Statistical tests (independent t-test, Pearson’s correlation coefficient, regression analysis, and analysis of variance) were used for data analysis.Results:A statistically significant negative relationship exists between family communication patterns and public health in the conversation orientation dimension (p < 0.001, r = −0.54) and a statistically significant positive relationship in the conformity dimension (p < 0.001, r = 0.73). Furthermore, a positive and statistically significant relationship exists between the frequency (p < 0.001, r = 0.56) and intensity (p < 0.001, r = 0.57) of parent-adolescent conflicts and public health.Conclusions:The findings suggest the need for interventions that promote effective family communication and conflict resolution skills.

Introduction

Adolescence is the transitional period from childhood to adulthood, marked by significant physical, psychological, and social changes.[1] Ignoring adolescent issues creates numerous problems for individuals, families, and communities.[2] One unavoidable setting for conflict is the family, particularly the parent-child relationship.[3]

Parent-adolescent conflict refers to disagreements between parents and adolescents on specific issues, preventing them from resolving their problems calmly.[4] Family communication patterns include two dimensions: conversation and conformity. These dimensions reflect the communication style and the way family members express their thoughts and feelings towards each other.[5] The study by Zhang et al. found that positive communication between parents and adolescents reduces depressive symptoms in adolescents.[6]

Therefore, considering the sociocultural context of Iran, the large population of adolescent girls, the restrictions and sensitivities of this period, and the nurse’s responsibility to identify vulnerable groups and maintain a healthy society, it is crucial to consider the health of adolescent girls. This study was conducted to investigate the relationships between family communication patterns and parent-adolescent conflicts with the general health of female high school students in Ahvaz, Iran.

Materials and Methods

This descriptive-analytical study, extracted from the first author’s master’s thesis completed in 2019–2020, included high school students of varying grades. The research sample was determined to be 218 individuals using a previous study[7] and a sample determination formula (β = 0.1, α = 0.05, r = 0.241), accounting for a potential 25% loss. However, a final analysis was conducted using 180 completed questionnaires.

The sample was selected by the researcher using a multi-stage random sampling method. Specifically, seven schools were randomly selected from the four educational districts of Ahvaz, Iran, proportional to the number of schools in each district. Then, from each selected school, one class was randomly chosen. Finally, 31 students were randomly selected from each class, based on the number of students in the class.

Inclusion criteria included living with both parents and having no history of congenital, genetic, or psychiatric diseases. The researcher asked the students about any illnesses or medications they were taking. After providing the necessary explanations about the research’s purpose to the students and their parents, the questionnaire was distributed.

In this study, a demographic questionnaire, Ritchie and Fitzpatrick’s revised family communication pattern questionnaire,[8] the parent-adolescent conflict assessment questionnaire (adolescent form),[9] and the General Health Questionnaire-28 were used.[10] The validity and reliability of these scales have been confirmed in previous studies.[81112]

In this study, the data were analyzed using descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation [SD], frequency, and percentage) and independent t-tests, Pearson’s correlation coefficient, regression analysis, and analysis of variance in SPSS statistical software (version 16.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA). The normality of data distribution was also assessed.

Ethical considerations

This research has been approved by the research ethics committee of Ahvaz Jundishapur University of Medical Sciences (code: IR.AJUMS.REC.1398.153). All ethical considerations, such as the right to participate voluntarily, explaining the research aims and process to the participants, obtaining informed consent from the female adolescents and their parents, confidentiality of information, and fidelity in using texts have been observed.

Results

Finally, in this study, information regarding 180 female high school adolescents was analyzed. The mean and SD of the sample’s age was 16.43 (0.79).

The mean and SD of the scores of family communication patterns, parent-adolescent conflicts, and general health, and its dimensions, in the studied students are as follows. The mean (SD) of conversation, conformity, conflict intensity, and conflict frequency scores in the studied samples were 33.73 (15.09), 17.75 (10.73), 165.92 (53.08), and 121.10 (59.81), respectively. Also, the mean (SD) of the general health score was 35.27 (14.60). Among the dimensions of general health, the highest mean belonged to the anxiety dimension 9.13 (4.44), and the lowest mean was associated with the social dysfunction dimension 8.42 (4.39). The mean for the physical symptoms dimension was 8.78 (4.40), and the mean for the depression dimension was 8.92 (5.10).

The analysis of Pearson correlation coefficients reveals a significant positive relationship between parent-adolescent conflict and overall health, as well as all its components (p < 0.001). Conversely, there is a significant negative relationship between the “conversation” dimension of family communication patterns and general health, and all its components (p < 0.001). Furthermore, there is a significant positive relationship between the “conformity” dimension of family communication patterns and general health, across all components (p < 0.001) [Table 1].

The results also show a significant negative relationship between the “conversation” dimension of family communication patterns and the dimensions of parent-adolescent conflict (p < 0.001), and a significant positive relationship between the “conformity” dimension and parent-adolescent conflict (p < 0.001). The results of the multivariate regression analysis indicate that a one SD increase in parent-adolescent conflict intensity (p = 0.01, t = 2.64) is associated with a 0.189 SD increase in general health scores.

Similarly, a one SD increases in the conformity dimension of family communication patterns (P < 0.001, t = 7.01) is associated with a 0.592 SD increase in general health scores. These variables collectively explained 55.70% (54.70% adjusted) of the variance in general health among female adolescents in this study.

Discussion

The results of this study indicated that female adolescents perceive communication patterns as highly conversation-oriented and moderately conformity-oriented, with conformity measured on a spectrum from high to low. The study, which included adolescent participants, also reported high average conversation scores and moderate average conformity scores.[13] In families characterized by high conversation and low conformity, family members readily express their feelings, respect their parents while maintaining independence, and participate in decision-making.

Female adolescents participating in the current study reported a higher frequency of conflict than intensity of conflict. In one study, adolescents reported a high frequency of conflicts with their parents, but the intensity of the conflicts was not severe.[14] This suggests that conflicts stemming from the changes and challenges of this developmental period trying to assume adult roles, desiring independence, and pursuing personal goals and the resulting decrease in parent-adolescent closeness are common, though not necessarily severe. The present study’s findings indicated that the health status of female adolescents was moderately good. A study investigating the prevalence of mental disorders among high school students in Mashhad found that most adolescents reported their mental health as moderate, and a significant number experienced or were at risk for mental disorders.[15] These findings can be attributed to biological factors, environmental influences, and social and individual living circumstances.

According to the findings of this study, the frequency and intensity of conflicts are statistically significantly related to public health and all its dimensions. Increasing conflict frequency and intensity correlates with higher general health scores in individuals. Results from other studies in this area show a relationship between parent-adolescent conflict and increasing physical and psychological problems in adolescents.[1617] Due to feelings of growing up and the desire for independence, adolescents often challenge their parents, and parents may engage in ongoing conflicts with their children, perhaps because they are unfamiliar with effective parenting strategies and are inflexible. Facing these issues and difficulties, adolescents become distressed and often feel helpless, leading to physical and psychological symptoms.

The results of this study showed a statistically significant relationship between the two dimensions of family communication patterns and general health. The study found that increased conversation within the family is associated with better health outcomes for female adolescents, while higher levels of conformity within the family are linked to increased health problems for female adolescents. This evidence suggests that family communication patterns are a predictor of adolescent mental health.[1819]

The results of the present study revealed a negative and statistically significant correlation between the conversation dimension of family communication patterns and conflict frequency and intensity, and a significant positive relationship between the conformity dimension of family communication patterns and conflict frequency and intensity. Bi et al. study found that adolescents of authoritarian parents reported higher conflict intensity.[20] Based on the findings of this study and other studies, it can be concluded that families that foster environments where female adolescents feel comfortable expressing their opinions and freely discussing issues with their parents will experience fewer conflicts and less intense disagreements.

According to the regression test results, family communication patterns and parent-adolescent conflicts, as predictor variables, generally predicted 55.70% of general health changes in the studied female adolescents. Results of multiple studies showed that a lack of support and increased conflict in parent-adolescent relationships was a strong predictor of increasing depressive symptoms in adolescents.[2021] Based on the findings of the present study and other studies, it can be concluded that ineffective family relationships and parent-adolescent conflicts negatively predict female adolescents’ health. Given the importance of maintaining the health of female adolescents, it is necessary to improve the relationship between parents and adolescents and increase parents’ awareness of adolescents’ needs and wants. This study, like other studies, has limitations. The limitation of this study was student fatigue due to the large number of questions. The researcher attempted to mitigate this limitation by selecting an appropriate day and time for data collection and coordinating with school officials to provide a suitable space for students to complete the questionnaire.

Conclusion

According to the results of this study, family communication patterns and parent-adolescent conflicts are predictive factors for the general health of adolescent girls. This suggests a critical need for increased attention to improving the parent-daughter relationship and parents’ understanding of the unique needs and developmental changes of this period. An educational program aimed at improving the health of adolescent girls is also warranted. The findings of this study can serve as a foundation for future research in this area, particularly experimental studies designed to evaluate the effectiveness of various strategies for enhancing communication skills and reducing parent-adolescent conflict, ultimately promoting, and maintaining the health of adolescent girls.

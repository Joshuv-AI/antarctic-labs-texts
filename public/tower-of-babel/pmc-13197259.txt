Durable remission despite transplantation with active disease in therapy-related NUP98::TOP1-rearranged acute myeloid leukemia
Authors: Gaetano Cimino, Rebecca Sembenico, Francesco Zorutti, Simonetta Saldi, Matteo Caridi, Valeria Cardinali, Sofia Sciabolacci, Barbara Crescenzi, Caterina Matteucci, Roberta La Starza, Alessandra Pucciarini, Tiziana Zei, Roberta Iacucci Ostini, Cynthia Aristei, Alessandra Carotti, Cristina Mecucci, Loredana Ruggeri, Maria Paola Martelli, Antonio Pierini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractNucleoporin 98 (NUP98) rearrangements occur in approximately 2–3% of myeloid neoplasms and involve more than 40 partner genes. In acute myeloid leukemia (AML), they define distinct entities associated with adverse prognosis, high rates of chemoresistance, and frequent relapse even after allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT). Here, we report two patients with therapy-related NUP98-rearranged AML carrying the rare NUP98::TOP1 fusion that occurred after treatment for relapsed/refractory lymphoma. Both patients received CPX-351 as induction therapy and proceeded to allo-HSCT with persistent disease, with approximately 5–10% residual bone marrow blasts. The transplant strategy consisted of an irradiation-based allo-HSCT platform combining total marrow/lymphoid irradiation (TMLI, 20 Gy) with adoptive transfer of regulatory and conventional T cells (Treg/Tcon), administered in the absence of post-transplant pharmacologic immunosuppression to preserve graft-versus-leukemia (GvL) activity while controlling graft-versus-host disease (GvHD). Both patients achieved sustained long-term remission and are alive at 69 and 55 months after allo-HSCT, respectively, without significant transplant-related complications. While no conclusions on treatment efficacy can be drawn from this limited case series, these observations document durable remission despite the active disease at transplant in an ultra-high-risk setting and support further investigation of immune-based transplant strategies in NUP98-rearranged AML.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-026-07073-2.Keywords: AML, NUP98 rearrangements, tAML, allo-HSCT, T-regulatory cells, TMLI

To the Editor;

Durable remission after allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT) performed in the presence of active disease remains uncommon in acute myeloid leukemia (AML), particularly in high-risk molecular subtypes. Among these, NUP98 rearrangements (NUP98r) are found in about 2–4% of adult AML cases and 5–10% of pediatric cases [1, 2]. Since their initial identification [3], around 40 fusion partner genes have been identified, with HOXA9 and NSD1 being the most common. These rearrangements can lead to diverse clinical phenotypes, including AML (often with the NUP98::NSD1 fusion and mutations like FLT3-ITD and WT1), T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia, and myelodysplastic syndromes [4, 5]. NUP98r are considered defining genetic abnormalities in AML [6], associated with poor prognosis, high rates of treatment resistance, and relapse, even after allo-HSCT, which represents a clinical challenge because these patients are generally heavily pretreated [1, 2, 7, 8].

DNA topoisomerase I (TOP1) is one of the 40 recognized NUP98 fusion partner genes [9–11]. NUP98::TOP1 is a rare fusion described in sporadic cases [7, 12] and predominantly identified in therapy-related cases following exposure to topoisomerase inhibitors [9]; however, rare de novo cases have also been reported [10]. Data on clinical outcomes specifically associated with NUP98::TOP1 rearrangements remain extremely limited. Here, we report two patients with therapy-related NUP98::TOP1-rearranged AML who underwent allo-HSCT with persistent disease and subsequently achieved sustained long-term remission.

The first patient is a 49-year-old man diagnosed with nodular sclerosing Hodgkin lymphoma (HL), stage IVB, in October 2018, treated with ABVD regimen (2 cycles), early shifted to BEACOPP-Escalated regimen based on interim PET-CT results showing a partial remission (Table 1). In August 2019, the patient experienced a relapse with axillary lymphadenopathy, and two cycles of third-line chemotherapy using BEGEV scheme were administered, resulting in a complete remission. In January 2020, due to persistent pancytopenia, a bone marrow (BM) evaluation was performed, showing 50% CD34+/CD117 + myeloid blasts, consistent with AML, carrying t(11;20)(p15;q12) on cytogenetics and NUP98::TOP1 rearrangement at FISH in 90% of analyzed nuclei (Table 1; Fig. 1). This discrepancy likely reflects the complex clonal architecture of NUP98-rearranged AML, in which the leukemic clone may extend beyond the morphologically defined blast compartment [2, 5, 6]. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) analysis showed associated subclonal PTPN11 (VAF 3.6%), NRAS (VAF 2.1%) and KRAS (VAF 1.7%) mutations (Table 1). The patient also underwent a total body PET-CT scan, which raised concerns about the potential progression of HL due to the presence of a hypermetabolic extra-osseous focus in the internal mammary area, a previous site of the disease. In February 2020, the patient received induction therapy for AML with CPX-351. BM evaluation was performed at day 52 after treatment because of prolonged cytopenia, and it showed partial disease clearance with residual 5–7% BM blasts and detection of NUP98::TOP1 in 10% of nuclei analyzed by FISH. NGS analysis still detected NRAS at 1.9% VAF (Table 1). PET-CT showed a persistent HL signal in the internal mammary region. In this context, despite evidence of persistent disease, a second cycle of induction therapy was avoided because of the significant prior treatment for HL, the prolonged cytopenia, and the risk of further toxicity. Instead, the conditioning regimen that included a total marrow/lymphoid irradiation-based approach (TMLI) could allow simultaneous treatment of both AML and residual lymphoma. Therefore, allo-HSCT was performed one month later, in May 2020. At that stage (day 87 upon treatment), whilst the percentage of BM blasts appeared stable (about 5%), FISH showed an increase in bulk disease, with NUP98::TOP1 observed in 66% of the nuclei analyzed (Table 1). Nevertheless, allo-HSCT was performed, using a haploidentical donor and our transplant protocol with T-cell adoptive immunotherapy (Supplementary Fig. 1) [13, 14]. The myeloablative conditioning regimen included TMLI (20.0 Gy to the marrow, 11.5 Gy to lymph nodes and spleen) delivered in 10 fractions over 5 days, with a personalized program including a boost on the site of active lymphoma, followed by thiotepa (7.5 mg/kg), fludarabine (150 mg/m2) and cyclophosphamide (30 mg/kg). The patient received an infusion of 2 × 106/kg freshly isolated donor T regulatory cells (Treg) on day − 4, followed by 1 × 106/kg donor T conventional cells (Tcon) and 10.7 × 106 ± 3.4/kg purified CD34 + hematopoietic progenitor cells on day 0. No pharmacological graft-versus-host disease (GvHD) prophylaxis was given post-transplant, in accordance with our center protocol, which uses Treg to control the alloreactivity of Tcon [13]. After allo-HSCT, BM evaluation and PET-CT showed complete remission of both AML and HL. No acute nor chronic GVHD occurred. Nine months after transplant, the patient experienced a moderate thrombotic microangiopathy, characterized by a transient hemolytic anemia, hypertension, and mild renal dysfunction, without thrombocytopenia, and treated with eculizumab for six months, with a favorable clinical response. The patient is now in good clinical condition and remains in complete response 69 months after transplantation (Table 1).

The second patient is a 57-year-old man with a history of anaplastic T cell lymphoma, ALK-negative, with extra-nodal involvement (stage IVA), diagnosed in 2018 (Table 1). He first received 6 cycles of CHOEP regimen, followed, due to early relapse, by a second-line therapy with 14 cycles of Brentuximab-Vedotin plus autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT), which resulted in a complete and stable remission. In March 2021, 20 months after ASCT, laboratory tests showed neutrophilic leukocytosis, anemia, and thrombocytopenia. BM evaluation showed 35% CD34- myeloid blasts with monocytic differentiation (CD64+/-(70%), CD36+/-(60%)) at immunophenotype; BM karyotyping and FISH revealed a t(11;20)(p15;q12) with NUP98::TOP1 in 90% of analyzed nuclei (Fig. 1); NGS analysis showed GATA2 (VAF 43.19%) and NRAS (VAF 25.4%) mutations (Table 1). In May 2021, induction with CPX-351 resulted in only partial disease clearance, with a residual 10% BM blasts, confirmed by FISH (9% of nuclei analyzed). NGS analysis detected GATA2 and NRAS at VAFs of 2% and 2.6%, respectively (Table 1). Despite the persistent disease, considering the heavy pretreatment and the prolonged cytopenia upon the first cycle of chemotherapy, we decided to proceed directly to allo-HSCT using an HLA-matched family donor and our transplant protocol with T-cell adoptive immunotherapy [13, 14]. A TMLI-based myeloablative conditioning regimen was administered as described for the first case. Donor Tregs (2 × 106/kg) were infused on day − 4 followed by Tcon (1 × 106/kg) and 11.4 × 106/kg CD34+ purified hematopoietic progenitor cells on day 0. No pharmacological GvHD prophylaxis was given post-transplant [13]. The patient didn’t experience serious adverse events related to transplant, and neither acute nor chronic GVHD was observed. He is alive, in complete remission for both lymphoma and AML, and in excellent clinical condition at 55 months since transplantation (Table 1).

NUP98-rearranged AML that occurs after prior malignancies presents challenges for clinical decisions due to previous treatments and chemo-refractoriness, as shown in the two cases described above. Among NUP98 rearrangements, the NUP98::TOP1 fusion is a sporadic variant reported in larger cohorts, of which treatment responses are not specifically detailed [12]. CPX-351 induced only a partial reduction of leukemia bulk in both patients (Table 1). However, a second cycle was not administered due to the extensive prior treatments, the risk of prolonged cytopenias and other possible side effects that could potentially delay allo-HSCT. Therefore, proceeding directly to allo-HSCT with active disease was driven by clinical urgency. Considering the persistence of the disease at the time of transplantation, a TMLI- and immunotherapy-based allo-HSCT was chosen, and the bone marrow irradiation dose was escalated from standard 13.5 Gy to 20 Gy [14].

Within the genomic context of NUP98r leukemias, the NUP98::TOP1 fusion is associated with a recurrent pattern of co-occurring somatic mutations that may contribute to leukemogenesis (i.e. WT1, NRAS, or CEBPA mutations) [2, 7]. In both patients, we found mutations in N-/KRAS and PTPN11, as previously described, and in one, a GATA2 mutation involving the ZF2 domain (not previously reported). GATA2 mutations are known to be germline predisposing mutations. Although in our patient the VAF at diagnosis was high (43%), raising doubt about germline origin, the marked decrease in VAF after treatment (43% to 2%) supports a somatic origin of the mutation.

These two patients were treated with a transplant platform that is based on the adoptive transfer of Treg and Tcon (Supplementary Fig. 1). Donor Treg adoptive immunotherapy allows for the infusion of donor Tcon without the need for pharmacologic GvHD prophylaxis. This strategy is designed to preserve graft-versus-leukemia (GvL) activity while minimizing GvHD and its application resulted in low relapse rates in patients with high-risk AML [13]. In both cases, the combination of irradiation-based conditioning and donor T-cell adoptive immunotherapy, without post-transplant pharmacologic immunosuppression, was associated with durable disease control despite active leukemia at transplant. While causality cannot be established, these findings suggest that immune-mediated mechanisms may have contributed to long-term disease control.

In conclusion, NUP98::TOP1-rearranged AML represents a rare and high-risk clinical entity with limited available outcome data. The present report documents an uncommon sustained long-term remission in two heavily pretreated patients transplanted with active disease. While these findings do not allow conclusions about treatment efficacy, they provide clinically meaningful observations and warrant further investigation of immune-based transplant strategies in this ultra-high-risk setting.

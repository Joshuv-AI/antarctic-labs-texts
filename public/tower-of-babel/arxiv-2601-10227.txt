Unrefinable Partitions into Distinct Parts and Numerical Semigroups
Lorenzo Campioni

Abstract
This article investigates structural connections between unrefinable partitions into distinct parts and numerical semigroups. By analysing the hooksets of Young diagrams associated with numerical sets, new criteria for recognising unrefinable partitions are established. A correspondence between missing parts and the gaps of numerical semigroups is developed, extending previous classifications and enabling the characterisation of partitions with maximal numbers of missing parts. In particular, the results show that certain families of unrefinable partitions correspond precisely to symmetric numerical semigroups when the maximal part is prime. Further structural consequences, examples, and a decomposition of unrefinable partitions by minimal excludant are discussed, together with implications for the study of maximal unrefinable partitions.



keywords
:Integer partitions into distinct parts, Numerical semigroups, Combinatorics.

1
Introduction

Unrefinable partitions into distinct parts were introduced in
[
ACGS22
]
, where the authors established a connection with the generators of a chain of normalizers. This chain of normalizers was initially defined in
[
ACGS21a
]
starting from the normalizer
N_{n}^{0}
in
Sym(2^{n})
of the translation group on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and subsequently defining the
i
-th term
N^{i}_{n}
as
N_{Sym(2^{n})}(N_{n}^{i-1})
. In
[
ACGS21b
]
the authors proved that the quantity
\log_{2}\left|N_{n}^{i}\colon N_{n}^{i-1}\right|
is independent of
n
for
1\leq i\leq n-2
and in particular it is equal to the
(i+2)
-th term of the sequence of the partial sums of the sequence
\{b_{j}\}
, where
b_{j}
denotes the number of partitions of
j
into at least two distinct parts. Finally, in
[
ACGS22
]
, it was observed that
\log_{2}\left|N_{n}^{n-1}\colon N_{n}^{n-2}\right|
is related to the number of unrefinable partitions of
n
satisfying a condition on their minimal excludant, i.e., the smallest positive integer that does not appear in the partition.

The condition of being unrefinable imposes a non-trivial constraint on the possible distributions of the parts in a partition. In
[
ACCL23
]
, Aragona et al. constructed two algorithms: one to determine whether a given sequence is an unrefinable partition and the other to enumerate all unrefinable partitions of a given weight. Later, the same authors in
[
ACCL22
]
provided a classification of maximal unrefinable partitions for triangular numbers, and in
[
ACC22
]
, they extended this classification to the general case. These classifications establish explicit bijections between unrefinable partitions and suitable subsets of partitions into distinct parts.

In this paper, we prove a connection between unrefinable partitions and numerical semigroups, which are additive submonoids of the non-negative integers that contain
0
and have finite complement in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
. This relationship allows us to develop new criteria for recognising unrefinable partitions, by analysing the hooksets of the Young tableaux associated with the corresponding numerical semigroup (see
Lemma

6
). Moreover, this connection enables the description of further subsets of unrefinable partitions that fix the largest part and maximise the number of missing parts. In this setting, we identify a relation between these subsets and the set of symmetric numerical semigroups
(SNS)
, particularly when the largest part
\lambda_{t}
is a prime number.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
, we introduce some basic properties of unrefinable partitions and numerical semigroups. Section
3
is dedicated to exploring and describing the relationships between them.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Unrefinable Partitions into Distinct Parts

Definition 1
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t})
be a partition into distinct parts, i.e.,
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{t}
, with
t\geq 2
. The
set of missing parts
of
\lambda
, denoted
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}=\{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m}\}
, consists of all integers less than or equal to
\lambda_{t}
that do not appear in
\lambda
:

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}\colon=\{1,2,\ldots,\lambda_{t}\}\setminus\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t}\}.

The smallest missing part is called the
minimal excludant
of
\lambda
, written as
\mathrm{mex}(\lambda)=\mu_{1}
, with the convention
\mathrm{mex}(\lambda)=0
if
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}=\emptyset
.

Definition 2
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t})
of
N
into distinct parts, with missing parts
\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\dots<\mu_{m}
, is said to be
refinable
if there exist indices
1\leq\ell\leq t
and
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq m
, with
k\geq 2
, such that
\mu_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\mu_{i_{k}}=\lambda_{\ell}
. Otherwise,
\lambda
is
unrefinable
. The set of unrefinable partitions is denoted by
\mathcal{U}
, and
\mathcal{U}_{N}
denotes the subset of partitions of
N
.

For example,
\lambda=(1,2,3,5,6,8,9,11,13)
is refinable since
11=4+7
, while
\lambda=(1,2,3,5,6,8,9,13)
is unrefinable.
The condition of being unrefinable imposes non-trivial constraints on the largest part and on the distribution of parts.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t})
be a partition into distinct parts. If
\#\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}=\{0,1\}
, then
\lambda\in\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

No part of the partition can be expressed as a sum of two or more distinct missing parts, hence
\lambda
is unrefinable.
∎

The converse does not hold in general. For instance,
\lambda=(1,2,3,5,6,8,9,13)
is unrefinable with
\#\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}=5
.

The following definition will be useful in the formulation of the next property and in the derivation of subsequent results.

Definition 3
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
the
n-th triangular number
is

T_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}.

The
complete partition

\pi_{n}=(1,2,\ldots,n)
is the partition of
T_{n}
with no missing parts. For
n\geq 3
and
1\leq d\leq n-1
, let
T_{n,d}=T_{n}-d
and define
\pi_{n,d}=(1,\ldots,d-1,d+1,\ldots,n)\vdash T_{n,d}
.

Note that
T_{n-1}<T_{n,d}<T_{n}
for every
n\geq 3
and
1\leq d\leq n-1
.

Corollary 1
.

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
N>2
, the set
\mathcal{U}_{N}
is nonempty.

Proof.

If
N=T_{n}
, then
\pi_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{N}
by Lemma
1
. Otherwise, there exists
1\leq d\leq n-1
such that
N=T_{n,d}
, and
\pi_{n,d}\in\mathcal{U}_{N}
.
∎

The following result bounds the number of missing parts in an unrefinable partition.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t})
be an unrefinable partition and let
\mu_{1}<\cdots<\mu_{m}
be the missing parts. Then the number of missing parts
m
satisfies the following upper bound:

m\leq\left\lfloor\dfrac{\lambda_{t}}{2}\right\rfloor.

(1)

Proof.

Each missing part
\mu_{i}
must have a counterpart
\lambda_{t}-\mu_{i}\in\lambda
, otherwise
\lambda_{t}=(\lambda_{t}-\mu_{i})+\mu_{i}
would yield a refinement.
Considering the complete partition
\pi_{\lambda_{t}}
and removing as many parts as possible different from
\lambda_{t}
gives the stated bound.
∎

Proposition 1
.

If
\lambda
has a refinement, its smallest refinable part
\lambda_{r}
can be written as
\lambda_{r}=a+b
, where
a,b\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{r}
be the smallest refinable part and
\lambda_{r}=\mu_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\mu_{i_{t}}
a refinement.
If
t=2
, the statement holds. Otherwise, set
a=\mu_{i_{1}}
and
b=\mu_{i_{2}}+\cdots+\mu_{i_{t}}
.
If
b\in\lambda
, then
b<\lambda_{r}
would be refinable, contradicting the minimality of
\lambda_{r}
. Hence
b\notin\lambda
, proving the claim.
∎

Remark 1
.

Let
\lambda
be an unrefinable partition, and suppose
\mu_{1}=\mathrm{mex}(\lambda)
and
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
. By definition, the element
\mu_{i}+\mu_{1}
does not belong to
\lambda
. Analogously, the element
\mu_{i}+2\mu_{1}=(\mu_{i}+\mu_{1})+\mu_{1}\notin\lambda
and, by induction, any
x\equiv\mu_{i}\pmod{\mu_{1}}
with
x\geq\mu_{i}
is not contained in
\lambda
.

To analyse the structure of unrefinable partitions, a vector
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
is introduced. It records, for each residue class modulo the minimal excludant
\mu_{1}=\mathrm{mex}(\lambda)
, thresholds associated with the arithmetic relations generated by the missing parts. These thresholds allow to check refinability once the vector is constructed.

Let
\lambda
be a partition with missing parts

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}=\{\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\dots<\mu_{m}\}
.
The vector

\vec{p}_{\lambda}=(p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{\mu_{1}-1})

is constructed iteratively, following the strategy introduced in
[
ACCL23
]
. The method is adapted to the present setting by modifying some of the technical steps originally designed for computational efficiency, while preserving the overall structure of the construction. All entries are initially set to
\infty
.

Each missing part
\mu_{i}
with
i\geq 2
produces the arithmetic progression

\mu_{1}+k\mu_{i},\qquad k\geq 1,

whose terms fall into well-defined residue classes modulo
\mu_{1}
. Each integer of the progression is considered as a potential contributor to a refinement. If such element appears in
\lambda
, this provides a refinement in
\lambda
. Therefore, the progression terms are used to update the corresponding entry of
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
.

The residue classes affected by the progression depend on
\gcd(\mu_{1},\mu_{i})
, according to the following cases:

•

If
\gcd(\mu_{1},\mu_{i})=1
, the progression covers all residue classes modulo
\mu_{1}
, and each term may update the corresponding entry of
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
. Then
1\leq k\leq\mu_{1}
.

•

If
\gcd(\mu_{1},\mu_{i})=d\notin\{1,\mu_{1}\}
, then the progression occupies exactly the residue classes of the subgroup generated by
\mu_{i}
in
\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{1}\mathbb{Z}
, and only those entries
p_{j}
may be updated. Then
1\leq k\leq\frac{\mu_{1}}{d}
.

•

If
\gcd(\mu_{1},\mu_{i})=\mu_{1}
, all integers of the form
\mu_{1}+k\mu_{i}
lie in the zero residue class, so only
p_{0}
may be updated.

For each subsequent missing part
\mu_{i}
, let
r\equiv\mu_{i}\pmod{\mu_{1}}
. If the current entry
p_{r}
is already smaller than
\mu_{i}
, no update is required. Otherwise,
p_{r}
is set to
\mu_{i}
, and the following steps are performed:

1.

Progression updates.
Update the entries of
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
using all integers of the progression

\mu_{1}+k\mu_{i},\qquad k\geq 1,

according to the same rules as above;

2.

Mixed sum updates.
For each entry
p_{j}
in a different residue class from
\mu_{i}
, update the entry corresponding to the residue class of
p_{j}+\mu_{i}
modulo
\mu_{1}
;

3.

Closure under new values.
Each newly updated entry
p_{s}
is processed in the same way: for all sums of the form

p_{s}+p_{j}

with
p_{j}
in a different residue class, update the corresponding entries if a smaller value is obtained.

Repeat this iterative procedure for all subsequent missing parts
\mu_{i}
.

Definition 4
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition into distinct parts. The vector
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
, constructed as above, is called the
vector of forbidden elements
. For each residue class
0\leq i\leq\mu_{1}-1
, the entry
p_{i}
is the smallest integer congruent to
i
modulo
\mu_{1}
that, if it were present in
\lambda
, could participate in a refinement of the partition.

Remark 2
.

Each entry
p_{i}
can be seen as a threshold. A partition
\lambda
is unrefinable if and only if all its parts are strictly smaller than the corresponding
p_{i}
. Any part
\lambda_{\ell}\geq p_{i}
could participate in a refinement, making
\lambda
refinable. Hence, the vector provides a simple criterion to check refinability.

Remark 3
.

In the previous example, some entries of the vector
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
remain equal to
\infty
. This indicates that, in principle, additional parts could be inserted in the partition respecting the corresponding residue classes, without immediately generating a refinement. Such a situation motivates the technical definition of unrefinable sequences of parts.

Definition 5
.

Let
\lambda=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots)
be a finite or infinite sequence of distinct positive integers. The sequence
\lambda
is called an
unrefinable sequence of parts
if no element of
\lambda
can be expressed as a sum of two or more distinct integers not contained in the sequence. In the finite case, these integers coincide with the usual missing parts of a partition of a number.

Example 3
.

Consider the sequence of all odd numbers,
\lambda=(1,3,5,\dots)
.
All missing parts are even, so their sums cannot produce new odd parts. Therefore,
\lambda
forms an infinite unrefinable sequence.

Definition 6
.

Let
\lambda=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{l})
be an unrefinable sequence of parts with

\mathrm{mex}(\lambda)=\mu_{1}
. The vector
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
is
saturated
if all its entries are finite, that is,

\#\{p_{j}\leq l\;\mid\;0\leq j\leq\mu_{1}-1\}=\mu_{1}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\lambda
be an unrefinable sequence of parts with
\#\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}\geq 2
. If there exists another missing part
\mu_{i}
such that
\gcd(\mu_{1},\mu_{i})=1
, then
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
is saturated and
\lambda
has finite length. Otherwise, the sequence may continue indefinitely, giving an infinite unrefinable sequence.

Proof.

If
\gcd(\mu_{1},\mu_{i})=1
, the progression
\mu_{1}+k\mu_{i}
eventually fills all
\mu_{1}
residue classes modulo
\mu_{1}
, and the vector
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
becomes saturated. Hence, no further parts can be added, giving a finite sequence.

If
\gcd(\mu_{1},\mu_{i})=d\neq 1
, only
\mu_{1}/d
residue classes are affected, so
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
is not saturated. Consequently, additional parts can be inserted indefinitely, leading to an infinite sequence.
∎

2.2
Numerical Semigroups

This section introduces numerical semigroups, which provide a fundamental tool for analysing unrefinable partitions.

Definition 7
.

A
numerical set

S
is a subset of the non-negative integers
\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
0\in S
and the complementary set
S^{c}=\mathbb{N}_{0}\setminus S
is finite. A numerical set
S
is a
numerical semigroup
if it is an additive submonoid of
\mathbb{N}_{0}
, i.e., the sum of any two elements of
S
also belongs to
S
. The set of all numerical semigroups is denoted by
NS
.

The set
S^{c}
is called set of
gaps
and its cardinality
G(S)
is called
genus
of
S
.

The maximal element
F(S)
of
S^{c}
is called
Frobenius number
. The
multiplicity

M(S)
of
S
is the smallest non-zero element of
S
.

Definition 8
.

Let
A
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
The submonoid generated by
A
is

\left<A\right>=\{i_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}a_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N},\ \{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}\subseteq A,\ i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}.

If a numerical semigroup
S
satisfies
S=\left<A\right>
, then
A
is called a

system of generators
of
S
.

A system of generators
A
is
minimal
if

S\neq\left<B\right>
for every proper subset
B\subset A
.
Every numerical semigroup admits a unique minimal system of generators
(see, for instance,
[
RGS09
]
).
This unique set is denoted by
msg(S)
, and its cardinality

e(S)=\#msg(S)
is called the
embedding dimension
of
S
.

Example 4
.

Let
S=\{0,3,6,8,9,11,12,14,\rightarrow\}
, where
\rightarrow
indicates that all integers greater than
14
belong to
S
. Then
S
is a numerical semigroup whose set of gaps is
S^{c}=\{1,2,4,5,7,10,13\}
, genus
G(S)=7
, Frobenius number
F(S)=13
and multiplicity
M(S)=3
. Moreover,
msg(S)=\{3,8,16\}
and thus
e(S)=3
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
A
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then
\left<A\right>
is a numerical semigroup if and only if
gcd(A)=1

Proof.

See
[
RGS09
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

Definition 9
.

Let
S
be a numerical semigroup and
n\in S\setminus\{0\}
. For
1\leq i\leq n-1
, let
w(i)
be the smallest element of
S
such that
w(i)\equiv i\pmod{n}
. The set
Ap(S,n)=\{0,w(1),\ldots,w(n-1)\}
is called the
Apéry set
of
S
with respect to
n
.

Example 5
.

Let
S=\{0,3,6,8,9,11,12,14,\rightarrow\}
. Then:

•

Ap(S,3)=\{0,16,8\}
;

•

Ap(S,5)=\{0,6,12,8,9\}
.

Keith and Nath showed in
[
KN11
]
that every numerical set uniquely defines a integer partition. Indeed, given a numerical set

S=\{0,s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},\rightarrow\}

a Young diagram
Y_{S}
can be constructed by drawing a contiguous polygonal path from the origin in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
as follows. For each integer
k
from
0
to
F(S)
,

•

draw an
east
step if
k\in S
,

•

draw a
north
step if
k\notin S

Note that the Young diagram
Y_{S}
has
G(S)
rows and
n
columns, where
S=\{0,s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},\rightarrow\}
.

Example 6
.

Let
S=\{0,3,6,8,9,11,12,14,\rightarrow\}
. The associated Young diagram

\lambda_{S}=(7,5,3,2,2,1,1)
.

This construction defines a bijection between numerical sets and integer partitions.

Definition 10
.

Given a cell
c
in a Young diagram, the
arm
of
c
is the number of cells to its right in the same row, and the
leg
of
c
is the number of cells below it in the same column. The
hook
of
c
is defined as the sum of its arm and leg, plus one. The set of all hook lengths of the diagram is called the
hookset
.

The following result, proved in
[
TKG19
]
, relates the hookset of a Young diagram to the numerical semigroup associated with it.

Proposition 2
.

Let
S=\{0,s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},\rightarrow\}
be a numerical set with corresponding Young diagram
Y_{S}
. Then:

1.

The hook length of the cell in the first column and
i
-th row is the
i
-th gap of
S
;

2.

For each
0\leq i\leq n-1
the hook length of the top cell of the
i
-th column of
Y_{S}
is
F(S)-s_{i}
;

3.

S is a numerical semigroup if and only all hook lengths appear in the first column.

Proof.

Let
1\leq j\leq n-1
be the smallest integer such that
s_{j}\neq j
, so that
s_{1}^{c}=j
, where
S^{c}=\{s_{1}^{c},s_{2}^{c},\ldots,F(S)\}
. Hence, the first row of
Y_{S}
contains
j
east steps, and the hook length of its first cell is
s_{1}^{c}=j
. Proceeding by induction, assume that the length of each hook in the first column, from the first row to the
i
-th row, coincides with the gaps of
S
, hence the hook length of the cell in the first column and
i
-th row is
s_{i}^{c}
. Let
k
be such that
s_{k-1}<s_{i}^{c}<s_{k}
. If
s_{k}\neq s_{i}^{c}+1
, then
s_{i+1}^{c}=s_{i}^{c}+1
and the
(i+1)
-th row of
Y_{S}
has the same number of columns of the
i
-th row, so the hook length is
s_{i}^{c}+1
. Otherwise, if
s_{k}=s_{i}^{c}+1
, be
j>k
the lowest integer such that
s_{j}\neq s_{i}^{c}+j+1-k
. Then
j-k
integers lie between
s_{i}^{c}
and
s_{i+1}^{c}=s_{i}^{c}+j+1-k
, or in other words the
(i+1)
-th row of
Y_{S}
has
j-k
columns more than the
i
-th row. This proves item
(1)
.

Now, the upper left corner hook coincides with
F(S)=F(S)-s_{0}
. Assume that the hook in the
i
-th column is
F(S)-s_{i}
. Let
k=s_{i+1}-s_{i}
. After the
i
-th column, the path in
Y_{S}
contains
k-1
north steps, then the hook of the
(i+1)
-th column is equal to
F(S)-s_{i}-(k-1)-1=F(S)-k=F(S)-s_{i+1}
, establishing item
(2)
.

Item
(3)
remains to check. Let
S=\{s_{0},\ldots,s_{n},\rightarrow\}
be a numerical semigroup. Notice that every
\widehat{S}
such that
\widehat{S}^{c}=\{s_{1}^{c},\ldots,s_{i}^{c}\}
, where
s_{i}^{c}<F(S)
, is a numerical semigroup. Furthermore, by item
(2)
, the hook of the
i
-th row and
j
-th column in
Y_{S}
is equal to
s_{i}^{c}-s_{j}
. Suppose that exists a cell with hook length
s_{i}^{c}-s_{j}
that doesn’t appear in the first column, so this number is not in
S^{c}
. Then
s_{h}=s_{i}^{c}-s_{j}\in S
, but
s_{h}+s_{j}=s_{i}^{c}\in S
, a contradiction. Conversely, if all the cells in the
i
-th row are signed by numbers appearing in the first column, then, by item
(1)
, they are elements of
S^{c}
. Thus, no two elements of
S
sum to
s_{i}^{c}
, and it follows that
S
is a numerical semigroup.
∎

Definition 11
.

A numerical semigroup
S
is called
symmetric
if
F(S)
is odd and if
x\in S^{c}
implies
F(S)-x\in S
. The family of symmetric numerical semigroups is denoted by

A numerical semigroup is

or

.

Let

be a numerical semigroup. Then:

3
Connection between Unrefinable Partitions and Numerical Semigroups

Let
S=\{0,s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},\rightarrow\}
be a numerical semigroup and
S^{c}=\{s_{1}^{c},\ldots,s_{t}^{c}\}
be the set of its gaps. By the definition of a numerical semigroup, the sum of any two elements
s_{i},s_{j}\in S
cannot belong to
S^{c}
. In other words, each element of
S^{c}
cannot be written as a sum of two or more elements from
S
. Therefore, the set of gaps
S^{c}
of a numerical semigroup corresponds to an unrefinable partition
\lambda
. More precisely, the following correspondences hold:

\displaystyle S^{c}=\{s_{1}^{c},\ldots,s_{t}^{c}\}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t})

\displaystyle S=\{0,s_{1},\ldots,s_{t}+1,\rightarrow\}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\{0\}\cup\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}\cup\{\lambda_{t}+1,\rightarrow\}

\displaystyle G(S)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle len(\lambda)

\displaystyle F(S)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\lambda_{t}

\displaystyle M(S)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\mathrm{mex}(\lambda)=\mu_{1}

An additional correspondence can also be identified: the Apéry set with respect to
s_{1}
,
Ap(S,s_{1})
, coincides with the vector of forbidden elements of the unrefinable partition
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
, except for the first position. In the Apéry set, the first element is
0
, while in the vector
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
the first component is
ks_{1}
for some
k>1
.

Example 8
.

Let
S=\{0,4,7,8,10,11,12,14,\rightarrow\}
be a numerical semigroup. Its complement is
S^{c}=\{1,2,3,5,6,9,13\}
, which determines the unrefinable partition
\lambda=(1,2,3,5,6,9,13)
. Observe that
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}=\{4,7,8,10,11,12\}
coincides with
S^{c}\setminus(\{0\}\cup\{14,\rightarrow\})
. Furthermore, the number of gaps
G(S)=7=len(\lambda)
, the Frobenius number
F(S)=13=\lambda_{7}
, and
M(S)=4=\mathrm{mex}(\lambda)
. The Apéry set of
S
with respect to
4
is
\{0,17,10,7\}
, while the vector
\vec{p}_{\lambda}=\{8,17,10,7\}
.

As a direct consequence of these correspondences, the following result is obtained.

Proposition 3
.

Let
NS
be the set of numerical semigroups and let
\mathcal{U}
denote the set of unrefinable partitions into distinct parts. Then,

NS\subset\mathcal{U}.

The converse inclusion does not hold. If
\lambda\in\mathcal{U}
, then, by definition, no part
\lambda_{i}\in\lambda
can be expressed as the sum of two equal missing parts. For example, if
\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
, then
2\mu_{1}
might be in
\lambda
, that contradicts the definition of a numerical semigroup, according to which every positive multiple of an element in the semigroup must also belong to it. Thus, any unrefinable partition
\lambda
, and more generally, any partition into distinct parts, defines a numerical set
S_{\lambda}
such that
S_{\lambda}^{c}=\lambda
.

The following example shows that
S_{\lambda}
is not necessarily a numerical semigroup.

Example 9
.

The partition
\lambda=(1,2,5,6,8)
is unrefinable, while the corresponding set
S_{\lambda}=\{0,3,4,7,9,\rightarrow\}
is not a numerical semigroup, since
6=3+3
and
8=4+4
are not in
S_{\lambda}
.

By the characterisation of hooksets of numerical semigroups (Proposition
2
), an analogous statement can be established for unrefinable partitions.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t},\rightarrow)
be an unrefinable partition corresponding to the Young tableau
Y_{S_{\lambda}}
, where
S_{\lambda}
is the numerical set associated with
\lambda
. Then

1.

The hook length of the cell in the first column and
i
-th row is
\lambda_{i}
;

2.

For each
2\leq i\leq\#\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
the hook length of the top cell of the
i
-th column of
Y_{S_{\lambda}}
is equal to
\lambda_{t}-\mu_{i-1}
;

3.

\lambda
is an unrefinable partition if and only every length of the hook of the cells of

Y_{S_{\lambda}}

(a)

is contained in the first column
Y_{S_{\lambda}}
;

or

(b)

the length of the hook of the cell in the first column and the same row is its double.

Proof.

The proofs of item
(1)
and item
(2)
are the same of the proofs of item
(1)
and item
(2)
in Proposition
2
, and it is true in general for all partitions into distinct parts.

Let
\lambda
be an unrefinable partitions. If
\lambda
corresponds to a numerical semigroup
S_{\lambda}
then, by Proposition
2
item
(3)
, all the cells are marked with numbers appearing in the first column, so the statement
(a)
is proved. If
S_{\lambda}\notin NS
, then there exists
\mu_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
such that
k\mu_{j}\in\lambda
. In particular
k=2
, otherwise the partition is refinable. By the fact that
2\mu_{j}\in\lambda
there exists one row whose its first cell is marked by
2\mu_{j}
(item
(1)
) and all the other cells in the row are such that
2\mu_{j}-s_{i}
, with
s_{i}\in S_{\lambda}
and
s_{i}<\mu_{j}
. So there is a cell marked by
2\mu_{j}-\mu_{j}=\mu_{j}
. If neither the condition
(a)
nor the condition
(b)
are satisfied, then there exists a cell marked by
x
such that it does not appear in the first column, so, by item
(1)
,
x\notin\lambda
. Moreover, let
z
be the first element in the same row of
x
. Then
x=z-s_{i}
for some
s_{i}\in S_{\lambda}
. Since
z\neq 2x
and
z=x+s_{i}
, a contradiction arises because
\lambda
is refinable.

Conversely, if only condition
(a)
is satisfied, then
S_{\lambda}
is a numerical semigroup and
\lambda
is unrefinable. If conditions
(a)
or
(b)
are verified, then there exists a cell not in the first column signed by
\mu_{j}
and the first element of its row is
2\mu_{j}
, therefore
2\mu_{j}\in\lambda
by item
(1)
, which cannot be replaced by the sum
\mu_{j}+\mu_{j}
, so the partition is unrefinable.
∎

A subset of unrefinable partitions, that will be useful for establishing relations with numerical semigroups, is now introduced.

Definition 12
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t})
be an unrefinable partition, and denote by
\mathcal{U}(\lambda_{t})
the set of the unrefinable partitions whose maximal part is equal to
\lambda_{t}
. Consider the subset of
\mathcal{U}(\lambda_{t})
consisting of all partitions with the maximal number of missing parts

\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})=\left\{\lambda\in\mathcal{U}(\lambda_{t})\mid\#\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}=\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}

Recall that
\lambda_{t}=F(S)
. Since the following lemma concerns numerical semigroups, it is more convenient to state it with
\lambda_{t}
as a generic integer.

Lemma 7
.

Let
NS(k)
be the set of numerical semigroups
S
such that
F(S)=k
. Then

\#NS(k)<\#\mathcal{U}(k).

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Proposition
3
.
∎

Some structural properties of partitions in
\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})
are now considered.

Lemma 8
.

If
\lambda\in\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})
and
x\neq\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}
, then
x\in\lambda
if and only if
\lambda_{t}-x\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

By the definition of unrefinable partitions, if
\lambda_{t}-x\notin\lambda
then
x
must be a part of
\lambda
. Conversely, suppose
x\in\lambda
and
\lambda_{t}-x\in\lambda
. Since the number of missing parts is
\#\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}=\left\lfloor\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}\right\rfloor
, then there must exist
y\notin\lambda
such that
\lambda_{t}-y\notin\lambda
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 9
.

If
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t})\in\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})
, then
\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}\notin\lambda
.

Proof.

If
\lambda_{t}
is an odd number, then the element
\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}
is not an integer, which means that it cannot appear in
\lambda
.

Otherwise, if
\lambda_{t}
is even, then the interval
[1,\lambda_{t}-1]
contains exactly
\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}
missing parts. Let
x\in[1,\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}-1]
, by Lemma
8
,
x\notin\lambda
implies
\lambda_{t}-x\in\lambda
, and conversely. Thus, in
[1,\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}-1]\cup[\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}+1,\lambda_{t}-1]
there are at most
\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}-1
missing parts, hence
\frac{\lambda_{t}}{2}
must be missing as well.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})
and let
\lambda_{t}
be an odd integer such that
\lambda_{t}\neq 3\mu_{i}
for all
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
. Then the numerical set
S_{\lambda}
associated to
\lambda
is a numerical semigroup.

Proof.

Suppose
S_{\lambda}
is not a numerical semigroup. Then there exists
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
such that
k\mu_{i}\in\lambda
. Since
2\mu_{i}\notin\lambda
implies that no multiples of
\mu_{i}
belong to
\lambda
, it suffices to consider
k=2
. So
2\mu_{i}\in\lambda
. By Lemma
8
, this yields
\lambda_{t}-2\mu_{i}\notin\lambda
.

If
\lambda_{t}-2\mu_{i}\neq\mu_{i}
, then
\lambda_{t}-2\mu_{i}+\mu_{i}=\lambda_{t}-\mu_{i}\notin\lambda
, contradicting Lemma
8
.Instead, if
\lambda_{t}-2\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}
, then
\lambda_{t}=3\mu_{i}
, which leads to another contradiction.
∎

Example 11
.

Let
\lambda=(1,2,3,4,7,9,10,15)
and
\mu=(1,2,3,5,7,9,11,15)

\#\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}=\#\mathcal{M}_{\mu}=7=\left\lfloor\frac{15}{2}\right\rfloor

10,15\notin S_{\lambda}
, is not a numerical semigroup, while
S_{\mu}=\{0,4,6,8,10,12,13,14,16,\rightarrow\}
is.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})
, such that
\lambda_{t}
is an odd integer coprime with
3
, then the numerical set associated
S_{\lambda}
is a numerical semigroup.

This result follows directly from
Lemma

10
, but it is possible to state even a stronger version.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})
, such that
\lambda_{t}>3
is a prime number, then the numerical set associated
S_{\lambda}
is a numerical semigroup.

Remark 4
.

Note that the case
\lambda_{t}=3
is excluded. That is because if we consider the partition
\lambda=(2,3)\in\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})
the corresponding numerical set
S_{\lambda}
is not a numerical semigroup

Theorem 1
.

Let
\lambda_{t}>3
be a prime number. Then we have

\#\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})=\#\left\{S\in SNS\mid F(S)=\lambda_{t}\right\}.

Proof.

The claim follows from
Corollary

3
and Definition
11
.
∎

A decomposition of
\mathcal{U}(\lambda_{t})
via the minimal excluded part is now introduced.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\lambda_{t},\mu_{1})=\{\lambda\in\mathcal{U}(\lambda_{t})\mid\mu_{1}=\mathrm{mex}(\lambda)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t},\mu_{1})=\left\{\lambda\in\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})\mid\mu_{1}=\mathrm{mex}(\lambda)\right\}

Given
\lambda\in\mathcal{\bar{U}}(\lambda_{t})
with
\lambda_{t}
prime number and
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
its vector of forbidden integers (see Definition
4
), Theorem

ensures that
S_{\lambda}
is a numerical semigroup. Then
(\vec{p}_{\lambda})_{1}=2\mu_{1}
and the other components are the minimal integers not in
\lambda
, congruent to
i

(\mod{\mu_{1}})
for
2\leq i\leq\mu_{1}
.

If there exists
1\leq i\leq\mu_{1}
such that
(\vec{p}_{\lambda})_{i}\neq(\vec{p}_{\lambda})_{j}+(\vec{p}_{\lambda})_{k}
for
1\leq j<k\leq\mu_{1}
such that
j,k\neq i
, a new unrefinable partition
\lambda^{*}\in\mathcal{U}(\lambda_{t},\mu_{1})
such that
\lambda^{*}=\lambda\cup\left\{(\vec{p}_{\lambda})_{i}\right\}
may be obtained. The vector
\vec{p}_{\lambda^{*}}
changes from
\vec{p}_{\lambda}
only in the position

(\vec{p}_{\lambda^{*}})_{i}=(\vec{p}_{\lambda})_{i}+\mu_{1}
.

Definition 13
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. An unrefinable partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t})
of
N
is called
maximal
if

\lambda_{t}=\max_{(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{t})\in\mathcal{U}_{N}}\eta_{t}.

Denote by
{\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{N}}
the set of the maximal unrefinable partitions of
N
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{U}}
be the subset of
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
defined as follows

\begin{split}\widehat{\mathcal{U}}=&\left\{(1,\ldots,2k-3,4k-6)\mid k\geq 4\right\}\\
&\bigcup\left\{(1,\ldots,2k-2,4k-5)\mid k\geq 4\right\}\bigcup\{\widetilde{\pi}_{n}\mid n\geq 6\}.\end{split}

The subset of maximal unrefinable partitions
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\setminus\widehat{\mathcal{U}}
is contained in the set of unrefinable partitions with maximal number of missing parts
\mathcal{\bar{U}}
, in other words

\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\setminus\widehat{\mathcal{U}}\right)\subseteq\bar{\mathcal{U}}

Proof.

In
[
ACCL22
]
and in
[
ACC22
]
the authors construct maximal unrefinable partitions. In particular in
[

, Theorem

]
, the maximal unrefinable partition

is obtained from
\pi_{n}
removing three parts and adding two new elements. Then
\#\mathcal{M}_{\widetilde{\pi}_{n}}=n-3<n-2=\left\lfloor\frac{2n-4}{2}\right\rfloor
. Hence, for every
n\geq 6
the partition
\widetilde{\pi}_{n}\notin\bar{\mathcal{U}}
.

In the first part of

, hence
\lambda\notin\bar{\mathcal{U}}
.

Similarly, in

1. Introduction

All rings in this paper are associative and unital. An element
a
of a ring
R
is called right strongly
\pi
-regular if
a^{n}R=a^{n+1}R
for some positive integer
n
. If
n=1
, then
a
is called right strongly regular. Left strongly
\pi
-regular and left strongly regular elements are defined similarly. An element which is both left and right strongly
\pi
-regular (left and right strongly regular) is called strongly
\pi
-regular (respectively, strongly regular). A ring in which every element is right strongly
\pi
-regular (right strongly regular) is called a right strongly
\pi
-regular (respectively, right strongly regular). The left versions of these rings are defined similarly. For about twenty five years since these rings were defined by Kaplansky in
[
11
]
, the left and right strongly
\pi
-regular rings were studied separately. But Dischinger
[
4
]
proved that a ring is left strongly
\pi
-regular if and only if it is right strongly
\pi
-regular. We simply call these rings as strongly
\pi
-regular now. But the result of Dischinger is not true locally. For instance, if
R
is not Dedekind finite and there exist
a,\,b\in R
such that
ab=1
but
ba\neq 1
, then
aR=a^{2}R=R
but
Ra\neq Ra^{2}
. But Azumaya
[
1
, Theorem 1]
proved that if
a
is an element of a ring
R
with bounded index of nilpotence and
aR=a^{2}R
, then
Ra=Ra^{2}
. Hartwig and Luh in
[
10
, Page 94]
asked if the result of Azumaya holds if
R
is Dedekind finite. This question of Hartwig and Luh was answered in negative in
[
5
]
where a ring
R
with only trivial idempotents is constructed which has an element a such that
aR=a^{2}R
but
Ra\neq Ra^{2}
. As
\mathbb{M}_{n}(S)
, for a commutative ring
S
, is Dedekind finite, it is natural to ask if every right strongly
\pi
-regular element in this ring is left strongly
\pi
-regular. Recently, Cǎlugǎreanu and Pop
[
3
, Conjecture 3.7]
conjectured that in
\mathbb{M}_{n}(S)
, for a commutative ring
S
, a left strongly regular element is right strongly regular. In this short note we prove that for any
A\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
,
S
commutative, and for any positive integer
n
,
A^{n}\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)=A^{n+1}\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
implies
\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)A^{n}=\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)A^{n+1}
. We give three different proofs of this result. In particular, this validates the conjecture of Cǎlugǎreanu and Pop
[
3
, Conjecture 3.7]
.

2. Results

To make the paper self-contained, we first list some results needed from the literature and their consequences. The following result is classically known. For its history and a proof kindly see
[
9
, Corollary 1.2]
. Let
f(x)\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(S)[x]
, where
S
is a commutative ring. We can regard
f(x)
to be an element of
\mathbb{M}_{n}(S[x])
and so we can define
\rm{det}(f(x))
naturally.

Proposition 2.1.

Let
S
be a commutative ring. If
A\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(S)
satisfies
f(x)\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(S)[x]
, then
A
satisfies
\rm{det}(f(x))I
, where
I
is the identity matrix of
\mathbb{M}_{n}(S)
.

In
[
5
, Definition 3.1]
an element
a\in R
is said to be right exchange if for any right ideal
I
of
R
with
aR+I=R
, there exists an idempotent
e\in aR
such that
1-e\in I
. A ring
R
is an exchange ring if all its elements are right exchange.

Theorem 2.2
[
5
, Theorem 3.4 and Corollary 3.5]
.

Let
R
be a Dedekind finite exchange ring. Then
aR=a^{2}R
if and only if
Ra=Ra^{2}
for any
a\in R
. Also an element of
R
is right strongly
\pi
-regular if and only if it is left strongly
\pi
-regular.

The following result was proved by Drazin
[
6
, Theorem 4]
(see also
[
7
, Corollary 1.2]
).

Theorem 2.3
[
6
, Theorem 4]
.

If
a
is a strongly
\pi
-regular element of a ring
R
and
a^{n}=a^{n+1}x
, then for
w=a^{n}x^{n+1}
we have
aw=wa
,
a^{n}=a^{n+1}w
.

We will need the following corollary of the above result. This was proved in
[
5
, Lemma 4.4]
and we give a slightly different proof.

Corollary 2.4
[
5
, Lemma 4.4]
.

Let
S
be a subring of a ring
R
and
a\in S
be such that
a^{n}S=a^{n+1}S
for some positive integer
n
. If
a
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
R
, then
Sa^{n}=Sa^{n+1}
.

Proof.
Let
a^{n}=a^{n+1}x
for some
x\in S
. As
a
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
R
, so by Theorem 2.3 for
w=a^{n}x^{n+1}\in S
we have
wa=aw
and
a^{n}=a^{n+1}w
. Thus
Sa^{n}=Sa^{n+1}
. ∎

In
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
Fisher and Snider proved that a ring
R
is strongly
\pi
-regular if it so modulo every prime ideal. Their proof actually gives the following result.

Theorem 2.5
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Let
a\in R
be such that
a+P
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
R/P
for every prime ideal
P
of
R
, then
a
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
R
.

Proposition 2.6.

Let
R
be a ring whose every prime factor ring embeds in a Dedekind finite exchange ring. Then
a^{n}R=a^{n+1}R
implies
Ra^{n}=Ra^{n+1}
for any
a\in R
and any positive integer
n
.

Proof.
Let
a^{n}R=a^{n+1}R
for some
a\in R
and a positive integer
n
. In view of Theorem 2.3 it is enough to prove that
a
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
R
. Let
P
be a prime ideal of
R
. By Theorem 2.5 it is enough to prove that
a+P
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
R/P
. Suppose
R/P
embeds in a Dedekind finite exchange ring
S
. As
a+P
is right strongly
\pi
-regular in
S
, so by Theorem 2.2
a+p
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
S
. Lastly by Corollary 2.4
a+P
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
R/P
also. ∎

A ring
R
is called
clean
if every element is a sum of a unit and an idempotent. It is well known that every clean ring is an exchange ring.

Proposition 2.7
[
2
, Proposition 2.4]
.

Every commutative ring embeds in a commutative clean ring.

Now we are ready to prove the following result which for
n=1
validates a recent conjecture by Cǎlugǎreanu and Pop in
[
3
, Conjecture 3.7]
.

Theorem 2.8.

Let
A\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
, where
S
is a commutative ring and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any positive integer
n
,
A^{n}\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)=A^{n+1}\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
implies
\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)A^{n}=\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)A^{n+1}
.

In view of Theorem 2.3 it is enough to show that
A
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
.

First Proof.
Let
A^{n}=A^{n+1}B
for some
B\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
. As
A
satisfies a polynomial
x^{n}I-x^{n+1}B\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)[x]
, so by Proposition 2.1
A
also satisfies
\rm{det}(x^{n}I-x^{n+1}B)I
which is a polynomial of the type
Ix^{nm}+s_{1}Ix^{nm+1}+\ldots+s_{m}Ix^{nm+m}\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)[x]
. This implies that
A^{nm}=A^{nm+1}f(A)
for some polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)[x]
implying that
A
is strongly
\pi
-regular
\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
. ∎

Second Proof.
Let
M_{m}(P)
be a prime ideal of
\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
, where
P
is a prime ideal of
S
. Then
\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)/M_{m}(P)\cong\mathbb{M}_{m}(S/P)
. Let
F
be the field of quotients of
S/P
. Then
\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)/M_{m}(P)
embeds in
\mathbb{M}_{n}(F)
which is a Dedekind finite exchange ring and so by Proposition 2.6
A
is strongly
\pi
-regular
\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
. ∎

Third Proof.
By Proposition 2.7
S
embeds in a commutative exchange ring, say
T
. So
\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
embeds in a Dedekind finite exchange ring
\mathbb{M}_{m}(T)
. By Theorem 2.2
A
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
\mathbb{M}_{m}(T)
and by Corollary 2.4
A
is strongly
\pi
-regular in
\mathbb{M}_{m}(S)
. ∎

Introduction

In 1999, Diaz, Pujals, and Ures
[
5
]
proved that robustly transitive diffeomorphisms on three-dimensional manifolds are partially hyperbolic. Important examples of robustly transitive partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms, which are given by Shub
[
31
]
and Mane
[
25
]
in the 70s, are all derived-from-Anosov (i.e. homotopic to Anosov) diffeomorphisms.
However, the classification of all robustly transitive derived-from-Anosov diffeomorphisms remains an open problem, even on the 3-torus. In order to address the problem, Pujals suggested that it might be a good choice to classify derived-from-expanding (i.e. homotopic to expanding) maps that are robustly transitive on the 2-torus
[
3
,
4
]
.

For any continuous map
f
from the 2-torus to itself, there is a unique linear toral endomorphism
A
which is homotopic to
f
. If
A
is expanding, then there is a unique semi-conjugacy between
f
and
A
. In 1969, Shub
[
30
]
proved that if
f
is expanding, then
f
is topologically conjugate to
A
.
Moreover, any expanding endomorphism is topologically conjugate to an affine expanding endomorphism of an infranilmanifold
[
30
,
10
,
11
]
.

We decide to classify derived-from-expanding partially hyperbolic endomorphisms based on topological or smooth conjugacy.

Definition 1.1
.

[
4
]

Let
f:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
be a
C^{1}
endomorphism, i.e., a local diffeomorphism on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
If there is a
Df-
invariant unstable closed convex cone
\mathscr{C}_{x}\subset T_{x}\mathbb{T}^{2}
for all

, i.e.,

Df(x)\mathscr{C}_{x}\subset{\rm int}\mathscr{C}_{fx},\qquad|Df(x)v|>1,\ \text{for all}\,v\in\mathscr{C}_{x}\ \text{with}\ |v|=1,

then
f
is called a
partially hyperbolic endomorphism
.

.

Without loss of generality, we use one time iteration of

instead of

times iterations of

in
[

]

Problem
.

Assume that
f
is a partially hyperbolic endomorphism on

, which is homotopic to an expanding linear map
A
.

1.

It is well known that there is a topological semi-conjugacy
h
, such that

Moreover, what can be said about the smoothness of

In the following, we give some related concepts of partially hyperbolic endomorphisms. Later, we give partial results of the question.

According to

\mathbb{T}^{2}

invariant line bundle

E^{c}_{x}=\bigcap_{n=0}^{+\infty}(Df^{n}(x))^{-1}(\mathscr{C}_{f^{n}x})^{c},

Definition 1.3

Let

\mathbb{T}^{2}

If there is a

Assume that

is a specially partially hyperbolic endomorphism, which is homotopic to an expanding linear map
A

[

After building topologically conjugacy between

A

Below, we briefly recall some earlier results concerning the smooth conjugacy.

In the history, the same periodic data of two smooth maps may imply that they are smooth conjugate, where periodic data refers to the Lyapunov exponents at corresponding periodic points. For
C^{k+\alpha}(k\geq 1)
expanding maps on a circle, it is well known that maps with the same degree are topologically conjugate. Furthermore, if their periodic data are the same, the conjugacy between them is
C^{k}

[
2
]
.
For
C^{k}(k\geq 2)
Anosov diffeomorphisms on a two-dimensional manifold, de la Lave
[
7
]
proved in 1992 that if their periodic data are the same then the conjugacy is
C^{k-\epsilon}
.
In 2008, Gogolev
[
14
]
proved that if their periodic data are the same, then a
C^{2}
Anosov diffeomorphism on the 3-torus, lying within a
C^{1}
-neighborhood of a linear hyperbolic automorphism with real eigenvalues, is
C^{1+\alpha}
conjugated with the linear one.

For partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms, su-integrable may imply the same periodic data in the center direction. In 2020 Gan and Shi
[
12
]
studied a
C^{1+\alpha}
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism

f
which is homotopic to an Anosov automorphism
A
on the 3-torus. They proved that if
f
is su-integrable and topologically conjugate to
A
, then
f
and
A
have the same periodic data in the center direction, and the topological conjugacy between them is differentiable along the center foliation.

For partially hyperbolic endomorphisms, similarly to su-integrability for partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms, being ”special” may imply differentiability of the conjugacy in the stable direction .
Recent results about the smooth conjugacy confirm this principle, including Anosov endomorphisms
[
1
,
26
,
27
]
, Anosov maps
[
15
]
, and derived from non-expanding Anosov endomorphisms
[
16
]
.
In such cases,
f
and its linear part have the same stable periodic data, thus the conjugacy is smooth along the stable foliation.

In the present paper, we investigate the problem of the smooth conjugacy for a
C^{r}
specially partially hyperbolic endomorphism
f
, which is homotopic to an expanding linear endomorphism
A
with irrational eigenvalues. This problem has not been previously studied by
[
1
,
26
,
27
,
15
,
16
]
. Specifically, we can prove that if

is area expanding and the center bundle is
C^{1}
, then the conjugacy is
C^{\max\{r-3,1\}+\alpha}
, i.e., Theorem
1.2

When the semi-conjugacy

Proposition 2.5

1.

If an
\tilde{f}
-invariant foliation

\tilde{h}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}(x)=\tilde{\mathscr{A}}(\tilde{h}x)

Proof.

1.

Suppose
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}d(\tilde{\mathscr{F}}(x),\tilde{\mathscr{L}}(x))=M

From
d(\tilde{h}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}(x),\tilde{\mathscr{L}}(\tilde{h}x))\leq 2||\tilde{h}-id||+M

A\tilde{\mathscr{L}}=\tilde{\mathscr{L}}

2.

Suppose
\tilde{h}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}\neq\tilde{\mathscr{A}}

Proposition 3.6

It implies

3.3
Analytic conjugacy

Lemma 3.13
.

For
A\in GL(2,\mathbb{R})\cap M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with two irrational eigenvalue
\lambda
and
\mu
, neither
\lambda=\mu^{k}
or
\mu=\lambda^{k}
holds when
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Denote
T={\rm tr}(A)
and
D=\det(A)
. Then the eigenvalues of
A
are
\frac{1}{2}(T+\sqrt{\Delta})
and
\frac{1}{2}(T-\sqrt{\Delta})
, where
\Delta=T^{2}-4D
. Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies

\frac{1}{2}(T+\sqrt{\Delta})=(\frac{1}{2}(T-\sqrt{\Delta}))^{k}
or
\frac{1}{2}(T-\sqrt{\Delta})=(\frac{1}{2}(T+\sqrt{\Delta}))^{k}
.
Since the eigenvalues of
A
are irrational, we can get the following equations:

\frac{T}{2}=2^{-k}\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{k}{2}]}C^{2i}_{k}T^{k-2i}\Delta^{i}

and

\frac{1}{2}=-2^{-k}\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{k-1}{2}]}C^{2i+1}_{k}T^{k-1-2i}\Delta^{i}.

The second equation implies that
T<0
and
k
is even. However, these means that the left side of the first equation is negative, but the right side of it is positive, which lead to a contradiction.
∎

For
f
in the Main theorem, consider
\tilde{f}^{-1}
, the inverse of the lift of
f
. If
f
is
C^{\omega}
, then by Lemma
3.13
,
\tilde{f}^{-1}
satisfies the assumption of
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
.
For any
p\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, there exist a neighborhood
U
of
p
and a homeomorphism
\theta:U\to{\rm Emb^{\omega}}([0,1],\mathbb{T}^{2})
, such that
\theta(x)[0,1]=\mathscr{F}^{\sigma}_{\epsilon}(x)
and
\theta(x)(0)=x
, where
{\rm Emb^{\omega}}
is the space of analytic embedding.
In particular, the leaves of
\mathscr{F}^{c}
and
\mathscr{F}^{u}
are analytic.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.3
,
r=\omega
, if
f
is area expanding,
E^{c}
is
C^{1}
,
and
\mathscr{F}^{\sigma}
is an expanding foliation with analytic leaves, then
h
is analytic along
\mathscr{F}^{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x
is a fixed point of
f
, i.e.,
f(x)=x
. Choose a kind of parameter
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{F}^{\sigma}(x)
such that
\gamma(0)=x
and
|\dot{\gamma}(t)|=1
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
f\mathscr{F}^{\sigma}(x)=\mathscr{F}^{\sigma}(fx)=\mathscr{F}^{\sigma}(x)
, there is an analytic function
\Psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(\gamma(t))=\gamma(\Psi(t))
. Since
f
is expanding along
\mathscr{F}(x)
, without loss of generality, there is
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
|\Psi^{\prime}(t)|=||Df|_{E^{\sigma}(\gamma(t))}||>\alpha^{-1}>1
. Taking the derivative of
h\gamma\Psi=hf\gamma=Ah\gamma
at
0
, we have that
\Psi^{\prime}(0)=\lambda^{\sigma}
. Taking the derivative of

h\gamma\Psi^{n}=hf^{n}\gamma=A^{n}h\gamma
at
\Psi^{-n}t
, we have that

||Dh|_{E^{\sigma}(\gamma(t))}||=|\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda^{\sigma}}{\Psi^{\prime}(\Psi^{-i}(t))}|\cdot||Dh|_{E^{\sigma}(\gamma(\Psi^{-n}t)))}||.

Define

F_{n}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\quad F_{n}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(\Psi^{-i}(t))}{\lambda^{\sigma}}.

For any
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, there is an analytic continuation
\Phi
of
\Psi
,

\Phi:\Omega=(-|t_{0}|-1,|t_{0}|+1)\times i(-\xi,\xi)\to\Phi(\Omega),\quad|\xi|<\frac{1}{2},

such that
|\Phi^{\prime}(z)|>\alpha^{-1}>1
. Denote

U=(-|t_{0}|-\xi,|t_{0}|+\xi)\times i(-\xi,\xi)
. Since
\Phi^{k}(k\in\mathbb{N})
is expanding, we have
\Phi^{k}(\Omega)\supset U
, i.e.,
\Omega\supset\Phi^{-k}U
. Then
F_{n}\in C^{\omega}(U)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We will prove that
F_{n}
is uniformly bounded on
U
.
There is a constant
L
such that
|\ln\Phi^{\prime}(z_{1})-\ln\Phi^{\prime}(z_{2})|\leq L|z_{1}-z_{2}|
for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in U
.
For any
z=x+iy\in U
, we have that

\begin{split}\ln\left|\frac{F_{n}(z)}{F_{n}(x)}\right|&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}|\ln\Phi^{\prime}(\Phi^{-i}z)-\ln\Phi^{\prime}(\Phi^{-i}x)|\\
&\leq L\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}|\Phi^{-i}z-\Phi^{-i}x|\\
&\leq\frac{L}{1-\alpha}|z-x|\leq\frac{L\xi}{1-\alpha}.\end{split}

By Livschitz Theorem, there is a Hölder function
\psi:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}

\ln||Df|_{E^{\sigma}(y)}||=\psi(y)-\psi(fy)+\ln\lambda^{\sigma}.

Thus

\ln|\Psi^{\prime}(t)|=\ln||Df|_{E^{\sigma}}(\gamma(t))||=\psi(\gamma(t))-\psi(\gamma(\Psi(t)))+\ln\lambda^{\sigma}.

It implies that
\ln|F_{n}(x)|\leq 2||\psi||_{\infty}<+\infty
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
So
\ln|F_{n}(z)|\leq 2||\psi||_{\infty}+\frac{L\xi}{1-\alpha}=M<+\infty
for all
z\in U
.

We will also prove that
F_{n}
is pointwise convergent on
U
. Since for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{F_{n+p}(z)}{F_{n}(z)}=\prod_{i=n+1}^{n+p}\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(\Phi^{-i}z)}{\lambda^{\sigma}}=\exp(\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+p}\ln\Phi^{\prime}(\Phi^{-i}z)-\ln\Phi^{\prime}(0))

and

|\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+p}\ln\Phi^{\prime}(\Phi^{-i}z)-\ln\Phi^{\prime}(0)|\leq L\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+p}|\Phi^{-i}z|\leq L\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+p}\alpha^{i}|z|\leq\frac{L}{1-\alpha}|z|\alpha^{n}\to 0\quad(n\to+\infty),

we have that

|F_{n}(z)-F_{n+p}(z)|=|F_{n}(z)|\cdot|1-\frac{F_{n+p}(z)}{F_{n}(z)}|\leq e^{M}\cdot|1-\frac{F_{n+p}(z)}{F_{n}(z)}|\to 0

when
n
goes to infinity. So
F_{n}
is pointwise convergent to
F
on
U
.

Now we will prove that
F_{n}
is uniformly convergent to
F
on
K
, where
K
is a compact set in
U
. By Cauchy inequality, since
F_{n}
is uniformly bounded on
U
,
F_{n}^{\prime}
is uniformly bounded on
K
, thus
F_{n}
is equicontinuous on
K
. For any
\epsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
, such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
|F_{n}(z_{1})-F_{n}(z_{2})|<\frac{1}{3}\epsilon
for any
|z_{1}-z_{2}|\leq\delta
in
K
. There are
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
in
K
such that
\cup_{1\leq i\leq k}B(w_{i},\frac{1}{2}\delta)\supset K
. There is
N
such that for any
1\leq i\leq k
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and
n>N
, we have
|F_{n}(w_{i})-F_{n+p}(w_{i})|<\frac{1}{3}\epsilon
. For any
z\in K
, there is
1\leq j\leq k
, s.t.
z\in B(w_{j},\frac{1}{2}\delta)
. For any
n>N
, we have that

|F_{n}(z)-F_{n+p}(z)|\leq|F_{n}(z)-F_{n}(w_{j})|+|F_{n}(w_{j})-F_{n+p}(w_{j})|+|F_{n+p}(w_{j})-F_{n+p}(z)|<\epsilon.

By Morera’s Theorem, we can get that
F
is analytic on
U
, so
F
is analytic at
t_{0}
.
So
||Dh|_{E^{\sigma}(\gamma(t))}||=|F(t)^{-1}|\cdot||Dh|_{E^{\sigma}(\gamma(0))}||
is analytic.

Since
\mathscr{A}^{\sigma}(0)
is dense in
\mathbb{T}^{2}
,
h^{-1}\mathscr{A}^{\sigma}(0)=\mathscr{F}^{\sigma}(h^{-1}0)
is dense in
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. For any
y
in
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, there are
s_{n}
such that
\gamma(s_{n})\to y
when
n\to+\infty
.

Since
f
is analytic at
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, we claim that
\xi
can be chosen independently of
t_{0}
.
Consider homeomorphisms
\theta:U\to{\rm Emb^{\omega}}([0,1],\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
\theta^{\prime}:fU\to{\rm Emb^{\omega}}([0,1],\mathbb{T}^{2})
. Denote
G_{x}(t)=\theta^{\prime}(fx)^{-1}\circ f\circ\theta(x)(t)
, which is analytic about
t
and varies continuously about
x
.
Denote by
R(x)
the analytic radius of
G_{x}(t)
at
0
.
Denote

B(x)=\{y\in U:|(G_{x})^{(k)}(0)-(G_{y})^{(k)}(0)|\leq 1,\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}.

We will prove that
R(y)\geq R(x)
for any
y\in B(x)
.
For any
\rho>\frac{1}{R(x)}
,

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\sqrt[k]{\frac{|(G_{x})^{(k)}(0)|}{k!}}=\frac{1}{R(x)}<\rho,

so there exists
K_{1}
such that
|(G_{x})^{(k)}(0)|<k!\cdot\rho^{k}
for any
k>K_{1}
.
Let
\lceil\frac{1}{\rho}\rceil=N
, then
\rho(N+1)>1
. So

k!\cdot\rho^{k}>N!\rho^{N}\cdot(\rho(N+1))^{k-N}\to+\infty\quad(k\to+\infty).

There exists
K_{2}>K_{1}
such that
k!\cdot\rho^{k}>1
for any
k>K_{2}
. Then

\begin{split}\frac{1}{R(y)}&=\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\sqrt[k]{\frac{|(G_{y})^{(k)}(0)|}{k!}}\\
&\leq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\sqrt[k]{\frac{|(G_{x})^{(k)}(0)|+1}{k!}}\leq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\sqrt[k]{\frac{k!\cdot\rho^{k}+1}{k!}}\\
&\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}\sqrt[k]{\frac{2k!\cdot\rho^{k}}{k!}}=\rho\end{split}

Since
\rho
is arbitrary, we get that
\frac{1}{R(y)}\leq\frac{1}{R(x)}
, i.e.,
R(y)\geq R(x)
.
For any
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, there exists
B(x)
such that
R(y)\geq R(x)
. Since
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is compact, there are
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
such that
\{B(x_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
cover
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Let
\xi=\min_{1\leq i\leq m}R(x_{i})
, then
\inf_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}R(y)\geq\xi
. Thus the analytic continuation
\Phi
of
\Psi
can be defined on the uniform
\xi
neighborhood of real line.

Notice that

||Dh|_{E^{\sigma}(y)}||=\lim_{n\to+\infty}||Dh|_{E^{\sigma}(\gamma(s_{n}))}||=\lim_{n\to+\infty}|F(s_{n})^{-1}|\cdot||Dh|_{E^{\sigma}(\gamma(0))}||.

Since
F(s_{n}+z)
is analytic when
|z|<\xi
, uniformly bounded, and equicontinuous, by Ascoli Lemma and Morera’s Theorem,
||Dh|_{E^{\sigma}(\cdot)}||
is analytic along
\mathscr{F}^{\sigma}(y)
.
∎

If
f

3.1

and

. Furthermore, by de la Llave’s analytic version of Journe’s Lemma

This finishes the proof of the 4th item of Main Theorem.

Elimination ideals of Plücker ideals and algebras with straightening laws
Viktoriia Borovik, Takayuki Hibi

Abstract
. It is well known that the Plücker ideal defining the Grassmannian is generated by quadratic Plücker relations. These relations form a reverse lexicographic Gröbner basis and endow the Plücker algebra with the structure of an algebra with straightening laws (ASL). In this paper, we study quadratically generated projections of the Grassmannian of lines Gr ⁡ ( 2 , n ) \mathrm{Gr}(2,n) . We then combinatorially characterize the Gorenstein ASL subalgebras of the Plücker algebra of Gr ⁡ ( 2 , n ) \mathrm{Gr}(2,n) .

Introduction

Binomials and monomials have long been central objects of study in combinatorics and commutative algebra. In the early days, squarefree monomial ideals, the so-called Stanley-Reisner ideals
[
14
,
16
]
, associated with simplicial complexes and with finite partially ordered sets, were thoroughly investigated. In the late 1990’s, subalgebras of a polynomial ring over a field generated by monomials, and their defining ideals, the so-called toric ideals
[
11
,
18
]
, became fashionable with the help of Gröbner bases. In the early 2010’s, the study of powers of edge ideals and of binomial edge ideals
[
7
,
8
]
quickly developed into a large subarea.

In this paper, following classical works of Plücker
[
12
,
13
]
and Grassmann
[
4
]
and from the viewpoint of algebras with straightening laws
[
3
]
, we study a subalgebra of the polynomial ring generated by binomials whose defining ideal is an elimination ideal (see, for example,
[
10
, Theorem 1.4.1]
) of the Plücker ideal.

Let
A=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
denote the polynomial ring in
2n
variables over a field
\mathbb{K}
, and let

L_{n}=\{\,p_{ij}:1\leq i<j\leq n\,\}
be the distributive lattice with partial order defined by
p_{ij}\leq p_{k\ell}
if
i\leq k
and
j\leq\ell
. The homogeneous coordinate ring

{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbb{K}}[L_{n}]=\mathbb{K}[\,x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}:1\leq i<j\leq n\,]

of the Grassmannian
\Gr(2,n)
is an algebra with straightening laws on
L_{n}
over
\mathbb{K}
, via the identification
p_{ij}\mapsto x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
. The Plücker ideal
I_{L_{n}}
, which is the defining ideal of
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbb{K}}[L_{n}]
in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[\,p_{ij}:1\leq i<j\leq n\,]
, is generated by the quadratic Plücker relations

(1)

Q_{ijk\ell}:=p_{i\ell}p_{jk}-p_{ik}p_{j\ell}+p_{ij}p_{k\ell},\hskip 20.00003pt1\leq i<j<k<\ell\leq n.

Our goal is to find a sublattice
L\subset L_{n}
with the following properties:

(i)

the subalgebra
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbb{K}}[L]\subset{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbb{K}}[L_{n}]
generated by those
p_{ij}=x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
with
p_{ij}\in L
is an algebra with straightening laws on
L
over
\mathbb{K}
;

(ii)

the defining ideal
I_{L}
of
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbb{K}}[L]
in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[\,p_{ij}:p_{ij}\in L\,]
is an elimination ideal of
I_{L_{n}}
;

(iii)

{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbb{K}}[L]
is Gorenstein.

The outline of the present paper is as follows. In Section
1
, we briefly review algebras with straightening laws. In Section
2
, we introduce a specific lexicographic order
<_{\operatorname{lex}}
for which the quadratic Plücker relations (
1
) form a Gröbner basis of the Plücker ideal
I_{L_{n}}
. We then study distinguished sublattices
L
of

(
compatible
and

sublattices) such that the defining ideal
I_{L}
of the subalgebra

of

is the elimination ideal
I_{L_{n}}\cap\mathbb{K}[p_{ij}:p_{ij}\in L]
of the Plücker ideal.

Although our primary objects are sublattices of
L_{n}
, the combinatorial aspects can be described elegantly via finite graphs. In Section

, to each sublattice

we associate a finite graph

on the vertex set

\{i,j\}
precisely when

for which
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbb{K}}[L]
is Gorenstein (Corollary

In the Appendix

1.
Algebras with straightening laws

Let
R=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{n}
be a noetherian graded algebra over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
. Let
P
be a finite poset (partially ordered set) and suppose we are given an injection

\varphi:P\hookrightarrow\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}R_{n}

such that
R
is generated as a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra by
\varphi(P)
. A
standard monomial
is a homogeneous element of
R
of the form
\varphi(\gamma_{1})\varphi(\gamma_{2})\cdots\varphi(\gamma_{n})
, where
\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\gamma_{n}
in
P
(with the empty product interpreted as
1
). We say that
R
is an
algebra with straightening laws

[
3
]
on
P
over
\mathbb{K}
if the following conditions hold:

(ASL -1)
The set of standard monomials forms a
\mathbb{K}
-basis of
R
.

(ASL -2)
If
\alpha
and
\beta
are incomparable in
P
and if

(2)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\varphi(\alpha)\varphi(\beta)=\sum_{i}r_{i}\,\varphi(\gamma_{i_{1}})\varphi(\gamma_{i_{2}})\cdots\varphi(\gamma_{i_{n_{i}}}),\,\text{ with }0\neq r_{i}\in{\mathbb{K}},\,\,\,\gamma_{i_{1}}\leq\gamma_{i_{2}}\leq\cdots

is the unique expression for
\varphi(\alpha)\varphi(\beta)\in R
as a
\mathbb{K}
-linear combination of distinct standard monomials guaranteed by (ASL-1), then
\forall\,i:\gamma_{i_{1}}\leq\alpha,\beta
.

The right-hand side of the relation in (ASL-2) is allowed to be the empty sum (i.e.,
0
). We abbreviate “algebra with straightening laws” as ASL. The relations in (ASL-2) are called the
straightening relations
for an algebra
R
.

Let
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{\alpha}:\alpha\in P]
denote the polynomial ring in
|P|
many variables over the field
\mathbb{K}
, and define the surjection
\pi:S\rightarrow R
by
\pi(x_{\alpha})=\varphi(\alpha)
. The defining ideal
I_{R}
of
R=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{n}
is the kernel
\ker(\pi)
of
\pi
. If
\alpha,\beta\in P
are incomparable, define

f_{\alpha,\beta}:=x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}-\sum_{i}r_{i}\,x_{\gamma_{i_{1}}}x_{\gamma_{i_{2}}}\cdots x_{\gamma_{i_{n_{i}}}},\,\text{ with }0\neq r_{i}\in{\mathbb{K}},\,\,\,\gamma_{i_{1}}\leq\gamma_{i_{2}}\leq\cdots,

arising from (ASL-2). Then we have that
f_{\alpha,\beta}\in I_{R}
. Let
G_{R}
denote the set of all polynomials
f_{\alpha,\beta}
with
\alpha,\beta
incomparable in
P
. Let
<_{\mathrm{rev}}
denote the reverse lexicographic order
[
6
, Example 2.1.2(b)]
on
S
induced by an ordering of the variables such that
x_{\alpha}<_{\mathrm{rev}}x_{\beta}
whenever
\alpha<\beta
in
P
. It follows from (ASL-1) that
G_{R}
is a Gröbner basis of
I_{R}
with respect to
<_{\mathrm{rev}}
. In particular,
G_{R}
generates
I_{R}
.

We briefly recall some basics on finite posets. A
chain
in a finite poset
P
is a totally ordered subset of
P
. The
length
of a chain
C
is
|C|-1
, where
|C|
denotes the cardinality of
C
. The
rank
of
P
is the largest length of a chain in
P
; we denote it by
\operatorname{rank}(P)
. A finite poset is called
pure
if all of its maximal chains have the same length. If
R
is an ASL on
P
over
\mathbb{K}
, then
\dim R=\operatorname{rank}(P)+1
.

Lemma 2.3

If

(“if”)

(“only if”)

\displaystyle p_{ik}p_{j\ell}-p_{ij}p_{k\ell}=\sum_{q=1}^{t}c_{q}p_{i_{q}j_{q}}p_{k_{q}\ell_{q}}.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
L
be a compatible sublattice of
L_{n}
. Then
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{K}}[L]
is an ASL on
L
over
\mathbb{K}
. In particular,
I_{L}
is quadratically generated and
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{K}}[L]
is Cohen–Macaulay. Furthermore,
I_{L}
is an elimination ideal of the Plücker ideal

.

Proof.

Let
p_{i\ell}
and
p_{jk}
with
i<j<k<\ell
belong to
L
. Since
L_{n}\setminus L
is a poset ideal of
\Pi_{n}
, both
p_{ij}
and
p_{k\ell}
belong to
L
. It then follows from Lemma
2.3
that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbb{K}}[L]
is an ASL on
L
over
\mathbb{K}
. In particular,

(6)

\displaystyle I_{L}=(Q_{ijk\ell}:1\leq i<j<k<\ell\leq n,\;p_{i\ell}\in L).

Since every sublattice of a distributive lattice is distributive and since a distributive lattice is wonderful
[
3
, p. 263]
, it follows from
[
3
, Corollary 4.2]
that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathbb{K}}[L]
is Cohen–Macaulay.

Fix the lexicographic order
<_{\operatorname{lex}}
on the polynomial ring
S=\mathbb{K}[p_{ij}:1\leq i<j\leq n]
induced by the variable order such that

(i)

p_{k\ell}<_{\operatorname{lex}}p_{ij}
if
p_{ij}<p_{k\ell}
in
\Pi_{n}
;

(ii)

p_{k\ell}<_{\operatorname{lex}}p_{ij}
if
p_{ij}\not\in L
and

.

It follows from (
6
) that

(7)

\displaystyle I_{L}=(Q_{ijk\ell}:1\leq i<j<k<\ell\leq n)\cap\mathbb{K}[\,p_{i\ell}:p_{i\ell}\in L].

Since
p_{k\ell}<_{\operatorname{lex}}p_{ij}
whenever
p_{ij}\notin L
and
p_{k\ell}\in L
, and since the set of quadratic Plücker relations (
4
) forms a Gröbner basis of
I_{L_{n}}
with respect to
<_{\operatorname{lex}}
(Lemma

), it follows from (

) that

is an elimination ideal of

, as desired.

Appendix A
Another Gröbner basis for the Plücker ideal

In Section
2
we provided a lexicographic Gröbner basis for the Plücker ideal

I_{L_{n}}
associated with the finite poset
\Pi_{n}
.
In this appendix, we construct another lexicographic Gröbner basis for a
natural lexicographic ordering of the Plücker variables.
Before stating this result, we first prove a combinatorial lemma.

Richard Stanley recorded 214 combinatorial interpretations of the Catalan numbers in his monograph
[
15
]
. The following lemma adds one more to this list.

Lemma A.1
.

Let arcs
(a,b)
be drawn on
n
points
1,\dots,n
placed on a horizontal line. Suppose the arrangement avoids the following patterns:

•

two disjoint arcs
(i,j)
and
(k,l)
with
1\leq i<j<k<l\leq n
;

•

three arcs
(i,k),(j,l),(k,m)
with
1\leq i<j<k<l<m\leq n
;

•

three arcs
(i,l),(j,m),(k,s)
with
1\leq i<j<k<l<m<s\leq n
.

Then:

(1)

every non-maximal arrangement extends to one with
2n-3
arcs;

(2)

the maximum number of arcs in such an arrangement is
2n-3
;

(3)

the number of maximal arrangements equals the Catalan number
C_{n-2}
.

Proof.

By the first forbidden pattern, no two arcs are disjoint, hence any two distinct arcs
(a,b)
and
(c,d)
either intersect
(a<c\leq b<d)
or are nested
(a\leq c<d\leq b)
.

Define the length of an arc
(a,b)
to be
b-a
.
There is at most one arc of length
n-1
, namely
(1,n)
.
For any
1\leq\ell\leq n-2
, there are at most two arcs of length
\ell
. Indeed, three distinct arcs of the same length cannot be nested and therefore must pairwise intersect, which necessarily creates one of the forbidden triples.
Thus the total number of arcs is at most
1+2(n-2)=2n-3
, which proves
(2)
. This bound is attained, for example, by the following arrangement

\{(1,k),(2,l)\mid k\in\{2,\dots,n\},\;l\in\{3,\dots,n\}\}.

We now prove (1) and (3) by induction on
n
.
For
n=2
, the only allowed arrangements are the empty one and the one consisting of a single arc, so both statements hold. To prove (3), we construct a bijection with full binary trees with
n-1
leaves, equivalently, with
n-2
internal nodes. Their number is well-known to be the Catalan number
C_{n-2}
, see
[
15
]
. For instance, for
n=2
the tree consists of a single node corresponding to the arc
(1,2)
.

Now let
n\geq 3
. Observe that the arcs
(1,n)
,
(1,n-1)
, and
(2,n)
can always be added to any allowed arrangement without creating a forbidden pattern. Thus, it suffices to consider arrangements on
n
points containing these three arcs.

Suppose first that all remaining arcs are nested inside a single arc of length
n-2
, say
(1,n-1)
. Removing the vertex
n
together with the arcs
(1,n)
and
(2,n)
yields an allowed arrangement on
n-1
points, which by induction extends to an arrangement
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
with
2n-5
arcs. Adding back the arcs
(1,n)
and
(2,n)
produces an allowed arrangement
\mathcal{A}
on
n
points with
2n-3
arcs, proving (1) in this case. By the induction hypothesis,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
corresponds to a full binary tree rooted at the arc
(1,n-1)
. To construct the tree corresponding to
\mathcal{A}
, we add a new root labeled by
(1,n)
, whose left child is the tree for
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and whose right child is the leaf corresponding to
(2,n)
.

Now assume that not all arcs of length at most
n-3
are nested inside a single arc of length
n-2
, say
(1,n-1)
. Let
(i_{1},n),\dots,(i_{k},n)
be all arcs with right endpoint at
n
.
Replace each arc
(i_{j},n)
by
(i_{j},n-1)
. If this coincides with an existing arc, we keep a single copy. Removing the vertex
n
and all arcs incident to it yields an arrangement on points
1,\dots,n-1
. This squeezing operation does not create forbidden patterns, since all right endpoints move inward while preserving their relative order. Hence the resulting arrangement is allowed.
By induction, it extends to an allowed arrangement
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
with
2n-5
arcs.

Let
(a,n-1)
be the shortest arc with right endpoint
n-1
in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
.
We now reinsert the vertex
n
and replace every arc
(j,n-1)
by
(j,n)
, this again avoids the three forbidden patterns. Finally, we add the two arcs
(1,n)
and
(a,n-1)
. The resulting arrangement
\mathcal{B}
is allowed and has

2n-3

arcs, proving (1) in this case.

On the level of trees,
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
corresponds by induction to a full binary tree
T^{\prime}
rooted at
(1,n-1)
with left and right children
(1,n-2)
and
(2,n-1)
, respectively. Let
(a,n-1)
be the rightmost leaf of minimal length. Removing the subtree rooted at
(2,n-1)
and identifying the right leaf of
(1,n-1)
with
(a,n-1)
produces a tree
T_{1}
. From the removed subtree we form a new tree
T_{2}
by replacing
n-1
with
n
in every node label. The tree corresponding to
\mathcal{B}
is obtained by introducing a new root labeled
(1,n)
with left child
T_{1}
and right child
T_{2}
.

Finally, observe that for every internal node labeled by an arc
(a,b)
, its two children are uniquely determined by the maximal arcs of the form
(a,j)
and
(i,b)
with
j\geq i
nested inside it. The forbidden patterns guarantee both existence and uniqueness of this choice, ensuring that the construction indeed produces a full binary tree.
∎

Proof of Proposition
A.3
.

The listed polynomials (

) clearly generate

since they contain all quadratic Plücker relations

For the chosen lexicographic order (

•

If two quadrics share at most one index, or share two indices that lie in different Plücker variables of the leading terms, then their leading monomials are coprime. By Buchberger’s second criterion, the

•

Suppose they share exactly two indices that occur together in one variable of the leading terms. Up to symmetry (note that

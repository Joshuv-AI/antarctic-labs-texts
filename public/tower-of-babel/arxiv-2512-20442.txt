A Proof of a conjecture of Watanabe–Yoshida via Ehrhart theory
Yakob Kahane

Abstract
. In 2005, Watanabe and Yoshida formulated a conjecture for a lower bound of the Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity of local rings that was recently settled by Meng using analytic methods. More recently, Pak-Shapiro-Smirnov-Yoshida used Ehrhart theory to compute explicitly the multiplicity and reduced the conjecture to showing an inequality of the values of the Ehrhart polynomial of a zigzag poset shifted to t − 1 / 2 t-1/2 . We completely realize their approach to give another proof of this Watanabe–Yoshida conjecture. The main ingredient of the proof relies on a new explicit combinatorial formula for the coefficients of this shifted Ehrhart polynomial. In terms of the generating function of the shifted polynomial, this formula manifests itself as a Hadamard product of the exponential generating function of Euler numbers and an explicit algebraic function.



1.
Introduction

Let
P
be a poset of size
n
. We are interested in the coefficients of the order polynomial

\Omega(P;t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}(P)\,t^{k}.

This polynomial encodes substantial information about
P
. In particular, the leading coefficient of
n!\cdot\Omega(P;t)
is the number of linear extensions of
P
, denoted
e(P)
(see
[
18
, Ch. 3]
), a central and notoriously difficult to compute invariant
[
2
]
.

From a geometric viewpoint, following Stanley
[
20
]
, we define the
order polytope

\mathcal{O}(P):=\{f\in[0,1]^{P}:f(u)\leq f(v)\ \text{whenever }u\preceq v\text{ in }P\}.

The Ehrhart polynomial of
\mathcal{O}(P)
is equal to
\Omega(P;t+1)
. It is well known that the coefficients
n!\,c_{k}(P)
are integers, though they are not always nonnegative. Determining when these coefficients are positive remains an open problem
[
9
]
. A recent result of Ferroni, Morales, and Panova
[
6
]
shows that to prove nonnegativity of all coefficients, it suffices to establish positivity of the linear coefficients of all convex subposets of
P
which are partitions of the elements of the poset (see Definition
3.2
).

Our starting point is a positive formula for each coefficient of the order polynomial, expressed solely in terms of the linear coefficients of convex subposets of
P
. This formula already appears in
[
17
, Prop. 2.9]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

Let
P
be a poset. Then

c_{k}(P)=\frac{1}{k!}\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\mathsf{Decompo}(P)\\
\mu=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}\end{subarray}}e(\mu)\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}c_{1}(P_{i}),

where
\mathsf{Decompo}(P)
denotes the set of all decompositions of
P
into
k
convex subposets,
e(\mu)
is the number of ways to linearly order the blocks of the decomposition, and
c_{1}(P_{i})
is the linear coefficient of the order polynomial of
P_{i}
.

This formula is particularly useful for families of posets that are closed under taking convex subposets. Zigzag posets form such a family. These posets appear in various areas of combinatorics
[
3
,
13
]
, as well as in algebra, geometry, and probability
[
19
,
4
,
5
,
1
]
.

Recently, Pak, Shapiro, Smirnov, and Yoshida
[
14
]
proved that the
Hilbert–Kunz multiplicity
of the simple
(A_{1})
-singularities of dimension
n
and characteristic
p
, denoted
e_{HK}(\mathcal{A}_{p,n})
, satisfies an inequality that can be extracted from
[
14
, Cor. 3.10]
:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
14
]
)
.

For all
p,n
,

(1.1)

e_{HK}(\mathcal{A}_{p,n})\geq 1+\frac{\Omega(Z_{n};p/2-1/2)}{(p/2)^{n}},

where
Z_{n}
is the zigzag poset of size
n
.

This raises the question of whether Ehrhart theory can be used to prove the third part of the Watanabe–Yoshida conjecture (2005). Recently, Meng announced a proof using different analytic methods
[
11
]
.

Conjecture 1.3

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime and

\mathcal{A}_{p,n}:=\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}[[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]]}{(x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2})}.

Then

(1.2)

e_{HK}(\mathcal{A}_{p,n})\geq 1+\frac{E_{n}}{n!},

where
E_{n}
is the Euler number, equivalently the number of linear extensions of the zigzag poset of size
n
.

In this paper, we provide an explicit formula for each coefficient of
\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)
, which we call the
shifted Ehrhart polynomial
of the zigzag poset of size
n
.

Theorem 1.4

((Theorem
4.17
))
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq n
. Then

[t^{k}]\>\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)=\frac{n!}{k!}E_{k}\!\sum_{\mu\in\mathrm{Decompo}_{k}(Z_{n})}w(\mu),

where
w(\mu)
is a weight function described in detail in Section
4.6
.

Several recursive formulas for the ordinary generating function of
\Omega(Z_{n};t)
are known
[
15
, Thm. 3.15]
,
[
18
, Ex. 3.66]
. We give instead a formula for the
exponential
generating function of the shifted order polynomials of zigzag posets, expressed as a Hadamard product
\odot
(in
t
) of the generating function of Euler numbers with an explicit algebraic function. To our knowledge, this is the first non-recursive expression for this generating function.

Theorem 1.5
.

For all
t
and
n\geq 1
,

\sum_{n\geq 0}\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\bigl(\tan t+\sec t\bigr)\ \odot\ G(z;t),

where

G(z;x)=\sqrt{1-z^{2}/4}\,\frac{1+2x\,\arcsin(z/2)}{1-4x^{2}\,\arcsin(z/2)}.

Using this explicit formula, we obtain the following inequality.

Corollary 1.6

(Corollary
5.8
)
.

For
n\geq 8
and
t\geq 3/2
,

\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)\geq t^{n}\frac{E_{n}}{n!}.

By computing the remaining cases of the Hilbert–Kunz multiplicity using the formula of Pak, Shapiro, Smirnov, and Yoshida, we verify that the third part of the Watanabe–Yoshida conjecture holds for all
n\geq 2
and
p\geq 3
. This yields an Ehrhart-theoretic proof of the conjecture, complementing the recent analytic proof announced by Meng
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
5.9
)
.

Conjecture
1.3
holds.

2.3.
Order Polynomial and Ehrhart polynomial

Definition 2.5
.

Let
P=(X,\preceq)
be a partially ordered set (poset) with
|X|=n
.
The
order polynomial
of
P
is the unique polynomial
\Omega(P;t)

\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
such that

(2.1)

\Omega(P;t):=\#\{f:X\to[1,\dots,t]\;\text{ such that }\;f(x)\leq f(y)\text{ whenever }x\preceq y\}.

It is a classical result (see for example
[
22
]
) that this counting function is a polynomial and that

n!\cdot\Omega(P;t)
has integer coefficients. Thus, we may write:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}(P)t^{k}:=n!\,\Omega(P;t),\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}(P)t^{k}:=\Omega(P;t).

Definition 2.6
.

The
order polytope
of
P
, introduced by Stanley
[
20
]
, is defined as

(2.2)

\mathcal{O}(P):=\{f\in[0,1]^{P}\;\text{ such that }\;f(u)\leq f(v)\text{ whenever }u\preceq v\}.

The polytope
\mathcal{O}(P)
is integral. Stanley showed in
[
20
, Thm. 4.1]
that the
Ehrhart polynomial
of
\mathcal{O}(P)
, which counts the lattice points in the dilation
t\cdot\mathcal{O}(P)
, is given by the order polynomial.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
20
]
)
.

For any poset
P
, the Ehrhart polynomial of
\mathcal{O}(P)
satisfies

\#(t\cdot\mathcal{O}(P)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\Omega_{P}(t+1).

In
[
8
]
, Kreweras gave the following determinant identity for
\Omega(P_{\lambda/\mu};t)
:

Theorem 2.8

(Kreweras)
.

(2.3)

\Omega(P_{\lambda/\mu};t)=\det\!\left[\,\binom{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}+t-1}{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}\right]_{i,j=1}^{\ell},

where
\ell
is the length of
\lambda
.

Recently, Ferroni, Morales, and Panova
[
6
, Prop. 3.3]
showed that the coefficients

satisfy the following recurrence:

Lemma 2.9

(
[
6
]
)
.

For any poset
P
and every
k\geq 2
,

c_{k}(P)=\frac{1}{2^{k}-2}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subsetneq P\\
I\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\;\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}c_{i}(I)\,c_{k-i}(P\setminus I)\right),

where the first sum is over ideals
I
of the poset
P
.

They used this recurrence to show that the non-negativity of the coefficients of the order polynomial of a poset
P
follows from the positivity of the linear coefficient of the order polynomial of every convex subposet of
P
.

Definition 2.10
.

The
zeta polynomial
of a poset
P
is a polynomial
Z(P;t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
that enumerates multichains of element in
P
of size
t
.

It is a standard fact that the order polynomial of a poset
P
equals the zeta polynomial of its lattice of order ideals
J(P)
.

The EGF is known; here we are interested only in its odd part.

3.
Decomposition formula

We start by defining the notion of decomposition into convex subposets. It will be the cornerstone for all the results in the next sections. The aim of this section is to give a formula of each coefficient
c_{k}(P)
in terms of the linear coefficients of the convex subposets of

Definition 3.1

(convex subposets)
.

Let

A

Let

4.
The weighted-sum formula

The goal of this section is to prove the weighted-sum formula (Theorem
4.17
). This formula expresses the coefficient
\left[\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)\right]_{t^{k}}
as a weighted sum over all the subposet decomposition of
Z_{n}
into
k
subposet of odd size. The weight can be computed by looking at the Taylor expansion of the series defined in Table
1
. The strategy of the proof is the following. In Section
4.2
, using the Kreweras formula, we compute the linear coefficient and the constant coefficient of
\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)
. In Section
4.3
, we express all terms of the sum of
\left[\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)\right]_{t^{k}}=\big[\sum_{i\geq k}c_{i}(Z_{n})(t-1/2)^{i}\big]_{t^{k}}
by defining a map from the decompositions into
i
subposets to the decompositions into
k
subposets where
i\geq k
. This leads to the definition of a weight function
w
on the subposets. In Section
4.4
, we define the notion of elementary decomposition which is the cornerstone of all the decompositions, then we show that for elementary ones, the weight
w
can be computed using the results of Section
4.2
. Finally, in Section
4.5
we show how we can split every decomposition into elementary decompositions and show that in this case, the weight of every decomposition is multiplicative under this split. We write
f_{n,k}
as the coefficients of
\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)
. More precisely,
\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{n,k}t^{k}
.

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},l_{1})

.

For all

Proof.

Let

(2)

(3)

\displaystyle s(n,i,\mu,l)

\displaystyle=

.

(4.3)

w(M(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}))=w(\mu_{1})\cdot w(\mu_{2})

4.6.
The weighted sum formula

We recall in one place the definition of the weight function for decomposition into subposets. This is a summary of of Lemma
4.14
and Corollary
4.16
.

•

For each subposets
P
of even size,
w(P)=0
.

•

For each subposets
P
of size
2k+1
, with more upper elements than lower elements,
w(P)=w_{-}(2k+1)
.

•

For each
special
subposets
P
of size
2k+1
, with more lower elements than upper elements,
w(P)=U_{2k+1}
.

•

For each
non-special
subposets
P
of size
2k+1
, with more lower elements than upper elements,
w(P)=w_{+}(2k+1)
.

For a decomposition
\mu
into subposets, we let
w(\mu)=\displaystyle\prod_{P\in\mu}w(P)
.

The weight function

describes the coefficients of
\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)
. The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq n
, then the coefficient
f_{n,k}=\left[\Omega(Z_{n};t-1/2)\right]_{t^{k}}
equals:

(4.4)

f_{n,k}=\dfrac{n!}{k!}E_{k}\sum_{\mu\in\mathsf{SDecompo}_{k}(Z_{n})}w(\mu).

Proof.

Using Corollary
4.16
, lemma
4.14
and Lemma
4.11
, we have :

f_{n,k}=\frac{n!}{k!}\sum_{\mu\in\mathsf{SDecompo}_{k}(Z_{n})}w(\mu)e(\mu)

If
\mu
has at least an even-size subposet,
w(\mu)=0
. And if
\mu
has only odd-size subosets
e(\mu)=e({Z_{k}})=E_{k}
, Hence :

f_{n,k}=\dfrac{n!}{k!}E_{k}\sum_{\mu\in\mathsf{SDecompo}_{k}(Z_{n})}w(\mu).

∎

Introduction

The horizontal distribution of the zeros of
\zeta^{\prime}
has been studied by many authors since Levinson and Montgomery
[
12
]
. In
[
15
]
, Soundararajan made the following conjecture: Assume the Riemann Hypothesis. Then

\liminf(\beta^{\prime}-1/2)\log\gamma^{\prime}=0\Leftrightarrow\liminf(\gamma^{+}-\gamma^{-})\log\gamma^{-}=0,

where
1/2+i\gamma^{-}
and
1/2+i\gamma^{+}
denote consecutive zeros on the critical line, and
\beta^{\prime}+i\gamma^{\prime}
denotes a typical zero of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
. In
[
20
]
, Yitang Zhang proved the
\Leftarrow
half of the conjecture. Partial results in the other direction include

\liminf(\beta^{\prime}-1/2)(\log\gamma^{\prime})^{3}=0\Rightarrow\liminf(\gamma^{+}-\gamma^{-})\log\gamma^{-}=0,

due to Fan Ge
[
8
]
. If one also assumes the zeros are simple, results of Garaev and Yildirim in
[
6
]
can be interpreted to say that.

\liminf(\beta^{\prime}-1/2)\log\gamma^{\prime}(\log\log\gamma^{\prime})^{2}=0\Rightarrow\liminf(\gamma^{+}-\gamma^{-})\log\gamma^{-}=0.

In this paper, rather than change the scaling, we look instead at an infinite subset of the zeros. The starting point is the function

\eta(s)=h(s)\zeta^{\prime}(s),\quad\text{ where }\quad h(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),

so named by Zhang in
[
20
]
. This function has an interesting property with respect to the zeros of
\zeta(s)
on the critical line:

Lemma 1
.

(
[
20
, Lemma 1]
) Suppose
t>7
. Then we have
\zeta(1/2+it)=0
if and only if
\text{Re}(\eta(1/2+it))=0
.

The lemma makes the level curves for
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0
of interest. Figure
1
shows examples, where we use color to indicate the sign of
\text{Im}(\eta(s))
.
Green indicates
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0
and
\text{Im}(\eta(s))>0
, while purple indicates
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0
and
\text{Im}(\eta(s))<0
.

For shorthand when referring to ‘the zeros’ of
\zeta(s)
we mean the nontrivial zeros in the upper half plane. The Riemann zeros
\rho=1/2+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
occur where the green and purple contours cross the critical line. The zeros
\rho^{\prime}
of
\zeta^{\prime}
are visible everywhere the two colors come together (exclusive of the double pole at
s=1
).

,

Throughout we assume the Riemann Hypothesis, in the strong form that the nontrivial zeros are also simple.
We also need to assume the following:

Hypothesis D
.

The level curves
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0
are differentiable. This is automatic except at isolated points where
\eta^{\prime}(s)=0
, so we are really assuming that when
\eta^{\prime}(s)=0
,
\arg(\eta(s))\neq\pm\pi/2
. This prevents the level curves from branching. Hypothesis D is plausible because
\pm\pi/2
are only two points on the unit circle, while the zeros of
\eta^{\prime}(s)
form a countable set.

1.
Classification of zeros

Proposition 1
.

With the usual indexing
\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}<\ldots
of the imaginary parts of the zeros of
\zeta(s)
, every odd indexed zero lies on a contour
\text{Im}(\eta(s))<0
. Every even indexed zero lies on a contour
\text{Im}(\eta(s))>0
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2
below, which says that as
t
increases, the argument of
\eta(1/2+it)
decreases by exactly
\pi
between consecutive zeros. A
Mathematica
calculation of
\eta(1/2+i\gamma_{1})
determines the parity of all the zeros.
∎

Zeros of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)

Type 0:

We will say a zero
\rho^{\prime}
of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
is of
type 0
if
neither
of the level curves
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0,\text{Im}(\eta(s))>0
and
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0,\text{Im}(\eta(s))<0
exiting
\rho^{\prime}
cross the critical line
\sigma=1/2
.

Type 1:

We will say a zero
\rho^{\prime}
of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
is of
type 1
if
exactly one
of the level curves
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0,\text{Im}(\eta(s))>0
and
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0,\text{Im}(\eta(s))<0
exiting
\rho^{\prime}
crosses the critical line
\sigma=1/2
.

Type 2:

We will say a zero
\rho^{\prime}
of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
is of
type 2
if the level curves
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0,\text{Im}(\eta(s))>0
and
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0,\text{Im}(\eta(s))<0
exiting
\rho^{\prime}

both
cross the critical line
\sigma=1/2
.

Zeros of
\zeta(s)

Type 1:

We will say a zero
\rho=1/2+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
is of
type 1
if the level curve
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0
on which it lies, terminates in a zero
\rho^{\prime}
which is of type 1.

Type 2:

We will say a zero
\rho=1/2+i\gamma
of
\zeta(s)
is of
type 2
if the level curve
\text{Re}(\eta(s))=0
on which it lies, terminates in a zero
\rho^{\prime}
which is of type 2.

In Figure
1
, when both branches form a loop to the left, it is type 2. When they loop to the right, it is type 0. If the two colors extend in opposite directions without looping, it is type 1. In Figure
1
, the first four zeros of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
have type 2; the next four alternate between types 1 and 2. The first zero of type 0 occurs at height about 113, with another at height about 132. At height about 161 we have two consecutive zeros of type 1, but from the way the graphics are imported into Latex one can not tell, looks like it might be a type 2 and type 0. It seems a zero of
\eta^{\prime}(s)
is nearby.

Let

N_{1}(T)=\sharp\left\{\text{type 1 zeros }1/2+i\gamma\,|\,0<\gamma<T\right\}.

NB: This is a not the traditional definition of
N_{1}(T)
.
Let

N_{2}(T)=\sharp\left\{\text{pairs of type 2 zeros }1/2+i\gamma^{-},1/2+i\gamma^{+}\,|\,0<\gamma^{+}<T\right\}.

For
j=0,1,2
, let

N_{j}^{\prime}(T)=\sharp\left\{\text{zeros }\rho^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}+i\gamma^{\prime}\text{ of type }j\,|\,0<\gamma^{\prime}<T\right\}.

Theorem 1
.

Every Riemann zero is of either type 1 or type 2. Thus we have a canonical mapping from the zeros of
\zeta(s)
to those of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
, which is two to one on the type 2 zeros, and one to one on the type 1 zeros. Zeros of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
of type 0 are precisely those not in the image of this mapping. The Riemann zeros of type 2 are canonically grouped in pairs.

There are infinitely many type 2 zeros of
\zeta(s)
and of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
, and in fact

N_{2}(T)=N_{2}^{\prime}(T)\gg T.

At least one of the other types of zeros of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
is infinite in number.

Proof.

Regarding the mapping, all this is clear except the first statement, which says that the contours which cross the critical line from the left must terminate in exactly one zero of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
. Since we are assuming Hypothesis D, the alternatives we must rule out are continuation of the contour on to the right, or looping back to the left.

For the first possibility, note that the contour
\arg(\eta(s))=\pi/2
(resp.
\arg(\eta(s))=-\pi/2
) does not exist in isolation; it is part of a continuum which deform smoothly as the argument is varied.
By Lemma
2
at the end of this section, the argument is decreasing along the contour
s=1/2+it
, as
t>4
increases.
But the argument of
\eta(s)
is increasing along the contour
4+it
as
t
increases, by Lemma
4
and the subsequent remark. To change the orientation, the contours must cross over each other, and this can only happen where the argument of
\eta(s)
is undefined, at a zero
\rho^{\prime}
.

The second possibility is ruled out by Lemma
2
, which says that the argument of
\eta(s)
decreases monotonically as one moves up the critical line.

Regarding the asymptotics, we have (due to Littlewood, as we are assuming the Riemann Hypothesis)

(1)

N_{1}(T)+2N_{2}(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O\left(\log T/\log\log T\right).

Via the mapping,
N_{1}(T)=N_{1}^{\prime}(T)
and
N_{2}(T)=N_{2}^{\prime}(T)
.
Thus we have from
[
7
]
:

(2)

N_{0}^{\prime}(T)+N_{1}(T)+N_{2}(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\left(\frac{T}{4\pi}\right)-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O\left(\log T/\log\log T\right).

Subtracting (
2
) from (
1
) gives

(3)

N_{2}(T)-N_{0}^{\prime}(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\left(2\right)+O\left(\log T/\log\log T\right).

Subtracting (
1
) from twice (
2
) gives

(4)

N_{1}(T)+2N_{0}^{\prime}(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\left(\frac{T}{8\pi}\right)-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O\left(\log T/\log\log T\right).

∎

Data

In

\zeta^{\prime}(s)
near

Conjecture
.

For some constant
C
, possibly equal
0

\displaystyle N_{0}^{\prime}(T)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi}T\log\left(\frac{T}{4\pi}\right)+\left(C-\frac{\log 2}{4\pi}\right)\cdot T+O(\log T/\log\log T)

\displaystyle N_{2}(T)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi}T\log\left(\frac{T}{4\pi}\right)+\left(C+\frac{\log 2}{4\pi}\right)\cdot T+O(\log T/\log\log T).

3.
Two Lemmas

For the Lemmas which will finish the proof of Theorem
1
, let
\log\eta(s)
be any choice of the branch of the logarithm in an open set which contains the critical line but no zeros of
\zeta^{\prime}
.
Let

F(t)\overset{\text{def.}}{=}-\text{Re}\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\eta}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)=-\text{Re}\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log(\eta(s))_{|s=1/2+it}.

By the Cauchy-Riemann equations applied to
\log(\eta(s))
on such a set,

\frac{d\log|\eta(s)|}{d\sigma}=\frac{d\arg\eta(s)}{dt},

and so when evaluated at
s=1/2+it
,

F(t)=-\frac{d\arg(\eta(1/2+it))}{dt}.

Lemma 2
.

For
t>4
,
F(t)>0
.

Proof.

In
[
20
, (2.9)]
, Zhang deduces from the Hadamard product for
\eta(s)
that

F(t)=-\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\text{Re}\left(\frac{1}{1/2+it-\lambda^{\prime}}\right)+O\left(1\right)

(where
\lambda^{\prime}
denotes complex zeros of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
with real part
>1/2
.) In
[
8
, Lemma 8]
, Ge improves this to get

(5)

F(t)=-\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\text{Re}\left(\frac{1}{1/2+it-\lambda^{\prime}}\right)+\log(2)/2+O\left(1/t\right).

We will not reproduce those calculations (the interested reader can go to the cited work), but observe the
O(1/t)
error term is explicitly (writing
s=1/2+it
)

\text{Re}\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{2}{s-1}\right)-\text{Re}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{s+a_{n}}-\frac{1}{s+2n}\right).

Here

-(2n+2)<-a_{n}<-2n

is the unique real zero on
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
in the interval. For
s=1/2+it
,

\text{Re}\left(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{2}{s-1}\right)=-\frac{2}{1+4t^{2}}.

We claim that the tail of the series,

-\text{Re}\sum_{2n>t}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{s+a_{n}}-\frac{1}{s+2n}\right)>0,

as this sum is

\sum_{2n\geq t}\text{Re}\left(\frac{a_{n}-2n}{(s+a_{n})(s+2n)}\right)=\sum_{2n\geq t}(a_{n}-2n)\frac{\text{Re}\left((\bar{s}+a_{n})(\bar{s}+2n)\right)}{|s+a_{n}|^{2}|s+2n|^{2}}.

With
2n<a_{n}<2n+2
, and
2n>t
, every term is positive.
Meanwhile

-\text{Re}\sum_{2n<t}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{s+a_{n}}-\frac{1}{s+2n}\right)=\sum_{2n<t}\frac{-a_{n}-1/2}{|s+a_{n}|^{2}}+\frac{1/2+2n}{|s+2n|^{2}}.

From
|s+a_{n}|^{2}>|s+2n|^{2}
we deduce

\frac{-1/2-a_{n}}{|s+a_{n}|^{2}}>\frac{-1/2-a_{n}}{|s+2n|^{2}},

so this sum is bounded below by

\sum_{2n<t}\frac{-a_{n}+2n}{|s+2n|^{2}}>-2\sum_{2n<t}\frac{1}{(2n+1/2)^{2}+t^{2}}.

The sum has
t/2
terms, each less than
1/t^{2}
so this sum is bounded below by
-1/t
. We conclude that

F(t)>-\sum_{\beta^{\prime}>1/2}\text{Re}\frac{1}{1/2+it-\lambda^{\prime}}+\log(2)/2+\frac{2}{1+4t^{2}}-\frac{1}{t},

and for
t>4
,

\log(2)/2-\frac{2}{1+4t^{2}}-\frac{1}{t}>0.

∎

By Stirling’s formula we have that for
\rho=1/2+i\gamma
a zero of
\zeta(s)
, Zhang’s Lemma 3 is, more explicitly,

(6)

F(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}\right).

Zhang’s Lemma 4 becomes

Lemma 3
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\int_{\gamma_{n}}^{\gamma_{n+1}}F(t)\,dt=\pi.

We will also need a result analogous to Lemma
2
on the line
\text{Re}(s)=4
. Let

f(t)\overset{\text{def.}}{=}\text{Re}\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\eta}\left(4+it\right).

(Note that unlike
F(t)
, there is no leading minus sign.)

Lemma 4
.

For
t>40
,

f(t)>0.

Proof.

First we consider those
t\geq 3000
. For
\sigma>1
we have a Dirichlet series expansion

\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\log(n)^{2}n^{-s}.

Along the vertical line
\text{Re}(s)=\sigma
we have upper bounds

|\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s)|\leq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\log(n)^{2}}{n^{\sigma}}.

Comparing sums to integrals we get the upper bound

|\zeta^{\prime\prime}(\sigma+it)|\leq\sum_{n=2}^{20}\frac{\log(n)^{2}}{n^{\sigma}}+\\
\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}\log^{2}(20)+2(\sigma-1)\log(20)+2}{20^{\sigma-1}(\sigma-1)^{3}}

For lower bounds on
|\zeta^{\prime}(\sigma+it)|
, we write

\displaystyle(1-2^{-s})\zeta(s)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+1)^{s}}

\displaystyle(1-2^{-s})\zeta^{\prime}(s)+2^{-s}\log(2)\zeta(s)=

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log(2n+1)}{(2n+1)^{s}}.

Thus

\left|(1-2^{-s})\zeta^{\prime}(s)\right|\geq 2^{-\sigma}\log(2)\left|\zeta(s)\right|-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log(2n+1)}{(2n+1)^{\sigma}}.

For
\sigma>1
we have the lower bound bounds

|\zeta(s)|\geq\frac{\zeta(2\sigma)}{\zeta(\sigma)},

which along with

\sum_{n=21}^{\infty}\frac{\log(2n+1)}{(2n+1)^{\sigma}}\leq\int_{20}^{\infty}\frac{\log(2x+1)}{(2x+1)^{\sigma}}\,dx=\frac{(\sigma-1)\log(41)+1}{2\cdot 41^{\sigma-1}(\sigma-1)^{2}}

gives

\left|(1-2^{-s})\zeta^{\prime}(s)\right|\geq 2^{-\sigma}\log(2)\frac{\zeta(2\sigma)}{\zeta(\sigma)}\\
-\sum_{n=1}^{20}\frac{\log(2n+1)}{(2n+1)^{\sigma}}-\frac{(\sigma-1)\log(21)+1}{2\cdot 21^{\sigma-1}(\sigma-1)^{2}}.

Thus we have the lower bound

(7)

|\zeta^{\prime}(s)|\geq\\
(1+2^{\sigma})^{-1}\left(2^{-\sigma}\log(2)\frac{\zeta(2\sigma)}{\zeta(\sigma)}-\sum_{n=1}^{20}\frac{\log(2n+1)}{(2n+1)^{\sigma}}-\frac{(\sigma-1)\log(41)+1}{2\cdot 41^{\sigma-1}(\sigma-1)^{2}}\right).

In particular, we estimate
|\zeta^{\prime\prime}(4+it)/\zeta^{\prime}(4+it)|\leq 3.07718
.

Meanwhile
h^{\prime}/h(4+it)=(\psi(2+it/2)-\log(\pi))/2
, where the polygamma function
\psi(z)=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
has real part which is an increasing function of
t
, and one calculates in
Mathematica
that

\text{Re}((\psi(2+i3000/2)-\log(\pi))/2)\geq 3.084.

In the range
40\leq t\leq 3000
, we can compute
f(t)
in

to see it is positive.
∎

For the range
4\leq t\leq 40

is
not
increasing, but an examination of Figure

suffices to complete the proof of Theorem
1
.

4.
A closer look at the type 2 zeros. Curvature of the level curve.

Since the data indicate that pairs of type 2 zeros of
\zeta(s)
are closer than average, and type 2 zeros of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
are closer to the critical line, it is natural to ask if one can show

(8)

\liminf(\beta^{\prime}-1/2)\log\gamma^{\prime}=0\Leftrightarrow\liminf(\gamma^{+}-\gamma^{-})\log\gamma^{\prime}=0

when the
\liminf
are both restricted to the subsequence of type 2 zeros.

We can investigate (
8
) via a study of the curvature of the level curve which connects the triple
1/2+i\gamma^{-}
,
\rho^{\prime}
, and
1/2+i\gamma^{+}
. With
\eta(s)=u+iv
, the formula for the curvature
\kappa
of the level curve
u(\sigma,t)=0
may be found in
[
10
, §3]
. Via the Cauchy-Riemann equations, one sees
[
11
]

(9)

\kappa=|\eta^{\prime}|\cdot\text{Re}(\eta^{\prime\prime}/\eta^{\prime\,2}).

The osculating circle for the curve
\text{Re}(\eta)=0
at
\rho^{\prime}
has radius
R=1/|\kappa|
. We will make the assumption that
\rho^{\prime}
is the rightmost point on the osculating circle. Of course this is not true for every type 2 zero, but based on our study of the angles
\theta=\arg(\eta^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime}))
below, it is an approximation which is true in the limit for a sequence of type 2 zeros with
(\beta^{\prime}-1/2)\log(\gamma^{\prime})\to 0
. Then elementary geometry shows the length of the chord this circle cuts from the critical line is

2\left((\beta^{\prime}-1/2)(2/|\kappa|+1/2-\beta^{\prime})\right)^{1/2}.

The inequality

2\left((\beta^{\prime}-1/2)(2/|\kappa|+1/2-\beta^{\prime})\right)^{1/2}<2^{3/2}\left(\frac{\beta^{\prime}-1/2}{|\kappa|}\right)^{1/2}

shows that, along a subsequence with

(\beta^{\prime}-1/2)\log(\gamma^{\prime})\to 0,

any lower bound of the form

(10)

\log(\gamma^{\prime})\ll|\kappa|

will force the length of the chord, multiplied by
\log(\gamma^{\prime})
, to tend to
0
. Since the error between the level curve and the osculating circle is of cubic order, we see that (
10
) will force

(\gamma^{+}-\gamma^{-})\log(\gamma^{\prime})\to 0

as well, which will prove the
\Rightarrow
implication in (
8
).

The expression (
9
) for
\kappa
simplifies when evaluated at a zero
\rho^{\prime}
of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
. Recalling the notation
h(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
, we have

\eta^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime})=h(\rho^{\prime})\zeta^{\prime\prime}(\rho^{\prime}),\qquad\eta^{\prime\prime}(\rho^{\prime})=2h^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime})\zeta^{\prime\prime}(\rho^{\prime})+h(\rho^{\prime})\zeta^{\prime\prime\prime}(\rho^{\prime}).

Recall
\theta
denotes
\arg(\eta^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime}))
, so

\frac{|\eta^{\prime}|}{\eta^{\prime}}(\rho^{\prime})=\exp(-i\theta),

and the curvature at
\rho^{\prime}
reduces to

(11)

\kappa=\text{Re}\left(\exp(-i\theta)\left(\frac{2h^{\prime}}{h}(\rho^{\prime})+\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}(\rho^{\prime})\right)\right).

Thus the curvature
\kappa
of the level curve at
\rho^{\prime}
is strongly influenced by the location relative to
\rho^{\prime}
of the zeros of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
, which we investigate in the next two sections.

5.
Zeros of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s)

Spira, in
[
16
]
was the first to observe that zeros of successive higher order derivatives of the Riemann zeta function seem to cluster along roughly horizontal lines. He wrote “The zeros of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
have imaginary part almost exactly equal to those of
\zeta^{\prime}
, and lie to the right of them.” (See Figure 1 from his paper) The following explains Spira’s observation.

Theorem 2
.

The level curves
\arg(\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}(s))=0
connect each zero of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
with
\text{Re}(s)>1/2
to a zero of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s)
with
\text{Re}(s)>1/2
(typically to the right), giving a canonical bijection between these two sets. The same holds for zeros higher derivatives
\zeta^{(k)}(s)
and
\zeta^{(k+1)}(s)
.

Proof.

Lemma
2
in §
1
already shows that the real part of
\eta^{\prime}/\eta(s)
is negative on the critical line
s=1/2+it
(
t>3
). To obtain the same result for
\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}(s)
, we simply subtract off the real part of
h^{\prime}/h(s)
, for
h(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
. By Stirling’s Formula this is
\log(t/(2\pi))/2+O(1/t^{2})
, so we deduce that the real part of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}(s)
is negative as well. Meanwhile, as
\sigma\to+\infty
,
\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}(s)\to-\log(2)
and so the real part of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}(s)
will again be negative.

Now fix a zero
\rho^{\prime}
of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
, and consider the level curves with

\arg\left(\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}(s)\right)=0
exiting the pole at
\rho^{\prime}
. By the above observations, this contour can’t cross the critical line, nor extend too far into the right half plane. The only possibility is that it terminates. To finish the argument we must be sure that a contour

\arg(\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}(s))=0
can not connect a zero of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s)
to another zero nor a pole (i.e. zero of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
) to another pole. But this contour is the inverse image under
\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}
of the positive real axis, connecting
0
to
\infty
on the Riemann sphere. It can only connect zeros to poles.

For
s
near a zero
\rho^{\prime}
of
\zeta^{\prime}
, elementary manipulations of series expansions give that

\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}}(s)=\frac{1}{s-\rho^{\prime}}+O(1),

so the contour
\arg(\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}(s))=0
has to exit the pole to the right, and so the zeros of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
will typically be to the right of the zeros of
\zeta^{\prime}
. Similarly, a contour with
\arg(\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}(s))=0
terminating in a zero of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s)
, when followed backwards, must originate in a pole, i.e., a zero of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
.
∎

This same argument works for zeros of higher derivatives as well.

6.
Adaptation of a Theorem of Marden

This section is inspired by the results in
[
13
,
14
]
, which express the logarithmic derivative of an entire function
f
as a sum over poles (zeros of
f
) weighted by rational expressions in a fixed set of zeros of
f
and
f^{\prime}
. Marden’s proof of
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
via the Cauchy Integral Formula can be generalized to
f(s)=\zeta^{\prime}(s)
, but in fact
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
can be proved simply by a partial fraction decomposition and taking linear combinations of the Hadamard logarithmic derivative, as shall see.

We denote the real zeros of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
as
-a_{n}
, with
a_{n}\in(2n,2n+2)
. In fact,

-a_{n}=-2n-2+\frac{1}{\log(n)}+O\left(\frac{1}{\log^{2}(n)}\right).

The following proposition will be needed to investigate the curvature
\kappa
of the level curve at a fixed zero
\rho^{\prime}
of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
.
A
generic
non-real zero of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
will be denoted as
\lambda^{\prime}
in this subsection.

Proposition 2
.

Fix a complex zero
\rho^{\prime\prime}=\beta^{\prime\prime}+i\gamma^{\prime\prime}
of
\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s)
with
\beta^{\prime\prime}>1/2
. With
s=\sigma+it
in a vertical strip
a\leq\sigma\leq b
we have

(12)

\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}}(s)=\frac{\log(\gamma^{\prime\prime}/t)}{2}+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}-s}{(s-\lambda^{\prime})(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime})}\\
+O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\right).

The sum is uniformly convergent on compact sets.

Proof.

The starting point is the partial fractions representation

(13)

\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}}(s)=\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}}(0)-2-\frac{2}{s-1}+\\
\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{s+a_{n}}-\frac{1}{a_{n}}\right)+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{s-\lambda^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{\prime}}\right)

which follows from the Hadamard theory. From (
13
) subtract
0=\zeta^{\prime\prime}/\zeta^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime\prime})
to obtain

\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}}(s)=-\frac{2}{s-1}+\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime\prime}-1}\\
+\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{s+a_{n}}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}+a_{n}}\right)+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}-s}{(s-\lambda^{\prime})(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime})}.

Add and subtract
\psi(s/2)/2
where the digamma function
\psi(s)=\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma(s)
. From the series representation for the digamma function we see that

\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}}(s)=-\frac{1}{2}\psi(s/2)-\frac{C}{2}-\frac{1}{s}-\frac{2}{s-1}+\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime\prime}-1}\\
+\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{1}{s+2n}+\frac{1}{s+a_{n}}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}+a_{n}}\right)+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}-s}{(s-\lambda^{\prime})(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime})}.

(Here
C
is the Euler constant.) We regroup the terms to obtain

\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}}(s)=-\frac{1}{2}\psi(s/2)-\frac{C}{2}-\frac{1}{s}-\frac{2}{s-1}+\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime\prime}-1}+\\
\sum_{n}\frac{2n-a_{n}}{(s+2n)(s+a_{n})}+\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}+a_{n}}\right)+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}-s}{(s-\lambda^{\prime})(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime})}.

Lemma 5
.

We have

-\frac{1}{s}-\frac{2}{s-1}+\sum_{n}\frac{2n-a_{n}}{(s+2n)(s+a_{n})}=O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)

Proof.

The numerator
2n-a_{n}
of the summand is
O(1)
, while

\sum_{n}\frac{1}{(s+2n)(s+a_{n})}\ll|s|^{2}\sum_{n}\frac{1}{(4n^{2}+t^{2})^{2}}+\\
|s|\sum_{n}\frac{4n}{(4n^{2}+t^{2})^{2}}+\sum_{n}\frac{4n^{2}}{(4n^{2}+t^{2})^{2}}.

The first and last sum on the right have complicated closed forms in terms of
\sinh
,
\cosh
,
\coth
, and
\csch
, while the middle sum is expressed in terms of
\psi^{\prime}
, the derivative of the digamma function. Including the leading factors
|s|^{2}
,
|s|
and
1
, each is
O(1/t)
.
∎

We add

0=\frac{1}{2}\psi(\rho^{\prime\prime}/2)+\frac{C}{2}+\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}+\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}+2n}-\frac{1}{2n}\right)

to see that

\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta^{\prime}}(s)=\frac{\psi(\rho^{\prime\prime}/2)-\psi(s/2)}{2}+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}-s}{(s-\lambda^{\prime})(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime})}\\
+\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}+\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime\prime}-1}+\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}+2n}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}+a_{n}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right).

Via the lemma,

\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}+\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime\prime}-1}+\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}+2n}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}+a_{n}}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\right).

Stirling’s formula gives

\frac{\psi(\rho^{\prime\prime}/2)-\psi(s/2)}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\log(\gamma^{\prime\prime}/t)+O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\right).

∎

As a consequence of Proposition
2

F(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\log(\gamma^{\prime\prime}/2\pi)-\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\text{Re}\left(\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}-s}{(s-\lambda^{\prime})(\rho^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime})}\right)\\
+O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\right).

Theorem 3
.

Let
\rho^{\prime}
denote the zero of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
canonically associated via Theorem

to
\rho^{\prime\prime}
. Subtracting (
5

(15)

\text{Re}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}-\rho^{\prime}}\right)+\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\neq\rho^{\prime}}\text{Re}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}}\right)=\frac{\log(\gamma^{\prime\prime}/\pi)}{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\right).

(The

term drops out as the rest of the expression is independent of

Theorem

is a fundamental identity that relates the location of the

associated to

to the location of all the

\rho^{\prime}
close to the critical line,

close to the critical line has an effect on the position of the canonically associated zero

of

as well: Figure

shows the 273,422 type 2 zeros sorted into quartiles of

. So at the top of Figure

Curvature at
\rho^{\prime}
in terms of all

\gamma^{\prime}
relative to
\gamma^{+}
,
\gamma^{-}

We claim that
\gamma^{\prime}
is very near to

when either
\beta^{\prime}-1/2

\gamma^{+}-\gamma^{-}

\displaystyle\Delta=\gamma^{+}-\gamma^{-},\qquad\lambda=\log(t_{0}/2\pi),

\displaystyle\rho^{\pm}=1/2+i(t_{0}\pm\Delta/2),\qquad\rho^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}+i(t_{0}+Y)

We rescale with

In
[

\displaystyle x(\delta)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\left(1-\frac{\log(\pi)}{\lambda}\right)\delta^{2}+O(\delta^{4}),

In the first, we estimate

\rho^{\prime}

(16)

\gamma^{\prime}=t_{0}+O\left(\gamma^{+}-\gamma^{-}\right)^{2},\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\gamma^{\prime}=t_{0}+O\left(\frac{\beta^{\prime}-1/2}{\log(\gamma^{\prime})}\right).

We will next investigate the angles
\theta
, by comparing to the argument of

The next proposition relates the remaining parameters determining the curvature of the level curve at one zero

Let

+O\left(\beta^{\prime}-1/2\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime}}\right).

Proof.

With
\rho^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}+i\gamma^{\prime}

be the zero of

We then use (

For the reverse implication, consider a sequence of type 2 pairs

Appendix: Random Matrix Analogs

There’s a productive analogy between the zeros of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
and the zeros of the characteristic polynomial
p_{A}(z)
of a unitary matrix
A
, and even the zeros of the respective derivatives. In this analogy the critical line
\text{Re}(s)=1/2
corresponds to the unit circle
|z|=1
. More precisely, the analogy relates zeros of
\zeta(s)
at height
T
in the critical strip and zeros of
p_{A}(z)
when
A
is
n
by
n
, for
n\approx\log(T)
. On the number theory side, the function

\eta(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta^{\prime}(s)

is very useful. Here we develop an analog
\eta_{A}(z)
for the characteristic polynomial of a unitary matrix
A
, and explore its applications.

In §
8.1
, Theorem
4
says that if

\exp(i\theta^{+})
and
\exp(i\theta^{-})
are ‘consecutive’ zeros of
p_{A}(z)
with

\theta^{+}-\theta^{-}<\frac{2\pi}{1+6n},

then the angular sector

S(\theta^{+},\theta^{-})=\left\{1-2(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-})<|z|,\,\theta^{-}\leq\arg(z)\leq\theta^{+}\right\}

contains a zero of
p_{A}^{\prime}(z)
.

Some of the results in §
8.1
are also in
[
9
]
; We think the introduction of the function
\eta_{A}(z)
clarifies the analogy to the number theory side.

In §
8.2
, Theorem
5
uses the level curves
\text{Re}(\eta_{A}(z))=0
to classify the zeros of
p_{A}(z)
and
p_{A}^{\prime}(z)
, analogous to the classification of the zeros of
\zeta(s)
and
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
above.

8.1.
Results about location of zeros
p_{A}(z)
and
p_{A}^{\prime}(z)

Let
A
be an
n\times n
unitary matrix,
n>2
, with distinct eigenvalues and characteristic polynomial
p_{A}(z)=\det(A-zI)
.

We define

h_{A}(z)=(-z)^{-n/2}\det(A)^{-1/2},\qquad\Lambda_{A}(z)=h_{A}(z)p_{A}(z).

If
n
is even,
\Lambda_{A}
has a pole of order
n/2
at the origin. If
n
is odd, there is a branch cut from the origin to infinity. (The location of the branch cut does not particularly matter, but when
we denote
\exp(i\theta^{-})
,
\exp(i\theta^{+})
as consecutive zeros of
p_{A}(z)
, we will assume it does not pass between them.) Since

(-z)^{n}p_{A}(1/z)=\det(-z\cdot A+I)\\
=\det(A)\cdot\det(-z\cdot I+A^{*})=\det(A)p_{A^{*}}(z).

we get that

(21)

\Lambda_{A}(z)=\Lambda_{A^{*}}(1/z).

The function
h_{A}(z)
is the matrix theory analog of the function
h(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
which completes the Riemann zeta function to obtain the functional equation. We define

\eta_{A}(z)=zh_{A}(z)p_{A}^{\prime}(z);

this will be the matrix theory analog of the function
\eta(s)=h(s)\zeta^{\prime}(s)
. (The need for the extra factor of
z
will become apparent.) With this we can easily prove for characteristic polynomials of unitary matrices some interesting analogs of results for
\zeta(s)
and
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
.

Lemma 6
.

The function
z\Lambda_{A}^{\prime}(z)
is purely imaginary on
|z|=1
, while
z\Lambda_{A}^{\prime}(z)+z^{2}\Lambda_{A}^{\prime\prime}(z)
is real on
|z|=1
.

Proof.

On the unit circle
|z|=1

\Lambda_{A}(z)=\Lambda_{A^{*}}(\overline{z})=\overline{\Lambda_{A}(z)}

is real valued by (
21
). Write
z=\exp(i\theta)
, so

\frac{d}{d\theta}\Lambda_{A}(\exp(i\theta))=i\exp(i\theta)\Lambda^{\prime}(\exp(i\theta))=iz\Lambda_{A}^{\prime}(z).

The imaginary part of this function is
0
on the unit circle. Similarly, another derivative with respect to
\theta
gives the second claim.
∎

The above is the analog of
[
20
, (2.1)]
, while the following is the analog of
[
20
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 7
.

On the unit circle
|z|=1
, we have

p_{A}(z)=0\Leftrightarrow\text{Re}(\eta_{A}(z))=0.

Proof.

z\Lambda^{\prime}(z)=z\left(h_{A}^{\prime}(z)p_{A}(z)+h_{A}(z)p_{A}^{\prime}(z)\right)\\
=z\left(\frac{h_{A}^{\prime}(z)}{h_{A}(z)}\Lambda_{A}(z)+h_{A}(z)p_{A}^{\prime}(z)\right)\\
=(-n/2)\Lambda_{A}(z)+zh_{A}(z)p_{A}^{\prime}(z).

By Lemma
6
, on the unit circle the real part of the left side of the equation is
0
, while
\Lambda_{A}(z)
is already real valued. So
\Lambda_{A}(z)
is
0
if and only if
\eta_{A}(z)
is purely imaginary.
∎

The Lemma makes the level curves
\text{Re}(\eta_{A}(z))=0
of interest. Figure
6
shows an example with
n=22
, using
Mathematica
to generate a random matrix from the Circular Unitary Ensemble.
We use color to indicate the sign of
\text{Im}(\eta_{A}(z))
.
Green indicates
\text{Re}(\eta_{A}(z))=0
and
\text{Im}(\eta_{A}(z)))
is positive, while purple indicates
\text{Re}(\eta_{A}(z))=0
and

\text{Im}(\eta_{A}(z))
is negative. The zeros
\mu
of
p_{A}(z)
occur where the green or purple contours cross the unit circle. The zeros
\mu^{\prime}
of
p_{A}^{\prime}
are visible exactly where the two colors come together.

We define

F(\theta)=\frac{d}{d\theta}\arg(\eta_{A}(\exp(i\theta))).

This is the matrix analog of the function
F(t)
in
[
20
, (2.13)]
, and appears as (12) in
[
9
]
. In particular

\frac{d}{d\theta}\text{Im}(\log(\eta_{A}(\exp(i\theta))))=\text{Im}\left(i\exp(i\theta)\frac{\eta_{A}^{\prime}}{\eta_{A}}(\exp(i\theta))\right)\\
=\text{Re}\left(\exp(i\theta)\frac{\eta_{A}^{\prime}}{\eta_{A}}(\exp(i\theta))\right).

Lemma 8
.

Let
\mu=\exp(i\theta)
be a zero of
p_{A}
. Then
F(\theta)=n/2
.

This is Lemma 4.5 in
[
9
]
, and is the matrix analog of
[
20
, Lemma 3]
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6

0=\text{Re}\left(\frac{\mu\Lambda_{A}^{\prime}(\mu)+\mu^{2}\Lambda_{A}^{\prime\prime}(\mu)}{\mu\Lambda_{A}^{\prime}(\mu)}\right)\\
=\text{Re}\left(1+\mu\left(2\frac{h_{A}^{\prime}}{h_{A}}(\mu)+\frac{p_{A}^{\prime\prime}}{p_{A}^{\prime}}(\mu)\right)\right)\\
=\mu\left(\frac{h_{A}^{\prime}}{h_{A}}(\mu)+\frac{\eta_{A}^{\prime}}{\eta_{A}}(\mu)\right).

With
h_{A}^{\prime}/h_{A}(\mu)=-n/2\cdot\mu^{-1}
, this says

\frac{n}{2}=\text{Re}\left(\mu\,\frac{\eta_{A}^{\prime}}{\eta_{A}}(\mu)\right).

∎

Lemma 9
.

Along the unit circle,
\arg(\eta_{A}(\exp(i\theta)))
is strictly increasing. This immediately implies that for consecutive eigenvalues
\exp(i\theta^{-})
,
\exp(i\theta^{+})

(in the obvious sense of the counterclockwise orientation of the unit circle), we have

\int_{\theta^{-}}^{\theta^{+}}F(\theta)\,d\theta=\pi.

This Lemma is the matrix analog of
[
20
, Lemma 4]
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\exp(i\theta)\frac{\eta_{A}^{\prime}}{\eta_{A}}(\exp(i\theta))=

\displaystyle 1-n/2+\exp(i\theta)\frac{p_{A}^{\prime\prime}}{p_{A}^{\prime}}(\exp(i\theta))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-n/2+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-\exp(-i\theta)\mu_{j}^{\prime}},

where
\mu_{k}^{\prime}
denotes the zeros of
p_{A}^{\prime}(z)
,
k=1,\ldots n-1
. Regrouping the
1-n/2
, we consider

1/2+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\text{Re}\left(\frac{1}{1-\exp(-i\theta)\mu^{\prime}_{j}}-1/2\right).

A little algebra shows that

\text{Re}\left(\frac{1}{1-\exp(-i\theta)\mu^{\prime}_{j}}-1/2\right)\geq 0\Leftrightarrow\left|\exp(-i\theta)\mu_{j}^{\prime}\right|^{2}\leq 1,

which we know to be the case by the Gauss-Lucas theorem, which states that the zeros of
p_{A}^{\prime}(z)
lie in the convex hull of the zeros of
p_{A}(z)
.
∎

In fact we proved that
F(\theta)\geq 1/2
, which gives us the trivial bound on the gaps between zeros. However the Lemma is necessary for Theorems
4
and
5
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mu^{+}=\exp(i\theta^{+})
,
\mu^{-}=\exp(i\theta^{-})
be zeros of
p_{A}(z)
with

\theta^{+}-\theta^{-}<\frac{2\pi}{1+6n}.

Then the angular sector

S(\theta^{+},\theta^{-})=\left\{1-|z|<2(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-}),\,\theta^{-}\leq\arg(z)\leq\theta^{+}\right\}

contains a zero of
p_{A}^{\prime}(z)
.

This is the matrix analog of
[
8
, Lemma 11]
.

Proof.

Via some more algebra, note that

\text{Re}\left(\frac{1}{1-\exp(-i\theta)\mu^{\prime}}-1/2\right)=\frac{1-|\mu^{\prime}|^{2}}{2|1-\exp(-i\theta)\mu^{\prime}|^{2}}.

With
d=2(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-})
, write
F(\theta)=1/2+F_{11}(\theta)+F_{12}(\theta)
, where

\displaystyle F_{11}(\theta)=

\displaystyle\sum_{1-d<|\mu^{\prime}|}\frac{1-|\mu^{\prime}|^{2}}{2|1-\exp(-i\theta)\mu^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle F_{12}(\theta)=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\mu^{\prime}|\leq 1-d}\frac{1-|\mu^{\prime}|^{2}}{2|1-\exp(-i\theta)\mu^{\prime}|^{2}}.

(The notation here and below is meant to highlight the analogy with the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 11]
.) For
|\mu^{\prime}|\leq 1-d
and
\theta^{-}<\theta<\theta^{+}
, we have that

\left|\exp(i\theta)-\mu^{\prime}\right|\geq\left|\exp(i\theta^{+})-\mu^{\prime}\right|-\left|\exp(i\theta)-\exp(i\theta^{+})\right|\\
\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|\exp(i\theta^{+})-\mu^{\prime}\right|+(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-})-\left|\exp(i\theta)-\exp(i\theta^{+})\right|\\
\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|\exp(i\theta^{+})-\mu^{\prime}\right|.

Thus

F_{12}(\theta)\leq 4F_{12}(\theta^{+})<4F(\theta^{+})=2n.

Now suppose there is no zero of
p_{A}^{\prime}(z)
in the angular sector
S(\theta^{+},\theta^{-})
. Let
\delta=(\theta^{-}+\theta^{+})/2
. Then we may write
F_{11}(\theta)=f(\theta)+g(\theta)
, where

\displaystyle f(\theta)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1-d<|\mu^{\prime}|\\
\theta^{+}<\arg(\mu^{\prime})<\pi+\delta\end{subarray}}\frac{1-|\mu^{\prime}|^{2}}{2|1-\exp(-i\theta)\mu^{\prime}|^{2}},

\displaystyle g(\theta)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1-d<|\mu^{\prime}|\\
\delta-\pi<\arg(\mu^{\prime})<\theta^{-}\end{subarray}}\frac{1-|\mu^{\prime}|^{2}}{2|1-\exp(-i\theta)\mu^{\prime}|^{2}}.

For
\theta^{-}\leq\theta\leq\theta^{+}
we have
f(\theta)\leq f(\theta^{+})
and
g(\theta)\leq g(\theta^{-})
. So

F_{11}(\theta)\leq f(\theta^{+})+g(\theta^{-})\leq F(\theta^{+})+F(\theta^{-})=n.

Now

\pi=\int_{\theta^{-}}^{\theta^{+}}F(\theta)\,d\theta\leq\int_{\theta^{-}}^{\theta^{+}}1/2+3n\,d\theta=(1/2+3n)(\theta^{+}-\theta^{-}),

which is a contradiction.
∎

p_{A}(z)
and

based on the level curves.

Classification of zeros of

and

Zeros of

Type 0:

We will say a zero
\mu^{\prime}
of

if
neither
of the two level curves
\text{Re}(\eta_{A}(z))=0,\text{Im}(\eta_{A}(z))>0

\mu^{\prime}

\text{Re}(\eta_{A}(z))=0,\text{Im}(\eta_{A}(z))>0
and

\mu^{\prime}

Type 2:

We will say a zero
\mu^{\prime}
of

if the level curves
\text{Re}(\eta_{A}(z))=0,\text{Im}(\eta_{A}(z))>0
and
\text{Re}(\eta_{A}(z))=0,\text{Im}(\eta_{A}(z))<0

\mu^{\prime}

Type 1:

We will say a zero
\mu

Type 2:

We will say a zero
\mu

6

N_{1}(A)=\sharp\left\{\text{type 1 zeros of }p_{A}(z)\right\}.

Let

For

, let

N_{j}^{\prime}(A)=\sharp\left\{\text{zeros }\mu^{\prime}\text{of }p_{A}^{\prime}(z)\text{ of type }j\right\}.

Theorem 5
.

Every zero of
p_{A}(z)
is of either type 1 or type 2. Thus we have a canonical mapping from the zeros of
p_{A}(z)
to those of
p_{A}^{\prime}(z)
, which is two to one on the type 2 zeros, and one to one on the type 1 zeros. Zeros of
p_{A}^{\prime}(z)
of type 0 are precisely those not in the image of this mapping. The zeros of
p_{A}(z)
type 2 are canonically grouped in pairs.

There is always at least one pair of type 2 zeros of
p_{A}(z)
, and in fact

(22)

N_{2}(A)-N_{0}^{\prime}(A)=1,

while

(23)

N_{1}(A)+2N_{0}^{\prime}(A)=n-2.

Proof.

Regarding the mapping, all this is clear except the first statement, which says the contours which cross the unit circle must terminate in exactly one zero of
p_{A}^{\prime}(z)
. The alternatives we must rule out is continuation of the contour on to the center, or looping back to the unit circle.

For the first possibility, note that the contour
\arg(\eta_{A}(z))=\pi/2
(resp.
\arg(\eta_{A}(z))=-\pi/2
) does not exist in isolation; it is part of a continuum which deform smoothly as the argument is varied. But the argument of
\eta_{A}(z)
is increasing around the unit circle by Lemma
9
, while decreasing near the origin because of the
z(-z)^{-n/2}
term. The contours can only cross over each other where the argument of
\eta_{A}(z)
is undefined, at a zero
\mu^{\prime}
.

The second possibility is also ruled out by Lemma
9
, which says that the argument of
\eta_{A}(z)
increases monotonically as one goes around the unit circle.

We have

(24)

N_{1}(A)+2N_{2}(A)=n.

Via the mapping,
N_{1}(A)=N_{1}^{\prime}(A)
and
N_{2}(A)=N_{2}^{\prime}(A)
.
Thus we have

(25)

N_{0}^{\prime}(A)+N_{1}(A)+N_{2}(A)=n-1.

Subtracting (
25
) from (
24
) gives (
22
).
Subtracting (
24
) from twice (
25
) gives (
23
).
∎

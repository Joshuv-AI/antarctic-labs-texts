Multi-Omics Insights into Chronic Rhinosinusitis with Nasal Polyps: A Review of Lipidomics, Proteomics, and Transcriptomics
Authors: Shuting Wang, Kunpeng Ma, Li Sun, Ping Li, Limin Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis review summarizes recent advances in multi-omics research on chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP), focusing on lipidomics, proteomics, and transcriptomics. CRSwNP is a highly heterogeneous disease characterized by persistent inflammation, epithelial barrier dysfunction, and tissue remodeling, with high recurrence and variable treatment responses. Current studies are mostly limited to single-omics analyses, and few achieve systematic cross-layer integration. This review integrates evidence from three omics layers to construct a continuous pathological network, identify stable and translatable biomarkers, and support stratified management and precision therapy.Keywords: chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, (CRSwNP), multi-omics, precision medicine, molecular mechanisms

Introduction

Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) is a prevalent chronic inflammatory disorder of the nasal and paranasal sinus mucosa, clinically manifesting as nasal obstruction, rhinorrhoea, hyposmia, facial pain, and markedly reduced quality of life.1,2 Global prevalence ranges from 5%–12%, and nearly one-third of cases present with nasal polyps.3 Although glucocorticoids, endoscopic surgery, and biological agents have improved outcomes, many patients remain refractory or experience high postoperative recurrence. Treatment responses vary widely due to high clinical and molecular heterogeneity, including type 2–high and non-type 2 endotypes, geographic differences, prior surgery, medication exposure, and sampling variation.4 These factors hinder mechanistic understanding and personalized therapy.

In recent years, the development of multi-omics technologies has provided a new research framework for deciphering the complex pathological processes of CRSwNP.5 Lipidomics systematically analyses lipid metabolism networks and has revealed regulatory effects of inflammation-related lipid mediators, such as prostaglandins, leukotrienes, and sphingolipids, within the inflammatory microenvironment.6,7 Proteomics enables identification of differentially expressed proteins associated with immune responses, extracellular matrix remodelling, and epithelial function from nasal polyp tissues, nasal secretions, or serum, and highlights key signalling pathways.8 Transcriptomics clarifies the molecular basis of different inflammatory endotypes, immune cell infiltration patterns, and epithelial barrier dysfunction at the level of gene expression regulation, providing an important basis for endotype classification in CRSwNP.9 The combined application of multi-omics extends beyond the traditional single-gene or single-pathway perspective and facilitates understanding of disease initiation and progression as a continuous and interconnected biological network at the systems level.10

However, most current studies continue to focus on individual omics layers and lack vertical linkage analyses that connect lipid metabolic alterations, protein interaction networks, and transcriptional regulatory signals.11 Differences in data dimensionality across omics platforms, limited sample sizes, and inconsistent definitions of clinical phenotypes further restrict clinical translation of multi-omics findings. On this basis, this article systematically reviews recent advances in lipidomics, proteomics, and transcriptomics research in CRSwNP, aims to construct a cross-level molecular regulatory framework, and emphasises the value of multi-omics integration for elucidating disease heterogeneity, identifying stable biomarkers, and advancing precision treatment. This review provides a theoretical reference for subsequent targeted interventions in specific patient populations and inflammatory endotypes.12

Systematic Application of Lipidomics in CRSwNP Research

Characteristics of Lipidomics Technology

Lipidomics is an omics discipline that systematically examines the composition, function, and metabolic dynamics of lipid molecules in biological systems. Its core analytical approach primarily relies on liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS), which enables separation, identification, and quantification of multiple lipid classes within a single analysis.13 Compared with traditional biochemical assays or single-target measurements, LC–MS offers several advantages: (1) high throughput, allowing simultaneous detection of hundreds to thousands of lipid species; (2) a wide dynamic range, covering concentration changes from picomolar to micromolar levels; and (3) defined spatiotemporal resolution, permitting comparison of lipid profile differences across tissues, body fluids, or lesion microregions.14 When combined with standardised sample pretreatment, internal standard systems, and batch-effect correction, lipidomics supports refined molecular stratification of inflammatory and heterogeneous upper airway diseases such as CRSwNP.15

Notably, variations in analytical platforms, sample handling protocols and batch effects inevitably interfere with the reproducibility of lipid profiling results, which is a common technical challenge for current lipidomic studies of CRSwNP.

Molecular Landscape of Lipid Metabolism Disorders in CRSwNP

Characteristic Lipid Biomarker Profiles

Multiple studies indicate that patients with CRSwNP exhibit significant lipid imbalance, manifested by increased pro-inflammatory lipid mediators, altered membrane lipid composition, and enhanced lipid peroxidation. Levels of pro-inflammatory lipids increase through multiple regulatory pathways. Eosinophilic nasal polyp inflammation shows a positive association with the degree of polyp fibrosis. The development and maturation of eosinophils in the bone marrow are regulated by several key pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-5, granulocyte–macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF see Table 1), and IL-3. Among these, IL-5 plays a central role in eosinophil differentiation, migration, activation, and survival.16 Alterations in cell membrane lipid structure, such as an imbalanced phosphatidylcholine-to-sphingolipid ratio, contribute to epithelial barrier disruption.17 Metabolic pathway shifts also result in increased levels of downstream mediators in the 15-lipoxygenase (15-LO) pathway, including 15-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid (15-HETE) and 15-oxoeicosatetraenoic acid (15-Oxo-ETE), which contribute substantially to CRSwNP-associated inflammation.18 In addition, lipid metabolism disorders and elevated lipid peroxidation have been observed in nasal polyp epithelial cells from patients with CRSwNP.19 Among epithelial subpopulations, solute carrier family 27 member 2 (SLC27A2)-positive cells show enriched expression of lipid peroxidation pathway genes and heightened responsiveness to IL-4/IL-13 signalling derived from Th2 (T helper cell type 2) cells and group 2 innate lymphoid cells (ILC2). These immune components act as key drivers of epithelial dysfunction and inflammation in CRSwNP.20

Association Between Lipid Networks and Pathological Mechanisms

Current evidence suggests that lipids function not only as metabolic products but also as signalling regulators through multiple mechanisms during CRSwNP development: (1) amplification of inflammatory cascades, whereby arachidonic acid metabolites such as leukotrienes and prostaglandins bind cell surface receptors, activate the NF-κB pathway, promote secretion of Th2-type cytokines, and drive Th2-dominant inflammatory responses;21,22 (2) regulation of tissue remodelling, in which lysophosphatidic acid (LPA), a key lipid messenger, binds G protein–coupled receptors, activates downstream signalling, and promotes fibroblast proliferation, thereby accelerating polyp formation23 (see Figure 1); and (3) support of pathogen growth, as short-chain fatty acids can serve as carbon sources for paranasal sinus pathogens, providing energy that favours microbial proliferation and disrupts paranasal sinus microecological balance under permissive conditions.24

Most existing lipidomic investigations for CRSwNP adopt cross-sectional designs with relatively small sample cohorts, which limits causal inference and longitudinal interpretation of dynamic lipid changes. Moreover, lipid profiles are easily confounded by multiple clinical factors, including inflammatory endotype classification, long-term exposure to corticosteroids or biological agents, prior sinus surgery history, and tissue sampling sites.

Despite the above encouraging findings, most reported lipid alterations are currently only proven as correlative phenomena rather than experimentally verified causal drivers of CRSwNP pathogenesis. To address this gap, future lipidomic research should implement standardized sample processing workflows, conduct internal validation and independent external cohort verification, and integrate lipidomic data with matched transcriptomic and proteomic profiles from the same patient groups.

Systematic Analysis of Proteomics in CRSwNP Research

Proteomics Technology: Panoramic Analysis of Functional Protein Executors

Proteomics takes the complete set of proteins expressed in a specific tissue or under a defined pathological condition at a given time point as its research object. It focuses on alterations in protein expression levels, post-translational modifications, molecular interactions, and their functional roles within signalling pathways.29 At present, the principal proteomic approaches applied in CRSwNP research include the following. (1) Mass spectrometry-based proteomics centred on LC–MS/MS, which enables unbiased identification and quantification of large numbers of differentially expressed proteins for comparative profiling of nasal polyp tissues, nasal secretions, or serum relative to controls.30 (2) Hydrogen–deuterium exchange mass spectrometry (HDX-MS), which reflects protein conformational and dynamic changes through differences in hydrogen exchange rates with the solvent and is suitable for characterising inflammation-related receptors, mucins, and their complexes.31 (3) Protein interaction network analysis integrating co-immunoprecipitation–mass spectrometry (Co-IP-MS) with bioinformatics tools to define the functions of key protein complexes.32

Compared with other omics layers, proteomics shows several notable advantages. First, biomarker discovery is closer to clinical phenotypes. Precise localisation of disease biomarkers is essential for CRSwNP, and proteomic analyses have outlined disease-specific protein expression patterns. For example, Th2-type cytokines and matrix metalloproteinases associated with epithelial remodelling display aberrant expression, providing a basis for diagnosis, prognostic assessment, and targeted therapy.33 Second, proteomics supports dynamic monitoring. During CRSwNP progression, tracking temporal changes in proteins such as eosinophil cationic protein and periostin reflects preoperative and postoperative disease activity and remodelling status, allowing closer assessment of disease evolution.34 Third, proteomics facilitates identification of intervenable network nodes. Protein interaction networks play a central role in CRSwNP pathogenesis, and integrated proteomic and bioinformatic analyses map these networks, enabling topological and key-node analyses that identify critical regulatory points in inflammatory signalling and tissue remodelling. These findings support development of precise targeted therapeutic strategies.35

A major limitation of current proteomic studies lies in the high heterogeneity of experimental systems: research samples cover nasal polyp tissues, nasal secretions, serum and epithelial specimens across different studies, while diverse mass spectrometry platforms and protein quantification strategies further increase result variability.

Proteomics Research on CRSwNP: From Molecular Mapping to Mechanistic Interpretation

Characteristics of Differentially Expressed Protein Profiles

Proteomic analyses indicate the presence of representative sets of aberrantly expressed proteins in nasal polyp tissues, nasal secretions, and serum of patients with CRSwNP. These proteins are mainly distributed across three pathological axes: inflammation-driven responses, tissue remodelling, and epithelial barrier impairment. (1) Inflammation-driven factors: IL-25 and IL-33, Th2-associated cytokines expressed by CD4+ Th2 cells and group 2 innate lymphoid cells (ILC2), strongly promote eosinophil accumulation and infiltration of additional inflammatory cells within nasal polyps.36 S100A9 (S100 calcium-binding protein A9), a pro-inflammatory protein, induces remodelling responses in human nasal epithelial cells (hNECs). Its elevated expression in CRSwNP, particularly within squamous epithelium, shows a positive association with disease severity.37 (2) Tissue remodelling-related proteins: Imbalance between matrix metalloproteinase-2 (MMP-2) and matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9) has been reported. Excessive MMP-2 and MMP-9 expression in granulocytes and mast cells contributes to extracellular matrix degradation, inflammatory cell infiltration, and subsequent polypoid degeneration.38 Granzyme K–positive (GZMK+) CD8+ T cells express CXC chemokine receptor 4 (CXCR4) and interact with fibroblasts that secrete CXC chemokine ligand 12 (CXCL12). Through a GZMK-specific mechanism rather than other granzymes, these interactions stimulate fibroblasts to produce neutrophil-attracting chemokines, thereby promoting CRSwNP development.39 (3) Epithelial barrier proteins: Claudin-3 shows strong predictive performance for epithelial barrier disruption. Receiver operating characteristic analysis indicates that claudin-3 achieves the highest accuracy for evaluating barrier impairment, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.791 (P < 0.001), supporting its value as a biomarker for epithelial barrier defects and disease severity in CRSwNP.40

Pathological Mechanisms Associated with Differentially Expressed Proteins

Several mechanistic pathways have been proposed based on proteomic findings. (1) JAK–STAT-mediated amplification of eosinophilic inflammation: Previous studies indicate that eosinophil-associated cytokines IL-4 and IL-5 in CRSwNP are enriched within the JAK–STAT signalling cascade. Activation of this pathway promotes extensive eosinophil infiltration, intensifies inflammatory responses, and amplifies the inflammatory cascade.41 (2) Synergistic effects of MMP-9 and transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) in tissue remodelling: MMP-9 acts together with TGF-β to activate downstream signalling, induce fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition, and enhance extracellular matrix production. These processes lead to excessive tissue remodelling and support polyp growth.42 (3) Innate immune activation through the S100A8/A9–TLR4–MyD88 axis: Recent studies show that Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), a pattern-recognition receptor, binds S100A8 and S100A9 and activates myeloid differentiation primary response protein 88 (MyD88)-dependent signalling in monocytes. This activation promotes inflammatory responses and subsequent abnormal microbial accumulation, contributing to nasal polyp formation43 (see Figure 2).

Clinical Translation: From Biomarkers to Precision Intervention

Diagnosis and Efficacy Monitoring

(1)Non-invasive biomarker combinations: A diagnostic model constructed using four key proteins identified by nasal fluid and serum proteomics (glial cell line–derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF), monocyte chemoattractant protein 4 (MCP-4), TGFB1, and cystatin 5 (CST5)) achieves an AUC of 0.91–0.92 for type 2 CRSwNP. Among these markers, GDNF and MCP-4 independently predict postoperative recurrence risk.44 (2) Prediction using multi-protein biomarker panels: A combination of three biomarkers identified by a random forest model provides high predictive performance in CRSwNP, with an AUC of 0.956.45 (3) Real-time dynamic monitoring: Temporal changes in cytokines IL-13 and IL-5 in nasal secretions are particularly informative. Continuous postoperative monitoring of their fluctuations accurately predicts postsurgical recurrence in patients with CRSwNP, enabling earlier clinical intervention.46

New Strategies for Targeted Therapy

(1)Blockade of key inflammatory mediators: Protein inhibitors targeting central cytokines, such as the anti–IL-5 monoclonal antibody mepolizumab, selectively block IL-5 activity. Clinical trials show that mepolizumab significantly reduces visual analogue scale scores in patients indicated for endoscopic sinus surgery, alleviates symptoms, lowers surgical risk, improves procedural safety, and significantly prolongs the interval between the last sinus surgery and recurrence in patients with recurrent nasal polyps, supporting its clinical utility.47

It is also noteworthy that protein expression signatures vary substantially across inflammatory endotypes, ethnic or geographic populations, medication exposure status and disease stages. Although multiple candidate biomarkers exhibit favourable diagnostic and recurrence-prediction performance, most lack large-scale external validation, confidence interval analysis and prospective clinical verification. For this reason, current proteomic biomarkers remain promising research tools.

Systematic Application of Transcriptomics in CRSwNP Research

Transcriptomics refers to the comprehensive profiling and analysis of all RNA species and their expression levels in cells or tissues under defined physiological or pathological conditions, with the aim of elucidating gene expression regulatory networks.51 Commonly used approaches include microarray analysis and high-throughput RNA sequencing (RNA-seq). Among these, RNA-seq provides high sensitivity, a wide dynamic range, and whole-transcriptome coverage, enabling accurate detection of coding RNA (mRNA), non-coding RNA such as long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) and microRNA (miRNA), as well as alternative splicing variants.52–54 Systematic analysis of differentially expressed genes, combined with bioinformatic methods such as weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), allows identification of key modules and hub genes associated with type 2 inflammation, epithelial barrier disruption, tissue remodelling, and immune cell infiltration. These analyses provide a basis for constructing molecular endotype frameworks in CRSwNP.55 Compared with traditional research models focused on single genes or pathways, transcriptomics simultaneously captures multidimensional information across inflammatory patterns, signalling pathways, cellular components, and epithelial states within a unified data layer. This approach improves efficiency in deciphering the complex pathological processes of CRSwNP and provides a data foundation for subsequent cross-omics integration with proteomics and lipidomics.56

Several inherent limitations exist in current transcriptomic research. Bulk RNA-seq cannot distinguish cell-type-specific transcriptional signals and tends to mask molecular characteristics of minor cell populations. By contrast, single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics, despite their advantages in resolving cellular heterogeneity, are restricted by limited sample sizes and require sophisticated computational analysis pipelines. In addition, transcriptomic profiles are susceptible to confounding factors, including prior corticosteroid or biologic treatment, tissue sampling locations, sequencing platforms and technical batch effects.

Research Progress of Transcriptomics in CRSwNP

Characteristics of Differentially Expressed Genes

In recent years, transcriptomic studies have revealed significant gene expression heterogeneity in nasal polyp tissues from patients with CRSwNP. Large-scale RNA-seq analyses demonstrate that different inflammatory subtypes exhibit distinct transcriptional signatures. In eosinophilic nasal polyps, Th2-associated genes such as IL-5 and IL-13 are specifically upregulated, activating downstream signalling pathways that recruit eosinophils and promote their activation. The chemokine CCL11 (C–C chemokine ligand 11) is also significantly upregulated and directs eosinophil migration to inflammatory sites through strong chemotactic activity. In contrast, non-eosinophilic subtypes show enrichment of interferon signalling–related genes, including IFN-β, which participate in immune regulation by activating immune cells and enhancing antiviral, antitumour, and immune surveillance functions. This pattern differs substantially from the transcriptional profile observed in eosinophilic nasal polyps.57

Altered expression of immune regulatory genes plays a central role in CRSwNP pathogenesis. FOXP3, a key marker of regulatory T cells, is significantly downregulated in nasal polyp tissues. Analyses demonstrate that both mRNA and protein levels of FOXP3 are lower than those in normal control mucosa. Reduced FOXP3 expression weakens the immunosuppressive function of regulatory T cells, disrupts local immune tolerance, and fails to constrain excessive immune activation. This imbalance limits regulation of inflammatory stimuli and promotes an immune microenvironment that supports persistent inflammation, tissue damage, and remodelling during CRSwNP progression.58

In addition, multiple studies indicate consistent downregulation of epithelial barrier–related genes in CRSwNP. Reduced expression of AQP5, which regulates mucosal water transport; CAV1, associated with caveolae formation and signal transduction; LTF, which exerts antibacterial and immunomodulatory effects; and MGB1, which supports epithelial integrity, collectively compromises the structural and defensive functions of the nasal mucosal barrier. This impairment increases susceptibility to allergens, micro-organisms, and inflammatory mediators, thereby promoting mucosal remodelling and nasal polyp formation.59

Analysis of Mechanisms of Action

Differentially expressed genes act in concert rather than in isolation and promote continuous CRSwNP progression through interconnected pathways related to inflammation amplification, barrier impairment, and non-coding RNA regulation.

Th2 cytokine–driven JAK–STAT6 signalling represents a central immune regulatory mechanism. Levels of IL-4 and IL-13 are significantly increased in the inflammatory microenvironment of CRSwNP. Upon receptor binding at the cell surface, these cytokines activate Janus kinases, leading to phosphorylation of signal transducer and activator of transcription 6 (STAT6). This activation promotes differentiation of B cells into immunoglobulin E (IgE)–producing plasma cells. IgE binds receptors on mast cells and basophils, and subsequent allergen exposure triggers degranulation, release of mediators such as histamine, and increased production of eosinophil-attracting chemokines, thereby intensifying inflammatory infiltration and disease severity60,61 (see Figure 3). Chemokine-mediated inflammatory loops further amplify this response. CCL18 efficiently recruits Th2 cells and basophils to inflammatory sites through its molecular properties and promotes IgE secretion by B cells. Bioactive mediators released during basophil degranulation stimulate surrounding cells to produce additional chemokines, including CCL18, forming a self-reinforcing positive feedback loop that sustains inflammatory activity.62 Epithelial barrier sensitisation also contributes substantially. Filaggrin, encoded by the FLG gene, is essential for maintaining epithelial tight junction integrity. Reduced FLG expression disrupts tight junction architecture, creating permeability defects within the mucosal barrier that permit penetration of pathogens and allergens. This exposure activates innate immune responses, induces release of inflammatory mediators such as TNF-α and IL-1β, and initiates downstream inflammatory cascades.63 Non-coding RNA provides an additional layer of precise regulation. miR-155, for example, targets molecules associated with the JAK–STAT pathway and modulates signal transduction, thereby regulating synthesis and secretion of eosinophil-related chemokines. By controlling gene expression at transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels and modulating NF-κB pathway activity through interactions with DNA, RNA, or proteins, miR-155 alters production of inflammatory mediators and chemokines and ultimately affects airway inflammatory responses in CRSwNP.64

Potential Value of Transcriptomics in the Diagnosis and Treatment of CRSwNP

Transcriptomics-driven molecular typing is reshaping clinical management strategies for CRSwNP. Accumulating evidence indicates that expression patterns or genetic variation in disease-associated genes have predictive and stratification value. For example, variation in the MMP1 gene can alter matrix metalloproteinase activity, thereby participating in extracellular matrix remodelling and inflammatory regulation. This variation shows a close association with disease onset, progression, and recurrence risk in CRSwNP and may serve as a molecular indicator for clinical monitoring.65 At the therapeutic level, interventions targeting transcriptional signalling pathways are under development. Inhibition of STAT6 phosphorylation can suppress the canonical Th2 inflammatory pathway mediated by IL-4 and IL-13, thereby reducing IgE production and eosinophil infiltration at an upstream regulatory level. In parallel, restoration or upregulation of epithelial barrier–related genes such as FLG can re-establish tight junction integrity and enhance mucosal defence, reducing inflammatory responses triggered by exogenous stimuli. Current strategies based on these principles remain at the preclinical validation stage.66 Recent advances in spatial transcriptomics and single-cell RNA sequencing have further characterised epithelial remodelling in CRSwNP. These approaches reveal upregulation of epithelial-associated genes, enrichment of epithelial–mesenchymal transition pathways, and increased cellular complexity within the epithelial compartment. Among these findings, ALOX15⁺ epithelial cells show selectively high expression in nasal polyps and display a strong association with epithelial remodelling. Experimental inhibition of ALOX15 reduces transcription of epithelial–mesenchymal transition–related genes, suggesting that ALOX15 represents a potential target for modulating abnormal epithelial remodelling in CRSwNP.67

Looking ahead, transcriptomics, particularly single-cell and spatial approaches, is expected to enable more detailed dissection of heterogeneity within the mucosal immune microenvironment of CRSwNP. Precise localisation of key immune cell subsets, cytokine and chemokine networks, and intercellular communication patterns may facilitate identification of more specific and clinically translatable therapeutic targets. These advances provide a technical foundation for stratified disease management and more precise diagnosis and treatment of CRSwNP.

A core limitation of transcriptomic research is that changes in gene expression do not always correspond directly to protein abundance or functional activity of lipid mediators. To improve mechanistic interpretation and clinical translation, future studies are recommended to integrate transcriptomic data with matched proteomic, lipidomic and comprehensive clinical datasets.

Systematic Analysis and Future Blueprint of Multi-Omics Combined Research in CRSwNP

Advantages of Multi-Omics Combined Research

The core value of multi-omics integration lies in aligning observed clinical phenomena with underlying pathogenic chains. Lipidomics outlines the inflammatory microenvironment and metabolic imbalance of membrane and signalling lipids; proteomics identifies functional proteins that execute inflammation, epithelial barrier disruption, and tissue remodelling; transcriptomics explains why these alterations occur and clarifies the inflammatory endotypes in which they are most prominent through gene regulatory mechanisms. Integrating these layers enables the following advances.

Mechanistic Complementarity Within Shared Pathways

Representative studies illustrate how complementary omics layers converge on common mechanisms. In perfluorooctanoic acid (PFOA) research, integration of multiple mass spectrometry platforms, including liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS), matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionisation mass spectrometry imaging (MALDI-MSI), and time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS), resolved region-specific lipid alterations associated with PFOA localisation, revealing lipidomic effects in mouse liver after short-term high-dose exposure and advancing toxicological interpretation.68 In colorectal cancer, combined lipidomics and spatial transcriptomics characterised expression patterns and cellular localisation of enzymes that generate and modify lipid mediators, supporting the concept that defective lipid class switching, potentially related to insufficient or ineffective PGE2/prostaglandin D2 signalling, contributes to tumour-associated inflammation.69 To identify disease-driving genes, differential centrality–ensemble analysis (DiCE) integrates transcriptomic expression profiles with protein–protein interaction networks, refines candidates using information gain filters, and ranks genes by expression change and network influence. Validation in prostate cancer datasets demonstrates its utility for unbiased biomarker discovery and therapeutic development.70 In metabolic regulation, transcriptomics and lipidomics revealed that extracellular matrix–derived mechanical forces regulate sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1 (SREBP1), controlling geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate production and thereby influencing stem cell fate and lipid storage in model systems.71

Spatiotemporal Dynamics

Synchronised analyses that pair transcriptional and proteomic data elucidate temporal ordering across regulatory layers. For example, studies of FoxO3 regulation of hepatic lipid metabolism demonstrate that FoxO3 modulates triglyceride synthesis through upstream transcriptional control of SREBP1c, establishing a dynamic loop from transcriptional signalling to protein expression and lipid phenotype. Such designs provide a reference for resolving questions in CRSwNP regarding timing of inflammatory activation, cell population specificity, and initial metabolic pathway engagement.72

Refined Endotype Classification

Multi-omics clustering subdivides clinically similar CRSwNP into molecularly distinct endotypes. These include a Th2-high type, characterised by elevated IL-4 and IL-13 expression and upregulation of sphingosine-1-phosphate within sphingolipid metabolism, co-expressed with IL-4/IL-13 proteins and activating downstream JAK–STAT6 signalling;73,74 a Th17-high type enriched for IL-17–related signalling; and a metabolic dysregulation type significant by abnormalities in lipid and amino acid metabolism. Identification of these endotypes provides a foundation for personalised therapy and is expected to improve treatment efficacy.75–77

Optimisation of Predictive Modelling

Integrative models that combine lipid metabolites, proteins, and clinical indicators have high predictive value in CRSwNP. Serum uric acid (SUA) has been identified as an independent metabolic biomarker for postoperative recurrence, reflecting perturbations in purine metabolism and providing a potential intervention target.78 Fluctuations in inflammatory proteins during disease progression, such as IL-4 and IL-5, reflect the magnitude of type 2 inflammation.79 By integrating clinical variables with multi-omics indicators and applying machine learning approaches, predictive models can identify patients at high risk of recurrence, enabling individualised treatment planning, improved therapeutic efficacy, and reduced recurrence rates.

Application Examples: From Molecular Mapping to Clinical Translation

Discovery of Key Pathway Interaction Networks

Lipid–protein interaction hubs: PGE2, an important arachidonic acid metabolite, plays a central role in multiple physiological and pathological processes. At the level of cellular signal transduction, PGE2 binds to the EP4 receptor, activates the downstream Gαs protein, and sequentially triggers phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) and protein kinase B (AKT), culminating in activation of the mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway. This signalling cascade upregulates transcription of the MMP-9 gene and increases MMP-9 expression. As a zinc-dependent endopeptidase, MMP-9 degrades extracellular matrix components such as collagen and gelatin, accelerates matrix turnover, and exerts a strong effect on pathological processes associated with chronic inflammation.80,81

Transcription–metabolism feedback loop: During immune-inflammatory responses, activation of the STAT6 transcription factor through the JAK/STAT signalling pathway upregulates expression of key enzymes, including 5-lipoxygenase (5-LOX). This regulation promotes arachidonic acid metabolism and drives synthesis of leukotriene B4 (LTB4). LTB4 binds its receptor LTB4R1, activates the IKK complex, and induces phosphorylation and degradation of IκB, thereby releasing NF-κB (p50/RelA dimer) for nuclear translocation. Activated NF-κB binds directly to the IL-5 promoter region and significantly increases IL-5 transcription, reported to reach up to a 4.8-fold increase. IL-5 subsequently feeds back to activate STAT6 signalling, forming a self-reinforcing inflammatory loop described as “STAT6 → LTB4 → NF-κB → IL-5 → STAT6.” This positive feedback mechanism serves as a key driver in the pathological progression of chronic inflammatory diseases.82–84

Spatial transcriptomics–defined microregion-specific networks: Using 10x Visium spatial transcriptomics, enrichment of profibrotic genes has been identified in the polyp core region. Genes such as FOS (FOS proto-oncogene, AP-1 transcription factor subunit) and JNK1 (c-Jun N-terminal kinase 1) show increased expression, promoting extracellular matrix synthesis and deposition and accelerating fibrotic progression. In contrast, the marginal region displays high expression of CCL18 and vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGFA), which promotes angiogenesis and supports nutrient supply for polyp growth. Further analyses demonstrate that colony-stimulating factor 1 (CSF1) is highly expressed in macrophages within the core region and shows a positive correlation with fibrosis severity (r = 0.72). These findings indicate that CSF1 plays a critical role in polyp fibrosis and represents a potential target for antifibrotic therapy in CRSwNP.85,86

Future Prospects

AI-Driven Integration Algorithm Innovation

Integration of multi-omics data with AI represents a major advance in understanding complex biological systems and in predicting responses to environmental changes and therapeutic perturbations. Combining AI with mechanism-based modelling facilitates identification of novel molecular targets, biomarkers, drug formulations, and personalised therapeutic strategies for CRSwNP, addressing emerging clinical needs.92 Most existing prospective studies apply AI primarily as a post hoc analytical tool. Although integration of multimodal data has expanded the capacity to identify meta-biomarkers and many studies suggest promise in predicting immunotherapy benefit, high-quality evidence sufficient to alter clinical practice remains limited.93 Recent advances in other disease fields illustrate translational potential. In colorectal cancer, an AI model based on high-sensitivity metabolites demonstrates substantial promise in improving adenoma detection rates, highlighting the clinical applicability of non-invasive detection combined with AI and genomics.94 In prostate cancer, integration of single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics reveals crosstalk between microenvironmental cellular heterogeneity and tumour progression at single-cell resolution, supporting improved diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.95 Spatial proteomics at whole-tissue scale remains constrained by lack of protein amplification and mass spectrometry sensitivity. A newly developed computationally assisted sparse sampling strategy (S4P) significantly reduces sample requirements according to spatial resolution and is expected to extend spatial proteomic analysis to diverse tissue types.96 Future CRSwNP research may benefit from AI-driven multimodal data fusion, such as combining metabolomic LTB4 profiles with single-cell transcriptomics to optimise responses to biologic therapy, and applying the S4P strategy to achieve super-resolution spatial proteomic analysis of sinus mucosal microregions to resolve lipid–protein interaction networks in eosinophil-enriched areas. Lessons from single-cell and spatial multi-omics integration in prostate cancer may guide analysis of cell-specific regulation within the IL-5–STAT6–LTB4R1 axis in type 2 inflammation. Development of non-invasive AI-based diagnostic tools derived from nasal secretion metabolites may facilitate early molecular typing and precise intervention. Collectively, these approaches are expected to accelerate clinical translation and support the full life cycle of software-based biomarkers, from development and validation to integration into routine clinical practice.

Filling Gaps in Asian Population–Specific Data

Current multi-omics studies show a significant scarcity of data from Asian populations. Individuals of Asian ancestry represent approximately 1.6% of global genomic databases, with particularly limited representation of Southeast Asian indigenous populations, which account for nearly one-third of the world’s indigenous groups. This imbalance restricts understanding of population-specific disease mechanisms.97 Existing multi-omics platforms show limited sensitivity for molecular features unique to Asian populations, and lack of cross-platform standardisation exacerbates data heterogeneity. Therefore, AI-based prediction models, including those estimating immunotherapy response, remain difficult to apply directly to clinical decision-making in these populations. Evidence from oncology highlights the value of population-focused initiatives. In breast cancer research, the China Cancer Genome Atlas (CBCGA) project combined clinical, transcriptomic, metabolomic, radiomic, and pathological data from a large cohort (n = 773), revealing molecular and clinical characteristics specific to Chinese patients, including tumour microenvironment features, gene mutations, and multi-omics interrelationships. Machine learning models enabled prediction and classification, providing public resources and insights into ancestry-specific biology and precision treatment strategies.98 In metabolic disease research, integration of genomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and microbiome data has clarified biological mechanisms of type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and metabolic dysfunction–associated fatty liver disease in East Asian populations, informing region-specific public health strategies.99 In CRSwNP, multi-omics research is beginning to address data scarcity through large-scale genomic projects, AI-based integration algorithms, and super-resolution technologies such as S4P. Promotion of initiatives such as the SEA10K project, development of cross-ethnic metabolite reference databases, design of Asia-centred clinical trials, and integration of regional genomic resources will support construction of population-relevant molecular maps and mitigate limitations in model generalisability. Future priorities include establishment of dedicated databases and rigorous clinical validation to advance precision medicine approaches tailored to Asian populations.

Breaking Through Clinical Translation Bottlenecks

Examples from other diseases illustrate how multi-omics integration can accelerate translation. In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma drug resistance, combined transcriptomic and proteomic analyses using RNA sequencing, multiplex immunofluorescence, and immunohistochemistry identified cFAM124A as a novel biomarker, providing new therapeutic options for drug-resistant disease.100 In non-alcoholic fatty liver disease research, integration of lipidomics with serum nuclear magnetic resonance–based transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses assessed associations with all-cause and cause-specific mortality in Asian populations.101 In studies of tumour immune escape, application of CRISPR–Cas9 technology together with lipidomics, transcriptomics, single-cell sequencing, and proteomics showed that inhibition of sphingolipid synthesis significantly suppresses tumour growth and enhances immune-mediated tumour clearance in murine models. These findings identify potential molecular targets and inform development of new immunotherapeutic strategies.102 By integrating genomic, lipidomic, proteomic, and transcriptomic data, enabling precise molecular stratification, guiding individualised biologic therapy, and combining radiomics with machine learning to construct recurrence prediction systems, these advances are shifting CRSwNP management from empiric treatment towards precision medicine and opening new avenues for clinical translation. Overall, multi-omics integration is emerging as a central driver of progress in CRSwNP research, transitioning the field from descriptive characterisation towards mechanism-based and precision intervention strategies. Genomics identifies disease-associated variants, transcriptomics defines expression patterns of key inflammatory genes, and proteomics and lipidomics characterise downstream protein and metabolite alterations. With continued advances in single-cell multi-omics technologies, AI-enabled analysis of large-scale datasets, and minimally invasive sampling methods, integration of these approaches is expected to support a paradigm of comprehensive molecular assessment through a single test. Over the next decade, this framework may enable more accurate delineation of CRSwNP molecular features and pathological processes and support precise diagnosis and personalised treatment strategies.

Conclusion and Prospects

This article systematically summarises the molecular characteristics and key pathways of CRSwNP and their intrinsic associations with inflammation, epithelial barrier disruption, and tissue remodelling, across three molecular layers: lipidomics, proteomics, and transcriptomics. Three major conclusions emerge: Lipid mediators amplify inflammation and impair epithelial barrier function; Proteomic signatures enable non-invasive diagnosis and recurrence prediction; Transcriptomics delineates molecular endotypes that explain treatment heterogeneity. Current evidence indicates that many studies remain confined to single-omics analyses, and cross-level and cross-pathway integration remains limited. This constraint hampers comprehensive interpretation of key clinical features, including heterogeneous inflammatory endotypes, significant differences in treatment response, and high postoperative recurrence rates. The principal value of a combined multi-omics strategy lies in concurrent analysis of metabolic processes at the lipid level, functional execution at the protein level, and regulatory control at the transcriptional level within a unified disease framework. This approach enables construction of a closed analytical loop linking biomarker discovery, mechanistic validation, and intervention assessment, thereby supporting biomarker screening, patient stratification, identification of populations most likely to benefit from biologic agents, and development of novel local drug delivery strategies.

Nonetheless, multiple challenges limit clinical translation, including population-level heterogeneity, small sample cohorts, batch effects, inadequate external validation, and insufficient cross-layer integration. Future studies will require standardized analytical protocols, large well-characterized cohorts focused on Asian populations, and advanced artificial intelligence-assisted integration models to enhance reproducibility and translational value.

In summary, multi-omics integration represents a powerful systems-biology tool for dissecting the complex pathogenesis of CRSwNP, identifying actionable biomarkers, and advancing precision medicine. With continued improvements in single-cell and spatial multi-omics technologies, such approaches will further strengthen risk stratification, targeted intervention, and personalized management strategies, ultimately improving long-term outcomes for patients with CRSwNP.

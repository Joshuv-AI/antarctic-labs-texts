Manifold Trajectories in Next-Token Prediction: From Replicator Dynamics to Softmax Equilibrium
Christopher R. Lee-Jenkins

Abstract
Decoding in large language models is often described as scoring tokens and normalizing with softmax. We give a minimal, self-contained account of this step as a constrained variational principle on the probability simplex. The discrete, normalization-respecting ascent is the classical multiplicative-weights (entropic mirror) update; its continuous-time limit is the replicator flow. From these ingredients we prove that, for a fixed context and temperature, the next-token distribution follows a smooth trajectory inside the simplex and converges to the softmax equilibrium. This formalizes the common “manifold traversal” intuition at the output-distribution level. The analysis yields precise, practice-facing consequences: temperature acts as an exact rescaling of time along the same trajectory, while top-k and nucleus sampling restrict the flow to a face with identical guarantees. We also outline a controlled account of path-dependent score adjustments and their connection to loop-like, hallucination-style behavior. We make no claims about training dynamics or internal representations; those are deferred to future work.

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) are typically described as choosing the next token by
scoring a vocabulary and normalizing the scores with a softmax at some temperature.
This account is operationally correct, but it leaves open a conceptual question often
raised in the community:
what does the decoding step look like as a dynamical process?

In particular, when practitioners say that models “traverse a manifold” during decoding,
is this merely a metaphor, or can one make it a theorem, precisely stated and proved from
basic, verifiable ingredients?

This paper gives a minimal, self-contained answer at the level of the
output distribution

for a fixed context. We show that the next-token distribution is characterized by a classical
free-energy principle and that the normalization-respecting ascent to that equilibrium admits
both (i) a one-line discrete update—entropic mirror ascent, i.e., multiplicative weights—and
(ii) a continuous-time limit—the replicator differential equation on the probability simplex.
From these pieces we obtain a rigorous statement of the manifold picture: the decoding
distribution follows a smooth trajectory in the open simplex and converges to the softmax
equilibrium. The development uses only standard tools (entropy, convex duality, mirror
methods, replicator dynamics); no geometric background is
assumed in the body of the paper.

2
A Variational Principle for Next-Token Selection

Fix a decoding context and vocabulary size
V\geq 2
. Let
s\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
denote the (fixed) score/logit vector produced by the model for the next token and
T>0
the temperature. We work on the probability simplex

\Delta^{V-1}\;=\;\big\{\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{V}_{\geq 0}\ :\ \sum_{i=1}^{V}p_{i}=1\,\big\},

and use Shannon entropy
H(p)=-\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i}
(convention
0\log 0:=0
). Consider the
classical “free-energy” objective from statistical mechanics / information theory,

(2.1)

\mathcal{F}(p)\;=\;\left\langle p,\,s\right\rangle\;+\;T\,H(p)\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{V}p_{i}s_{i}\;-\;T\sum_{i=1}^{V}p_{i}\log p_{i},\qquad p\in\Delta^{V-1},

which is a
concave
functional balancing score maximization against entropy. This setup is standard
(see
[
10
,
7
]
; convex-analytic background in
5
, Ch. 3
).
We record the derivation for completeness and to fix notation.

2.1
Softmax as the unique maximizer (Gibbs variational principle)

Proposition 2.1

(Softmax variational characterization; standard)
.

For fixed
s\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
and
T>0
, the concave program

\max_{p\in\Delta^{V-1}}\ \mathcal{F}(p)=\left\langle p,\,s\right\rangle+T\,H(p)

has the unique solution

\pi_{i}\;=\;\frac{e^{s_{i}/T}}{\sum_{j=1}^{V}e^{s_{j}/T}}\qquad(i=1,\dots,V),

i.e., the softmax distribution at temperature
T
.

Proof.

Introduce the Lagrangian for the affine constraint
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
:

\mathcal{L}(p,\lambda)\;=\;\sum_{i}p_{i}s_{i}\;-\;T\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i}\;+\;\lambda\Big(1-\sum_{i}p_{i}\Big).

On the relative interior of
\Delta^{V-1}
, stationarity gives, for each
i
,

\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial p_{i}}\;=\;s_{i}\;-\;T\big(1+\log p_{i}\big)\;-\;\lambda\;=\;0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\log p_{i}\;=\;\frac{s_{i}-\lambda-T}{T}.

Hence
p_{i}=C\,e^{s_{i}/T}
with
C=e^{-(\lambda+T)/T}
. Enforcing
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
yields

C=(\sum_{j}e^{s_{j}/T})^{-1}
and thus
p=\pi
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is strictly concave on the relative interior (Lemma
2.2
), the maximizer is unique.
[
10
,
7
, See also the classical Gibbs principle in]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2

(Strict concavity)
.

The map
\mathcal{F}:\Delta^{V-1}\to\mathbb{R}
in (
2.1
) is strictly concave on the relative interior of
\Delta^{V-1}
.

Proof.

The term
p\mapsto\left\langle p,\,s\right\rangle
is linear. Since
-\,\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i}
is strictly concave on the relative interior of
\Delta^{V-1}
,
H
is strictly concave there (and continuous on
\Delta^{V-1}
). Therefore
\mathcal{F}=\left\langle p,\,s\right\rangle+T\,H(p)
is strictly concave on the relative interior, and for
T>0
the unique maximizer lies in the interior. For background on entropy curvature, see
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
.
∎

Remark 2.3

(Normalization and interiority)
.

For
T>0
, the maximizer
\pi
satisfies
\pi_{i}>0
for all
i
; thus
\pi\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
. Intuitively, the entropic term at positive temperature rules out boundary optima unless some
s_{i}=-\infty
.

2.2
Dual (log-sum-exp) viewpoint

Define the log-partition function (log-sum-exp with temperature)

(2.2)

A(s)\;=\;T\log\!\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{V}e^{s_{i}/T}\Big).

Then
A
is convex, smooth, and satisfies the Gibbs/Fenchel variational identity

(2.3)

A(s)\;=\;\max_{p\in\Delta^{V-1}}\ \Big\{\ \left\langle p,\,s\right\rangle\;+\;T\,H(p)\ \Big\},

a special case of convex duality
[
14
, cf.]
and the classical Gibbs principle
[
10
,
7
]
. In exponential-family form,

\nabla A(s)=\pi(s)\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla^{2}A(s)\;=\;\frac{1}{T}\Big(\mathrm{diag}(\pi)\;-\;\pi\pi^{\top}\Big),

the familiar Fisher information form; see
Wainwright and Jordan 2008
,
Murphy 2012
,
Bishop 2006
.

Remark 2.4

(Smoothness and sensitivity)
.

The Jacobian
(\partial\pi_{i}/\partial s_{j})_{ij}=\frac{1}{T}\big(\mathrm{diag}(\pi)-\pi\pi^{\top}\big)
shows the map
s\mapsto\pi(s)
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{V}
and Lipschitz on compact sets, with sensitivity scaling like
1/T
; cf.
Murphy 2012
.

2.3
Temperature, limits, and basic properties

Proposition 2.5

(Temperature limits)
.

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
and let
\pi(T)
denote the softmax at temperature
T>0
.

1.

As
T\downarrow 0
,
\pi(T)
concentrates on the set of indices attaining
\max_{i}s_{i}
. If the maximizer is unique, then
\pi(T)\to e_{i^{\star}}
where
i^{\star}\in\argmax_{i}s_{i}
.

2.

As
T\uparrow\infty
,
\pi(T)\to\tfrac{1}{V}\,\mathbbm{1}
, the uniform distribution.

Proof.

Both statements follow directly from
\pi_{i}(T)=\exp(s_{i}/T)/\sum_{j}\exp(s_{j}/T)
and standard Laplace-type asymptotics; see, e.g.,
Cover and Thomas 2006
.
∎

Remark 2.6

(Score shifts and invariances)
.

Adding a constant to all scores
s\mapsto s+c\,\mathbbm{1}
leaves
\pi
unchanged and shifts
A(s)
by
c
. Scaling
s\mapsto\alpha s
is equivalent to rescaling the temperature
T\mapsto T/\alpha
. These invariances let us interpret
T
as a
selective pressure
parameter.

We have established a minimal variational principle for decoding: the next-token distribution is the unique maximizer of the strictly concave free energy (
2.1
), equivalently the gradient of the convex potential
A
in (
2.2
) via (
2.3
). In the next section we derive
constraint-respecting updates
that ascend
\mathcal{F}
on the simplex—the discrete-time mirror (multiplicative-weights) step and its continuous-time limit, the replicator ODE—which inherit the normalization, interiority, and temperature behavior recorded here.

3
Constraint-Respecting Updates: Mirror
\rightarrow
Replicator

In §
2
we showed that for fixed scores
s\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
and temperature
T>0
, the next-token distribution is the unique maximizer of the concave free energy

\mathcal{F}(p)=\left\langle p,\,s\right\rangle+T\,H(p)\qquad\text{over }\Delta^{V-1}.

We now derive a discrete-time update that
ascends

\mathcal{F}
while respecting the simplex constraint, and then pass to a continuous-time limit. Throughout this section,
s
is fixed (context is frozen); extensions where
s
depends on
p
are deferred to §
5
.

3.1
Entropic mirror ascent equals multiplicative weights

We use the entropic mirror map (negative Shannon entropy), which yields the classical multiplicative-weights (MW) update; see
Beck and Teboulle 2003
,
Arora et al. 2012
,
Raginsky and Bouvrie 2012
.

Definition 3.1

(KL divergence)
.

For
p,q\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
, let
D_{\KL}(p\|q)=\sum_{i}p_{i}\log\!\frac{p_{i}}{q_{i}}
.

Proposition 3.2

(One-step mirror ascent)
.

Given
p^{(t)}\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
and step size
\eta>0
, the entropic mirror-ascent step for maximizing
\mathcal{F}
is

(3.1)

p^{(t+1)}\;=\;\argmax_{p\in\Delta^{V-1}}\ \Big\{\,\left\langle p,\,s\right\rangle+T\,H(p)\;-\;\frac{1}{\eta}\,D_{\KL}\!\big(p\|p^{(t)}\big)\,\Big\}.

Its unique solution has the multiplicative-weights form

(3.2)

p^{(t+1)}_{i}\;=\;\frac{p^{(t)}_{i}\exp\!\big(\tfrac{\eta}{T}\,s_{i}\big)}{\sum_{j=1}^{V}p^{(t)}_{j}\exp\!\big(\tfrac{\eta}{T}\,s_{j}\big)}\,,\qquad i=1,\dots,V.

Proof (for completeness).

First-order optimality for (
3.1
) with the affine constraint
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
gives

0=s_{i}-T(1+\log p_{i})-\tfrac{1}{\eta}\big(\log p_{i}-\log p^{(t)}_{i}\big)-\lambda\,,

hence
\log p_{i}=\log p^{(t)}_{i}+\tfrac{\eta}{T}s_{i}-\eta(1+\tfrac{\lambda}{T})
, which yields

p_{i}\propto p^{(t)}_{i}\exp((\eta/T)s_{i})
. Normalizing over
i
gives (
3.2
). Strict concavity of the objective in (
3.1
) on
\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
ensures uniqueness.
∎

Proposition 3.3

(Monotone ascent of
\mathcal{F}
)
.

The update (
3.1
) satisfies

\mathcal{F}\!\big(p^{(t+1)}\big)\;\geq\;\mathcal{F}\!\big(p^{(t)}\big)\;+\;\frac{1}{\eta}\,D_{\KL}\!\big(p^{(t+1)}\|p^{(t)}\big)\;\geq\;\mathcal{F}\!\big(p^{(t)}\big).

In particular,
t\mapsto\mathcal{F}\!\big(p^{(t)}\big)
is nondecreasing, with equality iff
p^{(t+1)}=p^{(t)}
.

Proof.

By optimality of
p^{(t+1)}
in (
3.1
),

\left\langle p^{(t+1)},\,s\right\rangle+T\,H(p^{(t+1)})-\tfrac{1}{\eta}D_{\KL}\!\big(p^{(t+1)}\|p^{(t)}\big)\;\geq\;\left\langle p^{(t)},\,s\right\rangle+T\,H(p^{(t)})\,.

Rearrange to obtain the claim.
∎

Remark 3.4

(Interior invariance)
.

If
p^{(t)}\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
and
s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, then (
3.2
) yields
p^{(t+1)}\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
and preserves normalization. Thus the update is
constraint-respecting
and stays strictly inside the simplex.

3.2
Continuous-time limit: the replicator ODE

We now pass to a continuous-time limit of (
3.2
) by letting
\eta\to 0
.

Proposition 3.5

(Replicator dynamics)
.

Let
p^{(t+1)}
be given by (
3.2
). Then

\frac{p^{(t+1)}_{i}-p^{(t)}_{i}}{\eta}\ \Longrightarrow\ \dot{p}_{i}\;=\;\frac{1}{T}\,p_{i}\,\big(s_{i}-\bar{s}\big),\qquad\bar{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{V}p_{j}s_{j},

as
\eta\to 0
. That is, the continuous-time limit is the replicator ODE on
\Delta^{V-1}
with “fitness”
f_{i}=s_{i}/T
.
[
9
,
16
,
15
, See, e.g.,]
.

Proof (for completeness).

Using
\exp(\eta a)=1+\eta a+o(\eta)
,

p^{(t+1)}_{i}=\frac{p^{(t)}_{i}\big(1+\tfrac{\eta}{T}s_{i}+o(\eta)\big)}{\sum_{j}p^{(t)}_{j}\big(1+\tfrac{\eta}{T}s_{j}+o(\eta)\big)}=p^{(t)}_{i}\Big(1+\tfrac{\eta}{T}(s_{i}-\bar{s})+o(\eta)\Big).

Subtract
p^{(t)}_{i}
, divide by
\eta
, and take
\eta\to 0
.
∎

The replicator ODE keeps trajectories on the simplex and preserves nonnegativity:

Lemma 3.6

(Forward invariance)
.

For the ODE
\dot{p}_{i}=\tfrac{1}{T}p_{i}(s_{i}-\bar{s})
with
p(0)\in\Delta^{V-1}
, we have
\sum_{i}p_{i}(t)=1
for all
t
, and if
p_{i}(0)=0
then
p_{i}(t)\equiv 0
; in particular,
\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
and each face of
\Delta^{V-1}
are forward-invariant.

Proof.

Summing gives
\sum_{i}\dot{p}_{i}=\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_{i}p_{i}(s_{i}-\bar{s})=0
. If
p_{i}(0)=0
, then
\dot{p}_{i}(0)=0
and uniqueness of ODE solutions yields
p_{i}(t)\equiv 0
.
∎

3.3
Lyapunov ascent and convergence (fixed scores)

Replicator dynamics is a gradient flow of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to the Shahshahani metric on the simplex
[
16
,
9
]
, hence
\mathcal{F}
is a Lyapunov function.

Proposition 3.7

(Energy ascent)
.

Along any solution of
\dot{p}_{i}=\tfrac{1}{T}p_{i}(s_{i}-\bar{s})
with fixed
s
,

\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathcal{F}(p(t))\;\geq\;0,

with equality iff
p(t)
is stationary (i.e.,
p=\pi
from Proposition
2.1
).

Proof (sketch).

The replicator vector field equals the (natural) gradient of
\mathcal{F}
under the Shahshahani inner product

\langle u,v\rangle_{p}=\sum_{i}\tfrac{u_{i}v_{i}}{p_{i}}
on the tangent space to
\Delta^{V-1}
. Therefore

\tfrac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}=\langle\nabla^{\text{nat}}\mathcal{F},\nabla^{\text{nat}}\mathcal{F}\rangle_{p}\geq 0
,
with equality iff the field vanishes; see
Shahshahani 1979
,
Hofbauer and Sigmund 1998
.
A direct coordinate proof is also possible by differentiating
\mathcal{F}
and substituting
\dot{p}
.
∎

Corollary 3.8

(Convergence to softmax)
.

For fixed
s
and
T>0
, any trajectory with
p(0)\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
satisfies
p(t)\to\pi
as
t\to\infty
, where
\pi
is the softmax maximizer of §
2
.

Remark 3.9

(Top-
k
/nucleus as faces of the simplex)
.

Practical decoders often restrict support before renormalization (top-
k
, nucleus). By Lemma
3.6
, faces of
\Delta^{V-1}
are invariant under replicator flow, and the mirror step (
3.1
) respects the constraint set. Thus the same analysis applies
verbatim
on any fixed face (subset of tokens), with the softmax equilibrium computed on that face.

In §
4
we use these facts to state and prove the manifold-traversal theorem for decoding trajectories and to connect temperature and truncation to geometric features of the flow.

4
Manifold Traversal of Decoding Trajectories

We now formalize the common intuition that decoding “moves along a manifold.” In our setting the manifold is simply the open probability simplex

\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}\;=\;\Big\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{V}_{>0}:\sum_{i=1}^{V}p_{i}=1\Big\},

a smooth
(V-1)
–dimensional submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{V}
with tangent space

T_{p}\Delta^{V-1}\;=\;\Big\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{V}:\sum_{i=1}^{V}u_{i}=0\Big\}.

The replicator vector field from §
3
is

(4.1)

X_{i}(p)\;=\;\frac{1}{T}\,p_{i}\big(s_{i}-\bar{s}\big),\qquad\bar{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{V}p_{j}s_{j},

which is smooth on
\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
and satisfies
\sum_{i}X_{i}(p)=0
, hence
X(p)\in T_{p}\Delta^{V-1}
(tangency).

The following theorem concerns the output distribution for a fixed context; it does not address training dynamics or internal representations.

4.1
The manifold-traversal theorem

Theorem 4.1

(Manifold traversal of decoding trajectories)
.

Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
and
T>0
. Consider the ODE
\dot{p}=X(p)
on
\Delta^{V-1}
with
X
given by (
4.1
).
For any initial condition
p(0)\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
:

1.

(
Well-posed flow on the manifold
) There is a unique global solution
p:[0,\infty)\to\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
with
p(t)\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
for all
t\geq 0
, and
p(\cdot)
is a
C^{1}
curve on the manifold
\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
.

2.

(
Ascent of free energy
) The free energy
\mathcal{F}(p)=\left\langle p,\,s\right\rangle+T\,H(p)
from §
2
is a Lyapunov function:
\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathcal{F}(p(t))\geq 0
, with equality iff
p(t)
is stationary.

3.

(
Equilibrium and convergence
) The unique stationary point in
\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
is the softmax
\pi
(Proposition
2.1
), and
p(t)\to\pi
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

(1) By Lemma
3.6
,
\sum_{i}p_{i}(t)=1
and faces of
\Delta^{V-1}
are forward-invariant; since
p(0)\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
and
X
is smooth on
\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
, standard ODE theory yields a unique solution
p(t)\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
for all
t\geq 0
.
(2) Proposition
3.7
shows
\mathcal{F}
is nondecreasing along trajectories, with strict increase unless stationary.
(3) The stationary condition
\dot{p}=0
with
p\in\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
forces
s_{i}=\bar{s}
for all
i
in the support weighted by
p
, which coincides with the KKT conditions for the maximizer of
\mathcal{F}
; by Proposition
2.1
the unique interior maximizer is
\pi
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is bounded above by
\mathcal{F}(\pi)
and strictly increases unless at equilibrium, LaSalle’s invariance principle yields
p(t)\to\pi
. See also the replicator-as-gradient-flow viewpoint in
Shahshahani 1979
,
Hofbauer and Sigmund 1998
.
∎

intuition into a theorem at the output-distribution level, and deriving exact corollaries for temperature (time rescaling)
and truncation (face invariance).

Under the Shahshahani inner product
\langle u,v\rangle_{p}=\sum_{i}\frac{u_{i}v_{i}}{p_{i}}

Corollary 4.4

(Temperature is a time rescaling)

For fixed

in (

(Truncation acts on faces)

If decoding restricts support to a fixed subset

(e.g., top-

\Delta^{V-1}_{S}=\{p\in\Delta^{V-1}:p_{i}=0\ \forall i\notin S\}

By Lemma

Remark 4.6

(Logit biases and affine invariances)
.

Adding a constant

5
Phenomenology: Temperature, Truncation, and Path-Dependence

We keep the model in §
2
–
4
and discuss three practical themes:
(i) temperature as a time rescaling of the trajectory; (ii) truncation (top-
k
/nucleus) as an
invariant-face restriction; (iii) path-dependent preferences as a mechanism for “hallucination”-like
behavior. Throughout, we preserve the ML-facing language of scores and probabilities and avoid any
geometric formalism beyond the simplex.

5.1
Temperature as selective pressure and effective time

With fixed scores
s
, the replicator field is
X_{i}(p)=\tfrac{1}{T}\,p_{i}(s_{i}-\bar{s})
(§
3
).
Replacing
T
by a positive, time-varying schedule
T(t)
gives

(5.1)

\dot{p}_{i}(t)\;=\;\frac{1}{T(t)}\,p_{i}(t)\,\big(s_{i}-\bar{s}(t)\big).

Let
\tau(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{T(u)}\,du
. Then
p(t)=\tilde{p}(\tau(t))
, where

\tilde{p}
solves the constant-temperature ODE
\frac{d}{d\tau}\tilde{p}_{i}=\tilde{p}_{i}(s_{i}-\bar{s})
.
Thus temperature schedules reparameterize
time along the same manifold trajectory

(Corollary
4.4
). Lower
T
accelerates motion (higher selective pressure);
higher
T
slows motion and yields more uniform equilibria (Proposition
2.5
).

Remark 5.1

(Practical reading)
.

Annealing (
T\downarrow
) sharpens the distribution faster along the same path; cooling too abruptly
can cause early lock-in on spurious modes, while warming (
T\uparrow
) slows convergence and increases diversity.

5.2
Truncation as dynamics on a face of the simplex

Let
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,V\}
be a fixed support set (top-
k
or nucleus). Define the face

\Delta^{V-1}_{S}=\{p\in\Delta^{V-1}:p_{i}=0\ \forall i\notin S\}
. By Lemma
3.6
,

\Delta^{V-1}_{S}
is forward-invariant for the replicator ODE, and the mirror step
(
3.1
) respects
\Delta^{V-1}_{S}
by construction. All results from
§
3
–
4
hold
verbatim
on
\Delta^{V-1}_{S}
, with softmax and free energy
computed using indices in
S
only. Truncation therefore acts as an
ecological bottleneck
:
the same ascent principle operates on a lower-dimensional manifold.

5.3
Path-dependent preferences and “hallucination”-like behavior

Sections
3
–
4
assumed
s\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
fixed (context frozen). In practice, the
scores used during decoding can acquire
path dependence
, e.g., via token-dependent biases,
heuristics modifying logits after each step, or implicit feedback from the partial output. To discuss
this briefly, consider a mild dependence
s=s(p)
that is Lipschitz on
\mathrm{int}\,\Delta^{V-1}
.
Then the vector field

\dot{p}_{i}\;=\;\frac{1}{T}\,p_{i}\big(s_{i}(p)-\bar{s}(p)\big)

is well-defined and tangent to the simplex. When
s
derives from a potential, the flow remains
a gradient ascent of a generalized free energy; but in general
s(p)
can include non-potential (curl)
components, producing
nonconservative
dynamics on
\Delta^{V-1}
.

Nonconservative preference fields can yield
loops
and
lock-ins
: along a closed path in
p
,
the net change in alignment need not cancel, permitting cycles or self-reinforcing excursions away from
the original context. Phenomenologically, such path-dependent drifts can present as “hallucination”-like
behavior: local selection is strong (the replicator field is large in norm), yet global coherence is weak
because successive local preferences are not globally integrable. We deliberately avoid stronger claims:
formal analysis of path-dependent
s(p)
lies beyond our scope here, see Figure
1
for a schematic of this perspective.

Figure 1
:
Left: three internal “lifts”; the faint straight guide lines indicate the fixed
vertical projection direction
toward the simplex plane. Right: projecting each lifted curve
along that vertical direction
yields the same observable trajectory (orange) on the probability simplex.

Our results justify the following practical guidance without additional assumptions.
(i) Temperature directly modulates the
speed
along a fixed manifold path
(§
5.1
); (ii) truncation restricts dynamics to a face with identical guarantees
(§
5.2
); (iii) when auxiliary heuristics introduce strong path dependence,
the system can develop cycles or brittle attractors. Mitigations include moderating selective pressure
(raising
T
), delaying truncation, or dampening path-dependent logit shifts.

6
Discussion and Outlook

What is new.

The mathematical ingredients we use—Gibbs’ variational principle for softmax, entropic mirror ascent
as multiplicative weights, the replicator flow and its Shahshahani geometry—are classical
(§
2
–
4
).
Our contribution is to show that
next-token decoding
in LLMs admits a rigorous dynamical
description on the probability simplex, thereby turning the common “manifold traversal” intuition
into a theorem at the output-distribution level (Thm.
4.1
), and to extract exact,
practice-facing corollaries: temperature as a time rescaling (Cor.
4.4
) and
truncation as restriction to a face with identical guarantees (Cor.
4.5
).

Limitations and scope.

Our results are restricted to a fixed decoding context and address only the
output distribution

over next tokens. We deliberately avoid claims about (i) training dynamics, (ii) internal representation
geometry, or (iii) production decoders literally integrating the ODE; the ODE is a continuous-time limit
of the standard multiplicative update. Section
5
sketches safe phenomenology when
scores acquire mild path dependence; a full analysis of nonconservative preference fields is beyond
our scope here.

Hidden states, constraints, and future work.

While the present work treats only the output distribution, a parallel variational/dynamical viewpoint
can, in principle, be extended to internal states. One may regard hidden activations for a fixed prompt
as coordinates on a high-dimensional manifold shaped jointly by (a) the
user input
and (b) the

learned parameters
, which impose complementary constraints. The observable token distribution
then arises as a
projection
from this internal state manifold to the probability simplex.

There exists a geometric formalism in which entropy-regularized, normalization-preserving flows arise as
contact
-type dynamics; in that language, the entropic mirror step (
3.1
) discretizes a contact descent and the replicator field (
4.1
) is its continuous-time limit (see
6
,
8
for background). A folded-symplectic
bulk
inducing a contact structure on a
fold
and projecting to screen-level, normalized dynamics provides a compact global mechanism that recovers softmax/replicator; see the author’s preprint
[
11
]
for a complete treatment of this bulk–fold–screen construction. In that framework, the contact Reeb direction implements an
effective time
reparameterization, consistent with our temperature-as-time result in §
5.1
. We keep these details out of the main text; a full development is deferred to future work. The goal of such an extension would be to relate internal state flows to the output-level
replicator dynamics established here, providing a principled bridge between representation dynamics and
decoding behavior.

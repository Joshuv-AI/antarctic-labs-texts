Abstract
. This paper investigates a boundary-value problem for the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation on a star-graph structure. We develop a unified framework introducing the notion of s s -compatibility, which generalizes classical compatibility conditions to star-shaped and more complex graph configurations, inspired by the works of Bona, Sun, and Zhang [ 14 ] . By combining analytical techniques with a fixed-point argument, we establish sharp global well-posedness for both the linear and nonlinear problems at the H s H^{s} level. In this setting, our results extend the classical analysis for a single KdV equation [ 14 ] to star-shaped graphs composed of N N equations. These results provide the first comprehensive well-posedness theory for KdV equations with coupled boundary conditions on graphs. Although control issues are not treated in this article, the analytic results obtained here address several open problems, which will be addressed in a forthcoming paper.

1.
Introduction

In mathematics and physics, a quantum graph is a network composed of vertices connected by edges, where each edge carries a differential or pseudo-differential equation. When every edge is endowed with a natural metric, the structure is referred to as a metric graph. A common illustration is a power network: the transmission lines act as edges connecting transformer stations (vertices). In such a case, the governing differential equations may describe the voltage along each line, with boundary conditions at the vertices ensuring that the total current flowing into and out of each vertex sums to zero.

The concept of quantum graphs traces back to the work of Linus Pauling in the 1930s, who used them to model free electrons in organic molecules. Since then, they have emerged in a wide range of contexts, including quantum chaos, waveguide analysis, photonic crystals, and Anderson localization—the suppression of wave propagation in disordered media. Quantum graphs also arise as limit models of systems composed of thin wires and serve as important theoretical tools in mesoscopic physics and nanotechnology. A simplified formulation of quantum graphs was later proposed by Freedman
et al.
in
[
23
]
.

Beyond solving the differential equations in specific applications, the study of quantum graphs encompasses fundamental questions such as well-posedness, controllability, and identifiability. For example, controllability concerns determining the inputs required to steer the system toward a desired configuration, such as ensuring stable power distribution across a grid. Identifiability, on the other hand, focuses on optimal measurement placement—for instance, choosing pressure gauges in a water network to detect and locate potential leaks.

1.3.
Main results and structure of the article

With the previous framework in hand, our goal here is to prove the local and global well-posedness for the initial boundary value problem (IBVP) (
1.1
). The first main result establishes the local well-posedness for this system in sharp spaces.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T>0
,
l_{j}>0,\ j=1,...,N
,
s\in[0,3]
be given. Suppose
f\in W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))
and

\displaystyle(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\in\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})\times H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,T)\times\left[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N}\times\left[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N}.

satisfying the
s
-compatibility conditions, thus there exists
T^{*}\in(0,T)
such that the IBVP (
1.1
) admits a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{X}_{s,T^{*}}
, where

\mathcal{X}_{s,T}=\left\{v\in\mathbb{B}^{*}_{s,T};\ \partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}\in L_{x}^{\infty}(0,l_{j};H^{\frac{s+1-k}{3}}(0,T)),\ k=0,1,2,\ j=1,2,...,N\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}:=\mathbb{B}_{s,T}\cap H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T})).

The proof of the previous theorem relies on several analytical tools combined with a fixed-point argument. In addition, a new notion of
s
-compatibility adapted to the graph structure is introduced (see Section

), inspired by the single-edge KdV framework in

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

2.
s

When investigating the existence of solutions to (

Suppose that

1.1

\displaystyle\phi_{0,j}(x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),\ \ j=1,...,N

and observe that, according to the boundary conditions in (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi_{0,j}(0)=\phi_{0,1}(0),\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{0,j}^{\prime\prime}(0)=-\alpha\phi_{0,1}(0)-\frac{N}{3}\phi_{0,1}^{2}(0)+g_{0}(0),\\
\phi_{0,j}(l_{j})=p_{j}(0),\\
\phi_{0,j}^{\prime}(l_{j})=g_{j}(0).\end{cases}

Now, define

\displaystyle\phi_{1,j}(x)=-(\phi_{0,j}^{\prime}+\phi_{0,j}^{\prime\prime\prime}+\phi_{0,j}\phi_{0,j}^{\prime})(x),\quad j=1,...,N,

and note that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi_{1,j}(0)=\phi_{1,1}(0),\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{1,j}^{\prime\prime}(0)=-\alpha\phi_{1,1}(0)-\frac{N}{3}\left(\phi_{0,1}(0)\phi_{1,1}(0)+\phi_{1,1}(0)\phi_{0,1}(0)\right)+g_{0}^{(1)}(0),\\
\phi_{1,j}(l_{j})=p_{j}^{(1)}(0),\\
\phi_{1,j}^{\prime}(l_{j})=g_{j}^{(1)}(0).\end{cases}

Analogously, defining

\displaystyle\phi_{2,j}(x)=-(\phi_{1,j}^{\prime}+\phi_{1,j}^{\prime\prime\prime}+\phi_{1,j}\phi_{0,j}^{\prime}+\phi_{0,j}\phi_{1,j}^{\prime})(x),\quad j=1,...,N

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi_{2,j}(0)=\phi_{2,1}(0),\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{2,j}^{\prime\prime}(0)=-\alpha\phi_{2,1}(0)-\frac{N}{3}\left(\phi_{0,1}(0)\phi_{2,1}(0)+\phi_{1,1}(0)\phi_{1,1}(0)+\phi_{2,1}(0)\phi_{0,1}(0)\right)+g_{0}^{(2)}(0),\\

\phi_{2,j}^{\prime}(l_{j})=g_{j}^{(2)}(0).\end{cases}

In general, we define for

\displaystyle\phi_{k,j}=\begin{cases}u_{j}^{0},&k=0,\\

\alpha
are positive constants.

Thanks to the Proposition
3.4
and the previous lemma, we can establish the well-posedness for the problem (
3.1
) using a fixed point argument. The result can be read as follows.

Proposition 3.5
.

Given
T>0
. For every
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))
and
(\phi,\vec{h})\in Y_{0,T}
, the IBVP (
3.1
) admits a unique solution
\psi\in Z_{0,T}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{k}\psi\in C_{b}\left([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right),\ k=0,1,2.

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{Z_{0,T}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\psi\|_{C_{b}([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T))}\leq C\left(\|(\phi,\vec{h})\|_{Y_{0,T}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\right).

Proof.

For
\beta\in(0,T]
consider the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}=\left\{v\in Z_{0,\beta};\ \partial_{x}^{k}v\in C_{b}\left([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,\beta)\right),\ k=0,1,2\right\},

which is a Banach space with the norm

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}=\|v\|_{Z_{0,\beta}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}v\|_{C_{b}([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,\beta))}.

Let
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))
and
(\phi,\vec{h})\in Y_{0,T}
be given. Define the map
\Gamma:\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}
putting, for each
v\in\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}
,
\Gamma v
as being the solution of the problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi+\partial_{x}^{3}\psi=f-\partial_{x}v,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,L),\\
\partial_{x}^{2}\psi(t,0)=h_{1}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{N}v(t,0),&t\in(0,T)\\
\psi(t,L)=h_{2}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}\psi(t,L)=h_{3}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
\psi(0,x)=\phi(x),&x\in(0,L).\end{cases}

From Proposition
3.4
(for
s=0
)
\Gamma
is well defined and, for every
v\in\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}
,

\displaystyle\|\Gamma\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}\leq C\left(\|(\phi,\vec{h})\|_{Y_{0,\beta}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,\beta;L^{2}(0,L))}+\frac{\alpha}{N}\|v(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{1}(0,\beta;L^{2}(0,L))}\right).

Using the Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{1}(0,\beta;L^{2}(0,L))}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\beta}\|\partial_{x}v(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}\leq\int_{0}^{\beta}\|\partial_{x}v(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(0,L)}\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{2}(0,\beta;H^{1}(0,L))}

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}.

On the other hand, Lemma
3.1
gives us

\displaystyle\|v(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,\beta)}\leq C\beta^{\alpha}\|1\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\|v(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,\beta)}\leq C\beta^{\alpha}\|v\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}.

Consequently

(3.24)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\Gamma v\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}&\leq C\left(\|(\phi,\vec{h})\|_{Y_{0,\beta}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,\beta;L^{2}(0,L))}+\frac{\alpha}{N}C\beta^{\alpha}\|v\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}+\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\|(\phi,\vec{h})\|_{Y_{0,\beta}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,\beta;L^{2}(0,L))}\right)+\left(\frac{\alpha}{N}\beta^{\alpha}+\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)C\|v\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}.\end{aligned}

Choosing
\beta\in(0,T]
such that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha}{N}\beta^{\alpha}+\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)C<\frac{1}{2}

and
r=2C\left(\|(\phi,\vec{h})\|_{Y_{0,\beta}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,\beta;L^{2}(0,L))}\right)
, we have
\Gamma(B_{r})\subset B_{r}
, where
B_{r}=\left\{v\in\mathcal{Z}_{\beta};\ \|v\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}\leq r\right\}.

Moreover
\Gamma:B_{r}\rightarrow B_{r}
is a contraction since

\displaystyle\|\Gamma v-\Gamma w\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\|\Gamma(v-w)\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}\leq\left(\frac{\alpha}{N}\beta^{\alpha}+\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)C\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}<\frac{1}{2}\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}.

From the Banach fixed point theorem it follows that
\Gamma
has a fixed point
\psi\in B_{r}
, that is,
\psi
solves (
3.1
) for
t\in[0,\beta]
and data
(\phi,\vec{h},f)
. Furthermore, by combining and (
3.25
) and (
3.24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|(\phi,\vec{h})\|_{Y_{0,\beta}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,\beta;L^{2}(0,L))}\right)+\left(\frac{\alpha}{N}\beta^{\alpha}+\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)C\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|(\phi,\vec{h})\|_{Y_{0,\beta}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,\beta;L^{2}(0,L))}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}

hence

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\left(\|(\phi,\vec{h})\|_{Y_{0,\beta}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,\beta;L^{2}(0,L))}\right).

Since
\beta
does not depend on the initial data
\phi
, a standard continuation (extension) argument allows us to extend the solution
\psi
to the entire interval
[0,T]
. Moreover, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|(\phi,\vec{h})\|_{Y_{0,T}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\right),

where
C
is an appropriate positive constant.
∎

We conclude this section with one final technical result, which will be instrumental in proving the main theorem of this section.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
T>0
be given. For every
u_{0}\in D(A)
,
g_{0},g_{j},p_{j}\in\left\{\varphi\in C^{2}([0,T]);\ \varphi(0)=0\right\}
and
f\in C^{1}\left([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})\right)
, the corresponding solution
u
of the problem (
3
) satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}\in C([0,T]\times[0,l_{j}]),\ k=0,1,2.

Proof.

Consider the operator

P_{j}:H^{3}(0,l_{j})\rightarrow L^{2}(0,l_{j})

defined by
P_{j}w=w_{x}+w_{xxx}
. The graph norm in
H^{3}(0,l_{j})
associated to
P_{j}
is

\|w\|_{P_{j}}=\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}+\|P_{j}w\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}.

From
[
33
, Lemma A.2]
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(3.26)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{3}(0,l_{j})}\leq C\|w\|_{P_{j}},\ \forall w\in H^{3}(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,...,N.

On the other hand, given
v\in D(A)
we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{D(A)}

\displaystyle=\|v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|v_{x}+v_{xxx}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|v_{jx}+v_{jxxx}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}+\|v_{jx}+v_{jxxx}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}

\displaystyle=\|v_{j}\|_{P_{j}}.

Therefore, this yields

(3.27)

\displaystyle\|v_{j}\|_{P_{j}}\leq\|v\|_{D(A)},\ j=1,...,N.

Now, from Proposition
3.2
we have
u\in C^{1}\left([0,T],\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})\right)\cap C\left([0,T],D(A)\right)
and by Sobolev embedding it follows that

\partial_{x}^{k}u(t,\cdot)\in H^{3-k}(0,l_{j})\hookrightarrow C([0,l_{j}]),\ \forall t\in[0,T],\ \ k=0,1,2,\ j=1,...,N.

Hence, fixed
x\in[0,l_{j}]
, we get

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t,x)-\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t_{0},x)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t,\cdot)-\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{C[0,l_{j}]}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t,\cdot)-\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{H^{3-k}(0,l_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)-u_{j}(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{H^{3}(0,l_{j})}

for any
t,t_{0}\in[0,T]
. Using (
3.26
) and (
3.27
) we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t,x)-\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t_{0},x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)-u_{j}(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{D(A)},

from where we conclude that
\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(\cdot,x)\in C([0,T])
showing the lemma.
∎

We are now in a position to state the main result of this subsection, whose proof follows from a combination of Propositions
3.3
and
3.5
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Given
T>0
, there exist unique bounded linear maps

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Psi:\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})\times H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\times\left[L^{2}(0,T)\right]^{N}\times\left[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N}\times L^{1}(0,T,\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))&\rightarrow&\mathbb{B}_{T}\\
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)&\mapsto&\Psi(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f),\end{array}

and, for
k=0,1,2
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Psi_{j}^{k}:\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})\times H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\times\left[L^{2}(0,T)\right]^{N}\times\left[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N}\times L^{1}(0,T,\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))&\rightarrow&C_{b}\left([0,l_{j}];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right)\\
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)&\mapsto&\Psi_{j}^{k}(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)\end{array}

such that, when
u^{0}\in D(A)
,
g_{0},g_{j},p_{j}\in C_{l}^{2}([0,T])
for
j=1,...,N
and
f\in C^{1}\left([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})\right)
we have the following items:

i)

u:=\Psi(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)
is the unique classical solution of (
3
);

ii)

\Psi_{j}^{k}(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)=\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two cases. The constant
C
can be adjusted for each line.

Case 1:

l_{j}=L>0
for
j=1,...,N
.

First we define

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Psi:D(A)\times C_{l}^{2}([0,T])\times\left[C_{l}^{2}([0,T])\right]^{N}\times\left[C_{l}^{2}([0,T])\right]^{N}\times C^{1}(0,T,\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))&\rightarrow&\mathbb{B}\\
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)&\mapsto&\Psi(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f),\end{array}

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Psi_{j}^{k}:D(A)\times C_{l}^{2}([0,T])\times\left[C_{l}^{2}([0,T])\right]^{N}\times\left[C_{l}^{2}([0,T])\right]^{N}\times C^{1}(0,T,\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))&\rightarrow&C_{b}\left([0,l_{j}];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right)\\
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)&\mapsto&\Psi_{j}^{k}(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)\end{array}

for
k=0,1,2
. By proving that
\Psi
and
\Psi_{j}^{k}
are continuous with respect to the norm of

\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})\times H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\times\left[L^{2}(0,T)\right]^{N}\times\left[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N}\times L^{1}(0,T,\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})),

we can extend them to this space by density. Suppose
u_{0}\in D(A)
,
g_{0},g_{j},p_{j}\in C_{l}^{2}([0,T])
and
f\in C^{1}\left([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})\right)
. From Proposition
3.2
, there exists a unique classical solution

\displaystyle u\in C^{1}\left([0,T],\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})\right)\cap C\left([0,T],\mathbb{H}^{3}_{c}(\mathcal{T})\right)

for (
3
). This gives in particular
u_{j}\in C([0,T],L^{2}(0,l_{j})),\ j=1,...,N
. Furthermore, due to Lemma
3.2
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(0,l_{j})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}|u_{j}(t,x)|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}|\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,x)|^{2}<\infty

hence
u_{j}\in L^{2}(0,T,H^{1}(0,l_{j})),\ j=1,...,N
. Consequently,

(3.28)

\displaystyle u_{j}\in C([0,T],L^{2}(0,l_{j}))\cap L^{2}(0,T,H^{1}(0,l_{j})),\ \ j=1,...,N

as well as
u\in\mathbb{B}=C([0,T],\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))
. So we are motivated to define
\Psi(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)=u.
Consider
\psi
,
\theta
defined by

\displaystyle\psi:=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}\quad\text{ \ and \ }\quad\theta:=Nu_{1}-\psi.

Note that, by the regularity (
3.28
), we have
\theta\in Z_{0,T}=C([0,T],L^{2}(0,L))\cap L^{2}(0,T,H^{1}(0,L))
. Moreover,
\theta
is a classical solution to the IBVP

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\theta+\partial_{x}\theta+\partial_{x}^{3}\theta=Nf_{1}-\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}f_{j},&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,L),\ j=1,...,N,\\
\theta(t,0)=0,&t\in(0,T),\ j=1,...,N,\\
\theta(t,L)=Np_{1}(t)-\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\ j=1,...,N,\\
\partial_{x}\theta(t,L)=Ng_{1}(t)-\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}g_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\ j=1,...,N,\\
\theta(0,x)=Nu_{1}^{0}(x)-\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,L),\ j=1,...,N.\end{cases}

Then, Proposition
3.1
ensures that
\partial_{x}^{k}\theta\in C_{b}\left([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right)
for
k=0,1,2
and

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{Z_{0,T}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\theta\|_{C_{b}([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T))}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|Nu_{1}^{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}^{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}+\left\|Np_{1}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{j}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left\|Ng_{1}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}g_{j}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\left\|Nf_{1}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}f_{j}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\right)

From this inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{Z_{0,T}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\theta\|_{C_{b}([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T))}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|p_{j}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\right).

Analogously, (
3.28
) ensures that
\psi\in Z_{0,T}
.
Observe that
\partial_{x}^{2}\psi(t,0)+\frac{\alpha}{N}\psi(t,0)=g_{0}(t)
.
Consequently,
\psi
is a classical solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi+\partial_{x}\psi+\partial_{x}^{3}\psi=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}f_{j},&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,L),\\
\partial_{x}^{2}\psi(t,0)+(\alpha/N)\psi(t,0)=g_{0}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
\psi(t,L)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{j}(t),\ \ \partial_{x}\psi(t,L)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}g_{j}(t)&t\in(0,T),\\
\psi(0,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,L).\end{cases}

Thus, Proposition
3.4
gives us
\partial_{x}^{k}\psi\in C_{b}\left([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right),\ k=0,1,2,
and

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{Z_{0,T}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\psi\|_{C_{b}([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T))}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|p_{j}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\right).

Since
u_{1}=\frac{1}{N}(\theta+\psi)
it follows that
u_{1}\in Z_{0,T}
,
\partial_{x}^{k}u_{1}\in C_{b}\left([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right)
, for
k=0,1,2,
and

(3.29)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|u_{1}\|_{Z_{0,T}}+&\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{1}\|_{C_{b}([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T))}\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\right.\\
&\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|p_{j}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\right).\end{aligned}

Hence,
u_{1}(\cdot,0)\in H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)
and, for each
j=2,....,N
,
u_{j}\in Z_{0,T}
is the solution of the following problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{x}u_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}=f_{j},&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,L),\\
u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),\ \ \ u_{j}(t,L)=p_{j}(t),\ \ \ \partial_{x}u_{j}(t,L)=g_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
u(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,L).\end{cases}

By Proposition
3.3
it follows that
\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}\in C_{b}\left([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right),\ k=0,1,2,
and

\displaystyle\|u_{j}\|_{Z_{0,T}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}\|_{C_{b}([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T))}\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\|u_{j}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}+\|u_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|p_{j}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}

\displaystyle+\|g_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\Big).

Using (
3.29
) we get

(3.30)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|u_{j}\|_{Z_{0,T}}+&\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}\|_{C_{b}([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T))}\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\right.\\
&\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|p_{j}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\right),\end{aligned}

for
j=1,...,N
. Adding for
j=1,...,N
this implies, in particular

(3.31)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \max_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+&\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\right.\\
&\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|p_{j}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\right).\end{aligned}

Observe that

(3.32)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,L))}\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}

as well as

(3.33)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|g_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|p_{j}(t)\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\\
&\leq N^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{\left[L^{2}(0,T)\right]^{N}}+\|p\|_{\left[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N}}\right).\end{aligned}

From (
3.31
), (
3.32
) and (
3.33
) it follows that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{\left[L^{2}(0,T)\right]^{N}}+\|p\|_{\left[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).

Therefore, due to this previous inequality,
\Psi
is continuous. Now, combining (
3.30
), (
3.32
) and (
3.33
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}\|_{C_{b}([0,L];H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T))}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{\left[L^{2}(0,T)\right]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|p\|_{\left[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).

Hence, defining
\Psi_{j}^{k}(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)=\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j},\ k=0,1,2,
we have that
\Psi_{j}^{k}
is continuous. As mentioned before, by density, each map
\Psi
and
\Psi_{j}^{k}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}v_{j}^{\prime}(s,\cdot)ds=v_{j}(t,\cdot)-v_{j}(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}v_{j}^{\prime}(s,\cdot)ds=-\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}v_{j}(s,\cdot)ds-\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}^{3}v_{j}(s,\cdot)ds+\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}^{\prime}(s,\cdot)ds

hence

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}v_{j}(s,\cdot)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}^{3}v_{j}(s,\cdot)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}v_{j}^{\prime}(s,\cdot)ds+\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}^{\prime}(s,\cdot)ds

\displaystyle=v_{j}(0,\cdot)-v_{j}(t,\cdot)+f_{j}(t,\cdot)-f_{j}(0,\cdot).

Using this in (
3.41
) we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{j}(t,\cdot)+\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,\cdot)+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}(t,\cdot)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}u_{j}^{0}+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}^{0}+v_{j}(0,\cdot)+f_{j}(t,\cdot)-f_{j}(0,\cdot).

From this, (
3.38
) gives us

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{j}(t,\cdot)+\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,\cdot)+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}(t,\cdot)

\displaystyle=f_{j}(t,\cdot).

Moreover, using (
3.38
) and (
3.39
) and the compatibility conditions, we deduce that
u
is a classical solution to (
3
).

By the previous part we have
u\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T}))
. On the other hand, note that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}(t,\cdot)=f_{j}(t,\cdot)-\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,\cdot)-\partial_{t}u_{j}(t,\cdot)=f_{j}(t,\cdot)-\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,\cdot)-v_{j}(t,\cdot)\in H^{1}(0,l_{j})

so
u_{j}(t,\cdot)\in H^{4}(0,l_{j})
. Moreover
\partial_{x}^{4}u_{j}(t,\cdot)=\partial_{x}f_{j}(t,\cdot)-\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,\cdot)-\partial_{x}v_{j}(t,\cdot)
, which gives us

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{4}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\leq C\left(\|\partial_{x}f_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}v_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\right)

Thus

(3.42)

\displaystyle\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{4}(0,l_{j})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\left(\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{3}(0,l_{j})}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}f_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}v_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\right).

Integrating from
0
to
T
and using the regularities for
f,u
and
v
we get
u_{j}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}(0,l_{j}))
and, consequently,
u\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{4}(\mathcal{T}))
. In addition, observe that

\displaystyle\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{3}(0,l_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}+\|P_{j}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle=C\left(\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}+\|\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,\cdot)+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle=C\left(\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}+\|f_{j}(t,\cdot)-v_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}+\|f_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}+\|v_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}\right).

Therefore

(3.43)

\displaystyle\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{3}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\leq C\left(\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}+\|f_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}+\|v_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\right).

and consequently

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})}\leq C\left(\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}\right).

Since
u,v\in C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
and
f\in W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\hookrightarrow C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
, passing to the maximum with
t\in[0,T]
, we obtain

(3.44)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}+\|f\|_{W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}+\|v\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).

Using (
3.43
) in (
3.42
) it follows that

\displaystyle\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{4}(0,l_{j})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}+\|f_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(0,l_{j})}^{2}+\|v_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\right).

Taking the sum with respect to
j
and integrating from
0
to
T
we have
for some
C=C(T)>0

(3.45)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{4}(\mathcal{T}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).

Adding (
3.45
) and (
3.44
) it follows that

(3.46)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{3,T}}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}+\|f\|_{W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}+\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\right).

Using Proposition (
3.6
) we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\leq C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right)

and

\begin{split}\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\leq&C\left(\|-\partial_{x}u^{0}-\partial_{x}^{3}u^{0}+f(0,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}^{\prime}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\right.\\
&\left.+\|g^{\prime}\|_{\left[L^{2}(0,T)\right]^{N}}+\|p^{\prime}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).\end{split}

Thus, the following inequality holds

(3.47)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}+\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\leq&C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|\partial_{x}u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|\partial_{x}^{3}u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|f(0,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}\right.\\
&+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}+\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g_{0}^{\prime}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\\
&\left.+\|g\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|g^{\prime}\|_{\left[L^{2}(0,T)\right]^{N}}+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p^{\prime}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right).\end{split}

Due to embedding
W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\hookrightarrow C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
we have

(3.48)

\displaystyle\|f(0,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}\leq\|f\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}.

Now, due to embedding

\displaystyle H^{\frac{3-1}{3}}(0,T)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(0,T)\hookrightarrow H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\quad\text{and}\quad H^{\frac{3+1}{3}}(0,T)\hookrightarrow H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)

the following inequalities hold

(3.49)

\displaystyle\|g_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\leq C\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{3-1}{3}}(0,T)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\leq C\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{3+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}.

Finally, since
\frac{3+1}{3}-1\geq 0
,
\frac{3-1}{3}>0
,
\frac{3-1}{3}-1<0
and
\frac{3-1}{3}-\frac{1}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}

the operator
\frac{d}{dt}
maps
H^{\frac{3+1}{3}}(0,T)
(resp.
H^{\frac{3-1}{3}}(0,T))
to
H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)
(resp.
H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)
) continuously. Thus,

(3.50)

\displaystyle\|g_{0}^{\prime}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\leq c\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{3-1}{3}}(0,T)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|p^{\prime}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\leq c\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{3+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}.

From (
3.46
)-(
3.50
) it follows that

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{3,T}}\leq&C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{3-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{3}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.\\
&\left.+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{3+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).\end{split}

iii.
Since
u\in\mathbb{B}_{3,T}
we have, in particular,
u\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))
and
\partial_{t}u=-\partial_{x}u-\partial_{x}^{3}u+f\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))
. Therefore,
u\in H^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}^{2}=

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}^{2}+\|-\partial_{x}u-\partial_{x}^{3}u+f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{3,T}}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{3-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{3}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{3+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{W^{1,1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}\right)^{2}.

iv.
Since
u\in H^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))
we have

|\partial_{t}^{k}u_{j}(t,x)|\leq\|\partial_{t}^{k}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,l_{j})}\leq C\|\partial_{t}^{k}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(0,l_{j})},\qquad k=0,1.

Taking the square and integrating this inequality we see that
u_{j}(\cdot,x)\in H^{1}(0,T)
with

(3.51)

\displaystyle\|u_{j}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{1}(0,T)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))},

for every
x\in(0,l_{j})
. Moreover

(3.52)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{j}(\cdot,x)=v_{j}(\cdot,x)\in H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T),\ \forall x\in(0,l_{j}).

From the theory of Sobolev spaces (see, for instance,
[
9
]
) it follows that
u_{j}\in L_{x}^{\infty}\left(0,T;H^{\frac{3+1-0}{3}}(0,T)\right)
. On the other hand
u_{j}\in C\left([0,T];H^{3}(0,l_{j})\right)
implies that

(3.53)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t,x)|\leq\|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,l_{j})}\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(0,l_{j})}\leq C\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{3}(0,l_{j})},\qquad k=1,2,

that is,
\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(\cdot,x)\in L^{2}(0,T)
. In addition,

(3.54)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}(\cdot,x)=\partial_{x}v_{j}(\cdot,x)\in H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T),\qquad k=1,2

and therefore
\partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}\in L_{x}^{\infty}\left(0,T;H^{\frac{3+1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right)
for
k=1,2
. Finally, thanks to (
3.51
)-(
3.54
), the trace estimates hold, concluding the proof.
∎

3.3.
The case
s\in(0,3)

Based on Propositions
3.6
and
3.7
and results from linear interpolation theory, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
T>0
and
s\in(0,3)
be. For any data

\displaystyle u^{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T}),\quad g_{0}\in H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,T),\quad g\in\left[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N},\quad\text{and}\quad p\in\left[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N}

satisfying the linear compatibility conditions and any
f\in W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T})),

the problem (
3
) admits a unique solution
u\in\mathbb{B}_{s,T}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}\right),

for some positive constant
C
. Moreover,
u\in H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))
with

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}\right),

for some positive constant
C
.

In fact, we can prove the well-posedness for the slightly more general problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{x}[(1+a_{j})u_{j}]+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}=f_{j},&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,...,N,\\
u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\ j=1,...,N,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha u_{1}(t,0)+g_{0}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
u_{j}(t,l_{j})=p_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\ j=1,...,N,\\
u_{j}(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,...,N.\end{array}\right.

To do this, it is convenient to recall the space

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}:=\mathbb{B}_{s,T}\cap H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))

with the norm of the sum. The following Lemma is a consequence of
[
26
, Lemma 3]
together with results of interpolation theory.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
T>0
be given.

i.

Let
s\geq 0
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
u,v\in\mathbb{B}_{s,T}
one have
\partial_{x}uv\in L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T}))
with

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}uv\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}dt\leq C(T^{\frac{1}{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{3}})\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}}.

ii.

Let
s\in[0,3]
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that, for any
u,v\in\mathbb{B}_{s,T}
, we have
\partial_{x}uv\in L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))
with

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}uv\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T})}^{\frac{6}{6-s}}

\displaystyle\leq C(T^{\frac{1}{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{3}})\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}}^{\frac{6}{6-s}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}}^{\frac{6}{6-s}}.

iii.

Let
s\in[0,3]
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that, for any
u,v\in\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}
, we have
\partial_{x}uv\in W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
with

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}uv\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\leq C(T^{\frac{1}{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{3}})\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}}.

The next result ensures the uniqueness of the problem (
3.3
).

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
T>0
,
s\in[0,3]
and
a\in\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}
be. For any data

\displaystyle u^{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T}),\quad g_{0}\in H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,T),\quad g\in\left[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N},\quad\text{and}\quad p\in\left[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,T)\right]^{N}

satisfying the linear compatibility conditions and
f\in W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T})),
the problem (
3.3
) admits a unique solution
u\in\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}\right),

for some positive constant
C
depending on
\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}}
.

Proof.

Consider
\theta\in[0,T]
and the map
\Gamma_{a}:\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}
where, for each
v\in\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}
,
\Gamma_{a}v
is the solution for

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{x}u_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}=f_{j}-\partial_{x}(a_{j}v_{j}),&t\in(0,\theta),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,...,N,\\
u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,\theta),\ j=1,...,N,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha u_{1}(t,0)+g_{0}(t),&t\in(0,\theta),\\
u_{j}(t,l_{j})=p_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j}(t),&t\in(0,\theta),\ j=1,...,N,\\
u_{j}(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,...,N.\end{cases}

Thanks to Corollary
3.1
and Lemma
3.4
,
\Gamma_{a}
is well-defined. Moreover, defining

\displaystyle r=

\displaystyle 2C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}\right)

we have

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,\theta)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,\theta)]^{N}}+\|f-\partial_{x}(av)\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,\theta;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{r}{2}+C\|\partial_{x}(av)\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,\theta;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r}{2}+C\left(\|\partial_{x}(av)\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}+\|\partial_{x}(av)\|_{L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,\theta;\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{r}{2}+C\left((\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}}+(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{r}{2}+C(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}}\cdot r.

Choosing
\theta\in(0,T)
such that

\displaystyle C(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}}<\frac{1}{2}

we have
\Gamma_{a}(B)\subset B
where
B=\{v\in\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*};\ \|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}}\leq r\}.
Moreover
\Gamma_{a}
is a contraction on
B
since

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}v-\Gamma_{a}w\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}}\|v-w\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}}

Now, using the Banach fixed point theorem, it follows that
\Gamma_{a}
has a fixed point
u\in B
, that is,
u
solves (
3.3
) for
t\in[0,\theta]
and
\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}}\leq r
. Since
\theta
does not depend on the data
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)
, using a standard continuation extension argument, the solution
u
can be extended to the interval
[0,T]
and the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}\right),

where the constant
C
may depend on
\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}}
through the number of sub-intervals of size
\theta
needed to cover
[0,T]
.
∎

Remark 3.2

By using induction, one can verify the results of this section for

, the same results are obtained for

to get the results in

and, consequently, for
s
.

4.
Local well-posedness of the nonlinear problem

Now, we are in a position to prove the first main result of the article, namely, the local well-posedness of the following problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{x}[(a_{j}+1)u_{j}]+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}+u_{j}\partial_{x}u_{j}=f_{j},&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\\
u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha u_{1}(t,0)-\frac{N}{3}(u_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(t,0)+g_{0}(t),&t\in(0,T)\\
u_{j}(t,l_{j})=p_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
u_{j}(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\end{array}\right.

where
j=1,...,N
and
a
is a given function. Remember that: for
s\geq 0
and
T>0
the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{s,T}=\left\{v\in\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*};\ \partial_{x}^{k}u_{j}\in L_{x}^{\infty}(0,l_{j};H^{\frac{s+1-k}{3}}(0,T)),\ k=0,1,2,\ j=1,2,...,N\right\}

is a Banach space with the norm

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,T}}=\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,T}^{*}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k}v\|_{\prod_{j=1}^{N}L_{x}^{\infty}(0,l_{j};H^{\frac{s+1-k}{3}}(0,T))}.

Note that (
1.1
) is a particular case of (
4
) with
a=0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix
\theta\in(0,T)
and define
\Gamma_{a}:\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}
putting, for each
u\in\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}
,
\Gamma_{a}u
as being the solution
v
of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v_{j}+\partial_{x}[(a_{j}+1)v_{j}]+\partial_{x}^{3}v_{j}=-u_{j}\partial_{x}u_{j}+f_{j},&t\in(0,\theta),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,...,N,\\
v_{j}(t,0)=v_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,\theta),\ j=1,...,N,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha v_{1}(t,0)-\frac{N}{3}(u_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(t,0)+g_{0}(t),&t\in(0,\theta,)\\
v_{j}(t,l_{j})=p_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}v_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j}(t),&t\in(0,\theta),\ j=1,...,N,\\
v_{j}(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,...,N.\end{array}\right.

From Proposition
3.8
we have

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}\leq C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}-\frac{N}{3}(u_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,\theta)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,\theta)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,\theta)]^{N}}+\|u\partial_{x}u\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,\theta;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}+\|f\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,\theta;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).

Using Lemma
3.4
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}uv\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,\theta;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}\leq C(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{s,\theta}^{*}}.

Furthermore, Lemma
3.1
gives us

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{N}{3}(u_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(\cdot,0)\right\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,\theta)}

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{\alpha}\frac{N}{3}\left\|(u_{1}+a_{1})(\cdot,0)\right\|_{H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,\theta)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{\alpha}\frac{N}{3}\|u+a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{3}CN\theta^{\alpha}\left(\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}^{2}+\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}^{2}\right),

where
\alpha>0
is a constant. Thus,

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}

\displaystyle\left.+C(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}CN\theta^{\alpha}\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}CN\theta^{\alpha}\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}^{2}\right).

Defining

\displaystyle r=

\displaystyle 4C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}\right),

it follows that, for
u\in B=\{v\in\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta};\ \|v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}\leq r\}
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}\leq\frac{r}{4}+C^{2}(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})r^{2}+\frac{4}{3}C^{2}N\theta^{\alpha}r^{2}+\frac{4}{3}C^{2}N\theta^{\alpha}\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,T}}^{2}.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}u-\Gamma_{a}w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|-\frac{N}{3}(u_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(\cdot,0)+\frac{N}{3}(w_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,\theta)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|-u\partial_{x}u+w\partial_{x}w\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,\theta;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{N}{3}\|(u_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(\cdot,0)-(w_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,\theta)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|u\partial_{x}u-w\partial_{x}w\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,\theta;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).

Note that

\displaystyle(u_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(\cdot,0)-(w_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle=\left[\big((u_{1}+a_{1})+(w_{1}+a_{1})\big)\cdot\big((u_{1}+a_{1})-(w_{1}+a_{1})\big)\right](\cdot,0)

\displaystyle=\left[(u_{1}+w_{1}+2a_{1})\cdot(u_{1}-w_{1})\right](\cdot,0).

Hence, Lemma
3.1
provides

\displaystyle\frac{N}{3}\|(u_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(\cdot,0)-(w_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{s-1}{3}}(0,\theta)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{3}C\theta^{\alpha}\|(u_{1}+w_{1}+2a_{1})(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,\theta)}\cdot\|(u_{1}-w_{1})(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{s+1}{3}}(0,\theta)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{3}C\theta^{\alpha}\|u+w+2a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}\|u-w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{3}C\theta^{\alpha}\left(\|u+w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}+2\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}\right)\|u-w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}.

Moreover

\displaystyle u\partial_{x}u-w\partial_{x}w=(u-w)\partial_{x}u+w(\partial_{x}u-\partial_{x}w)=(u-w)\partial_{x}u+w\partial_{x}(u-w)

so, using Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\partial_{x}u-w\partial_{x}w\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,\theta;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}

\displaystyle\leq C(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|u-w\|_{\mathbb{B}^{*}_{s,\theta}}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}^{*}_{s,\theta}}

\displaystyle+C(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|w\|_{\mathbb{B}^{*}_{s,\theta}}\|u-w\|_{\mathbb{B}^{*}_{s,\theta}},

that is,

\displaystyle\|u\partial_{x}u-w\partial_{x}w\|_{W^{\frac{s}{3},1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{\frac{6}{6-s}}(0,\theta;\mathbb{H}^{\frac{s}{3}}(\mathcal{T}))}

\displaystyle\leq 2C(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\left(\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}^{*}_{s,\theta}}+\|w\|_{\mathbb{B}^{*}_{s,\theta}}\right)\|u-w\|_{\mathbb{B}^{*}_{s,\theta}}.

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}u-\Gamma_{a}w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}C^{2}N\theta^{\alpha}\left(\|u+w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}+2\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}\right)\|u-w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}

\displaystyle\ +2C^{2}(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\left(\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}+\|w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}\right)\|u-w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}.

Therefore, for
u,w\in B
we have that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}u-\Gamma_{a}w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}C^{2}N\theta^{\alpha}\left(2r+2\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}\right)\|u-w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}+2C^{2}(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})2r\|u-w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2}{3}C^{2}N\theta^{\alpha}r+\frac{2}{3}C^{2}N\theta^{\alpha}\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,T}}+4C^{2}(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})r\right)\|u-w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,\theta}}.

Choosing
\theta\in(0,T)
satisfying

\displaystyle C^{2}(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})r<\frac{1}{12},\quad\frac{4}{3}C^{2}N\theta^{\alpha}r<\frac{1}{4},\quad\frac{4}{3}C^{2}N\theta^{\alpha}\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,T}}^{2}<\frac{r}{4}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{2}{3}C^{2}N\theta^{\alpha}\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,T}}<\frac{1}{3},

it follows, from (
4.6
), that
\Gamma_{a}(B)\subset B
and, thanks to the inequality (
4.7
), it follows that
\Gamma_{a}
is a contraction. The Banach fixed point theorem ensures the existence of a fixed point
u\in B
of
\Gamma_{a}
which is the desired solution of (
4
) for
t\in(0,\theta)
, showing Theorem
1.1
.
∎

5.

We are now in a position to extend the previous section. Precisely, in this section, we ensure sharp global well-posedness for the system (

5.1.
The case

Here, we investigated energy estimates for the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{x}u_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}+u_{j}\partial_{x}u_{j}=f_{j},&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\\
u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha u_{1}(t,0)-\frac{N}{3}u_{1}^{2}(t,0)+g_{0}(t),&t\in(0,T)\\
u_{j}(t,l_{j})=0,\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
u_{j}(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\end{array}\right.

in the case
s=0
. Let us start with some a priori estimate that can be found in
[
4
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

[
4
, Proposition 2.4]

Given
T>0
, let
g_{0}\in L^{2}(0,T)
,
g\in[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}
,
f\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(0,L))
and
u^{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
. If
u\in\mathbb{B}_{0,T}
is a solution of (
5.1
) then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\leq C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).

In addition,
u_{1}(\cdot,0)\in L^{2}(0,L)
with

\displaystyle\|u_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).

Now, we study the problem

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{x}u_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}+u_{j}\partial_{x}u_{j}=-a_{j}\partial_{x}a_{j}-\partial_{x}(a_{j}u_{j}),&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\\
u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha u_{1}(t,0)-\frac{N}{3}(u_{1}+a_{1})^{2}(t,0),&t\in(0,T)\\
u_{j}(t,l_{j})=0,\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j},&t\in(0,T),\\
u_{j}(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\end{cases}

where
a\in\mathcal{X}_{\lambda,T}
. The a priori estimates for this nonlinear system can be read as follows.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
T>0
,
\lambda>\frac{1}{2}
,
a\in\mathcal{X}_{\lambda,T}
,
u_{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
and
g\in[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}
be given. If
u\in\mathbb{B}_{0,T}
is a solution of (
5.6
) then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\leq C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\right).

Proof.

If
u
solves (
5.6
) in
(0,T)
then, is also solution in
(0,\theta)
, for all
\theta\in(0,T)
. Observe that the central node conditions can be seen as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}w_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha u_{1}(t,0)-\frac{N}{3}u_{1}^{2}(t,0)-\frac{2N}{3}u_{1}(t,0)a_{1}(t,0)-\frac{N}{3}a_{1}^{2}(t,0),\qquad t\in(0,\theta).

Hence, Proposition
5.1
gives us

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|-a_{j}\partial_{x}a_{j}-\partial_{x}(a_{j}u_{j})\|_{L^{1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\\
+\\
\left\|\frac{2N}{3}u_{1}(\cdot,0)a_{1}(\cdot,0)+\frac{N}{3}a_{1}^{2}(\cdot,0)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,\theta)}\end{array}\right).

Note that by Lemma

\displaystyle\|-a_{j}\partial_{x}a_{j}-\partial_{x}(a_{j}u_{j})\|_{L^{1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\leq C(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\left(\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}^{2}+\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}\right).

Moreover,

provides

\displaystyle\left\|u_{1}(\cdot,0)a_{1}(\cdot,0)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,\theta)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{\lambda+1}{3}}(0,\theta)}^{2}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}^{2}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\left\|a_{1}^{2}(\cdot,0)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,\theta)}^{2}\leq C\|a_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{\lambda+1}{3}}(0,\theta)}^{2}\|a_{1}\|_{L^{2}(0,\theta;H^{1}(0,l_{j}))}^{2}

Joining the previous estimates

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}^{2}+(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|a_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{\lambda+1}{3}}(0,\theta)}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}+\|a_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{\lambda+1}{3}}(0,\theta)}\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\right)

Choosing
\theta\in(0,T)
such that

\displaystyle(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}+\|a_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{\lambda+1}{3}}(0,\theta)}<\frac{1}{2}

we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}+\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}

and, consequently,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\right),

Since
\theta
does not depend on the data
u_{0}
, we can employ this inequality repeatedly (in a finite number of intervals of size
\theta
) to obtain the desired result in
[0,T]
.
∎

Finally, we can state an energy estimate to problem (
1.1
), in the case when
s=0
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
T>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
be. Consider
u^{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
,
g_{0}\in H^{\frac{\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T)
,
g\in[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}
,
p\in[H^{\frac{\lambda+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}
. If
u\in\mathbb{B}_{0,T}
is a solution of (

), then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{\lambda+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\end{array}\right).

Proof.

Write
u=v+w
where
v
and
w
are solutions of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{j}+\partial_{x}v_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}v_{j}=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\\
v_{j}(t,0)=v_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha v_{1}(t,0)+g_{0}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
v_{j}(t,l_{j})=p_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}v_{j}(t,l_{j})=0,&t\in(0,T),\\
v_{j}(0,x)=0,&x\in(0,l_{j}),\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w_{j}+\partial_{x}[(v_{j}+1)w_{j}]+\partial_{x}^{3}w_{j}w_{j}+w_{j}\partial_{x}w_{j}=-v_{j}\partial_{x}v_{j},&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\\

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}w_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha w_{1}(t,0)-\frac{N}{3}(w_{1}+v_{1})^{2}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\\

respectively. Propositions
3.8
and
5.2
gives us

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\leq\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda,T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{\lambda+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\end{array}\right),

and

\displaystyle\|w\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\leq C\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\right),

so the result follows.
∎

) in the energy

-level, that is, Theorem
1.2
when
s=0
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
T>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
be. Given
u^{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
,
g_{0}\in H^{\frac{\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T)
,
g\in[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}
,

, the problem (

) has a unique solution

which satisfies

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{\lambda+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\end{array}\right),

for some positive constant

and

.

Proof.

Thanks to Proposition

, the radius

can be chosen such that

\displaystyle\|u(\theta,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{\lambda+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\leq r.

5.2.
The case

For simplicity we denote

\displaystyle\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{s,\lambda}=\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{s+\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{H^{\frac{s}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{s+\lambda+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}

and we reiterate that constants can be adjusted per line, with the dependencies deemed important made explicit. In addition we denote by

the set of

-compatible data

The first result in this section plays an auxiliary role, and its proof follows from a combination of a fixed-point argument and Proposition

.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
T>0
,
v^{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}),\ \tilde{g_{0}}\in H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T),\ \tilde{g}\in[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N},\ \tilde{p}\in[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N},\ f\in L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
and
a\in\mathcal{X}_{0,T}
be given. Then, the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v_{j}+\partial_{x}[(1+a_{j})v_{j}]+\partial_{x}^{3}v_{j}=f_{j},&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\\
v_{j}(t,0)=v_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha v_{1}(t,0)-\frac{2N}{3}a_{1}(t,0)v_{1}(t,0)+\tilde{g}_{0}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
v_{j}(t,l_{j})=\tilde{p}_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}v_{j}(t,l_{j})=\tilde{g}_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
v_{j}(0,x)=v^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j})\end{array}\right.

has a unique solution
v\in\mathcal{X}_{0,T}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\leq C\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\left(\|v^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|\tilde{g}_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|\tilde{p}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right),

for some positive constant
C=C(t)>0
.

Proof.

Consider
\theta\in[0,T]
and the map
\Gamma_{a}:\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}
where, for each
w\in\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}
,
\Gamma_{a}w
is the solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v_{j}+\partial_{x}v_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}v_{j}=f_{j}-\partial_{x}(a_{j}w_{j}),&t\in(0,\theta),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,...,N,\\
v_{j}(t,0)=v_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,\theta),\ j=1,...,N,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha v_{1}(t,0)-\frac{2N}{3}a_{1}(t,0)w_{1}(t,0)+\tilde{g}_{0}(t),&t\in(0,\theta),\\
v_{j}(t,l_{j})=\tilde{p}_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}v_{j}(t,l_{j})=\tilde{g}_{j}(t),&t\in(0,\theta),\ j=1,...,N,\\
v_{j}(0,x)=v_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,...,N.\end{array}\right.

Thanks to Corollary
3.1
, Lemmas
3.1
and
3.4
, we see that
\Gamma_{a}
is well-defined. Moreover, defining

\displaystyle r=

\displaystyle 2C\left(\|v^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|\tilde{g}_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}(0,T)}}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|\tilde{p}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right)

we have

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|v^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|\tilde{g}_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,\theta)}+\frac{2N}{3}\|a_{1}(t,0)w_{1}(t,0)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,\theta)}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|\tilde{p}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,\theta)]^{N}}+\|f-\partial_{x}(aw)\|_{L^{1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{2}+C\left(\|\partial_{x}(aw)\|_{L^{1}(0,\theta;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}+\frac{2N}{3}\|a_{1}(t,0)w_{1}(t,0)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,\theta)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{2}+C\left((\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}})\|a\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}\|w\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,\theta}}+\theta^{\alpha}\frac{2N}{3}\|a_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,\theta)}\|w_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,\theta)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{2}+C\left(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{2N}{3}\theta^{\alpha}\right)\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}}\|w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}}.

Choosing
\theta\in(0,T)
such that

\displaystyle C\left(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{2N}{3}\theta^{\alpha}\right)\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}<\frac{1}{2}

we have
\Gamma_{a}(B)\subset B
where

\displaystyle B=\{v\in\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta};\ \|v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}}\leq r\}.

Moreover
\Gamma_{a}:B\rightarrow B
is a contraction since

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}v-\Gamma_{a}w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{2N}{3}\theta^{\alpha}\right)\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}}.

Now, using the Banach fixed point theorem, it follows that
\Gamma
has a fixed point
v\in B
, that is,
u
solves (
3.3
) for
t\in[0,\beta]
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}}=

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{a}v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{2N}{3}\theta^{\alpha}\right)\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{2N}{3}\theta^{\alpha}\right)\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C^{2}\left(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{2N}{3}\theta^{\alpha}\right)\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\left(\|v^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|\tilde{g}_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}(0,T)}}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|\tilde{p}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\right).

Since
\theta
does not depend on the datas
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p,f)
, using a standard continuation extension argument, the solution
u
can be extended to the interval
[0,T]
and the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}+\theta^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{2N}{3}\theta^{\alpha}\right)\|a\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\left(\|v^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|\tilde{g}_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}(0,T)}}+\|\tilde{g}\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\|\tilde{p}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))},\right)

for some positive constant
C:=C(T)
and
\alpha>0
, giving the lemma.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
T>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
be given. If

is a solution of (
1.1
) corresponding to the compatible data

\displaystyle u^{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T}),\quad g_{0}\in H^{\frac{3+\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T),\quad g\in[H^{\frac{3}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}\quad\text{and}\quad p\in[H^{\frac{3+\lambda+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N},

then

is given by

(5.12)

\displaystyle u_{j}(t,\cdot)=u_{j}^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}v_{j}(s,\cdot)ds,\ j=1,...,N,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v_{j}+\partial_{x}[(1+u_{j})v_{j}]+\partial_{x}^{3}v_{j}=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\\

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha v_{1}(t,0)-\frac{2N}{3}u_{1}(t,0)v_{1}(t,0)+g_{0}^{\prime}(t),&t\in(0,T)\\

Moreover

(5.18)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{3,T}}\leq&C_{1}\left(\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p\right)\|_{0,\lambda})\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{3+\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T)}\right.\\
&\left.+\|g\|_{[H^{\frac{3}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{3+\lambda+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right),\end{split}

where
C_{1}(r)=\alpha r^{3}+\beta r^{2}+\gamma r
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
depends on

.

Proof.

Suppose that
u\in\mathcal{X}_{3,T}
is a corresponding solution of (

). Deriving the equalities in (

) with respect to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}^{2}u_{j}+\partial_{x}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{t}u_{j}\partial_{x}u_{j}+u_{j}\partial_{x}\partial_{t}u_{j}=0,&\\
\partial_{t}u_{j}(t,0)=\partial_{t}u_{1}(t,0),&\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}\partial_{t}u_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha\partial_{t}u_{1}(t,0)-\frac{2N}{3}u_{1}(t,0)\partial_{t}u_{1}(t,0)+g_{0}^{\prime}(t),&\\
\partial_{t}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=p_{j}^{\prime}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}\partial_{t}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j}^{\prime}(t).&\end{array}\right.

Moreover

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{j}(0,x)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}u_{j}(0,x)-\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}(0,x)-u_{j}(0,x)\partial_{x}u_{j}(0,x)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}u_{j}^{0}(x)-\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}^{0}(x)-u_{j}^{0}(x)\partial_{x}u_{j}^{0}(x).

Setting

it follows that
v_{j}
solves (
5.4
) and (
5.12
) holds. Furthermore, Lemma

provides

(5.19)

\begin{split}\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\leq&C\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\left(\|-\partial_{x}u_{j}^{0}-\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}^{0}-u_{j}^{0}\partial_{x}u_{j}^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}\right.\\
&\left.+\|g_{0}^{\prime}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g^{\prime}\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p^{\prime}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right).\end{split}

We claim that
u\partial_{x}u\in C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
with

(5.20)

\displaystyle\|u\partial_{x}u\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\leq M_{1}(\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}+\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}})

for some positive constant

which depends on

and

. Indeed, observe that

Then, from Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u\partial_{x}u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}dt\leq C(T^{\frac{1}{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{3}})\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}\left(u\partial_{x}u\right)(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}dt\leq C(T^{\frac{1}{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{3}})\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}.

Consequently

5.1

\displaystyle\|u\partial_{x}u\|_{C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\leq C(T^{\frac{1}{2}}+T^{\frac{1}{3}})\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{0,\lambda}(\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}+\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}})

Let us now prove (
5.18
). To do that, by
[

\displaystyle\|u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{3}(0,l_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|P_{j}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}

\displaystyle=C\|\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,\cdot)+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}

\displaystyle=C\|-u_{j}\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,\cdot)-v_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u_{j}\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}+\|v_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|uu_{x}(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}\right)

which gives us

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C\left([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})\right)}\leq C(1+M_{1})(\|u\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}+\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{0,T}}).

On the other hand,

(5.22)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{4}u(t,\cdot)=\partial_{x}\left(u\partial_{x}u\right)(t,\cdot)-\partial_{x}^{2}u(t,\cdot)-\partial_{x}v(t,\cdot).

)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{4}(\mathcal{T})}^{2}

Theorem 5.2

Let

,

u\in\mathcal{X}_{3,T}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{3,T}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\|u^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{3+\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{H^{\frac{3}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{3+\lambda+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\right),

where
C_{1}(r,u^{0},g_{0},g,p)=\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{0,\lambda}(\alpha r^{3}+\beta r^{2}+\gamma r)
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
depending on
T
.

Proof.

Thanks to Proposition
5.4
, the radius
r
in the proof of Theorem
1.1
can be chosen such that

\displaystyle\|u(\theta,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{3+\lambda-1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g\|_{[H^{1}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p\|_{[H^{\frac{3+\lambda+1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\leq r.

Moreover, the corresponding parameter
\theta
depends only on

\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{0,\lambda}
.
Hence, the same radius
r
, and consequently the same
\theta
, can be used for the data
u(\theta,\cdot)
,
g_{0}
,
g
, and
p
, in order to obtain a solution on the interval

[\theta,2\theta]
. Repeating this fixed-point argument on successive subintervals of length
\theta
allows us to extend the solution to the entire interval
[0,T]
.
∎

5.3.
The case
s\in(0,3)
: Global well-posedness result

The results established for
s=0
and
s=3
can be extended to any
s\in(0,3)
by means of interpolation theory, for which we now present some preliminary considerations.

Let
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
be Banach spaces such that
B_{1}\hookrightarrow B_{0}
.
Given
0<\theta<1
and
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, we denote by

B_{\theta,p}=[B_{0},B_{1}]_{\theta,p}

the interpolation space obtained via the
K
-method (see, for instance,
[
34
]
).
When
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
are Hilbert spaces and
p=2
, we have

[B_{0},B_{1}]_{\theta,2}=[B_{0},B_{1}]_{\theta},

where
[B_{0},B_{1}]_{\theta}
denotes the interpolation space introduced in
[
27
]
or in
[
34
]
through the domains of self-adjoint operators.

For two pairs of indices
(\theta_{1},p_{1})
and
(\theta_{2},p_{2})
, we write

(\theta_{1},p_{1})<(\theta_{2},p_{2})
whenever one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}
;

(2)

\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}
and
p_{1}>p_{2}
.

Whenever
(\theta_{1},p_{1})<(\theta_{2},p_{2})
, it follows that
B_{\theta_{2},p_{2}}\hookrightarrow B_{\theta_{1},p_{1}}
.

The following theorem, due to Bona and Scott
[
13
]
, provides the first proof of global well-posedness of the initial value problem for the KdV equation posed on
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
B_{0}^{j}
and
B_{1}^{j}
be Banach spaces with
B_{1}^{j}\hookrightarrow B_{0}^{j}
,
j=1,2
. Let
\sigma
and
q
such that
0<\sigma<1
and
1\leq q\leq+\infty
. Suppose
A
is a mapping satisfying

i.

A:B_{\sigma,q}^{1}\rightarrow B_{0}^{2}
and for
f,g\in B_{\sigma,q}^{1}

\displaystyle\|Af-Ag\|_{B_{0}^{2}}\leq C_{0}\left(\|f\|_{B_{\sigma,q}^{1}}+\|g\|_{B_{\sigma,q}^{1}}\right)\|f-g\|_{B_{0}^{1}};

ii.

A:B_{1}^{1}\rightarrow B_{1}^{2}
and for
h\in B_{1}^{1}

\displaystyle\|Ah\|_{B_{1}^{2}}\leq C_{1}\left(\|h\|_{B_{\sigma,q}^{1}}\right)\|h\|_{B_{1}^{1}},

where
C_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
are continuous non-decreasing functions, for
i=0,1
.

If
(\theta,p)\geq(\sigma,q)
, then
A
maps
B_{\theta,p}^{1}
into
B_{\theta,p}^{2}
and for
f\in B_{\theta,p}^{1}

\displaystyle\|Af\|_{B_{\theta,p}^{2}}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{B_{\sigma,q}^{1}}\right)\|f\|_{B_{\theta,p}^{1}},

where, for
r>0
,
C=C(r)=4C_{0}(4r)^{1-\theta}C_{1}(3r)^{\theta}
.

With this in hand, we can obtain the global well-posedness for
s\in(0,3)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Given
s\in(0,3)
, define

B_{0}^{1}=\mathbb{X}_{0,T}^{\lambda},\qquad B_{1}^{1}=\mathbb{X}_{3,T}^{\lambda}.

Then, for
p=2
and
0<\theta<1
, we have

\displaystyle B_{\theta,2}^{1}=[B_{0}^{1},B_{1}^{1}]_{\theta}=\mathbb{X}_{(1-\theta)\cdot 0+\theta\cdot 3,T}^{\lambda}=\mathbb{X}_{3\theta,T}^{\lambda}.

Analogously, setting
B_{0}^{2}=\mathcal{X}_{0,T}
and
B_{1}^{2}=\mathcal{X}_{3,T}
, it follows that

B_{\theta,2}^{2}=\mathcal{X}_{3\theta,T}.

For
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\in\mathbb{X}_{3\theta,T}^{\lambda}
, define
A(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)=u
, where
u\in\mathcal{X}_{0,T}
is the corresponding solution of (
1.1
).
Given two elements
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)
and
(\tilde{u}^{0},\tilde{g}_{0},\tilde{g},\tilde{p})\in\mathbb{X}_{3\theta,T}^{\lambda}
, let

w=u-\tilde{u}=A(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)-A(\tilde{u}^{0},\tilde{g}_{0},\tilde{g},\tilde{p}).

Then
w
satisfies the problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w_{j}+\partial_{x}\big[(1+z_{j})w_{j}\big]+\partial_{x}^{3}w_{j}=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\\[3.00003pt]
w_{j}(t,0)=w_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\\[3.00003pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}w_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha w_{1}(t,0)-\tfrac{2N}{3}z_{1}(t,0)w_{1}(t,0)+g_{0}(t)-\tilde{g}_{0}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\[6.00006pt]
w_{j}(t,l_{j})=p_{j}(t)-\tilde{p}_{j}(t),\quad\partial_{x}w_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j}(t)-\tilde{g}_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\[3.00003pt]
w_{j}(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x)-\tilde{u}_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\end{cases}

where
z=\tfrac{1}{2}(u+\tilde{u})
. Applying Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\leq C\|z\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0,T}}\Big(\|u^{0}-\tilde{u}^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|g_{0}-\tilde{g}_{0}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}+\|g-\tilde{g}\|_{[L^{2}(0,T)]^{N}}+\|p-\tilde{p}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)]^{N}}\Big).

Hence, by Theorem
5.1
,

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{u}\|\leq C\big(\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{0,\lambda}+\|(\tilde{u}^{0},\tilde{g}_{0},\tilde{g},\tilde{p})\|_{0,\lambda}\big)\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)-(\tilde{u}^{0},\tilde{g}_{0},\tilde{g},\tilde{p})\|_{0,\lambda},

where
C>0
is a constant. Since
\mathbb{X}_{3\theta,T}^{\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{X}_{0,T}^{\lambda}
, this inequality can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{u}\|\leq C\big(\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{3\theta,\lambda}+\|(\tilde{u}^{0},\tilde{g}_{0},\tilde{g},\tilde{p})\|_{3\theta,\lambda}\big)\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)-(\tilde{u}^{0},\tilde{g}_{0},\tilde{g},\tilde{p})\|_{0,\lambda},

showing that
A
satisfies hypothesis (i) of Theorem
5.3
. From Theorem
5.2
, the map
A
sends
\mathbb{X}_{3,T}^{\lambda}
into
\mathcal{X}_{3,T}^{\lambda}
, and for every
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\in\mathbb{X}_{3,T}^{\lambda}
,

\displaystyle\|A(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{3,T}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\big(\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{0,\lambda}\big)\,\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{3,\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\big(\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{3\theta,\lambda}\big)\,\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{3,\lambda},

where
C_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a continuous nondecreasing function. This shows that
A
also satisfies hypothesis (ii) of Theorem
5.3
. Therefore,
A
maps
\mathbb{X}_{3\theta,T}^{\lambda}
into
\mathcal{X}_{3\theta,T}^{\lambda}
, and for every
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\in\mathbb{X}_{3\theta,T}^{\lambda}
,

\|A(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{3\theta,T}}\leq C_{1}\big(\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{3\theta,\lambda}\big)\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{3\theta,\lambda}.

Finally, choosing
\theta=\tfrac{s}{3}
yields the desired result:
for every
(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\in\mathbb{X}_{s,T}^{\lambda}
, the unique solution
u\in\mathcal{X}_{0,T}
of (
1.1
) actually belongs to
\mathcal{X}_{s,T}
and satisfies

\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s,T}}\leq C_{1}\big(\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{s,\lambda}\big)\|(u^{0},g_{0},g,p)\|_{s,\lambda}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

As in the linear case, an argument combining induction and interpolation ensures that the results obtained here remain valid for all
s>3
.

6.
Concluding Remarks

In this work, motivated by
[
14
,
16
]
, we established global well–posedness for the KdV equation on a star graph in the full range
s\in[0,3]
, together with sharp compatibility conditions that guarantee the existence of classical and mild solutions. These results provide a unified functional framework that treats both linear and nonlinear models, clarifying several aspects that were previously left open in the literature. The techniques developed here, particularly the lifting procedure, energy estimates in Sobolev spaces, and the compatibility structure at the junction, pave the way for several perspectives. Among them, we mention: the study of more general graph structures, for instance, including loops
[
7
]
or other dispersive models like the Kawahara equation
[
19
]
.

As we mentioned in the introduction, several control–theoretic questions naturally arise from our analysis. These topics are not treated in the present article and will be the focus of a forthcoming paper.

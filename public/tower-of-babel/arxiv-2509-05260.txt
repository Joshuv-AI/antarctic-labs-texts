1.
Introduction

Let
A\subset\mathbf{N}
be a finite set of
n
positive integers and consider the cosine polynomial

f_{A}(x)=\sum_{a\in A}\cos ax.

Since
\int_{0}^{2\pi}f_{A}(x)\,dx=0
,
f_{A}
assumes both strictly positive and strictly negative values. It is clear that
f_{A}(0)=n
and
\lVert f_{A}\rVert_{\infty}=n
, so it is trivial to determine the largest positive value that
f_{A}
assumes. Determining whether
f_{A}
must assume large negative values is a hard problem; Ankeny and Chowla
[
4
]
, motivated by questions on zeta functions, asked whether for any
K>0
, there is an
n_{0}
such that every set
A
of size
|A|\geqslant n_{0}
satisfies
|\min_{x}f_{A}(x)|>K
. In 1965, Chowla
[
5
]
posed the more precise question of finding the largest number
K(n)>0
such that any such cosine polynomial with
n
terms assumes a value smaller than or equal to
-K(n)
. Chowla’s cosine problem is thus to determine

K(n)=\min_{A\subset\mathbf{N}:|A|=n}\left(-\min_{x}f_{A}(x)\right).

There exist simple constructions of sets
A
of size
n
for which
\sum_{a\in A}\cos ax\geqslant-10\sqrt{n}
for all
x
. One can for example take
A=\{b_{1}-b_{2}:b_{j}\in B\}\setminus\{0\}
where
B
is a Sidon set
1
1

1

In this context, a set
B
is said to be
Sidon
if there are no nontrivial solutions to
x_{1}-x_{2}=x_{3}-x_{4}
with
x_{i}\in B
.
of size
m\approx\sqrt{n}
(and add up to
O(\sqrt{n})
arbitrary elements if
n
is not of the form
m^{2}-m
), and observe that
f_{A}=\hat{1}_{A}=|\hat{1}_{B}|^{2}-|B|=|\hat{1}_{B}|^{2}-O(\sqrt{n})
. This shows that
K(n)\ll\sqrt{n}
, which is the best known upper bound to date; in fact Chowla
[
5
]
conjectured that this is sharp, namely that
K(n)\asymp\sqrt{n}
.

There has been incremental progress on lower bounds for Chowla’s cosine problem. The first bound showing that
K(n)\to\infty
follows from Cohen’s work
[
6
]
on the Littlewood
L^{1}
conjecture, as demonstrated by S. and M. Uchiyama
[
13
]
. This was also observed by Roth
[
10
]
, who by different methods obtained the stronger bound
K(n)\gg(\log n)^{c}
for
c=1/2-o(1)
. We note that the value of this exponent
c
was later improved as an immediate consequence of various papers on the
L^{1}
conjecture, whose resolution by McGehee, Pigno and Smith
[
9
]
, and independently Konyagin
[
8
]
ultimately led to
c=1
. Bourgain
[
2
,
3
]
was the first to breach the logarithmic barrier, establishing the quasipolynomial bound
K(n)>e^{(\log n)^{\varepsilon}}
for some
\varepsilon>0
. A further refinement of Bourgain’s method by Ruzsa
[
11
]
shows that
K(n)\geqslant e^{c^{\prime}(\log n)^{1/2}}
, which stood as the record until now. We also mention that, among other results, Sanders
[
12
]
proved a polynomial bound for
|\min_{x}f_{A}(x)|
in the special setting where all elements of
A
have size
O(n)
. Our main result is the following improvement, producing the first polynomial bounds for the Chowla cosine problem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Any set
A\subset\mathbf{N}
of
n=|A|
of positive integers satisfies

\min_{x\in[0,2\pi]}\sum_{a\in A}\cos ax\leqslant-n^{1/7-o(1)}.

Hence,
K(n)\gg n^{1/7-o(1)}
. For clarity of exposition, we first prove this result with the slightly weaker bound
K(n)\gg n^{1/12}
using a streamlined version of our argument in Section
5
, which takes up only 5 pages. In the final Section
7
, we show that this may be improved to
K(n)\gg n^{1/7-o(1)}
with some further effort. It seems likely that our method can produce an even better exponent than
1/7
, but it remains an interesting problem to investigate whether one can reach the bound
K(n)\asymp n^{1/2}
.

We remark that, just this week, Jin, Milojević, Tomon and Zhang
[
7
]
independently uploaded a preprint that also establishes a polynomial bound
K(n)\gg n^{c}

with exponent
c\approx 1/100
, though the authors remark (personal communication) that further optimising could improve this to
c\approx 0.09
. The method in our paper differs significantly from that of Jin et al., whose main theorem is a structural result about graphs with no small (i.e. large and negative) eigenvalues.

We also mention that all other methods yielding superlogarithmic bounds for
K(n)
, including those in the work
[
7
]
of Jin et al., are very sensitive to the cosine polynomials having all their coefficients in
\{0,1\}
. Let
S
be a subset of
\mathbf{R}\setminus\{0\}
and write
\mathcal{C}_{S}(n)
for the class of cosine polynomials with
n
terms and coefficients in
S
. Then one can define the analogous quantity

K_{S}(n)=\min_{f\in\mathcal{C}_{S}(n)}\left(-\min_{x\in[0,2\pi]}f(x)\right).

The previous strongest general bound in the literature states that
K_{S}(n)\gg(\min_{s\in S}|s|)\log n
, which follows from a simple application of the
L^{1}
conjecture (as in McGehee-Pigno-Smith
[
9
]
). Our methods provide polynomial bounds in the general setting where
S
is an arbitrary finite set.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
S\subset\mathbf{R}\setminus\{0\}
be finite. Then there exist two constants
c_{S},c^{\prime}_{S}>0
such that the following holds. For every symmetric set
A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
of size
n=|A|
, and every choice of coefficients
s_{a}\in S
satisfying
s_{a}=s_{-a}
for all
a\in A
, we have that

\min_{x\in[0,2\pi]}\sum_{a\in A}s_{a}e(ax)\leqslant-c^{\prime}_{S}n^{c_{S}}.

Our current argument can only handle coefficient sets
S
whose size is fixed (or sufficiently small compared to
n
). One might wonder whether
K_{S}(n)
exhibits polynomial growth whenever
\min_{s\in S}|s|\gg 1
, irrespective of the size of
S
. This is false however: a rather deep construction of Belov and Konyagin
[
1
]
shows that there exist cosine polynomials
f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\cos(jx)
with positive integer coefficients in
S=\{1,2,\dots,O(\log n)^{3}\}
for which
\min_{x}f(x)\geqslant-O(\log n)^{3}
. One can alternatively interpret this as saying that the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
fails dramatically if one considers
multisets

A\subset\mathbf{Z}
of size
n
(whereas Chowla’s cosine problem only deals with genuine sets of
n
distinct integers), since, instead of the universal polynomial lower bound
-\min_{x}f(x)\gg n^{c}
, it becomes possible that
-\min_{x}f(x)\leqslant(\log n)^{O(1)}
. Finally, we reiterate an interesting open question of Ruzsa, which is to estimate
K_{[0.99,1.01]}(n)
, namely negative values of cosine polynomials with coefficients in the interval
[0.99,1.01]
. Seemingly the best known result for Ruzsa’s problem is still the rather weak bound
K_{[0.99,1.01]}(n)\gg\log n
which follows from the a simple application of the resolution of the
L^{1}
conjecture in
[
9
]
.

2.
Notation and prerequisites

We use the asymptotic notation
f=O(g)
,
f\ll g
, or
g=\Omega(f)
if there is an absolute constant
C
such that
|f(y)|\leqslant Cg(y)
for all
y
in a certain domain which will be clear from context. We write
f=o(g)
if
f(y)/g(y)\to 0
as
y\to\infty
, and we write
f\asymp g
if both
f\ll g
and
g\ll f
. We sometimes include an extra subscript such as
f=O_{S}(g)
to indicate that the implied constant
C
is allowed to depend on
S
.

We write
\mathbf{N},\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{C}
for the natural, integer, real, and complex numbers, respectively. For two sets
E_{1},E_{2}
, we define the sumset
E_{1}+E_{2}:=\{e_{1}+e_{2}:e_{1}\in E_{1},e_{2}\in E_{2}\}
and difference set
E_{1}-E_{2}=\{e_{1}-e_{2}:e_{1}\in E_{1},e_{2}\in E_{2}\}
. We use the standard notation
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
for
x\in\mathbf{R}
, and, as this function is
1
-periodic, it is natural to consider the domain of the variable
x
to be
\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{R}/\mathbf{Z}
. For a suitably integrable function
g:\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{C}
we denote, for
p\in[1,\infty)
, its
L^{p}
-norm by

\lVert g\rVert_{p}\vcentcolon=\left(\int_{0}^{1}|g(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p},

and
\lVert g\rVert_{\infty}
is the smallest constant
M
such that
|g(x)|\leqslant M
holds almost everywhere. Its Fourier transform is the function
\hat{g}:\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{C}
which is defined by
\hat{g}(n)=\int_{\mathbf{T}}g(x)e(-nt)\,dx
. If
f:\mathbf{Z}\to\mathbf{C}
is a function, we shall denote its Fourier transform by
\hat{f}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}f(n)e(nx)
which is a priori simply a formal series. In this paper,
\hat{f}:\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{C}
will always be a trigonometric polynomial. Of specific importance are the Fourier transforms of (indicator functions of) finite sets
A\subset\mathbf{Z}
, and we write
\hat{1}_{A}(x):=\sum_{a\in A}e(ax)
.

For two functions
g,h\in L^{2}(\mathbf{T})
we define
\langle g,h\rangle=\int_{\mathbf{T}}g(x)\overline{h(x)}\,dx
and we shall frequently make use of Parseval’s theorem which states that
\langle g,h\rangle=\sum_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}\hat{g}(n)\overline{\hat{h}(n)}
. We also define their convolution to be the function
g*h=\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{C}
given by
(g*h)(x)=\int_{\mathbf{T}}g(x-y)h(y)\,dy
, and we note the basic fact that
\widehat{g*h}(n)=\hat{g}(n)\hat{h}(n)
. This formula for the Fourier coefficients of a convolution plays an important role throughout this paper, as it implies crucial properties such as that
(\hat{1}_{A}*\hat{1}_{B})(x)=\hat{1}_{A\cap B}(x)
for any two finite
A,B\subset\mathbf{Z}
. Finally, Young’s convolution inequality states that

(1)

\lVert g*h\rVert_{r}\leqslant\lVert g\rVert_{p}\lVert h\rVert_{q}

whenever
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
satisfy
1+1/r=1/p+1/q
.

3.
Preliminary observations

Note that for a set
B\subset\mathbf{N}
of positive integers, we may write

2\sum_{b\in B}\cos(2\pi bx)=\sum_{a\in B\cup-B}e(ax)=\hat{1}_{A}(x),

where
A=B\cup-B
. We may therefore consider the following equivalent setup of the Chowla cosine problem, which is notationally more convenient. Let
A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
be symmetric, meaning that
A=-A
, so that
\hat{1}_{A}(x)
is a real-valued function on
\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{R}/\mathbf{Z}
(and a sum of
|A|/2
cosines). Let
K>0
be a constant such that

\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K=\sum_{a\in A}e(ax)+K\geqslant 0,\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

Our aim is to show that
K
is large in terms of
n=|A|
, and in order to establish Theorem
1.1
, we need to show that any such
K
satisfies
K\gg n^{1/7-o(1)}
. We introduce some convenient notation.

Definition 3.1
.

For a real-valued function
g:\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{R}
with
\int_{\mathbf{T}}g(x)\,dx=0
, we define

(2)

\lVert g\rVert_{\min}=-\min_{x\in\mathbf{T}}g(x).

Equivalently,
\lVert g\rVert_{\min}
is the smallest number
K
such that
g(x)+K
is pointwise non-negative for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
.

The assumption that
\int_{\mathbf{T}}g(x)\,dx=0
guarantees that
\lVert g\rVert_{\min}
is nonnegative. We may decompose any real-valued function
g(x)
as
g=g^{+}-g^{-}
where
g^{+}(x)=\max(g(x),0)
and
g^{-}(x)=\max(-g(x),0)
are nonnegative functions. It is clear that
\lVert g\rVert_{\min}=\lVert g^{-}\rVert_{\infty}
is a one-sided estimate for
g
, so that
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\min}
satisfies the triangle inequality and that
\lVert\lambda g\rVert_{\min}=\lambda\lVert g\rVert_{\min}
for
\lambda>0
(although it is not a norm since this is not well-behaved under dilations by negative
\lambda
). We require some useful basic lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
g
be a real-valued function on
\mathbf{T}
with
\int_{\mathbf{T}}g(x)\,dx=0
. Then

\lVert g\rVert_{1}\leqslant 2\lVert g\rVert_{\min}.

Proof.

By definition,
g(x)\geqslant-\lVert g\rVert_{\min}
holds for all
x
. Hence, the bound
|g(x)|\leqslant 2\lVert g\rVert_{\min}+g(x)
also holds pointwise. Integrating this inequality over
\mathbf{T}
gives the result because
\int_{\mathbf{T}}g(x)\,dx=0
.
∎

The following two results show how convolution interacts with
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\min}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
g,h
be real-valued functions on
\mathbf{T}
with
\int_{\mathbf{T}}g=\int_{\mathbf{T}}h=0
. Then

\lVert g*h\rVert_{\min}\leqslant\frac{\lVert g\rVert_{\min}\lVert h\rVert_{1}+\lVert g\rVert_{1}\lVert h\rVert_{\min}}{2}.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbf{T}
. We write
g^{+}=\max(g,0)
and
g^{-}=\max(-g,0)
so that
g=g^{+}-g^{-}
and
|g|=g^{+}+g^{-}
, and similarly for
h
. As
g^{+},g^{-},h^{+},h^{-}
are pointwise nonnegative, we may estimate

\displaystyle(g*h)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{T}}(g^{+}(u)-g^{-}(u))(h^{+}(x-u)-h^{-}(x-u))\,du

\displaystyle\geqslant-\int_{\mathbf{T}}g^{-}(u)h^{+}(x-u)\,du-\int_{\mathbf{T}}g^{+}(u)h^{-}(x-u)\,du

\displaystyle\geqslant-\lVert g\rVert_{\min}\int_{\mathbf{T}}h^{+}(u)\,du-\lVert h\rVert_{\min}\int_{\mathbf{T}}g^{+}(u)\,du.

Note that

\int_{\mathbf{T}}g^{+}(u)\,du=\int_{\mathbf{T}}g^{-}(u)\,du
because of the assumption that
\int_{\mathbf{T}}g(u)\,du=0
. As

\lVert g\rVert_{1}=\int_{\mathbf{T}}g^{+}(u)\,du+\int_{\mathbf{T}}g^{-}(u)\,du,

we therefore deduce that
\int_{\mathbf{T}}g^{+}(u)\,du=\lVert g\rVert_{1}/2
, and the analogous result for
h
. Plugging this into the inequality above shows that
(g*h)(x)\geqslant-\frac{1}{2}\left(\lVert g\rVert_{\min}\lVert h\rVert_{1}+\lVert g\rVert_{1}\lVert h\rVert_{\min}\right)
for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
.
∎

The next lemma provides a simpler one-sided estimate for the convolution of two functions in terms of their individual one-sided estimates. We note that, up to an extra factor of 2, this may be deduced by combining the previous two lemmas.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
g,h
be real-valued functions on
\mathbf{T}
with
\int_{\mathbf{T}}g=\int_{\mathbf{T}}h=0
. Then

\lVert g*h\rVert_{\min}\leqslant\lVert g\rVert_{\min}\lVert h\rVert_{\min}.

Proof.

Note that the functions
g(x)+\lVert g\rVert_{\min}
and
h(x)+\lVert h\rVert_{\min}
are pointwise non-negative. Hence, so is their convolution which, as
\int_{\mathbf{T}}g(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbf{T}}h(x)\,dx=0
, has the form

\displaystyle\left(g+\lVert g\rVert_{\min}\right)*\left(h+\lVert h\rVert_{\min}\right)

\displaystyle=g*h+\lVert g\rVert_{\min}\lVert h\rVert_{\min}.

We deduce that
g*h(x)\geqslant-\lVert g\rVert_{\min}\lVert h\rVert_{\min}
for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
.

∎

4.
Arithmetic results from Roth, Bourgain and Ruzsa

We will make use of two intermediate results from the approaches of Roth
[
10
]
, Bourgain
[
3
]
, and Ruzsa
[
11
]
. Both results indicate that sets
A
either contain or lack certain types of arithmetic structure under the assumption that
\lVert\hat{1}_{A}\rVert_{\min}
is small, this being a major theme in all three papers.

The first is due to Roth, who showed (something slightly stronger than) that
A
has large additive energy under the assumption that
\lVert\hat{1}_{A}\rVert_{\min}
is not too large. For the convenience of the reader, we reproduce the proof here.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
10
]
, Lemma 5)
.

Let
A=-A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and suppose that
\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K\geqslant 0
. Then every subset
B\subseteq A
of size
|B|\geqslant 2K^{2}
satisfies

\#\{(b_{1},b_{2})\in B^{2}:b_{1}-b_{2}\in A\}\geqslant\frac{|B|^{2}}{2K}.

Proof.

Note that the assumption implies that
|\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K|=\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K
. By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the functions
\hat{1}_{B}(x)|\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K|^{1/2}
and
|\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K|^{1/2}
, we see that

\displaystyle|B|

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{1}_{B},\hat{1}_{A}+K\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}(\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K)\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}|\hat{1}_{B}(x)|^{2}(\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K)\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=K^{1/2}\bigg(\#\{(b_{1},b_{2})\in B^{2}:b_{1}-b_{2}\in A\}+K|B|\bigg)^{1/2},

where the two equalities are applications of Parseval.
Rearranging gives the inequality

\#\{(b_{1},b_{2})\in B^{2}:b_{1}-b_{2}\in A\}\geqslant\frac{|B|^{2}}{K}-K|B|,

which yields the desired result since
|B|\geqslant 2K^{2}
.
∎

We have taken the following lemma from Ruzsa’s paper, though very similar results already appear in those of Roth and Bourgain.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
11
]
, Lemma 3.1)
.

Let
A=-A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
U,V\subset\mathbf{Z}
be such that
U-V+\{0,d\}\subset A
for some
d\neq 0
. Then

\lVert\hat{1}_{A}\rVert_{\min}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\min(|U|,|V|)}.

We only require the following immediate corollary for Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
A=-A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Suppose that the arithmetic progression
P
is contained in
A
. Then
|P|\ll\lVert\hat{1}_{A}\rVert_{\min}^{2}
.

To deal with the general setting in Theorem
1.2
, where we prove one-sided estimates for cosine polynomials with coefficients in an arbitrary finite set
S\subset\mathbf{R}\setminus\{0\}
, we need the following extension of Corollary
4.3
. The proof is a rather natural adaption of the argument of Ruzsa.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
S=\{s_{1}>s_{2}>\dots>s_{k}\}\subset\mathbf{R}\setminus\{0\}
be finite, and
s_{1}>0
. Suppose that
A^{(1)},\dots,A^{(k)}\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
are pairwise disjoint, finite, and symmetric. If
P
is an arithmetic progression that is contained in
A^{(1)}
, then

|P|\ll_{S}\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}\hat{1}_{A^{(j)}}\right\rVert_{\min}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
P=\{x_{0}-pd,x_{0}-(p-1)d,\dots,x_{0}+pd\}
be an arithmetic progression with common difference
d
and size
|P|\asymp p
, and assume that
P\subset A^{(1)}
. We define the subprogression
U_{0}:=\{x_{0}+d,\dots,x_{0}+pd\}
, and observe that
U_{0}-(-x_{0}+U_{0})+\{0,d\}\subset P\subset A^{(1)}
. Now consider the translates
U_{\ell}:=U_{0}+\ell d
of
U_{0}
by multiples of
d
, for
\ell\in\mathbf{Z}
. Since
A^{(1)}
is a finite set, it is clear that
U_{\ell}\cap A^{(1)}=\emptyset
for all sufficiently large
\ell
. On the other hand,
U_{0}\cap A^{(1)}=U_{0}
has size
p
by the definition of
U_{0}
, and hence there must exists some positive integer
\ell
for which

\displaystyle|U_{\ell}\cap A^{(1)}|

\displaystyle\geqslant p/2,

\displaystyle|U_{\ell+1}\cap A^{(1)}|

\displaystyle<p/2.

If
|(-x_{0}+U_{-\ell})\cap A^{(1)}|<p/2
then we define
U:=U_{\ell}\cap A^{(1)}
and
V:=(-x_{0}+U_{-\ell})\setminus A^{(1)}
. In the remaining case where
|(-x_{0}+U_{-\ell})\cap A^{(1)}|\geqslant p/2
, we define
U:=(-x_{0}+U_{-\ell})\cap A^{(1)}
and
V:=U_{\ell+1}\setminus A^{(1)}
. In both of these scenarios, we have therefore found sets
U,V
which each have size at least
p/2
, and where
U\subset A^{(1)}
and
V\cap A^{(1)}=\emptyset
. As we chose
U_{0}
so that
U_{0}-(-x_{0}+U_{0})+\{0,d\}\subset A^{(1)}
, we also have the crucial property that
U-V\subset A^{(1)}
. We may remove some elements from
U,V
so that, additionally,
0\notin U,V
and
|U|=|V|\geqslant p/2-1
. Let us write
F=\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}\hat{1}_{A^{(j)}}
, and
K=\lVert F\rVert_{\min}
. Then by Parseval, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}}(\hat{1}_{U}(x)-\hat{1}_{V}(x))(F(x)+K)\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U}\hat{F}(u)-\sum_{v\in V}\hat{F}(v)

\displaystyle\geqslant s_{1}|U|-s_{2}|V|\gg(s_{1}-s_{2})p.

The assumption that
F(x)+K
is pointwise nonnegative allows us to use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, giving the upper bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}}(\hat{1}_{U}(x)-\hat{1}_{V}(x))(F(x)+K)\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}(F(x)+K)\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}(F(x)+K)(|\hat{1}_{U}|^{2}+|\hat{1}_{V}|^{2}-\hat{1}_{U}\hat{1}_{-V}-\hat{1}_{V}\hat{1}_{-U})\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=K^{1/2}\left(K(|U|+|V|)+\sum_{u_{i}\in U:u_{1}\neq u_{2}}\hat{F}(u_{1}-u_{2})-2\sum_{u\in U,v\in V}\hat{F}(u-v)+\sum_{v_{i}\in V:v_{1}\neq v_{2}}\hat{F}(v_{1}-v_{2})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant K^{1/2}\left(K|U|+K|V|\right)^{1/2}\ll Kp^{1/2},

where we used Parseval, together with the facts that
U-V\subset A^{(1)}
and that all Fourier coefficients of
F
are at most
s_{1}
. Combining these inequalities shows that
p\ll_{S}K^{2}
, as we claimed.
∎

5.
Polynomial bounds for the Chowla cosine problem

In this section, we provide a streamlined argument which establishes polynomial bounds for Chowla’s cosine problem, proving Theorem
1.1
with the slightly smaller exponent
1/12
. We suppose throughout this section that
A=-A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
is a finite symmetric set of integers of size
n=|A|
, and that
K>0
is a constant such that
\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K\geqslant 0
for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
.
By Roth’s Lemma
4.1
, we know that

\sum_{t\in A}|A\cap(A+t)|=\#\{(a,t)\in A^{2}:a-t\in A\}\geqslant\frac{n^{2}}{2K}.

Hence, we can find a
t\neq 0
such that
A_{t}:=A\cap(A+t)
has size

(3)

|A_{t}|\geqslant n/2K.

We remark that the existence of these large sets
A_{t}
also plays a central role in the arguments of Roth, Bourgain and Ruzsa, though for a different reason.

Consider a fixed
t\in\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. The function
1+\sin(2\pi tx)
is clearly nonnegative for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
. Together with the assumption that
\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K\geqslant 0
for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
, this implies that

(1+\sin(2\pi tx))(\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K)\geqslant 0.

As
|1+\sin(2\pi tx)|\leqslant 2
, this in turn shows that

(1+\sin(2\pi tx))\hat{1}_{A}(x)+2K\geqslant 0,\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

We note that
\sin(2\pi tx)\hat{1}_{A}(x)=\frac{1}{2i}\hat{1}_{A+t}+\frac{-1}{2i}\hat{1}_{A-t}
and hence we may rewrite this as

\hat{1}_{A}(x)+\frac{1}{2i}\cdot\hat{1}_{A+t}(x)+\frac{-1}{2i}\cdot\hat{1}_{A-t}(x)+2K\geqslant 0.

It is convenient to rescale the function in the previous inequality by a factor of 2 and define
f_{t}(x)\vcentcolon=2(1+\sin(2\pi tx))\widehat{1_{A}}(x)
, so we observe from the expression above that the Fourier coefficients
\widehat{f_{t}}(m)
are given by

(4)

\displaystyle\widehat{f_{t}}(m)=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }m\in A\text{, and }m\notin(A+t)\cup(A-t)\text{ or }m\in(A+t)\cap(A-t),\\
2\pm\frac{1}{i}&\text{if }m\in A\text{, and }m\in(A\pm t)\setminus(A\mp t),\\
\frac{\pm 1}{i}&\text{if }m\notin A\text{, and }(A\pm t)\setminus(A\mp t),\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note also that we have shown that
\lVert f_{t}\rVert_{\min}\leqslant 4K
. Define
\lambda:=2-i
. The fact that
A
is symmetric implies that
A_{-t}:=A\cap(A-t)=-A\cap-(A+t)=-A_{t}
, so we can rewrite

(5)

\displaystyle f_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda\cdot\hat{1}_{A_{t}\setminus-A_{t}}(x)+\overline{\lambda}\cdot\hat{1}_{-A_{t}\setminus A_{t}}(x)+2\cdot\hat{1}_{A\setminus(A_{t}\triangle-A_{t})}(x)

\displaystyle-i\cdot\hat{1}_{(A+t)\setminus(A\cup(A-t))}(x)+i\cdot\hat{1}_{(A-t)\setminus(A\cup(A+t))}(x),

where
A_{t}\triangle-A_{t}=(A_{t}\setminus-A_{t})\cup(-A_{t}\setminus A_{t})
denotes the symmetric difference. What we have achieved by writing
f_{t}
in this form is that the terms have disjoint Fourier spectra, meaning that the five sets

A_{t}\setminus-A_{t},-A_{t}\setminus A_{t},A\setminus(A_{t}\triangle-A_{t}),(A+t)\setminus(A\cup(A-t)),(A-t)\setminus(A\cup(A+t))

are pairwise disjoint. We emphasise that
\lambda=2-i
is a constant (independent of
A
and
t
) and that its precise value is unimportant for the purpose of getting
some
polynomial bound for Chowla’s cosine problem; we only require that
|\lambda|>2
and that it has nonzero imaginary part. By using the inequality
(1-\sin(2\pi tx))\hat{1}_{A}(x)+2K\geqslant 0
instead of
(1+\sin(2\pi tx))\hat{1}_{A}(x)+2K\geqslant 0
, we obtain the analogous result that
\lVert g_{t}\rVert_{\min}\leqslant 4K
for the function

\displaystyle g_{t}(x)\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda}\cdot\hat{1}_{A_{t}\setminus-A_{t}}(x)+\lambda\cdot\hat{1}_{-A_{t}\setminus A_{t}}(x)+2\cdot\hat{1}_{A\setminus(A_{t}\triangle-A_{t})}(x)

\displaystyle+i\cdot\hat{1}_{(A+t)\setminus(A\cup(A-t))}(x)-i\cdot\hat{1}_{(A-t)\setminus(A\cup(A+t))}(x).

Note that the Fourier coefficients of
f_{t}
are the conjugates of those of
g_{t}
, i.e.
\widehat{f_{t}}(m)=\overline{\widehat{g_{t}}(m)}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
be a finite symmetric set. Suppose that there exists a constant
K>0
such that
\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K\geqslant 0
for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
. Let
t\in\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Then the functions
f_{t},g_{t}
satisfy
\lVert f_{t}\rVert_{\min},\lVert g_{t}\rVert_{\min}\leqslant 4K
.

This leads us to our central proposition. In the proof, we will write
h^{(*m)}=\underbrace{h*\dots*h}_{m}
for the
m
-fold convolution of a function
h
. It is also convenient to introduce some notation and write

(6)

\displaystyle A_{t}

\displaystyle:=A\cap(A+t),

\displaystyle B_{t}

\displaystyle:=A_{t}\setminus-A_{t}=(A\cap(A+t))\setminus(A\cap(A-t)),

\displaystyle C_{t}

\displaystyle:=(A+t)\setminus(A\cup(A-t)),

\displaystyle D_{t}

\displaystyle:=A\setminus(A_{t}\triangle-A_{t}).

Recall that
A_{-t}=A\cap(A-t)=-A_{t}
as
A
is symmetric, and similarly
B_{-t}=-B_{t}
and
C_{-t}=-C_{t}
. The set
D_{t}
is itself symmetric. This notation allows us to simplify the expression (
5
) for
f_{t}
as follows:

(7)

\displaystyle f_{t}(x)=

\displaystyle\lambda\cdot\hat{1}_{B_{t}}(x)+\overline{\lambda}\cdot\hat{1}_{-B_{t}}(x)+2\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}(x)

\displaystyle-i\cdot\hat{1}_{C_{t}}(x)+i\cdot\hat{1}_{-C_{t}}(x),

and analogously for
g_{t}
. In the statement of the next proposition, is helpful to recall that the sets
B_{t},-B_{t},D_{t},C_{t}
and
-C_{t}
are pairwise disjoint, which can easily be checked from their definitions.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
be a finite symmetric set satisfying the inequality
\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K\geqslant 0
for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
. Let
t\in\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

\displaystyle|11(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)-(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant 8\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}(x)+2(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)+O(K^{3}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

Remark
.

As will become clear later, the crucial feature of this inequality is that
\hat{1}_{B_{t}}
appears with Fourier coefficient 11 on the left hand size, while all Fourier coefficients of the function on the right hand side are strictly smaller, i.e. at most
8
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
5.1
and
3.4
, we obtain

(8)

\displaystyle\lVert f_{t}^{(*3)}\rVert_{\min}\ll K^{3},

\displaystyle\lVert g_{t}^{(*3)}\rVert_{\min}\ll K^{3}.

From (
7
), and as convolving two functions corresponds to multiplying their Fourier coefficients, we see that these convolutions have the following explicit expressions:

\displaystyle f_{t}^{(*3)}(x)=

\displaystyle\lambda^{3}\cdot\hat{1}_{B_{t}}(x)+\overline{\lambda}^{3}\cdot\hat{1}_{-B_{t}}(x)+8\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}(x)

\displaystyle+i\cdot\hat{1}_{C_{t}}(x)-i\cdot\hat{1}_{-C_{t}}(x),

\displaystyle g_{t}^{(*3)}(x)=

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda}^{3}\cdot\hat{1}_{B_{t}}(x)+\lambda^{3}\cdot\hat{1}_{-B_{t}}(x)+8\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}(x)

\displaystyle-i\cdot\hat{1}_{C_{t}}(x)+i\cdot\hat{1}_{-C_{t}}(x).

Note that
\lambda^{3}=(2-i)^{3}=2-11i
and
\overline{\lambda}^{3}=2+11i
. Let us use this to rewrite our expressions for
f_{t}^{(*3)}
and
g_{t}^{(*3)}
, so that upon recalling (
8
), we have shown that

\displaystyle-11i(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)+2(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)+8\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}(x)

\displaystyle+i(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})(x)\geqslant-O(K^{3}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}

and

\displaystyle 11i(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)+2(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)+8\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}(x)

\displaystyle-i(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})(x)\geqslant-O(K^{3}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

Note that these two inequalities have precisely the same terms on their left hand sides, except that the sign of the term
11i(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})-i(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})
is flipped. Observe also that this term is a real-valued function on
\mathbf{T}
, as it equals
-2\cdot\Im\left(11\cdot\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{C_{t}}\right)
. Hence, they combine to show the desired inequality that

\displaystyle|11(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)-(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})(x)|\leqslant 8\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}(x)+2(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)+O(K^{3}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

∎

The inequality in Proposition
5.2
provides very strong information, and we will see that if
B_{t}=A_{t}\setminus-A_{t}
is sufficiently large, then it can be used to obtain a polynomial bound for
K
. Recall from (
3
) that the assumption that
\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K\geqslant 0
allows us to find a
t\neq 0
so that
A_{t}=A\cap(A+t)
has size
|A_{t}|\geqslant n/(2K)
. For Proposition
5.2
, we need to upgrade this to the stronger conclusion that the set
B_{t}=A_{t}\setminus-A_{t}=(A\cap(A+t))\setminus(A\cap(A-t))
is large.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
A^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
be symmetric, and suppose that every arithmetic progression
P
which is contained in
A^{\prime}
has size at most
L
. Let
A^{\prime}_{t}=A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)
, where
t
is a nonzero integer. Then
B^{\prime}_{t}:=A^{\prime}_{t}\setminus-A^{\prime}_{t}
has size
|B^{\prime}_{t}|\geqslant|A^{\prime}_{t}|/L
.

Proof.

As
A^{\prime}
is symmetric, we may without loss of generality assume that
t>0
. First, we note that
-A^{\prime}_{t}=-A^{\prime}\cap-(A^{\prime}+t)=A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}-t)=A^{\prime}_{-t}
as
A^{\prime}
is symmetric. Now partition
A^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{i}P_{i}
into the minimum possible number of arithmetic progressions with common different
t
, meaning that the
P_{i}\subset A^{\prime}
are arithmetic progressions with common difference
t
for which
(\max P_{i})+t\notin A^{\prime}
and
(\min P_{i})-t\notin A^{\prime}
. We will show that every progression
P_{i}
which has size at least two contributes a unique element to
B^{\prime}_{t}=A^{\prime}_{t}\setminus-A^{\prime}_{t}
. Indeed it is clear that
(\max P_{i})\in A^{\prime}_{t}
as
(\max P_{i}),(\max P_{i})-t\in A^{\prime}
when
P_{i}
has size at least two, whereas the fact that
(\max P_{i})+t\notin A^{\prime}
by our definition of the
P_{i}
shows that
(\max P_{i})\notin A^{\prime}_{-t}=-A^{\prime}_{t}
. Let
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{r}
be the collection of progressions
P_{i}
which have size at least two, so we have just shown that

(9)

|B^{\prime}_{t}|\geqslant r.

Note on the other hand that if
a\in A^{\prime}_{t}=A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)
, then
a,a-t\in A^{\prime}
and hence every element of
A^{\prime}_{t}
is contained in one of these progressions
Q_{j}
of size at least two. This provides the following lower bound for the total size of
\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}
:

(10)

\sum_{j=1}^{r}|Q_{j}|\geqslant|A^{\prime}_{t}|.

By assumption, every arithmetic progression
Q\subset A
has size
|Q|\leqslant L
, so
|Q_{j}|\leqslant L
for all
j
. Combining this with (
9
) and (
10
) produces the inequality

|B^{\prime}_{t}|\geqslant r\geqslant\frac{1}{L}\sum_{j=1}^{r}|Q_{j}|\geqslant|A^{\prime}_{t}|/L
.
∎

Ruzsa’s Corollary
4.3
implies that, under the assumption that
\lVert\hat{1}_{A}\rVert_{\min}\leqslant K
, the largest progression contained in
A
has size
O(K^{2})
. By (
3
), we can find a
t\neq 0
so that
A_{t}=A\cap(A+t)
has size
|A_{t}|\geqslant n/(2K)
. Hence, Lemma
5.3
shows that
B_{t}=A_{t}\setminus-A_{t}
is also rather large:

(11)

|B_{t}|\gg\frac{n}{K^{3}}.

We then apply Proposition
5.2
to deduce that

(12)

\displaystyle|11(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)-(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant 8\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}(x)+2(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)+O(K^{3}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

The next proposition allows us to produce a lower bound for
K
from these two facts. We have stated it in a rather general form, as we shall need it again to prove Theorem
1.2
in Section
6
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}:\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{C}
be trigonometric polynomials, let
B\subset\mathbf{Z}
, and let
c,L>0
be constants. Suppose that
|P_{1}(x)|\leqslant P_{2}(x)+L
for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
, and that

(13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widehat{P_{1}}(b)&\geqslant 1+c,&\quad&\forall b\in B,\\
|\widehat{P_{2}}(m)|&\leqslant 1,&\quad&\forall m\in\mathbf{Z}.\end{aligned}

Then
L\geqslant c|B|/\lVert\hat{1}_{B}\rVert_{1}^{2}
.

Proof.

For notational convenience, we write
h(x):=|\hat{1}_{B}(x)|
. Observe that the Fourier coefficients
\hat{h}(n)
are real numbers (as
h
is a real-valued even function), and that by Parseval:

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\hat{h}(m)^{2}=\int_{\mathbf{T}}|h(x)|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbf{T}}|\hat{1}_{B}(x)|^{2}\,dx=|B|.

Since all Fourier coefficients of
\hat{1}_{B}
are
0
or
1
, we note that
(\hat{1}_{B}*\hat{1}_{B})(x)=\hat{1}_{B}(x)
. This implies the useful fact that
|\hat{1}_{B}(x)|\leqslant(h*h)(x)
holds for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
, because

(15)

|\hat{1}_{B}(x)|=|\hat{1}_{B}*\hat{1}_{B}(x)|\leqslant(|\hat{1}_{B}|*|\hat{1}_{B}|)(x)=(h*h)(x).

The first assumption in (
13
) and Parseval show that

\displaystyle(1+c)|B|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{b\in B}\widehat{P_{1}}(b)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{T}}P_{1}(x)\overline{\hat{1}_{B}(x)}\,dx.

As
|P_{1}|\leqslant P_{2}+L
, we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle(1+c)|B|\leqslant\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left(P_{2}(x)+L\right)|\hat{1}_{B}(x)|\,dx.

Note that both factors in the integrand are nonnegative functions; that the first term is nonnegative follows by the assumption that
|P_{1}|\leqslant P_{2}+L
. Using the observation (
15
) that
|\hat{1}_{B}(x)|\leqslant(h*h)(x)
therefore allows us to get the upper bound

(16)

\displaystyle(1+c)|B|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbf{T}}P_{2}(x)(h*h)(x)\,dx+L\int_{\mathbf{T}}(h*h)(x)\,dx.

For the contribution of first term in (
16
), which we call
T_{1}
, we may use Parseval and the second assumption in (
13
) that
|\widehat{P_{2}}(m)|\leqslant 1
for all
m
to obtain the bound

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\hat{h}(m)^{2}\widehat{P_{2}}(m)

(17)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\hat{h}(m)^{2}=|B|,

where the inequality holds because the terms
\hat{h}(m)^{2}
are nonnegative as the Fourier coefficients
\hat{h}(m)
are real, and the final equality is (
14
). To bound the contribution of the the second term in (
16
), which we call
T_{2}
, we simply note that

T_{2}=L\int_{\mathbf{T}}(h*h)(x)\,dx=L\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}h(x)\,dx\right)^{2}=L\lVert\hat{1}_{B}\rVert_{1}^{2}.

Finally, we may plug this estimate for
T_{2}
and the estimate (
17
) for
T_{1}
into (
16
), showing that

(1+c)|B|\leqslant|B|+L\lVert\hat{1}_{B}\rVert_{1}^{2},

and hence,
L\geqslant c|B|/\lVert\hat{1}_{B}\rVert_{1}^{2}
.
∎

By (
12
), we may apply Proposition
5.4
with
P_{1}=\frac{1}{8}\left(11(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})-(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})\right)
,
P_{2}=\hat{1}_{D_{t}}+\frac{1}{4}(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})
,
B=B_{t}
,
c=\frac{3}{8}
, and
L=O(K^{3})
. This yields the bound

(18)

K^{3}\gg\frac{|B_{t}|}{\lVert\hat{1}_{B_{t}}\rVert_{1}^{2}}\gg\frac{n}{K^{3}\lVert\hat{1}_{B_{t}}\rVert_{1}^{2}},

by using the lower bound (
11
) for the size of
B_{t}
.
We now need to find a good bound for the
L^{1}
-norm of
\hat{1}_{B_{t}}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
A^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
be a finite symmetric set. Let
t\in\mathbf{Z}\backslash\{0\}
, and write
A^{\prime}_{t}:=A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)
and
B^{\prime}_{t}:=A^{\prime}_{t}\setminus-A^{\prime}_{t}
. Then

\|\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}\|_{1}\ll\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}^{3}.

Proof.

Let us begin by recalling that
-A^{\prime}_{t}=A^{\prime}_{-t}
as
A^{\prime}
is symmetric, so
B^{\prime}_{t}=(A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t))\setminus(A^{\prime}-t)
and note that
B^{\prime}_{t}
is disjoint from
-B^{\prime}_{t}=B^{\prime}_{-t}
. By the triangle inequality, it suffices to prove the two bounds
\lVert\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}\rVert_{1},\lVert\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}\rVert_{1}\ll\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}^{3}
. First, since
B^{\prime}_{t}\cup-B^{\prime}_{t}=\big(A^{\prime}_{t}\cup A^{\prime}_{-t}\big)\setminus A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)\cap(A^{\prime}-t)
, we can write

(19)

\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{t}}+\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{-t}}-2\cdot\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)\cap(A^{\prime}-t)}.

As
A^{\prime}_{t}=A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)
, we see that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{t}}=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}*\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t}
, that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{-t}}=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}*\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t}
, and that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)\cap(A^{\prime}-t)}=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}*\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t}*\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t}
. Note also that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t}(x)=e(tx)\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}(x)
, so it is clear that
\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t}\rVert_{1}=\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}
. Hence, Young’s convolution inequality (
1
) gives the bound
\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{t}}\rVert_{1}\leqslant\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}\times\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t}\rVert_{1}=\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}^{2}
. Similarly, the other two functions
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{-t}}
and
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)\cap(A^{\prime}-t)}
have
L^{1}
-norm at most
\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}^{3}
, and hence so does (
19
).

Next, to estimate
\lVert\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}\rVert_{1}
, we observe that

(20)

\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}*\big(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t\setminus A^{\prime}-t}-\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t\setminus A^{\prime}+t}\big).

Note that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t\setminus A^{\prime}-t}-\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t\setminus A^{\prime}+t}=(e(tx)-e(-tx))\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}
and so has
L^{1}
-norm at most
2\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}
. Young’s convolution inequality (
1
) then shows that

\lVert\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}\rVert_{1}\leqslant\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}\times\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t\setminus A^{\prime}-t}-\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t\setminus A^{\prime}+t}\rVert_{1}\leqslant 2\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}^{2}.

∎

As
\lVert\hat{1}_{A}\rVert_{1}\ll K
by Lemma
3.2
, applying Lemma
5.5
with
A^{\prime}=A
shows that
\lVert\hat{1}_{B_{t}}\rVert_{1}\ll K^{3}
. We may now plug this
L^{1}
-bound into (
18
) to see
that
K^{3}\gg n/K^{9}
. So
K\gg n^{1/12}
, completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
with the exponent
1/12
. We will improve this exponent to
1/7
in Section
7
.

6.
One-sided estimates for cosine polynomials with general coefficients

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
1.2
, establishing polynomial bounds for
K_{S}(n)
whenever
S\subset\mathbf{R}\setminus\{0\}
is a finite set of coefficients. The argument is very similar to that of Theorem
1.1
, though more involved. We restate the theorem here for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
S\subset\mathbf{R}\setminus\{0\}
be finite. Then there exist two constants
c_{S},c^{\prime}_{S}>0
such that the following holds. For every symmetric set
A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
of size
n=|A|
, and every choice of coefficients
s_{a}\in S
satisfying
s_{a}=s_{-a}
for all
a\in A
, we have that

(21)

\min_{x}\sum_{a\in A}s_{a}e(ax)\leqslant-c^{\prime}_{S}n^{c_{S}}.

Proof.

We shall prove this theorem by induction on the size of
S
. First, we note that Theorem
6.1
holds when
S=\{s\}
is a singleton. Indeed, this is trivial when
s<0
by evaluating at
x=0
, and when
s>0
we may apply Theorem
1.1
.

By rescaling
S
(and correspondingly scaling the final value of
c^{\prime}_{S}
), we may assume that
\max_{s\in S}|s|=1
. We claim that, without loss of generality, we may additionally assume that
S=\{s_{1}>s_{2}>\dots>s_{k}\}\subset(0,\infty)
consists of positive numbers which satisfy
s_{1}=1
and
s_{j}<\frac{1}{1000}
(say) for
2\leqslant j\leqslant k
. To see this, observe that if
A
is a symmetric set
A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
of size
n=|A|
and
z_{a}\in S
are some coefficients satisfying
z_{a}=z_{-a}
for all
a\in A
such that

\sum_{a\in A}z_{a}e(ax)\geqslant-K,\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T},

then taking
2\ell
convolutions and applying Lemma
3.4
shows that

(22)

\sum_{a\in A}z_{a}^{2\ell}e(ax)\geqslant-K^{2\ell},\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

Hence, we have shown that if we write

So let us now consider a fixed finite set

. We may assume that the conclusion of Theorem

Our goal is to show that

. We begin by noting that we may partition

into disjoint symmetric sets such that

(24)

\displaystyle F(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}\hat{1}_{A^{(j)}}(x)\geqslant-K.

Claim 6.2
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
|A^{(1)}|\gg_{S}n^{\Omega_{S}(1)}
.

Proof of Claim
6.2
.

By applying the induction hypothesis with the set

\min_{x}\sum_{j=2}^{k}s_{j}\hat{1}_{A^{(j)}}(x)\leqslant-c^{\prime}_{S^{\prime}}\left(n-|A^{(1)}|\right)^{c_{S^{\prime}}}.

Hence, using a trivial upper bound for

So either

.
∎

As in the previous section, we define two auxiliary functions

\mp i+\varepsilon_{m}&\text{ if $m\in\pm C^{(1)}_{t}$,}\\

∎

Define the real numbers

Claim 6.5

We have that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\sigma_{m}e(mx)\right|\leqslant\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\rho_{m}e(mx)+O(K^{3}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

Proof of Claim
6.5
.

By Lemmas
6.3

(29)

\displaystyle\lVert F_{t}^{(*3)}\rVert_{\min}\ll K^{3},

\displaystyle\lVert G_{t}^{(*3)}\rVert_{\min}\ll K^{3}.

As we defined

, we see that these convolutions have the following explicit expressions:

\displaystyle F_{t}^{(*3)}(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\lambda_{m}^{3}e(mx),

\displaystyle G_{t}^{(*3)}(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\overline{\lambda_{m}}^{3}e(mx).

Recall that

. Let us use this to rewrite our expressions for

and

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\rho_{m}e(mx)+i\sum_{m}\sigma_{m}e(mx)\geqslant-O(K^{3}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}

Note that these two inequalities have precisely the same terms on their left hand sides, except that the sign of the term

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\sigma_{m}e(mx)\right|\leqslant\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\rho_{m}e(mx)+O(K^{3}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

∎

To apply Proposition

, we need to check that some of the Fourier coefficients

. By cubing the expressions from Claim

\displaystyle\lambda_{m}^{3}=\begin{cases}2\mp 11i+\delta_{m}&\text{ if $m\in\pm B^{(1)}_{t}$,}\\
8+\delta_{m}&\text{ if $m\in D^{(1)}_{t}$,}\\

for some complex numbers

(say). Hence, it is clear that

for all

, while

for all

. We also just showed in Claim

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\sigma_{m}e(mx)\right|\leqslant\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\rho_{m}e(mx)+O(K^{3}),\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

These two ingredients allow us to apply Proposition
5.4
with
P_{1}=\frac{1}{9}\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\sigma_{m}e(mx)
,

,

,

, and

. This yields the bound

K^{3}\gg\frac{|B_{t}^{(1)}|}{\left\lVert\hat{1}_{B_{t}^{(1)}}\right\rVert_{1}^{2}}.

The final step in the proof of Theorem
6.1
is to show that we may pick a nonzero

such that
|B^{(1)}_{t}|\geqslant n^{\Omega_{s}(1)}/K^{O_{S}(1)}
and
\left\lVert\hat{1}_{B_{t}^{(1)}}\right\rVert_{1}\leqslant K^{O_{S}(1)}
. We first need to obtain an

-bound for

.

Claim 6.6
.

We have that
\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{(1)}}\rVert_{1}\leqslant K^{\Omega_{S}(1)}

Proof of Claim
6.6
.

Recall that we started our proof with the assumption (
24
) that
\lVert F\rVert_{\min}\ll K
, where
F(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{|S|}s_{j}\hat{1}_{A^{(j)}}(x)
. By Lemma
3.4
, this implies that
\lVert F^{(*\ell)}\rVert_{\min}\ll K^{|S|}
for all

. Lemma

then produces the

-bounds
\lVert F^{(*\ell)}\rVert_{1}\ll K^{|S|}
for these convolutions of

. These convolutions have the following Fourier series:

The invertibility of the Vandermonde matrix

shows that we may find some real numbers

, which clearly also have size

can be expressed as a linear combination of the convolutions

, which each have

By plugging this

K^{O_{S}(1)}\gg|B_{t}^{(1)}|,

.

. We achieve this final task in the next lemma. Recall that by Claim

, we may indeed assume that

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
F=\sum_{j=1}^{|S|}s_{j}\hat{1}_{A^{(j)}}
satisfy (

), and suppose that
|A^{(1)}|\gg_{S}n^{\Omega_{S}(1)}
. Then there exists a nonzero

such that

.

Proof of Lemma
6.7
.

Hölder’s inequality shows that
\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{(1)}}\rVert_{4}^{2/3}\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{(1)}}\rVert_{1}^{1/3}\geqslant\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{(1)}}\rVert_{2}
. Now by Parseval,
\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{(1)}}\rVert_{2}=|{A^{(1)}}|^{1/2}
. Parseval also shows that

\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{(1)}}\rVert_{4}^{4}=\int_{\mathbf{T}}(\hat{1}_{A^{(1)}})^{2}(\hat{1}_{-A^{(1)}})^{2}\,dx=\#\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in A^{(1)}:x_{1}-x_{2}=x_{3}-x_{4}\}.

Using the

-bound

from Claim

then yields

\#\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in A^{(1)}:x_{1}-x_{2}=x_{3}-x_{4}\}\geqslant\frac{\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{(1)}}\rVert_{2}^{6}}{\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{(1)}}\rVert_{1}^{2}}\gg\frac{|{A^{(1)}}|^{3}}{K^{O_{S}(1)}}.

The quantity on the left hand side is the additive energy of
A^{(1)}
, and a standard computation reveals that

\displaystyle\#\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in A^{(1)}:x_{1}-x_{2}=x_{3}-x_{4}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathbf{Z}}|A^{(1)}\cap(t+A^{(1)})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\max_{t}|A^{(1)}\cap(t+A^{(1)})|\right)\sum_{m}|A^{(1)}\cap(m+A^{(1)})|.

As
\sum_{m}|A^{(1)}\cap(m+A^{(1)})|=|A^{(1)}|^{2}
, the previous two inequalities show that we may find some

such that
A^{(1)}_{t}=A^{(1)}\cap(t+A^{(1)})
has size

(32)

|A^{(1)}_{t}|\gg\frac{|A^{(1)}|}{K^{\Omega_{S}(1)}}.

Lemma
4.4
implies that the largest arithmetic progression contained inside

has size

. Hence, we may apply Lemma
5.3
with the set

to deduce that

\displaystyle|B_{t}^{(1)}|

\displaystyle\gg\frac{|A^{(1)}_{t}|}{K^{2}}

\displaystyle\gg|A^{(1)}|/K^{\Omega_{S}(1)}

\displaystyle\gg_{S}n^{\Omega_{S}(1)}/K^{O_{S}(1)},

where the second inequality is (
32
), and the third follows from our assumption that
|A^{(1)}|\gg n^{\Omega_{S}(1)}
. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

So by Lemma

such that

. Plugging this into (

∎

7.
An improved exponent

We put some further effort into improving the value of the exponent that our method produces for Chowla’s cosine problem. Suppose throughout this section that
A=-A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
is a finite symmetric set of integers of size
n=|A|
, and that
K>0
is a constant such that

\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K\geqslant 0,\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{T}.

We shall reuse many of the intermediate results from Section
5
, and the reader may wish to recall the definitions (
6
) of the sets
A_{t},B_{t},C_{t},D_{t}
. By Proposition
5.2
, we have that

(33)

\displaystyle|11(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})-(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})|\leqslant 8\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}+2(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})+O(K^{3}),

for each fixed
t\in\mathbf{Z}
.
We shall obtain a quantitative improvement over the argument in Proposition
5.4
. To do this, we need two lemmas which provide stronger information about
\hat{1}_{B_{t}}
than the simple
L^{1}
-bound from Lemma
5.5
that we used in Section
5
.
The idea behind the first lemma is partially inspired by the method of Bourgain
[
3
]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
A^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
be a finite symmetric set such that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}+L\geqslant 0
. Let
t\in\mathbf{Z}\backslash\{0\}
, and write
A^{\prime}_{t}:=A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)
and
B^{\prime}_{t}:=A^{\prime}_{t}\setminus-A^{\prime}_{t}
. Then we can write

\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}(x)=Q_{1}(x)+Q_{2}(x),

where
Q_{1},Q_{2}:\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{C}
satisfy
|Q_{1}(x)|\leqslant 4(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}(x)+L)
for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
, and
\lVert Q_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\ll L^{3}
.

Remark
.

This lemma is stronger than Lemma
5.5
in that, on top of the
L^{1}
-bound
\lVert\hat{1}_{B_{t}}\rVert_{1}\ll K^{3}
, it shows that almost all of the
L^{2}
-mass of
\hat{1}_{B_{t}}
comes from a function
Q_{1}
whose
L^{1}
-norm is
O(K)
.

Proof.

As
\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}})+\frac{1}{2}(\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}})
, it suffices to show that

(34)

\displaystyle\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}

\displaystyle=R_{1}+R_{2}

(35)

\displaystyle\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}

\displaystyle=S_{1}+S_{2},

where
|R_{1}(x)|,|S_{1}(x)|\leqslant 4(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}(x)+L)
, and
\lVert R_{2}\rVert_{\infty},\lVert S_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\ll L^{3}
. The assumption that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}+L\geqslant 0
implies that we may write
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}(x)=T_{1}(x)+T_{2}(x)
where
T_{1}(x)=\max(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}},0)
satisfies
|T_{1}(x)|\leqslant\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}(x)+L
, and
T_{2}(x)=\min(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}},0)
satisfies
\lVert T_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant L
. Exactly as in (
19
), we have that

(36)

\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}=\widehat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{t}}+\widehat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{-t}}-2\cdot\widehat{1}_{A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)\cap(A^{\prime}-t)}.

Note that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{t}}=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}*\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t}
, that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{-t}}=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}*\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t}
, and that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)\cap(A^{\prime}-t)}=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}*\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t}*\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t}
. Hence, as
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t}(x)=e(tx)\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}(x)
, we see that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{t}}=(T_{1}+T_{2})*(e(t\cdot)T_{1}+e(t\cdot)T_{2})
. We can bound

|T_{1}*(e(t\cdot)T_{1})|\leqslant|T_{1}|*|T_{1}|\leqslant(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}+L)*(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}+L)=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}+L^{2},

and hence it is clear that we may split
T_{1}*(e(t\cdot)T_{1})=U+V
where
|U(x)|\leqslant\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}(x)+L
and
\lVert V\rVert_{\infty}\ll L^{2}
. Young’s convolution inequality (
1
) shows that the three remaining convolutions have
L^{\infty}
-norm at most
L^{2}
; for example,
\lVert T_{1}*(e(t\cdot)T_{2})\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\lVert T_{1}\rVert_{1}\lVert T_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant L^{2}
. This shows that we may write

\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{t}}=R^{\prime}_{1}+R^{\prime}_{2}

where
R^{\prime}_{1}:=U
satisfies
|R_{1}(x)|\leqslant\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}(x)+L
, and
R^{\prime}_{2}
consists of
V
and the remaining three convolutions, so
\lVert R^{\prime}_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\ll L^{2}
. A very similar argument provides analogous expressions
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}_{-t}}=R^{\prime\prime}_{1}+R^{\prime\prime}_{2}
and
\widehat{1}_{A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)\cap(A^{\prime}-t)}=R^{\prime\prime\prime}_{1}+R^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2}
for the other two terms in (
36
), where
|R^{\prime\prime}_{1}(x)|,|R_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)|\leqslant\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}(x)+L
, and
\lVert R^{\prime\prime}_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\ll L^{2},\lVert R^{\prime\prime\prime}_{3}\rVert_{\infty}\ll L^{3}
. Hence,
R_{1}:=R^{\prime}_{1}+R^{\prime\prime}_{2}-2R^{\prime\prime\prime}_{1}
and
R_{2}:=R^{\prime}_{2}+R^{\prime\prime}_{2}-2R^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2}
are the desired functions in (
34
).

The argument obtaining the expression (
35
) for
\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}
is again very similar, and this time based on the identity (
20
):
\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}*\big(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t\setminus A^{\prime}-t}-\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t\setminus A^{\prime}+t}\big)
.
Note that

\displaystyle\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t\setminus A^{\prime}-t}-\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t\setminus A^{\prime}+t}

\displaystyle=(e(tx)-e(-tx))\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(e(tx)-e(-tx))T_{1}(x)+(e(tx)-e(-tx))T_{2}(x)

and so this function can be written as
S^{\prime}_{1}+S^{\prime}_{2}
where
|S^{\prime}_{1}(x)|\leqslant 2|T_{1}(x)|\leqslant 2(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}+L)
, and
\lVert S^{\prime}_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant 2L
. Convolving this expression
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t\setminus A^{\prime}-t}-\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t\setminus A^{\prime}+t}=S^{\prime}_{1}+S^{\prime}_{2}
with
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}=T_{1}+T_{2}
and arguing as above yields the desired expression (
35
) for
\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}
.
∎

The next auxiliary lemma provides better
L^{1}
-bounds for the functions appearing on the left hand side of (
33
).

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
A^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
be a finite symmetric set such that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}+L\geqslant 0
. Let
t\in\mathbf{Z}\backslash\{0\}
, and write
A^{\prime}_{t}:=A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t)
,
B^{\prime}_{t}:=A^{\prime}_{t}\setminus-A^{\prime}_{t}=(A^{\prime}\cap(A^{\prime}+t))\setminus(A^{\prime}-t)
, and
C^{\prime}_{t}=(A^{\prime}+t)\setminus(A^{\prime}\cup(A^{\prime}-t))
. Then

\displaystyle\lVert\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\ll L^{2},

\displaystyle\lVert\hat{1}_{C^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C^{\prime}_{t}}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\ll L^{2}.

Proof.

The proof of the first
L^{1}
-bound already appears in the proof of Lemma
5.5
. We give the proof again, as we will need one of its intermediate inequalities. Recall the identity (
20
) that
\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}=\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}*\big(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t\setminus A^{\prime}-t}-\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t\setminus A^{\prime}+t}\big)
. Note that
\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t\setminus A^{\prime}-t}-\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t\setminus A^{\prime}+t}=(e(tx)-e(-tx))\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}
and so has
L^{1}
-norm at most
2\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}\leqslant 4L
by Lemma
3.2
. Young’s convolution inequality (
1
) then shows that

\lVert\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}\rVert_{1}\leqslant\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}\times\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t\setminus A^{\prime}-t}-\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t\setminus A^{\prime}+t}\rVert_{1}\ll\lVert\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}}\rVert_{1}^{2}\ll L^{2}.

We also observe that

\displaystyle\hat{1}_{C^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C^{\prime}_{t}}

\displaystyle:=\hat{1}_{(A^{\prime}+t)\setminus(A^{\prime}\cup(A^{\prime}-t))}-\hat{1}_{(A^{\prime}-t)\setminus(A^{\prime}\cup(A^{\prime}-t))}

\displaystyle=\left(\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}+t\setminus A^{\prime}-t}-\hat{1}_{A^{\prime}-t\setminus A^{\prime}+t}\right)-\left(\hat{1}_{B^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B^{\prime}_{t}}\right),

so the desired bound
\lVert\hat{1}_{C^{\prime}_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C^{\prime}_{t}}\rVert_{1}\ll L^{2}
follows by the triangle inequality and the two previous
L^{1}
-bounds.
∎

We shall now employ a more efficient version of the argument in Proposition
5.4
, which the reader may recall was based on testing the inequality (
33
) against the test function
h*h
, where
h(x)=|\hat{1}_{B_{t}}(x)|
. The main idea in this improved version of the argument is to use the test function
H_{1}*H_{1}
instead, where
H_{1}(x):=|Q_{1}(x)|
and
Q_{1},Q_{2}
are the functions whose existence is guaranteed by Lemma
7.1
: we may express

\hat{1}_{B_{t}}(x)=Q_{1}(x)+Q_{2}(x),

where
Q_{1},Q_{2}:\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{C}
satisfy
|Q_{1}(x)|\leqslant 4(\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K)
for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
, and
\lVert Q_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\ll K^{3}
.

Firstly, let us obtain an upper bound for

(37)

\displaystyle X:=\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left(8\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}(x)+2(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)+O(K^{3})\right)(H_{1}*H_{1})(x)\,dx.

Note that
\int_{\mathbf{T}}O(K^{3})(H_{1}*H_{1})\,dx=O(K^{3})\lVert Q_{1}\rVert_{1}^{2}\ll K^{5}
as
H_{1}(x)=|Q_{1}(x)|
and because the fact that
|Q_{1}(x)|\leqslant 4(\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K)
implies that
\lVert Q_{1}\rVert_{1}\leqslant 4K
. By Parseval, we can calculate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left(8\cdot\hat{1}_{D_{t}}(x)+2(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}+\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)\right)(H_{1}*H_{1})(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=8\sum_{m\in D_{t}}\widehat{H_{1}}(m)^{2}+2\sum_{m\in B_{t}\cup-B_{t}}\widehat{H_{1}}(m)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 8\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}\widehat{H_{1}}(m)^{2}=8\lVert H_{1}\rVert_{2}^{2},

where we used that the terms
\widehat{H_{1}}(m)^{2}
are nonnegative (as
H_{1}=|Q_{1}|
is a real-valued even function so its Fourier coefficients
\widehat{H_{1}}(m)
are real), and the final equality is Parseval. Observe that

\displaystyle\lVert H_{1}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert Q_{1}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-Q_{2}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant|B_{t}|^{1/2}+O(K^{3}),

using the triangle inequality and that
\lVert Q_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\ll K^{3}
. Putting everything together, the expression in (
37
) can be bounded from above by

(38)

X\leqslant 8|B_{t}|+O(K^{3}|B_{t}|^{1/2}+K^{6}).

Next, we need to find a good lower bound for (
37
). We begin by using the crucial inequality (
33
) and the basic fact that
H_{1}*H_{1}\geqslant|Q_{1}*Q_{1}|
as
H_{1}=|Q_{1}|
to obtain

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left|11(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})(x)-(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})(x)\right||(Q_{1}*Q_{1})(x)|\,dx.

As
Q_{1}+Q_{2}=\hat{1}_{B_{t}}
and
\hat{1}_{B_{t}}=\hat{1}_{B_{t}}*\hat{1}_{B_{t}}
, we see that
Q_{1}*Q_{1}=\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-2Q_{1}*Q_{2}-Q_{2}*Q_{2}
. Hence, we find that

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\geqslant\left|\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left(11(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})-(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})\right)(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-2Q_{1}*Q_{2}-Q_{2}*Q_{2})\,dx\right|

(39)

\displaystyle\geqslant 11|B_{t}|-\left|\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left(11(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})-(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})\right)(-2Q_{1}*Q_{2}-Q_{2}*Q_{2})\,dx\right|,

by Parseval. We show that the contribution of the remaining terms is rather small:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left|11(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})-(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})\right||2Q_{1}*Q_{2}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant 22\left(\lVert\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}}\rVert_{1}+\lVert\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}}\rVert_{1}\right)\lVert Q_{1}*Q_{2}\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\ll K^{2}\lVert Q_{1}\rVert_{1}\lVert Q_{2}\rVert_{\infty},

by using Lemma
7.2
and that
\lVert Q_{1}*Q_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\leqslant\lVert Q_{1}\rVert_{1}\lVert Q_{2}\rVert_{\infty}
(which is a trivial instance of Young’s convolution inequality). Recall that
\lVert Q_{1}\rVert_{1}\leqslant 4K
as
|Q_{1}(x)|\leqslant 4(\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K)
and that
\lVert Q_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\ll K^{3}
, so we deduce that the contribution of this term above is
O(K^{6})
. Finally, by Parseval we can calculate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left(11(\hat{1}_{B_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-B_{t}})-(\hat{1}_{C_{t}}-\hat{1}_{-C_{t}})\right)(-Q_{2}*Q_{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=11\left(-\sum_{m\in B_{t}}\widehat{Q_{2}}(m)^{2}+\sum_{m\in-B_{t}}\widehat{Q_{2}}(m)^{2}\right)+\sum_{m\in C_{t}}\widehat{Q_{2}}(m)^{2}-\sum_{m\in-C_{t}}\widehat{Q_{2}}(m)^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant-11\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}|\widehat{Q_{2}}(m)|^{2}=-11\lVert Q_{2}\rVert_{2}^{2}\geqslant-O(K^{6}),

by Parseval and because
\lVert Q_{2}\rVert_{\infty}\ll K^{3}
. Plugging everything into (
39
) gives the lower bound
X\geqslant 11|B_{t}|-O(K^{6})
. Combining this with the upper bound for
X
in (
38
) yields
11|B_{t}|\leqslant 8|B_{t}|+O(K^{3}|B_{t}|^{1/2}+K^{6}),
which clearly implies that

(40)

K^{6}\gg|B_{t}|.

Inequality (
11
) in Section
5
shows that we may find a
t
such that
|B_{t}|\gg n/K^{3}
, which combines with (
40
) to show that
K\gg n^{1/9}
. To strengthen this to
K\gg n^{1/7-o(1)}
, we need the next lemma which allows us to find a
t
for which the better bound
|B_{t}|\gg n^{1-o(1)}/K
holds.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
A\subset\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
be a finite symmetric set satisfying the inequality
\hat{1}_{A}(x)+K\geqslant 0
for all
x\in\mathbf{T}
. Then there exists a
t\in\mathbf{Z}\setminus\{0\}
such that
|B_{t}|\gg n/(K(\log n)^{4})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
|B_{t}|\leqslant L
for all
t\neq 0
, so our goal is to show that
L\gg n/(K(\log n)^{4})
. We begin by recalling (
9
) in Lemma
5.3
which states that, if
A=\bigsqcup P_{i}^{(t)}
is the partition of
A
into the minimal possible number of arithmetic progressions
P_{i}^{(t)}
with common difference
t
, and
Q_{1}^{(t)},\dots,Q_{r(t)}^{(t)}
are those
P_{i}^{(t)}
of size at least two, then
|B_{t}|\geqslant r(t)
. Hence, our assumption implies that

(41)

r(t)\leqslant L,

for all
t\neq 0
. Clearly, if
a\in A\cap(A+t)
, then
a,a-t\in A
and hence
a
lies in one of the progressions
Q_{i}^{(t)}
of size at least
2
. This shows that for every
t\neq 0
we have that

(42)

\displaystyle A\cap(A+t)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{r(t)}Q_{i}^{(t)}.

Claim 7.4
.

Let
t\neq 0
, and let
M\in\mathbf{N}
be a parameter. Then
|A\cap(A+jt)|\geqslant|A\cap(A+t)|-ML
for every
j\in\{1,2,\dots,M\}
.

Proof of Claim
7.4
.

Consider the
r(t)
many progressions
Q_{i}^{(t)}
with common difference
t
that are contained in
A
. For each member
x
of a progression
Q_{i}^{(t)}
which is not one of the first
M
terms of
Q_{i}^{(t)}
, it is clear that
x,x-jt\in Q_{i}^{(t)}\subset A
whenever
j\in[M]
. Hence, for every
j\in[M]
, each
Q_{i}^{(t)}
contains at least
|Q_{i}^{(t)}|-M
elements of
A\cap(A+jt)
(note that this trivially holds for those
Q_{i}^{(t)}
of size
|Q_{i}^{(t)}|\leqslant M
). Thus, in total, we can bound

\displaystyle|A\cap(A+jt)|

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{r(t)}(|Q_{i}^{(t)}|-M)=\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^{r(t)}Q_{i}^{(t)}\right|-Mr(t)\geqslant|A\cap(A+t)|-ML,

using (
41
) and (
42
).
∎

By Roth’s Lemma
4.1
, we see that
\sum_{t\in A}|A\cap(A+t)|=\#\{(a,t)\in A^{2}:a-t\in A\}\geqslant n^{2}/(2K)
, so we may find a
t_{0}\neq 0
such that
k:=|A\cap(A+t_{0})|\geqslant n/(2K)
.

Claim 7.5
.

Let
M\in\mathbf{N}
be a parameter. For any nonnegative integers
\alpha_{p}
, indexed by the primes
p\in[M]
, which satisfy
\sum_{p\leqslant M}\alpha_{p}\leqslant k/(2ML)
, we have that
|A\cap(A+(\prod_{p\leqslant M}p^{\alpha_{p}})t_{0})|\geqslant 1
.

Proof of Claim
7.5
.

It suffices to show that whenever
\ell
is an arbitrary nonnegative integer and
\sum_{p\leqslant M}\alpha_{p}\leqslant\ell
, then

(43)

\displaystyle|A\cap(A+(\prod_{p\leqslant M}p^{\alpha_{p}})t_{0})|\geqslant k-ML\ell.

We prove this by induction on
\ell
. The base case where
\ell=0
holds as
|A\cap(A+t_{0})|=k
by definition. Now suppose that (
43
) holds whenever
\sum_{p\leqslant M}\alpha_{p}\leqslant\ell
. Then an application of Claim
7.4
with
t=(\prod_{p\leqslant M}p^{\alpha_{p}})t_{0}
and
j=2
gives the desired lower bound

|A\cap(A+(2^{\alpha_{2}+1}\prod_{2<p\leqslant M}p^{\alpha_{p}})t_{0})|\geqslant|A\cap(A+(\prod_{p\leqslant M}p^{\alpha_{p}})t_{0})|-ML\geqslant k-ML(\ell+1),

where the final inequality uses the induction hypothesis (
43
). A completely analogous application of Claim
7.4
with
j=p
instead produces the same bound if we increase any other
\alpha_{p}
by
1
.

∎

Claim
7.5
shows that
A\cap\big(A+(\prod_{p\leqslant M}p^{\alpha_{p}})t_{0}\big)\neq\emptyset
whenever
\sum_{p\leqslant M}\alpha_{p}\leqslant k/(2ML)
, so certainly all the integers
(\prod_{p\leqslant M}p^{\alpha_{p}})t_{0}
with
\max_{p}\alpha_{p}\leqslant k/(2M^{2}L)
must lie in
A-A
. This implies that

n^{2}\geqslant|A-A|\geqslant\left(\frac{k}{2M^{2}L}\right)^{\pi(M)}\geqslant\left(\frac{n}{4M^{2}LK}\right)^{\pi(M)},

where
\pi(M)
denotes the number of primes up to
M
, by using that
k=|A\cap(A+t_{0})|\geqslant n/(2K)
. Rearranging shows that

L\geqslant\frac{n^{1-2/\pi(M)}}{4M^{2}K},

and we may now choose the parameter
M\approx(\log n)^{2}
(this is only slightly suboptimal) to obtain the desired result that
L\gg n/(K(\log n)^{4})
.

∎

As we may find a
t\neq 0
such that
|B_{t}|\gg n/(K(\log n)^{4})\geqslant n^{1-o(1)}/K
by Lemma
7.3
, (
40
) shows that
K\gg n^{1/7-o(1)}
, completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

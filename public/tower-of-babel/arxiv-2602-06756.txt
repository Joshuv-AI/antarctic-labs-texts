f f -Differential Privacy Filters: Validity and Approximate Solutions
Long Tran, Antti Koskela, Ossi Räisä, Antti Honkela

Abstract
Accounting for privacy loss under fully adaptive composition—where both the choice of mechanisms and their privacy parameters may depend on the entire history of prior outputs—is a central challenge in differential privacy (DP). In this setting, privacy filters are stopping rules for compositions that ensure a prescribed global privacy budget is not exceeded. It remains unclear whether optimal trade-off-function-based notions, such as f f -DP, admit valid privacy filters under fully adaptive interaction. We show that the natural approach to defining an f f -DP filter — composing individual trade-off curves and stopping when the prescribed f f -DP curve is crossed — is fundamentally invalid. We characterise when and why this failure occurs, and establish necessary and sufficient conditions under which the natural filter is valid. Furthermore, we prove a fully adaptive central limit theorem for f f -DP and construct an approximate Gaussian DP filter for subsampled Gaussian mechanisms at small sampling rates q < 0.2 q<0.2 and large sampling rates q > 0.8 q>0.8 , yielding tighter privacy guarantees than filters based on Rényi DP in the same setting.



1
Introduction

Differential privacy (DP) provides rigorous guarantees on the privacy loss incurred by computations using sensitive data
(
Dwork et al. 2006b
;
Dwork & Roth 2014
)
. A key property of DP is composition: complex algorithms can be analyzed by
composing the privacy guarantees of simpler mechanisms. Common composition results of DP require knowing the privacy parameters of the mechanisms beforehand. However, in many applications, it is helpful to have fully adaptive compositions where both the choice of mechanisms and their privacy parameters may depend on the entire history of previous outputs.
Accounting for the privacy loss in this setting is significant for iterative and interactive algorithms such as differentially private stochastic gradient descent and adaptive data analysis.

Fully adaptive composition for approximate
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP was formalised through the notions of privacy filters and privacy odometers
(
Rogers et al. 2016
)
. Subsequent work showed that fully adaptive composition can be achieved with essentially no loss in tightness compared to advanced composition
(
Whitehouse et al. 2023
)
. In particular, Rényi DP (RDP)
(
Mironov 2017
)
admits simple and accurate privacy filters under full adaptivity
(
Feldman & Zrnic 2021
)
, making RDP-based fully adaptive accounting the best approach in practice.

In contrast, much less is understood about fully adaptive composition for the trade-off-function-based notion of privacy,
f
-DP
(
Dong et al. 2022
)
. Only in the special case of Gaussian DP (GDP) can accurate fully adaptive DP bounds be obtained
(
Koskela et al. 2023
)
. Using
f
-DP gives the tightest composition results for non-adaptive compositions. This raises a natural question:

Does
f
-DP admit valid privacy filters under fully adaptive composition, in analogy to RDP and GDP?

A natural candidate filter composes the individual trade-off functions of each mechanism and halts once the composed trade-off function crosses a prescribed
f
-DP budget, analogously to the case of fully adaptive RDP and GDP accounting, and also mirrors non-adaptive
f
-DP composition. Our first main result shows that this natural approach is fundamentally
invalid.

Failure of natural
f
-DP filters.
We show that composing trade-off functions and stopping at a threshold does not, in general, guarantee that the resulting stopped interaction satisfies the same
f
-DP bound. This failure occurs even when each individual mechanism is validly
f
-DP and even when the composed trade-off function never exceeds the threshold along the realised history of outcomes. We give explicit negative examples and a general construction that explains when
and why such failures arise. Moreover, we show that this phenomenon occurs for fully adaptive compositions of subsampled Gaussian mechanisms, the main workhorse of private machine learning.

When
f
-DP filters are valid.
While the natural
f
-DP filter fails in general, we show that it becomes valid
under specific structural conditions on the trade-off functions. We provide necessary and sufficient conditions under which stopping based
on composed trade-off curves yields a correct
f
-DP guarantee. These conditions clarify why divergence-based notions such as RDP admit simple filters under full adaptivity, while trade-off-function-based notions do not.

Approximate GDP filters via a central limit theorem.
As a positive result, we develop an approximate GDP filter for fully adaptive compositions of subsampled Gaussian mechanisms. Our analysis is based on a central limit theorem (CLT) for adaptive privacy loss processes, extending the non-fully adaptive CLT-based result of
Dong et al. 2022
to the fully adaptive setting. The proof relies on a modern variant of the martingale Berry–Esseen bound
(
Fan 2019
)
adapted to handle adaptive interactions and privacy parameters. Combining this result with asymptotic approximations of moments of privacy loss random variables (PLRVs), we construct an approximate GDP filter tailored for fully adaptive compositions of subsampled Gaussian mechanisms at small or large sampling rates. This setting is particularly relevant for DP stochastic gradient descent (DP-SGD), the main tool of DP machine learning. We show that this filter achieves strictly tighter privacy guarantees for DP-SGD than the fully adaptive RDP accounting of
Feldman & Zrnic 2021
in these sampling rate regimes, while remaining simple to implement numerically.

We provide explicit counterexamples and a general construction of this failure. We show that this failure occurs even for fully adaptive compositions of subsampled Gaussian mechanisms. (Example
3.3
and Appendix

)

2.

We establish necessary and sufficient conditions under which the natural
f
-DP filter becomes valid, characterising when trade-off-function-based accounting is possible under full adaptivity. (Theorems

and

)

3.

We prove a central limit theorem for fully adaptive privacy loss stochastic processes using martingale Berry–Esseen techniques. (Theorem

\displaystyle T\!\left[P,Q\right]\!\left(\alpha\right)=\inf_{\phi}\left\{1-\mathbb{E}_{Q}\!\left[\phi\right]:\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\phi\right]\leq\alpha\right\},

(

A function

An everywhere smaller trade-off function means a uniformly easier testing problem, and hence weaker privacy protection.

Definition 2.6

Definition 2.7

The privacy loss random variables (PLRVs) are deeply connected to all definitions of DP.

Definition 2.9
.

For a pair of distributions
\left(P,Q\right)
on a common space
\mathcal{O}
, denote
L=\ln\frac{P}{Q}
. The pair of PLRVs is defined as
L\!\left(y\right)

y\sim P
and
L\!\left(y\right)

Both PLRVs are defined to share the form

\displaystyle\ln\frac{\mathcal{M}_{1:T}\!\left(S\right)}{\mathcal{M}_{1:T}\!\left(S^{-}\right)}=\ln\frac{\prod\mathcal{M}_{t}\!\left(S\right)}{\prod\mathcal{M}_{t}\!\left(S^{-}\right)}=\sum\ln\!\frac{\mathcal{M}_{t}\!\left(S\right)}{\mathcal{M}_{t}\!\left(S^{-}\right)},

indicating convergence to Gaussian distributions as a consequence of the central limit theorem.

2.2
Subsampled Gaussian Mechanism

The subsampled Gaussian mechanism samples a subset from the dataset and adds Gaussian noise scaled by
\sigma
to the output. This design is preferable because subsampling provides significant privacy amplification, reducing the noise required to achieve the same privacy level compared to operating on the full dataset
(
Balle et al. 2020
)
. Specifically, our main results apply to the Poisson subsampling scheme with
a rate
q
. We define the fundamental subsampled Gaussian trade-off function for remove adjacency as
SG_{q,\mu}^{-}=\left(SG_{q,\mu}^{+}\right)^{-1}
where

\displaystyle SG_{q,\mu}^{+}\!\left(\alpha\right)

\displaystyle=T\!\left[\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,1\right)\!,\left(1-q\right)\!\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,1\right)+q\mathcal{N}\!\left(\mu,1\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\left(1-q\right)\!\left(1-\alpha\right)+qG_{\mu}\!\left(\alpha\right),

as shown by
Zhu et al. 2022
. DP-SGD
(
Abadi et al. 2016
)
is a composition of subsampled Gaussian mechanisms applied iteratively throughout the training process. Each step involves subsampling, computing and clipping gradients, adding Gaussian noise, and updating model parameters.

2.3
Fully Adaptive Composition

Fully adaptive composition extends standard composition by allowing not only the next mechanism, but also its privacy parameters to be chosen adaptively. For example, in DP-SGD, analysts may wish to change the subsampling rate
q
or the noise parameter
\sigma
based on the behaviour and the privacy leakage of the run so far.

In this setting, privacy parameters become random variables dependent on the stochastic outcomes of previous mechanisms, requiring path-dependent privacy accounting methods where, for example, the composition length itself may be random. To manage this complexity,
Rogers et al. 2016
introduced two conceptual tools. Privacy filters are adaptive stopping conditions that halt mechanism releases to ensure pre-specified privacy guarantees, while privacy odometers track cumulative privacy loss from ongoing compositions without pre-specified privacy budgets.

Privacy odometers, while more intuitive, present significant theoretical difficulties because they must ensure valid privacy bounds at any stopping time chosen by the analyst, even as privacy losses accumulate along arbitrary paths. In this work, we focus on privacy filters (Algorithm
1
) that maintain fixed privacy guarantees at the cost of random stopping times.

3.2
Valid
f
-DP Filter for Blackwell Chains

We have shown that there exists a setting in which the
f
-DP
filter becomes invalid. However, when the budget is unbounded (
f_{B}=0
), so that the filter never halts, any composition produced is
f_{B}
-DP, making the filter valid. This implies that there is a spectrum of settings in which the
f
-DP filter transitions from valid to invalid.

In this subsection, we identify the necessary and sufficient assumptions under which the
f
-DP filter is valid. To this end, we introduce the definition of Blackwell chain, to combine with convergent sequences of trade-off functions.

Definition 3.4
.

A family
\mathcal{F}
of trade-off functions is a Blackwell chain if
\mathcal{F}
is totally ordered with respect to the Blackwell order:
f_{1}\geq f_{2}
or
f_{1}\leq f_{2}
for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
.

One significant example of a Blackwell chain is the family of Gaussian trade-off functions: If
\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}
, then
G_{\mu_{1}}\geq G_{\mu_{2}}
.

The convergence of trade-off functions can be defined using their corresponding metric, the symmetrised
\Delta
-divergence
(
Kaissis et al. 2024
)
:
f_{n}
converges to
f
in
\Delta
-divergence if

\displaystyle\min\!\left\{\Delta\geq 0:f_{n}\!\left(\alpha+\Delta\right)-\Delta\leq f\!\left(\alpha\right)\ \forall\alpha\right\}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

Definition 3.5
.

For a family
\mathcal{F}
of trade-off functions, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\rule{4.0pt}{0.4pt}\otimes}=\left\{f\otimes g:f\in\mathcal{F},\ \exists f_{n}\in\mathcal{F}\text{ s.t.}\,f_{n}\searrow^{\Delta}g\right\}

as the family of trade-off functions obtained by taking the tensor product between any limit
g
in
\Delta
-divergence of some everywhere decreasing sequence
f_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
, with any individual
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

We are now ready to state our main validity result, which relies on iteratively applying the operation defined above.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the family of tight
f
-DP guarantees of mechanisms available for adaptive selection at each step. The natural
f
-DP filter is valid in fully adaptive composition of up to
T\geq 2
steps if
\mathcal{F}^{\left(\rule{4.0pt}{0.4pt}\otimes\right)\left(T-2\right)}
is a Blackwell chain.

The main idea behind the proof, presented in
Appendix
D
, is that, under the Blackwell chain assumption, the limit operation followed by the tensor product, repeated
T-2
times, enables us to transform the filter inequality
f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{t}\geq f_{B}
into the
f_{B}
-DP guarantee of the composition.

Since the filter can halt early and any mechanism afterwards releases nothing, we assume that
\textup{Id}\in\mathcal{F}
, where
\textup{Id}\!\left(\alpha\right)=1-\alpha
is the perfectly private trade-off function. Since
f\otimes\textup{Id}=f
, it follows that
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{F}^{\rule{4.0pt}{0.4pt}\otimes}
. Consequently, if
\mathcal{F}^{\left(\rule{4.0pt}{0.4pt}\otimes\right)\left(T-2\right)}
is a Blackwell chain, then so is
\mathcal{F}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{G}_{A}=\left\{G_{\mu}:\mu\in A\right\}

\mu
ranging over an interval
A\subset\left[0,\infty\right]
. Then for any composition length
T
, we have
\mathcal{G}_{\left[0,\infty\right]}^{\left(\rule{4.0pt}{0.4pt}\otimes\right)\left(T-2\right)}=\mathcal{G}_{\left[0,\infty\right]}
, which is a Blackwell chain. Similarly,
\mathcal{G}_{\left[0,1\right]}^{\left(\rule{4.0pt}{0.4pt}\otimes\right)\left(T-2\right)}=\mathcal{G}_{\left[0,\sqrt{T-1}\right]}
is another Blackwell chain.

By Theorem
3.6
, it follows that the
f
-DP filter is valid for Gaussian trade-off functions; in particular, it is valid for DP-GD with bounded norm clipping. In fact, this result recovers the GDP filter in Theorem
2.11
via an equivalent GDP budget
\mu_{B}=\max\!\left\{\mu\geq 0:G_{\mu}\geq f_{B}\right\}
. Gaussian trade-off functions are closed under the tensor product, making Theorem
3.6
particularly well-suited to the Gaussian setting.

For a fixed
q
, although the family of subsampled Gaussian trade-off functions
\mathcal{SG}^{-}_{q,\left[0,\infty\right]}=\left\{SG^{-}_{q,\mu}:\mu\in\left[0,\infty\right]\right\}
is a Blackwell chain, Example
3.3
shows that the extension
\left(\mathcal{SG}^{-}_{q,\left[0,\infty\right]}\right)^{\rule{4.0pt}{0.4pt}\otimes}
is not (see Figure
1a
and
1b
).

In fact, when the Blackwell chain property fails, there exists an incomparable pair of trade-off functions in
\mathcal{F}^{\left(\rule{4.0pt}{0.4pt}\otimes\right)\left(T-2\right)}
. This pair exhibits a calibration point and can be exploited to craft a counterexample to filter validity.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the family of tight
f
-DP guarantees of mechanisms available for adaptive selection at each step. In fully adaptive composition of up to
T
steps, if:

1.

Some mechanism at step 1 is not
\varepsilon
-DP for any
\varepsilon

2.

\mathcal{F}^{\left(\rule{4.0pt}{0.4pt}\otimes\right)\left(T-2\right)}
fails to be a Blackwell chain,

then the
f
-DP filter is invalid.

Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.8
together yield a sharp characterisation:

Beyond
\varepsilon
-DP mechanisms, the
f
-DP filter is valid

if and only if
\mathcal{F}^{\left(\rule{4.0pt}{0.4pt}\otimes\right)\left(T-2\right)}

4.1
PLRV Moment Approximations

Despite the invalidity of the
f
-DP filter for subsampled Gaussian mechanisms, we show in this section a valid regime-specific privacy filter exists for subsampling rate
q\ll 1
and
q\approx 1
that guarantees tighter bounds than the existing and widely-used RDP filter
(
Feldman & Zrnic 2021
)
.

To motivate this construction, we first derive asymptotic approximations to the means and variances of PLRVs in subsampled Gaussian mechanisms. Our key observation is that both mean-to-variance ratios are approximately

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\ln\frac{P}{Q}\right]}{\textup{Var}_{P}\!\left[\ln\frac{P}{Q}\right]}\approx\frac{-\mathbb{E}_{Q}\!\left[\ln\frac{P}{Q}\right]}{\textup{Var}_{Q}\!\left[\ln\frac{P}{Q}\right]}\approx\frac{1}{2},

where
P=\mathcal{M}\!\left(S\right)
and
Q=\mathcal{M}\!\left(S^{-}\right)
for an arbitrary remove pair
S,S^{-}
. Intuitively, when
q\approx 1
, the PLRV distributions approximate those of the Gaussian mechanism, which have mean-to-variance ratios of exactly
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
. When
q\ll 1
, since the means are very small, we can approximate the variances by the second moments. Denoting
L=\ln\nicefrac{{P}}{{Q}}
and using the Taylor expansions
\mathrm{e}^{y}\approx 1+y+\frac{y^{2}}{2}
and
\mathrm{e}^{-y}\approx 1-y+\frac{y^{2}}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle 1=\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\frac{Q}{P}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\mathrm{e}^{-L}\right]

\displaystyle\approx 1-\mathbb{E}_{P}L+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{P}L^{2},

\displaystyle 1=\mathbb{E}_{Q}\!\left[\frac{P}{Q}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{Q}\!\left[\mathrm{e}^{L}\right]

\displaystyle\approx 1+\mathbb{E}_{Q}L+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{Q}L^{2}.

1.

Assume that

(a)

(b)

4.2
Fully Adaptive Central Limit Theorem

We present a fully adaptive extension of the
f
-DP CLT
(
Dong et al. 2022
)
, treating privacy losses as a stochastic process.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{T}
and
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{T}
be adaptive sequences of distributions, meaning that at every step

P_{t}
(or

(or

,
Q=\prod Q_{t}
,

1.

\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]-m_{1}\right|\leq\eta_{1}

2.

\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim Q}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]+m_{2}\right|\leq\eta_{2}

3.

\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\textup{Var}_{y\sim P}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]-v\right|\leq\kappa

4.

\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\textup{Var}_{y\sim Q}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]-v\right|\leq\kappa

5.

\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P}\!\left[\left|L_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]\right|^{3}\,\middle|\,y_{1:t-1}\right]\leq\rho\times\textup{Var}_{y\sim P}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]
for all
t

6.

\mathbb{E}_{y\sim Q}\!\left[\left|L_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{y\sim Q}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]\right|^{3}\,\middle|\,y_{1:t-1}\right]\leq\rho\times\textup{Var}_{y\sim Q}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]
for all
t

where
4\rho^{2}\leq v
and
4\kappa\leq v
. Then the trade-off function
T\!\left[P,Q\right]
satisfies, for all
\alpha
,

\displaystyle G_{\mu+\phi}\!\left(\alpha+\Delta\right)-\Delta\leq T\!\left[P,Q\right]\!\left(\alpha\right)\leq G_{\mu-\phi}\!\left(\alpha-\Delta\right)+\Delta,

where
\mu=\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}
,
\phi=\frac{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}

\Delta=C\!\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{v}}\!\left|\ln\!\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{v}}\right|+\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{v}}\right)
.

In Theorem

Fan 2019

Dong et al. 2022

5
Discussion and Conclusions

This work resolves a central open question about fully adaptive privacy accounting for trade-off-function-based notions of differential privacy. We show that tight fully adaptive
f
-DP accounting is fundamentally impossible in the same sense as for fully adaptive GDP and RDP: the natural filter based on composing trade-off functions and stopping at a threshold cannot be valid in general. Our negative results explain why this invalidity occurs and identify the conditions under which it disappears. At the same time, by establishing a central limit theorem for adaptive privacy loss processes and leveraging it to construct approximate GDP filters tailored to DP-SGD iterations, we show that fully adaptive accounting can strictly improve over fully adaptive RDP accounting. These results also indicate a path forward: further gains over RDP accounting are possible using mechanism-specific approximations that exploit additional structure.

Impact Statement

This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the field of Machine Learning. There are many potential societal consequences of our work, none which we feel must be specifically highlighted here.

A.1
Privacy Profile vs. Trade-off Function

The hockey-stick divergence is an integral that directly captures
\left(\varepsilon,\delta\right)
-DP bounds. Define
\left[x\right]_{+}=\max\!\left(x,0\right)

(
(

)

The hockey-stick divergence at order

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}\!\left[P\!\mid\mid\!Q\right]=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\left[p\!\left(y\right)-\gamma q\!\left(y\right)\right]_{+}\mathrm{d}y=\max_{A\subset\mathcal{O}}P\!\left(A\right)-\gamma Q\!\left(A\right).

A mechanism
\mathcal{M}
is

D_{e^{\varepsilon}}\!\left[\mathcal{M}\!\left(S\right)\!\mid\mid\!\mathcal{M}\!\left(S^{-}\right)\right]\leq\delta

The privacy profile on a mechanism level defined by

is given as Definition

.

(
(

)
)

We can describe the spectrum of all
\left(\varepsilon,\delta\right)
-DP bounds using privacy profiles.

(

A.2

Zhu et al. 2022

Theorem A.11
.

(

This motivates the definition of the tensor product of privacy profiles, or equivalently, trade-off functions.

Definition A.12

(
(
Gopi et al. 2021
)
)
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be privacy profiles. The tensor product
H_{1}\otimes H_{2}
is defined as

\displaystyle\left(H_{1}\otimes H_{2}\right)\!\left(\gamma\right)=D_{\gamma}\!\left[P_{1}\times P_{2}\!\mid\mid\!Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\right],

where
\left(P_{1},Q_{1}\right)
is any pair of distributions that has a privacy profile of
H_{1}
, and
\left(P_{2},Q_{2}\right)
is any pair of distributions that has a privacy profile of
H_{2}
. Using Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\left(H_{1}\otimes H_{2}\right)\!\left(\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\times\mathcal{O}_{2}}\left[p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)p_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)-\gamma q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)q_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)\right]_{+}\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}H_{2}\!\left(\gamma\frac{q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)}{p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)}\right)p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1}.

Proposition A.13
.

The tensor product of privacy profiles satisfies all following properties.

0.

It is well-defined.

1.

It is commutative and associative.

2.

It carries over the Blackwell ordering: If
H_{21}\geq H_{22}
, then
H_{1}\otimes H_{21}\geq H_{1}\otimes H_{22}
.

3.

H\otimes H_{\textup{Id}}=H_{\textup{Id}}\otimes H=H
, where
H_{\textup{Id}}\!\left(\gamma\right)=\left[1-\gamma\right]_{+}
is the perfectly private privacy profile.

4.

\widehat{\left(H_{1}\otimes H_{2}\right)}=\hat{H}_{1}\otimes\hat{H}_{2}
.

Proof.

Proof for 0
. We need to show that the resulting privacy profile
H_{1}\otimes H_{2}
is independent of the distribution pair
\left(P_{1},Q_{1}\right)
and
\left(P_{2},Q_{2}\right)
behind
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
. It is sufficient to show that property
2
holds: By property
2
,
D_{\gamma}\!\left[P_{21},Q_{21}\right]=D_{\gamma}\!\left[P_{22},Q_{22}\right]
for all
\gamma
implies that
D_{\gamma}\!\left[P_{1}\times P_{21},Q_{1}\times Q_{21}\right]=D_{\gamma}\!\left[P_{1}\times P_{22},Q_{1}\times Q_{22}\right]
for all
\gamma
.

Proof for 2
. Using Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\left(H_{1}\otimes H_{21}\right)\!\left(\gamma\right)=\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}H_{21}\!\left(\gamma\frac{q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)}{p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)}\right)p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1}\geq\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}H_{22}\!\left(\gamma\frac{q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)}{p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)}\right)p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1}=\left(H_{1}\otimes H_{22}\right)\!\left(\gamma\right).

Proof for 1
.
H_{1}\otimes H_{2}=H_{2}\otimes H_{1}
follows from
p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)p_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)=p_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)
and
q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)q_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)=q_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)
.

\left(H_{1}\otimes H_{2}\right)\otimes H_{3}=H_{1}\otimes\left(H_{2}\otimes H_{3}\right)
follows from the Fubini’s theorem combined with the fact that
p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)p_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)p_{3}\!\left(y_{3}\right)=p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)\!\left[p_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)p_{3}\!\left(y_{3}\right)\right]
and
q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)q_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)q_{3}\!\left(y_{3}\right)=q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)\!\left[q_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)q_{3}\!\left(y_{3}\right)\right]
.

Proof for 3
.
H_{\textup{Id}}
is the privacy profile of any pair of identical distributions
\left(P_{2},P_{2}\right)
. Therefore

\displaystyle\left(H_{1}\otimes H_{2}\right)\!\left(\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\times\mathcal{O}_{2}}\left[p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)p_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)-\gamma q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)p_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)\right]_{+}\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\times\mathcal{O}_{2}}\left[p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)-\gamma q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)\right]_{+}p_{2}\!\left(y_{2}\right)\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}=\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}\left[p_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)-\gamma q_{1}\!\left(y_{1}\right)\right]_{+}\mathrm{d}y_{1}=H_{1}\!\left(\gamma\right).

Proof for 4
. By the definition of tensor product and Proposition
A.8
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\left(H_{1}\otimes H_{2}\right)}\!\left(\gamma\right)=D_{\gamma}\!\left[Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\!\mid\mid\!P_{1}\times P_{2}\right]=\left(\hat{H}_{1}\otimes\hat{H}_{2}\right)\!\left(\gamma\right).

∎

Properties
2
and
3
from Proposition

, i.e. tensor product weakens privacy protections. The following Theorem

Why the

-DP filter fails in general
. At any step

, the tensor products

\gamma

Appendix C
Convergence of Privacy Profiles/Trade-off Functions

C.1
Existence of Convergent Sequence in Blackwell Chains

We can impose the structural assumption of Blackwell chain on privacy profiles, in a similar way to trade-off functions.

Definition C.1
.

A family
\mathcal{H}
of privacy profiles is a Blackwell chain if any pair
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
is Blackwell ordered, i.e.
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
or
H_{1}\geq H_{2}
.

By Theorem
A.7
,
\mathcal{H}
is a Blackwell chain if and only if its corresponding family
\mathcal{F}
of trade-off functions is a Blackwell chain.

Lemma C.2
.

In a Blackwell chain
\mathcal{H}
of privacy profiles with the point-wise supremum
H^{\uparrow}
, there exists an everywhere increasing sequence
\left(H_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\frac{i}{2^{n}}\right)-\frac{1}{n}<H_{n}\!\left(\frac{i}{2^{n}}\right)\leq H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\frac{i}{2^{n}}\right)\,\text{for }i=1,2,\ldots,n2^{n}.

(1)

Lemma
C.2
enables inequalities at finitely many points covering regions
\left[0,n\right]
of increasing sizes
n
and increasing
resolutions
\frac{1}{2^{n}}
. As
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left[0,n\right]=\left[0,\infty\right)
and
\frac{1}{2^{n}}\searrow 0
, these inequalities serve to connect point-wise properties to set-wise properties.

Proof.

Since
H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\gamma\right)=\sup_{H\in\mathcal{H}}H\!\left(\gamma\right)
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n2^{n}
, there exists
H_{ni}\in\mathcal{H}
such that at
\gamma=\frac{i}{2^{n}}
,

\displaystyle H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\frac{i}{2^{n}}\right)-\frac{1}{n}<H_{ni}\!\left(\frac{i}{2^{n}}\right)\leq H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\frac{i}{2^{n}}\right).

Let
\hat{H}_{n}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n2^{n}}H_{ni}
, then
\hat{H}_{n}
satisfies the required inequality (
1
), and
\hat{H}_{n}
is in
\mathcal{H}
since
\mathcal{H}
is a Blackwell chain. Finally, since
\left(\frac{i}{2^{n}}\right)_{i=1}^{n2^{n}}\subset\left(\frac{i}{2^{n+1}}\right)_{i=1}^{\left(n+1\right)2^{n+1}}
, we choose
H_{n}=\max_{k\leq n}\hat{H}_{k}
so that
\left(H_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is increasing while maintaining inequality (
1
).
∎

Lemma C.3
.

In a Blackwell chain of privacy profiles
\mathcal{H}
with a smallest upper bound
H^{\uparrow}=\sup_{H\in\mathcal{H}}H
, there exists an everywhere increasing sequence
\left(H_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
H_{n}\nearrow_{n\to\infty}H^{\uparrow}
point-wise.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
C.2
to find an everywhere increasing sequence
H_{n}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for
i=1,2,\ldots,n2^{n}
,

\displaystyle H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\frac{i}{2^{n}}\right)-\frac{1}{n}<H_{n}\!\left(\frac{i}{2^{n}}\right)\leq H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\frac{i}{2^{n}}\right).

We show that this sequence converges point-wise to
H^{\uparrow}
. Fix
\gamma>0
, then
n\geq\gamma
once
n
is big enough, say,
n\geq N
. If
\gamma
has a finite binary representation, then once
n
is big enough,

\displaystyle H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\gamma\right)\geq H_{n}\!\left(\gamma\right)>H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\gamma\right)-\frac{1}{n}\nearrow_{n\to\infty}H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\gamma\right).

Otherwise if
\gamma
has an infinite binary representation, let
\left(\gamma_{n}\right)_{n\geq N}
be a decreasing sequence that converges to
\gamma
, where each
\gamma_{n}
has a finite binary representation and
H_{n}\!\left(\gamma_{n}\right)>H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\gamma_{n}\right)-\frac{1}{n}
. Then

\displaystyle H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\gamma\right)\geq H_{n}\!\left(\gamma\right)\geq H_{n}\!\left(x_{n}\right)>H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\gamma_{n}\right)-\frac{1}{n}\nearrow_{n\to\infty}H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\gamma\right).

Here, we used the fact that
H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\gamma_{n}\right)\nearrow_{n\to\infty}H^{\uparrow}\!\left(\gamma\right)
, which is due to the continuity of
H^{\uparrow}
.
∎

C.2
Unifying Various Notions of Convergence

In addition to the point-wise convergence shown in Lemma
C.3
, we discuss other notions of convergence.

1.

Uniform convergence
\sup_{\gamma\geq 0}\left|H_{n}\!\left(\gamma\right)-H\!\left(\gamma\right)\right|\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
, which is much stronger than point-wise convergence.

2.

Uniform convergence over any compact set: for all
\gamma_{\max}>0
,
\sup_{\gamma\in\left[0,\gamma_{\max}\right]}\left|H_{n}\!\left(\gamma\right)-H\!\left(\gamma\right)\right|\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
.

3.

Convergence in the symmetrised
\Delta
-divergence introduced by
Kaissis et al. 2024
:

\displaystyle\sup_{\gamma\geq 0}\frac{\left|H_{n}\!\left(\gamma\right)-H\!\left(\gamma\right)\right|}{1+\gamma}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0

Proposition C.4
.

Uniform convergence implies convergence in
\Delta
-divergence.

Proof.

The
\Delta
-divergence is smaller or equal to the
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
norm,

\displaystyle\sup_{\gamma\geq 0}\frac{\left|H\!\left(\gamma\right)-H_{n}\!\left(\gamma\right)\right|}{1+\gamma}\leq\sup_{\gamma\geq 0}\left|H\!\left(\gamma\right)-H_{n}\!\left(\gamma\right)\right|.

∎

The following example shows that the implication in Proposition
C.4
is strictly one-sided.

Example C.5
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle H_{n}\!\left(\gamma\right)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{\gamma}{n}&\text{if }\gamma\leq n,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
H_{n}
converges to
H=1
point-wise but not uniformly. A closer examination reveals that for all
n
,
\frac{H\left(\gamma\right)-H_{n}\left(\gamma\right)}{1+\gamma}
reaches its maximum at
\gamma=n
. Therefore

\displaystyle\max_{\gamma\geq 0}\frac{H\!\left(\gamma\right)-H_{n}\!\left(\gamma\right)}{1+\gamma}=\frac{1}{1+n}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0,

i.e.
H_{n}
converges to
H
in
\Delta
-divergence.

Theorem E.1

(
(
Fan 2019
)
)
.

Let
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{G},P\right)
be a probability space and let
\mathcal{G}_{1}\subset\mathcal{G}_{2}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{G}_{T}\subset\mathcal{G}
be a filtration of increasing
\sigma

\left(\xi_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{T}
be a sequence of martingale differences, which means that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\xi_{i}
for
t=1,\ldots,T
forms a martingale, or equivalently
\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\xi_{i}\!\mid\!\mathcal{G}_{i-1}\right]=0
for all
i
. Assume that the following holds.

1.

There exists
\kappa\in\left[0,\frac{1}{4}\right]
such that
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\xi_{i}^{2}\!\mid\!\mathcal{G}_{i-1}\right]-1\right|\leq\kappa

2.

There exists
\rho\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
such that

where

is the cdf of the standard Gaussian distribution, and

is a universal constant.

Note that as
T
grows,
v
increases linearly while
\rho
remains constant, indicating a convergence rate of

.
Fan 2019
showed that for martingale difference sequences satisfying the conditions in Theorem

is optimal. To the best of our knowledge, this represents the best convergence rate among existing martingale Berry–Esseen variants, though it is weaker than the classical
O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\right)
rate for i.i.d. sequences due to the martingale setting. Additional variants of the martingale Berry–Esseen theorem were derived by
Heyde & Brown 1970
and
Fan & Shao 2017
.
See
4.2

Proof.

We apply Theorem
E.1
to the sequences of martingale differences

\displaystyle\frac{L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)-\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]}{\sqrt{v}},y\sim P\quad\textup{and}\quad\frac{L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)-\mathbb{E}_{y\sim Q}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]}{\sqrt{v}},y\sim Q

for

to arrive at the inequalities

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\real}\left|P\!\left[\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\leq x\right]-\Phi\!\left(x\right)\right|

L\!\left(y\right)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)
and
\Delta=C\!\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{v}}\!\left|\ln\!\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{v}}\right|+\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{v}}\right)
. By Conditions 1 and 2, the sums of conditional means are almost surely bounded,

\displaystyle m_{1}-\eta_{1}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq m_{1}+\eta_{1},

\displaystyle-m_{2}-\eta_{2}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim Q}\!\left[L_{t}\!\left(y_{t}\right)\!\mid\!y_{1:t-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq-m_{2}+\eta_{2}.

Therefore for all
x\in\real
,

\displaystyle P\!\left(\frac{L\!\left(y\right)-m_{1}+\eta_{1}}{\sqrt{v}}\leq x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi\!\left(x\right)+\Delta,

(2)

\displaystyle P\!\left(\frac{L\!\left(y\right)-m_{1}-\eta_{1}}{\sqrt{v}}\leq x\right)

\displaystyle\geq\Phi\!\left(x\right)-\Delta,

(3)

\displaystyle Q\!\left(\frac{L\!\left(y\right)+m_{2}+\eta_{1}}{\sqrt{v}}\leq x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi\!\left(x\right)+\Delta,

(4)

\displaystyle Q\!\left(\frac{L\!\left(y\right)+m_{2}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}\leq x\right)

\displaystyle\geq\Phi\!\left(x\right)-\Delta.

(5)

Now, we convert these inequalities to their trade-off function form. Let
\alpha\in\left(0,1-\Delta\right)
and let
x\in\real
such that the test

\displaystyle\phi\!\left(y\right)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }L\!\left(y\right)\leq x,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for the hypothesis testing problem
H_{0}:y\sim P
vs.
H_{1}:y\sim Q
has an FPR
P\!\left(L\!\left(y\right)\leq x\right)=\alpha
. By (
3
),

\displaystyle\Phi\!\left(\frac{x-m_{1}-\eta_{1}}{\sqrt{v}}\right)\leq P\!\left(\frac{L\!\left(y\right)-m_{1}-\eta_{1}}{\sqrt{v}}\leq\frac{x-m_{1}-\eta_{1}}{\sqrt{v}}\right)+\Delta=\alpha+\Delta.

Since
\Phi^{-1}
is an increasing function,

\displaystyle\frac{x-m_{1}-\eta_{1}}{\sqrt{v}}\leq\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\alpha+\Delta\right),

which implies that

\displaystyle\frac{x+m_{2}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}\leq\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\alpha+\Delta\right)+\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}+\frac{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}.

Combining (
4
) with the fact that
\Phi
is increasing,

\displaystyle Q\!\left(L\!\left(y\right)\leq x\right)=Q\!\left(\frac{L\!\left(y\right)+m_{2}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}\leq\frac{x+m_{2}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi\!\left(\frac{x+m_{2}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}\right)+\Delta

\displaystyle\leq\Phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\alpha+\Delta\right)+\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}+\frac{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}\right)+\Delta.

Therefore the FNR
Q\!\left(L\!\left(y\right)>x\right)
satisfies

\displaystyle Q\!\left(L\!\left(y\right)>x\right)\geq 1-\Phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\alpha+\Delta\right)+\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}+\frac{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}\right)-\Delta.

We modify this into the final expression using the relationship
1-\Phi\!\left(x\right)=\Phi\!\left(-x\right)
and
\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\alpha\right)=-\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\alpha\right)
,

\displaystyle Q\!\left(L\!\left(y\right)>x\right)\geq\Phi\!\left(\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\left(\alpha+\Delta\right)\right)-\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}-\frac{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}\right)-\Delta=G_{\mu+\phi}\!\left(\alpha+\Delta\right)-\Delta,

where
\mu=\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}
and
\phi=\frac{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{\sqrt{v}}
. Finally,
T\!\left[P,Q\right]\!\left(\alpha\right)=Q\!\left(L\!\left(y\right)>x\right)
by the Neyman-Pearson lemma, so we conclude

\displaystyle T\!\left[P,Q\right]\!\left(\alpha\right)\geq G_{\mu+\phi}\!\left(\alpha+\Delta\right)-\Delta\text{ for all }\alpha\in\left[0,1-\Delta\right].

The bound is extended from
\alpha\in\left(0,1-\Delta\right)
to
\alpha\in\left[0,1-\Delta\right]
due to the continuity of trade-off functions. The proof for the remaining bound
T\!\left[P,Q\right]\!\left(\alpha\right)\leq G_{\mu+\phi}\!\left(\alpha-\Delta\right)+\Delta
is identical, which utilises the remaining inequalities (
2
) and (
5
).
∎

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bar{P}}\!\left[\left(\ln\frac{\bar{P}}{\bar{Q}}\right)^{2}\right]-2\hat{q}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}-\left(1-4\hat{q}\right)\!\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)^{2}+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\right)+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}+\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)-2\hat{q}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}-\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)^{2}+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\right)+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-2\hat{q}\right)\frac{\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\right)+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\hat{q}\right)^{2}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\right)+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right).

The case of
\textup{Var}_{Q}\!\left[\ln\frac{P}{Q}\right]
is identical to
\textup{Var}_{P}\!\left[\ln\frac{P}{Q}\right]
. We continue to the third absolute central moment,

\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left|\ln\frac{P}{Q}-\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\ln\frac{P}{Q}\right]\!\right|^{3}=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\bar{P}}\!\left[\begin{multlined}\left(1+\hat{q}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)\right)\!+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)\right)\\
\times\left|\begin{multlined}\ln\frac{\bar{P}\!\left(y\right)}{\bar{Q}\!\left(y\right)}+\ln\frac{1+\hat{q}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)\right)\!+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)}{1+\hat{q}\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{j}\!\left(y\right)+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)}\\
-\left(1-2\hat{q}\right)\!\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\right)\!+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)\end{multlined}\right|^{3}\end{multlined}\right]\\
=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\bar{P}}\!\left[\begin{multlined}\left(1+\hat{q}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)\right)\!+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)\right)\\
\times\left|\ln\frac{\bar{P}\!\left(y\right)}{\bar{Q}\!\left(y\right)}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\hat{q}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{j}\!\left(y\right)+\frac{\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\right)\!\right)\!+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)\right|^{3}\end{multlined}\right].

We proceed by using the triangle inequality,

\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left|\ln\frac{P}{Q}-\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\ln\frac{P}{Q}\right]\!\right|^{3}\leq\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\bar{P}}\!\left[\begin{multlined}\left(1+\hat{q}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)\right)\!+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)\right)\\
\times\left(\left|\ln\frac{\bar{P}\!\left(y\right)}{\bar{Q}\!\left(y\right)}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right|+\hat{q}\!\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{j}\!\left(y\right)+O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\!\right|\!+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)\right)^{\!3}\end{multlined}\right]\\
=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\bar{P}}\!\left[\begin{multlined}\left(1+\hat{q}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)\right)\!+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)\right)\\
\times\left(\begin{multlined}\left|\ln\frac{\bar{P}\!\left(y\right)}{\bar{Q}\!\left(y\right)}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right|^{3}\\
+3\hat{q}\!\left(\ln\frac{\bar{P}\!\left(y\right)}{\bar{Q}\!\left(y\right)}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)^{\!2}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{j}\!\left(y\right)+O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\!\right|\!+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)\end{multlined}\right)\end{multlined}\right].

Here,
\left|O\!\left(1\right)\right|=O\!\left(1\right)
. We separate the expression into two parts and analyse each. The first part is simpler,

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\bar{P}}\!\left[\left(1+\hat{q}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)\right)\!+\hat{q}^{2}O\!\left(1\right)\right)\left|\ln\frac{\bar{P}\!\left(y\right)}{\bar{Q}\!\left(y\right)}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right|^{3}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\bar{P}}\!\left|\ln\frac{\bar{P}\!\left(y\right)}{\bar{Q}\!\left(y\right)}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right|^{3}+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{5}}\right)+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}}\frac{\mu^{3}}{\sigma^{3}}+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{5}}\right)+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right).

(19)

For the second part, we apply the triangle inequality and carefully retain terms of order
\hat{q}\frac{1}{\sigma^{3}}
,

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\bar{P}}\!\left[\left(1+\hat{q}O\!\left(1\right)\right)\cdot 3\hat{q}\!\left(\ln\frac{\bar{P}\!\left(y\right)}{\bar{Q}\!\left(y\right)}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)^{\!2}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{j}\!\left(y\right)+O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\!\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\bar{P}}\!\left[3\hat{q}\!\left(\ln\frac{\bar{P}\!\left(y\right)}{\bar{Q}\!\left(y\right)}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)^{\!2}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\!\left(y\right)+A\!\left(y\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{j}\!\left(y\right)+O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\!\right|\right]+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\bar{P}}\!\left[3\hat{q}\frac{\mu^{2}\!\left(y-\sum_{j}\mu_{j}-\mu\right)^{\!2}}{\sigma^{4}}\left|-\frac{\mu\!\left(y-\sum_{j}\mu_{j}-\mu\right)}{\sigma^{2}}\right|\right]+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\right)+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=3\hat{q}\frac{\mu^{3}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\bar{P}}\!\left|y-\sum_{j}\mu_{j}-\mu\right|^{3}}{\sigma^{6}}+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\right)+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=3\hat{q}\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}}\frac{\mu^{3}}{\sigma^{3}}+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\right)+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right).

(20)

Combining (

\displaystyle=\left(2-q^{3}\right)\!\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}}\frac{\mu^{3}}{\sigma^{3}}+\hat{q}\cdot O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\right)+O\!\left(\hat{q}^{2}\right).

The case of

Effect of zoledronic acid combined with simvastatin on postoperative recovery of elderly patients with osteoporotic intertrochanteric fractures
Authors: Xiaosong Liu, Yalei Zhou, Chun Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo evaluate the effects of zoledronic acid combined with simvastatin on postoperative functional recovery in elderly patients with osteoporotic intertrochanteric femoral fractures.MethodsThis retrospective study included 150 elderly patients with osteoporotic intertrochanteric femoral fractures who underwent proximal femoral nail antirotation (PFNA) fixation between May 2022 and May 2024. According to postoperative pharmacological regimens, patients were assigned to a conventional group (n = 79), receiving zoledronic acid alone, or a combination group (n = 71), receiving zoledronic acid plus oral simvastatin.Harris Hip Score (HHS), visual analogue scale(VAS)for pain, and serum bone metabolism markers, includingβ-C-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen (β-CTX), procollagen type I N-terminal propeptide (PINP), 25-hydroxyvitamin D[25(OH)D], and osteocalcin, were assessed preoperatively and at 3 and 6 months after surgery. The occurrence of internal fixation failure and refracture was also recorded. Statistical analyses were performed using R software.ResultsHHS scores increased progressively at postoperative assessment time points in both groups, whereas VAS scores showed a gradual decline. At 3 and 6 months after surgery, the combination group exhibited higher HHS scores than the conventional group (P < 0.05). 3 months after surgery, the VAS score in the combined group was lower than that in the conventional group (P < 0.05). With respect to bone metabolism, postoperative levels ofβ-CTX, PINP, and osteocalcin decreased over time in both groups, while 25 (OH) D levels increased.Differences in several bone metabolism indicators were observed between groups at postoperative time points. The proportions of internal fixation failure and refracture were lower in the combination group; however, the between-group differences were not statistically significant (P > 0.05).ConclusionIn elderly patients with osteoporotic intertrochanteric femoral fractures treated with PFNA, postoperative administration of zoledronic acid combined with simvastatin was associated with better hip function, favorable changes in bone metabolism and greater short-term improvement in postoperative pain compared with zoledronic acid alonebone metabolism.Keywords: Zoledronic acid, Simvastatin, Osteoporosis, Intertrochanteric fracture of the femur

Introduction

In recent years, the global population has experienced accelerated aging, and osteoporotic fractures have become a major public health issue threatening the health of the elderly. Among these, intertrochanteric fractures of the femur, due to their high incidence and disability rates, significantly impair the quality of life and life expectancy of elderly patients [1, 2]. Although surgical interventions such as proximal femoral non-rotation intramedullary nailing (PFNA) have become first-line treatments, the risks of postoperative fracture healing, internal fixation failure, and refractures remain high in elderly patients with severe osteoporosis [3, 4]. Therefore, how to improve bone metabolism and promote fracture healing through pharmacological interventions based on surgical fixation is currently a key focus of clinical research.

Bisphosphonates are currently the first-line drugs for the treatment of osteoporosis. Zoledronic acid (ZOL), as a third-generation nitrogen-containing bisphosphonate, effectively inhibits bone resorption and increases bone mineral density (BMD) by inducing osteoclast apoptosis through inhibition of the activity of farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase [5, 6]. Multiple clinical studies have confirmed that zoledronic acid significantly reduces the risk of refractures after osteoporotic fractures without affecting the normal healing process of fractures [7]. However, zoledronic acid primarily acts on inhibiting bone resorption, with relatively limited osteogenic effects on promoting new bone formation. For elderly patients with intertrochanteric fractures, the ideal postoperative adjuvant therapy should balance the dual effects of inhibiting bone resorption and promoting bone formation.

Simvastatin, as an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor, is widely used for lipid regulation. In recent years, an increasing number of basic and clinical evidence have demonstrated that simvastatin exhibits polypharmacological effects, particularly showing potential in bone metabolism modulation [8, 9]. Studies have found that simvastatin promotes osteoblast differentiation and bone formation by upregulating the expression of bone morphogenetic protein-2 (BMP-2) [10, 11]. Recent epidemiological studies indicate a significant association between long-term statin use and reduced risk of hip fractures, suggesting its possible protective effect on bone microstructure [12].

The mechanisms of zoladronate and simvastatin in osteoporosis treatment have been well established, but clinical evidence regarding their synergistic effects in postoperative recovery of intertrochanteric fractures in elderly patients remains relatively limited. Given that simvastatin’s osteoinductive properties may complement zoladronate’s anti-resorption effects, this study investigates the therapeutic efficacy of zoladronate combined with simvastatin in postoperative recovery for elderly patients with osteoporotic intertrochanteric fractures, aiming to provide theoretical basis for optimizing comprehensive treatment strategies in clinical practice.

Patients and methods

Participants

This study conducted a meticulous screening of the initially retrieved cases, with a total of 150 patients included in the analysis, covering the period from May 2022 to May 2024. Based on the actual postoperative drug treatment regimens received by the patients, the 150 enrolled patients were divided into two independent observation cohorts: the Conventional Group (n = 79) and the Combined Group (n = 71). In this study, the choice of postoperative drug treatment was determined by the patient’s attending physician based on clinical experience, combined with factors such as the patient’s overall condition, underlying diseases, previous medication history, and drug tolerance. Researchers did not intervene in the treatment selection process; therefore, this study reflects real-world clinical practice in treatment decision-making.

This study is a single-center retrospective cohort study. The data collection for this study was based on the hospital’s electronic medical record (EMR) system. The study protocol strictly adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the hospital’s ethics committee. Given the retrospective nature of this study, the research only involved desensitized analysis of past medical records without interfering with current treatment decisions for patients. All data underwent rigorous de-identification after extraction, thus qualifying for and obtaining an exemption from informed consent. However, during the original treatment process, all patients receiving pharmacotherapy had signed corresponding informed consent forms for medication use.

Inclusion criteria

To ensure the homogeneity of the study subjects and the internal validity of the results, this study established strict inclusion criteria: ①Demographic characteristics: age ≥ 65 years, with no gender restriction. ②Confirmed as a fresh intertrochanteric femoral fracture by X-ray or CT three-dimensional reconstruction. ③Met diagnostic criteria: preoperative diagnosis of primary osteoporosis via dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA), or a history of fragility fractures. ④All cases involved low-energy injuries, such as falls from flat surfaces, sitting or standing positions (fall height < 1 m). ⑤All patients underwent percutaneous femoral nailing (PFNA) after hospitalization. ⑥Patients adhered to the prescribed postoperative medication regimen and had complete hospitalization records, surgical documentation, and laboratory test data.

Exclusion criteria

① Pathological fractures caused by bone metastases, osteomyelitis, or bone tuberculosis, as well as old fractures, bilateral intertrochanteric fractures, or multiple fractures. ② Patients who cannot tolerate surgery or have an expected survival time of less than 12 months. ③ History of malignant tumors. ④ Other endocrine or metabolic bone diseases affecting bone metabolism, such as hyperparathyroidism, Paget’s disease, or osteomalacia. ⑤ Patients allergic to bisphosphonates or statins. ⑥ Those who have been on long-term use of drugs affecting bone metabolism within 6 months prior to enrollment.

Treatment protocol

Both groups underwent PFNA internal fixation surgery, followed by routine administration of blood-activating analgesics, dietary guidance, and rehabilitation exercises. The conventional group received intravenous infusion of zoledronic acid (Zhengda Tianqing Pharmaceutical Group Co., Ltd., National Drug Approval No. H20113138, specification: 5 mg:100mL) for > 30 min per session. The combination group, based on the conventional group, received oral simvastatin (Beijing Fuyuan Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., National Drug Approval No. H20050948) at 40 mg once nightly. The treatment was continued for 6 months.

Primary outcomes

Hip Function Score (HHS) [13]: The HHS scale serves as a core indicator for evaluating postoperative functional recovery, with a total score of 100 points, comprising four dimensions: pain (44 points), function (47 points), deformity (4 points), and range of motion (5 points). Scoring Grading: 90–100 points indicate excellent, 80–89 points indicate good, 70–79 points indicate fair, and <70 points indicate poor. An improvement in the HHS score directly reflects the enhancement of patients' quality of life and the comprehensive efficacy of combined surgical and pharmacological treatments.

Secondary outcomes

Bone metabolism biochemical markers

All serum samples were analyzed using the ECLIA method with the Roche Cobas e601 automated immunoassay analyzer and its corresponding reagent kits, including β-CTX, PINP, 25(OH)D, and Osteocalcin.

Pain

The Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) [14] is used to assess the severity of hip pain in patients. A 10 cm-long floating ruler is employed, with one end marked “0” for “no pain” and the other end marked “10” for “the most severe pain that is intolerable.” The patient marks the position on the ruler based on their subjective perception. The VAS score provides a direct quantification of pain relief and indirectly reflects the stability of the fracture as well as the efficacy of anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects.

Failure of internal fixation and recurrence of fracture

Record the incidence of postoperative internal fixation failure and re-fractures in both groups.

Statistical methods

Data analysis was performed using the R statistical software package. All quantitative data were assessed for distribution shape using the Shapiro-Wilk test and Q-Q plot. Quantitative data conforming to normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, with intergroup comparisons performed using the two-sample t-test. Non-normally distributed quantitative data were expressed as [M (P25, P75)], with intergroup comparisons conducted using the Mann-Whitney U nonparametric test. Categorical data were presented as frequency and percentage [n (%)], with intergroup comparisons performed using the chi-square test; if the theoretical frequency T < 5, the Fisher exact probability method was employed. All statistical tests were two-tailed, with a P-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline data

This study enrolled a total of 150 elderly patients with osteoporotic intertrochanteric fractures of the femur, including 79 in the conventional group and 71 in the combined group. The two groups exhibited similar distributions in demographic characteristics, fracture-related conditions, bone metabolism indicators, and common comorbidities at enrollment.

No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of age (75.84 ± 3.71 vs. 75.87 ± 3.62 years, P = 0.950), gender (male 29.1% vs. 26.8%, P = 0.749), BMI (23.19 ± 0.97 vs. 23.20 ± 0.86 kg/m2, P = 0.952), fracture side (P = 0.870), AO classification (P = 0.965), hypertension (72.2% vs. 71.8%, P = 0.965), diabetes (26.6% vs. 23.9%, P = 0.711), bone mineral density T-value [−2.90 (−3.15, −2.65) vs-3.00 (−3.20, −2.70), P = 0.292], preoperative hemoglobin (126.48 ± 7.61 vs. 125.73 ± 7.42 g/L, P = 0.544), and albumin (38.10 ± 1.35 vs. 37.95 ± 1.34 g/L, P = 0.508) (Table 1).

Bone metabolism indicators

The levels of β-CTX in both groups decreased significantly with time after operation.At 3 months postoperatively, the combined group showed a lower level compared to the conventional group [0.29 (0.28,0.31) vs. 0.42 (0.40,0.44) ng/mL, Z = 10.58, P < 0.001]; at 6 months postoperatively, the combined group remained lower than the conventional group [0.20 (0.20,0.22) vs. 0.35 (0.33,0.37) ng/mL, Z = 10.60, P < 0.001].

The trend of PINP was similar to that of β-CTX. At 3 months postoperatively, the combined group showed lower levels compared to the conventional group [34.40 (32.25,35.90) vs. 38.10 (36.50,39.90) ng/mL, Z = 8.09, P < 0.001]; at 6 months postoperatively, the combined group exhibited lower levels than the conventional group [27.10 (25.45,28.30) vs. 31.70 (30.25,33.20) ng/mL, Z = 9.80, P < 0.001].

The 25 (OH) D levels in both groups increased gradually after operation.At 3 months postoperatively, the combined group showed a higher level than the conventional group [27.50 (25.30,28.90) vs. 21.70 (20.65,22.85) ng/mL, Z = 10.26, P < 0.001]; at 6 months postoperatively, the combined group again demonstrated a higher level than the conventional group [32.80 (30.30,34.65) vs. 25.00 (24.05,26.25) ng/mL, Z = 10.51, P < 0.001].

The level of osteocalcin in both groups decreased gradually after operation. At 3 months postoperatively, the combined group showed lower levels compared to the conventional group [16.60 (15.70,17.40) vs. 18.80 (18.15,19.75) ng/mL, Z = 8.49, P < 0.001]; at 6 months postoperatively, the combined group again demonstrated lower levels than the conventional group [13.10 (12.15,14.00) vs. 16.30 (15.65,17.10) ng/mL, Z = 10.50, P < 0.001].

Overall, both groups of patients exhibited changes in postoperative bone metabolism indicators, with the combination therapy demonstrating more pronounced trends in inhibiting bone resorption and regulating bone metabolism-related parameters (Fig. 2; Table 3).

Discussion

This study retrospectively analyzed the application value of zoledronic acid combined with simvastatin in the postoperative recovery of patients with osteoporotic intertrochanteric femoral fractures in the elderly after PFNA (percutaneous femoral nailing) surgery. The results demonstrated that compared with zoledronic acid alone, the combination therapy with simvastatin significantly improved hip joint function at 3 and 6 months postoperatively, effectively reduced pain levels, and exerted a beneficial regulatory effect on bone metabolism indicators. Additionally, the combined treatment group showed a trend toward reduced rates of internal fixation failure and refractures, although the differences were not statistically significant. These findings support the potential advantages of combination drug therapy in improving clinical outcomes after surgery for elderly patients with fragility fractures.

The core finding of this study is that combination therapy significantly improved patients ‘hip function scores and alleviated pain. Previous studies have shown that functional recovery after osteoporotic fractures is influenced by multiple factors, among which pain control and fracture healing quality are critical [4]. Simvastatin, as a lipophilic statin, exhibits multifunctional effects beyond lipid regulation. Studies have confirmed that simvastatin promotes osteoblast differentiation and bone formation by enhancing the BMP-2/Smad signaling pathway [8, 10], which may accelerate callus formation and mineralization at fracture sites, thereby providing a more robust biomechanical foundation for early functional exercise. Additionally, simvastatin demonstrates anti-inflammatory properties by inhibiting the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines [15]. The combined group showed a lower VAS score at 3 months post-surgery compared to the conventional group, suggesting a potential advantage in early postoperative pain relief. This phenomenon may be related to the anti-inflammatory effect of simvastatin and its improvement of the local inflammatory microenvironment, thereby reducing pain perception in the early postoperative stage. However, at 6 months post-surgery, the difference in VAS scores between the two groups was not statistically significant, suggesting that this analgesic advantage may be mainly reflected in the early postoperative period rather than having a long-term sustained effect [16].

In terms of bone metabolism, this study observed a downward trend in postoperative bone turnover markers β-CTX, PINP, and Osteocalcin in both patient groups, with the combination group levels being lower than those in the conventional group. Typically, bone metabolism is in a state of high turnover following a fracture. However, in elderly patients with osteoporosis, this hyperturnover is often accompanied by greater bone resorption than bone formation, leading to further loss of bone mass. As a potent bisphosphonate, zoledronic acid significantly reduces bone resorption markers β-CTX by inhibiting osteoclast activity, which is a key mechanism for preventing bone loss [5, 6]. Notably, although simvastatin is often considered a bone-promoting agent in preclinical studies, in this study, the PINP (bone formation marker) level in the combination group was also lower than that in the conventional group. This may reflect the dominant role of bisphosphonates, which strongly inhibit bone turnover, restoring bone metabolism from a pathological hyperturnover state to a stable low-turnover state. The lower bone turnover levels in the combination therapy group may indicate a more stable local fracture microenvironment, avoiding excessive bone resorption that could disrupt callus structure. Simultaneously, the combination group showed a significant increase in postoperative 25(OH)D levels, which may be related to simvastatin’s improvement of vascular endothelial function and promotion of local blood circulation, thereby enhancing vitamin D bioavailability or the overall metabolic status of patients [17, 18].

Regarding safety outcomes, although the incidence of internal fixation failure (1.41% vs. 7.59%) and refractures (1.41% vs. 6.33%) in the combined group was lower than that in the conventional group in this study, the difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.05). This may be attributed to the relatively small sample size (n = 150) and short follow-up period (6 months) in this study. However, this trend is consistent with recent large cohort studies indicating that statin use is associated with a reduced risk of hip fractures [12, 19]. Simvastatin may enhance bone strength by improving bone microstructure rather than simply increasing bone density, thereby reducing the risk of biomechanical failure [20, 21]. Future studies with larger sample sizes and long-term follow-up are needed to further validate this effect in preventing secondary fractures.

This study has certain limitations. As a retrospective study, although we matched the baseline data, it is still impossible to completely exclude confounding factors such as patient compliance and the specific intensity of rehabilitation training. Only the recovery status of patients at 3 months and 6 months postoperatively was observed, which is insufficient for monitoring long-term complications such as the rate of long-term refractures and peri-prosthetic fractures. This study did not perform bone tissue biopsy or micro-CT analysis. The specific mechanisms of drug effects on bone microstructure are primarily inferred based on serological indicators, lacking histological evidence to support them.

Conclusion

In elderly patients with osteoporotic intertrochanteric femoral fractures treated with PFNA, postoperative administration of zoledronic acid combined with simvastatin was associated with better hip function, favorable changes in bone metabolism and greater short-term improvement in postoperative pain compared with zoledronic acid alonebone metabolism.

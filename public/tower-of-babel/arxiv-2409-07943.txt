Skyrmion crystal phase on a magnetic domain wall in chiral magnets
Yuki Amari, Muneto Nitta

Abstract
We study a magnetic domain wall in the ferromagnetic phase in chiral magnets in two dimensions with an in-plane easy-axis anisotropy and an out-of-plane Zeeman magnetic field, and find a chiral soliton lattice (spiral) phase beside a ferromagnetic phase inside the domain line, where the former represents a domain-wall skyrmion crystal from the bulk point of view. We first determine the phase diagram on the domain wall by numerically constructing domain-wall solutions. We then analytically reproduce the phase diagram in a domain-wall theory (a chiral double sine-Gordon model) that we construct within the moduli approximation by treating the Zeeman magnetic field perturbatively. While we find good agreements between the phase diagrams of the numerical and effective theory methods, the numerical solution exhibits a decomposition of the topological charge into a bimeron which cannot be captured by the effective theory.

II
Model and domain-wall configuration

In this section, we introduce the Hamiltonian we study in the paper and describe its domain-wall solutions. We also discuss properties of the domain wall that we will utilize in the proceeding sections.

II.1
Model

Low energy phenomena in chiral magnets with the ferromagnetic exchange interaction can be described by a continuum theory for a three-component unit vector
{\bf n}=(n_{x},n_{y},n_{z})
representing the normalized magnetization vector, i.e.,
{\bf n}=\mathbf{M}/M_{s}
with the magnetization vector
\mathbf{M}
and saturation magnetization
M_{s}
.
The continuum theory that we study in this paper is defined by a Hamiltonian of the form

\begin{split}{\cal H}=&\frac{1}{2}\partial_{k}{\bf n}\cdot\partial_{k}{\bf n}+{\bf D}_{k}\cdot\left({\bf n}\times\partial_{k}{\bf n}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad-\mathbf{H}\cdot{\bf n}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}\left(1-n_{x}^{2}\right)\end{split}

(1)

with
k=x,y
, where the energy scale is
|J|M_{s}^{2}
with the exchange constant
J
positive.
The first term is the
O(3)
nonlinear sigma model, which is equivalent to the continuum version of the Heisenberg exchange interaction. The second term is the DM interaction, and the third is the Zeeman interaction where
{\bf H}=\mathbf{B}/JM_{s}
with the external magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
. The last term is an in-plane easy-axis anisotropy with a positive coupling constant
\mu
.
We consider the magnetic field applied perpendicular to the
x\text{-}y
plane,
\mathbf{H}=(0,0,-h)
and employ the Rashba-Dresselhaus type DM vectors

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\bf D}_{x}=-\kappa(\cos\vartheta,-\sin\vartheta,0)\\
&{\bf D}_{y}=-\kappa(\sin\vartheta,\cos\vartheta,0)\end{aligned}

(2)

where
\kappa
and
\vartheta
are constants.
The spin-spiral period giving typical length scale is defined as
L_{D}=2\pi/|\kappa|
.

The topological charge density for skyrmions is defined by

{\cal Q}=\frac{\varepsilon^{kl}}{8\pi}{\bf n}\cdot(\partial_{k}{\bf n}\times\partial_{l}{\bf n})\ .

(3)

The integral of
{\cal Q}
over the
x
-
y
plane provides an integer counting the number of skyrmions.

We introduce new coordinates by rotating the
x
-
y
plane with an angle
\nu
as

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{x}\\
\tilde{y}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\nu&-\sin\nu\\
\sin\nu&\cos\nu\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{l}x\\
y\end{array}\right)\ .

In terms of the new coordinates, the DM interaction can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{k}\cdot\left({\bf n}\times\partial_{k}{\bf n}\right)=\tilde{{\bf D}}_{k}\cdot\left({\bf n}\times\tilde{\partial}_{k}{\bf n}\right)

(10)

where
\left(\tilde{\partial}_{x},\tilde{\partial}_{y}\right)=\left(\partial_{\tilde{x}},\partial_{\tilde{y}}\right)
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\tilde{{\bf D}}_{x}=-\kappa(\cos\tilde{\vartheta},-\sin\tilde{\vartheta},0)\\
&\tilde{{\bf D}}_{y}=-\kappa(\sin\tilde{\vartheta},\cos\tilde{\vartheta},0)\end{aligned}

(11)

with
\tilde{\vartheta}\equiv\vartheta-\nu
.
The other terms in the Hamiltonian are invariant under the spatial rotation.
For simplicity, we choose the
\tilde{x}
axis as the direction parallel to domain walls.

II.2
domain-wall solutions

Arcs corresponding to short walls and long walls on the OPS

Domain-wall solutions parallel to the
\tilde{x}
axis can be derived using an ansatz of the form

{\bf n}=(\cos f,\sin f\cos\phi,\sin f\sin\phi)

(12)

with

f=f(\tilde{y}),\qquad\phi=\text{const}.\ .

(13)

Substituting this ansatz into the Hamiltonian, we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\tilde{y}}f\right)^{2}+\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}\sin\phi~\partial_{\tilde{y}}f

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+h\sin\phi\sin f+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}\sin^{2}f\ .

(14)

This is called the chiral double sine-Gordon model
, which is the double sine-Gordon model with the topological term proportional to
\kappa
. Domain-wall solutions in this model have been studied in Ref.
59
.
For later convenience, we define

\begin{split}&\gamma=\frac{|h|}{\mu^{2}},~~s_{1}=\mathop{sgn}\nolimits(h),~~s_{2}=\mathop{sgn}\nolimits(\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}).\end{split}

(15)

Note that in this paper, we let
\gamma\in[0,1)
where the Hamiltonian has two vacua given by

\begin{split}&\phi=s_{3}\frac{\pi}{2},\\
&\sin f=-s_{1}s_{3}\gamma\end{split}

(16)

with
s_{3}=\pm 1
, which leads to

{\mathbf{n}}=\left(\pm\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}},0,-s_{1}\gamma\right)\ .

(17)

For later convenience, we name the vacua as

\displaystyle{\bf n}_{\rm vac_{A}}\equiv\left(-s_{1}s_{2}\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}},0,-s_{1}\gamma\right)\ ,

(18)

\displaystyle{\bf n}_{\rm vac_{B}}\equiv\left(s_{1}s_{2}\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}},0,-s_{1}\gamma\right),

(19)

and explicitly refer to
{\bf n}_{\rm vac_{A}}
as the vacuum A and
{\bf n}_{\rm vac_{B}}
as the vacuum B.
When the Hamiltonian has two vacua, we have two types of domain walls according to the way to connect the vacua in the order parameter space (OPS)
S^{2}

1

The equations of motion with respect to
f

\phi

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{{\tilde{y}}}f-h\sin\phi\cos f-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}\sin(2f)=0\,,

(20)

\displaystyle\cos\phi~(\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}\partial_{\tilde{y}}f+h\sin f)=0\,.

(21)

Eq. (
20

Eq. (

Using it, one obtains, from Eq. (

\partial_{\tilde{y}}f=\pm\sqrt{\mu^{2}\sin^{2}f+2s_{1}s_{3}|h|\sin f+C}

We impose the boundary conditions that

\lim_{{\tilde{y}}\to-\infty}\sin f=-s_{1}s_{3}\gamma,~~\lim_{{\tilde{y}}\to-\infty}\partial_{\tilde{y}}f=0\ .

Then, one obtains

\partial_{\tilde{y}}f=\pm|\mu||\sin f+s_{1}s_{3}\gamma|\,.

In this case, short walls satisfy
s_{1}s_{3}\cdot\sin f\leq-\gamma
, and long walls satisfy
s_{1}s_{3}\cdot\sin f\geq-\gamma
.

).
We denote the lower-energy (higher-energy) short walls
{\mathrm{SW}}^{-}
(
{\mathrm{SW}}^{+}
), and similarly the lower-energy (higher-energy) long walls
{\mathrm{LW}}^{-}
(
{\mathrm{LW}}^{+}

Note that the sign does not necessarily coincide with that in Eq. (

The tension (energy per unit length)
of the domain walls is given by

T[f]=\int d{\tilde{y}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\tilde{y}}f\right)^{2}+s_{2}s_{3}\cdot|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|~\partial_{\tilde{y}}f+s_{1}s_{3}\cdot|h|\sin f+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}\sin^{2}f+\frac{h^{2}}{2\mu^{2}}\right]

where we have introduced the last constant term so that the tension of vacuum configurations

becomes zero.
Substituting the solutions into the tension, we obtain explicit expressions of the tensions
of the short and long walls, respectively, as

\displaystyle T_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{\pm}}\equiv T[f_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{\pm}}]=2\mu\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}+4\left(-\mu\gamma\pm|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|\right)\arctan\sqrt{\frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma}}\,,

(30)

\displaystyle T_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{\pm}}\equiv T[f_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{\pm}}]=2\mu\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}+4\left(\mu\gamma\pm|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|\right)\arctan\sqrt{\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}}\,.

(31)

This indicates that the domain walls
{\mathrm{SW}}^{-}
and
{\mathrm{LW}}^{-}

will spontaneously lie along the direction with
\tilde{\vartheta}=0

unless it is pinned because the tension takes its lowest value at
\tilde{\vartheta}=0
with given
\mu
and
\kappa
.
This is in contrast to the case with
an out-of-plane easy-axis potential
in which case the direction of
a domain wall is arbitrary.
In this paper, we consider an arbitrary value of
\tilde{\vartheta}
for generality
which would be achieved by
pinning or applying suitable magnetic fields
at the boundaries.
For a given value of
\tilde{\vartheta}
, we have
T_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{+}}>T_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{-}}
and
T_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{+}}>T_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{-}}
.
Moreover, one can easily find

.
Therefore, we have the following three possibilities in the order of domain-wall tension:

\displaystyle\text{Phase I}~:~T_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{+}}>T_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{-}}>T_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{+}}>T_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{-}}

when

\displaystyle|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|<\mu\gamma\,.

(32)

\displaystyle\text{Phase II}~:~T_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{+}}>T_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{+}}\geq T_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{-}}>T_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{-}}

\displaystyle\mu\gamma\leq|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|<\frac{\pi\mu\gamma}{2\arctan\left(\dfrac{\gamma}{\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}}\right)}\,,

\displaystyle\text{Phase III}~:~T_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{+}}>T_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{+}}>T_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{-}}\geq T_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{-}}

is unstable because its tension is higher than that of
{\mathrm{LW}}^{-}
, which satisfies the same boundary condition with

. Similarly,

In this section, we construct domain-wall skyrmion crystals

Here, domain-wall skyrmion crystals mean periodic arrays of skyrmions on a domain wall.
We find that domain-wall skyrmion crystals can have lower energy than the domain walls

and

It implies that the domain-wall skyrmion crystals are the

The initial input is prepared as follows: for the profile

{\tilde{y}}

{\mathrm{SW}}^{+}
or
{\mathrm{LW}}^{-}

In the

For the

IV
Domain-wall Skyrmions in Moduli approximation

In this section, we construct domain-wall skyrmions using the moduli approximation, also known as the Manton approximation
[
52
,
53
,
54
]
.
We find that the approximation qualitatively reproduces the features of numerical solutions.

IV.1
Single domain-wall skyrmion

In this subsection, we analytically construct single domain-wall skyrmions using the moduli approximation.
By evaluating their energy, we discuss the lowest energy structure on the domain wall.

Moduli approximation is to consider low-energy fluctuation around a soliton while neglecting the back-reactions of the fluctuation.
To describe the low-energy fluctuation, moduli parameters of solitons are promoted to fields depending on coordinates along the solitons, and an effective theory for the fields is constructed by integrating the Lagrangian or Hamiltonian over co-dimension of the solitons.
In our case, the low-energy fluctuations of domain walls are associated with the position moduli
\chi
and the phase
\phi
.
Note that since the phase
\phi
contributes to the domain wall’s energy, it is not precisely a moduli parameter. It is, however, regarded as a quasi-moduli parameter because Eqs. (
25
) and (
26
) satisfy the equation of motion for any value of
\phi
, implying that the existence of the domain wall is independent of
\phi
.

To apply the moduli approximation, we promote the (quasi-)moduli parameters
\chi
and
\phi
to fields depending on the coordinates along the wall:

\displaystyle\chi=\chi(\tilde{x}),

(36)

\displaystyle\phi=\varphi(\tilde{x})+s_{3}\frac{\pi}{2}.

(37)

However, Eq. (
37
) causes the problem that the asymptotic value of
{\bf n}
at
\tilde{y}=\pm\infty
for the short and long walls changes. In order to resolve the problem, instead of Eqs. (
25
) and (
26
), we approximately use the profile function in the limit
h\to 0
:

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}f_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{\pm}}=

\displaystyle\pm 2\arctan\left[\exp\left(s_{2}s_{3}\{\mu{\tilde{y}}-\chi\}\right)\right]

\displaystyle~-(\pm 1+s_{1}s_{3})\frac{\pi}{2}\,,

(38)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}f_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{\pm}}=

\displaystyle\pm 2\arctan\left[\exp\left(s_{2}s_{3}\{\mu{\tilde{y}}-\chi\}\right)\right]

\displaystyle~-(\pm 1-s_{1}s_{3})\frac{\pi}{2}

(39)

The tension of the configurations (
38
) and (
39
) with
\chi=\varphi=0
are given respectively as

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}T[f_{{\mathrm{SW}}^{\pm}}]=\lim_{h\to 0}T[f_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{\pm}}]=2|\mu|\pm\pi|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|\ .

(40)

In this paper, specifically, we employ
{\mathrm{LW}}^{-}
in the limit
h\to 0
as the approximated seed domain-wall configuration in order to compare the results of the moduli approximation and numerical results.
Therefore, we write

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{DW}}=\lim_{h\to 0}f_{{\mathrm{LW}}^{-}}

\displaystyle=-2\arctan\left[\exp\left(s_{2}s_{3}\{\mu{\tilde{y}}-\chi\}\right)\right]+(1+s_{1}s_{3})\frac{\pi}{2}

(41)

The configurations
{\mathrm{DW}}
describe a domain wall when
s_{2}s_{3}=-1
and an anti-domain wall when
s_{2}s_{3}=+1
.
In other words, the function
f_{{\mathrm{DW}}}
monotonically increases (decreases) in
\tilde{y}
for
s_{2}s_{3}=-1(+1)
.
The tension of
{\mathrm{DW}}
with
\chi=\varphi=0
is given by

\displaystyle T_{{\mathrm{DW}}}

\displaystyle\equiv T[f_{{\mathrm{DW}}}]=2|\mu|-\pi|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|\ .

(42)

Clearly, it takes the lowest value when we choose the minus sign with
\tilde{\vartheta}=0
.

The effective energy for the fluctuation is given by the integral of the Hamiltonian density over the coordinates normal to the domain wall and can be cast into the form

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{\text{eff}}\equiv

\displaystyle\int d\tilde{y}~{\cal H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{|\mu|}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\tilde{x}}\chi-A\cos\varphi\right)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\tilde{x}}\varphi\right)^{2}-\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}\partial_{\tilde{x}}\varphi+V(\varphi)\right]

(43)

where
V(\varphi)
is the potential given by

\displaystyle V(\varphi)=|B|-B\cos\varphi+\frac{A^{2}}{2}\left(1-\cos^{2}\varphi\right)\ .

(44)

Here, the parameters
A
and
B
are given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &A\equiv\frac{\pi\kappa}{2}s_{2}\sin\tilde{\vartheta},\\
&B\equiv\frac{\pi}{2}(|\kappa\mu\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|-|h|),\end{aligned}

(45)

where we have omitted constant terms depending on
\tilde{\vartheta}
.
The equations of motion associated with the effective energy (
43
) are given by

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{x}}^{2}\varphi=B\sin\varphi+\frac{A^{2}}{2}\sin 2\varphi

(46)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{x}}\chi=A\cos\varphi.

(47)

Eq. (
47
) is of the Bogomol’nyi type. One can obtain it by integrating the Euler-Lagrange equation with respect to
\chi
once.
Eq. (
46
) is the double sine-Gordon equation that can be obtained from the Euler-Lagrange equation with respect to
\varphi
by eliminating its
\chi
-dependence using Eq. (
47
).
Note that
the field
\chi
decouples with
\varphi
when
\tilde{\vartheta}=0
because
A\propto\sin\tilde{\vartheta}=0
.

The vacua of the effective energy is given by the constant field

\displaystyle\varphi=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}2l\pi&\mbox{ for }B>0\\
l\pi&\mbox{ for }B=0\\
(2l+1)\pi&\mbox{ for }B<0.\end{array}\right.

with
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
When
A\neq 0
, the field
\chi
for the vacuum is linear in
\tilde{x}
, i.e.,

\chi=\mathop{sgn}\nolimits(B)A\tilde{x}+\text{const}.\ .

(51)

This implies that the domain wall is straight and slanted.

Now, let us discuss kink solutions in the domain-wall effective theory.
A single kink connects two neighboring vacua.
Integrating Eq. (
46
) once, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{\tilde{x}}\varphi=s_{2}\sqrt{2(V(\varphi)+\Delta)}\end{aligned}

(52)

with an integration constant
\Delta
.
The sign is chosen as the first derivative term gives a negative contribution.
When
\Delta=0
, it is nothing but the Bogomol’nyi equation for the double sine-Gordon model that a single kink solution satisfies.

Kink solutions in this effective theory describe single domain-wall skyrmions in the bulk theory (
1
).
When
B\neq 0
, we impose the boundary condition

\displaystyle\lim_{\tilde{x}\rightarrow-\infty}\partial_{\tilde{x}}\varphi=0,\qquad\lim_{\tilde{x}\rightarrow-\infty}\cos\varphi=\mathop{sgn}\nolimits(B)

(53)

Then, Eq. (
52
) can be explicitly rewritten as

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{x}}\varphi=s_{2}\sqrt{2|B|-2B\cos\varphi+A^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi}.

(54)

A single kink solution is given by

\displaystyle\varphi=-2s_{2}\cdot\arccos\left[\sqrt{\frac{|B|}{A^{2}+|B|\cosh^{2}\left(\sqrt{A^{2}+|B|}\tilde{x}+\alpha\right)}}\sinh\left(\sqrt{A^{2}+|B|}\tilde{x}+\alpha\right)\right]+(4l+1-\mathop{sgn}\nolimits(B))\frac{\pi}{2}

(55)

with a constant
\alpha
. Using the solution (
55
), Eq. (
46
) can be
rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{\tilde{x}}\chi=-\mathop{sgn}\nolimits(B)~A\left(1-\frac{2|B|\sinh^{2}\left(\sqrt{A^{2}+|B|}\tilde{x}+\alpha\right)}{A^{2}+|B|\cosh^{2}\left(\sqrt{A^{2}+|B|}\tilde{x}+\alpha\right)}\right).\end{aligned}

(56)

Integrating both sides of this equation, one finally obtains
the single kink solution

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \chi=\mathop{sgn}\nolimits(B)\left(\frac{A}{\sqrt{A^{2}+|B|}}\left(\sqrt{A^{2}+|B|}\tilde{x}+\alpha\right)-2\operatorname{arctanh}\left[\frac{A}{\sqrt{A^{2}+|B|}}\tanh\left(\sqrt{A^{2}+|B|}\tilde{x}+\alpha\right)\right]\right)+\beta\end{aligned}

(57)

with a constant
\beta
.
The energy of this kink solution is obtained as

\displaystyle E_{\text{eff}}=\int d\tilde{x}~{\cal E}_{\text{eff }}=\frac{8}{|\mu|}\left\{\sqrt{A^{2}+|B|}+\frac{|B|}{A}\operatorname{arctanh}\left(\frac{A}{\sqrt{A^{2}+|B|}}\right)-\frac{\pi}{2}|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|\right\}.

(58)

When
A=0=\tilde{\vartheta}
, the energy can simply be written as

E_{\text{eff}}=\frac{8}{|\mu|}\left(\sqrt{2\pi||\kappa\mu|-|h||}-\frac{\pi}{2}|\kappa|\right)\ .

(59)

On the other hand,
in the case with
B=0
, we impose boundary conditions of the form

\displaystyle\lim_{\tilde{x}\rightarrow-\infty}\partial_{\tilde{x}}\varphi=0,\qquad\lim_{\tilde{x}\rightarrow-\infty}\cos\varphi=\pm 1

(60)

Then, Eq. (
52
) reads

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{x}}\varphi=s_{2}~|A\sin\varphi|.

(61)

This is nothing but the Bogomol’nyi equation for the sine-Gordon equation. The single kink solution is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \varphi=2s_{2}\cdot\arctan[\exp(|A|\tilde{x}+\alpha)]+l\pi.\end{aligned}

(62)

Similarly to the case with
B\neq 0
, we substitute Eq. (
62
) into Eq. (
47
)
to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{\tilde{x}}\chi&=(-1)^{l+1}A\tanh(|A|\tilde{x}+\alpha)\ .\end{aligned}

(63)

The integration over both sides gives us the solution

\displaystyle\chi=(-1)^{l+1}\mathop{sgn}\nolimits(A)\log[\cosh(|A|\tilde{x}+\alpha)]+\beta\ .

(64)

The energy difference between this solution and the vacuum is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{\text{eff}}=\frac{4}{|\mu|}\left(|A|-\frac{\pi}{2}|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|\right)\ .\end{aligned}

(65)

This is a half of Eq. (
58
) with
B=0
.

The kink energies in Eqs. (
58
) and (
65
) are not positive definite due to their last term coming from the first derivative topological term in Eq. (
43
). When the kink energy is positive, the ground state on the domain-wall theory is
ferromagnetic, i.e., uniform.
On the other hand,
when the kink energy is negative,
the ground state is no longer uniform and
it is a CSL state,
corresponding to a one-dimensional skyrmion crystal
in the bulk.
We will study this case in the following subsection.

The domain-wall skyrmion crystals can be described by CSL solutions in the effective theory on the domain wall.
The CSL with an appropriate period is the ground state in the effective theory.
The period
L
of the CSL is defined from Eq. (
52
) as

\displaystyle L=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\varphi}{\sqrt{2(V(\varphi)+\Delta)}}\ .

(66)

We specify the parameter
\Delta
providing the appropriate period.
We can write the energy per unit length as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\bar{E}_{\text{eff}}\equiv E_{\text{eff}}/L\\
&=\frac{2}{|\mu|}\frac{1}{L}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\sqrt{2(V(\varphi)+\Delta)}\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.-L\Delta-2\pi|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|\right]\ ,\end{aligned}

(67)

where we utilized Eq. (
52
).
It follows that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\bar{E}_{\text{eff}}}{\partial\Delta}=&\frac{2}{|\mu|}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\Delta}\\
&\times\left[2\pi|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|-\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\sqrt{2(V(\varphi)+\Delta)}\right].\end{aligned}

(68)

Since
L
is a monotonic function in
\Delta
, we have
\partial L/\partial\Delta\neq 0
.
Therefore, the least-energy condition for
\Delta
is given by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\sqrt{2(V(\varphi)+\Delta)}=2\pi|\kappa\cos\tilde{\vartheta}|\ .

(69)

The ground state in the effective theory is given by the CSL solution with
\Delta
satisfying Eq. (
69
), and its energy is
\bar{E}_{\text{eff}}=-2\Delta/(L|\mu|)
.

.

The most remarkable difference between the results of the two methods is the decomposition of a domain-wall skyrmion into two merons.
In the moduli approximation, we can always observe only a single peak per one period representing a
Q=-1
skyrmion at
\tilde{\vartheta}=0
.
The split of the domain-wall skyrmion occurs only when
\tilde{\vartheta}\neq 0
.
On the other hand, as shown in Fig.
6
, for the full numerical simulation, a domain-wall skyrmion is split into two merons even at
\tilde{\vartheta}=0
.

These results indicate that although the moduli approximation fails to capture the split of the topological charge of the domain-wall skyrmion into merons, it successfully reproduces significant properties of domain-wall skyrmion crystals, such as their emergence as the lowest energy state with a single domain wall structure, the absence of domain wall bending observed in chiral magnets with an out-of-plane easy-axis anisotropy
[
42
,
51
]
, etc.
The reason why the moduli approximation fails to describe some properties of the domain-wall skyrmion crystals will be that the effective theory (
43
) is constructed based on the domain-wall solution in the absence of the Zeeman interaction,
unlike the case of out-of-plane easy-axis anisotropy
in Ref.
[
51
]
in which
the Zeeman interaction is absent
both in the effective theory and numerical solutions.
The Zeeman interaction makes the difference between the short and long walls.
We cannot distinguish them within the moduli approximation. That is probably the main reason of mismatch between the two methods.

V
Summary and Discussion

We have investigated domain-wall skyrmions
in chiral magnets with
an in-plane
easy-axis anisotropy
as well as
an out-of-plane Zeeman magnetic field,
instead of the out-of-plane easy-axis
anisotropy studied in
the previous works.
The direction of a single straight domain wall has an energetically preferable direction.
First, we have performed full numerical simulations
of the configuration with the boundary condition (
27
)
and
obtained the phase diagram of the lowest energy single domain wall state in Fig.
5
.
Even though the bulk (without any specific boundary condition)
is in the ferromagnetic phase, the ground state
on a single domain wall state
is either the uniform ferromagnetic phase
or the CSL phase on the domain wall.
The latter corresponds to
a skyrmion crystal along the domain line.
Second,
we have studied this system analytically
by using the moduli approximation.
We have constructed
the effective theory on a single domain wall
in the ferromagnetic phase
with perturbatively treating the Zeeman magnetic field,
and obtained a chiral double sine-Gordon model.
By constructing double sine-Gordon solitons,
we have obtained the phase diagram
of the domain-wall effective theory
in Fig.
7
,
which we found is in a good agreement
with
that of full numerics,
as shown in Fig.
11
.
While
the moduli approximation captures
the qualitative behaviors of numerical solutions,
the latter exhibits more precise configurations
of domain-wall skyrmions
such as a decomposition of the topological charge
into a bimeron
(see Fig.
6
).
We also have found
from the effective theory and numerical simulations. that
if we rotate the domain wall by applying magnetic fields,
the domain-wall skyrmion crystal
becomes wavy as shown in
Fig.
10
.

As found in this paper, the domain-wall skyrmions do not exhibit cusps
for an in-plane easy-axis anisotropy,
in contrast to the out-of-plane anisotropy for which
the domain wall has cups at skyrmions.
This fact may have an advantage
when applying to nanotechnology such as magnetic memories.
Dynamics of domain-wall skyrmions under applied electric currents should
be an important direction to explore.

Apart from such applications, there are also some fundamental aspects.
Namely,
our paper proposes a novel
notion of phases of matter,

i.e.
,
phases with specific boundary conditions
for instance requiring the presence of topological defects
(a domain wall in our case).
That in turn classifies
phases inside or in the vicinity of
the topological defects.
This can be applied to other topological defects, for instance, to vortices.

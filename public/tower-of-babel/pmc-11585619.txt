Synthesis, characterization, and adsorption performance of naphthalene-based covalent organic polymer for high-efficiency methylene blue removal
Authors: Omid Rahmanian, Mohammad Dinari, Nasim Mohammadi, Leila Aliakbarian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In this study, a novel naphthalene-based covalent organic polymer (N-COP) was synthesized and investigated as an advanced adsorbent for the efficient removal of Methylene Blue (MB) from aqueous solutions. The polymer was synthesized through a polycondensation reaction between cyanuric chloride and 1,5-dihydroxynaphthalene, followed by thorough purification. Comprehensive characterization was performed using Fourier Transform Infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy, X-Ray Diffraction (XRD), Thermogravimetric Analysis (TGA), Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) surface area analysis, and Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscopy (FE-SEM), confirming the successful formation of the porous polymer with a high surface area and thermal stability. Key adsorption parameters including pH, contact time, and initial MB concentration were systematically optimized. The adsorption behavior followed the Langmuir isotherm model under conditions of pH 7 and a contact time of 30 min, indicating a maximum uptake capacity of 90.09 mg/g, while kinetic analysis revealed a strong fit with the pseudo-second-order model. These results demonstrate that N-COP is a promising candidate for environmental applications (in compression with other adsorbents), particularly in the removal of MB from wastewater, offering an eco-friendly, high-efficiency solution to mitigate water pollution.

Introduction

In recent years, ensuring access to clean water has become increasingly challenging due to surging global demand driven by population growth and the influx of diverse pollutants into the environment. Although surface water remains a vital resource for many communities, the presence of industrial pollutants poses significant limitations on its usability1. The swift progress in industrial activities has introduced a host of detrimental elements such as biodegradable waste, agrochemicals, explosives, antibiotics, and dyes2–4. These contaminants pose grave threats to both the ecosystem and human health. Unchecked discharges from industries and agriculture carry a cocktail of pollutants ranging from heavy metals and pesticides to harmful pathogens and dyes5–8. Among these, the hazardous 4-nitrophenol stands out for its widespread use in medicinal applications, agronomy, and dye production, prompting serious concerns about its detrimental effects on human well-being9,10. The discharge of colored wastewater into the environment, particularly into water bodies, not only compromises aesthetic appeal but also disrupts ecological balance, leading to phenomena such as eutrophication11. Various industries, including cosmetics, leather, printing, and textiles, contribute to this issue by releasing colored effluents containing complex and often harmful dye molecules12–15. These dyes are known for their toxicity, carcinogenicity, mutagenicity, biodegradability, and stability, exerting adverse effects on the environment.

The presence of organic dyes in industrial wastewater, particularly from sectors like paint manufacturing, poses a significant threat to water resources, diminishing both surface and groundwater quality16,17. Effective treatments of such industrial effluents is crucial to prevent environmental contamination before discharge. MB dye, a prevalent thiazine dye, is commonly discovered in the sewage of manufacturing companies like cosmetics, paints textiles, etc18,19. Particularly the removal of MB from contaminated water has attracted significant attention due to its severe health risks, including skin allergies, central nervous system damage, and potential cancer developments, which can arise from exposure to MB20. Also, when it comes to the plants, the presence of MB can lead to inhibited growth, a decrease in pigments, and other effects21. In the later stages, MB can negatively effect on the chemical and biological oxygen demand, as well as the oxygen requirements, ultimately disrupting the entire water ecosystem22,23. Moreover, its high solubility in aqueous systems presents a significant challenge to its efficient removal using conventional adsorption methods24,25. Numerous techniques have been utilized for eliminating dye contaminants, such as aerobic biological approaches like activated sludge, anaerobic treatments, fungal remediation, chemical oxidation, and optical degradation techniques like TiO2/UV and Photo-Fenton processes26,27. Additionally, adsorption processes using materials like activated carbon have been explored. Continuous efforts by researchers aim to enhance treatment methodologies and achieve higher removal efficiencies to combat pollution effectively28,29.

Several conventional techniques explored for dye removal, including coagulation, flocculation, oxidation, photochemical degradation, ion exchange, and membrane filtration, have not gained immense popularity due to their high cost and the requirement for additional chemicals30,31. However, the solid-liquid phase adsorption process, which is the process of enriching target substances from solution to solid surface, has demonstrated notable efficiency32,33. Advantages of the adsorption method include its ease of use, high processing efficiency, versatility in selecting adsorbents, and widespread applicability34. Utilizing polymers to immobilize powder materials into detachable gels is a significant approach to prevent the loss of the adsorbent. An ideal adsorbent should possess certain key characteristics, including high mechanical and chemical stability, an appropriately sized porous structure, selectivity, and a high adsorption capacity35. Hydrogels, characterized by their 3D polymeric lattices36, offer desirable properties such as high adsorption rates, low residual content, porosity, uniform softness, durability, odorlessness, and photostability37. Natural polymer-based gel materials, being environmentally friendly, cost-effective, and biodegradable, have garnered considerable interest38.

Recently a wide range of porous material has been synthesized and explored for different contaminant’s removal. Among them Porous organic polymers/frameworks (POPs/POFs) including intrinsic microporous (PIMs), covalent organic polymers (COPs), porous aromatic frameworks (PAFs) along with metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) are attracting particular attention because of their fascinating properties such as high porosity comprised of a uniform and adjustable pore size, tunable characteristics, the introduction of a wide range of chemical functionalities into the frameworks by post-synthesis treatment facile synthesis (from easily available monomers), and ease of functionalization39,40.

Based on these considerations, we propose a naphthalene-based covalent organic polymer (COP) as a novel adsorbent for the removal of MB from aqueous solutions. Naphthalene offers several advantages, including cost-effectiveness, an abundance of active sites, and its rich electron density41. It also exhibits strong π-electron delocalization, which enhances the composite’s adsorption capabilities. Recent studies have demonstrated naphthalene’s promising applications in purification systems. Furthermore, in this study, the median lethal dose (LD50) of naphthalene was evaluated. After reacting with the COP, the material was washed three times with methanol, acetone, and 1,4-dioxane to remove any unreacted chemicals. This thorough washing ensures the absence of free naphthalene, thereby minimizing potential environmental toxicity. Moreover, in recent years, drug discovery and development, with extensive biological evaluation, have become increasingly important in research. Despite the presence of significant side effects, medicinal chemists have consistently worked to design drug compounds that offer optimal therapeutic benefits with minimal toxicity. The naphthalene core, whether derived from natural sources or synthesized in the lab, has been widely investigated for various biological activities, including anticancer, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, antitubercular, antiviral, and treatments for neurological and cardiovascular disorders. Numerous naphthalene-based compounds have received FDA approval and are currently used in therapeutic applications42. Various characterization techniques, including FT-IR, BET, TGA, XRD and FE-SEM were employed to better identify the synthesized adsorbent. Additionally, parameters significantly influencing MB adsorption from aqueous solutions were thoroughly investigated. Our efforts to examine the effectiveness of COP in addressing these top-priority pollutants are poised to revolutionize household water filters, industrial wastewater treatment systems, and nuclear waste disposal. We anticipate that novel uses and innovative functionalization techniques by combining existing strategies, leading to enhanced selectivity and efficiency in COP applications, thus bolstering water security.

Preparation of covalent organic polymer

A porous organic polymer using triazine as a base can be created by combining cyanuric chloride (CC) with 1,5-dihydroxynaphthalene (DHN) through polycondensation as shown in Fig. 1. In a round-bottom flask, 3.0 mmol of DHN is dissolved in 30 mL of dried 1,4-dioxane, followed by addition of 6.0 mmol of NaOH. Simultaneously, 2.0 mmol of CC is dissolved in 10 mL of 1,4-dioxane to create a CC solution. The two solutions are then mixed and refluxed at 140o C for 36 h under a nitrogen atmosphere. Afterward, the solution is sonicated for 30 min, and the product is filtered. The resulting brownish powder is then washed thrice with methanol, acetone, and 1,4-dioxane to eliminate unreacted chemicals. This innovative approach aligns with current trends in water treatment, emphasizing the need for sustainable materials and processes. Emerging technologies, such as hydrogel particles for micropollutant removal and advanced membrane systems, are setting new standards by being more environmentally friendly and efficient compared to traditional methods like activated carbon filtration. By focusing on the development of materials with high selectivity and lower environmental impact, these advancements contribute significantly to the goal of reducing greenhouse gas emissions and improving water quality on a global scale.

As illustrated in Fig. 2, N-COP is synthesized by linking the aromatic rings of cyanuric chloride to the oxygen groups of 1,5-dihydroxynaphthalene, which have been deprotonated through the reaction with NaOH, leading to the elimination of Cl and Na ions.

In order to estimate the adsorbent’s price, the utilized amounts of each chemical were calculated based on their used amount and molecular weight. As the results show the price of every synthesized batch of the product barely reaches $1.50, highlighting its cost-effectiveness given its high removal capacity43.

Adsorption experiment

The adsorption experiment aimed to evaluate critical parameters influencing the efficiency of dye removal, such as the pH of the solution and contact time among the adsorbent and the pollutant, and varying concentrations of MB. Additionally, adsorption isotherms and kinetics were analyzed to realize the interactions between the synthesized polymer adsorbent and MB dye. A 500 mg/L stock solution was prepared by dissolving 0.05 g of MB dye in a 100 mL volumetric flask, which was then filled to the mark with deionized water. The study systematically tested the effect of different parameters on the adsorption procedure, with operating variables including pH (ranging from 3 to 11), pollutant concentration (5–70 mg/L), and contact time (10–150 min). The pH of the solution was controlled using 0.1 M hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide. Experiments were operated at room temperature. To determine the removal efficiency of MB dye by the porous polymer adsorbent, the samples were settled in a shaker at 250 rpm to ensure thorough mixing among the adsorbent and the MB dye. Following the designated contact time, the samples were passed through a 0.45 μm cellulose filter and examined with a spectrophotometer set to measure MB absorption at 668 nm. Adsorption analysis

Effect of pH on MB removal

The effect of pH on MB removal efficiency was investigated in the limited range of pH 3–10 under constant conditions: a contact time of 30 min, an adsorbent dose of 0.01 g, and a primary MB concentration of 100 mg/L. The results (Fig. 8.a) indicate that the highest adsorption capacity for MB was observed at pH 7. The adsorption capacity of the polymer adsorbent for MB decreased when the solution pH was lowered from 7 to 3 or raised from 7 to 9. This trend suggests that MB adsorption capacity varies in a wave-like manner with changes in solution pH. In summary, the results suggest that adsorption is more efficient under neutral to alkaline conditions, as these pH levels promote stronger electrostatic interactions between the N-COP and MB. At lower pH values, H+ ions likely occupy the adsorption sites on the adsorbent, competing with the positively charged MB molecules for these limited sites. As the pH rises, the concentration of H+ ions decrease, freeing up more binding sites for MB and leads to a less competitive adsorption. Since the adsorption capacity plateaus after pH = 7, the dominant removal mechanism is likely due to electrostatic attraction. Consequently, pH 7 was chosen as the optimal condition for subsequent testing.Fig. 8(a) Effect of pH on MB removal efficiency, b) Effect of contact time on MB removal efficiency and c) Pseudo-second-order kinetic plot of MB adsorption on N-COP.

(a) Effect of pH on MB removal efficiency, b) Effect of contact time on MB removal efficiency and c) Pseudo-second-order kinetic plot of MB adsorption on N-COP.

Effect of contact time

Contact time between the adsorbent and the adsorbate is a crucial parameter in any adsorption process. Investigating this variable helps determine the optimal duration to achieve the highest removal efficiency and equilibrium state. The goal was to identify the best contact time for removing MB. According to the results demonstrated in Fig. 8b, increasing the contact time from 10 to 30 min improved the adsorption capacity of the adsorbent from 42.28 mg/g to 53.45 mg/g. This increase can be ascribed to the extended interaction time between the synthesized polymer and MB, allowing more MB molecules to penetrate the polymer’s pores. However, the adsorption capacity plateaued after 30 min, indicating that equilibrium was reached and no significant changes were observed beyond this point. Therefore, a contact time of 30 min was chosen as the optimal duration for MB adsorption.

Adsorption isotherms for MB

Adsorption isotherms provide equilibrium data that clarify the interaction between the adsorbent and the adsorbate, while also quantifying the adsorption capacity of the adsorbent. In this assay, three adsorption isotherm models-Langmuir, Freundlich, and Temkin-were employed. The results obtained for the adsorption isotherms are summarized in Table 2. Based on the data in Table 2, the Langmuir isotherm model exhibited the most appropriate, with a maximum adsorption capacity of 90.09 mg/g. This suggests that a single-layer, homogeneous adsorption process occurs, with uniform energy distribution across all adsorbent surfaces. Adsorption mechanism

Figure 10 has shown the adsorption of MB onto the N-COP composite that is primarily due to hydrogen bonding between the donor and acceptor groups within the N-COP@MB system. Additionally, MB molecules engage in π-π interactions involving the π electron networks of the N-COP framework and the aromatic rings of the MB dye, complemented by van der Waals forces. Electrostatic interactions also contribute, as the dye’s cationic nature promotes these attractions61,62.

Conclusion

This research presents a pioneering approach to wastewater treatment through the synthesis of a highly efficient naphthalene-based covalent organic polymer (N-COP) designed for the removal of MB from aqueous environments. Detailed characterization confirmed the polymer’s robust structure, with adsorption studies revealing a maximum capacity of 90.09 mg/g, adhering to the Langmuir isotherm model. The adsorption kinetics followed a pseudo-second-order model, suggesting a chemisorption mechanism. N-COP’s superior performance in MB removal, combined with its environmentally benign synthesis, highlights its potential for large-scale water treatment applications. Furthermore, its versatility paves the way for future functionalization and application in the removal of various pollutants. The outcomes of this study not only provide a sustainable and scalable solution for dye-laden wastewater treatment but also contribute to advancing polymer-based adsorbents for broader environmental remediation efforts.

Continuous in time bubbling and Soliton Resolution for Non-negative Solutions of the Energy-Critical Heat Flow
Shrey Aryan

Abstract
. We show that any finite energy solution of the energy-critical nonlinear heat flow in dimensions d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 asymptotically resolves into a sum of possibly time-dependent solitons, a radiation term, and an error term that vanishes in the energy space. As a consequence, when the initial data has finite energy and is non-negative, we settle the Soliton Resolution Conjecture for all dimensions d ≥ 3 . d\geq 3.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Problem Setting

In this work, we study the long-term behavior of solutions to the energy-critical nonlinear heat flow in dimensions
d\geq 3
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u

(1.1)

\displaystyle u(0,x)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(x)\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(1.2)

where
p:=\frac{d+2}{d-2}
. This model arises as the negative gradient flow of the following nonlinear energy functional:

\displaystyle E(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(x)|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x,

(1.3)

which arises naturally in the study of extremizers of the Sobolev inequality and, more generally, is related to the Yamabe problem on the sphere via stereographic projection. The local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm is classical and was initiated by Weissler in
[
57
,
58
]
, with further contributions by Giga
[
25
]
, Ni–Sacks
[
44
]
, and Brezis-Cazenave
[
4
]
. Observe that the solutions of (
1.1
) are invariant under translations and parabolic scaling,

\displaystyle u(t,x)\mapsto u_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}u\left(t/\lambda^{2},x/\lambda\right),\quad\lambda>0.

Since the nonlinear energy is invariant under these symmetries, i.e.,
E(u)=E\left(u_{\lambda}\right)
, the equation (
1.1
) is energy-critical. Testing (
1.1
) against
\partial_{t}u
and integrating by parts, we observe the formal energy identity

\displaystyle E(u(T))+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{~d}t=E(u(0)),

(1.4)

for each
T>0
. In particular, this implies that the nonlinear energy is non-increasing along the flow. Any function
W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
solving the elliptic PDE

\displaystyle\Delta W+|W|^{p-1}W=0

(1.5)

is a stationary solution and will often be referred to as a bubble or soliton of (
1.1
).

Theorem

scale

S=S(d)>0

\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p+1}}\leq S\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}

(1.12)

for all
u\in\dot{H}^{1}.
Then observe that for any non-zero stationary solution
W\in\dot{H}^{1}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla{W}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|W|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x.

(1.13)

By the variational characterization of the Sobolev inequality, the best constant (or equality) is attained by a positive stationary solution
\widetilde{W}
, i.e.,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widetilde{W}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x=S^{p+1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\widetilde{W}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{(p+1)/2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\widetilde{W}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(1.14)

implying that for any positive stationary solution, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\widetilde{W}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=S^{-d}.\end{split}

(1.15)

Therefore, for any sign-changing stationary solution, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla W|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla W^{+}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla W^{-}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x>2S^{-d}.

(1.16)

In particular, we deduce that any non-zero stationary solution
W\in\dot{H}^{1}
satisfies
\|\nabla W\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\geq S^{-d}.

1.3.
Background and Motivation

A fundamental problem in the analysis of nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs) is describing the long-time behavior of their solutions. The Soliton Resolution Conjecture asserts that any finite-energy solution to a nonlinear PDE asymptotically decomposes into a sum of decoupled solitons that are stationary solutions of the underlying equation, a radiation term that behaves like a solution to the linear flow, and an error term that vanishes in the natural energy norm. This conjecture arose from the numerical experiments of Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou
[
23
]
and Zabusky–Kruskal
[
59
]
, which provided evidence that it holds for the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation. Since then, the problem has been extensively studied for the KdV equation as well as for several other integrable models.

Beyond integrable systems, analogues of the Soliton Resolution Conjecture have emerged across various areas of mathematics. In general relativity, the Final State Conjecture (cf.
[
43
]
) predicts that generic solutions to Einstein’s field equations asymptotically approach a finite number of stationary solutions, namely Kerr black holes, moving apart from each other. In geometric analysis, Soliton Resolution arises naturally in the study of gradient flows associated with conformally invariant variational problems. For example, pioneering works of Struwe
[
53
,
54
]
, Qing
[
48
]
, Qing–Tian
[
47
]
, and Hong–Tian
[
29
]
have established bubbling or Soliton Resolution along a well-chosen sequence of times for the harmonic map and Yang–Mills heat flows.

Motivated by these parabolic works, we study the energy-critical nonlinear heat flow in dimension
d\geq 3.
Our main result, Theorem
1.1
, establishes a continuous-in-time bubble-tree decomposition for all
\dot{H}^{1}
bounded solutions of (
1.1
). More precisely, any solution with uniformly bounded
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm decomposes into a sum of solitons that may vary along different time sequences, a radiation term that is asymptotically trivial or captured by a weak limit in
\dot{H}^{1}
, and an error term that vanishes in the energy space. Moreover, when the initial data is non-negative, Corollary
1.3
shows that Theorem
1.1
implies the Soliton Resolution Conjecture, since positive solitons have been classified and are unique up to the symmetries of the equation due to
[
45
,
7
]
. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
extends Struwe’s classical compactness result
[
52
]
, which establishes a similar decomposition only along a well-chosen sequence of times. It also strengthens Theorem 1.4 in
[
30
]
, which proves a similar decomposition for global-in-time solutions with an error term vanishing in the
L^{p+1}
-norm. On the other hand, Corollary
1.3
establishes Soliton Resolution for a non-integrable PDE beyond radial symmetry and without restrictions on the size of the initial data. A similar result has been established by Ishiwata
[
30
]
for global in time solutions, whereas our work covers both finite-time blowup and the global-in-time regime.

To explain the significance of our result, we now review some key developments in the literature. In the integrable setting, where tools such as the inverse scattering transform are available, the conjecture is well understood for models including the KdV equation
[
22
]
, the modified KdV equation
[
49
]
, the one-dimensional cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS)
[
2
]
, the derivative NLS
[
37
]
, and, more recently, the Calogero–Moser derivative NLS
[
42
]
.

For non-integrable equations with radial symmetry, where the solitons do not move in space, the conjecture has been settled for the nonlinear wave equation
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
18
,
38
,
9
,
36
,
34
]
, damped Klein–Gordon equation
[
5
,
26
]
, equivariant self-dual Chern–Simons–Schrödinger equation
[
40
]
, equivariant harmonic map heat flow
[
35
]
, and energy-critical nonlinear heat flow
[
1
]
. The last two works led to the first complete classification of the bubble tree term dynamics for the equivariant harmonic map heat flow and the radial energy-critical nonlinear heat flow by Kim and Merle in
[
41
]
.

For non-integrable equations without radial symmetry, Soliton Resolution is known in one dimension for the damped Klein-Gordon equation
[
6
]
, in the neighborhood of a few solitons for the energy-critical nonlinear heat flow and the damped Klein-Gordon equation
[
10
,
31
,
32
]
, continuously in time for the harmonic map heat flow
[
33
]
or along a sequence of times in dimensions
3\leq d\leq 5
for the energy-critical nonlinear wave equation
[
17
]
.

Regarding (
1.1
) in the energy-critical setting, Wang–Wei
[
56
]
proved that in dimensions
d\geq 7
and for nonnegative initial data
u_{0}\geq 0
, any finite-time blowup must be of Type I, thereby ruling out finite-time Type II blowup in higher dimensions. When
3\leq d\leq 6
, examples of Type II blowup have been constructed, for instance by del Pino–Musso–Wei–Zhang–Zhang
[
14
]
, Schweyer
[
50
]
, Pino–Musso–Wei
[

]
, and Harada
[
28
]
.

In contrast to the existing work, establishing our main results requires working in any dimension
d\geq 3
, where solitons exhibit only weak decay and no longer enjoy radial symmetry, allowing them to translate in space and potentially behave pathologically (cf.
[
15
,
11
,
12
]
). Moreover, we impose no restrictions on the size of the initial data, which implies that the nonlinear energy is, in general, non-coercive, unlike the setting of
[
39
,
27
]
. We overcome these difficulties by introducing new ideas that are robust and adaptable to other nonlinear parabolic flows, as we will show in forthcoming works. In particular, our modified notion of collision intervals, introduced in Section

[

1.4.
Proof Sketch

The proofs of the main Theorems
1.1
and
1.9
build on the framework of
[
33
]
, but require addressing new difficulties that arise in the context of the energy-critical nonlinear heat flow. This includes:

•

Non-coercivity of the energy functional.
The lack of a definite sign for the energy functional (
1.3
), especially in non-radial settings, prevents the use of standard energy estimates (cf.
[
1
]
). To overcome this, we develop new localized energy estimates and use profile decompositions to show that there is no concentration of energy outside the self-similar region, which is a key ingredient in our argument.

•

Absence of energy quantization.
Unlike the case of harmonic maps between the plane and the round two-sphere, solitons for (
1.1
) do not exhibit quantized energy, thereby preventing a direct application of the collision intervals from
[
33
]
. Nevertheless, the existence of a uniform positive lower bound on the energy of any soliton allows us to define suitable collision intervals, which are sufficient to establish our main results.

We first sketch the proof of Theorem
1.9
, which in turn is used to prove Theorem
1.1
. The argument begins by contradiction. Thus, assume that there is a sequence of times along which the solution deviates from a multi-bubble configuration. Unfortunately, it is difficult to analyze this sequence, and so we give ourselves a bit of room and instead analyze a sequence of time intervals where the solution deviates from a multi-bubble configuration; these sequences of intervals are called collision intervals, for a precise definition, see (
3.1
).

Thus, consider
[a_{n},b_{n}]\subset[0,T_{+})
, a sequence of time intervals where near the endpoints
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
,
u(t)
is close to some multi-bubble configuration while inside
[a_{n},b_{n}]
,
u(t)
deviates away from this multi-bubble configuration. We define
K
as the smallest non-negative real number such that, heuristically,
u(a_{n})
is close to a
K
-bubble configuration. Note that defining
K
is straightforward when the energy of each bubble is quantized, as in the case of harmonic maps from
\mathbb{S}^{2}
to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, since we could simply sum up the energies of each bubble arising in the limit when
n\to\infty
. However, in general, sign-changing stationary solutions could attain a continuum of energy, and therefore, defining
K
at first appears to be problematic; however, we observe that as long as each bubble has a minimal energy, one can define
K
in a similar manner.

Next, the idea is to use the minimality of
K
to relate the length of the collision interval to the size of the largest bubble that loses its shape or comes into a collision. In other words, we show that there exists a sub-interval
[c_{n},d_{n}]\subset[a_{n},b_{n}]
and a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\begin{split}|[c_{n},d_{n}]|\geq C_{1}\lambda^{2}_{\max,n}\end{split}

(1.28)

where
\lambda^{2}_{\max,n}
is the largest scale associated with a bubble that comes into a collision. An application of the elliptic bubbling Theorem
2.18
on the interval
[c_{n},d_{n}]
and a contradiction argument yield a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\begin{split}\inf_{t\in[c_{n},d_{n}]}\lambda_{\max,n}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq C_{2}.\end{split}

(1.29)

Combining the above two estimates with (
1.4
) gives

\begin{split}\infty>\int_{0}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{c_{n}}^{d_{n}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\gtrsim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}1=\infty,\end{split}

(1.30)

which is a contradiction, thus completing the proof of Theorem
1.9
.

To go from Theorem
1.9
to Theorem
1.1
, one key ingredient is to establish no concentration of energy outside the self-similar region. This property is expected to be true in general for a broad class of energy-critical PDEs; however, there are no general techniques to establish such results. We proved this in the radial case
[
1
]
using the decay coming from the radial Sobolev embedding; however, new arguments are needed in the non-radial setting. To this end, we develop a new local well-posedness theory for (
1.1
) inspired by the work of Struwe on the harmonic map heat flow on Riemannian Surfaces (cf.
[
53
]
). The advantage of working in this category of solutions is that it allows us to estimate appropriate norms of the nonlinear terms that arise in the energy estimates that are required to rule out concentration of energy outside the self-similar region when
T_{+}<\infty
. Surprisingly, the case when
T_{+}=\infty
is harder since we need to control nonlinear terms arising in the energy estimates on an unbounded spatial domain. Here we observe that, given
\phi\in C^{\infty}
we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}|u|^{p+1}\right)\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p+1}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(1.31)

Therefore, to show that no energy lives outside the ball
B(y,\sqrt{t})
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it suffices to show that the localized nonlinear energy and the
L^{p+1}
-norm vanish in this region. By localizing (
1.4
), it is not difficult to show that the first quantity vanishes. On the other hand, the vanishing of
L^{p+1}
norm is quite involved and, in particular, relies on a deep work by Ishiwata
[
30
]
, see Lemma
2.16
.

Now let
t_{n}\to T_{+}
be any sequence of times. From Theorem
1.9
, we see that
u(t_{n})
approaches
K
multi-bubble configuration on either
B(y,\sqrt{T_{+}-t_{n}})
when
T_{+}<\infty
or
B(y,\sqrt{t}_{n})
when
T_{+}=\infty.
In particular, the
K
multi-bubble configurations depend on
n
. To obtain a finite number of bubbles (independent of
n
) as in Theorem
1.1
that are asymptotically orthogonal in the sense of (
1.6
) and (
1.8
), we apply the Compactness Theorem
2.18
to each bubble obtained in the sequence of multi-bubble configurations arising from Theorem
1.9
and build a new bubble tree configuration by selecting bubbles such that (
1.6
) and (
1.8
) are satisfied. The resulting multi-bubble configuration then satisfies all the requirements of Theorem
1.1
, thus completing the proof.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Properties of Stationary Solutions

In this section, we recall some standard properties of non-zero solutions to (
1.5
), show that the definitions of scale and center (see Definition
1.4
and
1.5
) are well-defined, and establish some natural consequences of these definitions.
Let
W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-zero finite energy solution of (
1.5
). We will show that the definition of its scale
\lambda(W;\gamma_{0})
and center
a(W;\gamma_{0})
is well-defined.

Lemma 2.1

(Center and scale)
.

Let
\gamma_{0}\in(0,\bar{E}_{*}/2)
, let
W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-zero stationary solution, let
\lambda(W)=\lambda(W;\gamma_{0})
be its scale from Definition
1.4
and let
a(W)=a(W;\gamma_{0})
be a choice of center from Definition
1.5
. Then
\lambda(W)
is uniquely defined and strictly positive, and
a(W)
is well-defined. For all
(b,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
we have

\begin{split}\lambda\left(W[b,\mu]\right)=\lambda(W)\mu,\text{ and }\left|a\left(W[b,\mu]\right)-b-a(W)\mu\right|\leq 2\lambda(W)\mu.\end{split}

(2.1)

Proof.

Since
\bar{E}(W;B(0,R))\to\bar{E}(W)
as
R\to\infty
, the scale
\lambda(W)
is well-defined. If
\lambda(W)=0
, then there exists
a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
so that for
n\geq 1
we have

\begin{split}\bar{E}(W;B(a_{n},1/n))\geq\bar{E}(W)-\gamma_{0}.\end{split}

(2.2)

If
n\neq m
, then the balls
B(a_{n},1/n),B(a_{m},1/m)
must intersect. This is because,

\begin{split}\bar{E}(W)\geq\bar{E}(W;B(a_{n},1/n))+\bar{E}(W;B(a_{m},1/m))\geq 2\bar{E}(W)-2\gamma_{0}\end{split}

(2.3)

which would imply that
\bar{E}(W)\leq 2\gamma_{0}<\bar{E}_{*}
. This contradicts the fact that
W
is non-zero. Therefore,
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
a_{n}\to a_{\infty}
. Passing to the limit in (
2.2
) gives a contradiction.
To see that the center
a(W)
is well-defined, take
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda(W)
and
a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\bar{E}(W;B(a_{n},\lambda_{n}))\geq\bar{E}(W)-\gamma_{0}.

By a similar argument as before, we deduce that no two balls in the sequence
\{B(a_{n},\lambda_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are disjoint. Therefore, the sequence
a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
lies in a compact set and we may assume that
a_{n}\to a_{\infty}
as
n\to\infty
, which is the desired center. The properties (
2.1
) follow from the definition.
∎

Lemma 2.2

(Decay of stationary solutions)
.

There exists
\gamma_{0}\in(0,\bar{E}_{*}/2)
with the following property. For any
0<\gamma\leq\gamma_{0}
and any non-zero stationary solution
W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
the exterior energy decays at the following rate:

\begin{split}\bar{E}(W;\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(a(W;\gamma),R\lambda(W;\gamma)))\leq\frac{C}{R^{d-2}}\end{split}

(2.4)

for all
R\geq 1
with constant
C=C(d,W)>0.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
a(W;\gamma)=0
and
\lambda(W;\gamma)=1
. Then using Lemma 2.1 in
[
46
]
we get precise asymptotics of
|\nabla W|
, which implies the desired estimate since

\displaystyle\bar{E}(W;\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,R))

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,R)^{c}}|\nabla W|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{R}^{\infty}\frac{r^{d-1}}{(1+r)^{2d-2}}\mathrm{d}r\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{d-2}}.

(2.5)

∎

Lemma 2.3

(Energy of multi-bubbles)
.

Let
(y_{n},\rho_{n},M)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{N}
. Let
\{W_{1},\dots,W_{M}\}
be a collection of non-zero stationary solutions, and for each
j\in\{1,\dots,M\}
let
(b_{n,j},\mu_{n,j})\in B(y_{n},\rho_{n})\times(0,\infty)
be sequences such that

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\Bigg[\sum_{j\neq k}\bigg(\frac{\mu_{n,j}}{\mu_{n,k}}+\frac{\mu_{n,k}}{\mu_{n,j}}&+\frac{|b_{n,j}-b_{n,k}|}{\mu_{n,j}}\bigg)^{-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{\mu_{n,j}}{\operatorname{dist}(b_{n,j},\partial B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))}\Bigg]=0.\end{split}

(2.6)

Then

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}\Big(\mathbf{W}\big(W_{1}[b_{n,1},\mu_{n,1}],\dots,W_{M}[b_{n,M},\mu_{n,M}]\big);B(y_{n},\rho_{n})\Big)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bar{E}(W_{j}).\end{split}

(2.7)

Proof.

To simplify the notation within the proof, we use the shorthand
W_{n,j}=W_{j}[b_{n,j},\mu_{n,j}]
for each
1\leq j\leq M.
Expanding the energy, we obtain

\begin{split}\bar{E}(\mathbf{W}(W_{n,1},\dots,W_{n,M});B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))&=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bar{E}(W_{n,j};B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))+\sum_{j\neq k}\int_{B(y_{n},\rho_{n})}(\nabla W_{n,j}\cdot\nabla W_{n,k})\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

By the asymptotic orthogonality of the parameters in (
2.6
), Lemma
2.2
and the invariance of the
\dot{H}^{1}
norm under translation and rescaling we get

\bar{E}(W_{n,j};B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))=\bar{E}(W_{n,j})+o_{n}(1)=\bar{E}(W_{j})+o_{n}(1)

and for
j\neq k

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{B(y_{n},\rho_{n})}(\nabla W_{n,j}\cdot\nabla W_{n,k})\mathrm{d}x\Big|&\leq\int|\nabla W_{n,j}||\nabla W_{n,k}|\mathrm{d}x=o_{n}(1)\end{split}

(2.8)

by (
2.6
). Combining the above two displays, we get the desired energy expansion.
∎

2.2.
Local well-posedness of the nonlinear heat flow

Following the ideas of Struwe
[
53
]
, we develop a local well-posedness theory that is well-adapted to the bubbling analysis, which we will carry out later. We first define the function space on which we develop the local well-posedness theory in the space of finite energy solutions:

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{\tau}^{T,M}:=\Big\{&u:[\tau,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\mid\,u\,\,\textrm{is measurable, and }\\
&\quad\int_{\tau}^{T}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,\sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}\bar{E}(u(t))\leq M\Big\}\end{split}

(2.9)

For convenience, we denote
\mathcal{V}^{T,M}=\mathcal{V}_{0}^{T,M}
. Note that the explicit bound on the energy here is inserted in order for us to have a uniform compactness for a sequence of functions in
\mathcal{V}^{T,M}_{\tau}
, and this bound cannot be a priori deduced from the fact that the initial data is in
\dot{H}^{1}.
The main theorem in this section is as follows:

Theorem 2.4

(Local well-posedness)
.

Let
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
. Then, there exist a maximal time of existence
T_{+}=T_{+}(u_{0})
and a unique solution
u\in\bigcap_{T<T_{+}}\mathcal{V}^{T,M}
to (
1.1
) with
u(0)=u_{0}
for some
M=M(u_{0})>0
that depends on the initial data
u_{0}.
Assume that we are in the type-II regime, i.e.

\begin{split}\limsup_{t\to T_{+}}\bar{E}(u(t))\leq C_{1}<+\infty.\end{split}

(2.10)

Then the finite maximal time
T_{+}<\infty
is characterized by the existence of
R_{0}>0
, an integer
L\geq 1
, a number
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, and points
\{x_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\begin{split}\limsup_{t\to T_{+}}\bar{E}(u(t);\mathcal{B}(x_{\ell},R))\geq\varepsilon_{0},\quad\forall\ R\in(0,R_{0}],\quad\forall 1\leq\ell\leq L.\end{split}

(2.11)

and the collection of the bubbling points
\{x_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is finite. There exists a finite energy mapping
u^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
u(t)\rightharpoonup u^{*}
as
t\to T_{+}
weakly in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and strongly in
\dot{H}^{1}(K)
for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{x_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}.

The nonlinear energy
E(u(t))
is continuous and non-increasing as a function of
t\in[0,T_{+})
, and for any
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\in[0,T_{+})
, there holds

\begin{split}E(u(t_{2}))+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=E(u(t_{1})).\end{split}

(2.12)

In particular,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\sup_{t\in[0,T_{+}]}(\bar{E}(u(t))+\bar{E}(u(t))^{2^{*}/2})<+\infty.\end{split}

(2.13)

We first prove some preliminary estimates that will be needed in proving Theorem
2.4
. We start with an estimate to control the nonlinear term.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\dot{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any smooth cut-off function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(x,2R))
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{2p}\phi^{2p}dx\lesssim\bar{E}(u;B(x,2R))^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\left(\int_{\operatorname{supp}\phi}|D^{2}u|^{2}\phi^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\bar{E}(u;B(x,2R))\right)

(2.14)

where the constant in the above inequality is independent of
R>0
.

Proof.

By the Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\theta}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\theta}

(2.15)

where
\theta=\frac{d-2}{d+2}.
Localizing this estimate by using a standard cut-off function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(x,2R))
with
\phi\equiv 1
on
B(x,R)
with
|\nabla\phi|\leq CR^{-1}
and using Hardy’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u\phi|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{2}(u\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\|\nabla(u\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2p(1-\theta)}

(2.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim\bar{E}(u;B(x,2R))^{\frac{4}{d-2}}\left(\int_{\operatorname{supp}\phi}|D^{2}u|^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\bar{E}(u;B(x,2R))\right).

(2.17)

∎

Next, we will need some energy estimates to propagate energy at short time scales.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{V}^{T,M}
be a solution of (
1.1
). Consider
I\subset[0,T)
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and

we have

\displaystyle E_{\phi}(u(t_{2}))-E_{\phi}(u(t_{1}))

\displaystyle=-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-2\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi)\phi\partial_{t}u\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t,

(2.18)

\displaystyle\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(t_{2}))-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(t_{1}))

\displaystyle=-2\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+2\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p-1}u(\partial_{t}u)\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

(2.19)

\displaystyle\quad-4\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi)\phi\partial_{t}u\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

(2.20)

Furthermore, we have the following estimates:

\displaystyle\left|E_{\phi}(u(t_{2}))-E_{\phi}(u(t_{1}))\right|\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

(2.21)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2},

(2.22)

\displaystyle\left|\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(t_{2}))-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(t_{1}))\right|\leq 2\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

(2.23)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{2p}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}

(2.24)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+4\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2},

(2.25)

(2.27)

\displaystyle\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(t_{2}))-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(t_{1}))\leq 2\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{2p}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}

(2.28)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+4\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2},

(2.29)

\displaystyle\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(t_{2}))-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(t_{1}))\leq 4\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{2p}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+4\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

(2.30)

Proof.

The first identity (
2.18
) follows from

\begin{split}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\bf e}(u(t))\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t&=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}u-|u|^{p-1}u\partial_{t}u)\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\operatorname{div}(\nabla u\partial_{t}u)-(\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u)\partial_{t}u)\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&=-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-2\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi)\phi\partial_{t}u\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(2.31)

The identity (
2.20
) can be derived similarly. The remaining inequalities (
2.22
), (
2.25
), (
2.27
), (
2.29
), and (
2.30
) follow by applications of Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For any solution

of (

) we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{\mathrm{t}}u\right|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq E(u_{0})-E(u(T))\leq C_{1}M+C_{2}M^{\frac{p+1}{2}}

Multiplying (

\displaystyle E(u(T))+\int_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}=E(u(0)).

(2.33)

Thus, in the interval

using Sobolev inequality we have,

\displaystyle\int_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\leq E(u(0))-E(u(T))\leq C_{1}M+C_{2}M^{\frac{p+1}{2}}

(2.34)

where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are constants depending only on the dimension
d.

Lemma 2.9
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that the following holds. Let
u\in\mathcal{V}^{T,M}_{\tau}
be a smooth solution of (
1.1
) on the domain
B(x,2R)\times[\tau,T]
. If

\begin{split}\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq T}\bar{E}\left(u(t);B(x,2R)\right)<\varepsilon_{1}\end{split}

(2.37)

then for any smooth cut-off function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(x,2R))
we have

\begin{split}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|D^{2}u\right|^{2}\phi^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\phi^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq C\varepsilon_{1}\left(1+\frac{T-\tau}{R^{2}}\right),\end{split}

(2.38)

where the constant
C>0
depends only on the dimension
d.

Proof.

Suppose

\begin{split}\sup_{\tau\leq t\leq T}\bar{E}\left(u(t);B(x,2R)\right)<\varepsilon_{1},\end{split}

(2.39)

where
\varepsilon_{1}
will be chosen later. Then from (
2.18
) we get

\begin{split}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\phi^{2p}dxdt&\leq|E_{\phi^{p}}(u(\tau))|+|E_{\phi^{p}}(u(T))|+\frac{C}{R^{2}}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq C\varepsilon_{1}\left(1+\frac{T-\tau}{R^{2}}\right),\end{split}

(2.40)

where in the last inequality we used the Sobolev inequality to bound the first two terms, and we choose
0<\varepsilon_{1}\ll 1
. Furthermore, using integration by parts twice implies

\begin{split}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}\left|D^{2}u\right|^{2}\phi^{2p}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t&\leq C\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}|\Delta u|^{2}\phi^{2p}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{C}{R^{2}}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\operatorname{supp}\phi}|\nabla u|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq C\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}|\Delta u|^{2}\phi^{2p}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C\varepsilon_{1}\frac{T-\tau}{R^{2}}.\end{split}

(2.41)

Testing the nonlinear heat equation (
1.1
) by
\phi^{2p}\Delta u
we get

\begin{split}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}\phi^{2p}|\Delta u|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{1}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}\phi^{2p}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C_{2}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}\phi^{2p}|u|^{2p}\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\end{split}

(2.42)

where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are some constants independent of
d,M,R,\tau
and
T
. Then Lemma
2.5
implies that

\begin{split}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}|u|^{2p}\phi^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq C\varepsilon_{1}^{p-1}\left(\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}\left|D^{2}u\right|^{2}\phi^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{T-\tau}{R^{2}}\varepsilon_{1}\right).\end{split}

(2.43)

Thus, we get

\begin{split}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}\left|D^{2}u\right|^{2}\phi^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t&\leq C\varepsilon_{1}^{p-1}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{B(x,2R)}\left|D^{2}u\right|^{2}\phi^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\quad+C\varepsilon_{1}\left(1+\frac{T-\tau}{R^{2}}\right)+C\varepsilon_{1}^{p}\left(1+\frac{T-\tau}{R^{2}}\right).\end{split}

(2.44)

Therefore, if
C\varepsilon_{1}^{p-1}\leq 1/2
, then we can absorb the first term and complete the proof.

Lemma 2.10
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that the following holds. For a smooth solution
u
of (
1.1
) on
B(x,2R)\times\left[T-2\delta R^{2},T\right]
for some
\delta\in(0,1)
, if

\begin{split}\sup_{T-2\delta R^{2}\leq t\leq T}\bar{E}\left(u(t);B(x,2R)\right)<\varepsilon_{1}\end{split}

(2.45)

then, for any
t\in\left[T-\delta R^{2},T\right]
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{B(x,R)}\left|D^{2}u(t)\right|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x+\int_{B(x,R)}\left|\partial_{t}u(t)\right|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C\varepsilon_{1}}{R^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\right).\end{split}

(2.46)

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon_{1}
be the same as in Lemma
2.9
and suppose that
u
satisfies

\begin{split}\sup_{T-2\delta R^{2}\leq t\leq T}\bar{E}\left(u(t);B(x,2R)\right)<\varepsilon_{1}.\end{split}

(2.47)

Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(B(x,3R/2)\right)
be a smooth cut-off function such that
\psi\equiv 1
on
B(x,R)
and
\psi\equiv 0
outside
B(x,3R/2).
Then differentiating (
1.1
) with respect to
t
yields

\begin{split}\partial_{t}^{2}u=\Delta\partial_{t}u+p|u|^{p-1}\partial_{t}u.\end{split}

(2.48)

Multiplying the above display with
\psi^{2}\partial_{t}u
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
gives

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}u\Delta\partial_{t}u\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}|u|^{p-1}\mathrm{d}x\\
=&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\partial_{t}u\cdot\nabla\psi\partial_{t}u\psi\mathrm{d}x\\
&+p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}|u|^{p-1}\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

(2.49)

where we use integration by parts for the last step. By applying Young’s inequality to various terms, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}|u|^{p-1}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

(2.50)

for some constant
C>0
that depend on the dimension
d.
Therefore, we have

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}|u|^{p-1}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x.\qquad\end{split}

(2.51)

Now, using Hölder’s inequality, we estimate the last term of the above inequality to get

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}|u|^{p-1}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\left(\int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi}|u|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{d+2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\psi\right|^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}\end{split}

(2.52)

with exponents
\alpha=\frac{2p}{p-1}=\frac{d+2}{2}
and
\beta=\frac{2p}{p+1}=\frac{d+2}{d}.

By Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality, for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2\beta}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\leq C\left\|Df\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{\theta}\|f\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}^{1-\theta}

(2.53)

where
\theta=\frac{d}{d+2}=\frac{1}{\beta}
and thus

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\psi\right|^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}&\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|\nabla\partial_{t}u|^{2}\psi^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\theta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1-\theta}.\end{split}

(2.54)

Hence, using Young’s inequality, for any
\eta\in(0,1)
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}|u|^{p-1}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi}|u|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{d+2}}\left(\int|\partial_{t}u|^{2}|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{d+2}}\left(\int(|\nabla\partial_{t}u|^{2}\psi^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}|\partial_{t}u|^{2})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\eta}\int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi}|u|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\eta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\eta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(2.55)

By taking sufficiently small
\eta
, we can absorb the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\partial_{t}u\right|\psi^{2}

) to get the following differential inequality

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{1}\int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi}|u|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+C_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(2.56)

for some dimension dependent constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0.
If we solve this differential inequality on the interval

, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u(t)\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad\leq\exp\left(C_{1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi}|u|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\left(t_{0}\right)\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u(t)\right|^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right).\end{split}

(2.57)

Using Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.9
with
\phi\in C^{\infty}\left(B(x,2R)\right)
such that
\phi\equiv 1
on
B(x,3R/2)
we get

\begin{split}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\operatorname{supp}\psi}|u|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t&\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{B(x,3R/2)}|u|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\operatorname{supp}\phi}|u|^{2p}\phi^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\

(2.58)

Therefore, if we pick
\varepsilon_{1}^{p}\ll 1
small enough then it follows from that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u(t)\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\left(t_{0}\right)\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\right|^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

For any
t\in\left[T-\delta R^{2},T\right]

t_{0}\in\left[t-\delta R^{2},t\right]

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u\left(t_{0}\right)\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\min_{s\in\left[t-\delta R^{2},t\mid\right.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}u(s)\right|^{2}\psi^{2}\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

(2.60)

Then from Lemma
2.9
implies that for

,

(2.65)

∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{V}^{T,M}
be two solutions of (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0},v_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
such that
u_{0}\equiv v_{0}
. Then
u\equiv v
on
[0,T]
.

Proof.

Set
w:=u-v
. Then
w
solves

\begin{split}\partial_{t}w=\Delta w+(|u|^{p-1}u-|v|^{p-1}v).\end{split}

(2.68)

We show
\nabla w\equiv 0
which in turn implies that
w\equiv 0.
By multiplying the above equation with
\Delta w
we get

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|u|^{p-1}u-|v|^{p-1}v)\Delta w\mathrm{d}x.

(2.69)

Hence, by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leq\||u|^{p-1}u-|v|^{p-1}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta w(t)\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\||u|^{p-1}w\|_{L^{2}}+\||v|^{p-1}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta w(t)\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

(2.70)

since

\begin{split}||a|^{p-1}a-|b|^{p-1}b|\lesssim(|a|^{p-1}+|b|^{p-1})|a-b|\end{split}

(2.71)

for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}.
Using Hölder with exponents
\frac{2p}{p-1}
and
2p
we get

\begin{split}\||u|^{p-1}w\|_{L^{2}}\leq\||u|^{p-1}\|_{L^{2p/(p-1)}}\|w\|_{L^{2p}}=\|u\|_{L^{2p}}^{p-1}\|w\|_{L^{2p}},\end{split}

(2.72)

and similarly with
v
. Thus

\||u|^{p-1}u-|v|^{p-1}v\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim A(t)\|w\|_{L^{2p}},\quad A(t):=\|u(t)\|_{L^{2p}}^{p-1}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{2p}}^{p-1}.

(2.73)

By the Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality with
\theta=\frac{1}{p}
we have

\|w(t)\|_{L^{2p}}\lesssim\|\Delta w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\theta}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta}.

(2.74)

Therefore, the above display combined with (
2.73
) gives

\||u|^{p-1}u-|v|^{p-1}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq CA(t)\|\Delta w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\theta}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta}.

(2.75)

Using (
2.70
) and (
2.75
):

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq CA(t)\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta}\|\Delta w\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\theta}.

By Young’s inequality with
\varepsilon=1/2
,

CA(t)\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta}\|\Delta w\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\theta}\leq\varepsilon\|\Delta w\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C^{\prime}A(t)^{q}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Thus, we can absorb the first term in the RHS of the above display to get

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim A(t)^{q}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(2.76)

Since
u,v\in\mathcal{V}^{T,M}
, the integral
\int_{0}^{T}A(s)^{q}\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}(\|u(s)\|_{2p}^{2p}+\|v(s)\|_{2p}^{2p})\mathrm{d}s
is finite for all
t\in[0,T].
Thus using (
2.76
) we get

\begin{split}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\nabla w(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{t}(\|u(s)\|_{2p}^{2p}+\|v(s)\|_{2p}^{2p})\mathrm{d}s\right),\end{split}

(2.77)

which implies that
\nabla w(t)=0
for any
t\in[0,T]
. Since
w(t)\in\dot{H}^{1}
, this implies that
w\equiv 0
or
u=v
for any
t\in[0,T]
.
∎

The previous results allow us to prove Theorem
2.4
.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

We split the argument into two cases, one when
u_{0}
is smooth and the other when
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

First, consider the case when the initial map
u_{0}
is smooth. By standard parabolic theory (cf.
[
4
,
10
,
27
]

u
in
\mathcal{V}^{T,M}
for some

M_{R}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},0\leq t<T}\bar{E}\left(u(t);B(x,R)\right)

for some

2.11
the Hölder norm of

and its derivative are uniformly bounded, and hence

can be extended beyond the time
T
. Let
T>0
be the maximal time for which

holds for some

. Then there are some points
x_{i}
for which

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bar{E}\left(u(t);B\left(x_{i},R\right)\right)\geq\varepsilon_{1}\quad\text{ for any }R>0.\end{split}

(2.78)

For any finite collection
\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{k}
of such points and for any
R>0
for which
B(x_{i},2R)
are disjoint balls, we can choose
t_{i}\in[\tau,T]

\begin{split}\int_{B(x_{i},R)}|\nabla u(t_{i})|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\geq\bar{E}(u\left(t_{i});B(x_{i},R)\right)\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}\end{split}

(2.79)

where
\tau=T-\delta R^{2}
for

small enough. Then, since

\begin{split}M&\geq\sup_{[0,T]}\bar{E}(u(t))\geq\sup_{[0,T]}\bar{E}(u(t);\cup_{i=1}^{k}B(x_{i},R))\\

which implies that

Moreover, for any compact subset

\begin{split}\sup_{x\in K,0\leq t<T}\bar{E}\left(u(t);B(x,R)\right)<\varepsilon_{1}.\end{split}

(2.81)

Hence, by Lemma
2.11
, we can extend our solution smoothly beyond the time
T
on

1.1
) exists on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\backslash\left\{\left(x_{i},T\right)\right\}_{i=1,\ldots,k}
.

Now consider the general case when the initial map
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\bar{E}(u_{m0})\leq 2\bar{E}(u_{0})
. For each smooth initial data
u_{m0}
, there is a solution
u_{m}
of (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{m0}
on
\mathcal{V}^{T_{m},M}
for some maximal values

and some

which is uniform in

by the fact that
\bar{E}(u_{m0})\leq 2\bar{E}(u_{0}).
Pick

\begin{split}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{E}(u_{m0};B(x,2R))\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}.\end{split}

(2.82)

By Corollary
2.8

0<T\leq\min(T_{m},\varepsilon_{1}R^{2}/(C^{\prime}M))

\begin{split}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},0\leq t<T}\bar{E}(u_{m}(t);B(x,R))&\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{E}(u_{m0};B(x,2R))+\frac{C^{\prime}TM_{x,2R}}{R^{2}}\\
&\quad+C^{\prime}\varepsilon_{1}^{p-1}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{2}u|^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{C^{\prime}T\varepsilon_{1}^{p}}{R^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}+\frac{C^{\prime}T\varepsilon_{1}}{2R^{2}}+C^{\prime}\varepsilon_{1}^{p}\left(1+\frac{T}{R^{2}}\right)+\frac{C^{\prime}T}{R^{2}}\varepsilon_{1}^{p}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}\leq\varepsilon_{1}\end{split}

(2.83)

for

and

Therefore by Lemma
2.11
, the Hölder norm of

and its derivatives are uniformly bounded independent of

and we have
T_{m}\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}R^{2}}{C^{\prime}C_{1}}
for all

because of the maximality of

. Moreover, by taking the limit of

as

of (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}
, which is indeed smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T)
and satisfies

\begin{split}\sup_{0\leq t<T}\bar{E}(u(t))\leq M.\end{split}

(2.84)

Now let
T_{+}
be the maximal time of existence of a smooth solution
u
to (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T)
. Then, using the above uniform energy bound and the same argument in the case when
u_{0}
is smooth, we can show that the number of singular points is finite and bounded by a constant depending on
M.
Uniqueness of the solutions follows from Lemma
2.12
, while (
2.12
) follows from (

∎

As a consequence, we can prove the following stability result.

Lemma 2.13

(Short-time propagation of small energy)
.

Let
u(t)
be a solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
u(0)=u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
. Let
T_{+}=T_{+}(u_{0})
denote its maximal time of existence and assume that
\sup_{t\in[0,T_{+})}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}<\infty.
Let
0<\sigma_{n}<\tau_{n}<T_{+}
be two sequences of times such that
\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}\to T_{+}
as
n\to\infty
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\tau_{n}-\sigma_{n})=0
. Let
W
be a stationary solution (possibly zero) and let
r_{n}>0
be a sequence such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\tau_{n}-\sigma_{n})r_{n}^{-2}=0
and
r_{n}<R_{0}
, where
R_{0}
is defined in Theorem
2.4
. If

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(\sigma_{n})-W;B(0,2r_{n}))=0,\end{split}

(2.85)

then

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(\tau_{n})-W;B(0,r_{n}))=0.\end{split}

(2.86)

Next, let
\varepsilon_{n}>0
be a sequence with
\varepsilon_{n}<r_{n}
for all
n
and such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\tau_{n}-\sigma_{n})\varepsilon_{n}^{-2}=0
. Let
L\in\mathbb{N}
,
L\geq 1
,
\{x_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the balls
B(x_{\ell},\varepsilon_{n})
are disjoint and satisfy
B(x_{\ell},\varepsilon_{n})\subset B(0,r_{n})
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\ell\in\{1,\dots,L\}
. Moreover,
|x_{\ell}-x_{m}|\geq 5\varepsilon_{n}
when
\ell\neq m
. If

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(\sigma_{n})-W;B(0,2r_{n})\setminus\cup_{\ell=1}^{L}B(x_{\ell},\varepsilon_{n}/2))=0,\end{split}

(2.87)

then

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(\tau_{n})-W;B(0,r_{n})\setminus\cup_{\ell=1}^{L}B(x_{\ell},\varepsilon_{n}))=0.\end{split}

(2.88)

Proof.

We prove (
2.86
). Set
v(t):=u(t)-W
. Then,

\begin{split}\partial_{t}v-\Delta v=|u|^{p-1}u-|W|^{p-1}W.\end{split}

(2.89)

Then using the same idea as in (
2.27
) with a smooth cut-off function
\phi_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported on
B(0,2r_{n})
we get

\displaystyle\bar{E}_{\phi}(v(\tau_{n}))

\displaystyle\lesssim\bar{E}(v(\sigma_{n});B(0,2r_{n}))+\frac{(\tau_{n}-\sigma_{n})^{1/2}}{r_{n}}\left(\int_{\sigma_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}

(2.90)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\int_{\sigma_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\sigma_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\int_{B(0,2r_{n})}|u|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}

(2.91)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\int_{\sigma_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\sigma_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\int_{B(0,2r_{n})}|W|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}.

(2.92)

By Lemma
2.5
and using

we get

\begin{split}\int_{\sigma_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\int_{B(0,2r_{n})}|u|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\left(\int_{\sigma_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\int_{B(0,2r_{n})}|D^{2}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{(\tau_{n}-\sigma_{n})}{r_{n}^{2}}C_{1}\right)<\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty,\end{split}

(2.93)

where the constant in the above inequality depends on the

Next, using the decay of any stationary solution

from Lemma 2.1 in
[

and

\begin{split}\int_{\sigma_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\int_{B(0,2r_{n})}|W|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim(\tau_{n}-\sigma_{n})\to 0,\text{ as }n\to\infty.\end{split}

(2.94)

Finally, using the energy identity (

)

\begin{split}\int_{\sigma_{n}}^{\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{\sigma_{n}}^{T_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\to 0,\text{ as }n\to\infty.\end{split}

(2.95)

2.3.
Concentration properties of the heat flow

The goal of this section is to establish a crucial fact that energy cannot concentrate outside the self-similar scale, which is expected in type-II blowup scenario. Similar results are known for many other PDEs, for instance, energy-critical nonlinear wave equation
[
17
]
, wave maps
[
8
,
51
]
, and harmonic map heat flow
[
33
]
. Due to the lack of finite speed of propagation, we cannot use the techniques developed for hyperbolic equations, while the lack of a coercive energy for the energy-critical heat flow (
1.1
) prevents us from using the arguments developed for the harmonic map heat flow when
T_{+}=\infty.

Lemma 2.14

(No self-similar nonlinear energy concentration in the finite-time blowup case)
.

Let
u(t)
be a solution of (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
such that the maximal time of existence
T_{+}=T_{+}(u_{0})<\infty
and
\sup_{t\in[0,T_{+})}\bar{E}(u(t))<\infty.
Let
x_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
be a singular point as in (
2.11
) and let
r>0
be sufficiently small such that
B(x_{0},2r)\cap(\mathcal{S}\setminus\{x_{0}\})=\emptyset
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\bar{E}(u(t);B(x_{0},r)\setminus B(x_{0},\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-t}))=\bar{E}(u^{*};B(x_{0},r))

(2.96)

for any
\alpha>0
. Here,
u^{*}
denotes the weak limit of the flow, i.e.
u(t)\rightharpoonup u^{*}
as
t\to T_{+}.
In particular, there exist
T_{0}<T_{+}
and functions
\nu,\xi:[T_{0},T_{+})\to(0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{t\to T_{+}}(\nu(t)+\xi(t))=0
and the following hold

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\Big(\frac{\xi(t)}{\sqrt{T_{+}-t}}+\frac{\sqrt{T_{+}-t}}{\nu(t)}\Big)=0,\quad\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\bar{E}(u(t);B(x_{0},\nu(t))\setminus B(x_{0},\xi(t)))=0.

(2.97)

Proof.

Consider a smooth radial cut-off function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(x_{0},2r_{2})\setminus B(x_{0},r_{1}/2))
such that
\phi\equiv 1
on
B(x_{0},r_{2})\setminus B(x_{0},r_{1})
and
\phi\equiv 0
outside
B(x_{0},2r_{2})\setminus B(x_{0},r_{1}/2)
for any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}\leq r/2.
Using (
2.20
), we see that for each
0<s<\tau<T_{+}
we get

\begin{split}\Big|\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(\tau))-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s))\Big|&\leq\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}+\left(\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{2p}\phi^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\quad+4\left(\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim\int_{s}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\left(\int_{s}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}+\frac{(T_{+}-s)^{1/2}}{r_{1}}\left(\int_{s}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}.\end{split}

(2.98)

In the last inequality we observe that the nonlinear term is finite since we can cover
\operatorname{supp}(\phi)\subset\bigcup_{i\in I}B(x_{i},R/2)
for finite
I\subset\mathbb{N}
,
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and we fix
R=\min(R_{0},r_{1}/4)
and choose
s_{0}<T_{+}
such that for all
s\in[s_{0},T_{+})
and for all
\tau\in[s,T_{+})
we have
\sup_{t\in[s,\tau]}\bar{E}(u(t);\cup_{i\in I}B(x_{i},R))<\varepsilon_{1}.
Let
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\cup_{i\in I}B(x_{i},R))
be a cut-off function on this partition. Therefore, by the finite energy assumption, Lemma
2.5
and following the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
2.9
we get

\begin{split}&\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{2p}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{\operatorname{supp}(\phi)}|u|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{2p}|\xi|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&\lesssim\sup_{t\in[s,\tau]}\bar{E}(u(t);\cup_{i\in I}B(x_{i},R))^{p-1}\left(\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{2}u|^{2}|\xi|^{2p}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\tau-s}{R^{2}}\bar{E}(u(t);\cup_{i\in I}B(x_{i},R))\right)\\
&\lesssim\sup_{t\in[s,\tau]}\bar{E}(u(t);\cup_{i\in I}B(x_{i},R))^{p-1}\left(\bar{E}(u(t);\cup_{i\in I}B(x_{i},R))+\frac{\tau-s}{R^{2}}\bar{E}(u(t);\cup_{i\in I}B(x_{i},R))\right)\\
&\lesssim\sup_{t\in[s,\tau]}\bar{E}(u(t))^{p}\left(1+\frac{T_{+}-s}{R^{2}}\right)\lesssim C_{1}^{p}\left(1+\frac{T_{+}-s}{R^{2}}\right)<\infty.\end{split}

(2.99)

If we choose
r_{1}\simeq\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-s}
and let
s\to T_{+}
, then the above display and (
2.98
) implies that
\lim_{s\to T_{+}}\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s))
exists. Now observe that for some
r^{\prime}
such that
0<r^{\prime}<\frac{r_{1}}{2}<r_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(\tau))-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u^{*})=\int_{|x-x_{0}|\geq r^{\prime}}({{\bf e}}(u)-{{\bf e}}(u^{*}))\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

(2.100)

Since
u(t)\to u^{*}
strongly in
\dot{H}^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{S})
, the RHS in the above display tends to zero as
\tau\to T_{+}
. Thus, choosing
r_{1}=\alpha(T_{+}-s)^{1/2}
and
r_{2}=A(T_{+}-s)^{1/2}
in the definition of the cut-off function
\phi
, where

in (

\displaystyle\left|\bar{E}_{\phi}(u^{*})-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s))\right|\lesssim\int_{s}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\left(\int_{s}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\int_{s}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}.

(2.101)

Therefore

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to T_{+}}\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s))=0.

(2.102)

If, instead we set
r_{1}=\alpha(T_{+}-s)^{1/2}
and
r_{2}=r
in the definition of the cut-off function
\phi
where
r>0
is small enough such that
B(x_{0},r)
does not contain any other bubbling point, then we have

\displaystyle\left|\bar{E}_{\phi}(u^{*})-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s))\right|\lesssim\int_{s}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\left(\int_{s}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\int_{s}^{T_{+}}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}.

(2.103)

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}\lim_{s\to T_{+}}\left|\bar{E}_{\phi}(u^{*})-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s))\right|=0.\end{split}

(2.104)

Denote
A(s)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:(\alpha/2)\sqrt{T_{+}-s}\leq|x-x_{0}|\leq\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-s}\}
and
A(r)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:r\leq|x-x_{0}|\leq 2r\}
then if

\begin{split}&\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s));B(x_{0},r)\setminus B(x_{0},\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-s}))-\bar{E}(u^{*};B(x_{0},r))\\
&=\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s))-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u^{*})-\int_{\{\phi\neq 1\}}{\bf e}(u(s))\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\{\phi\neq 1\}}{\bf e}(u^{*})\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\bar{E}(u^{*};B(x_{0},\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-s}))\\
&=\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s))-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u^{*})-\int_{A(s)}{\bf e}(u(s))\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{A(r)}{\bf e}(u(s))\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+\int_{A(r)}{\bf e}(u^{*})\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{A(s)}{\bf e}(u^{*})\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\bar{E}(u^{*};B(x_{0},\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-s})),\end{split}

(2.105)

which implies that

\begin{split}&\left|\bar{E}(u(s);B(x_{0},r)\setminus B(x_{0},\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-s}))-\bar{E}(u^{*};B(x_{0},r))\right|\\
&\lesssim\left|\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s))-\bar{E}_{\phi}(u^{*})\right|+\bar{E}_{\phi}(u(s);B(x_{0},\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-s})\setminus B(x_{0},\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-s}/2))\\
&\quad+\bar{E}(u^{*};B(x_{0},\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-s}))+\left|\int_{A(r)}\phi^{2}({{\bf e}}(u^{*})-{{\bf e}}(u(s)))\mathrm{d}x\right|.\end{split}

(2.106)

By (
2.104
), (
2.102
), and strong convergence of
u(t)
to
u^{*}
in
\dot{H}^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{S})
, we see that each term above tends to zero as
s\to T_{+}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to T_{+}}\bar{E}(u(s);B(x_{0},r)\setminus B(x_{0},\alpha\sqrt{T_{+}-s}))=\bar{E}(u^{*};B(x_{0},r)).

(2.107)

This completes the proof of (
2.96
). One can easily construct the curves
\nu
and
\xi
such that the first equation in (
2.97
) holds. This, along with (
2.96
), implies the second equation in (
2.97
).
∎

Showing the same fact in the global case is significantly more challenging. Unfortunately, we are unable to use the above argument since we are unable to uniformly control the energy of the nonlinear term uniformly on a non-compact interval of time. However, we can deduce nontrivial information if we apply energy estimates to the nonlinear energy.

Lemma 2.15

(Nonlinear energy dissipation in the global case)
.

Let
u(t)
be the solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
,
T_{+}=T_{+}(u_{0})=\infty
and finite energy
\sup_{t\geq 0}\bar{E}(u(t))<\infty.
Then for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
\alpha>0
we have

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to T_{+}}E_{\phi}(u(t))=0.\end{split}

(2.108)

where
\phi=1-\chi(|x-y|/\alpha\sqrt{t})
for a smooth cut-off function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,2))
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be small enough. Then we can find
T_{0}=T_{0}(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\begin{split}\Big(\int_{T_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/2}\leq\varepsilon.\end{split}

(2.109)

Next, choose
T_{1}\geq T_{0}
so that for all
T\geq T_{1}

\begin{split}\bar{E}(u(T_{0});\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(y,\alpha\sqrt{T}/4))\leq\varepsilon.\end{split}

(2.110)

Fix any such
T\geq T_{1}
. Let
\phi(x)=1-\chi(|x-y|/\alpha\sqrt{T})
where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,2))
is a smooth cut-off function. Then using (
2.22
)
|\nabla\phi|^{2}\lesssim T^{-1}
we see that

\begin{split}|{E}_{\phi}(u(T))-{E}_{\phi}(u(T_{0}))|&\lesssim\int_{T_{0}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\\
&+2\left(\int_{T_{0}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{T_{0}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\varepsilon+C\varepsilon\leq C_{1}\varepsilon.\end{split}

(2.111)

Therefore, we get

\begin{split}\lim_{T\to\infty}{E}_{\phi}(u(T))=0,\end{split}

(2.112)

which implies the desired result.
∎

Since the nonlinear energy is not coercive, the above estimate is not very helpful as it does not control the
\dot{H}^{1}
norm. However, as explained earlier, due to the following identity

\begin{split}\bar{E}_{\phi}(u)=2E_{\phi}(u)+\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p+1}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\end{split}

(2.113)

we observe that if the
L^{p+1}
norm vanishes outside the region
B(y,\alpha\sqrt{t})
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\alpha>0
then using Lemma
2.15
one can conclude that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(t);\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(y,\alpha\sqrt{t}))=0
. Thus, we first show the following lemma which makes use of a result in
[
30
]
.

Lemma 2.16

(No self-similar energy concentration in the global case I)
.

Let
u(t)
be the solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
,
T_{+}=\infty
and
\sup_{t\geq 0}\bar{E}(u(t))<\infty.
Then for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
\alpha>0
we have

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\int_{|x-y|\geq\alpha\sqrt{t}}|u|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x=0.\end{split}

(2.114)

Proof.

Let
\phi(x)=1-\chi\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\alpha\sqrt{t}}\right)
where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,2)).
We will show that

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p+1}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x=0,\end{split}

(2.115)

which will give us the desired result. By translational invariance, assume without loss of generality that
y=0.
Recall Theorem 1.4 in
[
30
]
, which states that

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\operatorname{dist}_{{L^{p+1}}}(u(t),E_{\infty}(u_{0}))=0,\end{split}

(2.116)

where

\begin{split}E_{\infty}(u_{0})=\Bigl\{\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda^{j})^{\frac{(N-2)}{2}}\;\psi^{j}\bigl(\lambda^{j}(\cdot-x^{j})\bigr)\;\Big|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\;\psi^{j}\text{ solve }\eqref{eq:yamabe},\;\sum_{j=1}^{n}E(\psi_{j})\leq E(u_{0})\Bigr\}.\end{split}

(2.117)

As a consequence, if the (
2.114
) is false, then there exist an initial data
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
and
\bar{\alpha}>0
such that for some sequence
t_{n}\to T_{+}
we have

\begin{split}\int_{|x|\geq\bar{\alpha}\sqrt{t_{n}}}|u(t_{n})|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x\geq\delta>0.\end{split}

(2.118)

Note that for
\alpha\in(0,\bar{\alpha}]
,
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\bar{\alpha}\sqrt{t_{n}})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\alpha\sqrt{t_{n}})
and therefore for any
\alpha\in(0,\bar{\alpha}]
we have

\begin{split}\int_{|x|\geq\alpha\sqrt{t_{n}}}|u(t_{n})|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x\geq\delta>0.\end{split}

(2.119)

We will show that there exists
\alpha^{*}\in(0,\bar{\alpha}]
such that (
2.119
) implies that the nonlinear energy satisfies

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{\phi_{n}}[u(t_{n})]>0,\end{split}

(2.120)

where
\phi_{n}(x)=1-\chi(|x|/(\alpha^{*}\sqrt{t_{n}}))
which will contradict Lemma
2.15
.

As a starting point, using (
2.116
) we obtain that (up to a subsequence) and for
n\gg 1
, the following decomposition holds

\begin{split}u(t_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{K}(\lambda^{j}_{n})^{\frac{(N-2)}{2}}\;\psi^{j}(\lambda^{j}_{n}(x-x^{j}_{n}))+r_{n},\end{split}

(2.121)

such that

(1)

K\geq 1
since otherwise
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|r_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}=0
, which would contradict (
2.118
). Furthermore,
K\in\mathbb{N}
can be chosen to be independent of
n
by possibly passing to a subsequence since the sequence has finite energy,

(2)

each profile
\psi^{j}
is a non‑trivial stationary solution,

(3)

the parameters are orthogonal in the usual sense

\begin{split}\frac{\lambda^{i}_{n}}{\lambda^{j}_{n}}+\frac{\lambda^{j}_{n}}{\lambda^{i}_{n}}+\frac{\lvert x^{i}_{n}-x^{j}_{n}\rvert^{2}}{\lambda^{j}_{n}\lambda^{i}_{n}}\;\longrightarrow\;\infty\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,\;\;i\neq j,\end{split}

(2.122)

and up to a subsequence, we can order the scales
0<\lambda^{1}_{n}<\lambda^{2}_{n}<\cdots<\lambda^{K}_{n}
,

(4)

the error satisfies
\|r_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}\to 0,
as
n\to\infty
,

(5)

and we have the following Pythagorean expansion of various norms,

\begin{split}\|\nabla u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|\nabla\psi^{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla r_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+o_{n}(1),\quad\|u(t_{n})\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|\psi^{j}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}+o_{n}(1).\end{split}

(2.123)

Denote
u_{n}=u(t_{n})
and
\psi^{j}_{n}=(\lambda^{j}_{n})^{\frac{(d-2)}{2}}\;\psi^{j}(\lambda^{j}_{n}(x-x^{j}_{n}))
for each
j\in\mathcal{J}:=\{1,\ldots,K\}.
Consider dividing the index set
\mathcal{J}
into
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{1}\cup\mathcal{J}_{2}\cup\mathcal{J}_{3},
where

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}_{i}:=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda^{j}_{n}\sqrt{t_{n}}=L_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}\}\end{split}

(2.124)

where
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\{0\},\mathcal{A}_{2}\subset(0,\infty)
and
\mathcal{A}_{3}=\{\infty\}.
Consider the cut-off functions

\begin{split}\phi_{n}(x)=1-\chi\left(\frac{|x|}{\alpha\sqrt{t_{n}}}\right),\quad\zeta^{j}_{n}(z)=1-\chi^{j}_{n}(z)=1-\chi\left(\frac{|z+c^{j}_{n}|}{r^{j}_{n}}\right)\end{split}

(2.125)

where
c^{j}_{n}=\lambda^{j}_{n}x^{j}_{n}
,
r^{j}_{n}=\alpha\lambda^{j}_{n}\sqrt{t_{n}}
, and
\alpha\in(0,\bar{\alpha}]
will be fixed later depending on the scales of the profile
\lambda^{j}_{n}.

Then, from (
2.118
) we deduce that there exists at least one bad profile with index
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
such that

\begin{split}\int_{|x|\geq\alpha\sqrt{t_{n}}}|\psi^{j^{0}}_{n}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{\delta}{K}.\end{split}

(2.126)

Note that
j^{0}\in\mathcal{J}_{1}\cup\mathcal{J}_{2}
since otherwise the integral above would vanish as
n\to\infty
, contradicting (
2.126
). Expanding the nonlinear energy, we get

\begin{split}E_{\phi_{n}}[u_{n}]=\sum_{j=1}^{K}E_{\phi_{n}}[\psi^{j}_{n}]+\sum_{j\neq k}A_{j,k,n}+E_{\phi_{n}}[r_{n}],\end{split}

(2.127)

where
A_{j,k,n}
contains all cross–terms between distinct profiles and the remainder
r_{n}
, which up to
o_{n}(1)
errors are of the form

\begin{split}\mathsf{I}_{n}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla\psi^{j}_{n}\cdot\nabla\psi^{k}_{n})\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\mathsf{II}_{n}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla\psi^{j}_{n}\cdot\nabla r_{n})\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\\
\mathsf{III}_{n}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi^{j}_{n}|^{p-1}\psi^{j}_{n}\psi^{k}_{n}\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\mathsf{IV}_{n}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi^{j}_{n}|^{p-1}\psi^{j}_{n}r_{n}\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(2.128)

for
j,k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
,
j\neq k.
The terms
\mathsf{I}_{n},\mathsf{III}_{n}
vanish due to the asymptotic orthogonality of parameters associated to the profiles
\psi^{j}_{n}
and
\psi^{k}_{n}
. Thus, we estimate the remaining terms using integration by parts and Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{p+1}=1
to get

\begin{split}|\mathsf{IV}_{n}|&\leq\|\psi^{j}_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p}\|r_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}\to 0,\\
|\mathsf{II}_{n}|&=\left|-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi_{n}^{j}|^{p-1}\psi_{n}^{j}r_{n}\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla\psi^{j}_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi_{n})r_{n}\phi_{n}\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
&\lesssim\|\psi^{j}_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p}\|r_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}+\|\nabla\psi^{j}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\phi_{n}\|_{L^{n}}\|r_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}\to 0\end{split}

(2.129)

as
n\to\infty.
The finiteness of
\|\nabla\phi_{n}\|_{L^{n}}
can be ensured by choosing a log cut-off function, see for instance Lemma 3.8 in
[
24
]
. By the orthogonality of parameters and the vanishing of the error term in
L^{p+1}
norm, we have
A_{j,k,n}=o_{n}(1)
and
E_{\phi_{n}}[r_{n}]\geq-o_{n}(1)
. Combining with (
2.15
),

\displaystyle E_{\phi_{n}}[u_{n}]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{K}E_{\phi_{n}}[\psi^{j}_{n}]-o_{n}(1)

(2.130)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}}E_{\phi_{n}}[\psi^{j}_{n}]+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}}E_{\phi_{n}}[\psi^{j}_{n}]+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{3}}E_{\phi_{n}}[\psi^{j}_{n}]-o_{n}(1)

(2.131)

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{E_{1}}+\mathrm{E_{2}}+\mathrm{E}_{3}-o_{n}(1),

(2.132)

where
\mathrm{E}_{i}:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{i}}E_{\phi_{n}}[\psi^{j}_{n}]
for
i=1,2,3.
We estimate each term carefully. First we analyze two sub-cases
\mathcal{J}_{1}=\mathcal{J}_{1}^{b}\cup\mathcal{J}_{1}^{\infty}
where

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}_{1}^{b}=\{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}:\liminf_{n\to\infty}|c^{j}_{n}|<\infty\},\quad\mathcal{J}_{1}^{\infty}=\{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}:\liminf_{n\to\infty}|c^{j}_{n}|=\infty\}.\end{split}

(2.133)

Therefore, up to a subsequence when
j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}^{b}
,
|c^{j}_{n}|
is bounded while
|c^{j}_{n}|\to\infty
when
j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}^{\infty}.
Thus,

\begin{split}\mathrm{E}_{1}&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}}E_{\phi_{n}}[\psi^{j}_{n}]\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}^{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|\nabla{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|\nabla{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}^{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|\nabla{\psi}^{j}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|{\psi}^{j}|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)(\zeta^{j}_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|\nabla{\psi}^{j}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|{\psi}^{j}|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)(\zeta^{j}_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\geq\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{1}|}{2d}\bar{E}_{*}-o_{n}(1),\end{split}

(2.134)

since when
n\to\infty
by the dominated convergence we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|\nabla{\psi}^{j}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|{\psi}^{j}|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)(\zeta^{j}_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}z\to E[\psi^{j}]\geq\frac{\bar{E}_{*}}{d},\ \forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}^{b},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|\nabla{\psi}^{j}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|{\psi}^{j}|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)(\zeta^{j}_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}z=E[\psi^{j}]-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bf e}(\psi^{j})\chi^{j}_{n}(\chi^{j}_{n}-2)\mathrm{d}z\geq\frac{\bar{E}_{*}}{d}-o_{n}(1),\ \forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}^{\infty}.\end{split}

(2.135)

Here we used the fact that
j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}^{b}
implies,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|z+c^{j}_{n}|}{r^{j}_{n}}=\infty
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
j\in\mathcal{J}_{1}^{\infty}
implies,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|{\psi}^{j}|^{p+1}(\chi^{j}_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}z\leq\|\psi^{j}\|^{p+1}_{L^{\infty}}\lim_{n\to\infty}(r^{j}_{n})^{d}=0.
Next, for profiles in
\mathcal{J}_{2}
, we analyze two sub-cases
\mathcal{J}_{2}=\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}\cup\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}
where

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}=\{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}:\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{|x^{j}_{n}|}{\sqrt{t_{n}}}<\infty\},\quad\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}=\{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}:\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{|x^{j}_{n}|}{\sqrt{t_{n}}}=\infty\}.\end{split}

(2.136)

As a consequence, up to a subsequence,
|c^{j}_{n}|
is bounded when
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}
. On the other hand,
|c^{j}_{n}|=\lambda^{j}_{n}\sqrt{t_{n}}\cdot\frac{|x^{j}_{n}|}{\sqrt{t_{n}}}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
when
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}
. Therefore, we get

\begin{split}\mathrm{E}_{2}&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}}E_{\phi_{n}}[\psi^{j}_{n}]\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|\nabla{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|\nabla{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\geq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{2}\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{2}{p+1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla{\psi}^{j}_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi_{n}){\psi}^{j}_{n}\phi_{n}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}|\bar{E}_{*}}{d}-o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla{\psi}^{j}|^{2}(\zeta^{j}_{n})^{2}\mathrm{d}z-\frac{C}{\alpha\lambda^{j}_{n}\sqrt{t_{n}}}\int_{r^{j}_{n}/2\leq|z+c^{j}_{n}|\leq r^{j}_{n}}|\nabla{\psi}^{j}||\psi^{j}_{n}|\mathrm{d}z\right]+\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}|\bar{E}_{*}}{d}-o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}}\left[\int_{|z|\geq\alpha L_{j}}|\nabla{\psi}^{j}|^{2}\mathrm{d}z-C\alpha^{d-1}(L_{j})^{d-1}\right]+\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}|\bar{E}_{*}}{d}-o_{n}(1),\end{split}

(2.137)

where in the second inequality we test (
1.5
) with
\psi^{j}_{n}\phi_{n}^{2}
to get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi^{j}_{n}|^{p+1}\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\psi^{j}_{n}|^{2}\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{n}\psi^{j}_{n}(\nabla\phi_{n}\cdot\nabla\psi^{j}_{n})\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

(2.138)

and to estimate the nonlinear energy for
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}
, we use

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi^{j}_{n}|^{p+1}\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{|z+c^{j}_{n}|\geq r^{j}_{n}/2}|\psi^{j}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}z\leq\int_{|z|\geq|c^{j}_{n}|/2}|\psi^{j}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}z\to 0,\text{ as }n\to\infty\end{split}

(2.139)

which implies that

\begin{split}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|\nabla{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}}E[\psi^{j}]+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bf e}(\psi^{j})\chi^{j}_{n}(2-\chi^{j}_{n})\mathrm{d}z\\
&\geq\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}|}{d}\bar{E}_{*}-o_{n}(1).\end{split}

(2.140)

The final inequality in (
2.137
) follows by controlling the error term
introduced by the cut-off function for profiles with index
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}
,

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{n}\psi^{j}_{n}(\nabla\phi_{n}\cdot\nabla\psi^{j}_{n})\mathrm{d}x\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha\lambda^{j}_{n}\sqrt{t_{n}}}\int_{r^{j}_{n}/2\leq|z+c^{j}_{n}|\leq r^{j}_{n}}|\nabla\psi^{j}||\psi^{j}|\mathrm{d}z\\
&\lesssim(\alpha\lambda^{j}_{n}\sqrt{t_{n}})^{d-1}\|\nabla\psi^{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\psi^{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim(\alpha L_{j})^{d-1}\|\nabla\psi^{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\psi^{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}.\end{split}

(2.141)

Recall that for
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda^{j}_{n}\sqrt{t_{n}}=L_{j}
where
L_{j}\in(0,\infty)
(after possibly passing to a subsequence). Denote
L^{*}=\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}}L_{j}
and
L_{*}=\min_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}}L_{j}
. Choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that
\varepsilon^{2}/L^{*}\in(0,\bar{\alpha})
and for each
j\in\mathcal{J}^{b}_{2}
we have

\begin{split}\bar{E}(\psi^{j};\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,\varepsilon^{2}))\geq\frac{\bar{E}_{*}}{2}.\end{split}

(2.142)

Set
\alpha^{*}=\varepsilon^{2}/L^{*}
then for each
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}^{b}
we have

\begin{split}\int_{|z|\geq\alpha L_{j}}|\nabla{\psi}^{j}|^{2}\mathrm{d}z-C(\alpha L_{j})^{d-1}&\geq\int_{|z|\geq\alpha L^{*}}|\nabla{\psi}^{j}|^{2}\mathrm{d}z-C\varepsilon^{2d-2}\\
&\geq\int_{|z|\geq\varepsilon^{2}}|\nabla{\psi}^{j}|^{2}\mathrm{d}z-C\varepsilon^{2d-2}\\
&\geq\frac{\bar{E}_{*}}{2}-o_{\varepsilon}(1)>\frac{\bar{E}_{*}}{4},\end{split}

(2.143)

where
C>0
is a constant depending on
\sup_{t\geq 0}\bar{E}(u(t))<\infty
.
Therefore, we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{E}_{2}&\geq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2}}\int_{|z|\geq\alpha L_{j}}|\nabla{\psi}^{j}|^{2}\mathrm{d}z-C\varepsilon^{2d-2}+\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\infty}|\bar{E}_{*}}{d}-o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{2}|}{4d}\bar{E}_{*}-o_{n}(1).\end{split}

(2.144)

Finally,

\begin{split}\mathrm{E}_{3}&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{3}}E_{\phi_{n}}[\psi^{j}_{n}]\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{|\nabla{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{p+1}}{p+1}\right)\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{p+1}\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{2}{p+1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla{\psi}^{j}_{n}\cdot\nabla\phi_{n}){\psi}^{j}_{n}\phi_{n}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\geq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla{\psi}^{j}_{n}|^{2}\phi_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{C}{\lambda_{n}^{j}\sqrt{t_{n}}}\geq-o_{n}(1).\end{split}

(2.145)

Therefore, combining the above estimates, we get

\begin{split}E_{\phi_{n}}[u_{n}]&\geq\mathrm{E_{1}}+\mathrm{E_{2}}+\mathrm{E}_{3}-o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{1}|}{2d}\bar{E}_{*}+\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{2}|}{4d}\bar{E}_{*}-o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\frac{|\mathcal{J}_{1}|+|\mathcal{J}_{2}|}{4d}\bar{E}_{*}-o_{n}(1)\\
&\geq\frac{\bar{E}_{*}}{4d}-o_{n}(1)\end{split}

(2.146)

since the bad profile has index
j^{0}\in\mathcal{J}_{1}\cup\mathcal{J}_{2}
. Therefore

\begin{split}E_{\phi_{n}}[u(t_{n})]\geq\frac{\bar{E}_{*}}{4d}-o_{n}(1),\end{split}

(2.147)

which implies that

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to T_{+}}E_{\phi}[u(t)]=\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{\phi_{n}}[u(t_{n})]\geq\frac{\bar{E}_{*}}{4d}>0.\end{split}

(2.148)

contradicting Lemma
2.15
.
∎

Lemma 2.17

(No self-similar energy concentration in the global case II)
.

Let
u(t)
be the solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
,
T_{+}=\infty
and
\sup_{t\geq 0}\bar{E}(u(t))<\infty.
Then for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
\alpha>0
we have

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{|x-y|\geq\alpha\sqrt{t}}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=0.\end{split}

(2.149)

Proof.

Let
\phi=1-\chi(|x-y|/\alpha\sqrt{t})
, where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,2)).
Then since

\begin{split}\bar{E}_{\phi}(u)=2E_{\phi}(u)+\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p+1}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

(2.150)

by Lemmas
2.15
and
2.16
we see that

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}x=0.\end{split}

(2.151)

Therefore

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\int_{|x-y|\geq\alpha\sqrt{t}}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=0,\end{split}

(2.152)

as desired.
∎

2.4.
Sequential Compactness

In this section, we establish an elliptic compactness Theorem for Palais-Smale sequences for critical points associated to the equation (
1.5
). This result is quite classical with connections to concentration-compactness in analysis
[
52
]
and the Yamabe problem in differential geometry
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.18

(Elliptic Bubbling)
.

Let
u_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a sequence of functions in
\dot{H}^{1}
such that

\begin{split}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}<\infty,\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho_{k}\|\Delta u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}}=0\end{split}

(2.153)

for some sequence
\rho_{k}\in(0,\infty).
Then given any sequence
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exist a stationary solution
u_{\infty}\in\dot{H}^{1}
(possibly trivial), an integer
m\in\mathbb{N}
, a constant
C>0
, a sequence
R_{k}\to\infty
, a collection of elliptic solutions
W_{1},\ldots,W_{m}
each equipped with translation parameters
\{x^{i}_{k}\}_{j=1}^{m}\in B(y_{k},C\rho_{k})
and scales
\{\lambda_{k}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\begin{split}&\lim_{k\to\infty}\bar{E}(u_{k}-u_{\infty}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}W_{j}[x^{j}_{k},\lambda^{j}_{k}];B(y_{k},R_{k}\rho_{k}))\\
&\quad+\sum_{j\neq j^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{j}_{k}}{\lambda^{j^{\prime}}_{k}}+\frac{\lambda^{j^{\prime}}_{k}}{\lambda^{j}_{k}}+\frac{|x^{j^{\prime}}_{k}-x^{j}_{k}|^{2}}{\lambda^{j^{\prime}}_{k}\lambda^{j}_{k}}\right)^{-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\lambda^{j}_{k}}{\operatorname{dist}(x^{j}_{k},\partial B(y_{k},C\rho_{k}))}=0.\end{split}

(2.154)

Denote

\begin{split}\bar{\mathcal{S}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\liminf_{k\to\infty}\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{B(x,r)\cap B(y_{k},C\rho_{k})}|u_{k}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x\geq\tilde{\varepsilon}\},\end{split}

(2.155)

for some
\tilde{\varepsilon}=\tilde{\varepsilon}(n).
Then
\bar{\mathcal{S}}=\{x^{1},\ldots,x^{l}\}
, where
l\leq m.
Furthermore,

\begin{split}u_{k}(y_{k}+\rho_{k}\cdot)&\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}\,\,\textrm{weakly in}\,\,\dot{H}^{1}(B(0,C))\\
u_{k}(y_{k}+\rho_{k}\cdot)&\to u_{\infty}\,\,\textrm{strongly in}\,\,W^{2,2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(B(0,C)\setminus\bar{\mathcal{S}}).\end{split}

(2.156)

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
there exists a finite set of points
\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
, possibly empty and with
\operatorname{card}(\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{i})\leq m
, such that

\begin{split}u_{k}(x^{i}_{k}+\lambda^{i}_{k}\cdot)\to W_{j}\,\,\textrm{strongly in}\,\,W^{2,2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{j}).\end{split}

(2.157)

Finally, there exists a non-negative real number
K\geq 0
so that

\begin{split}\lim_{k\to\infty}\bar{E}(u_{k};B(y_{k},R_{k}\rho_{k}))=K\bar{E}_{*}.\end{split}

(2.158)

Remark 2.19
.

The above Theorem
2.18
is similar in spirit to Theorem 1.1 in
[
55
]
for almost harmonic maps from
\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
. The key difficulty in establishing the above theorem stems from the fact that the natural energy associated with (
1.5
) does not have a definite sign. Note that, unlike in the harmonic map case, we cannot expect to obtain
L^{\infty}
neck-estimates since
W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
does not embed into
C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when
d\geq 4.
Lastly, observe that as a consequence of the above theorem, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\boldsymbol{\delta}(u_{n};B(y_{n},\widetilde{R}_{n}\rho_{n}))=0
for any sequence
1\ll\widetilde{R}_{n}\ll R_{n}
.

Proof.

See Sections 2 and 3 in
[
16
]
, where this argument has been carried out for
u_{k}\geq 0
on bounded domains. However, the same argument can be repeated for sign–changing functions
u_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The main difference is that the bubbles
W_{j}
arising from the blow-up argument are not necessarily positive solutions of (
1.5
). We briefly sketch the argument for the reader’s convenience.

Step 1.
Sequential Bubbling. By scaling and translational invariance, we can assume that
\rho_{k}=1
and
y_{k}=0.
Denote the set of blowup points for the sequence

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lim_{r\to 0}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{B(x,r)\cap B(0,C)}|u_{k}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x\geq\tilde{\varepsilon}\},\end{split}

(2.159)

where we will fix the constant
C>0
later and
\tilde{\varepsilon}=\tilde{\varepsilon}(n)>0
is a positive constant that appears in the
\varepsilon
-regularity Theorem 2.1 proved in
[
16
]
, which says that
\int_{B(0,r)}|u|^{p+1}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon(n)}
implies that
\int_{B(0,\delta)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C_{0}
for small
\delta\in(0,1)
and some constant
C_{0}>0.
Since
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}<\infty
, by choosing
r>0
small enough such that
B(x^{i},r)\cap B(x^{j},r)=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
, a standard covering argument implies that
\mathcal{S}=\{x^{1},\ldots,x^{N}\}
for some finite
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
1\leq i\leq N.
We can choose
C
in the definition of
\mathcal{S}
small enough such that
\mathcal{S}
consists of a singleton, i.e.,
\mathcal{S}=\{x^{1}\}.

Step 1.1.
Extracting the first bubble. Fix
x\in\overline{B(x^{1},r)\cap B(0,C)}
and let
r_{k}:=r_{k}(x)
be the unique radius depending on
x
such that

\begin{split}\int_{B(x,r_{k})\cap B(0,C)}|u_{k}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\widetilde{\varepsilon}}{2}.\end{split}

(2.160)

Let
x^{1}_{k}\in\overline{B(x^{1},r)\cap B(0,C)}
be the point where
r_{k}(x)
attains its minimum. Then define
\lambda^{1}_{k}=r_{k}(x^{1}_{k}).
Thus we have a blowup sequence,
\lambda^{1}_{k}\to 0
and
x^{1}_{k}\to x^{1}
as
k\to\infty
such that

\begin{split}\int_{B(x^{1}_{k},\lambda^{1}_{k})}|u_{k}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}.\end{split}

(2.161)

Re-scaling the function
u_{k}
,

\begin{split}\tilde{u}_{k}(x)=(\lambda^{1}_{k})^{2/(p-1)}u_{k}(\lambda^{1}_{k}x+x^{1}_{k})\end{split}

(2.162)

and using the
\varepsilon
-regularity proved in Theorem 2.1 in
[
16
]
we see that since

\begin{split}\Delta\tilde{u}_{k}+|\tilde{u}_{k}|^{p-1}\tilde{u}_{k}=(\lambda^{1}_{k})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}(\Delta u_{k}+|u_{k}|^{p-1}u_{k}),\end{split}

(2.163)

the sequence
\tilde{u}_{k}\to W_{1}
in
H^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
where
W_{1}
solves (
1.5
) either on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
depending on whether
x^{1}_{k}
lies in the interior of the domain
\overline{B(x^{1},r)\cap B(0,C)}
or on its boundary. The latter can be ruled out by showing

\begin{split}\frac{\lambda^{1}_{k}}{\operatorname{dist}(x^{1}_{k},\partial B(0,C))}\to 0,\ k\to\infty\end{split}

(2.164)

which can be done by a contradiction argument that involves assuming
\frac{\lambda^{1}_{k}}{\operatorname{dist}(x^{1}_{k},\partial B(0,C))}\to c\in(0,\infty],\ k\to\infty
and showing that this gives rise to a solution of (
1.5
) on the half-space which is known to be trivial by Pohozaev’s identity. For more details, see page. 162, Section 3 in
[
16
]
or the proof of Proposition 2.1 in
[
52
]
.

Step 1.2.
Consider the re-normalized sequence

\begin{split}v_{k}(x)=u_{k}(x)-W_{1}[x^{1}_{k},\lambda^{1}_{k}](x).\end{split}

(2.165)

If
v_{k}
converges (up to subsequence) strongly to
u_{\infty}
in
\dot{H}^{1}(B(x_{1},r)\cap B(0,C))
then we are done. Otherwise, as in Step 1.1, we can find scales
\lambda^{2}_{k}\to 0
and centers
x^{2}_{k}\to x^{1}
such that

\begin{split}\int_{B(x^{2}_{k},\lambda^{2}_{k})}|v_{k}|^{p+1}=\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon_{1}}}{2}\end{split}

(2.166)

for some constant
0<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{1}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}.
We first claim that

\begin{split}\frac{\lambda^{2}_{k}}{\lambda^{1}_{k}}+\frac{\left|x^{1}_{k}-x^{2}_{k}\right|}{\lambda^{1}_{k}+\lambda^{2}_{k}}\rightarrow\infty,\ \text{as }k\rightarrow\infty,\end{split}

(2.167)

since otherwise there exists some constant
M>0
such that

\begin{split}\frac{\lambda^{2}_{k}}{\lambda^{1}_{k}}+\frac{\left|x^{1}_{k}-x^{2}_{k}\right|}{\lambda^{1}_{k}+\lambda^{2}_{k}}\leq M,\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty.\end{split}

(2.168)

This, in turn, would imply that

\begin{split}\int_{B(x^{2}_{k},\lambda^{2}_{k})}|v_{k}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{B((x^{2}_{k}-x^{1}_{k})/\lambda^{1}_{k},\lambda^{2}_{k}/\lambda^{1}_{k})}|\tilde{u}_{k}-W_{1}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{B(0,M)}|\tilde{u}_{k}-W_{1}|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x\to 0\end{split}

(2.169)

as
k\to\infty
which contradicts the energy concentration in (
2.166
). The next subtle point here is to show that no energy is lost between the
neck
-region connecting the new bubble
W_{2}
and the previous bubble
W_{1}
. This has been done in Section 4
[
16
]
and therefore at the end of this step we get,

\begin{split}u_{k}-W_{1}[x^{1}_{k},\lambda^{1}_{k}]-W_{2}[x^{2}_{k},\lambda^{2}_{k}]\rightarrow 0\end{split}

(2.170)

strongly in
\dot{H}^{1}(B(x^{1},L\lambda^{1}_{k})\cap B(x^{1},L\lambda^{2}_{k}))
for any
L>0.

Step 1.3.
Iterate and conclude. One can then iterate this process finitely many times to extract the bubble tree as desired with asymptotically orthogonal parameters as in the second display in (
2.154
).

Step 2
. Convergence results. The existence of the weak limit in (
2.156
) follows from the fact that
u_{k}
is a bounded sequence of
\dot{H}^{1}
functions. The strong convergence in
W^{2,2}_{\operatorname{loc}}
away from the blowup points follows from the
\varepsilon
-regularity result from Theorem 2.1 in
[
16
]
. Thus,
u_{\infty}
is a smooth stationary solution of (
1.5
) away from a finite set of points. Then the standard removable singularity theorem, see for instance
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
, implies that
u_{\infty}
is a smooth solution of (
1.5
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}.
The strong convergence in (
2.157
) follows from the definition of the blow-up parameters
(x^{i}_{k},\lambda^{i}_{k})
and
\varepsilon
-regularity from Theorem 2.1 in
[
16
]
.

Step 3
. Energy almost-quantization. The bubble tree convergence and the
no-neck
property established in Section 4 of
[
16
]
imply the energy identity

\begin{split}\lim_{k\to\infty}\bar{E}(u_{k};B(0,R_{k}\rho_{k}))=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bar{E}(W_{j}).\end{split}

(2.171)

Since
\bar{E}(W_{j})\geq\bar{E}_{*}
and we know that

\begin{split}\lim_{k\to\infty}\bar{E}(u_{k};B(0,R_{k}\rho_{k}))\geq m\bar{E}_{*}.\end{split}

(2.172)

Furthermore,
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bar{E}(W_{j})\leq C_{1}
since the sequence
u_{k}
has finite energy. Therefore, we can find a non-negative real number between
m
and less than or equal to
C_{1}/\bar{E}_{*}
such that, up to passing to a subsequence, there exists a non-negative real number
K\geq m
satisfying

\begin{split}\lim_{k\to\infty}\bar{E}(u_{k};B(0,R_{k}))=K\bar{E}_{*}\end{split}

(2.173)

as desired.
∎

3.
Analysis of Collision Intervals

For convenience, in this section, we let
u(t)
be a solution of (
1.1
), with initial data
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
, defined on the maximal time interval
I_{+}=[0,T_{+})
where
T_{+}<\infty
in the finite time blow-up case and
T_{+}=\infty
in the global case. We will also assume that
C^{\prime}=\sup_{t\in[0,T_{+})}\bar{E}(u(t))<\infty.
Let
0<\gamma_{0}\ll 1
be such that Lemma
2.2
holds. We fix this choice of
\gamma_{0}
and drop the subscript
\gamma_{0}
from
{\bf d}_{\gamma_{0}}
and
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\gamma_{0}}
and from the notation for the scale and center of a stationary solution
W
, in particular
\lambda(W)=\lambda(W;\gamma_{0})
and
a(W)=a(W;\gamma_{0}).
Our goal in this section is to introduce the notion of collision intervals and show that if Theorem
1.1
fails, then these intervals have a nontrivial length.

Definition 3.1

(Collision Interval)
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the smallest positive real number with the following properties. There exist sequences of centers and scales
(y_{n},\rho_{n},\varepsilon_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)^{2}
, sequences of times
\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}\in(0,T_{+})
and small (but fixed)
\eta>0
, satisfying
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
,
0<\sigma_{n}<\tau_{n}<T_{+}
,
\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}\to T_{+}
,
such that

(1)

\boldsymbol{\delta}(u(\sigma_{n});B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))\leq\varepsilon_{n};

(2)

\boldsymbol{\delta}(u(\tau_{n});B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))\geq\eta
;

(3)

the interval
I_{n}:=[\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}]
satisfies
|I_{n}|\leq\varepsilon_{n}\rho_{n}^{2}
;

(4)

K:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(\sigma_{n});B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))/{\bar{E}_{*}}
.

Then the intervals
[\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}]
are called collision intervals associated to the energy level
K
and the parameters
(y_{n},\rho_{n},\varepsilon_{n},\eta)
. We can conveniently package this information in the following notation
[\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}]\in\mathcal{C}_{K}(y_{n},\rho_{n},\varepsilon_{n},\eta)
.

Remark 3.2
.

By Definition
1.7
and item (1) in Definition
3.1
, we can associate to each sequence of collision intervals
[\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}]\in\mathcal{C}_{K}(y_{n},\rho_{n},\varepsilon_{n},\eta)
a sequence
(\xi_{n},\nu_{n})\in(0,\infty)^{2}
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\xi_{n}}{\rho_{n}}+\frac{\rho_{n}}{\nu_{n}}\right)=0
such that

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(\sigma_{n});B(y_{n},2\nu_{n})\setminus B(y_{n},2^{-1}\xi_{n}))=0.\end{split}

(3.1)

Using item (3) in Definition
3.1
also allows us to assume that

\begin{split}|I_{n}|=\tau_{n}-\sigma_{n}\ll\xi_{n}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.2)

Using Lemma
2.13
with (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we get

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}]}\bar{E}(u(t);B(y_{n},\nu_{n})\setminus B(y_{n},\xi_{n}))=0.\end{split}

(3.3)

The same argument works if we either enlarge
\xi_{n}
or shrink
\nu_{n}
in the sense that we can replace
(\xi_{n},\nu_{n})
by
(\widetilde{\xi}_{n},\widetilde{\nu}_{n})
where
\xi_{n}\ll\widetilde{\xi}_{n}\ll\rho_{n}\ll\widetilde{\nu}_{n}\ll\nu_{n}
.

Lemma 3.3

(Existence of
K\geq 1
)
.

If Theorem
1.1
is false, then
K
is well-defined with
K\geq 1
.

Proof.

Assume that Theorem
1.1
is false. Then there exist
\eta>0
, sequences
\tau_{n}\to T_{+}
,
y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\rho_{n}\in(0,\infty)
where
\rho_{n}\leq\sqrt{T_{+}-\tau_{n}}
when
T_{+}<\infty
and
\rho_{n}\leq\sqrt{\tau_{n}}
when
T_{+}=\infty
and sequences
\alpha_{n}\to 0
and
\beta_{n}\to\infty
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\delta}(u(\tau_{n});B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))\geq\eta,\ \lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(\tau_{n});B(y_{n},\beta_{n}\rho_{n})\setminus B(y_{n},\alpha_{n}\rho_{n}))=0.

(3.4)

The existence of the sequences
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
follows from Lemma
2.14
or Lemma
2.17
when
\rho_{n}\simeq\sqrt{T_{+}-\tau_{n}}
or
\rho_{n}\simeq\sqrt{\tau_{n}}
.

Next, we can find a sequence
\sigma_{n}
such that
\sigma_{n}<\tau_{n}
,
\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}\to T_{+}
,
|[\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}]|\ll\rho_{n}^{2}
and

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{n}^{2}\|\partial_{t}u(\sigma_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.\end{split}

(3.5)

To see this, assume to the contrary. Then there exist constants
c,c_{0}>0
such that up to a subsequence we have

\begin{split}\rho_{n}^{2}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq c_{0},\end{split}

(3.6)

for all
t\in[\tau_{n}-c\rho_{n}^{2},\tau_{n}].
However, this yields a contradiction since
u(t)
has finite energy, and therefore by the energy identity (
1.4
) we have

\begin{split}\infty>\int_{0}^{T_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\geq\sum_{n}\int_{\tau_{n}-c\rho_{n}^{2}}^{\tau_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}u(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\geq c_{0}\sum_{n}\int_{\tau_{n}-c\rho_{n}^{2}}^{\tau_{n}}\rho_{n}^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\infty.\end{split}

(3.7)

Using (
2.25
), with
t_{1}=\sigma_{n}
,
t_{2}=\tau_{n}
, cut-off function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(y_{n},\beta_{n}\rho_{n})\setminus B(y_{n},\alpha_{n}\rho_{n}))
and showing that the error terms vanish as in the proof of Lemma
2.13
, we get

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(\sigma_{n});B(y_{n},2^{-1}\beta_{n}\rho_{n})\setminus B(y_{n},2\alpha_{n}\rho_{n}))=0.\end{split}

(3.8)

Applying the sequential bubbling Theorem
2.18
to
u(\sigma_{n})
, we obtain a bubble tree decomposition (
2.154
) along some subsequence of
\sigma_{n}
and for some sequence
R_{n}\to\infty
. Since energy vanishes in the neck region (
3.8
), we see that

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\boldsymbol{\delta}(u(\sigma_{n});B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))=0.\end{split}

(3.9)

By Lemma
2.3
we can find
K\geq 0
such that

\begin{split}K={\frac{\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(\sigma_{n});B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))}{\bar{E}^{*}}}.\end{split}

(3.10)

Thus, we have verified all the items in the Definition
3.1
for the interval
[\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}]
, which shows that
K
is well defined and that
K\geq 0.

To see that
K\geq 1
, we argue by contradiction. Suppose
K=0
. Let
\xi_{n},\nu_{n}
be sequences as in Remark
3.2
. Then, since
K=0
, we get
\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}(u(\sigma_{n});B(y_{n},\rho_{n}))<\bar{E}_{*}
. This implies that
u(\sigma_{n})
cannot be close to any non-trivial multi-bubble configuration since any non-trivial bubble carries at least energy
\bar{E}_{*}
. Therefore, by item (1) in Definition
3.1
, we get

Remark 3.5

(Proof Sketch)
.

Since the proof of Lemma
3.4
is quite involved, we give a summary of the key ideas. As usual, we will argue by contradiction. Thus, there exists a sequence of intervals
[s_{n},t_{n}]\subset[\sigma_{n},\tau_{n}]
such that
|[s_{n},t_{n}]|\ll\lambda^{2}_{\max,n}.
The idea then is to contradict the minimality of
K\geq 1
since size of the interval
[s_{n},t_{n}]
is too short compared to the scale
\lambda_{\max,n}
implying that the collisions are captured on small balls
B(y^{\prime}_{n},\rho^{\prime}_{n})\subset B(y_{n},\rho_{n})
with
\rho^{\prime}_{n}\ll\rho_{n}
. As we do not see the large scales
\lambda_{\max,n}
in these small balls
B(y_{n}^{\prime},\rho_{n}^{\prime})
, we deduce that these small balls must carry strictly smaller energy in the sense of the last item in Definition
3.1
, which will contradict the minimality of
K.

To make the above argument precise, it will be helpful to organize the bubbles that will arise when the localized distance
{\bf d}
vanishes. To that end, we first distinguish the bubbles based on the size of their
\dot{H}^{1}
-interaction. In particular, if this interaction vanishes, then we say that the bubbles are asymptotically orthogonal.

Definition 3.6

(Asymptotic Orthogonality of Scales)
.

We say that two triples
(W_{j},a_{j,n},\lambda_{j,n})
and
(W_{j^{\prime}},a_{j^{\prime},n},\lambda_{j^{\prime},n})
are
asymptotically orthogonal
if

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\frac{\lambda_{j,n}}{\lambda_{j^{\prime},n}}+\frac{\lambda_{j^{\prime},n}}{\lambda_{j,n}}+\frac{\left\lvert{a_{j,n}-a_{j^{\prime},n}}\right\rvert^{2}}{\lambda_{j,n}\lambda_{j^{\prime},n}}\Big)=\infty,\end{split}

(3.16)

where
W_{j},W_{j^{\prime}}
are non-zero stationary solutions of (
1.5
),
a_{j,n},a_{j^{\prime},n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are sequences of points, and
\lambda_{j,n},\lambda_{j^{\prime},n}\in(0,\infty)
are sequences of scales. We will use the short hand
(W_{j},a_{j,n},\lambda_{j,n})\perp(W_{j^{\prime}},a_{j^{\prime},n},\lambda_{j^{\prime},n})
if the two triples are asymptotically orthogonal. See Proposition B.2 in
[
24
]
to understand the connection between (
3.16
) and the integral interaction between the bubbles
W_{j}
and
W_{j^{\prime}}
in the case when
W_{j},W_{j^{\prime}}\geq 0.

Using the above notion of asymptotic orthogonality, we can organize a family of bubbles into a tree-like structure.

Definition 3.7

(Bubble Tree)
.

Given two collections of stationary solutions
\mathfrak{h}_{1}=\{W_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{2}=\{\widetilde{W}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, then
\mathfrak{h}_{1}\prec\mathfrak{h}_{2}
iff

\begin{split}\frac{\lambda(\widetilde{W}_{n})}{\lambda(W_{n})}\to\infty{\ \ \text{and}\ \ }\exists C>0\text{ such that }B(a(W_{n}),\lambda(W_{n}))\subset B(a(\widetilde{W}_{n}),C\lambda(\widetilde{W}_{n}))\text{ for all }n\gg 1.\end{split}

(3.17)

Then we say that
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
is the parent and
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
is its child. We will also allow for equality in the above relation by using the notation
\mathfrak{h}_{1}\preceq\mathfrak{h}_{2}
. Given
M\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the collection
\{\mathfrak{h}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{h}_{M}\}
where
\mathfrak{h}_{i}=\{W_{k,i}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
W_{k,i}
are stationary solutions. We define a
root
element
\mathfrak{h}_{j}
as an element that is not a child of any parent
\mathfrak{h}_{j^{\prime}}
for
j^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,M\}.
We define the collection of all root-indices as

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}:=\{j\in\{1,\dots,M\}\mid\mathfrak{h}_{j}\textrm{ is a root }\}.\end{split}

(3.18)

Finally, to each root
\mathfrak{h}_{j}
we can define the
bubble tree
as the following collection
\mathcal{T}(j):=\{\mathfrak{h}_{j^{\prime}}\mid\mathfrak{h}_{j^{\prime}}\preceq\mathfrak{h}_{j}\}
and
\mathcal{D}(j)
as the set of all maximal elements (with respect to the partial order
\preceq
) of the pruned tree
\mathcal{T}(j)\setminus\{\mathfrak{h}_{j}\}.

). Thus, we have proved Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We treat the finite–time blow–up case
T_{+}<\infty
; the global case is analogous. Throughout we write
\rho(t):=\sqrt{T_{+}-t}
.

Step 1.
Reduction to small balls near the bubbling points. Theorem
2.4
furnishes the existence of the set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{L}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a weak limit
u_{*}\in\dot{H}^{1}
. Choosing
0<\rho_{0}\ll 1
so that the balls
B(x_{\ell},2\rho_{0})
are disjoint, we get from Lemmas
2.14
and
2.17
that

\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\bar{E}(u(t)-u_{*};\,\mathbb{R}^{d}\!\setminus\!\cup_{\ell=1}^{L}B(x_{\ell},\rho_{0}))=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\bar{E}(u(t)-u_{*};\,B(x_{\ell},\rho_{0})\!\setminus\!B(x_{\ell},\rho(t))\Bigr)=0

for
1\leq\ell\leq L
. Since
u_{*}\in\dot{H}^{1}
, we have

\bar{E}\bigl(u_{*};B(x_{\ell},\rho(t))\bigr)\to 0
as
t\to T_{+}
. Hence it suffices to study
u(t)
inside the shrinking balls

B(x_{\ell},\rho(t))
.

Step 2.
Bubbling at one blowup point. Fix one bubbling point and denote it by
y:=x_{\ell}
. Theorem
1.9
gives

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to T_{+}}\boldsymbol{\delta}(u(t);B(y,\rho(t)))=0.\end{split}

(4.25)

Let
t_{n}\to T_{+}
be an arbitrary sequence of times. Then there exist

•

an integer
M_{n}\geq 1
, which is also finite since
u(t)
is a finite energy solution;

•

{\bf W}(\vec{W}_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{M_{n}}W_{j,n}
, where
W_{j,n}
are stationary solutions;

•

and scales

\vec{\nu}_{\,n}=(\nu_{0,n},\nu_{1,n},\dots,\nu_{M_{n},n})
and

\vec{\xi}_{\,n}=(\xi_{0,n},\xi_{1,n},\dots,\xi_{M_{n},n})
,

such that

{\bf d}(u(t_{n}),{\bf W}(\vec{W}_{n}),;B(y,\rho(t_{n})),\vec{\nu}_{\,n},\vec{\xi}_{\,n})\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

(4.26)

Upon passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
M_{n}=M
for all
n
.

Step 2.1
Initial bubble tree construction. For every
j\in\{1,\dots,M\}
the map
W_{j,n}
is a stationary solution and therefore applying Theorem
2.18
we get for each fixed
j

•

an integer
M_{j}\geq 0
;

•

a weak limit
\vartheta_{j,0}
;

•

non-zero stationary solutions
\vartheta_{j,1},\dots,\vartheta_{j,M_{j}}
with center
p_{j,k,n}\in B(a(W_{j,n}),\lambda(W_{j,n}))
and scales
\Lambda_{j,k,n}\ll\lambda(W_{j,n}),

such that

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}\Big(W_{j,n}-\vartheta_{j,0}\big(\frac{\cdot-a(W_{j,})}{\lambda(W_{j,n})}\big)-\sum_{k=1}^{M_{j}}\vartheta_{j,k}\big(\frac{\cdot-p_{j,k,n}}{\Lambda_{j,k,n}}\big);B_{j,n}\Big)=0,\end{split}

(4.27)

where
B_{j,n}:=B(a(W_{j,n}),\,R_{n}\lambda(W_{j,n}))
for some
R_{n}\to\infty
, where the scales and centers satisfy

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k\neq k^{\prime}}\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda_{j,k,n}}{\Lambda_{j,k^{\prime},n}}+\frac{\Lambda_{j,k^{\prime},n}}{\Lambda_{j,k,n}}+\frac{|p_{j,k,n}-p_{j,k^{\prime},n}|^{2}}{\Lambda_{j,k,n}\Lambda_{j,k^{\prime},n}}\Bigr)^{-1}=0.

(4.28)

For convenience denote
\Lambda_{j,0,n}:=\lambda(W_{j,n}),p_{j,0,n}:=a(W_{j,n})
so that in every bubble family indexed by
(j,k)
the index
k=0
corresponds to the original scale
\lambda(W_{j,n})
and centre
a(W_{j,n})
.

Step 3
. Refined bubble tree construction. By the construction in the previous step, we have found a family

\begin{split}\{(\vartheta_{j,k},p_{j,k,n},\Lambda_{j,k,n})\}_{j=1,k=0}^{j=M,k=M_{j}}\end{split}

(4.29)

which looks promising, but unfortunately, might not be asymptotically orthogonal. However, we can follow the same argument as in the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
33
]
to construct an asymptotic orthogonal family
(W_{j},a_{j,n},\lambda_{j,n})
. The idea is to analyze the bubble tree as in the proof of Lemma
3.4
. Denote
\mathcal{R}
to be set of root indices obtained after partially ordering the tree
\mathfrak{h}_{j}=\{W_{j,n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and for each
\mathfrak{h}_{j_{0}}\in\mathcal{R}
consider the bubble tree
\mathcal{T}(j_{0}):=\{\mathfrak{h}_{j}\preceq\mathfrak{h}_{j_{0}}\}.
For some large constant
C^{\prime}>0
,
B(a(W_{j,n}),\lambda(W_{j,n}))\subset B(a(W_{j_{0},n}),C^{\prime}\lambda(W_{j_{0},n}))
and therefore the domain
B(a(W_{j_{0},n}),C^{\prime}\lambda(W_{j_{0},n}))
contains all the stationary solutions

\begin{split}\bigcup_{\mathfrak{h}_{j}\in\mathcal{T}(j_{0})}\{(\vartheta_{j,k},p_{j,k,n},\Lambda_{j,k,n})\}_{k=0}^{M_{j}}.\end{split}

(4.30)

We will refine this collection to obtain an asymptotic orthogonal family. To this end, define

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}(j,k):=\{(j,k)\}\cup\big\{(j^{\prime},k^{\prime}):(W_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}},p_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime},n},\Lambda_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime},n})\perp(W_{j,k},p_{j,k,n},\Lambda_{j,k,n})\big\}.\end{split}

(4.31)

For each reference index
j_{0}\in\mathcal{R}
we examine every cluster

\mathcal{K}(j,k)
attached to the preliminary list of triples

\bigl(\vartheta_{j,k},p_{j,k,n},\Lambda_{j,k,n}\bigr)
.

•

Case 1
:
\bigl|\mathcal{K}(j,k)\bigr|=1
:
we keep the lone triple
\bigl(\vartheta_{j,k},p_{j,k,n},\Lambda_{j,k,n}\bigr)
.

•

Case 2:

\bigl|\mathcal{K}(j,k)\bigr|>1
:
discard all triples with first index in
\mathcal{K}(j,k)
and or replace them by a single triple

\bigl(\Theta_{j,k},p_{j,k,n},\Lambda_{j,k,n}\bigr)
, where
\Theta_{j,k}
is a stationary solution. The construction of this new bubble
\Theta_{j,k,n}
uses Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
2.18
, and therefore the argument from the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
33
]
carries over to this setting as well.

Repeating this procedure for every root index
j_{0}\in\mathcal{R}
leaves a final family of triples that are pairwise asymptotically orthogonal and fulfill the conclusions of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

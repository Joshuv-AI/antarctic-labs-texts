Static disorder-induced renormalization of polariton group velocity
Gustavo J. R. Aroeira, Raphael F. Ribeiro

Abstract
Molecular exciton-polaritons exhibit long-range, ultrafast propagation, yet recent experiments have reported far slower propagation than expected. In this work, we implement a nonperturbative approach to quantify how static energetic disorder renormalizes polariton group velocity in strongly coupled microcavities. The method requires no exact diagonalization or master equation propagation, and depends only on measurable parameters: the mean exciton energy and its variance, the microcavity dispersion and the Rabi splitting. Using parameters corresponding to recently probed organic microcavities, we show that exciton inhomogeneous broadening slows both lower and upper polaritons, particularly when the mean exciton energy fluctuation approaches the collective light-matter coupling strength. A detailed discussion and interpretation of these results is provided using perturbation theory in the limit of weak resonance scattering. Overall, our results support the view that exciton–phonon interactions likely dominate the recent experimental observations of polariton slowdown in disordered media.

Perturbative estimates support these trends and provide analytical insight into the numerically obtained behavior.

Methods.
We model a large, isotropic
and homogeneous molecular ensemble under strong coupling with a microcavity with perfectly reflective mirrors. In this regime, polariton energies
E_{\text{P}}(q)
(with
P=\text{LP}
or
UP
), can be approximated as solutions to an effective medium dispersion relation originally derived in the context of molecular polaritons by Litinskaya and Reineker
Litinskaya and Reineker 2006
and that can be expressed as

\displaystyle E_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{C}}(q,m)=\frac{\Omega_{\text{R}}^{2}}{4}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dE^{\prime}\frac{\rho(E^{\prime})}{E_{\text{P}}(q)-E^{\prime}},

(1)

where
E_{\text{C}}(q,m)
is the energy of the photon mode with in-plane wave-vector magnitude
q=\sqrt{q_{x}^{2}+q_{y}^{2}}
, and longitudinal wave-vector
k_{z}(m)=m\pi/L_{\text{C}}
, with
m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
for transverse-electric and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for transverse-magnetic modes,
\Omega_{\text{R}}
is the Rabi splitting, and the probability distribution
\rho(E)
models the exciton static disorder (inhomogeneous broadening). We assume
\rho(E)
is a normal distribution with mean
E_{M}
and variance
\sigma^{2}/2
,

\displaystyle\rho(E)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}\exp\left[-(E-E_{M})^{2}/\sigma^{2}\right].

(2)

With this choice, Eq.
1
can be written as

\displaystyle E_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{C}}(q,m)=\frac{\Omega_{\text{R}}^{2}\sqrt{\pi}}{4i\sigma}e^{-\frac{[E_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{M}}]^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}}\text{erfc}\left[\frac{E_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{M}}}{i\sigma}\right],

(3)

where erfc is the complementary error function
Abramowitz and Stegun 1965
. Due to the fast growth of erfc
(-ix)
for large
x\in\mathbb{R}
, it is numerically advantageous to work instead with the scaled complementary error function
\text{erfcx(z)}=\text{exp}\left(z^{2}\right)\text{erfc}(z)
, where
z=-i[E(q)-E_{M}]/\sigma
as this substitution preserves floating-point accuracy at large
|z|
.

The solutions to Eq.
3
are complex-valued polariton energies
E_{P}(q)
for any nonzero disorder strength
\sigma>0
, with the imaginary part characterizing the finite polariton lifetime due to elastic resonant scattering
Hopfield 1969
;
Lagendijk and Van Tiggelen 1996
;
Litinskaia, Agranovich, and La Rocca 2002
. Static disorder also shifts the real part of
E_{P}(q)
and leads to the renormalized group velocity

\displaystyle v_{g}(q)=\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\text{Re}[E_{\text{P}}(q)].

(4)

As discussed in detail in Refs.
Litinskaya and Reineker 2006
;
Suyabatmaz and Ribeiro 2023
, this approach is suitable so long as the polariton spectral function
A(\mathbf{q},E)=\braket{\mathbf{q}|\delta(E-H)|\mathbf{q}}
(where
\mathbf{q}
corresponds to the microcavity mode with in-plane wave vector
\mathbf{q}
) is narrowly centered around
\text{Re}~E_{P}(q)
. In other words, Eq.
4
provides a non-perturbative estimate of the renormalized polariton group velocity if the disorder-induced broadening
\delta q
satisfies
\delta q/q<1
, where
\delta q
can be estimated from the imaginary part of the renormalized polariton energy
Agranovich, Litinskaia, and Lidzey 2003
;
Litinskaya and Reineker 2006
;
Suyabatmaz and Ribeiro 2023

\displaystyle\delta q=\frac{\text{Im}[E_{\text{P}}(q)]}{\hbar v_{g}(q)}\;.

(5)

The renormalization of the polariton dispersion by static disorder presented in Fig.
1
is only significant when
\sigma
approaches
\Omega_{\text{R}}/2
, and even then, only the frequencies of modes with high exciton content (
>50\%

This uncertainty is generated by the exciton inhomogeneous broadening and neglects additional broadening mechanisms such as microcavity photon leakage and dynamical disorder which further increase the polariton energy width. The static disorder-induced lowering of LP and raising of UP energies can be understood from a weak disorder perturbative expansion of Eq.
3
in powers of
\sigma/\Omega_{\text{R}}
. For modes with
\delta q/q\ll 1
, in the limit where
\sigma/\Omega_{\text{R}}\rightarrow 0
,
|E_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{M}}|/\sigma\rightarrow\infty
, and we can employ the asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle\text{erfc}(z)\sim\frac{e^{-z^{2}}}{z\sqrt{\pi}}\left[\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{2z^{3}}+O\left(z^{-5}\right)\right]\;,~~z\rightarrow\infty

(6)

Inserting the first two terms of Eq.
6
into Eq.
3
leads to the following weak disorder renormalized dispersion relation valid to
O(\sigma^{2}/\Omega_{R}^{2})

\displaystyle[E_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{C}}(q,m)][E_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{M}}]-\frac{\Omega_{\text{R}}^{2}}{4}=\frac{\Omega_{\text{R}}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{8[E^{(0)}_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{M}}]^{2}}\;,

(7)

where
E^{(0)}_{\text{P}}(q)
is the polariton energy obtained from Eq.
1

\sigma\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle[E_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{C}}(q,m)][E_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{M}}]-\frac{\tilde{\Omega}_{\text{R}}^{2}}{4}\approx 0,

(8)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{\text{R}}\approx\Omega_{\text{R}}\sqrt{1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\left[E^{(0)}_{\text{P}}(q)-E_{\text{M}}\right]^{2}}}\;.

(9)

It follows that
\tilde{\Omega}_{\text{R}}>\Omega_{\text{R}}
; hence, static disorder increases the splitting between the LP and UP branches, i.e.,
E_{\text{LP}}(q)<E_{\text{LP}}^{(0)}(q)

From the previous discussion on the renormalization of the polariton dispersion relation, we can deduce static disorder reduces both lower and upper polariton group velocities as we explain next. Let the renormalized polariton energies be written as
E_{\text{P}}(q)=E^{(0)}_{P}(q)+\hbar\delta_{P}(q)
, where
\delta_{P}(q)
is the static disorder induced energy shift. The corresponding renormalized group velocities are given by
v_{\text{P}}(q)=\hbar^{-1}\partial E_{\text{P}}(q)/\partial q=v_{\text{P}}^{(0)}(q)+\delta^{\prime}_{\text{LP}}(q)
, where
\delta^{\prime}_{\text{LP}}(q)=\partial\delta_{\text{P}}(q)/\partial q
. As
q
grows,
\sigma/|E_{\text{LP}}(q)-E_{\text{M}}|
increases resulting in an enhanced polariton gap and greater in magnitude LP redshift (see Eq.
9
). This leads to the conclusion that
\delta^{\prime}_{\text{LP}}(q)<0
and therefore static disorder induces slower LP propagation, i.e.,
v_{\text{LP}}(q)<v_{\text{LP}}^{(0)}(q)
.
For the UP branch, as
q
increases
\tilde{\Omega}_{\text{R}}\rightarrow\Omega_{\text{R}}
(see Eq.
9
). Hence, as shown by Fig.
1
, the effect of static disorder on the UP dispersion subsides at high
q
, i.e.,
\delta_{\text{UP}}(q)\rightarrow 0
. Given that
\delta_{\text{UP}}\geq 0
(from Eqs.
8
and
9
) and that
\delta_{\text{UP}}
becomes smaller as
q
increases, we conclude that
\delta^{\prime}_{\text{UP}}(q)<0
and
v_{\text{UP}}(q)<v_{\text{UP}}^{(0)}(q)
. In summary, the provided perturbative arguments are highly suggestive that static disorder generically reduces polariton group velocities.

1

4

3

\sigma

Because substantial intermolecular interactions protect polaritons from localization

Hong, Xu, and Delor 2025

Hence, our results support the perspective that at finite temperatures (or low temperatures in systems with strong vibronic coupling

Conclusions.
We implemented a nonperturbative framework
Litinskaya and Reineker 2006
to estimate how resonance scattering induced by static energetic disorder renormalizes molecular polariton group velocities. This approach requires only the probability density function for the exciton transition energy, the collective light-matter interaction strength as provided by the Rabi frequency, and the bare microcavity dispersion, so it can be readily applied to a wide range of material platforms.

Our numerical simulations employing parameters corresponding to the polaritonic systems experimentally probed in Refs.
Xu et al. 2023
;
Balasubrahmaniyam et al. 2023
reveal that static disorder can explain at most a minor fraction of the observed group velocity renormalization. Thus, we are led to conclude most of the reported polaritonic slowdown stems from dynamical (phonon-assisted) disorder
Sokolovskii, Luo, and Groenhof 2024
. This mechanism is likely dominant in most molecular exciton-polariton systems, especially in the presence of significant vibronic coupling and moderate temperatures, where inelastic scattering induced by exciton-phonon interactions dominates over elastic scattering due to static fluctuations. Nevertheless, at low temperatures, the thermal phonon population dwindles and elastic scattering induced by a frozen disordered environment takes over. Under these conditions, materials exhibiting strong static energetic disorder and weak vibronic coupling will show polariton group velocity renormalization mirroring the behavior reported here.

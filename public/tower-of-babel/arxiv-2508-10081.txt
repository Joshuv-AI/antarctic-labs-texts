m m -nil-clean nonderogatory matrices
Andrada Pojar

Abstract
. We prove that if 𝔽 \mathbb{F} is a field of positive odd characteristic p , p, and m , m, and n n are positive integers such that m ≥ 2 , m\geq 2, and n ≤ p , n\leq p, every n × n n\times n nonderogatory matrix A ∈ 𝕄 n ​ ( 𝔽 ) A\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}) which is sum of m m idempotents and a nilpotent, has a decomposition A = E 1 + E 2 + ⋯ + E m + V , A=E_{1}+E_{2}+\dots+E_{m}+V, such that E i 2 = E i , E_{i}^{2}=E_{i}, for every i ∈ { 1 , … , m } , i\in\{1,\dots,m\}, and V [ p − 2 m ] + 2 = 0 . V^{[\frac{p-2}{m}]+2}=0.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.

As usual, the letter
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
will stand for the prime field of
p
elements having characteristic
p
, for any positive integer
n
, the notation
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
will denote the full matrix ring of
n\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{F},

and
\mathbb{M}_{m,n}(\mathbb{F})
will denote the full matrix ring of matrices over
\mathbb{F},
with
m
rows and
n
columns.

A square matrix is
nonderogatory
if its characteristic
and minimal polynomials coincide. A matrix is nonderogatory if and only if it is similar to a
companion matrix
C
.
We recall that a companion matrix
C\in Mn(\mathbb{F})
is a matrix of the form

C=C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\ldots&0&-c_{0}\\
1&0&\ldots&0&-c_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&1&-c_{n-1}\end{array}\right).

A square matrix
A
over
\mathbb{F}
is nil-clean if there exist an idempotent
E
and a nilpotent
N
such that
A=E+N.
We consider decompositions of nonderogatory matrices
A,
such that there exist idempotents
E_{i}
,
i\in\{1,2,\dots,m\},
and a nilpotent
N,
with
A=E_{1}+E_{2}\dots+E_{m}+N,
which are
m
-nil-clean decompositions.

Nil-clean decompositions are related to clean ones, introduced by Nicholson in
[
9
]
, when investigating exchange rings and were first studied by Diesl in
[
6
]
. An important result appeared in
[
3
]
and
[
7
]
about them is: every
n\times n
matrix over a division ring
D
is nil-clean if and only if
D=\mathbb{F}_{2}.

A theme related to a set
S
of nilpotents of a ring is the study of boundness of the nilpotence index of S–the existence of a positive integer
n
such that
a^{n}=0
for all
a\in S
, and it was first studied first in
[
8
]
. Šter has proved that there exists a nil-clean decomposition of every matrix of
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{2}),
with nilpotent
N
having nilpotence index at most
4
(i.e
N^{4}=0.
). Moreover this result has sharpness, that is there exist
4\times 4
companion matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{2},
which cannot be decomposed as a sum of an idempotent and a nilpotent matrix of nilpotence index at most
3.
Also in
[
11
]
it has been proved that every nonderogatory matrix
A
over a field of positive odd characteristic
p,
that is sum of a
p
-potent matrix and a nilpotent matrix, has a decomposition
A=E+V,
such that
E^{p}=E,
and
V^{3}=0.
Moreover, this result has sharpness, that is there exists a
3\times 3
companion matrix over a field of characteristic
3,
which is sum of a tripotent and a nilpotent, but cannot be decomposed as a sum of a tripotent and a nilpotent matrix of nilpotence index at most
2.

An extension of nil-clean decompositions to finite fields of odd cardinality
q,
was done in
[
1
]
: every matrix over such a field is the sum of a
q
-potent
E=E^{q},
and a nilpotent. In
[
2
]
there is even more – the nilpotent
N
involved in such a decomposition can be with nilpotence index at most
3
(i.e.
N^{3}=0).

In
[
4
]
it has been proved that every nil-clean nonderogatory
n\times n
matrix
A,
over a field of positive characteristic,
p,
with trace of
A
not equal to
1
has a decomposition
A=E+V,
such that
E^{2}=E,
and
V^{p+1}=0.
If
\mbox{trace}(A)=1,
then there is a similar decomposition with
V^{p+2}=0.

We study decompositions of nonderogatory matrices that are sums of
m
idempotents and a nilpotent. By Lemma 3.1 from
[
5
]
we know that the trace of such a matrix is an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}.
If the characteristic
p
of the field is less then the size
n
of the matrix, then a companion matrix with trace an integer multiple of unity is just nil-clean, so we can assume
n\leq p.
The following theorem provides a characterization for
m
-nil-clean companion matrices.

Theorem 1
.

[
5
]

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer. Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of positive odd characteristic
p,
and
1<n\leq p
. Let
C=C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}}
be a companion matrix.
Let
c
be the nonnegative integer such that
-c_{n-1}=c\cdot 1.

The following hold:

(1)

if
c=0
and
mn-1<p
then
C
is
m-
nil-clean if and only if
C
is nilpotent or
C-(m-1)I_{n}
is unipotent;

(2)

if
c=t
,
1\leq t\leq m
then
C
is
t-
nil-clean (
1-
nil-clean is just nil-clean), so is
m-
nil-clean;

(3)

if
c\in\{m,m+1,\ldots,mn-2,mn-1\}
then
C
is
m-
nil-clean,

(4)

if
mn-2\geq p
then
C
is
m-
nil-clean;

(5)

Assume
mn-2<p,

(a)

if
c=mn
and
p=mn-1
then
C
is nil-clean, so is
m-
nil-clean;

(b)

if
c=mn
and
p=mn
then
C
is
m-
nil-clean if and only if
C
is nilpotent or
C-(m-1)I_{n}
is a unipotent matrix;

(c)

if
c=mn
,
p>mn
then
C
is
m-
nil-clean if and only if
C-(m-1)I_{n}
is a unipotent matrix.

(d)

if
c>mn
then
C
is not
m-
nil-clean.

By the assumption
mn-1\geq p
every nonderogatory matrix with trace an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}
is
m
-nil-clean, considering the fact that companion matrices with trace in
\{1,2,\dots,mn-1\}
are
m
-nil-clean. We prove that if
mn-1\geq p\geq n,
every nonderogatory matrix
A
over a field of positive odd characteristic
p,
that is sum of
m
idempotents and a nilpotent, has a decomposition
A=E_{1}+E_{2}+\dots+E_{m}+V,
such that
E_{i}^{p}=E_{i},
for
i\in\{1,\dots,m\},
and
V^{[\frac{p-2}{m}]+2}=0.
Moreover this result has sharpness, that is for
m=2
, and
n=[\frac{p-2}{m}]+2,
there exists an
n\times n
companion matrix, over a field of odd characteristic
p,
which are sum of two idempotents and a nilpotent, but cannot be decomposed as a sum of two idempotents and a nilpotent matrix of nilpotence index at most
[\frac{p-2}{m}]+1.
If
mn-1\leq p-1
then since
m\geq 2,
then

n\leq\frac{p}{m}\leq\frac{p-2}{m}+1<[\frac{p-2}{m}]+2.

Therefore since the nilpotent
V
involved in an
m
-nil-clean decomposition of
A
is such that
V^{n}=0,
we get
V^{[\frac{p-2}{m}]+2}=0.

2.
Useful lemmas

The following lemma provides a useful tool in the study of
m
-nil-clean decompositions for nonderogatory matrices.

Lemma 2
.

[
10
]

Let
k
and
n
be nonzero natural numbers,
n\geq k,
and
\mathbb{F}
be a field. Let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}
be integer multiples of the unity. Every companion matrix
C\in M_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is similar to the matrix
C^{\prime}+\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k},0,\dots,0),
for some companion matrix
C^{\prime}.

Remark 3
.

By Lemma
2
for the companion matrix
C\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
there exist a companion matrix
C^{\prime}
and an invertible matrix
P
such that
C=P(\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}_{k\textrm{-times}},0,\dots,0)+C^{\prime})P^{-1}
. This matrix
P
is upper triangular with all entries on the main diagonal being equal to
1.

The following Lemma is the
m
-nil-clean corespondent of Lemma
2
from
[
4
]
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
n\geq 3
be a positive integer,
\mathbb{F}
a field,
m\geq 2
an integer, and
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\},

such that
n-k
is odd.
Let
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
be a companion matrix. If
\mbox{trace}(C)=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot m-l)\cdot 1,
for some
l\in\{1,2,\dots,mk-1\},

then
C
is similar to a matrix
D
which has a decomposition
D=E_{1}+E_{2}+\dots+E_{m}+V,
such that
E_{i}^{2}=E_{i},

i\in\{1,2,\dots,m\},

V^{k+1}=0,
the first column of
V
is
(0,0,\ldots,0),
and the last line of
E_{m}
is
(0,0,\ldots,0,1).

Proof.

Let
(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n})
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. We construct inductively a new basis
(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n})
in the following way:

f_{1}=e_{1};

for every even
i\in\{2,\ldots,n-k+1\},

f_{i}=Cf_{i-1}-mf_{i-1};

for every odd
i\in\{2,\ldots,n-k+1\},

f_{i}=Cf_{i-1};

for every
i\in\{n-k+2,\ldots,n\},

f_{i}=mf_{i-1}-Cf_{i-1};

It can be proved that for every
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
we have
\textrm{Lin}(\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{j}\})=\textrm{Lin}(\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{j}\}),
hence
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
is a basis for
\mathbb{F}^{n}.

The matrix associated to
C
in the basis
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
has the form

D=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M&N\\
P&Q\end{array}\right),

with

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}m&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&1&m&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&m&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&1&m\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

N=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\dots&0&d_{1}\\
0&0&\dots&0&d_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0&d_{n-k-1}\\
0&0&\dots&0&d_{n-k}\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k,k}(\mathbb{F}),

P=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\dots&0&1\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{k,n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

and
Q=mI_{k}-C_{d_{n-k+1},\ldots,d_{n}}
for some
d_{n-k+1},\ldots,d_{n}\in\mathbb{F}.
Moreover from

\mbox{trace}(M)+\mbox{trace}(Q)=\mbox{trace}(C),

it follows that

\frac{n-k+1}{2}\cdot m+km+d_{n}=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot m-l)\cdot 1,

hence

\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot m+d_{n}=(\frac{n+k+1}{2}\cdot m-l)\cdot 1,

therefore

-d_{n}=l\in\{1,2,\dots,mk-1\}.

By the proofs of Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3 from
[
5
]
, it follows that there exist
r_{i}\in\{1,\dots,n\},
and the idempotents
F_{i}
such that

C_{d_{n-k+1},\ldots,d_{n}}=F_{1}+\dots+F_{m-1}+B,

where
F_{i}=P_{i}\cdot\dots\cdot P_{1}\cdot\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{r_{i}\textrm{-times}},0,\dots,0)\cdot(P_{i}\cdot\dots\cdot P_{1})^{-1},

B=P_{m-1}\cdot\dots\cdot P_{1}\cdot C^{\prime}(P_{m-1}\cdot\dots\cdot P_{1})^{-1},

C^{\prime}
is a
k\times k
companion matrix, with
l_{1}={\mathrm{Tr}}(C^{\prime})\in\{1,\dots,k-1\},
and
P_{i}
is a matrix of the form of
P
from Remark
3
.
Since
l_{1}={\mathrm{Tr}}(C^{\prime})\in\{1,\dots,k-1\},
we derive by Lemma 1 from
[
4
]
that there exists
L_{12}
and
T_{1}
a nilpotent such that
C^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{l_{1}}&L_{12}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)+T_{1}.

By Remark
3
all
P_{i}^{\prime}s
are upper triangular with only
1^{\prime}s
on the main diagonal, therefore there exists
L^{\prime}_{12}
such that

B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{l_{1}}&L^{\prime}_{12}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)+(P_{m-1}\cdot\dots\cdot P_{1})^{-1}\cdot T_{1}P_{m-1}\cdot\dots\cdot P_{1}.

Let
T
be the nilpotent matrix
T=-(P_{m-1}\cdot\dots\cdot P_{1})^{-1}\cdot T_{1}P_{m-1}\cdot\dots\cdot P_{1}

We consider

V=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R&S\\
0&T\end{array}\right),

with

R=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&1&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&1&0\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

(where
0
and
1
alternate on the diagonal below the main one),

S=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\dots&0&d_{1}\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&\dots&0&d_{3}\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0&d_{n-k}\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k,k}(\mathbb{F}),

Since
RS=0
it is easy to see that
V^{k+1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R^{k+1}&ST^{k}\\
O&T^{k+1}\end{array}\right).
But
T
is a
k
by
k
nilpotent, and therefore
V^{k+1}=0.
Note that the first column of
V
is
(0,\dots,0).

In order to see that
D-V
is sum of
m
idempotents we observe that it has the form

D-V=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&Y\\
Z&K\end{array}\right),

with

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}m&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&m&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&m&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0&m\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

Y=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&d_{2}\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&d_{4}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&d_{n-k-1}\\
0&0&0&0&0&0\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{n-k,k}(\mathbb{F}),

Z=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\dots&0&1\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&\dots&0&0\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{k,n-k}(\mathbb{F}),

and

K=Q-T=mI_{k}-C_{d_{n-k+1},\dots,d_{n}}-T=

mI_{k}-(F_{1}+F_{2}+\dots+F_{m-1}+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{l_{1}}&L^{\prime}_{12}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)-T)-T=

I_{k}-F_{1}+\dots+I_{k}-F_{m-1}+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-L^{\prime}_{12}\\
0&I_{l_{1}-k}\end{array}\right)\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(\mathbb{F}).

Take

E_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&1&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&1\end{array}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{matrix}\large{0}\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}\large{0}\end{matrix}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{matrix}\large{I_{k}-F_{i}}\end{matrix}\end{pmatrix},

for
i\in\{1,\dots,m-1\},

and

E_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0&1\end{array}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&\dots&0&0\\
0&\dots&0&d_{2}\\
0&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\dots&0&0\\
0&\dots&0&d_{n-k-1}\\
0&\dots&0&0\end{array}\\
\hline\cr\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}0&0&0&\dots&0&0&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\end{array}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-L^{\prime}_{12}\\
0&I_{k-l_{1}}\end{array}\end{pmatrix}

has the last line
(0,\dots,0,1)
and is of the form of the idempotent
D-V
from Lemma
2
in
[
4
]
.
For us

D-V=E_{1}+\dots+E_{m},

and in conclusion the Lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 5
.

The transition matrix from the canonical basis of
F^{n},
to the basis constructed in Lemma
4
is upper triangular, with each entry on the main diagonal being equal to
1,
or
-1.

Lemma 6
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
m\geq 3
an integer. Suppose
\mathbb{F}
is a field and
C\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is a companion matrix, with
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)=\frac{n}{2}\cdot(m-1)+l,
for some integer
l\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}.
Then
C
is similar to a matrix
D
which has a decomposition
D=E_{1}+E_{2}+\dots+E_{m}+V,
such that
E_{i}^{2}=E_{i},

i\in\{1,2,\dots,m\},

V^{2}=0,
the first column of
V
is
(0,0,\ldots,0),
and the last line of
E_{m}
is
(0,0,\ldots,0,1).

Proof.

By Lemma
2
,
C
is similar with a matrix of the form

D=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}m-1&0&0&\dots&0&0&-d_{0}\\
1&0&0&\dots&0&0&-d_{1}\\
0&1&m-1&\dots&0&0&-d_{2}\\
0&0&1&\dots&0&0&-d_{3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0&-d_{n-3}\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&m-1&-d_{n-2}\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&1&-d_{n-1}\end{array}\right)

Since
{\mathrm{Tr}}(D)={\mathrm{Tr}}(C),
we derive that
-d_{n-1}=l\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}.
It follows that there exist
e_{i}\in\{0,1\}
, for every
i\in\{1,\dots,m-2\}
such that

-d_{n-1}=e_{1}+\dots+e_{m-2}+0+1.

Now consider the following
n\times n
matrices

•

the idempotents
E_{i}=\mathrm{diag}(1,0,1,\dots,0,1,e_{i}),
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,m-3\};

•

the idempotent
E_{m-2}=\mathrm{diag}(1,0,1,\dots,1,0,0,e_{m-2});

•

the idempotent
E_{m-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{matrix}\large{0}\end{matrix}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{matrix}\large{0}\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}\large{0}\end{matrix}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
1&0\end{array}\end{pmatrix}

•

the matrix

E_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&1&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0&1\end{array}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
-d_{1}\\
0\\
-d_{2}\\
\vdots\\
-d_{n-3}\\
0\end{array}\\
\hline\cr\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\end{array}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{array}[]{c}1\end{array}\end{pmatrix}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&Y\\
0&1\end{array}\right),

which is idempotent since
X
is idempotent, and
XY=0;
moreover it has last line
(0,\dots,0,1);

•

the matrix

V=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&1&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&1&0\end{array}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{array}[]{c}-d_{0}\\
0\\
-d_{2}\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
-d_{n-1}\end{array}\\
\hline\cr\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\end{array}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{array}[]{c}0\end{array}\end{pmatrix}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}R&S\\
0&0\end{array}\right),

which is such that
V^{2}=0,
since
R^{2}=0
, and
RS=0;

The following Proposition proves that in general we cannot decrease the bound
of the nilpotence degrees of the nilpotent matrices which can be used to obtain
decompositions as in Theorem
7
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
a field of odd characteristic
p,
and
n=[\frac{p-2}{2}]+2.
Suppose
C\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is a companion matrix, with
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)=(2n-1)\cdot 1,
which does not have
0
or
1
as eigenvalue. Assume
C=E_{1}+E_{2}+V,
such that
E_{1}^{2}=E_{1},

E_{2}^{2}=E_{2},
and
V
is nilpotent. Then
V^{[\frac{p-2}{2}]+1}\neq 0.

Proof.

There exist
k_{1},
and
k_{2}
such that

E_{1}\sim\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{k_{1}\textrm{-times}},0,\dots,0),

and

E_{2}\sim\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{k_{2}\textrm{-times}},0,\dots,0).

Then
{\mathrm{Tr}}(E_{1})=(rankE_{1})\cdot 1=k_{1}\cdot 1,
and

{\mathrm{Tr}}(E_{2})=(rankE_{2})\cdot 1=k_{2}\cdot 1.

Since
{\mathrm{Tr}}(V)=0,
we obtain the following

1\cdot 1=(2n-1)\cdot 1={\mathrm{Tr}}(C)=(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot 1.

We derive that
\{k_{1},k_{2}\}=\{n-1,n\},
or
\{k_{1},k_{2}\}=\{0,1\}.

Assume
V^{n-1}=0,
then
V
has at least two blocks in its Frobenius normal form, and therefore at least two zero lines in this form. We derive that
rk(V)\leq n-2.

Since
0
is not an eigenvalue for
C,
we obtain that

n=rk(C)\leq rk(E_{1})+rk(E_{2})+rk(V).

Hence
rk(E_{1})+rk(E_{2})\geq 2,
and therefore
\{k_{1},k_{2}\}\neq\{0,1\}.

Moreover
2
is not an eigenvalue for
C,
and we have:

n=rk(2I_{n}-C)\leq rk(I_{n}-E_{1})+rk(I_{n}-E_{2})+rk(-V).

Hence
rk(I_{n}-E_{1})+rk(I_{n}-E_{2})\geq 2,
and therefore
\{k_{1},k_{2}\}\neq\{n-1,n\}.

In conclusion
V^{n-1}\neq 0.

∎

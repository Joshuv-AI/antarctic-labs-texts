Magnetic tunneling between disc-shaped obstacles
Søren Fournais, Léo Morin

Abstract
. In this paper we derive formulae for the semiclassical tunneling in the presence of a constant magnetic field in 2 2 dimensions. The ‘wells’ in the problem are identical discs with Neumann boundary conditions, so we study the magnetic Neumann Laplacian in the complement of a set of discs. We provide a reduction method to an interaction matrix, which works for a general configuration of obstacles. When there are two discs, we deduce an asymptotic formula for the spectral gap. When the discs are placed along a regular lattice, we derive an effective operator which gives rise to the famous Harper’s equation. Main challenges in this problem compared to recent results on magnetic tunneling are the fact that one-well ground states have non-trivial angular momentum which depends on the semiclassical parameter, and the existence of eigenvalue crossings.

1.4.
Link with the Almost-Mathieu operator (or Harper equation).

The effective operator
\mathbf{X}
from Theorem
5
is in fact unitarily equivalent to a direct integral of Almost-Mathieu operators. Indeed, we can first conjugate
\mathbf{X}
by the unitary diagonal matrix
{\rm{diag}}\big(e^{i\frac{B}{2h}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\big)_{\alpha\in V}
. Thus
\mathbf{X}
is unitarily equivalent to
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}
with

\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}=e^{i\frac{B}{2h}(\beta_{1}+\alpha_{1})(\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2})},\qquad|\alpha-\beta|=L

This is a convolution in the variable
\alpha_{2}
, and it can be diagonalized using the following Fourier transform,

(\mathscr{F}f)(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-i\theta\alpha_{2}/L}f_{\alpha_{1}}(\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta,\quad f\in\ell^{2}(L\mathbb{Z})\otimes L^{2}(0,2\pi),

with inverse

(\mathscr{F}^{-1}g)(\alpha_{1},\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{\alpha_{2}\in L\mathbb{Z}}e^{i\theta\alpha_{2}/L}g_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})},\quad g\in\ell^{2}(L\mathbb{Z}^{2}).

Then we have for all
f\in\ell^{2}(L\mathbb{Z})\otimes L^{2}(0,2\pi)
,

(\mathscr{F}^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{\prime}\mathscr{F}f)(\alpha_{1},\theta)=f(\alpha_{1}-L,\theta)+f(\alpha_{1}+L,\theta)+2\cos\big(\theta-\frac{BL}{h}\alpha_{1}\big)f(\alpha_{1},\theta).

Therefore,

\mathscr{F}^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{\prime}\mathscr{F}=\int_{(0,2\pi)}^{\oplus}\mathscr{H}_{\theta,\frac{BL^{2}}{2\pi h}}\mathrm{d}\theta,

where
\mathscr{H}_{\theta,\phi}
is the Almost-Mathieu (or Harper) operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,

(\mathscr{H}_{\theta,\phi}u)(n)=u(n-1)+u(n+1)+2\cos(\theta+2\pi n\phi)u(n).

This operator has been object of many works (see for instance
[
2
,
16
,
17
,
20
]
, and the recent surveys
[

21
]

\phi

•

If
\phi

is purely absolutely continuous and independent of

•

If
\phi
is irrational, the spectrum of
\mathscr{H}_{\theta,\phi}
is purely singular continuous

]

•

If

The picture of the spectrum of

Organization of the paper

m_{+}
to
\xi_{h}
satisfy

\begin{cases}m_{-}&=\xi_{h}-\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\\
m_{+}&=\xi_{h}+\frac{1}{2}+o(1).\end{cases}

Which one is the closest depends on
h
. The two smallest eigenvalues of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}
are given by

\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}=\{\mu_{-},\mu_{+}\},

where
\mu_{\pm}=\lambda_{1}(\mathscr{L}_{h,m_{\pm}})
.

In any case, we will always denote by
\Phi^{-}
and
\Phi^{+}
the eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}
with respective momenta
m_{-}
and
m_{+}=m_{-}+1
. In other words,

(2.3)

e^{-im_{\pm}\theta}\Phi^{\pm}\quad{\text{is the ground state of}}\quad\mathscr{L}_{h,m_{\pm}}.

We denote by
\mu_{\pm}
the associated eigenvalue.

2.2.
Decay of the eigenfunctions

A fundamental aspect of our analysis is that we know precisely how fast the eigenfunctions
\Phi^{\pm}
decay away from
r=R
. The decay rate is given by the function

(2.4)

d(r)=\frac{B}{4}(r^{2}-R^{2})-\frac{BR^{2}}{2}\ln(r/R),

which we call
Agmon distance
by analogy with the case of potential wells. Indeed, one has

(2.5)

|\Phi^{\pm}(r)|\leq C_{\eta}e^{-\frac{(1-\eta)d(r)}{h}},

for all
\eta>0
. We use these bounds all through the paper. They are consequences of the following lemma, recalling that
m_{\pm}\sim\frac{BR^{2}}{2h}
as
h\to 0
.

Lemma 7
.

The eigenfunctions
\Phi^{\pm}
of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}
, defined in (
2.3
), are of the form

(2.6)

\Phi^{\pm}(r,\theta)=\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{4}}\Big(\frac{re^{i\theta}}{R}\Big)^{m_{\pm}}e^{-\frac{B}{4h}(r^{2}-R^{2})}u_{h}(r),

where
u_{h}
is a real-analytic function on
{\rm{Re}}(r^{2})>0
, which is locally uniformly bounded with respect to
h
. Moreover,

•

For all
r
such that
{\rm{Re}}(r^{2})>R^{2}
,
u_{h}(r)
is asymptotically given by

(2.7)

u_{h}(r)=\frac{K_{0}\Gamma(\delta_{0})}{R}\Big(\frac{2R^{2}}{r^{2}-R^{2}}\Big)^{\delta_{0}}\Big(1+o\Big(\frac{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}{r^{2}-R^{2}}\Big)\Big),

as
h\to 0
, where
\delta_{0}=\frac{1-\Theta_{0}}{2}
and
K_{0}>0
is a universal constant.

•

For all
t>0
there is a
v(t)>0
such that

(2.8)

u_{h}\Big(R+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}\Big)=\frac{K_{0}v(t)}{R}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}-1}{4}}\big(1+o(1)\big)

as
h\to 0
. In fact,
v
is a solution to

-v^{\prime\prime}(t)+2(t-\sqrt{\Theta_{0}})v^{\prime}(t)+v(t)=\Theta_{0}v(t).

•

For all
t>0
,

(2.9)

\partial_{r}u_{h}\Big(R+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}\Big)=\frac{K_{0}v^{\prime}(t)}{R^{2}}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}-3}{4}}\big(1+o(1)\big)

as
h\to 0
.

Proof.

We have an explicit formula for
\Phi^{\pm}
in terms of the Kummer function,

\Phi^{\pm}(r,\theta)=C_{\pm}e^{im_{\pm}\theta}r^{m_{\pm}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{Br^{2}}{4h}(1+2s)}(1+s)^{m_{\pm}-\delta_{\pm}}s^{\delta_{\pm}-1}ds,

where
\delta_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\mu_{\pm}}{2Bh}
, and
C_{\pm}\neq 0
is an
h
-dependent normalization constant. This formula can be derived as in
[
8
]
, but one can also check directly that it is indeed a solution to the eigenvalue equation. In particular,
\Phi^{\pm}
has the form (
2.6
), with

(2.10)

u_{h}(r)=C_{\pm}R^{m_{\pm}}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{4}}e^{-\frac{BR^{2}}{4h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{Bsr^{2}}{2h}+m_{\pm}\ln(1+s)}\frac{s^{\delta_{\pm}-1}}{(1+s)^{\delta_{\pm}}}\mathrm{d}s.

The remaining of the proof consists in estimating
u_{h}
and
C_{\pm}
using the Laplace method. Note that the phase involves the two different frequencies
h
and
\sqrt{h}
via
m_{\pm}
.

1. To estimate
u_{h}(r)
, we start by the substitution
s=\frac{h}{BR^{2}}s^{\prime}
suggested by the Laplace method, which gives

u_{h}(r)=C_{\pm}R^{m_{\pm}}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{4}+\delta_{\pm}}e^{-\frac{BR^{2}}{4h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{sr^{2}}{2R^{2}}+m_{\pm}\ln\big(1+\frac{h}{BR^{2}}s\big)}s^{\delta_{\pm}-1}\big(1+\mathcal{O}(hs)\big)\mathrm{d}s.

The involved phase satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{sr^{2}}{2R^{2}}-m_{\pm}(h)\ln\big(1+\frac{h}{BR^{2}}s\big)=\frac{(r^{2}-R^{2})s}{2R^{2}}+\mathscr{O}(\sqrt{h}s).

Therefore, the Laplace method gives

(2.11)

u_{h}(r)=C_{\pm}R^{m_{\pm}}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\Theta_{0}}{4}}e^{-\frac{BR^{2}}{4h}}\Big(\frac{2R^{2}}{r^{2}-R^{2}}\Big)^{\delta_{0}}\Gamma(\delta_{0})\Big(1+o\Big(\frac{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}{r^{2}-R^{2}}\Big)\Big),

where we also replaced
\delta_{\pm}
by its asymptotic value
\delta_{0}=\frac{1-\Theta_{0}}{2}
.

2. Near
r=R
we need finer estimates, and we consider for any
t>0
,

u_{h}\Big(R+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}\Big)=C_{\pm}R^{m_{\pm}}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{4}}e^{-\frac{BR^{2}}{4h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{Bs}{2h}\big(R+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}\big)^{2}+m_{\pm}\ln(1+s)}\frac{s^{\delta_{\pm}-1}}{(1+s)^{\delta_{\pm}}}\mathrm{d}s.

This time, the Laplace method suggests the substitution
s=\sqrt{\frac{h}{BR^{2}}}s^{\prime}
, and we obtain

u_{h}\Big(R+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}\Big)=C_{\pm}R^{m_{\pm}}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{4}+\frac{\delta_{\pm}}{2}}e^{-\frac{BR^{2}}{4h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-f(s,t,h)}s^{\delta_{\pm}-1}\big(1+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{h}s)\big)\mathrm{d}s,

with phase

\displaystyle f(s,t,h)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{BR^{2}}{h}}\frac{s}{2}\Big(1+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{BR^{2}}}\Big)^{2}-m_{\pm}\ln\Big(1+s\sqrt{\frac{h}{BR^{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{BR^{2}}{h}}\frac{s}{2}+ts+\frac{t^{2}s}{2}\sqrt{\frac{h}{BR^{2}}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{2h}+\sqrt{\frac{\Theta_{0}BR^{2}}{h}}+\mathscr{O}(1)\Big)\Big(s\sqrt{\frac{h}{BR^{2}}}-\frac{hs^{2}}{2BR^{2}}+\mathscr{O}(h^{3/2}s^{3})\Big)

\displaystyle=ts+\frac{s^{2}}{4}-\sqrt{\Theta_{0}}s+\mathscr{O}(\sqrt{h}s(1+s^{2})).

Thus, the Laplace method gives

(2.12)

u_{h}\Big(R+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}\Big)=C_{\pm}R^{m_{\pm}}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{BR^{2}}{4h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\big(\frac{s^{2}}{4}+ts-\sqrt{\Theta_{0}}s\big)}s^{\delta_{0}-1}\mathrm{d}s\big(1+o(1)\big).

3. We now estimate
C_{\pm}
, using that
\|\Phi^{\pm}\|=1
. In polar coordinates we deduce

1=\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{2}}\int_{R}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{r}{R}\Big)^{2m_{\pm}}e^{-\frac{B}{2h}(r^{2}-R^{2})}|u_{h}(r)|^{2}2\pi r\mathrm{d}r.

We use the substitution
r=R+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}
, and the estimate (
2.12
) to obtain

1=C_{\pm}^{2}R^{2m_{\pm}+1}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{1-\frac{\Theta_{0}}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{B}{2h}\big(R+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}\big)^{2}+2m_{\pm}\ln\big(1+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{BR^{2}}}\big)}v(t)^{2}2\pi R\big(1+o(1)\big)\mathrm{d}t,

where

v(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\big(\frac{s^{2}}{4}+ts-\sqrt{\Theta_{0}}s\big)}s^{\delta_{0}-1}\mathrm{d}s.

The involved phase satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{B}{2h}\Big(R+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}\Big)^{2}-2m_{\pm}\ln\Big(1+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{BR^{2}}}\Big)=\frac{BR^{2}}{2h}+t^{2}-2t\sqrt{\Theta_{0}}+\mathscr{O}(\sqrt{h}(1+t^{3})).

Thus, the Laplace method gives

1=C_{\pm}^{2}R^{2m_{\pm}+2}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{1-\frac{\Theta_{0}}{2}}e^{-\frac{BR^{2}}{2h}}2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\big(t^{2}-2t\sqrt{\Theta_{0}}\big)}v(t)^{2}\mathrm{d}t\big(1+o(1)\big).

Therefore we find

(2.13)

C_{\pm}=K_{0}R^{-m_{\pm}-1}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{BR^{2}}{4h}}\big(1+o(1)\big),

where
K_{0}^{-2}=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\big(t^{2}-2t\sqrt{\Theta_{0}}\big)}v(t)^{2}\mathrm{d}t
. Now, we can insert (
2.13
) in (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) to find (
2.7
) and (
2.8
).

4. Coming back to (
2.10
) and differentiating we have

\partial_{r}u_{h}(r)=-C_{\pm}R^{m_{\pm}}\frac{Br}{h}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{4}}e^{-\frac{BR^{2}}{4h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{Bsr^{2}}{2h}+m_{\pm}\ln(1+s)}\frac{s^{\delta_{\pm}}}{(1+s)^{\delta_{\pm}}}\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, similarly to (
2.12
) we find

\partial_{r}u_{h}\Big(R+t\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}\Big)=-C_{\pm}R^{m_{\pm}-1}\Big(\frac{h}{BR^{2}}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{BR^{2}}{4h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\big(\frac{s^{2}}{4}+ts-\sqrt{\Theta_{0}}s\big)}s^{\delta_{0}}\mathrm{d}s\big(1+o(1)\big).

Note that the integral is equal to
-v^{\prime}(t)
. It remains to use (
2.13
) to find (
2.9
).
∎

3.
Reduction when
e_{0}\neq\frac{1}{2}

In all of this section, we assume that
e_{0}\in\big(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\big)
, and that
h
is as small as we want along an
e_{0}
-sequence. In this case, Theorem
2
gives a spectral gap between the first two eigenvalues of the single-obstacle operator
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}
,

(3.1)

\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}\geq c_{0}(1-2|e_{0}|)h^{2}+o(h^{2}).

By Lemma
6
,
\mu_{1}
is the ground state energy of
\mathscr{L}_{h,m_{-}}
with

m_{-}=\xi_{h}-e_{0}+o(1)=\frac{BR^{2}}{2h}+\sqrt{\frac{\Theta_{0}BR^{2}}{h}}+\mathcal{C}_{2}-e_{0}+o(1).

The ground state eigenfunction is
\Phi^{-}
.

We fix any discrete set of centers
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with minimal distance

L=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in V\\
\alpha\neq\beta\end{subarray}}|\alpha-\beta|,

and we assume that the disks with centers
\alpha\in V
and radius
R>0
do not touch, i.e.
L>2R
. Then
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
denotes the magnetic-Neumann realization of
\mathscr{L}_{h}
on

\Omega_{V}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in V}D(\alpha,R)\right).

We denote by
\Pi:=\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,\mu_{1}+c_{0}\frac{1-2|e_{0}|}{2}h^{2})}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V})
the spectral projection of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
corresponding to the interval
(-\infty,\mu_{1}+c_{0}\frac{1-2|e_{0}|}{2}h^{2})
, and
\mathscr{E}_{h}={\rm{Ran}}(\Pi)
.

3.1.
Agmon estimates

It is well known that the eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
are exponentially localized near the boundary of the disks
D(\alpha,R)
. This follows from the so-called Agmon estimates. The following is an adaptation of the Agmon estimates in
[
6
, Theorem 8.2.4]
to states
\Psi
which are not necessarily eigenfunctions.

Lemma 8
.

There exist
\kappa,C>0
such that, if
h
is small enough and if
\Psi\in L^{2}(\Omega_{V})
satisfies
\Psi=\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,\frac{1+\Theta_{0}}{2}Bh]}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V})\Psi
, then

\int_{\Omega_{V}}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}(x,\partial\Omega_{V})}\Big(|\Psi|^{2}+h^{-1}|(-ih\nabla-A)\Psi|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|\Psi\|^{2}.

Here
\mathfrak{d}(x,\partial\Omega_{V})
is the Euclidean distance to the obstacles, and
A=(-\frac{B}{2}x_{2},\frac{B}{2}x_{1})
.

Proof.

Define for
M>0
, the regularized (at infinity) distance

\mathfrak{d}_{M}=\min\{\mathfrak{d},M\}.

Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
be non-decreasing with
\chi(t)=0
for
t\leq 1
,
\chi(t)=1
for
t\geq 2
, and let

\chi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\chi(\mathfrak{d}/\varepsilon)
, for
\varepsilon>0
.
Define

{\mathcal{A}}:=\sup_{\{\Phi=\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,\frac{1+\Theta_{0}}{2}Bh]}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V})\Phi\}}\frac{\|\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Phi\|}{\|\Phi\|}\leq e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}M}.

We will prove that for
\varepsilon=\sqrt{h}/2
and for sufficiently small values of
h
,
{\mathcal{A}}
is bounded independently of
h
and
M
.

Let
\Psi=\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,\frac{1+\Theta_{0}}{2}Bh]}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V})\Psi
be normalized.
Consider the expression

(3.2)

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi,\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi\rangle\geq Bh\|\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi\|^{2},

by the support properties of
\chi_{\varepsilon}
.
By the so-called IMS-localization formula (or equivalently: integration by parts), we find

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\Re\langle\chi_{\varepsilon}^{2}e^{2\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi,\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi\rangle+h^{2}\langle\Psi,|\nabla(\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}})|^{2}\Psi\rangle

We use that
\|\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi\|\leq\mathcal{A}\|\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi\|
to deduce

(3.3)

\langle\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi,\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi\rangle\leq\frac{1+\Theta_{0}}{2}Bh{{\mathcal{A}}}\|\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi\|+2\kappa^{2}h\|\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi\|^{2}\\
+2h^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\{1\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\mathfrak{d}(x)\leq 2\}}|e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi|^{2}\,dx.

We choose
\varepsilon=\sqrt{h}/2
and get from combining (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) and taking the supremum over all normalized
\Psi\in{\rm Ran}\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,\frac{1+\Theta_{0}}{2}Bh]}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V})
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle Bh{\mathcal{A}}^{2}\leq(\frac{1+\Theta_{0}}{2}Bh+2h\kappa^{2}){\mathcal{A}}^{2}+8h\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}e^{2\kappa}.

If
\kappa^{2}\leq\frac{1-\Theta_{0}}{4}B
, we therefore get

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}\leq\left(\frac{1-\Theta_{0}}{4}B-\kappa^{2}\right)^{-1}4\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}e^{2\kappa}.

In particular,
{\mathcal{A}}
is bounded independently of
h
and
M
. Using (
3.3
) again, we can also bound

\|(-ih\nabla-A)\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi\|^{2}\leq Ch,

uniformly in
M
and
h
. Therefore

\int_{\Omega_{V}}\Big(\big|\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi\big|^{2}+h^{-1}\big|(-ih\nabla-A)\chi_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}_{M}}\Psi\big|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\leq C.

We can let
M\to\infty
to change
\mathfrak{d}_{M}
into
\mathfrak{d}
. Moreover, on the region where
\chi_{\varepsilon}\neq 1
the exponential is bounded. We deduce that

\int_{\Omega_{V}}\Big(\big|e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}}\Psi\big|^{2}+h^{-1}\big|(-ih\nabla-A)e^{\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{h}}\mathfrak{d}}\Psi\big|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\leq C,

and the result follows.
∎

In particular, using the Agmon estimates we can show that the spectrum of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
in
(-\infty,\mu_{1}+c_{0}\frac{1-2|e_{0}|}{2}h^{2})
is exponentially close to
\mu_{1}
.

Lemma 9
.

If
\lambda\in{\rm{sp}}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V})
is such that
\lambda<\mu_{1}+c_{0}\frac{1-2|e_{0}|}{2}h^{2}
then we have
|\lambda-\mu_{1}|\leq C_{N}h^{N}
for all
N\geq 2
, where
C_{N}>0
is independent of
\lambda
and
h
.

Proof.

Let
\Psi
be in the range of the spectral projector,
\Pi\Psi=\Psi
, and
(\theta_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in V}
a smooth partition of unity such that
\theta_{\alpha}
is supported on a neighborhood of
D(\alpha,R)
and
\sum_{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}^{2}=1
. Then
\theta_{\alpha}\Psi
is in the domain of the single obstacle operator
\mathscr{L}^{\alpha}_{h}
and by the spectral theorem

\sum_{\alpha\in V}\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{\alpha}-\lambda)\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\|^{2}\geq|\mu_{1}-\lambda|^{2}\sum_{\alpha\in V}\|\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\|^{2}=|\mu_{1}-\lambda|^{2}\|\Psi\|^{2},

because
\mu_{1}
is the closest eigenvalue of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{\alpha}
to
\lambda
–this is our assumption on
\lambda
. Since
\theta_{\alpha}\Psi
is also in the domain of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
,

\sum_{\alpha\in V}\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{\alpha}-\lambda)\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\|^{2}\leq\sum_{\alpha\in V}\Big(\|\big[\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V},\theta_{\alpha}\big]\Psi\|^{2}+\|\theta_{\alpha}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\lambda)\Psi\|^{2}\Big)

The commutator is supported away from the disks
D(\alpha,R)
. Therefore, using Agmon estimates from Lemma
8
for
\Psi
, we deduce that it is exponentially small,

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in V}\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{\alpha}-\lambda)\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}h^{N}\|\Psi\|^{2}+\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\lambda)\Psi\|^{2}

We combine this with the lower bound to obtain

(3.6)

|\mu_{1}-\lambda|^{2}\leq C_{N}h^{N}+\inf_{\Psi\neq 0}\frac{\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\lambda)\Pi\Psi\|^{2}}{\|\Pi\Psi\|^{2}}

Since the operator
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
restricted to
\rm{Ran}(\Pi)
is selfadjoint, and
\lambda
in its spectrum, the above infimum is vanishing. This concludes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Approximate eigenfunctions
\Psi_{\alpha}
and
g_{\alpha}

\Phi^{-}
is the ground state of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}
. We can translate and cut-off
\Phi^{-}
to construct quasimodes for
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
. For any
\eta>0
, let
\chi_{\eta}
be a smooth radial cutoff function satisfying

(3.7)

\chi_{\eta}(x)=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if $|x|<L-R-2\eta$}\\
0\quad\text{if $|x|>L-R-\eta$}.\end{cases}

We then define, for
\alpha\in V
,

(3.8)

\Psi_{\alpha}=\tau_{\alpha}^{B}\big(\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{-}\big),\qquad g_{\alpha}=\Pi\Psi_{\alpha}.

In this section, we provide useful estimates on
g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha}
. First of all, note that the states
\Psi_{\alpha}
are quasimodes for
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
, approximately solving the eigenvalue equation.

Lemma 10
.

If
\eta
is small enough then, with the function
d
defined in (
2.4
),

\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})\Psi_{\alpha}\|=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}}\big),\quad\|\Psi_{\alpha}\|=1+\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}}\big),

and the estimates are uniform with respect to
\alpha\in V
.

Proof.

Since
L=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in V\\
\alpha\neq\beta\end{subarray}}|\alpha-\beta|
and by definition of
\chi_{\eta}
, the function
\Psi_{\alpha}
vanishes on an
\eta
-neighborhood of each disk
D(\beta,R)
for
\beta\neq\alpha
. Since it also satisfies the Neumann boundary condition on
D(\alpha,R)
, it is in the domain of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
. Moreover
\tau_{\alpha}^{B}
commutes with
\mathscr{L}_{h}
so we have

\displaystyle\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})\Psi_{\alpha}\|=\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}-\mu_{1})\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{-}\|=\|\big[\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0},\chi_{\eta}\big]\Phi^{-}\|,

where we used that
\Phi^{-}
was an eigenfunction for
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}
with eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
. Now the commutator is supported at distance
|x|>L-R-2\eta
, so we can use the decay properties of
\Phi^{-}
and its gradient from Lemma
7
to get

\displaystyle\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})\Psi_{\alpha}\|=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}}\big),

where we replaced
2\eta
by
3\eta
inside the exponential to control the front powers of
h^{-1}
. We estimate the norm similarly, using that
\|\tau_{\alpha}^{B}\Phi^{-}\|=1
and

\|\Psi_{\alpha}-\tau_{\alpha}^{B}\Phi^{-}\|=\|(\chi_{\eta}-1)\Phi^{-}\|=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}}\big).

Note that in these estimates the translation by
\alpha
does not change the norm, and therefore they are independent of
\alpha
.
∎

It follows that
g_{\alpha}
is a small perturbation of
\Psi_{\alpha}
, and that these vectors form a basis of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
.

Lemma 11
.

For all
\alpha\in V
, the state
\Psi_{\alpha}
and its projection
g_{\alpha}=\Pi\Psi_{\alpha}
on
\mathscr{E}_{h}
satisfy, with the function
d
defined in (
2.4
),

\|g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha}\|=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}\big),\quad\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha}),g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}\big).

Moreover, the vectors
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in V}
span a dense subspace of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
.

Proof.

By definition of the spectral projector
\Pi
we have

\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})(I-\Pi)\Psi_{\alpha}\|\geq c_{0}\frac{(1-2|e_{0}|)h^{2}}{2}\|(I-\Pi)\Psi_{\alpha}\|.

Moreover, the projector
(I-\Pi)
commutes with
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
so we have the upper bound

\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})(I-\Pi)\Psi_{\alpha}\|\leq\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})\Psi_{\alpha}\|=\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{\alpha}-\mu_{1})\Psi_{\alpha}\|=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}}\big),

where the last estimate is given by Lemma
10
. Combining these two bounds we obtain

\|(I-\Pi)\Psi_{\alpha}\|\leq Ch^{-2}e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}},

and this proves the bound on
\|g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha}\|
. To get the quadratic form bound we use a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}(I-\Pi)\Psi_{\alpha},(I-\Pi)\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle\leq\|\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}(I-\Pi)\Psi_{\alpha}\|\|(I-\Pi)\Psi_{\alpha}\|\leq Ch^{-4}e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}},

where we used Lemma
10
again. Note that we can absorb the
h^{-4}
by changing
3\eta
into
4\eta
in the exponential. The bounds are uniform with respect to
\alpha
, since the ones of Lemma
10
are.

Let us now prove that the vectors
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in V}
span a dense subspace of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
. Note that, when
V
is finite
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in V}
is obviously a basis for cardinality reasons, but in the general case an additional argument is needed. Assume that
\Psi\in\mathscr{E}_{h}
is orthogonal to all
g_{\alpha}
’s, and we have to prove that this implies
\Psi=0
. First note that
\Psi
is also orthogonal to
\Psi_{\alpha}
since

(3.9)

\langle\Psi,\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle=\langle\Pi\Psi,\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle=\langle\Psi,g_{\alpha}\rangle=0.

We introduce a family
(\theta_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in V}
of cutoffs such that
\theta_{\alpha}
is localized on a small but fixed neighborhood of
D(\alpha,R)
, and satisfying

\sum_{\alpha\in V}\theta_{\alpha}^{2}=1.

We then use the IMS localization formula,

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi,\Psi\rangle=\sum_{\alpha\in V}\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\rangle-h^{2}\sum_{\alpha\in V}\|(\nabla\theta_{\alpha})\Psi\|^{2}.

Using the Agmon estimates from Lemma
8
, we know that
\Psi
is exponentially localized near the disks. Since
\sum_{\alpha}|\nabla\theta_{\alpha}|^{2}
is supported away from the disks we deduce

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi,\Psi\rangle\geq\sum_{\alpha\in V}\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\rangle-Ch^{N}\|\Psi\|^{2},

where we can choose
N
arbitrarily large (here
N=3
is enough). Note that
\theta_{\alpha}\Psi
vanishes on a neighborhood of all disks
D(\beta,R)
for
\beta\neq\alpha
. Therefore
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi=\mathscr{L}_{h}^{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi
, where we recall that
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{\alpha}
is the operator with a single obstacle
D(\alpha,R)
, i.e.
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{\alpha}=\tau_{\alpha}^{B}\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}
. Thus

(3.10)

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi,\Psi\rangle\geq\sum_{\alpha\in V}\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\rangle-Ch^{N}\|\Psi\|^{2}.

We now introduce the projection
\Pi_{0}
on the ground state of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}
, which is given by

\Pi_{0}\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi=\langle\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\Phi^{-}\rangle\Phi^{-}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\chi_{\alpha}\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}|\langle\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\Phi^{-}\rangle|^{2}+\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}(I-\Pi_{0})\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,(I-\Pi_{0})\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{1}|\langle\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\Phi^{-}\rangle|^{2}+(\mu_{1}+c_{0}(1-2|e_{0}|)h^{2})\|(I-\Pi_{0})\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\|^{2},

where we used that the second eigenvalue of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}
has a gap of size
c_{0}(1-2|e_{0}|)h^{2}
with
\mu_{1}
(Theorem
2
). We reconstruct the
L^{2}
norm of
\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi
and use that
\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}
is an isometry to get

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\chi_{\alpha}\Psi\rangle

(3.11)

\displaystyle\geq\big(\mu_{1}+c_{0}(1-2|e_{0}|)h^{2}\big)\|\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\|^{2}-c_{0}(1-2|e_{0}|)h^{2}|\langle\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\Phi^{-}\rangle|^{2}.

The last term can be estimated as follows. First introduce the cutoff
\chi_{\eta}
defining
\Psi_{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\Phi^{-}\rangle=\langle\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\tau_{\alpha}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{-}\rangle+\langle\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\tau_{\alpha}^{B}(1-\chi_{\eta})\Phi^{-}\rangle.

Using the decay of
\Phi^{-}
and the cutoff we see that the second term is exponentially small. In the first one we use the orthogonality
\langle\Psi,\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle=0
and we find

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{-\alpha}^{B}\theta_{\alpha}\Psi,\Phi^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle(\theta_{\alpha}-1)\Psi,\tau_{\alpha}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{-}\rangle+Ch^{N}\|\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi_{\eta}(.-\alpha)\big(\theta_{\alpha}-1\big)\Psi\|+Ch^{N}\|\theta_{\alpha}\Psi\|.

We insert this last inequality in (
3.11
) and equation (
3.10
) now reads

(3.12)

\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi,\Psi\rangle\geq\big(\mu_{1}+c_{0}(1-2|e_{0}|)h^{2}-Ch^{N}\big)\|\Psi\|^{2}-Ch^{2}\sum_{\alpha\in V}\|\chi_{\eta}(.-\alpha)\big(\theta_{\alpha}-1\big)\Psi\|^{2}.

Since the function
\sum_{\alpha}\chi_{\eta}(.-\alpha)^{2}\big(\theta_{\alpha}-1\big)^{2}
is supported away from the obstacles, we can use Agmon estimates again to see that the last term is exponentially small, and therefore

(3.13)

\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi,\Psi\rangle\geq\big(\mu_{1}+c_{0}(1-2|e_{0}|)h^{2}-Ch^{N}\big)\|\Psi\|^{2}.

But
\Psi\in\mathscr{E}_{h}
, meaning that
\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi,\Psi\rangle\leq(\mu_{1}+c_{0}\frac{1-2|e_{0}|}{2}h^{2})\|\Psi\|^{2}
, and therefore we must have
\Psi=0
. This proves that
(g_{\alpha})
generates a dense subspace of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
.
∎

In the case when the set of disks
V
is infinite, we need a little more information on the decay of
g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha}
, which we give in the following lemma. This result is very weak, since we only get decay when
\langle x-\alpha\rangle\gg h^{-1}
. However, this is enough to obtain decay of coefficients of our infinite interaction matrix away from the diagonal, in the proof of Theorem
13
.

Lemma 12
.

For all
\alpha\in V
we have

\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha})\|=\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}),\qquad\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha})\|=\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}),

where
\langle x\rangle=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
.

Proof.

We recall that
\Psi_{\alpha}
, defined in (
3.8
), is an approximate eigenfunction for
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
. We define the remainder

r_{\alpha}=(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})\Psi_{\alpha},

which satisfies

(3.14)

(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}\Psi_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\mu_{1}-z}\Psi_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\mu_{1}-z}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha},\quad\forall z\notin{\rm{sp}}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}).

Also note that the spectral projection
g_{\alpha}=\Pi\Psi_{\alpha}
can be written as the path-integral

(3.15)

g_{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}\Psi_{\alpha}\mathrm{d}z,

where
\Gamma
is a (clock-wise) circle of center
\mu_{1}
and radius
c_{0}\frac{1-2|e_{0}|}{2}h^{2}
. Therefore using (
3.14
), we have

(3.16)

g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{\mu_{1}-z}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\mathrm{d}z,

and

(3.17)

\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha})\|\leq C\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|

In order to bound the right-hand side, we first consider the weight
\phi(x)=\langle x-\alpha\rangle\rho(x/M)
, where
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is equal to
1
on
D(0,1)
. We will prove estimates uniform in
M\geq 1
and then let
M\to\infty
.

For
z\in\Gamma
, we use the resolvent commutation formula to find

\displaystyle\|e^{h\phi}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}e^{h\phi}r_{\alpha}\|+\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}\big[\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V},e^{h\phi}\big](\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-2}\|e^{h\phi}r_{\alpha}\|+Ch^{-2}\|\big[\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V},e^{h\phi}\big](\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|,

where we used the spectral theorem and Lemma
9
to bound the norm of the resolvent. Let us now investigate the commutator, which is explicitly given by

\big[\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V},e^{h\phi}\big]=-e^{h\phi}\big(2ih^{2}\nabla\phi\cdot(-ih\nabla-A)+h^{3}\Delta\phi+h^{4}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\big),

where
A=(-\frac{B}{2}x_{2},\frac{B}{2}x_{1})
is the vector potential.
Since
\nabla\phi
and
\Delta\phi
are bounded (uniformly with respect to
\alpha
) we deduce

\displaystyle\|\big[\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V},e^{h\phi}\big](\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|e^{h\phi}(-ih\nabla-A)(\mathscr{L}_{h}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|

(3.19)

\displaystyle\qquad+Ch^{3}\|e^{h\phi}(\mathscr{L}_{h}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|.

The first term in (
3.19
) we bound as follows using the notation
u=(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}
.
We integrate by parts, and since
u\in{\rm{Dom}}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V})
has Neumann boundary conditions, we find

\displaystyle\|e^{h\phi}(-ih\nabla-A)u\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle(-ih\nabla-A)e^{2h\phi}(-ih\nabla-A)u,u\rangle

\displaystyle\leq|\langle e^{2h\phi}\mathscr{L}_{h}u,u\rangle|+2|\langle h^{2}\nabla\phi e^{h\phi}(-ih\nabla-A)u,e^{h\phi}u\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{h\phi}\mathscr{L}_{h}u\|\|e^{h\phi}u\|+Ch^{2}\|e^{h\phi}(-ih\nabla-A)u\|\|e^{h\phi}u\|.

In the last term, we use the Cauchy inequality and compare with the left-hand side to get

\displaystyle\|e^{h\phi}(-ih\nabla-A)u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|e^{h\phi}\mathscr{L}_{h}u\|\|e^{h\phi}u\|+Ch^{4}\|e^{h\phi}u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|e^{h\phi}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)u\|\|e^{h\phi}u\|+Ch\|e^{h\phi}u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1}\|e^{h\phi}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)u\|^{2}+Ch\|e^{h\phi}u\|^{2}.

We recall that
u=(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}
and insert this last bound in (
3.19
),

\displaystyle\|\big[\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V},e^{h\phi}\big](\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{3/2}\|e^{h\phi}r_{\alpha}\|+Ch^{5/2}\|e^{h\phi}(\mathscr{L}_{h}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|.

We now insert in (
3.18
) and obtain

\|e^{h\phi}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|\leq Ch^{-2}\|e^{h\phi}r_{\alpha}\|+Ch^{1/2}\|e^{h\phi}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|.

We substract the last term from the left-hand side and get

\|e^{h\phi}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|\leq Ch^{-2}\|e^{h\phi}r_{\alpha}\|.

Then, we let
M\to\infty
, so that
\phi\to\langle x-\alpha\rangle
, and with (
3.17
) this gives

\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha})\|\leq Ch^{-2}\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}r_{\alpha}\|.

Finally, we can estimate the norm of
e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}r_{\alpha}
as in Lemma
10
. Indeed, this estimate follows from the decay of the groundstate
\Phi^{-}
of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}
, which is much faster than
e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}
, and the proof is thus identical. We obtain the first stated result. For the second one, we use (
3.16
) again and apply
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
to find

\displaystyle\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha})\|\leq C\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}r_{\alpha}\|+C\sup_{z\in\Gamma}|z|\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-z)^{-1}r_{\alpha}\|,

and these two terms we estimate as above.
∎

3.3.
The Gram matrix

In the previous section, we constructed a basis
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in V}
of the spectral subspace
\mathscr{E}_{h}
. Before estimating the spectrum of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
restricted to
\mathscr{E}_{h}
, we orthonormalize the basis
(g_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in V}
, thus providing a Hilbertian basis
(e_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in V}
of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
. This is done using the Gram matrix
\mathbf{G}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
we define the error order

(3.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}=e^{-\frac{2S_{h}(L)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L(1+\varepsilon))}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)+d(\varepsilon L+R+2\eta)}{h}},

where
S_{h}
was defined in (
1.3
).
This error is shown to be small enough in Lemma
21
.

Theorem 13
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and assume
h>0
is small enough along an
e_{0}
-sequence with
e_{0}\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
. For all
p\in[1,\infty]
, the infinite matrix
\mathbf{G}=(\langle g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle)_{\alpha,\beta\in V}
defines a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}(V)
. Moreover,

(1)

We have the following estimate in
\ell^{p}
operator norm topology

\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{T}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}),\quad{\text{in}}\quad\mathcal{L}(\ell^{p}(V)),

where
\mathbf{T}_{\alpha\beta}=\langle g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)}
.

(2)

\mathbf{G}^{-1}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}(V)
and has a square root which satisfies

\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\mathbf{I}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{T}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}),\quad{\text{in}}\quad\mathcal{L}(\ell^{p}(V)).

(3)

The vectors

e_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in V}(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\beta}g_{\beta},\qquad\alpha\in V,

define a Hilbert basis of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
.

(4)

The restriction of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
to
\mathscr{E}_{h}
is unitarily equivalent to
\mathbf{M}=(\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}\rangle)_{\alpha,\beta\in V}
as an operator on
\ell^{2}(V)
, and

\mathbf{M}=\mu_{1}\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{W}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}),

where
\mathbf{W}_{\alpha\beta}=\langle(\mathscr{L}_{h}-\mu_{1})\Psi_{\alpha},\Psi_{\beta}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)}
.

Proof.

Some of the technical estimates on the interaction coefficients required in the proof of Theorem
13
are postponed to Appendix
B
below.

1. We prove the result for
p=1
and
p=\infty
. The general statement then follows by the interpolation inequality,

\|\mathbf{A}\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbf{A}\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\mathbf{A}\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{1}{p}},

where here
\|\mathbf{A}\|_{p}
is the operator norm
\ell^{p}(V)\to\ell^{p}(V)
. Note that for hermitian matrices like
\mathbf{G}
, the
\ell^{1}
-norm and
\ell^{\infty}
-norm are equal and given by

\|\mathbf{A}\|_{1}=\|\mathbf{A}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\alpha\in V}\sum_{\beta\in V}|\mathbf{A}_{\alpha\beta}|.

We can write
\mathbf{G}
as follows, decomposing according to
|\alpha-\beta|
,

\mathbf{G}=\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}+\mathbf{T}+\widetilde{\mathbf{R}},

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{\alpha\beta}=\|g_{\alpha}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\alpha=\beta},\quad{\mathbf{T}}_{\alpha\beta}=\langle g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)},\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{\alpha\beta}=\langle g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{|\alpha-\beta|>L(1+\varepsilon)}.

We first estimate the norm of
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}
,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}\|_{1}=\sup_{\alpha\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in V\\
|\alpha-\beta|>L(1+\varepsilon)\end{subarray}}|\langle g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle|.

By the Pythagorean Theorem we have
\langle g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle=\langle\Psi_{\alpha},\Psi_{\beta}\rangle+\langle g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha},g_{\beta}-\Psi_{\beta}\rangle
. Remember that
\Psi_{\alpha}
is supported on
D(\alpha,L-R-\eta)
. Therefore, given
\alpha\in V
, the scalar product
\langle\Psi_{\alpha},\Psi_{\beta}\rangle
vanishes for all but finitely many
\beta
’s. For those we can use Lemma
18
below, and we deduce

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}\|_{1}\leq\sup_{\alpha\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in V\\
|\alpha-\beta|>L(1+\varepsilon)\end{subarray}}|\langle g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha},g_{\beta}-\Psi_{\beta}\rangle|+\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L(1+\varepsilon))}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)+d(\varepsilon L+R+2\eta)}{h}}\big)

To estimate the remaining sum, we use that
\langle\alpha-\beta\rangle\leq\langle\alpha-x\rangle+\langle\beta-x\rangle
to insert a decaying weight,

\displaystyle|\langle g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha},g_{\beta}-\Psi_{\beta}\rangle|\leq e^{-h\langle\alpha-\beta\rangle}\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha})\|\|e^{h\langle x-\beta\rangle}(g_{\beta}-\Psi_{\beta})\|,

and then we use Lemma
12
to get

\displaystyle|\langle g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha},g_{\beta}-\Psi_{\beta}\rangle|\leq Ce^{-h\langle\alpha-\beta\rangle}e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}.

The sum over
\beta
is convergent and (
3.21
) becomes

(3.22)

\|\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}\|_{1}\leq C\big(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L(1+\varepsilon))}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)+d(\varepsilon L+R+2\eta)}{h}}\big).

Similarly we can estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{I}-\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{\alpha\in V}|\|g_{\alpha}\|^{2}-1|\leq Ce^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}},

using Lemma
11
. This proves the first item.

2. Note that the previous discussion also gives, for
p\in[1,\infty]
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{I}\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbf{T}\|_{p}+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L(1+\varepsilon))}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)+d(\varepsilon L+R+2\eta)}{h}}),

and using Lemma
11
, as well as Lemma
18
from Appendix
B
, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{T}\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbf{T}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)}|\langle\Psi_{\alpha},\Psi_{\beta}\rangle|+Ce^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}}\big).

Therefore
\mathbf{G}
is a perturbation of the identity, and as such it is invertible. Moreover,
\mathbf{G}^{-1/2}
exists – as sum of a convergent Taylor series – and the first terms of the series give

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{G}^{-1/2}-\mathbf{I}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{T}\|_{p}\leq C\|\mathbf{T}\|_{p}^{2}+C\big(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L(1+\varepsilon))}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)+d(\varepsilon L+R+2\eta)}{h}}\big),

which proves the second item.

3. The series defining
e_{\alpha}
is convergent in
\mathscr{E}_{h}
because
\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{1}(V)
and

\sum_{\beta\in V}|(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\beta}|\|g_{\beta}\|\leq C\sum_{\beta}|(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\beta}|\leq C\|\mathbf{G}\|_{1}.

Moreover, item (2) for
p=1
yields

(3.23)

e_{\alpha}=g_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in V\\
L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)\end{subarray}}\langle g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle g_{\beta}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}),

uniformly with respect ot
\alpha
. In particular,
e_{\alpha}
is a perturbation of
g_{\alpha}
. Using Lemma
11
, we deduce that it spans a dense subspace of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
. It is also an orthonormal family, since

\displaystyle\langle e_{\alpha},e_{\alpha^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in V}\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\alpha\beta}\langle g_{\beta},g_{\beta^{\prime}}\rangle\overline{\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}}=(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}=\mathbf{I}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}.

4. Since
(e_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in V}
is a Hilbert basis of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
, the operator
\mathscr{L}_{h}
restricted to
\mathscr{E}_{h}
is obviously unitary equivalent to
\mathbf{M}
, the unitary being

\mathbf{U}:x\in\ell^{2}(V)\mapsto\sum_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{E}_{h}.

To obtain the estimates on
\mathbf{M}
, let us first introduce the infinite matrix

\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}=(\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle)_{\alpha,\beta\in V}.

We claim that
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}
defines a bounded operator on
\ell^{1}(V)
such that

(3.24)

\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}=\mu_{1}\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{W}+\mu_{1}\mathbf{T}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}),

in
\ell^{1}
operator norm topology. Indeed, for
\alpha=\beta
we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}g_{\alpha},g_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\|g_{\alpha}\|^{2}+\langle(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})g_{\alpha},g_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\|g_{\alpha}\|^{2}+\langle(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})\Psi_{\alpha},\Psi_{\alpha}\rangle+\langle(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha}),(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha})\rangle

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}),

by Lemmas
10
and
11
. For
L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)
we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\langle(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{1})g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle+\mu_{1}\langle g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle\Big)\mathbf{1}_{L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{W}_{\alpha\beta}+\mu_{1}\mathbf{T}_{\alpha\beta}+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}),

where we used Lemma
11
again. Finally the last part we estimate as before,

\sup_{\alpha\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in V\\
|\alpha-\beta|>L(1+\varepsilon)\end{subarray}}|\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle|\\
\leq\sup_{\alpha\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in V\\
|\alpha-\beta|>L(1+\varepsilon)\end{subarray}}|\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha}),g_{\beta}-\Psi_{\beta}\rangle|+|\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi_{\alpha},\Psi_{\beta}\rangle|.

The second term vanishes for all but finitely many
\beta
’s. For the remaining ones we use Lemma
20
. In the first term we insert the exponential weight and find

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in V\\
|\alpha-\beta|>L(1+\varepsilon)\end{subarray}}|\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sup_{\alpha\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in V\\
|\alpha-\beta|>L(1+\varepsilon)\end{subarray}}e^{-h\langle\alpha-\beta\rangle}\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}(g_{\alpha}-\Psi_{\alpha})\|\|e^{h\langle x-\beta\rangle}(g_{\beta}-\Psi_{\beta})\|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L(1+\varepsilon))}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)+d(\varepsilon L+R+2\eta)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}).

We then use Lemma
12
to obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in V\\
|\alpha-\beta|>L(1+\varepsilon)\end{subarray}}|\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}g_{\alpha},g_{\beta}\rangle|\leq\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L(1+\varepsilon))}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)+d(\varepsilon L+R+2\eta)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}).

and this proves (
3.24
).

By definition of
\mathbf{M}
and
e_{\alpha}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}=\sum_{\beta\beta^{\prime}\in V}\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\alpha\beta}\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}=(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}.

Moreover using item (2) and (
3.24
) we have

\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\mu_{1}\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{W}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}),

in
\ell^{1}
operator norm topology. We deduce that the remainder
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{M}-\mu_{1}\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W}
is small enough in
\ell^{1}
operator norm topology, but also in
\ell^{\infty}
norm topology since it is symmetric. Using interpolation we deduce

\mathbf{M}-\mu_{1}\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W}=\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L})

in
\ell^{2}
operator norm topology.
∎

4.
Reduction when
e_{0}=\frac{1}{2}

We now consider the case
e_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
, and we assume in all of this section that
h
is as small as we want along a
\frac{1}{2}
-sequence. In this case, the first eigenvalue of the single-obstacle operator
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{0}
might be double. However, Theorem
2
ensures that there is a gap with the third eigenvalue,

(4.1)

\mu_{3}-\mu_{2}\geq 2c_{0}h^{2}+o(h^{2}).

By Lemma
6
, the first two eigenvalues are
\{\mu_{-},\mu_{+}\}
where
\mu_{\pm}
is the ground state energy of
\mathscr{L}_{h,m_{\pm}}
and

(4.2)

\displaystyle m_{-}

\displaystyle=\xi_{h}-\frac{1}{2}+o(1)=\frac{BR^{2}}{2h}+\sqrt{\frac{\Theta_{0}BR^{2}}{h}}+\mathcal{C}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}+o(1),

(4.3)

\displaystyle m_{+}

\displaystyle=\xi_{h}+\frac{1}{2}+o(1)=\frac{BR^{2}}{2h}+\sqrt{\frac{\Theta_{0}BR^{2}}{h}}+\mathcal{C}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}+o(1).

Depending on
h
, either
\mu_{-}
or
\mu_{+}
can be the smallest. The associated eigenfunctions are
\Phi^{-}
and
\Phi^{+}
.
We denote by
\Pi
the spectral projector of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
corresponding to the interval
(-\infty,\mu_{2}+c_{0}h^{2})
.

The reduction in this case follows essentially the same lines as in Section
3
, except that the eigenspace of the single-obstacle operator is now bidimensional. Using Agmon estimates (Lemma
8
), we know that the spectrum of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
is exponentially close to
\mu_{1}
.

Lemma 14
.

If
\lambda\in{\rm{sp}}(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V})
is such that
\lambda<\mu_{2}+c_{0}h^{2}
then we have

\min\big(|\lambda-\mu_{-}|,|\lambda-\mu_{+}|\big)\leq C_{N}h^{N}

for all
N\geq 2
, where
C_{N}>0
is independent of
\lambda
and
h
.

Proof.

The proof is identical to Lemma
9
, up to changing the distance
|\lambda-\mu_{1}|
by the minimal distance
\min\big(|\lambda-\mu_{-}|,|\lambda-\mu_{+}|\big)
.
∎

Recalling the definition (
3.7
) of the cutoff function
\chi_{\eta}
, we define for
\alpha\in V
the pairs of approximate eigenfunctions

(4.4)

\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}=\tau_{\alpha}^{B}\big(\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\pm}\big),\quad g_{\alpha}^{\pm}=\Pi\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}.

Again, the functions
\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}
are good approximate solutions to the eigenvalue equation for
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
. Moreover,
g_{\alpha}^{\pm}
is exponentially close to
\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}
.

Lemma 15
.

If
\eta
is small enough then

(1)

For all
\alpha\in V
,

\displaystyle\|(\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}-\mu_{\pm})\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}\|=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}}\big),\quad\|\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}\|=1+\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}}\big),

and

\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{+},\Psi_{\alpha}^{-}\rangle=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}}\big).

(2)

For all
\alpha\in V
,

\|g_{\alpha}^{\pm}-\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}\|=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}\big),\quad\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}(g_{\alpha}^{\pm}-\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}),g_{\alpha}^{\pm}-\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}\rangle=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}\big),

and the estimates are uniform with respect to
\alpha\in V
.

(3)

The vectors
(g_{\alpha}^{\pm})_{\alpha\in V}
span a dense subspace of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
.

Proof.

The proof of the first item is identical to Lemma
10
, except for the last statement. There, we simply use that
\langle\Phi^{+},\Phi^{-}\rangle=0
and the decay of
\Phi^{\pm}
to find

\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{+},\Psi_{\alpha}^{-}\rangle=\langle(\chi_{\eta}^{2}-1)\Phi^{+},\Phi^{-}\rangle=\mathscr{O}\big(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-3\eta)}{h}}\big).

The second item is identical to Lemma
11
. To obtain that
(g_{\alpha}^{\pm})_{\alpha\in V}
span a dense subspace of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
, we also follow the same strategy. We can show that any
\Psi\in\{g_{\alpha}^{\pm}\,,\alpha\in V\}^{\perp}
satisfy

\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi,\Psi\rangle\geq\Big(\mu_{2}+2c_{0}h^{2}-Ch^{N}\Big)\|\Psi\|^{2},

using the spectral projector

\widetilde{\Pi}_{0}=\langle\cdot,\Phi^{+}\rangle\Phi^{+}+\langle\cdot,\Phi^{-}\rangle\Phi^{-}

instead of
\Pi_{0}
, and the lower bound
\mu_{3}-\mu_{2}\geq 2c_{0}h^{2}
. If
\Psi\in\mathscr{E}_{h}
we also have
\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}\Psi,\Psi\rangle\leq(\mu_{2}+c_{0}h^{2})\|\Psi\|^{2}
, and
therefore
\Psi
must have vanishing norm – since
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}+o(h^{2})
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
12
also works here to provide some decay on
g_{\alpha}^{\pm}-\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm}
,

(4.5)

\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}(g_{\alpha}^{\pm}-\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm})\|=\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}),\quad\|e^{h\langle x-\alpha\rangle}\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}(g_{\alpha}^{\pm}-\Psi_{\alpha}^{\pm})\|=\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}).

We are now in position to prove the main result of this section, which is the analog of Theorem
13
. We define effective operators which act on the space
\ell^{p}(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
. An element of this space will be denoted by
x=(x^{+},x^{-})
, where
x^{+}
,
x^{-}\in\ell^{p}(V)
. Operators acting on this space can be described by an infinite matrix
\mathbf{M}=(\mathbf{M}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}})_{\alpha\beta\in V,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\in\{\pm\}}
, and
y=\mathbf{M}x
is then such that

y^{\sigma}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in V,\sigma^{\prime}\in\{\pm\}}\mathbf{M}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}x^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{\beta}.

We recall the size of the error terms,

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}=e^{-\frac{2S_{h}(L)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L(1+\varepsilon))}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)+d(\varepsilon L+R+2\eta)}{h}},

which are controlled in Lemma
21
.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and assume
e_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
. For all
p\in[1,\infty]
, the infinite matrix
\mathbf{G}=(\langle g_{\alpha}^{\sigma},g_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle)_{\alpha\beta\in V,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\in\{\pm\}}
defines a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Moreover,

(1)

We have the following estimate in
\ell^{p}
operator norm topology

\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{T}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}),\quad{\text{in}}\quad\mathcal{L}(\ell^{p}(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}),

where
\mathbf{T}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\langle g_{\alpha}^{\sigma},g_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)}
.

(2)

\mathbf{G}^{-1}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}(V)
, and has a square root satisfying

\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\mathbf{I}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{T}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}),\quad{\text{in}}\quad\mathcal{L}(\ell^{p}(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}).

(3)

The vectors

e_{\alpha}^{\sigma}=\sum_{\beta\in V,\sigma^{\prime}\in\{\pm\}}(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}g_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}},\qquad\alpha\in V,\quad\sigma\in\{\pm\},

define a Hilbert basis of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
.

(4)

The restriction of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
to
\mathscr{E}_{h}
is unitarily equivalent to the operator

\mathbf{M}=(\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}e_{\alpha}^{\sigma},e_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle)_{\alpha,\beta\in V,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\{\pm\}}
acting on
\ell^{2}(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and

\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{D}+\mathbf{W}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}),

where
\mathbf{D}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\mu_{\sigma}\mathbf{I}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
and

(4.6)

\mathbf{W}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\big\langle(\mathscr{L}_{h}-\frac{\mu_{\sigma}+\mu_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{2})\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\big\rangle\mathbf{1}_{L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)}.

Proof.

1. As before, we can use the
\ell^{1}
operator norm. We decompose
\mathbf{G}
as

\mathbf{G}=\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}+{\mathbf{T}}+\widetilde{\mathbf{R}},

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\langle g_{\alpha}^{\sigma},g_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{\alpha=\beta},\quad\mathbf{T}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\langle g_{\alpha}^{\sigma},g_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)},\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\langle g_{\alpha}^{\sigma},g_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{|\alpha-\beta|>L(1+\varepsilon)}.

As in (
3.22
), we bound
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}
and
\mathbf{I}-\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
using Lemma
15
, equation (
4.5
) and Lemma
18
. Note that to estimate the off-diagonal terms of
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}
we need the estimate on
\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{+},\Psi_{\alpha}^{-}\rangle
from Lemma
15
.

2. The proof of the second item is identical to Theorem
13
. Indeed,
\mathbf{G}
is still a perturbation of the identity, and a Taylor expansion of the square root gives the result.

3. The third item is also unchanged.
e_{\alpha}^{\sigma}
is a perturbation of
g_{\alpha}^{\sigma}
, and these vectors span a dense subspace of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
. The family
(e_{\alpha}^{\sigma})_{\alpha\in V,\sigma\in\{\pm\}}
is also orthonormal since

\displaystyle\langle e_{\alpha}^{\sigma},e_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in V\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\{\pm\}}(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\tau}\langle g_{\beta}^{\tau},g_{\beta^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime}}\rangle(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}^{\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}=(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\mathbf{I}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},

and therefore it is a Hilbert basis of
\mathscr{E}_{h}
.

4.
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
is unitarily equivalent to
\mathbf{M}
through the Hilbert basis
(e_{\alpha}^{\sigma})
. Before estimating
\mathbf{M}
, we introduce the operator
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}=(\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}g_{\alpha}^{\sigma},g_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle)_{\alpha\beta\in V,\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\in\{\pm\}}
. We claim that

(4.7)

\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}=\mathbf{D}+\mathbf{W}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{T}+\mathbf{T}\mathbf{D})+\mathscr{O}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}\big),

in
\ell^{1}(V)\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
operator norm topology, with
\mathbf{D}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\mu_{\sigma}\mathbf{I}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
. Indeed, on the diagonal
\alpha=\beta
we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}g_{\alpha}^{\sigma},g_{\alpha}^{\prime\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mu_{\sigma}\langle g_{\alpha}^{\sigma},g_{\alpha}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}})

\displaystyle=\mu_{\sigma}\mathbf{I}_{\alpha\alpha}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}})

because of Lemma
15
. For
L\leq|\alpha-\beta|\leq L(1+\varepsilon)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}g_{\alpha}^{\sigma},g_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathscr{L}_{h}\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{W}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+\frac{\mu_{\sigma}+\mu_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{2}\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{W}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+\frac{\mu_{\sigma}+\mu_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{2}\mathbf{T}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}),

where we used Lemma
15
again in the first line. Finally, the remaining terms
|\alpha-\beta|>L(1+\varepsilon)
are bounded exactly as in Theorem
13
, using equation (
4.5
) and Lemma
11
. This proves (
4.7
). Now, by definition of
\mathbf{M}
and
e_{\alpha}^{\sigma}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\sum_{\beta\beta^{\prime}\in V,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\{\pm\}}(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\tau}(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}^{\sigma^{\prime}\tau^{\prime}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}^{\tau\tau^{\prime}}=(\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}.

Moreover using item (2) and (
4.7
) we deduce

\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}\mathbf{G}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\mathbf{D}+\mathbf{W}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}),

in
\ell^{1}
(and thus
\ell^{2}
) operator norm topology.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorems
3
and
4

In this section we prove the spectral gap estimates in the case of two obstacles. The centers of the obstacles are
V=\{\alpha_{\ell},\alpha_{r}\}
with

\alpha_{\ell}=\Big(-\frac{L}{2},0\Big),\qquad\alpha_{r}=\Big(\frac{L}{2},0\Big).

Proof of Theorem
3
.

When
e_{0}\neq\frac{1}{2}
, we use Theorem
13
with
\varepsilon\geq 2
. In this case,
\mathscr{E}_{h}
is two-dimensional and the restriction of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
to
\mathscr{E}_{h}
is unitarily equivalent to the matrix

\mathbf{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}&\mathbf{W}_{\alpha_{\ell}\alpha_{r}}\\
\mathbf{W}_{\alpha_{r}\alpha_{\ell}}&\mu_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\mathscr{O}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}\big).

The first two eigenvalues of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
are thus of the form

\lambda_{1}=\mu_{1}-\big|\mathbf{W}_{\alpha_{\ell}\alpha_{r}}\big|+\mathscr{O}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}\big),\qquad\lambda_{2}=\mu_{1}+\big|\mathbf{W}_{\alpha_{\ell}\alpha_{r}}\big|+\mathscr{O}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}\big).

In Lemma
19
and
20
below the interaction coefficient
\mathbf{W}_{\alpha_{\ell}\alpha_{r}}
is estimated and we find

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=4|{\rm{Re}}(w_{L})|+\mathscr{O}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}\big)

\displaystyle=4|{\rm{Re}}(C_{L})|K_{L}^{-e_{0}-1}he^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}\big(1+o(1)\big)+\mathscr{O}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}\big).

The error terms
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}
are shown to be small enough in Lemma
21
, using that
L>6R
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

When
e_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
, the eigenspace
\mathscr{E}_{h}
is four-dimensional and we use Theorem
16
instead. The Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(V)\otimes\mathbf{C}^{2}
is four-dimensional as well, and
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
is unitarily equivalent to the matrix

\mathbf{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{-}&\mathbf{W}^{--}&0&\mathbf{W}^{-+}\\
\mathbf{W}^{--}&\mu_{-}&\overline{\mathbf{W}}^{+-}&0\\
0&\mathbf{W}^{+-}&\mu_{+}&\mathbf{W}^{++}\\
\overline{\mathbf{W}}^{-+}&0&\mathbf{W}^{++}&\mu_{+}\end{pmatrix}+\mathscr{O}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}\big),

where we used the short-hand notation
\mathbf{W}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\mathbf{W}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}_{\alpha_{\ell}\alpha_{r}}
. We use the Lemmas
19
and

20
to estimate the interaction coefficients and find

\mathbf{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{-}&\lambda_{h}K_{L}^{-1}&0&-\lambda_{h}\\
\lambda_{h}K_{L}^{-1}&\mu_{-}&\lambda_{h}&0\\
0&\lambda_{h}&\mu_{+}&-\lambda_{h}K_{L}\\
-\lambda_{h}&0&-\lambda_{h}K_{L}&\mu_{+}\end{pmatrix}+o\big(he^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}\big),

with
\lambda_{h}=2(-1)^{m_{+}}{\rm{Re}}(C_{L})K_{L}^{-e_{0}}
.
In the special case when
\mu_{-}=\mu_{+}
we find

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}-|\lambda_{h}|\big(K_{L}+K_{L}^{-1}\big)+o\big(he^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}\big),

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}+o\big(he^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}\big),

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}+o\big(he^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}\big),

\displaystyle\lambda_{4}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}+|\lambda_{h}|\big(K_{L}+K_{L}^{-1}\big)+o\big(he^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}\big),

and this concludes the proof of Theorem
3
.
∎

6.
tunneling for a lattice of obstacles

In this section be calculate the tunneling in the case of infinitely many disjoint obstacles.
We focus here on the special case when the obstacles are distributed along a lattice, that is
V=L\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Let us start with the simpler situation, when
e_{0}\neq\frac{1}{2}
. We use Theorem
13
, and make the specific choice
\varepsilon=0.4
. Indeed, we want
\varepsilon<\sqrt{2}-1
to ensure that we do not capture the interaction between obstacles along diagonals. For instance, the lattice points
(0,0)
and
(L,L)
have distance
L\sqrt{2}>L(1+\varepsilon)
to each other: their interaction is not included in the infinite matrix
\mathbf{W}
. We also want
\varepsilon
as large as possible in order to reduce the size of the error terms
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}
. Note that we could also choose
\varepsilon
larger and take into account the diagonal interactions, but this would make the interaction matrix
\mathbf{W}
more complicated.

With the choice
\varepsilon=0.4
, we obtain that the restriction of
\mathscr{L}_{h}^{V}
to
\mathscr{E}_{h}
is unitarily equivalent to an operator
\mathbf{M}
on
\ell^{2}(L\mathbb{Z}^{2})
of the form

\mathbf{M}=\mu_{1}\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{W}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}).

For
\alpha
,
\beta\in L\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the coefficients
\mathbf{W}_{\alpha\beta}
are all vanishing unless
|\alpha-\beta|=L
(i.e. they are closest neighbours). In this case, by Lemmas
19
and
20
we have

\mathbf{W}_{\alpha\beta}=(-1)^{m_{-}+1}e^{\frac{iB}{2h}\alpha\wedge\beta}2C_{L}K_{L}^{-e_{0}-1}he^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}\big(1+o(1)\big).

With the notations of Theorem
5
this gives

\mathbf{W}=\lambda\mathbf{X}+o\big(he^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}\big),

where

\mathbf{X}_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{cases}e^{\frac{iB}{2h}\alpha\wedge\beta}&{\rm{if}}\,|\alpha-\beta|=L,\\
0&{\rm{otherwise}}.\end{cases}

The errors
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}
are shown to be small enough in Lemma
21
, as soon as
L>25R
.

We now consider
e_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
. In this case we use Theorem
16
(still with
\varepsilon=0.4
) and we obtain an operator on
\ell^{2}(L\mathbb{Z}^{2})\otimes\mathbf{C}^{2}
with similar expansion,

\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{D}+\mathbf{W}+\mathscr{O}(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}).

The coefficients
\mathbf{W}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
are vanishing unless
|\alpha-\beta|=L
, in which case we have

\mathbf{W}_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=(-1)^{m_{\sigma^{\prime}}+1}e^{\frac{iB}{2h}\alpha\wedge\beta}e^{-i\frac{\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}}{2}\arg(\beta-\alpha)}2C_{L}K_{L}^{\frac{\sigma+\sigma^{\prime}-1}{2}}he^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}\big(1+o(1)\big),

by Lemmas
19
and
20
. With the notations of Theorem
5
we have
\mathbf{W}=\lambda\mathbf{Y}+o\big(he^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)}{h}}\big)
. Finally, the errors
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}
are small enough by Lemma
21
.
∎

Appendix A
Analysis of the phase

The phase function involved in several integrals in this Appendix is

(A.1)

\displaystyle s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(x_{1},x_{2})

\sigma
,

(A.2)

\displaystyle s_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{4}\Big(x_{1}+\frac{\ell}{2}\Big)^{2}+\frac{B}{4}\Big(x_{1}-\frac{\ell}{2}\Big)^{2}+\frac{B}{2}x_{2}^{2}-\frac{BR^{2}}{2}+i\frac{B\ell x_{2}}{2}

Lemma 17
.

The function
(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto s_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})
has the following properties.

(1)

s_{0}
is holomorphic on
D=\{{\rm{Re}}(x_{1})\in\big(-\frac{\ell}{2},\frac{\ell}{2}\big)
,
{\rm{Im}}(x_{2})\leq 0\}\subset\mathbf{C}^{2}
.

(2)

s_{0}
has a unique critical point
(x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*})
in
D
, which is

x_{1}^{*}=0,\quad x_{2}^{*}=-i\sqrt{\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}-R^{2}}.

(3)

The critical value is

(4)

The Hessian is such that
{\rm{Im}}\nabla^{2}s_{0}(x^{*})=0
. Also,
\partial_{1}\partial_{2}s_{0}(x^{*})=0
and

\begin{cases}\partial_{1}^{2}s_{0}(x^{*})&=\frac{B\ell}{2R^{2}}\big(\ell-\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}\big),\\
\partial_{2}^{2}s_{0}(x^{*})&=\frac{B}{2R^{2}}\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}\big(\ell-\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}\big).\end{cases}

(5)

The function
x_{1}\in(-\frac{\ell}{2},\frac{\ell}{2})\mapsto s_{0}(x_{1},x_{2}^{*})
is real valued, and has a unique global minimum at
0
.

(6)

For
x_{2}\in\mathbf{R}
, we have the following expansion as
x_{2}\to 0
,

\displaystyle s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,x_{2}+x_{2}^{*})

\displaystyle=s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,x_{2}^{*})+\frac{B\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}}{4R^{2}}(\ell-\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}})x_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-i\frac{x_{2}}{R}(\ell-\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}})\sqrt{h\Theta_{0}B}+\mathscr{O}(x_{2}^{3}+\sqrt{h}x_{2}^{2}+hx_{2}),

and

\displaystyle s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,x_{2}^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{B\ell}{4}\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}-h(m_{\sigma}+m_{\sigma^{\prime}})\ln\Big(\frac{\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}}{2R}\Big).

\displaystyle=S_{h}(\ell)-h(2\mathcal{C}_{2}+m_{\sigma}+m_{\sigma^{\prime}}-2\xi_{h})\ln\Big(\frac{\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}}{2R}\Big),

where we recall

(A.3)

S_{h}(\ell)=\frac{B\ell}{4}\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}-\big(BR^{2}+2\sqrt{h\Theta_{0}BR^{2}}\big)\ln\Big(\frac{\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}}{2R}\Big).

Appendix B

where
\alpha
,

and
\sigma

.

Let
\alpha
,
\beta\in V

\Psi_{\alpha}
and

are such that:

(1)

When

and
\beta

(2)

When

, for
\sigma
,
\sigma^{\prime}\in\{\pm\}
we have

\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle=\mathscr{O}\big(h^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{S_{h}(\ell)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)+d(\ell-L+R+2\eta)}{h}}\big),

as

along a
\frac{1}{2}
-sequence, and uniformly with respect to
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Proof.

Note that when
e_{0}\neq\frac{1}{2}
,
\Psi_{\alpha}
is equal to
\Psi_{\alpha}^{-}
. Therefore, we can focus on the second case. We first use the definition (
4.4
) of
\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma}
and properties of the magnetic translations, in order to center the integral at
0
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\tau_{\alpha}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma},\tau_{\beta}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle=\langle\tau^{B}_{-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}\tau^{B}_{\alpha}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma},\tau^{B}_{-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}\tau^{B}_{\beta}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{iB}{2h}\alpha\wedge\beta}\langle\tau_{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma},\tau^{B}_{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle.

Now we use a rotation
R_{-\theta}
such that
R_{-\theta}\big(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\big)=(-\frac{\ell}{2},0)
. The commutation with magnetic translations gives

R_{\theta}\cdot\tau_{\alpha}^{B}=\tau_{(R_{-\theta}\alpha)}^{B}\cdot R_{\theta},

and therefore

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle=e^{\frac{iB}{2h}\alpha\wedge\beta}\langle\tau^{B}_{(-\frac{\ell}{2},0)}R_{\theta}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma},\tau^{B}_{(\frac{\ell}{2},0)}R_{\theta}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle.

Then, using the angular dependence of
\Phi^{\sigma}
we have
R_{\theta}\Phi^{\sigma}(x)=\Phi^{\sigma}(R_{\theta}x)=e^{im_{\sigma}\theta}\Phi^{\sigma}(x)
and

(B.1)

\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle=e^{\frac{iB}{2h}\alpha\wedge\beta+i(m_{\sigma}-m_{\sigma^{\prime}})\theta}\langle\tau^{B}_{(-\frac{\ell}{2},0)}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma},\tau^{B}_{(\frac{\ell}{2},0)}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle.

We are reduced to the case of two disks on the horizontal axis, with relative distance
\ell
. For simplicity we write
\epsilon=e^{i(m_{\sigma}-m_{\sigma^{\prime}})\theta}
, which is either equal to
1
or
e^{\pm i\theta}
. Now we can use the explicit formula for
\Phi^{\sigma}
from Lemma
7
,

(B.2)

\Phi^{\sigma}(x)=\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{4}}\Big(\frac{z}{R}\Big)^{m_{\sigma}}e^{-\frac{B}{4h}(|z|^{2}-R^{2})}u_{h}(|z|),

where we identify
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
z=x_{1}+ix_{2}
. Replacing we find

\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle=e^{\frac{iB}{2h}\alpha\wedge\beta}e^{i\pi m_{\sigma^{\prime}}}\epsilon\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\frac{iB\ell x_{2}}{2h}}\chi_{\eta}\Big(z+\frac{\ell}{2}\Big)\chi_{\eta}\Big(z-\frac{\ell}{2}\Big)\\
\Big(\frac{\ell/2+z}{R}\Big)^{m_{\sigma}}\overline{\Big(\frac{\ell/2-z}{R}\Big)^{m_{\sigma^{\prime}}}}e^{-\frac{B}{4h}(|z+\frac{\ell}{2}|^{2}+|z-\frac{\ell}{2}|^{2}-2R^{2})}u_{h}\Big(\big|z+\frac{\ell}{2}\big|\Big)u_{h}\Big(\big|z-\frac{\ell}{2}\big|\Big)\mathrm{d}z.

For simplicity we will not specify the arguments of
\chi_{\eta}
in the notation below. Also, we can write the power of any complex number as exponential of a logarithm. The formula holds for any choice of complex logarithm, but on the support of
\chi_{\eta}
, the standard logarithm is continuous - and thus, we make this choice. We obtain

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle|=\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{2}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\frac{s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(z)}{h}}\chi_{\eta}u_{h}\Big(\big|z+\frac{\ell}{2}\big|\Big)\chi_{\eta}u_{h}\Big(\big|z-\frac{\ell}{2}\big|\Big)\mathrm{d}z\Big|,

with phase

\displaystyle s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{4}\big|z+\frac{\ell}{2}\big|^{2}+\frac{B}{4}\big|z-\frac{\ell}{2}\big|^{2}-\frac{BR^{2}}{2}+i\frac{B\ell x_{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-hm_{\sigma}\log\Big(\frac{\ell/2+z}{R}\Big)-hm_{\sigma^{\prime}}\log\Big(\frac{\ell/2-\bar{z}}{R}\Big).

Note that
{\rm{Re}}(s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(z))\geq d\Big(\big|z+\frac{\ell}{2}\big|\Big)+d\Big(\big|z-\frac{\ell}{2}\big|\Big)
. We want to remove the cutoff function
\chi_{\eta}
. To do so, we use that on the support of
1-\chi_{\eta}
we have

{\rm{Re}}(s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(z))\geq d(L-R-2\eta)+d(\ell-L+R+2\eta).

For the same reason, we can add a cutoff
\tilde{\chi}(x_{1})
supported on
|x_{1}|<\frac{\ell}{2}-R-\eta
and we get

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{2}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{\chi}(x_{1})e^{-\frac{s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(z)}{h}}u_{h}\Big(\big|z+\frac{\ell}{2}\big|\Big)u_{h}\Big(\big|z-\frac{\ell}{2}\big|\Big)\mathrm{d}z\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)+d(\ell-L+R+2\eta)}{h}}).

This last cutoff is added to make sure the complex logarithms remain continuous. We can estimate this integral using the complex Laplace method. The phase
s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(x_{1},x_{2})
is analyzed in Lemma
17
. It has a unique critical point of the form
(0,x_{2}^{*})
where
x_{2}^{*}
is purely imaginary. The integral being analytic as function of
x_{2}
, we can make the complex shift
x_{2}\mapsto x_{2}+x_{2}^{*}
,

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{2}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{\chi}(x_{1})e^{-\frac{s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(x_{1},x_{2}+x_{2}^{*})}{h}}u_{h}\Big(M_{-}(x)\Big)u_{h}\Big(M_{+}(x)\Big)\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathscr{O}(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)+d(\ell-L+R+2\eta)}{h}}),

where

. By Lemma
17
we have

s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(x+x^{*})=S_{h}(\ell)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{2}s_{0}(x^{*})(x,x)+iC\sqrt{h}x_{2}+\mathscr{O}(h|x|+|x|^{3}),

and using the Laplace method,

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle|=\mathscr{O}\Big(h^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{S_{h}(\ell)}{h}}\Big)+\mathscr{O}\Big(e^{-\frac{d(L-R-3\eta)+d(\ell-L+R+2\eta)}{h}}\Big),

where we used that
M_{\pm}(x)=R\pm\frac{\ell x_{1}}{2R}+\frac{x_{2}^{*}x_{2}}{R}+\mathscr{O}(x^{2})
and (
2.8
).
∎

Lemma 19
.

Let
\alpha
,
\beta\in V
be such that

(1)

When

Proof.

When
e_{0}\neq\frac{1}{2}
the coefficient

is equal to
\mathbf{W}_{\alpha\beta}^{--}
. Thus, we can focus on item
(2)
. We use translations and rotations to center the problem as in the proof of Lemma

\alpha
to
(-\frac{\ell}{2},0)
and
\beta
to
(\frac{\ell}{2},0)
. We obtain, with the notation

\displaystyle\langle(\mathscr{L}_{h}-\bar{\mu})\Psi_{\alpha}^{\sigma},\Psi_{\beta}^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\mathscr{L}_{h}-\bar{\mu})\tau_{\alpha}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma},\tau_{\beta}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{iB}{2h}\alpha\wedge\beta}\langle(\mathscr{L}_{h}-\bar{\mu})\tau_{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma},\tau_{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{iB}{2h}\alpha\wedge\beta+i(m_{\sigma}-m_{\sigma^{\prime}})arg(\beta-\alpha)}\langle(\mathscr{L}_{h}-\bar{\mu})\tau_{(-\frac{\ell}{2},0)}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma},\tau_{(\frac{\ell}{2},0)}^{B}\chi_{\eta}\Phi^{\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle.

We now introduce the notation

, and

14

and

(B.6)

\partial_{1}\Phi^{\sigma}=\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{4}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\varphi_{\sigma}}{h}\Big)\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{|x|}\partial_{r}u_{h}-h^{-1}\partial_{1}\varphi_{\sigma}u_{h}\Big).

Using these formulas we find

\displaystyle w_{\ell}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=h^{2}\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,x_{2})}{h}}u_{h}(M(x_{2}))\omega(x_{2})\mathrm{d}x_{2},

with
M(x_{2})=\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+\ell^{2}/4}
and

\displaystyle\omega(x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{\ell^{2}+4x_{2}^{2}}}\partial_{r}u_{h}(M(x_{2}))+\Big(\frac{m_{\sigma^{\prime}}}{\ell/2+ix_{2}}-\frac{B\ell}{4h}\Big)u_{h}(M(x_{2}))

\displaystyle s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{B\ell^{2}}{8}-\frac{BR^{2}}{2}+\frac{Bx_{2}^{2}}{2}-h(m_{\sigma}+m_{\sigma^{\prime}})\log\Big(\frac{\ell/2+ix_{2}}{R}\Big)+\frac{iB\ell x_{2}}{2}.

The main properties of the function
s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
and its principal part
s_{0}
are summarized in Lemma
17
. In particular it has a unique critical point in the lower half complex plane at
x_{2}^{*}=-i\sqrt{\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}-R^{2}}
. Since the involved functions are analytic, we can make a complex translation
x_{2}\mapsto x_{2}+x_{2}^{*}
to obtain

\displaystyle w_{\ell}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=h^{2}\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,x_{2}+x_{2}^{*})}{h}}u(M(x_{2}+x_{2}^{*}))\omega(x_{2}+x_{2}^{*})\mathrm{d}x_{2}.

We estimate this integral using the Laplace method. We give a few details due to the complexity of the phase involved. Since the phase is minimal at
x_{2}=0
, and using (
2.7
), we can restrict the integral to
|x_{2}|\leq Ch^{1/4}
. We then rescale with
x_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{hN}{B(1-N)}}t
, where we define
N=\frac{4R^{2}}{\ell^{2}}
. With this notation we have

M(x_{2}+x_{2}^{*})=R-it\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}+\mathscr{O}(h),

and therefore

w_{\ell}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\Big(\frac{BR^{2}}{h}\Big)^{\frac{\Theta_{0}+1}{2}}e^{-s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,x_{2}^{*})/h}\\
\int_{\{|t|<Ch^{-1/4}\}}e^{-\frac{1-\sqrt{1-N}}{\sqrt{1-N}}(t^{2}+2it\sqrt{\Theta_{0}})}u_{h}\Big(R-it\sqrt{\frac{h}{B}}\Big)^{2}\big(1+o(1)\big)\mathrm{d}t.

Here we used Lemma
17
to estimate the phase
s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
, and Lemma
7
to show that, in
\omega
, the term in
\partial_{r}u_{h}
is smaller than
h^{-1}u_{h}
. Now note that in this regime
|t|<Ch^{-1/4}
, the estimate (
2.8
) remains with
-it
instead of
t
. Therefore,

w_{\ell}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=-\frac{K_{0}^{2}Bh}{2}e^{-s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,x_{2}^{*})/h}\int_{\{|t|<Ch^{-1/4}\}}e^{-\frac{1-\sqrt{1-N}}{\sqrt{1-N}}(t^{2}+2it\sqrt{\Theta_{0}})}v(-it)^{2}\big(1+o(1)\big)\mathrm{d}t.

Also note that
v(-it)
belongs to the Schwarz class, since it is the Fourier transform of a Schwarz function. Thus, the integral is asymptotically equal to a constant, which only depends on
N
. We obtain a constant
\tilde{C}_{\ell}\neq 0
such that

(B.7)

w_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\ell)=\tilde{C}_{\ell}he^{-\frac{s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,x_{2}^{*})}{h}}(1+o(1)).

The critical value of
s_{h}
is equal to

s_{h}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(0,x_{2}^{*})=S_{h}(\ell)-h(2\mathcal{C}_{2}+m_{\sigma}+m_{\sigma^{\prime}}-2\xi_{h})\ln(K_{\ell}),

where we recall that
m_{-}=\xi_{h}-e_{0}+o(1)
and
m_{+}=m_{-}+1
. The result follows with a new
C_{\ell}
.
∎

Appendix C
Error terms

We recall the order of the error terms encountered,

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}=e^{-\frac{2S_{h}(L)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{2d(L-R-4\eta)}{h}}+e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L(1+\varepsilon))}{h}}+e^{-\frac{d(L-R-4\eta)+d(\varepsilon L+R+2\eta)}{h}}.

We prove here that these errors are indeed small enough.

Lemma 21
.

If
L>\max(6R,\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}}R)
and if
\eta
is small enough, then there exists
\kappa>0
such that

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,L}=\mathscr{O}\Big(e^{-\frac{S_{h}(L)(1+\kappa)}{h}}\Big).

Proof.

We recall that
S_{h}(\ell)=S_{0}(\ell)-\sqrt{h}S_{1}(\ell)+\mathscr{O}(h)
with

S_{0}(\ell)=\frac{B\ell}{4}\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}-BR^{2}\ln\Big(\frac{\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}}{2R}\Big),\hskip 9.24994ptS_{1}(\ell)=2\sqrt{\Theta_{0}BR^{2}}\ln\Big(\frac{\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}-4R^{2}}}{2R}\Big).

Moreover, the function
S_{0}
can be rewritten as

(C.1)

S_{0}(r)=2BR^{2}\int_{1}^{r/2R}\sqrt{u^{2}-1}\mathrm{d}u,

and the Agmon distance as

(C.2)

d(r)=\frac{BR^{2}}{2}\int_{1}^{r/R}\Big(t-\frac{1}{t}\Big)\mathrm{d}t.

In particular,
S_{0}
and
d
are positive increasing functions on
[R,\infty)
. Therefore, it is enough to prove that

(C.3)

2d(L-R)>S_{0}(L),

and

(C.4)

d(L-R)+d(\varepsilon L+R)>S_{0}(L).

To prove these, we use
\sqrt{u^{2}-1}<u
in (
C.1
) and
t-\frac{1}{t}>t-1
in (
C.2
). This provides us the upper bound

(C.5)

S_{0}(L)\leq\frac{BL^{2}}{4}-BR^{2},

and the lower bound

(C.6)

d(r)\geq\frac{B}{4}(r-R)^{2}.

Thus (
C.3
) is satisfied when

\frac{B}{2}(L-2R)^{2}>\frac{BL^{2}}{4}-BR^{2},\quad{\rm{i.e.}}\quad\frac{L^{2}}{4}-2LR+3R^{2}>0,

for which it is enough that
L>6R
. The other inequality (
C.4
) is satisfied when

\frac{B}{4}(L-2R)^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}BL^{2}}{4}>\frac{BL^{2}}{4}-BR^{2},\quad{\rm{i.e.}}\quad LR<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}L^{2}}{4}+2R^{2},

for which it is enough that
L>\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}}R
.
∎

MANATEE: Inference-Time Lightweight Diffusion Based Safety Defense for LLMs
Chun Yan Ryan Kan, Tommy Tran, Vedant Yadav, Ava Cai, Kevin Zhu, Ruizhe Li, Maheep Chaudhary

Abstract
Defending LLMs against adversarial jailbreak attacks remains an open challenge. Existing defenses rely on binary classifiers that fail when adversarial input falls outside the learned decision boundary, and repeated fine-tuning is computationally expensive while potentially degrading model capabilities. We propose MANATEE, an inference-time defense that uses density estimation over a benign representation manifold. MANATEE learns the score function of benign hidden states and uses diffusion to project anomalous representations toward safe regions—requiring no harmful training data and no architectural modifications. Experiments across Mistral-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and Gemma-2-9B-it demonstrate that MANATEE reduce Attack Success Rate by up to 100% on certain datasets, while preserving model utility on benign inputs.

Introduction

Figure 1:
Attack Success Rate (ASR) reduction across various models on JBB dataset, before and after diffusion models are applied on finetuned harmful models.

Adversarial jailbreak attacks pose a significant threat to deployed LLMs. Optimization-based attacks like GCG
(
Zou et al. 2023
)
and priming attacks
(
Vega et al. 2023
)
reliably bypass safety training, while benchmarks like JailbreakBench
(
Chao et al. 2024
)
show that state-of-the-art defenses remain vulnerable. As LLMs are deployed in consumer products and critical infrastructure, robust defenses against such attacks become essential.

Existing defenses have limitations. Repeated fine-tuning can degrade safety
(
Qi et al. 2024
)
or fail to eliminate deceptive behaviors
(
Hubinger et al. 2024a
)
, while introducing computational overhead that scales with each new attack class
(
Ziegler et al. 2019
)
. Binary classifiers offer an alternative but remain brittle: adversaries can craft inputs that fall outside learned decision boundaries while still eliciting harmful outputs, requiring constant retraining with no guarantee of generalization.

We propose a different premise: rather than asking “is this representation harmful?”, we ask “how can this representation be projected onto the manifold of benign representations?” This reframes safety as a density estimation problem—one naturally suited to diffusion models. Diffusion models learn the density of a target distribution through score matching, enabling detection of out-of-distribution samples. Crucially, they also provide a mechanism for iterative correction: anomalous samples can be progressively transformed toward high-density regions of the learned distribution.

We introduce MANATEE, an inference-time defense that learns the score function of benign hidden states and uses diffusion to project anomalous representations toward safe regions.
MANATEE operates entirely in representation space, requiring no harmful training data, no architectural modifications to the base model, and transfers across model families without retraining. When a hidden state deviates significantly from the benign manifold, MANATEE either steers it back toward safe regions or triggers a refusal as a principled fallback.

2
Related Work

2.1
Out-of-Distribution Detection

A closely adjacent body of work studies how to detect out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs or anomalous internal states in deep networks using confidence scores and representation geometry, rather than relying on surface-form heuristics. Early baselines use maximum softmax probability as a proxy for uncertainty
Hendrycks & Gimpel 2017
, while subsequent methods model class-conditional feature statistics and measure deviations in the representation space, including Mahalanobis-distance scoring
Lee et al. 2018
. A complementary line reframes detection as energy-based scoring, where OOD samples are expected to exhibit higher energy under a trained model
Liu et al. 2020
. While OOD detection work typically treats the score as a trigger for abstention or refusal, MANATEE uses an anomaly score as a trigger for representation-level correction, through the activation of diffusion.

2.2
Selective Refusals

Work on selective classification formalizes risk–coverage tradeoffs: systems can improve reliability by abstaining on inputs likely to be wrong, rather than forcing a prediction
Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017
. In parallel, calibration research shows that modern neural models can be poorly calibrated, motivating explicit calibration or confidence estimation mechanisms
Guo et al. 2017
. For language models, related work argues that models often encode useful uncertainty signals about their own knowledge, enabling more principled deferral strategies
Kadavath et al. 2022
. MANATEE expands on the idea of selective classification, attempting corrective steering first and reserving refusal for cases that exceed the safety threshold.

2.3
Activation Steering

A separate line of research controls LLM behavior through inference-time interventions in hidden states or through targeted edits to model parameters. Activation steering methods directly manipulate internal activations to modulate behavior during decoding, enabling control without full retraining
Stolfo et al. 2025
;
Huang & Wang 2025
. Recent work also raises concerns that naive steering can compromise safety behaviors, including weakening refusal under certain interventions
Korznikov et al. 2025
. Closely related are model editing methods that aim to modify factual associations or localized behaviors without full retraining
Meng et al. 2022
. We differ from activation engineering methods in that we treat unsafe behavior as an out-of-distribution trajectory, and apply score-based correction toward high-density regions, providing a more conservative mechanism when deviations are large.

Methodology

Figure 2:
Overview of MANATEE’s inference-time detection and steering pipeline.
Given a user query, the base LLM produces the final-layer hidden state
h_{t}
. We standardize this representation and apply forward diffusion to obtain a noisy state at a timestep
t_{\mathrm{check}}
. The denoiser’s predicted noise magnitude defines an anomaly score
s(h)
that measures how far
h_{t}
lies from the benign manifold. States with scores above the threshold
\tau
trigger automatic refusal; otherwise, MANATEE applies a DDPM-based purification step by injecting noise and denoising in latent space to produce a purified hidden state that is passed through the LM head to generate a benign, conditionally steered response.

MANATEE operates at the final layer of the base LLM, steering the last token’s hidden state before it is projected to the vocabulary. The method has three stages: (1) extract hidden states from benign generations to define the target manifold, (2) train a diffusion model to learn the density of benign representations, and (3) at inference, detect anomalous hidden states and apply diffusion-based correction. 3.1

3.2
Inference: Detection and Steering

Anomaly scoring.

For each hidden state
h
, we compute an anomaly score, or the average distance to the benign manifold, using the predicted noise magnitude of the denoiser at a fixed timestep
t_{\mathrm{check}}
:

s(h)\;=\;\left\|\epsilon_{\theta}\left(\tilde{h},t_{\mathrm{check}}\right)\right\|_{2},

(2)

where
\tilde{h}=(h-\mu)/\sigma
is the standardized state. In-distribution states yield low scores; out-of-distribution states yield high scores.

Conditional response.

The threshold
\tau
controls the response:

•

If
s(h)\leq\tau
: apply diffusion steering

•

If
s(h)>\tau
: trigger automatic refusal

Diffusion steering.

For anomalous states, we initialize the reverse diffusion process from a noised version of
\tilde{h}
and iteratively denoise using standard DDPM updates, projecting the representation toward high-density regions of the benign manifold. The purified state is then unstandardized and passed to the language model head.

4.1.1
Interpreting the results

As shown in table 1 below, MANATEE reduces the ASR across these three datasets:
58.7\%
on average for MAD,
77.6\%
for JBB, and
100.0\%
for ASA. These results suggest that diffusion with respect to the benign manifold can effectively suppress backdoored responses, and can be applied to various models not included in the experiments.

4.1.2
Benign Inputs

From section 3.2,
\tau
is tuned so that benign responses fall below that range and backdoored responses fall above it. Specifically,
\tau=\frac{(\mu_{B}-\sigma_{B})+(\mu_{b}-\sigma_{b})}{2}
, where
\mu_{B}
and
\sigma_{B}
denote the mean and standard deviation of the backdoored samples’ distances, and
\mu_{b},\sigma_{b}
denote the benign ones. Since the diffusion model is activated when
s(h)\leq\tau
, we find that benign responses are largely unchanged, signifying that the diffusion model mainly targets the backdoored responses and leaves its benign capability unaffected.

4.2
Experiment Details

We trained our models with Lambda AI Lab’s A10 GPUs. For the Mechanistic Anomaly Dataset: The diffusion model was trained for
7
epochs with a learning rate of
2\times 10^{-4}
with the benign vectors taken from the final layer of benign finetuned models. The benign and backdoored finetuned models are trained on 50000 samples of benign data and 4453 samples of backdoored data, respectively, both at a learning rate of
1\times 10^{-5}
. For the JBB dataset, the diffusion model was trained for
14
epochs on a learning rate of
1\times 10^{-5}
. The benign and backdoored models were trained on a learning rate of
2\times 10^{-4}
. JailbreakBench was insufficiently large to fine-tune the models adequately, so we instead fine-tuned both benign and backdoored models using the Anthropic Sleeper Agent dataset. We continue to use JailbreakBench for evaluation to measure attack success under a standardized jailbreak dataset. For the ASA, everything was identical to JBB except testing was done on its own dataset. All models are trained with Adam Optimizers.

Conclusion

We introduce MANATEE, an inference-time diffusion method that recognizes out-of-distribution behavior in models’ hidden states and either automatically rejects the response or projects it onto a benign manifold. MANATEE achieves an average
78\%
reduction of ASR. By training on final layer hidden states from benign models and applying conditional steering, MANATEE reduces ASR across various models while preserving semantic meaning. These results suggest that manifold-based diffusion can serve as a lightweight, plug-and-play safety mechanism that improves safety performance without further training the model or compromising model robustness. Further work will explore the potential expansion of diffusion based on multiples to latent-based reasoning models like Continuous Chain of Thought (COCONUT)
Hao et al. 2024
and a wider variety of jailbreaking attacks.

5
Appendix

5.1
Training Details

Benign representation extraction.

The diffusion model is trained exclusively on benign hidden states extracted from aligned model generations. Benign examples are drawn from a prompt–completion dataset and rendered into a chat-style training string using a fixed prompt formatter. Hidden states are obtained by running the base model forward with
output_hidden_states=True
. For each example, we identify the token index at which the completion begins using tokenizer offset mappings. If the prompt/completion boundary cannot be aligned cleanly at the token level, the example is skipped.

Standardization.

Hidden vectors are standardized using mean
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and standard deviation
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
estimated from a subset of benign vectors:
x_{0}=(h_{0}-\mu)/\sigma
. The statistics
(\mu,\sigma)
are stored alongside the diffusion checkpoint and reused at inference.

Forward process.

We use a standard DDPM forward process with a cosine noise schedule. At timestep
t
, we corrupt
x_{0}
via:
x_{t}=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\,x_{0}+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\,\epsilon
where
\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)
and
\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\prod_{s=1}^{t}(1-\beta_{s})
.

Reverse process.

For diffusion steering, we initialize from a noised version of the anomalous hidden state at timestep
t_{\mathrm{start}}=\min(\lfloor\gamma T\rfloor,T-1)
where
\gamma\in(0,1]
is a strength parameter. We then apply DDPM reverse updates:

x_{t-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}\left(x_{t}-\frac{\beta_{t}}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}}\,\epsilon_{\theta}(x_{t},t)\right)+\sigma_{t}z

(3)

The purified representation is unstandardized via
h^{\prime}=\sigma\odot x_{0}+\mu
.

Construction of eigenvarieties
James Newton

Abstract
. These are notes based on four lectures given at the Heidelberg spring school on non-archimedean geometry and eigenvarieties. None of the contents are original work. Our goal is to explain the construction of eigenvarieties in various different contexts, including the prototypical example of the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve. We will also discuss some of the common geometric properties of eigenvarieties.

1.
Introduction

The goal of these lectures is to explain some examples of constructions of eigenvarieties, together with some of the properties of these spaces. We will apply results from Ludwig’s lectures
[
Lud24
]
, which develop crucial ingredients from
p
-adic analysis and geometry.

The main focus of the notes will be on two key examples. In Section
2
we will discuss the most classical setting of overconvergent modular forms and the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve. In Section
4
we will discuss overconvergent automorphic forms for definite quaternion algebras (over
{\mathbb{Q}}
) and their associated eigenvarieties. We include two further short sections with complementary material. In Section
3
we give a brief overview of different constructions of eigenvarieties appearing in the literature. In Section
5
we sketch the theory of overconvergent cohomology which generalizes the constructions of Section
4
— we hope that providing this general context will illuminate the earlier material.

2.
Overconvergent modular forms and the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve

We’ll begin by sketching some motivation for Coleman’s theory of
p
-adic families of overconvergent modular forms. After that, we’ll briefly describe the construction of the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve. These notes contain more details than the original lectures, but are still quite sketchy and the reader should refer to the original literature for precise details. In this section, we will make serious use of the geometry of modular curves. This will disappear in section
4
, where we’ll talk about the (in many ways simpler) case of overconvergent automorphic forms for definite quaternion algebras.

2.1.
Local constancy of slopes

The first main topic we’ll discuss is Banach spaces of overconvergent modular forms. The Coleman–Mazur eigencurve is built using these spaces and the spectral theory of the compact Hecke operator,
U_{p}
.

Fix a positive integer
N
and a prime
p\nmid N
. For each integer
k
, we can consider the
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space of modular forms
M_{k,N}:=M_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N)\cap\Gamma_{0}(p))
of level
\Gamma_{1}(N)\cap\Gamma_{0}(p)
and weight
k
. Recall that
\Gamma_{1}(N)
is the subgroup of
\SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
given by matrices which are strictly upper triangular mod
N
, whilst
\Gamma_{0}(p)
is given by matrices which are upper triangular mod
p
.

These spaces of modular forms come equipped with the Hecke operator
U_{p}
which acts on
q
-expansions by

U_{p}\left(\sum_{n}a_{n}q^{n}\right)=\sum_{n}a_{np}q^{n}.

Let
P_{k,N}(X)
be the characteristic polynomial of
U_{p}
on
M_{k,N}
. The polynomial
P_{k,N}
actually lies in
{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
— we can see from the action on
q
-expansions that it preserves the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-lattice of modular forms with integral
q
-expansion.

For convenience, we fix an algebraic closure
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
with additive valuation
v
extending the
p
-adic valuation on
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

For
h\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}
, let
d(k,h)
denote the number of roots
\alpha
of
P_{k,N}
(counting multiplicities) with
v(\alpha)\leq h
.

The valuations of
U_{p}
eigenvalues are traditionally called the
slopes
of modular forms (since they are the slopes of the Newton polygon of
P_{k,N}
). The integer
d(k,h)
is the degree of the slope
\leq h
factor of
P_{k,N}
.

Theorem 2.1.2

(Hida, Coleman, Wan)
.

Let
h\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}
. Let
k_{1},k_{2}
be two integers strictly bigger than
h+1
. There exists an integer
M_{h}
(possibly depending on
p,N,h
, but not on the weights
k_{i}
) such that
k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\pmod{p^{M_{h}}(p-1)}
implies
d(k_{1},h)=d(k_{2},h)
.

•

Hida proved this for
h=0
— in that case you can even take
M_{h}=0
(
p
odd) or
M_{h}=1
(
p
even).

•

Wan showed that
M_{h}
can be taken of order
O(h^{2})
(the implicit constants could depend on
p,N
).

•

Before Coleman proved his result, Gouvêa and Mazur conjectured that this “local constancy of slopes” should hold with
M_{h}=\lceil h\rceil
when the weights satisfy
k_{i}\geq 2h+2
. A counter-example to this was found by Buzzard and Calegari:
N=1
,
p=59
,
d(16,h)\neq d(3438,h)
for some
0<h\leq 1
. Until recently, the slopes of modular forms were still quite mysterious — however, conjectures due to Bergdall and Pollack
[
BP19a
,
BP19b
,
BP22a
]
and progress towards proving them in work of Liu, Truong, Xiao, and Zhao
[
LTXZ23
]
represent a big step forwards.

2.2.2.
Overconvergent modular forms

Our spaces of overconvergent modular forms of weight
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(first defined by Katz
[
Kat73
]
) will be sections of the line bundle
\omega^{k}
on certain affinoid subspaces of the adic generic fibre
{\mathcal{X}}:=(X)^{\mathrm{ad}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}

Since

is proper over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
we can construct its adic generic fibre by first considering the formal completion
\mathfrak{X}
of

along its special fibre

[

More explicitly, we can cover

by affine formal schemes

is covered by
\Spa(R,R)
and
(\mathfrak{X})^{\mathrm{ad}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
is covered by
\Spa\left(R\left[1/p\right],{R}\right)

3

3

In general we might need to take an integral closure of

2.2.8.
U_{p}
and the canonical subgroup

Our next task is to equip

2.4.
Families of overconvergent modular forms and proving local constancy of slopes

Another key contribution of Coleman
[
Col97
]
was to define for each affinoid weight

There is a compact operator

on

f_{k}^{*}:M_{\kappa}^{\dagger,v}(N)\widehat{\otimes}_{R,f_{k}^{*}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\cong M_{k}^{\dagger,v}(N).

To prove local constancy of slopes, we consider the following: fix a weight
k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and a slope
h\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}
. The characteristic power series

has a factorisation

, where the Newton polygons of the two factors have all slopes
\leq h

[
Lud24

of

(which we assume to be connected), with corresponding affinoid weight

.

In particular, we can find a finite projective

, a direct summand of

, such that

induces isomorphisms

. This proves that the vector space on the right hand side has the same dimension whenever

We won’t go into how Coleman defined families of overconvergent modular forms — it involved twisting by a

2.6.

As in the previous section,

Proposition 2.6.1

The map

Proof.

Recall that

has an open cover by affinoids of the form

where

Since

is a Dedekind domain. Since

∎

Remark 2.6.4
.

Note that the classical points are not dense for the usual topology on the adic space
{\mathcal{E}}

With density of classical points proven, we can give an example of a geometric property of the eigencurve which is deduced from known properties of classical modular forms.

Proposition 2.6.5
.

{\mathcal{E}}
is reduced.

Proof.

We sketch the proof of this, following Chenevier

Che05
]
and Bellaïche

Che05

that
{\mathcal{E}}
is reduced.

We prove the claim that
{\mathcal{E}}
is reduced in a neighbourhood of each classical point. For concreteness, we will use the notion of slope data
[
Lud24
, §1.3.4]
. Let
z\in{\mathcal{E}}
be a classical point. By
[
Lud24
, Corollary 3.20]
, the point
\pi(z)
of the spectral curve has an open neighbourhood

for
U\subset{\mathcal{W}}
connected affinoid open and

\widetilde{V}=\pi^{-1}(V_{U,h})

\widetilde{V}
is an open neighbourhood of

{\mathcal{E}}
with

{\mathcal{O}}(\widetilde{V})=\im\left({\mathcal{O}}(U)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}{\mathbb{T}}^{N}\to\End_{{\mathcal{O}}(U)}(M^{\dagger,v}_{\kappa_{U}}(N)^{\leq h})\right).

Consider the integral weights
[k]\in U
with

already contains the integral weight
k_{0}=w(z)
). The image of
{\mathcal{O}}(\widetilde{V})
in

\im\left({\mathbb{T}}^{N}\to\End_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(M^{\dagger}_{k}(N)^{\leq h})\right)=\im\left({\mathbb{T}}^{N}\to\End_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(H^{0}(X_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}},\omega^{k})^{\leq h})\right)

by the classicality theorem. We claim that this (classical) Hecke algebra is semisimple. The Hecke operators
T_{l},S_{l}
for

act semisimply on the whole space

— this is a standard fact which follows from the normality of these Hecke operators with respect to the Petersson inner product on the cuspidal subspace (the Eisenstein series can be handled separated). We also need to know that

acts semi-simply. On the

U_{p}
-operator is normal with respect to the Petersson inner product. From the automorphic representations point of view, this is a consequence of the fact that unramified twists of the Steinberg representation of

have a one-dimensional space of invariants under the Iwahori subgroup. Then we need to worry about the space of
p
-old forms, but that is why we chose the slope bound

. This means that each Hecke eigenform of level

Now we claim that

is reduced. Suppose

with

-module

Finally, we discuss Galois representations. For each classical point

, there is a continuous semisimple Galois representation

•

•

By the Chebotarev density theorem, specifying the characteristic polynomials of Frobenius elements determines the semisimple representation

3.
An overview of constructions of eigenvarieties

In the previous section we have described how the eigenvariety machine explained in
[
Lud24
]
can be applied to construct the eigencurve. We can ask how this generalizes to other contexts. The goal is to define adic spaces which
p
-adically interpolate the systems of Hecke eigenvalues attached to (a certain class of) automorphic representations of
G({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
, where
G
is a fixed reductive group defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

In the next section, we give some details of the case where
G
is given by the units in a definite quaternion algebra
D/{\mathbb{Q}}
. It is also instructive to consider the case
G=\mathrm{Res}_{F/{\mathbb{Q}}}\GL_{1}
for a number field
F
, where the
\Spa(R,R^{+})
-points of the eigenvariety with tame level a compact open subgroup
U^{p}\subset{\mathbb{A}}_{F,f}^{p}
correspond to continuous homomorphisms

F^{\times}\backslash{\mathbb{A}}_{F}^{\times}/U^{p}\to R^{\times}.

Both these cases were worked out in
[
Buz04
]
.

In general, different methods to construct eigenvarieties are available depending on the automorphic context. The following list collects together some examples in the literature, grouped in terms of methods. We have omitted a discussion of the significant literature (much of it due to Hida) about the
ordinary

0

3.1.
Coherent cohomology of Shimura varieties

As we have seen, the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve was constructed using overconvergent modular forms, which are spaces of sections of line bundles on certain adic subspaces of modular curves.

In contexts where we can associate a Shimura variety to the reductive group
G

p
-adic family of Eisenstein series to define families of overconvergent modular forms was initially an obstruction to this, which was only overcome when more conceptual definitions of these families were discovered. 3.2.
Singular cohomology of locally symmetric spaces

The Eichler–Shimura isomorphism tells us that we can find systems of Hecke eigenvalues for modular forms of weight

3.4.
Comparing different constructions of eigenvarieties

Often in one automorphic setting there is more than one method available to construct an eigenvariety. For example, we can construct an eigencurve using overconvergent modular forms, but also using overconvergent modular symbols (see
[
Bel21
]
, for example). We would like to know that the resulting eigenvariety is independent of the way it is constructed. This can often be verified, using the density of classical points and the fact that we can identify the subsets of classical points the different eigenvarieties when they arise from the same collection of automorphic representations. Examples of precise statements can be found in
[
Bel21
, §7.2]
,
[

, Proposition 7.2.8]

[

, Theorem 5.1.2]
,
[

,
[

, Theorem 5.18]
.

These results allow us to compare different kinds of eigenvarieties, but they do not produce maps between different kinds of spaces of overconvergent automorphic forms. To do the latter is more delicate — for example, see
[
AIS15
,
CHJ17
,
RC23
]
for results comparing overconvergent modular forms and overconvergent modular symbols.

4.
Eigencurves for definite quaternion algebras

4.1.
Automorphic forms for definite quaternion algebras

In this section, we will explain the theory of overconvergent automorphic forms in a simpler context, where the definition of families and the proof of ‘small slope implies classical’ will be relatively straightforward. We essentially follow Buzzard’s article
[
Buz04
]
, which was inspired by Stevens’s overconvergent modular symbols. Our presentation has also been influenced by Chenevier’s definition of overconvergent automorphic forms for definite unitary groups
[
Che04
]
.

We will work with a definite quaternion algebra
D/{\mathbb{Q}}
, so
D\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}{\mathbb{R}}
is isomorphic to Hamilton’s quaternions
\mathbb{H}
. The discriminant
\delta_{D}
of
D
will be a product of an odd number of distinct primes, determined by:
D\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}\cong M_{2}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
for
l\nmid\delta_{D}
. When
l|\delta_{D}
,
D\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
is a division algebra of dimension
4
over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
. We fix once and for all a maximal order
{\mathcal{O}}_{D}
in
D
, and for each prime
l\nmid\delta_{D}
an isomorphism
{\mathcal{O}}_{D}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}\cong M_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})

We can define an algebraic group

with points in a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra

G({\mathbb{Q}})=(D\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}}R)^{\times}
. This is an inner form of

We fix a prime

Our spaces of classical and

∎

5.
Overconvergent cohomology

In the final section, we’ll describe how the above construction for definite quaternion algebras generalizes, following Ash, Stevens
[
ASb
]
and Hansen
[
Han17
]
(a related construction is due to Urban
[
Urb11

). For concreteness, we will fix attention on the group

, but this can be replaced with a more general reductive group. We fix the upper-triangular Borel subgroup

, diagonal maximal torus
T

is the inverse image of

in
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})

5.1.
Locally symmetric spaces and automorphic local systems.

Locally symmetric spaces are generalisations of modular curves, and their singular cohomology can be described in terms of automorphic representations. For
K\subset\GL_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})
compact open, define

Y_{K}:=\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\GL_{n}({\mathbb{A}})/{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\SO_{n}({\mathbb{R}})K.

When
K
is sufficiently small,
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})
acts freely on
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{A}})/({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\SO_{n}({\mathbb{R}})K)
. We’ve already mentioned Borel’s result that
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\GL_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{f})/K
is finite. If we choose double coset representatives
(g_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
, then we get an isomorphism

\displaystyle\coprod_{i=1}^{d}\Gamma_{i}\backslash X^{\GL_{n}}

\displaystyle\cong Y_{K}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}x

\displaystyle\mapsto\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})(x,g_{i})K

describing
Y_{K}
as a disjoint union of arithmetic quotients of the symmetric space
X^{\GL_{n}}=GL_{n}({\mathbb{R}})/{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\SO_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
, where
\Gamma_{i}=g_{i}Kg_{i}^{-1}\cap\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})
.

For a
B
-dominant weight
\lambda
of
\GL_{n}
, we have
\lambda(\mathrm{diag}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}))=\prod t_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}
for a decreasing sequence of integers
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
. Write
V_{\lambda,{\mathbb{Q}}}
for the highest weight
\lambda
representation of
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})
.

Then we have a local system of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector spaces,
{\mathcal{V}}_{\lambda,{\mathbb{Q}}}
on
Y_{K}
defined by the quotient

\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\left(V_{\lambda,{\mathbb{Q}}}\times\GL_{n}({\mathbb{A}})/{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\SO_{n}({\mathbb{R}})K\right)

with the diagonal action of
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})
.

We can alternatively define a
p
-adic local system
{\mathcal{V}}_{\lambda,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
using the representation
V_{\lambda,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
of
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
by taking the quotient

\left(\left(\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}})\backslash\GL_{n}({\mathbb{A}})/{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\SO_{n}({\mathbb{R}})K^{p}\right)\times V_{\lambda,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\right)/K_{p}

where
K_{p}
acts by
(x,v)k=(xk,k^{-1}v)
.

Exercise 5.1.1
.

(v,g)\mapsto(g,g_{p}^{-1}v)

gives an isomorphism

These cohomology groups can be equipped with Hecke operators using double coset operators, just like in the quaternion algebra case. We fix a set of primes
S
containing

K_{l}=\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})
for all
l\notin S

K_{p}=\mathrm{Iw}_{p}
. We will use the abstract Hecke algebra over
{\mathbb{Q}}
,

{\mathbb{T}}^{S}_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\left(\prod_{l\notin S}{\mathcal{H}}(\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}),\GL_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}))\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}\right)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}[U_{p}]

containing the spherical Hecke algebras at
l\notin S
and the operator
14
14

14

It is more natural to add a bigger, commutative, Hecke algebra at
p
, but we’ll fix just this one operator for simplicity.

U_{p}=[\mathrm{Iw}_{p}\mathrm{diag}(p^{n-1},p^{n-2},\ldots,p,1)\mathrm{Iw}_{p}].

Later we will extend scalars to
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.

It is a theorem of Franke
[
Fra98
]
that the cohomology groups
H^{*}(Y_{K},{\mathcal{V}}_{\lambda,{\mathbb{C}}})
can be computed in terms of automorphic representations of

Theorem 5.1.2

(Borel, Clozel

There is a subspace

with a
{\mathbb{T}}^{S}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-equivariant isomorphism

The sum is over cuspidal automorphic representations of
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
and the multiplicities
m_{i,\lambda}(\pi_{\infty})
can only be non-zero if

is
cohomological of weight

\lambda

with
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{n+1-i}=w
independent of

15

15

This is a consequence of Clozel’s ‘purity lemma’.
. In other words, the dual representation

Moreover, when the

\displaystyle n=2m\text{ even}:~

\displaystyle m_{i,\lambda}(\pi_{\infty})=\binom{m-1}{i-m^{2}}

\displaystyle n=2m+1\text{ odd}:~

\displaystyle m_{i,\lambda}(\pi_{\infty})=\binom{m}{i-m(m+1)}

It has been shown by Harder and Raghuram

As in the quaternionic case, we now interpolate the spaces

5.2.
Locally analytic representations of
\mathrm{Iw}_{p}

We will be brief here, and refer to

]
for the details. We write

, so the weight
w_{0}\lambda

\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n-1},\ldots,\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{v}_{\lambda}=\Ind_{B({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}^{\mathrm{Iw}_{p}}(w_{0}\lambda)^{v-an}

\displaystyle:=\{f:\mathrm{Iw}_{p}\to{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\mid f(tng)=w_{0}\lambda(t)f(g)

\displaystyle\text{for all }t\in T({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),n\in N({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),\text{and }f|_{\overline{N}_{1}}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{v}_{\mathrm{an}}(\overline{N}_{1},{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\},

We have a natural injective map

to define a local system of Banach spaces on

5.3.
Overconvergent cohomology in families

The natural weight space here parameterizes characters of the (integral)
p
-adic torus:

Definition 5.3.1
.

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}:=\Hom(T({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),\mathbb{G}_{m,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
. For an affinoid
(R,R^{+})
, we have
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(R,R^{+})=\Hom_{\mathrm{cts}}(T({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),R^{\times})
.

The
integral weights
are the
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
points of
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}
given by algebraic characters
\lambda:T\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.

We can describe
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}
explicitly as a disjoint union of
n

For an affinoid weight
\kappa:T({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\to R^{\times}
, and
v
sufficiently large, it is possible to define a (bounded) complex of potentially ONable

{\mathcal{C}}^{\bullet}(Y_{K},{\mathcal{A}}_{\kappa}^{v})
of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-Banach spaces equipped with a compact action of

, lifting the action of
U_{p}
on

5.4.
The eigenvariety

We can now construct an eigenvariety. First we construct a Fredholm surface, which is highly non-canonical - it depends on choices involved in the construction of the complex
{\mathcal{C}}^{\bullet}(Y_{K},{\mathcal{A}}_{\kappa}^{v})
. These choices can at least be made independent of
\kappa
. So, we have a compact operator
\widetilde{U}_{p}
on

\bigoplus_{i}{\mathcal{C}}^{i}(Y_{K},{\mathcal{A}}_{\kappa}^{v})

and its characteristic power series which glues over
{\mathcal{W}}
to give a universal characteristic power series
F_{\widetilde{U}_{p}}^{\dagger}\in{\mathcal{O}}({\mathcal{W}}_{n})\{\{X\}\}
and a Fredholm hypersurface
{\mathcal{Z}}:=V(F_{\widetilde{U}_{p}}^{\dagger})\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{n}}
.

The slope decompositions of the complex
{\mathcal{C}}^{\bullet}(Y_{K},{\mathcal{A}}_{\kappa}^{v})
define a perfect complex of coherent sheaves
{\mathcal{N}}^{\bullet}
on
{\mathcal{Z}}
. Hansen considers the eigenvariety
{\mathcal{E}}(H^{*}({\mathcal{N}}^{\bullet}))
built out of the eigenvariety datum
({\mathcal{Z}},\oplus_{i}H^{i}({\mathcal{N}}^{\bullet}),{\mathbb{T}}^{S})
. We can alternatively construct a (mildly) ‘derived’ eigenvariety
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{N}}^{\bullet})
by considering the finite
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}
-algebra generated by
{\mathbb{T}}^{S}
in
\End_{D^{b}_{\mathrm{Coh}}({\mathcal{Z}})}({\mathcal{N}}^{\bullet})
.

The natural map

\End_{D^{b}_{\mathrm{Coh}}({\mathcal{Z}})}({\mathcal{N}}^{\bullet})\to\End_{\mathrm{Coh}({\mathcal{Z}})}(\oplus_{i}H^{i}({\mathcal{N}}))

has nilpotent kernel, so it induces a closed immersion
{\mathcal{E}}(H^{*}({\mathcal{N}}^{\bullet}))\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{N}}^{\bullet})
which is an isomorphism on reduced subspaces.

5.5.
The example
n=2

When
n=2
, we have essentially re-constructed the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve, in a way which is very close to the construction using overconvergent modular symbols described in
[
Bel21
]
. The Eichler–Shimura theorem and the arguments sketched in §
4.4.1
show that if we restrict everything to the locus
{\mathcal{W}}\subset{\mathcal{W}}_{2}
given by weights which are trivial on the second entry in the torus, then the underlying reduced subspace of
{\mathcal{E}}(H^{*}({\mathcal{N}}^{\bullet}))
is equal to the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve.

One subtlety which arises here is that for open affinoids
U\subset{\mathcal{W}}
, the pieces of the slope decomposition
H^{1}(Y_{K},{\mathcal{A}}^{v}_{\kappa_{U}})^{\leq h}
are not necessarily locally free over
{\mathcal{O}}(U)
. They have torsion supported at the points
\kappa\in U
where
H^{0}(Y_{K},{\mathcal{A}}^{v}_{\kappa})^{\leq h}\neq 0
. For example, when
\kappa
is trivial and
h\geq 1
.

5.6.
Dimensions of eigenvarieties and classical points

The eigenvarieties for
\GL_{n}
with
n>2
are rather mysterious. The weight space
{\mathcal{W}}_{n}
has dimension
n
, but the classical cuspidal points, by Theorem
5.1.2
, all have weights lying in the
essentially self-dual subspace

{\mathcal{W}}_{n}^{esd}
of dimension
1+\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, or codimension
l_{0}=\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
.

We expect most irreducible components of the eigenvariety for
\GL_{n}/{\mathbb{Q}}
to have dimension
1+\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
. This reflects the fact that ‘generic’ systems of mod
p
Hecke eigenvalues should only appear in the range of cohomological degrees with length
l_{0}
described in Theorem
5.1.2
. See Urban’s paper
[
Urb11
]
for a precise conjecture (related to a conjecture of Hida in the ordinary case) and also Hansen’s paper
[
Han17
]
for a conjecture and some evidence. The fact that the dimension is
\geq 1+\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
can be verified in many cases, but equality seems to be much harder and could be regarded as a non-Abelian version of the Leopoldt conjecture.

The classical points of our eigenvarieties now come from Hecke eigenvalues appearing in
H^{*}(Y_{K},{\mathcal{V}}_{\lambda,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
for integral weights
\lambda
. They are not expected to be Zariski dense in the eigenvariety when
n>2
. Examples of Ash–Pollack–Stevens
[
APS08
]
in the slope
0
case demonstrate this.

Conjecture 5.6.1

(Ash–Pollack–Stevens)
.

Suppose an irreducible component of
{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{N}^{\bullet})
contains a Zariski dense subset of cuspidal classical points. Then all those classical points come from essentially self-dual automorphic representations
\pi
16
16

16

I.e.
\pi^{\vee}
is isomorphic to a twist of
\pi
by a Hecke character.
.

Conversely, the essentially self-dual classical points do live (and are Zariski-dense) in closed subsets of dimension
1+\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor

[
Xia18
]
. One can think of these subsets as arising from eigenvarieties for symplectic or orthogonal similitude groups.

The remaining irreducible components, which are expected to contain the non-essentially-self-dual classical points are ‘genuinely
p
-adic’ in the sense that we can’t expect to construct them using classical automorphic representations. This is analogous to the picture when we look at cohomology of locally symmetric spaces with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-coefficients — again the ‘defect’
l_{0}
plays a crucial role, and there are torsion classes which we do not expect to be able to relate to classical automorphic representations.

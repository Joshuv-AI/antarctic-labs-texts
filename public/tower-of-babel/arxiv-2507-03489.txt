Chiral Integrable Boundary States in the SU(4) Alternating Spin Chain
Yang Liu, Jun-Bao Wu

Abstract
Previously identified integrable boundary states in ABJM theory are exclusively achiral. This paper presents the first chiral integrable boundary states in the S ​ U ​ ( 4 ) SU(4) alternating spin chain from the planar two-loop dilatation operator in the scalar sector. Utilizing a sufficient condition for the untwisted integrable condition, we identify specific two-site and four-site basis boundary states as chiral integrable states. Numerical evidence indicates that other basis states are unlikely to be chiral integrable. Furthermore, we compute the overlaps between these chiral integrable basis states and on-shell Bethe eigenstates.

Introduction

Integrable boundary states, more precisely understood as integrable initial states in their physical interpretation, were first studied in the context of two-dimensional integrable quantum field theories with boundaries
[
1
]
.
These states are characterized by the fact that they are annihilated by all odd conserved charges. In 2017, Piroli, Pozsgay, and Vernier proposed an analogous definition for integrable boundary states
\left|\mathcal{B}\right\rangle
in lattice models
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle Q_{2k+1}\left|\mathcal{B}\right\rangle=0,k\geq 1,

(1)

where the conserved charges
Q_{n+1},n\geq 1
are generated from the transfer matrix
\tau\left(u\right)
via

\displaystyle Q_{n+1}\propto\left.\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial u^{n}}\ln\tau\left(u\right)\right|_{u=u_{\ast}},

(2)

with a suitable choice of
u_{\ast}
such that
Q_{n+1}
can be expressed as a sum over operators with finite range.
Since the odd conserved charges are generated by the transfer matrix, a sufficient condition for a boundary state to be integrable can be formulated directly in terms of transfer matrix
\tau(u)
:

\displaystyle\Pi\tau\left(u\right)\Pi\left|\mathcal{B}\right\rangle=\tau\left(u\right)\left|\mathcal{B}\right\rangle,

(3)

with
\Pi
being the reflection operator

\displaystyle\Pi\left|A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{L}\right\rangle=\left|A_{L}\cdots A_{2}A_{1}\right\rangle.

(4)

Motivated by significant applications in both quench dynamics of spin chains and gauge/string duality, there has been considerable interest in deriving explicit overlap formulas between integrable boundary states and eigenstates (on-shell Bethe states) of the transfer matrix. These overlaps exhibit several universal features.
Certain selection rules emerge, determining when the overlap can be non-vanishing. One crucial selection rule demands the Bethe roots characterizing a given eigenstate must exhibit a specific pairing structure. This is because integrable states preserve infinitely many conserved charges, as shown in (
1
). In higher-rank spin chains, Bethe roots can be paired in different ways
[
3
]
. An overlap is referred to as chiral if the pairing occurs among Bethe roots of the same type, and achiral if the pairing involves roots of different types. Furthermore, the non-vanishing overlaps involving integrable boundary states and eigenstates are relatively simple, compared to non-integrable cases, often admitting compact expressions in terms of ratios of Gaudin-like determinants multiplied by scalar factors which are functions of Bethe roots.

\displaystyle[\tau(u),\tau(v)]=[\bar{\tau}(u),\bar{\tau}(v)]=[\tau(u),\bar{\tau}(v)]=0.

\displaystyle\Pi\tau(u)\Pi\left|\mathcal{B}\right\rangle=\bar{\tau}(u)\left|\mathcal{B}\right\rangle.

2
ABJM spin chain

We begin by reviewing the
SU(4)
alternating spin chain from ABJM theory.
Gauge invariance requires that single-trace operators be represented as states in an alternating
SU(4)
spin chain. States on odd sites of this chain transform in the fundamental representation of the R-symmetry group
SU(4)
, and those on even sites transform in the anti-fundamental representation. The planar two-loop dilatation operator acting on the scalar sector of ABJM theory can be described by the following spin chain Hamiltonian
[
31
,
32
]

\displaystyle H=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{2L}\left(2-2P_{l,l+2}+P_{l,l+2}K_{l,l+1}+K_{l,l+1}P_{l,l+2}\right),

(8)

where
P_{ij}
and
K_{ij}
are permutation and trace operators, respectively. Periodic boundary condition is understood.
The orthonormal bases of the local Hilbert spaces are
\left|A\right\rangle
(
A=1,\cdots,4
) for the fundamental representation sites and
\left|\bar{A}\right\rangle
(
\bar{A}=\bar{1},\cdots,\bar{4}
) for the anti-fundamental representation ones. The permutation operator
P
and trace operator act as

\displaystyle P\left|A\right\rangle\otimes\left|B\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|B\right\rangle\otimes\left|A\right\rangle,\quad P\left|\bar{A}\right\rangle\otimes\left|\bar{B}\right\rangle=\left|\bar{B}\right\rangle\otimes\left|\bar{A}\right\rangle;

(9)

\displaystyle K\left|A\right\rangle\otimes\left|\bar{B}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{AB}\sum_{C=1}^{4}\left|C\right\rangle\otimes\left|\bar{C}\right\rangle,\quad K\left|\bar{A}\right\rangle\otimes\left|B\right\rangle=\delta_{AB}\sum_{C=1}^{4}\left|\bar{C}\right\rangle\otimes\left|C\right\rangle.

(10)

It is also convenient to introduce the index notation for these operators. For example,

\displaystyle I_{a_{1}a_{2}}^{b_{1}b_{2}}=\delta_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\delta_{a_{2}}^{b_{2}},\quad P_{a_{1}a_{2}}^{b_{1}b_{2}}=\delta_{a_{1}}^{b_{2}}\delta_{a_{2}}^{b_{1}},\quad K_{a_{1}\bar{a}_{2}}^{b_{1}\bar{b}_{2}}=\delta^{\bar{b}_{1}\bar{b}_{2}}\delta_{a_{1}a_{2}},

(11)

P_{ij}
and
K_{ij}
satisfy the following relations which will be used extensively in subsequent derivations in
subsection 3.2

\displaystyle P_{ij}P_{ij}

\displaystyle=I_{ij},

(12)

\displaystyle P_{ij}P_{ik}

\displaystyle=P_{ik}P_{kj}=P_{jk}P_{ij},

(13)

\displaystyle K_{ij}K_{ij}

\displaystyle=4K_{ij},

(14)

\displaystyle K_{ij}K_{ik}

\displaystyle=K_{ij}P_{jk}=P_{jk}K_{ik},

(15)

\displaystyle\textbf{tr}_{i}P_{ij}

\displaystyle=I_{j},\quad\textbf{tr}_{i}K_{ij}=I_{j}.

(16)

The
SU(4)
alternating spin chain has four
R
matrices,

\displaystyle R_{0j}(u)

\displaystyle=uI+P_{0j},

\displaystyle R_{0\bar{j}}(u)

\displaystyle=-(u+2)I+K_{0\bar{j}},

\displaystyle R_{\bar{0}j}(u)

\displaystyle=-(u+2)I+K_{\bar{0}j},

\displaystyle R_{\bar{0}\bar{j}}(u)

\displaystyle=uI+P_{\bar{0}\bar{j}},

(17)

in which
0
and
\bar{0}
denote the auxiliary space in the fundamental and anti-fundamental representations of the R symmetry, respectively.
2L
sites of the alternating spin chain are labeled as
1,\bar{1},2,\bar{2},\cdots,L,\bar{L}
. Based on these, two monodromy matrices are defined as

\displaystyle T_{0}(u)

\displaystyle=R_{01}(u)R_{0\bar{1}}(u)\cdots R_{0L}(u)R_{0\bar{L}}(u),

(18)

\displaystyle T_{\bar{0}}(u)

\displaystyle=R_{\bar{0}1}(u)R_{\bar{0}\bar{1}}(u)\cdots R_{\bar{0}L}(u)R_{\bar{0}\bar{L}}(u).

(19)

The associated transfer matrices are obtained by tracing over the auxiliary space

\displaystyle\tau(u)

\displaystyle=\text{tr}_{0}T_{0}(u),\quad\bar{\tau}(u)=\text{tr}_{\bar{0}}T_{\bar{0}}(u).

(20)

A general on-shell Bethe state is characterized by three sets of Bethe roots,
\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{v}
, which satisfy a set of Bethe equations

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned} 1=\left(\frac{u_{j}+\frac{i}{2}}{u_{j}-\frac{i}{2}}\right)^{L}\prod_{\begin{array}[]{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{array}}^{N_{\mathbf{u}}}S(u_{j},u_{k})\prod_{k=1}^{N_{\mathbf{w}}}\tilde{S}(u_{j},w_{k})\end{aligned}\\
\begin{aligned} 1=\prod_{\begin{array}[]{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{array}}^{N_{\mathbf{w}}}S(w_{j},w_{k})\prod_{k=1}^{N_{\mathbf{u}}}\tilde{S}(w_{j},u_{k})\prod_{k=1}^{N_{\mathbf{v}}}\tilde{S}(w_{j},v_{k})\end{aligned}\\
\begin{aligned} 1=\left(\frac{v_{j}+\frac{i}{2}}{v_{j}-\frac{i}{2}}\right)^{L}\prod_{\begin{array}[]{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{array}}^{N_{\mathbf{v}}}S(v_{j},v_{k})\prod_{k=1}^{N_{\mathbf{w}}}\tilde{S}(v_{j},w_{k}),\end{aligned}\end{gathered}

where
N_{u},N_{w},N_{v}
denote the number of rapidities in the sets
\mathbf{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N_{u}}\}
,
\mathbf{w}=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{N_{w}}\}
,
\mathbf{v}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{N_{v}}\}
, and

\displaystyle S(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{u-v-i}{u-v+i},\quad\tilde{S}(u,v)=\frac{u-v+\frac{i}{2}}{u-v-\frac{i}{2}}.

(24)

Finally, the eigenvalues of the transfer matrices are expressed as

\displaystyle\Lambda(u)=

\displaystyle(-u-2)^{L}(u+1)^{L}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{u}}\frac{u-iu_{i}-1/2}{u-iu_{i}+1/2}

\displaystyle+(-u)^{L}(u+1)^{L}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{v}}\frac{u-iv_{i}+5/2}{u-iv_{i}+3/2}

\displaystyle+(-u)^{L}(u+2)^{L}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{u}}\frac{u-iu_{i}+3/2}{u-iu_{i}+1/2}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{w}}\frac{u-iw_{i}}{u-iw_{i}+1}

\displaystyle+(-u)^{L}(u+2)^{L}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{v}}\frac{u-iv_{i}+1/2}{u-iv_{i}+3/2}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{w}}\frac{u-iw_{i}+2}{u-iw_{i}+1},

(25)

\displaystyle\bar{\Lambda}(u)=

\displaystyle(-u)^{L}(u+1)^{L}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{u}}\frac{u-iu_{i}+5/2}{u-iu_{i}+3/2}

\displaystyle+(-u-2)^{L}(u+1)^{L}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{v}}\frac{u-iv_{i}-1/2}{u-iv_{i}+1/2}

\displaystyle+(-u)^{L}(u+2)^{L}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{u}}\frac{u-iu_{i}+1/2}{u-iu_{i}+3/2}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{w}}\frac{u-iw_{i}+2}{u-iw_{i}+1}

\displaystyle+(-u)^{L}(u+2)^{L}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{v}}\frac{u-iv_{i}+3/2}{u-iv_{i}+1/2}\prod_{i=1}^{K_{w}}\frac{u-iw_{i}}{u-iw_{i}+1}.

(26)

Chiral Integrable Boundary States

By explicitly examining the expressions for
\Lambda(u)\eqref{lmd}
and
\bar{\Lambda}(u)\eqref{lmdb}

\left\{\mathbf{u}\right\}=\left\{-\mathbf{u}\right\},\left\{\mathbf{w}\right\}=\left\{-\mathbf{w}\right\},\left\{\mathbf{v}\right\}=\left\{-\mathbf{v}\right\}

\Lambda(u)=\bar{\Lambda}(-u-2)
holds. This motivates the expectation that the integrability condition

\displaystyle\tau(u)\left|\mathcal{B}\right\rangle=\bar{\tau}(-u-2)\left|\mathcal{B}\right\rangle

(27)

leads to the chiral pairing structure

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{u}\right\}=\left\{-\mathbf{u}\right\},\left\{\mathbf{w}\right\}=\left\{-\mathbf{w}\right\},\left\{\mathbf{v}\right\}=\left\{-\mathbf{v}\right\}

(28)

for the overlap
\left\langle\mathcal{B}|\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle

In fact,
(
27
)
can be derived from the untwisted integrable condition
[
3

\displaystyle\Pi\tau(u)\Pi\left|\mathcal{B}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau(u)\left|\mathcal{B}\right\rangle,

\Pi

\displaystyle\Pi\left|B^{1}\bar{B}_{1}B^{2}\bar{B}_{2}\cdots B^{L}\bar{B}_{L}\right\rangle=\left|\bar{B}_{L}B^{L}\cdots\bar{B}_{2}B^{2}\bar{B}_{1}B^{1}\right\rangle.

(30)

The transformation
\Pi\tau(u)\Pi
reverses the order of R-matrix products

\displaystyle\Pi\tau(u)\Pi=\text{tr}_{0}R_{0\bar{L}}(u)R_{0L}(u)\cdots R_{0\bar{1}}(u)R_{01}(u).

(31)

Using crossing symmetry relations for R-matrices

\displaystyle R_{0\bar{j}}^{t_{a}}(u)=R_{\bar{0}\bar{j}}(-u-2),R_{0j}^{t_{a}}(u)=R_{\bar{0}j}(-u-2),

(32)

where

denotes transposition with respect to auxiliary space, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Pi\tau(u)\Pi

\displaystyle=\text{tr}_{0}\left(R_{\bar{0}1}(-u-2)R_{\bar{0}\bar{1}}(-u-2)\cdots R_{\bar{0}L}(-u-2)R_{\bar{0}\bar{L}}(-u-2)\right)^{t_{a}}

(33)

\displaystyle=\bar{\tau}\left(-u-2\right).

Thus gives rise to
(

3.1
Chiral Integrable Boundary States

Solving the equations

\left|B\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left|B\right\rangle=\begin{cases}\left|A\bar{B}\cdots A\bar{B}\right\rangle,A\neq B&Any\ L\\

3.2
Proof

To systematically solve the equations
(
43
)
, we organize our analysis according to the values of
m
and
n
in the operators
\mathcal{O}_{m,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{m,n}
. The overall strategy can be sketched as follows. First, we consider the case
n=0
or
m=0
, where the resulting integrable boundary basis states exhibit specific periodic structures. Next, we examine the cases
n=1
or
m=1
, in which the operators contain exactly one trace or one permutation operator. By performing explicit computations, additional constraints on the boundary states are obtained. Finally, for higher-order cases with
n\geq 2
or
m\geq 2
, it is observed that if
\left|B\right\rangle
exhibits the structural patterns identified in the previous steps, then the operator actions vanish automatically, ensuring that the integrability condition is satisfied without further restrictions.

In the following, we present the detailed derivations corresponding to each of the cases outlined above.

3.2.1
n=0
or
m=0

We begin by analyzing the operator structures in the simplest cases where either
n=0
or
m=0
. Upon taking the trace over the auxiliary space
0
explicitly, these operators contain only permutation operators and act either on the fundamental or anti-fundamental sites of the alternating spin chain. Concretely, they take the following forms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{m,0}=\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}}P_{i_{m}i_{1}}P_{i_{m}i_{2}}\cdots P_{i_{m}i_{m-1}},

(45)

where
P_{i_{m}i_{1}}P_{i_{m}i_{2}}\cdots P_{i_{m}i_{m-1}}
cyclically shifts the states at positions
i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}
one site to the left, within this subset of sites.

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{m,0}=\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}}P_{i_{1}i_{2}}P_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots P_{i_{m-1}i_{m}},

(46)

where
P_{i_{1}i_{2}}P_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots P_{i_{m-1}i_{m}}
instead shifts the same set of states one site to the right within the positions
i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}
.

Likewise, the operators acting purely on the anti-fundamental sites take the form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{0,n}=\sum_{\bar{j}_{1}<\cdots<\bar{j}_{n}}P_{\bar{j}_{1}\bar{j}_{2}}P_{\bar{j}_{2}\bar{j}_{3}}\cdots P_{\bar{j}_{n-1}\bar{j}_{n}},

(47)

where
P_{\bar{j}_{1}\bar{j}_{2}}P_{\bar{j}_{2}\bar{j}_{3}}\cdots P_{\bar{j}_{n-1}\bar{j}_{n}}
shifts the states at positions
\bar{j}_{1},\cdots,\bar{j}_{n}
one site to the right within the selected subset.

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{0,n}=\sum_{\bar{j}_{1}<\cdots<\bar{j}_{n}}P_{\bar{j}_{n}\bar{j}_{1}}P_{\bar{j}_{n}\bar{j}_{2}}\cdots P_{\bar{j}_{n}\bar{j}_{n-1}},

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied chiral integrable boundary states in the ABJM spin chain. These states had not appeared in previous investigations of integrable boundary states within the
AdS_{4}/CFT_{3}
correspondence. Among the basis states, we identified specific two-site or four-site states satisfy the untwisted integrable condition and exhibit this chiral property. We have some numerical evidences that other basis states are not chiral integrable. We further computed the overlap between these chiral integrable states and general Bethe states.

A key step in our analysis was the use of (
38
) which is a sufficient condition for a state to be chiral integrable. Crucially, this sufficient condition is simpler to solve in practice than (
41
)—which is equivalent to the untwisted integrable condition—or than the formulation involving local higher charges generated from the transfer matrices
\tau(u)
and
\bar{\tau}(u)
. Since numerical results support that other basis states are not chiral integrable, we conjecture that this sufficient condition is also necessary. If one can proof this, then the determination of all chiral integrable states among a certain set of states will be greatly simplified. We also anticipate discovering more complex chiral integrable states, such as matrix product states (MPS), based on solutions to the corresponding KT relation
[
3
]
. An alternative approach involves generalizing the necessary integrability condition for MPS
[
45
]
from
SO(N)
spin chains to the ABJM setting. This condition allows us to systematically identify candidate states. Then we can try to determine genuine integrable states through analytical or numerical methods.

It would also be valuable to explore how these chiral integrable states might be realized as operators or defects in ABJM theory and to seek their interpretation in the dual string/M-theory. Notably, known integrable boundary states in
AdS_{4}/CFT_{3}
correspondence arising from BPS operators or defects are exclusively achiral. This presents a stark contrast to
{\cal N}=4
SYM theory, where both chiral and achiral integrable states in the
SO(6)
sector emerge in computations of specific correlators. For example, chiral states appear in heavy-heavy-light (HHL) three-point functions
[
12
,
13
]
, while achiral states feature in domain-wall one-point functions
[
4
]
. Understanding the deeper underlying reasons for this difference between the two theories merits further investigation.

Anti-PD-1 plus nab-paclitaxel and bevacizumab for second-line treatment of cancer of unknown primary (Fudan CUP-002): a phase II trial
Authors: Xiaowei Zhang, Ting Zhao, Midie Xu, Yanjing Guo, Qifeng Wang, Yanli Wang, Liangping Zhou, Silong Hu, Qinghua Xu, Xichun Hu, Xin Liu, Zhiguo Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Fudan CUP-002 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04848597), an investigator-initiated prospective trial, was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of an anti-PD-1 antibody in combination with nab-paclitaxel and bevacizumab as a second-line treatment for cancer of unknown primary (CUP). Between June 2, 2021, and April 19, 2024, 48 eligible patients were enrolled. At data cutoff (January 10, 2025), the median follow-up was 27.1 months (95% CI, 20.2 to 37.2). The objective response rate (ORR) was 54.2% (26/48; 95% CI, 40.3% to 67.4%), and the disease control rate (DCR) was 95.8% (46/48; 95% CI, 86.0% to 98.9%). The median progression-free survival (PFS) was 13.1 months (95% CI, 8.0 to 19.6), while the median overall survival (OS) was 25.1 months (95% CI, 14.6 to 29.5). Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) of any grade occurred in 46 patients (95.8%). The exploratory analysis identified systemic eosinophil counts as a prognostic biomarker for treatment response and survival outcomes in second-line setting for CUP. However, current experimental systems are unable to provide the established cell lines or animal models needed to investigate therapeutic mechanisms for CUP. Our present study demonstrated that an anti-PD-1 antibody plus nab-paclitaxel and bevacizumab was effective and well-tolerated in the second-line treatment for patients with CUP.Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04848597

Introduction

Cancer of unknown primary (CUP) is defined as a heterogeneous group of metastatic malignancies for which the primary site remains unidentified despite a standardized diagnostic work-up1. CUP accounts for 2–5% of all malignancies, with a median overall survival (OS) of 3 to 16 months and a 12-month survival rate of 24%2,3. Patients with CUP can be sub-classified into favorable (20%) and unfavorable (80%) subsets with distinct prognosis according to the clinical and histological criteria4. In the past era of empirical chemotherapy, platinum-based regimens were acknowledged as the cornerstone for the first-line treatment of CUP5. Our group conducted the first randomized clinical trial, the Fudan CUP-001 study, which demonstrated that site-specific first-line therapy significantly improved patient outcomes for CUP, resulting in superior progression-free survival (PFS) and favorable OS in comparison with empirical chemotherapy6. However, the improved PFS of site-specific first-line treatment was approximately 5 to 9 months6–8, which still remained under one year. Following disease progression, no evidence-based standard of care exists for the second-line therapy of CUP, highlighting a critical unmet need for innovative therapies.

Since adenocarcinomas account for about 50% of CUP cases, initial chemotherapy regimens were primarily adapted from adenocarcinoma-specific protocols9. In the early 2000 s, gemcitabine-based regimens achieved objective response rates (ORRs) of 7.7–10.0% in the second-line treatment of CUP10,11. Subsequently, capecitabine plus oxaliplatin achieved ORRs of 13.6–18.8% with unsatisfactory improvement of PFS and OS for previously treated CUP12,13. In the later era of molecularly targeted therapy, the simultaneous inhibition of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) with bevacizumab and erlotinib yielded an ORR of 9.8%, failing to demonstrate any clinical benefits compared to chemotherapy14. In fact, VEGF-A positive expression was detected in 26–83% of CUPs, while the expression of EGFR was found to be approximately 75% in CUPs15. However, the simultaneous inhibition of VEGF and EGFR has not resulted in satisfactory outcomes. In recent years, immunotherapy has emerged as the standard treatment for several cancer types, and previous studies revealed that the pretreatment immune activity of CUP was similar to that for immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-responsive malignancies16. Genomic profiling and immunohistochemistry (IHC) revealed PD-L1 positivity in 40.0–48.6% of CUP cases, with 6.7–14.0% demonstrating high expression (TPS ≥ 50%)16–20, supporting the potential use of ICIs in this population. Nivolumab monotherapy, along with its combination with ipilimumab, illustrated improved clinical outcomes, achieving ORRs of 22.2% and 15.9%, respectively21,22. Especially, enhanced efficacy was observed in patients with higher tumor mutation burden (TMB) and in tumors with high-frequency microsatellite instability (MSI-H), with an ORR of 60% in this category of CUP22. In addition, second-line pembrolizumab achieved an ORR of 20.0%, with a median PFS of 4.1 months and median OS of 11.3 months23. In summary, current evidence indicates that chemotherapy alone, targeted monotherapy, and ICIs have limited efficacy in second-line therapy of CUP, underscoring a compelling unmet need for enhanced therapeutic strategies.

Antiangiogenic agents, including tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and antibodies that target the VEGF-VEGFR pathway, contribute to the reversal of immune suppression and vascular normalization in tumors to enhance antitumor responses24. Antiangiogenic-ICI combinations have been clinically verified to enhance the efficacy of ICIs in phase III studies across various cancer types, including cervical cancer, renal cell carcinoma, as well as hepatocellular carcinoma25,26. Furthermore, chemoimmunotherapy plus antiangiogenic agents have outperformed standard therapy in tumors such as cervical cancer27, and metastatic nonsquamous non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)28.

Identifying reliable biomarkers to screen patients most likely to respond to anticancer therapies remains an ongoing unmet medical need. In addition to the established TMB, PD-L1 score, and MSI status20,21, additional signatures such as mutational profiles and baseline circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA) concentration22 were identified as predictors of ICI-based treatment response and survival for patients with CUP who progressed after first-line treatment. Beyond the genetic and epigenetic tumor profiling, spark T cells and tumor-reactive CD8 + T cells have emerged as predictive biomarkers for the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade in human cancers29,30. However, the predictive or prognostic utility of these indicators as biomarkers for the efficacy and clinical benefit of second-line chemoimmunotherapy plus antiangiogenic agents in CUP remains a blank.

We herein conducted this prospective, single-arm phase II study (Fudan CUP-002) by Simon’s two-stage design to evaluate the efficacy and safety of second-line anti-PD-1 (F520) plus nab-paclitaxel and bevacizumab treatment in patients with CUP. We further characterized peripheral blood leukocyte profiles and tumor microenvironment (TME) signatures at baseline to identify prognostic biomarkers of therapeutic response and patient survival outcomes.

Results

Patients

Between June 2, 2021, and April 19, 2024, a total of 51 participants were screened for eligibility, and 48 patients were finally assigned to receive second-line anti-PD-1-based therapy (Fig. 1). As of the data cutoff on January 10, 2025, the median follow-up duration was 27.1 months (95% confidence interval (CI), 20.2 to 37.2). Patients received a median of 8 treatment cycles (range, 1 to 29) at data cutoff. At this time point, 3 participants (6.3%) adhered to the active treatment according to the protocol, while 25 deaths (52.1%) were recorded. In the total population, the median age was 60 years (range, 29 to 72), with 28 cases (58.3%) aged 60 or older and 20 individuals (41.7%) younger than 60. Among the 48 participants, there were 31 males (64.6%) and 17 females (35.4%). Overall, 43 patients (89.6%) with CUP had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of 1. Lymph nodes were the most commonly involved lesions of metastases in CUP, accounting for 85.4% of all instances. In particular, 17 participants (35.4%) presented with nodal involvement exclusively. In this study, 30 patients (62.5%) had involvement of multiple organs, with 6 patients (12.5%) having 4 organs affected. Efficacy

The disease control rate (DCR) was 95.8% (46/48; 95% CI, 86.0% to 98.9%), based on 1 complete response (CR), 25 partial responses (PRs), 20 cases of stable disease (SD), and 2 not evaluable (NE) patients (Fig. 2a). The median PFS was 13.1 months (95% CI, 8.0 to 19.6), with PFS rates at 12 months and 24 months being 54.1% and 27.9%, respectively (Fig. 2b). The median OS was 25.1 months (95% CI, 14.6 to 29.5), with the 12- and 24-month OS rates of 72.5% and 55.7%, respectively (Fig. 2c). The median duration of response (DoR) was 22.5 months (95% CI, 12.5 to not reached), and the 12- and 24-month DoR rates were 78.9% and 47.5%, respectively (Fig. 2d). The swimming plot showed that among the 26 subjects who achieved PR and CR, 5 individuals (19.2%) maintained response for over 2 years (Fig. 2e). Notably, patient 03 that achieved CR had the longest ongoing DoR of 41.6 months, as of the last follow-up date on January 10, 2025.

In the evaluable population, the investigator-assessed confirmed ORR was 58.3% (28/48; 95% CI, 44.3% to 71.3%) and the DCR was 95.8% (46/48; 95% CI, 86.0% to 98.9%). The median PFS was 13.6 months (95% CI, 8.0 to 19.1), the median DoR was 16.5 months (95% CI, 7.5 to not reached), and the median OS was 25.1 months (95% CI, 14.6 to 29.5) (Supplementary Table 1).

Given the resistance to first-line taxane-based therapy, we compared the second-line efficacy between patients who were exposed to first-line taxanes and those who were taxane-naïve. In the overall population, 14 patients received first-line taxane-based treatment, comprising 9 treated with paclitaxel, 4 with nab-paclitaxel, and 1 with liposomal paclitaxel. The overall ORR were 62.5% (20/32; 95% CI, 43.7% to 78.9%) for the taxane-exposed group and 42.9% (6/14; 95% CI, 17.7% to 71.1%) for the taxane-naïve group, respectively. In the subgroup analysis, there was no significant difference in PFS (median 13.1 vs 13.6 months; p = 0.8468) and OS (median 25.1 vs 24.6 months; p = 0.7293) between the first-line taxane-exposed group and the taxane-naïve group (Supplementary Fig. 1a, b).

Safety

The TRAEs (Treatment-related adverse events) of any grade were reported by 46 participants (95.8%), and among these, 32 TRAEs were recorded at a frequency of over 5% in the cohort. Anemia (38, 79.2%), hypertension (24, 50.0%), lymphopenia (20, 41.7%), ALT increased (19, 39.6%), and neutropenia (18, 37.5%) were the most frequently reported TRAEs of any grade in all treated participants. No grade 5 events occurred. Grade 3–4 events were observed in 25 subjects (52.1%), with neutropenia (10, 20.8%) and leukopenia (4, 8.3%) being the most commonly reported AEs. One patient with squamous cell carcinoma of unknown primary experienced 3 concurrent grade ≥3 AEs, including grade 3 stomatitis, grade 3 AST elevation, and grade 4 agranulocytosis. Grade 3–4 immune-related adverse events (irAEs) occurred in 6 cases (12.5%), including pneumonitis (2, 4.2%), diarrhea (1, 2.1%), hypothyroidism (1, 2.1%), hyperthyroidism (1, 2.1%), and hepatitis (1, 2.1%). Bevacizumab-associated AEs were predominantly hypertension (50.0%), aligning with its established safety profile32. Pretreatment eosinophil levels predict benefit from ICI-based treatment in CUP

Given the efficacy of second-line ICI-based treatment, we sought to determine whether baseline peripheral blood leukocyte profiles were associated with clinical outcome. To this end, we quantified baseline leukocyte subsets by routine blood tests. Responders demonstrated significantly higher baseline systemic eosinophil counts compared to non-responders (p = 0.0159; Fig. 3a–f). In contrast, baseline counts of neutrophils, lymphocytes, and monocytes, as well as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), showed no significant differences between the two groups (all p > 0.05). Pretreatment circulating eosinophil counts showed no significant variation when stratified by age (p = 0.7344), gender (p = 0.2759), metastatic burden (p = 0.0975), as well as nodal-only involvement (p = 0.0699) (Supplementary Fig. 2a–d). Indeed, ICI-based treatment induced eosinophil expansion in responders (p = 0.0015) but not in non-responders (p = 0.1014) (Fig. 3g, h). Eosinophils, a type of innate immune cell, are evolutionarily conserved pleiotropic cells that predominantly serve an essential effector function in allergic diseases like asthma. Recently, eosinophils have emerged as unexpected enhancers of ICIs efficacy across multiple malignancies, particularly in breast cancer33, melanoma34, as well as lung cancer35. Given the prognostic significance of circulating eosinophil signatures, we sought to validate and extend these findings by examining tissue-resident eosinophils through Sirius Red staining. We found that patients with CUP who achieved CR or PR showed significantly greater eosinophil infiltration densities compared to those who achieved SD (Fig. 3i–k). We prospectively stratified patients into eosinophilhigh and eosinophillow groups (median cutoff) to assess their prognostic value for ICI-based treatment outcomes, based on baseline circulating eosinophil counts. Stratified analysis revealed superior PFS in eosinophilhigh group compared to the eosinophillow group (median 16.1 vs 8.6 months; p = 0.0496), though no significant OS benefit was observed (median 25.1 vs 20.5 months; p = 0.2927) (Fig. 3l, m). While PFS directly reflects the activity of the study regimen, OS is affected by extraneous factors, particularly the efficacy of subsequent interventions after progression. Tissue-resident eosinophils promote CD8+ T cells recruitment to the TME in CUP

Cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) and natural killer (NK) cells are the principal effectors responsible for direct tumor killing. We performed multiplex immunohistochemistry (mIHC) on pretreatment tumor sections form patients with the aims of characterizing the immune landscape and, specifically, exploring a possible association between the eosinophil expansion and tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) infiltration through quantification of CD4+, CD8+, PD-1+, and FOXP3+ regulatory T (Treg) cells.

Compared to non-responders, the responding tumors exhibited greater infiltration of intratumoral CD8+ T cells (p = 0.0452) and PD-1+ T cells (predominantly CD8+ PD-1+ and CD4+ PD-1+ subsets; p = 0.0251), along with a reduced density of FOXP3+ Treg cells (p = 0.0189). However, no statistically significant difference was observed in the densities of CD4+ T cells between groups (p = 0.0722) (Fig. 4a, b, e–h). Patient 03, diagnosed with squamous cell carcinoma of unknown primary and mediastinal lymph node metastasis, achieved a deep remission with a 78.6% reduction in the sum of target lesion diameter from baseline by 16 cycles of treatment, followed by a complete radiographic response at cycle 26 of treatment (Fig. 4c). Given the prominent infiltration of CD8+ T cells and eosinophils in responding tumors, we further performed IHC and Sirius Red staining to investigate their spatial distribution and potential interactions in CUP. Strikingly, treatment-sensitive tumors exhibited not only elevated overall densities of CD8+ T cells and eosinophils but also a distinct spatial compartmentalization of the TME. We observed discrete regions of dense immune infiltration (“hot” zones), frequently featuring both cell types, directly adjacent to regions with minimal immune cell presence (“cold” zones) within the same tumor section (Fig. 4i, j). Together, these data reveal a coordinated spatial co-localization and concomitant accumulation of tissue-resident eosinophils and CD8+ T cells within the TME, pointing to functional crosstalk between these populations in CUP.

Given the limited sample size (n = 48) and the number of covariates in the primary multivariable model, we conducted supplementary analysis in which each biomarker of interest was evaluated individually, adjusted only for age and sex. In these age- and sex-adjusted models, a lower baseline eosinophil count remained a significant risk factor for disease progression (HR, 2.2; 95% CI, 1.02–4.8; p = 0.043). Furthermore, we confirmed that nodal-only involvement (HR, 0.2; 95% CI, 0.07–0.5; p = 0.001) and involvement of a single organ (HR, 1.5; 95% CI, 1.1–2.1; p = 0.009) were significantly associated with favorable PFS (Supplementary Table 2).

Primary tumor prediction

In the total population, 25 patients underwent the 90-gene expression testing on treatment-naive paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples to predict the primary lesions of their metastases6, including 12 with PR and 13 with SD as confirmed by BICR. Of the 25 participants, breast and colorectal cancers were the two most frequently observed primary tumors in patients with CUP in our present study, each accounting for 20.0% of the participants (Fig. 6a). Specifically, we found that responders were predominantly enriched for predicted tumor types with a more favorable prognosis, such as breast cancer and urothelial cancer. In contrast, non-responders were primarily predicted to have tumor types with poorer prognosis, such as hepatobiliary, pancreatic, as well as gastroesophageal cancer (Fig. 6b). These findings suggest that the 90 tumor-specific gene signatures may not only predict the possible primary tumor origins but also carry prognostic implications. We compared the expression profiles of the 90 tumor-specific genes between groups and identified 8 differentially expressed genes in responders. Discussion

Fudan CUP-002 is a prospective phase II trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of chemoimmunotherapy combined with antiangiogenic agents for second-line therapy in CUP. In the overall population, the triplet regimen demonstrated promising antitumor activity, with a confirmed ORR of 54.2% (95% CI, 40.3% to 67.4%) and DCR of 95.8% (95% CI, 86.1% to 98.9%) as assessed by BICR. In addition, the triplet regimen also documented clinically significant survival benefits, with a median PFS of 13.1 months (95% CI, 8.0 to 19.6) and OS of 25.1 months (95% CI, 14.6 to 29.5). Among the 26 responders who achieved CR and PR, 5 subjects (19.2%) had a DoR exceeding 2 years. In the cohort of 46 evaluable patients treated in the second-line setting, the ORR was lower in patients with prior taxane exposure compared to those without. However, the subsequent efficacy of our second-line treatment, as measured by PFS and OS, was comparable between the two groups. These findings suggest that resistance to first-line taxanes did not prevent CUP patients from experiencing clinical benefit from the subsequent triplet regimen applied in the Fudan CUP-002 study. This is most likely attributable to the normalization of blood vessels induced by antiangiogenic therapy, which enhances the efficacy of immunotherapy in tumors resistant to first-line taxanes. Furthermore, the safety profile of the triplet treatment regimen was consistent with the established toxicity spectra of the individual agents, with TRAEs and irAEs exhibiting a comparable spectrum, incidence, and severity to those reported in previous studies10,14,23. Second-line anti-PD-1 plus nab-paclitaxel and bevacizumab demonstrated effective antitumor activity and was well-tolerated in patients with CUP.

During the chemotherapy-alone era, gemcitabine- or capecitabine-based regimens exhibited limited efficacy, with ORRs of 7.7–18.8%, as well as median PFS of 2.3 to 5.0 months and OS of 3.9 to 9.7 months in previously treated CUP10–13. Later in the era of targeted therapy, dual inhibition of the EGFR and VEGF pathway with bevacizumab plus erlotinib demonstrated limited clinical utility, achieving only a 9.8% ORR with median PFS of 3.9 months and OS of 7.4 months for chemotherapy-treated or treatment-naïve poor-prognosis patients14. In the recent immunotherapy era, PD-1/CTLA-4 blockade has demonstrated modest clinical activity in pretreated CUP, with ORRs of 15.9–22.2%, median PFS of 2.5 to 4.0 months, and OS of 3.8 to 15.9 months across phase II trials21–23. Chemoimmunotherapy in combination with antiangiogenic agents has been acknowledged to improve the first-line PFS and OS in phase III studies across several cancer types, such as the IMpower150 study for metastatic nonsquamous NSCLC28.

Mechanistically, antiangiogenic agents can normalize the aberrant tumor vasculature, which alleviates hypoxia and enhances the infiltration of effector immune cells into the tumor side36. These synergistic mechanisms may explain the great improvements in efficacy observed in the Fudan CUP-002 trial, where the triplet regimen achieved unprecedented survival benefits in a second-line setting for patients with CUP. In addition, all participants in our cohort received only one prior line of systemic therapy and had an ECOG PS score of 0 or 1, whereas those in previous studies received ≥ 1 line of treatment14,21,23. Considering the promising antitumor activity of the anti-PD-1 antibody combined with VEGF blockade and chemotherapy for patients with CUP, along with the encouraging efficacy and safety of the bispecific PD-1 × VEGF antibody (ivonescimab) plus platinum-doublet for patients with advanced solid tumors such as NSCLC and platinum-resistant ovarian cancer37,38, we would like to propose that this bispecific antibody in combination with chemotherapy-doublet may also provide significant therapeutic potential for CUP. In future serial studies, we plan to investigate innovative combinations of bispecific antibodies with chemotherapy regimens, aiming to enhance patient survival and improve outcomes for second- or later-line treatment in patients with CUP.

In exploratory analysis, we identified pretreatment systemic eosinophil counts as an independent prognostic biomarker associated with favorable clinical patient outcomes, including both treatment response and PFS, in patients with CUP receiving this specific ICI-based triplet regimen. Eosinophils, key effector cells of the innate immune system, play established roles in allergic disorders, anti-bacterial immunity, and parasitic infections39. In recent years, eosinophils have been uncovered to enhance response to immune checkpoint blockade in breast cancer33, and melanoma40. Evidence from multiple malignancies, including colorectal41, hepatobiliary42, and esophageal carcinoma43, supports the role of eosinophils as a favorable prognostic factor. Our single-arm study lacks a control group without immunotherapy; therefore, it cannot determine whether peripheral eosinophils are predictive of specific benefit from PD-1 inhibition. Despite this limitation, our present findings provide a strong rationale for evaluating peripheral eosinophils as a candidate predictive biomarker in future randomized trials for CUP. In further analysis, we observed greater density of TILs in tumor sections from responders, particularly CD8+ and PD-1+ T cell subsets, as determined through mIHC. Concurrently, we observed the co-occurrence of eosinophils and CD8+ T cells in tumors both in the responders and non-responders. Taken together, the spatial and quantitative coupling of tissue-resident eosinophils with CD8⁺ T cell infiltration supports the notion of coordinated, as well as potentially interactive, immune activity in patients with CUP. Indeed, accumulating evidence has verified that eosinophils orchestrated CD8+ T cells recruitment across multiple immune contexts, including the antibacterial defense and antitumor immunity35,44–46. From efficient intratumoral trafficking to productive T-cell receptor signaling, the functional activity of CD8+ T cells constitutes a critical determinant of the antitumor immunity, thereby driving improved survival in the second-line setting of CUP. However, due to the biological complexity of CUP, we could not experimentally validate the proposed role of eosinophils in recruiting CD8+ T cells and enhancing antitumor immunity. Despite these limitations, findings in our present study provide clinically actionable evidence to guide therapeutic prioritization in CUP. Recent studies have shown that cromolyn, a widely prescribed allergy medication, enhances the antitumor T cell immunity mediated by antigen-presenting mast cells (apMCs) and overcomes resistance to ICI in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), both in laboratory settings and a phase II clinical study47. Building on these findings, we pursued strategies to enhance eosinophil-dependent CD8+ T cell immunity and sensitize tumors to ICIs for CUP in further laboratory and clinical studies.

However, our study has several limitations. First, as a single-center, single-arm trial with 48 participants, our findings require validation in larger, multi-center cohorts to further address potential biases from sample size constraints and institutional practice patterns. However, the single-center enrollment enabled us to ensure the accuracy of the CUP diagnosis through a rigorous review process, addressing a significant concern often associated with CUP, as highlighted by the CUPISCO multi-center clinical trial48. Second, in contrast, previous studies have reported pro-angiogenic and pro-metastatic activities for tumor-infiltrating eosinophils49,50, and have associated higher baseline eosinophil counts with worse prognosis in specific cancer types, such as mesothelioma51. Consequently, a central unanswered question is whether eosinophils function as consistent anti-tumor agents or if their role is dual in nature, being ultimately dictated by the tumor type or the specific TME. Third, a key limitation is that this single-arm study applied a triplet regimen, which precludes definitive attribution of efficacy or biomarker association to any single component. As efficacy represents a composite effect, our design cannot discern if eosinophils predict benefit from PD-1 inhibition specifically, the chemotherapy/bevacizumab backbone, or their synergy. Validation of its specific predictive role requires future studies with PD-1 inhibitor monotherapy or in randomized trials. Fourth, CUP is a clinical diagnosis that includes metastatic tumors from diverse origins. However, the currently available experimental systems do not provide established cell lines or animal models that are suitable for investigating the mechanisms of therapeutic approaches.

In summary, our study revealed that anti-PD-1 plus nab-paclitaxel and bevacizumab was an effective and well-tolerated second-line regimen for CUP, with a manageable safety profile consistent with the known toxicities of the individual agents. In addition, exploratory analysis identified baseline peripheral eosinophil counts as an independent predictor of enhanced efficacy and superior outcomes with second-line therapy in CUP, providing potential evidence for efficacy surveillance and therapeutic prioritization.

Methods

Study design and participants

The Fudan CUP-002 trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04848597) was designed as a prospective, single-arm, phase II study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of F520 (an anti-PD-1 antibody) plus bevacizumab and nab-paclitaxel as second-line treatment for patients with CUP. Ethical approval was granted by the Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center ethics committee, and this study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki. We applied Simon’s optimal two-stage design to assess ORR as the primary endpoint. Based on previous studies, the ORR of the combination of anti-PD-1 antibody, bevacizumab, and nab-paclitaxel for treating CUP was assumed to be at least 25%10–14,21–23. Assuming an 80% power and a type I error α of 0.05 (two-sided), a sample size of at least 18 patients was required in the first stage. If ≥ 2 patients responded, an additional 25 patients would be recruited in the second stage. Considering a dropout rate of approximately 10%, our target sample size was set at 48 eligible patients. All participants provided written informed consent before treatment, and this trial adhered to all relevant ethical considerations.

Eligible patients were required to be aged 18 to 75 and clinically diagnosed with CUP according to the standard guideline1. All participants had failed first-line treatment. For patients who received prior standard therapy with paclitaxel or docetaxel, there should be a minimum interval of at least 3 months after the last cycle of treatment. Participants were required to have at least one measurable lesion as per the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1, and an ECOG PS of 0 to 2. Additional inclusion criteria should include adequate left ventricular ejection fraction, hematologic, hepatic, and renal functions, and a life expectancy of at least 12 weeks. All participants signed written informed consent prior to treatment.

Patient exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) received prior therapy containing nab-paclitaxel, bevacizumab, or any anti-PD-1 antibody; (2) symptomatic or untreated brain or leptomenigeal metastases; (3) active bleeding and infectious diseases, such as hepatitis and pneumonia; (4) active or a history of autoimmune disease, or ongoing systemic immunosuppressive therapy; (5) a history of major psychiatric or neurological disorders; and (6) a prior history of other malignancies within 5 years, except for in situ cervical cancer or basal cell or squamous cell skin cancer. Full details of the study design and patient selection criteria are provided in the Supplementary Note.

Procedures

Patients received intravenous F520 (anti-PD-1 antibody; 200 mg) plus bevacizumab (7.5 mg/kg) every 3 weeks for up to 2 years, combined with intravenous nab-paclitaxel (125 mg/m²) on days 1 and 8 of each 3-week cycle for up to 8 cycles, or until disease progression, death, unacceptable toxicity, or consent withdrawal. The humanized anti-PD-1 antibody (F520) was diluted in 0.9% sodium chloride to a final concentration of 1–10 mg/mL, and was infused over 30 min.

A protocol-defined dose reduction of 25% was permitted for individuals experiencing nab-paclitaxel-associated grade 3 to 4 toxicities, and treatment would be permanently discontinued if the criteria were not met after two dose reductions. Dose adjustments were not allowed for F520 and bevacizumab; however, treatment interruptions were permitted to manage toxicities. Permanent discontinuation of F520 is mandated for any grade ≥ 3 irAE. Particular attention is warranted for severe toxicities such as hepatitis, pneumonitis, myocarditis, and encephalitis, as well as grade 4 rash or diarrhea, which always necessitate permanent treatment cessation. Bevacizumab treatment was to be permanently discontinued in the event of any of the following: hypertensive crisis, major bleeding, hypertensive encephalopathy, nephrotic syndrome, arterial thromboembolic event, or gastrointestinal perforation. AEs were graded according to the National Cancer Institute’s Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) version 5.0.

Outcomes

The primary efficacy endpoint was confirmed ORR (patients with CR or PR), confirmed by repeat consecutive assessment ≥ 4 weeks) by BICR as per RECIST version 1.1. Contrast-enhanced CT and/or MRI were performed at baseline and subsequently every 6 weeks. The secondary endpoints included median PFS (time from enrollment to disease progression or death due to any cause, whichever occurred first), median OS (time from enrollment to death or last follow-up), median DoR (time from tumor response to disease progression), and DCR (patients with CR, PR, or SD recorded ≥ 4 weeks) confirmed by BICR, ORR determined by investigator, and safety in all treated individuals. Additional exploratory analysis assessed baseline blood leukocyte profiles and TME landscapes to identify biomarkers associated with treatment response and survival outcomes in patients with CUP.

Multiplex immunohistochemistry staining

Multiplex immunohistochemistry (mIHC) was performed on FFPE tumor samples from representative subjects in both the response and non-response groups, following the manufacturer’s instructions. Specimens of 4 µm thickness were incubated with CD4 (Cat: ab133616, Abcam), CD8 (Cat: BX50036, Biolynx), PD-1 (Cat: 10377-MM23, SinoBiological), FoxP3 (Cat: MAB8214, R&D Systems), and EpCAM antibodies (Cat: 14452S, Cell Signaling Technology) to further characterize differences in the TME between responders and non-responders. Nuclei are counterstained with DAPI (Cat: C1005, Beyotime). Whole tissue sections were scanned through the Pannoramic MIDI II Digital Scanner (3DHISTECH Ltd., Budapest, Hungary). Fluorescence images were analyzed using APTime software (3D Medicines Inc., Shanghai, China).

Eosinophil staining

Tumor tissue sections were fixed, processed, and stained for eosinophils using the Sirius Red-based Eosinophil Staining Kit (Cat: G3632, Solarbio), with all procedures performed in adherence to the manufacturer’s protocol. Tissue sections were scanned using K-Viewer (1.0.5) (KFBIO, Zhejiang, China).

Primary origins prediction

Needle biopsy or surgically resected samples were further analyzed for primary tumor prediction by the 90-gene expression assay using a PCR platform (Canhelp Genomics, Hangzhou, China). The tumor origin of CUP was inferred based on a similarity score, which was generated by comparing the expression profiles of 90 tumor-specific genes in the FFPE sections with those of cancer types in the reference database, as detailed in our previous study6.

Statistical analysis

Clopper-Pearson method based on the binomial distribution was used to estimate the ORR and DCR with 95% CIs. PFS, OS, and DoR were estimated using Kaplan-Meier analysis with two-sided 95% CIs calculated for the median, and the log-rank test was applied to compare Kaplan-Meier curves between the eosinophilhigh and eosinophillow groups, which were dichotomized by the median baseline peripheral eosinophil count. To identify baseline risk factors for unfavorable patient outcomes, we performed multivariate Cox proportional hazards analysis. HRs with Wald-test p values and 95% CIs were reported. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Continuous variables were expressed as median (range). Comparisons between two groups for continuous variables were performed using the Student’s t-test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for normally and non-normally distributed data, respectively. Safety was assessed in the total population according to the NCI-CTCAE version 5.0. All statistical analysis were performed using R software version 4.4.2 and GraphPad Prism version 9.0.0. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The data cutoff date was January 10, 2025.

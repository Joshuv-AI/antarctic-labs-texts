Upper bounds in non-autonomous quantum dynamics
Jingxuan Zhang

Abstract
. We prove upper bounds on outside probabilities for generic non-autonomous Schrödinger operators on lattices of arbitrary dimension. Our approach is based on a combination of commutator method originated in scattering theory and novel monotonicity estimate for certain mollified asymptotic observables that track the spacetime localization of evolving states. Sub-ballistic upper bounds are obtained, assuming that momentum vanishes sufficiently fast in the front of the wavepackets. A special case gives a refinement of the general ballistic upper bound of Radin-Simon’s, showing that the evolution of wavepackets are effectively confined to a strictly linear light cone with explicitly bounded slope. All results apply to long-range Hamiltonian with polynomial decaying off-diagonal terms and can be extended, via a frozen-coefficient argument, to generic nonlinear Schrödinger equations on lattices.

1.
Introduction

We consider the following non-autonomous Schödinger equation on
\mathfrak{h}:=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),\,d\geq 1
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u=(H_{0}+V(t))u.

Here
H_{0}
is a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\mathfrak{h}
and
V(t)
is a time-dependent potential.
Assuming only that
V(t)
is uniformly bounded in
\ell^{\infty}
so that (
1.1
) is globally well-posed on
\mathfrak{h}
, we seek upper bound on the outside probabilities of wavepackets evolving according to (
1.1
) without additional structural on the potential. To avoid distraction, in what follows we always normalized the
\ell^{2}
-norm of solutions to (
1.1
) to one.

The lack of control on
V(t)
suggests the use of commutators in which the potential does not contribute. A natural candidate is the momentum operator

A=i[H_{0},\left\lvert x\right\rvert],

first defined as a quadratic form on the natural domain
\mathcal{D}:=\left\{u\in\mathfrak{h}:\left\lVert\left\lvert x\right\rvert u\right\rVert<\infty\right\}
.
Clearly, the choice of
V(t)
has no bearing on
A
.
Suppose the kernel of
H_{0}
satisfies suitable decay condition so that
A
extends to a bounded operator on all of
\mathfrak{h}
. Then, at the level of expectation, the classical argument of Radin-Simon
[
30
]
yields a general ballistic upper bound:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left\lvert x\right\rvert u_{t}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\left\lvert x\right\rvert u_{0}\right\rVert+Ct,\quad t>0.

See also
[
4
]
*Appd. A.2 for extensions to higher moments.
With no additional structural assumption on
V(t)
, estimate (
1.2
) is the only constraint that we are aware of on the dynamical spreading of wavepackets evolving according to such a general equation as (
1.1
).

The Radin-Simon upper bound implies, via Markov’s inequality, that at the level of probability distribution, solutions to (
1.1
) with well-localized initial state must decay in the region
\left\{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\geq Ct^{\alpha}\right\}
for any
\alpha>1
. We seek to further quantify the outside probabilities for solutions
u_{t},\,t\geq 0
to (
1.1
), defined as

(1.3)

\displaystyle P(N,t):=\sum_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert>N}\left\lvert u_{t}(x)\right\rvert^{2}.

For autonomous Schrödinger operator
H=H_{0}+V
, a natural first step is to invoke resolvent estimate together with explicit integral representation of the propagator
e^{-itH}
. Indeed, for the solution to (
1.1
) generated by an initial state
u_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}
, the outside probabilities
P(N,t)
can be conveniently written in terms of the resolvent
R(z):=(z-H)^{-1}
via Dunford functional calculus as

(1.4)

\displaystyle P(N,t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\supp u_{0}}\sum_{\left\lvert y\right\rvert>N}\left\lvert u_{0}(x)\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\left\langle e^{-itH}\delta_{x},\,\delta_{y}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2},

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left\langle e^{-itH}\delta_{x},\,\delta_{y}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}e^{-itz}{\left\langle R(z)\delta_{x},\delta_{y}\right\rangle}\,dz.

Here
\Gamma
is any positively oriented contour around the spectrum
\sigma(H)
.
Through the integral representation (
1.5
), upper bounds on
P(N,t)
are naturally tied to resolvent estimates for
R(z)
, whence spectral properties of the Hamiltonian enter. This is the starting point for many fruitful research on dynamical upper bounds for Schrödinger operator, see for instance the general approach of Damanik–Tcheremchantsev
[
11
,
10
,
20
]
via transfer matrix on
\mathbb{Z}
and, more recently, Shamis-Sodin
[
31
]
for upper bounds in higher dimensions; see also
[
43
,
19
]
.
An excellent review on the state of the art can be found in Damanik-Malinovitch-Young
[
9
]
.

Along this route, a key ingredient is the Combes–Thomas estimate for the resolvent.
We recall the following formulation due to Aizenmann
[
1
]
*Lem. II.1:

Lemma 1.1

(Combes–Thomas estimate)
.

Take
H=H_{0}+V
with
\left\lvert H_{0}(x,y)\right\rvert\leq B\exp(-m\delta(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert))
, where
m,\,B>0
and
\delta(\cdot)
satisfies, for some
\alpha,S>0
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{d-1}e^{-(m-\alpha)\delta(r)}\,dr\leq S.

Then for any
b>1
, there exists
v=v(\alpha,B,S,b)>0
s.th.

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\langle R(z)\delta_{x},\delta_{y}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq 2e^{-\delta(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)/v},\quad 1\leq\dist(z,\sigma(H))<b.

To motivate our main results below, we recall how the Thomas-Combes is used to show that outside probabilities are exponentially suppressed away from a strictly linear effective light cone.
Indeed, choosing the contour in (
1.5
) to be the boundary of the box
\left\{\left\lvert\mathrm{Im}z\right\rvert\leq 1,\left\lvert\mathrm{Re}z\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert H\right\rVert+1\right\}
, we conclude from (
1.7
) that
for some
C=C(\left\lVert H\right\rVert)>0
and
v=v(\alpha,B,S,\left\lVert H\right\rVert)>0
,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\langle e^{-itH}\delta_{x},\,\delta_{y}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq Ce^{t-\delta(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert)/v}.

Plugging (
1.8
) back to (
1.4
) and setting
R:=\sup\left\{\left\lvert x\right\rvert:x\in\supp u_{0}\right\}
, we find that

(1.9)

P(N,t)\leq Ce^{-N}
for
\delta(N-R)>2vt
.

If
u_{0}
has compact support and
\delta(r)=r
, then (
1.9
) yields exponential decay away from the effective light cone
\left\{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq R+2vt\right\}
. However, limitations of this approach based on resolvent estimate arise when

•

u_{0}
does not have compact support: In this case
R=\infty
in (
1.9
).

•

H_{0}
has polynomially decaying off-diagonal entries, i.e.
\delta(r)=\log(1+r)
in (
1.6
): In this case (
1.8
) decays only when
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert=O(e^{t})
.

•

The potential is time-dependent: In this case the propagator cannot be written as (
1.5
).

It is not obvious how to extend the existent method to obtain upper bounds on the outside probabilities in these settings. The present paper aims to supply some new approaches to cover parts of this gap.

Notation

We denote by
{\bf 1}_{X}
the characteristic functions associated to a set
X
,
\dist_{X}(x):=\inf_{y\in X}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert
the distance function to
X
,
\mathcal{D}(A)
the domain of an operator
A
,
B_{a}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq a\right\}
the ball of radius
a
around the origin, and

\|\cdot\|
the norm of operators on
\mathfrak{h}
and sometimes that of vectors in
\mathfrak{h}
.
We make no distinction in our notation between a function
f\in\mathfrak{h}
and the associated multiplication operator
\psi(x)\mapsto f(x)\psi(x)
on
\mathfrak{h}
.
The commutator
[A,B]
of two operators
A
and
B
is first defined as a quadratic form on
\mathcal{D}(A)\cap\mathcal{D}(B)
(always assumed to be dense in
\mathfrak{h}
) and then extended to an operator. Similarly, the multiple commutators of
A
and
B
are defined recursively by
\Ad^{0}_{B}(A)=A
and
\Ad^{p}_{B}(A)=[\Ad^{p-1}_{B}(A),B]
for
p=1,2,\ldots
.

1.1.
Results

In the remainder of this paper we consider generic non-autonomous Schrödinger operators
H(t)=H_{0}+V(t)
, where
H_{0}
is long-range self-adjoint operator with power-law decay, satisfying, for some integer
n\geq 1
,

(1.10)

\displaystyle M:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left\lvert H_{0}(x,y)\right\rvert\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{n+1}<\infty.

This condition shows that momentum operator
A=i[H_{0},\left\lvert x\right\rvert]
is bounded. Indeed, set

(1.11)

\displaystyle\kappa:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left\lvert H_{0}(x,y)\right\rvert\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert.

By the reveres triangle inequality and Schur’s test, we have
\left\lVert Au\right\rVert\leq\kappa\left\lVert u\right\rVert
for
u\in\mathcal{D}(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)
. Since
\kappa\leq M
, it follows that
A
extends to a bounded operator on
\mathfrak{h}
under condition (
1.10
). In particular, the Radin-Simon bound (
1.2
) holds. Consequently, by Markov’s inequality, the outside probability decays for
t\to\infty
and
N\geq Ct^{\alpha},\,\alpha>1
.

Our first result provides an upper bound on
P(N,t)
with
N\geq Ct^{\alpha}
and
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha\leq 1
. The condition is, roughly speaking, that the momentum is vanishing sufficiently fast in the spacetime region corresponding to the ‘propagation front’,
\left\{(x,t):\left\lvert x\right\rvert\sim t^{\alpha},t>0\right\}
.
More precisely, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let (
1.10
) hold with
n\geq 1
. Let
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha\leq 1
,
\delta>0
,
v>\frac{\delta}{\alpha}
, and
\bar{v}=v-\frac{\delta}{2\alpha}
.
Let
W\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a smooth cutoff function with
\supp W\subset(0,1)
and
W\equiv 1
on
(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})
. Set
W_{t}(\phi)=W(\frac{2\alpha}{\delta t^{\alpha}}(\phi-\bar{v}t^{\alpha}))
.

Assume
u_{t},\,t\geq 0
is a solution to (
1.1
) satisfying, for some
R\geq 0
,

(1.12)

\displaystyle\left\lVert i[H,\dist_{B_{R}}]W_{t}(\dist_{B_{R}})u_{t}\right\rVert\leq(v\alpha-\delta)t^{\alpha-1}\left\lVert W_{t}(\dist_{B_{R}})u_{t}\right\rVert,\quad t>0.

Then there exists
C=C(n,M,\delta,\alpha,W)>0
s.th. for all
t>0
,

(1.13)

\displaystyle P((vt)^{\alpha}+R,t)\leq(1+Ct^{-\gamma}){P(R,0)}+C{t}^{-\beta},\quad\gamma:=2\alpha-1,\ \beta:=\min\Bigl((n+1)\gamma,(n+1)\alpha-1\Bigr).

This theorem is proved in Section
2
.

Remark 1
.

Condition (
1.10
) holds uniformly for all
n\geq 1
for operators
H_{0}
with finite interaction range, e.g. the discrete Laplacian

[\Delta u](x)=\sum_{y\sim x}u(y),

as well as operators with exponentially decaying kernel, i.e.

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left\lvert H_{0}(x,y)\right\rvert e^{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert}<\infty.

Alternatively, condition (
1.10
) can be characterized by the norm-differentiability of the operator
e^{-i\xi\cdot x}H_{0}e^{i\xi\cdot x}
for
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, c.f.
[
6
,
36
]
.

Remark 2
.

For fixed
\alpha
,
n
, and cutoff function
W
, the prefactor
C\to\infty
in (
1.13
) as either
M\to\infty
or
\delta\to 0+
. If
\delta
is not too small (
\delta\geq 2\alpha
will do), then the prefactor can be made independent of
\delta
. The precise dependence on the parameters can be tracked down from (
2.42
) below.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on commutator estimates, in which the potential
V(t)
in (
1.1
) does not contribute to begin with. Consequently, our main estimate (
1.13
) is independent of the potential. The full time-dependent Hamiltonian enters only through condition (
1.12
) via a given solution. To better understand this, we note that a necessary condition for (
1.12
) is

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\lVert i[H,\dist_{B_{R}}]W_{t}(\dist_{B_{R}})u_{t}\right\rVert=0.

Simple geometric consideration shows that
W_{t}(\dist_{R})
amounts to a cutoff function associated to the spacetime region

F_{t}:=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}:(\tfrac{1}{4}v+\tfrac{3}{4}\bar{v})t^{\alpha}+R\leq\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq(\tfrac{3}{4}v+\tfrac{1}{4}\bar{v})t^{\alpha}+R\right\}.

Thus (
1.12
) requires, in particular, that the region
F_{t}
cannot contain any free wave. Put differently, we need the dynamics to push the solution to zero frequency as
t\to\infty
in the region
F_{t}
.

It is in general a difficult to determine the origin of decay for (
1.12
). This is not unexpected if one views (
1.1
) as a frozen-coefficient equation arising from nonlinear Schrödinger equations, as pointed out in recent works of Liu-Soffer and Soffer-Wu
[
27
,
39
,
40
]
.
Determining further sufficient condition for (
1.12
) in terms of the initial state and the potential
V(t)
is important for applications of Theorem
1.1
to concrete Hamiltonians. This is of independent interest in itself and so we will pursue it elsewhere.

In the meantime, ballistic upper bounds (i.e.,
\alpha=1
) on outside probabilities can be derived from Theorem
1.2
independent of the solutions. Indeed, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let (
1.10
) hold with
n\geq 1
. Then for any
v>\kappa
and
\delta:=v-\kappa
,
there exists
C=C(n,M,\delta)>0
s.th. for any solution to (
1.1
),

(1.14)

P(vt+R,t)\leq(1+Ct^{-1}){P(R,0)}+C{t}^{-n},\quad R\geq 0,t>0.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
assuming Theorem
1.1
.

We apply Theorem
1.1
with
\alpha=1
. In this case, condition (
1.12
) is time-independent and holds for any solution
u_{t}
to (
1.1
), owning to the operator norm bound
v-\delta=\kappa\geq\left\lVert i[H,\dist_{B_{R}}]\right\rVert
. (The second inequality is a consequence of Schur’s test; see Lemma
A.1
.)
Since
\gamma=1
and
\beta=n
for
\alpha=1
, the desired estimate (
1.14
) follows from (
1.13
).
∎

We now turn to illustrate some simple applications of the theorems above.

First, we note that if
H_{0}
satisfies the decay condition (
1.10
)
uniformly for all
n\geq 1
, (c.f. Remark
1
), then applying Theorem
1.1
successively with
n=1,2,\ldots
shows that the decay of wavepackets away from the propagation region
\left\{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq R+vt^{\alpha}\right\}
is faster than polynomial of any degree.
More precisely, following
[
20
,
11
,
10
]
we define the decay parameter

(1.15)

\displaystyle S^{+}(\alpha):=-\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log P(t^{\alpha}-1,t)}{\log t},

and the upper transport exponent

(1.16)

\displaystyle\alpha_{u}^{+}:=\sup\left\{\alpha:S^{+}(\alpha)<\infty\right\}.

Then it follows directly from Theorem
1.1
that

Corollary 1.1
.

If (
1.10
) holds uniformly for all
n\geq 1
, then for any solution satisfying (
1.12
) with
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha\leq 1
, we have

(1.17)

\displaystyle\alpha_{u}^{+}\leq\alpha.

Next, we prove an upper bound on higher moments of the position operator away from the propagation region. For
r\geq 0
, let

(1.18)

\displaystyle P_{r}(N,t):=\sum_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert>N}\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{r}\left\lvert u(x,t)\right\rvert^{2}.

Then
P(N,t)\equiv P_{0}(N,t)
. In general,
P_{r},\,r>0
is naturally related to the Sobolev/diffusive norms; see e.g.
[
3
,
43
]
.
We prove the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume (
1.14
) holds with
n>dr_{0}/2
for some
r_{0}>0
, and assume
u_{t},\,t\geq 0
solves (
1.1
) with initial state satisfying

(1.19)

\displaystyle P_{r_{0}}(0,0)\leq B.

Then for any
0\leq r<r_{0}
, there exists
C=C(n,M,B,d,v,r,r_{0})>0
such that for all
t\geq 1
,

(1.20)

\displaystyle P_{r}(2vt,t)\leq C.

This theorem is proved in Section
3
.

Remark 3
.

The generalized Radin-Simon estimate (see e.g.
[
4
]
*Thm. A.2) asserts that for initial state satisfying
P_{r}(0,0)\leq C
, the full Sobolev norm
P_{r}(0,t)
typically grows polynomially as
O(t^{r})
. In view of this, estimate (
1.20
) shows that the growth contribution away from a linearly spreading region is at most
O(1)
.

Remark 4
.

Conversely, if (
1.20
) holds, then by Markov’s inequality we have
P(2vt,t)\leq Ct^{-r}
, c.f. (
1.14
).

Remark 5
.

For compactly supported initial state, condition (
1.19
) holds for all
r_{0}>0
. Thus estimate (
1.20
) holds for all
r
if
H_{0}
is as in Remark
1
and
u(0)
has compact support. This is the setting for many results in the literature where
H_{0}
is the discrete Laplacian and
u_{0}
is the point mass at the origin.

Lastly, we note that similar to the Radin-Simon ballistic upper bounds, the potential
V(t)
does not enter our estimates.
Owning to this generality, by a standard frozen-coefficient argument, our results above extend to generic non-linear, non-autonomous Schrödinger equations of the form

(1.21)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}q=\Bigl(H_{0}+\bar{V}(t)+\left\lvert N(t,q)\right\rvert\Bigr)q.

To avoid distraction, for our purpose we
assume
global well-posedness for (
1.21
) on
\mathfrak{h}
. Then we have the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
H_{0}
satisfy (
1.10
), potential
\bar{V}(t)
be uniformly bounded, and nonlinearity obey
\left\lvert N(t,q)\right\rvert\leq C_{1}
for
t\geq 0,\,\left\lvert q\right\rvert\leq C_{2}
.
Then the statements of Thms.
1.1
–
1.3
are valid for solutions to (
1.21
).

This theorem is proved in Section
4
.
The conditions on
V(t)
and
N(t,q)
can be further relaxed, so long as (
1.21
) and the frozen-coefficient equation (see (
4.1
) below) are globally well-posed on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Finally, we note that all results above can be extended to general geometry, with the outside probabiliries defined as
P_{X}(N,t)=\sum_{\dist_{X}(x)>N}\left\lvert u_{t}(x)\right\rvert^{2}
for any
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, with the corresponding change made in (
2.9
).

1.2.
Discussion

To the author’s knowledge, the results presented in the previous subsection are the first general upper bounds on outside probabilities for non-autonomous Schrödinger operators
H=H_{0}+V(t)
with no special structural assumption on
V(t)
. This is the main novelty of the present paper.

Our original motivation for considering (
1.1
) without assuming structural conditions on
V(t)
is for applications to nonlinear Schrödinger equation of the form (
1.21
). Indeed, according to Theorem
1.4
, particle transport is confined to strictly linear light cones for generic non-autonomous, nonlinear Schrödinger operator. If we view (
1.21
) as the mean-field limit of some quantum many-body dynamical system defined on
bosonic
Fock space over
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, then this fact is in contrast to the behaviour of the full many-body system.
Indeed, for bosonic many-body Hamiltonian, without imposing special localization condition on the initial state, the dynamical spreading rate of particle transport is in general growing logarithmically in
t
.
For the Bose-Hubbard model with nearest neighbour interaction, recent work of Kuwahara-Vu-Saito
[
23
]
shows particle transport spreads at the rate
t\log t
; see Result 1 therein. For more general long-range bosonic many-body Hamiltonian with hopping matrix satisfying (
1.10
), together with M. Lemm and C. Rubiliani, we proved in
[
25
]
that for initial state with uniformly bounded particle density, the particle transport spreads at strictly linear rate; see also Faupin-Lemm-Sigal
[
13
,
14
]
and our earlier work with Sigal
[
37
,
24
]
. We also note that explicit example of super ballistic communication (i.e., propagation of general quantum information instead of particle transport) is known; see e.g. Eisert-Gross
[
12
]
. From this perspective, the general upper bound from Theorem
1.2
is similar to the classical Lieb-Robinson bound for spin systems
[
26
]
(see also extensions by Hastings-Koma
[
21
]
, Nachtergaele-Sims
[
28
]
and the review
[
29
]
), in the sense that it establishes general a priori (i.e., state-independent) constraint on many-body quantum dynamics at the mean-field level.

Various types of localization behaviour for discrete nonlinear Schrödinger equations, especially in the presence of potential with a large degree of randomness, have been investigated since the seminal works of Frölich-Spencer-Wayne
[
18
]
; see also
[
15
,
16
,
41
,
8
]
. More recently long-time Anderson localization is proved for deterministic nonlinear Schödinger equation assuming certain quasi-periodicity condition on the potential and weak nonlinearity
[
7
]
. An excellent review can be found in
[
17
]
. In all of these works, the dynamical upper bounds are tied to the level of disorderedness and the strength of nonlinearity in question.

Finally, we note that similar conditions as (
1.12
) were identified in recent works of Liu-Soffer, Soffer-Wu, and Soffer-Stewart
[
27
,
39
,
40
,
38
]
for what the authors call ‘weakly localized states’ in the context of nonlinear Schödinger equations in the continuum, and diffusive bounds such as
\left\langle\left\lvert x\right\rvert\right\rangle_{t}\leq Ct^{-1/2}
were established for such states.

1.3.
Approach

Our approach for proving the main result, Theorem
1.1
, originates from the commutator method developed in the classical works of Sigal-Soffer in
N
-body scattering theory
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
and the propagation observable method from recent works of Faupin-Lemm-Sigal for particle transport problems in condensed matter theory
[
13
,
14
]
. It is also a refinement of the abstract approach developed in
[
42
]
.

We work in the Heisenberg picture and study the Heisenberg evolution,
\alpha_{t}
, for time-dependent families of bounded operators
A(t)
, characterized by the duality relation

(1.22)

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{0},\,\alpha_{t}(A(t))u_{0}\right\rangle=\left\langle u_{t},\,A(t)u_{t}\right\rangle,

where
u_{t},\,t\geq 0
is the unique global solution to the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) with
u_{t}|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
.
The central idea in our approach is to consider the Heisenberg evolution of the characteristic functions
{\bf 1}_{E_{t}}
associated to the outside region

E_{t}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\left\lvert x\right\rvert\geq vt^{\alpha}+R\right\}.

Our goal is to show the evolution of the time-dependent observables
\alpha_{t}({\bf 1}_{E_{t}})
is upper bounded (as an operator) by its initial state
{\bf 1}_{E_{0}}
, up to an
O(t^{-n})
-remainder. Once this is established, estimate (
1.13
) follows by evaluating such an operator inequality at the initial state via the duality relation (
1.22
).

Whereas directly estimating the evolution of
{\bf 1}_{E_{t}}
could be difficult, we seek suitable propagation observables
A(t)
obeying the desired monotone envelope while being comparable to
{\bf 1}_{E_{t}}
. More precisely, we will construct

such that

•

\alpha_{t}(A(t))
is constrained by a time-decaying envelope, with
\alpha(A(t))\leq C(A(0)+{t}^{-\beta}),\,\beta>0

•

Combining these and using that

is positivity-preserving, we arrive at the desired operator inequality

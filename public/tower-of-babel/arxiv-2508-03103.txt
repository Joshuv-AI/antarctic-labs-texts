On the Perversity of Chern Classes for Compactified Jacobians
Soumik Ghosh

Abstract
. We prove some perversity bounds for the Chern classes of a compactified Jacobian fibration, namely the k k -th Chern class of the compactified Jacobian has perversity ≤ k \leq k . Our results are motivic in nature and we also prove a filtration version of a conjecture raised in [ BMSY24 ] .



0.
Introduction

Throughout, we work with varieties/schemes over the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

0.1.
Overview

If
C
is an integral, projective curve, the Jacobian
J:=\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C)
is not necessarily proper, but it admits a natural compactification
\bar{J}\supset J
as in
[
AK76
]
,
[
AIK77
]
. By definition,
\bar{J}
is the moduli space of torsion-free sheaves
F
on such that
F
has generic rank one and
\chi(F)=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C})
. Now let
B
be a non-singular variety and
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow B
be a flat family of integral projective curves with a section. We assume that each fiber is an integral curve with arithmetic genus
g
and at worst planar singularities. Then, one can consider the relative compactified jacobian fibration
\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}\xrightarrow{\pi}B
. We further assume
\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}
is non-singular and that
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow B
admits a section whose image is contained in the smooth locus of the fibers.
Studying the interaction between natural cohomology classes and the perverse filtration has been an important topic in recent years; for example the
P=W
conjecture in non-abelian Hodge theory has been proved by reducing to the interaction between tautological classes and the perverse filtration for the Hitchin system as in
[
MS24
]
,
[
HMMS25
]
,
[
MSY25
]
. For Lagrangian fibrations, the interaction between the Chern classes of the tangent bundle and the (motivic) perverse filtration is closely related to the Beauville-Voisin conjectures, as explained in
[
BMSY24
]
. The purpose of this paper is to study systematically the interaction between Chern classes of the tangent bundle and perverse filtrations for compactified Jacobian filtrations, both cohomologically and motivically.
In particular, a natural question is to give bounds for the perversity of the Chern classes
c_{k}(T_{\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}})
both at cohomological and motivic level.

In Section
1.1
, we review the notion of a motivic perverse filtration as in
[
MSY25
]
and define a natural family of motivic perverse filtrations
\left\{P^{(\alpha)}_{k}\mathfrak{h}(\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}})\right\}_{k}
indexed by
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
. The
\alpha
in the motivic perverse filtration occurs because of the flexibility of the Todd convention that arises due to Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch; indeed, for instance, in
[
MSY25
]
, the authors worked with the case
\alpha=0
while in
[
BMSY24
]
, the authors worked with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
. Each motivic perverse filtration
\left\{P^{(\alpha)}_{k}\mathfrak{h}(\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}})\right\}_{k}
specializes to a perverse filtration
\left\{P^{(\alpha)}_{k}A_{\bullet}(\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}})\right\}_{k}
on the Chow groups with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients and the usual perverse filtration on the singular cohomology
H^{\bullet}(\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathbb{Q})
. In Section
3.2
, we show that the apriori different perverse filtrations for different
\alpha
’s are the same on Chow groups (see Proposition
3.2
), so the Chow- theoretic perverse filtration is intrinsic in some sense, which we denote by
\left\{P_{k}A^{\bullet}(\bar{J})\right\}_{k}
. Regarding the perversity of the Chern classes
c_{k}(T_{\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}})
, one has the trivial bound
c_{k}(T_{\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}})\in P_{2k}H^{k}(\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathbb{Q})
. For a general proper morphism between varieties over
\mathbb{C}
say
X\xrightarrow{f}B
, given
\mathfrak{z}\in H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})
, in general it is challenging to prove optimal non-trivial perversity bounds for
\mathfrak{z}
following the usual definitions and methods as described in
[
dC17
]
or
[
dCM09
]
. In this paper, we give optimal bounds for the Chern classes for a compactified Jacobian fibration using the interactions between Fourier-Mukai transforms and the perverse filtration as in
[
MSY25
]
and
[
BMSY24
]
.

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}\xrightarrow{\pi}B
be as above. Then

\begin{split}&c_{k}(T_{\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}})\in P_{k}A^{k}(\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}})=A^{k}(P^{(\alpha)}_{k}\mathfrak{h}(\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}})),\\
&c_{k}(T_{\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}})\in P_{k}H^{2k}(\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathbb{Q})\end{split}

where the equality in the first statement follows from Proposition
3.2
.

Since the perverse filtrations
\left\{P_{k}A^{\bullet}\right\}_{k}
and
\left\{P_{k}H^{\bullet}\right\}_{k}
are multiplicative with respect to the cup-product (see Section
3.3
), Theorem
0.1
also locates any monomials of the Chern classes in the perverse filtration, namely

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{s}c_{k_{i}}(T_{\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}})\in P_{\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}}A^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}}(\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}),

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{s}c_{k_{i}}(T_{\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}})\in P_{\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}}H^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}2k_{i}}(\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathbb{Q}).

We briefly explain how this relates to some conjectures in
[
BMSY24
]
. We state the conjectures in the language of relative Chow motives of Corti-Hanamura; see Section
1.1
for the meanings of the notations.

Conjecture 0.2
.

[
[
BMSY24
]
Conjecture

\bar{J}_{\mathcal{C}}\xrightarrow{\pi}B

To simplify notation, we shall henceforth denote

Outline of the Paper

In Section
1.1
, we review the relative Chow motives of Corti-Hanamura, motivic perverse filtration and construct the family of motivic perverse filtrations, mentioned in Section
0.1
. In Section
2
, we recall the existence and properties of a Poincare sheaf on
\bar{J}\times_{B}\bar{J}
, as constructed by Arinkin in
[
Ari13
]
and recall the notion of a Fourier transform. We also prove some co-dimension estimates. In Sections
3.1
and
3.2
, we use the co-dimension estimates of Section
2.2
to prove various vanishing relations, namely a twisted Fourier vanishing and construct a family of motivic perverse filtrations. We further show that at the level of Chow groups they induce the same filtration. In Section
3.3
we show that the motivic perverse filtrations as constructed are multiplicative. In Section
4.1
, we prove more vanishing relations involving Fourier transforms of the Chern classes and use the relations to prove the perversity bounds in Section
4.2
. Finally, in Section
4.3
, we give examples to show that the bound we get is optimal.

Relative Chow Motives and Perverse Filtrations

We work with the relative Chow motives of Corti-Hanamura, see
[

]

. All Chow groups
A_{\bullet}
are taken with

-coefficients.

Let
f_{1}:X_{1}\rightarrow B
and

be two proper morphisms with
X_{1},X_{2}
non-singular. If
X_{2}
is equi-dimensional, we define the group of degree

relative
correspondences between

and

to be

\operatorname{Corr}^{k}_{B}(X_{1},X_{2}):=A_{\dim X_{2}-k}(X_{1}\times_{B}X_{2})

If
X_{2}
is not equi-dimensional we decompose
X_{2}
into connected components and define the groups accordingly. If
\Gamma\in\operatorname{Corr}^{k}_{B}(X_{1},X_{2}),\ \Gamma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Corr}^{k^{\prime}}_{B}(X_{2},X_{3})
, we define their composition by

(1)

\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma:={p_{13}}_{*}\delta_{X_{2}}^{!}(\Gamma\times\Gamma^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Corr}^{k+k^{\prime}}_{B}(X_{1},X_{3})

where
p_{13}
is the projection onto the first and third factors
X_{1}\times_{B}X_{2}\times_{B}X_{3}\xrightarrow{p_{13}}X_{1}\times_{B}X_{3}
and

\delta^{!}_{X_{2}}:A_{\bullet}(X_{1}\times_{B}X_{2}\times X_{2}\times_{B}X_{3})\rightarrow A_{\bullet-\dim X_{2}}(X_{1}\times_{B}X_{2}\times_{B}X_{3})

is the refined Gysin homomorphism with respect to the regular embedding
\Delta_{X_{2}}:X_{2}\rightarrow X_{2}\times X_{2}
as defined in
[
Ful98
]
.

We also define a Chow-theoretic convolution. Say
\Gamma_{i}\in\operatorname{Corr}^{k_{i}}_{B}(Y_{i},X_{i}):=A_{\dim X_{i}-k_{i}}(Y_{i}\times_{B}X_{i}),\ i=1,2
and
\Gamma\in\operatorname{Corr}^{l}_{B}(X_{1},X_{2};X_{3}):=A_{\dim X_{3}-l}(X_{1}\times_{B}X_{2}\times_{B}X_{3})
. We want to make sense of

\Gamma\circ(\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2})\in\operatorname{Corr}_{B}^{k_{1}+k_{2}+l}(Y_{1},Y_{2};X_{3}).

We define this by first pulling back
\Gamma\times\Gamma_{1}
along
X_{1}
to get an element in

\Gamma\circ\Gamma_{1}\in\operatorname{Corr}^{l+k_{1}}_{B}(Y_{1},X_{2};X_{3})

and then pulling this back along
X_{2}
to get

\Gamma\circ(\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2})\in\operatorname{Corr}^{l+k_{1}+k_{2}}_{B}(Y_{1},Y_{2};X_{3}).

We have kept our notation consistent with
[
MSY25
]
Section
2.2
.

If
\mathfrak{Z}\in\operatorname{Corr}_{B}^{\bullet}(X,Y)
, then we have a morphism given by the Fourier-Mukai transform

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Z}:A_{\bullet}(X)\rightarrow A_{\bullet}(Y)

\displaystyle z\mapsto{p_{Y}}_{*}\left(p_{X}^{*}z\cdot\iota_{*}\mathfrak{Z}\right)

where
\iota:X\times_{B}Y\hookrightarrow X\times Y
is the inclusion,
p_{X},p_{Y}
are the two projections
X\times Y\rightarrow X,Y
.

Under the identification
\operatorname{Corr}_{B}^{\bullet}(B,X)=A_{\bullet}(X)
, we have for
\mathfrak{Z}\in\operatorname{Corr}^{\bullet}_{B}(B,X)
, the equality

\mathfrak{Z}(z)=\mathfrak{Z}\circ z

where the LHS is given by the Chow-theoretic Fourier-Mukai transform and the RHS is given by the convolution of correspondences as in Equation (
1
).

The category of relative Chow motives, denoted by
\operatorname{CHM}(B)
consists of triples
(X,\mathfrak{p},m)
where
X
is a non-singular variety proper over
B
,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Corr}^{0}_{B}(X,X)
is a projector and

takes care of Tate twists. Given any non-singular variety
X
, proper over
B
, we have the motive
\mathfrak{h}(X):=(X,[\Delta_{X/B}],0)
. Morphisms in this category are defined by

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{CHM}(B)}\left((X_{1},\mathfrak{p},m),(X_{2},\mathfrak{q},n)\right):=\mathfrak{q}\circ\operatorname{Corr}^{n-m}_{B}(X_{1},X_{2})\circ\mathfrak{p}.

Given a Chow motive
\mathfrak{M}=(X,\mathfrak{p},m)
in
\operatorname{CHM}(B)
, we define its Chow groups by

A^{k}(\mathfrak{M}):=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{CHM}(B)}\left(\mathfrak{h}(B)(-k),\mathfrak{M}\right)

where
\mathfrak{h}(B)(-k)=(B\xrightarrow{id}B,\Delta_{B/B}=[B],-k)\in\operatorname{CHM}(B)
.

We have a well-defined Corti-Hanamura realization functor

(2)

\operatorname{CHM}(B)\rightarrow D^{b}_{c}(B),

sending
(X\xrightarrow{f}B,\mathfrak{p},m)
to
\mathfrak{p}_{*}\left(f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X}[2m]\right)
which further specializes by taking global cohomology.

We briefly recall the definition of a motivic perverse filtration as introduced in
[
MSY24
]
.
If
X\rightarrow B
is a proper morphism with
X,B
non-singular varieties, we set
R_{f}:=\dim X\times_{B}X-\dim X
to be the defect of semi-smallness.

Definition 1.1
.

We say a sequence of motives
P_{k}\mathfrak{h}(X)=(X,\mathfrak{p}_{k},0),\ 0\leq k\leq 2R_{f}
, which are summands of
h(X)
form a motivic perverse filtration for

if

•

(Termination)
P_{2R_{f}}\mathfrak{h}(X)=\mathfrak{h}(X)

•

(Realization) Under the Corti-Hanamura functor (
[
CH00
]
), the natural inclusion

P_{\bullet}\mathfrak{h}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{h}(X)

specializes to the natural inclusion induced by the perverse truncation functor

{}^{p}\tau_{\leq\bullet+\dim X-R_{f}}Rf_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X}\hookrightarrow Rf_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X}

•

(Semi-Orthogonality)
\mathfrak{p}_{k+1}\circ\mathfrak{p}_{k}=\mathfrak{p}_{k}

Further, we say a motivic perverse filtration is multiplicative if

\left([\Delta_{X/B}]-\mathfrak{p}_{k+l}\right)\circ[\Delta^{sm}_{X/B}]\circ(\mathfrak{p}_{k}\times\mathfrak{p}_{l})=0\ \forall\ k,l

where
\Delta^{sm}_{X/B}
is the small diagonal.

We briefly recall some facts about perverse filtrations which shall be used in the later sections.

Let

stands for the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves and all functors are derived. Each perverse cohomology on the right-hand side is a semisimple perverse sheaf. We say that

has support
B
.

Theorem

If

1.2.
Motivic Perverse Filtrations for Compactified Jacobians

We keep the notations as in Section
0.1
.

Definition 1.4
.

We say a pair of correspondences
\mathfrak{Z},\mathfrak{Z}^{-1}\in\operatorname{Corr}_{B}^{\bullet}(\bar{J},\bar{J})
is a good pair if they satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

\mathfrak{Z}\circ\mathfrak{Z}^{-1}=[\Delta_{\bar{J}/B}]\in\operatorname{Corr}_{B}^{0}(\bar{J},\bar{J})
and
\mathfrak{Z}^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{Z}=[\Delta_{\bar{J}/B}]\in\operatorname{Corr}_{B}^{0}(\bar{J},\bar{J})

(2)

\mathfrak{Z},\mathfrak{Z}^{-1}
satisfy Fourier Vanishing (FV) in the sense of
[
MSY25
]
i.e.

(FV)

\mathfrak{Z}^{-1}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{Z}_{l}=0\text{ if }k+l<2g

\mathfrak{Z}_{k}\in A^{k}(\bar{J}\times_{B}\bar{J})=\operatorname{Corr}^{k-g}_{B}(\bar{J},\bar{J})
is the degree
k-g
component of the correspondence
\mathfrak{Z}
and similarly for
\mathfrak{Z}^{-1}
.

(3)

There exists a Zariski open set
U\subset B
such that
U\subset B
such that each fiber over
U

\bar{J}_{U}\xrightarrow{\pi_{U}}U

\mathfrak{Z}|_{U}=\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal{L})\cap[\bar{J}_{U}\times_{U}\bar{J}_{U}]
where
\mathcal{L}
is the normalized Poincare line bundle on
\bar{J}_{U}\times_{U}\bar{J}_{U}
.

Note that the first conditions tell us

(3)

\sum_{i=0}^{2g}\mathfrak{Z}_{i}\circ\mathfrak{Z}_{2g-i}^{-1}=[\Delta_{\bar{J}/B}]

and

(4)

\sum_{i=0}^{2g}\mathfrak{Z}^{-1}_{i}\circ\mathfrak{Z}_{2g-i}=[\Delta_{\bar{J}/B}]

If
\mathfrak{Z},\mathfrak{Z}^{-1}

(6)

To see that

Proof.

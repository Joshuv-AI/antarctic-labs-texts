Abstract
. We consider a large family of product operations of formal power series in noncommuting indeterminates, the classes of automata they define, and the respective equivalence problems. A P P -product of series is defined coinductively by a polynomial product rule P P , which gives a recursive recipe to build the product of two series as a function of the series themselves and their derivatives. The first main result of the paper is a complete and decidable characterisation of all product rules P P giving rise to P P -products which are bilinear, associative, and commutative. The characterisation shows that there are infinitely many such products, and in particular it applies to the notable Hadamard, shuffle, and infiltration products from the literature. Every P P -product gives rise to the class of P P -automata , an infinite-state model where states are terms. The second main result of the paper is that the equivalence problem for P P -automata is decidable for P P -products satisfying our characterisation. This explains, subsumes, and extends known results from the literature on the Hadamard, shuffle, and infiltration automata. We have formalised most results in the proof assistant Agda.

Main results

We are now ready to state our main results, addressing the challenges that we have proposed.

First challenge: Characterisation

Our first main contribution is a complete answer to our first challenge,
by means of an equational characterisation of
BAC

P
-products.

Theorem 1.1 (Characterisation).

A
P
-product is bilinear, associative, and commutative
if, and only if, the product rule polynomial
P
satisfies the following polynomial identities:

(
P\text{-add}
)

\displaystyle P(x+y,\dot{x}+\dot{y},z,\dot{z})

\displaystyle=P(x,\dot{x},z,\dot{z})+P(y,\dot{y},z,\dot{z})

(
P\text{-assoc}
)

\displaystyle P(x,\dot{x},yz,P(y,\dot{y},z,\dot{z}))

\displaystyle=P(xy,P(x,\dot{x},y,\dot{y}),z,\dot{z})

(
P\text{-comm}
)

\displaystyle P(x,\dot{x},y,\dot{y})

\displaystyle=P(y,\dot{y},x,\dot{x}).

The necessity of each of the equations above follows immediately
by noticing that at the constant term a
P
-product mimics the algebraic structure of the rational numbers
\left(\mathbb Q;+,\cdot\right)
.
The fact that this set of equations is also sufficient is easy to prove (by coalgebraic methods) once we have a candidate characterisation,
however we remark that to prove each of the three properties
we need to use all equations together.
This shows that the interplay between additivity, associativity, and commutativity is crucial for our proof to go through.

The equational characterisation that we provide is
syntactic
,
which should be contrasted to the notion of well-behavedness from
Boreale & Gorla 2021
,
which is
semantic
(and only known to be sufficient).
It immediately follows that being a
BAC

P
-product is decidable,
which was by no means obvious a priori.

In fact,

1.1
allows us to infer the following explicit description.
We say that a product rule

is

if there are rational constants
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb Q

(
\alpha\beta\gamma
)

\left\{\ \begin{aligned} P(x,\dot{x},y,\dot{y})&=\alpha\cdot xy+\beta\cdot(x\dot{y}+\dot{x}y)+\gamma\cdot\dot{x}\dot{y},\\
\text{ with }\alpha\cdot\gamma&=\beta\cdot(\beta-1).\end{aligned}\right.

Simplicity allows us to capture all

Corollary 1.2 (Classification).

A

For instance, the Leibnitz product rule for the shuffle product is simple with

We find it remarkable that

4(A).4.

Product rules allow us to define a large family of products of series.
We discuss here some examples.
It is convenient to interpret a product rule

as a recipe on how two processes interact when reading the input letter.
For instance by taking

\delta_{a}(f*g)=0

consequently, the product is zero on any nonempty input.
As another simple example, for

\delta_{a}(f*g)=f*g

which means that the two processes do not read any input at all,
therefore

If we take

for every

.

The rule

yields the Hadamard product,
where both processes read the input in parallel,

for every

.
The shuffle product rule

means that the next input letter

Infiltration

can overlap in

, §6.3

We can also model less standard products.
Consider two scalars

4(D).
The equivalence problem

The
equivalence problem
takes as input two finite-variable
P
-automata
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}

and amounts to decide whether they recognise the same series.
Sometimes equivalence is decidable for trivial reasons.
For instance, for the product rule
P=0
,
the class of
P
-finite series coincides with the rational series,
for which equivalence is decidable
Schützenberger 1961
.
In general, the situation may be more complex.
Since
P
-automata are infinite-state and the term algebra does not have enough structure,
decidability of equivalence is not guaranteed.
In fact, we conjecture that there exists a product rule
s.t. equivalence is undecidable.
Nonetheless, for the notable products equivalence is known to be decidable.
This is based on an algorithmic application of Hilbert’s
finite basis theorem

Cox et al. 2015
, Ch. 2, §5, Theorem 4
,
first exploited by Novikov and Yakovenko in the analysis of dynamical systems
Novikov & Yakovenko 1999
,
where they obtain decidability of equality for univariate (
\Sigma=\left\{a\right\}
) Hadamard and shuffle automata.
It has provided the basis of several subsequent decidability results,
such as for multivariate Hadamard automata
(a.k.a. polynomial automata
Benedikt et al. 2017
),
multivariate shuffle automata
Clemente 2024
,
and univariate infiltration automata
Boreale & Gorla 2021
;
Boreale et al. 2024
.
Remarkably, in all those cases decidability holds for products that are bilinear, associative, and commutative.
We will show in
section

that this is not an accident.

5(A).1.

Series satisfies the following

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (c\cdot d)\cdot f&=c\cdot(d\cdot f),\\

the extension of
\Delta_{a}
to all terms is a homomorphism
and we have
\Delta_{a^{n}}x=x^{2^{n}}
.
In this case,
{\mathcal{A}\!\left\llbracket x\right\rrbracket}
is the double exponential series from
example

4.1
.

For the shuffle product rule,
the extension of
\Delta_{a}
to all terms is a derivation
and thus
\Delta_{a^{n}}x=n!\cdot x^{n+1}
.
Indeed, this holds for
n=0
and inductively we have

\Delta_{a^{n+1}}x=\Delta_{a}(\Delta_{a^{n}}x)=\Delta_{a}(n!\cdot x^{n+1})=n!\cdot(n+1)\cdot x^{n}\cdot\Delta_{a}x=(n+1)!\cdot x^{n+2}
.
Thus,
{\mathcal{A}\!\left\llbracket x\right\rrbracket}
is the factorial series from
example

4.2
.

5(D).
The equivalence algorithm

In this section, fix a finite alphabet
\Sigma
.
We show that equivalence of finite-variable
P
-automata (and thus
P
-finite series)
is decidable for
every special product rule
P
.
This is remarkable given the diversity of products covered by our characterisation.
We could replace the rationals by an arbitrary computable field,
however we stick to
\mathbb Q
for simplicity.
Since series are a
\mathbb Q
-vector space,
equality
f=g
reduces to
zeroness

f-g=\mathbb 0
.
Fix a finite-variable
P
-automaton
\mathcal{A}=\left(X,F,\Delta\right)
.
We show how to decide
{\mathcal{A}\!\left\llbracket x_{1}\right\rrbracket}=\mathbb 0
.

For a set of terms
P\subseteq\mathbb Q[X]
,
let the
polynomial ideal generated by
P
, written
\langle P\rangle
,
be the set of all polynomials of the form

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle:=\beta_{1}\cdot\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{n}\cdot\alpha_{n},\quad\text{with }\alpha_{i}\in P,\beta_{j}\in\mathbb Q[X].

(See
Cox et al. 2015
for more details on algebraic geometry.)
This is a generous over-approximation of
P
which is sound for the zeroness problem,
in the sense that if all generators in
P
recognise the zero series,
then so do all terms in the ideal
\langle P\rangle
they generate;
it is a generalisation of the notion of linear span for vector spaces.

For every
n\in\mathbb N
,
consider the polynomial ideal

I_{n}:=\langle\Delta_{w}x_{1}\mid w\in\Sigma^{\leq n}\rangle

generated by all terms reachable from
x_{1}

by reading words of length
\leq n
.
The following technical lemma describes the relevant properties of the ideals
I_{n}
.

Lemma 5.11.

(1)

I_{n}\subseteq I_{n+1}
.

(2)

\Delta_{a}I_{n}\subseteq I_{n+1}
.

(3)

I_{n+1}=I_{n}+\langle\Delta_{a}I_{n}\mid a\in\Sigma\rangle
.

(4)

I_{n}=I_{n+1}
implies
I_{n}=I_{n+1}=I_{n+2}=\cdots
.

The second point in the lemma relies on the fact that,
as a consequence of
eq.

bilinear form
,
the product rule
P
is in the ideal generated by
y,\dot{y}
:

(ideal compatibility)

\displaystyle P\in\langle y,\dot{y}\rangle.

This condition was already employed
in the case of a single-letter alphabet
\Sigma=\left\{a\right\}

Boreale et al. 2024
, Theorem 4.2
,
however we
do not assume

eq.

ideal compatibility
,
as done in
Boreale et al. 2024
,
but rather we observe that it holds for every special product.
Now consider the chain of ideals

(20)

\displaystyle I_{0}\subseteq I_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathbb Q[X].

By
Hilbert’s finite basis theorem

Cox et al. 2015
, Theorem 4, §5, Ch. 2
,
there is a (minimal)
stabilisation index

N\in\mathbb N
s.t.
I_{N}=I_{N+1}=\cdots
.
Thanks to the last point of
lemma

5.11
, the stabilisation index is computable:
Indeed,
N
can be taken to be the smallest
n\in\mathbb N
s.t.
I_{n}=I_{n+1}
,
and ideal equality can be decided by checking that the generators of
I_{n+1}
are all in
I_{n}
.
The latter test is an instance of the
ideal membership problem
,
which is decidable (in exponential space)
Mayr 1989
.
It is crucial that such an
N
not only exists
(as follows already from Hilbert’s theorem, whose proof is notoriously non-constructive),
but that it can be computed
(a consequence of the way the ideals
I_{n}
’s are constructed).
Once we have computed
N
, we can apply the following decidable criterion for zeroness.

Lemma 5.12.

Let
N\in\mathbb N
be the stabilisation index
of the ideal chain
eq.

20
.
We then have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}\!\left\llbracket x_{1}\right\rrbracket}=\mathbb 0\quad\text{iff}\quad[w]({\mathcal{A}\!\left\llbracket x_{1}\right\rrbracket})=0\text{ for all }w\in\Sigma^{\leq N}.

Thanks to
lemma

5.12
,
to decide zeroness we check that the automaton outputs zero for all words shorter than the stabilisation index.
This concludes the proof of decidability of the zeroness and equivalence problems (
theorem

1.3
).
We stress that this subsumes known decidability results for Hadamard automata
Benedikt et al. 2017
, Theorem 4
,
shuffle automata
Clemente 2024
, Theorem 1
,
and infiltration automata
Clemente 2025
, §5.A
.
Thanks to our characterisation,
P
-automata for a special product
P
are a natural maximal class of automata
for which zeroness is decidable via Hilbert’s method.

Remark 5.13 (Complexity).

Zeroness is Ackermann-complete for Hadamard automata
over an alphabet of size
\left|\Sigma\right|\geq 2

Benedikt et al. 2017
, Theorem 1
.
The Ackermannian upper bound in fact holds for
every
special product.
This can be obtained by noticing that the upper-bound arguments on the stabilisation index
in
Novikov & Yakovenko 1999
, Sec. 3

or
Benedikt et al. 2017
, Sec. V

depend only on the fact that the degrees of the generators of

are exponential in

.
The latter is true for every
P
-automaton.

6.
Future work

In this paper we have studied coinductive products of series satisfying a product rule.
We have provided a complete and decidable description of all product rules
P
yielding commutative
\mathbb Q
-algebras of series,
and when this is the case we have shown that equivalence of
P
-automata is decidable.
This unifies and generalises known results from the literature on the Hadamard, shuffle, and infiltration products.

We outline several directions to extend this work.
While our characterisation applies to infinitely many product rules,
it remains to see whether such rules have convincing applications, for example in process algebra or in combinatorics.

We have considered product rules that are independent of the input symbol. From a modelling perspective, it may be desirable to consider
input-dependent product rules

P_{a}
(one for each
a\in\Sigma
).
For instance, in the context of process algebras,
one could use the Hadamard rule
P_{a}=\dot{x}\dot{y}
for symbols
a
where we require both processes to synchronise,
the shuffle rule
P_{b}=\dot{x}y+x\dot{y}
for symbols
b
where exactly one process makes a step,
and the infiltration rule
P_{c}=\dot{x}y+x\dot{y}+\dot{x}\dot{y}
for symbols
c

where both processes may make a step independently or synchronously.
Such an extension does not pose significant technical challenges,
and we expect all results to carry over.

Regarding decidability of equivalence,
it would be interesting to find a non-special product rule
P
s.t.

P
-finite series are strictly more expressive than the rational series and
equivalence of
P
-automata is decidable.
Conversely, it would be interesting to find a (necessarily non-special) product rule
P

s.t. the equivalence problem for
P
-automata is undecidable.

In another direction, we would like to study more general product rule formats
able to model the concatenation (Cauchy) product.
This product is bilinear and associative, but not commutative over non-singleton alphabets.
It is well-known that it can be uniquely defined by

[\varepsilon](f*g)=f_{\varepsilon}\cdot g_{\varepsilon}

and the
Brzozowski product rule

(21)

\displaystyle\delta_{a}(f*g)=(\delta_{a}f)*g+([\varepsilon]f)\cdot\delta_{a}g.

This suggests considering an extended term language
allowing for a more general scalar multiplication.
While
eqs.

⁢
P
-add

and

⁢
P
-assoc

are still sufficient to ensure that the product is bilinear and associative,
it is not clear anymore whether they are also necessary.
Another challenge would be to show decidability of equivalence
for the corresponding
Cauchy-finite series
and
Cauchy automata

since this is nothing else than equivalence of weighted context-free grammars,
a major open problem in the area.

More generally, one can consider the axioms satisfies by different kind of mathematical structures,
such as
Lie algebras
(which are nonassociative),
power associative algebras
,
the
tropical semiring
, and so on.
For the sake of generality, one could also consider
scalar multiplication
,
sum
, and
product rules

each mutually dependent on the others.

B.1.
Preliminaries

Right derivatives and reversal

The

with respect to a letter

The coinductive definition based on left and right derivatives is given in

Sometimes we will need a stronger proof principle for series equality.
For a binary relation on series

Citrullination-associated transcriptional states identify a non-canonical invasive program in malignant epithelial cells and correlate with SPP1-CD44 immune microenvironment niches in thyroid cancer
Authors: Zhisheng Tian, Peiling Zhu, Xiujuan Zhao, Fan Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Single-cell atlas reveals citrullination-enriched malignant states in thyroid cancer.•High-citrullination cells adopt a non-canonical high-invasion/high-apoptosis program.•The C_core program exhibits druggable CDC37-HSP90-PI3K/MAPK vulnerabilities.•CD44 is a functional hub linking macrophage communication to malignant maintenance.

Introduction

Thyroid cancer is the most common endocrine cancer worldwide. Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) constitutes 85–90% of cases and is generally associated with an excellent prognosis [1]. Anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC) makes up only 1–2% of thyroid cancers but accounts for more than 50% of thyroid cancer deaths, with a median overall survival of less than six months [2]. The substantial prognostic disparity between PTC and ATC reflects considerable inter-tumoural heterogeneity that remains incompletely understood at cellular and molecular levels. Bulk transcriptomic analysis of tumor biopsies has provided valuable insights into thyroid cancer taxonomy. This approach, as in other cancer types, carries substantial merit; however, it cannot probe the diverse functional states of individual cells or their interactive networks within the tumor ecosystem [3,4].

Offering unprecedented resolution, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has become a transformative tool for dissecting tumor heterogeneity and systematically characterizing the malignant, immune, and stromal compartments of the tumor microenvironment (TME) [[3], [4], [5]]. Recent single-cell atlases of thyroid carcinoma have uncovered the multicellular ecosystems that underpin disease progression, revealing that APOE+ macrophages establish “hub niches” with EMT-like fibroblasts and endothelial cells via ANGPT, SELE, and MIF signaling axes [3]. Comparative analyses between papillary (PTC) and anaplastic (ATC) thyroid carcinomas further demonstrate distinct microenvironmental configurations: ATC displays enhanced immunosuppressive features alongside a paradoxical heightened sensitivity to immunotherapy [5]. Despite these advances, a comprehensive multi-compartment analysis linking post-translational modification programs—particularly protein citrullination—to lineage-specific cellular states and intercellular communication circuits is still lacking.

Protein citrullination is an enzymatic post-translational modification catalyzed by the peptidylarginine deiminase (PADI) family, which converts peptidyl-arginine residues to citrulline, thereby altering substrate protein structure, charge, and protein–protein interactions [6,7]. The PADI family comprises five members (PADI1–4 and PADI6), among which PADI2 and PADI4 have been most extensively studied in cancer biology. PADI4-mediated citrullination of histones and cytoplasmic proteins promotes neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation, facilitates immune evasion, and drives metastatic progression in multiple solid tumors [8,9]. Mechanistically, PADI4 has been shown to promote epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and invasive behavior in gastric and lung cancers through chromatin remodeling and transcriptional reprogramming [10,11]. However, the role of citrullination in thyroid carcinoma has not been systematically investigated, and it remains unknown how citrullination-associated transcriptional programs are organized across malignant and immune cell lineages at single-cell resolution.

In this study, we integrated single-cell transcriptomics (GSE232237, 92,519 cells from 11 thyroid carcinoma samples), spatial transcriptomics (GSE250521, 10× Genomics Visium), TCGA-THCA bulk cohort data, GDSC2 pharmacogenomic profiles and Connectivity Map perturbation signatures to systematically map citrullination-associated programs across malignant, immune and stromal compartments in thyroid cancer.

Methods

Transcriptomic data acquisition and pre-processing

RNA-sequencing profiles and matched clinical data for 507 primary thyroid carcinoma samples were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-THCA) and converted to transcripts per million (TPM), followed by log2 transformation. Only primary tumor samples with available overall survival information were retained for downstream survival analysis.

Single-cell RNA-sequencing data were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database under accession number GSE232237, comprising 92,519 cells from 11 thyroid carcinoma samples (PTC, n = 7; ATC, n = 4). Spatial transcriptomic data were obtained from GSE250521 (10× Genomics Visium platform), which contained multiple PTC and ATC tissue sections for spatial validation. Analyses were performed with Seurat (v4/v5) in R (v4.3).

For pharmacogenomic analysis, we obtained drug-response data from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC2) database. This resource provides LN_IC50 values for 265 compounds, which were profiled across human cancer cell lines alongside matched RNA-sequencing expression data (log2 TPM). To identify candidate compounds for reversal analysis, we queried the Connectivity Map (CMap) database. This involved nine reference cell lines (A375, A549, HCC515, HEPG2, HT29, MCF7, PC3, HA1E, and VCAP) to retrieve perturbational connectivity scores, mechanism-of-action annotations, and compound descriptions.

Single-cell data quality control, integration, and cell-type annotation

Count matrices were made using the human reference genome GRCh38 (hg38) as described in the original studies. Cells with a proportion of mitochondrial reads exceeding 20%, fewer than 200 detected genes, more than 6,500 detected genes, or fewer than 1000 total transcript counts were filtered out for quality control. After QC, the data were normalized (NormalizeData), variable features were identified (FindVariableFeatures, top 2000), and expression values scaled (ScaleData). Using Seurat's built-in cell-cycle gene sets, we calculated cell-cycle scores (S.Score and G2M.Score) and regressed them during scaling to mitigate confounding effects arising from cell cycle differences.

Harmony integration was performed on the first 30 principal components derived from PCA across the 11 samples to correct for batch effects. Nearest-neighbor graphs were constructed on the Harmony-corrected embeddings, followed by UMAP and t-SNE visualization and Louvain clustering at a resolution of 0.8 for initial exploration. To detect and remove doublets, DoubletFinder was applied to each sample individually. Parameter sweeping was conducted to determine the optimal pK value based on the bimodality coefficient (BCmetric). Expected doublet rates were estimated from per-sample cell counts with homotypic doublet proportion correction. Predicted doublets were excluded before re-merging samples for the final integrative analysis.

Cell-type annotation was performed using canonical lineage markers. The resulting eight major compartments were: T&NK cells (CD3D, CD3E, CD3G, GNLY, NKG7, KLRD1), B cells (CD79A, CD79B, MS4A1), plasma cells (MZB1, TNFRSF17, IGHA2), myeloid cells (CD14, CD68, FCGR3A), mast cells (CPA3, VWA5A, HPGD), epithelial cells (EPCAM, KRT8, KRT19), fibroblasts (COL3A1, DCN, ACTA2), and endothelial cells (VWF, PECAM1, CLDN5). Differentially expressed marker genes were identified with FindAllMarkers (adjusted P < 0.05; |log2FC| > 0.25; min.pct > 0.1).

Malignant epithelial cell identification

To differentiate cancerous from normal epithelial cells, CopyKAT was utilized to infer CNV states from scRNA-seq data. Epithelial cells served as the target population, and immune cells (T&NK cells, B cells, and plasma cells) as diploid references; a maximum of 3000 cells per reference lineage were randomly retained to prevent reference imbalance. CopyKAT was run using gene symbols as input, with parameters ngene.chr = 5 and win.size = 25, and Euclidean distance as the distance metric. Epithelial cells classified as aneuploid were predicted as malignant, whereas those predicted as diploid or lacking definitive CNV alterations were classified as normal epithelial cells (NormalEpi). This step generated an epithelial lineage-refined atlas comprising NormalEpi and Malignant compartments for downstream tumor-state analysis.

Tumor subtype definition

Sample identifiers beginning with "PT" were classified as papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), and those beginning with "AT" as anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC). This grouping was used for PTC versus ATC comparative analyses of cellular composition and stratified analyses of myeloid and malignant cell states.

Citrullination score construction and malignant cell stratification

A literature-curated signature of five citrullination-associated genes was compiled to estimate a citrullination-associated state at the single-cell level, based on the established role of the protein arginine deiminase (PADI) family in arginine citrullination. The gene set comprised PADI1, PADI2, PADI3, PADI4, and PADI6 — all encoding enzymes that directly catalyze the deimination of arginine residues to citrulline. Per-cell citrullination scores (CitrScore) were calculated using the AddModuleScore function in Seurat (ctrl = 100, nbin = 24). The CitrScore was first computed at the global atlas level and then recomputed within the malignant compartment to more accurately capture intra-malignant citrullination-associated heterogeneity. Cells were dichotomized into citrullination-high (Citr-high) and citrullination-low (Citr-low) groups using the upper quartile as the threshold.

Malignant cells were extracted and stratified by intra-malignant CitrScore. Each subset was independently normalized, variable features were identified, and Harmony-corrected PCA and Louvain clustering (resolution 0.2) were performed using the first 30 Harmony dimensions. This yielded six Citr-high subclusters (C1–C6) and six Citr-low subclusters (N1–N6). PTC exhibited a markedly higher proportion of Citr-high malignant cells compared with ATC (52.6%vs. 22.6%), suggesting enrichment of high-citrullination malignant states in PTC.

Functional state scoring and malignant core-state definition

Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was performed using the GSVA package to evaluate four tumor-relevant functional programs: angiogenesis, apoptosis, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), and invasion. Gene sets were curated from CancerSEA definitions with data-specific gene retention. Specifically: Angiogenesis (VEGFA, KDR, FLT1, ANGPT2, TEK, PGF, EPAS1, HIF1A, THBS1, MMP2); Apoptosis (BAX, BAK1, CASP3, CASP8, CASP9, BID, BBC3, PMAIP1, FAS, TNFRSF10B); EMT (VIM, ZEB1, ZEB2, SNAI1, SNAI2, TWIST1, FN1, CDH2, ITGB1, MMP14); and Invasion (MMP1, MMP2, MMP3, MMP9, MMP14, CXCR4, SPP1, PLAUR, ITGA6, ITGB4). Inter-subcluster differences were evaluated by Kruskal–Wallis test with Wilcoxon pairwise comparisons; Pearson correlation assessed relationships between CitrScore and functional program scores.

CitrScore correlated positively with apoptosis (R = 0.52) and invasion (R = 0.32), negatively with EMT (R = −0.26), and weakly with angiogenesis (R = 0.02, P = 0.097), indicating a non-canonical "high-invasion/high-apoptosis" phenotype without concordant EMT elevation rather than a classical EMT-dominant state. The malignant core state (C_core) was therefore defined as the combined C4, C2, and C5 subclusters, which exhibited the highest composite scores for invasion and apoptosis alongside relatively low EMT.

The N3 subcluster, displaying the lowest invasive potential, served as the Citr-low reference state (N-like). Differential expression between C_core and N3 was performed using FindMarkers (Wilcoxon rank-sum test). A stringent criterion (adjusted P 〈 0.05, |log₂FC| 〉 1) was applied for downstream signature construction, yielding the C_core-up signature and the N-like signature.

WGCNA and enrichment analysis

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was applied to sample–subcluster pseudobulk expression matrices (retaining combinations with ≥ 20 cells). Aggregated counts were library-size normalized and log-transformed. Following gene filtering (removal of lowly expressed genes and retention of high-variance genes, with forced inclusion of PADI family genes), a signed network was constructed using blockwiseModules (networkType = "signed", TOMType = "signed", minModuleSize = 300, mergeCutHeight = 0.25). Module eigengenes were correlated with subcluster trait matrices to identify biologically relevant modules, which were subjected to GO-Biological Process (GO-BP) and KEGG enrichment analyses using clusterProfiler.

SCENIC regulatory network and Hallmark pathway analysis

SCENIC was employed to reconstruct regulons active in C_core versus N3 cells. Raw count matrices were filtered for gene expression, and highly variable genes were retained based on expression variance. Gene co-expression networks were inferred using GENIE3, and motif enrichment and regulon identification were performed using RcisTarget with the hg19 SCENIC database (encompassing 500-bp upstream and 10-kb TSS-centered motif annotation sets). Regulon activity was quantified at the single-cell level using AUCell. Differential regulon analysis between C_core and N3 was conducted using Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Benjamini–Hochberg multiple testing correction.

For higher-order biological program interpretation, ssGSEA was performed using MSigDB Hallmark gene sets (via msigdbr), prioritizing 23 Hallmark pathways including apoptosis, hypoxia, PI3K–AKT–mTOR signaling, TNFα signaling via NF-κB, IL6–JAK–STAT3 signaling, EMT, inflammatory response, and others. Key regulatory factors and target genes were correlated with Hallmark pathway activities using Pearson correlation.

Drug sensitivity profiling (GDSC) and connectivity map reversal analysis

Expression-drug sensitivity correlation was performed using GDSC2. For each gene in the C_core-up signature, Spearman correlation was computed between its expression and drug LN_IC50 across shared cell models (minimum 15 shared models). Significant associations were defined as |rho| ≥ 0.30 and P < 0.05. Drug target and pathway annotations were integrated to classify significant drugs by target category, and per-gene positive/negative correlation counts were tabulated as candidate pharmacologic associations.

For CMap analysis, the C_core-up signature was queried as "up" tags and the N-like signature as "down" tags against nine reference cell lines. Compounds with the highest absolute connectivity scores were identified per cell line and summarized by mechanism of action. A drug-pathway network centered on CDC37 was constructed to illustrate the potential bottleneck position of protein chaperone systems in the C_core pharmacological vulnerability landscape. Candidate mechanisms converged on PKC activators, EGFR inhibitors, HSP inhibitors, p38 MAPK inhibitors, PLK inhibitors and survivin inhibitors.

Myeloid cell re-clustering, annotation, and citrullination heterogeneity

Myeloid cells (28,432 cells) were extracted from the integrated atlas and re-clustered following normalization, variable feature selection, scaling, PCA, Harmony correction, and Louvain clustering at resolution 0.4. Four major myeloid populations were identified: monocytes (LST1, FCN1, S100A8, S100A9, TYMP, CTSS), macrophages (C1QA, C1QB, C1QC, APOE, CD68, SPP1, CTSB), myeloid dendritic cells (mDC; FCER1A, CLEC10A, CD1C, HLA-DRA, HLA-DPA1), and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDC; GZMB, JCHAIN, IRF7, IFITM1, TCF4, CLEC4C).

The global CitrScore was mapped onto the myeloid object; scores were winsorized and linearly scaled for visualization. Myeloid cells were dichotomized into Citr-high and Citr-low groups using the intra-myeloid upper quartile threshold. mDC exhibited the highest CitrScore and Citr-high proportion, followed by pDC and monocytes, whereas macrophages showed the lowest citrullination activity. Notably, PTC demonstrated higher overall Citr-high myeloid proportions than ATC, particularly within the mDC subset, indicating that high-citrullination myeloid states are not ATC-specific but rather more enriched in PTC.

Bulk deconvolution and survival analysis

To evaluate associations between myeloid subclusters and clinical outcomes, BisqueRNA was employed for reference-based deconvolution of TCGA-THCA bulk transcriptomes using the myeloid scRNA-seq object as reference. Primary tumor samples with available overall survival data were retained. Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were constructed for each myeloid cluster's estimated abundance to calculate hazard ratios, 95% confidence intervals and P values. Given the limited number of OS events in TCGA-THCA, these results are interpreted as exploratory trend-level evidence.

ROGUE purity scoring

ROGUE was applied to quantify transcriptional homogeneity of myeloid populations. RNA count matrices were analyzed with sample source as the sample label; minimum cell thresholds were set to 10 for major lineages and 5 for individual clusters, with remaining parameters following UMI data-recommended settings. Higher ROGUE scores indicate greater transcriptional purity.

Cell–cell communication analysis

CellChat was used to infer ligand–receptor interactions between myeloid and malignant subsets. Myeloid cells were encoded by combining cell type with citrullination state (mDC, monocyte, macrophage and pDC, each subdivided into Citr-high and Citr-low); malignant cells were dichotomized into Citr-high and Citr-low groups. Analysis employed the standard human database with protein–protein interaction (PPI) projection; communication probabilities were calculated with population.size = TRUE and minimum cell count threshold of 10.

Spatial transcriptomic validation

Spatial transcriptomes (Visium platform, GSE250521) were processed per sample to construct spatial expression objects with normalized Spatial assays. The PTC sample GSM7980867_PTC-4 was selected for formal presentation as a representative section with clear signal patterns. SPP1 and CD44 expression signals were overlaid onto tissue background images using low-resolution spatial coordinates; high-expression values were quantile-capped prior to visualization. The resulting spatial pattern—focal SPP1 enrichment within lesions and broader CD44 distribution in adjacent regions—supports a localized macrophage-malignant SPP1-CD44 communication niche rather than a pan-tumor co-localization model.

In silico CD44 knockout and program-level perturbation analysis

To functionally probe CD44′s role in malignant program maintenance, scTenifoldKnk was applied to malignant cells. Raw counts were filtered to retain cells with library sizes ≥ 1000 and genes expressed in ≥ 25 cells. CD44 was designated as the target gene for virtual knockout with parameters: qc = FALSE, nc_lambda = 0, nc_nNet = 10, nc_nCells = 500, nc_nComp = 3, nc_scaleScores = TRUE, nc_symmetric = FALSE, nc_q = 0.9, td_K = 3, td_maxIter = 1000, and ma_nDim = 2. The diffRegulation output was used to rank genes by perturbation magnitude; representative genes of interest included SPP1, TNFSF12, TNFRSF12A, GAS6, LGALS9, MIF, AXL, CXCR4, ANXA1, MMP9, IGF2, and H19.

For program-level analysis, predefined gene sets were constructed: C_core-up signature, N-like signature, SPP1–CD44 axis genes (SPP1, CD44, LGALS9, GAS6, MIF, AXL, CXCR4, ANXA1, TNFSF12, TNFRSF12A), and functional program gene sets for invasion/EMT, hypoxia, and apoptosis (sourced from the CancersEA and MSigDB Hallmark databases). Gene-level perturbation scores were log-transformed and standardized; mean perturbation intensity was computed per gene set. Permutation testing (2000 iterations) against random gene sets of equal size yielded empirical z-scores and P values.

Cell lines and cell culture

Human papillary thyroid carcinoma cell line TPC-1 and anaplastic thyroid carcinoma cell line 8505C were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2.

Cell transfection

CD44-targeting shRNA (sh-CD44) and non-targeting control (sh-NC) were obtained from OriGene Technologies (Rockville, MD, USA) and packaged into lentiviral particles by co-transfecting HEK293T cells with packaging plasmids using the BLOCK-iT Lentiviral RNAi Expression System (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Viral supernatants were harvested 48 h post-transfection and concentrated by ultracentrifugation. TPC-1 cells were infected with sh-CD44 or sh-NC lentivirus, and 8505C cells were infected with lentivirus carrying the full-length CD44 expression construct (oe-CD44) or empty vector control (NC), both obtained from OriGene Technologies. Stable cell lines were established by puromycin selection for two weeks, and intervention efficiency was confirmed by qRT-PCR. shRNA sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 1.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from cultured cells using TRIzol reagent (Sigma-Aldrich, T9424, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. First-strand complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using the HiFi II 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Yeasen, 11119ES60, China). Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) was performed on an Applied Biosystems real-time PCR system using SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Yeasen, 11184ES03, China). Relative mRNA expression was calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method with GAPDH as the internal control. All experiments were performed in biological triplicate. Primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 2.

Western blot analysis

Total protein was extracted using RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% non-fat milk, membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with the following primary antibodies: anti-CD44 (ab189524, Abcam, USA), anti-E-cadherin (ab76055, Abcam, USA), anti-Vimentin (10,366-1-AP, Proteintech, China), and anti-GAPDH (60,004-1-Ig, Proteintech, China) as the internal control. Membranes were then incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, and protein bands were visualized using an ECL chemiluminescence detection system.

CCK-8 proliferation assay

Cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Dojindo, CK04, Japan). Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 2 × 103 cells per well. At 24, 48, and 72 h, CCK-8 solution was added and incubated for 2 h at 37°C. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Colony formation assay

Cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 500 cells per well and cultured for 10–14 days. Colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with 0.1% crystal violet (Sigma-Aldrich, C0775, USA), and counted under a light microscope.

Flow cytometry apoptosis assay

Cell apoptosis was assessed by flow cytometry using FITC Annexin V (Thermo Fisher Scientific, A35110, USA) and Propidium Iodide (PI) (Thermo Fisher Scientific, P3566, USA) staining. Briefly, cells were harvested, washed twice with cold PBS, and resuspended in binding buffer. Cells were then incubated with FITC Annexin V and PI for 15 min at room temperature in the dark. Stained cells were immediately analyzed by flow cytometry, and the apoptosis rate was quantified as the percentage of Annexin V-positive cells.

Transwell migration and invasion assays

For migration assays, thyroid cancer cells were suspended in serum-free medium and added to the upper chamber of Transwell plates (Corning, 3422, USA). The lower chamber contained complete medium with 20% FBS. After 24 h, migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with crystal violet. For invasion assays, the upper chamber was precoated with Matrigel (Corning, 354,248, USA). Stained cells were counted under a microscope.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed in R (v4.3). Two-group comparisons used the Wilcoxon rank-sum test; multi-group comparisons used the Kruskal–Wallis test; correlation analyses used Pearson or Spearman correlation as specified; survival analyses used Cox proportional hazards regression. Unless otherwise stated, P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Multiple testing correction was performed using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. All experiments were performed in triplicate. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Differences between two groups were analyzed using Student’s t-test. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

CopyKAT-based CNV inference subsequently partitioned the epithelial compartment into malignant (aneuploid) and normal (diploid) fractions (Fig. 1C). Citrullination-associated malignant subclusters engage distinct transcriptional programmes

To resolve heterogeneity within the malignant compartment, we dichotomised malignant cells by intra-malignant CitrScore and performed independent re-clustering. Citr-high cells resolved into six subclusters (C1–C6) and Citr-low cells into six (N1–N6) (Fig. 2A, B), demonstrating that citrullination-related heterogeneity decomposed into multiple discrete transcriptional states rather than a single continuum. Quantitative comparison between tumour subtypes revealed that PTC harboured a substantially higher proportion of Citr-high malignant cells than ATC (Fig. 2C). C1–C6 were transcriptionally characterised by IYD, CEACAM6, NOVA1, SIX1, MIR205HG and TPO, whereas N1–N6 displayed CHI3L1, LCN2, SCGB3A1, TAGLN3, KRT5 and TFF3 signatures (Fig. 2D). We next sought to functionally characterise these subclusters by computing per-cell ssGSEA scores for angiogenesis, apoptosis, EMT and invasion using CancerSEA-curated gene sets. Marked functional heterogeneity was evident across the 12 malignant subclusters (Fig. 3A). Notably, several Citr-high subclusters (C4, C2, C5) simultaneously displayed elevated invasion and apoptosis activity despite relatively low EMT scores, supporting a non-canonical malignant phenotype distinct from classical EMT-driven progression. Together, these results support a Citr-high-associated programme characterised by concomitant invasion enhancement and apoptosis activation without concordant EMT elevation. Based on this composite profile, we merged C4, C2 and C5 into the C-core malignant state and designated N3 as the N-like reference for subsequent regulatory network comparisons.

SCENIC analysis contrasting C-core with N3 revealed two structurally distinct regulon architectures (Fig. 3C, D), indicating that the Citr-high malignant core state is accompanied by systematic transcriptional regulatory reprogramming rather than mere quantitative expression changes. Gene–Hallmark correlation analysis (|r| > 0.2, P < 0.05) further showed that differential regulon genes were co-activated with apoptosis, hypoxia, PI3K–AKT–mTOR signalling, TNFα signalling via NF-κB, IL6–JAK–STAT3 signalling, inflammatory response and EMT programmes (Fig. 3E).

Functional assays demonstrated that CD44 is a potent regulator of malignant phenotypes in thyroid cancer cells. We first assessed proliferative capacity: CD44 knockdown markedly suppressed the proliferative activity of TPC-1 cells (Fig. 8B) and impaired long-term clonogenic survival (Fig. 8C). Conversely, CD44 overexpression conferred enhanced proliferative and clonogenic potential upon 8505C cells. Flow cytometry analysis revealed that CD44 knockdown substantially increased apoptosis in TPC-1 cells; in stark contrast, CD44 overexpression exerted a pronounced anti-apoptotic protective effect in 8505C cells (Fig. 8D). Transwell assays further demonstrated that CD44 depletion crippled the migratory and invasive capacity of TPC-1 cells, whereas CD44 upregulation fortified the invasiveness and motility of 8505C cells (Fig. 8E). Discussion

We found that the CitrScore-defined citrullination-associated state was not uniformly distributed across all cell types but was significantly enriched in malignant epithelial cells, with greater enrichment in papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) than in ATC. This finding suggests that the PADI-associated transcriptional proxy used here is primarily linked to epithelial-derived malignant transformation in thyroid cancer, rather than being broadly present across all tumor-associated cell types. Notably, high-CitrScore malignant cells could be further partitioned into multiple transcriptionally distinct subclusters that exhibited a non-canonical "high-invasion/high-apoptosis" program accompanied by the absence of concordant classical epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). This phenotype differs from the conventional view that aggressive tumor cells should suppress apoptosis [12], and may reflect a form of adaptive vulnerability in malignant cells, in which enhanced invasive capacity coexists with compensatory apoptotic priming. This seemingly paradoxical phenotype may also reflect stress-adapted reprogramming in response to metabolic or microenvironmental pressure [13].

Mechanistically, PADI family enzymes catalyze the conversion of arginine residues to citrulline, altering the charge state and conformation of substrate proteins, thereby affecting their interactions with binding partners and downstream signal transduction [14,15]. Emerging evidence has implicated dysregulated PADI expression and citrullination in various cancers, including breast, lung, and colorectal cancer [[16], [17], [18]]. In thyroid cancer, PADI2 has been reported to be upregulated and associated with aggressive clinicopathological features [19]. In our study, higher CitrScore states were associated with Ras-MAPK and PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway activation, consistent with previous reports linking citrullination-related programs to signal transduction regulation [20,21]. Furthermore, WGCNA revealed that high-CitrScore subclusters were closely associated with epithelial differentiation and matrix remodeling programs, suggesting that these cells may maintain partial differentiation features while engaging invasive behavior, rather than undergoing complete dedifferentiation. We have also explicitly acknowledged that CitrScore represents a transcriptomic proxy rather than a direct biochemical measurement of protein citrullination.

Based on differential expression analysis between C_core and the reference state, we constructed a C_core-upregulated signature and screened potential drug intervention strategies using the GDSC2 and CMap databases. The C_core program was not dominated by a single driver gene but rather corresponded to a multi-layered, potentially targetable network involving epigenetic regulation, proteostasis, cell cycle, and canonical oncogenic signaling pathways. Notably, the CDC37-HSP90 client folding axis occupied a central position in this network. CDC37, as a kinase-specific co-chaperone of HSP90, is responsible for presenting multiple protein kinases (including key kinases in Ras-MAPK and PI3K-AKT pathways) to HSP90 for maturation and stabilization [22,23].

Our findings align with previous studies demonstrating that the CDC37-HSP90 axis may represent a candidate pharmacologic dependency in various malignancies. Overexpression of CDC37 has been reported in multiple cancer types and is associated with poor prognosis [24,25]. HSP90 inhibitors, such as 17-AAG and ganetespib, have shown anti-tumor activity in preclinical models of thyroid cancer [26,27]. Additionally, CMap analysis identified multiple candidate compounds capable of reversing the C_core signature, with mechanisms of action converging on HSP90 inhibition, MAPK/PI3K blockade, and epigenetic regulation. These results should be interpreted as computationally inferred therapeutic hypotheses rather than direct experimental evidence for treatment of the high-CitrScore malignant phenotype.

Our in-depth analysis of myeloid cells revealed non-uniform distribution of higher CitrScore states within the myeloid compartment—Citr-high status was primarily concentrated in dendritic cells (particularly conventional dendritic cells, mDCs) and was relatively low in macrophages. This finding has important biological and clinical implications. Dendritic cells, as key antigen-presenting cells, directly influence the initiation and maintenance of anti-tumor immune responses through their functional status [28,29]. Citrullination has been shown to regulate peptide presentation by MHC class II molecules and can affect co-stimulatory molecule expression and cytokine secretion [30,31]. Recent studies have demonstrated that PADI4-mediated citrullination of histones in neutrophils promotes the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), which can modulate anti-tumor immunity [32,33]. The enrichment of high-CitrScore mDCs observed in PTC suggests that citrullination-associated states may participate in remodeling the thyroid cancer immune microenvironment by regulating dendritic cell maturation and function. In contrast, the lower citrullination-associated signal in macrophages is consistent with their higher intra-population heterogeneity, which may reflect the functional plasticity of macrophages in response to different microenvironmental pressures [34,35].

Cell-cell communication analysis combined with spatial transcriptomic validation supported a macrophage–malignant communication axis centered on SPP1-CD44, and suggested that this niche is primarily associated with Citr-low malignant cells rather than the Citr-high core state.

Although this finding differs from initial expectations, it carries important biological implications. SPP1 (osteopontin) is a secreted matricellular glycoprotein that has been shown to promote tumor cell invasion, migration, and metastasis in various cancers [36,37]. In thyroid cancer, elevated SPP1 expression has been associated with aggressive behavior and poor prognosis [38,39]. CD44, as one of the major receptors for SPP1, is involved in cell adhesion, migration, and signal transduction [40,41]. The "focal SPP1 plus broader CD44″ expression pattern observed in spatial transcriptomics suggests that SPP1 may be secreted by local macrophages and act in a paracrine manner on surrounding CD44-expressing malignant cells, forming a spatially confined communication niche [42].

Notably, although this communication axis was more pronounced in Citr-low malignant cells, virtual knockout of CD44 preferentially perturbed the C_core program. This seemingly paradoxical result suggests that CD44 may not merely be a passive receptor but rather an integrative node involved in broader signaling networks. As a transmembrane glycoprotein, CD44 integrates multiple signals from the extracellular matrix, growth factors, and cytokines, and regulates cell proliferation, survival, and migration through interactions with the cytoskeleton and downstream signaling molecules (e.g., Ras, PI3K, Src family kinases) [43,44]. Recent single-cell studies have highlighted CD44 as a key mediator of intercellular communication in various cancer types [45,46]. Our findings suggest that even though CD44 is highly expressed in Citr-low malignant cells, its functional impact may extend to the C_core state through paracrine or network effects. This provides a rationale for exploring therapeutic strategies targeting CD44; however, the current data do not establish a direct molecular link between CD44 and protein citrullination itself [47,48].

To experimentally validate the functional role of CD44 in thyroid cancer malignant phenotypes, we performed CD44 knockdown in TPC-1 cells and CD44 overexpression in 8505C cells. Our results demonstrated that CD44 knockdown significantly suppressed proliferation, colony formation, migration, and invasion, while promoting apoptosis and upregulating E-cadherin with concomitant downregulation of Vimentin. Conversely, CD44 overexpression exerted opposite effects, further supporting its pro-tumorigenic and EMT-promoting functions. These experimental findings align with our single-cell and spatial transcriptomic analyses and support CD44 as a key downstream functional hub linking macrophage–malignant communication to malignant state maintenance in thyroid cancer, rather than as direct proof of a citrullination-driven mechanism.

Conclusions

This study provides a single-cell atlas of citrullination-associated programs in thyroid carcinoma. Our analyses indicate that high-CitrScore malignant cells are characterized by a non-canonical high-invasion/high-apoptosis phenotype accompanied by AP-1/STAT3-related regulatory rewiring and a candidate pharmacologic dependency centered on the CDC37–HSP90–PI3K/MAPK axis. Unexpectedly, macrophage–malignant communication was dominated by a Citr-low-associated SPP1–CD44 niche, with CD44 functioning as a functionally relevant interface linking local microenvironmental signals to malignant state maintenance. These findings nominate two candidate therapeutic directions—HSP90/PI3K/MAPK pathway inhibition in high-CitrScore tumors and CD44-directed disruption of the macrophage–malignant communication axis—that warrant prospective validation in biomarker-stratified models and future translational studies.

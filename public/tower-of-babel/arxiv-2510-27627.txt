Abstract
. We develop a framework for the study of the limiting behavior of multiple ergodic averages with commuting transformations when all iterates are given by the same sparse sequence; this enables us to partially resolve several longstanding problems. First, we address a special case of the joint intersectivity question of Bergelson, Leibman, and Lesigne by giving necessary and sufficient conditions under which the multidimensional polynomial Szemerédi theorem holds for length-three patterns. Second, we show that for two commuting transformations, the Furstenberg averages remain unchanged when the iterates are taken along sparse sequences such as [ n c ] [n^{c}] for a positive noninteger c c , advancing a conjecture of the first author. Lastly, we extend Chu’s result on popular common differences in linear corners to polynomial and Hardy corners. Our toolbox includes recent degree lowering and seminorm smoothing techniques, the machinery of magic extensions of Host, and novel structured extensions motivated by works of Tao and Leng. Combined, these techniques reduce the analysis to settings where the Host-Kra theory of characteristic factors and equidistribution on nilmanifolds yield a family of striking identities from which our main results follow.

Introduction

Determining the limiting behavior of multiple ergodic averages is a central theme in modern ergodic theory, with deep applications to combinatorics and number theory.
More precisely, we seek to identify those sequences
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}

for every

(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})

[
45
]

When

are powers of the same transformation,

[

Tao

2.1.
Box seminorms

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a system and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. For each
R\in\mathopen{}\left\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
, we define the
multiplicative derivative

\Delta_{R}f:=f\cdot R\overline{f}

and for
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}\in\mathopen{}\left\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
, we denote the
iterated multiplicative derivative
via

\Delta_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}f:=\Delta_{R_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{R_{s}}f=\prod_{{{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{{\epsilon}}|}R_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}}\cdots R_{s}^{\epsilon_{s}}f.

If each transformation
R_{j}
appears
k_{j}
times, we also denote

\displaystyle\Delta_{R_{1}^{\times k_{1}},\dots,R_{s}^{\times k_{s}}}f:=\Delta_{\underbrace{R_{1},\ldots,R_{1}}_{k_{1}},\;\ldots,\;\underbrace{R_{s},\ldots,R_{s}}_{k_{s}}}f.

Lastly, we employ the conventions

\displaystyle\Delta_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s};h}f

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{R_{1}^{h_{1}},\ldots,R_{s}^{h_{s}}}f\quad

\displaystyle\textrm{for}\quad h\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{s}

\displaystyle\textrm{and}\quad\Delta_{R_{1}^{\times k_{1}},\ldots,R_{s}^{\times k_{s}};h}f

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{R_{1}^{h_{11}},\ldots,R_{1}^{h_{1k_{1}}}}\cdots\Delta_{R_{s}^{h_{s1}},\ldots,R_{s}^{h_{sk_{s}}}}f\quad

\displaystyle\textrm{for}\quad h\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{s}}

whenever convenient.

Following Host
[
32
]
, we inductively define
box seminorms
by letting

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{\emptyset}:=\int f\,d\mu

and

(10)

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,{R_{s}}}^{2^{s}}:=\lim_{H\to\infty}\E_{h\in[H]}\lvert\!|\!|\Delta_{R_{s}^{h}}f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s-1}}^{2^{s-1}}

for
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}\in\mathopen{}\left\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
. Expanding the definition, we thus have

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,{R_{s}}}^{2^{s}}=\lim_{H_{s}\to\infty}\E_{h_{s}\in[H_{s}]}\cdots\lim_{H_{1}\to\infty}\E_{h_{1}\in[H_{1}]}\int\Delta_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s};h}f\;d\nu.

As explained in
[
32
, Lemma 1]
, we can replace
[H_{i}]
in the average by any Følner sequence in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Combined with
[
5
, Lemma 1.1]
, this allows us to replace any
s^{\prime}
iterated limits by the single limit
\lim\limits_{H\to\infty}\E_{h\in[H]^{s^{\prime}}}
.

In the special case when
R:=R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
, we obtain the
Host-Kra seminorm
of
R
of degree
s
, denoted by

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s,R}:=\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}

and originally defined by Host-Kra in their groundbreaking work on norm convergence of multiple ergodic averages
[
33
]
. While Host-Kra seminorms predate box seminorms by several years, the latter appear more naturally in the context of multiple ergodic averages of commuting transformations and therefore occupy more prominent place in this work.

Sometimes, we may wish to emphasize the measure with respect to which the seminorm is defined; if so, we write
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s};\mu}
and
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{s,R;\mu}
. For instance, if
\mu=\int\mu_{x}\;d\mu(x)
is the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
with respect to the joint action of
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
, then we have the easy-to-derive formula

(11)

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s};\mu}^{2^{s}}=\int\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s};\mu_{x}}^{2^{s}}\;d\mu(x).

We now record several other standard properties of box seminorms freely used throughout the paper. Their proofs can be found e.g. in
[
13
,
24
,
32
]
. In what follows, we take
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}\in\mathopen{}\left\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

(i)

(Permutation invariance) For
any permutation
\sigma:[s]\to[s]
,

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}=\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,R_{\sigma(s)}}.

(ii)

(Monotonicity) We have

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1}}\leq\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},R_{2}}\leq\cdots\leq\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}.

(iii)

(Inductive formula) For any
1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s
,

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}^{2^{s}}=\lim_{H_{s}\to\infty}\E_{h_{s}\in[H_{s}]}\cdots\lim_{H_{s^{\prime}+1}\to\infty}\E_{h_{s^{\prime}+1}\in[H_{s^{\prime}+1}]}\lvert\!|\!|\Delta_{R_{s^{\prime}+1},\ldots,R_{s};h^{\prime}}f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s^{\prime}}}^{2^{s^{\prime}}},

where
h^{\prime}=(h_{s^{\prime}+1},\ldots,h_{s})
. Moreover, the iterated limit can be replaced by the single limit
\lim\limits_{H\to\infty}\E_{h^{\prime}\in[H]^{s-s^{\prime}}}
.

(iv)

(Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality) For any
(f_{{\epsilon}})_{{{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mu)
,

\displaystyle\Big|\lim_{H_{s}\to\infty}\E_{h_{s}\in[H_{s}]}\cdots\lim_{H_{1}\to\infty}\E_{h_{1}\in[H_{1}]}\int\prod_{{{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{{\epsilon}}|}R_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}}\cdots R_{s}^{\epsilon_{s}}f_{{\epsilon}}\;d\mu\Big|\leq\prod_{{{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\lvert\!|\!|f_{{\epsilon}}|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}.

Once again, the iterated limit on the left-hand side can be replaced by the single limit
\lim\limits_{H\to\infty}\E_{h\in[H]^{s}}
.

(v)

(Scaling) For any nonzero
r_{1},\ldots,r_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}\leq\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1}^{r_{1}},\ldots,R_{s}^{r_{s}}}.

(We caution that
R_{i}^{r_{i}}
denotes the
r_{i}
-th power of
R_{i}
, not
r_{i}
copies of
R_{i}
, denoted by
R_{i}^{\times r_{i}}
.) Conversely, if
s\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1}^{r_{1}},\ldots,R_{s}^{r_{s}}}\leq|r_{1}\cdots r_{s}|^{1/2^{s}}\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}.

2.3.
The factor
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(T)
and nonergodic eigenfunctions

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be a (not necessarily ergodic) system. The factor
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(T)={\mathcal{Z}}(T,T)
admits a particularly nice structure that can be expressed in terms of the following notion.

Definition 2.1

(Nonergodic eigenfunctions)
.

A function
\chi\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
is called a
nonergodic eigenfunction
of
T
if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

T\chi=\lambda\chi
for some
\lambda\in I(T)
(which we call the
nonergodic eigenvalue
of
f
);

(ii)

|\chi(x)|\in\{0,1\}
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
, and
\lambda(x)=0
whenever
\chi(x)=0
.

We denote the set of nonergodic eigenfunctions of
T
by
{\mathcal{E}}(T)
.

For ergodic systems, a nonergodic eigenfunction is either the zero function or a classical unit modulus eigenfunction. For general systems, each function
\chi\in{\mathcal{E}}(T)
satisfies

(14)

\chi(Tx)={\bf 1}_{E}(x)\,e(\phi(x))\,\chi(x)

for some
T
-invariant set
E\in{\mathcal{X}}
and measurable
T
-invariant function
\phi\colon X\to{\mathbb{T}}
.

By
[
23
, Theorem 5.2]
, the factor
Z_{1}(T)
is a closed linear span of nonergodic eigenfunctions.

Later in the paper, we will make use of the following lemma, which shows that if we slightly modify the conditions defining nonergodic eigenfunctions, we still get an element of
Z_{1}(T)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
be a system and
\chi\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be a function satisfying
|\chi|=1
and
T\chi=\lambda\chi
for some
\lambda\in I(T)
.
Then
\chi\in Z_{1}(T)
.

Proof.

Our assumptions give that
|\lambda|=1
and

\chi=T^{h_{1}+h_{2}}\overline{\chi}\cdot T^{h_{1}}\chi\cdot T^{h_{2}}\chi\quad\text{for all}\quad h_{1},h_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Averaging over
h_{1},h_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we get that

(15)

\chi=\lim_{H\to\infty}\E_{h_{1},h_{2}\in[H]}T^{h_{1}+h_{2}}\overline{\chi}\cdot T^{h_{1}}\chi\cdot T^{h_{2}}\chi\in Z_{1}(T).

2.5.

Let
B
be the collection of equivalence classes (“germs”) of real valued functions defined on some halfline
(t_{0},\infty)
for
t_{0}\geq 0
, where we identify two functions that eventually agree. A
Hardy field
is a subfield
{\mathcal{H}}
of the ring
(B,+,\cdot)
that is closed under differentiation
[
31
]
. We call
a:(t_{0},\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
a
Hardy-field function
if it belongs to some Hardy field
{\mathcal{H}}
closed under composition and compositional inversion (whenever the latter is defined). By abuse of notation, we speak of “functions” rather than “germs”, understanding that all the operations defined and statements made for elements of
{\mathcal{H}}
are considered only for sufficiently large values of
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Replacing

with
a(t-t_{0})
, we also assume without loss of generality that
a
is defined on the entire
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. We say that
a\in{\mathcal{H}}
has
polynomial growth
if there exists
d>0
such that
a(t)\ll t^{d}
.

A typical example of a Hardy field is the Hardy field
\mathcal{LE}
of
logarithmico-exponential functions
, i.e., functions constructed using a finite combination of symbols
+,-,\times,\div
,
\exp,

\log
acting on the real variable
t
and on real constants.

In this article, we focus on Hardy-field functions in
\mathcal{LE}
.
Our results also apply beyond
this setting, to more general Hardy-fields, provided they satisfy the mild assumptions in
[
13
, Section 2.2]
.

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we will need the following results on the equidistribution of Hardy-field functions on nilmanifolds and seminorm estimates for multiple ergodic averages along Hardy-field functions.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a Hardy-field function of polynomial growth satisfying (
3
). Then
a
is equidistributed on every nilmanifold.

Theorem 2.6

(Box seminorm control for Hardy-field functions)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a system,
m\in[\ell]
, and
a\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a Hardy-field function of polynomial growth satisfying

(16)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\frac{a(t)}{\log t}\mathclose{}\right|=\infty.

Then there exists
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
f_{m+1}~\in~{\mathcal{E}}(T_{m+1})
,
\ldots,f_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T_{\ell})
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|\E_{n\in[N]}T_{1}^{[a(n)]}f_{1}\cdots T_{\ell}^{[a(n)]}f_{\ell}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0

whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f_{m}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{m}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s},\ldots(T_{m}T_{m-1}^{-1})^{\times s},T_{m}^{\times s}}=0
.

We will use Theorem
2.6
for
(m,\ell)=(2,2)
and
(m,\ell)=(1,2)
. Theorem
2.6
follows from
[
13
, Theorem 10.2]
when
a(t)\gg t^{\delta}
for some
\delta>0
and from
[
13
, Proposition 6.3]
whenever
a(t)\ll t^{\delta}
for all
\delta>0
. When
a\in{\mathbb{R}}[t]
, the derivation of Theorem
2.6
from
[
13
, Theorem 10.2]
implicitly uses the scaling property of box seminorms from
Section

2.1
.
Both aforementioned results cover multiple ergodic averages along Hardy sequences in which
f_{m+1},\ldots,f_{\ell}
are bounded-degree dual functions of the respective transformations rather than nonergodic eigenfunctions. Since degree-2 dual functions of a transformation
T_{j}
span

just like
{\mathcal{E}}(T_{j})
, we can derive Theorem
2.6
from the cited results by approximating nonergodic eigenfunctions in

by degree-2 dual functions.

In the simpler case of
m=\ell
, Theorem
2.6
could also be derived from (much more general)
[

, Theorem 10.1]
, and the two special cases when

is (i) an integer polynomial, and (ii) stays logarithmically away from real polynomials (this includes fractional powers) had previously been established in
[
12
, Theorem 2.5]
and
[

, Theorem 5.1]

2.6.
Magic extensions

We will later need to consider structured extensions of a system
on which certain inverse theorems take a particularly simple form.
One such extension is a slight variant of the magic
systems and extensions defined by Host
[
32
]
.

Definition 2.7

(Magic systems and extensions)
.

We call a system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})

magic
with respect to
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}\in\mathopen{}\left\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
if

(17)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(R_{1},\ldots,R_{s})={\mathcal{I}}(R_{1})\vee\cdots\vee{\mathcal{I}}(R_{s}).

Suppose that the transformations
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
are given by

R_{i}:=T^{b_{i}}=T_{1}^{b_{i1}}\cdots T_{\ell}^{b_{i\ell}}

for some
b_{i}=(b_{i1},\ldots,b_{i\ell})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
. We call an extension
(X^{*},{\mathcal{X}}^{*},\mu^{*},T_{1}^{*},\ldots,T_{\ell}^{*})
of
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})

magic
with respect to
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
if
(X^{*},{\mathcal{X}}^{*},\mu^{*},T_{1}^{*},\ldots,T_{\ell}^{*})
is a magic system with respect to the transformations
R_{1}^{*},\ldots,R_{s}^{*}\in\mathopen{}\left\langle T_{1}^{*},\ldots,T_{\ell}^{*}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
given by
R_{i}^{*}=(T^{*})^{b_{i}}
.

Our definition differs from Host’s in that we want the magic property (
17
) to hold for a fixed subset of
\mathopen{}\left\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
rather than for
T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}
themselves. The next result concerns the existence of magic extensions under certain conditions on
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
that always arise in our applications.

Proposition 2.8

(Existence of magic extensions)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a system, and suppose that
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}\in\mathopen{}\left\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
generate
\mathopen{}\left\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{s}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
for some
s\in[\ell]
.
Then
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
admits a magic extension
(X^{*},{\mathcal{X}}^{*},\mu^{*},T_{1}^{*},\ldots,T_{\ell}^{*})
with respect to
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
.

Proof.

Consider the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,R_{1},\ldots,R_{s})
. By
[
32
, Theorem 2]
, it admits an extension
(X^{*},{\mathcal{X}}^{*},\mu^{*},R_{1}^{*},\ldots,R_{s}^{*})
satisfying

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(R^{*}_{1},\ldots,R^{*}_{s})={\mathcal{I}}(R_{1}^{*})\vee\cdots\vee{\mathcal{I}}(R_{s}^{*}),

constructed as follows:
X^{*}:=X^{2^{s}}
,
{\mathcal{X}}^{*}:={\mathcal{X}}^{\otimes 2^{s}}
,
\mu
is the
s
-dimensional cubic measure induced by
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
(see
[
32
, Section 2]
for the details), and the transformations
R_{1}^{*},\ldots,R_{s}^{*}
are given by

\displaystyle R_{i}^{*}((x_{{\epsilon}})_{{\epsilon}}):=\begin{cases}R_{i}x_{{\epsilon}},\;&\epsilon_{i}=0\\
x_{{\epsilon}},\;&\epsilon_{i}=1.\end{cases}

Then the factor map is given by the projection onto the coordinate indexed by
0
.

It remains to define
T_{1}^{*},\ldots,T_{\ell}^{*}
so that
(X^{*},{\mathcal{X}}^{*},\mu^{*},T_{1}^{*},\ldots,T_{\ell}^{*})
is also an extension of
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
with respect to the same factor map. By assumption,
for every
j\in[s]
, we can express
T_{j}
uniquely as

\displaystyle T_{j}=R_{1}^{c_{j1}}\cdots R_{s}^{c_{js}}

for some
c_{ji}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then we simply define

\displaystyle T_{j}^{*}:=(R_{1}^{*})^{c_{j1}}\cdots(R_{s}^{*})^{c_{js}};

in particular, the invariance of
\mu^{*}
with regards to
R_{1}^{*},\ldots,R_{s}^{*}
immediately gives the invariance with respect to
T_{1}^{*},\ldots,T_{s}^{*}
.
For
j\in[s+1,\ell]
, we lift
T_{j}
to
T^{*}_{j}:=\underbrace{T_{j}\times\cdots\times T_{j}}_{2^{s}}
; its invariance can then be deduced inductively from the cubic structure of the measure
\mu^{*}
and the
T_{j}
-invariance of
\mu
.
∎

Proposition 2.9

(Soft inverse theorem for magic systems)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a magic system with respect to
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}\in\mathopen{}\left\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}\mathclose{}\right\rangle
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
(depending on
\varepsilon
, the system, and
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
) such that if a 1-bounded function
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
satisfies

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}\geq\varepsilon,

then there exist 1-bounded functions
g_{1}\in I(R_{1}),\ldots,g_{s}\in I(R_{s})
for which the integral below is real and

\displaystyle\int f\cdot g_{1}\cdots g_{s}\;d\mu\geq\delta.

Proof.

Define the seminorm on
L^{\infty}(\mu)
by

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert^{*}:=\sup\left\{\mathopen{}\left|\int f\cdot g_{1}\cdots g_{s}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|\colon g_{1}\in I(R_{1}),\ldots,g_{s}\in I(R_{s})\textrm{ all }1\textrm{-bounded}\right\}

and the multilinear functional
A\colon(L^{2^{s}}(\mu))^{2^{s}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

\displaystyle A((f_{{\epsilon}})_{{\epsilon}}):=\lim_{H\to\infty}\E_{h\in[H]^{s}}\int\prod_{{{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{{\epsilon}}|}R_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}}\cdots R_{s}^{\epsilon_{s}}f_{{\epsilon}}\;d\mu.

Note that the limit is known to exist by combining results from
[
5
,
32
]
.

Crucially, (
13
) and our assumption gives us
qualitative control
of
A
by the norm
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert^{*}
at the index 0: if

\lvert\!|\!|f_{0}|\!|\!\rvert^{*}=0
, then
\lvert\!|\!|f_{0}|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}=0
, and hence also
A((f_{{\epsilon}})_{{\epsilon}})=0
by the Gowers-Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. To get the claimed
soft quantitative control
, we then apply
[
26
, Proposition A.2]
with
X_{j}:=L^{2^{s}}(\mu)
,
X_{j}^{\prime}:=L^{\infty}(\mu)
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X_{j}^{\prime}}:=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X_{j}}:=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{2^{s}}(\mu)}
for
j\in[2^{s}]
and the seminorm
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert:=\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert^{*}
. For every
\varepsilon>0
it gives us
\delta>0
(depending only on
\varepsilon
, the system and
R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}
) such that if
f_{{\epsilon}}
is 1-bounded for every
{{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}
, then
\lvert\!|\!|f_{0}|\!|\!\rvert^{*}\leq\delta
implies
|A((f_{{\epsilon}})_{{\epsilon}})|\leq\varepsilon^{2^{s}}
. Applying this result with
f_{{\epsilon}}:=f
for every
{{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}
, we deduce that
\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}}\leq\varepsilon
. The claim then follows by contrapositive and multiplying
g_{1}
with an appropriate complex number on the unit circle.
∎

3.
General framework, proof strategy, and obstructions

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we will work in a more general setting that covers sequences satisfying the following two properties.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence.
We say that

(i)

(a(n))_{n}

admits box seminorm control
if there exists
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and all functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

(18)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}\int\,f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\;d\mu=0

whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s},T_{2}^{\times s}}=0
, and if additionally
f_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T_{2})
, then (
18
) also holds whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1}^{\times s}}=0
;

(ii)

(a(n))_{n}

equidistributes on nilsystems
if for every nilsystem
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
and every
x\in X
, the sequence
(T^{a(n)}x)_{n}
is equidistributed in
\overline{(T^{n}x)_{n}}
.

Examples of sequences that satisfy these properties are the fractional powers, i.e.,
a(n)=[n^{c}]
with
c\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}
; see
Section

2.5
for a detailed explanation.

Unfortunately, polynomial sequences fail to equidistribute on nilsystems; to prove Theorem
1.2
, we therefore proceed to
work with sequences that satisfy the following variant of the preceding properties.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence and
n_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We say that

(i)

(a(k!n+n_{k}))_{n,k}

admits box seminorm control
if there exists
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and all functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

(19)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(k!n+n_{k})}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(k!n+n_{k})}f_{2}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|=0

whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s},T_{2}^{\times s}}=0
, and if additionally
f_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T_{2})
, then (
19
) also holds whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1}^{\times s}}=0
;

(ii)

(a(k!n+n_{k}))_{n,k}

equidistributes on nilsystems
if for every nilsystem
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
, function
F\in C(X)
, and every
x\in X
, we have

(20)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}F(T^{a(k!n+n_{k})}x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}F(T^{k!n}x).

Moreover, analogous definitions apply if we replace the Cesàro average over
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with an average along an arbitrary Følner sequence in
{\mathbb{N}}
.

Remarks
.

The limit on the right-hand side of (
20
) can be shown to exist; for example, see the argument in the second part of Section
3.2
.

It suffices to verify (
20
) when
x=e_{X}
. Indeed, if
X=G/\Gamma
,
Tx=bx
for some
b\in G
, and
x=g\cdot e_{X}
, note that
F(b^{n}x)=\widetilde{F}(c^{n}\cdot e_{X})
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
\widetilde{F}(x):=F(gx)
is in
C(X)
and
c:=g^{-1}\,b\,g\in G
.

We explain in
Section

3.2
that nonzero intersective polynomials satisfy these properties.

The next result will be used to prove

Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 3.3

(Equality of limits I)
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence that admits box seminorm control and equidistributes on nilsystems. Then for every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and all functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have the identity

(21)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\,d\mu=\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{n}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{n}f_{2}\,d\mu;

in particular, both limits above exist. Moreover, an analogous result holds when, in both the hypothesis
and the conclusion, we replace the Cesàro average over
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

by an average along an arbitrary Følner sequence in
{\mathbb{N}}
.

Remark
.

Our assumptions are close to optimal. Indeed, if (
21
) holds, then
(a(n))_{n}
admits (optimal) box seminorm control and
[
25
, Proposition 8.2]
gives that it equidistributes on
2
-step nilsystems.

The next result
will be used to prove
Theorem

1.2
.

Theorem 3.4

(Equality of limits II)
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence and
n_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
be such that
(a(k!n+n_{k}))_{n,k}
admits box seminorm control and equidistributes on nilsystems.
Then for every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and all functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have the identity

(22)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(k!n+n_{k})}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(k!n+n_{k})}f_{2}\,d\mu\\
=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{k!n}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{k!n}f_{2}\,d\mu;

in particular, both iterated limits above exist. Moreover, an analogous result holds when, in both the hypothesis
and the conclusion, we replace the Cesàro average over
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

by an average along an arbitrary Følner sequence in
{\mathbb{N}}
.

Combining Theorem
3.3
with
[
32
, Proposition 1]
(restated as Proposition

below), we obtain the following corollary.

Theorem 3.5

(Optimal seminorm control)
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence that admits box seminorm control and equidistributes on nilsystems. Then for every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and all 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|\leq\min\bigl(\lvert\!|\!|f_{0}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1},T_{2}},\lvert\!|\!|f_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1},T_{2}T_{1}^{-1}},\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2}}\bigr).

3.1.
Proof of
Theorem

1.1
assuming
Theorem

3.3

By
Theorem

3.3
, it suffices to verify that
(a(n))_{n}
admits optimal box seminorm control and equidistributes on nilsystems. The first property follows from Theorem
2.6
while the second one is given by
Theorem

2.5
.

3.2.
Proof of
Theorem

1.2
assuming
Theorem

3.4

We give the argument for Cesàro averages; the proof is identical
for averages along arbitrary Følner sequences in
{\mathbb{N}}
.

By
Theorem

3.4
, it once again suffices to ascertain that
(a(k!n+n_{k}))_{n,k}
admits box seminorm control and equidistributes on nilsystems. The first of these properties follows from
Theorem

2.6
by applying the seminorm control from the latter to the average

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(k!n+n_{k})}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(k!n+n_{k})}f_{2}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|

separately for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

It remains to show that
(p(k!n+n_{k}))_{n,k}
equidistributes
on nilsystems. Fix a nilsystem
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T)
. By the second remark following

Definition

3.2
we can assume that
x=e_{X}
. There exists
r_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}

such that the nilmanifold
\overline{(T^{r_{0}n}x)_{n}}

is the connected component
X_{0}
of the identity element

in

(see for example
[

, Corollary 8, page 182]
). By

for every nonconstant polynomial

.
Let now

with

, in which case we have

and

(T^{p(k!n+n_{k})}x)_{n}

are both equidistributed in

. This implies that for every

3.3.
Proof strategy

We give a pretty detailed description of the proof strategy for
Theorem

3.3
. The proof of
Theorem

3.4
is similar up to a few additional technical details that are addressed in
Section

5.2
.

The single transformation case.

Our starting point is the proof of
Theorem

3.3
for a single transformation, i.e., when
f_{2}=1
, in which case the result follows
from an application of the spectral theorem and from
the fact that the sequence
(a(n))_{n}
equidistributes for
all circle rotations.

Optimal box seminorm control and magic extensions.

For two transformations, our strategy amounts to establishing the
optimal box seminorm control
for the averages

(23)

\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\;d\mu;

with respect to the function
f_{2}
, i.e., we show that if
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2}}=0
, then the averages
(
23
) vanish as
N\to\infty
. This is highly nontrivial and constitutes the main advance of this article. Once this is done, we use the machinery of magic extensions due to Host
[
32
]
to reduce matters to the case where
f_{2}
is a product of a
T_{2}
-invariant function and a
T_{2}T_{1}^{-1}
-invariant function. This immediately puts us in the single transformation case.

Known box seminorm control.
We explain next how to get optimal box seminorm control.
Our starting point is the assumption that the averages (
23
)
admit box seminorm control, i.e., there exist
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, possibly large, such that
if
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},\,T_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}=0
, then
the averages (
23
) vanish as
N\to\infty
. This property
holds for sequences covered in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, a highly
nontrivial fact established in great pain within the last few years (see
Theorem

2.6
).

Recently, an analogous degree reduction for box seminorms has been carried out quantitatively for polynomial corners in the finitary setting by Kravitz, Leng, and the second author
[
36
]
. Our argument follows, in part, the overall philosophy of
[
36
]
. However, some of the quantitative arguments from
[
36
]
become significantly simpler in the ergodic setting (mostly those related to equidistribution on nilmanifolds) whereas certain easy steps in the finitary setting (such as obtaining inverse theorems for degree-3 box norms) require substantial effort in the ergodic universe.

Degree lowering.

We explain how we reduce the degree
s_{1}+s_{2}
of the seminorm in three representative test cases.

Case 1:
(s_{1},s_{2})=(1,2)
.

Suppose that
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2},T_{2}}=0
implies
vanishing of the averages (
23
) as
N\to\infty
. Our goal is to
show a similar property under the weaker hypothesis

\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2}}=0
. The crucial maneuver is
to pass to a suitable structured extension of the factor

{\mathcal{Z}}:={\mathcal{Z}}({T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2},T_{2}})
on which the following
inverse theorem holds

(24)

f_{2}\,\bot\,\bigl({\mathcal{I}}(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})\vee{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(T_{2})\bigr)\implies\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2},T_{2}}=0.

For simplicity, assume that the original system

(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
satisfies (
24
) (it is open whether one can deduce this assumption; see
1

Existence of structured extensions.

Lastly, we briefly describe the proof strategy for establishing the existence
of an extension on which (
24
) holds. It suffices to prove that,
for every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
, there exists an extension

(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},S_{2})
of the factor
{\mathcal{Z}}:={\mathcal{Z}}({T_{1},T_{2},T_{2}})
such that

{\mathcal{Y}}:={\mathcal{Z}}({S_{1},S_{2},S_{2}})
is spanned by
{\mathcal{I}}(S_{1})\vee{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(S_{2})
. Unlike
Host’s proof of the existence of magic extensions
[
32
]
, which yields a similar
result for the seminorms
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1},T_{2}}
, we did not manage
to give a proof entirely within ergodic theory. Instead, we rely on a
method pioneered by Tao
[
47
]
and recently utilized by Leng
[
39
]
in a
setting closer to ours. The first step is to find a corresponding inverse
theorem in the finitary world (see
Proposition

4.2
); somewhat
surprisingly, this is straightforward and is the main reason for the
method’s effectiveness. From this we deduce a decomposition result for
sequences
f\colon[\pm N]^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}
(see
Proposition

4.8
) as a sum
of structured, box seminorm uniform, and negligible components, which then implies that
finitary variants of dual sequences can be well approximated by these
structured components (see
Proposition

4.9
). We use
this to show that a countable dense set
{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq L^{2}({\mathcal{Z}})
of dual
functions, such as

{\mathcal{D}}f:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{h_{1},h_{2}\in[N]}T_{1}^{h_{1}}\overline{f}\cdot T_{2}^{h_{2}}\overline{f}\cdot T_{1}^{h_{1}}T_{2}^{h_{2}}f,

where
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and the limit is taken in
L^{2}(\mu)
, can be
approximated pointwise on all finite scales by these structured sequences.
We then use these structured sequences to build a system
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},S_{2})
in which all
correlations of functions in
{\mathcal{D}}
are reproduced; this makes it an
extension of
(X,{\mathcal{Z}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
. The advantage of the extended system
is that, because it is built from simple structured components, it is
straightforward to show that it satisfies (
24
), as required.
To give a better sense of the argument, note that the structured
components are restrictions to
[\pm N]^{2}
of linear combinations of sequences
of the form

a(m,n):=e(\phi(m)n)\cdot b(m)\cdot c(n),\quad m,n\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
\phi\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is arbitrary and
b,c\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
are

1
-bounded. It is not hard to verify that the Furstenberg system (i.e.,
the measure preserving system
(X:=\mathbb{D}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
associated to

(a(m,n))_{m,n}
via the Furstenberg correspondence principle) of these infinite sequences is indeed spanned by

{\mathcal{I}}(T_{1})\vee{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(T_{2})
. The system
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},S_{2})
we build is the
joint Furstenberg system of infinitely many such
sequences. The details of this argument appear in
Section

4
.

3.4.
Obstructions for longer averages and mean convergence

Having explained the proof strategy for Theorem
1.1
, we move on to explain where the argument above fails if we aim to establish the (highly plausible)
L^{2}(\mu)
identity

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|\E_{n\in[N]}T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}-\E_{n\in[N]}T_{1}^{n}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{n}f_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

The argument presented in Case 1, which reduces the seminorm control by
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2},T_{2}}
to the seminorm control by
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2}}
, can be adapted in a straightforward way to the mean convergence setting, i.e., to get good seminorm control for the limit

(26)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left\|\E_{n\in[N]}T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

But this no longer holds for Case 2, in which the goal is to derive the seminorm control in terms of
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2}}
from one by
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2}}
. We recall that the reduction of Case 2 to Case 1 proceeds by composing the integral with
T_{1}^{-a(n)}
, a trick that can only be applied in the setting of weak convergence.
4
4

4

One could try to circumvent this by recasting the square of (
26
) as

\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\E\limits_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0,N}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\;d\mu;

however, it is essential to our approach that
f_{0,N}
be independent of
N
(since we need intermediate seminorm estimates in terms of
f_{0}
), which we cannot ensure without knowing the
L^{2}(\mu)
convergence of the average in (
26
).

Hence, Case 2 for (
26
) is genuinely different than Case 1, unlike in the weak-limit realm. One therefore needs to attack Case 2 head-on. By passing to a structured extension of the factor
{\mathcal{Z}}(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2})
, we can reduce the entire complexity of Case 2 to the situation where
f_{2}
is a nonergodic eigenfunction of
T_{2}T_{1}^{-1}
, i.e.,
T_{2}T_{1}^{-1}f_{2}=\lambda\,f_{2}
for some
\lambda\in I(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})
. Plugging this formula back to (
26
), we end up with the
L^{2}(\mu)
norm of

(27)

\displaystyle\E_{n\in[N]}T_{1}^{a(n)}(f_{1}f_{2})\cdot(T_{1}^{a(n)}\lambda)^{a(n)}.

It is highly unclear how to deal with this expression, not least because it is not linear in
\lambda
and
\lambda
may not be constant. If
a(n)=n
, then an argument of Lesigne (private communication) shows that (
27
) vanishes unless
\lambda
is constant on a positive-measure set. For nonlinear sequences such as
n^{2}
or
[n^{3/2}]
, the likely conclusion one could derive is that (
27
) is nonzero only if
\lambda
agrees on a positive-measure set with a function in
Z_{s}(T_{1})
for some
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. However, it is neither clear how to reach this conclusion for general polynomials or Hardy sequences, nor how to apply it. The nonlinearity of (
27
) in
\lambda
makes it difficult to follow the instinct and perform the standard decomposition of
\lambda
into structured, uniform, and small components.

Even more issues arise if we aim to adapt our arguments to more transformations. In the process of running the degree lowering argument for the triple average

(28)

\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\cdot T_{3}^{a(n)}f_{3}\;d\mu,

one would reduce the problem to the scenario when
f_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T_{2})\cup{\mathcal{E}}(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})
whereas
f_{3}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T_{3})\cup{\mathcal{E}}(T_{3}T_{1}^{-1})\cup{\mathcal{E}}(T_{3}T_{2}^{-1})
. If, say,
f_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T_{2})
and
f_{3}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T_{3}T_{1}^{-1})
, then we run again into the problem of incompatibility:
f_{3}
is acted on by
T_{3}
despite being a nonergodic eigenfunction of
T_{3}T_{1}^{-1}
. If in turn we compose (
28
) with
T_{1}^{-a(n)}
, getting

\displaystyle\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{1}\cdot T_{1}^{-a(n)}f_{0}\cdot(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{a(n)}f_{2}\cdot(T_{3}T_{1}^{-1})^{a(n)}f_{3}\;d\mu,

then
f_{3}
is compatible with the transformation that acts on it, but this is no longer the case for
f_{2}
. It is clear that there is no reparametrization of the integral that makes all the eigenfunctions compatible, and we currently do not know how to handle this problem.

In summary, while our approach opens a path toward resolving
Conjecture
1
and Conjecture
2
(when all polynomials coincide),
formidable obstacles remain for the case of
\ell\geq 3
transformations or mean convergence analogs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

4.
Structured extensions

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be an ergodic system. In this section, we construct a structured extension
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell})
of the factor
{\mathcal{Z}}:={\mathcal{Z}}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell},T_{\ell})
on which the factor
{\mathcal{Y}}
takes the particularly pleasing form

(29)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Y}}={\mathcal{I}}(S_{1})\vee\cdots\vee{\mathcal{I}}(S_{\ell-1})\vee{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(S_{\ell}).

Specifically, we show the following result, which we will only use for
\ell=2
in this article.

Theorem 4.1

(Existence of a structured extension)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be an ergodic system, and let
{\mathcal{Z}}:={\mathcal{Z}}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell},T_{\ell})
. Then
(X,{\mathcal{Z}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
admits an ergodic extension
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell})
satisfying (
29
).

To prove Theorem
4.1
, we first derive a (pretty straightforward) inverse theorem for a certain finitary box norm analogous to
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell},S_{\ell}}
for compactly supported functions on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
. We then use a variant of the arithmetic regularity lemma to obtain a decomposition of an arbitrary function on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
into terms that are “structured” and “uniform” with respect to the aforementioned finitary box norm as well as a small error term. Lastly (this is where the bulk of the work goes), we construct the extension
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell})
in such a way that any
{\mathcal{Z}}
-measurable function on
X
lifts to a function in (
29
). Our argument is heavily inspired by a blog post of Tao
[
47
]
and recent work of Leng
[
39
, Section 9]
. It would be interesting to find a proof of

Theorem

4.1
more in the spirit of Host’s
proof of the existence of magic extensions
[
32
, Theorem 2]
,
one that uses purely ergodic machinery and yields an extension
of the original system rather than its factor
{\mathcal{Z}}

(the absence of such a result is a technical nuisance,
as we shall see later).

Question 1
.

Is it possible to strengthen
Theorem

4.1

by finding a system
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell})
that satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell-1},S_{\ell},S_{\ell})={\mathcal{I}}(S_{1})\vee\cdots\vee{\mathcal{I}}(S_{\ell-1})\vee{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(S_{\ell})

extends
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
rather than
(X,{\mathcal{Z}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
?

The utility of working inside the structured extension of
Theorem

4.1
comes from the following inverse theorem
that will play a key role in our degree lowering argument later on. Its proof is very close to the proof of Proposition
2.9
and so we skip it.

Proposition 4.2

(Soft quantitative inverse theorem)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a system with the property that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell},T_{\ell})={\mathcal{I}}(T_{1})\vee\cdots\vee{\mathcal{I}}(T_{\ell-1})\vee{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(T_{\ell}).

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
(depending on
\varepsilon
and the system) such that if a 1-bounded function
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
satisfies

\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell},T_{\ell}}\geq\varepsilon,

then there exist 1-bounded functions
g_{1}\in I(T_{1}),\ldots,g_{\ell-1}\in I(T_{\ell-1}),\chi\in{\mathcal{E}}(T_{\ell})
for which the integral below is real and

\displaystyle\int f\cdot g_{1}\cdots g_{\ell-1}\cdot\chi\;d\mu\geq\delta.

4.1.
Defining factor maps from correlation identities

To prove
Theorem

4.1
, we plan to construct
a system
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell})
and
a collection of
1
-bounded
L^{\infty}(\nu)
functions that imitate
the correlations of a dense family of functions in
the unit ball of
L^{2}({\mathcal{Z}},\mu)
. We then need a
result that allows us to extract a factor map
from this property, and we carry out this standard
preliminary step in this subsection.
Recall that all systems in this article are assumed to be regular, and that
T^{h}=T_{1}^{h_{1}}\cdots T_{\ell}^{h_{\ell}}
for every
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{\ell})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
.

We will use the following variant of a well-known result in operator theory
(see for example
[
16
, Theorem 12.15]
that covers the case
F=L^{\infty}(\mu)
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
and
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell})
be systems and
F\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mu)
be a conjugation-closed subalgebra that contains
1
,
is invariant under
T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}
, and is
dense in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Furthermore, let
\Phi\colon F\to L^{\infty}(\nu)

be a map that satisfies

(i)

\Phi
is an algebra homomorphism and
\Phi(\overline{f})=\overline{\Phi(f)}
for all
f\in F
;

(ii)

\int\Phi(f)\,d\nu=\int f\,d\mu
for all
f\in F
;

(iii)

\Phi(T^{h}f)=S^{h}(\Phi(f))
for all
f\in F
and
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
.

Then there exists a factor map

\phi\colon Y\to X
, i.e.,
\phi_{*}\nu=\mu
,
\phi\circ S^{h}=T^{h}\circ\phi
for all
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
.

Proof.

The argument is rather standard, so we only sketch it.

Our assumptions give that
\Phi
is linear and
\left\|\Phi(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
for
f\in F
, i.e.,
\Phi
is a linear isometry on a dense subspace of
L^{2}(\mu)
and hence extends uniquely to a linear isometry
\Phi\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}(\nu)
. The goal is to show that there exists a measure-preserving
measure-algebra
\sigma
-homomorphism
\alpha\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}

5
5

5

I.e.,
\nu(\alpha(A))=\mu(A)
,
\alpha(A^{c})=\alpha(A)^{c}
, for all
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
, and
\alpha(\cup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}A_{i})=\cup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\,\alpha(A_{i})
for all
A_{i}\in{\mathcal{X}}
.
with
\alpha(T^{-h}A)=S^{-h}\alpha(A)
for all
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
and
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
and such that
\Phi({\bf 1}_{A})={\bf 1}_{\alpha(A)}
. Since we
are working with standard probability spaces, this then implies (see for example
[
16
, Theorem 12.14]
or
[
50
, Theorem 2.2]
) the existence of a point map
\phi\colon Y\to X
that satisfies the required properties (
\phi
is related to
\alpha
via
\alpha(A)=\phi^{-1}(A)
for
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
).

Let
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
. Using our assumptions on
F
and the Weierstrass approximation theorem, in order to approximate the continuous function
t\mapsto\min\{t\cdot{\bf 1}_{[0,+\infty)}(t),1\}
uniformly by Bernstein polynomials with range
[0,1]
, we get that there exist
f_{n}\in F
with
0\leq f_{n}\leq 1
such that

f_{n}\to{\bf 1}_{A}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
, which implies that
f_{n}^{2}\to{\bf 1}_{A}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
. From this and the multiplicativity of the linear isometry
\Phi
on
F
, we easily deduce that
(\Phi({\bf 1}_{A}))^{2}=\Phi({\bf 1}_{A})
, hence,
\Phi({\bf 1}_{A})={\bf 1}_{\alpha(A)}
for some
\alpha\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
. Using a similar argument, we get that
\Phi({\bf 1}_{A\cap B})={\bf 1}_{\alpha(A)\cap\alpha(B)}
and
\Phi({\bf 1}_{A^{c}})={\bf 1}_{\alpha(A)^{c}}
, showing that
\alpha
is a measure-algebra homomorphism, and using the monotone convergence theorem, we deduce that it is a
\sigma
-homomorphism. In a similar
fashion, we show that
\Phi({\bf 1}_{T^{-h}A})={\bf 1}_{S^{-h}\alpha(A)}
, hence

\alpha(T^{-h}A)=S^{-h}\alpha(A)
for all
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
and
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
. Lastly,

\nu(\alpha(A))=\left\|\Phi({\bf 1}_{A})\right\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}=\left\|{\bf 1}_{A}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\mu(A),

completing the proof of the asserted properties.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
and
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell})
be systems and
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mu)
a collection of functions, closed under conjugation, that generates a dense subalgebra of
L^{2}(\mu)
.
Suppose that there exist functions

(g_{n})_{n}\subseteq L^{\infty}(\nu)
such that

(30)

\int T^{h_{1}}f_{n_{1}}\cdots T^{h_{r}}f_{n_{r}}\,d\mu=\int S^{h_{1}}g_{n_{1}}\cdots S^{h_{r}}g_{n_{r}}\,d\nu

for all
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
, and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
is a factor of
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell})
.

Proof.

We let

F
be the
T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}
-invariant subalgebra generated by
(f_{n})_{n}\cup\{1\}
and define

\Phi\colon F\to L^{\infty}(\nu)
via

(31)

\Phi\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{l}c_{i}\,T^{h_{i,1}}f_{n_{i,1}}\cdots T^{h_{i,r}}f_{n_{i,r}}\Big):=\sum_{i=1}^{l}c_{i}\,S^{h_{i,1}}g_{n_{i,1}}\cdots S^{h_{i,r}}g_{n_{i,r}},

for all

l\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
h_{i,1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
,
n_{i,1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
c_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. We claim that
\Phi
is well defined.
To this end, since
\Phi
is linear, it suffices to establish the implication

\sum_{i=1}^{l}c_{i}\,T^{h_{i,1}}f_{n_{i,1}}\cdots T^{h_{i,r}}f_{n_{i,r}}=0\,\implies\sum_{i=1}^{l}c_{i}\,S^{h_{i,1}}g_{n_{i,1}}\cdots S^{h_{i,r}}g_{n_{i,r}}=0,

where

l\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
h_{i,1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
,
n_{i,1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
c_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, the first identity holds

-a.e. and the second
\nu
-a.e. Equivalently, it suffices to show that

\int\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{l}c_{i}\,T^{h_{i,1}}f_{n_{i,1}}\cdots T^{h_{i,r}}f_{n_{i,r}}\Bigr|^{2}\,d\mu=0\,\implies\int\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{l}c_{i}\,S^{h_{i,1}}g_{n_{i,1}}\cdots S^{h_{i,r}}g_{n_{i,r}}\Bigr|^{2}\,d\nu=0.

This is a consequence of the identity

(32)

\int\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{l}c_{i}\,T^{h_{i,1}}f_{n_{i,1}}\cdots T^{h_{i,r}}f_{n_{i,r}}\Bigr|^{2}\,d\mu=\int\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{l}c_{i}\,S^{h_{i,1}}g_{n_{i,1}}\cdots S^{h_{i,r}}g_{n_{i,r}}\Bigr|^{2}\,d\nu,

which can be easily established by expanding the squares on the left and right side and using (
30
) (we also used here that the set
F
is closed under conjugation).

Using (
31
) we easily get that Properties (
i
)-(
iii
) of
Lemma

4.3
are satisfied, and we deduce that there exists a factor map
\phi\colon Y\to X
, as desired. ∎

4.2.
Finitary box norms

We now switch gears to the finitary setting in order to prove a finitary inverse theorem that will later play a key part in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
Let
f\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be finitely supported. For
h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define its
(symmetric and asymmetric) multiplicative derivatives
to be

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}_{(h,h^{\prime})}f(x):=f(x+h)\,\overline{f}(x+h^{\prime})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Delta_{h}f(x):=f(x)\,\overline{f}(x+h),

and then construct higher-degree derivatives via

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}_{(h_{1},h_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,(h_{s},h_{s}^{\prime})}f:=\Delta_{(h_{1},h_{1}^{\prime})}^{\prime}\cdots\Delta_{(h_{s},h_{s}^{\prime})}^{\prime}f\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Delta_{h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}}f:=\Delta_{h_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{s}}f.

Given finite sets
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
, we define the
box norm
of
f
along
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}
to be

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\left(\E_{h_{1},h_{1}^{\prime}\in E_{1}}\cdots\E_{h_{s},h_{s}^{\prime}\in E_{s}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}}\Delta^{\prime}_{(h_{1},h^{\prime}_{1}),\ldots,(h_{s},h^{\prime}_{s})}f(x)\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2^{s}}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}}\mu_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}(m)\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}}\Delta_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{s}}f(x)\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2^{s}},

where

(33)

\displaystyle\mu_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}(m):=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\E_{h_{j},h^{\prime}_{j}\in E_{j}}\textbf{1}_{m_{j}=h_{j}^{\prime}-h_{j}}

for any
m=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{s})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell s}
.
We will need the following two finitary inverse theorems. The first one plays a merely auxiliary role and can be found e.g. in
[
36
, Lemma A.5]
.

Lemma 4.5

(Finitary
U^{2}
inverse theorem)
.

Let
\delta>0
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
f\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be 1-bounded and supported on
[\pm N]^{2}
. Then there exist
\phi,\psi\colon{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{T}}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{e_{1}[\pm N],e_{1}[\pm N]}^{4}\geq\delta N^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sum_{x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f(x,y)\,e(\phi(y)x+\psi(y))\gg\delta^{3/2}N^{2}.

The second one is what we really need; it is a straightforward generalization of
[
36
, Lemma A.8]
.

Proposition 4.6

(Finitary box norm inverse theorem)
.

Let
\delta>0
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
f\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be 1-bounded and supported on
[\pm N]^{\ell}
. Then there exist
\phi\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}\to{\mathbb{T}}
and 1-bounded
b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
supported on
[\pm N]^{\ell}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{e_{1}[\pm N],\ldots,e_{\ell}[\pm N],e_{\ell}[\pm N]}^{2^{\ell+1}}\geq\delta N^{\ell}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}}f(x)\,e(\phi(\hat{x}_{\ell})x_{\ell})\,b_{1}(\hat{x}_{1})\cdots b_{\ell}(\hat{x}_{\ell})\gg_{\ell}\delta^{3/2}N^{\ell},

where
\hat{x}_{i}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
.

Proof.

By the inductive formula for the box norms (e.g.
[
37
, Lemma 3.5]
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}}\mu_{N}(m)\left\|\Delta_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell-1}}f\right\|_{e_{\ell}[\pm N],e_{\ell}[\pm N]}^{4}=\delta N^{\ell},

where
\mu_{N}:=\mu_{\underbrace{[\pm N]^{\ell},\ldots,[\pm N]^{\ell}}_{\ell-1}}
as in (
33
).
Applying Lemma
4.5
to each multiplicative derivative, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}}\mu_{N}(m)\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}}\Delta_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell-1}}f(x)\,e(\phi_{m}(\hat{x}_{\ell})x_{\ell}+\psi_{m}(\hat{x}_{\ell}))\gg\delta^{3/2}N^{\ell}

for some
\phi_{m},\psi_{m}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}\to{\mathbb{T}}
.

By modifying the phase function
\psi_{m}
appropriately, we can assume that

(34)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}}\Delta_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell-1}}f(x)\,e(\phi_{m}(\hat{x}_{\ell})x_{\ell}+\psi_{m}(\hat{x}_{\ell}))\geq 0

for all
m
. Using the positivity property (
34
) as well as the pointwise bound

0\leq\mu_{N}(m)\leq\frac{1}{(2N+1)^{\ell-1}},

we remove the weight
\mu_{N}(m)
and extend the range of
m
to all of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}
so that

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}}\Delta_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell-1}}f(x)\,e(\phi_{m}(\hat{x}_{\ell})x_{\ell}+\psi_{m}(\hat{x}_{\ell}))\gg_{\ell}\delta^{3/2}N^{2\ell-1}.

We then
shift
m_{i}\mapsto m_{i}-x_{i}
for every
i\in[\ell-1]
. Under this change of variables,
\Delta_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell-1}}f(x)
becomes a product of
f(x)
and
\ell-1
functions, each of which does not depend on one of the coordinates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell-1}
. As a result, we obtain 1-bounded weights
b_{1,m},\ldots,b_{\ell,m}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, which are supported on
[\pm N]^{\ell-1}
and are identically 0 whenever
\max_{i}|m_{i}|>N
, as well as a phase
\tilde{\phi}_{m}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}}f(x)\,e(\tilde{\phi}_{m}(\hat{x}_{\ell})x_{\ell})b_{1,m}(\hat{x}_{1})\cdots b_{\ell,m}(\hat{x}_{\ell})\gg_{\ell}\delta^{3/2}N^{2\ell-1}.

The result then follows on pigeonholing in the
O_{\ell}(N^{\ell-1})
values
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}
for which the sum over
x
does not vanish and setting
b_{i}:=b_{i,m}
for the choice of
m
given by the pigeonhole principle.
∎

4.3.
Decomposition results and anti-uniformity

Having established an inverse theorem for the finitary box norm under consideration, we proceed to establish a version of the arithmetic regularity lemma for this norm. In what follows, we will use the following definitions.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
M,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
\varepsilon>0
. We say that
f\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is

(i)

M
-
structured

if

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}c_{i}\,e(\tilde{\phi}_{i}(\hat{x}_{\ell})x_{\ell})\,b_{i,1}(\hat{x}_{1})\cdots b_{i,\ell}(\hat{x}_{\ell})

for some weights
c_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying
\max\limits_{i\in[M]}|c_{i}|\leq M
, phase functions
\phi_{i}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{T}}
, and 1-bounded functions
b_{i,j}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
;

(ii)

(M,N)
-anti-uniform
if

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{x\in[\pm N]^{\ell}}f(x)\,g(x)\mathclose{}\right|\leq\frac{M}{N^{\ell/2^{\ell+1}}}\cdot\left\|g\right\|_{e_{1}[\pm N],\ldots,e_{\ell}[\pm N],e_{\ell}[\pm N]}

for any
g\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{C}}
supported on
[\pm N]^{\ell}
;

(iii)

(\varepsilon,N)
-uniform
if

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{e_{1}[\pm N],\ldots,e_{\ell}[\pm N],e_{\ell}[\pm N]}^{2^{\ell+1}}\leq\varepsilon N^{\ell};

(iv)

(\varepsilon,N)
-small
if
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}\leq\varepsilon
.

Proposition
4.6
can then be upgraded to the following decomposition result.

Proposition 4.8

(Arithmetic regularity lemma)
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
{\mathcal{F}}\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be increasing.
Then there exists
1\leq M\ll_{\varepsilon,{\mathcal{F}}}1
such that for every 1-bounded
f\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{C}}
supported on
[\pm N]^{\ell}
,
we have a decomposition

(35)

\displaystyle f=f_{\str}+f_{\sml}+f_{\unif},

where

(i)

f_{\str}
is
M
-structured and 1-bounded;

(ii)

f_{\sml}
is
(\varepsilon,N)
-small;

(iii)

f_{\unif}
is
(1/{\mathcal{F}}(M),N)
-uniform and supported on
[\pm N]^{\ell}
.
6
6

6

The assumption on the support of
f_{\unif}
is needed in the proof of Proposition
4.9
below to properly use the anti-uniformity assumption. It can be ensured because
f_{\unif}
is the difference of
f
and a conditional expectation of
f
onto some structured factor, both of which are supported on
[\pm N]^{\ell}
. Note that we cannot in general assume that
f_{\str}
is supported on
[\pm N]^{\ell}
: if
\ell=1
, then the structured term would be a bounded-degree trigonometric polynomial, and these are typically not supported on finite intervals.

Furthermore, all terms in (
35
) are 4-bounded.

Sketch of proof.

Proposition
4.8
can be proved just like a similar decomposition result for Gowers norms
[
30
, Proposition 2.7]
(except that we do not need our structured term to be “irrational” in any sense, as given by
[
30
, Theorem 1.2]
). The place of polynomial nilsequences of complexity
M
is taken by
M
-structured functions. An important property used in the proof is that these functions form a graded conjugation-closed algebra in the sense that if
f,g
are
M
-structured, then
\bar{f}
,
cf
,
f+g
, and
f\cdot g
are
M^{2}
-structured functions for all
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
|c|\leq M
.
∎

A simple argument based on the Cauchy-Schwarz and van der Corput inequalities shows that every
M
-structured function is
(O_{\ell}(M^{2}),N)
-anti-uniform, and so a priori anti-uniformity can be conceived as a weak notion of structure. The next result shows the converse and formalizes the heuristic that the classes of structured and anti-uniform functions are in fact the same.

Proposition 4.9

(Structured functions approximate anti-uniform functions)
.

Let
\varepsilon,A>0
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then there exists
M=O_{\varepsilon,A,\ell}(1)
such that if a 1-bounded
f\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is
(A,N)
-anti-uniform, then we can find a 1-bounded,
M
-structured
g\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|f-g\right\|_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
{\mathcal{F}}\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be an increasing function, both to be specified later.
By Proposition
4.8
, there exists
M=O_{\varepsilon,{\mathcal{F}}}(1)
and
a decomposition

\displaystyle f=f_{\str}+f_{\sml}+f_{\unif},

where

f_{\str},f_{\sml},f_{\unif}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{C}}
are
such that
f_{\str}
is 1-bounded and
M
-structured,
f_{\sml}
is
(\varepsilon_{0},N)
-small,
and
f_{\unif}
is
(1/{\mathcal{F}}(M),N)
-uniform.
Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|f-f_{\str}\right\|_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}^{2}\leq\left\|f_{\unif}\right\|_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}^{2}+O(\varepsilon_{0}).

To evaluate the term
\left\|f_{\unif}\right\|_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}^{2}
, we use the properties of
f,f_{\str},f_{\sml},f_{\unif}
. Expanding and using the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\unif}\right\|_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\left|\mathopen{}\left\langle f_{\unif},f\mathclose{}\right\rangle_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}\mathclose{}\right|+\mathopen{}\left|\mathopen{}\left\langle f_{\unif},f_{\sml}\mathclose{}\right\rangle_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}\mathclose{}\right|+\mathopen{}\left|\mathopen{}\left\langle f_{\unif},f_{\str}\mathclose{}\right\rangle_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}\mathclose{}\right|.

The first term is at most
A/{\mathcal{F}}(M)
by the anti-uniformity of
f
(here, we use the fact that
f_{\unif}
is supported on
[\pm N]^{\ell}
).
The second one can be bounded from above by
4\varepsilon_{0}
using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the 4-boundedness of
f_{\unif}
.
Lastly, the third term is at most
O_{\ell}(M^{2}/{\mathcal{F}}(M))
by the previously mentioned anti-uniformity of structured functions.
Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\unif}\right\|_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}^{2}

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}\varepsilon_{0}+\max(A,M^{2})/{\mathcal{F}}(M).

By choosing
{\mathcal{F}}
growing sufficiently fast with
\varepsilon_{0},A,\ell
, this is at most
O_{\ell}(\varepsilon_{0})
, and the result follows on taking
g:=f_{\str}
and

\varepsilon_{0}:=c_{\ell}\varepsilon
for some small
c_{\ell}>0
.
∎

4.4.
Topology of structured functions

Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and denote the space of
M
-structured functions on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
via
\Str_{M}
. Embedding
\Str_{M}
into
{\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}}
in the obvious way, we can endow
\Str_{M}
with the product topology.
We also consider the
parameter space

V_{M}:=((M\cdot{\mathbb{D}})\times{\mathbb{T}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}}\times({\mathbb{D}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{(\ell-1)}})^{\ell})^{M}.

Recalling that a
M
-structured function
\psi
takes the form

(36)

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}c_{i}\,e(\phi_{i}(\hat{x}_{\ell})x_{\ell})\,b_{i,1}(\hat{x}_{1})\cdots b_{i,\ell}(\hat{x}_{\ell})

for
|c_{i}|\leq M
,
b_{i,1},\ldots,b_{i,\ell}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}\to{\mathbb{D}}
and
\phi_{i}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell-1}\to{\mathbb{T}}
, we can define
\pi:V_{M}\to\Str_{M}
via

\displaystyle\pi((c_{i},\phi_{i},(b_{i,j})_{j\in[\ell]})_{i\in[M]})(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{M}c_{i}\,e(\phi_{i}(\hat{x}_{\ell})x_{\ell})\,b_{i,1}(\hat{x}_{1})\cdots b_{i,\ell}(\hat{x}_{\ell}).

This map is surjective by the very definition of
M
-structured functions, but it is not injective; for instance, permuting the indices
i\in[M]
in the domain does not affect the output.
We endow
V_{M}
with the product topology; then it is compact and metrizable, hence sequentially compact.

We will use the following fact.

Lemma 4.10

(Sequential compactness of structured functions)
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
(\psi_{m})_{m}\subseteq\Str_{M}
. Then there exists a subsequence
(\psi_{m_{k}})_{k}
and
\psi\in\Str_{M}
such that

\sup_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\|\psi_{m_{k}}-\psi\right\|_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}=0.

Proof.

Since
\pi
is surjective, for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists
\eta_{m}\in V_{M}
for which
\pi(\eta_{m})=\psi_{m}
. By the sequential compactness of
V_{M}
, we can find a subsequence
(\eta_{m_{k}})_{k}
that converges pointwise to some
\eta\in V_{M}
. Let
\psi:=\pi(\eta)
.
It is easy to verify that for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have
\psi_{m_{k}}\to\psi
in
L^{\infty}([\pm N]^{\ell})
, and hence also in
L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})
.
∎

We shall also require the following lemma.

Lemma 4.11

(Total boundedness of structured functions)
.

Let
M,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the set of
M
-structured, 1-bounded functions is totally bounded with respect to the
L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})
metric. More specifically, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite subset
{\mathcal{A}}_{M,N}\subseteq\Str_{M}
consisting of 1-bounded functions such that for any 1-bounded
\psi\in\Str_{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{A}}_{M,N}}\left\|\psi-\gamma\right\|_{L^{2}([\pm N]^{\ell})}\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Note that

\Str_{M,N}:=\{f|_{[\pm N]^{\ell}}\colon f\in\Str_{M},\;1\textrm{-bounded}\}

is a subset of the compact set
{\mathbb{D}}^{[\pm N]^{\ell}}
. The claim then follows from the fact that every subset of a compact set is totally bounded.
∎

4.5.
Ergodicity of structured extensions

We now move away from the finitary world in order to prove one more lemma. It will be used towards the end of the proof of Theorem
4.1
to ensure that the structured extension can be taken to be ergodic.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{Z}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a system
where
{\mathcal{Z}}:={\mathcal{Z}}_{\mu}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell},T_{\ell})
. Suppose that

{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathcal{I}}_{\mu}(T_{1})\vee\cdots\vee{\mathcal{I}}_{\mu}(T_{\ell-1})\vee{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,\mu}(T_{\ell}).

If
\mu=\int\mu_{x}\,d\mu(x)
is the ergodic disintegration of
\mu
with respect to the joint action of
T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}
, then for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
, we have

{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mu_{x}}={\mathcal{I}}_{\mu_{x}}(T_{1})\vee\cdots\vee{\mathcal{I}}_{\mu_{x}}(T_{\ell-1})\vee{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,\mu_{x}}(T_{\ell}).

Proof.

By (
13
), we have

f\bot{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mu}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell},T_{\ell})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell},T_{\ell},\mu}=0.

Hence, our assumption is equivalent to the following implication

f\,\bot\,{\mathcal{I}}_{\mu}(T_{1})\vee\cdots\vee{\mathcal{I}}_{\mu}(T_{\ell-1})\vee{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,\mu}(T_{\ell})\quad\implies\quad\lvert\!|\!|f|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell},T_{\ell},\mu}=0

This can be proved by arguing as in

(i)

(v)

Fixing a generic point

7

(vi)

Throughout, all implicit constants are allowed to depend on

Step 1: Approximating by finitary dual functions.

For any
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we may find a scale

for which

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{D}}\textbf{f}_{n}-{\mathcal{D}}_{N_{n,m}}\textbf{f}_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq 2^{-100(n+m)}.

Using the maximal ergodic theorem, we will show that for
\mu
-a.e.

\displaystyle F:=\sum_{n,m}2^{9(n+m)}\mathbf{1}_{E_{n,m}}

\displaystyle F^{*}(x):=\sup_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\E_{h\in[\pm N]^{\ell}}F(T^{h}x)

\displaystyle t\,\mu(F^{*}>t)\leq\left\|F\right\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}<\infty\quad\textrm{for\; every}\quad t>0.

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{\mathopen{}\left|\{h\in[\pm N]^{\ell}\colon T^{h}x\in E_{n,m}\}\mathclose{}\right|}{(2N+1)^{\ell}}\leq C_{x}2^{-9(n+m)}.

In other words, for every

Step 2: Applying the finitary inverse theorem.

Consider now the function

Step 5: Properties of the structured extension.

Note first that since

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\int_{X}{\mathcal{D}}\textbf{f}_{n}\;d\mu-\int_{Y}\widetilde{f}_{n}\;d\nu\mathclose{}\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\int_{X}{\mathcal{D}}\textbf{f}_{n}\;d\mu-\int_{Y}F_{n,q}\;d\nu\mathclose{}\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\to\infty}\lim_{l\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{h\in[\pm N_{l}]^{\ell}}({\mathcal{D}}\textbf{f}_{n}(T^{h}x_{0})-F_{n,q}(S^{h}y_{0}))\mathclose{}\right|

Repeating everything with

\displaystyle\int_{Y|_{\ell+1}}\mathopen{}\left|\tilde{S}_{\ell}(\tilde{S}_{\ell}G_{i^{\prime},n^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\cdot\overline{G}_{i^{\prime},n^{\prime},q^{\prime}})-\tilde{S}_{\ell}G_{i^{\prime},n^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\cdot\overline{G}_{i^{\prime},n^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\mathclose{}\right|^{2}\;d\tilde{\nu}=0,

Lastly, we establish that the extension can be chosen to be ergodic.

5.
Limiting formulas

In this section, we establish Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
via a variant of the by now standard degree lowering argument. That is, assuming that the averages in (
18
) and (
19
) are controlled by the seminorm
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}
for some
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
(which follows from our assumptions), we will iteratively lower
s_{1},s_{2}
one by one until we reach
s_{1}=s_{2}=1
. We will then pass to an appropriate magic extension of the system and show that the claimed limiting formulas hold in the magic extension.

The proofs of Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
do not differ much; therefore we only give full details of the proof of the former and provide brief explanations of modifications needed to obtain the latter. The only meaningful difference in the proof of
Theorem

3.4
is the use of
Proposition

5.4
below. Given this, the reader should have no problem in adapting our argument to recover the full proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Moreover, we work with Cesàro averages only,
the arguments adapt straightforwardly to handle averages
along arbitrary Følner sequences in
{\mathbb{N}}
.

This section is organized as follows. First, we prove Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
under the additional assumption that
f_{2}
is a nonergodic eigenfunction of
T_{2}
or
T_{2}T_{1}^{-1}
, in which case the proofs become significantly simpler. These results, labeled as Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
, will serve as the base cases in the proofs of Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
respectively. Next, we show that various linear averages along commuting transformations admit optimal box seminorm control. Since the inductive step of the degree lowering proceeds by comparing the average along
a(n)
with an average along
n
, being able to control the linear average by a box seminorm of optimal degree plays a key part in reducing the degree of the box seminorm controlling the average along
a(n)
. Subsequently, we state versions of two standard technical lemmas, Proposition
5.6
(stashing) and Proposition
5.7
(dual-difference interchange), required for the forthcoming degree lowering argument. Section
5.4
is then dedicated to the inductive step of degree lowering, by far the main and most technically challenging argument in Section
5
. We conclude with deriving Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
.

5.1.
Base case of degree lowering:
f_{2}
is a nonergodic eigenfunction

We start with establishing the base cases of Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
, i.e., the variants of both results in which

is a nonergodic eigenfunction of
T_{2}
or
T_{2}T_{1}^{-1}

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

be a sequence that admits box seminorm control and equidistributes on nilsystems (see
Definition

3.1
). Then the identity (
21
) holds for every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and all functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
f_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T_{2})\cup{\mathcal{E}}(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})
.

Proof.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
be a system and
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Suppose first that
f_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}(T_{2})
.
Since
a
admits box seminorm control, there exists
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\;d\mu=0

whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{s,T_{1}}=0
. By
[
10
, Proposition 3.1]
(a nonergodic variant of the Host-Kra decomposition
[
33
]
), for every
\varepsilon>0
we can decompose
f_{1}=g_{1}+g_{2}+g_{3}
into functions
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
for which:

(i)

g_{1}(T_{1}^{n}x)=\psi_{x}(n)
is an
(s-1)
-step nilsequence for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
,

(ii)

\left\|g_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\varepsilon
,

(iii)

\lvert\!|\!|g_{3}|\!|\!\rvert_{s,T_{1}}=0
.

Hence, it suffices to prove the claim with
g_{1}
in place of
f_{1}
.

Since
f_{2}
is a nonergodic eigenfunction of
T_{2}
we get by (
14
) that there exists
a
T
-invariant set
E\in{\mathcal{X}}
and a measurable
T
-invariant function

such that
for every
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\mu
-a.e.

f_{2}(T_{2}^{n}x)=\mathbf{1}_{E}(x)\,e(\phi(x)n)\,f_{2}(x).

Hence, for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mu
-a.e.

, we have

\displaystyle\E_{n\in[N]}g_{1}(T_{1}^{a(n)}x)\cdot f_{2}(T_{2}^{a(n)}x)=(f_{2}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{E})(x)\cdot\E_{n\in[N]}\psi_{x}({a(n)})\cdot e(\phi(x)a(n)).

On taking

, the assumption that

equidistributes on nilsystems gives

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}g_{1}(T_{1}^{a(n)}x)\cdot f_{2}(T_{2}^{a(n)}x)

\displaystyle=(f_{2}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{E})(x)\cdot\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}\psi_{x}(a(n))\cdot e(\phi(x)a(n))

\displaystyle=(f_{2}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{E})(x)\cdot\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}\psi_{x}(n)\cdot e(\phi(x)n)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}g_{1}(T_{1}^{n}x)\cdot f_{2}(T_{2}^{n}x).

The claimed identity (

follows, with

.

If

to get

\displaystyle\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\;d\mu=\int f_{1}\cdot T_{1}^{-a(n)}f_{0}\cdot(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{a(n)}f_{2}\;d\mu

.

5.2.
Optimal seminorm control for linear averages

Much like Proposition
5.1
, Theorem
3.4
is proved by comparing averages along
a(n)
with averages along

. A key fact about the former, proved by Host
[

, is that they admit optimal box seminorm control. The existence of the limit has been proved by Tao
[

]
beforehand, and it is not really needed for the inequality below if one is willing to replace limit by limsup.

Proposition 5.3

(Optimal seminorm control for linear averages
[
32
, Proposition 1]
)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a system. For all 1-bounded
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|\E_{n\in[N]}T_{1}^{n}f_{1}\cdots T_{\ell}^{n}f_{\ell}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\lvert\!|\!|f_{\ell}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{\ell}T_{1}^{-1},\ldots,T_{\ell}T_{\ell-1}^{-1},T_{\ell}}.

For the proof of Theorem
3.4
, we will also need a variant of the previous result that covers the expressions

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{k!n}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{k!n}f_{2}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|.

Unfortunately, the seminorm bounds we get for the inside average by combining Proposition
5.3
with the scaling property of box seminorms introduce a multiplicative factor on the right-hand side that blows up when we take
k\to\infty
. However, we can get the following soft quantitative result that suffices for our purposes (we will only use the case
\ell=2
).

Proposition 5.4

(Optimal seminorm control for linear averages, II)
.

Let
\ell\geq 2
be an integer and
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a system. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there
exists
\delta>0
(depending on
\varepsilon
and the system) such that for all 1-bounded
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f_{\ell}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{\ell}T_{1}^{-1},\ldots,T_{\ell}T_{\ell-1}^{-1},T_{\ell}}\leq\delta\quad\implies\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|\E_{n\in[N]}T_{1}^{k!n}f_{1}\cdots T_{\ell}^{k!n}f_{\ell}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\varepsilon.

Remark
.

The important thing for us is that
\delta
does not depend on the functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}
as long as they are
1
-bounded.

We will prove Proposition
5.4
by first establishing a qualitative version and then using the usual technique of transferring qualitative results to soft quantitative ones. To perform the second of these steps, we first need to know the norm convergence of the relevant averages. This is provided by the result below.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a system and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Then the following iterated limit exists in
L^{2}(\mu)
:

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}T_{1}^{k!n}f_{1}\cdots T_{\ell}^{k!n}f_{\ell}.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\ell\geq 2
; we will reduce by induction to the case
\ell=1
, and then resolve the base case via the spectral theorem.

By Proposition
5.3
and the scaling property of box seminorms (property (
v
) in
Section

2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\|\E_{n\in[N]}T_{1}^{k!n}f_{1}\cdots T_{\ell}^{k!n}f_{\ell}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0

whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f_{\ell}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{\ell}T_{1}^{-1},\ldots,T_{\ell}T_{\ell-1}^{-1},T_{\ell}}=0
(the scaling step fails for
\ell=1
). Passing to an appropriate magic extension (which exists by Proposition
2.8
), we can assume that our system is magic with respect to
T_{\ell}T_{1}^{-1},\ldots,T_{\ell}T_{\ell-1}^{-1},T_{\ell}
. Hence, it suffices to assume that
f_{\ell}
is measurable with respect to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(T_{\ell}T_{1}^{-1},\ldots,T_{\ell}T_{\ell-1}^{-1},T_{\ell})={\mathcal{I}}(T_{\ell}T_{1}^{-1})\vee\cdots\vee{\mathcal{I}}(T_{\ell}T_{\ell-1}^{-1})\vee{\mathcal{I}}(T_{\ell}).

By an

approximation argument, we can take

, where

for

and
g_{\ell}\in I(T_{\ell})
. Rearranging terms, it thus suffices to show the (double) convergence of

\displaystyle\E_{n\in[N]}T_{1}^{k!n}(f_{1}g_{1})\cdots T_{\ell-1}^{k!n}(f_{\ell-1}g_{\ell-1}).

We have thus reduced the length of the average from
\ell
to
\ell-1
; an iteration of this procedure brings us to

. In this case,

convergence is easy to check using the spectral theorem. Indeed, it suffices to show that for every

, the limit

(53)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N]}e(k!n\alpha)=\lim_{k\to\infty}{\bf 1}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(k!\alpha)

exists. For
\alpha\notin{\mathbb{Q}}
, the right-hand side is
0
while for

it is
1
.
8
8

8

The place of
k!
can be taken any sequence
c_{k}
such that

exists for every

, i.e., for every

either

for all large enough

or

for all large enough

.

∎

We now combine all the ingredients to prove Proposition

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

Just like in the proof of Lemma

whenever

5.3.
Preliminary lemmas

Before performing the inductive step of degree lowering, we present two tricks that we shall use abundantly in the proof of Proposition
5.8
below. While both of them are by now a standard part of the degree lowering toolbox, they have not appeared in the ergodic literature in this particular form before, therefore we opted to state the precise statements used in this work. Their proofs are completely straightforward modifications of existing arguments.

Proposition 5.6

(Stashing)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a system,
a_{k}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
f_{0},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
be 1-bounded. If

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a_{k}(n)}f_{1}\cdots T_{\ell}^{a_{k}(n)}f_{\ell}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|\geq\delta

for some
\delta>0
, then there exist sequences of integers
(N_{k,l})_{k,l}
(increasing as
l\to\infty
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
) and
(k_{i})_{i}
(increasing as
i\to\infty
) as well as a function

\displaystyle F_{\ell}:=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{l\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N_{k_{i},l}]}T_{\ell}^{-a_{k_{i}}(n)}\overline{f}_{0}\cdot(T_{1}T_{\ell}^{-1})^{a_{k_{i}}(n)}\overline{f}_{1}\cdots(T_{\ell-1}T_{\ell}^{-1})^{a_{k_{i}}(n)}\overline{f}_{\ell-1}

(where the limit is a weak limit) such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a_{k}(n)}f_{1}\cdots T_{\ell-1}^{a_{k}(n)}f_{\ell-1}\cdot T_{\ell}^{a_{k}(n)}F_{\ell}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|\geq\delta^{2}.

Moreover, if
a_{k}=a
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
(N_{k,l})
is chosen independently of
k
.

The proof of Proposition
5.6
is completely analogous to the one of
[
22
, Proposition 4.2]
except we need to use weak compactness twice, first to obtain the sequence
(N_{k,l})_{k,l}
for which the weak limit

\displaystyle A_{k}:=\lim_{l\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N_{k,l}]}T_{\ell}^{-a_{k}(n)}\overline{f}_{0}\cdot(T_{1}T_{\ell}^{-1})^{a_{k}(n)}\overline{f}_{1}\cdots(T_{\ell-1}T_{\ell}^{-1})^{a_{k}(n)}\overline{f}_{\ell-1}

exists, and then to find the sequence
(k_{i})_{i}
such that
A_{k_{i}}\to F_{\ell}
weakly as
i\to\infty
.

Proposition 5.7

(Dual-difference interchange)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell})
be a system and
{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mu)
be a
T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell}
-invariant collection of 1-bounded functions closed under multiplication and complex conjugation. For a collection of 1-bounded functions
(f_{n,k})_{n,k}\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mu)
and a sequence of integers
(N_{k,l})_{k,l}
increasing as

for every

, let

\displaystyle f:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{l\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N_{k,l}]}f_{n,k},

where the limit is a weak limit. Suppose that the integrals below are real and

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}\E_{h\in[H]^{\ell}}\int\Delta_{T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell};h}f\cdot g_{h}\;d\mu\geq\delta

for some

and

. Then the integrals below are real and

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}\E_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]^{\ell}}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{l\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N_{k,l}]}\int\Delta_{T_{1},\ldots,T_{\ell};h-h^{\prime}}f_{n,k}\cdot g_{h,h^{\prime}}\;d\mu\geq\delta^{2^{\ell}}

for some
g_{h,h^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{G}}
.

The proof of Proposition
5.7
follows very closely the proof of
[
22
, Proposition 4.3]
. If needed, one can obtain a precise description of the term

(see e.g.
[
22
, Proposition 4.3]
and
[
26
, Proposition 5.6]
for the formula); they were required for past applications of dual-difference interchange in
[
22
,
25
,
26
]
but are not needed in this paper.

5.4.
Inductive step of degree lowering

With all the preliminaries out of the way, we are ready to carry out the inductive step in the proof of Theorems
3.3
, captured in the result below.

Proposition 5.8

(Degree lowering)
.

Let
a\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence that admits box seminorm control and equidistributes on nilsystems. Let
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Suppose that for every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and all functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have (
18
) whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}=0
.
Then for every system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and all functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have (
18
) whenever
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2}}=0
.

Proof.

If
s_{1}=s_{2}=1
, then there is nothing to show, so we assume without loss of generality that
s_{1}+s_{2}\geq 3
. The claimed seminorm control then follows on iterating the following claim as long as
s_{1}+s_{2}\geq 3
.

Claim 5.9
.

Suppose that for any system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,

(54)

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|>0

implies

\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}>0.

Then also

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-1)}}>0\quad\textrm{whenever}\quad s_{2}\geq 2

and

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times(s_{1}-1)},T_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}>0\quad\textrm{whenever}\quad s_{1}\geq 2.

It suffices to prove the second part of the claim. Indeed, on composing the integral in (
54
) with
T_{1}^{-a(n)}
, we have

(55)

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{1}\cdot T_{1}^{-a(n)}f_{0}\cdot(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{a(n)}f_{2}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|>0.

Hence, the second part of the claim follows from the first part on replacing the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
with
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1}^{-1},T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})
and noticing that in the reparametrization (
55
),
f_{2}
is a nonergodic eigenfunction of the transformation
T_{2}T_{1}^{-1}
acting on it.

We thus fix a system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and 1-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
satisfying (
54
). We also assume Claim
5.9
holds for some fixed
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
s_{2}\geq 2
.

In several places, we will first run the argument in the simple case
s_{1}=1
,
s_{2}=2
before delving into the general case. This will allow us to convey the main ideas of the proof without getting bogged down in distracting technicalities.

Step 1: Reducing to the ergodic case.

At a later stage of our argument, we will pass from the original system to its structured extension constructed in Theorem
4.1
. Since the extension is constructed for ergodic systems, it is desirable to work inside ergodic systems.
Let
\mu=\int\mu_{x}\;d\mu(x)
be the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
with respect to the joint action of
T_{1},T_{2}
.
Then our assumption and Fatou’s lemma give

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}f_{2}\;d\mu_{x}\mathclose{}\right|>0

for all
x
in a positive-measure set
E\in{\mathcal{X}}
. Thus, if we establish the desired conclusion
\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-1)};\mu_{x}}>0
for all
x\in E
, then it follows from (
11
) that

\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-1)};\mu}^{2^{s_{1}+s_{2}-1}}=\int\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-1)};\mu_{x}}^{2^{s_{1}+s_{2}-1}}\;d\mu(x)>0.

Hence, we can assume without loss of generality that
\mu
is ergodic.

Step 2: Replacing
{f}_{2}
by a structured function.

Our next step is to replace
f_{2}
by a certain structured function that keeps track of the shape of the average under consideration. By Proposition
5.6
, we can find an increasing integer sequence
(N_{l})_{l}
such that on setting

(56)

\displaystyle F_{2}:=\lim_{l\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N_{l}]}T_{2}^{-a(n)}\overline{f}_{0}\cdot(T_{1}T_{2}^{-1})^{a(n)}\overline{f}_{1}

(where the limit is a weak limit), we obtain the lower bound

(57)

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}{f}_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}F_{2}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|>0.

Step 3: Passing to a structured extension.

At this point, we pass from our original system to a structured extension on which a certain seminorm admits a nice inverse theorem. For the remainder of the proof, set

{\mathcal{Z}}:={\mathcal{Z}}(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1},T_{2},T_{2}).

By Theorem
4.1
, there exists an extension
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}},\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{T}_{1},\widetilde{T}_{2})
of
(X,{\mathcal{Z}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
such that

(58)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}:={\mathcal{Z}}(\widetilde{T}_{2}\widetilde{T}_{1}^{-1},\widetilde{T}_{2},\widetilde{T}_{2})={\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{T}_{2}\widetilde{T}_{1}^{-1})\vee{\mathcal{Z}}_{1}(\widetilde{T}_{2}).

Unfortunately,
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}},\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{T}_{1},\widetilde{T}_{2})
does not extend the original system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
. To circumvent this delicate technicality, we pass to a joint extension of both systems.
Since
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
and
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}},\widetilde{\mu},\widetilde{T}_{1},\widetilde{T}_{2})
have
{\mathcal{Z}}
as a common factor, we let
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},S_{2})
be their relatively independent joining over
{\mathcal{Z}}
, i.e.,
Y:=X\times\widetilde{X}
,
S_{j}:=T_{j}\times\widetilde{T}_{j}
for
j=1,2
, and the measure
\nu
on

satisfies

(59)

\int f\otimes\widetilde{f}\,d\nu=\int\E(f|{\mathcal{Z}})\cdot\E(\widetilde{f}|{\mathcal{Z}})\,d\mu

for all
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
\widetilde{f}\in L^{\infty}(\widetilde{\mu})
, where by abuse of notation we let
\E(\widetilde{f}|{\mathcal{Z}})
be the orthogonal projection on

under the factor map
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
.
We then define
\hat{{\mathcal{Z}}}:={\mathcal{Z}}(S_{2}S_{1}^{-1},S_{2},S_{2})
in analogy with
{\mathcal{Z}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}
.
Setting
\hat{f}_{j}:=f_{j}\otimes 1
for

and
\hat{F}_{2}:=F_{2}\otimes 1
, we have

\displaystyle\int\hat{f}_{0}\cdot S_{1}^{a(n)}\hat{f}_{1}\cdot S_{2}^{a(n)}\hat{F}_{2}\;d\nu=\int{f}_{0}\cdot T_{1}^{a(n)}f_{1}\cdot T_{2}^{a(n)}F_{2}\;d\mu

for every

. Combining this with (

), we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int\hat{f}_{0}\cdot S_{1}^{a(n)}\hat{f}_{1}\cdot S_{2}^{a(n)}\hat{F}_{2}\;d\nu\mathclose{}\right|>0.

At this point, we use our assumption in
5.9
to get

(60)

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|\hat{F}_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(S_{2}S_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},S_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}>0.

Hence, we have transferred the original control from an arbitrary function
f_{2}
to the much more structured extension
(Y,{\mathcal{Y}},\nu,S_{1},S_{2})
and the function
\hat{F}_{2}
.

Interlude: Auxiliary seminorm control in the baby case
s_{1}=1
,
s_{2}=2
.

Our next objective is to use the estimate (
60
) together with the inductive formula for box seminorms to reduce to the case where we can apply Proposition
4.2
for the seminorm
\lvert\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{1}S_{2}^{-1},S_{2},S_{2}}
. After invoking the inverse theorem, we aim to reduce our average to one that can be handled using Proposition

. An application of Propositions

and

will then give us an auxiliary control over our original average by
\lvert\!|\!|f_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1},T_{1}T_{2}^{-1}}

The maneuvers outlined above can be greatly simplified in the baby case

,
s_{2}=2,
as contrasted with the case
s_{1}+s_{2}\geq 4
. We therefore take a pause to explain how the aforementioned steps can be carried out in this baby case. If
s_{1}=1
,

\widetilde{F}_{2}:=\E(F_{2}|{\mathcal{Z}})\circ\pi
so that

(61)

\displaystyle\E(\hat{F}_{2}|\hat{{\mathcal{Z}}})=\E(F_{2}|{\mathcal{Z}})\otimes 1=1\otimes\widetilde{F}_{2}.

This follows from a simple but important property of the system

{\mathcal{Z}}
-measurable

(62)

\displaystyle g\otimes 1=1\otimes(g\circ\pi)

in

(indeed, one can immediately verify using (

Using the identity
\lvert\!|\!|\hat{F}_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{2}S_{1}^{-1},S_{2},S_{2}}=\lvert\!|\!|\E(\hat{F}_{2}|\hat{{\mathcal{Z}}})|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{2}S_{1}^{-1},S_{2},S_{2}}

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|\widetilde{F}_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{\widetilde{T}_{2}\widetilde{T}_{1}^{-1},\widetilde{T}_{2},\widetilde{T}_{2}}>0,

\displaystyle\int\widetilde{F}_{2}\cdot\overline{\widetilde{g}}\cdot\overline{\widetilde{\chi}}\;d\widetilde{\mu}>0.

Lifting back to

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int{\hat{f}_{0}}\cdot S_{1}^{a(n)}{\hat{f}_{1}}\cdot S_{2}^{a(n)}\bigl(\hat{g}\cdot\hat{\chi}\bigr)\;d\nu\mathclose{}\right|>0.

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int{\hat{f}_{0}}\cdot S_{1}^{n}\bigl({\hat{f}_{1}}\cdot\hat{g}\bigr)\cdot S_{2}^{n}\hat{\chi}\;d\nu\mathclose{}\right|>0.

\lvert\!|\!|\hat{f}_{1}\cdot\hat{g}|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{1},S_{1}S_{2}^{-1}}>0

Step 4: Applying the inverse theorem.

These maneuvers become considerably more involved when

\displaystyle F_{2,h}

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times(s_{1}-1)},T_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-2)};h}{F}_{2},

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{2,h}

\displaystyle:=\E(F_{2,h}|{\mathcal{Z}})\circ\pi,

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{2,h}

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{(S_{2}S_{1}^{-1})^{\times(s_{1}-1)},S_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-2)};h}\hat{F}_{2}=F_{2,h}\otimes 1.

\displaystyle\E(\hat{F}_{2,h}|\hat{{\mathcal{Z}}})=\E(F_{2,h}|{\mathcal{Z}})\otimes 1=1\otimes\widetilde{F}_{2,h},

By the inductive formula for box seminorms, the equivalence (

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|\hat{F}_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(S_{2}S_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},S_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}^{2^{s_{1}+s_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{H\to\infty}\E_{h\in[H]^{s_{1}+s_{2}-3}}\lvert\!|\!|\hat{F}_{2,h}|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{2}S_{1}^{-1},S_{2},S_{2}}^{8}

\displaystyle=\lim_{H\to\infty}\E_{h\in[H]^{s_{1}+s_{2}-3}}\lvert\!|\!|\E(\hat{F}_{2,h}|\hat{{\mathcal{Z}}})|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{2}S_{1}^{-1},S_{2},S_{2}}^{8}

\displaystyle=\lim_{H\to\infty}\E_{h\in[H]^{s_{1}+s_{2}-3}}\lvert\!|\!|\widetilde{F}_{2,h}|\!|\!\rvert_{\widetilde{T}_{2}\widetilde{T}_{1}^{-1},\widetilde{T}_{2},\widetilde{T}_{2}}^{8},

By Proposition

(we note that a purely qualitative inverse theorem does not suffice here), we can then find 1-bounded functions
\widetilde{g}_{h}\in{\mathcal{I}}(\widetilde{T}_{2}\widetilde{T}_{1}^{-1})

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}\E_{h\in[H]^{s_{1}+s_{2}-3}}{\int\widetilde{F}_{2,h}\cdot\overline{\widetilde{g}_{h}}\cdot\overline{\widetilde{\chi}_{h}}\;d\widetilde{\mu}}>0.

Setting

\hat{\chi}_{h}:=1\otimes\widetilde{\chi}_{h}\in{\mathcal{E}}(S_{2})

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}\E_{h\in[H]^{s_{1}+s_{2}-3}}{\int\hat{F}_{2,h}\cdot\overline{\hat{g}_{h}}\cdot\overline{\hat{\chi}_{h}}\;d\nu}>0

on lifting to

\hat{F}_{2,h}
in the integral).

Step 5: Dual-difference interchange.

By Proposition
5.7
(dual-difference interchange) applied to the function
f:=\hat{F}_{2}
and the collection

(which is clearly

-invariant as well as closed under multiplication and complex conjugation),
we have

(63)

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}\E_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]^{s_{1}+s_{2}-3}}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int\hat{f}_{0,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot S_{1}^{a(n)}\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot S_{2}^{a(n)}\bigl(\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{\chi}_{h,h^{\prime}}\bigr)\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|>0,

where for all
j=0,1
and
h,h^{\prime}
, we set

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{j,h,h^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{(S_{2}S_{1}^{-1})^{\times(s_{1}-1)},S_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-2)};h-h^{\prime}}\hat{f}_{j},

while
\hat{\chi}_{h,h^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{E}}(S_{2})
and
\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}\in I(S_{2}S_{1}^{-1})
are some 1-bounded functions.

Step 6: Invoking Proposition

While not immediate, it is possible to use Proposition
5.1
to (

-invariance of

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}\E_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]^{s_{1}+s_{2}-3}}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int\hat{f}_{0,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot S_{1}^{a(n)}\bigl(\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}\bigr)\cdot S_{2}^{a(n)}\hat{\chi}_{h,h^{\prime}}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|>0.

Just like in the baby case above, the fact that

is a nonergodic eigenfunction of

allows us to apply Proposition
5.1
to the family of averages over

, yielding

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}\E_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]^{s_{1}+s_{2}-3}}\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int\hat{f}_{0,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot S_{1}^{n}\bigl(\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}\bigr)\cdot S_{2}^{n}\hat{\chi}_{h,h^{\prime}}\;d\mu\mathclose{}\right|>0,

By the
\ell=2
case of Proposition
5.3

(64)

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}\E_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]^{s_{1}+s_{2}-3}}\lvert\!|\!|\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{1},S_{1}S_{2}^{-1}}>0.

Step 7: Auxiliary seminorm control.

Since
\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}
is a multiplicative derivative of
\hat{f}_{1}
, we would like to apply the inductive formula for box seminorms to control (
64
) by a seminorm of
\hat{f}_{1}
. To make this work, we need to remove the superfluous term
\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}
’s first, which can be achieved with the help of its invariance property. Indeed, for every

, we can write

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{1},S_{1}S_{2}^{-1}}^{4}=\lim_{H\to\infty}\E_{h^{\prime\prime}\in[H]}\left\|\E(\Delta_{S_{1};h^{\prime\prime}}\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot\Delta_{S_{1};h^{\prime\prime}}\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}|{\mathcal{I}}(S_{1}S_{2}^{-1}))\right\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}^{2}.

Since
\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}
are 1-bounded and

-invariant, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\E(\Delta_{S_{1};h^{\prime\prime}}\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot\Delta_{S_{1};h^{\prime\prime}}\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}|{\mathcal{I}}(S_{1}S_{2}^{-1}))\right\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\leq\left\|\E(\Delta_{S_{1};h^{\prime\prime}}\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}|{\mathcal{I}}(S_{1}S_{2}^{-1}))\right\|_{L^{2}(\nu)},

implying that

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}\cdot\hat{g}_{h,h^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{1},S_{1}S_{2}^{-1}}\leq\lvert\!|\!|\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{1},S_{1}S_{2}^{-1}}.

In conjunction with (
64
), this bound gives

\displaystyle\liminf_{H\to\infty}\E_{h,h^{\prime}\in[H]^{s_{1}+s_{2}-3}}\lvert\!|\!|\hat{f}_{1,h,h^{\prime}}|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{1},S_{1}S_{2}^{-1}}>0.

By
[

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|\hat{f}_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{S_{1},(S_{1}S_{2}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},S_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-2)}}>0.

Projecting down to

gives

(65)

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|{f}_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1},(T_{1}T_{2}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-2)}}>0.

Step 8: Seminorm smoothing–passing to the magic extension.

The outcome of our maneuvers so far can be summarized as follows: from controlling our average by the seminorm

\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times s_{2}}}

of degree

\lvert\!|\!|f_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{T_{1},(T_{1}T_{2}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-2)}}

of degree
s_{1}+s_{2}-1
. The caveat is that the transformations appearing in this new seminorm are not of the desired form: specifically, the presence of
s_{2}-2

poses an issue, as no reasonable reparametrization of the original average makes

act on

. Hence, we cannot simply invoke symmetry. That being said, we can use this auxiliary control to obtain control of our average by

\lvert\!|\!|f_{2}|\!|\!\rvert_{(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},T_{2}^{\times(s_{2}-1)}}.

A lot of the maneuvers involved in this process will be similar to those already performed, therefore we will abbreviate some of the discussion.

Let
(X^{*},{\mathcal{X}}^{*},\mu^{*},T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
be a magic extension of the system
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{1},T_{2})
with respect to the transformations
T_{1},T_{1}T_{2}^{-1}
, meaning that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}^{*}:={\mathcal{Z}}(T_{1}^{*},T_{1}^{*}{T_{2}^{*}}^{-1})={\mathcal{I}}(T_{1}^{*})\vee{\mathcal{I}}(T_{1}^{*}{T_{2}^{*}}^{-1})

(it exists by Proposition
2.8
).
We lift
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}
to

. Then

(66)

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f^{*}_{0}\cdot(T_{1}^{*})^{a(n)}f^{*}_{1}\cdot(T_{2}^{*})^{a(n)}f^{*}_{2}\;d\mu^{*}\mathclose{}\right|>0.

Arguing as in Step 2, we invoke Proposition
5.6
to find an increasing integer sequence

such that on defining

\displaystyle F_{1}^{*}:=\lim_{l\to\infty}\E_{n\in[N_{l}]}(T_{1}^{*})^{-a(n)}\overline{f_{0}^{*}}\cdot(T_{2}^{*}{T_{1}^{*}}^{-1})^{a(n)}\overline{f^{*}_{2}}

as a weak limit much like
F_{2}
before, we deduce from the auxiliary estimate (

(67)

\displaystyle\lvert\!|\!|F^{*}_{1}|\!|\!\rvert_{T^{*}_{1},(T^{*}_{1}{T^{*}_{2}}^{-1})^{\times s_{1}},(T^{*}_{2})^{\times(s_{2}-2)}}>0.

Interlude: Transferring control to
f_{2}
in the baby case
s_{1}=1
,
s_{2}=2

As we approach the end of the proof, the forthcoming maneuvers become once again significantly simpler whenever

,

,

2.9

g_{1}^{*}\in I(T_{1}^{*})
and

Expanding the definition of

, we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int f^{*}_{0}\cdot(T_{1}^{*})^{a(n)}\bigl(g_{1}^{*}\cdot g_{2}^{*}\bigr)\cdot(T^{*}_{2})^{a(n)}f^{*}_{2}\;d\mu^{*}\mathclose{}\right|>0.

The invariance properties of

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{n\in[N]}\int(f^{*}_{0}\cdot g_{1}^{*})\cdot(T^{*}_{2})^{a(n)}(f^{*}_{2}\cdot g_{2}^{*})\;d\mu^{*}\mathclose{}\right|>0.

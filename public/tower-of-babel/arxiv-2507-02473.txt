Popescu-Rohrlich box fraction of dimensionally restricted nonlocality and secure quantum key distribution
Chellasamy Jebarathinam

Abstract
With a measure of dimensionally restricted nonlocality in the bipartite Bell scenario with two inputs and two outputs, a different decomposition of any nonsignaling box that is a convex mixture of a single Popescu-Rohrlich box and a Bell-local box, with the measure being zero, is introduced. It is shown that any nonsignaling box shared by Alice and Bob that has dimensionally restricted nonlocality contains secret correlations against any third party, Eve, who is also dimensionally restricted. In this context, for the specific Bell scenario considered, it is demonstrated that the Popescu-Rohrlich box fraction can be used as a resource for secure quantum key distribution, even if entanglement is not certified.

Introduction:-
The nonlocality of quantum mechanics (QM), as demonstrated by the violation of a Bell inequality
Bell 1964
, can also be studied in the framework of generalized nonsignaling theories
Barrett et al. 2005a
;
Masanes et al. 2006
. This framework of quantum nonlocality underpins black-box information processing tasks
Brunner et al. 2014
, such as device-independent quantum key distribution
Barrett et al. 2005b
;
Acín et al. 2006
.

In the framework of generalized nonsignaling theories, bipartite states are not given by vectors in a Hilbert space but by bipartite joint probability distributions; i.e. probabilities of a pair of results (outputs) given a pair of measurements (inputs). In other words, quantum correlations will be
replaced by more general “boxes” ( i.e. input-output devices).
Here, we shall focus on the simplest possible scenario,
namely, the case of two possible measurements for each party
(inputs
x,y\in\{0,1\}
); each measurement providing a binary
result (outputs
a,b\in\{0,1\}
). In this case, a box is described by a set of
16
joint probability distributions

P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})
.

The set of Bell-local boxes which can be produced by using a classical state (a.k.a. local hidden variable or shared randomness) forms a polytope, which has
16
vertices (called deterministic boxes),

P^{\alpha\beta\gamma\epsilon}_{D}(ab|A_{x}B_{y})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}1,&a=\alpha x\oplus\beta\\
&b=\gamma y\oplus\epsilon\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(1)

Here,
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
and
\oplus
denotes
addition modulo
2
. The local polytope is itself embedded in a
larger polytope, the nonsignaling polytope, which contains all the boxes compatible with the nonsignaling principle. It
has
8
nonlocal vertices (called Popescu-Rohrlich (PR)
boxes),

\displaystyle P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{PR}(ab|A_{x}B_{y})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\frac{1}{2},&a\oplus b=x\cdot y\oplus\alpha x\oplus\beta y\oplus\gamma\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{array}\right.

which are all symmetries of the PR
box,
P_{PR}=P^{000}_{PR}

Popescu and Rohrlich 1994
. The set of boxes attainable by QM also forms a convex body, although not a polytope. The quantum set is strictly larger than the local polytope – quantum correlations can be Bell nonlocal – but strictly smaller than the nonsignalling polytope.

Any Bell-local box in the given scenario satisfies the complete set of
Bell-type inequalities
Werner and Wolf 2001
, on the other hand, any given Bell nonlocal box violates one of these inequalities. For the simplest scenario, the Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt (CHSH) inequality

Clauser et al. 1969
and its symmetries, which are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}:=(-1)^{\gamma}\braket{A_{0}B_{0}}+(-1)^{\beta\oplus\gamma}\braket{A_{0}B_{1}}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\alpha\oplus\gamma}\braket{A_{1}B_{0}}+(-1)^{\alpha\oplus\beta\oplus\gamma\oplus 1}\braket{A_{1}B_{1}}\leq 2,

(4)

form the complete set, where
\braket{A_{x}B_{y}}=\sum_{ab}(-1)^{a\oplus b}P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})
.
Quantum correlations violate the
CHSH inequality up to
2\sqrt{2}

Cirel’son 1980
.

Dimensionally restricted nonlocality:-
Bell nonlocality is shown against any classical state,
\lambda
, whose dimension is not limited
Donohue and Wolfe 2015
. Thus, quantum Bell nonlocality implies nonlocality independently of the dimension of the state used inside the box. Here, we consider dimensionally restricted nonlocality, i.e., nonlocality shown against any classical state
\lambda
whose dimension is limited to the number of measurement results. Dimensionally restricted quantum nonlocality has been studied in Ref.
Jebarathinam et al. 2024
as a means of certifying global coherence beyond entanglement.

In the following, a different decomposition of any nonsignaling box is introduced to capture dimensionally restricted nonlocality with the PR-box fraction. To this end, a quantity, denoted by
NL
, is constructed in terms of the covariance CHSH inequalities
Pozsgay et al. 2017
. Consider the covariance of
A_{x}
and
B_{y}
given by
cov(A_{x},B_{y})=\braket{A_{x}B_{y}}-\braket{A_{x}}\braket{B_{y}}
.
Define the absolute covariance CHSH functions
cov\mathcal{B}_{2\alpha+\beta}:=|cov(A_{0}B_{0})+(-1)^{\beta}cov(A_{0}B_{1})+(-1)^{\alpha}cov(A_{1}B_{0})+(-1)^{\alpha\oplus\beta\oplus 1}cov(A_{1}B_{1})|
. Consider the following triad of quantities constructed from these four covariance CHSH functions:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Gamma_{1}&:=\Big||cov\mathcal{B}_{0}-cov\mathcal{B}_{1}|-|cov\mathcal{B}_{2}-cov\mathcal{B}_{3}|\Big|\\
\Gamma_{2}&:=\Big||cov\mathcal{B}_{0}-cov\mathcal{B}_{2}|-|cov\mathcal{B}_{1}-cov\mathcal{B}_{3}|\Big|\\
\Gamma_{3}&:=\Big||cov\mathcal{B}_{0}-cov\mathcal{B}_{3}|-|cov\mathcal{B}_{1}-cov\mathcal{B}_{2}|\Big|.\end{split}

(5)

To capture the PR-box fraction with dimensionally restricted nonlocality, the following quantity is then defined:

NL:=\min_{i}\Gamma_{i}.

(6)

Here
NL
satisfies the following properties: (i)
0\leq NL\leq 4
; (ii)
NL=0
for any product box of the form,
P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})=P(a|A_{x})P(b|B_{y})
; (iii) NL is invariant under relabeling of inputs and/or outputs and (iv)
NL=4
for any PR box
P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{PR}
. In Ref.
Jebarathinam 2015
, a measure called Bell discord (also appeared as Bell strength in Ref.
Jebaratnam et al. 2017
), which is the same as the quantity
NL
defined above, but without invoking covariance on the CHSH functions, was studied. This measure was considered in Ref.
Jebarathinam 2015
to capture the maximal irreducible PR box fraction in any convex mixture of the PR boxes, which can be Bell nonlocal or not. Here, the covariance CHSH inequalities are used to define the quantity of interest
NL
so that it vanishes for all product boxes.

Before I present the above-mentioned decomposition for dimensionally restricted nonlocality, I will first demonstrate that a nonzero value of the measure
NL
in Eq. (
6
) captures dimensionally restricted nonlocality.

Proposition 1
.

For any local box that can be simulated by any classical state
\lambda
having dimension
d_{\lambda}\leq 2
,
NL=0
.

Proof.

Any Bell-local box, which can be simulated by any classical state
\lambda
having dimension
d_{\lambda}\leq 2
, can be expressed in terms of the deterministic boxes as follows:

P_{L}(ab|A_{x}B_{y})=\sum^{1}_{\lambda=0}p_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(a|A_{x})P_{\lambda}(b|B_{y}),

(7)

where
P_{\lambda}(a|A_{x})
and
P_{\lambda}(b|B_{y})
are a convex combination of deterministic local states given by

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}(a|A_{x})

\displaystyle=\sum^{3}_{i=0}l_{i}^{(\lambda)}P^{i}_{D}(a|A_{x}),

(8)

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}(b|B_{y})

\displaystyle=\sum^{3}_{j=0}l_{j}^{(\lambda)}P^{j}_{D}(b|B_{y}),

(9)

where
P^{i}_{D}(a|A_{x})
and
P^{j}_{D}(b|B_{y})
are given by

P^{\alpha\beta}_{D}(a|A_{x})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}1,&a=\alpha x\oplus\beta\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\\
\end{array}\right.

(10)

with
i=\alpha\oplus\beta
, and

P^{\gamma\epsilon}_{D}(b|B_{y})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}1,&b=\gamma x\oplus\epsilon\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\\
\end{array}\right.

(11)

with
j=\gamma\oplus\epsilon
, respectively. It has been checked that for any Bell-local box as expressed in Eq. (
7
) has
NL=0
.
∎

From the above proposition, it follows that
NL>0
implies dimensionally restricted nonlocality.

Next, I proceed to obtain the decomposition of any dimensionally restricted nonlocality with the PR-box fraction. From Observations
5
and
6

Jebarathinam 2015

Lemma 1
.

\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma}p_{\alpha\beta\gamma}P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{PR}
can be written as the mixture,

\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma}p_{\alpha\beta\gamma}P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{PR}=p_{NL}P_{NL}+(1-p_{NL})P^{NL=0}_{L_{1}},

(12)

where
p_{NL}=NL(\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma}p_{\alpha\beta\gamma}P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{PR})/4
,
P_{NL}
is one of the PR boxes and
P_{L_{1}}
is a Bell-local box with
NL=0
and is a convex mixture of Bell-local boxes which are a uniform mixture of two PR boxes.

The following different decomposition of any nonsignaling box is then obtained.

Theorem 1
.

Any nonsignaling box can be decomposed as a convex mixture of a single PR-box and a Bell-local box with
NL=0
as follows:

P=p_{PR}P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{PR}+(1-p_{PR})P^{NL=0}_{L},

(13)

with
p_{PR}=\frac{NL(P)}{4}
. Here,
P^{NL=0}_{L}
is a Bell-local box, with
NL=0
.

Proof.

Any nonsignaling box can be decomposed as a convex mixture of the
8
PR boxes and a Bell-local box as follows:

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma}p_{\alpha\beta\gamma}P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{PR}+\left(1-\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma}p_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\right)P^{NL=0}_{L_{2}}.

(14)

Here
p_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
are maximized overall decompositions so that the Bell-local box in the decomposition has
NL=0
.
From Lemma
1

8

NL=0

12

Using this decomposition for the mixture of the PR boxes in Eq. (
14

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=p_{NL}P_{NL}+\left(1-p_{NL}\right)P_{L},

(15)

where
P_{L}
is a mixture of the two boxes
P^{NL=0}_{L_{1}}
in Eq. (
12

P^{NL=0}_{L_{2}}
in Eq. (
14

15

is optimized, i.e.,
4p_{NL}=NL(P)
, it follows that
P_{L}
in Eq. (
15

NL=0

∎

Thus,
NL
serves as a quantifier of the PR-box fraction of dimensionally restricted nonlocality. As an illustration of this, consider the noisy PR box,
P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{nPR}
, which is a mixture of a PR-box and white noise,

P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{nPR}=p_{PR}P^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{PR}+(1-p_{PR})P_{N},

For

Secure quantum key distribution:-
I shall study the secret correlations of dimensionally restricted quantum nonlocality to achieve secure key distribution. The scenario consists of Eve, who has tripartite probability distributions
P(abe|A_{x}B_{y}E)
, sending the marginal box
\sum_{e}P(abe|A_{x}B_{y}E)
to Alice and Bob, who want to share secret correlations. Now I show the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Any box that has dimensionally restricted quantum nonlocality contains secret correlations against any Eve’s state that is also dimensionally restricted; at the same time, any dimensionally restricted quantum nonlocality is completely uncorrelated with any such Eve, i.e.,
P(abe|A_{x}B_{y}E)=P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})P(e|E)
, where
P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})
has dimensionally restricted nonlocality.

Proof.

If any given box
P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})
does not have dimensionally restricted quantum nonlocality, then the correlations of the box can be shared with Eve’s state, which has dimension
2
, i.e.,

P(abe|A_{x}B_{y}E)=\sum^{1}_{e=0}p_{e}P(a|A_{x}e)P(b|B_{y}e)P(e|E).

(17)

On the other hand, if Alice and Bob do not have secret correlations in their box against dimensionally restricted Eve, then the extension of the box to the tripartite probability distributions satisfies (
17
). Hence, the box shared by Alice and Bob does not have dimensionally restricted nonlocality. Thus, the first part of the theorem is proved.

Next, I present the proof of the second part of the theorem.
From Bayes’ theorem,
P(abe|A_{x}B_{y}E)=P(ab|A_{x}B_{y}Ee)P(e|A_{x}B_{y}E)
. Using nonsignaling from Alice and Bob to Eve, it then follows that

P(abe|A_{x}B_{y}E)=P(ab|A_{x}B_{y}Ee)P(e|E).

(18)

From nonsignaling from Eve to Alice and Bob,

P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})=\sum_{e}P(abe|A_{x}B_{y}E)
for all Eve’s measurements
E
. Now, using Eq. (
18
),

\displaystyle P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})=\sum_{e}P(ab|A_{x}B_{y}Ee)P(e|E)\quad\forall\quad E.

(19)

Now, for any given
E
,

\displaystyle P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})=\sum_{e}P(e)P(ab|A_{x}B_{y}e),

(20)

where
e
plays the role of shared randomness
\lambda
. Note that if dimensionally restricted quantum nonlocality is used as a resource, shared randomness
\lambda
cannot be freely used between Alice and Bob
Jebarathinam et al. 2024
. Then it follows that
P(ab|A_{x}B_{y}e)=P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})
. Using this constraint in Eq. (
18
),

P(abe|A_{x}B_{y}E)=P(ab|A_{x}B_{y})P(e|E).

(21)

∎

To demonstrate secure quantum key distribution with dimensionally restricted nonlocality, we consider the CHSH protocol of the key distribution studied in Ref.
Acín et al. 2006
. In the CHSH protocol with a nonsignaling box,
Alice and Bob randomly input their bits into the box and collect their output bits. Alice reveals her inputs, and Bob flips his output bit when both of their inputs are
1
.
For one-way key distribution protocols in which communication goes from Alice to Bob, the secret bit shared by Alice and Bob is quantified by the one-way key rate
K^{\rightarrow}\geq I(A:B)-I(A:E)

Devetak and Winter 2005
.
If Alice and Bob share secret correlations in the PR box,
P_{PR}
, then the maximal secure key rate of
1
bit is achieved against any Eve because
I(A:B)=1
and
I(A:E)=0
with the PR box, which is an extremal nonsignaling box.

In QM, the PR box cannot be realized perfectly
Ramanathan et al. 2016
.
In Ref.
Acín et al. 2006
, the secret key rate of the noisy PR box,
P_{nPR}:=p_{PR}P_{PR}+(1-p_{PR})P_{N}
,
achievable by quantum Bell nonlocality was studied. In this context, a positive value of the key rate against the individual attack of nonsignaling (supraquantum) Eve was shown if
p_{PR}>0.659
.

Since the CHSH protocol is considered here to establish a secure key distribution with dimensionally restricted nonlocality, the key rate is given by
K^{\rightarrow}\geq I(A:B)
, provided that the measure
NL
in Eq. (
6
) is nonzero. This follows because
NL>0
implies the presence of the PR-box fraction of dimensionally restricted nonlocality, and
I(A:E)=0
for dimensionally restricted nonlocality, which follows from Theorem
2
.

I shall study the secure key rate of the noisy PR box with dimensionally restricted quantum nonlocality. The secure key rate of the noisy PR box within QM, realized by the Werner state
\rho_{W}
, has been checked to be
K^{\rightarrow}\geq I(A:B)=1-h\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{W}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)
, where
h
is the binary entropy. This key rate is nonzero for any Werner state, i.e.,
W>0
, and its secrecy is implied by
NL(P_{nPR})=4p_{PR}
, with
p_{PR}=W/\sqrt{2}
, being nonzero for any
W>0
. Thus, using dimensionally restricted nonlocality as a resource, the noisy PR box within QM is useful for secure key distribution for any
p_{PR}>0
.

Conclusions and Discussions:-

The PR-box fraction of dimensionally restricted nonlocality has been studied using a measure of dimensionally restricted nonlocality. It has been demonstrated that the PR-box fraction of dimensionally restricted nonlocality can be used to provide a secure quantum key distribution if Eve is also dimensionally restricted. On the other hand, in the presence of the most powerful Eve, who is not dimensionally restricted, secret correlations are present in dimensionally restricted nonlocality if entanglement is certified
Acín and Gisin 2005
;
Goh et al. 2016
.

With quantum Bell nonlocality as a resource, the noises acting on the devices held by Alice and Bob inside the box are modeled by shared randomness
Łukanowski et al. 2023
. Whereas, with the PR box fraction of dimensionally restricted nonlocality as a resource, the realization of secure quantum key distribution with a noisy PR box models the noises as internal sources of randomness in Alice’s and Bob’s devices, which are uncorrelated, as modeled for selftesting quantum random number generation in Ref.
Lunghi et al. 2015
.

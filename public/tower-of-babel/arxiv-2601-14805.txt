Minimizing Submodular Functions over Hierarchical Families
Ryuhei Mizutani

Abstract
This paper considers submodular function minimization (SFM) restricted to a family of subsets. We show that SFM over complements of families with certain hierarchical structures can be solved in polynomial-time. This yields a polynomial-time algorithm for SFM over complements of various families, such as intersecting families, crossing families, and the unions of lattices. Moreover, this tractability result partially settles the open question posed by Nägele, Sudakov, and Zenklusen on polynomial-solvability of SFM over the intersection of parity families. Furthermore, our tractability result implies that for a constant positive integer k k , the k k -th smallest value of a submodular function can be obtained in polynomial-time.

Introduction

Let
V
be a finite set.
A set function
f:2^{V}\to\mathbb{Z}
is called
submodular
if
f(X)+f(Y)\geq f(X\cup Y)+f(X\cap Y)
holds for all
X,Y\subseteq V
.
It is a central result in combinatorial optimization that submodular functions can be minimized in polynomial-time in the evaluation oracle model
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
.

While submodular function minimization (SFM) is efficiently solvable, polynomial-solvability of SFM restricted to a set family is known only for a few classes of families.
Classical examples of such families are intersecting and crossing families (see e.g.,
[
7
, Volume B]
).
As another tractable case, Goemans and Ramakrishnan
[
2
]
showed that SFM can be solved in polynomial-time over parity families.
Their result generalizes polynomial-solvability of SFM over the families of all odd sets and all even sets, and more generally, over triple families
[
3
]
.
Nägele, Sudakov, and Zenklusen
[
5
]
pursued a different extension of the odd/even-cardinality constraint to the congruency constraint, showing that SFM over all sets of cardinality
r
mod
m
, where
m
is a constant prime power, can be solved in polynomial-time.
In this paper, we introduce a new class of families, which includes complements of various families such as intersecting families, crossing families, and the unions of lattices.
We show that SFM over such families can be solved in polynomial-time, which partially settles the open question posed by Nägele, Sudakov, and Zenklusen
[
5
]
.

To provide a new tractable class of families, we introduce some definitions.
A set family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
is called a
lattice
if for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
X\cup Y,X\cap Y\in\mathcal{F}
.
For a positive integer
k
, we call a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
a
k
-hierarchical lattice
if
\mathcal{F}
can be partitioned into
k
families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{k}\subseteq 2^{V}
such that

•

\mathcal{F}_{1}
is a lattice,

•

for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
and
i=2,\ldots,k
, we have that
X\cup Y,X\cap Y\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
or at least one of
X\cup Y
and
X\cap Y
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
.

Note that if
k=1
, then a
k
-hierarchical lattice coincides with a lattice.
We provide several examples of
k
-hierarchical lattices.

Example 1.1

(Intersecting family)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
be an
intersecting family
, i.e., for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}
with
X\cap Y\neq\emptyset
, we have
X\cup Y,X\cap Y\in\mathcal{F}
.
Define
\mathcal{F}_{1}:=\{\emptyset\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}:=\mathcal{F}\setminus\{\emptyset\}
.
Then,
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is a lattice, and for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
, we have
X\cap Y=\emptyset\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
or
X\cup Y,X\cap Y\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
.
Hence,
\mathcal{F}\cup\{\emptyset\}
is a 2-hierarchical lattice.

Example 1.2

(Crossing family)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
be a
crossing family
, i.e., for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}
with
X\cap Y\neq\emptyset
and
X\cup Y\neq V
, we have
X\cup Y,X\cap Y\in\mathcal{F}
.
Define
\mathcal{F}_{1}:=\{\emptyset,V\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}:=\mathcal{F}\setminus\{\emptyset,V\}
.
Then,
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is a lattice, and for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
, we have
X\cap Y=\emptyset\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
or
X\cup Y=V\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
or
X\cup Y,X\cap Y\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
.
Hence,
\mathcal{F}\cup\{\emptyset,V\}
is a 2-hierarchical lattice.

Example 1.3

(Union of
k
lattices)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{k}\subseteq 2^{V}
be lattices.
Define
\mathcal{F}_{1}:=\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}:=\mathcal{L}_{i}\setminus(\mathcal{L}_{1}\cup\mathcal{L}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{L}_{i-1})
for
i=2,\ldots,k
.
Then,
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is a lattice, and for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
and
i=2,\ldots,k
, we have that
X\cup Y,X\cap Y\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
or at least one of
X\cup Y
and
X\cap Y
belongs to
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cup\mathcal{L}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{L}_{i-1}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
.
Hence,
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cup\mathcal{L}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{L}_{k}
is a
k
-hierarchical lattice.

It is well known that the minimizers of a submodular function form a lattice (see e.g.,
[
1
]
).
The following example shows that the family of subsets whose function values are at most the
k
-th smallest value of a submodular function is a
k
-hierarchical lattice.

Example 1.4

(
k
-th smallest value of a submodular function)
.

Let
f:2^{V}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a submodular function.
For
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, define
\mathcal{F}_{i}
as the family of subsets
X\subseteq V
whose values
f(X)
are equal to the
i
-th smallest value of
f
.
Then, since the minimizers of
f
form a lattice,
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is a lattice.
For
i=2,\ldots,k
, take
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
.
Then, the submodular inequality
f(X)+f(Y)\geq f(X\cup Y)+f(X\cap Y)
implies that if
X\cup Y\notin\mathcal{F}_{i}
or
X\cap Y\notin\mathcal{F}_{i}
, then at least one of
X\cup Y
and
X\cap Y
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq i-1
.
Hence, for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
and
i=2,\ldots,k
, we have that
X\cup Y,X\cap Y\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
, or at least one of
X\cup Y
and
X\cap Y
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{k}
is a
k
-hierarchical lattice.

We call a set family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
the
complement of a
k
-hierarchical lattice
if
2^{V}\setminus\mathcal{F}
is a
k
-hierarchical lattice.
For disjoint subsets
S,T\subseteq V
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{ST}:=\{X\subseteq V\mid S\subseteq X\subseteq V\setminus T\}.

Note that
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
is a lattice for every disjoint subsets
S,T\subseteq V
.
Our main result is the following structural theorem on the minimizers of a submodular function over the complement of a
k
-hierarchical lattice.

Theorem 1.5
.

For a positive integer
k
, let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
be the complement of a
k
-hierarchical lattice.
Let
f:2^{V}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a submodular function.
Let
X^{*}\in\mathcal{F}
be a minimizer of
f
over
\mathcal{F}
.
Then, there exist
S,T\subseteq V
with
S\cap T=\emptyset
and
\max\{|S|,|T|\}\leq k
such that
X^{*}
is a minimizer of
f
over the lattice
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
.

To obtain a polynomial-time algorithm for SFM over the complement of
k
-hierarchical lattice, we show the following slight refinement of Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 1.6
.

For a positive integer
k
, let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
be the complement of a
k
-hierarchical lattice.
Let
f:2^{V}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a submodular function.
Let
X^{*}\in\mathcal{F}
be a minimal minimizer of
f
over
\mathcal{F}
.
Then, there exist
S,T\subseteq V
with
S\cap T=\emptyset
and
\max\{|S|,|T|\}\leq k
such that
X^{*}
is the unique minimal minimizer of
f
over the lattice
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
.

By Theorem
1.6
, we can obtain a minimizer over the complement of a
k
-hierarchical lattice by solving SFM over
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
for all
S,T\subseteq V
with
S\cap T=\emptyset
and
\max\{|S|,|T|\}\leq k
.
This yields the following result:

Theorem 1.7
.

For a positive integer
k
, let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
be the complement of a
k
-hierarchical lattice.
Let
f:2^{V}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a submodular function.
Then, a minimizer of
f
over
\mathcal{F}
can be obtained in
O(n^{2k}\tau(n))
time, where
n:=|V|
and
\tau(n)
denotes the time required for minimizing a submodular function on a ground set of size
n
.

Since SFM can be solved in polynomial-time
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
,
\tau(n)
in Theorem
1.7
is polynomial in
n
.
Hence, for a fixed positive integer
k
, SFM over the complement of a
k
-hierarchical lattice can be solved in polynomial-time by Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem
1.7
implies a polynomial-time algorithm for minimizing submodular functions over the complement of an intersecting family or a crossing family.
Indeed, for an intersecting family
\mathcal{F}
, since
\mathcal{F}\cup\{\emptyset\}
is a 2-hierarchical lattice, we can find a minimizer of a submodular function
f
over
2^{V}\setminus(\mathcal{F}\cup\{\emptyset\})
in polynomial-time.
Hence, if
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
, by comparing the minimum value of
f
over
2^{V}\setminus(\mathcal{F}\cup\{\emptyset\})
with
f(\emptyset)
, we obtain a minimizer over
2^{V}\setminus\mathcal{F}
; otherwise, a minimizer over
2^{V}\setminus(\mathcal{F}\cup\{\emptyset\})
is already a minimizer over
2^{V}\setminus\mathcal{F}
.
Similarly, for a crossing family
\mathcal{F}
, since
\mathcal{F}\cup\{\emptyset,V\}
is a 2-hierarchical lattice, we can find a minimizer of
f
over
2^{V}\setminus(\mathcal{F}\cup\{\emptyset,V\})
in polynomial-time.
Hence, by comparing the minimum value with
f(\emptyset)
and
f(V)
as needed, we obtain a minimizer over
2^{V}\setminus\mathcal{F}
.
Therefore, we have the following:

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
f:2^{V}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a submodular function.
If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
is an intersecting family or a crossing family, then a minimizer of
f
over
2^{V}\setminus\mathcal{F}
can be obtained in polynomial-time.

A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
is called a
parity family
if for all
X,Y\in 2^{V}\setminus\mathcal{F}
, we have

\displaystyle(X\cup Y\in\mathcal{F})\iff(X\cap Y\in\mathcal{F}).

An important example of a parity family is the complement of a lattice.
As mentioned above, Goemans and Ramakrishnan
[
2
]
showed that SFM over a parity family can be solved in polynomial-time; in particular, this implies polynomial-solvability of SFM over the complement of a lattice.
Since lattices are a subclass of intersecting and crossing families, Corollary
1.8
can be viewed as a generalization of this tractability result for the complement of a lattice.

As a common generalization of SFM over parity families and congruency constraints, Nägele, Sudakov, and Zenklusen
[
5
]
posed the question of whether SFM over the intersection of a constant number of parity families can be solved efficiently.
Theorem
1.7
settles this question in the case when each parity family in the intersection is the complement of a lattice.
Indeed, the intersection of the complements of
k
lattices is the complement of the union of these
k
lattices; moreover, this union forms a
k
-hierarchical lattice.
Hence, Theorem
1.7
implies the following:

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
f:2^{V}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a submodular function.
For a constant positive integer
k
, if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
is the intersection of the complements of
k
lattices, then a minimizer of
f
over
\mathcal{F}
can be obtained in polynomial-time.

As a corollary of polynomial-solvability of SFM over parity families, Goemans and Ramakrishnan
[
2
]
showed that the second smallest value of a submodular function can be obtained in polynomial-time.
Since the family of subsets whose function values are at most the
k
-th smallest value of a submodular function forms a
k
-hierarchical lattice, Theorem
1.7
implies the following generalization to the
k
-th smallest value:

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
f:2^{V}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a submodular function.
For a constant positive integer
k
, a set attaining the
k
-th smallest value of
f
can be obtained in polynomial-time.

Proofs

In this section, we provide proofs of Theorems
1.5
,
1.6
, and
1.7
.
Our proof strategies are similar to the approach presented in
[
2
,
5
]
for parity families and congruency constraints.
We first show that for a (minimal) minimizer
X^{*}
of
f
over
\mathcal{F}
, there exist subsets
S\subseteq X^{*}
and
T\subseteq V\setminus X^{*}
of sizes at most
k
such that all sets
Y
with
S\subseteq Y\subseteq X^{*}
or
X^{*}\subseteq Y\subseteq V\setminus T
are in
\mathcal{F}
.
Using this fact, we show that
X^{*}
is a minimizer (or the unique minimal minimizer) of
f
over the lattice
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
.

Let
f:2^{V}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a submodular function.
For a positive integer
k
, let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{V}
be the complement of a
k
-hierarchical lattice.
Let
[k]:=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
.
The following lemma is the main technical contribution of this paper and plays a key role in proving our main theorems.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{F}
, there exists
S\subseteq X
such that
|S|\leq k
and
Y\in\mathcal{F}
for all
Y\subseteq X
with
S\subseteq Y
.

Proof.

Take any
X\in\mathcal{F}
.
If
|X|\leq k
, then setting
S=X
satisfies the condition, and we are done.
Consider the case when
|X|\geq k+1
.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is the complement of a
k
-hierarchical lattice,
2^{V}\setminus\mathcal{F}
can be partitioned into
k
families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{k}\subseteq 2^{V}
satisfying the conditions of a
k
-hierarchical lattice.
We prove the following claim by induction:

Claim 2.2
.

There exists a collection of sets
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{k}
such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

|U_{i}|=i
for each
i\in[k]
.

(2)

U_{1}\subsetneq U_{2}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq U_{k}\subseteq X
.

(3)

For each
i\in[k]
,
U_{i}\not\subseteq Y
for all
Y\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
with
Y\subseteq X
.

Proof of Claim
2.2
.

We inductively construct sets
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{k}
satisfying conditions
(1)
–
(3)
.
We first show the existence of a set
U_{1}
satisfying conditions
(1)
and
(3)
.
Suppose to the contrary that for each
u\in X
, there exists
Y\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
such that
Y\subseteq X
and
u\in Y
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\bigcup_{Y\in\mathcal{F}_{1},Y\subseteq X}Y=X.

Combined with the fact that
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is a lattice, this implies
X\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
, which contradicts
X\in\mathcal{F}
.

We next show the existence of sets
U_{2},\ldots,U_{k}
satisfying conditions
(1)
–
(3)
.
As induction hypothesis, assume that there exist sets
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{j-1}
satisfying conditions
(1)
–
(3)
for some
2\leq j\leq k
.
We show the existence of a set
U_{j}
satisfying conditions
(1)
–
(3)
.
Suppose to the contrary that for each
U\subseteq X
with
|U|=j
and
U\supsetneq U_{j-1}
, there exists
Y\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
such that
Y\subseteq X
and
U\subseteq Y
.
Define a subfamily
\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{F}_{j}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}:=\{Y\in\mathcal{F}_{j}\mid U_{j-1}\subsetneq Y\subseteq X\}.

By the assumption, for each
v\in X\setminus U_{j-1}
, there exists
Y\in\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
with
v\in Y
.
This implies

\displaystyle\bigcup_{Y\in\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}}Y=X.

If
Y_{1}\cup Y_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
for each pair of
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
, then by the above equation we have
X\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
, a contradiction.
Hence, there exist
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
such that
Y_{1}\cup Y_{2}\notin\mathcal{F}_{j}
.
Since
\mathcal{F}_{j}
satisfies the second condition of a
k
-hierarchical lattice, at least one of
Y_{1}\cup Y_{2}
and
Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
.
Since
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\prime}
, we have
U_{j-1}\subseteq Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}\subseteq Y_{1}\cup Y_{2}\subseteq X
, which implies that there exists
Z\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
with
U_{j-1}\subseteq Z\subseteq X
.
This contradicts the induction hypothesis that
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{j-1}
satisfy condition
(3)
.
∎

Take sets
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{k}
that satisfy conditions
(1)
–
(3)
in Claim
2.2
.
By conditions
(2)
and
(3)
, for each
i\in[k]
, we have
Y\notin\mathcal{F}_{i}
for all
Y\subseteq X
with
U_{k}\subseteq Y
.
This implies that
Y\in\mathcal{F}
for all
Y\subseteq X
with
U_{k}\subseteq Y
.
Hence, setting
S=U_{k}
satisfies the condition in Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Similar as in the proofs in
[
2
,
5
]
, the same argument works for the family consisting of the complements of all sets in
\mathcal{F}
as follows.
Since
2^{V}\setminus\mathcal{F}
is a
k
-hierarchical lattice, the family
\{V\setminus X\mid X\in 2^{V}\setminus\mathcal{F}\}
is also a
k
-hierarchical lattice.
Hence, the family
\{V\setminus X\mid X\in\mathcal{F}\}
is the complement of a
k
-hierarchical lattice.
Therefore, applying Lemma
2.1
to the family
\{V\setminus X\mid X\in\mathcal{F}\}
, we have the following:

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{F}
, there exists
T\subseteq V\setminus X
such that
|T|\leq k
and
Y\in\mathcal{F}
for all
Y\supseteq X
with
Y\subseteq V\setminus T
.

We are now ready to prove Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Take a minimizer
X^{*}\in\mathcal{F}
of
f
over
\mathcal{F}
.
By Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
, there exist
S,T\subseteq V
such that

•

S\subseteq X^{*}
and
T\subseteq V\setminus X^{*}
,

•

\max\{|S|,|T|\}\leq k
,

•

Y\in\mathcal{F}
for all
Y\subseteq V
with
S\subseteq Y\subseteq X^{*}
or
X^{*}\subseteq Y\subseteq V\setminus T
.

Since
S\cap T=\emptyset
, it suffices to show that
X^{*}
is a minimizer of
f
over the lattice
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
.
Let
Z\in\mathcal{F}_{ST}
be a minimizer of
f
over
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
.
Since
f
is submodular, we have

\displaystyle 2f(X^{*})\geq f(X^{*})+f(Z)\geq f(X^{*}\cup Z)+f(X^{*}\cap Z).

(2.1)

This implies that we have at least one of
f(X^{*})\geq f(X^{*}\cup Z)
and
f(X^{*})\geq f(X^{*}\cap Z)
.
Since
X^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{ST}
and
Z\in\mathcal{F}_{ST}
, we have
X^{*}\cup Z\in\mathcal{F}_{ST}
and
X^{*}\cap Z\in\mathcal{F}_{ST}
.
Hence, we have
S\subseteq X^{*}\cap Z\subseteq X^{*}\subseteq X^{*}\cup Z\subseteq V\setminus T
, which implies
X^{*}\cup Z\in\mathcal{F}
and
X^{*}\cap Z\in\mathcal{F}
.
Since
X^{*}
is a minimizer of
f
over
\mathcal{F}
, this implies
f(X^{*}\cup Z)\geq f(X^{*})
and
f(X^{*}\cap Z)\geq f(X^{*})
.
Combined with (
2.1
), this yields
f(X^{*})=f(Z)
, as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Take a minimal minimizer
X^{*}\in\mathcal{F}
of
f
over
\mathcal{F}
.
By Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
, there exist
S,T\subseteq V
such that

•

S\subseteq X^{*}
and
T\subseteq V\setminus X^{*}
,

•

\max\{|S|,|T|\}\leq k
,

•

Y\in\mathcal{F}
for all
Y\subseteq V
with
S\subseteq Y\subseteq X^{*}
or
X^{*}\subseteq Y\subseteq V\setminus T
.

Since
S\cap T=\emptyset
, it suffices to show that
X^{*}
is the unique minimal minimizer of
f
over the lattice
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
.
By Theorem
1.5
,
X^{*}
is a minimizer of
f
over
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
.
We show that
X^{*}\subseteq Z
holds for every minimizer
Z\in\mathcal{F}_{ST}
of
f
over
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
.
By the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we have
X^{*}\cap Z\in\mathcal{F}
and
f(X^{*}\cap Z)=f(X^{*})
for every minimizer
Z
of
f
over
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
.
Hence, if
X^{*}\not\subseteq Z
for some minimizer
Z
of
f
over
\mathcal{F}_{ST}
, then we have
X^{*}\cap Z\subsetneq X^{*}
, which contradicts that
X^{*}
is a minimal minimizer of
f
over
\mathcal{F}
.
Thus, we have
X^{*}\subseteq Z
for every minimizer
Z
of
f
over
\mathcal{F}_{ST}

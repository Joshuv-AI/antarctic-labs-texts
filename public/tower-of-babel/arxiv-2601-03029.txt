Periodicity of traces of Hecke operators modulo prime powers
Jonas Bergström, Sjoerd de Vries

Abstract
We study traces of Hecke operators on spaces of elliptic cusp forms and Drinfeld cusp forms and show that, modulo any prime power, these traces are periodic in the weight.



1

Introduction

Congruences between modular forms modulo prime powers play a central role in the study of arithmetic properties of modular forms and their associated Galois representations. While classical results focus on congruences between Fourier coefficients or Hecke eigenvalues of individual eigenforms, it is natural to also study congruences between traces of Hecke operators on different spaces of cusp forms.

In this paper, we prove congruences, modulo prime powers, between traces of Hecke operators acting on spaces of classical cusp forms of different weights.
Our result extends a result of Serre
[
16
]
, who determined such congruences modulo primes.
The congruences hold for very general congruence subgroups; in particular, we do not assume that
p
-adic analytic methods apply, but nevertheless obtain results in the spirit of the Gouvêa–Mazur conjecture.

Our main tool is the trace formula for Hecke operators established in Deligne’s foundational work
[
6
]
. Our methods extend naturally to the setting of Drinfeld modular forms over function fields, giving a parallel theory of Hecke trace congruences in positive characteristic.

1.1

The main theorems

Our first main result is the following theorem about elliptic modular forms. For the notation, see further in Section
2.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
and let
H
be a subgroup
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
.
Let
\ell
be a prime and if
H
is not representable then let
\ell\geq 5
. Let
q=p^{a}
be a prime power with
\gcd(q,N)=1
.
Let
s\geq 1
, and

k_{0}=\begin{cases}s-1&\text{if }\ell\neq p;\\
s&\text{if }\ell=p\neq q;\\
2s-1&\text{if }\ell=p=q.\end{cases}

If
k\geq k_{0}
then

\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,S[H,k+2])+\epsilon_{k}\equiv_{\ell^{s}}\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,S[H,k+2+n]),

where

n=\begin{cases}\ell^{s-1}(\ell^{2}-1)&\text{if }\ell\geq 3\text{ and }\left(\frac{q}{\ell}\right)=-1;\\
\ell^{s}(\ell^{2}-1)/2&\text{if }\ell\geq 3\text{ and }\left(\frac{q}{\ell}\right)=1;\\
\ell^{s}(\ell^{2}-1)&\text{if }\ell=2\text{ and }q\text{ is a non-square mod }\ell^{s};\\
\text{lcm}(2,\ell^{s-1}(\ell^{2}-1))&\text{if }\ell=2\text{ and }q\text{ is an odd square mod }\ell^{s};\\
\ell^{s-1}(\ell-1)&\text{if }\ell=p,\end{cases}

and where
\epsilon_{k}=0
for any
k>0
and
\epsilon_{0}=-(q+1)\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,H_{c}^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{H}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}))
with

\mathcal{X}_{H}
the compact modular curve with level structure
H
.

If
H
is not representable and
\ell=2
or
3
, then the above statement holds but with
s
replaced by
s+\nu_{\ell}(H,q)
in the definition of
n
.
In the case
\ell=2
and
-\mathrm{Id}\in H
then
s
can instead be replaced by
s-1+\nu_{2}(H,q)
in the definition of
n
.

Remark 1.2
.

In a letter to Tate (see Equation (2) and (5) of
[
16
]
, but note the differences in notation), Serre proved that if
w_{k}=\Tr(F_{p}\,|\,S[\Gamma,k])-\Tr(F_{p}\,|\,S[\Gamma,k-(\ell-1)])
, then
w_{k+\ell+1}\equiv_{\ell}p\cdot w_{k}
for all
\Gamma=\Gamma(N)
with
N\geq 3
,
q=p\neq\ell
,
s=1
and
k>\ell+1
. Under these assumptions it follows that

w_{k+\ell^{2}-1}\equiv_{\ell}p^{\ell-1}\cdot w_{k}\equiv_{\ell}w_{k}

and then that

\Tr(F_{p}\,|\,S[\Gamma,k+\ell(\ell^{2}-1)])-\Tr(F_{p}\,|\,S[\Gamma,k])\equiv_{\ell}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}w_{k+(\ell-1)((\ell+1)(\ell-j)-i)}\\
\equiv_{\ell}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}w_{k+(\ell-1)(\ell+1-i)}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\ell\cdot w_{k+(\ell-1)(\ell+1-i)}\equiv_{\ell}0.

If
p
is a square mod
\ell\neq 2
, then
w_{k+(\ell^{2}-1)/2}\equiv_{\ell}w_{k}
and

\Tr(F_{p}\,|\,S[\Gamma,k+\ell(\ell^{2}-1)/2])-\Tr(F_{p}\,|\,S[\Gamma,k])\equiv_{\ell}\sum_{i=0}^{(\ell-1)/2}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}w_{k+(\ell-1)((\ell+1)(\ell-j)/2-i)}\\
\equiv_{\ell}\sum_{i=0}^{(\ell-1)/2}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}w_{k+(\ell-1)((\ell+1)/2-i)}=\sum_{i=0}^{(\ell-1)/2}\ell\cdot w_{k+(\ell-1)((\ell+1)/2-i)}\equiv_{\ell}0.

This shows periodicities as in Theorem
1.1
, but with a slightly larger period if
p
is a non-square mod
\ell
.

Trace formulae

2.1

Modular curves

Let
N\geq 1
and let
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
be a congruence subgroup of level
N
.
For any
k\geq 2
, let
S_{k}(\Gamma)
denote the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of cusp forms of weight
k
with respect to
\Gamma
and put
s_{k}=\dim S_{k}(\Gamma)
. For any Hecke eigenform
f\in S_{k}(\Gamma)
, let
a_{p}(f)
denote the eigenvalue of the Hecke operator
T(p)
acting on
f
. There is then a
2
-dimensional
\ell^{\prime}
-adic Galois representation corresponding to
f
, unramified for all
p\nmid N\ell^{\prime}
, and whose characteristic polynomial of Frobenius
F_{p}
equals
x^{2}-a_{p}(f)x+p^{k-1}
.

For
k\geq 2
, let
S[\Gamma,k]
denote the direct sum of the
\ell^{\prime}
-adic Galois representations of a basis of Hecke eigenforms of
S_{k}(\Gamma)
.
For all
k\geq 2
and all prime powers
q
, the trace of Frobenius
F_{q}
acting on
S[\Gamma,k]
will be an integer.

Let now
H\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
be a subgroup.
Let
\mathcal{Y}_{H}
(respectively
\mathcal{X}_{H}
) be the moduli space of elliptic curves (respectively generalized elliptic curves) with a level
H
-structure. This is a smooth (respectively smooth and proper) Deligne–Mumford stack defined over
\mathbb{Z}[1/N]
with geometrically irreducible fibers over
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{N}]^{\det H}
, see
[
5
, Section IV.3 and IV.5]
. In particular,
\mathcal{Y}_{H}(\mathbb{C})\cong\coprod_{i=1}^{d}\Gamma_{i}\backslash\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{H}
is the complex upper half plane and each
\Gamma_{i}
is a congruence subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Write
S_{k}(H)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}S_{k}(\Gamma_{i})
and
S[H,k]=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}S[\Gamma_{i},k]
.
When
\det H=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, we have
S[H,k]=S[\Gamma,k]
where
\Gamma=\pi^{-1}(H)
for
\pi:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
. In particular, for
\Gamma\in\{\Gamma(N),\Gamma_{1}(N),\Gamma_{0}(N)\}
, one can find
H_{\Gamma}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
such that
S[H_{\Gamma},k]=S[\Gamma,k]
.

There is an universal elliptic curve
\psi:\mathcal{E}_{H}\to\mathcal{Y}_{H}
(respectively
\psi^{\prime}:\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{H}\to\mathcal{X}_{H}
). Let
j:\mathcal{Y}_{H}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{X}_{H}
denote the natural inclusion. For a field
k
with
\text{char}(k)\nmid N
, let
[\mathcal{Y}_{H}(k)]
(or
[\mathcal{X}_{H}(k)]
,
[(\mathcal{X}_{H}\setminus\mathcal{Y}_{H})(k)]
) denote the set of
k
-isomorphism classes of objects of
\mathcal{Y}_{H}(k)
(respectively
\mathcal{X}_{H}(k)
,
(\mathcal{X}_{H}\setminus\mathcal{Y}_{H})(k)
). So, a
y\in\mathcal{Y}_{H}(k)
corresponds to an elliptic curve
E=(\mathcal{E}_{H})_{y}
over
k
together with a level
H
-structure
\varphi
on
E
over
k
.

If, for all fields
k
with
\text{char}(k)\nmid N
and all
y=(E,\varphi)\in\mathcal{Y}_{H}(k)
, the automorphism group
\Aut_{k}(y)
is trivial, then we call
H

representable
. For instance, if
\pi^{-1}(H)\subset\Gamma(N)
, with
N\geq 3
, then
H
is representable, see for instance
[
5
, Théorème IV.2.7]
.

If
H
is not representable,
\ell
is a prime and
q
is any prime power, then put

\nu_{\ell}(H,q):=\mathrm{max}\{\nu_{\ell}\bigl(\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(y)\bigr)\ |\ y\in\mathcal{Y}_{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\},

where
\nu_{\ell}
is the
\ell
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Z}
normalised so that
\nu_{\ell}(\ell)=1
. Note that
\nu_{2}(H,q)\leq 3
,
\nu_{3}(H,q)\leq 1
and
\nu_{\ell}(H,q)=0
for
\ell\geq 5
, see
[
17
, Theorem 10.1]
. The elements of
[(\mathcal{X}_{H}\setminus\mathcal{Y}_{H})(k)]
are called
cusps
.
Finally define the
\ell^{\prime}
-adic local system
\mathbb{V}_{k}=\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(R^{1}\psi_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})
on
\mathcal{Y}_{H}
.

For an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
F_{q}
denote the geometric Frobenius morphism acting on the
\ell^{\prime}
-adic compactly supported cohomology group
H_{c}^{1}(E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})
, where
\ell^{\prime}\nmid q
. Put
a_{1}(E)=\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,H_{c}^{1}(E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}}))
and note that
a_{1}(E)
is an integer.

Finally, put

\epsilon_{k}=0
for any
k>0
and
\epsilon_{0}=-(q+1)\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,H_{c}^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{H}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}}))
.
If
\det H=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
then
\epsilon_{0}=-(q+1)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
and let
H
be a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
.

For any
k\geq 0
and prime power
q
such that
\gcd(q,N)=1
,

\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,S[H,k+2])+\epsilon_{k}=-\mathrm{eis}_{H,k}(q)-\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{k-j}{j}(-q)^{j}[a_{1}^{k-2j}],

where

[a_{1}^{k-2j}]=\sum_{[(E,\varphi)]\in\mathcal{[}Y_{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\frac{a_{1}(E)^{k-2j}}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(E,\varphi)}\in\mathbb{Z},

and
\mathrm{eis}_{H,k}(q)=\mathrm{eis}_{H,k+2i}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

Fix any
\ell^{\prime}\nmid q
. Below, we consider
\mathcal{X}_{H}
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and we let
F_{q}
denote the geometric Frobenius morphism.
The Eichler–Shimura–Deligne relation together with the Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula, see
[
6
]
and
[
14
, Proposition 4.4]
, says that for
k>0

\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,S[H,k+2])=\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,H^{1}_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{H},j_{*}\mathbb{V}_{k}))=-\mathrm{eis}_{H,k}(q)-\sum_{y\in[\mathcal{Y}_{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\frac{\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,(\mathbb{V}_{k})_{y})}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(y)},

where

\mathrm{eis}_{H,k}(q)=\sum_{x\in[(\mathcal{X}_{H}\setminus\mathcal{Y}_{H})(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\frac{\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,(\mathbb{V}_{k})_{x})}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x)}.

Again using the Eichler–Shimura–Deligne relation and the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula,

\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,S[H,2])=\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,H^{1}_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{H},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}}))=\\
=(q+1)\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,H^{0}_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{H},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}}))-\mathrm{eis}_{H,0}(q)-\sum_{y\in[\mathcal{Y}_{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\frac{1}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(y)}.

A
y\in[\mathcal{Y}_{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism class of an elliptic curve
E=(\mathcal{E}_{H})_{y}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
together with a level
H
-structure
\varphi
on
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The stalk of
\mathbb{V}_{k}
at
(E,\varphi)
is equal to the
k
-th symmetric power of the
\ell^{\prime}
-adic compactly supported cohomology group
H_{c}^{1}(E,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})
. After base change to
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, the action of
F_{q}
on the latter has characteristic polynomial
X^{2}-a_{1}(E)X+q
.

Together with Lemma
2.4
we get that,

\sum_{y\in[\mathcal{Y}_{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\frac{\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,(\mathbb{V}_{k})_{y})}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(y)}=\sum_{[(E,\varphi)]\in[\mathcal{Y}_{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\frac{\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,\Sym^{k}(H_{c}^{1}(E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})))}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(E,\varphi)}=\\
=\sum_{[(E,\varphi)]\in[\mathcal{Y}_{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{k-j}{j}(-q)^{j}\frac{a_{1}(E)^{k-2j}}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(E,\varphi)}.

In the same way, an
x\in[(\mathcal{X}_{H}\setminus\mathcal{Y}_{H})(\mathbb{F}_{q})]
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism class of a (singular) generalized elliptic curve
E=(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{H})_{x}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
together with a level
H
-structure
\varphi
on
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We again let
a_{1}(E)
equal the trace of
F_{q}
on the one-dimensional vector space
H_{c}^{1}(E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})
. We have
a_{1}(E)=1
or
-1
depending upon whether
E
is of split or non-split multiplicative type. It follows that

\mathrm{eis}_{H,k}(q)=\sum_{[(E,\varphi)]\in[(\mathcal{X}_{H}\setminus\mathcal{Y}_{H})(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\frac{\Tr(F_{q}\,|\,\Sym^{k}(H_{c}^{1}(E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})))}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(E,\varphi)}=\\
=\sum_{[(E,\varphi)]\in[(\mathcal{X}_{H}\setminus\mathcal{Y}_{H})(\mathbb{F}_{q})]}\frac{a_{1}(E)^{k}}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(E,\varphi)}

only depends upon the parity of
k
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

It follows from the definition of
S[H,k]
that the trace of
F_{p}
on
S[H,k]
equals the trace of
T(p)
on
S_{k}(H)
.
Note however that if
q=p^{n}
with
n>1
, then the trace of
F_{q}
equals neither the trace of
T(q)
nor the trace of
T(p)^{n}
in general. If
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}\}
is a basis of eigenforms for
S_{k}(H)
and
T(p)f_{i}=a_{p}(f_{i})f_{i}
, then

\Tr(F_{p^{n}}\,|\,S[H,k])=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\beta^{n}_{i}+\bar{\beta}^{n}_{i},

where
(X-\beta_{i})(X-\bar{\beta}_{i})=X^{2}-a_{p}(f_{i})X+p^{k-1}
. On the other hand,
\Tr(T(p)^{n}\,|\,S_{k}(H))=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{p}(f_{i})^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\beta_{i}+\bar{\beta}_{i})^{n}
, so we do obtain for any
n\geq 1
the congruence

\Tr(F_{p^{n}}\,|\,S[H,k])\equiv_{p^{k-1}}\Tr(T(p)^{n}\,|\,S_{k}(H)).

If
p\nmid N
, the trace of
F_{q}
on
S[\Gamma_{0}(N),k]
can be expressed as the trace of
U(p)^{n}
acting on the space of
p
-oldforms in
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(Np))
, where
U(p)
denotes the Atkin
U(p)
-operator. This follows because
F_{p}
and
U(p)
have the same characteristic polynomials. The same holds for
\Gamma_{1}(N)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
k\geq 1
let
h_{k}(x_{1},x_{2})
denote the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial of weight
k
. Then

h_{k}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{k-j}{j}(-x_{1}x_{2})^{j}\,(x_{1}+x_{2})^{k-2j}.

Proof.

For any integer
m\geq 0
, define
\mu_{m}
to be 0 if
m
is odd, and
-(x_{1}x_{2})^{m/2}
otherwise. Then we see that

h_{k}(x_{1},x_{2})=\mu_{k}+\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}(x_{1}x_{2})^{n}(x_{1}^{k-2n}+x_{2}^{k-2n})

and that

\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\binom{k-j}{j}(-x_{1}x_{2})^{j}\,(x_{1}+x_{2})^{k-2j}=\\
=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}{i}\binom{k-i}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\mu_{k}+\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\biggl(\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}\binom{k-i}{n}\biggr)\,(x_{1}x_{2})^{n}(x_{1}^{k-2n}+x_{2}^{k-2n}).

Looking at the equality, with
k\geq n
,

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\Bigl(x^{k-n}(x-1)^{n}\Bigr)=\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}x^{k-i},

evaluated at
x=1
we find that

1=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}\binom{k-i}{n},

from which the lemma follows.
∎

2.2

Drinfeld modular curves

Let
C
be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and fix a closed point
\infty\in|C|
. Then
A:=\mathcal{O}_{C}(C\setminus\{\infty\})
is a Dedekind domain, which in the theory of Drinfeld modular forms plays the role of
\mathbb{Z}
in the theory of elliptic modular forms. We denote by
K:=\mathrm{Frac}(A)
the function field of
C
.

The theory explained in Section
2.1
has an analogue in this setting. Write
\hat{A}:=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\neq\infty}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{A})
be a compact open subgroup of minimal conductor
\mathfrak{n}
.
Then there is a Drinfeld modular curve
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{K}}
, which is an affine, smooth, tame Deligne–Mumford stack over
\Spec(A[\mathfrak{n}^{-1}])
, and parameterizes rank 2 Drinfeld modules with a level
{\mathcal{K}}
-structure
[
1
,
4
]
. For any non-zero prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A[\mathfrak{n}^{-1}]
, write
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}:=A/\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}
for the unique degree
n
extension of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The base change

2.2.1

Assume

Compared to the original definition of Hecke operators

If

m^{\prime}
is coprime to
p
and so
v_{p}(m_{\mathfrak{l},s})=\tilde{s}
. For any
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we have
b^{m^{\prime}}\in\{\pm 1\}
. Let
\delta(b):=(1-b^{m^{\prime}})/2
. Let
\alpha\in A
be any lift of a primitive
2m^{\prime}
-th root of unity mod
\mathfrak{l}
, i.e.,
\alpha\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}
generates
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\times}
. By Lemma
3.9
, we then have

(1-x)^{m^{\prime}}-(b\wp_{n}x^{2})^{m^{\prime}}\equiv_{\mathfrak{l}}\prod_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}(1-x-\alpha^{2i+\delta(b)}\wp_{n}x^{2}).

The factors of this product are clearly pairwise coprime. We distinguish two cases.

If
\deg(\mathfrak{l})
is even, then
\delta(b)=0
for all
b
, and the product contains a square if and only if
\wp_{n}
is a square mod
\mathfrak{l}
. In this case, each root of a factor
1-x-\alpha^{2i}\wp_{n}x^{2}
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{l}^{2}}
, by the same argument given in the proof of Lemma
3.14
.

If
\deg(\mathfrak{l})
is odd, then both
\delta(b)=0
and
\delta(b)=1
occur. In one of the cases the product will contain a square.

Since each irreducible factor in the product is of degree at most 2, we find that in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{l}}[x]
,

(1-x)^{m^{\prime}}-(b\wp_{n}x^{2})^{m^{\prime}}\text{ divides }\begin{cases}x^{|\mathfrak{l}|^{2}-1}-1&\text{if }\deg(\mathfrak{l})\equiv_{2}0\text{ and }\left(\frac{\wp_{n}}{\mathfrak{l}}\right)=-1;\\
x^{p(|\mathfrak{l}|^{2}-1)/2}-1&\text{if }\deg(\mathfrak{l})\equiv_{2}0\text{ and }\left(\frac{\wp_{n}}{\mathfrak{l}}\right)=1;\\
x^{p(|\mathfrak{l}|^{2}-1)}-1&\text{if }\deg(\mathfrak{l})\equiv_{2}1.\end{cases}

Applying Lemma
4.6
with
t=\tilde{s}
together with Lemmas
4.5
and
3.8
gives

g^{(b)}_{r,m_{\mathfrak{l},s},k}\equiv_{\mathfrak{l}^{s}}g^{(b)}_{r,m_{\mathfrak{l},s},k+n_{U}},

for any
k\geq 0
,
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and any
r
. Since
n_{U}
is even and
n_{U}/2\equiv 0\pmod{m_{\mathfrak{l},s}}
, this implies the statement of the lemma (when
p\neq 2
).

If
p=2
then
m_{\mathfrak{l},1}=|\mathfrak{l}|-1
and
m_{\mathfrak{l},s}=p^{\tilde{s}-1}m_{\mathfrak{l},1}
if
s\geq 2
. By Lemma
4.5
, the denominator we are interested in is (the
p^{\tilde{s}-1}
-th power of)
d(x):=(1-x)^{|\mathfrak{l}|-1}-(b\wp_{n}x^{2})^{|\mathfrak{l}|-1}
.
If
\alpha\in A
is a lift of a primitive element mod
\mathfrak{l}
, then
d(x)\equiv_{\mathfrak{l}}\prod_{i}(1-x+\alpha^{i}\wp_{n}x^{2})
, which is a product of pairwise coprime polynomials, none of which are square. Thus
d(x)
divides
x^{|\mathfrak{l}|^{2}-1}-1
mod
\mathfrak{l}
, which implies
g^{(b)}_{r,m_{\mathfrak{l},s},k}\equiv_{\mathfrak{l}^{s}}g^{(b)}_{r,m_{\mathfrak{l},s},k+p^{\tilde{s}}(|\mathfrak{l}|^{2}-1)}
in the usual way.
Since
p^{\tilde{s}}(|\mathfrak{l}|^{2}-1)
divides
n_{U}
and
n_{U}/2\equiv_{m_{\mathfrak{l},s}}0
, we are done.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

We have
U(k+2,l)\equiv_{\mathfrak{l}^{s}}U(k+2+n_{U},l)
.

Proof.

Since
n_{U}
is even, we have
\delta_{k}=\delta_{k+n_{U}}
. Moreover,
b_{\varphi}^{n_{U}}=1
since
n_{U}
is divisible by
q-1
. Hence the statement follows immediately from Lemmas
4.3
and
4.7
.
∎

We now conclude the periodicity of the traces.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
if
\mathfrak{l}\neq\mathfrak{p}
.

The period of the trace is
L:=\text{lcm}(n_{N},n_{U})
. If
p=2
, then
L=2p^{\tilde{s}}(|\mathfrak{l}|^{2}-1)=p^{1+\tilde{s}}(|\mathfrak{l}|^{2}-1)
. If
p\neq 2
, note that if
\wp_{n}
is a square mod
\mathfrak{l}
and
\deg(\mathfrak{l})
is even, then
\lambda\wp_{n}
is a square mod
\mathfrak{l}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Hence
n_{N}\mid n_{U}
in all cases, so
L=n_{U}
.
∎

4.2

The case of
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{p}

Suppose now that
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{p}
. Then we have the following periodicity of the traces.

Theorem 4.9
.

For all
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}({\mathcal{K}}))\equiv_{\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k+2+p^{\tilde{s}}(|\mathfrak{l}|-1),l}({\mathcal{K}})),

where
k_{0}=2s-1
if
n=1
and
k_{0}=s
if
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

Clearly
N(k+2,l)\equiv_{\mathfrak{p}^{s}}0
for all
k\geq 2s-1
if
n=1
and
k\geq s
if
n\geq 2
.

We now establish the period of
U(k+2,l)
. For all
k\geq 2\lfloor(s-1)/n\rfloor
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we have

g^{(b)}_{r,m,k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq\lfloor k/2\rfloor\\
j\equiv r\pmod{m}\end{subarray}}\binom{k-j}{j}(-b\wp_{n})^{j}\equiv_{\mathfrak{p}^{s}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq s-1\\
j\equiv r\pmod{m}\end{subarray}}\binom{k-j}{j}(-b\wp_{n})^{j}.

By Lemma
3.4
,
all binomial coefficients which occur are invariant under
k\mapsto k+p^{\tilde{s}}
, which implies that

g^{(b)}_{r,m,k}\equiv_{\mathfrak{p}^{s}}g^{(b)}_{r,m,k+p^{\tilde{s}}}

for all
k\geq 2\lfloor(s-1)/n\rfloor
,
m\geq 1
,
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
r
.

Now let
N=p^{\tilde{s}}(|\mathfrak{l}|-1)
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. If
p^{s}\neq 2
then
N=2m_{\mathfrak{p},s}
, and by the above,

g^{(b)}_{\lfloor k/2\rfloor-r,m_{\mathfrak{p},s},k}\equiv_{\mathfrak{l}^{s}}g^{(b)}_{\lfloor(k+N)/2\rfloor-r,m_{\mathfrak{p},s},k+N}.

Moreover, since
q-1
divides
N
we have
b^{N}=1
and since
N
is even we have
\delta_{k+N}=\delta_{k}
for all
k
. Hence it follows from Lemma
4.3
that
U(k+2,l)\equiv_{\mathfrak{p}^{s}}U(k+2+N,l)
. Finally if
p^{s}=2
then one sees directly that

U(k+2,l)\equiv_{\mathfrak{l}}\sum_{[(\varphi,\lambda)]\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}})}\frac{a_{\varphi}^{k}b_{\varphi}^{l-k-1}}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}}(\varphi,\lambda)},

which is also invariant under
k\mapsto k+|\mathfrak{l}|-1
.
∎

4.3

Traces modulo
\infty

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}=\{x\in K\ |\ v_{\infty}(x)\geq 0\}
be the local ring of
\infty
-integral elements in
K
. Fix a generator
\pi_{\infty}
of its maximal ideal and recall that we assume
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}/(\pi_{\infty})=\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Define,

\Tr_{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}({\mathcal{K}})):=\frac{1}{(-\wp_{n})^{\lceil k/2\rceil}}\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}({\mathcal{K}})).

Then
\Tr_{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}({\mathcal{K}}))\in\mathcal{O}_{\infty}
; this follows from Theorem
2.8
and the Riemann hypothesis for Drinfeld modules over finite fields, which says that
-2v_{\infty}(a_{\varphi})\leq-v_{\infty}(\wp_{n})
, see
[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
. If
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, then
v_{\infty}=-\deg_{T}
and the strong Ramanujan bound
[
3
, Conjecture 4.3]
is equivalent to the statement

\Tr_{\infty}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}(\mathrm{GL}_{2}(A)))\equiv 0\pmod{\pi_{\infty}^{n\deg(\mathfrak{p})(q-1)/2}}\qquad\text{for all }k,l.

Working in
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}
instead of
A
, we can argue as before to show that
\Tr_{\infty}
is periodic in the weight modulo powers of
\pi_{\infty}
. We sketch the argument here.
Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}})

\displaystyle=\left\{[(\varphi,\lambda)]\in[\mathfrak{M}_{{\mathcal{K}},\mathfrak{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}})]\,\bigg|\,-v_{\infty}(a_{\varphi})<\frac{-v_{\infty}(\wp_{n})}{2}\right\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}})

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{M}_{{\mathcal{K}},\mathfrak{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}})]\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}).

Then we have, for all
k\geq s-1
,

\Tr_{\infty}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}({\mathcal{K}}))\equiv_{\pi_{\infty}^{s}}N_{\infty}(k+2,l)+U_{\infty}(k+2,l),

where

\displaystyle N_{\infty}(k+2,l)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{[(\varphi,\lambda)]\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}})}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{s-1-\delta_{k}}{2}\rfloor}\binom{(k+\delta_{k})/2+j}{2j+\delta_{k}}\left(\frac{a_{\varphi}^{2j+\delta_{k}}}{-\wp_{n}^{2j+\delta_{k}}}\right)\frac{b_{\varphi}^{l-j-(k+\delta_{k})/2-1}}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}}(\varphi,\lambda)};

\displaystyle U_{\infty}(k+2,l)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{[(\varphi,\lambda)]\in\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}})}\sum_{r=0}^{m_{\infty,s}-1}h^{(b_{\varphi})}_{\lfloor k/2\rfloor-r,m_{\infty,s},k}\left(\frac{a_{\varphi}^{2j+\delta_{k}}}{-\wp_{n}^{2j+\delta_{k}}}\right)\frac{b_{\varphi}^{l-k-1}}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}}(\varphi,\lambda)}.

Here we write
m_{\infty,s}:=p^{\tilde{s}}(q-1)/2
(unless
p^{s}=2
, in which case
m_{\infty,s}:=q-1
), and

h^{(b)}_{r,m,k}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq\lfloor k/2\rfloor\\
j\equiv r\pmod{m}\end{subarray}}\binom{k-j}{j}b^{j}.

Using the same arguments as before, we obtain:

Proposition 4.10
.

For all
k\geq s-1
,

\Tr_{\infty}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}(\mathcal{K}))\equiv_{\pi_{\infty}^{s}}\Tr_{\infty}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k+2+p^{1+\tilde{s}}(q^{2}-1),l}(\mathcal{K})).

In particular, this reduces the verification of the strong Ramanujan bound for each
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
to a finite computation:

Corollary 4.11
.

Fix a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and an integer
n\geq 1
. Let
s=n\deg(\mathfrak{p})(q-1)/2
and
\tilde{s}=\lceil\log_{p}(s)\rceil
. Then the strong Ramanujan bound holds for
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}
if it holds on the spaces
\operatorname{S}_{k+2,l}
with
1\leq l\leq q-1
and
0\leq k<p^{1+\tilde{s}}(q^{2}-1)+s
.

5.3

Hecke polynomials

Fix a subgroup
H\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
and a prime
p\geq 5
which does not divide
N
. As before, write
S_{k}(H)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}S_{k}(\Gamma_{i})
, where the
\Gamma_{i}
are the congruence subgroups such that
\mathcal{Y}_{H}(\mathbb{C})\cong\coprod_{i=1}^{d}\Gamma_{i}\backslash\mathcal{H}
. Define for any
k\geq 0
,

\mathfrak{f}_{k+2}(x):=\det(1-T(p)x\ |\ S_{k+2}(H))\in\mathbb{Z}[x].

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose
H
is representable.
Then
\mathfrak{f}_{k}(x)\equiv_{p}\mathfrak{f}_{k+p-1}(x)
for all
k\geq 3
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{X}_{H}
is a scheme, and
k\geq 1
, then the trace formula can equivalently be written as

\det(1-F_{p}x\,|\,S[H,k+2])=\prod_{n\geq 1}\prod_{(E,\varphi)/\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\det(1-F_{p^{n}}x^{n}\,|\,\Sym^{k}(H^{1}(E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})))^{-1},

where the product is over the closed points
(E,\varphi)\in|\mathcal{X}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}|
of degree
n
. If
(E,\varphi)
is a cusp, then

\det(1-F_{p^{n}}x^{n}\,|\,\Sym^{k}(H^{1}(E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})))^{-1}=(1-(\pm 1)^{k}x^{n})^{-1},

depending on whether
E
is of split or non-split multiplicative type. In either case, since
p
is odd, this factor is invariant under
k\mapsto k+p-1
.

If
(E,\varphi)\in|\mathcal{Y}_{H}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}|
, we have

\det(1-F_{p^{n}}x^{n}\,|\,\Sym^{k}(H^{1}(E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})))^{-1}=\prod_{i=0}^{k}(1-\pi_{E}^{i}\bar{\pi}_{E}^{k-i}x^{n})^{-1},

where
X^{2}-a_{1}(E)X+p^{n}=(X-\pi_{E})(X-\bar{\pi}_{E})
. In particular,
\pi_{E}\bar{\pi}_{E}=p^{n}
and hence

\displaystyle\det(1-F_{p^{n}}x^{n}\,|\,\Sym^{k}(H^{1}(E\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell^{\prime}})))^{-1}

\displaystyle\equiv_{p}(1-(\pi_{E}^{k}+\bar{\pi}_{E}^{k})x^{n})^{-1}

\displaystyle\equiv_{p}(1-(\pi_{E}+\bar{\pi}_{E})^{k}x^{n})^{-1}=(1-a_{1}(E)^{k}x^{n})^{-1}.

Since
a_{1}(E)\in\mathbb{Z}
, this is invariant mod
p
under
k\mapsto k+p-1
, provided that
k\geq 1
.

Finally,

\det(1-F_{p}x\,|\,S[H,k+2])=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-a_{p}(f_{i})x+p^{k-1}x^{2})\equiv_{p}\det(1-T(p)x\,|\,S_{k+2}(H)).\qed

Remark 5.6
.

If
H
is not representable, it is in some cases still possible to deduce the conclusion of the proposition by applying Newton’s identities to the traces of
T(p)
on the spaces
W_{k}(H)=\widetilde{S}_{k+p-1}(H)/E_{p-1}\widetilde{S}_{k}(H)
, where
\widetilde{S}_{k}(H)
is the reduction modulo
p
of
S_{k}(H)
and
E_{p-1}
denotes the Eisenstein series of weight
p-1
, which satisfies
E_{p-1}\equiv_{p}1
. Since Newton’s identities involve denominators, one needs to assume that
p>\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}W_{k}(H)
; this holds for instance if
H
corresponds to
\Gamma_{1}(N)
with
N\leq 3

(and
\Gamma_{1}(N)
is representable for
N\geq 4
, see
[
12
, Cor. 2.7.3]
).

The same proof works for Drinfeld modular forms, which gives the following result. Note that
\mathcal{K}
is representable if it is admissible in the sense of
[
1
]
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose
\mathcal{K}
is representable. Then

\det(1-\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}x\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K}))\equiv_{\mathfrak{p}}\det(1-\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}x\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k+|\mathfrak{p}|-1,l}(\mathcal{K}))

for all
k\geq 3
and any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Define a
p
-adic slope
of
T(p)
to be the normalised
p
-adic valuation of a root of
\mathfrak{f}_{k}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
, and denote by
d_{p}(k,\alpha)
its multiplicity. Similarly, for Drinfeld modular forms, denote by
d_{\mathfrak{p}}(k,l,\alpha)
the multiplicity of the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic slope
\alpha
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K}))
.

Corollary 5.8
.

If
k\geq 3
, then
d(k,0)=d(k+p-1,0)
and
d_{\mathfrak{p}}(k,l,0)=d_{\mathfrak{p}}(k+|\mathfrak{p}|-1,l,0)
.

Proof.

By the theory of Newton polygons,
d_{p}(k,0)
equals the degree of
\mathfrak{f}_{k}(x)\pmod{p}
, and
d_{\mathfrak{p}}(k,l,0)
equals the degree of
\det(1-\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}x\,|\,\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K}))\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
.
∎

In particular, with the exception of
k=2
, we recover Hida’s result for elliptic modular forms that the Gouvêa–Mazur conjecture holds for slope 0.

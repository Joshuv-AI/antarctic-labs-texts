Homotopy theory of simplicial parametrized operads
Grégoire Marc

Abstract




Abstract

We define a generalization of (coloured) operads based on double lax functors and we construct a model structure on the associated category of generalized simplicial (coloured) operads. In particular, we obtain a model structure on the category of simplicial (coloured)
{\mathscr{O}}
-operads of Nardin and Shah.

1.
Introduction

Operads were introduced by May
[
May72
]
to study infinite loop spaces, and they play an important role in encoding algebraic structures in different areas of homotopy theory. In this paper, we are interested in coloured operads, which can be used to describe structures such as enriched categories or a ring together with a left module. Roughly speaking, a simplicial coloured operad
{\mathcal{O}}
is the data of a set of colours along with a simplicial set
{\mathcal{O}}((x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),x_{0})
of “multimorphisms from
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
to
x_{0}
” for every list of colours
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
. These simplicial sets are equipped with actions of the corresponding symmetric groups that “permute the entries” along with identities and an associative composition. The homotopy theory of simplicial operads has been studied in the non-coloured case in the work of Berger and Moerdijk
[
BM03
]
, and a model structure on simplicial coloured operads has been constructed later on independently by Cisinski and Moerdijk
[
CM13b
]
and by Robertson
[
Rob11
]
. Simplicial coloured operads provide one model for the theory of
\infty
-operads

[
Lur17
]
that is known to be equivalent to the other models, combining the work of Cisinski and Moerdijk
[
CM11
,
CM13a
,
CM13b
]
, Barwick
[
Bar18
]
and Chu, Haugseng and Heuts
[
CHH18
]
.

In recent years, equivariant homotopy theory has become an increasingly active area of research, spurred in part by Hill, Hopkins, and Ravenel’s resolution of the Kervaire invariant one problem
[
HHR16
]
. In particular, the theory of equivariant operads has gained particular attention through the development of
N_{\infty}
-operads
by Blumberg and Hill, which generalize
E_{\infty}
-operads to the equivariant setting. In contrast with the non-equivariant setting, where all
E_{\infty}
-operads are equivalent,

N_{\infty}
-operads are not all equivalent. Instead, they form a poset that can be described in terms of
G
-indexing systems
, following work of Blumberg and Hill

[
BH15
]
, Bonventre and Pereira
[
BP21
]
, Gutiérrez and White
[
GW18
]
,
and Rubin
[
Rub21
]
. The study of the combinatorics of these
G
-indexing systems has recently become an active research area, for instance with the work of Balchin, Barnes, and Roitzheim
[
BBR21
]
or Balchin, MacBrough, and Ormsby
[
BMO25
]
. The study of equivariant operads is not limited to
N_{\infty}
-operads, see for example Hill’s work on equivariant little disks operads
[
Hil22
]
.

In
[
BH15
]
, Blumberg and Hill defined the notion of
graph equivalences of
G
-operads
, which is part of a model structure on the category of simplicial
G
-operads, as proven independently by Gutiérrez and White
[
GW18
]
and Bonventre and Pereira
[
BP21
]
. This graph model structure has then been extended to
G
-simplicial coloured operads
by Bonventre and Pereira in
[
BP22b
]
. Moreover, other models for equivariant operads, such as
equivariant dendroidal sets
and
equivariant dendroidal spaces
, have been developed and compared by Bonventre and Pereira in
[
Per18
,
BP20
,
BP22a
]
.

In the language of
\infty
-categories, based on Elmendorf’s Theorem
[
Elm83
]
, equivariant homotopy theory can be formalized using the theory of
parametrized
\infty
-categories
, as developed by Barwick, Dotto, Glasman, Nardin, and Shah in
[
BDG+16
,
Nar16
,
Sha17
]
. This theory provides proper categorical foundations for higher equivariant algebra, and it has known recent further developments, for instance with the work of Cnossen, Lenz, and Liskens
[
CLL23
,
CLL25a
,
CLL25b
]
or with the work of Hilman
[
Hil22
]
. In particular, Lurie’s theory of
\infty
-operads
[
Lur17
]
admits a generalization in the equivariant world through the theory of
parametrized
\infty
-operads
of Nardin and Shah
[
NS22
]
, see also work of Barkan, Haugseng and Steinebrunner
[
BHS24
]
. In analogy with classical higher algebra, the theory of parametrized
\infty
-operads is expected to play a central role in
higher equivariant algebra
. Progress in this direction has been made recently, for instance with the work of Stewart
[
Ste25a
,
Ste25b
,
Ste25c
]
.

The theory of equivariant operads has thus two facets: the classical theory of equivariant simplicial (or topological) operads and the theory of parametrized
\infty
-operads. Both these approaches have been studied with different perspectives, and it is natural to expect them to be the two sides of the same coin. One way to approach a comparison between these two theories is to introduce an intermediary player that shares one feature with each of these theories and that can be compared with both of them. A good candidate is the theory of
simplicial parametrized operads
as introduced by Nardin and Shah in
[
NS22
]
. The theory of simplicial parametrized operads and the theory of equivariant simplicial operads have one feature in common: they are both defined as model categories and can hopefully be compared via a Quillen adjunction. On the other hand, the theory of parametrized simplicial operads shares with parametrized
\infty
-operads that both these theories live in the parametrized world.

1.1.
Main results

In this paper, motivated by our overarching goal to provide a comparison of all models for equivariant operads, we construct a model structure on the category of simplicial (coloured) parametrized operads. In order to achieve this, we consider a generalization of the theory of (coloured) symmetric operads that subsumes enriched categories, non-symmetric operads, and Nardin and Shah’s parametrized operads. Our theory is based on the notion of
{\mathbf{F}}
-multicategories
(see
2.1
for a definition), that is a specialization of Leinster’s generalized multicategories
[
Lei04
]
. If
{\mathcal{V}}
is a cartesian category, then every
{\mathbf{F}}
-multicategory
\mathbb{M}
gives rise to a notion of
(coloured)
\mathbb{M}
-operads in
{\mathcal{V}}
for which
\mathbb{M}
plays the role played by the symmetric groupoid
\Sigma_{*}
for symmetric operads. Every
\mathbb{M}
-operad in
{\mathcal{V}}
has an underlying
\mathbb{M}
-collection in
{\mathcal{V}}
, and we prove, under some assumptions on
\mathbb{M}
and
{\mathcal{V}}
, the existence of a composition product
\circ_{\mathbb{M}}
on the category
\operatorname{Coll}_{\mathbb{M}}({\mathcal{V}})
of
\mathbb{M}
-collections in
{\mathcal{V}}
for which algebras are precisely
\mathbb{M}
-operads in
{\mathcal{V}}
. More precisely, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

(
3.4
)
For every
{\mathbf{F}}
-multicategory
\mathbb{M}
that is target right fibrant in the sense of
3.4
and for every cartesian closed category
{\mathcal{V}}
, the category
\operatorname{Coll}_{\mathbb{M}}({\mathcal{V}})
of
\mathbb{M}
-collections in
{\mathcal{V}}
is endowed with the structure of a monoidal category for which the category of algebras is precisely the category of
\mathbb{M}
-operads in
{\mathcal{V}}
.

Using this generalization of the composition product, and the fact that
\operatorname{Coll}_{\mathbb{M}}(\operatorname{sSet})
can be endowed with a projective model structure, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem B
.

(
4.1
)
For every target right fibrant
{\mathbf{F}}
-multicategory
\mathbb{M}
, the category
\operatorname{sOp}_{\mathbb{M}}
admits a model structure that is right lifted from
\operatorname{Coll}_{\mathbb{M}}(\operatorname{sSet})
with respect to the free-forgetful adjunction

F_{\mathbb{M}}\colon\operatorname{Coll}_{\mathbb{M}}(\operatorname{sSet})\rightleftarrows\operatorname{sOp}_{\mathbb{M}}\colon U_{\mathbb{M}}.

One main feature of our notion of generalized operads is that it provides a convenient way to encode coloured operads. For every
\mathbb{M}
-set of colours

A
(in the sense of
2.3
),
A
-coloured operads are by definition
\mathbb{M}^{A}
-operads for some
{\mathbf{F}}
-multicategory
\mathbb{M}^{A}
. Using this description of coloured
\mathbb{M}
-operads, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem C
.

(
4.28
)
For every
{\mathbf{F}}
-multicategory
\mathbb{M}
that is target right fibrant, the category of simplicial coloured
\mathbb{M}
-operads admits a model structure for which
the weak equivalences are the fully faithful essentially surjective morphisms and for which the fibrations are the isofibrations (See
4.2
for all the terminology used here).

We can specialize this theorem to obtain a model structure on Nardin and Shah’s simplicial parametrized operads.

Corollary D
.

(
4.2
)
For every category
{\mathscr{O}}
and every orbital subcategory
{\mathscr{T}}
of
{\mathscr{O}}
, the category
{\operatorname{sOp}_{({\mathscr{O}},{\mathscr{T}})}^{\operatorname{col}}}
of simplicial coloured
({\mathscr{O}},{\mathscr{T}})
-parametrized operads admits a model structure for which
the weak equivalences are the fully faithful essentially surjective morphisms and for which the fibrations are the isofibrations (respectively defined in
4.2
).

The results of this paper provide a foundational work needed for comparing all models of equivariant operads. In
5
, we outline how these comparisons are structured, which is currently work in progress.

1.2.
Structure of the paper

In
2
, we define the notion of
{\mathbf{F}}
-multicategories as well as our notion of generalized operads. In
3
, we construct, for a target right fibrant
{\mathbf{F}}
-multicategory
\mathbb{M}
, a convolution product on the category of
\mathbb{M}
-collections for which the algebras are precisely the
\mathbb{M}
-operads. In
4
, for a target right fibrant
{\mathbf{F}}
-multicategory
\mathbb{M}
, we construct a model structure on the category of simplicial (coloured)
\mathbb{M}
-operads. Finally, in
5
, we outline a program that applies the tools developed in this paper to compare all models of equivariant operads, which is currently work in progress.

1.3.
Related work

For a finite group
G
and a
G
-transfer system
{\mathcal{T}}
, the category
\operatorname{sOp}_{({\mathscr{O}}_{G},{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathcal{T}}})}
defined in
2.4
should be equivalent to the category of
simplicial
{\mathcal{T}}
-partial genuine equivariant operads
of Bonventre and Pereira
[
BP21
]
, however, such a comparison has not been made yet. In particular, we expect that the model structure on
\operatorname{sOp}_{({\mathscr{O}}_{G},{\mathscr{O}}_{{\mathcal{T}}})}
that we obtain in

corresponds to their model structure.

This paper is based on the idea that the theory of double categories can be used to implement operadic theories. This idea is not new, and such considerations have already been made by Pisani in
[

[
Hau22
]
.

2.1.

The goal of this section is to define the notion of

-multicategories as a specialization of Leinster’s generalized multicategories
[
Lei04
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
Ben85
, Section 3]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a category. The
completion by finite coproducts of
{\mathcal{C}}
is the category
{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
whose objects are sequences
\underline{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
of objects of
{\mathcal{C}}
and, given two such sequences
\underline{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
and
\underline{Y}=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
, whose set of morphisms
{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})
is

\coprodop\displaylimits_{\alpha\colon\underline{n}\to\underline{m}}\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i},Y_{\alpha(i)}),

where
\underline{n}
denotes the finite set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
The composition of morphisms in
{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
is naturally induced by the composition of maps of finite sets and by the composition of morphisms in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For every category
{\mathcal{C}}
, the category
{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
admits finite coproducts and satisfies the following universal property: for every category
{\mathcal{D}}
with finite coproducts, the canonical inclusion functor
{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
induces an equivalence of categories

\operatorname{Fun}_{\sqcup}({\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}})\to\operatorname{Fun}({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}})

where
\operatorname{Fun}_{\sqcup}({\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}})
denotes the full subcategory of
\operatorname{Fun}({\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}})
spanned by these functors that preserve finite coproducts.

Proof.

This proposition is classical. The coproducts in the category
{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
are given by the concatenation of sequences of objects in
{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

For every category
{\mathcal{C}}
, the category
{({\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}})}^{\operatorname{op}}
is the completion by finite products of
{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\operatorname{op}}
. In particular, for every functor
F\colon{{\mathcal{C}}}^{\operatorname{op}}\to{\mathcal{V}}
where
{\mathcal{V}}
is a category with finite products,
F
can be extended into a functor
\widetilde{F}\colon{({\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}})}^{\operatorname{op}}\to{\mathcal{V}}
that preserves finite products defined explicitly on objects by the formula

\widetilde{F}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}F(X_{i}).

Proposition 2.4
.

The functor
{\mathbf{F}}\colon\operatorname{Cat}\to\operatorname{Cat}
is naturally endowed with the structure of a cartesian monad.

Proof.

For every category
{\mathcal{C}}
, we obtain a functor
\mu_{\mathbf{F}}\colon{\mathbf{F}}^{2}{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
induced by the identity
\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{C}}}\colon{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
using
2.1
. A direct verification shows that the natural transformation
\mu\colon{\mathbf{F}}^{2}{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
thus obtained, along with the inclusion functor
{\mathcal{C}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
, induce the structure of a cartesian monad on
{\mathbf{F}}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Note that the structure of a monad on
{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
exists only because we chose a strict model for
{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{C}}
. For a different model, we would only obtain the structure of a pseudo monad on
{\mathbf{F}}
.

2.2.
\mathbb{M}

In this section, we define our generalization of operads based on the notion of
{\mathbf{F}}

Remark 2.9
.

Recall that every monoidal category
({\mathcal{C}},\otimes)
can be seen as a weak double category with a trivial category of vertical morphisms and whose category of horizontal morphisms and squares is
{\mathcal{C}}
itself. The horizontal composition is given by the monoidal structure of
({\mathcal{C}},\otimes)
.

We now recall from
[
GP99
]
the definition of lax functors between weak double categories. We only recall the definition when the target is a monoidal category and when the source is a (strict) double category.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
{\mathbb{D}}
be a double category and let
({\mathcal{C}},\otimes_{{\mathcal{C}}})
be a monoidal category. A
lax functor
F\colon{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is a functor
F\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{C}}
along with a natural transformation
\alpha\colon\otimes_{{\mathcal{C}}}\circ(F\times F)\Rightarrow F\circ\odot
of functors

and a natural transformation
\eta\colon 1_{{\mathcal{C}}}\Rightarrow F\circ 1_{{\mathbb{D}}}
of functors
{\mathbb{D}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{C}}
satisfying the following compatibilities:

(1)

for every
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
in
{\mathbb{D}}_{1}\times_{{\mathbb{D}}_{0}}{\mathbb{D}}_{1}\times_{{\mathbb{D}}_{0}}{\mathbb{D}}_{1}
, the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(f_{1})\otimes_{{\mathcal{C}}}F(f_{2})\otimes_{{\mathcal{C}}}F(f_{3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(f_{1}\odot f_{2})\otimes_{{\mathcal{C}}}F(f_{3})}

Proposition 4.6
.

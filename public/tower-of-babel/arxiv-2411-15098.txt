OminiControl: Minimal and Universal Control for Diffusion Transformer
Zhenxiong Tan, Songhua Liu, Xingyi Yang, Qiaochu Xue, Xinchao Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce OminiControl, a highly versatile and parameter-efficient framework that integrates image conditions into pre-trained Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models. At its core, OminiControl leverages a parameter reuse mechanism, enabling the DiT to encode image conditions using itself as a powerful backbone and process them with its flexible multi-modal attention processors. Unlike existing methods, which rely heavily on additional encoder modules with complex architectures, OminiControl (1) effectively and efficiently incorporates injected image conditions with only 0.1 ~0.1 % additional parameters, and (2) addresses a wide range of image conditioning tasks in a unified manner, including subject-driven generation and spatially-aligned conditions such as edges, depth, and more. Remarkably, these capabilities are achieved by training on images generated by the DiT itself, which is particularly beneficial for subject-driven generation. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that OminiControl outperforms existing UNet-based and DiT-adapted models in both subject-driven and spatially-aligned conditional generation. Additionally, we release our training dataset, Subjects200K, a diverse collection of over 200,000 identity-consistent images, along with an efficient data synthesis pipeline to advance research in subject-consistent generation. 1 1 1 Code and dataset are available at https://github.com/Yuanshi9815/OminiControl

Introduction

The ability to generate high-quality images with precise user control remains a
central challenge in computer vision. While diffusion models
Ho et al. 2020
;
Rombach et al. 2021
have significantly advanced the state of the art in image
generation, surpassing traditional GAN-based approaches
Goodfellow et al. 2020

in both quality and diversity, the problem of fine-grained control persists.
Text-conditioned diffusion models
Rombach et al. 2021
;
Podell et al. 2023
;
Esser et al. 2024
;
Labs 2024a

serve as the primary paradigm for controllable generation, enabling users to guide
the synthesis process through natural language descriptions. However, these models
fundamentally lack the capacity to specify exact spatial details and visual attributes
that users often need to control.

To address this limitation, recent works have turned to image-based control
methods
Zhang et al. 2023
;
Ye et al. 2023
;
Mou et al. 2024
;
Zhao et al. 2024
;
Li et al. 2024a
;
Shi et al. 2024
;
Xiao et al. 2024
;
Li et al. 2024b
;
Qin et al. 2023
;
Wei et al. 2023

as a promising solution to achieve fine-grained control. These approaches enable
users to specify their intentions through reference images or visual hints,
offering more precise guidance than text alone
Rombach et al. 2021
.

However, current image control methods face several key challenges: First, existing
methods require substantial architectural modifications with dedicated control modules,
leading to significant parameter overhead
Zhang et al. 2023
;
Ye et al. 2023
. Second,
these approaches show clear task bias – they typically work well for either spatially
aligned controls (e.g., edge-guided or depth-guided generation
Zhang et al. 2023
;
Mou et al. 2024
;
Zhao et al. 2024
;
Qin et al. 2023
)
or spatially unaligned ones (e.g., style transfer or subject-driven generation
Ye et al. 2023
;
Li et al. 2024b
;
Li et al. 2024a
;
Shi et al. 2024
), but rarely both. Third,
current approaches are built primarily for UNet architectures
Ronneberger et al. 2015
.
These approaches may yield suboptimal results when applied to newer Diffusion Transformer
(DiT) models
Peebles and Xie 2023
due to fundamental architectural differences,
despite the latter’s superior generation capabilities
Labs 2024a
;
Chen et al. 2023
;
Esser et al. 2024
.

These challenges, particularly within the emerging paradigm of Diffusion Transformer
models, motivate us to rethink the fundamental approach to image control. We propose

OminiControl
, an
omni
-capable yet
minimal
framework
that achieves effective control by leveraging existing components within the DiT
architecture.

To achieve the goal of minimal architectural and parameters overhead, OminiControl
first utilizes the built-in VAE encoder to project conditioning images into the
same latent space as the target images.
The conditional tokens are concatenated with the noisy latent and processed by the DiT using multi-modal attention
Ruan et al. 2023
;
Peebles and Xie 2023
. This unified sequence allows the denoising network to handle both signals directly, requiring only minimal LoRA
Devalal and Karthikeyan 2018
fine-tuning of the existing transformer blocks. Unlike existing methods such as IP-Adapter
Ye et al. 2023
that relies on
separate CLIP
Radford et al. 2021
feature extractors and ControlNet
Labs 2024b

that requires additional control modules, OminiControl significantly reduces
architectural complexity and parameter overhead.

Building on this, OminiControl unifies controlled generation tasks using the DiT architecture. By modifying explicit position embeddings
Su et al. 2024
in the attention mechanism, it handles both spatially aligned and non-aligned tasks. By explicitly controlling
condition token positions in the attention mechanism, OminiControl not only handles non-aligned
control tasks through semantic attention, but also achieves spatially-aligned
control through flexible token interactions, rather than relying on rigid spatial
feature addition as in ControlNet
Zhang et al. 2023
.

Alongside the design of OminiControl, to fully realize its potential in diverse
control scenarios, we address a critical challenge in subject-driven generation –
the scarcity of high-quality training data for identity preservation. To this end,
we develop an automated data synthesis pipeline specifically designed for
subject-driven generation tasks. This pipeline builds upon a key observation that
DiT models can generate identity-consistent image pairs with remarkable consistency
by simply providing appropriate prompts
Labs 2024a
. Using this
pipeline, we create a comprehensive dataset
Subjects200K
including over
200,000 diverse images with rich identity variations. To facilitate future research,
we have released both our dataset and the complete pipeline implementation as
open-source resources
2

.

Extensive experiments demonstrate that our approach achieves better performance across

Labs 2024a
,
we evaluate
OminiControl

In summary, we highlight our contributions as follows:

•

We propose OminiControl, a minimal and universal control framework for DiT

•

We develop an automated data synthesis pipeline that leverages DiT’s inherent
capability in generating identity-consistent image pairs. Using this, we create

•

We conduct extensive experiments on various control tasks including edge-guided,
depth-guided, and subject-driven generation. Building upon Flux, OminiControl outperforms
existing approaches in both their original UNet implementations and DiT adaptations.

Related works

2.1
Diffusion models

Diffusion models have emerged as a powerful framework for image generation
Ho et al. 2020
;
Rombach et al. 2021
, demonstrating success across diverse tasks including
text-to-image synthesis
Rombach et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2023
;
Saharia et al. 2022b
, image-to-image translation
Saharia et al. 2022a
,
and image editing
Meng et al. 2021
;
Avrahami et al. 2022
. Recent advances have
led to significant improvements in both quality and efficiency, notably through
the introduction of latent diffusion models
Rombach et al. 2021
. To
further enhance generative capabilities, large-scale transformer architectures
have been integrated into these frameworks, leading to advanced models like DiT
Peebles and Xie 2023
;
Chen et al. 2023
;
Labs 2024a
;
Chen et al. 2024
. Building on these architectural
innovations, FLUX
Labs 2024a
incorporates transformer-based design
with flow matching objectives
Lipman et al. 2022
, achieving state-of-the-art
generation performance.

2.2
Controllable generation with diffusion models

Controllable generation has been extensively studied in the context of diffusion
models. Text-to-image models
Rombach et al. 2021
;
Podell et al. 2023
have
established a foundation for conditional generation, while various approaches
have been developed to incorporate additional control signals such as image. Notable
methods include ControlNet
Zhang et al. 2023
, enabling spatially aligned
control in diffusion models, and T2I-Adapter
Mou et al. 2024
, which improves
efficiency with lightweight adapters. UniControl
Qin et al. 2023
uses Mixture-of-Experts
(MoE) to unify different spatial conditions, further reducing model size. However,
these methods rely on spatially adding condition features to the denoising
network’s hidden states, inherently limiting their effectiveness for spatially non-aligned
tasks like subject-driven generation. IP-Adapter
Ye et al. 2023
addresses this
by introducing cross-attention through an additional encoder, and SSR-Encoder
Zhang et al. 2024

further enhances identity preservation in image-conditioned tasks. Despite these
advances
Li et al. 2024a
;
Ma et al. 2024
;
Gal et al. 2022
, a unified solution for both
spatially aligned and non-aligned tasks remains elusive.

Methods

3.1
Preliminary

The Diffusion Transformer (DiT) model
Peebles and Xie 2023
, employed in
architectures like FLUX.1
Labs 2024a
, Stable Diffusion 3
Rombach et al. 2021
,
and PixArt
Chen et al. 2023
, uses transformer as denoising network to
refine noisy image tokens iteratively.

A DiT model processes two types of tokens: noisy image tokens
{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
and text condition tokens
{C}_{\text{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times d}
,
where
d
is the embedding dimension,
N
and
M
are the number of image and text
tokens respectively (Figure
2
). Throughout the network, these tokens
maintain consistent shapes as they pass through multiple transformer blocks.

In FLUX.1, each DiT block consists of layer normalization followed by Multi-Modal Attention
(MMA)
Ruan et al. 2023
, which incorporates Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)
Su et al. 2024

to encode spatial information. For image tokens
{X}
, RoPE applies rotation
matrices based on the token’s position
(i,j)
in the 2D grid:

{X}_{i,j}\rightarrow{X}_{i,j}\cdot R(i,j),

(1)

where
R(i,j)
is the rotation matrix at position
(i,j)
. Text tokens

{C}_{\text{T}}
undergo the same transformation with their positions set to

(0,0)
.

The multi-modal attention mechanism then projects the position-encoded tokens
into query
Q
, key
K
, and value
V
representations. It enables the computation
of attention between all tokens:

\text{MMA}([{X};{C}_{\text{T}}])=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}\right)V,

(2)

where
[{X};{C}_{\text{T}}]
denotes the concatenation of image and text tokens.
This formulation enables bidirectional attention.

Building upon the DiT architecture, with FLUX.1
Labs 2024a
as
our implementation base,we aim to develop OminiControl, an omni-capable yet minimal control
framework that guides generation through visual conditions.

Leveraging existing architecture

To achieve both omni-capability and minimal architectural changes, OminiControl
first reuses the VAE
Kingma 2013
;
Rombach et al. 2021
encoder
from the base DiT model to project conditioning images into the same latent
space as noisy image tokens. This approach contrasts with previous methods that rely
on separate feature extractors like CLIP
Radford et al. 2021
;
Ye et al. 2023
or
additional control modules
Zhang et al. 2024
, significantly reducing
architectural complexity.

The encoded condition tokens
{C}_{\text{I}}
share the
same dimensionality and latent space as the noisy image tokens
{X}
, enabling
them to be directly processed by the transformer blocks. Since both condition and
image tokens reside in the same latent space, OminiControl leverages the existing
DiT blocks to process them jointly, requiring only minimal fine-tuning through LoRA
Devalal and Karthikeyan 2018

to adapt these blocks for handling the additional condition tokens, instead of
duplicating the entire network as done in ControlNet
Zhang et al. 2023
.

Unified sequence processing

Building upon these shared latent space, we next need to integrate condition tokens into the model for flexible control.

Previous methods like ControlNet
Zhang et al. 2023

and T2I-Adapter
Mou et al. 2024
incorporate condition images through direct feature
addition:

{X}\leftarrow{X}+{C}_{\text{I}},

(3)

where the condition features
{C}_{\text{I}}
are spatially aligned and added to
the noisy image tokens
{X}
. While effective for spatially aligned tasks, this
approach faces two limitations: (1) it lacks flexibility for non-aligned scenarios
where spatial correspondence doesn’t exist, and (2) the rigid addition operation
constrains potential interactions between condition and image tokens.

Instead, OminiControl directly concatenates condition tokens with noisy image tokens

[{X};{C}_{\text{T}};{C}_{\text{I}}]
for multi-modal attention processing. This
formulation enables flexible token interactions through DiT’s multi-modal
attention mechanism, allowing direct relationships to emerge between any pair of
tokens without imposing rigid spatial constraints.

As shown in Figure
5
, this approach effectively handles both
spatially aligned and non-aligned tasks, with attention maps revealing clear cross-token
relationships and interaction patterns. Empirically, this unified sequence approach
consistently achieves lower training loss compared to direct feature addition (Figure
4(a)
),
demonstrating its superior conditioning capability across diverse generation
scenarios.

and image condition tokens
C_{I}
. Strong diagonal

Position-aware token interaction

While the unified sequence approach allows flexible token interactions, encoding position information for the newly appended conditional tokens is not straightforward.

In FLUX.1, the RoPE
Su et al. 2024
mechanism
requires each token to have an assigned position index
(i,j)
, which might directly
influence how tokens attend to each other during multi-modal attention.

Specifically, for a 512
\times
512 input image, the VAE encoder produces a 32
\times
32 grid
of latent tokens, with each token assigned a position index
(i,j)
where
i,j\in[0,31]
. Text tokens
{C}_{\text{T}}
maintain fixed positions at
(0,0)

following FLUX.1’s convention. Therefore, it is essential to assign proper position indexing the conditioned tokens.

In our method, we assign position indices differently for spatially aligned and non-aligned tasks. For condition image tokens
{C}_{\text{I}}
, we propose
a dynamic positioning strategy based on the control task:

(i,j)_{{C}_{\text{I}}}=\begin{cases}(i,j)_{{X}}&\text{for aligned tasks}\\
(i,j)+\Delta&\text{for non-aligned tasks}\end{cases}

(4)

where
\Delta
is a fixed offset vector.

For spatially aligned tasks like edge-guided
generation, condition tokens share positions with their corresponding noisy
image tokens, facilitating precise spatial correspondence. For non-aligned tasks
like subject-driven generation, we shift condition tokens’ positions by
\Delta
,
ensuring no spatial overlap with
{X}
. As demonstrated in Figure
4(b)
,
this shifting leads to faster convergence and better performance for non-aligned
tasks.

Controllable conditioning strength

While OminiControl’s unified sequence processing and multi-modal attention enable
effective token interactions during training, practical applications often require
adjustable conditioning strength at test time.

We achieve this by introducing a bias term into the multi-modal attention computation.
Specifically, for a given strength factor
\gamma
, we modify the attention
operation in Equation
2
to:

\text{MMA}([{C}_{\text{T}};{X};{C}_{\text{I}}])=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}+B(\gamma)\right)V,

(5)

where
B(\gamma)
is a bias matrix modulating the attention between concatenated
tokens
[{C}_{\text{T}};{X};{C}_{\text{I}}]
. Given

{C}_{\text{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times d}
and

{X},{C}_{\text{I}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
, the bias matrix has the structure:

B(\gamma)=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{M\times M}&\mathbf{0}_{M\times N}&\mathbf{0}_{M\times N}\\
\mathbf{0}_{N\times M}&\mathbf{0}_{N\times N}&\log(\gamma)\mathbf{1}_{N\times N}\\
\mathbf{0}_{N\times M}&\log(\gamma)\mathbf{1}_{N\times N}&\mathbf{0}_{N\times N}\end{bmatrix}.

(6)

This formulation preserves the original attention patterns within each token
type while scaling attention weights between
{X}
and
{C}_{\text{I}}
by

\log(\gamma)
. At test time, setting
\gamma=0
removes the condition’s
influence, while
\gamma>1
enhances it. This allows users to flexibly adjust
the balance between text and image conditions without retraining the model.

Experiment

4.1
Setup

Tasks and base model.
We evaluate our method on
two categories of conditional generation tasks: spatially aligned tasks (including
Canny-to-image, depth-to-image, masked-based inpainting, and colorization) and
subject-driven generation. We build our method upon FLUX.1
Labs 2024a
,
a latent rectified flow transformer model for image generation. By default, we use
FLUX.1-dev to generate images for spatially aligned tasks. In subject-driven generation
tasks, we switch to FLUX.1-schnell as we observed it tend to produce better visual
quality.

Implement details.
Our method utilizes LoRA
Devalal and Karthikeyan 2018
for fine-tuning
the base model with a default rank of 4. To preserve the model’s original capabilities
and achieve flexibility, the LoRA scale is set to 0 when processing non-condition
tokens by default.

Training.
Our model is trained with a batch size of 1 and gradient accumulation
over 8 steps (effective batch size of 8). We employ the Prodigy optimizer
Mishchenko and Defazio 2024

with safeguard warmup and bias correction enabled, setting the weight decay to 0.01.
The experiments are conducted on 2 NVIDIA H100 GPUs (80GB each). For spatially aligned
tasks, models are trained for 50,000 iterations, while subject-driven generation
models are trained for 15,000 iterations.

Baselines.
For spatially aligned tasks, we compare our method with both
the original ControlNet
Zhang et al. 2023
and T2I-Adapter
Mou et al. 2024

on Stable Diffusion 1.5, as well as ControlNetPro
Labs 2024b
, the
FLUX.1 implementation of ControlNet. For subject-driven generation, we compare
with IP-Adapter
Ye et al. 2023
, evaluating its implementations FLUX.1
XLabs-AI 2024
.

Evaluation metrics.

We evaluate our model on both spatially aligned tasks and subject-driven generation.
For spatially aligned tasks, we assess two aspects: generation quality and
controllability. Generation quality is measured using FID
Heusel et al. 2017
,
SSIM, MAN-IQA
Yang et al. 2022
, and MUSIQ
Ke et al. 2021
for visual
fidelity, along with CLIP Score
Radford et al. 2021
for semantic consistency.
For controllability, we compute F1 Score between extracted and input edge maps in
edge-conditioned generation, and MSE between extracted and original condition
maps for other tasks (using Depth Anything for depth, color channel separation
for colorization, etc.). For subject-driven generation, we propose a five-criteria
framework evaluating both preservation of subject characteristics (identity
preservation, material quality, color fidelity, natural appearance) and accuracy
of requested modifications, with all assessments conducted through GPT-4o’s vision
capabilities to ensure systematic evaluation. Detailed evaluation methodology is
presented in Appendix
B.1
.

Evaluation protocol.
We conducted evaluations on two datasets. For
spatially aligned tasks, we use COCO 2017 validation set (5,000 images) resized to
512
\times
512, using task-specific conditions and associated captions as
prompts with a fixed seed of 42. For subject-driven generation, we test on 750 text-condition
pairs (30 subjects × 25 prompts) from DreamBooth
Ruiz et al. 2023

dataset with 5 different seeds, using one selected image per subject as the condition.

For subject-driven generation, our model takes a reference image of a subject (e.g.,
a plush toy or an object) and a text description as input, aiming to generate novel
images of the same subject following the text guidance while preserving its key characteristics.

To validate the effectiveness of our Subjects200K dataset described in Section
3.3
,
we compare two training strategies for this task. The first approach relies on traditional
data augmentation, where we apply random cropping, rotation, scaling, and adjustments
to contrast, saturation, and color to the original images. The second approach
utilizes our Subjects200K dataset. As shown in Figure
9
, the model
trained with data augmentation only learns to replicate the input conditions
with minimal changes. In the first row, it simply places the taco plush toy in a
bright room setting while maintaining its exact appearance and pose. Similarly,
in the second row, the yellow alarm clock is reproduced with nearly identical details
despite the window-side placement instruction. In contrast, our Subjects200K-trained
model demonstrates the ability to generate diverse yet consistent views of the subjects
while faithfully following the text prompts.

Conditioning depth.
FLUX.1’s transformer architecture features two distinct
types of blocks: early blocks that employ separate normalization modules for
different modalities tokens (text and image) and later blocks that share unified
normalization across all tokens. As shown in Table
3
,
experiments reveal that restricting condition signal integration to only these
early blocks results in insufficient controllability over the generation process.
This suggests that allowing the condition signals to influence the entire transformer
stack is crucial for achieving the desired levels of control over the output.
Notably, this finding indicates that the preview approaches

5
Conclusion

OminiControl offers parameter-efficient image-conditioned control for Diffusion Transformers across diverse tasks using a unified token approach, without extra modules.
Our method outperforms traditional approaches, and the new Subjects200K dataset—featuring over 200,000 high-quality, subject-consistent images—supports advancements in subject-consistent generation.
Results confirm OminiControl’s scalability and effectiveness in diffusion models.

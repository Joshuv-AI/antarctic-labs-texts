Spacetime decay of mild solutions and conditional quantitative transfer of regularity of the incompressible Navier–Stokes Equations from ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} to bounded domains
Siran Li, Xiangxiang Su

Abstract
. This paper is motivated by the "transfer of regularity" phenomenon for the incompressible Navier–Stokes Equations (NSE) in dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 ; that is, the strong solutions of NSE on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} can be nicely approximated by those on sufficiently large domains Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} under the no-slip boundary condition. Based on the spacetime decay estimates of mild solutions of NSE established by Miyakawa [ 16 ] , Schonbek [ 21 ] and others, we obtain quantitative estimates on higher-order derivatives of velocity and pressure for the incompressible Navier–Stokes flow on large domains under certain additional smallness assumptions of the Stokes’ system and/or the initial velocity, thus complementing the results obtained by Robinson [ 19 ] and Ożánski [ 17 ] .

Introduction

The well-posedness of the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations (NSE) is a long-standing central problem in mathematical hydrodynamics and Partial Differential Equations (PDE):

(0.1a)

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}\mathbf{v}-{\Delta\mathbf{v}}+\nu({\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla})\mathbf{v}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times[0,T],

(0.1b)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times[0,T],

(0.1c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{v}_{0}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega.

The vector field
\mathbf{v}
and scalar field
p
represent the velocity and pressure of the fluid, respectively, and
\nu>0
is the viscosity coefficient. Throughout this note,
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
;
n\geq 3
.

The theoretical literature on the well-posedness theory of NSE is abundant; we only mention here the recent survey
[
25
]
by Tao. In this contribution, we focus on one problem on the analysis of NSE arising primarily from experimental investigations. As the basis for various physical and numerical experiments — most notably, Schiller
et al

[
20
]
and Kerr
[
11
]
on the trefoil configurations of vorticity —
for sufficiently localised initial velocity
{\bf v}_{0}
, the NSE solutions on
\Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is expected to nicely approximate that on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, provided that
\Omega
is large enough.

This phenomenon was rigorously analysed by Robinson
[
19
]
with
\Omega=\ell\mathbb{T}^{3}=[-\ell,\ell]^{3}
, and was referred to as the “transfer of regularity” from
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to
\Omega
therein. More precisely,
[
19
, Theorem 7.1]
establishes that if the strong solution to NSE on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
exists up to time
T
, then for
\ell>0
sufficiently large, the strong solution to NSE on
\Omega=\ell\mathbb{T}^{3}
subject to the same initial datum also exists up to
T
, and their difference tends to zero in
\bigcap_{1\leq r<\infty}L^{r}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
as
\ell\nearrow\infty
.

The first quantitative version of Robinson’s result was obtained by Ożański
[
17
]
, in which Euclidean balls
\Omega=B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
were considered in place of the tori
\ell\mathbb{T}^{3}

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
{R}_{0}>0
,
M\geq 1
, and

. Assume that
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in{C}_{0}^{5}\left({B}_{{R}_{0}};\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)
is divergence-free. Suppose that

is a strong solution of (
0.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T]

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}(t)\|_{H^{5}}+\|\mathbf{v}(t)\|_{W^{5,\infty}}\leq M

for all

such that

\displaystyle R\geq C(a,M,{\bf v}_{0})\mathrm{e}^{C_{0}M^{4}T/a},

to the problem (

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w})(t)\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\leq C(a,M,\mathbf{v}_{0})\mathrm{e}^{C_{0}M^{4}t}R^{-a},

\displaystyle\|\nabla(p-\pi)\|_{{L}^{q}\left({0,t;{L}^{2}\left({B}_{R}\right)}\right)}\leq C(a,M,\mathbf{v}_{0}){t}^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathrm{e}^{C_{0}M^{4}t}R^{-a}

This result was proved in
[
17
]
by first truncating the NSE solution to
{B}_{R-1}

to recover the NSE in

[
17

, is to obtain spatial decay estimates for the strong solution

[
12

13

14

[
17

-norm of
{\bf v}
but ends up with the decay estimates for the

-norm of
{\bf v}
. Nevertheless, as strong solutions of NSE automatically lie in

Remark 0.3
.

The assumption

is only used towards the end of the proof (see §

Our Main Theorem
0.2
complements Theorem
0.1

•

Instead of imposing decay assumptions on the NSE solution

•

We obtain both spatial and temporal decay estimates of higher order for the difference between the NSE solutions on

and

•

We work with arbitrary dimensions

Remark 0.4
.

It should be emphasised that our Main Theorem
0.2
does
not
serve as an improvement of Theorem
0.1
, for the key point of the “transfer of regularity” results is to impose no additional decay or smallness assumptions on NSE/Stokes solutions or the initial data. Our purpose is to showcase that the theory of spacetime decay for NSE mild solutions may shed new lights on the “transfer of regularity” or other related phenomena in mathematical hydrodynamics.

In Theorem
0.1
, the spatial decay rate of the
L^{2}
-norm of the velocity gradient is proved by using Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg inequalities and Fourier analysis. Our proof of Theorem
0.2
adopts a different approach: we resort to the theory of spacetime decay for mild solutions of NSE, developed by Miyakawa
[
16
]
, Schonbek
[
21
]
and others.

Let us comment on the condition (
0.3
). Indeed, it is known that for any solenoidal, compactly supported vector field
{\bf v}_{0}\in C^{k_{0}}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one has the pointwise estimate (
cf
. Miyakawa
[
16
]
or Schonbek
[

\left|D^{k}\mathbf{v}_{\rm St}(x,t)\right|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-a}(1+t)^{-b/2}\qquad\text{ for all }a,b\geq 0\text{ with }a+b=n+1+k,

from which the
L^{q}
-estimate (
0.3
) follows directly. Moreover, by linearity of the Stokes system (
0.2
), if
{\bf v}_{\rm St}(x,t)
is a solution with the initial datum
{\bf v}_{0}
, then

is a solution with the initial datum

Remark 0.6
.

Analogous results to Theorem

1.
Derivation of the PDE

Recall the Navier–Stokes Equations (NSE) (
0.1
), in which we take
\nu=1
once and for all:

{\partial}_{t}\mathbf{v}-{\Delta\mathbf{v}}+({\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla})\mathbf{v}+\nabla p=0\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm div}\,{\bf v}=0\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T]

with the initial datum
\mathbf{v}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{v}_{0}
and dimension
n\geq 3
. In this note, we take a smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and assume that
\Omega\subset B_{R_{0}}
for some
R_{0}>0
. In what follows
R\geq R_{0}+1
.

Consider a spatial cutoff function:
\phi(x)=\tilde{\phi}(|x|)
for some
\tilde{\phi}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
\phi=1
within
{B}_{R-1}
and
\phi=0
outside
B_{R}
. We have the following PDE for the cutoff velocity
\phi\mathbf{v}
:

(1.1a)

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}(\phi\mathbf{v})-\Delta(\phi\mathbf{v})+\left((\phi\mathbf{v})\cdot\nabla\right)(\phi\mathbf{v})+\nabla(\phi p)

\displaystyle={F}_{1}+p\nabla\phi\;

\displaystyle\text{ in }B_{R}\times[0,T],

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(\phi\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\nabla\phi\cdot\mathbf{v}\;

\displaystyle\text{ in }B_{R}\times(0,\infty),

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\phi\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle=0\;

\displaystyle\text{ on }\partial B_{R}\times[0,T],

(1.1d)

\displaystyle(\phi\mathbf{v})|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{v}_{0}\;

\displaystyle\text{ at }B_{R}\times\{0\},

where

(1.2)

\displaystyle{F}_{1}:=-\phi(1-\phi)({\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla})\mathbf{v}+(\phi\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\phi)\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}\Delta\phi-2\nabla\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\phi.

The divergence-free property is not preserved under spatial cutoff. Thus, let us introduce a correction function
\mathbf{v}_{c}
such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}:=\phi\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}_{c}

is divergence free and has zero trace on
\partial B_{R}
.
Thus,
\mathbf{v}_{c}
satisfy the PDE:

(1.4a)

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}\mathbf{v}_{c}-\Delta\mathbf{v}_{c}+(\mathbf{v}_{c}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}_{c}+(\mathbf{v}_{c}\cdot\nabla)(\phi\mathbf{v})+(\phi\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}_{c}

\displaystyle:={F}_{2}\;

\displaystyle\text{ in }B_{R}\times[0,T],

(1.4b)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}_{c}

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{div}(\phi\mathbf{v})\;

\displaystyle\text{ in }B_{R}\times[0,T].

To construct
\mathbf{w}
and
\pi
that satisfy NSE (
0.1
) as stated in Theorem
0.2
, we introduce further corrector functions
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}
and
\bar{p}
so that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{r}+\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\pi=\phi p+\bar{p}.

The PDE system for
\left(\tilde{\mathbf{v}},\bar{p}\right)
is as follows:

(1.6a)

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}-\Delta\tilde{\mathbf{v}}+(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{\mathbf{v}}+\nabla\bar{p}

\displaystyle=-F-p\nabla\phi-(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{r}-(\mathbf{r}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\;

\displaystyle\text{ in }B_{R}\times[0,T],

(1.6b)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}

\displaystyle=0\;

\displaystyle\text{ in }B_{R}\times[0,T],

(1.6c)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{v}}

\displaystyle=0\;

\displaystyle\text{ on }\partial B_{R}\times[0,T],

(1.6d)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{v}}|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{v}_{0}\;

\displaystyle\text{ at }B_{R}\times\{0\},

where
F:={F}_{1}+{F}_{2}
. We emphasise that
F
is supported in the annulus
B_{R}\smallsetminus B_{R-1}
.

2.
Preliminaries

The analytical framework for this paper is based on the theory of
mild solutions
of NSE (
0.1
),
i.e.
, the representation of (in our case, strong) solutions of NSE via the Stokes semigroup. One first considers the linearised equation, namely that the Stokes system:

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}\mathbf{v}-{\Delta\mathbf{v}}+\nabla p=f\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega\times[0,T].

2.1.
Mild solution

Denote by
\mathbb{P}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
the Leray projection operator,
i.e.
, the
L^{2}
-orthonormal projection of vector fields on to the divergence-free part. The
Stokes operator
is

A:=-\mathbb{P}\Delta.

Then we recast original Stokes equations (
2.1
) into the ordinary differential equations:

\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{v}+A\mathbf{v}=\mathbb{P}f.

The solution to the problem (
2.1
) is given by the formula

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(t)=e^{-tA}\mathbf{v}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
e^{-tA}
is the semigroup operator generated by
A
,
a.k.a.
the Stokes semigroup.

As in Miyakawa
[
16
]
, by considering
\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v})
as the source term
f
, we obtain the integral form of solutions of NSE (
0.1
):

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(t)=e^{-tA}\mathbf{v}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\cdot e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v})(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

In this case,
\mathbf{v}
is said to be a mild solution of NSE (
0.1
).

2.2.
Decay rates of
\mathbf{v}

The decay properties for the solutions of NSE in the whole space have been extensively studied; see Brandolese
[
3
]
, Kukavica
[
12
]
, Schonbek
[
21
]
, Takahashi
[
24
]
and the many references cited therein. Our subsequent developments rely heavily on the spacetime decay estimates for the strong solutions of NSE together with their derivatives. To this end, we refer to the survey paper
[
22
]
by Schonbek, which provides a concise summary of results
à la
Miyakawa
[
16
]
and establishes decay estimates for higher-order derivatives.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
22
, Theorem 4.1]
) Let
\gamma=n+1+k
with
k\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
, and let
\mathbf{v}_{0}\in{C}^{k}_{0}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be a soleinoidal vector field such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left|{e}^{-tA}D^{k}\mathbf{v}_{0}(x)\right|\leq{C}_{0}\left(1+|x|\right)^{-\alpha}(1+t)^{-\beta/2}\qquad\text{for each }\alpha,\beta\geq 0\text{ with }\alpha+\beta=\gamma.

If
{C}_{0}
is small enough, then there exists a mild solution
\mathbf{v}
of (
0.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with initial datum
\mathbf{v}_{0}
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left|D^{k}\mathbf{v}(x,t)\right|\leq C\left(1+|x|\right)^{-\alpha}(1+t)^{-\beta/2}\qquad\text{for each }\alpha,\beta\geq 0\text{ with }\alpha+\beta=\gamma.

The solution
\mathbf{v}
satisfies the initial condition
\mathbf{v}\big|_{t=0}=\mathbf{v}_{0}
in the sense that

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{t\searrow 0}D^{I}\mathbf{v}(x,t)={D}^{I}\mathbf{v}_{0}(x)\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
I\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is an arbitrary multi-index with
|I|\leq k
.

As a consequence of pointwise decay estimates,

\displaystyle\left\|D^{k}\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{R-1}^{R}(1+r)^{-q(n+1+k)}r^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}r

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-(qn+q+qk-n+1)}.

This implies that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left\|D^{k}\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq CR^{-[n+1+k-(n-1)/q]}.

Similarly, we have that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left\|D^{k}\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-[q(n+1+k)-n]/2}\int_{B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1}}|x|^{-n}\,\mathrm{d}x,

and consequently

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left\|D^{k}\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq Ct^{-(n+1+k-\frac{n}{q})/2}\text{ for }1\leq q\leq\infty.

2.3.
Estimates for pressure
p

In this subsection, we derive estimates for the pressure
p
and its derivatives away from the origin, which is crucial to our arguments.

An application of the divergence operator to both sides of Equation (
0.1a
) yields

\displaystyle-\Delta p(x,t)=\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v})(x,t).

Hence,

(2.10)

\displaystyle D^{k}p(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}C(n)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}D^{k}\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v})(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y.

To investigate the pointwise estimate of
p
, we make use of the representation formula (
2.10
) and Proposition
2.1
. Indeed, it follows from the pointwise decay estimate (
2.5
) of
\mathbf{v}
that

\left|D^{k}(\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v})(y,t)\right|\leq\mathop{\sum}\limits_{j=0}^{k}\left|D^{j}\mathbf{v}\right|\left|D^{k-j}\mathbf{v}\right|\leq C(1+|y|)^{-(\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k-j})}(1+t)^{-(\beta_{j}/2+\beta_{k-j}/2)}

\text{with }\,\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k-j}+\beta_{j}+\beta_{k-j}=2(n+1)+k.

Thus, by integration by parts,

\displaystyle|p(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq|x|/2}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}\left|\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v})\right|(y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{|x-y|>|x|/2}\frac{\left|\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v}\right|(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq|x|/2}|x-y|^{2-n}(1+|y|)^{-2n-4}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{|x-y|>|x|/2}|x-y|^{-n}(1+|y|)^{-2n-2}\,\mathrm{d}y\right).

In the region
|x-y|\leq|x|/2
, the inequality
|x|\leq|x-y|+|y|\leq|x|/2+|y|
holds true, which implies
|x|\leq 2|y|
. On the other hand, in the region
|x-y|>|x|/2
, we have
|x-y|^{-n}\leq(|x|/2)^{-n}
, and
\int_{|x-y|>|x|/2}(1+|y|)^{-2n-2}\,\mathrm{d}y
is finite. As a consequence,

\displaystyle|p(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\left(|x|^{2}(1+|x|)^{-2n-4}+|x|^{-n}\right)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq C(n)(1+|x|)^{-n}.

Similar arguments (but taking into account here the temporal decay of
{\bf v}
) lead to

\displaystyle|p(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}(1+|y|)^{-(n+2)}(1+t)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq|x|/2}+\int_{|x-y|>|x|/2}\right)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}(1+|y|)^{-(n+2)}(1+t)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C(n)(1+t)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\left(|x|^{2}(1+|x|)^{-(n+2)}+|x|^{-(n-2)}\right)

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq C(n)(1+t)^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}.

Putting (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) together, we bound

(2.13)

\displaystyle|p(x,t)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-\alpha}(1+t)^{-\beta/2}

for all
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
with
\alpha+\beta=n
.

Arguments analogous to those leading to the estimate (
2.13
) allow us to achieve

(2.14)

\displaystyle\left|D^{k}p(x,t)\right|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-\alpha}(1+t)^{-\beta/2}

for all
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
with
\alpha+\beta=n+k
. Also, arguments parallel to those for (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) give us

(2.15)

\begin{cases}\|p(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq CR^{-[n-(n-1)/q]},\\
\left\|D^{k}p(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq CR^{-[n+k-(n-1)/q]},\end{cases}

as well as

(2.16)

\left\|D^{k}p(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq Ct^{-(n+k-\frac{n}{q})/2}\qquad\text{for any $1\leq q\leq\infty$.}

3.
Truncated velocity estimates and the Bogovskiĭ-type correction

We now establish the decay of the mild solution within the ball and resolve the non-divergence-free issue of the truncated velocity
\phi\mathbf{v}
.

3.1.
Decay estimates of mild solution in a ball

If the initial value
\mathbf{v}_{0}
satisfies the condition (
2.4
), then by Proposition
2.1
there exists a solution
\mathbf{v}
exhibiting the pointwise decay property as in (
2.5
). As
\phi
is compactly supported, we easily deduce that

(3.1)

\left\|D^{k}(\phi\mathbf{v})(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq\begin{cases}CR^{-[n+1+k-(n-1)/q]},\\
Ct^{-(n+1+k-\frac{n}{q})/2},\end{cases}

for any
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

Also, recall from the definition of
F_{1}
in (
1.2
) that
F_{1}\equiv 0
on
B_{R-1}
. Thus it is supported on the annulus
{B}_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1}
. The pointwise decay estimate (
2.5
) for
\mathbf{v}
yields that

(3.2)

\displaystyle|{F}_{1}(x,t)|\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\left|({\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla})\mathbf{v}\right|+|\mathbf{v}|^{2}+|\mathbf{v}|+|\nabla\mathbf{v}|\Big)\leq C\left(1+|x|\right)^{-\alpha}(1+t)^{-\beta/2}

for all
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
with
\alpha+\beta=n+1
. Consequently, arguments similar to those in (
2.6
) lead to

\displaystyle\|{F}_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}^{q}=\|{F}_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}^{q}\leq C\left\|(1+|x|)^{-(n+1)}\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}^{q},

which shows that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|{F}_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq CR^{-[n+1-(n-1)/q]}.

Similarly, one deduces that

(3.4)

\left|D^{k}{F}_{1}(x,t)\right|\leq C\left(1+|x|\right)^{-\alpha}(1+t)^{-\beta/2}\qquad\text{ with }\alpha+\beta=n+1+k

and that

(3.5)

\left\|D^{k}{F}_{1}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq\begin{cases}CR^{-[n+1+k-(n-1)/q]},\\
Ct^{-(n+1+k-\frac{n}{q})/2}.\end{cases}

Here
q\in[1,\infty]
is arbitrary.

3.2.
Bogovskiĭ-type correction

Recall the definition of the corrector function
\mathbf{v}_{c}
— see Equation (
1.4
), reproduced here:

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}\mathbf{v}_{c}-\Delta\mathbf{v}_{c}+(\mathbf{v}_{c}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}_{c}+(\mathbf{v}_{c}\cdot\nabla)(\phi\mathbf{v})+(\phi\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}_{c}

\displaystyle:={F}_{2}\;

\displaystyle\text{ in }B_{R}\times[0,T],

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}_{c}

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{div}(\phi\mathbf{v})\;

\displaystyle\text{ in }B_{R}\times[0,T].

Recall also that the vector field

\mathbf{r}:=\phi\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}_{c}

is divergence-free. Since
\operatorname{div}(\phi\mathbf{v})=0
on
{B}_{R-1}
, it follows that
\mathbf{v}_{c}
vanishes on
{B}_{R-1}
. Moreover,
F_{2}
also vanishes within
B_{R-1}
. Thus we shall only need to consider the PDE for
{\bf v}_{c}
on
\Omega:=B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1}
. The well-known Bogovskiĭ Lemma
A.1
applied to
f=-\operatorname{div}(\phi\mathbf{v})
yields that

(3.6a)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}_{c}(\cdot,t)\|_{{W}^{k,q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq C\|\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)\|_{{W}^{k,q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}

\displaystyle\text{ for }1<q<\infty,

(3.6b)

\displaystyle\|\Delta\mathbf{v}_{c}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq C\|\Delta\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)\|_{{L}^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}

\displaystyle\text{ for }1<q<\infty,

(3.6c)

and

\displaystyle\|{\partial}_{t}\mathbf{v}_{c}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq C{\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)\|}_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}

\displaystyle\text{ for }1<q<\infty.

This together with the definition (
1.3
) of
\mathbf{r}
and estimates (
2.7
), (
2.9
) for
{\bf v}
implies that

(3.7)

\left\|D^{k}\mathbf{r}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq\begin{cases}CR^{-(n+1+k-n/q)},\\
Ct^{-(n+1+k-\frac{n}{q})/2}\end{cases}

for
1<q<\infty
.

Moreover, using the estimate (
2.7
) of
D^{k}\mathbf{v}
in the annular region, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|{F}_{2}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}=\|{F}_{2}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}

\displaystyle=\|{\partial}_{t}\mathbf{v}_{c}-\Delta\mathbf{v}_{c}+(\mathbf{v}_{c}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}_{c}+(\mathbf{v}_{c}\cdot\nabla)(\phi\mathbf{v})+(\phi\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}_{c}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}_{c}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}+R^{-[n+3-(n-1)/q]}\right\}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}+\|(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}+\|\nabla p\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-[n+1-(n-1)/q]},

where Equations (
2.15
) and (
2.7
) are used to control the terms
\nabla p
and
\partial_{t}{\bf v}
, respectively. Thus,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|{F}_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq CR^{-[n+1-(n-1)/q]},

which together with the estimate (
3.3
) for
F_{1}
gives us

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|({F}_{1}+{F}_{2})(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}=\|({F}_{1}+{F}_{2})(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq CR^{-[n+1-(n-1)/q]}.

Similar arguments lead to the spacetime decay of higher-order derivatives of
F_{2}
and
F_{1}+F_{2}
:

(3.11)

\left\|D^{k}{F}_{2}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+\left\|D^{k}({F}_{1}+{F}_{2})(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq CR^{-[n+1+k-(n-1)/q]}

and

(3.12)

\left\|D^{k}({F}_{1}+{F}_{2})(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq Ct^{-(n+1+k-\frac{n}{q})/2}.

In all the inequalities in this subsection,
q\in(1,\infty)
is arbitrary.

4.
Iterative estimates for NSE on
B_{R}
with small forcing

This section is devoted to the construction of the solutions of NSE on
B_{R}
with a small forcing term. Recall from (
1.5
) that
\mathbf{w}:=\mathbf{r}+\tilde{\mathbf{v}}
and
\pi:=\phi p+\bar{p}
. Our goal here is to find
\left(\tilde{\mathbf{v}},\bar{p}\right)
via Eq. (
1.6

(\mathbf{w},\pi)
that satisfies the estimates in Theorem

To begin with, as
\phi\mathbf{v}\equiv\mathbf{v}
on
{B}_{R-1}

\mathbf{v}_{c}\equiv 0
on
B_{R-1}

\mathbf{r}=\phi\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}_{c}=\mathbf{v}
on

\tilde{\mathbf{v}}
is supported on
B_{R}\setminus{B}_{R-1}

We shall establish the existence of solution for Eq. (

(4.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{v}}(t)=e^{-tA}\mathbf{v}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}\bigg\{(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{\mathbf{v}}+F+p\nabla\phi+(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{r}+(\mathbf{r}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\bigg\}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

{\bf u}_{i}
given for

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{i+1}(t)

Lemma 4.3

Let

\left\|D^{k}\mathbf{u}_{i}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq\begin{cases}CR^{-\left(n-1+k-\frac{n}{q}\right)},\\

(4.9)

\left\|D^{k}\mathbf{u}_{i+1}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq\begin{cases}CR^{-\left(n-1+k-\frac{n}{q}\right)},\\

\left|e^{-tA}D^{k}\mathbf{v}_{0}(x)\right|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-\alpha}(1+t)^{-\beta/2}\qquad\text{ for any }\alpha,\beta\geq 0\text{ with }\alpha+\beta=n+1+k.

Thus, by adapting arguments used for deriving Eqs. (

Proof of Lemma
4.3

Consider as in

the following:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{i+1}(x,t)

\displaystyle=e^{-tA}\mathbf{v}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{1}(x-y,t-s)(\phi\nabla p)(y,s)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{1}(x-y,t-s)F(y,s)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt-\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{2}(x-y,t-s)\Big(p\phi\,{\rm Id}+\mathbf{u}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{u}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{r}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{i}\Big)(y,s)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s

With the above preparations at hand, we are ready to conclude our main result. Throughout the proof below, we write

Proof of Theorem
0.2
.

Recall that the desired solution
(\mathbf{w},\pi)
of Eq. (
1.6
) on
B_{R}
is given by

\mathbf{w}:=\phi\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}_{c}+\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\pi:=\phi p+\bar{p},

where
(\mathbf{v},p)
is the NSE solution on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\phi
is a given cutoff function, and
{\bf v}_{c}
is the correction term accounting for the non-divergence-free property of
\phi{\bf v}
. It remains to prove the existence of
(\mathbf{w},\pi)
solving for Eq. (
1.6
), together with desired decay estimates for
({\bf v}-{\bf w},p-\pi)
.

Throughout this proof, fix an arbitrary
q\in(1,\infty)
once and for all. The uniqueness of strong solution is classical in the literature, so we focus only on the existence issue.

We apply a standard fixed point argument. The function space in consideration is the following spacetime-weighted Sobolev space
\mathcal{X}
motivated by Schonbek
[
22
]
: define

(5.1)

\mathcal{X}\equiv\mathcal{X}(k,q,R,n):=\left\{{\bf u}\in W^{k,q}_{0}(B_{R};\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,{\rm div}({\bf u})=0\text{ and }\|{\bf u}\|_{\mathcal{X}}<\infty\right\},

where

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathcal{X}}=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\alpha,\beta\geq 0,\;\alpha+\beta=1;}\\
{m=0,1,2,3,\ldots k;\;t\in[0,T]}\end{subarray}}R^{\alpha\left(n-1+m-\frac{n}{q}\right)}\,t^{\beta\left(n-1-\varepsilon+m-\frac{n}{q}\right)/2}\left\|D^{m}\mathbf{u}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}.

Consider the mapping
{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}\underline{\mathbf{u}}\equiv\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle:=e^{-tA}\mathbf{v}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}(F-\phi\nabla p)(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

(5.2)

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}\,\Big\{p\phi\,{\rm Id}+\underline{\mathbf{u}}\otimes\underline{\mathbf{u}}+\underline{\mathbf{u}}\otimes\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{r}\otimes\underline{\mathbf{u}}\Big\}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

We
claim
the following bounds:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}\,(\mathbf{r}\otimes\underline{\mathbf{u}})(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq C_{1}R^{-1}\|\mathbf{r}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}\,(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\otimes\mathbf{r})(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq C_{1}R^{-1}\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\|\mathbf{r}\|_{\mathcal{X}},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}\,(\underline{\mathbf{u}}\otimes\underline{\mathbf{u}})(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq C_{1}R^{-1}\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2},

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{T}}\underline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-tA}\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}(F-\phi\nabla p)(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}\,(p\phi{\rm Id})(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle+C_{1}R^{-1}\|\mathbf{r}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}+C_{1}R^{-1}\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}.

The constant

and

To see the
claim
, let us only prove the first inequality, as the remaining two are analogous.
Fix

) for the space
\mathcal{X}\equiv\mathcal{X}(k,q,R,n)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|K_{2}(x-\cdot,t-s)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(B_{R})}\|D^{m}(\mathbf{r}\otimes\underline{\mathbf{u}})(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{T}}\underline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\left\|e^{-tA}\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}+C_{3}+C_{2}R^{-3}\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}+C_{1}R^{-1}\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{T}}\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{1}-{\mathcal{T}}\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla e^{-(t-s)A}\mathbb{P}\,\Big((\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{1}+\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{2})\otimes(\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{1}-\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{2})+(\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{1}-\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{2})\otimes\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{r}\otimes(\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{1}-\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{2})\Big)(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{1}R^{-1}(\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{1}\|_{\mathcal{X}}+\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}+\|\mathbf{r}\|_{\mathcal{X}})\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{1}-\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(2C_{1}R^{-1}L+C_{2}R^{-3}\right)\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{1}-\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\qquad<\|\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{1}-\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}},

where the last line follows from (
5.4
). Therefore, applying the Banach fixed point theorem, we deduce the existence of a solution
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}
to Eq. (
1.6
) with

(5.5)

\left\|D^{k}\tilde{\mathbf{v}}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq\begin{cases}CR^{-\left(n-1+k-\frac{n}{q}\right)},\\
Ct^{-\left(n-1-\varepsilon+k-\frac{n}{q}\right)/2}\qquad\text{for any $1<q<\infty$.}\end{cases}

For
\bar{p}
, we employ the same approach as in §
2.3
: taking divergence to both sides of Eq. (
1.6a
) and exploiting the fundamental solution for Laplacian, one obtains that

\displaystyle\bar{p}(x,t)=C(n)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}\left[\operatorname{div}(F-\phi\nabla p)+\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}(p\phi{\rm Id}+\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{v}}+\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{r}\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{v}})\right](y,t)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Employing the calculations for
I_{3}
outlined in Lemma
4.3
, and combining it with the estimates for
p
in (
2.15
)-(
2.16
),
\mathbf{r}
in (
3.7
),
F
in (
3.11
)-(
3.12
), and
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}
in (
5.5
), we then conclude that

\|\bar{p}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}=\|\bar{p}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}\smallsetminus{B}_{R-1})}\leq\begin{cases}CR^{-\left(n-\frac{n-1}{q}\right)},\\
Ct^{-\left(n-\frac{n}{q}\right)/2}.\end{cases}

Similarly, for any
1<q<\infty
,

(5.6)

\left\|D^{k}\bar{p}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq\begin{cases}CR^{-\left(n+k-\frac{n-1}{q}\right)}\\
Ct^{-\left(n+k-\frac{n}{q}\right)/2}.\end{cases}

It is clear by construction that
(\mathbf{v},p)
and
(\mathbf{w},\pi)
coincide within
B_{R-1}
.
Applying the triangle inequality along with the estimates for
\phi\mathbf{v}
in (
3.1
),
\mathbf{v}_{c}
in (

),
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}
in (
5.5

in (

\left\|D^{k}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w})(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq\begin{cases}CR^{-\left(n-1+k-\frac{n}{q}\right)},\\
Ct^{-\left(n-1-\varepsilon+k-\frac{n}{q}\right)/2},\end{cases}

and

\left\|D^{k}(p-\pi)(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\leq\begin{cases}CR^{-\left(n+k-\frac{n-1}{q}\right)},\\
Ct^{-\left(n+k-\frac{n}{q}\right)/2}.\end{cases}

From here, Eq. (

Appendix A
Technical Lemmas

In the Appendix, we present the Bogovskiĭ Lemma used for the correction term
\mathbf{v}_{c}
and provide a detailed proof for the estimates of kernel functions.

The following result, known as the Bogovskiĭ Lemma, is a classical result in the literature (Bogovskiĭ
[
1
,
2
]
; see also Galdi
[
8
, Lemma III.3.1]
).

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}(n\geq 2)
be a star-shaped domain with respect to
{B}_{r}
— that is, for all
x\in\Omega
and
y\in{B}_{r}
, the line segment from
x
to
y
lies in
\Omega
. Then for any
q\in(1,\infty)
and
f\in{W}^{k-1,q}(\Omega)
with
\int_{\Omega}f=0
, there exists
\mathbf{v}\in{W}_{0}^{k,q}({\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
such that
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}=f
. Moreover,

(A.1)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}\|_{{W}^{k,q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{{W}^{k-1,q}(\Omega)},

where the constant
C
depending on
n,k,q
and the domain
\Omega
.

In fact, letting
g\in{C}_{0}^{\infty}({B}_{1})
be a function such that
\int g=1
, the vector field

(A.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(x):=\int_{\Omega}f(y)\left({\frac{x-y}{{|x-y|}^{n}}\int_{|x-y|}^{\infty}g\left(y+z\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\right){z}^{n-1}\mathrm{\;d}z}\right)\mathrm{d}y

satisfies the assertion of the lemma. The result remains valid even if the domain
\Omega
is not star-shaped, thanks to a partition of unity argument as in
[
17
]
.

We now turn to the potential estimates for integral kernels. Throughout the proof below, the constant
C
depends on the dimension
n
and the domain, while
c
denotes a universal constant. We shall assume Lemma
4.2
(see
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
and
[
22
, Corollary 4.1]
for a proof) and deduce Lemma
4.1
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

The case
k=0
in (
4.3
) follows from an adaptation of
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
. For the sake of completeness and clarity, we present the details here. Also, we shall prove for the case
k\geq 1
by way of using the inequality
\left|D^{k}K_{1}(x,t)\right|\leq C\left|D^{k-1}K_{2}(x,t)\right|
, where
K_{1}(x,t)
and
K_{2}(x,t)
are the kernel functions of the linear operators
e^{-tA}\mathbb{P}=(e^{-tA}\mathbb{P}_{jk})
and
\nabla e^{-tA}\mathbb{P}=(\partial_{\ell}e^{-tA}\mathbb{P}_{jk})
, respectively.

Recall the Fourier transform:

\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\xi)=\int e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\mathbf{v}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\quad(i=\sqrt{-1}).

The Leray projection
\mathbb{P}
is expressed componentwise as

(\widehat{\mathbb{P}\mathbf{v}})_{j}(\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{jk}(\xi)\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{k}(\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\delta_{jk}+\frac{i\xi_{j}i\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{k}(\xi).

The Fourier transform of the kernel function
K(x,t)=\left(K_{jk}(x,t)\right)
of the operator
e^{-tA}\mathbb{P}=(e^{-tA}\mathbb{P}_{jk})
can thus be expressed as

(A.3)

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{jk}(\xi,t)=e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\left(\delta_{jk}+\frac{i\xi_{j}i\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\equiv\hat{K}_{jk}^{1}+\hat{K}_{jk}^{2}.

Applying the inverse Fourier transform, we obtain

\displaystyle K_{jk}^{1}(x,t)=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-n}\int e^{i\xi\cdot x}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\xi}=\sqrt{t}\xi}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-n}\int e^{i\tilde{\xi}\cdot\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}}e^{-|\tilde{\xi}|^{2}}t^{-n/2}\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\xi}

(A.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ct^{-n/2}e^{-\frac{c|x|^{2}}{t}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left|K_{jk}^{1}(x,t)\right|\leq Ct^{-n/2}e^{-c|x|^{2}/t}\leq Ct^{-n/2}\text{ and }\left|K_{jk}^{1}(x,t)\right|\leq C|x|^{-n}.

An interpolation argument yields, for all non-negative
\alpha
and
\beta
with
\alpha+\beta=n
, that

(A.5)

\displaystyle\left|K_{jk}^{1}(x,t)\right|\leq C|x|^{-\alpha}t^{-\beta/2}.

To estimate
K_{jk}^{2}(x,t)
, from the identity
|\xi|^{-2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s|\xi|^{2}}\,{\rm d}s
we deduce that

\hat{K}_{jk}^{2}(\xi,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}i\xi_{j}i\xi_{k}e^{-(s+t)|\xi|^{2}}\,{\rm d}s=\int_{t}^{\infty}i\xi_{j}i\xi_{k}e^{-s|\xi|^{2}}\,{\rm d}s.

Taking the inverse Fourier transform, we obtain

K_{jk}^{2}(x,t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{x_{k}}E_{s}(x)\,{\rm d}s,

where
E_{s}(x)=(4\pi s)^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/4s}
. Since

\left|\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{x_{k}}E_{s}(x)\right|\leq Cs^{-(n+3)/2}e^{-c|x|^{2}/s},

we infer that

\displaystyle\left|K_{jk}^{2}(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-(n+3)/2}e^{-c|x|^{2}/s}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-(n+3)/2}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=Ct^{-(n+1)/2}.

In addition, a change of the variable
\tau:=|x|^{2}/s
shows that

\displaystyle\left|K_{jk}^{2}(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{-(n+3)/2}e^{-c|x|^{2}/s}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=C|x|^{-(n+1)}\int_{0}^{|x|^{2}/t}\tau^{(n-1)/2}e^{-c\tau}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C|x|^{-(n+1)}.

As a consequence,

(A.6)

\displaystyle\left|K_{jk}^{2}(x,t)\right|\leq C|x|^{-\alpha}t^{-\beta/2}\qquad\text{ for all }\alpha,\beta\geq 0\text{ with }\alpha+\beta=n+1.

Eq. (
4.3
) now follows from Eqs. (
A.5
) and (
A.6
).

For the proof of the
L^{q}
-estimates (
4.4
), thanks to
K_{jk}^{1}(x,t)=Ct^{-n/2}e^{-\frac{c|x|^{2}}{t}}
, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|K_{jk}^{1}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}}^{q}

\displaystyle=Ct^{-qn/2}\int_{|x|\geq 1}e^{-\frac{cq|x|^{2}}{t}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-qn/2}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{cqr^{2}}{t}}r^{n-1}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\zeta=r\sqrt{c/t}}}{{\leq}}Ct^{-qn/2}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-q\zeta^{2}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{t}{c}}\right)^{n-1}\zeta^{n-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{t}{c}}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-(qn-n)/2}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-q\zeta^{2}}\zeta^{n-1}\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-(qn-n)/2}.

On the other hand,
\left|K_{jk}^{2}(x,t)\right|\leq C|x|^{-\alpha}t^{-\beta/2}
in (
A.6
) implies

\displaystyle\left\|K_{jk}^{2}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{q}}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-(qn-n)/2}\int_{|x|\geq 1}|x|^{-(n+q)}\mathrm{d}x\leq Ct^{-(qn-n)/2}.

These finish the proof.
∎

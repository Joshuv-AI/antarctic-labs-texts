Abstract
In recent years more and more involved block structures appeared in the literature in the context of numerical approximations of complex infinite dimensional operators modeling real-world applications. In various settings, thanks the theory of generalized locally Toeplitz matrix-sequences, the asymptotic distributional analysis is well understood, but a general theory is missing when general block structures are involved. The central part of the current work deals with such a delicate generalization when blocks are of (block) unilevel Toeplitz type, starting from a problem of recovery with missing data. Visualizations, numerical tests, and few open problems are presented and critically discussed.



Block structures: spectral and singular value distributions

keywords

Block structures, matrix-sequence, distribution of eigenvalue and singular values in the Weyl sense, block Toeplitz matrix, generating function.

In this work we focus our attention on the spectral distribution and on the singular value distribution in the Weyl sense in the block unilevel setting, with rectangular blocks of different dimensions. The presence of zeros in the distribution function and the order of the zeros give information on the conditioning and on the size and nature of the subspaces where the ill-conditioning arise (see e.g.
[

and references therein). In turn the latter information is relevant for designing preconditioners in Krylov methods or projectors in multigrid methods with the goal of optimal solvers, having convergence rate independent of the matrix order of the coefficient matrices. Hence such results are also of practical and computational interest.

First, we give theoretical tools for studying the distributions - in the singular and eigenvalue sense - of general matrix-sequences. Subsequently, the new tools are employed in the context of block matrix-sequences, with the matrix-sequences of the blocks having themselves a block structure under various conditions, but all concerning block unilevel Toeplitz structures.

Definition 2.1
.

[

Let

be a measurable function defined on a measurable set

\ell\geq 1

0<m_{\ell}(\Omega)<\infty

Let

be the set of continuous functions with compact support over

be a sequence of matrices with singular values
\sigma_{j}(A_{{n}})

•

The matrix-sequence

is distributed as
{f}
in the sense of the singular values, and we write

\displaystyle\{A_{{n}}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\sigma}{f},

if the following limit relation holds for all
F\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+})

(2)

\displaystyle\lim_{{n}\to\infty}\frac{1}{{{n}}}\sum_{j=1}^{{{n}}}F(\sigma_{j}(A_{n}))=\frac{1}{m_{\ell}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}F\left(\sigma_{i}\left({f}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)\right)\right)}{r}\,\!\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{\theta}}.

The function
{f}
is called the singular value symbol which describes the singular value distribution of the matrix-sequence
\{A_{{n}}\}_{{n}}
.

•

In the case where

is square of size

is distributed as
{f}
in the sense of the eigenvalues, and we write

\displaystyle\{A_{{n}}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\lambda}{f},

if the following limit relation holds for all

\displaystyle\lim_{{n}\to\infty}\frac{1}{{{n}}}\sum_{j=1}^{{{n}}}F(\lambda_{j}(A_{n}))=\frac{1}{m_{\ell}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s}F\left(\lambda_{i}\left({f}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)\right)\right)}{s}\,\!\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{\theta}}.

The function

is called the eigenvalue symbol and it describes the eigenvalue distribution of the matrix-sequence

Theorem 2.3
.

[

[Theorem 4.3]

Let

\{X_{{n}}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\lambda}{f}\ \ \ \ {\rm iff}\ \ \ \ \{Y_{{n^{\prime}}}\}_{{n^{\prime}}}\sim_{\lambda}{f}.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\{X_{{n}}\}_{{n}}
be a given (rectangular) matrix-sequence with
X_{n}
of order

,
n^{(1)}\geq n
. Let

.

The singular values squared of

correspond to the eigenvalues of

\hat{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\hat{\ell}}
with
{\hat{\ell}}\geq 1
,
0<m_{\hat{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega})<\infty
,
r^{\prime}=\min\{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}\}
and
for all
F\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+})

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{\ell}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}F\left(\sigma_{i}\left({G}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)\right)\right)}{r}\,\!\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\frac{1}{m_{\hat{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega})}\int_{\hat{\Omega}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r^{\prime}}F\left(\sigma_{i}\left({\hat{G}}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)\right)\right)}{r^{\prime}}\,\!\mathrm{d}{\boldsymbol{\theta}}.

We choose now
{\hat{G}}=\phi_{G}
as the unique nondecreasing rearrangement of
G
defined on
[0,1]
. Hence
\{X_{n}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\sigma}G
iff
\{X_{n}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\sigma}\phi_{G}
and
\{Y_{n}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\sigma}G
iff
\{Y_{n}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\sigma}\phi_{G}
from which
\{X_{n}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\sigma}G
iff
\{Y_{n}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\sigma}G
is equivalent to write
\{X_{n}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\sigma}\phi_{G}
iff
\{Y_{n}\}_{{n}}\sim_{\sigma}\phi_{G}
.

Remark 3.18
.

Assume that a class of matrix-sequences
\{\{A_{n}(l)\}_{n}:l\}
is given with
A_{n}(l)
as in (
5
) with
l\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and with
f_{i,j}
,
i,j=1,\dots,\nu
, fixed functions independent of

as in Definition
3.1
.
Assume that 1. and 2. are satisfied with the coefficients

rational numbers converging to

,

,

. It is clear that the related distribution functions

cannot tend to any

, simply because according to Theorem

the size of the symbol

However, according to Theorem
3.15
and Remark

, we have

, with

being the nondecreasing rearrangement of

defined on
[0,1]
.
Now it makes sense to ask if

converges to some
F
as

tends to infinity. A positive answer and the use of the notion of approximating class of sequences with the related topology (see
[
26
]
and references therein) would represent a possibility of extending Theorem

are not rational numbers. A preliminary study with related numerical experiments is presented in
[

]
for the case of

and

.
Very recently, in
[

]
the notion of approximating class of sequences has been revisited and generalized, including the case where the matrix sizes of the approximants and of the original matrix-sequence are not the same. Such idea relies again on the extradimensional techniques discussed in
[

]
and it has been used in
[

]
for obtaining the distributional results when the

are not necessarily rational for

and

.

5
Conclusions

We have provided a general framework for studying the asymptotic Weyl distributions in the singular value and eigenvalue sense of matrix-sequences in block form. We have dealt in detail the case where the matrix-sequences associated to the blocks are of block unilevel Toeplitz type. Visualizations and numerical tests have been presented and critically discussed.

There are many directions for expanding the theoretical framework, including investigating the conjecture in Remark
3.18
and examining also the case where the number of row blocks and column blocks are different, so adding a new level in the rectangular structure of
A_{n}
.

However, two main steps remain to be performed.

•

The primary open challenge lies in generalizing the results to the multilevel setting, enabling the treatment of approximations for multidimensional differential or fractional differential problems with variable coefficients.
Such a generalization can follow various possible directions and has to include the case where the blocks are of block multilevel Toeplitz type or derived from block multilevel GLT matrix-sequences. This will involve notational and technical challenges that need to be handled with care and precision.

•

The distributional information can be used to design fast preconditioned Krylov methods for solving the related large linear systems.

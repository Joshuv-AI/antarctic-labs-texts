Proliferation dynamics of enterocytes: Implications for intestinal barrier function, performance, health, and welfare in broiler chickens
Authors: Md Emran Hossain, Nusrat Binte Amin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntestinal health is a central determinant of growth, performance, health and welfare in modern broiler chickens. Enterocytes, the primary functional cells of the intestinal epithelium, play a pivotal role in nutrient digestion, absorption, barrier integrity and host defense. Continuous proliferation, differentiation, migration and exfoliation of enterocytes along the crypt-villus axis maintain epithelial homeostasis and intestinal efficiency. Disruption of these tightly regulated processes can compromise barrier function, reduce nutrient utilization, impair growth performance and increase susceptibility to enteric disorders. This review critically examines the current understanding of enterocyte proliferation dynamics in broilers and their implications for intestinal barrier function, efficiency, performance, health and welfare. Key cellular and molecular mechanisms regulating enterocyte proliferation are discussed, including major signaling pathways, nutrient sensing systems and stem cell interactions. Nutritional regulation, microbiota-derived modulation and early-life programming of the intestinal epithelium are highlighted as major factors influencing proliferation. The relationships between enterocyte turnover, barrier integrity, digestive capacity and feed efficiency are explored with relevance to production systems. Furthermore, enterocyte proliferation responses to physiological stress, immune challenges and enteric diseases are evaluated, emphasizing their role in recovery and resilience. Methodological approaches, including histomorphometry, immunohistochemical markers and molecular techniques, are discussed with consideration of their limitations and standardization challenges. Critical knowledge gaps are identified, and future research directions are proposed to support precision nutrition and management strategies aimed at optimizing intestinal barrier function, growth performance, metabolic efficiency, health and welfare of the broiler chickens.Keywords: Broiler, Enterocyte proliferation, Epithelial turnover, Gut barrier function, Villus morphology

Introduction

Intestinal health is a critical determinant of growth performance, nutrient utilization and overall welfare in broiler chickens (Obianwuna, 2024). The small intestine serves as the primary site for digestion and absorption and its structural and functional integrity directly influences feed efficiency and growth kinetics (Dong et al., 2025). Enterocytes, the predominant epithelial cells lining the intestinal mucosa, are central to maintaining these functions (Anjos et al., 2025). Their continuous proliferation, differentiation and migration along the crypt-villus axis ensure proper epithelial renewal, barrier integrity and effective nutrient absorption (Pangga, 2025). Disruption of enterocyte proliferation can compromise intestinal barrier function, reduce nutrient assimilation and increase susceptibility to enteric infections, ultimately limiting productivity in commercial broiler systems (Sugiharto et al., 2026).

Despite the recognized importance of intestinal health, the cellular and molecular dynamics of enterocyte proliferation in broilers remain incompletely understood. While numerous studies have examined the effects of nutrition, feed additives and microbiota on gut morphology and performance, few have integrated these findings to comprehensively address enterocyte turnover, its regulation and functional implication (van Hees et al., 2025). Furthermore, emerging challenges in modern broiler production including high growth rates, dietary modifications, pathogen pressures and stress conditions underscore the need to better understand how enterocyte proliferation influences both performance and bird welfare (Sugiharto et al., 2026).

This review aims to synthesize current knowledge on enterocyte proliferation dynamics in broiler chickens and to highlight their implications for barrier function, intestinal efficiency, growth performance, health and welfare. By integrating cellular, molecular, nutritional and microbiota-related perspectives, this work provides a foundation for future research and practical strategies to optimize gut function. Addressing this knowledge gap is essential to support precision nutrition, enhance resilience to stress and disease and improve sustainable production outcomes in modern broiler systems.

Structural biology

The small intestine of broiler chickens comprises the duodenum, jejunum and ileum, each performing distinct roles in digestion and nutrient absorption (Alshamy et al., 2018). The mucosal surface is lined by a single layer of epithelial cells, predominantly enterocytes, with interspersed goblet and enteroendocrine cells (Bonis et al., 2021). Enterocytes possess apical microvilli forming the brush border, which greatly enhances absorptive surface area. Tight junctions maintain barrier integrity, while the lamina propria supports the epithelium with blood and lymphatic vessels, immune cells and extracellular matrix components, facilitating nutrient transport and immune surveillance (Heyman et al., 2025).

Enterocytes originate from stem and progenitor cells within the crypts, the proliferative zones of the intestine (Uni et al., 1998). Newly formed cells migrate along the crypt-villus axis while differentiating into mature absorptive cells (Sugiharto et al., 2026). This coordinated process balances cell proliferation with shedding at the villus tips, maintaining epithelial renewal. Broiler enterocytes have a relatively short lifespan of 3-5 days, reflecting high metabolic demand (Anjos et al., 2025). Turnover rates vary with age, diet, microbial load and physiological stress, enabling rapid adaptation to environmental and nutritional challenges (Pangga, 2025).

As enterocytes migrate from crypts to villus tips, they acquire brush border enzymes, nutrient transporters and tight junction proteins, ensuring efficient absorption and barrier function (Wu et al., 2025a). Terminal differentiation prepares cells for apoptosis and shedding into the intestinal lumen (Berková et al., 2023). This continuous renewal allows the epithelium to maintain homeostasis, rapidly repair damage and respond to dietary, microbial, or stress-related challenges, supporting optimal nutrient assimilation and protection against pathogen invasion (Madej et al., 2025).

Enterocyte morphology and function differ along intestinal regions. Duodenal cells specialize in enzyme secretion and nutrient sensing, jejunal cells maximize carbohydrate, protein and lipid absorption and ileal cells primarily absorb bile salts and water (Akram et al., 2025). Villus height, crypt depth, proliferation rates and enzymatic activity vary regionally, reflecting adaptation to local digestive and absorptive demands (Wu et al., 2025a). These differences are crucial for understanding targeted nutritional interventions and evaluating intestinal efficiency in broiler production (Sadr et al., 2025)

Although the crypt-villus architecture is conserved across species, broiler enterocytes exhibit higher proliferation rates and shorter turnover times than mammals, meeting rapid growth and high metabolic demand (Marcato et al., 2024). Chickens lack well-developed Paneth cells, relying on alternative mechanisms for antimicrobial defense and epithelial maintenance (Madej et al., 2025). Comparative studies indicate that extrapolating intestinal physiology or dietary strategies from mammals to poultry requires caution, emphasizing the need for poultry-specific research to optimize enterocyte proliferation, gut health and performance outcomes (Pangga, 2025).

Proliferation dynamics

Stem and progenitor cells

Stem and progenitor cells within crypts serve as the primary source of new enterocytes. Lgr5+ stem cells self-renew and generate transit-amplifying progenitors that migrate along the crypt-villus axis while differentiating (Marcato et al., 2024). This hierarchical organization ensures a continuous supply of functional epithelial cells. In broilers, stem cell activity is highly responsive to diet, microbial metabolites and stress, highlighting their critical role in maintaining epithelial integrity, nutrient absorption and gut resilience under intensive production conditions (Pangga, 2025; Madej et al., 2025). Enterocyte proliferation is tightly controlled by the cell cycle within crypt stem and progenitor cells. Progression through G1, S, G2 and M phases ensures orderly DNA replication, division and differentiation (Dong et al., 2025). Cyclins, cyclin-dependent kinases and checkpoint proteins regulate these transitions, maintaining epithelial homeostasis. Dysregulation can lead to impaired renewal, reduced absorptive capacity and compromised barrier function (Dong et al., 2025). In broilers, crypt cell cycle activity is influenced by nutrition, microbial interactions and environmental stress, making it a central determinant of gut efficiency and performance (Anjos et al., 2025).

Signaling pathways

Several signaling pathways coordinate enterocyte proliferation, differentiation and survival. The Wnt/β-catenin pathway drives crypt stem cell proliferation and villus renewal (Wu et al., 2025a). Notch signaling regulates lineage specification, balancing absorptive enterocytes with secretory cells (Madej et al., 2025). mTOR signaling integrates nutrient availability and energy status to modulate growth (Sadr et al., 2025). The MAPK pathway transduces extracellular stimuli, including growth factors and stress signals, to control proliferation and differentiation (Pangga, 2025). These pathways interact dynamically, allowing the intestinal epithelium to respond to dietary, microbial and physiological cues. This group governs the fundamental control of intestinal stem cell renewal within the crypt region and ensures continuous epithelial turnover. The Wnt beta-catenin pathway sustains stemness and drives proliferation through transcription of growth-promoting genes (Ding et al., 2024, Zhang et al., 2025a). Notch signaling regulates lineage allocation, determining whether progenitor cells remain proliferative or differentiate into absorptive or secretory cells. The Hippo YAP TAZ pathway senses tissue architecture and mechanical cues, adjusting proliferative activity during regeneration and repair (Quan et al., 2025).

Together, these pathways maintain crypt villus homeostasis, coordinate epithelial renewal and prevent uncontrolled expansion or premature differentiation of enterocytes. Enterocyte proliferation is tightly linked to nutrient availability and cellular energy balance. The mTOR pathway functions as a central nutrient sensor, responding to amino acids, glucose and energy status to regulate protein synthesis and cell growth (Liang et al., 2023). Adequate dietary supply supports anabolic processes required for rapid epithelial turnover. Microbiota derived metabolites, particularly short chain fatty acids, serve as both energy substrates and epigenetic modulators influencing gene expression related to proliferation (Liu et al., 2021). These mechanisms ensure that intestinal growth aligns with dietary intake, metabolic capacity and overall physiological condition.

Apoptosis, proliferation and regulation

Apoptosis at the villus tip complements crypt proliferation, maintaining epithelial homeostasis (Wu et al., 2025b). Programmed cell death removes damaged or aged enterocytes, preventing compromised barrier function (Sugiharto et al., 2026). A balance between apoptosis and proliferation ensures constant villus architecture and absorptive efficiency (Dong et al., 2025) Disruption of this balance, due to stress, infection, or poor nutrition, can lead to villus atrophy, impaired nutrient absorption and increased susceptibility to enteric diseases, emphasizing the importance of coordinated regulation for broiler health and performance (Akram et al., 2025).

Growth factors and endocrine mediators stimulate mitogenic signaling cascades that enhance enterocyte proliferation and mucosal expansion (Figure 1). Activation of epidermal growth factor receptor triggers downstream MAPK and PI3K Akt pathways, promoting cell cycle entry and survival (Quan et al., 2025). Insulin like growth factors support anabolic metabolism, protein synthesis and anti-apoptotic signaling in proliferating cells (Vaccaro et al., 2022). Hormones such as glucagon-like peptide 2 and growth hormone enhance crypt depth and intestinal surface area by stimulating stem and progenitor cell activity (Fesler et al., 2020). These systemic and paracrine signals integrate physiological status, growth demands and production stage to regulate epithelial proliferation (Jia et al., 2025). Immune derived signals modulate epithelial proliferation during homeostasis, stress and disease. Cytokines like interleukin-6 and interleukin-22 stimulate regenerative responses following epithelial damage by enhancing crypt cell proliferation and barrier repair (Baxter et al., 2019). Tumour necrosis factor alpha can exert dual roles, either promoting regeneration at controlled levels or inhibiting proliferation during excessive inflammation (Vaccaro et al., 2022). These mediators coordinate crosstalk between immune and epithelial cells, enabling adaptive remodelling of mucosa which is essential to balance defense mechanisms with controlled epithelial renewal.

Genetic factors

Genetic factors play a central role in regulating the proliferation dynamics of enterocytes in broiler chickens by modulating intestinal stem cell activity, crypt development, epithelial renewal, and nutrient-responsive signaling pathways. Rapidly growing broiler genotypes exhibit enhanced enterocyte proliferation and accelerated epithelial turnover, primarily through the activation of conserved molecular pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin, Notch, PI3K/Akt/mTOR, Hippo, and MAPK signaling, which collectively regulate intestinal stem cell self-renewal, transit-amplifying cell expansion, and differentiation along the crypt-villus axis. Genetic selection for improved growth performance and feed efficiency has been associated with increased crypt cell mitosis, greater villus surface area, elevated expression of proliferation markers including PCNA and Ki67, and enhanced nutrient transporter activity, thereby supporting superior absorptive capacity and intestinal maturation in modern broilers. Moreover, genotype-dependent regulation of transcription factors, growth factors, cytokines, and epigenetic modulators influences enterocyte lineage commitment, barrier integrity, and epithelial regeneration under physiological and environmental stress conditions. Emerging evidence also suggests that genetic variation affects responsiveness to dietary amino acids, minerals, microbiota-derived metabolites, and endocrine signals through mTOR-mediated protein synthesis and Wnt-Notch crosstalk, ultimately shaping intestinal development and metabolic efficiency.

Nutritional modulations

Early-life nutrition

Nutritional interventions during the early post-hatch period strongly influence long-term enterocyte proliferation and intestinal proliferation (Shehata et al., 2021). Early provision of energy, amino acids, vitamins and functional additives accelerates crypt growth, enhances villus architecture and improves epithelial turnover (Elokil et al., 2022). Such early-life programming establishes a foundation for efficient nutrient absorption, enhanced barrier integrity and resilience to stress or pathogens. By optimizing enterocyte proliferation from hatch, broilers can achieve improved growth performance, gut health and welfare, supporting sustainable production under intensive management systems (Jha et al., 2019).

Physical characteristics of feed, including particle size, texture and form, influence mechanical stimulation of the intestine and enterocyte proliferation (Novotný et al., 2023). Coarser particles promote gizzard development, slow digesta passage and enhance villus growth and nutrient absorption (Zaefarian et al., 2016). Feeding frequency and early access to feed post-hatch stimulate crypt activity and epithelial renewal (Chaiyasing et al., 2024). Optimized feed structure and feeding strategies improve intestinal efficiency, enhance nutrient utilization and support growth performance (Nawab et al., 2025a). These approaches are particularly important under commercial production conditions where rapid intestinal development is critical for broiler health and welfare.

Across the compiled studies (Table 1), pelleting-related factors consistently modulated enterocyte proliferation, with approximately 70-80% reporting enhanced villus height, crypt depth, and villus to crypt ratio under pelleted, coarse-particle, and high-durability conditions, indicating accelerated epithelial turnover and improved barrier renewal. Mechanistically, structural stimulation of the gizzard and altered digesta flow appear central to crypt cell proliferation and villus maturation. Moderate fibre 3-5% and sodium bentonite further potentiate proliferative responses, suggesting synergistic effects on mucosal kinetics. In contrast, pellet diameter, conditioning regimes, and fat inclusion show negligible or inconsistent effects, highlighting mechanistic redundancy. Key gaps include limited molecular validation and weak linkage to barrier integrity markers. Future work should integrate pellet architecture with enterocyte kinetics, microbiota, and functional barrier outcomes.

Macro and micronutrients

Energy intake is a primary determinant of enterocyte proliferation and intestinal epithelial turnover (Marcato et al., 2024). Carbohydrates and digestible fats provide ATP required for crypt cell division, differentiation and maintenance of brush border structures (Sadr et al., 2025). Inadequate energy reduces villus height and crypt activity, impairing nutrient absorption and barrier integrity (Anjos et al., 2025) High metabolic demand in rapidly growing broilers makes energy availability critical for epithelial renewal (Madej et al., 2025). Moreover, dietary energy interacts with amino acids, vitamins and microbial metabolites, influencing intestinal efficiency, growth performance and resilience against stressors and enteric challenges (Pangga, 2025). Amino acids directly regulate enterocyte proliferation, differentiation and barrier function (Sadr et al., 2025). Glutamine and glutamate serve as major energy substrates for rapidly dividing crypt cells and support tight junction integrity (Marcato et al., 2024). Threonine contributes to mucin synthesis, reinforcing the mucus layer, while arginine enhances nitric oxide production and vascularization in the lamina propria (Akram et al., 2025). Balanced amino acid supply promotes villus height, crypt depth and absorptive capacity (Madej et al., 2025). Deficiencies disrupt epithelial turnover, reduce nutrient assimilation and compromise intestinal health, negatively affecting growth performance and overall welfare in broiler chickens (Wu et al., 2025a) (Tables 2 and 3).

Fatty acids are essential for enterocyte membrane synthesis and functional integrity (Dong et al., 2025). Long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids improve membrane fluidity, modulate cell signaling and influence inflammatory responses (Pangga, 2025). Adequate lipid intake supports brush border formation, crypt proliferation and epithelial differentiation (Marcato et al., 2024). Fatty acid deficiency reduces villus height, absorptive surface area and nutrient transport efficiency (Akram et al., 2025). Additionally, dietary lipids provide energy for rapidly dividing cells, enhancing epithelial renewal and intestinal resilience (Madej et al., 2025). Proper fatty acid provision is therefore critical for maintaining gut integrity, nutrient utilization and growth performance in broiler chickens (Sugiharto et al., 2026).

Dietary fatty acid sources consistently modulate enterocyte proliferation and intestinal function, with most omega-3 studies (≈75-85%) reporting enhanced villus height, crypt activity, and villus to crypt ratio alongside improved tight junction integrity and reduced inflammation, indicating accelerated epithelial renewal and strengthened barrier function (Table 4–5). Mechanistically, EPA, DHA, and ALA improve membrane composition, reduce oxidative stress, and regulate permeability, with nano-emulsions and blended sources showing synergistic and targeted effects. Saturated fatty acids present more context-dependent outcomes, supporting energy supply and moderate proliferative responses but with fewer studies available. Key gaps include limited dose optimization and molecular validation. Future work should integrate fatty acid signaling with enterocyte kinetics and barrier functionality.

Vitamins and trace minerals are vital cofactors for enterocyte proliferation and intestinal function. Vitamins A, D, E and B complex regulate cell division, differentiation and antioxidant defense (Wu et al., 2025a). Trace minerals such as zinc, selenium and copper support crypt activity, villus maintenance and enzymatic processes (Marcato et al., 2024). Deficiencies impair epithelial renewal, reduce villus height and compromise barrier integrity, increasing susceptibility to stress and enteric infections (Sadr et al., 2025). Hence, adequate micronutrient supply is essential to sustain intestinal efficiency, growth performance and overall gut health in broiler chickens (Madej et al., 2025).

In this review, most studies (≈70-85%) reporting improved villus morphology, antioxidant status, and nutrient absorption. Zinc, selenium, and iron emerge as key drivers of epithelial renewal, acting through oxidative stress reduction, enzyme activation, and cellular proliferation pathways. Synergistic combinations such as zinc with selenium or copper with zinc further amplify barrier integrity and microbial modulation, indicating interactive effects. Organic and nano forms often show superior bioavailability and efficacy compared to inorganic sources. However, inconsistencies in dose ranges and limited molecular evidence persist. Future studies should clarify optimal inclusion strategies and link mineral signaling with enterocyte kinetics and barrier regulation.

Similarly, vitamin supplementation consistently supports enterocyte proliferation and intestinal functionality, with most studies (≈75-85%) reporting improvements in villus morphology, absorptive surface area, and barrier integrity (Table 6–7). Antioxidant vitamins such as C and E reduce oxidative stress and preserve epithelial structure, while vitamins A and D₃ regulate mucosal immunity, tight junction dynamics, and gene expression linked to epithelial renewal. B vitamins contribute indirectly by reducing inflammation, apoptosis, and supporting metabolic pathways essential for enterocyte turnover. Recurring patterns indicate synergistic effects under stress conditions, particularly heat and disease challenges. However, mechanistic depth and dose optimization remain limited. Future research should integrate vitamin signaling with molecular markers of enterocyte proliferation and barrier function.

Gut microbiota, dysbiosis and interactions

The intestinal microbiota of broiler chickens is a complex community of bacteria, fungi and protozoa that varies along the digestive tract (Deng et al., 2025). Dominant bacterial phyla include Firmicutes, Bacteroidetes and Proteobacteria, each contributing to digestion, metabolite production and immune modulation (Pangga, 2025). Microbiota composition is influenced by diet, age, housing and antibiotic exposure. A balanced microbial ecosystem supports crypt cell proliferation, villus maintenance and barrier function. Disruptions in composition can reduce nutrient absorption and impair epithelial turnover, affecting growth and gut health (Karimi et al., 2024). Lactobacillus and Bacillus species dominate the evidence, promoting epithelial renewal through microbial balance, pathogen suppression, and short chain fatty acid production, particularly butyrate, which directly fuels enterocytes. Multi-strain formulations show synergistic effects, further amplifying barrier function and nutrient efficiency. Despite strong consistency, variation in strains and doses limits comparability. Key gaps in current study include mechanistic validation at molecular level and long-term responses (Table 8). Future research should standardize probiotic strategies and link microbial signaling with enterocyte kinetics and barrier regulation.

Microbial metabolites, including short-chain fatty acids, lactate and bioactive compounds, directly influence enterocyte proliferation and differentiation (Kim et al., 2025). Butyrate, for example, serves as a primary energy source for colonocytes, modulates Wnt/β-catenin signaling and enhances tight junction integrity (Yan and Ajuwon, 2017). Other metabolites regulate mucin secretion, antioxidant responses and epithelial repair mechanisms. These signals coordinate crypt activity and villus renewal, linking microbial metabolism to epithelial homeostasis. In broilers, metabolite-driven signaling is critical for maintaining gut efficiency, barrier function and resilience against pathogens (Yang et al., 2024). In the current review, nearly all studies (≈90-95%) reporting increased villus height, improved villus to crypt ratio, enhanced tight junction integrity, and reduced pathogenic load (Table 9). Mechanistically, synergistic interactions between probiotics and prebiotic substrates promote microbiota remodelling and SCFA production, especially butyrate, which directly fuels epithelial renewal and strengthens barrier resilience. Multi strain and fermented product combinations further amplify cytokine regulation, antioxidant defense, and mucosal recovery under stress. However, variability in formulations and doses limits comparability. Future research should standardize synbiotic designs and integrate molecular pathways of enterocyte turnover with functional gut health outcomes.

Imbalance in the gut microbiota, or dysbiosis, disrupts epithelial proliferation and intestinal homeostasis (Attia et al., 2025). Pathogenic bacteria can induce inflammation, oxidative stress and apoptosis, reducing crypt proliferation and villus height (Tretiak et al., 2025). Dysbiosis impairs nutrient absorption, compromises barrier function and increases susceptibility to enteric infections such as coccidiosis or necrotic enteritis (Wu et al., 2025b). Maintaining microbial balance is therefore essential to support continuous enterocyte renewal, optimal intestinal efficiency and overall health in broilers, particularly under intensive production conditions (Zhao et al., 2025).

Early colonization by commensal microbiota influences intestinal development and enterocyte proliferation (Elokil et al., 2022). Microbial signals stimulate crypt maturation, villus elongation and immune system development, establishing long-term gut functionality (Kim et al., 2025). Interactions between host pattern recognition receptors and microbial metabolites modulate epithelial cell proliferation and barrier integrity. Proper host-microbiota crosstalk enhances nutrient absorption, resilience to pathogens and gut efficiency (Yue et al., 2025). In broilers, optimizing early microbial colonization through management or nutritional strategies supports intestinal maturation, health and performance throughout the entire production cycle (Hu et al., 2025).

Phytogenics, nucleotides and novel additives

Phytogenics, including essential oils and plant-derived bioactives, stimulate enterocyte proliferation through antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. These compounds modulate gut microbiota, reduce oxidative stress and enhance crypt-villus renewal (Ceylan et al., 2025). Regular supplementation improves villus height, crypt depth and barrier integrity, supporting nutrient absorption and resilience to enteric challenges. Phytogenic feed additives represent a natural strategy to enhance intestinal function, growth performance and overall health in broiler production (Bonos et al., 2023). In the compiled studies (Table 12), bioactive compounds consistently enhance enterocyte proliferation and intestinal barrier function, with nearly all studies (≈85-95%) reporting improved villus height, strengthened tight junctions, reduced oxidative stress, and better nutrient efficiency. Synergistic nano-emulsions and phytogenic blends show amplified effects on villus architecture and feed efficiency. However, variability in compound forms, doses, and bioavailability limits direct comparison. Key gaps include limited molecular validation and long-term gut integrity outcomes. Hence, future work should integrate phytogenic signaling with enterocyte proliferation dynamics and functional barrier physiology.

Exogenous enzymes, organic acids and emulsifiers modulate enterocyte proliferation by improving nutrient digestibility and reducing intestinal stress (Ayalew, 2022). Enzymes enhance substrate availability for crypt cells, organic acids lower gut pH and inhibit pathogenic bacteria and emulsifiers improve fat digestion and membrane integrity (Chukwudi et al., 2025). These additives collectively support villus growth, crypt activity and epithelial turnover. By optimizing nutrient absorption and gut environment, they enhance barrier function, feed efficiency and overall intestinal health in broilers (Ayalew, 2022). Accordingly, emulsifier supplementation consistently enhances enterocyte proliferation and intestinal morphology, with all studies reporting improved villus height, villus to crypt ratio, and lipid absorption efficiency, indicating strong support for epithelial renewal and barrier maturation. Lysolecithin, glycerol monostearate, phospholipids, and bile acid-based emulsifiers show the most consistent effects by improving micelle formation, reducing lipid stress, and stimulating crypt cell proliferation. Synergistic blends and enzyme linked emulsifiers further amplify nutrient uptake and mucosal development, particularly under low energy or high fat diets. However, mechanistic evidence at cellular and molecular levels remains limited, and dose optimization is inconsistent. Future research should link emulsifier driven lipid metabolism with enterocyte kinetics and tight junction regulation (Tables 13 and 14).

Dietary nucleotides from yeast or purified sources support intestinal barrier integrity and productivity in broiler chickens by enhancing enterocyte proliferation, epithelial renewal and mucosal development. Exogenous nucleotide supply stimulates DNA and RNA synthesis in rapidly dividing enterocytes, resulting in increased villus height, balanced crypt depth and thicker mucosa, which collectively enlarge the absorptive surface and improve nutrient digestion and absorption (Perricone et al., 2025). Nucleosides such as adenosine, cytidine, uridine and guanosine further strengthen intestinal defense by upregulating mucin production and tight junction proteins including MUC2 and occludin, thereby maintaining barrier integrity during pathogen or toxin challenge (Wu et al., 2018). Enhanced epithelial turnover also supports immune function through higher immunoglobulin A activity and improved lymphoid organ development (Kreuz et al., 2020).

These structural and functional improvements lead to better feed conversion ratio, metabolic stability, disease resistance and stress resilience, ultimately improving growth performance, health status and welfare of broiler chickens (Hassanein et al., 2023). Under stress or pathogenic challenge, nucleotides preserve epithelial architecture, upregulate mucin and immune markers, and reduce oxidative damage. However, in the compiled studies (Table 15), dose-response relationships remain poorly defined and long-term effects are underexplored. Future work should integrate nucleotide metabolism with enterocyte kinetics, barrier resilience, and immune-epithelial crosstalk for better understanding.

Trace minerals, nanoparticles and emerging feed additives influence enterocyte proliferation by providing cofactors for enzymatic activity, supporting epithelial repair and enhancing cellular signaling. Zinc, selenium and copper promote crypt proliferation and antioxidant defense, while nanoparticles improve bioavailability and targeted delivery. Novel additives, including functional peptides and postbiotics, modulate epithelial turnover and gut microbiota interactions (Han et al., 2024). These interventions support villus integrity, nutrient absorption and barrier function, contributing to optimized intestinal efficiency and broiler performance (Hosseintabar-Ghasemabad et al., 2024).

Environmental factors

Environmental and management factors strongly influence intestinal development, barrier integrity and nutrient utilization in broiler chickens. Optimal temperature, proper lighting, early feeding, clean water and good litter quality support normal enterocyte proliferation, villus growth and crypt turnover, enhancing absorptive capacity and overall gut efficiency (Stamilla et al., 2021). Conversely, heat or cold stress, high stocking density, poor air quality, chronic noise or handling stress, prolonged fasting, excessive pathogen or mycotoxin exposure and unbalanced dietary fiber can impair epithelial renewal, shorten villi, disrupt tight junctions and increase intestinal permeability (Stamilla et al., 2021; Dong et al., 2025). Controlled microbial exposure, probiotic supplementation, appropriate feed particle size and enriched environments mitigate stress effects, support epithelial regeneration, maintain mucosal integrity and optimize nutrient absorption, thereby improving growth performance, health and welfare in broilers (Mountzouris et al., 2007; Amerah et al., 2007).

In the compiled studies (Table 16), environmental factors exert a strong and consistent influence on enterocyte proliferation and intestinal barrier function, with most studies (≈80-90%) reporting that stressors such as heat, crowding, ammonia exposure, fasting, and mycotoxins suppress crypt cell proliferation, reduce villus height, and increase epithelial permeability. In contrast, optimal thermal conditions, adequate lighting, early feeding, and enriched environments promote crypt activity and villus development through endocrine and metabolic regulation. Management practices such as stocking density, litter and water quality, and feed structure further modulate gut maturation via microbiota and inflammatory pathways. Overall, environmental stress consistently impairs epithelial renewal, while supportive systems enhance barrier integrity. Future work should integrate environmental stressors with molecular markers of enterocyte kinetics and resilience pathways.

Effects of enterocyte proliferation

Barrier functions

Tight junction proteins, including claudins, occludins and zonula occludens, are critical for maintaining epithelial barrier integrity. Enterocyte proliferation and proper differentiation ensure continuous renewal of these junctions, preventing paracellular leakage of luminal antigens, toxins and pathogens (Xie et al., 2025). Disruption in tight junction expression or function weakens the epithelial barrier, leading to increased intestinal permeability. In broilers, adequate nutrition, microbial balance and stress management are essential to maintain tight junction integrity, supporting gut homeostasis and overall health (Cuccato et al., 2022a).

Effective barrier function depends on the balance between enterocyte proliferation in crypts and differentiation along the villus. Rapid and coordinated renewal ensures mature cells with intact tight junctions and absorptive capacity reach the villus tip. Delayed proliferation or abnormal differentiation reduces barrier strength, compromises nutrient absorption and increases susceptibility to pathogens (Zhang et al., 2019). Optimizing proliferation through diet, microbiota modulation and management practices enhances intestinal integrity, supporting performance, gut health and resilience in broiler chickens (Aruwa et al., 2021), (Bonis et al., 2021). Goblet cells secrete mucins that form the mucus layer, providing a protective barrier against mechanical and microbial insults. Enterocyte proliferation influences goblet cell density and mucus turnover, while microbial metabolites modulate mucin production (Duangnumsawang et al., 2021). Adequate mucus protects the epithelium, facilitates nutrient absorption and prevents pathogen adherence. Disruption of this interaction reduces barrier function, increases inflammation and compromises gut health. Maintaining balanced proliferation and goblet cell activity is therefore essential for intestinal efficiency and broiler welfare (Duangnumsawang et al., 2021).

Oxidative stress and inflammatory responses can impair enterocyte proliferation and tight junction integrity (Liu et al., 2022). Reactive oxygen species damage cellular components, while cytokine-mediated inflammation induces apoptosis and disrupts villus architecture (Mishra and Jha, 2019). Chronic stress, infections, or poor nutrition exacerbate these effects, reducing barrier function and epithelial renewal. Strategies to mitigate oxidative stress, including antioxidants, balanced nutrition and microbial modulation, support enterocyte proliferation, preserve mucosal integrity and enhance intestinal efficiency in broiler chickens (Mishra and Jha, 2019). Compromised barrier function allows luminal pathogens, toxins and antigens to translocate, triggering inflammation, reduced nutrient absorption and growth retardation. Villus atrophy and crypt hyperplasia further impair intestinal efficiency (Nguyen, 2021). Enterocyte proliferation is central to restoring barrier integrity, maintaining absorptive surface area and supporting gut resilience (Mann et al., 2024). In broilers, impaired barrier function reduces feed efficiency, performance and welfare, emphasizing the critical link between epithelial renewal, barrier maintenance and sustainable production outcomes (De Jong et al., 2025).

Nutrient utilization

The absorptive capacity of the intestine is largely determined by the surface area of enterocytes, including villus height, microvilli length and overall epithelial coverage. Enhanced proliferation and proper differentiation increase the number of functional cells, expanding the absorptive surface (Alagbe, 2025). Greater surface area improves digestion and nutrient uptake, supporting rapid growth in broilers (Nguyen, 2021). Conversely, reduced enterocyte proliferation or villus atrophy diminishes absorptive efficiency, limiting energy and nutrient availability and adversely affecting growth performance and overall intestinal efficiency (Comi et al., 2025).

Enterocyte proliferation affects the expression of brush border enzymes and nutrient transporters critical for carbohydrate, protein and lipid digestion. Adequate epithelial renewal ensures continuous enzyme activity and transporter availability along the villus (Mann et al., 2024). Impaired proliferation reduces enzyme density and nutrient transporter expression, decreasing nutrient assimilation (Sadr et al., 2025), (Mann et al., 2024). Optimizing proliferation through diet, microbiota modulation, or additives enhances enzymatic activity, supports efficient nutrient uptake and contributes to better growth performance and intestinal efficiency in broiler production systems (Sadr et al., 2025).

Villus morphology, including height, width and crypt depth, reflects enterocyte proliferation and intestinal functionality (Saini, 2022). Taller, well-structured villi provide greater surface area for nutrient absorption, while shallow crypts indicate lower proliferative activity. Improved villus architecture is closely associated with higher feed efficiency and nutrient utilization (Gilani et al., 2021). Structural changes resulting from diet, stress, or infection directly impact feed conversion ratios, growth rates and overall performance, highlighting the importance of maintaining proper villus development through intestinal epithelial renewal (Wang et al., 2025).

Efficient enterocyte proliferation and intestinal renewal directly influence feed conversion ratio (FCR) in broilers. A healthy epithelium maximizes nutrient absorption, reduces digestive losses and minimizes energy wasted on repair or immune responses (Sadr et al., 2025). Impaired proliferation or villus atrophy increases FCR, decreasing growth efficiency and economic returns (Scott-Cook et al., 2025). Optimizing intestinal epithelial dynamics through diet, microbial management and stress mitigation is therefore essential to improve FCR, support broiler performance and maintain intestinal health and overall production sustainability (Xiong et al., 2024).

Enterocyte turnover is metabolically demanding, consuming energy and amino acids for proliferation, differentiation and apoptosis. While essential for maintaining gut integrity, excessive turnover increases energy expenditure, potentially reducing net growth efficiency (Akram et al., 2024). Balancing proliferation with metabolic demand is crucial to optimize intestinal health without compromising growth performance. Nutritional strategies, feed additives and microbiota modulation can influence turnover rates, ensuring that enterocyte renewal supports both intestinal functionality and metabolic efficiency in broilers (Caldas, 2016).

Performance and carcass traits

Enterocyte turnover rates and proliferation dynamics vary across production phases, influencing growth kinetics. Early post-hatch intestinal development sets the foundation for nutrient absorption, while mid- and late-growth phases require continued epithelial renewal to sustain feed efficiency and growth (Urban et al., 2025). Stress, diet changes, or infections during these phases can impair enterocyte proliferation, reducing performance (Tretiak et al., 2025). Managing gut health throughout the production cycle ensures consistent growth kinetics, efficient nutrient utilization and optimal production performance in commercial broiler operations (Akram et al., 2024).

Enterocyte proliferation affects both feed utilization and nutrient retention. A healthy, proliferative epithelium maximizes digestion and absorption, reducing nutrient losses in feces and increasing retention efficiency (Scott-Cook et al., 2025). In contrast, compromised proliferation reduces digestive capacity, potentially increasing voluntary feed intake to meet metabolic demands. Strategies that promote epithelial renewal, such as optimized amino acid supply, energy provision, or probiotics, improve nutrient retention, maintain gut integrity and ensure that feed intake is efficiently converted into growth rather than wasted energy (Sadr et al., 2025).

Enterocyte proliferation directly influences nutrient absorption, which drives growth rate and body weight gain in broilers. Efficient renewal and differentiation ensure that villi maintain maximum surface area for digestion, allowing rapid assimilation of proteins, carbohydrates and lipids. Impaired proliferation reduces absorptive efficiency, limiting growth potential (Mushtaq et al., 2025). Enhancing epithelial turnover through nutrition, microbial modulation, or feed additives supports optimal villus architecture, accelerates weight gain and contributes to the high growth performance demanded in modern broiler production systems (Sakr et al., 2025).

The functional state of the intestinal epithelium influences carcass composition and meat yield. Adequate enterocyte proliferation ensures efficient nutrient absorption and energy availability, supporting muscle development and optimal deposition of lean tissue (Alagbe, 2025). Villus atrophy or reduced proliferation compromises nutrient efficiency, leading to lower carcass weight, reduced breast muscle yield and suboptimal body composition (Salahi et al., 2025). Maintaining intestinal health through targeted nutrition, microbial balance and early-life programming directly contributes to meat quality and commercial production outcomes in broilers (Gao et al., 2025b).

Efficient enterocyte proliferation enhances nutrient absorption, feed conversion and growth, directly impacting economic returns. Reduced proliferation increases feed costs, limits growth and elevates susceptibility to disease, leading to higher production losses (Urban et al., 2025). Strategies targeting epithelial renewal, including optimized nutrition, feed additives and microbial management, improve performance, reduce mortality and enhance profitability (Salahi et al., 2025). Understanding the link between gut health and economic outcomes allows producers to implement interventions that support both sustainable and cost-effective broiler production systems (Xiong et al., 2024).

Health and disease resistance

Enterocyte proliferation is critical for maintaining intestinal integrity during enteric infections (Wu et al., 2025a). Rapid renewal replaces damaged or infected epithelial cells, restoring barrier function and limiting pathogen translocation (Sugiharto et al., 2026). Reduced proliferation during infections can exacerbate mucosal damage, increase inflammation and impair nutrient absorption (Dong et al., 2025). Supporting epithelial renewal through nutrition, microbiota modulation, or feed additives enhances resilience, promotes faster recovery and mitigates the negative impact of pathogens on growth performance and overall gut health in broiler chickens (Comi et al., 2025). Common broiler enteric diseases, including coccidiosis, necrotic enteritis and dysbacteriosis, disrupt enterocyte proliferation and villus architecture (Mesa-Pineda et al., 2021). Parasite or bacterial infection triggers epithelial apoptosis, villus atrophy and crypt hyperplasia, compromising nutrient absorption and barrier integrity (Goo and Kim, 2025). Maintaining optimal enterocyte renewal mitigates tissue damage, supports nutrient assimilation and facilitates faster recovery (Attia et al., 2022). Targeted interventions such as amino acid supplementation, probiotics, or feed additives help preserve epithelial function, reduce disease severity and sustain growth performance during enteric challenges (Opazo et al., 2025).

Enterocyte proliferation is closely linked to intestinal immune responses. Cytokines released by immune cells, including interleukins and interferons, regulate crypt proliferation and epithelial repair (Silva, 2025). Conversely, proliferating enterocytes influence immune signaling and mucosal defense (Duangnumsawang et al., 2023). Balanced crosstalk between epithelial and immune cells ensures rapid recovery from infections and prevents chronic inflammation (Ghiselli et al., 2021). Disruption of this interaction, due to stress or pathogenic insult, reduces epithelial renewal, compromises barrier integrity and diminishes overall health and disease resilience in broiler chickens (Liu et al., 2023).

Following epithelial injury, coordinated enterocyte proliferation and differentiation restore villus structure and absorptive capacity (Lv et al., 2025). The speed and efficiency of recovery depend on crypt stem cell activity, nutrient availability and microbial balance (Quan et al., 2025). Accelerated renewal reduces pathogen invasion, restores digestive function and limits production losses (Guo et al., 2023a). In broilers, promoting recovery through early nutritional interventions, feed additives and microbiota management enhances resilience, improves performance and ensures gut homeostasis is maintained even under intensive production conditions (Wang et al., 2024).

In antibiotic-free systems, maintaining enterocyte proliferation is critical for gut health and disease resistance (Idowu et al., 2025). Reduced antimicrobial support increases pathogen pressure, making epithelial renewal essential to prevent barrier disruption and nutrient loss (Oke et al., 2025). Nutritional strategies, probiotics, prebiotics and functional feed additives support enterocyte proliferation, enhance immunity and limit disease impact (Opazo et al., 2025). Efficient epithelial renewal ensures optimal nutrient absorption, performance and welfare, providing a sustainable approach for maintaining productivity in antibiotic-free broiler production (Lv et al., 2025).

Welfare issues

Intestinal health is a key indicator of broiler welfare, as a functional gut supports nutrient absorption, barrier integrity and disease resilience (De Jong et al., 2025). Enterocyte proliferation maintains villus architecture, crypt activity and epithelial renewal, directly reflecting gut functionality (Castro et al., 2024). Impaired proliferation leads to malabsorption, increased susceptibility to enteric infections and compromised growth, which negatively impact welfare (Pangga, 2025). Monitoring epithelial turnover and gut morphology provides an objective measure of welfare status and can guide management strategies to improve broiler health and performance (Idowu et al., 2025).

Chronic stress, including heat stress, disrupts enterocyte proliferation and intestinal homeostasis (Kikusato and Toyomizu, 2023). Elevated corticosterone levels reduce crypt cell proliferation, impair villus integrity and compromise barrier function (Liu et al., 2025a). This decreases nutrient absorption and increases susceptibility to enteric pathogens (Ncho, 2025). Maintaining epithelial renewal under stress conditions is critical for gut resilience, nutrient efficiency and overall health. Nutritional and environmental interventions (Nambapana and Jayasena, 2024) that support enterocyte turnover mitigate the negative effects of stress, enhancing broiler welfare and sustaining growth performance. Inflammation resulting from infections, dysbiosis, or dietary imbalance affects enterocyte proliferation and contributes to intestinal discomfort (Ducatelle et al., 2025). Villi atrophy and epithelial damage reduce absorptive capacity, trigger nociceptive responses and impair welfare (De Jong et al., 2025). Adequate enterocyte renewal minimizes tissue damage, restores barrier integrity and reduces inflammatory signaling (Anjos et al., 2025). Supporting epithelial health is therefore essential not only for growth performance but also to alleviate pain and discomfort associated with compromised intestinal function in broiler chickens (Pont et al., 2021).

Targeted nutritional interventions, including balanced amino acids, vitamins, trace minerals and functional additives, support enterocyte proliferation and villus maintenance (Obianwuna, 2024). Management strategies such as optimal feed form, early feeding, environmental control and stress reduction enhance epithelial turnover and gut integrity (Anjos et al., 2025). These combined approaches improve intestinal efficiency, nutrient absorption and resilience to disease, translating directly into better welfare outcomes (Salahi et al., 2025). Maintaining gut health through enterocyte renewal ensures both physiological and behavioral indicators of well-being in broilers (Anjos et al., 2025).

Optimizing enterocyte proliferation and gut health aligns with ethical responsibility in poultry production (Dong et al., 2025). Healthy intestines reduce disease incidence, minimize pain and improve nutrient utilization, supporting animal welfare alongside productivity (Haque et al., 2020). Strategies promoting epithelial renewal, such as precision nutrition, microbiota modulation and stress mitigation, provide sustainable, welfare-conscious production (Barekatain et al., 2021). Understanding and managing intestinal function not only enhances performance but also ensures ethical standards are met, fulfilling societal and industry expectations for the humane and responsible rearing of broiler chickens (De Jong et al., 2025).

Assessment techniques

Histomorphometry is a foundational technique for evaluating enterocyte proliferation, villus height, crypt depth and villus-to-crypt ratio. Tissue sections are stained and measured under light microscopy, providing structural indicators of epithelial turnover and gut health. Changes in villus architecture and crypt depth reflect proliferation rates and absorptive capacity (Belote et al., 2023). While simple and widely used, histomorphometry requires standardized sampling, consistent section orientation and precise measurement techniques to ensure reliable comparisons across treatments and studies in broiler intestinal research (Alagbe, 2025). Immunohistochemistry enables direct detection of proliferating cells using markers such as PCNA, Ki-67 and BrdU incorporation. PCNA and Ki-67 indicate active cell cycle phases, while BrdU labels newly synthesized DNA. These markers quantify proliferation rates and identify spatial distribution of proliferating cells along the crypt-villus axis. They provide higher specificity than morphology alone, allowing evaluation of subtle treatment effects (Mirzaeian et al., 2024). Standardized protocols, optimized antibody selection and careful quantification are essential to ensure reproducibility in broiler enterocyte studies (Dovmark et al., 2022).

Advanced imaging techniques, such as confocal microscopy, electron microscopy and live-cell imaging, provide detailed visualization of enterocyte morphology, junctional complexes and intracellular structures. These methods allow three-dimensional reconstruction of villi, real-time observation of cell migration and evaluation of microvillus integrity (Wan et al., 2022). Coupled with fluorescent labeling, imaging techniques enhance quantification of proliferation, differentiation and apoptosis (Akram et al., 2024). While resource-intensive, they provide high-resolution insights into epithelial dynamics, complementing histological and molecular approaches in broiler intestinal research (Tretiak et al., 2025).

Molecular techniques, including RT-qPCR and transcriptome analysis, assess expression of proliferation-related genes, such as cyclins, growth factors and Wnt signaling components. These approaches reveal regulatory mechanisms driving enterocyte renewal and complement histological findings. Gene expression profiling allows detection of early changes in proliferation, differentiation and stress responses (Sadr et al., 2025). Integration with functional assays and histological data provides a comprehensive understanding of epithelial turnover, offering insights into nutrition, management and additive interventions that influence broiler intestinal health (Na et al., 2021). Assessing enterocyte proliferation faces challenges, including variability in sampling sites, tissue orientation, staining protocols and marker specificity. Inter-laboratory differences complicate data comparison, while resource constraints limit access to advanced imaging or molecular techniques. Standardization of methodologies, consistent protocols and combined multi-level approaches are essential to improve reproducibility. Addressing these challenges ensures reliable assessment of enterocyte proliferation, enabling accurate evaluation of nutrition, additives, microbiota and management interventions in broiler intestinal health and performance studies.

Overall implications

Early-life nutrition and management are critical for intestinal proliferation and long-term gut function. Strategies such as immediate post-hatch feeding, early provision of amino acids, functional additives and probiotics promote crypt maturation, villus elongation and epithelial turnover. Early intervention enhances barrier function, resilience to pathogens and nutrient absorption, establishing a foundation for optimal growth. Implementing targeted early-life practices ensures broilers achieve improved performance, intestinal efficiency and welfare throughout the production cycle.

Precision nutrition, based on age, growth phase and intestinal condition, enables targeted support of enterocyte proliferation and epithelial health. Coupling dietary strategies with gut health monitoring through histology, biomarkers and microbiome analysis allows timely intervention to maintain epithelial turnover and villus integrity. Such approaches optimize nutrient absorption, feed efficiency and growth performance. Integrating precision nutrition with real-time gut monitoring facilitates adaptive management strategies for commercial broiler production, improving productivity, health and welfare outcomes.

Diet formulation plays a central role in maintaining enterocyte proliferation and intestinal health. Adequate provision of energy, digestible proteins, amino acids, vitamins, minerals and functional feed additives supports crypt cell proliferation, villus growth and epithelial differentiation. Tailored nutrient balance enhances absorptive capacity, barrier integrity and nutrient utilization. By integrating knowledge of enterocyte dynamics into diet design, producers can improve gut efficiency, growth performance and overall health in broiler chickens, while reducing susceptibility to enteric disorders.

Maintaining enterocyte proliferation and intestinal integrity reduces production losses caused by malabsorption, disease, or stress-induced gut dysfunction. Optimized epithelial renewal enhances nutrient utilization, feed conversion and resistance to enteric infections. Implementing dietary, microbial and management interventions that promote villus growth, crypt activity and barrier function mitigates morbidity, improves growth consistency and reduces mortality. Gut-focused strategies translate directly into higher productivity, better carcass traits and economic benefits for commercial broiler operations. Optimizing enterocyte proliferation supports sustainable, welfare-oriented broiler production. Healthy intestines reduce disease risk, improve feed efficiency and lower environmental nutrient losses. Gut-targeted nutrition and management strategies reduce reliance on antibiotics, enhance resilience to stress and improve overall bird welfare. Integrating enterocyte-focused approaches into production systems aligns with ethical, environmental and economic goals, ensuring sustainable growth, reduced resource wastage and the responsible, welfare-conscious rearing of broiler chickens in modern poultry production.

Gaps and future directions

Despite extensive research on enterocyte proliferation, standardized biomarkers for assessing epithelial turnover in broilers are lacking (Cloft et al., 2023). Variability in histological markers, immunohistochemical techniques and molecular assays complicates comparisons across studies (Cuccato et al., 2022b). Developing validated, reproducible biomarkers would enable precise quantification of proliferation rates, differentiation and apoptosis. Standardization would improve experimental reliability, facilitate evaluation of nutritional and management interventions and support broader application in commercial broiler production, advancing our understanding of intestinal health and performance outcomes.

Enterocyte turnover is metabolically demanding, yet the energetic cost associated with crypt proliferation, differentiation and apoptosis in broilers remains poorly quantified (Macelline et al., 2025). Understanding the balance between nutrient allocation for epithelial renewal versus growth is critical for optimizing feed formulation and energy efficiency (Nawab et al., 2025b). Investigating the energy demands of epithelial maintenance under varying nutritional, environmental, or stress conditions could inform strategies to enhance intestinal efficiency, reduce metabolic wastage and improve growth performance and sustainability in commercial broiler production.

While microbial metabolites are known to influence enterocyte proliferation, the interplay between the gut microbiome and host signaling pathways remains incompletely understood (Yang et al., 2022). Detailed mechanistic studies linking microbial composition, metabolite profiles and epithelial renewal are limited (Dittoe et al., 2022). Integrating microbiome analysis with host transcriptomics, proteomics and metabolomics could reveal critical interactions that regulate crypt activity, villus architecture and barrier integrity. Such insights are essential for designing interventions that enhance gut health and growth performance in broilers.

Most studies on enterocyte proliferation are conducted under controlled experimental conditions, which may not reflect commercial production environments (Kers et al., 2022). Variability in diet composition, pathogen exposure, stress and management can influence proliferation dynamics, limiting the applicability of laboratory findings (Kers et al., 2022; Muyyarikkandy et al., 2023). Bridging this translational gap requires field-based validation of interventions, including feed additives, nutritional strategies and microbiota modulation, to ensure practical relevance for improving gut health, performance and welfare in commercial broiler operations.

Advances in omics technologies, including genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics and metabolomics, offer unprecedented opportunities to study enterocyte proliferation (Naeem, 2026). Coupled with high-resolution imaging and computational modeling, these tools can elucidate regulatory networks, identify novel biomarkers and quantify epithelial turnover dynamics (Neethirajan, 2023). Applying integrative omics approaches in poultry will allow comprehensive analysis of nutrient, microbial and environmental influences on intestinal health. Future research leveraging these technologies can enhance precision nutrition, management and feed additive strategies to optimize enterocyte proliferation and broiler performance.

Different feed additives vary in their potency and mechanisms affecting enterocyte proliferation. Enzymes primarily improve substrate availability, phytogenics modulate oxidative stress and microbial balance and minerals or nanoparticles enhance enzymatic cofactors and epithelial signaling (Han et al., 2024). Understanding comparative efficacy allows producers to select combinations that synergistically improve villus height, crypt activity, nutrient absorption and barrier integrity. Tailored strategies can maximize enterocyte renewal, enhance gut health and improve growth performance in broiler chickens (Katu et al., 2025). While feed additives enhance enterocyte proliferation and gut health, safety, regulatory compliance and sustainability are critical. Additives must be non-toxic, approved by authorities and environmentally responsible (Sampath and Kim, 2025). Overuse or inappropriate combinations can disrupt gut microbiota or induce metabolic stress. Sustainable selection of additives ensures long-term benefits for intestinal efficiency, bird welfare and production profitability. Integrating safe, effective and eco-friendly feed additives will support optimal enterocyte proliferation, gut integrity and sustainable broiler production (Sarmad, 2024).

Conclusions

Enterocyte proliferation is a central regulator of intestinal integrity, linking epithelial renewal with nutrient absorption, barrier function, and overall performance in broiler chickens. The coordinated balance between crypt cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis sustains villus architecture and ensures efficient digestive and absorptive capacity. Evidence synthesized in this review demonstrates that nutrition, microbiota, environmental conditions, and management practices collectively modulate these dynamics, thereby directly influencing growth efficiency, health status, and welfare outcomes.

Despite these advances, important knowledge gaps persist regarding standardization of biomarkers for epithelial turnover, quantification of energetic costs associated with continuous cell renewal, and mechanistic integration of host–microbiota signaling pathways. Variability in methodologies and limited large-scale field validations constrain comparability and practical application. Addressing these gaps through coordinated protocols, multi-omics integration, and production-scale studies will be essential to strengthen mechanistic understanding for improving translational relevance in commercial broiler production.

From the universal applied perspective, strategic optimization of enterocyte proliferation offers one of the most practical avenues to enhance intestinal efficiency, resilience, and productivity in modern broiler production systems. Precision nutrition, targeted microbiota modulation, and improved environmental management can collectively support epithelial renewal, strengthen barrier function, and reduce disease susceptibility. Integrating these approaches into production systems will contribute to improved feed efficiency, reduced reliance on antibiotics, and more sustainable and welfare-oriented broiler production.

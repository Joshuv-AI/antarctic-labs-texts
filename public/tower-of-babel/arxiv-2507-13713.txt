Arithmetic monodromy of hyper-Kähler varieties over p p -adic fields
Kazuhiro Ito, Tetsushi Ito, Teruhisa Koshikawa, Teppei Takamatsu, Haitao Zou

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the p p -adic and ℓ \ell -adic monodromy operators associated with hyper-Kähler varieties over p p -adic fields, in connection with Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky Lie algebras. We investigate a conjectural relation between the nilpotency indices of these monodromy operators on higher-degree cohomology groups and on the second cohomology, which may be viewed as an arithmetic analogue of Nagai’s conjecture for degenerations of hyper-Kähler manifolds over a disk. We verify this arithmetic version of Nagai’s conjecture for hyper-Kähler varieties over p p -adic fields, assuming they belong to one of the four known deformation types. As part of our approach, we introduce a new method to analyze the p p -adic cohomology of hyper-Kähler varieties via Sen’s theory.

1.
Introduction

We fix a prime number
p
throughout the paper.
Let
K
be a complete discretely valued field of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
with perfect residue field
k
. Let
\ell
be a prime number.
Let
X
be a smooth proper variety over
K
. Then we obtain Galois representations

\phi_{\ell,i}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}\nolimits(\overline{K}/K)\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(H^{i}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell}))

for integers
0\leq i\leq 2\dim X
.
(Here
\overline{K}
is an algebraic closure of
K
and
X_{\overline{K}}\coloneqq X\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
.)
If
\ell\neq p
, Grothendieck’s
\ell
-adic monodromy theorem in
[
SGA7-I
]
implies that there is an open subgroup
I
of the inertia subgroup
I_{K}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}\nolimits(\overline{K}/K)
such that the restriction of
\phi_{\ell,i}
on
I
is
unipotent
, i.e., there is an integer
\nu\geq 0
such that

(\phi_{\ell,i}(g)-1)^{\nu+1}=0
for all
g\in I
.
We call the minimal integer
\nu
with this property the (
\ell
-adic) monodromy nilpotency index in degree
i
. Denote it by
\nu_{\ell,i}
. If
\ell=p
, a similar monodromy nilpotency index
\nu_{p,i}
can be defined via Fontaine’s formalism on potentially semistable
p
-adic Galois representations.

Problem 1.1
.

How can one determine the monodromy nilpotency index
\nu_{\ell,i}
for a prime
\ell
(including
\ell=p
) in a degree
0\leq i\leq 2\dim X
?

In this paper, we will focus on
1.1
in the case that
X
is a
hyper-Kähler variety
. In complex geometry, hyper-Kähler varieties are also known as irreducible holomorphic symplectic (IHS) varieties, which are higher-dimensional analogues of K3 surfaces and form one of the three building blocks of compact Kähler manifolds with trivial first Chern class.
As with K3 surfaces, the notion of hyper-Kähler variety extends naturally to general base fields, at least in characteristic zero.
We will see that the rich geometric structure would lead to an ingredient for the
1.1
for hyper-Kähler varieties over
K
.

1.1.
Nagai’s conjecture and arithmetic analogues

In studying a projective degeneration of a complex hyper-Kähler variety, Nagai proposed the following conjecture on the nilpotency indices of local monodromy operators:

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
Nag08
, Conjecture 5.1]
)
.

Denote
\Delta=\{z\in{\mathds{C}}\;|\;\lvert z\rvert<1\}
for the open disk. Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\Delta
be a degeneration of hyper-Kähler varieties of dimension
2n
over
\Delta^{*}=\Delta\setminus\{0\}
.
Consider the (log-)monodromy operator
N_{i}
on the Betti cohomology
H^{i}(X,{\mathds{Q}})
of a smooth fiber
X=\pi^{-1}(t)
at
t\in\Delta^{*}
.
The monodromy operators satisfy

\nu(N_{2i})=i\nu(N_{2})

for every
0\leq i\leq n
. Here
\nu(N)
is the
nilpotency index
of a nilpotent linear operator
N\in\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(V)
on a finite-dimensional vector space over a field, i.e.,

\nu(N)\coloneqq\min\{m\in{\mathds{Z}}_{\geq 0}\mid N^{m+1}=0\}.

The local monodromy theorem implies that
\nu(N_{2})\in\left\{0,1,2\right\}
(cf.
[
Lan73
, (A.2)]
), and we say that the projective degeneration
\mathcal{X}/\Delta
is of Type I, II, or III respectively.
1.2
posits a deep relation between these three degeneration types and monodromy nilpotency indices in higher degrees, which has been studied by many people.

•

If
\mathcal{X}/\Delta
is of Type I (
\nu(N_{2})=0
) or Type III (
\nu(N_{2})=2)
, then Nagai’s conjecture is confirmed by Kollár–Laza–Saccà–Voisin (cf.
[
Kol+18
, Theorem 1.7, Proposition 7.14 (2)]
). Soldatenkov
[
Sol20
, Corollary 3.6]
gives a proof for possibly
non-projective
degenerations of Type I.

•

Nagai’s conjecture is still widely open for Type II (
\nu(N_{2})=1
) degenerations in general. Recently, Green–Kim–Laza–Robles
[
Gre+22
]
proved Conjecture
1.2
when
X
is in one of the four known deformation classes (i.e.,
K3^{[n]}
-type,
\mathop{\mathrm{Kum}}\nolimits_{n}
-type,
\mathop{\mathrm{OG}}\nolimits_{6}
-type, and
\mathop{\mathrm{OG}}\nolimits_{10}
-type). Their method involves a detailed study on the action of the
Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky (LLV) Lie algebra
on the cohomology.

•

In
[
HM23
]
, Huybrechts–Mauri provide a different viewpoint for the Type II degenerations by the perverse filtration attached to an isotropic class.

It is generally believed that projective models over the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of
K
are arithmetic analogues of “degenerating families over the unit disk
\Delta
”; see
[
Del71
, Table 9.1]
for example. Thus, it is natural to ask an arithmetic analogue of
1.2
. More precisely, we formulate the following two conjectures.

If
\ell\neq p
, Grothendieck’s
\ell
-adic monodromy theorem we mentioned before actually asserts that there is a nilpotent operator
N_{\ell,i}\in\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell}}(H^{i}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell}))
such that

\phi_{\ell,i}(\sigma)=\exp(t_{\ell}(\sigma)N_{\ell,i})

for any
\sigma\in I\subset I_{K}
,
where
t_{\ell}\colon I_{K}\to{\mathds{Z}}_{\ell}
is the composition of
\ell
-primary tame quotient of the inertia group with a fixed trivialization
{\mathds{Z}}_{\ell}(1)\simeq{\mathds{Z}}_{\ell}
.

Conjecture 1.3

(
\ell
-adic analogue of Nagai’s conjecture)
.

For a hyper-Kähler variety
X
over
K
of dimension
2n
, the monodromy operators satisfy

\nu(N_{\ell,2i})=i\nu(N_{\ell,2})

for every
0\leq i\leq n
.

If
\ell=p
, by applying Fontaine’s functor
\Dpst(-)

for potentially semistable representations,
we obtain a
K_{0}^{\ur}
-vector space

\Dpst(H^{i}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{p}))

with a nilpotent operator

N_{p,i}
, where
K_{0}^{\ur}
is the maximal unramified extension of the fraction field
K_{0}
of the ring of Witt vectors
W(k)
.
See
Section

2.3
below.

Conjecture 1.4

(
p
-adic analogue of Nagai’s conjecture)
.

For a hyper-Kähler variety
X
over
K
of dimension
2n
, the monodromy operators satisfy

\nu(N_{p,2i})=i\nu(N_{p,2})

for every
0\leq i\leq n
.

We explore these two conjectures and, in particular, confirm their validity for hyper-Kähler varieties of four known deformation types.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler variety over
K
of dimension
2n
.
Assume that
X
is one of four types:
K3^{[n]}
-type,
\mathop{\mathrm{Kum}}\nolimits_{n}
-type,
\mathop{\mathrm{OG}}\nolimits_{6}
-type, or
\mathop{\mathrm{OG}}\nolimits_{10}
-type (see
2.3
).
Then Conjecture
1.3

and
Conjecture
1.4

hold true for
X
.

Furthermore, we have a geometric description for the monodromy nilpotency index
\nu(N_{\ell,2})
, which does not depend on
\ell
(including
\ell=p
), in terms of the reduction type of
Kuga–Satake abelian varieties
for any hyper-Kähler variety
X
over
K
with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
(see
5.2
).
We thus obtain the notion of reduction type for hyper-Kähler varieties
X
over
K
; we say that
X
has
Type I, II, or III reduction
if
\nu(N_{\ell,2})=0,1\text{ or }2
respectively. By definition, we have
\nu_{\ell,i}=\nu(N_{\ell,i})
. We actually obtain a satisfactory answer to
1.1
for hyper-Kähler varieties in these four types combining with
1.5
.
A direct consequence of our discussion, together with Poincaré duality, is the following:

Corollary 1.6
.

Assume
X
satisfies conditions in
1.5
. We have

\nu(N_{p,2i})=\nu(N_{\ell,2i})

for any prime
\ell
and any
0\leq i\leq 2n
.

1.2.
General remarks on arithmetic Nagai’s conjectures

As in
[
Gre+22
]
, the theory of LLV Lie algebras plays a crucial role in our proof.
Verbitsky’s description of the graded algebra generated by the second cohomology inside the full cohomology (often referred to as the Verbitsky component) implies that for any hyper-Kähler variety
X
over
K
of dimension
2n
,
the inequality

\nu(N_{\ell,2i})\geq i\nu(N_{\ell,2})

holds for every
0\leq i\leq n
and
\ell
.
Since we have
\nu(N_{\ell,2i})\leq 2i
in general, this implies
1.3

and

1.4
when
X
has Type III reduction; see
7.5
.

A crucial step in the study of the case where
X
has Type I or Type II reduction is to show that the
\ell
-adic and
p
-adic monodromy operators in all (even) degrees are contained in the LLV Lie algebra.
In fact, this inclusion is enough to prove the conjectures in the Type I case; see
7.6
.

For the four known deformation types, this can be deduced from the Mumford–Tate conjecture for the full cohomology; see
Section

4
.
On the other hand, for
p
-adic monodromy operators, we provide an alternative approach using Sen’s theory as a new input.
This enables us to prove that
p
-adic monodromy operators lie in the LLV Lie algebra without using the Mumford–Tate conjecture, and even without assuming that our hyper-Kähler varieties belong to one of the four known deformation types.
See
Section

6
for details.

Remark 1.7
.

Along the way, we also present a Kuga–Satake type construction for higher-degree
p
-adic cohomology groups of hyper-Kähler varieties.
As another application of our calculations, we will see that for any hyper-Kähler variety
X
over
K
with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
, the monodromy operator on
\Dpst(H^{i}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{p}))
satisfies
Griffiths transversality
for all
i
.
See
Section

6.2
for details.

Remark 1.8
.

Unlike the
p
-adic version, we can prove
\ell
-adic Nagai’s conjecture (
1.3
) of Type I only for the four known deformation types.
It is unclear how to show that the
\ell
-adic monodromy operators lie in the LLV Lie algebra in general.

In the case of Type II reduction,
we have to investigate the irreducible decomposition of the full cohomology with respect to the action of the LLV Lie algebra.
This decomposition is called the
LLV decomposition
.
In
[
Gre+22
]
, Green–Kim–Laza–Robles gave a criterion for the validity of Nagai’s conjecture in terms of a representation-theoretic condition on the LLV decomposition.
Then they proved Nagai’s conjecture for the four known deformation types by verifying this representation-theoretic condition.
We employ the same strategy to establish the arithmetic analogues; see
7.7
.

Finally, we remark that we can also compute nilpotency indices in odd degrees in some cases by a similar strategy; see
Section

7.3
.
For example, we can prove the following result, as observed by Soldatenkov in the topological case (cf.
[
Sol20
, Theorem 3.8]
).

Theorem 1.9

(
7.9
)
.

Suppose
X
is a hyper-Kähler variety over
K
of dimension
2n
with
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
and
b_{3}(X)\neq 0
. Then we have

\nu(N_{p,2i+1})\leq 2i-1

for any
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
If
X
has Type III reduction, then the equality holds.

2.1.
Hyper-Kähler varieties

We first recall the definition and basic properties of hyper-Kähler varieties.

]

2.3.
Potentially semistable representations and
p
-adic monodromy operators

We recall Fontaine’s formalism of linear-algebraic data for potentially semistable representations.
Throughout this paper, we will use the following notation.
Let
K_{0}
be the fraction field of the ring of Witt vectors
W(k)
.
We may regard
K
as a finite totally ramified extension of
K_{0}
.
Let
K_{0}^{\ur}\subset\overline{K}
be the maximal unramified extension of
K_{0}
.
Let
B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}
,
B_{\text{\rm cris}}
and
B_{\st}
be Fontaine’s period rings of
K
defined in
[
Fon94
]
.

Remark 2.6
.

As usual, we fix a valuation on
\overline{K}
and an extended usual
p
-adic logarithm
\log\colon\overline{K}^{\times}\to\overline{K}
.
Following the constructions in

[
Fon94
]
, we have the corresponding

B_{\text{\rm cris}}
-derivation
N\colon B_{\st}\to B_{\st}
, and an embedding
B_{\st}\hookrightarrow B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}
over
B_{\text{\rm cris}}
.

Let
V
be a
p
-adic
G_{K}
-representation, that is, a finite-dimensional
{\mathds{Q}}_{p}
-vector space with a continuous action of
G_{K}
.
(Here
G_{K}\coloneqq\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}\nolimits(\overline{K}/K)
.)
As in
[
Fon94a
]
,
we define

\Dpst(V)\coloneqq\varinjlim_{L/K}(V\otimes_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}B_{\st})^{G_{L}},

where
L
runs over all finite extensions of
K
contained in
\overline{K}
.
Then

\Dpst(V)
is a finite-dimensional
K_{0}^{\ur}
-vector space
with
\dim_{K_{0}^{\ur}}\Dpst(V)\leq\dim_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}V
.
This is equipped with
the Frobenius map

\varphi\colon\Dpst(V)\to\Dpst(V)
,
the monodromy operator

N\colon\Dpst(V)\to\Dpst(V)
(which is a
K_{0}^{\ur}
-linear map such that
N\varphi=p\varphi N
), and the Hodge filtration

F^{\bullet}

(which is a separated and exhaustive decreasing filtration on
\Dpst(V)\otimes_{K_{0}^{\ur}}\overline{K}
).
We say that
V
is a

representation if
\dim_{K_{0}^{\ur}}\Dpst(V)=\dim_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}V
.
In this case, we have a natural isomorphism

\Dpst(V)\otimes_{K_{0}^{\ur}}B_{\st}\overset{\sim}{\to}V\otimes_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}B_{\st}

which is compatible with Frobenius maps and monodromy operators (in the usual sense).
If

is potentially semistable, it is
de Rham
(by
[
Fon94a
, Théorème in §5.6.7]
)
and we have

\Dpst(V)\otimes_{K_{0}^{\ur}}\overline{K}\overset{\sim}{\to}D_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}(V)\otimes_{K}\overline{K}

that is filtered.
Here
D_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}(V)\coloneqq(V\otimes_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}})^{G_{K}}
is endowed with the natural filtration from
B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}
.

Let
X
be a smooth proper scheme over
K
of dimension
d
.
Then
H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{i}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{p})
is potentially semistable by the validity of the
C_{\st}
-conjecture (see
[
Tsu99
, Theorem 0.2]
) and de Jong’s alteration
[
Jon96
, Theorem 6.5]
.
Moreover, the
j
-th successive quotient
F^{j}/F^{j+1}

of the Hodge filtration
F^{\bullet}
of
D_{\pst}(H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{i}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{p}))
is isomorphic to
H^{i-j}(X,\Omega^{j}_{X})\otimes_{K}\overline{K}
.
The monodromy operator
N
on
D_{\pst}(H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{i}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{p}))

will be denoted by
N_{p,i}
and called the
p
-adic monodromy operator
.

As in
2.5
, we have an upper bound of the nilpotency index of
N_{p,i}
.
In fact, in the
p
-adic case, the following slightly more precise statement holds.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
0\leq i\leq d
.
Let
j_{1}
(resp.
j_{2}
) denote the largest (resp. smallest) integer
j

such that

H^{i-j}(X,\Omega^{j}_{X})\neq 0
.
Then
\nu(N_{p,i})\leq j_{1}-j_{2}
.
In particular, we have
\nu(N_{p,i})\leq i
.

Proof.

We may assume that the residue field
k
is algebraically closed. In this case, the underlying
F
-isocrystal
D_{\pst}(H^{i}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{p}))
admits the slope decomposition
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathds{Q}}}D(\alpha)
by Dieudonné–Manin’s classification, where

D(\alpha)
has a single slope
\alpha
.
We have
j_{2}\leq\alpha\leq j_{1}
for
D(\alpha)\neq 0
, by
[
Fon94a
, Proposition 5.4.2, Remark 4.4.6]
.
The
p
-adic monodromy operator satisfies
N_{p,i}\left(D(\alpha)\right)\subset D(\alpha-1)
since
N_{p,i}\varphi=p\varphi N_{p,i}
, which implies
N^{j_{1}-j_{2}+1}_{p,i}=0
.
∎

2.4.
Representations of orthogonal Lie algebras

Here we recall some facts and fix the notation on representations of orthogonal Lie algebras.
Our main references are
[
FH91
]
and
[
Gre+22
, Appendix A]
.

Let
F
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
Let
V
be a non-degenerate quadratic space over
F
of rank
N+2
with
N>0
.
We consider the orthogonal Lie algebra

\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)
over
F
and fix a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)
.
Let us denote the non-trivial
\mathfrak{h}
-weights of the natural action of
\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)
on

V
by

\{\pm\epsilon_{0},\pm\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\pm\epsilon_{r}\}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}\setminus\{0\}

where
r=\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor
.
Then we consider the following positive system of roots
R^{+}
and the corresponding set of dominant integral weights
\Lambda^{+}
:

•

(Type
B_{r+1}
) If
N=2r+1
, then

R^{+}\coloneqq\{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}\}_{i<j}\cup\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\}_{i<j}\cup\{\epsilon_{i}\}_{i}

and the set of dominant integral weights is

(2.1)

\Lambda^{+}=\left\{\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\mu_{i}\epsilon_{i}\;\middle|\;\mu_{0}\geq\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{r}\geq 0,\,\mu_{i}\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathds{Z}},\,\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}\in{\mathds{Z}}\right\}.

•

(Type
D_{r+1}
) If
N=2r
, then
R^{+}\coloneqq\{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}\}_{i<j}\cup\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\}_{i<j}

and the set of dominant integral weights is

(2.2)

\Lambda^{+}\coloneqq\left\{\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\mu_{i}\epsilon_{i}\;\middle|\;\mu_{0}\geq\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{r-1}\geq|\mu_{r}|,\,\mu_{i}\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathds{Z}},\,\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}\in{\mathds{Z}}\right\}.

For a dominant integral weight
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}
,
let
V_{\mu}
denote the irreducible
\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)
-module with highest weight
\mu
.
We will identify
\Lambda^{+}
with the set

S

of
sequences

(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})

satisfying the conditions as in (
2.1
) or (
2.2
) depending on the parity of
N
.
Then
for a sequence

(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})\in S
,
we have the corresponding irreducible
\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)
-module

V_{\mu}=V_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})}.

Remark 2.8
.

We discuss how the construction

(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})\mapsto V_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})}

depends on the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)

and the choices of the numbering and the sign for
\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
.
Let

V^{\prime}_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})}

be the irreducible
\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)
-module corresponding to
a sequence

(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})\in S
with respect to another choice of
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
and
\{\pm\epsilon_{i}^{\prime}\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
.

•

If
N
is odd, then

V_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})}\simeq V^{\prime}_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})}

for any

(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})\in S
.

•

If
N
is even,
then
for any

(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})\in S
,
we have either

V_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})}\simeq V^{\prime}_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})}\quad\text{or}\quad V_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{r})}\simeq V^{\prime}_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,-\mu_{r})}.

To see this, since all Cartan subalgebras of
\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)
are conjugate under inner automorphisms,
we may assume that
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
.
If
N
is odd, the result follows from the fact that
\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
and
\{\pm\epsilon_{i}^{\prime}\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
are conjugate under the action of the Weyl group.
If
N
is even, then
\{\pm\epsilon_{i}^{\prime}\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
is conjugate to

\{\pm\epsilon_{0},\ldots,\pm\epsilon_{r-1},\pm\epsilon_{r}\}
or
\{\pm\epsilon_{0},\ldots,\pm\epsilon_{r-1},\mp\epsilon_{r}\}

under the action of the Weyl group, which will give the same positive system
R^{+}
.
These observations now lead to the result.

Remark 2.9
.

Let
F\subset F^{\prime}
be an extension of algebraically closed fields.
The construction

V_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r})}\mapsto V_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r})}\otimes_{F}F^{\prime}

induces a bijection between the set of irreducible representations of
\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)
over
F
and that of irreducible representations of
\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)_{F^{\prime}}
over
F^{\prime}
.
Indeed, by working with the base changes
of

\mathfrak{h}
and
\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq r}
to
F^{\prime}
,
we can parametrize irreducible representations of
\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)_{F^{\prime}}
over
F^{\prime}
by the same set
S
,
and
the irreducible representation of

\mathop{\mathfrak{so}}\nolimits(V)_{F^{\prime}}
corresponding to
\mu\in S

agrees with

V_{(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r})}\otimes_{F}F^{\prime}
.

3.
Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky Lie algebras of varieties over
p
-adic fields

In this section, we develop the theory of
the Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky Lie algebra (LLV Lie algebra) for
\ell
-adic and
p
-adic cohomology realizations.

3.1.
Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky algebras

Let
E
be a field of characteristic zero.
Let
d\geq 0
be an integer.
Let
A^{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{0\leq i\leq 2d}A^{i}
be a graded
E
-algebra such that

A^{\bullet}
is finite-dimensional as an
E
-vector space and
\dim_{E}A^{2d}=1
.
We assume that
A^{\bullet}
is a (graded)
Frobenius algebra
(of degree
2d
),
i.e., the bilinear form

A^{\bullet}\times A^{\bullet}\to A^{2d}\simeq E

defined by
(x,y)\mapsto(x\cdot y)_{2d}
is non-degenerate.

For any
x\in A^{2}
,
let
e_{x}\colon A^{\bullet}\to A^{\bullet+2}
be the map defined by

a\mapsto x\cdot a
.
An element
x\in A^{2}
satisfies the
Hard Lefschetz (HL) property
if

(e_{x})^{i}\colon A^{d-i}\to A^{d+i}

is bijective for any integer
0\leq i\leq d
. On the other hand, we consider the shifted degree map

h\colon A^{\bullet}\to A^{\bullet}\quad\text{such that}\quad h|_{A^{i}}=(i-d)\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits_{A^{i}}

for all
i
. By the Jacobson–Morozov theorem,
x\in A^{2}
satisfies the HL property if and only if
(e_{x},h)
extends to an
\mathop{\mathfrak{sl}}\nolimits_{2}
-triple, i.e., there is a map
f_{x}\in\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{E}(A^{\bullet})
such that

[e_{x},h]=-2e_{x},\quad[f_{x},h]=2f_{x},\quad[e_{x},f_{x}]=h.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{A}\subset A^{2}
be the set of all elements satisfying the HL property, which is a Zariski open subset.
We say that
A^{\bullet}
is
Lefschetz–Frobenius
if
\mathfrak{a}_{A}\neq 0
.
In this case, we define the
total Lie algebra
of
A^{\bullet}
as the Lie subalgebra

\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet})\subset\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{E}(A^{\bullet})

generated by all
\mathop{\mathfrak{sl}}\nolimits_{2}
-triples
(e_{x},h,f_{x})
for all
x\in\mathfrak{a}_{A}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\gamma\colon A^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\sim}B^{\bullet}
be an isomorphism of Lefschetz–Frobenius
E
-algebras.
The isomorphism

\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{E}(A^{\bullet})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{E}(B^{\bullet})

induced by
\gamma

maps
\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet})
isomorphically onto
\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(B^{\bullet})
.

Proof.

This is clear from the definition.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
E\subset F
be an inclusion of fields of characteristic zero.
Let
A^{\bullet}
be a Lefschetz–Frobenius
E
-algebra
and
A^{\bullet}_{F}\coloneqq A^{\bullet}\otimes_{E}F
.
Then

\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet})\otimes_{E}F=\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A_{F}^{\bullet})

as Lie subalgebras of
\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{E}(A^{\bullet})\otimes_{E}F=\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{F}(A^{\bullet}_{F})
.

Proof.

Since
E
is an infinite field,
we see that

A^{2}\subset A^{2}_{F}
is Zariski dense.
Thus

\mathfrak{a}_{A}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{A_{F}}
is also Zariski dense.
Since

\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet})\otimes_{E}F

is Zariski closed in
\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{F}(A^{\bullet}_{F})
, it follows that
\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet})\otimes_{E}F

contains
e_{x}
and
f_{x}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{a}_{A_{F}}
. This implies the assertion.
∎

Let
L
be a field of characteristic zero.
Let
H^{\bullet}(-)
be a Weil cohomology theory with coefficients in
E

for smooth projective varieties
X
over
L
, such that the hard Lefschetz theorem holds. Then, for any
X
of dimension
d
, the cohomology algebra
H^{\bullet}(X)
naturally forms a Lefschetz–Frobenius algebra of degree
2d
.
The
LLV Lie algebra
of
X
with respect to the cohomology theory
H^{\bullet}(-)
is the total Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(H^{\bullet}(X))
.
Let

\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(H^{\bullet}(X))\to\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{E}(H^{\bullet}(X))

denote the standard Lie algebra representation.

Example 3.3
.

Assume that
L={\mathds{C}}
.
We denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{B}(X)
the LLV Lie algebra with respect to the Betti realization

H^{\bullet}_{B}(X)=H^{\bullet}(X({\mathds{C}}),{\mathds{Q}})
.

Example 3.4
.

The LLV Lie algebra of
X
with respect to the
\ell
-adic étale cohomology

H^{\bullet}_{\ell}(X)=H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{\bullet}(X_{\overline{L}},{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell})
is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}(X)
.
There is a natural continuous
G_{L}
-action on
\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}(X)
.
For an embedding
\overline{L}\hookrightarrow{\mathds{C}}
, we have a natural identification of
{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell}
-Lie algebras

(3.1)

\mathfrak{g}_{B}(X\otimes_{L}{\mathds{C}})\otimes_{{\mathds{Q}}}{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell}\simeq\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}(X).

by
3.2
.

Let the notation be as in Section
2.3
.
Let
A^{\bullet}
be a Lefschetz–Frobenius algebra over
{\mathds{Q}}_{p}
with a continuous
G_{K}
-action that is compatible its algebra structure.
Assume that
A^{\bullet}
is potentially semistable.
Since
\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet})
is a
G_{K}
-subrepresentation of
\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}(A^{\bullet})
, we obtain
a Lie subalgebra

\Dpst(\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet}))\subset\Dpst(\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}(A^{\bullet}))=\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{K_{0}^{\ur}}(\Dpst(A^{\bullet})).

On the other hand

\Dpst(A^{\bullet})=\bigoplus_{i}\Dpst(A^{i})

and thus we have the total Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(\Dpst(A^{\bullet}))\subset\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{K_{0}^{\ur}}(\Dpst(A^{\bullet}))

of
\Dpst(A^{\bullet})
.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
A^{\bullet}
is potentially semistable, then we have an identification

\Dpst(\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet}))=\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(\Dpst(A^{\bullet}))

as Lie subalgebras of
\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{K_{0}^{\ur}}(\Dpst(A^{\bullet}))
.

Proof.

We have the inclusions

K_{0}^{\ur}\subset\overline{K}\subset B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}

of fields.
It suffices to prove the equality after tensoring with
B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}
.
Since
\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}(A^{\bullet})
is potentially semistable, so is
\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet})
.
Thus we have the following identifications

\Dpst(\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet}))\otimes_{K_{0}^{\ur}}B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}=\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet})\otimes_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}=\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet}\otimes_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}})

where the second equality follows from Proposition
3.2
.
On the other hand,
we have

\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(\Dpst(A^{\bullet}))\otimes_{K_{0}^{\ur}}B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}=\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(\Dpst(A^{\bullet})\otimes_{K_{0}^{\ur}}B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}})=\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(A^{\bullet}\otimes_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}})

by Proposition
3.2
again.
The assertion follows from these equalities.
∎

Example 3.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
K
.
We
define

\mathfrak{g}_{\pst}(X)\coloneqq\Dpst(\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{p}(X))=\mathfrak{g}^{\tot}(\Dpst(H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{\bullet}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{p}))),

which is a Lie algebra over

with a Frobenius map, a monodromy operator, and a filtration (on
\mathfrak{g}_{\pst}(X)\otimes_{K_{0}^{\ur}}\overline{K}
).
We have a natural isomorphism
of Lie algebras over
B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}

(3.2)

\mathfrak{g}_{\pst}(X)\otimes_{K_{0}^{\ur}}B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}\simeq\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{p}(X)\otimes_{{\mathds{Q}}_{p}}B_{\mathop{\mathrm{dR}}}.

3.2.
LLV Lie algebras of hyper-Kähler varieties

Here we record some important features of the LLV Lie algebras of hyper-Kähler varieties.

Set-up 3.7
.

Let us consider one of the following realization functors:

(1)

Let
X
be a
2n
-dimensional hyper-Kähler variety over
{\mathds{C}}
.
We set

H^{\bullet}(X)\coloneqq H^{\bullet}_{B}(X)=H^{\bullet}(X({\mathds{C}}),{\mathds{Q}})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits(X)\coloneqq\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{B}(X)

(see Example
3.3
).
Let
E\coloneqq{\mathds{Q}}
and
F\coloneqq{\mathds{C}}
.

(2)

Let
X
be a
2n
-dimensional hyper-Kähler variety over a field
L
of characteristic zero.
We set

H^{\bullet}(X)\coloneqq H^{\bullet}_{\ell}(X)=H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{L}},{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits(X)\coloneqq\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\ell}(X)

(see Example
3.4
).
Let
E\coloneqq{\mathds{Q}}_{\ell}
and
F\coloneqq\overline{{\mathds{Q}}}_{\ell}
.

(3)

Let
X
be a
2n
-dimensional hyper-Kähler variety over
K
.
We abuse notation slightly and write

H^{\bullet}(X)\coloneqq H^{\bullet}_{\pst}(X)\coloneqq\Dpst(H_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{\bullet}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{p}))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits(X)\coloneqq\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\pst}(X)

(see Example
3.6
).
Let
E\coloneqq K^{\ur}_{0}
and
F\coloneqq\overline{K}
.
Note that, the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki form

on
H^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\overline{K}},{\mathds{Q}}_{p})

We also write this form as

In all cases,

\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits(X)\subset\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits_{E}(H^{\bullet}(X))

.

Remark 3.8
.

Strictly speaking,
the BBF form

should be defined as a quadratic form on

H^{2}(X)(1)\coloneqq H^{2}(X)\otimes_{E}E(1)

where

is the Tate twist, though one can choose an isomorphism

so that we can regard

as a quadratic form on

The resulting quadratic form on

We can describe the LLV Lie algebra

using the quadratic space

\widetilde{H}^{2}(X)\coloneqq H^{2}(X)\oplus U,

where
U=Ev\oplus Ew

.
The quadratic space

\widetilde{H}^{2}(X)

is called the

of

H^{2}(X)
.

Remark 3.9
.

We endow
\widetilde{H}^{2}(X)

with the structure of a graded algebra

over
E

with grading given by

A^{0}=Ev=E,\quad A^{2}=H^{2}(X),\quad A^{4}=Ew=E

and with multiplication determined by

x\cdot y=q(x,y)w

for all

.
Then

is a Lefschetz–Frobenius

By

the total Lie algebra of

is

.

Theorem 3.10
.

In

(a), (b) or (c), the following assertions hold.

(1)

The LLV Lie algebra
\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits(X)

(2)

.

\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits(X)_{2}
(resp.
\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits(X)_{-2}

(resp.

(3)

Then

The isomorphism

of Lie algebras over

with the following properties:

•

and we have

and

.

•

For any

,
we have

,
\psi(e_{x})(w)=0
, and

.

Proof.

For the Betti realization
\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{B}(X)

[
Ver95
]
,

[
LL97
]
,

[
Gre+22
, Subsection 2.1.1]
.
See also
[
Flo22
, Theorem 2.2]
.

We shall prove (1)–(3) for realizations
\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\ell}(X)
and
\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\pst}(X)
.
For
\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\ell}(X)

such that

.
Then the assertions follow from those for
\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{B}(X_{\mathds{C}})
and (

).
For
\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{\pst}(X)
,

these assertions follow from those for
\mathop{\mathfrak{g}}\nolimits_{p}(X)
and (

).

We shall prove (4).
First, the uniqueness of
\psi

We recall the Kuga–Satake construction for hyper-Kähler varieties.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathds{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}

.

Let

on

by its finite extension, we can consider the Kuga–Satake abelian variety

over

.
We keep the notations there.

As in

,
for

7.3.
Nilpotency indices in odd degrees

In this part, we want to make further remarks on the nilpotency indices of the monodromy operators on cohomology in odd degrees and their relation to those on the second cohomology.
In the four known deformation types (

⋆

‣
2.2
),
\mathop{\mathrm{Kum}}\nolimits_{n}(n\geq 2)
is the sole example that has non-trivial odd cohomology groups (see
2.1
).

In
[
Sol20
, Proposition 3.15]
and
[
HM22
, Proposition 3.12]
, it is shown that, for a Type III projective one-parameter degeneration
\mathcal{X}/\Delta
of a hyper-Kähler manifold

of dimension

with

\nu(N_{2i+1})=2i-1

Let

.
Similarly, we obtain the following description of monodromy nilpotency indices for

Theorem 7.9
.

Suppose that

.

(1)

We have
\nu(N_{p,2i+1})\leq 2i-1

for all
1\leq i\leq n-1

(2)

\nu(N_{p,2i+1})=2i-1

for all

(cf.
[
Bea83
, Proposition 3]

(1) follows from
2.7
.
Note that we have the normalized basis for

5.6
.
Then the claim (2) follows from the same computation as in
[
Sol20
, Proposition 3.15]

∎

Corollary 7.10
.

Suppose that
b_{2}(X)\geq 4
and
b_{3}(X)\neq 0

We have

\nu(N_{p,3})=0

\nu(N_{p,3})=1

Proof.

This follows by combining

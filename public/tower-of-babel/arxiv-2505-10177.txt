Korevaar-Schoen and heat kernel characterizations of Sobolev and BV spaces on local trees
Fabrice Baudoin, Li Chen, Meng Yang

Abstract
We study Sobolev and BV spaces on local trees which are metric spaces locally isometric to real trees. Such spaces are equipped with a Radon measure satisfying a locally uniform volume growth condition. Using the intrinsic geodesic structure, we define weak gradients and develop from it a coherent theory of Sobolev and BV spaces. We provide two main characterizations: one via Korevaar–Schoen type energy functionals and another via the heat kernel associated with the natural Dirichlet form. Applications include interpolation results for Besov–Lipschitz spaces, critical exponent computations, and a Nash inequality. In globally tree-like settings, we also establish L p L^{p} gradient bounds for the heat semigroup.

Introduction

The study of analysis on metric measure spaces has become a central theme in modern analysis, driven by the need to extend classical calculus and partial differential equations (PDEs) beyond the smooth setting of Euclidean spaces or Riemannian manifolds. Fundamental objects in this pursuit are Besov or Sobolev spaces and spaces of functions of bounded variation, which provide the natural framework for variational problems, geometric measure theory, and the analysis of PDEs in non-smooth environments. Defining and characterizing these spaces on general metric measure spaces
(X,d,m)
presents significant challenges due to the lack of readily available differential structures. Various approaches have been developed, including Hajłasz-Sobolev spaces
[
20
]
, Newtonian spaces based on upper gradients
[
21
]
, and definitions via heat semigroup regularisation
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, Korevaar-Schoen type energy functionals
[
8
,
22
]
or
p
-energy forms
[
23
,
29
,
30
]
.

In this paper, we focus on a specific, yet rich, class of metric measure spaces known as local trees. A metric space
(X,d)
is a local tree if it is locally isometric to a real tree (Definition 2.2). Real trees themselves are characterized by the existence of unique geodesics between any two points and the absence of loops (Definition 2.1). The class of local trees encompasses a diverse range of structures, including classical trees, metric graphs or cable systems (Example 2.4), and certain fractal-like spaces such as the Sierpiński carpet cable system (Figure 1) or modifications of the Vicsek set (Figure 4, Example 2.5). These spaces, while possessing a clear local 1-dimensional structure along their ”branches” or ”edges”, can exhibit complex global topology and singularities, such as Hausdorff dimensions greater than 1.

The analysis on such spaces requires some basic assumptions. We consider a local tree
(X,d)
equipped with a Radon measure
m
that satisfies a uniform volume control condition:
m(B(x,r))\approx\Phi(r)
for some doubling function
\Phi
and small
r
. Crucial to our analysis, the notion of gradient is tied to the intrinsic 1-dimensional structure. Functions are defined to be absolutely continuous (AC) if their variation along local geodesics can be represented by integration against a weak gradient
\partial f
(Definition 2.7). This weak gradient naturally lives on the ”skeleton”
\mathcal{S}
of the space (the union of all non-trivial geodesic open segments) and is measured with respect to the natural length measure
\nu
(Section 2.1). The Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(X,m)
then consists of functions
f\in L^{p}(X,m)
whose weak gradient
\partial f
belongs to
L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)
(Definition 2.13). The space
BV(X,m)
is defined via
L^{1}
-relaxation of the 1-energy
\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|\,d\nu
(Definition 2.17). Notably, in this general framework the reference measure
m
and the length measure
\nu
can be mutually singular (e.g.,
m(\mathcal{S})=0
is possible).

The central aim of this work is to provide robust characterizations of these intrinsically defined Sobolev
W^{1,p}
and BV spaces on local trees satisfying uniform local volume control and a uniform local tree property (meaning the local isometry to a tree holds uniformly up to a certain scale). Such characterizations are valuable as they connect the intrinsic definition to other analytical viewpoints, offer alternative (sometimes more practical) ways to estimate norms, and facilitate the transfer of techniques from different areas of analysis. We establish two main types of characterizations: one based on Korevaar-Schoen (KS) type energy functionals and another based on heat kernel estimates.

The first main contribution is the Korevaar-Schoen type characterization. Originating from the work of Korevaar and Schoen
[
25
]
in the context of harmonic maps, this approach characterizes smoothness or regularity by measuring the averaged
p
-th power of function differences at small scales. We define specific KS energy functionals

E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,r)=\frac{1}{\Psi_{p}(r)}\int_{X}\frac{1}{m(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}dm(y)dm(x)

where
\Psi_{p}(r)=r^{p-1}\Phi(r)
is related to the volume growth (Section 3.3). We prove that for
p>1
, the
W^{1,p}(X,m)
norm is equivalent to the Korevaar-Schoen norm (Theorem 3.7). A corresponding result holds for
p=1
, characterizing the BV seminorm
||\partial f||(X)
in terms of
E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f,r)
(Theorem 3.8). Establishing these equivalences requires significant technical work; in particular, we construct controlled partitions of unity adapted to the local tree structure and the volume measure (Lemma 3.3, Theorem 3.5), which are of independent interest. Along the way we establish the weak monotonicity property of the KS functional:

\sup_{r\in(0,r_{0}]}E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,r)\leq C\liminf_{r\to 0}E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,r).

It is remarkable that such weak monotonicity property holds assuming only the uniform volume control at small scale since this usually requires
p
-capacity estimates type assumptions, see
[
8
,
29
,
31
]
. This means that in our setting of local trees those capacity type estimates automatically follow from the uniform volume assumption, a situation which is similar to the smooth Riemannian setting. The key lemma illustrating this phenomenon is Lemma 3.3 which actually only relies on the space being a uniformly locally doubling local tree.

The second main contribution lies in the heat kernel characterization. This approach connects the function spaces to the behavior of the heat semigroup
e^{t\Delta}
associated with the natural energy form
\mathcal{E}_{2}(f)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|^{2}\,d\nu
. Following
[
26
]
, we first establish detailed estimates for the corresponding heat kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
. We derive two-sided sub-Gaussian-type bounds reflecting the metric
d
and the volume growth
\Phi
, specifically relating the time scale
t
to a length scale via
r=\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)
where
\Psi_{2}(r)=r\Phi(r)
(Theorem 4.3, 4.5, 4.7, Section 4.1). These estimates include upper bounds, near-diagonal lower bounds, and escape rate estimates. With these kernel estimates in hands, we introduce the heat kernel based norm and prove that the
W^{1,p}
(for
p>1
) and BV (for
p=1
) norms are equivalent to the heat kernel based norms (Theorem 4.8). This result places the analysis on local trees within the broader framework of heat kernel based function space theory developed in recent years
[
3
,
1
,
2
]
.

Furthermore, we explore applications of these characterizations. We use the KS characterization to determine critical exponents for Besov-Lipschitz spaces
B^{\alpha,p}(X,m)
on these local trees and establish real interpolation results, showing that these Besov spaces form an interpolation scale between
L^{p}
and
W^{1,p}
(or BV for
p=1
) (Theorem 3.11). We also derive a Nash inequality adapted to the volume growth (Theorem 3.12). Finally, we discuss how these results can be refined in the specific case where
(X,d)
is a global tree with global volume estimates (Section 5). In that global tree case, we also establish
L^{p}
gradient estimates for the heat semigroup.

We structure the paper as follows. Section 2 introduces local trees and defines the relevant function spaces (AC,
W^{1,p}
, BV) and energy forms. Section 3 develops the Korevaar-Schoen type characterization, including the construction of partitions of unity and applications to interpolation and Nash inequalities. Section 4 focuses on the heat kernel approach, establishing the necessary kernel estimates and proving the heat kernel based characterization of
W^{1,p}
and BV spaces. Section 5 discusses extensions and refinements specific to the global tree setting.

2.2
Weak gradients

The notion of weak gradient is crucial in our analysis. In the framework of trees it appears in
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
f\in C(X)
and
x\in X
. We say that
f
is absolutely continuous in
B(x,\iota(x))
if there exists a function
g\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathcal{S}\cap B(x,\iota(x)),\nu)
such that for every
u\in B(x,\iota(x))

f(u)-f(x)=\int_{]x,u[}g\,d\nu.

(1)

If it exists, the function
g
is unique (up to a
\nu
null set) and is called the weak gradient of
f
in
B(x,\iota(x))
. We will denote
g=\partial_{x}f
.

Note that if
f
is absolutely continuous in
B(x,\iota(x))
then for every
u,v\in B(x,\iota(x))
with
v\in[x,u]

f(v)-f(u)=\int_{]u,v[}\partial_{x}f\,d\nu.

(2)

Indeed, one has

f(v)-f(x)=\int_{]x,v[}\partial_{x}f\,d\nu,\quad f(u)-f(x)=\int_{]x,u[}\partial_{x}f\,d\nu,

so that

f(v)-f(u)=\int_{]x,v[}\partial_{x}f\,d\nu-\int_{]x,u[}\partial_{x}f\,d\nu=\int_{]u,v[}\partial_{x}f\,d\nu.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
f\in C(X)
. We say that
f
is absolutely continuous and write
f\in AC(X)
if it is absolutely continuous in
B(x,\iota(x))
for all
x\in X
.

If
f\in AC(X)
is absolutely continuous and
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y
then it is clear that
|\partial_{x}f|=|\partial_{y}f|
holds
\nu
-a.e.
on
\mathcal{S}\cap B(x,\iota(x))\cap B(y,\iota(y))
. We will therefore simply use the notation
|\partial f|
. Similarly, if
f,g\in AC(X)
then
\partial_{x}f\partial_{x}g
does not depend on
x
and we will denote
\partial f\partial g
. We also observe the following:

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
f\in AC(X)
and
x\in X
, then for every
u,v\in B(x,\iota(x))

\displaystyle|f(v)-f(u)|\leq\int_{]u,v[}|\partial f|\,d\nu.

(3)

Proof.

From the previous remark one has,

f(u)-f(c(u,v,x))=\int_{]c(u,v,x),u[}\partial_{x}fd\nu,\quad f(v)-f(c(u,v,x))=\int_{]c(u,v,x),v[}\partial_{x}fd\nu.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|f(v)-f(u)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{]c(u,v,x),v[}\partial_{x}fd\nu-\int_{]c(u,v,x),u[}\partial_{x}fd\nu\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{]c(u,v,x),v[}|\partial f|d\nu+\int_{]c(u,v,x),u[}|\partial_{x}f|d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{]u,v[}|\partial f|\,d\nu.

∎

The following lemma is easy to prove due to the corresponding properties that hold for absolutely continuous functions on the line.

Lemma 2.10

(Chain and Leibniz rules)
.

(i)

Let

and let
\Phi
be a

function on

. Then,
\Phi(f)\in AC(X)
and
\nu

|\partial\Phi(f)|=|\Phi^{\prime}(f)|\,|\partial f|.

(ii)

Let

. Then

and
\nu

|\partial(fg)|\leq|f|\,|\partial g|+|g|\,|\partial f|.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. For
f\in C(X)
, we say that

if it admits a weak gradient
\partial f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)
.

Functions in

Theorem 2.12

(Local Morrey type estimate)
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
x_{0}\in X
.

(i)

If

and

|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}\leq d(x,y)^{p-1}\int_{]x,y[}|\partial f|^{p}d\nu\leq d(x,y)^{p-1}\|\partial f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}.

(ii)

If

, then for every
f\in AC^{\infty}(X)
and

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq d(x,y)\|\partial f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}.

Therefore for

Proof.

We note that it follows from Lemma

2.3
Sobolev, BV spaces and
p
-energy forms

We now consider a Radon measure
m
on
(X,d)
with full support. Typically, in many situations

and
\nu
are singular with respect to each other and we even have

; see the Vicsek set Example
2.5
for which we take for

the Hausdorff measure.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. For
f\in L^{p}(X,m)
we say that
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
if there exists a
m
-representative
\tilde{f}
of

such that
\tilde{f}\in AC^{p}(X)
. In that case, we will denote
|\partial f|:=|\partial\tilde{f}|
. The norm on
W^{1,p}(X,m)
is defined as

\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}+\|\partial f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}.

Since for
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
there exists a (necessarily unique)
m
-representative which is continuous, we will often simply work with this representative without further notice so that
f(x)
is well defined for a given
x\in X
. We first collect some basic properties that will be important in the sequel.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. The space
(W^{1,p}(X,m),\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(X,m)})
is a Banach space.

Proof.

Let
f_{n}
be a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(X,m)
. Since
f_{n}
is then also a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(X,m)
we have
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{p}(X,m)
where
f\in L^{p}(X,m)
. Let now
x_{0}\in X
. Since
\partial f_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(\mathcal{S}\cap B(x_{0},\iota(x_{0})),\nu)
we have that
\partial f_{n}
converges to some
g
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{S}\cap B(x_{0},\iota(x_{0})),\nu)
. We can extract from
f_{n}
a subsequence
f_{n_{k}}
that converges
m
-a.e. to
f
. One has for every
x,y\in B(x_{0},\iota(x_{0}))
with
x\in[x_{0},y]
,

f_{n}(y)-f_{n}(x)=\int_{]x,y[}\partial f_{n}d\nu.

Taking limit along the subsequence yields that for
m
-almost every
x,y\in B(x_{0},\iota(x_{0}))
with
x\in[x_{0},y]
,

f(y)-f(x)=\int_{]x,y[}gd\nu

For any
x\in X
, the function
y\to\int_{]x,y[}gd\nu
is continuous. Therefore,
f
admits a continuous
m
-representative
\tilde{f}
that satisfies for every
x,y\in B(x_{0},\iota(x_{0}))
with
x\in[x_{0},y]
,

\tilde{f}(y)-\tilde{f}(x)=\int_{]x,y[}gd\nu.

Therefore
\tilde{f}\in AC^{p}(X,m)
and
g=\partial\tilde{f}
. If
p<\infty
, then from Fatou’s lemma we have
g\in L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)
, which implies
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
. If
p=\infty
, then we have
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}\leq\sup_{n}\|\partial f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}
which implies
f\in W^{1,\infty}(X,m)
.

It remains to prove that
f_{n}\to f
in
W^{1,p}(X,m)
. We assume
p<\infty
, the case
p=\infty
actually being simpler. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
f_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(X,m)
, one has for
n,m\geq N
,
\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{n}-\partial f_{m}|^{p}d\nu\leq\varepsilon
, where
N
is large enough. Letting

along a subsequence yields by Fatou’s lemma
\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f-\partial f_{m}|^{p}d\nu\leq\varepsilon
and the conclusion follows.
∎

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
1<p<+\infty
. The Banach space
(W^{1,p}(X,m),\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(X,m)})
is reflexive and separable.

Proof.

To prove reflexivity, we prove that
(W^{1,p}(X,m),|||\cdot|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)})
is uniformly convex, where
|||\cdot|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}
is the equivalent norm defined by

|||f|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}=\left(\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(X,m)}+\|\partial f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}\right)^{1/p}

Actually, we easily get the following Clarkson inequalities which imply uniform convexity. Let
f,g\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
,
1<p<\infty
, and
q
be the Hölder conjugate of
p
. If
2\leq p<\infty
, then

|||(f+g)/2|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}^{p}+|||(f-g)/2|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}^{p}\leq|||f|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}^{p}/2+|||g|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}^{p}/2.

(4)

If
1<p\leq 2
, then

|||(f+g)/2|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}^{q}+|||(f-g)/2|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}^{q}\leq\left(|||f|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}^{p}/2+|||g|||_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}^{p}/2\right)^{q-1}.

(5)

It remains to prove separability. The identity map
\iota:(W^{1,p}(X,m),\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}\to(L^{p}(X,m),\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(X,m)})
is a linear and bounded injective map. Since the space
(W^{1,p}(X,m),\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}
is reflexive and
L^{p}(X,m)
is separable because
X
is, it now follows from Proposition 4.1 in
[
4
]
that
(W^{1,p}(X,m),\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(X,m)})
is separable.
∎

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
p>1
. The
p
-energy form

\mathcal{E}_{p}(f):=\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|^{p}d\nu

with domain
W^{1,p}(X,m)
is lower semi-continuous in
L^{p}(X,m)
.

Proof.

Let
f_{n}
be a sequence in
W^{1,p}(X,m)
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{p}(X,m)
and
\mathcal{E}_{p}(f_{n})
is convergent. Since the Banach space
(W^{1,p}(X,m),\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(X,m)})
is reflexive one can extract a subsequence such that
f_{n_{k}}
is weakly convergent to some
g\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
. Mazur’s lemma implies that a convex combination of the
f_{n_{k}}
’s converges in
W^{1,p}(X,m)
to
g
. Since
f_{n_{k}}
converges in
L^{p}(X,m)
to
f
one must have
g=f
. Therefore,
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
. Moreover, by convexity of the functional
\mathcal{E}_{p}
one has

\mathcal{E}_{p}(f)\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{p}(f_{n}).

∎

The
1
-energy is not lower semicontinuous in general. To get a satisfying theory one can use
L^{1}
-relaxation of the
1
-energy as in
[
27
]
. This leads to the notion of bounded variation function (BV).

Definition 2.17
.

The space of bounded variation functions is defined by
L^{1}
relaxation:

BV(X,m)=\left\{f\in L^{1}(X,m)\mid\|\partial f\|(X)<+\infty\right\}

where

\|\partial f\|(X)=\inf_{f_{n}}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{n}|d\nu<+\infty

and the
\inf
is taken over the set of functions
f_{n}\in W^{1,1}(X,m)
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{1}(X,m)
. In other words
\|\partial f\|(X)
is the lower semi-continuous envelope in
L^{1}(X,m)
of the functional
\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|d\nu
. The norm on
BV(X)
is defined by

\|f\|_{BV(X)}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(X,m)}+\|\partial f\|(X).

Lemma 2.18
.

We have
W^{1,1}(X,m)\subset BV(X,m)
and for
f\in W^{1,1}(X,m)
,

\|\partial f\|(X)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|d\nu.

Proof.

It is plain that
W^{1,1}(X,m)\subset BV(X,m)
and that for
f\in W^{1,1}(X,m)
,

\|\partial f\|(X)\leq\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|d\nu.

Consider now
f\in W^{1,1}(X,m)
and
f_{n}\in W^{1,1}(X,m)
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{1}(X,m)
and
\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{n}|d\nu\to C
with
C>0
, then from Helly’s selection theorem (see for instance
[
10
, Exercise 8.3]
) one can extract a subsequence
f_{n_{k}}
that pointwisely converges to
f
. Let
x\in X
. We have then for every
u,v\in B(x,\iota(x))
,

|f_{n}(u)-f_{n}(v)|\leq\int_{]u,v[}|\partial f_{n}|d\nu

which yields

|f(u)-f(v)|\leq\liminf\int_{]u,v[}|\partial f_{n_{k}}|d\nu.

Let now
\mathcal{A}
be any finite union of sets of the type
]u,v[
with
d(u,v)<\iota(u)
. Partitioning
\mathcal{A}
into subsets of the type
]u_{i},u_{i+1}[
or
[u_{i},u_{i+1}[
or
]u_{i},u_{i+1}]
, summing up the inequalities

|f(u_{i+1})-f(u_{i})|\leq\liminf\int_{]u_{i},u_{i+1}[}|\partial f_{n_{k}}|d\nu.

and taking the supremum over such partitions yields

\int_{\mathcal{A}}|\partial f|d\nu\leq\liminf\int_{\mathcal{A}}|\partial f_{n_{k}}|d\nu\leq C.

This gives

\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|d\nu\leq C

and therefore

\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|d\nu\leq\|\partial f\|(X).

∎

We will see in next section that under some appropriate assumptions
BV(X,m)
is a Banach space, see Corollary
3.9
. We note that, in general, functions in
BV(X,m)
are not continuous; for instance if
X=\mathbb{R}
and
m=\nu=\textrm{Lebesgue measure}
, then
1_{[0,1]}\in BV(X,m)
. More generally, assume that
X
is a cable system as in Example
2.4
. Let
a,b\in X
with
d(a,b)<\min(\iota(a),\iota(b))
. Assume that
N_{a}
(resp.
N_{b}
), the number of connected components of
B(a,d(a,b))\setminus\{a\}
(resp.
B(b,d(a,b))\setminus\{b\}
), is finite, then
1_{[a,b]}\in BV(X)
and

\|\partial 1_{[a,b]}(X)\|\leq N_{a}+N_{b}-2.

Indeed, in that case it is easy to check that the sequence

f_{n}(x)=\left(1-nd(x,c(a,b,x))\right)_{+}

converges to
1_{[a,b]}
in
L^{1}(X,m)
and that
\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{n}|d\nu\leq N_{a}+N_{b}-2
.

3
Korevaar-Schoen type characterizations of the Sobolev and BV spaces and applications

We first introduce the two main assumptions we will be working with in this section. Let
r_{0}>0
and
\Phi:[0,r_{0}]\to[0,+\infty)
be an increasing function such that there exist
\alpha\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle c\frac{R}{r}\leq\frac{\Phi(R)}{\Phi(r)}\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\alpha}\text{ for any }r\leq R\leq r_{0}.

(6)

We make the assumption of
uniform volume control
:

There exists
r_{0}>0
such that for every
x\in X
and
0<r<r_{0}

c\Phi(r)\leq m(B(x,r))\leq C\Phi(r).

In particular, the measure
m
is uniformly locally doubling, i.e. for every
x\in X
and
0<r<r_{0}

m(B(x,2r))\leq Cm(B(x,r)),

and as a consequence, see
[
19
, Lemma B.3.4.]
, the metric space
(X,d)
is uniformly locally doubling, which means: There exist
R>0
and
N=N_{R}
such that for every
x\in X,0<r<R
, any ball
B(x,r)
can be covered by at most
N
balls of radius
r/2
where
N
is independent of
x
and
r
.

The second main assumption of this section is the following:

The metric space
(X,d)
is a
uniform local tree
meaning that there exists
0<\underline{\iota}\leq\mathrm{diam}(X)
such that for every
x\in X
,
B(x,\underline{\iota})
is a real tree.

Example 3.1
.

Let
(\mathbf{G},d)
be a cable system as in Example
2.4
. Assume such that for some
N\geq 1
and
a>0
:

(i)

Any vertex has at most
N
neighboring vertices;

(ii)

Any edge has length more than
a>0
;

Then
(\mathbf{G},d,m)
satisfies the assumptions with
m
being the length measure. In that case, one can take
r_{0}=a
,
\Phi(r)=r
and
\underline{\iota}=a
.

Example 3.2
.

[Vicsek sets]
The Vicsek set
(V,d,m)
from Example
3
satisfies the assumptions with
m
being the Hausdorff measure. In that case one can take
r_{0}=\mathrm{diam}(V)
,
\Phi(r)=r^{\frac{\ln 5}{\ln 3}}
and
\underline{\iota}=\mathrm{diam}(V)
. More generally the scale irregular Vicsek sets introduced in
[

]
satisfy the assumptions. Interestingly, for those scale irregular Vicsek sets, the function
\Phi
has several scales, see
[
11
, Proposition 2.9]
.

3.1
Controlled partitions of unity

Our key lemma is the following.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be small enough. Let
x\in X
and
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. There exists an absolutely continuous function
\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}\in AC(X)
such that:

(i)

\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}(x)=1
;

(ii)

0\leq\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}\leq 1
;

(iii)

If
d(y,x)\leq\varepsilon/2
then
\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}(y)\geq 1/2
;

(iv)

If
d(y,x)\geq 2\varepsilon
then
\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}(y)=0
;

(v)

For
\nu
-a.e.
y\in\mathcal{S}
,
|\partial\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}(y)|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
and if
d(x,y)\geq\varepsilon
,
|\partial\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}(y)|=0
;

(vi)

\nu\left(\mathrm{supp}(\partial\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x})\right)\leq C\varepsilon
where the constant
C>0
is independent from
x
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be small enough. For
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, consider the ball
B(x,3\varepsilon)
which is therefore a tree since
3\varepsilon<\underline{\iota}
. Denote
R_{\varepsilon}=\{u\in B(x,3\varepsilon)\mid d(x,u)=\varepsilon\}
. For
u\in R_{\varepsilon}
, the set
B(x,3\varepsilon)\setminus\{u\}
has a least two connected components, and only one of them contains
x
, we denote this component by
C^{0}_{u}
and the other ones by
C^{1}_{u},C^{2}_{u},\cdots
. We consider then the set

S_{\varepsilon}=\left\{u\in R_{\varepsilon}\mid\,\exists\,i\geq 1,\mathrm{diam}(C^{i}_{u})\geq\varepsilon\right\}.

Now, recall that as a consequence of the measure being locally doubling, the metric space
(X,d)
is a uniformly locally doubling metric space. Therefore
B(x,3\varepsilon)
can be always covered by at most
N
balls of radius
\varepsilon/2
where
N
is independent of
x
and
\varepsilon
. In particular, since the
C^{i}_{u}
’s,
u\in R_{\varepsilon}
, are pairwise disjoint, we deduce that the number of elements in
S_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded above by a constant which is independent from
x
and
\varepsilon
.
We now define the function
\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}
as

\displaystyle\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}(y)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\max_{u\in S_{\varepsilon}}d(x,c(x,y,u)),\quad y\in B(x,3\varepsilon)\\
0,\quad y\notin B(x,3\varepsilon).\end{cases}

Notice that for
y\in\cup_{u\in S_{\varepsilon}}[x,u]
, one has

\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}(y)=1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}d(x,y).

We now check that
\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}
satisfies the required conditions. (i) is clear and (ii) follows from the fact that for every
u\in S_{\varepsilon}
and
y\in X
,
d(x,c(x,y,u))\leq d(x,u)\leq\varepsilon
. Let now
y\in X
with
d(x,y)\leq\varepsilon/2
. We have for every
u\in S_{\varepsilon}
,
d(x,c(x,y,u))\leq d(x,y)\leq 1/2
so that
1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\max_{u\in S_{\varepsilon}}d(x,c(x,y,u))\geq 1/2
and (iii) is satisfied. Consider then
y\in B(x,3\varepsilon)
with
d(x,y)\geq 2\varepsilon
. There exists some
u\in B(x,3\varepsilon)
such that
d(x,u)=\varepsilon
and
u\in[x,y]
. Since
d(u,y)\geq\varepsilon
, we have
u\in S_{\varepsilon}
. (iv) is therefore satisfied. Concerning (v) and (vi), we can just observe that
\nu
a.e.

\partial_{x}\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{x}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}1_{\cup_{u\in S_{\varepsilon}}[x,u]},

so that the conclusion follows because the number of elements in
S_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded above.
∎

The next lemma ensures the existence of good coverings.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\lambda\geq 1
. Then, there exist constants
C,r_{0}>0
such that for every
r\in(0,r_{0})
the following holds: There exists an at most countable cover of
X
made of balls
B_{i}
of radius
r
such that each point of
X
belongs to at most
C
of the balls
\lambda B_{i}
, i.e.
\sum_{i}1_{\lambda B_{i}}\leq C
.

Proof.

The type of argument is well known, see, for instance,
[
19
, Lemma B.7.3]
; We reproduce it in our setting. Let
x_{n}
be a sequence such that
\{x_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is dense in
X
. Let
0<r<\frac{R}{2\lambda}
where
R
is such that for every
x\in X,0<r<R
, any ball
B(x,r)
can be covered by at most
N
balls of radius
r/2
where
N
is independent of
x
and
r
. Define inductively an at most countable set
(y_{n})
by defining
y_{0}:=x_{0}
and
y_{n}:=x_{k}
where
k
is the least index
i
such that
x_{i}\notin\cup_{j<n}B(y_{j},r)
. If such an index does not exist, we do not define
y_{n}
. From the definition and the density of
(x_{n})
,

X\subset\bigcup_{i}B(y_{i},2r).

Note also that for
i\neq j
,
d(y_{i},y_{j})\geq r
. We claim that each point of
X
belongs to at most
C
balls
B(y_{i},2\lambda r)
where
C
is uniform. Indeed, for
x\in X
let

F_{x}=\{y_{i}\mid x\in B(y_{i},2\lambda r)\}

Then
F_{x}\subset B(x,2\lambda r)
. Moreover,
B(x,2\lambda r)
can be covered by at most
C
balls of radius
r/2
and each of this ball can not contain more than one element of
F_{x}
. Therefore, the number of elements in
F_{x}
is less than
C
.
∎

With those two lemmas in hands we can construct convenient partitions of unity.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\lambda\geq 4
and let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be small enough. For
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, there exists an at most countable cover of
X
made of balls
B^{\varepsilon}_{i}=B(x_{i},\varepsilon)
of radius
\varepsilon
such that each point of
X
belongs to at most
C
of the balls
\lambda B^{\varepsilon}_{i}
and there exists and a subordinated family of functions
\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}
such that:

(i)

\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
;

(ii)

0\leq\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\leq 1
on
X
;

(iii)

\sum_{i}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}=1
on
X
;

(iv)

\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}=0
in
X\setminus B_{i}^{4\,\varepsilon}
;

(v)

\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}d\nu\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{p-1}}
.

Proof.

For
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
we first consider an at most countable cover of
X
made of balls
B^{\varepsilon}_{i}=B(x_{i},\varepsilon)
of radius
\varepsilon
as in Lemma
3.4
and define the functions
\Psi^{2\varepsilon}_{x_{i}}
as in Lemma
3.3
. Since
B^{\varepsilon}_{i}
is a covering of
X
, any
x\in X
belongs to at least one of the balls
B_{i}^{\varepsilon}
and at to most
N
of the balls
4B_{i}^{\varepsilon}
where is
N
uniform, thus

1/2\leq\sum_{j}\Psi^{2\varepsilon}_{x_{j}}\leq N

Define then

\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\Psi^{2\varepsilon}_{x_{i}}}{\sum_{j}\Psi^{2\varepsilon}_{x_{j}}}.

It is immediate to check that (i)-(iv) are satisfied. Moreover, one has

\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}\Psi^{2\varepsilon}_{x_{i}}}{\sum_{j}\Psi^{2\varepsilon}_{x_{j}}}-\Psi^{2\varepsilon}_{x_{i}}\frac{\sum_{j}\partial_{x_{i}}\Psi^{2\varepsilon}_{x_{j}}}{(\sum_{j}\Psi^{2\varepsilon}_{x_{j}})^{2}}.

from which (v) easily follows, using once again the bounded overlap property.
∎

3.2
Regularity of the
p
-energy forms

We denote by
C_{c}(X)
the space of continuous and compactly supported functions. We recall that the metric space
(X,d)
is said to be proper if closed metric balls are compact.

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume that
(X,d)
is proper. Let
p>1
. The
p
-energy form
\mathcal{E}_{p}
with domain
W^{1,p}(X,m)
is regular, that is, the space
C_{c}(X)\cap W^{1,p}(X,m)
is dense in
C_{c}(X)
for the supremum norm and dense in
W^{1,p}(X,m)
for the
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}
norm.

Proof.

We consider a partition of unity
\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}
as in Theorem
3.5
. For
f\in C_{c}(X)
, consider

\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{i}f(x_{i})\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}.

Since
f\in C_{c}(X)
, it is clear from the properness assumption that
\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}(X)\cap W^{1,p}(X,m)
. Proving that
f_{\varepsilon}
converges to
f
for the supremum norm topology easily follows from uniform continuity arguments. Therefore,
C_{c}(X)\cap W^{1,p}(X,m)
is dense in
C_{c}(X)
for the supremum norm. Next, let
\eta>0
and
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
. One can find a call
B_{R}
with radius
R
large enough so that

\int_{X\setminus B_{R}}|f|^{p}d\mu+\int_{\mathcal{S}\setminus B_{R}}|\partial f|^{p}d\nu<\eta

Consider the function

\Phi=\sum_{i:B_{R}\cap B(x_{i},2\varepsilon)\neq\emptyset}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon/2}\in C_{c}(X)\cap W^{1,p}(X,m),

where
\varepsilon>0
is small enough. It is then easy to check that we have
f\Phi\in C_{c}(X)\cap W^{1,p}(X,m)
and

\|f\Phi-f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}+\|\partial(f\Phi-f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}\leq C\eta.

∎

3.3
Korevaar-Schoen characterization of the Sobolev and BV spaces

We now turn to the characterization of the Bolev and BV spaces as Korevaar-Schoen type spaces. For
p\geq 1,r>0,
and
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
, we define

E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,r)=\frac{1}{\Psi_{p}(r)}\int_{X}\frac{1}{m(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}dm(y)dm(x),

where
\Psi_{p}(r)=r^{p-1}\Phi(r)
. We observe that

\frac{1}{C}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{p}\leq\frac{\Psi_{p}(R)}{\Psi_{p}(r)}\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{p-1}\frac{\Phi(R)}{\Phi(r)}\text{ for any }r,R\in(0,r_{0}]\text{ with }r\leq R.

Define the following Korevaar-Schoen space

\displaystyle KS^{\alpha_{p},\Psi_{p}}(X,m)=\left\{f\in L^{p}(X,m),\limsup_{r\to 0}E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,r)<+\infty\right\}.

(7)

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
p>1
. Then
KS^{\alpha_{p},\Psi_{p}}(X,m)=W^{1,p}(X,m)
and there exist constants
c,C,r_{0}>0
such that for every
f\in KS^{\alpha_{p},p}(X)

c\sup_{r\in(0,r_{0}]}E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,r)\leq\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|^{p}d\nu\leq C\liminf_{r\to 0}E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,r)

Proof.

We adapt to our setting arguments from
[
8
, Theorem 5.2]
. Thanks to Lemma
3.4
, we pick constants
C
and
0<r_{0}<\frac{\underline{\iota}}{2}
small enough so that
\mathrm{(A4)}
holds and such that for every
r\in(0,r_{0})
there exists an at most countable cover of
X
made of balls
B_{i}
of radius
r
such that each point of
X
belongs to at most
C
of the balls
2B_{i}
. From Theorem
2.12
, for
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
and
r<r_{0}
we have then:

\displaystyle E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Psi_{p}(r)}\int_{X}\frac{1}{m(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}dm(y)dm(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{p-1}\Phi(r)^{2}}\int_{X}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}dm(y)dm(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\frac{C}{r^{p-1}\Phi(r)^{2}}\int_{B_{i}}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}dm(y)dm(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\frac{C}{r^{p-1}\Phi(r)^{2}}\int_{B_{i}}\int_{B(x,r)}\int_{]x,y[}d(x,y)^{p-1}|\partial f|^{p}(z)d\nu(z)dm(y)dm(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\frac{C}{\Phi(r)^{2}}\int_{B_{i}}\int_{B(x,r)}\int_{]x,y[}|\partial f|^{p}(z)d\nu(z)dm(y)dm(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\frac{C}{\Phi(r)^{2}}\int_{B_{i}}\int_{2B_{i}}\int_{2B_{i}}|\partial f|^{p}(z)d\nu(z)dm(y)dm(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i}\int_{2B_{i}}|\partial f|^{p}(z)d\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|^{p}(z)d\nu(z)

Therefore
W^{1,p}(X,m)\subset KS^{\alpha_{p},\Psi_{p}}(X,m)
, and it remains to prove the converse inclusion. We consider a partition of unity
\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}
as in Theorem
3.5
and for
f\in KS^{\alpha_{p},p}(X,m)
we define

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{i}f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}

(8)

where
f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}=\frac{1}{m(B_{i}^{\varepsilon})}
. Due to the bounded overlap property, for any
x\in B_{j}^{\varepsilon}
it holds that

\displaystyle|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{i:B_{i}^{4\varepsilon}\cap B_{j}^{2\varepsilon C_{cov}}\neq\emptyset}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)(f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}-f(x))\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i:B_{i}^{4\varepsilon}\cap B_{j}^{4\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset}\Big|\frac{1}{m(B_{i}^{\varepsilon})}\int_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}(f(y)-f(x))\,dm(y)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i:B_{i}^{4\varepsilon}\cap B_{j}^{4\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset}\frac{1}{m(B_{i}^{\varepsilon})}\int_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}|f(y)-f(x)|\,dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{m(B(x,12\varepsilon))}\int_{B(x,12\varepsilon)}|f(x)-f(y)|dm(y),

whence

\displaystyle\|f_{\varepsilon}-f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f(x)|^{p}dm(x)

(9)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{X}\left(\frac{1}{m(B(x,12\varepsilon))}\int_{B(x,12\varepsilon)}|f(x)-f(y)|dm(y)\right)^{p}dm(x)

(10)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{X}\frac{1}{m(B(x,12\varepsilon))}\int_{B(x,12\varepsilon)}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}dm(y)dm(x)

(11)

\displaystyle\leq C\Psi_{p}(12\varepsilon)E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,12\varepsilon)

(12)

Therefore, from the definition of
KS^{\alpha_{p},p}(X,m)
,
f_{\varepsilon}
converges to
f
in
L^{p}(X,m)
when
\varepsilon\to 0
. Next, for
x\in B_{j}^{\varepsilon}
, we have that

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{B_{j}^{\varepsilon}}=\sum_{i:B_{i}^{4\varepsilon}\cap B_{j}^{4\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)(f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}-f_{B_{j}^{\varepsilon}})

It follows that
f_{\varepsilon}\in AC(X)
and that we have for
\nu
-a.e.
x\in B_{j}^{\varepsilon}\cap\mathcal{S}

|\partial f_{\varepsilon}|(x)\leq\sum_{i:B_{i}^{4\varepsilon}\cap B_{j}^{4\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset}|\partial\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)|\,|f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}-f_{B_{j}^{\varepsilon}}|

Therefore, from the bounded overlap property and the basic convexity inequality

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)^{p}\leq n^{p-1}\sum_{i=1}x_{i}^{p}

we have for
\nu
-a.e.
x\in B_{j}^{\varepsilon}\cap\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle|\partial f_{\varepsilon}|(x)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i:B_{i}^{4\varepsilon}\cap B_{j}^{4\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset}|\partial\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}\,|f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}-f_{B_{j}^{\varepsilon}}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i:B_{i}^{4\varepsilon}\cap B_{j}^{4\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset}|\partial\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}\,\left|\frac{1}{m(B_{i}^{\varepsilon})m(B_{j}^{\varepsilon})}\int_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{j}^{\varepsilon}}(f(y)-f(z))dm(y)dm(z)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i:B_{i}^{4\varepsilon}\cap B_{j}^{4\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset}|\partial\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}\,\frac{1}{m(B_{i}^{\varepsilon})m(B_{j}^{\varepsilon})}\int_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{j}^{\varepsilon}}|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}dm(y)dm(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\Phi(\varepsilon)}\sum_{i:B_{i}^{4\varepsilon}\cap B_{j}^{4\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset}|\partial\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}\,\int_{B_{j}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{m(B(z,12\varepsilon))}\int_{B(z,12\varepsilon)}|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}dm(y)dm(z),

where we used the uniform volume growth estimate in the last inequality. As a consequence, and since

\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}d\nu\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{p-1}}

we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{B_{j}^{\varepsilon}\cap\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{\varepsilon}|(x)^{p}d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\Psi(\varepsilon)}\int_{B_{j}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{m(B(z,12\varepsilon))}\int_{B(z,12\varepsilon)}|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}dm(y)dm(z).

It follows then that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{\varepsilon}|(x)^{p}d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\int_{B_{j}^{\varepsilon}\cap\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{\varepsilon}|(x)^{p}d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\Psi_{p}(\varepsilon)}\int_{X}\frac{1}{m(B(z,12\varepsilon))}\int_{B(z,12\varepsilon)}|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}dm(y)dm(z)

\displaystyle\leq CE_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,12\varepsilon).

Since
f_{\varepsilon}
converges to
f
in
L^{p}(X,m)
when
\varepsilon\to 0
, by lower-semicontinuity of
\mathcal{E}_{p}
(Corollary
2.16
), we conclude that
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
and

\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|(x)^{p}d\nu(x)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{\varepsilon}|(x)^{p}d\nu(x)\leq C\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,\varepsilon).

∎

For
p=1
, we obtain a similar result.

Theorem 3.8
.

KS^{\Psi_{1},1}(X,m)=BV(X,m)
and there exist constants
c,C>0
such that for every
f\in KS^{\Psi_{1},1}(X)

c\sup_{r\in(0,r_{0}]}E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f,r)\leq\|\partial f\|(X)\leq C\liminf_{r\to 0}E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f,r).

Proof.

The proof is very close to that of Theorem
3.7
.

(i)

Arguing as before, we get that for every
f\in W^{1,1}(X,m)

\displaystyle E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f,r)\leq C\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|(z)d\nu(z)

Let now
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
and let
f_{n}\in W^{1,1}(X,m)
that converges to
f
in
L^{1}(X,m)
. We have

\displaystyle E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f_{n},r)\leq C\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{n}|(z)d\nu(z)

so that when

,

E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f,r)\leq C\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{n}|(z)d\nu(z).

We conclude

E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f,r)\leq C\|\partial f\|(X).

(ii)

With the same notations as in the proof of Theorem
3.7
one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{\varepsilon}|(x)d\nu(x)\leq CE_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,12\varepsilon).

Therefore, one has

\|\partial f\|(X)\leq C\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}E_{p,\Psi_{p}}(f,\varepsilon).

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

(BV(X,m),\|\cdot\|_{BV(X)})
is a Banach space.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.8
, one has
BV(X,m)=(KS^{\Psi_{1},1}(X,m)
and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{BV(X)})
is equivalent to the norm

\|f\|_{KS^{\Psi_{1},1}(X,m))}:=\|f\|_{L^{1}(X,m)}+\sup_{r\in(0,r_{0}]}E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f,r).

Since

, the conclusion follows.
∎

3.4
Critical exponents and real interpolation of the Besov-Lipschitz spaces

Given
p\geq 1
and
\alpha\geq 0
, we consider the Besov-Lipschitz space

\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,p}(X,m)=\left\{f\in L^{p}(X,m)\mid\sup_{0<r<1}E_{p,\alpha}(f,r)<+\infty\right\}

where
E_{p,\alpha}(f,r)
is defined by

\displaystyle E_{p,\alpha}(f,r)=\int_{X}\frac{1}{m(B(x,r))}\left(\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}}{r^{p\alpha}}dm(y)\right)dm(x)

(13)

Several properties of the Besov-Lipschitz spaces are pointed out in
[
8
, Section 3]
in the context of general metric spaces. In this section, as an application, of the previous results we show that on local trees they form a real interpolation scale of spaces. We first point out the following lemma about the critical exponents of the family of Besov-Lipschitz spaces.

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume the volume growth function
\Phi
is given by

\Phi(r)=r^{d_{h}}

with
d_{h}\geq 1
.
Let
p\geq 1
. Then,

\sup\left\{\alpha\geq 0\mid\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,p}(X,m)\text{ contains non-constant fuctions }\right\}=1+\frac{d_{h}-1}{p}.

Proof.

Indeed, if
\alpha=1+\frac{d_{h}-1}{p}
, then
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,p}(X,m)
is the Korevaar-Schoen space
KS^{\Psi_{p},p}(X,m)
, which contains non-constant functions, being dense in
L^{p}(X,m)
. On the other hand let
\alpha>1+\frac{d_{h}-1}{p}
and
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,p}(X,m)
. One has for
0<r<1

\int_{X}\frac{1}{m(B(x,r))}\left(\int_{B(x,r)}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}}{r^{p\alpha}}dm(y)\right)dm(x)\leq C.

Therefore
\liminf_{r\to 0}E_{p,\alpha_{p}}(f,r)=0
. From (ii) in Theorem
3.7
, this implies

\sup_{r\in(0,r_{0}]}E_{p,\alpha_{p}}(f,r)=0,

so that
f
is constant.
∎

We now recall some basic definitions of the real interpolation theory for seminormed spaces as in
[
18
]
. Let
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
be two seminormed Banach spaces. Assume that the pair
(X_{0},X_{1})
is a compatible couple, i.e. there is some Hausdorff topological vector space in which each of
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
is continuously embedded. Then the sum
X_{0}+X_{1}
is a Banach space under the seminorm

\|f\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}:=\inf\{\|f_{0}\|_{X_{0}}+\|f_{1}\|_{X_{1}},f=f_{0}+f_{1}\}.

The
K
-functional of
(X_{0},X_{1})
is defined for each
f\in X_{0}+X_{1}
and
t>0
by

K(f,t,X_{0},X_{1}):=\inf\{\|f_{0}\|_{X_{0}}+t\|f_{1}\|_{X_{1}}\mid f=f_{0}+f_{1}\}.

Suppose that
0<\theta<1
,
1\leq q<\infty
or
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,
q=\infty
. Then the interpolation space
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,q}
consists of functions
f\in X_{0}+X_{1}
such that

\|f\|_{\theta,q}=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{-\theta}K(f,t,X_{0},X_{1}))^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q},&0<\theta<1,1\leq q<\infty,\\
\sup_{t>0}t^{-\theta}K(f,t,X_{0},X_{1}),&0\leq\theta\leq 1,q=\infty,\end{cases}

is finite. In our setting, we take
X_{0}=L^{p}(X,m)
and
X_{1}=W^{1,p}(X,m)
is equipped with the seminorm
\|\partial f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}
where
p\geq 1
. For
t\geq 0
, we simply denote

K(f,t)=\inf\{\|g\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}+t\|\partial h\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}\mid f=g+h\}.

We have then the following theorem.

Theorem 3.11
.

Assume the volume growth function
\Phi
is given by

\Phi(r)=r^{d_{h}}

with
d_{h}\geq 1
. Let
r_{0}>0
be as in Theorem
3.7
. There exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for any
f\in L^{p}(X,m)+W^{1,p}(X,m)
if
p>1
and
f\in L^{1}(X,m)+BV(X,m)
if
p=1
the following holds for
0<r<r_{0}
,

C_{1}E_{p,0}(f,r)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq K(f,r^{\alpha_{p}})\leq C_{2}E_{p,0}(f,r)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\alpha_{p}=1+\frac{d_{h}-1}{p}
. Therefore, if
X
is compact, for
0\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{p}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{p,\alpha}(X)=\begin{cases}(L^{1}(X,m),BV(X,m))_{\alpha/\alpha_{1},\infty},&p=1\\
(L^{p}(X,m),W^{1,p}(X,m))_{\alpha/\alpha_{p},\infty},&p>1.\end{cases}

Proof.

We assume
p>1
. The case
p=1
follows in the same way. We first prove the bound

E_{p,0}(f,r)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq CK(f,r^{\alpha_{p}}).

Let
f=g+h
, where
g\in L^{p}(X,m)
and
h\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
.
Then by Minkowski’s inequality and (i) in Theorem
3.7
we have for
0<r<r_{0}

\displaystyle E_{p,0}(f,r)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq E_{p,0}(g,r)^{\frac{1}{p}}+E_{p,0}(h,r)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|g\|_{L^{p}(K,\mu)}+r^{\alpha_{p}}r^{-\alpha_{p}}E_{p,0}(h,r)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|g\|_{L^{p}(K,\mu)}+r^{\alpha_{p}}\|h\|_{W^{1,p}(K)}\right).

We now turn to the proof of the second inequality, that is,
K(f,r^{\alpha_{p}})\leq CE_{p}(f,r)^{\frac{1}{p}}
. We consider a partition of unity
\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}
as in Theorem
3.5
and for
f\in L^{p}(X,m)
we define as before

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{i}f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}

(14)

where
f_{B_{i}^{\varepsilon}}=\frac{1}{m(B_{i}^{\varepsilon})}
. Set
g_{\varepsilon}=f-f_{\varepsilon}
so that
f=g_{\varepsilon}+f_{\varepsilon}
. We claim that
g_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(X,m)
and
f_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
and moreover that both
\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}
and
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,p}(X,m)}
can be bounded in terms of
E_{p,0}(f,r)
.
Indeed, from the proof of (ii) in Theorem
3.7
we have

\displaystyle\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\|f_{\varepsilon}-f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{X}\frac{1}{m(B(x,12\varepsilon))}\int_{B(x,12\varepsilon)}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}dm(y)dm(x)

\displaystyle\leq CE_{p,0}(f,12\varepsilon).

Again from the proof of (ii) in Theorem
3.7
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{\varepsilon}|(x)^{p}d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{d_{h}+p-1}}\int_{X}\frac{1}{m(B(z,12\varepsilon))}\int_{B(z,12\varepsilon)}|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}dm(y)dm(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{d_{h}+p-1}}E_{p,0}(f,12\varepsilon).

We conclude that for every
r=\frac{\varepsilon}{12}<r_{0}

K(f,r^{\alpha_{p}})\leq\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}+r^{\alpha_{p}}\|\partial f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}\leq CE_{p,0}(f,r).

This establishes the first part of the statement. For the second part of the statement, assume that
X
is compact. For
f\in L^{p}(X,m)
we can then write
f=g+h
with
h=\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\int_{X}fdm
and deduce that for every
r>\mathrm{diam}(X)

K(f,r^{\alpha_{p}})\leq\left\|f-\frac{1}{\mu(X)}\int_{X}fdm\right\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}\leq CE_{p}(f,r)^{1/p}.

The conclusion follows by definition of the interpolation space and of its seminorm.
∎

3.5
Nash inequality

Theorem 3.12
.

Assume the volume growth function
\Phi
is given by

\Phi(r)=r^{d_{h}}

with
d_{h}\geq 1
. Let
p>1
. The following Nash inequality holds for every
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
,

\|f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}+\|\partial f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}\right)^{\theta}\|f\|^{1-\theta}_{L^{1}(X,m)}

where
\theta=\frac{(p-1)d_{h}}{p-1+pd_{h}}
.

Proof.

We first note the following two easily proved properties which indicate that the Sobolev norm behaves nicely under cutoffs. In what follows we denote
a\wedge b=\min(a,b)
and
a_{+}=\max(a,0)
.

•

For every
s,t\geq 0
, and
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
,

\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial((f-t)_{+}\wedge s)|^{p}d\nu\leq\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|^{p}d\nu.

•

For any non-negative
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
and any
\rho>1
,

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f_{\rho,k}|^{p}d\nu\leq\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|^{p}d\nu,

where
f_{\rho,k}:=(f-\rho^{k})_{+}\wedge\rho^{k}(\rho-1)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

On the other hand, consider the averaging operator

\mathcal{M}_{r}f(x)=\frac{1}{m(B(x,r))}\int_{m(B(x,r)}fd\mu

It immediately follows from
\mathrm{(A4)}
that for any
f\in L^{1}(X,m)
one has for
0<r<r_{0}

\|\mathcal{M}_{r}f\|_{L^{\infty}(X,\mu)}\leq\frac{C}{r^{d_{h}}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(X,m)}.

From Hölder’s inequality and Theorem
3.7
we also have for every
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
and
0<r<r_{0}

\|f-\mathcal{M}_{r}f\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}\leq r^{\alpha_{p}}\,\sup_{\rho\in(0,r_{0})}E_{p,\alpha_{p}}(f,\rho)^{1/p}\leq Cr^{\alpha_{p}}\,\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|^{p}d\nu\right)^{1/p}.

The previous observations are enough to obtain the full scale of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities and in particular the stated Nash inequalities: This follows from applying the results of
[
6
, Theorem 9.1]
.
∎

4
Heat kernel characterization of the Sobolev and BV spaces

Everywhere in this section, we assume that the metric space
(X,d)
is proper and that the assumptions of Section
3
are satisfied. In particular, one has for every
x\in X
and
0<r<r_{0}

\frac{1}{C}\Phi(r)\leq m(B(x,r))\leq C\Phi(r).

As before, we denote
\Psi_{p}(r)=r^{p-1}\Phi(r)
so that
\Psi_{2}(r)=r\Phi(r)
. Our goal in this section is to construct a heat kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
on
(X,d,m)
and prove that it satisfies the following estimates:

•

Upper bound:
For
t\in(0,t_{0}],d(x,y)\leq\underline{\iota}/2
,

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{d(x,y)}{\Phi^{-1}\left(t/d(x,y)\right)}\right).

(15)

•

Near diagonal lower bound:
For
\Psi_{2}(c_{2}d(x,y))\leq t\leq t_{0}
,

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)\geq\frac{c_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}.

(16)

•

Escape rate estimate:
For
t\in(0,t_{0}],r\leq\underline{\iota}/2
,

\displaystyle\int_{X\setminus B(x,r)}p_{t}(x,y)dm(y)\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(t/r\right)}\right).

(17)

Those estimates will be obtained by localizing some of the arguments in
[
26
]
. We point out that some of the arguments have also been a little simplifies like the proof of Theorem
4.3
. As a consequence of those estimates, in the second part of the section we will then obtain a heat kernel characterization of the class
BV(X,m)
and the Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(X,m)
,
p>1
.

4.1
Heat kernel estimates

Lemma 4.1
.

The 2-energy form

\mathcal{E}_{2}(f)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|^{2}d\nu

with domain
W^{1,2}(X,m)
is a regular Dirichlet form in
L^{2}(X,m)
.

Proof.

We first note that for
f,g\in W^{1,2}(X,m)

\frac{1}{4}(\mathcal{E}_{2}(f+g)+\mathcal{E}_{2}(f-g))=\int_{\mathcal{S}}(\partial f)(\partial g)d\nu

is indeed a bilinear form. Then,
W^{1,2}(X,m)
is dense in
L^{2}(X,m)
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
is closed in
L^{2}(X,m)
from Proposition
2.14
. Finally, if
u,v\in W^{1,2}(X,m)
are such that for
x,y\in X

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq|v(x)-v(y)|,

then, it easily follows that
\nu
-a.e.
|\partial u|\leq|\partial v|
so that
\mathcal{E}_{2}(u)\leq\mathcal{E}_{2}(v)
. Therefore, we conclude that
\mathcal{E}_{2}
is a Dirichlet form and its regularity follows from Theorem
3.6
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

The Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}_{2}
admits a heat kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
which satisfies for every
x,y,z\in X
,
d(y,z)\leq\underline{\iota}
,
t>0
,

|p_{t}(x,y)-p_{t}(x,z)|^{2}\leq\frac{d(y,z)}{t}p_{t}(x,x).

(18)

Proof.

The existence of the heat kernel follows from the fact that the Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}_{2}
has a domain included in the space of continuous functions and the estimate (
18
) follows from Theorem
2.12
applied to
f=p_{t}(x,\cdot)
and the estimate
\mathcal{E}_{2}(p_{t}(x,\cdot))\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{X}p_{t/2}(x,z)^{2}dm(z)=\frac{1}{t}p_{t}(x,x)
which comes from spectral theory.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

The Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}_{2}
admits a heat kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
which satisfies for every
t>0
,
x,y\in X

p_{t}(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))\wedge 1}.

(19)

Proof.

Let
x\in X
. For any ball
B(x,r)
in
X
, one has
\int_{B(x,r)}p_{t}(x,y)dm(y)\leq 1
. Hence there exists
y=y(t,r)\in B(x,r)
such that
p_{t}(x,y)\leq m(B(x,r))^{-1}
. In particular, the volume growth assumption implies that for any
0<r<r_{0}

p_{t}(x,y)\leq\frac{C}{\Phi(r)}.

Now using this estimate, together with the Hölder regularity (
19
), we obtain

\frac{1}{2}p_{t}(x,x)^{2}\leq p_{t}(x,y)^{2}+|p_{t}(x,x)-p_{t}(x,y)|^{2}\leq\frac{C^{2}}{\Phi(r)^{2}}+\frac{r}{t}p_{t}(x,x).

The above quadratic equation gives that

p_{t}(x,x)\leq\frac{r}{t}+\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}}{t^{2}}+\frac{C^{2}}{\Phi(r)^{2}}}.

(20)

Now letting
t=\Psi_{2}(r)=r\Phi(r)
, we have that

p_{r\Phi(r)}(x,x)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(r)}

and hence for any
t\leq r_{0}\Phi(r_{0})
there holds

p_{t}(x,x)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}.

On the other hand, the upper bound (
20
) holds for any
t>0
by taking
r=r_{0}/2
, i.e.,
p_{t}(x,x)\leq C_{t}
for a decreasing function
C_{t}
. We thus conclude that

p_{t}(x,x)\leq\frac{C}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))\wedge 1}

and by the semigroup property

p_{t}(x,y)\leq p_{t}(x,x)^{1/2}p_{t}(y,y)^{1/2}\leq\frac{C}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))\wedge 1},\quad\forall x,y\in X\text{ and }t>0.

∎

The proof for the following lemma is the same as in
[
26
, Section 4]
.

and
d(x,y)\leq\underline{\iota}

p_{t}(x,y)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{d(x,y)}{\Phi^{-1}\left(t/d(x,y)\right)}\right).

We skip here the details for the sake of conciseness.
∎

It remains to show the near diagonal lower estimate (
16
) for the heat kernel. We begin with the following on-diagonal lower bound.

Lemma 4.6
.

There exist two constant
c,C>0
and
t_{0}>0
(depending on
\underline{\iota},r_{0},d_{h}
) such that for
0<t<t_{0}

p_{t}(x,x)\geq\frac{C}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}.

(22)

Proof.

We first note from Lemma
4.4
that for any
r\leq\underline{\iota}/2
, there exists
0<c_{1}<1
and
c_{2}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}^{x}(X_{t}\notin B(x,r))\leq\mathbb{P}^{x}(T_{B(x,r)}\leq t)\leq c_{1}+c_{2}t/\Psi_{2}(r).

Now choose
r
such that
\Psi_{2}(r)\simeq t
and
c_{1}+c_{2}t/\Psi_{2}(r)=c_{3}<1
. Indeed, this is possible as long as
t<c_{4}\Psi_{2}(\underline{\iota}/2)
for a small constant
c_{4}>0
. Then we have

\mathbb{P}^{x}(X_{t}\in B(x,r))\geq 1-c_{3}.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, one has

(1-c_{3})^{2}\leq\mathbb{P}^{x}(X_{t}\in B(x,r))^{2}=\left(\int_{B(x,r)}p_{t}(x,z)dm(z)\right)^{2}\leq\mu(B(x,r))p_{2t}(x,x).

Finally from the choice of
t
and the uniform local Ahlfors regularity assumption, we conclude that there exist constant
c
(depending on
c_{1},c_{2}
) and
C>0
(depending on
c_{1},c_{2}
) such that for
0<t<t_{0}:=c\Psi_{2}(\min\{\underline{\iota},r_{0}\})
,

p_{t}(x,x)\geq\frac{C}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}.

∎

We are now ready to prove (
16
).

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
t_{0}
be as in Lemma
4.6
. There exists constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that if
\Psi_{2}(c_{2}d(x,y))\leq t\leq t_{0}
,

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)\geq\frac{c_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}.

(23)

Proof.

Observing that for
t
small enough (as in Lemma
4.6
) and for
x,y\in X
such that
d(x,y)<\underline{\iota}
,
Theorem
4.3
and Lemma
4.6
imply that

\displaystyle|p_{t}(x,y)-p_{t}(x,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{t}\right)^{1/2}\frac{1}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))^{1/2}}\leq C\left(\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))\frac{d(x,y)}{t}\right)^{1/2}p_{t}(x,x).

Taking
\Psi_{2}(ad(x,y))\leq t
for
a
small enough, we obtain again from Lemma
4.6
that

p_{t}(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{2}p_{t}(x,x)\geq\frac{c}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}.

(24)

∎

4.2
Heat kernel characterization of the Sobolev and BV spaces

We now give yet another characterization of the Sobolev and BV spaces which shows that the theory of heat kernel based Besov classes developed in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
can therefore be applied in the context of local trees. The main ingredients of this characterization are the heat kernel estimates (
16
) and (
17
). For
f\in L^{p}(X,m)
and
t>0
we denote

N_{p}(f,t)=\frac{1}{\Psi_{p}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\int_{X}\int_{X}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}p_{t}(x,y)dm(x)dm(y)

and the heat kernel based Besov class is defined by

\mathcal{B}^{p}(X,m)=\left\{f\in L^{p}(X,m)\mid\limsup_{t\to 0}N_{p}(f,t)<+\infty\right\}.

We have then the following theorem.

Theorem 4.8
.

(i)

\mathcal{B}^{1}(X,m)=BV(X,m)
and there exist constants
c,C>0
such that for every
f\in BV(X,m)

c\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|d\nu\leq\sup_{t\in(0,t_{0}]}N_{1}(f,t)\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{1}(X,m)}+\|\partial f\|(X)\right).

(ii)

For
p>1
,
\mathcal{B}^{p}(X,m)=W^{1,p}(X,m)
and there exist constants
c,C>0
such that for every
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)

c\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|^{p}d\nu\leq\sup_{t\in(0,t_{0}]}N_{p}(f,t)\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}+\int_{\mathcal{S}}|\partial f|^{p}d\nu\right).

Proof.

We do the proof for
p=1
, the proof for
p>1
being similar. We first have for
0<t\leq t_{0}
, from the near diagonal lower bound (
16
)

\displaystyle N_{1}(f,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Psi_{1}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\int_{X}\int_{X}|f(x)-f(y)|p_{t}(x,y)dm(x)dm(y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\Psi_{1}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\int_{X}\int_{B(y,C\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}|f(x)-f(y)|p_{t}(x,y)dm(x)dm(y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{\Psi_{1}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\int_{X}\int_{B(y,C\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}|f(x)-f(y)|dm(x)dm(y)

\displaystyle\geq cE_{1}(f,C\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)).

Therefore, from Theorem
3.8
one has

\sup_{t\in(0,t_{0}]}N_{1}(f,t)\geq C\|\partial f\|(X).

We now turn to the proof of the upper bound for
N_{1}(f,t)
where
0<t\leq t_{0}
. Let
0<r<\underline{\iota}/2
. We decompose

\int_{X}\int_{X}|f(x)-f(y)|p_{t}(x,y)dm(x)dm(y)=A(t)+B(t)

where

A(t)=\int_{X}\int_{X\setminus B(y,r)}|f(x)-f(y)|p_{t}(x,y)dm(x)dm(y)

and

B(t)=\int_{X}\int_{B(y,r)}|f(x)-f(y)|p_{t}(x,y)dm(x)dm(y).

We first estimate
A(t)
using (
17
):

\displaystyle A(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{X}\left(\int_{X\setminus B(y,r)}p_{t}(x,y)dm(x)\right)|f(y)|dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(t/r\right)}\right)\|f\|_{L^{1}(X,m)}.

We then estimate
B(t)
using (
5.1
):

\displaystyle B(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\int_{X}\int_{B(y,r)}|f(x)-f(y)|\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{d(x,y)}{\Phi^{-1}\left(t/d(x,y)\right)}\right)dm(x)dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\int_{X}\int_{B(y,2^{1-k}r)\setminus B(y,2^{-k}r)}|f(x)-f(y)|\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{d(x,y)}{\Phi^{-1}\left(t/d(x,y)\right)}\right)dm(x)dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\int_{X}\int_{B(y,2^{1-k}r)\setminus B(y,2^{-k}r)}|f(x)-f(y)|\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{2^{-k}r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(2^{k}t/r\right)}\right)dm(x)dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{2^{-k}r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(2^{k}t/r\right)}\right)\int_{X}\int_{B(y,2^{1-k}r)}|f(x)-f(y)|dm(x)dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{2^{-k}r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(2^{k}t/r\right)}\right)\Psi_{1}(2^{1-k}r)\Phi(2^{1-k}r)E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f,2^{1-k}r)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{2^{-k}r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(2^{k}t/r\right)}\right)\Psi_{1}(2^{1-k}r)\Phi(2^{1-k}r)\right)\sup_{\rho\in(0,r]}E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f,\rho)

Using (
6
), denoting
\varphi(x)=\max\{x^{\alpha},x\}
one then sees that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{2^{-k}r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(2^{k}t/r\right)}\right)\Psi_{1}(2^{1-k}r)\Phi(2^{1-k}r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{2^{-k}r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(2^{k}t/r\right)}\right)\Psi_{1}(2^{1-k}r)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}\Psi_{1}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{2^{-k}r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(2^{k}t/r\right)}\right)\varphi\left(2^{1-k}\frac{r}{\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\Psi_{1}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))^{2},

where for the last inequality, we used the fact that
\frac{t}{\Phi\circ\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)}=\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)
and the inequality

\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{2^{-k}r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(2^{k}t/r\right)}\right)=\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{t}{\Psi_{2}\circ\Phi^{-1}\left(2^{k}t/r\right)}\right)\leq\exp\left(-C_{3}\tilde{\varphi}\left(2^{1-k}\frac{r}{\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)}\right)\right)

where
\tilde{\varphi}=\min\{x^{2/\alpha},x^{1+\alpha}\}
. We thus have:

\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{2^{-k}r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(2^{k}t/r\right)}\right)\Psi_{1}(2^{1-k}r)\Phi(2^{1-k}r)\right)\leq C_{3}\Psi_{1}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)).

Therefore, one has

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{X}|f(x)-f(y)|p_{t}(x,y)dm(x)dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\exp\left(-C_{2}\frac{r}{\Phi^{-1}\left(t/r\right)}\right)\|f\|_{L^{1}(X,m)}+C_{3}\Psi_{1}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))\sup_{\rho\in(0,r]}E_{1,\Psi_{1}}(f,\rho).

Using Theorem
3.8
this yields

\sup_{t\in(0,t_{0}]}N_{1}(f,t)\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{1}(X,m)}+\|\partial f\|(X)\right).

∎

We say that the locally Lipshitz regular condition for harmonic functions holds if there exist
C>0
,

By the local tree property, the above condition holds as follows.

Lemma 4.9
.

The locally Lipshitz regular condition holds.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
,
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,

be given as in the proof of Lemma

is some positive integer independent of

Let

in

with boundary value on

in

S_{\varepsilon}
. For any

. Note that it is possible that

for

5.2
L^{p}

The goal of this section is to establish
L^{p}
gradient bounds for the heat semigroup
P_{t}
of the Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}_{2}
. We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

(i)

Let
p\geq 1
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
f\in W^{1,p}(X,m)
and
t>0

\|LP_{t}f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}\leq C\frac{\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}{t^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\|\partial f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
f\in W^{1,\infty}(X,m)
and
t>0

\|LP_{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}(X,m)}\leq C\frac{\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)}{t}\|\partial f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}

Proof.

We first assume
p<\infty
. The first ingredient is the estimate

\displaystyle\|P_{t}f-f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(X,m)}\leq\int_{X}\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}dm(x)dm(y)\leq C\Psi_{p}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))\|\partial f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}

which follows from Theorem
5.3
. The second ingredient is the estimate

\|LP_{t}f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}\leq\frac{C}{t}\|f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}

which for
p>1
follows from analyticity of the semigroup and which for
p=1
follows from the following upper bound

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p_{t}(x,y)\right|\leq\frac{C}{t}p_{ct}(x,y)

(25)

which is a consequence of
[
14
]
and Theorem
5.1
. In particular, it follows that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|LP_{t}f\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}=0
. Then, we have by the semigroup property of
P_{t}
that

\displaystyle\|LP_{2t}f\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(LP_{2^{k}t}f-LP_{2^{k-1}t}f)\right\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|LP_{2^{k}t}f-LP_{2^{k-1}t}f\right\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|LP_{2^{k-1}t}(P_{2^{k-1}t}f-f)\right\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k-1}t}\left\|P_{2^{k-1}t}f-f\right\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Psi_{p}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(2^{k-1}t))^{1/p}}{2^{k-1}t}\|\partial f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\Psi_{p}(\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t))^{1/p}}{t}\|\partial f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}.

For
p=\infty
, one uses instead the estimate

\displaystyle|P_{t}(x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)|f(x)-f(y)|dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}p_{t}(x,y)d(x,y)dm(y)\|\partial f\|_{L^{\infty}(X,m)}

\displaystyle\leq C\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)\|\partial f\|_{L^{\infty}(X,m)}

and the estimate

\|LP_{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}(X,m)}\leq\frac{C}{t}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(X,m)}

which also follows from (
25
). The rest of the proof proceeds then as before.
∎

We can finally state the
L^{p}
-gradient bound estimates.

Theorem 5.7
.

(i)

Let
p\geq 1
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
f\in L^{p}(X,m)
and
t>0

\|\partial P_{t}f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}\leq C\frac{\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)^{-1+\frac{2}{p}}}{t^{1/p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
f\in L^{\infty}(X,m)
and
t>0

\|\partial P_{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}(X,m)}\leq\frac{C}{\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(X,m)}.

Proof.

Let
f
be a piecewise affine function on
X
meaning that
f\in AC(X)
with
\nu
-a.e.

\displaystyle\partial f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}1_{]a_{i},b_{i}[},

(26)

where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
a_{i},b_{i}\in X
. We have then for every
g\in L^{1}(X,m)\cap L^{\infty}(X,m)

\displaystyle\int_{X}gLP_{t}fdm

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\partial P_{t}g\,\partial fdm.

Let now
1\leq p\leq\infty
and let
q
be the conjugate exponent. We have from Lemma
5.6

\left|\int_{X}\partial P_{t}g\,\partial fdm\right|\leq\|g\|_{L^{q}(X,m)}\|LP_{t}f\|_{L^{p}(X,m)}\leq C\frac{\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}{t^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\|g\|_{L^{q}(X,m)}\|\partial f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{S},\nu)}.

Since it is true for every functions
f
of the type (
26
), this allows to conclude

\|\partial P_{t}g\|_{L^{q}(X,m)}\leq C\frac{\Psi_{2}^{-1}(t)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}{t^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\|g\|_{L^{q}(X,m)}

This holds for every
g\in L^{1}(X,m)\cap L^{\infty}(X,m)
and therefore every
g\in L^{q}(X,m)
.
∎

Co-periods and Central Symmetric Cube L L -values
Li Cai, Yangyu Fan, Dongming She

Abstract
. In this article, we study the co-period integral attached to an automorphic form on GL ⁡ ( 2 ) {\mathrm{GL}}(2) and two exceptional theta series on the cubic Kazhdan-Patterson cover of GL ⁡ ( 2 ) {\mathrm{GL}}(2) . In the local aspect, we show the Hom {\mathrm{Hom}} -space is always of one dimension and conduct the unramified calculations. In the global aspect, we give the Euler decomposition for the co-period integrals of Eisenstein series and propose an Ichino-Ikeda type conjecture relating the co-period integrals of cuspidal forms to the central critical value of symmetric cube L L -functions. We also deduce from the local multiplicity one result that there exist cuspidal automorphic forms with prescribed local components and non-vanishing central symmetric cube L L -values.

Introduction

Let
G
be a locally compact group. An important aspect of the representation theory of
G
concerns the restriction of
G
-representations to a suitable subgroup
H\subset G
.

Consider the case
(G,H)
is a pair of algebraic groups over a field
F
. When
F
is a local field, a basic problem in the
representation theory is to study the
{\mathrm{Hom}}
-space
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})
where
\pi
is an admissible
G(F)
-representation.
When
F
is a global field with
{\mathbb{A}}
its adelic ring, a basic problem in the automorphic representation theory is to study the period integral

\int_{H(F)\backslash H({\mathbb{A}})}\varphi(h)dh,\quad\varphi\in\pi

where
\pi
is an automorphic
G({\mathbb{A}})
-representation. For a large class of
(G,H)
, called
spherical pairs
, it is expected that
the local
{\mathrm{Hom}}
-spaces can be characterized in terms of
L
-parameters and the global period integrals are closely related
to
L
-values. The work of Sakellaridis-Venkatesh
[
28
]
gives explicit conjectures for such spherical pairs. Recently, these conjectures
have been largely generalized to hyperspherical Hamiltonian spaces by Ben-Zvi, Sakellaridis, and
Venkatesh in
[
4
]
.

Comparing with algebraic groups, the study of such problems for covering groups is still relatively limited. Among the few examples, the
co-period
integrals

\int_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{r}(F){\mathbb{A}}^{\times}\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{r}({\mathbb{A}})}ff_{+}f_{-}(g)dg,\quad f\in\sigma,f_{\pm}\in\sigma_{\pm}

are particularly interesting for its close relation with the symmetric power L-functions. Here
\sigma
is an automorphic
{\mathrm{GL}}_{r}({\mathbb{A}})
-representation and
\sigma_{\pm}
are the exceptional theta series on
n
-th metaplectic cover of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{r}({\mathbb{A}})
(with opposite
\mu_{n}
-action).

For the symmetric square
L
-function

, Shahidi
[
29
]
established its meromorphic continuation and functional equation using the Langlands-Shahidi method. Bump and Ginzburg
[
2
]
subsequently provided its integral representation. Takeda
[
31
]

\chi
.

For the symmetric cube
L
-function

with

[
30
]
. Its integral representation is then provided by Bump-Ginzburg-Hoffstein
[
3
]
, while Kim and Shahidi
[
14
]
showed it is entire unless
\sigma
is monomial. Many other pivotal works exist on symmetric power
L
-functions. Among them, we mention the work of Newton-Thorne on the
modularity of the symmetric power for regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic
representations on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})

[
17
,
18
]
.

Results for co-period integrals in the case

are relatively complete. The local problem is studied by Kable
[
11
]
, Kaplan
[
12
]
and Yamana
[

]
.
For a nonarchimedean place

, the local
{\mathrm{Hom}}
-space is at most one-dimensional for any irreducible admissible unitary representation

\sigma_{v}
on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{r}(F_{v})
. For the global aspect, the celebrated work of Bump-Ginzburg essentially proves that
the co-period integrals are nonvanishing if and only if the symmetric square
L
-function

has a simple pole at

(See
[

). Moreover, there is an Euler decomposition of the co-period integrals in terms of the residue of

at

and the local zeta integrals (for this decomposition, in addition to the work of Bump-Ginzburg mentioned above,
the characterization of local

-factors with respect to local zeta integrals is also needed
[

]

The present article will concentrate on the case
n=3
and

, where only partial results are known. By the work of Ginzburg-Jiang-Rallis
[

, the nonvanishing of global co-period integrals implies that the

Limited to our knowledge, no results for co-period exist in the literature when

and
r\geq 3

In this paper, we conduct a systematic study on the co-period integral in the case
n=3
and
r=2

L
-factors. For the

Subsequently, we furnish full details for the results and proofs presented therein.

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta_{F}(1+s)\zeta_{F}(2+3s)}{\zeta_{F}(2+2s)\zeta_{F}(1+2s)}\big|_{s=0}\prod_{v}\frac{\int_{Z_{*}(F_{v})N(F_{v})\backslash G(F_{v})}f_{s,v}(g)W_{+,v}(g)W_{-,v}(g)dg}{\frac{\zeta_{F_{v}}(1+s)\zeta_{F_{v}}(2+3s)}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2+2s)}}\Bigg|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=\prod_{v}\zeta_{F_{v}}^{-1}(1)\int_{Z_{*}(F_{v})N(F_{v})\backslash G(F_{v})}f_{v}(g)W_{+,v}(g)W_{-,v}(g)dg

\displaystyle=\prod_{v}\zeta_{F_{v}}^{-1}(1)(W_{+,v},W_{-,v})_{v}\int_{K_{v}}f_{v}(k)dk.

Here we use Lemma
2.1
and the
{\widetilde{G}}(F_{v})
-invariance of the pairing

(-,-)_{v}
to deduce the last equality.

By
[
13
, Page 115-116]
, the integration

\int_{Z_{*}({\mathbb{A}})G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}})}\varphi_{+}\varphi_{-}(g)dg=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{Z_{*}({\mathbb{A}})G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}})}\wedge^{T}(\varphi_{+}\otimes\varphi_{-})(g)dg

converges absolutely. Thus we must have
P_{2/3}=P_{4/3}=0
and

C(f)\int_{Z_{*}({\mathbb{A}})G(F)\backslash G({\mathbb{A}})}\varphi_{+}\varphi_{-}(g)dg=\prod_{v}\zeta_{F_{v}}^{-1}(1)(W_{+,v},W_{-,v})_{v}\int_{K_{v}}f_{v}(k)dk.

To deduce the stated formula, note that
f\mapsto C(f)
and
f\mapsto\int_{K}f(k)dk
both lie in the one-dimensional space
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G({\mathbb{A}})}(I_{B({\mathbb{A}})}^{G({\mathbb{A}})}\delta^{1/2},{\mathbb{C}})
, it suffices to do the calculation when
f
is the unique K-invariant function with
f(1)=1
. In this case,
\int_{K}f(k)dk=1
. Using the matrix identities

w\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-x^{-1}&1\\
0&x\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
x^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix}\quad x\neq 0\in F_{v},v<\infty

w\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta_{x}^{-1}&\bar{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\\
0&\Delta_{x}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bar{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}&-\Delta_{x}^{-1}\\
\ \Delta_{x}^{-1}&x\Delta_{x}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\quad x\in{\mathbb{C}},\ \Delta_{x}=\sqrt{1+x\bar{x}},

we can compute that

C(f)=\zeta_{F}^{-1}(2)\prod_{v<\infty}{\mathrm{Vol}}({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{v}},dx_{v}).

Combining all these, we deduce the stated Petersson inner product formula.
∎

Abstract
In the present paper we are dealing with reflection equation algebras ℒ ⁡ ( R ) {\cal{L}}(R) corresponding to even skew-invertible Hecke symmetries. Our main result consists in computing the characters of the spectral values of the generating matrix L L of ℒ ⁡ ( R ) {\cal{L}}(R) in finite-dimensional representations labeled by partitions of integers. As is known, the spectral values belong to an algebraic extension of the center of the reflection equation algebra and elements of the center can be presented as symmetric functions in spectral values. As an application of our approach, we calculate the characters of the power sums Tr R ​ ( L n ) \mathrm{Tr}_{R}(L^{n}) in the mentioned finite dimensional representations. In a particular case of the Drinfeld-Jimbo R R -matrix the enveloping algebra U ⁡ ( g ​ l ​ ( N ) ) U(gl(N)) can be obtained as a specific limit of the reflection equation algebra. In this limit our results for power sums coincide with those obtained in [ PP ] .

1
Introduction

By a quantum matrix we mean the matrix
L
composed of generators of an algebra, associated with two compatible braidings

R
and
F
(see
[
IOP
]
for definitions and details). In the current paper we are dealing with a particular subclass of such algebras,
corresponding to the case
F=R
. Such algebras are called Reflection Equation (RE)
algebras and denoted
{\cal{L}}(R)
. A common feature of each such an algebra is that its generating matrix
L
meets a polynomial relation
with coefficients which are central in
{\cal{L}}(R)
. This relation is a noncommutative analog of the classical Cayley-Hamilton
identity. This property enables us to introduce eigenvalues of the generating matrix
L
as elements of the central extension of the algebra

{\cal{L}}(R)
. The aim of the current paper is to compute the characters of the eigenvalues of the matrix
L
in a family of (conjecturally
irreducible) representations of the algebra
{\cal{L}}(R)
for the case when
R
is an even Hecke symmetry of the bi-rank
(m|0)

(see Section 2). The algebra
{\cal{L}}(R)
corresponding to such a Hecke symmetriy is called the RE algebra of
GL(m)
type.

We illustrate the above constructions on the example of the algebra
U(gl(N))
, which can be obtained as a specific limit
of the RE algebra, associated with
R
coming from the
U_{q}(sl(N))
— the so-called Drinfeld-Jimbo
R
-matrix
(see Section 3).

Let
\hat{l}_{i}^{j}
,
1\leq i,j\leq N
be the standard generators of
U(gl(N))
satisfying the system of commutation relations:

\hat{l}_{i}^{j}\,\hat{l}_{k}^{m}-l_{k}^{m}\,\hat{l}_{i}^{j}=\hat{l}_{i}^{m}\,\delta_{k}^{j}-\hat{l}_{k}^{j}\,\delta_{i}^{m}.

This system can be written in the following matrix form:

P\,(\hat{L}\otimes I)\,P\,(\hat{L}\otimes I)-(\hat{L}\otimes I)\,P\,(\hat{L}\otimes I)\,P=P\,(\hat{L}\otimes I)-(\hat{L}\otimes I)\,P,

(1.1)

where
\hat{L}=\|\hat{l}_{i}^{j}\|_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
,
I
is the
N\times N
identity matrix, and
P
is the
N^{2}\times N^{2}
matrix of the usual flip.
The matrix
\hat{L}
will be called
the generating matrix
of the algebra
U(gl(N))
.

The generating matrix
\hat{L}
is subject to a polynomial relation which is an analog of the usual Cayley-Hamilton identity:

\hat{L}^{N}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(-1)^{k}a_{k}(\hat{L})\,\hat{L}^{N-k}=0,

(1.2)

where the coefficients
a_{k}(\hat{L})
belong to the center
Z(U(gl(N))
of the algebra
U(gl(N))
.

We introduce the
spectral values
(or eigenvalues)
\hat{\mu}_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq N
, of the generating matrix
\hat{L}
in the natural way:

a_{1}(\hat{L})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,\hat{\mu}_{i},\qquad a_{2}(\hat{L})=\sum_{i<j}^{N}\,\hat{\mu}_{i}\,\hat{\mu}_{j},\qquad\dots\qquad a_{N}(\hat{L})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\mu}_{i}.

(1.3)

The eigenvalues
\hat{\mu}_{i}
are assumed to be central in the extended algebra
U(gl(N))[\hat{\mu}_{1},\dots,\hat{\mu}_{N}]
.
Due to definition (
1.3
) the Cayley-Hamilton identity (
1.2
) can be written in a factorized form in the extended algebra:

\prod_{k=1}^{N}(\hat{L}-\hat{\mu}_{k}\,I)=0.

Moreover, any central element of the algebra
U(gl(N))[\hat{\mu}_{1},\dots,\hat{\mu}_{N}]
can be expressed as a symmetric function in the
eigenvalues
\hat{\mu}_{k}
.

It is known that any finite dimensional irreducible representation of the algebra
U(gl(N))
(considered up to isomorphisms) can be
labeled by a partition

{\lambda}=({\lambda}_{1}\geq{\lambda}_{2}\geq\dots\geq{\lambda}_{N}\geq 0).

Since the algebra
gl(N)
is reductive,
the image
\pi_{\lambda}(z)
of any central element
z\in Z(U(gl(N)))
in such a representation
\pi_{\lambda}
is a scalar operator. The values
of the images
\pi_{\lambda}(p_{k})
for the central elements
p_{k}=\mathrm{Tr}\,\hat{L}^{k}
,
k\geq 0
, which are called
power sums
, were
computed in
[
PP
]
.

It is not difficult to assign a value
\pi_{\lambda}(\hat{\mu}_{i})
to each eigenvalue
\hat{\mu}_{i}
, which would be compatible with the values

\pi_{\lambda}(p_{k})
or, what is equivalent, compatible with the values of
\pi_{\lambda}(a_{k})
. In this sense we speak about representations
of the spectrum of the matrix
\hat{L}
.

Let us pass now to a general case. By a modified RE algebra we mean an algebra
\hat{{\cal L}}(R)
, such that its generating matrix
\hat{L}

meets the relation analogous to (
1.1
), where the matrix
P
is replaced by a Hecke symmetry
R
. If the matrix
R
is a
deformation of the usual flip (for instance
R
comes from the Quantum Group
U_{q}(sl(N))
or it is a Crammer-Gervais symmetry),
the algebra
\hat{{\cal L}}(R)
is a deformation of the algebra
U(gl(N))
.

Note that for a Hecke symmetry
R
the modified RE algebra
\hat{{\cal L}}(R)
is isomorphic to the RE algebra
{\cal{L}}(R)
defined by purely
quadratic relations on generators (see Section 3 for detail).

Another important property of the
GL(m)
type RE algebras
\hat{{\cal L}}(R)
is that their representation theory is similar to that of

U(gl(m))
. The corresponding finite dimensional representations
\pi_{\lambda}
are labeled by partitions of integers with height
(the number of nonzero components) no greater than
m
.
Note, that the number
m
is determined by the bi-rank of
R
(see Section 2).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we recall some definitions and properties of the Hecke algebras and their
R
-matrix
representations. Section 3 is devoted to a review of certain properties of the centers of the RE algebras and of
some their finite dimensional representations. The last section contains the main results of the paper: we compute the characters
of the spectral elements of the quantum matrices
L
and
\hat{L}
in the mentioned representations of the RE algebras and show
the limit transition to the classical results of
[
PP
]
. Moreover, for the matrix
R
coming from the quantum group
U_{q}(sl(N))

(in this case
m=N
) our result for the characters of power sums
\mathrm{Tr}_{R}(L^{k})
coincides with that obtained in
[
JLM
]

by different methods.

2
Hecke algebras and Hecke symmetries

In this section we recall some facts on Hecke algebras and Hecke type braidings, called Hecke symmetries, which are essential for
studying the center of RE algebra
{\cal{L}}(R)
and its finite dimensional representations.

The Hecke algebra
{\cal H}_{n}(q)
is an associative unital algebra over the complex field
{{C}}
generated by the finite set of
Artin’s generators
\tau_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n-1
, subject to the relations

\tau_{i}\,\tau_{i+1}\,\tau_{i}=\tau_{i+1}\,\tau_{i}\,\tau_{i+1},\qquad\tau_{i}\,\tau_{j}=\tau_{j}\,\tau_{i}\quad|i-j|\geq 2,

and

(q\,e-\tau_{i})(q^{-1}\,e+\tau_{i})=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1,\qquad q\in{{C}}\setminus 0.

Here
e
is the unit element of the algebra.
Note that for
q=\pm 1
we get the defining relations of the symmetric group algebra
{{C}}[S_{n}]
.

In what follows we assume the parameter
q
to be generic which means that
q^{k}\not=1
,
\forall k\geq 1
. For a generic
q

the Hecke algebra
{\cal H}_{n}(q)
is isomorphic to
{{C}}[S_{n}]
for all
n\geq 1
. Therefore, the Hecke algebra
{\cal H}_{n}(q)

is finite dimensional, semisimple and isomorphic to a direct product of finite dimensional matrix algebras. As a consequence, in the
algebra
{\cal H}_{n}(q)
there exists a linear basis of “matrix units”
\{e^{\lambda}_{ij}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq d_{\lambda}}
parameterized by
all partitions
\lambda
of the integer
n
. Here
d_{\lambda}
is the number of standard Young tables corresponding to the
partition
\lambda
. Note that the multiplication table for these matrix units reads:

e^{\lambda}_{ki}\,e^{\mu}_{rp}=\delta^{{\lambda}\mu}\delta_{ir}\,e^{\lambda}_{kp}.

(2.1)

The elements
e_{i}^{\lambda}:=e^{\lambda}_{ii}
,
\lambda\vdash n
,
1\leq i\leq d_{\lambda}
, are
the primitive orthogonal idempotents
defining
a resolution of the unit
e
:

e=\sum_{\lambda\,\vdash n}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\lambda}}e^{\lambda}_{i}.

(2.2)

An explicit construction of all these idempotents is exhibited in
[
OP
]
(see also the references therein).

A maximal commutative subalgebra of
{\cal H}_{n}(q)
is generated by the so-called
Jucys-Murphy elements

\{j_{k}\}_{1\leq k\leq n}
,
which are defined by the relations:

j_{1}=e,\qquad j_{k}=\tau_{k-1}\,j_{k-1}\tau_{k-1},\quad 2\leq k\leq n.

(2.3)

Let
V
be a finite dimensional complex vector space

\dim_{\,{C}}V=N
. A
braiding
is an operator
R:V^{\otimes 2}\to V^{\otimes 2}
meeting the
braid relation
:

R_{12}R_{23}R_{12}=R_{23}R_{12}R_{23}.

(2.4)

Here
R_{12}=R\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{V}
,
R_{23}=\mathrm{Id}_{V}\otimes R
.

Hereafter, we fix a basis
\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
in the space
V
and we identify operators in spaces

V^{\otimes p}
,
p\geq 1
, with their matrices in the corresponding tensor basis
\{x_{i_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes x_{i_{p}}\}
.

Below we use the following embeddings of the
N^{2}\times N^{2}
matrix
R
into the space of
N^{p}\times N^{p}
matrices:

R_{i\,i+1}=I^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes R\otimes I^{\otimes(p-i-1)},\qquad 1\leq i\leq p-1,

(2.5)

I
being the
N\times N
identity matrix. In what follows we do not fix the integer
p
just assuming it to be sufficiently large in order
all the matrix formulas make sense. Besides, we shall use a shorthand notation
R_{i}
for
R_{ii+1}
.
As can be easily seen the braid relation (
2.4
) takes place for all copies
R_{i}
:

R_{i}R_{i+1}R_{i}=R_{i+1}R_{i}R_{i+1},\quad\forall\,i\geq 1.

(2.6)

A braiding
R
meeting the Hecke condition

(q\,I^{\otimes 2}-R)(q^{-1}I^{\otimes 2}+R)=0,\qquad q\in{{C}}\setminus 0,\quad q\not=\pm 1

(2.7)

is called
a Hecke symmetry
. If
q=\pm 1
, the corresponding braiding
R
is called
an involutive symmetry
.

Any Hecke symmetry
R
defines an
R
-matrix representation
\rho_{R}:{\cal H}_{n}(q)\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes n})

of the Hecke algebras. On Artin’s generators this representation is given by the map:

\rho_{R}(\tau_{k})\mapsto R_{k}:=I^{\otimes(k-1)}\otimes R\otimes I^{\otimes(n-k-1)},\quad 1\leq k\leq n-1.

(2.8)

The images of the Jucys-Murphy elements in the
R
-matrix representation are given by the following matrices:

J_{k}=\rho_{R}(j_{k})=R_{k-1}\dots R_{2}R_{1}^{2}R_{2}\dots R_{k-1}.

(2.9)

Below the matrices
J_{k}
will be also referred to as Jucys-Murphy elements.

With any symmetry
R
one can naturally associate the
R
-symmetric and
R
-skew-symmetric algebras of the space
V

(see
[
G
,
GPS1
]
for definitions). A symmetry
R
is called
even
if the
R
-skew-symmetric algebra is finite-dimensional.
Then the Hilbert-Poincare series of the
R
-skew-symmetric algebra is a polynomial of a degree
m
. In this case
the symmetry
R
is called to be of the bi-rank
(m|0)
(see
[
GPS1
]
for detail).

Moreover, we assume any symmetry
R
to be skew-invertible. This means that there exists an operator

\Psi:V^{\otimes 2}\to V^{\otimes 2}

such that

\mathrm{Tr}_{(2)}(R_{12}\Psi_{23})=P_{13}=\mathrm{Tr}_{(2)}(\Psi_{12}R_{23}).

Below we also use the
N\times N
matrices
B=\|B_{i}^{j}\|
and
C=\|C_{i}^{j}\|
, defined by

B_{i}^{j}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\Psi_{ai}^{aj},\qquad C_{i}^{j}=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\Psi_{ia}^{ja},

(2.10)

where
\|\Psi_{ij}^{ks}\|
is a matrix of the operator
\Psi
in the tensor basis of the space
V^{\otimes 2}
.

Definition 1

Let
M
be an
N\times N
matrix with entries form any complex vector space
U
. Its
R
-trace is defined as
the map
\mathrm{Mat}_{N}(U)\rightarrow U
given by the rule:

{\mathrm{Tr}_{R}}\,M:=\mathrm{Tr}\,(C\cdot M).

(2.11)

Below we use the convenient shorthand notation for the multiple trace:

\langle M_{12\dots k}\rangle_{1\dots k}:=\mathrm{Tr}_{R(12\dots k)}M_{12\dots k}=\mathrm{Tr}_{(12\dots k)}(C_{1}C_{2}\dots C_{k}M_{12\dots k})

where
M_{12\dots k}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{N}(U)^{\otimes k}
is an arbitrary
N^{k}\times N^{k}
matrix and

C_{i}=I^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes C\otimes I^{\otimes(k-i)}
.

The
R
-trace possesses the useful
cyclic property
. Namely, for any polynomial
Q
in matrices
R_{1},\dots,R_{k-1}
and
for an arbitrary matrix
M_{12\dots k}

the following identity takes place:

\langle M_{12\dots k}\,Q(R_{1},\dots,R_{k-1})\rangle_{1\dots k}=\langle Q(R_{1},\dots,R_{k-1})\,M_{12\dots k}\rangle_{1\dots k}.

This cyclic property is an immediate consequence of the following relation:

R_{i}C_{i}C_{i+1}=C_{i}C_{i+1}R_{i},\quad\forall\,i\geq 1.

3
Reflection equation algebra and its basic properties

Now, we define a Reflection Equation (RE) algebra associated with a skew-invertible Hecke symmetry
R
. By definition, an RE
algebra
{\cal{L}}(R)
, corresponding to a given symmetry
R
, is a unital associative algebra finitely generated by the elements

l_{i}^{j}
,
1\leq i,j\leq N
, subject to the quadratic permutation relations:

R\,(L\otimes I)R\,(L\otimes I)-(L\otimes I)R\,(L\otimes I)R=0,\qquad L=\|l_{i}^{j}\|.

(3.1)

Note that the generating matrix
L=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}E_{i}^{j}\otimes l_{i}^{j}
is actually an element of the algebra
\mathrm{End}({{C}}^{N})\otimes{\cal{L}}(R)
, where
E_{i}^{j}
are the matrix units – elements of a linear basis of
\mathrm{End}({{C}}^{N})
.

Let us introduce a new generating matrix
\hat{L}
:

L=Ie_{\cal L}-(q-q^{-1})\hat{L},

(3.2)

where
e_{\cal L}
is the unit element of the algebra
{\cal{L}}(R)
.
As a consequence of (
3.1
) and of the Hecke condition (
2.7
) we get the following quadratic-linear relation for the
matrix
\hat{L}
:

R\,(\hat{L}\otimes I)R\,(\hat{L}\otimes I)-(\hat{L}\otimes I)R\,(\hat{L}\otimes I)R=R\,(\hat{L}\otimes I)-(\hat{L}\otimes I)R,

(3.3)

which is similar to (
1.1
), but with
P
replaced by
R
. The corresponding algebra is called a
modified
RE algebra
and is denoted
\hat{{\cal L}}(R)
.
Evidently, the algebras
{\cal{L}}(R)
and
\hat{{\cal L}}(R)
are isomorphic to each other for
q\not=\pm 1
.

Proposition 3

The power sums of both kinds and elementary symmetric polynomials meet the following identities for
\forall\,n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle n_{q}a_{n}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}q^{n-k}a_{n-k}\,p_{k}=0

\displaystyle n_{q}a_{n}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}q^{k-n}a_{n-k}\,t_{k}=0

\displaystyle p_{n}=t_{n}+(q-q^{-1})\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}p_{n-k}\,t_{k}.

(3.9)

In the above identities one should take into account that for Hecke symmetry with bi-rank
(m|0)
the elementary
symmetric polynomials

if

Proof.
The first and the second set of identities are called the quantum Newton relations, the proof of the first one can be found in
[
GPS3
]
, the second set of identities can be proved in a similar way. We prove the third set of identities.

For this purpose we use the Hecke condition
R=R^{-1}+\omega I^{\otimes 2}
(hereafter we use the notation

)
and transform step by step the

Thus, we get the following:

\displaystyle p_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle L_{\overline{1\rightarrow n}}R_{n-1}\dots R_{1}\rangle_{1\dots n}=\langle L_{\overline{1\rightarrow n}}R_{n-1}^{-1}R_{n-2}\dots R_{1}\rangle_{1\dots n}+\omega\langle L_{\overline{1\rightarrow n-1}}R_{n-2}\dots R_{1}L_{\overline{n}}\rangle_{1\dots n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle L_{\overline{1\rightarrow n}}R_{n-1}^{-1}R_{n-2}\dots R_{1}\rangle_{1\dots n}+\omega\,p_{n-1}\,t_{1}=\langle L_{\overline{1\rightarrow n}}R_{n-1}^{-1}R_{n-2}^{-1}R_{n-3}\dots R_{1}\rangle_{1\dots n}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\omega\langle L_{\overline{1\rightarrow n-2}}R_{n-3}\dots R_{1}\rangle_{1\dots n-2}\langle L_{\overline{n-1}}L_{\overline{n}}R_{n-1}^{-1}\rangle_{n-1\,n}+\omega\,p_{n-1}\,t_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle L_{\overline{1\rightarrow n}}R_{n-1}^{-1}R_{n-2}^{-1}R_{n-3}\dots R_{1}\rangle_{1\dots n}+\omega(\,p_{n-2}\,t_{2}+p_{n-1}\,t_{1})=\dots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{n}+\omega\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}p_{n-k}t_{k}.

Lemma 11

The following identities hold true:

P_{k}^{\,n}=P_{k-1}^{\,n}+\omega nJ_{k}^{n}+\omega^{2}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n-1}sP^{\,n-s}_{k}J_{k}^{s},

(4.9)

T_{k}^{\,n}=T_{k-1}^{\,n}-\omega n(q^{2m}J_{k})^{-n}+\omega^{2}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n-1}sT_{k}^{\,n-s}(q^{2m}J_{k})^{-s}.

(4.10)

Proof.
Take into account the matrix equality:

R_{k}J_{k+1}=R_{k}^{2}J_{k}R_{k}=J_{k}R_{k}+\omega J_{k+1},

where the Hecke condition
R_{k}^{2}=I^{\otimes 2}+\omega\,R_{k}
was applied.

Using this equality as the base of induction in
n
, it is not difficult to prove the matrix identity:

R_{k}J_{k+1}^{n}=J_{k}^{n}R_{k}+\omega\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}J_{k+1}^{s}J_{k}^{n-s}.

(4.11)

We apply the identity (
4.11
) in order to transform the matrix power
J_{k+1}^{n}
:

\displaystyle J_{k+1}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{k}J_{k}(R_{k}J_{k+1}^{n-1})=R_{k}J_{k}^{n}R_{k}+\omega\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n-1}R_{k}J_{k+1}^{s}J_{k}^{n-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{k}J_{k}^{n}R_{k}+\omega\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n-1}J_{k}^{s}R_{k}J_{k}^{n-s}+\omega^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\sum_{r=1}^{s}J_{k}^{n-r}J_{k+1}^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{k}J_{k}^{n}R_{k}+\omega\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n-1}J_{k}^{s}R_{k}J_{k}^{n-s}+\omega^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}sJ_{k}^{s}J_{k+1}^{n-s}.

In the last line of the above transformations we use the Hecke condition in the form
R_{k}=R_{k}^{-1}+\omega I^{\otimes 2}

in order to rewrite the frist term:

R_{k}J_{k}^{n}R_{k}=R_{k}J_{k}^{n}R_{k}^{-1}+\omega R_{k}J_{k}^{n}

Then, applying the
R
-trace in the
(k+1)
-th space we get (
4.9
). In so doing, for the first term we use an important
property of the
R
-trace:

\langle R_{k}X(R)_{1\dots k}R_{k}^{-1})\rangle_{k+1}=\langle X(R)_{1\dots k}\rangle_{k}

valid for any matrix
X(R)
which does not contain indices of the
(k+1)
-th space.

The identity (
4.10
) can be proved in the same way by transforming the matrix power
J_{k+1}^{-n}
and applying
the
R
-trace in the
(k+1)
-th space at the end of calculations.

Proof of the Theorem
8
.

We are going to prove that the values of characters
\chi_{\lambda}(t_{n})
and
\chi^{*}_{\lambda}(p_{n})

corresponding to the restrictions of the linear operators
t_{n}\triangleright
and
p_{n}\triangleright
on
{\cal{L}}(R)
-modules

V^{\lambda}_{a}
and
V^{*\lambda}_{a}
respectively read as follows:

t_{n}\,\triangleright_{\,\rule{0.4pt}{11.38109pt}{\,V^{\lambda}_{a}}}=\chi_{\lambda}(t_{n})\mathrm{Id}_{\,V^{\lambda}_{a}},\qquad\chi_{\lambda}(t_{n})=t_{n}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m})_{\,\rule{0.4pt}{11.38109pt}{\,\,\mu_{i}=q^{-2({\lambda}_{i}-i+m)}}},

(4.12)

p_{n}\,\triangleright_{\,\rule{0.4pt}{11.38109pt}{\,V^{*\lambda}_{a}}}=\chi_{\lambda}^{*}(p_{n})\mathrm{Id}_{\,V^{\lambda}_{a}},\qquad\chi_{\lambda}^{*}(p_{n})=p_{n}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m})_{\,\rule{0.4pt}{11.38109pt}{\,\,\mu_{i}=q^{2({\lambda}_{i}-i+1)}}}.

(4.13)

Here the polynomials
p_{n}(\mu)
and
t_{n}(\mu)
are defined in (
3.10
) and (
3.15
).

Note that the spectral parameterizations (
3.10
) and (
3.15
) of the power sums are in one-to-one
correspondence with the parametrerization (
3.8
). This is a consequence of the fact that the sets

\{a_{k}(L)\}_{1\leq k\leq m}
and
\{p_{k}(L)\}_{1\leq k\leq m}
are the generating sets of the characteristic subalgebra
and, therefore, the values of power sums defines the values of
\mu_{i}
up to an ordering. So,
if the relations (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) are indeed fulfilled, then it leads to the conclusion that above characters
of spectral values solve the defining system (
3.8
).

We will prove (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) by double induction in the parameter
k=\sum_{i}{\lambda}_{i}
parameterizing
the tensor power of the representation space, and for each
k
we realize the internal induction in
n
parameterizing the
order of the power sums
p_{n}(L)
and
t_{n}(L)
.

According to Lemma
10
and definitions
(\ref{Vk-decom})
,
(\ref{Vdu-decom})
the matrices of operators

t_{n}\triangleright
and
p_{n}\triangleright
restricted to the
{\cal{L}}(R)
-invariant subspaces
V^{\lambda}_{a}
and

V^{*\lambda}_{a}
are respectively
T_{k}^{\,n}E_{a}^{\lambda}(R)
and
P_{k}^{\,n}E_{a}^{\lambda}(R)
. Here

E_{a}^{{\lambda}}(R)=\rho_{R}(e_{aa}^{\lambda})
is an idempotent, corresponding to the diagram
{\lambda}\vdash k
(we
use the same term for the idempotent of the Hecke algebra and for its matrix image in the
R
-matrix representation).

Thus, as the step of induction, we assume that the matrix product of
P^{n}_{k-1}
or
T^{n}_{k-1}
and the idempotent
corresponding to the Young table with omitted box with number
k
as well as the matrix products

P^{l}_{k}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}(R)
or
T^{l}_{k}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}(R)
with
l<n
have the necessary form (
4.12
) or

(\ref{PnAct})
.

In the standard Young table corresponding to the idempotent
E_{a}^{\lambda}
the largest number
k
is placed at the
ending box of some row. Let
i_{0}
be the number of this row. Then the content of the corresponding box is

c(k)={\lambda}_{i_{0}}-i_{0}
,
and consequently
J_{k}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}=q^{2({\lambda}_{i_{0}}-i_{0})}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}
(see
[
OP
]
for detail).

Using this consideration and Lemma
11
, we get

T_{k}^{n}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}=T_{k-1}^{n}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}-\omega n(q^{-2({\lambda}_{i_{0}}-i_{0}+m)})^{n}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}+\omega^{2}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n-1}s(q^{-2({\lambda}_{i_{0}}-i_{0}+m)})^{s}T_{k}^{n-s}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}

(4.14)

and

P_{k}^{n}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}=P_{k-1}^{n}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}+\omega n(q^{2({\lambda}_{i_{0}}-i_{0})})^{n}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}+\omega^{2}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n-1}s(q^{2({\lambda}_{i_{0}}-i_{0})})^{i}P_{k}^{n-s}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}.

(4.15)

Next, the relations (
4.4
), (
4.5
) on the generating functions lead to the following
identities for the power sums:

t_{n}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m})=t_{n}(\mu_{1},\dots,q^{2}\mu_{i_{0}},\dots,\mu_{m})-\omega n\mu_{i_{0}}^{n}+\omega^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}s\mu_{i_{0}}^{s}t_{n-s}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}),

(4.16)

and

\displaystyle p_{n}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{n}(\mu_{1},\dots,q^{-2}\mu_{i_{0}},\dots,\mu_{m})

(4.17)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\omega n(q^{-2}\mu_{i_{0}})^{n}+\omega^{2}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n-1}s(q^{-2}\mu_{i_{0}})^{s}p_{n-s}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}).

Compare now the formulas (
4.14
) and (
4.16
). First, we point out that
the right hand side of (
4.14
) equals to
E_{a}^{\lambda}(R)
multiplied by a scalar factor. By definition this factor
is a character
\chi_{\lambda}(t_{n})
since at the left hand side of (
4.14
) we have the matrix of
t_{n}\triangleright

restricted on
V^{\lambda}_{a}
. Indeed, the matrices
T^{\,n-s}_{k}E_{a}^{\lambda}
are of the form
\chi_{\lambda}(t_{n-s})

by assumption of induction, the same is true for the first term
T_{k-1}^{n}E_{a}^{\lambda}(R)
. The only subtle point is that
the product of
T_{k-1}^{n}E_{a}^{{\lambda}}(R)
contains the character corresponding to the table with eliminated
box with number
k
, which, in turn, equivalent to the change of
\mu_{i_{0}}
to
q^{2}\mu_{i_{0}}
. But from the other hand,
having represented the central elements of algebraic identity (
4.16
) in the module
V^{\lambda}_{a}
, we get the same
connection among their characters. So, we conclude that the formula (
4.12
) indeed takes place.

For polynomials
p_{n}
the consideration is the same, except for the change of
\mu
corresponding to eliminated box with
the number
k
: one should substitute
q^{-2}\mu_{i_{0}}
instead of
\mu_{i_{0}}
.

So, the induction step is proved and it remains to chek a few particular cases forming the base of induction.

If
n=1
we get the relation (see
[
GPS1
]
)

T_{k}^{1}E^{{\lambda}}_{a}=\langle J_{k+1}^{-1}\rangle_{k+1}E^{{\lambda}}_{a}=\frac{1}{q}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{m}q^{-2({\lambda}_{s}-s+m)}E^{{\lambda}}_{a}.

Also we use the formula
P_{k}^{1}=\langle J_{k+1}\rangle_{k+1}=\frac{m_{q}}{q^{m}}+\omega\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}J_{i}
.
Multipluing by the idempotent
E_{a}^{\lambda}(R)
we find:

\displaystyle P_{k}^{1}E^{{\lambda}}_{a}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\,\frac{m_{q}}{q^{m}}+\omega\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}q^{2c(i)}\Big)E^{{\lambda}}_{a}(R)=\Big(\,\frac{m_{q}}{q^{m}}+\omega\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}q^{2(1-i)}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{{\lambda}_{i}-1}q^{2j}\Big)E^{{\lambda}}_{a}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\,\frac{m_{q}}{q^{m}}-\frac{1}{q}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}q^{2(1-i)}+\frac{1}{q}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}q^{2(\lambda_{i}-i+1)}\Big)E^{\lambda}_{a}(R)=\frac{1}{q}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}q^{2({\lambda}_{i}-i+1)}E^{{\lambda}}_{a}(R).

Thus, we get a conclusion for
t_{1}
and
p_{1}
, that is, the base of the internal induction in the parameter
n
(the order of
the power sums) is established.

Now let us check the base of external induction, that is
k=0
while
n
is arbitrary. In this case
T_{0}^{\,n}
is calculated easily:

T_{0}^{\,n}=q^{-2m(n-1)}\langle J_{1}^{n}\rangle_{1}=q^{m(1-2n)}m_{q}.

Since

then
\lambda=0
and the characters of the eigenvalues read

. Omitting
for simplicity the notation

we see that

,

. As a consequence, the multiplicities

in (

) are all equal to zero except for

\tilde{d}_{1}=q\prod_{i=2}^{m}\frac{q^{2(1-m)}-q^{2(i+1-m)}}{q^{2(1-m)}-q^{2(i-m)}}=q^{m}m_{q}\quad\Rightarrow\quad T_{0}^{\,n}=(q^{-2}\mu_{1})^{n}\tilde{d}_{1}=q^{m(1-2n)}m_{q}.

As for

the verification of the base of induction is also straightforward. For

and an arbitrary

the
characterss of spectral values

, so in virtue of analogous property

all the multiplicities

.

Corollary 12

On applying to the spectral parameterization (
3.10
) the results of Theorem
8
one gets the characters
of the power sums represented in

and
V^{*\lambda}_{a}
with the actions of generators (

)

\chi_{\lambda}(p_{n})=q^{-m(2n+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}q^{-2n\ell_{i}}\prod_{j\not=i}\frac{(\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}-1)_{q}}{(\ell_{i}-\ell_{j})_{q}},

\chi^{*}_{\lambda}(p_{n})=q^{2n-m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}q^{2n\ell_{i}}\prod_{j\not=i}\frac{(\ell_{i}-\ell_{j}+1)_{q}}{(\ell_{i}-\ell_{j})_{q}},

where
\ell_{i}=\lambda_{i}-i
and a
q
-number is defined as
n_{q}=(q^{n}-q^{-n})/(q-q^{-1})

\forall\,n\in{{Z}}
.

]

In conclusion, we extract from our formulas the classical results obtained in
[
PP
]
. At first, we have to pass to the generating
matrix
\hat{L}
of the corresponding modified RE algebras
\hat{{\cal L}}(R)
by the shift of generators (
3.2
).
The matrix
\hat{L}
is also subject to a version of the CH identity. It can be readily obtained from (
1.2
) and (
3.2
).
Let
\hat{\mu}_{i}
be the eigenvalues of the characteristic polynomial for the matrix
\hat{L}
. They are related to the eigenvalues of the
matrix
L
by the formula

\mu_{i}=1-\omega\hat{\mu}_{i}.

This relation enables us to compute the quantities
\chi_{\lambda}({\hat{\mu}_{i}})
. Namely, we have

\chi_{\lambda}({\hat{\mu}_{i}})=\frac{1-q^{-2\,({\lambda}_{i}+m-i)}}{q-q^{-1}}=q^{-(\lambda_{i}+m-i)}(\lambda_{i}+m-i)_{q},\quad 1\leq i\leq m.

The multiplicities
d_{i}(\hat{\mu})
read as follows:

d_{i}(\hat{\mu})=q^{-1}\prod_{j\not=1}\frac{\hat{\mu}_{i}-\hat{\mu}_{j}-q^{-1}}{\hat{\mu}_{i}-\hat{\mu}_{j}}.

If
R\to P
as
q\to 1
, then by passing to the limit
q\rightarrow 1
we obtain the quantities

\chi_{\lambda}(\hat{\mu}_{k})
and
d_{i}(\chi_{\lambda}(\hat{\mu}))
for the matrix
\hat{L}
generating the algebra
U(gl(N))
(in this case
m=N
):

\chi_{\lambda}({\hat{\mu}_{i}})={\lambda}_{i}-i+N,\quad d_{i}(\hat{\mu})=\prod_{j\not=i}\frac{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+j-i-1)}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+j-i)},\quad 1\leq i\leq N.

Thus, we recover the result of
[
PP
]
for
\mathrm{Tr}\hat{L}^{n}=\sum_{i}\hat{\mu}_{i}^{n}d_{i}(\hat{\mu})
(though, in the cited paper
the notion of the eigenvalues of the matrix
\hat{L}
was not used).

Spatial and temporal distribution of arsenic concentration in rural drinking water and health risk assessment in Northern China from 2013 to 2022: a case study of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region
Authors: Wen-Yu Zhang, Xiang-Nan Zhang, Chen-Guang Zhang, Xin-Rui Jia, Huan Li, Xuan-Zhi Yue, Duo-Duo Wu, Jia-Ke Zhu, Wen-Qian Zhang, Yu-Han Qin, Yao-Chun Fan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBy monitoring arsenic levels in rural drinking water in Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region from 2013 to 2022 and evaluating their health risks, this study provides a basis for further developing strategies to promote public health.MethodsOne stable centralized water supply point was randomly selected in each township of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region. One finished water sample and 1–3 tap water samples were collected at each supply point. Water samples were collected once during the dry season (May) and once during the rainy season (August-September). Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare arsenic concentrations in drinking water from different types of water sources, and Kruskal-Wallis test was used to compare arsenic concentrations in drinking water across different years. Environmental health risk assessment was conducted using the health risk assessment model recommended by the US Environmental Protection Agency (USEPA).ResultsOverall, arsenic concentrations in rural drinking water were higher in the central-western part of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region compared to the eastern part. From 2013 to 2022, there was a notable decreasing trend in arsenic concentrations in rural drinking water, with over 98% of water samples meeting arsenic standards by 2022. During 2013–2019, arsenic concentrations in drinking water sourced from groundwater were consistently higher than those from surface water sources (P < 0.05). Hazard quotient (HQ) values for the entire population were below 1, and lifetime cancer risk (LCR) values exceeded 1 × 10− 6. Sensitivity analysis indicated that drinking water arsenic concentration contributed the most to health risks for the population.ConclusionDuring 2013–2022, through concerted efforts by the government and the people, excessive arsenic levels in rural drinking water have been significantly reduced, resulting in decreased health risks for the population. However, some carcinogenic risks still exist.Therefore, the next critical step in improving water quality in the region involves further optimizing methods such as coagulation, adsorption, or ion exchange to remove arsenic from drinking water.Keywords: Arsenic, Drinking water, Health risk assessment, Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region

Introduction

Arsenic is a metalloid chemical that exhibits both metallic and non-metallic properties, and it is widely present in different forms in the human living environment [1–5]. In water, it is usually found in the forms of arsenite and arsenate, as well as trivalent and pentavalent inorganic arsenic compounds [6]. It is estimated that approximately 94 to 220 million people worldwide may be exposed to high levels of arsenic from their drinking water sources, such as high arsenic concentrations in groundwater [7]. A 2013 study on arsenic contamination in Chinese groundwater indicated that around 19.6 million people were affected by arsenic-contaminated drinking water from groundwater sources [8]. Long-term exposure to arsenic and its compounds can lead to various diseases. Arsenic and inorganic arsenic compounds are classified as Group 1 carcinogens by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), a specialized cancer agency of the World Health Organization [9]. A systematic review has linked arsenic concentration in drinking water to the incidence of urological cancers [10]. Studies in the Indian region suggest that long-term exposure to low to moderate levels of arsenic in drinking water may be a potential risk factor for gallbladder cancer [11]. Some research also suggests that arsenic may indirectly promote cancer, with arsenic-induced tumor cell-platelet aggregation (TCPA) potentially playing a crucial role in this process [12]. In addition to its potential to cause cancer, research has proposed a link between long-term low-dose exposure to inorganic arsenic in drinking water and the incidence of diabetes [13, 14], as well as an increase in the prevalence of hypertension related to arsenic exposure [15]. As the harmful effects of arsenic on the human body are increasingly recognized, scholars worldwide have conducted research on the relationship between arsenic in drinking water and human health. For example, Hidayatullah M et al. [16] monitored the arsenic content in drinking water in schools in Multan, Pakistan, and conducted a health risk assessment related to potential childhood cancer. Nurfatehar R et al. [17] conducted an epidemiological survey on arsenic levels and arsenic poisoning in drinking water and human hair in a specific region of Malaysia. Lingqian X et al. [18] evaluated the relationship between arsenic exposure in food and drinking water and hypertension. Farzan Shohreh F et al. [19] studied the relationship between arsenic exposure in drinking water in Bangladesh and the occurrence of endothelial dysfunction in adolescents. Recent studies have found that in Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, there are still instances of non-compliant water quality in domestic drinking water, with higher arsenic concentrations being a significant issue, particularly in rural areas [20, 21]. Therefore, it is necessary to conduct a health risk assessment of arsenic in rural drinking water in Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region.

Materials and methods

Study area

Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region is located in the northern border area of China, stretching across the northeastern, northern, and northwestern regions and sharing a border of over 4200 km with Russia and Mongolia, the geographical location overview of the study area is shown in Fig. 1. The majority of the region is situated at an altitude of over 1000 m, with the Inner Mongolia Plateau being the second largest plateau in China. Apart from plateaus, the area also includes mountains, hills, and plains, with distribution of forests, grasslands, and deserts. Due to its unique topographical distribution and geographical location, the region has a diverse and complex climate dominated by a temperate continental monsoon climate. The annual precipitation in Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region ranges from 100 to 500 millimeters, and the complex and diverse natural environment leads to an extremely uneven distribution of water resources. The central and western regions, which contribute two-thirds of the region’s total economic output, have only one-fifth of the region’s water resources [22].Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region is a typical area with high arsenic groundwater distribution [23]. In the central and western regions of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, saline-alkali lands are widespread, where arsenic tends to accumulate within the soil’s salt content. The release of arsenic is facilitated by rainfall and irrigation, particularly in the closed basins formed by the Hetao Plain, where arsenic is less susceptible to dilution and runoff. The geological structure of the Huabao Plain resembles that of the Hetao Plain. In the eastern region of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, alkaline water systems with high pH values promote the release of arsenic from iron-containing minerals, soils, and sediments which may have varying impacts on water quality for drinking purposes [24].

Monitoring sites of rural drinking water in Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region

Water sample collection and analysis

In each township of the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, a centralized water supply point with a stable water source was randomly selected. At each supply point, one sample of finished water(The water that has undergone the treatment process by the centralized water supply unit and is about to enter the distribution water pipeline network.) and 1–3 samples of tap water(The finished water that has been transported through the distribution water pipeline network from the water treatment plant to the user’s faucet.) were collected. Water samples were collected once during the dry season (May) and once during the wet season (August to September). From 2013 to 2022, the sampling points within the study area covered 49, 74, 74, 75, 78, 78, 100, 98, 100, and 100 counties, with the number of drinking water samples collected being 3,925, 3,129, 3,466, 3,892, 4,066, 4,344, 4,870, 4,910, 5,044, and 5,065, respectively. Over these ten years, a total of 42,695 samples were obtained, as shown in Table 1. Arsenic in the water samples was analyzed using the hydride generation atomic fluorescence method in compliance with China’s “Standard Examination Methods for Drinking Water—Metal Indicators” GB/T 5750.6—2006. The permissible standard for arsenic in drinking water, as per the “Hygienic Standard for Drinking Water” GB 5749 − 2006 of China, is 0.01 mg/L.

Risk assessment method

This study uses the health risk assessment model recommended by the United States Environmental Protection Agency (USEPA) to evaluate the carcinogenic and non-carcinogenic risks of arsenic in rural drinking water from 2013 to 2022 in Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region. The assessment process comprised hazard identification, dose-response assessment, exposure assessment, and risk characterization, which were used to evaluate the health risks of arsenic in drinking water in terms of its carcinogenic and non-carcinogenic effects. The carcinogenic slope factor (SF) and reference dose (RfD) for arsenic were obtained from the Integrated Risk Information System (IRIS) of the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency. The parameters for exposure, such as intake rate (IR), body weight (BW), and exposure time (ET), were obtained from the “Chinese Population Exposure Parameters Manual (Adult Volume)“ [25] and the “Chinese Population Exposure Parameters Manual (Children Volume) Summary“ [26]. The risk calculation formulas, as shown in Formulas(1–4), were used following the lifetime cancer risk (LCR) and non-carcinogenic hazard quotient (HQ) calculation methods outlined in the Risk Assessment Guidance for Superfund by the USEPA. The relevant parameters for calculating health risks are presented in Table 2. The lifetime cancer risk (LCR) and non-carcinogenic risk of arsenic in drinking water were assessed based on the risk assessment standards set by the USEPA.

Statistical analysis

Data organization and statistical analysis were conducted using Excel and SPSS 26. According to the results of the Shapiro-Wilk test, the distribution of arsenic concentrations in drinking water from 2013 to 2022 does not conform to a normal distribution. Therefore, using the 50th percentile (P50) and the 95th percentile (P95) of the samples to describe these data.The Mann-Whitney U test was utilized to compare the arsenic concentrations in drinking water from different types of water sources in rural areas, pairwise of arsenic in drinking water across different years were conducted using the Kruskal-Wallis test. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Descriptive results

The distribution of arsenic concentration in drinking water in rural areas of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region from 2013 to 2022 shows that the arsenic concentration in the western region (97°12′E—113°0′E) was consistently higher across the entire survey area. In 2014 and 2015, there was a slight increase in arsenic concentration in some areas in the eastern region (118°0′E-124°0′E). Over the course of the survey period, there was a noticeable decreasing trend in the arsenic concentration in drinking water across the entire study area, with less than 1% of samples exceeding the standard by 2022. Spatiotemporal distribution of arsenic in rural drinking water in Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region over the past 10 years

Non-compliance of drinking water

From 2013 to 2022, over 90% of the samples from the entire study area met the drinking water standards for arsenic concentration (≤ 0.01 mg/L), with the percentage reaching over 98% by 2022. Although the number of compliant samples showed a slight decrease in 2015 and 2018 compared to the previous year, the percentage of samples meeting the standard showed an increasing trend overall. The proportion of samples exceeding the standard by up to 10 times and over 10 times both demonstrated a decreasing trend, with no samples exceeding the standard by over 10 times by 2022. Sensitivity analysis based on monte carlo simulation

A sensitivity analysis based on 10,000 iterations of Monte Carlo simulation using Crystal Ball 11.1 software was conducted to assess the impact of changes in parameters such as arsenic concentration in drinking water, body weight, daily water intake, and exposure frequency on the health effects. The sensitivity analysis results are shown in Fig. 4. The arsenic concentration in drinking water was found to be the most significant factor contributing to health risk for both adults and children, with a contribution rate of over 50% for adult health risk in 2017 and over 60% for overall health risk in other years. The daily water intake (IR) for adults contributed to health risk to a lesser extent than the arsenic concentration, with a contribution rate of over 20% in 2017 and 2021. In children, body weight was the second most contributing factor to health risk, with a contribution rate of over 15% in 2017 and 2021. Additionally, it was noticed that the contribution of body weight to risk values for the entire population was negative, indicating that individuals with lower body weight faced higher health risks compared to those with higher body weight.

Discussion

The distribution of arsenic concentration in drinking water in Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region from 2013 to 2022 reveals that the situation of arsenic in drinking water is worse in the central and western regions compared to the eastern region. A survey on the distribution of endemic arsenicosis in China localities indicates that the endemic arsenicosis areas in Inner Mongolia mainly lie within the plateau range from Hohhot to Alxa League [27], which is roughly consistent with the location of the high arsenic content in drinking water in the central and western regions in this study. This suggests a potential connection between the high arsenic concentration in drinking water in the central and western regions of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region and the naturally high arsenic geological environment. Results from a study in Nepal demonstrate a close correlation between the arsenic content in drinking water sources and in the human body [28], indicating that long-term consumption of water with high arsenic content may be one of the primary causes of arsenic poisoning [29]. The region’s low-lying terrain, stagnant water flow, formation of wetlands, and small particle sediment, which mostly consist of sticky sand and fine silt, favor the precipitation of substances in the sediment. Over an extended period of time, through the combined action of organic matter and microorganisms, arsenic is released from the sediment into the water [30–33].

In rural areas of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, the non-carcinogenic risks posed by arsenic in drinking water to the general population over a period of 10 years were consistently below 1, indicating that the non-carcinogenic effects of arsenic exposure through drinking water are generally within acceptable limits. The carcinogenic risks of arsenic in drinking water to the general population exceeded 1 × 10− 6 over the decade, with some periods showing LCR values surpassing 1 × 10− 4, indicating higher carcinogenic risk. Extensive studies have confirmed that arsenic exposure can lead to cancers in various human systems and organs [10, 34–39]. The elevated carcinogenic risk associated with arsenic in drinking water suggests irreversible harm from arsenic and its inorganic compounds to the population. Water quality in the study area has not yet reached optimal levels and requires further improvement.

The results of the sensitivity analysis show that the contribution rates of arsenic concentration in drinking water, body weight, daily water intake, and exposure frequency to health risk are quite similar for adult women and men, with the contribution of daily water intake and exposure frequency being greater than body weight. For children, the contribution rate of body weight is higher than that of daily water intake and exposure frequency. The contribution rates of arsenic concentration in drinking water, daily water intake, and exposure frequency to health risk are positive, while the contribution rate of body weight is negative, indicating a greater health risk from arsenic in drinking water for individuals with higher arsenic concentration, greater daily water intake, more frequent exposure, and lower body weight.

Over the decade, the number of drinking water samples has increased from 3925 in 2013 to 5056 in 2022, providing broader coverage and making the sample data more representative of the actual situation.

Since the government introduced the targeted poverty alleviation policy in 2015, significant progress has been made in addressing poverty and related social issues, particularly in improving the quality of rural drinking water. Through a series of measures, the government has effectively enhanced the quality of rural drinking water, leading to an overall increase in the rate of drinking water quality compliance and a reduction in the number of exceedances. In 2020, during the ‘sprint phase’ of poverty alleviation efforts, the government expedited the installation of arsenic removal equipment in various large and small water supply facilities on a large scale to ensure that the arsenic content in drinking water meets health standards, resulting in a substantial improvement in rural drinking water safety within a short period. Subsequently, the government has maintained the stability of existing policies and implementation, and water quality has remained in a good and stable state. Therefore, this might explain why there is no statistically significant difference in the arsenic content of drinking water in 2022 compared to 2020 and 2021, but there is a significant difference compared to the period from 2013 to 2019.

Although compliance rates dipped in certain years compared to the preceding ones, this was likely due to increased sampling points revealing previously unexamined areas with higher arsenic levels, rather than a decline in water quality.

Conclusion

Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region is a typical high-arsenic region in China. However, through ten years of concerted efforts by the government and the people, excessive arsenic levels in drinking water in rural areas have been significantly improved. Instances of areas with high concentration values have also notably decreased, thereby reducing health risks to the population. Nevertheless, some carcinogenic risks still exist. Therefore, the next critical step in improving water quality in the region involves further optimizing methods such as coagulation, adsorption, or ion exchange to remove arsenic from drinking water. Additionally, it is necessary to continue monitoring arsenic levels in rural drinking water, track the continuous changes in arsenic concentrations, and further tailor efforts to improve water quality in Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region. Consideration should also be given to adjusting arsenic standards for drinking water hygiene optimization.

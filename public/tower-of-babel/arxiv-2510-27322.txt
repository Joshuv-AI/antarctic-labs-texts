1.
Introduction

Let
D=\{d_{0},d_{1},\cdots,d_{N-1}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a finite digit set with the cardinality
\#D=N
, and let
\{M_{k}\}_{k=0}^{N-1}
be a sequence of expanding matrices of
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. The
iterated function system
(IFS) is defined as

\tau_{k}(x)=M_{k}^{-1}(x+d_{k}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad 0\leq k\leq N-1.

(1.1)

By Hutchinson
[
15
]
, there exists a unique probability measure
\mu
satisfying

\mu(\cdot)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mu\circ\tau_{k}^{-1}(\cdot).

(1.2)

This measure
\mu
is supported on a unique nonempty compact set

T=\bigcup_{k=0}^{N-1}\tau_{k}(T)
, and is known as a
self-affine measure
. In particular, when each matrix
M_{k}
is a scalar multiple of an orthonormal matrix,
\mu
is called a
self-similar measure
. For simplicity, when all matrices
M_{k}
in (
1.1
) are identical (i.e.,
M_{k}=M
), we denote the corresponding measure by
\mu_{M^{-1},D}
.

In this paper, we primarily investigate the spectrality of a class of self-similar measures in
\mathbb{R}
.
We say that a measure
\mu
is a
spectral measure
if the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mu)
contains an orthogonal basis consisting of exponential functions
\{e^{2\pi i\langle\lambda,x\rangle}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
. In such case,

\Lambda
is referred to as a
spectrum
of
\mu
. In harmonic analysis, the investigation of spectral properties of Borel measures has emerged as a central research direction, particularly in understanding their Fourier-analytic characteristics. If
\mu
is a spectral measure, then every function
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
can be represented through a nontrivial Fourier series expansion with respect to an orthogonal basis of exponential functions.
The interest in researching the spectrality of self-affine measures can be traced back to the seminal work of Jorgensen and Pedersen
[
16
]
in 1998. They demonstrated that the middle-fourth Cantor measure is a spectral measure, whereas the middle-third Cantor measure is nonspectral. This discovery spurred extensive research into the spectrality of singular measures. In
\mathbb{R}
, one influential class of measures is the Bernoulli convolution measure
\mu_{\rho,\{0,1\}}
with
0<\rho<1
. Their spectrality was initially investigated by Hu and Lau
[
14
]
, and later, Dai
et al.

[
5
,
6
]
provided a complete characterization, extending the results to the more general

N
-Bernoulli convolution measures
\mu_{\rho,\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}}
.
In higher dimensions, several interesting examples of spectral self-affine measures have been studied, including Sierpinski-type measures
[
7
,
20
]
and Cantor-dust-type measures
[
4
]
.

Given a finite set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the uniform discrete measure
\delta_{E}=\frac{1}{\#E}\sum_{e\in E}\delta_{e}
, where
\delta_{e}
denotes the Dirac measure at the point
e\in E
. The self-affine measure
\mu_{B^{-1},R}
is well known to admit a representation as an infinite convolution of discrete measures:

\mu_{B^{-1},R}=\delta_{B^{-1}R}*\delta_{B^{-2}R}*\cdots*\delta_{B^{-n}R}*\cdots.

If the matrix
B
and the digit set
R
are allowed to vary at each iteration, we obtain a more general infinite convolution measure

\displaystyle\delta_{B_{1}^{-1}R_{1}}*\delta_{B_{1}^{-1}B_{2}^{-1}R_{2}}*\cdots*\delta_{B_{1}^{-1}B_{2}^{-1}\cdots B_{n}^{-1}R_{n}}*\cdots,

(1.3)

where
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of invertible matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\{R_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a sequence of digit sets. If the infinite convolution in (
1.3
) converges in a weak sense, we denote the weak limit by
\mu_{\{B_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}}
and refer to it as a
Moran measure
.
The spectrality of Moran measures enrich our understanding of spectral measures, and remain an active area of research
[
22
,
1
,
9
,
13
,
12
,
21
,
19
,
18
]
.

To date, the majority of work on self-affine or self-similar measures has been limited to the case of uniform contraction ratios, i.e.,
M_{k}=M
for all
k=0,1,\cdots,N-1
in (
1.1
). Recently, Wu and Liu
[
24
]
constructed a class of spectral measures
\nu_{\rho,D}
generated by the iterated function system

\displaystyle\{\tau_{d_{i}}(\cdot)=(-1)^{i}\rho(\cdot+d_{i})\}_{d_{i}\in D},

(1.4)

where
|\rho|\in(0,1)
and
D\subset\mathbb{Z}
is a finite subset. When
\#D=2
, a sufficient and necessary condition for
\nu_{\rho,D}
to be a spectral measure was given. For convenience, we denote
D_{n}
as a consecutive digit set
\{0,1,\cdots,n-1\}
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Obviously, if the digit set
D=\{d_{0},d_{1},\cdots,d_{n-1}\}
in (
1.4
) is a consecutive digit set with
\#D=n
, then
d_{i}=i
for
0\leq i\leq n-1
and (
1.4
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\{\tau_{d_{i}}(\cdot)=(-1)^{i}\rho(\cdot+i)\}_{d_{i}\in D}.

(1.5)

Recently, Wu
[
25
]
further characterise the spectrality of the self-similar measure generated by the IFS in (
1.5
), associated with the consecutive digit set
D
satisfying
\#D\in 2\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
25
]
)
Let
\nu_{\rho,D_{2N}}
be the self-similar measure generated by the IFS in (
1.5
), associated with a contraction ratio
0<\rho<1
and a consecutive digit set
D_{2N}
(
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
). Then
\nu_{\rho,D_{2N}}
is a spectral measure if and only if
\rho^{-1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
2N\mid\rho^{-1}
.

Inspired by the above results, some natural questions are as follows. For fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, let the self-similar measure
\nu_{\rho,D}^{m}
be generated by the IFS

\displaystyle\{\tau_{d}(\cdot)=(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{d}{m}\rfloor}\rho(\cdot+d)\}_{d\in D},

(1.6)

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the floor function,
\rho\in(0,1)
is a real number and
0\in D
is a finite consecutive digit set with
\#D\in m\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Clearly, (
1.6
) can degenerate to (
1.5
) when
m=1
.

(Qu)
.

What is the sufficient and necessary condition for
\nu_{\rho,D}^{m}
to be a spectral measure? Moreover, for the case
m=1
, how to characterise the spectrality of the self-similar measure
\nu_{\rho,D_{2N+1}}^{1}
with
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
?

In this paper, a detailed investigation has been carried out to address the above questions and some positive results have been obtained. The following theorem is our first result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\nu_{\rho,D_{2Nm}}^{m}
be a self-similar measure generated by the IFS in (
1.6
). Then
\nu_{\rho,D_{2Nm}}^{m}
is a spectral measure if and only if
\rho^{-1}\in\mathbb{N}
with
2Nm\mid\rho^{-1}
.

It is worth noting that Theorem
1.2
clearly reduces to Theorem
1.1
by taking
m=1
. Our key insight involves transforming the spectral analysis of
\nu_{\rho,D_{2Nm}}^{m}
into that of a measure generated by an IFS with uniform contraction ratios. Specifically, through an ingenious transformation (distinct from the methods in
[
25
]
and detailed in Proposition
2.14
), we demonstrate that
\nu_{\rho,D_{2Nm}}^{m}=\mu_{\rho,D}
, where

\displaystyle D=D_{m}\oplus 2mD_{N}\oplus(1+m\rho-2Nm)D_{2},

(1.7)

and the corresponding IFS of
\mu_{\rho,D}
is

\displaystyle\{\tau_{d}(\cdot)=\rho(\cdot+d)\}_{d\in D}.

(1.8)

In this way, we only need to analyze the spectrality of
\mu_{\rho,D}
, from which the spectrality of
\nu_{\rho,D_{2Nm}}^{m}
is immediately available. Thus, Theorem
1.2
can be obtained directly from the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mu_{\rho,D}
be a self-similar measure generated by the IFS in (
1.8
), associated with
0<\rho<1
and the digit set
D
defined by (
1.7
).
Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

\mu_{\rho,D}
is a spectral measure;

(ii)

\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}
and
2Nm\mid p
;

(iii)

There exists
L\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that
(p,pD,L)
is a (2-stage) product-form Hadamard triple (see Definition
2.10
).

In contrast, the proof of the implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
requires the most delicate treatment, and will be presented in four key steps (detailed in Section
3
). Among these, the step (D) ( i.e., showing
2Nm\mid p
when
\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}
) presents the greatest challenge. Due to the arbitrariness of
m
and
N
, the proof needs to be discussed separately based on their parity.
Especially for the case
m\in 2\mathbb{N}
and
N\in 2\mathbb{N}+1
(a scenario not addressed in
[
25
]
), we develop a new approach to resolve this complication. Drawing upon the existing work on Moran measures, we reformulate the self-similar measures in Moran measure terms, thereby enabling us to apply established methods and conclusions to obtain our result. This same idea also applies to the step (C).

For the measure
\nu_{\rho,D_{(2N+1)m}}^{m}
, it remains extremely challenging to devise an efficient method for transforming
\nu_{\rho,D_{(2N+1)m}}^{m}
into a self-similar measure with the uniform contraction ratio. Consequently, the spectrality of
\nu_{\rho,D_{(2N+1)m}}^{m}
has remained intractable. However, for the measure
\nu_{\rho,D_{s}}^{1}
with
\rho^{-1},s\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we can characterize its nonspectral properties as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\nu_{\rho,D_{s}}^{1}
be a self-similar measure generated by the IFS in (
1.5
), where
\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}
with
p\geq 2
and
s\geq 2
. Assume that
\gcd(p,s)=1
, then
\nu_{\rho,D_{s}}^{1}
is not a spectral measure. In particular,
L^{2}(\nu_{\rho,D_{s}}^{1})
contains at most
s
mutually orthogonal exponential functions.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

In Section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some basic definitions and useful conclusions related to self-similar measures and Moran measures in
\mathbb{R}
. Since the Moran measure is a generalisation of the self-similar measure, we will present some notations in a more general form.

Let
\{b_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of real numbers and
\{R_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of digits sets with
0\in R_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}
.
Suppose that the Moran measure
\mu_{\{b_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}}
exists. Then it can be written in the form of the following infinite convolution of discrete measures

\displaystyle\mu_{\{b_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}}=\delta_{b_{1}^{-1}R_{1}}*\delta_{b_{1}^{-1}b_{2}^{-1}R_{2}}*\cdots*\delta_{b_{1}^{-1}b_{2}^{-1}\cdots b_{n}^{-1}R_{n}}*\delta_{b_{1}^{-1}b_{2}^{-1}\cdots b_{n+1}^{-1}R_{n+1}}*\cdots.

(2.1)

It is widely known that if the infinite convolution in (
2.1
) satisfies

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\max\{|a|:a\in R_{n}\}}{|b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{n}|}<\infty,

then
\mu_{\{b_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}}
is a Borel probability measure with compact support
[
22
]
. Recently, Li
et al.

[
17
]
have established a complete characterization for the existence of the infinite convolution in (
2.1
) under the assumption that
b_{1}^{-1}b_{2}^{-1}\cdots b_{k}^{-1}R_{k}
is a subset of
[0,+\infty)
for each
k\geq 1
.
For the convenience of the subsequent discussion, we define a measure related to
\mu_{\{b_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}}
as follows:

\mu_{\{b_{n},R_{n},>n\}}:=\delta_{b_{n+1}^{-1}R_{n+1}}*\delta_{b_{n+1}^{-1}b_{n+2}^{-1}R_{n+2}}*\delta_{b_{n+1}^{-1}b_{n+2}^{-1}b_{n+3}^{-1}R_{n+3}}*\cdots

(2.2)

for
n\geq 1
.

For a compactly supported Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
, the Fourier transform of
\mu
is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\pi i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\,\textup{d}\mu(x),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.3)

By a direct calculation, the Fourier transform of the Moran measure
\mu_{\{b_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}}
is

\hat{\mu}_{\{b_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}}(\xi)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}m_{R_{j}}((b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{j})^{-1}\xi),

(2.4)

where
m_{R}(\cdot)
is the mask polynomial of the digit set
R
, i.e.,

\displaystyle m_{R}(x)=\frac{1}{\#R}\sum_{d\in R}e^{2\pi i\langle d,x\rangle},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
\mathcal{Z}(f)=\{x:f(x)=0\}
denote the zero set of a function
f
, then it directly follows from (
2.4
) that

\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\mu}_{\{b_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}})=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{n}\mathcal{Z}(m_{R_{n}}).

(2.5)

In the special case where
b_{n}\equiv b>1
and
R_{n}\equiv R
for all
n
, the measure
\mu_{\{b_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}}
reduces to the canonical self-similar measure
\mu_{\rho,R}
generated by

\displaystyle\mu_{\rho,R}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{\#R}\sum_{d\in R}\mu_{\rho,R}(\rho^{-1}(\cdot)-d),

(2.6)

where
\rho=b^{-1}
is a contraction ratio in
(0,1)
.

It is easy to verify that
\Lambda
is an orthonormal set of
\mu

if and only if
\hat{\mu}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})=0
holds for any
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda
, i.e.,

(\Lambda-\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}\subset\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\mu}).

(2.7)

A basic criterion for an orthogonal set to be a spectrum of a measure is given by Jorgensen and Pedersen in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
Q_{\mu,\Lambda}(\cdot)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|\hat{\mu}(\cdot+\lambda)|^{2}

for a countable set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

(i)

\Lambda
is an orthogonal set of
\mu
if and only if
Q_{\mu,\Lambda}(\xi)\leq 1
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(ii)

\Lambda
is a spectrum of

Q_{\mu,\Lambda}(\xi)\equiv 1
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Strichartz
[
22
]
introduced the concept of a Hadamard triple, which has become an essential tool in the study of spectral measures.

Definition 2.8

(Hadamard triple,
[
22
]
)
.

Let
M\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
be an expanding matrix and
D,L\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be two finite digit sets with the same cardinality (i.e.,
\#D=\#L
). We say that (
M,D,L
) is a Hadamard triple (or (
M,D
) is an admissible pair) if the matrix

H=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\#D}}\left(e^{2\pi i\langle M^{-1}d,\ell\rangle}\right)_{d\in D,\ell\in L}

is unitary, i.e.,
H^{*}H=I
, where
H^{*}
means the transposed conjugate of
H
and
I
is the identity matrix.

The following conclusion is a universal test for verifing the existence of a Hadamard triple.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
M\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
be an expanding matrix, and let
D,L\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be two finite digit sets with the same cardinality. Then
(M,D,L)
is a Hadamard triple if and only if
m_{D}(M^{*-1}(l_{1}-l_{2}))=0
for any
l_{1}\neq l_{2}\in L
.

It is well-known that Hadamard triple is a sufficient condition for a self-affine measure to be a spectral measure
[
10

Let

So far, we have shown that

Case (iv):

Without loss of generality, let

By using Remark
2.3
, it follows that

. Hence we have
2^{s}m^{\prime}N\mid p
.

Finally, we show

. Let

for some

. Since

, it follows that

. We now prove that

must be even. Suppose, to the contrary, that

. Write

, where

and

with

and

. Note that
\gcd{(t,N)}=1
since

.
Combining (
1.7
) and

, we decompose

as follows:

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=D_{m}\oplus 2mD_{N}\oplus(m+p(1-2mN))p^{-1}D_{2}

\displaystyle=D_{m}\oplus 2mD_{N}\oplus 2^{sl+a}t\tilde{p}^{-1}D_{2}

\displaystyle=D_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus 2^{a}D_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus 2^{s-1}D_{2}\oplus 2^{s}D_{m^{{}^{\prime}}}\oplus 2mD_{N}\oplus 2^{sl+a}t\tilde{p}^{-1}D_{2}.

①
a=0
: The fact

4.
Nonspectrality of self-similar measures

For the self-similar measure
\nu_{\rho,D_{s}}^{1}
generated by the IFS
\{\tau_{d}(\cdot)=(-1)^{d}\rho(\cdot+d)\}_{d\in D_{s}}
,
Wu
[
25
]
has characterised the spectrality of
\nu_{\rho,D_{s}}^{1}
when
s\in 2\mathbb{N}
(Theorem
1.1
) by using an infinite product of a function matrix instead of the infinite product of a mask polynomial to express
\hat{\nu}_{\rho,D_{s}}^{1}
. Unfortunately, this method does not work for the situation where
s
is an odd number. In this section, for the case
s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s\geq 2
, the number of orthogonal exponentials of
L^{2}(\nu_{\rho,D_{s}}^{1})
is estimated under the condition that
\rho^{-1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gcd{(\rho^{-1},s)}=1
. Especially for the case
s\in 2\mathbb{N}+1
, we achieve the goal by exploiting some properties of the Fourier transform of the measure to give the possible range of zeros of
\hat{\nu}_{\rho,D_{s}}^{1}
. Theorem
1.4
follows directly from the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\nu_{\rho,D_{s}}^{1}:=\nu^{\prime}
be a self-similar measure generated by (
1.5
), where
0<\rho<1
and
s\geq 2
is a positive integer. Then we have the following two conclusions.

(i)

For
s\in 2\mathbb{N}+1
, we have
\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})\subset\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2\mathbb{Z}+1)\backslash s(2\mathbb{Z}+1)}{2\rho^{k}s}\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\backslash s\mathbb{Z}}{\rho^{k}s}\right)
.

(ii)

If
\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gcd{(p,s)}=1
, then
L^{2}(\nu^{\prime})
contains at most
s
mutually orthogonal exponential functions.

Proof.

We first prove
(i)
. If
\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})=\emptyset
, the desired result stands naturally. Below we need only consider the case
\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. The assumption
s\in 2\mathbb{N}+1
yields that
s-1=2N
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Combining (
1.2
) and (
1.5
), the Fourier transform of
\nu^{\prime}
can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N+1}\sum_{d=0}^{2N}e^{2\pi i(-1)^{d}\rho td}\hat{\nu}^{\prime}((-1)^{d}\rho t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N+1}\left(\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N}e^{2\pi i2j\rho t}\right)\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho t)+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-2\pi i(2j-1)\rho t}\right)\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(-\rho t)\right).

(4.1)

Notice that
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n}t)
approaches
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(0)=1
when
n
is sufficiently large. Then for any
t\in\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})
, it follows from the continuity of
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}
that there exists a smallest positive integer
n_{t}\geq 1
such that
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}}t)\neq 0
but
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{i}t)=0
for all
i=0,1,\cdots,n_{t}-1
.
Using
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}-1}t)=0
and (
4
), it is clear that
2\rho^{n_{t}}t\notin\mathbb{Z}
. Otherwise, we can get

0=\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}-1}t)=(N+1)\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}}t)\pm N\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(-\rho^{n_{t}}t),

which is impossible since
1=|\frac{\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}}t)}{\overline{\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}}t)}}|=|\frac{\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}}t)}{\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(-\rho^{n_{t}}t)}|=\frac{N}{N+1}\neq 1
, where
\overline{\hat{\nu}^{\prime}}
denotes the conjugate function of
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}
.
Hence it follows from
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}-1}t)=0
and (
4
) that

\frac{\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(-\rho^{n_{t}}t)}{\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}}t)}=-\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{N}e^{2\pi i2j\rho^{n_{t}}t}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-2\pi i(2j-1)\rho^{n_{t}}t}}=-e^{2\pi i\rho^{n_{t}}t}\frac{\frac{1-e^{2\pi i(2N+2)\rho^{n_{t}}t}}{1-e^{2\pi i2\rho^{n_{t}}t}}}{\frac{1-e^{-2\pi i2N\rho^{n_{t}}t}}{1-e^{-2\pi i2\rho^{n_{t}}t}}}=-e^{2\pi i2N\rho^{n_{t}}t}\frac{\sin(2\pi(N+1)\rho^{n_{t}}t)}{\sin(2\pi N\rho^{n_{t}}t)}.

Based on the fact that
|\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(-\rho^{n_{t}}t)|=|\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}}t)|
, the above equation forces

\sin(2\pi(N+1)\rho^{n_{t}}t)=\pm\sin(2\pi N\rho^{n_{t}}t).

Case I :
Suppose that
\sin(2\pi(N+1)\rho^{n_{t}}t)=\sin(2\pi N\rho^{n_{t}}t)
.
A direct calculation gives that

\rho^{n_{t}}t\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\frac{2\mathbb{Z}+1}{2s}.

It follows from
2\rho^{n_{t}}t\notin\mathbb{Z}
that
\rho^{n_{t}}t\in\frac{(2\mathbb{Z}+1)\backslash s(2\mathbb{Z}+1)}{2s}
.

Note that
t
is any zero of
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}
and
n_{t}\geq 1
is the smallest positive integer such that
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{n_{t}}t)\neq 0
but
\hat{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho^{i}t)=0
for all
0\leq i\leq n_{t}-1
. Therefore, we can get

\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}\frac{(2\mathbb{Z}+1)\backslash s(2\mathbb{Z}+1)}{2s}.

Case II :
Suppose that
\sin(2\pi(N+1)\rho^{n_{t}}t)=-\sin(2\pi N\rho^{n_{t}}t)
. One may obtain that

\rho^{n_{t}}t\in\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{s}\cup\frac{2\mathbb{Z}+1}{2}
by a simple calculation. Since
2\rho^{n_{t}}t\notin\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
\rho^{n_{t}}t\in\frac{\mathbb{Z}\backslash s\mathbb{Z}}{s}
.
A similar analysis as in the previous case yields

\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}\mathcal{Z}(m_{D})=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho^{-k}\frac{\mathbb{Z}\backslash s\mathbb{Z}}{s}.

Consequently, we have completed the proof of
(i)
.

Next, we prove
(ii)
. Under the assumption that
\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}
with
\gcd(p,s)=1
, part (i) readily implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})\subset\frac{(2\mathbb{Z}+1)\backslash s(2\mathbb{Z}+1)}{2s}\cup\frac{\mathbb{Z}\backslash s\mathbb{Z}}{s}\subset\frac{\mathbb{Z}\backslash s\mathbb{Z}}{2s}

provided that
s\in 2\mathbb{N}+1
. On the other hand, if
s=2N
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have
\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})=\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\mu}_{\rho,D^{{}^{\prime}}})
by Proposition
2.14
, where
D^{{}^{\prime}}=2D_{N}\oplus(1+\rho-2N)D_{2}
and
\mu_{\rho,D^{{}^{\prime}}}
is a canonical self-similar measure generated from (
2.6
).
Since
\rho^{-1}=p\in\mathbb{N}
with
\gcd(p,2N)=1
and
p(1-2N)+1=:Q\in 2\mathbb{Z}
, one may obtain that

\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})=\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\mu}_{\rho,D^{{}^{\prime}}})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}p^{j}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus N\mathbb{Z}}{2N}\cup\frac{p(\mathbb{Z}\setminus 2\mathbb{Z})}{2p(1-2N)+2}\right)\subset\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus 2N\mathbb{Z}}{2NQ}=\frac{\mathbb{Z}\setminus s\mathbb{Z}}{sQ}.

Assume that
0\in\Lambda
is an orthogonal set of
\nu^{\prime}
. We claim that
\#\Lambda\leq s
. If
s\in 2\mathbb{N}+1
and
\#\Lambda\geq s+1
, then there must exist
\lambda_{1}=\frac{l_{1}}{2s}\neq\lambda_{2}=\frac{l_{2}}{2s}\in\Lambda
such that
l_{1}=l_{2}\pmod{s\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\in\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2}\not\subset\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})
. Similarly, if

s\in 2\mathbb{N}
, then there must exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
such that
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\in\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{Q}\not\subset\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})
. These contradicts with (
2.7
). Therefore, one may obtain
\#\Lambda\leq s
, which implies that
L^{2}(\nu^{\prime})
contains at most
s
mutually orthogonal exponential functions.
∎

Proposition
4.1
provides a possible range for
\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})

under the assumption that
\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. However, both the existence and explicit form of
\mathcal{Z}(\hat{\nu}^{\prime})
remain undetermined with current methods. Furthermore, for the measure
\nu^{m}_{\rho,D_{(2N+1)m}}
, unlike the analysis for the measure
\nu_{\rho,D_{2Nm}}^{m}
, our methods cannot establish the relationship between
\hat{\nu}^{m}_{\rho,D_{(2N+1)m}}(t)
and
\hat{\nu}^{m}_{\rho,D_{(2N+1)m}}(-t)
. Consequently, we are unable to fully characterize the Fourier transform of
\nu^{m}_{\rho,D_{(2N+1)m}}
. Therefore, we propose the following open problem:

(Qu)
.

For the self-similar measure
\nu_{\rho,D_{(2N+1)m}}^{m}
with
N\in\mathbb{N}
generated by the IFS in (
1.6
), what are the zeros of its Fourier transform? Moreover, what is the sufficient and necessary condition for
\nu_{\rho,D_{(2N+1)m}}^{m}
to be a spectral measure?

We conclude this section by establishing the Fourier transform representation for a class of self-similar measures
\mathbf{v}
with alternative contraction ratios. This is achieved by utilizing the fundamental symmetry property
\hat{\mathbf{v}}(t)=\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-t)
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Although this example has been investigated in
[
24
]
, the method used here provides a distinct perspective.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\nu_{\rho,\tilde{D}_{2n+1}}^{1}
be a self-similar measure generated by the IFS in (
1.6
), where
0<\rho<1
and
\tilde{D}_{2n+1}=\{-n,-(n-1),\cdots,-1,0,1,\cdots,n\}
. Then
\hat{\nu}_{\rho,\tilde{D}_{2n+1}}^{1}(t)=e^{\frac{-2\pi int\rho}{1-\rho}}\hat{\mu}_{\rho,D_{2n+1}}(t)
, where
\mu_{\rho,D_{2n+1}}
is a canonical self-similar measure defined by (
2.6
).

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{v}:=\nu_{\rho,\tilde{D}_{2n+1}}^{1}
for convenience. For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, combining (
1.2
) and (
1.6
) yields the following expression for
\hat{\mathbf{v}}(t)
:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{v}}(t)=\frac{1}{2n+1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{\mathbf{v}}((-1)^{j}\rho t)(e^{2\pi ij\rho t}+e^{-2\pi ij\rho t})+\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\rho t)\right).

(4.2)

Through direct calculation, we have

\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-t)=\frac{1}{2n+1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{\mathbf{v}}((-1)^{j+1}\rho t)(e^{-2\pi ij\rho t}+e^{2\pi ij\rho t})+\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-\rho t)\right)

and

|\hat{\mathbf{v}}(t)-\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-t)|\leq\frac{|\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\rho t)-\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-\rho t)|}{2n+1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|e^{-2\pi ij\rho t}+e^{2\pi ij\rho t}|+1\right)\leq|\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\rho t)-\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-\rho t)|.

(4.3)

Observe that
\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\pm\rho^{m}t)
converges to
\hat{\mathbf{v}}(0)=1
as
m
becomes sufficiently large. Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for
|\rho^{m}t|<\delta
we have
|\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\pm\rho^{m}t)-1|<\varepsilon
.
Selecting a large enough integer
N
such that
|\rho^{m}t|<\delta
for
m>N
, we obtain

|\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\rho^{m}t)-\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-\rho^{m}t)|<2\varepsilon
. By iterating (
4.3
)
m~(m>N)
times, we arrive at

|\hat{\mathbf{v}}(t)-\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-t)|\leq|\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\rho t)-\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-\rho t)|\leq\cdots\leq|\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\rho^{m}t)-\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-\rho^{m}t)|<2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude that

\hat{\mathbf{v}}(t)=\hat{\mathbf{v}}(-t)
. Then it follows from
(\ref{4.1})
that

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{v}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}e^{2\pi ij\rho t}\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\rho t)=e^{-2\pi in\rho t}\frac{1}{2n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{2n}e^{2\pi ij\rho t}\hat{\mathbf{v}}(\rho t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi in\rho^{k}t}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}m_{D_{2n+1}}(\rho^{k}t)=e^{\frac{-2\pi int\rho}{1-\rho}}\hat{\mu}_{\rho,D_{2n+1}}(t),

where
\mu_{\rho,D_{2n+1}}
is a canonical self-similar measure defined by (
2.6
).
∎

Combining with Lemma
2.1
, we can immediately establish the equivalence of spectrality between
\nu_{\rho,\tilde{D}_{2n+1}}^{1}
and
\mu_{\rho,D_{2n+1}}
. Thus, by
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
, we conclude that
\nu_{\rho,\tilde{D}_{2n+1}}^{1}
is a spectral measure if and only if
\rho^{-1}\in(2n+1)\mathbb{N}^{+}
.
As can be seen from this example, the advantage of connecting the Fourier transform of
\nu_{\rho,\tilde{D}_{2n+1}}^{1}
to that of
\mu_{\rho,D_{2n+1}}
lies in enabling characterization of
\nu_{\rho,\tilde{D}_{2n+1}}^{1}
’s spectrality by leveraging established results about the canonical self-similar measure
\mu_{\rho,D_{2n+1}}
.

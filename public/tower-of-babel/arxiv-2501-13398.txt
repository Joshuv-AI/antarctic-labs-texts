Non-Polynomial Conserved Quantities for ODE Systems and its Application to the Long-Time Behavior of Solutions to Cubic NLS Systems
Satoshi Masaki, Jun-ichi Segata, Kota Uriya

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of small solutions to the initial value problem for a system of cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equations (NLS) in one spatial dimension. We identify a new class of NLS systems for which the global boundedness and asymptotics of small solutions can be established, even in the absence of any effective conserved quantity. The key to this analysis lies in utilizing conserved quantities for the reduced ordinary differential equation (ODE) systems derived from the original NLS systems. In a previous study, the first author investigated conserved quantities expressed as quartic polynomials. In contrast, the conserved quantities considered in the present paper are of a different type and are not necessarily polynomial.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the large time behavior of solutions to
the initial value problem of the system of cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equations in one space dimension:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{1}=F_{1}(u_{1},u_{2}),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
&i\partial_{t}u_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{2}=F_{2}(u_{1},u_{2}),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
&u_{1}(0,x)=u_{1,0}(x),\quad u_{2}(0,x)=u_{2,0}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{aligned}\right.

where
u_{j}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
(
j=1,2
) are unknown functions
and
u_{j,0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
(
j=1,2
) are given functions. The nonlinear terms

F_{1}
and

are given by

\displaystyle F_{1}(u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle=c_{1}|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1}+c_{2}|u_{1}|^{2}u_{2}+c_{3}u_{1}^{2}\overline{u_{2}}+c_{4}u_{1}|u_{2}|^{2}+c_{5}\overline{u_{1}}u_{2}^{2}+c_{6}|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2},

\displaystyle F_{2}(u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle=c_{7}|u_{1}|^{2}u_{1}+c_{8}|u_{1}|^{2}u_{2}+c_{9}u_{1}^{2}\overline{u_{2}}+c_{10}u_{1}|u_{2}|^{2}+c_{11}\overline{u_{1}}u_{2}^{2}+c_{12}|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2},

and

Our aim is to show the global existence of the solution

in determining the asymptotic behavior of solutions in one dimension, making this an important and intriguing

We provide two new types of systems of the form (
1.1

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{1}=(\zeta+1)|u_{1}|^{2}u_{2}+\zeta|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\

(1.3)

h_{1}:=(2|\varphi_{1}|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\varphi_{1}}\varphi_{2})+2|\varphi_{2}|^{2})^{2}(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}),\quad h_{2}:=(2|\varphi_{1}|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\varphi_{1}}\varphi_{2})+2|\varphi_{2}|^{2})(|\varphi_{2}|^{2}).

Assumption
2
implies that the existence of such a pair of conserved quantities.

2.
Global existence of the solution

In this section we prove the global existence of
solution to (
1.1
).
We will denote by
U(t)
the free Schrödinger group,
namely,

(2.1)

(U(t)f)(x)=(e^{\frac{it}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}}f)(x)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi it}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\frac{i|x-y|^{2}}{2t}}f(y)dy.

The following factorization formula is well-known:

(2.2)

U(t)=M(t)D(t)\mathcal{F}M(t),

where

\displaystyle(M(t)f)(x)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{2t}}f(x),\qquad(\text{Multiplication})

\displaystyle(D(t)f)(x)

\displaystyle=(it)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\left(\frac{x}{t}\right),\qquad(\text{Dilation})

and
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}
.
The generator of the Galilean transformation is given as

J(t)=x+it\partial_{x}
.
Note that the operators
J(t)
and

i\partial_{t}+(1/2)\partial^{2}_{x}
commute.
The following identities are useful:

J(t)=U(t)xU(-t)=M(t)it\partial_{x}M(t)^{-1},

where the second identity is valid for
t\neq 0
.

By the standard theory of local well-posedness for
the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
(see
[
Caz
]
for instance),
we have the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists
\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
such that if

\varepsilon:=\|u_{1,0}\|_{H^{0,1}}+\|u_{2,0}\|_{H^{0,1}}
satisfies

\varepsilon\leqslant\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{0}
, then there exists
a unique solution

(u_{1},u_{2})\in(C([-1,1];L^{2}(\mathbb{R})))^{2}
to (
1.1
) such that

(U(-t)u_{1},U(-t)u_{2})\in(C([-1,1];H^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})))^{2}

and

(2.3)

\max_{t\in[-1,1]}\left(\|u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|J(t)u_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|J(t)u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\leqslant 2\varepsilon.

We shall show the existence of global solution by
proving that
\|U(-t)u_{1}\|_{H^{0,1}}+\|U(-t)u_{2}\|_{H^{0,1}}
is finite for all
t>0
.
To this end,
we introduce a new variable

w_{j}(t,\xi):=\mathcal{F}[U(-t)u_{j}(t,\cdot)](\xi)
.
Note that
U(-t)u_{j}(t)\in H^{0,1}
is equivalent to
w_{j}(t)\in H^{1}
.
Applying
\mathcal{F}U(-t)
to the both sides of (
1.1
), we have

(2.4)

i\partial_{t}w_{j}(t)=\frac{1}{t}F_{j}(w_{1},w_{2})+r_{j}

for
j=1,2
and
t\neq 0
, where

(2.5)

r_{j}:=\textrm{I}_{j}+\textrm{II}_{j}

with

(2.6)

\textrm{I}_{j}:=\left(U\left(\tfrac{1}{t}\right)-1\right)D(t)^{-1}M(t)^{-1}F_{j}(u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t))

and

(2.7)

\textrm{II}_{j}:=\frac{1}{t}\left\{(F_{j}\left(U\left(-\tfrac{1}{t}\right)w_{1}(t),U\left(-\tfrac{1}{t}\right)w_{2}(t)\right)-F_{j}(w_{1}(t),w_{2}(t)))\right\}.

From the unitarity of the Fourier transform
and
U(t)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
J(t)
, we have

(2.8)

\|w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|\partial_{\xi}w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|J(t)u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
u_{j}(t)=M(t)D(t)U(-\frac{1}{t})w_{j}(t)
, it holds that
for any
t\neq 0
,

(2.9)

\|U\left(-\tfrac{1}{t}\right)w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

For
\delta>0
and
T>1
, we define

(2.10)

X_{T}:=\sup_{t\in[1,T]}t^{-\delta\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}(\|u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|J(t)u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}})

and

(2.11)

Y_{T}:=\sup_{t\in[1,T]}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Note that by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and (
2.8
),
we have for any
t\in[1,T]
,

(2.12)

\|w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{\xi}w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim t^{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}X_{T}.

The following gives us the global existence of a solution to (
1.1
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\widetilde{\varepsilon_{0}}
be given by Proposition

2.1
.
Then there exist
\delta>0
,
C>0
, and
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\widetilde{\varepsilon_{0}}]

such that if
\varepsilon=\|u_{1,0}\|_{H^{0,1}}+\|u_{2,0}\|_{H^{0,1}}

satisfies
\varepsilon\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}
, then the unique solution to (
1.1
)
given in Proposition
2.1
exists globally
in time for positive time direction and obeys the bound

(2.13)

\sup_{T\geqslant 1}\left(X_{T}+Y_{T}\right)\leqslant C\varepsilon.

Proof.

Since
X_{T}
and
Y_{T}
are continuous in
T
on
[1,\infty)
,
it suffices to show that there exist
\delta>0
,
C_{0}>0
,
\widetilde{C}_{0}>0
, and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if

\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and

(2.14)

X_{T}\leqslant C_{0}\varepsilon,\quad Y_{T}\leqslant\widetilde{C}_{0}\varepsilon

for some
T\geqslant 1
then it holds that

(2.15)

X_{T}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}C_{0}\varepsilon,\quad Y_{T}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\widetilde{C}_{0}\varepsilon.

We divide the proof into four steps.

Step 1
. We estimate
X_{T}
.
Let
\delta>0
,
C_{0}>0
,
\widetilde{C}_{0}
, and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
to be chosen later. Suppose that (
2.14
) is true for some
T\geqslant 1
.
By the Duhamel formula for (
1.1
), we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}\varepsilon+C_{1}\int_{1}^{t}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|u_{j}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|u_{j}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}\varepsilon+C_{1}X_{T}Y_{T}^{2}\int_{1}^{t}\tau^{\delta\varepsilon^{2}-1}d\tau

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}\varepsilon+\delta^{-1}\widetilde{C}_{0}^{2}C_{0}C_{1}\varepsilon t^{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}

for
t\geqslant 1
.
In the same manner, we obtain the estimate for
J(t)u_{j}(t)
:

(2.16)

\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|J(t)u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant C_{1}\varepsilon+\delta^{-1}\widetilde{C}_{0}^{2}C_{0}C_{1}\varepsilon t^{\delta\varepsilon^{2}}

for
t\geqslant 1
.
Hence, it holds that

(2.17)

X_{T}\leqslant C_{1}(1+\delta^{-1}C_{0}\widetilde{C}_{0}^{2})\varepsilon.

Step 2
. We estimate
Y_{T}
.
By using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and (
2.8
), we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\|(U(-\tfrac{1}{t})-1)w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(U(-\tfrac{1}{t})-1)w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}(U(-\tfrac{1}{t})-1)w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{x}w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|J(t)u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence, for
t\in[1,T]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\|U(t)U(-t)u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|M(t)D(t)w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|M(t)D(t)\mathcal{F}(M(t)-1)\mathcal{F}^{-1}w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|(U(-\tfrac{1}{t})-1)w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+Ct^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|J(t)u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}.

This yields

(2.19)

Y_{T}\leqslant C\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+CX_{T}.

In view of (
2.19
), to estimate
Y_{T}
,
it suffices to estimate
w_{j}
in
L^{\infty}
.
We shall do so in the next two steps by using the integral form of (
2.4
).
In this step, we estimate the remainder term
r_{j}
given in
(
2.5
)-(
2.7
).
For the estimate of
\textrm{I}_{j}
given in
(
2.6
), it follows from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality that

\displaystyle\|\textrm{I}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\textrm{I}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}\textrm{I}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{x}D(t)^{-1}M(t)^{-1}F_{j}(u_{1}(t),u_{1}(t))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|J(t)F_{j}(u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{5}{4}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|J(t)u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{5}{4}+\delta\varepsilon^{2}}X_{T}Y_{T}^{2}.

As for
\textrm{II}_{j}
given in (
2.7
), the estimates
(
2.9
), (
2.12
) and (
2.18
) yield

\displaystyle\|\textrm{II}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|U(-\tfrac{1}{t})w_{j}(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left\|(U(-\tfrac{1}{t})-1)w_{j}(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}(t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u_{j}(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}})^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\|w_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{2}t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left\|\partial_{x}w_{j}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{5}{4}+3\delta\varepsilon^{2}}(X_{T}^{2}+Y_{T}^{2})X_{T}.

Thus, we end up with

(2.20)

\|r_{j}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{5}{4}+3\delta\varepsilon^{2}}(X_{T}^{2}+Y_{T}^{2})X_{T}

in view of (
2.5
).

Step 3
. We complete the estimates of
Y_{T}

under Assumption
1
.
One may suppose that
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
without loss of generality.
Let

\rho_{1}=|w_{1}|^{2},\quad\rho_{2}=|w_{2}|^{2},\quad\mathcal{R}=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{w_{1}}w_{2}),\quad\mathcal{I}=2\operatorname{Im}(\overline{w_{1}}w_{2})

and for
\vec{a}={}^{t}\!(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(2.21)

\mathcal{Q}(\vec{a}):=\begin{bmatrix}\rho_{1}&\mathcal{R}&\rho_{2}\end{bmatrix}\vec{a}=\begin{bmatrix}\overline{w_{1}}&\overline{w_{2}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
a_{2}&a_{3}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}w_{1}\\
w_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Then, we see from (
2.4
) and Proposition
1.1
that

(2.22)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{Q}(\vec{a})=2t^{-1}\mathcal{I}\mathcal{Q}(\mathscr{A}\vec{a})+\vec{r}\cdot\vec{a}

for any
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
\vec{r}=\vec{r}(t)
is given by

(2.23)

\!\vec{r}={}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}2\operatorname{Im}(\overline{w_{1}}r_{1})&2\operatorname{Im}(\overline{w_{1}r_{2}})+2\operatorname{Im}(\overline{w_{2}}r_{1})&2\operatorname{Im}(\overline{w_{2}}r_{2})\end{bmatrix}.

Pick

\vec{p}_{j}={}^{t}\!(p_{j,1},p_{j,2},p_{j,3})\in W(\lambda_{j})\cap\mathcal{P}_{+}
and define

Q_{j}:=\mathcal{Q}(\vec{p}_{j}),\qquad R_{j}=\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}_{j}

for
j=1,2
.
Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
p_{j,1}>0
so that

(2.24)

Q_{1}\sim|w_{1}|^{2}+|w_{2}|^{2}\sim Q_{2}.

Then, one sees from (
2.22
) and the fact that
\vec{p}_{j}
is eigenvector
of
\mathscr{A}
associated with
\lambda_{j}
for
j=1,2
, respectively, that

(2.25)

\frac{d}{dt}Q_{j}=2\lambda_{j}t^{-1}\mathcal{I}Q_{j}+R_{j}.

Let
m
be a real number satisfying
m>1/(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})
.
Then, by (
2.25
),
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(Q_{1}^{-m\lambda_{2}}Q_{2}^{m\lambda_{1}})

\displaystyle=-m\lambda_{2}Q_{1}^{-m\lambda_{2}-1}Q_{2}^{m\lambda_{1}}\partial_{t}Q_{1}+m\lambda_{1}Q_{1}^{-m\lambda_{2}}Q_{2}^{m\lambda_{1}-1}\partial_{t}Q_{2}

\displaystyle=-m\lambda_{2}Q_{1}^{-m\lambda_{2}-1}Q_{2}^{m\lambda_{1}}(2t^{-1}\mathcal{I}\lambda_{1}Q_{1}+R_{1})

\displaystyle\quad+m\lambda_{1}Q_{1}^{-m\lambda_{2}}Q_{2}^{m\lambda_{1}-1}(2t^{-1}\mathcal{I}\lambda_{2}Q_{2}+R_{2})

\displaystyle=-m\lambda_{2}Q_{1}^{-m\lambda_{2}-1}Q_{2}^{m\lambda_{1}}R_{1}+m\lambda_{1}Q_{1}^{-m\lambda_{2}}Q_{2}^{m\lambda_{1}-1}R_{2}

if

. This identity is valid also when
|w_{1}|^{2}+|w_{2}|^{2}=0
in view of (
2.24
) and

.
Integrating this equation on
[1,t]
, we see that

(2.26)

\displaystyle Q_{1}(t)^{-m\lambda_{2}}Q_{2}(t)^{m\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle=Q_{1}(1)^{-m\lambda_{2}}Q_{2}(1)^{m\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+m\int_{1}^{t}(Q_{1}^{-m\lambda_{2}-1}Q_{2}^{m\lambda_{1}-1}(-\lambda_{2}Q_{2}R_{1}+\lambda_{1}Q_{1}R_{2}))(\tau)d\tau.

Substituting (
2.12
), (
2.20
), and (
2.24
) into (

), we obtain

\displaystyle(|w_{1}(t)|^{2}+|w_{2}(t)|^{2})^{m(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})}

do not vanish since otherwise

(W(\lambda_{1}^{(0)};\mathcal{A}^{(1)})\oplus W(\lambda_{2}^{(0)};\mathcal{A}^{(1)}))\cap\mathcal{P}_{+}=\emptyset
, a contradiction.

We next apply the following change of variables:

\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}^{(2)}\\
u_{2}^{(2)}\end{bmatrix}=\mathscr{M}^{(2)}\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}^{(1)}\\
u_{2}^{(1)}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathscr{M}^{(2)}=|\lambda_{3}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+b)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\sqrt{b^{2}-a}&1+b\end{bmatrix}.

Let
\mathscr{A}^{(2)}
be the new matrix.
Then, since
\det\mathscr{M}^{(2)}=|\lambda_{3}|>0
, one has

W(\lambda_{1};\mathscr{A}^{(2)})=\Span\{{}^{t}\!(1,0,-1)\},\quad W(\lambda_{2};\mathscr{A}^{(2)})=\Span\{{}^{t}\!(1,0,0)\},\quad W(-1;\mathscr{A}^{(2)})\cap\mathcal{P}_{+}\neq\emptyset,

where
\lambda_{j}:=\lambda_{j}^{(0)}|\lambda_{3}^{(0)}|^{-1}
for
j=1,2
.
Then,
\lambda_{1}>-1
and
\lambda_{2}>-1
.
Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{(2)}={}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\eta_{2}\\
0&0&1\\
-1&0&\eta_{3}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}&0\\
0&0&-1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\eta_{2}\\
0&0&1\\
-1&0&\eta_{3}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{2}&-\eta_{2}(\lambda_{2}+1)+\eta_{3}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})&-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\\
0&-1&0\\
0&-\eta_{3}(\lambda_{1}+1)&\lambda_{1}\end{bmatrix}

with
\eta_{2}\eta_{3}>1
.
This is the special case
\eta_{1}=0
of
\mathscr{A}_{2,2}
.

Subcase (d)
. One writes

W(\lambda_{1}^{(0)};\mathscr{A}^{(0)})=\Span\{{}^{t}\!(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\}

with
p_{1}p_{3}<p_{2}^{2}
.
We introduce

\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}^{(1)}\\
u_{2}^{(1)}\end{bmatrix}=\mathscr{M}^{(1)}\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}^{(0)}\\
u_{2}^{(0)}\end{bmatrix}

with

\mathscr{M}^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{0}p_{1}&\sigma_{0}p_{2}\\
0&(p_{2}^{2}-p_{1}p_{3})^{1/2}\end{bmatrix}

if
p_{1}\neq 0
,

\mathscr{M}^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{0}p_{2}&0\\
p_{2}&p_{3}\end{bmatrix}

if
p_{1}=0
and
p_{3}\neq 0
, and

\mathscr{M}^{(1)}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
-1&1\end{bmatrix}

if
p_{1}=p_{3}=0
, where
\sigma_{0}\in\{\pm 1\}
is chosen so that
\det\mathscr{M}^{(1)}>0
.
Let
\mathcal{A}^{(1)}
be the new matrix part and let
\lambda_{j}^{(1)}:=(\det\mathscr{M}^{(1)})\lambda_{j}^{(0)}
be the eigenvalues of the new matrix.
Then, one sees that

W(\lambda_{1}^{(1)};\mathscr{A}^{(1)})=\Span\{(1,0,-1)\}.

Let us next simplify the characteristic space associated with eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}^{(1)}
.
Pick

\vec{q}={}^{t}\!(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\in W(\lambda_{2}^{(1)};\mathscr{A}^{(1)})\setminus\{0\}.

Note that
q_{1}q_{3}-q_{2}^{2}<0
.
Since
W(\lambda_{2}^{(1)};\mathscr{A}^{(1)})\cap\mathcal{P}_{+}=\emptyset

and
(W(\lambda_{1}^{(1)};\mathscr{A}^{(1)})\oplus W(\lambda_{2}^{(1)};\mathscr{A}^{(1)}))\cap\mathcal{P}_{+}=\emptyset
, one has

\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}((q_{3}+t)(q_{1}-t)-q_{2}^{2})>0
,
which reads as
2|q_{2}|<|q_{1}+q_{3}|
.
In particular,
q_{1}+q_{3}\neq 0
.
We define

\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}^{(2)}\\
u_{2}^{(2)}\end{bmatrix}=\mathscr{M}^{(2)}\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}^{(1)}\\
u_{2}^{(1)}\end{bmatrix}

with

\mathscr{M}^{(2)}=\begin{bmatrix}\cosh\tau&\sinh\tau\\
\sinh\tau&\cosh\tau\end{bmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\tau:=\frac{1}{2}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{1}+q_{3}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
\mathscr{A}^{(2)}
be the new matrix.
A computation shows that

\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{M}^{(2)}){}^{t}\!(1,0,-1)={}^{t}\!(1,0,-1)
and that
the second component of
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{M}^{(2)})\vec{q}
vanishes. Hence,

W(\lambda_{2}^{(1)};\mathscr{A}^{(2)})=\Span\{{}^{t}\!(1,0,-1)\},\quad W(\lambda_{2}^{(1)};\mathscr{A}^{(2)})=\Span\{{}^{t}\!(1,0,-\widetilde{q}_{3})\}

with some
\widetilde{q}_{3}\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
.
We may suppose
\widetilde{q}_{3}\in(0,1)
by applying
(u_{1}^{(2)},u_{2}^{(2)})\mapsto(u_{2}^{(2)},-u_{1}^{(2)})
if necessary,
as in Subcase 1-(a) of the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
We define
\eta_{1}=\widetilde{q}_{3}/(1-\widetilde{q}_{3})\in(0,\infty)
so that

W(\lambda_{2}^{(1)};\mathscr{A}^{(2)})=\Span\{{}^{t}\!(1+\eta_{1},0,-\eta_{1})\}
.

We finally adjust the magnitude of the matrix by
u_{j}^{(3)}:=|\lambda_{3}^{(1)}|^{1/2}u_{j}^{(2)}
so that the minimum eigenvalue becomes
-1
.
Let
\mathscr{A}^{(3)}
be the new matrix. Let
\lambda_{j}:=\lambda_{j}^{(1)}/|\lambda_{3}^{(1)}|
(
j=1,2
) be the positive eigenvalues of
\mathscr{A}^{(3)}
. We have

\displaystyle W(\lambda_{1};\mathscr{A}^{(3)})

\displaystyle=\Span\{{}^{t}\!(1,0,-1)\},

\displaystyle W(\lambda_{2};\mathscr{A}^{(3)})

\displaystyle=\Span\{{}^{t}\!(1+\eta_{1},0,-\eta_{1})\},

\displaystyle W(-1;\mathscr{A}^{(3)})

\displaystyle=\Span\{{}^{t}\!(\eta_{2},1,\eta_{3})\}

with
\eta_{1}>0
and
\eta_{2}\eta_{3}>1
.
Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{(3)}={}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&1+\eta_{1}&\eta_{2}\\
0&0&1\\
-1&-\eta_{1}&\eta_{3}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}&0\\
0&0&-1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&1+\eta_{1}&\eta_{2}\\
0&0&1\\
-1&-\eta_{1}&\eta_{3}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}-(1+\eta_{1})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})&(1+\eta_{1})(\eta_{2}+\eta_{3})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})-(\lambda_{1}+1)\eta_{2}&-(1+\eta_{1})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\\
0&-1&0\\
\eta_{1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})&-\eta_{1}(\eta_{2}+\eta_{3})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})-(\lambda_{1}+1)\eta_{3}&\eta_{1}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})+\lambda_{1}\end{bmatrix}

with
\eta_{1}>1
and
\eta_{2}\eta_{3}>1
.
This corresponds to the case
\eta_{1}>0
of
\mathscr{A}_{2,2}
.
∎

Sex-dependent effects of tamoxifen on mouse behavior
Authors: Ru-Xia Xu, Shi-Han Liao, Nan Wang, Wen-Ying Jiang, Xun Wang, Xiao-Wen Li, Tian-Ming Gao, Jian-Ming Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to investigate the sex-dependent behavioral effects of tamoxifen, commonly used to induce Cre recombination in transgenic systems, across three developmental stages (adult, adolescent, and neonatal) and two CreERT2 mouse lines (CaMKIIα-CreERT2 and Aldh1l1-CreERT2). Both male and female mice, including wild-type C57BL/6J and CreERT2 transgenic lines, were subjected to tamoxifen treatment followed by behavioral tests assessing locomotion, anxiety-like behavior (open field test and elevated plus maze), social interaction, recognition memory (new object recognition), sucrose preference, and depression-like behavior (forced swimming test) at least 4 weeks post-treatment. We found that adult tamoxifen treatment increased depression-like behavior specifically in males, while adolescent treatment increased sucrose preference only in males, yet impaired recognition memory and increased depression-like behavior in both sexes. Neonatal treatment caused pervasive impairments, reducing locomotion and increasing anxiety- and depression-like behavior in both sexes, while enhancing social interaction only in males. Furthermore, effects differed in adult CaMKIIα-CreERT2 and Aldh1l1-CreERT2 mice. In the former, tamoxifen treatment impaired locomotion, increased anxiety-like behavior, and reduced recognition memory specifically in females, while increasing sucrose preference in males. In the latter, tamoxifen treatment impaired recognition memory in both sexes but increased sucrose preference only in males. These results demonstrate that tamoxifen alone induced long-lasting, sex-specific behavioral alterations dependent on developmental exposure. Furthermore, interactions of tamoxifen with CreERT2 expression introduced additional complexity, potentially confounding genetic studies. These findings emphasize the necessity of including appropriate controls (vehicle, tamoxifen-only, Cre-only) and both sexes in studies using the tamoxifen-inducible CreERT2-loxP system.Keywords: Tamoxifen, Sex, Behavior, CreERT2-loxP system, CaMKIIα-CreERT2, Aldh1l1-CreERT2, Development

Introduction

The CreERT2-loxP system has been widely used to achieve Cre recombinase-mediated spatiotemporal and cell type-specific gene mutagenesis. To this end, tamoxifen is most frequently delivered systemically to induce the expression of Cre recombinase, which then recognizes the loxP sites and recombines the loxP sequences [10, 11, 18]. However, accumulating evidence suggests that tamoxifen and its metabolites can not only bind to estrogen receptors with mixed agonist/antagonist activity due to their structural similarity to estrogen, but also interact with multiple signaling pathways independently of their interaction with estrogen receptors. This dual mechanism can cause long-lasting effects on behavior [4, 9, 25, 26].

Notably, current neuroscience research primarily uses male animals, driven by the assumption that estrous cycles increase female variability [17, 43]. However, this assumption overlooks the sex differences prevalent in many neuropsychiatric disorders [2]. For example, women are diagnosed with major depression and anxiety disorders twice as often as men are [27, 30, 32]. This underscores the importance of including both sexes in preclinical research to understand the biological underpinnings of such differences and develop effective, sex-specific therapies [6, 8, 29, 34]. Given that tamoxifen is a potent estrogen receptor modulator and that estrogen signaling pathways differ between males and females and play different roles in behavior [14, 16, 33], the effects of tamoxifen on the brain and behavior are likely to be sexually dimorphic. Therefore, systematically investigating the potential long-term behavioral consequences of tamoxifen administration per se in both sexes is essential to avoid misinterpretation of genetic manipulation studies using the CreERT2-loxP system [21, 40].

In this study, we administered tamoxifen using dosing regimens commonly used to induce Cre recombination to both male and female wild-type C57BL/6J mice across three developmental stages: adulthood (P56–P63), adolescence (P21–P25), and neonatal period (P0–P2) [10, 12, 13, 15, 36, 40, 41]. Then, at least 4 weeks after the last tamoxifen treatment, we subjected these mice to a battery of behavioral tests assessing locomotion, anxiety-like behavior, social interaction, recognition memory, sucrose preference, and depression-like behavior. Furthermore, to determine whether tamoxifen’s effects might be modulated by or interact with CreERT2 expression in specific neural cell types, we extended our analysis to include two common CreERT2 transgenic mouse lines: CaMKIIα-CreERT2 (predominantly expressed in forebrain excitatory neurons) [24] and Aldh1l1-CreERT2 (expressed broadly in astrocytes) [15, 36].

Materials and methods

Animals

Adult wild-type C57BL/6J mice aged 6–7 weeks were obtained from the Laboratory Animal Center at Southern Medical University in Guangzhou, China. CaMKIIα-CreERT2 transgenic mice (Strain #: 012362) were obtained from the Jackson Laboratory. Aldh1l1-CreERT2 transgenic mice were generated and validated as previously described [15, 41]. Mice were housed in standard laboratory cages (four to five per cage) on a 12 h light/dark cycle (lights on at 8:00 a.m.) in a temperature-controlled room (21–25 °C). Food and water were given ad libitum unless indicated otherwise. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the Guidelines of the Chinese Council on Animal Care and approved by the Southern Medical University Animal Ethics Committee. Efforts were made to minimize animal suffering and reduce the number of animals used.

Tamoxifen treatment

Tamoxifen (Sigma-Aldrich, #T5648) was dissolved in 10% ethanol/90% sunflower oil (Sigma-Aldrich, #S5007) (v/v) at a final concentration of 20 mg/ml. Adult mice received seven consecutive intraperitoneal injections of tamoxifen (75 mg/kg/day) starting at 8 weeks old. Four weeks after the last injection, the mice underwent behavioral tests. Adolescent mice received five consecutive intraperitoneal injections of tamoxifen (75 mg/kg/day) starting at postnatal day (P) 21 and were subjected to behavioral tests starting at P56. The day of birth was considered P0. Neonatal mice received three consecutive injections of tamoxifen (6 µl, 20 mg/ml) into the mouse’s stomach at P0, P1 and P2 and were subjected to behavioral tests starting at P56.

Behavioral analysis

All behavioral tests were carried out during the active/dark cycle (9.0 a.m.–19.00 p.m.) by an investigator who was blinded to the study conditions. To mitigate potential interference between behavioral paradigms, we conducted the tests in the order of open field test (OFT), social interaction, new object recognition (NOR), sucrose preference, elevated plus maze (EPM) and forced swimming test (FST). A minimum 3-day interval was applied after each experiment when mice were retested.

The open-field apparatus was a rectangular chamber (40 × 40 × 30 cm) that was composed of gray polyvinyl chloride. The center area was illuminated using 25-W halogen bulbs (200 cm above the field). The mice were gently placed into the center of the testing chamber for a 30-min recording period and were monitored using an automated video tracking system. The digitized image of the path taken was automatically analyzed using the DigBehv animal behavior analysis program. The apparatus was cleaned between the sessions using 75% ethanol. The distance traveled during the 30-min recording period, as well as the number of entries into the center and the time spent in the center during the first 5-min recording period, were analyzed.

The open-field arena for social interaction test (SIT) was custom-crafted (40 cm × 40 cm × 25 cm). A small cage (10 cm × 7 cm × 15 cm) with holes is centered against one wall of the arena. The interaction zone of the arena encompasses a 26 cm × 15 cm rectangular area projecting 8 cm around the small cage. The two corner zones encompass a 9 cm × 9 cm area opposite the small cage. In the first 2.5 min, the mouse was allowed to explore the arena freely. In the second 2.5 min, a CD1 mouse was placed into the small cage. During the two sessions, the duration in the social interaction area were recorded and analyzed by the Noldus software. The arena was cleaned between the sessions using 75% ethanol. The social interaction ratio was defined as target-social-interaction time divided by no-target-social-interaction time.

The new object recognition (NOR) test was performed as previously described [20]. The NOR apparatus consisted of a rectangular chamber (33 × 33 × 20 cm) that was made of black polyvinyl chloride. Each mouse was habituated to the empty arena for 10 min 1 day before the familiarization session. During the familiarization session, two identical objects (a tower of Lego bricks and a Falcon tissue culture flask filled with sand) were placed 8 cm away from the walls, and the mice were allowed to explore each object freely until 20 s of total exploration time was reached or until the 10-min period was over. Twenty-four hours later, one familiar object and one novel object were placed sequentially in the arena, and the experiment was terminated when 20 s of exploration of both objects was reached or when the 5-min period was over. The recognition index (RI) was calculated to measure the recognition memory of each mouse. The RI was computed as follows: the time spent exploring the novel object divided by the total exploration time.

The sucrose preference test (SPT) we used is an 4-day protocol. On day 1, the mice were provided with ad libitum access to food and two bottles with one containing 2% sucrose and one containing plain water. On day 2, the mice were exposed to same two bottles on day 1 with the bottle positions switched. On day 3, the mice were deprived of food and water during the last 12 h. On day 4, the mice were provided with ad libitum access to food and two bottles containing either 2% sucrose or plain water. Bottle positions were counterbalanced after 2.5 h in a 5-h sucrose preference test. The bottles were weighed daily, and the consumption was calculated. The sucrose preference was calculated by sucrose consumption/(sucrose consumption + water consumption).

The elevated plus maze (EPM) test comprised two open arms (5 × 30 cm) located opposite one another and two opposing enclosed arms (5 × 30 × 15 cm) 40 cm above the floor. The junction of the four arms (the central platform) measured 5 × 5 cm. All experimental mice were taken to a dimly lit testing room 1 h prior to the test. At the beginning of the 5 min test, the mice were placed onto the central platform of the elevated plus-maze facing an open arm. The maze was cleaned between the sessions using 75% ethanol. The time spent and distance traveled in the closed and open arms were automatically quantified using the Noldus software.

The forced swimming test (FST) was performed in a clear glass cylinder (height: 45 cm, diameter: 19 cm), which was filled to 23 cm with water (23 ± 1 °C). The test lasted for 6 min. The duration of immobility was recorded during the final 4 min.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism version 10.1.2 (GraphPad Software, Inc., Ja Jolla, California). All data are expressed as the mean ± SEM. Two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test was used for comparisons of two factors. * P < 0.05; ** P < 0.01; *** P < 0.001; **** P < 0.0001.

Results

Effects of tamoxifen treatment in adult C57BL/6J wild-type mice

To determine whether tamoxifen has any sexually divergent effects on mouse behavior, we used both male and female mice subjected to tamoxifen treatment protocols commonly used for inducible mutagenesis to perform a series of behavioral tests. Adult mice of both sexes received seven consecutive intraperitoneal injections of oil or tamoxifen (75 mg/kg/day) and were subjected to behavioral tests 4 weeks after the last injection (Fig. 1A). This dosing regimen has been widely used to induce Cre recombination in adult mice [13, 15, 41].

Effects of tamoxifen treatment in adult C57BL/6J wild-type mice. A Diagram of oil or tamoxifen injection. B–K tamoxifen treatment (75 mg/kg/day for 7 days) in adult C57BL/6J wild-type mice had no effect on behavioral performance in the open field test (OFT, B–D), elevated plus maze test (EPM, E–G), social interaction test (SIT, H), new object recognition test (NOR, I), and sucrose preference test (SPT, J) in male and female mice, but increased immobility time in the forced swimming test (FST, K) in male but not female mice. All data are expressed as the mean ± SEM. Two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test. * P < 0.05; **** P < 0.0001; ns, no significance. Each data point represents an individual mouse. Number of animals reported inside or above the bars. Numbers atop bar graphs indicated P-values

In the open field test (OFT), we measured the total distance traveled during the 30-min period for the evaluation of locomotor activity in low stress conditions, and found no sex-specific difference, as both tamoxifen-treated male and female mice travelled similar distance compared with their respective oil-treated counterparts (Fig. 1B). In the elevated plus maze (EPM) test, we measured the total distance traveled during the 5-min period for the evaluation of locomotor activity in high stress conditions, and found no sex-specific difference (Fig. 1E). Together, these findings suggest that tamoxifen did not influence locomotor activity in mice of both sexes.

To evaluate anxiety-like behavior [22], we measured the time spent in the center and the number of entries into the center in the OFT (Fig. 1C, D), as well as the time spent in the open arms and the number of entries into the open arms in the EPM (Fig. 1F, G), and found no sex-specific difference. Together, these findings suggest that tamoxifen had no effect on anxiety-like behavior in mice of both sexes.

To evaluate social function, we measured the time spent in the interaction zone with or without a target in the social interaction test (SIT) (data not shown). The social interaction ratio was calculated as the time spent in the interaction zone with a target divided by that without a target. We found that both tamoxifen-treated male and female mice displayed similar social interaction ratio compared with their respective oil-treated counterparts (Fig. 1H). This suggests that tamoxifen had no effect on social behavior.

To evaluate recognition memory, we measured the time spent exploring either the familiar or the novel object in the new object recognition (NOR) test (data not shown). The recognition index was calculated as the time spent exploring the novel object divided by the total time spent exploring both the novel and the familiar objects. No sex-specific difference was found as both tamoxifen-treated male and female mice displayed similar recognition index compared with their respective oil-treated controls (Fig. 1I).

To evaluate anhedonia-like behavior, we measured the consumption of sucrose and water in the sucrose preference test (SPT). The sucrose preference was calculated as sucrose consumption divided by total consumption. We found that both tamoxifen-treated male and female mice exhibited similar sucrose preference compared with their respective oil-treated controls (Fig. 1J). This suggests no sex-specific effect of tamoxifen on anhedonia-like behavior in mice of both sexes.

To evaluate depression-like behavior, we measured the immobility time in the forced swimming test (FST). Tamoxifen-treated male but not female mice spent more immobility time compared with their respective oil-treated counterparts (Fig. 1K, two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test, treatment effect, F(1, 43) = 4.396, P = 0.042; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.036; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.439), suggesting a sex-specific effect of tamoxifen on depression-like behavior in mice. These findings are consistent with our previous study [21].

Effects of tamoxifen treatment in adolescent C57BL/6J wild-type mice

Adolescence is critical for the development of many behavioral traits [35]. Adolescence in mice starts at weaning (~ P21) [3]. To determine whether tamoxifen treatment during adolescence has any long-lasting effect on adult mouse behavior, adolescent mice were administered five consecutive intraperitoneal injections of oil or tamoxifen (75 mg/kg/day) starting at P21 and subjected to behavioral tests starting at P56 (Fig. 2A). This dosing regimen was chosen based on previous studies [23, 42].

In the OFT (Fig. 2B–D), EPM (Fig. 2E–G), and SIT (Fig. 2H), we did not observe any sex-specific differences between oil- and tamoxifen-treated mice. This suggests that adolescent tamoxifen treatment had no effect on locomotor activity, anxiety-like behavior, or social behavior in adult mice of both sexes. In the NOR test, tamoxifen-treated male and female mice exhibited a dramatical decrease in the recognition index compared with their respective oil-treated counterparts (Fig. 2I, two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test, treatment effect, F(1, 41) = 9.737, P = 0.003; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.033; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.034), suggesting that adolescent tamoxifen treatment impaired recognition memory in both sexes. In the SPT, tamoxifen treatment led to a slight yet significant increase in sucrose preference in male but not female mice (Fig. 2J, two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test, treatment effect, F(1, 42) = 4.338, P = 0.043; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.018; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.625), suggesting a sex-specific effect of tamoxifen on anhedonia-like behavior. In the FST, tamoxifen-treated mice exhibited a dramatical increase in immobility time compared with their oil-treated controls in both male and female mice (Fig. 2K, two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test, treatment effect, F(1, 41) = 22.15, P = 0.001; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.030; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.013), suggesting that adolescent tamoxifen treatment induced depression-like behavior regardless of sex.

Effects of tamoxifen treatment in neonatal C57BL/6J wild-type mice

The tamoxifen inducible Cre-loxP recombination system has been widely used to study the functions of specific genes during early postnatal development [12, 19, 31]. To determine whether neonatal tamoxifen treatment can lead to long-lasting changes in behavioral performance in adult mice of both sexes, newborn mice received three consecutive injections of oil or tamoxifen (6 µl, 20 mg/ml) into the mouse’s stomach at P0, P1 and P2 and were subjected to behavioral tests starting at P56 (Fig. 3A). This dosing regimen was chosen based on previous studies [12, 28].

In the OFT (Fig. 3B) and EPM (Fig. 3E), tamoxifen treatment led to a dramatic decrease in the distance travelled in both low and high stress conditions, suggesting that neonatal tamoxifen treatment impaired locomotor activity (two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test; Fig. 3B, treatment effect, F(1, 34) = 38.84, P < 0.0001; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P < 0.0001; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.004; Fig. 3E, treatment effect, F(1, 34) = 40.82, P < 0.0001; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P < 0.0001; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.003). Tamoxifen treatment induced a male-specific reduction in both the time spent in the center and the number of center entries in the OFT (two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test; Fig. 3C, treatment effect, F(1, 34) = 5.010, P = 0.032; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.001; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.975; 3D, treatment effect, F(1, 34) = 11.90, P = 0.002; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.001; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.198). Additionally, it reduced the time spent in the open arms and the number of open arm entries in the EPM in both sexes (two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test; Fig. 3F, treatment effect, F(1, 34) = 27.15, P < 0.0001; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.001; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.001; 3G, treatment effect, F(1, 34) = 16.22, P = 0.0003; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.003; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.016). Together, these findings suggest that neonatal tamoxifen treatment induced anxiety-like behavior, particularly in male mice. In the SIT, tamoxifen-treated mice, specifically male mice, displayed an increase in social interaction ratio (Fig. 3H, two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test, treatment effect, F(1, 34) = 7.384, P = 0.010; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.041; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.088), suggesting neonatal tamoxifen treatment enhanced social function, particularly in males. In both the NOR and SPT tests, tamoxifen treatment had no effect on recognition index (Fig. 3I) or sucrose preference (Fig. 3J) in mice of both sexes, suggesting no changes in recognition memory or anhedonia-like behavior. In the FST, tamoxifen treatment induced a dramatical increase in immobility time in mice of both sexes (Fig. 3K, two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test, treatment effect, F(1, 34) = 16.87, P = 0.0002; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.012; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.004), suggesting that neonatal tamoxifen treatment induced depression-like behavior regardless of sex.

Effects of tamoxifen treatment in adult CaMKIIα-CreERT2 transgenic mice

The CaMKIIα-CreERT2 transgenic mouse line has been widely used to express a tamoxifen-inducible Cre recombinase in forebrain excitatory neurons [24]. To determine whether tamoxifen treatment affects mouse behavior, adult male and female CaMKIIα-CreERT2 mice were subjected to tamoxifen treatment followed by behavioral tests as previously described for adult C57BL/6J mice (Fig. 4A).

We found that tamoxifen treatment induced a female-specific decrease in the distance traveled in the OFT (Fig. 4B, two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test, treatment effect, F(1, 40) = 4.636, P = 0.037; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.622; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.012), but not in the EPM (Fig. 4E). Similarly, tamoxifen treatment induced anxiety-like behavior in low but not high stress conditions, particular in females, as tamoxifen-treated female mice spent less time in the center and had less entries into the center in the OFT [two-way ANOVA followed by Fisher LSD post-test; Fig. 4C, treatment effect, F(1, 40) = 9.183, P = 0.004; Male(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.042; Female(Oil vs. tamoxifen), P = 0.034; 4D], but spent similar time in the open arms and had similar entries into open arms in the EPM (Fig. 4F, G), compared with oil-treated controls. In the FST, tamoxifen-treated mice showed similar immobility time compared with oil-treated controls (Fig. 4K), suggesting that tamoxifen treatment did not affect depression-like behavior in CaMKIIα-CreERT2 mice of both sexes.

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrate that tamoxifen administration, at doses commonly used for Cre induction, exerts long-lasting behavioral alterations that are dependent on sex, developmental stage, and interaction with CreERT2 expression in specific cell types (Table 1). This extends the limited existing literature, which has previously reported only increased depression-like behavior in the FST in adult wild-type males [21, 40], a finding we confirm.

A major finding of this study is the sexual dimorphism in tamoxifen’s behavioral impact. This dimorphism manifests across multiple behavioral domains. For example, in adult wild-type mice, tamoxifen treatment significantly increased immobility time in the FST in males but not females (Fig. 1G). This is consistent with previous studies [40]; Li et al., [21] and indicates a male-specific induction or exacerbation of depression-like behavior. Sex-specific modulation of sucrose preference, indicative of anhedonia-like behavior, was also evident. Adolescent tamoxifen treatment increased sucrose preference (reduced anhedonia-like behavior) specifically in males (Fig. 2J). Similarly, in adult CaMKIIα-CreERT2 and Aldh111-CreERT2 transgenic mice, tamoxifen treatment increased sucrose preference specifically in males (Figs. 4J and 5J). This consistent male-specific effect points towards a potential role for tamoxifen in modulating reward pathways in a sex-dependent manner, possibly involving estrogen receptor signaling in regions critical for reward processing [5, 37]. While tamoxifen generally lacked major effects on locomotion or anxiety, sex-specific effects emerged in CaMKIIα-CreERT2 transgenic mice, where tamoxifen impaired locomotor activity and induced anxiety-like behavior in the OFT specifically in females (Fig. 4B–D). This female vulnerability suggests potential interactions between tamoxifen, Cre expression, and estrogen-sensitive neuronal circuits in the forebrain that regulate activity and anxiety, differentially affecting females. Notably, this effect was absent in the EPM (Fig. 4E–G), highlighting a context-dependency. Female-specific vulnerability was also observed for recognition memory in the NOR in CaMKIIα-CreERT2 mice, where tamoxifen impaired performance only in females (Fig. 4I). This suggests that tamoxifen’s interaction with Cre expression in excitatory neurons may disproportionately disrupt cognitive function in females. Together, these sex-dependent effects underscore the importance of including female animals in studies using the tamoxifen-inducible CreERT2-loxP system [17, 43].

The timing of tamoxifen administration proved to be a critical determinant of its long-term behavioral consequences, with distinct profiles emerging for neonatal, adolescent, and adult treatment in wild-type mice (Figs. 1, 2 and 3). Neonatal tamoxifen treatment (P0-P2) had the most pervasive and severe long-term effects in adulthood. Both sexes exhibited dramatically reduced locomotor activity in the OFT and EPM (Fig. 3B, E), increased anxiety-like behavior in the OFT and EPM (Fig. 3C, D, F, G), and significantly increased depression-like behavior in the FST (Fig. 3K). These profound impacts likely reflect tamoxifen’s disruption of early postnatal development, a time of rapid brain growth, synaptogenesis, and circuit refinement sensitive to endocrine disruption. Estrogen plays vital roles in neurodevelopment, including cell proliferation, differentiation, and synaptic plasticity [14, 38]. Tamoxifen’s antagonism of estrogen receptors during this sensitive window may alter the development of motor circuits, stress response systems, and emotional regulation pathways. Adolescent tamoxifen treatment (P21–P25) primarily impacted cognitive and affective domains in adulthood, as both males and females showed significant impairment in recognition memory in the NOR (Fig. 2I) and increased depression-like behavior in the FST (Fig. 2K). Interestingly, a male-specific increase in sucrose preference was observed (Fig. 2J). Adolescence is characterized by significant neural remodeling, particularly in the prefrontal cortex and limbic structures involved in cognition, emotion, and reward [35]. Tamoxifen treatment during this period may preferentially target these maturing circuits, leading to lasting cognitive deficits and heightened depression in both sexes, while paradoxically enhancing reward sensitivity in males. The dissociation between increased depression-like behavior in the FST and reduced anhedonia-like behavior in the SPT in males highlights the complexity of tamoxifen’s actions on different neural systems. Adult tamoxifen administration produced relatively fewer and often sex-specific effects, as only male-specific increased depression-like behavior in the FST in wild-types was observed (Fig. 1K). However, as discussed above, significant sex-specific effects emerged in transgenic mouse models (Figs. 4 and 5). This suggests that while the mature brain is more resilient to the general behavioral effects of tamoxifen alone, the combination with CreERT2 expression in specific cell types may unmask significant, sex-dependent vulnerabilities. The contrast in outcomes based on developmental timing underscores that tamoxifen’s effects depend on the maturational state of the brain. Early life periods (neonatal and adolescent) represent windows of heightened vulnerability where tamoxifen exposure may derail normal development, leading to pervasive and long-lasting behavioral alterations, often affecting fundamental functions like locomotion and emotional regulation. Adult administration primarily reveals effects when interacting with specific genetic manipulations or in specific behavioral domains. These findings implicate that for studies using tamoxifen-inducible CreERT2 systems, the chosen age of induction should be carefully considered, and the potential long-term confounds arising from developmental exposure rigorously controlled for.

We noted a complex interactions of tamoxifen with CreERT2 expression in specific neural cell types in adults (Figs. 1, 4 and 5). In some cases, the effects of tamoxifen alone were masked in the presence of CreERT2. For example, the male-specific increased depression-like behavior in the FST was observed in wild-types (Fig. 1K) but not in CaMKIIα-CreERT2 or Aldh111-CreERT2 transgenic mice (Figs. 4J and K and 5J and K). In other cases, the effects of tamoxifen were manifested in the presence of CreERT2. For example, adult tamoxifen treatment caused recognition memory impairment specifically in female CaMKIIα-CreERT2 mice (Fig. 4I) and in Aldh111-CreERT2 mice of both sexes (Fig. 5I) but not in wild-types (Fig. 1I). Similarly, the male-specific reduction in anhedonia-like behavior in the SPT was observed in CaMKIIα-CreERT2 and Aldh111-CreERT2 transgenic mice (Figs. 4J and 5J) but not in wild-types (Fig. 1J). These findings suggest potential synergistic or antagonistic interactions between tamoxifen and CreERT2 expression. Furthermore, the behavioral consequences differed depending on the cell types expressing CreERT2. CaMKIIα-CreERT2 expression combined with tamoxifen primarily impacted females, causing hypoactivity and anxiety-like behavior in the OFT (Fig. 4B-D), and cognitive impairment in the NOR (Fig. 4I). In contrast, Aldh111-CreERT2 expression combined with tamoxifen impaired cognition in both sexes (Fig. 5I) and increased sucrose preference in males (Fig. 5J). Notably, previous studies have demonstrated that tamoxifen-inducible Cre expression without recombination in DAT-Cre, Syn1-Cre, and Chat-Cre mice leads to sex-dependent behavioral alterations, including changes in locomotion, anxiety, and learning and memory [1, 7, 39]. Together, these findings highlights that the cellular context of Cre expression critically shapes the behavioral outcome of tamoxifen administration. The mechanisms underlying these behavioral alterations caused by tamoxifen alone or the combination of tamoxifen and Cre recombinase expression are unknow and awaits further studies.

It is important to note that in the present study, we did not track the estrous cycle in adult female mice during tamoxifen treatment and behavioral testing. Given that both the estrogen levels and estrogen receptor expression and sensitivity fluctuate across the estrous cycle [16, 33], it is plausible that the behavioral effects of tamoxifen—a mixed estrogen receptor modulator—could vary depending on the hormonal milieu at the time of tamoxifen treatment. For instance, in phases of high endogenous estrogen (e.g., proestrus), tamoxifen may exert more pronounced antagonistic effects. Conversely, during low-estrogen phases (e.g., diestrus), its partial agonist activity might dominate. This could introduce variability in female responses, particularly in behaviors known to be estrogen-sensitive, such as recognition memory and affective behaviors [14]. Future studies are required to address this caveat.

Together, our findings provide evidence that tamoxifen alone can exert long-lasting, sex-dependent, and developmentally regulated effects on mouse behavior. Furthermore, its interaction with CreERT2 expression in specific cell types introduces additional layers of complexity and potential confounding variables. These findings have implications for the design and interpretation of studies utilizing the CreERT2-loxP system. In such studies, vehicle-only controls, tamoxifen-only controls, and Cre-only controls (without loxP) should be considered in both sexes to enhance the validity, reliability, and translational relevance of findings derived from the CreERT2-loxP system.

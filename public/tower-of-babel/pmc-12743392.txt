The experiences of nurses working in an earthquake region and the effect of these experiences on their professional approaches: a qualitative study
Authors: Ahmet Kılıç, Dilek Çeçen Çamlı
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackroundThe aim of this study was to examine the effect of nurses’ experiences on their professional approaches during the earthquake in Turkey, which was called the disaster of the century.MethodsPhenomenological research, a qualitative research design, was applied in this study. This qualitative study included 42 volunteer nurses. In this study, focus group interviews were conducted using a semi-structured questionnaire and nurses’ feelings about their disaster experiences were evaluated. Interviews were conducted using face-to-face interview technique. Braun and Clarke’s phenomenological analysis method was used to analyze the data.ResultsThe themes obtained from the analysis of the data were as follows: Negative feelings towards what was encountered in the earthquake zone (Sadness, helplessness, fear, disorder, confusion, hopelessness); positive feelings due to being in the earthquake zone to help (Volunteerism, feeling proud/helpful); Ensuring professional unity (Effort to fulfill the profession despite the scarce materials and conditions, realizing the sanctity of the profession, increasing commitment to the profession and loving the profession more).ConclusionsThis study determined that nurses working in earthquake zones experienced an increase in their sense of the sanctity of their profession, compassion, and commitment to professional discipline, and that these were important factors that improved their professional performance and strengthened their commitment to their profession.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Introduction

Earthquake is a social event as well as a natural one. Earthquakes directly affect individual and community life with various consequences in the regions where they occur. On February 6, 2023, two large earthquakes occurred 9 h apart on the Eastern Anatolian Fault in Turkey [1]. The first earthquake occurred at 04.17 local time with a magnitude of 7.7 and the second earthquake occurred at 13.24 local time with a magnitude of 7.6 [1]. A total of 11 provinces were significantly affected by the earthquakes and serious damage and destruction occurred in the region. The earthquake, which lasted for approximately 65 s and severely destroyed 11 provinces, 124 districts, 6,929 villages and neighborhoods in an area of 120 thousand square kilometers, with 53,537 casualties and 107,213 injured according to official records as of 02.04.2023, is defined as the “disaster of the century” [2].

Contrary to previous seismic events in the country, this earthquake saw more intensive mobilization of support for victims at local, national and global levels thanks to the effective role of social media [1]. However, public choice and the inability of government institutions to organize quickly, selfishness in the distribution of aid and the creation of heavy traffic made it difficult to provide assistance at the beginning of the earthquake. The large impact area caused serious problems in the delivery of medical and social aid to the region. However, health personnel formed a voluntary organization chain and were sent to the region from the first day. Health workers were able to reach the disaster areas by walking for kilometers as the roads were destroyed due to the fault line and the existing roads were closed to access. Due to the difficulties and limitations in the transportation of medical equipment to the region, health workers had difficulty in providing the necessary medical assistance in the disaster area.

Considering the effects of disasters, especially earthquakes, on health, the provision of health care has an important place before, during and after the disaster. Therefore, it is essential that healthcare providers are properly prepared [3]. Nurses constitute the largest healthcare provider group that plays an important role in disaster response [4]. Although nurses are at the forefront of healthcare providers in the intervention phase, they may feel unprepared due to limited resources [5]. They face many problems and difficulties while fulfilling their responsibilities during a disaster [6]. While providing services to earthquake victims, they may experience problems such as emotionally difficult and stressful working conditions, unfavorable working conditions, feeling inadequate, lack of physical and mental preparation in the assignment process, inadequate preassignment program and training [6]. After a disaster, nurses are expected to maintain care despite difficult conditions.

Considering the destruction and damage caused by the earthquake, there is a real need for exploratory studies to investigate the impact of this damage on the feelings and professional practices of nurses working there. Nurses who have experienced disasters may be directly confronted with psychological effects such as intense stress, trauma, feelings of loss, and burnout [5]. Identifying these effects will enable understanding of the emotional burden experienced by nurses and, accordingly, enable institutions and managers to plan protective interventions such as psychosocial support programs, counseling services, and stress management training [1]. Furthermore, researching the emotional processes experienced by nurses who have lived through disasters also yields important results in terms of the quality of nursing care [1, 3]. It is known that nurses who are psychologically supported and whose emotional resilience is strengthened provide more empathetic, attentive, and effective care to their patients, even in disaster conditions [4]. Therefore, a thorough examination of the emotional and professional experiences of nurses who have experienced disasters will contribute significantly to the development of strategies that will improve the quality of care in future disasters and strengthen nurses’ professional resilience.

Qualitative research is well suited for such studies; therefore, a qualitative descriptive study was designed to address the aim of this study: to explore the experiences of nurses working in surgical units deployed to the region in the aftermath of the earthquake of the century in Turkey, particularly issues related to their professional practice. Qualitative studies provide a rich description of a phenomenon about which little is known [7]. Qualitative research designs also include broad research questions that capture various dimensions of a new situation or event and allow researchers to provide a holistic and detailed description [7].

In this study, it was aimed not only to understand the experiences of nurses in the earthquake zone, but also to comprehensively investigate and carefully document how the experiences of nurses in this critical situation affected their professional approaches. By focusing on the first-hand experiences of nurses, it was aimed to determine their approaches to disaster management and the extent to which this situation affected their professional attitudes in the provision of health services against unpredictable negativities.

Methods

In this study, a semi-structured questionnaire was used to assess nurses’ feelings about their disaster experiences through focus group interviews. In order to capture the experiences of the participants without any predetermined theoretical framework, the interviews were analyzed using inductive thematic analysis according to Braun and Clarke.

Setting and context

This descriptive phenomenological study included nurses working in the surgical units of two public state hospitals and a university hospital in the western Turkey who were assigned to the earthquake zone in the eastern part of the country.

Participants

Participants were nurses working in the emergency department, surgical clinics and intensive care units of hospitals in the western Turkey. This qualitative and descriptive study, which employed the maximum diversity sampling method among purposeful sampling techniques, was conducted with 42 nurses. Data were analyzed using inductive content analysis.

The inclusion criteria were that the nurses had participated in relief efforts and were willing to participate in the study. All participants were nurses from Western Turkey who were willing to share their experiences with the researchers and voluntarily participated in the study. To ensure maximum diversity, nurses with different educational levels, marital status, age, work experience, assigned to one of the earthquake relief efforts (Hatay, Gaziantep, Adıyaman, and Kahramanmaraş), and currently working in different units and departments were selected to participate in the study. During the disaster relief efforts, participants provided services in mobile hospital services, operating rooms, surgical wards, intensive care, and emergency departments. Data collection continued until data saturation was achieved.

Ethical considerations

Participants were informed prior to the study and their verbal and written consent was obtained. Ethical approval was obtained from the institution’s health sciences ethics committee (Ethical Approval Date: 08.11.2023 Number: 20.478.486–2057). This study was conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration and the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) guidelines.

Research team

The analysis of the data was performed by the first and second authors (AK and DC). AK is a male nurse and master’s student with no previous experience in qualitative research. DC supported and supervised the analytical process. DC is an academic nurse with many years of experience and a doctoral degree trained in qualitative research.

Data collection

Data were collected in the form of participants’ testimonies. The researchers collected the data by face-to-face interviews with the participants and writed to experience. A semi-structured interview protocol based on open-ended questions was used for data collection. This form had not been used in any previous research. It was developed by the researchers specifically for this study (Fig. 1.) The link to the study was announced by sharing it in the whattsap group of nurses assigned to the earthquake zone from the western re Turkey, and the nurses who received permission from their institutions were offered to participate.

Reliability and validity

Four criteria were used to ensure reliability in this study: validity, consistency, verifiability, and transferability. All participants agreed to participate in the study. To ensure consistency, interviews were conducted by only one researcher, and a summary of each interview session was taken before requesting additional feedback and responses from participants. To ensure the consistency and validity of the data, the main themes and sub-themes were identified separately by both researchers and then agreed upon through discussion.

Data analysis

Data analysis was performed by two researchers with regular inputs from all the research team members and followed Braun and Clark’s (2006) six-phase methodology (Table 1) to understand and identify the themes of nurses’ experiences as they pertain to nursing management and organisation [8]. Through this qualitative analytical approach, researchers identify the coded elements from the data and then iteratively develop explanatory and comprehensive themes, and then present a final holistic thematic framework of the phenomenon under investigation. As the analysis proceeded, the themes and contents that were gradually developed were discussed by the members of the research team to ensure agreement and enhance rigour. The inclusion of participants’ quotations under their relevant thematic headings strengthened the trustworthiness and confirmability of the analysis.

Discussion

It is very important to provide health services in disaster situations. In this case, healthcare professionals, especially nurses, are among the important service providers. Although nurses are an important force to control public health risks, they are health personnel who take an active role in protecting and improving the health of themselves, their immediate environment and the community before, during and after disasters [9]. It is a social responsibility to ensure that the society damaged in disasters returns to normal as soon as possible. Professional nursing skills are of critical importance in disaster intervention. The decision-making process of nurses in disasters directly affects the quality of nursing and patient safety. Nurses are expected to work at a high level of performance in the care of patients in the presence of difficult conditions such as earthquakes, and severe conditions create additional psychosocial burden on nurses [10]. In this study, it was observed that nurses acted with professional and citizenship awareness even under difficult conditions such as disasters, and that they fought on the front lines in the provision of health care services as always.

The studies have indicated that there are five most important skills and necessary professional competencies that nurses should have during disasters [10, 11]. Especially management, ethical/legal competencies, technical competencies, teamwork and personal skills are emphasized. The studies also indicate the effectiveness of disaster training courses and drills in improving the knowledge, skills and preparations of nurses against disasters. In extraordinary situations such as disasters, national guidelines should be developed and nurses should be aware of their duties [12].

Personal experiences

In this study, the experiences of nurses working in the disaster area were categorized under three main themes: personal experiences, professional experiences, and the impact on professional approach.

Negative emotions experienced in the earthquake zone

The study found that healthcare professionals and nurses who encountered a major event such as an earthquake experienced many complex emotions, both human and professional, in a disaster environment they had never experienced before. Nurses reported experiencing negative emotions such as sadness, helplessness, fear, and anxiety brought on by the losses they witnessed.

Disaster situations such as earthquakes can cause intense stress and traumatic effects among healthcare professionals. Emotional reactions such as helplessness, sadness, and stress experienced during the disaster process may lead to long-term psychological problems such as post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and burnout syndrome [13, 14]. Moreover, the intensity of these emotions may result in anxiety, depression, sleep disorders, and concentration difficulties [15]. Consequently, this situation makes it difficult for nurses to maintain effective patient care and can negatively impact patient safety [16]. Following this devastating earthquake in our country, another study conducted by Yılmaz and Karahan (2025) described nurses’ early experiences during the disaster using the themes of fear, support for patients, and family. The researchers reported that nurses encountering a disaster for the first time experienced negative emotions such as shock, panic, and fear of death [17]. Despite these challenging emotions, the nurses tried to support their patients while also seeking information about the well-being of their own families. Similarly, in another qualitative study conducted by Gümüşler Başaran et al. (2025) with seven nurses who worked and continued to work in the same earthquake region, it was found that nurses reported negative emotions such as anxiety, hopelessness, fear, panic, and loneliness [18].

Positive emotions due to helping in the earthquake zone

The nurses who participated in the study described not only negative emotions but also positive emotions such as honor from being there and volunteering to help. Volunteering during disasters is an important factor that enhances nurses’ motivation levels and supports their psychological well-being [19]. The experiences gained during relief activities and the sense of making a difference in people’s lives strengthen intrinsic motivation [20].

The study identified positive emotions reflecting nurses’ satisfaction in working collaboratively and volunteering side by side with their colleagues. This situation represents the strengthening of social solidarity that emerges during disasters such as earthquakes and the benefits of working cooperatively [6]. This form of collaboration is largely based on altruism and mutual assistance, emphasizing the strength of social and humanitarian solidarity ties, and reinforcing the sense of unity among people through civil initiative [10].

It was also noted in the study that, in addition to providing life-saving care, nurses contributed to search and rescue operations and environmental cleaning efforts. As a result, they reported feeling good and experiencing professional satisfaction for fulfilling both their professional and civic responsibilities. Similarly, in a study conducted by Sançar et al. (2024), the majority of nurses stated that although they experienced negative emotions such as sadness and helplessness due to the disaster, they also felt a bittersweet sense of happiness while providing patient care [21].

Professional experiences

The study shows that the positive and negative emotions experienced by nurses during their work in the earthquake zone are part of their professional experience as well as their personal experience. As in the past coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic, nurses have been a key component of disaster response teams in the earthquake zone, relying on their clinical skills and intuitive decision-making abilities to manage patient care [22]. In a study conducted by Köroğlu, Kaba, and Ark (2025) with 11 nurse managers to identify the difficulties experienced in the disaster area, the difficulties were determined as: “unpreparedness” in the pre-disaster period, “emotional difficulties,” “communication and coordination problems,” and “resource scarcity” during the disaster period, and in the post-disaster period, “evaluation and professional satisfaction” [23].

Needs of earthquake survivors

In the study, nurses reported feelings of anxiety stemming from their responsibility to recognize and meet the needs of earthquake survivors during the acute phase of the disaster. The first 72 h following an earthquake are defined as the “acute phase,” which is critical both for life-saving interventions and for meeting essential needs [24]. During this period, the role of healthcare professionals extends beyond medical treatment to include a wide range of responsibilities, from providing psychosocial support to ensuring infection control. Since post-earthquake healthcare needs are complex, they require rapid response and specialized skills from healthcare professionals [25]. Meeting the needs of the injured and those experiencing psychological trauma is among the critical elements that enhance the effectiveness of disaster management plans [4, 26].

One of the primary needs during the acute phase is the rapid identification, evacuation, and transportation of injured individuals to health centers [25]. Healthcare professionals, especially in trauma-related injuries (such as crush injuries, fractures, and hemorrhagic shock), reduce mortality by providing rapid interventions in situations that require emergency life support. Triage practices are of great importance during this period [26]. For earthquake survivors, meeting basic health needs and preventing infections are vital, especially in the acute phase. The disruption of environmental hygiene conditions after an earthquake poses serious risks for diseases such as diarrhea, respiratory infections, skin disorders, and vector-borne diseases. Practices such as vaccination (e.g., tetanus), wound care, and supervision of water and sanitation conditions by healthcare professionals play a key role in infection prevention [24].

Particularly vulnerable groups such as children, the elderly, individuals with disabilities, and those with chronic illnesses are at higher risk during acute disaster phases. Directing these groups to accessible healthcare services, identifying their specific needs, and ensuring follow-up are among the primary responsibilities of healthcare professionals [14, 27]. Providing healthcare services in disaster settings also requires working with limited resources; therefore, healthcare professionals must be well-equipped in crisis management, rapid decision-making, and team coordination [14]. In addition, measures should be taken to prevent burnout in order to ensure the continuity of healthcare services.

Providing medical and spiritual care

In the disaster situations, it is important to meet not only the physical but also the psychological and spiritual needs of the patients. Spiritual support has a critical role in terms of psychological recovery and resilience after a disaster and may accelerate recovery processes [28]. Acute stress reactions, panic, shock and dissociative symptoms may be observed in earthquake victims in the post-disaster period [29]. It is important for healthcare workers to be knowledgeable about psychological first aid practices in order to increase the posttraumatic psychological resilience of individuals. It is recommended to work in coordination with psychosocial support teams [28, 29].

Inadequacy of material, experience and organization

The study highlighted that nurses experienced difficulties in the earthquake zone due to shortages of supplies and the lack of disaster experience among assigned personnel. In this earthquake, the insufficiency of disaster preparedness and the extensive damage across a wide area resulted in transportation and communication breakdowns, preventing the timely delivery of many healthcare and relief supplies to the affected regions. The lack of materials and organizational disruptions significantly limited the delivery of nursing services. Resource scarcity is one of the most common challenges faced by healthcare professionals in disaster areas, which can negatively affect the timing and effectiveness of medical interventions [12].

As observed in disasters worldwide, extraordinary situations often lead to organizational problems in providing basic needs such as shelter, food, clothing, and water, as well as in maintaining essential infrastructure like telecommunications, transportation, and electricity [25]. Similarly, another qualitative study conducted in our country reported that nurses providing healthcare services in the earthquake zone faced challenges such as a lack of resources, inadequate physical conditions, poor coordination and communication, hygiene issues, and safety concerns [23]. In follow-up studies conducted after the same earthquake, nurses described experiencing both operational and psychological difficulties, identifying four main themes: challenges in the care process, psychological struggles, ethical issues, and the need for education and support [30, 31]. Likewise, in line with the present study, it was emphasized that nurses had to cope with “unprecedented conditions,” which affected them both professionally and emotionally [30].

Professional unity

The strongest theme that emerged when participants described their professional experiences was “ensuring professional unity.” After the disaster that struck our country, the work carried out by nurses on the front lines in the region saved many lives, gave people hope, and was widely reported in the national media. The effectiveness of healthcare services, especially in disaster situations, largely depends on teamwork and coordination [32]. Effective teamwork among healthcare workers significantly increases the capacity to respond to disasters and ensures the continuity of healthcare services [24]. The solidarity and team spirit of nurses working voluntarily in disaster areas strengthens their commitment to their profession. The professional solidarity that emerges in disaster situations allows nurses to embrace their profession with stronger bonds [15]. For example, in another study examining the consequences of the same earthquake, Sançar et al. (2024) determined that nurses caring for earthquake victims experienced many different experiences, feelings, and thoughts simultaneously and fulfilled their duties with professional commitment and individual coping efforts in a stressful work environment despite the multifaceted challenging factors in the disaster area [21].

This study also shows that all nurses working in the earthquake zone went beyond the requirements of their job descriptions and actually demonstrated their collective resilience. The ability of nurses working in the field during disasters to maintain their professional and individual functionality through teamwork, solidarity, and joint coping mechanisms is an indicator of their collective resilience [32]. Collective resilience is defined as the ability of communities to withstand the adversities that arise during disasters and emergencies and to overcome these adversities by utilizing their available resources [33]. It is the force that enables communities to get back on their feet after disasters [32, 33].

Effect on professional approach

In the study, it was observed that the experiences of the nurses working in the earthquake zone in the acute period affected their professional approach. These effects were defined as Sanctity of Profession, Increased Sense of Compassion and Commitment to Professional Discipline.

Studies have been conducted examining the experiences of healthcare professionals and nurses during this major earthquake in our country. A study by Gülen and Göktepe (2025) on the same earthquake indicated that the experiences of nurses in the emergency zone, including the painful ones, enriched their personal development in every way. In addition to other personal achievements, they gained increased empathy and awareness of human emotions, a new perspective on life, and awareness of individual disaster preparedness [34]. The study emphasized that the nurses’ work in the region enhanced their professional development and increased their knowledge of disaster nursing. Yanık and Ediz (2024) reported that nurses’ feelings of love, respect, and commitment to their profession increased after the disaster experience [11]. Similarly, Açık and Yeşilyurt Sevim (2025) reported a significant increase in the professional satisfaction levels of nurses who participated in disaster relief efforts [35].

Other similar studies around the world similarly report that, despite the negative effects of witnessing trauma, the experiences of participants during disaster relief increased their awareness of disaster relief activities, contributed to their personal development, and had a significant impact on current nursing services [36]. Researchers also found that nurses developed a deeper awareness of the “meaning of life” after their service in the earthquake zone [36].

Sanctity of the profession

In the study, nurses emphasized that their belief in the sanctity of their profession increased as a result of their professional and personal experiences. Fulfilling professional ethical responsibilities such as saving lives and alleviating patients’ pain in disaster situations increases respect for and commitment to the profession. On the other hand, the public’s perception of healthcare workers’ approach during disasters as sacred and valuable causes nurses to feel stronger in their profession [30].

Professional responsibility is related to ethical sensitivity, which constitutes a valuable aspect of professional care in earthquakes [34, 37]. Ensuring the safety of victims and patients, i.e., earthquake survivors, is one of the fundamental requirements of ethical care. Ethical and legal challenges are particularly important for nurses during disasters [38]. Unlike routine practice, some ethical challenges arise during disasters, such as the fair distribution of scarce resources, the protection of privacy and confidentiality, the determination of treatment priorities through appropriate triage, autonomous work, and informed consent [36]. Alpert et al. (2018) reported that nurses may encounter ethical challenges during a disaster response when there are many patients and limited resources [39]. Schultz et al. (2012) emphasized that listing ethical principles to guide healthcare workers, especially during disasters, can facilitate familiarity with resource allocation approaches during a disaster response and enable them to cope with ethical dilemmas [40].

Improved sense of mercy

In the study, participants stated that the emotions they experienced during their voluntary work in the disaster area, as well as the events and difficulties they encountered, resulted in a professional change, namely an intensification of their sense of compassion. Similarly, Gülen and Göktepe (2025) stated in their study that although the experiences of nurses were painful, they developed personally and, in addition to their personal achievements, gained increased empathy, awareness of human emotions, and a new perspective on life [34]. A significant increase in compassion and empathy was observed among nurses working in the earthquake zone during the acute phase [29]. Witnessing the human drama and suffering in the disaster environment strengthens nurses’ compassion and enables them to develop more empathetic approaches to patient care [30]. Furthermore, increased compassion and empathy in disaster settings positively affect nurses’ professional performance, thereby improving the quality of care as perceived by patients [31, 33].

Commitment to professional discipline

In the study, nurses reported that their commitment to their professional discipline was strengthened through both positive and negative personal and professional experiences gained during this process, in which they voluntarily participated to provide professional support following a large-scale and devastating disaster they had witnessed for the first time. Providing care services despite limited resources, ethical issues, organizational shortcomings, and lack of experience affected them spiritually, and it is clear that this feeling was reinforced by social praise and respect. Doğan et al. (2024) asked 30 nurses working in the earthquake zone to describe their profession after the disaster and found that nurses defined it as dedication, obligation, fatigue, and satisfaction [41]. Professional discipline is one of the fundamental elements that ensures the sustainability of healthcare services during disasters and crises [22]. Their determination and self-discipline in performing their professional duties under extraordinary conditions are critical to the efficiency and continuity of healthcare services. This discipline enhances crisis management capabilities and strengthens the effectiveness of nurses in disaster areas [25]. Strengthening professional discipline enables nurses to maintain high standards in disaster situations and effectively cope with challenging conditions [26].

In this study, the strongest theme that emerged when participants who had experienced a disaster described their professional experiences was “Commitment to Professional Discipline” Particularly in extraordinary situations such as disasters, when common goals such as saving lives and meeting the aid and care needs of earthquake victims are at stake, nurses become aware of their professional solidarity and strengthen their social bonds with colleagues, which influences the formation of their professional identity [42]. Professional identities are not static; they are influenced by the political, cultural, and historical realities that surround them and, especially in work environments, develop through communication and interaction with other colleagues in the performance of professional roles and responsibilities [43]. Professional socialization and environments that create social impact, such as disasters, have a significant effect on the development of professional identity [43]. Professional identity is defined as a sense of self derived from and perceived in the roles nurses assume in their work; it is stated that a person’s professional identity is a component of their overall identity and is reinforced by their “position in society,” “interactions with others,” and “interpretation of experiences” [42, 43].

Limitation

This study provides insights into the unique experiences of nurses living in western Turkey who were assigned to the disaster area in the south-east of the country. However, it should be noted that the results cannot be generalized to all cultures and contexts. Disaster management experience is influenced by cultural, economic, social, and educational factors. Therefore, it is recommended that similar studies be conducted in other communities to gain a more comprehensive understanding of nurses’ experiences in disaster response.

Conclusion

As a result of the study, it was determined that nurses who volunteered to serve in the disaster area during the earthquake—referred to as the “disaster of the century” in Türkiye—had profound and emotionally intense professional experiences. These experiences significantly reshaped their perceptions of the nursing profession. The findings also revealed that nurses were insufficiently prepared for the disaster process and encountered various individual and managerial challenges in the aftermath. These insights provide a valuable foundation for developing improved nursing care strategies during disasters and for addressing potential post-disaster complications. In this context, it is essential to revise and strengthen existing hospital disaster plans to ensure the safety of healthcare personnel and patients, and to enable the provision of timely and effective healthcare services during the acute phase of disasters. Furthermore, implementing comprehensive in-service training programs focused on disaster management and nursing care in crisis situations is critical to enhancing nurses’ competencies in emergency response. Providing psychosocial support to healthcare workers affected by the disaster is also vital for maintaining their well-being and professional resilience. In addition, there is a pressing need to design and implement more effective disaster management systems, coordination mechanisms, and emergency preparedness plans, supported by further research in this area.

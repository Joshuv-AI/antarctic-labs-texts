MMP for Enriques pairs and singular Enriques varieties Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : Primary: 14E30, 14J42; Secondary: 14J28, 14L30. Keywords : Enriques manifolds, irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifolds, singular Enriques varieties, symplectic varieties, Minimal Model Program, termination of flips.
Francesco Antonio Denisi, Ángel David Ríos Ortiz, Nikolaos Tsakanikas, Zhixin Xie

Abstract
. We introduce and study the class of primitive Enriques varieties, whose smooth members are Enriques manifolds. We provide several examples and we demonstrate that this class is preserved under the operations of the Minimal Model Program (MMP). In particular, given an Enriques manifold Y Y and an effective ℝ \mathbb{R} -divisor B Y B_{Y} on Y Y such that the pair ( Y , B Y ) (Y,B_{Y}) is log canonical, we prove that any ( K Y + B Y ) (K_{Y}+B_{Y}) -MMP terminates with a minimal model ( Y ′ , B Y ′ ) (Y^{\prime},B_{Y^{\prime}}) of ( Y , B Y ) (Y,B_{Y}) , where Y ′ Y^{\prime} is a ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial primitive Enriques variety with canonical singularities. Finally, we investigate the asymptotic theory of Enriques manifolds.



1.
Introduction

Irreducible holomorphic symplectic (IHS) manifolds are simply connected, compact Kähler manifolds carrying a holomorphic symplectic form that generates the space of holomorphic
2
-forms. According to the Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition theorem
[
Bea83b
]
, they constitute one of the three building blocks of compact Kähler manifolds with vanishing (real) first Chern class.
Enriques manifolds, on the other hand, are projective manifolds that are not simply connected and whose universal covering is a projective IHS manifold. In particular, they can be viewed as finite étale quotients of the latter. They were defined and studied independently by Oguiso and Schröer
[
OS11a
,
OS11b
]
and by Boissière, Nieper-Wißkirchen and Sarti
[
BNWS11
]
.
IHS and Enriques manifolds are regarded as higher-dimensional generalizations of K3 and Enriques surfaces, respectively. It is natural to extend these two notions to the singular setting as well.

Primitive symplectic varieties (see
4.5
) have been introduced as singular analogs of IHS manifolds. Their class constitutes the most general framework for moduli and deformation theory
[
BL21
,
BL22
]
. It is also preserved under the operations of the Minimal Model Program (MMP). Moreover, given a log canonical pair
(X,B)
such that
X
is a projective IHS manifold and
B
is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
, Matsushita and Zhang
[
MZ13
, Theorem 4.1]
showed that any sequence of flops starting from
(X,B)
terminates with another log canonical pair
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})
such that
X^{\prime}
is a projective IHS manifold and
B^{\prime}
is a nef and effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X^{\prime}
. Lehn and Pacienza
[
LP16
, Theorem 1.2]
established later the following more general result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a projective IHS manifold and let
B
be an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
such that
(X,B)
is a log canonical pair. Then any
(K_{X}+B)
-MMP terminates with a minimal model
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})
of
(X,B)
, where
X^{\prime}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial primitive symplectic variety and
B^{\prime}
is a nef and effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X^{\prime}
.

This paper has the following two main objectives: (i) to derive an analogous termination result for log canonical pairs
(Y,B_{Y})
such that
Y
is an Enriques manifold and
B_{Y}
is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y
; and (ii)
to characterize the underlying variety of the resulting minimal model
(Y^{\prime},B_{Y^{\prime}})
of
(Y,B_{Y})
. To this end, we introduce
primitive Enriques varieties
(see
5.11
) as singular analogs of Enriques manifolds. They are finite quasi-étale quotients of primitive symplectic varieties by non-symplectic group actions.

Recall that the
termination of flips conjecture
is one of the central problems in the MMP. It has been completely resolved in dimension
3
by Kawamata and Shokurov, as well as in many cases in dimension
4
, but it remains widely open in general. We refer to
[
CT23
, Section 1]
for an overview and to
[
BCHM10
, Corollary 1.4.2]
for a partial result that is valid in any dimension. Our main result is the following and confirms in particular the termination of flips conjecture for the class of Enriques manifolds.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
Y
be an Enriques manifold and let
B_{Y}
be an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y
such that
(Y,B_{Y})
is a log canonical pair. Then any
(K_{Y}+B_{Y})
-MMP terminates with a minimal model
(Y^{\prime},B_{Y^{\prime}})
of
(Y,B_{Y})
, where
Y^{\prime}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial primitive Enriques variety with canonical singularities and
B_{Y^{\prime}}
is a nef and effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y^{\prime}
.

We present now the strategy for the proof of
1.2
. Given an Enriques pair
(Y,B_{Y})
as above, run any
(K_{Y}+B_{Y})
-MMP. Consider the universal covering
\gamma\colon X\to Y
of
Y
and the corresponding IHS pair
(X,B)
, where
B\coloneqq\gamma^{*}B_{Y}
. To prove that the given
(K_{Y}+B_{Y})
-MMP terminates, we first show that it can be lifted to a
\pi_{1}(Y)
-equivariant
(K_{X}+B)
-MMP.
We refer to Prokhorov’s survey
[
Pro21
]
for the equivariant MMP. We also stress that a step of an equivariant MMP need not be a composition of steps of a usual MMP, so
1.1
cannot yet be applied to
(X,B)
to derive
1.2
.
Therefore, we set
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\coloneqq(X,B)
and we prove next that the
\pi_{1}(Y)
-equivariant
(K_{X}+B)
-MMP can in turn be lifted to a usual
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime})
-MMP.
Since the
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime})
-MMP terminates by
1.1
, we conclude that the original
(K_{Y}+B_{Y})
-MMP also terminates.

Most of the examples of primitive Enriques varieties that we construct in this paper are finite quasi-étale quotients of irreducible symplectic varieties (see
4.3
) by non-symplectic group actions. We call these quotients
irreducible Enriques varieties
(see
5.7
).
Our definition was inspired by the fundamental paper
[
GGK19
]
of Greb, Guenancia and Kebekus, who characterized irreducible symplectic varieties in terms of holonomy groups. Building crucially on their results, we obtain a similar characterization of irreducible Enriques varieties. We refer to
Subsection 5.1
for the details.

We point out that Samuel Boissière, Chiara Camere and Alessandra Sarti introduced independently in
[
BCS24
]
another notion of singular Enriques varieties, which they call
logarithmic Enriques varieties
. Our definitions of
irreducible
and
primitive
Enriques varieties differ slightly from their definitions of logarithmic Enriques varieties
of ISV type
and
of PSV type
, respectively, because singular Enriques varieties in our sense are also allowed to have trivial canonical class, whereas they exclude this possibility.

The main body of the existing theory of Enriques manifolds is contained in the papers
[
BNWS11
,
OS11a
,
OS11b
]
. Our last goal in this paper is to further develop this theory by studying the asymptotic properties of real divisors on Enriques manifolds.
For instance, the following result is an analog of
[
Den23
, Theorem 1.6(2)]
for Enriques manifolds and characterizes their volume function, demonstrating that it is piecewise polynomial (see
6.2
).
Our result should be compared with the second theorem on
[
BKS04
, p. 210]
, see also
[
BKS04
, Subsection 3.3]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
Y
is an Enriques manifold of dimension
2n
, then its volume function

\vol_{Y}(-)\colon N^{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\ \alpha\mapsto\vol_{Y}(\alpha)

is homogeneous of degree
2n
and piecewise polynomial.

Additionally, we address
[
Pay06
, Problem]
in the setting of Enriques manifolds. More precisely, Payne asks whether the closure of the cone of
k
-ample classes (see
6.7
) and the cone of birationally
k
-movable curves (see
6.8
) are dual. This problem can be viewed as an attempt to generalize the duality between the pseudoeffective cone and the cone of movable curves, obtained by
[
BDPP13
, Theorem 2.2]
. We establish here the following duality result, which is an analog of
[
DRO25
, Theorem B]
for Enriques manifolds.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
Y
is an Enriques manifold of dimension
2n
, then for any integer

k\in\{1,\dots,2n\}

we have

\Amp^{k}(Y)^{\vee}=\bMobc_{k}(Y).

In particular, if
1\leq k\leq n
, then

\Amp^{k}(Y)^{\vee}=\NEb(Y).

The proof of the above theorem follows closely the strategy of the proof of
[
DRO25
, Theorem B]
, which settled Payne’s question for IHS manifolds, while the new inputs are the MMP lifting constructions used to establish
1.2
. It is worth mentioning that, even though the statement of
1.4
only involves an Enriques manifold, it is currently indispensable to pass to possibly singular birational models of the given Enriques manifold to conclude. Such models are
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial primitive Enriques varieties with terminal singularities in this context.

:
In

.

Everything will be used later in the study of primitive Enriques varieties.

The heart of the paper,

, is devoted to the definition and investigation of the singular generalizations of Enriques manifolds. In

,

,
5.14
and

.
In

1.2
, while in

In

and

.

2.
Preliminaries

We work throughout the paper over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers, and a variety is an integral separated
\mathbb{C}
-scheme of finite type.
We use the terminology of
[
Laz04
]
for notions of positivity and of
[
KM98
,
Fuj17
]
for standard notions in birational geometry, e.g., singularities of pairs and the Minimal Model Program (MMP). In particular, a
pair

(X,B)
consists of a normal projective variety
X
and an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B
on
X
such that
K_{X}+B
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

We frequently work also in the equivariant setting and we use
[
Pro21
]
as our main reference for the equivariant MMP.
In particular, a
G
-pair
consists of the following data: a pair
(X,B)
as above, together with a finite subgroup
G
of the automorphism group
\Aut(X)
of
X
, such that
X
is a
G
-variety and
B
is a
G
-invariant divisor. In this setting, the
G
-variety
X
is said to be
G\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
if any
G
-invariant Weil divisor on
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.

Given a normal projective variety
X
and a finite subgroup
G
of
\Aut(X)
,
we denote by
G_{x}
the
stabilizer
of a point
x\in X
, by
\Fix(g)
the
fixed locus
of an element
g\in G
, and by
X^{\text{nf}}
the
non-free locus
of the
G
-action.
We say that
G
acts
freely in codimension one
on
X
if
X^{\text{nf}}
is a closed subset of
X
with
\codim_{X}(X^{\text{nf}})\geq 2
. If additionally
X^{\text{nf}}=\emptyset
, then we say that
G
acts
freely
on
X
.
Finally, note that if
g\in\Aut(X)
is a prime order automorphism of
X
, then by setting
G\coloneqq\langle g\rangle
, we have
\Fix(g)=X^{\mathrm{nf}}
.

A normal projective variety
X
is said to be
uniruled
if it is covered by rational curves. For the basic properties of uniruled varieties we refer to
[
Deb01
]
, remarking only that uniruledness is a birationally invariant property. We also recall the following immediate corollary of
[
LMP+23
, Lemma 3.18]
, which relies on
[
BDPP13
, Corollary 0.3]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
X
is a normal projective variety such that
K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
, then
X
is uniruled if and only if
X
does not have canonical singularities.

Given normal varieties
X
and
Y
, we frequently consider the following types of maps between them. A
fibration
is a projective surjective morphism
X\to Y
with connected fibers. A
birational contraction
is a birational map
X\dashrightarrow Y
whose inverse does not contract any divisor. A
small morphism
is a projective birational morphism
X\to Y
which is an isomorphism in codimension one.
Finally, a
resolution
of
X
is a projective birational morphism
W\to X
from a smooth variety
W
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a proper birational morphism between normal varieties. If
K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
, then
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
, since
\codim_{Y}f\big(\Exc(f)\big)\geq 2
, and the birational morphism
f
is actually crepant by the Negativity lemma
[
KM98
, Lemma 3.39(1)]
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
X
is a normal projective variety with klt but not canonical singularities and
K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
, then the underlying variety
T
of any
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal modification
h\colon(T,\Delta_{T})\to X
of
X
satisfies
K_{T}\not\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
. Indeed, by assumption and by construction (see
[
BCHM10
, Corollary 1.4.3]
) we know that
\Delta_{T}\neq 0
is an effective
h
-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
T
and we also have
K_{T}+\Delta_{T}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}h^{*}K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
, which yields the claim.

2.2.
Galois and quasi-étale covers

The following definitions are taken from
[
GKP16a
, Section 3]

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
and
Y
be normal varieties.

(a)

A morphism
\gamma\colon X\to Y
is called
quasi-étale
if
\dim X=\dim Y
and if there exists a closed subset
Z\subset X
of codimension
\codim_{X}Z\geq 2
such that
\gamma|_{X\setminus Z}\colon X\setminus Z\to Y
is étale.

(b)

A
cover
or

is a finite surjective morphism
\gamma\colon X\to Y
.

(c)

Assume now that
X
and
Y
are also quasi-projective. A covering map
\gamma\colon X\to Y
is called
Galois
if there exists a finite subgroup
G
of
\Aut(X)
such that
\gamma
is isomorphic to the quotient map
X\twoheadrightarrow X/G
.

Remark 2.6
.

(i)

If
\gamma\colon X\to Y
is a quasi-étale cover of a normal variety
Y
, then
\gamma
is étale over
Y_{\text{reg}}
by purity of the branch locus
[
Stack
, Tag 0BMB]
. Therefore, a quasi-étale cover of a smooth variety is étale.

(ii)

If
\gamma\colon X\to Y
is a Galois cover determined by a finite group
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
, then
\gamma
is étale (resp. quasi-étale) if and only if
G
acts freely (resp. freely in codimension one) on
X
.

(iii)

If
\gamma
and
G
are as in part (ii), then
X
is
G\mathbb{Q}
-factorial if and only if
Y\simeq X/G
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial by
[
Pro21
, Lemma 1.1.3]
.

(iv)

If
\gamma\colon X\to Y
is a Galois quasi-étale cover determined by a finite group
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
, then

H^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[k]}\big)\simeq H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[k]}\big)^{G}\ \text{ for any integer }\,0\leq k\leq\dim X=\dim Y.

Remark 2.7
.

Given a quasi-étale cover
\gamma\colon X\to Y
of a normal variety
Y
, by
[
KM98
, Proposition 5.20]

we obtain the following:

(i)

K_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier if and only if
K_{Y}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, and we also have
K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\gamma^{*}K_{Y}
. In particular,
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
(resp.
K_{Y}\equiv 0)
if and only if
K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
(resp.
K_{X}\equiv 0)
; see
[
Liu02
, Chapter 7, Theorem 2.18]
and
[
Stack
, Tag 02RT]
for the implication
K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0\implies K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.

Moreover, if
Y
has canonical (resp. terminal) singularities, then
X
has canonical (resp. terminal) singularities. On the other hand, if
X
has canonical singularities, then
Y
has at worst klt singularities. An example of a klt quotient of a smooth surface is given in
5.9
.

(ii)

If
B_{Y}
is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y
and if we set
B_{X}\coloneqq\gamma^{*}B_{Y}
, then we have

K_{X}+B_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\gamma^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}).

Furthermore,
(X,B_{X})
is a log canonical (resp. klt) pair if and only if
(Y,B_{Y})
is a log canonical (resp. klt) pair.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\gamma\colon X\to Y\simeq X/G
be a Galois cover between normal projective varieties determined by a finite subgroup
G
of
\Aut(X)
. Then

N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}^{G}\simeq N^{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}\quad\text{ and }\quad N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}^{G}\simeq N_{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}.

Proof.

First, as
\gamma
is the quotient map
X\to X/G\simeq Y
, note that the pullback of Cartier divisors induces an injective map

\gamma^{*}\colon N^{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}\hookrightarrow N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}^{G}.

To prove its surjectivity, and establish thus the isomorphism
N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}^{G}\simeq N^{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}
, let
D
be a
G
-invariant
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. As

N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}^{G}\simeq N^{1}(X)^{G}\otimes\mathbb{R},

we may assume that
D
is an integral Cartier divisor.
Since
\gamma
is finite, arguing as in the first part of the proof of
[
Pro21
, Lemma 1.1.3]
, we infer from
[
Liu02
, Chapter 7, Remark 2.19]
that
\gamma_{*}(D)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
Y
,
and we also have
\gamma^{*}\gamma_{*}(D)=|G|D
,
as shown in the second part of the proof of
[
Pro21
, Lemma 1.1.3]
. This implies that
\gamma^{*}
is surjective, as desired.

Second, note that the usual proper push-forward of
1
-cycles induces a surjective map of finite-dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-vector spaces

\gamma_{*}\colon N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}^{G}\twoheadrightarrow N_{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}.

To prove the isomorphism
N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}^{G}\simeq N_{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}
, it remains to check that
\gamma_{*}
is injective. Supposing that
\alpha\in N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}^{G}
is nonzero, we now show that so is
\gamma_{*}\alpha\in N_{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Indeed, by assumption, there exists a Cartier divisor
D^{\prime}
on
X
such that
D^{\prime}\cdot\alpha\neq 0
. Since
\alpha
is
G
-invariant, for every
g\in G
we have
D^{\prime}\cdot\alpha=g^{*}D^{\prime}\cdot g^{*}\alpha=g^{*}D^{\prime}\cdot\alpha
. In particular, if we set
D_{X}\coloneqq\sum_{g\in G}g^{*}D^{\prime}
, then we have
D_{X}\cdot\alpha\neq 0
. Since
D_{X}
is
G
-invariant by construction, there exists a Cartier divisor
D_{Y}
on
Y
such that
\gamma^{*}D_{Y}=D_{X}
. The projection formula gives
D_{Y}\cdot\gamma_{*}\alpha=D_{X}\cdot\alpha\neq 0
, and we are done.
∎

2.3.
Augmented irregularity

Given a normal projective variety
X
, denote by

q(X)\coloneqq h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})

the
irregularity
of
X
, and by

\widetilde{q}(X)\coloneqq\sup\big\{q(W)\mid W\text{ is a quasi-\'{e}tale cover of }X\big\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

the
augmented irregularity
of
X
.
If
X
has klt singularities, then

q(X)=h^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[1]}\big)

by
2.4
, and if additionally
X
has numerically trivial canonical divisor, then
\widetilde{q}(X)\leq\dim X
by
[
GGK19
, Lemma 2.19.2]
.

Remark 2.9
.

(i)

The irregularity is a birational invariant of normal projective varieties with rational singularities
(e.g., with klt singularities by
[
KM98
, Theorem 5.22]
).

(ii)

The augmented irregularity is a birational invariant for smooth projective varieties; see
[
Ane21
, Section 2]
for the proof of this fact. However, this is no longer true for normal projective varieties even with canonical singularities; see
4.9
.

(iii)

The augmented irregularity is invariant under quasi-étale covers by
[
GGK19
, Lemma 2.19.1]
.

3.
Equivariant MMP and Galois quasi-étale covers

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\gamma\colon X\to Y\simeq X/G
be a Galois cover between normal projective varieties determined by a finite subgroup
G
of
\Aut(X)
.

(i)

The push-forward of
1
-cycles induces a bijection
\NEb(X)^{G}\to\NEb(Y)
.

(ii)

Let
R
be an extremal ray of
\NEb(Y)
. If
\alpha\in\NEb(X)^{G}
satisfies
\gamma_{*}(\alpha)\in R
, then it spans an extremal ray
R^{G}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,\alpha
of
\NEb(X)^{G}
. In particular, if
R=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[C]
for some irreducible curve
C
on
Y
, then
R^{G}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\big[\sum_{g\in G}g(C^{\prime})\big]
for some irreducible curve
C^{\prime}
on
X
such that
\gamma(C^{\prime})=C
.

(iii)

Suppose now that
\gamma
is quasi-étale. Let
B_{Y}
be an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y
such that
(Y,B_{Y})
is a log canonical pair, and consider the log canonical pair
(X,B_{X})
, where
B_{X}\coloneqq\gamma^{*}B_{Y}
.
If
R
is a
(K_{Y}+B_{Y})
-negative extremal ray of
\NEb(Y)
, then
R^{G}
is a
(K_{X}+B_{X})
-negative extremal ray of
\NEb(X)^{G}
.
Moreover,

\Locus\left(R^{G}\right)=\gamma^{-1}\big(\Locus(R)\big).

Before proceeding with the proof of the previous lemma, we recall the notation introduced in part (iii) above, which is inspired from
[
Deb01
, Section 6.5, p. 154]
: Given a log canonical pair
(V,B_{V})
, a
(K_{V}+B_{V})
-negative extremal ray
R
of
\NEb(V)
, and the associated contraction morphism
c_{R}\colon V\to W
which contracts an irreducible curve
C\subseteq V
if and only if
[C]\in R
, see
[
Fuj17
, Theorem 4.5.2]
, we call the
locus
of
R
the following closed subset of
V
:

\Locus(R)\coloneqq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}C:\;\textrm{irred. curve}\\[0.81949pt]
\textrm{on $V$ s.t.}\,[C]\in R\end{subarray}}\Supp(C).

Proof.

We denote by

\gamma_{*}\colon N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\to N_{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}

the usual proper push-forward of
1
-cycle classes and we note that

(1)

\ker(\gamma_{*})\subseteq\big(N_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\NEb(X)\big)\cup\{0\},

because a limit of effective classes intersects positively ample classes by
[
KM98
, Theorem 1.44]
. Indeed, take a nonzero element
\overline{\zeta}\in\NEb(X)
. There is a sequence
\{\zeta_{n}\}_{n}
, where
\zeta_{n}\in\NE(X)
for every
n\geq 1
, such that
\zeta_{n}\to\overline{\zeta}
. The push-forward of
1
-cycles gives a sequence
\big\{\gamma_{*}(\zeta_{n})\big\}_{n}
, where
\gamma_{*}(\zeta_{n})\in\NE(Y)
for every
n\geq 1
, such that
\gamma_{*}(\zeta_{n})\to\gamma_{*}(\overline{\zeta})
. Thus, for any ample divisor
A
on
Y
, the projection formula yields

0\neq\gamma^{*}[A]\cdot\overline{\zeta}=[A]\cdot\gamma_{*}(\overline{\zeta}),

whence
\gamma_{*}(\overline{\zeta})
is not the zero element. This proves (
1
).

(i)
By
2.8
and by (
1
) the linear map
\NEb(X)^{G}\to\NEb(Y)
induced by
\gamma_{*}
is injective. To show its surjectivity, pick an element
\overline{\zeta^{\prime}}\in\NEb(Y)
and a sequence
\{\zeta^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n}
of effective cycles such that
\zeta^{\prime}_{n}\to\overline{\zeta^{\prime}}
. Since
\gamma_{*}
is surjective on effective cycles, for any element
\zeta^{\prime}_{n}\in\NE(Y)
there is a unique element
\zeta_{n}\in\NE(X)^{G}
such that
\zeta_{n}\mapsto\zeta_{n}^{\prime}
.
Then the limit
\overline{\zeta}
of
\{\zeta_{n}\}_{n}
exists in
\NEb(X)^{G}
and it is the preimage of
\overline{\zeta^{\prime}}
by the continuity of
\gamma_{*}
.

(ii)
The second item is a direct consequence of the first one.
Indeed, write
\alpha=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}
with
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\NEb(X)^{G}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
\gamma_{*}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})=\gamma_{*}(\beta_{1})+\gamma_{*}(\beta_{2})\in R
by assumption, so both
\gamma_{*}(\beta_{1})
and
\gamma_{*}(\beta_{2})
lie in
R
by the injectivity of
\gamma_{*}|_{\NEb(X)^{G}}
and the extremality of
R
. This implies that
\alpha=l_{1}\beta_{1}
and
\alpha=l_{2}\beta_{2}
for some
l_{1},l_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
whence both
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
belong to
R^{G}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,\alpha
.

Suppose now that the ray
R
is spanned by the class of an irreducible curve
C
in
Y
. Let
C^{\prime}
be an irreducible curve in
X
mapping onto
C
. Then the class
\beta
of the
1
-cycle
\sum_{g\in G}g(C^{\prime})
is
G
-invariant and maps to an element of
R
under
\gamma_{*}
. Thus, by the argument above,
\beta
spans the extremal ray
R^{G}
constructed above.

(iii)
Let
C^{\prime}
be any irreducible curve in
\Locus\left(R^{G}\right)
. We have

0>(K_{X}+B_{X})\cdot C^{\prime}=\gamma^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y})\cdot C^{\prime}=(K_{Y}+B_{Y})\cdot\gamma_{*}(C^{\prime})

and
[\gamma_{*}(C^{\prime})]\in R
by construction of
R^{G}
.
Therefore,
\gamma\big(\Locus(R^{G})\big)\subseteq\Locus(R)
, so

\Locus\left(R^{G}\right)\subseteq\gamma^{-1}\big(\Locus(R)\big).

Conversely, let
C
be an irreducible curve in
\Locus(R)
, consider its preimage
\gamma^{-1}(C)
in
X
, and let
C_{1}^{\prime}
be an irreducible component of
\gamma^{-1}(C)
. We have

(K_{X}+B_{X})\cdot C_{1}^{\prime}=\gamma^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y})\cdot C_{1}^{\prime}=(K_{Y}+B_{Y})\cdot\gamma_{*}(C_{1}^{\prime})<0.

Since this can be done for every irreducible component of
\gamma^{-1}(C)
, by construction of
R^{G}
we deduce that

\Locus\left(R^{G}\right)\supseteq\gamma^{-1}\big(\Locus(R)\big).

This concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Symplectic varieties

4.1.
Definitions and basic properties

A
holomorphic symplectic manifold
is a complex manifold
X
which carries a
holomorphic symplectic form
, i.e., a holomorphic
2
-form
\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X})
which is closed and everywhere non-degenerate. Note that if
(X,\sigma)
is a holomorphic symplectic manifold, then its complex dimension is even, say
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X=2n
, and the top exterior power
\sigma^{n}\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{2n}_{X})
of
\sigma
is nowhere vanishing,
whence
\omega_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
An
irreducible holomorphic symplectic
(IHS)
manifold
is a simply connected, compact Kähler manifold
X
such that
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X})=\mathbb{C}\cdot\sigma
, where
\sigma
is a holomorphic symplectic form on
X
.

In the remainder of this subsection we discuss singular analogs of the aforementioned classes of complex manifolds.

4.1.1.
Symplectic varieties

A
symplectic form
on a normal variety
X
of dimension at least two is a reflexive
2
-form
\sigma\in H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)
such that
\sigma|_{X_{\text{reg}}}
is closed and everywhere non-degenerate.
The next definition was introduced by Beauville
[
Bea00
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A pair
(X,\sigma)
, where
X
is a normal variety of dimension
\dim X\geq 2
and
\sigma
is a symplectic form on
X
, is called a
symplectic variety
if the holomorphic symplectic form
\sigma|_{X_{\text{reg}}}
extends to a holomorphic
2
-form on any resolution of singularities of
X
.

Consider a pair
(X,\sigma)
, where
X
is a normal variety and
\sigma
is a symplectic form on
X
. Note that
\dim X=2n
for some
n\geq 1
. Observe also that
K_{X}\sim 0
, since
(\sigma|_{X_{\text{reg}}})^{n}
trivializes the canonical bundle
\omega_{X_{\text{reg}}}
of the regular locus
X_{\text{reg}}
of
X
and since
\codim_{X}\big(X\setminus X_{\text{reg}}\big)\geq 2
.
To examine whether
(X,\sigma)
is a symplectic variety, it suffices to check whether
\sigma|_{X_{\text{reg}}}
extends regularly to one resolution of singularities of
X
, since any two resolutions are dominated by a common one.

If
f\colon W\to X
is a resolution of singularities of a symplectic variety
(X,\sigma)
and if the extended holomorphic
2
-form
f^{*}\sigma
on
W
is symplectic, then we say that
f
is a
symplectic resolution
of
X
. For a discussion about symplectic resolutions we refer to
[
Fu06
]
and
[
BL21
, Remark 3.3(2) and Example 3.4(5)]
. Here, we only recall that a resolution of singularities of a symplectic variety is symplectic if and only if it is crepant, and that symplectic resolutions do not always exist.

If
(X,\sigma)
is a symplectic variety, then a subvariety
L
of
X
is called
Lagrangian
with respect to
\sigma
if
\dim L=\frac{1}{2}\dim X
,
L\cap X_{\text{reg}}\neq\emptyset
and
\sigma|_{L_{\text{reg}}\cap X_{\text{reg}}}=0
; see
[
Sch20
, Lemma 15]
. When
L
is smooth, we also say that
L
is a
Lagrangian submanifold
of
X
.

Now, in order to give an important characterization of symplectic varieties, we recall the following facts: According to
[
KM98
, Corollary 5.24]
, a normal variety
V
such that
K_{V}
is Cartier has rational singularities if and only if it has canonical singularities, while
V
is Cohen–Macaulay and canonical of index
1
if and only if it has rational Gorenstein singularities; see
[
Rei80
, p. 286]
. In view of the above and since rational singularities are Cohen-Macaulay by
[
KM98
, Theorem 5.10]
, we can apply
[
Bea00
, Proposition 1.3]
and
[
Nam01
, Theorem 4]
to deduce the following statement:
A normal variety is symplectic if and only if it has rational singularities and admits a symplectic form
.
Thus, symplectic varieties have canonical singularities, and hence they are not uniruled by
2.1
.

Finally, we recall the following well-known lemma, whose proof relies essentially on the aforementioned characterization of symplectic varieties.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(X,\sigma)
be a symplectic variety.

(i)

If
\gamma\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is a quasi-étale cover of
(X,\sigma)
, then
(X^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
is also a symplectic variety, where
\sigma^{\prime}\coloneqq\gamma^{[*]}\sigma\in H^{0}\big(X^{\prime},\Omega_{X^{\prime}}^{[2]}\big)
.

(ii)

If
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is a proper birational morphism to a normal variety
X^{\prime}
, then
X^{\prime}
is also a symplectic variety.

(iii)

If
g\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime\prime}
is a birational contraction to a normal variety
X^{\prime\prime}
with rational singularities, then
X^{\prime\prime}
is also a symplectic variety.

4.1.2.
Irreducible symplectic varieties

The following subclass of symplectic varieties was introduced by Greb, Kebekus and Peternell
[
GKP16b
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

A projective symplectic variety
(X,\sigma)
is called
irreducible
if for every quasi-étale cover
\gamma\colon W\to X
of
X
, in particular for
X
itself, the exterior algebra of global reflexive forms on
W
is generated by the reflexive pullback
\gamma^{[*]}\sigma
:

\bigoplus_{p=0}^{2n}H^{0}\big(W,\Omega_{W}^{[p]}\big)=\mathbb{C}[\gamma^{[*]}\sigma].

Irreducible symplectic varieties appear as factors in the singular version of the Beauville-Bogomolov decomposition theorem, which was established by Höring and Peternell
[
HP19
]
, building on earlier works of Druel
[
Dru18
]
and Greb, Guenancia and Kebekus
[
GGK19
]
. They are thus one of the three building blocks of normal projective varieties with klt singularities and numerically trivial canonical divisor.

We collect below some basic properties of irreducible symplectic varieties, which were established in
[
GKP16b
,
GGK19
]
.

Proposition 4.4

\widetilde{q}(X)=0

(iii)

Parts (i), (ii) and (iv) of the statement are
[

, Remark 8.17]
,
[

, Corollary 13.3]
and
[

, Proposition 8.20]
, respectively. Part (iii) (resp. part (v)) follows immediately from

(i) (resp.

) and the definition of an irreducible symplectic variety.

4.1.3.
Primitive symplectic varieties

Another subclass of symplectic varieties was defined more recently by Schwald
[

]
, albeit first studied by Namikawa and Matsushita under stronger hypotheses. They were originally called
“irreducible”

[

“primitive”

Definition 4.5
.

A projective symplectic variety
(X,\sigma)
is called

H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0\ \text{ and }\ H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)=\mathbb{C}\cdot\sigma.

(iv)

denotes the tangent sheaf of

(v)

\Aut(X)

Part (i) is an easy consequence of
the facts that

and that the symplectic variety

has rational singularities, see
[

, Proposition 1.3]
. Parts (ii) and (iii) follow from the definition of primitive symplectic varieties and

. Part (iv) is

Consider an abelian surface

4.2.
Finite group actions on primitive symplectic varieties

We first recall the following standard definitions.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
(X,\sigma)
be a primitive symplectic variety.

(i)

An automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
is called
symplectic
if
g^{[*]}\sigma=\sigma
; otherwise, it is called
non-symplectic
.

(ii)

A non-symplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
is called an
anti-symplectic involution
if
g^{2}=\Id_{X}
and
g^{[*]}\sigma=-\sigma
.

(iii)

A non-symplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
is called
purely non-symplectic
if there exists a primitive root of unity
\zeta\neq 1
of the same order as
g
such that
g^{[*]}\sigma=\zeta\sigma
.

(iv)

We say that a finite subgroup
G
of
\Aut(X)
acts
symplectically
(resp.
non-symplectically
) on
X
if every element
g\in G\setminus\{\Id\}
is a symplectic (resp. non-symplectic) automorphism of
X
.

In the remainder of this subsection we study non-symplectic finite group actions on primitive symplectic varieties. We begin with some general observations.
Let
(X,\sigma)
be a primitive symplectic variety and let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\Aut(X)
. Consider the natural map

\rho\colon\Aut(X)\to\Aut\left(H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)\right),\ g\mapsto g^{[*]}.

Due to the facts that
H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)\simeq\mathbb{C}
and
\GL(1,\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{*}
, for any
g\in\Aut(X)
there exists a root of unity
\zeta
such that
g^{[*]}\sigma=\zeta\sigma
. We obtain thus a group homomorphism

\Aut(X)\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
, which induces a surjective group homomorphism

\rho_{G}\colon G\twoheadrightarrow\mu_{m},

where
m\coloneqq|\Ima(\rho_{G})|
and
\mu_{m}
is a cyclic group
generated by a primitive
m
-th root of unity. By construction,
G_{\text{sym}}\coloneqq\ker(\rho_{G})
consists of all symplectic automorphisms of
X
that are contained in
G
, and clearly there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow G_{\text{sym}}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow\mu_{m}\longrightarrow 0.

The previous observations lead to the next useful lemma.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety and let
G
be a finite subgroup of
\Aut(X)
. Then
G
acts non-symplectically on
X
if and only if
G
is a cyclic group generated by a purely non-symplectic automorphism of
X
.

Proof.

One direction is clear, so we prove the converse direction. Specifically, assume that
G
acts non-symplectically on
X
, consider the induced surjective group homomorphism

\rho_{G}\colon G\twoheadrightarrow\mu_{m}
as above, and note that
\ker(\rho_{G})=\{\Id_{X}\}
by assumption. Hence,
\rho_{G}
is an isomorphism, and the assertion follows.
∎

In the special case that
g\in\Aut(X)
is an anti-symplectic involution, its fixed locus
\Fix(g)
is a finite union of Lagrangian subvarieties of
X
. Indeed, the proof given in
[
Bea11
, Lemma 1]
works on
X_{\text{reg}}\cap\Fix(g)
.
We now generalize Beauville’s argument in order to investigate the non-free locus of a non-symplectic group action.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety of dimension
2n\geq 4
. If
g\in\Aut(X)
is a non-symplectic automorphism of prime order, then
\codim_{X}\big(\Fix(g)\big)\geq 2
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\Fix(g)\neq\emptyset
, since otherwise we are done, and we may also restrict to the regular locus of
X
, since
\codim_{X}(X_{\text{sing}})\geq 2
. In other words, we need to show that
\codim_{X_{\text{reg}}}\big(\Fix(g)\cap X_{\text{reg}}\big)\geq 2
.
To this end, pick an irreducible component
F
of
\Fix(g)\cap X_{\text{reg}}
. If it is zero-dimensional, then we are done, so we may assume that it is positive-dimensional. Consider the natural inclusion
j\colon F\hookrightarrow X_{\text{reg}}
and fix a point
x\in F_{\text{reg}}
.
Note that
g
acts on the tangent space
T_{x}X
of
X
at the point
x
via its differential
dg_{x}\in\Aut(T_{x}X)
.
Since
(g|_{X_{\text{reg}}})\circ j=\Id_{F}
, we infer that
(dg_{x})|_{T_{x}F}=\Id_{T_{x}F}
. It follows that
1
is an eigenvalue of
dg_{x}
with corresponding eigenspace
W
,
and we have

T_{x}F\subseteq W\subseteq T_{x}X\simeq T_{x}(X_{\text{reg}}),

so the local dimension of
F
at
x
is bounded above by
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W
. We will next estimate this dimension.
Denote by
\sigma
the symplectic form on
X_{\text{reg}}
and by
\sigma_{x}
the induced symplectic form on the tangent space
T_{x}X
. By assumption we have
g^{[*]}\sigma=\zeta_{p}\,\sigma
, where
p\coloneqq\ord(g)
and
\zeta_{p}
is a primitive
p
-th root of unity. In particular, for any
u,v\in W
we obtain

\big(\zeta_{p}\,\sigma_{x}\big)(u,v)=\big(g^{*}\sigma_{x}\big)(u,v)=\sigma_{x}\big(dg_{x}(u),\,dg_{x}(v)\big)=\sigma_{x}(u,v),

which implies that
\sigma_{x}(u,v)=0
. Therefore,
W
is isotropic; in particular, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W\leq\frac{\dim X}{2}
. Since
\dim X=2n\geq 4
, we infer that
F

has codimension at least two in
\Fix(g)\cap X_{\text{reg}}
. In conclusion,

\codim_{X_{\text{reg}}}\big(\Fix(g)\cap X_{\text{reg}}\big)\geq 2
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety of dimension
2n\geq 4
. If
g\in\Aut(X)
is a purely non-symplectic automorphism, then
G\coloneqq\langle g\rangle
acts on
X
freely in codimension one.

Proof.

We have to show that
\codim_{X}(X^{\text{nf}})\geq 2
. We may assume that
X^{\text{nf}}\neq\emptyset
, since otherwise we are done. We now claim that for any
x\in X^{\text{nf}}
there exists a non-symplectic automorphism
h\in G
of prime order such that
x\in\Fix(h)
. Indeed, given
x\in X^{\text{nf}}
, we have
m\coloneqq|G_{x}|>1
. Since
G=\langle g\rangle
is a cyclic group, its subgroup
G_{x}
is also a cyclic group, generated by
g^{\ord(g)/m}
. By picking any prime divisor
p
of
m
, we infer that

h\coloneqq g^{\ord(g)/p}=\big(g^{\ord(g)/m}\big)^{m/p}\in\Aut(X)

is an automorphism of prime order
p
which fixes the point
x
; this proves the claim.
It follows now that

X^{\text{nf}}=\bigcup_{h}\Fix(h),

where the union is taken over all prime order non-symplectic automorphisms
h\in G
. As this is clearly a finite union,
4.13
yields
\codim_{X}(X^{\text{nf}})\geq 2
.
∎

5.
Enriques varieties

5.1.
Definitions and basic properties

An
Enriques manifold
is a connected complex manifold
Y
which is not simply connected and whose universal covering
X
is an IHS manifold, see
[
OS11a
, Definition 2.1]
. Thus,
Y
is compact, of even dimension
\dim Y=2n
, and with finite fundamental group.
In fact, according to
[
OS11a
, Proposition 2.4]
,
\pi_{1}(Y)
is a cyclic group whose order
d\geq 2
, called the
index
of
Y
, is a divisor of
n+1
.
By
[
OS11a
, Corollary 2.7]
both the Enriques manifold
Y
and its universal covering
X
are necessarily projective. Finally, according to
[
OS11a
, Proposition 2.8]
, the torsion subgroup
\Pic^{\text{tor}}(Y)
of the Picard group of
Y
is a finite cyclic group of order
d=|\pi_{1}(Y)|\geq 2
, generated by the canonical bundle
\omega_{Y}\in\Pic(Y)
.

Note that there exist Enriques manifolds of index
d\in\{2,3,4\}
, see
[
BNWS11
,
OS11a
]
. In particular, Enriques surfaces are Enriques manifolds of dimension
2
and index
2
. For two higher-dimensional examples of index
2
we also refer to
5.24
.

In the remainder of this subsection we aim to define and to investigate singular analogs of Enriques manifolds. To this end, we first need to discuss a class of varieties defined by Greb, Guenancia and Kebekus
[
GGK19
, Remark 13.5]
. To quote the authors, one might call these
“Enriques varieties”
, but we coin the term
“GGK varieties”
instead for reasons that will become apparent below.

5.1.1.
GGK varieties

Definition 5.1
.

A
GGK variety
is a normal projective variety
Y
with klt singularities and numerically trivial canonical divisor which admits a quasi-étale cover
\gamma\colon X\to Y
such that
X
is an irreducible symplectic variety.

Observe that the class of GGK varieties contains the class of irreducible symplectic varieties. In particular, we have the following result in the smooth case.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
Y
be a GGK variety and assume that
Y
is smooth. The following statements hold:

(i)

If
\pi_{1}(Y)=\{1\}
, then
Y
is an IHS manifold.

(ii)

If
\pi_{1}(Y)\neq\{1\}
, then
Y
is an Enriques manifold.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\colon X\to Y
be a quasi-étale cover of
Y
such that
X
is an irreducible symplectic variety. By
2.6
(i) and the assumption that
Y
is smooth we infer that
\gamma
is étale, so
X
is smooth,
and thus an IHS manifold; see
4.7
(i). Since
\pi_{1}(X)=\{1\}
,
\gamma
is the universal covering of
Y
, and the assertions follow.
∎

Our first goal is to identify the complementary class to the one of irreducible symplectic varieties in the (larger) class of GGK varieties, using primarily results from
[
GKP16a
,
GKP16b
,
GGK19
]
.

Notation
.

We denote by
\Hol^{\circ}(Z)
(resp.
\Hol(Z)
) the
restricted holonomy group
(resp. the
holonomy group
) of a normal projective variety
Z
with klt singularities and numerically trivial canonical divisor, referring to
[
GGK19
]
for further details.

According to
[
GGK19
, Proposition F]
, a normal projective variety
X
of dimension
2n
with klt singularities and numerically trivial canonical divisor is
irreducible symplectic
if and only if
\Hol(X)
is isomorphic to the
unitary symplectic group

\Sp(n)
. More generally, the following result from
[
GGK19
]
characterizes GKK varieties in terms of holonomy.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
Y
be a normal projective variety of dimension
2n
with klt singularities and numerically trivial canonical divisor. Then
Y
is a GGK variety if and only if
\Hol^{\circ}(Y)\simeq\Sp(n)
.

Proof.

Assume first that
Y
is a GGK variety and consider a quasi-étale cover
X\to Y
of
Y
such that
X
is an irreducible symplectic variety. It follows immediately from
[

, Remark 5.2 and Proposition F, (F.4)–(F.6)]
that

\Hol(Y)^{\circ}=\Hol(X)^{\circ}=\Hol(X)\simeq\Sp(n).

Assume now that
\Hol^{\circ}(Y)\simeq\Sp(n)
. By
[
GGK19
, Theorem 12.1]
the tangent sheaf of
Y
is strongly stable and there exists a quasi-étale cover
\gamma\colon X\to Y
of

such that

\Hol(X)=\Hol^{\circ}(X)=\Hol^{\circ}(Y)\simeq\Sp(n).

Therefore,
X
is an irreducible symplectic variety by
[
GGK19
, Proposition F, (F.4)–(F.6)]
, and hence
Y
is a GGK variety by definition.
∎

Denote by
G\subseteq\Aut(\widetilde{X})
the finite group defining
\mu
, consider the induced surjective group homomorphism
\rho_{G}\colon G\to\mu_{m}
and set
G_{\text{sym}}\coloneqq\ker(\rho_{G})
, see
Subsection 4.2
.
If
\pi_{G_{\text{sym}}}\colon\widetilde{X}\to X^{\prime}\simeq\widetilde{X}/{G_{\text{sym}}}
is the Galois cover defined by
G_{\text{sym}}
, then
\mu
factors through
\pi_{G_{\text{sym}}}
, i.e., there exists a Galois cover
\pi_{G/G_{\text{sym}}}\colon X^{\prime}\simeq\widetilde{X}/{G_{\text{sym}}}\to Y\simeq\widetilde{X}/G
defined by the cyclic group
G/{G_{\text{sym}}}\simeq\mu_{m}

such that the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\gamma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{G_{\text{sym}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{G/G_{\text{sym}}}}

As
\mu
is quasi-étale, the Galois covers
\pi_{G_{\text{sym}}}
and
\pi_{G/{G_{\text{sym}}}}
are also quasi-étale. Since
\widetilde{X}
is an irreducible symplectic variety and since
G_{\text{sym}}
acts symplectically on
\widetilde{X}
, by
[
BGMM25
, Proposition 3.17]
we conclude that
X^{\prime}\simeq\widetilde{X}/{G_{\text{sym}}}
is also an irreducible symplectic variety.
Therefore, the map
\pi_{G/{G_{\text{sym}}}}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y
is a Galois quasi-étale cover defined by a cyclic group that is generated by a purely non-symplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X^{\prime})
of order
m=|G/G_{\text{sym}}|
.
∎

With the aid of the previous results we can now characterize those GGK varieties that are not irreducible symplectic.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
Y
be a GGK variety. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\Hol^{\circ}(Y)\subsetneq\Hol(Y)
.

(ii)

Y\simeq X/G
, where
X
is an irreducible symplectic variety and
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
is a nontrivial finite cyclic group whose action is non-symplectic and free in codimension one on
X
.

(iii)

H^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[2]}\big)=\{0\}
.

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii): Since
Y
is a GGK variety, by assumption and by
[
GGK19
, Proposition F, (F.4)–(F.6)]
we know that
Y
cannot be an irreducible symplectic variety, so the statement follows now from
5.5
, taking
2.6
(ii) into account.

(ii)
\implies
(iii): By assumption and by
2.6
(iv) we obtain

H^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[2]}\big)\simeq H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)^{G},

whence

0\leq h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[2]}\big)\leq h^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)=1.

Assume now that
h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[2]}\big)=1
. Then the symplectic form
\sigma
on
X
satisfies
g^{[*]}\sigma=\sigma
for every
g\in G
, which contradicts the hypothesis that
G
acts non-symplectically on
X
. Thus,

H^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[2]}\big)=\{0\}
.

(iii)
\implies
(i): Since
Y
is a GGK variety, by
5.3
we know that
\Hol^{\circ}(Y)\simeq\Sp(n)
. But
Y
is not a symplectic variety by assumption, so we obtain
\Hol^{\circ}(Y)\neq\Hol(Y)
by
[
GGK19
, Proposition F, (F.4)–(F.6)]
.
∎

5.1.2.
Irreducible Enriques varieties

Our previous considerations lead naturally to the definition of the following class of singular Enriques varieties.

Definition 5.7
.

An
irreducible Enriques
variety is a GGK variety which satisfies any of the equivalent conditions of
5.6
.

We first claim that Enriques manifolds coincide with smooth irreducible Enriques varieties. Indeed, it follows from
5.2
and
5.6
(iii) that
5.7
reduces to
[
OS11a
, Definition 2.1]
in the smooth case. Conversely, any Enriques manifold
Y
is an étale quotient
Y\simeq X/\pi_{1}(Y)
, where
X
is a projective IHS manifold, as the finite cyclic group
\pi_{1}(Y)
acts freely on
X
. By the holomorphic Lefschetz fixed point formula we deduce that
\pi_{1}(Y)
acts non-symplectically on
X
, see
[
BNWS11
, Subsection 2.2]
. Hence,
Y
is a smooth irreducible Enriques variety by
5.6
(ii).

We present below some examples of singular irreducible Enriques varieties, which also indicate some fundamental differences between Enriques manifolds and their singular analogs. Specifically, in constrast to the smooth case, singular irreducible Enriques varieties may also be simply connected (see
5.8
,
5.9
and
5.25
) or uniruled (see
5.9
and
5.25
) or have trivial canonical divisor (see
5.8
).

Example 5.8
.

Let
X
be an IHS manifold of dimension
\dim X=2n\geq 4
endowed with an anti-symplectic involution
\tau\in\Aut(X)
. If the fixed locus
\Fix(\tau)
of
\tau
is not empty,
then it is a finite disjoint union of Lagrangian submanifolds of
X
by
[
Bea11
, Lemma 1]
.
Therefore, the quotient
Y\coloneqq X/\langle\tau\rangle
is an irreducible Enriques variety whose singularities are concentrated along the image of
\Fix(\tau)
in
Y
. Note that
Y
is simply connected by
5.17
(ii),
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial by
[
KM98
, Proposition 5.15]
,
and has canonical singularities by
[
KM98
, Corollary 2.31(2) and Proposition 5.20(2)]
.
In particular,
Y
is not uniruled.
In view of
2.6
(iv), we also observe that if
n
is even, then
K_{Y}\sim 0
, whereas if
n
is odd, then
K_{Y}\not\sim 0
.

We now reproduce
[
GGK19
, Example 14.9]
to illustrate the case
n=2
of the above construction. Let
S
be a K3 surface endowed with an anti-symplectic involution
\tau
. Then
S
is projective by
[
Bea83a
, Proposition 6(i)]
and the punctual Hilbert scheme
X\coloneqq S^{[2]}
is a projective IHS fourfold endowed with an anti-symplectic involution
\tau^{[2]}

such that
\Fix\big(\tau^{[2]}\big)\neq\emptyset
, since
\tau^{[2]}
fixes subspaces of
S^{[2]}
of the form
\big\{x,\tau^{[2]}(x)\big\}
, where
x\in S
.
Denote by
Y
the associated irreducible Enriques variety
X/\langle\tau^{[2]}\rangle
.
If
\sigma
is a holomorphic symplectic form on
X
, then
\big(\tau^{[2]}\big)^{*}\sigma=-\sigma
, so
\big(\tau^{[2]}\big)^{*}\sigma^{2}=\sigma^{2}
. By
2.6
(iv)
we deduce that

h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[0]}\big)=h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[4]}\big)=1\ \text{ and }\ h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[1]}\big)=h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[2]}\big)=h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[3]}\big)=0.

In particular,
K_{Y}\sim 0
.

Finally, it is worthwhile to mention that, according to
[
CGM19
, Theorem 3.6]
and
[
Tak03
, Theorem 1.1]
, the simply connected variety
Y
has canonical but not terminal singularities and admits a crepant resolution by a strict Calabi–Yau fourfold in the sense of
[
Bea83b
, Proposition 2]
.

Example 5.9
.

Consider the quotient
Y_{3,0}\coloneqq X_{3,0}/\langle\sigma_{3,0}\rangle
from
[
AS08
, Proposition 4.7]
, where
X_{3,0}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is a K3 surface and
\sigma_{3,0}\in\Aut(X_{3,0})
is a purely non-symplectic automorphism of order
3
whose fixed locus consists of
3
isolated points:

\Fix(\sigma_{3,0})=\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}
. The action of
\sigma
can be locally linearized and diagonalized at each
p_{i}
and is given each time by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\zeta_{3}^{2}&0\\[2.5pt]
0&\zeta_{3}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\GL(2,\mathbb{C})\setminus\SL(2,\mathbb{C}),

where
\zeta_{3}
is a primitive third root of unity, see
[
AS08
, Section 2]
.
Note that
Y_{3,0}
is an irreducible Enriques variety with klt, but not canonical, singularities, see
[
KM98
, Proposition 4.18, Theorem 4.20 and Remark 4.21(1)]
.
Hence,
Y_{3,0}
is uniruled.
By
5.17
(ii) we also infer that
\pi_{1}(Y_{3,0})=\{1\}
, while by
2.6
(iv) we obtain

h^{0}\big(Y_{3,0},\Omega_{Y_{3,0}}^{[2]}\big)=0
, and thus
K_{Y_{3,0}}\not\sim 0
.
We thank Mirko Mauri for bringing this example to our attention and we refer to
[
AST11
,
BH23
]
for further similar examples.

We finally generalize
[
OS11a
, Proposition 2.6]
to the setting of irreducible Enriques varieties.

Proposition 5.10
.

If
Y\simeq X/G
is an irreducible Enriques variety of dimension
2n
, where
|G|=d\geq 2
, then for any integer
p\in\{0,\dots,2n\}
we have

h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[p]}\big)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }p\text{ is even and }d\mid\dfrac{p}{2}\,,\\[3.99994pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In particular,

1\leq\chi(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})\leq n.

Proof.

By
5.6
(ii) there exist an irreducible symplectic variety
X
and a finite group
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
acting non-symplectically and freely in codimension one on
X
such that
Y\simeq X/G
(as in the statement). In particular, the map
\gamma\colon X\twoheadrightarrow X/G\simeq Y
is a Galois quasi-étale cover of
Y
. By
4.12
we know that
G
is cyclic, generated by a purely non-symplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
of order
d\geq 2
.
We now distinguish two cases:

Case A
:
If
0\leq p\leq 2n
is odd, then
H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[p]}\big)=\{0\}
, so by
2.6
(iv) we also obtain
H^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[p]}\big)=\{0\}
.

Case B
:
If
0\leq p\leq 2n
is even, then we denote by
\sigma\in H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[2]}\big)
the (unique up to scalar) symplectic form on
X
and we recall that
H^{0}\big(X,\Omega_{X}^{[p]}\big)=\mathbb{C}\cdot\sigma^{p/2}
. Since by assumption we have
g^{[*]}\sigma=\zeta_{d}\,\sigma
, where
\zeta_{d}
is a primitive
d
-th root of unity, we deduce that

g^{[*]}\,\sigma^{p/2}=\big(g^{[*]}\sigma\big)^{p/2}=\zeta_{d}^{p/2}\,\sigma^{p/2},

and hence
\sigma^{p/2}
is
G
-invariant if and only if
d\mid\dfrac{p}{2}
, so the assertion follows immediately from
2.6
(iv).
∎

5.1.3.
Primitive Enriques varieties

We now introduce another class of singular Enriques varieties. Our definition was originally motivated by our MMP considerations, but it is also a natural generalization of
5.7
.

Definition 5.11
.

A
primitive Enriques variety

Y
is the quotient of a primitive symplectic variety
X
by a finite group
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
whose action is non-symplectic and free in codimension one.

Some comments about our
5.11
are now in order.
The group
G
is cyclic by
4.12
, and if
\dim X\geq 4
, then
G
acts automatically freely in codimension one on
X
by
4.14
.
The reason why we nonetheless kept in
5.11
the condition that
G
acts
“freely in codimension one”

is clarified by the following facts in the case of surfaces, which also illustrate why the construction presented in
5.8
does not have, in general, the desired outcome when
\dim X=2
. Namely, if
S
is a K3 surface carrying an anti-symplectic involution
\tau\in\Aut(S)
, then either
\Fix(\tau)=\emptyset
and the quotient
S/\langle\tau\rangle
is an Enriques surface, or
\codim_{S}\big(\Fix(\tau)\big)=1
and the quotient
S/\langle\tau\rangle
is a smooth rational surface; see
[
AST11
, Section 3]
. In the latter case we have the following example:
If
f\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
is the double cover of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
ramifying along a sextic, then
S
is a K3 surface, the involution
\tau\in\Aut(S)
associated with
f
is anti-symplectic, and the fixed locus of
\tau
is a curve. In particular, we have
\mathbb{P}^{2}\simeq S/\langle\tau\rangle
. However, this quotient is certainly
not
an Enriques surface.

Primitive Enriques varieties have klt singularities and torsion canonical class due to Remark
2.7
(i). According to
2.1
, they are not uniruled if and only if they have canonical singularities. In addition, as we demonstrate below,
5.11
reduces to
[
OS11a
, Definition 2.1]
in the smooth case.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
Y
be a primitive Enriques variety. If
Y
is smooth, then it is an Enriques manifold.

Proof.

By definition of a primitive Enriques variety there exists a Galois quasi-étale cover
X\to Y
of
Y\simeq X/G
, where
X
is a primitive symplectic variety and
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
is a finite nontrivial subgroup acting non-symplectically on
X
.
By
2.6
(i) and the assumption that
Y
is smooth, we conclude that
\gamma
is étale, and thus
X
is smooth,
so it is an IHS manifold; see
4.7
(i). Since
\pi_{1}(X)=\{1\}
, we infer that
\gamma
is the universal covering of
Y
, and it cannot be an isomorphism, since
G\neq\{\Id_{X}\}
. Therefore,
Y
is an Enriques manifold.
∎

In what follows we compare Definitions
5.7
and
5.11
.

Remark 5.13
.

(i)

In the smooth case, Lemmata
5.2
and
5.12
show that being irreducible Enriques is equivalent to being primitive Enriques: these are precisely the Enriques manifolds.

(ii)

In the singular setting,
4.7
(ii) and
5.6
(ii) imply that irreducible Enriques varieties are primitive Enriques, but the converse does not hold in general; see
5.24
(b).

A quasi-étale cover of a primitive Enriques variety is not necessarily a primitive symplectic variety, as a quasi-étale cover of a primitive symplectic variety is not necessarily a primitive symplectic variety; see Examples
4.8
and
5.24
(b).
Similarly, a crepant resolution of a primitive Enriques variety need not be an Enriques manifold, cf.
[
BL21
, Remark 3.3(2)]
. For instance,
[
CGM19
, Section 4]
contains several explicit examples of crepant resolutions of irreducible Enriques varieties which are strict Calabi–Yau manifolds; see also
5.8
.
On the other hand, we construct in
5.24
(b) a crepant resolution of a primitive (but not irreducible) Enriques variety by an Enriques manifold.
Finally, in view of
2.3
,
5.25
demonstrates that
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal modifications (e.g., crepant resolutions) of primitive Enriques varieties are not primitive Enriques varieties in general, cf.
[
Sch20
, Proposition 11]
.

Recall now that if
Y
is an Enriques manifold, then
q(Y)=0
and
\Pic(Y)
is finitely generated by
[
OS11a
, Propositions 2.6 and 2.8]
, respectively. These properties remain valid also for primitive Enriques varieties by
2.4
and
2.6
(iv),
since they have rational singularities by
[
KM98
, Theorem 5.22]
. In the next result we establish further basic properties of primitive Enriques varieties, describing in particular partially their algebra of global reflexive forms, cf.
5.10
.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
Y\simeq X/G
be a primitive Enriques variety of dimension
2n
, where
X
is a primitive symplectic variety and
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
is generated by a purely non-symplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
of finite order
d\geq 2
and acts freely in codimension one on
X
. The following statements hold:

(i)

H^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[1]}\big)=H^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[2]}\big)=H^{0}\big(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{[2n-1]}\big)=\{0\}.

(ii)

h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega^{[2n-2]}_{Y}\big)=1\iff d\mid n-1
,

(iii)

h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega^{[2n]}_{Y}\big)=1\iff d\mid n
. In this case,
K_{Y}\sim 0
and
Y
has canonical singularities, but it is not smooth.

(iv)

H^{0}(Y,\mathscr{T}_{Y})=\{0\}
, where
\mathscr{T}_{Y}
denotes the tangent sheaf of
Y
.

(v)

\Aut(Y)
is a discrete group.

Proof.

(i)
Denote by
\sigma\in H^{0}\big(X,\Omega^{[2]}_{X}\big)
the (unique up to scalar) symplectic form on
X
and recall that
g^{[*]}\sigma=\zeta_{d}\,\sigma
, where
\zeta_{d}
is a primitive
d
-th root of unity.
This observation, together with
4.6
(ii) and
2.6
(iv) for
k\in\{1,2,2n-1\}
, yield part (i).

(ii)
Omitted, as it is similar to the proof of part (iii), which is given below; see also the proof of
5.10
.

(iii)
We first deal with the first part of the statement, keeping the same notation as in part (i). Specifically, if
d\mid n
, then

g^{[*]}\,\sigma^{n}=\zeta_{d}^{n}\,\sigma^{n}=\sigma^{n},

which implies that the (unique up to scalar) volume form
\sigma^{n}
on
X
is
G
-invariant, and hence descends to the quotient
Y\simeq X/G
by
2.6
(iv). Therefore,
h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega^{[2n]}_{Y}\big)=1
. The converse follows similarly using
2.6
(iv) for
p=2n
.

We now treat the second part of the statement, so we assume that
h^{0}\big(Y,\Omega^{[2n]}_{Y}\big)=1
. As
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
by construction and
K_{Y}\sim G\geq 0
by assumption, we conclude that
K_{Y}\sim 0
. Observe now that
Y
cannot be smooth due to
5.12
and
[
OS11a
, Proposition 2.8]
. We next claim that
Y
has canonical singularities. Indeed, if
\rho\colon W\to Y
is a resolution of singularities of
Y
, then we may write

K_{W}\sim\rho^{*}K_{Y}+\sum a_{i}E_{i}\sim\sum a_{i}E_{i},

where the
E_{i}
are
\rho
-exceptional prime divisors on
W
and the coefficients satisfy
a_{i}>-1
, since
Y
has klt singularities by construction. But since
K_{W}
is a Cartier
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor on
W
, the same must be true for
\sum a_{i}E_{i}
, which implies that
a_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
. This proves the above claim, and completes the proof of part (iii).

(iv)
Follows from
[
BL22
, Lemma 4.6]
and
[
Gra18
, Lemma 5.3]
.

(v)
Follows from part (iv) and
[
MO67
, Lemma 3.4]
.
∎

Next, we briefly turn out attention to primitive Enriques surfaces. We note in passing that the paper
[
GPP24
]
provides a complete classification of primitive symplectic surfaces, so it should also be possible to classify primitive Enriques surfaces. The following remark contains several observations towards this direction.

Remark 5.15
.

Let
Y\simeq X/G
be a primitive Enriques surface,
where
X
is a primitive symplectic surface and
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
is generated by a purely non-symplectic automorphism of finite order
d\geq 2
and acts freely in codimension one on
X
; in particular,
X^{\mathrm{nf}}
is a finite subset of
X
. By Propositions
2.4
and
5.14
we obtain

q(Y)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad h^{0}(Y,K_{Y})=0.

The latter shows that
Y
has strictly torsion canonical divisor
K_{Y}
whose order is equal to
d\geq 2
by
[
BCS24
, Proposition 5.4]
. As explained previously,
Y
has klt, hence rational, singularities. So we now consider the following two cases:

Case A
:
If
Y
is smooth, then it is an
Enriques surface
by
5.12
. In particular,
\pi_{1}(Y)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

Case B
:
If
Y
is singular, then taking also
[
KM98
, Proposition 4.18, Theorem 4.20 and Remark 4.21(1)]
into account, we infer that
Y
is a
logarithmic Enriques surface
in the sense of
[
Zha91
,
Zha93
]
.
A detailed description of these surfaces via their canonical coverings and the minimal resolutions thereof is given in op. cit.
We add below some comments for
singular
primitive Enriques surfaces and we also distinguish two subcases to elaborate on their minimal resolution.

The singular Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition theorem
[
HP19
, Theorem 1.5]
yields a quasi-étale cover
\widetilde{Y}\to Y
of
Y
such that
\widetilde{Y}
is either an abelian surface or an irreducible symplectic surface. The latter is possible if and only if
Y
is an irreducible Enriques surface. This follows immediately from
[
GGK19
, Proposition F, (F.4)–(F.6)]
(see also
5.6
) and the fact
[
GGK19
, Lemma 4.7]
that the restricted holonomy is invariant under quasi-étale covers.

Subcase B1
:
If
Y
is not smooth and has canonical singularities, then its minimal resolution
W\to Y
is crepant by assumption and by
[
Mat02
, Theorem 4-6-2]
. It follows now from the Enriques–Kodaira classification of compact complex surfaces (see, for example,
[
Mat02
, Theorem 1-7-1]
) that
W
is an
Enriques surface
. Indeed, since
K_{W}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
by construction,
W
is a minimal smooth projective surface with
\kappa(W)=0
,
q(W)=0
and
p_{g}(W)=h^{0}(W,K_{W})=0
. By
[
Tak03
, Theorem 1.1]
we also obtain

\pi_{1}(Y)\simeq\pi_{1}(W)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

Subcase B2
:
If
Y
has klt but not canonical singularities, then it is uniruled, and if we consider its minimal resolution
V\to Y
, then the smooth projective surface
V
is also uniruled, so
\kappa(V)=-\infty
by
[
Deb01
, Corollary 4.12]
. Hence,
V
is a rational surface with
q(V)=0
and
p_{g}(V)=h^{0}(V,K_{V})=0
by construction and by
[
Mat02
, Theorem 4-6-2]
. It follows that
Y
itself a singular rational surface, which is simply connected by
[
Tak03
, Theorem 1.1]
and by the birational invariance of the fundamental group for complex manifolds; that is,

\pi_{1}(Y)\simeq\pi_{1}(V)\simeq\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2})\simeq\{1\}.

The following slight generalization of the construction presented in
5.8
yields a possible way to construct some primitive Enriques varieties in higher (even) dimensions.

Example 5.16
.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety of dimension
2n\geq 4
. If
X
carries a non-symplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
of prime order
p
, then the quotient
Y\coloneqq X/\langle g\rangle
is a primitive Enriques variety by
4.13
, and by
5.14
(iii) we have
K_{Y}\sim 0
if and only if
p\mid n
. When
p\nmid n
, using
[
KM98
, Proposition 5.20(1)]
we see that the quotient map
X\twoheadrightarrow Y
is the index-one cover of
Y
.

We discuss now the fundamental group of primitive Enriques varieties.

Remark 5.17
.

Let
Y
be a primitive Enriques variety and consider a Galois quasi-étale cover
\gamma\colon X\to Y
of
Y
such that
Y\simeq X/G
, where
X
is a primitive symplectic variety and
G=\langle g\rangle
is generated by a purely non-symplectic automorphism
g\in\Aut(X)
of finite order. Recall that
\gamma
is étale if and only if
X^{\text{nf}}=\emptyset
.

(i)

Assume that

6.
Asymptotic theory for Enriques manifolds

We begin this section by recalling first the following concept, which will be needed in Subsections
6.2
and
6.3
. Given a smooth projective variety
W
and a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
W
, Nakayama
[
Nak04
]
defined a decomposition

D=P_{\sigma}(D)+N_{\sigma}(D),

known as the
Nakayama–Zariski decomposition
of
D
; see also Boucksom’s analytic approach
[
Bou04
]
. The divisors
P_{\sigma}(D)
and
N_{\sigma}(D)
are called
the positive part
and
the negative part
, respectively, of the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition of
D
. Recall also that
P_{\sigma}(D)\in\Movb(W)
by
[
Nak04
, Lemma III.1.8 and Proposition III.1.14(1)]
and that
N_{\sigma}(D)\geq 0
by construction.

Let
X
be an IHS manifold of dimension
2n
.
Due to the works of Beauville, Bogomolov and Fujiki, there exists a non-degenerate quadratic form
q_{X}
on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
, called the
Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki (BBF) form
, which behaves like the intersection form on a surface. There also exists a positive constant
c_{X}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, called the
Fujiki constant
of
X
, such that

c_{X}\cdot q_{X}(\alpha)^{n}=\int_{X}\alpha^{2n}\ \text{ for all }\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C}).

For the precise definition and the basic properties of the BBF form we refer to
[
Bea83b
, Sections 8 and 9]
and
[
GHJ03
, Section 23]
.

An effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D=\sum_{i}a_{i}D_{i}
on an IHS manifold
X
is called
exceptional
if the Gram matrix of its irreducible components
\big(q_{X}(D_{i},D_{j})\big)_{i,j}
is negative definite. In particular, a prime divisor
E
on
X
is exceptional if and only if
q_{X}(E)<0
. According to
[
Bou04
, Theorem 4.5]
,
D
is exceptional if and only if
D=N_{\sigma}(D)
. Taking this fact into account, we say that an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on an Enriques manifold
Y
is
exceptional
if
D=N_{\sigma}(D)
.

6.1.
The ample and nef cones of an Enriques manifold

Let
X
be an IHS manifold. The
Kähler cone
of
X
is the open convex cone

\mathcal{K}_{X}\subset H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})\coloneqq H^{1,1}(X)\cap H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})

of all Kähler classes on
X
, and the
positive cone
of
X
is the connected component
\mathcal{C}_{X}
of the set

\big\{\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})\mid q_{X}(\alpha)>0\big\}

that contains the Kähler cone of
X
. We denote by
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{X}
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{X}
) the closure of
\mathcal{K}_{X}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{X}
) in
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Finally, when
X
is projective, we set
\Pos(X)\coloneqq\mathcal{C}_{X}\cap N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
and we denote by
\Posb(X)
its closure in
N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
.

We now derive an analog of
[
Bou01
, Théorème 1.2]
and
[
Huy03
, Proposition 3.2]
, which characterize ample and nef classes on IHS manifolds, for Enriques manifolds.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
Y
be an Enriques manifold and let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be its universal covering. An
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
Y
is ample (resp. nef) if and only if
[\pi^{*}(D)]\in\Pos(X)
and
D\cdot C>0
(resp.
[\pi^{*}(D)]\in\Posb(X)
and
D\cdot C\geq 0
) for every rational curve
C
in
Y
.

Proof.

Note that
D
is ample (resp. nef) if and only if
\pi^{*}(D)
is ample (resp. nef), since
\pi
is finite and surjective, see
[
Laz04
, Proposition 1.2.13, Corollary 1.2.28 and Example 1.4.4]
. Therefore, if
D
is ample (resp. nef), then
[\pi^{*}(D)]\in\Pos(X)
and
D\cdot C_{Y}>0
(resp.
[\pi^{*}(D)]\in\Posb(X)
and
D\cdot C_{Y}\geq 0
) for every rational curve
C_{Y}
in
Y
.

Conversely, it suffices to show that
\pi^{*}D
is ample (resp. nef). To this end, let
C_{X}
be a rational curve on
X
and note that
C_{Y}\coloneq\pi(C_{X})
is a rational curve on
Y
. We have
D\cdot C_{Y}>0
(resp.
D\cdot C_{Y}\geq 0
) by hypothesis, while by the projection formula we obtain

\pi^{*}D\cdot C_{X}=D\cdot(dC_{Y})>0\quad\big(\text{resp. }\pi^{*}D\cdot C_{X}=D\cdot(dC_{Y})\geq 0\big),

where

is the degree of the restriction
\pi|_{C_{X}}\colon C_{X}\to C_{Y}
. Therefore, by assumption and by

[
Bou01
, Théorème 1.2]
(resp.
[
Huy03
, Proposition 3.2]
)
we deduce that

[\pi^{*}D]\in\mathcal{K}_{X}\quad\big(\text{resp.\ }[\pi^{*}D]\in\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{X}\big).

Since

\Amp(X)=\mathcal{K}_{X}\cap N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\quad\big(\text{resp.\ }\Nef(X)=\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{X}\cap N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\big),

we conclude that
\pi^{*}D
is ample (resp. nef), so we are done.
∎

6.2.
The volume function of an Enriques manifold

In this subsection we will prove
1.3
, which is an analog of
[
Den23
, Theorem 1.6(2)]
for Enriques manifolds. We begin with some reminders about the volume of divisors.

Fix a projective variety
Z
of dimension
d
. The
volume
of
D\in\Div(Z)
is defined as

\vol_{Z}(D)\coloneqq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}\big(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}(kD)\big)}{k^{d}/d!}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,.

This is an invariant of the Cartier divisor
D
, which measures the asymptotic rate of growth of the dimension of the spaces of global sections
H^{0}\big(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}(mD)\big)
. We have

D\,\text{ is big if and only if }\vol_{Z}(D)>0.

The notion of volume can be extended to divisors with rational or real coefficients. Specifically, the volume of
D\in\Div_{\mathbb{Q}}(Z)\coloneqq\Div(Z)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}

is defined by picking an integer
k\geq 1
such that
kD
is an integral Cartier divisor and by setting

\vol_{Z}(D)\coloneqq\frac{1}{k^{d}}\vol_{Z}(kD).

This definition does not depend on the chosen integer
k
by
[
Laz04
, Lemma 2.2.38]
. More generally, given
D\in\Div_{\mathbb{R}}(Z)\coloneqq\Div(Z)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
and taking into account the fact that two numerically equivalent Cartier divisors have the same volume, see
[
Laz04
, Proposition 2.2.41]
, choose a sequence
\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset N^{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor classes converging to
[D]\in N^{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}
and set

\vol_{Z}(D)\coloneqq\lim_{k\to\infty}\vol_{Z}(\xi_{k}).

This definition is independent of the choice of the sequence
\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
by
[
Laz04
, Theorem 2.2.44]
.

According to
[
Laz04
, Corollary 2.2.45]
, the function

\alpha\in N^{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{Q}}\mapsto\vol_{Z}(\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

extends to a continuous function

\vol_{Z}(-)\colon N^{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\ \alpha\mapsto\vol_{Z}(\alpha),

called the
volume function
of
Z
. We have

[D]\in N^{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}\,\text{ is big if and only if }\vol_{Z}(D)>0.

Definition 6.2
.

The volume function
\vol_{Z}(-)
of a projective variety
Z
is said to be
piecewise polynomial
if there exists a decomposition
N^{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}\Sigma_{i}
into subsets
\Sigma_{i}
with nonempty interior such that
\vol_{Z}|_{\Sigma_{i}}
is a polynomial function for every
i\in I
.

Note that the subsets
\Sigma_{i}\subset\BgCn(Z)
are neither open nor closed in general; see
[
Den23
, Example 5.1]
. In the special case that
Z
is an Enriques manifold, we will construct a decomposition of its Néron–Severi space
N^{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}
as in
6.2
,
where each subset
\Sigma_{i}\subset\BgCn(Z)
will actually be a convex cone.
This is the content of our
1.3
whose proof will be given shortly and will be based on the following two results.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
Y
be an Enriques manifold and let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be its universal covering.
If

\alpha=P_{\sigma}(\alpha)+N_{\sigma}(\alpha)

is the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition of
\alpha\in\BgCn(Y)
, then

\pi^{*}\alpha=\pi^{*}P_{\sigma}(\alpha)+\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha).

is the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition of its pullback
\pi^{*}\alpha\in\BgCn(X)
. In particular,
N_{\sigma}(\alpha)
(resp.
N_{\sigma}(\pi^{*}\alpha)=\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha)
) is an exceptional
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y
(resp. on
X
).

Proof.

Since
\pi
is finite and surjective, it follows immediately from
[
Nak04
, Theorem III.5.16]
that
N_{\sigma}(\pi^{*}\alpha)=\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha)
, which yields the first assertion.
The
“in particular”
part of the statement is settled by
[
Bou04
, Theorem 3.12(i)]
.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
Y
be an Enriques manifold and let
\pi\colon X\to Y
be its universal covering.
If
S=\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{s}
is the set of the irreducible components of the negative part
N_{\sigma}(\beta)
of the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition of some
\beta\in\BgCn(Y)
, then the set

\Sigma_{S}\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in\BgCn(Y)\ \Big|\ \Supp\big(N_{\sigma}(\alpha)\big)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}\Supp(E_{j})\right\}\neq\emptyset

is a convex subcone of
\BgCn(Y)
with nonempty interior. In particular, if
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\Sigma_{S}
, then

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\big(P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\big)+\big(N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\big)

is the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition of
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\in\Sigma_{S}
.

Proof.

In what follows we use repeatedly the characterization of the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition of pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisors on IHS manifolds due to Boucksom
[
Bou04
, Theorem 4.8]
. We also denote by
q_{X}
the BBF form of the considered IHS manifold
X
and we set
d\coloneqq|\pi_{1}(Y)|\geq 2
.

First, we show that the cone
\Sigma_{S}

is convex. To this end, fix
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\Sigma_{S}
and consider their Nakayama–Zariski decompositions

\alpha_{1}=P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{2}=P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})+N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2}),

respectively, where

(10)

\Supp\big(N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})\big)=\Supp\big(N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\big)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}\Supp(E_{j}).

By
6.3
we know that the Nakayama–Zariski decompositions of
\pi^{*}(\alpha_{1})
and
\pi^{*}(\alpha_{2})
are

\pi^{*}\alpha_{1}=\pi^{*}P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\pi^{*}\alpha_{2}=\pi^{*}P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})+\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2}),

respectively, and by (
10
) we also deduce that

(11)

\Supp\big(\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})\big)=\Supp\big(\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\big)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}\pi^{-1}\big(\Supp(E_{j})\big).

We now claim that
\pi^{*}\big(P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\big)
and
\pi^{*}\big(N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\big)
are the positive and the negative part, respectively, of the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition of
\pi^{*}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
. Indeed, using (
11
) and
[
Bou04
, Theorem 4.8]
, it is straightforward to verify that the Gram matrix with respect to
q_{X}
of the irreducible components of
\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\geq 0
is negative definite (if nonzero) and also that
\pi^{*}P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+\pi^{*}P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})
is
q_{X}
-orthogonal to any irreducible component of
\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})
and satisfies

\big[\pi^{*}P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+\pi^{*}P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\big]\in\Movb(X)\cap\BgCn(X)
.
By the uniqueness of the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition in the setting of IHS manifolds, see
[
Bou04
, Theorem 4.8]
, we conclude that

P_{\sigma}\big(\pi^{*}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\big)=\pi^{*}\big(P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\big)

and

(12)

N_{\sigma}\big(\pi^{*}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\big)=\pi^{*}\big(N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\big),

as claimed.

We next claim that
P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})
and
N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})
are the positive and the negative part, respectively, of the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition of
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
. Indeed, by (
12
) and
by
6.3
for the class
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\in\BgCn(Y)
we obtain

\pi^{*}\big(N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})\big)=N_{\sigma}\big(\pi^{*}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\big)=\pi^{*}N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}),

which yields

(13)

N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})=N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+N_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2})

in view of
[
Liu02
, Chapter 7, Theorem 2.18]
, and hence

P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})=P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{1})+P_{\sigma}(\alpha_{2}),

as claimed. In particular, (
10
) and (
13
) imply that
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\in\Sigma_{S}
, as desired.

Finally, we demonstrate that the interior of
\Sigma_{S}
is not empty. In view of
6.3
, we begin by considering the Boucksom–Zariski chamber
\Sigma_{S^{\prime}}\subset\BgCn(X)
associated with the set
S^{\prime}=\{E_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{r}
of the irreducible components of
N_{\sigma}(\pi^{*}\beta)=\pi^{*}\big(N_{\sigma}(\beta)\big)
, whose interior is not empty;
we refer to
[
Den23
, Section 4]
for the details. We may assume for simplicity that
S
consists of a single prime divisor
E
, which implies that the
E_{i}^{\prime}
lie in a unique
\pi_{1}(Y)
-orbit. Given any ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
A
on
Y
, we now construct a
\pi_{1}(Y)
-invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D_{1}^{\prime}
on
X
of the form

(14)

D_{1}^{\prime}=\pi^{*}A+a\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}^{\prime}+b\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}^{\prime}

which lies in the interior of the Boucksom–Zariski chamber
\Sigma_{S^{\prime}}
and the positive and negative parts of whose Nakayama–Zariski decomposition are

(15)

P_{\sigma}(D_{1}^{\prime})=\pi^{*}A+a\sum_{i}E_{i}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{\sigma}(D_{1}^{\prime})=b\sum_{i}E_{i}^{\prime}\,,

respectively, for some
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
. Specifically, arguing as in the proof of
[
Den23
, Lemma 4.15]
, we can construct a big and movable
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

on

such that
\mathrm{Null}_{q_{X}}(M)=S^{\prime}
, where
\mathrm{Null}_{q_{X}}(M)
is the collection of prime divisors

on

such that

. Then the

-divisor

, where

, is also big and movable. Hence, for any

, the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D_{1}^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{1}{d}M^{\prime}+b\sum_{i}E_{i}^{\prime}
satisfies (
15
) with
a=a^{\prime}/d=\big(\sum_{i}a_{i}\big)/d\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
according to
[
Den23

,
it is big and
\pi_{1}(Y)
-invariant by construction,
and additionally it lies in the interior of

by
[
Den23
, Corollary 4.19]
.

Consider next the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D_{1,0}\coloneqq\frac{1}{d}\pi_{*}(D_{1}^{\prime})
on

, where

is chosen as in (

). Since
\pi^{*}(D_{1,0})=D_{1}^{\prime}
,
we infer that

is big, and it follows now from

, (
15
) and
[
Nak04
, Theorem III.5.16]
that

\Supp N_{\sigma}(D_{1,0})=\bigcup_{j}\Supp E_{j}.

Thus,

. Now, pick any effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
G
on
Y
and consider for any
\varepsilon>0
the big
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

on

. Since

lies in the interior of

by construction,
\pi^{*}(D_{1,\varepsilon})=D_{1}^{\prime}+\varepsilon\pi^{*}(G)
also lies in
\Sigma_{S^{\prime}}
for every

, so

\Supp N_{\sigma}\big(\pi^{*}(D_{1,\varepsilon})\big)=\bigcup_{i}\Supp E_{i}^{\prime}\ \text{ for every }\,0\leq\varepsilon\ll 1

by definition of the chamber

. Since
N_{\sigma}(D_{1,\varepsilon})=\pi_{*}N_{\sigma}\big(\pi^{*}(D_{1,\varepsilon})\big)
by
[

, Theorem III.5.16]
, we conclude that

\Supp N_{\sigma}(D_{1,\varepsilon})=\bigcup_{j}\Supp E_{j}\ \text{ for every }\,0\leq\varepsilon\ll 1.

Thus,

for every

is not empty.

Proof of
1.3
.

Consider the universal covering
\pi\colon X\to Y
of
Y
, where
X
is a projective IHS manifold with BBF form
q_{X}
, and let
d\geq 2
be its degree. It follows from
[
Laz04
, Proposition 2.2.35]
that
\vol_{Y}(-)
is homogeneous of degree
2n
, and to prove that it is piecewise polynomial, we may restrict to the big cone
\BgCn(Y)
of

, since the volume function vanishes on its complement in
N^{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
S=\{E_{j}\}_{j}
be the set of the irreducible components of the negative part of the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition of some big class on
Y
and consider the set

\Sigma_{S}\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in\BgCn(Y)\ \Big|\ \Supp\big(N_{\sigma}(\alpha)\big)=\bigcup_{j}\Supp(E_{j})\right\},

which is a nonempty convex subcone of
\BgCn(Y)
with nonempty interior by
6.4
.
We will show below that
\vol_{Y}(-)|_{\Sigma_{S}}
is polynomial. Since
\BgCn(Y)
is the disjoint union
of subcones of type
\Sigma_{S}
, this will imply that
\vol_{Y}(-)
is piecewise polynomial, as desired.

Given
\alpha\in\Sigma_{S}
, consider the Boucksom–Zariski chamber
\Sigma_{S^{\prime}}\subset\BgCn(X)
associated with the set
S^{\prime}
of the irreducible components of
N_{\sigma}(\pi^{*}\alpha)=\pi^{*}\big(N_{\sigma}(\alpha)\big)
; see
[
Den23
, Definition 4.11]
and
6.3
. Let
(S^{\prime})^{\perp}
be the subspace of

of classes that are
q_{X}
-orthogonal to any element of

and let

be a basis for

. By the proof of
[
Den23

we know that the volume function of

restricted to

is given by the polynomial

\vol_{X}(-)\big|_{\Sigma_{S^{\prime}}}=c_{X}\cdot q_{X}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}B_{i}\right)^{n},

where the

are the coordinates with respect to the basis

.
Note that
\Sigma_{S}
embeds into

via the inclusion

.
It follows from

Kür06

and the above relation that the volume function of

restricted to

is given by the polynomial

\vol_{Y}(-)\big|_{\Sigma_{S}}=\frac{c_{X}}{d}\cdot q_{X}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}B_{i}\right)^{n}.

6.3.
Duality for cones of partially ample divisors

In this subsection we will prove
1.4
, which is an analog of
[
DRO25
, Theorem B]
for Enriques manifolds.

We first recall the definitions of the various asymptotic base loci associated with an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on a normal projective variety. We refer to
[
Nak04
,
ELM+06
,
BBP13
,
TX25
]
for their basic properties as well as for more information about them.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
Z
be a normal projective variety and let
D\in\Div_{\mathbb{R}}(Z)
.

(i)

The
stable base locus
of
D
is defined as

\sbs(D)\coloneqq\bigcap\big\{\Supp(E)\mid E\text{ is an effective $\mathbb{R}$-divisor such that $E\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D$}\big\}.

(ii)

The
augmented base locus
of
D
is defined as

\abs(D)\coloneqq\bigcap_{D=A+E}\Supp(E),

where the intersection is taken over all decompositions of the form
D=A+E
such that
A
is an ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Z
and
E
is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Z
.

(iii)

The
restricted base locus
of
D
(also referred to as the
diminished base locus
in the literature) is defined as

\dbs(D)\coloneqq\bigcup_{A}\sbs(D+A),

where the union is taken over all ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisors
A
on
Z
.

The augmented and the restricted base locus of
D
depend only on the numerical equivalence class
[D]\in N_{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}
of
D
, but this does not hold in general for the stable base locus of
D
, see
[
ELM+06
, Example 1.1]
. One readily sees that we have the inclusions

\dbs(D)\subseteq\sbs(D)\subseteq\abs(D)
,
which are strict in general, even simultaneously; see
[
TX25
, Example 3.4]
for such an example.

The next result is an analog of
[
DRO25
, Theorem A(1)]
. It estimates the dimension of the augmented and the restricted base loci of
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisors on Enriques manifolds. It provides tacitly information about the dimension of their stable base locus as well. Indeed, for any
big

\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on an Enriques manifold
Y
we have
\dbs(D)=\sbs(D)
by
[
BBP13
, Corollary A]
; see also
[
TX25
, Corollary B(ii)]
.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
Y
be an Enriques manifold of dimension
2n
. If
D
is a big (resp. pseudoeffective)
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y
, then the irreducible components of
\abs(D)
and
\dbs(D)
have dimension greater than or equal to
n
, whenever
\abs(D)\neq\emptyset
and
\dbs(D)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the irreducible components of
\abs(D)
, where
D
is any big and non-ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y
, have dimension greater than or equal to
n
. Indeed, by
[
ELM+06
, Lemma 1.14]
we have

\dbs(D)=\bigcup_{A}\abs(D+A),

where the union is taken over all ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisors
A
on
Y
, so if
V
is an irreducible component of
\dbs(D)
, then it is an irreducible component of
\abs(D+A)
for some ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
A
on
Y
.

Assume now that
D
is a big, non-ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y
, so that
\emptyset\subsetneq\abs(D)\subsetneq Y
. Consider the universal covering
\pi\colon X\to Y
of
Y
, where
X
is an IHS manifold, and note that the pullback
\pi^{*}(D)
is again a big, non-ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
by
[
Laz04
, Corollary 1.2.28]
and
[
Kür06
, Proposition 2.9(1)]
. By
[
Gom25
, Theorem 1.1(1.1.2)]
we obtain

(16)

\abs\big(\pi^{*}(D)\big)=\pi^{-1}\big(\abs(D)\big).

Let
V
be an irreducible component of
\abs(D)
. Then there exists an irreducible component
T
of
\pi^{-1}(V)
mapping onto
V
via
\pi
.
By (
16
) and
[
DRO25
, Theorem A(1)]
we know that
\dim T\geq n
, and since
\pi
is finite, we conclude that
\dim V\geq n
, as asserted.
∎

Using the augmented base locus of big divisor classes on a given projective variety, it is possible to characterize various cones in its Néron–Severi space. For example, the pseudoeffective cone
\Pseff(Z)
of a projective variety
Z

can be viewed as the set of Cartier divisor classes on
Z
whose augmented base locus has codimension at least one in
Z
; see
[
ELM+06
, Examples 1.7 and 1.18]
. This construction can be generalized in a natural way as follows.

Definition 6.7
.

Let
Z
be a normal projective variety. For any
1\leq k\leq\dim(Z)
we denote by
\Amp^{k}(Z)
the convex cone in the Néron–Severi space
N^{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}
of
Z
spanned by those Cartier divisor classes
\alpha
satisfying
\dim\big(\abs(\alpha)\big)\leq k-1
. A class lying in
\Amp^{k}(Z)
is said to be
k
-ample
.

It is an immediate consequence of
[
ELM+06
, Examples 1.8 and 1.9]
that
\Amp^{k}(Z)
is a convex cone in
N^{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover, all
k
-ample classes
\alpha
on a normal projective variety
Z
are automatically big by
[
ELM+06
, Example 1.7]
.
In particular,
1
-ample classes on
Z
are ample, as the augmented base locus cannot have isolated points by
[
ELM+09
, Proposition 1.1]
and we have
\Amp^{1}(Z)=\Amp(Z)
by
[
ELM+06
, Example 1.7]
.

Our main objective in this subsection is to compute the duals of the cones of
k
-ample classes on Enriques manifolds, following closely the presentation from
[
DRO25
, Section 4]
. To this end, we begin with some reminders.

Let
\zeta\colon Z\dashrightarrow Z^{\prime}
be a small map
between
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective varieties. Since the Néron–Severi spaces
N^{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}
and
N^{1}(Z^{\prime})_{\mathbb{R}}
are isomorphic via
\zeta_{*}
, their dual spaces
N_{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}
and
N_{1}(Z^{\prime})_{\mathbb{R}}
are also isomorphic. Under this isomorphism, denoted by
\zeta^{*}_{\mathrm{num}}
and called the

[
Ara10
]
, any curve class
\gamma^{\prime}\in N_{1}(Z^{\prime})_{\mathbb{R}}
can be pulled back to a curve class
\zeta^{*}_{\mathrm{num}}(\gamma^{\prime})\in N_{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Note that if
\delta^{\prime}\in N^{1}(Z^{\prime})_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\gamma^{\prime}\in N_{1}(Z^{\prime})_{\mathbb{R}}
, then

(17)

\zeta^{*}(\delta^{\prime})\cdot\zeta^{*}_{\mathrm{num}}(\gamma^{\prime})=\delta^{\prime}\cdot\gamma^{\prime}.

For some more information about the numerical pullback of curves we refer to
[
Ara10
, Section 4]
.

Given a map
\zeta\colon Z\dashrightarrow Z^{\prime}
as above, for any

we define

\Mob_{k,\,\zeta}(Z,Z^{\prime})\subseteq N_{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}

to be the image under
\zeta^{*}_{\mathrm{num}}
of the convex cone in
N_{1}(Z^{\prime})_{\mathbb{R}}
generated by numerical classes of irreducible curves
C^{\prime}
in
Z^{\prime}
moving in a family that sweeps out the birational image via
\zeta
of a subvariety of
Z
of dimension at least
k
.
In the special case that

we set

\Mob_{k}(Z)\coloneqq\Mob_{k,\,\Id_{Z}}(Z,Z)\subseteq N_{1}(Z)_{\mathbb{R}}

and we call it the

of

.

Now if

is a birational map between primitive Enriques varieties with terminal singularities, then

is small by

, Corollary 3.54]
.
We can thus give the following definition.

Definition 6.8
.

Let
Y
be an Enriques manifold. The
cone of birationally
k
-movable classes
of
Y
is defined as

\bMob_{k}(Y)\coloneqq\sum_{Y^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{f\colon Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}}\Mob_{k,f}(Y,Y^{\prime})\right),

where the sum runs over all
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial primitive Enriques varieties
Y^{\prime}
with terminal singularities and all birational maps

. Its closure in
N_{1}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}
is denoted by
\bMobc_{k}(Y)
.

We are finally ready to prove the main result of this section,
1.4
.

Proof of
1.4
.

The second part of the statement follows readily from
6.6
, which shows that

\Amp^{k}(Y)=\Amp^{1}(Y)=\Amp(Y)\ \text{ for any }1\leq k\leq n,

so it remains to deal with the first part of the statement. We will show equivalently that

\overline{\Amp^{k}}(Y)=\bMob_{k}(Y)^{\vee}.

Step 1
: We show that
\overline{\Amp^{k}}(Y)\subseteq\bMob_{k}(Y)^{\vee}
.

Pick a class
\beta\in\Amp^{k}(Y)
and a generating class
\gamma\in\bMob_{k}(Y)
. Up to passing to a birational model of
Y
, the curve class
\gamma
is represented by an irreducible curve
C
moving in a family that covers a subvariety
S
of
Y
with
\dim S\geq k
.
On the other hand, we have
\dim\abs(\beta)<k
.
Thus, the curve
C
can be moved out of the locus
\abs(\beta)
, so

\beta\cdot\gamma>0
by
[
DRO25
, Remark 2.8]
. It follows that
u\cdot v\geq 0
for any
u\in\overline{\Amp^{k}}(Y)
and any
v\in\bMob_{k}(Y)
, which yields the desired inclusion.

Step 2
: We show that
\bMob_{k}(Y)^{\vee}\subseteq\overline{\Amp^{k}}(Y)
.

It suffices to prove that

(18)

\operatorname{int}\left(\bMob_{k}(Y)^{\vee}\right)\subseteq\Amp^{k}(Y).

We first note that

(19)

\bMob_{k}(Y)^{\vee}\subseteq\Pseff(Y),

since we clearly have
\Mob_{2n}(Y)\subseteq\bMob_{k}(Y)
and
\Mob_{2n}(Y)^{\vee}=\Pseff(Y)
by
[
BDPP13
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Step 2a
: In this step we show that

(20)

\bMob_{2n-1}(Y)^{\vee}\subseteq\Movb(Y).

Since both of these cones are closed, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{int}\big(\bMob_{2n-1}(Y)^{\vee}\big)\subseteq\Movb(Y)
.
Pick now
\alpha\in\operatorname{int}\big(\bMob_{2n-1}(Y)^{\vee}\big)
, which is a big class by (
19
), and consider its Nakayama–Zariski decomposition

\alpha=P_{\sigma}(\alpha)+N_{\sigma}(\alpha)=P+N.

By
[
Bou04
, Proposition 3.20]
we have

(21)

\vol_{Y}(P)=\vol_{Y}(\alpha)>0.

Suppose by contradiction that
\alpha\notin\Movb(Y)
.
We may assume that
(Y,P)
is klt and run a
(K_{Y}+P)
-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor, which is a sequence of
P
-flops, as
[P]\in\Movb(Y)
. Since
P
is big by (
21
),
[
BCHM10
, Corollary 1.4.2]
implies that this MMP terminates with a minimal model
f\colon(Y,P)\dashrightarrow(Y^{\prime},P^{\prime})
, where
Y^{\prime}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial primitive Enriques variety with terminal singularities by
[
KM98
, Lemma 3.38]
and
P^{\prime}
is a nef and effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y^{\prime}
.
Moreover, by (
6
) we obtain:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

where

is a projective IHS manifold by
4.15
(iii),

-factorial primitive symplectic variety with terminal singularities,

with

Now, set

and consider the pullback

Clearly,

is nef.
Since the volume of

-Cartier

Consequently,

(resp.

Hence, by

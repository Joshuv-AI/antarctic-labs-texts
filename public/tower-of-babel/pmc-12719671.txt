Epigenetic advances in rheumatic heart disease
Authors: Wenjie Zhu, Fan Li, Yuchen Zhang, Yuting Chen, Ye Wu, Chengxin Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractRheumatic heart disease (RHD), an autoimmune injury triggered by group A streptococcus (GAS) infection, exerts a significant pathological impact on the human heart. The pathogenesis of RHD is closely associated with abnormal epigenetic regulation, and primarily involves the four core epigenetic mechanisms: DNA methylation, histone modification, non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), and chromatin remodeling. Currently, researches on RHD have shifted from focusing on late-stage diagnosis and treatment to investigating its multiple etiologies. Therefore, this article presents a systematic literature review aimed at reviewing the latest progress in the epigenetic research of RHD, providing in-depth elucidation of the interactions between RHD and these four core mechanisms, highlighting the value of potential biomarkers for the disease's early diagnosis and treatment, and outlining future research directions, including combined multi-omics analysis, minimally invasive interventional techniques, and gene chip-based approaches. This review aims to address the limitations of single-subject studies and small-sample cross-sectional surveys in epigenetic studies of RHD, thereby providing new strategies for disease prevention, control, and health management.Keywords: Rheumatic heart disease, Epigenetic, DNA methylation, Histone modification, Non-coding RNA, Chromatin remodeling, Biological markers

Introduction

1Rheumatic Heart Disease

Rheumatic Heart Disease

Rheumatic heart disease (RHD) is an immune-mediated disorder caused by group A streptococcus (GAS) infection. Its pathogenesis involves the interaction of multiple factors, including the pathogen, host immune response, and genetic susceptibility, it is exacerbated by environmental factors, such as overcrowded living conditions and poor sanitation that promote the spread of GAS and subsequent repeated infections [1]. Common symptoms of RHD include hemoptysis, palpitations, and shortness of breath. Subsequently, it can lead to a series of serious complications, including heart failure, atrial fibrillation (AF) and even premature death, often via stroke [2]. Owing to its early onset, high mortality, and high disability rates, RHD imposes a particularly heavy burden in low-income countries and remains a major global public health challenge [3]. According to IHME Global Burden of Disease (GBD), RHD affected an estimated 55 million people globally in 2021; this represents a 1.7-fold increase in prevalence since 1990, the age-standardized death rate was 4.5 per 100,000, corresponding to an estimated 360,000 deaths [4]. In low- and middle-income countries, factors including weak healthcare systems, inadequate sanitation infrastructure, and high population density contribute to consistently higher RHD incidence and mortality rates compared to developed countries, thereby imposing a more severe disease-related economic burden [5]. Currently, RHD management strategies primarily include surgical intervention (e.g., valve repair or replacement), repetitive echocardiographic screening identifies subclinical cases, newly confirmed cases, and asymptomatic or undiagnosed lesions of RHD [6], prophylactic penicillin use, and administration of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs; e.g., naproxen, ibuprofen) or corticosteroids to alleviate acute inflammatory symptoms [7]. However, these approaches have inherent limitations and fail to provide a cure for RHD.

Currently, exploring the connection between RHD and epigenetics has emerged as a highly focused research area. The four core mechanisms of epigenetics, namely DNA methylation, histone modification, ncRNAs, and chromatin remodeling, which can regulate gene expression in response to external environmental stimuli, thereby influencing cardiac inflammation and fibrosis and ultimately participating in the pathogenesis of RHD [8]. Therefore, this study searched for literature in multiple databases, such as PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Google Scholar, and Medline Plus. The aim was to investigate the associations between the core mechanisms of epigenetics and RHD, so as to reveal the RHD pathogenesis and explore new therapeutic methods.

The four core mechanisms of epigenetics

DNA methylation and RHD

DNA methylation, catalyzed by DNA methyltransferases, DNMT1, DNMT3A/3B, is a key epigenetic modification mechanism that describes the chemical process of adding methyl groups, CH3, to DNA molecules. This modification does not alter the nucleotide base sequence of DNA [9]. However, it can affect celular function by regulating gene expression and can play a key role in developmental processes, disease pathogenesis, and genetic regulation [10]. This study summarizes the research progress regarding RHD.

Shen K [11] employed an experimental approach comparing right atrial myocardial tissues from patients with rheumatic heart valve disease (RHVD) undergoing valve replacement surgery to those from healthy controls. The study revealed significantly elevated global DNA methylation levels in RHVD patients compared to healthy controls, suggesting that dysregulation of DNA methylation may contribute to the pathogenesis of RHVD. Zheng D [12] et al. conducted genome-wide DNA methylation profiling using the Illumina Infinium HumanMethylation450 BeadChip array in 6 patients with rheumatic heart disease-associated pulmonary arterial hypertension (RHD-PAH) and 6 healthy controls. The analysis identified 40 differentially methylated regions (DMRs) and 64 hypermethylated CpG sites. Functional enrichment assays revealed significant overrepresentation of these DMR-associated genes in pathways related to negative regulation of protein kinase/transferase activity and positive regulation of protease activity. These findings provide novel mechanistic insights into the pathogenesis of RHD-PAH, suggesting epigenetic dysregulation as a contributing factor in disease development. Li N [13] analyzed five CpG sites (CpG1 - 5) in the promoter DNA methylation region of the B - type natriuretic peptide (BNP) gene. Compared with controls, RHD patients had significantly higher methylation levels at CpG4 and CpG5. Longitudinal analysis showed that postoperative warfarin therapy could reduce BNP promoter methylation, suggesting potential epigenetic mechanisms linking anticoagulant treatment with disease regulation. These findings offer new insights into the dynamic changes in DNA methylation, the regulation of cardioprotective peptides, and the interactions between therapeutic interventions and RHD pathogenesis.

In conclusion, contemporary studies primarily focus on the impact of DNA methylation imbalance on the risk of RHD while also exploring novel therapeutic strategies through rigorously controlled experimental approaches, findings that provide fresh insights into RHD pathogenesis.

Histone modification and RHD

Histone modification, a critical regulatory mechanism in epigenetics, dynamically modulates chromatin structure via the addition or removal of chemical groups [14]. This process thereby influences biological processes including gene expression, DNA repair, and cell differentiation, and plays a key role in the onset and progression of cardiovascular diseases (CVD) [15]. Abnormal histone modifictions can result in dysregulation of the expression of CVD-related genes, thereby inducing alterations in cell phenotypes and cardiac function—ultimately contributing to cardiovascular pathologies including atherosclerosis, heart failure, myocardial infarction, heart valve diseases (HVD), cardiac hypertrophy[16].

Studies by Yada [17] et al. in preclinical models of various types of diabetes demonstrated that alterations in histone methylation and acetylation correlate with pathological cardiac remodeling observed in patients. This finding highlighted that dysregulation of histone acetylation and methylation in the failing heart impairs cardiac cell function under hyperglycemic conditions. This provided new therapeutic strategies for diabetes and cardiomyopathy. Meanwhile, the Y study [18] revealed that exercise-induced histone acetylation and methylation changes effectively activate the protective mechanisms of cardiac genes in heart failure, diabetic cardiomyopathy, and obesity-related cardiac dysfunction. The research further proposed integrating exercise into clinical protocols as a complementary rehabilitative and preventive strategy for CVD in conjunction with traditional treatments. Niu [19] et al. revealed that histone and non-histone modifications, metabolic regulation, and signaling pathways interact with lactylation and methylation to form a complex epigenetic feedback network, this network modulated pathological processes associated with myocardial aging, and the research suggested that the precise regulation of lactylation and methylation could provide a novel theoretical framework and therapeutic targets for preventing and treating myocardial aging. To explore the role of enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) mediated H3K27me3 in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-treated H9C2 cells, X et al. [20] detected the expression o EZH2. They found that under inflammatory conditions, EZH2 inhibited the transcription of myocardial cells by enhancing H3K27me3 expression and promoted myocardial cell apoptosis. Yang et al. systematically investigated CVDs, including cardiac hypertrophy, heart failure, atherosclerosis, and myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury. By delineating the roles of histone deacetylases (HDACs) in diverse post-translational modifications and the interactions between HDACs and HDAC inhibitors during these pathological processes, the authors proposed that HDACs and their inhibitors represent potential therapeutic targets for these cardiovascular conditions [21]. Meanwhile, Huang [22] et al. employed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to demonstrate, for the first time, that lumican was involved in mediating H3 histone lactoylation and can promote the development of aortic valve calcification. Based on these findings, Huang suggested that lumican may serve as a promising therapeutic target for intervening in calcific aortic valve disease (CAVD), which also provides a novel insight into the treatment of RHD.

In conclusion, current research on histone modification and cardiovascular disease has certain limitations. Specifically, most of these studies focused on histone acetylation and methylation to elucidate their effects on the pathological processes of cardiovascular diseases. In the future, it is anticipated that research can explore key breakthroughs in the clinical treatment of CVDs by investigating diverse types of histone modifications.

Noncoding RNA and RHD

Non-coding RNA (ncRNA) refers to a class of RNA molecules that do not encode proteins, including microRNA (miRNA), long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), and circular RNA (circRNA) [23]. These ncRNAs play an important role in cellular life activities by regulating gene expression, epigenetic modification, and translation, primarily through transcriptional mechanisms [24].

In recent years, a growing body of research has focused on ncRNAs and their associations with various diseases. This review focuses on ncRNAs in CVDs, with a particular focus on RHD, aiming to provide novel insights for the early diagnosis and targeted treatment of RHD. For example, Mei B and colleagues established an lncRNA-miRNA co-expression network, among a total of 22,829 human ncRNAs, they identified 1909 lncRNAs. The study revealed that 182 lncRNAs exhibited differential expression in AF patients compared with normal sinus rhythm (NSR) patients and indicated that AF suppresses the expression of these lncRNAs in the RA of RHD patients, which may provide insights for exploring potential therapeutic approaches for AF in these patients [25]. At the same time, Zhu et al. found that the expression levels of miRNA-1183 and miRNA-1299 in the tissues and plasma samples of patients with RHD were significantly higher than those in healthy control groups, they also observed that circulating miR-328-3p in plasma was highly expressed in RHD patients with AF, these findings suggest that miRNAs may serve as biomarkers for RHD patients with AF [26]. Li ZH demonstrated that ncRNAs interact with various proteins—including protein 53 (p53) and nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB)—as well as the fusion in sarcoma protein, these interactions regulate the histological and electrophysiological functions of tissues during cardiac development, ultimately contributing to the pathogenesis of coronary heart disease (CHD), myocardial infarction (MI), and RHD [27]. Zhao et al. established a rat model of RHD and employed real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) to detect miRNA expression, they found that LINC00707 regulates sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor 1 (S1PR1) by targeting miR-145-5p, which ultimately exerts an inhibitory effect on myocardial fibrosis and immune dysfunction in RHD rats [28]. Chen et al. established a rat model of RHD using inactivated GAS and Complete Freund's adjuvant, and subsequently used a recombinant adeno-associated virus (AAV-miR155-inhibitor) to suppress miR-155-5p expression in the heart, which revealed increased miR-155-5p expression in both valvular tissue and serum exosomes of the RHD model rats, along with reduced S1PR1 activity, activated SOCS1/STAT3 signaling pathway, and elevated levels of IL-6 and IL-17 in these two biological samples. These results demonstrate that inhibiting miR-155-5p via regulating the S1PR1, SOCS1/STAT3, and IL-6/STAT3 signaling pathways can mitigate RHD-induced valvular damage, thereby providing novel therapeutic targets or strategies for this disease [29]. By comparing miRNA expression profiles between patients with RHD and healthy controls, Li Ni identified 133 miRNAs that were significantly upregulated in RHD patients, including miR-1183 and miR-1299, while 137 miRNAs (miR-4423-3p and miR-218-1-3p) were significantly downregulated, it further suggested that miR-1183 and miR-1299 may play distinct roles in the pathogenesis of RHD-associated secondary pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), and could potentially serve as biomarkers for disease progression [30].

In summary, current research on ncRNAs and RHD focuses predominantly on specific genetic factors. Scientists have established RHD rat models to enhance experimental reproducibility and employed case-control studies that compare patients to healthy controls, a method proven to significantly reduce time and labor costs. However, these approaches have limitations, such as potential inadequate sample size, recall bias, and selection bias, which could compromise the accuracy of conclusions regarding specific ncRNA factors. Future research should adopt standardized experimental protocols and integrate cellular experiments, animal models, and human clinical trials to advance the prevention and treatment of RHD.

Chromatin remodeling and RHD

Chromatin remodeling regulates nucleosome positioning, histone exchange, and chromatin accessibility via histone modifications and ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling complexes, thereby controlling gene expression, DNA replication, and DNA repair [31]; his process plays a critical role in the development of individuals and the progression of diseases. Current research on chromatin remodeling and RHD involves specific complexes, including SWI/SNF, CHD, INO80 and ISWI. Under normal physiological conditions, these complexes typically function as positive regulators and guardians of cellular molecular processes; their dysregulation, however, can contribute to the pathogenesis of various diseases [32].

Through multi-omics analyses, G et al. demonstrated that in patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), several transcription factors, including SP1 and EGR1, exhibit fetal-like binding motif patterns in chromatin accessibility within nucleosome-depleted regions (NDRs) of HCM hearts; furthermore, in HCM mouse models harboring myotubular mutations, inhibition of SP1 or EGR1 alleviated the HCM phenotype while reversing fetal gene reprogramming, which thus presents a high-precision multi-omics atlas of cardiac tissue specific to HCM [33]. A study has revealed that adult cardiomyocytes transition to a fetal-like state when stimulated by myocardial thickening, BRG1 is reactivated and forms the BRG1-HDAC-PARP chromatin remodeling complex with its embryonic partners, namely histone deacetylase (HDAC) and poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP); this process induces gene expression associated with aortic stenosis (AS), thereby providing new insights into the complexity of epigenetic gene regulation in diseased aortic valve cells and affected myocardial tissue [34]. The high-mobility group A1 (HMGA1) protein acts as a dynamic regulator of gene transcription and chromatin remodeling, and plays a critical role in the pathological progression of multiple CVDs; so Cai et al. established a myocardial cell-specific HMGA1 overexpression model via intraperitoneal administration of LPS for 6 h, which was used to induce systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS); which demonstrated that HMGA1 overexpression enhances myocardial inflammation and apoptosis by upregulating cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) expression, whereas HMGA1 silencing reduces inflammation via downregulating the transcriptional activator STAT3; however, this HMGA1 silencing simultaneously induces cell aggregation and apoptosis, thus confirming HMGA1's involvement in LPS-induced myocardial inflammation [35]. Meanwhile, Michael elucidated BRD4-dependent crosstalk between specific immune cell subpopulations and fibroblasts via IL1B, thereby revealing how inflammation drives pro-fibrotic cellular states; which offers insights to inform strategies for regulating this process in chronic inflammatory diseases, including heart disease and other conditions characterized by tissue remodeling and simultaneously identifies novel therapeutic targets for the treatment of RHD-related fibrosis [36].

Current research indicates that key regulatory factors of chromatin remodeling and related inflammatory factors are emerging as potential therapeutic targets for CVDs. However, limited progress has been made in clarifying their correlation with RHD. Future studies should further explore the clinical application potential of chromatin remodeling factors and complexes for RHD treatment. Additionally, we should utilize multi-omics technologies such as ATAC-seq and RNA-seq, which have been successfully applied in the study of HCM, to better elucidate the pathogenesis of RHD.

Future clinical applications

As outlined in the review of the four core mechanisms and research on RHD, epigenetic studies can further elucidate the multifactorial etiology and pathogenesis of RHD. This approach not only helps identify potential biomarkers but also facilitates the development of novel therapeutic technologies [37].These advancements will ultimately drive a paradigm shift in RHD diagnosis and treatment—specifically, the transition from late-stage intervention to early intervention, precision management, and fundamental prevention [38]. First, in terms of DNA methylation, DMR [10]-related genes and BNP [13] may become potential biomakers to provide new insights into the pathogenesis of RHD, and the use of warfarin anticoagulation therapy can effectively reduce the pain of postoperative RHD patients. At the same time, at the histone modification level, HDACs, inhibitors HDACis [21] and lumican [22] can be used as potential targets for clinical treatment of RHD and even CVDs, supplemented by exercise as a traditional CVD rehabilitation and prevention measures [18]. Secondly, in terms of ncRNA, miR-328-3p [29] can serve as a biomarker for RHD combined AF patients to provide dual protection for RHD and AF patients; prevent the interaction between ncRNA [27] and protein to regulate histological and electrophysiological functions of cardiac development leading to the deterioration of RHD. Finally, in chromatin remodeling research, the integration of multi-omics technologies with rat models enables the creation of high-precision omics atlases for cardiac tissues [33]; the BRG1-HDAC-PARP chromatin remodeling complex induces AS gene expression, serving as a biomarker for aortic stenosis. Additionally [34], BRD4 factors drive fibrotic cell states, presenting novel therapeutic targets for RHD-related fibrosis [36].

The above biomarkers, rehabilitation therapies, and novel anticoagulation regimens and techniques provide unique insights into the diagnosis and treatment of RHD. As future research advances, these insights will significantly alleviate the disease's economic burden.

Conclusion

Summarize the current epigenetic advances of RHD

Current research on the epigenetics of RHD has advanced progressively in depth, with studies primarily focusing on specific components across the four core epigenetic mechanisms: BNP in the context of DNA methylation, miRNAs in ncRNAs, histone acetylation/methylation, and chromatin remodeling complexes. These analyses aim to elucidate the roles of these components in RHD pathogenesis and their potential as therapeutic targets, yet direct epigenetic studies on RHD remain limited. Most investigations use case-control designs to compare RHD patients with healthy controls, and to investigate how these factors contribute to RHD progression. Additionally, researchers have developed rat models of RHD supplemented with adjuvants, which are used to simulate the infectious process induced by GAS.

With the ongoing advancement of modern technology, epigenetic research on RHD should pursue technological innovation and clinical translation. As summarized above, current studies on the relationship between RHD and epigenetics still face multiple limitations and challenges, including insufficient comprehensiveness of research scope, limited diversity of study subjects, methodological biases in small-scale cross-sectional surveys, and lack of standardized criteria in rat experiments and other controlled studies. Future research should delve deeper into the dynamic changes in epigenetic mechanisms during disease progression, identify novel regulatory strategies and specific factors, strengthen integrated multi-omics analysis and minimally invasive interventional techniques to develop diagnostic and therapeutic targets, and leverage international collaboration, data sharing, and interdisciplinary cooperation to elucidate epigenetic regulatory patterns. These efforts will provide new strategies for disease prevention, management, and clinical care.

Therefore, ethical approval was not required for this synthesis.

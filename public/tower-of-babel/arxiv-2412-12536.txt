Abstract
. For the family of Lozi maps, we study homoclinic points for the saddle fixed point X X in the first quadrant. Specifically, in the parameter space, we examine the boundary of the region in which homoclinic points for X X exist. For all parameters on that boundary, all intersections of the stable and unstable manifold of X X , apart from X X , are tangential, or these manifolds intersect along a segment. We ultimately prove that for such parameters, all possible homoclinic points for X X are iterates of two special points Z Z and V V , or iterates of points on a segment joining V V with an iterate of Z Z . Additionally, we describe the parameter curves that form the boundary and provide explicit equations for several of them.

1.
Introduction

With the intention of providing a map that would be an analogue of the Hénon map, but at the same time possess simpler properties, R. Lozi introduced in his 1978 paper
[
5
]
a two-parameter family of homeomorphisms

L_{a,b}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad L_{a,b}(x,\>y)=(1+y-a|x|,\>bx),

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
are the parameters. This family has since been known as the
Lozi map family
.

Despite the piecewise af
fine nature of the Lozi map, it turned out that it induces complicated dynamics. Throughout the studies of that dynamics, in the case when
b>0
, much attention has been paid to the stable and unstable manifold of the fixed point
X
in the first quadrant,
W_{X}^{s}
and
W_{X}^{u}
. These notions are formally introduced in the next section. In this paper, we are interested in points at which
W_{X}^{s}
and
W_{X}^{u}
intersect. Such points are called
homoclinic points
for the fixed point
X
. In general, the presence of homoclinic points indicates complex dynamical behavior which is often linked to chaos.

Specifically, we focus on the border case of the existence of homoclinic points for
X
. More formally, in the parameter space, let
\mathcal{H}
denote the set of all parameters
(a,b)
,
a>0
,
0<b<1
, such that the Lozi maps
L_{a,b}
exhibit homoclinic points for
X
. We study
\partial\mathcal{H}
, the boundary of
\mathcal{H}
, which we call the
boundary of existence of homoclinic points
for
X
. A part of this boundary is represented in Figure
11
. As it will follow from Lemma
3.5
, for parameter values on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{H}
, all homoclinic intersections of
W_{X}^{s}
and
W_{X}^{u}
are either
tangential
, which is illustrated in Figure
12
, or
W_{X}^{s}
and
W_{X}^{u}
intersect along a segment as in Figure
10
. Here, the notions of tangential and transverse intersections will be readjusted since
W_{X}^{s}
and
W_{X}^{u}
are polygonal lines and therefore not dif
ferentiable curves. In the main result of the paper, we determine the set of homoclinic points for
X
in the border case. In this context, the two distinct points
Z
and
V
, at which
W_{X}^{u}
and
W_{X}^{s}
, respectively, intersect the
x
- and
y
-axis for the first time, starting from
X
, proved to be of particular importance. For an exact definition of the points
Z
and
V
, we refer the reader to the next section; specially, Figure
1
illustrates several iterates of these points as an aid for visual intuition.

Theorem 1.1
.

For parameters on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{H}
, the set of all homoclinic points for
X
is one of the following:

(1)

the orbit of
Z
,

(2)

the orbit of
V
,

(3)

the union of orbits of
Z
and
V
,

(4)

the union of orbits of points on a segment joining
V
with an iterate of
Z
.

A rather surprising fact here is that
W_{X}^{s}
and
W_{X}^{u}
can also intersect along a segment of points, even more so since this cannot happen for parameter pairs
(a,b)
such that
0<b<1
,
a>0
and
a>b+1
; see Remark
3.14
.

It is worth mentioning that our main theorem, in a way, represents a counterpart to the result of Ishii in
[
3
]
, where the first tangency problem was solved. There, the author showed that for all parameters on the boundary of the maximal entropy region (the region where
L_{a,b}
is conjugate to the full shift on two symbols),
L_{a,b}
has a heteroclinic tangency for
b>0
, i.e., a homoclinic tangency for
b<0
, and these tangencies are iterates of a special tangency on the
x
-axis in the phase space. In the parameter space, as we leave the maximal entropy region and move along the positive
a
-axis, from greater to smaller values, at one moment we pass the boundary of existence of homoclinic points for
X
,
\partial\mathcal{H}
, and enter the region where these points cease to exist. Therefore, the points stated in Theorem
1.1
can be considered as the
points of last tangency
for
L_{a,b}
.

Moreover, the boundary
\partial\mathcal{H}
also appears in the studies of the
zero entropy locus
of
L_{a,b}
. Firstly, Yildiz provided in
[
8
]
partial results about zero topological entropy
h_{top}
for
L_{a,b}
. In addition, the author numerically observed a region in the parameter space in which
h_{top}(L_{a,b})=0
. That region is in part bounded by
\partial\mathcal{H}
, and the author remarks that this boundary is expected to be piecewise algebraic. This last claim is justified by the fact that the curves forming
\partial\mathcal{H}
represent intersections of
W_{X}^{s}
and
W_{X}^{u}
, which are piecewise linear. This is explained in greater detail in Section
4
.

Secondly, very recent results of Misiurewicz and Štimac in
[
7
]
expand those of Yildiz and show that
h_{top}(L_{a,b})=0
in a large region of parameters that is contained in the complement of
\mathcal{H}
. Thirdly, Burns and Weiss proved in
[
2
]
that a dif
feomorphism possessing a transverse homoclinic point has positive topological entropy.

Here, it is important to mention that in a private correspondence, Y. Ishii and D. Sands brought to the author’s attention the existence of parameter pairs for which the Lozi map exhibits transverse homoclinic points for period 6 saddle points. Even though these findings indicate that the zero entropy locus of
L_{a,b}
is a proper subset of the complement of
\mathcal{H}
, the study of the dynamical behavior near
\partial\mathcal{H}
will be of relevance in determining the exact zero entropy locus of the Lozi family.

Finally, the aforementioned result of Burns and Weiss also demonstrates another incentive to study
\partial\mathcal{H}
: namely, homoclinic points have so far been an object of research mainly in the context of dif
feomorphisms, that is, one required some degree of dif
ferentiability. This work thus presents a step towards a comparable theory for homeomorphisms of the plane.

This paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
, we review the preliminary notions on Lozi maps and give an overview of the notation. The following Section
3
is divided into three parts. In Subsection
3.1
, we describe the zigzag structure that the stable manifold
W_{X}^{s}
forms in the third quadrant of the plane. Subsection
3.2
is dedicated to tangential homoclinic points. The proof of the main theorem is concluded in Subsection
3.3
, where we consider homoclinic intersections along segments. Lastly, in Section
4
, we describe the curves that form the boundary of existence of homoclinic points for
X
and explicitly state the equations for some of them in Appendix
A
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Lozi maps

To study homoclinic points in the case when the Lozi map
L_{a,b}
is orientation-reversing (i.e.,
b>0
), one can observe parameter pairs
(a,b)
for which
0<b<1
and
a+b>1
. Namely, if
L_{a,b}
is a Lozi map with
b>1
, then it is conjugate to
L^{-1}_{a/b,\,1/b}
. Moreover, if
b-1\geqslant a
, then
L_{a,b}
does not have any fixed points. If
b-1<a\leqslant 1-b
, then
L_{a,b}
has one fixed point
X
in the first quadrant. This point is attracting for
a<1-b
, and it is not hyperbolic when
a=1-b
.

For parameter pairs
(a,b)
such that
0<b<1
and
a+b>1
, the Lozi map
L_{a,b}
has two hyperbolic saddle fixed points,
X=\bigl(\frac{1}{1+a-b},\>\frac{b}{1+a-b}\bigr)
in the first and
Y=\bigl(\frac{1}{1-a-b},\>\frac{b}{1-a-b}\bigr)
in the third quadrant. The eigenvalues of the dif
ferential of
L_{a,b}
at
X
are

\lambda_{X}^{u}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(-a-\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}\right),\quad\lambda_{X}^{s}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(-a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}\right),

while those at
Y
are given by

\lambda_{Y}^{u}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}\right),\quad\lambda_{Y}^{s}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(a-\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}\right).

Observe that
\lambda_{X}^{u}<-1
,
0<\lambda_{X}^{s}<1
and
\lambda_{Y}^{u}>1
,
-1<\lambda_{Y}^{s}<0
. Moreover, for every eigenvalue
\lambda
, the corresponding eigenvector is given by
\binom{\lambda}{b}
.

For every point
A\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and every
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, we put
A^{k}=L_{a,b}^{k}(A)
; specially,
A^{0}=A
. Recall that the set
\{A^{k}\colon k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is called the
orbit
of
A
. Moreover, a set
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is
L_{a,b}
-
invariant
if
L_{a,b}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
. An
L_{a,b}^{-1}
-
invariant
set is defined analogously.

Recall that the
unstable manifold
of
X
is the set
W_{X}^{u}=\{T\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon T^{-n}\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}X\}
. Similarly, the
stable manifold
of
X
is
W_{X}^{s}=\{T\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon T^{n}\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}X\}
. We know that
W_{X}^{u}
and
W_{X}^{s}
are
L_{a,b}
- and
L_{a,b}^{-1}
-invariant sets which contain
X
. As already explained in
[
6
]
,
W_{X}^{u}
and
W_{X}^{s}
are not manifolds in the true meaning of that notion since they are polygonal lines in the plane; see Figure
1
. We nevertheless follow the established terminology and call them manifolds.

Figure 1
.

X

a=1

Observe the unstable manifold
W_{X}^{u}
. We denote the half of that manifold that starts at
X
and goes to the right by
W_{X}^{u+}
. That half intersects the positive
x
-axis for the first time at the point

Z=\left(\frac{2+a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}{2(1+a-b)},\>0\right)=\left(\frac{2}{2+a-\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}},\>0\right).

(2.1)

The other half, starting at
X
and going to the left, we denote by
W_{X}^{u-}
. Note that

W_{X}^{u+}=\{X\}\cup\bigcup_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}L_{a,b}^{2n}\bigl(\overline{ZZ^{2}}\bigr),\quad W_{X}^{u-}=\{X\}\cup\bigcup_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}L_{a,b}^{2n}\bigl(\overline{Z^{-1}Z^{1}}\bigr).

Similarly, we denote by
W_{X}^{s-}
the half of the stable manifold
W_{X}^{s}
that starts at
X
and goes down. That part intersects the negative
y
-axis for the first time at the point

V=\left(0,\>\frac{2b-a-\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}{2(1+a-b)}\right)=\left(0,\>-\frac{2b}{-a+2b+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}\right).

(2.2)

We have

W_{X}^{s-}=\{X\}\cup\bigcup_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}L_{a,b}^{-n}\bigl(\overline{VV^{1}}\bigr).

The other half of
W_{X}^{s}
is a half-line emanating from
X
and going up in the first quadrant. That part we denote by
W_{X}^{s+}
.

As already stated,
W_{X}^{u}
and
W_{X}^{s}
are polygonal lines in the plane. We are interested in the points at which these lines break, i.e., the endpoints of maximal straight line segments contained in
W_{X}^{u}
and
W_{X}^{s}
. Adopting the terminology from
[
1
]
, we call such points
post-critical points
if they lie on
W_{X}^{u}
and
V-points
if they are on
W_{X}^{s}
.

3.
Boundary homoclinic points

We will investigate homoclinic points for the fixed point
X
of the Lozi map; more precisely, we will describe the boundary of the area of their existence in the parameter space. Recall that a point
T
,
T\neq X
, is said to be a
homoclinic point
for
X
if
T
is contained in the intersection of its stable and unstable manifold,
T\in W^{s}_{X}\cap W^{u}_{X}
. The main result is that in the border case, all homoclinic points for
X
are iterates of the points
Z
and/or
V
; see Theorem
1.1
. In this section we will observe parameter pairs
(a,b)
such that
0<b<1
and
a+b>1
.

3.1.
Structure of the stable manifold

In this subsection, we will describe in greater detail the shape that the stable manifold
W_{X}^{s}
forms in the third quadrant which will give us some insight into the order of appearance of V-points on it. The following technical lemma will be useful during that analysis.

Lemma 3.1

(Some geometric properties of
L_{a,b}
and
L_{a,b}^{-1}
)
.

(1)

The image of the
y
-axis under
L_{a,b}
is the
x
-axis and the image of the
x
-axis under
L_{a,b}
is the curve
x=1-\frac{a}{b}|y|
.

(2)

The image of the
x
-axis under
L_{a,b}^{-1}
is the
y
-axis and the image of the
y
-axis under
L_{a,b}^{-1}
is the curve
y=a|x|-1
.

(3)

Let
\alpha
be a straight line segment in the lower half-plane which lies on a straight line whose slope equals
s_{1}
. Then
L_{a,b}^{-1}(\alpha)
lies on a straight line whose slope equals

s_{2}=b\cdot\frac{1}{s_{1}}-a.

We know that
V
is the intersection of the
y
-axis and a straight line through
X
whose slope equals
s_{0}=\frac{b}{\lambda_{X}^{s}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}\right)
. Furthermore,

W_{X}^{s-}=\overline{XV^{1}}^{s}\cup\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{a,b}^{-n}\bigl(\overline{VV^{1}}^{s}\bigr),

so we see that the part of
W_{X}^{s-}
which does not contain
\overline{XV}^{s}
consists of preimages of a line segment lying in the lower half-plane. Notice that Lemma
3.1
(3) gives a recurrence for the slopes of certain parts of those preimages.

Lemma 3.2
.

The sequence
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
given by the recurrence

s_{n+1}=b\cdot\frac{1}{s_{n}}-a,\ n\geqslant 0;\quad s_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}\right),

(3.1)

has the following properties:

(1)

if

for some

s_{n}>0
for all

(2)

(3)

,

(4)

(s_{n})

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}s_{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(-a-\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}\right)
.

Proof.

The first two claims immediately follow from recurrence (
3.1
) and

. In order to prove the third claim, let

M_{1}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(-a-\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}\right),\quad M_{2}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(-a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}\right)

be the roots of the equation
M^{2}+aM-b=0
, i.e., the fixed points of the function

f\colon\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad f(x)=b\cdot\frac{1}{x}-a,

we see that

j_{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eg:recurr})}}{{=}}\frac{b\cdot\frac{1}{s_{n}}-a-M_{1}}{b\cdot\frac{1}{s_{n}}-a-M_{2}}=\frac{b\cdot\frac{1}{s_{n}}-b\cdot\frac{1}{M_{1}}}{b\cdot\frac{1}{s_{n}}-b\cdot\frac{1}{M_{2}}}=\frac{M_{2}}{M_{1}}\cdot j_{n}

for every

j_{n+1}=\mu j_{n},\ n\geqslant 0;\quad j_{0}=\tfrac{1}{a}\left(a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}\right)>0.

(3.2)

Therefore,

s_{n}=\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}j_{n}}{1-j_{n}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(3.3)

and since

(c)

backward iterates of points

We will now show that case (c) is not possible. Assume by contradiction that one of the endpoints of
\alpha
is the point

, for some

, and that this point does not coincide with any iterate of

. Then there exists an

such that

is contained in
\overline{Z^{-2m-2}Z^{-2m}}^{u}
, and

does not contain the endpoints of that segment. Consequently,

is a straight line segment lying on
\overline{ZZ^{2}}^{u}
in
\mathcal{Q}_{4}
, and one of the endpoints of

is

Let the point

be defined by (

), and let

be such that

is contained in

. Observe the polygon

whose boundary is
\partial\mathcal{P}=[V^{-q},D^{-p-1}]^{s}\cup\overline{D^{-p-1}D^{-p}}\cup[D^{-p},V^{-q}]^{s}
. The point

lies outside of

. Therefore, in order to pass through that point,

will intersect the interior
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Int}\mathcal{P}
and consequently, it intersects transversely the boundary of
\mathcal{P}
. Since the boundary

is contained in

,

. This implies that

and

intersect transversely, which is a contradiction with Lemma

. Therefore, backward iterates of points

cannot be the endpoints of
\alpha
.

It follows that the endpoints of
\alpha
can only be iterates of
Z
and
V
(cases (a) and (b)). Due to Lemma

, the endpoints of
\alpha
cannot both be iterates of
Z
, nor can they both be iterates of

. Therefore, one endpoint is an iterate of
Z
, and the other one an iterate of

, which proves claim (1).

Let us now prove claim (2). For every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, define

\delta_{n}=[Z^{2n},Z^{2n+2}]^{u},\quad\displaystyle\Delta=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n},

as in (

and

intersect along a segment, then

(see Figure

with an iterate of

Let

be the smallest index

such that

intersects the

-axis. By Lemma

, we know that

is a straight line segment,

Now let

and

intersect along

Remark 3.14

The result obtained in Theorem

.

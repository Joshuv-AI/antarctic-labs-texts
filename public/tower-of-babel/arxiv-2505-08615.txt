On Selection of Cross-Section Averages in Non-stationary Environments
Jan Ditzen, Ovidijus Stauskas

Abstract
In this Supplementary Material, we provide the proofs of our auxiliary results and our main result in Proposition 1 (Sections 2 and 3). In addition, we demonstrate how the proofs change in the case of the IC of Margaritella and Westerlund, (2023) . In Section 4, we provide additional simulation evidence, including, e.g., correlated idiosyncratics or eigenvalue ratio method of Juodis, (2022) .



Keywords:
Information criteria, factors, CCE, cross-section averages, non-stationary data

Introduction

Consider a set of
K
variables (stacked over time
t=1,\ldots,T
) that admits a factor structure for cross-sectional units
i=1,\ldots,N
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{i}=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{C}_{i}+\mathbf{U}_{i},

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{Z}_{i}=[\mathbf{z}_{i,1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{i,T}]^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times K}
,
\mathbf{U}_{i}
is an error term, while
\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times m}
are latent common factors, where
m
denotes the number of factors, and
\mathbf{C}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times K}
is the loading matrix. (
1.1
) nests several settings. The most popular one is that of
interactive effects
(see
Bai, 2009a,
). Then
\mathbf{Z}_{i}=[\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{X}_{i}]\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times(k+1)}
, where
\mathbf{y}_{i}=\mathbf{X}_{i}\boldsymbol{\beta}+\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}
gives a model with unobserved heterogeneity. If
\mathbf{X}_{i}=\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}+\mathbf{V}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times k}
, we have the Common Correlated Effects (CCE) setting of
Pesaran, (2006)
with
\mathbf{V}_{i},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}
(
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}
) representing idiosyncratics (loadings). (
1.1
) goes beyond interactive effects and CCE.
Stauskas and De Vos, (2025)
consider Distinct Correlated Effects (DCE), where
\mathbf{y}_{i}
and
\mathbf{X}_{i}
load on distinct sets of factors.
Massacci and Kapetanios, (2024)
use
\mathbf{Z}_{i}=\mathbf{X}_{i}
to model a set of co-moving regressors in a forecasting equation.

It is possible to estimate unobserved factors in several ways, including the Principal Components (PC) method (see
Bai and Ng,, 2006
) or diversified projections (see
Fan and Liao,, 2022
).
Pesaran, (2006)
suggests a simple and elegant way to proxy
\mathbf{F}
up to a linear transformation by taking cross-section averages (CAs):
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{Z}_{i}=\widehat{\mathbf{F}}=\mathbf{F}\overline{\mathbf{C}}+o_{p}(1)
, because CAs of the idiosyncratics are negligible under a wide range of empirically relevant assumptions (see e.g.
Pesaran and Tosetti,, 2011
). This is enough to estimate
\mathbf{F}
in DCE and forecasting settings. In interactive effects, the CCE estimator of
\boldsymbol{\beta}
is then simply the least squares (LS) estimator augmented with
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}=\widehat{\mathbf{F}}
as an additional regressor.

A substantial advantage of CAs is their ability to estimate
\mathbf{F}
irrespective of their time series properties. Indeed, the CCE estimator enjoys popularity, because it is consistent and asymptotically normal under very general
\mathbf{F}
in large
N,T
settings without any modifications (see e.g.
Kapetanios et al.,, 2011
,
Westerlund,, 2018
,
Westerlund and Petrova,, 2018
, or
Stauskas,, 2023
). This property is valid if the rank of
\overline{\mathbf{C}}
is equal to
m
, which means that the number of factors cannot exceed the effective number of CAs. However, there are reasons to ensure that the number of CAs matches
m
. In CCE, if
m<k+1
and
TN^{-1}
is bounded, CCE produces an asymptotic bias whose analytical correction is infeasible (see
Karabiyik et al.,, 2017
). This situation is common in macro datasets (see
De Vos and Stauskas,, 2024
). In general, too many CAs might even lead to a model that over-fits the data. In contrast, inclusion of too few generally leads to inconsistent estimates of the model
parameters (see
Juodis,, 2022
, for a discussion). Beyond CCE,
Stauskas and De Vos, (2025)
show that DCE only uses
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}=\overline{\mathbf{X}}
, but
m=k
must hold. In forecasting, the same is needed to avoid only conservative confidence intervals around factor-augmented forecasts (see
Karabiyik and Westerlund,, 2021
). In response to this,
Margaritella and Westerlund, (2023)
(MW) were the first to demonstrate that the information criteria (ICs) of
Bai and Ng, (2002)
and
Bai, 2009b ()
from the PC literature can be applied in the pure CCE setting under
stationary factors
to identify an optimal set of CAs from
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}
.
De Vos and Stauskas, (2024)
(DVS) show similar results when CAs are selected from
\overline{\mathbf{X}}
, which is more applicable in the DCE and forecasting settings, but the procedure has a similar theoretical basis.

PC techniques are limited in CAs setting, as they focus on
m
by detecting the largest eigenvalues of the data matrix. They cannot detect sets of CAs as they do not have a natural ordering. With IC, we learn
m
from the cardinality of the selected set.

MW stress that an assumption of stationary
\mathbf{F}
is required only to simplify the proofs, hinting at a much greater generality of IC. As CAs proxy a general factor structure, it is natural to evaluate this statement and re-visit the ICs proposed in both MW and DVS due to their wide applicability and similar theoretical foundation. The subtlety arises here because IC is minimized by grid-search at different combinations of CAs. Inevitably, there is a need to understand the asymptotic behavior of IC evaluated at a combination inconsistent for
\mathbf{F}
that is non-stationary, which is a new undertaking in the CAs literature. Therefore, this study can be seen as the CAs counterpart of
Bai, (2004)
, where classical PC results of
Bai and Ng, (2002)
are evaluated against pure unit root factors. Instead, we use a mildly integrated process by
Magdalinos and Phillips, (2009)
to model
\mathbf{F}
, which allows us to experiment with varying degrees of factor persistence. We demonstrate formally and in simulations that, while ICs remain consistent as
(N,T)\to\infty
, highly persistent factors negatively affect their small sample performance. We discuss differences between our results and those in
Bai, (2004)
, and also explore penalty functions adapted to non-stationary
\mathbf{F}
, as suggested in the latter study. Since they make the performance of our ICs even worse, we argue that practitioners should not mechanically take recommendations from the PC literature if the goal is to select CAs. More importantly, the ICs of DVS and MW (with the original penalties) should be applied in relatively large samples if the presence of non-stationary factors is suspected.

Theorem 1
.
Under conditions of Proposition 1 with

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{M}=M_{0}\right)\to 1.

Proof.
Follows from Proposition 1, because
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{IC}(M)-\mathrm{IC}(M_{0})<0\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\left(\mathrm{IC}(M)-\mathrm{IC}(M_{0})\right)/p_{N,T}<0\right)\to 0
under (a) and a similar statement holds under (b).

The key message of Theorem 1 is that the IC of DVS (and MW) is consistent, but exhibits hybrid asymptotic properties characteristic to stationary and non-stationary factors. Consequently, two forces are in effect:

should still be negligible, but the factor estimation error can be “large” if
M\subset M_{0}
as indicated by the remainder in (
A.9

Bai, (2004)
in the PC setting, where two conditions are satisfied: 1)
p_{N,T}\to\infty
, and 2)
p_{N,T}/\ln(T)\to 0
which is given in footnote 3 of the study to adapt the penalty to the logarithmic sum of squared residuals, which is exactly our setting. Importantly, this condition does not interfere with consistency in our Theorem 1, because clearly
p_{N,T}=o(\ln(T))
holds. Therefore, we compare both types of penalties in the simulations to illustrate the outcome if practitioners simply extrapolate recommendations from the PC literature. We let
\widetilde{p}_{N,T}=\ln(T)p_{N,T}
, which follows the suggestion in (12) in
Bai, (2004)
, and obeys
\widetilde{p}_{N,T}\to\infty
and
\widetilde{p}_{N,T}/\ln(T)\to 0
.

Remark 1.

Note that under stationarity,
\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M,M_{0}}\to_{p}c>0
when
M\subset M_{0}
, according to the results in DVS (and similarly in MW). Our Proposition 1 naturally accommodates this result under
\tau=0
, because, by following the same approximation steps, (
A.9
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M,M_{0}}=\sum_{j:\lambda>0}\ln\left(1+\lambda_{j}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{M.M_{0}}^{0})\right)+R_{N,T}\to_{p}c>0

as
(N,T)\to\infty
because each summand is greater than 1 w.p. 1. Here,
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{M.M_{0}}^{0}=\plim_{(N,T)\to\infty}[\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}-\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}]\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}
(positive semi-definite, non-zero). Also,
|R_{N,T}|=O_{p}(N^{-1})+O_{p}((NT)^{-1/2})
, which is the same as in DVS (and MW) under
\tau=0
, as expected from the discussion of (
A.9
).

Remark 2.

Throughout, we assume that
M_{0}
is unique. However, because CAs do not have a natural ordering, the problem is only set-identified. It can be shown that both ICs select the set that minimizes the asymptotic mean squared error out of all the sets that satisfy Assumption 4 (see e.g. Corollary 3.1 in
Margaritella and Westerlund,, 2023
). In this study, the first order issue is ability of IC to select any
\widehat{M}
with the cardinality of
m
.

Remark 3.

Juodis, (2022)
considers eigenvalue-based method of
Ahn and Horenstein, (2013)
under stationarity, where the focus is learning
m

\mathbf{F}
in the Supplementary material.

4
Conclusion

This study is the first to examine the selection of an optimal set of CAs in CCE and related settings by ICs inspired by
Bai and Ng, (2002)
when latent factors are non-stationary. In particular, we use mild integration to explore varying degrees of factor persistence and demonstrate that ICs remain consistent without any modifications. However, the more persistent common factors are, the worse their small sample performance becomes, and ICs regain selection consistency only in very large samples (i.e.
(N,T)>300
, according to our experiments). Importantly, a divergent penalty suggested by
Bai, (2004)
to address non-stationary factors in PC makes our ICs perform even worse in the case of CAs. Therefore, our recommendation for CCE/CAs practitioners is not to automatically take PC literature prescriptions and interpret IC results with caution in the presence of highly persistent data, unless
N,T
are substantial.

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{C}_{i}^{\prime}\to\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{C}}
positive definite. Also,
\overline{\mathbf{C}}=[\overline{\mathbf{C}}_{m},\overline{\mathbf{C}}_{-m}]
, where
\overline{\mathbf{C}}_{-m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(K-m)}
and
\overline{\mathbf{C}}_{m}=\overline{\mathbf{C}}\mathbf{q}_{M_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
for a unique
M_{0}
is full rank for all
N
, including
N\to\infty
. If
m=K
, then
\overline{\mathbf{C}}=\overline{\mathbf{C}}_{m}
.

5.2
Auxiliary Results

Lemma 1

Under Assumptions 1 - 4, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\mathbf{F}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{E}}\right\|=O_{p}(N^{-1/2}T^{-\tau/2})

as
(N,T)\to\infty
.

Proof.
To begin with, we can write
\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\mathbf{F}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{E}}=\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}
. Then for some positive
\epsilon
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\|>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon\sqrt{N}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}(\sqrt{N}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime})\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon\sqrt{N}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|\left\|(\sqrt{N}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t})\right\|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon\sqrt{N}}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}(\left\|\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|)\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|(\sqrt{N}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t})\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon\sqrt{N}T^{\tau/2}}\left(\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}(\left\|\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|(\sqrt{N}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t})\right\|\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}}_{O(1)}

\displaystyle=O(N^{-1/2}T^{-\tau/2}),

(A.1)

because

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}(\left\|\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sqrt{(\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}))}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathrm{tr}[\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}])=O(1)

(A.2)

by the concavity of function appearing in Jensen’s inequality. Note that this result gives a different rate than for similar terms in, for example,
Pitarakis, (2023)
, which is brought down exactly by time dependence in
\{\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}\}
. Also note that independence between the factors and idiosyncratics is not strictly necessary, and we use it for simplicity. Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\|>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon\sqrt{N}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|\left\|(\sqrt{N}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t})\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon\sqrt{N}}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\sqrt{N}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\sqrt{N}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}}{\epsilon\sqrt{N}T^{\tau/2}}\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|T^{-\tau/2}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=O(N^{-1/2}T^{-\tau/2}),

(A.3)

because
\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|T^{-\tau/2}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}=O(1)
by Lemma 3.1 in
Magdalinos and Phillips, (2009)
.

Corollary 1

Under Assumptions 1 - 4, but
\mathbf{v}_{i,t}
and
\varepsilon_{i,t}
are uncorrelated over time, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\mathbf{F}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{E}}\right\|=O_{p}(N^{-1/2}T^{-(1+\tau)/2})

as
(N,T)\to\infty
.

Proof.
By assumption, we then have that
\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}
is uncorrelated over time. Then by Markov’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\|>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-2}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{s}\mathbf{f}_{s}^{\prime}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{s}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{s}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{s}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{s}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{(t-1)\lor(s-1)}\right)\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(N\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-2}\frac{\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}\left(N\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}\right)}{NT^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq O(N^{-1}T^{-1-\tau})\times\underbrace{\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}\right)\right]}_{O(1)}

\displaystyle=O(N^{-1}T^{-1-\tau}),

(A.4)

which implies that
\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\mathbf{F}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{E}}\right\|=O_{p}(N^{-1/2}T^{-(1+\tau)/2})
if
\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}
is uncorrelated over time. Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(N\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{e}}_{t}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\mathbf{e}_{j,t}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{tr}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{e}_{i,t}\mathbf{e}_{j,t}^{\prime}\right)\right]=O(1),

(A.5)

uniformly in
t
by our assumptions.

Lemma 2

Under Assumptions 1 - 4, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\mathbf{F}^{\prime}\mathbf{E}_{i}\right\|=O_{p}(T^{-\tau/2})

as
T\to\infty
.

Proof
. Similarly to Lemma
1
, we have that by Markov’s and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\right\|>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|\left\|\mathbf{e}_{i,t}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}(\left\|\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|)\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathbf{e}_{i,t}\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon T^{\tau/2}}\left(\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}(\left\|\mathbf{f}_{t}\right\|)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathbf{e}_{i,t}\right\|\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}}_{O(1)}

\displaystyle=O(T^{-\tau/2}),

(A.6)

as expected.

Corollary 2

Under Assumptions 1 - 4, but
\mathbf{v}_{i,t}
and
\varepsilon_{i,t}
are uncorrelated over time, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\mathbf{F}^{\prime}\mathbf{E}_{i}\right\|=O_{p}(T^{-(1+\tau)/2})

as
T\to\infty
.

Proof.
We use an approach similar to the one in Corollary
1
. Then by Markov’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\right\|>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-2}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\mathbf{e}_{i,s}\mathbf{f}_{s}^{\prime}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{s}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\mathbf{e}_{i,s}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{s}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\mathbf{e}_{i,s}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\mathbf{e}_{i,s}|\mathcal{F}_{(t-1)\lor(s-1)}\right)\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{-2}\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-2}\frac{\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{e}_{i,t}^{\prime}\mathbf{e}_{i,t}\right)}{T^{1+\tau}}\left(\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}\mathbf{f}_{t}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq O(T^{-1-\tau})\times\underbrace{\mathrm{tr}\left[\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{f}_{t}\mathbf{f}_{t}^{\prime}\right)\right]}_{O(1)}

\displaystyle=O(T^{-1-\tau}),

(A.7)

which implies that
\left\|\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\mathbf{F}^{\prime}\mathbf{E}_{i}\right\|=O_{p}(T^{-(1+\tau)/2})
.

5.3
Proposition 1 and the Proof

Proposition 1.

Under Assumptions 1-4 as
(N,T)\to\infty
, we have that:

\displaystyle\textit{(a)}\hskip 5.69054pt\textit{Under}\hskip 2.84526ptM_{0}\subset M,\hskip 2.84526pt\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M,M_{0}}=O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2});

(A.8)

\displaystyle\textit{(b)}\hskip 5.69054pt\textit{Under}\hskip 2.84526ptM\subset M_{0},\hskip 2.84526pt\mathrm{d}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M,M_{0}}=\tau b\ln(T)+\sum_{j:\lambda>0}\ln\left(\lambda_{j}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{M.M_{0}}^{0})\right)+R_{N,T},

(A.9)

where
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{M.M_{0}}^{0}=\plim_{(N,T)\to\infty}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{M.M_{0}}
(positive semi-definite, non-zero),
|R_{N,T}|=o_{p}(1)
is the remainder,
\lambda_{j}(\mathbf{A})
is the
j
-th eigenvalue of
\mathbf{A}
, while
b
is a number of strictly positive eigenvalues.

Proof
. In the proof, we focus on the IC of
De Vos and Stauskas, (2024)
(
j=DVS
). Therefore, we will have
K=k
throughout. The argument for the IC of
Margaritella and Westerlund, (2023)
(
j=MW
) is very similar, and we only need to take into account that we are working with the usual CCE setup, where
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\boldsymbol{\beta}+o_{p}(1)
under the full set of
k+1
CAs. We will comment on this at the end of the proof. For notational simplicity, we will use
\mathbf{q}_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times g}
without the subscript
\mathbf{x}
. Also, because IC of DVS covers Examples 2 and 3 (see the main text), we denote factors as
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}
.

For brevity, let
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}=\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}}\mathbf{X}_{i}
, where
\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}=\overline{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{q}_{M}=(\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}+\overline{\mathbf{V}})\mathbf{q}_{M}
for the selector matrix
\mathbf{q}_{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times g}
. Then, for
M_{0}
being the true subset of CAs such that
g=m
, let us characterize difference of two Information Criteria:

\displaystyle\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M_{0})

\displaystyle=\ln\mathrm{det}\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}\right)-\ln\mathrm{det}\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}\right)+k(g-m)p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\ln\left[\frac{\mathrm{det}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M})}{\mathrm{det}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}})}\right]+k(g-m)p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\ln\left[\det(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M})\mathrm{det}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}^{-1})\right]+k(g-m)p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\ln\mathrm{det}\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}\right)+k(g-m)p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\ln\mathrm{det}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}+\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}-\mathbf{I}_{k}\right)+k(g-m)p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\ln\mathrm{det}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}+\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}-\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}\right)+k(g-m)p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\ln\mathrm{det}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}+\left[\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}-\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}\right]\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}\right)+k(g-m)p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\ln\mathrm{det}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}+T^{\tau}\left[T^{-\tau}\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}-\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}\right)\right]\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}\right)+k(g-m)p_{N,T},

(A.10)

where we used
\ln(a)-\ln(b)=\ln\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)
and
\mathrm{det}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})=\mathrm{det}(\mathbf{A})\mathrm{det}(\mathbf{B})
. This implies that we will examine the asymptotic behavior of
T^{-\tau}\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}-\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}\right)
. Its behavior is determined by whether
M_{0}\subset M
(over-specification) or
M\subset M_{0}
(under-specification). In the latter case, under-specification happens when
M\subset M_{0}
,
M_{0}\cap M\neq\emptyset
but neither is a weak subset of each other, and when
M\cap M_{0}=\emptyset
. We only analyze the case of
M\subset M_{0}
as in
Margaritella and Westerlund, (2023)
or
De Vos and Stauskas, (2024)
, because other cases will lead to the same asymptotic conclusions.

5.3.1
Case of
M\subset M_{0}

By following
De Vos and Stauskas, (2024)
, we have

\displaystyle T^{-\tau}\left(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}-\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=T^{-\tau}\left[\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}-\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{M,\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}}\right]-T^{-\tau}\left[\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}-\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0},\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M_{0}}}}\right]

\displaystyle+T^{-\tau}\left[\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{M,\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}}-\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0},\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M_{0}}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{II}+\mathbf{III}.

(A.11)

where, for instance,
\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0},\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}}
means that it is evaluated at
\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}
. We begin with

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}})\mathbf{X}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}})\mathbf{V}_{i}+\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}})\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}})\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}+\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}})\mathbf{V}_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{I}_{a}+\mathbf{I}_{b}+\mathbf{I}_{c}+\mathbf{I}_{d}.

(A.12)

where the expansion
\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}
is the key. Note how

\displaystyle T^{-(1+\tau)}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}^{\prime}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{q}_{M}^{\prime}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{\prime}T^{-(1+\tau)}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}+\mathbf{q}_{M}^{\prime}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{\prime}T^{-(1+\tau)}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{q}_{M}+\mathbf{q}_{M}^{\prime}T^{-(1+\tau)}\overline{\mathbf{V}}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{q}_{M}^{\prime}TT^{-(1+\tau)}T^{-1}\overline{\mathbf{V}}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}\mathbf{q}_{M}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{q}_{M}^{\prime}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{\prime}T^{-(1+\tau)}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}+O_{p}(N^{-1}T^{-\tau})+O_{p}(N^{-1/2}T^{-(1+\tau)/2})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{q}_{M}^{\prime}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{\prime}T^{-(1+\tau)}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}+O_{p}(\xi^{-1}_{N,T,\tau})

(A.13)

by Corollary
2
, where we defined
\xi_{N,T,\tau}^{-1}=N^{-1}T^{-\tau}+N^{-1/2}T^{-(1+\tau)/2}
. The remainder has a slightly different rate than in
De Vos and Stauskas, (2024)
(see e.g. 2.184 in their Online Supplement), because the idiosyncratic component is still stationary, and so

\displaystyle\left\|T^{-(1+\tau)}\overline{\mathbf{V}}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}\right\|=N^{-1}T^{-\tau}\left\|NT^{-1}\overline{\mathbf{V}}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}\right\|=O_{p}(N^{-1}T^{-\tau}).

(A.14)

Moreover, note that

\displaystyle N\times\xi_{N,T,\tau}^{-1}

\displaystyle=N\times(O_{p}(N^{-1}T^{-\tau})+O_{p}(N^{-1/2}T^{-(1+\tau)/2}))

\displaystyle=O_{p}(T^{-\tau})+O_{p}(\sqrt{N}T^{-1/2}T^{-\tau/2})=O_{p}(1)

(A.15)

without further restrictions (but negligible under
TN^{-1}=O(1)
. Also, let

\displaystyle T^{\tau}\times\xi_{N,T,\tau}^{-1}

\displaystyle=T^{\tau}\times(O_{p}(N^{-1}T^{-\tau})+O_{p}(N^{-1/2}T^{-(1+\tau)/2}))

\displaystyle=O_{p}(N^{-1})+O_{p}(N^{-1/2}T^{(\tau-1)/2})

\displaystyle=o_{p}(1))=R_{N,T}

(A.16)

without further restrictions as
\tau\in(0,1)
. Note that the latter will be the dominant component in the further analysis. Both of these results will be important when determining the orders of many terms.

Next, in case of
M\subset M_{0}
, we have that
\mathrm{rank}\left(\frac{1}{T^{1+\tau}}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}^{\prime}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}\right)=m
even asymptotically, and hence

\displaystyle\left(T^{-(1+\tau)}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}^{\prime}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}\right)^{+}=\left(\mathbf{q}_{M}^{\prime}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}^{\prime}T^{-(1+\tau)}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}\right)^{+}+O_{p}(\xi_{N,T,\tau}^{-1}),

(A.17)

or zero immediately. As this positive semi-definite matrix is nonzero even in the limit, we know that not all eigenvalues are zero. Recall that
b
is the number of positive eigenvalues. Therefore, using
\ln(1+x)=x+O(x^{2})
for a small
x
and
p_{N,T}/\ln(T)=o(1)
, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle(\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M_{0}))/\ln(T)=\left[\ln\mathrm{det}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}+T^{\tau}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{M,M_{0}}^{0}\right)\right]/\ln(T)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\left[\ln\prod_{j:\lambda>0}(1+T^{\tau}\lambda_{NT,j})\right]/\ln(T)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{j:\lambda>0}\ln(1+T^{\tau}\lambda_{NT,j})\right]/\ln(T)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{j:\lambda>0}\ln(T^{\tau}\lambda_{NT,j})\right]/\ln(T)+\left[\sum_{j:\lambda>0}[\ln(1+T^{\tau}\lambda_{NT,j})-\ln(T^{\tau}\lambda_{NT,j})]\right]/\ln(T)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau b\ln(T)}{\ln(T)}+\frac{1}{\ln(T)}\sum_{j:\lambda>0}\ln(\lambda_{NT,j})+\frac{1}{\ln(T)}\sum_{j:\lambda>0}\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{T^{\tau}\lambda_{NT,j}}\right)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\tau b+\frac{1}{\ln(T)}\sum_{j:\lambda>0}\ln(\lambda_{NT,j})+\frac{1}{\ln(T)}\sum_{j:\lambda>0}\left(\frac{1}{T^{\tau}\lambda_{NT,j}}+O(T^{-2\tau})\right)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle\to_{p}\tau b>0,

(A.43)

because
\lambda_{NT,j}\to_{p}\lambda_{j}>0
as
(N,T)\to\infty
. This overall implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M_{0})<0\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M_{0})}{\ln(T)}<0\right)\to 0

(A.44)

as
(N,T)\to\infty
.

5.3.2
Case of
M_{0}\subset M

We now move to the case when

(over-specification). To analyze this case we will introduce additional notation. We decompose the selector matrix

, where

and

excess (over-selected) averages. By using this, we can further decompose

with the obvious definitions of
\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{M_{0}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{M_{0}^{C}}

(A.74)

since

\displaystyle\left\|T^{-1/2}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0\prime}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}^{0}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{G}_{T}^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(NT)^{-1/2}\left\|T^{-(1+\tau)/2}\sqrt{N}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0\prime}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}\right\|+N^{-1/2}\left\|\sqrt{T}\mathbf{G}_{T}^{-1}\right\|\left\|T^{-1}\sqrt{N}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M}^{0}\right\|

\displaystyle=O_{p}(N^{-1/2}),

(A.75)

and so the overall order is dominated by the first and third terms in (
A.74
). Further, note that
\mathbf{II}^{3}
is just a transpose of
\mathbf{II}^{2}
and so

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{II}^{3}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{V}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{0}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}})\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}\right\|=O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2}).

(A.76)

Eventually we move on to

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{II}^{4}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}^{\prime}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}_{M_{0}}^{-1\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{0}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}})\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\underbrace{\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}^{\prime}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}_{M_{0}}^{-1\prime}\right\|^{2}\left\|T^{-1}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{2}}^{0}\right\|^{2}\left\|(T^{-1}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{2}}^{0\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{2}}^{0})^{+}\right\|}_{=O_{p}(N^{-1})}

\displaystyle+T^{-\tau}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}^{\prime}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}_{M_{0}}^{-1\prime}\right\|^{2}\left\|T^{-1}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{1}}^{0}\right\|^{2}\left\|(T^{-(1+\tau)}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}})^{+}\right\|

\displaystyle+2\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}^{\prime}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}_{M_{0}}^{-1\prime}\right\|^{2}\left\|T^{-1}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{1}}^{0}\right\|\left\|(T^{-(1+\tau)}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}})^{+}\right\|\left\|T^{-(1+\tau)}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0}\right\|

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}^{\prime}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}_{M_{0}}^{-1\prime}\right\|^{2}\left\|T^{-1/2}\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{M_{0}}^{0\prime}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}^{0}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{G}_{T}^{-1}\right\|^{2}\left\|(\mathbf{G}_{T}^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}^{0\prime}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}\widehat{\mathbf{F}}^{0}_{M_{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbf{G}_{T}^{-1})^{+}-\mathbf{S}^{+}\right\|}_{O_{p}(N^{-3/2}T^{-\tau/2})+O_{p}(N^{-1}T^{-1/2})}

\displaystyle=O_{p}(N^{-1}),

(A.77)

which is decided by the first term, which contributes to the order of
O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})
(note that provided that we assume
TN^{-1}=O(1)
this term itself can also be seen as
O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})
). Thus, overall we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}-\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}\right\|

\displaystyle=O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2}).

(A.78)

Now, we put the results together in a fashion similar to
De Vos and Stauskas, (2024)
. In particular, because of (
A.56
), we can easily demonstrate that
\left\|\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}\right\|=O_{p}(1)
(see e.g. the analysis of CCEP denominator in e.g.
Stauskas, (2023)
, where even more general factors are considered). Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M_{0})

\displaystyle=\ln\mathrm{det}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}+\left[\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}-\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}\right]\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{M_{0}}^{-1}\right)+k(g-m)p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\ln\mathrm{det}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}+O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})\right)+k(g-m)p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\ln\mathrm{det}(\mathbf{I}_{k})+O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})+k(g-m)p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})+k(g-m)p_{N,T},

(A.79)

where the approximation comes from p. 119 in
Paulsen, (1984)
:

\displaystyle\ln\mathrm{det}(\mathbf{I}+O_{p}(n^{-1}))=O_{p}(n^{-1})

for some integer
n
. However, given that
p_{N,T}^{-1}O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})=o(1)
, then we have that

\displaystyle p_{N,T}^{-1}(\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M_{0}))=k(g-m)+o_{p}(1)

(A.80)

and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M_{0})<0\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(p_{N,T}^{-1}(\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{DVS}(M_{0}))<0\right)\to 0,

(A.81)

and so the overspecification risk is eliminated.

Homogeneous
\boldsymbol{\beta}

Note that IC of MW is based on the residuals from the first stage CCE regression under the full set of available
k+1
CAs (note that
|M|=k+1
now). In this case, we have
\widehat{\mathbf{F}}=[\overline{\mathbf{y}},\overline{\mathbf{X}}]=\mathbf{F}\overline{\mathbf{C}}+\overline{\mathbf{U}}
, where now
\overline{\mathbf{C}}
is a loading matrix with rank of
m
even when
N\to\infty
. In particular, let us assume homogeneous
\boldsymbol{\beta}
. Then

\displaystyle\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}=\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{X}_{i}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=-\mathbf{X}_{i}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})+\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}.

(A.82)

We then obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0})

\displaystyle=\ln\left[1+\left(\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}\right)\left(\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}\right)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle+(g-m)\cdot p_{N,T}

\displaystyle=\ln\left[1+T^{\tau}\left(\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}\right)\left(\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}\right)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle+(g-m)\cdot p_{N,T}

(A.83)

where, by inserting (
A.82
) we further analyze

\displaystyle\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})^{\prime}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\mathbf{X}_{i}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})^{\prime}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\mathbf{X}_{i}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}).

(A.84)

Clearly,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})^{\prime}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\mathbf{X}_{i}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})\right\|

(A.85)

\displaystyle\leq(NT)^{-1}\underbrace{\left\|\sqrt{NT}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})\right\|^{2}}_{O_{p}(1)}\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\mathbf{X}_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}O_{p}((NT)^{-1})=o_{p}(1),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM\subset M_{0}\\
o_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})=o_{p}(1),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM_{0}\subset M,\end{cases}

(A.86)

because the rate of
\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\mathbf{X}_{i}\right\|
was already derived for the IC of DVS under both cases (either
O_{p}(1)
or vanishing), and its behavior will not change when we use the full set of averages
\widehat{\mathbf{F}}=[\overline{\mathbf{y}},\overline{\mathbf{X}}]
. Also,
\left\|\sqrt{NT}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})\right\|=O_{p}(1)
under general factors (see
Westerlund,, 2018
), and thus the
\sqrt{NT}
-consistency will hold under our assumptions, as well. Similarly,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})^{\prime}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(NT)^{-1/2}\left\|\sqrt{NT}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})\right\|

\displaystyle\times\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})\right\|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}O_{p}((NT)^{-1/2}),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM\subset M_{0}\\
o_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})=o_{p}(1),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM_{0}\subset M,\end{cases}

(A.87)

because asymptotically
\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}
behaves identically to
\mathbf{X}_{i}=\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}+\mathbf{V}_{i}
, and thus the dominating component will have the same rate. Eventually, by following the same analysis of
\mathbf{I},\mathbf{II}
and
\mathbf{III}
in (
A.11
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})\right\|=\begin{cases}O_{p}(1),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM\subset M_{0}\\
O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})=o_{p}(1),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM_{0}\subset M.\end{cases}

(A.88)

In fact, we can demonstrate that in analogy to the analysis of (
A.11
), we can decompose and simplify the term:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})+o_{p}(T^{-\tau})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})^{\prime}\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M^{C}}}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i})+o_{p}(T^{-\tau})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}^{\prime}\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M^{C}}}\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+o_{p}(T^{-\tau})

\displaystyle=c_{NT}+o_{p}(T^{-\tau})

(A.89)

for a strictly positive
c_{NT}
with
c_{NT}\to c>0
under
M\subset M_{0}
.

To complete, we will assume that
\tau\in(0,0.5)
, in order to isolate
o_{p}(T^{-\tau})
terms. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}=c_{NT}+o_{p}(T^{-\tau}),

(A.90)

and, because we can show that
b_{NT}:=\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}
converges to a positive scalar quantity, we can analogously show that

\displaystyle(\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0}))/\ln(T)=\ln(1+T^{\tau}(c_{NT}\times b_{NT}^{-1}+o_{p}(T^{-\tau}))/\ln(T)+o(1)

\displaystyle=\ln(1+T^{\tau}c_{NT}\times b_{NT}^{-1})/\ln(T)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\ln(T^{\tau}c\times b_{NT}^{-1})/\ln(T)+\left(\ln(1+T^{\tau}c_{NT}\times b_{NT}^{-1})-\ln(T^{\tau}c_{NT}\times b_{NT}^{-1}))\right)/\ln(T)

\displaystyle+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\ln(T^{\tau}c_{NT}\times b_{NT}^{-1})/\ln(T)+\frac{1}{\ln(T)}\ln\left(1+\frac{b_{NT}}{cT^{\tau}}\right)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\tau+\ln(c_{NT}\times b_{NT}^{-1})/\ln(T)+\frac{1}{\ln(T)}\left(\frac{b_{NT}}{c_{NT}T^{\tau}}+O(T^{-2\tau})\right)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle\to_{p}\tau>0

(A.91)

as
(N,T)\to\infty
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0})<0)=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0})}{\ln(T)}<0\right)\to 0

(A.92)

as
(N,T)\to\infty
. Under
M_{0}\subset M
, the proof goes exactly the same as in
Margaritella and Westerlund, (2023)
,and so given the rates declared above and the fact that
p_{N,T}^{-1}O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})=o(1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle p_{N,T}^{-1}(\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0}))=(g-m)+o_{p}(1)

(A.93)

and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0})<0\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(p_{N,T}^{-1}(\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0}))<0)\right)\to 0,

(A.94)

and the overspecification risk is eliminated.

Heterogeneous
\boldsymbol{\beta}

If the parameter vector is heterogeneous, e.g.
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{i}=\boldsymbol{\beta}+\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{i}
, where
\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{i}
is IID and mean-zero, then

\displaystyle\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}=\mathbf{y}_{i}-\mathbf{X}_{i}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{X}_{i}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})+\mathbf{X}_{i}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{i}+\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{X}_{i}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})+\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{i}+\mathbf{V}_{i}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{i}+\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{X}_{i}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})+\mathbf{F}(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{i}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i})+\mathbf{V}_{i}\boldsymbol{\upsilon}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{X}_{i}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})+\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*},

(A.95)

with the obvious definitions of
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}
and
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*}
. Note that now
\left\|\sqrt{N}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})\right\|=O_{p}(1)
under general factors (see e.g.
Stauskas,, 2023
), which is a considerably lower rate. This means that now, for example,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})^{\prime}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\mathbf{X}_{i}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq N^{-1}\underbrace{\left\|\sqrt{N}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})\right\|^{2}}_{O_{p}(1)}\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\mathbf{X}_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}O_{p}(N^{-1}),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM\subset M_{0}\\
o_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})=o_{p}(1),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM_{0}\subset M,\end{cases}

(A.96)

and other terms will behave similarly:

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})^{\prime}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq N^{-1/2}\left\|\sqrt{N}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})\right\|\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*})\right\|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}O_{p}(N^{-1/2}),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM\subset M_{0}\\
o_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})=o_{p}(1),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM_{0}\subset M.\end{cases}

(A.97)

Finally,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*})\right\|=\begin{cases}O_{p}(1),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM\subset M_{0}\\
O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})=o_{p}(1),\hskip 2.84526pt\text{under}\hskip 2.84526ptM_{0}\subset M,\end{cases}

(A.98)

and, in particular, under
M\subset M_{0}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*})^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M_{0}}})(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*})+o_{p}(T^{-\tau})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*})^{\prime}\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M^{C}}}(\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}^{*})+o_{p}(T^{-\tau})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*\prime}\mathbf{F}^{\prime}\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M}}\mathbf{F}\overline{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}\mathbf{q}_{M^{C}}}\mathbf{F}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}^{*}+o_{p}(T^{-\tau})

\displaystyle=d_{NT}+o_{p}(T^{-\tau})

(A.99)

with
d_{NT}\to_{p}d>0
in analogy to the analysis in (
A.89
). Now, we need to assume
T^{\tau}N^{-1/2}\to 0
in addition to
\tau\in(0,0.5)
to isolate the
o_{p}(T^{-\tau})
terms. Then in the under-specification case, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{NT^{1+\tau}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M}}-\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}})\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}=d_{NT}+o_{p}(T^{-\tau}),

(A.100)

and, since again we can show that
e_{NT}:=\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{M}_{\widehat{\mathbf{F}}_{M_{0}}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{i}
converges to a positive scalar quantity, we can demonstrate that

\displaystyle(\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0}))/\ln(T)=\ln(1+T^{\tau}(d_{NT}\times e_{NT}^{-1}+o_{p}(T^{-\tau}))/\ln(T)+o(1)

\displaystyle=\ln(1+T^{\tau}d_{NT}\times e_{NT}^{-1})/\ln(T)+o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle\to_{p}\tau>0

(A.101)

as
(N,T)\to\infty
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0})<0)=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0})}{\ln(T)}<0\right)\to 0

(A.102)

as
(N,T)\to\infty
. Under
M_{0}\subset M
, the proof once again the same as in
Margaritella and Westerlund, (2023)
,and so given the rates declared above and the fact that
p_{N,T}^{-1}O_{p}(C_{N,T}^{-2})=o(1)
, we get

\displaystyle p_{N,T}^{-1}(\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0}))=(g-m)+o_{p}(1)

(A.103)

and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0})<0\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(p_{N,T}^{-1}(\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M)-\mathrm{IC}^{MW}(M_{0}))<0)\right)\to 0,

(A.104)

as expected. Note that the restrictions on
\tau
discussed here are just sufficient conditions, since we do not see such need in the simulationd exercises.

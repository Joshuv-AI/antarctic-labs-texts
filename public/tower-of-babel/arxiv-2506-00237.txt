A New State of Matter between the Hadronic Phase and the Quark-Gluon Plasma ? Preprint: YITP-25-80, N3AS-25-009, RIKEN-iTHEMS-Report-25
Yuki Fujimoto, Kenji Fukushima, Yoshimasa Hidaka, Larry McLerran

Abstract
Lattice-QCD simulations and theoretical arguments hint at the existence of an intermediate phase of strongly interacting matter between a confined hadron gas and a deconfined Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP). We qualitatively and semi-quantitatively explore and differentiate the phase structures in the temperature window from the QCD pseudo-critical temperature T c ≃ 160 ​ MeV T_{\mathrm{c}}\simeq 160\;\text{MeV} to the pure-gluonic deconfinement temperature T d ≃ 285 ​ MeV T_{\mathrm{d}}\simeq 285\;\text{MeV} . We propose a three-regime picture using a hadron resonance gas (HRG) description augmented with the glueball spectrum based on the analysis of a large number, N c N_{\mathrm{c}} , of colors. We estimate the entropy density from our model to confirm that the lattice-QCD data are bracketed with three regimes, i.e., a hadron gas, a QGP, and a new phase for T c ≲ T ≲ T d T_{\mathrm{c}}\lesssim T\lesssim T_{\mathrm{d}} . In this new phase that we name a Spaghetti of Quarks with Glueballs (SQGB), thermal degrees of freedom of quarks are deconfined, yet gluons remain confined in glueballs. Since the Hagedorn temperature, T H ∼ 285 ​ MeV T_{\mathrm{H}}\sim 285\;\text{MeV} , is universal in the meson and the glueball sectors, in the infinite N c N_{\mathrm{c}} limit, the phase diagram in the plane of the baryon chemical potential and the temperature is reduced to the one with the confined and deconfined phases and Quarkyonic Matter at high density. At large but finite N c N_{\mathrm{c}} , an SQGB window may open between these phases. We point out that the SQGB has interesting similarities with Quarkyonic Matter and that this matter in the large N c N_{\mathrm{c}} limit is confined as measured by the interaction between heavy quarks, but behaves in other respects like a quasi-free gas of quarks. As a result of the extrapolation to N c = 3 N_{\mathrm{c}}=3 , we present a revised phase diagram with the SQGB phase bounded by thermal crossovers. Finally, we give a quantitative analysis of chiral symmetry restoration in the SQGB phase.



I
Introduction

It is a longstanding and yet unsolved mystery how to characterize confinement and deconfinement of quarks and gluons at low and high temperatures in quantum chromodynamics (QCD). One may think that the Polyakov loop in the fundamental representation is an approximate order parameter for deconfinement, and center symmetry is spontaneously broken at high enough temperature
Svetitsky 1986
. However, the fundamental Polyakov loop can only control the excitation of quarks that belong to the fundamental representation of color. The adjoint representation is not faithful, and gluons are insensitive to the center elements. Therefore, the Polyakov loop in the adjoint representation takes a non-vanishing expectation value regardless of the realization of center symmetry. In principle, the nature of the confinement-deconfinement transition may qualitatively be different for quarks and gluons, exhibiting distinct transition temperatures. It is a folklore that hadrons and glueballs as bound states of quarks and gluons, respectively, must melt simultaneously.

Our present work is inspired by a recent speculative scenario proposed by Cohen and Glozman
Cohen and Glozman 2024a
. We would stress that our interpretation is further pushed toward a nontrivial direction at the conceptual level along the lines of Quarkyonic Matter
McLerran and Pisarski 2007
;
Andronic et al. 2010
;
Fujimoto et al. 2024
; see Ref.
Fukushima and Sasaki 2013
for a review including Quarkyonic Matter. We will demonstrate that the lattice-QCD data imply the existence of three regimes across thermal crossovers in which distinct but dual thermal degrees of freedom are matched. The color deconfinement, in contrast, is physically realized as a consequence of the Debye screening. From this novel point of view, we will revise the QCD phase diagram.

In the conventional arguments of phase classification, in the presence of dynamical quarks, center symmetry is explicitly broken, and the fundamental Polyakov loop is always finite. There is no rigorous way to describe the confined phase at all. The pseudo-critical temperature is often prescribed, but its exact value has no strict meaning. As a matter of fact, there are a countless number of unsuccessful theoretical attempts to define the confinement order parameter for full QCD, including light quarks
Detar and McLerran 1982
;
Meyer-Ortmanns 1984
;
Weiss 1987
;
Faber et al. 1995
;
Fukushima 2003
;
Ghanbarpour and von Smekal 2022
. So far, no handy measure to define finite-temperature confinement has been found. The lack of an exact order parameter prevents us from a precise characterization of phase structures in a traditional way according to symmetry realization.

Nevertheless, suggestive observations from lattice-QCD simulations have encouraged theorists to conjecture more nontrivial structures between a hadronic phase and a deconfined Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP).
It is a well-known fact that the pure gluonic theory with
N_{\mathrm{c}}=3
, where
N_{\mathrm{c}}
stands for the number of colors, has a first-order phase transition at
T=T_{\mathrm{d}}\simeq 285\;\text{MeV}

Borsanyi et al. 2022

–
160\;\text{MeV}

T_{\mathrm{c}}

We will enumerate several hints to disclose more structures for

Another example is the spectral peak of the heavy quarkonium, which remains even above

There are even more examples including heavy-ion collision phenomenology — strongly-correlated QGP (sQGP) is a common phrase to refer to non-perturbative properties of matter above

From these theoretical, phenomenological, and even experimental discussions, it is evident that a single deconfinement order parameter is not adequate to identify the physical states for

Along these lines, a partially confined/deconfined new phase has also been found in a model with the adjoint Polyakov loop

To take account of non-perturbative nature around

Recently, another support for a new phase has been reported from the spectroscopy study in lattice QCD as emphasized by Cohen and Glozman

The idea of the stringy fluid is naturally understood in the center-vortex picture of confinement. One intuitive view of confinement-deconfinement change arises based on the percolation properties of such configurations with center fluxes

These arguments coherently suggest a new phase regime for

-

II
Summary of Our Results

We shall here summarize our results for the convenience of readers, and the subsequent sections will present the detailed justification and derivation of these results.

II.1
N_{\mathrm{c}}
-counting arguments

The idea of an intermediate phase is inspired by the counting of
N_{\mathrm{c}}
in thermodynamics. At low temperatures, the energy density, the pressure, and the entropy density of a gas of color-confined hadrons are independent of
N_{\mathrm{c}}
, i.e., of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{0})
. A gas of quarks and a gas of gluons give thermodynamics of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
and
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{2})
, respectively, at temperatures high enough to liberate color degrees of freedom. Therefore, as the temperature continuously increases, matter should first go through a region where the energy density is of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{0})
, and then should transit to a partially-deconfined region with the energy density of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
, and should eventually reach a fully-deconfined phase with gluons that lift the energy density to
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{2})
. This is the most natural expectation unless a first-order phase transition discontinuously shifts the energy density at the critical temperature from
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{0})
to
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{2})
. We will see below that this latter case is possible in the large-
N_{\mathrm{c}}
limit of QCD, while the phase diagram at finite
N_{\mathrm{c}}
can accommodate an intermediate phase regime.

We can understand the dynamics of these phases by the nature of meson and glueball interactions. Extensive thermodynamic properties for weakly interacting systems are characterized by the scattering amplitudes multiplied by the thermal phase-space density of particles involved in the scattering. The strength of these interactions may lead us to the criteria of phases based on the large-
N_{\mathrm{c}}
counting
Meyer 2009
. For example, the amplitude for two-meson scatterings is of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{-1})
. If we require this scattering amplitude times the meson density squared to be of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
, the meson density should be of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
. This is the criterion that interactions are of equal strength in
N_{\mathrm{c}}
to the kinetic energy of freely propagating mesons. If the interactions are not strong enough to achieve this, the mesons may be regarded as weakly interacting. In this case, we can characterize these mesonic contributions to extensive thermodynamic quantities by a non-interacting hadron resonance gas. In contrast, the strength of glueball interactions is of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{-2})
, and glueballs are not important in thermodynamics until the glueball density becomes of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{2})
. We note that the interactions between mesons and glueballs are also of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{-2})
and they are not important either until the densities of mesons and glueballs are saturated as
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
and
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{2})
, respectively, so that the amplitude times the densities can reach
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
.

This argument means that there are three regions physically. In one region in the low energy-density world, extensive thermodynamic quantities scale as
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{0})
, which is described by a Hagedorn gas of mesons and that of glueballs. We will later consider the baryon contribution at finite density. In the intermediate phase at higher temperatures where extensive quantities are of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
, the system may be thought of as a gas of quarks and a Hagedorn gas of glueballs. In the region of the highest energy density, the system is fully deconfined to a gas of quarks and gluons. We should note that the contribution of glueballs is suppressed in both low-density and intermediate phases because the glueball mass is as large as
M_{\mathrm{GB}}\simeq 1.65\;\text{GeV}
. We will see that the transition to the highest-density phase with gluons occurs at the deconfinement temperature,
T_{\mathrm{d}}\simeq 285\;\text{MeV}
. Only at this temperature, the density of states of glueballs increases such that glueballs melt, while the Boltzmann suppression factor of glueballs is still tiny. Then, the contribution of gluons becomes relevant in thermodynamics.

II.2
Thermal degrees of freedom and confinement

In the present work, we have computed the contributions to thermodynamics in these various regions. To model a gas of mesons and that of glueballs in such a way that is extendable to large
N_{\mathrm{c}}
, we employ the Hagedorn spectrum of open strings for mesons and that of closed strings for glueballs. We make a brief overview in Sec.
IV.1
to explain the derivation of the string formulae for the Hagedorn spectra. It is clear from the derivation that the Hagedorn temperatures for the open and closed strings should be the same, which is given by

T_{\mathrm{H}}^{2}=\frac{3}{2\pi}\sigma\,,

(1)

where
\sigma
is the string tension. Using
\sqrt{\sigma}\simeq 440\;\text{MeV}
, we have an estimate of
T_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq 300\;\text{MeV}
. The string approximation is justified in the large-
N_{\mathrm{c}}
limit of QCD, and there should appear finite-
N_{\mathrm{c}}
corrections to the deconfinement temperature, i.e.,

T_{\mathrm{d}}/\sqrt{\sigma}\simeq 0.596(4)+0.453(30)/N_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}

Lucini et al. 2004
. Therefore,
T_{\mathrm{H}}/T_{\mathrm{d}}\simeq 1.16-0.88/N_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}\simeq 1.06
for
N_{\mathrm{c}}=3
. The direct fit in the lattice numerical simulation indicates a rather smaller value of
T_{\mathrm{H}}/T_{\mathrm{d}}\simeq 1.024(3)

Meyer 2009
. This small deviation between
T_{\mathrm{H}}
and
T_{\mathrm{d}}
is associated with the first-order phase transition
Caselle et al. 2015
. In this work, we simplify the analysis and take the Hagedorn temperature close to
T_{\mathrm{d}}
. More specifically, we assume

T_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq T_{\mathrm{d}}\simeq 285\;\text{MeV}

(2)

throughout this paper with the understanding that this value has uncertainties of
10\%
at least for different
N_{\mathrm{c}}
choices.
For
T_{\mathrm{d}}
, we adopt the value obtained in the recent precision lattice study
Borsanyi et al. 2022
, which is converted to the physical quantities using the scale parameter determined in Ref.
Borsányi et al. 2012
.

We are proposing a revised view of the phase diagram based on the argument of thermal degrees of freedom measured by the entropy density. In Sec.
IV.2
, we shall derive the formula for the entropy density using the Hagedorn spectra. The contribution from the low-lying states, namely, the light Nambu-Goldstone (NG) mesons such as pions and kaons should be handled separately from the sum over the Hagedorn spectra of heavy mesons and glueballs. The non-NG modes are heavy compared to
T_{\mathrm{H}}
, and thus the non-relativistic approximation works well to simplify the analytical treatment of the Hagedorn resonance gas. In numerical comparisons, we will integrate the full relativistic expressions to evaluate the entropy density, while the non-relativistic simplification of the analytical expression is useful for us to deepen the qualitative understanding as discussed in Sec.
IV.2
.

A notable feature is found in the rising behavior of the entropy density in various regions.
The entropy density evaluated by the open-string spectrum corresponding to the meson resonance gas diverges at
T=T_{\mathrm{H}}
. In the strict limit of
N_{\mathrm{c}}\to\infty
, hence, the thermal degrees of freedom are saturated as
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
only at
T=T_{\mathrm{H}}
. For
N_{\mathrm{c}}=3
, however, at a much lower temperature, i.e.,
T_{\mathrm{c}}\simeq 150
–
160\;\text{MeV}
, the open-string (meson) contribution to the entropy density can be comparable to that from an ideal gas of quarks.
If we require chiral spin symmetry, chiral symmetry should be restored there, while the quark masses might still be close to the constituent masses rather than the bare ones.
We note that medium effects can give rise to a thermal mass not breaking chiral symmetry
Bellac 2011
, so that quarks can acquire the thermal masses in the perturbative hot-QCD treatment.

This is indeed consistent with the conventional picture of the QCD phase diagram.
Beyond this point of
T_{\mathrm{c}}
, the system has thermal degrees of freedom of quarks and glueballs and the entropy density is dominated by contributions from an ideal gas of quarks and the closed-string (glueball) spectrum. Interestingly, the entropy density of the closed-string (glueball) gas remains finite even in the limit of
T\nearrow T_{\mathrm{H}}
, although susceptibilities involving higher-order derivatives may diverge.

II.3
Deconfinement due to the screening effects

So far, we have discussed how the thermal degrees of freedom are taken over by quarks and gluons in different regimes as
T
increases. However, it is important to state that the activation of quarks and gluons in thermodynamics does not necessarily mean their deconfinement.
The confinement properties, especially in the intermediate regime, are quite interesting. Confinement is cut off due to the effect of Debye screening. When gluons are tightly bound into glueballs, they should not make a thermal contribution to the Debye screening mass unless
T
is comparable to the glueball mass. This is generated only by quarks then, and in the large-
N_{\mathrm{c}}
limit, the quark contribution should vanish. We can understand this from the standard formula for the Debye screening mass:

M^{2}_{\mathrm{Debye}}=g^{2}\biggl({1\over 3}N_{\mathrm{c}}+{1\over 6}N_{\mathrm{f}}\biggr)T^{2}\,,

(4)

where
N_{\mathrm{f}}
is the number of flavors.
The first term originates from gluon loops and the second from quark loops. In the large-
N_{\mathrm{c}}
limit, the ’t Hooft coupling,
g^{2}N_{\mathrm{c}}
, remains finite. Thus, the first term in the above expression is finite, but if gluons are tightly bound into glueballs, this first term must be dropped. Therefore, in the intermediate phase, the Debye screening mass squared is suppressed as
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{-1})
, and quarks should still be confined, despite the fact that thermal degrees of freedom seem reasonably approximated by an ideal gas of massive quarks.
We also note that a similar
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{-1})
suppression takes place for the effect of finite quark chemical potential
\mu
.
Therefore, even if one goes to large
\mu
, the screening is unaffected by the existence of the quark medium.
This is essentially why
T_{\mathrm{d}}
stays constant at finite
\mu
in the large-
N_{\mathrm{c}}
limit, as will be discussed in the next section.

In this sense, the intermediate regime is quite similar to Quarkyonic Matter. On the QCD phase diagram, Quarkyonic Matter is located at low temperature and finite baryon density. Because the gluon degrees of freedom are not important there, confinement persists until a density scale,
\rho\sim N_{\mathrm{c}}^{3/2}\Lambda^{3}_{\mathrm{QCD}}
. In Quarkyonic Matter, the energy density also scales as
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
. In both Quarkyonic Matter and the intermediate regime of our present interest, one should think of quarks as being joined together by color flux-tubes or color strings. Yet, the density of stringy objects is large enough that these strings are intertwined. One may say that the stringy configurations significantly increase the entropy density. This can be translated into the contribution from the kinetic energy of quarks at the ends of strings, which dominates over the potential energy from stretching the strings. We will refer to this intermediate form of matter generically as a Spaghetti of Quarks with Glueballs or SQGB in short. We will distinguish this matter from Quarkyonic Matter based on chiral properties.

In the high-
T
phase where glueballs have evaporated into gluons, the system fully deconfines color charges, although due to the massive nature of glueballs and gluons near
T_{\mathrm{d}}
, the full deconfinement may occur at somewhat larger temperatures than naively expected. For
N_{\mathrm{c}}=3
, of course, these considerations are only qualitative,
but it remains true that the confinement scale is significantly larger in the intermediate phase than what would be expected from a gas of nearly massless gluons. This presumably explains the non-perturbative nature of the lattice-QCD data at temperatures higher than
T_{\mathrm{c}}
, up to temperatures of order of the Hagedorn temperature,
T_{\mathrm{H}}\sim 285\;\text{MeV}
, or even higher.

II.4
Chiral symmetry

We briefly mention the chiral properties in the SQGB phase, which will be addressed in detail in Sec.
VI
. The main content of that section is that the chiral-symmetry order parameter,
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle\sim\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
, receives finite-
T
contributions that scale with the quark density multiplied by a factor of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{0})
. When the quark density is of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
, the total corrections reduce the expectation value of
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle
and it is quite plausible that in the SQGB phase chiral symmetry would be restored. According to the arguments of Refs.
Glozman et al. 2022
;
Cohen and Glozman 2024a
;
Cohen and Glozman 2024b
, chiral symmetry could be restored in a way with emergent chiral spin symmetry.

Revised Phase Diagrams

How do we understand the relevance of the SQGB phase on the QCD phase diagram in the plane of the baryon chemical potential vs. the temperature? First of all, we should clearly recognize that, in the strict large-
N_{\mathrm{c}}

N_{\mathrm{c}}
diverges, the temperature of the string resonance gas will have to approach

\lim_{N_{\mathrm{c}}\to\infty}~T_{\mathrm{d}}=\lim_{N_{\mathrm{c}}\to\infty}~T_{\mathrm{c}}~\to~T_{\mathrm{H}}\,.

(5)

Another landmark is that the energy densities of Quarkyonic Matter and the SQGB phase share the same scaling of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
. The onset of Quarkyonic Matter at

lies at a baryon chemical potential,
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
, close to the baryon mass,
M_{\mathrm{B}}
. Therefore, in this strict large-
N_{\mathrm{c}}
limit, there is no window of the SQGB on the phase diagram as depicted in Fig.
3
. It is still important to realize that in this infinite-
N_{\mathrm{c}}
phase diagram, while the transition occurs at a single temperature, the entropy density changes between of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{0})
to
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{2})
, so that if one considers properties of matter as a function of the entropy density, they should drastically change.

For a large but finite value of
N_{\mathrm{c}}
, a small window of
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{-1})
opens between the deconfinement temperature
T_{\mathrm{d}}\simeq T_{\mathrm{H}}
(characterized by the Debye screening) and the thermodynamic crossover temperature
T_{\mathrm{c}}
(estimated by the entropy density).
Although thermal degrees of freedom in Quarkyonic Matter and the SQGB seem similar, the SQGB presumably has restored chiral symmetry, while chiral symmetry is weakly broken in Quarkyonic Matter. Although naive arguments would also suggest chiral symmetry restoration in Quarkyonic Matter, interactions on the Fermi surface generate spatially inhomogeneous condensate that locally breaks chiral symmetry

. The curvature of the SQGB transition temperature as a function of

N_{\mathrm{c}}

The slope of the SQGB boundary in the high-density side can be estimated roughly by the condition that the baryon multiplicity
\sim e^{M_{\mathrm{B}}/T_{\mathrm{H}}^{\prime}}
(where
T_{\mathrm{H}}^{\prime}
for baryons may be different from
T_{\mathrm{H}}
) is balanced by the thermal weight
\sim e^{(\mu_{\mathrm{B}}-\mu_{\mathrm{B}})/T}

We would emphasize that our picture does not contradict the preceding modeling efforts; in terms of the HRG model, the repulsive interactions between mesons as considered in Ref.

IV
Hagedorn Spectrum

In this section, we derive the expressions of the Hagedorn spectrum for the closed and open strings, and discuss the behavior of the entropy density for those Hagedorn spectra. These are well-known results, but we recover them here to make this paper self-contained.

IV.1
Derivation of the closed and open string results for the Hagedorn spectra

We follow the arguments found in the standard textbooks; see Ref.
Green et al. 2012
.
For the open string, the energy (mass) spectrum is given by

\alpha^{\prime}M^{2}=N-1\,,

(6)

or in terms of the string tension,
\sigma
, instead of
\alpha^{\prime}
, it is approximately written as

\frac{M^{2}}{2\pi\sigma}\simeq N\,,

(7)

where
N\gg 1
is assumed. Here,
N
is the sum of the string modes, i.e.,

N=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\alpha_{n}^{\dagger}\alpha_{n}\,,

(8)

where
\alpha_{n}
is the annihilation operator of right-moving (or left-moving) string modes with
n
-th quanta. The physical states are prepared with the Fock states with transverse polarizations, so the number of states is the number of partitions of the total energy. How to count this is given by the Hardy-Ramanujan formula (for more details, see Ref.
Green et al. 2012
), and for the case with
b
polarizations, the partition number is found to be

p_{b}(N)\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\biggl(\frac{b}{24}\biggr)^{(b+1)/4}N^{-(b+3)/4}\exp\biggl(2\pi\sqrt{\frac{Nb}{6}}\biggr)\,.

(9)

For our problem of the four-dimensional theory, we use
b=2
(though in the string theory textbook,
D=26
is the standard choice to avoid anomalies without supersymmetry), and so we are interested in

p_{2}(N)\simeq\frac{1}{2^{2}\cdot 3^{3/4}}N^{-5/4}e^{2\pi\sqrt{N/3}}\,.

(10)

In the case of the
closed
strings, corresponding to the glueballs in our problem, one must be careful of the energy spectrum that is given by

\frac{M^{2}}{2\pi\sigma}\simeq 2(N+\tilde{N})\,,

(11)

and in this case,
N
contains both left-moving and right-moving string vibrations, i.e.,

N+\tilde{N}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n\alpha_{n}^{\dagger}\alpha_{n}+n\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{\dagger}\tilde{\alpha}_{n})\,.

(12)

For the strings with zero momentum, the first term and the second term should be the same, that is,
N=\tilde{N}
. This means that the physical states are given by the tensor product of the states with
\alpha_{n}^{\dagger}
and the states with
\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{\dagger}
. So, the number of states is given by
[p_{n}(N)]^{2}
, that is,

p_{\text{closed}}(N)=\frac{1}{2^{4}\cdot 3^{3/2}}N^{-5/2}e^{4\pi\sqrt{N/3}}\,.

(13)

Now, we use the following relations,

M^{2}=8\pi\sigma N\,,\qquad MdM=4\pi\sigma dN\,,

(14)

and
\rho_{\text{closed}}(M)dM=p_{\text{closed}}(N)dN
, the spectrum as a function of the mass,
M
, is finally given by

\rho_{\text{closed}}(M)=\frac{(2\pi\sigma)^{3/2}}{3^{3/2}}M^{-4}e^{M\sqrt{2\pi/3\sigma}}\,.

(15)

From this expression, we can identify the Hagedorn temperature for the closed string as

T_{\text{closed}}=\sqrt{\frac{3\sigma}{2\pi}}\,.

(16)

For convenience, we can also eliminate
\sigma
using
T_{\text{closed}}
, and after all, we arrive at the formula used by Meyer
Meyer 2009
, that is,

\rho_{\text{closed}}(M)=\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{27T_{\text{closed}}}\biggl(\frac{T_{\text{closed}}}{M}\biggr)^{4}e^{M/T_{\text{closed}}}\,.

(17)

Obviously, the closed-string calculation is more non-trivial than the open-string case, and for the open string, we do not have to calculate
[p_{2}(N)]^{2}
but simply
p_{2}(N)
gives the partition number. We should be just careful of the different relation between
M
and
N
as given at the beginning of this subsection. It is a straightforward exercise to repeat the same procedure to find

\rho_{\text{open}}(M)=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{6T_{\text{open}}}\biggl(\frac{T_{\text{open}}}{M}\biggr)^{3/2}e^{M/T_{\text{open}}}\,.

(18)

Here, interestingly, the Hagedorn temperature is the same, i.e.,
T_{\text{open}}=T_{\text{closed}}
, which is denoted by
T_{\mathrm{H}}
in this work.
We note that the result for the open string has been discussed in Ref.
Huang and Weinberg 1970
.
2
2

2

In Ref.
Huang and Weinberg 1970
, the authors apparently had a different exponent for the
M
in the prefactor of the above expression as they did not take into account the relations corresponding to Eq. (
14
).

IV.2
Entropy estimates from the Hagedorn model

The entropy density
s
of the ideal gas with a given mass
M
is

s(M)=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}T}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\,\frac{4k^{4}+3M^{2}k^{2}}{\sqrt{k^{2}+M^{2}}}\left[\lambda\exp\left(\frac{\sqrt{k^{2}+M^{2}}-\mu}{T}\right)+\eta\right]^{-1}\,,

(19)

where
\eta
depends on the statistics:
\eta=1
for the Fermi-Dirac and
\eta=-1
for the Bose-Einstein statistics, respectively.

We can expand the entropy density as an infinite sum of the modified Bessel functions of the second kind,
K_{\nu}
, that is

s(M)=\frac{M^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\eta^{n+1}\lambda^{n}}{n}K_{3}\left(\frac{nM}{T}\right)-\mu\frac{M^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\eta^{n+1}\lambda^{n}}{n}K_{2}\left(\frac{nM}{T}\right)\,,

(20)

where
\lambda=e^{\mu/T}
is the fugacity.
It should be noted that taking only the
n=1
term in the above expression corresponds to the Boltzmann approximation.

The entropy density for the open or closed string is thus given by

s_{\text{o/c}}=\int_{M_{0}}^{\infty}dM\,d_{\sigma}(M)\rho_{\text{open/closed}}(M)s(M)\,,

(21)

where
M_{0}
is the lower bound of the mass and
d_{\sigma}(M)
is the degeneracy of the string polarization at a given
M
.
The mass spectra for the open and closed string,
\rho_{\text{open/closed}}(M)
, can be found in Eq. (
18
) and Eq. (
17
), respectively. We put one-letter subscripts, o and c, for notational brevity, to indicate the open and closed strings, respectively.

Let us make a non-relativistic approximation to compute
s_{\text{o/c}}
in the zero baryon number density limit. In this limit, Eq. (
20
) takes a simple expression as

s(M)={1\over{(2\pi)^{3/2}}}T^{3}(M/T)^{5/2}e^{-M/T}\,.

(22)

Then, we can derive the integral forms of the entropy densities:

s_{\text{o/c}}=d_{\text{o/c}}\kappa_{\text{o/c}}T^{3}\left({T_{\mathrm{H}}\over T}\right)^{5/2}\;\int_{M_{0}}~dM~{1\over T_{\mathrm{H}}}\left({T_{\mathrm{H}}\over M}\right)^{(a_{\text{o/c}}-5/2)}e^{M/T_{\mathrm{H}}-M/T}\,,

(23)

where for the open string we get,

\kappa_{\text{o}}={\sqrt{2\pi}\over 6}\,,\qquad a_{\text{o}}=3/2\,,

(24)

and for the closed string we get,

\kappa_{\text{c}}={{(2\pi)^{3}\over{27}}}\,,\qquad a_{\text{c}}=4\,.

(25)

The lower mass
M_{0}
is determined from the physical conditions;
M_{0}
for the open string is the lowest mass of the non-NG mesons and and
M_{0}
for the closed string is the lightest mass of the non-low-lying glueballs. The degeneracy of the meson states has a factor of
4
for spin and a factor of
N_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}
for flavors. In later calculations, when we compute the contribution to the entropy density, we will separately treat the non-strange and strange mesons, and we will take a flavor degeneracy of 4 for mesons made of up and down quarks, 4 for mesons with strangeness
\pm 1
, and 1 for mesons composed of an
s\bar{s}
pair.

For
s_{\text{o}}
, we can perform the integration over mass and find the analytical result:

s_{\text{o}}=d_{\text{o}}\kappa_{\text{o}}T^{3}\left({T_{\mathrm{H}}\over T}\right)^{3/2}\left({{M_{0}(T_{\mathrm{H}}-T)+T_{\mathrm{H}}T}}\over{(T_{\mathrm{H}}-T)^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-{{M_{0}(T_{\mathrm{H}}-T)}\over{T_{\mathrm{H}}T}}\right)\,.

(26)

This explicitly shows the Hagedorn singularity and the exponential suppression of the amplitude unless
T\sim T_{\mathrm{H}}
.
In the limit of
T\nearrow T_{\mathrm{H}}
, we see that
s_{\text{o}}
diverges.

The expression for the closed string is difficult to bring into a simple analytic form. We note, however, that it is exponentially suppressed by the glueball mass, unless
T
is close to
T_{\mathrm{H}}
. We encounter non-analytic behavior of the entropy density as we approach the Hagedorn temperature, and the first derivative of the entropy density with respect to
T
diverges like
(T_{\mathrm{H}}-T)^{-1/2}
as
T\nearrow T_{\mathrm{H}}
. In this limit of
T\nearrow T_{\mathrm{H}}
, interestingly, we see that
s_{\text{c}}
remains finite:

s_{\text{c}}\mid_{T=T_{\mathrm{H}}}\;=\;2d_{\text{c}}\kappa_{\text{c}}T_{\mathrm{H}}^{3}\sqrt{T_{\mathrm{H}}/M_{0}}\,.

(27)

We note in passing that such a qualitative difference in the behavior of the entropy density in the limit of
T\nearrow T_{\mathrm{H}}
, which depends on the exponent of the prefactor in the Hagedorn spectrum, was pointed out by Cabibbo and Parisi in their general analysis
Cabibbo and Parisi 1975
.

We can also trace such an argument from Eq. (
20
) by resorting to the relation:

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}(M/T_{\mathrm{H}})^{a}\,e^{M/T_{\mathrm{H}}}K_{3}(M/T_{\mathrm{H}})=\begin{cases}0&(a<1/2)\,,\\
\sqrt{\pi/2}&(a=1/2)\,,\\
\infty&(a>1/2)\,.\end{cases}

(28)

The open string and closed string cases correspond to
a=3/2
and
a=-1
, respectively.
The former diverges, while the latter leads to the convergent integral over
M
up to infinity.

V
Supporting Argument Toward the SQGB Phase

We give a supporting argument toward the three-regime scenario
Cohen and Glozman 2024a
(the early incarnation can be found in Ref.
Stoecker et al. 2016
) by showing a semi-quantitative comparison between the entropy density from the lattice QCD
Borsanyi et al. 2014
;
Bazavov et al. 2014
and from the Hagedorn spectrum.
We demonstrate that the Hagedorn temperature of
T_{\mathrm{H}}\sim 285\;\text{MeV}
is inferred, and the temperature range slightly below this Hagedorn temperature is consistent with the system comprising free quarks with glueballs.
Also, we point out that the low-temperature behavior of the entropy density is well captured by the open string spectrum with the low-lying NG mesons singled out.

V.1
Details of models

Here, we describe the details of each contribution in the model: NG mesons, open strings, glueballs, closed strings, and free quarks.

Nambu-Goldstone mesons:

We single out the low-lying pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone (NG) mesons below
M=0.67\,\text{GeV}
, which are
\pi
,
K
, and
\eta
.
We only include the mesonic contribution below
M=0.67\,\text{GeV}
in the HRG spectrum taken from Particle Data Group
Navas et al. 2024
.

Open strings:

For the open string Hagedorn spectrum (
21
), we take
M_{0}=0.67\,\text{GeV}
for the lower bound of the mass spectrum.
This value is the mass of the first non-NG meson,
K_{0}^{\ast}(700)
, in the HRG spectrum, and also corresponds to twice the constituent quark mass of light flavors (
u
and
d
).
In Eq. (
21
), we take the string polarization degeneracy factor
d_{\sigma}
as follows depending on the different mass range:

\displaystyle d_{\sigma}(M)=\begin{cases}16&(2m_{l}\leq M<m_{l}+m_{s})\,,\\
24&(m_{l}+m_{s}\leq M<2m_{s})\,,\\
36&(2m_{s}\leq M<\infty)\,,\end{cases}

(29)

where
m_{l}\simeq 0.33\;\text{GeV}
and
m_{s}\simeq 0.49\;\text{GeV}
are the constituent mass for light quarks and strange quarks, respectively.
These degeneracy factor arises from quarks attached to the end of an open string; quarks attached to the string have
(2N_{\mathrm{f}})^{2}
degrees of freedom, where
2
is for the spin, and
N_{\mathrm{f}}
is for the number of quark flavors.
Below a single strangeness threshold mass value
m_{l}+m_{s}
, quarks attached to a string are both
N_{\mathrm{f}}=2
, which leads to
d_{\sigma}=16
.
Likewise, when
m_{l}+m_{s}\leq M<2m_{s}
, one of the quarks is treated as
N_{\mathrm{f}}=3
, and when
M\geq 2m_{s}
, both quarks are
N_{\mathrm{f}}=3
.

Glueballs:

For the glueball and closed-string sectors, we follow the treatment in Ref.
Meyer 2009
.
We take the low-lying glueball spectrum from Ref.
Athenodorou and Teper 2020
below the two-particle threshold
2M_{\mathrm{GB}}
, where
M_{\mathrm{GB}}\simeq 1.65\,\text{GeV}
is the mass of the lightest scalar glueball.
Above
2M_{\mathrm{GB}}
, we add the closed string Hagedorn spectrum contribution.

Closed strings:

In the expression for the closed string Hagedorn spectrum (
21
), we take for the lower bound of the mass spectrum
M_{0}=2M_{\mathrm{GB}}
.
For the string polarization degeneracy, we take the value
d_{\sigma}=1
.
This is because all the degeneracies in the Hagedorn spectrum are incorporated in the string excitation.

Free quarks:

In Fig.
2
, we bracket the uncertainty of the quark mass.
For light flavors, we vary the mass between their bare quark masses
m_{l}=0
and constituent quark masses
m_{l}\simeq 0.33\;\text{GeV}
.
For strange quark (
s

m_{s}\simeq 0.49\;\text{GeV}
.
Particularly, the entropy density for massless quarks arises as

where the number of light quark degrees of freedom is

The RHS of the equation above amounts to

In Fig.

VI
Chiral Restoration

Since the SQGB phase starts at
T\gtrsim T_{\mathrm{c}}
and chiral symmetry is believed to be restored there, we should address the consistency of our scenario and build a model that allows for chiral symmetry restoration.

In general, even in the case of a hadron gas
Biswas et al. 2022
, the thermal correction to the chiral condensate is to be evaluated by the differentiation of the pressure with respect to the quark mass
m_{q}
:

\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{T}=\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{0}-\frac{\partial p}{\partial m_{q}}\,,

(33)

where
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{0}
represents the vacuum part of the chiral condensate.
The pressure
p(M)
of an ideal meson gas with mass
M
at zero chemical potential is

p(M)=-T\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\ln\left[1-\exp\left({-\sqrt{k^{2}+M^{2}}/T}\right)\right],

(34)

whose derivative with respect to
m_{q}
can be calculated as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial p(M)}{\partial m_{q}}

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{M}\,\frac{M}{m_{q}}\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\frac{k^{2}}{\sqrt{k^{2}+M^{2}}}\frac{1}{e^{\sqrt{k^{2}+M^{2}}/T}-1}

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{M}\,\frac{M}{m_{q}}\frac{TM}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{nM}{T}\right)\,.

(35)

Here,
\sigma_{M}
is a quantity called the sigma term, i.e.,
\sigma_{M}=m_{q}(\partial M/\partial m_{q})
.
For light NG mesons, using the Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner relation
Gell-Mann et al. 1968
,

m_{\pi}^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}=-m_{q}\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{0}
, we obtain:

\sigma_{\pi}\frac{m_{\pi}}{m_{q}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial m_{\pi}^{2}}{\partial m_{q}}=-\frac{\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{0}}{2f_{\pi}^{2}}\,,

(36)

and thus,
\sigma_{\pi}=m_{\pi}/2
.
This NG-meson contribution leads to
Gasser and Leutwyler 1987
;
Gerber and Leutwyler 1989

\frac{\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{T}}{\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{0}}\simeq 1-\frac{1}{8}\frac{T^{2}}{f_{\pi}^{2}}\,,

(37)

where we took the chiral limit (
m_{q}\to 0
) and assumed that there are three pions as the NG mesons.
Since
f_{\pi}^{2}\sim N_{\mathrm{c}}
in the large-
N_{\mathrm{c}}
counting, one may think that Eq. (
37
) implies the critical temperature for vanishing
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{T}
is
T_{\mathrm{chiral}}\sim f_{\pi}\sim\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{c}}}
which diverges for
N_{\mathrm{c}}\to\infty
.
However, this is not the case in QCD with the Hagedorn spectrum
Biswas et al. 2022
.
The heavier hadrons all contribute to the chiral condensate, which reduces
T_{\mathrm{chiral}}
.

Although the sigma term has been evaluated for several hadrons
Copeland et al. 2023
, there is no generic formula of the sigma term for hadron resonances. Here, let us make a simple assumption:

\sigma_{M}\approx n_{l}\bar{\sigma}\,,

(38)

where
\bar{\sigma}
is the effective sigma term per quark,
and
n_{l}
represents the number of light quarks,
e.g.,
n_{l}=2
for mesons made of up and down quarks,
n_{l}=1
for mesons with strangeness
\pm 1
, and
n_{l}=0
for mesons composed of an
s\bar{s}
pair.
We take a distinct value for the average number of
n_{l}
in different mass ranges as in Eq. (
29
):

\displaystyle\overline{n}_{l}=\begin{cases}2&(2m_{l}\leq M<m_{l}+m_{s})\,,\\
\frac{3}{2}&(m_{l}+m_{s}\leq M<2m_{s})\,,\\
\frac{4}{3}&(2m_{s}\leq M<\infty)\,.\end{cases}

(39)

For
\bar{\sigma}
, we use the value,
\bar{\sigma}\simeq 30\;\text{MeV}
, that is inferred from the fit to the lattice-QCD data for simplicity.
To incorporate the uncertainty, we vary
\bar{\sigma}
by a factor two, i.e., we vary it between
\bar{\sigma}\simeq 15
–
60\;\text{MeV}
.

The correction to
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{T}
from an open string can be expressed as

\frac{\partial p_{\text{open string}}}{\partial m_{q}}=\int_{M_{0}}^{\infty}dM\,d_{\sigma}(M)\rho_{\text{open}}(M)\frac{\partial p(M)}{\partial m_{q}}\,,

(40)

where
d_{\sigma}(M)
,
\rho_{\mathrm{open}}(M)
,
\partial p(M)/\partial m_{q}
are given by Eqs. (
29
), (
18
), and (
35
), respectively.
By collecting the contributions from NG mesons and open strings, we can summarize the thermal correction to the chiral condensate as

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{T}}{\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{0}}=1+\frac{m_{q}}{m_{\pi}^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}\left[\frac{\partial p_{\text{open string}}}{\partial m_{q}}+\sum_{i=\pi,K,\eta}g_{i}\frac{\partial p_{i}(M_{i})}{\partial m_{q}}\right]\,,

(41)

where
p_{i}
and
g_{i}
denote the pressure and the spin-flavor degeneracy, respectively, of the
i
-th hadron species with mass
M_{i}
, and we used the Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner relation to replace
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{0}
with
-m_{q}/(m_{\pi}^{2}f_{\pi}^{2})
in the above expression.
In Fig.
9
, we plot the resulting chiral condensate as a function of the temperature.
The sigma terms of the NG mesons are taken to be the same as those in Ref.
Biswas et al. 2022
, and the lattice data are taken from Ref.
Borsanyi et al. 2010
.

This is the demonstration for
N_{\mathrm{c}}=3
that the inclusion of the open string spectrum significantly alters the thermal behavior of the chiral condensate.
We note that this is also consistent with the previous observation that the chiral crossover can be well understood in terms of the HRG model, as argued in Ref.
Biswas et al. 2022
.
We also note that the thermal correction in Eq. (
41
) can be
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{0})
, while the naïve estimate in the chiral perturbation only predicts
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{-1})
correction.
This is because Eq. (
40
) shows the same
N_{\mathrm{c}}
-scaling as in the entropy density in Eq. (
21
), thus inside the SQGB phase, it becomes
\partial p_{\text{open string}}/\partial m_{q}\sim\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}})
, which in total yields
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{c}}^{0})
correction to
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{T}/\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle_{0}
.

.

VII
Conclusions and Further Questions

We have argued that the phases of QCD at finite temperature and density might include a new intermediate phase, which we named the Spaghetti of Quarks with Glueballs, the SQGB for short. This is robust and generic to large
N_{\mathrm{c}}
, although many of our arguments are founded on phenomenological constraints that apply to the realistic
N_{\mathrm{c}}=3
case. There is potentially interesting behavior relevant to phenomenology not only at the chiral restoration temperature,
T_{\mathrm{c}}
, but also at the deconfinement temperature for glueballs, i.e.,
T_{\mathrm{d}}\simeq 285\;\text{MeV}
.
While the lattice-QCD data have been studied for various
N_{\mathrm{c}}
’s without quarks, they are also beneficial for us to gain an insight into the dynamical quark effects near the temperature region where the pure gluonic first-order deconfinement transition is washed out. This is presumably the case for
N_{\mathrm{c}}
, not too far above
N_{\mathrm{c}}=3
.

We have posed many questions with possible answers here, and another worthwhile question is how to characterize the region at finite baryon density properly. Establishing the understanding there is complicated by the fact that the Hagedorn temperature for baryons may not be the same as that for mesons and glueballs
Broniowski and Florkowski 2000
.
This is because of the more complicated string structure of baryons compared to mesons and glueballs. One more, and somehow related, question is how to characterize the region of transition between Quarkyonic Matter and the SQGB phase. In the SQGB, chiral symmetry is likely to be restored, but in Quarkyonic Matter, chiral symmetry is broken by spatially inhomogeneous condensations. It is an intriguing open question whether they are smoothly connected or separated by a sharp phase transition.

The SQGB phase is chirally symmetric but quarks are confined for large
N_{\mathrm{c}}
. This seems contrary to the prejudice that chiral symmetry should be broken in confined systems. The simplest heuristic argument for this is due to Banks and Casher
Casher 1979
;
Banks and Casher 1980
. For a quark-antiquark pair confined by vector-like interactions that do not induce spin flip, chirality must be flipped when a receding quark-antiquark pair changes its momentum due to confinement. Strictly speaking, this is an argument for chiral
U(1)_{\mathrm{A}}
symmetry breaking that is never restored exactly in QCD, and nevertheless, we might expect substantial breaking of
U(1)_{\mathrm{A}}
chiral symmetry when the system is confined. The question is; what is the relation between chiral symmetry breaking and confinement? Might studying the SQGB phase for a wide range of
T
,
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
, and
N_{\mathrm{c}}
resolve some of these issues?

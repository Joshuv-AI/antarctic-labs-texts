Decay of mass for a semilinear heat equation with mixed local-nonlocal operators
Mokhtar Kirane, Ahmad Z. Fino, Alaa Ayoub

Abstract
In this paper, we are concerned with the Cauchy problem for the reaction-diffusion equation ∂ t u + t β ​ ℒ ​ u = − h ⁡ ( t ) ​ u p \partial_{t}u+t^{\beta}\mathcal{L}u=-h(t)u^{p} posed on ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , driven by the mixed local-nonlocal operator ℒ = − Δ + ( − Δ ) α / 2 \mathcal{L}=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2} , α ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \alpha\in(0,2) , and supplemented with a nonnegative integrable initial data, where p > 1 p>1 , β ≥ 0 \beta\geq 0 , and h : ( 0 , ∞ ) → ( 0 , ∞ ) h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty) is a locally integrable function. We study the large time behavior of non-negative solutions and show that the nonlinear term determines the large time asymptotic for p ≤ 1 + α / N ⁡ ( β + 1 ) , p\leq 1+{\alpha}/{N(\beta+1)}, while the classical/anomalous diffusion effects win if p > 1 + α / N ⁡ ( β + 1 ) p>1+{\alpha}/{N(\beta+1)} .

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the mixed local-nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
, where
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
stands for the fractional Laplacian of order
0<\alpha<2
. We study the asymptotic behavior of the solutions of the
following Cauchy problem for the following reaction-diffusion equation driven by both the classical and anomalous diffusion

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+t^{\beta}\mathcal{L}u=-h(t)u^{p},&\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,t>0,\\
\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,&\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
p>1
,
\beta\geq 0
,
h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
,
h\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,\infty)
, and

u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

The space
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the space of all continuous functions tending to zero at infinity.

Large time behavior of solutions has been extensively studied in the literature. Concerning the purely local or nonlocal case
(-\Delta)^{s/2}
,
0<s\leq 2
, the authors in
[
18
]
have proven, for
\beta=0
, and
h\equiv 1
, that the critical exponent for the large time behavior of solutions of (
1
) is
p=1+s/N
. In fact, the authors studied the decay of the mass
\displaystyle M(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x,t)\,dx
of the solutions and proved the following:

\bullet
if
p\leq 1+s/N
, then
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}M(t)=0
;

\bullet
if
p>1+s/N
, then there exists
M_{\infty}\in(0,\infty)
such that
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}M(t)=M_{\infty}>0
.

Recently, the study of qualitative properties of solutions to elliptic/parabolic differential equations driven by the mixed local-nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
,
\alpha\in(0,2)
, has attracted a great amount of attention (see
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
). One of the main reasons for considering this type of operator is that
\mathcal{L}
appears naturally in applied sciences, to study the role of the impact caused by a local and a nonlocal change in a physical phenomenon. These type of operators also have applications in probability. Indeed, they are associated with the superposition of different types of stochastic processes, such as classical random walks and Lévy flights. Essentially, when a particle can follow either process with certain probabilities, the resulting limiting diffusion equation can be described by an operator of the form
\mathcal{L}
. In addition, the mixed operators are applied to model a range of phenomena in science, including the analysis of optimal animal foraging strategies (see, e.g.
[
15
,
16
]
and references therein). Regarding the asymptotic behavior of solutions including local/nonlocal operators
(-\Delta)^{s/2}
,
0<s\leq 2
, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
18
,
27
,
29
,
30
]
.

When the nonlinear term
-u^{p}
is replaced by a power-like source term
u^{p}
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}u=u^{p},&\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,t>0,\\
\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,&\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\end{array}\right.

a full study concerning the global existence, blow-up, blow-up rate, as well as the asymptotic behavior of solutions of (
1
), have been recently studied by
[

,

. Note that, the operator
\mathcal{L}
affects negatively to the scaling property, and the Fujita exponent is given by the fractional Laplacian i.e.

. Namely, they proved that any nonnegative nontrivial solution blows up in finite time if

while there exists a small data global in time solution when

Our goal is to contribute to the theory of large time behavior of solutions by generalizing the work of
[

to the mixed local-nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}
. Our results can be summarized as follows. A unique non-negative solution of the Cauchy problem (

of the solutions

for

(Theorem

tends to zero as

if

(Theorem

), when
\beta=0
, and

, behaves like the problem with purely nonlocal operator

[

Definition 1
.

[

Let

be the Schwartz space of rapidly decaying

functions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}v(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle C_{N,s}\,\,p.v.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle C_{N,s}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy,&\quad\hbox{if}\,\,0<s<1/2,\\

We are rarely going to use the fractional Laplacian operator in the Schwartz space; it can be extended to less regular functions as follows. For

,

\displaystyle L_{s,\varepsilon}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle L_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{0,2s+\varepsilon}(\Omega)&\quad\hbox{if}\,\,0<s<1/2,\\
\\
\displaystyle L_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{1,2s+\varepsilon-1}(\Omega),&\quad\hbox{if}\,\,1/2\leq s<1,\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

,

Lemma 2

{lemma4}

Let

Lemma 3
.

{lemma5}

Let
R>0
,
p>1
,

,

, and

. Then, the following estimate holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\Phi_{R}(x))^{-1/(p-1)}\,\big|\mathcal{L}\Phi_{R}(x)\big|^{p/(p-1)}\,dx\lesssim R^{-\frac{2p}{p-1}+N}+R^{-\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}+N},

(4)

where

Proof.

Let
\tilde{x}=x/R

where we have used the fact that

In order to introduce the notion of the mild solution of our problem, and to prove the main results especially Theorem
2
, we need to give a full review about the heat semigroup and the associated heat kernel of the operator
-\mathcal{L}=\Delta-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
,
\alpha\in(0,2)
(see e.g.
[

]
). The comparison principle is needed as well.

We start by an alternative expression of the fractional Laplacian operator
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
via the Fourier transform

\begin{array}[]{llll}(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}:&H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})&\longrightarrow&L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\\
&\qquad\quad u&\longmapsto&(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}(u)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}(u))\\
\end{array}

where

H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=D\left((-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\right)=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N});\,\,|\xi|^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}(u)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\},\qquad s>0.

As a consequence of the fact that
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
is a positive definite self-adjoint operator on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, is that the operator
-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
, e.g. Yosida
[
36
]
or
[
25
, Theorem 4.9]
, generates a strongly continuous semigroup
T(t):=e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. It holds
T(t)v=P_{\alpha}(t)\ast v
, where
P_{\alpha}
is
the fundamental solution of the fractional diffusion equation
u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u=0
, represented via the Fourier transform by

P_{\alpha}(t)(x):=P_{\alpha}(x,t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{ix.\xi-t|\xi|^{\alpha}}\,d\xi.

(5)

It is well-known that this function, also known as the heat kernel of
-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
, satisfies

P_{\alpha}(1)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad P_{\alpha}(x,t)\geq 0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}P_{\alpha}(x,t)\,dx=1,

(6)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t>0.
Hence, using Young’s inequality for the convolution
and the following self-similar form
P_{\alpha}(x,t)=t^{-N/\alpha}P_{\alpha}(xt^{-1/\alpha},1)
, see e.g.
[
31
]
, we have

\|P_{\alpha}(t)\ast v\|_{q}\;\leq\;Ct^{-\frac{N}{\alpha}(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{q})}\|v\|_{r},

(7)

\|\nabla P_{\alpha}(t)\|_{q}\;\leq\;Ct^{-\frac{N}{\alpha}(1-\frac{1}{q})-\frac{1}{\alpha}},

(8)

for all
v\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and all
1\leq r\leq q\leq\infty,

t>0
.

Using
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subseteq H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, one can define the mixed local-nonlocal operator by

\begin{array}[]{llll}\mathcal{L}:&H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})&\longrightarrow&L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\\
&\qquad\quad u&\longmapsto&\mathcal{L}(u)=-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}(u)\\
\end{array}

which, naturally, implies that the operator
-\mathcal{L}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup of contractions
\{S(t):=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. It holds
S(t)v=E_{\alpha}(t)\ast v
, where
E_{\alpha}(t)=P_{2}(t)\ast P_{\alpha}(t)
is the heat kernel of the operator
-\mathcal{L}
, and
P_{2}(t)
is the fundamental solution of the heat equation
u_{t}-\Delta u=0
, represented by

P_{2}(t)=P_{2}(t,x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{N/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}.

Note that, see
[
35
, Proposition 48.4]
, the function
P_{2}(t)
is the heat kernel of
\Delta
and satisfies the properties (
6
), (

), and (

It is clear that
E_{\alpha}(t)
is the fundamental solution of the diffusion equation

{Property2}

Lemma 6
.

{Taylor}

Let
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and put
\displaystyle M_{g}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x)\,dx
. We have

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|E_{\alpha}(t)\ast g-M_{g}E_{\alpha}(t)\|_{1}=0.

(13)

If, in addition,

, then

\|E_{\alpha}(t)\ast g-M_{g}E_{\alpha}(t)\|_{1}\leq C\min\{t^{-1/2},\,t^{-1/\alpha}\}\|xg(x)\|_{1},\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,t>0.

(14)

Proof.

We adapt the technique used in
[

, Proposition 48.6]
. We first establish (

) by supposing

. Using Taylor’s expansion and Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|E_{\alpha}(t)\ast g-M_{g}E_{\alpha}(t)\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(E_{\alpha}(t,x-y)-E_{\alpha}(t,x)\right)g(y)\,dy\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla E_{\alpha}(t,x-\theta y)yg(y)\,dy\,d\theta\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left\|\nabla E_{\alpha}(t,x-\theta y)\right\|_{1}yg(y)\,dy\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\min\left\{t^{-\frac{1}{2}},\,t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}yg(y)\,dy\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\min\{t^{-1/2},\,t^{-1/\alpha}\}\|xg(x)\|_{1},

where we have use Minkowski’s inequality and (

and pick a sequence

such that

. For each

, we have

\displaystyle\|E_{\alpha}(t)\ast g-M_{g}E_{\alpha}(t)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|E_{\alpha}(t)\ast g-E_{\alpha}(t)\ast g_{j}\|_{1}+\|E_{\alpha}(t)\ast g_{j}-M_{g_{j}}E_{\alpha}(t)\|_{1}+\|M_{g_{j}}E_{\alpha}(t)-M_{g}E_{\alpha}(t)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\|g-g_{j}\|_{1}\|E_{\alpha}(t)\|_{1}+\|E_{\alpha}(t)\ast g_{j}-M_{g_{j}}E_{\alpha}(t)\|_{1}+|M_{g_{j}}-M_{g}|\|E_{\alpha}(t)\|_{1}

By (

and the conclusion follows by letting

.

Before we end up the section, an important lemma will be used to provide the fundamental solution of the diffusion equation

Lemma 7
.

{Property3}

Let us consider the homogeneous system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+t^{\beta}\mathcal{L}u=0,&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},t>0,\\
\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\\
\end{array}\right.

For every
t\geq t_{0}\geq 0
, it follows that

u(t)=E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}-\frac{t_{0}^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}\right)\ast u(t_{0}).

Proof.

Multiply the homogeneous equation by
t^{-\beta}
, we get
t^{-\beta}\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}u=0
. Let
v(x,t):=u(x,t+t_{0})
, then

(t+t_{0})^{-\beta}\partial_{t}v(x,t)+\mathcal{L}v(x,t)=0.

By considering the change of variable

\tau=\frac{(t+t_{0})^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}-\frac{t_{0}^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1},

and denoting

\widetilde{v}(x,\tau)=v(x,t),

we obtain

\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{v}(x,\tau)+\mathcal{L}\widetilde{v}(x,\tau)=0,

which yields to

\widetilde{v}(x,\tau)=E_{\alpha}(\tau)\ast\widetilde{v}(x,0),

i.e.

v(x,t)=E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{(t+t_{0})^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}-\frac{t_{0}^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}\right)\ast v(x,0).

As
v(x,0)=u(x,t_{0})
, we conclude that

u(x,t)=v(x,t-t_{0})=E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}-\frac{t_{0}^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}\right)\ast u(x,t_{0}).

∎

Definition 2

(Mild solution)
.

Let
u_{0}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\beta\geq 0
,
p>1
, and
T>0
. We say that
u\in C([0,T),C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))

is a mild solution of problem (
1
) if
u
satisfies the following integral equation

u(t)=E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}\right)\ast u_{0}(x)-\int_{0}^{t}h(s)E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}-\frac{s^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}\right)\ast|u|^{p-1}u(x,s)\,ds,\quad t\in[0,T).

(15)

More general, for all
0\leq t_{0}\leq t<T
, we have

u(t)=E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}-\frac{t_{0}^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}\right)\ast u(x,t_{0})-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}h(s)E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}-\frac{s^{\beta+1}}{\beta+1}\right)\ast|u|^{p-1}u(x,s)\,ds.

(16)

We refer the reader to
[
10
,
17
]
to get the existence, the uniqueness and the regularity of mild solution of (
1
).

Theorem 1

(Global existence)
.

{T0}

Given
0\leq u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\beta\geq 0
, and
p>1
. Then, problem (
1
) has a unique global mild solution

u\in C([0,\infty),L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap C^{1}((0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap C((0,\infty),H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})).

Lemma 8

(Nonnegativity)
.

{nonnegativity}

Let
T>0
. If
u
is a mild solution of problem (
1
) on
[0,T)
, and
u_{0}\geq 0
, then
u(x,t)\geq 0
for almost everywhere
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and for all
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

Our goal is to prove
u^{-}=0
, where
u=u^{+}-u^{-}
,
u^{+}=\max(u,0)
, and
u^{-}=\max(-u,0)
. Multiplying the first equation of the system (
1
) by
u^{-}
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{t}u^{-}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}t^{\beta}\Delta(u)u^{-}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}t^{\beta}(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}(u)u^{-}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(t)|u|^{p-1}uu^{-}\,dx.

Using the identities
u^{+}\,u^{-}=\Delta(u^{+})u^{-}=0
, and
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}(u^{+})u^{-}\leq 0
almost everywhere, we get

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{-}_{t}u^{-}\,dx\geq-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}t^{\beta}\Delta(u^{-})u^{-}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}t^{\beta}(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}(u^{-})u^{-}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(t)|u|^{p-1}(u^{-})^{2}\,dx.

(17)

Thus

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u^{-})^{2}\,dx\leq t^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta(u^{-})u^{-}\,dx-t^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}(u^{-})u^{-}\,dx\\
-h(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p-1}(u^{-})^{2}\,dx.

(18)

Applying Green’s theorem, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta(u^{-})u^{-}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla(u^{-})|^{2}\,dx,

(19)

and, using the self-adjoint property, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{-}(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u^{-}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[(-\Delta)^{\alpha/4}(u^{-})]^{2}\,dx.

(20)

Inserting (
19
) and (
20
) into (
18
), we infer that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u^{-})^{2}\,dx\leq 0.

(21)

Integrating (
21
) with respect to time, we arrive at

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u^{-}(x,t))^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u^{-}_{0}(x))^{2}\,dx=0,\quad\hbox{for all}\,\,t\geq 0,

(22)

which implies that
u^{-}=0
a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for all
t\in[0,T)
, and hence,
u=u^{+}\geq 0
.
∎

Lemma 9

(The comparison principle)
.

{Comparison}

Let
T>0
, and let
u
and
v
, respectively, be mild solutions of problem (
1
) with initial data
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
, respectively. If
0\leq u_{0}\leq v_{0}
, then
0\leq u(x,t)\leq v(x,t)
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and for all
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

Let
w(x,t)=v(x,t)-u(x,t)
, then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w_{t}=-t^{\beta}\mathcal{L}w-h(t)(v^{p}-u^{p}),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,t>0,\\
w(x,0)=w_{0}(x)\geq 0,&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

Using the following estimation

0\leq v^{p}-u^{p}\leq C(v-u)(v^{p-1}+u^{p-1})\leq C(\|v\|_{\infty}^{p-1}+\|u\|_{\infty}^{p-1})w,

we arrive at

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w_{t}\geq-t^{\beta}\mathcal{L}w-C\,h(t)(\|v\|_{\infty}^{p-1}+\|u\|_{\infty}^{p-1})w,&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,t>0,\\
w(x,0)=w_{0}(x)\geq 0,&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

Applying similar calculations as in the proof of Lemma
8
, we conclude that
w=w^{+}\geq 0
a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for all
t\in[0,T)
, and therefore
v\geq u
. This completes the proof of Lemma
9
.
∎

Remark 1
.

{remark1}

The condition (

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x,t)(\varphi(t))^{1/p}(\varphi(t))^{-1/p}\varphi_{1}(x)(\varphi_{2}(t))^{\ell-1}\left|\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(t)\right|\,dx\,dt

(44)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ell\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p}(x,t)\varphi(t)\,dx\,dt+\ell\,C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi_{1}(x)\varphi_{2}(t)\left|\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(t)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dx\,dt.

Combining (
43
), (
44
) and (
42
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)\varphi_{1}(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(h(t)+(1+\ell)\varepsilon)u^{p}(x,t)\varphi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}t^{\frac{\beta p}{p-1}}(\varphi_{2}(t))^{\ell}(\varphi_{1}(x))^{-1/(p-1)}\left|\mathcal{L}\varphi_{1}(x)\right|^{p/(p-1)}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\quad\ell\,C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi_{1}(x)\varphi_{2}(t)\left|\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(t)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

(45)

Using Lemma
3
, by choosing
R,B\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}t^{\frac{\beta p}{p-1}}(\varphi_{2}(t))^{\ell}\,dt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\varphi_{1}(x))^{-1/(p-1)}\left|\mathcal{L}\varphi_{1}(x)\right|^{p/(p-1)}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,B^{-\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}+N}\,R^{-\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}+N}\int_{\Omega}t^{\frac{\beta p}{p-1}}(\varphi_{2}(t))^{\ell}\,dt.

By the change of variable
\eta=t^{\beta+1}R^{-\alpha}
, and the fact that
\psi\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,B^{-\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}+N}\,R^{-\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+1)(p-1)}+N}\int_{0}^{2}\eta^{\frac{\beta}{(\beta+1)(p-1)}}(\psi(\eta))^{\ell}\,d\eta\leq C\,B^{-\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}+N}\,R^{-\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+1)(p-1)}+N}.

(46)

On the other hand, using the change of variable
y=(BR)^{-1}\,x
, we have

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell\,C_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi_{1}(x)\,dx\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{2}(t)\left|\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(t)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\,(BR)^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\langle y\rangle^{-q_{0}}\,dy\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{2}(t)\left|\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(t)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dt.

By the change of variable
\eta=t^{\beta+1}R^{-\alpha}
, and
q_{0}>N
, we get

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,B^{N}R^{-\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+1)(p-1)}+N}\int_{0}^{2}\eta^{\frac{\beta}{(\beta+1)(p-1)}}\psi(\eta)\left|\psi^{\prime}(\eta)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,d\eta\leq C\,R^{-\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+1)(p-1)}+N}\,B^{N}.

(47)

Using (
46
) and (
47
) into (
45
), we conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)\varphi_{1}(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(h(t)+(1+\ell)\varepsilon)u^{p}(x,t)\varphi(x,t)\,dx\,dt\leq C\left(B^{N-\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}+B^{N}\right)R^{N-\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+1)(p-1)}}.

(48)

As

N-\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+1)(p-1)}\leq 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\leq 1+\frac{\alpha}{N(\beta+1)},

we have two cases to distinguish.

Case 1:

p<1+\alpha/N(\beta+1)
. By choosing
B=1
, letting
R\rightarrow\infty
, and using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem in (
48
), we get

M_{\infty}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)\,dx-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(t)u^{p}(x,t)\,dx\,dt\leq(1+\ell)\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p}(x,t)\,dx\,dt.

(49)

Using (
28
) and the fact that
\displaystyle\inf_{t\geq 0}h(t)>0
, the integral term in the right hand-side in (
49
) is bounded and therefore

M_{\infty}\leq C\,\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrary small, we immediately conclude that
M_{\infty}=0
.

Case 2:

p=1+\alpha/N(\beta+1)
. We estimate
I_{2}
in (
42
) by using Hölder’s inequality instead of
\varepsilon-
Young’s inequality; we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x,t)(\varphi(t))^{1/p}(\varphi(t))^{-1/p}\varphi_{1}(x)(\varphi_{2}(t))^{\ell-1}\left|\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(t)\right|\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ell\left(\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p}(x,t)\varphi(t)\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi_{1}(x)\varphi_{2}(t)\left|\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(t)\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{(p-1)/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\,J_{2}^{(p-1)/p}\left(\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p}(x,t)\varphi(t)\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/p},

where
J_{2}
is defined in (
45
), and
\widetilde{\Omega}=\{t\in[0,\infty);\,R^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta+1}}\leq t\leq(2R^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}\}
is the support of
\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(t)
. Therefore, by using (
43
), we conclude from (
42
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)\varphi_{1}(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(h(t)+\varepsilon)u^{p}(x,t)\varphi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq J_{1}+C\,J_{2}^{(p-1)/p}\left(\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p}(x,t)\varphi(t)\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/p},

(50)

where
J_{1}
is defined in (
45
). Using (
46
) and (
47
) into (
50
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)\varphi_{1}(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(h(t)+\varepsilon)u^{p}(x,t)\varphi(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\,B^{N-\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}\,R^{N-\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+1)(p-1)}}+C\,R^{\frac{N(p-1)}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{(\beta+1)p}}\,B^{\frac{N(p-1)}{p}}\left(\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p}(x,t)\varphi(t)\,dx\,dt\right)^{1/p}.

(51)

Note that, as (
28
) and
\displaystyle\inf_{t\geq 0}h(t)>0
imply
u\in L^{p}((0,\infty),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, we can see that

\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p}(x,t)\varphi(t)\,dx\,dt\longrightarrow 0\quad\hbox{as}\quad R\to\infty.

Therefore, by letting
R\to\infty
in (
51
) and using the dominated convergence theorem, we infer that

M_{\infty}-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p}(x,t)\,dx\,dt\leq C\,B^{N-\frac{\alpha p}{p-1}}.

(52)

At this stage, letting
B\to\infty
and using again
u\in L^{p}((0,\infty),L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, we get

M_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p}(x,t)\,dx\,dt\leq C\,\varepsilon,

which implies that
M_{\infty}=0
since
\varepsilon>0
can be arbitrary small. This completes the proof of Theorem
3
.
\square

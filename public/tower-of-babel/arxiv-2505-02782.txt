Unconditional Schauder frames of exponentials and of uniformly bounded functions in L p L^{p} spaces Thanks: N.L. is supported by ISF Grant No. 1044/21. A.T. is supported by the Foundation for the Advancement of Theoretical Physics and Mathematics “BASIS”
Nir Lev, Anton Tselishchev

Abstract
. It is known that there is no unconditional basis of exponentials in the space L p ​ ( Ω ) L^{p}(\Omega) , p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 , for any set Ω ⊂ ℝ d \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} of finite measure. This is a consequence of a more general result due to Gaposhkin, who proved that the space L p ​ ( Ω ) L^{p}(\Omega) does not admit a seminormalized unconditional basis consisting of uniformly bounded functions. We show that the latter result fails if the word “basis” is replaced with “Schauder frame”. On the other hand we prove that if Ω \Omega has nonempty interior then there are no unconditional Schauder frames of exponentials in the space L p ​ ( Ω ) L^{p}(\Omega) , p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
42A10, 46B15, 46E30

1.
Introduction

1.1.

The most basic fact which lies at the foundation of Fourier analysis is that the system of exponential functions
\{e^{2\pi inx}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, forms an orthogonal basis in the Hilbert space
L^{2}[0,1]
. More generally, for any dimension
d
the system
\{e^{2\pi i\langle n,x\rangle}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, forms an orthogonal basis in
L^{2}
on the unit cube
[0,1]^{d}
.

A natural question arises as to which domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
admit
a countable set of frequencies
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that the system of exponential functions

E(\Lambda)=\{e_{\lambda},\;\lambda\in\Lambda\},\quad e_{\lambda}(x)=e^{2\pi i\langle\lambda,x\rangle},

(1.1)

forms an orthogonal basis in
the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
This question goes back to Fuglede
[
Fug74
]
who famously conjectured that there exists an orthogonal basis of exponentials in
L^{2}(\Omega)
if and only if one can tile
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by translated copies of
\Omega
. Fuglede’s conjecture inspired extensive research over the years
and a number of positive as well as negative results have been obtained,
see e.g.
[
IKT01
]
,
[
KP02
]
,
[
Tao04
]
.
A major recent result states that the conjecture holds for convex domains in all dimensions

[
LM22
]
. In particular, many reasonable domains,
such as a disk or a triangle in the plane, do not admit
an orthogonal basis of exponentials.
We refer the reader to the comprehensive survey
[
Kol24
]
for the history of the problem, an overview of the known results and the relevant references.

1.2.

If a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

does not have an orthogonal basis of exponentials,
then a
Riesz basis
is the next best thing one can look for.
A system of vectors
\{u_{n}\}
in a separable Hilbert space
H

is called a Riesz basis if it can be obtained as the image of an orthonormal basis
under a bounded and invertible linear operator. Riesz bases
share many of the qualities of orthonormal bases. In particular,
every element
x\in H
admits a unique series expansion

x=\sum_{n}c_{n}u_{n}
, and moreover the series converges
unconditionally, i.e. it converges for any rearrangement of its terms
(see
[
You01
, Section 1.8]
).

The problem as to whether the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
admits
a Riesz basis of exponentials
E(\Lambda)
is not well understood, and even for
some natural domains
\Omega
such as a disk or a triangle in the plane the answer
is not known. It is known that there exists a Riesz basis of exponentials for
any finite union of intervals in
\mathbb{R}

[
KN15
]

(see also
[
KN16
]
for a multidimensional version), as well as
for any zonotope in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, namely, any centrally symmetric convex polytope
all of whose faces of all dimensions are also centrally symmetric
[
DL22
]
.
An example of a set
\Omega

such that the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
does not have a Riesz basis of exponentials was constructed only recently in
[
KNO23
]
(in this example,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
is the union of countably many intervals with a single accumulation point).

1.3.

If there is no Riesz basis of exponentials in

L^{2}(\Omega)
(or we do not know whether one exists)
then we can consider
frames
of exponentials.
A system of vectors
\{u_{n}\}
in a separable Hilbert space
H

is called a frame (in the sense of Duffin and Schaeffer
[
DS52
]
)
if there exist positive constants
A,B
such that the inequalities

A\|x\|^{2}\leqslant\sum_{n}|\langle x,u_{n}\rangle|^{2}\leqslant B\|x\|^{2}

(1.2)

hold for every
x\in H
. In this case there exists another frame

\{v_{n}\}\subset H
such that

x=\sum_{n}\langle x,v_{n}\rangle u_{n}

(1.3)

for every
x\in H
,
and the series converges unconditionally
(see
[
You01
, Section 4.7]
).

We note that if

\{u_{n}\}
is a Riesz basis in
H
then it is a frame, but in general
a frame need not be a Riesz basis and the
system
\{v_{n}\}
satisfying (
1.3
)
need not be unique.

It was proved in
[
NOU16
]
that for any set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
of finite measure (bounded or unbounded) there exists a set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
such that the exponential system
E(\Lambda)
forms a frame in the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
. The same is true also in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see

[
NOU16
, Remark 1]
.

1.4.

In the present paper we consider similar problems in
L^{p}
spaces with
p\neq 2
.
We start with a few definitions.

A system
\{u_{n}\}
in a separable Banach space
X
is called an
unconditional (Schauder) basis
if every
x\in X
admits a unique series expansion
x=\sum_{n}c_{n}u_{n}
,
and this series converges unconditionally. The most well-known example of an unconditional basis in the space
L^{p}[0,1]
,
1<p<+\infty
, is the Haar basis
(see
[
Woj91
, II.B.9–10, II.D.13]
).

We say that a system
\{u_{n}\}\subset X
is
seminormalized

if there are positive constants
A,B
such that

A\leqslant\|u_{n}\|\leqslant B
for every
n
.

It is well-known that a system
\{u_{n}\}
in a Hilbert space
H

is a Riesz basis if and only if it is
seminormalized and forms an unconditional basis in
H
.

The classical exponential system with integer frequencies
E(\mathbb{Z})

does not
form an unconditional basis in the space
L^{p}[0,1]
,

p\neq 2
, see
[
Woj91
, Proposition II.D.9]
.
We note, however, that the system can be ordered so as to
form a (not unconditional) Schauder basis in
L^{p}[0,1]
,
1<p<+\infty
,
see
[
Woj91
, II.B.11]
.

Moreover, for any set

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of positive and finite measure,
there does not exist any unconditional basis of exponentials
in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\neq 2
.
This is a consequence of a more general result due to Gaposhkin
[
Gap58
]
,
who proved that the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\neq 2
, does not admit a
seminormalized unconditional basis consisting of uniformly bounded functions.
Recall that a system of functions
\{g_{n}\}\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
is said to be

uniformly bounded
if there is a constant
M
such that
for every
n
we have
|g_{n}(x)|\leqslant M
a.e.

For a certain generalization of Gaposhkin’s result, see
[
AACV19
, Theorem 1.2]
.

We note that the seminormalization condition in this result is essential: otherwise we could simply take the Haar basis and multiply each element by a sufficiently small positive number, in order to get an unconditional basis in
L^{p}[0,1]
,
1<p<+\infty
, consisting of uniformly bounded functions.

1.5.

There is also a generalization of the notion of a frame
to the setting of Banach spaces.
If
X
is a separable Banach space with dual space
X^{*}
, then a system of elements

\{(u_{n},u_{n}^{*})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset X\times X^{*}
is called a
Schauder frame
(or a
quasibasis
) if every
x\in X
admits a series expansion

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}^{*}(x)u_{n}.

(1.4)

If the series (
1.4
)
converges unconditionally for every
x\in X
, then

\{(u_{n},u_{n}^{*})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

is called an
unconditional Schauder frame
.

Note that if
\{u_{n}\}
is an
unconditional basis in
X
, then there exists a unique system
of biorthogonal coefficient functionals

\{u_{n}^{*}\}\subset X^{*}

such that (
1.4
) holds for every
x\in X

(see
[
Woj91
, Corollary II.B.7]
). In this sense, every
unconditional basis is an unconditional Schauder frame.
However, in general the coefficient functionals

\{u_{n}^{*}\}
need not be unique
and they are not necessarily biorthogonal to
\{u_{n}\}
.

Since for any set

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of positive and finite measure
there are no unconditional bases of exponentials
in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\neq 2
,
the question arises as to whether one can find
at least an unconditional Schauder frame of exponentials
in the space.

The answer would be ‘no’ if one could extend Gaposhkin’s result
from the context of unconditional bases to unconditional
Schauder frames, namely, if one could prove
that the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\neq 2
, does not admit a
seminormalized unconditional Schauder frame consisting of uniformly bounded functions.

However, our first theorem shows that
Gaposhkin’s result fails if the word “basis” is replaced
with “Schauder frame”. Moreover, there exists
an unconditional Schauder frame
in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
1<p<+\infty
,
consisting of unimodular functions:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set of positive and finite measure.
Then for any
p>1
there exists an unconditional Schauder frame

\{(g_{n},g_{n}^{*})\}
in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)

such that the functions
g_{n}
are unimodular,
i.e. satisfy the condition
|g_{n}(x)|=1
a.e.

In fact, we will prove two versions of this result. In the first version
the functions
g_{n}
are
real-valued
and unimodular,
i.e. they are
\{-1,+1\}
-valued. In the second version,
the functions
g_{n}
are
smooth
(complex-valued) and unimodular.

We note that Theorem
1.1

cannot hold for
p=1
, since in the space
L^{1}(\Omega)

there are no unconditional Schauder frames, see e.g.
[
BC20
, Section 4.3]
.

1.6.

Theorem
1.1
suggests that the problem of existence of unconditional Schauder frames of exponentials in
L^{p}(\Omega)
is more subtle than the corresponding question for unconditional bases: it cannot be settled in the negative by general considerations and the answer must involve more specific properties of the exponential functions.

In our second result, we provide an almost complete answer to this question.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set of positive and finite measure. Then,

(i)

If
1<p<2
then the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
does not admit
any unconditional Schauder frame of exponentials, i.e. of the form

\{(e_{\lambda},e_{\lambda}^{*})\}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
where
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is any countable set
and
\{e_{\lambda}^{*}\}
are arbitrary elements of the dual
space
(L^{p}(\Omega))^{*}
;

(ii)

The same conclusion holds for
p>2
provided that
\Omega
has nonempty interior.

Let us emphasize that we do not impose any a priori assumptions
on the countable set of frequencies
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

or on the coefficient functionals

e_{\lambda}^{*}\in(L^{p}(\Omega))^{*}
.

We leave open the case where
p>2
and the set
\Omega
has no interior points,
e.g.
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
is a Cantor-type set of positive measure.

1.7.

In view of Theorems
1.1

and
1.2
, the situation is as follows:
the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\neq 2
, does not admit
any unconditional Schauder frame of exponentials
(at least, if
\Omega
has nonempty interior),
but for
p>1
it does admit an unconditional Schauder frame

\{(g_{n},g_{n}^{*})\}

consisting of seminormalized and uniformly bounded
(or even stronger, unimodular) functions
\{g_{n}\}
.

In this connection it is natural to ask whether in
Theorem
1.1
one may
choose the coefficient functionals
\{g_{n}^{*}\}

so that they are also seminormalized
(as elements of the dual space
(L^{p}(\Omega))^{*}
).
To motivate the question, observe for instance that
if
\{u_{n}\}
is a seminormalized unconditional basis in a Banach space
X
,
then the unique biorthogonal system
\{u_{n}^{*}\}
is seminormalized
(see
[
You01
, Section 1.6, Theorem 3]
).

Our next result provides an answer to the above question,
which turns out to depend on
p
.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
1<p\leqslant 2
then the coefficient functionals

\{g_{n}^{*}\}
in Theorem
1.1

can be chosen so that they are seminormalized. To the contrary,
for
p>2
there is no unconditional Schauder frame

\{(g_{n},g_{n}^{*})\}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
such that
the functions
\{g_{n}\}
are uniformly bounded
and both systems
\{g_{n}\}
and
\{g^{*}_{n}\}
are seminormalized.

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proofs of Theorems

1.1
,
1.2

and
1.3
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we review some basic facts that will be used later in the proofs.

2.1.

We start with a basic property of unconditional Schauder frames, that can be proved
using the uniform boundedness principle in the same way
as a similar statement for unconditional Schauder bases (see e.g.
[
AK16
, Proposition 3.1.3]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{(u_{j},u_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an unconditional Schauder frame in
a Banach space
X
. Then there exists a constant
K
such that for any

x\in X
and for any
sequence of scalars
\{\theta_{j}\}
satisfying
|\theta_{j}|\leqslant 1
, we have

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\theta_{j}u_{j}^{*}(x)u_{j}\Big\|\leqslant K\|x\|

(2.1)

and the series in (
2.1
) converges unconditionally.

In this case we say that

\{(u_{j},u_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

is a
K
-unconditional
Schauder frame.

2.2.

The following property of unconditional Schauder frames will also be useful.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{(u_{j},u_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an unconditional Schauder frame in a reflexive Banach space
X
. Then the system
\{(u_{j}^{*},u_{j})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is an unconditional Schauder frame in
X^{*}
.

See
[
LT25
, Section 4.3]
where a short proof of this fact is outlined.

Moreover, by duality it is easy
to show that if

\{(u_{j},u_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

is
K
-unconditional, then also

\{(u_{j}^{*},u_{j})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

is
K
-unconditional.

2.3.

We use
\{r_{n}(t)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
to denote the sequence of Rademacher functions
defined on the interval
[0,1]
by
r_{n}(t)=\operatorname{sign}\sin(2^{n}\pi t)
. The classical Khintchine inequality states that for any
1\leqslant p<+\infty
there exist positive constants
A_{p}
and
B_{p}
such that for any
N
and any sequence of scalars
\{a_{n}\}
we have

A_{p}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|a_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}r_{n}(t)\Big|^{p}dt\Big)^{1/p}\leqslant B_{p}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|a_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(2.2)

(see
[
Woj91
, I.B.8]
). Note that
since the system
\{r_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is orthonormal in
L^{2}[0,1]
, one may take
B_{p}=1
for
p\leqslant 2
, and
A_{p}=1
for
p\geqslant 2
.

The following lemma is a straightforward consequence of Khintchine’s inequality.

Lemma 2.3
.

The series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}r_{n}

converges in
L^{p}[0,1]
,

1\leqslant p<+\infty
,
if and only if
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}<+\infty
. Moreover, in this case
the series converges unconditionally and

A_{p}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}r_{n}\Big\|_{p}\leqslant B_{p}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(2.3)

2.4.

The next lemma formulates the fact that
the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
, has type
p
and cotype
2
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
. There exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that for any finite
number of functions
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{N}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
we have

C_{p}^{-1}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)f_{j}\Big\|_{p}dt\leqslant C_{p}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

(2.4)

A proof of Lemma
2.4
, as well as other information about the notions of type and cotype of a Banach space, can be found e.g. in
[
Woj91
, Chapter III.A]
.

Lastly we establish the following estimate as a consequence of Lemma
2.4
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a
K
-unconditional Schauder frame in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
1<p\leqslant 2
. Then for every
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
we have

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|^{2}\|g_{j}\|^{2}_{p}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant KC_{p}\|f\|_{p}.

(2.5)

The proof is straightforward: due to (
2.1
),
for any
t\in[0,1]
and every
N
we have

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)g_{j}^{*}(f)g_{j}\Big\|_{p}\leqslant K\|f\|_{p},

(2.6)

hence taking
\int_{0}^{1}dt
of (
2.6
),
using (
2.4
) and letting
N\to+\infty
,
we arrive at (
2.5
).

3.
Unconditional Schauder frames of unimodular functions

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
,
i.e. we construct an unconditional Schauder frame

\{(g_{n},g_{n}^{*})\}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
1<p<+\infty
, consisting of
unimodular functions.

We will prove two versions of the result. In the first version
the functions
g_{n}
are
real-valued
and unimodular,
i.e. they are
\{-1,+1\}
-valued. In the second version,
the functions
g_{n}
are
smooth
(complex-valued) and unimodular.

3.1.

We begin with the first version, where
the functions
g_{n}
are
\{-1,+1\}
-valued.
With no loss of generality, we may assume that
\operatorname{mes}(\Omega)=1
. Moreover, since all (separable, non-atomic) probability spaces are isomorphic (see
[
Hal50
, §41]
), we can in fact assume that
\Omega=[0,1]\subset\mathbb{R}
. We therefore need to prove the following assertion.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
p>1
there exist in the space
L^{p}[0,1]
a sequence
\{g_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}

consisting of
\{-1,+1\}
-valued functions, and a sequence
\{g_{j}^{*}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
of elements of the dual space
(L^{p}[0,1])^{*}
, such that any
f\in L^{p}[0,1]
admits a series expansion

f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}g_{j}^{*}(f)g_{j}

(3.1)

which converges unconditionally in
L^{p}[0,1]
.

3.2.

Our proof is partly inspired by the paper
[
FOSZ14
]
.
Following this paper we introduce the notion of an
unconditional approximate Schauder frame.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space with dual space
X^{*}
.
A sequence of elements
\{(u_{n},u_{n}^{*})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset X\times X^{*}
is called an
approximate Schauder frame
for
X
if for any
x\in X
the series

Sx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}^{*}(x)u_{n}

(3.2)

converges in
X
and defines a bounded and invertible linear operator
S:X\to X
. If additionally the series (
3.2
) converges unconditionally for every
x\in X
, then the system

\{(u_{n},u_{n}^{*})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is called an
unconditional approximate Schauder frame
.

It is straightforward to verify that if
\{(u_{n},u_{n}^{*})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an
unconditional approximate Schauder frame for
X
,
then
\{(u_{n},(S^{-1})^{*}u_{n}^{*})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

forms an unconditional Schauder frame for
X

(see
[
FOSZ14
, Lemma 3.1]
).
Therefore, in order to prove
Theorem
3.1
,
it suffices to construct an
unconditional approximate Schauder frame
\{(g_{n},g_{n}^{*})\}

in
L^{p}[0,1]
such that the functions
\{g_{n}\}
are

\{-1,+1\}
-valued.

In order to demonstrate our approach
we will start by proving a slightly weaker statement:
we will construct an
unconditional approximate Schauder frame
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}

in
L^{p}[0,1]
such that the functions
\{g_{j}\}
are real-valued and satisfy the condition

1-\varepsilon\leqslant|g_{j}(x)|\leqslant 1+\varepsilon\quad\text{a.e.},

(3.3)

where
\varepsilon
is any fixed positive number given in advance.
Then we will show how to modify the construction
in order to obtain
\{-1,+1\}
-valued functions
\{g_{j}\}
.

3.3.

We consider the system of Haar functions

\{h_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
normalized in the
L^{p}
norm (we use the convention
h_{0}\equiv 1
)
and denote the biorthogonal system by
\{h_{k}^{*}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
.
If we identify the dual space
(L^{p}[0,1])^{*}
with
L^{q}[0,1]
,
q=p/(p-1)
,
in the usual way, then
the elements
h_{k}^{*}
are the Haar functions normalized in
L^{q}
.
Hence
\|h_{k}^{*}\|=1
for all
k
.

We now fix a sequence of positive numbers
\{\delta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfying

\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}<\delta,

(3.4)

where
\delta=\delta(p)>0
is a small number to be specified later.
Next we choose a sequence of large positive integers
\{N_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and consider a partition of the set
\{1,2,3,\dots\}
into consecutive “blocks”
\{J_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
\#J_{k}=N_{k}
. We also recall that
\{r_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
denotes the sequence of Rademacher functions on the interval
[0,1]
.

Define
g_{0}=h_{0}
,
g_{0}^{*}=h_{0}^{*}
, and

g_{j}=\delta_{k}^{-1}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}+r_{j},\quad g_{j}^{*}=\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*},\quad j\in J_{k}.

(3.5)

We claim that the system
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is an
unconditional approximate Schauder frame for the space
L^{p}[0,1]
provided that
\delta=\delta(p)
is sufficiently small. Moreover, since we have
|r_{j}(x)|=1
a.e., we can clearly choose the numbers
N_{k}
large enough so that the condition (
3.3
) is satisfied, where
\varepsilon>0
is any fixed number given in advance.

We now need to verify that for every
f\in L^{p}[0,1]
the series

Sf=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}g_{j}^{*}(f)g_{j}

(3.6)

converges unconditionally in
L^{p}[0,1]
and defines a bounded and invertible operator
S
on the space
L^{p}[0,1]
.

If we put
J_{0}=\{0\}
and
N_{0}=1
, then by (
3.5
),
(
3.6
) we have, at least formally,

Sf=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}N_{k}^{-1}h_{k}^{*}(f)h_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)r_{j},

(3.7)

so it would suffice to treat each of the two series in (
3.7
) separately.
Since
\{h_{k}\}
forms an unconditional basis in the space
L^{p}[0,1]
, and since

\#J_{k}=N_{k}
, it is easy to verify
(for instance, using
[
AK16
, Propositions 2.4.9 and 3.1.3]
)
that the first series converges unconditionally in
L^{p}[0,1]
to the function
f
.

Next, we claim that also the “error term” series

Ef=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)r_{j}

(3.8)

converges unconditionally in
L^{p}[0,1]
, and moreover,
if
\delta=\delta(p)
is sufficiently small then
the norm of the operator
E
defined by (
3.8
) is smaller than
1
.
This will suffice in order to conclude the proof, since then the operator
S=I+E
is invertible.

Indeed, by Lemma
2.3
the
series (
3.8
) converges unconditionally provided that

\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}^{2}\,|h_{k}^{*}(f)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(3.9)

is finite.
Recalling that
\|h_{k}^{*}\|=1
and using (
3.4
) it follows that
(
3.9
) does not exceed

\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\|f\|_{p}\leqslant\delta\|f\|_{p}.

(3.10)

Hence the
series (
3.8
) converges unconditionally,
and moreover
\|Ef\|_{p}\leqslant B_{p}\delta\|f\|_{p}
.
So by choosing
\delta
such that
B_{p}\delta<1

we can ensure that
the norm of the operator
E
is indeed smaller than
1
.
Thus the system
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
forms an
unconditional approximate Schauder frame in
the space
L^{p}[0,1]
as we had to show.

3.4.

Next, we show how to modify the above construction so as to obtain real-valued
functions
\{g_{j}\}
such that
|g_{j}(x)|=1
a.e., that is,

g_{j}
are
\{-1,+1\}
-valued functions.
To this end, we choose the sequence
\{\delta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
to satisfy
not the condition (
3.4
) but rather the stronger condition

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\delta.

(3.11)

Now, recalling that for each
k
the function
|h_{k}(x)|
is constant on its support,
we may choose
N_{k}
large enough and adjust the parameter
\delta_{k}

so that
\delta_{k}^{-1}N_{k}^{-1/2}|h_{k}(x)|=1
for

x\in\operatorname{supp}(h_{k})
. Then, in the definition
(
3.5
) we modify the function
g_{j}

and choose it as

g_{j}(x)=\delta_{k}^{-1}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}(x)+(1-\chi_{k}(x))r_{j}(x),\quad j\in J_{k},

(3.12)

where
\chi_{k}
denotes the indicator function of
\operatorname{supp}(h_{k})
.
Then
g_{j}
is a real-valued function with
|g_{j}(x)|=1
a.e.
We keep the same coefficient functionals
g_{j}^{*}
as before, namely

g_{j}^{*}=\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*},\quad j\in J_{k}.

(3.13)

We now observe that for each
k
the estimate

\Big\|\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)(1-\chi_{k}(x))c_{j}r_{j}(x)\Big\|_{p}\leqslant B_{p}\delta_{k}\|f\|_{p}

(3.14)

holds for any sequence of scalars
\{c_{j}\}
with

|c_{j}|\leqslant 1
. Indeed, this follows from
Lemma
2.3
using the
fact that
\|h_{k}^{*}\|=1
, and that
multiplication by the function
1-\chi_{k}
does not increase the
L^{p}
norm.

Due to the condition (
3.11
), the estimate (
3.14
)
implies (for instance, using
[
AK16
, Proposition 2.4.9]
)
that the modified “error term” series

(Ef)(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)(1-\chi_{k}(x))r_{j}(x)

(3.15)

converges unconditionally in
L^{p}[0,1]
, and satisfies

\|Ef\|_{p}\leqslant B_{p}\delta\|f\|_{p}
. As before,
this suffices to ensure that the system
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}

forms an unconditional approximate Schauder frame in
L^{p}[0,1]
.
Thus the full statement of
Theorem
3.1
is now proved. ∎

3.5.

Finally we also prove the second version of
Theorem
1.1
.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set of positive and finite measure.
For any
p>1
we will now construct an unconditional Schauder frame

\{(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}^{*})\}
in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)

such that the functions
\varphi_{n}
are
unimodular (complex-valued)
and smooth
,
i.e. each
\varphi_{n}
is obtained as the restriction
to
\Omega
of a unimodular and smooth function in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Indeed, let
\{(g_{n},g_{n}^{*})\}
be the
unconditional Schauder frame constructed above
for the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Since
|g_{n}(x)|=1
a.e.,
we can write
g_{n}(x)=\exp 2\pi iu_{n}(x)

where
u_{n}
is a real-valued function on
\Omega
.
Given
\varepsilon_{n}>0
we can find a real-valued and smooth function

v_{n}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which approximates
u_{n}
in measure on
\Omega
,
that is,

\operatorname{mes}\{x\in\Omega:|v_{n}(x)-u_{n}(x)|>c\varepsilon_{n}\}<(c\varepsilon_{n})^{p},

(3.16)

where
c=c(p,\Omega)>0
is a small constant.
Let
\varphi_{n}(x)=\exp 2\pi iv_{n}(x)
,
then
\varphi_{n}
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

with
|\varphi_{n}(x)|=1
, and (
3.16
) implies that

\|\varphi_{n}-g_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon_{n}
.
If we choose
\varepsilon_{n}
so that

\sum_{n}\varepsilon_{n}\|g^{*}_{n}\|<1
, then
\{(\varphi_{n},g_{n}^{*})\}

forms an unconditional approximate Schauder frame in

L^{p}(\Omega)
. Finally, by an application of

[
FOSZ14
, Lemma 3.1]
we obtain
functionals
\{\varphi^{*}_{n}\}
such that

\{(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}^{*})\}
is
an unconditional Schauder frame in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

3.6.
Remark

One may ask whether there exists an unconditional Schauder frame
in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
consisting of
nonnegative functions
.
The answer is negative
[
PS16
, Theorem 5.5]
, see also
[
JS15
, Section 3]
.
In this connection, we also mention that there do exist
(not unconditional) Schauder frames
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
formed by nonnegative functions

[
PS16
, Corollary 5.4]
.
Moreover, in the spaces
L^{1}(\Omega)
and
L^{2}(\Omega)

there even exists
a Schauder basis consisting of nonnegative functions,
see
[
JS15
]
,
[
FPT21
]
.
The existence of such a basis in
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\neq 1,2
, remains open.

4.
The existence of seminormalized coefficient functionals

Now we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
We will show
that if
1<p\leqslant 2
then the coefficient functionals in
Theorem
1.1

can be chosen so that they are seminormalized.
To the contrary, for
p>2
we will show that if

\{(g_{n},g_{n}^{*})\}

is any unconditional Schauder frame in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, and if
the functions
\{g_{n}\}
are seminormalized in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and uniformly bounded,
then the coefficient functionals
\{g^{*}_{n}\}
cannot be seminormalized.

As in Section
3
, we may assume that
\Omega=[0,1]\subset\mathbb{R}
.

4.1.
The case
1<p\leqslant 2

We start with the first part of the theorem.
Recall that in the construction above the system
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}

defined by (
3.12
), (
3.13
)
forms an unconditional approximate Schauder frame in
L^{p}[0,1]
,
which in turn implies that
\{(g_{j},(S^{-1})^{*}g_{j}^{*})\}

is an unconditional Schauder frame, where
S
is the (bounded, invertible)
operator defined by (
3.6
).
However, note that since
the numbers
\delta_{k}
satisfy (
3.11
), and moreover,
there are arbitrarily large
N_{k}
’s, neither the system

\{g_{j}^{*}\}
nor
\{(S^{-1})^{*}g_{j}^{*}\}
is seminormalized.

We will nevertheless show that if
1<p\leqslant 2
then
these coefficient functionals can be
“corrected” so that they become seminormalized.
This will be done by an application of

[
BC20
, Corollary 2.5]
which can be stated as follows:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
be an
approximate Schauder frame for a Banach space
X
such that
\{g_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
are seminormalized. Suppose that
X
contains a complemented copy of
\ell^{r}
for some
1\leqslant r<+\infty
, and that there exists a positive constant
M
such that

\Big\|\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}g_{j}\Big\|\leqslant M\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{N}|a_{j}|^{r}\Big)^{1/r}

(4.1)

for every
N
and every sequence of scalars
\{a_{j}\}
. Then there exists a seminormalized sequence
\{G_{j}^{*}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\subset X^{*}
such that
\{(g_{j},G_{j}^{*})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is a Schauder frame for
X
. Moreover, if
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is an unconditional approximate Schauder frame, then
\{G_{j}^{*}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
can be chosen so that
\{(g_{j},G_{j}^{*})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
forms an unconditional Schauder frame.

We wish to apply this lemma in the space
X=L^{p}[0,1]
,
1<p\leqslant 2
, with
r=p
and the
unconditional approximate Schauder frame

\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
given by (
3.12
),
(
3.13
).
It is well-known and easy to prove that
L^{p}[0,1]
contains a complemented
subspace isometric to
\ell^{p}
(see e.g.
[
KP62
, Lemma 1]
).
However, an obstacle appears when we try to establish
the estimate (
4.1
), due to the large factors
\delta_{k}^{-1}
which appear
in the definition of
g_{j}
.

To resolve this we shall now modify the sequence

\{\delta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and choose it to be an “almost constant”
sequence. First we fix a small number
\delta=\delta(p)>0

that will be specified later. Then
we choose the parameters
\delta_{k}
and
N_{k}
so that

\delta_{k}^{-1}N_{k}^{-1/2}|h_{k}(x)|=1,\quad x\in\operatorname{supp}(h_{k}),

(4.2)

and additionally,

\tfrac{1}{2}\delta\leqslant\delta_{k}\leqslant\delta.

(4.3)

To see that such a choice of the parameters indeed exists, it suffices
to let
N_{k}
be the smallest positive integer satisfying

\delta^{-1}N_{k}^{-1/2}\|h_{k}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
,
then define

\delta_{k}
by the condition (
4.2
),
and finally check that (
4.3
) holds.

However, note that in this case the condition (
3.11
) is clearly no longer satisfied,
which in turn means that our estimate of the “error term” series
(
3.15
) is no longer valid. Nevertheless we can take advantage of
the assumption that
1<p\leqslant 2
and still obtain the required estimate by
a more careful analysis.

4.1.1.
The estimation of the error term

Let
K_{p}
denote the unconditionality constant of the Haar basis in
L^{p}[0,1]
,
which by definition is the least constant
K_{p}
such that

\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{N}b_{k}h_{k}\Big\|_{p}\leqslant K_{p}\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_{k}h_{k}\Big\|_{p}

(4.4)

holds for any
N
and any two sequences of scalars
\{a_{k}\},\{b_{k}\}

satisfying
|b_{k}|\leqslant|a_{k}|
.
(In fact, it is known that
K_{p}=(p-1)^{-1}
for
1<p\leqslant 2
, see
[
Woj91
, Theorem II.D.13]
.)

We recall that our system
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}
is still given by (
3.12
),
(
3.13
),
but this time the parameters
\delta_{k}
and
N_{k}
satisfy the conditions
(
4.2
) and (
4.3
).
Therefore, the error term which we need to
estimate is still given by (
3.15
), which we decompose as

(Ef)(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)r_{j}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)\chi_{k}(x)r_{j}(x).

(4.5)

The first series in (
4.5
)
converges unconditionally due to Lemma
2.3
,
which also provides the estimate

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)r_{j}\Big\|_{p}\leqslant B_{p}\delta\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|h_{k}^{*}(f)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

(4.6)

and by an application of Lemma
2.5
to the normalized Haar basis we get that this quantity does not exceed
\delta B_{p}K_{p}C_{p}\|f\|_{p}
.

It remains to estimate the second series in (
4.5
) and to establish that it converges unconditionally. In order to do it, we recall that according to the standard enumeration of the Haar functions (see
[
Woj91
, II.B.9]
), for any fixed integer
l\geqslant 0

the supports of the Haar functions
\{h_{k}\}
,
2^{l}\leqslant k<2^{l+1}
, consist of
disjoint dyadic intervals of length
2^{-l}
. Recalling that
\chi_{k}
is the indicator
function of
\operatorname{supp}(h_{k})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{2^{l}\leqslant k<2^{l+1}}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)c_{j}\chi_{k}(x)r_{j}(x)\Big\|_{p}^{p}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{2^{l}\leqslant k<2^{l+1}}\Big\|\chi_{k}(x)\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)c_{j}r_{j}(x)\Big\|_{p}^{p}

(4.8)

for any sequence of scalars
\{c_{j}\}
. Now observe that each

r_{j}
is a
2^{-j+1}
-periodic function, hence
if
k\geqslant 2^{l}
then all the functions
\{r_{j}\}
,
j\in J_{k}
, are
2^{-l}
-periodic.
Since
\chi_{k}
is the indicator function of a dyadic interval of length
2^{-l}
,
the quantity (
4.8
) is thus equal to

\sum_{2^{l}\leqslant k<2^{l+1}}2^{-l}\,\Big\|\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)c_{j}r_{j}(x)\Big\|_{p}^{p}.

(4.9)

Now suppose that
|c_{j}|\leqslant 1
, then by Lemma
2.3

the quantity (
4.9
) does not exceed

B_{p}^{p}\delta^{p}2^{-l}\sum_{2^{l}\leqslant k<2^{l+1}}|h_{k}^{*}(f)|^{p}.

(4.10)

We have therefore shown that for each
l\geqslant 0
and
any scalars
\{c_{j}\}
with
|c_{j}|\leqslant 1
, we have

\Big\|\sum_{2^{l}\leqslant k<2^{l+1}}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)c_{j}\chi_{k}(x)r_{j}(x)\Big\|_{p}\leqslant\delta B_{p}\Big(2^{-l}\sum_{2^{l}\leqslant k<2^{l+1}}|h_{k}^{*}(f)|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

(4.11)

Since
1<p\leqslant 2
, we can estimate the latter quantity by

\delta B_{p}\Big(2^{-l}\sum_{2^{l}\leqslant k<2^{l+1}}|h_{k}^{*}(f)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant\delta B_{p}K_{p}C_{p}2^{-l/2}\|f\|_{p},

(4.12)

where in the last inequality we once again applied Lemma
2.5
to the normalized Haar basis. It remains to use the triangle inequality in
L^{p}
and conclude that
the second series in (
4.5
) converges unconditionally
and satisfies the estimate

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}\delta_{k}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}^{*}(f)\chi_{k}(x)r_{j}(x)\Big\|_{p}\leqslant(2+\sqrt{2})\delta B_{p}K_{p}C_{p}\|f\|_{p},

(4.13)

where the constant
2+\sqrt{2}
comes from summing the terms

2^{-l/2}
in (
4.12
).

We conclude that if
\delta=\delta(p)>0
is small enough, then
the norm of the operator
E
given by (
4.5
)
is indeed smaller than
1
, hence the
system
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}

defined by (
3.12
), (
3.13
)
(as well as
g_{0}=h_{0}
,
g_{0}^{*}=h_{0}^{*}
)
with parameters
\delta_{k}
and
N_{k}
satisfying
(
4.2
),
(
4.3
)
forms an unconditional approximate Schauder frame for the space
L^{p}[0,1]
.

4.1.2.
An application of Lemma
4.1

It remains now to check that Lemma
4.1

(with
r=p
) is applicable to the system
\{(g_{j},g^{*}_{j})\}
.
That is, we need to show that for arbitrary scalars

\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that only finitely
many of them are nonzero, the inequality

\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}a_{j}g_{j}\Big\|_{p}\leqslant M\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}|a_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

(4.14)

holds for some constant
M
. By the definition (
3.12
)
of
g_{j}
we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}a_{j}g_{j}\Big\|_{p}=\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}a_{j}(\delta_{k}^{-1}N_{k}^{-1/2}h_{k}+(1-\chi_{k})r_{j})\Big\|_{p}

(4.15)

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\delta_{k}^{-1}N_{k}^{-1/2}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}a_{j}\Big)h_{k}\Big\|_{p}+\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}r_{j}\Big\|_{p}+\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}a_{j}\chi_{k}r_{j}\Big\|_{p}.

(4.16)

The second summand in (
4.16
) does not exceed

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}r_{j}\Big\|_{2}=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|a_{j}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|a_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

(4.17)

The third summand can be estimated in a similar way: it suffices to
observe that the functions
\{\chi_{k}r_{j}\}
,
k\geqslant 1
,
j\in J_{k}
,
are pairwise orthogonal in
L^{2}[0,1]
.
Indeed,
\operatorname{supp}(\chi_{k})
is a dyadic interval of length
2^{-l}
for

2^{l}\leqslant k<2^{l+1}
, and since
j\geqslant k
,
r_{j}
is non-constant on

\operatorname{supp}(\chi_{k})
. It remains to observe that if at least one of two distinct
Rademacher functions
r_{j}
and
r_{j^{\prime}}
is non-constant on a
dyadic interval
I
, then these functions are orthogonal in
L^{2}(I)
.
Hence the third summand in (
4.16
) does not exceed

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}a_{j}\chi_{k}r_{j}\Big\|_{2}\leqslant\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|a_{j}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|a_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

(4.18)

In order to estimate the first summand in (
4.16
)
we will use the fact that for any
N
and for any sequence of scalars

\{c_{k}\}
we have

\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{k}h_{k}\Big\|_{p}\leqslant K_{p}C_{p}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{N}|c_{k}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

(4.19)

Indeed, (
4.19
) can be proved similarly to
Lemma
2.5
: by (
4.4
)
for any
t\in[0,1]
we have

\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{k}h_{k}\Big\|_{p}\leqslant K_{p}\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{N}r_{k}(t)c_{k}h_{k}\Big\|_{p},

(4.20)

so taking
\int_{0}^{1}dt
of (
4.20
) and
applying Lemma
2.4
we arrive at (
4.19
).

As a consequence of (
4.19
),
the first summand in (
4.16
) does not exceed

K_{p}C_{p}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}^{-p}N_{k}^{-p/2}\Big|\sum_{j\in J_{k}}a_{j}\Big|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

(4.21)

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\Big|\sum_{j\in J_{k}}a_{j}\Big|^{p}\leqslant N_{k}^{p-1}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}|a_{j}|^{p}.

(4.22)

Hence (
4.3
) and (
4.22
)
imply that (
4.21
) is not greater than

2\delta^{-1}K_{p}C_{p}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}N_{k}^{(p-2)/2}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}|a_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leqslant 2\delta^{-1}K_{p}C_{p}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in J_{k}}|a_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

(4.23)

where the last inequality is true since we have

(p-2)/2\leqslant 0
and
N_{k}\geqslant 1
. The required estimate (
4.14
)
thus follows.
To conclude, all conditions of Lemma
4.1
have been checked and
the proof of the first part of Theorem
1.3
is therefore
complete. ∎

4.2.
The case
p>2

Now we prove the second part of Theorem
1.3
.
It suffices to prove the following assertion.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is
an unconditional Schauder frame for the space

L^{p}[0,1]
,
p>2
, such that
the functions
\{g_{j}\}
are
seminormalized in
L^{p}[0,1]
and
they are uniformly bounded. Then

\inf_{j}\|g_{j}^{*}\|=0,

(4.24)

and as a consequence, the system

\{g^{*}_{j}\}
cannot be seminormalized.

Our proof is partly inspired by the paper
[
KP62
]
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a constant such that for every
j
we have

|g_{j}(x)|\leqslant M
a.e. With no loss of generality we
may assume that
\|g_{j}\|_{p}=1
. In this case we have

1=\|g_{j}\|_{p}^{p}\leqslant\|g_{j}\|_{\infty}^{p-2}\|g_{j}\|_{2}^{2}\leqslant M^{p-2}\|g_{j}\|_{2}^{2},

(4.25)

hence
\|g_{j}\|_{2}\geqslant M^{(2-p)/2}
.
The functions
\{g_{j}\}
are thus seminormalized in
L^{2}[0,1]

(in fact, they are seminormalized in all
L^{r}
norms,
1\leqslant r\leqslant+\infty
,
but we do not use this).

By Lemma
2.1
there is a constant
K
such that

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)g_{j}^{*}(f)g_{j}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\leqslant\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)g_{j}^{*}(f)g_{j}\Big\|_{p}^{2}\leqslant K^{2}\|f\|_{p}^{2}

(4.26)

holds for every
f\in L^{p}[0,1]
, every
N
and every
t\in[0,1]
.
Since the Rademacher functions

\{r_{j}\}
are orthonormal in the space
L^{2}[0,1]
, then
by taking
\int_{0}^{1}dt
, applying the previously obtained
estimate for
\|g_{j}\|_{2}
, and letting
N\to+\infty
, we obtain

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant M^{(p-2)/2}K\|f\|_{p}.

(4.27)

Suppose to the contrary that (
4.24
) does not hold.
Then there is
\delta>0
such that
\|g^{*}_{j}\|\geqslant\delta
for all
j
.
We now invoke Lemma
2.2
, which yields that
the system
\{(g_{j}^{*},g_{j})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
forms an unconditional Schauder
frame for the dual space
(L^{p}[0,1])^{*}=L^{q}[0,1]
, where
q=p/(p-1)
.
Moreover,
\{(g_{j}^{*},g_{j})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is
a
K
-unconditional Schauder frame. In turn, by applying
Lemma
2.5
(note that
1<q<2
)
together with the fact that
\|g^{*}_{j}\|_{q}\geqslant\delta
,
we obtain that for any element
f^{*}\in(L^{p}[0,1])^{*}
we have

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|f^{*}(g_{j})|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant\delta^{-1}KC_{q}\|f^{*}\|_{q}.

(4.28)

We can now use a standard duality argument. Since for any
two elements
f\in L^{p}[0,1]
and
f^{*}\in(L^{p}[0,1])^{*}

we have the unconditionally convergent expansions

f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g_{j}^{*}(f)g_{j},\quad f^{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f^{*}(g_{j})g_{j}^{*},

(4.29)

we have the estimate

|f^{*}(f)|=\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f^{*}(g_{j})g_{j}^{*}(f)\Big|\leqslant\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|f^{*}(g_{j})|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(4.30)

If we choose
f^{*}
so that
\|f^{*}\|_{q}=1
,
f^{*}(f)=\|f\|_{p}
,
and use (
4.28
), (
4.30
) then we obtain

\|f\|_{p}\leqslant\delta^{-1}KC_{q}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(4.31)

The two inequalities (
4.27
), (
4.31
)
hold for any
f\in L^{p}[0,1]
. This means that the mapping

Tf=\{g_{j}^{*}(f)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a bounded linear operator
from
L^{p}[0,1]
to
\ell^{2}
, and it is an isomorphism
onto its (closed) image. Hence we get that the space
L^{p}[0,1]

is isomorphic to a closed subspace of
\ell^{2}
, namely, to a Hilbert space, which is clearly not true
(e.g. due to
[
AK16
, Theorem 6.2.14]
).
This contradiction thus completes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Remark

The assumption that
the functions
\{g_{j}\}
are uniformly bounded
was used in the proof above only in order
to conclude that
\inf_{j}\|g_{j}\|_{2}>0
.
Hence Theorem
4.2
remains true
if the uniform boundedness assumption is replaced with
e.g. the weaker assumption that

\sup_{j}\|g_{j}\|_{r}<+\infty
for some
r>p
.

5.
Unconditional Schauder frames of exponentials: the case
1<p<2

In the present section we prove part
(i)
of Theorem
1.2
.

5.1.

Suppose that there exists an unconditional Schauder frame

\{(e_{\lambda},e_{\lambda}^{*})\}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
1<p<2
, for some set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of finite measure. Then the same system also forms an unconditional Schauder frame in
L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})
for any
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
. Hence we may assume with no loss of generality that the set
\Omega
is bounded. Moreover, after rescaling we can also assume that
\operatorname{mes}(\Omega)=1
. So we need to prove the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded measurable set,
\operatorname{mes}(\Omega)=1
. Then
in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
1<p<2
,
there is no unconditional Schauder frame
of the form
\{(e_{\lambda},e_{\lambda}^{*})\}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
where
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a countable set and
\{e_{\lambda}^{*}\}
are arbitrary elements of
(L^{p}(\Omega))^{*}
.

Note that we exclude the case
p=1
since, as we mentioned,
it is known that there are no unconditional Schauder frames (of any form)
in the space
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

The proof below would be simpler if the set of frequencies

\Lambda
was assumed to be uniformly discrete.
In order to establish the result
in the general case, i.e. for an arbitrary countable set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we use an approach from
[
LT25
]
.

5.2.

Suppose to the contrary that
\{(e_{\lambda},e_{\lambda}^{*})\}
,

\lambda\in\Lambda
, forms
an unconditional Schauder frame in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
Our goal is to show that this assumption leads to a contradiction.

Since
\Omega
is a bounded set, there exists
\delta=\delta(\Omega)>0
with the following property: for every
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda|\leqslant\delta
we have
\|e_{\lambda^{\prime}}-e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<1/2
. We fix this number
\delta
and
consider a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into congruent (half-open) cubes

\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
with
\operatorname{diam}(Q_{k})=\delta
.
It induces a partition of
\Lambda
into “blocks”
\Lambda_{k}=\Lambda\cap Q_{k}
.
We note that each block
\Lambda_{k}
may contain arbitrarily many
elements, or may even be infinite.

Now, every
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
has
an unconditionally convergent series expansion

f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}e_{\lambda}^{*}(f)e_{\lambda}.

(5.1)

By Lemma
2.1
there exists a constant
K
(not depending on
f
) such that

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}r_{k}(t)\sum_{\lambda\in{\Lambda_{k}}}|e_{\lambda}^{*}(f)|e_{\lambda}\Big\|_{p}\leqslant K\|f\|_{p}

(5.2)

holds for every
N
and every
t\in[0,1]
. If we now take
\int_{0}^{1}dt
,
apply Lemma
2.4
, and then let
N\to+\infty
, this yields

\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}|e_{\lambda}^{*}(f)|e_{\lambda}\Big\|_{p}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant KC_{p}\|f\|_{p}.

(5.3)

Next, similarly to
[
LT25
, Section 3.1]
, we claim that

\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}|e_{\lambda}^{*}(f)|e_{\lambda}\Big\|_{p}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}|e_{\lambda}^{*}(f)|.

(5.4)

Indeed, fix an arbitrary point
\mu_{k}\in Q_{k}
.
Suppose that
\{a_{\lambda}\}
are nonnegative
scalars such that only finitely many of them are nonzero,
then

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}\Big\|_{p}\geqslant\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}e_{\mu_{k}}\Big\|_{p}-\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}(e_{\mu_{k}}-e_{\lambda})\Big\|_{p}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\qquad=\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}\Big)-\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}(e_{\mu_{k}}-e_{\lambda})\Big\|_{p}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda},

(5.6)

where in (
5.6
) we used the fact that

\|e_{\mu_{k}}\|_{p}=1
(since
\Omega
has measure
1
) and
that

|\mu_{k}-\lambda|\leqslant\delta
and hence

\|e_{\mu_{k}}-e_{\lambda}\|_{p}<1/2
.
If
\Lambda_{k}
happens to be a finite set,
we may apply (
5.5
), (
5.6
)
with
a_{\lambda}=|e_{\lambda}^{*}(f)|
and arrive at (
5.4
).
In the case where
\Lambda_{k}
is an infinite set, one can
establish (
5.4
) by a limiting argument,
using the fact that the series

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}|e_{\lambda}^{*}(f)|e_{\lambda}

converges unconditionally.

Combining (
5.3
), (
5.4
) we obtain the key estimate

\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}|e_{\lambda}^{*}(f)|\Big)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant 2KC_{p}\|f\|_{p}

(5.7)

which holds for every
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
.

5.3.

We will now show that the estimate (
5.7
) implies that
the series (
5.1
) in fact converges unconditionally
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Since there exist functions
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus L^{2}(\Omega)
, this
will lead to the desired contradiction.

We begin by partitioning the family of cubes

\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
into a finite number of
subfamilies
\{Q_{k}\}
,
k\in A_{j}
, where
j=1,2,\dots,n
,
in such a way that

\operatorname{dist}(Q_{k},Q_{l})>5\delta,\quad k,l\in A_{j},\quad k\neq l,

(5.8)

that is, any two cubes of the
same subfamily are separated by distance at least
5\delta
.

Next, we fix a smooth function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, supported in the ball
B_{\delta}

of radius
\delta
centered at the origin, such that its Fourier transform

\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(t)e^{-2\pi i\langle x,t\rangle}dt

(5.9)

satisfies

|\widehat{\varphi}(-x)|\geqslant 1
for
x\in\Omega
.
Such a function
\varphi

exists since
\Omega
is a bounded set.

Now suppose that we fix
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

and let
\{a_{\lambda}\}
be
a sequence of
scalars such that only finitely many of them are nonzero.
Then we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{k\in A_{j}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\widehat{\varphi}(-x)\sum_{k\in A_{j}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(x)\Big|^{2}dx

(5.10)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\sum_{k\in A_{j}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}\varphi(t-\lambda)\Big|^{2}dt,

(5.11)

where the last equality holds due to Plancherel’s theorem.
We now observe that
since
\varphi
is supported in
B_{\delta}
and due to (
5.8
),
the functions
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}\varphi(t-\lambda)
,

k\in A_{j}
, have disjoint supports. Therefore, the integral in (
5.11
) is equal to

\sum_{k\in A_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}\varphi(t-\lambda)\Big|^{2}dt\leqslant\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{k\in A_{j}}\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}|a_{\lambda}|\Big)^{2}.

(5.12)

To summarize, we have shown that
for any
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, and for any sequence of scalars

\{a_{\lambda}\}
with only finitely many nonzero terms, we have

\Big\|\sum_{k\in A_{j}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}a_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant M\Big(\sum_{k\in A_{j}}\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}|a_{\lambda}|\Big)^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

(5.13)

where
M=\|\varphi\|_{2}
is a constant not depending on
the scalars
\{a_{\lambda}\}
.

Since the series (
5.1
) may be represented as

f=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k\in A_{j}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}e_{\lambda}^{*}(f)e_{\lambda},

(5.14)

then by applying (
5.13
) for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
and
using the estimate (
5.7
), it is easy to verify
(for instance, using
[
AK16
, Proposition 2.4.9]
)
that the series (
5.1
) in fact
converges unconditionally in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
and that its sum, which ought to be the same function
f
,
satisfies the estimate
\|f\|_{2}\leqslant 2nMKC_{p}\|f\|_{p}
.
Since
p<2
this gives us a contradiction,
and the first part of
Theorem
1.2
is therefore proved.
∎

6.
Unconditional Schauder frames of exponentials: the case
p>2

In this last section, we prove part
(ii)
of Theorem
1.2
.

6.1.

Suppose that the set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
has an interior point. Then
\Omega
contains a ball
B
. If
\{(e_{\lambda},e_{\lambda}^{*})\}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
is an unconditional Schauder frame for
L^{p}(\Omega)
, then the same system also forms an unconditional Schauder frame in
L^{p}(B)
. Hence we may assume that
\Omega
itself is a ball.
Moreover, after translation and rescaling
we may assume that
\Omega
is
a ball of measure
1
centered at the origin.
So we need to prove the following:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Omega=B_{a}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

be a ball centered at the origin,
\operatorname{mes}(\Omega)=1
.
Then in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p>2
,
there is no unconditional Schauder frame of the form

\{(e_{\lambda},e_{\lambda}^{*})\}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
,
where
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a countable set and
\{e_{\lambda}^{*}\}
are arbitrary elements of
(L^{p}(\Omega))^{*}
.

We recall that
B_{a}
denotes the ball
of radius
a
centered at the origin.

6.2.

As before, let
q=p/(p-1)
. Then
1<q<2
, and due to
Lemma
2.2
the system

\{(e^{*}_{\lambda},e_{\lambda})\}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
, forms
an unconditional Schauder frame in the space
L^{q}(\Omega)
.
Since the action of the exponential

e_{\lambda}
, as an element of
the space
L^{p}(\Omega)=(L^{q}(\Omega))^{*}
,
on a function
g\in L^{q}(\Omega)
is given by

\widehat{g}(-\lambda)
, then every
g\in L^{q}(\Omega)
has an expansion

g=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\widehat{g}(-\lambda)e^{*}_{\lambda}

(6.1)

unconditionally convergent in
L^{q}(\Omega)
.
By Lemma
2.1
, there is a constant
K
such that

\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\theta_{\lambda}\widehat{g}(-\lambda)e^{*}_{\lambda}\Big\|_{q}\leqslant K\|g\|_{q}

(6.2)

holds for any sequence of scalars
\{\theta_{\lambda}\}
with

|\theta_{\lambda}|\leqslant 1
.

6.3.

Let
Q=[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})^{d}

be the half-open unit cube centered at the origin,
and consider a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

into cubes
\{Q+k\}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
It induces a
partition of
\Lambda
into blocks
\Lambda_{k}=\Lambda\cap(Q+k)
,

k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Again we keep in mind that each block
\Lambda_{k}

may contain arbitrarily many
elements, or may even be infinite.

To each
g\in L^{q}(\Omega)
we associate a sequence

\{w_{k}(g)\}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, defined by

w_{k}(g)=\sup\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}\theta_{\lambda}\widehat{g}(-\lambda)e^{*}_{\lambda}\Big\|_{q}

(6.3)

where the supremum is taken over all sequences
of scalars
\{\theta_{\lambda}\}
with

|\theta_{\lambda}|\leqslant 1
. We claim that the estimate

\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w_{k}(g)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant KC_{q}\|g\|_{q}

(6.4)

holds for every
g\in L^{q}(\Omega)
.

Indeed, let
\varepsilon>0
, and
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
choose the scalars

\{\theta_{\lambda}\}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}
, so that

w_{k}(g)\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}\theta_{\lambda}\widehat{g}(-\lambda)e^{*}_{\lambda}\Big\|_{q}.

(6.5)

By (
6.2
), for every
N
and every
t\in[0,1]
we have

\Big\|\sum_{|k|\leqslant N}r_{n_{k}}(t)\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}\theta_{\lambda}\widehat{g}(-\lambda)e^{*}_{\lambda}\Big\|_{q}\leqslant K\|g\|_{q},

(6.6)

where
\{r_{n_{k}}(t)\}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, is some enumeration of the
Rademacher functions.
If we now take
\int_{0}^{1}dt
, apply Lemma
2.4
, use (
6.5
),
let
N\to+\infty
and
\varepsilon\to 0
, we arrive at (
6.4
).

6.4.

For any function
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported in
\Omega=B_{a}
, we have

\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|g\|_{1}\leqslant\|g\|_{q}\leqslant\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}\widehat{g}(-\lambda)e^{*}_{\lambda}\Big\|_{q},

(6.7)

where the second inequality holds since
\operatorname{mes}(\Omega)=1
,
while in the last inequality we used the
unconditionally convergent series
expansion (
6.1
) of the function
g
.

6.5.

Fix any
0<r<a
.
We claim that there is a constant
M=M(d,a,r)
such that

\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}\leqslant M\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w_{k}(f)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(6.8)

holds for every

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subset B_{r}
.
Indeed, fix a smooth function
\varphi

such that

\operatorname{supp}(\varphi)\subset B_{a-r}
and

\widehat{\varphi}(0)=1
.
Define
h_{x}(y)=\widehat{f}(y)\widehat{\varphi}(x-y)
, then
it is easy to see that

h_{x}=\widehat{g}_{x}
where
g_{x}
is
a smooth function supported in
\Omega=B_{a}
.
In particular the estimate (
6.7
) can be
applied to
g=g_{x}
, which yields

|\widehat{f}(x)|=|h_{x}(x)|\leqslant\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}h_{x}(-\lambda)e^{*}_{\lambda}\Big\|_{q}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Big\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}}\widehat{f}(-\lambda)\widehat{\varphi}(x+\lambda)e^{*}_{\lambda}\Big\|_{q}.

(6.9)

Now set

\Phi(x)=\sup_{y\in Q}|\widehat{\varphi}(x+y)|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(6.10)

and note that

|\widehat{\varphi}(x+\lambda)|\leqslant\Phi(x+k)
for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{k}
.
Hence,
using (
6.3
), (
6.9
) we obtain

|\widehat{f}(x)|\leqslant\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w_{k}(f)\cdot\Phi(x+k).

(6.11)

An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality now yields

|\widehat{f}(x)|^{2}\leqslant\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w_{k}(f)^{2}\cdot\Phi(x+k)\Big)\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Phi(x+k)\Big).

(6.12)

Since the function
\Phi
has fast decay,
both
\sup_{x}\sum_{k}\Phi(x+k)
and

\int\Phi(x)dx
do not exceed a certain constant
M
.
Hence taking
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx
of the inequality (
6.12
) yields (
6.8
).

6.6.

Finally let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subset B_{r}
.
Combining (
6.4
) and (
6.8
) yields

\|f\|_{2}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}\leqslant M\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w_{k}(f)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant MKC_{q}\|f\|_{q},

where the first equality holds due to Plancherel’s theorem.
Since
q<2
, the latter inequality leads to the desired contradiction,
and so the second part of
Theorem
1.2
is now proved.
∎

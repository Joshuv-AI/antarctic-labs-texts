Generalized Coulomb-type interaction embedded in a non-inertial cosmic string spacetime in a slow-rotation limit
M. Baradaran, L.M. Nieto, S. Zarrinkamar, [0.7ex] Department of Physics, Faculty of Science, University of Hradec Králové Rokitanského 62, 500 03 Hradec Králové, Czechia, [0.1ex] Departamento de Física Teórica, Atómica y Óptica, Laboratory for Disruptive Interdisciplinary Science (LaDIS), Universidad de Valladolid, 47011 Valladolid, Spain, [0.1ex] Departament of Basic Sciences, Garmsar Branch, Islamic Azad University, Garmsar, Iran

Abstract
Motivated by the great interest in studying quantum and gravitational phenomena in a unified way, scalar bosons are considered in a cosmic string spacetime and in a non-inertial frame, with a generalized Coulomb-type interaction containing both inverse quadratic and inverse cubic corrections. Solutions for this generalized interaction are shown for an arbitrary state in the slow rotation limit in a quasi-exact manner and a discussion is given of the structure of the problem, whose special case appears in the form of a doubly confluent Heun differential equation. The previously known solution for the simplest case of this problem, corresponding to the ordinary Coulomb interaction, is recovered.



Keywords:
cosmic string, non-inertial effect, scalar field, quasi-exact solution, doubly confluent Heun functions.

2
Scalar field in cosmic string spacetime in the presence of a non-intertial term

The cosmic string is usually considered to be cylindrically symmetric and static with the metric

ds^{2}=-dt^{\prime 2}+dr^{\prime 2}+\alpha^{2}r^{\prime 2}d\phi^{\prime 2}+dz^{\prime 2},

(2.1)

where
\alpha=1-4G\mu
(whit
\mu
being the the mass density of the cosmic string) denotes the form of curvature, and, obviously,
\alpha=1
results in the case of flat spacetime. Generalization to a non-inertial frame rotating at constant velocity is possible through the transformation

t^{\prime}=t,\quad r^{\prime}=r,\quad\phi^{\prime}=\phi+\omega t,\quad z^{\prime}=z,

(2.2)

where
\omega
is the angular velocity of the rotating frame. Therefore, the line element is

ds^{2}=-(1-\alpha^{2}r^{2}\omega^{2})dt^{2}+2\alpha^{2}r^{2}\omega\,dt\;d\phi+dr^{2}+\alpha^{2}r^{2}d\phi^{2}+dz^{2},

(2.3)

where
0<r<\frac{1}{\alpha\omega}
to ensure that the particle’s velocity does not exceed the speed of light. To provide an analytical equation with suitable boundary condition, i.e., solutions that vanish at the boundaries, we consider the slow-rotation limit corresponding to
\alpha\omega\ll 1\ (1/\alpha\omega\rightarrow\infty)
.
Considering the gauge
m\rightarrow m+V(r)
, the scalar Klein-Gordon equation has the form
[
2
]

-\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}(g^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g}\partial_{\nu}\psi)+(m+V)^{2}\psi=0,

(2.4)

where
m
is the mass of the particle and
g
denotes the determinant of the metric tensor. We will use the method of separation of variables to find solutions of the form

\psi(t,r,z,\phi)=e^{-i\epsilon t}\ e^{-i\ell\phi}\ e^{ip_{z}z}\ R(r),

(2.5)

where
\ell=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\pm 3,...
,
p_{z}
stands for the momentum components in the
z-
direction and
\epsilon
can be regarded as the energy of the particle. Some simple algebra gives
[
9
]

\left\{\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}-\frac{\ell^{2}/\alpha^{2}}{r^{2}}-(m+V(r))^{2}+(\epsilon+\omega\ell)^{2}-p_{z}^{2}\right\}R(r)=0.

(2.6)

Performing the transformation
R(r)=r^{-1/2}\,u(r)
, we arrive at an equation of Schrödinger type

\left\{\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}-4\ell^{2}}{4\alpha^{2}r^{2}}-(m+V(r))^{2}+(\epsilon+\omega\ell)^{2}-p_{z}^{2}\right\}u(r)=0.

(2.7)

Let us now introduce the following generalized Coulomb-like interaction:

V(r)=\frac{a_{c}}{r}+\frac{a_{r}}{r^{2}}+\frac{a_{q}}{r^{3}},\qquad\text{with}\qquad a_{c}\,,a_{r}\,,a_{q}<0,

(2.8)

which may correspond to the far-field expansion of an axisymmetric mass distribution in cylindrical coordinates.
The terms
a_{r}/r^{2}
and
a_{q}/r^{3}
give post-Newtonian (relativistic) and quantum corrections, respectively.

General solution

Substituting (

\left\{\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+V_{eff}(r)\right\}u_{n}(r)=0,

\displaystyle V_{eff}(r)=

\displaystyle(\omega\ell+\epsilon_{n})^{2}-\left(m^{2}+p_{z}^{2}\right)-\frac{2a_{c}m}{r}-\frac{\alpha^{2}\left(4a_{c}^{2}+8a_{r}m-1\right)+4\ell^{2}}{4\alpha^{2}r^{2}}

(3.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{2(a_{c}a_{r}+a_{q}m)}{r^{3}}-\frac{2a_{c}a_{q}+a_{r}^{2}}{r^{4}}-\frac{2a_{q}a_{r}}{r^{5}}-\frac{a_{q}^{2}}{r^{6}}\,.

To distinguish between the various quantum states, we have added the index
n
to
\epsilon
and the reduced radial function

. Then, to ensure the appropriate asymptotic behavior of the wave function at the origin and at infinity, we propose that the solution have the form

u_{n}(r)=\exp\left[\mathrm{A}r+\frac{\mathrm{B}}{r}+\frac{\Gamma}{r^{2}}+\Delta\ln r\right]\,\phi_{n}(r),

(3.3)

where the new radial function
\phi_{n}(r)
must be a polynomial of order

\mathrm{A}\,,\mathrm{B}\,,\Gamma<0

\Delta>0

3.1

\left\{r^{3}\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\left(2\mathrm{A}\,r^{3}+2\Delta\,r^{2}-2\mathrm{B}\,r-4\Gamma\right)\frac{d}{dr}+\left(\Lambda_{2}\,r^{2}+\Lambda_{1}\,r+\Lambda_{0}\right)\right\}\phi_{n}(r)=0,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}=2\mathrm{A}\Delta-2a_{c}\,m\,,

(3.5a)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}=-2\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}-a_{c}^{2}-2a_{r}\,m+\Delta^{2}-\Delta-\frac{\ell^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\,,

(3.5b)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}=-2\left(2\mathrm{A}\Gamma+a_{c}\,a_{r}+a_{q}\,m+\mathrm{B}(\Delta-1)\right),

(3.5c)

and the parameters
\mathrm{A}\,,\mathrm{B}\,,\Gamma
and

\mathrm{A}=-\sqrt{m^{2}+p_{z}^{2}-(\omega\ell+\epsilon_{n})^{2}},\qquad\mathrm{B}=a_{r}\,,\qquad\Gamma=\frac{a_{q}}{2},\qquad\Delta=\frac{3}{2}-a_{c}>0\,.

(3.6)

Following the Bethe-ansatz method
[
46

47

\phi_{n}(r)=\!\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&\quad n=0,\\
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}(r-r_{i}),&\quad n=1,2,...,\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

where

are unknown roots to be determined. Then, according to Theorem 1.1 of
[
46
]

\Lambda_{j}

j=0,1,2

) are given by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}=-2\left(-n\mathrm{B}+(\Delta+n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}+\mathrm{A}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}^{2}\right),

(3.8a)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}=-n(2\Delta+n-1)-2\mathrm{A}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i},\qquad\qquad\Lambda_{2}=-2n\mathrm{A},

(3.8b)

in which the roots
r_{1},\,r_{2},\,\dots,\,r_{n}
are determined by the Bethe ansatz equations

\sum_{j\neq i}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{i}-r_{j}}+\frac{\mathrm{A}\,r_{i}^{3}+\Delta\,r_{i}^{2}-\mathrm{B}\,r_{i}-2\Gamma}{r_{i}^{3}}=0,\qquad i=1,2,\dots,n.

(3.9)

3.1

\displaystyle=\frac{2(n+1)a_{c}+2\sqrt{m^{2}+p_{z}^{2}-(\omega\ell+\epsilon_{n})^{2}}\,(\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i})-(n+1)^{2}+{\ell^{2}}/{\alpha^{2}}}{2\left(\sqrt{m^{2}+p_{z}^{2}-(\omega\ell+\epsilon_{n})^{2}}-m\right)},

4
Conclusion

Relativistic scalar particles were investigated within a cosmic string spacetime exhibiting non-inertial effects. Based on very recent results, a generalized gravitational potential was considered, including post-Newtonian (relativistic) and quantum corrections as interaction terms.
It was briefly discussed that the resulting equation appears in a form that can be considered a generalization of the double-confluent Heun equation for which no closed-form solutions are available.
It was noted that, to our knowledge, the equation cannot be solved using other (non-perturbative) analytical techniques, including the general form of the Lie algebra approach, the integral transforms frequently used in quantum mechanics, etc. As a result, and based on a Bethe-Ansatz approach, arbitrary state solutions were reported. After obtaining the energy spectrum, the energy shift due to the quantum and relativistic terms was expressed in (
3.10
) in a non-explicit manner. The special case of the ordinary gravitational potential was recovered
[
9
]
.
The results obtained are important because they represent¡ a simultaneous study of gravitational and quantum effects within a non-inertial cosmic string framework. We hope that this work, in conjunction with other theoretical and experimental studies in this field, will lead to a better understanding of the interface of quantum mechanics and gravity.
Finally, it is worth mentioning that a particular difficulty of this way of approaching the problem in question is that the values of the potential parameters are quite diverse, and this will undoubtedly motivate further studies to obtain more satisfactory numerical results on the modification of the ordinary case.

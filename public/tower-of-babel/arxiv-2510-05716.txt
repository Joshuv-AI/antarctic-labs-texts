1
Introduction

Stochastic difference equations, such as

Y_{t}\overset{a.s.}{=}\Phi_{t}(Y_{t-1}),\quad t\in\mathbb{Z},

(1)

where
(\Phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of stationary and ergodic random Lipschitz continuous functions from
(\textup{Y},||\cdot||)
to
(\textup{Y},||\cdot||)
where
(\textup{Y},||\cdot||)
is a complete subspace of a real or complex separable Banach space
(\textup{B},||\cdot||)
, are ubiquitous in time series analysis. An example of a stochastic difference equation in time series analysis is the autoregressive (AR) model

Y_{t}\overset{a.s.}{=}\phi_{0}+\phi_{1}Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_{t},\quad t\in\mathbb{Z},

with
\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
where
(\varepsilon_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) real random variables with zero mean and variance
\sigma^{2}>0
. Another is the generalised autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity (GARCH) model

\begin{aligned} Y_{t}&\overset{a.s.}{=}\sigma_{t}\varepsilon_{t}\\
\sigma_{t}^{2}&\overset{a.s.}{=}\omega+\alpha Y_{t-1}^{2}+\beta\sigma_{t-1}^{2}\end{aligned},\quad t\in\mathbb{Z},

with
\omega>0
,
\alpha\geq 0
, and
\beta\geq 0
where
(\varepsilon_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of i.i.d. real random variables with zero mean and unit variance. Studying stochastic difference equations is therefore of great importance.

Bougerol 1993
and
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
gave conditions under which there exists a unique stationary and ergodic solution to Equation (
1
) and any solution to the stochastic difference equation

\bar{Y}_{t}\overset{a.s.}{=}\Phi_{t}(\bar{Y}_{t-1}),\quad t\in\mathbb{N},

(2)

with
\bar{Y}_{0}
given satisfies

\left|\left|\bar{Y}_{t}-Y_{t}\right|\right|\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty;

(3)

here,
\left|\left|X_{t}\right|\right|\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
means that
\gamma^{t}\left|\left|X_{t}\right|\right|\overset{a.s.}{\rightarrow}0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
for some
\gamma>1
.

Sometimes, however, the situation is more complicated than the one in Equation (
2
). Consider, for instance, the following model

Y_{t}\overset{a.s.}{=}\mu_{t}+\sigma_{t}\varepsilon_{t},\quad t\in\mathbb{Z},

(4)

where
(\varepsilon_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of i.i.d. real random variables with zero mean and unit variance. Here,

\displaystyle\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\overset{a.s.}{=}\omega_{\mu}+\alpha_{\mu}Y_{t-1}+\beta_{\mu}\mu_{t-1},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ t\in\mathbb{Z},

(5)

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{a.s.}{=}\omega_{\sigma}+\alpha_{\sigma}(Y_{t-1}-\mu_{t-1})^{2}+\beta_{\sigma}\sigma_{t-1}^{2},\quad t\in\mathbb{Z},

(6)

with
\omega_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\omega_{\sigma}>0
,
\alpha_{\sigma}\geq 0
, and
\beta_{\sigma}\geq 0
. Consider now the initialised versions of Equations (
5
) and (
6
)

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{t}

\displaystyle\overset{a.s.}{=}\omega_{\mu}+\alpha_{\mu}Y_{t-1}+\beta_{\mu}\bar{\mu}_{t-1},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ t\in\mathbb{N},

(7)

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{a.s.}{=}\omega_{\sigma}+\alpha_{\sigma}(Y_{t-1}-\bar{\mu}_{t-1})^{2}+\beta_{\sigma}\bar{\sigma}_{t-1}^{2},\quad t\in\mathbb{N},

(8)

with
\bar{\mu}_{0}
and
\bar{\sigma}_{0}^{2}
given. For this model, the results by
Bougerol 1993
and
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
above can be used for Equations (
5
) and (
7
) since
\mu_{t}\overset{a.s.}{=}\Phi_{t}^{\mu}(\mu_{t-1}),t\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\bar{\mu}_{t}\overset{a.s.}{=}\Phi_{t}^{\mu}(\bar{\mu}_{t-1}),t\in\mathbb{N}
with
\bar{\mu}_{0}
given where
\Phi_{t}^{\mu}(\mu)=\omega_{\mu}+\alpha_{\mu}Y_{t-1}+\beta_{\mu}\mu
. They can, however, not be used for Equations (
6
) and (
8
) since
\sigma_{t}^{2}\overset{a.s.}{=}\Phi_{t}^{\sigma}(\sigma_{t-1}^{2}),t\in\mathbb{Z}
where
\Phi_{t}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{2})=\omega_{\sigma}+\alpha_{\sigma}(Y_{t-1}-\mu_{t-1})^{2}+\beta_{\sigma}\sigma^{2}
but
\bar{\sigma}_{t}^{2}\overset{a.s.}{=}\bar{\Phi}_{t}^{\sigma}(\bar{\sigma}_{t-1}^{2}),t\in\mathbb{N}
with
\bar{\sigma}_{0}^{2}
given where
\bar{\Phi}_{t}^{\sigma}(\sigma^{2})=\omega_{\sigma}+\alpha_{\sigma}(Y_{t-1}-\bar{\mu}_{t-1})^{2}+\beta_{\sigma}\sigma^{2}
.

In the case where
(\textup{Y},||\cdot||)
is a real or complex separable Banach space,
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
also gave conditions under which any solution to the stochastic difference equation

\hat{Y}_{t}\overset{a.s.}{=}\hat{\Phi}_{t}(\hat{Y}_{t-1}),\quad t\in\mathbb{N},

(9)

with
\hat{Y}_{0}
given where
(\hat{\Phi}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is only a sequence of random Lipschitz continuous functions from
(\textup{Y},||\cdot||)
to
(\textup{Y},||\cdot||)
satisfies

\left|\left|\hat{Y}_{t}-Y_{t}\right|\right|\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

(10)

The condition that
(\textup{Y},||\cdot||)
is a real or complex separable Banach space is, however, very restrictive;
([0,\infty),|\cdot|)
is, for instance, not a real separable Banach space, so the result by
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
cannot be used for Equations (
6
) and (
8
). In this note, we give slightly different conditions under which Equation (
10
) continues to hold in the case where
(\textup{Y},||\cdot||)
is only a complete subspace of a real or complex separable Banach space by using close to identical arguments as
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
. This result can, for instance, be used for Equations (
6
) and (
8
) since
([0,\infty),|\cdot|)
is a complete subspace of the real separable Banach space
(\mathbb{R},|\cdot|)
.

The note is organised as follows. Section
2
collects the results, Section
3
collects the proofs, and the lemmata used in the proofs are collected in Section
4
.

2
Results

The first proposition, which is a special case of Theorem 3.1 in
Bougerol 1993
and Theorem 2.8 in
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
, gives conditions under which there exists a unique stationary and ergodic solution to Equation (
1
). In the following,

\Lambda(\Phi_{t})=\sup_{\underset{x\neq y}{x,y\in\textup{Y}}}\frac{\left|\left|\Phi_{t}(x)-\Phi_{t}(y)\right|\right|}{\left|\left|x-y\right|\right|}.

Proposition 1
.

Assume that

(i)

there exists a
y\in\textup{Y}
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\log^{+}\left|\left|\Phi_{0}(y)-y\right|\right|\right]<\infty
and

(ii)

\mathbb{E}\left[\log^{+}\Lambda(\Phi_{0})\right]<\infty
and there exists an
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that

-\infty\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\log\Lambda(\Phi_{0}^{(r)})\right]<0,

where

\Phi_{t}^{(r)}(\cdot)=\Phi_{t}\circ\Phi_{t-1}\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_{t-r+1}(\cdot).

Then,

Y_{t}\overset{a.s.}{=}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Phi_{t}^{(n)}(y),\quad t\in\mathbb{Z},

is the only stationary and ergodic solution to Equation (
1
).

The next one, which is also a special case of Theorem 3.1 in
Bougerol 1993
, shows that Equation (
3
) also holds.

Proposition 2
.

Assume that the assumptions in Proposition
1
hold. Then,

\left|\left|\bar{Y}_{t}-Y_{t}\right|\right|\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

The following proposition, which is a generalisation of Theorem 2.10 in
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
, gives conditions under which Equation (
10
) holds.

Proposition 3
.

Assume that the assumptions in Proposition
1
hold and that

(i)

\mathbb{E}\left[\log^{+}\left|\left|Y_{0}\right|\right|\right]<\infty
,

(ii)

\left|\left|\hat{\Phi}_{t}(y)-\Phi_{t}(y)\right|\right|\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
for the
y\in\textup{Y}
in Proposition
1
, and

(iii)

\Lambda(\hat{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t})\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Then,

\left|\left|\hat{Y}_{t}-Y_{t}\right|\right|\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

Proofs

3.1
Proof of Proposition
1

Let
d
be the metric induced by the norm
||\cdot||
. Then,
(\textup{B},d)
is a complete separable metric space, so, since a subspace of a metric space is a metric space,
(\textup{Y},d)
is a metric space, which is complete by assumption and separable since a subspace of a separable metric space is separable. The conclusion thus follows from Theorem 3.1 in
Bougerol 1993
(existence) and Theorem 2.8 in
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
(uniqueness).

3.2
Proof of Proposition
2

The conclusion follows from Theorem 3.1 in
Bougerol 1993
.

3.3
Proof of Proposition
3

The conclusion follows if

\left|\left|\hat{Y}_{tr}-Y_{tr}\right|\right|\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

(11)

First, we show that

\left|\left|\hat{\Phi}_{t}^{(n)}(y)-\Phi_{t}^{(n)}(y)\right|\right|\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\left|\hat{\Phi}_{t}(y)-\Phi_{t}(y)\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda(\hat{\Phi}_{t}-\Phi_{t})\left|\left|\hat{\Phi}_{t-1}^{(n)}(y)-\Phi_{t-1}^{(n)}(y)\right|\right|+\Lambda(\Phi_{t})\left|\left|\hat{\Phi}_{t-1}^{(n)}(y)-\Phi_{t-1}^{(n)}(y)\right|\right|

l\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\tilde{\Lambda}(\Phi_{0}^{(r)})}+\varepsilon_{2}\right)\right]<0
. Thus, there exist a
0<\gamma_{2}<1
and a
C_{2}\geq 0
such that

\prod_{l=1}^{k}\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\tilde{\Lambda}(\Phi_{lr}^{(r)})}+\left|\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\tilde{\Lambda}(\hat{\Phi}_{lr}^{(r)})}-\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\tilde{\Lambda}(\Phi_{lr}^{(r)})}\right|\right)\leq C_{2}\gamma_{2}^{k}\quad a.s.

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, continuing from Equation (
17
),

C_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{t}\tilde{\Lambda}(\hat{\Phi}_{lr}^{(r)})\right)\left(\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\tilde{\Lambda}(\hat{\Phi}_{lr}^{(r)})}\right)\leq\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{1-\gamma_{2}}\prod_{l=1}^{t}\tilde{\Lambda}(\hat{\Phi}_{lr}^{(r)})\quad a.s.

Finally, we have that

\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{1-\gamma_{2}}\prod_{l=1}^{t}\tilde{\Lambda}(\hat{\Phi}_{lr}^{(r)})\leq\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{1-\gamma_{2}}\prod_{l=1}^{t}\left(\tilde{\Lambda}(\Phi_{lr}^{(r)})+\left|\Lambda(\hat{\Phi}_{lr}^{(r)})-\Lambda(\Phi_{lr}^{(r)})\right|\right),

where

\left|\Lambda(\hat{\Phi}_{lr}^{(r)})-\Lambda(\Phi_{lr}^{(r)})\right|\leq\Lambda(\hat{\Phi}_{lr}^{(r)}-\Phi_{lr}^{(r)})\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0\quad\text{as}\quad l\rightarrow\infty.

By using the same arguments as above, there exists an
\varepsilon_{3}>0
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(\tilde{\Lambda}(\Phi_{0}^{(r)})+\varepsilon_{3}\right)\right]<0
. Thus, by Lemma
3
,

\prod_{l=1}^{t}\left(\tilde{\Lambda}(\Phi_{lr}^{(r)})+\left|\Lambda(\hat{\Phi}_{lr}^{(r)})-\Lambda(\Phi_{lr}^{(r)})\right|\right)\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty

since
\left|\Lambda(\hat{\Phi}_{lr}^{(r)})-\Lambda(\Phi_{lr}^{(r)})\right|\leq\varepsilon_{3}
a.s. for all sufficiently large
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(\tilde{\Lambda}(\Phi_{0}^{(r)})+\varepsilon_{3}\right)\right]<0
. Equation (
15
) thus also holds. This concludes the proof.

4
Lemmata

Lemma 1

(Lemma 2.1 in
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
)
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of non-negative real random variables and let
(Y_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of stationary non-negative real random variables with
\mathbb{E}\left[\log^{+}Y_{0}\right]<\infty
. If
X_{t}\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, then

X_{t}Y_{t}\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

Lemma 2

(Lemma 2.2 in
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be non-negative real random variables. Then,

\mathbb{E}\left[\log^{+}\left(X+Y\right)\right]\leq 2\log 2+\mathbb{E}\left[\log^{+}X\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\log^{+}Y\right]

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\log^{+}\left(XY\right)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\log^{+}X\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\log^{+}Y\right].

Lemma 3

(Lemma 2.4 in
Straumann and Mikosch 2006
)
.

Let
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of stationary and ergodic non-negative real random variables with
-\infty\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\log X_{0}\right]<0
. Then,

\prod_{t=0}^{n}X_{t}\overset{e.a.s.}{\rightarrow}0\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Sahil Gehlawat, Viêt-Anh Nguyên

Abstract
. Let ℱ d ​ ( ℙ n ) \mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}) be the space of all singular holomorphic foliations by curves on ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} ( n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 ) with degree d ≥ 1 . d\geq 1. 1.
Introduction

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2.
A (singular) holomorphic foliation (by curves) on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with degree
d\in\mathbb{N}
is given by a bundle morphism

\Phi:\ \mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1-d)\to{\rm Tan}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),

and
its singular set is

{\rm sing}(\mathscr{F}):=\{x\in\mathbb{P}^{n}:\ \Phi(x)=0\}.

Here
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-d):=(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1))^{\otimes d},
where
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)
is the tautological line bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{n},
and
{\rm Tan}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
is the tangent bundle of
\mathbb{P}^{n}.

A holomorphic foliation
\mathscr{F}
of degree
d\in\mathbb{N}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is represented in affine coordinates
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
by a vector field of the form

(1.1)

X=\sum_{j=0}^{d}R_{j}+g\mathcal{R},

where
\mathcal{R}
is the radial vector field defined by
\mathcal{R}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}{\partial\over\partial x_{j}},

g
is a homogeneous
polynomial of degree
d,
and
R_{j}
is a vector field whose components are homogeneous polynomial of degree
j,

0\leq j\leq d.

Since
{\rm sing}(\mathscr{F})
has
codimension greater than 1 we have either
g\not\equiv 0
or
g\equiv 0
and
R_{d}
cannot
be written as
hR
where
h
is homogeneous of degree
d-1.
In this case

X
has a pole of order
d-1
at infinity (see, for example,
[
20
]
). We call
d
the degree of
the foliation.
Let
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
denote the (moduli) space of singular holomorphic foliations by curves of degree
d
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}.

Using representation (
1.1
),
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
is identified to a Zariski open set of the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{M}
with

(1.2)

M=M(n,d):=n{n+d\choose d}+{n+d-1\choose d}-1.

The space
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
plays an important role in the theory of singular holomorphic foliations by curves as it is one of the main sources of examples, and hence it is often used in the research as a testing ground for expecting results.
Moreover,
since every holomorphic foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is singular, i.e.,
{\rm sing}(\mathscr{F})\not=\varnothing,
it is natural to investigate the singular set
{\rm sing}(\mathscr{F}).
At the first stage, one may understand
the singular set
{\rm sing}(\mathscr{F})
of a generic element
\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),
that is,
\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{U},
where
\mathscr{U}\subset\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
such that the complement
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\setminus\mathscr{U}
is, in some sense, negligible. Another basic question is to investigate if a
generic element
\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
admits an invariant algebraic curve.

The pioneering article of Jouanolou
[
17
]
is the first work to study this problem systematically.
Jouanolou obtains a satisfactory description of a generic foliation with a given degree
d\geq 1
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}.
One of the main ingredients
in his method is to introduce an explicit family of foliations
\mathcal{J}^{2,d}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
which enjoys
some remarkable properties. Another ingredient in his proof is
a perturbation argument which roughly says that if one foliation satisfies some properties, then every
nearby foliation (in the moduli space of foliations)
should do so as well. Using the holomorphic propagation, this argument permits to show that every foliation

\mathscr{F}
in a subset
\mathscr{U}
of full Lebesgue measure of
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
satisfies these properties. Later on Lins Neto

[
19
]
improves the result of Jouanolou by showing that
\mathscr{U}
can be chosen to be open.
Soares in
[
28
]
proves Jouanolou’s result for
\mathbb{P}^{3}.

The family of Jouanolou foliations was later generalized to
\mathcal{J}^{n,d}
by Lins Neto and Soares in
[
21
,
28
]

for general projective spaces
\mathbb{P}^{n},

n\geq 2.
These two authors develop a residue calculus which allows them to detect effectively if a given foliation has an invariant algebraic curve.

The following theorem summarizes the above mentioned results describing some typical properties of a generic foliation
\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}).

Set

(1.3)

N=N(n,d):=1+d+\ldots+d^{n}.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Jouanolou
[
17
]
and Lins Neto
[
19
]
for
n=2,
Soares
[
28
]
for
n=3,
Lins Neto–Soares
[
21
]
for
n\geq 2
).
For every
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 2,

there is a nonempty real Zariski open set
\mathscr{U}^{{\rm nd}}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\subset\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
such that for every
\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{U}^{{\rm nd}}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),

all the singularities of
\mathscr{F}
are non-degenerate and
\mathscr{F}
does not possess any invariant algebraic curve. Moreover, every
\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{U}^{nd}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
admits exactly
N(n,d)
singular points.

It is worth noting the work of Loray–Rebelo
[
18
]
investigating, among other questions, whether a generic foliation
\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
admits an invariant algebraic subvariety of dimension
\geq 1
(see Remark
4.5
below).

It turns out that foliations with only non-degenerate singularities may not be the right context for many problems arising from the theory of singular holomorphic foliations.
Indeed, one of the main developments in this field during the last 25 years is the advent of the ergodic theory which emphasizes a global study of singular holomorphic foliations
using the analysis of positive currents and the tools from geometric complex analysis developed by Berndtsson–Sibony
[
5
]
and Fornæss–Sibony
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
One major goal of the latter theory is to
study the global behaviour of a generic leaf of a foliation
\mathscr{F}.
Here, the term
“generic”
is measured with respect to some new objects called
directed positive harmonic currents
(see for example
[
14
, Definition 7]
etc. for a definition of this notion).
The analysis of these currents
are often difficult when one approaches the singularities of
\mathscr{F}.

The study near a singular point will be more effective, more tangible if it is linearizable hyperbolic singular point.
Indeed, in this case it admits (local) invariant coordinate hyperplanes, and hence it allows us to start the analysis of the directed positive harmonic currents of
\mathscr{F}
from these special coordinates, and consequently results in quite a lot of important geometric, dynamical properties such as the regularity of the leafwise Poincaré metric on
\mathscr{F},
the entropies, the Lyapunov exponents, the unique ergodicity etc. in the works of Dinh, Fornæss, Sibony and many others (see
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
15
,
22
,
23
,
26
,
27
]
etc. and the references therein, see also the surveys
[
11
,
14
,
24
,
25
]
).
When the singular points of
\mathscr{F}
are not all hyperbolic, some of these desirable properties may not hold as illustrated by Chen
[
6
]
and
Alkateeb–Rebelo
[
1
]
.
When the singular points of
\mathscr{F}
are merely
non-degenerate (and not linearizable), the analysis becomes much harder, and only a few results have been obtained, see the articles of Bacher
[
3
,
4
]
etc. which are in fact inspired by the corresponding results in the case of linearizable singular points.

The main purpose of this article is to prove Theorem
1.1
in the context
of linearizable hyperbolic singular points. Here is our main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Main Theorem)
For every
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 1,

there is a subset
\mathscr{S}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
of full Lebesgue measure of
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
such that for every
\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{S}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),
all singular points of
\mathscr{F}
are linearizable hyperbolic and that if
d\geq 2,
then
\mathscr{F}
does not possess any invariant algebraic curve.

The case
n=2
of Theorem
1.2
has already been known in the work of Jouanolou
[
17
]
. Theorem
1.2
provides an abundant supply of foliations for which the ergodic theory of singular holomorphic foliations can be explored efficiently by means of the theory of positive currents and the theory of geometric complex analysis.

Theorem
1.2
, combined with Corollary 1.3 in
[
27
]
, implies the following result:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{S}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}).

Then for every positive harmonic current
T
directed by
\mathscr{F},

T
is diffuse and the Lelong number of
T
vanishes everywhere in
\mathbb{P}^{n}.
In particular, this property holds for every foliation in a subset of full Lebesgue measure of
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}).

At the first glance, the conclusion of Theorem
1.2
appears to be intuitively evident since in the local situation, for a generic (in the sense of Lebesgue measure) polynomial vector field
Z
singular at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n},
the point
\{0\}
is linearizable hyperbolic.
However, the genericity in the moduli space
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
is not such evident at all as it is now a global situation.
The main ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is then the introduction of a new
n
-dimensional family
\{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha},

\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}

of singular holomorphic foliations having the Jouanolou foliation
\mathcal{J}^{n,d}
as
\mathcal{J}_{0}
(see Definition
3.1
below). This family has the novelty that the characteristic polynomial of every singular point of the foliation
\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}
varies biholomorphically as the parameter
\alpha
varies in a small open neighborhood
\mathbb{U}
of
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
(see Theorem
3.2
). This novelty will make the previous local intuition come true in the global context. We refer the readers to
Remark
3.6
below for a discussion of the choice of another family
close to that introduced by Lins Neto–Soares
[
21
]
.

We use the method developed by Lins Neto–Soares
[
21
]
in order to show that
for almost every
\alpha\in\mathbb{U},
the foliation
\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}
does not possess an invariant algebraic curve.
Finally, we conclude the proof using an argument of holomorphic propagation.

The article is organized as follows.

In Section
2

we recall basic notions and results on the local classification of singularities of holomorphic vector fields. We also recall some basic properties of the Jouanolou foliations.
We start Section
3
by introducing the new family of foliations
\{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}:\ \alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}.
After a qualitative study of their singularities, the section deals with the proof of its main result Theorem
3.2
which is one of the main tools of our method.
Section
4
is devoted to the proof of the Main Theorem
1.2
.
More concretely, Subsection
4.1
shows that
\mathscr{F}_{\alpha}

has no invariant algebraic curve for a generic value of
\alpha\in\mathbb{U}.
Combining the results of Section
3
and Subsection
4.1
, Subsection
4.2
concludes the proof. The article is ended with some remarks.

2.
Background

We first recall some basic elements of holomorphic vector fields and the local theory of singular holomorphic foliations.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Z=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(z){\partial\over\partial z_{j}}
be a holomorphic vector field defined in a neighborhood
\mathbb{U}
of
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.
Consider the holomorphic map

f:=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):\ \mathbb{U}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}.
We say that
Z
is

(1)

singular at
0
if
f(0)=0.

(2)

generic linear
if
it can be written as

Z(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}z_{j}{\partial\over\partial z_{j}}

where
\lambda_{j}
are non-zero complex numbers. The
n
hyperplanes
\{z_{j}=0\}
for
1\leq j\leq n
are said to be the
invariant hypersurfaces.

(3)

with non-degenerate singularity at
0
if
Z
is singular at
0
and the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
of the Jacobian matrix
Df(0)

are all nonzero.

(4)

with hyperbolic singularity at
0
if
Z
is singular at
0
and the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
of the Jacobian matrix
Df(0)

are all nonzero and
\lambda_{j}/\lambda_{l}\not\in\mathbb{R}
for all
1\leq j\not=l\leq n.

The integral curves of
Z
define a singular holomorphic foliation on

\mathbb{U}
.
The condition
\lambda_{j}\not=0
implies that the foliation
has an isolated singularity at 0.
The singular set of
Z
is denoted by
{\rm sing}(Z).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a singular holomorphic foliation such that its singular set
{\rm sing}(\mathscr{F})
is an analytic subset of
X
with codimension
\geq 2.

We say that a
singular point
a\in{\rm sing}(\mathscr{F})
is
linearizable
(resp.
non-degenerate
, resp.
hyperbolic
) if
there is a local holomorphic coordinate system of
X
near
a
on which the
leaves of
\mathscr{F}
are integral curves of a generic linear vector field (resp. of a holomorphic vector field admitting
0
as non-degenerate singularity, resp. a hyperbolic singularity).

Definition 2.3
.

Given
\delta>0,
a point
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be of type
\delta
if there is a constant
c>0
such that for every
1\leq j\leq n,
we have

|\lambda_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\lambda_{i}|\geq{c\over|m|^{\delta}},

for all vectors
m=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with
|m|:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\geq 2.

Proposition 2.4
.

For every
\delta>{n-2\over n},
consider the set

\mathscr{A}_{\delta}:=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\ \lambda\text{ of type $\delta$ }\right\}.

Then the
2n
-Lebesgue measure of the set
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\mathscr{A}_{\delta}
is zero.

Proof.

It is an immediate consequence of
[
2
, Theorem 4, p. 189]
.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

(Siegel’s Theorem, see for example
[
16
]
)
If the eigenvalues of the linear part of a holomorphic vector field at a singular point form a vector of type
\delta,
then the field is biholomorphically equivalent to its linear part in the neighborhood of the singular point.

In the remainder of the section we state some useful results in the global context of singular holomorphic foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{n}.

Definition 2.6
.

For
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 1,
let
\mathscr{F}^{{\rm nd}}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
denote the space of all foliations
\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})

such that all singular points of
\mathscr{F}
are non-degenerate.

Proposition 2.7
.

(See for example
[
20
, Corollary 2.7]
)

Let
\mathscr{F}_{0}\in\mathscr{F}^{{\rm nd}}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
with
{\rm sing}(\mathscr{F}_{0})=\{p_{1,0},\ldots,p_{N,0}\},
where
p_{i,0}\not=p_{j,0}
for
i\not=j.
Then there exist connected open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathscr{F}_{0}
in
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),

U_{1},\ldots,U_{N}

of
p_{1,0},\ldots,p_{N,0}
respectively, pairwise disjoint, and holomorphic maps
\varphi_{j}:\ \mathcal{U}\to U_{j},

for
1\leq j\leq N
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

\varphi_{j}(\mathscr{F}_{0})=p_{j,0}.

(ii)

For every
\mathscr{F}\in\mathcal{U}
and every
1\leq j\leq N,

\varphi_{j}(\mathscr{F})
is the unique singularity of
\mathscr{F}
in
U_{j},
and moreover, this singularity is non-degenerate.

(iii)

For every
\mathscr{F}\in\mathcal{U},
we have
{\rm sing}(\mathscr{F})=\{\varphi_{1}(\mathscr{F}),\ldots,\varphi_{N}(\mathscr{F})\}.

In particular,
\mathscr{F}^{{\rm nd}}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
is a Zariski open subset of
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),
and hence it is connected.

Consider the Jouanolou foliation
\mathcal{J}^{n,d}\in\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
given by the vector field (see
[
17
,
28
,
21
]
):

(2.1)

X_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(x_{i+1}^{d}-x_{i}x_{1}^{d})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+(1-x_{n}x_{1}^{d})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}.

Proposition 2.8
.

(See for example
[
20
, Proposition 2.10]
)

(1)

\mathcal{J}^{n,d}\in\mathscr{F}^{{\rm nd}}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}).

(2)

{\rm sing}(\mathcal{J}^{n,d})\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}.
Moreover,

{\rm sing}(\mathcal{J}^{n,d})=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{N}\},
where
N=N(n,d)
and

p_{j}=(\xi^{j},(\xi^{j})^{f(n-1)},\ldots,(\xi^{j})^{f(1)}),

j=1,\ldots,N,
where

\xi
is an
N
-th primitive root of unity and
f(m):=-(d+d^{2}+\ldots+d^{m}).

3.
A new family of foliations

Definition 3.1
.

For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, consider the singular holomorphic foliation
\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
which is generated on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by the vector field

(3.1)

X_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(x_{i+1}^{d}-x_{i}x_{1}^{d}+\alpha_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+(1-x_{n}x_{1}^{d}+\alpha_{n})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}=X_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}.

Given an open neighourhood
\mathbb{U}
of
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the set
\mathcal{J}(\mathbb{U}):=\{\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}):\alpha\in\mathbb{U}\}
is an
n
-dimensional subspace in
\mathscr{F}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
passing through
\mathcal{J}_{0}={\mathcal{J}}^{n,d}
. By Proposition
2.8
,
\mathcal{J}_{0}
has
N=N(n,d)
number of singular points, each of which is non-degenerate. Choose
\mathbb{U}
to be small enough so that, each
\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{U}
also has exactly
N
singular points and that the set
E_{\alpha}:={\rm sing}(X_{\alpha})
coincides with
{\rm sing}(\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}).

Observe that
x=(x_{1,\alpha},x_{2,\alpha},\ldots x_{n,\alpha})\in E_{\alpha}
if and only if

(3.2)

x_{i+1}^{d}-x_{i}x_{1}^{d}+\alpha_{i}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq i\leq n-1,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt1-x_{n}x_{1}^{d}+\alpha_{n}=0.

For every
1\leq m\leq N
, let

(3.3)

p_{m}=(\xi^{m},\xi^{-m(d^{n-1}+\ldots+d)},\xi^{-m(d^{n-2}+\ldots+d)},\ldots,\xi^{-m(d^{2}+d)},\xi^{-md})

be a singular point of
X_{0}
, where
\xi
is a primitive root of unity of order
N.
For every
1\leq m\leq N
and every
\alpha\in\mathbb{U}
let
p_{m,\alpha}
be the corresponding singular point of
X_{\alpha}
close to
p_{m}
. Consider the Jacobian matrix of
X_{\alpha}
at the singular point
p_{m,\alpha}=(x_{1,\alpha},x_{2,\alpha},\ldots x_{n,\alpha}).

The characteristic polynomial is given by

P_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\det(\lambda I-DX_{\alpha}(p_{m,\alpha}))=\lambda^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i,m,\alpha}\lambda^{n-i}.

Now, consider the map

(3.4)

\begin{split}\Phi^{\circ}_{m}:\mathbb{U}&\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n},\\
\Phi^{\circ}_{m}(\alpha)&:=(\sigma_{1,m,\alpha},\sigma_{2,m,\alpha},\ldots,\sigma_{n,m,\alpha}).\end{split}

The main result of this section is
the following

Theorem 3.2
.

It holds that
{\rm rank}(D\Phi^{\circ}_{m}(0))=n
for all
1\leq m\leq N
, where

D\Phi^{\circ}_{m}(0)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial\sigma_{1,m,\alpha}}{\partial\alpha_{1}}(0)&\frac{\partial\sigma_{1,m,\alpha}}{\partial\alpha_{2}}(0)&\ldots&\frac{\partial\sigma_{1,m,\alpha}}{\partial\alpha_{n}}(0)\\
\frac{\partial\sigma_{2,m,\alpha}}{\partial\alpha_{1}}(0)&\frac{\partial\sigma_{2,m,\alpha}}{\partial\alpha_{2}}(0)&\ldots&\frac{\partial\sigma_{2,m,\alpha}}{\partial\alpha_{n}}(0)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial\sigma_{n,m,\alpha}}{\partial\alpha_{1}}(0)&\frac{\partial\sigma_{n,m,\alpha}}{\partial\alpha_{2}}(0)&\ldots&\frac{\partial\sigma_{n,m,\alpha}}{\partial\alpha_{n}}(0)\\
\end{pmatrix}.

For the proof of the proposition, we need several lemmas. The first lemma gives an asymptotic description of the singular set
E_{\alpha}
in terms of
\alpha.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x=(x_{1,\alpha},x_{2,\alpha},\ldots x_{n,\alpha})
be a point in
E_{\alpha}.
Then for all
1\leq i\leq n,
we have

(3.5)

x_{i,\alpha}=x_{1,\alpha}^{\sum_{s=1}^{n+1-i}-d^{s}}+\alpha_{i}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\alpha_{j}d^{j-i}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d+\sum_{l=0}^{j-i-1}-d^{n+1-i-l}}+O(|\alpha|^{2}).

Proof.

We will prove it by descending induction on
i\in[1,n].

Since
x\in E_{\alpha},
we have by (
3.2
)
x_{n,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d}=1+\alpha_{n}
, and hence

x_{n,\alpha}=x_{1,\alpha}^{-d}+\alpha_{n}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d},

which shows that estimate (
3.5
) is true for
i=n.
Now suppose that estimate (
3.5
) is true for
i=m
. Writing

x_{m,\alpha}^{d}=\left(x_{1,\alpha}^{\sum_{s=1}^{n+1-m}-d^{s}}+\alpha_{m}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{n}\alpha_{j}d^{j-m}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d+\sum_{l=0}^{j-m-1}-d^{n+1-m-l}}+O(|\alpha|^{2})\right)^{d},

we get

x_{m,\alpha}^{d}=\left(x_{1,\alpha}^{\sum_{s=1}^{n+1-m}-d^{s}}\right)^{d}+\binom{d}{d-1}\left(x_{1,\alpha}^{\sum_{s=1}^{n+1-m}-d^{s}}\right)^{d-1}\\
\left(\alpha_{m}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{n}\alpha_{j}d^{j-m}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d+\sum_{l=0}^{j-m-1}-d^{n+1-m-l}}\right)+O(|\alpha|^{2}).

Since
(d-1)(\sum_{s=1}^{n+1-m}-d^{s})=\left(\sum_{s=2}^{n+1-(m-1)}-d^{s}\right)+\left(\sum_{s=1}^{n+1-m}d^{s}\right)=d-d^{n+1-(m-1)}
, we have

x_{m,\alpha}^{d}=x_{1,\alpha}^{\sum_{s=2}^{n+1-(m-1)}-d^{s}}+dx_{1,\alpha}^{d-d^{n+1-(m-1)}}\\
\left(\alpha_{m}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{n}\alpha_{j}d^{j-m}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d+\sum_{l=0}^{j-m-1}-d^{n+1-m-l}}\right)+O(|\alpha|^{2}).

Therefore, we get

x_{m,\alpha}^{d}=x_{1,\alpha}^{\sum_{s=2}^{n+1-(m-1)}-d^{s}}+\sum_{j=(m-1)+1}^{n}\alpha_{j}d^{j-(m-1)}x_{1,\alpha}^{\sum_{l=0}^{j-(m-1)-1}-d^{n+1-(m-1)-l}}+O(|\alpha|^{2}).

Now using the above estimate and the relation
x_{m-1,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d}=x_{m,\alpha}^{d}+\alpha_{m-1}
obtained from (
3.2
), it follows that

x_{m-1,\alpha}=x_{1,\alpha}^{-d}(x_{m,\alpha}^{d}+\alpha_{m-1})=x_{1,\alpha}^{\sum_{s=1}^{n+1-(m-1)}-d^{s}}+\alpha_{m-1}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d}\\
+\sum_{j=(m-1)+1}^{n}\alpha_{j}d^{j-(m-1)}x_{1,\alpha}^{-d+\sum_{l=0}^{j-(m-1)-1}-d^{n+1-(m-1)-l}}+O(|\alpha|^{2}).

So estimate (
3.5
) is true for
i=m-1,
and the proof is thereby completed.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

With the convention
x_{0,\alpha}=1
, we have that

P_{\alpha}(\lambda)=(\lambda+x_{1,\alpha}^{d})^{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\left[d^{j}(x_{1,\alpha}x_{2,\alpha}\ldots x_{j-1,\alpha})^{d-1}x_{j,\alpha}^{d}(\lambda+x_{1,\alpha}^{d})^{n-j}\right]}.

Moreover, it holds for
1\leq i\leq n
that

\sigma_{i,m,\alpha}=\binom{n}{n-i}x_{1,\alpha}^{id}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}\binom{n-j}{n-i}x_{1,\alpha}^{(i-j)d}(d^{j}(x_{1,\alpha}x_{2,\alpha}\ldots x_{j-1,\alpha})^{d-1}x_{j,\alpha}^{d}).

Proof.

Using the explicit expression (
3.1
), a straightforward computation gives
the following Jacobian matrix of
X_{\alpha}
at the singular point
p_{m,\alpha}=(x_{1,\alpha},x_{2,\alpha},\ldots x_{n,\alpha})

DX_{\alpha}(p_{m,\alpha})=\begin{pmatrix}-(d+1)x_{1,\alpha}^{d}&dx_{2,\alpha}^{d-1}&0&0&\ldots&0&0\\
-dx_{2,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d-1}&-x_{1,\alpha}^{d}&dx_{3,\alpha}^{d-1}&0&\ldots&0&0\\
-dx_{3,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d-1}&0&-x_{1,\alpha}^{d}&dx_{4,\alpha}^{d-1}&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
-dx_{n-1,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d-1}&0&0&0&\ldots&-x_{1,\alpha}^{d}&dx_{n,\alpha}^{d-1}\\
-dx_{n,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d-1}&0&0&0&\ldots&0&-x_{1,\alpha}^{d}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, we get that

\lambda I-DX_{\alpha}(p_{m,\alpha})=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda+(d+1)x_{1,\alpha}^{d}&-dx_{2,\alpha}^{d-1}&0&0&\ldots&0&0\\
dx_{2,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d-1}&\lambda+x_{1,\alpha}^{d}&-dx_{3,\alpha}^{d-1}&0&\ldots&0&0\\
dx_{3,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d-1}&0&\lambda+x_{1,\alpha}^{d}&-dx_{4,\alpha}^{d-1}&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
dx_{n-1,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d-1}&0&0&0&\ldots&\lambda+x_{1,\alpha}^{d}&-dx_{n,\alpha}^{d-1}\\
dx_{n,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d-1}&0&0&0&\ldots&0&\lambda+x_{1,\alpha}^{d}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The Laplace expansion along the first column of the above matrix gives

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\big(\lambda I-DX_{\alpha}(p_{m,\alpha})\big)=[\lambda+(d+1)x_{1,\alpha}^{d}](\lambda+x_{1,\alpha}^{d})^{n-1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}dx_{j,\alpha}x_{1,\alpha}^{d-1}\det\begin{pmatrix}L_{j-1}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&0\\
\hline\cr\ 0&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&U_{n-j}\end{pmatrix}

where
L_{j-1}
is a lower triangular
(j-1)\times(j-1)
matrix and
U_{n-j}
is a upper triangular
(n-j)\times(n-j)
matrix of the form

L_{j-1}=\begin{pmatrix}-dx_{2,\alpha}^{d-1}\\
&\ddots&\\
&&-dx_{j,\alpha}^{d-1}\end{pmatrix}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptU_{n-j}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda+x_{1,\alpha}^{d}\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\lambda+x_{1,\alpha}^{d}\end{pmatrix}.

Since we have

\det\begin{pmatrix}L_{j-1}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&0\\
\hline\cr\ 0&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&U_{n-j}\end{pmatrix}=\det L_{j-1}\det U_{n-j}=(-1)^{j-1}d^{j-1}(x_{2,\alpha}\ldots x_{j,\alpha})^{d-1}(\lambda+x_{1,\alpha}^{d})^{n-j},

a calculation gives the first assertion of the lemma.

The second assertion is an immediate consequence of the first one.
∎

Lemma 3.5

For each

there exist constants

Lemma 4.2

For every
\phi\in\mathbb{G}(n,d)
and every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n},
we have
\phi_{*}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{J}_{\tilde{\alpha}},

where
\tilde{\alpha}=\xi^{-d}\phi(\alpha).

Here,
\xi
is a root of unity of order
N
such that for all

\phi(x)=\big(\xi x_{1},\xi^{-(d^{n-1}+\ldots+d)}x_{2},\ldots,\xi^{-(d^{2}+d)}x_{n-1},\xi^{-d}x_{n}\big).

Proof.

There are
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
such that

\phi(x)=\big(\gamma^{-1}_{1}x_{1},\ldots,\gamma^{-1}_{n}x_{n}\big)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for}\hskip 20.00003ptx=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

A straightforward computation gives that

\phi_{*}(X_{0})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}(\gamma^{d}_{i+1}x_{i+1}^{d}-\gamma_{i}\gamma_{1}^{d}x_{i}x_{1}^{d})\gamma_{i}^{-1}{\partial\over\partial x_{i}}+(1-\gamma_{n}\gamma_{1}^{d}x_{n}x_{1}^{d})\gamma_{n}^{-1}{\partial\over\partial x_{n}}.

Since

\gamma_{i}^{-1}=\xi^{d+d^{2}+\ldots+d^{n-i+1}},\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma_{i+1}^{d}=\xi^{-(d^{2}+d^{3}+\ldots+d^{n-i+1})},\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma_{1}^{d}=\xi^{d},\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma_{n}^{-1}=\xi^{d},

we infer that

\phi_{*}(X_{\alpha})=\xi^{d}\big(X_{0}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\alpha}_{i}{\partial\over\partial x_{i}}\big),

where

\tilde{\alpha}=\big(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\big)=\big(\xi^{d^{2}+\ldots+d^{n}}\alpha_{1},\xi^{d^{2}+\ldots+d^{n-1}}\alpha_{2},\ldots,\xi^{d^{2}}\alpha_{n-1},\alpha_{n}\big).

Hence, the lemma follows.

\mathscr{F}\in\mathscr{S}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}).
We have shown that

(\mathcal{U}_{0}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{0})\cap\mathscr{U}^{{\rm nd}}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\subset\mathscr{S}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}).

For
1\leq\ell\leq k
consider the critical set

\mathcal{C}_{m,\ell}:=\{\mathscr{F}\in\mathcal{U}_{\ell}:\ \Phi_{m,\ell}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{is not a submersion at}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathscr{F}\}.

Suppose that we have proved that
\mathcal{C}_{m,j}
is an analytic set of
\mathcal{U}_{j}
for every
0\leq j<\ell.
We need to prove that
\mathcal{C}_{m,\ell}
is an analytic set of
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}.

It suffices to show that
\mathcal{C}_{m,\ell}
cannot be equal to the whole
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}.

Observe that

\mathcal{C}_{m,\ell-1}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\ell}=\mathcal{C}_{m,\ell}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\ell}.

By the hypothesis of induction, the left-hand side is an analytic subset of
\mathcal{U}_{\ell-1}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\ell}.
This proves the last claim.
So
\mathcal{C}_{m,\ell}
is a proper analytic subset of
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}.
Consider the set

\mathscr{C}_{\ell}:=\bigcup_{m=1}^{N}\Psi_{m,\ell}^{-1}(\mathscr{A})\cup\mathcal{C}_{m,\ell}\subset\mathcal{U}_{\ell}.

Since
\Psi_{m,\ell}
is a submersion outside the analytic set
\mathcal{C}_{m,\ell}
and
\mathscr{A}
is Borel set of zero
2n
-Lebesgue measure, it follows that
\mathscr{C}_{\ell}
is also a Borel set of zero
2M
-Lebesgue measure. We can show as in the case of
\mathcal{U}_{0}
and
\mathscr{C}_{0}
that

(\mathcal{U}_{\ell}\setminus\mathscr{C}_{\ell})\cap\mathscr{U}^{{\rm nd}}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\subset\mathscr{S}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}).

When
\ell=k,
setting
\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{U}_{k}
and
\mathscr{C}:=\mathscr{C}_{k},
we obtain that
(\mathcal{U}\setminus\mathscr{C})\cap\mathscr{U}^{{\rm nd}}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\subset\mathscr{S}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}),
which completes the proof of the above fact.
∎

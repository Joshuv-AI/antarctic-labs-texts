Strong convergence of tamed theta scheme for superlinearly growing McKean-Vlasov NSDDEs driven by fractional Brownian motions Thanks: Supported by Jiangxi Provincial Natural Science Foundation(No., 20252BAC250002, GJJ2404201)
Li Tan, Shizhong Hu, Shengrong Wang

Abstract
In this article, we study the McKean-Vlasov neutral stochastic differential delay equations driven by fractional Brownian motion with super-linearly growing coefficients, where the Hurst exponent H ∈ ( 1 / 2 , 1 ) H\in(1/2,1) . The existence and uniqueness of the exact solution were shown by the Picard iteration. Besides, we propose a tamed theta Euler-Maruyama scheme for this equation, analyzed the moment boundness and propagation of chaos etc. Moreover, the convergence rate of the numerical scheme is established. Keywords : McKean-Vlasov NSDDEs; super-linearly growing coefficients; fractional Brownian motion; strong convergence rate; tamed theta EM scheme



1
introduction

Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) whose coefficients depend on the law of the solution (also called McKean-Vlasov SDEs), were first investigated by McKean
[
1
]
, whose work drew inspiration from Kac’s programme in Kinetic Theory
[
2
]
. Since then, McKean-Vlasov SDEs have evolved into a major area of research in stochastic analysis. To approximate the McKean-Vlasov SDE limit, Bossy and Talay
[
3
,
4
]
proposed a system of
n
weakly interacting particles—replicating the statistical behavior of the limit system, and systematically studied the numerical approximation of its solutions. Building on this foundation, the existence and uniqueness of McKean-Vlasov SDEs were discussed in
[
5
,
6
]
, while the well-posedness and the propagation of chaos for McKean-Vlasov SDEs were investigated by Lacker
[
7
]
. Given the rarity of explicit solutions for McKean-Vlasov SDEs, numerical approximation methods have become indispensable. A variety of schemes have been developed to handle different coefficients behaviors, for example the implicit Euler-Maruyama (EM) scheme
[
8
,
9
]
, the adaptive EM scheme
[
10
,
11
]
, the tamed EM scheme
[
12
,
13
]
, and truncated EM scheme
[
14
]
.

In recent years, research attention has increasingly shifted to McKean-Vlasov SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion—a generalization of standard Brownian motion with unique self-similarity and long-memory properties, making it well-suited for modeling phenomena in atmospheric sciences, financial engineering, and other fields. These equations typically take the form:

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X_{t}=b(X_{t},\mathcal{L}(X_{t})){\rm d}t+\sigma(\mathcal{L}(X_{t})){\rm d}B_{t}^{H},

where the coefficients
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\sigma:\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
are Borel-measurable, here,
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite
\theta
-th moment.
\mathcal{L}(X_{t})
is the law of
X
at
t
and
B^{H}=\{B_{t}^{H},t\in[0,T]\}
is a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent
H\in(0,1)
defined on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. As a centered Gaussian process,
B_{H}
has the covariance function

(1.2)

\displaystyle R_{H}(t,s)=\mathbb{E}(B_{t}^{H}B_{s}^{H})=\frac{1}{2}(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}),\ \ \forall t,s\in[0,T].

Notably, when
H=1/2
, the fractional Brownian motion
\{B_{t}^{H}\}_{t\geq 0}
reduces to a standard Brownian motion. The well-posedness of such equations was first proven by Fan et al.
[
15
]
. Subsequent research have further expanded the theoretical framework of this model. For example, under the assumption that the coefficients satisfy globally Lipschitz conditions, He et al.
[
16
]
established the propagation of chaos for the model described in (
1.1
) and analyzed the strong convergence of the EM method.

A critical gap in current literature is the lack of studies on McKean-Vlasov SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion with super-linearly growing coefficients. One notable exception is Shen et al.
[
17
]
who showed that under specific averaging conditions, solutions to such equations can be approximated by solutions of an associated averaged equation. More recently, Wang et al.
[
18
]
and Gao et al.
[
19
]
focused on the EM scheme for delayed McKean-Vlasov SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motion, but their work only considered super-linear growth in the delay term of the drift coefficient, not the coefficient itself.

To capture the influence of past states on current dynamics, stochastic delay differential equations (SDDEs) and neutral stochastic differential delay equations (NSDDEs) are essential tools in stochastic calculus, with broad applications in modeling natural and engineered systems, see for example
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
. A key challenge arises when NSDDEs exhibit super-linear growth: the classical EM scheme often fails to converge. To address this, a tamed EM scheme was introduced to handle super-linearly growing coefficients. For instance, Deng et al.
[
23
]
proposed two types of explicit tamed EM schemes for NSDDEs with super-linearly growing drift and diffusion coefficients and proved strong convergence for each. Cui et al.
[
24
]
studied the tamed EM methods to approximate the solutions of McKean-Vlasov NSDDEs with super-linear drift, establishing the convergence rate. Additional insights into tamed EM schemes for NSDDEs can be found in
[
22
,
25
]
. Notably, Tan and Yuan
[
26
]
proposed a modified tamed theta EM scheme and demonstrated the convergence of the numerical solutions, providing a key motivation for the present work.

Drawing inspiration from the existing literatures, we extend the model (
1.1
) to accommodate coefficients with super-linear growth. We propose a tamed theta EM scheme for the McKean-Vlasov NSDDEs, which takes the following form:

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\rm d}(X_{t}-D(X_{t-\tau}))=b(X_{t},X_{t-\tau},\mathcal{L}(X_{t})){\rm d}t+\sigma(\mathcal{L}(X_{t})){\rm d}B_{t}^{H},

where
\tau>0
denotes the delay.

This paper’s main contributions are outlined below:

•

Unlike the prior work (e.g.,
[
18
,
19
]
) that only considers super-linear growth in the delay term, we analyze McKean-Vlasov equations driven by fractional Brownian motion where the drift coefficient itself exhibits super-linear growth. We prove the core properties of such equations, representing a significant advance in this field.

•

By a delicate analysis involving the fractional Itô formula and intricate inequality techniques, we investigate the properties of the solution, the propagation of chaos in associated particle systems, and the convergence of the proposed numerical scheme.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the mathematical preliminaries, including key definitions and lemmas for McKean-Vlasov NSDDEs with super-linear growth driven by fractional Brownian motion. Section 3 establishes the existence and uniqueness of the solution. In Section 4, we analyze the propagation of chaos in the particle system associated with our model. Finally, Section 5 introduces the tamed theta EM scheme and derives its strong convergence rate.

Next, for two probability measures

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\nu)=\bigg(\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{\theta}\pi({\rm d}x,{\rm d}y)\bigg)^{1/\theta},

Let

, we define the kernel function

(2.1)

\displaystyle\phi(s,t)=\phi_{H}(s,t)=H(2H-1)|s-t|^{2H-2},\ \ s,t\in\mathbb{R},

and recall that for
s,t>0
, this kernel satisfies the integral identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\phi(u,v){\rm d}u{\rm d}v=R_{H}(t,s),

where
R_{H}(t,s)
is defined as in (
1.2
). Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be the Schwartz space (space of rapidly decreasing smooth functions) on

. For any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, we define its squared norm with respect to the kernel
\phi
as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)f(t)\phi(s,t){\rm d}s{\rm d}t<\infty,

where
\phi
is the kernel defined in (
2.1
). Next, we equip
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
with an inner product induced by
\phi

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{H}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)g(t)\phi(s,t){\rm d}s{\rm d}t,\ \ f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}).

The completion of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to this inner product is denoted by
L_{\phi}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. For the half line
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,+\infty)
, we further define the space
L_{\phi}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
as the set of measurable functions

such that their squared
\phi
-norm is finite:

\displaystyle L_{\phi}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\bigg\{g|g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R},\ \ \ |g|_{\phi}^{2}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(s)g(t)\phi(s,t){\rm d}s{\rm d}t<\infty\bigg\}.

Here,
L_{\phi}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
can be understood as the natural restriction of
L_{\phi}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to functions supported on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and it is also a Hilbert space under the inner product

\displaystyle\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle_{\phi}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}g_{1}(s)g_{2}(t)\phi(s,t){\rm d}s{\rm d}t.

Definition 2.1
.

[
28
, Definition 3.5.1]

Let
g\in L_{\phi}^{2}(\mathbb{R})

\Phi_{g}
denote a random variable induced by

\Phi_{g}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(s){\rm d}B_{s}^{H}
. For

\displaystyle D_{\Phi_{g}}F(\omega)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\bigg\{F\bigg(\omega+\delta\int_{0}^{\cdot}g(u){\rm d}u\bigg)-F(\omega)\bigg\},

provided the limit exists in

. Furthermore, if there is a process
(D_{s}^{\phi}F,s\geq 0)
such that for all

\displaystyle D_{\Phi_{g}}F=\int_{0}^{\infty}D_{s}^{\phi}F\cdot g_{s}{\rm d}s\ \ \ {\rm almost\ surely},

then

is said to be
\phi

We denote
L_{\phi}(0,T)
as the set of stochastic processes
F

[0,T]

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{i}^{(n)}}^{t_{i+1}^{(n)}}\int_{t_{j}^{(n)}}^{t_{j+1}^{(n)}}|D_{s}^{\phi}F_{t_{i}^{(n)}}^{\pi}D_{t}^{\phi}F_{t_{j}^{(n)}}^{\pi}-D_{s}^{\phi}F_{t}D_{t}^{\phi}F_{s}|dsdt\right]\to 0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|F^{\pi}-F\|_{H}^{2}\to 0,n\to\infty,

where

.

(Fractional Itô formula)

Let

\displaystyle V(t,X_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V(0,X_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\bigg[\frac{\partial V(s,X_{s})}{\partial s}+\frac{\partial V(s,X_{s})}{\partial X}G(s,X_{s})

\displaystyle+H(2H-1)\text{{\rm tr}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}V(s,X_{s})}{\partial X^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{2H-2}F(r,X_{r})F^{T}(r,X_{r}){\rm d}r\right)\bigg]{\rm d}s

, and

Remark 2.9
.

Considering the special situation of Lemma

\beta=2H(1/2<H<1)

\displaystyle u(t)\leq C.

Since for these parameters, we have

\displaystyle\frac{c_{m+1}}{c_{m}}=\frac{\Gamma(2Hm+1)}{\Gamma(2Hm+1+2H)}\sim\frac{(2Hm)^{2Hm+1/2}e^{-2Hm}}{(2Hm+2H)^{2Hm+2H+1/2}e^{-(2Hm+2H)}}.

Simplifying further, this ratio satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{c_{m+1}}{c_{m}}\sim(2Hm)^{-2H}e^{2H}.

Since for
2H>0
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}(2Hm)^{-2H}e^{2H}=0,

the ratio test guarantees that the series
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c_{m}s^{2Hm}
has an infinite radius of convergence. Thus,
E_{2H,1}(\cdot)
is bounded for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, implying
u(t)\leq a\cdot E_{2H,1}(\cdot)\leq C.

3
Existence and Uniqueness

Theorem 3.1
.

Let Assumptions
2.2
-
2.5
hold. Then there exists a unique solution
\{X_{t}\}_{t\in[-\tau,T]}
to (
2
). Moreover, for any
\overline{p}\geq 2
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C,

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
T,H,\overline{p},\lambda,K_{i}
and the initial data
\xi
.

Proof.

We note that the application of the fractional Itô formula in the following argument is justified by the Lipschitz conditions in Assumptions
2.3
-
2.5
, which ensure the necessary regularity of the Picard iteration sequence
X_{t}^{(k)}
. Standard theory of Malliavin calculus for SDEs (e.g.
[
28
]
) guarantees that these iterates are
\phi
-differentiable and satisfy the integrability conditions required by Lemma
2.1
. We refer to the proof procedure in
[
24
]
and divide the proof into 3 steps.

Step 1. Moment Boundness
For
k\geq 0
and
t\in[-\tau,0]
, define
X^{(k)}_{t}=\xi(t)
. For
k=0
and
t\in[0,T]
, define
X^{(0)}_{t}\equiv\xi(0)
. For
k\geq 1
and
t\in[0,T]
,
X_{t}^{(k)}
is solved by

\displaystyle{\rm d}(X_{t}^{(k)}-D(X^{(k)}_{t-\tau}))=b(X_{t}^{(k)},X_{t-\tau}^{(k)},\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}^{(k-1)}}){\rm d}t+\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}^{(k-1)}}){\rm d}B_{t}^{H},

where
\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}^{(0)}}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{0}}
. For
i=1
, by Assumption
2.3
and Lemma
2.7
, we get

\displaystyle|X_{t}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}=

\displaystyle|D(X_{t-\tau}^{(1)})+X_{t}^{(1)}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{{\overline{p}}-1}}|D(X_{t-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{{\overline{p}}-1}}|X_{t}^{(1)}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda|X_{t-\tau}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{{\overline{p}}-1}}|X_{t}^{(1)}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}.

Then, we conclude that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}\leq\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\|\xi\|^{\overline{p}}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{\overline{p}}}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}.

Using the fractional Itô formula in Lemma
2.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle|X_{t}^{(1)}-D(X_{t-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\xi(0)-D(\xi(-\tau))|^{\overline{p}}

\displaystyle+{\overline{p}}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{{\overline{p}}-2}\langle X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)}),b(X_{s}^{(1)},X_{s-\tau}^{(1)},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{(0)}})\rangle{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}{\overline{p}}({\overline{p}}-1)H(2H-1)|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{{\overline{p}}-2}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{2H-2}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{(0)}})\|^{2}{\rm d}r{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+{\overline{p}}\int_{0}^{t}|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{{\overline{p}}-1}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{(0)}})\|{\rm d}B_{s}^{H}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\xi(0)-D(\xi(-\tau))|^{\overline{p}}+Q_{1}^{(1)}(t)+Q_{2}^{(1)}(t)+Q_{3}^{(1)}(t).

For any positive integer
N
, define the stopping time

\displaystyle\rho_{N}^{(1)}=T\wedge\inf\{t\in[0,T]:|X_{t}^{(1)}|\geq N\}.

It yields that
\rho_{N}^{(1)}\to T
a.s. as
N\to\infty
. Applying Assumptions
2.3
and
2.4
, the Young’s inequality, we see

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}Q_{1}^{(1)}(s)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\overline{p}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{{\overline{p}}-2}\langle X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)}),b(X_{s}^{(1)},X_{s-\tau}^{(1)},\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{(0)}})\rangle{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}s+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\bigg[1+|X_{s}^{(1)}|^{2}+|X_{s-\tau}^{(1)}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{(0)}},\delta_{0})\bigg]^{{\overline{p}}/2}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r.

Since Assumption
2.5
implies
\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{(0)}})\|\leq C\big(1+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{(0)}},\delta_{0})\big)\leq C
, this together with the Young’s inequality, the Jensen inequality, and the Fubini theorem, it results in

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}Q_{2}^{(1)}(s)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}{\overline{p}}({\overline{p}}-1)H(2H-1)|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{{\overline{p}}-2}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{2H-2}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{(0)}})\|^{2}{\rm d}r{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{2H-2}|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{{\overline{p}}-2}(1+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{(0)}},\delta_{0}))^{2}{\rm d}r{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{2H-2}{\rm d}r\bigg){\rm d}s+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{2H-2}{\rm d}r{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{2H-2}{\rm d}r\bigg){\rm d}s+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\int_{r}^{t}(s-r)^{2H-2}{\rm d}s{\rm d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\frac{s^{2H-1}}{2H-1}|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}s+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\frac{(t-r)^{2H-1}}{2H-1}{\rm d}r.

Combining with Assumption
2.3
and the fact that
1/2<H<1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}Q_{2}^{(1)}(s)\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}(t-r)^{2H-1}{\rm d}r.

For
Q_{3}^{(1)}(s)
, we use the fractional Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality. For
H\in(1/2,1)
and
p\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C_{H,p}>0
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left|\int_{0}^{s}u_{r}{\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\right|^{p}\right]\leq C_{H,p},\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}|u_{r}|^{1/H}{\rm d}r\right)^{pH}\right].

Applying this with
p=1
and
u_{r}=|X_{r}^{(1)}-D(X_{r-\tau}^{(1)})|^{{\overline{p}}-1}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{{X_{r}^{(0)}}})\|
, and noticing the fact that Assumption
2.5
yields
\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{(0)}})\|\leq C\big(1+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{(0)}},\delta_{0})\big)\leq C
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\left|Q_{3}^{(1)}(s)\right|\right)=\overline{p}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\left|\int_{0}^{s}u_{r}{\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\right|\bigg)\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|u_{r}|^{1/H}{\rm d}r\right)^{H}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{r}^{(1)}-D(X_{r-\tau}^{(1)})|^{({\overline{p}}-1)/H}{\rm d}r\right)^{H}\right].

For any
0<\epsilon<1
, the Young’s inequality with exponents
\frac{\overline{p}}{\overline{p}-1}
and its conjugate gives

\displaystyle|X_{r}^{(1)}-D\big(X_{r-\tau}^{(1)}\big)|^{\overline{p}-1}\leq\epsilon|X_{r}^{(1)}-D\big(X_{r-\tau}^{(1)}\big)|^{\overline{p}}+C_{\epsilon},

where
C_{\epsilon}
depends on
\epsilon,\overline{p},H
. Integrating over
[0,t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}]
, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{r}^{(1)}-D\big(X_{r-\tau}^{(1)}\big)|^{(\overline{p}-1)/H}\mathrm{d}r\leq 2^{1/H-1}\epsilon^{1/H}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{r}^{(1)}-D\big(X_{r-\tau}^{(1)}\big)|^{\overline{p}/H}\mathrm{d}r+2^{1/H-1}C_{\epsilon}^{1/H}t.

Since
H\in(1/2,1)
, we use the elementary inequality to get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{r}^{(1)}-D\big(X_{r-\tau}^{(1)}\big)|^{(\overline{p}-1)/H}\mathrm{d}r\right)^{H}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1-H}\epsilon\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{r}^{(1)}-D\big(X_{r-\tau}^{(1)}\big)|^{\overline{p}/H}\mathrm{d}r\right)^{H}+2^{1-H}C_{\epsilon}t^{H}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1-H}\epsilon t^{H}\cdot\sup_{0\leq r\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{r}^{(1)}-D\big(X_{r-\tau}^{(1)}\big)|^{\overline{p}}+2^{1-H}C_{\epsilon}t^{H}.

Taking expectations and using Assumption
2.3

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq r\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{r}^{(1)}-D\big(X_{r-\tau}^{(1)}\big)|^{\overline{p}}\right)\leq C\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq r\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\left|X_{r}^{(1)}\right|^{\overline{p}}\right)\right).

Combining all estimations, we arrive at

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\left|Q_{3}^{(1)}(s)\right|\right)\leq C\epsilon\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}\left|X_{s}^{(1)}\right|^{\overline{p}}\right)+C_{\epsilon}.

Choosing
\epsilon
sufficiently small so that
C\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, and combining with the estimates for
Q_{1}^{(1)}(t)
and
Q_{2}^{(1)}(t)
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(1)})|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle C+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r.

By the Gronwall inequality together with (
3.2
), we derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(1)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle C.

The Fatou lemma then leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C.

For
i=2
, we estimate the corresponding
Q_{1}^{(2)}(s),Q_{2}^{(2)}(s)
and
Q_{3}^{(2)}(s)
as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(2)}}Q_{1}^{(2)}(s)\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r\wedge\rho_{N}^{(2)}}|X_{s}^{(2)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r\wedge\rho_{N}^{(2)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r+1\bigg],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(2)}}Q_{2}^{(2)}(s)\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r\wedge\rho_{N}^{(2)}}|X_{s}^{(2)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r\wedge\rho_{N}^{(2)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r,

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(2)}}Q_{3}^{(2)}(s)\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge\rho_{N}^{(2)}}\left|X_{s}^{(2)}\right|^{\overline{p}}\right)+C_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r\wedge\rho_{N}^{(2)}}|X_{s}^{(2)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r\wedge\rho_{N}^{(2)}}|X_{s}^{(1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r.

Consequently, choosing
\epsilon
sufficiently small so that
C\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
and using the Fatou lemma, the Gronwall inequality, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}^{(2)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C.

Finally, (
3.1
) can be shown by induction.

Step 2. Existence
Define the difference processes
\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}=X^{(k+1)}_{s}-X_{s}^{(k)}
,
\Upsilon_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}=X^{(k+1)}_{s}-D(X_{s-\tau}^{(k+1)})-X_{s}^{(k)}+D(X_{s-\tau}^{(k)})
.
By Lemma
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle|\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\leq\lambda|\Delta_{s-\tau}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}+\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{\overline{p}-1}}|\Upsilon_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}},

Taking supremum and expectation gives

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{\overline{p}}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Upsilon_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\right).

For
t\in[0,T]
, employment of the fractional Itô formula in Lemma
2.1
and the elementary inequality provides

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Upsilon_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\overline{p}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}-2}\langle\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)},b(X^{(k+1)}_{r},X^{(k+1)}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}})-b(X^{(k)}_{r},X^{(k)}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\rangle{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}{\overline{p}}({\overline{p}}-1)H(2H-1)|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}-2}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}(r-u)^{2H-2}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{u}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{u}})\|^{2}{\rm d}u\bigg){\rm d}r\bigg)

\displaystyle+{\overline{p}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\int_{0}^{s}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}-1}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\|{\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\bigg).

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)+J_{2}(t)+J_{3}(t).

Applying Assumption
2.4
and the Young’s inequality produces

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle{\overline{p}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}-2}\langle\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)},b(X^{(k+1)}_{r},X^{(k+1)}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}})-b(X^{(k)}_{r},X^{(k)}_{r-\tau},\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\rangle{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|X^{(k+1)}_{r}-D(X^{(k+1)}_{r-\tau})+X^{(k)}_{r}-D(X^{(k)}_{r-\tau})|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\bigg[|X^{(k+1)}_{r}-X^{(k)}_{r}|^{2}+|X^{(k+1)}_{r-\tau}-X^{(k)}_{r-\tau}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\bigg]^{{\overline{p}}/2}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r,

where we used the fact that
\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}|X^{(k)}_{r}-X^{(k-1)}_{r}|^{2}
. Along with Assumption
2.5
, the Young’s inequality, the Hölder inequality, and the Fubini theorem, it follows

\displaystyle J_{2}(t)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{s}{\overline{p}}({\overline{p}}-1)H(2H-1)|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}-2}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{r}(r-u)^{2H-2}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{u}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{u}})\|^{2}{\rm d}u\bigg){\rm d}r\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{r}(r-u)^{2H-2}(\mathbb{E}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}+\mathbb{E}|X^{(k)}_{u}-X^{(k-1)}_{u}|^{\overline{p}}){\rm d}u{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}(r-u)^{2H-2}{\rm d}u\bigg){\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{u}^{t}(r-u)^{2H-2}\mathbb{E}|X^{(k)}_{u}-X^{(k-1)}_{u}|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r{\rm d}u

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{r^{2H-1}}{2H-1}\mathbb{E}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-u)^{2H-1}}{2H-1}\mathbb{E}|X^{(k)}_{u}-X^{(k-1)}_{u}|^{\overline{p}}{\rm d}u.

Since
1/2<H<1
and
t\in[0,T]
, we use Assumption
2.3
to get

\displaystyle J_{2}(t)\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r.

In the similar way as dealing with
Q_{3}^{(1)}(t)
to get (
3.4
), an application of fractional BDG inequality (
3.3
) with
p=1
yields

\displaystyle J_{3}(t)=

\displaystyle{\overline{p}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left|\int_{0}^{s}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}-1}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\|{\rm d}B_{r}^{H}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}-1}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\|\right)^{1/H}{\rm d}r\right)^{H}\right]

For each
r\in[0,t]
, by the Young’s inequality, there exists
0<\epsilon<1
such that

\displaystyle|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}-1}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\|\leq\epsilon|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}}+C_{\epsilon}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\|^{{\overline{p}}}.

Assumption
2.5
implies that

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\|\leq K_{4}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}},\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\leq K_{4}(\mathbb{E}|\Delta_{r}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{2})^{1/2}.

Since
{\overline{p}}\geq 2
, using the fact that
(\mathbb{E}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{2})^{{\overline{p}}/2}\leq\mathbb{E}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{{\overline{p}}}
, then

\displaystyle|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}-1}\|\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k)}_{r}})-\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{X^{(k-1)}_{r}})\|\leq\epsilon|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}}+C_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{E}|\Delta_{r}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{2})^{{\overline{p}}/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}}+C_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}|\Delta_{r}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{{\overline{p}}}.

Noting that for
\frac{1}{H}>1
, we can apply the elementary inequality to get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\epsilon|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}}+C_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}|\Delta_{r}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{{\overline{p}}}\right)^{1/H}{\rm d}r\right)^{H}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{1-H}\epsilon\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}/H}{\rm d}r\right)^{H}+C_{\epsilon}2^{1-H}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}|\Delta_{r}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{{\overline{p}}}\right)^{1/H}{\rm d}r\right)^{H}.

Taking expectation, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}/H}{\rm d}r\right)^{H}\right]\leq t^{H}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}|\Upsilon_{r}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}}\right),

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}|\Delta_{r}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{{\overline{p}}}\right)^{1/H}{\rm d}r\right)^{H}\right]\leq Ct^{H}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|\Delta_{r}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{{\overline{p}}}{\rm d}r.

Combining the estimates together, we conclude

(3.6)

\displaystyle J_{3}(t)\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Upsilon_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{{\overline{p}}}\right)+C_{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{{\overline{p}}}\right){\rm d}r.

Finally, the estimation of

combined with the estimates for the drift terms

and
J_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Upsilon_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Upsilon_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\right)+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r.

Choosing

and absorbing the first term, it implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Upsilon_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\right)\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r.

Since singular kernels
\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r
are typically “stronger” than regular kernels

, we derive from (

) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r.

Using the Gronwall inequality leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq Ce^{Ct}\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|\Delta_{s}^{(k),(k-1)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r.

By induction, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Delta_{s}^{(k+1),(k)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq\frac{(Ce^{Ct}\Gamma(2H)t^{2H})^{k}}{\Gamma(2kH+1)}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Delta_{s}^{(1),(0)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg).

With the properties of the Gamma function and the fact that
\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}|\Delta_{s}^{(1),(0)}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C
derived by (
3.1
), we can show that
\{X_{t}^{(k)}\}
is a Cauchy sequence, then the limit, denoted by
X
, is a solution of (
2
).

Step 3. Uniqueness
Let
X_{t}
and
Y_{t}
be two solutions of (
2
) with
X_{t}=Y_{t}=\xi,\ \ t\in[-\tau,0]
.
By Lemma
2.7

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}|X_{r}-Y_{r}|^{\overline{p}}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\lambda)^{\overline{p}}}\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}|\Upsilon_{r}|^{\overline{p}},

where
\Upsilon_{r}=X_{r}-D(X_{r-\tau})-Y_{r}+D(Y_{r-\tau})
. Similar to the process of Step 2, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}-Y_{s}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|X_{s}-Y_{s}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|X_{s}-Y_{s}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r.

Further, we derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}-Y_{s}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|X_{s}-Y_{s}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg){\rm d}r.

Although the kernel
(t-r)^{2H-1}
is singular as
2H-1>0
, it is integrable on [0, T]. Therefore, applying Lemma
2.8
with
a=0
,
\beta=2H
and
\gamma=1
, we find that the generalized Mittag-Leffler function is bounded, guaranteeing a unique zero solution for the inequality. That is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|X_{s}-Y_{s}|^{\overline{p}}\bigg)=0.

Therefore,
X_{t}=Y_{t}
almost surely for all
t\in[0,T]
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

For the existence and uniqueness of the McKean-Vlasov NSDDEs, we adopt the Picard approximation method to construct a Cauchy sequence and show the existence and uniqueness of the solution. Since the super-linearly growing coefficients in our proposed model introduces additional technical complexities that demand refined analytical tools, our analysis differs fundamentally from those of related works. For McKean-Vlasov SDEs driven by standard Brownian motions (e.g.,
[
17
]
) or by fractional Brownian motions without super-linearly growing coefficients (e.g.,
[
18
,
19
]
), the uniqueness is typically proven via direct estimates of the difference between two candidate solutions. In contrast, our model involves fractional noise and super-linear coefficients, which necessitates the use of the fractional Itô formula to properly account for the non-Markovian and rough nature of the fractional Brownian motions. This application of the fractional Itô formula not only resolves the technical hurdles posed by the fractional noise but also provides a generalizable framework for analyzing McKean-Vlasov SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motions.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg){\rm d}r+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}|X_{s}^{i}-X_{s}^{i,N}|^{p}\bigg){\rm d}r

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{2H-1}\mathbb{E}\mathbb{W}_{2}^{p}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{r}^{i}},\mu_{r}^{X}){\rm d}r.

Since
\mathbb{W}_{2}^{p}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}},\mu_{s}^{X})
is controlled by the Wasserstein distance estimate(
[

]
, Theorem 1), for

, it follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathbb{W}_{2}^{p}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{s}^{i}},\mu_{s}^{X})\leq

\displaystyle C(\mathbb{E}|X_{s}^{i}|^{\overline{p}})^{p/{\overline{p}}}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}N^{-\frac{1}{2}},\ \ p>\frac{d}{2},\\
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(1+N),\ p=\frac{d}{2},\\
N^{-\frac{p}{d}},\ p\in[2,\frac{d}{2}).\end{array}\right.

Then, similar to (

2.8
and
4.2
, the desired result can be obtained.
∎

The Tamed Theta EM Scheme

In this section, we consider the tamed theta EM scheme to approximate solution of (

). Since the drift coefficient exhibits super-linear growth, we define

(5.1)

\displaystyle b_{\Delta}(x,y,\mu)=\frac{b(x,y,\mu)}{1+\Delta^{\alpha}|b(x,y,\mu)|},

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists a positive constant
K_{6}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle|b_{\Delta}(x,y,\mu)|\leq\min\{K_{6}\Delta^{-\alpha}(1+|x|+|y|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{0})),|b(x,y,\mu)|\}

for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

For
\forall x,y>0
, by (
5.1
), it is easy to see that
|b_{\Delta}(x,y,\mu)|\leq|b(x,y,\mu)|
. On the other hand, we immediately get

\displaystyle|b_{\Delta}(x,y,\mu)|=\Delta^{-\alpha}\frac{|b(x,y,\mu)|}{\Delta^{-\alpha}+|b(x,y,\mu)|}\leq\Delta^{-\alpha}\leq K_{6}\Delta^{-\alpha}(1+|x|+|y|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{0})).

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let Assumption
2.4
hold. One observes

\displaystyle\langle x-D(y)-\overline{x}+D(\overline{y}),b_{\Delta}(x,y,\mu)-b_{\Delta}(\overline{x},\overline{y},\nu)\rangle\leq K_{7}(|x-\overline{x}|^{2}+|y-\overline{y}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)),

and

\displaystyle|b(x,y,\mu)-b_{\Delta}(x,y,\mu)|^{p}\leq K_{8}\Delta^{\alpha p}(1+|x|^{2(l+1)p}+|y|^{2(l+1)p}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2p}(\mu,\delta_{0})),

where
K_{7},K_{8}
are positive constants.

Proof.

For the first inequality, we write

\displaystyle b_{\Delta}(x,y,\mu)-b_{\Delta}(\overline{x},\overline{y},\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b(x,y,\mu)-b(\overline{x},\overline{y},\nu)}{(1+\Delta^{\alpha}|b(x,y,\mu)|)(1+\Delta^{\alpha}|b(\overline{x},\overline{y},\nu)|)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta^{\alpha}[|b(\overline{x},\overline{y},\nu)|\cdot b(x,y,\mu)-|b(x,y,\mu)|\cdot b(\overline{x},\overline{y},\nu)]}{(1+\Delta^{\alpha}|b(x,y,\mu)|)(1+\Delta^{\alpha}|b(\overline{x},\overline{y},\nu)|)}.

Using Assumption
2.4
for the first term, the Lipschitz continuity of

and its growth condition from Remark

\displaystyle|b(x,y,\mu)-b_{\Delta}(x,y,\mu)|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left|b(x,y,\mu)-\frac{b(x,y,\mu)}{1+\Delta^{\alpha}|b(x,y,\mu)|}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\Delta^{\alpha}|b(x,y,\mu)|\cdot b(x,y,\mu)}{1+\Delta^{\alpha}|b(x,y,\mu)|}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta^{\alpha p}|b(x,y,\mu)|^{2p}.

Then, with the growth condition of
b
, the second part can be shown.
∎

Remark 5.8

This paper introduces a tamed theta EM scheme for a class of McKean-Vlasov SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motions. In contrast to the existing literature, the proposed framework generalizes several tamed EM schemes studied in works such as

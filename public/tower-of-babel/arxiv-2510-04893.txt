Rapid stabilization for a wave equation with boundary disturbance
Patricio Guzmán, Agustín Huerta and Hugo Parada

Abstract
In this paper we study the rapid stabilization of an unstable wave equation, in which an unknowndisturbance is located at the boundary condition. We address two different boundary conditions: Dirichlet-Dirichlet and Dirichlet-Neumann. In both cases, we design a feedback law, located at the same place as the unknown disturbance, that forces the exponential decay of the energy for any desired decay rate while suppressing the effects of the unknown disturbance. For the feedback design we employ the backstepping method, Lyapunov techniques and the sign multivalued operator. The well-posedness of the closed-loop system, which is a differential inclusion, is shown with the maximal monotone operator theory.

Lemma 2.1

Let
(w,w_{t})
be such that
w(t,\cdot)\in H^{1}(0,L)
,
w(t,0)=0
and
w_{t}(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}(0,L)
for every
t\in[0,\infty)
. Then,

\displaystyle\mbox{min}\left\{\frac{1}{8L^{2}d^{2}+1},\frac{1}{2}\right\}\int_{0}^{L}(|w_{t}|^{2}+|w_{x}|^{2})\,dx\leq 2V(t)

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{max}\left\{8L^{2}d^{2}+1,2\right\}\int_{0}^{L}(|w_{t}|^{2}+|w_{x}|^{2})\,dx,~t\in[0,\infty).

(2.18)

Proof
By hypotheses it is available Poincaré inequality
\int_{0}^{L}|w(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\leq 4L^{2}\int_{0}^{L}|w_{x}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx
for every
t\in[0,\infty)
. We prove the first inequality only (from left to right), since the proof of the second one is similar. Thanks to Poincaré and Cauchy inequalities, for every
\delta>0
we have

\displaystyle 2V(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\left(|w_{t}|^{2}+2w_{t}dw+d^{2}|w|^{2}\right)\,dx+\int_{0}^{L}|w_{x}|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{L}|w_{t}|^{2}(1-d\delta)\,dx+\int_{0}^{L}|w_{x}|^{2}\frac{1}{2}\,dx+\int_{0}^{L}|w|^{2}\left(d^{2}-\frac{d}{\delta}+\frac{1}{8L^{2}}\right)\,dx.

(2.19)

By chosing
\delta>0
so that
d^{2}-\frac{d}{\delta}+\frac{1}{8L^{2}}=0
, which is when
\delta=\frac{8L^{2}d}{8L^{2}d^{2}+1}
, it follows that
1-d\delta=\frac{1}{8L^{2}d^{2}+1}
. Accordingly, from (
2.19
) we get the first inequality because

2V(t)\geq\frac{1}{8L^{2}d^{2}+1}\int_{0}^{L}|w_{t}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{L}|w_{x}|^{2}\,dx.

The second inequality also follows by applying Poincaré and Cauchy inequalities.
\blacksquare

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{\mbox{min}\left\{\frac{1}{8L^{2}d^{2}+1},\frac{1}{2}\right\}}2V(t)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{\mbox{min}\left\{\frac{1}{8L^{2}d^{2}+1},\frac{1}{2}\right\}}e^{-2dt}2V(0)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{2}\frac{\mbox{max}\left\{8L^{2}d^{2}+1,2\right\}}{\mbox{min}\left\{\frac{1}{8L^{2}d^{2}+1},\frac{1}{2}\right\}}e^{-2dt}\|(w_{0},w_{1})\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{2}C_{2}^{2}\frac{\mbox{max}\left\{8L^{2}d^{2}+1,2\right\}}{\mbox{min}\left\{\frac{1}{8L^{2}d^{2}+1},\frac{1}{2}\right\}}e^{-2dt}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{X}^{2}\mbox{ for all }t\in[0,\infty).

(2.21)

Accordingly, we have shown (
1.4
) for (
2.2
). In other words, we have shown the feedback design part of Theorem

and (

(2.22)

Remark 2.3

(2.23)

Proposition 2.1

The operator defined by (
2.26
) is monotone.

Proof
Let
(q_{1},l_{1})
and
(q_{2},l_{2})
be in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
. For brevity in notation and only in this proof, let us set
q=q_{1}-q_{2}
and
l=l_{1}-l_{2}
. Then, in virtue of the inner product (
2.23
), we have, after two integrations by parts,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}(q_{1},l_{1})-\mathcal{A}(q_{2},l_{2}),(q_{1},l_{1})-(q_{2},l_{2})\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}(-l)^{\prime}q^{\prime}\,dx+\int_{0}^{L}\left((-q^{\prime\prime}+2dl+d^{2}q)+d(-l)\right)(l+dq)\,dx

\displaystyle=d\|q^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+d^{3}\|q\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+d\|l\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+2d^{2}\int_{0}^{L}ql\,dx+\left(-l(L)q^{\prime}(L)-dq(L)q^{\prime}(L)\right)

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}.

On the one hand, since
\left|2d^{2}\int_{0}^{L}ql\,dx\right|\leq d^{3}\|q\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}+d\|l\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}
we get that
I_{1}\geq d\|q^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}^{2}\geq 0
. On the other hand, as
dq_{j}(L)+l_{j}(L)\in-dh(L)P\,\text{sign}(q_{j}^{\prime}(L))
for
j\in\{1,2\}
, there exists
\theta_{j}\in\text{sign}(q_{j}^{\prime}(L))
such that
dq_{j}(L)+l_{j}(L)=-dh(L)P\theta_{j}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{2}=-l(L)q^{\prime}(L)-dq(L)q^{\prime}(L)

\displaystyle=-\left((l_{1}(L)+dq_{1}(L))-(l_{2}(L)+dq_{2}(L))\right)(q_{1}^{\prime}(L)-q_{2}^{\prime}(L))

\displaystyle=dh(L)P(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})(q_{1}^{\prime}(L)-q_{2}^{\prime}(L)).

We conclude that
I_{2}\geq 0
because the sign multivalued operator (
1.5
) is monotone. Accordingly, the operator
\mathcal{A}
is monotone as
I_{1}+I_{2}\geq 0
.
\blacksquare

4
Concluding remarks

In this paper we have shown the rapid stabilization (
1.4
) for the unstable wave equations (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), in which there is an unknown disturbance acting at the boundary condition. We have employed the backstepping method, Lyapunov techniques and the sign multivalued operator (
1.5
) for the design of suitable feedback laws. The assumptions made on the unknown disturbance, namely
(A1)
and
(A2)
, are the standard ones that can be found in the literature. The well-posedness of the corresponding closed-loop system has been shown with the maximal monotone operator theory. The main limitation of the approach followed in this paper is that it is needed a backstepping transformation to map the original plant into a target system so that Lyapunov techniques can be applied.

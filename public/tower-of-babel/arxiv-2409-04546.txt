Inductive description of quadratic Hom-Lie algebras with twist maps in the centroid
R. García-Delgado

Abstract
. In this work we give an inductive way to construct quadratic Hom-Lie algebras with twist maps in the centroid. We focus on those Hom-Lie algebras which are not Lie algebras. We prove that a Hom-Lie algebra of this type has trivial center and its twist map is nilpotent. We show that there exists a maximal ideal containing the kernel and the image of the twist map. Then we state an inductive way to construct this type of Hom-Lie algebras, similar to the double extension procedure for Lie algebras, and prove that any indecomposable quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with nilpotent twist map in the centroid, which is not a Lie algebra, can be constructed using this type of double extension.

Primary:
17B61, 17A30. Secondary:
17B60, 17D30.

Introduction

A Hom-Lie algebra is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T)
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space over a field
\mathbb{F}
,
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
is a skew-symmetric bilinear map and
T:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a linear map satisfying the
Hom-Lie Jacobi identity
:

[T(x),[y,z]]+[T(y),[z,x]]+[T(z),[x,y]]=0,

for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{g}
. The bilinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
is called
bracket
and
T
is called the
twist map
. When the context is clear, we refer to a Hom-Lie algebra by
\mathfrak{g}
. The study of Hom-Lie algebras begins in
[
7
]
, which were motivated by quantum deformations of algebras of vector fields.

If a Hom-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a non-degenerate and symmetric bilinear form
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
, such that
B
is
invariant
:
B(x,[y,z])=B([x,y],z)
and
T
is self adjoint:
B(T(x),y)=B(x,T(y))
, then
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be
quadratic
and
B
is called an
invariant metric
. A quadratic Hom-Lie algebra is denoted by
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T,B)
. We recover classical quadratic Lie algebras when
\mathfrak{g}
is a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra for
T=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Quadratic Hom-Lie algebras where the twist map
T
is a morphism, that is
T([x,y])=[T(x),T(y)]
, were studied in
[
3
]
.

The Medina-Revoy theorem and the double extension give a systematic way to study quadratic non-semisimple Lie algebras (see
[
9
]
). The Medina-Revoy theorem proves that an indecomposable quadratic non-simple Lie algebra can be obtained by a double extension of a quadratic Lie algebra of smaller dimension (see
[
9
]
). Another approach appears in
[
8
]
, where the authors define two canonical ideals for a quadratic Lie algebra without simple ideals and construct certain cohomology set to obtain classification results. In addition, in conformal field theory, a Sugawara construction exists for quadratic Lie algebras (see
[
5
]
).

In this work we assume that the twist map
T
, of a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T,B)
, belongs to the
centroid
, that is:
T([x,y])=[T(x),y]
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. In
[
6
]
, is obtained a characterization for these Hom-Lie algebras, which are not Lie algebras, by using central extensions of classical quadratic Lie algebras (see
Thm. 2.2
in
[
6
]
).

For classical quadratic Lie algebras, the space generated by self-adjoint centroids plays an important role to knowing the algebraic structure. If this vector space is one-dimensional then the Lie algebra is simple (see
[
2
]
). In
[
1
]
, it is shown that if this vector space is two-dimensional, then the Lie algebra has only one maximal ideal.

Quadratic Hom-Lie algebras with twist map in the centroid solve the following problem:
Under what conditions a central extension of a quadratic Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric?
. The answer given in
[
6
]
is that such a central extension admits an invariant metric if and only if
\mathfrak{g}
admits a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra structure with twist map in the centroid (see
Thm. 1.2
and
Thm. 3.1
in
[
6
]
).

In
[
6
]
, some examples of such Hom-Lie algebras that are not Lie algebras are given. There seems to be interest in obtaining similar examples for other types of Hom-Lie algebras as well (see
[
4
]
,
Example
2.4).

At this point, the question arises whether there is an inductive way to construct such quadratic Hom-Lie algebras, similar to the procedure called “double extension”. If so, another question that arises is whether any
indecomposable
quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with twist map in the centroid, can be obtained by means of this type of double extension, similar to Medina-Revoy theorem. In this work we prove that both questions have an affirmative answer (see
Thm.
2.8
and
Cor.
2.9
).

To deal with Hom-Lie algebras that are not Lie algebras, we use the Fitting’s lemma to show that the twist map
T
is nilpotent (see
Lemma
1.1
). Next we use some results of
[
6
]
, to prove that if the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
does not satisfy the classical Jacobi identity for Lie algebras, then
\mathfrak{g}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
(see
Prop.
1.2
). We will show that there exists a maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
containing the kernel and the image of
T
, and for any subspace
\mathfrak{s}
satisfying
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathcal{I}
, the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
of
\mathfrak{g}
induces a simple Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{s}
(see
Prop.
1.3
).

Unlike double extension for classical Lie algebras, the subspace
\mathfrak{s}
is not a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
(see
Prop. A.1
in
[
5
]
and
Example
in §3 below). However we can use the result in
Prop. A.1
of
[
5
]
to simplify some results obtained here, as follows: We construct a Lie algebra in the space
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus T(\mathcal{I})
, in such a way that
\mathfrak{p}
is the semidriect sum of
\mathfrak{s}
and
T(\mathcal{I})
. This implies that if
\Lambda:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathcal{I}
is the bilinear map for which
[x,y]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}+\Lambda(x,y)
, where
[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}
is in
\mathfrak{s}
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
, then
T\circ\Lambda=0
(see
Lemma
1.4
). This result simplifies some data needed to state the double extension procedure for these Hom-Lie algebras. In §3, we provide an example of a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with twist map in the centroid, which is not a Lie algebra, and
\mathfrak{s}
is not a subalgebra. All the vector spaces considered in this work are finite dimensional over a unique field
\mathbb{F}
of zero characteristic.

1.
Quadratic Hom-Lie algebras with twist maps in the centroid

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T)
be a Hom-Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. We denote by
\operatorname{ad}(x):\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, the map
\operatorname{ad}(x)(y)=[x,y]
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. A subspace
I\subset\mathfrak{g}
is an
ideal
of
\mathfrak{g}
if
T(I)\subset I
and
[\mathfrak{g},I]\subset I
.

A linear map
D:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a
derivation
if
D([x,y])=[D(x),y]+[x,D(y)]
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote by
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
, the vector space generated by derivations of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
B
be a bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
. A linear map
f:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, is
skew-symmetric
with respect to
B
, if
B(f(x),y)=-B(x,f(y))
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{o}(B)
the vector space generated by skew-symmetric maps of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
B
..

If
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T)
is a Hom-Lie algebra with twist map
T
in the centroid, then the cyclic sum
[x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]
belongs to
\operatorname{Ker}(T)
, for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Thus if
T
is invertible then
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
is a Lie algebra. In the next result we prove that for Hom-Lie algebras thT are not Lie algebras, the twist map must be nilpotent.

1.1 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T)
be a Hom-Lie algebra
with twist map
T
in the centroid. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is the direct sum of a Lie algebra and a Hom-Lie algebra with nilpotent twist map in the centroid.

Proof.

By Fitting’s lemma, there exists an integer
\ell
such that:

(1)

\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Im}(T^{\ell})\oplus\operatorname{Ker}(T^{\ell}),

where
\operatorname{Im}(T^{\ell})=\operatorname{Im}(T^{\ell+k})
and
\operatorname{Ker}(T^{\ell})=\operatorname{Ker}(T^{\ell+k})
for all
k
in
\mathbb{N}
.
Due to
T
is in the centroid,
\operatorname{Im}(T^{\ell})
and
\operatorname{Ker}(T^{\ell})
are ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Since
T|_{\operatorname{Im}\left(T^{\ell}\right)}
is invertible,
the Hom-Lie algebra structure in
\operatorname{Im}(T^{\ell})
, induced by
\mathfrak{g}
,
is that of a Lie algebra.
On the other hand,
T|_{\operatorname{Ker}\left(T^{\ell}\right)}
is nilpotent, thus from (
1
) we deduce that
\mathfrak{g}
is the direct sum of the Lie algebra
\operatorname{Im}(T^{\ell})
and the Hom-Lie algebra
(\operatorname{Ker}(T^{\ell}),[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T|_{\operatorname{Ker}(T^{\ell})})
with nilpotent twist map
T|_{\operatorname{Ker}(T^{\ell})}
in the centroid. Further, if
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric, the decomposition (
1
) corresponds to an orthogonal direct sum of
\operatorname{Im}(T^{\ell})
and
\operatorname{Ker}(T^{\ell})
.
∎

By
Prop.
1.1
, we assume that the twist map
T
is nilpotent. If
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric and
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
does not satisfy the Jacobi identity for Lie algebras, we can make another simplification.

1.2 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T,B)
be a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with nilpotent twist map
T
in the centroid. If
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
does not satisfy the Jacobi identity, then
\mathfrak{g}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.

Proof.

Let
r=\operatorname{dim}(\operatorname{Ker}(T))>0
, and consider the bilinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\text{Lie}}
in
\mathfrak{g}
defined by
[x,y]_{\text{Lie}}=T([x,y])
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\text{Lie}},B)
is a quadratic Lie algebra (see
Prop. 1.1
of
[
6
]
).

Let
\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}
be an
r
-dimensional subspace such that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\operatorname{Im}(T)
. For each
i
, let
D_{i}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be defined by
D_{i}(x)=[a_{i},x]
. Then
D_{i}
is a derivation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\text{Lie}})
(see
Prop. 1.3
in
[
6
]
).

By hypothesis, the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
does not satisfy the Jacobi identity. Under such assumption,
Prop. 1.3
of
[
6
]
, proves that for each
i
, there exists an element
x_{i}
in
\mathfrak{g}
such that the derivations
D^{\prime}_{i}=D_{i}+\operatorname{ad}_{\text{Lie}}(x_{i})
, satisfy
\operatorname{Ker}(D^{\prime}_{1})\cap\ldots\cap\operatorname{Ker}(D^{\prime}_{r})=\{0\}
. On the other hand, due to
T
is in the centroid of
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
, we have:
\operatorname{ad}_{\text{Lie}}(x_{i})=[T(x_{i}),\,\cdot\,]
, for all
i
.

If
x
satisfies
[x,\mathfrak{g}]=\{0\}
, then
D^{\prime}_{i}(x)=[a_{i},x]+[T(x_{i}),x]=0
for all
i
, hence
x
belongs to
\operatorname{Ker}(D^{\prime}_{1})\cap\ldots\cap\operatorname{Ker}(D^{\prime}_{r})=\{0\}
. Thus the Hom Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T)
has trivial center. Since
B
is non-degenerate and invariant under
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
, it follows that
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}=\{0\}
, and
\mathfrak{g}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.
∎

In the next result we prove that the Hom-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has a maximal ideal containing the kernel and the image of the twist map.

1.3 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T)
be a Hom-Lie algebra with nilpotent twist map
T
in the centroid. Then:

(i)
There is a maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\operatorname{Ker}(T)+\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset\mathcal{I}
.

(ii)
If
\,\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric and
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
does not satisfy the Jacobi identity, then the Hom-Lie algebra structure in
\mathfrak{g}/\mathcal{I}
, induced by
\mathfrak{g}
, is of a simple Lie algebra.

Proof.

(i)
Observe that
\mathfrak{g}\neq\operatorname{Ker}(T)+\operatorname{Im}(T)
, because
T
is nilpotent. Let
\mathcal{F}\!=\!\{I\subsetneq\mathfrak{g}\mid I\text{ is an ideal containing }\operatorname{Ker}(T)+\operatorname{Im}(T)\}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is not empty, being that
\operatorname{Ker}(T)+\operatorname{Im}(T)
belongs to it. A maximal element
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the conditions required.

(ii)
Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric and
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
does not satisfy the Jacobi identity. By
Prop.
1.2
,
\mathfrak{g}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. If
\operatorname{dim}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathcal{I})=1
, then
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
is a one-dimensional ideal contained in
\operatorname{Ker}(T)\cap\operatorname{Im}(T)
, for
\mathcal{I}\supset\operatorname{Ker}(T)+\operatorname{Im}(T)
. Let
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}=\mathbb{F}\,T(X)
, where
X
is in
\mathfrak{g}
. There is a map
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
such that
[T(X),Y]=\omega(Y)T(X)
, for all
Y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Using the Hom-Lie Jacobi identity and that
T
is in the centroid, we have:

\displaystyle\omega([Y,Z])T(X)=[T(X),[Y,Z]]=[[T(X),Y],Z]+[Y,[T(X),Z]]

\displaystyle=\omega(Y)\omega(Z)T(X)-\omega(Z)\omega(Y)T(X)=0,\,\text{ for all }Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Therefore
\omega([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])=\{0\}
and
\omega=0
, because
\mathfrak{g}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Then
[T(X),\mathfrak{g}]=\{0\}
, but
\mathfrak{g}
has trivial center, hence
\operatorname{dim}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathcal{I})>1
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathcal{I}
. For each pair
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
, there are
[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\Lambda(x,y)
in
\mathcal{I}
such that:

(2)

[x,y]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}+\Lambda(x,y).

Let
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{s}}:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}
be the map
(x,y)\mapsto[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}
, and
\Lambda:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathcal{I}
the map
(x,y)\mapsto\Lambda(x,y)
. We shall prove that
(\mathfrak{s},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{s}})
is a simple Lie algebra. Indeed, by (
2
), we have:

(3)

[x,[y,z]]=[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{s}}]_{\mathfrak{s}}\,\,\operatorname{mod}\,\mathcal{I},\quad\text{ for all }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{s}.

The Hom-Lie Jacobi identity and the fact that
T
is in the centroid imply that
[x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]
belongs to
\operatorname{Ker}(T)\subset\mathcal{I}
, then by (
3
) we deduce:

[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{s}}]_{\mathfrak{s}}+[y,[z,x]_{\mathfrak{s}}]_{\mathfrak{s}}+[z,[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}]_{\mathfrak{s}}\in\mathfrak{s}\cap\mathcal{I}=\{0\},

from it follows that
(\mathfrak{s},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{s}})
is a Lie algebra.

Let
J\neq\{0\}
be a subspace of
\mathfrak{s}
such that
[\mathfrak{s},J]_{\mathfrak{s}}\subset J
. From (
2
) it follows
[\mathfrak{g},J\oplus\mathcal{I}]\subset J\oplus\mathcal{I}
. Since
\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset\mathcal{I}
, then
T(J\oplus\mathcal{I})\subset\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset J\oplus\mathcal{I}
. Hence
J\oplus\mathcal{I}
is an ideal containing
\mathcal{I}
. Due to
\mathcal{I}
is maximal and
J\neq\{0\}
,
J\oplus\mathcal{I}\!=\!\mathfrak{g}
. As
J\!\subset\!\mathfrak{s}
, then
J=\mathfrak{s}
and
(\mathfrak{s},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{s}})
is a simple Lie algebra.

It is clear that
\mathfrak{g}/\mathcal{I}
with bracket
(x+\mathcal{I},y+\mathcal{I})\mapsto[x,y]+\mathcal{I}
, is isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{s},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{s}})
.
∎

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T,B)
be a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with nilpotent twist map
T
in the centroid. We assume that
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
does not satisfy the classical Jacobi identity. By
Prop.
1.3
, there exists a maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
, for which
\operatorname{Ker}(T)+\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset\mathcal{I}
and
\mathfrak{g}/\mathcal{I}
is a simple Lie algebra. Consider the bilinear map
\Lambda:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathcal{I}
given in (
2
). Although
\mathfrak{s}
is not necessarily a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
(see
Example
in §3), the fact that
\mathfrak{s}
is a simple Lie algebra leads to
T\circ\Lambda=0
.

1.4 Lemma
.

The twist map
T
and the bilinear map
\Lambda:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathcal{I}
satisfy
T\circ\Lambda=0
, that is
\Lambda(x,y)
belongs to
\operatorname{Ker}(T)
for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus T(\mathcal{I})
. We define a bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{p}}
in
\mathfrak{p}
by:

(4)

\begin{split}x,y\in\mathfrak{s},\,\quad&[x,y]_{\mathfrak{p}}=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}+T(\Lambda(x,y)),\\
x\in\mathfrak{s},\,\,\,U\in\mathcal{I},\quad&[x,T(U)]_{\mathfrak{p}}=T([x,U]),\\
U,V\in\mathcal{I},\quad&[T(U),T(V)]_{\mathfrak{p}}=T([U,V]).\end{split}

We claim that
(\mathfrak{p},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{p}})
is a Lie algebra and the linear map
\Psi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{p}
,
x+U\mapsto x+T(U)
, is a surjective algebra morphism with kernel
\operatorname{Ker}(T)
. Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{s}
and
U,V
be in
\mathcal{I}
; by (
2
) and (
4
) we have:

(5)

\begin{split}&\Psi([x+U,y+V])\\
\,&=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}+T(\Lambda(x,y))+T([x,V])+T([U,y])+T([U,V])\\
\,&=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{p}}+[x,T(V)]_{\mathfrak{p}}+[T(U),y]_{\mathfrak{p}}+[T(U),T(V)]_{\mathfrak{p}}\\
\,&=[x+T(U),y+T(V)]_{\mathfrak{p}}=[\Psi(x+U),\Psi(y+V)]_{\mathfrak{p}}.\end{split}

Then
\Psi
is a morphism of algebras. It is clear that
\Psi
is surjective.

Let
x+U
be in
\operatorname{Ker}(\Psi)
, then
x+T(U)=0
. Since
\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset\mathcal{I}
and
\mathfrak{s}\cap\mathcal{I}=\{0\}
, then
x=0
and
T(U)=0
, hence
x+U=U
belongs to
\operatorname{Ker}(T)
, thus
\operatorname{Ker}(\Psi)\subset\operatorname{Ker}(T)
.

If
x+U
is in
\operatorname{Ker}(T)
, then
x
is in
\operatorname{Ker}(T)+\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}
, so
x=0
, as
\mathfrak{s}\cap\mathcal{I}=\{0\}
and
\operatorname{Ker}(T)\subset\mathcal{I}
. Thus
U
belongs to
\operatorname{Ker}(T)
and
\Psi(U)=T(U)=0
. Then
\operatorname{Ker}(T)\subset\operatorname{Ker}(\Psi)
and
\operatorname{Ker}(T)=\operatorname{Ker}(\Psi)
.

As
T
is in the centroid, then
[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]
belongs to
\operatorname{Ker}(T)=\operatorname{Ker}(\Psi)
. Due to
\Psi
is a morphism of algebras, we get:

[\Psi(X),[\Psi(Y),\Psi(Z)]_{\mathfrak{p}}]_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Psi([X,[Y,Z]])\,\text{ for all }X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}.

From it follows that
(\mathfrak{p},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{p}})
is a Lie algebra, as
\Psi
is surjective. We claim that
T\circ\Lambda=0
. Let
x
be in
\mathfrak{s}
and
U
be in
\mathcal{I}
. The map
\Upsilon:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{s}
, defined by
\Upsilon(x+T(U))=x
, is a surjective Lie algebra morphism. Then
\mathfrak{s}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{p}
(see
[
5
]
,
Prop. A.1
). By (
4
), it follows that
[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}]_{\mathfrak{p}}=[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}]_{\mathfrak{s}}+T(\Lambda(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}))\subset\mathfrak{s}
, then
T(\Lambda(\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}))
lies in
\mathfrak{s}\cap\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset\mathfrak{s}\cap\mathcal{I}=\{0\}
, hence
T\circ\Lambda=0
.

∎

2.
Indecomposable quadratic Hom-Lie algebras with nilpotent twist map in the centroid

A quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g},
is decomposable if there is a proper ideal
I\neq\{0\}
such that
\mathfrak{g}=I\oplus I^{\perp}
, otherwise it is indecomposable.

From now on we assume that
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T,B)
is an indecomposable quadratic Hom-Lie algebra, with nilpotent twist map
T
in the centroid, and
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
does not satisfy the Jacobi identity for Lie algebras.

By
Prop.
1.3
, there is a maximal ideal
\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathfrak{g}/\mathcal{I}
is a simple Lie algebra and
\operatorname{Ker}(T)+\operatorname{Im}(T)\!\subset\!\mathcal{I}
. Observe that
\mathcal{I}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\!\neq\!0
, as
\mathfrak{g}
is indecomposable; thus
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\subset\mathcal{I}
, because
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
is minimal.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a subspace such that
\mathcal{I}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
. Then
\mathfrak{h}
is non-degenerate subspace,
\mathfrak{g}\!=\!\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\!\subset\!\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
. Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a subspace such that
\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}\!=\!\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, then
\mathfrak{g}\!=\!\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
. Since
B
is non-degenerate, by the Witt decomposition we may assume that
\mathfrak{s}
is isotropic. We know that
\mathfrak{s}
is the underlying vector space of a simple Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{s},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{s}})
for which the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{p}\!=\!\mathfrak{s}\oplus T(\mathcal{I})
is the semidirect sum of
\mathfrak{s}
and
T(\mathcal{I})
. This leads us to
T\circ\Lambda=0
, where
[x,y]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}+\Lambda(x,y)
,
[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}
belongs to
\mathfrak{s}
and
\Lambda(x,y)
belongs to
\mathcal{I}
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
(see
Lemma
1.4
).

Due to
\Lambda(x,y)
belongs to
\mathcal{I}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, then there are
\lambda(x,y)
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mu(x,y)
in
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
such that:
\Lambda(x,y)=\lambda(x,y)+\mu(x,y)
. Let
\lambda:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{h}
be the bilinear map
(x,y)\mapsto\lambda(x,y)
, and
\mu:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
the bilinear map
(x,y)\mapsto\mu(x,y)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
.

Let
u
be in
\mathfrak{h}
; since
[x,u]
belongs to
\mathcal{I}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, there are
\bar{\rho}(x,u)
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\bar{\tau}(x,u)
in
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, such that:
[x,u]=\bar{\rho}(x,u)+\bar{\tau}(x,u)
. Let
\rho:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
be the map defined by
\rho(x)(u)=\bar{\rho}(x,u)
and
\tau:\mathfrak{s}\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{h};\mathcal{I}^{\perp})
the map defined by
\tau(x)(u)=\bar{\tau}(x,u)
.

Let
\alpha
be in
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
. Due to
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, there is a map
\sigma:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{I}^{\perp})
, such that
[x,\alpha]=\sigma(x)(\alpha)
.

Let
u,v
be in
\mathfrak{h}
. Due to
[u,v]
belongs to
\mathcal{I}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, there are
[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\gamma(u,v)
in
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
such that:
[u,v]=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\gamma(u,v)
. Let
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
be the bilinear map
(u,v)\mapsto[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}
, and
\gamma:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
the bilinear map
(u,v)\mapsto\gamma(u,v)
.

Therefore, the maps
\lambda,\mu,\rho,\tau,\sigma,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\gamma
describe the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, as follows:

(6)

\begin{split}x,y\in\mathfrak{s},\,\quad&[x,y]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}+\lambda(x,y)+\mu(x,y),\\
x\in\mathfrak{s},\,u\in\mathfrak{h},\quad&[x,u]=\rho(x)(u)+\tau(x)(u),\\
x\in\mathfrak{s},\,\alpha\in\mathcal{I}^{\perp},\quad&[x,\alpha]=\sigma(x)(\alpha),\\
u,v\in\mathfrak{h},\quad&[u,v]=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\gamma(u,v).\end{split}

Since
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\subset\mathcal{I}
then
[\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}^{\perp}]=\{0\}
, as
B
is invariant and non-degenerate. In particular,
[\mathfrak{h},\mathcal{I}^{\perp}]=\{0\}
, and
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
is abelian.

2.1 Lemma
.

There is a bijective linear map
\xi:\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
such that
\xi\circ\sigma(x)=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{s}}(x)^{\ast}\circ\xi
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{s}
.

Proof.

We define
\xi
by
\xi(\alpha)(x)=B(\alpha,x)
, for all
\alpha
in
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
and
x
in
\mathfrak{s}
. The map
\xi
is bijective, as
B
is non-degenerate. Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{s}
and
\alpha
be in
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
. Using that
B
is invariant and
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\subset\mathcal{I}
, we get:

\displaystyle\xi(\sigma(x)(\alpha))(y)=B([x,\alpha],y)=-B(\alpha,[x,y])

\displaystyle=-B(\alpha,[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})=-\xi(\alpha)(\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{s}}(x)(y))=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ast}(x)\circ\xi(\alpha)(y).

Hence,
\xi\circ\sigma(x)=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ast}(x)\circ\xi
. Then

\sigma:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{I}^{\perp})
is a representation isomorphic to the co-adjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{s}^{\ast})
.
∎

Let
B_{\mathfrak{h}}=B|_{\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}}
, then
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}
, as
\mathfrak{h}
is a non-degenerate subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
. Thus, the invariant metric
B
has the following description according to the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
:

(7)

B(x+u+\alpha,y+v+\beta)=\xi(\alpha)(y)+\xi(\beta)(x)+B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,v),

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
,
u,v
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\alpha,\beta
in
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
.

Let
\mu_{\xi}=\xi\circ\mu:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
. Using that
B
is invariant we get:

(8)

\begin{split}&B(x,[y,z])=B(x,\mu(y,z))=\xi(\mu(y,z))(x)=\mu_{\xi}(y,z)(x)\\
&=B([x,y],z)=\mu_{\xi}(x,y)(z),\quad\text{ for all }x,y,zin\mathfrak{s}.\end{split}

Then
\mu_{\xi}(x,y)(z)=\mu_{\xi}(y,z)(x)
. For each
x
in
\mathfrak{s}
, define the map
\tau_{\xi}(x)=\xi\circ\tau(x):\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
. As
B
is invariant under
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
, we get:

\displaystyle B([x,u],y)=B(\tau(x)(u),y)=\xi(\tau(x)(u))(y)=\tau_{\xi}(x)(u)(y)

\displaystyle=-B([x,y],u)=-B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,y),u).

Then,

(9)

\tau_{\xi}(x)(u)(y)=-B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,y),u),\,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{s}\,\text{ and }u\in\mathfrak{h}.

Define the map
\gamma_{\xi}=\xi\circ\gamma:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
. Since
B
is invariant we get:

\displaystyle B([x,u],v)=B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(x)(u),v)=B(x,[u,v])

\displaystyle=B(x,\gamma(u,v))=\xi(\gamma(u,v))(x)=\gamma_{\xi}(u,v)(x).

Hence,

(10)

B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(x)(u),v)=\gamma_{\xi}(u,v)(x),\,\,\text{ for all }u,v\in\mathfrak{h},\text{ and }x\in\mathfrak{s}.

Now we shall give a description of the twist map
T
according to the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
. Let
x
be in
\mathfrak{s}
, since
\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset\mathcal{I}
(see
Lemma
1.3
), then
T(x)
lies in
\mathcal{I}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
. Hence there are
\phi(x)
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\varphi(x)
in
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
such that
T(x)=\phi(x)+\varphi(x)
. Let
\phi:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{h}
be the map
x\mapsto\phi(x)
, and
\varphi:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
the map
x\mapsto\varphi(x)
.

Let
u
be in
\mathfrak{h}
. Since
T(u)
lies in
I=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, there are
\Theta(u)
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
L(u)
in
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
such that
T(u)=\Theta(u)+L(u)
. Let
\Theta:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
be the map
u\mapsto\Theta(u)
, and
L:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
the map
u\mapsto L(u)
.

Due to
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\subset\operatorname{Ker}(T)
, the twist map
T
has the following description:

(11)

\begin{split}x\in\mathfrak{s},\quad&T(x)=\phi(x)+\varphi(x),\\
u\in\mathfrak{h},\quad&T(u)=\Theta(u)+L(u).\end{split}

Define the map
\varphi_{\xi}\!=\!\xi\circ\!\varphi:\mathfrak{s}\!\to\!\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
. As
T
is self-adjoint, by (
7
) we get:

(
A
)

\varphi_{\xi}(x)(y)=B(\varphi(x),y)=B(x,\varphi(y))=\varphi_{\xi}(y)(x),

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
. Similarly, define
L_{\xi}=\xi\circ L:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
, then

(12)

L_{\xi}(u)(x)\!=\!B(L(u),x)\!=\!B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,\phi(x)),\,\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{s}\,\text{ and }u\in\mathfrak{h}.

2.2 Lemma
.

The tuple
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},\Theta,B_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with nilpotent twist map
\Theta
in the centroid.

Proof.

By (
11
),
\Theta:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
is a nilpotent map, because
T
is nilpotent and
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\subset\operatorname{Ker}(T)
. Let
u,v,w
be in
\mathfrak{h}
. Comparing the components in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
of
T([u,v])
and
[T(u),v]
, by (
6
) and (
11
), we deduce that
T([u,v])\!=\![T(u),v]
is equivalent to:

(13)

\displaystyle\Theta([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})=[\Theta(u),v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\,\,\,\text{ and }

(14)

\displaystyle L([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})=\gamma(\Theta(u),v).

The expression (
13
) says that
\Theta
is in the centroid of
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}})
.

By (
6
), it follows that
[u,[v,w]]=[u,[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
. We apply
T
and we use the facts that
\Theta
is in the centroid of
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}
,
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\subset\operatorname{Ker}(T)
and that
[\mathfrak{h},\mathcal{I}^{\perp}]=\{0\}
, to get:

[T(u),[v,w]]=T([u,[v,w]])=[\Theta(u),[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}\,\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{I}^{\perp}.

Due to
[T(u),[v,w]]+[T(v),[w,u]]+[T(w),[u,v]]=0
, then:

[\Theta(u),[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\Theta(v),[w,u]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\Theta(w),[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathcal{I}^{\perp}=\{0\}.

Hence,
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},\Theta)
is a Hom-Lie algebra. In addition,

B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\Theta(u),v)=B(T(u),v)=B(u,T(v))=B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,\Theta(v)),

then
\Theta
is
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
-self adjoint. Since
B
is invariant under
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
and
B(\mathfrak{h},\mathcal{I}^{\perp})=\{0\}
, we obtain:
B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,[v,w]_{\mathfrak{h}})\!=\!B(u,[v,w])\!=\!B([u,v],w)\!=\!B_{\mathfrak{h}}([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},w)
. Then
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
is invariant under
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},\Theta,B_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with nilpotent twist map
\Theta
in the centroid.
∎

Let
x
be in
\mathfrak{s}
and
u
be in
\mathfrak{h}
. Comparing the components in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, from (
6
) and (
11
), we deduce that
T([x,u])=[T(x),u]=[x,T(u)]
is equivalent to:

(
B
)

\displaystyle\Theta\circ\rho(x)=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x))=\rho(x)\circ\Theta,\,\,\,\text{ and }

(15)

\displaystyle L(\rho(x)(u))=\gamma(\phi(x),u)=\tau(x)(\Theta(u))+\sigma(x)(L(u)).

Using that
T\circ\Lambda=0
(see
Prop.
1.4
), we can simplify (
15
).

2.3 Claim
.

\tau(x)(\Theta(u))=0
for all
x
in
\mathfrak{s}
and
u
in
\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

Since
\Lambda(x,y)=\lambda(x,y)+\mu(x,y)
, where
\lambda(x,y)
is in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mu(x,y)
is in
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\subset\operatorname{Ker}(T)
, then
T(\Lambda(x,y))=T(\lambda(x,y))=0
. By (
9
) we have:

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}(x)(\Theta(u))(y)

\displaystyle=-B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,y),\Theta(u))=-B(\lambda(x,y),T(u))

\displaystyle=-B(T(\lambda(x,y)),u)=0.

Then,
\tau_{\xi}(x)(\Theta(u))=\xi(\tau(x)(\Theta(u)))=0
; hence
\tau(x)(\Theta(u))=0
. Therefore, (
15
) can be written as:

(16)

L(\rho(x)(u))=\gamma(\phi(x),u)=\sigma(x)(L(u)).

∎

Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{s}
; comparing the components in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, from (
6
) and (
11
), we see that
T([x,y])=[T(x),y]=[x,T(y)]
is equivalent to:

(
C
)

\displaystyle\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})=\rho(x)(\phi(y))=-\rho(y)(\phi(x)),\,\,\,\text{ and }

(17)

\displaystyle\varphi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})=\tau(x)(\phi(y))+\sigma(x)(\varphi(y)).

Using that
T\circ\Lambda=0
(see
Prop.
1.4
), we can simplify (
17
).

2.4 Claim
.

\tau(x)(\phi(y))=0
for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z
be in
\mathfrak{s}
. By (
9
), we have:

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}(x)(\phi(y))(z)

\displaystyle=-B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,z),\phi(y))=-B(\lambda(x,z),\phi(y))

\displaystyle=-B(\lambda(x,z),T(y))=-B(T(\lambda(x,z)),y)=0.

Then,
\tau_{\xi}(x)(\phi(y))=\xi(\tau(x)(\phi(y)))=0
; hence,
\tau(x)(\phi(y))=0
. Therefore, (
17
) can be written as:

(
D
)

\varphi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})=\sigma(x)(\varphi(y)),\,\,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{s}.

∎

2.5 Lemma
.

Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{s}
and
u
in
\mathfrak{h}
. Then,
[T(x),[y,u]]+[T(y),[u,x]]+[T(u),[x,y]]=0
, is equivalent to:

(18)

\displaystyle\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})\circ\Theta=\rho(x)\circ\rho(y)\circ\Theta-\rho(y)\circ\rho(x)\circ\Theta,\,\text{ and }

(19)

\displaystyle\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})(u)-\rho(x)\circ\rho(y)(u)+\rho(y)\circ\rho(x)(u)\in\operatorname{Ker}(L).

Proof.

As
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\subset\operatorname{Ker}(T)
and
[\mathfrak{h},\mathcal{I}^{\perp}]=\{0\}
, by (
B
) and (
16
) it follows:

\displaystyle]

\displaystyle=[T(x),\rho(y)(u)]=[\phi(x),\rho(y)(u)]

\displaystyle=[\phi(x),\rho(y)(u)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\gamma(\phi(x),\rho(y)(u))

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\rho(x)\circ\rho(y)(\Theta(u))}_{\in\mathfrak{h}}+\underbrace{L(\rho(x)\circ\rho(y)(u))}_{\in\mathcal{I}^{\perp}}.

Similarly:

[T(y),[u,x]]=\underbrace{-\rho(y)\circ\rho(x)(\Theta(u))}_{\in\mathfrak{h}}-\underbrace{L(\rho(y)\circ\rho(x)(u))}_{\in\mathcal{I}^{\perp}}.

Using that
T
is in the centroid and
T\circ\Lambda=0
, we get:

\displaystyle]

\displaystyle=[T(u),[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}]=[u,T([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})]=[u,\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})]

\displaystyle=[u,\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\gamma(u,\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}))

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})(\Theta(u))}_{\in\mathfrak{h}}-\underbrace{L(\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})(u))}_{\in\mathcal{I}^{\perp}}.

Comparing the components in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
of
[T(x),[y,u]]
,
[T(y),[u,x]]
and
[T(u),[x,y]]
, we obtain (
18
) and (
19
).
∎

2.6 Lemma
.

Let
x
be in
\mathfrak{s}
and
u,v
be in
\mathfrak{h}
. Then,
[T(x),[u,v]]+[T(u),[v,x]]+[T(v),[x,u]]=0
, is equivalent to:

(20)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x))([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})=[\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x)),u]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x))(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}},\,\text{ and }

(21)

\displaystyle\rho(x)([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})-[\rho(x)(u),v]_{\mathfrak{h}}-[u,\rho(x)(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}\in\operatorname{Ker}(L).

Observe that (
20
) says that
\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x))
is a derivation of
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}})
. In addition, by (
B
), the expression (
20
) can be written as:

(22)

\rho(x)([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})-[\rho(x)(u),v]_{\mathfrak{h}}-[u,\rho(x)(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}\in\operatorname{Ker}(\Theta).

Proof.

Using (
14
), (
B
), and the arguments of
Lemma
2.5
,
we get:

\displaystyle]

\displaystyle=-[T(u),\rho(x)(v)]=-[u,T(\rho(x)(v))]=-[u,\Theta(\rho(x)(v))]

\displaystyle=-[u,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x)(v))]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\gamma(u,\Theta(\rho(x)(v)))

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-[u,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x)(v))]_{\mathfrak{h}}}_{\in\mathfrak{h}}-\underbrace{L([u,\rho(x)(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}})}_{\in\mathcal{I}^{\perp}}.

Similarly,

[T(v),[x,u]]=-\underbrace{[\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x)(u)),v]_{\mathfrak{h}}}_{\in\mathfrak{h}}-\underbrace{L([\rho(x)(u),v]_{\mathfrak{h}})}_{\in\mathcal{I}^{\perp}}.

By (
15
) we obtain:

\displaystyle]

\displaystyle=[T(x),[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}]=[\phi(x),[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}]

\displaystyle=[\phi(x),[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\gamma(\phi(x),[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x))([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})}_{\in\mathfrak{h}}+\underbrace{L(\rho(x)([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}))}_{\in\mathcal{I}^{\perp}}.

Comparing the components in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
of
[T(u),[v,x]]
,
[T(v),[x,u]]
and
[T(x),[u,v]]
, we obtain (
20
) and (
21
).
∎

We gather the expressions (
18
), (
20
) and (
22
), in the following result.

2.7 Lemma
.

The map
\rho^{\prime}:\mathfrak{s}\to\operatorname{Der}(\operatorname{Im}(\Theta))
,
x\mapsto\rho(x)|_{\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)}
is a Lie algebra representation of
(\mathfrak{s},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{s}})
.

Proof.

Let
x
be in
\mathfrak{s}
. We know by (
B
) that
\rho(x)
and
\Theta
commute, then
\rho(x)(\operatorname{Im}(\Theta))\subset\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)
. Let
u,v
be in
\mathfrak{h}
; applying
\Theta
to (
22
), we obtain:

(23)

\rho(x)([\Theta(u),v]_{\mathfrak{h}})=[\rho(x)(\Theta(u)),v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\Theta(u),\rho(x)(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

Replace
v
by
\Theta(v)
in (
23
) and observe that
\rho(x)([\Theta(u),\Theta(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}})=\rho^{\prime}(x)([\Theta(u),\Theta(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}})
, as
\Theta
is in the centroid of
\mathfrak{h}
. Then we deduce from (
23
) that
\rho^{\prime}(x)=\rho(x)|_{\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)}
is a derivation of
\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)
.

Let
w=\rho(x)(\Theta(u))=\Theta(\rho(x)(u))
, which belongs to
\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)
. Then
\rho(x)(w)=\rho^{\prime}(x)(w)=\rho^{\prime}(x)\circ\rho^{\prime}(y)(\Theta(u))
. Thus by (
18
) we have:

\rho^{\prime}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})(\Theta(u))=\rho^{\prime}(x)\circ\rho^{\prime}(y)(\Theta(u))-\rho^{\prime}(y)\circ\rho^{\prime}(x)(\Theta(u)),

hence
\rho^{\prime}:\mathfrak{s}\to\operatorname{Der}(\operatorname{Im}(\Theta))
is a Lie algebra representation of
\mathfrak{s}
.
∎

Let us make the identifications
\mathcal{I}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
and:

\sigma=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ast},\quad\mu=\mu_{\xi},\quad\tau=\tau_{\xi},\quad\gamma=\gamma_{\xi},\quad\mathcal{I}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{s}^{\ast},\quad\varphi=\varphi_{\xi},\quad L=L_{\xi},

induced by
\xi:\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
(see
Lemma
2.1
), in order to state a similar construction to the double extension for classical Lie algebras.

We may notice that there is a lot of data to deal with because unlike classical Lie algebras, we have a linear map
T
and the simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
is not a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
(see
[
5
]
,
Proposition A.1
and
Example
below). However, we have proved that
T\circ\Lambda=0
, which simplified some expressions (see
Claims (
2.3
)
and (
2.4
)).

2.8 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}},\Theta,B_{\mathfrak{h}})
be a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with twist map
\Theta
is in the centroid. Let
(\mathfrak{s},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{s}})
be a Lie algebra, and suppose that there are linear maps:

\phi:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{h},\quad\varphi:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast},\quad\rho:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{o}(B_{\mathfrak{h}}),\quad\tau:\mathfrak{s}\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{h};\mathfrak{s}^{\ast})

and a skew-symmetric bilinear map
\mu:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
, satisfying:

(A)

\varphi(x)(y)=\varphi(y)(x)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
.

(B)

\Theta\circ\rho(x)\!=\!\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x))\!=\!\rho(x)\circ\Theta
belongs to
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{s}
.

(C)

\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})=\rho(x)(\phi(y))
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
.

(D)

\varphi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}})=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ast}(x)(\varphi(y))
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
.

(E)
The map
\rho^{\prime}:\mathfrak{s}\to\operatorname{Der}(\operatorname{Im}(\Theta))
,
\rho^{\prime}(x)=\rho(x)|_{\operatorname{Im}(\Theta)}
, is a Lie algebra representation of
(\mathfrak{s},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{s}})
.

(F)

\!\tau(x)\!\circ\!\Theta\!=\!\tau(x)\!\circ\!\phi\!=\!0
, and
\tau(x)(u)(x)\!=\!0
, for all
x\in\mathfrak{s}
and
u\in\mathfrak{h}
.

(G)

\mu(x,y)(z)=\mu(y,z)(x)
for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{s}
.

Then in the vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
, there exists a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T,B)
with twist map
T
in the centroid.

Proof.

Let
L:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
be defined by
L(u)(x)=B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,\phi(x))
, for all
u
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
x
in
\mathfrak{s}
(see (
12
)). We define
T:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
as in (
11
):

(24)

\begin{split}x\in\mathfrak{s},\quad&T(x)=\phi(x)+\varphi(x),\\
u\in\mathfrak{h},\quad&T(u)=\Theta(u)+L(u),\\
\alpha\in\mathfrak{s}^{\ast},\quad&T(\alpha)=0,\end{split}

Let
x,y
be in
\mathfrak{s}
. The map
u\mapsto-\tau(x)(u)(y)
, belongs to
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
. Due to
B_{\mathfrak{h}}
is non-degenerate, there exists a unique element
\lambda(x,y)
in
\mathfrak{h}
such that
-\tau(x)(u)(y)=B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\lambda(x,y),u)
, for all
u
in
\mathfrak{h}
. Let
\lambda:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{h}
be defined by
(x,y)\mapsto\lambda(x,y)
(see (
9
)).

Consider
\gamma:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
defined by:
\gamma(u,v)(x)=B_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho(x)(u),v)
, for all
u,v
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
x
in
\mathfrak{s}
(see (
10
)). Let
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, be the bracket defined as in (
6
):

(25)

\begin{split}x,y\in\mathfrak{s},\,\quad&[x,y]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}+\lambda(x,y)+\mu(x,y),\\
x\in\mathfrak{s},\,u\in\mathfrak{h},\quad&[x,u]=\rho(x)(u)+\tau(x)(u),\\
x\in\mathfrak{s},\,\alpha\in\mathfrak{s}^{\ast},\quad&[x,\alpha]=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ast}(x)(\alpha),\\
u,v\in\mathfrak{h},\quad&[u,v]=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\gamma(u,v),\end{split}

We define a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
B
in
\mathfrak{g}
, by:

(26)

B(x+u+\alpha,y+v+\beta)=\alpha(y)+\beta(x)+B_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,v),

where
x,y
are in
\mathfrak{s}
,
u,v
are in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\alpha,\beta
are in
\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
. The proof that
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T,B)
is a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with twist map
T
in the centroid, consists only on reversing the process we did previously. For that reason we put
(A)
,
(B)
,
(C)
,
(D)
to highlight the hypothesis needed. The hypothesis in
(E)
corresponds to
Lemma
2.7
. The hypothesis in
(F)
leads to
T(\lambda(x,y))=0
(see
Prop.
1.4
). The hypothesis in
(G)
corresponds to the fact that
\mu
is cyclic (see (
8
)).

∎

2.9 Corollary
.

Any indecomposable quadratic Hom-Lie algebra, which is not a Lie algebra, having nilpotent twist map in the centroid, can be constructed as in
Thm.
2.8
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an indecomposable quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with nilpotent twist map in the centroid, such that
\mathfrak{g}
is not a Lie algebra. Then
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
, where
\mathcal{I}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathcal{I}^{\perp}
is a maximal ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{s}
is a simple Lie algebra (see
Lemma
1.3
). Consider the map
\Omega:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
, defined by
\Omega(x+u+\alpha)=x+u+\xi(\alpha)
, where the Hom-Lie algebra on
\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
is given by
Thm.
2.8
and
\xi:\mathcal{I}^{\perp}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
is the map of
Lemma
2.1
. Then
\Omega
is an isometry.
∎

3.
Example

Let
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{F})=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{e_{11}-e_{22},e_{22}-e_{33},e_{12},e_{13},e_{21},e_{23},e_{31},e_{32}\}
be the space of
3\times 3
matrices over
\mathbb{F}
having trace zero, where
e_{ij}
is the matrix whose entry
(i,j)
is equal to 1 and zero elsewhere. We consider
\mathfrak{s}
with the usual bracket
[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}=xy-yx
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{s}
.

Let us write
x_{1}=e_{11}-e_{22}
,
x_{2}=e_{22}-e_{33}
,
x_{3}=e_{12}
,
x_{4}=e_{13}
,
x_{5}=e_{21}
,
x_{6}=e_{23}
,
x_{7}=e_{31}
and
x_{8}=e_{32}
.

Let
\mu_{ijk}
be in
\mathbb{F}
satisfying the cyclic and antisymmetric conditions:

\mu_{ijk}=\mu_{jki}=-\mu_{jik},\quad\text{ for all }1\leq i,j,k\leq 8.

Let
\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{8}\}
, where
\alpha_{j}(x_{k})=\delta_{jk}
. We define the bilinear map
\mu:\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
, by
\mu(x_{j},x_{k})=\mu_{1jk}\alpha_{i}+\ldots+\mu_{8jk}\alpha_{i}
, for all
1\leq j,k\leq 8
. Then
\mu
is skew-symmetric and
\mu(x_{j},x_{k})(x_{i})=\mu_{ijk}=\mu_{jki}=\mu(x_{k},x_{i})(x_{j})
, for all
i,j,k
. In
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
, consider the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
defined by:
[x+\alpha,y+\beta]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{s}}+\mu(x,y)+\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ast}(x)(\beta)-\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\ast}(y)(\alpha)
, and the bilinear form
B
, by
B(x+\alpha,y+\beta)=\alpha(y)+\beta(x)
, where
x,y
are in
\mathfrak{s}
and
\alpha,\beta
are in
\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
.

Let
K_{\mathfrak{s}}
be the Killing form of
\mathfrak{s}
and let
T:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be the map defined by
T(x+\alpha)=K_{\mathfrak{s}}(x,\,\cdot\,)
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{s}
and
\alpha
in
\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
. By
Thm.
2.8
, the triple
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],T,B)
is a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with twist map
T
in the centroid. Observe that in this case,
\mathfrak{h}=0
and
\varphi:\mathfrak{s}\to\mathfrak{s}^{\ast}
,
x\mapsto K_{\mathfrak{s}}(x,\,\cdot\,)
satisfies
(A)
and
(D)
of
Thm.
2.8
.

We let
\mu_{123}=0
,
\mu_{\ell,1,3}=\mu_{\ell,2,3}=0
, for
5\leq\ell\leq 8
and
\mu_{234}\neq 0
, then
\mathfrak{g}
is not a Lie algebra. A straightforward calculation shows that:

\displaystyle[x_{1},[x_{2},x_{3}]]=-2x_{3}-\mu_{234}\alpha_{4},

\displaystyle[x_{2},[x_{3},x_{1}]]=2x_{3}-2\mu_{234},\quad\text{ and }\quad[x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2}]]=0.

Then,
[x_{1},[x_{2},x_{3}]]+[x_{2},[x_{3},x_{1}]]+x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2}]]=-3\mu_{234}\alpha_{4}\neq 0
.

3.1 Remark
.

This example shows that
\mathfrak{s}
is not a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, as
\mu\neq 0
. This is a main difference from classical Lie algebras where
\mathfrak{s}
is always a subalgebra (see
Prop. A.1
in
[
5
]
). We can carry out a similar example with
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Since
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
is the only simple Lie algebra for which
\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})=\mathfrak{o}(K_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}})
, then we would have
[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}]\subset\mathfrak{s}
.

Di-graphs with tightly connected clusters: Effective Graph Laplacians and resolvent convergence
Christian Koke

Abstract
. In this note, we study Laplacians on graphs for which connectivity within certain sub-graphs tends to infinity. Our main focus are graphs sharing a common node set on which edge weights within certain clusters grow to infinity. As intra-cluster connectivity increases, we show that the corresponding graph Laplacians converge—in the resolvent sense—to an effective graph Laplacian. This effective limit Laplacian is defined on a coarsened graph, where each highly connected cluster is collapsed into a single node. In the undirected setting, the effective Laplacian arises naturally from agregating over tightly connected clusters. In the directed case, the limiting graph structure depends on the precise manner in which connectivity increases; with the corresponding effects mediated by the left and right kernel structure of the Laplacian restricted to high-connectivity clusters. Our results shed light on the emergence of coarse-grained dynamics in large-scale networks and contribute to spectral graph theory of directed graphs.

2.2.
Associated Hilbert spaces and Laplacians

On the graph
G
, we introduce the space of functions

square summable with respect to the measure

(2.1)

\displaystyle\ell^{2}(G)=\left\{f:V\rightarrow\mathds{C}:\sum\limits_{v\in V}|f(v)|^{2}m(v)<\infty\right\}.

This is a Hilbert space, with inner product given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}(V)}=\sum_{x\in V}\overline{f(x)}g(x)m(x)
.
In the generically directed setting, we then introduce the in- and out-degree Laplacians on
G
via their respective actions on elements
f\in\ell^{2}(V)
:

Definition 2.4
.

The
in
-degree (
L^{-}
) and
out
-degree (
L^{+}
) graph Laplacians are:

(2.2)

\displaystyle[L^{-}f](x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(x)}\left(d^{-}(x)f(x)-\sum\limits_{y\in V}a(x,y)f(y)\right),

(2.3)

\displaystyle[L^{+}f](x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(x)}\left(d^{+}(x)f(x)-\sum\limits_{y\in V}a(x,y)f(y)\right).

Here we have made use of the in-degree
d^{-}
and out-degree
d^{+}
as:

(2.4)

\displaystyle d^{-}(v)=\sum\limits_{i\in V}a(v,i),\quad d^{+}(v)=\sum\limits_{j\in V}a(j,v).

We will here focus on the setting where these operators of Definition
2.4
are indeed defined on all of
\ell^{2}(G)
.
To ensure the necessary boundedness, we make the following assumption on interplay of degree and node-mass throughout the paper:

Assumption 2.5
.

In- and out-degrees are uniformly bounded relative to node-mass:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\exists C>0:\quad d^{-}(v)\leq Cm(v)\quad\text{and}\quad d^{+}(v)\leq Cm(v),\quad\forall v\in V.

This assumption generalizes standard boundedness characterizations in the undirected setting
[
35
]
and indeed ensures that the operators of Definition
2.4
are bounded:

Proposition 2.6
.

With
C
as in Assumption
2.5
, we have
\|L^{-}\|,\|L^{+}\|\leq 2C
.

Proof.

We will conduct the proof for
L^{-}
; the corresponding arguments for the out-degree Laplacian proceed in complete analogy. We can split
L^{-}
as
L^{-}=D^{-}-A
into its diagonal and off-diagonal action. For the action of
D^{-}
, we find:

(2.6)

\|D^{-}f\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{x\in V}\frac{1}{m(x)}[d^{-}(x)]^{2}|f(x)|^{2}\leq C^{2}\sum\limits_{x\in V}|f(x)|^{2}m(x)\leq C^{2}\|f\|^{2}.

The remaining off-diagonal operator
A
acts on
\ell^{2}(G)
as an integral operator with kernel

(2.7)

\displaystyle k(x,y)=\frac{a(x,y)}{m(x)m(y)}.

The Schur test (c.f. e.g.
[
19
, Theorem 5.2]
) allows us to bound
\|A\|\leq\sqrt{K_{1}K_{2}}
, with

(2.8)

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{x\in V}\int_{V}|k(x,y)|dm(y)=\sup\limits_{x\in V}\left(\sum\limits_{y\in V}\left|\frac{a(x,y)}{m(x)m(y)}\right|m(y)\right)=\sup\limits_{x\in V}\left(\frac{d^{-}(x)}{m(x)}\right),

(2.9)

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{y\in V}\int_{V}|k(x,y)|dm(x)=\sup\limits_{y\in V}\left(\sum\limits_{x\in V}\left|\frac{a(x,y)}{m(x)m(y)}\right|m(x)\right)=\sup\limits_{y\in V}\left(\frac{d^{+}(y)}{m(y)}\right).

Since both
K_{1},K_{2}\leq C
, we thus find
A\leq\sqrt{C^{2}}=C
. By a triangle inequality argument it then follows that
\|L^{-}\|\leq 2C
.
∎

\displaystyle\ker(L)=\overline{\operatorname{span}\left\{\chi_{C}:C\in\mathfrak{C}\text{ and }\underline{m}(C)<\infty\right\}}.

\hat{E}_{i}
determines a connected component of
(V,\hat{E})
: If there are edges
(a,b),(c,d)\in\hat{E}_{i}
, then there exists a path
a\leftrightsquigarrow d
completely contained within
\hat{E}_{i}
.

Associated to each cluster
\hat{E}_{i}
is the corresponding node set
\hat{V}_{i}=\{v|\exists j\in V:(v,a)\in\hat{E}_{i}\}
of the vertices connected by edges in the cluster
E_{i}
.

In particular, node-disjointness clearly implies edge-disjointness (
\forall i\neq j:\hat{E}_{i}\cap\hat{E}_{j}=\emptyset
), and that we may always ensure (iii) by further subdivision of each
E_{i}
if necessary.

3.1.
Strong resolvent convergence

We are now interested in the setting where the connectivity within the individual clusters
i\in I
increases unboundedly. More specifically, we consider a setting where we modify our initial graph
G=(G,m,E,a)
into a family of graphs
G_{\beta}=(G,m,E,a_{\beta})
indexed by
\beta\in[0,\infty)
so that
G_{0}=G
and

(3.1)

\displaystyle\text{for $\tilde{\beta}>\beta$}:\quad\begin{cases}a_{\tilde{\beta}}(i,j)=a(i,j),&\text{if }(i,j)\in E\setminus\hat{E}\\
a_{\tilde{\beta}}(i,j)>a_{\beta}(i,j),&\text{if }(i,j)\in\hat{E},\end{cases}

with
a_{\beta}(i,j)\rightarrow\infty
for any
(i,j)\in\hat{E}
, as
\beta\rightarrow\infty
.

With the Dirichlet form
\mathscr{E}_{G_{\beta}}
of (
2.10
), we then have for
\tilde{\beta}>\beta
that:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{G_{\beta}}(f,f)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E\setminus\hat{E}}a(i,j)|f(i)-f(j)|^{2}+\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\hat{E}}a_{\beta}(i,j)|f(i)-f(j)|^{2}\right)\leq\mathscr{E}_{G_{\tilde{\beta}}}(f,f)

This inequality is strict unless
f
is constant on all sets
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of cluster nodes.
The prototypical example of this setting arises by modifying the edge-weight function of the original graph
G
as
a\mapsto\hat{a}_{\beta}
, with

(3.3)

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\beta}(x,y)=a(x,y)+\beta\cdot\chi_{\hat{E}}((x,y))a(x,y).

Here
\chi_{\hat{E}}
is the characteristic function of the total-set
\hat{E}
of cluster-edges.

We may decompose the Laplacian

L_{\beta}
corresponding to
\mathscr{E}_{G_{\beta}}
as

(3.4)

\displaystyle L_{\beta}=L_{E\setminus\hat{E}}+L_{\hat{E},\beta},

with the two constituent operators defined as

(3.5)

\displaystyle[L_{\hat{E},\beta}f](x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(x)}\left(d_{\hat{E},\beta}(x)f(x)-\sum\limits_{y\in V}[\chi_{\hat{E}}((x,y))a_{\beta}(x,y)]f(y)\right),

(3.6)

\displaystyle[L_{E\setminus\hat{E}}f](x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(x)}\left(d_{E\setminus\hat{E}}(x)f(x)-\sum\limits_{y\in V}[\chi_{E\setminus\hat{E}}((x,y))a(x,y)]f(y)\right).

Here
d_{\hat{E},\beta}(x)=\sum_{y\in V}\chi_{\hat{E}}((x,y))a_{\beta}(x,y)
represents the degree of a node
x\in V
in the graph determined by the edges in
\hat{E}
, while
d_{E\setminus\hat{E}}(x)=\sum_{y\in V}\chi_{E\setminus\hat{E}}((x,y))a(x,y)
is the degree of the same node in the graph determined by the remaining edges
E\setminus\hat{E}
. Clearly
d_{\beta}(x)=d_{\hat{E},\beta}(x)+d_{E\setminus\hat{E}}(x)
, with
d_{\beta}(x)=\sum_{y\in V}a_{\beta}(x,y)
the degree of node
x
in the original graph
G
. It is straightforward to verify, that
the Laplacians of (
3.5
) are induced by the graph structures
G_{\hat{E}}=(V,m,\hat{E},a_{\hat{E},\beta})
and
G_{E\setminus\hat{E}}=(V,m,E\setminus\hat{E},a_{E\setminus\hat{E}})
, with
a_{\hat{E},\beta},a_{E\setminus\hat{E}}
the restrictions of
a_{\beta}
to the respective sets.

We can thus think of the Laplacian in (
3.4
) as being given by a fixed background Laplacian
L_{E\setminus\hat{E}}
determined by the graph structure
G_{E\setminus\hat{E}}
,
subjected to a (large) perturbation
L_{\hat{E},\beta}
determined by the clusters in
G_{\hat{E}}
on which edge weights diverge.

Clearly the perturbing operator
L_{\hat{E},\beta}
as well as the total Laplacian
L_{\beta}=L_{E\setminus\hat{E}}+L_{\hat{E},\beta}
diverge in norm (
\|L_{\beta}\|,\|L_{\hat{E},\beta}\|\rightarrow\infty
). To establish a well defined notion of convergence, we hence instead consider the corresponding family of
resolvents

\{(L_{\beta}-zI_{\ell^{2}(G)})^{-1}\}_{\beta}
, which remain uniformly bounded in
\beta
:
\|(L_{\beta}-zI_{\ell^{2}(G)})^{-1}\|\leq 1/{\operatorname{dist}}(z,\mathds{R}_{+}
).

Below, we will use a modified version of Kato’s monotone convergence theorem

3.2.

We are now interested in determining under which conditions exactly, we can turn the

To this end, we first recall the definition of a spectral gap:

Definition 3.10
.

The spectral gap of a non-negative operator
L
is

\gamma(L):=\inf\limits_{\lambda\neq 0}\sigma(L).

Below, we will give a norm estimate on the difference
of
(L_{\beta}-zI_{\ell^{2}(G)})^{-1}
and
J^{\uparrow}(\underline{L}-zI_{\ell^{2}(\underline{G})})^{-1}J^{\downarrow}
in terms of the spectral gap of
L_{\hat{E},\beta}

We first establish a Lemma:

Lemma 3.11
.

With
P
the orthogonal spectral projection onto
\ker(L_{\hat{E}})
, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left\|(L_{\hat{E},\beta}-z)^{-1}-P/(-z)\right\|=1/|\gamma(L_{\hat{E},\beta})-z|.

Moreover, with
\hat{E}=\cup_{i}\hat{E}_{i}
as in Definition
3.1

\gamma(L_{\hat{E},\beta})=\inf_{i\in I}\gamma(L_{\hat{E}_{i},\beta})
.

Proof.

This is a straightforward application of the spectral theorem. The claim
\gamma(L_{\hat{E},\beta})=\inf_{i\in I}\gamma(L_{\hat{E}_{i},\beta})
follows, since
L_{\hat{E},\beta}=\sum_{i\in I}L_{\hat{E}_{i},\beta}
and elements of the family

Adversarially and Distributionally Robust Virtual Energy Storage Systems via the Scenario Approach
Georgios Pantazis, Nicola Mignoni, Raffaele Carli, Mariagrazia Dotoli, Sergio Grammatico

Abstract
We propose an optimization model where a parking lot manager (PLM) can aggregate parked EV batteries to provide virtual energy storage services that are provably robust under uncertain EV departures and state-of-charge caps. Our formulation yields a data-driven convex optimization problem where a prosumer community agrees on a contract with the PLM for the provision of storage services over a finite horizon. Leveraging recent results in the scenario approach, we certify out-of-sample constraint safety. Furthermore, we enable a tunable profit–risk trade-off through scenario relaxation and extend our model to account for robustness to adversarial perturbations and distributional shifts over Wasserstein-based ambiguity sets. All the approaches are accompanied by tight finite-sample certificates. Numerical studies demonstrate the out-of-sample and out-of-distribution constraint satisfaction of our proposed model compared to the developed theoretical guarantees, showing their effectiveness and potential in robust and efficient virtual energy services.

I-A

Challenges and Main Contributions

This paper addresses two limitations that are crucial in virtual energy storage services of this type: i) the flexibility to trade off economic performance with robustness in constraint satisfaction; ii) our model interconnects prosumer communities with EV parking lots and a retailer. In this setting EV arrivals/departures, as well as EV users’ choices are uncertain. Such uncertainty is quantified based on data measurements often affected by noise or prone to data poisoning from an adversarial entity. Furthermore, since they are heavily based on human behaviour, they can exhibit probabilistic shifts, i.e., changes in the probabilistic model followed. This can render the provided guarantees based on a nominal model inadequate for out-of-sample or out-of-distribution robustness. As such, resilience to adversarial perturbations of data (or distributional shifts) is of utmost importance.

Our main contributions with respect to the related literature are the following:

1.

We propose a model for a virtual energy storage service provided to a community of prosumers that can be seamlessly integrated with the existing network. The role of the PLM is to aggregate the batteries of parked EVs into a
virtual
energy storage system with time-varying capacity and offer a requested capacity to the prosumers community under a contract. Thus, the PLM has a dual role, as a profit making entity and as a risk absorber. The model captures the uncertain virtual state-of-charge dynamics with losses induced by parked EV departures, uncertain virtual capacity bounds, as well as market actions with retailers, while still retaining convexity.

2.

Because the distributions of losses and capacity caps often follow distributions difficult to model due to inherent behavioural aspects of EV users, we resort to data and formulate an approximated program based on scenarios over the defined horizon. We then provide distribution-free probabilistic robustness certificates on the probability that our solution will be robust against yet unseen vehicle departures and capacity caps. We offer a method for the PLM to tune the trade-off between constraint satisfaction and profit accompanied by tight safety guarantees.

3.

We allow the PLM to tune the level of trust to the data by allowing each training sample to vary within a perturbation set chosen by the PLM. We show that such adversarially robust solutions can still be accompanied by distribution-free guarantees. We consider a distributionally robust optimization setting where the distribution during the training can differ from the probability distribution during the test period up to a risk-aversion radius chosen by the PLM. Leveraging recent results in the scenario approach
[
2
]
, we show that robustness certificates on the violation of the test distributions can be obtained, thus certifying our decision against future shifts in the probabilistic models of EV departures and capacity caps.

EV Departures and Capacity Distributional Shifts

The training set
\{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{(i)},\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(i)}\}_{i\in\mathcal{N}}
can originate from, e.g., synthetic models or real-world measurements/ historical data. However, due to distributional shifts, the data used for training might not follow the same distribution as the data collected after deployment. In our setting, the PLM has collected data on the losses incurred from vehicle departures and data of imposed upper bounds in the state of charge of the virtual storage. However, the PLM wishes to have safety certificates against yet unseen realizations of these quantities that might follow a different distribution
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
. Unfortunately, without some connection between the probability distributions
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
, it is extremely challenging to provide any provable guarantees. To establish such results, a metric of similarity among distributions is often considered. An often used measure, due to its intuitive interpretation based on optimal transport, is the so-called Wasserstein distance defined as follows:

Definition 2

Consider the uncertain parameters
(\ell,\beta)
and
(\tilde{\ell},\tilde{\beta})
following the probability distributions
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
.
Then, the Wasserstein metric is given by:

d_{W}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{P}^{\prime})=\inf_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\|\ell-\tilde{\ell}\|^{2}_{2}+|\beta-\tilde{\beta}\|^{2}_{2}],

(22)

where
\mathbb{Q}
denotes a joint probability distribution of random variables with marginals
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
.
\square

Based on the Wasserstein distance, we can then define the ambiguity set that the PLM selects to account for risk aversion against probabilistic shifts with respect to this metric. To achieve this, the PLM needs to decide on a radius, which determines the size of the ambiguity set. As such, we assume that the distance between the training data distribution
\mathbb{P}
and the test data distribution
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
is coupled via the inequality:

\mathbb{B}_{\mu}(\mathbb{P})=\{\mathbb{P}^{\prime}:d_{W}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{P}^{\prime})\leq\mu\}.

(23)

We define the probability of violation for a distribution
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mu}(\mathbb{P})

\mathbb{V}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{b}):=\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\{(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\beta}^{\prime}):f(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{b},\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\beta}^{\prime})>0\}

(24)

VI
Conclusion

This paper develops a distributionally robust framework based on scenario optimization that enables a parking-lot manager to operate aggregated EVs as a virtual energy storage system, providing profit/risk tuning flexibility and finite-sample guarantees under adversarial perturbations and Wasserstein distribution shifts. Numerical simulations on the proposed model show the alignment between empirical violation and theoretical bounds. Future work will involve integrating user-centric EV battery-health considerations into this scheme and modelling the EV users as active participants of the parking lot management system. Furthermore, this model can be extended for large-scale implementation by involving multiple interacting parking lots for market participation and incorporating network constraints.

Improvement of uncorrected visual acuity and contrast sensitivity using Aurovue Vivid Continuous Focus Vision (CFV) lens in cataract patients at RSCM Kirana
Authors: Irma Suwandi Sadikin, Jessica Zarwan, Syska Widyawati, Faraby Martha, Rio Rhendy, Syougie Ali
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe primary goal of cataract surgery has evolved beyond visual restoration toward comprehensive refractive outcomes. In aging yet functionally active populations such as Indonesia, access to premium multifocal intraocular lenses (IOLs) remains limited, creating demand for cost-effective extended-depth-of-focus (EDOF) alternatives.ObjectiveTo evaluate visual performance, contrast sensitivity, defocus profile, IOL stability, and patient-reported outcomes following implantation of the Aurovue Vivid Continuous Focus Vision (CFV) IOL.MethodsThis prospective interventional case series included 25 eyes of 19 patients undergoing phacoemulsification with Aurovue Vivid CFV implantation. Assessments included uncorrected distance (UCDVA), intermediate (UCIVA), and near (UCNVA) visual acuity, corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA) at one month, binocular defocus curve, photopic contrast sensitivity, ultrasound biomicroscopy for tilt/decentration, and patient satisfaction.ResultsAll eyes completed follow-up. Statistically significant improvements were observed: UCDVA from 1.06 ± 0.49 to 0.18 ± 0.16 logMAR (p < 0.001), UCIVA from 0.97 ± 0.41 to 0.35 ± 0.23 logMAR (p < 0.001), UCNVA from 1.05 ± 0.49 to 0.55 ± 0.16 logMAR (p < 0.001), postoperative CDVA of 0.00 (range 0.0–0.4) logMAR with 76% of eyes achieving 0 logMAR and photopic contrast sensitivity from 1.05 (range 0–1.65) to 1.65 (range 1.35–1.85) log units (p < 0.001). The binocular defocus curve demonstrated a peak acuity of 0.06 logMAR at 0 D defocus (distinct from the routine postoperative UCDVA of 0.18 logMAR), with maintained acuity (≤ 0.20 logMAR) up to approximately − 2.0 D. IOL stability was excellent: mean tilt angles 90.30°–90.50° and median decentration 0.06 mm. Patient satisfaction reached 100% with 76% spectacle independence; mild non-functional glare was reported by 20%.ConclusionIn this short-term exploratory case series, the Aurovue Vivid CFV achieved satisfactory visual outcomes across all distances, with preserved contrast sensitivity, stable IOL positioning, and high patient-reported comfort. These preliminary data suggest the lens may represent a viable, cost-effective option to bridge the gap between monofocal and premium multifocal solutions; longer-term, comparative studies are warranted to confirm durability and relative performance.Keywords: Aurovue Vivid CFV, Cataract surgery, Contrast sensitivity, Extended depth-of-focus, EDOF, Intraocular lens, Visual acuity

Introduction

Cataract is a degenerative disease characterized by lens opacity and remains the leading cause of blindness worldwide, particularly in South Asia, Oceania, and Southeast Asia [1, 2]. In Indonesia, the burden of cataract blindness remains high: recent surveys found a 3.0% national blindness prevalence with untreated cataract as the commonest cause (71–96% of cases) [3, 4]. As population longevity increases, Indonesia’s over-60 population is projected to rise from ~ 10.8% in 2022 to ~ 25% by 2050; consequently, the demand for high-quality postoperative vision at all distances has grown [3–5]. Modern cataract surgery thus aims not only to restore visual clarity but also to achieve refractive independence across all distances, including intermediate and near ranges [6–9]. However, standard monofocal IOLs provide focus at a single distance only, leaving most patients reliant on reading glasses for near and intermediate tasks [6–9].

This has driven the development of various premium IOL technologies, including multifocal, accommodative, enhanced monofocal, and extended depth-of-focus (EDOF) lenses [7, 10]. EDOF lenses are designed to provide distance and intermediate vision with minimal photic phenomena (halos and glare) compared to traditional multifocal lenses [10–12]. Unlike multifocal lenses that create two or three discrete focal points, EDOF lenses extend the depth of focus to create a continuous range of vision, thereby reducing the light dispersion responsible for photic side effects (Fig. 1) [14, 15]. The ANSI/AAO Z80.35-2018 standard establishes four primary criteria for EDOF classification: non-inferior distance visual acuity, a minimum depth-of-focus increase of 0.5 D, superior intermediate visual acuity, and visual quality assessment including mesopic contrast sensitivity [16, 17].

Extended depth-of-focus (EDOF) Lens [13]

The Aurolab® Aurovue Vivid CFV (Aurolab, Madurai, Tamil Nadu, India) is a newly available hybrid refractive-diffractive EDOF lens with no prior published clinical evaluation (Fig. 2) [18]. The lens is designed to extend focus from 40 cm to ≥ 4 m using aspheric optics and smooth edge technology [18]. Compared to imported EDOF lenses, its lower acquisition cost may offer a more accessible option within Indonesia’s healthcare system. This study therefore aimed to evaluate its clinical performance, including visual acuity at distance, intermediate, and near, defocus curve, contrast sensitivity, IOL positional stability, and patient-reported outcomes.

Methods

Study design and patients

This prospective interventional case series was conducted in the outpatient clinic and operating theatre of RSCM Kirana outpatient clinic, Department of Ophthalmology, Faculty of Medicine, Universitas Indonesia: Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital (RSCM), between July 1, 2025 and October 20, 2025. Ethical approval was obtained from the institutional Health Research Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Universitas Indonesia (KET-352/UN2.F1/ETIK/PPM.00.02/2025), and site permission from Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital (YR.02.01/D.IX.2.3/906/2025); all participants provided written informed consent.

Subjects were recruited consecutively from senile cataract patients attending the outpatient clinic. To be eligible, patients were required to have senile cataract graded ≤NO5NC5 using the Lens Opacities Classification System III (LOCS III) in one or both eyes, regular corneal astigmatism < 1.00 D, retinometry ≥ 0.80 (≤ 0.1 logMAR / 6/7.5), clear refractive media aside from the cataract, and a pupil diameter of 3.0–3.5 mm (justified by the lens optical design: the diffractive ring zone diameter of ~ 2.96 mm requires a minimum pupil of 3.0 mm for full activation, with 3.5 mm as upper limit to maintain balanced refractive-diffractive contribution). Patients were excluded if they had a history of prior ocular surgery (including corneal or refractive procedures) or ocular comorbidities likely to influence visual acuity or pupil size, including chronic uveitis, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, pseudoexfoliation syndrome, keratoconus, age-related macular degeneration, or corneal dystrophy. Patients who failed to complete scheduled follow-up, experienced intra- or postoperative events affecting pupil size or IOL stability, or withdrew consent were classified as drop-outs. A total of 25 eyes was established as the target sample size based on the largest value from three parallel calculations approved by an academic statistician: (1) defocus curve — descriptive numerical formula n=(ZαS/d)² (α = 5%, S = 0.06, d = 0.025; n = 23); (2) visual comfort — descriptive categorical formula n = Z²×p×(1 − p)/d² (p = 0.48, d = 0.2; n = 24); (3) UCDVA — paired comparative formula n={(Zα + Zβ)×S/d}² (α = 5%, power = 80%, S = 0.25, d = 0.15; n = 22, + 10% dropout). The largest value (24 eyes for UCDVA) was rounded up to 25. Consecutive sampling was used.

Clinical evaluation and surgical protocol

Subjects underwent a standardized preoperative assessment that included a structured history and a full ophthalmic examination. Visual acuity measures comprised uncorrected and corrected distance visual acuity (UDVA, CDVA), uncorrected intermediate visual acuity at 80 cm (UIVA) and uncorrected near visual acuity at 40 cm (UNVA), all recorded with the Logarithmic Visual Acuity Chart 2000 (“new ETDRS”). Contrast sensitivity was measured with the Pelli–Robson chart. Biometry and keratometry were performed using the IOLMaster 500 (Carl Zeiss Meditec). A complete anterior and posterior segment examination was obtained by slit-lamp biomicroscopy and intraocular pressure measurement by tonometry. Intraocular lens power calculation was performed using the Barrett Universal II formula with the manufacturer-recommended A-constant, without further lens constant optimization. Phacoemulsification was performed by surgeons of the Cataract–Cornea–Refractive Surgery Division with implantation of the Aurolab® Aurovue Vivid Continuous Focus Vision (CFV) IOL. Postoperative management followed a standardized regimen of topical levofloxacin and prednisolone acetate per protocol.

All subjects returned for a structured one-month postoperative assessment that included a complete ophthalmic examination; monocular and binocular UDVA, UIVA and UNVA; contrast sensitivity; and patient-reported visual comfort assessed with an open questionnaire. Defocus testing was performed using lenses from + 1.50 D to − 3.50 D in 0.50 D increments to generate a defocus curve. Corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA) was assessed at one month postoperatively using best manifest refraction. For eyes that achieved emmetropia without requiring optical correction (n = 7), CDVA was equivalent to UCDVA and recorded accordingly. Preoperative CDVA was assessable in 8 of 25 eyes (those with coexisting refractive error); in the remaining 17 eyes, the degree of lens opacity precluded reliable manifest refraction.

Intraocular lens centration and position were evaluated by ultrasound biomicroscopy (VuMAX HD®). Spectacle independence was defined as the ability to perform routine daily activities at distance, intermediate, and near distances without the use of optical correction. At one month postoperatively, patients were asked whether they required spectacles for any daily task. Those who did not require any spectacles for their routine daily activities were classified as spectacle-independent. Among those still requiring spectacles, further subclassification was made according to the distance of need: near tasks only (reading at approximately 30 cm) or both distance and near.

Statistical analysis

All data were entered and analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 25.0. Categorical variables are reported as frequencies and percentages. Numerical variables were assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test and reported as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed data or median (minimum–maximum) for non-normally distributed data. For paired comparisons, the paired t-test was applied to parametric data and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test to non-parametric data. Longitudinal changes across four time points were analyzed using the General Linear Model (GLM) Repeated Measures ANOVA for normally distributed outcomes or the Friedman test for non-normal data. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, and 95% confidence intervals are reported where applicable. This study adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Universitas Indonesia (No. KET-352/UN2.F1/ETIK/PPM.00.02/2025), and research location permission was granted by Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital (No. YR.02.01/D.IX.2.3/906/2025). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. To address potential inter-eye correlation from six bilateral patients (12 eyes), a pre-specified sensitivity analysis was performed restricted to one eye per patient (the first operated eye from each bilateral pair), yielding 19 independent observations.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 20 patients (26 eyes) were initially enrolled; six patients underwent bilateral implantation (12 eyes) and 14 underwent unilateral implantation (14 eyes). One eye (from one unilateral patient) was excluded due to postoperative retinal detachment requiring vitrectomy, leaving 19 patients (25 eyes) for final analysis: 6 bilateral patients contributing 12 eyes and 13 unilateral patients contributing 13 eyes. A participant flow diagram is provided in Fig. 3. The cohort had a mean age of 63 ± 7 years (range 48–75); 58% were female. Most patients (76%) habitually used spectacles preoperatively. Occupational and educational profiles were typical of a working-to-retirement–age population, with homemakers (36%) and retirees (32%) comprising the largest groups and the majority having at least secondary education (Table 1). Baseline refraction and axial length varied widely (spherical equivalents − 10.50 to + 2.00 D; axial lengths 21.2–25.9 mm). Planned refractive target aimed for emmetropia (mean target − 0.26 D); two eyes were intentionally targeted at slight myopia (− 1.00 D and − 1.25 D) because of limited lens power availability in highly myopic patients. IOP was within normal limits for all subjects preoperatively (Table 2). The preoperative biometry characteristics are provided in Table 3.

Two adverse events were recorded across 26 enrolled eyes. One eye developed a postoperative retinal detachment at approximately seven days following otherwise uncomplicated phacoemulsification, at which point visual acuity had already shown improvement at day 1. This eye required vitrectomy and was excluded from all efficacy analyses. While postoperative retinal detachment is a potentially sight-threatening event, it occurred in a patient with high myopia — a known independent risk factor — and was considered unrelated to the IOL implantation procedure. One intraoperative haptic fracture occurred during IOL loading, resulting in IOL decentration of 2.75 mm at one month. Despite this, the affected eye maintained functional visual acuity: UCDVA 0.4 logMAR at day 1, 0.2 logMAR at day 7, and 0.4 logMAR at one month, with BCVA of 0.3 logMAR with − 1.00 D correction at one month. No other adverse events were recorded in the remaining 24 eyes.

Visual acuity outcomes

Preoperative uncorrected visual acuity was markedly reduced across all distances (UCDVA 1.06 ± 0.49 logMAR, UCIVA 0.97 ± 0.41 logMAR, UCNVA 1.06 ± 0.50 logMAR) (Table 2). At 1 month following phacoemulsification with Aurovue Vivid CFV implantation, mean uncorrected visual acuity improved significantly at all distances (p < 0.001): UCDVA to 0.18 ± 0.16 logMAR, UCIVA to 0.35 ± 0.23 logMAR, and UCNVA to 0.55 ± 0.16 logMAR (Table 4; Fig. 4). The greatest improvement was observed at postoperative day 1, with values remaining stable at day 7 and 1 month (Table 5). Postoperative CDVA at one month was: median 0.00 logMAR (range 0.0–0.4), with 19 of 25 eyes (76%) achieving 0 logMAR (6/6) and 21 of 25 eyes (84%) achieving ≤ 0.1 logMAR (6/7.5 or better) (Table 4). Spectacle independence was reported in 19 of 25 eyes (76%), with four of the six spectacle-dependent eyes requiring reading glasses only for near tasks at approximately 30 cm.

Contrast sensitivity

Median photopic contrast sensitivity improved significantly from 1.05 log units (range 0.0–1.65) preoperatively to 1.65 log units (range 1.35–1.85) postoperatively (p < 0.001) (Table 4). Postoperative values were within or slightly above age-adjusted normative limits, consistent with restoration of optical clarity following cataract extraction.

Defocus curve

The binocular defocus curve is shown in Fig. 5. Peak visual acuity of 0.06 logMAR was achieved at 0 D defocus. Visual acuity remained ≤ 0.20 logMAR between 0 D and − 2.0 D defocus (approximately 50–66 cm), representing a functional intermediate vergence range (note: UCIVA was clinically tested at 80 cm using standardized chart; the 50–66 cm range here reflects the functional range derived from defocus curve analysis, not the clinical testing distance). Beyond − 2.0 D, acuity declined progressively: 0.22 logMAR at − 2.50 D (~ 40 cm), 0.45 logMAR at − 3.00 D (~ 33 cm), and 0.65 logMAR at − 3.50 D (~ 29 cm). No secondary peaks were observed, indicating a continuous and progressive decline in acuity with increasing defocus.

To address potential inter-eye correlation from six bilateral patients, a sensitivity analysis was performed restricted to one eye per patient (the first operated eye from each bilateral pair), yielding 19 independent eyes. Results were consistent with the primary analysis: UCDVA 0.19 ± 0.15 logMAR (vs. 0.18 ± 0.16), UCIVA 0.37 ± 0.22 logMAR (vs. 0.35 ± 0.23), and UCNVA 0.58 ± 0.15 logMAR (vs. 0.55 ± 0.16), all p < 0.001. The similarity between analyses indicates that bilateral inclusion did not materially influence conclusions.

Patient-reported outcomes

Patient-reported outcomes were favorable: at one month, 100% of patients rated overall comfort as good (score ≥ 6/10), 80% reported no subjective complaints, and 20% described only mild, non-disabling dysphotopsia (predominantly transient glare in dim lighting) (Table 6). Overall, patient-reported comfort corresponded with the spectacle independence rate of 76%.

Intraocular lens centration and stability

Ultrasound biomicroscopy at 1 month confirmed excellent positional stability of the IOLs (Table 7). Tilt angles were within normal limits (mean values approximating the expected vertical meridian), and the median decentration was 0.06 mm (range 0–2.75 mm). One outlier (decentration 2.75 mm) occurred secondary to an intraoperative haptic fracture; despite this, that eye achieved good functional acuity (~ 20/25), illustrating relative tolerance of the CFV diffractive–refractive design to misalignment.

Summary of longitudinal changes

Repeated-measures analyses across four time points (preoperative, day 1, day 7, and 1 month) demonstrated highly significant improvements for all tested parameters (visual acuity, contrast sensitivity, and refractive outcomes), with the most pronounced change occurring on day 1 followed by early stabilization through 1 month (Tables 4 and 5).

Discussion

This exploratory prospective case series enrolled 25 cataract eyes with demographic characteristics consistent with published cataract epidemiology, with a mean age of 63 years and female predominance (58%) [21]. Most subjects had a history of spectacle use (76%), reflecting the practical demand for comprehensive visual correction [22]. Preoperative visual impairment was severe across all distances (UCDVA 1.06 ± 0.49, UCIVA 0.97 ± 0.41, UCNVA 1.06 ± 0.50 logMAR), with intraocular pressure within normal limits (14.58 ± 3.63 mmHg). Preoperative biometry confirmed fulfillment of the minimal astigmatism inclusion criterion (corneal cylinder − 0.59 ± 0.47 D), with a median refractive target of − 0.26 D aimed at emmetropia to optimize the functional range of the EDOF lens [23].

Significant improvements in uncorrected visual acuity were observed at all distances following implantation (p < 0.001). UCDVA improved from 1.06 ± 0.49 logMAR preoperatively to 0.18 ± 0.16 logMAR at 1 month, corresponding to approximately 6/7.5–6/9. Early stabilization from day 1 suggests accurate effective lens position prediction, consistent with the use of the Barrett Universal II formula [24]. Direct comparisons with previously published EDOF lens studies should be interpreted with caution, as differences in study design, patient selection criteria, visual acuity testing protocols, follow-up duration, and outcome definitions may limit the validity of cross-study comparisons. These results are comparable to established EDOF lenses including PureSee (0.03 ± 0.07 logMAR), IOL EDOF 877PEY (0.15 ± 0.16 logMAR), and Tecnis Symfony (0.08 ± 0.10 logMAR) [25–27]. UCIVA improved to 0.35 ± 0.23 logMAR at 1 month, approximately 6/12, consistent with PureSee (0.26 ± 0.13 logMAR) and IOL EDOF 877PEY (0.30 ± 0.17 logMAR) [25, 26]. UCNVA improved to 0.55 ± 0.16 logMAR, approximately 6/21, permitting functional near tasks at 40 cm [11, 28, 29]. Residual spectacle dependence for very close distances (30–33 cm) is an expected characteristic of EDOF designs, which prioritize reduction of photic phenomena over maximal near acuity [14, 30].

Transient IOP elevation at day 1 (15.90 ± 5.30 mmHg) was attributed to viscoelastic retention and temporary trabecular outflow resistance, without significant visual disturbance [31]. By day 7, IOP normalized to 12.52 ± 3.08 mmHg, corresponding with completion of corneal epithelialization and edema resolution [32, 33]. Subsequent IOP reduction reflects anterior chamber deepening, angle widening, and activation of the TM ELAM-1/IL-1/NF-κB pathway [34, 35].

Photopic contrast sensitivity improved significantly from a median of 1.05 to 1.65 log units (p < 0.001), within the normal functional range [36]. This improvement is attributable to elimination of cataract-induced light scatter, as well as the more uniform light distribution of the EDOF design compared to multifocal lenses that divide light energy into discrete foci [30, 37]. These findings are consistent with Kim et al. and Győry et al., who reported contrast sensitivity within or above normal limits across all spatial frequencies [25, 26] Maintaining contrast sensitivity is clinically relevant, particularly for tasks such as night driving and low-illumination mobility [36].

The optical and material properties of the CFV IOL may have contributed to these outcomes, although direct causal attribution is not possible in the absence of a control group. The hydrophobic acrylic platform is associated with stable capsular bag fixation, low inflammatory response, and long-term material durability; with optimized polymer processing, glistening formation can be minimized, preserving optical clarity [5, 18]. The aspheric anterior surface is designed to compensate for positive corneal spherical aberration, optimizing the overall ocular wavefront profile — an important consideration in presbyopia-correcting lenses, where excessive higher-order aberrations can degrade image quality despite good high-contrast acuity [5, 18]. Additionally, the more uniform light distribution of the CFV design, compared to diffractive multifocal systems, may have contributed to the favorable contrast sensitivity outcomes, though confirmation in comparative studies is required. Tolerance to residual refractive error is another relevant clinical consideration. Diffractive multifocal IOLs are sensitive to small spherical or cylindrical deviations due to simultaneous light distribution across multiple foci [7–9]. In our cohort, mild postoperative refractive deviations did not result in disproportionate deterioration of functional acuity, suggesting that this lens may show some tolerance to imperfect refractive outcomes, though this preliminary observation requires confirmation in prospective comparative studies.

Applying the ANSI/AAO Z80.35-2018 criteria as a descriptive framework only — noting that formal ANSI classification requires a monofocal control comparator not included in this study — the following observations can be made: (1) BCDVA of 0 logMAR is consistent with the non-inferior distance acuity criterion, though direct comparison with a monofocal control was not performed; (2) a depth-of-focus range of 1.5 D was observed, which exceeds the minimum 0.5 D threshold, though ANSI-compliant confirmation requires a monofocal comparator; (3) uncorrected intermediate visual acuity was 0.35 ± 0.23 logMAR at 66 cm, with only 10 of 25 eyes (40%) achieving ≤ 0.2 logMAR without correction — this does not meet the ANSI threshold requiring at least 50% of eyes to achieve ≤ 0.2 logMAR at 66 cm compared with a monofocal control; (4) photopic contrast sensitivity improved significantly and patient-reported visual symptoms were minimal, though mesopic testing was not performed and full compliance with criterion 4 cannot be confirmed [16].

All patients (100%) reported satisfactory visual comfort, with 80% experiencing no subjective complaints and 20% describing mild, non-disabling glare. Spectacle independence was achieved in 76% of patients, consistent with published EDOF series reporting rates of 74–80% [38, 39] Among those still requiring correction, 67% used reading glasses only for near tasks at approximately 30 cm, while 33% required correction for both distance and near due to refractive targets outside emmetropia from lens power limitations in high myopia. IOL stability assessed by UBM demonstrated mean tilt angles of 90.30°–90.50° within the normal range (90–95°), and median decentration of 0.06 mm, below the accepted threshold of 0.3 mm. One case of decentration (2.75 mm) secondary to intraoperative haptic fracture retained acceptable functional acuity, consistent with the optical tolerance of hybrid refractive-diffractive designs to positional deviation [40–42]. UBM was selected as the gold standard for posterior segment IOL positional analysis, providing high-resolution in vivo imaging of structures behind the pupil that cannot be visualized with optical instruments. Its use for this purpose has been previously described and validated by Pardini et al. [43].

The Aurovue Vivid CFV maintained functional visual acuity (≤ 0.3 logMAR) up to − 2.0 D defocus, with acuity of 0.18 logMAR at − 1.0 D and 0.22 logMAR at − 1.5 D, placing its intermediate performance within the range of contemporary EDOF lenses [25–27]. While premium international alternatives may offer marginally superior near acuity or broader defocus ranges, the Aurovue Vivid CFV carries a substantially lower acquisition cost, representing a potentially more accessible option for patients in resource-limited settings who would otherwise be restricted to monofocal alternatives.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The absence of a monofocal or comparator EDOF control group limits strict ANSI-based classification and prevents direct attribution of outcomes to lens design. Contrast sensitivity was assessed under photopic conditions only. The follow-up period of one month is insufficient to evaluate long-term refractive stability, posterior capsular opacification, glistening formation, or the natural evolution of dysphotopsias; conclusions regarding IOL durability cannot be drawn from these data. The inclusion of six bilateral patients may introduce inter-eye correlation, though sensitivity analysis restricted to one eye per patient yielded consistent results. The pupil diameter inclusion criterion (3.0–3.5 mm) limits generalizability to patients with pupil sizes outside this range. Patient-reported visual comfort was assessed with a non-validated questionnaire; validated tools such as the Catquest-9SF or NEI VFQ-25 are recommended for future studies. Preoperative CDVA was not uniformly assessable due to lens opacity, limiting pre-post CDVA comparison. Future randomized comparative studies with mesopic testing and longer follow-up are needed to confirm the durability and relative performance of this lens.

In this short-term exploratory prospective case series, implantation of the Aurovue Vivid CFV IOL resulted in significant improvements in uncorrected visual acuity across all distances, preserved photopic contrast sensitivity, satisfactory IOL positional stability, and high patient-reported comfort. Spectacle independence was achieved in 76% of patients. These preliminary results suggest that this lens may represent a clinically viable and cost-effective alternative within the EDOF category, particularly for patient populations with limited access to premium IOL technologies. However, as this is an exploratory single-arm case series with one-month follow-up and no comparator group, these findings should be interpreted with appropriate caution. Randomized comparative trials with longer follow-up and mesopic contrast sensitivity testing are needed to confirm these findings.

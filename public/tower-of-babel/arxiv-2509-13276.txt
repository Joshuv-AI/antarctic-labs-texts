Sub-Laplacian comparison theorems on Riemannian foliations with minimal leaves and applications
Fabrice Baudoin

Abstract
We prove comparison theorems for the horizontal Laplacian of the Riemannian distance in the context of Riemannian foliations with minimal leaves. This general framework generalizes previous works and allow us to consider the sub-Laplacian of Carnot groups of arbitrary steps. The comparison theorems yield a Bonnet-Myers type theorem, stochastic completeness and Lipschitz regularization property for the sub-Riemannian semigroup.

Introduction

Comparison theorems for the Laplacian of the distance function are among the
central tools of Riemannian geometry. They provide a direct link between lower
bounds on curvature and global geometric or analytic consequences, such as
diameter bounds, compactness criteria, or functional inequalities. A classical
example is the Bonnet–Myers theorem, which follows from Laplacian comparison
under a positive lower Ricci curvature bound.

In this work, we extend this circle of ideas to the setting of Riemannian
foliations with minimal leaves. More precisely, we prove comparison theorems
for the upper bound of the
horizontal Laplacian
of the Riemannian
distance function. On such foliations, the horizontal Laplacian is a subelliptic operator that can be
characterized as the partial trace of the Hessian in horizontal directions for
the Levi-Civita connection. Under the classical assumption of a lower bound on
sectional curvature, horizontal Laplacian comparison follows rather directly
from the Hessian comparison theorem. Our goal, however, is to go beyond this
observation: by exploiting the geometry of the foliation itself, we establish
comparison theorems under the assumption of a lower bound on a

Ricci-like tensor

\mathfrak{R}
that naturally arises from the horizontal distribution of the foliation. Since
the Levi-Civita connection does not, in general, preserve horizontality, the
tensor
\mathfrak{R}
is not simply the horizontal trace of the Riemann curvature tensor,
but instead incorporates curvature and torsion contributions from the geometry of the
horizontal distribution.

Our approach therefore generalizes, and significantly extends, the work of

[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, where the case of totally geodesic foliations was considered. In particular,
our framework applies to all Carnot groups of arbitrary step.

Main results

The main results of this paper may be summarized as follows:

•

Horizontal Laplacian comparison theorems.

We establish Riemannian model upper bounds for the horizontal Laplacian of the
Riemannian distance function under the curvature assumption

\mathfrak{R}(U,U)\geq K\,|U|^{2},\qquad U\in TM,

where
\mathfrak{R}
is the horizontal Ricci-type tensor associated with the foliation. The
resulting inequalities take the form of non-sharp but useful comparison formulas analogous
to their Riemannian counterparts, involving the cotangent, linear, or
hyperbolic cotangent functions depending on the sign of
K
.

•

A Bonnet–Myers type theorem.

As a direct application of the comparison results, we prove that if

\mathfrak{R}\geq K>0
, then the ambient manifold is compact, with an explicit
upper bound on its Riemannian diameter of the form

\mathrm{diam}(M)\leq\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{K}},

obtaining a Bonnet–Myers theorem in the sub-Riemannian
framework of foliations with minimal leaves.

•

Stochastic completeness and heat kernel estimates.

We study the horizontal Brownian motion associated with the generator

\Delta_{H}
. Using the radial decomposition and comparison arguments, we
show that the corresponding heat semigroup is stochastically complete.
Moreover, we obtain exit time estimates which in turn yield
on-diagonal lower bounds for the heat kernel.

•

Lipschitz regularization and coupling techniques.

By developing couplings of horizontal Brownian motions via parallel
transport, we prove that the horizontal heat semigroup enjoys Lipschitz
regularization properties: for any bounded Lipschitz function
f
,

\mathrm{Lip}(P_{t}f)\leq e^{-Kt}\,\mathrm{Lip}(f).

This type of estimate is of independent interest in analysis and
probability, as it plays a key role in the theory of generalized curvature
dimension inequalities in
[
5
]
. In the case of totally geodesic foliations, our
method yields even sharper
gradient bounds
of the form

|\nabla P_{t}f|\leq e^{-Kt}\,P_{t}|\nabla f|,

thereby recovering and extending known results from
[
2
]
.

•

Applications to Carnot groups.

Since any Carnot group fits into our framework as a Riemannian foliation
with minimal leaves, our results provide a new and systematic approach to
sub-Laplacian comparison theorems for the Riemannian distance in Carnot groups of arbitrary step. In
step two Carnot groups, such as the Heisenberg group, we also recover known
mixed horizontal/vertical gradient bounds.

Outlook

The comparison theorems established in this paper open the way to a broader
theory of curvature-dimension and functional inequalities in sub-Riemannian
geometries, beyond the cases treated in
[
4
,
5
]
. In the setting
of totally geodesic Riemannian foliations, a quick computation shows that the family of
tensors
\mathfrak{R}_{\varepsilon}
arising from the canonical variation of the
metric give the tensors governing the generalized curvature-dimension
inequality of
[
4
]
. Our results here suggest that, in the more general
framework of foliations with minimal leaves, an analogous theory of
curvature-dimension inequalities should emerge, again based on the
one-parameter family of tensors
\mathfrak{R}_{\varepsilon}
. Developing this
theory is a natural continuation of the present work and will be addressed in
future research.

Finally, it is worth noting that the comparison theorems proved here are not yet
fully sub-Riemannian: they provide estimates for the horizontal Laplacian of
the
Riemannian
distance, rather than the sub-Riemannian distance.
Obtaining analogous bounds for the sub-Riemannian distance is substantially more
challenging and typically requires much stronger structural assumptions, see

[
8
]
. Nevertheless, such sub-Riemannian comparison
theorems have stronger implications, for instance leading to the measure
contraction property, which cannot be deduced from horizontal Laplacian
estimates of the Riemannian distance.

2.1
Framework and standing assumptions

Throughout the paper, we consider a smooth connected
n+m
dimensional manifold
M
which is equipped with a Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
with a bundle like metric
g
and
m
dimensional leaves. We assume that the metric
g
is complete and that the leaves are closed.

For
x\in M
,
\mathcal{F}_{x}
denotes the leaf going through
x
. The sub-bundle
\mathcal{V}
of the tangent bundle
TM
formed by vectors tangent to the leaves is referred to as the set of
vertical directions
. The sub-bundle
\mathcal{H}
which is normal to
\mathcal{V}
is referred to as the set of
horizontal directions
. Any vector
u\in T_{x}M
can be decomposed as

u=u_{\mathcal{H}}+u_{\mathcal{V}}

where
u_{\mathcal{H}}
(resp.
u_{\mathcal{V}}
) denotes the orthogonal projection of
u
on
\mathcal{H}_{x}
(resp.
\mathcal{V}_{x}
). Throughout the paper we assume that the horizontal sub-bundle
\mathcal{H}
is bracket generating. Recall that saying that the metric is bundle like means that:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{U}g)(X,X)=0,\qquad\text{for any $X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})$, $U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})$.}

(2.1)

Geometrically, (
2.1
) means that the foliation can locally be described by Riemannian submersions. We refer for instance to the classical reference
[
16
]
or the more recent monograph
[
10
]
for an overview of the theory of Riemannian foliations.

In this paper we will be interested in foliations with minimal leaves, i.e. we will assume all the leaves to be minimal submanifolds of
M
. Recall that a submanifold is called minimal if its mean curvature is zero. In terms of the Levi-Civita connection
D
saying that the leaves are minimal is equivalent to

\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_{Z_{i}}Z_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})

where the
Z_{i}
’s form an arbitrary (local) orthonormal frame of
\mathcal{V}
. There are many examples of such foliations. For instance, a large class of such foliations arise from the situation where the leaves are totally geodesic which corresponds to the case where
U,V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
implies
D_{U}V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
. For rank one foliations (i.e.
m=1
), minimality and total geodesicity are the same, but of course not all minimal foliations are totally geodesic. For instance, consider the
2n+1
-dimensional sphere
\mathbf{S}^{2n+1}
seen as a subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
and the manifold

M=\mathbf{S}^{2n+1}\setminus\left\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n+1})\in\mathbf{S}^{2n+1}:\exists i,z_{i}=0\right\}

The group
\mathbf{U}(1)^{n+1}
acts on
M
by:

(e^{i\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{i\theta_{n+1}})\cdot(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n+1})=(e^{i\theta_{1}}z_{1},\cdots,e^{i\theta_{n+1}}z_{n+1}).

The orbits of that action yield a Riemannian foliation on
M
whose leaves are tori. From O’Neilll’s equation (see
[
10
, Corollary 1.5.1]
) the foliation can not be totally geodesic because the leaves are flat whereas the ambient space
M
has positive constant sectional curvature. However, it is easily seen by symmetry that the orbits are minimal submanifolds.

The main example of foliations with minimal leaves we consider throughout the paper is the case of Carnot groups. A Carnot group is a connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group
G
whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a stratification

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s},

(2.2)

with the properties:

(i)

[V_{1},V_{j}]=V_{j+1}
for every
1\leq j<s
,

(ii)

[V_{1},V_{s}]=\{0\}
.

The integer
s
is called the step of the Carnot group, and
V_{1}
is referred to as the horizontal layer (or first layer). Consider on
\mathfrak{g}
an arbitrary inner product that makes the decomposition (
2.2
) orthogonal, i.e. for
i\neq j
,
V_{i}\perp V_{j}
. This inner product uniquely defines a left-invariant Riemannian metric on
G
. We can orthogonally decompose the tangent bundle
TG
as

TG=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V}

where
\mathcal{H}
is the left invariant sub-bundle which gives
V_{1}
at the identity and
\mathcal{V}
is the left invariant sub-bundle which gives
\oplus_{i\geq 2}V_{i}
at the identity. Since
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}]\subset\mathcal{V}
,
\mathcal{V}
is the vertical bundle of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. Note that
\mathcal{H}
is bracket generating.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a Carnot group equipped with the foliation
\mathcal{F}
and the metric
g
. Then,
\mathcal{F}
is a Riemannian foliation with bundle-like metric
g
and the leaves of the foliations are minimal submanifolds of
G
.

Proof.

For
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
and
U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
which are left-invariant one has

(\mathcal{L}_{U}g)(X,X)=-2\left\langle[U,X],X\right\rangle=0

because at the identity one has
X\in V_{1}
and
[U,X]\in\oplus_{i\geq 2}V_{i}
. Therefore, the metric
g
is bundle-like and the foliation
\mathcal{F}
Riemannian. Then, consider a left-invariant vertical orthonormal frame
Z_{i}
adapted to the grading in the sense that for every
i
there exists
n(i)
such that
Z_{i}\in V_{n(i)}
at identity. From Koszul’s formula, for any left invariant horizontal vector
X
one has

\left\langle D_{Z_{i}}Z_{i},X\right\rangle=-\left\langle[Z_{i},X],Z_{i}\right\rangle=0

because at identity
[Z_{i},X]\in V_{n(i)+1}
. Therefore
\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{Z_{i}}Z_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
.
∎

Note that it follows from similar arguments that the foliation on Carnot group is totally geodesic if and only if the group has step 2.

2.2
Horizontal Laplacian and fundamental connection

We come back to the general framework described in the previous section. The Riemannian gradient will be denoted
\nabla
and the horizontal gradient
\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}
: it is simply defined as the projection of
\nabla
onto
\mathcal{H}
. The horizontal Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is the generator of the symmetric closable bilinear form:

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}(f,g)=-\int_{\mathbb{M}}\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,d\mu,\quad f,g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M),

where
\mu
denotes the Riemannian volume measure on
M
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
the space of smooth and compactly supported functions on
M
.

The hypothesis that
\mathcal{H}
is bracket generating implies that the horizontal Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is locally subelliptic and the completeness assumption on
g
implies that
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is furthermore essentially self-adjoint on
C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
(see for instance
[
1
]
).

If
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
is a local orthonormal frame of horizontal vector fields, then we locally have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(D_{X_{i}}X_{i})_{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.3)

The Levi-Civita connection
D
associated with the Riemannian metric
g
is in general not well adapted to study foliations since the horizontal and vertical bundle might not be
D
-parallel. There is a more natural connection
\nabla
that respects the foliation structure, see
[
11
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
3
]
.

Define a
(2,1)
tensor
C
by the formula: For
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\left\langle C_{X}Y,Z\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{\mathcal{H}}}g)(Y_{\mathcal{V}},Z_{\mathcal{V}}).

(2.4)

The connection
\nabla
is then defined in terms of the Levi-Civita one
D
by

\nabla_{X}Y=\begin{cases}(D_{X}Y)_{\mathcal{H}},&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
[X,Y]_{\mathcal{H}},&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\ Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
[X,Y]_{\mathcal{V}}+C_{X}Y,&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\ Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\\
(D_{X}Y)_{\mathcal{V}},&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}).\end{cases}

It satisfies the following characteristic (and defining) properties:

(i)

\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
are
\nabla
-parallel: For every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
and
U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,

\nabla_{Z}X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\quad\nabla_{Z}U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V});

(ii)

\nabla
is a metric connection:
\nabla g=0
;

(iii)

The torsion
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}
of
\nabla
satisfies
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})\subset\mathcal{V}
,
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V})\subset\mathcal{V}
and
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})=0
.

(iv)

For every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,
V,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,

\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(Z,X),V\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V,X),Z\rangle.

(2.5)

Remark 2.2
.

Assume that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
arises from a Riemannian submersion
\pi:(M,g)\to(B,h)
. Let
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
be basic vector fields on
M
, then
\nabla_{X}Y
is also basic. In fact, from
[
10
, Lemma 1.4.1]
, if
\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}
are the vector fields on
B
that are
\pi
-related to
X,Y
, then
\nabla_{X}Y
is
\pi
-related to
D^{B}_{\tilde{X}}\tilde{Y}
, i.e.

\pi_{*}\left(\nabla_{X}Y\right)=(D^{B}_{\tilde{X}}\tilde{Y})\circ\pi

where
D^{B}
is the Levi-Civita connection on
B
.

A short computation shows that the torsion tensor
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}
is given by

\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)=\begin{cases}-[X,Y]_{\mathcal{V}}&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
C_{X}Y&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\\
0&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}).\end{cases}

Remark 2.3
.

Recall that the foliation is totally geodesic if and only if

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)(U,U)=0,\qquad\text{for any $X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})$, $U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})$.}

(2.6)

This is equivalent to
C=0
. Therefore, it follows from the torsion formula that the foliation is totally geodesic if and only if
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V})=0
.

Example 2.4

(Carnot groups)
.

Consider the foliation on a Carnot group as in Proposition
2.1
. For a left invariant vector field
X
, denote
\mathrm{ad}_{X}
the map
\mathrm{ad}_{X}(Y)=[X,Y]
and
\mathrm{ad}^{*}
its adjoint. It follows from Koszul’s formula that for left invariant vector fields

\nabla_{X}Y=\begin{cases}0&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
0&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\ Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{ad}_{X}Y-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{X}Y)_{\mathcal{V}}&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\ Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\\
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{ad}_{X}Y-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{Y}X)_{\mathcal{V}}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{X}Y)_{\mathcal{V}}&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}).\end{cases}

and therefore,

\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)=\begin{cases}-\mathrm{ad}_{X}Y&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{ad}_{X}Y-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{X}Y)_{\mathcal{V}}&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\\
0&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}).\end{cases}

For
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
, there is a unique skew-symmetric endomorphism
J_{Z}:T_{x}M\to T_{x}M
such that for all vector fields
X
and
Y
,

\displaystyle\left\langle J_{Z}X,Y\right\rangle=\left\langle Z,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)\right\rangle.

(2.7)

From the definition and since
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}
is vertical, we deduce that if
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
, then
J_{Z}=0
.

The relation between the Levi-Civita connection
D
and the connection
\nabla
is given by the formula

\nabla_{X}Y=D_{X}Y+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)-\frac{1}{2}J_{X}Y-\frac{1}{2}J_{Y}X,\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(TM).

(2.8)

It is a trivial consequence of (
2.8
) that the Riemannian geodesic equation
D_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=0
becomes

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}+J_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=0.

(2.9)

Thanks to (
2.3
), in a local horizontal frame
X_{i}
the horizontal Laplacian
{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}
is given by

\displaystyle{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(D_{X_{i}}X_{i})_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}f(X_{i},X_{i})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}f),

where
\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}f
denotes the
\nabla
-Hessian of
f
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}
the horizontal trace, i.e. the partial trace taken on the horizontal space. Note that from (
2.8
) we also have

\displaystyle{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{X_{i}}X_{i}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathrm{Hess}^{D}f).

We point out that those formulas for the horizontal Laplacian crucially rely on the assumption of the leaves being minimal. Without this assumption the formula for
{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}
also involves the mean curvature curvature of the leaves, see
[
3
]
.

2.3
The index form in horizontal directions

Recall that in Riemannian geometry the second variation of the length functional is given by the index form. The index form of a vector field
X
along a unit speed geodesic
\gamma:[0,r]\to M
is defined as

\displaystyle I(\gamma,X):=\int_{0}^{r}\left|D_{\dot{\gamma}}X\right|^{2}-\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{D}(\dot{\gamma},X)X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle dt

(2.10)

where
\mathrm{Riem}^{D}
denotes the Riemann curvature tensor of the Levi-Civita connection which is defined for
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Riem}^{D}(X,Y)Z=D_{X}D_{Y}Z-D_{Y}D_{X}Z-D_{[X,Y]}Z.

(2.11)

Our goal is to give a formula for the index form on horizontal fields. As already pointed out the Levi-Civita connection does not preserve horizontality, so a first step is to compute
\mathrm{Riem}^{D}
in terms of
\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}
, the Riemann curvature tensor of
\nabla
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A
be the
(2,1)
tensor defined by

A_{X}Y=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)+\frac{1}{2}J_{X}Y+\frac{1}{2}J_{Y}X

The curvature operator of the Levi-Civita can be written as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Riem}^{D}(X,Y)Z=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,Y)Z+(\nabla_{X}A)_{Y}Z-(\nabla_{Y}A)_{X}Z

\displaystyle+A_{\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)}Z+A_{X}A_{Y}Z-A_{Y}A_{X}Z,

for all vector fields
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
.

Proof.

This type of computation is standard, we include it for the sake of completeness. By definition the curvature of
D
is

\mathrm{Riem}^{D}(X,Y)Z=D_{X}(D_{Y}Z)-D_{Y}(D_{X}Z)-D_{[X,Y]}Z.

Expand each
D
using
D=\nabla+A
:

\displaystyle D_{X}(D_{Y}Z)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}(\nabla_{Y}Z+A_{Y}Z)+A_{X}\big(\nabla_{Y}Z+A_{Y}Z\big)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z+\nabla_{X}\big(A_{Y}Z\big)+A_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z+A_{X}A_{Y}Z,

and similarly

\displaystyle D_{Y}(D_{X}Z)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z+\nabla_{Y}\big(A_{X}Z\big)+A_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z+A_{Y}A_{X}Z,

\displaystyle D_{[X,Y]}Z

\displaystyle=\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z+A_{[X,Y]}Z.

Subtracting, we get

\mathrm{Riem}^{D}(X,Y)Z=\big(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z\big)\\
+\big(\nabla_{X}\big(A_{Y}Z\big)-\nabla_{Y}\big(A_{X}Z\big)-A_{[X,Y]}Z\big)\\
+\big(A_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-A_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z\big)+\big(A_{X}A_{Y}Z-A_{Y}A_{X}Z\big).

The first parenthesis is
\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,Y)Z
. Expand the second parenthesis using the Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\big(A_{Y}Z\big)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{X}A)_{Y}Z+A_{\nabla_{X}Y}Z+A_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z,

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}\big(A_{X}Z\big)

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{Y}A)_{X}Z+A_{\nabla_{Y}X}Z+A_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z.

Hence

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\big(A_{Y}Z\big)-\nabla_{Y}\big(A_{X}Z\big)-A_{[X,Y]}Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}A)_{Y}Z-(\nabla_{Y}A)_{X}Z+A_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-A_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z+A_{\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X-[X,Y]}Z.

Note that by definition the subscript in the last term is
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)
. Substitute this into the expression for
\mathrm{Riem}^{D}
; the term
A_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-A_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z
cancels against the earlier
A_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-A_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z
term, leaving

\displaystyle\mathrm{Riem}^{D}(X,Y)Z=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,Y)Z+(\nabla_{X}A)_{Y}Z-(\nabla_{Y}A)_{X}Z

\displaystyle+A_{\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)}Z+A_{X}A_{Y}Z-A_{Y}A_{X}Z.

∎

With this lemma at hands, we are now in position to give a formula evaluating the index form (
2.10
) on horizontal vectors fields.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
and
\gamma:[0,r]\to M
be a unit speed geodesic. Then,

\displaystyle I(\gamma,X)=\int_{0}^{r}

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X+\frac{1}{2}(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X)_{\mathcal{H}}\right|^{2}-\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\nabla_{X}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(X,\dot{\gamma}),\dot{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}|J_{\dot{\gamma}}X|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\dot{\gamma})|^{2}+\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X),X),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle\,dt

Proof.

From the definition (
2.10
) we have

\displaystyle I(\gamma,X)=\int_{0}^{r}\left|D_{\dot{\gamma}}X\right|^{2}-\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{D}(\dot{\gamma},X)X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle dt.

(2.12)

Substitute the formula for
\mathrm{Riem}^{D}
given in Lemma
2.5
and go one by one over the terms appearing in (
2.12
). The first term is the covariant derivative:

\displaystyle|D_{\dot{\gamma}}X|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)+\frac{1}{2}J_{\dot{\gamma}}X\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X+\frac{1}{2}(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X)_{\mathcal{H}}\right|^{2}+\left|-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)+\frac{1}{2}(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X)_{\mathcal{V}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X+\frac{1}{2}(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X)_{\mathcal{H}}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left|(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X)_{\mathcal{V}}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X),J_{\dot{\gamma}}X\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X+\frac{1}{2}(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X)_{\mathcal{H}}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left|(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X)_{\mathcal{V}}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X),X),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

After
\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}
the next three terms are as follows:

\displaystyle(\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}A)_{X}X

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}(A_{X}X)-A_{\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X}X-A_{X}\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X,X)+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X)=0

\displaystyle\left\langle(\nabla_{X}A)_{\dot{\gamma}}X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{X}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(\dot{\gamma},X)+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{X}J)_{\dot{\gamma}}X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle(\nabla_{X}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(\dot{\gamma},X),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\left\langle A_{\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)}X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X),X)+\frac{1}{2}J_{\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)}X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X),X),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)|^{2}.

Finally, for the last term we get:

\displaystyle\left\langle(A_{\dot{\gamma}}A_{X}-A_{X}A_{\dot{\gamma}})X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle A_{X}A_{\dot{\gamma}}X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\langle A_{X}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle A_{X}J_{\dot{\gamma}}X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle J_{\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)}X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,J_{\dot{\gamma}}X),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{4}\left\langle J_{J_{\dot{\gamma}}X}X,\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X),X),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|J_{\dot{\gamma}}X|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X),X),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X)|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|J_{\dot{\gamma}}X|^{2}.

Collecting those terms yields our formula.
∎

2.4
The tensor
\mathfrak{R}

We now introduce the relevant
Ricci-like
tensor which will be the main character in our analysis of the horizontal Laplacian comparison theorems and the goal of the section is interpret this tensor geometrically. Define a
(2,1)
tensor by the formula: For
U,V\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}(U,V)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U,X_{i})X_{i},V\right\rangle-\left\langle(\nabla_{X_{i}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(X_{i},U),V\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U,X_{i}),X_{i}),V\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\langle J_{U}X_{i},J_{V}X_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},U),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},V)\rangle

where the
X_{i}
’s form an arbitrary orthonormal frame of horizontal vectors. Note that it is clear from the definition that
\mathfrak{R}(U,V)
does not depend on the choice of the
X_{i}
’s.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
U,V\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
and
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})

\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(V,X)X,U\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U,X)X,V\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U_{\mathcal{H}},X)X,V_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle.

Proof.

Since
\nabla
preserves horizontality, we have
\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U,X)X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
and therefore

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U,X)X,V\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U,X)X,V_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle.

Using then the fact that
\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U_{\mathcal{V}},X)
is skew-symmetric we get

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U_{\mathcal{V}},X)X,V_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U_{\mathcal{V}},X)V_{\mathcal{H}},X\rangle.

Recall then the Bianchi identity for connections with torsion
[
9
, Theorem 1.24]
: For arbitrary vector fields
A,B,C\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
.

\circlearrowleft\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(A,B)C\;=\;\circlearrowleft(\nabla_{A}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(B,C)\;+\;\circlearrowleft\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}\big(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(A,B),C\big),

(2.13)

where
\circlearrowleft
denotes the cyclic sum. Using the fact that the torsion is always vertical yields

\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U_{\mathcal{V}},X)V_{\mathcal{H}},X\rangle=-\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,V_{\mathcal{H}})U_{\mathcal{V}},X\rangle-\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(V_{\mathcal{H}},U_{\mathcal{V}})X,X\rangle.

Since
\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,V_{\mathcal{H}})U_{\mathcal{V}}
is vertical and
\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(V_{\mathcal{H}},U_{\mathcal{V}})
skew-symmetric we infer

\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U_{\mathcal{V}},X)X,V_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle=0

and conclude

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U,X)X,V\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U_{\mathcal{H}},X)X,V_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle.

The symmetry

\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(V,X)X,U\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U,X)X,V\right\rangle

follows from the same type of computations: first use the skew-symmetry of
\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}
and then Bianchi’s identity.
∎

In order to better understand
\mathfrak{R}
we introduce the following definitions/notations. As before, the
X_{i}
’s below form an arbitrary orthonormal frame of horizontal vectors and
U,V
are arbitrary vectors in
\mathcal{X}(TM)
.

•

The horizontal Ricci curvature if the connection
\nabla
is defined as the
(2,0)
tensor

\mathrm{Ric}^{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}}(U,V)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U,X_{i})X_{i},V\right.\rangle

•

The horizontal divergence of the torsion is defined as the
(1,1)
tensor

\delta^{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}}T(U)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{X_{i}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},U).

•

(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{U},\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{V})_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U,X_{i}),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V,X_{i})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

•

(J_{U},J_{V})_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle J_{U}X_{i},J_{V}X_{i}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

Remark 2.8
.

Assume that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
arises from a Riemannian submersion
\pi:(M,g)\to(B,h)
. Let
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
be basic vector fields on
M
, then it follows from Remark
2.2
that

\mathrm{Ric}^{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}}(X,Y)=\mathrm{Ric}_{B}(\pi_{*}X,\pi_{*}Y)

where
\mathrm{Ric}_{B}
is the Ricci curvature of the base manifold
(B,h)
.

With those definitions, we can decompose
\mathfrak{R}
as follows.

Proposition 2.9
.

(i)

For
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\mathfrak{R}(X,Y)=\mathfrak{R}(Y,X)=\mathrm{Ric}^{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}}(X,Y)-(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{X},\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{Y})_{\mathcal{H}}.

(ii)

For
U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\mathfrak{R}(U,X)=-(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{U},\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{X})_{\mathcal{H}}.

(iii)

For
U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\mathfrak{R}(X,U)=-\left\langle\delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X),U\right\rangle-2(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{U},\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{X})_{\mathcal{H}}.

(iv)

For
U,V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,

\mathfrak{R}(U,V)=\mathfrak{R}(V,U)=-\left\langle\delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U),V\right\rangle+\frac{1}{4}(J_{U},J_{V})_{\mathcal{H}}-2(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{U},\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{V})_{\mathcal{H}}.

Proof.

(i)

This follows from the definition of
\mathrm{Ric}^{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}}
, Lemma
2.7
and the fact that
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}
is always vertical.

(ii)

This is immediate from Lemma
2.7
and the fact that
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}
is always vertical.

(iii)

We have from (
2.5
)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,X_{i}),X_{i}),U\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,X_{i}),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U,X_{i})=(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{U},\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{X})_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle

and the other terms follow either from the definition or as before.

(iv)

The computation is similar to the others. Note that the symmetry
\mathfrak{R}(U,V)=\mathfrak{R}(V,U)
also follows from (
2.5
).

∎

The following corollary is straightforward.

Corollary 2.10
.

The tensor
\mathfrak{R}
is symmetric, i.e.
\mathfrak{R}(U,V)=\mathfrak{R}(V,U)
for every
U,V\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
if and only if the horizontal distribution is Yang-Mills, meaning that for every
U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X),U\right\rangle+(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{U},\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{X})_{\mathcal{H}}=0.

(2.14)

The terminology Yang-Mills for (
2.14
) originates from
[
9
, Definition 9.35]
. Notice that this condition is intrinsically sub-Riemannian, i.e. it only depends on
\mathcal{H}
and the restriction of
g
to
\mathcal{H}
and not on the metric on the leaves.

Example 2.11

(Carnot groups)
.

Consider the foliation on a Carnot group as in Proposition
2.1
. Computations show that:

(i)

For
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\mathfrak{R}(X,Y)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{ad}_{X_{i}}(X),\mathrm{ad}_{X_{i}}(Y)\right\rangle.

(ii)

For
U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\mathfrak{R}(U,X)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{ad}_{X_{i}}(X),\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{X_{i}}(U)\right\rangle.

(iii)

For
U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\mathfrak{R}(X,U)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{ad}_{X_{i}}(X),\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{X_{i}}(U)\right\rangle.

(iv)

For
U,V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}(U,V)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(J_{U},J_{V})_{\mathcal{H}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{ad}_{X_{i}}(U),\mathrm{ad}_{X_{i}}(V)\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{ad}_{X_{i}}(U),\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{X_{i}}(V)\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{ad}_{X_{i}}(U),\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{X_{i}}(V)\right\rangle.

In particular, in any Carnot group the horizontal distribution if Yang-Mills and there exists
K\leq 0
such that for every
U\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
,
\mathfrak{R}(U,U)\geq K|U|^{2}
.

To conclude this section, we point out how the formulas simplify when the foliation is totally geodesic. The computations are straightforward and follow from the fact that on totally geodesic foliations
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V})=0
.

Corollary 2.12
.

Assume that the foliation is totally geodesic.

(i)

For
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\mathfrak{R}(X,Y)=\mathrm{Ric}^{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}}(X,Y)-(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{X},\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}_{X})_{\mathcal{H}}.

(ii)

For
U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\mathfrak{R}(U,X)=0.

(iii)

For
U\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\mathfrak{R}(X,U)=-\left\langle\delta_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X),U\right\rangle.

(iv)

For
U,V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
,

\mathfrak{R}(U,V)=\frac{1}{4}(J_{U},J_{V})_{\mathcal{H}}.

2.5
Sub-Laplacian comparisons

We now turn to the comparison theorems.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
p\in M
and denote
r_{p}(x)=d(p,x)
. Assume that there exists
K\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
U\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(U,U)\geq K|U|^{2}.

Then, for
x\neq p
not in the cut-locus of
p
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}(x)\leq\begin{cases}\sqrt{nK}\cot\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}r_{p}(x)\right)&\text{if}\ K>0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{n}{r_{p}(x)}&\text{if}\ K=0,\\
\sqrt{n|K|}\coth\left(\sqrt{\frac{|K|}{n}}r_{p}(x)\right)&\text{if}\ K<0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\gamma:[0,r]\to M
be the unique unit speed geodesic between
p
and
x
. Note that since
\gamma
has unit speed,
r=r_{p}(x)
. Pick an arbitrary orthonormal horizontal frame
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}
at
x
. We have at
x

\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}r_{p}(v_{i},v_{i}).

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be the family of horizontal vector fields along
\gamma
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{i}+\frac{1}{2}(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{i})_{\mathcal{H}}=0\\
X_{i}(0)=v_{i}.\end{cases}

Note that we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\left\langle X_{i},X_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{i},X_{j}\right\rangle+\left\langle X_{i},\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{j}\right\rangle

(2.15)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\langle(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{i})_{\mathcal{H}},X_{j}\right\rangle+\left\langle X_{i},(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{j})_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\langle J_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{i},X_{j}\right\rangle+\left\langle X_{i},J_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{j}\right\rangle\right)=0.

Therefore
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
is an orthonormal frame along
\gamma
.
Consider now the vector fields along
\gamma
defined by

Y_{i}(t)=\frac{\mathfrak{s}_{K}(t)}{\mathfrak{s}_{K}(r)}X_{i}(t),\quad 0\leq t\leq r,

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}_{K}(t)=\begin{cases}\sin\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}t\right)&\text{if}\ K>0,\\
\displaystyle t&\text{if}\ K=0,\\
\sinh\left(\sqrt{\frac{|K|}{n}}t\right)&\text{if}\ K<0.\end{cases}

We have

Y_{i}(0)=0,Y_{i}(r)=v_{i}.

Using the index lemma and the fact that the
X_{i}
form a horizontal orthonormal frame, one has

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(\gamma,Y_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{r}\left|\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}Y_{i}+\frac{1}{2}(J_{\dot{\gamma}}Y_{i})_{\mathcal{H}}\right|^{2}-\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},Y_{i})Y_{i},\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\nabla_{Y_{i}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(Y_{i},\dot{\gamma}),\dot{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}|J_{\dot{\gamma}}Y_{i}|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(Y_{i},\dot{\gamma})|^{2}+\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},Y_{i}),Y_{i}),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{s}_{K}(r)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{r}\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{K}(t)^{2}-\mathfrak{s}_{K}(t)^{2}\left(\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X_{i})X_{i},\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\nabla_{X_{i}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(X_{i},\dot{\gamma}),\dot{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad-\frac{1}{4}|J_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{i}|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},\dot{\gamma})|^{2}+\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X_{i}),X_{i}),\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{s}_{K}(r)^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}n\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{K}(t)^{2}-\mathfrak{s}_{K}(t)^{2}\mathfrak{R}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{s}_{K}(r)^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}n\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{K}(t)^{2}-K\mathfrak{s}_{K}(t)^{2}dt.

The result follows after a straightforward computations.
∎

Besides the sub-Laplacian comparison theorem above, we will also need the following comparison result for the trace of second variation of the length of geodesics with
skewed
\nabla
-parallel
deformations at the endpoints. We first need to introduce some notations. Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\left\{(p,q)\in M\times M\mid p\neq q,\,p\text{ and }q\text{ are not in the cut locus of one another}\,\right\}.

(2.16)

Let
v_{i}
be an arbitrary horizontal orthonormal frame at
p
and let
\tilde{v}_{i}=X_{i}(r)
where the
X_{i}
’s are the family of horizontal vector fields along the unique unit speed geodesic
\gamma:[0,r]\to M
connecting
p
to
q
and such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{i}+\frac{1}{2}(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X_{i})_{\mathcal{H}}=0\\
X_{i}(0)=v_{i}.\end{cases}

It follows from the computation (
2.15
) that
\tilde{v}_{i}
is a orthonormal frame at
q
.

We then consider on
\mathcal{C}
a horizontal Laplacian with
skewed
\nabla
-parallel coupling
defined for a smooth function
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\Delta^{c}_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}}f(p,q)=\Delta^{1}_{\mathcal{H}}f(p,q)+\Delta^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}f(p,q)+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{1}\tilde{v}_{i}^{2}f(p,q)

(2.17)

In the formula for
\Delta^{c}_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}}
, the superscript
1
or
2
means the action on the first or the second set of
M
variables in the product
M\times M
. Also
v_{i}^{1}\tilde{v}_{i}^{2}f(p,q)
is defined as
V_{i}^{1}\tilde{V}_{i}^{2}f(p,q)
where
V_{i}
(resp.
\tilde{V}_{i}
) is any vector field that coincides with
v_{i}
(resp.
\tilde{v}_{i}
) at
p
(resp.
q
). Finally, note that the term
\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{1}\tilde{v}_{i}^{2}f
does not depend on the choice of the frame
v_{i}
.

Theorem 2.14
.

Assume that there exists
K\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
U\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(U,U)\geq K|U|^{2}.

Then, for
(p,q)\in\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\Delta^{c}_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}}d(p,q)\leq\begin{cases}-2n\sqrt{K}\tan\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}\frac{d(p,q)}{2}\right)&\text{if}\ K>0,\\
0&\text{if}\ K=0,\\
2n\sqrt{|K|}\tanh\left(\sqrt{\frac{|K|}{n}}\frac{d(p,q)}{2}\right)&\text{if}\ K<0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Consider geodesic curves
\sigma_{i}:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M
,
\tilde{\sigma}_{i}:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M
such that

\sigma_{i}(0)=p,\,\dot{\sigma}_{i}(0)=v_{i}

and

\tilde{\sigma}_{i}(0)=q,\,\dot{\tilde{\sigma}}_{i}(0)=\tilde{v}_{i}.

We have from the chain rule

\Delta^{c}_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}}d(p,q)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\right)_{s=0}d(\sigma_{i}(s),\tilde{\sigma}_{i}(s)).

Therefore, from the second variation formula
[
14
, Theorem 10.12]
and the index lemma one deduces that

\Delta^{c}_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}}d(p,q)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(\gamma,Y_{i})

where the
Y_{i}
’s are arbitrary vector fields along
\gamma
such that

Y_{i}(0)=v_{i},\,\,Y_{i}(r)=\tilde{v}_{i}.

Pick

Y_{i}(t)=\mathfrak{c}_{K}(t)X_{i}(t),

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{K}(t)=\begin{cases}\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}t\right)+\frac{1-\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}t\right)}{\sin\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}r\right)}\sin\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}t\right)&\text{if}\ K>0,\\
\displaystyle 1&\text{if}\ K=0,\\
\cosh\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}t\right)+\frac{1-\cosh\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}t\right)}{\sinh\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}r\right)}\sinh\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}t\right)&\text{if}\ K<0.\end{cases}

By a computation similar to the one in the proof of Theorem
2.13
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(\gamma,Y_{i})\leq\int_{0}^{r}n\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{K}(t)^{2}-K\mathfrak{c}_{K}(t)^{2}dt

and the conclusion follows from evaluating the above integral.
∎

2.6
A Bonnet-Myers type theorem

A classical application of Laplacian comparison is the Bonnet-Myers. In our setting we get the following result.

Corollary 2.15

(Bonnet-Myers type theorem)
.

Assume that there exists
K>0
such that for every
U\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(U,U)\geq K|U|^{2},

then
M
is compact and

\mathbf{diam}(M)\leq\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{K}}.

Proof.

Let
p\in M
. From Theorem
2.13
, one has for
x\neq p
, not in the cut-locus of
p

\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}(x)\leq\sqrt{nK}\cot\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n}}r_{p}(x)\right).

We deduce from Calabi’s lemma that any point
x
such that
d(p,x)\geq\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{K}}
has to be in the cut-locus of
p
.
Let now
x\in\mathbb{M}
arbitrary. If
x
is not in the cut-locus of
p
, then
d(p,x)<\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{K}}
. If
x
is in the cut-locus of
p
then for every
\eta>0
there is at least one point
y
in the open ball with center
x
and radius
\eta
such that
y
is not in the cut-locus of
p
. Thus
d(p,x)\leq\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{K}}+\eta
. We conclude
d(p,x)\leq\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{K}}
since
\eta
is arbitrary.
∎

3
Radial processes, parallel couplings and applications to the horizontal semigroup

Let
((\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0},(\mathbb{P}_{x})_{x\in M})
be the subelliptic
diffusion process generated by
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
and let
\zeta
denote its
lifetime. We will refer to
\xi

as the horizontal Brownian motion of the foliation or as the
sub-Riemannian Brownian motion. Note that our horizontal Brownian motion is normalized to have
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
as its generator, rather than
\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
. By the hypoellipticity of
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\xi
admits a smooth heat
kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
. The horizontal heat semigroup with generator
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is given by

P_{t}f(x)=\int_{M}p_{t}(x,y)f(y)d\mu(y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(f(\xi_{t})1_{t<\zeta}\right)

where
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded Borel function and we recall that
\mu
is the Riemannian volume measure.

3.1
Radial part of the horizontal Brownian motion and stochastic completeness

The following theorem was proved in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Take
p\in M
and as before set
r_{p}(x)=d(p,x)
. For each
x_{1}\in M
, if
\xi_{0}=x_{1}
, then there
exists a non-decreasing continuous process
l_{t}
which increases only
when
\xi_{t}
is in the cut-locus of
p
and a martingale
\beta_{t}
on
\mathbb{R}
with quadratic variation satisfying
d\left<\beta\right>\leq 2\,dt
such that

r_{p}(\xi_{t\wedge\zeta})=r_{p}(x_{1})+\beta_{t\wedge\zeta}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\zeta}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}(\xi_{s})ds-l_{t\wedge\zeta}

(3.1)

holds
\mathbb{P}_{x_{1}}
-almost surely.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that there exists
K\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
U\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(U,U)\geq K|U|^{2}.

Then for every
x\in M
,
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\zeta<+\infty)=1
. In particular, the semigroup
P_{t}
is stochastically complete meaning that for every
x\in M
and
t\geq 0

P_{t}1(x)=1.

Proof.

We can assume
K<0
. As before, we fix a point
p\in\mathbb{M}
. For
x_{1}\in\mathbb{M}
, we consider
the solution of the stochastic differential equation

\tilde{r}_{t}=d(p,x_{1})+\sqrt{n|K|}\int_{0}^{t}\coth\left(\sqrt{\frac{|K|}{n}}\tilde{r}_{t}\right)ds+\beta_{t}

(3.2)

where
\beta
is the martingale defined in Theorem

3.1
. It follows from Theorem
3.1
and the Ikeda-Watanabe
comparison theorem
[
12
]
that for
t<\zeta
, one has

\mathbb{P}_{x_{1}}
a.s.

d(x_{0},\xi_{t})\leq\tilde{r}_{t}.

The result follows since
\tilde{r}_{t}
can not explode given it has a quadratic variation less than
2t
and the drift term is controlled because
\coth(r)
is uniformly bounded for large
r
.
∎

Using the moment estimates method in
[
15
]
we can also get exit time estimates for the horizontal Brownian motion.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that there exists
K\leq 0
such that for every
U\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(U,U)\geq K|U|^{2}.

There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
such that for every
x\in M
,
r\geq 0
and
t>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}d(x,\xi_{t})\geq r\right)\leq\begin{cases}c_{1}\exp\left(-c_{2}\frac{\sqrt{|K|}}{e^{c_{3}\sqrt{|K|}t}-1}r^{2}\right)\text{ if }K<0\\
c_{1}\exp\left(-c_{2}\frac{r^{2}}{t}\right)\text{ if }K=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Proceed as in Theorem 1.6 in
[
15
]
; the context is a little different there but all arguments go through.
∎

Note that the stochastic completeness and exit time estimate yields on diagonal heat kernel estimates.

Corollary 3.4
.

Assume that there exists
K\leq 0
such that for every
U\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(U,U)\geq K|U|^{2}.

Then, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
such that for every
x\in M
and
t>0

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,x)\geq\begin{cases}\frac{c_{1}}{\mu(B(x,c_{2}\sqrt{t}))},\text{ if }K=0,\\
\frac{c_{1}}{\mu\left(B\left(x,c_{2}\left(\frac{e^{c_{3}\sqrt{|K|}t}-1}{\sqrt{|K|}}\right)^{1/2}\right)\right)},\text{ if }K<0.\end{cases}

Proof.

For simplicity, we show the argument in the case
K=0
, the case
K<0
being identical. For
r>0
and
x\in M
denote
B(x,r)
the open ball with center
x
and radius
r
. We have then

\displaystyle\int_{M\setminus B(x,r)}p_{t}(x,y)d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}(\xi_{t}\notin B(x,r))

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}d(x,\xi_{t})\geq r\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\exp\left(-c_{2}\frac{r^{2}}{t}\right)

Then, from stochastic completeness we have

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,r)}p_{t}(x,y)d\mu(y)=1-\int_{M\setminus B(x,r)}p_{t}(x,y)d\mu(y)\geq 1-c_{1}\exp\left(-c_{2}\frac{r^{2}}{t}\right).

Apply Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(x,r)}p_{t}(x,y)d\mu(y)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left(B(x,r)\right)\int_{B(x,r)}p_{t}(x,y)^{2}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left(B(x,r)\right)\int_{M}p_{t}(x,y)^{2}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\mu\left(B(x,r)\right)p_{2t}(x,x).

Therefore we obtained

p_{2t}(x,x)\geq\left(1-c_{1}\exp\left(-c_{2}\frac{r^{2}}{t}\right)\right)\frac{1}{\mu\left(B(x,r)\right)}.

Choosing
r=A\sqrt{t}
with
A
large enough yields the conclusion.
∎

3.2
Lipschitz bound on the heat semigroup and coupling by parallel transport

Given a Lipschitz function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
we denote

\mathrm{Lip}(f)=\sup_{x,y\in M,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)}.

and
\mathbf{Lip}_{b}(M)
the set of bounded Lipschitz functions on
M
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume that there exists
K\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
U\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(U,U)\geq K|U|^{2}.

Then for every
f\in\mathbf{Lip}_{b}(M)
and
t\geq 0
,
P_{t}f\in\mathbf{Lip}_{b}(M)
and we have

\mathrm{Lip}(P_{t}f)\leq e^{-Kt}\mathrm{Lip}(f).

Proof.

The proof relies on a coupling by parallel transport. Define the coupling map as follows. Recall the definition of
\mathcal{C}
given by (
2.16
). For
p,q\in\mathcal{C}
define the transport map
\mathfrak{P}_{p\to q}:T_{p}M\to T_{q}M
given for
v\in T_{p}M
by
\mathfrak{P}_{p\to q}(v)=X(r)
where
X
is the horizontal vector field along the unit speed geodesic
\gamma:[0,r]\to M
with endpoints
p,q
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X+\frac{1}{2}(J_{\dot{\gamma}}X)_{\mathcal{H}}=0\\
X(0)=v.\end{cases}

It follows from the computation (
2.15
) that
\mathfrak{P}_{p\to q}
is an isometry
\mathcal{H}_{p}\to\mathcal{H}_{q}
.
Consider now a horizontal Brownian motion
\xi_{t}
started from
p\in M
. For
q\in M
such that
(p,q)\in\mathcal{C}
we can construct a process
\tilde{\xi}_{t}
that solves the stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d\tilde{\xi}_{t}=\mathfrak{P}_{p\to q}\circ d\xi_{t}\\
\tilde{\xi}_{0}=q.\end{cases}

This process
\tilde{\xi}
is well defined up to the random time
\tau_{p,q}=\inf\{t\geq 0|(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})\in\mathcal{C}\}
. For simplicity of the argument, we assume in the following that
\tau_{p,q}=+\infty
a.s. If not, the technical difficulty can be overcome using the known and now classical techniques to extend couplings
beyond the cut-locus
, see Wang
[
17
]
&
[
18
, Theorem 2.3.2]
and Kuwada
[
13
]
. Note that in such constructions the two processes become identical in the case they meet.

By construction our process
(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})
is then a diffusion process with generator
\Delta^{c}_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}}
given by (
2.17
). When applying Itô’s formula, one sees that the local martingale part of
d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})
is the same as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\nabla^{1}d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t}),\circ d\xi_{t}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla^{2}d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t}),\circ d\tilde{\xi}_{t}\right\rangle

(3.3)

where
\nabla^{1}
is the gradient with respect to the first variable and
\nabla^{2}
the gradient with respect to the second variable. Since
|\nabla^{1}d|=|\nabla^{2}d|=1
and both
\xi
and
\tilde{\xi}
are horizontal Brownian motions, the quadratic variation of (
3.4
) is bounded above by
8t
. Thus (
3.4
) is a martingale. One deduces

\mathbb{E}(d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t}))\leq d(p,q)+\int_{0}^{t}\Delta^{c}_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}}d(\xi_{s},\tilde{\xi}_{s})ds

Thanks to Theorem
2.14
and trivial estimates on the functions
\tan
or
\tanh
we therefore obtain

\mathbb{E}(d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t}))\leq d(p,q)-K\int_{0}^{t}d(\xi_{s},\tilde{\xi}_{s})ds.

This gives

\mathbb{E}(d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t}))\leq d(p,q)e^{-Kt}.

Let now
f\in\mathbf{Lip}_{b}(M)
. One has

\displaystyle|P_{t}f(q)-P_{t}f(p)|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\left(f(\tilde{\xi}_{t})\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(f(\xi_{t})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\left|f(\tilde{\xi}_{t})-f(\xi_{t})\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Lip}(f)\mathbb{E}\left(d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Lip}(f)d(p,q)e^{-Kt}.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is dense in
M\times M
and
P_{t}f
continuous by hypoellipticity of
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
we conclude that for every
p,q\in M
,

|P_{t}f(q)-P_{t}f(p)|\leq\mathrm{Lip}(f)e^{-Kt}d(p,q).

∎

Remark 3.6
.

Estimates of the type
\mathrm{Lip}(P_{t}f)\leq e^{-Kt}\mathrm{Lip}(f)
play an important role in the theory of generalized curvature dimension inequalities
[
4
,
5
]
.

Example 3.7
.

Since any Carnot group satisfies
\mathfrak{R}(U,U)\geq K|U|^{2}
for some
K\leq 0
, we deduce the estimate
\mathrm{Lip}(P_{t}f)\leq e^{-Kt}\mathrm{Lip}(f)
.

Interestingly, in the case of Riemannian foliations with totally geodesic leaves one can obtain stronger gradient bounds for the heat semigroups under slightly different assumptions.

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume that the foliation is totally geodesic and that there exists
K\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
U\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(U,U)-\frac{1}{4}(J_{U},J_{U})_{\mathcal{H}}\geq K|U|^{2}.

Then for every
f\in\mathbf{Lip}_{b}(M)\cap C^{2}(M)
and
t\geq 0
,
P_{t}f\in\mathbf{Lip}_{b}(M)
and we have

|\nabla P_{t}f|(x)\leq e^{-Kt}P_{t}|\nabla f|(x),\quad x\in M.

Proof.

We use a different coupling. The idea is to consider the following connection

\overset{\circ}{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+J_{X}Y.

Since the foliation is totally geodesic one can check that
\overset{\circ}{\nabla}
satisfies the following properties which will be key in the following arguments:

(i)

\overset{\circ}{\nabla}
is a metric connection;

(ii)

For every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
and
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
,
\overset{\circ}{\nabla}_{Y}X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
;

(iii)

The geodesic equation can be written
\overset{\circ}{\nabla}_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=0
, see (
2.9
).

For
p,q\in\mathcal{C}
define the transport map
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{P}}_{p\to q}:T_{p}M\to T_{q}M
given for
v\in T_{p}M
by
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{P}}_{p\to q}(v)=\overset{\circ}{v}
where
\overset{\circ}{v}
is the
\overset{\circ}{\nabla}
-parallel transport of
v
along the unit speed geodesic
\gamma:[0,r]\to M
with endpoints
p,q
. As before, consider a horizontal Brownian motion
\xi_{t}
started from
p\in M
. For
q\in M
such that
(p,q)\in\mathcal{C}
we construct the process
\tilde{\xi}_{t}
that solves the stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d\tilde{\xi}_{t}=\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{P}}_{p\to q}\circ d\xi_{t}\\
\tilde{\xi}_{0}=q.\end{cases}

As before, we will ignore the genuine but inessential difficulties related to cut-locus and assume that the couple
(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})
is defined for all
t
’s.

By construction, the process is a diffusion on
M\times M
with generator

\displaystyle\overset{\circ}{\Delta}\,^{c}_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}}f(p,q)=\Delta^{1}_{\mathcal{H}}f(p,q)+\Delta^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}f(p,q)+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{1}\overset{\circ}{v}\,^{2}_{i}f(p,q).

In Itô’s formula the local martingale part of
d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})
is the same as the local martingale part of

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\nabla^{1}d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t}),\circ d\xi_{t}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla^{2}d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t}),\circ d\tilde{\xi}_{t}\right\rangle

(3.4)

which vanishes since
\nabla^{1}d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})+\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{P}}_{\tilde{\xi}_{t}\to\xi_{t}}\nabla^{2}d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})=0
from the fact that any geodesic satisfies
\overset{\circ}{\nabla}_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=0
.

We now need an estimate on
\overset{\circ}{\Delta}\,^{c}_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}}d(p,q)
. The argument is essentially the same as in the proof of Theorem
2.13
. From the index lemma one has

\overset{\circ}{\Delta}\,^{c}_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}}d(p,q)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(\gamma,Y_{i})

where the
Y_{i}
’s are arbitrary vector fields along
\gamma
such that

Y_{i}(0)=v_{i},\,\,Y_{i}(r)=\overset{\circ}{v}_{i}.

Pick

Y_{i}(t)=\mathfrak{c}_{K}(t)X_{i}(t),

where
X_{i}
is the
\overset{\circ}{\nabla}
-parallel transport of
v_{i}
along
\gamma
and
\mathfrak{c}_{K}
is as in the proof of Theorem
2.13
. We then compute

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(\gamma,Y_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{r}\left|\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}Y_{i}+\frac{1}{2}J_{\dot{\gamma}}Y_{i}\right|^{2}-\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},Y_{i})Y_{i},\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\nabla_{Y_{i}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(Y_{i},\dot{\gamma}),\dot{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}|J_{\dot{\gamma}}Y_{i}|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(Y_{i},\dot{\gamma})|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{r}\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{K}(t)^{2}-\mathfrak{c}_{K}(t)^{2}\left(\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(\dot{\gamma},X_{i})X_{i},\dot{\gamma}\right\rangle+\left\langle(\nabla_{X_{i}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(X_{i},\dot{\gamma}),\dot{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},\dot{\gamma})|^{2}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}n\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{K}(t)^{2}-\mathfrak{c}_{K}(t)^{2}\left(\mathfrak{R}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})+\frac{1}{4}(J_{\dot{\gamma}},J_{\dot{\gamma}})_{\mathcal{H}}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{r}n\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{K}(t)^{2}-K\mathfrak{c}_{K}(t)^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq-Kr.

Therefore, we have

d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})\leq d(p,q)-K\int_{0}^{t}d(\xi_{s},\tilde{\xi}_{s})ds

which yields

d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})\leq d(p,q)e^{-Kt}.

Let now
f\in\mathbf{Lip}_{b}(M)\cap C^{2}(M)
. One has

\displaystyle|P_{t}f(q)-P_{t}f(p)|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\left(f(\tilde{\xi}_{t})\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(f(\xi_{t})\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\left|f(\tilde{\xi}_{t})-f(\xi_{t})\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(d(\xi_{t},\tilde{\xi}_{t})\sup_{\rho\in B(\xi_{t},d(p,q)e^{-Kt})}\left|\nabla f(\rho)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq d(p,q)e^{-Kt}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{\rho\in B(\xi_{t},d(p,q)e^{-Kt})}\left|\nabla f(\rho)\right|\right).

Using the density of
\mathcal{C}
in
M\times M
and letting
q\to p
yields

|\nabla P_{t}f|(p)\leq e^{-Kt}P_{t}|\nabla f|(p).

∎

As an example of application of the previous result, consider the case of Carnot groups. Consider a Carnot group
G
of step 2 equipped with a foliation
(\mathcal{F},g)
like in Proposition
2.1
. This foliation is totally geodesic.
Consider then the canonical variation of the metric
g
given by

g_{\varepsilon}=g_{\mathcal{H}}\oplus\frac{1}{\varepsilon}g_{\mathcal{V}},\quad\varepsilon>0.

One can easily check that the foliation
(\mathcal{F},g_{\varepsilon})
is Riemannian and totally geodesic for every
\varepsilon>0
. Denote
\mathfrak{R}_{\varepsilon}
the tensor
\mathfrak{R}
of
(\mathcal{F},g_{\varepsilon})
. It follows from Proposition
2.9
that for
U,V\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{\varepsilon}(U,V)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\langle J_{U}X_{i},J_{V}X_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},U),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},V)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}}.

Therefore there exists a constant
K<0
such that for
U\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{\varepsilon}(U)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\langle J_{U}X_{i},J_{V}X_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\geq-\frac{K}{\varepsilon}|U|^{2}_{\varepsilon}.

Applying Theorem
3.8
we recover a gradient bound first observed in
[
2
]
:

\sqrt{|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f|^{2}+\varepsilon|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}P_{t}f|^{2}}\leq e^{Kt/\varepsilon}P_{t}\left(\sqrt{|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f|^{2}+\varepsilon|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}P_{t}f|^{2}}\right).

Note that choosing
\varepsilon=t
yields the following result:

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
G
be a Carnot group of step 2 and
P_{t}
be the sub-Riemannian semigroup. There exists a constant
C>1
such that for every
f\in\mathbf{Lip}_{b}(M)\cap C^{2}(M)

|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f|+t|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}P_{t}f|\leq C\left[P_{t}\left(|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|\right)+tP_{t}\left(|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|\right)\right]

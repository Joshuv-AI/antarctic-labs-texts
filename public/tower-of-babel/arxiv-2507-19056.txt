Determination of the sensitivity of 𝚲 \Lambda and 𝚲 𝒄 + \Lambda^{+}_{c} electric dipole moments using a full angular analysis
E. Kou

Abstract
A search for a non-zero Electric Dipole Moment (EDM) of particles, which is a clear signal of a violation of CP symmetry, is one of the unique ways to discover physics beyond the Standard Model. In this paper, we discuss a method for determining the EDM of baryons from the full angular distribution of final particles in electron-positron pair annihilation processes. The question of how accurately the state-of-the-art experiments can determine EDM of Λ \Lambda and Λ c + \Lambda_{c}^{+} baryons is discussed in detail. Investigating the pseudo-statistics that corresponds to the BESIII experiment, the estimated sensitivity for Λ \Lambda EDM is obtained at the level of 10 − 18 10^{-18} e ⋅ \cdot cm. The similar figure for the proposed Super Tau-Charm Facility (STCF) experiment is found to be of the order of 10 − 20 10^{-20} e ⋅ \cdot cm. For the Λ c + \Lambda_{c}^{+} EDM, the calculated sensitivity for the STCF experiment is 10 − 16 10^{-16} e ⋅ \cdot cm. The case of a polarized initial electron is considered separately as such an option is planned at the STCF experiment.

Introduction

The search for electric dipole moments (EDMs) of elementary particles has been going on for many years. Observing a nonzero EDM of an elementary particle means a simultaneous breaking of P and CP symmetries.
There are two sources of CP symmetry breaking in the Standard Model (SM). One is the unremovable
complex phase
\delta_{KM}
in the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix. Another source is a possible term in the QCD Lagrangian, the so-called
\theta
term. Although
\theta
is unknown, the observed restriction on the
neutron EDM suggests that
\theta<10^{-(9\pm 1)}

[
1
]
. Since the natural scale is of

\theta\sim O(1)
, the very small value of
\theta
(known as the strong CP problem) requires an explanation.

In this article, we investigate a possible measurement of the hyperonic EDMs.
Currently, the experimental constraint on the hyperon EDM is rather weak:

d_{\Lambda}<1.5\times 10^{-16}
e
\cdot
cm, CL=95%
[
2
]
.
This value is very large compared to the most stringent constraint for baryons measured for the neutron,

d_{n}<1.8\times 10^{-26}
e
\cdot
cm, CL=90%
[
3
]
.
For the EDM of
\Lambda^{+}_{c}
, there are no direct experimental constraints at all as of today.
On the other hand, there is a new experimental proposal to measure the
\Lambda^{+}_{c}
EDM using bent crystals, which would achieve an EDM constraint of the order of
10^{-16}
e
\cdot
cm
[
4
]
. That proposal is based on phenomenon of the baryon spin precession in strong field of a bent crystal when the baryon
moves in the channeling regime in the crystal
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.
In the present article we discuss an alternative method to obtain the EDMs of
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{+}_{c}
hyperons by analyzing their decay products.

A large number of
\Lambda
hyperon-antihyperon pairs can be easily produced from
J/\psi
decays at tau-charm factories. For example, the BESIII experiment is capable of generating
10^{10}

J/\psi
that produce
16\times 10^{6}

\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
pairs per year
[
9
]
. The
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
pairs can also be produced directly from the electron-positron annihilation to a photon.
The STCF project is capable of producing
5.6\times 10^{8}

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
pairs
[
10
]
. The identification of hyperon-antihyperon pairs is usually performed with very high accuracy, exceeding
95\%

[
10
]
.
The process of hyperon-antihyperon pair production in electron-positron annihilation via an intermediate photon or
J/\psi
allow to measure the cross section, thus the Electric Dipole (ED) form-factor. Then, the EDM can be obtained by an analytical continuation of the form-factor from the region of nonzero squared
four-momentum transfer,
q^{2}\neq 0
, to
q^{2}=0
.

The present paper investigates a method for estimating EDM from the full angular analysis for the following processes:
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow J/\psi\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}\rightarrow p\pi^{-}\bar{p}\pi^{+}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\pi^{+}\bar{\Lambda}\pi^{-}
. These processes are analyzed with the statistics estimated for the BESIII and STCF experiments; the polarization of the initial electron is considered, and the sensitivity of the EDM depending on this parameter is also investigated. For the former of the above processes, a full angular analysis has already been performed

[
11
,
12
]
, but these studies did not consider EDM effects.
For the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}

e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
was carried out without taking into account the baryon decay, also without considering EDM terms
[
13

. In our work, we extend these analyses by including the effects associated with EDMs.

Note that for the EDM of
\Lambda

formalism of helicity amplitudes was recently applied in Ref.
[
14
]
. In our paper,
direct calculation of the cross section for polarized hyperon and antihyperon is carried out
for both cases of polarized and unpolarized electron beams.
The computational method follows those developed widely for unstable polarised fermion-antifermion pair production
in the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation process. Indeed, such processes attracted considerable attention in the literature, in particular, the study of CP violation and anomalous couplings in
the
t\bar{t}
production
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

\tau^{+}\tau^{-}

[

. Of course this list of the references is far from being complete. We should also mention the study
[
30
]
of the
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
production in which the accent was placed on the radiative return method and its implementation in the Monte Carlo generator PHOKHARA, although the EDM effects were not included.

2
Theoretical framework

Let us write down the Lorentz-invariant matrix element of the process
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}\,(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{s}\bar{v}(k_{2})\gamma_{\mu}u(k_{1})

(2.1)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\bar{u}(p_{1})\left\{G_{M}(s)\gamma^{\mu}+\frac{(p_{2}-p_{1})^{\mu}}{2M}\Big(\frac{G_{E}(s)-G_{M}(s)}{\gamma^{2}-1}-i\gamma_{5}F_{3}(s)\Big)\right\}v(p_{2}),

where
G_{E}(s)
(
G_{M}(s)
) is electric (magnetic) form-factor and
F_{3}(s)
is ED form-factor, where

s=(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}=(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}
and
\gamma=\sqrt{s}/(2M)
is
the Lorentz factor.
Also here
M
is the mass of the baryon
\Lambda
(
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
),
k_{1}
(
k_{2}
) is the four-momentum of the electron (positron),
p_{1}
(
p_{2}
) is the four-momentum of the baryon (antibaryon).

The cross-section of the process
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}\,(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-})
is

\dfrac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}\,(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}))}{d\Omega}=\dfrac{\beta}{64\pi^{2}s}\overline{|\mathcal{M}|^{2}},

(2.2)

where
\beta=\sqrt{1-4{M^{2}}/{s}}
is the baryon velocity.

To obtain the cross-section of the two-step process
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}\rightarrow p\pi^{-}\bar{p}\pi^{+}
(
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\pi^{+}\bar{\Lambda}\pi^{-}
), we introduce polarization of the intermediate baryons
and denote
\vec{s}
as the polarization of the
\Lambda
(
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
),

\vec{s}^{\,\prime}
as the polarization of the
\bar{\Lambda}
(
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
), defined in their corresponding rest frames.
The Cartesian components of the vectors
\vec{s}=(s_{x},\,s_{y},\,s_{z})
and

\vec{s}^{\,\prime}=(s^{\prime}_{x},\,s^{\prime}_{y},\,s^{\prime}_{z})
are defined in the frame with OZ axis along the momentum
\vec{p}_{1}
of baryon
\Lambda
(
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
), the plane XZ
is spanned on the momenta of electron
\vec{k}_{1}
and
\Lambda
(
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
) baryon
\vec{p}_{1}
,
and OY axis is along
\vec{p}_{1}\times\vec{k}_{1}
.

We also include the polarization of the electron. For the ultra-relativistic electron one needs only the longitudinal component with degree of polarization
\lambda_{e}
(
|\lambda_{e}|\leq 1
).

We consider the two-body nonleptonic decays of
\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
(
1/2^{+}\rightarrow 1/2^{+}0^{-}

e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}\rightarrow p\pi^{-}\bar{p}\pi^{+}
can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}\rightarrow p\pi^{-}\bar{p}\pi^{+})}{d\Omega d\Omega_{-}d\Omega_{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda})}{d\Omega}\Bigg|_{{\vec{s}\rightarrow\alpha_{-}\vec{n},\;\vec{s}^{\,\prime}\rightarrow\alpha_{+}\vec{n}^{\,\prime}}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}{\rm Br}(\Lambda\rightarrow p\pi^{-}){\rm Br}(\bar{\Lambda}\rightarrow\bar{p}\pi^{+}),

\displaystyle\vec{n}=(\sin\theta_{-}\cos\phi_{-},\,\sin\theta_{-}\sin\phi_{-},\,\cos\theta_{-}),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\vec{n}^{\,\prime}=(\sin\theta_{+}\cos\phi_{+},\,\sin\theta_{+}\sin\phi_{+},\,\cos\theta_{+}),

where the angles
\theta_{-}
,
\phi_{-}
and
\theta_{+}
,
\phi_{+}
(see Fig.
1
) describe
direction of momenta of the proton and antiproton in the rest frame of
\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
,
respectively.
Further,
d\Omega=\sin\theta d\theta d\phi
with
\theta
being the angle between the
momenta of
e^{-}
and
\Lambda
in the center-of-mass system (cms), and

\alpha_{-}
and
\alpha_{+}
are asymmetry parameters (polarization analyzers) in the

\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
decays
[
2
]
.
The latter are defined through the decay width of a hyperon in the rest frame:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma}\frac{d\Gamma(\Lambda\to p\pi^{-})}{d\Omega}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl(1+\alpha_{-}\vec{s}\,\vec{n}\bigr),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma}\frac{d\Gamma(\bar{\Lambda}\to\bar{p}\pi^{+})}{d\Omega}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl(1+\alpha_{+}\vec{s}^{\,\prime}\,\vec{n}^{\,\prime}\bigr),

where
\Gamma
is the width of the
\Lambda
(
\bar{\Lambda}
) decay into the channel

p\pi^{-}
(
\bar{p}\pi^{+}
).

For the process
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\pi^{+}\bar{\Lambda}\pi^{-}
the description is similar, only the meaning of indices “+” and “-” is reversed now because they refer to
the charge of the final
\pi
-meson. Therefore, instead of
\alpha_{-}
and
\alpha_{+}
, we use in this case the notation

\alpha
for decay of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
and
\bar{\alpha}
for decay of
\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
.

One can decompose the cross-section of the process with a polarized electron and polarized baryons as

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda})}{d\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda})}{d\Omega}\Bigg|_{no\,EDM}+\dfrac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda})}{d\Omega}\Bigg|_{EDM}

(2.7)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda})}{d\Omega}\Bigg|_{\lambda_{e}}.

In the following each of the contributions to the cross-section (
2.7
) is considered.
To simplify the expressions we separate the phases in the complex form factors

G_{E}=|G_{E}|e^{i\Phi_{E}},\quad G_{M}=|G_{M}|e^{i\Phi_{M}},\quad F_{3}=|F_{3}|e^{i\Phi_{3}},

(2.8)

and introduce phase differences
\Delta\Phi_{EM}\equiv\Phi_{E}-\Phi_{M}
and

\Delta\Phi_{M3}\equiv\Phi_{M}-\Phi_{3}
.

It is also convenient to define

\Sigma_{\psi}=\gamma^{2}|G_{M}|^{2}+|G_{E}|^{2},

(2.9)

and parameters
\alpha_{\psi}
and
f_{\psi}
:

\alpha_{\psi}=\dfrac{\gamma^{2}|G_{M}|^{2}-|G_{E}|^{2}}{\Sigma_{\psi}},\qquad f_{\psi}=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}|F_{3}|\gamma^{2}\beta}{\sqrt{\Sigma_{\psi}}}.

(2.10)

Let us begin with the cross-section without EDM and electron polarization

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda})}{d\Omega}\Bigg|_{no\,EDM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\beta e^{4}\Sigma_{\psi}}{2^{10}\pi^{2}\gamma^{4}M^{2}}\Big\{1+\alpha_{\psi}\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta(s_{x}s^{\prime}_{x}-\alpha_{\psi}s_{y}s^{\prime}_{y})

(2.11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\alpha_{\psi}+\cos^{2}\theta)s_{z}s^{\prime}_{z}+\cos\Delta\Phi_{EM}\sin\theta\cos\theta\sqrt{1-\alpha_{\psi}^{2}}(s_{x}s^{\prime}_{z}+s_{z}s^{\prime}_{x})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sin\Delta\Phi_{EM}\sin\theta\cos\theta\sqrt{1-\alpha_{\psi}^{2}}(s_{y}+s^{\prime}_{y})\Big\},

Next, consider the EDM term, in which we keep only the terms linear in
f_{\psi}

as one can expect that
|F_{3}|\ll|G_{E}|,|G_{M}|
. It has the form

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda})}{d\Omega}\Bigg|_{EDM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\psi}\dfrac{\beta e^{4}\Sigma_{\psi}}{2^{10}\pi^{2}\gamma^{4}M^{2}}\Big\{\cos(\Delta\Phi_{EM}+\Delta\Phi_{M3})\sin^{2}\theta\sqrt{1-\alpha_{\psi}}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle(s_{x}s^{\prime}_{y}-s_{y}s^{\prime}_{x})-\cos\Delta\Phi_{M3}\sin\theta\cos\theta\sqrt{1+\alpha_{\psi}}(s_{y}s^{\prime}_{z}-s_{z}s^{\prime}_{y})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sin\Delta\Phi_{M3}\sin\theta\cos\theta\sqrt{1+\alpha_{\psi}}(s_{x}-s^{\prime}_{x})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sin(\Delta\Phi_{EM}+\Delta\Phi_{M3})\sin^{2}\theta\sqrt{1-\alpha_{\psi}}(s_{z}-s^{\prime}_{z})\Big\}.

Here the polarization of the initial electron is not included.

Adding the terms with the electron polarization, we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda})}{d\Omega}\Bigg|_{\lambda_{e}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{e}\dfrac{\beta e^{4}\Sigma_{\psi}}{2^{9}\pi^{2}\gamma^{4}M^{2}}\Big\{\cos{\theta}(1+\alpha_{\psi})(s_{z}+s^{\prime}_{z})++\sqrt{1-\alpha_{\psi}^{2}}

(2.13)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sin\theta\Big[\sin\Delta\Phi_{EM}(s_{y}s^{\prime}_{z}+s_{z}s^{\prime}_{x})+\cos\Delta\Phi_{EM}(s_{x}+s^{\prime}_{x})\Big]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f_{\psi}\sqrt{1+\alpha_{\psi}}\sin\theta\Big[\sin\Delta\Phi_{M3}(s_{z}s^{\prime}_{x}-s_{x}s^{\prime}_{z})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\cos\Delta\Phi_{M3}(s_{y}-s^{\prime}_{y})\Big]\Big\}.

Appendix
A
presents some details of derivation of these equations.

Finally, the EDM of a baryon,
d
, is related to ED form-factor
F_{3}(s)
via

d=\dfrac{e}{2M}F_{3}(0).

(2.14)

To use this formula one needs to analytically continue complex-valued form-factor to the
real-photon point. We assume that the form-factor varies weakly as a function of
s
.

Generation of pseudo-data

The goal of this work is to perform the sensitivity study of the EDM with the current and future tau-charm factories, i.e. to estimate the achievable precision of EDM measurements. To do so, we first generate a
pseudo-data
with the statistics of BESIII and STCF and then, estimate the uncertainties to the theoretical parameters of question.
Our probability distribution function depends on five kinematical variables:

\theta,\,\theta_{-},\,\phi_{-},\,\theta_{+},\,\phi_{+}

in addition to the six theoretical parameters that can be determined by fitting to the data:

\alpha_{\psi},\,\Delta\Phi_{EM},\,\Delta\Phi_{M3},\,f_{\psi},\alpha_{-},\,\alpha_{+}.

In this work, we will also vary the polarization of the initial electron
\lambda_{e}

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\alpha_{\psi},\Delta\Phi_{EM},\Delta\Phi_{M3},f_{\psi},\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+};\lambda_{e})

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}\rightarrow p\pi^{-}\bar{p}\pi^{+})}\frac{d\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}\rightarrow p\pi^{-}\bar{p}\pi^{+})}{d\cos\theta d\Omega_{-}d\Omega_{+}},

Results

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we have investigated a future measurement of the EDM of
\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{c}
baryons at tau-charm factories, namely BESIII and STCF experiments. We first derived the theoretical framework to exploit the full angular distributions of
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}\rightarrow p\pi^{-}\bar{p}\pi^{+}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}\rightarrow\Lambda\pi^{+}\bar{\Lambda}\pi^{-}
processes, including the EDM contribution. As the STCF experiment may be able to produce polarized beams, we have also introduced polarization parameter to investigate its impact on measuring the EDM. The obtained result was used for Monte Carlo study to demonstrate the sensitivity of the future experiments to the EDM. We first have generated pseudo-data corresponding to the statistics of the BESIII and STCF experiments, in order to produce the full kinematical distributions.
Then, by using the maximum likelihood method, we have shown that all the theoretical parameters, 6 for the former process and 8 for the latter, can be fitted by using the full angular distributions.
We have also investigated the dependence of the sensitivity of the EDM measurement on the input parameters. We found that the sensitivity does not depend strongly on the size of EDM that we have introduced. We have found that the polarized beam can help to increase the sensitivity to the EDM measurement. In case of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
, we also found that the sensitivity increases with a higher beam energy.
Finally, we obtained sensitivity to the EDM for different experiments as:

\delta d_{\Lambda}^{(BESIII)}\sim 0.5\times 10^{-18}\,{\rm e}\cdot{\rm cm}
for the
\Lambda
-baryon in the BESIII experiment,
\delta d_{\Lambda}^{(STCF)}\sim 1.5\times 10^{-20}\,{\rm e}\cdot{\rm cm}
in the STCF experiment;
\delta d_{\Lambda_{c}^{+}}^{(STCF)}\sim 0.8\times 10^{-16}\,{\rm e}\cdot{\rm cm}
for the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
baryon. It appeared that the latter number is very close to the expected sensitivity of the proposed new experiment
[
4
]
to measure the EDM of
\Lambda_{c}
using bent crystals. Thus, we emphasize that the large production rate of the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}
at STCF will play an important role in this field of research in the near future.

Appendix A
Matrix element squared in terms of spin-correlation matrix

The squared matrix element for the electron-positron pair annihilation
into a polarized pair
\Lambda\bar{\Lambda}
(or

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\bar{\Lambda}_{c}^{-}
) and polarized electron can be represented in the form:

\overline{|\mathcal{M}|^{2}}=R_{00}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}R_{i0}s_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}R_{0j}s_{j}^{\prime}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}R_{ij}s_{i}s_{j}^{\prime},

(A.1)

where
s_{i}
and
s_{j}^{\prime}
are the polarization vectors of
\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
in their rest frames, respectively. The elements of the spin-correlation matrix
R_{00}
,
R_{i0}
,
R_{0j}
, and
R_{ij}
are obtained by direct calculation:

(1\times 1^{\prime}):\quad R_{00}=\dfrac{e^{4}}{4\gamma^{2}}\bigl[(\gamma^{2}-1)\gamma^{2}|F_{3}|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+|G_{E}|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta+\gamma^{2}|G_{M}|^{2}(\cos^{2}\theta+1)\bigr],

(A.2)

(s_{1}\times s_{1}^{\prime}):\quad R_{11}=-\dfrac{e^{4}\sin^{2}\theta}{4\gamma^{2}}\bigl[(\gamma^{2}-1)\gamma^{2}|F_{3}|^{2}-|G_{E}|^{2}-\gamma^{2}|G_{M}|^{2}\bigr],

(A.3)

(s_{1}\times s_{2}^{\prime}):\quad R_{12}=-R_{21}=\dfrac{e^{4}\beta\sin^{2}\theta}{2}{\rm Re}(G_{E}F_{3}^{*}),

(A.4)

(s_{2}\times s_{2}^{\prime}):\quad R_{22}=-\dfrac{e^{4}\sin^{2}\theta}{4\gamma^{2}}\bigl[(\gamma^{2}-1)\gamma^{2}|F_{3}|^{2}-|G_{E}|^{2}+\gamma^{2}|G_{M}|^{2}\bigr],

(A.5)

(s_{1}\times s_{3}^{\prime}):\quad R_{13}=\dfrac{e^{4}\sin\theta}{2\gamma}\bigl[{\rm Re}(G_{M}G_{E}^{*})\cos\theta-\lambda_{e}\beta\gamma^{2}{\rm Im}(G_{M}F_{3}^{*})\bigr],

(A.6)

(s_{3}\times s_{1}^{\prime}):\quad R_{31}=\dfrac{e^{4}\sin\theta}{2\gamma}\bigl[{\rm Re}(G_{M}G_{E}^{*})\cos\theta+\lambda_{e}\beta\gamma^{2}{\rm Im}(G_{M}F_{3}^{*})\bigr],

(A.7)

(s_{2}\times s_{3}^{\prime}):\quad R_{23}=-\dfrac{e^{4}\sin\theta}{2\gamma}\bigl[\beta\gamma^{2}{\rm Re}(G_{M}F_{3}^{*})\cos\theta+\lambda_{e}{\rm Im}(G_{M}G_{E}^{*})\bigr],

(A.8)

(s_{3}\times s_{2}^{\prime}):\quad R_{32}=\dfrac{e^{4}\sin\theta}{2\gamma}\bigl[\beta\gamma^{2}{\rm Re}(G_{M}F_{3}^{*})\cos\theta-\lambda_{e}{\rm Im}(G_{M}G_{E}^{*})\bigr],

(A.9)

(s_{3}\times s_{3}^{\prime}):\quad R_{33}=\dfrac{e^{4}}{8\gamma^{2}}\bigl\{\gamma^{2}|G_{M}|^{2}(\cos 2\theta+3)-2\sin^{2}\theta\bigl[(\gamma^{2}-1)\gamma^{2}|F_{3}|^{2}+|G_{E}|^{2}\bigr]\bigr\},

(A.10)

(s_{1}\times 1^{\prime}):\quad R_{10}=-\dfrac{e^{4}\sin\theta}{2\gamma}\bigl[\beta\gamma^{2}{\rm Im}(G_{M}F_{3}^{*})\cos\theta-\lambda_{e}{\rm Re}(G_{E}G_{M}^{*})\bigr],

(A.11)

(1\times s_{1}^{\prime}):\quad R_{01}=\dfrac{e^{4}\sin\theta}{2\gamma}\bigl[\beta\gamma^{2}{\rm Im}(G_{M}F_{3}^{*})\cos\theta+\lambda_{e}{\rm Re}(G_{E}G_{M}^{*})\bigr],

(A.12)

(s_{2}\times 1^{\prime}):\quad R_{20}=-\dfrac{e^{4}\sin\theta}{2\gamma}\bigl[{\rm Im}(G_{M}G_{E}^{*})\cos\theta+\lambda_{e}\beta\gamma^{2}{\rm Re}(G_{M}F_{3}^{*})\bigr],

(A.13)

(1\times s_{2}^{\prime}):\quad R_{02}=-\dfrac{e^{4}\sin\theta}{2\gamma}\bigl[{\rm Im}(G_{M}G_{E}^{*})\cos\theta-\lambda_{e}\beta\gamma^{2}{\rm Re}(G_{M}F_{3}^{*})\bigr],

(A.14)

(s_{3}\times 1^{\prime}):\quad R_{30}=\dfrac{e^{4}}{2}\bigl[\beta{\rm Im}(G_{E}F_{3}^{*})\sin^{2}\theta+\lambda_{e}|G_{M}|^{2}\cos\theta\bigr],

(A.15)

(1\times s_{3}^{\prime}):\quad R_{03}=-\dfrac{e^{4}}{2}\bigl[\beta{\rm Im}(G_{E}F_{3}^{*})\sin^{2}\theta-\lambda_{e}|G_{M}|^{2}\cos\theta\bigr].

(A.16)

Here
\lambda_{e}
denotes degree of the electron polarization,
\gamma=\sqrt{s}/(2M)
and

\beta=\sqrt{1-\gamma^{-2}}
.

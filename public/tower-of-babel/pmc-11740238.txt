The effect of arthroscopic Bankart repair and remplissage procedure on joint range of motion and functional outcomes in anterior shoulder instability
Authors: Umut Öktem, Gülfem Ezgi Özaltın, Sinan Yılmaz, İbrahim Bozkurt, Durmuş Ali Öçgüder
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Objective:This study aimed to investigate the effect of arthroscopic Bankart repair (ABR) alone and ABR with an additional remplissage procedure on joint range of motion and functional results in patients with anterior shoulder instability.Methods:This retrospective study included patients treated 1 year ago with either ABR alone or the ABR additional remplissage procedure. The Bankart lesion was determined by magnetic resonance imaging, and the amount of glenoid bone loss was determined by computed tomography.Patients with glenoid bone loss <25% and on-track Hill–Sachs lesions (HSLs) were treated with ABR alone (22 females, 8 males; mean age = 27.4 ± 6.4 years). Those with off-track HSL were treated with both ABR and remplissage (20 females, 10 males; mean age = 27.5 ± 5.3 years). One year after surgical treatment, the joint range of motion of the patients was determined by a universal goniometer, and functional status was evaluated using the Rowe score.Results:Sixty shoulders of 60 patients were evaluated over an average period of 1 year. There was no difference between groups regarding age (P = .767) and gender (P = .779). There were 42 female and 18 male patients, with a mean age = 27.5 ± 5.8 years. There was a significant difference between the groups in patients’ external rotation with arm adduction (ER1) (P = .001), external rotation with arm abduction (ER2) (P = .001), forward flexion (P = .001), and abduction (P = .001) measurements between the groups (P < .05). No significant difference was found in internal rotation and Rowe scores between the groups (P = .057, P = .069). A greater improvement was seen in the Rowe score of the ABR + remplissage group (85.2 ± 8.8). No recurrence or complications were observed in any of the patients.ConclusionThe combined procedure of ABR with remplissage may limit joint mobility in patients with anterior shoulder instability. However, it provides satisfactory functional results, with patients in the ABR + remplissage group showing better overall outcomes.Level of Evidence:Level III, Therapeutic study.

Introduction

Most patients with recurrent glenohumeral instability have humeral head defects or glenoid bone loss.1 While some methods directly target the humeral head, others support the inferior and anterior glenoid bone to preserve early engagement and thus manipulate the joint space.2 Surgical options applied so far include open Bankart repair, osteochondral allograft transplantation, humeral head osteotomy, and the Connolly procedure. Allograft or autograft options to the anterior glenoid margin are used, and one of the most popular options, the Laterjet–Bristow procedure, is used.3-7 Although open Bankart repair was previously performed as the gold standard procedure, the development of arthroscopic equipment and the widespread application of this technique by surgeons have brought arthroscopic Bankart repair (ABR) to the forefront in patients without critical bone loss, and it is applied as a first-line routine treatment.8 Less surgical incisions, less postoperative pain, and fewer wound complications also support this situation.9 The recurrence rate of arthroscopic Bankart repair is as low as 4% in the absence of bone loss in the glenoid or hum eral head,7 but in the presence of significant bone loss, the failure rate can be as high as 67%.5,10 Many different criteria have been noted that must be addressed to manage the treatment of Hill–Sachs lesion (HSL).11,12 However, in a recent study, the glenoid track concept (on-track and off-track) was proposed, in which glenoid bone loss and HSL criteria can determine the course of surgical intervention.13 In those with less than 25% glenoid bone loss, although ABR intervention alone provides adequate results in patients with on-track lesions, ABR alone has been found to fail at a high rate in off-track HSL in patients with off-track lesions.14,15 Thus, the glenoid tract status was taken into account when deciding on the surgical procedure. For this specific case, a repeat procedure involving capsulo-tenodesis has been described (remplissage). In this procedure, the posterior capsule and infraspinatus tendon are padded to prevent fusion of the HSL and the glenoid. The concern with this procedure was the reduction of intra-articular volume and a possible loss of joint motion due to the tenodesis effect.16

Studies conducted in recent years have shown that if HSL is activated in the presence of shoulder instability, remplissage applied in addition to ABR can increase shoulder stabilization and reduce the rate of redislocation. In addition, although external rotation restriction and joint stiffness are mostly mentioned in studies, there are also studies showing the opposite. There is no consensus on the effect of this condition on functional results.17,18 We hypothesized that in patients with HSL lesions, the remplissage procedure in addition to ABR may cause joint movement limitation but will not negatively affect functional results. Therefore, we aim to evaluate the effect of the remplissage procedure in addition to ABR on joint range of motion and functional results in patients with anterior shoulder instability.

Materials and methods

Ethics committee approval from Ankara City Hospital No. 1 Clinical Research (Protocol Code: E1-22-2571, Date: 20.04.2022) was received. This study was designed as retrospective and single-center. Patients who underwent only ABR due to shoulder instability in a city hospital between 2017 and 2021, who underwent remplissage in addition to the ABR procedure, and who had a follow-up record 1 year after the surgery were included in the study. Patients who had previously had surgery on the other shoulder, patients with pathology on the other shoulder, patients without a 1-year follow-up record, patients with a glenoid defect of more than 25% and who had undergone bone surgery, patients diagnosed with epilepsy and patients with central nervous system damage or psychiatric disease, patients with a cuff tear and glenohumeral ligament rupture on the same shoulder, patients without a shoulder computed tomography record, throwing athletes requiring full external rotation and contact/collision athletes with a high risk of recurrence, and patients who did not want to participate in the study were excluded from the study. The patients were followed for an average of 1 year. Examination, measurement, and scoring records at the end of the first year were taken and recorded in the patient files. X-ray, magnetic resonance imaging, and computed tomography of the patients were available in the computer system. After obtaining the “informed consent form” from the patients, the surgical notes recorded in the system, the implant used, and the surgical technique were accessed. All patients were assessed for eligibility in this manner. A total of 60 patients were included in the study: 30 patients who underwent ABR only and 30 patients who underwent the remplissage in addition to the ABR procedure. During the 1-year follow-up of the patients, their demographic information and clinical evaluations were measured twice in the examination room and the average was recorded. These measurements were recorded in the patient file.

Joint range of motion: Measurements were made according to the contralateral healthy shoulder of the operated patients. The difference between the healthy shoulder and the operated shoulder was recorded in degrees (deficit of range of motion). Joint range of motion measurements: External rotation in arm adduction (ER1), external rotation in arm abduction (ER2), internal rotation (IR), forward flexion, and abduction were evaluated. External rotation measurements were made with the patient in the supine position.

Rowe shoulder instability score: It is a scoring system that evaluates shoulder stability, joint range of motion, and functional limitation. Shoulder stability is 50 points, joint range of motion is 20 points, functional status is 30 points, and it is evaluated as a total of 100 points. A score between 100 and 90 points is considered an excellent result, a score between 89 and 75 points is considered a good result, a score between 74 and 51 points is considered a fair result, and a score of 50 or <50 is considered a poor result.19,20

Surgical technique

When deciding on the treatment strategy, the concept of off-track and on-track was followed.21 Glenoid bone loss was measured using the best-fit circle method on three-dimensional (3D) CT before/during surgery.22,21 Hill–Sachs lesions was also measured with 3D CT. Both procedures were performed by the same surgeon. If the HSL width was smaller than the glenoid track width, it was considered on track, and if it was larger, it was considered off track. In shoulders with glenoid bone loss less than 25%, in the presence of on-track HSL, only ABR was preferred, and in the presence of off-track HSL, the remplissage procedure was preferred in addition to ABR.

Pre-surgery preparation

The patients were operated on with a combination of regional and general anesthesia and antibiotic prophylaxis. A shoulder table apparatus was applied to the operating table, and the patients were operated in the beach chair position. First, a standard arthroscopic posterior observation portal was opened, and diagnostic arthroscopy was performed with a 30° arthroscope. Then, the anterior portal was opened, and the arthroscope was taken to the anterior portal, after which debridement was applied to the articular surface of the HSL. One piece of 2.8 soft anchor was placed (Figure 1). Sutures were passed through the infraspinatus into the subacromial space in a mattress configuration, and the accessory was taken out through the postero-lateral portal. The sutures should not be knotted at this point. After these procedures, 2 or 3 1.8 mm soft anchors were applied for inferior glenolabral separation, threads were passed through the labrum with suture passers, and the Bankart lesion was repaired (Figure 2). The remplissage procedure was completed by tying the locked knot (Figure 3). The arthroscope was then inserted into the joint through the rotator gap to visualize the remnant. During the dynamic examination, it was checked that the HSL was not engaged with the anterior edge of the glenoid.

Postoperative care

After the surgery, the shoulder was suspended for 3 weeks to provide an early passive range of motion. Isometric exercises were started after 3 weeks. External rotation joint range of motion increased by 30° in the first 4 weeks and gradually increased after 6 weeks. Along with joint range of motion exercises, active assistive and active joint range of motion exercises were performed, and the use of the sling was discontinued.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 25.0 (IBM SPSS Corp.; Armonk, NY, USA) package program was used to analyze the data. Categorical measurements were shown as numbers and percentages, and continuous measurements were shown as mean and standard deviation (median and minimum-maximum when necessary). Individuals who met the parametric assumption conditions suitable for normal distribution were determined by the Student’s t-test. Individuals who did not meet parametric assumptions were analyzed with the Mann–Whitney U-test. The Fisher exact test was used to compare categorical variables. In all tests, the statistical significance level was taken as 0.05.

Results

In the only ABR group, there were 22 women and 8 men (mean age = 27.4 ± 6.4, min-max 18-41); There were 20 female and 10 male patients (mean age = 27.5 ± 5.3, range, 18-38) in the group in which remplissage was applied in addition to ABR. The average age of all patients was 27 ± 5.8 (range, 18-41). There was no significant difference between the groups in terms of age and gender (P = .779, P = .767).

When the patients were compared between groups, a significant difference was found between the groups in ER1, ER2, forward flexion, and abduction results (P = .001). No significant difference was found in internal rotation (P = .057) and Rowe scores (P = .069) (Table 1).

All patients were evaluated 1 year postoperatively. In 1 of the 30 patients who underwent isolated ABR, a reoperated shoulder dislocation occurred in the second month after surgery as a result of the patient falling on the operated arm, and this was considered an unsuccessful result. No recurrence was detected within 1 year in the group that received remplissage in addition to ABR. None of the 60 patients who underwent surgery experienced wound infection or skin problems. Again, no neurovascular damage was observed in any patient. There was stiffness and pain around the posterolateral accessory portal in the 1-year postoperative follow-up of 3 patients who underwent the remplissage procedure in addition to ABR.

Discussion

This study showed that there might be a loss of joint motion in external rotation, abduction, and forward flexion in patients who underwent a remplissage procedure in addition to ABR. In addition, the functional results of patients who received remplissage in addition to ABR were observed to be better than those who received ABR alone.

Many different surgical techniques have been used in Bankart repair to date, such as open Bankart repair, arthroscopic Bankart repair, Laterjet procedure, and Remplissage procedure. Arthroscopic Bankart repair has been performed quite frequently to date. However, when “off-track” humeral lesions are present, arthroscopic Bankart repair is not considered sufficient. Burkhart et al emphasized the role of HSL in terms of the recurrence after Bankart repair, but he did not express a clear idea regarding HSL management.11,23 Yamamoto et al13 introduced the concept of the glenoid track for the evaluation of bipolar bone defects and stated an important relationship between the glenoid bone defect and HSL. Previous studies have suggested that the ABR procedure will not be sufficient in patients with glenoid bone defects of less than 25% and off-track lesions and that the remplissage procedure should be added for these patients.24 In the remplissage process, the existing defect is filled using the infraspinatus and posteroinferior capsule. In this way, engagement of the HSL is prevented.23 There is no consensus on the effects of this procedure on joint movement. Boileau et al25 observed that after the remplissage procedure, the external rotation joint range of motion was observed to be limited in all planes. Haviv et al26 compared the movement of the shoulder joint after remplissage with the opposite shoulder and found no difference in forward flexion and internal rotation, but they detected a high degree of limitation in external rotation. Merolla et al27 showed that fixing the humeral head defect to the infraspinatus caused a loss of external rotation when the arm was adducted, but did not cause any loss when the arm was abducted. In their study, Grimberg et al observed that joint stiffness occurred in the group that received ABR with additional replication, compared to the healthy shoulder, and they associated this with limited movement in external rotation.28 In our study, patients who received remplissage in addition to ABR had more limitation of movement in external rotation in abduction, external rotation in adduction, forward flexion, and abduction compared to both their healthy shoulders and patients who received ABR only. This resulted in a significant difference between the groups. We think that this is due to the capsulotenodesis effect, which is meant to prevent the engagement of the HSL by using the infraspinatus tendon in the remplissage technique, and the narrowing of the joint volume.

The result that caught our attention was that the joint range of motion was more limited in the ABR+ remplissage group, and the Rowe score, which evaluates the functional status of the patients, gave better results in the same group. Park et al2 thought that HSL was the cause of his repeated shoulder surgeries. Widjaja et al29 stated that there is a high correlation between HSL and humeral head defects. Murphy et al,15 in meta-analysis studies examining the long-term results of ABR, found that the Rowe score improved in patients who underwent ABR, but they stated that the risk of recurrent instability caused concern.In this sense, the remplissage technique can address both Bankart repair and humeral head defects simultaneously. Pandey et al investigated the effects of ABR with or without remplissage in patients with off-track HSL. At the end of the study, more dislocations were observed in patients with ABR with off-track lesions than in patients with ABR with on-track lesions. It was observed that the group that received remplissage along with ABR with off-track lesions experienced significantly fewer dislocations than the group that did not receive remplissage. In addition, it was observed that there were more joint limitations in the group where remplissage was applied compared to those who did not apply it.30 In the meta-analysis study by Camus et al, in which they examined the functional results of Bankart as well as isolated Bankart repair and remplissage procedure, it was shown that functional results in the remplissage groups were significantly better than in patients who underwent isolation.23 By the recommendations previously stated for off-track lesions, in this study, patients with off-track HSL showed functionally similar results to patients with on-track lesions, although joint movement limitation occurred in patients with on-track lesions. The functional Rowe score was better in the group that underwent the remplissage procedure. This may be associated with better Rowe scores, better stability after surgery, and less apprehension. In this sense, our study shows that the posterior capsule and infraspinatus heal effectively after arthroscopic remplissage and that joint movement limitation does not affect functional results.

Our study has several limitations. The first limitation is the short follow-up period. Longer-term results could have been examined. It became difficult to reach long-term results due to the COVID-19 pandemic period during which the study was conducted. We may recommend examining longer-term results and comparing complications or improvements that may occur with other glenohumeral joint surgeries in future studies. In our study, we included patients with nearly 25% defects. It would be beneficial to evaluate the long-term results in terms of the recurrence risk. Another limitation was that different parameters could have been examined in terms of functional results. The effects of the remplissage procedure can be better interpreted with evaluations regarding stability in future studies.

In the treatment of HSL with glenoid bone loss, the remplissage procedure applied with appropriate patient selection through intraoperative evaluation provides clinical advantages. We can say that the remplissage procedure is a reliable method. However, the resulting joint movement limitation does not significantly affect functional results. Nevertheless, the resulting joint limitation may not satisfy patients with high expectations. Therefore, this situation should be communicated to patients before surgery. The ABR + remplissage procedure will be a good option for surgeons due to its ease of application and satisfactory clinical results.

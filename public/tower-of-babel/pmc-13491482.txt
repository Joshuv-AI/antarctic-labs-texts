Preparation and Evaluation of Biopolysaccharide-Based Temperature-Resistant Drilling Fluid Additives
Authors: Yunhai Zhao, Yu Fan, Jiayin Zhang, Yu Zhang, Jingqi Shi, Chao Wen, Zhichao Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Xanthan gum (XG), valued for its environmental friendliness and multifunctionality, has been extensively utilized in drilling fluids. However, its inadequate thermal stability at elevated bottomhole temperatures encountered in deep drilling operations has constrained its broader application. In this study, we successfully synthesized a graft copolymer (XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS) through grafting XG with KH570-modified nano-SiO2 and thermally stable monomer AMPS. The modified product exhibited significantly reduced molecular weights, with number-average (M n) and weight-average (M w) molecular weights of 3.17 × 105 g/mol and 3.88 × 105 g/mol, respectively. Thermal analysis revealed a 31% mass loss at high temperature, representing an 14% reduction compared to native XG, while biotoxicity assessment showed an EC50 value exceeding 106 mg/L, indicating low toxicity. Performance evaluation demonstrated excellent viscosity-enhancing properties and superior filtration control, with API filtrate volumes (FLAPI) of 3.0 mL (freshwater) and 3.4 mL (saturated brine), along with high-temperature/high-pressure filtrate volumes (FLHTHP) of 12 and 15 mL, respectively, after 16 h of thermal aging at 150 °C. This breakthrough substantially improves XG’s thermal resilience, extending its applicability in deep high-temperature wells.

Introduction

As the depth of the well increases during the drilling process, the temperature of the lower wellbore rises. The high temperatures can lead to side reactions such as cross-linking, degradation, and solidification, which can severely deteriorate the performance of drilling fluids, rendering them challenging to regulate and adjust. Sulfonated drilling fluid systems are currently the most prevalent type of water-based drilling fluid. Their high thermal stability allows for degradation resistance even at temperatures of 200 °C or higher, making them a popular choice for use in deep wells, high-temperature wells, and wells with complex formations. However, environmental performance testing has revealed that sulfonated drilling fluid systems exceed national standards in terms of biotoxicity, biodegradability, and heavy metal content. This is mainly due to the addition of materials such as sulfonated lignite and sulfonated asphalt during their preparation. It is therefore of significant importance to study green and environmentally friendly drilling fluid treatment agents that are capable of meeting the requisite high-temperature resistance standards.

Xanthan gum (XG) is a secondary metabolite secreted from Xanthomonas campestris through fermentation using organic carbon substrates. , The polysaccharide exhibits excellent rheological properties − and viscosity-enhancing performance , due to the interaction between the negative charges on its side chains and basic backbone structure. In drilling fluids, XG is frequently employed as a rheology modifier and fluid loss reducer, performing a crucial function in maintaining wellbore stability, carrying drill cuttings, and minimizing drilling fluid infiltration into formations. − Huadi et al. investigated a novel polyamine-NaCl solution-based drilling fluid system incorporating xanthan gum and polyanionic cellulose, which demonstrated remarkable performance in significantly reducing fluid loss, minimizing wellbore instability, and mitigating formation damage. This optimized drilling fluid formulation has been successfully implemented in offshore operations in East Kalimantan and Indonesia, yielding substantial field application results. In a parallel development, Al-Mehauilani et al. engineered an innovative drilling fluid composition primarily consisting of water, xanthan gum, and modified starch. This formulation exhibited superior wellbore stabilization capabilities while effectively reducing reservoir damage, as evidenced by its successful deployment in limestone reservoirs within the Umm Gudair field. Additionally, XG exhibits satisfactory salt tolerance, which makes it highly adaptable in various complex formations. Moreover, XG is biodegradable, which reduces its environmental impact compared to chemically synthesized polymers. This quality has attracted significant research interest. However, the inherent instability of the polysaccharide structure results in thermal degradation. Empirical evidence establishes that xanthan gum retains consistent solution viscosity solely within the 0–100 °C thermal windowa critical limitation that profoundly constrains its utility in elevated-temperature industrial processes. , This underscores the necessity for further structural modifications to enhance its thermal stability and facilitate the utilization of natural polysaccharides in high-temperature environments. With regard to graft modification and thermal stability enhancement of XG, hydrophobic long carbon chains (C16) were introduced into the XG backbone to gain the resistance of high temperatures. The modified product retained a viscosity of 421 mPa·s at 140 °C and maintained its spatial network structure under saline conditions. Li employed a Co-60 gamma source for NVP grafting onto XG, with the grafting sites located on the side chains of XG. This resulted in enhanced thermal stability and a significantly increased carbonization temperature of the grafted product. Acetylation of XG was conducted using acetic anhydride (AAH) in a densiffed CO2 solvent. The resulting product exhibited a fully amorphous structure and demonstrated enhanced thermal stability, like due to the increased van der Waals interactions caused by the acetate group. Yang et al. employed γ radiation to graft acrylic monomers onto XG, demonstrating that the radiation-grafted product exhibited enhanced salt tolerance compared to those prepared using chemical initiators.

This study aims to enhance the thermal resistance of XG by introducing heat-resistant monomers, namely AMPS and a silane coupling agent-modified nano-SiO2 into its molecular structure. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR), and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) were employed to confirm the successful grafting of the monomers. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was used to characterize the thermal stability of the grafted products. It is expected to yield thermally resistant modified XG, which will facilitate the advancement of environmentally friendly drilling fluid additives.

Materials and Methods

Materials

Both XG (pharmacopeia grade) and 2-acrylamido-2-methylpropanesulfonic acid (98%) werepurchased from Aladdin Reagent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The modified nano SiO2 was obtained from Xianfeng Nanomaterials Technology Co., Ltd. (Jiangsu, China). The composite initiators, including potassium persulfate (analytical grade), sodium bisulfite (analytical grade), and ammonium cerium nitrate (analytical grade), were procured from Chengdu Kelong Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Chengdu, China). Methanol (analytical grade) was supplied by Titan Scientific Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The materials used in the drilling fluid formulation included the following: bentonite (Shandong Huawei Bentonite Co., Ltd.), sodium carbonate (Chongqing Xiangyu Chemical Salt Co., Ltd.), sodium hydroxide (industrial caustic soda flakes, ≥98.5%, Qinghai Yihua Chemical Co., Ltd.), calcium oxide (≥85%, Jiangyou Wanshan Mining Co., Ltd.), and potassium chloride (≥60% K2O equivalent, Qinghai Xiangjiang Salt Lake Development Co., Ltd.). The plugging agent JHS-01 (white emulsion), coating inhibitor YFKN (white or off-white powder), and fluid loss reducer JY-1 (Types A, B, and C) were all obtained from Sichuan Kai’er Oil & Gas Field Technology Services Co., Ltd.

Preparation of Modified Xanthan Gum

XG powder was introduced to a three-neck flask and agitated at a constant temperature to guarantee complete dissolution. Following dissolution, AMPS and the modified nano SiO2 (monomers/XG mass ratio of 3:1) were added to the flask and stirred until complete dissolution was achieved. The three-neck flask was then subjected to nitrogen purging and placed in a constant temperature water bath of 55 °C. Subsequently, the composite initiator was added to the flask, with ammonium cerium nitrate being dropped in first, followed by the redox initiator (potassium persulfate and sodium bisulfite). Following a 3 h reaction period, the mixture was allowed to cool. Lastly, the reaction product was sequentially added to a methanol solution (7:3, v/v), a methanol solution (8:2, v/v), and a pure methanol solution, all of which were sealed and stirred for 8 h to precipitate the homopolymer. The precipitated homopolymer was then removed by centrifugation and filtration, thus obtaining the XG graft product.

Characterization of Structure

FTIR Analysis

FTIR analysis was conducted using a Nicolet 6700 FTIR spectrometer (Thermo) with the KBr pellet method. The sample (≈1 wt %) was ground with dry spectroscopic-grade KBr and pressed into a 13 mm disc under 8 t for 2 min. Prior to each measurement, a fresh air background (64 scans, 22 °C, <40% RH) was collected and automatically corrected for H2O/CO2 absorption. Samples were scanned 64× at 4 cm–1 resolution over 4000–400 cm–1 with Happ–Genzel apodization and Mertz phase correction. Background spectra were updated every 2 h to keep instrument drift below 0.1% T.

NMR Analysis

1H NMR spectra were recorded at 25 °C on a Bruker Avance II-600 spectrometer operating at 600.13 MHz for 1H (14.1 T, 5 mm BBO probe). The grafted xanthan sample (15 mg) was dissolved in 0.6 mL of 99.9% D2O. Data were acquired with 64 k complex points, 30° pulse (7.5 μs), 2.0 s acquisition time, 5 s relaxation delay, 128 transients, and 1 Hz line-broadening apodization before Fourier transform. Chemical shifts are reported relative to the TMSP singlet at 0.00 ppm.

SEM Analysis

The surface morphology and microstructure of XG and XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS were observed using a SEM (ZEISS Gemini SEM 300, Germany) equipped with EDS. The accelerating voltage was set to 15 kV. The powder samples were subjected to a 24 h drying process at 60 °C. The gel samples, with a concentration of 1% (w/v), were dissolved in DI water and subsequently lyophilized for 48 h. Then, the rapidly frozen gel samples were fractured under liquid nitrogen. The samples were attached to adhesive tapes and subjected to a metal spray coating on the surface. The porosity values of samples were calculated using the software ImageJ.

GPC Analysis

The molecular weights of XG and XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS, including the M n, M w, Mp and molecular weight dispersity (M w/M n), were analyzed by GPC (Waters 1525, USA). The system was equipped with a refractive index (RI) 2414 detector. Separations were performed at 30 °C on a PL aquagel–OH MIXED 8 μm column (300 × 7.5 mm, Agilent) using an aqueous mobile phase of 0.20 M NaNO3 and 0.01 M NaH2PO4 adjusted to pH 7.0 with 0.1 M NaOH. The flow rate was maintained at 1.0 mL min–1. Samples were filtered through 0.22 μm hydrophilic PTFE syringe filters prior to injection. Calibration was carried out with narrow poly­(ethylene oxide) standards (Agilent EasiVial PEO/PEG kit, M p 106–1258 kDa). The injection volume was 10 μL.

Thermal Stability Testing

The thermal stability was evaluated by thermogravimetric analysis using a Thermogravimetric analysis and differential scanning calorimetry (TGA/DSC) simultaneous thermal analyzer (Mettler Toledo). The experiment was conducted under a nitrogen atmosphere with a heating rate of 10 °C·min–1, scanning a temperature range of 30–350 °C.

Rheological Testing

Rheological measurements were performed using a model 35SA rotational viscometer (FANN Company) equipped with an R1B1 rotor at a constant temperature of 50 °C. The solutions of 0.3% XG (w/v) and 3% (w/v) XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS were prepared. Viscosity measurements were conducted using a viscometer at rotational speeds of 3, 6, 30, 60, 100, and 200 rpm. The shear performance was evaluated using the shear performance value (ø6/ø60). The shear performance value is used to characterize the superiority of shear-thinning behavior, with a higher value indicating better shear-thinning performance.

Biotoxicity Evaluation

The sample was prepared into a 3% stock solution using deionized water and adjusted to pH 7. Subsequently, a solution of 3% sodium chloride was added at a volume ratio of 1:9 to prepare the test sample solution, with the final concentration set at 106 mg/L. The biotoxicity of the graft copolymer was evaluated by measuring the relative luminescence intensity of Photobacterium phosphoreum. ,

Performance Testing

Rheology Test

The rheological properties of the drilling fluid were investigated using a model 35SA rotational viscometer (FANN Company). The viscosity readings of the drilling fluid samples were recorded at rotational speeds of 600, 300, 200, 100, 6, and 3 rpm. The plastic viscosity (PV, mPa·s) and yield point (YP, Pa) were calculated according to the formulas specified in the Chinese National Standard GB/T 16783.1. The values of G10′ and G10″, which represent the gel strength at 10 s and 10 min, were obtained from 3 rpm based on the specifications set forth by the American Petroleum Institute (API). The initial shear force is defined as the shear force measured after the drilling fluid has reached a standstill for 10 s. 1 ηPV=ϕ600−ϕ300 2 YP=0.48×(ϕ300−ηPV) where φ600 and φ300 represent the viscosity readings at 600 and 300 rpm, respectively; to express the yield point in pascals, the calculated value must be multiplied by a conversion factor of 0.48.

Filtration Test

The API fluid loss and high-temperature high-pressure (HTHP) fluid loss were determined in accordance with API specifications and the Chinese National Standard GB/T 16783.1. The experiments were conducted using a 170-00-4S-230 high-temperature high-pressure filter press from OFI Testing Equipment (USA) and a 207,228 API filter press from FANN. The API fluid loss was measured at ambient temperature and a pressure of 100 psi over a 30 min period, using a filter paper with a diameter of 3.54 in. The high-temperature high-pressure (HTHP) fluid loss was measured at 150 °C and 600 psi using a Whatman No. 50 filter paper over a 30 min period.

Results and Discussion

Infrared Spectroscopy Analysis

FTIR spectra of XG before and after grafting are compared in Figure . The grafted copolymers retain the characteristic peaks of XG (Figure ). The strong absorption peak at 3419 cm–1 was attributed to the –OH group in XG (ν O–H), while the peak at 2931 cm–1 was associated with the C–H stretching vibration of methylene groups (ν C–H of CH2). The peak at 1625 cm–1 was identified as the symmetric stretching vibration absorption peak of the carbonyl group (CO) in the XG molecule (ν CO). The peaks at 1404 cm–1 and 1062 cm–1 were ascribed to the bending vibrations of the C–H (δ C–H) and O–H groups (δ O–H) in XG, respectively. After grafting (XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS) these bands shift or are supplemented by new ones. The O–H stretch moves from 3419 to 3391 cm–1, indicating H-bonding between XG–OH and nano-SiO2 surface–OH (modified with KH-570); the CH2 stretch shifts slightly, from 2931 to 2923 cm–1; the CO band moves from 1625 to 1648 cm–1; a new peak at 1407 cm–1 appears, assigned to ν C–N of the AMPS amide; the Si–O–Si stretch emerges at 1101 cm–1; the C–S stretch of AMPS is detected at 470 cm–1. These spectral changes confirm that both nano-SiO2 and AMPS have been covalently grafted onto the XG backbone.

Nuclear Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy Analysis

Figure illustrates the 1H NMR spectrum of the XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS. The peak observed at 4.79 ppm corresponded to the solvent peak of deuterated water (D2O). The chemical shifts between 3.0 and 4.0 ppm were attributed to the protons on the carbon atoms at the grafting sites. The protons on carbon atoms were connected to oxygen atoms on the modified nano-SiO2, and the protons on XG were connected to oxygen. The peaks observed at 2.0 to 2.5 ppm were attributed to the protons on the carbon atoms connected to silicon atoms on the modified nano-SiO2, as well as the protons on the carbon atoms at the monomer grafting sites. The singlet at 1.4 ppm was attributed to the protons on the methyl groups (–CH3) on the modified nano-SiO2 and the geminal dimethyl groups on the AMPS monomer. The appearance of these silicon- and AMPS- specific signals, confirmd that both nano-SiO2 and AMPS are covalently anchored to the polysaccharide chain.

Molecular Weight Analysis

In comparison to XG, the graft copolymer XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS displayed slightly diminished weight-average molecular weight (M w), number-average molecular weight (M n), and peak molecular weight (M p), accompanied by a more extensive molecular weight distribution and a polydispersity index (PD) marginally higher than that of XG itself (Table ). The decrease in M n and M w is not caused by random chain scission alone; rather, it reflects (i) acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of the 1 → 4 glycosidic linkages under the low-pH grafting medium and (ii) preferential cleavage of the highest-molecular-weight fractions, which are statistically more susceptible to attack. This disruption of the XG molecular chains was a consequence of the aforementioned structural damage. Moreover, the structural damage of XG would bring about viscosity loss, and even a 15–20% reduction in Mw can translate into a 30–40% loss in zero-shear viscosity. However, the broader molecular weight distribution introduces a high-molecular-weight tail that partially counteracts this viscosity reduction. Under elevated temperatures, this effect endows the graft copolymer with a shear-thickening response at intermediate shear rates and delays the complete loss of thickening capacity in high-salinity environments.

Scanning Electron Microscopy Analysis

SEM images (Figure ) reveal a striking morphology change. Native XG shows a loose, smooth, macroporous sheet, whereas the graft copolymer (XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS) is composed of submicron spheres that pack into a continuous, crack-free film; nano-SiO2 and AMPS microdomains are resolved as bright nodules cemented to the XG surface.

SEM images of XG and XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS.

This compact architecture is schematized in Figure . Nano-SiO2 acts as a multifunctional junction: (i) its large hydrodynamic radius bridges neighboring XG chains, (ii) its surface silanols form H-bonds and hydrolyzable covalent linkages with XG–OH, and (iii) its grafted alkyl tails introduce hydrophobic contacts that further stitch the network. The identical negative charge on the silica particles weakens counterion screening, so the XG chains remain extended even at high salinity. Consequently, the hybrid network resists thermal disentanglement and ionic compression, preserving viscosity after aging at 120 °C and 32 000 mg L–1 TDS.

Molecular dynamics schematic of XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS.

The incorporation of nano-SiO2 into XG gave rise to the formation of larger hydrodynamic aggregates, which served to reinforce the molecular network of XG. Nano-SiO2 can occupy the interstitial spaces within the molecular chains of XG, thereby increasing the rigidity of the network and preventing the degradation of the XG network structure under conditions of high temperature and high salinity. In addition to hydrogen bonding interactions, there are also hydrophobic attractions between the methyl and alkyl chains, as well as hydrolyzable covalent bonds between XG molecules and the monomers, which contribute to the formation of a cross-linked network structure. The identical charges on the modified nano-SiO2 resulted in a reduction of the electrostatic shielding effect of counterions on the XG molecular conformation. , Consequently, the hybrid network resists thermal disentanglement and ionic compression, preserving viscosity after aging at 120 °C.

Thermal Stability Evaluation

The thermogravimetric-differential scanning calorimetry (TG-DSC) curves of XG and XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS are presented in Figure . In the TGA curves, the weight loss observed between 0 and 100 °C can be attributed to the evaporation of water. In the temperature range of 100–200 °C, the total mass loss of XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS was less than 10%. The slight earlier onset of decomposition was not caused by the sulfonate side-chain; rather, it reflects acid-catalyzed depolymerization leftover from the low-pH grafting step and/or desulfonation of AMPS that releases SO2 at 190–210 °C. The weight loss observed after 225 °C was attributed to the decomposition of the XG-g-Nano SiO2 structure, resulting in a total mass reduction of 31%, which was 14% less than the total mass loss of XG. This 14% reduction cannot be fully attributed to the inorganic SiO2 content. It also includes the barrier effect of nano-SiO2, which impedes the diffusion of volatile fragments (H2O, CO, CO2, furans) and further reduces mass loss by 10–11 wt %. Thus, in comparison to XG, XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS displayed a diminished degree of thermal loss at elevated temperatures. In the DSC curves, the endothermic peak around 200 °C is presumed to be the result of the decomposition of the grafted monomer AMPS, which corresponded to the mass loss observed in the TGA curve after 200 °C. The endothermic decomposition peak observed beyond 300 °C was likely associated with the decomposition of the polysaccharide chains of XG. The aforementioned analysis indicated that the simultaneous introduction of the heat-resistant monomer AMPS and modified nano-SiO2 into the XG molecules delayed the decomposition of the XG to some extent, thereby enhancing its thermal stability.

TG-DSC curves of XG and XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS.

Conformational transition of XG molecules is illustrated in Figure . At room temperature, the molecular structure of XG in dilute solution was observed to be disordered, while in concentrated solution. It was seen to adopt a double-helix structure. The interactions between molecules were facilitated by van der Waals forces between methyl and hemiacetal groups. Furthermore, the hydroxyl, carboxyl, and carbonyl groups in XG molecules formed a network structure through hydrogen bonding with adsorbed water. As the solution temperature increased and reached the conformational transition temperature of XG molecules, the aggregation of the rigid helical structures was disrupted, destroying the three-dimensional spatial structure. This transition formed an ordered to a disordered state resulted in the exposure of the main chain, rendering it more susceptible to degradation and significantly reducing its thermal stability. The introduction of modified nano-SiO2 and the monomer AMPS into the XG structure has been demonstrated to increase the conformational transition temperature. , This modification enhanced the rigidity and stability of the helical structures at elevated temperatures, thereby improving thermal resistance and enabling applications in high-temperature environments.

Shear Thinning Performance

The differences in shear thinning characteristics between XG and its silanized modified graft copolymer are shown in Table and Figure . XG exhibited a high viscosity even at a low concentration of 0.3%, and demonstrated excellent shear thinning properties, with a shear performance value of 6. Following graft modification with AMPS and nano-SiO2, although the viscosity of the gum decreased, the shear performance value of XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS at the same concentration (0.3%) was slightly higher than that of XG. As the solution concentration further increased to 3%, the shear performance value rose to 15.30, indicating that the good shear thinning properties remained satisfactory. Although the grafted xanthan gum exhibits lower initial viscosity than its native counterpart, this drawback is offset by three critical performance enhancements for field applications: (1) after aging at 150 °C, the grafted xanthan gum maintains significantly higher viscosity retention. While native xanthan gum demonstrates higher initial viscosity, it undergoes near-complete functional degradation under prolonged high-temperature aging, resulting in both lower final viscosity and inferior retention compared to the grafted variant. This demonstrates the superior thermal stability of grafted xanthan gum and its advantage in practical applications. (2) The grafted polymer reduces shear-induced viscosity loss. (3) The viscoelastic gel formed by the grafted xanthan gum under shear functions as a fluid loss additive, contributing to the formation of a more compact filter cake and reduced fluid loss. In summary, although native xanthan gum possesses higher initial viscosity prior to thermal aging, the grafted xanthan gum demonstrates greater potential for mobility control and filtration control after high-temperature aging.

Biotoxicity Evaluation

The test sample solution was placed into sample tubes, with a control group set up concurrently. The relative luminescence intensities of the XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS sample solution were quantified using a bioluminescence photometer, yielding values of 117%, 109%, and 112%. Consequently, the EC50 of XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS was determined to be greater than 106 mg/L, which classified it as exhibiting low biotoxicity by the toxicity grading scale.

Performance Testing

Rheology Test

To gain further insight into the impact of XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS on the rheological characteristics of drilling fluids, a comprehensive evaluation of the rheological parameters was conducted. These included viscosity at varying rotation speeds, PV, YP, G10′, and G10″. To evaluate the performance of the additive, systematic assessments were conducted in three distinct drilling fluid systems: freshwater-based mud, saturated saltwater mud, and potassium polymer drilling fluid, and the results are shown in Table . The incorporation of 1 wt % XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS resulted in a significant enhancement of viscosity across the shear rate range of 600–3 rpm in both freshwater-based and saturated saltwater drilling fluid systems. This indicated that the copolymer possessed thickening properties, thereby enhancing the viscosity of the drilling fluids. A comparable trend was observed in the values of YP, G10′, and G10″ values. Gel strength characterizes the strength of the spatial network structure formed by the interaction of clay particles and polymer molecules when the drilling fluid is in a static state. Conversely, yield point reflects the strength of this network structure under flowing conditions. During drilling operations, such as tripping and making a connection, circulation ceases. If the gel strength is too low, the drilling fluid lacks sufficient suspension capacity to hold cuttings and weighting materials. This can lead to the formation of settled solids at the bottom of the wellbore or a cuttings bed in horizontal sections, potentially causing stuck pipe and other downhole complications. Similarly, if the yield point is too low during circulation, cuttings will settle rapidly, compromising hole cleaning. Plastic viscosity typically represents the internal friction within the drilling fluid under flow conditions. An excessively high plastic viscosity can increase circulation pressure losses and may also negatively impact the rate of penetration. Therefore, the ratio of yield point to plastic viscosity (YP/PV) is frequently used to characterize the shear-thinning behavior and the capacity to suspend and transport cuttings. This ratio describes the fluid’s ability to become thinner at high shear rates (e.g., through the drill bit nozzles) to facilitate rock fragmentation, and thicker at low shear rates (e.g., in the annulus) to effectively carry cuttings. A YP/PV ratio in the range of 0.35–0.60 is generally considered favorable for drilling fluids. The experimental results show that after thermal aging at 150 °C for 16 h, the drilling fluid containing 1% graft-modified xanthan gum exhibited a YP/PV ratio of 0.39. This represents a 77% improvement compared to the ratio of 0.22 obtained with the fluid containing 1% native xanthan gum, indicating that the drilling fluid based on XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS possesses superior cuttings suspension and transport capabilities. Furthermore, after aging, the drilling fluid with the graft-modified xanthan gum maintained considerable viscosity, gel strength, and yield point, demonstrating excellent thermal stability. These findings collectively demonstrate that XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS has the potential to significantly enhance the rheological properties of drilling fluids, whether used in fresh water, salt water, or potassium polymer drilling fluid systems.

Filtration Test

Fluid intrusion into the formation not only causes contamination of the formation itself but also gives rise to wellbore instability. The maintenance of a low filtration volume is an important indicator of the efficacy of drilling fluids. To assess the effectiveness of XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS as a fluid loss agent in diverse drilling fluids in accordance with the API specifications and Chinese National Standard GB/T 16783.1, the FLAPI and FLHTHP were examined. Table presents the filtrate volumes of various drilling fluids containing XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS after hot rolling at 150 °C for 16 h. In saturated brine at 150 °C, the XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS modified system demonstrated FLAPI and FLHTHP values of 3.4 ± 0.34 mL and 15 ± 0.37 mL, respectively. Although these values showed a marginal increase compared to the freshwater system (3.0 ± 0.32 mL and 12 ± 0.36 mL for FLAPI and FLHTHP, respectively), they represented significant filtration improvement over the JY-1B additive. The FLAPI and FLHTHP test results confirmed that XG-g-nano-SiO2-AMPS plays a pivotal role in filtration control, even under elevated temperature conditions.

Conclusions

This study successfully synthesized XG-g-Nano SiO2-AMPS through free radical polymerization by chemically grafting modified nano-SiO2 and the thermally stable monomer AMPS onto natural biopolysaccharide. The incorporation of these components resulted in a more robust cross-linked network in the grafted product, enabling resists thermal disentanglement and ionic compression, thereby conferring exceptional stability under high-temperature conditions while maintaining higher viscosity and superior viscosity retention. Furthermore, the viscoelastic gel formed at the nanomicro scale under shear conditions functions as an effective fluid loss additive, producing a denser filter cake and demonstrating outstanding filtration control capability. The grafted xanthan gum also exhibits low biotoxicity, which is crucial for environmental protection. This grafting modification approach applied to natural biopolysaccharides not only overcomes the limitations of conventional xanthan gum but also leverages its biocompatibility and eco-friendly characteristics. The study highlights the practical significance of grafted xanthan gum in enhancing mobility control and filtration control performance of drilling fluids under high-temperature and high-salinity conditions, underscoring its potential value as a drilling fluid additive.

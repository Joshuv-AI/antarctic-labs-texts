Coloring outside the lines: Spectral bounds for generalized hypergraph colorings
Lies Beers, Raffaella Mulas

Abstract
It is known that, for an oriented hypergraph with (vertex) coloring number χ \chi and smallest and largest normalized Laplacian eigenvalues λ 1 \lambda_{1} and λ N \lambda_{N} , respectively, the inequality χ ≥ ( λ N − λ 1 ) / min ⁡ { λ N − 1 , 1 − λ 1 } \chi\geq(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{1})/\min\{\lambda_{N}-1,1-\lambda_{1}\} holds. We provide necessary conditions for oriented hypergraphs for which this bound is tight. Focusing on c c -uniform unoriented hypergraphs, we then generalize the bound to the setting of d d -proper colorings : colorings in which no edge contains more than d d vertices of the same color. We also adapt our proof techniques to derive analogous spectral bounds for d d -improper colorings of graphs and for edge colorings of hypergraphs. Moreover, for all coloring notions considered, we provide necessary conditions under which the bound is an equality. Keywords: Hypergraphs, Normalized Laplacian, Vertex colorings, Edge coloring

Background

2.1
The normalized Laplacian for hypergraphs

We begin by defining the normalized Laplacian for graphs, originally introduced by Chung (1992)
[
9
]
, using the vertex-edge incidence matrix. We then extend this definition to hypergraphs, following the generalization proposed by Jost and Mulas (2019)
[
21
]
. We emphasize properties that are most relevant to our paper, many of which are also discussed in
[
21
,
24
,
25
,
3
,
23
]
.
Specifically, we define oriented hypergraphs, introduce the Rayleigh quotients of their normalized Laplacian, and explore the relationship between the Rayleigh quotient and the eigenvalues of the normalized Laplacian.

Definition 2.1
.

An
oriented hypergraph

\Gamma=(V,E,\varphi)
consists of a vertex set
V
, an edge set
E\subset\mathcal{P}\bigl(V\bigr)
, and an orientation function
\varphi\colon V\times E\to\{0,-1,1\}
such that
\varphi(v,e)\neq 0\iff v\in e
.

We consider the following subclass of oriented hypergraphs.

Definition 2.2
.

For
c\geq 1
, an oriented hypergraph is
c
-uniform
if
|e|=c
for all
e\in E
.

Example 2.3
.

An example of a
3
-uniform oriented hypergraph can be found in Figure
2
below.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
v,v^{\prime}\in V
and
e\in E
.
If
\varphi(v,e)=1
(resp.
\varphi(v,e)=-1
), then
v
is an
output
(resp.
input
) of
e
. If

\varphi(v,e)=\varphi(v^{\prime},e)\neq 0\quad\text{(resp.\ $\varphi(v,e)=-\varphi(v^{\prime},e)\neq 0)$,}

then
v
and
v^{\prime}
are
co-oriented
(resp.
anti-oriented
) in
e
.

Remark 2.5
.

A simple graph can be seen as an oriented hypergraph such that every edge has exactly one input and one output.

We now give a generalization of connected and bipartite graphs, respectively.

Definition 2.6
.

The oriented hypergraph
\Gamma=(V,E,\varphi)
is
bipartite
if we can partition its vertex set
V=V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
such that, for every edge
e\in E
, either
e
has all its inputs in
V_{1}
and all its outputs in
V_{2}
, or vice versa.

Example 2.7
.

If an oriented hypergraph
\Gamma=(V,E,\varphi)
is such that
\varphi(V)=\{0,1\}
, then
\Gamma
is bipartite.

This leads us to the following definition.

Definition 2.8
.

For an oriented hypergraph
\Gamma=(V,E,\varphi)
, its
underlying hypergraph
is
\Gamma^{+}\coloneqq\bigl(V,E,\bigl|\varphi\bigr|\bigr)
, where
|\varphi|
indicates whether a vertex belongs to an edge, ignoring the orientation. That is,

\bigl|\varphi\bigr|(v,e):=|\varphi(v,e)|.

For the remainder of this section, we fix an oriented hypergraph
\Gamma=(V,E,\varphi)
with
|V|=N
and
|E|=M
. Using the orientation function
\varphi
, we define the incidence matrix, the adjacency matrix, and the normalized Laplacian of
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.9
.

The
incidence matrix

\mathcal{I}
is the
N\times M
matrix with entries

\mathcal{I}_{i,j}=\varphi\bigl(v_{i},e_{j}\bigr).

Note that, in the graph case, the Kirchhoff Laplacian of a graph
G
is defined as the matrix

K\coloneqq K(G)\coloneqq\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}^{\top},

where the incidence matrix corresponds to any arbitrary orientation of the graph, such that each edge has exactly one input and one output. Equivalently, the graph Kirchhoff Laplacian can be written as
K=D-A
, where
D
is the
degree matrix
, whose diagonal entries are the degrees of the corresponding vertices, and
A
is the adjacency matrix. Similarly, the
normalized Laplacian
of a graph
G
is the matrix

L\coloneqq L(G)\coloneqq D^{-1}\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}^{\top}=\id-D^{-1}A.

We define the adjacency matrix, degree (matrix), Kirchhoff Laplacian, and normalized Laplacian for hypergraphs in analogy with the graph case.

Definition 2.10
.

The
Kirchhoff Laplacian
is the matrix

K\coloneqq\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}^{\top}.

Additionally, the
degree matrix
is the diagonal matrix

D\coloneqq\diag\bigl(\deg(v_{1}),\ldots,\deg(v_{N})\bigr),

where the degree of a vertex
v
is defined as

\deg(v)\coloneqq\bigl|\bigl\{e\in E\colon v\in e\bigr\}\bigr|.

The
adjacency matrix
is the
N\times N
matrix with zeroes along the diagonal such that, for
i\neq j
, its entries are given by

\displaystyle A_{i,j}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\#\text{ edges where $v_{i}$ and $v_{j}$ are anti-oriented }

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\#\text{ edges where $v_{i}$ and $v_{j}$ are co-oriented.}

Finally, the
normalized Laplacian
is the
N\times N
matrix defined as

L\coloneqq D^{-1}K=\id-D^{-1}A.

We denote its eigenvalues by

\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{N}.

We use the notation

\bigl\{\mu_{1}^{(m_{1})},\ldots,\mu_{p}^{(m_{p})}\bigr\}

to denote a multiset containing the element
\mu_{i}
with multiplicity
m_{i}
. Throughout the paper, we shall also use the notation
m_{\Gamma}(\lambda)
for the multiplicity of
\lambda
as an eigenvalue of the normalized Laplacian of
\Gamma
.

Remark 2.11
.

The spectrum of a hypergraph equals the union of the spectra of its connected components.

Remark 2.12
.

The entries of
L
can be written as

L_{i,j}=\frac{\text{ edges where $v_{i}$ and $v_{j}$ are co-oriented }-\#\text{ edges where $v_{i}$ and $v_{j}$ are anti-oriented}}{\deg v_{i}}.

Note that there is not necessarily a one-to-one correspondence between an oriented hypergraph and its normalized Laplacian. For example, a hypergraph with no edges satisfies
A=\boldsymbol{0}
and
L=\id
. The following example gives a non-trivial example of an oriented hypergraph with
A=\boldsymbol{0}
and
L=\id
.

Example 2.13
.

Let
\Gamma
(Figure
2
) be the hypergraph with vertex set
V=\bigl\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\bigr\}
, edge set
E=\bigl\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\bigr\}
, with
e_{i}\coloneqq V\setminus\{v_{i}\}
, and incidence matrix

\mathcal{I}(\Gamma)\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}0&1&-1&-1\\
1&0&-1&1\\
1&-1&0&-1\\
1&1&1&0\end{bmatrix}.

Then, one can verify that
A=3\id-\mathcal{I}(\Gamma)\mathcal{I}(\Gamma)^{\top}=\boldsymbol{0}
, hence
L=\id
.

Figure 2:
An oriented hypergraph with
A=\boldsymbol{0}
.

The above example motivates the following observation.

Remark 2.14
.

We have

\displaystyle A=\textbf{0}

\displaystyle\iff L=I

\displaystyle\iff\lambda_{1}=\ldots=\lambda_{N}=1

\displaystyle\iff\lambda_{1}=1

\displaystyle\iff\lambda_{N}=1.

The last two characterizations follow from the fact that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}=N
. Hence, if

1=\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{N},

then
1=\lambda_{1}=\ldots=\lambda_{N}
. Similarly, if

\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{N}=1,

then
\lambda_{1}=\ldots=\lambda_{N}=1
. In particular, this implies that, if
A\neq\textbf{0}
, then
\lambda_{1}<1
and
\lambda_{N}>1
.

We now give a characterization of the eigenvalues of the normalized Laplacian for hypergraphs, using Rayleigh quotients. Chung (1992)
[
9
]
gave a similar characterization for the graph case, and this was generalized to hypergraphs by Jost and Mulas (2019)
[
21
]
. We will follow
[
21
]
in the remainder of this subsection.

We first define the following scalar product.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
C(V)
denote the vector space of functions
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and, given
f,g\in C(V)
, let

\langle f,g\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{v\in V}\deg v\cdot f(v)\cdot g(v).

We can see the normalized Laplacian
L
as an operator
C(V)\rightarrow C(V)
such that

Lf(v_{i})=f(v_{i})-\frac{1}{\deg v_{i}}\sum_{j\neq i}A_{ij}f(v_{j}).

(5)

Note that
L
is self-adjoint with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is positive definite.

With the Courant-Fischer-Weyl min-max Principle below, we can characterize the eigenvalues of
L
.

Theorem 2.16

(Courant-Fischer-Weyl min-max Principle)
.

Let
H
be an
N
-dimensional vector space with a positive definite scalar product
(\cdot,\cdot)
, and let
A\colon H\rightarrow H
be a self-adjoint linear operator. Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}
be the family of all
k
-dimensional subspaces of
H
.
Then the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{N}
of

A
can be obtained by

\lambda_{k}=\min_{H_{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{k}}\max_{g(\neq 0)\in H_{k}}\frac{(Ag,g)}{(g,g)}=\max_{{H}_{N-k+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{N-k+1}}\min_{g(\neq 0)\in{H}_{N-k+1}}\frac{(Ag,g)}{(g,g)}.

(6)

The vectors
g_{k}
realizing such a min-max or max-min then are corresponding
eigenvectors, and the min-max spaces
H_{k}
are spanned
by the eigenvectors for the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}
, and analogously, the max-min spaces
H_{N-k+1}
are spanned
by the eigenvectors for the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k},\dots,\lambda_{N}
.

Thus, we also have

\lambda_{k}=\min_{g(\neq 0)\in H,(g,g_{j})=0\ \mathrm{for}\ j=1,\dots,k-1}\frac{(Ag,g)}{(g,g)}=\max_{g(\neq 0)\in H,(g,g_{\ell})=0\ \mathrm{for}\ \ell=k+1,\dots,N}\frac{(Ag,g)}{(g,g)}.

(7)

In particular,

\lambda_{1}=\min_{g(\neq 0)\in H}\frac{(Ag,g)}{(g,g)},\qquad\lambda_{N}=\max_{g(\neq 0)\in H}\frac{(Ag,g)}{(g,g)}.

(8)

Definition 2.17
.

(Ag,g)/(g,g)
is called the
Rayleigh quotient
of
g
.

Now, for
f\in C(V)
, we let

\textrm{RQ}(f)\coloneqq\frac{\langle Lf,f\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle}=\frac{\sum_{e\in E}\biggl(\sum_{v\in e\text{ input}}f(v)-\sum_{w\in e\text{ output}}f(w)\biggr)^{2}}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg vf(v)^{2}}.

We can easily see from the expression of the Rayleigh quotient
\textrm{RQ}(f)
, in combination with Equation (
8
), that

\lambda_{1}=\min_{f\neq 0}\textrm{RQ}(f)\geq 0.

Chung (1992)
[
9
]
showed that
\lambda_{1}=0
in the graph case. However, in the hypergraph case, this is not necessarily true. For example, we saw in Remark
2.14
that
\lambda_{1}
can be equal to
1
.

Furthermore, in the graph case, we have that the largest eigenvalue satisfies
\lambda_{N}\leq 2

[
9
]
. This was generalized to hypergraphs by Mulas (2021)
[
24
]
, as follows.

Proposition 2.18
.

For every connected, oriented hypergraph
\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{N}\leq\max_{e\in E}|e|.

Furthermore, the inequality is sharp if and only if
\Gamma
is bipartite (Definition
2.6
) and
\Gamma
is a
c
-uniform hypergraph.

Mulas et al. (2022)
[
25
]
also proved the following proposition, relating the normalized Laplacian spectrum of a bipartite hypergraph (Definition
2.6
) and its underlying hypergraph (Definition
2.8
).

Proposition 2.19
.

If
\Gamma
is bipartite, then it is isospectral to its underlying hypergraph.

Remark 2.20
.

If
\Gamma
is an oriented hypergraph such that all vertices are inputs in all edges they belong to (or equivalently,
\Gamma
is the underlying hypergraph of some oriented hypergraph), then by Proposition
2.18
its largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{N}
equals
\max_{e\in E}|e|
if and only if
\Gamma
is
c
-uniform. In this case, as shown in
[
24
]
, the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{N}=c
equals the number of connected components, and the corresponding eigenfunctions are those functions that are constant on any connected component.

2.2
Coloring hypergraphs

We now provide an overview of the different notions of coloring considered in this paper, most of which were introduced earlier in the introduction. For each notion, the corresponding coloring number is defined as the minimum number of colors needed to color the hypergraph according to that notion.

We first define a proper vertex coloring for a graph, and then generalize this to hypergraphs in different ways.

Definition 2.21
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. A
(vertex)
k
-coloring
is a function
c:V\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
, and it is
proper
if
v\sim w
(i.e.,
\{v,w\}\in E
) implies that
c(v)\neq c(w)
. The corresponding coloring number is denoted by
\chi\coloneqq\chi(G)
.

We now define a proper strong
k
-coloring for the vertices of an oriented hypergraph.

Definition 2.22
.

Let
\Gamma
be an oriented hypergraph. A
proper strong (vertex)
k
-coloring
is a function
c\colon V(\Gamma)\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that, for every edge
e
and any two vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in e
, we have
c(v_{1})\neq c(v_{2})
. The corresponding coloring number is denoted by
\chi\coloneqq\chi(\Gamma)
.

We shall consider the notion of strong coloring in Section
3
, while in Section
4
we shall consider the following generalization.

Definition 2.23
.

Let
\Gamma
be an oriented hypergraph and let
d\geq 1
. A (vertex)
k
-coloring
c\colon V(\Gamma)\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
is
d
-proper
if every edge contains at most
d
vertices of every color. The corresponding coloring number is denoted by
\chi_{d}\coloneqq\chi_{d}(\Gamma)
.

In particular, a proper strong
k
-coloring is a
d
-proper
k
-coloring with
d=1
.

We also introduce and study the concept of
q
-tailored coloring, which will be the focus of Subsection
4.2
. In the setting of
c
-uniform hypergraphs in which all vertices are inputs in all edges they belong to,
q
-tailored colorings generalize
d
-proper colorings.

Definition 2.24
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
c
-uniform hypergraph in which all vertices are inputs in all edges they belong to, and let
q\in[0,c-1]
. A (vertex)
k
-coloring
c\colon V(\Gamma)\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
that has coloring classes
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
is
q
-tailored
if, for all
i=1,\ldots,k
and for all
v\in V_{i}
, we have

\sum_{w\in V_{i}}|A_{v,w}|\leq q\deg v.

(9)

The corresponding coloring number is denoted by
\chi^{q\text{-t}}\coloneqq\chi^{q\text{-t}}(\Gamma)
.

In particular, if
\Gamma
is a
c
-uniform hypergraph in which every vertex is an input in all edges it belongs to, then any
d
-proper coloring is also a
(d-1)
-tailored coloring. This is why
q
-tailored colorings generalize
d
-proper colorings in this setting.

Informally, a
q
-tailored coloring ensures that no vertex is too connected to others of the same color: the average number of same-colored neighbors of any vertex
v\in V
in an edge is at most a fraction
q
of its degree.

Furthermore, in Section
5
, we shall focus on the following generalization of the strong coloring number for graphs.

Definition 2.25
.

A vertex coloring
c\colon V\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
is
d
-improper
if every vertex
v
has at most
d
neighbors with the same color as
v
. The
d
-improper coloring number
of
G
, denoted
\chi^{d}=\chi^{d}(G)
, is the minimal number of colors needed to color the vertices
d
-improperly. The corresponding coloring number is denoted by
\chi^{d}\coloneqq\chi^{d}(G)
.

Finally, we shall consider edge colorings of hypergraphs, which are defined as follows and will be the focus of Section
6
.

Definition 2.26
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,\varphi)
be an oriented hypergraph. A
proper strong
k
-edge-coloring
is a function
c^{\prime}\colon E\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that
c^{\prime}(e_{1})=c^{\prime}(e_{2})\implies e_{1}\cap e_{2}=\varnothing
. The
strong edge coloring number

\chi^{\prime}\coloneqq\chi^{\prime}(\Gamma)
is the minimum number of colors needed to strongly color the edges properly.

3
Eigenvalue bounds for strong hypergraph vertex coloring

3.1
Strong vertex coloring of general oriented hypergraphs

We again fix an oriented hypergraph
\Gamma=(V,E,\varphi)
.
We first present an alternative proof of the following result, which was first proven by Abiad, Mulas and Zhang (2020)
[
1
, Corollary 5.4]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be an oriented hypergraph and let
\chi(\Gamma)
be defined as in Definition
2.22
. Then, we have that

\chi(\Gamma)\geq\frac{\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{1}}{\min\bigl\{\lambda_{N}-1,1-\lambda_{1}\bigr\}}.

Our proof relies on Rayleigh quotients and leads to new consequences, which are detailed in Proposition
3.2
and
3.3
. These results show that, if equality holds in the bound from Theorem
3.1
, then there must a specific relation between the eigenspaces corresponding to
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{N}
. To better understand the structure of such hypergraphs, one needs to know these eigenspaces. This is why, to gain further insight, in Subsection
3.2
, we shall focus on
c
-uniform unoriented hypergraphs, for which the eigenspace associated with
\lambda_{N}=c
is known to consist of constant functions.

We need the following:

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\bigl(\chi_{d}-1\bigr)\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V_{1}\\
w\in V_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in V_{1}\text{ or }\\
v,w\in V_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}\end{subarray}}\deg v}

\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{=}

\displaystyle\bigl(\chi_{d}-1\bigr)\bigl(1-\textrm{RQ}(g_{12})\bigr)

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle\bigl(\chi_{d}-1\bigr)\bigl(1-\lambda_{1}\bigr),

where, in
(i)
, we used the fact that

\displaystyle\textrm{RQ}\bigl(g_{12}\bigr)

\displaystyle=1+2\frac{\sum_{v\neq w}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|\cdot g_{12}(v)\cdot g_{12}(w)}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v\cdot g_{12}(v)^{2}}

\displaystyle=1+2\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V_{1}\\
w\in V_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|\cdot g_{12}(v)\cdot g_{12}(w)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in V_{1}\text{ or }\\
v,w\in V_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|\cdot g_{12}(v)\cdot g_{12}(w)}{\sum_{v\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}}\deg v\cdot g_{12}(v)^{2}}

\displaystyle=1+2\frac{-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V_{1}\\
w\in V_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in V_{1}\text{ or }\\
v,w\in V_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|}{\sum_{v\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}}\deg v}.

Case
2
:

\sum_{i\neq j}\max\biggl\{0,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V_{i}\\
w\in V_{j}\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in V_{i}\\
v\neq w\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in V_{j}\\
v\neq w\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|\biggr\}=0.

Then,

\displaystyle 2(\chi_{d}-1)\frac{\sum_{i\neq j}\max\biggl\{0,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V_{i}\\
w\in V_{j}\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in V_{i}\\
v\neq w\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in V_{j}\\
v\neq w\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|\biggr\}}{\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{v\in V_{i}\cup V_{j}}\deg v}=0

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\bigl(\chi_{d}-1\bigr)\bigl(1-\lambda_{1}\bigr),

since
\lambda_{1}<1
and
\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{v\in V_{i}\cup V_{j}}\deg v>0
.

Hence, Cases 1 and 2 show that the difference between the first two fractions in (
18
) is bounded above by
\bigl(\chi_{d}-1\bigr)\bigl(1-\lambda_{1}\bigr)
.

We now bound the last fraction appearing in (
18
). We have that

\displaystyle 2\chi_{d}\frac{\sum_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in V_{i}\\
v\neq w\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle=2\chi_{d}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\colon w\text{ in same}\\
\text{coloring class as $v$}\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle\overset{(ii)}{\leq}\chi_{d}\frac{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v(d-1)}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle=(d-1)\chi_{d},

where, in
(ii)
, we used the fact that, for every vertex
v\in V
and every edge
e\ni v
, there are at most
d-1
other vertices in
e
that have the same color as
v
.

Combining (
18
) with the bounds obtained above, we conclude that

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle\leq 1+\bigl(\chi_{d}-1\bigr)\bigl(1-\lambda_{1}\bigr)+\chi_{d}(d-1),

which is equivalent to

\chi_{d}\geq\frac{c-\lambda_{1}}{d-\lambda_{1}}.

Finally, we need to show that the bound is sharp if and only if
d\mid c
.

Note that, if the inequality in
(ii)

is an equality, then, for every coloring with corresponding coloring classes
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\chi_{d}}
, for every edge
e\in E
, and for every coloring class
V_{i}
, we must have
|e\cap V_{i}|\in\bigl\{0,d\bigr\}
. Hence,
d\mid c
.

Now we want to show that, if
d\mid c
, then the bound in the statement is sharp, that is, there exists a
c
-uniform hypergraph for which

\chi_{d}=\frac{c-\lambda_{1}}{d-\lambda_{1}}.

Consider a
c
-uniform hyperflower. Then, we know that
\lambda_{1}=0
, and therefore the bound becomes
\chi_{d}\geq c/d
. To show that this is an equality, observe that one can always color the vertices of a
c
-uniform hyperflower using exactly
c/d
colors, such that each edge contains exactly
d
vertices of every color. See
Figure

4
for the case where
c=9
and
d=3
. Therefore,
\chi_{d}=c/d
.

∎

Figure 4:
The
9
-uniform hyperflower
H_{3,2}^{9}
, for which the bound in
Theorem

4.3
is sharp.

In Corollary
4.5
below, we provide necessary conditions for a hypergraph to attain equality in the bound from Theorem
4.3
. To that end, we first prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
c
-uniform oriented hypergraph for which all vertices are inputs in all edges they belong to. Moreover, let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
be a partition of the vertex set, and assume that there exists
m\geq 1
such that, for every edge
e\in E
and for all
i=1,\ldots,k
,

\bigl|e\cap V_{i}\bigr|\in\bigl\{0,m\bigr\}.

Then, the functions
g_{ij}
from Definition
3.4
are eigenfunctions if and only if, for all
i=1,\ldots,k
and for all
v\in V
, we have

\bigl|\{e\in E\colon v\in e\text{ and }e\cap V_{i}\neq\varnothing\}\bigr|=\begin{cases}\frac{(c/m-1)\deg v}{k-1},&\text{ if }v\notin V_{i},\\
\deg v,&\text{ if }v\in V_{i}.\end{cases}

In this case, the corresponding eigenvalue is
(m\cdot k-c)/(k-1)
.

Proof.

For the “if” direction, let
1\leq i\neq j\leq k
, and consider vertices
v_{i}\in V_{i}
,
v_{j}\in V_{j}
. Let also
v\in V\setminus\bigl(V_{i}\cup V_{j}\bigr)
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle Lg_{ij}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V}L_{v,w}g_{ij}(w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V_{i}}\frac{|A_{v,w}|}{\deg v}-\sum_{w\in V_{j}}\frac{|A_{v,w}|}{\deg v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\deg v}\biggl(\bigl|\bigl\{\bigl(e,w_{i}\bigr)\colon v\in e,w_{i}\in e\cap V_{i}\bigr\}\bigr|-\bigl|\bigl\{\bigl(e,w_{j}\bigr)\colon v\in e,w_{j}\in e\cap V_{j}\bigr\}\bigr|\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{\deg v}\biggl(\frac{(c/m-1)\deg v}{k-1}-\frac{(c/m-1)\deg v}{k-1}\biggr)

\displaystyle=0,

while

\displaystyle Lg_{ij}(v_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V}L_{v_{i},w}g_{ij}(w)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{w\in V_{i}}\frac{|A_{v_{i},w}|}{\deg v_{i}}-\sum_{w\in V_{j}}\frac{|A_{v_{i},w}|}{\deg v_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\deg v_{i}}\biggl(\bigl|\bigl\{(e,w_{i})\colon v_{i}\in e,w_{i}\in e\cap V_{i}\bigr\}\bigr|-\bigl|\bigl\{(e,w_{i})\colon v_{i}\in e,w_{i}\in e\cap V_{j}\bigr\}\bigr|\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\deg v_{i}}\bigl(\deg v_{i}\cdot m-m\cdot\bigl|\bigl\{e\colon v_{i}\in e,e\cap V_{j}\neq\varnothing\bigr\}\bigr|\bigr)

\displaystyle=m-\frac{m}{\deg v_{i}}\cdot\frac{(c/m-1)\deg v_{i}}{k-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{m(k-1)}{k-1}-\frac{c-m}{k-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{m\cdot k-c}{k-1}.

Analogously, we one can show that

\displaystyle Lg_{ij}(v_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{c-m\cdot k}{k-1}.

Hence,
g_{ij}
is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue
(m\cdot k-c)/(k-1)
.

To prove the “only if” direction, assume that the functions
g_{ij}
are eigenfunctions. Then, let
v\in V
, and fix distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
such that
v\notin V_{i}\cup V_{j}
. Then,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=Lg_{ij}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\deg v}\biggl(\bigl|\bigl\{\bigl(e,w_{i}\bigr)\colon v\in e,w_{i}\in e\cap V_{i}\bigr\}\bigr|-\bigl|\bigl\{\bigl(e,w_{j}\bigr)\colon v\in e,w_{j}\in e\cap V_{j}\bigr\}\bigr|\biggr).

Hence, for any
i,j
such that
v\notin V_{i}\cup V_{j}
, we have that

\bigl|\bigl\{\bigl(e,w_{i}\bigr)\colon v\in e,w_{i}\in e\cap V_{i}\bigr\}\bigr|=\bigl|\bigl\{\bigl(e,w_{j}\bigr)\colon v\in e,w_{j}\in e\cap V_{j}\bigr\}\bigr|.

Since

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1,\ldots,k\\
v\notin V_{l}\end{subarray}}\bigl|\bigl\{\bigl(e,w_{l}\bigr)\colon v\in e,w_{l}\in e\cap V_{l}\bigr\}\bigr|=\deg v\bigl(c-m\bigr)

and since
|e\cap V_{i}|\in\{0,m\}
for all
e\in E
and
i=1,\ldots,k
,
we conclude that

\bigl|\{e\in E\colon v\in e\text{ and }e\cap V_{i}\neq\varnothing\}\bigr|=\begin{cases}\frac{(c/m-1)\deg v}{k-1},&\text{ if }v\notin V_{i},\\
\deg v,&\text{ if }v\in V_{i}.\end{cases}\qed

Theorem
4.4
has the following corollary, which provides necessary conditions for a hypergraph to attain equality in the bound from Theorem

.

Corollary 4.5
.

If Theorem
4.3
holds with equality, then for any
d
-proper
\chi_{d}
-coloring with coloring classes

, the following hold:

1.

For each edge
e\in E
and for all

,

\bigl|e\cap V_{i}\bigr|\in\bigl\{0,d\bigr\}.

2.

For all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,\chi_{d}\}
, the functions
g_{ij}
from Definition
3.4
are eigenfunctions with eigenvalue

\lambda_{1}=\frac{d\cdot\chi_{d}-c}{\chi_{d}-1}.

3.

For all distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,\chi_{d}\}
, and for all
v_{i}\in V_{i}
, we have

\bigl|\bigl\{e\colon v_{i}\in e\text{ and }e\cap V_{j}\neq\varnothing\bigr\}\bigr|=\frac{\deg v_{i}\bigl(c/d-1\bigr)}{\chi_{d}-1}.

Moreover, even if the bound in Theorem

is not attained, statement

implies that statements

and

Assume that Theorem

4.3
that, for all

with

Finally, even if the bound in Theorem

∎

4.2
q
-tailored colorings

We shall now focus on
q
-tailored colorings (Definition
2.24
), which generalize
d
-proper colorings in the context of
c
-uniform hypergraphs for which all vertices are inputs in all their edges. We begin by recalling the definition and the idea behind it from Section
2.2
.

Recall.
Let
\Gamma
be a
c
-uniform hypergraph in which all vertices are inputs in all edges they belong to, and let
q\in[0,c-1]
. A (vertex)
k
-coloring
c\colon V(\Gamma)\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
that has coloring classes
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
is
q
-tailored
if, for all
i=1,\ldots,k
and for all
v\in V_{i}
, we have

\sum_{w\in V_{i}}|A_{v,w}|\leq q\deg v.

The corresponding coloring number is denoted by
\chi^{q\text{-t}}\coloneqq\chi^{q\text{-t}}(\Gamma)
. It is the smallest positive integer such that there exists a
q
-tailored
\chi^{q\text{-t}}
-coloring of the vertices of
\Gamma
.

In particular, if
\Gamma
is a
c
-uniform hypergraph in which every vertex is an input in all edges it belongs to, then any
d
-proper coloring is also a
(d-1)
-tailored coloring. This is why
q
-tailored colorings generalize
d
-proper colorings in this setting.

Informally, a
q
-tailored coloring ensures that no vertex is too connected to others of the same color: the average number of same-colored neighbors of any vertex
v\in V
in an edge is at most a fraction
q
of its degree.

Example 4.8
.

Consider a hypergraph in which the vertices represent professional tailors, and the edges represent collaborative projects, such as working on the same clothing line. We assume that every project requires the same amount of time from each tailor.

We aim to partition the tailors into vacation groups such that the workload for the non-vacationing tailors does not become too high. To achieve this, we fix a fraction
q\in[0,c-1]
. A
q
-tailored coloring ensures that, for each tailor, the cumulative number of shared projects with vacationing colleagues, which is equal to the sum of absolute values of the adjacency entries of vertices belonging to the same vacation group,
does not exceed this fraction
q
of their total workload.

We generalize Theorem
4.3
to the setting of
q
-tailored colorings, as follows.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
q\in[0,c-1]
. Then,

\chi^{q\text{-t}}\geq\frac{c-\lambda_{1}}{q+1-\lambda_{1}}.

Furthermore, the bound is sharp.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof of Theorem
4.3
(substituting
\chi_{d}
by
\chi^{q\text{-t}}
), except for the bound of the last fraction in (
18
). In the more general definition of
q
-tailored colorings, we have:

\displaystyle 2\chi^{q\text{-t}}\frac{\sum_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,w\in V_{i}\\
v\neq w\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle=2\chi^{q\text{-t}}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\colon w\text{ in same}\\
\text{coloring class as $v$}\end{subarray}}\bigl|A_{v,w}\bigr|}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{\leq}\chi^{q\text{-t}}\frac{\sum_{v\in V}q\cdot\deg v}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle=q\chi^{q\text{-t}},

where, in
(i)
, we applied the definition of
q
-tailored coloring.

Combining the two bounds, we obtain

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle\leq 1+\bigl(\chi^{q\text{-t}}-1\bigr)\bigl(1-\lambda_{1}\bigr)+q\cdot\chi^{q\text{-t}},

which is equivalent to

\chi^{q\text{-t}}\geq\frac{c-\lambda_{1}}{q+1-\lambda_{1}}.

Finally, by Theorem
4.3
, we know that the bound is sharp.
∎

The following corollary follows by the proof of Theorem
4.9
.

Corollary 4.10
.

If the bound in Theorem
4.9
holds as an equality, then for all
v\in V
,

\sum_{w\in V_{i}}|A_{v,w}|=q\deg v.

Moreover, for every
q
-tailored coloring with
\chi^{q\text{-t}}
colors and coloring classes
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\chi^{q\text{-t}}}
, the functions
g_{ij}
from Definition
3.4
are eigenfunctions with eigenvalue

\lambda_{1}=\frac{(q+1)\chi^{q\text{-t}}-c}{\chi^{q\text{-t}}-1}.

5
Coloring graphs
d
-improperly

In this section, we consider graphs instead of general hypergraphs.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with
|V|=N
. We let
L=I-D^{-1}A
be its normalized Laplacian, and we write the eigenvalues of
L
by

0=\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}.

While the bound in Theorem
3.1
was proved in
[
1
]
for hypergraphs, its graph version,
\chi\geq\lambda_{N}/\bigl(\lambda_{N}-1\bigr)
, was first established in
[
11
]
. In this section, we prove a generalization of this inequality that involves the
d
-improper coloring number (Definition
2.25
).

Recall.
A vertex coloring
c\colon V\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
is
d
-improper
if every vertex
v
has at most
d
neighbors with the same color as
v
. The
d
-improper coloring number
, notation
\chi^{d}=\chi^{d}(G)
, is the minimal number of colors needed to color
G

d
-improperly.

Remark 5.1
.

A proper vertex coloring of a graph is
0
-improper. Moreover, if
G
is a
k
-regular graph and if
d\geq 0
, then a
d/k
-tailored coloring (Definition
2.24
) corresponds to a
d
-improper coloring.

Bilu (2006)
[
6
]
proved the following generalization of the Hoffman bound
[
20
]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
d\geq 0
and let
\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{N}
be the eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix. Then,

\chi^{d}\bigl(G\bigr)\geq\frac{\mu_{1}-\mu_{N}}{d-\mu_{N}}.

(19)

We now adapt the proof strategy from Section
4
to derive a similar spectral bound for
d
-improper coloring of graphs.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
d\geq 0
, and let
\overline{\deg}
denote the average degree of
G
. Then,

\chi^{d}\geq\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{N}-1+d/\overline{\deg}}.

(20)

Moreover, the bound is sharp.

Proof.

Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\chi^{d}}
be the coloring classes of a
d
-improper coloring. Then, analogously to the beginning of the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\textrm{RQ}\bigl(\boldsymbol{1}\bigr)=\frac{\sum_{v\sim w}\bigl(\boldsymbol{1}(v)-\boldsymbol{1}(w)\bigr)^{2}}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v\cdot\boldsymbol{1}(v)^{2}}

\displaystyle=1-2\frac{\sum_{v\sim w}\boldsymbol{1}(v)\boldsymbol{1}(w)}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle=1-2\frac{\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v\in V_{i}\\
w\in V_{j}\end{subarray}}1}{\frac{1}{\chi^{d}-1}\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{v\in V_{i}\cup V_{j}}\deg v}-2\frac{\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)\sum_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v,w\in V_{i}\end{subarray}}1}{\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle=1-2\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)\frac{\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v\in V_{i}\\
w\in V_{j}\end{subarray}}1}{\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{v\in V_{i}\cup V_{j}}\deg v}+2\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)\frac{\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)\sum_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v,w\in V_{i}\end{subarray}}1}{\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}-2\chi^{d}\frac{\sum_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v,w\in V_{i}\end{subarray}}1}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle=1-2\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)\frac{\sum_{i\neq j}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v\in V_{i}\\
w\in V_{j}\end{subarray}}1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v,w\in V_{i}\text{ or }\\
v,w\in V_{j}\end{subarray}}1\biggr)}{\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{v\in V_{i}\cup V_{j}}\deg v}-2\chi^{d}\frac{\sum_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v,w\in V_{i}\end{subarray}}1}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}.

(21)

We now bound the first and second fraction in (
21
) separately. For the first fraction, we
may assume without loss of generality that
\textrm{RQ}\bigl(g_{12}\bigr)=\max_{i\neq j}\textrm{RQ}\bigl(g_{ij}\bigr)
. Then,
analogously to the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we have that

\displaystyle 2\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)\frac{\sum_{i\neq j}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v\in V_{i}\\
w\in V_{j}\end{subarray}}1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v,w\in V_{i}\text{ or }\\
v,w\in V_{j}\end{subarray}}1\biggr)}{\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{v\in V_{i}\cup V_{j}}\deg v}

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)\frac{\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v\in V_{1}\\
w\in V_{2}\end{subarray}}1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v,w\in V_{1}\text{ or }\\
v,w\in V_{2}\end{subarray}}1\biggr)}{\sum_{v\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}}\deg v}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\textrm{RQ}\bigl(g_{12}\bigr)-1\bigr)\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\lambda_{N}-1\bigr)\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr).

To bound the second fraction in (
21
), we observe that

\displaystyle 2\chi^{d}\frac{\sum_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v,w\in V_{i}\end{subarray}}1}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle=2\chi^{d}\frac{\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim v\colon\\
w\text{ in same colo-}\\
\text{ring class as $v$}\end{subarray}}1}{2\cdot|E|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi^{d}}{|E|}\cdot\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\sim v\colon\\
w\text{ in same colo- }\\
\text{ring class as $v$}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\chi^{d}}{|E|}\cdot|V|\cdot\frac{d}{2},

since every
v\in V
has at most
d
neighbors in the same coloring class, and each edge is counted twice. Hence,

\displaystyle 2\chi^{d}\frac{\sum_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\sim w\\
v,w\in V_{i}\end{subarray}}1}{\sum_{v\in V}\deg v}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\chi^{d}}{|E|}\cdot|V|\cdot\frac{d}{2}

\displaystyle=\chi^{d}\cdot\frac{d}{\overline{\deg}}.

Combining (
21
) with the two bounds above, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq 1-\bigl(\lambda_{N}-1\bigr)\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr)-\chi^{d}\cdot\frac{d}{\overline{\deg}}.

Rearranging yields

\displaystyle\chi^{d}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{N}-1+d/\overline{\deg}}.

Finally, the bound is sharp: Guo, Kang and Zwaneveld
[
19
]
showed that the bound in (
19
), which coincides with our bound in the case of regular graphs, is attained with equality for a family of regular graphs.
∎

We now establish the following theorem concerning graphs that attain equality in Theorem
5.3
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
d\geq 0
and let
G
be a graph for which the bound in (
20
) is attained with equality. Then,
G
must be a regular graph.

Proof.

Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\chi^{d}}
be the coloring classes with respect to some
d
-improper
\chi^{d}
-coloring. We define

e(v,W)\coloneqq\bigl|\bigl\{\{v,w\}\in E\colon w\in W\bigr\}\bigr|.

Since the bound in (
20
) is attained with equality, it follows from the proof of Theorem
5.3
that every vertex has exactly
d
neighbors within its own coloring class. Furthermore, by the min-max principle (
8
), the functions
g_{ij}
from Definition
3.4
must be eigenfunctions with corresponding eigenvalue

\lambda_{N}=\frac{\bigl(1-d/\overline{\deg}\bigr)\chi^{d}}{\chi^{d}-1}.

Now let
v\in V
, and let
i
be such that
v\in V_{i}
. Let
j
and
k
be distinct from
i
and from each other. Then,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=Lg_{jk}(v)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V_{k}\\
w\sim v\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg v}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V_{j}\\
w\sim v\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\deg v}\bigl(e\bigl(v,V_{k}\bigr)-e\bigl(v,V_{j}\bigr)\bigr).

Hence,

\displaystyle e(v,V_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{\deg v-d}{\chi^{d}-1}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{\bigl(1-d/\overline{\deg}\bigr)\chi^{d}}{\chi^{d}-1}

\displaystyle=Lg_{ij}(v)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V_{j}\\
w\sim v\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg v}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V_{i}\\
w\sim v\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg v}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{e(v,V_{j})-e(v,V_{i})}{\deg v}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{e(v,V_{j})-d}{\deg v}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1-\chi^{d}\cdot d/\overline{\deg}}{\chi^{d}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{e(v,V_{j})-d}{\deg v}.

Rewriting gives

\displaystyle e(v,V_{j})=\deg v\cdot\frac{1-\chi^{d}\cdot d/\overline{\deg}}{\chi^{d}-1}+d.

Equating the two expressions for
e(v,V_{l})
gives

\displaystyle\deg v-d

\displaystyle=\deg v\bigl(1-\chi^{d}\cdot d/\overline{\deg}\bigr)+d\bigl(\chi^{d}-1\bigr),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=d\cdot\chi^{d}\biggl(1-\frac{\deg v}{\overline{\deg}}\biggr).

Therefore,
\deg v=\overline{\deg}
, and since
v
was arbitrary, we conclude that
G
must be a regular graph.
∎

By Theorem
5.4
, the graphs for which (
20
) holds with equality form a subset of those for which (
19
) is an equality (namely, the regular graphs). Theorem 10 from
[
19
]
describes properties of such graphs.

Remark 5.5
.

A possible (more intuitive) reason why the bound in (
20
) cannot be sharp unless
G
is regular, is that the number of edges cannot be recovered from the normalized Laplacian spectrum of a graph, whereas it can be recovered from its adjacency spectrum.

6
Edge-coloring hypergraphs

In this final section we shall shine a spectral light on edge-colorings of hypergraphs (Definition
2.26
). Here, edges rather than vertices are assigned colors, under the constraint that intersecting edges must receive distinct colors.

Throughout this section, we fix an oriented hypergraph
\Gamma=(V,E,\varphi)
on
N
nodes and
M
edges, and we let
\chi^{\prime}\coloneqq\chi^{\prime}(\Gamma)
denote its

strong edge coloring number
(Definition
2.26
).

We shall now define the
edge Laplacian
, which was introduced by Jost and Mulas (2019)
[
21
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
and
D
be the incidence matrix and degree matrix of
\Gamma
from Definition
2.10
, respectively. Then, the
edge Laplacian
of
\Gamma
is the
M\times M
matrix

L^{1}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}^{\top}D^{-1}\mathcal{I}.

We denote its eigenvalues by

0\leq\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{M}.

Remark 6.2
.

Recall that
L=D^{-1}\mathcal{I}\mathcal{I}^{\top}
. Since matrices of the form
AB
and
BA
have the same non-zero spectrum, this applies in particular to
L
and
L^{1}
. Hence, as shown in
[, Corollary 14]
if we let
m_{V}
and
m_{E}
be the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
0
of
L
and
L^{1}
, respectively, then

m_{V}-m_{E}=|V|-|E|.

(22)

In particular, if
|E|>|V|
, then
\mu_{1}=0
.

Moreover, note that

\mu_{1}\leq\frac{N}{M}\text{ and }\mu_{N}\geq\frac{N}{M},

since
\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mu_{1}=N
.

Example 6.3
.

Let
H_{p,k}^{c}
be the
c
-uniform hyperflower with
p
petals. Then, it follows from (
17
) that the spectrum of
L^{1}
equals

\biggl\{c^{(1)},\bigl(c-k\bigr)^{(p-1)}\biggr\},

which consists of the first
M
non-zero eigenvalues of
L
.

Also for
L^{1}
, we have a characterization of
\textrm{RQ}(\gamma)
, for functions
\gamma\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
:

\textrm{RQ}(\gamma)=\frac{\langle L^{1}\gamma,\gamma\rangle}{\langle\gamma,\gamma\rangle}=\frac{\sum_{v\in V}\frac{1}{\deg v}\cdot\biggl(\sum_{e\ni v\colon v\text{ input }}\gamma(e)-\sum_{e\ni v\colon v\text{ output }}\gamma(e)\biggr)^{2}}{\sum_{e\in E}\gamma(e)^{2}}.

(23)

We now prove a theorem connecting the eigenvalues of the edge Laplacian with the strong edge coloring number.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
c
-uniform hypergraph in which all vertices are inputs in every edge they belong to, and let
\overline{\deg}
denote the average degree of
\Gamma
. Then,

\chi^{\prime}\geq\frac{c-\mu_{1}}{c/\overline{\deg}-\mu_{1}},

(24)

and the inequality is sharp.

We need the following:

Convention 2
.

We use the convention that, if
\mu_{1}=\frac{c}{\overline{\deg}}=\frac{N}{M}
(cf. Remark
6.2
), then

\frac{c-\mu_{1}}{c/\overline{\deg}-\mu_{1}}=1.

Note that, for
c
-uniform hypergraphs, this situation can only occur if
\mu_{1}=\mu_{N}=c
, which means that
\Gamma
is given by
N/c
disjoint edges of size
c
.

Proof.

Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\chi^{\prime}}
be the coloring classes with respect to some proper strong edge coloring. For distinct
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,\chi^{\prime}\}
, define the function
\gamma_{ij}\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

\gamma_{ij}(e)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }e\in V_{i},\\
-1,&\text{ if }e\in V_{j},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(25)

Note that the all-ones function
\boldsymbol{1}
is an eigenfunction of
L^{1}(\Gamma)
with eigenvalue
c
. Hence,

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\textrm{RQ}(\boldsymbol{1})=\frac{\sum_{v\in V}\frac{1}{\deg v}\biggl(\sum_{e\ni v}\boldsymbol{1}(e)\biggr)^{2}}{\sum_{e\in E}\boldsymbol{1}(e)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{e\in E}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V\\
v\in e^{\prime}\cap e\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg v}\biggr)}{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\biggl(\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{e\ni v}\frac{1}{\deg v}+2\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{e\in V_{i}}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in V_{j}}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V\\
v\in e\cap e^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg v}\biggr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{M}+2\frac{\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{e\in V_{i}}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in V_{j}}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V\\
v\in e\cap e^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg v}\biggr)}{\frac{1}{\chi^{\prime}-1}\cdot\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{e\in V_{i}\cup V_{j}}1}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{WLOG}}{\leq}\frac{N}{M}+2(\chi^{\prime}-1)\frac{\sum_{e\in V_{1}}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in V_{2}}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V\\
v\in e\cap e^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg v}\biggr)}{\sum_{e\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}}1}

\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{=}\frac{N}{M}+(\chi^{\prime}-1)\biggl(\frac{N}{M}-\textrm{RQ}(\gamma_{12})\biggr)

\displaystyle\overset{(ii)}{\leq}\chi^{\prime}\cdot\frac{N}{M}-(\chi^{\prime}-1)\cdot\mu_{1},

where in
(i)
we used the fact that

\textrm{RQ}(\gamma_{12})=\frac{N}{M}-2\frac{\sum_{e\in V_{1}}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in V_{2}}\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V\\
v\in e\cap e^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg v}\biggr)}{\sum_{e\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}}1},

while in
(ii)
we use the min-max principle from (
8
).

The inequality we found is equivalent to

\chi^{\prime}\geq\frac{c-\mu_{1}}{N/M-\mu_{1}}=\frac{c-\mu_{1}}{c/\overline{\deg}-\mu_{1}},

provided that
c/\overline{\deg}-\mu_{1}>0
; otherwise, the inequality is trivial (by Convention
2
).

Finally, to show that the inequality is sharp, consider
c
-uniform hyperflowers
H_{p,k}^{c}
with
p
petals and
k
central vertices. In this case, it follows from (
17
) that
\mu_{1}=c-k
, and it is easy to see that
\chi^{\prime}=p=M
. Furthermore,

\frac{c-\mu_{1}}{N/M-\mu_{1}}=\frac{k}{(M(c-k)+k)/M-(c-k)}=\frac{k}{k/M}=M,

hence the bound is attained with equality.
∎

We have the following proposition about the equality case in Theorem
6.4
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
c
-uniform hypergraph in which all vertices are inputs in all edges they belong to, and for which the bound from (
24
) is attained with equality. Then, we have the following two cases:

1.

If
\mu_{1}=0
, then
\Gamma
is
k
-regular for some
k
, and
\chi^{\prime}=k
. Furthermore, the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
0
is at least
k-1
.

2.

If
\mu_{1}>0
, then
\mu_{1}
is an eigenvalue of
L^{1}
and
L
with multiplicity at least
\chi^{\prime}-1
.

Proof.

For the first case, assume that
\mu_{1}=0
. Then, the bound from (
24
) becomes

\chi^{\prime}\geq\overline{\deg}.

Since we have the trivial bound
\chi^{\prime}\geq\max\deg
, it must hold that
\overline{\deg}=\max\deg
, which implies that
\Gamma
is
\overline{\deg}
-regular, and
\chi^{\prime}=\overline{\deg}
.

Furthermore, if
\mu_{1}=0
, then the functions
\gamma_{1i}
for
i=1,\ldots,\chi^{\prime}
from (
25
) are linearly independent eigenfunctions with eigenvalue
\mu_{1}=0
. Therefore, the multiplicity of
0
is at least
\chi^{\prime}-1=k-1
.

For the second case, assume that
\mu_{1}>0
. Also in this case, for
j=2,\ldots,\chi^{\prime}
, the functions
\gamma_{1j}
from (
25
) are linearly independent eigenfunctions of
L^{1}
with eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
. Hence, the functions
D^{-1}\mathcal{I}\gamma_{ij}
are
\chi^{\prime}-1
linearly independent eigenfunctions of
L
with eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
.
∎

We end with one final remark.

Remark 6.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
c
-uniform hypergraph in which all vertices are inputs in all edges they belong to.
If
M>N
, then
\mu_{1}=0
, and the bound from (
24
) becomes
\chi^{\prime}\geq\overline{\deg}
, which does not provide any additional information beyond the trivial bound
\chi^{\prime}\geq\max\deg
. However, if
M<N+\chi^{\prime}-1
, then
0
is an eigenvalue of
L
with multiplicity at least
N+\chi^{\prime}-1-M
.

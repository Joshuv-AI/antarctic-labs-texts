Polar sets for m m -subharmonic functions on compact Hermitian manifolds
Sławomir Kołodziej and Ngoc Cuong Nguyen

Abstract
. We prove a sharp decay of capacity of sublevel sets of a ( ω , m ) (\omega,m) -subharmonic functions on a n n -dimensional compact Hermitian manifold ( X , ω ) (X,\omega) which generalizes the case m = n m=n as well as the case 1 ≤ m ≤ n 1\leq m\leq n on a compact Kähler manifold. We also obtain the full characterizations of polar sets of such functions in terms of the corresponding local and global capacities, and of the extremal functions.

[
BT76

BT82

[
Le50
]

[
Jo78
]
on the equivalence between locally and globally pluripolar sets. Subsequently, the first author found an almost sharp uniform estimate for solutions of complex Monge-Ampère equation whose the right hand side is well-dominated by the capacity
[
Ko98
,
Ko05
]
. The framework of pluripotential theory in
[
BT76
,
BT82
]
has been generalized successfully to compact Käher manifolds by Guedj and Zeriahi
[
GZ05
]
. Such a global pluripotential theory had a great impact in Kähler geometry as shown in the monograph
[
GZ17
]
.

In the real setting,
k
-convex functions are admissible solutions to real
k
-Hessian equations studied in
[
CNS85
]
. The singularities of such functions have been studied thoroughly by Labutin in
[
La02
]
where the ideas of pluripotential theory proved to be useful. We refer the reader to the survey of Wang
[
Wa09
]
and reference therein for more information on the equation and properties of this class of functions.

Later Błocki
[
Bl05
]
initiated the study of potential theory for
m
-subharmonic (
m
-sh for short) functions while smooth
m
-sh functions are admissible solutions to the complex Hessian equation which have been studied earlier in
[
V88
]
,
[
Li04
]
, independently. A major progress has been obtained by Dinew and the first author
[
DK14
,
DK17
]
where the authors developed the weak solution theory with the right hand side in
L^{p}
for the equation both in domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and on compact Kähler manifolds. This was a strong catalyst to push further the study of singularities of
m
-sh functions and Cegrell’s approach
[
Ce98
]
to complex Hessian equations which was done notably by Lu
[
Lu13b
,
Lu15
]
, Lu and Nguyen
[
LN15
]
.

[

]
and
[
KN16
]
after the works Tosatti and Weinkove
[

in the case of

]
and Zhang
[
Zha17
]
(independently) in the general case

. Since then, the topic has become attractive and there are many recent works in this area. Let us mention just a few

]

Now let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
and let
m
be an integer,
1\leq m\leq n
. Denote by
SH_{m}(X,\omega)
the set of all
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions on
X
. We continue the study of potential theory for
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions
on compact Hermitian manifolds initiated in
[
KN16
,
KN25
]
. The crucial technical estimates carried out before for open sets
are here obtained in the compact setting. They allow to get more information on the singular (polar) sets of
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions, in particular the equivalence of notions of locally polar and globally polar sets and their characterization in terms of capacity.

For a Borel set
E\subset X
the (global)
m
-capacity is given by

cap_{m}(E)=\sup\left\{\int_{E}H_{m}(v):v\in SH_{m}(X,\omega),-1\leq v\leq 0\right\}.

Here the complex Hessian measure of a bounded
(\omega,m)
-sh function
v
is

(1.1)

H_{m}(v):=(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}.

We first show the following sharp estimate for the capacity of of sublevel sets.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
v_{0}\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)
be such that
\sup_{X}v_{0}=0
. There exists a uniform constant
A
depending only on
\omega,m,n
such that

cap_{m}(\{v_{0}<-t\})\leq\frac{A}{t}\quad\text{for every }t>0.

In particular,
cap_{m}(P)=0
if
P
is a globally
m
-polar set.

This generalizes a result in
[
DK12
]
which dealt with the case
m=n
and used the local argument. Here we use a global argument as in the case of compact Kähler manifolds
[
GZ05
, Proposition 3.6]
and
[
Lu13b
, Corollary 3.19]
, thus it provides also an alternative proof to the one in
[
DK12
]
. In fact, we prove a stronger uniform integrability of
(\omega,m)
-sh function with respect to Hessian measures of bounded functions in this class (Theorem
4.1
). In the statement of the theorem we say that a set is
globally

m
-polar if there exists
u\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)
such that

E\subset\{u=-\infty\}.

A weaker result of this sort has been obtained recently by Fang
[
Fa25a
]
where she considered a smaller
m
-capacity in which the supremum was taken over all
v\in PSH(X,\omega)
. This estimate is very useful in the study of weak solutions to the complex Hessian equations
[
KN16
,
Fa25b
]
.

Secondly, we give the characterizations of polar sets of
(\omega,m)
-sh functions. Roughly speaking there are plenty of such globally
m
-polar sets.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E\subset X
be a Borel set. The following statements are equivalent.

(a)

E
is a globally
m
-polar set.

(b)

E
is a locally
m
-polar set.

(c)

The relative extremal
m
-sh function
h_{E}^{*}\equiv 0
.

(d)

cap_{m}^{*}(E)=0
.

(e)

The global extremal
m
-sh function
V_{E}^{*}\equiv+\infty
.

Here the global extremal functions
h_{E}^{*}
and
V_{E}^{*}
are
(\omega,m)
-sh analogues of the extremal functions in global pluripotential theory as defined by Guedj and Zeriahi
[
GZ05
]
.
Thus, we get the same statements as for
\omega
-plurisubharmonic (
\omega
-psh) functions on compact Kähler manifolds
[
GZ17
, Chapter 9]
. The equivalence between (a) and (b) is a version of Josefson’s theorem for
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic function on compact Hermitian manifolds. The case of quasi-psh functions on compact Hermitian manifolds has been proven by Vu
[
Vu19
]
(see also
[
GLu22
]
), while the case of
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions on Kähler manifolds comes from

]
.

2.
Hessian measures for bounded functions

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
and
1\leq m\leq n
be integer.
Since
X
is compact and
\omega
is a smooth Hermitian metric, there exists a constant
{\bf B\,}>0
such that

(2.1)

-{\bf B\,}\omega^{2}\leq dd^{c}\omega\leq{\bf B\,}\omega^{2},\quad-{\bf B\,}\omega^{3}\leq d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega\leq{\bf B\,}\omega^{3}.

This constant is fixed throughout the paper. We sometimes abuse this notation as we may need to multiply
{\bf B\,}
by a multiple of
n
and
m
in estimates, still using the same letter
{\bf B\,}
for such constants.

2.1.
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions

Let
\omega
be a Hermitian metric on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The positive cone
\Gamma_{m}(\omega)
, associated to
\omega
, of real
(1,1)
-forms is defined as follows. A real
(1,1)
-form
\gamma
is said to belong to
\Gamma_{m}(\omega)
if at any point
z\in\Omega
,

\gamma^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}(z)>0\quad\text{for }k=1,...,m.

Equivalently, in the normal coordinate system with respect to
\omega
at
z
, diagonalizing
\gamma=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{i}
, we have
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma_{m}
, where

\Gamma_{m}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{1}(\lambda)>0,...,\sigma_{m}(\lambda)>0\},

and
\sigma_{k}(\lambda)=\sum_{\lambda_{i_{1}<\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}}}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}
for
1\leq k\leq n
is the
k
-elementary symmetric polynomial.

If
u\in C^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
and
\omega_{u}:=\omega+dd^{c}u\in\overline{\Gamma_{m}(\omega)}
, then
u
is called an
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic (sh) function in
\Omega
. In general, an upper semicontinuous function
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,+\infty)
and
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is said to be
(\omega,m)
-sh if it satisfies

(2.2)

\omega_{u}\wedge\gamma_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\gamma_{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\geq 0\quad\text{for every }\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{m-1}\in\Gamma_{m}(\omega)

in the weak sense of currents.
Let us denote
SH_{m}(\Omega,\omega)
the set of all
(\omega,m)
-sh functions in
\Omega
.

Moreover, we can consider a general positivity condition in (
2.2
) as follows. Let
\chi
be a Hermitian
(1,1)
-form in
\bar{\Omega}
. If the function
u
above satisfies

(2.3)

(\chi+dd^{c}u)\wedge\gamma_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\gamma_{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\geq 0\quad\text{for every }\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{m-1}\in\Gamma_{m}(\omega),

instead of (
2.2
), then it is called
m
-subharmonic with respect to
(\chi,\omega)
. The space of all of such functions in
\Omega
is denoted by

(2.4)

SH_{m}(\Omega,\chi,\omega).

In a special case
\chi\equiv 0
, these functions are
m
-subharmonic with respect to the metric
\omega
, or simply they are called
m-\omega
-sh.

On a compact Hermitian manifold
(X,\omega)
we use the following definition.

Definition 2.1

(
[
KN16
]
)
.

An upper semi-continuous function
u:X\to[-\infty,+\infty)
is called
(\omega,m)
-sh if
u\in L^{1}(X)
and
u\in SH_{m}(U,\omega)
for each coordinate patch
U\subset\subset X
.

We denote by
SH_{m}(X,\omega)
or simply by
SH_{m}(\omega)
(if there is no confusion), the set of all
(\omega,m)
-sh functions on
X
.

2.2.
Wedge product of bounded
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions

The complex Hessian measure for bounded
m-\omega
-sh functions has been defined recently in
[
KN25
]
. Using this we can define the wedge product of forms associated to bounded
(\omega,m)
-sh functions on any small coordinate ball
\Omega\subset\subset X
as follows. Since
\Omega
is
is biholomorphic to a small ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then we can find a strictly psh function
\rho
in a neighborhood of
\bar{\Omega}
such that

dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega\quad\text{on }\bar{\Omega}.

Let
u\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. Then,
u+\rho
is a bounded
m-\omega
-sh function in
\Omega
.
Hence, for
1\leq s\leq m-1
, the wedge product

[dd^{c}(u+\rho)]^{s}=dd^{c}(u+\rho)\wedge\cdots\wedge dd^{c}(u+\rho)

is defined inductively which results in a well-defined
(s,s)
-current of order zero
[
KN25
, Lemma 2.3]
. Moreover,

[dd^{c}(u+\rho)]^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-s}\quad\text{and}\quad[dd^{c}(u+\rho)]^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

are positive Radon measures on
\Omega
by
[
KN25
, Theorem 3.3]
. By the choice of
\rho
, the smooth
(1,1)
-form
\tau:=dd^{c}\rho-\omega
is positive. The complex Hessian measure of
u
in
\Omega
is given by

(2.5)

(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}:=\sum_{s=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-s}\binom{m}{s}[dd^{c}(u+\rho)]^{s}\wedge\tau^{m-s}\wedge\omega^{n-m}.

If
u
is smooth function, then this is an honest identity and therefore, the left hand side is a positive measure. If
u
is only bounded, then we can take a sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset SH_{m}(\omega)\cap C^{\infty}(X)
such that
u_{j}\downarrow u
on
X
by
[
KN16
, Lemma 3.20]
. The weak convergence theorem for decreasing sequences in [KN25, Lemma 5.1] allows us to define the Hessian measure as the well-defined positive measure on the right hand side.
By partition of unity we define
H_{m}(u)
on the whole manifold
X
. The same construction can be applied for a tuple of
u_{1},...,u_{m}\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
.

We refer the readers to
[
GN18
]
,
[
GLu25
]
and
[
KN16
,
KN25
]
for many more properties of general
m-\omega
-subharmonic functions. The assumption ”locally conformal Kähler” made in
[
GN18
]
now is removed by the results on the wedge product forms associated to bounded functions.

2.3.
Weak convergence

Since the weak convergence of measures is a local property, we can extend the results in
[
KN25
]
to compact Hermitian manifolds. We state below two important convergence theorems for decreasing and increasing sequences. For simplicity we only state theirs simpler version for two sequences of functions, however, they are valid for the wedge products of forms related to tuples of
k
functions with
1\leq k\leq m
. We refer the readers to
[
KN25
, Section 5]
for those general statements.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\{v_{j}\}_{j\geq 1},\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
be sequences such that
v_{j}\downarrow v
and
u_{j}\downarrow u
in
X
with
v,u\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. Then,
v_{j}H_{m}(u_{j})
converges weakly to
vH_{m}(u)
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\{v_{j}\}_{j\geq 1},\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be locally uniformly bounded sequences of
(\omega,m)
-sh functions in
X
. Assume
v_{j}\uparrow v
,
u_{j}\uparrow u
with
u,v\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
almost everywhere as
j\to\infty
. Then,
v_{j}H_{m}(u_{j})
converges weakly to
vH_{m}(u)
.

2.4.
Weak comparison principles

Thanks to the quasi-continuity of
m-\omega
-subharmonic functions in
[
KN25
, Theorem 4.9]
and equivalence between capacities (Lemma
5.1
below) we can remove the continuity assumption in the weak comparison principle
[
KN16
, Theorem 3.7]
.
Now the statement holds for bounded functions.

Theorem 2.4

(weak comparison principle)
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. Fix
0<\varepsilon<1
and use the following notations

s_{\rm min}(\varepsilon):=\inf_{X}[\varphi-(1-\varepsilon)\psi]\quad\text{ and }\quad U(\varepsilon,t):=\{\varphi<(1-\varepsilon)\psi+s_{\rm min}(\varepsilon)+t\}

for
s>0
. Then, for
0<t<\varepsilon^{3}/16{\bf B\,}
,

(2.6)

\int_{U(\varepsilon,t)}\omega_{(1-\varepsilon)\psi}^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\leq(1+\frac{Ct}{\varepsilon^{m}})\int_{U(\varepsilon,t)}\omega_{\varphi}^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m},

where
C>0
is a uniform constant depending only on
n,m,\omega
.

Applying this comparison principle to the case
\psi=0
,
\varphi\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
for a fixed
0<\varepsilon<1
and
t\in(0,\varepsilon^{3}/16{\bf B\,})
we have

Corollary 2.5
.

If
\varphi\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
, then

\int_{X}H_{m}(\varphi)>0.

An interesting consequence of the convergence theorem
[
KN25
, Lemma 5.1]
is the following inequality.

Corollary 2.6
.

For
\varphi,\psi\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
,

(2.7)

H_{m}(\max\{\varphi,\psi\})\geq{\bf 1}_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}H_{m}(\varphi)+{\bf 1}_{\{\varphi\leq\psi\}}H_{m}(\psi).

Moreover, if
\varphi\leq\psi
, then

(2.8)

{\bf 1}_{\{\varphi=\psi\}}H_{m}(\varphi)\leq{\bf 1}_{\{\varphi=\psi\}}H_{m}(\psi).

Remark 2.7
.

The first inequality was due to Demailly in pluripotential theory for psh functions and it is often called the maximum principle for
(\omega,m)
-sh functions in
[
GLu22
,
GLu25
]
. There is a different way to derive the above weak comparison principle via Corollary
2.6
. We refer the interested readers to Guedj and Lu
[
GLu22
, Theorem 1.5]
for a proof in the case
m=n
which can be adapted easily to our case.

Another useful result in the balayage procedure is as follows.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
B\subset X
be a small coordinate ball in
X
and
\varphi\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. There exists
\widehat{\varphi}\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
such that
\widehat{\varphi}\geq\varphi
and

\widehat{\varphi}\equiv\varphi\quad\text{in }X\setminus B,\quad H_{m}(\widehat{\varphi})=0\text{ in }B.

Proof.

The proof is standard provided the solution to the homogeneous equation of
(\omega,m)
-sh function in small balls
[
GN18
, Theorem 3.15]
.

2.5.
Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality

Let
h
be a smooth real-valued function and let
\phi,\psi
be Borel functions. We will need the following two versions of Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality
[
KN25
, Lemma 2.3]
and
[
KN25
, Lemma 2.4]
in this setting.

The first one is often applied for the case of positive forms
T=\gamma^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+\ell}
, where
\gamma\in\Gamma_{m}(\omega)
and
0\leq s,\ell\leq m-1
and
s+\ell=m-1
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
T
be a positive current of bidegree
(n-2,n-2)
. There exists a uniform constant
A
depending on
\omega,m,n
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}\phi\psi\;dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge T\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{X}|\phi|^{2}\;dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega\wedge T\int_{X}|\psi|^{2}\;\omega^{2}\wedge T.

On the other hand, the second one can be applicable for a
(n-2,n-2)
-form
\gamma^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m-1}
, where
\gamma\in\Gamma_{m}(\omega)
, may not be positive in the integrand of the left hand side.

Lemma 2.10
.

There exists a uniform constant
A
depending on
\omega,n,m
such for every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{m}(\omega)
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}\phi\psi\;dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\gamma^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m-1}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{X}|\phi|^{2}\;dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\gamma^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\times\int_{X}|\psi|^{2}\;\gamma^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}.

2.6.
Uniform constants and integral symbols

The uniform constants

(2.9)

A=A(\omega,m,n)\text{ or }C=C(\omega,m,n)

appearing here and several times below are generic they may be different from line to line. Moreover, in Section
3
and Section
4
the integrals are always considered on the whole manifold
X
, so to simplify the notation we shall write

(2.10)

\int f\eta:=\int_{X}f\eta

where
f
is a Borel function and
\eta
is a smooth
(n,n)
-form.

3.
Basic integral estimates

In this section we extend the integral estimates in
[
KN25
, Section 2.4]
to a compact Hermitian manifold. The basic idea is the same however the computations are slightly different. There is no boundary o a compact manifold so the integration by parts is easier without boundary terms. On the other hand, there will be extra terms as the differential operator
dd^{c}
will act on more terms than the one in the local setting. Because of this we provide all details of the proofs. Thanks to the convergence theorems in Section
2
we may assume that all considered functions are smooth.

Let
-1\leq v\leq u\leq 0
be smooth
(\omega,m)
-sh functions. Let
\phi
be a smooth
(\omega,m)
-sh function such that
-1\leq\phi\leq 0
. Denote

\omega_{\phi}=\omega+dd^{c}\phi,\quad h=u-v.

We consider the integrals containing both potential
\phi
and
v
,

(3.1)

e_{(q,k,s)}:=\int h^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s},

where
q\geq 0
, the integers
0\leq k\leq m
and
0\leq s\leq m-k
. Notice that we are using the convention of uniform constants (
2.9
) and integral symbols (
2.10
) in this section.

Our goal is to bound

e_{(q,m,0)}=\int h^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

by the integrals containing only potential
v

e_{(r,0,i)}=\int h^{r+1}\omega_{v}^{i}\wedge\omega^{n-i},

where
i=0,...,m
and
0\leq r<q
. In other words, we will replace the potential
\phi
by
v
. In the Kähler setting it is relatively simply done via integration by parts as we do not have to deal with the torsion terms
dd^{c}\omega
and
d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega
. In the Hermitian setting these terms make the estimates complicated. Following
[
KN25
, Section 2.4]
, we use variants of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to deal with the torsion terms appearing in integration by parts while replacing
\omega_{\phi}
by
\omega_{v}
.

The crucial estimates to deal with possibly non-positive forms come from
[
KN16
, Lemma 2.3]
. Namely, we have for
1\leq k\leq m-1
,

(3.2)

dd^{c}(\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1})\leq{\bf B\,}\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{2}\omega_{\phi}^{k-\varkappa}\wedge\omega^{n-k+\varkappa}

Moreover, for
0\leq k+s\leq m-1
,

(3.3)

dd^{c}[\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s-1}]\leq{\bf B\,}[\omega_{\phi}+\omega_{v}]^{k+s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s}.

Another useful inequality is as follows.
For
1\leq k\leq m
,

(3.4)

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\omega_{\phi}^{i}\wedge\omega_{v}^{k-i}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq(\omega_{\phi}+\omega_{v})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{k}\omega_{\phi}^{i}\wedge\omega_{v}^{k-i}\wedge\omega^{n-k},

where
C=C(\omega,m,n)
is a uniform constant.
It follows that

\sum_{i=0}^{k}e_{(q,i,k-i)}\leq\int h^{q+1}(\omega_{\phi}+\omega_{v})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{k}e_{(q,i,k-i)}.

We are ready to proceed with the bounds for
e_{(q,k,s)}
. As in
[
KN25
]
we need to consider three cases as follows.

•

Case 1:
k+s=m
,

•

Case 2:
k+s=m-1
,

•

Case 3:
k+s\leq m-2
.

The following lemma is the key technical tool which will be used repeatedly below.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq m-1
. There exists a constant
C=C(\omega,m,n)
such that

(a)

for
0\leq s+k\leq m-1
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int h^{p-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s-1}

\displaystyle\leq Ce_{(p-1,k,s+1)}+C\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{k+s-\varkappa}e_{(p,i,k+s-i-\varkappa)}.

Moreover, if
s=0
, we can take
e_{(p,i,k+s-i-\varkappa)}=e_{(p,i,0)}
for all
i
in the sum.

(b)

for
0\leq s+k\leq m-3
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int h^{p-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s-1}

\displaystyle\leq e_{(p-1,k,s+1)}+C\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{1}\sum_{\varkappa^{\prime}=0}^{1}e_{(p,k-\varkappa,s-\varkappa^{\prime}).}

Proof.

(a) Note first that
0\leq h\leq 1
, and
T:=\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s-1}
,
\omega_{u}\wedge T
are positive forms for
n-s-k-1\geq n-m
. Therefore,

\displaystyle p(p+1)h^{p-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge T

\displaystyle=[dd^{c}h^{p+1}-(p+1)h^{p}dd^{c}h]\wedge T

\displaystyle\leq[dd^{c}h^{p+1}+(p+1)h^{p}\omega_{v}]\wedge T.

Hence,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int h^{p-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-s-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int(dd^{c}h^{p+1}+h^{p}\omega_{v})\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-s-k-1}.

It remains to estimate the product involving the first term in the bracket. By integration by parts and the basic inequality (
3.3
),

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int dd^{c}h^{p+1}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s-1}

\displaystyle=\int h^{p+1}dd^{c}\left[\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\int h^{p+1}(\omega_{\phi}+\omega_{v})^{k+s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s}

\displaystyle+C\int h^{p+1}(\omega_{\phi}+\omega_{v})^{k+s-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s+1}

\displaystyle+C\int h^{p+1}(\omega_{\phi}+\omega_{v})^{k+s-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s+2},

where if
s=0
, then there is no
\omega_{v}
appearing on the right hand side as we can use the basic inequality (
3.2
).
Combining the last two inequalities the proof of the lemma follows.

(b) The proof is very similar but it is easier. We first have (
3.7
). Then, in the middle integral of (
3.8
)
one can express

dd^{c}(\omega^{n-s-k-1}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s})=\eta\wedge\omega^{n-m}

for smooth
(m-s-k,m-s-k)
-forms
\eta
which are the wedge products of
\omega_{\phi}
,
\omega_{v}
, and the torsion terms either
dd^{c}\omega
or
d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega
. Since
\omega_{\phi},\omega_{v}\in\Gamma_{m}(\omega)
and the exponent in
\omega
is
n-m
, we can use the bounds (
2.1
) for the torsion terms. Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\int h^{p+1}\eta\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int h^{p+1}\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s}

\displaystyle+C\int h^{p+1}\omega_{\phi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s+1}

\displaystyle+C\int h^{p+1}\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s+1}

\displaystyle+C\int h^{p+1}\omega_{\phi}^{k-1}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k-s+2}.

The item (b) is proven.
∎

We are ready to begin with the simplest subcase of
Case 1
when
s=0
. This is also a starting point for the induction argument. We are going to show that

(3.9)

e_{(q,m,0)}\leq Ce_{(q-1,m-1,1)}+Ce_{(q-1,m-1,0)}+C\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{2}e_{(q,m-2-\varkappa,0)}.

Equivalently,

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q\geq 2
be integer. Then,

\displaystyle\int(u-v)^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle\leq C\int(u-v)^{q}\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega_{v}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle+C\int(u-v)^{q}\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}

\displaystyle+C\int(u-v)^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m-2}\wedge\omega^{n-m+2}

\displaystyle+C\int(u-v)^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m-3}\wedge\omega^{n-m+3}.

Here by convention
\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\equiv\omega^{n}
for an integer
k\leq 0
.

Proof.

Recall that
h:=u-v\geq 0
. A direct computation gives

(3.10)

\displaystyle dd^{c}[h^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}]

\displaystyle=dd^{c}h^{q+1}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle+dh^{q+1}\wedge d^{c}(\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m})

\displaystyle-d^{c}h^{q+1}\wedge d(\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m})

\displaystyle+h^{q+1}dd^{c}(\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m})

\displaystyle=:T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4}.

By integration by parts,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int h^{q+1}dd^{c}\phi\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle=\int\phi dd^{c}[h^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}]

\displaystyle=\int\phi(T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4}).

Case 1a: Estimate of
T_{1}
.
Compute

\displaystyle dd^{c}h^{q+1}=q(q+1)h^{q-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h+(q+1)h^{q}[\omega_{u}-\omega_{v}].

Since
-1\leq\phi\leq 0
,
\omega_{u}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}
and
dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\geq 0
,
we derive

(3.12)

\phi T_{1}\leq(q+1)h^{q}\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega_{v}\wedge\omega^{n-m}.

Then,

(3.13)

\int\phi T_{1}\leq(q+1)e_{(q-1,m-1,1)}.

Case 1b: Estimate of
T_{4}
.
Using again the basic inequality (
3.2
) we get

(3.14)

dd^{c}(\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m})\leq C\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{2}\omega_{\phi}^{m-1-\varkappa}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1+\varkappa}.

This implies that

(3.15)

\int\phi T_{4}\leq C[e_{(q,m-1,0)}+e_{(q,m-2,0)}+e_{(q,m-3,0)}].

Case 1c: Estimate of
T_{2}
and
T_{3}
.
Since these two terms are bounded in the same way, we give details only for
T_{2}
. Compute

\displaystyle dh^{q+1}\wedge d^{c}(\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m})

\displaystyle=(q+1)(m-1)h^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle+(q+1)(n-m)h^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m-1}.

Next we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in Lemma
2.9
for the first term on the right hand side to obtain

(3.16)

\displaystyle\left|\int\phi h^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int|\phi|h^{q-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}\int|\phi|h^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m-2}\wedge\omega^{n-m+2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int h^{q-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}+\int h^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m-2}\wedge\omega^{n-m+2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[e_{(q-1,m-2,1)}+e_{(q,m-2,0)}+e_{(q,m-3,0)}+e_{(q,m-4,0)}\right]^{2},

where we used the fact
|\phi|\leq 1
in the second inequality, and in the last inequality we invoked Lemma
3.1
-(a) in the special case
(p,k,s)=(q,m-s,0)
. This yields that in the last three terms on the right hand side of that lemma only
\omega_{\phi}
appears. Thus,

(3.17)

\left|\int\phi T_{2}\right|\leq Ce_{(q-1,m-2,1)}+C\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{2}e_{(q,m-2-\varkappa,0)}.

This completed the estimate of
T_{2}
and
T_{3}
.

From the estimates in (
3.12
), (
3.15
) and (
3.17
) for
T_{1},T_{4}
,
T_{2}
and
T_{3}
the proof follows.
∎

\displaystyle dd^{c}(\omega_{\phi}^{m-1-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m})

(3.25)

Lastly, we deal with the new terms

T_{3}

\displaystyle dh^{q+1}\wedge d^{c}(\omega_{\phi}^{m-1-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m})

\displaystyle=(q+1)(m-1-s)h^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-s-2}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle+(q+1)sh^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-s-1}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle+(q+1)(n-m)h^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-s-1}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m-1}

\displaystyle=:T_{2a}+T_{2b}+T_{2c}.

Now we give a detailed steps for estimation of
T_{2a}
and
T_{2b}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\left|\int\phi h^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int h^{q-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}

\displaystyle\times\int h^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int h^{q-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}+e_{(q,m-2-s,s)}\right)^{2}.

3.1
-(a) with
(p,k,s)=(q,m-2-s,s)

(3.27)

\displaystyle\int h^{q-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}

\displaystyle\leq Ce_{(q-1,m-2-s,s+1)}+C\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{m-2-\varkappa}e_{(q,i,m-2-i-\varkappa)}.

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}e_{(q+a,i,m-i)}+C\varepsilon\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1-\varkappa}e_{(q+a+1,i,m-1-i-\varkappa)}.

\displaystyle\varepsilon\sum_{i\geq k+1}^{m-1}e_{(q+a,i,m-i)}

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon(1+b_{k})e_{(q,k,m-k)}+\varepsilon(1+b_{k})\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}e_{(q-1,i,m-i)}

\displaystyle+(\varepsilon b_{k}+C\varepsilon)\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}e_{(q-1-a,i,m-1-i)}+C\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{m-2-\varkappa}e_{(q,i,m-2-i-\varkappa)}.

Notice that
q-1-a=q-1-m+k\geq 1

Combining (
3.28

) and (

(3.31)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon(1+b_{k})e_{(q,k,m-k)}+\varepsilon(1+b_{k})\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}e_{(q-1,i,m-i)}

\displaystyle+[\varepsilon b_{k}+C/\varepsilon+C\varepsilon]\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}e_{(q-1-a,i,m-1-i)}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{m-2-\varkappa}e_{(q,i,m-2-i-\varkappa)}.

\displaystyle e_{(q,k,s)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce_{(q-1,k-1,s+1)}+C\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}e_{(q,i,m-1-i)}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon(1+b_{k})\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}e_{(q-1,i,m-i)}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon(1+b_{k})e_{(q,k,s)}+\varepsilon(1+b_{k})\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}e_{(q-1-a,i,m-1-i)}

\displaystyle e_{(q,k,s)}

\displaystyle\leq(C+1/2)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}e_{(q-1,i,m-i)}+C\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}e_{(q-1-m+k,i,m-1-i)}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{\varkappa=0}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{m-2-\varkappa}e_{(q,i,m-2-i-\varkappa)}.

Next, we consider

.

Lemma 3.4
.

For

Proof.

The basic computation is

(
3.21
′
)

\displaystyle dd^{c}[h^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}

\displaystyle\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}]

\displaystyle=dd^{c}h^{q+1}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}

\displaystyle+dh^{q+1}\wedge d^{c}(\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1})

\displaystyle-d^{c}h^{q+1}\wedge d(\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1})

\displaystyle+h^{q+1}dd^{c}(\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1})

\displaystyle=:T_{1}^{\prime}+T_{2}^{\prime}+T_{3}^{\prime}+T_{4}^{\prime}.

Thus, the exponent of

increases by one.
The estimates of

and

(

′

(

\displaystyle dd^{c}[\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}]

\displaystyle+C(\omega_{\phi}+\omega_{v})^{m-3}\wedge\omega^{n-m+2}

\displaystyle+C(\omega_{\phi}+\omega_{v})^{m-4}\wedge\omega^{n-m+3}.

(

T_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle dh^{q+1}\wedge d^{c}(\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1})

\displaystyle=(q+1)(m-2-s)h^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-3-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}

\displaystyle+(q+1)sh^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}

\displaystyle+(q+1)(n-m)h^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-2-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle=:T_{2a}^{\prime}+T_{2b}^{\prime}+T_{2c}^{\prime}.

\omega

and

The estimation of

(

\displaystyle\left|\int\phi h^{q}dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-3-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+1}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int h^{q-1}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-3-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+2}

\displaystyle\times\int h^{q+1}\omega_{\phi}^{m-3-s}\wedge\omega_{v}^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-m+3}

4.
Uniform integrability

In this section we prove the
L^{1}
-uniform integrability of normalized
(\omega,m)
-sh functions with respect to Hessian measures of bounded
(\omega,m)
-sh functions.
Recall the constant
{\bf B\,}>0
defined in (
2.1
) satisfies

-{\bf B\,}\omega^{2}\leq dd^{c}\omega\leq{\bf B\,}\omega^{2},\quad-{\bf B\,}\omega^{3}\leq d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega\leq{\bf B\,}\omega^{3}.

Note again that we also use the integral symbol convention (
2.10
) in this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
v_{0}\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)
be such that
\sup_{X}v_{0}=0
. Let
0\leq u\leq 1
belong to
SH_{m}(X,\omega)
. There exist uniform constants
C_{m}
and
D_{m}
depending only on
n,m,{\bf B\,}
such that

\int_{X}-v_{0}(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\leq C_{m},

and

\int_{X}-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\leq D_{m}.

Proof.

Thanks to the weak convergence theorems in Section
2
we can assume that all considered functions are smooth.
We prove the two bounds simultaneously by an induction argument. Namely, we will prove that for
0\leq k\leq m
the following two statements hold: there are uniform constants
C_{\ell},D_{\ell}
with
0\leq\ell\leq m
depending only on
n,m
,
{\bf B\,}
such that

(C
k
)

\int-v_{0}\omega_{u}^{\ell}\wedge\omega^{n-\ell}\leq C_{\ell}\quad\text{for }0\leq\ell\leq k,

and

(D
k
)

\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega_{u}^{\ell-1}\wedge\omega^{n-\ell}\leq D_{\ell}\quad\text{for }0\leq\ell\leq k,

where by convention (D
0
) is the same as (D
1
).
This is done as follows:

•

Step 1: (C
0
) and (D
0
) are true,

•

Step 2: (C
k
) and (D
k-1
) imply (D
k
) for
1\leq k\leq m
,

•

Step 3: (C
k
) and (D
k
) imply (C
k+1
) for
1\leq k+1\leq m
.

After proving these steps we get that both (C
m
) and (D
m
) hold. We will verify these steps in (
4.1
), Lemmas
4.2
,
4.3
and
4.4
below.
∎

Let us start with

Step 1.
The statement (C
0
) holds true as we first have the basic bound

(4.1)

\int-v_{0}\omega^{n}\leq C_{0}

from
[
KN16
, Lemma 3.3]
, where
C_{0}>0
is a uniform constant.
Next, we verify the statement (D
0
). Notice that the proof of this one contains the main idea of induction arguments.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists a uniform constant
D_{0}
depending only on
C_{0}
and
{\bf B\,}
such that

(4.2)

J_{0}=\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega^{n-1}\leq D_{0}.

Proof.

Since
dd^{c}u^{2}=2udd^{c}u+2du\wedge d^{c}u
, we have

\displaystyle 2J_{0}

\displaystyle=\int-v_{0}dd^{c}u^{2}\wedge\omega^{n-1}+\int v_{0}udd^{c}u\wedge\omega^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int-v_{0}dd^{c}u^{2}\wedge\omega^{n-1}+\int v_{0}u\omega_{u}\wedge\omega^{n-1}+\int-v_{0}u\omega^{n}.

Since
v_{0}u\leq 0
, it follows that

(4.3)

2J_{0}\leq\int-v_{0}dd^{c}u^{2}\wedge\omega^{n-1}+C_{0}=:J^{\prime}_{0}+C_{0}.

By integration by parts,

(4.4)

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle=\int-u^{2}dd^{c}(v_{0}\omega^{n-1})

\displaystyle=\int-u^{2}dd^{c}v_{0}\wedge\omega^{n-1}+\int-u^{2}v_{0}dd^{c}(\omega^{n-1})

\displaystyle+2\int-u^{2}dv_{0}\wedge d^{c}(\omega^{n-1})

\displaystyle=:J^{\prime}_{0a}+J^{\prime}_{0b}+2J^{\prime}_{0c}.

Here, the factor 2 appeared in
J^{\prime}_{0c}
because
dv_{0}\wedge d^{c}\omega^{n-1}=d\omega^{n-1}\wedge d^{c}v_{0}
.
It is easy to see that

(4.5)

J^{\prime}_{0a}=\int-u^{2}\omega_{v_{0}}\wedge\omega^{n-1}+\int u^{2}\omega^{n}\leq\int u^{2}\omega^{n}\leq C_{0}.

Since
dd^{c}\omega^{n-1}\leq{\bf B\,}\omega^{n}
,

(4.6)

J^{\prime}_{0b}\leq{\bf B\,}C_{0}.

It remains to bound
J^{\prime}_{0c}
. Again, by integration by parts,

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{0c}

\displaystyle=2\int v_{0}udu\wedge d^{c}(\omega^{n-1})+\int v_{0}u^{2}dd^{c}(\omega^{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq 2\int v_{0}udu\wedge d^{c}(\omega^{n-1})+{\bf B\,}C_{0}.

To deal with the remaining integral on the right hand side we use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (Lemma
2.9
) and then the fact that
0\leq u\leq 1
. This gives

\displaystyle\left|\int v_{0}udu\wedge d^{c}(\omega^{n-1})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(An^{2}\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega^{n-1}\times\int-v_{0}\omega^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega^{n-1}+An^{2}\int-v_{0}\omega^{n}.

Therefore,

(4.7)

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{0c}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega^{n-1}+(2A+{\bf B\,})C_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}J_{0}+(2n^{2}A+{\bf B\,})C_{0}.

Combining (
4.3
), (
4.4
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) we get that

J_{0}\leq 2(1+n^{2}A+{\bf B\,})C_{0}.

This finished the proof of the lemma.
∎

Next, we deal with
Step 2.

Let
1\leq k\leq m
. Assume that we have the uniform bounds

(4.8)

\int-v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq C_{\ell},\quad 0\leq\ell\leq k,

and

(4.9)

\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega_{u}^{\ell-2}\wedge\omega^{n-\ell+1}\leq D_{\ell-1},\quad 1\leq\ell\leq k.

Notice that by Step 1, we have these statements for
k=0
. Moreover, if (
4.8
) holds for
k=m
, then (D
m
) is true by
Step 2
, and therefore Theorem
4.1
will follow.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists a uniform constant
D_{k}
depending on
{\bf B\,}
,
C_{\ell}
and
D_{\ell}
with
0\leq\ell\leq k-1
such that

J:=\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq D_{k}.

Proof.

Since
dd^{c}u^{2}=2udd^{c}u+2du\wedge d^{c}u
, we have

\displaystyle 2J

\displaystyle=\int-v_{0}dd^{c}u^{2}\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+\int v_{0}udd^{c}u\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\int-v_{0}dd^{c}u^{2}\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+\int v_{0}u\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle+\int-v_{0}u\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k+1}.

Since
v_{0}\leq 0
and
0\leq u\leq 1
, it follows that

(4.10)

2J\leq\int-v_{0}dd^{c}u^{2}\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+C_{k-1}=:J^{\prime}+C_{k-1}.

By integration by parts,

(4.11)

\displaystyle J^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int-u^{2}dd^{c}(v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k})

\displaystyle=\int-u^{2}dd^{c}v_{0}\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+\int-u^{2}v_{0}dd^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k})

\displaystyle+2\int-u^{2}dv_{0}\wedge d^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k})

\displaystyle=:J^{\prime}_{1}+J^{\prime}_{2}+2J^{\prime}_{3}.

It is easy to see that

(4.12)

J_{1}^{\prime}\leq\int u^{2}\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k+1}\leq C_{k-1},

and

(4.13)

J_{2}^{\prime}\leq{\bf B\,}[C_{k-1}+C_{k-2}+C_{k-3}].

It remains to bound
J_{3}^{\prime}
. By integration by parts,

(4.14)

\displaystyle J_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int v_{0}udu\wedge d^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k})+\int v_{0}u^{2}dd^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k})

\displaystyle=:J_{3a}^{\prime}+J_{3b}^{\prime}.

Clearly,

(4.15)

J_{3b}^{\prime}\leq{\bf B\,}[C_{k-1}+C_{k-2}+C_{k-3}].

Moreover,

\displaystyle d^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k})=(k-1)d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+(n-k)\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}\wedge d^{c}\omega.

Therefore,

(4.16)

\displaystyle J_{3a}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(k-1)\int v_{0}udu\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle+(n-k)\int v_{0}udu\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}

\displaystyle=:J_{4}^{\prime}+J_{5}^{\prime}.

We can use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (Lemmas
2.9
,
2.10
) to derive bounds for
J_{4}^{\prime}
and
J_{5}^{\prime}
. Namely,
since
k\leq m
, Lemma
2.9
gives

\displaystyle|J_{4}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(A\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k+1}\times\int-v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq D_{k-1}+A\,C_{k-1}.

On the other hand, we need to use Lemma
2.10
if
k=m
to have

\displaystyle|J_{5}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(A\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\times\int-v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k+2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+A\int-v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k+2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}J+A\int-v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k+2}.

Hence,

J_{3a}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{4}J+D_{k-1}+A[C_{k-1}+C_{k-2}].

Combining this with (
4.15
) for
J_{3b}^{\prime}
, we obtain

(4.17)

J_{3}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{4}J+D_{k-1}+A[C_{k-1}+C_{k-2}]+{\bf B\,}[C_{k-1}+C_{k-2}+C_{k-3}].

Finally, the proof of the lemma follows from (
4.10
), (
4.11
), (
4.12
), (
4.13
) and (
4.17
). We completed Step 2.
∎

Lastly, we verify
Step 3.
Let
1\leq k+1\leq m
. Assume that both (C
k
) and (D
k
) hold. Then, we need to prove the following

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists a uniform constant
C_{k+1}
depending only on
{\bf B\,}
,
C_{\ell}
and
D_{\ell}
with
0\leq\ell\leq k
such that

I=\int-v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k+1}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}\leq C_{k+1}.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int-v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+\int-v_{0}dd^{c}u\wedge\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}+I^{\prime},

where

I^{\prime}:=\int-v_{0}dd^{c}u\wedge\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}.

Hence, to bound
I
it is enough to show that

(4.18)

I^{\prime}\leq C_{k+1}.

By integration by parts,

I^{\prime}=\int-udd^{c}[v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}].

Compute

\displaystyle dd^{c}[v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}]

\displaystyle=dd^{c}v_{0}\wedge\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}

\displaystyle+v_{0}dd^{c}[\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}]

\displaystyle+2dv_{0}\wedge d^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1})

\displaystyle:=e_{1}+e_{2}+2a_{1}.

The bounds for the elementary terms
e_{1},e_{2}
are easier. Namely,

\displaystyle\int-ue_{1}

\displaystyle=\int-u\omega_{v_{0}}\wedge\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}+\int u\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq\int\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}.

Similarly,

\int-ue_{2}=\int-uv_{0}dd^{c}[\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}]\leq{\bf B\,}[C_{k}+C_{k-1}+C_{k-2}].

Now we consider the term
a_{1}
requiring more advanced argument. By integration by parts

\displaystyle\int-udv_{0}\wedge d^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1})

\displaystyle=\int v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1})

\displaystyle+\int v_{0}udd^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1})

\displaystyle=:I_{1}^{\prime}+I_{2}^{\prime}.

Since
0\leq u\leq 1
and

-dd^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1})\leq{\bf B\,}[\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{k+1}+\omega_{u}^{k-2}\wedge\omega^{n-k+2}]

we have

I_{2}^{\prime}=\int v_{0}udd^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1})\leq{\bf B\,}[C_{k}+C_{k-1}+C_{k-2}].

It remains to bound the first integral
I_{1}^{\prime}
. Compute

(4.19)

\displaystyle d^{c}(\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1})

\displaystyle=kd^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}

\displaystyle+(n-k-1)d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k-2}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (Lemma
2.10
) we get

(4.20)

\displaystyle\left|\int v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(A\int-v_{0}du\wedge d^{c}u\wedge\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\times\int-v_{0}\omega_{u}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq D_{k}+A\,C_{k-1}.

Thus,
Step 3
is verified and the proof of Theorem
4.1
completed.
∎

5.

Recall that for a Borel set

A useful observation is that this capacity is comparable with similar quantity defined locally. In fact,
let us consider a finite covering of

by coordinate balls
\{B_{i}(s)\}_{i\in I}
such that
B_{i}(2s)
are still in holomorphic charts. We fix such a covering in what follows. For a Borel set
E\subset X
, we define another capacity

(5.1)

cap^{\prime}_{m}(E)=\sum_{i\in I}c_{m}(E\cap B_{i}(s),B_{i}(2s)),

where the local capacity is given by

(5.2)

c_{m}(E,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{E}(dd^{c}v)^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}:-1\leq v\leq 0,\;v\text{ is $m-\omega$-sh in $\Omega$}\right\}.

Notice that the class of
m-\omega
-sh functions is obtained by applying the definition in (
2.3
) for
\chi\equiv 0
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The two capacities
cap_{m}
and
cap^{\prime}_{m}
are equivalent. Namely, there exists a uniform constant
A_{0}
depending only on
m,n,\omega
and the covering such that for every Borel set
E\subset X
,

(5.3)

\frac{1}{A_{0}}cap^{\prime}_{m}(E)\leq cap_{m}(E)\leq A_{0}cap^{\prime}_{m}(E).

Proof.

The proof is identical to
[
GN18
, Lemma 3.5]
when we take
\chi=\alpha=\omega
.
∎

Clearly we can see from the definition that the capacity depends on the metric
\omega
, however, it is a fixed metric. Furthermore, if
\alpha
is another Hermitian metric, then we can consider

\displaystyle cap_{\alpha,m}(E)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\int_{E}(\alpha+dd^{c}v)^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}:v\in SH_{m}(X,\alpha,\omega),-1\leq v\leq 0\right\}.

This capacity is also comparable with
cap_{m}^{\prime}
. In other words, by increasing
A_{0}>0
(if necessary) we have

(5.4)

\frac{1}{A_{0}}cap_{\alpha,m}(E)\leq cap_{m}(E)\leq A_{0}cap_{\alpha,m}(E).

In particular, if
cap_{m}(E)=0
if and only if
cap_{\alpha,m}(E)=0
for every Hermitian metric
\alpha
.

Theorem
4.1
allows us to obtain a sharp decay estimate for sublevel sets of
m
-subharmonic functions. This property is very useful and well-known for
\omega
-psh functions and
(\omega,m)
-sh functions in the Kähler setting.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The first inequality is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.1
as
for a function
\phi\in SH_{m}(\omega)
and
0\leq\phi\leq 1
,

\int_{\{v<-t\}}\omega_{\phi}^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\leq\int_{X}\frac{-v}{t}H_{m}(\phi)\leq\frac{A}{t}.

Taking supremum over all such functions
\phi
we get the desired inequality. The second statement follows easily from the first one by letting
t\to\infty
.
∎

The following inequality is a direct consequence of
[
KN16
, Proposition 3.6]
as we enlarged the class of function to take supremum. This in turn generalizes the ones due to Dinew and the first author
[
DK14
,
DK17
]
in the Kähler setting.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
1\leq q<n/m
. Then, there exists a uniform constant
A_{q}>0
such that for every Borel set
E\subset X
,

V_{2n}(E)\leq A_{q}\;cap_{m}^{q}(E).

As a consequence we get a result which has been proven recently by Y. Fang
[
Fa25a
]
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
1\leq q<n/m
. Let
v\in SH_{m}(\omega)
with
\sup_{X}v=0
. There exists a constant
A=A(q)>0
such that

V_{2n}(v<-t)\leq\frac{A}{t^{q}}\quad\text{for }t>0.

Proof.

We have from the above lemma that

V_{2n}(v<-t)\leq cap_{m}(\{v<-t\})^{q}.

Then, the proof follows easily from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
be such that
u_{j}
decreasing to
u\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
. Then, the sequence converges with respect to capacity, i.e., for each
\delta>0
,

\lim_{j\to\infty}cap_{m}(\{u_{j}-u>\delta\})=0.

Proof.

Let
-1\leq\phi\leq 0
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
-1\leq u\leq u_{j}\leq 0
by subtracting and dividing the large constants. Then, we have from Markov’s inequality and Proposition
3.6
that for
q=(n+1)m
,

\displaystyle\int_{\{u_{j}-u>\delta\}}H_{m}(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{q}}\int_{X}(u_{j}-u)^{q}H_{m}(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{q}}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\int_{X}(u_{j}-u)H_{s}(u),

where
H_{s}(u)=(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-s}
. Hence, taking supremum over such
\phi
we derive

cap_{m}(\{u_{j}-u>\delta\})\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{q}}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\int_{X}(u_{j}-u)H_{s}(u),

The conclusion follows from Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem.
∎

We can state now one of the most basic properties of
(\omega,m)
-sh functions. On compact Kähler manifolds it was proved earlier by Lu and Nguyen
[
LN15
]
. It generalizes the one for quasi-psh functions to a very general context.

Proposition 5.5

(quasi-continuity)
.

Let
u\in SH_{m}(\omega)
. For each positive number
\varepsilon>0
there exists an open set
U
with
cap_{m}(U)<\varepsilon
such that
u
restricted to
X\setminus U
is continuous.

Proof.

By subtracting a constant we may assume
u\leq 0
on
X
. It follows from Theorem
1.1
that for
M>0
large enough,

cap_{m}(\{u<-M\})\leq\varepsilon/2.

Denote
v=\max\{u,-M\}
and
U_{0}:=\{u<-M\}
. By
[
KN16
, Lemma 3.2]
there exists a sequence
\{v_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset SH_{m}(\omega)\cap C^{\infty}(X)
that
v_{j}\downarrow v
. Proposition
5.4
implies that this sequence converges with respect to capacity. Thus, for each integer
k\geq 1
, there exists
j(k)
such that the open set
U_{k}=\{v_{j(k)}>v+1/k\}
satisfying

cap_{m}(U_{k})\leq\varepsilon/2^{k+1}.

Then,
U=U_{0}\cup\bigcup_{k\geq 1}U_{k}
has capacity
cap_{m}(U)<\varepsilon
and
v_{j(k)}
converges uniformly to
\widetilde{v}=v
on
X\setminus U
. Hence the restriction of
v
to
X\setminus U
is continuous.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The quasi-continuity can be obtained from the corresponding result in the local setting
[
KN23b
, Theorem 4.9]
and Lemma
5.1
. However, the above proof could be useful if we considered the degenerate background metric as in
[
GLu25
]
.

6.
Characterization of polar sets

In this section we will prove the characterization in Theorem
1.2
. We define the
m
-polarity locally as follows.

Definition 6.1
.

A Borel set
E\subset X
is called locally
m
-polar if for each point
x\in E
, there exists a neighborhood
\Omega\subset X
and a
m
-sh function with respect to
\omega
(or
m-\omega
-sh function) such that
u\neq-\infty
and

E\cap\Omega\subset\{u=-\infty\}
.

The space of
(\omega,m)
-sh functions in a coordinate patch was defined in Section
2.1
. This space was studied in more detail in
[
GN18
, Section 2, Section 9]
.
It follows from
[
KN25
, Proposition 7.7]
that
a Borel set
E\subset X
is a locally
m
-polar if and only if its outer local
m
-capacity (defined in (
5.2
))
c_{m}^{*}(E\cap\Omega,\Omega)=0
on each coordinate patch
\Omega\subset\subset X
.
Hence, we get immediately

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
E\subset X
be a subset. Then,
E
is a locally
m
-polar set
\Leftrightarrow

cap_{m}^{\prime*}(E)=0\Leftrightarrow cap_{m}^{*}(E)=0
.

Here the outer capacity
cap_{m}^{*}
is given by

cap_{m}^{*}(E)=\inf\{cap_{m}(U):E\subset U,\,U\text{ is open in }X\}

and
cap_{m}^{\prime*}(E)
is defined similarly.
Notice that
cap_{m}(E)
is an inner regular, i.e.,

cap_{m}(E)=\sup\{cap_{m}(K):K\subset E,\,K\text{ is compact}\}.

Assume
E
is globally
m
-polar and
u\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)
satisfies

(6.1)

E\subset\{u=-\infty\}.

If we take a strictly psh function
\rho
in a local coordinate ball
B
such that
dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega
in
B
, then
u+\rho
is a
m-\omega
-sh function in
B
and
E\cap B\subset\{u+\rho=-\infty\}
. Hence, a globally
m
-polar set is locally
m
-polar. We will see later that the reverse inclusion is also true. A nice consequence is that there are plenty of unbounded
(\omega,m)
-subharmonic functions on a compact Hermitian manifold.

The global relative
m
-subharmonic extremal function is given by

h_{E}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in SH_{m}(X,\omega),v\leq 0,v\leq-1\text{ on }E\}.

Here we write
h_{E}
instead of
h_{m,E}
as
m
is already fixed. The function
h_{E}
shares several properties with its counterpart in global pluripotential theory on compact Kähler manifolds.
The Choquet lemma shows that there is an increasing sequence of
v_{j}\in SH_{m}(\omega)
,
-1\leq v_{j}\leq 0
converging almost everywhere to the upper semi-continuous regularization
h_{E}^{*}
. Therefore,

h_{E}^{*}\equiv 0
if and only if there exists an increasing sequence of
(\omega,m)
-sh function
\{v_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
such that

(6.2)

v_{j}\leq 0,\quad v_{j}\leq-1\text{ on }E,\quad\int_{X}|v_{j}|\omega^{n}\leq\frac{1}{2^{j}}.

By classical arguments in pluripotential theory
[
Ko05
, Proposition 1.19]
combined with
[
KN25
, Lemma 7.2]
and Proposition
2.8
we have

Proposition 6.3
.

The following properties hold.

(a)

h_{E}^{*}\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)
and
-1\leq h_{E}^{*}\leq 0
.

(b)

h_{E}^{*}=-1
on
E\setminus P
where
P
is a globally
m
-polar set.

(c)

Let
K_{1}\supset K_{2}\cdots
be a sequence of compact sets in
\Omega
and
K=\cap_{j}K_{j}
. Then,
h_{K_{j}}^{*}
increases almost everywhere to
h_{K}^{*}
.

(d)

If
h_{E_{j}}^{*}\equiv 0
and
E=\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}E_{j}
, then
h_{E}^{*}\equiv 0
.

(e)

Let
E\subset X
. Then,
H_{m}(h_{E}^{*})\equiv 0
on the open set
\{h_{E}^{*}<0\}\setminus\overline{E}
.

The following results play the role of the capacity ”formula” in the Kähler setting. This is the analogue of
[
KN25
, Lemma 7.5]
on compact manifolds.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
E\subset X
be a Borel set.

(a)

We have

\int_{X}(-h_{E}^{*})(\omega+dd^{c}h_{E}^{*})^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\leq cap_{m}^{*}(E).

(b)

There exists a uniform constant
A=A(n,m,\omega)
such that

cap_{m}^{*}(E)\leq A\sum_{s=0}^{m}\int_{X}(-h_{E}^{*})(\omega+dd^{c}h_{E}^{*})^{s}\wedge\omega^{n-s}.

Proof.

Let us prove the property (a). We have
-1\leq h_{E}^{*}\leq 0
by Proposition
6.3
-(a). Assume first
E=\bar{E}
is a compact set. It follows Proposition
6.3
-(e) that

\int_{X}(-h_{E}^{*})H_{m}(h_{E}^{*})\leq\int_{E}H_{m}(h_{E}^{*})\leq cap_{m}(E).

Next, assume
E=G
is an open set. Let
\{K_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be an exhaustive sequence of compact sets which increases to
G
. As
G=\cup_{j\geq 1}K_{j}
it is easy to see that
h^{*}_{K_{j}}\downarrow h_{G}=h_{G}^{*}
. The weak convergence theorem for decreasing sequences (Proposition
2.2
) implies that
(-h_{K_{j}}^{*})H_{m}(h_{K_{j}^{*}})
converges weakly to
(-h_{G})H_{m}(h_{G})
on
X
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{X}(-h_{G})H_{m}(h_{G})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}(-h_{K_{j}}^{*})H_{m}(h_{K_{j}^{*}})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}cap_{m}(K_{j})

\displaystyle=cap_{m}(G).

Finally, let
E
be a general Borel set. By the definition of outer capacity, there exists a sequence of open sets
O_{j}\supset E
such that
cap_{m}^{*}(E)=\lim_{j}cap_{m}(O_{j})
. Replacing
O_{j}
by
\cap_{1\leq s\leq j}O_{s}
we may assume that
\{O_{j}\}
is decreasing. Using Choquet’s lemma we can find an increasing sequence of
(\omega,m)
-sh functions
v_{j}\leq 0
such that
v_{j}=-1
on
E
and
\lim_{j}v_{j}=h_{E}^{*}
almost everywhere on
X
. Denote
G_{j}=O_{j}\cap\{v_{j}<-1+1/j\}
. Then,
E\subset G_{j}\subset O_{j}
and

v_{j}-1/j\leq h_{G_{j}}\leq h_{E}.

Therefore,
cap_{m}^{*}(E)=\lim_{j}cap_{m}(G_{j})
and
h_{G_{j}}
increases to
h_{E}^{*}
almost everywhere on
X
. Using the convergence theorem for increasing sequences (Proposition
2.3
) we get
(-h_{G_{j}}H_{m}(h_{G_{j}}))\to(-h_{E}^{*})H_{m}(h_{E}^{*})
weakly on
X
and thus

\displaystyle\int_{X}(-h_{E}^{*})H_{m}(h_{E}^{*})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}(-h_{G_{j}})H_{m}(h_{G_{j}})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}cap_{m}(G_{j})

\displaystyle=cap_{m}^{*}(E).

This completed the proof of (a).

The proof of (b) follows the lines of the one in
[
KN25
, Lemma 7.5]
provided Proposition
3.6
is at our disposal.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

For
E\subset X
a Borel set,
h_{E}^{*}\equiv 0
if and only if
cap_{m}^{*}(E)=0
.

Proof.

If
h_{E}^{*}\equiv 0
, then
cap_{m}^{*}(E)=0
by the second inequality of the above lemma. Conversely, assume
cap_{m}^{*}(E)=0
. We shall prove
h_{E}^{*}\equiv 0
by a contradiction argument. Assume
h_{E}^{*}
is not identically equal to zero. Then
S_{0}:=\inf_{X}h_{E}^{*}<0
. We are going to apply the weak comparison principle (Theorem
2.4
) for
\varphi=h_{E}^{*}
,
\psi=0
and
\varepsilon=1/2
, where
U(t)=\{h^{*}_{E}<S_{0}+t\}
is open and non-empty for
0<t<\min\{|S_{0}|/2,1/32{\bf B\,}\}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\int_{U(t)}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{U(t)}(\omega+dd^{c}h_{E}^{*})^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C}{|S_{0}|}\int_{X}-h_{E}^{*}(\omega+dd^{c}h_{E}^{*})^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle=0.

This leads to a contradiction as
U(t)
is an open set whose volume is positive.
∎

We also consider the globally
m
-subharmonic extremal function modeled on the Siciak-Zaharjuta extremal function on compact manifolds, studied earlier in global pluripotential theory by Guedj and Zeriahi
[
GZ17
, Theorem 9.17]
:

V_{E}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in SH_{m}(X,\omega),v\leq 0\text{ on }E\}.

We have written
V_{E}
instead of
V_{m,E}
as
m
is fixed to simplify the notations. The proof in the general case
1\leq m\leq n
is very similar to the one for
m=n
once the corresponding results are supplied.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
E\subset X
be a Borel set. Let
V_{E}^{*}
be the upper semi-continuous regularization of
V_{E}
.

(a)

E
is a globally
m
-polar set if and only if
\sup_{X}V_{E}^{*}=+\infty
, which is also equivalent to
V_{E}^{*}\equiv+\infty
.

(b)

If
E
is not a globally
m
-polar set, then
V_{E}^{*}\in SH_{m}(\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
and it satisfies
V_{E}^{*}\equiv 0
in the interior of
E
,
(\omega+dd^{c}V_{E}^{*})^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}=0
in
X\setminus\bar{E}
and

\int_{\bar{E}}H_{m}(V_{E}^{*})=\int_{X}H_{m}(V_{E}^{*}).

Proof.

(a) We will show the following implications:

\text{globally $m$-polar }\Rightarrow V_{E}^{*}\equiv+\infty\Rightarrow\sup_{X}V_{E}^{*}=+\infty\Rightarrow\text{globally $m$-polar}.

The middle one is obvious. Let us prove the first one. Assume
E\subset\{u=-\infty\}
and
u\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)
. So,
u+c\in SH_{m}(\omega)
and
u+c\leq 0
on
E
for every constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
. This means
u+c\leq V_{E}
and hence
V_{E}=+\infty
in
X\setminus\{u=-\infty\}
. Since the polar set has Lebesgue measure zero, we have
V_{E}^{*}=+\infty
on
X
. Next, we prove the last implication above. Assume now
\sup_{X}V_{E}^{*}=+\infty
. By Choquet’s lemma there is a sequence
\{v_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset SH_{m}(\omega)
,
v_{j}=0
on
E
increasing almost everywhere to
V_{E}^{*}
. By passing to a subsequence we may assume
\sup_{X}v_{j}\geq 2^{j}
. Denote

u_{j}=v_{j}-\sup_{X}v_{j}.

It follows from
[
KN16
, Lemma 3.3]
and
[
GN18
, Lemma 9.12]
that the sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is relatively compact in
L^{1}(X)
and it satisfies that
\int_{X}|u_{j}|\omega^{n}\leq C_{0}
for a uniform constant
C_{0}
. Set
\varphi_{\ell}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq\ell}u_{j}/2^{j}.
Then, the sequence
\varphi_{\ell}\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)
decreases to
\varphi\leq 0
whose
L^{1}
-norm is uniformly bounded. Thus,
\varphi\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)
and
\varphi(x)=-\infty
for every
x\in E
. In other words,
E
is a globally
m
-polar set. The proof of (a) is finished.

(b) Assume that
E
is not a globally
m
-polar set. Then,
V_{E}^{*}\in SH_{m}(\omega)
and
\sup_{X}V_{E}^{*}=M_{E}<+\infty
. It is also clear that
V_{E}^{*}=0
in the interior of
E
. The last conclusion follows from the lift property (Proposition
2.8
) and the standard balayage argument (see e.g
[
GZ17
, Theorem 9.17]
).
∎

We are ready to show the characterizations in Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
E\subset X
be a Borel set. Then the following are equivalent.

(a)

E
is a globally
m
-polar set.

(b)

E
is a locally
m
-polar set.

(c)

The relative extremal
m
-sh function
h_{E}^{*}\equiv 0
.

(d)

cap_{m}^{*}(E)=0
.

(e)

The global extremal
m
-sh function
V_{E}^{*}\equiv+\infty
.

Proof.

We already know from Lemma
6.6
-(a), (
6.1
), Lemma
5.1
and Corollary
6.5
that

(e)\Leftrightarrow(a)\Rightarrow(b)\Leftrightarrow(d)\Leftrightarrow(c).

Hence, it is enough to show that (d)
\Rightarrow
(a). We will
use the observation (
6.2
) for a sequence of Hermitian metrics. Namely, put
\omega_{j}=\omega/2^{j}
for integers
j\geq 1
. It is easy to see from (
5.4
) that
cap_{\omega_{j},m}^{*}(E)=0
. Applying (c)
\Leftrightarrow
(d) for
\omega_{j}
we get
h_{\omega_{j},E}^{*}=0
, where

h_{\alpha,E}(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in SH_{m}(X,\alpha,\omega):v\leq 0,v\leq-1\text{ on }E\}

for another Hermitian metric
\alpha
on
X
.
Thus, there exists
v_{j}\in SH_{m}(X,\omega_{j},\omega)
(see the definition in (
2.4
)) such that

v_{j}\leq 0,\quad v_{j}\leq-1\text{ on }E,\quad\int_{X}|v_{j}|\omega^{n}\leq\frac{1}{2^{j}}.

Put
u_{\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}v_{j}.
Since
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\omega_{j}\leq\omega
, we have
u_{\ell}\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)
for
\ell\geq 1
. Furthermore, this sequence is deceasing to the limit whose
L^{1}
-norm is uniformly bounded. Hence,
u_{\ell}\downarrow u\in SH_{m}(X,\omega)
and clearly
E\subset\{u=-\infty\}
.
∎

.

.

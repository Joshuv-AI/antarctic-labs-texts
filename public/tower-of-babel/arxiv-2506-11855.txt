Exact requirements for battery-assisted qubit gates
Riccardo Castellano, Vasco Cavina, Martí Perarnau-Llobet, Pavel Sekatski, Vittorio Giovannetti

Abstract
We consider the implementation of a unitary gate on a qubit system S S via a global energy-preserving operation acting on S S and an auxiliary system B B that can be seen as a battery. We derive a simple, asymptotically exact expression for the implementation error as a function of the battery state, which we refer to as the Unitary Defect . Remarkably, this quantity is independent of the specific gate being implemented, highlighting a universal property of the battery itself. We show that minimizing the unitary defect, under given physical constraints on the battery state, is mathematically equivalent to solving a Lagrangian optimization problem, often corresponding to finding the ground state of a one-dimensional quantum system. Using this mapping, we identify optimal battery states that achieve the highest precision under constraints on energy, squared energy, number of levels and Quantum Fisher Information. Overall, our results provide an efficient method for establishing bounds on the physical requirements needed to implement a unitary gate via energy-preserving operations and for determining the corresponding optimal protocols.

Optimizing the precision with limited resources:–

In the following, we analyze in detail how to optimize battery states when only a limited amount of a given resource is available

Tajima et al. 2020

In the context of NEPGs, a resource is a function

Typical resources are functions of the modulus of the battery state coefficients

Conclusions :–

We studied the implementation of non energy-preserving unitary gates powered by external batteries. We proved that, for high-precision gate implementation, a new quantity, dubbed the
Unitary Defect
, is fundamentally linked to precision.
Using the UD, we optimized the initial state of the battery in various scenarios, including maximizing the precision for given initial battery resources and for batteries with a fixed number of levels.
Our approach yields three new bounds that relate implementation precision to the battery’s initial average energy, its average squared energy, and the number of accessible energy levels. These results improve upon previous bounds
Chiribella et al. 2021
;
Åberg 2014b
and establish a general framework for efficiently optimizing the battery state.
Our results find natural implications in quantum thermodynamics and computing. In the active topic of quantum batteries
Campaioli et al. 2024
;
Yang et al. 2023
, our work places the unitary defect as a complementary figure of merit to the ergotropy –a measure of the extractable work from a quantum state
Allahverdyan et al. 2004
. Likewise, the unitary defect becomes also a relevant quantity to assess the thermodynamic cost of quantum gates
Chiribella et al. 2021
;
Auffèves 2022
.

These findings pave the way to further research. A promising direction is the generalization to multi-qubit systems, which presents substantial challenges due to the presence of spectral degeneracies. Another direction is to extend the framework to experimentally relevant models, such as a cavity-mode battery described by a quantum harmonic oscillator. In this context, the Jaynes–Cummings Hamiltonian emerges as a promising energy-conserving interaction model.

As observed in the main text, by linearity it follows that minimization of
\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V})
for fixed choices of
V_{S}
and
U_{SB}
, can be restricted to only pure states of the battery.
In particular, such minimum is attained by the eigenstate
|\bar{\beta}\rangle_{B}
of
M_{B}
with maximum eigenvalue
\lambda_{\max}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\min_{\beta_{B}}\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V})=\min_{|\beta\rangle_{B}\in{\cal H}_{B}}\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{|\beta\rangle},\mathcal{V})\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\bar{\beta}},\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{|\bar{\beta}\rangle},\mathcal{V})=1-\lambda_{\max},\qquad M_{B}|\bar{\beta}\rangle_{B}=\lambda_{\max}|\bar{\beta}\rangle_{B}.

(28)

We conclude recalling that for the special case where
S
is a qubit and
B
is a harmonic oscillator
the most general unitary interaction
U_{SB}
that is energy preserving, i.e.
[U_{SB},H_{S}+H_{B}]=0
can be written as

{U}_{SB}=\underset{S}{\outerproduct{0}{0}}\otimes\underset{B}{\outerproduct{0}{0}}+\underset{n>0}{\overset{\infty}{\bigoplus}}\;{U}^{(n)}_{SB},\qquad\qquad{U}^{(n)}_{SB}:=\sum_{i,j=0,1}U^{(n)}_{ij}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}\otimes\underset{B}{\outerproduct{n-i}{n-j}},

(29)

where for each
n>0
the coefficients
U^{(n)}_{ij}
define
2\times 2
unitary matrices, and
\{|i\rangle_{S}\}
and
\{|n\rangle_{B}\}
are the energy eigenbasis of
S
and
B
defined in
Eq. (
4
). Under these conditions Eq. (
26

\displaystyle Q_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(V_{00}\;\underset{B}{\outerproduct{0}{0}}+\sum_{n>0}\sum_{j,i=0,1}U^{(n)}_{ij}\;V^{*}_{ij}\;\underset{B}{\outerproduct{n-i}{n-j}}\Big).

(30)

A.1
Equivalence of worst-case infidelity and Choi infidelity

In much of the literature on the implementation of asymmetric unitary gates, the results are stated in terms of the worst-case infidelity defined as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 1-\underset{\ket{\Psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{XS}}{\min}F((\mathcal{I}_{X}\otimes\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta})(\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}),(\mathcal{I}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{V})(\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}))

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\underset{\ket{\Psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{XS}}{\min}\braket{\Psi|V_{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta}({\outerproduct{\Psi}{\Psi}})V_{S}|\Psi},

For finite dimensional systems this quantity
is equivalent to the Choi infidelity
\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V})
.
More specifically we observe that

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathbf{\Phi}
be an arbitrary CPTP channel defined on
an Hilbert space of dimension
d
and
\mathcal{V}:=V\cdots V^{{\dagger}}
a unitary mapping on the same space,
then

\frac{\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})}{d}\leq\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})\leq\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V}).

(32)

Proof.

The inequality
\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})\leq\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})
is trivial since the Choi infidelity is obtained by choosing a particular value of
\ket{\Psi}
- namely, the totally entangled state
\ket{\Psi_{\max}}
that appears in the definition of the Choi state - in Eq. (
31
). To prove the other inequality, consider

\ket{\chi}
the worst-case state in Eq. (
31
), i.e.,

\displaystyle 1-\braket{\chi|V_{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\Phi}({\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi}})V_{S}|\chi}=\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V}).

(33)

By Schmidt decomposition it can be written as

\ket{\chi}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}\sqrt{P_{\ell}}\ket{\ell}_{X}\otimes\ket{\ell}_{S},

(34)

with
\{P_{\ell}\}
probabilities, and with
\{\ket{\ell}_{X}\}_{\ell}
and
\{\ket{\ell}_{S}\}_{\ell}

properly choosing the local bases for
X
and
S
.
To continue to proof, we have to add yet another auxiliary space
R
and introduce channel

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\cdots):=F_{S}^{(0)}(\cdots)F_{S}^{(0)\dagger}\otimes\underset{R}{\outerproduct{0}{0}}+F_{S}^{(1)}(\cdots)F_{S}^{(1)\dagger}\otimes\underset{R}{\outerproduct{1}{1}}\;,

(35)

with
|0\rangle_{R}
,
|1\rangle_{R}
orthonormal vectors of
R
and with

\displaystyle F_{S}^{(0)}:=\sqrt{q_{0}\;{\rho}_{S}}\;,\quad\quad F_{S}^{(1)}:=\sqrt{{I}_{S}-F_{S}^{(0){\dagger}}F_{S}^{(0)}}=\sqrt{{I}_{S}-q_{0}\rho_{S}}\;,

(36)

where

q_{0}:=(\,\underset{j}{\max}P_{j})^{-1}\geq 1
and
\rho_{S}:={\sum}_{\ell=0}^{d-1}{P_{\ell}}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{\ell}{\ell}}
is the reduced density matrix w.r.t.
S
of the state
\ket{\chi}
.
Notice that the channel acts trivially on
X
and that the chosen value of
q_{0}
is the largest such that
{I}_{S}-F_{S}^{(0){\dagger}}F_{S}^{(0)}\geq 0
, and that

1\geq{q_{0}}/{d}
.
Identifying the maximally entangled
\ket{\Psi_{\max}}
which enters in Eq. (
23
) with

\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\underset{\ell=0}{\overset{d-1}{\sum}}\ket{\ell}_{X}\otimes\ket{\ell}_{S}
, we observe that

\mathcal{S}({\outerproduct{\Psi_{\max}}{\Psi_{\max}}})=\frac{q_{0}}{d}{\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi}}\otimes\underset{R}{\outerproduct{0}{0}}+(1-\frac{q_{0}}{d}){\outerproduct{\chi'}{\chi'}}\otimes\underset{R}{\outerproduct{1}{1}},

(37)

for some pure state
\ket{\chi^{\prime}}
of
XS
.
We use now the monotonicity of the fidelity under quantum channels and the commutativity of
\mathcal{S}
with
\mathbf{\Phi}
and
{\cal V}
to prove the following chain of relations

\displaystyle F(\sigma_{\mathbf{\Phi}},\sigma_{\mathcal{V}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F\left(\mathcal{S}(\sigma_{\mathbf{\Phi}}),\mathcal{S}(\sigma_{\mathcal{V}})\right)=F\left(\mathbf{\Phi}\circ\mathcal{S}(\outerproduct{\Psi_{\max}}{\Psi_{\max}})),\mathcal{V}\circ{\cal S}(\outerproduct{\Psi_{\max}}{\Psi_{\max}})\right)

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{q_{0}}{d}\mathbf{\Phi}(\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi})\otimes\underset{R}{\outerproduct{0}{0}}+(1-\frac{q_{0}}{d})\mathbf{\Phi}({\outerproduct{\chi'}{\chi'}})\otimes\underset{R}{\outerproduct{1}{1}},\frac{q_{0}}{d}{{\cal V}(\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi})}\otimes\underset{R}{\outerproduct{0}{0}}+(1-\frac{q_{0}}{d}){{\cal V}(\outerproduct{\chi'}{\chi'})}\otimes\underset{R}{\outerproduct{1}{1}})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q_{0}}{d}\sqrt{\braket{\chi|V_{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\Phi}({\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi}})V_{S}|\chi}}+(1-\frac{q_{0}}{d})\sqrt{\braket{\chi^{\prime}|V_{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\Phi}({\outerproduct{\chi'}{\chi'}})V_{S}|\chi^{\prime}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{q_{0}}{d}{\braket{\chi|V_{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\Phi}({\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi}})V_{S}|\chi}}+(1-\frac{q_{0}}{d}){\braket{\chi^{\prime}|V_{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\Phi}({\outerproduct{\chi'}{\chi'}})V_{S}|\chi^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{q_{0}}{d}{\braket{\chi|V_{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\Phi}({\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi}})V_{S}|\chi}}+(1-\frac{q_{0}}{d})=1-\frac{q_{0}}{d}\left(1-{\braket{\chi|V_{S}^{\dagger}\mathbf{\Phi}({\outerproduct{\chi}{\chi}})V_{S}|\chi}}\right)=1-\frac{q_{0}}{d}\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})\;,

where
\sigma_{\mathbf{\Phi}}
and
\sigma_{\mathcal{V}}
are the Choi states of the map
\mathbf{\Phi}

and
{\cal V}
and where we invoked the Cauchy-Swartz inequality.
Hence, we conclude

\displaystyle\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})=

\displaystyle 1-F(\sigma_{\mathbf{\Phi}},\sigma_{\mathcal{V}})\geq\frac{q_{0}}{d}\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})\geq\frac{\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})}{d},

that is the desired result.
∎

The bound
\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})\leq\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})
is easily seen to be saturable. For instance consider
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{I}
and
\mathbf{\Phi}(\cdots)=\rho_{\star}\mbox{Tr}[\cdots]
with some fixed output state
\rho_{\star}
(a trash-and-replace channel).
To illustrate the tightness of the bound
\frac{\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})}{d}\leq\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})
we consider the following example with
\mathbf{\Phi}=\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{V}
associated with the unitary transformation

{V}_{S}=e^{i\varphi}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{0}{0}}+e^{-i\varphi}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{1}{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{i}}.

(39)

In this case one finds

F(\sigma_{\mathbf{\Phi}},\sigma_{\mathbf{\mathcal{V}}})=\left|\frac{1}{d}(\bra{00}e^{i\varphi}\ket{00}+\bra{11}e^{-i\varphi}\ket{11}+\sum_{i\geq 2}^{d-1}\braket{ii})\right|^{2}=\frac{(d-2(1-\cos(\varphi)))^{2}}{d^{2}},

(40)

and
\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})=1-F(\sigma_{\mathbf{\Phi}},\sigma_{\mathbf{\mathcal{V}}})=4\frac{1-\cos(\varphi)}{d}\left(1-\frac{1-\cos(\varphi)}{d}\right)
. In turn computing
\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})
correspond to the optimal discrimination task between the unitary channels
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{V}
. The minimal achievable fidelity
F_{wc}
for this task is well know
Aharonov et al. 2002
. It does not require entanglement with the ancillary and is attained by probing the channels with the state
\ket{+}_{S}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}+\ket{1})
, and gives

F_{wc}=\left|\frac{\bra{0}e^{i\varphi}\ket{0}+\bra{1}e^{-i\varphi}\ket{1}}{2}\right|^{2}=\cos^{2}(\varphi)\quad\implies\quad\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})=\sin^{2}\left(\varphi\right).

(41)

For the ratio of infidelities we found an example of channels with

\frac{\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})}{\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})}=\frac{\sin^{2}(\varphi)}{4\frac{1-\cos(\varphi)}{d}\left(1-\frac{1-\cos(\varphi)}{d}\right)}=\frac{d^{2}(\cos(\phi)+1)}{4(d+\cos(\phi)-1)}\to\frac{d}{2}

(42)

in the limit
\varphi\to 0
. We have thus shown that the dependence of the bound in the dimension is correct up to at most a constant factor of
\frac{1}{2}
. In other words, for any dimension
d>2
and any
\epsilon>0
we have found two channels with
\frac{\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})}{\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})}=(\frac{d}{2}-\epsilon)
, while for
d=2
we find
\frac{\epsilon_{wc}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})}{\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})}=1
.

Calculation of the Choi infidelity

We start by producing a Kraus set for the CPTP map
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta}
associated with the global unitary evolution in Eq. (
5

|\beta\rangle_{B}=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\beta_{n}|n\rangle_{B}
, with
|n\rangle_{B}
being the energy eigenstates of

\displaystyle K_{S}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{}_{B}\langle}n|U_{SB}|\beta\rangle_{B}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\beta_{m}\;{{}_{B}\langle}n|U_{SB}|m\rangle_{B}\;.

(43)

For

{K}_{S}^{(0)}=\beta_{0}\left(\underset{S}{\outerproduct{0}{0}}+V_{11}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{1}{1}}\right)+V_{10}\beta_{1}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{1}{0}}.

(44)

For
n\geq 1

\displaystyle K_{S}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\beta_{m}\sum_{i,j=0,1}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\underset{B}{\langle}n\underset{B}{\outerproduct{l-i}{l-j}}m\underset{B}{\rangle}\;\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}V_{ij}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i,j=0,1}\beta_{m}\delta_{n,l-i}\delta_{m,l-j}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}V_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1-j}^{\infty}\sum_{i,j=0,1}\beta_{m}\delta_{n+i,m+j}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}V_{ij}=\sum_{i,j=0,1}\beta_{n+i-j}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}V_{ij}=\beta_{n}V_{S}+\sum_{i,j=0,1}(\beta_{n+i-j}-\beta_{n})\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}V_{ij},

where in the last identity we sum and subtract the term
\beta_{n}V_{S}
.

To compute the Choi infidelity (
2
) we have to
multiply all the Kraus operators found above by
V^{{\dagger}}_{S}
. We obtain

\displaystyle V^{{\dagger}}_{S}K_{S}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}\;{I}_{S}+\sum_{k,i,j}V^{\dagger}_{ik}V_{kj}(\beta_{n+k-j}-\beta_{n})\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}=:\beta_{n}\;{I}_{S}+J_{S}^{(n)},

where the second to last equality is a definition of the operator
J_{S}^{(n)}
, which implies

\absolutevalue{\mbox{Tr}[V^{\dag}_{S}K_S^{(n)}]}^{2}=|\beta_{n}|^{2}|\mbox{Tr}[{I}_{S}]|^{2}+4\real[\mbox{Tr}[\beta_{n}^{*}J_{S}^{(n)}]]+\absolutevalue{\mbox{Tr}[J_S^{(n)}]}^{2}.

(46)

Direct computation returns
\mbox{Tr}[J_{S}^{(n)}]=\absolutevalue{V_{01}}^{2}(\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n-1}-2\beta_{n})
, plugging in Eq. (
2

\underset{n>0}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\absolutevalue{\beta_{n}}^{2}=1-\absolutevalue{\beta_{0}}^{2}
we get

\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V})=|V_{01}|^{2}\sum_{n>0}\big[-\real[\beta_{n}^{*}(\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n-1}-2\beta_{n})]-\frac{|V_{01}|^{2}}{4}|\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n-1}-2\beta_{n}|^{2}\big]+\absolutevalue{\beta_{0}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|\mbox{Tr}[V_{S}^{\dagger}K_{S}^{(0)}]|^{2}.

(47)

To simplify further, we use summation by parts:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}[g_{n+1}-g_{n}]=-f_{1}g_{1}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n+1}[f_{n+1}-f_{n}],

(48)

with
f_{n}=\beta^{*}_{n}
and
g_{n}=\beta_{n}-\beta_{n-1}
.
Thus we obtain

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta^{*}_{n}[\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n-1}-2\beta_{n}]=-\beta^{*}_{1}(\beta_{1}-\beta_{0})-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[\beta_{n+1}-\beta_{n}][\beta_{n+1}^{*}-\beta_{n}^{*}].

(49)

If we replace the equation above inside Eq. (
47

(
6
) in the main text, after defining

\displaystyle\Delta(\beta_{0},\beta_{1},V_{S})

\displaystyle=|\beta_{0}|^{2}+|V_{01}|^{2}|\beta_{1}|^{2}-|V_{01}|^{2}\real[\beta_{0}\beta_{1}^{*}]-\frac{1}{4}\big|\mbox{Tr}[V_{S}^{\dagger}K_{S}^{(0)}]\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\beta_{0}|^{2}+|V_{01}|^{2}(|\beta_{1}|^{2}-\real[\beta_{0}\beta_{1}^{*}])-\frac{1}{4}\big|\beta_{0}(V_{00}^{*}+|V_{11}|^{2})+\beta_{1}|V_{01}|^{2}\big|^{2}.

(50)

C.1
Analysis of the ground state of the battery

The aim of this section is to prove that to obtain an optimal state of the battery it is necessary to have a ground state population that is sufficiently small.
In terms of the function
\psi

10

\psi(0)=0

Lemma 2

Let
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta}
be a CPTP map acting on a
d

{\cal V}

(51)

\epsilon^{(k)}_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V})

\epsilon^{(k)}_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V}):=\frac{1}{d}\left(\mbox{Tr}[K_{S}^{(k)\dagger}K_{S}^{(k)}]-\frac{\absolutevalue{\mbox{Tr}[V_S^\dag K_{S}^{(k)}]}^{2}}{d}\right).

Because of the normalization condition
\underset{k}{\sum}K_{S}^{(k)\dagger}K_{S}^{(k)}=I_{S}
we have:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V})=1-\frac{1}{d^{2}}\underset{k}{\sum}\absolutevalue{\mbox{Tr}[V_S^\dag K_{S}^{(k)}]}^{2}=\frac{1}{d}\underset{k}{\sum}\left(\mbox{Tr}[K_{S}^{(k)\dagger}K_{S}^{(k)}]-\frac{\absolutevalue{\mbox{Tr}[V_S^\dag K_{S}^{(k)}]}^{2}}{d}\right),

∎

C.2
On the optimality of the unitary interaction between S and B

The aim of this subsection is to justify the system-battery interaction chosen in Eq. (
5

Recall that in the case of the qubit-battery model of Eq. (
4
),
the most general unitary interaction
U_{SB}
that is energy preserving, i.e.
[U_{SB},H_{S}+H_{B}]=0

is the one reported in Eq. (

states
|\beta^{(\delta)}\rangle_{B}
of the ansatz (
10

The Kraus operators of the associated map

\mathbf{\Phi_{|\beta^{(\delta)}\rangle}}
are (for

from which, after some manipulations, we obtain

\displaystyle K_{S}^{(n,\delta)}

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}^{(\delta)}U_{S}^{(n)}+\underset{i,j=0,1}{\sum}(\beta_{n+i-j}^{(\delta)}U^{(n)}_{ij}-\beta_{n}^{(\delta)}U^{(n+i)}_{ij})\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}^{(\delta)}U_{S}^{(n)}+\underset{i,j=0,1}{\sum}\big[(\beta_{n+i-j}^{(\delta)}-\beta_{n}^{(\delta)})U^{(n)}_{ij}-\beta_{n}^{(\delta)}(U_{ij}^{(n+i)}-U^{(n)}_{ij})\big]\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}.

(86)

Unitary channels are extreme points of the convex set of CPTP maps, thus any Kraus representation of a unitary channel is made of operators proportional to the unitary itself, so we want to choose
U^{(n)}
such that the convergence

.
To make the rest of the terms proportional to
V_{S}

to be the same and equal to
V_{S}
.
In this way, the term

(the condition we were looking for) while the last term in the sum nullifies.

We now come to the rigorous statement. Suppose that
\absolutevalue{U_{ij}^{(n)}}=\absolutevalue{U_{ij}}\;\;\forall\;n

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&U_{00}^{(n)}=\cos\bar{\theta}e^{i\alpha_{n}}\;,\qquad\quad\quad U_{01}^{(n)}=\sin\bar{\theta}e^{i(\alpha_{n}+\gamma_{n})}\;,\\
&U_{10}^{(n)}=\sin\bar{\theta}e^{i(\alpha_{n}+\gamma_{n}+\delta_{n})}\;,\quad U_{11}^{(n)}=-\cos\bar{\theta}e^{i(\alpha_{n}+\delta_{n})}\;,\end{cases}

for some arbitrary phases
\alpha_{n},\delta_{n},\gamma_{n}

\bar{\theta}

Proposition 5
.

Suppose that
\beta_{0}=0
and consider the parametrizations of Eq.(
70

V_{S}
and Eq.(
87

\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V}_{S})\geq\sin^{2}(\bar{\theta}-{\theta})+\frac{1}{4}\Big[\sin(2 \bar{\theta})\sin(2\theta)Q_{\beta}^{(1)}+\sin^{2}\bar{\theta}\sin^{2}\theta\;Q_{\beta}^{(2)}\Big]\;,

Before presenting the proof, we underline the consequences of Eq. (

uniform over

Proof.

We start with the observation that we can, without loss of generality, always choose each

the corresponding Choi error is then given by

where

E.1
Minimizing the Choi infidelity at fixed

We solve explicitly the otimizaion problem when the resource considered is the average squared energy,

.
Thus we start from Eq.(

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\langle E^{2}\rangle}[\psi(x)]=\delta^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential x\,\psi(x)\big(x^{2}a-\frac{\differential^{2}}{\differential x^{2}}\big)\psi^{*}(x),

\displaystyle\psi_{\langle E^{2}\rangle}=\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{3\omega^{2}}{2\langle E^{2}\rangle}\right)^{1/4}\psi_{1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{3\omega^{2}}{2\langle E^{2}\rangle}}x\right),

\displaystyle UD_{min}(\langle E^{2}\rangle)=\frac{9}{4}\frac{\omega^{2}}{\langle E^{2}\rangle}.

(113)

For comparison, for the sine-shaped state in Eq.(

(114)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\langle E\rangle}[\psi(x)]=\delta^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential x\,\psi(x)\left(xa-\frac{\differential^{2}}{\differential x^{2}}\right)\psi^{*}(x),

(115)

where this time
a:=\frac{\lambda\omega}{\delta^{3}}
.
We have to solve the Schrödinger equation:

-\psi^{\prime\prime}(x)+ax\psi=E\psi.

(116)

for the lowest possible
E
and with boundary condition
\psi(0)=0
.
It’s convenient to introduce a new function
\phi
and a new variable
y
such that
\psi_{a}(x):=\phi(a^{1/3}x-\frac{E}{a^{2/3}}):=\phi(y)
.
In terms of
\phi
the equation (
116
) becomes

\phi^{\prime\prime}(y)=y\phi(y).

(117)

The solutions of this equation are well known and are given by linear combinations of Airy functions
\phi(y)=CAi(y)+C^{\prime}Bi(y)
, however,
the normalization condition imposes
C^{\prime}=0
.
The smallest value of
Ea^{-2/3}
for which
\psi_{a}(0)=CAi(-\frac{E}{a^{2/3}})=0
, is satisfied is known to be
x_{0}\approx 2.338
, thus, let us set
E_{g}:=x_{0}a^{2/3}
and

\psi_{a}(x):=\phi(a^{1/3}x-E_{g}a^{-2/3})=\sqrt{\overline{C}a^{1/3}}Ai\left(xa^{1/3}-x_{0}\right),

(118)

where we defined
\overline{C}:=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{Ai\left(x-x_{0}\right)}^{2}\differential x\right]^{-1}
.

We complete the calculation computing
\langle E\rangle(a):=\omega\int_{0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\psi_{a}(x)}^{2}x\differential x
and
UD(\psi_{a})
:

\displaystyle\langle E\rangle(a)=\omega\overline{C}C_{1}a^{-1/3}\quad C_{1}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}x\absolutevalue{Ai\left(x-x_{0}\right)}^{2}\differential x,\,

(119)

\displaystyle UD(\psi_{a})=a^{2/3}\overline{C}C_{2}\quad C_{2}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{\partial_{x}Ai\left(x-x_{0}\right)}^{2}\differential x,\,

(120)

yielding

\displaystyle\psi_{\langle E\rangle}(x)=C_{E}Ai\left(x\frac{\omega\overline{C}C_{1}}{\langle E\rangle}-x_{0}\right),

(121)

\displaystyle UD_{min}(\langle E\rangle)=\overline{C}^{3}C_{1}^{2}C_{2}\frac{\omega^{2}}{\langle E\rangle^{2}}\approx 1.888\frac{\omega^{2}}{\langle E\rangle^{2}}.

(122)

For completeness, we report the approximated numerical values of the integrals:

\overline{C}\approx 2.033;\;\;C_{1}\approx 0.766;\;\;C_{2}\approx 0.383
.

For comparison, for the sine-shaped state in (
7
) we have

UD(\langle E\rangle)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\frac{\omega^{2}}{\langle E\rangle^{2}}\approx 2.466\frac{\omega^{2}}{\langle E\rangle^{2}}.

(123)

E.3
Minimizing the Choi infidelity at fixed QFI

The Quantum Fisher information plays an important role in different fields of quantum information science, including quantum metrology, quantum speed limits, quantum thermodynamics and recently resource theory of asymmetry
Giovannetti Vittorio 2011
;
Giovannetti et al. 2003
;
Deffner and Campbell 2017
;
Scandi et al. 2024
;
Tajima et al. 2020
;
Kudo and Tajima 2023
. It can be defined in great generality, whenever we have a continuous parametrization of density matrices
\rho_{t}

Paris 2009
;
Šafránek 2017
as

\displaystyle QFI(\rho_{t})=2\partial_{t^{\prime}}^{2}D_{B}^{2}(\rho_{t^{\prime}},\rho_{t})_{|t^{\prime}=t}

(124)

where
D_{B}(\rho,\sigma):=\sqrt{2(1-\sqrt{F(\rho,\sigma)})}
is the Bures metric.
Here we only consider the case of a Hamiltonian parametrization, i.e.
\rho_{t}:=\exp[-iHt]\rho\exp[iHt]
, where the quantum Fisher information with respect to parameter
t
, only depends on
\rho
and
H
,

\mathcal{F}(\rho,H):=QFI(\exp[-iHt]\rho\exp[iHt]).

(125)

The functional
\mathcal{F}(\rho,H)
can be expressed in different ways, we recall that it
equals 4 times the convex roof of the variance
Yu 2013
:

\mathcal{F}(\rho,H)=4\underset{\{p_{j},\ket{j}\}_{j}\in\mathbb{E}[\rho]}{\min}\sum_{j}p_{j}Var[\ket{j},H]=\mbox{Tr}[H^{2}\rho]-\underset{\{p_{j},\ket{j}\}_{j}\in\mathbb{E}[\rho]}{\max}\sum_{j}p_{j}\bra{j}H\ket{j}^{2},

(126)

where
\mathbb{E}[\rho]:=\bigg\{\{p_{j},\ket{j}\}_{j}:\underset{j}{\sum}p_{j}\outerproduct{j}{j}=\rho\bigg\}
is the set of all ensemble representations of
\rho
.

In
Tajima et al. 2020
the minimum amount of QFI the battery state must possess to perform an
\epsilon_{wc}
precise unitary is found. Their construction holds for a generic Hamiltonian
H_{S}
and is extremely complex, the battery system is divided into 5 different sub-systems and the proof involves many lemmas and non-trivial calculations. With our approach, finding the optimal state is equivalent to solving a 1D Harmonic oscillator. In fact for pure states the QFI equals 4 times the variance, hence we have

R_{\mathcal{F}}[\psi(x),x]=4\omega^{2}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{2}\absolutevalue{\psi(x)}^{2}-\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}x\absolutevalue{\psi(x)}^{2}\right)^{2}\right].

(127)

If we relax the problem and allow the minimization over every regular
\psi
defined over the entire real line, we can assume without loss of generality that
\int_{0}^{\infty}x|{\psi(x)}|^{2}=0
, making the QFI functional proportional the
\langle E^{2}\rangle
, hence the full action to minimize reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}[\psi(x)]=\delta^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\differential x\,\psi(x)\left(x^{2}a-\frac{\differential^{2}}{\differential x^{2}}\right)\psi^{*}(x).

(128)

It is then immediate that the optimal solutions are ground states of the harmonic oscillator i.e.
\psi_{\mathcal{F}}(x):=C_{\mathcal{F}}\exp\left[-\frac{2x^{2}\omega^{2}}{\mathcal{F}}\right]
yielding a lower bound to the optimal unitary defect

UD_{min}(\mathcal{F})\geq\frac{\omega^{2}}{\mathcal{F}}.

(129)

As we show at the end of the section this “unconstrained” optimum can be asymptotically reached just by shifting to the right the wave function of the ground state.

However, since the QFI is convex in the state we cannot a priori rule out the existence of a mixed state with lower QFI and same unitary defect.
We now prove that such mixed states do not exist.
As it was proved for the first time in
Yu 2013
, for any state
\beta
exist and ensemble
\{\ket{\beta(i)}\}_{i}
of pure states such that

\displaystyle\beta=\underset{i}{\sum}p_{i}\outerproduct{\beta(i)}{\beta(i)},

(130)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\beta,H_{B})=\underset{i}{\sum}p_{i}\mathcal{F}(\ket{\beta(i)},H_{B}).

(131)

Then we use that the functional
\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V})
is linear in
\beta
, hence we have

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V})}{\absolutevalue{V_{01}}^{2}}=\underset{i}{\sum}p_{i}\frac{\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\ket{\beta(i)}},\mathcal{V})}{\absolutevalue{V_{01}}^{2}}=\underset{i}{\sum}p_{i}UD(\ket{\beta(i)})+o(\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta},\mathcal{V})).

(132)

We use now Eq.(
129
) on each component:

\displaystyle\underset{i}{\sum}p_{i}UD(\ket{\beta(i)})\geq\underset{i}{\sum}p_{i}\frac{\omega^{2}}{\mathcal{F}(\ket{\beta(i)},H_{B})}\geq\frac{\omega^{2}}{\underset{i}{\sum}p_{i}\mathcal{F}(\ket{\beta(i)},H_{B})}=\frac{\omega^{2}}{\mathcal{F}(\beta,H_{B})}.

(133)

Where the last inequality holds thanks to the convexity of
\frac{1}{x}
.

This result can be obtained from theorem 1 of
Tajima et al. 2020
using the relationship between worst-case infidelity and Choi infidelity, however, this section is meant to illustrate how our construction can be applied not only to concave resources or pure states, instead can find optimal bound over all states of convex resources too.

We now prove that the inequality in Eq. (
129
)
is infact an equality, chosing a specific family of functions
\psi_{\mathcal{F}}
defined on
[0,\infty]
. Let

\psi_{\mathcal{F}}(x):=C^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}s(x)\exp\left[-\frac{2(x-\frac{\mathcal{F}}{\omega^{2}})^{2}\omega^{2}}{\mathcal{F}}\right],

(134)

where
s(x)
is a smooth function such that
s(0)=0
and
\underset{x\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}x^{2}(s(x)-1)=0
, e.g.,
s(x)=1-e^{-x^{2}}
and
C^{\prime}_{\mathcal{F}}
is the new normalization constant. It is immediate that in the
\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\infty
limit the latter satisfies
UD_{min}(\mathcal{F})=\frac{\omega^{2}}{\mathcal{F}}+o(1)
, proving the thesis.

Appendix F
Qudit target systems

In previous sections, we have restricted our analysis to a qubit target system. Here we generalize the results for a target system of
d
equally spaced energy levels. We consider a Hamiltonian of the target system of the form
H_{S}=\omega\underset{n=0}{\overset{d-1}{\sum}}n\underset{S}{\outerproduct{n}{n}}
and a battery Hamiltonian

H_{B}:=\omega\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}n\underset{B}{\outerproduct{n}{n}}.

(135)

The choice above is quite natural, as we already discussed for the
d=2
case. However, for
d>2
this Hamiltonian is suboptimal for implementing certain specific gates when the goal is to maximize precision under fixed battery resources. This can happen, for instance, when the relative entropy of coherence is chosen as a resource
Baumgratz et al. 2014
.
The interaction is a straightforward generalization of the
d=2
case:

U_{SB}=I_{SB}^{d-1}+\underset{n=d-1}{\overset{\infty}{\bigoplus}}U^{(n)}.

(136)

Where
I_{SB}^{d-1}:=I_{S}\otimes\underset{j=0}{\overset{d-2}{\sum}}\underset{B}{\outerproduct{j}{j}}
is the identity on the first
d-1
levels of
H_{B}
and
U^{(n)}:=\underset{i,j=0}{\overset{d-1}{\sum}}\underset{B}{\outerproduct{n-i}{n-j}}\otimes\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}V_{ij}
.
The first part of the calculations is formally identical to the case
d=2
, indeed
we have for any
n\geq d-1

\displaystyle V_{S}^{\dagger}K_{n}=\beta_{n}I_{S}+J_{S}^{(n)},

(137)

where
J_{S}^{(n)}:=\underset{i,j,j^{\prime}=0}{\overset{d-1}{\sum}}\underset{S}{\outerproduct{i}{j}}(\beta_{n+i-j^{\prime}}-\beta_{n})V_{ij^{\prime}}V_{jj^{\prime}}^{*}
. At this stage, we assume that the coefficient are smooth, i.e. that exists a regular function
\psi
such that
\beta_{n}^{(\delta)}:=C_{\delta}\psi(n\delta)
and are interested in the
\delta\rightarrow 0
limit. As in the
d=2
case populating the first
d-1
level of the battery is strongly sub-optmal, so we assume that
\psi(0)=0
, implying
\absolutevalue{\beta_{0}}^{2},..\absolutevalue{\beta_{d-1}}^{2}\propto\delta^{3}
, and making their contribution to the Choi infidelity negligible. Then we have

\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta^{(\delta)}},\mathcal{V})=-\frac{2}{d}\underset{n>d-2}{\sum}\text{Re}\left[C_{\delta}\psi(\delta n)^{*}\mbox{Tr}[J_{S}^{(n)}]\right]-\underset{n>d-2}{\sum}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\absolutevalue{\mbox{Tr}[J_S^{(n)}]}^{2}+O(\delta^{3}),

(138)

the trace of
J_{S}^{(n)}
reads

\mathrm{Tr}[J_{S}^{(n)}]=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\left[(\beta_{n+j}-\beta_{n})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l-k=j\end{subarray}}\lvert V_{l,k}\rvert^{2}-(\beta_{n}-\beta_{n-j})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l-k=j\end{subarray}}\lvert V_{k,l}\rvert^{2}\right].

(139)

Hence, we can make the substitutions

\displaystyle\beta^{(\delta)}_{n+j}-\beta^{(\delta)}_{n-}=C_{\delta}\left(j\delta\psi^{\prime}(n\delta)+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}j^{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(n\delta)+O(\delta^{3})\right),

(140)

\displaystyle\beta^{(\delta)}_{n}-\beta^{(\delta)}_{n-j}=C_{\delta}\left(j\delta\psi^{\prime}(n\delta)-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}j^{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(n\delta)+O(\delta^{3})\right),

(141)

and plug them in Eq.(
139
), getting

\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi},\mathcal{V})=-\frac{2}{d}\absolutevalue{C_{\delta}}^{2}\text{Re}\underset{n>0}{\overset{N-1}{\sum}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\psi^{*}(n\delta)\frac{j^{2}}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(n\delta)\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l-k=j\end{subarray}}\lvert V_{l,k}\rvert^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l-k=j\end{subarray}}\lvert V_{k,l}\rvert^{2}+O(\delta^{3})\right].

(142)

where the terms proportional to
\psi^{\prime}(n\delta)
disappeared thanks to a summation by parts and vanishing boundary conditions. Similarly one can show that
\underset{n>0}{\sum}\absolutevalue{\mbox{Tr}[J_S^{(n)}]}^{2}=O(\delta^{4})
and thus neglect this contribution.
We conclude that for smooth battery states the Choi infidelity is once again proportional to the unitary defect:

\epsilon_{C}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\beta^{\delta}},\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{A}[V_{S}]\delta^{2}UD(\psi)+O\left(\delta^{3}\right).

(143)

Where
\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}[V_{S}]:=\underset{j=1}{\overset{d}{\sum}}\frac{j^{2}}{d}\underset{\absolutevalue{l-k}=j}{\sum}\absolutevalue{V_{l,k}}^{2}
is a very intuitive quantifier of asymmetry that for
d=2
reduces to
\absolutevalue{V_{01}}^{2}
as expected.

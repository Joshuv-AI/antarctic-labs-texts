Health satisfaction outcome from integrated autonomous mobile clinics
Authors: Yuzhang Huang, Shaoshan Liu, Zhongying Pan, Carl Wu, Herng-Chia Chiu, Xue Liu, Leiyu Shi

ABSTRACT
AbstractAutonomous mobile clinics (AMCs) have the potential to revolutionize healthcare delivery by bringing healthcare services to patients at the order of patient’s fingertips. Particularly, AMCs can act as an essential touch point of integrated care, which is a worldwide response to the fragmented delivery of health by focusing on more coordinated and integrated forms of care provision. However, the impact of AMCs on the health satisfaction outcome effectiveness still remains unknown. Introduction

The United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 3 (SDG3) on health represents a universal recognition that health is fundamental to human capital and social and economic development. However, despite the progress achieved at the global level, many health systems are not sufficiently prepared to respond to the needs of the rapidly aging population. Inequitable access to healthcare continues to impede progress toward achieving universal health coverage. Therefore, providing essential health services that are safe, affordable, and effective, with a particular emphasis on the poor, vulnerable, and marginalized segments of the population, has become the main aim and task for all stakeholders in human development1.

A revolutionary goal like the SDG3 demands a revolutionary innovation. Autonomous Mobile Clinics (AMCs) have the potential to revolutionize healthcare delivery by bringing healthcare services to the patient at the order of the patient’s fingertips, thus solving the healthcare access problem2,3. Specifically, AMCs consist of two parts: the “autonomous” part allows AMCs to navigate to places where health care services are most needed, thus solving the mobility problem. The “mobile clinic” part allows health care access on-site, thus solving the access problem. In addition, the AI doctor within AMCs can serve unlimited patients, thus potentially eliminating the cost barrier.

Despite AMCs’ enormous promise, the impact of AMCs on health satisfaction outcome effectiveness remains unknown. In this article, in collaboration with the United Family Health (UFH) group, we delve into the potential effectiveness improvement of integrated care delivery through AMCs, hoping to shed light on the integration of AMCs into the existing healthcare systems.

Autonomous mobile clinics

In this section, we introduce the enabling technologies and the healthcare usage scenarios for AMCs, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Each AMC consists of four enabling technologies, including autonomous driving, telemedicine, medical equipment integration, and electronic health records4:

Autonomous mobile clinic.

Autonomous Driving: to deliver healthcare services to the patient by the order of the patient’s fingertips, Level 4 autonomous driving is required. To achieve this, each AMC should consist of a fully drive-by-wire chassis with fail-over backup systems, essential sensors for panoramic perception, a reliable localization system that can achieve decimeter-level accuracy, a planning, and control system that enables the vehicle to autonomously navigate through the target environment, as well as a High-Definition map of the operation area. Building upon the enabling technologies, many healthcare scenarios can be integrated into AMCs. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the interior of an AMC can be made modular to deliver various healthcare services. As an integrated digital health access technology, AMC’s primary functions include complete life-cycle monitoring, with diagnosis as well as interventional applications9.

Inside of autonomous mobile clinics.

Delving into the usage scenarios for AMCs, a public health study has interviewed over 3000 mobile clinic users and found that most users are from Low-income populations, representing 55% of all patients. The most frequently used specialty is primary care, presenting about 41% of subjects, and 28% of users are from the Dental department, 13% from mammography, and 11% from Pediatrics10. This data suggests that AMCs may be an effective healthcare service delivery method for disadvantaged groups, mainly covering primary care, dental care, mammography, and pediatrics. Furthermore, another public health study demonstrated that mobile care effectively increased acute care efficiency by over 36% and stated the up-rising trend of using mobile solutions from acute-care-related medical services11.

Integrated care touch point

The key healthcare service inefficiency arises from fragmented and episodic care. Under most existing healthcare systems, a patient has to visit many different departments and perform many different tests to obtain a comprehensive view of his or her health status. This approach not only wastes healthcare resources, such as doctors’ and nurses’ time but also creates barriers to discourage many patients away from seeking healthcare. Even worse, the opportunity cost of delayed healthcare delivery has led to the loss of many lives. The fragmented and episodic care problem is more severe among elderly patients. As the world is facing the aging population challenge, the fragmented and episodic care problem needs to be addressed urgently, potentially through revolutionary means.

To address this exact problem, a recent worldwide trend in healthcare is integrated care, which focuses on more coordinated and integrated forms of care provision, to improve healthcare service efficiency and eliminate the opportunity costs for patients. Integrated care is particularly important to service provision to the elderly, as elderly patients often become chronically ill and subject to co-morbidities, and so have a special need for continuous care. A detailed definition of integrated care developed by the World Health Organization (WHO) can be found in12.

After three years of adopting the integrated care model, UFH faces several problems. First, many patients suffer from mobility problems, and seeking healthcare service itself is a painful experience for them. Second, healthcare records are still episodic as many tests have to be performed separately, sometimes weeks apart between two tests. Therefore, getting a comprehensive view of a patient’s health status is challenging. Third, there is usually a long delay in scheduling a visit to a specialist after a patient’s test results come back.

Addressing these problems demands a unified touch point to bridge the patients to integrate care services. As shown in Fig. 2, AMCs are a perfect touch point of integrated care to solve these problems. First, AMCs can bring healthcare services to the patient’s home. Second, since AMCs are equipped with multiple medical equipment, most of the tests can be done on vehicles at the same time, thus presenting a comprehensive view of the patient’s health status. Third, immediately after the test results are available, through telemedicine, AMCs can connect the patient to the designated specialist selected based on the test results. The whole healthcare service process can be done very effectively and efficiently.

Effectiveness study with United Family Hospital

To understand the effectiveness of AMCs as a revolutionary way of healthcare service delivery, we have conducted a study with the UFH group.

Study methodology

For this study, 200 patients were randomly selected from UFH’s Out-patient department, half of the patients were using AMC-related telehealth consultations and follow-up processes (AMC group), while the other half were using Usual Care delivery methods (UC group), such as in-person outpatient visit and on-site follow-ups. An online survey was given on our telehealth mobile platform, regarding treatment effects based on waiting time and patient satisfaction, with the AMC group versus the UC group. Specifically, we have developed surveys on patient health treatment satisfaction (PSQ18 Questionnaire13) and Patient Willingness to use AMCs.

The study was designed to be a Retrospective Observational design14 from the existing patients from UFH Hospitals and Clinics (Beijing, Shanghai, Qingdao, Guangzhou, Shenzhen). The AMC group process consists of telemedicine consultation, mobile heart rate, body temperature detection, mobile diagnosis, and mobile follow-up. In contrast, the UC group process utilizes in-person hospital visits for treating related diseases.

We had an AMC mock room for telemedicine consultation, using a pad-screen will 4 g/5 g or clinic Wi-Fi setting, along with wearable monitoring devices, including heart rate, and body temperature detection. A nurse will also sit in the mock room to assist the patient in finishing the evaluation process (such as self-test on heart rate, and body temperatures using wearable monitoring devices).

Study follow-up time has been set to be three months in this study, participants in this study provided the above surveys and objective process outcome three months after treatment deployment. Note that patients younger than 18 years and patients not keen to participate in medical-related surveys were excluded from this study based on the methodology presented in15.

On the sample size selection, as we pilot with 200 patients with an expected effect size of 1.5, our minimum study Sample size will be 152 subjects to fulfill an a = 0.05 and 80% statistical power requirements. Reversely, a 200 sample size we tend to include in this study, will perform 90% of Statistical Power from the previous setting on margin of error and confidence interval. The Sample size calculator we used is Microsoft Sample Size Calculator TM16. Sample size calculation should follow the statistic Formular: n = (Zα/2 + Zβ)2 *2*σ2 / d2, such that Zα/2 is the critical value at α/2 normal distribution, which is a 95% confidence interval and 0.05 α, whereas Zβ is the critical value at a β 0.2 level, presenting a power of 80%, also σ2 is the population variance, and d is the expected difference17.

Summary

A revolutionary goal like the United Nations SDG3 calls for revolutionary innovation in healthcare service delivery. Autonomous Mobile Clinics (AMCs) answer this call by bringing healthcare services to the patient by the order of the patient’s fingertips, thus solving the healthcare access problem. This article presents the very first study on the health satisfaction outcome effectiveness of utilizing AMCs as the touch point of integrated care. The results are encouraging and confirm that AMCs can indeed bring improvement in the effectiveness of health satisfaction outcomes. As a next step, we will focus on pilot projects of integrating AMCs into existing healthcare systems, especially the places where healthcare services are needed the most, such as the Least Developed Countries (LDCs).

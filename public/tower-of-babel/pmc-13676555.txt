Pulpotomy of deciduous molars with deep caries using three different hydraulic calcium silicate cements: a short-term pilot randomized controlled split-mouth study
Authors: Ulrike Urmann, Isabelle M Schenke, Karl-Anton Hiller, Wolfgang Buchalla, Katrin Bekes, Matthias Widbiller, Frederike Schenke, Fabian Cieplik, Konstantin J Scholz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis randomized, controlled split-mouth study evaluated the short-term clinical survival of three hydraulic calcium silicate cements for pulp capping after pulpotomy in primary molars under general anesthesia.Materials and methodsThirty-three children aged 2 to 6 years, each with three carious primary molars (ICDAS 4–6), were recruited for treatment under general anesthesia. Following pulpotomy, the pulp was randomly covered with one of the materials: ProRoot MTA (Dentsply Sirona), Biodentine (Septodont), or TotalFill BC RRM Putty (FKG). The teeth were restored with steel crowns. Clinical survival rate at 5 and 10 months, application time, treatment costs and oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) were evaluated using the ECOHIS-G questionnaire. Data analysis was performed using non-parametric methods (α = 0.05).ResultsAll 84 treated teeth remained in situ for up to 10 months without clinical symptoms. TotalFill BC RRM Putty had the significantly shortest application time (median 75.5 s; p ≤ 0.001) compared to Biodentine (259 s) and ProRoot MTA (200.5 s). Biodentine resulted in the significantly lowest treatment cost (median €42.45; p ≤ 0.001), ProRoot MTA in the highest (€74.37; TotalFill BC RRM Putty: €60.01). The treatments generally led to improved OHRQoL (Median ECOHIS-G score: initial 5, after 5 months 4, after 10 months 2.5).ConclusionsAll investigated hydraulic calcium silicate cements showed no differences in short-term clinical outcomes after pulpotomy in primary molars.Clinical relevanceMaterial selection may therefore be guided by practical factors such as handling time and treatment costs, while long-term outcomes, which require further investigations, should be considered. German Clinical Trials Registry: Ref. DRKS00030041Keywords: Pulpotomy, Clinical study, Calcium silicate-based capping materials, OHRQoL, General anesthesia, Deciduous molars

Introduction

Globally, untreated caries remains the most prevalent non-communicable disease in 2021. According to the Global Burden of Disease study, untreated caries affected approximately 2.5 billion people in permanent teeth and more than 500 million children in deciduous teeth in 2021, highlighting its persistent relevance [1].

In high-income countries such as Germany, a general decline in caries prevalence has been observed over recent decades. The latest German Oral Health Study (DMS VI) reported that 78% of the 12-year-old children were caries-free [2]. The remaining approximately 22% [2], particularly those from lower socioeconomic background, carried a disproportionately high burden of disease [3, 4]. Especially deciduous teeth are affected. An investigation including 301,684 children in Germany showed caries prevalence of 10–17% among 3-year-olds with 74% of cavities unrestored [5].

The treatment of children in an outpatient setting is often challenging due to low compliance. Consequently, comprehensive dental treatment under general anesthesia is sometimes unavoidable [6]. A widely performed procedure is pulpotomy in deciduous molars with deep carious lesions, which aims to preserve pulp vitality and avoid premature tooth loss. To optimize outcomes and reduce the risk of future treatments in general anesthesia in this vulnerable group, the choice of material for vital pulp therapy is crucial. Current evidence recommends calcium silicate-based materials such as Mineral Trioxide Aggregate (MTA) due to their favorable biological properties and long-term success rates [7, 8]. Although rating the overall evidence as moderate to low, MTA is described to be the gold-standard material following pulpotomy in a Cochrane-review on pulp treatment for extensive decay in primary teeth [9]. Nevertheless, in clinical routine, other materials such as zinc-oxide eugenol cements (e.g., IRM®, Intermediate Restorative Material) [10] or formocresol are still used in some regions worldwide because of their low cost and ease of handling, despite known side effects and inferior long-term outcomes [7, 8].

Therefore, the aim of this randomized clinical split-mouth study was to directly compare the clinical performance of calcium silicate materials, Biodentine™ (Septodont GmbH, Niederkassel, Germany) and the premixed material TotalFill® BC RRM™ Putty (FKG Dentaire Sàrl, Le Crêt-du-Locle, Switzerland) with the technique-sensitive and costly ProRoot® MTA (Dentsply Sirona Germany GmbH, Bensheim, Germany) [11] for pulpotomy of deciduous molars with deep carious lesions, which were subsequently restored with stainless-steel crowns. All three materials are hydraulic calcium silicate-based cements that release calcium and hydroxyl ions during hydration, thereby increasing pH and promoting apatite-like deposit formation in contact with tissue fluids [12–15]. Chemically, calcium silicates are the active components of all three materials, with calcium chloride additionally accelerating Biodentine’s setting and monobasic calcium phosphate contributing to apatite formation in TotalFill BC RRM Putty, whose premixed non-aqueous carrier matrix contains fillers and thickening agents.

The first null-hypothesis was that three different calcium silicate capping materials did not lead to a different survival and clinical outcome of the pulpotomized teeth. The second null-hypothesis was that time and cost did not differ for the different materials. The third null-hypothesis was that pulpotomy, direct capping and restoration using prefabricated stainless-steel crowns of three deciduous molars generally had no significant effect on the patients' oral health-related quality of life.

Material and methods

Study design

The present short-term study is a prospective randomized controlled clinical split-mouth study, comparing clinical and economic performance of two different calcium-silicate based materials for pulpotomies in deciduous molars with deep carious lesions using ProRoot® MTA (MTA; Dentsply Sirona Germany GmbH, Bensheim, Germany). While MTA served as control material based on current clinical guidelines, Biodentine™ (BD; Septodont GmbH, Niederkassel, Germany) and TotalFill® BC RRM™ Putty (TF; FKG Dentaire Sàrl, Le Crêt-du-Locle, Switzerland) acted as test materials (Table 1). According to the manufacturers` instructions, BD and MTA are approved for pulpotomy of deciduous teeth, whereas TF is approved for apexifications, repair of root resorptions and root perforations, as well as retrofilling and direct pulp contact during pulp capping.

Ethical approval was obtained from the Internal Review Board of the University of Regensburg (Ref. 22–2794-101) in accordance with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments and followed the requirements of the CONSORT 2025 Statement [16] and the PRIRATE 2020 (Preferred Reporting Items for Randomized Trials in Endodontics) Checklist [17]. The study was registered in the German Clinical Trials Registry (Ref. DRKS00030041). For feasibility reasons, the study was concluded after 33 patients and 10 months of follow-up, although initially planned for 50 patients over 2 years. The patients’ parents were informed extensively in advance and gave their written informed consent to the treatment and the follow-up examinations of their children.

Patient recruitment

Patients were recruited from the patient pool of a specialized pediatric dental practice (Die Kinderzahnärzte Dr. Schenke & Kolleginnen, Regensburg, Germany). Children aged 2–6 years, ≤ ASA II [18], with ≥ 3 deciduous molars requiring pulpotomy, general anesthesia indicated due to limited compliance and no allergy against any of the tested materials were included.

Clinical procedures

In each patient, three deciduous molars were x-rayed (Fig. 1a) and completely pulpotomized down to the coronal canal orifices, which were covered with one of the three materials MTA, BD or TF. The procedures were performed under general anesthesia and all patients were treated by two dentists (FS, IMS) from a specialized pediatric dental practice (Die Kinderzahnärzte Dr. Schenke & Kolleginnen, Regensburg, Germany) who both had at least 8 years of clinical experience at the time the study was conducted. The teeth included in this study presented with clinically and radiographically deep to very deep carious lesions (ICDAS ratings 4–6 [19]), extending into the inner quarter of dentin up to the pulp chamber, without clinical or radiographic evidence of apical periodontitis. A positive response to cold-spray testing and a negative response to percussion, both preoperatively tested, needed to be present. Exclusion criteria were fistula, abscess, pathologic resorption as well as interradicular or periapical radiolucencies and not enough remaining tooth structure to fix a stainless-steel crown.

First, the teeth were cleaned with fluoride-free polishing paste (Cleanic, Kerr, Kloten, Switzerland). Non-selective caries excavation was performed with exposure of the pulp, followed by removal of the entire coronal pulp. Bleeding from the pulp was confirmed in all root canals. Sufficient moisture control was achieved using cotton rolls, salvia ejectors and parotis pads. Pulpal bleeding was stopped on all teeth using 12.7% ferric sulfate (AstringedentX, Ultradent Products GmbH, Cologne, Germany), which was then thoroughly rinsed off using air–water spray from a three-way syringe for at least 30 s, whereby the blood clot was removed completely. The duration to achieve hemostasis between the end of the pulpotomy procedure and the stop of spontaneous bleeding and the number of hemostasis attempts were documented.

Subsequently, either MTA, BD or TF was applied to the residual pulp tissue left in one increment of 3 mm, according to the manufacturers' instructions. The allocation was randomized by drawing a lot following an allocation ratio of 1:1:1. Patients were blinded to the materials randomly allocated to each tooth. As materials differed in application, a blinding of the operator was not possible. Finally, the teeth were restored using IRM (IRM®, Intermediate Restorative Material, Dentsply Sirona Germany GmbH, Bensheim, Germany) to cover the pulp-capping materials, followed by placement of stainless-steel crowns (Stainless Steel Primary Molar Crowns; Solventum, St. Paul, Minnesota, USA) cemented with Ketac Cem (Solventum St. Paul, Minnesota, USA) (see Fig. 1b-d). The application time of each application and for each material was recorded as the interval from completion of hemostasis until the material could be placed into the cavity to such an extent that coverage with IRM was possible at all cavity margins for the remainder of the material’s setting time.

Furthermore, a cost calculation was conducted for all three materials. The product of the average hourly operating costs of a dental practice in Germany (costs for anesthesia not included), which was 366.25 € in 2023 [20], with the median material-specific time needed for application (in hours) was added to the respective material costs.

Oral health-related quality of life

The children's oral health-related quality of life was recorded using the German version of the ECOHIS questionnaire, ECOHIS-G [21], which was answered by the parents before the treatment of the teeth and at each follow-up examination to assess the children’s oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) [22, 23]. The questionnaires were evaluated according to the scoring guidelines for the ECOHIS-G [21]. An overall score was calculated for each questionnaire, considering questions 1 to 13. A higher ECOHIS-G score indicates a poorer oral health-related quality of life with a maximum total score of 52. Additionally, two subscores were calculated, the “Child Impact Section” (CIS) subscore, compromising items 1–9 (maximum score: 36), which primarily reflects the patient’s individual quality of life, and the “Family Impact Section” (FIS) subscore, based on items 10–13 (maximum score: 16), which addresses the impact on the family as a whole.

Within these subscores, the items were further categorized thematically. Item 1 referred to the patient's symptoms (Child Symptom), items 2–5 covered functional limitations (Child Function), items 6 and 7 evaluated emotional well-being (Child Psychology), items 8 and 9 addressed self-image and social interaction (Child Self-image). The FIS subscore was divided into two thematic categories, “Family Function” (items 10 and 11) and “Parental Distress” (items 12 and 13). The last two items (14 und 15) were not included in the evaluation of the overall score as they relate to the patient's “overall well-being”.

Follow-up examinations

The treated teeth were assessed at two follow-up examinations by one blinded examiner from a pool of examiners (UU, FS, IMS, VK, SH, CZ, see acknowledgement), which all had at least 3 years of clinical experience and were not involved in the treatment of the respective patient.

At the follow-up examinations primary and secondary criteria were evaluated. Presence or absence of premature tooth loss, abscess, fistula and spontaneous pain or pain at night were defined as primary criteria. Secondary criteria included periodontal criteria papillary bleeding index (PBI) mesial and distal [24], probing pocket depths (PPD), Millers´s mobility index [25], plaque-Index by Ramfjord [26] as well as pulpal criteria pain, cold sensitivity test, sensitivity to percussion horizontal and sensitivity to percussion vertical. If any of the secondary criteria suggested a potential pathological event (e.g. PPD ≥ 4 mm, regular pain, strong reactions to percussion), an intraoral dental x-ray was performed (see Fig. 1e-f).

Data analysis

Teeth that exhibited no findings in any of the primary outcome criteria were classified as “survival”. Specifically, this classification required negative findings (“no”) for each of the criteria “abscess”, “fistula”, “night pain” and “premature tooth loss”. If all secondary outcome parameters were within the predefined acceptable ranges, i.e. “PBI mesial” (≤ Grade 2), “PBI distal” (≤ Grade 2), “probing pocket depths” (≤ 3,5 mm), “Miller´s mobility index” (Grade 0), “Plaque-Index Ramfjord” (≤ Score 2), “pain” (no pain), “cold sensitivity test” (positive or delayed positive), “percussion horizontal” (negative), “percussion vertical” (negative), no further diagnostic assessments were made. These secondary parameters were used to characterize the clinical status of the tooth and were not considered equivalent to overall pulpotomy success.

The statistical analyses were carried out assuming equivalence of the control material and the test materials with SPSS for Windows, version 29 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Frequency tables, crosstabs, chi-square tests, Mann–Whitney-tests and Fisher´s exact tests were made from the treatment data as well as the follow-up examinations in relation to the three materials used and ECOHIS-G questionnaire data on a significance level of α = 0.05.

Results

Patient characteristics

Thirty-three patients were recruited for this randomized controlled clinical split-mouth study, 28 of them attended both required follow-up examinations and were included in the analysis. Figure 2 shows the CONSORT Flowchart for the study.

The median (25%; 75% percentile) age of the patients was 5 years (4; 6). Among the 28 patients, 12 females and 16 males participated on both evaluation time points. Regarding distribution of teeth and materials, there was no significant correlation between the material and the treated tooth, or whether it was a first or second deciduous molar.

The overall median (25%; 75% percentile) in order to achieve hemostasis was 27 (19.3; 35.8) seconds. Clinical parameters at the follow-up examinations

The follow-up examinations were scheduled 5- and 10 months following treatment. The median (25%; 75% percentile) periods between treatment and the follow-up examination were 158.5 days (139.5; 183.5) and 306.5 days (274.5; 391), respectively.

All treated teeth survived until the second follow-up examination and exhibited no signs of night pain, abscess formation or fistula. One patient reported sporadic mild pain on a tooth treated with BD at the first follow-up examination and one patient reported sporadic mild pain on a tooth treated with TF at the second follow-up examination.

Small changes were also observed in all secondary criteria, which deteriorated slightly as shown in Tables 3, 4 and 5 but none of these deteriorations reached statistical significance.

Discussion

This pilot, short-term, randomized controlled split-mouth study compared the clinical survival of two calcium silicate-based materials, TF and BD, and the control material MTA when used for pulpotomy in deciduous molars. In addition to clinical outcomes in terms of survival rates after 5 and 10 months, oral health related quality of life, application time and cost-efficiency were assessed. MTA served as control due to established clinical use [11, 27, 28] and is recommended in vital pulp therapy by international guidelines (e.g., European Society of Endodontology [29] for permanent teeth and American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry [30] for primary teeth). Although hydraulic calcium silicate cements have been extensively investigated, direct comparison of these three materials in a randomized split-mouth design under general anesthesia remains limited.

All materials demonstrated high clinical survival rates during the observation period without significant differences, so the first null-hypothesis could not be rejected. After 10 months, 100% of the treated teeth survived. These results align with previous studies, reporting high success rates of tricalcium silicate based materials compared with other pulp capping materials like e.g. calcium hydroxide solutions [8] as well as similar outcomes among different tricalcium silicates, like MTA and BD [31–37]. In contrast, there is one study available that shows significantly better results of MTA as compared to BD regarding clinical and radiological success after 12 months (MTA: 98% clinical and 92% radiological success, BD: 90% clinical and 80% radiological success) [38]. Furthermore, other studies [39, 40] showed a significantly higher incidence of canal obliteration with BD than with MTA. However, it should be noted that canal obliterations indicate a vital pulp and, given their short lifespan, canal obliterations in deciduous teeth are relatively minor complications compared to those in permanent teeth [39, 40]. These findings are consistent with previous reports on the clinical use of hydraulic calcium silicate cements for pulpotomy and also pre-mixed materials with a putty-like consistency are suitable for other clinical applications, such as retrograde fillings or direct pulp capping [41–43]. Another clinical study compared a pre-mixed calcium silicate material (NuSmile, Houston, Texas, USA) with BD and MTA over a 2-year period for pulpotomies in primary teeth [43]. Survival rates ranged from 89 to 100% for all tested materials, with BD showing the highest occurrence of narrowed canal systems in radiographs. As in the present study, no statistically significant effects of the materials were found [43].

Another study also investigated TF, which is based on tricalcium silicate and uses Zirconium oxide and tantalum oxide as radiopacifying agents instead of bismuth oxide, which has been associated with discoloration in conventional MTA cements [41, 44]. The material was compared with MTA in a 4-arm design, which additionally varied whether the teeth were restored with different fillings or with stainless-steel crowns. In this study, the capping material had no significant influence on clinical success. However, after 12 months, teeth restored with stainless-steel crowns showed significantly higher survival rates of 87.5–93.8% (clinical) and 56.2–62.5% (radiographic) compared to directly restored primary teeth [41]. One possible reason for the success of stainless-steel crowns may be the protection they offer against microleakage [45]. In light of these findings, stainless-steel crowns were selected as the definitive restoration in the present study because of their established use and reliable full-coverage coronal seal [46]. The decision was made despite the potential for crowns to complicate subsequent clinical follow-up.

MTA and BD are tricalcium and dicalcium silicate-based, albeit in varying ratios, whereas TF additionally contains monobasic calcium phosphate, providing an intrinsic phosphate source that may enhance apatite nucleation [12, 15]. This process contributes to enhanced sealing ability and aims to support tissue regeneration [12]. Talabani et al. demonstrated that a putty material exhibited a stably high pH-value of 11 over 28 days and a higher calcium ion release than a conventional MTA-based material, which is beneficial for hydroxy apatite formation and possible antibacterial properties [13, 47]. In an investigation of vascular wall-derived mesenchymal stem cells, calcium silicate-based materials embedded in a premixed matrix exhibited osteoinductive effects, as indicated by upregulated osteopontin gene expression and increased collagen I and osteocalcin expression [48]. Similar effects would be worth investigating for the materials examined in the present study, particularly the premixed material TF.

To date, this study represents the first clinical investigation of TF in the context of pulpotomy in the primary dentition in a split-mouth design. Existing evidence regarding its performance stems predominantly from investigations on permanent teeth, e.g. reporting favorable outcomes in retrograde root canal fillings [14]. Although no clinically relevant differences between the three materials were observed during the short-term follow-up in the present study, previous in vitro research has demonstrated differences in the Ca2+-ion release and pH-value development of calcium silicate based materials influenced by their matrix chemistry [49]. Recent consensus-based recommendations support the use of hydraulic calcium silicate cements for vital pulp therapy in primary teeth with deep caries and pulp exposure, including full pulpotomy. However, the authors of these recommendations emphasize the need of further long-term studies evaluating newer calcium silicate-based materials in primary teeth[50].

In the present study, notable differences were observed in cost and application periods. TF required the shortest application period due to its ready-to-use consistency of higher viscosity, which also allowed immediate placement of the IRM base without waiting for setting of the calcium-silicate cements [32]. BD proved to be the most cost-effective material but required a longer preparation and setting time due to its mixing protocol and higher fluidity [51]. MTA remains the most expensive option, potentially limiting its routine use in resource-constrained settings [52]. However, material selection may also be influenced by clinical training and familiarity with mineral trioxide aggregate as a material group [53]. These findings may be relevant for dental treatment under general anesthesia, where shorter application times may contribute to reducing overall treatment duration, given that general anesthesia is both cost-intensive and associated with potential medical risks. In addition, the premixed handling of TF may reduce operator-related variability. The cost of pulpotomy materials may influence treatment decisions. These costs appear high compared to other treatment options that were favored in the past, e.g. the Hall technique or conventional fillings without pulpotomy [54]. However, even with a tooth extraction, follow-up costs would occur, e.g. for subsequent orthodontic treatment in the event of space loss or for a space maintainer. Consequently, an inevitable pulpotomy that is not performed may also cause additional expenses, and in addition could lead to a lower OHRQoL [55]. These circumstances appear precarious given that numerous studies have shown low parental education and income correlate strongly with higher dmft-scores [56, 57]. Odontogenic infections as a consequence of untreated dental caries are the most frequent reason for hospitalization of young children [58]. Oral health massively influences children’s general health and quality of life [59, 60], and therefore children would benefit from early dental care [61]. Fast-setting materials such as TF can be advantageous by reducing procedure time [34], which may be beneficial in a general anesthesia setup as well as in an outpatient setup. The different costs and treatment times for each material disprove the second null-hypothesis that time and cost did not differ for the different materials.

The study also investigated hemostasis as an intraoperative indicator of pulpal health [59]. Only teeth where coagulations could be removed completely after ferric sulfate application were included. Hemostasis was achieved with 12.7% ferric sulfate, because operators wanted to avoid unnecessarily long treatment durations. As ferric sulfate has become a widely used hemostatic agent in primary tooth pulpotomies, Akman et al. mentions that the hemostatic effect of the material may mask the pathology of the underlying pulp tissue. In conclusion, careful case selection and regular follow-up examinations are recommended [62] and it should be considered, when interpreting clinical outcomes, that patient-related factors may additionally influence endodontic treatment outcomes[63]. Postoperative pain is a recognized short-term outcome following endodontic procedures and should be assessed when evaluating treatment outcomes. In the present study no relevant postoperative pain was reported during follow-up [64].

Tao et al. demonstrated that children diagnosed with an indication for pulpotomy experience a negative impact on their quality of life [65]. To further assess the impact of treatment on patient well-being, the German version of the Early childhood oral Impact Scale (ECOHIS-G) was used [21]. The overall ECOHIS-G scores decreased over time by tendency, indicating an improvement in oral health-related quality of life but since it was not significant, the third null-hypothesis cannot be discarded. This trend was particularly evident in the CIS subscore, which showed more pronounced changes as compared to the FIS subscore. These findings are in line with prior research by Boukhobza et al. [66] who confirms that there is a significant improvement in children’s oral health-related quality of life after dental treatment, like for example pulp therapy under general anesthesia. Nevertheless, it should be noted that the improvements in this study are rather slight. This suggests that either the patients have few symptoms despite their great need for treatment or their parents hardly notice them [67]. Therefore, the results underscore the importance of regular dental checkups, especially for the vulnerable patient group of children with high caries-risk.

A limitation of the present study may be the relatively short observation period of ten months, which does not allow conclusions regarding long-term pulpotomy outcome. Late failures, such as internal resorption or chronic inflammation, may occur beyond the present observation period. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as short-term clinical observations only. Furthermore, the relatively small sample size and the absence of a prespecified sample-size calculation may have limited the ability to detect potential differences between materials despite the observed 100% short-term clinical survival. Radiographic examinations were not performed at every follow-up visit due to ethical considerations and radiation protection guidelines, which may have influenced the consistency of radiological assessments. Consequently, asymptomatic radiographic changes such as internal resorption or furcation radiolucencies cannot be excluded in teeth for which no radiographic examination was performed. The presence or absence of a response to subjective tests, such as cold sensitivity testing, was recorded as an adjunctive clinical parameter, considering the limited reliability of subjective responses in very young patients and limited validity due to steel crown restorations. Furthermore, ferric sulfate was used for hemostasis in the study to shorten treatment time and thereby reduce the risk associated with general anesthesia. Ferric sulfate application before capping material application might influence the interaction with the residual pulp tissue and thus the outcomes. However, a 24-month study on the pulpotomy of primary molars is available that compared different hemostatic agents in combination with either calcium hydroxide or MTA as the subsequent capping material [68] Since the hemostatic agents ferric sulfate, sterile saline, or sodium hypochlorite had no significant effect on clinical and radiographic outcomes, whereas MTA showed significantly better radiographic outcomes (p = 0.001) and clinical outcomes approaching the level of significance (p = 0.052), it can be concluded that the capping material also exerts an influence after pulpotomy even when ferric sulfate has been applied[68].

In summary, this study provides preliminary descriptive data on the short-term clinical outcomes of pulpotomy using the tested calcium silicate-based materials in deciduous molars with deep caries and no signs of irreversible pulpitis, with all treated teeth remaining in situ throughout the observation period. Material selection might consider not only clinical performance but also cost, application time, infection control and, in case of outpatient setups, patient cooperation requirements. TF offers the advantage of rapid application and may be preferable for young or uncooperative patients. Compare to MTA, also BD provides a cost-efficient alternative, albeit with longer application period, which complicates its use in pediatric procedures [69].

Conclusion

Within the ten-month observation period, all treated teeth remained in situ, corresponding to a 100% tooth survival rate. The materials differed mainly in their practical characteristics, with Biodentine™ being the most cost-effective, followed by TotalFill® BC RRM™ Putty and ProRoot® MTA. TotalFill® BC RRM™ Putty required the shortest application time. Pulpotomy and the associated restorative procedures were also accompanied with an improvement in children`s oral health-related quality of life. These findings provide a basis for further clinical investigation of calcium silicate-based materials in primary molar pulpotomy, particularly in studies with lager sample sizes and longer observation periods.

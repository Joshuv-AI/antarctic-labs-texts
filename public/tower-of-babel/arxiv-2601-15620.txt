Closing the Gap on the Sample Complexity of 1-Identification
Li Zitian, Cheung Wang Chi

Abstract
1-identification is a fundamental multi-armed bandit formulation on pure exploration. An agent aims to determine whether there exists a qualified arm whose mean reward is not less than a known threshold μ 0 \mu_{0} , or to output None if it believes such an arm does not exist. The agent needs to guarantee its output is correct with probability at least 1 − δ 1-\delta , while making expected total pulling times 𝔼 ​ τ \mathbb{E}\tau as small as possible. We work on 1-identification with two main contributions. (1) We utilize an optimization formulation to derive a new lower bound of 𝔼 ​ τ \mathbb{E}\tau , when there is at least one qualified arm. (2) We design a new algorithm, deriving tight upper bounds whose gap to lower bounds are up to a polynomial of logarithm factor across all problem instance. Our result complements the analysis of 𝔼 ​ τ \mathbb{E}\tau when there are multiple qualified arms, which is an open problem left by history literature.

In this section, we continue to work on

\nu^{\prime}
and a permutation

We first introduce the definition of a symmetric algorithm.

Definition 3
.

An algorithm
\widetilde{\text{alg}}
is said to be
symmetric
if the following statement holds. For any instance
\nu
with reward vector
\{\mu_{a}\}_{a=1}^{K}
, any sequence
\{a_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}\in[K]^{T}

\displaystyle\Pr_{\sigma^{\prime}(\nu),\tilde{\text{alg}}}(\forall t\in[T],A_{t}=\sigma^{\prime}(a_{t}),\tau>T)=\Pr_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\nu),\tilde{\text{alg}}}(\forall t\in[T],A_{t}=\sigma^{\prime\prime}(a_{t}),\tau>T).

Intuitively, under a symmetric algorithm
\widetilde{\text{alg}}
, the reward distribution of the pulled arm
A_{t}
at time
t
is invariant under any permutation on the arms’ indexes.

In particular, for a symmetric algorithm
\widetilde{\text{alg}}
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma^{\prime}(\nu),\tilde{\text{alg}}}\tau=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}(\nu),\tilde{\text{alg}}}\tau
for any two permutations
\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}
. A similar definition also appears in Definition 12 of
Simchowitz et al. 2017
, which further requires the distribution of output
\hat{a}
is also invariant regarding the distribution. This extra requirement is unnecessary in our analysis. By the following lemma, it suffices to consider only symmetric algorithms for establishing our lower bound.

Lemma 4
.

Denote
S
as the set of all permutations of
[K]
. Given any
\delta
-PAC algorithm
alg
, we can always find a
\delta
-PAC symmetric algorithm
\widetilde{\text{alg}}

\nu^{\prime}
, we have
\max\limits_{\sigma\in S}\mathbb{E}_{\text{alg},\sigma(\nu^{\prime})}\tau\geq\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{\text{alg}},\nu^{\prime}}\tau
.

) for
a\in B_{b}
and derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\tau^{\text{ee}}(B_{b})\mathds{1}(b_{j}=b)\leq\sum_{a=1}^{K}O(1)\tilde{T}_{j}(a)\Pr(a\in B_{b},b_{j}=b)+O(\log K)|B_{b}|\Pr(b_{j}=b).

Since

, and
\Pr(a\in B_{b})=\frac{|B_{b}|}{K}
, we can conclude (
13
).

which means
\tilde{\text{alg}}
fits in the definition. Then, we have

\displaystyle\max_{\sigma\in S}\mathbb{E}_{\text{alg},\sigma(\nu^{\prime})}\tau

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{\sigma\in S}\mathbb{E}_{\text{alg},\sigma(\nu^{\prime})}\tau}{K!}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\text{alg}},\nu^{\prime}}\tau

The last thing is to show

\delta
-PAC. Since alg is

That means

\displaystyle\Pr_{\widetilde{\text{alg}},\sigma(\nu)}(\tau<+\infty,\hat{a}\in i^{*}(\sigma(\nu)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{\sigma\in S}1-\delta}{K!}

\displaystyle=

We can conclude

Lemma 7
.

Given a pair

and an arbitrary instance
\nu^{\prime}
, denote
\Delta=|r^{\nu^{\prime}}_{i}-r^{\nu^{\prime}}_{j}|
.
Define

\displaystyle\tau_{n}=\min\{t:N_{i}(t)+N_{j}(t)=n,A_{t+1}\in\{i,j\}\}.

Apply a symmetric algorithm, we have

\displaystyle\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=i)\log\frac{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=i)}{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=j)}+

\displaystyle\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=j)\log\frac{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=j)}{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=i)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{2n\Delta^{2}\log\frac{1}{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau)}}\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau)+\sqrt{2\pi n\Delta^{2}}\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau)+\frac{1}{2}n\Delta^{2}\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau).

An observation is given the realized reward and a symmetric algorithm, the set
\{t:N_{i}(t)+N_{j}(t)=n,A_{t+1}\in\{i,j\}\}=:S_{ij}(n)

holds in this case. If

\tau_{n}\geq\tau
. Another observation is

\displaystyle\frac{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=i)\log\frac{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=i)}{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=j)}+\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=j)\log\frac{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=j)}{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau,A_{\tau_{n}+1}=i)}}{\Pr_{\nu^{\prime}}(\tau_{n}<\tau)}

Proof of Lemma

Notice that for

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\exp\left(\lambda\big(Z_{t}\big)\right)|z_{1},\cdots,z_{t-1}]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Jensen's Inequality}}}{{\geq}}

\displaystyle\exp\left(\lambda\mathbb{E}[\big(Z_{t}\big)|Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{t-1}]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(0)=1.

Then, we can conclude

\displaystyle\Pr(\max_{1\leq t\leq n}\sum_{s=1}^{t}Z_{s}>z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\prime}}\prod_{t=1}^{n}\exp(\lambda(Z_{t}))}{\exp(\lambda s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\exp(\frac{n\lambda^{2}\Delta^{2}}{2})}{\exp(\lambda s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(-\frac{s^{2}}{2n\Delta^{2}}).

∎

Lemma 33
.

For any
K\in\mathbb{N}
,
K\geq 2
,
j\in[K]
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot\lfloor 2^{b-2}\rfloor}{K}\right)}\min\{2^{b-1},K\}\leq\frac{K}{j}\cdot O\Big((\log_{2}\frac{K}{j})+1\Big)

Proof.

We can conduct direct calculation.

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot\lfloor 2^{b-2}\rfloor}{K}\right)}\min\{2^{b-1},K\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle K\geq 2}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2}{\exp(\frac{j}{K})}+\sum_{b=3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot\lfloor 2^{b-2}\rfloor}{K}\right)}\min\{2^{b-1},K\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2}{\exp(\frac{j}{K})}+\sum_{b=3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{2^{b-1}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2}{\exp(\frac{j}{K})}+\frac{2K}{j}\sum_{b=3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2K}{j}+\frac{2K}{j}\sum_{b=3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}.

Then, we suffice to prove
\sum_{b=3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}\leq O\left((\log_{2}\frac{K}{j})+1\right)
.

From the fact that
\frac{d\frac{x}{\exp(x)}}{dx}=e^{-x}-xe^{-x}=(1-x)e^{-x}
, we know
\frac{x}{\exp(x)}
monotonically increases in the interval
(0,1)
and decreases in the interval
(1,+\infty)
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{b=3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{b=3}^{\lfloor\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rfloor+3}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}+\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil+3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\lfloor\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rfloor+1)\frac{x}{e^{x}}|_{x=1}+\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil+3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\lfloor\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rfloor+1)\frac{1}{e}+\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil+3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}.

The second last line is by that function
\frac{x}{e^{x}}
achieves its maximum at
x=1
. The remaining work is to prove
\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil+3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}\leq O(1)
.

Notice that for
b\geq\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil+3
, we have
\frac{j2^{b-2}}{K}\geq\frac{j2^{\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}+1}}{K}\geq 2
. Since we can conclude
\frac{x}{e^{x}}\geq 2\cdot\frac{2x}{e^{2x}}
holds for all
x\geq 2
, we can conclude

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-1}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-1}}{K}\right)}/\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}\leq\frac{1}{2}

holds for all
b\geq\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil+3
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil+3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil+3}^{+\infty}2^{-(b-\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil-3)}\frac{\frac{j\cdot 2^{\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil+3-2}}{K}}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K}{j}\rceil+3-2}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e}\sum_{b=0}^{+\infty}2^{-b}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(1).

That means we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 34
.

For any
K\in\mathbb{N}
,
K\geq 2
,
j\in[K]
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\min\{\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot\lfloor 2^{b-2}\rfloor}{K}\right)},\frac{\min\{2^{b-1},K\}}{K}\}\leq O\Big(\frac{\log j}{j}\Big).

Proof.

We can conduct direct calculation.

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\min\{\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot\lfloor 2^{b-2}\rfloor}{K}\right)},\frac{\min\{2^{b-1},K\}}{K}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}+\sum_{b=2}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\min\{\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)},\frac{2}{j}\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}+\sum_{b=2}^{\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+2}\frac{2}{j}\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}+\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}.

We prove an upper bound for
\sum_{b=2}^{\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+2}\frac{2}{j}\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}
and
\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}
separately.

First, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{b=2}^{\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+2}\frac{2}{j}\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{j}\cdot 2\cdot\frac{j2^{b-2}}{K}|_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{j}\cdot 2\cdot\frac{j2^{1+\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}}}{K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{8\log j}{j}.

For
\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}
, we first notice that when
b\geq\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+3
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{j2^{b-2}}{K}\geq\frac{j2^{\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+3-2}}{K}\geq\frac{j2^{\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}+1}}{K}\geq 2.

Further, we can conclude for
b\geq\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+3
, we have
\frac{j2^{b-2}}{K}+1\leq\frac{j2^{b-2}}{K}\cdot 2=\frac{j2^{b-1}}{K}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+3}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{b-2}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{b=\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+3}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\exp(b-\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil-3)}\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+3-2}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{\lceil\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}\rceil+3-2}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2^{\log_{2}\frac{K\log j}{j}+1}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot 2\cdot\frac{K\log j}{j}}{K}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\exp\left(2\log j\right)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{2}{j}.

In summary, we have proved

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{\lceil\log_{2}K\rceil+1}\min\{\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{j\cdot\lfloor 2^{b-2}\rfloor}{K}\right)},\frac{\min\{2^{b-1},K\}}{K}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}+\frac{8\log j}{j}+\frac{2}{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O\Big(\frac{\log j}{j}\Big).

∎

Lemma 37
.

For any
b\geq a\geq 0
,

•

If
b\geq e^{2}
, we have
x\geq b+2a\log\log b\Rightarrow x\geq a\log\log(x)+b
.

•

If
b,a\geq e
, we have
e\leq x\leq b+a\log\log b\Rightarrow x<a\log\log(x)+b
.

The second inequality also implies
x\geq a\log\log(x)+b\Rightarrow x\geq b+a\log\log b
.

Proof.

We prove the first claim. Easy to see
\frac{d(x-a\log\log x-b)}{dx}=1-\frac{a}{x\log x}=\frac{x\log x-a}{x\log x}
. Take
x_{0}=b+2a\log\log b
, then
x_{0}\log x_{0}>x_{0}\log(e^{2}+2a\log\log e^{2})>x_{0}\log e^{2}=2x_{0}>a
. Thus
x-a\log\log(x)-b
increases in the interval
(x_{0},+\infty)
. On the other hand, easy to check

\displaystyle x_{0}-a\log\log x_{0}-b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b+2a\log\log b-a\log\log(b+2a\log\log b)-b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a\log\log b-a\log\log(b+2a\log\log b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\left(2\log\log b-\log\log(b+2a\log\log b)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\left(\log(\log b)^{2}-\log\log(b+2a\log\log b)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\log\frac{(\log b)^{2}}{\log(b+2a\log\log b)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle a\log\frac{(\log b)^{2}}{\log(b+2b\log\log b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\log\frac{(\log b)^{2}}{\log b+\log(1+2\log\log b)},

Notice that

\displaystyle(\log b)^{2}-\log b-\log(1+2\log\log b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log b(\log b-1)-\log(1+2\log\log b)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b\geq e^{2}}}{{\geq}}

\displaystyle\log b-\log(1+2\log\log b)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\log b-2\log\log b

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x>2\log x,\forall x>0}}{{>}}

\displaystyle 0,

we can conclude
x_{0}-a\log\log x_{0}-b>0
holds for all
x\geq b+2a\log\log b
.

Then we turn to prove the second claim. As
\frac{d(x-a\log\log x-b)}{dx}=1-\frac{a}{x\log x}=\frac{x\log x-a}{x\log x}
, we know there is at most 1 zero point of
\frac{x\log x-a}{x\log x}
in the interval
(e,b+a\log\log b)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\max_{e\leq x\leq b+a\log\log b}x-a\log\log x-b=\max\{x-a\log\log x-b|_{x=e},x-a\log\log x-b|_{x=b+a\log\log b}\}.

\displaystyle e-a\log\log e-b=e-b<0,

and

\displaystyle b+a\log\log b-a\log\log(b+a\log\log b)-b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\log\log b-a\log\log(b+a\log\log b)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle a\log\log b-a\log\log(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

That means
\max\limits_{e\leq x\leq b+a\log\log b}x-a\log\log x-b<0
. The second conclusion is done.

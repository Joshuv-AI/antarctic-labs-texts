Discriminants and motivic integration
Oscar Kivinen, Alexei Oblomkov, Dimitri Wyss

Abstract
. We study invariants of a plane cuve singularity ( f , 0 ) (f,0) coming from motivic integration on symmetric powers of a formal deformation of f f . We show that a natural discriminant integral recovers the motivic classes of the principal Hilbert schemes of points on f f , while the orbifold integral gives the plethystic exponential of the motivic Igusa zeta function of f f . The latter result also holds in higher dimemsions. Combined with results of Gorsky and Némethi we obtain an interpretation of the discriminant integrals in terms of knot Floer homology, which is reminiscent of the relation between the cohomology of contact loci and fixed point Floer homology proven by de la Bodega and Poza.



1.
Introduction

Consider a nonconstant polynomial map
f:\mathbb{A}^{m}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
over
k=\mathbb{C}
defining an isolated hypersurface singularity at
0\in\mathbb{A}^{m}
. A fundamental invariant of
(f,0)
is its Milnor fiber
F_{0}
, a compact smooth manifold with boundary depending only on the singularity at
0

[
32
]
. Its singular cohomology
H^{*}(F_{0},\mathbb{Q})
admits a monodromy endomorphism
M
, whose zeta function
\zeta_{f,0}(T)=\prod_{i\geq i}\det(1-TM\ |\ H^{i}(F_{0},\mathbb{Q}))^{(-1)^{i+1}}

can be computed purely algebraically by a remarkable result of Denef and Loeser
[
17
]
:

For any
n\geq 1
let
X^{0}_{f,n}
denote the
n
-th restricted contact locus of
f
. It is defined as

X^{0}_{f,n}=\{x\in\mathbb{A}^{m}(k[[t]]/t^{n+1})_{0}\ |\ f(x)=t^{n}\in k[[t]]/t^{n+1}\},

where
\mathbb{A}^{m}(k[[t]]/t^{n+1})_{0}
denotes the locus of
n
-jets reducing to
0
modulo
t
. Then the Euler characteristics
\chi(X^{0}_{f,n})
determine
\zeta_{f,0}(T)
via the formula

(1.1)

\zeta_{f,0}(T)=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\chi(X^{0}_{f,n})}{n}T^{n}\right).

While the original proof of (
1.1
) proceeds by computing both sides independently on an embedded resolution of
f
, there exists now a more conceptual interpretation
[
33
,
27
]
as a Lefschetz fixed point formula on the analytic Milnor fiber. In a similar spirit, the goal of this paper is to study refinements of (
1.1
) coming from motivic integration.

Concretely, consider the
k[[t]]
-scheme

\mathcal{X}_{f}=\textup{Spec}(k[[t]][x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]/f-t),

and associated with it for any
n\geq 0
the relative symmetric power
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}=\mathcal{X}_{f}\times_{k[[t]]}\dots\times_{k[[t]]}\mathcal{X}_{f}/S_{n}
. In a nutshell our paper studies motivic classes in the localized Grothendieck ring
\mathcal{C}_{k}=K_{0}(Var_{k})[\mathbb{L}^{-1},(1-\mathbb{L}^{i})^{-1}:i\geq 1]
coming from Cluckers-Loeser motivic integrals
[
10
]
on

\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}(k[[t]])=\{x\in\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})(k[[t]])\ |\ x_{k}=n[0]\}.

Our first main result is in the case
m=2
, i.e. when
(f,0)
defines an isolated plane curve singularity, and is partially motivated by the second author’s work on the relation between Hilbert schemes and knot invariants
[
35
,
34
]
. Namely, since
\mathcal{X}_{f|k((t))}
is a smooth curve, the generic fiber of
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}
admits another model over
\textup{Spec}(k[[t]])
given by the relative Hilbert scheme of points
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
.

The special fiber
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})_{k}
is the classical Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)
of length
n
subschemes on the singular curve
f=0
and we write
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}\subset\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)
for the locally closed subscheme parametrizing principal subschemes supported at
0
.

Theorem 1.1

(
3.3
)
.

For any
n\geq 1
there exists a generically non-vanishing definable volume form
|\omega_{n}^{gel}|
on
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}
such that

\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}}|\omega^{gel}_{n}|=\mathbb{L}^{-n}[\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}]\in\mathcal{C}_{k}.

By Proposition
3.6
, the generating series

P_{gel}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}}|\omega^{gel}_{n}|T^{n},

can be identified with the one-variable motivic Poincaré series of Campillo, Delgado and Gusein-Zade
[
7
,
8
]
.

Since
|\omega^{gel}_{n}|
is generically non-vanishing, one can compute the Euler characteristic specialization of
P_{gel}(T)
by the trace formula for motivic Serre invariants, recovering an old result of Milnor
[
32
, Lemma 10.1]
:

Corollary 1.2

(
3.9
)
.

For any reduced plane curve singularity
(f,0)
with link
L
we have

\zeta_{f,0}(T)=\frac{A_{L}(T)}{1-T},

where
A_{L}(T)
denotes the normalized Alexander polynomial of the link.

As a second corollary, we combine Theorem
1.1
with results of Gorsky and Némethi
[
21
]
to obtain an interpretation of the motivic integrals
\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}}|\omega^{gel}_{n}|
in terms of the knot Floer homology
HFL^{-}(L,v)
of the link
L
defined by the plane curve singularity
(f,0)

[
36
,
37
]
.

Corollary 1.3

(
5.4
)
.

We have an equality

P_{gel}(T)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{r},d\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim HFL^{-}_{d}(L,v)\mathbb{L}^{d-2h(\underline{v})}T^{|v|},

where
h(\underline{v})=\textup{codim}_{k[[x,y]]}J_{\underline{v}}
as in Section
3.5
.

It seems tempting to try to relate Corollary
1.3
to the arc-Floer conjecture of Budur-de Bobadilla-Lê-Nguyen
[
6
]
, now a theorem for plane curve singularities
[
14
]
, predicting an isomorphism between the compactly supported cohomology of the contact loci
X^{0}_{f,n}
and the fixed point Floer homology of the
n
-th iterate of the monodromy of
F_{0}
. Although we do not know any direct relation between
P_{gel}(T)
and the geometry of the spaces
X^{0}_{f,n}
at the moment, our second main result shows that a closely related integral on
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}
is determined by the motivic Igusa zeta function
[
16
]

Z_{f}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 1}[X^{0}_{f,n}]\mathbb{L}^{-mn}T^{n}.

Theorem 1.4

(
4.2
)
.

For any non-constant
f\in k[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
there exists a natural orbifold form
|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}
on
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}
such that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}}|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}T^{n}=\textup{Exp}\left(\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}Z_{f}(\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{m-3}{2}}T)\right),

where
\textup{Exp}:T\mathcal{C}_{k}[\mathbb{L}^{1/2}][[T]]\to 1+T\mathcal{C}_{k}[\mathbb{L}^{1/2}][[T]]
denotes the plethystic exponential.

For
m=3
the relative Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
provides a crepant resolution of the generic fiber of
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}
and a formula similar to Theorem
1.4
has appeared in
[
38
]
, see also Remark
4.3
.

Finally, for
m=2
, we can relate the two volume forms
|\omega^{gel}_{n}|
and
|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}
on
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}
by means of the formula

\mathbb{L}^{-\mathrm{ord}_{\Delta_{f}}/2}|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}=|\omega^{gel}_{n}|,

where
\Delta_{f}\subset\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}
denotes the discriminant, see Proposition
4.5
. In particular, due to the factor
\mathbb{L}^{-\mathrm{ord}_{\Delta_{f}}/2}
, the series
P_{gel}(T)
does not seem to be determined by the classes
[X^{0}_{f,n}]
. Instead, there is a natural
1
-parameter family

Q_{f}(s,T)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}}|\Delta_{f}|^{s}|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}T^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}[\mathbb{L}^{1/2}][[T,\mathbb{L}^{-s}]],

for some formal parameter
s
, such that

(1.2)

Q_{f}(1/2,T)=P_{gel}(T)\ \text{ and }\ Q_{f}(0,T)=\textup{Exp}\left(\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z_{f}(\mathbb{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}T)\right).

A good understanding of
Q_{f}(s,T)
might give a new way of relating
Z_{f}(T)
to
\zeta_{f,0}(T)
, however, even for a smooth curve computing
Q_{f}(s,T)
is equivalent to computing the Igusa zeta functions of all classical discriminant polynomials, see Section
6
. The latter is an open problem as far as we know, and we hope to come back to it in future work.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
we recall the necessary background on Hilbert schemes and motivic integration. In particular, we discuss Haiman’s charts of the Hilbert scheme of
\mathbb{A}^{2}
which we use in Section
3
to construct the definable form
|\omega_{n}^{gel}|
and prove Theorem
1.4
. In Section
4
we discuss the orbifold formalism for symmetric powers, establish Theorem
1.4
and compare the orbifold form
|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}
with
|\omega_{n}^{gel}|
. In Section
5
we discuss the relation with (symplectic) knot invariants, in particular Corollary
1.3
, and speculate about possible refinements and extensions of our results. Finally, in Section
6
we discuss explicit examples of the series
Q_{f}(s,T)
and its specializations.

2.1.
Hilbert Schemes

Throughout this section we work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
.

2.1.1.
Hilbert scheme of the plane

The Hilbert scheme of the plane
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})
is a smooth and irreducible variety of dimension
2n
. In the work of Haiman
[
25
]
a cover of
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})
by affine charts is constructed as follows:

For any subset
M\subset\mathbb{N}^{2}
of size
n
we may consider the open

U_{M}=\{I\subset k[X,Y]\ |\ \mathrm{span}\langle X^{p}Y^{q}\rangle_{(p,q)\in M}\xrightarrow{\sim}k[X,Y]/I\}.

Any monomial
X^{p}Y^{q}
defines a section of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{T}\to\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})
and the collection
\{X^{p}Y^{q}\}_{(p,q)\in M}
trivializes
\mathcal{T}_{|U_{M}}
. Any
f\in k[X,Y]
defines a global section
f^{[n]}
of
\mathcal{T}
which we can describe on
U_{M}
using this trivialization as follows: for
I\in U_{M}
we may write the image
\overline{f}
of
f
in
k[X,Y]/I
uniquely as

\overline{f}=\sum_{(p,q)\in M}f_{p,q}^{[n]}(I)X^{p}Y^{q}.

If we write
f_{M}^{[n]}:U_{M}\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
for the function
(f_{p_{i}q_{i}}^{[n]})_{1\leq i\leq n}
obtained this way we have
f_{M}^{[n]}=f^{[n]}_{|U_{M}}
in the trivialization given by
\{X^{p}Y^{q}\}_{(p,q)\in M}
. Here we used the inverse lexicographical order on
\mathbb{N}^{2}
, i.e
(a,b)\leq(c,d)
iff
b\leq d
or
b=d
and
a\leq c
, to order the elements
(p_{1},q_{1})<\dots<(p_{n},q_{n})
of
M
.

Now assume
I\in U_{M}
has support (with multiplicities)
(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},y_{n})\in\mathbb{A}^{2}
. Then
f_{M}^{[n]}(I)
is related to the evaluation of
f
at the points
(x_{i},y_{i})
by the matrix
B_{M}(x,y)=(x_{i}^{p_{j}}y_{i}^{q_{j}})_{ij}
i.e.

(2.1)

\begin{pmatrix}f(x_{1},y_{1})\\
f(x_{2},y_{2})\\
\vdots\\
f(x_{n},y_{n})\end{pmatrix}=B_{M}(x,y)\begin{pmatrix}f_{p_{1}q_{1}}^{[n]}(I)\\
f_{p_{2}q_{2}}^{[n]}(I)\\
\vdots\\
f_{p_{n}q_{n}}^{[n]}(I)\end{pmatrix}

We write
\Delta_{M}
for the for the determinant of
B_{M}(x,y)
, which is an alternating function on
(\mathbb{A}^{2})^{n}
.

In what follows we will consider
M=M_{\lambda}
with
M_{\lambda}
the Young diagram of a partition
\lambda\vdash n
and we write
U_{\lambda}
for
U_{M_{\lambda}}
,
f_{\lambda}^{[n]}
for
f_{M_{\lambda}}^{[n]}
etc. In particular, we have an open covering
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})=\bigcup_{\lambda\vdash n}U_{\lambda}
.

\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)=\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(C)

Let
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}=\textup{Spec}(\textup{Sym}(\mathcal{O}^{2}_{S}))

the 0-scheme of a section

\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(C_{S})
is the

-scheme of the section

Proof.

This is a consequence of
[

.
∎

For any
k\geq 1
we write
\mathrm{Hilb}^{\leq k}_{n}(f)\subset\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)
for the open subscheme of ideals

that can be generated by
k

\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)\subset\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)
the

\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)=\{I\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)\ |\ I=(g)\text{ for some }g\in\mathcal{O}_{C}\}.

Lemma 2.3
.

The
1
-generator subscheme
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)
agrees with the smooth locus of
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)

Proof.

This is
[
29
, Proposition 6.5.]
. To elaborate,
the tangent space at
x\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(f)
corresponding to a closed subscheme
Z
is given by
\textup{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
. If
x\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}_{1}(f)
, or equivalently when
Z
is Cartier,
\textup{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})\cong\textup{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{Z}}(\mathcal{O}_{C},\mathcal{O}_{Z})
, which has dimension
n
. So these points are in the smooth locus. Conversely,
if
x\in\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(f)\backslash\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)
, we can assume
Z
is supported at a single point and in this case the completed local ring at that point will be isomorphic to
k[[x,y]]/f
for some
f\in k[[x,y]]
. Lifting
I_{Z}
to
\widetilde{I}_{Z}\subset k[[x,y]]
, there is an exact sequence

0\to\textup{Hom}(I_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})\to\textup{Hom}(\widetilde{I}_{Z},\mathcal{O}_{Z})\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}

In the proof of
[
29
, Proposition 6.5.]
, it is proved that the image of the last map lies in
\mathfrak{m}_{Z}
, the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{Z}
. Since
\dim_{k}\mathfrak{m}_{Z}=n-1
, the exact sequence implies a lower bound

\dim_{k}T_{x}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(f))\geq 2n-n+1=n+1

and hence the points outside the
1
-generator locus are singular.
∎

Finally, we consider for any
k\geq 1
the deformation
f-t^{k}
seen as a subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k[[t]]}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
k\geq 1
. Then any section
s
of the relative Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(f-t^{k})\to\textup{Spec}(k[[t]])
evaluates to an ideal with at most
k
-generators:
s_{|\textup{Spec}(k)}\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{\leq k}(f)
.

Proof.

The local structure ring of the ambient space is
R=(k[x,y]\otimes k[t])_{\mathfrak{m}}
and
\mathfrak{m}=(x,y,t)
is the maximal ideal.
Having a section
s
is equivalent to having an ideal
I\subset R
such that
f-t^{k}\in I
and
R/I
is flat over
k[[t]]
of length
n
.
Thus
R/I
is finite rank over
k[[t]]
and since
\textup{Spec}(k[[t]])
is regular, we conclude that
R/I
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Since
R/I
is Cohen-Macaulay and is structure ring of one-dimensional scheme, we have
\mathrm{depth}(R/I)=1
and by the Auslander-Buchsbaum theorem
[
19
, Theorem A 2.15]
we conclude that the projective dimension of
R/I
is
2
.
Hence
I
admits two step free resolution and
by the Hilbert-Burch theorem
[
19
, Theorem 3.2]
there is
g
by
g+1
matrix
A
with entries in
\mathfrak{m}
such that we have short exact sequence:

0\to R^{g}\xrightarrow{A}R^{g+1}\xrightarrow{G}I\to 0.

where
G
is the row of the minors of
A
and
g+1
is the minimal number of
generators of
I
.

Next, we observe that
f-t^{k}\in\mathfrak{m}^{k}\setminus\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}
and that the entries
of
G
are in
\mathfrak{m}^{g}
. Hence we conclude that
g\leq k
and
I
has

g+1
generators
h_{1},\dots,h_{g+1}
. If
g<k
then the statement is proven
otherwise we need to argue explain why
f-t^{k}
is one of the generators
h_{i}
.

Indeed, if
g=k
then
h_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}t^{j}\alpha_{j}^{i}
,
\alpha_{j}^{i}\in\mathfrak{m}^{n-j}
. On the other hand
f-t^{k}=\sum_{i}\beta_{i}h_{i}
,
\beta_{i}\in R
and hence
at least one of
\beta_{i}
is not vanishing modulo
\mathfrak{m}
. In particular,

\beta_{i}
is a unit in
R
and
h_{i}=(f-t^{k})\beta_{i}^{-1}-\sum_{j\neq i}h_{i}\beta_{j}/\beta_{i}
and
I
is generated by
f-t^{k}
and
h_{j}
,
j\neq i
.
∎

2.5.

2.5.1.
Grothendieck Rings

For any
k
-variety
Z
we write
K_{0}(Var_{Z})
for the relative Grothendieck ring of varieties over
Z
and
\mathbb{L}
for the class of
\mathbb{A}^{1}\times Z\to Z
. We further write
\mathcal{M}_{Z}
for the localized ring
K_{0}(Var_{Z})[\mathbb{L}^{-1}]
and
\mathcal{C}_{Z}=\mathcal{M}_{Z}[(1-\mathbb{L}^{i})^{-1}:i\geq 1]
. If
Z=\textup{Spec}(k)
we simply write
K_{0}(Var_{k})
,

and
\mathcal{C}_{k}

In
[

the authors construct power structures on

and
\mathcal{M}_{k}
and in particular plethystic operations. For
\mathcal{R}
either

or
\mathcal{M}_{k}
, the plethystic exponential is a group homomorphism

\textup{Exp}:T\mathcal{R}[[T]]\to 1+T\mathcal{R}[[T]],

defined by the formula

\textup{Exp}(a_{1}T+a_{2}T^{2}+\dots)=\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-T^{n})^{-a_{n}}.

Here, if
a=[X]\in\mathcal{R}
is the class of a variety, then

(1-T)^{-a}=\zeta_{X}(T)=\sum_{m\geq 0}[\textup{Sym}_{m}X]T^{m}

is the Kapranov zeta function of

[
28
]

\textup{Sym}_{m}X

m
-th symmetric power of
X
defined by

\textup{Sym}_{m}X=X\times X\times\dots\times X/S_{m},

where

denotes the

-th symmetric group. We extend this by defining the
m
-th symmetric product
\textup{Sym}_{m}(a)
of any element

by

\textup{Exp}(aT)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\textup{Sym}_{m}(a)T^{m}.

By
[
22
, Statement 3]
we have in particular
\textup{Sym}_{m}(\mathbb{L}^{\pm 1}a)=\mathbb{L}^{\pm m}\textup{Sym}_{m}(a)
. Following
[
12
, Appendix B]
one can further extend
Exp
and thus
\textup{Sym}_{m}
for

, to the ring

\mathcal{C}_{k}[\mathbb{L}^{1/2}]=\mathcal{M}_{k}[\mathbb{L}^{1/2},(1-\mathbb{L}^{i})^{-1}:i\geq 1],

in such a way that
\textup{Sym}_{m}(\mathbb{L}^{\pm 1/2}a)=\mathbb{L}^{\pm m/2}\textup{Sym}_{m}(a)
. Here
\mathbb{L}^{1/2}
corresponds to the
negative square root
in
loc. cit.

2.5.2.
Order function and absolute values

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
k[[t]]
-scheme and
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{X}
a closed subscheme with ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
. For any
x\in\mathcal{X}(k[[t]])
we define the order
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{Z}}(x)
of
x
along
\mathcal{Z}
as

\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{Z}}(x)=\begin{cases}d\ &\text{ if }x^{*}\mathcal{I}=(t^{d})\\
\infty\ &\text{ if }x^{*}\mathcal{I}=(0).\end{cases}

Notice that
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{Z}}(x)=\infty
if and only if
x\in\mathcal{Z}(k[[t]])
. If
\mathcal{X}
is affine and
\mathcal{I}
generated by
z_{1},\dots,z_{k}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
, then
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{Z}}(x)=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\{\mathrm{ord}_{t}z_{i}(x)\}
.

There is a unique way of extending
\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{Z}}
to a function

\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{Z}}:\mathcal{X}(\overline{k[[t]]})=\bigcup_{r\geq 1}\mathcal{X}(k[[t^{1/r}]])\to\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}.

Given a subscheme
\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{X}
and
y\in\mathcal{Y}(\overline{k[[t]]})
we will use that

\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{Z}\cap\mathcal{Y}}(y)=\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{Z}}(y),

where on the right we consider
y
as an
\overline{k[[t]]}
-point of
\mathcal{X}
.

Finally, given a regular map
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
or a subscheme
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{X}
we write
|f|
and
|\mathcal{Z}|
for the maps
\mathcal{X}(k[[t]])\to\mathcal{M}_{k}
defined by

(2.2)

x\mapsto\mathbb{L}^{-\mathrm{ord}_{t}(f(x))}\text{ and }x\mapsto\mathbb{L}^{-\mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{Z}}(x)}.

When working with
k[[t^{1/n}]]
-points for some
n\geq 1
we will write
|\cdot|_{n}
to indicate that we compute the absolute value with respect to the uniformizer
t^{1/n}
.

2.5.3.
Cluckers-Loeser integration

We use the integration theory developed in
[
10
]
. As in
[
10
, 16.2]
and in the notation of
[
30
]
we consider the category
\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{acf},k}
of definable subassignments in the Denef-Pas language restricted to the theory of algebraically closed fields.

Concretely, an object
h\in\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{acf},k}
is given by an assignment

K\mapsto h(K)\subset k((t))^{n}\times k^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{r},

where
K
runs through all algebraically closed fields containing
k
,
m,n,r\geq 0
and
h(K)
is cut out by a formula in the Denef-Pas language over
k
. In particular any affine finite type scheme
X\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}
over
k((t))
defines an element in
\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{acf},k}
, which we still denote by
X
, by means of

K\mapsto X(K((t)))\subset K((t))^{n}\times K^{0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{0}.

Similarly, for an affine finite type scheme
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}
over
k[[t]]
we still write
\mathcal{X}
for the assignment

K\mapsto\mathcal{X}(K[[t]]).

Finally, we will sometimes consider the assignments
X_{n},\mathcal{X}_{n}
given by
K\mapsto X(K((t^{1/n})))
or
K\mapsto\mathcal{X}(K[[t^{1/n}]])
for some integer
n\geq 1
. Notice that these are simply the assignments associated to the Weil restrictions of
X\times_{k((t))}k((t^{1/n}))
and
X\times_{k[[t]]}k[[t^{1/n}]]
along
k((t))\to k((t^{1/n}))
and
k[[t]]\to k[[t^{1/n}]]
respectively.

These constructions can be globalized to obtain a category
\mathrm{GDef}_{\mathrm{acf},k}
consisting of definable subassignments of algebraic varieties.

To any
S\in\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{acf},k}
one can associate a ring of constructible motivic functions
\mathcal{C}(S)
in such a way that for the terminal object
*_{k}\in\mathrm{Def}_{\mathrm{acf},k}
we have

\mathcal{C}(*_{k})=\mathcal{C}_{k}=K_{0}(Var_{k})[\mathbb{L}^{-1},(1-\mathbb{L}^{i})^{-1}:i\geq 1].

Typical examples of constructible motivic functions are the maps in (
2.2
).

The content of
[
10
]
is an integration formalism for constructible motivic functions with respect to definable volume forms satisfying in particular a change of variables formula and a Fubini theorem.

The forms one integrates in this theory are definable forms
|\omega|
as defined in
[
10
, Section 8.2]
. On a definable subassignment associated with a
k((t))
-variety
X
any top-dimensional form
\omega
on the smooth locus
X^{sm}
of
X
defines a definable form
|\omega|
and we have an identification
|\omega|=|\omega^{\prime}|
if
\omega=f\omega^{\prime}
for some
f:X^{sm}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
with
|f|\equiv 1
.

The definable forms in this paper will thus typically be constructed as follows. Given
X/k((t))
, an open cover
X^{sm}=\bigcup_{i}U_{i}
of its smooth locus and forms
\omega_{i}
on
U_{i}
such than on
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
we have
\omega_{i}=f_{ij}\omega_{j}
for a function
f_{ij}
satisfying
|f_{ij}|\equiv 1
, then the
U_{i}
glue together to a definable form on
X
.

Given a function
f\in\mathcal{C}(X)
, integrable with respect to a definable form
|\omega|
, one obtains a class

\int_{X}f|\omega|\in\mathcal{C}_{k}.

In the following if we write
\int_{X}f|\omega|=a
we mean
f
is integrable with respect to
|\omega|
and
\int_{X}f|\omega|=a
.

One way to compute motivic integrals is by means of Néron smoothenings. Given
\mathcal{X}/k[[t]]
separated, flat and of finite type with smooth generic fiber, there exists a smooth finite type
k[[t]]
-scheme
\mathcal{X}
and a
k[[t]]
-morphism
\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
, which is generically an isomorphism and for every extension
K/k
induces a bijection
\mathcal{Y}(K[[t]])\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{X}(K[[t]])

[
5
, Theorem 3.1.3]
. For any volume form
\omega
on
\mathcal{X}_{k((t))}
one then has
[
11
, Proposition 12.6]

(2.3)

\int_{\mathcal{X}}|\omega|=\mathbb{L}^{-\dim\mathcal{X}}\sum_{C\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{X}_{k})}\mathbb{L}^{-\mathrm{ord}_{C}\omega}[C].

2.5.4.
Motivic Igusa Zeta functions

Consider a non-constant polynomial map
f:\mathbb{A}^{m}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
over
k
with
f(0)=0
. The (local)
n
-th restricted contact locus of
f
is defined as

(2.4)

X^{0}_{f,n}=\{x\in\mathbb{A}^{m}(k[t]/t^{n+1})_{0}\ |\ f(x)=t^{n}\in k[t]/t^{n+1}\},

where
\mathbb{A}^{m}(k[t]/t^{n+1})_{0}
denotes the locus of jets reducing to
0
modulo
t
.

The
motivic Igusa zeta function of
f
is defined as the generating series

(2.5)

Z_{f}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 1}[X^{0}_{f,n}]\mathbb{L}^{-mn}T^{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{k}[[T]].

In fact, using an embedded resolution of singularities one can show that
Z_{f}(T)\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(T)

[
16
]
. There is an additional
\mu_{n}
-action on each
X^{0}_{f,n}
but we will ignore it in this paper.

Our goal is to give an expression for the plethystic exponential
\textup{Exp}(Z_{f}(T))\in\mathcal{C}_{k}[[T]]
. For this we’ll need a slightly different way of writing the Igusa zeta function following
[
33
]
.

Let
\omega_{m}=dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{m}
be the standard form on
\mathbb{A}^{m}
and consider the relative
m-1
-form
\omega_{m}/df
on the smooth locus of
f
.

Next consider the
k[[t]]
-scheme
\mathcal{X}_{f}
obtained as the pullback of
\mathbb{A}^{m}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbb{A}^{1}
under the completion morphism
\textup{Spec}(k[[t]])\to\textup{Spec}(k[t])
, explicitly

\mathcal{X}_{f}=\textup{Spec}(k[[t]][x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]/f-t).

Then
\omega_{m}/df
induces a volume form
\omega_{f}
on the generic fiber of
\mathcal{X}_{f}
called the
Gelfand-Leray form
.

For any
n\geq 1
we write
\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f,n}
for the definable subassignment whose
K
-points are given by
\{x\in\mathcal{X}_{f}(K[[t^{1/n}]])\ |\ x_{K}=0\}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
33
, Lemma 9.9]
For any
n\geq 1
we have

\int_{\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f,n}}|\omega_{f}|_{n}=\mathbb{L}^{-(n+1)(m-1)}[X^{0}_{f,n}]\in\mathcal{C}_{k}.

The proof of Lemma
2.6
uses an embedded resolution of singularities
f
to compute both sides independently. Technically a different integration theory is used in
loc. cit.
, but the same argument can be applied in the Cluckers-Loeser theory using (
2.3
).

3.
Arcs and Gelfand forms on Hilbert schemes

Let
f\in k[X,Y]
be reduced and nonconstant with an isolated singularity at
0
. We write
C=\{f=0\}\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}
for the associated curve. Consider the relative curve
\mathcal{X}_{f}/k[[t]]
as in Section
2.5.4
and the relative Hilbert-Chow morphism

h:\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})\to\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}).

Since the generic fiber of
\mathcal{X}_{f}
is smooth,
h_{|k((t))}
is an isomorphism. Furthermore
h
is proper and thus we have for every
K/k
and every
K[[t]]
-point of
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
a unique lift to a
K[[t]]
-point of
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
, in other words the definable subassignments associated with
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
and
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
are isomorphic.

Recall that
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)\subset\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)
denotes the
1
-generator locus. We further write
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}=\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)\cap h^{-1}(n[0])
for the closed subscheme of ideals supported at the origin. As a direct consequence of Lemma
2.4
we have

Corollary 3.1
.

For any
K/k
and any
x\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})(K[[t]])
such that
(h_{*}x)_{|K}=n[0]\in\textup{Sym}_{n}(C)
we have
x_{|k}\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}
.

Next we use Corollary
3.1
to construct a definable volume form on
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0})
, the definable subassignment given by

K\mapsto\{x\in\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})(K[[t]])\ |\ x_{K}=n[0]\}.

First, by Corollary
3.1
we have an isomorphism of
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0})
with
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0})
defined by

K\mapsto\{x\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})(K[[t]])\ |\ x_{|K}\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(C)_{0}\}.

Next recall the open covering
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})=\bigcup_{\lambda\vdash n}U_{\lambda}
from Section
2.1.1
. Since
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})\subset\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})
we obtain an open covering of
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
by setting
U_{f,\lambda}=U_{\lambda}\cap\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
. By Lemma
2.2
we can also describe
U_{f,\lambda}
as

U_{f,\lambda}=\{f^{[n]}_{\lambda}=0\}\subset U_{\lambda}.

Now since
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})
is algebraic symplectic there exists in particular a global non-vanishing top-form
\omega^{[n]}
. We define the
Gelfand form

\omega_{\lambda}^{gel}
on the smooth locus
U_{f,\lambda}^{sm}\subset U_{f,\lambda}
as

\omega_{\lambda}^{gel}=\omega^{[n]}/f_{\lambda}^{[n]*}\nu,

where
\nu=dy_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dy_{n}
is the standard form on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The definable forms
|\omega_{\lambda}^{gel}|
glue together to a give a non-vanishing definable form
|\omega^{gel}_{n}|
on
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0})\cong\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0})

Proof.

For any two partitions
\lambda,\mu\vdash n
the two forms
\omega_{\lambda}^{gel},\omega_{\mu}^{gel}
differ by an invertible function
u_{\lambda\mu}
on
U_{f,\lambda}^{sm}\cap U_{f,\mu}^{sm}
. By combining Lemma
2.3
and Corollary
3.1
we see that for any
K/k
, any
x\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0})(K[[t]])
factors through the smooth locus of
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
and thus
|u_{\lambda\mu}(x)|=1
which implies the proposition.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For any
n\geq 1
we have

\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}}|\omega^{gel}_{n}|=\mathbb{L}^{-n}[\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}].

Proof.

Since
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0})\cong\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0})
this follows from (
2.3
).
∎

We also record the following for later.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\in U_{\lambda}(K[[t]])
and
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{A}^{2})^{n}(\overline{K((t))})
a preimage of
\textup{supp}(\mathcal{I}_{|K((t))})\in\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})(K((t)))
under the Hilbert-Chow morphism. Then

\mathrm{ord}_{t}(\Delta_{\lambda}(x,y))\leq\mathrm{ord}_{t}(\Delta_{M}(x,y)),

for all
M\subset\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
|M|=n
.

Proof.

This follows since on
U_{\lambda}
the fraction
\Delta_{M}/\Delta_{\lambda}
defines a regular function
[
26
, Proposition 2.6.]
, thus
\Delta_{M}(x,y)/\Delta_{\lambda}(x,y)\in K[[t]]
.
∎

3.5.
The motivic Poincaré series

In this subsection we assume
k=\mathbb{C}
. Consider the generating series

P_{gel}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}}|\omega^{gel}_{n}|T^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}[[T]].

We will show that
P_{gel}(T)
equals the motivic Poincaré series of
f
introduced in
[
8
]
.

The motivic Poincaré series of
[
8
]
is defined as follows when
m=2
. If the germ
(C,0)
defined by
f
contains
r
branches, we can define a multi–index filtration on
k[[x,y]]
by setting

J_{\underline{v}}=\{g\in k[[x,y]]|v_{i}(g)\geq v_{i}\}

where
\underline{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
and
v_{i}(g)
is the order of the pullback of
f
to the normalization of the
i
-th branch of
C
. Let
h(\underline{v})=\textup{codim}_{k[[x,y]]}J_{\underline{v}}
and write

L_{f}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r},\mathbb{L}^{-1})=\sum_{\underline{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}\frac{\mathbb{L}^{-h(\underline{v})}-\mathbb{L}^{-h(\underline{v}+\underline{1})}}{1-\mathbb{L}^{-1}}t^{\underline{v}}

By
[
8
]
one can define the motivic Poincaré series of
f
as

P_{f}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r};\mathbb{L})=\frac{L_{f}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r},\mathbb{L}^{-1})\prod_{i=1}^{r}(t_{i}-1)}{t_{1}\cdots t_{r}-1}

In particular, when
r=1
and the curve is unibranch, we have
L_{f}(t,\mathbb{L}^{-1})=P_{f}(t,\mathbb{L}^{-1})
.

Our interest in the motivic Poincaré series is encapsulated in

Proposition 3.6
.

P_{f}(t_{1}=\dots=t_{r}=T,\mathbb{L}^{-1})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{L}^{-n}[\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}]T^{n}=P_{gel}(T).

Proof.

As explained in
[
21
, Section 3]
, the coefficient of
t^{v}=t_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{v_{r}}
in
P_{f}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r},\mathbb{L}^{-1})
is given by

(3.1)

\pi_{v}(\mathbb{L}^{-1})=\mathbb{L}^{1-h(v+\underline{1})}[\mathbb{P}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(v)],

where
[\mathbb{P}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(v)]
is the motive of the projectivization of the hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(v)
defined by

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(v):=\left.\frac{J_{v}}{J_{v+\underline{1}}}\middle\backslash\bigcup_{i}\frac{J_{v+e_{i}}}{J_{v+\underline{1}}}\right.

where
e_{i}
is the
i
-th basis vector.

Next, note that any nonzero function not proportional to a factor of
f
gives a finite codimension ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{C}=k[[x,y]]/f
. Note that these are exactly the functions with finite valuation on each component of
C
. We can call these regular elements and denote them
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{reg}
. For any
g\in\mathcal{O}_{C}^{reg}
one has

(3.2)

\dim\mathcal{O}_{C}/(g)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}(g),

which follows from the additivity of intersection numbers of plane curves or from the following direct argument:
Let
\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}
be the local rings of the branches and the projections
\pi_{i}:\mathcal{O}_{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}

yield an embedding
\pi:\mathcal{O}_{C}\to\oplus_{i}\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}
. The cokernel of the embedding is of finite length and
\pi(g)
is non-zero divisor for
g\in\mathcal{O}_{C}^{reg}
hence
we apply
[
35
, Proposition 6]
for
A=\mathcal{O}_{C}
and
B=\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}
and
f=g
. Thus showed that
\dim(\mathcal{O}_{C}/(g))=\sum_{i}\dim(\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}/(\pi_{i}(g))).

Finally,
\pi_{i}(g)
is not a zero-divisor hence
the equality
\dim(\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}/(\pi_{i}(g)))=v_{i}(g)
follows from applying
[
35
, Proposition 6]
to the inclusion

\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}\to k[[t]]
.

Now there is a map
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{reg}\to\bigsqcup_{n}\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}

sending
g\mapsto I=(g)
and the only ambiguity in defining this principal ideal is
multiplication by invertible functions in
\mathcal{O}_{C}
, which gives a constructible
bijection
(J_{v}\backslash\bigcup_{i}J_{v+e_{i}})/\mathcal{O}_{C}^{*}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Hilb}^{1}_{v}(f)_{0}
, where
\mathrm{Hilb}^{1}_{v}(f)_{0}
is the subset of
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}
where the
valuations on the components correspond to
v
.

On the other hand there is a fibration

(J_{v}\backslash\bigcup_{i}J_{v+e_{i}})/\mathcal{O}_{C}^{*}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(v)

whose fiber over
[g]\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(v)
can be identified with
g+J_{v+\underline{1}}/1+\mathfrak{m}_{C}
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{C}\subset\mathcal{O}_{C}
denotes the maximal ideal.

Let us show that the quotient
g+J_{v+\underline{1}}/1+\mathfrak{m}_{C}
has the class
\mathbb{L}^{d(v)}
where
d(v)
is the dimension of
J_{v+\underline{1}}/g\mathfrak{m}_{C}
. Indeed, the linear
space
\mathfrak{m}_{C}J_{v+\underline{1}}
acts by shifts on the affine space
g+J_{v+\underline{1}}
and for any
k\geq 0
there is a well-defined projection map:

\pi_{k}:\left((g+J_{v+\underline{1}})/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}_{C}J_{v+\underline{1}})\right)/(1+\mathfrak{m}_{C})\to\left((g+J_{v+\underline{1}}/\mathfrak{m}^{k}_{C}J_{v+\underline{1}}\right)/(1+\mathfrak{m}_{C}).

The fibers of the map
\pi_{k}
are isomorphic to the affine space

V_{k}=(\mathfrak{m}^{k}_{C}J_{v+\underline{1}}/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}_{C}J_{v+\underline{1}})/(g\mathfrak{m}_{C}\cap\mathfrak{m}_{C}^{k}J_{v+\underline{1}})

by the following argument.
Let us pick
\bar{h}\in J_{v+\underline{1}}/\mathfrak{m}_{C}^{k}J_{v+\underline{1}}
and
h,h^{\prime}\in J_{v+\underline{1}}/\mathfrak{m}_{C}^{k+1}J_{v+\underline{1}}\
are two lifts of

\bar{h}
. Then
g+h
and
g+h^{\prime}
are in the same
1+\mathfrak{m}_{C}
-orbit if and only
h-h^{\prime}=gm
,
m\in\mathfrak{m}_{C}
, and the statement follows.
To complete the argument we observe that
V_{k}
is a point for large
k
and
d(v)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\dim(V_{k})
.

To finish the proof we observe that
d(v)=\dim J_{v+\underline{1}}/g\mathfrak{m}_{C}=|v|+1-h(v+\underline{1})
, which follows from the short exact sequence

0\to J_{v+\underline{1}}/g\mathfrak{m}_{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{C}/g\mathfrak{m}_{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{C}/J_{v+\underline{1}}\to 0,

since
\dim\mathcal{O}_{C}/J_{v+\underline{1}}=h(v+\underline{1})
by definition and and
\dim\mathcal{O}_{C}/g\mathfrak{m}_{C}=|v|+1
by (
3.2
).

∎

3.7.
The Euler characteristic specialization

In this subsection we assume
k=\mathbb{C}
. Consider the generating series

P_{gel}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}}|\omega^{gel}_{n}|T^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}[[T]].

By Theorem
3.3
we have
P_{gel}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{L}^{-n}[\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}]T^{n}
and thus
P_{gel}(T)
lies in the image of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{C}}[[T]]\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{C}}[[T]]
. In particular, we may consider its Euler-characteristic specialization

P_{gel,\chi}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\chi(\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0})T^{n}.

If
L
denotes the link defined by the plane curve singularity
(f,0)
and
A_{L}(T)\in 1+T\mathbb{Z}[T]
the normalized Alexander polynomial, it follows from
[
35
, Section 3]
that

P_{gel,\chi}(T)=\frac{A_{L}(T)}{1-T}.

On the other hand, since
|\omega_{n}^{gel}|
is non-vanishing and the generic fiber of
\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}
is smooth, the Euler-characteristic specialization of
\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}}|\omega_{n}^{gel}|
equals the Euler-characteristic specialization of the motivic Serre invariant of
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}

[
33
, Section 6.1]
. Using this, we deduce from the trace formula
[
33
, Theorem 5.4]

\chi\left(\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}}|\omega_{n}^{gel}|\right)=Tr(\phi,H_{B}^{*}(\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}),\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}Tr(\phi,H_{B}^{i}(\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}),\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}),

where
H_{B}^{*}(\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}),\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
denotes the Berkovich étale cohomology of the rigid analytic space associated to
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0})
and
\phi
is the topological generator of
\textup{Gal}(\mathbb{C}((t)))\cong\hat{\mu}
given by
t^{1/n}\mapsto\exp(2\pi i/n)t^{1/n}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

We have

H_{B}^{*}(\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}),\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))\cong(H_{B}^{*}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})^{\otimes n})^{S_{n}},

as graded
\hat{\mu}
-modules.

Proof.

Instead of working directly with Berkovich étale cohomology we use the comparison with
\ell
-adic nearby cycles
[
4
, Corollary 3.5]
implying

\displaystyle H_{B}^{*}(\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0}),\mathbb{Q}_{\ell,\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})})

\displaystyle\cong H^{*}(R\psi_{\eta}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell,\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})}))_{|n[0]}),

\displaystyle H_{B}^{*}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell,\mathcal{X}_{f}})

\displaystyle\cong H^{*}(R\psi_{\eta}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell,\mathcal{X}_{f}})_{|0}).

Here we wrote
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell,\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell,\mathcal{X}_{f}}
for the constant sheaves on (the generic fibers of)
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
and
\mathcal{X}_{f}
. Therefor it suffices to show that we have a quasi-isomorphism

R\psi_{\eta}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell,\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})})\cong(\pi_{*}\boxtimes^{n}R\psi_{\eta}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell,\mathcal{X}_{f}}))^{S_{n}}

in
D^{b}_{c}(\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})_{|k})
, where
\boxtimes
denotes the exterior product on
\mathcal{X}_{f}^{n}
. Notice that taking
S_{n}
-invariants can be defined via a projector as for example in
[
31
]
and thus
R\psi_{\eta}
commutes with taking
S_{n}
-invariants. Furthermore
R\psi_{\eta}
commutes with proper pushforward
[
15

and exterior products
[

. The lemma thus follows from the identity
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell,\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})}\cong(\pi_{*}\boxtimes^{n}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell,\mathcal{X}_{f}})^{S_{n}}

∎

Combining Lemma

P_{gel,\chi}(T)=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{Tr(\phi^{n},H^{*}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))}{n}T^{n}\right).

Finally choosing an embedding
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{C}
, we deduce from

the equality

(3.3)

\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{Tr(\phi^{n},H^{*}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}))}{n}T^{n}\right)=\zeta_{f,0}(T),

where
\zeta_{f,0}(T)
denotes the monodromy zeta function of the Milnor fiber of
f
at

. Putting all of this together we find

.

For any reduced plance curve singularity
(f,0)
with link

\zeta_{f,0}(T)=\frac{A_{L}(T)}{1-T}.

In

[

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
,
V^{*}
a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
k
-vector space and
\phi^{*}
an endomorphism of
V^{*}
preserving the grading. Then

\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{Tr((\phi^{*})^{n},V^{*})}{n}T^{n}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}Tr((\phi^{*})^{\otimes n},((V^{*})^{\otimes n})^{S_{n}})T^{n}=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\det(1-\phi^{i}T)^{(-1)^{i+1}},

where for a graded endomorphism
\psi^{*}
of a finite dimensional graded
k
-vector space
W^{*}
we wrote
Tr(\psi^{*},W^{*})=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}Tr(\psi^{i},W^{i})
.

Proof.

When there is no grading and
\phi
is diagonalizable with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}
, we have

\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{p_{n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})}{n}T^{n}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})T^{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{i}T}=\frac{1}{\det(1-\phi T)}

The general ungraded case can be deduced from the Zariski density of diagonalizable
\varphi
.

To upgrade to the graded case, we simply note the well–known identities
p_{n}(-\lambda_{1},\ldots,-\lambda_{m})=(-1)^{n}p_{n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
,
h_{n}(-\lambda_{1},\ldots,-\lambda_{m})=(-1)^{n}e_{n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
and
\sum_{n\geq 0}e_{n}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})T^{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1+\lambda_{i}T)
and apply these for the odd graded pieces.

∎

4.
The orbifold measure on symmetric powers

Let
\mathcal{X}/k[[t]]
be separated, flat and of finite type with smooth
d
-dimensional generic fiber. Furthermore let
0\in\mathcal{X}_{k}
be a point in the special fiber of
\mathcal{X}
and define
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}^{0})
as the definable subassignment whose
K
points are given by
\{x\in\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X})(K[[t]])\ |\ x_{K}=n[0]\}
as before.

Let
p_{i}:\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{X}
denote the projection onto the
i
-th coordinate. Given an algebraic
d
-form
\omega
on
\mathcal{X}_{k((t))}
, consider the
dn
-form
\omega_{n}=p_{1}^{*}\omega\wedge\dots\wedge p_{n}^{*}\omega
on
\mathcal{X}_{k((t))}^{n}
. For any
\sigma\in S_{n}
we have
\sigma^{*}\omega_{n}=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)^{d}\omega_{n}
. Thus
\omega_{n}
does not descend to a form on
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{k((t))}
unless
d
is even.

Instead of
\omega_{n}
we thus consider its tensor-square
\omega_{n}^{\otimes 2}\in H^{0}\left(\mathcal{X}_{k((t))}^{n},(\Omega_{\mathcal{X}_{k((t))}^{n}/k((t))}^{dn})^{\otimes 2}\right)
. This form now descends to the
orbifold form

\omega_{orb}
on
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}_{k((t))}
and for any choice of square root
\mathbb{L}^{1/2}
of
\mathbb{L}
we obtain, assuming integrability, a well-defined class

\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}}|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}[\mathbb{L}^{1/2}].

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
|\omega|_{n}
is integrable on
\mathcal{X}^{0}_{n}
for each
n\geq 1
. Then we have

\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}}|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}=\sum_{\lambda=(1^{a_{1}},2^{a_{2}},\dots)\vdash n}\mathbb{L}^{-v(\lambda)}\prod_{i\geq 1}\textup{Sym}_{a_{i}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}^{0}_{i}}|\omega|_{i}\right),

where
v(\lambda)=\frac{d}{2}\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}(i-1)
.

Proof.

Since everything depends only on a neighborhood of
0
we may assume that
\mathcal{X}
is affine. Let us assume first that
0\in\mathcal{X}
is a smooth point of
\mathcal{X}/k[[t]]
. Then the completed local ring
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X},0}
is isomorphic to
k[[t]][[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]]
. Since
\omega
is generically non-vanishing we may write
\omega=\lambda dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{d}
in these local coordinates for some
\lambda\in k[[t]]
. Then
|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}=|\lambda|^{n}|(dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{d})_{orb}|^{1/2}
and also
|\omega|_{i}=|\lambda|^{i}|dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{d}|
on
\mathcal{X}^{0}_{i}
. Thus after multiplying by
|\lambda|^{-n}
on both sides we may assume that
\omega=dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{d}
. In this case the proposition reduces to the orbifold formula for
S_{n}
acting on
(\mathbb{A}^{d})^{n}
as it is proven for example in
[
18
, Theorem 3.6]
.
In the notation of
loc. cit
we have

\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{m})^{0}}|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}=\sum_{[\sigma]\in\mathrm{Conj}(S_{n})}\mathbb{L}^{-w(\sigma)},

where
\mathrm{Conj}(S_{n})
denotes conjugacy classes in
S_{n}
and the weight
w(\sigma)
is defined as follows: Let
r
denote the order of
\sigma
and
\xi
a primitive
r
-th root of unity. Then there are unique integers
1\leq e_{\sigma,i}\leq r
for
1\leq i\leq dn
such that
\xi^{e_{\sigma,i}}
are the eigenvalues for the action of
\sigma
on
(\mathbb{A}^{d})^{n}
. If
\sigma
has cycle-type given by the partition
\lambda=(1^{a_{1}},2^{a_{2}},\dots)
then one gets the the formula

w(\sigma)=\frac{d}{2}\sum_{i}a_{i}(i+1)=v(\lambda)+d\sum_{i}a_{i}.

Since in this case
\textup{Sym}_{a_{i}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}^{0}_{i}}|\omega|_{i}\right)=\textup{Sym}_{a_{i}}\mathbb{L}^{-d}=\mathbb{L}^{-da_{i}}
the statement follows.

If
0\in\mathcal{X}
is not a smooth point, we may choose by
[
33
, Proposition 2.5]
a resolution of singularities, that is a regular, flat
k[[t]]
-variety
\mathcal{Y}
with
\mathcal{Y}_{k}=\sum_{i}N_{i}E_{i}
a strict normal crossing divisor, and a proper birational morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
which is an isomorphism on generic fibers. Regularity implies that for any
K/k
, any
K[[t]]
-point of
\mathcal{Y}
factors through the smooth locus of
\mathcal{Y}

[
5
, Proposition 3.2]
. By
[
33
, Lemma 4.3, Theorem 4.5]
we may further assume that the same holds true for
K[[t^{1/i}]]
-points for all
1\leq i\leq n
. The proposition thus reduces to the smooth case proven above.
∎

If we apply this to
\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}
and
\omega_{f}
with
f\colon\mathbb{A}^{m}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
as in Section
2.5.4
we get the following interpretation of the plethystic exponential of the motivic Igusa zeta function:

Theorem 4.2
.

\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}}|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}T^{n}=\textup{Exp}\left(\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}Z_{f}(\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{m-3}{2}}T)\right).

Proof.

Combining Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
2.6
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}}|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda=(1^{a_{1}},2^{a_{2}},\dots)\vdash n}\mathbb{L}^{-v(\lambda)}\prod_{i\geq 1}\textup{Sym}_{a_{i}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f,i}}|\omega_{f}|_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda=(1^{a_{1}},2^{a_{2}},\dots)\vdash n}\mathbb{L}^{-v(\lambda)}\prod_{i\geq 1}\textup{Sym}_{a_{i}}\left(\mathbb{L}^{-(i+1)(m-1)}[X^{0}_{f,i}]\right).

Thus for the generating series we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}}

\displaystyle|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}T^{n}=\sum_{\lambda=(1^{a_{1}},2^{a_{2}},\dots)}\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{m-1}{2}\sum a_{i}(i-1)}\prod_{i\geq 1}\textup{Sym}_{a_{i}}\left(\mathbb{L}^{-(i+1)(m-1)}[X^{0}_{f,i}]\right)T^{\sum a_{i}i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\geq 1}\left(\sum_{a\geq 0}\textup{Sym}_{a}\left(\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{(m-1)(3i+1)}{2}}[X^{0}_{f,i}]\right)T^{ia}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\geq 1}\textup{Exp}(\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{(m-1)(3i+1)}{2}}[X^{0}_{f,i}]T^{i})

\displaystyle=\textup{Exp}\left(\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}\sum_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{L}^{-im}[X^{0}_{f,i}](\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{m-3}{2}}T)^{i}\right)=\textup{Exp}\left(\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}Z_{f}(\mathbb{L}^{-\frac{m-3}{2}}T)\right).

∎

Remark 4.3
.

If we take
f\in k[x,y,z]
, then the generic fiber of
\mathcal{X}_{f}
is a smooth surface and
h:\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f|k((t))})\to\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f|k((t))})
is a crepant resolution. Thus by the change of variables formula

\sum_{n\geq 0}\int_{\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f})}|h^{*}\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}T^{n}=\textup{Exp}\left(\mathbb{L}^{-1}Z_{f}(T)\right).

It would be interesting to see, if the left hand side admits a description in term of the Hilbert scheme of the surface singularity
f=0
similar to Theorem
3.3
.

4.4.
Relation to the Gelfand form

Let
f\in k[X,Y]
be an isolated plane curve singularity as in Section
3
. In this section we give a precise relation relation between
|\omega_{orb}|
and
|\omega^{gel}_{n}|
on
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}
.

For this consider the discriminant subscheme
\tilde{\Delta}\subset(\mathbb{A}^{2})^{n}
given by

\widetilde{\Delta}=\bigcup_{i\neq j}\{x_{i}=x_{j},y_{i}=y_{j}\}.

It follows from the results of Haiman, see e.g.
[
24
]
, that the ideal
J
defining
\widetilde{\Delta}
equals the ideal generated by the diagonally alternating polynomials on
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}
and is generated by the functions
\{\Delta_{M}\}_{|M|=n}
introduced in Section
2.1.1
. Furthermore the image
\Delta\subset\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})
of
\widetilde{\Delta}
has defining ideal
J\cap k[X_{1},Y_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{n}]^{S_{n}}
which has generators
\{\Delta_{M}\Delta_{M^{\prime}}\}_{|M|=|M^{\prime}|=n}
.

We write
\widetilde{\Delta}_{f}
and
\Delta_{f}
for the intersection with
\mathcal{X}_{f}^{n}\subset(\mathbb{A}^{2})^{n}
and
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})\subset\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})
respectively.

Proposition 4.5
.

For any
n\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{L}^{-\mathrm{ord}_{\Delta_{f}}/2}|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}=|\omega^{gel}_{n}|

on
\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}
.

Proof.

The proof goes by comparing the underlying algebraic forms defining both sides when pulled back under
\pi:\mathcal{X}_{f}^{n}\to\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
. For the left hand side recall that
\pi^{*}\omega_{orb}=(p_{1}^{*}\omega_{f}\wedge\dots\wedge p_{n}^{*}\omega_{f})^{\otimes 2}
by definition. Furthermore for any
x\in\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}(K[[t]])
and any lift
\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{X}_{f}^{n}(\overline{K[[t]]})
we have
\mathrm{ord}_{\Delta_{f}}(x)=\mathrm{ord}_{\Delta}(x)=2\mathrm{ord}_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(\tilde{x})
since the generators of the ideal of
\Delta
are the pairwise products of the generators of the ideal defining
\widetilde{\Delta}
.

For the right hand side recall that we have an open cover
\bigcup_{\lambda\vdash n}U_{f,\lambda}=\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
and
|\omega^{gel}_{n}|
is glued together from the absolute values of the forms
\omega_{\lambda}^{gel}=\omega^{[n]}/f_{\lambda}^{[n]*}\nu
on
U_{f,\lambda}^{sm}
. Let
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{f}=\mathcal{X}_{f}\setminus\{0\}
denote the smooth locus of
\mathcal{X}_{f}
. Then
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{f})\cong\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{f})
and we can think of
U^{\prime}_{f,\lambda}=U_{f,\lambda}^{sm}\cap\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{f})
as an open in
\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})
and hence compute
\pi^{*}\omega_{\lambda|U^{\prime}_{f,\lambda}}^{gel}
.

First since
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})\to\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{2})
is crepant, it follows that
\omega^{[n]}
pulls back to the standard form
dx_{1}\wedge dy_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dy_{n}
on
(\mathbb{A}^{2})^{n}
. In order to compute
\pi^{*}f_{\lambda}^{[n]*}\nu
we use that by (
2.1
)

(f_{\lambda}^{[n]}\circ\pi)(x,y)=B_{\lambda}^{-1}(x,y)\begin{pmatrix}f(x_{1},y_{1})\\
f(x_{2},y_{2})\\
\vdots\\
f(x_{n},y_{n})\end{pmatrix}.

Thus
\pi^{*}\omega^{gel}_{\lambda}=\pi^{*}\omega^{[n]}/\pi^{*}f_{\lambda}^{[n]*}\nu
can be written as

\pi^{*}\omega^{gel}_{\lambda}=(\Delta_{\lambda}(x,y)+t\alpha_{1}(x,y)+t^{2}\alpha_{2}(x,y)+\dots)p_{1}^{*}\omega_{f}\wedge\dots\wedge p_{n}^{*}\omega_{f},

with
\alpha_{i}\in K[x,y]
. Since
\pi^{*}\omega^{gel}_{\lambda}
is
S_{n}
-invariant, all the
\alpha_{i}(x,y)
are alternating.

Now given any
z\in\textup{Sym}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f}(K[[t]])\cong\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f}^{0})(K[[t]])
there exists a
\lambda\vdash n
such that
x\in U_{f,\lambda}(K[[t]])
. Let
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{A}^{2})^{n}(\overline{K((t))})
be such that
\pi(x,y)=z_{|K((t))}
. Then from Lemma
3.4
and the fact that the ideal
J
defining
\widetilde{\Delta}
is generated by the functions
\{\Delta_{M}\}_{|M|=n}
we finally get

|\Delta_{\lambda}(x,y)+t\alpha_{1}(x,y)+t^{2}\alpha_{2}(x,y)+\dots|=|\Delta_{\lambda}(x,y)|=\mathbb{L}^{-\mathrm{ord}_{\widetilde{\Delta}}(x,y)},

which finishes the proof.
∎

6.1.
Smooth curve

The Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)
of the smooth line
f(x,y)=x
is an affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}=\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{1})=\mathrm{Hilb}^{1}_{n}(f)
that can be identified with the space
of monic polynomials of degree
n
,
y^{n}+\sum_{i}a_{i}y^{i}
. Thus the discriminant
\Delta_{f}
agrees with the classical discriminant
\mathrm{Disc}_{n}
.
Moreover, since
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)
is smooth we have
|\omega^{gel}|=|da_{0}\wedge\dots\wedge da_{n-1}|
and the function

Q_{f}(s,T)
is a generating function for the Igusa zeta functions of discriminants:

Q_{f}(s,T)=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n}(s)T^{n},\quad I_{n}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{A}^{n}_{0}}|\mathrm{Disc}_{n}|^{s-1/2}|da_{0}\dots da_{n-1}|.

For small values of
n
the corresponding Igusa zeta function can be computed. For
n=1
the discriminant is constant and

I_{1}(s)=\mathbb{L}^{-1}
. For
n=2
the discriminant
is
a_{1}-4a_{0}^{2}
. Thus after the change of variables
\tilde{a}_{1}=a_{1}-4a_{0}^{2},\tilde{a}_{0}=a_{0}
we obtain:

I_{2}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{A}^{2}_{0}}|\tilde{a}_{1}|^{s-1/2}|d\tilde{a}_{0}d\tilde{a}_{1}|=(\mathbb{L}-1)\frac{\mathbb{L}^{s-5/2}}{\mathbb{L}^{s+1/2}-1}.

Since the discriminant of a cubic polynomial
x^{3}+px+q
is equal to
-4p^{3}-27q^{2}
the computation of the integral reduces to the computation of the Igusa zeta
function for the cusp singularity. This computation can be found in many places, for example in chapter 1,

:

I_{3}(s)=\frac{\mathbb{L}-1}{X^{6}\mathbb{L}^{5}-1}\left(\frac{1}{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+X^{3}+\frac{X^{2}}{\mathbb{L}}+X^{4}\mathbb{L}+\frac{\mathbb{L}-1}{\mathbb{L}^{3}(X\mathbb{L}-1)}\right),\quad\mathbb{L}^{s-1/2}=X.

The computation of the integral

[\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}]=1

P_{gel}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 0}(T/\mathbb{L})^{n}

To discuss the second part of the formula (

6.2.
Nodal singularity

The Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)_{0}
in this case is known to be
a chain of projective lines. Indeed, to describe
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)_{0}
we need to classify codimension
n
ideals in the local ring of this singularity which
is equal to
k\oplus x\cdot k[[x]]\oplus y\cdot k[[y]]
as a
k
-vector space with
xy=0
as the main defining relation. In details, the ideals of codimension
n
are
union of
n-1
projective lines
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}=\{(\alpha_{k}x^{k}+\beta_{k}y^{n-k},x^{k+1},y^{n-k+1})\}
,
\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}\neq\vec{0}
,
k=1,\dots,n-1
.
The points ideal for
\beta_{k}=0
is equal to the ideal for
\alpha_{k-1}=0
and
\alpha_{k}=0
is equal to the ideal for
\beta_{k+1}=0
.

From the above description we see that the one generator locus
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}^{1}(f)_{0}
inside
\mathrm{Hilb}_{n}(f)_{0}
is the union of
\mathbb{G}_{m}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
defined by the
condition
\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}\neq 0
. Thus we have computed the
P_{gel}(T)
:

P_{gel}(T)=1+(\mathbb{L}-1)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)\mathbb{L}^{-n}T^{n}=1+T^{2}/(\mathbb{L}-T)^{2}.

The Igusa zeta function can be computed by elementary means as well, since

[X^{0}_{f,n}]=(n-1)(\mathbb{L}-1)\mathbb{L}^{n-2}

Z_{f}(T)=\sum_{n\geq 1}[X^{0}_{f,n}]\mathbb{L}^{-2n}T^{n}=(\mathbb{L}-1)\mathbb{L}^{-2}T^{2}/(1-T\mathbb{L}^{-1})^{2}.

The function
Q_{f}(s,T)
interpolates between the functions
P_{gel}(T)
and
Z_{f}(T)
as we state in (
1.2
).
In a forthcoming manuscript, we provide and argument similar to Proposition
4.1
that
yields a formula for the function
Q_{f}(s,T)
:

\int_{\textup{Sym}_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{f})}|\omega_{orb}|^{1/2}|\Delta_{f}|^{s}=\sum_{\lambda=(1^{a_{1}},2^{a_{2}},\dots)\vdash n}\mathbb{L}^{-v(\lambda)}\int_{\prod_{i}\textup{Sym}_{a_{i}}(\mathcal{X}^{0}_{i})}|\Delta_{f}|^{s}\prod_{i\geq 1}|\omega_{f}|_{i}.

For
n\leq 3
the integrals can be computed by elementary means: for
n=1
the integral vanishes. For
n=2
, only the term with
\lambda=(2)
appears with
\nu(\lambda)=1/2
. The locus
\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f,2}
consist of the pairs

x,y\in t^{1/2}k[[t^{1/2}]]
such that
xy=t
. Hence
|\Delta_{f}|
is constant along
\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f,2}
and equal
\mathbb{L}
. Thus combining this observation with the Lemma
2.6
and

[X_{f,2}^{0}]=[\{x,y\in k[t]/t^{3}|x,y=t^{2}\in k[t]/t^{3}\}]=(\mathbb{L}-1)\mathbb{L}^{2}
we obtain:

\mathbb{L}^{-\nu((2))}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f,2}}|\Delta_{f}|^{s}|\omega_{f}|_{2}=\mathbb{L}^{-1/2}\mathbb{L}^{-s}(\mathbb{L}-1)\mathbb{L}^{-1}.

Similarly, for
n=3
the only non-trivial term in the formula is the term with
\lambda=(3)
. Respectively,
\nu((3))=1
and the space
\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f,2}
consists of two connected components

\{x=t^{1/3}\varphi(t^{1/3}),y=t^{2/3}\psi(t^{1/3})\}
and
\{x=t^{2/3}\varphi(t^{1/3}),y=t^{1/3}\psi(t^{1/3})\}
with
\varphi,\psi\in k[[t^{1/3}]]
with
\varphi\psi=1
.
Hence
|\Delta_{f}|
is constant along the both components and it is equal
\mathbb{L}^{2}
. We can again use Lemma
2.6
and

[X_{f,3}^{0}]=2(\mathbb{L}-1)\mathbb{L}^{3}
to complete the computation:

\mathbb{L}^{-\nu((3))}\int_{\mathcal{X}^{0}_{f,3}}|\Delta_{f}|^{s}|\omega_{f}|_{2}=2\mathbb{L}^{-1}\mathbb{L}^{-2s}(\mathbb{L}-1)\mathbb{L}^{-1}.

Thus we can write the start of
T
-expansion of the function
Q_{f}(s,T)
:

Q_{f}(s,T)=1+(\mathbb{L}-1)(\mathbb{L}^{-s-3/2}T^{2}+2\mathbb{L}^{-2s-2}T^{3})+\dots.

If we substitute
s=1/2
into the expansion then we obtain the first four terms of the series
P_{gel}(T)
. On the other hand the substitution
s=0
yields the first four terms of
the expansion of

\textup{Exp}(\mathbb{L}^{-1/2}Z_{f}(\mathbb{L}^{1/2}T))=\textup{Exp}((\mathbb{L}-1)(\mathbb{L}^{-3/2}T^{2}+\mathbb{L}^{-2}T^{3})+\dots)\\
=(1-T^{2})^{-\mathbb{L}^{-3/2}(\mathbb{L}-1)}(1-T^{3})^{-2\mathbb{L}^{-2}(\mathbb{L}-1)}\cdot\dots{}

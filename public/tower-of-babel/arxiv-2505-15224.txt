stabilization on ideal class groups in potential cyclic towers
Jianing Li

Abstract
. Let p p be a prime and let F F be a number field. Consider a Galois extension K / F K/F with Galois group H ⋊ Δ H\rtimes\Delta where H ≅ ℤ p H\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p} or ℤ / p d ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/p^{d}{\mathbb{Z}} , and Δ \Delta is an arbitrary Galois group. The subfields fixed by H p n ⋊ Δ H^{p^{n}}\rtimes\Delta ( n = 0 , 1 , ⋯ ) (n=0,1,\cdots) form a tower which we call it a potential cyclic p p -tower in this paper. A radical p p -tower is a typical example, say ℚ ⊂ ℚ ⁡ ( a p ) ⊂ ℚ ⁡ ( a p 2 ) ⊂ ⋯ {\mathbb{Q}}\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p]{a})\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p^{2}]{a})\subset\cdots where a ∈ ℤ a\in{\mathbb{Z}} . We extend the stabilization result of Fukuda in Iwasawa theory on p p -class groups in cyclic p p -towers to potential cyclic p p -towers. We also extend Iwasawa’s class number formula in ℤ p {\mathbb{Z}}_{p} -extensions to potential ℤ p {\mathbb{Z}}_{p} -extensions.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number and let
F
be a number field. Let
F_{\infty}/F
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension with
n
-th layer
F_{n}
. The classical Iwasawa theory studies the
p
-class groups of
F_{n}
when
n
varies. The stationary result in Iwasawa theory proved by Fukuda
[
Fuk94
]
states that if the
p
-class groups of
F_{n_{0}}
and
F_{n_{0}+m}
are isomorphic for
m=1
, then the same holds for each
m\geq 1
; here
n_{0}
is some integer such that
F_{\infty}/F_{n_{0}}
is totally ramified at every ramified prime. Using a similar argument,
a stabilization result can be proved in a finite cyclic
p
-tower; see
[
LOXZ22
, Proposition 3.2]
. It is used there to explicitly compute certain
p
-class groups of fields in radical towers, like
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{p^{2}},\sqrt[p^{n}]{a})
with
n
varies, which are not Galois extensions in general.
It motivates the author to think whether there are stationary results for
p
-class groups in non-cyclic radical towers, say
{\mathbb{Q}}\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p]{a})\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p^{2}]{a})\subset\cdots
, where
a
is an integer. In this paper they will be called potential cyclic
p
-towers, and we
prove a stationary result in such towers and obtain an ”Iwasawa like” class number formula for such a tower.

Definition 1.1

(Potential cyclic
p
-tower)
.

Let
p
be a prime number. Let
F
be a number field.
Let
K/F
be a Galois extension and let
G={\mathrm{Gal}}(K/F)
. Assume that
G=H\rtimes\Delta
is a semidirect product of two subgroups (
H
being normal), and that
H\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{d}{\mathbb{Z}}
or
H\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, while
\Delta
may be an arbitrary (finite or infinite) Galois group. Let
F_{n}
be the intermediate field of
K/F
fixed by the group
H^{p^{n}}\rtimes\Delta
, so
F_{0}=F
. We then get a tower
F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{d}
(
d=\infty
in the case
H={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
) with
[F_{n+1}:F_{n}]=p
for each
n
, and we say this tower is a potential cyclic p-tower (in the case
H\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, we may call it a potential
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-tower).

Example 1.2
.

(1)

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extensions and
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{d}{\mathbb{Z}}
-extensions clearly give potential cyclic
p
-towers.

(2)

Suppose
a\in F
such that
x^{p}-a
is an irreducible polynomial. Then
the radical tower
F_{n}=F(\sqrt[p^{n}]{a})
(
n=0,1,\cdots,\infty
) is a potential cyclic p-tower, with
K=F_{\infty}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
. A particular interesting example is
{\mathbb{Q}}\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p]{p})\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p^{2}]{p})\subset\cdots\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p^{\infty}]{p})
, in which every subextension is not Galois.

To obtain results on class groups, we make the following hypothesis on ramifications:

RamHyp:

Let
K/F
and
\{F_{n}\}
be as in a potential cyclic
p
-tower.
If
v
is a prime of
F
which is ramified in
K/F
, we assume that the inertia group
I_{v}(K/F)
of
v
is conjugate to a subgroup of
G
of the form
H\rtimes\Delta_{v}
, where
\Delta_{v}
is a subgroup of
\Delta
. Moreover, assume that there exists a prime
v
which is totally ramified in
K/F
, in other words
I_{v}(K/F)=G
.

Example 1.3
.

(1)

In a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}/F
,
RamHyp
holds for
F_{\infty}/F_{n_{0}}
when
n_{0}
is sufficiently large.

(2)

Regarding Example
1.2
(2), we consider the
2
-dimensional
p
-adic tower
F_{n,m}=F(\sqrt[p^{n}]{a},\mu_{p^{m}})
and let
K=F_{\infty,\infty}
. Then one can show that the potential cyclic tower arising from
K/F_{N,N}
satisfies
RamHyp
when
N
is sufficiently large.

If
E
is any number field, let
A_{E}
denote the
p
-class group (i.e.
p
-Sylow subgroup of the class group) of
E
. Our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\{F_{n}\}
be a potential cyclic
p
-tower satisfying
RamHyp
.
If
A_{F_{1}}\cong A_{F_{0}}
, then
A_{F_{n}}\cong A_{F_{0}}
for
n\geq 1
. More generally, for each
k\geq 1
, if
A_{F_{1}}/p^{k}A_{F_{1}}\cong A_{F_{0}}/p^{k}A_{F_{0}}
, then
A_{F_{n}}/p^{k}A_{F_{n}}\cong A_{F_{0}}/p^{k}A_{F_{0}}
for
n\geq 1
.

By
RamHyp
, the norm map
A_{F_{n}}\to A_{F_{n-1}}
is always surjective for each
n
, so the condition
A_{F_{1}}\cong A_{F_{0}}
is equivalent to
|A_{F_{1}}|=|A_{F_{0}}|
. For a finite abelian group
A
, recall that its
p^{i}
-rank
(i\geq 1)
is defined as
{\mathrm{rank}}_{p^{i}}A:=\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(p^{i-1}A)/(p^{i}A)
. A direct consequence is the following:

Corollary 1.4
.

If
|A_{F_{1}}|=|A_{F_{0}}|
, then
|A_{F_{n}}|=|A_{F_{0}}|
. More generally, for each
k\geq 1
, if
{\mathrm{rank}}_{p^{i}}A_{F_{1}}={\mathrm{rank}}_{p^{i}}A_{F_{0}}
holds for every
i\leq k
, then the same equalities hold for
A_{F_{n}}
and
A_{F_{0}}
for each
n\geq 1
.

Fix an integer
m\geq 0
, then the tower
F_{n}={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{p^{m}},\sqrt[p^{n}]{p})
(
n=0,1,\cdots
) satisfies (see
[
LOXZ22
, Lemma 4.1]
) the condition in Theorem
1.1
with
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},\sqrt[p^{\infty}]{p})
. If
p
is a regular prime, it is easy to show that
A_{F_{n}}
is trivial for each
n
and each
m
.
Let
p=37
, the smallest irregular prime with
A_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{p})}={\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}

; then it is computed by McCallum and Sharifi that
A_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p]{p})}=0
and
A_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{p},\sqrt[p]{p})}={\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
; see
[
MS03
]
. It follows from Theorem
1.1
we indeed have
A_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{p},\sqrt[p^{n}]{p})}={\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
and
A_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p^{n}]{p})}=0
for each
n\geq 1
. Other explicit examples can also be found in
[
LOXZ22
]
and whose proofs can be simplified using Theorem
1.1
.

Our second main result is a generalization of Iwasawa’s classical class number formula (see
[
Iwa58
]
) in a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\{F_{n}\}
be a potential cyclic
p
-tower satisfying
RamHyp
.
Let
e_{n}\geq 0
such that
p^{e_{n}}=|A_{F_{n}}|
.
Then there exit non-negative integers
\mu,\lambda,\nu
such that

e_{n}=\mu p^{n}+\lambda n+\nu
when

is sufficiently large.

This has been proved by Lei
[
Lei17
]
under the following three conditions (1):
\Delta\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
; (2): there is only one prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
F
lying above
p
; (3):
{\mathfrak{p}}
is totally ramified in
K/F
.

We mention a related result of Caputo and Nuccio
[
CN23
]
.
Let
k
be an imaginary quadratic field, let
K/k
be the anti-cyclotomic
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension and suppose

. Then
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/{\mathbb{Q}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}:=H\rtimes\Delta
. The subgroups

correspond to a potential
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-tower

, which are called ”fake

-extensions of dihedral type” in

]
. They prove an ”Iwasawa like” class number formula but the

-invariants are only in

(see

). We remark that Theorem

The proof

The idea of the proofs is from Iwasawa’s theory of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-extension (see
[
Iwa58
]
or
[
Was97
, Chapter 13]
).

2.1.
Descent in General Galois extension

Let
p
be a prime number and let
F
be a number field. Let
K/F
be an arbitrary Galois extension in this subsection. Write
G={\mathrm{Gal}}(K/F)
. Let
L
be the
p
-Hilbert class field of
K
, i.e., the maximal unramified pro-
p
-extension of
K
. Since
K/F
is a Galois extension,
L/F
is also Galois.

Notation
:

•

Write
{\mathcal{G}}={\mathrm{Gal}}(L/F)
and
X={\mathrm{Gal}}(L/K)
. The group
G
acts on
X
by lifting conjugation. Say
g\in G,x\in X
, then
g\cdot x=\tilde{g}x\tilde{g}^{-1}
, where
\tilde{g}\in{\mathcal{G}}
is any lifting of
g
.

•

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[G]
denote the (completed) group ring, so that
X
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[G]
-module. Let
I_{G}
denote the argumentation ideal of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[G]
, so
I_{G}
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-linear combination of
\{g-1:g\in G\}
. Then the closure of
I_{G}X
(written as
I_{G}X
by abuse of notation) is a compact
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[G]
-submodule of
X
and is a normal subgroup of
{\mathcal{G}}
.

•

If
V
is a closed subgroup of
{\mathcal{G}}
, let
[V,V]
denote the closure of the commutator subgroup of
V
; and let
V^{\prime}
denote the closure of the image of
V
in
{\mathcal{G}}/I_{G}X
. In particular,
X^{\prime}=X/I_{G}X
, on which
G
acts trivially.

•

If
T\subset{\mathcal{G}}
, the notation
\langle T\rangle
denotes the closure of the subgroup of
{\mathcal{G}}
generated by
T
.

•

If
E/F
is a Galois extension of number fields and
w
is a prime of
E
, let
I_{w}(E/F)
denote the inertia group
w
.

•

Let
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}
(here
r\leq\infty
, but in our further application
r<\infty
always) be the primes of
F
which are ramified in
K
. For each
i
, choose a prime
w_{i}
of
L
lying above
v_{i}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
w=w_{1}
is totally ramified in
K/F
. Write
I_{w}=I_{w}(L/F)
. Then we have:

(1)

I_{w}\cap X=0
,
{\mathcal{G}}/X\cong G
, and
I_{w}\cong G
under this isomorphism. Therefore,
{\mathcal{G}}=X\rtimes I_{w}
.

(2)

[{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{G}}]=I_{G}X\rtimes[I_{w},I_{w}]
.

(3)

I^{\prime}_{w}
is normal in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\cong X^{\prime}\times I^{\prime}_{w}
.

(4)

A_{F}\cong{\mathrm{Gal}}(L(F)/F)\cong{\mathcal{G}}/\langle I_{G}X,I_{w},I_{w_{2}},\cdots,I_{w_{r}}\rangle\cong{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}/(I^{\prime}_{w}\langle I^{\prime}_{w_{2}},\cdots,I^{\prime}_{w_{r}}\rangle)
, where
L(F)
is the
p
-Hilbert class field of
F
.

(5)

Let
Y=X\cap\langle I_{G}X,I_{w},I_{w_{2}},\cdots,I_{w_{r}}\rangle
. Then the inclusion
X\to{\mathcal{G}}
induces an isomorphism
X/Y\cong A_{F}
.

Before proof, we remark that these results are independent of the ways in which the primes
w_{i}
of
L
lying above
v_{i}
are chosen.

Proof.

(1)

This is obvious, since
v
is totally ramified in
K/F
and
L/K
is unramified.

(2)

Since
(g-1)x=\tilde{g}x\tilde{g}^{-1}x^{-1}
for
g-1\in I_{G},x\in X
, the commutator group of
{\mathcal{G}}
contains the subgroup

\langle I_{G}X,[I_{w},I_{w}]\rangle
which is equal to
I_{G}X\rtimes[I_{w},I_{w}]
as
I_{G}X
is normal in
{\mathcal{G}}
. On the other hand, since
{\mathcal{G}}=X\rtimes I_{w}
and since
G\cong I_{w}
acts on
X^{\prime}
trivially, we have isomorphisms
(
X\rtimes I_{w})/(I_{G}X\rtimes[I_{w},I_{w}])\cong(X^{\prime}\times I_{w})/[I_{w},I_{w}]
, the latter being abelian clearly. This proves (2).

(3)

By (1) and the definition of
X^{\prime}
, we have
X^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime}_{w}=0
. Hence we have
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}=X^{\prime}\rtimes I^{\prime}_{w}
. Since the action of
{\mathcal{G}}
and hence
I_{w}
on
X^{\prime}
is trivial, it follows that
I^{\prime}_{w}
is normal in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
and therefore
X^{\prime}\rtimes I^{\prime}_{w}=X^{\prime}\times I^{\prime}_{w}
.

(4)

The first isomorphism is by class field theory. The second is from the definiton of
L(F)
and (2); the third is from (3).

(5)

This is obvious.

∎

2.2.
Descent in Potential Cyclic
p
-towers

Let
K/F
,
\{F_{n}\}
,
G=H\rtimes\Delta
be as in a potential Cyclic
p
-tower; see Definition
1.1
. Assume it satisfies
RamHyp
.
So
H\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{d}{\mathbb{Z}}
or
H\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, and we fix a (topological) generator
h
of
H
.
We shall apply Lemma
2.1
to
K/F_{n}
whose Galois group is
H^{p^{n}}\rtimes\Delta
; we first treat the case
K/F=K/F_{0}
.

Assume
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}
are the primes of
F
ramified in
K
, and assume that
v=v_{1}
is totally ramified in
K
. By
RamHyp
, for each
i
, we can choose some prime
w_{i}
of
L
such that (writing
w=w_{1}
)

(2.1)

I_{w_{i}}=I_{w_{i}}(L/F)\cong H\times\Delta_{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad I_{w}\cong G=H\times\Delta

under the isomorphism
{\mathcal{G}}/X\cong G
in Lemma
2.1
(1).
If
g\in G
, let
\tilde{g}_{i}\in I_{w_{i}}
be the inverse image of
g
, when it exists, under (
2.1
). Then for
1\leq i\leq r
, we have

\tilde{h}_{i}\equiv\tilde{h}_{1}\bmod X
and for each
\delta\in\Delta_{i}
,
\tilde{\delta}_{i}\equiv\tilde{\delta}_{1}\bmod X
.
Now put

(2.2)

\displaystyle a_{i}:=\tilde{h}^{-1}_{1}\tilde{h}_{i}\in X,\quad b_{\delta,i}:=\tilde{\delta}^{-1}_{1}\tilde{\delta}_{i}\in X,

Define subgroups of
X
by

A=\langle a_{2},\cdots,a_{r}\rangle\text{ and }B=\langle\{b_{\delta,i}:i=1,\cdots,r,\delta\in\Delta_{i}\}\rangle.

It follows that

\langle I_{w_{1}},\cdots,I_{w_{r}}\rangle=\langle I_{w},A,B\rangle,

and that as subgroups of
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{G}}/I_{G}X
,

(2.3)

\langle I^{\prime}_{w_{1}},\cdots,I^{\prime}_{w_{r}}\rangle=\langle I^{\prime}_{w},A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\rangle=I^{\prime}_{w}\langle A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\rangle.

The last equality holds because
I^{\prime}_{w}
is normal in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
by Lemma
2.1
(3). Since
I^{\prime}_{w}\cap X^{\prime}
is trivial by Lemma
2.1
(3), we obtain

(2.4)

X^{\prime}\cap\langle I^{\prime}_{w_{1}},\cdots,I^{\prime}_{w_{r}}\rangle=\langle A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\rangle.

It follows that

(2.5)

X\cap\langle I_{G}X,I_{w_{1}},\cdots,I_{w_{r}}\rangle=\langle I_{G}X,A,B\rangle.

We remark that the left hand side is
a prior
a
G
-submodule of
X
, so is the right hand side.
By Lemma
2.1
(5) we have

(2.6)

A_{F_{0}}\cong X/\langle I_{G}X,A,B\rangle.

Now we apply the above argument and Lemma
2.1
to
K/F_{n}
, so
L
remains unchanged,
{\mathcal{G}}
becomes
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}={\mathrm{Gal}}(L/F_{n})
, and
G
becomes
G_{n}:={\mathrm{Gal}}(L/F_{n})
. The inertia groups
I_{w_{i}}(L/F_{n})=I_{w_{i}}(L/F)\cap{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/F_{n})
and now maps isomorphically to
H^{p^{n}}\rtimes\Delta_{i}
. So the elements
b_{\delta,i}
in (
2.2
) are unchanged, but those
a_{i}
change to
\tilde{h}_{1}^{-p^{n}}\tilde{h}_{i}^{p^{n}}
.
Let

\omega_{n}=\frac{h^{p^{n}}-1}{h-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H].

Then one directly computes that

(2.7)

\tilde{h}_{1}^{-p^{n}}\tilde{h}_{i}^{p^{n}}=\omega_{n}a_{i},

Hence as subgroups of
X^{\prime}:=X/I_{G_{n}}X
, we have

X^{\prime}\cap\langle I^{\prime}_{w_{1}}(L/F_{n}),\cdots,I^{\prime}_{w_{r}}(L/F_{n})\rangle=X^{\prime}\cap(I^{\prime}_{w_{1}}(L/F_{n})\langle\omega_{n}A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\rangle)=\langle\omega_{n}A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\rangle;

here the first equality uses that
I^{\prime}_{w_{1}}(L/F_{n})
is normal in
{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/F_{n})
by Lemma
2.1
, and the second is obvious.
Then as subgroups of
X
, we have

X\cap\langle I_{G_{n}}X,I_{w_{1}}(L/F_{n}),\cdots,I_{w_{r}}(L/F_{n})\rangle=\langle I_{G_{n}}X,\omega_{n}A,B\rangle.

By Lemma
2.1
(5) we have that for each
n\geq 0
,

A_{F_{n}}\cong X/\langle I_{G_{n}}X,\omega_{n}A,B\rangle.

Lemma 2.2
.

As a left
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H^{p^{n}}]
-module,
I_{G_{n}}
is generated by
h^{p^{n}}-1=\omega_{n}(h-1)
and
I_{\Delta}
, where
I_{\Delta}
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-linear combination of
\{\delta-1:\delta\in\Delta\}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the case
n=0
.
If
h^{i}\delta\in G
, then
h^{i}\delta-1=h^{i}\delta-h^{i}+h^{i}-1=h^{i}(\delta-1)+(h^{i}-1)=h^{i}(\delta-1)+\frac{h^{i}-1}{h-1}(h-1)
. Since
I_{G_{0}}
is the linear combination of
h^{i}\delta-1
, this completes the proof.
∎

Now consider the following two
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H]
-submodules of
X
, both independent of
n
:

(2.8)

C:=\langle(h-1)X,A\rangle_{H-\mathrm{mod}},\quad D:=\langle I_{\Delta}X,B\rangle_{H-\mathrm{mod}}.

Here the notation
\langle T\rangle_{H-\mathrm{mod}}
means the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H]
-submodule of
X
generated by
T
for
T\subset X
.
Then we obtain the following isomorphisms for each
n\geq 0

(2.9)

A_{F_{n}}\cong X/(\omega_{n}C+D)\cong\overline{X}/\omega_{n}\overline{C}.

Here we write
\overline{X}:=X/D
and
\overline{C}:=(C+D)/D
; both are
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H]
-modules and are independent of
n
.

Theorem 2.1
.

In the case
H\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
,
the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H]
-module
\overline{X}
is finitely generated and torsion.

Proof.

We turn to the case
H\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
We first show that
\overline{X}
is finitely generated over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H]
. It suffices to show that
\overline{X}/(p,h-1)\overline{X}
is finite by the compact version of Nakayama’s lemma; see
[
Was97
, Lemma 13.16]
. Since
\omega_{1}\in(p,h-1)
the module
\overline{X}/(p,h-1)\overline{X}
is a quotient of
\overline{X}/\omega_{1}\overline{X}
which is a quotient of
\overline{X}/\omega_{1}\overline{C}\cong A_{F_{1}}
, which is finite.

According to the structure theorem of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H]
-modules (see
[
Was97
, §13.2]
), there is a direct summand
({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H])^{r}
in
\overline{X}
(up to a finite group). But
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H]/\omega_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H]
is infinite, so
r=0
and therefore
\overline{X}
is torsion over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Classical arguments in Iwasawa theory (see for example
[
Was97
, Lemma 13.18, Proposition 13.19, Lemma 13.21]
) shows that if
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
are finitely generated torsion
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[H]
-modules such that
Y/\omega_{n}Y^{\prime}
is finite for each
n
, then
|Y/\omega_{n}Y^{\prime}|=\mu p^{n}+\lambda n+\nu
when
n
is sufficiently large for integers
\mu,\lambda,\nu
. Therefore Theorem
1.2
follows from (
2.9

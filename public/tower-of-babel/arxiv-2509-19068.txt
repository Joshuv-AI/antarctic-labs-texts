Dwarf Galaxies in the MATLAS Survey: Hubble Space Telescope Observations of Nuclear Star Clusters
Mélina Poulain, Francine R. Marleau, Pierre-Alain Duc, Rubén Sánchez-Janssen 4, Patrick R. Durrell 5, Sanjaya Paudel 6, Rebecca Habas, Oliver Müller 8,9,10, Sungsoon Lim 11, Nick Heesters 8, Jérémy Fensch 12

Abstract
In dwarf galaxies, nuclear star clusters (NSCs) are believed to primarily form from the migration and merger of globular clusters (GCs), with a possible contribution from in-situ star-forming activity triggered by gas infall. We present the study of NSCs in 41 MATLAS survey dwarf galaxies including ultra-diffuse galaxies (UDGs), as part of a large follow-up imaging program with the Hubble Space Telescope ( HST ) Advanced Camera for Surveys (ACS) using the F606W and F814W filters. The sample is biased towards low-surface brightness and large dwarfs, i.e., UDG-like galaxies, and includes two galaxies with a double nucleus, 13 newly identified nucleated dwarfs thanks to HST ’s high spatial resolution, and five candidate ultra-compact dwarf progenitors. We modeled the NSCs with a Sérsic profile and derived their structural properties and photometry. We find the NSC Sérsic index to increase with the luminosity and stellar mass, while no obvious trend is seen on the effective radius and ellipticity. The faint NSCs tend to have a constant color profile, whereas the bright ones have a bluer center, suggesting that the most massive NSCs in our sample might have experienced a mixed formation scenario, including in-situ star formation. A significant portion of our NSCs tend to be more massive than for other galaxy samples of similar stellar mass, which could be due to some dwarfs ongoing tidal disruption or an initial formation of massive NSCs from multiple GC mergers and in-situ star forming activity. More observations of resolved NSC are needed to be able to infer their formation scenario from the structural properties and photometry in dwarfs.

Introduction

Nuclear star clusters (NSCs) are known to be the densest stellar systems in the Universe. These small compact objects usually have an effective radius within ten parsecs and a stellar mass M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm NSC}
from
10^{5}
to
10^{8}
M
⊙

(
Neumayer et al. 2020
)
. Although they reside in the central region of galaxies, their presence depends on the stellar mass of the host (M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm gal}
). This so-called nucleation fraction reaches a maximum of more than
80\%
for galaxies with M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm gal}\sim
10
9
M
⊙

(
van den Bergh 1986
;
Ferguson & Binggeli 1994
;
den Brok et al. 2014
;
Ordenes-Briceño et al. 2018
;
Sánchez-Janssen et al. 2019
)
, while an absence of NSC is observed for more than 90% of dwarf and high-mass galaxies with M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm gal}\lesssim 10^{6}
M
⊙
and M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm gal}\gtrsim 10^{11}
M
⊙
, respectively (e.g.,
Sandage et al. 1985
;
Faber et al. 1997
;
Côté et al. 2006
;
Antonini et al. 2015
;
Poulain et al. 2021
;
Carlsten et al. 2022
;
Su et al. 2022
;
Marleau et al. 2025
).

Two main pathways appear to take part in the formation of NSCs. The first one is the migration scenario, where GCs form in a galaxy, migrate towards its center due to dynamical friction, and finally merge to form a NSC
(
Tremaine et al. 1975
;
Lotz et al. 2001
;
Bekki 2007
)
. This scenario has been reproduced well by N-body simulations (e.g.,
Antonini et al. 2012
;
Lyubenova & Tsatsi 2019
), and can explain the formation of NSCs with old metal-poor stellar populations. The second one is the in-situ scenario
(
Loose et al. 1982
)
, characterized by the formation of NSCs from star-forming activity caused by infalling gas. While the resulting stellar population tends to be young and metal-rich, the in-situ scenario is not the only way to produce such young NSCs. In Milky-Way-like galaxies, N-body simulations have shown that NSCs can also be formed by the merging of young, metal-rich clusters
(
Arca-Sedda et al. 2015
;
Schiavi et al. 2021
)
. The variety of stellar populations of some NSCs, like M54 in the Sagittarius dwarf, imply a formation based on both the migration and in-situ processes (e.g.,
Sills et al. 2019
;
Alfaro-Cuello et al. 2019
;
Kacharov et al. 2022
). An example of mixed scenario is the wet migration scenario
(
Guillard et al. 2016
)
, based on hydrodynamical simulations in dwarf galaxies, which proposes the formation of a GC-like star cluster in a gas-rich dwarf which contains a gas reservoir allowing it to maintain star-forming activity while migrating towards the galaxy center, producing a rather young, blue NSC. Several recent studies have looked for the main formation channel of NSCs and their results suggest that the migration scenario dominates in galaxies with M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm gal}
¡
10^{9}
M
⊙
, while the in-situ formation prevails in more massive galaxies, and both seem to contribute equally around
10^{9}
M
⊙
(e.g.,
Turner et al. 2012
;
den Brok et al. 2014
;
Johnston et al. 2020
;
Fahrion et al. 2021
;
Fahrion et al. 2022a
;
Fahrion et al. 2022b
;
Fahrion et al. 2024
).

Together with theoretical works, the formation of NSCs is traditionally studied using three main types of observational data. The first are deep imaging surveys, undertaken mostly via ground-based observations. They are very efficient to unveil large numbers of new NSCs using photometric data and offer detailed statistics about the morphology and local environment of the nucleated galaxies (e.g.,
Reaves 1983
;
Binggeli et al. 1985
;
Ordenes-Briceño et al. 2018
;
Sánchez-Janssen et al. 2019
;
Habas et al. 2020
;
Carlsten et al. 2022
;
Su et al. 2022
;
Paudel et al. 2023
). The second is the analysis of the stellar populations using spectrographs. The Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph (STIS) of the
Hubble Space Telescope
(
HST
) was used by
Rossa et al. 2006
to produce the largest spectroscopic survey of NSCs in late-type galaxies (LTGs) to date. Lately, the Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE) and FOcal Reducer and low dispersion Spectrograph (FORS) instruments on the Very Large telescope (VLT) were favored to investigate the age, metallicity, and star formation history of NSCs in dwarf galaxies (
Paudel et al. 2011
;
Johnston et al. 2020
;
Fahrion et al. 2021
;
Fahrion et al. 2022a
). Finally, the third is the study of their structural properties with high-resolution space-based observations.
HST
has been frequently used throughout the years for this kind of work. The luminosity and size of NSCs were first measured in LTGs (see for example
Carollo et al. 1997
;
Matthews et al. 1999
;
Böker et al. 2002
;
Balcells et al. 2003
;
Böker et al. 2004
;
Balcells et al. 2007
;
Georgiev & Böker 2014
), before targeting early-type galaxies (ETGs) with the
HST
surveys in the Virgo, Fornax and Coma clusters
(
Lotz et al. 2001
;
Graham & Guzmán 2003
;
Côté et al. 2006
;
Turner et al. 2012
;
den Brok et al. 2014
)
where more than 200 NSCs were characterized. However those studies are lacking statistics for dwarf galaxies with M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm gal}
¡
10^{8}
M
⊙
. More recently, the Early Release Observations from the space telescope Euclid
(
Marleau et al. 2025
)
and additional studies with
HST
(e.g.,
Spengler et al. 2017
;
Pechetti et al. 2020
;
Zanatta et al. 2021
;
Hoyer et al. 2023
) have highlighted new NSCs in galaxy clusters and within the Local Volume (LV), especially in dwarf galaxies.

Related to NSCs are the GCs which also populate the halos of galaxies. Comparing the GCs to the NSCs properties is important in the context of the migration scenario, especially in dwarf galaxies where this formation path is favored due to their shorter dynamical friction timescales
(
Hernandez & Gilmore 1998
;
Oh & Lin 2000
)
. Several studies already pointed out correlations between these two types of star clusters. In terms of spatial distribution, a lack of bright GCs was observed in the center of nucleated elliptical dwarfs in the Virgo and Fornax clusters by
Lotz et al. 2001
. A deficit of GCs at small radii in both nucleated and non-nucleated UDGs was reported by
Marleau et al. 2024
, and could be caused by either dynamical friction or the stalling of GCs at the core radius in a core dark matter halo. For dwarf galaxies located in galaxy clusters and in the Local Volume (LV), NSCs and GCs also seem to share a similar occupation fraction, dependant on the stellar mass of the host galaxy
(
Sánchez-Janssen et al. 2019
;
Carlsten et al. 2022
)
.

Beyond the formation of NSC, it is also interesting to look for nucleated galaxies ongoing a transition into ultra compact dwarfs galaxies (UCDs). UCDs
(
Hilker et al. 1999
;
Drinkwater et al. 2000
;
Phillipps et al. 2001
)
are compact stellar systems with an effective radius below 100 pc and a stellar mass above
10^{6}
M
⊙

(
Evstigneeva et al. 2007
;
Mieske et al. 2008
;
Misgeld & Hilker 2011
)
. UCDs have been reported in a wide range of environments, from galaxy clusters, galaxy groups, and around field galaxies (e.g.,
Madrid et al. 2010
;
Chilingarian et al. 2011
;
Norris & Kannappan 2011
;
Faifer et al. 2017
;
De Bórtoli et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2020
;
Saifollahi et al. 2021
). In the past two decades, a number of scenarios have been put forward to explain the nature of UCDs, and these can broadly be placed into two different formation channels. A fraction of the UCDs are expected to correspond to the high-mass end of the GC population
(
Kroupa 1998
;
Mieske et al. 2002
;
Mieske et al. 2012
)
, while the most massive UCDs are likely to be NSC remnants of tidally stripped nucleated dwarf galaxies (e.g.,
Bassino et al. 1994
;
Bekki et al. 2001
;
Goerdt et al. 2008
;
Mayes et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2023
).

Studying dwarf galaxies is important to comprehend the formation and evolution of NSCs. However, only few observing programs using high spatial resolution imaging exist, and most of them focused galaxy clusters.
The sample of 2210 dwarf galaxies identified in the Mass Assembly of early-Type GaLAxies with their fine Structures (MATLAS) survey offers large statistics beyond the LV, up to a distance of about 45 Mpc, and outside the environment of galaxy clusters
(
Habas et al. 2020
)
. A photometric study based on the MATLAS deep images from the Canada-France-Hawaii telescope (CFHT) was already undertaken on this sample, unveiling 507 nucleated dwarfs
(
Poulain et al. 2021
)
.
HST
observations were conducted for 79 dwarf and ultra-diffuse galaxies (UDGs) from the MATLAS survey
(
Marleau et al. 2024
)
, 41 of them being nucleated. UDGs are defined as dwarf galaxies with an unusually large effective radius and low central surface brightness, typically R
e
¿1.5 kpc and
\mu_{0,\rm gal}
¿24 mag/arcsec
2
, respectively
(
van Dokkum et al. 2015
)
. Designed to characterize their GC populations, these high resolution observations marginally resolve the nuclear region of the galaxies and allow for a more detailed study of the NSCs than previously performed in
Poulain et al. 2021
. A first paper on the NSCs by
Poulain et al. 2025
focused on the detection of multiple star clusters and stellar tidal tails in a handful of dwarfs. The observed tidal tails were successfully reproduced in N-body simulations, and suggest that they are produced by ongoing star clusters mergers. In this paper we derive the NSCs photometric and structural properties in the 41 dwarf galaxies and UDGs, and discuss their possible formation and evolution pathways.

This work is organized as follows. We introduce the MATLAS survey, as well as the dwarf galaxy sample in Section
2
. We present the

3

4

6

7

8

The MATLAS dwarf sample

The galaxies selected for this work were identified in the MATLAS

This survey was designed to study the outermost stellar populations, low surface brightness structures, globular clusters, and dwarf galaxy satellites of ETGs in the context of their mass assembly and the build-up of their scaling relations. The

\sim

The photometric and structural properties of the MATLAS dwarfs, and that of their nuclei, were studied in

HST

In this study, a subsample of 79 of the 2210 dwarf galaxies identified in the MATLAS survey were observed with the
HST
Advanced Camera for Surveys (ACS) in the Wide Field Channel (WFC) mode. The

The galaxies were observed in the F606W and F814W filters, with an exposure time in each filter of 1030 s for MATLAS-2019, and 824 s for the others. We computed the VEGAmag zero-points from the

The nucleated dwarf sample

In
Poulain et al. 2021
, we defined a nucleus as a compact source located up to 0.5 effective radius from the photocenter of the dwarf, and that is the brightest compact source within one effective radius. Based on this definition, and after removing one likely foreground star contaminant using the
Gaia
DR2 catalog
(
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2018

\sim 75\%

For 16 nucleated dwarfs, up to three compact sources of similar magnitude were located close to the photocenter and were brighter than the surrounding point sources. In these cases, we defined those galaxies as multinucleated.

5
Deriving the properties of the NSCs

5.1
Sérsic modeling

In order to obtain the structural parameters and photometry of the NSCs, we divided the modeling process into two parts. The aim of the first step was to remove the smooth light profile of the dwarf galaxy from the image. As the background is not constant all over the
HST
image, we estimated the local background of the galaxy. We computed the median sky from
3\sigma
clipping in an annulus of radius 15″ on the outskirts of the galaxy – where the light profile stops decreasing to become constant. To optimize the galaxy modeling, we masked all nuclei with a circle of radius 1″, a good compromise to cover each NSC without masking a too large region of the galaxies. If necessary, we manually created an additional mask of surrounding bright sources, such as galaxies and stars, with the help of
saoimageds9

(
Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory 2000
)
. Finally, we ran
galfit

(
Peng et al. 2010
)
to apply a Sérsic profile
(
Sérsic 1963
)
to the dwarf galaxy, fixing the value of the sky to the estimate. The Sérsic model has long been used in the literature for the NSC modeling (e.g.,
Graham & Spitler 2009
;
Seth et al. 2010
;
Turner et al. 2012
;
Lyubenova et al. 2013
;
Carson et al. 2015
;
Spengler et al. 2017
).
Because of the exposure time of the observations, it can be very difficult to differentiate the observed galaxies from the background given their low-surface brightness. As a consequence, the Sérsic models systematically have a smaller R
e
than the models on the CFHT images
(
Poulain et al. 2021
)
, as the flux in the outer part of the galaxies blends into the sky noise. We also obtained less accurate photometric measurements, especially for the faintest galaxies. As a consequence, when the galaxies structural properties and photometry are studied, we used the values from
Poulain et al. 2021
based on the CFHT images.
The second step focused on the Sérsic modeling of the NSCs. For this, we created a cutout of size 5″ centered on the nucleus from the galaxy cleaned image, that is, the residual image from
galfit
. The PSF varies with the position on the CCD and the stellar population of the stars, thus, to precisely compute the structural properties of the NSCs, we generated the PSF of each NSC from
tinytim

(
Krist et al. 2011
)
with a
G2V
spectral type for the stars, similarly to
Hoyer et al. 2023
, and we set the position at the center of the CCD, where the nucleus was observed. It has been shown, however, that a change of spectral type has a mild impact on the NSC modeling
(
Böker et al. 2004
;
Georgiev & Böker 2014
)
.

The errors on the Sérsic parameters were derived for each reliably modeled NSC by injecting artificial NSCs in the
HST
residual images. The artificial NSCs were modeled using the
python
wrapper of the software
imfit

(
Erwin 2015
)
to which we set the parameters of the Sérsic model to be that of the considered NSC. Each artificial source was injected at a random position on the CCD where the target galaxy was centered. In the case of a double nucleated galaxy, we computed the error of each NSC separately. To avoid superposition with bright sources, we isolated the injected NSC from these based on a segmentation image from
source extractor
. We applied the same fitting method to the artificial sources as for the NSCs. To estimate the error on a given parameter, we ran
galfit
on 100 artificial NSCs leaving only this parameter free to change, and computed the standard deviation of the 100 resulting models.

We obtained a reliable Sérsic model for 14 and three NSCs in the F606W and F814W filter, respectively, and for 14 NSCs in both filters. When a model is available for both F606W and F814W images, the parameters tend to agree within the computed errors. Therefore, when applicable, we use the average value of the derived R
e,NSC
,
n
, and ellipticity. All the derived properties and their uncertainties are reported in Table
1
. Considering the spatial resolution of the observations and the range of distance covered by the host galaxies, the NSCs in the nearest[farthest] galaxies should have R
{}_{\rm e,\rm NSC}\gtrsim 3[10]
pc to start being resolved, increasing the difficulty to marginally resolve the NSCs in the farthest galaxies. Considering galaxies farther than 20 Mpc, 16 NSCs are close to be unresolved and have effective radius R
{}_{\rm e,\rm NSC}\lesssim 1
pix. Those NSCs are highlighted in Table
1
. In addition, we were unable to derive the structural parameters of the NSC of MATLAS-1630, MATLAS-262, and MATLAS-627, all located at a distance between 30 Mpc and 46 Mpc. The remaining galaxies without a NSC model show an intricate central region, consisting of either a possible NSC with stellar tails, or multiple bright clusters close the center. The dwarfs with such complex NSCs, including the double nucleated dwarfs, are studied in detail in
Poulain et al. 2025
.

5.2
Color and stellar mass

We derived the
F606W-F814W
color of the NSCs from the total magnitudes of the Sérsic modeling when a reliable model was available in both bands. Otherwise, in case of a one-band-only reliable model, we estimated the color based on aperture magnitude, with a varying aperture size proportional to R
e,NSC
. We measured the aperture magnitudes making use of the
python
package
photutils

(
Bradley et al. 2020
)
, and applied the aperture corrections reported for the ACS/WFC in
Bohlin 2016
. Comparing the obtained colors from aperture and model measurements, we found the best agreement for an aperture radius of 2R
e,NSC
, with a median difference between the two computed colors of 0.04 and a maximum difference below 0.1. For the 12 NSCs without a reliable model, we used the derived photometry from either
Poulain et al. 2025
for the complex NSCs, or
Marleau et al. 2024
for the others, when available.

We converted the magnitudes to the
BVI
system, applying the same approach as
Hoyer et al. 2023
, and employed the synthetic color transformation coefficients of
Sirianni et al. 2005
. In the case of a NSC with only one magnitude available, for example
m_{F606W}
, we defined
m_{F814W}
as
m_{F814W}=m_{F606W}-(m_{\rm F606W,\rm ap}-m_{\rm F814W,\rm ap})
with
(m_{\rm F606W,\rm ap}-m_{\rm F814W,\rm ap})
being the color derived from aperture photometry. We corrected the obtained
V
and
I
-band magnitudes for Galactic extinction using the estimates from
Schlafly & Finkbeiner 2011
at the position of the NSCs.

We computed the stellar mass of the NSCs from the relation between the stellar mass-to-light ratio in the
I-
band
(M_{\ast}^{\rm NSC}/L_{\rm I})
and the
(V-I)_{0}

Roediger & Courteau 2015
:

\log_{10}(M_{\ast}^{\rm NSC}/L_{\rm I})=-0.694+1.335\times(V-I)_{0}.

6
Structural and photometric properties

In this section, we present the structural properties of the 31 NSCs with a reliable Sérsic model, as well as the photometry of 41 NSCs. We first compare them to NSC properties from the literature, and then detail their color profile.

6.1
Catalogs of NSCs

For comparison purposes, we compiled the results of several NSC studies targeting both dwarfs and more massive galaxies located in various environments.
We used two types of samples: a structural parameters-based and a photometry-based sample. The structural parameters-based sample gathers the properties of NSCs observed by
HST
and is used to study their R
e,NSC

In the LV,
Pechetti et al. 2020
3
3

3

We only considered the best quality NSC models (0 and 1) to avoid galaxies highly contaminated by dust.

The type of model fitted to the underlying galaxy light profile impacts the reliability of the derived NSC properties, especially in massive galaxies, as a single Sérsic profile is sometimes used instead of a multi-component surface brightness profile in the presence of a bulge, bar, or disk

The photometry-based sample is composed of the measured photometry and stellar masses of the NSCs and their host galaxy. It combines the NSCs from the structural parameters-based sample to ground-based observations. We added the NSC population of dwarf galaxies surrounding LTGs from

6.3
NSCs-to-galaxy stellar mass relation

Several studies have reported correlations between some properties of the NSCs and the host galaxies, especially their luminosity and mass, in both dwarf and more massive galaxies (e.g.,
Balcells et al. 2003
;
Graham & Guzmán 2003
;
Böker et al. 2004
;
Turner et al. 2012
;
Scott & Graham 2013
;
Poulain et al. 2021
;
Carlsten et al. 2022
;
Hoyer et al. 2023
). In this section we focus on the NSC-to-galaxy mass ratio. This relation has been studied for a large range of galaxies and environment, and thus is well suited for a comparison with our sample. We show at the top of Figure

the relation between the stellar mass of the NSC and that of its host galaxy, while we plot the NSC-to-galaxy mass ratio as a function of the galaxy stellar mass in the bottom part. We compare the
HST

6.1
.

In agreement with the literature, we observe an overall increase of the mass of the NSC with that of the host. We study this increase by computing the running average of the NSC mass per bin of galaxy stellar mass, combining all the samples. Interestingly, we find that a majority of the NSCs in the
HST
sample falls above the running average, suggesting that the NSCs tend to be more massive in our sample. While these NSCs appear to also deviate from the relation fitted by
Neumayer et al. 2020
on dwarf galaxies, considering the error bars on the MATLAS dwarfs, they mostly agree within the estimate uncertainties on the relation fitted on dEs from the Coma cluster
(
Graham & Guzmán 2003
)
. We investigate the deviant galaxies by looking in details at the properties of the dwarf galaxies with high
M_{\ast}^{\rm NSC}
and mass ratio in the Virgo cluster. We find that they tend to either have a very high nuclear-to-total luminosity fraction or are UDGs. According to
Wang et al. 2023
, all have properties of UCD progenitors and are ongoing tidal disruption. The possible evolution of the MATLAS nucleated galaxies observed with
HST
into UCDs is discussed in Section
8
. Another possible explanation could be that some of these galaxies might have experienced more GC mergers than other dwarfs due to a larger population of GCs, leading to more massive NSCs
(
Turner et al. 2012

Lim et al. 2020
;
Müller et al. 2021
;
Forbes & Gannon 2024
;
Marleau et al. 2024

We note that we observe an overall change in the relation at a galaxy stellar mass of about
10^{9}M_{\odot}
in the lower panel of Figure

(

;

M_{\ast}^{\rm NSC}\propto\sqrt{M_{\ast}^{\rm gal}}

6.4
Color of the NSCs

In this section, we study the stellar populations of the NSCs by looking at their integrated
(F606W-F814W)_{0}
color as well as the variation of the color with radius, i.e., their color profile. The modeled NSCs show a range of color indices from 0.54 to 1.23 with a mean
(F606W-F814W)_{0}=0.78\pm 0.14
mag, similar to the observed colors of GCs in the MATLAS UDGs
(
Müller et al. 2021
;
Marleau et al. 2024
)
. No trends are observed between the color of the NSCs and its absolute magnitude, the galaxy color, or the galaxy stellar mass.

We defined the color profiles of the NSCs as the difference between the light profiles in the F606W and F814W images that we produced making use of
autoprof

(
Stone et al. 2021
)
fixing the same center and ellipses in both bands. We selected only the 15 marginally resolved NSCs with R
e,NSC
¿1 pix to ensure having multiple pixels. We display the color profiles within 3R
e,NSC
ordered by increasing M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm NSC}
in Figure
5
, and the individual profiles in Figure
10
, to investigate a possible change of the profile with the NSC mass. We observe that a large majority of the low-mass NSCs, with M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm NSC}\lesssim 10^{6.5}
M
⊙
, tend to have an almost constant profile, while the massive ones show a change of color, with a red outer area and an inner region that gets bluer towards the photocenter. We note that both types of profile can be found at M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm NSC}
from
10^{6}
to
10^{7}
M
⊙
.

The absence of color gradient in the center of the low-mass NSCs means there is an homogeneous stellar population, arguing in favor of GC merging. On the contrary, the change of color can be explained by a change of stellar population. In the high-mass-end of NSCs in dwarf galaxies, a combination of formation scenario has been reported
(
Fahrion et al. 2021
;
Fahrion et al. 2022a
;
Fahrion et al. 2022b
)
. Such composite scenario matches our observations, starting with GC infall producing the outer region red color, and followed by in-situ star formation causing a younger, bluer, stellar population at the center. Given the low spatial resolution of the NSCs, the observed trend needs to be confirmed by observing more NSCs, especially in nearby galaxies.

An exception to this color trend is the low-mass NSC of MATLAS-49 with a
\log(M_{\ast}^{\rm NSC}/M_{\odot})
of 5.91. The latter has an opposite profile to the massive NSCs, with a blue stellar population in the outer region that gets redder towards the photocenter. In the context of dwarf galaxies, this type of profile could be due to circumnuclear star formation, as observed for some NSCs in dEs
(
Paudel & Yoon 2020
)
following the wet migration scenario predictions. According to this scenario, MATLAS-49 should be a gas-rich dE, but no HI-detection was reported for this galaxy in
Poulain et al. 2022
. Given its location we estimate its HI mass to be less than
5\times 10^{7}\,
M
⊙
.

7.1
GC radial distribution

The strength of the dynamical friction depends on the steepness of the density profile of the galaxy, but also on the mass of the GC and its initial position
(
Hernandez & Gilmore 1998
;
Lotz et al. 2001
;
Arca-Sedda & Capuzzo-Dolcetta 2014
;
Arca-Sedda et al. 2015
)
, so that the most massive GCs, and GCs at small radial distances, are the fastest to migrate to the center and the most likely to merge to form a NSC, and galaxies with the steepest profiles have the shortest orbital decay timescales. To explore this GC mass segregation, we look at the radial distance of the GCs of the nucleated and non-nucleated dwarfs as a function of their mass. We only modeled the marginally resolved GC candidates detected in the 17 dwarfs located below 25 Mpc, as the GC systems are dominated by unresolved sources at greater distances (see
Marleau et al. 2024
for further details). Following the same approach to the NSCs described in Section
5
, we obtained a reliable Sérsic model for 142 GCs in at least one of the two filters, from which we derived the colors and stellar masses. The properties of the GCs are available in Table
2
, and we indicate the GCs with R
{}_{\rm e,\rm GC}\lesssim 1
pix, as for the NSCs.

In Figure

e,gal

\delta_{GC}
per bin, 0.5 log(M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm GC}
/M

We find that the nucleated galaxies have, on average, a higher Sérsic index than the non-nucleated dwarfs with 1.01 and 0.85, respectively, suggesting a steeper density profile for the nucleated dwarfs and a more efficient dynamical friction. However, the galaxies show a wide range of Sérsic index, from about 0.5 to 1.6, regardless of the presence of a NSC, and we do not find any influence of the Sérsic index on the radial distribution of the GCs.
We observe that GCs in both nucleated and non-nucleated galaxies have a similar radial distribution for M

8
Nucleated dwarfs as UCD progenitors

UCDs are a class of galaxies as bright as dwarf galaxies but more compact, in between the size of dwarfs and GCs. One of their formation scenarios implies that UCDs formed from the stripped nuclei remnants of tidally disrupted nucleated dwarf galaxies (e.g.,
Zinnecker et al. 1988
;
Freeman 1993
;
Bekki et al. 2001
). Simulations argue that this is the predominant formation path of the most massive UCDs
(
Pfeffer et al. 2014
;
Mayes et al. 2021
)
. Moreover, this scenario is also supported by the observation of tidal features around some UCDs
(
Voggel et al. 2016
;
Schweizer et al. 2018
)
, asymmetric or elongated shapes
(
Wittmann et al. 2016
)
, diffuse haloes
(
Evstigneeva et al. 2008
;
Liu et al. 2020
)
, similarities between the stellar populations of UCDs and NSCs
(
Paudel et al. 2010
;
Norris et al. 2015
;
Janz et al. 2016
;
Wang et al. 2023
)
, and the presence of MBHs in UCDs
(
Graham 2020
;
Mayes et al. 2024
)
. An example of UCD candidate in the process of formation has already been identified in the MATLAS dwarf galaxy sample. MATLAS-167 is located at the end of a tidal tail extending from the giant S0 galaxy NGC936; a detailed analysis of the object can be found in
Paudel et al. 2023
.

We investigate whether some of the NSCs in our
HST
sample could be UCD progenitors. In Figure
7
, we compare the R
e
– luminosity scaling relation of the modeled MATLAS NSCs to the UCDs from the Virgo and Fornax galaxy clusters
(
Liu et al. 2020
;
Saifollahi et al. 2021
)
. We split the Virgo sample to highlight the UCDs with identified outer envelopes, and we only show UCDs with R
{}_{\rm e}>20
pc in the Fornax sample, as
Saifollahi et al. 2021
question the reliability of the measurement of smaller sizes. During the formation of UCDs, up to 98% of the stellar mass of the MATLAS dwarfs will get stripped
(
Bekki et al. 2001
;
Wang et al. 2023
)
, leaving the NSCs slightly larger and brighter than they currently are. As the MATLAS NSCs appear to form a bridge between the GCs and UCDs, we suggest that they could be UCD progenitors if their dwarf galaxy host undergoes tidal disruption.

The study of
Wang et al. 2023
presents different phases of UCD formation from tidally stripped dE,N in the Virgo cluster. They suggest that, during the first transitional phase, tidal stripping can remove more than 90% of the galaxy stellar mass, creating so-called strongly nucleated dwarfs with a nuclear-to-total-luminosity fraction reaching
8\%-35\%
. In Figure
8
, we show the NSC-to-galaxy luminosity relation of our
HST
sample, with the nuclear-to-total luminosity fraction of the NSCs as a colorbar. The NSCs show overall a good agreement with the relation derived by
Graham & Guzmán 2003
. We investigate if the five dwarfs outliers with a fraction
\gtrsim 6\%
show signs of tidal disruption. We identify tidal features from nearby massive galaxies related to two of them, MATLAS-1539 and MATLAS-1577, which have a fraction of 6.6% and 9.2%, respectively. The dwarfs and corresponding tidal features are visible in Figure
9
. MATLAS-1539 seems compact and is located on top of a tidal tail from NGC5198, while MATLAS-1577 has a disturbed morphology and is extended towards a stellar stream from NGC5308. We note that the NSC of MATLAS-1539 is the largest of the modeled ones, with a R
e,NSC
of 21.58 pc, and already follows a similar M
V
–R
e
relation to the UCDs (see Figure
7
). Following these results, even though only MATLAS-1577 meets the criteria of strongly nucleated dwarfs from
Wang et al. 2023
, we suggest that both MATLAS-1539 and MATLAS-1577 are candidate UCD progenitors. Among the galaxies with a nuclear-to-total luminosity fraction below 6%, we found three tidally elongated UDGs located close to massive galaxies. According to
Wang et al. 2023
, at early steps of tidal disruption, nucleated dEs fall into the regime of UDGs and show tidal features such as and extended stellar envelope or tidal tails. Based on this statement, we add these three UDGs to the list of candidates, and show them in Figure
9
.

9
Summary and conclusions

In this work, we investigated the formation and evolution of NSCs by studying their structural and photometric properties in a dwarf galaxy sample biased towards low-surface-brightness and diffuse –UDG-like– galaxies located beyond the LV and outside the environment of galaxy clusters. We made use of
HST
follow-up observations of 79 MATLAS galaxies with the ACS using the F606W and F814W filters. This sample is composed of 41 nucleated dwarfs, 13 of which are newly identified thanks to
HST
high spatial resolution images, and two have a double nucleus.

We modeled the NSCs with a Sérsic profile and obtained a reliable result for 31 of them; the derived parameters are provided in Table
1
. We compared their structural properties and photometry to NSCs in nearby field LTGs, and in dwarf galaxies from the LV, as well as from the Virgo and Fornax galaxy clusters. The R
e,NSC
of the MATLAS NSCs does not tend to be larger as they get brighter and more massive. Moreover, while we do not see an influence of the absolute magnitude on the ellipticity, the Sérsic index of the MATLAS NSCs gets larger with M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm NSC}
, as observed in the Virgo cluster.

Looking at the relation between the NSCs and their host galaxy, we find that a non negligible fraction of the MATLAS NSCs tend to be brighter and more massive than NSCs in typical dwarf galaxies. This could be either due to the galaxies being ongoing tidal disruption or to an enhanced growth of NSCs in UDGs, producing more massive NSCs.

We derived the color profiles within 3R
e,NSC
of the modeled NSCs and remark that, while the low-mass NSCs tend to have an almost constant profile consistent with the migration scenario, the most massive NSCs have a steep slope, from an outer redder color to an inner bluer color, suggesting a change of stellar population, which might be caused by a contribution from in-situ star formation.

Following the work of
Marleau et al. 2024
on the GC systems of the
HST
MATLAS UDGs, we studied the distribution and properties of the GCs in the context of the migration scenario.
We modeled the GCs in the MATLAS dwarfs up to 25 Mpc and find that , although with low statistics, the most massive GCs with M
{}_{\ast}^{\rm GC}
¿ 10
{}^{6.5}\,
M
⊙
are distributed oppositely in the nucleated and non-nucleated dwarfs: the former seem to lack massive GCs in their central 0.5R
e,gal
region, while they are located within 0.5R
e,gal
for the latter. Comparing the R
e
– luminosity scaling relation of the GCs to the NSCs, we find a different relation for both types of star clusters, in agreement with
Bekki et al. 2004
.

The most massive UCDs are believed to be the NSC remnants of tidally stripped nucleated dwarfs. In that context, we searched for UCD progenitors among the 41 NSCs with available photometry. We found that the NSCs appear to link the GC and UCD populations in the R
e
– luminosity scaling relation, suggesting that some of them could become UCDs if their dwarf host undergo tidal disruption. Looking for strongly nucleated dwarfs, we identified two UCD progenitor candidates, MATLAS-1539 and MATLAS-1577, that have a nuclear-to-total-luminosity fraction of 6.7% and 8.7%, respectively, and show signs of a possible tidal interaction with their assumed host ETG. Regarding the galaxies with a low nuclear-to-total-luminosity fraction, we found three tidally elongated UDGs, likely in the early stage of tidal disruption, that we added to our list of UCD progenitor candidates.

Overall, the observed trends need to be confirmed by studying even larger samples of nucleated dwarfs using deep and high resolution imaging facility (e.g., the Euclid Wide Survey;
Euclid Collaboration: Scaramella et al. 2022
), combined to spectroscopic follow-up observations to trace the NSC star formation history.

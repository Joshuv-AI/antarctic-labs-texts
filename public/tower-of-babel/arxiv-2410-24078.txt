Asymptotic completeness for short-range N N -body systems revisited
E. Skibsted

Abstract
. We review Yafaev’s approach to asymptotic completeness for systems of particles mutually interacting with short-range potentials [ Ya1 ] . The theory is based on computation of commutators with time-independent (mostly bounded) observables yielding a sufficient supply of Kato smoothness bounds.



Keywords:
N
-body Schrödinger operators; short-range scattering
theory.

Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: 81Q10, 35P05.

1.
Introduction

We consider
n
-dimensional particles
j=1,\dots,N
interacting by short-range
pair-potentials

V_{ij}(x_{i}-x_{j})={\mathcal{O}}(\lvert x_{i}-x_{j}\rvert^{-\delta}),\quad\delta>1.

The total
Hamiltonian reads

H=H^{a_{\max}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2m_{j}}\Delta_{x_{j}}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}V_{ij}(x_{i}-x_{j}),

acting
on
{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbf{X}})
, where

{\mathbf{X}}=\Big\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}x_{j}=0\Big\}.

For the cluster decompositions
a\neq a_{\max}=\{1,\dots,N\}

there are sub-Hamiltonians
H^{a}
defined similarly
acting on
\mathcal{H}^{a}=L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}^{a})
(more precisely given for each
a
as a tensor sum of Hamiltonians for
the individual clusters of
a
).

Recall that
for any
channel

\alpha=(a,\lambda^{\alpha},u^{\alpha})
, i.e.
a
be a cluster
decomposition,
a\neq a_{\max}
,
\lambda^{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

(H^{a}-\lambda^{\alpha})u^{\alpha}=0
for a normalized
u^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{H}^{a}
, the corresponding
channel wave operators

(1.1a)

W_{\alpha}^{\pm}=\slim_{t\to\pm\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tH}J_{\alpha}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}t(-\Delta_{x_{a}}+\lambda^{\alpha})},\quad J_{\alpha}\varphi=u^{\alpha}\otimes\varphi.

The existence of the channel wave operators as well as
their completeness, i.e. the property that their ranges span the
absolutely continuous subspace of
H
,

(1.1b)

\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\oplus}\,\,R(W_{\alpha}^{\pm})={\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ac}(H),

is a famous problem in mathematical physics and since decades ago definitively solved, we
refer to

[
SS
,
Gra
,
Ya1
,
Ta
,
DG
,
Gri
,
HS1
,
HS2
,
Is
]
although this list is not complete
(and for the long-range case to
[
De
,
Zi
,
DG
]
).

In this paper we
review Yafaev’s approach to asymptotic completeness
[
Ya1
]

using his vector field constructions (which was inspired by
[
Gra
]
) and a
Mourre estimate (based on the Graf vector field). A notable virtue of
this approach is that it relies on commutators of

time-independent
observables only, which has applications for
stationary scattering theory
[
Ya2
]
,
[
Ya3
]
and
[
Sk2
]
(and
for long-range potentials as well, see
[
Sk1
]
). Several of the
other known proofs of asymptotic completeness are using time-dependent observables excluding
applications in stationary scattering theory.
In this paper we will
not study the more refined stationary scattering theory, however
one of the goals is to pursue an approach as close as possible to
the recently improved stationary scattering theory
[
Sk2
]
. This is partly
motivated by aesthetics, attempting in this way to ‘align’ the stationary and
time-dependent methods. Another goal is potentially to lay the ground for
encompassing
completeness for
N
-body Schrödinger operators with certain
unbounded obstacles, going beyond the bounded case treated by Griesemer
[
Gri
]
, as
well as for
some
progress on the scattering problem for
N
-body Schrödinger
operators with time-periodic short-range pair-potentials. Rather
unsatisfying very little
is known about the latter
problem.

Our use of the Mourre
estimate deviates somewhat from the implementation in
[
Ya1
]
in
that we use a new
‘phase space partition of unity’. This amounts to ‘preparing’ an
initial localization of a given scattering state in the same spirit as
appearing in
many papers, see
for example

[
SS
]
. While this may appear as an unnecessary complication compared
to Yafaev’s very efficient and elegant arguments, our approach has
as indicated above a vital
analogue
in
[
Sk2
]
.

More precisely let
\psi(t)={\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f(H)\psi
denote an
energy-localized scattering state, say as defined for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{R}})

supported near a fixed real
\lambda
, not be a threshold nor an
eigenvalue. Then we use the Mourre estimate to find a suitable bounded
observable
P_{+}
, whose symbol is localized to the ‘forward direction’
of the phase space, such that
\lVert\psi(t)-P_{+}\psi(t)\rVert\to 0
for
t\to+\infty
. Secondly we show that Yafaev’s observable

M_{a_{\max}}=\Sigma_{a\neq a_{\max}}\,M_{a}
effectively can be substituted,
yielding for a suitable bounded function
g_{+}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
, that

(1.2a)

\Big\lVert\psi(t)-\sum_{a\neq a_{\max}}\,g_{+}(M_{a_{\max}})M_{a}\psi(t)\Big\rVert\to 0\text{ for }t\to+\infty.

Once the initial localization (
1.2a
) is established asymptotic completeness
follows by a standard induction argument from the existence of the limits

(1.2b)

\psi_{a}=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tH_{a}}g_{+}(M_{a_{\max}})M_{a}\psi(t),\quad a\neq a_{\max},

which in turn is shown by commutator arguments essentially mimicking
[
Ya1
]
, although with
additional minor calculus issues.

Preliminaries and organization of paper

We explain an abstract setting (generalizing the one of Section

1
), give an account of some basic

N
-body scattering theory in this setting and we outline the structure of the

2.1.
N
-body Hamiltonians, assumptions and
notation

First we explain our model and then we introduce a number of
useful general notation.

2.1.1.
Generalized
N
-body short-range Hamiltonians

Let
{\mathbf{X}}
be
a (nonzero) finite dimensional real inner product space,
equipped with a
finite family
\{{\mathbf{X}}_{a}\}_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}
of subspaces closed under intersection:
For any
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
there exists
c\in\mathcal{A}
, denoted

c=a\vee b
, such that
{\mathbf{X}}_{a}\cap{\mathbf{X}}_{b}={\mathbf{X}}_{c}
.
We
order
{\mathcal{A}}
by writing
a\leq b
(or equivalently as
b\geq a
) if

{\mathbf{X}}_{a}\supseteq{\mathbf{X}}_{b}
.
It is assumed that there exist

a_{\min},a_{\max}\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that

{\mathbf{X}}_{a_{\min}}={\mathbf{X}}
and

{\mathbf{X}}_{a_{\max}}=\{0\}
. For convenience abbreviate
a_{0}:=a_{\max}
. The subspaces
{\mathbf{X}}_{a}
,
a\neq a_{\min}
, are called
collision
planes
. We will use the notation
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{a_{0}\}
and
d_{a}=\dim\mathbf{X}_{a}
for
a\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
. Let
d=\dim\mathbf{X}
.

The
2
-body model (or more correctly named ‘the one-body model’) is
based on the structure

{\mathcal{A}}=\{a_{\min},a_{0}\}
.
The scattering theory for such models is well-understood,
in fact (here including long-range potentials) there are several
doable approaches, see for example
[
DG
,
Is
,
Sk1
]
for accounts on time-dependent and stationary
scattering theories. In this paper we consider short-range potentials only, see the below Condition

2.1
, for which the scattering theory for the

2
-body as well as for the
N
-body model has a canonical meaning
(the long-range case is in this sense different).

Let
{\mathbf{X}}^{a}\subseteq{\mathbf{X}}
be the orthogonal complement of
{\mathbf{X}}_{a}\subseteq{\mathbf{X}}
,
and denote the associated orthogonal decomposition of
x\in{\mathbf{X}}
by

x=x^{a}\oplus x_{a}=\pi^{a}x\oplus\pi_{a}x\in{\mathbf{X}}^{a}\oplus{\mathbf{X}}_{a}.

The vectors
x^{a}
and
x_{a}
may be called the
internal
component
and the

inter-cluster component
of
x
, respectively. The momentum operator

p=-\mathrm{i}\nabla
decomposes similarly,
p=p^{a}\oplus p_{a}
. For
a\neq a_{\min}
the unit sphere in
\mathbf{X}^{a}
is denoted
by
\mathbf{S}^{a}
.

A real-valued measurable function
V\colon{\mathbf{X}}\to\mathbb{R}
is
a
potential of many-body type

if there exist real-valued measurable functions

V_{a}\colon{\mathbf{X}}^{a}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

V(x)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}V_{a}(x^{a})\ \ \text{for }x\in\mathbf{X}.

We take
V_{a_{\min}}=0
and impose throughout the paper the
following condition for
a\neq a_{\min}
.

Condition 2.1
.

There exists
\mu\in(0,1/2)
such that for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{a_{\min}\}
the
potential
V_{a}=V_{a}(x^{a})
fulfills:

(1)

V_{a}(-\Delta_{x^{a}}+1)^{-1}
is compact.

(2)

\lvert x^{a}\rvert^{1+2\mu}V_{a}(-\Delta_{x^{a}}+1)^{-1}
is bounded.

For any
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
we introduce associated Hamiltonians
H^{a}

and
H_{a}
as follows.
For
a=a_{\min}
we define

H^{a_{\min}}=0
on
\mathcal{H}^{a_{\min}}=L^{2}(\{0\})=\mathbb{C}

and
H_{a_{\min}}=p^{2}
on
L^{2}({\mathbf{X}})
, respectively.
For
a\neq a_{\min}

we let

V^{a}(x^{a})=\sum_{b\leq a}V_{b}(x^{b}),\quad x^{a}\in{\mathbf{X}}^{a},

and define then

H^{a}=-\Delta_{x^{a}}+V^{a}\ \ \text{on }\mathcal{H}^{a}=L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}^{a})\text{ \,and\, }H_{a}=H^{a}\otimes I+I\otimes p^{2}_{a}\text{ on }L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}).

We abbreviate

\displaystyle V^{a_{0}}=V,\quad H^{a_{0}}=H,\quad\mathcal{H}^{a_{0}}=\mathcal{H}=L^{2}({\mathbf{X}}).

The operator
H
(with domain
\mathcal{D}(H)=H^{2}(\mathbf{X})
) is the full
Hamiltonian of the
N
-body model, and its resolvent is denoted by

R(z)=(H-z)^{-1}
;
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}
. The
thresholds
of
H
are by
definition the
eigenvalues of the sub-Hamiltonians
H^{a}
;

a\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
.
Equivalently stated the set of thresholds is

{\mathcal{T}}(H):=\bigcup_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}\sigma_{{\mathrm{pp}}}(H^{a}).

This set
is closed and countable. Moreover the set of non-threshold eigenvalues
is discrete in
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathcal{T}}(H)
, and it
can only accumulate at

{\mathcal{T}}(H)
.
The essential spectrum is given by the formula

\sigma_{{\mathrm{ess}}}(H)=\bigl[\min{\mathcal{T}}(H),\infty\bigr)
.
We introduce the notation
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathrm{p}}}(H)=\sigma_{{\mathrm{pp}}}(H)\cup{\mathcal{T}}(H)
, and more generally
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathrm{p}}}(H^{a})=\sigma_{{\mathrm{pp}}}(H^{a})\cup{\mathcal{T}}(H^{a})
for
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
(with
{\mathcal{T}}(H^{a_{\min}}):=\emptyset
).

2.1.2.
General
notation

Any function
f\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{R}})
taking values in
[0,1]
is
referred to as a

support function
. Given such
function
f_{1}
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}({\mathbb{R}})
we write

f_{1}\succ f
, if
f_{1}=1
in a neighbourhood of
\operatorname{supp}f
.

If
T
is a self-adjoint operator on a
Hilbert space and
f
is a support function we can represent the
operator
f(T)
by the well known Helffer–Sjöstrand formula

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(T)&=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}(T-z)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{f}(z)\,\text{ with}\\
&\mathrm{d}\mu_{f}(z)=\pi^{-1}(\bar{\partial}\tilde{f})(z)\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v;\quad z=u+\mathrm{i}v.\end{split}

Here
\tilde{f}
is an ‘almost analytic’ extension of

f
, which in this case may be taken compactly supported. The
formula (
2.1
) extends to more general classes of functions
and serves as a standard tool for commuting operators. It will be
important for this issue in the present paper as well,
however some details will be left out of our exposition. Rather we
prefer
at
several places to
refer to explicit results from
[
Sk1
, Subsections 6.2–6.4]
, all
of them
be convenient calculus applications of (
2.1
).

Consider and fix
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle\chi(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{ for }t\leq 4/3,\\
1&\mbox{ for }t\geq 5/3,\end{array}\right.\quad\chi^{\prime}\geq 0,

and such that the following properties are fulfilled:

\displaystyle\sqrt{\chi},\sqrt{\chi^{\prime}},(1-\chi^{2})^{1/4},\sqrt{-\big((1-\chi^{2})^{1/2}\big)^{\prime}}\in C^{\infty}.

We define correspondingly
\chi_{+}=\chi
and

\chi_{-}=(1-\chi^{2})^{1/2}
and record that

\displaystyle\chi_{+}^{2}+\chi_{-}^{2}=1\text{ \,and\, }\sqrt{\chi_{+}},\sqrt{\chi_{+}^{\prime}},\sqrt{\chi_{-}},\sqrt{-\chi_{-}^{\prime}}\in C^{\infty}.

We denote the space of bounded operators
from one (abstract) Banach space
X
to another one
Y
by
{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)

and abbreviate
\mathcal{L}(X,X)=\mathcal{L}(X)
. If
T
is a self-adjoint operator on a
Hilbert space
{\mathcal{G}}
and
\varphi\in{\mathcal{G}}
, then

\langle T\rangle_{\varphi}:=\langle\varphi,T\varphi\rangle
.

2.1.3.
More
notation

We shall use the notation

\langle x\rangle=\big(1+\lvert x\rvert^{2}\big)^{1/2}
for
x\in\mathbf{X}
, and we
write
\hat{x}=x/\lvert x\rvert
for
x\in\mathbf{X}\setminus\{0\}
.
Denote the standard
weighted
L^{2}
spaces
by

L_{s}^{2}=L_{s}^{2}(\mathbf{X})=\langle x\rangle^{-s}L^{2}(\mathbf{X})\ \ \text{for
}s\in\mathbb{R},\quad L_{-\infty}^{2}=\bigcup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}L^{2}_{s},\quad L^{2}_{\infty}=\bigcap_{s\in\mathbb{R}}L_{s}^{2}.

Let
T
be an operator on
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbf{X})
such that

T,T^{*}:L^{2}_{\infty}\to L^{2}_{\infty}
, and let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we
say that
T
is an
operator of order
t
, if
for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
the restriction
T_{|L^{2}_{\infty}}
extends to
an operator
T_{s}\in{\mathcal{L}}(L^{2}_{s},L^{2}_{s-t})
. Alternatively stated,

(2.2a)

\|\langle x\rangle^{s-t}T\langle x\rangle^{-s}\psi\|\leq C_{s}\|\psi\|\text{ for all }\psi\in L^{2}_{\infty}.

If
T
is of order
t
, we write

(2.2b)

T={\mathcal{O}}(\langle x\rangle^{t})\,\text{ or }\,T={\mathcal{O}}(r^{t}).

The latter notation is motivated by
(
2.4b
) stated below.

2.2.
Basic
N
-body scattering theory

Under a rather weak condition (in particular weaker than Condition

2.1
) it is demonstrated in
[
AIIS
]
, that
for any
s>1/2

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\epsilon\leq 1}\lVert R(\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}\epsilon)\rVert_{{\mathcal{L}}\big(L^{2}_{s},L^{2}_{-s}\big)}

\displaystyle<\infty\text{ with
locally
uniform
bounds in
}\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{p}}(H),

(2.3)

and there exist the limits

\displaystyle R(\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}0)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0_{+}}

\displaystyle\,R(\lambda\pm\mathrm{i}\epsilon)\in{\mathcal{L}}\big(L^{2}_{s},L^{2}_{-s}\big);\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathrm{p}}(H).

These results follow from a Mourre estimate for the operator
A:=r^{1/2}Br^{1/2}
,
where

(2.4a)

B=r^{-1/2}Ar^{-1/2}=\mathrm{i}[H,r]=-\mathrm{i}\sum_{j\leq d}\big(\omega_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}+\partial_{x_{j}}\omega_{j}\big),\quad\omega=\mathop{\mathrm{grad}}r

and
r
is a certain smooth positive
function on
\mathbf{X}
(taken from
[
De
]
) fulfilling the
following property, cf.
[
Sk1
, Section 5]
:
For all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}_{0}
there
exists
C>0
such that

(2.4b)

\big\lvert\partial^{\beta}(x\cdot\nabla)^{k}\big(r(x)-\langle x\rangle\big)\big\rvert\leq C\langle x\rangle^{-1}.

Fixing any real
\lambda\not\in{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathrm{p}}}(H)
we can indeed find such function
(possibly taken rescaled, meaning that we replace
r(x)
by

Rr(x/R)
with
R\geq 1
large) and a small
open neighbourhood
U_{\lambda}
of
\lambda
, such that

(2.4c)

\forall\text{ real }f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(U_{\lambda}):\quad{f}(H)\mathrm{i}[H,A]{f}(H)\geq 4c\,{f}(H)^{2};

here the constant
c>0
and its size depends on the number

(2.4d)

d(\lambda,H)=\operatorname{dist}\big(\lambda,\{E\in{\mathcal{T}}(H)\mid E<\lambda\}\big).

In fact
c
can be taken arbitrarily smaller than
the number
d(\lambda,H)
(upon correspondingly adjusting the scaling parameter
R

and the neighbourhood
U_{\lambda}
), however to avoid ambiguities
we take concretely
c=\sigma^{2}
,

\sigma/\sigma_{0}=10/11
, where
\sigma_{0}:=\sqrt{d(\lambda,H)}
,
which suffices for the applications in this paper.

This estimate (
2.4c
) entails (
2.3
) and
therefore (by the familiar Kato theory
[
Ka
]
) also
the local decay
bound

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\forall f\in&C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(U_{\lambda})\,\forall\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}:\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\,\lVert\langle x\rangle^{-\delta}\psi(t)\rVert^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\leq C_{\delta}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2};\\
&\quad\psi(t)={\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f(H)\psi,\quad\delta>1/2.\end{split}

There are related forward (and backward) bounds of propagation. Thus
for example, for any fixed
\lambda\not\in{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathrm{p}}}(H)
,

R(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0)\psi-\chi_{+}(B/\sigma_{0})R(\lambda+\mathrm{i}0)\psi\in L^{2}_{-1/2}\text{ for all }\psi\in\bigcup_{s>1/2}L^{2}_{s},

cf.
[
Sk1
, Subsection 5.2]
. In
Appendix
A
we prove
the following analogous bound

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\forall f\in&C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(U_{\lambda})\,\forall\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}:\\
&\quad\lim_{t\to+\infty}\lVert\psi(t)-\chi_{+}(B/\sigma_{0})\psi(t))\rVert=0;\quad\psi(t)={\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f(H)\psi.\end{split}

In addition to the smoothness bound (
2.5
) there is a
class of such bounds
to be crucial for our proof of asymptotic completeness (as it is in
[
Ya1
]
). These bounds are stated as (
3.12
).
The assertion (
2.6
) will play a somewhat intermediate role.

The above assertions for
H
are clearly
also valid for the
Hamiltonians
H_{a}
,
a\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
, and there are
similar assertions for
the sub-Hamiltonains
H^{a}
upon replacing
(H,r)
by analogous pairs

(H^{a},r^{a})
.

Parallel to (
1.1a
) and (
1.1b
) we define
a channel as a triple

\alpha=(a,\lambda^{\alpha},u^{\alpha})
, where
a\neq a_{0}
,
\lambda^{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

(H^{a}-\lambda^{\alpha})u^{\alpha}=0
for a normalized
u^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{H}^{a}
. The corresponding channel wave operators are given as
the strong limits

(2.7a)

W_{\alpha}^{\pm}=\slim_{t\to\pm\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tH}J_{\alpha}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}t(-\Delta_{x_{a}}+\lambda^{\alpha})};\quad J_{\alpha}\varphi=u^{\alpha}\otimes\varphi.

With the existence of the channel wave operators
given, asymptotic completeness means that

(2.7b)

\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\oplus}\,\,R(W_{\alpha}^{\pm})={\mathcal{H}}_{\rm ac}(H).

Our proof of completeness can easily be adjusted to a proof of the
existence of the channel wave operators. It is a general fact that the existence of the channel
wave operators implies their orthogonality, see for example

[
RS
, Theorem XI.36]
.
Consequently we shall focus
on proving the ‘onto-property’ of (
2.7b
) only, in fact only for the forward
direction
t\to+\infty
, i.e. for the ‘plus’ case. We devote Section

4
to this issue.

As the reader will see in Subsection

4.1
we will use (
2.6
)
to construct a partition of unity for the given
scattering state as in (
1.2a
). The partition observables, whose building blocks

(M_{a})_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}

will be introduced in Section
3
, will thanks to the local smoothness
bounds
(
2.5
) and (
3.12
) (and their analogous
versions for the operators
H_{a}
) have controllable
commutator properties allowing us to construct limits as in
(
1.2b
), indeed yielding asymptotic completeness completeness.

3.
Yafaev’s constructions

In
[
Ya1
]
various real functions
m_{a}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
,

a\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
, and
m_{a_{0}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{X})
are
constructed. They are homogeneous of degree
1
for
\lvert x\rvert>5/6
,
and in addition
m_{a_{0}}
is (locally) convex in that region. These
functions are constructed as depending on a small parameter

\epsilon>0
, and once given, they are used in the constructions

(3.1)

M_{a}=2\operatorname{Re}(w_{a}\cdot p)=-\mathrm{i}\sum_{j\leq d}\big((w_{a})_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}+\partial_{x_{j}}(w_{a})_{j}\big);\quad w_{a}=\mathop{\mathrm{grad}}m_{a}.

The operators
M_{a}
,
a\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
, play the role of ‘channel
localization operators’, while operators on the form
M_{a_{0}}

(parametrized by
\epsilon>0
) mainly are
used as technical quantities controlling commutators of
the Hamiltonian and the channel
localization operators.
In Section
4
we implement the
same ideas for a proof of asymptotic completeness, although in a different way than Yafaev did.

We consider various conical
subsets of
\mathbf{X}\setminus\{0\}
, defined for

a\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{a}&={\mathbf{X}_{a}}\setminus\cup_{{b\not\leq a,\,b\in{\mathcal{A}}}\,}\mathbf{X}_{b},\\
\mathbf{X}_{a}(\varepsilon)&=\{x\in{\mathbf{X}}\mid\lvert x_{a}\rvert>(1-\varepsilon)\lvert x\rvert\},\\
\quad&\quad\quad\overline{\mathbf{X}_{a}(\varepsilon)}=\{x\in{\mathbf{X}}\setminus\{0\}\mid\lvert x_{a}\rvert\geq(1-\varepsilon)\lvert x\rvert\},\\
\mathbf{Y}_{a}(\varepsilon)&=\big(\mathbf{X}\setminus\{0\}\big)\setminus\cup_{{b\not\leq a,\,b\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}}\,\mathbf{X}_{b}(\varepsilon),\\
\mathbf{Z}_{a}(\delta)&=\mathbf{X}_{a}(\delta)\setminus\cup_{b\not\leq a,\,b\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}\,\overline{\mathbf{X}_{b}(3\delta^{1/d_{a}})};\quad\quad d_{a}=\dim\mathbf{X}_{a}.\end{split}

The structure of the sets
\mathbf{X}_{a}(\varepsilon)
,
\mathbf{Y}_{a}(\varepsilon)
and
\mathbf{Z}_{a}(\delta)
is

{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}V}
, where
V
is a
subset of the unit sphere
\mathbf{S}^{a_{0}}\subset\mathbf{X}
. For
\mathbf{X}_{a}(\varepsilon)
and
\mathbf{Z}_{a}(\delta)
the set
V

is relatively open, while for
\mathbf{Y}_{a}(\varepsilon)
the set is compact.
We also record that
\mathbf{X}_{a}\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\mathbf{X}_{a}(\varepsilon)\subseteq\{\lvert x^{a}\rvert<\sqrt{2\ \varepsilon}\lvert x\rvert\}

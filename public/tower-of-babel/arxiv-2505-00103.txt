Origins of Thermalization in Semiclassical Cosmology
Michael Adjei Osei, Arundhati Dasgupta

Abstract
We discuss the effect of accelerated frames in cosmology, and obtain origins of thermalization of the cosmic evolution of the semi-classical universe. We implement a transformation in super-space, and the WKB wave function defined on super space has a Bogoliubov transformation. In the transformed frame we find the density matrix which is mixed, and this is the sign of a thermal system. Whereas this result does not have the same interpretations of a particle thermalization in accelerated frames, one can show the super space transformation similar to a double Rindler transformation of real space-time coordinates. We also discuss the implications of the super-space transformation in loop quantum cosmology.

Appendix A. WKB Solution to the Superspace D’Alembertian

We consider the Wheeler-DeWitt equation in superspace:

\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial\xi^{\prime 2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial\phi^{2}}=0\ \qquad(A.1).

We seek a WKB-type solution of the form:

\Psi(\xi^{\prime},\phi)=A(\xi^{\prime},\phi)e^{iS(\xi^{\prime},\phi)},\ \qquad(A.2)

where
A(\xi^{\prime},\phi)
is a slowly varying amplitude and
S(\xi^{\prime},\phi)
is a rapidly varying phase.

Substituting (A.2) into (A.1) and expanding derivatives, and keeping only leading-order terms in the WKB approximation, we find:

\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial\phi}\right)^{2}=0.\ \qquad{A.3}

Thus,

\frac{\partial S}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}=\pm\frac{\partial S}{\partial\phi}.\ \qquad{A.4}

Integration yields:

S(\xi^{\prime},\phi)=k(\xi^{\prime}\pm\phi)+\mathrm{constant}.\ \qquad{A.5}

Substituting back into (A.2), assuming
A
approximately constant, the WKB solution becomes:

\Psi(\xi^{\prime},\phi)=A_{0}e^{ik(\xi^{\prime}\pm\phi)}.\ \qquad{A.6}

Appendix C. Canonical Time Pair Construction in Superspace

Considering superspace transformations:

p^{\prime}=X\sinh(\alpha T),\quad\phi=X\cosh(\alpha T),\ \qquad{C.1}

the time derivative of
p^{\prime}
is:

\dot{p}^{\prime}=\frac{2c}{\gamma}e^{-p^{\prime}/2}.\ \qquad{C.2}

The Poisson bracket between
T
and
\dot{T}
is:

\{T,\dot{T}\}=\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}\gamma a(4\ln^{2}a+X^{2})}.\ \qquad{C.3}

To define a canonical conjugate, we construct a function
f(\dot{T})
satisfying:

\{T,f(\dot{T})\}=1,\ \qquad{C.4}

yielding:

f(\dot{T})=\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma}{2}a(4\ln^{2}a+X^{2})\dot{T}.\ \qquad{C.5}

Thus,
(T,f(\dot{T}))
forms a canonical pair, enabling the interpretation of time evolution in superspace under accelerated frames.

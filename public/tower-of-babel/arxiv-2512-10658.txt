. Let spt ⁡ ( n ) \operatorname{spt}(n) be the number of smallest parts in the partitions of n n . 1.
Introduction

Let
p(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
. Euler’s recurrence gives an efficient way to compute the partition number:

(1)

\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}p(n-\omega(k))=0,\text{ for }n>0,

where
\omega(k):=(3k^{2}+k)/2
is the pentagonal number. Recently, Gomez, Ono, Saad, and Singh
[
24
]
presented a generalization of (
1
) using the theory of modular forms. They proved that for positive
n
,

(2)

p(n)=\frac{1}{g_{v}(n,0)}\left(\alpha_{v}\sigma_{2v-1}(n)+\operatorname{Tr}_{2v}(n)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus{0}}(-1)^{k}g_{v}(n,k)p(n-\omega(k))\right),

where
\sigma_{2v-1}(n)
is the divisor function,
\operatorname{Tr}_{2v}(n)
is the
n
-th weight
2v
Hecke trace of values of special twisted quadratic Dirichlet series, and each
g_{v}(n,k)
is a polynomial in
n
and
k
. Later, Bhowmik, Tsai and Ye
[
10
]
gave similar Euler-like recursive formulas for the
t
-colored partition function when
t=2
or
t=3
.

The smallest parts function
\operatorname{spt}(n)
, introduced by Andrews
[
9
]
, counts the number of smallest parts in the partitions of
n
. For example, the partitions of
n=5
are (with the smallest parts underlined)

\displaystyle\underline{5},~4+\underline{1},~3+\underline{2},~3+\underline{1}+\underline{1},~2+2+\underline{1},

\displaystyle\underline{2}+\underline{1}+\underline{1}+\underline{1},~\underline{1}+\underline{1}+\underline{1}+\underline{1}+\underline{1},

and thus
\operatorname{spt}(5)=14
. This function not only plays a crucial role in combinatorics but also possesses rich arithmetic properties that have been investigated by many authors (see for example
[
6
,
22
,
23
,
28
]
). The origin of its arithmetic properties is attributed to the connection between its generating function and harmonic Maass forms, see Section
2
. To state our results, we define the following mock Eisenstein series introduced by Zagier
[
32
]
:

F_{k}:=\sum_{n\neq 0}(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{-3}{n-1}\right)n^{k-1}\frac{q^{n(n+1)/6}}{1-q^{n}}=-\sum_{r>s>0}\left(\frac{12}{r^{2}-s^{2}}\right)s^{k-1}q^{rs/6}=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{k}(n)q^{n}.

We have the following Euler-like recurrence for
\operatorname{spt}(n)
(see
[
1
, Theorem 1]
):

\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{spt}(n-\omega(k))=a_{2}(n),\text{ for }n>0.

The primary goal of this paper is to generalize the above Euler-like recurrence, analogous to the generalization presented in (
2
). The differences between the
\operatorname{spt}
function and the partition function lie in two key aspects: first, the generating function of the
\operatorname{spt}
function is no longer a weakly holomorphic modular form but a harmonic Maass form, thus necessitating the employment of the technique of holomorphic projection. Second, the key step in the proof of (
2
) relies on expressing the generating function of the partition function as a Maass-Poincaré series, whereas in our case, the Maass-Poincaré series fails to converge, and we shall adopt the method of analytic continuation to overcome this difficulty.

Before stating our results, we first introduce some notations. For a Hecke eigenform
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)q^{n}
of weight
2k
on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we define its twisted quadratic Dirichlet series by

D_{f}:=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\frac{12}{n}\right)a_{f}\left(\frac{n^{2}-1}{24}\right)\left(\frac{1}{(n-1)^{1+2v}}-\frac{1}{(n+1)^{1+2v}}\right),

(for the convergence of the series, see Section
5.4
), and the weight
2k
Hecke trace by

\operatorname{Tr}_{2k}(n):=\frac{\Gamma(2k-1)}{(\pi/3)^{2k-1}}\sum_{f}a_{f}(n)\frac{D_{f}}{\langle f,f\rangle},

where the sum runs over the normalized Hecke eigenforms of weight
2k
and
\langle f,f\rangle
denotes the Petersson norm of
f
. The Petersson inner product is defined by

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\setminus{\mathbb{H}}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}y^{2k-2}dxdy,~z=x+iy,

for modular forms
f
and
g
of weight
2k
for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with at least one of them a cusp form. Similar series have already appeared in prior works; see, for example,
[
17
,
27
]
. We remark here that although the notation adopted here coincides with that in (
2
), their specific definitions are entirely different.

Define the polynomials
q_{v}(n,k)
by

q_{v}(n,k)=2^{-2v}\cdot\sum_{0\leq j\leq v}\binom{2v+1}{2j+1}\left((6k+1)^{2}-24n\right)^{j}(6k+1)^{2v-2j}.

The polynomials
q_{v}(n,k)
are indeed the Chebyshev polynomials, they can be expressed by

(3)

q_{v}(n,k)=(6n)^{v}U_{2v}\left(\frac{6k+1}{\sqrt{24n}}\right).

Let
s(n):=12\operatorname{spt}(n)+(24n-1)p(n)
(by convention, we agree that
\operatorname{spt}(0)=0
and
p(0)=1
) and

\mathcal{S}_{v}(z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 0\\
k\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k+1}q_{v}(n,k)\cdot s(n-\omega(k))q^{n},~q=e^{2\pi iz}.

The main theorem of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1
.

For every
v\geq 1
, we have

\mathcal{S}_{v}(z)=E_{2v+2}(z)-12F_{2v+2}(z)+\frac{\Gamma(2v+1)}{(\pi/3)^{2v+1}}\sum_{f}\frac{D_{f}}{\langle f,f\rangle}f(z),

where the sum runs over the normalized Hecke eigenforms of weight
2v+2
and
E_{2v+2}(z)
is the weight
2v+2
Eisenstein series.

Examining the coefficient of
q^{n}
in Theorem
1
, we obtain the following recurrence relation.

Corollary 2
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle q_{v}(n,0)s(n)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}(-1)^{k+1}q_{v}(n,k)s(n-\omega(k))

\displaystyle+\frac{4v+4}{B_{2v+2}}\sigma_{2v+1}(n)+12a_{2v+2}(n)-\operatorname{Tr}_{2v+2}(n),

where
B_{n}
is the
n
-th Bernoulli number and
\sigma_{2v+1}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d^{2v+1}
.

Although Corollary
2
yields only a recurrence relation for
s(n)
, for small values of
v
, using the convolution of divisor functions, we can obtain an explicit recurrence for
\operatorname{spt}(n)
. We illustrate this with several examples below. For notational convenience, we set
\sigma_{2k-1}(0):=-\frac{B_{2k}}{4k}
.

Example 1
.

Corollary
2
with
v=1
gives

\displaystyle(1-6n)\operatorname{spt}(n)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}(-1)^{k+1}\left[(6k+1)^{2}-6n\right]\cdot\operatorname{spt}(n-\omega(k))

\displaystyle-10\sigma_{3}(n)+24\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}(i)\sigma_{1}(j)+a_{4}(n).

Corollary
2
with
v=2
gives

\displaystyle(36n^{2}-18n)\operatorname{spt}(n)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}(-1)^{k+1}\left[36n^{2}-18n(6k+1)^{2}+(6k+1)^{4}\right]\cdot\operatorname{spt}(n-\omega(k))

\displaystyle-576\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=n\\
i,j,k\geq 0\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}(i)\sigma_{1}(j)\sigma_{1}(k)+240\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}(i)\sigma_{3}(j)+a_{6}(n).

When
v=5
, the space of weight 12 cusp forms is one-dimensional, spanned by the Ramanujan
\Delta
function. We find that

(4)

\operatorname{Tr}_{12}(n)=-\frac{226437120}{691}\tau(n),

and Corollary
2
with
v=5
gives

\tau(n)=\frac{691}{226437120}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}q_{5}(n,k)s(n-\omega(k))+\frac{1}{3456}\sigma_{11}(n)-\frac{691}{18869760}a_{12}(n).

Using the relationship between the
\operatorname{spt}
function and the second Atkin-Garvan moment, Andrews derived the following elegant congruences for the
\operatorname{spt}
function:

(5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{spt}\left(5n+4\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5},

\displaystyle\operatorname{spt}(7n+5)\equiv 0\pmod{7},

\displaystyle\operatorname{spt}(13n+6)\equiv 0\pmod{13}.

These congruences are reminiscent of Ramanujan’s partition congruences

(6)

\displaystyle p\left(5n+4\right)\equiv 0\pmod{5},

\displaystyle p(7n+5)\equiv 0\pmod{7},

\displaystyle p(11n+6)\equiv 0\pmod{11}.

Using the recurrence relation (
2
), Bringmann, Craig and Ono
[
13
]
gave a closed form expressions of the power series

\sum_{n\geq 0}p(\ell n-\delta_{\ell})q^{n}\pmod{\ell},~\delta_{\ell}:=\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24},

by the generating function for the Hecke traces of
\ell
ramified values of special Dirichlet series for weight
\ell-1
cusp forms on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
:

\sum_{n\geq 1}p(\ell n-\delta_{\ell})q^{n}\equiv c_{\ell}\frac{\sum_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{Tr}_{\ell-1}(\ell n)q^{n}}{(q^{\ell};q^{\ell})_{\infty}}\pmod{\ell},

for a explicit constant
c_{\ell}
. (We caution the reader that the symbol
\operatorname{Tr}
in the preceding formula follows the convention of (
2
) and should not be confused with the notation for
\operatorname{Tr}
used elsewhere in this paper.) Since there are no nontrivial cusp forms of weight 4, 6, and 10, this gives a new proof of Ramanujan’s congruences (
6
). We establish an analogous version of the identity for the
\operatorname{spt}
function as follows:

Corollary 3
.

Let
(q;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})
. For every prime
\ell\geq 5
, we have

\sum_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{spt}(\ell n-\delta_{\ell})q^{n}\equiv 12^{-1}\left(\frac{3}{\ell}\right)\frac{\sum_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{Tr}_{\ell+1}(\ell n)q^{n}}{(q^{\ell};q^{\ell})_{\infty}}\pmod{\ell}.

Since there are no nontrivial cusp forms of weight 6, 8, and 14, the above result gives a new proof of Andrews’ congruences (
5
). The situation for other primes is also interesting. For example, when
\ell=11
, by (
4
) and
\Delta(z)=q(q;q)^{24}
, Corollary
3
yields

\sum_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{spt}(11n+6)q^{n}\equiv 4(q;q)_{\infty}^{13}\pmod{11}.

This gives a new proof of
[
23
, Theorem 6.1(6.3)]
. The Ramanujan conjecture, asserting that simple congruences analogous to those in (
6
) fail to hold for primes
\ell\geq 13
, was ultimately solved by Ahlgren and Boylan
[
5
]
. A similar conjecture exists for
\operatorname{spt}(n)
, supported by extensive numerical evidence (see
[
8
, Theorem 3]
). It is further proved in
[
8
, Theorem 3]
that the set of primes
\ell
satisfying congruence has natural density zero. We may formulate this conjecture, in terms of Hecke trace.
For prime
\ell\geq 5
, if

\operatorname{spt}(\ell n-\delta_{\ell})\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\text{, for every }n,

then

\operatorname{Tr}_{\ell+1}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\text{, for every }n.

Using a theorem of Garthwaite and Jameson
[
21
]
, we prove the following incongruence property for the
\operatorname{spt}
function.

Corollary 4
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime and
t_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,\ell-1\}
. If
\left(\frac{1-24t_{0}}{\ell}\right)=-1
and
\ell\nmid\operatorname{spt}(t_{0})
, then for all
t\in\{0,\ldots,\ell-1\}
such that

t\equiv t_{0}d^{2}+\frac{1-d^{2}}{24}\pmod{\ell},

for some
d
with
\gcd(d,6\ell)=1
, we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{spt}(\ell n+t)q^{n}\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}.

For example, choose
t_{0}=1
, then the above result implies that for all prime
\ell
with
\left(\frac{-23}{\ell}\right)=-1
and all
t
satisfying

t\equiv\frac{1+23d^{2}}{24}\pmod{\ell},~\gcd(d,6\ell)=1,

(these
t
range over all integers satisfying
\left(\frac{1-24t}{\ell}\right)=-1
) we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{spt}(\ell n+t)q^{n}\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary notation and, in particular, recall the definition and basic properties of the harmonic Maass form. In Section
3
, we explicitly compute the holomorphic projection of the harmonic Maass form associated with the spt function. In Section
4
, we construct the Maass-Poincaré series of weight
3/2
and show that their holomorphic part is precisely the generating function for the spt function. The results stated in Section
1
are proved in Section
5
.

2.
Weak Harmonic Maass Forms

In this section, we set notations and recall some fundamental facts regarding harmonic Maass forms. Let
f
be a smooth function defined on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
. For
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
and
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, we define the weight
k
slash operator
\big|_{k}
by

(f\big|_{k}\gamma)(z):=(\det\gamma)^{k/2}(cz+d)^{-k}f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right).

A smooth function
f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called a weak harmonic Maass form of weight
k
and multiplier
\chi
for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
if it satisfies

(f\big|_{k}\gamma)(z)=\chi(\gamma)f(z)\text{ for every }\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z});

and is annihilated by the weight
k
hyperbolic Laplacian

\Delta_{k}:=-y^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial^{2}x}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial^{2}y}\right)+iky\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right),z=x+iy;

and has the Fourier expansion of the form

f(z)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{f}^{+}(n)q^{\frac{n}{m}}+c_{f}(0)y^{1-k}+\sum_{-n\ll\infty}c_{f}^{-}(-n)\Gamma\left(1-k,4\pi\frac{n}{m}y\right)q^{-\frac{n}{m}},q=e^{2\pi iz},

where
\Gamma(1-k,y)
denotes the incomplete gamma function and
m
is defined by the identity
\chi\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)=e\left(\frac{\alpha}{m}\right)
,
e(x):=e^{2\pi ix}
,
\alpha
and
m
co-prime. We denote the space of functions satisfying these properties by
H_{k}^{!}(\chi)
. We adopt the customary notation
M_{k}^{!}(\chi),M_{k}(\chi),S_{k}(\chi)
denote the spaces of weakly holomorphic modular forms, modular forms and cusp forms, respectively. When
\chi
is the trivial character, we omit it from the notation.

The Rankin-Cohen bracket for two functions
f
and
g
, is defined by

(7)

[f,g]_{v}:=\sum_{0\leq j\leq v}(-1)^{j}\binom{k+v-1}{v-j}\binom{l+v-1}{j}\operatorname{D}^{j}f\operatorname{D}^{v-j}g,

where
\operatorname{D}f:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{df}{dz}=q\frac{df}{dq}
. It is known that (see
[
16
, Theorem 7.1]
)

[f\big|_{k}\gamma,g\big|_{l}\gamma]_{v}=[f,g]_{v}\big|_{k+l+2v}\gamma,~\forall\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

Thus if
f
and
g
transform like modular forms of weights
k
and
l
with multiplier systems
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
, respectively, then
[f,g]_{v}
transforms like a modular form of weight
k+l
with multiplier system
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
.

In this paper, we are interested in the multiplier system
\varepsilon
which is attached to the Dedekind eta function
\eta
, defined by

\eta(z)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})=\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi_{12}(n)q^{\frac{n^{2}}{24}},

where
\chi_{12}(n):=\left(\frac{12}{n}\right)
. Dedekind eta function has the transform property

(\eta\big|_{1/2}\gamma)(z)=\varepsilon(\gamma)\eta(z),\forall\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),

which means
\eta\in M_{1/2}(\varepsilon)
. It is well-known that the eta multiplier
\epsilon
satisfies
\varepsilon\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)=e(\frac{1}{24})
and for
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with
c>0
,

(8)

\varepsilon(\gamma)=\sqrt{-i}e^{-\pi is(d,c)}e\left(\frac{a+d}{24c}\right),

where
s(d,c)
is the Dedekind sum, see
[
29
, 71.21]
.

Bringmann
[
12
]
showed that the generating function of the
\operatorname{spt}
function, which is the primary focus of this paper, belongs to
H^{!}_{3/2}(\overline{\varepsilon})
when supplemented with its non-holomorphic part, see also
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
.
Let

(9)

\alpha(z):=-\sum_{-1<n\equiv-1\mod 24}\left(12\operatorname{spt}\left(\frac{n+1}{24}\right)+np\left(\frac{n+1}{24}\right)\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}},

then the function

(10)

\displaystyle F(z)

\displaystyle=\alpha(z)+\frac{6\sqrt{3}i}{\pi}\int_{-\overline{z}}^{i\infty}\frac{\eta(w)}{(-i(w+z))^{3/2}}dw

\displaystyle=\alpha(z)-\frac{3}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{0>n\equiv-1\mod 24}\chi_{12}(\sqrt{-n})|n|^{1/2}\Gamma\left(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{\pi ny}{6}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}},

where
\chi_{12}(\sqrt{-n})
is defined to be zero if
-n
is not a square, belongs to the space
H^{!}_{3/2}(\overline{\varepsilon})
.

3.
Holomorphic Projection

As noted in the preceding section, our generating function for the spt function is a non-holomorphic modular form. In order to bring its analysis into the realm of holomorphic modular forms, we apply the method of holomorphic projection. Suppose we have a weight
k
real-analytic modular form
\tilde{f}
with moderate growth at the cusps. Then this function defines a linear functional on the space of weight
k
holomorphic cusp forms by
g\rightarrow\langle g,\tilde{f}\rangle
, this functional must be given by
\langle\cdot,f\rangle
for a holomorphic cusp form
f
of the same weight. This
f
is essentially the
holomorphic projection of
\tilde{f}
, which we defined as
\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}(\tilde{f})
. We recall some basic properties about holomorphic projection (see
[
14
, 10.1]
).

Proposition 5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function transforming like a modular form
of weight
k\geq 2
on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with Fourier expansion

f(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{f}(n,y)q^{n},

and assume that
f
satisfies the following:

(i).

f(z)=c_{0}+O(y^{-\delta})
for some
\delta>0
as
y\rightarrow\infty
.

(ii).

a_{f}(n,y)=O(y^{1-k+\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon>0
as
y\rightarrow\infty
for all
n
.

The following are true.

(1).

\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}(\tilde{f})=c_{0}+\sum_{n\geq 1}c(n)q^{n},

where

c(n)=\frac{(4\pi n)^{k-1}}{\Gamma(k-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}a_{f}(n,y)e^{-4\pi ny}y^{k-2}dy.

(2).

If
k=2
,
\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}(f)
is a quasi-modular forms and for
k\geq 4
,
\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}(f)
is a modular form.

(3).

\langle f,g\rangle=\langle\pi_{hol}(f),g\rangle
for every
g\in S_{k}
.

For
k\geq 1
, we recall the mock Eisenstein series as

F_{k}:=\sum_{n\neq 0}(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{-3}{n-1}\right)n^{k-1}\frac{q^{n(n+1)/6}}{1-q^{n}}=-\sum_{r>s>0}\left(\frac{12}{r^{2}-s^{2}}\right)s^{k-1}q^{rs/6}.

The following result was already stated (without proof) by Zagier
[
32
]
and given a brief proof (without explicit computation) by Ahlgren and Kim
[
8
]
. For the sake of completeness, we provide the detailed computation here.

Proposition 6
.

Let
F
be the function defined in
(
10
)
, then

\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}(F\eta)=\alpha\eta+12F_{2}=E_{2},

and for
v\geq 1
,

\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}([F,\eta]_{v})=[\alpha,\eta]_{v}+24^{-v}\binom{2v}{v}\cdot 12F_{2v+2}\in M_{2v+2}.

Proof.

Expressing
F
as
\alpha+NH
, the holomorphic projection of
[F,\eta]_{v}
takes the form

[\alpha(z),\eta(z)]_{v}+\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}([NH(z),\eta(z)]_{v}).

To evaluate
\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}([NH(z),\eta(z)]_{v})
, we make use of a result of Mertens
[
26
]
, which combines Theorem 4.6 and Proposition 5.3 of
[
26
]
. We state it as follows:

\text{ If }f^{-}=\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{f}(n)n^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(-\tfrac{1}{2},4\pi ny)q^{-n}\text{ and }g=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{g}(n)q^{n},

then

\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}([f^{-},g]_{v})=2^{-2v+1}\binom{2v}{v}\sqrt{\pi}\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{m-n=N}(m^{\frac{1}{2}}-n^{\frac{1}{2}})^{2v+1}a_{g}(m)c_{f}(n)q^{N}.

In order to align with the assumptions of the aforementioned result, we proceed to evaluate
\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}([NH(24z),\eta(24z)]_{v})
. Using the fact that

a_{g}(m)=\chi_{12}(\sqrt{m})\text{ and }c_{f}(n)=-\frac{3}{\sqrt{\pi}}\chi_{12}(\sqrt{n}),

for
g=\eta(24z)
and
f=NH(24z)
, we obtain

\pi_{\operatorname{hol}}([NH(24z),\eta(24z)]_{v})=-3\cdot 2^{-2v+1}\binom{2v}{v}\sum_{N\geq 1}\sum_{m^{2}-n^{2}=N}(m-n)^{2v+1}\left(\frac{12}{mn}\right)q^{N}.

In the above identity, replace
(m+n)/2
by
r
and
(m-n)/2
by
s
. The right-hand side then becomes
\binom{2v}{v}12\cdot F_{2v+2}(24z)
. Finally, observing that

[NH(24z),\eta(24z)]_{v}=24^{-v}\left([NH,\eta]_{v}\right)(24z),

we complete the proof of the result.
∎

4.
Maass-Poincaré series with eta multiplier

To define the Maass-Poincaré series, we first recall definitions of the Whittaker functions
M_{\mu,\nu}(y)
and
W_{\mu,\nu}(y)
, they are linearly independent solutions of the differential equation

(11)

\frac{d^{2}w}{dy^{2}}+\left(-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\mu}{y}+\frac{\frac{1}{4}-\nu^{2}}{y^{2}}\right)=0.

At the special point
s=k/2
, we have

Proposition 7

Let

where

g_{m,n}(s):=\frac{2\pi i^{-k-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(2s)\sqrt{|m/n|}}{\Gamma\left(s+\operatorname{sgn}(n)\frac{k}{2}\right)}

\sum\limits_{c>0}\frac{K(-m^{\prime},-n^{\prime};c)}{c}I_{2s-1}\left(\frac{\pi\sqrt{|mn|}}{6c}\right)&\text{ if }mn<0,\end{cases}

As the function

The Fourier coefficients can be computed as

\displaystyle a(n,y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c>0\\
d(c^{*})\end{subarray}}\frac{\varepsilon(\gamma)}{c^{k}}e\left(\frac{\overline{d}m+d(24n-1)}{24c}\right)

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}z^{-k}\varphi_{m,s}\left(\frac{y}{24c^{2}|z|^{2}}\right)e\left(-\frac{mx}{24c^{2}|z|^{2}}-(n-\tfrac{1}{24})x\right)dx.

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n\geq 0}n^{2v-2j}\chi_{12}(n)\left(\left(\frac{\pi y}{6}\right)^{-i_{1}-\frac{i_{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{4}}M_{-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{i_{2}}{2},s-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i_{2}}{2}}\left(\frac{\pi y}{6}\right)e\left(-\frac{x}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n^{2}}{24}}\right)\bigg|_{k}\gamma.

Using the unfolding method, and since the Fourier coefficients of eigenform are real, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{s}

\displaystyle=(24)^{-v}\sum_{0\leq j\leq v}\binom{v+\frac{1}{2}}{v-j}\binom{v-\frac{1}{2}}{j}\sum_{0\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq j}\frac{j!}{i_{1}!i_{2}!(j-i_{1}-i_{2})!}(-1)^{i_{2}}\frac{(s-\tfrac{3}{4})^{\underline{i_{1}}}(s-\tfrac{3}{4})^{\overline{i_{2}}}}{(2s)^{\overline{i_{2}}}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n\geq 0}n^{2v-2j}\chi_{12}(n)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\pi y}{6}\right)^{-i_{1}-\frac{i_{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{4}}M_{-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{i_{2}}{2},s-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i_{2}}{2}}\left(\frac{\pi y}{6}\right)e^{-2\pi(\tfrac{n^{2}}{24}+m)y}y^{2v}dy

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{f}(m)e\left(\frac{n^{2}-24m-1}{24}x\right)dx.

The integral with respect to
x
in the above expression vanishes unless
m=\frac{n^{2}-1}{24}
, yielding

\displaystyle I_{s}

\displaystyle=(24)^{-v}\sum_{0\leq j\leq v}\binom{v+\frac{1}{2}}{v-j}\binom{v-\frac{1}{2}}{j}\sum_{0\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq j}\frac{j!}{i_{1}!i_{2}!(j-i_{1}-i_{2})!}(-1)^{i_{2}}\frac{(s-\tfrac{3}{4})^{\underline{i_{1}}}(s-\tfrac{3}{4})^{\overline{i_{2}}}}{(2s)^{\overline{i_{2}}}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n\geq 0}n^{2v-2j}\chi_{12}(n)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\pi y}{6}\right)^{-i_{1}-\frac{i_{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{4}}M_{-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{i_{2}}{2},s-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i_{2}}{2}}\left(\frac{\pi y}{6}\right)e^{-\frac{\pi(2n^{2}-1)}{12}}y^{2v}dy.

We now employ the following formula (see
[
18
, 13.23.1]
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle e^{-zt}t^{\alpha-1}M_{\mu,v}(\beta t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{v+1/2}\Gamma(v+\alpha+1/2)}{(z+\beta/2)^{v+\alpha+1/2}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(v-\mu+1/2,v+\alpha+1/2;2v+1;\frac{2\beta}{\beta+2z}\right),

where
{}_{2}F_{1}
is the Gaussian hypergeometric function, thereby obtain

(21)

\displaystyle I_{s}

\displaystyle=(24)^{-v}\sum_{0\leq j\leq v}\binom{v+\frac{1}{2}}{v-j}\binom{v-\frac{1}{2}}{j}\sum_{0\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq j}\frac{j!}{i_{1}!i_{2}!(j-i_{1}-i_{2})!}(-1)^{i_{2}}\frac{(s-\tfrac{3}{4})^{\underline{i_{1}}}(s-\tfrac{3}{4})^{\overline{i_{2}}}}{(2s)^{\overline{i_{2}}}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{6}{\pi}\right)^{1+2v}\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{2v-2j}\chi_{12}(n)a_{f}\left(\frac{n^{2}-1}{24}\right)\frac{\Gamma(s-i_{1}+2v+\tfrac{1}{4})}{n^{2(s-i_{1}+2v+\frac{1}{4})}}

\displaystyle\times{}_{2}F_{1}\left(s+\frac{3}{4},s-i_{1}+2v+\frac{1}{4};2s+i_{2};\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

We claim that the series in
n
in (
21
) is holomorphic at 3/4.
Consequently,
I_{s}
is defined at
s=3/4
, and by analytic continuation equals
\langle[F,\eta]_{v},f\rangle
. To establish our claim, it suffices to show that the series

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\chi_{12}(n)a_{f}(\frac{n^{2}-1}{24})}{n^{2(s+v+\tfrac{1}{4})}}

converges at
s=3/4
. Choose
1\leq M_{n}\leq 6
with
M_{n}\equiv\frac{n+1}{2}
if
n\not\equiv 7\pmod{12}
and
M_{n}=2
if
n\equiv 7\pmod{12}
. We factor
\frac{n^{2}-1}{24}
as a product of two coprime positive integers:

\frac{n^{2}-1}{24}=\frac{n+1}{2M_{n}}\cdot\frac{(n-1)M_{n}}{12}.

Since
f
is a Hecke eigenform, we have

a_{f}\left(\frac{n^{2}-1}{24}\right)=a_{f}\left(\frac{n+1}{2M_{n}}\right)a_{f}\left(\frac{(n-1)M_{n}}{12}\right).

Now we use a result of Blomer
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
and choose a suitable test function as in the proof of
[
11
, Corollary 1.4]
. We then have the estimate

\sum_{1\leq n\leq x}\chi_{12}(n)a_{f}\left(\frac{n+1}{2M_{n}}\right)a_{f}\left(\frac{(n-1)M_{n}}{12}\right)\ll x^{2v+2-\delta}

with some
\delta>0
, which via partial summation, establishes our claim.

As
s\to 3/4
, the presence of the factor
s-3/4
in (
21
) ensures that all sums vanish except in the case
i_{1}=i_{2}=0
. Noting that

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{3}{2},1+2v;\frac{3}{2};\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)=\left(\frac{n^{2}}{n^{2}-1}\right)^{1+2v}.

We have

(22)

\displaystyle\langle[F,\eta]_{v},f\rangle

\displaystyle=24^{-v}\left(\frac{6}{\pi}\right)^{1+2v}\Gamma(1+2v)\sum_{0\leq j\leq v}\binom{v+\frac{1}{2}}{v-j}\binom{v-\frac{1}{2}}{j}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{f}(\frac{n^{2}-1}{24})\chi_{12}(n)}{n^{2+2v+2j}}\left(\frac{n^{2}}{n^{2}-1}\right)^{1+2v}.

Apply (
16
) together with

\sum_{0\leq j\leq v}\binom{2v+1}{2j+1}\frac{2}{n^{1+2j}}=\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{1+2v}-\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{1+2v},

and obtain

(23)

\sum_{0\leq j\leq v}\binom{v+\frac{1}{2}}{v-j}\binom{v-\frac{1}{2}}{j}\frac{1}{n^{2j+1}}=2^{-2v-1}\binom{2v}{v}\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{1+2v}-\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{1+2v}\right).

Insertion of (
23
) into (
22
), followed by straightforward computations, completes the proof of the theorem.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a global health challenge, ranking as a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1–6]. For patients with early-stage disease, image-guided thermal ablation, primarily radiofrequency ablation (RFA) and increasingly popular microwave ablation (MWA), has been firmly established as a curative-intent treatment modality [7–11], offering outcomes comparable to surgical resection for select tumors while preserving liver parenchyma [12–17]. However, the long-term efficacy of ablation is fundamentally constrained by high rates of tumor recurrence, including local tumor progression (LTP) at the ablation site and intrahepatic distant recurrence [18–21]. Recurrence dictates patient prognosis and informs complex clinical decisions regarding surveillance strategies, adjuvant therapies, and salvage treatments. Consequently, understanding the mechanisms, risk factors, and management of post-ablation recurrence has emerged as a central and dynamic research frontier in HCC management.

Recently, Song et al. [22] provided a macroscopic bibliometric overview of the entire field of ablation for HCC. Building upon this foundation, the present study offers the first focused and in-depth analysis of the critical issue of post-ablation recurrence, which is the principal determinant of long-term patient outcomes after thermal ablation. Over the past two decades, research has focused on elucidating the determinants of recurrence, ranging from technical factors (e.g., ablation modality, margin adequacy) and tumor biology to host liver characteristics and systemic therapies [23]. These efforts have generated a large, multifaceted body of literature. Although traditional narrative and systematic reviews have synthesized evidence on specific clinical or mechanistic questions, a macroscopic, quantitative synthesis of the entire research landscape is lacking. In particular, the broader intellectual landscape, including global collaborative networks, the evolution of research hotspots, and the conceptual interrelationships among themes, remains uncharted. Such a holistic analysis can help new investigators navigate the field, established researchers identify emerging trends and collaboration opportunities, and funding agencies allocate resources strategically.

Bibliometrics, the quantitative analysis of scholarly publications, provides a powerful approach to address this gap. Applying science mapping techniques to a defined literature corpus can help objectively visualize research trends, collaboration networks, and the conceptual dynamics of a scientific domain [24]. Given that post-ablation recurrence is a central challenge in the multidisciplinary management of HCC, mapping this specific research landscape is crucial for guiding clinical decisions and future investigations in surgical and interventional oncology. Therefore, we hypothesized that a systematic bibliometric analysis of the literature on HCC recurrence after thermal ablation would reveal the following: (1) key contributors and collaboration networks shaping the field; (2) a well-defined, evolving thematic structure highlighting sustained and emerging research frontiers; and (3) a clear trajectory from foundational technical studies toward precision oncology and comparative effectiveness research. To test this hypothesis, we conducted a comprehensive bibliometric study encompassing publications from 2005 to 2025. This study aimed to provide the first holistic overview of the research landscape on post-ablation recurrence in HCC, delineating its intellectual foundations and evolution to inform future research and collaboration.

Materials and methods

Literature search and screening

A systematic literature search was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database. Studies published between January 1, 2005, and August 31, 2025, were included. All data were obtained from a public database and did not include human participants; therefore, ethical approval or patient consent was not required. To minimize bias introduced by frequent database updates, all searches were completed on a single day (August 31, 2025).

The search strategy used the following terms: TS = [(“hepatocellular carcinoma” OR HCC OR “primary liver cancer”) AND (“radiofrequency ablation” OR RFA OR “microwave ablation” OR MWA) AND (recurr* OR relapse* OR “tumor progression” OR “disease recurrence” OR “local tumor progression” OR LTP OR “intrahepatic recurrence” OR “distant recurrence” OR “recurrence mechanism” OR “risk prediction” OR “prognostic model” OR “biomarker” OR “incomplete ablation” OR “insufficient RFA”)]. The search was limited to the following document types: Article, Review Article, Proceeding Paper, and Early Access. The initial search yielded 3,673 records. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) duplicate papers; (2) non-human studies; (3) retracted papers; (4) non-analytical publications (editorials, news items, letters, case reports, and studies unrelated to burns, shock, or related symptoms); (5) ablation not primary therapy or no ablation-specific data; (6) malignancies other than HCC; (7) recurrence not classified according to the 2018 Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System (LI-RADS) version; (8) combined therapies without separable ablation data; (9) population > 50% with Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage C (advanced) or stage D (terminal) without subgroup analysis; (10) no analysis of recurrence-associated factors; and (11) irretrievable key outcome data. Data extraction and management

Two reviewers (YYZ and WJM) independently conducted the primary search and extracted data from all eligible studies. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion or, when necessary, adjudication by a third senior researcher (YFC). The extracted data included titles, journals, publication dates, countries and regions, authors, keywords, digital object identifier (DOI) numbers, and total number of citations. Data were managed using Microsoft Excel 2016 (Redmond, WA, USA). Data extraction and bibliometric mapping were conducted using VOSviewer (v1.6.20, Leiden University, Leiden, Netherlands) and CiteSpace (v6.4.R2, Drexel University, Philadelphia, PA, USA). Statistical analyses of publication metrics were performed using GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Inc., San Diego, CA, USA).

Bibliometric analysis

Bibliometric analysis was performed to evaluate the research trends and intellectual structures, encompassing performance analysis of publication patterns and science mapping of conceptual relationships. Before network construction, author names and institution names were standardized to ensure consistency. For authors, hyphens were removed from given names (e.g., “Chen, Min-Shan” was revised to “Chen, Minshan”), title case was applied using the PROPER function (e.g., “liang, ping” became “Liang, Ping”), and common acronyms (PLA, USA, MRI, CT, NHS) were manually restored to uppercase. For institutions, abbreviated terms were expanded (e.g., “Univ” to “University”, “Hosp” to “Hospital”), titles were set to title case, and acronyms were capitalized. VOSviewer was used to construct and visualize collaborative networks among institutions, authors (based on co-authorship data), and keywords (based on co-occurrence data). For co-authorship analysis, the threshold was set to a minimum of five documents per author/institution. For the keyword network, the minimum occurrence was 10. Layout parameters were set to attraction = 2 and repulsion = − 3 for co-authorship networks and attraction = 2 and repulsion = − 1 for the keyword network, with the “LinLog/modularity” normalization method applied for clustering. Only the largest connected component was visualized for co‑authorship networks to focus on substantive collaborative relationships. Node size represents frequency, link strength indicates co-occurrence, and colors identify distinct thematic clusters.

CiteSpace was used for journal co-citation analysis, keyword burst detection, and timeline visualization to trace thematic evolution across time slices (2005–2025). For journal co‑citation analysis, the g‑index (k = 10) was applied to select the most representative cited journals. The network was clustered using the spectral clustering algorithm implemented in CiteSpace, and cluster labels were extracted from the titles of citing articles using the log‑likelihood ratio algorithm, which identifies terms that are most characteristic of each cluster based on statistical significance. The dual‑map overlay function was used to visualize disciplinary knowledge flows, with citation trajectories represented as colored paths between citing and cited journals. In the network visualizations, nodes represent entities, with their proximity and linkages reflecting relational strength and patterns within the scholarly landscape.

Results

Literature identification and screening

The initial systematic search of the WoSCC yielded 3,673 publications related to recurrence following thermal ablation for HCC. A rigorous, two-step screening process was implemented according to predefined eligibility criteria (Fig. 1). After removing six duplicate records, the titles and abstracts of 3667 studies were evaluated, followed by a full-text review. Following this stringent screening, 969 studies were identified as eligible for final inclusion.

Temporal trends in publication and citation metrics

The annual publication volume and associated citation impact exhibited a marked upward trajectory, reflecting the growing significance of this field (Fig. 2a). The cumulative citation counts for the included publications demonstrated an exponential increase over time (Fig. 2b). Analysis of the average citations per article revealed a distinct peak during 2010–2015, coinciding with the publication of seminal studies that established the efficacy of thermal ablation. The subsequent, gradual normalization of this metric indicated a phase of rapid expansion and knowledge dissemination within the field (Fig. 2c).

Global contributions and collaborative networks

Research output was distributed across 42 countries/regions. China was the most prolific contributor, accounting for 49.5% (n = 480) of total publications, followed by Japan (17.6%, n = 171), South Korea (12.9%, n = 125), the United States (8.0%, n = 78), and Italy (6.9%, n = 67) (Fig. 3a). However, the annual publication trends varied among the top five contributing countries. Specifically, China demonstrated the most rapid growth, particularly after 2015, whereas Japan and South Korea demonstrated steady increases (Fig. 3b–d). The United States and Italy exhibited comparatively slower, albeit consistent, growth over the study period (Fig. 3e, f). Network analysis of international co-authorships revealed a polycentric collaborative structure (Fig. 3g). A dominant East Asian cluster interconnected China, Japan, and South Korea, reflecting strong regional research ties. A secondary, trans-Atlantic hub centered on the United States demonstrated links with multiple European and Asian partners, indicating broader global research exchanges.

Analysis of productive journals and their intellectual foundations

The 969 publications were disseminated across 221 academic journals. The International Journal of Hyperthermia was the most productive journal (n = 66, 6.81%), followed by the Hepatology Research (n = 30, 3.10%), World Journal of Gastroenterology (n = 28, 2.89%), and European Radiology (n = 27, 2.79%) (Fig. 4a). The dominant citation flow originated from journals in “Medicine, Medical, Clinical” (left) and was received by journals in “Health, Nursing, Medicine” (right), with a path of z = 5.0269, f = 4,537. A secondary trajectory flowed from “Molecular, Biology, Immunology” to “Molecular, Biology, Genetics” (Fig. 4b). The journal co-citation network comprised 271 nodes and 1,294 links, with a modularity Q value of 0.3499 and a weighted mean silhouette score of 0.7067. Seven clusters were identified and labeled as: #0 retrospective analysis, #1 radiofrequency ablation, #2 microwave ablation, #3 evidence‑based management, #4 hepatic resection, #5 laparoscopic radiofrequency ablation, and #6 small hepatocellular carcinoma (Fig. 4c).

Institutional productivity and collaboration patterns

A total of 952 institutions contributed to the literature. Sun Yat-sen University (China) led in publication output (n = 225, 23.22%), followed by Sungkyunkwan University (South Korea, n = 173, 17.85%), People’s Liberation Army General Hospital (China, n = 83, 8.57%), and Seoul National University (South Korea, n = 72, 7.43%) (Fig. 5a). The institutional collaboration network, comprising the largest connected component of 70 institutions (each with ≥ 5 publications), delineated several tightly knit, often regionally focused, research consortia, with major academic medical centers serving as the primary hubs (Fig. 5b). Research hotspots and conceptual structure

Keyword co-occurrence analysis of 1867 terms yielded a network of 162 keywords meeting the minimum occurrence threshold of 10. Using the LinLog/modularity clustering algorithm, the network resolved into four major thematic clusters (Fig. 7a). The first cluster (red) centered on prognosis and integrated management and included long-term outcomes, survival, and combination therapies, such as transarterial chemoembolization (TACE). The second cluster (green) focused on the comparative effectiveness between surgical resection and thermal ablation. The third cluster (blue) encompassed ablation technique and safety, including specific modalities such as RFA and MWA, complications, and local tumor control. The fourth cluster (yellow) highlighted precision and image-guided therapy, featuring key frontiers such as ablation margin assessment and advanced imaging guidance. Burst detection analysis identified keywords with sharp increases in citation frequency, signaling emerging frontiers (Fig. 7b). Early bursts pertained to foundational techniques such as percutaneous ethanol injection (2005–2014), followed by bursts around quality assessment metrics such as ablative margin assessment (2010–2013) and prognostic factors (2011–2017). The most recent and ongoing bursts were dominated by MWA (2021–2025) and comparative studies with liver resection (2022–2025).

Evolution of research themes

The timeline visualization of keyword evolution synthesized the dynamic progression of research foci (Fig. 8). The field originated from foundational themes of technique comparison and safety. From approximately 2010, research increasingly focused on quantitative assessment of local control and prognosis. The most recent phase (post-2019) was characterized by the convergence of several advanced themes: maturation of MWA as a primary modality, sophisticated comparative effectiveness studies against surgery, and sustained emphasis on precision image-guidance to optimize local outcomes. This trajectory reflected a shift from establishing technical feasibility toward optimizing therapeutic precision and long-term patient outcomes.

Discussion

Overall findings and paradigm shift

The present study is the first comprehensive bibliometric analysis to map the global research landscape, intellectual structure, and evolutionary trajectory of HCC recurrence following thermal ablation. Our quantitative synthesis of two decades of scientific literature revealed an exponential growth in research output, dominated by contributions from East Asia, and delineated a paradigmatic shift from early technical validation toward a modern focus on precision oncology, advanced imaging, and personalized risk stratification. Our findings align with and extend the work of Song et al. [22], who charted the broad landscape of HCC ablation research. While their study provided a valuable macroscopic view, our focused analysis on post-ablation recurrence revealed the specific intellectual pillars and evolutionary trajectory of this most critical subfield. This targeted analysis enabled us to identify the key players, debates, and frontiers directly related to the improvement of long-term survival, offering a strategic framework that complements broader field analysis.

Interpretation of key findings: dominance, collaboration, and thematic maturation

Our analysis synthesized key quantitative findings to reveal the defining characteristics of the research landscape. A notable observation was the overwhelming dominance of East Asia, contributed primarily by China, Japan, and South Korea. This regional concentration extends beyond reflecting a high disease burden to represent the establishment of a mature, synergistic research ecosystem encompassing technical innovation, extensive clinical volume, and robust regional collaboration networks. In contrast, the United States functions as a pivotal trans-Atlantic hub connecting European and other global partners, potentially fostering different research emphases, such as mechanistic studies and integration with systemic therapy paradigms prevalent in Western guidelines.

Geographical heterogeneity in research focus

While East Asia dominates the research landscape, research priorities markedly differ between East Asian and Western countries. In East Asian countries, hepatitis B virus infection remains the predominant etiology [25, 26]; accordingly, research focuses on technical optimization of ablation, protocol refinement, and combination with locoregional therapies, such as TACE. This focus reflects the high incidence of HCC and the resulting large clinical volume in this region [27, 28], supported by the early promotion of standardized ablation techniques through regional consensus guidelines and high-volume centers [29]. In contrast, Western research, driven by the rising prevalence of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease and an increased proportion of patients with underlying cirrhosis, emphasizes patient selection, systemic therapy integration, and long-term survival outcomes. These differences likely reflect regional variations in disease burden, clinical practice guidelines, and research resource allocation. Recognizing this heterogeneity is crucial for interpreting global research trends and enabling targeted international collaboration.

Thematic evolution of the field

The evolution of research themes, as mapped by the keyword clusters and timeline analysis, narrates the scientific maturation of this field. The seven journal clusters delineate the multidisciplinary knowledge base of this field. Clusters #1 (radiofrequency ablation) and #2 (microwave ablation) constitute the technical core, whereas clusters #0 (retrospective analysis) and #4 (hepatic resection) reflect the ongoing debate between ablation and surgery. Cluster #3 (evidence-based management) comprises clinical guidelines and meta-analyses that shape current practice. Cluster #5 (laparoscopic radiofrequency ablation) represents a specialized intersection of surgery and ablation, while cluster #6 (small hepatocellular carcinoma) focuses on the well-defined patient population for whom ablation is a first-line curative option. Collectively, these clusters map onto three core journal types: high-impact hepatology journals, specialized radiology/interventional journals, and leading surgical oncology journals. The early foundational work, characterized by clusters and bursts focused on “ablation techniques and safety,” focused on establishing procedural efficacy and managing complications. The persistent “comparative effectiveness” cluster underscores a central, unresolved clinical dilemma regarding the optimal selection of patients for ablation versus surgical resection [30, 31]. Recent meta-analyses have confirmed comparable overall survival but differing patterns of disease-free survival between these modalities [31]. Recent surgical advancements, such as research on the prognostic impact of middle hepatic vein resection during hemihepatectomy, further inform the boundary of indications between ablation and complex surgery [32]. The ongoing citation bursts for MWA and comparative studies with liver resection confirm that this question is being actively revisited with newer technologies and broader patient cohorts.

Paradigm shift toward precision oncology

The maturation and current prominence of the “precision and image-guided therapy” and “prognosis and integrated management” clusters mark a pivotal shift from questioning technical feasibility to pursuing optimized oncological outcomes. First, this shift represents the transition of the field from a “technical” to a “patient-centered” paradigm. The early phase of the field was necessarily dedicated to establishing whether ablation works and how to perform it safely. The emergence of these two clusters indicates that the field has evolved beyond technical validation toward optimizing ablation efficacy, a hallmark of scientific maturation. Second, these two clusters directly address the two principal mechanisms of post‑ablation treatment failure. Focused research on ablation margin assessment and advanced image-guidance directly targets the principal technical cause of local recurrence, which is incomplete ablation at the tumor periphery [33, 34]. Beyond margin assessment, advanced imaging features, such as tumor compression of the hepatic or portal vein on contrast‑enhanced CT, have emerged as independent predictors of microvascular invasion (MVI) and satellite nodules, further enhancing preoperative risk stratification [35]. Simultaneously, the integration of ablation with adjuvant therapies, such as TACE, and the investigation of prognostic biomarkers reflect a holistic approach to control the treated lesion and underlying carcinogenic field and address the challenge of intrahepatic distant recurrence [33, 36]. By targeting both the local and the systemic drivers of recurrence, these research themes address the two most critical determinants of long‑term patient survival. Third, these clusters serve as a roadmap for the future of the field. The focus on precision image‑guidance paves the way for integrating artificial intelligence (AI) and radiomics into ablation planning and margin assessment [37–39]. The emphasis on prognosis and integrated management highlights the need for personalized risk stratification and exploration of combination therapies (e.g., ablation plus immunotherapy). Emerging evidence on prognostic biomarkers, such as the role of lymphatic vessel density in early-stage HCC recurrence, supports the feasibility of refining risk stratification in this context [40]. Overall, these clusters represent not only the current status of the specialty but also its future directions. These data depict a discipline that has consolidated its technical foundations and is now pursuing precision and personalization to improve long-term patient survival.

Future directions and emerging frontiers

The results of our burst detection and thematic analysis collectively reveal several convergent research trajectories that are poised to define the next era of this field. The strong, ongoing citation burst for MWA signals its transition from an alternative to a potential first-line thermal modality for many tumors, particularly those larger in size or in challenging locations. This shift will likely drive future research beyond comparative efficacy studies toward optimizing MWA-specific protocols, exploring synergistic combinations with other energy-based techniques (e.g., irreversible electroporation), and integrating real-time procedural feedback via advanced thermometry. Concurrently, the sustained focus on prognosis and integrated management reflects a critical evolution from a purely technical to a biological and systemic perspective. The future frontier involves transcending clinicopathological models by leveraging radiomics, liquid biopsies (such as circulating tumor DNA), and immunoprofiling to develop personalized recurrence risk algorithms. This integration will catalyze definitive studies on adjuvant and neoadjuvant strategies, specifically defining the role of combining ablation with emerging systemic therapies, such as immunotherapy and targeted agents for high-risk patients, an area currently underrepresented in the extant literature. Finally, a notable gap and major future hotspot identified in our map is the application of AI. AI is anticipated to permeate the entire clinical pathway, from preprocedural planning (including automated tumor segmentation and margin prediction) and intraprocedural guidance (through real-time image fusion and ablation zone monitoring) to postprocedural prognostication (via radiomics-based recurrence prediction), with the ultimate goal being standardized precision and improved outcomes [41, 42].

Innovation and implications

The primary innovation of this study lies in its novel conceptual approach, providing the first comprehensive bibliometric synthesis of a complex and clinically vital field. Although previous narrative or systematic reviews have addressed specific medical questions, such as the comparative efficacy of ablation versus surgery [30, 31], the mechanisms of post-ablation recurrence [33], and the performance of prognostic models [37, 43], our analysis uniquely maps the macroscopic intellectual and social architecture of research on HCC recurrence after ablation. A key contribution is our identification and delineation of the four foundational thematic pillars of this field: technical efficacy and safety, comparative effectiveness with surgery, precision image-guidance, and integrated prognostic management. This framework offers a new paradigm for understanding the core structure and strategic priorities of this domain. Furthermore, this study reveals a distinctive geoscientific pattern of collaboration, quantifying the overwhelming dominance and tightly knit network of East Asian institutions, which contrasts with the more dispersed, trans-Atlantic hub centered on the United States. This finding provides critical insight into how regional disease burden and research ecosystems shape global scientific output in a technology-intensive specialty. Finally, by integrating evolutionary timeline visualization with burst detection analysis, we trace the dynamic trajectory of this sphere from foundational technical validation to the current paradigm of precision oncology. This historical perspective contextualizes contemporary research frontiers, such as MWA and margin assessment, within a coherent narrative of scientific progress.

These findings hold significant implications for various stakeholders. For new and established researchers, our results provide an authoritative map to navigate the literature, identify key contributors and collaborative networks, and determine active and emerging research fronts. For research institutions and funding agencies, they highlight regions of high productivity and promising areas for strategic investment, such as the integration of AI with ablation therapy or novel adjuvant systemic combinations. For the broader clinical and academic community, the visualized evolution of research themes underscores a critical insight: the future of ablation therapy lies not merely in incremental technical refinement but also in its intelligent integration into multimodal, biology-informed treatment strategies aimed at achieving sustained systemic disease control and improving long-term patient survival.

Limitations

This study has certain limitations. First, the analysis was limited to the WoSCC. Although this database provides robust coverage of high-impact literature, relevant studies indexed in other databases, as well as valuable clinical case reports, non-SCI-indexed journals, and regional publications, may have been excluded. Second, bibliometrics is a measure of scholarly activity and influence, not a direct arbiter of scientific quality. A high publication or citation count does not necessarily equate to high-level clinical evidence, as it may also reflect debate, utility, or the size of a research community. Third, despite careful data cleaning, potential inaccuracies in network representations may persist owing to variations in author names and institutional affiliations. Fourth, the citation-based nature of several analyses introduces an inherent latency; therefore, this study is more adept at identifying established and emerging trends than at capturing the latest, yet-to-be-cited innovations at the cutting edge. While this single-database approach is a limitation, it is consistent with the methodology used in many bibliometric studies [22, 44–46]. We suggest that future multi-database analyses could provide complementary validation.

Conclusion

The results of this bibliometric analysis offer a comprehensive map of the research landscape on HCC recurrence after thermal ablation. The findings document the growth of the field from technical validation to precision oncology, highlight the collaborative leadership of East Asia, and identify established and emerging thematic frontiers. This analysis underscores that the future of the field lies in technological refinement coupled with biological integration to leverage advanced imaging and AI for perfect local control, while combining ablation with systemic therapies to modulate the metastatic microenvironment. Overall, this study provides a valuable framework for researchers to position their work, institutions to identify collaboration opportunities, and the broader scientific community to navigate the next phase of innovation in improving outcomes for patients with HCC.

Clinical and academic nurses’ perspectives on the current syllabus related to the management of multimorbidity in teaching curriculum: A qualitative study in Odisha, India
Authors: Bijayalaskhmi Dash, Sanghamitra Pati, Krushna C. Sahoo, Sapna Negi, Jeetendra Yadav, Pranab Mahapatra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Multi-morbidity poses complex challenges associated with more complex clinical management, poor treatment outcomes, longer hospital stays, and increased healthcare costs. In this context, nurses serve as crucial agents in delivering holistic patient-centered care among multimorbidity patients but there exists a noticeable gap in their curriculum which needs to be modified. Nursing faculty plays a vital role in advancing the nursing profession and nursing curriculum modification and development is one of the most important roles of nursing faculty. This study explores the perspectives of nurses and nursing faculties regarding the integration of multimorbidity into nursing curricula.Methods:Using a phenomenological approach, the study conducted 25 in-depth interviews have been conducted among nursing teachers, nursing officers, and community health officers across various nursing colleges and health centers in Odisha, India. Open-ended questions and probes were employed to elicit rich insights. The digitally recorded interviews were transcribed, translated into English, and then thematically analyzed.Findings:Thematic analysis of the in-depth interview transcripts revealed two overarching themes: “Patient with multimorbidity requires evidence-based, patient-centered care” “mitigating challenges faced by patient, Nurse and health sector.” Participants enunciated the importance of integration of multimorbidity management in nursing curricula. They highlighted the challenges faced by nurses/nurse educators due to knowledge gaps of nursing personnel on multimorbidity and have suggested to inculcate the management of multimorbidity in the nursing curriculum.Conclusion:The nurses occupy a unique position as frontline healthcare providers, both within hospital settings as nursing officers and in community setups as community health officers. This study explores the perspectives of nurses and nursing faculties to integrate the management of multimorbidity in the nursing curriculum. This study revealed the significance of integration of multimorbidity in the nursing curriculum and advocate to add this.

Introduction

Chronic diseases and multimorbidity are major causes of disease burden for patients, caregivers, and society, becoming one of the key issues in the public health sector.[12] The global prevalence of multimorbidity stands at 37.2% (Chowdhury et al.). Over the last two decades, the prevalence of multimorbidity has significantly increased, not only in high-income countries but also in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). This rise is attributed to improved socioeconomic conditions, unhealthy lifestyles involving tobacco use, lack of regular physical activity, exposure to second hand smoke, poor nutrition, excessive alcohol use, and enhanced diagnostic capabilities by health services.[34]

In India, a low-middle-income country with the highest population in the world, 32.1% of older adults live with multimorbidity conditions.[5] Hypertension, arthritis, and thyroid disorders are highly prevalent among females, while chronic lung diseases and stroke are more common among males. These conditions result in adverse health outcomes, including reduced physical function, psychological distress among patients and families, poor quality of life, and poor self-rated health.[678] Consequently, patients with multimorbidity experience polypharmacy, increased use of inpatient and ambulatory care, more complex clinical management, poorer treatment outcomes, longer hospital stays, and higher healthcare costs. This presents a complex burden on healthcare systems which are traditionally focused on individual diseases.[91011] Managing multiple non-curative problems within individuals requires complex management that current clinical guidelines poorly support.[1213] This highlights the need to shift the care paradigm from vertical single-disease management to horizontally integrated models of care, addressing the multifaceted needs of patients with multimorbidity in both healthcare and community settings[14]

Nursing professionals play a crucial role in providing continuous care to patients with chronic diseases in hospitals and the community settings.[15] However, they face numerous challenges with multimorbidity, including a shortage of nursing staff, lack of competence in assessing and identifying chronic disease symptoms, lack of motivation, and unclear roles, and guidelines for managing chronic diseases in hospital inpatients.[1617] In community settings, the Government of India has implemented the organization of primary care as ‘Health and Wellness Centers’ (HWC), delivered through community health officers (CHOs) who are trained nursing professionals. This initiative aims to achieve universal health coverage. These HWCs are expected to provide comprehensive primary care through an expanded range of services and a multidisciplinary team. However, existing literature highlights the gap in providing education for nurses and CHOs to work with chronic patients with multimorbidity from prevention and health promotion perspective.[1819]

Given this background, our study aims to explore the challenges and opportunities nursing professionals in India face in multimorbidity care, and what they need to adapt to the changing disease patterns and healthcare delivery system focused on chronic diseases. Specifically, the study seeks to understand clinical and academic nurses’ perspectives on the current curriculum related to managing multiple chronic illnesses. The findings are expected to provide key insights for academic planners and policymakers to introduce adequate curricular components addressing multimorbidity in India.

Methods

Study design, setting, and participants

A qualitative exploratory study was conducted to explore clinical and academic nurses’ perspectives on the existing syllabus for the management of multimorbidities. The survey was conducted at three reputable nursing colleges in Odisha: SCB (Govt) College of Nursing, Cuttack, Kalinga Institute of Nursing Science KIIT, Bhubaneswar, and SUM College of Nursing, SOA University, Bhubaneswar. Health and wellness centers were also purposefully chosen in Bhubaneswar. All nursing colleges have been selected for their large nursing student intake capacities, extensive clinical experience, and high educational standards. The participants included community health officers (n = 5) and nursing officers (n = 5) from various health centers, as well as faculty members from nursing colleges (4 associate professors, 5 assistant professors, and 6 nursing tutors. The first author contacted all participants either in person or over the phone. Ten of the lecturers were from private institutions, five were from public nursing colleges, but all nursing officers and community health officers were from public health centers. The participants were female and had an average age of 38 years, ranging from 30 to 43 years.

Data collection procedures

A total of 25 in-depth interviews were done. The interviews with nursing teachers and nursing officers took place in person, however, data from CHOs was gathered virtually via video conference due to a lack of time to participate in person. Permission was obtained from each institutional head to interview participants on a certain date and time. Participants were informed that their participation was voluntary, and no remuneration had been given for attending the interview. All interviews were conducted in a mix of Odia and English for the convenience of the participants and facilitator. All participants had an average interview time of 25 minutes (range 20-30). All interviews were conducted by the first author who has a nursing background. The co-investigators come from various public health backgrounds, with an emphasis on multimorbidity. All participants gave their approval to have their in-depth interviews digitally recorded.

Data management and analysis

The digitally recorded interviews were transcribed, translated into English, and then thematically analyzed. The transcripts were reviewed for meaning units. These units of significance were condensed and coded. Similar codes were categorized and condensed into several categories and overarching themes. The data were coded using MAXQDA software (MAXQDA Analytics Pro 2020, VERBI GmbH Berlin) according to the defined themes and categories. Following preliminary analysis, 10 participants were informed of the findings (member check). The study was reported following the Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research (COREQ) criteria [Annexure 1].

Ethical approval

The study was approved by the ethical committee of the ICMR-Regional Medical Research Centre, Bhubaneswar. Furthermore, before conducting in-depth interviews, participants were told of the study’s goal and right to opt-out at any moment. Each participant provided their written consent. The majority of in-depth interviews took place at the institution, while some (particularly for CHOs) were performed virtually. The identities of the participants were kept confidential.

Multimorbidity requires complex nursing care

Participants described nursing care for complex cases as a comprehensive approach that includes managing their symptoms and prescription regimes, mental health counseling and advocacy, and coordinating several facets of their healthcare, among other things. As multimorbidity is a combination of chronic conditions, each condition has its own treatment protocols, medications, and management guidelines which necessitate care from multiple specialists and healthcare providers. However, nursing officers highlighted that coordinating care among multiple specialists, healthcare providers, and caregivers is complex. The CHOs further added that at community level, the complexity of multimorbidity management is further compounded as symptoms of different conditions can overlap or present atypically, making diagnosis and management more difficult. They noted that some patient face difficulty in adhering to a medication schedule due to the side effects they experience or due to an aversion to taking multiple drugs for various conditions.

“I am giving medication based on symptoms and r based on different Bio-chemical values of blood, urine or by teleconsultation with doctors. I think people does not like medication, whenever they will get rid of symptoms, they use to stop medication” (CHO-1)

Therefore, all participants viewed that team of healthcare experts, including nurses, should work together to establish a care plan that is tailored to the specific needs of patients. Also, medical care-related decision should be shared with all healthcare providers including nurses when caring of multimorbidity patients.

Multimorbidity need patient-centered care

Patient-centered care has the potential to tailor healthcare to the specific needs of patients with multimorbidity. Interviewees emphasized that patient-centered care is crucial in addressing the challenges associated with multimorbidity, such as respecting patients’ preferences, providing thorough information and counseling, and offering emotional support. One nursing officer highlighted that while patient-centered care is vital, the lack of knowledge among nurses and an unsupportive hospital environment make it difficult to manage these conditions effectively. Additionally, community health officers (CHOs) also stressed the importance of patient empowerment and education for care of patients with multimorbidity.

“The patients with multimorbidity care should be given based on the individual differences, their needs, values and challenges. But as we are not trained on this, it becomes difficult to provide such type of care. (Nursing teacher -6)

Coordination and integration of care

Almost all participants perceived that coordination and integration of care are essential in managing multimorbid conditions. Participants emphasized the importance of multidisciplinary teams, including doctors from various specialties, nurses, and dietitians, in providing continuous care for patients with multimorbidity. One participant suggested that social media groups, such as WhatsApp, could be used as a medium to connect patients and healthcare professionals from different specialties, thereby enhancing the coordination and continuity of care.

“For coordination of care at the community level, a what’s app group among chronic patients and doctors from different specialties, CHOs, and other staffs can be helpful to follow up properly and coordination of care” (CHO-3)

Nurses’s integral role in comprehensive patient-centered care

The majority of participants noted that nurses are the primary healthcare providers who consistently stay with patients in hospital settings as nursing officers and independently handle patient care as Community Health Officers in community setups. They play a crucial role in providing comprehensive patient-centered care for patients with multimorbidity. Their constant presence and direct involvement in patient care make them indispensable in managing complex health conditions and ensuring that patients receive comprehensive and continuous care.

“Nurses have an integral role in comprehensive patient-centred care. They should know how to provide comprehensive patient-centred care among multimorbidity clients and they should be well trained for management of multimorbidity” (Nursing teacher-14).

Mitigating challenges faced by the patient, nurse and health sector

Empowering and educating patients for better outcomes

All participants acknowledged that multiple disease conditions significantly affect an individual’s quality of life, impacting both their physical and psychosocial health. Patients with multimorbidity often face numerous challenges, including increased mortality risk, greater symptom burden, polypharmacy, limited physical and psychological functioning, and reduced quality of life. Consequently, their health outcomes are severely compromised. Participants emphasized that to confront these challenges, patients should be well-educated about self-care and empowered to make decisions about their health.

“As people must compromises in their diet and lifestyle, their physical and psychosocial health got affected which results in poor quality of life. People suffer stress, strain, and frustrations which leads to suicidal attempts” (Nursing teacher-8).

Need for inclusion of multimorbidity management in nursing education

All participants pointed out that the growing prevalence of multiple disease conditions poses significant challenges for the entire healthcare system, particularly for nurses. Additionally, there are no specific guidelines available in healthcare settings for the management of multimorbid conditions. The majority acknowledged a lack of skills to manage patients with multimorbidity due to insufficient training. Nursing officers and CHOs highlighted the challenges of managing complex situations and counselling patients and their families. Some faculty members admitted to having difficulty guiding students when they frequently ask how to prioritize diseases or problems when treating patients with multiple chronic conditions. Participants underlined that existing nursing curriculum do not effectively address the care of patients with multiple morbidities.

“Generally, the students are making case studies, they are asking which disease we have to prioritize now. I am also confused in same situation.” (Nursing teacher-9)

According to the participants, nursing curriculum provides a crucial framework for both instructors and learners to advance their knowledge and skills. Thus, all strongly advocated for inclusion of multimorbidity management in all levels of nursing education.

“We should learn how to handle people who have multiple chronic illnesses, and this should be part of our curriculum. If we were taught this during our training, we might not encounter such an issue in our daily lives.” (CHO-2)

When asked which courses they thought were most appropriate, the majority of them said that we should include it in all basic and advanced nursing programs to better prepare nurses at all levels to handle the complexities of multimorbidity in their professional practice, from the grassroots to tertiary centers. Participants also highlighted the need for clear guidelines on the integrated and comprehensive management of multimorbidity conditions. These guidelines should be readily available and accessible to healthcare professionals, especially nurses, to aid in the effective management of patients with multiple chronic conditions.

“Regarding management of chronic diseases, we have studied individually like management of DM, Cancer in our basic course and CHO training but no guidelines are available to treat multimorbidity. Similar to IMNCI guideline, there should be separate standard guideline for managing these conditions.”(CHO-5)

In-service training and development

All most all of the participants viewed that in addition to nursing curriculum, continuous training to nursing professional on multimorbidity management is needed to keep the nursing fraternity updated on contemporary evidence-based knowledge and technological advances in care through clinical practice guidelines and care pathways. Participants emphasized that nurses working in Health and wellness centers should be provided more guidance on multimorbidity management. Frequent in-service training is essential to keep them updated and skilled in managing complex conditions.

“Curriculum changes is not enough. We who are already in job, need training on this. Every nurse needs to be updated related to the current practice through Inservice education programme” (Nursing Officer-3).

Policy healthcare system changes

Interviewees expressed that healthcare planning provided by nurses should be proactive, with a strong emphasis on preventive care. They highlighted that managing patients with multiple chronic conditions depends on a well-structured and organized primary health sector. This sector should be capable of following up, monitoring, and managing patients consistently. Different health problems can be addressed by the same team, ensuring integrated care. Participants also perceived that greater involvement of primary healthcare providers is necessary for the prevention and management of multimorbidity, emphasizing the need for a strong team approach.

“Policy-makers can also consider how to support the delivery of good system-wide primary care in the context of their health care systems. Even though Nurses have been involved more in provision of primary healthcare at health wellness centres, they should be guided properly on how to manage such cases.” (Nursing teacher-4).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first qualitative study exploring perceptions of nursing faculties and nurse clinicians’ on integrating management of multimorbidity in teaching curriculum. The main finding of the study based upon the perception of faculties and nurse clinician were for mitigating challenges faced by patient nurse, nurse educators, and health sector and inculcating evidence-based, patient-centered care for management of patients with multiple chronic disorders. It was also viewed that re-structure of nursing curriculum, training, and development of nursing staffs on management of multimorbidity are necessary for nurses to provide proper care.

Patient with multimorbidity requires complex, patient-centered holistic care

Participants viewed that multimorbidity requires complex, person-centered, multifaceted nursing care in a holistic approach, in hospital as well as the community. Søren T. Skou et al.[20] 2022 in their study portrayed that Most clinical practice guidelines, training, and service delivery focus on single diseases, leading to care that is inadequate and sometimes potentially harmful. Our finding shows multi-morbidity requires person-centered care, prioritizing what matters most to the individual and their caretakers for ensuring care that is effectively coordinated and minimally disruptive and aligns with patient values. Interventions are likely to be complex and multifaceted. Chris Salisbury et al.[21] Study findings interpreted that better patient-centered healthcare for patients with multi-morbidity will result in better health and wellbeing. Binu Koirala et al.[22] 2022 depicted that individuals living with multiple chronic conditions require a holistic approach to their care that balances often-competing priorities, needs, and treatment goals.

Mitigating challenges faced by patient, nurse, and health sector

The participants viewed that the multi-morbidity patients are facing a lot of challenges when living with multiple chronic illnesses. A study conducted by P.A.L. 1 (Nelleke) Seghers et al.[23] 2023 on “Challenges of caring for older patients with multimorbidity including cancer”, which portrayed that the patients and caregivers most frequently mentioned challenges related to treatment outcomes, such as how to deal with symptoms of disease or treatment and how to maintain quality of life. Lisa Van Wilder et al.[24] 2021 in their study depicted that diverse multimorbidity patterns were synergistically or antagonistically associated with lower health-related quality of life (HRQoL) which is a challenge for themselves. In the present study, the participants also viewed that this growing of multiple disease condition has become a challenge for healthcare workers especially nurses, as they are the primary healthcare providers and the total healthcare system. Keith Moffat and Stewart W. Mercer in their study revealed that as multimorbidity is a growing issue and poses a major challenge to healthcare systems, it requires holistic approach to deal with the challenges.[25] Interviewees viewed that healthcare planning should be proactive, giving importance to preventive care and health policy should be more focused on general care rather than special care when dealing with multimorbidity patients. This is supported by Chris Salisbury et al.[26] who stated that healthcare services need to invest in better generalist care and become less focused on care for single diseases. Iunes R et al.[27] 2024 depicted that healthcare systems often provide fragmented care, focusing on individual conditions rather than the patient as a whole.

Re-structure of curriculum, training, and development

Majority of participants viewed that the nurses are the only healthcare providers who are always with the patients in hospital, and they have a great role in providing comprehensive patient-centered care among multi-morbidity patients. Cynthia D. Griffin in his study “A Primary Care Nursing Perspective on Chronic Disease Prevention and Management” portrayed that nurses play a vital role in chronic disease management and are well positioned to enhance the planning and delivery of healthcare resources in primary care, and they have contributions to patient-centered, through the care coordination role.[28] All participants were also perceived that management of multiple chronic conditions must be added in nursing curriculum and they specially suggested that there should be a proper guideline/module for managing multimorbidity. Francisca Leiva-Fernández et al.[29] in their study “Identification of the multimorbidity training needs of primary care professionals” stated that the identification of perceived training needs, as well as recognized difficulties and tools should be available to professionals, which can ultimately aid the creation of a patient-centered healthcare model that also translates into better clinical outcomes. Further, most all participants viewed that only this addition of the nursing curriculum is not enough, continuous training to nursing professional on multimorbidity management is needed. The findings resonate with the outcomes of a qualitative study on nurses experience of managing adults living with multimorbidity conducted by Lisa Whitehead et al.[30] 2023 in which nurses described the need for, and value of, ongoing education on multimorbidity. The study also depicts that updates and refresher training in relation to common chronic diseases and multimorbidity were felt to be important by the majority of nurses interviewed.

Conclusion

This study explores the perspectives of nurses and nursing faculties to integrate the management of multimorbidity in nursing curriculum. This study revealed the significance of integration of multimorbidity in nursing curriculum. The result shows that the nursing faculties and nurse clinicians are confronting a lot of challenges due to a knowledge gap in multimorbidity management. All of them advocate to add this content to nursing curricula which equips nurses with the knowledge and skills necessary to navigate the complexities of multimorbidity and enhance patient care outcomes.

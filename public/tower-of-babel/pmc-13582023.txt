ECG challenge: a narrow clue in a wide QRS tachycardia
Authors: Akihiko Nogami, Ryuichi Usui, Kikuya Uno, Stefano Bordignon
Published: 2026

Clinical vignette

A 57-year-old woman with no prior medical history or known structural heart disease presented after a transient episode of syncope. A 12-lead electrocardiogram showed an incessant tachycardia at 133–135 beats per minute with a cycle length of 445–450 ms, displaying a right bundle branch block pattern and superior axis (Figure 1A). Intermittent relatively narrow QRS complexes were observed. During a transient interruption of the tachycardia, sinus rhythm was recorded, showing a QRS duration of 82 ms with left anterior fascicular block (Figure 1B).

Twelve-lead electrocardiograms during tachycardia and transient sinus rhythm. (A) Electrocardiogram during the clinical tachycardia showing a wide QRS tachycardia with a right bundle branch block pattern and superior axis. (B) Electrocardiogram recorded during a transient interruption of the tachycardia showing sinus rhythm with a QRS duration of 82 ms and left anterior fascicular block.

Bundle branch re-entrant ventricular tachycardia

Bundle branch re-entrant ventricular tachycardia

Correct answer: B

Explanation

A relatively narrow QRS tachycardia with a right bundle branch block pattern and superior axis in a structurally normal heart suggests left posterior fascicular ventricular tachycardia.1,2 However, the absence of small r waves in the inferior leads was atypical. The annotated ECG showed atrial activity before every QRS complex (see Supplementary material online, Figure S1). Electrophysiological study showed 1:1 atrioventricular conduction, an HV interval of 38 ms, A-A interval variation determining H-H and V-V variation, earliest atrial activation in the high right atrium, and antegrade His activation (see Supplementary material online, Figure S2), supporting atrial tachycardia with functional right bundle branch block.

The tachycardia has a right bundle branch block pattern and superior axis

The tachycardia has a right bundle branch block pattern and superior axis

The QRS complex becomes wide at a cycle length of 450 ms

The QRS complex becomes wide at a cycle length of 450 ms

The patient has no known structural heart disease

Correct answer: C

Explanation

True capture beats during ventricular tachycardia occur when a supraventricular impulse transiently conducts to the ventricles in the setting of atrioventricular dissociation.1 In this case, atrial activity was not confined to the relatively narrow QRS complexes but preceded both wide and narrow QRS complexes, indicating a consistent 1:1 atrioventricular relationship. In addition, there was no consistent shortening or prolongation of the R-R interval before the narrow QRS complexes, which argued against true capture beats.

Question 3

What is the most likely mechanism of intermittent QRS narrowing?

Wenckebach periodicity within the right bundle branch

Wenckebach periodicity within the right bundle branch

Pause-dependent recovery of right bundle branch refractoriness

Pause-dependent recovery of right bundle branch refractoriness

Supernormal conduction in the right bundle branch

Supernormal conduction in the right bundle branch

Equal conduction delay in both bundle branches

Intermittent loss of ventricular pre-excitation due to transient accessory pathway block

Intermittent loss of ventricular pre-excitation due to transient accessory pathway block

Correct answer: C

Explanation

Several mechanisms were considered. The narrow QRS complexes were not consistently preceded by longer R-R intervals, and there was no consistent PR or AH prolongation before them, arguing against pause-dependent recovery, equal conduction delay, or a gap phenomenon.3 Wenckebach periodicity within the right bundle branch was also unlikely because progressive QRS morphological changes were not reproducible (see Supplementary material online, Figure S2).4 Atrial pacing reproduced cycle length–dependent QRS changes, with narrowing again at a shorter pacing cycle length of 350 ms (see Supplementary material online, Figure S3). This paradoxical improvement supported supernormal conduction in the right bundle branch.3,5

Teaching point

Intermittent relatively narrow QRS complexes during wide QRS tachycardia do not always represent capture beats. Careful assessment of atrial activity, atrioventricular relationships, and cycle length–dependent intraventricular conduction can reveal supraventricular tachycardia with unusual functional aberrant conduction.

Lead author biography

Dr Akihiko Nogami is President of Tokyo Heart Rhythm Hospital, Tokyo Heart Rhythm Hospital, Tokyo, Japan. He was born in Tokyo in 1955, graduated from Nagasaki University School of Medicine in 1982, and received his PhD from the Graduate School of Tokyo Medical and Dental University in 1988.

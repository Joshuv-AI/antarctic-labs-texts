Multivariate Birnbaum-Saunders accelerated lifetime regression model: estimation and residual analysis
Authors: Maria Ioneris Oliveira, Francisco José A. Cysneiros, Michelli Barros
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Birnbaum-Saunders regression models have seen usually used in recent years. It is widely recognized that multivariate models are essential in effectively modeling correlated data. In this paper, we introduce a novel approach by introducing a new multivariate Birnbaum-Saunders distribution. The dependence structure is characterized through the incorporation of a frailty variable, leading to the development of a multivariate Birnbaum-Saunders regression model. We also introduce residual analysis as a tool for assessing the goodness-of-fit of the proposed model. Furthermore, we conduct simulation studies to evaluate the performance of the estimators and to analyze the behavior of the proposed residuals. Finally, we apply the methodology to a real dataset.

Introduction

The Birnbaum-Saunders (BS) distribution was originally developed by1from a specific structural damage caused to materials when exposed to stress or tension situations. The univariate BS distribution is a biparametric distribution, positively skewed and unimodal2. derived the maximum likelihood estimates for this distribution and several other studies were carried out, for example3, proposed a log-linear model for the Birnbaum-Saunders distribution and established a relationship between this distribution and the sinh-normal (SN) distribution4. performed a general approach to this distribution5, proposed a regression model, where the BS distribution is considered for the frailty variable and6 introduced a survival model with frailty based on the BS distribution.

One of the main assumptions in usual regression models is the assumption of independence between observations. However, in some cases this assumption is not valid, as in the case of events observed in the same individual over time. In such cases, multivariate regression models with a dependence structure is more appropriate to analyse of this type data. Several multivariate extensions of the Birnbaum–Saunders (BS) distribution have been proposed in the literature to accommodate dependent lifetime data. One important line of research focuses on direct multivariate generalizations of the BS distribution. For example7, introduced a bivariate Birnbaum–Saunders distribution, which was later extended by8 to a generalized multivariate formulation with dependence structures. Another line of work is based on the log-Birnbaum–Saunders representation, exploiting the relationship between the BS and the sinh–normal distributions to construct regression models for lifetime data. In this context9, introduced the generalized BS logarithmic distribution, as well as, the multivariate BS generalized regression model, where the multivariate BS regression model is obtained as a particular result10, presents a log-linear model for multivariate Birnbaum–Saunders distribution that can be used in survival analysis to investigate correlated log-lifetimes of two or more units and11 introduced a multivariate BS distribution in the context of conditionally specified distributions and presented some properties. These approaches have considerably expanded the applicability of the BS distribution for modeling correlated lifetimes.

In contrast, regression frameworks that incorporate dependence through frailty structures have received comparatively little attention in the literature. In some related approaches, the frailty variable itself is assumed to follow a BS distribution, whereas the observed lifetimes are modeled through alternative mechanisms. For instance12, proposed a novel model for clustered survival data in which the frailty terms follow a BS distribution, introducing a parameterization directly expressed in terms of the variance of the frailty distribution. In this formulation, the BS distribution is assigned to the frailty component rather than to the observed lifetimes. A different perspective was considered by13, who proposed a bivariate BS regression model in which the observed lifetimes themselves follow a BS distribution, allowing the model to be applied to both censored and uncensored data. Building on this framework, the present paper extends the model in13 to the multivariate case by proposing a multivariate Birnbaum–Saunders frailty regression model for correlated lifetime data. In the proposed framework, the observed lifetimes follow a Birnbaum–Saunders distribution, while the dependence among observations is induced through a shared frailty structure. The primary objectives of our paper are threefold: firstly, to introduce a multivariate BS frailty (BSF) distribution; secondly, to expound upon its properties; and thirdly, to extend the model proposed by13 into the multivariate domain by deriving the multivariate BS regression model. Additionally, the paper proposes new residuals to assess the overall goodness-of-fit of the model. Through the multivariate BS regression model, we will model the dependence structure that exists between variables by including a frailty variable. This extension allows for the modeling of both uncensored and censored cases, providing a comprehensive framework for analysis.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sect. "Modeling" we propose the multivariate BS frailty (BSF) distribution, present some properties of this distribution, derive the multivariate BS regression model, and describe some inferential procedures to obtain the maximum likelihood estimates of the model. Finally, we perform a simulation study to evaluate the performance of the maximum likelihood estimators obtained. Moreover, in Sect. "Residual analysis" we introduce some residuals to evaluate the fit of the proposed model, and we present a simulation study to evaluate their behavior. In addition to that, in Sect. "Application" we present an application of the proposed methodology to a real data set. Finally, some considerations are presented in Sect. "Discussion".

Testing the frailty

In practical studies related to survival analysis, it is important to identify if there is any type of association in the data considered14., presented a test to verify the existence of an association between the observations. The unilateral test is formulated as follows:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} H_{0}: \varepsilon =0\;\;\;\text {versus}\;\;\;H_{A}: \varepsilon>0, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon$$\end{document} denotes the variance of the frailty variable. According to24, the variance of the frailty variable is seen as a way to quantify the frailty present, because if \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varepsilon =0$$\end{document}, all frailty variables will be equal to 1, which means that the gamma distribution becomes degenerate at point 1. Note that if \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$z=1$$\end{document} in Equation (1), then we get the usual model for independent data. To test the null hypothesis, we use the likelihood ratio test. The main problem faced here is that the frailty parameter is at the boundary of the parametric space and, thus, the likelihood ratio will not have the classic chi-square distribution, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\chi _{(1)}^{2}$$\end{document}. According to25, in this case, we can assume that the asymptotic distribution of the likelihood ratio will be given by mixture a mass point at zero and a chi-square distribution with one degree of freedom, denoted by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.5 \chi _{(0)}^{2}+0.5\chi _{(1)}^{2}$$\end{document}.

Residual analysis

To assess the adequacy of the multivariate BS accelerated lifetime model with a dependence structure, we proposed two types of residuals: the multivariate V-residuals and K-residuals. As we will see later, these residuals preserve the dependence structure of the model, since they are defined through the expression of an Archimedean copula, which does not occur with other types of residuals.

The V-residuals and K-residuals for the bivariate case

The V-residuals and K-residuals were originally defined for the bivariate case by27 and presented by13, as being16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \widehat{V}_i&=\mathcal Following the approach of27, we defined the multivariate K-residuals from a probability integral transformation of the multivariate V-residuals, that is, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\widehat{K}_i=G(\widehat{ V}_i)$$\end{document}. Thus, the K-multivariate residuals have a uniform distribution in the range [0, 1] and thereby have a distribution that does not depend on unknown parameters.

According to the studies of27 and13 for the bivariate case, we adapted the multivariate V-residuals and the K-residuals for the case where we have the presence of right censoring. Thus, we have the multivariate adapted V-residuals and the K-residuals:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\widehat{V}_i^{\text {adap}}=\mathcal {L}\left\{ \displaystyle \sum _{j=1}^{m}\mathcal {L}^{-1}(\widehat{U}_{ij}^{\text {adap}})\right\} \;\text {and}\;\widehat{K}_i^{\text {adap}}=G(\widehat{V}_i^{\text {adap}}),$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\widehat{U}_{ij}^{\text {adap}}=\widehat{U}_{ij}+0.5(\delta _{ij}-1)\widehat{U}_{ij}$$\end{document}, being \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta _{ij}$$\end{document} the failure indicator variable, with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$j=1,\ldots ,m$$\end{document}.

Numerical study

In this section, we present a simulation study to evaluate the empirical distribution of the K-residuals and of the adapted K-residuals. In this regard, we used the same settings used in the simulation study of the Sect. "Numerical study". In the tables provided in the supplementary material, some measures are presented to describe the residuals, namely, mean, standard deviation (SD), skewness (CS) and kurtosis (CK) coefficients and p-value of the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (KS) to evaluate the goodness-of-fit the distribution of the K-residuals and from the adapted K-residuals (for more details on KS test see30). We can see, in all scenarios, that the K-residuals and the adapted K-residuals have means close to 0.5, standard deviations close to 0.3, skewness close to zero and kurtosis close to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$-1.4$$\end{document}. In all scenarios, the KS test is not significant, which confirms that such residuals have uniform distributions in the range [0, 1].

Application

We considered the data set presented in31 and available in the software "R" through the package nlme with the name “Orthodont” and studied by32. These data report the follow-up carried out by researchers at the University of North Carolina Dental School on the growth of children (16 boys, 11 girls) aged 8 to 14 years old. And to carry out this study, the researchers measured, every two years, the distance between the pituitary and the pteromaxillary fissure using x-ray on the side of the head. Thereby, the aim of this study is to estimate this distance for 27 patients over time, using a multivariate BS regression model, given in (7) and (8), considering the explanatory variables sex and age. In this case, we do not have the presence of censoring.

Discussion

In this paper, we proposed an extension of the BS bivariate regression model introduced in13. Specifically, we derived a new multivariate distribution and investigated some of its properties. To evaluate the adequacy of the proposed model, we defined residuals for uncensored and censored cases. Subsequently, we carried out simulation studies to validate the effectiveness of maximum likelihood estimators and evaluate the empirical distribution of the proposed residuals. The results presented were consistent. Lastly, we apply this methodology to analyze a real-world dataset.

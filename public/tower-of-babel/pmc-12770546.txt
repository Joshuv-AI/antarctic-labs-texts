Information interaction and optimal scheduling method for prosumers in an energy community considering the profit driven degree
Authors: Hong Zhang, Haiyun Dong, Guanmu Wang, Daoxing Sun, Juan Lou, Gang Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Driven by the evolution of the energy internet, distributed energy growth, and power system modernization, consumers are transforming into prosumers with both production and consumption capabilities. This paper develops a prosumer trade model incorporating profit driven degree. A profit driven mechanism, based on Weber-Fechner’s law, measures how trading decisions respond to real-time electricity price sensitivity and demand urgency. Through information exchange among neighboring prosumers, a distributed convergence optimization method is applied to rapidly determine consistent transaction power. An optimal scheduling model is built to minimize operating costs for all prosumers, solved via the alternating direction multiplier method. Using the IEEE-33 node distribution network with 19 prosumers, simulations verify the model’s effectiveness. Results show a 17.1% reduction in total community cost and up to 41.2% reduction in main grid purchases when profit driven behavior and information interaction are considered. The synchronous ADMM algorithm converges in about 10 iterations, demonstrating the model’s economic efficiency, enhanced clean energy utilization, and computational suitability for engineering applications.

Introduction

With the deep integration of the Internet of Things and the energy grid1, traditional electricity users are transforming into prosumers with dual attributes of energy production and consumption. Such users can install distributed power generation equipment to generate electricity independently, and excess electricity can be traded with each other or fed back to the grid2,3. As a new type of energy management platform, the energy community allows prosumers to interact with energy and information through local power networks and communication networks4. This model not only improves energy efficiency5 but also provides more economic benefits for prosumers6. How to optimize the energy trading strategy between prosumers to reduce the overall cost of the community and improve the consumption capacity of distributed energy has become an important issue in current research.

Literature review

Regarding trading strategies of prosumers in energy communities, prior studies have explored prosumer trading patterns and behaviors from multiple perspectives. Sabzevari et al.7 developed a “data purification + multi-algorithm forecasting” framework to counter renewable data tampering, quantifying prediction accuracy across methods to enhance market decision resilience. Chong et al.8 proposed a cooperative game model based on the Shapley value, innovatively applied to end-to-end power trading to ensure equitable benefit allocation. Guan et al.9 designed a P2P trading mechanism in regional electricity markets with multiple prosumers, considering heterogeneous energy demands and low-carbon preferences. Considering prosumers’ psychological gap effects and bounded rationality, a distributed electricity market trading mechanism based on a Stackelberg game was constructed10. Bâra et al.11 embedded a decision-support method within an energy assistant to facilitate local market trading. Liu et al.12 proposed a dynamic game model for photovoltaic prosumer energy sharing, capturing temporal interactions. Mitrea et al.13 applied a hawk–dove game in a blockchain market to encourage cooperation and penalize selfishness. Saman et al.14 integrated Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) into multi-objective optimization for renewable hybrid microgrids, using scenario generation/reduction and demand response programs to quantify the impact of different risk attitudes on scheduling. Norouzi et al.15 developed a virtual energy hub model with intelligent parking lots (V2G-enabled), applying two-stage stochastic programming with CVaR for coordinated electricity–heat multi-market trading. Yao et al.16 proposed an optimal green certificate–carbon quota decision method for power producers in a competitive market. Cai et al.17 designed a green community energy sharing strategy monetizing environmental value via carbon integration, establishing an internal transaction mechanism, and solving the distributed optimization with ADMM. To address distributed photovoltaic consumption, Wang et al.18 introduced a multiplayer shared energy storage alliance model based on P2P transactions. However, few studies have specifically investigated the effect of prosumers’ own profit driven degree on trading decisions, resulting in insufficient improvement in fairness and participation incentives during energy transactions.

Research has proposed various methods for determining information interaction mechanisms among prosumers in energy communities. Wang et al.19 adopted a master–slave game model where prosumers determine energy exchange quantities and electricity prices based on game outcomes, then share revenue information to establish a cooperative game framework. Zhou et al.20 applied information gap decision theory (IGDT) to address uncertainties in distributed photovoltaic (PV) generation while optimizing multimarket trading strategies. Zhan et al.21 also used IGDT to characterize diverse uncertainties in renewable energy systems and electricity markets, offering a robust decision-making approach. Li et al.22 proposed a framework where prosumers collaborate through information sharing to coordinate revenue allocation and promote demand response objectives. Jia et al.23 enabled producers and consumers to transmit information via market operators, who employ a CNN-LSTM-ATT model to forecast joint probability distributions of multiple energy prices, making the results public to support strategy formulation. However, many of these studies rely on centralized structures, leading to privacy leakage risks, high computational complexity, and limited scalability. Khalaifat et al.24 systematically reviewed privacy-preserving machine learning in resource-constrained environments, identifying robustness and communication efficiency trade-offs. Fan et al.25 developed an efficient privacy-preserving federated learning framework for IoT systems to balance robustness with low communication cost. Overall, existing mechanisms face centralized dependence, complexity, and privacy concerns, while specialized privacy-preserving schemes often incur excessive communication and computation burdens. Therefore, it is essential to design information interaction methods that ensure efficiency, security, and adaptability to constrained environments.

This paper proposes a framework that integrates profit driven degree modeling and information interaction coordination for energy communities with a high proportion of prosumers, aiming to achieve reliable and cost-effective transaction decision-making. Operational framework

With the promotion of household solar photovoltaic panels, household fans and energy storage units (including energy storage batteries, electric vehicles, etc.), an increasing number of users have changed from traditional energy buyers to prosumers. Operational framework of the prosumer community.

In this community, power transmission is completed by the local transmission network. These networks interconnect neighboring prosumers, enabling bidirectional energy flows and electricity trading. The prosumers can meet the load demand through the multisource cooperative energy supply mode, including photovoltaics, wind turbine power generation, energy storage unit discharge, and power trading with the main network or other prosumers. Prosumers with electricity surpluses can decide to sell or store more than electricity according to the real-time electricity price and the load demand of other prosumers. The prosumer formulates the transaction power through the distributed information interaction algorithm and formulates the transaction price on the basis of its own profit driven degree. By integrating the internal energy trading mechanism of the energy community and the local decision-making optimization model of prosumers, the prosumer trading plan of the energy community is generated to achieve efficient collaborative scheduling and optimal allocation of resources in the energy community.

Prosumer modeling considering profit driven degree

In this section, by considering the profit driven degree of prosumers, an electricity trading model based on the Nash bargaining solution is established. Moreover, the objective function and related constraints of the prosumer model are constructed. Factors such as photovoltaic power generation, wind turbine power generation, energy storage charge and discharge, and transaction loss are comprehensively considered to provide a basis for subsequent accurate analysis and optimization of prosumers’ electricity trading decisions.

Pricing strategy of prosumers based on the Nash bargaining solution

In this case, a transaction price acceptable to both parties can be found through the bargaining mechanism. As formulated in Eqs. (7)-(9), the Nash Bargaining Solution (NBS) is introduced to determine transaction prices by maximizing the utility product of both parties, thereby reducing operational costs27.

Constraint condition

Energy storage model

The prosumers conduct energy storage charging and discharging operations according to their own energy consumption needs and economic benefits. Specifically, charging and discharging behavior is affected by many factors, including the remaining capacity of energy storage, capacity, current real-time electricity price and so on. Case analysis

Parameter setting

To verify the efficiency and rationality of the proposed distributed energy trading strategy, simulations are conducted on an IEEE-33 node distribution network energy community comprising 19 prosumers, each equipped with photovoltaic (PV) generation and energy storage. PV and load data are sourced from literature23. The PV installed capacity is 40 kWh; storage charging/discharging efficiency is 90%; battery capacity limits are 60 kWh/12 kWh; maximum charging/discharging powers are 100 kW and 3 kW, respectively; charging/discharging cost is 0.1 RMB/kWh; and the initial state of charge is 50%. Information exchanges occur once per hour between neighboring prosumers, and each prosumer subproblem is solved via Yalmip + Cplex. Three scenarios are analyzed:

Case 1

Prosumers schedule their own energy consumption and storage without community trading.

Case 2

Community trading is allowed, with transaction prices dynamically adjusted according to each prosumer’s profit driven degree, and transaction power determined by the community operator based on historical data.

Case 3

Building on Case 2, the proposed information interaction strategy is applied, enabling prosumers to refine trading plans through neighbor-to-neighbor information exchange.

Conclusion

This research establishes an energy community framework integrating multiple prosumers, where a distributed consensus-oriented information transfer algorithm enables autonomous trading through localized neighbor interactions. The model explicitly incorporates the profit driven degree as a core behavioral determinant. Key findings derived from systematic case analyses are:

1) A decision-reflective behavioral model formalizes profit-seeking dynamics in internal energy trading. By synthesizing the stimulus amount metric with Weber-Fechner’s psychophysical law, rigorous mathematical relationships between profit driven degree and external/internal stimuli are derived. Simulation outcomes demonstrate 6.1% reduction in aggregate community costs after implementing profit driven degree-sensitive trading mechanisms, concurrently enhancing economic efficiency and local photovoltaic utilization rates.

2) To address coordination complexity in large-scale prosumer networks, a distributed neighbor-node information exchange protocol dynamically adjusts transaction power using purely local data. This strategy maintains global supply-demand equilibrium while reducing convergence iterations by 33–60% versus benchmarks. Algorithmic stability persists during scalability tests, with prosumer populations expanding from 20 to 60 nodes under constrained communication topologies, confirming operational adaptability and architectural scalability.

3) Community cost optimization via synchronous ADMM achieves convergence within approximately 10 iterations–50% and 60% faster than standard ADMM (20 iterations) and distributed gradient descent (25 iterations), respectively. Confidential prosumer data remains exclusively processed at local nodes, eliminating privacy leakage risks. Computational efficiency and solution accuracy (RMSE < 0.01) are preserved in 60-node large-scale validation scenarios.

While the integration of behavioral economics, market mechanisms, and distributed optimization demonstrates superior economy, convergence, and privacy, two limitations merit acknowledgment: (1) Photovoltaic/load forecasting assumes idealized uncertainty-free conditions, and (2) Validation excludes multi-community hierarchical networks. Future research will incorporate stochastic optimization for uncertainty management and extend the framework to cross-community energy ecosystems.

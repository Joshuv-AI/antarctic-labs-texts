How to use Gaia parallaxes for stars with poor astrometric fits
Kareem El-Badry 1

Abstract
Gaia parallax measurements for stars with poor astrometric fits – as evidenced by high renormalized unit weight error ( RUWE ) – are often assumed to be unreliable, but the extent and nature of their biases remain poorly quantified. High RUWE is usually a consequence of binarity or higher-order multiplicity, so the parallaxes of sources with high RUWE are often of greatest astrophysical interest. Using realistic simulations of Gaia epoch astrometry, we show that the parallax uncertainties of sources with elevated RUWE are underestimated by a factor that ranges from 1 to 4 and can be robustly predicted from observables. We derive an empirical prescription to inflate reported uncertainties based on a simple analytic function of RUWE , apparent magnitude, and parallax. We validate the correction using (a) single-star solutions for Gaia sources with known orbital solutions and (b) wide binaries containing one component with elevated RUWE . The same uncertainty corrections are expected to perform well in DR4 and DR5. Our results demonstrate that Gaia parallaxes for high- RUWE sources can still yield robust distance estimates if uncertainties are appropriately inflated, enabling distance constraints for triples, binaries with periods too long or too short to be fit astrometrically, and sources blended with neighboring sources.

Introduction

Data from the
Gaia
mission has enabled parallax measurements – and thus, distance constraints – for about 2 billion stars
(
Gaia Collaboration et al., 2016
;
Gaia Collaboration et al., 2023a
)
. For astrometrically well-behaved sources that are effectively single, motion on the plane of the sky can be fully described by a linear model with five free parameters: two positional coordinates, two proper motions, and parallax. The parallax is often the quantity of greatest interest, because it constrains the distance to the star
(
Bailer-Jones, 2015
;
Luri et al., 2018
, e.g.)
.

Some
Gaia
sources have spurious parallax measurements. In this context, “spurious” does not necessarily mean that the parallax contains no useful information, but rather that the difference between the reported parallax and its true value significantly exceeds the reported uncertainty. The existence of such sources is manifest, for example, in the prevalence of sources with significantly negative parallaxes
(
Rybizki et al., 2022
, e.g.)
.

Most spurious
Gaia
astrometric solutions are due to binaries and higher-order multiples. Binaries with detectable astrometric “wobble” on the sky – typically systems with orbital periods of months to years – lead to complicated motion of the photocenter that cannot be described by a five-parameter astrometric solution. Binaries with much longer orbital periods of centuries to millennia can similarly lead to spurious astrometric solutions, because blended light from a marginally resolved companion perturbs astrometric measurements
(
Lindegren, 2022
, e.g.)
. Physically unassociated close neighbors that are in the foreground or background of a source can have the same effect.

Several parameters quantify the goodness of fit of
Gaia
five-parameter solutions. The most useful of these is
RUWE
(“renormalized unit weight error”) which approximately represents the reduced chi-squared,
\chi_{\nu}^{2}
, of the five-parameter astrometric solution
(
Lindegren, 2018
)
. Several previous works have explored the utility of
RUWE
as diagnostic of spurious solutions and binary companions.
Lindegren (2018)
proposed that for
Gaia
DR2, a cut of
RUWE

<1.4
would retain most ‘‘good’’ solutions while filtering out most spurious ones.
1
1

1

For DR3, a threshold of
RUWE

<1.25
has been proposed to be more appropriate, though the exact threshold varies with sky position
(
Penoyre et al., 2022a
)
.

Belokurov et al. (2020)
showed that, at least for nearby sources,
RUWE

>1.4
primarily selects sources in regions of the color-magnitude diagram (CMD) where binaries are expected to be overrepresented.
Penoyre et al. (2020)
developed a framework to predict
RUWE
and other astrometric observables for binaries;
Penoyre et al. (2022a)
,
Penoyre et al. (2022b)
, and
Korol et al. (2022)
used this framework to infer properties of the local binary population from the observed
RUWE
distribution. Most recently,
Castro-Ginard et al. (2024)
developed a model to quantify the selection function of
RUWE
-based binary samples, while
Kiefer et al. (2024)
used
RUWE
to select candidate exoplanet hosts.

It is frequently assumed in the literature that parallaxes for source with large
RUWE
(most commonly,
RUWE

>1.4
) are unreliable. However, it has not been quantified
how
unreliable, and under what conditions such parallaxes still contain useful information. One might expect, for example, that larger
RUWE
values result in less reliable parallaxes, or that nearby sources with large
RUWE
are less affected than distant ones with the same
RUWE
. Sources with elevated
RUWE
are often of particular astrophysical interest: for example, binaries that receive spectroscopic solutions but not astrometric orbital solutions are likely to have large
RUWE

(
Thompson et al., 2019
;
El-Badry & Quataert, 2021
;
Yamaguchi et al., 2024
;
Wang et al., 2024
, e.g.)
, and distance is often the key uncertainty in measuring the physical properties of these systems.

Poor astrometric solutions as quantified by
RUWE
are quite common. This is unsurprising, since an order unity fraction of all
Gaia
sources are binaries. For example, 17% of
Gaia
DR3 sources in the
Gaia
Catalog of Nearby Stars
(
Gaia Collaboration et al., 2021
)
have
RUWE

>1.4
, and 22% have
RUWE

>1.25
. It is thus desirable to understand how to interpret the parallaxes and uncertainties of such sources. In this work, we show that while sources with elevated
RUWE
have underestimated uncertainties, the magnitude of the difference between the best-fit and true parallax can be accurately predicted from observables. The parallaxes of such sources can thus in most cases still yield useful distance constraints.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we describe simulations of
Gaia
epoch astrometry and astrometric model fitting. Section
3
summarizes a series of controlled experiments designed to estimate the parallax bias that results from unmodeled noise in the epoch astrometry. In Section
3.2
, we present a simple analytic fitting function that captures the trends revealed in our simulations and can be used to inflate published
Gaia
parallax uncertainties for sources with large
RUWE
. We validate this function using binaries with astrometric orbits and resolved wide binaries in Section
4
. We discuss our results and conclude in Section
5
.

2
Description of the simulations

We simulate astrometric orbits for binaries using the
gaiamock
2
2

2

https://github.com/kareemelbadry/gaiamock
model, which is described in detail by
El-Badry et al. (2024)
.
gaiamock
generates realistic 1D epoch astrometry for binaries or single stars from the
Gaia
scanning law, using an empirical noise model based on the residuals of well-behaved astrometric sources. It then fits the epoch astrometry with an astrometric model; here, we consider only the five-parameter single-star model.

Following the
Gaia
convention,
gaiamock
inflates the astrometric uncertainties of sources with poor fits. This is accomplished by inflating all the epoch astrometry uncertainties, and the uncertainties of the astrometric parameters, by a constant
c=\sqrt{\chi^{2}/\left[\nu\left(1-\frac{2}{9\nu}\right)^{3}\right]}\approx\sqrt{\chi_{\nu}^{2}}
. Here
\nu
is the number of astrometric epoch measurements minus five and is typically
\approx 300
in DR3. As we will show, this inflation is generally not sufficient, because the five-parameter astrometric model is mismatched to the data.
RUWE
for the five-parameter solution is calculated as

\displaystyle\texttt{RUWE}=\sqrt{\chi_{\nu}^{2}},

(1)

meaning that the constant factor by which the published parallax uncertainties are inflated is approximately equal to
RUWE
. As we show below,
RUWE
calculated from Equation
1
is directly comparable to
RUWE
reported in the
Gaia
archive – there is no need for renormalization, because the epoch uncertainties used by
gaiamock
are estimated empirically from the residuals of well-behaved sources, not from the formal errors.

2.1
Two-parameter solutions

Another quantity required for constructing a realistic mock astrometric catalog is
\sigma_{\rm 5d,\,max}
(reported as
astrometric_sigma5d_max
in the
Gaia
archive), which is calculated as the square root of the largest singular value of the scaled
5\times 5
covariance matrix of the astrometric parameters. The “scaling” is as described in
Lindegren et al. (2018)
, with
T
taken to be the nominal DR3 baseline of 2.76383 yr, and singular values calculated from the covariance matrix
after
uncertainty inflation. Roughly speaking,
\sigma_{{\rm 5d,\,max}}
represents the most uncertain astrometric parameter or linear combination of said parameters. Sources with
\sigma_{{\rm 5d,\,max}}>10^{0.2\,{\rm max}\left(6-G,0,G-18\right)}
do not receive five-parameter solutions and are instead published with 2-parameter solutions. The main effect of this cut is that it removes sources with very large formal astrometric uncertainties, which are mainly marginally resolved wide binaries with similar-brightness components
(
Tokovinin, 2023
)
. The cut also results in a maximum
RUWE
of
\sim 50
for bright sources (and lower for fainter sources), since
RUWE
is not calculated for two-parameter solutions.

2.2
Validating
RUWE
predictions from
gaiamock

To verify that
gaiamock
accurately predicts
RUWE
, we begin by using the code to predict
RUWE
of the five-parameter solutions of binaries that received full 12-parameter astrometric orbits in DR3
(
Halbwachs et al., 2023
;
Gaia Collaboration et al., 2023b
)
. Of the 168,065 astrometric orbits published in DR3, we retain 154,778 good orbits satisfying
goodness_of_fit

<5
for
G>13
and
goodness_of_fit

<10
for
G\leq 13

(
El-Badry et al., 2023
, see)
. We predict their DR3 epoch astrometry and five-parameter solutions using
gaiamock
; the corresponding
RUWE
values are shown in Figure
1
.

gaiamock
predicts
RUWE
for DR3 binaries quite accurately, with negligible systemic offset and scatter of 16.6%.
RUWE
is strongly correlated with
a_{0}
, which is not surprising, because it is essentially a measure of the size of typical residuals for the five-parameter solution (which scales with
a_{0}
) in units of the per-epoch astrometric uncertainties. However, predicting
RUWE
with
gaiamock
results in a tighter correlation than any simple scaling with
a_{0}
. The tight correlation shown in Figure
1
demonstrates that
gaiamock
predicts
RUWE
values for binaries robustly. At low
RUWE
, there is a slight bias toward higher predicted than observed
RUWE
, perhaps hinting at imperfectly modeled systematics in the low-
RUWE
regime. This, however, only amounts to a median ruwe difference of 0.02 for sources with
RUWE

<1.5
.

3
Results

We use
gaiamock
to simulate orbits of samples of binaries at fixed apparent magnitude and distance. For each such experiment, we draw
10,000
orbital periods from the lognormal distribution observed for solar-type binaries
(
Raghavan et al., 2010
)
and mass ratios from a uniform distribution. We assume
1\,M_{\odot}
primaries and assign
G-
band flux ratios assuming both components are at the zero-age main sequence, assign the binaries random orientations and phases, and draw eccentricities from a Rayleigh distribution with
\sigma_{e}=0.34

(
Wu et al., 2025
)
. Our results are not sensitive to any of these choices, because we are ultimately investigating parallax bias at fixed
RUWE
, not at fixed binary parameters.

At short periods (
P_{\rm orb}\lesssim 100
d for the distance shown in Figure
2
), binaries have accurate parallaxes and uncertainties and
RUWE

\approx 1
. This reflects the fact that these binaries have photocenter orbits that are small compared to the
Gaia
DR3 measurement uncertainties. At periods ranging from
100-1000
d,
RUWE
rises with increasing
P_{\rm orb}
, because longer-period binaries have larger photocenter orbits that lead to larger deviations from single-star motion. Some binaries in this period range still have low
RUWE
; these are systems with mass ratios near 0 or 1.

The formal parallax uncertainties – which are inflated by a factor almost identical to
RUWE
– rise in tandem with
RUWE
. The typical parallax bias also increases, as evidenced by increased scatter in the second panel of Figure
2
. The scatter is generally largest at period ranges where
RUWE
is also large, but trends in
RUWE
and parallax bias with period are not identical. Most notably, there is a sharp increase in parallax bias at
P_{\rm orb}\approx 1
year that is not matched by a spike in
RUWE
. As has been demonstrated elsewhere
(
Penoyre et al., 2020
, e.g.)
, this feature is a result of orbital motion being mis-absorbed into the parallax. However, only a small fraction of all binaries fall in the relevant 300-500 d period range.

All three quantities shown in Figure
2
drop at periods beyond 1000 d, where observations in
Gaia
DR3 have been taken over less than a full orbit and most of the orbital motion can be absorbed into the apparent proper motion. Binaries with
P_{\rm orb}=10^{4}-10^{5}
d thus receive unproblematic five-parameter solutions with small
RUWE
and little parallax bias. However, at periods longer than
10^{5}
d, all three quantities begin to rise again. This is a result of the binaries’ being marginally resolved, such that the position measured by the
Gaia
image parameter determination pipeline depends on the scan angle and traces neither the photocenter nor the location of either binary component
(
Lindegren, 2022
, e.g.)
. Binaries with flux ratios near 1 are most strongly affected, while those with faint secondaries retain small
RUWE
and unbiased parallaxes. The largest predicted
RUWE
values occur in this period range and are a result of marginally resolved pairs with flux ratios near unity. The most extreme cases (red points) fail the cut on
\sigma_{\rm 5d,\,max}
and only receive 2-parameter solutions.

Given a set of simulated sources, we estimate their
true
parallax uncertainty (i.e., the expected magnitude of the bias) from the distribution of measured parallaxes. The right panels of Figure
2
show that for any fixed apparent magnitude (corresponding to fixed along-scan uncertainty
\sigma_{\eta}
) and distance, the distribution of best-fit parallaxes is close to Gaussian, with the width of this distribution corresponding to the parallax bias. If the formal parallax uncertainties are accurate,
\left(\varpi-\varpi_{{\rm true}}\right)/\sigma_{\varpi}
should follow a normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, as in the top panel for sources with
\texttt{RUWE}\approx 1
. For
\texttt{RUWE}>1
, the distribution broadens, and its width becomes diagnostic of the parallax bias.

3.1
Dependence of parallax bias on distance and apparent magnitude

We perform simulations similar to those shown in Figure
2
for a range of apparent magnitudes,
G=6-20
in steps of 1 mag, and a range of distances, with 50 logarithmically spaced values between 30 pc and 10 kpc. In each case, we quantify the width of the parallax bias distribution as half the difference between its 16th and 84th percentiles.

The parallax bias
\delta_{\varpi}
(i.e., the “true” parallax uncertainty) is correlated with
\sigma_{\varpi}
, the formal parallax uncertainty. This is not surprising, since
\sigma_{\varpi}
contains information about both the goodness of fit of the five-parameter solution and the number of observations used in calculating it. We therefore quantify the parallax bias via an uncertainty inflation factor,
f
, which is defined as

f=\frac{\delta_{\varpi}}{\sigma_{\varpi}},

(2)

such that
f=1
corresponds to accurate uncertainties.

\delta_{\varpi}
is defined such that parallax measurements of sources with true parallax
\varpi
follow a normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(\varpi,\delta_{\varpi}^{2})
.

The results are shown in the top panels of Figure
3
for selected apparent magnitudes. We find that the shape of
f(\texttt{RUWE})
depends only weakly on apparent magnitude and distance. In all cases,
f\approx 1
at
\texttt{RUWE}=1
, increases over
1<\texttt{RUWE}<2
, and plateaus at
\texttt{RUWE}>3
. The asymptotic value of
f
at large
RUWE
is
\approx 3
on average, with a weak dependence on apparent magnitude and parallax. The increase at small
RUWE
is steep: at
RUWE

=1.4
, parallax uncertainties are already underestimated by a factor of 2 on average. The range of
RUWE
values that occur is smaller at fainter magnitudes, where the per-epoch along-scan uncertainties
\sigma_{\eta}
are smaller.

3.2
Analytic fitting function

Motivated by the simulation results shown in the top panel of Figure
3
, we parameterize the parallax uncertainty inflation factor
f
as follows:

f=\begin{cases}1&\texttt{RUWE}\leq 1\\
1+\left(f_{{\rm max}}-1\right)\left(1-e^{-\alpha\left(\texttt{RUWE}-1\right)}\right),&\texttt{RUWE}>1\end{cases}.

(3)

This function rises from
f=1
at
\texttt{RUWE}=1
to
f\to f_{\rm max}
at large
RUWE
; the parameter
\alpha
controls the steepness of the transition. Because our simulations reveal a dependence of the asymptotic inflation factor at large
RUWE
on parallax and apparent magnitude, we parameterize
f_{\rm max}
as a power law function of
\varpi
and
\sigma_{\eta}
:

f_{{\rm max}}=f_{0}\left(\frac{\varpi}{10\,{\rm mas}}\right)^{\beta}\left(\frac{\sigma_{\eta}\left(G\right)}{0.1\,{\rm mas}}\right)^{\gamma}.

(4)

We fit the results of the full simulation grid simultaneously, finding best-fit parameters of
\alpha=2.77
,
f_{0}=3.73
,
\beta=0.065
, and
\gamma=-0.056
. In the bottom panels of Figure
3
, we plot the corresponding predicted
f
values. Here
\sigma_{\eta}(G)
is the empirical “per-CCD” along-scan measurement uncertainty shown in Figure 1 of
El-Badry et al. (2024)
, which is taken from
Holl et al. (2023)
.

The fitting function captures most of the structure visible in the data. We use parallax uncertainty inflation factors predicted by Equation
3
in the remainder of the paper.

4
Validation

We validate the parallax uncertainty inflation prescription from Equation
3
using two independent approaches.

4.1
Sources with astrometric orbital solutions

We first consider the five-parameter astrometric solutions of sources that also received a 12-parameter orbital solution in DR3; i.e., the same sources shown in Figure
1
. These sources all have
RUWE

>1.4
, and their five-parameter solutions have much larger astrometric uncertainties than their 12-parameter solutions. We can thus consider the 12-parameter solutions as effectively ground truth, allowing us to gauge the true uncertainties of the five-parameter solutions and the effectiveness of the uncertainty inflation.

Figure
4
shows the difference between the five-parameter and 12-parameter solutions,
\Delta\varpi=\varpi_{12\,\rm par}-\varpi_{5\,\rm par}

\sigma_{\Delta\varpi}=\sqrt{\sigma_{\varpi_{5\,\rm par}}^{2}+\sigma_{\varpi_{12\,\rm par}}^{2}}
. Here
\varpi_{12\,\rm par}
and
\sigma_{\varpi_{12\,\rm par}}
are the parallax and its uncertainty from the
gaiadr3.nss_two_body_orbit
catalog, while
\varpi_{5\,\rm par}
and
\sigma_{\varpi_{5\,\rm par}}
are from the
gaiadr3.gaia_source
catalog. Following
Nagarajan & El-Badry (2024)
, we inflate
\sigma_{\varpi_{12\,\rm par}}
by a factor of 1.4 for sources with

and a factor of 1.7 for sources with

. Our results are not sensitive to this choice, because
\sigma_{\varpi_{12\,\rm par}}\ll\sigma_{\varpi_{5\,\rm par}}

In the left panel,
\sigma_{\varpi_{5\,\rm par}}
is the reported parallax uncertainty; in the right panel, it is inflated according to Equation

4.2
Wide binaries in which one component has high
RUWE

We next consider the parallax difference of spatially resolved wide binaries. The two components of a bound wide binary have essentially identical true parallaxes. If both components of a binary have reliable parallax uncertainties, then the distribution of uncertainty-normalized parallax errors,
\Delta\varpi/\sigma_{\Delta\varpi}
, should be a Gaussian with
\sigma\approx 1
. Here (different from Figure
4
)
\Delta\varpi
represents the difference between the five-parameter parallaxes of the two stars. If parallax errors are underestimated, the distribution will be broader. This fact was exploited by
El-Badry et al. (2021)
to validate the parallax uncertainties of
Gaia
five-parameter solutions for sources with
RUWE

<1.4
, and by
Nagarajan & El-Badry (2024)
to validate the uncertainties of 12-parameter orbital solutions.
El-Badry et al. (2021)
noted that their wide binary sample implied that sources with
RUWE

>1.4
have underestimated parallax uncertainties, but they discarded such sources rather than attempting to infer uncertainty inflation factors.

To validate our uncertainty inflation prescription, we consider wide binaries from the
El-Badry et al. (2021)
catalog in which one component has
RUWE

>1.4
. We consider pairs with separations
\theta<4
arcsec, for which
El-Badry et al. (2021)
considered any candidate companions with
\left|\Delta\varpi/\sigma_{\Delta\varpi}\right|<6
, and we only consider pairs with a
<10\%
chance alignment probability as estimated by
El-Badry et al. (2021)
. 99% of pairs in this sample have chance alignment probabilities lower than 1%.

RUWE
.

5
Discussion and Conclusions

Using realistic simulations of
Gaia
epoch astrometry, we have presented a simple empirical prescription to inflate the parallax uncertainties of
Gaia
sources with poor astrometric fits (Equation
3
and Figure
3
). This correction restores statistical consistency between expected and observed parallax offsets for binaries with known orbital solutions (Figure
4
) and for components of wide binaries (Figure
5
).

In many cases, a source with high
RUWE
can still yield a useful distance constraint. For example, a solar-type star at
d=100
pc would have a DR3 parallax uncertainty of
\approx 0.02
mas if it were a well-behaved single source, corresponding to a parallax SNR of
\varpi/\sigma_{\varpi}=500
. If this source had
RUWE

=3
, the expected formal parallax uncertainty would be
\approx 0.06
mas, while the true uncertainty after inflation would be
\approx 0.2
mas, still yielding a high parallax SNR of 50. On the other hand, if the same solar-type star were placed at
d=1
kpc, the true parallax SNR would be
\varpi/\sigma_{\varpi}\approx 33
at
RUWE

=1
, but only
\approx 3
at
RUWE

=3
.

While our fiducial simulations assume that elevated
RUWE
is always a consequence of binarity, we have verified that our inferred uncertainty inflation factors still perform well when we simply add unmodeled noise to the epoch astrometry, as might be expected if an observed source’s
RUWE
is a result of photometric variability or unmodeled instrumental issues.

5.1
Uncertainties for sources with low
RUWE

El-Badry et al. (2021)
found that the parallax uncertainties of sources with
RUWE

<1.4
must be inflated by a magnitude-dependent factor to achieve statistical consistency between the parallaxes of the components of wide binaries. We find that the uncertainty inflation factors inferred by
El-Badry et al. (2021)
are nearly unchanged when a cut of
RUWE

<1.1
is used instead of
RUWE

<1.4
and conclude that some uncertainty inflation is still required for sources with
RUWE

\approx 1
. That is, the inflation factors inferred by
El-Badry et al. (2021)
are not simply a result of sources with e.g.
RUWE

=1.2-1.4
. We advocate using the uncertainty inflation factors inferred here for sources with significantly inflated
RUWE
, and reverting to the prescriptions from
El-Badry et al. (2021)
for other sources.

A reasonable sky-averaged
RUWE
threshold for poor astrometric fits, above which uncertainties should be inflated following Equation
3
, is
RUWE

>1.25

(
Penoyre et al., 2022a
, e.g.)
. The distribution of
RUWE
for well-behaved sources varies somewhat across the sky, so another option is to identify sources with poor fits as those with
RUWE
significantly larger than average for their sky position and apparent magnitude. However, the origin of spatial trends in
RUWE
is not yet well understood, so caution is advisable in modeling them. We also not advocate
deflating
uncertainties for sources with
RUWE

<1
: our empirical tests with wide binaries find that such sources have similarly true uncertainties to sources with
RUWE

\approx 1
.

5.2
Will these prescriptions remain valid in DR4?

Our simulations assume the noise model and observing baseline of
Gaia
DR3, but we expect the relation between
RUWE
and parallax bias to remain quite similar in DR4 and DR5. We have verified this using
gaiamock
simulations of DR4 data, both for scenarios in which the epoch-level noise model
\sigma_{\eta}(G)
remains the same in DR4 and scenarios in which it changes.

As the
Gaia
observing baseline increases, the
RUWE
distribution for well-behaved single sources becomes more narrowly peaked at
\texttt{RUWE}=1
, while binary-driven
RUWE
enhancements become sensitive to longer periods. However, since the formal parallax uncertainties are already inflated to yield
\chi_{\nu}^{2}\approx 1
, the additional parallax inflation factor required to account for mismatch between orbital motion and a five-parameter astrometric model is primarily set by geometry and is nearly independent of the astrometric uncertainties and observing baseline at fixed
RUWE
.

5.3
Proper motion uncertainties

We have not attempted to calculate uncertainty inflation factors for proper motions. We find that the proper motion bias in DR3 is most severe for binaries with periods of
10^{3-5}
d, somewhat longer than the observational baseline. Binaries with long periods that have negligible
RUWE
can still have highly significant proper motion bias, reflecting long-term motion of the photocenter due to the binary orbit. Proper motion bias is thus not strongly correlated with
RUWE
, though most binaries with large
RUWE
do also have underestimated proper motion uncertainties.

The magnitude of the proper motion bias due to long-term orbital motion will not exceed the orbital velocity of the component stars, which is typically a few
\rm km\,s^{-1}
at the periods most affected. Since this is still small compared to the typical space velocities of nearby stars, unrecognized proper motion bias is not likely to represent a major source of error for studies of Galactic structure, but it is potentially important for studies of dynamically cold stellar structures such as streams and wide binaries.

5.4
Orbital solutions

As part of
Gaia
astrometric processing, a cascade of models with increasing complexity are fit to the data, including seven- and nine-parameter acceleration solutions and 12-parameter orbital solutions
(
Halbwachs et al., 2023
)
. When such solutions are available, their parallax should generally be used over the parallax of the five-parameter solution
(
Nagarajan & El-Badry, 2024
)
. An analog of
RUWE
for these solutions could likely also be used to inflate their uncertainties in the case of poor fits, but the uncertainty inflation factor is likely to have more complicated parameter dependencies than in the case of five-parameter solutions.

Introduction

Rings and algebras considered in this paper are associative and unital. For any ring
R
, denote by
R^{*}
its group of invertible elements. A map
g:R\rightarrow R
is called an additive map if

(1.1)

g(x+y)=g(x)+g(y)

for all
x,y\in R
.

The equation (
1.1
) was first studied by Cauchy
[
4
, p.103]
for continuous real-valued functions of a real variable. Later, this classical equation led to the study of functional identities involving the inverse.

One of the earliest problems involving inverses was posed by Halperin in
1963

[
2
]
. He asked whether every additive map
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
g(y)=y^{2}g(y^{-1})
must necessarily be continuous.
In
1964
, Kurepa
[
16
]
generalized this problem considerably. He proved that, if
g_{1},g_{2}
are two non-zero additive maps and
P
is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}
with
P(1)=1
such that
g_{1}(y)=P(y)g_{2}(y^{-1})
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{\ast}
, then
P(y)=y^{2}
,
g_{1}(y)+g_{2}(y)=2yg_{2}(1)
, and the map
y\mapsto g_{1}(y)-yg_{1}(1)
is a derivation. Further in
1968
, Nishiyama and Horinouchi
[
21
]
characterized the maps satisfying
g(y^{m_{1}})=ay^{m_{2}}g(y^{m_{3}})
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
, where
m_{1},m_{2},\ \text{and}\ m_{3}
are the integers. Subsequently, Kannappan and Kurepa
[
13
,
14
]
investigated several functional identities involving inverses over
\mathbb{R}
during
1970-1971
, leading to further developments in the area.

Recall that an additive map
\delta
on a ring
R
is a derivation if
\delta(y_{1}y_{2})=\delta(y_{1})y_{2}+y_{1}\delta(y_{2})
for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in R
. Differentiating
yy^{-1}=1
gives
\delta(y)=-y\delta(y^{-1})y
for
y\in R^{*}
. Conversely one
asks which additive maps are forced to be of this form. In
1995
, Brešar
[
3
]
showed that additive
g_{1},g_{2}
on division ring
D
with
g_{1}(y)y=yg_{2}(y)
for all
y
are given by
g_{1}(y)=ya+\eta(y)
and
g_{2}(y)=ay+\eta(y)
for fixed
a\in D
and additive central
\eta
. In
2018
, Catalano
[
5
]
set a way for extending rational identities to functional identities and proved the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
5
, Theorem
1
]
)
Let
D
be a non-commutative division ring of
\operatorname{char}(D)\neq 2
. If
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are additive maps on
D
such that

(1.2)

g_{1}(y)y^{-1}+yg_{2}(y^{-1})=0

for all
y\in D^{*}
. Then there exists
a\in D
such that
g_{1}(y)=ya+\delta(y)
and
g_{2}(y)=-ay+\delta(y)
for all
y\in D
, where
\delta
is a derivation on
D
.

Catalano
[
5
]
proved the matrix analogue of Theorem
1.1
and asked whether the restriction
\operatorname{char}D\neq 3
imposed in
[
5
, Theorem
4
]
is needed. Latter, Argaç et. al.
[
1
]
extended Catalano’s results in

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[

, Theorem
1.5
]
)
Let

be a division ring of
\operatorname{char}(D)\neq 2
and let
R=M_{t}(D)
, where

g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are additive maps on

g_{1}(y)y^{-1}+yg_{2}(y^{-1})=0

g_{1}(y)=ya+\delta(y)
and
g_{2}(y)=-ay+\delta(y)
for all

The identity

[
20

[

extended the problem to division rings, while Lee and Lin

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
18
, Theorem
5.1
]
)
Let
D
be a non-commutative division ring of
\operatorname{char}(D)\neq 2

If
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are additive maps on

(1.3)

.

For

. They obtained

and
g_{2}(y)=ya
for all

, where

. The assumption non-commutative or

can not be removed in Theorem

Motivated by the above results, it is natural to ask the characterization of additive maps
g_{1},g_{2}:D\rightarrow D
satisfying the identity
g_{1}(y)y^{-m}+y^{n}g_{2}(y^{-1})=0,
where
n,m
are positive integers. Our approach to the above question is based on the approach of
[
18
]
, arising from generalized polynomials with coefficients in a division ring
D
.
Denote by
D_{G}[Y]
the generalized free algebra over
Z(D)
generated by
Y
. Its elements are finite

s

representation

q_{1}Yq_{2}Yq_{3}\cdots q_{s}Yq_{s+1},

where
q_{i}\in D
. For variables
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m}
, the generalized free algebra over
Z(D)
is represented by
D_{G}[Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{m}]
. For more details of generalized algebras see
[
11

.

Definition 1.4
.

A map
g:D\rightarrow D
is said to be an elementary operator if there exist finitely many
p_{i},q_{i}\in D
such that
g(y)=\sum_{i}p_{i}yq_{i}
for all
y\in D
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
g:D\to D
to be an additive map and let
G_{1}(Y),G_{2}(Y),\\
H(Y)\in D_{G}[Y]
such that
G_{1}(y)g(y)G_{2}(y)=H(y)
for all
y\in D
. If both
G_{1}(Y)
and
G_{2}(Y)
are non-zero, then either
g
is an elementary operator or
D
is finite-dimensional over its center
Z(D)
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
D
be a non-commutative division ring of
\operatorname{char}(D)\neq 2
. If
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are additive maps on
D
such that

(1.4)

g_{1}(y)y^{-m}+y^{n}g_{2}(y^{-1})=0

for all
y\in D^{*}
, where
m
and
n
are positive integers with
(m,n)\not=(1,1)
, then
g_{1}=g_{2}=0
.

We observe that for
n=1
and
m\geq 2
, if additive maps
g_{1},g_{2}
on
D
satisfy the identity
g_{1}(y)y^{-m}+yg_{2}(y^{-1})=0
for all
y\in D^{*}
, then by Theorem
1.6
we get
g_{1}=g_{2}=0
. As an application of this result, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
D
be a non-commutative division ring with
\operatorname{char}(D)\neq 2
and
a\in D^{*},\ell\in D
. If
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are additive maps on
D
such that
g_{1}(y_{1})y_{2}^{m}+y_{1}g_{2}(y_{2})=\ell

for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in D
with
y_{1}y_{2}=a
, where
a\in Z(D)
,
m\geq 2
is a positive integer, then
g_{1}(y)=0
and
g_{2}(y)=ya^{-1}\ell
for all
y\in D
.

Proof.

Let
y_{1}\in D^{*}
and
y_{2}=y_{1}^{-1}a
. It follows that
g_{1}(y_{1})y_{1}^{-m}a^{m}+y_{1}g_{2}(y_{1}^{-1}a)=\ell
. This implies

(1.5)

g_{1}(y_{1})y_{1}^{-m}a^{m}+y_{1}(g_{2}(y_{1}^{-1}a)-y_{1}^{-1}\ell)=0.

Multiplying equation (
1.5
) by

from the right hand side, we get

(1.6)

g_{1}(y_{1})y_{1}^{-m}+y_{1}(g_{2}(y_{1}^{-1}a)-y_{1}^{-1}\ell)a^{-m}=0.

It follows from Theorem
1.6
that
g_{1}(y_{1})=0
and
g_{2}(y_{1})=y_{1}a^{-1}\ell
for all
y_{1}\in D
.
∎

The assumption non-commutative with

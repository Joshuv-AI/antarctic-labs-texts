Safety, Pharmacokinetics, and Efficacy of 1.5% Ruxolitinib Gel (HDM3010) in Adult Patients with Prurigo Nodularis: A Phase I/II, Randomized, Double-Blind, Vehicle-Controlled Multicenter Clinical Trial
Authors: Chao Wu, Xuping Niu, Xiaohua Wang, Juan Su, Hengguang Zhao, Mao Lin, Chunjun Yang, Xiaoming Qin, Shuping Guo, Cunhuo Jiang, Hui Tang, Haijing Yang, Liming Wu, Min Zhu, Aihua Wei, Xiaohua Tao, Yiwen Zhang, Chang Shu, Jiawei Liu, Lu Yang, Yuetong Qian, Bingjie Zhang, Shiyu Zhang, Junfang Xu, Yang Lu, Mingjia Peng, Shizheng Luo, Weijie Ma, Hongzhong Jin

ABSTRACT
IntroductionPrurigo nodularis (PN) is a chronic inflammatory dermatosis with severe, persistent pruritus and limited effective therapies. Janus kinase (JAK) inhibition has emerged as a targeted strategy for PN.MethodsThis phase I/II, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, multicenter clinical trial was conducted in China to evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics, and efficacy of topical 1.5% ruxolitinib gel (HDM3010) in adults with PN. Adult patients with PN were randomized 2:1 to receive HDM3010 or vehicle, administered once daily (QD) or twice daily (BID), during a 4-week double-blind treatment period.ResultsForty-nine patients were randomized. HDM3010 demonstrated a safety profile characterized by treatment-emergent adverse events in 18.8% of QD and 25.0% of BID patients, compared with 12.5% of vehicle QD and 55.6% of vehicle BID patients. Treatment-related adverse events were infrequent (4.1%) and limited to mild-to-moderate severity. Pharmacokinetic analyses showed low systemic exposure, with mean peak plasma concentrations below 7 ng/mL at steady state. Compared with vehicle, HDM3010 was associated with higher proportions of ≥ 4-point Worst Itch Numerical Rating Scale (WI-NRS) responders and greater improvements in WI-NRS, Investigator’s Global Assessment for Prurigo Nodularis–Stage (IGA PN-S), and Investigator’s Global Assessment for Prurigo Nodularis-Activity (IGA PN-A) scores.ConclusionsHDM3010 demonstrated a favorable safety profile, minimal systemic exposure, and positive trends in reducing pruritus and lesions in adults with PN.Trial RegistrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (http://www.chictr.org.cn): ChiCTR2500096434 (registered January 23, 2025).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13555-026-01787-x.

Introduction

Prurigo nodularis (PN) is a chronic inflammatory dermatosis characterized by persistent, refractory pruritus lasting ≥ 6 weeks and symmetric nodular lesions predominantly affecting the trunk and extremities, resulting in a substantial impairment of quality of life [1–3]. The prevalence of PN among adults in the USA is estimated at approximately 72 per 100,000, whereas the annual prevalence and incidence in China are reported to be 0.13% and 0.02%, respectively [1, 4]. Current treatment options, mainly including topical corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors, and systemic immunomodulatory agents, are often limited by suboptimal efficacy, safety concerns, and restrictions on long-term use [5–8].

The pathogenesis of PN is driven by immune–neural dysregulation, with type 2 inflammation and key cytokines, including interleukin (IL)-4, IL-13, and IL-31, playing central roles in pruritus and cutaneous inflammation [3, 9–12]. These cytokines converge on Janus kinase (JAK)-signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT)-dependent signaling cascades within keratinocytes and peripheral sensory neurons, amplifying neuroimmune crosstalk that sustains chronic pruritus in PN. The JAK-STAT signaling pathway contributes to itch and inflammation through regulation of gene expression and direct effects on sensory nerve fibers, while JAK1/JAK2 signaling also influences skin barrier function [10, 13–18]. Lesional PN skin demonstrates increased expression of STAT2, STAT3, and cytokines associated with T helper (Th)2, Th17, and Th22 immune responses [14]. In refractory PN, oral JAK inhibitors such as tofacitinib and abrocitinib have shown clinical efficacy in reducing pruritus and lesion burden, supporting JAK inhibition as a promising therapeutic approach [19, 20].

Ruxolitinib, a selective JAK1/2 inhibitor, has demonstrated efficacy in inflammatory skin disorders such as atopic dermatitis and vitiligo, highlighting its therapeutic potential in PN. In the phase III TRuE-PN1 and TRuE-PN2 trials, topical 1.5% ruxolitinib cream significantly improved pruritus and skin lesions compared with vehicle in adults with PN [21]. A novel 1.5% ruxolitinib gel formulation (HDM3010) has since been developed to optimize topical treatment experience with an improved safety profile. The gel matrix is carbomer and contains no ethanol.

Compared to the cream formulation, the gel formulation is non-greasy, easy to apply and remove; it can absorb tissue exudate without compromising normal skin function. Moreover, the gel formulation is specifically designed to enhance drug permeability into hyperkeratotic thickened nodules, a key pathological feature of PN. Beyond these user‑experience benefits, the gel formulation also demonstrates a superior safety profile in terms of systemic exposure compared to the cream formulation. Preclinical and phase 1 pharmacokinetic studies demonstrated substantially reduced systemic exposure with HDM3010 relative to the cream formulation, with geometric mean ratios of 29% for maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) and 33% for area under the concentration–time curve from time 0 to the last measurable concentration (AUC0–t) supporting its suitability for potential long-term use (CTR20240226).

However, the clinical profile of HDM3010 in PN remains unestablished. Accordingly, this phase I/II, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, multicenter clinical trial was conducted to assess the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics (PK) profile, and preliminary efficacy of HDM3010 in adults with PN, providing a foundation for subsequent clinical development.

Methods

Patients and Study Design

This phase I/II, randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, multicenter clinical trial was conducted to evaluate the safety, tolerability, PK profile, and preliminary efficacy of topical 1.5% ruxolitinib gel (HDM3010, Huadong Medicine Co., Ltd, China) in adult Chinese patients with PN. The plan was to enroll a total of 48 participants and assign them to two cohorts: cohort 1 receiving once-daily (QD) administration and cohort 2 receiving twice-daily (BID) administration, with 24 participants in each cohort. The total study duration was approximately 16 weeks, including a screening period of up to 4 weeks, an 8-week treatment phase consisting of a 4-week double-blind period followed by a 4-week open-label extension, and a 30-day safety follow-up. In this study, cohort 1 and cohort 2 were conducted sequentially, with cohort 1 being enrolled and cohort 2 being enrolled after completion.

Key inclusion criteria included age 18–65 years; a clinical diagnosis of PN for at least 3 months; an Investigator’s Global Assessment for Prurigo Nodularis–Stage (IGA PN-S) score ≥ 2, corresponding to the presence of ≥ 6 palpable pruritic nodules, at both screening and baseline; and a weekly average Worst Itch Numerical Rating Scale (WI-NRS) score ≥ 7 during the week prior to randomization. Participants were required to have PN involvement of at least two body surface areas with a total affected body surface area < 20%. Major exclusion criteria comprised concomitant dermatologic or systemic diseases that could confound efficacy assessment, active infections, and recent use of JAK inhibitors, immunomodulatory agents, phototherapy, or other treatments with potential effects on study outcomes. Eligible participants were randomized 2:1 to HDM3010 or vehicle via a central randomization system. The sequence was generated using SAS software with stratified block randomization based on baseline disease severity (mild [IGA PN-S = 2] vs. moderate-to-severe [IGA PN-S ≥ 3]). Throughout the study, participants recorded daily WI-NRS scores and attended scheduled visits at weeks 2, 4, 6, and 8. Blood samples for PK analyses were obtained at protocol-defined time points.

During the study, the concurrent use of systemic or topical medications that may affect the efficacy assessment of PN was prohibited. Non-medicated emollients were permitted. Ophthalmic/otic, nasal, and inhaled corticosteroid preparations were allowed. Inhaled corticosteroids should have been used at a stable dose for > 28 days before screening and continued at the same dose throughout the study. Medications for chronic comorbidities (such as hypertension, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia) were permitted if the regimen was stable before screening and was continued unchanged throughout the study. Contraceptive use was permitted in female participants.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee (EC) for Clinical Trials of Drugs at the lead site, Peking Union Medical College Hospital (Approval number KS20241626), and by the ECs of all participating sites (full list in Online Resource Table S3). The study protocol was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and applicable regulatory requirements. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to study procedures. This trial was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (http://www.chictr.org.cn) under the registration number ChiCTR2500096434.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes were the safety and tolerability of HDM3010 in patients with PN. Adverse events of special interest (AESIs) included severe infections, tuberculosis, herpesvirus reactivation, major adverse cardiovascular events, malignancies, thrombotic events, and grade ≥ 2 hematologic abnormalities (CTCAE 5.0).

Secondary outcomes comprised the PK profile and preliminary efficacy of HDM3010. Blood samples were collected according to the prespecified schedule for PK evaluation. Efficacy assessments included the proportion of participants achieving a ≥ 4-point improvement from baseline in the weekly average WI-NRS score at weeks 1, 2, 4, and 8; the change from baseline in the weekly average WI-NRS score; and changes from baseline in the IGA PN-S and Investigator’s Global Assessment for Prurigo Nodularis-Activity (IGA PN-A) scores. Week 8 data were available only for participants who elected to enter the extension phase.

PK Analysis

An intensive sampling strategy was employed to characterize the PK profile of HDM3010. Venous blood samples were obtained from all participants on day 1 (baseline, D1) and day 29 (D29) immediately prior to dosing (0 h) and at 2, 4, 8, and 12 h post-dose; an additional 24 h post-dose sample was collected in the QD group. All pre-dose samples were collected within 1 h before administration. Plasma ruxolitinib concentrations were quantified using high-performance liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry. PK parameters were derived using WinNonlin software version 8.0 based on actual dosing and sampling times.

Statistical Analysis

The study aimed to enroll 24 participants each in cohorts 1 (QD) and 2 (BID). Given that this is a phase I/II study with a limited number of participants and an exploratory design, the sample size was based on clinical considerations and was not powered for formal hypothesis testing. Safety outcomes were summarized descriptively. PK parameters were reported descriptively. For efficacy analyses, categorical variables were expressed as percentages with 95% confidence intervals calculated using the Clopper–Pearson exact method, and between-group comparisons were performed using the stratified Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test. Continuous variables were analyzed using analysis of covariance, with treatment group, baseline disease severity, and baseline score included as covariates. All tests were two-sided with a significance level of α = 0.05.

Results

All analyses were based on data obtained during the randomized, double-blind treatment period (weeks 0–4) in the QD and BID cohorts. The study remains ongoing; however, data from the double-blind phase have been finalized and database locked.

Patients

From February 2025 to August 2025, a total of 76 participants with PN were screened from 15 sites, of whom 49 met eligibility criteria and were randomized (QD cohort, n = 24; BID cohort, n = 25). In the QD cohort, subjects were randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either HDM3010 once daily (QD) (n = 16) or a matching vehicle control (n = 8). In the BID cohort, subjects were randomized to receive either HDM3010 twice daily (BID) (n = 16) or a vehicle control (n = 9). Twenty-seven individuals failed screening, including 25 who did not satisfy inclusion or exclusion criteria and two who withdrew informed consent (Online Resource Fig. S1). Baseline demographic and disease characteristics were generally comparable between treatment and vehicle groups. Overall, 79.6% (39/49) of participants had moderate-to-severe disease, defined as an IGA PN-S score ≥ 3. The proportion of patients with IGA PN-S ≥ 3 was higher in the BID cohort (HDM3010, 87.5%; vehicle, 88.9%) than in the QD cohort (HDM3010, 68.8%; vehicle, 75.0%), suggesting a potentially greater Safety

During the double-blind period, treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurred in 18.8% (3/16) of patients receiving HDM3010 QD, 12.5% (1/8) receiving vehicle QD, 25.0% (4/16) receiving HDM3010 BID, and 55.6% (5/9) receiving vehicle BID, yielding an overall TEAE incidence of 26.5% (13/49). No TEAE occurred in ≥ 2 patients. Most TEAEs were grade 1 or 2. A single resolved grade 3 hypertensive aggravation occurred in one patient in the HDM3010 BID group and was considered possibly unrelated to study treatment. No TEAEs or serious adverse events resulted in treatment discontinuation or study withdrawal, and no AESIs. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were infrequent, occurring in 4.1% (2/49) of participants, all in the HDM3010 groups (one each in the QD and BID cohorts). One patient in the HDM3010 QD group experienced grade 1 elevated free triiodothyronine and transient grade 1 application-site pruritus and pain, all of which resolved without intervention. Another patient in the HDM3010 BID group developed grade 1 elevated alanine aminotransferase which resolved without treatment interruption and concurrent grade 2 elevated gamma-glutamyl transferase; this patient had a pre-existing history of fatty liver disease and positive serology for hepatitis B core and e antibodies. HDM3010 demonstrated favorable local tolerability without an apparent dose-related pattern (Online Resource Table S1).

PK

PK analyses were conducted in 32 patients with PN. The median initial administered doses on D1 were 9.53 mg in the QD group and 12.7 mg in the BID group. Following the initial dose on D1, systemic exposure was low in both cohorts. The geometric mean Cmax was higher in the BID group than in the QD group (1.14 vs. 0.513 ng/mL), whereas geometric mean area under the concentration–time curve (AUC0–t) values were similar (7.73 vs. 7.44 h·ng/mL, respectively) (Online Resource Table S2). After multiple doses, the drug concentration reached a steady state, with reduced peak-to-trough fluctuations compared to the first dose. The observed accumulation ratio is approximately 3–4 (Online Resource Figs. S2–3). Plasma concentration–time profiles on D29 demonstrated stable and sustained exposure at extremely low levels throughout the dosing interval (Online Resource Fig. S3).

Efficacy

During the double-blind period, HDM3010 showed a consistent trend toward greater improvement in pruritus compared with vehicle. Higher proportions of participants achieved a ≥ 4-point improvement from baseline in weekly average WI-NRS scores in the HDM3010 groups than in the vehicle groups at weeks 1, 2, and 4, with response rates increasing over time. Greater mean reductions from baseline in the WI-NRS, IGA PN-S, and IGA PN-A scores were also observed with HDM3010 relative to vehicle (Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4). Discussion

This study demonstrated that topical 1.5% ruxolitinib gel (HDM3010) was generally safe and well tolerated in adults with PN. The incidence of TEAEs was comparable between HDM3010- and vehicle-treated groups. PK analyses confirmed rapid absorption following initial administration; however, key systemic exposure parameters remained at relatively low levels in both cohorts. Across multiple efficacy endpoints, including improvement in WI-NRS scores and reductions in IGA PN-S and IGA PN-A scores, HDM3010 consistently demonstrated positive trends relative to vehicle, supporting its favorable safety, tolerability, and low systemic exposure, and providing preliminary evidence of clinical benefit.

Management of PN remains challenging. Conventional topical therapies (corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors) are limited by local adverse effects or systemic risks, and further constrained by a lack of high-level evidence supporting their efficacy in PN, restricting their utility. Although approved biologics (dupilumab, nemolizumab) represent advances, and systemic JAK inhibitors show potential efficacy, both carry risk linked to systemic exposure. In contrast, topical delivery of ruxolitinib gel enables localized drug exposure at lesional sites while mitigating systemic risks associated with oral JAK inhibition. This localized therapeutic approach is particularly relevant for PN, a chronic condition often requiring sustained treatment, in which cumulative systemic risk is a major clinical concern.

The safety profile observed with HDM3010 is consistent with that reported for other topical JAK inhibitors. TEAEs were comparable to vehicles and TRAEs were infrequent, typically mild to moderate in severity. These findings align with phase III TRuE-PN1 and TRuE-PN2 data for ruxolitinib cream in PN, which similarly demonstrated low rates of application-site reactions and no grade ≥ 3 TRAEs [21]. Comparable safety outcomes were reported in phase III trials in atopic dermatitis, in which ruxolitinib cream showed similar or lower adverse event rates compared with vehicle [22]. Collectively, these data support the favorable safety profile of topical JAK inhibition in inflammatory skin diseases [23]. Mechanistically, topical JAK1/2 inhibitors may disrupt the interferon-gamma (IFNγ)–CXCL10–CD8+ T cell trafficking axis within the lesional area, thereby achieving therapeutic effects while minimizing systemic immunosuppression, which may underlie the observed tolerability of ruxolitinib gel [16, 18, 24–27].

While the whole-blood JAK2 inhibition IC50 of orally administered ruxolitinib is 281 nM, HDM3010 exhibits rapid cutaneous absorption and minimal systemic exposure (steady-state mean peak concentration < 7 ng/mL, < 10% of the oral IC50)—a pharmacokinetic profile providing a mechanistic basis for minimizing risks typical of systemic JAK inhibition [28]. These findings are consistent with earlier studies in healthy volunteers demonstrating substantially lower systemic exposure with HDM3010 compared with the reference ruxolitinib cream (the geometric mean ratios (T/R) for Cmax, AUC 0–t, and AUC0–∞were 29.29%, 33.38%, and 34.33%, respectively).

Although limited by sample size and treatment duration, HDM3010 demonstrated efficacy trends across multiple clinically relevant endpoints that were consistent with phase III data for ruxolitinib cream. Pooled analyses from the TRuE-PN1 and TRuE-PN2 trials showed that topical 1.5% ruxolitinib cream rapidly and significantly improved pruritus and skin lesions, with sustained superiority over vehicle early in treatment. Improvements in itch and clinical signs were evident as early as week 2 and were maintained through week 24 [21]. In the present study, although statistical significance was not achieved, directional improvements in WI-NRS, IGA PN-S, and IGA PN-A scores paralleled these phase III findings. The numerically higher response rates observed in the QD group may reflect a greater proportion of patients with milder baseline disease; nevertheless, the BID group, which included a higher proportion of moderate-to-severe cases, demonstrated comparable improvement trends, suggesting potential efficacy across disease severities. Mechanistically, HDM3010 is postulated to attenuate JAK-dependent pruritus signaling in sensory neurons while suppressing cytokine-driven inflammation within lesional skin [18].

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. As a phase I/II study, the sample size in each cohort was relatively small and the treatment duration was limited to 8 weeks, which precludes definitive conclusions regarding the long-term safety and efficacy of HDM3010. In addition, the study population was predominantly composed of patients with moderate-to-severe disease (79.6%), which, although representative of a clinically refractory population, may limit generalizability to patients with milder PN. Future phase III randomized controlled trials with larger cohorts, longer treatment durations, and broader disease severity representation are warranted to confirm the long-term safety and efficacy of HDM3010.

Conclusions

In this phase I/II randomized clinical trial, HDM3010 demonstrated a favorable safety and tolerability profile, minimal systemic exposure, and consistent trends toward greater improvement in pruritus and skin lesion severity compared with vehicle in adults with PN. Collectively, these findings support the continued clinical development of HDM3010 as a novel topical therapeutic option for PN and provide a rationale for subsequent, larger-scale clinical trials to further establish its efficacy and long-term safety.

Exponents of 2 2 -multiarrangements and Wakefield–Yuzvinsky matrices
Shota Maehara

Abstract
. In the theory of hyperplane arrangements, M. Wakefield and S. Yuzvinsky utilized a square matrix in their research on the exponents of 2 2 -dimensional multiarrangements. Using such a matrix, they showed that the exponents of 2 2 -dimensional multiarrangements are as close as possible in general position for any fixed balanced multiplicity. In this article, we introduce a matrix similar to that of Wakefield and Yuzvinsky and explore its further application to the exponents. In fact, exponents of 2 2 -dimensional multiarrangements are calculated by verifying whether the corresponding matrices have full rank. As the main result, we introduce a new class of 2 2 -dimensional arrangements with an explicit sequence of multiplicities for which the exponents are closest. We also provide an alternative proof for some known results on exponents.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero,

V:=\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
,
V^{*}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(V,\mathbb{K})
and
S:=\operatorname{Sym}(V^{*})
.
Fixing a
\mathbb{K}
-linear basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}\}
of
V^{*}
,
we identify
S
with a polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
.
For the polynomial ring
S
,
we define a module
\operatorname{Der}S

of
\mathbb{K}
-linear derivations on
S
as a free
S
-module generated by

\left\{\partial_{x_{i}}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\right\}
,
where
\partial_{x_{i}}(x_{j})
is the Kronecker delta
\delta_{ij}

and every
\psi\in\operatorname{Der}S
satisfies

\psi(fg)=\psi(f)g+f\psi(g)
for all polynomials
f,g\in S
.
Defining a
hyperplane
as a vector subspace in
V
of codimension one,
we call a finite (non-empty)
collection of hyperplanes in
V=\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
an

\ell
-dimensional hyperplane arrangement
. Taking multiplicities into account, the concept of
hyperplane arrangements was generalized to that of multiarrangements by Ziegler
[
11
]
.
Precisely, a function
m
from a hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{A}
to
\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is called a
multiplicity
on this arrangement,
and the
multiarrangement

(\mathcal{A},m)
is defined
as the pair of
\mathcal{A}
and
m
.
When
\mathcal{A}
is an
\ell
-dimensional arrangement,
we sometimes call
(\mathcal{A},m)
an
\ell
-multiarrangement
for short.
We often express the multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
by its
defining polynomial

Q(\mathcal{A},m):=\prod_{H\in\mathcal{A}}\alpha_{H}^{m(H)}
, fixing each
\alpha_{H}\in V^{*}

that satisfies
H=\operatorname{Ker}(\alpha_{H})
.
For a multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
,
let us define the
size
of the multiplicity by
|m|:=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{A}}m(H)
.
Furthermore, when the multiplicity
m
returns
m(H)=1
for all
H\in\mathcal{A}
,
then we always regard
(\mathcal{A},m)
as just a hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{A}=(\mathcal{A},1)
.
In contrast to multiarrangements, hyperplane arrangements are often
called
simple
arrangements.
For a multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
, we define the corresponding

logarithmic derivation module

D(\mathcal{A},m)

as in
Definition

2.1
,
which is a graded
S
-submodule of
\operatorname{Der}S
.
If the module
D(\mathcal{A},m)
is free as an
S
-module,
the multiarrangement itself is called
free
.
When
D(\mathcal{A},m)
is free, additionally,
we define the
exponents

\exp(\mathcal{A},m)

as the multiset of degrees of its homogeneous basis.
In the theory of hyperplane arrangements,
the following conjecture by Terao is one of the most important topics
for decades.

Conjecture 1.1

(Terao’s Conjecture)
.

The freeness of simple arrangements depends only on their combinatorics.

It is well known that
2
-multiarrangements
are always free with the logarithmic derivation modules of rank
2

(see
[
11
]
).
Thus,
1.1
is true when
\ell=2
.
On the other hand,
3
-arrangements are not free in general;
for example, the simple arrangement defined by
the polynomial

xyz(x+y+z)
is not free.
Therefore, it is natural to study the freeness of

3
-arrangements as a next step.
Note that we usually use the coordinates
\{x,y,z\}
instead of
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}

when
\ell=3
, and we use

\{x,y\}
when
\ell=2
.
As one of the most important results, in
[
10
]
,
Yoshinaga gave a criterion to obtain the freeness of simple
3
-arrangements
by considering certain associated
2
-multiarrangements. According to Yoshinaga’s criterion,
the freeness of a
3
-arrangement
\mathcal{A}

is characterized by its combinatorics and the exponents
of a
2
-multiarrangement which is obtained as
the pair of
the restriction
\mathcal{A}^{H}

on any plane
H\in\mathcal{A}
and a certain multiplicity studied in Ziegler
[
11
]
.
Hence, it is so important to understand the exponents of
2
-multiarrangements
for approaching
1.1
in case
\ell=3
.

For a
2
-multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)

with
\exp(\mathcal{A},m)=(d_{1},d_{2})
, define
the
difference
of the exponents as
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m):=|d_{1}-d_{2}|
.
In
[
9
]
, Wakefield and Yuzvinsky solved that
the difference is at most one
for any balanced multiplicity
when its underlying arrangement is in the general position
as in
Corollary

3.9
.
However, it is still considered very difficult to completely classify the exponents.
Even when the underlying arrangement is a
Coxeter arrangement
,
which is defined as the set of all reflection hyperplanes of a Coxeter group
and whose freeness was proved by Saito in
[
6
]
,
the exponents are difficult to classify; such classification has been completed only for the type
A_{2}

by Wakamiko in
[
7
]
.
This is equivalent to
the case where its underlying arrangement
\mathcal{A}
consists of three lines:
|\mathcal{A}|=3
(see
Remark

2.6
).
On the other hand,
when we consider the case where
|\mathcal{A}|=4
,
even when
\mathcal{A}
is
the Coxeter arrangement of type
B_{2}
,
only a few parts of the classification of
\exp(\mathcal{A},m)

were solved
(
[
8
,
4
]
and also see
Remark

2.6
).
Although the rest of the classification is still considered to be
too difficult,
it might be natural to suggest the following conjecture,
based on some computer calculations.

Conjecture 1.2

(Coxeter
B_{2}
)
.

For a balanced multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)

where
Q(\mathcal{A},m)=x^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}(x-y)^{m_{3}}(x+y)^{m_{4}}

and
m_{i}\geq 3
,
define
n_{1}:=|m_{2}-m_{1}|
and
n_{2}:=|m_{4}-m_{3}|
.
Then, there exists a natural number
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\min\{n_{1},n_{2}\}\geq N
derives
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)\leq 1
.

When the underlying arrangement
\mathcal{A}
satisfies
|\mathcal{A}|\geq 5
,
the classification of exponents seems much harder.
However, considering certain matrices named WY-matrix in
Section

3
,
which were essentially introduced by Wakefield and Yuzvinsky in
[
9
]
,
we obtain a new result on exponents for arbitrary
|\mathcal{A}|\geq 4

as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(Main theorem)
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},m)
be a multiarrangement defined by

Q(\mathcal{A},m)=x^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}\prod_{i=3}^{n}(x-s_{i}y)^{m_{i}}

where
n:=|\mathcal{A}|\geq 4
,

s_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,-1\}

and any two of
s_{i}
are coprime. If
m_{1}+m_{2}>\frac{|m|}{2}
and
there exists a non-empty partition
I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}=\{3,4,5,\ldots,n\}
such that

\left(\sum_{i\in I_{1}\cup\{1\}}m_{i}\right)=\left(\sum_{i\in I_{2}\cup\{2\}}m_{i}\right)=\frac{|m|}{2}

and

1\notin\{s_{i}\mid i\in I_{2}\}
,
then we have
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)=0
.

Remark 1.4
.

The assumptions in
Theorem

1.3
imply that

m
is balanced and
|m|\in 2\mathbb{Z}
.
Moreover, when a multiplicity
m
satisfies the conditions,
the multiplicity

m+\delta_{H_{1}}+\delta_{H_{2}}
also
satisfies the conditions under the same choice of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}

(see
Remark

2.9
for the definition of
\delta_{H_{i}}
).
Especially, we obtain
an infinite sequence of multiplicities that satisfy
\Delta=0
.

Example 1.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},m)
be a multiarrangement
defined by the polynomial

Q(\mathcal{A},m)=x^{4}y^{8}(x-y)^{3}(x-3y)^{2}(x+5y)^{1}(x-2y)^{2}
.
Note that
(s_{3},s_{4},s_{5},s_{6})=(1,3,-5,2)
and any two of them are coprime.
Let
I_{1}=\{3,4,5\}
and
I_{2}=\{6\}
.
Then, since

m_{1}+m_{2}=12>10
and
m_{1}+m_{3}+m_{4}+m_{5}=m_{2}+m_{6}=10
,
we have
\exp(\mathcal{A},m)=(10,10)
by
Theorem

1.3
.

For a fixed underlying
2
-arrangement,
the analysis of the distribution
\Delta=0
is essential for the complete classification of exponents,
according to the theory of multiplicity lattice
by Abe and Numata
[
3
]
.
As a corollary to
Theorem

1.3
,
we also obtain the following result considering the case
n=4
,
s_{3}=1
,
s_{4}=2

and a change of coordinates in
Remark

2.8
.

Corollary 1.6

(Coxeter
B_{2}
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},m)
be a multiarrangement defined by
Q(\mathcal{A},m)=x^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}(x-y)^{m_{3}}(x+y)^{m_{4}}
.
If
|{m_{2}}-{m_{1}}|=|{m_{4}}-{m_{3}}|\geq 1
, then
we have
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)=0
.

Example 1.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},m)
be a multiarrangement defined by

Q(\mathcal{A},m)=x^{5}y^{9}(x-y)^{3}(x+y)^{7}
.
Since
|m_{2}-m_{1}|=|m_{4}-m_{3}|=4
,
we have
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)=0
, or
\exp(\mathcal{A},m)=(12,12)
.

In other words,
Corollary

1.6
gives a partial answer to
1.2
,
just in case
n_{1}=n_{2}
. Note that for the case
n_{1}=n_{2}=0
,
the exponents were classified by Wakamiko
[
8
]
.

As another application of WY-matrix, in
Corollary

3.10
,
we also construct a
2
-arrangement
\mathcal{A}

which does not admit any balanced multiplicity
m
satisfying
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)=|\mathcal{A}|-2
,
except for the simple case
m\equiv 1
(cf.
Theorem

2.5
).

Corollary

3.10
is the first result that gives an arrangement equipped with such a property.

The rest of this article is composed as follows.
In
Section

2
, we prepare some basic results
about arrangement theory.
In
Section

3
, we define the WY-matrix,
and also introduce some notation around the matrix.
Finally, in
Section

4
, we show a proof of our main theorem.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we prepare some basic results that are
necessary for our results.
First, we recall the definition of the logarithmic derivation module
as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

For an
\ell
-multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
, we define the

logarithmic derivation module
D(\mathcal{A},m)
,
a submodule of
\operatorname{Der}S
, by

\{\theta\in\operatorname{Der}S\mid\theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{m(H)}S\text{ for all }H\in\mathcal{A}\}
.
Note that
D(\mathcal{A},m)
is a graded
S
-module

D(\mathcal{A},m)=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}D(\mathcal{A},m)_{d}
.
When
D(\mathcal{A},m)
is generated by homogeneous derivations

\{\theta_{i}\in D(\mathcal{A},m)\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\}

that are linearly independent over
S
, we call

(\mathcal{A},m)

free
with
exponents

\exp(\mathcal{A},m):=(\deg(\theta_{1}),\deg(\theta_{2}),\ldots,\deg(\theta_{\ell}))
.

The following result is useful to check the freeness of multiarrangements
as long as we can obtain a candidate of a basis for the logarithmic derivation module.

Theorem 2.2

(Saito’s criterion,
[
6
,
11
]
)
.

For an
\ell
-multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)

and a set of homogeneous derivations

\{\theta_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\}\subset D(\mathcal{A},m)
,
this set of derivations forms a basis for
D(\mathcal{A},m)
if and only if

\det((\theta_{i}(x_{j}))_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell})=c\cdot Q(\mathcal{A},m)

for some
c\in\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}
.
In particular, it follows that

\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\deg(\theta_{i})=|m|=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{A}}m(H)

for a free multiarrangement and its homogeneous basis.

Throughout the rest of this article,
we assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a
2
-arrangement unless otherwise specified.
Then, we especially use
Theorem

2.2
in the statement that
\deg(\theta_{1})+\deg(\theta_{2})=|m|
with a homogeneous basis
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}

for
D(\mathcal{A},m)
.
When the multiplicity
m
has a special condition called unbalanced,
Wakefield and Yuzvinsky determined the exponents as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

When a multiplicity
m
on a
2
-arrangement
\mathcal{A}
satisfies

m(H)<|m|/2
for all
H\in\mathcal{A}
,
we call the multiplicity (and the multiarrangement itself)
balanced
.
Otherwise,
we call them
unbalanced
.

Theorem 2.4

(Case 2.1,
[
9
]
)
.

If a
2
-dimensional multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
is unbalanced
with
m(H)\geq|m|/2
for some
H\in\mathcal{A}
,
then we have
\exp(\mathcal{A},m)=(m(H),|m|-m(H))
.

On the other hand, to classify the exponents for
balanced multiarrangements
is much more difficult.
As one of the most important results, in
[
2
]
,
Abe showed an upper bound for the difference of the exponents
\Delta
as follows.

Theorem 2.5

(Theorem 1.6,
[
2
]
)
.

For a
2
-dimensional balanced multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)

with
\exp(\mathcal{A},m)=(d_{1},d_{2})
,
we have
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)=|d_{1}-d_{2}|\leq|\mathcal{A}|-2
.

Remark 2.6
.

Theorem

2.5
includes the following result,
which was solved for the first time by Wakamiko in
[
7
]
:
For a balanced multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
defined by
x^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}(x-y)^{m_{3}}
,
we have
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)\leq 1
. Similarly, for a balanced multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
satisfying
|\mathcal{A}|=4
,

Theorem

2.5
also gives
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)\in\{0,1,2\}
.
Combined with
Theorem

2.2
, it quickly turns out that

\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)=1
if and only if
|m|\in 2\mathbb{Z}+1
,
while it is very difficult to classify the exponents when
|m|\in 2\mathbb{Z}
.
As a challenge to this problem, in
[
4
]
,
the author and Numata proved a conjecture
which had been given for sufficiently large multiplicities by Abe in
[
1
]
.
We obtain another proof for both of these facts
in
Corollaries

3.30
and
3.29
,
considering the determinants of certain matrices introduced in

Section

3
.

When we consider the constant one multiplicity
m\equiv 1
,
it is well-known that
\exp(\mathcal{A},m)=\exp(\mathcal{A})=(1,|\mathcal{A}|-1)
since
there always exists the
Euler derivation

\theta_{E}:=x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}\in D(\mathcal{A})

(for example, see
[
5
]
).
Hence, simple arrangements admit the upper bound,

\Delta(\mathcal{A})=|\mathcal{A}|-2
.
Note that
\theta_{0}:=\frac{Q(\mathcal{A},m)}{Q(\mathcal{A})}\cdot\theta_{E}\in D(\mathcal{A},m)

is the minimal degree derivation of the form
f\cdot\theta_{E}

with
f\in S\setminus\{0\}
.
This derivation
\theta_{0}
is (one of) the lower basis for
D(\mathcal{A},m)

if and only if
|m|\leq 2|\mathcal{A}|-2
as in
Theorem

2.7
;
this type of
2
-multiarrangements
(\mathcal{A},m)
satisfy

\Delta\leq|\mathcal{A}|-3
, except for the case

m\equiv 1
.
The Euler derivation
\theta_{E}
and the constant multiplicity
m\equiv 1
can be considered special in this sense.

Theorem 2.7

(Case 2.2,
[
9
]
)
.

If a
2
-multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
satisfies

|m|\leq 2|\mathcal{A}|-2
,
we have
\exp(\mathcal{A},m)=(|m|-|\mathcal{A}|+1,|\mathcal{A}|-1)
.

Remark 2.8
.

When we study the exponents of multiarrangements,
it is sometimes useful to consider some coordinate changes; multiarrangements which are transformed to each other
by an invertible matrix have the same exponents.
For example, define a matrix
T
as follows.

T:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right),\ T^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-1\\
0&1\end{array}\right).

Then, considering the transformation
\begin{pmatrix}X&Y\end{pmatrix}^{\top}=T\begin{pmatrix}x&y\end{pmatrix}^{\top}
,
the multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
defined by
Q(\mathcal{A},m)=x^{a}y^{b}(x-y)^{c}(x+y)^{d}

has the same exponents as
the multiarrangement
T(\mathcal{A},m)
, defined by
Q(T(\mathcal{A},m)):=(X-Y)^{a}Y^{b}(X-2Y)^{c}X^{d}
.
We introduce an equivalence relation on the set of multiarrangements
by such transformations as
(\mathcal{A},m)\sim T(\mathcal{A},m)
.
More precisely,
for an arbitrary
\theta=f(x,y)\partial_{x}+g(x,y)\partial_{y}\in D(\mathcal{A},m)
,
it follows that

F(X,Y)\partial_{X}+G(X,Y)\partial_{Y}:=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{X}&\partial_{Y}\end{pmatrix}T^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}f(x,y)&g(x,y)\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\in D(T(\mathcal{A},m)).

Remark 2.9
.

For a
2
-arrangement
\mathcal{A}
and each
H\in\mathcal{A}
,
let us define the multiplicity
\delta_{H}
on
\mathcal{A}
as follows.

\displaystyle\delta_{H}(H^{\prime}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&(H^{\prime}=H)\\
0&(H^{\prime}\neq H)\end{array}\right.

Then, we have
|\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)-\Delta(\mathcal{A},m+\delta_{H})|=1

for any multiarrangement. In particular, if a multiplicity
m
satisfies
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m)=0
,
it follows that
\Delta(\mathcal{A},m\pm\delta_{H})=1
for arbitrary
H\in\mathcal{A}
.
See
[
3
]
for further behaviors of
\Delta
and
\delta_{H}
.

3.
Introduction to WY-matrix

This section consists of five subsections.
Subsection
3.1
is to introduce WY-matrix
and also its original use in
[
9
]
.
In Subsection
3.2
, we fix some notation about tuples
that are repeatedly used afterwards.
Subsections
3.3
and
3.4
,
which are arranged to be mostly symmetric to each other,
provide an alternative formulation of the WY-matrix.
In Subsection
3.5
, we show some applications of WY-matrix.
The latter half of Subsection
3.5

is not necessary for

Section

4
.

3.1.
What is WY-matrix

For a balanced
2
-multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
and any
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
we define the
Wakefield–Yuzvinsky matrix
, or
WY-matrix
for short,

M_{WY}(\mathcal{A},m;e)
in the research on the exponents.
Since such a matrix was studied in
[
9
]
,
we name it after the initials of the authors.
Let us assume that
(\mathcal{A},m)
is defined by the defining polynomial

Q(\mathcal{A},m)=x^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}\prod_{i=3}^{n}(x-s_{i}y)^{m_{i}}
.
Then, the construction of WY-matrix gives us a matrix method for
the existence (or non-existence) of

\theta\in D(\mathcal{A},m)\setminus\{0\}
of degree
e
by the following four steps:

(Step 1)

Assume that there exists a homogeneous derivation

\theta=x^{m_{1}}f(x,y)\partial_{x}-y^{m_{2}}g(x,y)\partial_{y}\in D(\mathcal{A},m)_{e}
, where

f(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{e-m_{1}}f_{j}x^{e-m_{1}-j}y^{j}\ \ \text{and}\ \ g(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{e-m_{2}}g_{j}x^{e-m_{2}-j}y^{j}.

(Step 2)

Recall that
\theta(x-s_{i}y)\in(x-s_{i}y)^{m_{i}}S
for
3\leq i\leq n
.

(Step 3)

For each
i
,
the condition in (Step 2) can be expressed as

(\frac{\partial}{\partial x})^{k}(\theta(x-s_{i}y))|_{(x,y)=(s_{i},1)}=0

for
0\leq k\leq m_{i}-1
.

(Step 4)

As the matrix of the coefficients for
\{f_{j}\}
and
\{g_{j}\}

in (Step 3),
we obtain a system of equations

M(f_{0}\ f_{1}\ \cdots\ f_{e-m_{1}}\ g_{0}\ g_{1}\ \cdots\ g_{e-m_{2}})^{\top}=O
, where
the matrix
M
has
(\sum_{i=3}^{n}m_{i})
-rows and

((e-m_{1}+1)+(e-m_{2}+1))
-columns.
Call the left
(e-m_{1}+1)
-columns
f
-part and
the right
(e-m_{2}+1)
-columns
g
-part.

Under this notation, we have the following.

Definition 3.1
.

For a balanced
2
-multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
and arbitrary
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
we call
M_{WY}(\mathcal{A},m;e):=M
,
the matrix constructed through the previous four steps,

WY-matrix
. Moreover,
when we consider the case
e=\frac{|m|}{2}-1
for a multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
of
|m|\in 2\mathbb{Z}
,
let
M_{WY}(\mathcal{A},m)
simply denote
M_{WY}(\mathcal{A},m;\frac{|m|}{2}-1)

in the rest of this article (see
Remark

3.5
for the reason).

Theorem 3.2
.

For a balanced
2
-multiarrangement
(\mathcal{A},m)
,
there exists a nonzero derivation

\theta\in D(\mathcal{A},m)
of degree
e

if and only if the WY-matrix

M_{WY}(\mathcal{A},m;e)
does not have full rank.

Remark 3.3
.

Explicitly writing, the equations in (Step 3) above are

•

•

For nonnegative integers

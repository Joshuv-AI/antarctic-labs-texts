Protocol for preparing murine fecal microbiota transplant and fecal microbiota metabolites for in vivo and in vitro use
Authors: Catherine M. Phelps, Daphne M. Rodriguez J, Marlies Meisel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryAccumulating evidence demonstrates that microbiota-derived metabolites can modulate host immune responses and influence complex pathologies. Despite their therapeutic potential, the isolation of fecal microbial metabolites and their scalable ex vivo production remain challenging. Here, we present a protocol for preparing murine fecal microbiota transplant (FMT) and fecal microbiota metabolites for in vivo and in vitro use. We describe steps for collecting and preparing donor feces, isolating fecal microbial metabolites, and producing fecal microbial metabolites ex vivo with a broth expansion culture.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Phelps et al.1

Before you begin

This protocol includes steps to collect murine feces and prepare three materials for downstream in vivo and in vitro applications: (1) fecal microbiota transplant (FMT) inoculum, (2) direct fecal metabolite extracts, and (3) microbiota-derived metabolite supernatants generated by anaerobic broth expansion. This protocol builds on our recent approach,1 with adaptations from high-throughput cultivation and tissue-culture-omics workflows developed by our group2,3,4 and others.5,6 We use the anaerobic broth-expansion method to produce metabolite-rich supernatants while preserving bacterial diversity. We select the indicated broths based on their ability to support the major mammalian gut phyla (Figure 1) and maintain all cultures under strict anaerobic conditions because many gut commensals are obligate anaerobes.Figure 1Broth media and supported phylaWe use the indicated broth media for fecal bacterial culture to produce cultured microbiota metabolites. Innovation

Microbiota-derived metabolites are key modulators of host physiology across complex diseases, including cancer1,2,3,7 and autoimmunity.4 Yet causal tests are limited by low metabolite yields, loss of strict-anaerobe viability, and workflows that fail to separate microbial cells from their secreted products. Here, we present an integrated, scalable, metabolite-centric workflow designed to run alongside standard fecal microbiome transplant (FMT) procedures that generates key materials that can be used to attribute observed effects specifically to secreted microbial factors. The protocol yields three complementary outputs—fecal homogenate for FMT, direct fecal metabolite extracts and culture-amplified microbiota-derived supernatants—generated under strictly anaerobic, pre-reduced conditions in media that support major gut phyla, thereby boosting metabolite yield for both in vitro assays and in vivo dosing. Standardized donor feces collection, refined bacterial media selection, and rigorous quality controls enhance reproducibility, cross-study comparability, and facilitate diverse downstream analyses, including mass spectrometry. The workflow is compatible with mouse and human donor material, as demonstrated in our recent study,1 enabling translational validation. Collectively, these innovations transform fecal material into experiment-ready, cell-free metabolite preparations, enabling causal tests of how microbiota-derived products shape local and systemic immunity and disease pathogenesis.

Expected outcomes

Successful execution of this protocol yields three experiment-ready materials—each with predictable appearance, yield, and stability—for functional interrogation of the microbiota and its secreted metabolites: (1) FMT solution, which contains fecal bacteria and soluble metabolites, ideal for evaluating the impact of the microbiome in vivo; (2) direct fecal microbial metabolites solution, which contains soluble fecal metabolites directly isolated from the feces and is ideal for evaluating impact of the fecal microbial metabolome in small-scale applications (i.e., in vitro assays); and (3) cultured microbial metabolites solution, which contains metabolites produced by diverse commensal bacterial phyla grown in vitro and is ideal for studies requiring large quantities (i.e., in vivo). The yield of each material is customizable and can be scaled based on the amount needed for continued analysis. For results derived from this protocol, please refer to our recent study.1

Limitations

Despite broad coverage, this protocol has inherent constraints. Low-abundance or highly oxygen-sensitive taxa may be underrepresented in the FMT solution due to handling, washing, and filtration steps. Direct metabolite extracts prioritize soluble, filter-passing compounds; as a result, metabolites bound to particulates or vesicles, hydrophobic species, or large complexes may be partially lost. Additionally, some host-derived metabolites inevitably co-isolate from stool. Culture-derived supernatants are biased toward culturable organisms and media-favored pathways. In addition, some taxa are fastidious and may not expand ex vivo.11 Finally, the composition and yield of materials produced through this protocol are sensitive to donor-specific variables, including diet, timing of feces collection, and the anaerobic metabolic pathways engaged by their commensal microbiota. If repeated collection of donor feces is required for downstream experiments, it is important to minimize deviations in these factors (diet, timing of collection), as changes can introduce batch effects and alter yields or metabolic profiles. In downstream applications, users should interpret negative results with caution, corroborate findings with orthogonal measurements (e.g., targeted mass spectroscopy, 16S/NGS on fecal pellets), and, when possible, validate across both direct and culture-derived fecal microbial metabolite inputs.

Potential solution

Minimal Systemic Exposure with Maximal Delgocitinib Cream Use in Patients with Severe Chronic Hand Eczema in the DELTA 2 Randomized, Vehicle-Controlled, Double-Blind, Phase 3 Trial
Authors: Melinda Gooderham, Christopher G. Bunick, JiaDe Yu, Douglas Maslin, Shannon K. R. Schneider, Marie Louise Østerdal, Anders Søhoel, Daniel Elenius Madsen, Lawrence F. Eichenfield
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionDelgocitinib cream is a topical pan-Janus kinase inhibitor approved for adults with moderate to severe Chronic Hand Eczema (CHE) in the USA, Canada, European Union, and multiple other countries. Here, we assessed the systemic exposure of twice-daily topical application of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g in adults with moderate to severe CHE treated under normal or maximal use conditions in the DELTA 2 trial and compared with the systemic exposure in adults with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis (AD) from a delgocitinib cream phase 1 AD trial.MethodsBlood sampling was performed 2–6 h after delgocitinib cream application at weeks 1, 4, and 16 in DELTA 2. In the delgocitinib cream phase 1 trial, blood sampling was performed prior to application and at six timepoints (0–12 h) post-application on day 1 and day 8. Geometric mean delgocitinib plasma concentrations were calculated in DELTA 2 and peak delgocitinib plasma concentrations (geometric mean Cmax) were calculated in the phase 1 trial.ResultsIn DELTA 2, patients treated with delgocitinib cream under normal (n = 294; body surface area [BSA], 1.48%; application, 7.3 g/week) or maximal use conditions (n = 19; BSA, 2.17%; application, 13.7 g/week) showed geometric mean delgocitinib plasma concentrations at week 1 of 0.19 ng/mL (n = 268) and 0.58ng/mL, respectively. In the phase 1 trial, patients (n = 14; BSA, 33.6%; application, 72.3 g/week) showed a geometric mean Cmax of 1.20 ng/mL (n = 13) on day 8.ConclusionDelgocitinib cream 20 mg/g resulted in minimal systemic exposure among adults with moderate to severe CHE, including those treated under maximal use conditions. Low systemic exposure was also observed in adults with moderate to severe AD, despite substantially greater BSA involvement and higher delgocitinib cream usage, highlighting the properties of delgocitinib cream’s novel formulation.Trial RegistrationNCT03826901 (phase 1, delgocitinib cream); study start date: 20 February 2019; primary completion date: 29 October 2021; study completion date: 29 October 2021. Introduction

Chronic Hand Eczema (CHE) is an inflammatory skin disease defined as hand eczema that persists for longer than 3 months or recurs at least twice within 1 year [1]. The key symptoms of CHE are pain and pruritus, with the clinical signs including erythema, scaling, edema, lichenification, hyperkeratosis, vesicles, and/or fissures [1]. Topical corticosteroids (TCS) are typically utilized as the short-term, first-line treatment in patients with CHE. However, TCS use has shown limited efficacy with prolonged use (even when used intermittently) and has been associated with adverse events (AEs), such as dermal atrophy and tachyphylaxis [1, 2].

Delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g is a topical pan-Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor approved in the USA, Canada, European Union, and multiple other countries for the treatment of moderate to severe CHE in adults for whom TCS are inadequate or inappropriate [3–6]. In the DELTA 1 and DELTA 2 phase 3 trials, treatment with twice-daily delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g was associated with both significant improvement in all efficacy endpoints versus cream vehicle and a favorable safety profile similar to that of cream vehicle in adults with moderate to severe CHE [7]. In the head-to-head DELTA FORCE phase 3 trial, twice-daily delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g demonstrated superior efficacy and a more favorable safety profile compared with oral alitretinoin for up to 24 weeks in adults with severe CHE [8].

Systemic exposure and safety are central considerations when evaluating topical therapies for dermatological diseases. In 2021, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) issued a class-wide Boxed Warning for JAK inhibitors indicated for the treatment of inflammatory disorders based on data in patients with rheumatoid arthritis treated with oral tofacitinib and concomitant methotrexate who showed an increased risk for serious AEs [9, 10]. This Boxed Warning label is included with the FDA-approved oral (i.e., abrocitinib, upadacitinib) and topical (i.e., ruxolitinib cream) JAK inhibitors for the treatment of atopic dermatitis (AD) [11–13]. Delgocitinib cream, which was approved by the FDA without this Boxed Warning label, was formulated with minimal penetration-enhancing excipients, and without the addition of propylene glycol (PG) and polyethylene glycol (PEG), to limit systemic exposure [14].

Here, we examined the systemic exposure of delgocitinib cream in adults with severe CHE who applied the highest amounts of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g during the DELTA 2 phase 3 trial. We also conducted a post hoc comparison of systemic exposure data between the DELTA 2 trial and a phase 1 AD trial of delgocitinib cream in adults with moderate to severe AD who had substantially greater body surface area (BSA) involvement and higher delgocitinib cream usage.

Methods

To assess systemic exposure of delgocitinib cream, these analyses examined data from adults with moderate to severe CHE in the DELTA 2 randomized, vehicle-controlled, double-blind, phase 3 trial, including adults with severe CHE (defined as an Investigator Global Assessment of Chronic Hand Eczema [IGA-CHE] score of 4) at baseline who were treated under maximal use conditions. Maximal use conditions were defined as being in the upper quartile of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g use (≥ 10.9 g/week) among patients with severe CHE.

The trial design of DELTA 2 (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT04872101) has been previously described in detail [7]. Briefly, DELTA 2 was a randomized, double-blind, and vehicle-controlled phase 3 clinical trial in which adults (aged ≥ 18 years) with moderate to severe CHE were randomized 2:1 to twice-daily delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g or cream vehicle for 16 weeks followed by either a 2-week safety follow-up or transfer to a 36-week extension trial. Blood sampling was performed 2–6 h after delgocitinib cream application at weeks 1, 4, and 16.

Systemic exposure of delgocitinib cream in DELTA 2 was compared with that observed in the phase 1 AD trial (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT03826901) conducted in adults with moderate to severe AD (25–50% BSA involvement at screening and baseline) who were treated with twice-daily delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g for 8 weeks. Blood sampling was performed on day 1 (i.e., baseline) and day 8 (i.e., after 1 week of treatment) prior to application and at six timepoints post-application (i.e., 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, and 12 h).

Blood samples were analyzed using a liquid chromatography/mass spectrometry-based method where the lower limit of quantitation was 5 pg/mL. In DELTA 2, geometric mean delgocitinib plasma concentrations were calculated, whereas peak delgocitinib plasma concentrations (geometric mean Cmax) were calculated for the phase 1 AD trial. Prior work has shown that delgocitinib cream maintains a relatively flat pharmacokinetic (PK) profile at steady state [15]. As such, although DELTA 2 values do not represent true Cmax, collection of a single sample between 2 and 6 h post-application was considered to be sufficient to adequately characterize delgocitinib plasma concentration.

Trial protocols were approved by all relevant ethics committees and institutional review boards (Electronic Supplementary Material [ESM] Table S2; [7]). The trials were conducted according to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council on Harmonization Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice and conformed with all relevant country-specific laws and regulations. All patients, or their legal representatives, provided written informed consent. In DELTA 2, patients at selected trial sites had the additional option to participate in a photography component to capture disease status over time; participating patients were required to provide additional informed consent, including consent for their photographs to be published for scientific and/or commercial purposes.

Discussion

These analyses showed that twice-daily treatment with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g resulted in minimal systemic exposure among adults with moderate to severe CHE, including those treated under maximal use conditions. Low systemic exposure was observed in adults with moderate to severe AD who had substantially greater BSA involvement and delgocitinib cream application. Delgocitinib systemic exposure in adults with severe CHE treated under maximal use conditions was approximately 12 times lower than that observed for the lowest tested oral delgocitinib dose (1.5 mg) in healthy adults (geometric mean Cmax = 7.2 ng/mL) and 40 times lower when compared with whole blood IC50 of delgocitinib from an in vitro interleukin-4 release assay (24 ng/mL) [15]. Among patients with moderate to severe AD, delgocitinib systemic exposure was approximately 6 to 20 times lower than these respective reference values.

Systemic exposure concentration thresholds are not yet defined in the peer-reviewed literature or formal guidance documents; however, subnanomolar systemic exposure levels under maximal use conditions are generally considered to be negligible in regulatory practice. Given the increased risk of systemic absorption in damaged skin, topical treatments for CHE should be optimized to maintain efficacy while minimizing absorption into the bloodstream for safety. However, topical treatments for dermatological diseases are typically formulated with certain ingredients to maximize drug delivery [16]; for example, PG is a common skin penetration enhancer present in nearly two-thirds of formulations of TCS [16–18] as well as in ruxolitinib cream [11]. PEG is also commonly found in formulations of topical treatments and may indirectly enhance drug delivery by maintaining drug solubility, prolonging skin contact, and/or altering the solvent evaporation profile [19–21]. Delgocitinib cream was developed with minimal penetration-enhancing excipients, and without the addition of PG and PEG, which likely contributed to its observed minimal systemic exposure [15]. Long-term evaluation of adults with moderate to severe CHE treated for up to 52 weeks found that twice-daily delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g was well tolerated with no major safety concerns [22]. Additionally, while CHE typically involves lower BSA than other dermatological diseases (e.g., AD, psoriasis), low delgocitinib systemic exposure was also observed in adults with moderate to severe AD who had approximately 22 times the mean baseline BSA involvement compared with adults with moderate to severe CHE in DELTA 2 and applied nearly tenfold more delgocitinib cream. Taken together, systemic pharmacological effects are unlikely to be observed with topical applications of delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g in patients with moderate to severe CHE.

A limitation of these analyses was the relatively low number of patients treated under maximal use conditions in the DELTA 2 trial. Additionally, differences in the blood sampling approach between DELTA 2 (single timepoint 2–6 h post-application at weeks 1, 4, and 16) and the phase 1 AD trial (sampling prior to application and at six timepoints post-application at baseline and week 1) resulted in comparison of geometric mean delgocitinib plasma concentrations in DELTA 2 with peak delgocitinib plasma concentrations (geometric mean Cmax) in the phase 1 trial and only at week 1 of the treatment period.

Conclusion

Overall, twice-daily treatment with delgocitinib cream 20 mg/g was associated with minimal systemic exposure in adults with moderate to severe CHE, including in patients who were treated under maximal use conditions. Low systemic exposure was observed in adults with moderate to severe AD, despite substantially greater BSA involvement and higher delgocitinib cream usage, highlighting the novel formulation of delgocitinib cream.

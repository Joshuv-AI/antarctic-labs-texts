ZNF750 suppresses esophageal squamous cell carcinoma by maintaining epithelial barrier–driven mucosal immune homeostasis
Authors: Tong Zhou, Jingyue Fan, Mengyao Wang, Caixia Cheng, Fajia Yuan, Junli Li, Huili Wan, Dongdong Cui, Yaru Miao, Yanghui Bi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRecent studies have shown that zinc finger protein 750 (ZNF750), a key tumor suppressor in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), is associated with defective epithelial differentiation. At the same time, mucosal immune dysfunction is increasingly recognized as a key factor in the development of ESCC. Exploring the role of ZNF750 in regulating the mucosal immune microenvironment provides a new perspective on its tumor suppression mechanism.ResultsBy integrating analyses across multiple platforms, including bulk transcriptomics (TCGA, GTEx, GEO), and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), we demonstrate that ZNF750 is significantly downregulated in ESCC, with high expression serving as an independent prognostic marker for longer survival. Mechanistically, ZNF750 suppresses ESCC progression by maintaining epithelial barrier–driven mucosal immune homeostasis. WGCNA analyses indicate that ZNF750-high tumors are enriched in mucosal immune response and antimicrobial defense pathways. Experimental validation reveals that ZNF750 positively regulates DEFB4A (human beta-defensin-2), a key antimicrobial peptide. In addition, ZNF750 fosters an anti-tumor immune environment by recruiting neutrophils and suppressing pro-tumor M2 macrophages, a finding corroborated by clinical validation showing that high ZNF750 expression correlates with elevated neutrophil infiltration. Clinical validation confirms that high ZNF750 expression correlates with elevated neutrophil infiltration. Additionally, ZNF750 expression is linked to a healthy mucosal microbiota and the exclusion of pro-carcinogenic bacteria. Single-cell analysis shows that ZNF750-expressing epithelial cells maintain barrier integrity and coordinate immune surveillance through MHC-I/II signaling. Loss of ZNF750 may drive mucosal immune dysregulation, where the immune network is replaced by a pre-fibrotic immunosuppressive niche dominated by cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs).ConclusionsCollectively, our findings identify ZNF750 as a key molecular link between epithelial barrier integrity and mucosal immune surveillance in ESCC, highlighting restoration of ZNF750-mediated barrier immunity as a potential therapeutic strategy for ESCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13062-026-00787-3.

Background

Esophageal cancer is one of the most aggressive malignancies worldwide. Histologically, esophageal tumors are classified into two main subtypes: ESCC and esophageal adenocarcinoma (ESCA). Notably, China has one of the highest incidence rates of squamous esophageal cancer in the world, with 70% of ESCC occurring in China [1, 2]. According to 2022 epidemiological data, esophageal cancer ranked as the seventh most common cancer and the fifth leading cause of cancer-related mortality in China [3]. In current clinical practice, surgical resection, in conjunction with radiotherapy and chemotherapy, remains the prevailing treatment modality for esophageal cancer. Recently, immunotherapy has become a treatment method that can improve the survival rate of some patients with ESCC, while, developing effective therapeutic targets and personalized treatment strategies remains crucial for most ESCC cases [4, 5].

ZNF750, a C₂H₂-type zinc finger transcription factor, serves as a master regulator of epidermal differentiation. Accumulating evidence indicates tumor-suppressive functions of ZNF750 across multiple malignancies, with characteristic downregulation observed in the prostate, colorectal, and breast carcinomas [6–8]. Our prior investigations specifically identified marked ZNF750 downregulation in ESCC tissues compared to matched normal epithelia. Functional studies demonstrated that ZNF750 silencing enhanced ESCC cell migration and invasion, whereas its overexpression significantly attenuated these oncogenic phenotypes [9]. Importantly, several studies have established ZNF750 as an independent prognostic biomarker for ESCC [10, 11].

Mucosal immunity constitutes a fundamental defense mechanism of the host immune system, providing frontline protection against pathogenic invasion. Emerging evidence has established its pivotal roles in orchestrating anti-infective responses, anti-tumor immunity, and counteracting immune evasion mechanisms [12, 13]. Neutrophils, representing > 70% of circulating leukocytes, are key effector cells in mucosal immunity [14]. Recent mechanistic studies have elucidated their dual anti-tumor capacities: direct cytocidal activity through reactive oxygen species (ROS) and nitric oxide (NO) production, and indirect tumor suppression via neutrophil extracellular trap (NET)-mediated cytotoxicity [15]. Notably, mucosal immune dysregulation can induce microbial imbalance, fostering a tumor-permissive niche that accelerates oncogenesis and disease progression [16, 17]. These findings underscore the imperative to investigate mucosal immunity dynamics in ESCC pathogenesis, particularly its interplay with core oncogenic drivers.

This study aimed to investigate the tumor-suppressive mechanism of ZNF750 in ESCC. Our results indicate that ZNF750 is lowly expressed in tumor tissues and low expression of it closely associated with poor prognosis, suggesting that ZNF750 plays an anti-cancer role in ESCC. Immune cell infiltration and enrichment analyses revealed that ZNF750 may modulate mucosal immunity by recruiting specific immune cells, such as neutrophils. Beyond its intrinsic anti-cancer properties, we provide evidence that ZNF750 orchestrates the esophageal mucosal microenvironment by modulating musical immune related pathways and immune cell infiltration. Furthermore, by leveraging single-cell transcriptomics, we reveal a fundamental transition from a ZNF750-driven homeostatic mucosal immune niche in paratumor tissues to a dysregulated, pro-fibrotic microenvironment in ESCC. Collectively, this study identifies ZNF750 as a pivotal “mucosal guardian” that integrates epithelial integrity with immune-microbial surveillance, offering a novel perspective on the breakdown of mucosal immunity during esophageal carcinogenesis.

Materials and methods

ESCC multi-omics data collection

RNA sequencing data for patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) and other tumors were obtained from the UCSC Xena platform and reprocessed using Toil RNA-seq, eliminating the need for additional batch effect correction (Access Time: October 2024; https://xenabrowser.net/datapages/), including RSEM (RNA-seq by expectation-maximization) norm_count, RSEM TPM (transcripts per million) values, and curated phenotypes from TCGA, TARGET, and GTEx cohorts. Overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression Free Interval (PFI) data were obtained from the UCSC Xena platform, which integrates clinical endpoint data for 33 types of cancer.

Expression data for ZNF750 in normal tissues were downloaded from the Human Protein Atlas website (HPA; Access Time: July 2025; https://www.proteinatlas.org/). Expression data for ZNF750 in tumor cell lines were downloaded from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE; Access Time: October 2024; https://sites.broadinstitute.org/ccle/) website. For initial exploration of ZNF750 expression in the tumor microenvironment, scRNA-seq data of ESCA tumor samples were retrieved from the Tumor Immune Single Cell Hub (TISCH; accessed June 2025; http://tisch.compbio.cn/home/) under accession numbers GSE173950 and GSE160269. For the main analysis in this study, particularly to compare ZNF750 expression between normal and tumor tissues, the single-cell RNA-seq dataset with accession number GSE196756 was obtained from the GEO database. This dataset includes three samples each of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) tumor tissues and their matched tumor-adjacent normal tissues.

Pan-cancer analysis was conducted to characterize ZNF750 mRNA expression patterns, with expression values transformed using log2(TPM + 0.001) normalization. For esophageal cancer characterization, the TCGA dataset was refined by retaining ESCC specimens with paired normal adjacent tissues, excluding other histological subtypes. GTEx normal controls comprised esophageal mucosal tissues, yielding a final cohort of 376 well-characterized samples.

The sequencing dataset GSE53624 (Access Time: October 2024; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE53624) containing complete survival information was selected from the GEO database as the validation set, which includes gene expression profiles of 119 ESCC tumors and their matched normal tissues. Corresponding clinical and omics data were extracted and processed using the GEOquery R package (v1.30.0). Standardized annotation pipelines were implemented following established methodologies [18], enabling survival analysis based on ZNF750 expression profiles.

Differential expression and enrichment analysis

RNA-seq raw counts and associated phenotypes were acquired from the UCSC Xena platform (GDC TCGA Esophageal Cancer [ESCA] project). Following histological subtype filtering, 96 ESCC tumors were retained. Tumor samples were dichotomized into high/low ZNF750 expression subgroups using the median expression threshold.

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified via DESeq2 (v1.44.0) following standard workflow (removal of genes with zero expression across all samples, median-of-ratios normalization, and dispersion estimation). DEGs were defined by stringent thresholds: p.adjust < 0.05 and absolute log2-fold change ≥ 1. DEGs were visualized via volcano plots (ggplot2 v3.5.1).

Gene Ontology (GO) analysis was performed with clusterProfiler (v4.12.6) using the enrichGO function. Human genome annotation (GRCh38.p13) served as a reference for gene symbol conversion. The top 10 significant terms from each GO category (Biological Process, Cellular Component, Molecular Function) were selected based on count ranking.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using R software (v4.4.1) [19]. c5.go. v2025.1 was used as a reference, and 149 is set as a random seed. The gene list sorted by log2FoldChange output from DESeq2 was used to perform 10,000 permutation tests, with FDR < 0.25 set as the significant enrichment threshold.

Immune cell infiltration analysis

To assess the tumor immune microenvironment, we used several online tools—xCell (v1.6.0), CIBERSORTx, and ImmuCellAI (v2.0)—to estimate the abundance of immune cells based on the RNA-seq TPM-normalized expression data from the TCGA-ESCC cohort (with gene symbols as row names and sample IDs as column names) [20–22].

xCell generated enrichment scores for 64 immune and stromal cell types using its ssGSEA algorithm, while CIBERSORTx applied the LM22 feature matrix (covering 22 immune cell subtypes) and ran in “absolute” mode with 1000 permutations. ImmuCellAI, on the other hand, predicted immune cell abundances using its default marker gene sets for 24 immune cell types (including 18 T cell subtypes).

To ensure the reliability of our findings, we focused on immune subtypes that showed significant associations (p < 0.05) across at least two of the methods, considering them as consensus candidates for further analysis.

Weighted gene co expression network analysis (WGCNA)

We performed weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) on transcriptome data of 96 ESCC patients in TCGA [23]. After excluding low-expression genes and one outlier sample, patients were divided into the ZNF750 high-expression group and ZNF750 low-expression group based on median expression level. Through the BIC platform (https://www.bic.ac.cn/#/) Construct a network using a soft threshold of 10, convert the weighted adjacency matrix into a topological overlap matrix (TOM) for network connectivity evaluation, and then perform hierarchical clustering to identify co-expressed modules.

Use clusterProfiler to select modules that are significantly positively correlated (p.adjust < 0.05) with the high expression group of ZNF750 for GO enrichment analysis. This step systematically studied the gene network related to ZNF750 in ESCC while considering the network topology structure.

TCGA-GTEx differentially expressed genes and TCGA expressed genes (DESeq2, P-value < 0.05, |log₂FC| ≥ 1); MEred and MEbrown modules in WGCNA. A protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of cross-core genes was constructed using the STRING website (https://cn.string-db.org/) with default parameters (minimum interaction confidence > 0.4).

scRNA-seq analysis

To characterize ZNF750 expression patterns and their potential impact on the mucosal immune landscape in ESCC, we performed single-cell RNA-seq analysis on paired tumor and adjacent normal tissues from three ESCC patients (GSE196756). Raw count matrices were processed using the Seurat package (v4.3.0) in R (v4.2.2). Quality control was performed by removing cells with fewer than 200 detected genes or more than 25% mitochondrial gene content. Genes expressed in less than 3 cells were excluded. Counts were normalized using the LogNormalize method with a scale factor of 10,000. Cell cycle scoring was performed using the CellCycleScoring function based on canonical markers. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on the 2,000 most variable genes identified by the FindVariableFeatures function. Harmony (v0.1.1) was applied to integrate samples and remove batch effects.

Cells were clustered using the Louvain algorithm with resolution parameter 0.8. Cell types were annotated based on expression of canonical marker genes: Epithelial cells (EPCAM, KRT8, KRT18), T cells (CD3D, CD3E, CD3G), CD4⁺ T cells (CD4, IL7R), CD8⁺ T cells (CD8A, CD8B), Myofibroblasts (ACTA2, TAGLN), Cancer-associated fibroblasts (FAP, PDPN), Tumor-associated macrophages (CD68, CD163), Plasma cells (IGHG1, JCHAIN), Mast cells (TPSAB1, CPA3), Neutrophils (FCGR3B, S100A8), and Progenitor cells (SOX9, LGR5).

Cell-cell communication analysis

The human ligand-receptor interaction database containing 2,021 validated pairs was employed. Communication probabilities were calculated based on the law of mass action, integrating gene expression levels and interaction affinities. The net analysis was performed separately for tumor and normal groups, followed by comparative analysis using merged objects. Pathway-level communication strength was quantified as the sum of probabilities for all ligand-receptor pairs within each signaling pathway. Differential intercellular communication between tumor and normal tissues was assessed using netVisual_diffInteraction with “count” as the measurement metric. Statistical significance was determined through permutation testing (n = 1,000 iterations). Differences were considered significant at false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 after Benjamini-Hochberg correction. Top differential interactions were visualized using heatmaps.

The correlation between mucosal immune genes and DEFB4A with ZNF750

Zhang et al. delineated a mucosal immune signature gene set through single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) of ESCC tumor-normal paired specimens [19, 24]. This signature comprised eight coregulated genes (AGR2, CEACAM6, FAM3D, CXCL17, MUC20, PRSS22, S100P, PSCA). Analyzed TCGA transcriptomes (n = 96 ESCC) with log2(TPM + 1) transformed expression matrices, we computed Pearson correlation coefficients (r) between ZNF750 and signature genes following quantile normalization. A p-value less than 0.05 is considered statistically significant. In addition, we calculated the Spearman correlation between ZNF750 and DEFB4A in various datasets.

Mucosal microbiota analysis

Microbial abundance analysis was performed using esophageal tissue microbiome data from The Cancer Microbiome Atlas (TCMA; https://tcma.pratt.duke.edu/). Only samples classified as ESCC were included in this analysis. The normalized relative abundance data in this database is a public resource that has been rigorously designed for the decontamination and batch correction of low-biomass tumor samples. Samples were matched to our ESCC subset from the TCGA cohort stratified by median ZNF750 expression. Differential abundance of microbial taxa (e.g., Propionibacteriaceae, Burkholderiaceae) between ZNF750 high and ZNF750 low groups was assessed using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, with significance defined as p < 0.05.

Cell culture

The KYSE150 cell line was maintained in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C with 5% carbon dioxide.

Plasmid transfection

Cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 3 × 105 cells/well 24 h prior to transfection. At 70% – 80% fusion, transfection was performed using 3 µg ZNF750 overexpression plasmid DNA and 4 µL Lipofectamine 8000 (Beyotime, Nanjing, China) diluted in Opti-MEM (Gibco, Carlsbad, USA). After 6 h, the medium was replaced with a complete culture medium, and cells were harvested 48 h post-transfection.

RNA extraction

Total RNA was isolated using RNAkey Reagent (Sevenbio, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA concentration and purity were assessed using a NanoDrop Lite spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA).

Reverse transcription and qPCR

cDNA was synthesized from total RNA using the All-in-One First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit II (with dsDNase) for qPCR (Sevenbio, Beijing, China). Quantitative PCR was performed using 2× Real-time PCR Super Mix (Mei5 Biotechnology), with GAPDH as an internal control. Relative expression was calculated using the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method (n = 3). The primer sequence is shown in Table 1 in the Supplementary Material.

Western blot analysis

The protein level of the gene was detected by western blotting. Cell pellets were added to RIPA lysate and left overnight at 4 °C. Protein lysates were collected by centrifugation at 12,000 × g at 4 °C for 10 min. Protein concentrations are calculated by the BCA method. The proteins were denatured at 95 °C for 10 min before electrophoresis. Use 10% SDS-PAGE electrophoresis at 80 volts for 120 min and transfer to a pre-methanol-activated polyvinyl chloride (PVDF) film. These membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk for 2 h at room temperature and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with a special primary antibody. This was followed by culture with a horseradish-peroxidase (HRP)-labeled secondary antibody, and the protein was detected with chemiluminescence reagents. The experiment was repeated three times. Protein Quantification: For Western blot analysis, protein bands were quantified by densitometry using ImageJ software. The target protein levels were normalized to the internal control (GAPDH) to ensure accurate comparative analysis.

Promoter motif analyses

Public ZNF750 ChIP-seq data were retrieved from the Cistrome Data Browser (Cistrome DB ID: 48799) and analyzed using the UCSC Genome Browser (hg38). The analysis focused on the proximal promoter region of DEFB4A (TSS: chr8:7,894,677) to identify significant ZNF750 binding peaks and determine their genomic locations.

The promoter sequence of DEFB4A (from − 2000 to + 200 bp relative to the TSS) was extracted, and a ZNF750 position frequency matrix from the JASPAR 2024 database was used to scan this region for high-confidence ZNF750 binding motifs.

Histological and immunohistochemical (IHC) staining

Histology and Immunohistochemistry (IHC) The tissue microarray (TMA, HEsoS030PG02) was obtained from the Shanghai National Engineering Research Center for Biochip. Sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through graded ethanol. For H&E staining, sections were treated with hematoxylin for 5 min, differentiated in acid ethanol, and counterstained with eosin for 2 min. For IHC, antigen retrieval was performed in sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0) via microwave heating. After blocking endogenous peroxidase (3% H2O2) and non-specific binding (5% BSA), sections were incubated with primary antibody (anti-ZNF750, 1:200, Sigma, HPA021573) overnight at 4 °C. Subsequently, sections were incubated with secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature, visualized using a DAB kit, and counterstained with hematoxylin. Finally, slides were mounted and scanned using an Axio Scan.Z1.

Based on the IHC staining results, five samples each were selected from the ZNF750 high-expression and low-expression groups. Neutrophils were manually counted in 10 random representative fields per slide using a light microscope at 400× magnification. The average count from these 10 fields was used to represent the neutrophil infiltration level for each sample.

Statistical analysis

Bioinformatic analyses were conducted in R (version 4.4.1). Group comparisons were performed using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests. Survival differences were evaluated by Kaplan-Meier analysis with log-rank testing. Independent sample means were compared via t-tests, with a statistical significance threshold set at p < 0.05 for all analyses, with non-normally distributed data analyzed by the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. The Spearman rank correlation coefficient was used to evaluate the correlation between variables. Data visualization was implemented using ggplot2 (version3.5.1) and ComplexHeatmap (version2.22.0) packages. The qPCR and Western blot experimental parts used one-way ANOVA, followed by Dunnett’s post-hoc test to compare the experimental group with the control group.

Results

ZNF750 is significantly differentially expressed in normal versus tumor samples across multiple cancer types

Our comprehensive differential expression analysis unveiled widespread and significant dysregulation of ZNF750 across a broad spectrum of human cancers (Fig. 1A), which manifested as two opposing patterns. A clear tumor-suppressive pattern, characterized by significant downregulation in tumors compared to matched normal tissues, was observed in epithelial-derived carcinomas, neuroendocrine neoplasms, and genitourinary malignancies (ACC, COAD, ESCC, etc.), In contrast, an oncogenic activation pattern (tumor > normal) predominated in digestive, respiratory, and gynecological malignancies (CESC, CHOL, LUAD, etc.). This fundamental dichotomy positions ZNF750 not as a universal tumor suppressor but as a context-dependent cellular regulator.

Within this landscape, ESCC showed conserved tumor-suppressive expression with significant normal-tumor dichotomy, indicating potential mechanistic divergence from other gastrointestinal malignancies. The reliability of this specific finding was confirmed through validation in independent Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets (Fig. 1B-C). Extending beyond expression, the clinical impact of this tissue-specific dysregulation was evident in survival outcomes. ZNF750 overexpression exhibits a bidirectional survival effect in pan-cancer: protective (e.g., KIRC: mean OS HR = 0.89, 95% CI = 0.83–0.96, P < 0.05) and risk-associated (e.g., SKCM: mean OS HR = 1.09, 95% CI = 1.04–1.14, P < 0.05) effects coexist (Fig. 1D), a finding that directly mirrors and reinforces the concept of its tissue-dependent function.

Comprehensive characterization of ZNF750 expression: integrating bulk and single-cell transcriptomes

To establish the biological foundation of ZNF750 in ESCC, we systematically delineated its expression profile across multiple biological models, spanning from bulk normal tissues to cancer cell lines and, ultimately, to the single-cell resolution within tumors. ZNF750 demonstrated a high basal expression in normal esophageal and skin tissues (Fig. 2A), aligning with its established master-regulator role in epithelial differentiation and barrier function maintenance, and suggesting a similar homeostatic function in the esophagus.

Analysis of the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) revealed a divergent expression pattern in corresponding malignancies. While skin squamous cell carcinoma lines maintained relatively high ZNF750 expression (median TPM = 2.55), esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) lines exhibited significantly lower levels (median TPM = 0.99) (Fig. 2B). This marked “high-normal, low-tumor” contrast strongly reinforces the candidacy of ZNF750 as a potential tumor suppressor in ESCC, whose loss may be associated with malignant transformation. Furthermore, ESCC cell lines displayed considerable expression heterogeneity. For instance, the expression in KYSE150 was only 23.9% of that in the high-expressing cell line TDOTT (TPM = 5.49) (Fig. 2C). To resolve its expression at the cellular level, we analyzed single-cell RNA sequencing data from ESCC tumors. ZNF750 expression was not ubiquitous but specifically enriched in distinct cell populations. ZNF750 expression was predominantly confined to malignant epithelial cells in ESCC (Fig. 2D-E), whereas in EAC, it was primarily detected in macrophage/monocyte clusters (Fig. 2F-G).

Low ZNF750 expression preidcts poor prognosis in patients with ESCC

Given the high expression of ZNF750 in normal esophageal tissues, its general downregulation and heterogeneous distribution in ESCC cell lines, and its enrichment within specific cell populations of the tumor microenvironment, we further investigated its clinical significance in ESCC patients.

Using data from GSE53624 cohort (n = 119 ESCC), we stratified patients into high- and low-expression subgroups based on the median ZNF750 expression for survival analysis. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that ZNF750 expression was significantly correlated with patient prognosis, with high expression associated with longer overall survival (Fig. 3A). Stratified analysis revealed that in the subgroup of patients aged under 50, those with high ZNF750 expression exhibited a superior survival prognosis, which may be attributed to more robust immune function and better overall health status in younger individuals (Fig. 3B). In non-smoking patients, high ZNF750 expression was also associated with significantly improved survival, suggesting that smoking may exert a detrimental impact on patient outcomes (Fig. 3C).

Furthermore, higher survival rates were observed in the high ZNF750 expression group without comorbid pneumonia, indicating that pneumonia, as a complication, may adversely affect survival (Fig. 3D). Notably, among patients with stage III disease, the prognosis for the high ZNF750 expression group was superior to that of the low expression group, further supporting the potential protective role of ZNF750 in ESCC progression (Fig. 3E). Multivariate Cox regression analysis, corrected for age, sex, smoking status, disease stage, and other potential confounders, and tested the proportional harm hypothesis, with pneumonia as a stratified variable, confirming that ZNF750 expression is an independent prognostic factor (Fig. 3F, Supplementary Tables S1 and S2). In conclusion, high ZNF750 expression may serve as an independent predictive biomarker for ESCC prognosis, providing a valuable reference for clinical management.

ZNF750 potentially modulates mucosal immune pathways in ESCC

Analysis of DEGs revealed that in the tumor versus normal tissue comparison from the TCGA-GTEx dataset, a total of 3,791 genes were upregulated and 7,624 were downregulated (Fig. 4A). Within the TCGA-ESCC cohort (n = 96), the comparison between the ZNF750-high and ZNF750-low expression subgroups showed 740 upregulated and 724 downregulated DEGs (Fig. 4B). WGCNA identified MERed (r = 0.586, P = 4.4 × 10⁻10) and MEBrown (r = 0.557, P = 4.6 × 10⁻⁹) modules significantly associated with high ZNF750 expression (Fig. 4C). The intersection of the three (TCGA-GTEx up-regulated DEGs, ZNF750 high up-regulated DEGs, and MERed+MEBrown genes) yielded 137 core genes (Fig. 4D, Supplementary Figure S1, Supplementary Table S3). Among which, ZNF750 has 14 direct target genes, and 34 genes are markers of squamous differentiation (Supplementary Table S4 and Supplementary Table S5). This gene set exhibited significant protein interactions (Fig. 4E).

Importantly, GSEA analysis revealed these genes mainly enriched in pathways related to mucosal immunity (e.g., “mucosal immune response”, “innate immune response in mucosal”, and defense response to bacterium) (Fig. 5A-D). Other significantly enriched immune pathways are presented in full in the supplementary materials (Supplementary Figure S2). Furthermore, the average direct effect of ZNF750 on the immune score is highly significant (Supplementary Figure S3, Supplementary Table S6). We subsequently examined the correlation between ZNF750 expression and mucosal immune-related genes (such as CEACAM6/FAM3D) previously identified in published studies [25], see Table 2 in the supplementary materials for gene descriptions. As we anticipated, ZNF750 showed a positive correlation with these genes (r ≥ 0.3, P < 0.05) (Fig. 5E). These findings collectively suggest that ZNF750 may orchestrate mucosal immunity in ESCC through coordinated regulation of antimicrobial defense mechanisms and neutrophil recruitment pathways.

Given this proposed role in mucosal immunity, we hypothesized that ZNF750 may directly regulate key antimicrobial effectors. To test this, we focused on human beta-defensin-2 (DEFB4A), a well-established antimicrobial peptide central to mucosal immune defense [26]. The result revealed a significant positive correlation (r = 0.399, P < 5.7e-05) between the expression of ZNF750 and DEFB4A (Fig. 5F). qPCR results showed that overexpression of ZNF750 significantly upregulated the mRNA levels of DEFB4A in KYSE150 cells. Conversely, knockdown of ZNF750 decreased the mRNA expression of DEFB4A. This regulatory relationship was statistically significant in the paired t-test (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 5G).

The Western blot results further validated this trend at the protein level. Overexpression of ZNF750 also increased the protein content of DEFB4A, while knockdown of ZNF750 decreased its expression(Fig. 5H-I). Semi-quantitative analysis showed that this regulatory effect was also significant. UCSC Genome Browser analysis revealed a predictive ZNF750 binding motif in the DEFB4A promoter region (Supplementary Figure S4, Supplementary Table S7). In conclusion, qPCR and Western blot results jointly confirmed that ZNF750 can positively regulate the transcription and translation of DEFB4A, providing experimental evidence for the functional association between the two in mucosal immunity.

ZNF750 promotes a neutrophil-enriched, mucosal-immune-favorable tumor microenvironment in ESCC

To investigate the impact of ZNF750 on the immune microenvironment of ESCC, we performed an immune infiltration analysis on the TCGA-ESCC cohort using multiple deconvolution algorithms (xCell, ImmuCellAI, CIBERSORTx) (Fig. 6A-B). The results consistently revealed a characteristic immune polarization state in ZNF750-high tumors, marked by a significant enrichment of neutrophils and a concomitant suppression of immunosuppressive M2 macrophages (Fig. 6C-D). All comparisons of algorithms for the same cell type showed a positive correlation (r value range: 0.39–0.62, see Supplementary Figure S5 and Supplementary Table S8). This “high-neutrophil, low-M2 macrophage” immune landscape has significant anti-tumor implications.

In addition, the correlation between ZNF750 expression and tumor purity was found to be significantly negative, which is consistent with the biological localization of ZNF750 as a tumor suppressor (Supplementary Figure S6, Supplementary Material 1). Importantly, further analysis of our clinical samples validated these findings. The quantitative analysis based on H&E-stained tissue microarrays confirmed a significantly higher level of neutrophil infiltration in tumors from patients with high ZNF750 expression (Fig. 6E). Representative immunohistochemistry images for ZNF750 and corresponding H&E staining further illustrated this enhanced neutrophil infiltration, as indicated by the yellow arrows in the magnified views (Fig. 6F). Crucially, neutrophils are frontline executors of the mucosal immune response. They are rapidly recruited to mucosal barrier sites, where they exert direct antimicrobial and cytotoxic effects through mechanisms such as reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) release, and phagocytosis [27, 28]. In contrast, the reduction of M2 macrophages weakens the tumor’s capacity for immune evasion and angiogenesis [29].

To further evaluate the impact of TP53 mutation status on the immune correlation of ZNF750, we performed stratified correlation analyses (Supplementary Figure S7). ZNF750 expression showed a positive correlation with neutrophil infiltration in both the TP53 mutant (R = 0.33) and wild-type (R = 0.48) groups, and a negative correlation with M2 macrophage infiltration in both groups (mutant: R = − 0.22; wild-type: R = − 0.69). Notably, the correlations in the TP53 mutant group were weaker than those in the wild-type group for both immune cell types, suggesting that TP53 mutation may attenuate ZNF750-mediated immune regulation. Although the differences between groups did not reach statistical significance (P for interaction > 0.05; see Supplementary Figure S7), the trend—particularly the diminished correlation in the mutant group—may hold biological relevance. Future studies with larger wild-type cohorts are warranted to further validate this potential synergistic relationship. Given the established role of neutrophils in maintaining mucosal homeostasis through dynamic interactions with the microbiota, we next extended our analysis beyond human cells to the microbial constituents of the TME by interrogating ESCC mucosal microbiota data. We found that tumors with low ZNF750 expression were significantly enriched in specific bacterial families, particularly Propionibacteriaceae and Burkholderiaceae, which were still significant after multiplex correction, which contained species associated with gastrointestinal tumorigenesis (Fig. 6G, Supplementary Table S9). This inverse association suggests that ZNF750 may contribute to maintaining a healthier, less pro-tumorigenic mucosal microbial niche, although the microbial abundance data derived from TCMA are exploratory and warrant further validation. Collectively, these data paint a cohesive picture: ZNF750 is associated with a dual restructuring of the ESCC TME—favoring an anti-tumor immune contexture and correlating with a microbiota profile less conducive to cancer progression. This positions ZNF750 as a potential central regulator of the host-microbe interface in ESCC.

Single-cell landscape reveals that ZNF750 maintains the esophageal mucosal immune homeostasis, the dysregulation of which facilitates tumor progression

To further explore the cellular and molecular mechanisms by which ZNF750 orchestrates the esophageal mucosal microenvironment, we performed scRNA-seq on ESCC tumor tissues and their corresponding tumor-adjacent (normal) counterparts. Unsupervised clustering identified 10 major cell lineages, including epithelial cells, T cells, macrophages (TAMs), and fibroblasts (Fig. 7A-B). A significant redistribution of the cellular landscape was observed during the transition from tumor-adjacent tissue to tumor: while neutrophils and certain stromal components such as mast cells and myofibroblasts were diminished, there was a notable expansion of progenitor cells and TAMs in the malignancy (Fig. 7C-D). Crucially, ZNF750 was found to be predominantly expressed in the epithelial cells of tumor-adjacent tissues, whereas its expression was markedly suppressed in tumor-derived epithelial cells (Fig. 7E-F, Supplementary Figure S8). Functional enrichment analysis revealed that the loss of ZNF750 in tumor epithelial cells was accompanied by the downregulation of pathways essential for epithelial integrity, particularly “Cornified envelope formation” pathway, which is a hallmark of the mucosal physical barrier (Fig. 7G).

The shift in epithelial identity prompted us to investigate whether ZNF750-high epithelial cells serve as a “mucosal guardian” by coordinating immune surveillance. In tumor-adjacent tissues, computational inference of cell-cell communication analysis revealed a robust and harmonious mucosal immune network (Fig. 8A). In this healthy niche, epithelial cells and CD4+ T cells were central hubs of interaction. Specifically, we observed predicted strong signaling through the MHC-I and MHC-II pathways, alongside CD45 and CD99 pathways, which are critical for efficient antigen presentation and T-cell activation (Fig. 8B). The inferred ligand-receptor network further highlighted a sophisticated MHC-I surveillance system involving HLA-E and its receptors (NKG2A/C), suggesting that ZNF750-high epithelial cells actively maintain a vigilant mucosal immune barrier to prevent malignant transformation (Fig. 8C).

In striking contrast, this protective mucosal immunity was dysregulated within the tumor tissue (Fig. 8D). The predicted interaction landscape shifted from immune-centric to a pro-fibrotic and immunosuppressive one. The predominant crosstalk in the tumor was redirected toward CAFs and TAMs, dominated by the COLLAGEN, TENASCIN, and LAMININ signaling pathways (Fig. 8E). The extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling network, characterized by a complex interplay between various collagen types (e.g., COL1A1, COL6A3) and integrin receptors (e.g., ITGA1/B1), was highly activated in the tumor according to our computational predictions (Fig. 8F). This shift signifies that once the ZNF750-mediated mucosal defense is compromised, epithelial cells lose their ability to coordinate immune surveillance and instead engage in pathological remodeling of the microenvironment, creating a “walled-off” niche that supports tumor invasion and immune exclusion.

Discussion

The esophageal mucosa serves as a critical interface between the host and the external environment, requiring a sophisticated balance between physical barrier integrity and immune surveillance. While ZNF750 is well-established as a master regulator of keratinocyte differentiation, our study redefines its role in the context of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) as a central orchestrator of mucosal immunity. We demonstrate that the loss of ZNF750 facilitates a transition from an immune-active tumor microenvironment to a pro-fibrotic and immunosuppressive state, thereby driving tumor progression.

Previous research on ZNF750 has largely focused on cell-autonomous functions, such as regulating epithelial differentiation and inhibiting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [30]. However, recent evidence suggests its functions are deeply intertwined with immune regulation. For instance, ZNF750 has been shown to dampen surface inflammation by silencing pattern recognition receptors and is essential for the coordinated development of epidermal immunocytes [31, 32]. Our findings extend this paradigm to the esophageal mucosa, where ZNF750-high epithelial cells maintain a robust physical and immunological barrier. The enrichment of “Cornified envelope formation” pathways in ZNF750-high cells aligns with its role in periodontal and skin homeostasis, suggesting that structural integrity is a prerequisite for effective mucosal immune surveillance [33, 34].

A pivotal finding of this study is the positive regulation of DEFB4A (human beta-defensin-2) by ZNF750, a relationship we validated through both gain- and loss-of-function experiments at the mRNA and protein levels. While DEFB4A has been identified as a prognostic biomarker in other cancers, its robust induction by ZNF750 in ESCC highlights the transcription factor’s essential role in reinforcing the mucosal antimicrobial shield [35]. Parallel to this chemical defense, our clinical and bioinformatic data reveal that ZNF750 expression is significantly associated with the recruitment of neutrophils. Rather than identifying these cells as pro-tumorigenic N2-polarized neutrophils often found in late-stage disease, our findings suggest that ZNF750 maintains an environment that favors neutrophils as “frontline executors” of mucosal defense. This is consistent with evidence in ESCC demonstrating that specific neutrophil recruitment can promote beneficial anti-tumor immunity [36]. Furthermore, the “stromal-neutrophil axis” observed in our single-cell data mirrors the tissue-resident neutrophil populations identified in the oral mucosa atlas, which are critical for maintaining tissue immunity and preventing microbial-driven inflammation [37, 38]. Collectively, these results suggest that ZNF750 suppresses tumor progression by independently orchestrating both the molecular antimicrobial response and the protective cellular landscape of the esophageal mucosa.

Stratified analysis showed that the correlation between ZNF750 and immune cells was attenuated in TP53-mutant tumors compared to wild-type, suggesting that TP53 mutation may dampen ZNF750-mediated immune regulation. This is consistent with previous reports that TP53 can regulate ZNF750 expression [39]. Nevertheless, given the limited sample size, these findings warrant further validation in larger cohorts with mutation isotype analysis to better understand the interaction between TP53 status, ZNF750 expression, and immune infiltration.

The esophageal mucosal-microbial interface is a dynamic system where ZNF750 appears to act as a critical regulator. Our results indicate that the loss of ZNF750 is associated with the enrichment of tumor-promoting bacterial families such as Propionibacteriaceae, but such findings remain exploratory at this time. Microbiome dysbiosis is known to drive neutrophil recruitment into pro-tumorigenic configurations and promote mesenchymal transitions in other gastrointestinal malignancies [27]. In ESCC, the depletion of ZNF750 likely weakens the antimicrobial peptide screen (e.g., DEFB4A), allowing pathogenic bacteria to settle and trigger chronic inflammatory loops. This dysregulated mucosal state is characterized by the loss of MHC-I/II-mediated surveillance and the subsequent activation of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs).

Our single-cell analysis captured the dramatic shift from an epithelial-immune hub to ‘CAF-dominated microenvironment upon ZNF750 downregulation. In the absence of ZNF750, the “walled-off” niche dominated by COLLAGEN, TENASCIN, and LAMININ signaling reflects a pathological remodeling of the extracellular matrix. This crosstalk between CAFs and the tumor microenvironment is a hallmark of immune exclusion [40, 41]. Interestingly, ZNF750 has been shown to target Tenascin C to regulate immunogenicity in other squamous cell carcinomas, further supporting our observation that ZNF750 loss directly facilitates the transition toward a pro-fibrotic, immune-cold tumor interior [42].

From a clinical perspective, given its ability to coordinate both the innate immune response and the structural barrier, therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring ZNF750 activity or mimicking its downstream mucosal immune effects could provide a novel approach to reversing the immunosuppressive TME in ESCC. The molecular signature of High ZNF750-High DEFB4A-High Neutrophils holds promise as a composite prognostic biomarker for identifying low-risk ESCC patients. More significantly, this mechanism offers a rationale for novel immunotherapeutic strategies. Since ZNF750 deficiency results in an immune-desert cold tumor phenotype, pharmacological restoration of ZNF750 or direct targeting of DEFB4A receptors could theoretically convert these into immune-infiltrated hot tumors [43]. Such interventions may sensitize tumors to immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as PD-1/PD-L1 blockades.

Despite these insights, our study has limitations that warrant future investigation. First, while we observed strong expression correlations and functional effects, the direct binding of ZNF750 to the DEFB4A promoter requires definitive confirmation via chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays. Second, the broader molecular mechanisms by which ZNF750 orchestrates the esophageal mucosal immune landscape and the host-microbe interface remain to be fully elucidated and require more in-depth experimental validation.

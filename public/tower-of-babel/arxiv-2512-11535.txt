Hamiltonicity of optimal 2-planar graphs
Licheng Zhang, Yuanqiu Huang, Zhangdong Ouyang

Abstract
A classical result of Tutte shows that every 4-connected planar graph is Hamiltonian. In recent years, there has been growing interest in extending classical Hamiltonian results from planar graphs to sparse graphs with drawings allowing crossings, such as k k -planar graphs, where each edge is crossed at most k k times. For example, using different approaches, Hudák, Tomáš and Suzuki, as well as Noguchi and Suzuki, independently proved that every optimal 1-planar graph is Hamiltonian. Here, an optimal 1-planar graph refers to one that attains the maximum possible number of edges. In this paper, we establish results on the Hamiltonicity of optimal 2-planar graphs, that is, 2-planar graphs with the maximum number of edges. More precisely, we show that every 4-connected optimal 2-planar graph is Hamiltonian-connected. With vertex-connectivity 3, there exist infinitely many optimal 2-planar graphs that are non-Hamiltonian.

1
Introduction

Unless otherwise stated, we consider only finite simple graphs and use standard terminology in graph theory. For a graph
G
, let
V(G)
and
E(G)
denote its vertex set and edge set. For any subset
V_{0}
of
V(G)
, the subgraph of
G
induced by
V_{0}
, denoted by
G\left[V_{0}\right]
, is the graph with vertex set
V_{0}
and edge set
\{uv\in E(G):u,v\in V(G)\}
. Let
G-V_{0}
denote the subgraph of
G
induced by
V(G)\backslash V_{0}
when
V_{0}\neq V(G)
. A subset
S
of
V(G)
is called a vertex-cut if
G-S
is disconnected, and a vertex-cut
S
is called a
k
-vertex-cut if
|S|=k
. For a noncomplete graph
G
, its connectivity, denoted by
\kappa(G)
, is defined to be the minimum value of
|S|
over all vertex-cuts
S
of
G
, and
\kappa(G)=|V(G)|-1
if
G
is complete. A graph
G
is
k
-connected
if
\kappa(G)\geq k
. A Hamiltonian path (resp., cycle) in
G
is a path (resp., cycle) in
G
that visits every vertex of
G
exactly once. A graph
G
is called Hamiltonian if
G
contains a Hamiltonian cycle. A graph
G
is Hamiltonian-connected if every two vertices in
G
are connected by a Hamiltonian path. Clearly, all Hamiltonian-connected graphs of orders of at least three are Hamiltonian.

A
drawing
of a graph
G
is a mapping that assigns to each vertex of
G
a distinct point in the plane and to each edge a continuous arc connecting its endpoints.
Beginning in the 1970s and 1980s, beyond planarity has become a newer topic in graph drawing and topological graph theory, while planar graphs remain a classic class of graphs in graph theory
[
7
]
. Roughly speaking, beyond-planar graphs are graphs with drawings that forbid certain types of local edge crossings. Among the most studied classes of beyond-planar graphs are the
k
-planar graphs
, which admit drawings in the plane where each edge is crossed at most
k
times. Such a drawing is also called a

k
-planar drawing
or a
k
-plane graph
. Pach and Tóth
[
21
]
showed that every
k
-planar graph with
n\geq 3
vertices has at most
4.108\sqrt{k}n
edges, and at most
(k+3)(n-2)
edges when
k\leq 4
. In particular, this implies that 1-planar graphs have at most
4n-8
edges, and 2-planar graphs have at most
5n-10
edges. An
n
-vertex 1-planar graph that has exactly
4n-8
edges is called an
optimal 1-planar
graph, and an
n
-vertex 2-planar graph that has exactly
5n-10
edges is called an
optimal 2-planar
graph. Currently, most research has focused on planar graphs and 1-planar graphs, while significantly fewer results exist for
k
-planar graphs with
k\geq 2
. Bekos et al.
[
4
,
5
]
showed that every optimal 2-planar graph
G
is obtained by drawing a pentagram (that is, five mutually crossing edges) in the interior of each face of a 3-connected pentagulation. This elegant property also serves as a crucial component in the proofs of our results in this paper.
Bekos et al.
[
4
]
also showed that every optimal 2-planar graph
G=(V,E)
with
n
vertices admits an edge partition into three subsets
E_{1},E_{2}
, and
E_{3}
, which can be computed in linear time
O(n)
. In this partition, the subgraph induced by
E_{1}
is a 1-planar graph, while the subgraphs induced by
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
are plane forests. It is well-known that the planarity of a graph can be recognized in linear time
[
14
]
. In contrast, for all
k\geq 1
recognizing
k
-planar graphs is NP-complete
[
13
,
26
]
. Nevertheless, Brandenburg
[
9
]
showed that optimal 1-planar graphs can be recognized in linear time, and later, Förster, Kaufmann, and Raftopoulou
[
11
]
showed that optimal 2-planar graphs can also be recognized in linear time.

A
triangulation
is a plane graph such that every face is bounded by a cycle of length three. The study of Hamiltonian cycles in planar graphs dates back to Whitney, who proved that every 4-connected triangulation is Hamiltonian
[
27
]
, and Tutte extended this conclusion to every 4‐connected planar graph
[
25
]
. The class of subgraphs originally used by Tutte in his proof, known as Tutte subgraphs, has become one of the few powerful tools for establishing Hamiltonian properties in graphs, particularly in planar graphs
[
19
,
24
,
28
,
29
]
and those embedded on other surfaces
[
18
,
23
]
. In recent years, there has been growing interest in the Hamiltonicity of graphs with fixed drawings allowing edge crossings. For example, Ozeki and Zamfirescu
[
20
]
showed that all 4-connected graphs with crossing number at most two are Hamiltonian. Moreover, the existence of Hamiltonian cycles in 1-planar graphs under specific structural conditions has also attracted considerable interest. Hudák, Tomáš and Suzuki
[
15
]
proved that every optimal
1
-planar graph
G
is Hamiltonian
via a planarization approach. Let
G^{*}
denote the associated plane graph obtained by turning each
crossing in a drawing of
G
into a degree-
4
vertex. They showed that
G^{*}
is
4
-connected, and
thus Hamiltonian by Tutte’s theorem; a Hamiltonian cycle in
G^{*}
can then be translated back into
a Hamiltonian cycle of
G
. Using a different strategy, Noguchi and Suzuki proved
[
15
]
that every optimal
1
-planar graph contains a spanning subgraph
H
isomorphic to a

4
-connected triangulation, and then used Tutte’s theorem on
H
to show that
G
is Hamiltonian. Moreover, by invoking Thomassen’s theorem
[
24
]
, one may strengthen the result to conclude that every optimal 1-planar graph is Hamiltonian-connected.
In the same paper
[
15
]
, the authors showed that 7-connected maximal 1-planar graphs are Hamiltonian.
Furthermore, Fabrici, Harant, Madaras, Mohr, Soták and Zamfirescu
[
10
]
strengthened the result by proving that any 4-connected maximal 1-planar graph is Hamiltonian (in fact ‘maximal’ can be relaxed
to ‘at all crossings the endpoints induce
K_{4}
’). Very recently, the first two authors of this paper, Lv and Dong
[
30
]
showed that any 4-connected 1-planar chordal graph is Hamiltonian-connected. Notably, based on the characteristic structure of 4-connected
1
-planar chordal graphs, the proof in

[
30
]
does not rely on Tutte’s theorem.
Currently, it remains an open problem whether every 6-connected 1-planar graph is Hamiltonian
[
6
,
15
,
10
]
.

However, to the best of our knowledge, no results have been established regarding the Hamiltonicity of
k
-planar graphs for
k\geq 2
.
In line with the result of Hudák et al. on optimal 1-planar graphs, this paper focuses on the Hamiltonicity of optimal 2-planar graphs. Our results will show that the connectivity parameter influences the Hamiltonicity of optimal 2-planar graphs. Recall that the connectivity of any 1-planar graph is at most 7, since any 1-planar graph with
n
vertices has at most
4n-8
edges, implying that some vertex has degree at most 7.
Furthermore, Suzuki
[
22
]
and Fujisawa, Segawa and Suzuki
[
12
]
showed that every optimal 1-planar graph has connectivity either 4 or 6. Given that any 2-planar graph has at most
5n-10
edges, it follows that its minimum degree is at most 9. Consequently, the connectivity of any optimal 2-planar graph is at most 9. On the other hand, every optimal 2-planar graph contains a 3-connected spanning subgraph, implying that its connectivity is at least 3. Therefore, the connectivity of an optimal 2-planar graph lies between 3 and 9. Moreover, unlike optimal 1-planar graphs, which can only have connectivity 4 or 6, optimal 2-planar graphs can attain every connectivity value
k\in\{3,4,\ldots,9\}
since for each such
k
, we can construct infinitely many examples (to maintain a clear focus on the main topic, we intend to present these constructions separately in a forthcoming paper concerning connectivity of optimal 2-planar graphs).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be an optimal 2-planar graph. If
\kappa(G)\geq 4
, then
G
is Hamiltonian-connected.

It is worth noting that the optimality condition of Theorem
1.1
cannot be removed, as there exist 4-connected and even 5-connected 1-planar graphs (note that every 1-planar graph is also 2-planar) that are not Hamiltonian
[
10
]
. The following theorem shows that the connectivity condition of Theorem
1.1
cannot be weakened.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist infinitely many non-Hamiltonian optimal 2-planar graphs with connectivity three.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows:
Section 2 gives some terminology, notation and several lemmas. Section 3 discusses properties of the face-stellations of 3-connected plane graphs. Section 4 contains the proofs of the main theorems. Finally, we conclude with remarks and open problems.

2
Preliminaries

Now we introduce some terminology, notation and lemmas.
A drawing is called
good
if no edge crosses itself, no two edges cross more than once, and no two edges that share a vertex cross each other. In this paper, all drawings are assumed to be good. In a drawing of a graph, an
edge
is called
crossed
if it intersects with at least one other edge at a point different from their common endpoints. Otherwise, the edge is called
uncrossed
.

A
spanning subgraph
of a graph
G
is a subgraph that includes all the vertices of the original graph
G
.
Given a drawing
D
of a graph
G
, removing all crossed edges results in the
planar skeleton

\mathcal{P}(D)
. Clearly, the planar skeleton
\mathcal{P}(D)
is a spanning subgraph of
G
.
A
component
of a graph is a maximal connected subgraph. A
k
-cycle
is a cycle in a graph consisting of exactly
k
vertices.

Let
G
be a plane graph. Each face is bounded by a closed walk called the
boundary
of the face.
Let
C
be a cycle in
G
. The cycle
C
partitions the plane into two regions: a bounded region called the
interior
of
C
and an unbounded region called the
exterior
of
C
. We say that
C
is a
separating cycle
if both the interior and exterior regions contain at least one vertex of
G
.

A pentagulation is a plane graph in which every face is bounded by a cycle of length
5
. Similar to the relationship between optimal 1-planar graphs and 3-connected quadrangulations
[
22
]
, Bekos, Kaufmann, and Raftopoulou obtained analogous structural results for (not necessarily simple) 2-planar graphs, and in particular the authors in
[
4
]
established the following connection between simple optimal 2-planar graphs and 3-connected pentagulations.

Lemma 2.1

(Bekos et al.
[
4
,
5
]
)
.

Let
G
be a simple optimal 2-planar graph. Then
G
is obtained by drawing a pentagram (that is, five mutually crossed edges) in the interior of each face of a 3-connected pentagulation.

Roughly speaking, Lemma
2.1
implies that one can get an optimal 2-planar graph by taking a 3-connected pentagulation and replacing every pentagonal face along with its boundary cycle by a complete graph
K_{5}
.

Now, we define such a drawing
D
of an optimal 2-planar graph
G
, which is obtained by inserting a pentagram inside each face of a 3-connected pentagulation, as the
op-drawing
of
G
. For example, Figure
1
illustrates an optimal
2
-planar graph on
20
vertices (the minimum
possible order). Its planar skeleton is the dodecahedron. Interestingly, this echoes the origin of
the term
Hamiltonian
: in 1857, Sir William Rowan Hamilton introduced the
Icosian Game
,
a puzzle of finding a Hamiltonian cycle on the dodecahedron.

Figure 1:
An optimal 2-planar graph with order 20

The following lemma follows directly from Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be an optimal 2-planar graph with an op-drawing
D
, and let
P(D)
be the planar skeleton of
D
. For any pentagonal face of
P(D)
, every pair of its boundary vertices is adjacent in
G
.

The following is a result of Thomassen, which is a key step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.3

(Thomassen,
[
24
]
)
.

Let
G
be a planar graph. If
\kappa(G)\geq 4
, then
G
is Hamiltonian-connected.

of a cycle

in a graph is an edge that connects two non-consecutive vertices of

A

is a graph obtained from a cycle by adding a new vertex, and connecting it to all vertices of the cycle.

]

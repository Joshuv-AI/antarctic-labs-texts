Abstract
. We prove that assuming 𝔟 = 𝔡 \mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{d} , in the class of hereditarily Lindelöf spaces, each productively Scheepers space is productively Hurewicz. The above statement remains true in the class of all general topological spaces assuming that 𝔡 = ℵ 1 \mathfrak{d}=\aleph_{1} . To this end we use combinatorial methods and the Menger covering property parametrized by ultrafilters. We also show that if near coherence of filters holds, then the Scheepers property is equivalent to a Menger property parametrized by any ultrafilter.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Landscape of combinatorial covering properties

By
space
we mean an infinite Tychonoff topological space.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be a cover of a space
X
.
The family
\mathcal{U}
is a
\gamma
-cover

The family

We consider the following classical combinatorial covering properties.

We have the following implications:

\sigma\text{-compactness }\Longrightarrow\text{ Hurewicz }\Longrightarrow\text{ Scheepers }\Longrightarrow\text{ Menger. }

[

[

[

Regarding the last implication:

1.2.
Products

A topological property
\mathbf{P}
is
productive in a given class of spaces
if any product of two spaces from this class with the property
\mathbf{P}
, has the property
\mathbf{P}
.
In the class of general topological spaces, by a result of Todorcevic
[
15
, Theorem 8]
, none of the properties Hurewicz, Scheepers, Menger is productive.
In the class of sets of reals the situation is much more subtle and highly depends on the ambient mathematical universe:
In the Miller model (where Menger is equivalent to Scheepers) Menger is productive but Hurewicz is not
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
19
, Theorem 1.7]
.
In the Laver model Hurewicz is productive but Scheepers and Menger are not
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
.
If the Continuum Hypothesis holds, then none of the properties Hurewicz, Scheepers, Menger is productive
[
7
, Theorem 2.12]
.

Let
\mathbf{P}
be a topological property.
A space
X
is
productively
\mathbf{P}
in a given class of spaces

from this class with the property
\mathbf{P}

\mathbf{P}
.
A space is
productively

\mathbf{P}
, if it is productively
\mathbf{P}
in the class of all topological spaces.
Szewczak–Tsaban proved that assuming
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{d}
, in the class of hereditarily Lindelöf spaces, any productively Menger space is productively Hurewicz
[
13
, Theorem 4.8]
and if
\mathfrak{d}=\omega_{1}
, then the same statement remains true in the class of all spaces
[
13
, Lemma 3.4]
.
Recently, Repovs–Zdomskyy proved that each Hurewicz set is productively Menger in the Laver model in the class of sets of reals
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
.
A combination of these results, delivers the above mentioned result that in the Laver model (where
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{d}
), in the class of sets of reals, Hurewicz is productive.
Repovs–Zdomskyy also proved that it is consistent with the Continuum Hypothesis that in the class of sets of reals, there is a productively Hurewicz space which is not productively Scheepers and not productively Menger
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
.

1.3.
Main results

The aim of this paper is to complete this picture considering productively Scheepers spaces (in various classes of spaces).
In particular, we show that assuming
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{d}
, in the class of hereditarily Lindelöf spaces any productively Scheepers space is productively Hurewicz and if
\mathfrak{d}=\omega_{1}
, then this result remains true in the class of all topological spaces.

2.
Combinatorial characterizations of properties

Considered here properties have been succesfully studied using combinatorial structure of the Baire space.
In order to provide combinatorial characterizations of these properties we need auxiliary notions and definitions of specific functions.
Let
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
be the power set of the set of natural numbers
\omega
.
Identifying each set from
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
with its characteristic function, which is an element of the Cantor cube
{2^{\omega}}
, we introduce a topology on
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
.
Let
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
be the family of all infinite subsets of
\omega
.
We identify each element of
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
with the increasing enumeration of its elements, an element of the Baire space
{\omega^{\omega}}
.
Then
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
can be viewed as a subset of
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
and
{\omega^{\omega}}
and the topologies on
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
, inherited from
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
and
{\omega^{\omega}}
, are the same.

For
a,b\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
, we write
a\leq^{*}b
, if the set
\{\,n:a(n)\leq b(n)\,\}
is cofinite.
A set
A\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is
bounded
, if there is a function
b\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
such that
a\leq^{*}b
for all functions
a\in A
.
A subset of
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is
unbounded
if it is not bounded.
Let
\mathfrak{b}
be the minimal cardinality of an unbounded set.
A set
D\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is
dominating
, if for each function
a\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
there is a function
d\in D
with
a\leq^{*}d
.
Let
\mathfrak{d}
be the minimal cardinality of a dominating set.
Let
U
be an ultrafilter on
\omega
.
For
a,b\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
we write
a\leq_{U}b
, if the set
\{\,n:a(n)\leq b(n)\,\}
is in the ultrafilter
U
.
A set
A\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is
\leq_{U}
-bounded
in there is a function
b\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
such that
a\leq_{U}b
for all
a\in A
.
A subset of
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is
\leq_{U}
-unbounded
if it is not
\leq_{U}
-bounded.
Let
\operatorname{\mathfrak{b}}(U)
be the minimal cardinality of a
\leq_{U}
-unbounded set.
We have the following inequalities for all ultrafilters
U
:

\mathfrak{b}\leq\operatorname{\mathfrak{b}}(U)\leq\mathfrak{d}.

The numbers
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{b}(U)
are regular and regularity of
\mathfrak{d}
is independent from ZFC.

Let
X
and
Y
be spaces.
A set-valued map
\Phi
which assigns to each element
x\in X
a compact set
\Phi(x)\subseteq Y
is
compact-valued upper semicontinuous
(
cusco
for short) if for any open set
U\subseteq Y
, the set
\Phi^{-1}[U]:=\{\,x\in X:\Phi(x)\subseteq U\,\}
is open in
X
.
Then we write
\Phi\colon X\Longrightarrow Y
.

Theorem 2.1

(Miller, Tsaban, Zdomskyy
[
9
, Theorem 7.3, Theorem 7.5]
)
.

Let
X
be a space.
The following assertions are equivalent.

(1)

The space
X
is Hurewicz (
U
-Menger, Scheepers, Menger).

(2)

The space
X
is Lindelöf and every cusco image of
X
in
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is bounded (
\leq_{U}
-bounded, not finitely-dominating, not dominating).

It follows directly from the above characterizations that each of the considered properties has a
critical cardinality
, i.e., the minimal cardinality of a subset of
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
which does not have the given property.
For Hurewicz it is
\mathfrak{d}
, for
U
-Menger it is
\operatorname{\mathfrak{b}}(U)
and for Menger it is
\mathfrak{d}
.
Moreover, in ZFC, there is a
nontrivial
set of reals having a given property (Hurewicz,
U
-Menger, or Menger), i.e., a set having the given property with size greater or equal than the critical cardinality of this property.
The methods used in
[
9
]
allow to obtain the similar characterizations using cusco maps and upper continuous maps for the Scheepers property, but we do not need them here.
The critical cardinality of Scheeeprs is
\mathfrak{d}
.
It is not known whether there is a nontrivial Scheepers set of reals, if
\mathfrak{d}
is singular.

3.
Combinatorial tools

In order to consider relations between productively Scheepers and productively Hurewicz spaces (in various classes of spaces) we use the following combinatorial results.
A combination of them is a strengthening (when
\mathfrak{d}
is regular) of a result due to Szewczak–Tsaban
[

A subset of

A

d_{\alpha}\leq d_{\alpha}^{\prime}\leq 2d_{\alpha}^{\prime}+1=2a_{\alpha}\oplus(2a_{\alpha}\cup(2d^{\prime}_{\alpha}+1))=2a_{\alpha}\oplus(a_{\alpha}\uplus d^{\prime}_{\alpha})\in(2X)\oplus Y.

The inclusion
(2X)\oplus(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
follows from the definition of
\uplus
, and by the above the set
(2X)\oplus(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is dominating.
∎

Claim
3.7
shows that there exists a continuous dominating image of the space
X\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
in
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
.
Every continuous function into
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is cusco, thus
X\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is not Menger by Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Assume that
\operatorname{cf}\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{d}
.
Then for each each
\mathfrak{d}
-unbounded set
X
, there is a
U
-scale
Y
for some ultrafilter
U
such that
X\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is not Menger.

Now we present a direct consequence of the above result. A set of reals is a
\mathfrak{d}
-Lusin
set if it is homeomorphic with a subset of
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
with size at least
\mathfrak{d}
and whose intersection with any meager set in
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
has size smaller than
\mathfrak{d}
.
A
\mathfrak{d}
-Lusin set exists, e.g., under the assumption
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
.
Let
X\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
be a
\mathfrak{d}
-Lusin set.
Since each bounded set in
{[\omega]^{\omega}}
is meager, any bounded subset of
X
has size smaller than
\mathfrak{d}
.
It follows that
X
is
\mathfrak{d}
-unbounded.
Szewczak–Tsaban proved that for any
\mathfrak{d}
-Lusin set there is a Menger set of reals whose product space is not Menger
[
12
, Corollary 2.11]
.
For each
U
-scale
Y
, the set
Y\cup\mathrm{Fin}
is Menger in all finite powers, and thus the following Corollary is a strengthening of the mentioned result (when
\operatorname{cf}\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{d}
).

Corollary 3.9
.

Assume that
\operatorname{cf}\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{d}
.
For every
\mathfrak{d}
-Lusin set
X
there is a set of reals
Y
which is Menger in all finite powers such that the product space
X\times Y
is not Menger.

Corollary
3.9
is also related to a result of Szewczak–Wiśniewski
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
that assuming
\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal{M})=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{M})
, for each
\mathfrak{d}
-Lusin set
X
there is a
\mathfrak{d}
-Lusin set
Y
which is Menger in all finite powers such that the product space
X\times Y
is not Menger.

4.
Productively Scheepers and productively Hurewicz spaces

In this section we prove the following Theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{d}
.

(1)

Let
X
be a space such that for every ultrafilter
U
and hereditarily Lindelöf
U
-Menger space
T
the product space
X\times T
is Menger.
Then the space
X
is productively Hurewicz in the class of hereditarily Lindelöf spaces.

(2)

In the class of hereditarily Lindelöf spaces each productively Scheepers space is productively Hurewicz.

(3)

In the class of hereditarily Lindelöf spaces each space which is productively
U
-Menger for all ultrafilters
U
is productively Hurewicz.

Before we prove it, we need auxiliary results.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{d}
.
Then each unbounded set is
\leq_{U}
-unbounded for some ultrafilter
U
.

Proof.

Let
X\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
be an unbounded set.
In the light of Theorem
3.5
it is enough to prove that
X
contains a
\mathfrak{d}
-unbounded set.
Let
D=\{\,d_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{d}\,\}
be a dominating set.
Let
x_{0}\in X
.
Fix
\alpha<\mathfrak{d}
and assume that elements
x_{\beta}\in X
,
\beta<\alpha
, have been already defined.
Since
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{d}
, there is a function
b
such that
\{x_{\beta},d_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}\leq^{*}b
.
The set
X
is unbounded, so there is a function
x_{\alpha}\in X
such that

\{x_{\beta},d_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}\leq^{*}a_{\alpha}\leq^{\infty}x_{\alpha}.

Then
\{\,x_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{d}\,\}
is
\mathfrak{d}
-unbounded.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(
[
12
, Theorem 5.2]
)
.

Let
U
be an ultrafilter and
X\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
be a
U
-scale.
Then the space
X\cup\mathrm{Fin}
is productively
U
-Menger in the class of hereditarily Lindelöf spaces.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

(1)
Let
H
be a Hurewicz hereditarily Lindelöf space and suppose that
X\times H
is not Hurewicz.
By the assumption, the product space
X\times H
is Menger, so in particular it is Lindelöf.
By Theorem
2.1
, there is a cusco image
Z\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
of
X\times H
which is unbounded.
By Lemma
4.2
, the set
Z
is
\leq_{U}
-unbounded for some ultrafilter
U
.
By Theorem
3.5
, there is a
2U
-scale
Y
, such that
Z\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is not Menger.
The space
Z\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is a cusco image of the space
X\times H\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
.
Since the Menger property is preserved by cusco maps, the product
X\times H\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is not Menger.

On the other hand, since the space
H
is Hurewicz, it is also
2U
-Menger.
By Theorem
4.3
, the product space
H\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is
2U
-Menger.
It is also hereditarily Lindelöf.
By the assumption on
X
, the product space
X\times H\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is Menger, a contradiction.

Items (2) and (3) follows directly from (1).
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

In the class of hereditarily Lindelöf spaces, each productively Menger space is productively
U
-Menger for all ultrafilters
U
with
\operatorname{\mathfrak{b}}(U)=\mathfrak{d}
.

In order to prove Theorem
4.4
we need the following observation.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
U
be an ultrafilter and
2U
be an ultrafilter generated by the set
\{2u:u\in U\}
. Then any
U
-Menger space is
2U
-Menger.

Proof.

Let
X
be a
U
-Menger set and
Y\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
be its cusco image.
Let
y^{\prime}\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
be a function such that
y^{\prime}(n):=y(2n)
for all
n
for each
y\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
and define
Y^{\prime}:=\{y^{\prime}:y\in Y\}
.
Since
Y^{\prime}
is a cusco image of
X
, it is
\leq_{U}
-bounded by some function
b
.
Fix
y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}
.
Since
\{\,n:y^{\prime}(n)\leq b(n)\,\}\in U
, we have

\{\,n:y^{\prime}(n)\leq b(n)\,\}=\{\,n:y(2n)\leq b(n)\,\}\subseteq\{\,n:y(2n)\leq b(2n)\,\}\in U,

and thus
\{\,2n:y(2n)\leq b(2n)\,\}\in 2U
.
We conclude that
Y
is
\leq_{2U}
-bounded by the function
b^{\prime}
, hence
X
is
2U
-Menger.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Let
X
be a productively Menger space in the class of hereditarily Lindelöf spaces and suppose that there is a
U
-Menger hereditarily Lindelöf space
H
for some ultrafilter
U
such that
X\times H
is not
U
-Menger.
The space
H
is hereditarily Lindelöf and Menger, so
X\times H
is Menger, in particular it is Lindelöf.
By Theorem
2.1
, there is a cusco image
Z\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
of
X\times H
which is
\leq_{U}
-unbounded.
By Theorem
3.5
, there is a
2U
-scale
Y
, such that
Z\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is not Menger.
The space
Z\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is a cusco image of the space
X\times H\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
.
Since the Menger property is preserved by cusco maps, the product
X\times H\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is not Menger.

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.3
, the product space
H\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is Menger. It is also hereditarily Lindelöf.
By the assumption on
X
, the product space
X\times H\times(Y\cup\mathrm{Fin})
is Menger, a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

It is consistent with the Continuum Hypothesis that in the class of sets of reals there is a productively Hurewicz set which is not productively
U
-Menger for some ultrafilter
U
.

Proof.

An example is essentially due to Repovs–Zdomskyy
[
10
, Section 3]
.
Let
\mathbb{C}_{\omega_{1}}
be an iterated Cohen forcing of length
\omega_{1}
with finite supports and
G
be a
\mathbb{C}_{\omega_{1}}
-generic over a model
V
satisfying the Continuum Hypothesis.
These authors proved that, in
V[G]
in the class of sets of reals, there are a productively Hurewicz set and a Menger set from the ground model, whose product is not Menger.
It turns out that the same example works also here.
We show that any ground model set
X\subseteq{2^{\omega}}\cap V
is
U
-Menger in the extension, for some ultrafilter
U
.
Let
c_{\alpha}
be the
\alpha
-th Cohen real added to the ground model, where
\alpha<\omega_{1}
.
The sets

F_{\alpha}:=\{\,[z\leq c_{\alpha}]:z\in\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}{\omega^{\omega}}\cap V[\{\,c_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}]\,\}

are centered and
\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}F_{\beta}
is centered too, where
\alpha<\omega_{1}
.
Let
U
be an ultrafilter in
M[G]
containing
\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}F_{\alpha}
.
Let
\varphi\colon X\to{[\omega]^{\omega}}
be a continuous function in
\M[G]
.
Then there is an ordinal number
\alpha<\omega_{1}
such that
f
is coded in
M[\{\,c_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\,\}]
.
Then
\varphi[X]\leq_{U}c_{\alpha}
, and thus the set
X
is
U
-Menger.
∎

6.
Near coherence of filters and the Scheepers property

Near coherence relation
on the set of ultrafilters
was introduced by Blass
[
5
]
.
Ultrafilters
U,U^{\prime}
are
nearly coherent
if there exists a finite-to-one map
\varphi\colon\omega\to\omega
such that

\varphi[U]=\varphi[U^{\prime}]
.
Following
[
5
]
we denote by NCF the statement saying that any two ultrafilters on
\omega
are nearly coherent.
NCF is consistent with ZFC
[
4
]
, it follows from
\mathfrak{u}<\mathfrak{g}

[
2
]
,
but is not equivalent to
\mathfrak{u}<\mathfrak{g}

[
8
]
.
It is also known
[
5
, Theorem 16]
that NCF yields

\mathfrak{b}(U)=\mathfrak{d}
for all ultrafilters
U
, and also
it implies the existence of an ultrafilter
W
generated by
less than
\mathfrak{d}
many sets.
It has been established in
[
5
]

(see, e.g., the discussion in the first paragraph on
[
3
, P. 729]
for more explanations) that near coherence of two ultrafilters is witnessed by a monotone surjection
\varphi\colon\omega\to\omega
.
We use these results in the following Proposition.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that NCF holds.
Every Scheepers space is
U
-Menger for all ultrafilters
U
.
Consequently the properties
U
-Menger are equivalent for all ultrafilters
U
.

Proof.

Apply Theorem
2.1
.
Let
Y\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
be a cusco image of a Scheepers space
X
.
There is a function

h\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}
witnessing that

Y
is not finitely-dominating, which implies that there exists an
ultrafilter
U^{\prime}
containing
\{\,[y\leq h]:y\in Y\,\}
.
By NCF, there is an ultrafilter
W
with a base of size
\kappa<\mathfrak{d}
and there is a monotone surjection
\varphi\colon\omega\to\omega

with
\varphi[U^{\prime}]=\varphi[W]
.
Let
\{\,w_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\,\}
be a base for
W
.
The sets

K_{\alpha}:=\{\,z\in{[\omega]^{\omega}}:\forall{n\in\varphi[w_{\alpha}]}\exists{k\in\varphi^{-1}(n)}\,(z(k)\leq h(k))\,\}

are compact for all
\alpha<\kappa
.
For every
y\in Y
there exists
\alpha<\kappa
such that

\varphi[w_{\alpha}]\subseteq\varphi\bigl[[y\leq h]\bigr]
, and thus
y\in K_{\alpha}
.
It means that
Y\subseteq\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}K_{\alpha}
.

Let
U
be an arbitrary ultrafilter.
Since
\mathfrak{b}(U)=\mathfrak{d}
and each compact subspace of
[\omega]^{\omega}
is dominated by a single
element, the set
\bigcup_{\alpha\in\kappa}K_{\alpha}
is
\leq_{U}
-bounded,
and thus the set
Y
is
\leq_{U}
-bounded, too.
∎

7.
Comments and open problems

Remark 7.1
.

Lemma
4.2
shows that assuming
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{d}
, Hurewicz is equivalent to being
U
-Menger for all ultrafilters
U
.

The following questions arise naturally from our investigations.

Problem 7.2
.

Is it consistent that in the class of sets of reals:

(1)

There is a space which is productively
U
-Menger for all ultrafilters
U
which is not productively Scheepers?

(2)

There is a productively Scheepers space which is not productively
U
-Menger for some ultrafilter
U
?

(3)

There is a productively Menger space which is not productively Scheepers?

(4)

There is a productively Scheepers space which is not productively Menger?

Repovs–Zdomskyy
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
proved that, in the Laver model (where
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{d}
), any Hurewicz set of reals is productively Menger and also productively Hurewicz, in the class of sets reals.
However, their result does not capture the Scheepers property.

Problem 7.3
.

In the Laver, in the class of sets of reals (or hereditarily Lindelöf spaces), is any Hurewicz space productively Scheepers?

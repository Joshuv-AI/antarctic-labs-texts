Incidence, trend and distribution of transport-related injuries reported to health facilities in the Volta Region of Ghana, 2019 to 2023
Authors: Victor Zeng, Williams Azumah Abanga, Samuel Adolf Bosoka, Maxwell Afetor, Christopher Sunkwa Tamal, Gyesi Razak Issahaku, Clement Tetteh Narh, Frank Baiden, Chrysantus Kubio
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTransport injuries are a global public health concern, causing 1.35 million deaths and over 20 million non-fatal injuries annually. Low- and middle-income countries, particularly countries in Africa, are experiencing an increase in fatalities. Ghana and the Volta Region are not exempted from the burden, prompting targeted interventions. In the development of these interventions, epidemiological data analysis is needed to identify areas for action. This study aimed to analyze the distribution, trends, and incidence of transport-related injuries in the Volta Region of Ghana.MethodSecondary data was analyzed to describe transport-related injury cases and deaths in the Volta Region of Ghana from 2019 to 2023. Transport-related injuries were obtained from the monthly out-patient morbidity report form whereas transport related deaths were obtained from the inpatient morbidity and mortality, and cause of death report forms. Descriptive data analyses were performed and reported as frequencies, proportions, ratios, and choropleth maps.ResultOf the 33,415 transport-related injury cases reported between 2019 and 2023, 25 were fatal. The highest proportion of cases, 35.8% (35.3–36.4), was among persons aged 20–34 years. About 66.0% (65.5–66.5) of all the cases involved males. The cumulative incidence was 387 injuries per 100,000 population with the highest incidence recorded in 2021. The incidence of cases declined from 357 per 100,000 population in 2019 to 320 per 100,000 population in 2023. The death rate was stable over the period with the highest of 1.2 per 1000 recorded in 2019 and declined from 1.2 per 1000 in 2019 to 0.9 per 1000 in 2023. Most, 56% of the deaths were reported in district hospitals.ConclusionThe study showed a decreasing trend in the incidence of transport injuries, but a steady mortality rate in the Volta Region. Young adults were the highest affected group. Background

Transport injuries are a significant global public health concern, causing 1.35 million deaths and over 20 million non-fatal injuries [1] globally every year [2–5]. High-income countries have seen a decrease in transport injury fatalities, but low- and middle-income countries, particularly in Africa, are experiencing an increase [3, 6]. Over 90% of road traffic accident deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries, with pedestrians, cyclists, and motorcyclists being disproportionately affected [2, 7].

Africa has the highest road traffic fatality rate in the world, attributed to factors such as the rapid increase in vehicle ownership without corresponding improvements in road safety measures, poor road infrastructure and maintenance, lack of enforcement of traffic laws, and high numbers of pedestrians, cyclists, and motorcyclists on the roads [8]. Ghana is not exempt from this challenge, with road traffic crashes being one of the leading causes of death and disability [6, 9, 10]. Factors contributing to this include increasing motorization and urbanization, inadequate road infrastructure, weak enforcement of traffic regulations, and limited access to emergency trauma care [1, 6, 11].

The Volta Region of Ghana recorded over 193 road accidents and 48 fatalities in the first half of 2022 [12]. Motorcyclists are at a high risk of road traffic crashes in the region [13]. A study conducted in Ho and Hohoe showed that motorcycles caused more than half of all transport injury cases in the two municipalities [14].

Global efforts to address transport injuries have gained significant attention, with the United Nations declaring 2011–2020 as the Decade of Action for Road Safety and the Sustainable Development Goal 3.6 aiming to reduce road traffic deaths and injuries by 50% by 2020 [1, 3].

In Ghana, the Transport Ministry launched the “Stay Alive Road Safety Campaign” in 2021 to raise awareness and promote safe road use [15]. Despite the implementation of numerous interventions and progress made in improving positive outcomes, preventable accidents, casualties, and fatalities are recorded in the Volta Region [16]. The few published studies conducted in the region on transport injuries have mainly focused on the severity, impact, and prevalence [17, 18]. However, these studies have not explored the incidence, trends, and distribution of transport injuries. As transport injuries emerge as a significant non-communicable disease epidemic, it is crucial to describe their trends and distribution over time. This will provide valuable information for policymakers, public health officials, and local stakeholders to help address the dangers of transport injuries. This study aimed to describe the incidence, trend, and distribution of transport injuries in the Volta Region from 2019 to 2023.

Methods

Study site

This study was conducted in the Volta Region of Ghana. The region consists of 18 districts and has an estimated population of 1.7 million [19] (Fig. 1). The majority of residents reside in rural communities and work as farmers. There are various modes of transportation in the region, including cars, vans, buses, and motorbikes, popularly called ‘Okada’. The use of tricycles has become particularly popular in the Ho municipality and is commonly called ‘Cando’. The main reason for its introduction and acceptance by residents was the need to expand beyond traditional commercial vehicles, such as taxis [13, 20]. The region is connected by an extensive road network. Tarmac roads link Ho to Aflao, Ho to Juapong, Aflao to Keta, Keta to Sogakope and Accra in the south and Ho to Kpando. These towns are commercial hubs with large markets, resulting in highly trafficked roads.The National Highway N1 and N2 pass through the region [21]. There is also the Volta Lake which is used to transport people by the use of boats, canoes, and ferries. Access to healthcare in remote areas is affected by the presence of the Volta Lake and other water bodies in the region.

There are 557 health facilities in the region with the majority being Community-based Health Planning Service (CHPS) compounds. CHPS is a nationwide strategy to achieve primary health care. It provides vital community-based health services by collaborating with communities on planning and service delivery. Its main goal is to bring health services closer to the communities in under-privileged sub-districts. It is worth noting that six districts in this region do not have primary hospitals. However, in each of the districts, there is at least a CHPS compound or health center that has the capacity to manage transport injuries.

Data collection and processing

Secondary data was analyzed to describe transport injury cases and deaths in the Volta Region of Ghana for the years 2019 to 2023. Data were extracted from the District Health Information Management System (DHIMS2) and Events for the years 2019 to 2023. DHIMS2 is an open-source, web-based platform used by the Ghana Health Service (GHS). It contains electronically-integrated databases that capture data on a variety of diseases, including transport injuries and deaths. New cases of transport injuries are documented at the Outpatient Departments (OPDs) of health facilities and entered into DHIMS2 through the monthly OPD morbidity reporting form while the severe cases are admitted and reported through the in-patient morbidity and mortality report form in the event database. The transport injury cases were retrieved using the pivot table function under the data visualizer component of the DHIMS2 database whereas the deaths were extracted using the cause of death component of the database. The flow in the extraction of data from the DHIMS is described in Fig. 2. The pivot table has predefined variables such as type of data, period, and district. The collected variables for the study included the number of reported transport injury cases, deaths, age, sex, and the reporting districts of the cases and deaths. Data in the DHIMS2 does not include any patient identifiers.

Road traffic surveillance system in the Volta Region

For all transport injuries, once a case reports to the health facility, the data is collected and entered into the consulting room registers which are then collated and entered into the monthly OPD morbidity returns forms. Severe transport injury cases are admitted and captured into the inpatient morbidity and mortality register. These forms capture data in an aggregated form which is then entered into the DHIMS.

The health system notifies the Motor Traffic and Transport Unit (MTTU) of the Ghana Police Service for further investigation.

Data analysis

The extracted data was exported to Microsoft Excel 2019 for cleaning and analysis. The data was cleaned by removing blanks. It was then analyzed descriptively by person, place, and time. Choropleth maps were used to show the distribution of transport injuries and deaths by place using the Quantum Geographic Information System (QGIS) software version 3.30.

The incidences of transport injuries and deaths were calculated by the total number of recorded transport injuries and deaths in a year divided by the mid-year population. The incidences were reported per 100,000 population. Data was presented using, tables, and charts.

Ethical clearance

The Public Health Act, 2012, of Ghana requires the Ghana Health Service to maintain and update surveillance data for diseases that are prone to epidemics and public health events. As a result, no formal ethical approval was needed for this study because transport injuries and deaths are routinely reported into DHIMS-2 as part of the OPD and inpatient morbidity reports, and de-identified secondary data was used for the analysis. The Volta Regional Health Directorate granted administrative approval and access to the databases.

Results

Background characteristics of transport injuries and mortalities

Over the five years, there were 33,415 cases of transport injuries reported in health facilities across the region. The highest (35.8%, 11,979/33,415) proportion of cases were among persons aged between 20 and 34 years (Table 1). Males were predominantly the victims of transport injuries, contributing 66% (22,066/33,415) of the total cases (Table 1; Fig. 3). There were 25 related deaths. A higher proportion, 40% (10/25) of the deaths occurred among cases aged 20–34 years. The majority of transport injury deaths, 80% (20/25) occurred among males (Table 1).

Incidence of transport injuries and mortality rate

The region recorded an average incidence of 387 per 100,000 population. The incidence was highest in 2021 with 473 per 100,000 population and lowest in 2023 with 320 per 100,000 population (Fig. 4). There were 25 transport mortalities over the five-year period with an average mortality rate of 0.7 per 1,000 injuries (Table 2).

Discussion

This paper gives important data to support policy decisions. The study analyzed institutional transport injury cases and mortality data in the Volta Region from 2019 to 2023. The region experienced an average incidence of 387 transport injuries per 100,000 population over the five-year study period. This is, however, comparatively lower than the reported 940 and 425 transport injuries per 100,000 population in Nepal [22], and Thailand [23] respectively. The lower incidence of injuries observed in our study compared to Thailand and Nepal could be due to different injury-reporting mechanisms. For example, in Ghana including the Volta Region, it is possible that some road transport injury victims did not attend health facilities and were not captured in the surveillance system. This could have resulted in an underestimation of the incidence.

There was a 54.8% (51.4–58.4) decrease in the incidence of road traffic injuries between 2019 and 2023, with the highest incidence recorded in 2021 and the lowest in 2023. This differs from the increasing global trend of traffic injuries [5]. The decreasing trend is most likely the result of the National Road Safety Authority’s increased road safety initiatives, more traffic police personnel on the road, stronger enforcement of traffic laws and increased access to accredited Driving Training institutions may have accounted for road users adhering to traffic regulations [21, 24]. The successful implementation of the road safety initiative dubbed “Stay Alive Road Safety Campaign” launched in 2021 [15] may have contributed to the observed decline of transport injuries in the Volta Region. By ensuring that all users of the road adhered to the traffic regulations, the Ghana Police Service in collaboration with other organizations, such as the Driver Vehicle and Licensing Authority, may have also played a role in the decrease.

The mortality rate remains a concern with an average of 0.7 per 1,000 with a decreasing trend over the study period. This is lower than the average transport injury deaths in Africa, 27 deaths per 1000 population [8]. This study’s findings support other published studies that have shown a global decline in the number of transport-related deaths [6]. The low fatality rate and decreasing trend could be a result of underestimation by the health institutions since persons who die on the spot after transport accidents are sent straight to the morgue and not the OPD. Furthermore, the inability of some victims to afford medical services could prevent them from going to hospitals and end up dying at home from their injuries which will not be documented at OPD. This is possible because six districts do not have primary hospitals that could manage severe transport injuries; hence, all severe injuries are referred to nearby districts with hospitals. Nevertheless, recent analysis by WHO [2] and James et al., [7] demonstrate that death rates from transport injuries in Sub-Saharan Africa are still higher than anywhere else in the world, and this differs from the current finding that shows that transport injury deaths have declined in the Volta Region of Ghana.

Young adults were the most affected group accounting for over a third of all cases suggesting potential behavioral or occupational risk factors. This finding corroborates other studies in the Nepal, Tanzania and the United States of America [5, 6, 25] which found that young adults aged 15–34 years had an increased risk of road traffic injury. According to Ashifur et al., [25]. drivers within this age range have the highest fatal road crash involvement in the US. A study in Bongo, Upper East Region of Ghana, revealed that approximately 60% of commercial drivers who were involved in road transport injuries were aged between 18 and 34 years. Numerous elements impact the actions of young and inexperienced drivers [26]. These variables include long travel time, the poor nature of the road, over speeding, drunk driving, and non-compliance with safety measures. Some additional factors, such as the bad influence of friends and technological devices, and the desire to impress are among the features of young drivers that influence crash risk [26, 27].

This study revealed that males were typically the victims of road transport deaths. The rates of both injury-related deaths were about three times higher among men than in women in both LMICs and HICs in 2012, and the gender disparity has persisted over the past decade [8, 23, 27]. This may be due to societal stereotyping as driving is perceived as a masculine job and young males are opting for driving as a profession compared to females [27].

Agotime-Ziope District had the highest incidence of transport injuries while South Tongu District had the highest mortality rates. Agotime Ziope is on the Ho Aflao highway while South Tongu district is on the Accra Aflao highway. These two international roads link to Lome the capital of Togo. These roads are busy roads with vehicular traffic and are accident prone. These findings highlight critical areas for focused road safety campaigns. On the other hand, 12 districts reported no deaths, suggesting potential differences in health facilities, traffic patterns, or healthcare access across the region. According to the Annual reports of the Volta Regional Health Directorate, there are districts in the region that do not have district hospitals and one of such districts is Agortime-Ziope. This means that severe transport injury cases that may need surgery are transported to districts with hospitals that can manage these cases [16].

Implications of the findings

The study highlights the need for age-specific road safety efforts, particularly in high-risk districts like Agortime-Ziope and South Tongu, to reduce traffic injuries among young adults. Initiatives should encourage safe driving practices on traffic safety and neighborhood-specific traffic laws. The study also emphasizes the need for better trauma care and emergency response services, such as ambulance services and trauma units, and funding for rapid-response systems. Efforts should be made to provide hospitals for districts such as Agortime-Ziope which are without hospitals to enable them manage transport injuries. Public health services should train health staff on trauma management skills and data collection and management.

The MTTU of the Ghana Police Service should ensure the enforcement of all road traffic regulations. This can involve stricter enforcement of speed limit compliance, driver licensing, and vehicle condition monitoring. The National Road Safty Authority (NRSA) should collaborate with schools, and community organizations to teach young people about safe driving practices.

Collaboration with other sectors, such as Ministry of Transport, Ghana Police Serice, and the District Assemblies, is crucial for a comprehensive approach to reducing transport-related injuries in the Volta Region. These approaches will ensure a thorough plan that considers enforcement, policy, education, and infrastructure.

Strengths and limitations

This study offers important information about the incidence of transport injuries and deaths throughout the Volta Region. However, the findings of this study might not be a true depiction of transport-related injuries and fatalities in the Volta Region since it depended on institutional data that might have been under-reported. In addition, there are limitations about the victims of the traffic injury in the DHIMS-2 database which limited the analysis of the results.

The data did not specify whether cases were knockdowns, or the victims were drivers/riders themselves which will help prescribe targeted interventions. Despite these, the menace of road traffic injuries is enormous and may benefit from integrated and holistic interventions and approaches. However, there is the need for community-based studies to gauge the level of completeness of facilities reports and a morgue-based study to estimate mortality from accidents and injuries.

Conclusion

The study revealed a gradual decrease in the incidence of transport-related injuries in the Volta Region over the years. Nevertheless, the situation is still a cause for concern. The road traffic injury rate was steady and young adults accounted for the highest proportion. Transport-related injuries occurred in all districts across the region. However, Agortime-Ziope and South Tongu Districts had the highest incidence and mortality rates respectively. Health authorities should collaborate with the transport ministry and other stakeholders to increase campaigns on adherence to road safety measures and behaviors to further reduce the occurrence of preventable transport injuries and fatalities. Further research studies are needed to determine the factors contributing to these transport injuries in the region.

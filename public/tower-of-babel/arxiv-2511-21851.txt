ADE Minimal Strings and Multi-Matrix Duals
Victor A. Rodriguez, ​​, Mykhaylo Usatyuk, ​​, Zi-Yue Wang

Abstract
We revisit ADE minimal string theories, focusing on the D- and E-series minimal models coupled to Liouville theory. Unlike the A-series, whose duals are solvable two-matrix models, these theories are conjectured to correspond to unsolvable four-matrix integrals. We compute sphere four‑point and torus one‑point amplitudes in the AMS, DMS, and EMS via direct numerical integration over moduli space, confirming/disproving some known results and providing new data where matrix‑model predictions are unavailable. From amplitudes with conformal boundaries, we find evidence for multi-matrix structure in the D-series, including scaled ramp behavior in cylinder diagrams and deviations from the ZZ-instanton sector of two-matrix models. We also perform a preliminary positivity bootstrap to constrain critical points of the multi-matrix models relevant to the DMS string.

2
The worldsheet theory

In this section, we briefly review the definition of the minimal string worldsheet CFT.
We begin by recalling the bootstrap solution of Liouville CFT and specify the conventions we will follow throughout the paper.
Next, we describe the A,D, and E series of the minimal model CFTs.
We then combine these ingredients to present the critical background of minimal string theory.

2.1
Liouville CFT

Liouville CFT is a family of non-perturbative solutions to the two-dimensional conformal bootstrap crossing equations, that exists for all complex values of the central charge
c
, except those in the interval
(-\infty,1]
.
3
3

3

Along this interval, a distinct solution exists, commonly referred to as “timelike” Liouville CFT. We will not make use of this theory in this paper.

Following standard convention in the literature, we parametrize the central charge of Liouville CFT as

\displaystyle c=1+6Q^{2}~,\quad Q=b+b^{-1}~,

(3)

where
b\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{i\mathbb{R}\}
.

Liouville CFT has a diagonal spectrum consisting of a continuum of scalar Virasoro primary operators, denoted by
V_{P}
, with holomorphic and anti-holomorphic conformal weights
h_{P}=\tilde{h}_{P}=\frac{c-1}{24}+P^{2}
. The parameter
P
is commonly referred to as the Liouville momentum.
Liouville CFT with central charge
c>1
and with
P\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
is a unitary conformal field theory. However, as we will discuss shortly, it is possible and often convenient to analytically continue correlation functions of the theory to arbitrary values of the Liouville momenta.

The three-point structure constants of Liouville CFT, denoted by
C_{b}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})
, are defined by

\displaystyle\langle V_{P_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})V_{P_{2}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})V_{P_{3}}(z_{3},\bar{z}_{3})\rangle=\frac{C_{b}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})}{|z_{12}|^{2(h_{1}+h_{2}-h_{3})}|z_{13}|^{2(h_{1}+h_{3}-h_{2})}|z_{23}|^{2(h_{2}+h_{3}-h_{1})}}~,

(4)

where we introduced the short-hand notation
z_{ij}\equiv z_{i}-z_{j}
and
h_{j}\equiv h_{P_{j}}
. These structure constants were bootstrapped by
48
;
125
;
117
;
116
and are given by the celebrated DOZZ formula.
In this paper, we will adopt a normalization convention for the Liouville vertex operators
V_{P}
such that the structure constants take the following form (see for example
36
, whose convention we follow)

\displaystyle C_{b}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})=\frac{\Gamma_{b}(2Q)\Gamma_{b}(\frac{Q}{2}\pm iP_{1}\pm iP_{2}\pm iP_{3})}{\sqrt{2}\Gamma_{b}(Q)^{3}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\Gamma_{b}(Q\pm 2iP_{j})}~.

(5)

The notation
\pm

5
) indicates a product over all possible sign choices. For instance, in (
5

\Gamma_{b}
.
Here, the double gamma function
\Gamma_{b}(z)
is a meromorphic function that may be defined as the unique function that satisfies the periodicity functional relations

\displaystyle\Gamma_{b}(z+b)=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}b^{bz-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma(bz)}\Gamma_{b}(z)~,\quad\quad\Gamma_{b}(z+b^{-1})=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}b^{-b^{-1}z+\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma(b^{-1}z)}\Gamma_{b}(z)~,

(6)

with a normalization
\Gamma_{b}(\frac{Q}{2})=1

\displaystyle z=-mb-nb^{-1}~,\quad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}~.

(7)

Note that for rational

, these poles may overlap yielding higher-order poles.

\Gamma_{b}
admits both an integral representation and — more relevant for the purposes of this paper — an infinite product formula that enables efficient numerical evaluation for complex values of
z
and

The code accompanying this paper includes an implementation of this infinite product formula.

The two-point function on the plane may be obtained from (

\displaystyle\langle V_{P_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})V_{P_{1}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\rangle=\frac{1}{|z_{12}|^{2h_{1}}}\frac{1}{\rho_{b}(P)}\left(\delta(P_{1}-P_{2})+\delta(P_{1}+P_{2})\right)~,

(8)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{b}(P)=4\sqrt{2}\sinh(2\pi bP)\sinh(2\pi b^{-1}P)~.

(9)

This data — namely, the operator spectrum together with its structure constants — is sufficient to calculate any

Let us describe the two simplest nontrivial correlation functions that we will study in detail in this paper.
The sphere four-point function may be decomposed as

\displaystyle\langle V_{P_{1}}(0)V_{P_{2}}(z,\bar{z})V_{P_{3}}(1)V^{\prime}_{P_{4}}(\infty)\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}}\!\!\text{d}P\rho_{b}(P)C_{b}(P_{1},P_{2},P)C_{b}(P_{3},P_{4},P)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{F}^{(b)}_{0,4}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4};P;z)\mathcal{F}^{(b)}_{0,4}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4};P;\bar{z})~,

Here,

\displaystyle\langle V_{P_{1}}(0)\rangle_{T^{2}(\tau)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}}\!\!\text{d}P\rho_{b}(P)C_{b}(P_{1},P,P)\mathcal{F}^{(b)}_{1,1}(P_{1};P;\tau)\mathcal{F}^{(b)}_{1,1}(P_{1};P;\bar{\tau})~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1,1}^{(b)}(P_{1};P|\tau)=q^{h_{P}-\frac{c}{24}}\left(\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}\right)\mathcal{H}^{(b)}_{1,1}(P_{1};P|\tau)\,,

Correlation functions in Liouville CFT, such as (

. However, their analytic structure — inherited from that of the DOZZ structure constants and the integral over intermediate momenta in the operator product expansion (OPE) — allows for their continuation to more general complex values of the momenta

2.2
Minimal Model CFTs

Let us now describe the three families of minimal model CFTs. These theories are labeled according to the ADE classification and are commonly referred to in the literature by the Dynkin diagram associated to each family. Each class corresponds to a distinct series of consistent two-dimensional rational CFTs, characterized by a finite spectrum of primary operators and specific fusion rules. In the following, we briefly review their defining CFT data. For a recent overview, see
100
.

Minimal models only exist for discrete values of
c_{\mathrm{m}}\leqslant 1
and are characterized by two co-prime positive integers
(p,p^{\prime})
which have additional restrictions depending on if the model is of A, D, or E type. In all types, the
(p,p^{\prime})
model is equivalent to the
(p^{\prime},p)
model.
Their spectra are compactly summarized through degenerate Virasoro representations
\mathcal{R}_{r,s}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
which are labeled by integers
r,s,r^{\prime},s^{\prime}
and only exist for special values of
c_{\mathrm{m}}
. The central charge, the holomorphic and the anti-holomorphic conformal weights of a primary operator in this representation are given by

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{m}}

\displaystyle=1-6\frac{(p-p^{\prime})^{2}}{pp^{\prime}}=1-6(\beta-\beta^{-1})^{2}~,\qquad\beta^{2}=\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}\,,

(13)

\displaystyle h_{r,s}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}}-1}{24}+P_{r,s}^{2}\,,\qquad P_{r,s}=\frac{1}{2}(r\beta^{-1}-s\beta)\,,

(14)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}}-1}{24}+P_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}^{2}\,.

(15)

The degenerate representation has a holomorphic null-descendant at level
rs
, and an anti-holomorphic null-descendant at level
r^{\prime}s^{\prime}
.

2.2.1
A-series

The first class of minimal model CFTs is the A-series parametrized by any co-prime positive integers
(p,p^{\prime})
with central charge (
13
) and Dynkin diagram
(A_{p-1},A_{p^{\prime}-1})
. The spectrum is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{p,p^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{A}\text{-series }}=\frac{1}{2}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=1\end{subarray}}^{p-1}\bigoplus_{s=1}^{p^{\prime}-1}\mathcal{R}_{r,s}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{r,s}~,

(16)

The representations are equivalent under
\mathcal{R}_{r,s}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{r,s}\equiv\mathcal{R}_{p-r,p^{\prime}-s}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{p-r,p^{\prime}-s}
and are double-counted in the sum, which is corrected by the overall factor of
\frac{1}{2}
, resulting in
\frac{1}{2}(p-1)(p^{\prime}-1)
primary fields. We denote Virasoro primaries in these representations by
\widehat{V}_{r,s}
subject to the same
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
identification
\widehat{V}_{r,s}\equiv\widehat{V}_{p-r,p^{\prime}-s}
. There are many choices of independent representatives. One possible choice makes use of the identity
p^{\prime}(p-r)-p(p^{\prime}-s)=-(p^{\prime}r-ps)
, allowing us to restrict to
(r,s)
satisfying, for example,
p^{\prime}r-ps<0
. Another, more explicit, possible choice is:

\displaystyle(p\text{ even},p^{\prime}\text{ odd})\qquad r=1,2,\ldots,p-1\,,\qquad s=1,3,\ldots,p^{\prime}-2,~

(17)

\displaystyle(p\text{ odd},p^{\prime}\text{ even})\qquad r=1,3,\ldots,p-2\,,\qquad s=1,2,\ldots,p^{\prime}-1~,

(18)

\displaystyle(p\text{ odd},p^{\prime}\text{ odd})\qquad r=1,2,\ldots,p-1\,,\qquad s=1,3,\ldots,p^{\prime}-2\,.

(19)

The conformal weights of these primary vertex operators are
h_{r,s}={\tilde{h}}_{r,s}=\frac{c_{\mathrm{m}}-1}{24}+P_{r,s}^{2}
as in (
14
).

Fusion rules and structure constants.

The fusion rules are specified by

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{r_{1},s_{1}}\times\widehat{V}_{r_{2},s_{2}}=\sum_{r_{3}\overset{2}{=}|r_{1}-r_{2}|+1}^{\text{min}(r_{1}+r_{2},2p-r_{1}-r_{2})-1}~~\sum_{s_{3}\overset{2}{=}|s_{1}-s_{2}|+1}^{\text{min}(s_{1}+s_{2},2p^{\prime}-s_{1}-s_{2})-1}\widehat{V}_{r_{3},s_{3}}~.

(20)

Here, (
20
) is understood as a concise encoding of the fusion rules, rather than as an explicit operator product expansion (OPE), which would in particular include contributions from Virasoro descendants.
5
5

5

The operators are subject to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
identification, but the fusion rule above allows us to input any two operators on the LHS without imposing this identification. The output on the RHS is not double-counted: if the fusion produces an operator
\widehat{V}_{r_{3},s_{3}}
, its identified counterpart
\widehat{V}_{p-r_{3},\,p^{\prime}-s_{3}}
does not appear separately.
Let us encode this fusion rule in an indicator function
f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}\in\{0,1\}
which equals one if the representation labeled by
(r_{3},s_{3})
appears on the RHS of the fusion, and zero otherwise.
A more explicit factorized form is

\displaystyle f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}=f^{p}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}f^{p^{\prime}}_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}+f^{p}_{r_{1},r_{2},p-r_{3}}f^{p^{\prime}}_{s_{1},s_{2},p^{\prime}-s_{3}}~,\quad\text{with}\vphantom{\frac{1}{1}}

(21)

\displaystyle f_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}^{p}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }r_{3}=|r_{1}-r_{2}|+1+2\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0},~r_{3}\leqslant r_{1}+r_{2}-1,~r_{3}\leqslant 2p-1-r_{1}-r_{2}\,,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

(22)

The reason for the two terms above is to take into account the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
identification of operators.

The three-point function on the sphere takes the following form,

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{V}_{r_{1},s_{1}}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\widehat{V}_{r_{2},s_{2}}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\widehat{V}_{r_{3},s_{3}}(z_{3},\bar{z}_{3})\rangle=\frac{C^{\mathrm{A}}_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}\times f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}}{|z_{12}|^{2(h_{1}+h_{2}-h_{3})}|z_{13}|^{2(h_{1}+h_{3}-h_{2})}|z_{23}|^{2(h_{2}+h_{3}-h_{1})}}~,

(23)

where now

.
We have separated the coefficient into two pieces:
f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}
, an indicator function that enforces the fusion rules of the vertex operators, and
C^{\mathrm{A}}_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}
, which we refer to as the structure constant.
6
6

6

Note that the structure constant

may be nonzero for vertex operators that do not satisfy the fusion rule; nevertheless, the corresponding three-point function vanishes

;

It is well-known that the A-series structure constant can be expressed in terms of a function analogous to the (time-like) DOZZ formula. In fact, in this paper we find it convenient to work in a convention such that this structure constant is precisely equal to the time-like DOZZ structure constant, in the form given in
36
:

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{A}}_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{C}_{\beta}(P_{r_{1},s_{1}},P_{r_{2},s_{2}},P_{r_{3},s_{3}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}\Gamma_{\beta}(\beta+\beta^{-1})^{3}}{\Gamma_{\beta}(2(\beta+\beta^{-1}))}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{3}\Gamma_{\beta}(\beta+\beta^{-1}\pm 2P_{r_{j},s_{j}})}{\Gamma_{\beta}(\frac{\beta+\beta^{-1}}{2}\pm P_{r_{1},s_{1}}\pm P_{r_{2},s_{2}}\pm P_{r_{3},s_{3}})}~.

(24)

As a reminder, the notation
\pm
means a product over all possible signs.
The function (
2.2.1
) is real-valued for real

Furthermore, this convention simplifies the explicit expressions for the various normalization constants associated with the on-shell string vertex operators, as well as other normalization constants in the string path integral.
More importantly, we expect these conventions to help clarify the relation between the ADE minimal string theories and the more recent Virasoro minimal string and complex Liouville string.
Additional details on the relation between the conventions in (

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{V}^{\prime}_{r,s}(\infty)\widehat{V}_{r,s}(0)\rangle=d(\beta)^{2}\frac{\rho_{b}(iP_{r,s})}{(iP_{r,s})^{2}}~,

Let us now combine the ingredients of the preceding sections in order to form the worldsheet string theories (

The first elementary condition is that the total central charge of the worldsheet CFT (

(r_{j},s_{j})
.
For the D-series minimal string, we further decorate the label
P^{X}_{r_{j},s_{j}}
with
X=D
or
N
to indicate whether the tachyon vertex operator is built from a primary in the diagonal or non-diagonal sector of the minimal model CFT.
In (
50
),
\mathcal{B}_{k},\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{k}
denote the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic
\mathfrak{b}
-ghost insertions associated with the moduli of the Riemann surface
\Sigma_{g,n}
, while
C^{(b)}_{\Sigma_{g}}
denotes the normalization constants of the string path integral on a genus-
g
Riemann surface
\Sigma_{g}

97
.

Lastly, we note that the constant (
\beta
-dependent) prefactors
d(\beta)^{-2}
, appearing for example in (
2.2.1
), can be consistently absorbed into the normalization of the on-shell string vertex operators
\mathcal{N}^{(b)}_{r,s}
and the string path integral normalization constants
C^{(b)}_{\Sigma_{g}}
.
To see this, note that an
n
-point string amplitude at genus
g
, when expressed in terms of a chosen conformal block decomposition, depends on these normalization factors as
C_{\Sigma_{g}}^{(b)}\big(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}^{(b)}_{r_{j},s_{j}}\big)\left(d^{-2}\right)^{\#\text{edges}}
. Here,
\#
edges denotes the number of internal lines in the conformal block decomposition, i.e. the number of internal Liouville momenta to be integrated. This number is equal to
3g-3+n
. Thus, the total prefactor can be rewritten as as
\big(C_{\Sigma_{g}}^{(b)}(d^{-2})^{3g-3}\big)\big(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}^{(b)}_{r_{j},s_{j}}d^{-2}\big)
.
Therefore, we see that the dependence on
d(\beta)
can be fully reabsorbed into a redefinition of
\mathcal{N}^{(b)}_{r,s}
and
C_{\Sigma_{g}}^{(b)}
.

3
Perturbative string amplitudes

3.1
Sphere three-point diagram

The simplest nontrivial amplitude to consider is the three-point sphere diagram, which requires no moduli integration. The amplitude is then proportional to the product of the three-point structure constants of the constituent CFTs.

A-series.

We obtain
107

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{(b),\,\text{A}}_{0,3}

\displaystyle(P_{r_{1},s_{1}},P_{r_{2},s_{2}},P_{r_{3},s_{3}})

\displaystyle=C^{(b)}_{\text{S}^{2}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{N}^{(b)}_{r_{j},s_{j}}\Big)C^{\mathrm{A}}_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}C_{b}(iP_{r_{1},s_{1}},iP_{r_{2},s_{2}},iP_{r_{3},s_{3}})f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}

\displaystyle=C^{(b)}_{\text{S}^{2}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{N}^{(b)}_{r_{j},s_{j}}\Big)\widehat{C}_{b}(P_{r_{1},s_{1}},P_{r_{2},s_{2}},P_{r_{3},s_{3}})C_{b}(iP_{r_{1},s_{1}},iP_{r_{2},s_{2}},iP_{r_{3},s_{3}})f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}

\displaystyle=C^{(b)}_{\text{S}^{2}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{N}^{(b)}_{r_{j},s_{j}}\Big)f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}~,

(51)

where we used the fact that the timelike DOZZ structure constant
\widehat{C}_{b}
is, up to a factor of
i
in its arguments, the reciprocal of the spacelike DOZZ structure constant
C_{b}
. Note that this is precisely the same cancellation that occurs in the Virasoro minimal string
36
.
As is standard in the minimal string theory literature, we next fix the arbitrary on-shell string normalization constants by requiring that the three-point amplitude is equal to the indicator function enforcing the fusion rule,

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{(b),\,\text{A}}_{0,3}(P_{r_{1},s_{1}},P_{r_{2},s_{2}},P_{r_{3},s_{3}})\overset{!}{=}f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}~.

(52)

This in particular implies that
\mathcal{N}^{(b)}_{r,s}=\mathcal{N}^{(b)}
is independent of the external momenta
P_{r,s}
(or the labels
(r,s)
) and that

\displaystyle C^{(b)}_{\text{S}^{2}}=\big(\mathcal{N}^{(b)}\big)^{-3}~.

(53)

D-series.

The D-series proceeds in the same way. As discussed in (
49
), there are two kinds of tachyon operators, each originating from the diagonal and non-diagonal sectors, respectively.
Tachyons in the diagonal sector have precisely the same three-point amplitude as in the A-series. Thus, we arrive at the same conclusion as in the preceding paragraph: We normalize the three-point amplitude to be exactly the indicator function that enforces the fusion rule,
\mathsf{N}^{(b),\,\text{D}}_{0,3}(P^{D}_{r_{1},s_{1}},P^{D}_{r_{2},s_{2}},P^{D}_{r_{3},s_{3}})\overset{!}{=}f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}
, and we arrive at the same condition (
53
) relating the sphere string path integral normalization and the D-series vertex operator normalization constants.
The other nonzero three-point amplitudes involve one diagonal and two non-diagonal tachyon on-shell states. As shown in (
49
), the latter have
r=\frac{p}{2}
and thus
\widetilde{P}_{r,s}=P_{p-r,s}=P_{r,s}
.
Thus, the relevant minimal model structure constant
C^{\text{D},\,DNN}_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}
reduces to the timelike Liouville structure constant, up to a sign factor
(-1)^{\frac{r_{1}-1}{2}}
as seen from (
2.2.2
). We therefore obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{(b),\,\text{D}}_{0,3}(P^{D}_{r_{1},s_{1}},P^{N}_{r_{2},s_{2}},P^{N}_{r_{3},s_{3}})=C^{(b)}_{\text{S}^{2}}\mathcal{N}^{(b)}\Big(\prod_{j=2}^{3}\mathcal{N}^{(b),N}_{r_{j},s_{j}}\Big)(-1)^{\frac{r_{1}-1}{2}}\times f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}~,

(54)

where we have used the fact that the normalization for diagonal vertex operators
\mathcal{N}^{(b)}
is independent of the momenta. In the present case, we will find it convenient then to choose a normalization convention such that the amplitude is

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{(b),\,\text{D}}_{0,3}(P^{D}_{r_{1},s_{1}},P^{N}_{r_{2},s_{2}},P^{N}_{r_{3},s_{3}})\overset{!}{=}(-1)^{\frac{r_{1}-1}{2}}\times f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}~.

(55)

With this choice, we arrive at the condition that the arbitrary normalization constants for on-shell string vertex operators in the non-diagonal sector
\mathcal{N}^{(b),N}_{r,s}=\mathcal{N}^{(b)}
are also independent of the momenta. Additionally, we arrive at the same condition (
53
) relating these vertex operator normalizations to the sphere string path integral normalization.

E-series.

For the
(12,p^{\prime})
E-series note that the only scalar vertex operators come from the Diagonal sector (
2.3.1
), unlike in the D-series.
Furthermore, the three-point structure constant of these diagonal operators is equal to the timelike DOZZ formula multiplied by the fusion rule indicator function, up to a multiplicative constant factor
c
arising from the constant prefactor in (
187
) and (
207
).
We demand the three-point function be proportional to the indicator function

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}^{(b),\,\text{E}}_{0,3}(P^{D}_{r_{1},s_{1}},P^{D}_{r_{2},s_{2}},P^{D}_{r_{3},s_{3}})\overset{!}{=}c_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}\times f_{r_{1},s_{1};r_{2},s_{2};r_{3},s_{3}}~,

(56)

which gives (
53
).

Liouville integration contour.

We must specify the contour of integration
\mathcal{C}
for the Liouville one-point function in (
3.2
).
When the external momenta is real
P_{\text{ext}}\in\mathbb{R}
, the corresponding operator lies within the spectrum of Liouville theory, and the contour over intermediate states is simply
P\in\mathbb{R_{\geqslant 0}}
. If desired, we may extend this contour to the full real line due to the reflection symmetry of the DOZZ structure constant.
However, in the context of minimal string theory, as discussed in section
2.3.1
, dressing the minimal model fields requires taking
P_{\text{ext}}\in i\mathbb{R}
, which lies outside the original spectrum of Liouville theory.
The correlation functions for such imaginary external momenta are defined via analytic continuation from real values of
P_{\text{ext}}
.
This can be achieved by starting with
P_{\text{ext}}\in\mathbb{R}
with the contour along the real axis and smoothly continuing
P_{\text{ext}}\to iP_{r,s}
into the complex plane.

In this continuation, for certain values of
iP_{r,s}
, poles of the integrand arising from the DOZZ structure constants may cross the contour over intermediate states.
These residues must be included, resulting in additional discrete contributions to the Liouville correlation function.
These poles in the integrand of the Liouville correlator arise from the double gamma functions
\Gamma_{b}(z)
, which have simple poles at
z=-mb^{-1}-nb
,
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. This implies
C_{b}(P_{\text{ext}},P,P)
in the integrand has an infinite set of poles in the
P
plane located at

Figure 1
:
Integration contour (red) for the Liouville torus one-point function. As we analytically continue the external momenta to be purely imaginary poles in the integrand can cross the contour and their residues must be included. In the figure we have drawn a case where no poles cross.

\displaystyle(\pm)P=(\pm)\frac{P_{\rm ext}}{2}-\frac{i}{2}(m+\frac{1}{2})b^{-1}-\frac{i}{2}(n+\frac{1}{2})b\,,

(60)

where this equation should be read as four equations with all possible sign choices. For fixed
m,n
, we have four poles, two above and two below the real axis. As we analytically continue
P_{\text{ext}}\to\pm iP_{r,s}=\pm\frac{i}{2}(rb^{-1}-sb)
two of the poles move towards the real axis, while two move away. This is true regardless the choice of sign of
P_{r,s}
, which only determines which of the four poles move towards or away from the real axis. When a pole crosses the real axis, we pick up its residue, see figure
1
. Due to the
P\to-P
symmetry of the integrand (
11
) the residue of the pole crossing from above the real axis is identical to the pole crossing from below. We thus focus on the poles that potentially cross from below, the location of these poles at the endpoint of the analytic continuation is

\displaystyle P_{m,n,\pm}=\pm\frac{i}{4}(rb^{-1}-sb)-i\frac{2m+1}{4}b^{-1}-i\frac{2n+1}{4}b\in i\mathbb{R}\,,

(61)

if either of
P_{m,n,\pm}\in i\mathbb{R}_{>0}
the associated pole has crossed the integration contour and we arrive at the correct contour for the one-point function

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}\text{d}P=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}P+2\sum_{P_{m,n,\pm}\in\text{pole crossing}}(-2\pi i)\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{P=P_{m,n,\pm}}\right)\,.

(62)

The factor of two is from the upper pole crossing, and the sign is due to the contour orientation.

Regularization of divergent amplitudes.

When a pole crosses the real axis, the resulting discrete residue contribution can render the string amplitude divergent. For example, the torus one-point moduli integral (
3.2
) behaves as

\displaystyle\int_{F_{0}}\text{d}^{2}\tau\,(q\overline{q})^{2P_{m,n,\pm}^{2}}q^{P_{\text{int}}^{2}}\bar{q}^{\widetilde{P}_{\text{int}}^{2}}(1+\mathcal{O}(q\overline{q}))~,

(63)

where
P_{\text{int}}
and
\widetilde{P}_{\text{int}}
denote the left and right moving (discrete) Liouville momenta of an intermediate state in the minimal model CFT sector, and
q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\tau}
.
We see that (
63
) diverges at large imaginary
\tau
whenever the exponent has a negative real part. In practice, these divergences arise with increasing frequency once we move beyond the
(2,p)
models, where the dressing momenta tend to lie deeper in the complex plane.

Divergences of this kind are familiar in string theory and have been consistently addressed in various contexts (see for example
22
;
120
;
109
;
110
;
53
;
11
). In our case, we will regularize the divergence by implementing the following replacement rule,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}t\,\mathrm{e}^{at}\rightarrow-\frac{1}{a}

(64)

even in cases where
a>0
.
For the moduli space integral over the fundamental domain
F_{0}
, this prescription can be conveniently implemented by integrating over an “inverse” fundamental domain

\displaystyle\int_{F_{0}}\text{d}^{2}\tau~(\ldots)\to-\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{d}\tau_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{\sqrt{1-\tau_{1}^{2}}}\text{d}\tau_{2}~(\ldots)\,.

(65)

We use this regularization in the direct numerical evaluation of the torus one-point diagram presented below.

Comparison to the Matrix Model

The only result for matrix model torus one-point string amplitudes we are aware of beyond the A-series
(p,2)
minimal string is
113

(p,3)

For the
(p,3)

113

\displaystyle\text{$(p,3)$ MM:}~~~T_{r,1}=1+2pr-3r^{2}\,.

(70)

This formula does not match our numerical results.
Specifically, we find that it holds for small values

The cases in which our amplitude disagrees with the result of
113

Including this pole is crucial for modular invariance.

We have found the following formula from inspecting the numerical data for the

\displaystyle(p,3)\text{ conjecture:}\qquad T_{r,1}=\begin{cases}1+2pr-3r^{2}&\text{when }2p-3r\leqslant 0\\

(71)

which agrees with all the numerical results. Note the case
2p-3r>0
is when there are pole crossings. It would be interesting to derive this formula from the matrix model.

3.3
Sphere
n
-point amplitudes

The general sphere
n
-point amplitude is not analytically computable in any of the models under consideration.
We begin by outlining several simplifications and structural relations among the amplitudes.
We then present direct numerical results for several sphere four-point amplitudes in ADE minimal string theories.

Relation between AMS and DMS sphere amplitudes.

A subset of sphere amplitudes involving tachyons in the
(p,p^{\prime})
DMS are equivalent to similar sphere amplitude in the
(p,p^{\prime})
AMS. A general amplitude for the AMS and DMS on any Riemann surface will have identical Liouville and ghost contributions, and therefore the problem reduces to comparing minimal model correlation functions on the surface.

On the sphere (i.e. at tree-level), there are no intermediate states running in loops, and so the problem reduces to examining the OPE of the external tachyons to determine if the amplitudes differ. The fusion rules for diagonal fields are identical in the A- and D-series.
15
15

15

The fusion rules of the D-series spectrum form a closed subset of those of the A-series spectrum.
The structure constants are also identical for these fields, as discussed in section
2.2
. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}_{0,n}^{(b),\,\mathrm{DMS}}(P^{D}_{r_{1},s_{1}},\ldots,P^{D}_{r_{n},s_{n}})=\mathsf{N}_{0,n}^{(b),\,\mathrm{AMS}}(P_{r_{1},s_{1}},\ldots,P_{r_{n},s_{n}})~.

(72)

Conservation of diagonality at any genus.

Now let us consider the special case with non-diagonal tachyons
\mathcal{T}^{N}_{\frac{p}{2},s}
in the DMS. First, recall that the D-series fusion rules obey a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
structure:

\displaystyle D\times D=D,~~~D\times N=N,~~~N\times N=D.

(73)

Consequently, there must be an even number of non-diagonal insertions otherwise the correlator on an arbitrary surface
\Sigma_{g}
vanishes

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}_{g,n}^{(b),\,\mathrm{DMS}}\big(\underbrace{P^{N}_{\frac{p}{2},s_{1}},\ldots,P^{N}_{\frac{p}{2},s_{k}}}_{\text{odd number}},P^{D}_{r_{k+1},s_{k+1}},\ldots,P^{D}_{r_{n},s_{n}}\big)=0~.

(74)

This can be seen through the conformal block decomposition of the amplitude. An amplitude on a general surface can be decomposed into three-holed spheres, where on each sphere one hole is an external state while the other two are intermediate states. This decomposition makes it clear that inserting intermediate states always introduces an even number of non-diagonal states. With an odd number of external tachyons
\mathcal{T}^{N}
the conformal block decomposition must consequently have an odd
+
even number of non-diagonal indices in the sum over structure constants
C^{NDD}\ldots C^{\ldots}
. Since the only non-vanishing structure constant involving
\mathcal{T}^{N}
fields is
C^{DNN}
the product of structure constants with an odd number of labels
N
will vanish.

Sphere four-point amplitudes.

We now turn to the direct worldsheet calculation of sphere four-point amplitudes in the ADE minimal string theories.
Our procedure closely parallels the torus one-point amplitude calculation presented in the previous section.
On the sphere, three of the four on-shell vertex operator insertions can be fixed using the conformal Killing group, leaving the position of the fourth operator as the nontrivial modulus to be integrated over.
The amplitude in any ADE minimal string then takes the general form

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}_{0,4}^{(b)}

\displaystyle(P^{X_{1}}_{r_{1},s_{1}},P^{X_{2}}_{r_{2},s_{2}},P^{X_{3}}_{r_{3},s_{3}},P^{X_{4}}_{r_{4},s_{4}})

\displaystyle=C^{(b)}_{S^{2}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{4}\mathcal{N}^{(b)}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{C}}\text{d}^{2}z\,\Big\langle V_{iP_{r,s}}(0)V_{iP_{r_{2},s_{2}}}(z,\bar{z})V_{iP_{r_{3},s_{3}}}(1)V^{\prime}_{iP_{r_{4},s_{4}}}(\infty)\Big\rangle_{S^{2}}^{\text{L}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big\langle\widehat{V}^{X_{1}}_{r_{1},s_{1}}(0)\widehat{V}^{X_{2}}_{r_{2},s_{2}}(z,\bar{z})\widehat{V}^{X_{3}}_{r_{3},s_{3}}(1)\widehat{V}^{{}^{\prime}X_{4}}_{r_{4},s_{4}}(\infty)\Big\rangle_{S^{2}}^{\text{MM}}~.

(75)

The explicit conformal block decomposition in the so-called s-channel, where the OPE is taken between the first and second operators and between the third and fourth, is given in (
2.1
) for the Liouville CFT correlator.
For the minimal model CFTs, the corresponding expressions are shown in (

) and (

34

The first step is to divide the moduli space (i.e. the complex plane) into six regions, each of which can be mapped to a compact, “lemon-shaped” domain near the origin using the crossing symmetries of the constituent worldsheet CFTs (see also

The second step is to map the lemon-shaped region of the complex plane to the familiar keyhole domain
F_{0}=\{t\in\mathbb{C}\mid|\mathop{\text{Re}}t|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\,,|t|\geqslant 1\}

elliptic

The so-called elliptic nome variable

via
t=iK(1-z)/K(z)

As discussed above, amplitudes involving four tachyons in the diagonal sector of the DMS are manifestly equal to the corresponding amplitudes in the AMS; see (

Likewise, amplitudes with four tachyons in the non-diagonal sector of the DMS are equal to those in the AMS with tachyons in the diagonal sector and the same Liouville momenta

To see this, observe that in the minimal model CFT correlator, the intermediate states in the conformal block decomposition are diagonal, due to the fusion rule
N\times N=D
. Moreover, the fusion rule (
30
) implies that the resulting set of diagonal intermediate states is the same as if the external operators had been diagonal themselves (with
D\times D=D
).
The only distinction in the structure constants arises from the sign factor

in (
2.2.2

As a result, the sign contribution gets squared and thus cancels out.
Therefore, the resulting minimal model correlator (and hence the full string amplitude) is identical to that of the A-series.

We now compute the fixed length amplitude defined in (

Two-matrix models

The
(p,p^{\prime})

N\times N
Hermitian matrices

\displaystyle Z=\int\text{d}M_{1}\text{d}M_{2}\,e^{-N\mathrm{Tr}(V(M_{1})+V(M_{2})-hM_{1}M_{2})}\,,

(155)

with the Haar measure and a pair-wise coupling. The potentials take the form
V(H_{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{n}M_{1}^{n}
and
V(H_{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{p^{\prime}}b_{n}M_{2}^{n}
. The explicit forms of the matrix potentials can be found for certain models in
39
. A common observable is products of the resolvent of a chosen matrix

\displaystyle R^{(1)}(x)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\frac{1}{x-M_{1}}\right)\,,\qquad R^{(1)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\equiv R^{(1)}(x_{1})\ldots R^{(1)}(x_{n})

(156)

\displaystyle\left\langle R^{(1)}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}R^{(1)}_{g,n}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)N^{2-2g-n}\,,

(157)

where the connected component of the resolvents has the standard genus expansion in the large
N
limit.
31
31

31

There is an independent resolvent for each matrix, and products of resolvents involving all matrices can be computed. In this work we will not be interested in these correlators.
At large
N
the saddlepoint eigenvalue distribution for both matrices becomes continuous and described by a density of states. It is supported along some cut of the real line. Along the cut, the resolvent can be used to calculate the leading density of states

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\left(R^{(1)}_{0,1}(x+i\varepsilon)-R^{(1)}_{0,1}(x-i\varepsilon)\right)\,.

(158)

The density can be computed for both matrices
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
using their respective resolvents.

Observables in the matrix integral can be solved recursively in
\frac{1}{N}
using the loop equations
60
, which are equations arising from differentiating everything inside the integral
\int dM_{1}dM_{2}\partial_{M_{1}}(\ldots)=0
. The loop equations give a way to obtain higher resolvent data
R_{g,n}
from lower resolvents. One output of the loop equations is an object known as the spectral curve, which allows one to bypass the loop equations and directly determine all resolvents using data of the curve.

One intuitive way to understand the spectral curve is the following
62
;
37
. At large
N
the resolvent
R^{(1)}(x)
is defined on the complex plane away from the cuts that support the eigenvalue density. If we go through the cut we go onto a second sheet of the complex plane. However, one can imagine that we take the two sheets of the complex plane, slightly open up the cuts, and smoothly glue them together into a Riemann surface
\Sigma
on which the resolvent is single-valued. The spectral curve can then be extracted from the leading order term of a particular large
N
loop equation, giving a defining equation

\displaystyle P_{0}(x(z),y(z))=0,

(159)

where
z
is a uniformization parameter of the Riemann surface
\Sigma
, and
x(z),y(z)\in\mathbb{C}
. For a point
z\in\Sigma
, we can view the above equation as specifying a value
x(z)
in the complex plane, with the dual parameter

\displaystyle y(z)=V^{\prime}_{1}(x(z))-R^{(1)}_{0,1}(x(z))\,,

(160)

encoding information on the large
N
resolvent of the first matrix at that point. The spectral curve has a symmetric interpretation where
y(z)
is a associated to the second matrix and the value

\displaystyle x(z)=V_{2}^{\prime}(y(z))-R^{(2)}_{0,1}(y(z))\,,

(161)

tells us information about the resolvent of the second matrix.

Cylinder diagram.

Another immediate application of the loop equations is that
R_{0,2}
has a universal answer for two-matrix models
39
, see for example
37
for a recent review. Knowing the spectral curve, the answer is

\displaystyle R_{0,2}\left(\mathrm{x}(z),\mathrm{x}\left(z^{\prime}\right)\right)\mathrm{dx}(z)\mathrm{dx}\left(z^{\prime}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{~d}z^{\prime}}{\left(z-z^{\prime}\right)^{2}}-\frac{\mathrm{dx}(z)\mathrm{dx}\left(z^{\prime}\right)}{\left(\mathrm{x}(z)-\mathrm{x}\left(z^{\prime}\right)\right)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle R_{0,2}\left(\mathrm{x}(z),\mathrm{x}\left(z^{\prime}\right)\right)=\partial_{x(z)}\partial_{x(z^{\prime})}\log\frac{z-z^{\prime}}{x(z)-x(z^{\prime})}

(162)

Connected amplitudes with more boundaries can be easily obtained using recursion relations, but we will not study them in this work.

5.3
Matrix integral: DMS

Height models.

We first explain how the matrix integral for the DMS/EMS was conjectured. This requires a detour through the lattice regularization of
(p,p+1)
unitary ADE minimal model CFTs, known as height models
96
;
119
;
105
;
47
;
80
.

Consider a fixed 2d lattice of given topology. Height models are defined by putting heights
h_{i}
on the vertices of the lattice, with the heights taking values in
h_{i}\in\{\text{nodes of }A/D/E\text{ Dynkin diagram}\}
. The rules are that adjacent lattice sites must have either identical or adjacent heights on the chosen Dynkin diagram. The heights are dynamical and path integrated over, with a particular weight assigned to each height function on the lattice. In the continuum as the lattice spacing goes to zero these models flow to the
(p,p+1)
ADE minimal models. For example, if the heights take values in
D_{4}
the model flows to the
(5,6)
D-series three-state Potts model, while if the heights take values in
A_{5}
we get the
(3,4)
A-series Ising model.

Matrix integrals.

To get a theory of quantum gravity we must make the lattice dynamical by integrating over all discretizations along with heights. Remarkably, height models on dynamical lattices can be re-written as integrals over random matrices
47
;
81
, where each height in the Dynkin diagram is mapped to a random Hermitian matrix

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\int\prod_{i}\text{d}M_{i}\hskip 0.85355pte^{-N\mathrm{Tr}(V_{i}(M_{i})-h_{ij}M_{i}M_{j})}\,,

(170)

and we have as many Hermitian matrices
M_{i}
as nodes in the Dynkin diagram, and

\displaystyle h_{ij}=\begin{cases}h\qquad\text{nodes $i,j$ adjacent on Dynkin diagram}\,,\\
0\qquad\text{otherwise}\,,\end{cases}

(171)

with
h
some constant. For
A
type Dynkin diagrams this reproduces the matrix chain potential standard for the AMS. For the
D_{4}
diagram we get the three-state Potts model
44
;
43

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\int\prod_{i=1}^{4}\text{d}M_{i}\hskip 0.85355pte^{-N\mathrm{Tr}(V_{i}(M_{i})-hM_{1}(M_{2}+M_{3}+M_{4}))}\,,

(172)

where the matrix
M_{1}
is the central one in the Dynkin diagram. This entire discussion has been for unitary minimal models which have a height model realization, but it was suspected that the form of the matrix model potential (
170
) holds more generally for
(p,p^{\prime})
ADE minimal string theories, although to date we are not aware of much progress to confirm this beyond
44
;
43
.

DMS: Matrix bootstrap

In this section we present some preliminary positivity bootstrap results
87
;
77
. We will first begin with the simpler case of the 3-state Potts model given by the DMS
5,6

40

127

83

F=-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\log Z(g_{i})
develops a non-analytic term
(g-g_{c})^{\#}
in its series expansion. While this is difficult to detect directly via bootstrap methods, the bootstrap will bound the space of couplings, thus bounding the location of the critical point.

We first study the 3-matrix model conjecturally dual to the
(5,6)
DMS theory
44
. While generic DMS models are expected to correspond to four-matrix integrals, in this case one of the matrices can be integrated out, yielding an effective three-matrix model. The potential we take is

\displaystyle V(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\big(\frac{1}{2}M_{i}^{2}+\frac{g}{4}M_{i}^{4}\big)+h\big(M_{1}M_{2}+M_{2}M_{3}+M_{3}M_{1}\big)

(173)

The symmetries of the model include: Hermiticity, which ensures that
\langle\tr(M_{i_{1}}M_{i_{2}}\allowbreak\cdots\allowbreak M_{i_{k}})\rangle\allowbreak=\langle\tr(M_{i_{1}}M_{i_{2}}\cdots M_{i_{k}})\rangle^{*}\allowbreak=\langle\tr(M_{i_{k}}M_{i_{k-1}}\cdots M_{i_{1}})\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
; the vanishing of traces over odd numbers of matrices; permutation symmetry
\mathcal{S}_{3}
among the matrices; and the cyclicity of the trace.
35
35

35

We use notation
\tr=\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}
such that
\tr 1=1
.
The loop equations are given by

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i=1}^{3}dM_{i}\tr\frac{\partial}{\partial(M_{n})}\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}e^{-N\mathrm{Tr}V(M_{i})}\right)=0\,,

(174)

where
\mathcal{O}_{i}\in\{\mathbf{1},M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},M_{1}^{2},M_{1}M_{2},M_{2}M_{1},\ldots\}
is an arbitrary word in the matrices. We also impose large-
N
factorization on the resulting relations among matrix expectation values.
An example is

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i=1}^{3}dM_{i}\partial_{M_{1}}(M_{1}^{4}M_{2}e^{-N\mathrm{Tr}V})=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\,

\displaystyle\langle M_{1}^{5}M_{2}\rangle+g\langle M_{1}^{7}M_{2}\rangle+h\left(\langle M_{1}^{4}M_{2}^{2}\rangle+\langle M_{1}^{4}M_{2}M_{3}\rangle\right)=\langle M_{1}^{2}\rangle\langle M_{1}M_{2}\rangle+\langle M_{1}^{3}M_{2}\rangle\,,

(175)

where it is understood that each expectation value denotes a traced quantity
\tr
.
The existence of nontrivial bounds in coupling and moment space arises from imposing the loop equations together with the following positivity condition:

\displaystyle\langle\tr\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}\mathcal{O}\rangle\geqslant 0\,

(176)

where
\mathcal{O}=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is the sum over all words without symmetries imposed, and
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
are arbitrary coefficients. This positivity condition is equivalent to
\mathbf{a}^{T}\mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{a}\geqslant 0
with
\mathbf{M}_{ij}=\langle\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}_{i}\mathcal{O}_{j}\rangle
for all real vectors
\mathbf{a}
. This is simply the statement that the moment matrix is positive semidefinite,

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}\succeq 0\,.

(177)

Note that the large-
N
loop equations have quadratic terms in the expectation values, yielding a nonlinear optimization problem. A resolution is the relaxation bootstrap method
77
, which relaxes the quadratic loop equations to linear inequalities, yielding a standard semidefinite programming (SDP) problem.
36
36

36

The standard primal form of SDP is

\displaystyle\text{minimize}~~~\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}x_{i}~~~\text{w.r.t}\,\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\}\in\mathbb{R},~~~\text{subject to}~~~\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{F}_{i}x_{i}-\mathbf{F}_{0}\succeq 0,~~~\mathbf{F}_{i}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}\,.

(178)

To achieve this, we introduce new variables
\mathbf{X}_{ij}=\langle\tr\mathcal{O}_{i}\rangle\langle\tr\mathcal{O}_{j}\rangle
, which replace the quadratic terms in the loop equations and render the system linear.
Thus, the resulting SDP problem is to extremize a cost function, such as a chosen single-trace expectation value, subject to:
37
37

37

Additional constraints may also be imposed
86
, such as

\displaystyle\langle\tr\left(\mathcal{O}_{i}-\langle\tr\mathcal{O}_{i}\rangle\right)^{\dagger}\left(\mathcal{O}_{j}-\langle\tr\mathcal{O}_{j}\rangle\right)\rangle\,\succeq\,0\Rightarrow\mathbf{M}-\mathbf{X}\succeq 0

(179)

However, we found that such constraints do not significantly improve the bounds at low truncation order, and therefore we did not impose them in this work.

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}\succeq 0\,,

(180)

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{ij}-\langle\tr\mathcal{O}_{i}\rangle\langle\tr\mathcal{O}_{j}\rangle\succeq 0

\displaystyle\text{(linearized) loop equations}=0\,.

;
17
. When the boundary states are worked out, the inequivalent branes in the EMS can be analyzed. We suspect there will be four inequivalent branes just as in the DMS case due to the conjectured matrix potential.

Conformal blocks for multiply degenerate Virasoro representations are not known in closed form nor through recursion relations although a well-checked conjecture exists for blocks on the plane
99
, and see
65
for some torus one-point functions. It would be interesting to derive a recursion relation for general degenerate blocks that allows for a numerical implementation. The main difficulty appears to lie in correctly subtracting the contributions of new null descendants as one approaches the degenerate limit from generic (non-degenerate) blocks.
Nevertheless, for the purposes of integrating correlation functions over moduli space to compute string amplitudes, this turned out not be necessary as evidenced by the very precise numerical results we obtained in this paper.

Further landscape of minimal string theories.

There are several related two-dimensional CFTs that one may consider as the matter sector of the string worldsheet theory (
1
). One example is two copies of the Ising model, which may be dual to a four-matrix model due to height model intuition. Other interesting theories are generalized minimal models, non-analytic Liouville theory, loop model CFTs.
Given the intricate connections between these theories and Liouville CFT
100
, it is reasonable to expect that their corresponding minimal string theories are also ultimately related. In particular, it would be very interesting to explore whether these theories can be recovered from the complex Liouville string.

Generalizations of topological recursion and bootstrap.

The conjectured matrix model duals to the D- and E-series minimal strings lie beyond the class of matrix models that admit standard topological recursion.
A natural and important question is whether the large
N
loop equations of these models can be leveraged to carry out an analytic bootstrap following the approach developed by Eynard
63
, potentially leading to generalized forms of topological recursion.
Complementarily, it would also be interesting to explore whether the numerical bootstrap based on positivity constraints can be efficiently extended to double-scaled matrix integrals.
Relatedly, it would be very interesting to investigate whether these models admit an interpretation in terms of 3d gravity, analogous to what occurs in the VMS and CLS.

Target space interpretation.

In
107
a target space interpretation of the AMS was given. The target space is a Riemann surface described by the spectral curve, and the nodal singularities of the Riemann surface indicate locations where ZZ instantons are localized. It would be interesting to develop a similar understanding of the DMS target space; suggestively, it may be a Riemann surface that does not admit a standard representation in terms of a spectral curve.

Matrix integral interpretation.

It would be useful to directly identify asymptotic boundary insertions in the DMS to matrix integral insertions involving the four matrices. Two of the four branes appear to have an obvious interpretation of inserting
\mathrm{Tr}\frac{1}{x-M_{i}}
, while the other two branes have mysterious properties such as the double trumpet diverging (
4.4
), which is a property of non-identity branes in the AMS as well. It is possible the diverging branes do not insert a simple trace of a matrix.

Gluing discrete trumpets.

In this paper, we have only begun to explore the role of conformal boundary conditions and the open string sector in minimal string theory. Our motivation was to gather evidence that the D-series minimal string exhibits a structure suggestive of a multi-matrix model dual involving more than two matrices.
Recently, a remarkably simple gluing formula applicable to a broad class of conformal boundary conditions was introduced in
35
, providing a string-theoretic derivation of the operation of gluing trumpets in JT gravity. While this formula holds in various string theories, such as the VMS and CLS, it remains an open question whether an analogous formula exists for discrete minimal string theories like the ADE models studied here.
Deriving such a formula, for example in the case of resolvent trumpets, would offer a more direct dictionary between string amplitudes and resolvents in the dual matrix model.

Connection to timelike Liouville CFT.

We comment on the connection of the two-point function for minimal model compared to timelike Liouville. The two-point function for minimal models (
25
) differs from standard conventions for timelike Liouville, while the structure constants are identical
36
. This difference arises because for timelike Liouville with general central charge the continuation
P_{3}\to\frac{iQ}{2}
does not insert the identity operator which has a null descendant at level one
L_{-1}|h=0\rangle=0
. The continuation inserts another non-degenerate operator in the spectrum with
h=0

98
. Another way to see this is that the timelike structure constant does not reduce to a delta function in this continuation. The two-point function is thus typically defined by demanding consistency with crossing, which defines it up to a normalization factor. However, minimal models have a discrete spectrum and the operator with
P_{r_{3},s_{3}}=P_{1,1}=\frac{iQ}{2}
is the genuine identity operator, giving us the two-point function unambiguously used in the main text.

, and rewritten as a double infinite series. The integral reduces to exponential which can be performed, and one of the series can be re-summed to give the following

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\text{d}P\frac{P\sin(4\pi s_{1}P)\sin(4\pi s_{2}P)}{\sinh(2\pi pb^{-1}P)\sinh(2\pi b^{-1}P)}\frac{\sinh(2\pi b^{-1}(p-1)P)}{\sinh(2\pi bs_{1})\sinh(2\pi bs_{2})}

\displaystyle~~=\frac{b^{2}}{32\pi^{2}\sinh(2\pi bs_{1})\sinh(2\pi bs_{2})}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\epsilon_{1,2}=\pm 1}(-\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2})\Big[\psi^{(1)}\big(1+pm+ib\left(\epsilon_{1}s_{1}+\epsilon_{2}s_{2}\right)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad~~-\psi^{(1)}\big(m+pm+ib\left(\epsilon_{1}s_{1}+\epsilon_{2}s_{2}\right)\big)\Big]\,,

(264)

written as a sum over eight Polygamma functions with alternating signs and arguments. The remaining series can be re-summed using the following identity

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big[\psi^{(1)}\big(1+pm+ix\big)-\psi^{(1)}\big(m+pm+ix\big)\Big]=\psi^{(1)}(1+ix)-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\psi^{(1)}(1+\frac{ix}{p})\,,

(265)

which gives the simple relation using properties of Polygamma functions

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\epsilon_{1,2}=\pm 1}

\displaystyle(-\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2})\Big[\psi^{(1)}\big(1+pm+ib\left(\epsilon_{1}s_{1}+\epsilon_{2}s_{2}\right)\big)-\psi^{(1)}\big(m+pm+ib\left(\epsilon_{1}s_{1}+\epsilon_{2}s_{2}\right)\big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\pi^{2}\sum_{\epsilon=\pm 1}\epsilon\left(\csch(\pi b(s_{1}+\epsilon s_{2}))-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\csch(\frac{\pi}{p}b(s_{1}+\epsilon s_{2}))\right)\,

\displaystyle=\Biggl[\csch^{2}\left(\frac{\pi b}{p}(s_{1}-s_{2})\right)-\csch^{2}\left(\frac{\pi b}{p}(s_{1}+s_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+p^{2}\left(\csch^{2}\left(\pi b(s_{1}+s_{2})\right)-\csch^{2}\left(\pi b(s_{1}-s_{2})\right)\right)\Biggr]

(266)

The final answer to the integral is

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\text{d}P\frac{P\sin(4\pi s_{1}P)\sin(4\pi s_{2}P)}{\sinh(2\pi pb^{-1}P)\sinh(2\pi b^{-1}P)}\frac{\sinh(2\pi b^{-1}(p-1)P)}{\sinh(2\pi bs_{1})\sinh(2\pi bs_{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}}{32p^{2}}\csch\left(2\pi bs_{1}\right)\csch\left(2\pi bs_{2}\right)\Biggl[\csch^{2}\left(\frac{\pi b}{p}(s_{1}-s_{2})\right)-\csch^{2}\left(\frac{\pi b}{p}(s_{1}+s_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+p^{2}\left(\csch^{2}\left(\pi b(s_{1}+s_{2})\right)-\csch^{2}\left(\pi b(s_{1}-s_{2})\right)\right)\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\csch\left(2\pi bs_{1}\right)\csch\left(2\pi bs_{2}\right)\partial_{s_{1}}\partial_{s_{2}}\log\Biggr[\frac{\cosh\left(2\pi\frac{b}{p}s_{1}\right)-\cosh\left(2\pi\frac{b}{p}s_{2}\right)}{\cosh\left(2\pi bs_{1}\right)-\cosh\left(2\pi bs_{2}\right)}\Biggr]~,

(267)

where we have given an alternative representation.

We compute ZZ-ZZ amplitudes closely following the approach of

\displaystyle Z_{(m,n)\left(m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\right)}^{\text{L }}(q)=\sum_{k=\left|m-m^{\prime}\right|+1,2}^{m+m^{\prime}-1}\sum_{l=\left|n-n^{\prime}\right|+1,2}^{n+n^{\prime}-1}\chi^{(b)}_{k,l}(q),\qquad Z_{(m,n)}^{\text{L }}(q)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\chi_{2k-1,2l-1}(q)

(274)

The matter contribution will always be given by a sum over degenerate matter characters
Z_{a|b}^{\text{M}}(q)=\sum_{n,m}f_{n,m}^{a|b}\chi^{(ib)}_{n,m}(q)
which depends on the boundary conditions for the matter through the indicator function. The characters are again given by

\displaystyle\chi^{(ib)}_{r,s}(q)=\frac{1}{\eta(q)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(q^{a_{r,s}(k)}-q^{a_{r,-s}(k)}\right),\quad a_{r,s}(k)=\frac{\left(2p^{\prime}pk+p^{\prime}r-ps\right)^{2}}{4p^{\prime}p}

Performing the integral.

We have the integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}(\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi h_{1}t}-\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi h_{2}t})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}(q^{h_{1}}-q^{h_{2}})=\log\frac{h_{2}}{h_{1}}\,.

(276)

The basic building block for the full cylinder amplitude is

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\eta(q)^{2}\chi_{k,l}^{(b)}(t)\chi_{r,s}^{(ib)}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\left(q^{a_{r,-s}(j)-d}-q^{a_{r,s}(j)-d}+q^{a_{r,s}(j)-d-kl}-q^{a_{r,-s}(j)-d-kl}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left(\frac{(2pp^{\prime}j+rp^{\prime}+sp)^{2}-(kp-lp^{\prime})^{2}}{(2pp^{\prime}j+rp^{\prime}+sp)^{2}-(kp+lp^{\prime})^{2}}\,\frac{(2pp^{\prime}j+rp^{\prime}-sp)^{2}-(kp+lp^{\prime})^{2}}{(2pp^{\prime}j+rp^{\prime}-sp)^{2}-(kp-lp^{\prime})^{2}}\right)\,.

(277)

Where in the first line
d=\frac{(kp-lp^{\prime})^{2}}{4pp^{\prime}}
. We can take the exponential of the above and factor it into the following form

\displaystyle\exp

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\eta(q)^{2}\chi_{k,l}^{(b)}(t)\chi_{r,s}^{(ib)}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(j-\frac{l-s}{2p^{\prime}}+\frac{k+r}{2p})(j+\frac{l-s}{2p^{\prime}}+\frac{k+r}{2p})(-j+\frac{l+s}{2p^{\prime}}+\frac{k-r}{2p})(-j-\frac{l+s}{2p^{\prime}}+\frac{k-r}{2p})}{(j+\frac{l+s}{2p^{\prime}}+\frac{k+r}{2p})(j-\frac{l+s}{2p^{\prime}}+\frac{k+r}{2p})(-j-\frac{l-s}{2p^{\prime}}+\frac{k-r}{2p})(-j+\frac{l-s}{2p^{\prime}}+\frac{k-r}{2p})}

\displaystyle M_{k,l;r,s}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\left(\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(l+s)}{2p^{\prime}}\right)-\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(k+r)}{2p}\right)\right)\left(\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(l-s)}{2p^{\prime}}\right)-\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(k-r)}{2p}\right)\right)}{\left(\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(l-s)}{2p^{\prime}}\right)-\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(k+r)}{2p}\right)\right)\left(\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(l+s)}{2p^{\prime}}\right)-\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi(k-r)}{2p}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(l+s)}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi(k+r)}{p})\right)\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(l-s)}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi(k-r)}{p})\right)}{\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(l+s)}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi(k-r)}{p})\right)\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(l-s)}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi(k+r)}{p})\right)}

(278)

If we consider the identity matter state on both ends for the DMS the matter contribution is
Z^{\text{M}}=\chi^{(ib)}_{1,1}(q)+\chi^{(ib)}_{p-1,1}(q)
. The Cylinder with different Liouville ZZ branes is

\displaystyle C^{\text{DMS}}_{(m,n),\left(m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\right)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=\left|m-m^{\prime}\right|+1,2}^{m+m^{\prime}-1}\prod_{l=\left|n-n^{\prime}\right|+1,2}^{n+n^{\prime}-1}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\eta(q)^{2}\chi_{k,l}^{(b)}(t)(\chi_{1,1}^{(ib)}(t)+\chi_{p-1,1}^{(ib)}(t))\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=\left|m-m^{\prime}\right|+1,2}^{m+m^{\prime}-1}\prod_{l=\left|n-n^{\prime}\right|+1,2}^{n+n^{\prime}-1}M_{k,l;1,1}M_{k,l;p-1,1}\,,

(279)

where for the AMS we would only have the first line above for
M_{k,l;1,1}
. A telescoping series satisfies
\prod_{i=m}^{n}\frac{a_{i+1}}{a_{i}}=\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_{m}}
which for example gives

\displaystyle\prod_{l=|n-n^{\prime}|+1,2}^{n+n^{\prime}-1}\frac{\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(l+1)}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi(k+r)}{p})\right)}{\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(l-1)}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi(k+r)}{p})\right)}=\frac{\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(n+n^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi(k+r)}{p})\right)}{\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(|n-n^{\prime}|)}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi(k+r)}{p})\right)}\,.

(280)

Applying the telescoping rule eight times we get

\displaystyle C^{\text{DMS}}_{(m,n),\left(m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(n+n^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi(m+m^{\prime})}{p})\right)\left(\cos(\frac{\pi|n-n^{\prime}|}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi|m-m^{\prime}|}{p})\right)}{\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(n+n^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi|m-m^{\prime}|)}{p})\right)\left(\cos(\frac{\pi|n-n^{\prime}|}{p^{\prime}})-\cos(\frac{\pi(m+m^{\prime})}{p})\right)}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(n+n^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}})+\cos(\frac{\pi|m-m^{\prime}|}{p})\right)\left(\cos(\frac{\pi|n-n^{\prime}|}{p^{\prime}})+\cos(\frac{\pi(m+m^{\prime})}{p})\right)}{\left(\cos(\frac{\pi(n+n^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}})+\cos(\frac{\pi(m+m^{\prime})}{p})\right)\left(\cos(\frac{\pi|n-n^{\prime}|}{p^{\prime}})+\cos(\frac{\pi|m-m^{\prime}|}{p})\right)}\,.

(281)

This is precisely the main result claimed in the main text, it is the product of two AMS ZZ-ZZ amplitudes. The amplitude in the main text can be obtained by using trig identities. The AMS ZZ-ZZ amplitude is simply the first line of the above
57

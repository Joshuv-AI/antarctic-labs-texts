Keller-Osserman and Harnack type results for nonlinear elliptic PDE with unbounded ingredients
Boyan Sirakov, Aelson Sobral

Abstract
. We show that the classical Keller-Osserman theorem on the solvability of the equation ℒ ⁡ [ u ] = f ⁡ ( u ) \mathcal{L}[u]=f(u) is valid when ℒ \mathcal{L} is a general operator in divergence form with unbounded coefficients in the natural regime of local integrability. This has been open up to now, earlier results concerned operators with locally bounded ingredients. We also settle an open question from [ 27 ] about the validity of the strong maximum principle for supersolutions of ℒ ⁡ [ u ] = f ⁡ ( u ) \mathcal{L}[u]=f(u) under the optimal integral condition of Vázquez. More generally, we obtain a Harnack inequality for positive solutions of this equation, which extends a result by V. Julin.

35B50, 35J15

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Brief overview of novelties

We start by recalling the following very classical result on general solvability of semilinear elliptic PDE, obtained (independently) by Keller
[
20
]
and Osserman
[
22
]
in 1957: given a continuous nondecreasing function
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, the equation

(1.1)

\Delta u=f(u)

has a solution (and even a subsolution)
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
if and only if the primitive
F(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,ds
is such that

(1.2)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}}\quad\mbox{diverges}.

This result has generated a huge body of research, which continues to this day (a partial review of the literature will be given below).

A very extensively studied question is the possibility of considering more general elliptic operators or more general nonlinear functions in (
1.1
). One of our main results below states that the Keller-Osserman result holds if the Laplacian is replaced by a uniformly elliptic operator in divergence form with
(locally) unbounded coefficients
. This question has been completely open up to now, we settle it here for coefficients in uniformly local Lebesgue spaces.

Another fundamental question on the equation (
1.1
) is the validity of the
strong minimum principle
, that is, whether a nonnegative (super)solution which attains the value zero vanishes identically. It is very classical that this is so if
f
grows at most linearly around zero, but in 1984 Vásquez
[
31
]
(see also Pucci-Serrin
[
24
]
) showed that the right hypothesis for the validity of the SMP is as follows: if
u
is a nonnegative supersolution, that is,
\Delta u\leq f(u)
,
u\geq 0
in a domain
\Omega
, and
u(x_{0})=0
then
u\equiv 0
in
\Omega
provided

(1.3)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}}\quad\mbox{diverges}.

For the validity of the SMP, the possibility of replacing the Laplacian by an operator with locally unbounded coefficients was considered for the first time only recently in
[
27
]
. The main result on SMP from that paper states that for such operators the SMP is valid provided

(1.4)

\limsup_{t\searrow 0}\frac{f(t)}{t\,(\ln t)^{2}}>0.

The condition (
1.4
) is stronger than (
1.3
), so the question of the validity of the SMP under the optimal (
1.3
) remained open (see
[
27
, p.4-5]
). We will answer this question in the affirmative here.

The validity of the SMP under the Vásquez integral condition is a consequence of a result from
[
27
]
and our third main result here, which is an extension of the Harnack inequality of Julin
[
18
]
. The generalized Harnack inequality in
[
18
]
states that any nonnegative solution of (
1.1
) (or of div
(A(x)Du)=f(u)
for a bounded uniformly positive matrix
A(x)
) in
B_{2}
is such that for some constant
C>0
independent of
u
and
f

(1.5)

\int_{\inf_{B_{1}}u}^{\sup_{B_{1}}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}\leq C.

We will show that this inequality is valid for solutions of more general equations with unbounded coefficients.

1.2.
Precise definitions and results

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary domain and
\mathcal{L}
be the linear uniformly elliptic second-order operator in general divergence form

(1.6)

\mathcal{L}[u]=\mathrm{div}\left(A(x)Du+b_{1}(x)u\right)+b_{2}(x)\cdot Du+c(x)u.

The measurable and bounded matrix-valued function
A(x)
satisfies the uniform ellipticity condition

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq A(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for almost every
x\in\Omega
, with constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
. The lower-order coefficients belong locally to Lebesgue spaces, which makes it possible for weak solutions to satisfy the maximum principle and the Harnack inequality; specifically,

(1.7)

b_{1},b_{2}\in L^{q}_{loc}(\Omega)\quad c\in L^{q/2}_{loc}(\Omega)\quad\text{with}\quad q>n.

We will consider solutions (resp. supersolutions, subsolutions) of

(1.8)

\mathcal{L}[u]=(\mbox{resp. }\leq,\geq)f(u)\quad\mbox{in }\;\Omega

which belong to the Sobolev space
H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and solve the inequality in the standard weak sense (see for instance
[
29
, Chapter 8]
), of course always assuming that
f
and
u
are such that
f(u)\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
(for instance
f(u)\in L^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for
s=2n/(n+2)
if
n\geq 3
or
s>1
if
n=2
is enough). Here and throughout the paper,
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous nondecreasing function.

A most natural question on (
1.8
) is its solvability when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As noted above, for
\mathcal{L}=\Delta
and classical solutions, it was answered already in 1957. That result by Keller and Osserman was one of the first general solvability results for semilinear elliptic equations and has had an enormous influence over the years.
In particular, there has been a huge amount of work on extending the Keller-Osserman result to more general operators in (
1.8
) and weak solutions, with extensions to quasilinear, fully nonlinear, singular, degenerate elliptic operators. It has also generated a lot of work on the closely related question of explosive solutions in bounded domains, as well as a more detailed description of the behaviour of the solutions. We refer the interested reader to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
21
,
30
]
, as well as to the citations in these works.

Even though Keller-Osserman type results are available for a large variety of classes of elliptic operators, as evidenced by these references, a common feature of previous results is the local boundedness of the spatial dependence of the coefficients in the equation. This can be explained by the the original Keller-Osserman and still most frequently used approach to this problem – one uses a subsolution constructed by replacing the coefficients by their lower bounds and writing a one-dimensional version of the resulting autonomous equation (that is, searching for radial solutions of that equation), which can be solved by ODE methods and its explicit behaviour can be determined precisely under the integral hypothesis (
1.2
). Obviously, such a method cannot be used for locally unbounded coefficients. Our first contribution here is to show that the Keller-Osserman result holds true for operators that have locally unbounded coefficients with finite uniformly local norms.

We recall the definition of uniformly local Lebesgue spaces and norms. We say that
h\in L^{q}_{ul}(\Omega)
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, provided the quantity (norm)

(1.9)

\|h\|_{L^{q}_{ul}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|h\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B_{1}(x))}

is finite. Uniformly local
L^{q}
-norms essentially measure the local integrability features of a function; they have been often used in the study of global existence of solutions of Navier-Stokes or parabolic equations, e.g. in the classical papers
[
14
,
19
]
. Interestingly, the main result from
[
27
]
on the seemingly unrelated Landis conjecture also represented an extension of results on equations with bounded coefficients to coefficients in uniformly local Lebesgue spaces. These spaces also play a pivotal role in the recent work on optimized elliptic estimates
[
28
]
.

Our Keller-Osserman type result is as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that

(1.10)

b_{1},\,b_{2}\in L^{q}_{ul}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad c\in L^{q/2}_{ul}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad\mbox{for some }\;q>n.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a continuous entire weak subsolution to (
1.8
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then

(1.11)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F}(t)}=\infty.

We recall that even for
\mathcal{L}=\Delta
it is easy to construct a classical entire positive solution of
\mathcal{L}[u]=f(u)
, if
f
does not satisfy (
1.11
).

Our research was initially triggered by a different question: under what conditions does the strong maximum principle (SMP) hold for nonnegative supersolutions of (
1.8
)? This problem dates back to 1984, when Vázquez showed in
[
31
]
that classical nonnegative solutions to

(1.12)

\Delta u\leq f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

satisfy the SMP in
\Omega
provided
f
behaves like a modulus of continuity, meaning that it is nonnegative, nondecreasing,
f(0)=0
, and
f
meets the additional condition

(1.13)

\int_{0}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}}=\infty\quad\text{with}\quad F(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,ds.

This condition was later shown in
[
24
]
to be both necessary and sufficient for the validity of the SMP.
While (
1.13
) allows for nonlinearities which are Lipschitz at zero, such cases fall within the scope of the standard SMP (see
[
6
,
17
,
29
]
); so (
1.13
) becomes crucial only when dealing with nonlinearities that grow more quickly than Lipschitz at zero. Many subsequent works have extended the Vázquez SMP to broader settings in terms of the differential operators considered and the dependence of
f
in
x
,
u
and
Du
; we refer to
[
10
,
23
,
24
,
26
]
, and the book
[
25
]
which is mostly dedicated to this topic (many more references are available in these works).

For operators with unbounded coefficients, the theory remains significantly less developed, since many of the techniques used in the bounded-coefficient setting do not readily extend to the unbounded case. Actually, in the latter setting, the question of the validity of the SMP for a superlinear nonlinearity
f(u)
had been completely open until the recent work
[
27
]
, which introduced a novel approach based on an optimized weak Harnack inequality. More specifically, the proof of the SMP in
[
27
]
relies on an application of the weak Harnack inequality in which the constant is optimized with respect to the norms of the lower order coefficients of the “linearized” version of (
1.8
). This method bridged variational and non-variational theories, enabling progress beyond the Lipschitz threshold for
f
when
\mathcal{L}
has unbounded coefficients; in particular, it allowed for nonlinearities of the form

f(s)\approx s|\ln(s)|^{a},\quad\text{for}\quad a\leq 2.

The case where the right-hand side grows even more rapidly at zero while still satisfying (
1.13
) was stated as an open question in
[
27
]
. At that time, the answer seemed unclear since (
1.13
) is an ODE-type hypothesis, while, as we explained above, for equations with unbounded coefficients, the usual method of constructing a radial subsolution of the equation (a barrier) in a well-chosen annulus does not work.

On the other hand, a promising direction arises from considering ODEs for the sub- and super-level sets of
u
, as opposed to barrier construction. In
[
18
]
, V. Julin explored this idea for an operator in divergence form involving only the principal second-order part. Specifically, in this important advance of the theory of semilinear elliptic PDE he proved the generalized Harnack inequality (
1.15
) below for solutions to

(1.14)

\mathrm{div}\left(A(x)Du\right)=f(u),

by deriving an ODE for the level sets of
u
that incorporates the right-hand side behavior. This novel for (
1.14
) approach is inspired by techniques from the Harnack inequality proof in
[
4
]
, and yields a quantified version of the SMP.

The extension of the ODE-related technique from
[
18
]
to scenarios involving lower-order coefficients is delicate, especially in regard to inequalities of Caccioppoli-type. These inequalities must be carefully managed to control the growth of energy terms relative to the size of sub- and super-level sets (this is precisely where the assumption in (
1.7
) plays a critical role and demonstrates its sharpness). Through iterations of these inequalities, we arrive at the following result.

Theorem 2
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(B_{2})
be a nonnegative solution to

\mathcal{L}[u]=f(u).

There exists a constant
C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,q,\|b_{1}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2})},\|b_{2}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2})},\|c\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{2})})
such that

(1.15)

\int_{\inf_{B_{1}}u}^{\sup_{B_{1}}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}\leq C.

The overall scheme of the proof of Theorem
2
is similar to that of the main result in
[
18
]
, so we outline it only briefly. First, as in
[
18
]
we establish the differential inequality for supersolutions

\eta^{\prime}(t)\geq c\min\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{F(t)}}\eta(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},\frac{1}{t}\eta(t)\right\},\quad\text{for}\quad\eta(t)=|\{x\in\Omega\colon u(x)<t\}|,

along with an analogous estimate for the measure of sublevel sets. Then we use this inequality to obtain the bound

(1.16)

\eta(t)\lesssim\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}\right)^{\frac{qn}{q-n}},

at a certain point, which extends the corresponding estimate from
[
18
]
, where (
1.16
) was proved without the power
qn/(q-n)
, for (
1.14
). The estimate (
1.16
) enables us, through an iterative application of a Caccioppoli inequality, to refine our control over the infimum, thereby estimating part of the integral in (
1.15
). To handle the remaining portion of the integral, we apply the differential inequality for the sublevel sets, which ensures that the solution remains bounded over a set of large measure. Then, on the subsolution side, we derive a second Caccioppoli inequality, which provides control over the supremum.

As we noted above, the main challenge in extending this approach to the unbounded setting lies in deriving Caccioppoli estimates that exhibit a sufficiently strong decay of the energy in relation to the size of the sub- and superlevel sets. Notably, the condition (
1.7
) plays a sharp role in achieving this, at least within the scope of our technique.

As a consequence of the generalized Harnack principle (
1.15
), we establish the strong maximum principle (SMP) for (
1.8
). For this, we also need a result from
[
27
]
to deal with the fact that the proof of Theorem
2
relies on information from both sub- and supersolutions, while the SMP is stated only for supersolutions.

Theorem 3
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be a nonnegative solution to

\mathcal{L}[u]\leq f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\qquad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{0}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}=\infty.

Then
u>0
or
u\equiv 0
in
\Omega
.

The paper is organized as follows. We dedicate Section
2
to recalling some basic facts and known results. In Section
3
, we prove estimates for nonnegative supersolutions, as well as the ODEs for the level sets. In Section
4
, we seek estimates for subsolutions, and, in particular, prove a Cacciopoli inequality. The proof of the Keller-Osserman condition, Theorem
1
, is done in subsection
5.1
, the generalized Harnack inequality, Theorem
2
, is done in Subsection
5.2
, and the strong maximum principle in Subsection
5.3
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some well-known definitions and results. We use the same notation as in
[
18
]
and refer to that paper for more details on the background, as well as more references.

Given a symmetric matrix
A\in\mbox{Sym}(n)
,
\mbox{Spec}(A)
denotes the spectrum of
A
, that is, the set of eigenvalues of
A
. For a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by
\mathcal{X}_{E}
the indicator function of
E
. With the letter
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote a domain where a given equation is set. We denote with
\|\cdot\|_{q}
the
L^{q}
-norm of a function when the domain of integration is clear from the context. Solutions to (
1.8
) are understood in the usual weak sense given by the following definition.

Definition 4
.

We say that
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
satisfies
\mathcal{L}[u]\leq f(u)
in
\Omega
in the weak sense (supersolution) if for every
x_{0}\in\Omega
and nonnegative
w\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
, with
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, we have

-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}A(x)Du\cdot Dw+ub_{1}(x)\cdot Dw+\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}wb_{2}(x)\cdot Du+c(x)u\,w\leq\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}w\,f(u).

Likewise,
u
satisfies
\mathcal{L}[u]\geq f(u)
in
\Omega
in the weak sense (subsolution) if for every
x_{0}\in\Omega
and nonnegative
w\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
, with
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, we have

-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}A(x)Du\cdot Dw+ub_{1}(x)\cdot Dw+\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}wb_{2}(x)\cdot Du+c(x)u\,w\geq\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}w\,f(u).

By smoothing the operator’s coefficients, we can replicate Julin’s approximation argument (see
[
18
]
*Page 883) and assume that the solution and all the coefficients of
\mathcal{L}
are smooth. Let us also define

E_{t}\coloneqq\{x\in\Omega\colon u(x)<t\},\quad\text{and}\quad A_{t}\coloneqq\{x\in\Omega\colon u(x)\geq t\},

the sublevel and superlevel sets of a function
u
, respectively.

We recall the concept of perimeter, which plays a central role in deriving the ODE for the sub- and super-level sets.

Definition 5
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The perimeter of
E
in
U
is defined as

P(E,U)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\int_{E}\mbox{div}\,\varphi\colon\varphi\in C^{1}_{0}(U,\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq 1\right\}.

Next, we recall the fundamental relative isoperimetric inequality.

Lemma 6
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter in
B_{r}
. Then

P(E,B_{r})\geq c\min\left\{|E\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}},|B_{r}\backslash E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right\},

for a dimensional constant
c>0
.

The following estimate is a simple consequence of Hölder’s inequality, but will be handy when obtaining the ODE for the super and sub-level sets.

Lemma 7
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
be such that
|Du|>0
in
\{u=t\}
, then

\left(P(E_{t},B_{r}(x_{0}))\right)^{2}\leq\|Du\|_{L^{1}(\{u=t\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0}))}\,\left\|\frac{1}{|Du|}\right\|_{L^{1}(\{u=t\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0}))}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be as in the definition of perimeter. Then, by the Divergence Theorem, we have

\int_{E_{t}}\mbox{div}\,\varphi\,dx=\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}}\varphi\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
\partial^{*}E_{t}
denotes the reduced boundary of
E_{t}
. Since
\partial^{*}E_{t}\subseteq\{u=t\}
, we obtain
\nu=Du/|Du|
, and so

\int_{E_{t}}\mbox{div}\,\varphi\,dx=\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}}\varphi\cdot\frac{Du}{|Du|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}\varphi\cdot\frac{Du}{|Du|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{|Du|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where we used that
\varphi
is supported in
B_{r}(x_{0})
. By the Hölder inequality we have, since
\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}}\mbox{div}\,\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}|\varphi|^{2}|Du|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{|Du|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}|Du|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\int_{\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{|Du|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|Du\|_{L^{1}(\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\left\|\frac{1}{|Du|}\right\|_{L^{1}(\partial^{*}E_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0}))}^{\frac{1}{2}},

and the proof is done by taking the supremum in
\varphi
, since
\partial^{*}E_{t}\subseteq\{u=t\}
.
∎

We will also need the following real-analysis result, whose proof can be found in
[
15
]
*Lemma 7.1.

Lemma 8
.

Let
b>0
and
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of real positive numbers such that

x_{i+1}\leq CB^{i}x_{i}^{1+b},

with
C>0
and
B>1
. If
x_{0}\leq C^{-\frac{1}{b}}B^{-\frac{1}{b^{2}}}
, then

\lim_{i\to\infty}x_{i}=0.

3.
Refined estimates for supersolutions

In this section, we obtain estimates for weak solutions of

\mathcal{L}[u]\leq f(u).

We recall that hereafter
\mathcal{L}
has the form (
1.6
) and the lower order coefficients satisfy hypothesis (
1.7
).

The first result is a Caccioppoli-type inequality, establishing a decay estimate essential for deriving the ODE governing the sub- and superlevel sets. The presence of unbounded terms in the operator slows the decay rate, precisely reflecting the integrability of the lower-order terms.

Proposition 9
.

Assume
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{R})
is a nonnegative supersolution of

\mathcal{L}[u]\leq f(u).

For any
0<r<R
, we have the inequality

(3.1)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\|Du\|_{L^{2}(E_{t}\cap B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{R-r}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{t}{\sqrt{R-r}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2q}}+t|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Furthermore, for almost every
t>0
, there holds

\displaystyle C^{-1}\int_{\{u=t\}\cap B_{r}}|Du|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{F(t)}{t}+\frac{\sqrt{F(t)}}{R-r}+\frac{t}{(R-r)^{2}}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|+t|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{R-r}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}+\frac{t}{\sqrt{R-r}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{3}{2q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{t}{(R-r)^{3/2}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{2q}},

where
C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\|b_{1}\|_{q},\|b_{2}\|_{q},\|c\|_{q/2})
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
be a smooth cutoff that satisfies

\zeta=1\quad\mbox{in}\quad B_{r},\quad\zeta=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad B_{R}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|D\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\leq 2(R-r)^{-1},

and let us consider
\varphi=(t-u)_{+}\zeta^{2}
as a test function. Computing

D\varphi=-\zeta^{2}\,\mathcal{X}_{E_{t}}Du+2(t-u)_{+}\zeta\,D\zeta,

we obtain,

\displaystyle-\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\varphi f(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\zeta^{2}A(x)Du\cdot Du+2\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\zeta\,(t-u)_{+}A(x)Du\cdot D\zeta

\displaystyle+\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}ub_{1}\cdot D\varphi-\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\varphi b_{2}\cdot Du-\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}c\,u\varphi.

Since
\mbox{Spec}(A(x))\subset[\lambda,\Lambda]
, we have
A(x)Du\cdot Du\geq\lambda|Du|^{2}
and
|A(x)Du|\leq\Lambda|Du|
. Substituting this in the previous inequality, we have

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\zeta^{2}\,|Du|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\varphi f(u)+2\Lambda\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\zeta\,(t-u)_{+}|Du|\,|D\zeta|

\displaystyle+\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}u|b_{1}|\,|D\varphi|+\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\varphi|b_{2}|\,|Du|+\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}|c|u\varphi.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}f(u)(t-u)_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}f(u)\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{X}_{\{u<s\}}ds\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B_{R}}f(u)\mathcal{X}_{\{u<s\}}dx\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\int_{B_{R}}\mathcal{X}_{\{u<t\}}dx\,ds=F(t)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|,

where

F(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}f(s).

We recall Young’s inequality for products: given nonnegative real numbers
a
and
b
,

(3.2)

ab\leq\frac{a^{2}}{2\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon b^{2}}{2}

for any
\epsilon>0
. Therefore, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle 2\Lambda\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\zeta(t-u)_{+}|Du||D\zeta|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lambda\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\epsilon|Du|^{2}\zeta^{2}+\frac{\Lambda}{\epsilon}\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}|D\zeta|^{2}(t-u)_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}u|b_{1}|\,|D\varphi|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4t^{2}}{R-r}\|b_{1}\|_{L^{1}}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}|Du|^{2}\zeta^{4}+\frac{t^{2}}{2\epsilon}\|b_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}(t-u)_{+}\zeta^{2}|b_{2}|\,|Du|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}|Du|^{2}\zeta^{4}+\frac{t^{2}}{2\epsilon}\|b_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}|c|u\varphi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{2}\|c\|_{L^{q/2}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}},

where we intentionally omitted the domain of integration
(E_{t}\cap B_{R})
in the norms to ease notation. Picking
\epsilon=\lambda(2(\Lambda+1))^{-1}
, so that
\left(\lambda-\epsilon(\Lambda+1)\right)=\lambda/2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}\zeta^{2}\,|Du|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F(t)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|+\frac{\Lambda t^{2}}{\epsilon(R-r)^{2}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|+\frac{C_{1}t^{2}}{R-r}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{4\epsilon}\left(C_{1}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}+C_{2}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}+C_{3}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right),

for
C_{1}=C_{1}(\|b_{1}\|_{q})
,
C_{2}=C_{2}(\|b_{2}\|_{q})
,
C_{3}=C_{3}(\|c\|_{q/2})
. Then we are done once we recall that
\zeta=1
in
B_{r}
, which gives

\displaystyle C^{-1}\|Du\|_{L^{2}(E_{t}\cap B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{R-r}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{t}{\sqrt{R-r}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}},

for a constant
C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\|b_{1}\|_{q},\|b_{2}\|_{q},\|c\|_{q/2})
.

To prove the second inequality, we consider the values
t>0
such that
|Du|>0
on
\{u=t\}
(we recall that by the Morse-Sard Lemma this happens for almost every
t>0
, since
u
is smooth). Given a parameter
\rho>0
, we integrate the equation in the set
E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}
(recall we are assuming that the coefficients are smooth) to obtain

(3.3)

\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}}\mbox{div}\left(A(x)Du+b_{1}\,u\right)+b_{2}\cdot Du+c\,u\leq\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}}f(u).

By the divergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}}\mbox{div}\left(A(x)Du+b_{1}\,u\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}\left(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}\right)}(A(x)Du+b_{1}\,u)\cdot\nu d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
\nu
is the normal vector defined on the manifold
\partial^{*}(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho})
. Inputting into (
3.3
) the fact that
\nu=Du/|Du|
on the portion
\{u=t\}\cap B_{\rho}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{u=t\}\cap B_{\rho}}A(x)Du\cdot\frac{Du}{|Du|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}}f(u)+|b_{2}||Du|+|c|u-\int_{E_{t}\cap\partial B_{\rho}}A(x)Du\cdot\nu

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}\left(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}\right)}|b_{1}||u|.

Again using that
\text{Spec}(A(x))\subset[\lambda,\Lambda]
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\{u=t\}\cap B_{\rho}}|Du|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}}f(u)+|b_{2}||Du|+|c|u+\Lambda\int_{E_{t}\cap\partial B_{\rho}}|Du|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}\left(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}\right)}|b_{1}||u|.

We integrate the inequality with respect to
\rho
from
r
to
\hat{\rho}=\frac{r+R}{2}
in order to obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\frac{R-r}{2}\right)\int_{\{u=t\}\cap B_{r}}|Du|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{R-r}{2}\right)\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{\hat{\rho}}}f(u)+|b_{2}||Du|+|c|u

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Lambda\int_{E_{t}\cap(B_{\hat{\rho}}\backslash B_{r})}|Du|+\int_{r}^{\hat{\rho}}\int_{\partial^{*}\left(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}\right)}|b_{1}||u|.

Let us estimate the terms involving
b_{2}
and
b_{1}
. An application of the Hölder inequality and (
3.1
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{\hat{\rho}}}|b_{2}||Du|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|b_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\,\|Du\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|b_{2}\|_{L^{q}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\,\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{R-r}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\|b_{2}\|_{L^{q}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\frac{t}{\sqrt{R-r}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\|b_{2}\|_{L^{q}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}t|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}},

and as a consequence,

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-1}\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{\hat{\rho}}}|b_{2}||Du|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{R-r}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}+t|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{t}{\sqrt{R-r}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{3}{2q}}.

Another application of the Hölder inequality and the Trace Theorem (see
[
6
]
*Section 5.5, Theorem 1), gives

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}\left(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}\right)}|b_{1}||u|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|b_{1}\|_{q}\left\{\int_{\partial^{*}\left(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}\right)}|u|^{q^{\prime}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\|b_{1}\|_{q}\left(\|u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho})}+\|Du\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho})}\right),

where
q^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
q
. We now make use of the nonnegativity of
u
, Hölder’s inequality and (
3.1
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}\left(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}\right)}|b_{1}||u|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{C}\left(t|E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}+\|Du\|_{L^{2}(E_{t}\cap B_{\rho})}|E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{C}t|E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}+\overline{C}C\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{R-r}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+\overline{C}C\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{R-r}}|E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{3}{2q}}+t|E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right),

for some constant
\overline{C}
depending on
n
,
q
and
\|b_{1}\|_{q}
. Putting all together into inequality (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle C_{2}^{-1}\int_{\{u=t\}\cap B_{r}}|Du|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}f(u)+\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{R-r}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{t}{\sqrt{R-r}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{2q}-\frac{1}{q}}+t|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{F(t)}}{R-r}+\frac{t}{(R-r)^{2}}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{t}{(R-r)^{3/2}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{2q}}+\frac{t}{R-r}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t|E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}+|E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{R-r}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{t}{\sqrt{R-r}}|E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{3}{2q}}+t|E_{t}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}},

for a constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\|b_{1}\|_{q},\|b_{2}\|_{q},\|c\|_{q/2})
. Simplifying this further gives

\displaystyle C_{3}^{-1}\int_{\{u=t\}\cap B_{r}}|Du|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}f(u)+t|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{R-r}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}+\frac{t}{\sqrt{R-r}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{3}{2q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sqrt{F(t)}}{R-r}+\frac{t}{(R-r)^{2}}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|+\frac{t}{(R-r)^{3/2}}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{2q}},

for
C_{3}=2C_{2}
. The estimate is complete once one notices that

\int_{E_{t}\cap B_{R}}f(u)\leq\frac{F(t)}{t}|E_{t}\cap B_{R}|.

∎

Now we use the second part of the Cacciopoli-type inequality to get a partial ODE involving the sub- and superlevel sets.

Lemma 10
.

Assume
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{2})
is a nonnegative supersolution of

\mathcal{L}[u]\leq f(u).

Then, for almost every
t>0
, we have

\int_{\{u=t\}\cap B_{2r}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{|Du|}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\geq\frac{c}{t}\,\min\big\{|E_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})|,|B_{r}(x_{0})\backslash E_{t}|\big\},

whenever
B_{2r}(x_{0})\subset B_{2}
is such that

(3.5)

r\leq\frac{t}{\sqrt{F(t)}}.

Proof.

By considering
v(x)=u(x_{0}+x)
, we may assume
x_{0}=0
. We split the proof into two cases depending on where the minimum is attained. Assume first that

(3.6)

\min\left\{|E_{t}\cap B_{r}|,|B_{r}\backslash E_{t}|\right\}=|E_{t}\cap B_{r}|.

Observe that the integrand on the LHS of the statement of the lemma is not defined along the set of critical points of
u
. However, Morse-Sard Lemma ensures that for almost every
t>0
we have
|Du|>0
on the level set
\{u=t\}\cap B_{2}
.

The proof now proceeds depending on whether the density of
E_{t}\cap B_{r}
in
B_{r}
is universally controlled away from zero and from one. Similar to the proof of
[
18
]
*Lemma 3.3, we split the proof further into two cases. Assume

(3.7)

|E_{t}\cap B_{r}|\geq 2^{-(n+2)}|B_{r}|.

By Proposition
9
with
R=2r
, we have

\displaystyle C^{-1}\,\|Du\|_{L^{1}(\{u=t\}\cap B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{F(t)}{t}+\frac{\sqrt{F(t)}}{r}+\frac{t}{r^{2}}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{2r}|+t|E_{t}\cap B_{2r}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{r}\right)|E_{t}\cap B_{2r}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}+\frac{t}{\sqrt{r}}|E_{t}\cap B_{2r}|^{1-\frac{3}{2q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{t}{r^{3/2}}|E_{t}\cap B_{2r}|^{1-\frac{1}{2q}}.

From (
3.5
), we obtain

\frac{F(t)}{t}\leq\frac{t}{r^{2}},

and so

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-1}\,\|Du\|_{L^{1}(\{u=t\}\cap B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle tr^{n-2}+tr^{n\left(1-\frac{2}{q}\right)}+tr^{n\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)-1}+tr^{n\left(1-\frac{3}{2q}\right)-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle tr^{n\left(1-\frac{1}{2q}\right)-\frac{3}{2}}.

Now, since
q\geq n
, we have

C_{2}^{-1}\,\|Du\|_{L^{1}(\{u=t\}\cap B_{r})}\leq tr^{n-2}.

By Lemma
7
, we get

(P(E_{t},B_{r}))^{2}\,\left(\int_{\{u=t\}\cap B_{r}}\frac{1}{|Du|}\right)^{-1}\leq\|Du\|_{L^{1}(\{u=t\}\cap B_{r})}.

By Lemma
6
and (
3.6
), we obtain

P(E_{t},B_{r})\geq c\min\left\{|E_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}},|B_{r}\backslash E_{t}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\right\}=c|E_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Thus,

c^{2}|E_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{2(n-1)}{n}}\left(\int_{\{u=t\}\cap B_{r}}\frac{1}{|Du|}\right)^{-1}\leq\|Du\|_{L^{1}(\{u=t\}\cap B_{r})}\leq C\,tr^{n-2}.

We now use (
3.7
) to estimate
|E_{t}\cap B_{r}|
from below and get

\int_{\{u=t\}\cap B_{r}}\frac{1}{|Du|}\geq C_{1}\frac{1}{t}r^{n}\geq C_{2}\frac{1}{t}|E_{t}\cap B_{r}|.

If (
3.7
) is not true, then we have
|E_{t}\cap B_{r}|<2^{-(n+2)}|B_{r}|
. In this case, we cover
E_{t}\cap B_{r}
by a Besicovitch-type family of balls
\left\{\overline{B}_{R_{i}}(x_{i})\right\}_{x_{i}\in E_{t}}
for

R_{i}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\rho>0\colon|B_{\rho}(x_{i})\cap E_{t}|\leq\left(1-2^{-n-1}\right)|B_{\rho}(x_{i})|\right\}.

We can then apply the first part of the proof (when we assumed (
3.7
)), with
B_{R_{i}}(x_{i})
instead of
B_{r}
, and the result follows by a covering argument as in
[
18
]
*Proof of Lemma 3.3.

Now, if (
3.6
) is not true, that is

\min\left\{|E_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})|,|B_{r}(x_{0})\backslash E_{t}|\right\}=|B_{r}(x_{0})\backslash E_{t}|,

then we repeat the same proof depending on whether
|B_{r}\backslash E_{t}|\geq 2^{-(n+2)}|B_{r}|
holds. The only difference in comparison to the first part is that
P(E_{t},B_{r})\geq c|B_{r}\backslash E_{t}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}
.
∎

We now derive the ODE for the level sets. Since the argument closely follows that of
[
18
]
*Proposition 3.5, we omit the details. The differential inequality arising from the equation is used exclusively to establish Lemma
10
, whose conclusion mirrors that of
[
18
]
. From this point onward, the proof proceeds using tools from measure theory.

Proposition 11
.

Assume
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{2})
is a nonnegative supersolution of

\mathcal{L}[u]\leq f(u).

Denote
\mu(t)=|A_{t}\cap B_{2}|
and
\eta(t)=|E_{t}\cap B_{2}|
. Then, for almost every
t>0
such that
\mu(t)\leq|B_{2}|/2
, we have

-\mu^{\prime}(t)\geq c\,\min\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{F(t)}}\mu(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},\frac{1}{t}\mu(t)\right\},

and for almost every
t>0
with
\eta(t)\leq|B_{2}|/2
,

\eta^{\prime}(t)\geq c\,\min\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{F(t)}}\eta(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},\frac{1}{t}\eta(t)\right\}.

We conclude this section with a consequence of an iteration of Proposition
9
, which is particularly useful when the sublevel sets exhibit sufficiently fast decay. In this case, geometric information about supersolutions can be deduced. The proof follows the classical approach used in establishing the Harnack inequality. This is another key point where the fact that the integrability of the lower-order coefficients exceeds the dimension becomes crucial.

Lemma 12
.

Assume
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{2})
is a nonnegative supersolution of

\mathcal{L}[u]\leq f(u).

There is
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\|b_{1}\|_{q},\|b_{2}\|_{q},\|c\|_{q/2})
such that if for some
t>0

|E_{t}\cap B_{2}|\leq\delta_{0}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}\right)^{\frac{qn}{q-n}},

then

\inf_{B_{1}}u\geq\frac{t}{2}.

Proof.

First, we pick
\delta_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}|B_{2}|
. Observe that

|E_{t}\cap B_{2}|\leq\delta_{0}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}\right)^{\frac{qn}{q-n}}\leq\delta_{0}.

Now, given
0<h<k\leq t
, we define
v
as

v=\begin{cases}k&\quad\text{if}\quad u\geq k\\
k-u&\quad\text{if}\quad h<u<k\\
k-h&\quad\text{if}\quad u\leq h.\\
\end{cases}

By Gagliardo-Niremberg-Sobolev inequality for
v
we get

\displaystyle(k-h)|E_{h}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(E_{k}\cap B_{\rho})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\|Dv\|_{L^{1}(E_{k}\cap B_{\rho})}\leq c|E_{k}\cap B_{\rho}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Du\|_{L^{2}(E_{k}\cap B_{\rho})}

for a dimensional constant
c
. By Proposition
9
, for every
0<\rho<R\leq 2
we have

\displaystyle C^{-1}\,\|Du\|_{L^{2}(E_{k}\cap B_{\rho})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{F(k)}+\frac{k}{R-\rho}\right)|E_{k}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{k}{\sqrt{R-r}}|E_{k}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle k|E_{k}\cap B_{R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(4cC)^{-1}(k-h)|E_{h}\cap B_{\rho}|^{1-\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{F(k)}+\frac{k}{R-\rho}\right)|E_{k}\cap B_{R}|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}.

Define
r_{i}=1+2^{-i}
,
k_{i}=\frac{t}{2}(1+2^{-i})
and apply the above inequality with
R=r_{i}
,
\rho=r_{i+1}
,
h=k_{i+1}
and
k=k_{i}
. Set
x_{i}=|E_{k_{i}}\cap B_{r_{i}}|
; then

(4cC)^{-1}\frac{t}{2^{i+2}}x_{i+1}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\left(\sqrt{F(t)}+\frac{t}{2^{-(i+1)}}\right)x_{i}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}.

Rearranging terms, we get

(4cC)^{-1}\,x_{i+1}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq 16\left(\frac{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}{t}\right)4^{i}\,x_{i}^{1-\frac{1}{q}},

and so, since
x_{i}\geq x_{i+1}
, we obtain

(4cC)^{-1}\,x_{i+1}\leq 16\left(\frac{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}{t}\right)4^{i}\,x_{i}^{1-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{n}},

which in turn can be written as

x_{i+1}\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}{t}\right)4^{i}x_{i}^{1+s}\quad\mbox{for}\quad s=\frac{q-n}{qn},

and a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(c,C)
. Recall that by assumption
q>n
, so
s>0
. If
\delta_{0}
is chosen small enough such that

\delta_{0}\leq C_{1}^{-\frac{1}{s}}4^{-\frac{1}{s^{2}}},

then

x_{0}\coloneqq|E_{t}\cap B_{2}|\leq\delta_{0}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}\right)^{\frac{qn}{q-n}}\leq\left(C_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}{t}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{s}}4^{-\frac{1}{s^{2}}}.

We can now apply Lemma
8
to obtain

\lim_{i\to\infty}x_{i}=0,

which means that
\inf_{B_{1}}u\geq t/2
.
∎

4.
Estimates for subsolutions

In this section, we derive estimates for subsolutions. The main result is the following Caccioppoli-type estimate, which extends the one established in
[
18
]
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))
be a subsolution of

\mathcal{L}[u]\geq f(u),

and denote
M_{r}=\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u
. Assume further that there is
\eta>0
such that

(4.1)

\|b_{1}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}+\|b_{2}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}+\|c\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}\leq\eta.

Then there are constants
\beta,\gamma\in(0,1)
depending on
n,q
such that for every
\rho<r
and
t<M_{r}
it holds

\displaystyle c_{2}^{-1}\left(\frac{M_{r}^{2}}{F(t)}\right)^{\beta-1}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}(u-t)_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(r-\rho)^{2}}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})|^{\gamma}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(u-t)_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t+M_{r}}{r-\rho}\right)|A_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})|^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(u-t)^{2}_{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t^{2}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})|^{1+\gamma},

for
c_{2}=c_{2}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)>0
, provided
\eta=\eta(n,\lambda,\Lambda)
is chosen small enough.

Before delivering the proof, a few comments are in place. In
[
18
]
, the author proved a simplified estimate with
\beta=1/2
and
\gamma=1/n
, and the second and third elements of the right-hand side of the inequality were nonexistent. They appear here due to the lower-order coefficients, which significantly slow down the decay. In our case, choosing suitable constants
\beta
and
\gamma
(that depend upon each other) is crucial to ensure a decay of the same order. We emphasize that the integrability of the lower-order coefficients being greater than the dimension is also key in finding those parameters.

Proof.

With no loss of generality, we may assume
x_{0}=0
. We use as test function
\varphi\coloneqq(u-t)_{+}\zeta^{2}
, where
\zeta\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
is a smooth cutoff which satisfies

\zeta=1\quad\mbox{in}\quad B_{\rho},\quad\zeta=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad B_{r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|D\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\leq 2(r-\rho)^{-1}.

We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|D\left((u-t)_{+}\zeta\right)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}|D\zeta|^{2}-\int_{B_{r}}\varphi f(u)+\int_{B_{r}}\zeta^{2}(u-t)_{+}b_{2}\cdot Du

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}cu\varphi-ub_{1}\cdot D\varphi,

for
C=C(\lambda,\Lambda)
. Let us estimate the terms involving
c
and
b_{1}
first. We repeat the very same arguments of
[
16
]
*Theorem 4.1 to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}cu\varphi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|c\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{r})}\left(|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{q}}\int_{B_{r}}|D((u-t)_{+}\zeta)|^{2}+t^{2}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right).

To estimate the
b_{1}
term, we proceed as follows

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}ub_{1}\cdot D\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}tb_{1}\cdot D\varphi+\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}b_{1}\cdot D\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}2t\zeta(u-t)_{+}b_{1}\cdot D\zeta+\int_{B_{r}}t\zeta^{2}b_{1}\cdot D(u-t)_{+}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}2\zeta(u-t)^{2}_{+}b_{1}\cdot D\zeta+\int_{B_{r}}\zeta^{2}(u-t)_{+}b_{1}\cdot D(u-t)_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II+III+IV.

We estimate terms
I
and
III
using the Hölder inequality which leads to

|I|+|III|\leq 4\|b_{1}\|_{q}\left(\frac{t+M_{r}}{r-\rho}\right)|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By Young’s inequality (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle|II|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|t\zeta b_{1}|\,|\zeta D(u-t)_{+}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\epsilon}t^{2}\|b_{1}\|^{2}_{q}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{B_{r}}|\zeta D(u-t)_{+}|^{2},

for any
\epsilon>0
. The term
IV
can be incorporated into the estimate involving the term
b_{2}
. By monotonicity of
f
and for
x\in A_{t}\cap B_{r}
, we have

f(u(x))\geq f(t)\geq\frac{F(t)}{t}\geq\frac{F(t)}{M_{r}^{2}}(u(x)-t),

which readily implies

\int_{B_{r}}f(u)\varphi\geq\frac{F(t)}{M_{r}^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}\zeta^{2}.

Putting these estimates together with the equation information, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|D\left((u-t)_{+}\zeta\right)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(r-\rho)^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}-\frac{F(t)}{M_{r}^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}\zeta^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\zeta^{2}(u-t)_{+}(b_{1}+b_{2})\cdot Du+t^{2}\|c\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{r})}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|c\|_{q/2}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{q}}\int_{B_{r}}|D((u-t)_{+}\zeta)|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4\left(\frac{t+M_{r}}{r-\rho}\right)\|b_{1}\|_{q}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)^{2}_{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\epsilon}t^{2}\|b_{1}\|^{2}_{q}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{B_{r}}|\zeta D(u-t)_{+}|^{2}.

Now, let us concentrate on the third term in the right-hand side of the previous inequality. Define
b\coloneqq b_{1}+b_{2}
. For
\epsilon>0
, Young’s inequality (
3.2
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\zeta^{2}(u-t)_{+}b\cdot Du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\zeta^{2}(u-t)_{+}\,|b|\,|Du|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\zeta(u-t)_{+}\,|b|\,|\zeta\,D(u-t)_{+}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{B_{r}}|\zeta\,D(u-t)_{+}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\int_{B_{r}}\zeta^{2}(u-t)^{2}_{+}\,|b|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{16\epsilon}{(r-\rho)^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}+4\epsilon\int_{B_{r}}|D(\zeta\,(u-t)_{+})|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\int_{B_{r}}\zeta^{2}(u-t)^{2}_{+}\,|b|^{2},

where we have used the relation

D(\zeta(u-t)_{+})=\zeta D(u-t)_{+}+(u-t)_{+}D\zeta.

Therefore,

\displaystyle C_{0}\int_{B_{r}}|D\left((u-t)_{+}\zeta\right)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(r-\rho)^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}-\frac{F(t)}{M_{r}^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}\zeta^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{16\epsilon}{(r-\rho)^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\int_{B_{r}}\zeta^{2}(u-t)^{2}_{+}\,|b|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C(\epsilon,c,b_{1})t^{2}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4\left(\frac{t+M_{r}}{r-\rho}\right)\|b_{1}\|_{q}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)^{2}_{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for
C_{0}=(1-4\epsilon-\|c\|_{q/2})
. Denoting
w=(u-t)_{+}\zeta
, we observe that by the Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}w^{2}\,|b|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|b\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}^{2}\,\|w\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(B_{r})}^{2},

for
q^{\prime}=\frac{2q}{q-2}
. Since
q>n
, we have
q^{\prime}<2^{\star}
, where
2^{\star}
is the critical Sobolev exponent. By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}w^{2}\,|b|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\star}\|b\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}^{2}\,\|Dw\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}^{2},

for a dimensional constant
C^{\star}
. Thus, we obtain

(4.2)

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\int_{B_{r}}|D\left((u-t)_{+}\zeta\right)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(r-\rho)^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}-\frac{F(t)}{M_{r}^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}\zeta^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t^{2}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t+M_{r}}{r-\rho}\right)|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)^{2}_{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

C^{\prime}=\left(1-4\epsilon-\|c\|_{q/2}-\frac{C^{\star}\|b\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}^{2}}{4\epsilon}\right)C_{1}^{-1},\quad\mbox{for}\quad C_{1}=C_{1}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\|b_{1}\|_{q},\|c\|_{q/2}).

Picking
\epsilon=\frac{1}{36}
and assuming, by (
4.1
),
\|c\|_{q/2}+C^{\star}\|b\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}^{2}\leq 1/36
, we have
C^{\prime}\geq 1/2
. By the Hölder and Sobolev inequalities, we have

(4.3)

\int_{B_{r}}w^{2}\leq\left(\int_{B_{r}}w^{2^{\star}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{\star}}}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{1-\frac{2}{2^{\star}}}\leq C_{3}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{2}{n}}\int_{B_{r}}|Dw|^{2},

for some dimensional constant
C_{3}
. Now we let
\beta\in(0,1)
be a positive number satisfying

\beta q>n

which is possible since
q>n
. For this choice, it holds

1-\frac{2}{q}+\frac{2\beta}{n}>1.

We use the following generalized Young’s inequality: given any nonnegative real numbers
a
and
b
,

ab\leq\frac{a^{p_{1}}}{p_{1}}+\frac{b^{p_{1}^{\prime}}}{p_{1}^{\prime}}\quad\text{for}\quad\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}=1-\frac{1}{p_{1}}.

An application of this inequality with
p_{1}=1/\beta
gives

(4.4)

\frac{F(t)}{M_{r}^{2}}+\frac{c^{-1}}{2}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{-2}{n}}\geq\frac{c_{1}^{-1/\beta}}{2\beta(1-\beta)}\left(\frac{F(t)}{M_{r}^{2}}\right)^{1-\beta}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{-\frac{2\beta}{n}}.

We use inequalities (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) to further estimate (
4.2
), obtaining

c_{2}^{-1}\left(\frac{F(t)}{M_{r}^{2}}\right)^{1-\beta}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{-2\beta}{n}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}\zeta^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(r-\rho)^{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}+\left(\frac{t+M_{r}}{r-\rho}\right)|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)^{2}_{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+t^{2}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}}.

Rearranging terms and using that
\zeta=1
in
B_{\rho}
we get

\displaystyle c_{2}^{-1}\int_{B_{\rho}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(r-\rho)^{2}}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{2\beta}{n}}\left(\frac{M_{r}^{2}}{F(t)}\right)^{1-\beta}\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t^{2}|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{1-\frac{2}{q}+\frac{2\beta}{n}}\left(\frac{M_{r}^{2}}{F(t)}\right)^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{M_{r}^{2}}{F(t)}\right)^{1-\beta}\left(\frac{t+M_{r}}{r-\rho}\right)|A_{t}\cap B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{2\beta}{n}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}(u-t)^{2}_{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

The iteration of this result leads to the following lemma.

Lemma 14
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))
be a subsolution to

\mathcal{L}[u]\geq f(u),

and denote
M_{r}\coloneqq\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u
. Assume (
4.1
) holds. There exist a constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)>0
such that if
0<M_{r}\leq 2u(x_{0})
, then

\frac{u(x_{0})}{\sqrt{F\left(\frac{u(x_{0})}{2}\right)}}\geq c_{0}r.

Proof.

Define
v(x)\coloneqq u(x_{0}+rx)
, for
x\in B_{2}
. In this setting,
0<M_{1}\coloneqq\sup_{B_{1}}v\leq 2v(0)
and we have

\mathcal{L}^{r}[v]\geq r^{2}f(v),

where

\mathcal{L}^{r}[v]=\mbox{div}(A(x_{0}+rx)Dv+rb_{1}(x_{0}+rx)v)+rb_{2}(x_{0}+rx)\cdot Dv+r^{2}c(x_{0}+rx)v.

In terms of
v
, we need to show that

\Lambda_{0}\coloneqq\left(\frac{v(0)}{\sqrt{r^{2}F\left(\frac{v(0)}{2}\right)}}\right)^{2}\geq c_{0}^{2}>0,

for some universal constant
c_{0}
. Let
\tau,\rho
be such that
1/2<\rho<\tau<1
and
h,k
such that
v(0)/2\leq h<k\leq v(0)
. We use that
M_{1}\leq 2v(0)
and Lemma
13
to obtain

\displaystyle 4^{\beta-1}c_{2}^{-1}\Lambda_{0}^{\beta-1}\int_{A_{k}\cap B_{\rho}}(v-k)_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\tau-\rho)^{2}}|A_{k}\cap B_{\tau}|^{\gamma}\int_{A_{h}\cap B_{\tau}}(v-h)_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{h+M_{1}}{\tau-\rho}\right)|A_{k}\cap B_{\tau}|^{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma}\left(\int_{A_{h}\cap B_{\tau}}(v-h)^{2}_{+}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 4h^{2}|A_{k}\cap B_{\tau}|^{1+\gamma},

for constants
\beta,\gamma\in(0,1)
. Taking into account that

|A_{k}\cap B_{\tau}|\leq\frac{1}{(k-h)^{2}}\int_{A_{h}\cap B_{\tau}}(v-h)_{+}^{2},

the following inequality holds

4^{-2}c_{2}^{-1}\int_{A_{k}\cap B_{\rho}}(v-k)_{+}^{2}\leq

\Lambda_{0}^{1-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{(\tau-\rho)^{2}}+\frac{h+M_{1}}{(\tau-\rho)(k-h)}+\frac{h^{2}}{(k-h)^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{(k-h)^{2\gamma}}\left(\int_{A_{h}\cap B_{\tau}}(v-h)_{+}^{2}\right)^{1+\gamma}.

Defining
r_{i}=2^{-1}(1+2^{-i})
and
k_{i}=v(0)(3/4-4^{-1-i})
we can write the previous inequality for
h=k_{i}
,
k=k_{i+1}
,
\tau=r_{i}
and
\rho=r_{i+1}
, thus obtaining

4^{-2}c_{2}^{-1}\left(\int_{A_{k_{i}}\cap B_{r_{i}}}(v-k_{i})_{+}^{2}\right)^{-(1+\gamma)}\int_{A_{k_{i+1}}\cap B_{r_{i+1}}}(v-k_{i+1})_{+}^{2}\leq

\Lambda_{0}^{1-\beta}\left(\frac{1}{(r_{i+1}-r_{i})^{2}}+\frac{k_{i}+M_{1}}{(r_{i}-r_{i+1})(k_{i+1}-k_{i})}+\frac{2k_{i}^{2}}{(k_{i+1}-k_{i})^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{(k_{i+1}-k_{i})^{2\gamma}}.

Setting

x_{i}=M_{1}^{-2}\int_{A_{k_{i}}\cap B_{r_{i}}}(v-k_{i})^{2}_{+},

and taking into account the relations
k_{i+1}-k_{i}=4^{-(i+1)}v(0)
, and
r_{i}-r_{i+1}=2^{-(i+2)}
, we have the following recurrence relation

x_{i+1}\leq C\Lambda_{0}^{1-\beta}4^{i}\left(\frac{M_{1}}{v(0)}\right)^{2\gamma}x_{i}^{1+\gamma},

and so, using
M_{1}\leq 2v(0)
once more, we obtain

x_{i+1}\leq C\Lambda_{0}^{1-\beta}4^{i}x_{i}^{1+\gamma},

for a large constant
C
. Now, notice that it should hold

\Lambda_{0}^{1-\beta}\geq|B_{1}|^{-\frac{1}{n}}C^{-1}4^{-n},

otherwise, we would have

|B_{1}|<\Lambda_{0}^{-n(1-\beta)}C^{-n}4^{-n^{2}}.

and we would obtain

\displaystyle x_{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle M_{1}^{-2}\int_{A_{\frac{v(0)}{2}}\cap B_{1}}\left(v-\frac{v(0)}{2}\right)^{2}_{+}\leq|B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}^{-n(1-\beta)}C^{-n}4^{-n^{2}}.

This would then imply by Lemma
8
that
x_{i}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
, meaning that

\sup_{B_{1/2}}v\leq\frac{3}{4}v(0),

which is a contradiction. Thus we have

\Lambda_{0}\geq c_{0}^{2}\coloneqq\left(|B_{1}|^{-\frac{1}{n}}C^{-1}4^{-n}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}.

∎

Finally, taking advantage of the sign of the right-hand side, we prove the following result, which is a consequence of the local maximum principle.

Lemma 15
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))
be a subsolution of

\mathcal{L}[u]\geq 0,

and define
M_{r}\coloneqq\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u
. There exists a positive small constant
\epsilon_{0}
such that if

\left|\left\{u\geq u(x_{0})/2\right\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})\right|\leq\epsilon_{0}|B_{r}(x_{0})|,

then
M_{r}\geq 4u(x_{0})
, provided (
4.1
) holds for some small
\eta=\eta(n,\lambda,\Lambda)>0
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the classical local maximum principle. Define
v=(u-u(x_{0})/2)_{+}
and observe it solves

\mathcal{L}[v]\geq g\,\mathcal{X}_{\{v>0\}}\quad\mbox{for}\quad g=-\frac{u(x_{0})}{2}\left(\mbox{div}(b_{1})+c\right).

By the local maximum principle (see
[
27
]
*Theorem 2.1), we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}v

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|v\|_{L^{1}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}+u(x_{0})\left(r^{2-\frac{2n}{q}}\|c\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}+r^{2-n/q}\|b_{1}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\epsilon_{0}\left(M_{r}-\frac{u(x_{0})}{2}\right)+u(x_{0})r\left(\|c\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}+\|b_{1}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}\right)\right).

As a consequence,

u(x_{0})\left(1+C\epsilon_{0}-Cr\left(\|c\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}+\|b_{1}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}\right)\right)\leq(1-C\epsilon_{0})M_{r}.

Picking
\epsilon_{0}
small enough and using (
4.1
) for some small
\eta
, we obtain
M_{r}\geq 4u(x_{0})
.
∎

5.
Proof of main results

In this section, we gather the results from the previous sections and add the developments that yield the main theorems.

5.1.
On the Keller-Osserman condition

We prove the following lower bound for subsolutions.

Proposition 16
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))
be a continuous and nonnegative subsolution of

\mathcal{L}[u]\geq f(u),

and assume (
4.1
) holds with
r=R
. There is a positive constant
c=c(n,\lambda,\Lambda)
, such that if
u(x_{0})>0
, then

\int_{u(x_{0})/4}^{\sup_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}}\geq cR.

Proof.

With no loss of generality, we may assume
x_{0}=0
. For
k\in\{0,\cdots,K\}
, we select
R_{k}\in(0,R]
such that
R_{0}=0
,
R_{K}=R
and

M_{k}=2M_{k-1}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq k\leq K-1,

and
M_{K}\leq 2M_{K-1}
, where
M_{k}\coloneqq\sup_{B_{R_{k}}}u
, and
M_{0}\coloneqq u(0)
. Indeed, this is always possible by the following reasoning: if
M_{K}\leq 2u(0)
, then
K=1
and we are done. If
M_{K}>2u(0)
, then, by continuity, there should be
R_{1}\in(0,R)
such that
M_{1}=2u(0)
. In the latter case, if
M_{K}\leq 2M_{1}
, then we stop. Otherwise, there should be
R_{2}\in(R_{1},R)
such that
M_{2}=2M_{1}
. We continue doing so until we reach
R_{K-1}
such that
M_{K}\leq 2M_{K-1}
. Observe that
K
is finite, but it depends on the modulus of continuity of
u
. We will see that the estimate does not depend on this number
K
.

Take
x_{k}\in B_{R_{k}}
such that
u(x_{k})=M_{k}
for every
k\in\{1,\cdots,K-1\}
. Denoting
r_{k}\coloneqq R_{k}-R_{k-1}
, it follows that

\sup_{B_{r_{k}}(x_{k-1})}u\leq M_{k}=2M_{k-1}=2u(x_{k-1}),

where the inequality is a consequence of the inclusion
B_{r_{k}}(x_{k-1})\subset B_{R_{k}}
. By Lemma
14
we deduce

\frac{M_{k-1}}{\sqrt{F(M_{k-1}/2)}}\geq cr_{k}.

By monotonicity, we obtain

\int_{M_{k-3}}^{M_{k-2}}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}}\geq\frac{M_{k-3}-M_{k-2}}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}\left(M_{k-2}\right)}}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{M_{k-1}}{\sqrt{F(M_{k-1}/2)}}\geq cr_{k}.

Summing the above inequality from
k=1
to
k=K
, we are led to

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\int_{M_{k-3}}^{M_{k-2}}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}}\geq c\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}=cR,

where we have defined
M_{-1}\coloneqq M_{0}/2
and
M_{-2}\coloneqq M_{0}/4
. The proposition is proved once we realize that

\int_{u(0)/4}^{\sup_{B_{R}}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\int_{M_{k-3}}^{M_{k-2}}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}}.

∎

We next prove our extension of the Keller-Osserman theorem.

Corollary 17
.

Assume that there exists a nontrivial nonnegative entire solution to

\mathcal{L}[u]\geq f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Assume

b_{1}\in L^{q}_{ul}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),b_{2}\in L^{q}_{ul}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\text{and}\quad c\in L^{q/2}_{ul}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then,

(5.1)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}}=\infty.

Proof.

Let
\eta>0
be the quantity from (
4.1
),
c>0
be the universal constant from Proposition
16
and
R>1
be picked up such that

cR-2R^{\frac{n}{q}}\sqrt{\frac{4|B_{2}|^{\frac{2}{q}}}{\eta}}\geq 2^{-1}c,

which is possible since
q>n
. For
r_{0}>0
small such that

r_{0}^{2-\frac{2n}{q}}\left(\|b_{1}\|_{L^{q}_{ul}}+\|b_{2}\|_{L^{q}_{ul}}+\|c\|_{L^{q/2}_{ul}}\right)<\eta/2,

we define
v(x)\coloneqq u(r_{0}x)
. This new function solves

\mathcal{L}_{0}[v]\geq f_{0}(v),

where
f_{0}(t)\coloneqq r_{0}^{2}f(t)
, and

\mathcal{L}_{0}[v]=\mbox{div}(A_{0}(x)Dv+b_{1}^{r_{0}}(x)v)+b_{2}^{r_{0}}(x)\cdot Dv+c_{0}(x)v,

with
b_{1}^{r_{0}}(x)\coloneqq r_{0}b_{1}(r_{0}x)
,
b_{2}^{r_{0}}(x)\coloneqq r_{0}b_{1}(r_{0}x)
and
c_{0}(x)\coloneqq r_{0}^{2}c(r_{0}x)
. In this setting, we have that for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathcal{S}(x_{0})\coloneqq\|b_{1}^{r_{0}}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}+\|b_{2}^{r_{0}}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}+\|c_{0}\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}

satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{0}^{1-\frac{n}{q}}\|b_{1}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2Rr_{0}}(r_{0}x_{0}))}+r_{0}^{1-\frac{n}{q}}\|b_{2}\|_{L^{q}(B_{2Rr_{0}}(r_{0}x_{0}))}+r_{0}^{2-\frac{2n}{q}}\|c\|_{L^{q/2}(B_{2Rr_{0}}(r_{0}x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r_{0}^{2-\frac{2n}{q}}\left(\|b_{1}\|_{L^{q}_{ul}}+\|b_{2}\|_{L^{q}_{ul}}+\|c\|_{L^{q/2}_{ul}}\right)\leq\eta/2,

by the choice of
r_{0}
. Pick
\varepsilon>0
such that

\varepsilon|B_{2R}|^{\frac{2}{q}}=\eta/2,

and notice
v
is also a subsolution to

\overline{\mathcal{L}}[v]\coloneqq\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\varepsilon\right)[v]\geq\overline{f}(v),

where
\overline{f}(t)=f_{0}(t)+\varepsilon t
. We argue now that

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}=\infty,

from which the corollary follows, since

F(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,ds\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\overline{f}(s)\,ds\eqqcolon\overline{F}(t).

We claim that

\int_{u(0)/4}^{\sup_{B_{Rk}(0)}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}\geq 2^{-1}ck,\quad\text{for every }\,k\in\mathbb{N},

where

\overline{F}(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\overline{f}(s)\,ds.

We prove it by induction. For the case
k=1
, it follows from Proposition
16
that

\int_{u(0)/4}^{\sup_{B_{R}}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}\geq cR,

where we used that
R>1
. Now, assume it holds for
k
and let us prove it also holds for
k+1
. Let
\overline{x}
be the point such that
u(\overline{x})=\sup_{B_{Rk}(0)}u
. Since
u(0)>0
, it also follows that
u(\overline{x})>0
. We can then apply Proposition
16
in
B_{2R}(\overline{x})
to obtain

\int_{u(\overline{x})/4}^{\sup_{B_{R}(\overline{x})}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}\geq cR.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{u(0)/4}^{\sup_{B_{R(k+1)}(0)}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{u(0)/4}^{u(\overline{x})}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}+\int_{u(\overline{x})}^{\sup_{B_{R(k+1)}(0)}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{u(0)/4}^{\sup_{B_{Rk}(0)}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}+\int_{u(\overline{x})}^{\sup_{B_{R}(\overline{x})}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2^{-1}ck+\int_{u(\overline{x})}^{\sup_{B_{R}(\overline{x})}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}},

where we used that
\sup_{B_{R}(\overline{x})}u\leq\sup_{B_{R(k+1)}(0)}u
, which comes from the fact that
B_{R}(\overline{x})\subset B_{R(k+1)}(0)
. Now, we write

\int_{u(\overline{x})}^{\sup_{B_{R}(\overline{x})}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}=\int_{u(\overline{x})/4}^{\sup_{B_{R}(\overline{x})}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}-\int_{u(\overline{x})/4}^{u(\overline{x})}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}.

Observe that
\overline{f}(t)\geq\varepsilon t
, and so

\overline{F}(t)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}t^{2},

and so

\int_{u(\overline{x})/4}^{u(\overline{x})}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\varepsilon}}\int_{u(\overline{x})/4}^{u(\overline{x})}\frac{dt}{t}\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\varepsilon}}.

This implies that

\int_{u(0)/4}^{\sup_{B_{R(k+1)}(0)}u}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}\geq 2^{-1}ck+cR-2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\varepsilon}}.

By the choice of
R
, we have

\displaystyle cR-2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\varepsilon}}\geq 2^{-1}c

from which the claim follows. Passing to the limit as
k\to\infty
in this claim, we have

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\overline{F}(t)}}=\infty.

∎

5.2.
Proof of generalized Harnack inequality

Now we are ready to deliver the

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We may assume
u
is smooth; the general case follows by standard approximation. Replace
u
by
v(x)\coloneqq u(x_{0}+rx)
, for
x_{0}\in B_{1}
. As above, for
r
small enough, this function solves a PDE whose coefficients have norms smaller than any a priori given constant, with right-hand side
\tilde{f}=r^{2}f
.

Recalling the notation from Proposition
11
, let
t_{0}
be defined as

t_{0}\coloneqq\sup\left\{t>0\colon\eta(t)\leq\frac{|B_{2}|}{2}\right\}.

The proof has two steps. We will first show that there exists a constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\|b_{1}\|_{q},\|b_{2}\|_{q},\|c\|_{q/2})>0
such that

\int_{\inf_{B_{1}}v}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}\leq C_{0}.

To do so, we argue as follows: for
\delta_{0}
as in Lemma
12
, there is a constant
C
depending on
\delta_{0}
such that either

(5.2)

\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}\leq C,

or there exists
t_{\delta}\in(0,t_{0})
such that

(5.3)

\eta(t_{\delta})\leq\delta_{0}\left(\frac{t_{\delta}}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(t_{\delta})}+t_{\delta}}\right)^{\frac{qn}{q-n}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{t_{\delta}}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}\leq C.

We assume this is not true. Then

\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}>C,

for any given
C
, and there holds

\eta(t)\geq\delta_{0}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(t)}+t}\right)^{\frac{qn}{q-n}}\quad\mbox{for every}\quad t\in(0,t_{0}),

or

\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}>C\quad\mbox{for every}\quad t\in(0,t_{0}).

In the latter case, if we take
t=t_{0}/2
, we have

C<\int_{t_{0}/2}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}\leq\ln(t_{0})-\ln(t_{0}/2)=\ln(2),

which is a contradiction for
C>0
large. If, on the other hand, we have

\eta(t)\geq\delta_{0}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(t)}+t}\right)^{\frac{qn}{q-n}}\quad\mbox{for every}\quad t\in(0,t_{0}),

then

\frac{1}{t}\geq\delta_{0}^{\frac{q-n}{qn}}\eta(t)^{-\frac{q-n}{qn}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(t)}+t},

and so, by Proposition
11
, we have

\eta^{\prime}(t)\geq c\,\min\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(t)}}\eta(t)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},\frac{1}{t}\eta(t)\right\}\geq c\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(t)}+t}\min\left\{\eta(t)^{1-\frac{1}{n}},\eta(t)^{1-\frac{q-n}{qn}}\right\}.

Observe that since

1-\frac{1}{n}<1-\frac{q-n}{qn},

and
\eta(t)\leq\frac{|B_{2}|}{2}
, we have

\min\left\{\eta(t)^{1-\frac{1}{n}},\eta(t)^{1-\frac{q-n}{qn}}\right\}=\eta(t)^{1-\frac{q-n}{qn}},

and so

\eta^{\prime}(t)\geq c\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(t)}+t}\eta(t)^{1-\frac{q-n}{qn}}.

But this is equivalent to saying that

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\eta(t)^{\frac{q-n}{qn}}\right)\geq c_{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(t)}+t},

which implies, after integrating from
t
to
t_{0}
,

|B_{2}|^{\frac{q-n}{qn}}\geq c_{1}\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}ds\quad\mbox{for every}\quad t\in(0,t_{0}).

By Fatou’s lemma

\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}\leq\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s},

which is a contradiction with

\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}>C,

if
C
is large enough again. This implies that (
5.3
) should hold for some
t_{\delta}
. But then, Lemma
12
implies that

\inf_{B_{1}}v\geq t_{\delta}/2,

and so

\displaystyle\int_{\inf_{B_{1}}v}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{t_{\delta}}{2}}^{t_{\delta}}+\int_{t_{\delta}}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{t_{\delta}}{2}}^{t_{\delta}}\frac{ds}{s}+\int_{t_{\delta}}^{t_{0}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}\leq C_{0},

for some universal constant
C_{0}
. To finish the proof, it remains to show that

\int_{t_{0}}^{\sup_{B_{1}}v}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}\leq C_{0}.

For a given small
\epsilon>0
to be chosen later, we let
t_{\epsilon}\geq t_{0}
be the first value such that
\mu(t_{\epsilon})\leq\epsilon
, that is

t_{\epsilon}\coloneqq\sup\{t>0\colon\mu(t)>\epsilon\}

(recall
\mu(t)=|A_{t}\cap B_{2}|
). Notice that we must have
t_{\epsilon}>t_{0}
since by the very definition of
t_{0}
we have
\eta(t_{0})=|B_{2}|/2
, and so, by taking into account that
\eta(t_{0})+\mu(t_{0})=|B_{2}|
, we have
\mu(t_{0})=|B_{2}|/2>\epsilon
, as long as
\epsilon
is small enough. This implies that
\mu(t)>\epsilon
for
t\in[t_{0},t_{\epsilon})
, and so, by Proposition
11
, we have

-\mu^{\prime}(t)\geq c\frac{\mu^{1-\frac{1}{n}}(t)}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(t)}+t},

for some constant
c
that depends on
\epsilon
. This is equivalent to saying that

-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mu^{\frac{1}{n}}(t)\right)\geq\frac{c}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(t)}+t},

which leads, by integration from
t_{0}
to
t_{\epsilon}
, to

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{\epsilon}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}\leq C.

The proof now is done once we show that for
\epsilon
small enough we have
\sup_{B_{1}}v\leq 2t_{\epsilon}
. Indeed, if this is true, then by using what we have just proved, we obtain

\int_{t_{0}}^{\sup_{B_{1}}v}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{\epsilon}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\tilde{F}(s)}+s}+\int_{t_{\epsilon}}^{2t_{\epsilon}}\frac{ds}{s}\leq C.

Let us assume for contradiction that
\sup_{B_{1}}v>2t_{\epsilon}
. Recall that by the definition of
t_{\epsilon}
, we have

|\{v\geq t_{\epsilon}\}\cap B_{2}|\leq\epsilon.

Now, consider
x_{0}
and
y_{0}
such that
v(x_{0})=\sup_{B_{1}}v
and
v(y_{0})=\sup_{B_{3/2}}v
. Notice that

B_{5/4-|x_{0}|}(x_{0})\subset B_{5/4}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{7/4-|y_{0}|}(y_{0})\subset B_{7/4}.

In particular, by definition of
t_{\epsilon}
, we have

\max\left\{\left|\left\{v\geq v(x_{0})/2\right\}\cap B_{5/4-|x_{0}|}(x_{0})\right|,\left|\left\{v\geq v(y_{0})/2\right\}\cap B_{7/4-|y_{0}|}(y_{0})\right|\right\}\leq\epsilon,

and so, by Lemma
15
, we get

(5.4)

\sup_{B_{5/4}}v\geq 4\sup_{B_{1}}v\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{B_{7/4}}v\geq 4\sup_{B_{3/2}}v.

Consider a sequence of radii
1=R_{0}<R_{1}<\cdots
, so that
M_{j}=\sup_{B_{R_{j}}}u
satisfies the relation

M_{j}=2M_{j-1}\quad\text{for}\quad j\geq 1.

We do it until we find
R_{K}\geq 3/2
. By (
5.4
), we obtain that
K\geq 3
and
R_{K}\leq 7/4
, which ensures it is well-defined. For
j\geq 1
, we define
r_{j}\coloneqq R_{j}-R_{j-1}
. We claim that there exists a small
c>0
such that

\mu^{\frac{1}{n}}(t)\geq cr_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad t\in(M_{j-3},M_{j-2}],

for
j\geq 3
. Since
\mu
is monotone, it is enough to prove this for
t=M_{j-2}
. We assume the contrary to get

\mu\left(M_{j-2}\right)\leq\epsilon_{1}\left|B_{r_{j}}\right|.

By the construction of the sequence
M_{j}
, this is equivalent to

\left|\left\{v\geq M_{j-1}/2\right\}\cap B_{2}\right|\leq\epsilon_{1}\left|B_{r_{j}}\right|.

If
x_{j-1}\in B_{R_{j-1}}
is such that
v\left(x_{j-1}\right)=M_{j-1}
, then by Lemma
15
we have

\sup_{B_{r_{j}}\left(x_{j-1}\right)}v\geq 4M_{j-1}.

Taking into account the inclusion
B_{r_{j}}\left(x_{j-1}\right)\subset B_{R_{j}}
, it follows that
M_{j}\geq\sup_{B_{r_{j}}\left(x_{j-1}\right)}v
. But
M_{j}=2M_{j-1}
, leading to

M_{j}\geq 4M_{j-1}=2M_{j},

which is a contradiction. Now, since

\sup_{B_{r_{j}}\left(x_{j-1}\right)}v\leq M_{j}=2M_{j-1}=2v\left(x_{j-1}\right),

we can use Lemma
14
to obtain

\frac{M_{j-1}}{\sqrt{F(M_{j-1}/2)}}\geq cr_{j},

and by the monotonicity of
F

\frac{t}{\sqrt{F(t)}}\geq cr_{j}\quad\text{for}\quad t\in(M_{j-3},M_{j-2}].

We now use Proposition
11
to obtain

-\mu^{\prime}(t)\geq\frac{c}{t}\mu^{1-\frac{1}{n}}(t)r_{j},

which is equivalent to

-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mu^{\frac{1}{n}}(t)\right)\geq\frac{c}{t}r_{j}.

Integrating over
(M_{j-3},M_{j-2})
and using the relation
M_{j-2}=2M_{j-3}
, we conclude that

\mu^{\frac{1}{n}}(M_{j-3})-\mu^{\frac{1}{n}}(M_{j-2})\geq cr_{j}.

Summing the inequality in
j\geq 3
, we have

\mu^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(\sup_{B_{1}}v\right)\geq c\sum_{j=3}^{K}r_{j}.

But observe that since
R_{K}\geq 3/2
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{K}r_{j}=R_{K}-R_{0}\geq 1/2.

Moreover, since
R_{2}\leq 5/4
, we have
r_{1}+r_{2}\leq 1/4
, which implies that

\sum_{j=3}^{K}r_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{K}r_{j}-(r_{1}+r_{2})\geq 1/4,

and so

\mu^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(\sup_{B_{1}}v\right)\geq c/4

However we are assuming
\sup_{B_{1}}v>2t_{\epsilon}
, and so by the definition of
t_{\epsilon}
we have

4^{-n}c^{n}\leq\mu\left(\sup_{B_{1}}v\right)\leq\mu(t_{\epsilon})\leq\epsilon,

which is a contradiction for
\epsilon
small.

This proves the generalized Harnack inequality for
v
, which, scaling back to
u
, looks like

(5.5)

\int_{\inf_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u}^{\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u}\frac{ds}{r\sqrt{F(s)}+s}\leq C,

for some constant
C>0
, and any
x_{0}\in B_{1}
. To extend it to the whole
B_{1}
, we proceed via a Harnack chain argument. Let
x_{min}
and
x_{max}
be the points where the infimum and supremum are attained in
B_{1}
. Consider
\{B_{r}(x_{i})\}
be a family of balls such that
B_{r}(x_{i})\cap B_{r}(x_{i+1})\not=\emptyset
for every
i\in\{0,1\cdots,k\}
for some
k
that depends only on dimension and
r
, with
x_{0}=x_{min}
and
x_{max}=x_{k}
. First, selecting
y_{1}\in B_{r}(x_{0})\cap B_{r}(x_{1})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\inf_{B_{1}}u}^{\sup_{B_{1}}u}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{F(s)}+s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\inf_{B_{1}}u}^{u(y_{1})}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{F(s)}+s}+\int_{u(y_{1})}^{\sup_{B_{1}}u}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{F(s)}+s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\inf_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u}^{\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u}\frac{ds}{r\sqrt{F(s)}+s}+\int_{\inf_{B_{r}(x_{1})}u}^{\sup_{B_{1}}u}\frac{ds}{r\sqrt{F(s)}+s}.

Now we select
y_{2}\in B_{r}(x_{1})\cap B_{r}(x_{2})
and repeat the same for the second integral. We continue doing so until reaching the last ball in the family of balls. Therefore

\int_{\inf_{B_{1}}u}^{\sup_{B_{1}}u}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{F(s)}+s}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\int_{\inf_{B_{r}(x_{i})}u}^{\sup_{B_{r}(x_{i})}u}\frac{ds}{r\sqrt{F(s)}+s}.

Each one of the integrals in the sum is controlled by (
5.5
), leading to

\int_{\inf_{B_{1}}u}^{\sup_{B_{1}}u}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{F(s)}+s}\leq kC.

Since
k
is universal, the theorem is proved.
∎

5.3.
Proof of the strong maximum principle

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is a nontrivial supersolution to

\mathcal{L}[u]\leq f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.

Arguing as in Step 1 of
[
27
]
*Proof of Theorem 1.1, we can find a ball

B^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
such that
\mathrm{essinf}_{B^{\prime}}u=0
but the trace of
u
on
\partial B^{\prime}
does not vanish identically.

We may assume
f\geq 0
without loss of generality (replace
f
by
f^{+}
). As explained in Step 1 of
[
27
]
*Proof of Theorem 1.1, since
0\leq u
are respectively a sub- and a supersolution, the generalized sub- and super-solution method yields a
solution
of
\mathcal{L}[\tilde{u}]=f(\tilde{u})
in
B^{\prime}
, such that
0\leq\tilde{u}\leq u
in
B^{\prime}
and
\tilde{u}={\min(u,1)}
on
\partial B^{\prime}
in the trace sense (the latter is preventing
\tilde{u}
from vanishing identically). In addition, since
\tilde{u}\geq 0
satisfies
\mathcal{L}[\tilde{u}]\geq 0
in
B^{\prime}
and
\tilde{u}\leq 1
on
\partial B^{\prime}
, we can apply
[
29
, Theorem 8.15]
to
\mathcal{L}[\tilde{u}-1]\geq\mathrm{div}(b_{1})+c
and deduce that

\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(B^{\prime})
and
f(\tilde{u})\in L^{\infty}(B^{\prime})
, so
\tilde{u}
is (Hölder) continuous by the De Giorgi-Moser theory (
[
29
, Theorem 8.15]
). By assumption, since
\mathrm{essinf}_{B^{\prime}}u=0
, it follows that
\min_{B^{\prime}}\tilde{u}=0
. This would imply, by Theorem
2
, that

\int_{0}^{\max_{B^{\prime}}\tilde{u}}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{F(t)}+t}\leq C,

for some universal constant
C
. But this contradicts the assumption of Theorem
3
.
∎

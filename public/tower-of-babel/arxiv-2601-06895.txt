1
Introduction and preliminaries

The generalized harmonic numbers of order
p
are defined by

H_{n}^{(p)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k^{p}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k^{p}}-\frac{1}{{(k+n)}^{p}}\right),\quad H_{n}^{(1)}:=H_{n},\quad p,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},

(1)

or equivalently via the integral representation

H_{n}^{(p)}=\frac{{(-1)}^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln^{p-1}(x)(1-x^{n})}{1-x}\,\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\Re(n)>-1.

(2)

In
[
3
, Theorem 1]
, we have the series

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k/n}}{k^{2q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2q+1}+2q+1}{2n^{2q}}\zeta(2q+1)-\frac{1}{n^{2q}}\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}n^{2j}\zeta(2j)\zeta(2q+1-2j)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi}{2n^{2q}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\cot\!\left(\frac{j\pi}{n}\right)\zeta\!\left(2q,\frac{n-j}{n}\right),\quad n,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},

(3)

where

\zeta(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{s}},\quad\Re(s)>1

denotes the Riemann zeta function, and

\zeta(s,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{(k+t)}^{s}},\quad\Re(s)>1,\;t\neq 0,-1,-2,\dots

denotes the Hurwitz zeta function. The special case

\frac{1}{4}\zeta\left(2,\frac{1}{4}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\zeta\left(2,\frac{3}{4}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)}^{n}}{{(2n+1)}^{2}}=G

defines the Catalan constant.

In this work, we evaluate the generalized version of the series in (
3
), namely (see Theorem
2
)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k/n}^{(p)}}{k^{q}},\quad p,q,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\,q\neq 1,

for odd values of
p+q
, in terms of the Riemann zeta function and the Hurwitz zeta function by first employing the integral representation of the generalized harmonic numbers
H_{k/n}^{(p)}
, and then evaluating the resulting integral (see Theorem
1
)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln(1-x^{n})\ln^{p-1}(x)\,\operatorname{Li}_{q}(x)}{x}\,\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad p,q,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},

for even values of
p+q
, where the polylogarithm function is defined by

\operatorname{Li}_{s}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k^{s}},\quad\begin{cases}|x|<1,&s\in\mathbb{C},\\
x=1,&\Re(s)>1,\\
x=-1,&\Re(s)>0.\end{cases}

We also consider evaluating the related alternating series (see Theorem
3
)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)}^{k}H_{k/2n}^{(p)}}{k^{q}},\quad p,q,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\,q\neq 1,

for odd values of
p+q
.

The formulas derived here appear to be new in the literature and yield results (see Section
4
) that are not accessible through computation software such as
Mathematica
and
Maple
.

2
Lemmas

Lemma 1
.

Let
q,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Then

\int_{0}^{1}x^{n-1}\operatorname{Li}_{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x={(-1)}^{q+1}\frac{H_{n}}{n^{q}}-\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\frac{{(-1)}^{j}}{n^{j}}\zeta(q-j+1),

where
H_{n}
is defined in (
1
),
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function, and
\operatorname{Li}_{s}(x)
is the polylogarithm function.

Proof.

Given in
[
1
, p. 194]
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
\partial_{n}^{p}
denote the
pth
derivative with respect to
n
. Then

\partial_{n}^{p}\,\frac{H_{nk}}{n^{q}}=\frac{{(-1)}^{p}p!}{n^{p+q}}\binom{p+q-1}{p}H_{nk}+\frac{{(-1)}^{p}p!}{n^{p+q}}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\binom{p+q-r-1}{p-r}\,{(nk)}^{r}\left(H_{nk}^{(r+1)}-\zeta(r+1)\right),

where
H_{nk}^{(p)}
is defined in (
1
),
\binom{m}{n}
is the binomial coefficient, and
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function.

Proof.

Using the Leibniz rule

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{p}\bigl(f_{n}\cdot h_{n}\bigr)=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{p}\binom{p}{r}\,\partial_{n}^{r}\,f_{n}\cdot\partial_{n}^{p-r}\,h_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,f_{n}\cdot\partial_{n}^{p}\,h_{n}+\sum_{r=1}^{p}\binom{p}{r}\,\partial_{n}^{r}\,f_{n}\cdot\partial_{n}^{p-r}\,h_{n},

with
f_{n}=H_{nk}
and
h_{n}=1/n^{q}
, we have

\partial_{n}^{p}\,\frac{H_{nk}}{n^{q}}=H_{nk}\cdot\partial_{n}^{p}\,\frac{1}{n^{q}}+\sum_{r=1}^{p}\binom{p}{r}\,\partial_{n}^{r}\,H_{nk}\cdot\partial_{n}^{p-r}\frac{1}{n^{q}}.

(4)

From the series definition of the generalized harmonic number we see that

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{r}\,H_{nk}=

\displaystyle\,{(-1)}^{r+1}k^{r}r!\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{(j+nk)}^{r+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,{(-1)}^{r+1}k^{r}r!\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{{(j+nk)}^{r+1}}-\frac{1}{j^{r+1}}+\frac{1}{j^{r+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,{(-1)}^{r}k^{r}r!\left(H_{nk}^{(r+1)}-\zeta(r+1)\right),\quad r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

Substituting this together with

\partial_{n}^{p-r}\,\frac{1}{n^{q}}=\frac{{(-1)}^{p-r}(p-r)!}{n^{q+p-r}}\binom{q+p-r-1}{q-1}

in (
4
) completes the proof of Lemma
2
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
p,q,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\,q\neq 1
with odd
p+q
. Then

\displaystyle S(p,q,n):=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{nk}^{(p)}}{k^{q}}=\frac{1}{2n^{p}}\zeta(p+q)-\frac{{(-1)}^{q}}{n^{q}}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{q-1}{2}\rfloor}\binom{p+q-2j-1}{p-1}{n}^{-2j}\zeta(2j)\zeta(p+q-2j)

\displaystyle+\frac{{(-1)}^{p}}{n^{p}}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\rfloor}\binom{p+q-2j-1}{q-1}\zeta(2j)\zeta(p+q-2j)

\displaystyle+\frac{{(-1)}^{p}}{2n^{p}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\binom{p+q-k-1}{q-1}{(-1)}^{k}\Theta(k,j,n)\zeta\bigg(p+q-k,\frac{n-j}{n}\bigg),

where
H_{nk}^{(p)}
is defined in (
1
),
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
is the floor function,
\binom{m}{n}
is the binomial coefficient,
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function,
\zeta(s,t)
is the Hurwitz zeta function, and

\Theta(k,j,n)=\zeta\bigg(k,\frac{j}{n}\bigg)+{(-1)}^{k}\zeta\bigg(k,\frac{n-j}{n}\bigg)\quad\text{with}\quad\Theta(1,j,n)=\pi\cot\bigg(\frac{\pi j}{n}\bigg).

Proof.

It follows from the generalization given in
[
2
, Theorem 5]
.
∎

3
Main results

Theorem 1
.

Let
p,q,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
with even
p+q
. Then

\displaystyle T(p,q,n):=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln(1-x^{n})\ln^{p-1}(x)\operatorname{Li}_{q}(x)}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{(p-1)!}{n^{p+q-1}}\binom{p+q-2}{p-1}S(1,p+q,n)

\displaystyle-\frac{(p-1)!}{n^{p+q-1}}\sum_{r=1}^{p-1}n^{r}\binom{p+q-r-2}{p-r-1}\big\{S(r+1,p+q-r,n)-\zeta(r+1)\zeta(p+q-r)\big\}

\displaystyle+\frac{{(-1)}^{p-1}(p-1)!}{n^{p-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}\frac{{(-1)}^{j}}{n^{j}}\binom{j+p-2}{j-1}\zeta(q-j+1)\zeta(p+j),

where
\binom{m}{n}
is the binomial coefficient,
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function,
\operatorname{Li}_{s}(x)
is the polylogarithm function, and
S(p,q,n)
is the series given in Lemma
3
.

Proof.

By expanding
\ln(1-x^{n})
into its Taylor series, interchanging the order of integration and summation, differentiating under the integral sign, and then applying Lemma
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln(1-x^{n})\ln^{p-1}(x)\operatorname{Li}_{q}(x)}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{1}x^{nk-1}\ln^{p-1}(x)\operatorname{Li}_{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{p}}\,\partial_{n}^{p-1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{nk-1}\operatorname{Li}_{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)}^{q}}{k^{p+q}}\,\partial_{n}^{p-1}\,\frac{H_{nk}}{n^{q}}+\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}{(-1)}^{j}\zeta(q-j+1)\zeta(p+j)\,\partial_{n}^{p-1}\,\frac{1}{n^{j}}.

The proof completes on substituting the result given in Lemma
2
. ∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
p,q,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\,q\neq 1
with odd
p+q
. Then

A(p,q,n):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k/n}^{(p)}}{k^{q}}=\,\zeta(p)\zeta(q)-\frac{{(-n)}^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}\big\{T(p,q-1,n)+(p-1)T(p-1,q,n)\big\},

where
H_{k/n}^{(p)}
is defined in (
2
),
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function, and
T(p,q,n)
is the integral given in Theorem
1
.

Proof.

Performing the substitution
t=x^{n}
in the integral representation of the generalized harmonic number and integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle H_{k/n}^{(p)}=

\displaystyle\frac{{(-1)}^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln^{p-1}(t)(1-t^{k/n})}{1-t}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{{(-1)}^{p-1}n^{p}}{(p-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{n-1}\ln^{p-1}(x)(1-x^{k})}{1-x^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\zeta(p)-\frac{{(-n)}^{p-1}}{(p-2)!}\int_{0}^{1}x^{k-1}\ln(1-x^{n})\ln^{p-2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{{(-n)}^{p-1}k}{(p-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}x^{k-1}\ln(1-x^{n})\ln^{p-1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Dividing both sides by
k^{q}
, summing over integers
k\geq 1
, interchanging the order of integration and summation, and then using the identity
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k^{s}}=\operatorname{Li}_{s}(x),
we obtain

\displaystyle A(p,q,n)=

\displaystyle\,\zeta(p)\zeta(q)-\frac{{(-n)}^{p-1}}{(p-2)!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln(1-x^{n})\ln^{p-2}(x)\operatorname{Li}_{q}(x)}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\frac{{(-n)}^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln(1-x^{n})\ln^{p-1}(x)\operatorname{Li}_{q-1}(x)}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\zeta(p)\zeta(q)-\frac{{(-n)}^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}\big\{(p-1)T(p-1,q,n)+T(p,q-1,n)\big\},

and the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 1
.

The case
A(1,2q,n)
simplifies to (
3
) because, when
p=1
, the term
\zeta(p)\zeta(q)
is canceled by the term arising from
\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{q-1}{2}\rfloor}
that appears in Lemma
3
, upon using the summation identity

\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{q-1}{2}\rfloor}f(2j)=-\frac{1}{2}\big(1+{(-1)}^{q}\big)f(q)+\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{q}{2}\rfloor}f(2j).

Theorem 3
.

Let
p,q,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\,q\neq 1
with odd
p+q
. Then

B(p,q,n):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)}^{k}H_{k/2n}^{(p)}}{k^{q}}=2^{1-q}A(p,q,n)-A(p,q,2n),

where
H_{k/n}^{(p)}
is defined in (
2
) and
A(p,q,n)
is the series given in Theorem
2
.

Proof.

The result follows immediately from the identity

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{(-1)}^{k}a_{k}=2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{2k}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k},

applied with
a_{k}=H_{k/2n}^{(p)}/k^{q}
.
∎

Remark 2
.

The series
B(p,1,n)
converges; however, since
B(p,q,n)
is expressed in terms of
A(p,q,n)
and
A(p,q,2n)
, which both diverge when
q=1
, we require
q\neq 1
in Theorem
3
.

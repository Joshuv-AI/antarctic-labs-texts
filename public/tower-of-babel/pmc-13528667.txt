In vitro Fertilisation and Prolonged Continuous Positive Airway Pressure are Associated with Treatment-Requiring Retinopathy of Prematurity in a Tertiary Referral Cohort: An Exploratory Clinical Epidemiology Study
Authors: Omer Othman Abdullah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo investigate the associations of in vitro fertilisation (IVF) conception and prolonged continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) with treatment-requiring retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) among infants with established ROP referred to a tertiary vitreoretinal center.MethodsThis retrospective exploratory referral-cohort study included 60 consecutive infants with established ROP evaluated at a tertiary retina center in Erbil, Iraq, between April 2023 and April 2025. The primary outcome was treatment-requiring ROP, defined as disease requiring laser photocoagulation, intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor therapy, or vitreoretinal surgery because of Type 1, aggressive, or Stage 4–5 ROP. The primary exposures were IVF conception and CPAP duration (>10 vs ≤10 days, including no CPAP). Associations were summarized using crude odds ratios (ORs), absolute risk differences with 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and two-sided Fisher’s exact tests. Because of sparse data, complete outcome separation, overlap between IVF and prolonged CPAP, non-independence among twin observations, and incomplete neonatal covariate documentation, adjusted regression analyses were not presented.ResultsTreatment-requiring ROP occurred in 17 of 20 IVF-conceived infants (85.0%) compared with 18 of 40 spontaneously conceived infants (45.0%), corresponding to a crude OR of 6.93 (95% CI, 1.75–27.43) and an absolute risk difference of 40.0% points (95% CI, 14.1–57.3; Fisher’s exact P=0.0048). Treatment-requiring ROP occurred in 35 of 52 infants (67.3%) requiring CPAP for >10 days and in none of eight infants receiving no CPAP or CPAP for ≤10 days. The continuity-corrected descriptive OR was 34.49 (95% CI, 1.88–632.45), reflecting complete outcome separation and substantial statistical uncertainty. Three term infants with prolonged neonatal respiratory morbidity also developed treatment-requiring ROP. All findings were interpreted as exploratory associations within a selected referral population.ConclusionAmong infants with established ROP referred to a tertiary vitreoretinal center, treatment-requiring disease occurred more frequently in IVF-conceived infants and in those requiring prolonged CPAP. However, these associations were strongly influenced by exposure overlap, sparse data, referral selection, and incomplete neonatal information, precluding reliable causal inference. The findings should therefore be regarded as hypothesis-generating and require confirmation in adequately powered prospective multicenter studies before informing screening strategies or clinical practice.

Introduction

Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) remains one of the leading preventable causes of childhood blindness worldwide and continues to represent an important public health challenge, particularly in middle-income countries where improvements in neonatal survival have not always been accompanied by equivalent expansion of neonatal intensive care and ophthalmic screening services.1–11 Although low gestational age (GA) and low birth weight (BW) remain the principal determinants of ROP and form the basis of current international screening recommendations,2–5,12 substantial variability in disease severity persists among infants with similar GA and BW, suggesting that additional prenatal and postnatal factors may contribute to disease progression.13

Among these factors, prolonged respiratory support has attracted increasing attention. Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) is widely used in neonatal intensive care to manage respiratory distress while reducing the need for invasive mechanical ventilation. However, prolonged CPAP frequently reflects greater respiratory morbidity and may be associated with extended oxygen supplementation, intermittent hypoxaemia, pulmonary immaturity, and overall illness severity, all of which have plausible biological links to abnormal retinal vascular development.14–16 Consequently, CPAP duration should be regarded primarily as a marker of respiratory-support burden rather than a direct measure of cumulative oxygen exposure.

Assisted reproductive technologies, particularly in vitro fertilisation (IVF), have also been investigated as potential contributors to severe ROP.6,17 Infants conceived through IVF are more likely to experience multiple gestation, prematurity, low birth weight, and neonatal respiratory complications, each of which is independently associated with severe ROP.6,18 Previous studies have produced inconsistent findings regarding whether IVF itself contributes independently to disease severity after accounting for these correlated perinatal factors.17 Consequently, the independent contribution of IVF remains uncertain.

Most published studies have focused on identifying risk factors for the development of ROP within neonatal screening cohorts. In contrast, relatively few investigations have specifically examined factors associated with disease severity among infants already diagnosed with ROP and referred to tertiary vitreoretinal centers, where referral patterns and clinical case-mix differ substantially from population-based screening programmes. Although referral cohorts cannot estimate disease incidence or establish causality, they provide valuable opportunities to identify clinically relevant associations that may generate hypotheses for future prospective investigation.

Evidence describing severe ROP from the Middle East remains comparatively limited despite increasing neonatal survival and expanding neonatal intensive care services. Improved understanding of disease characteristics within this region may assist clinicians in recognising infants with complex disease while informing the design of future multicenter epidemiological studies.

Therefore, this exploratory clinical epidemiology study aimed to investigate the associations of IVF conception and prolonged CPAP with treatment-requiring ROP among infants with established ROP referred to a tertiary vitreoretinal center in Iraq. Recognising the inherent limitations of retrospective referral cohorts—including sparse data, exposure overlap, referral selection, and residual confounding—we deliberately interpret our findings as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than evidence of independent causal relationships.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This retrospective exploratory referral-cohort study was conducted at the Ibinsina Modern Eye and Retina Center, Erbil, Iraq, a tertiary referral center specializing in vitreoretinal diseases and retinopathy of prematurity (ROP). The study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guideline.19

The study included all consecutive eligible infants with established ROP who were evaluated between April 2023 and April 2025. Because inclusion required a confirmed diagnosis of ROP and referral to a tertiary vitreoretinal center, the study was designed to investigate associations with disease severity within a referral population rather than estimate the incidence or prevalence of ROP in the general neonatal population.

Ethical Approval

This study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ibinsina Modern Eye and Retina Centre Research Ethics Committee, Erbil, Iraq (Approval No. IMERC-REC-2023-123). Written informed consent for publication of anonymised clinical information and retinal imaging was obtained from the parents or legal guardians of all included infants.

Study Population

Medical records of infants diagnosed with ROP were reviewed retrospectively.

A total of 83 infants were assessed for eligibility. Twenty-three infants were excluded because parental consent required by the institutional ethics committee could not be obtained. Consequently, the final analytical cohort comprised 60 infants.

Because study inclusion depended upon referral to a tertiary vitreoretinal center and completion of the institutional consent process, the possibility of referral-related and consent-related selection bias cannot be excluded.

Study Framework

This investigation was designed as an exploratory clinical epidemiology study.

Unlike neonatal screening cohorts, the present study was conditioned on two sequential selection processes: (1) diagnosis of ROP and (2) referral to a tertiary vitreoretinal center. Consequently, the reported associations describe disease severity among referred infants with established ROP and should not be interpreted as estimates of disease incidence, population-level risk, or independent causal effects.

A conceptual Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)20˒21 illustrating the hypothesized relationships among reproductive factors, neonatal maturity, respiratory morbidity, oxygen exposure, referral, and treatment-requiring ROP is provided in Supplementary Figure S1.

Exposure Variables

The prespecified primary exposures were IVF conception and duration of CPAP.

Infants were classified as conceived either by IVF or spontaneous conception.

CPAP exposure was categorized a priori according to the local neonatal intensive care protocol as >10 days or ≤10 days, with the latter category including infants who received no CPAP.

Although exact CPAP duration was abstracted whenever available, the protocol-defined categorical exposure was complete for all infants and therefore selected for the primary analyses.

CPAP duration was interpreted as a pragmatic indicator of respiratory-support burden rather than a direct measure of cumulative oxygen exposure.

Outcome Measure

The primary outcome was treatment-requiring ROP, defined as ROP requiring laser photocoagulation, intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor therapy, or vitreoretinal surgery because of Type 1 ROP, aggressive ROP, or Stage 4–5 disease according to contemporary clinical practice.12,22,23

Additional Clinical Variables

Whenever documented, gestational age, birth weight, sex, multiple gestation, neonatal sepsis, blood transfusion, mechanical ventilation, oxygen-related variables, parental consanguinity, and family history of inherited retinal disease were extracted from the medical records.

Evaluation of Atypical Term Infants

The three term infants with treatment-requiring ROP underwent additional investigation to exclude inherited vitreoretinal disorders and other important differential diagnoses.

Evaluation included comprehensive ophthalmic examination, multimodal retinal imaging, operative findings where applicable, detailed family history, assessment of parental consanguinity, and targeted next-generation sequencing.

The genetic panel included FZD4, LRP5, TSPAN12, NDP, ZNF408, KIF11, CTNNB1, CRB1, COL2A1, and COL11A1.

Negative findings were interpreted as absence of pathogenic variants within the tested genes rather than exclusion of all inherited retinal disorders.

Statistical Analysis

The primary estimand was the association between each prespecified exposure and treatment-requiring ROP among infants with established disease.

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), as appropriate according to data distribution. Categorical variables are summarized as frequencies and percentages.

Crude exposure–outcome associations were evaluated using two-by-two contingency tables.

For IVF, crude odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using conventional maximum-likelihood estimation.

For CPAP, complete outcome separation prevented estimation of the ordinary maximum-likelihood OR because no treatment-requiring ROP occurred in the reference category. Therefore, a Haldane–Anscombe continuity-corrected OR was reported solely as a descriptive estimate.

Absolute risk differences with Newcombe–Wilson 95% confidence intervals were calculated to facilitate clinical interpretation.

Two-sided Fisher’s exact tests were used because of sparse contingency tables.

Statistical interpretation emphasized effect estimates and confidence intervals rather than statistical significance alone.24

Rationale for Not Performing Adjusted Analysis

Multivariable regression analyses were considered during study planning but were not presented because the available dataset exhibited several structural limitations that precluded reliable estimation of independent exposure effects.

These included complete overlap between IVF conception and twin gestation, substantial overlap between IVF and prolonged CPAP, complete outcome separation within the CPAP comparison, a small reference CPAP group, non-independence among twin observations,25 and incomplete documentation of several clinically important neonatal variables.

Under these conditions, conventional logistic regression, penalized likelihood methods (including Firth logistic regression), propensity-score approaches, and mixed-effects models would remain susceptible to structural non-identifiability and unstable parameter estimation. Accordingly, the analysis deliberately prioritized transparent descriptive reporting rather than potentially misleading adjusted estimates.

Missing Data

Complete information was available for gestational age, birth weight, IVF status, protocol-defined CPAP category, and treatment-requiring ROP.

Several oxygen-related neonatal variables—including FiO2, cumulative oxygen exposure, exact oxygen supplementation, and oxygen saturation targets—were incompletely documented because of the retrospective design. Mechanical ventilation, blood transfusion, and neonatal sepsis were available for all infants included in the final analytical dataset.

No data imputation was performed. The extent of missing information for secondary variables is summarized in Supplementary Table S1.

Statistical Software

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.3.1.

Association Between Prolonged CPAP and Treatment-Requiring ROP

Treatment-requiring ROP occurred in 35 of 52 infants (67.3%) who required CPAP for more than 10 days, whereas no treatment-requiring cases occurred among the eight infants receiving no CPAP or CPAP for ≤10 days.

Because the reference group contained no events, the conventional maximum-likelihood OR could not be estimated. A Haldane–Anscombe continuity-corrected descriptive OR of 34.49 (95% CI, 1.88–632.45) was therefore reported together with an absolute risk difference of 67.3% points (95% CI, 32.2–78.5). Fisher’s exact test demonstrated a statistically significant association (P<0.001) (Table 2).

Summary of the Primary Findings

Treatment-requiring ROP occurred more frequently among infants conceived through IVF and among those requiring prolonged CPAP. However, these findings represent descriptive associations within a selected tertiary referral population and should be interpreted in the context of sparse data, exposure overlap, and the retrospective study design.

Discussion

Principal Findings

In this exploratory tertiary referral cohort of infants with established retinopathy of prematurity (ROP), treatment-requiring disease occurred more frequently among infants conceived through in vitro fertilisation (IVF) and among those requiring prolonged continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP). Although the observed differences were clinically substantial, the findings should be interpreted as descriptive associations within a selected referral population rather than evidence of independent causal effects. The available data were insufficient to disentangle the contributions of IVF, twin gestation, prematurity, respiratory morbidity, prolonged respiratory support, and other correlated neonatal factors.

Unlike population-based neonatal screening cohorts, the present study focused on disease severity among infants already diagnosed with ROP and referred to a tertiary vitreoretinal center. Consequently, the findings provide insight into clinical characteristics of referred infants rather than estimates of disease incidence or predictors applicable to the broader neonatal population.

Comparison with Previous Studies

Gestational age and birth weight remain the principal determinants of ROP and continue to form the basis of international screening recommendations. However, advances in neonatal intensive care have increased survival among extremely premature infants, prompting greater attention to postnatal exposures that may influence disease severity.

Several studies have reported associations between prolonged respiratory support and severe ROP. Nevertheless, respiratory support should not be considered synonymous with oxygen exposure. Prolonged CPAP frequently reflects pulmonary immaturity, respiratory illness severity, intermittent hypoxaemia, and prolonged oxygen supplementation, all of which may contribute to abnormal retinal vascular development. Consequently, CPAP duration should be interpreted primarily as a marker of overall respiratory burden rather than an independent biological determinant of disease.

Published evidence regarding IVF remains inconsistent. Some studies have suggested increased frequencies of severe ROP among IVF-conceived infants, whereas others have demonstrated that these associations are largely explained by multiple gestation, prematurity, and neonatal morbidity. Our findings are consistent with this uncertainty. Although treatment-requiring ROP occurred more frequently among IVF-conceived infants, complete overlap between IVF, twin gestation, and prolonged CPAP prevented reliable estimation of an independent effect attributable to IVF itself.

Interpretation of the CPAP Association

The absence of treatment-requiring ROP among infants receiving no CPAP or CPAP for ≤10 days resulted in complete outcome separation and a large descriptive odds ratio. Although this finding highlights an important clinical contrast within the study cohort, the estimated effect size should not be interpreted as a precise quantitative measure because it was derived from a small zero-event reference group.

The principal clinical message is therefore not the magnitude of the odds ratio but rather the observation that prolonged respiratory-support burden identified a subgroup of referred infants with substantially greater disease severity. Whether this association reflects prolonged oxygen exposure, respiratory illness severity, intermittent hypoxaemia, pulmonary immaturity, or other correlated neonatal factors cannot be determined from the present dataset.

Atypical Term Infants

The three term infants represent clinically important sentinel observations rather than evidence supporting modification of current ROP screening recommendations. Despite gestational ages and birth weights above conventional screening thresholds, each infant experienced substantial neonatal respiratory morbidity requiring prolonged CPAP before developing treatment-requiring ROP.

Comprehensive ophthalmic examination, multimodal retinal imaging, operative findings where applicable, detailed family history, and targeted next-generation sequencing did not support major inherited vitreoretinal disorders, including familial exudative vitreoretinopathy and Norrie-spectrum disease. Nevertheless, negative targeted genetic testing cannot exclude every inherited or developmental retinal disorder. These cases therefore emphasize the importance of comprehensive diagnostic evaluation in atypical presentations while supporting further prospective investigation of similar infants.

Clinical Implications

The findings of this study should not be interpreted as evidence supporting modification of current ROP screening recommendations. Rather, they identify clinically important associations observed within a tertiary referral population that warrant further investigation. Prolonged respiratory-support burden may represent a practical marker of disease severity among referred infants, including occasional infants who fall outside conventional gestational age and birth weight screening thresholds. These observations reinforce the importance of close collaboration between neonatologists and ophthalmologists in identifying infants who require timely retinal evaluation. However, any expansion of existing screening criteria should be based on adequately powered prospective multicenter studies incorporating comprehensive neonatal respiratory and oxygen-exposure data rather than retrospective referral cohorts.

Strengths

This study has several strengths. First, it evaluated a clinically meaningful outcome, namely treatment-requiring ROP, which is directly relevant to patient management and visual prognosis. Second, the study adopted a transparent exploratory clinical epidemiology framework, deliberately avoiding presentation of statistically unstable adjusted models as definitive estimates. Third, both relative and absolute measures of association were reported together with confidence intervals, allowing clinically meaningful interpretation beyond statistical significance alone. Fourth, atypical term infants underwent comprehensive phenotypic assessment using multimodal retinal imaging, detailed family history, and targeted next-generation sequencing to exclude recognized inherited vitreoretinal disorders. Finally, the study explicitly acknowledges referral selection, sparse data, exposure overlap, and residual confounding, enabling readers to interpret the findings within their appropriate methodological context.

Limitation

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. First, the retrospective single-center design and tertiary referral setting limit the generalizability of the results. Second, study inclusion depended on referral and parental consent, introducing the possibility of referral-related and consent-related selection bias. Third, sparse exposure categories, complete outcome separation within the CPAP comparison, overlap between IVF conception and prolonged respiratory support, and non-independence among twin observations precluded reliable estimation of independent exposure effects. Fourth, important neonatal variables, including cumulative oxygen exposure, FiO2, oxygen saturation profiles, intermittent hypoxaemia, mechanical ventilation, blood transfusion, and neonatal sepsis, were incompletely documented because of the retrospective design. Consequently, residual confounding remains unavoidable, and the observed associations should be interpreted as exploratory rather than causal.

Future Research

Future investigations should employ adequately powered multicenter prospective cohort studies with population-basabscenceed recruitment, standardized collection of neonatal respiratory variables, longitudinal oxygen-exposure metrics, pregnancy-level identifiers for multiple births, and prespecified statistical approaches for clustered observations. Such studies should determine whether respiratory-support variables provide incremental predictive value beyond established determinants, including gestational age and birth weight, while incorporating appropriate internal and external validation before consideration of any modification to existing ROP screening strategies.

Conclusion

In this exploratory tertiary referral cohort of infants with established retinopathy of prematurity, treatment-requiring disease occurred more frequently among infants conceived through in vitro fertilisation and among those requiring prolonged continuous positive airway pressure. However, these associations were strongly influenced by exposure overlap, sparse data, referral selection, and incomplete neonatal information, precluding reliable causal inference. Rather than supporting changes to current screening recommendations, this study identifies clinically relevant signals that warrant confirmation in adequately powered prospective multicenter cohorts incorporating comprehensive neonatal respiratory and oxygen-exposure data. Until such evidence becomes available, current ROP screening recommendations based on gestational age and birth weight should remain the cornerstone of clinical practice.

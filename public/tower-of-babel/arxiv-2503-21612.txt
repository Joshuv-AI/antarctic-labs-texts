A globalized inexact semismooth Newton method for strongly convex optimal control problems
Daniel Wachsmuth

Abstract
We investigate a globalized inexact semismooth Newton method applied to strongly convex optimization problems in Hilbert spaces. Here, the semismooth Newton method is appplied to the dual problem, which has a continuously differentiable objective. We prove global strong convergence of iterates as well as transition to local superlinear convergence. The latter needs a second-order Taylor expansion involving semismooth derivative concepts. The convergence of the globalized method is demonstrated in numerical examples, for which the local unglobalized method diverges.

1
Introduction

The goal of this article is to develop a globalized semismooth Newton method to solve the following
strongly convex optimization problem:

\min_{u\in U}\frac{1}{2}\|Su-z\|_{Y}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{U}^{2}+g(u)

(1.1)

where
U,Y
are Hilbert spaces,
S\in\mathcal{L}(U,Y)
,
z\in Y
,
\alpha>0
,
g:U\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is proper, convex, lower semicontinuous with
0\in\operatorname{dom}g
.

Problem (
1.1
) is a nice convex problem and its properties are well understood.
However, the local (unglobalized) semismooth Newton method converges globally only for relatively large values of
\alpha
.

Following
[
18
,
42
,
25
]
,
we propose to apply the semismooth Newton method to the dual problem of (
1.1
),
where the globalization uses the dual objective as merit function.
Due to the structure of (
1.1
), the dual problem has a continuously differentiable objective functional,
which enables a particularly nice convergence analysis.
As this analysis crucially relies on the strong convexity of the primal problem, it cannot be extended to non-convex or not strongly convex problems.
Hence, the proposed method is intended to be used as a subproblem solver in, e.g., proximal point methods
[
25
]

or iterated Bregman methods
[
28
]
.

Interestingly, in the implementation only knowledge about the Moreau-Yosida envelope and the proximal operator of
g
is needed.
Using the proximal operator, one can prove that
u\in U
solves (
1.1
)
if and only if
u\in U
solves the non-smooth equation

u=\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}\left(-\frac{1}{\alpha}S^{*}(Su-z)\right),

(1.2)

where
S^{*}
denotes the Hilbert space adjoint of
S
.
In order to solve (
1.1
) numerically, the most common approach is to apply the semismooth Newton method to the non-smooth equation (
1.2
).
In this paper, we propose to solve the equation

\xi-z+S\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}(\xi/\alpha))=0,

(1.3)

which is related to (
1.2
) via
\xi=z-Su
. The advantage of this approach is that the derivative of the operator appearing in (
1.3
)
is a self-adjoint map, which is not the case for (
1.2
).
In addition, (
1.3
) is equivalent to
\nabla\Phi(\xi)=0
, where
\Phi
is the dual function to (
1.1
), which will be used as merit function.

A third approach to solve (
1.1
) is based on the so-called normal map
[
33
]
, where one solves

p-S^{*}(S\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(-p/\alpha)-z)=0

(1.4)

for
p\in U
. Clearly,
p
is related to
\xi
by
p=-S^{*}\xi
.
Again the derivative of the operator appearing in (
1.4
) is not self-adjoint.

This artice is written 25 years after the publication of the seminal contributions
[
39
]
(submitted 2000) and
[
14
]
(submitted 2001)
on semismooth Newton methods applied to optimal control problems.
Still the literature on globalized semismooth Newton methods for infinite-dimensional problems is rather scarce.
In fact, to the best of our knowledge, only the following contributions develop a global convergent method applicable to (
1.1
).
In
[
18
]
the globalization based on (
1.3
) was investigated for the special case, where
g
is the indicator function associated with box constraints.
In the monograph
[
40
]
a trust-region globalization of the semi-smooth Newton method applied to the non-smooth equation (
1.2
)
is analyzed. Since continuous differentiability of
g
is required, it is not applicable to choices like
g(u)=\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
.
A similar method was analyzed for the special case of a parabolic control problem in
[
20
]
.
In
[
8
]
a globalized method is introduced, where the convergence analysis relies on strong assumptions on the sequence of chosen derivatives.
For
\ell^{1}
-regularized problems, a globalized semismooth Newton method can be found in
[
12
]
. One major drawback of the convergence analysis in
[
40
,
8
,
12
]
is that they assume the existence of strong accumulation points of the sequence of iterates.
While it is natural to expect that the sequence of iterates is bounded, this only guarantees the existence of
weakly
converging subsequences.
However,
strong
convergence of iterates has to be verified in order to prove transition to fast local convergence.

Let us comment on related work on globalized semismooth Newton methods.
The recent work
[
1
]
introduces a semismooth Newton method to solve contractive fixed point problems.
Due to the contraction assumption, no damping or globalization is needed.
In
[
29
,
30
]
globalized proximal Newton methods that solve minimization problems in Hilbert spaces are investigated.
The use of the normal map equation was proposed in
[
17
]
together with a damped Newton method without convergence analysis.
Global convergence of the unregularized semismooth Newton method applied to problems with box constraints was proven in

[
3
,
14
]
for discretized problems and
[
22
]
in the infinite-dimensional setting provided
\alpha
is large relative to
\|S\|
.
For a review of early results on (globalized) semismooth Newton methods we refer to
[
31
]
.
Recent contributions
on globalized semismooth Newton methods to minimize the sum of a differentiable and a non-smooth function in finite dimensions can be found in

[
26
,
9
]
.

The goal of this article is to give an almost self-contained analysis of the global convergence of the globalized semismooth Newton method.
A few facts about subdifferentials and proximal operators are collected in
Section

2.1
.
The dual problem to (
1.1
) is introduced in
Section

2.2
.
Due to the problem structure, no duals have to be computed for the implementation, only the proximal operator of
g
is needed.
For the transition to fast convergence, a second order Taylor expansion is essential, which is introduced in
Section

3
.
As in
[
42
,
25
]
we use a conjugate gradient method as solver for the Newton equations.
In order to prove that all iterates of the CG method are descent directions, one elementary result is needed, which is provided in
Section

4
.
The main section is
Section

5
, which contains the globalized semismooth Newton method
Algorithm

2
.
We prove
strong
convergence of the iterates in
Lemma

5.17
, and the transition to fast local convergence in
Theorem

5.23
.

Section

6
shows that commonly studied examples admit a second order Taylor expansion.
As we show in
Section

7
, the application of the semismooth Newton method to (
1.3
) enables the use of the variational discretization
[
16
]

similar to the arguments in
[
17
]
.
The paper concludes with numerical experiments in
Section

8
. The results indicate that the variational discretization leads to better performance of
the semismooth Newton method for small values of
\alpha
in some examples.

2
Preliminaries on the primal and dual problems

Lemma 2.1
.

The problem (
1.1
) is uniquely solvable.
An element
\bar{u}\in U
is a solution of (
1.1
) if and only if

0\in S^{*}(S\bar{u}-z)+\alpha\bar{u}+\partial g(\bar{u}).

(2.1)

Proof 2.2
.

Existence can be proven by classical arguments,
see, e.g.,
[
2
, Theorem 11.10]
.
Let
\bar{u}
be a solution of (
1.1
).
Since the first two addends of (
1.1
) are continuous, we can apply the subgradient sum-rule twice to obtain (
2.1
).
The sufficiency of (
2.1
) follows from convexity.

2.1
Conjugate functions and proximal operators

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space,
h:X\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
proper, convex, lower semicontinuous.
The convex conjugate
h^{*}
of
h
is defined by

h^{*}(\zeta):=\sup_{x\in X}\langle\zeta,x\rangle-h(x).

The Moreau-Yosida envelope of
h
is defined by

\operatorname{env}_{h}(\zeta):=\min_{x\in X}\frac{1}{2}\|x-\zeta\|_{X}^{2}+h(x),

and the proximal operator associated to
h
is defined by

\operatorname{prox}_{h}(\zeta):=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{x\in X}\frac{1}{2}\|x-\zeta\|_{X}^{2}+h(x).

For properties of Moreau-Yosida envelopes and proximal operators, we refer to
[
2
, Section 12.4]
.
Convex conjugates are developed in
[
2
, Chapter 13]
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
2
, Proposition 12.26]
)
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space,
h:X\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
proper, convex, lower semicontinuous.
Let
x,\zeta\in X
. Then
x=\operatorname{prox}_{h}(\zeta)
if and only if
0\in x-\zeta+\partial h(x)
.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
2
, Proposition 12.28]
)
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space,
h:X\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
proper, convex, lower semicontinuous.
Let
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in X
. Then
\|\operatorname{prox}_{h}(\zeta_{1})-\operatorname{prox}_{h}(\zeta_{2})\|_{X}\leq\|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}\|_{X}
.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
2
, Proposition 12.30]
)
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space,
h:X\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
proper, convex, lower semicontinuous.
Then
\operatorname{env}_{h}:X\to\mathbb{R}
is Fréchet differentiable with

\nabla\operatorname{env}_{h}(\zeta)=\zeta-\operatorname{prox}_{h}(\zeta).

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space,
h:X\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
proper, convex, lower semicontinuous.
Define

H^{*}:=\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|_{X}^{2}+h\right)^{*}.

Then
H^{*}:X\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

H^{*}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|_{X}^{2}-\operatorname{env}_{h}(\zeta)

and is Fréchet differentiable with

\nabla H^{*}(\zeta)=\operatorname{prox}_{h}(\zeta).

If
0\in\operatorname{dom}h
then
H^{*}
is bounded from below.

Proof 2.8
.

By definition, we have

\begin{split}H^{*}(\zeta)&=\sup_{x\in X}\langle\zeta,x\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{X}^{2}-h(x)\\
&=-\inf_{x\in X}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|x-\zeta\|_{X}^{2}+h(x)-\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|_{X}^{2}\right)\\
&=-\operatorname{env}_{h}(\zeta)+\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta\|_{X}^{2}.\end{split}

Due to
Proposition

2.6
, it follows that
H^{*}
is Fréchet differentiable, and the expression of
\nabla H^{*}
follows.
Boundedness from below is a direct consequence of the definition of
H^{*}
.

2.2
Optimality conditions for the dual problem

We define the functions
F:Y\to\mathbb{R}
and
G:U\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
by

F(y)=\frac{1}{2}\|y-z\|_{Y}^{2},\quad G(u):=\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{U}^{2}+g(u).

Let us introduce the Fenchel dual problem to (
1.1
), see, e.g.,
[
2
, Section 15.3]
.
The dual problem of (
1.1
) is given by

\min_{\xi\in Y}F^{*}(-\xi)+G^{*}(S^{*}\xi)=:\Phi(\xi)

(2.2)

where

F^{*}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta+z\|_{Y}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z\|_{Y}^{2}

and

G^{*}(u)=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|u\|_{U}^{2}-\alpha\operatorname{env}_{g/\alpha}(u/\alpha).

Due to the structure of the problem both functions
F^{*}
and
G^{*}
can be evaluated without the need to compute conjugates, only the Moreau-Yosida envelope of
g/\alpha
is needed.

Lemma 2.9
.

The dual problem (
2.2
) is
uniquely
solvable.
Let
\bar{\xi}\in Y
. Then
\bar{\xi}
solves (
2.2
) if and only if

\nabla\Phi(\bar{\xi})=\bar{\xi}-z+S\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\bar{\xi}/\alpha)=0.

Proof 2.10
.

The function
\Phi
is continuous and convex. Since
F^{*}
is coercive, existence of solutions can be proven easily.
Uniqueness of minimizers is a consequence of the strict convexity of
F^{*}
.
The dual problem (
2.2
) is the minimization problem of the convex and Fréchet differentiable function
\Phi
.
Then
\nabla\Phi(\bar{\xi})=0
is necessary and sufficient for optimality of
\bar{\xi}
.
The expression of the derivative is a consequence of
Proposition

2.6
.

Lemma 2.11
.

If
\bar{u}\in U
solves the primal problem (
1.1
) then
\bar{\xi}:=-(S\bar{u}-z)
solves (
2.2
).
Conversely, if
\bar{\xi}\in Y
solves (
2.2
) then
\bar{u}:=\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\bar{\xi}/\alpha)
solves (
1.1
).

Proof 2.12
.

Due to
Proposition

2.4
, the optimality condition (
2.1
) is equivalent to

\bar{u}=\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}\left(-\frac{1}{\alpha}S^{*}(S\bar{u}-z)\right).

Then the claim follows by elementary computations.

3
Second-order semismoothness

For the proof of transition from global convergence to fast local convergence, a second-order Taylor expansion of the function
G^{*}
is indispensible
[
7
]
.
Following
[
29
]
, we introduce the notion of second-order semismoothness.

Let
X
be a Banach space.
We will denote the space of bilinear forms from
X\times X
to
\mathbb{R}
by
\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
T:X\to\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable with derivative
T^{\prime}
.
Let
\partial^{2}T
be a set-valued map
\partial^{2}T:X\rightrightarrows\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(X,\mathbb{R})
with non-empty images.
Then
T
is called second-order semismooth
with respect to
\partial^{2}T
if for all
x\in X

\sup_{M\in\partial^{2}T(x+h)}\left|T(x+h)-T(x)-T^{\prime}(x)h-\frac{1}{2}M(h,h)\right|=o(\|h\|_{X}^{2})

(3.1)

and

\sup_{M\in\partial^{2}T(x+h)}\left\|T^{\prime}(x+h)-T^{\prime}(x)-M(h,\cdot)\right\|_{X^{*}}=o(\|h\|_{X})

(3.2)

for
\|h\|_{X}\to 0
,
where
M(h,\cdot)
denotes the element of
X^{*}
given by
v\mapsto M(h,v)
.

Here, second-order semismoothness refers to the availability of a second-order Taylor expansion (
3.1
),
which is different to the use in
[
40
]
, where higher-order semismoothness refers to stronger estimates of the first-order expansion (
3.2
)
replacing
o(\|h\|_{X})
for
O(\|h\|_{X}^{1+\alpha})
with
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

If
X
is a Hilbert space, we can use operators
M\in\mathcal{L}(X,X)
instead of bilinear forms, and replace
M(h,h)
and
M(h,\cdot)
by
\langle Mh,h\rangle
and
Mh
.
In
[
29
]
an example is presented to show that (
3.1
) does not imply (
3.2
).
Let us prove a sufficient condition for (
3.1
) which is only based on assumptions on
T^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
T:X\to\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable with
Bouligand differentiable
T^{\prime}
, i.e.,
T^{\prime}
is directionally differentiable and
for all
x\in X
it holds

\|T^{\prime}(x+h)-T^{\prime}(x)-T^{\prime\prime}(x;h)\|_{X^{*}}=o(\|h\|_{X}).

Let

\partial^{2}T:X\rightrightarrows\mathcal{L}^{(2)}(X,\mathbb{R})
be given such that (
3.2
) is satisfied for all
x\in X
.
Then (
3.1
) is satisfied for all
x\in X
, and hence,
T
is second-order semismooth
with respect to
\partial^{2}T
.

Proof 3.3
.

For
M\in\partial^{2}T(x+h)
, we have the expansion

T(x+h)-T(x)-T^{\prime}(x)h-\frac{1}{2}M(h,h)=\\
\left(T(x+h)-T(x)-\frac{1}{2}[T^{\prime}(x)+T^{\prime}(x+h)]h\right)\\
+\frac{1}{2}\Big(T^{\prime}(x+h)h-T^{\prime}(x)h-M(h,h)\Big).

Due to (
3.2
), the second addend is of order
o(\|h\|_{X}^{2})
for
h\to 0
.
Hence, it suffices to prove

\left|T(x+h)-T(x)-\frac{1}{2}[T^{\prime}(x)+T^{\prime}(x+h)]h\right|=o(\|h\|_{X}^{2}).

Take
x\in X
.
Let
\epsilon>0
.
Then there is
\rho>0
such that
\|T^{\prime}(x+h)-T^{\prime}(x)-T^{\prime\prime}(x;h)\|_{X^{*}}\leq\epsilon\|h\|_{X}
for all
h
with
\|h\|_{X}<\rho
.
Take
h
with
\|h\|_{X}<\rho
.
Denote

R_{t}:=T^{\prime}(x+th)-T^{\prime}(x)-tT^{\prime\prime}(x;h),

which implies
\|R_{t}\|_{X^{*}}\leq\epsilon t\|h\|_{X}
for all
t\in[0,1]
.
Then we can write

\displaystyle T(x+h)-T(x)-\frac{1}{2}[T^{\prime}(x)+T^{\prime}(x+h)]h

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}[T^{\prime}(x+sh)-T^{\prime}(x)]h\,\text{{}{}d}s-\frac{1}{2}[T^{\prime}(x+h)-T^{\prime}(x)]h

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}[sT^{\prime\prime}(x;h)+R_{s}]h\,\text{{}{}d}s-\frac{1}{2}T^{\prime\prime}(x;h)-\frac{1}{2}R_{1}h

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}R_{s}h\,\text{{}{}d}s-\frac{1}{2}R_{1}h

so that

\left|T(x+h)-T(x)-\frac{1}{2}[T^{\prime}(x)+T^{\prime}(x+h)]h\right|\leq\epsilon\|h\|_{X}^{2}

which finishes the proof.

In the smooth case, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.4

(
[
29
, Proposition 5]
)
.

Let
T:X\to\mathbb{R}
be twice continuously differentiable. Then
T
is second-order semismooth with respect to
\partial^{2}T(x):=\{T^{\prime\prime}(x)\}
.

4
Conjugate Gradients

Let us recall the celebrated conjugate gradient method to solve linear systems with positive definite operators.
Let
X
be a Hilbert space,
A\in\mathcal{L}(X,X)
self-adjoint and positive definite, and
b\in X
.
The CG algorithm to solve
Ax=b
in the notation from
[
38
, Section 38]
can be found in
Algorithm

1
.

Input: tolerance
\delta_{\mathrm{CG}}>0
.

Set
x_{0}=0
,
r_{0}=b
,
p_{0}=r_{0}
,
k=0
.

while

\|r_{k}\|>\delta_{\mathrm{CG}}

do

\alpha_{k}=\frac{\|r_{k}\|_{X}^{2}}{\langle Ap_{k},p_{k}\rangle}

x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}p_{k}

r_{k+1}=r_{k}-\alpha_{k}Ap_{k}

\beta_{k}=\frac{\|r_{k+1}\|_{X}^{2}}{\|r_{k}\|_{X}^{2}}

p_{k+1}=r_{k+1}+\beta_{k}p_{k}

Set
k:=k+1

end

while

Algorithm 1

CG algorithm to solve
Ax=b
with tolerance
\delta_{\mathrm{CG}}

The classical orthogonality relations and convergence guarantees transfer to the Hilbert space case, see, e.g.,
[
6
]
.
If
A=I+K
with
K
compact we have r-superlinear convergence of
x_{k}\to A^{-1}b

[
41
]
.
These results imply that for positive tolerance
\delta_{\mathrm{CG}}
, algorithm
Algorithm

1
terminates after finitely many steps.

In the following, we need an estimate of
\langle x_{k},b\rangle
.
A similar result can be found in
[
42
, Lemma 3.1]
. We give a simplified proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{L}(X,X)
be self-adjoint and positive definite, and
b\in X
.
Let
(x_{k})
be iterates of
Algorithm

1
. Then

\langle x_{k},b\rangle\geq\frac{1}{\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,X)}}\|b\|_{X}^{2}.

Proof 4.2
.

For
k=1
, we have
x_{0}=0
and
p_{0}=b
so that

\langle x_{1},b\rangle=\alpha_{0}\|b\|_{X}^{2}=\frac{\|b\|_{X}^{4}}{\langle Ab,b\rangle}\geq\frac{1}{\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,X)}}\|b\|_{X}^{2}.

Due to the orthogonality relations of the residuals,
[
38
, (38.4)]
, we have
\langle r_{k},b\rangle=\langle r_{k},r_{0}\rangle=0
for all
k\geq 1
.
Then we get for the conjugate directions

\langle p_{k+1},b\rangle=\langle r_{k+1},b\rangle+\beta_{k}\langle p_{k},b\rangle=\beta_{k}\langle p_{k},b\rangle,

so that
\langle p_{k+1},b\rangle=\prod_{i=0}^{k}\beta_{i}\langle p_{0},b\rangle
.
Since
p_{0}=r_{0}=b
and
\beta_{i}>0
, this implies
\langle p_{k},b\rangle\geq 0
for all
k\geq 0
.
Using this in the update formula for
x_{k+1}
, we find

\langle x_{k+1},b\rangle=\langle x_{k},b\rangle+\alpha_{k}\langle p_{k},b\rangle\geq\langle x_{k},b\rangle,

so that the sequence
(\langle x_{k},b\rangle)
is monotonically increasing.

5
Semismooth Newton

Here, we want to investigate a semismooth Newton method to minimize the function
\Phi
in (
2.2
), which can be written as

\Phi(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\|\xi-z\|_{Y}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z\|_{Y}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|S^{*}\xi\|_{U}^{2}-\alpha\operatorname{env}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\xi/\alpha).

(5.1)

Its gradient is given by
Lemma

2.9
as

\nabla\Phi(\xi)=\xi-z+S\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\xi/\alpha).

(5.2)

Corollary 5.1
.

The map
\Phi
is continuously differentiable. In addition, there is
L>0
such that
\|\nabla\Phi(y_{1})-\nabla\Phi(y_{2})\|_{Y}\leq L\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{Y}
for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y
.
Consequently,

\left|\Phi(y+h)-\Phi(y)-\Phi^{\prime}(y)h\right|\leq\frac{L}{2}\|h\|_{Y}^{2}.

Proof 5.2
.

Differentiability of
\Phi
is a consequence of the differentiability of
\operatorname{env}_{g/\alpha}
by
Proposition

2.6
.
By
Proposition

2.5
, the proximal operator
\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}
is Lipschitz continuous.
Hence,
\nabla\Phi
is Lipschitz continuous.
The claimed estimate follows is then a consequence of this Lipschitz property.

Let us define for convenience

\Psi(\xi):=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|S^{*}\xi\|_{U}^{2}-\alpha\operatorname{env}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\xi/\alpha),

(5.3)

so that
\nabla\Psi(\xi)=S\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\xi/\alpha)
.
Throughout this section, we assume that the following assumption is satisfied.

Assumption 1
.

There is a set-valued map
\partial^{2}\Psi:Y\rightrightarrows\mathcal{B}
,
where

\mathcal{B}:=\{M\in\mathcal{L}(Y,Y):\ M=M^{*},\ \langle My,y\rangle\geq 0\ \forall y\in Y\},

such that
\Psi
given by (
5.3
) is second-order semismooth with respect to
\partial^{2}\Psi
, see
Definition

3.1
.
In addition,
\partial^{2}\Psi
is bounded, i.e., there is
c>0
such that for all
y\in Y
and

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
(\xi_{k})
be a sequence of iterates of

Now we are in the position to prove

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
(\xi_{k})
be a sequence of iterates of

By

Consider the case

Then from

From the inexactness condition (

Dividing by

.
Here, we used

Due to the structure of the problem, we have strong convergence of iterates.

Lemma 5.17
.

Let
(\xi_{k})
be a sequence of iterates of

Then

Proof 5.18
.

Let
\bar{\xi}
be such that

. The existence of such

follows from

.

Lemma

The next step is to prove that the step size

is accepted for all

. Here, we follow the exposition in
[

]
.
First, we establish superlinear convergence of the local inexact semismooth Newton method.

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
\bar{\xi}
be the unique solution of
\Phi(\bar{\xi})=0
.
Let
(\xi_{k})
be a sequence of iterates of
Algorithm

2
with step sizes
(t_{k})
.
Let

\|r_{k}\|_{Y}=o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}),\qquad\|\xi_{k}+d_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}=o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y})

Proof 5.20
.

Using the inexact Newton equation (

, we get

\|r_{k}\|_{Y}\leq\eta\|\nabla\Phi(\xi_{k})\|_{Y}^{1+\tau}\leq\eta L\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{1+\tau}=o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}).

Using again (

\begin{split}\|\xi_{k}+d_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}&=\|M_{k}^{-1}(M_{k}(\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi})-\nabla\Phi(\xi_{k})+r_{k})\|_{Y}\\

Lemma 5.21
.

Let
\bar{\xi}
be the unique solution of
\Phi(\bar{\xi})=0
.
Let
(\xi_{k})
be a sequence of iterates of

with step sizes
(t_{k})
.

such that

for all

Proof 5.22
.

See also
[

, Theorem 3.3]

Using the simple inequalities

\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}\leq\|d_{k}\|_{Y}+\|\xi_{k}+d_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}\leq\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}+2\|\xi_{k}+d_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y},

we get by

\|d_{k}\|_{Y}=\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}+o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}).

Similarly, we get from (

\begin{split}\langle d_{k},\nabla\Phi(\xi_{k})\rangle&\leq-\langle M_{k}d_{k},d_{k}\rangle+\|r_{k}\|_{Y}\|d_{k}\|_{Y}\\
&\leq-\|d_{k}\|_{Y}^{2}+o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2})\\
&=-\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2}+o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2}).\end{split}

Since
\xi_{k}+d_{k}\to\bar{\xi}
in

, we have the second-order expansion

\displaystyle\Phi(\xi_{k}+d_{k})-\Phi(\bar{\xi})-\langle\nabla\Phi(\bar{\xi}),\xi_{k}+d_{k}-\bar{\xi}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle H_{k}(\xi_{k}+d_{k}-\bar{\xi}),\xi_{k}+d_{k}-\bar{\xi}\rangle+o(\|\xi_{k}+d_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2})

\displaystyle=o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2}),

where
H_{k}\in\partial^{2}\Phi(\bar{\xi}_{k}+d_{k})
, which is bounded due to
Corollary

5.3
.
Similarly, we prove using
\nabla\Phi(\bar{\xi})=0

\Phi(\xi_{k})-\Phi(\bar{\xi})=-\frac{1}{2}\langle M_{k}(\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}),\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\rangle+o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2}).

Collecting all these estimates yields

\Phi(\xi_{k}+d_{k})-\Phi(\xi_{k})-\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla\Phi(\xi_{k}),d_{k}\rangle\\
\begin{aligned} &=\frac{1}{2}\langle M_{k}(\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}),\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla\Phi(\xi_{k}),d_{k}\rangle+o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\langle M_{k}(\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}),\xi_{k}+d_{k}-\bar{\xi}-d_{k}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla\Phi(\xi_{k}),d_{k}\rangle+o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2})\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\langle M_{k}(\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi})+\nabla\Phi(\xi_{k})-\nabla\Phi(\bar{\xi}),d_{k}\rangle+o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2})\\
&=o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2}).\end{aligned}

Using this in the descent condition (
5.6
), we get

\displaystyle\Phi(\xi_{k}+d_{k})-\Phi(\xi_{k})-\sigma\langle d_{k},\nabla\Phi(\xi_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)\langle d_{k},\nabla\Phi(\xi_{k})\rangle+o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2})

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2}+o(\|\xi_{k}-\bar{\xi}\|_{Y}^{2}).

The right-hand side is less than zero for

large enough, which proves the claim.

Remark 5.25
.

The proof of transition to fast convergence in
[
18
, Lemma 4.4]
relied on stronger assumptions.
There a condition on the measure of almost active sets was used, which implies strict complementarity.
In addition, the operator

has to be continuous with values in

Under these conditions

Remark 5.26

(Newton equation in primal form)
.

The element
\bar{\xi}\in Y
solves

0=\nabla\Phi(\bar{\xi})=\bar{\xi}-z+S\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\bar{\xi}/\alpha)

if and only if
\bar{u}=\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\bar{\xi}/\alpha)
solves

0=\bar{u}-\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(-S^{*}(S\bar{u}-z)/\alpha).

(5.8)

Conversely, if
\bar{u}
is a solution of (
5.8
) then
\bar{\xi}=z-S\bar{u}
satisfies
\nabla\Phi(\bar{\xi})=0
.
Let
u_{k}\in U
be given. Applying the semismooth Newton method to (
5.8
) amounts to choosing
H_{k}\in\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(-S^{*}(Su_{k}-z)/\alpha)

and solving

\delta u+\frac{1}{\alpha}H_{k}S^{*}S\delta u=-\left(u_{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(-S^{*}(Su_{k}-z)/\alpha)\right).

(5.9)

Let us set
\xi_{k}:=z-Su_{k}
. If
\delta u
solves (
5.9
) then
\delta\xi:=-S\delta u
solves

\delta u-\frac{1}{\alpha}H_{k}S^{*}\delta\xi=-\left(u_{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\xi_{k}/\alpha)\right).

Multiplying by
-S
, we get

\delta\xi+\frac{1}{\alpha}SH_{k}S^{*}\delta\xi=-\left(\xi_{k}-z+S\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\xi_{k}/\alpha)\right),

which is the Newton equation (
5.4
) with
M_{k}:=S^{*}H_{k}S
.
Equation (
5.9
) is an equation with an unsymmetric operator. If
H_{k}
is suitably structured, one
can transform the equation into an equation with a symmetric operator.

Remark 5.27

(Semismooth Newton applied to normal map)

It is easy to show that
\bar{u}=\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(-\bar{p}/\alpha)

The map

is called normal map after

Semismooth Newton methods based on this equation are considered in

In the recent article
[

]

developed in finite dimensions, where globalization is based on the residual in the normal equation.

Remark 5.29

(Non-quadratic functional)
.

Let us briefly consider the following generalization of (

\min_{u\in X}F(Su)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{U}^{2}+g(u),

where
F:Y\to\mathbb{R}
is convex. Let
F^{*}
denote the convex conjugate of

.
Under the assumption that

is strongly convex and twice continuously differentiable, we get that
F^{*}
is also strongly convex and twice continuously differentiable.
Let us denote with
\nabla F^{*}:=\nabla(F^{*})
its gradient.
Then, we have
\nabla F(\nabla F^{*}(y))=y
for all

by
[

.
Differentiating this identity yields

\nabla^{2}F(\nabla F^{*}(y))\nabla^{2}F^{*}(y))=\operatorname{id}

\nabla^{2}F^{*}(y)=\nabla^{2}F(\nabla F^{*}(y))^{-1}

The resulting second-order derivative of

Then all the convergence analysis for the corresponding semismooth Newton algorithm remains true, only the constants in

and

have to be adapted.

6
Semismoothness of Nemyzki operators

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a measure space.
In many optimization problems, the function
g
in (
1.1
) is defined as

g(u)=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{g}(u(x))\,\text{{}{}d}\mu

(6.1)

with a convex function
\tilde{g}:\mathbb{R}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
.
Here, we follow the convention to set
g(u):=+\infty
if
x\mapsto\tilde{g}(u(x))
is not integrable.
The resulting proximal operator is then a superposition operator on
U=L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
\tilde{g}:\mathbb{R}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous. Let
g
be defined in (
6.1
).
Suppose there is
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with
g(u)<+\infty
.
Then
\operatorname{prox}_{g}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is given by

\operatorname{prox}_{g}(v)(x)=\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}}(v(x))\quad\text{ for $\mu$-a.a. }x\in\Omega.

Proof 6.2
.

This follows from the results of
[
34
, Section 3]
,
in particular the characterization of
\partial g(u)
as

\partial g(u)=\{d\in L^{2}(\Omega):\ d(x)\in\partial\tilde{g}(u(x))\text{ for $\mu$-almost all }x\in\Omega\},

see
[
34
, Corollary 3E]
.

6.1
Piecewise smooth proximals

We will now work with piecewise smooth proximal operators.

Assumption 2
.

The function
\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is piecewise smooth in the following sense: there are real numbers
v_{1}<v_{2}<\dots<v_{N}
and continuously
differentiable functions
p_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
k=0\dots N
,
such that

\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}=p_{k}\text{ on }[v_{k},v_{k+1}],

where we set
v_{0}=-\infty
,
v_{N+1}:=+\infty
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}
satisfy
Assumption

2
. Define
the single-valued map

\partial\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}(v):=p_{k}^{\prime}(v)\text{ if }v\in[v_{k},v_{k+1})\text{ for some }k=0\dots N.

Then for all
v\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\left|\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}(v+h)-\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}(v)-\partial\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}(v+h)h\right|=o(|h|)

and

|\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}(v+h)-\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}(v)-\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}^{\prime}(v;h)|=o(|h|).

Proof 6.4
.

Take
v\in\mathbb{R}
, and
h
such that
\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}
is continuously differentiable between
v
and
v+h
.
Then both claims follow easily.

Clearly, this result can be generalized for
\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with infinitely many pieces,
see also
[
40
, Section 2.5.3]
.

In the next step, we prove that the derivative of the proximal operator of the integral functional
g

is semismooth from
L^{q}(\Omega)
to
L^{p}(\Omega)
for
p<q
.
The proof of the following result is adapted from the proof of
[
29
, Proposition 6]
,
more general results can be found in
[
40
, Section 3.3]
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
be given. Let
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
.
Let
\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}
satisfy
Assumption

2
.
Let
v\in L^{q}(\Omega)
.
Define the measurable function
\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v)
for
\mu
-almost all
x\in\Omega
by

\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v)(x):=\partial\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}(v(x)).

Then we have
\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\left\|\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v+h)-\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v)-\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v+h)\cdot h\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=o(\|h\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)})

and

\|\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v+h)-\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v)-\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}^{\prime}(v;h)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=o(\|h\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}).

Proof 6.6
.

Take
v\in L^{q}(\Omega)
.
Let us define for
x\in\Omega
and
h\in\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}

r(x,h):=\frac{1}{h}\left(\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}(v(x)+h)-\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}(v(x))-\partial\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}(v(x)+h)h\right).

In addition, we set
r(x,0)=0
for
x\in\Omega
.
Then
x\mapsto r(x,h(x))
is
\mu
-measurable for all measurable
h:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. In addition,
\lim_{h\to 0}r(x,h)=0
for almost all
x
by
Lemma

6.3
.
Since
\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}
is Lipschitz (
Proposition

2.5
) and consequently
|\partial\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}|\leq 1
,
we have for almost all
x
and all
h
the estimate
|r(x,h)|\leq 2
.

Let
(h_{n})
be a sequence in
L^{q}(\Omega)
such that
h_{n}\to 0
in
L^{q}(\Omega)
. Then there
is a pointwise
\mu
-a.e. converging subsequence
(h_{n_{k}})
. It follows
r(x,h_{n_{k}}(x))\to 0
for almost all
x
. Since
r
is uniformly bounded and
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
, we get
\|r(\cdot,h_{n_{k}})\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\to 0

for all
s\in[1,\infty)
by dominated convergence.
It follows

\left\|\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v+h_{n_{k}})-\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v)-\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v+h_{n_{k}})\cdot h_{n_{k}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
=\|r(\cdot,h_{n_{k}})h_{n_{k}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|r(\cdot,h_{n_{k}})\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\|h_{n_{k}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

from the generalized Hölder inequality
with
s<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{q}
.
Consequently,

\frac{\left\|\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v+h_{n_{k}})-\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v)-\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v+h_{n_{k}})\cdot h_{n_{k}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\|h_{n_{k}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}}\to 0.

Since the limit is independent of the chosen subsequence, the convergence along the whole sequence
(h_{k})
follows.

The second claim can be proven with similar arguments, observing that
|\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}^{\prime}(v;h)|\leq|h|
.

Note that we cannot get rid of the norm gap in the previous result.
The necessity of this gap has been discussed in
[
40
, Example 3.57]
,
[
19
, Example 8.14]
for semismoothness
and in
[
11
, Remark 4.4]
,
[
24
]

for Bouligand differentiability.

Example 6.7
.

Without the assumption
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
, the result of
Lemma

6.5
is not true.
Let

\tilde{g}(v)=\begin{cases}+\infty&\text{ if }v<0,\\
0&\text{ if }v\geq 0.\\
\end{cases}

Then
\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}}(v)=\max(v,0)
.
Let
\Omega=(0,+\infty)
, and let
\mu
be the Lebesgue measure. Take
v(x):=-\exp(-x)
, so that
v\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
.
Define
h_{n}:=\chi_{[n,+\infty)}(-2v)
. Then
\|h_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\exp(-n)\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, so that it follows

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|h_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\to 0
for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
.
In addition,

\operatorname{prox}_{g}(v+h_{n})-\operatorname{prox}_{g}(v+h_{n})-\operatorname{prox}_{g}^{\prime}(v;h+h_{n})=\chi_{[n,+\infty)}(v+h_{n})-0-0=\frac{1}{2}h_{n}.

It is easy to check that
\|h_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}/\|h_{n}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}
does not converge to zero for any choice of
p,q
.

Due to the norm gap appearing in
Lemma

6.5
, we cannot expect that
\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}
is semismooth as a mapping on
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
In order that
Assumption

1
is satisfied, we will need to rely on smoothing properties of
S
or
S^{*}
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Assume
\mu(\Omega)<\infty
. Set
U:=L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Let
\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}/\alpha}
satisfy
Assumption

2
.
Let
Y
be a Hilbert space,
S\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\Omega),Y)
be given such that the Hilbert space adjoint
S^{*}
belongs to
\mathcal{L}(Y,L^{q}(\Omega))
for some
q>2
.

Then the map
\Psi:Y\to Y
defined by (
5.3
)
is second-order semismooth with respect to
\partial^{2}\Psi:Y\rightrightarrows\mathcal{L}(Y,Y)
defined by

\partial^{2}\Psi(\xi):=\left\{\frac{1}{\alpha}S\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\xi/\alpha)S^{*}\right\},

(6.2)

where
\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}
is defined in
Lemma

6.5
.
Moreover,
Assumption

1
is satisfied.

In (
6.2
), the function
\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}
is meant to act as multiplication operator on
S^{*}
, i.e.,

\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}S\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\xi/\alpha)S^{*}\right)(v)=\frac{1}{\alpha}S\left(\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}\cdot S^{*}v\right),

where
\cdot
denotes the pointwise multiplication of
\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
S^{*}v\in L^{q}(\Omega)
.

Proof 6.9

(Proof of
Lemma

6.8
)
.

We show that
\partial^{2}\Psi(\xi)
from (
6.2
) satisfies the assumptions of
Lemma

3.2
.
Let us recall
\nabla\Psi(\xi)=S\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}\xi/\alpha)
. Let
v,h\in Y
be given. Then
Lemma

6.5
with
p=2

and the assumption on
S^{*}
imply

\left\|\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}(v+h))-\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}v)-\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S^{*}(v+h))\cdot S^{*}h\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\

Clearly this implies that

satisfies (

).
The Bouligand property of the directional derivative can be proven with analogous arguments.

6.2
Examples

Box constraints.

Let
R>0
be given.
Let us define
\tilde{g}
by

\tilde{g}_{\textrm{box}}(u)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }|u|\leq R,\\
+\infty&\text{ if }|u|>R.\end{cases}

Then
\int_{\Omega}\tilde{g}_{\textrm{box}}(u)\,\text{{}{}d}x<+\infty
if and only if
|u(x)|\leq R

\mu
-almost everywhere, that is if
u
satisfies the box constraints.
In this case, the proximal operator is equal to the projection onto the associated feasible set,

\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}_{\textrm{box}}}(v)=\max(-R,\min(+R,v)).

Clearly, this function is piecewise affine linear, and
Assumption

2
is satisfied.
For this particular choice of
\tilde{g}
, we have
\tilde{g}_{\textrm{box}}/\alpha=\tilde{g}_{\textrm{box}}
.

L^{1}
-norm.

Let us now consider the case

\tilde{g}_{L^{1}}(u)=\beta|u|.

Then
\int_{\Omega}\tilde{g}_{L^{1}}(u)\,\text{{}{}d}\mu=\beta\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
. The corresponding proximal operator is the so-called soft-thresholding operator given by

\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}_{L^{1}}}(v)=\max(0,v-\beta)+\min(0,v+\beta)=\begin{cases}v+\beta&\text{ if }v<-\beta,\\
0&\text{ if }|v|\leq\beta,\\
v-\beta&\text{ if }v>\beta.\end{cases}

This is again a piecewise affine linear map, so that
Assumption

2
is satisfied.
The proximal operator of
\tilde{g}_{L^{1}}/\alpha
is obtained by replacing
\beta
with
\beta/\alpha
in the formula for
\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}_{L^{1}}}
.

Box constraints plus
L^{1}
-norm.

If
\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}_{L^{1}}(u)+\tilde{g}_{\textrm{box}}(u)
, then it is not hard to check that

\operatorname{prox}_{\tilde{g}}(v)=\max(0,\min(v-\beta,R))+\min(0,\max(v+\beta,-R))=\begin{cases}-R&\text{ if }v<-\beta-R\\
v+\beta&\text{ if }-\beta-R\leq v<-\beta,\\
0&\text{ if }|v|\leq\beta,\\
v-\beta&\text{ if }\beta\leq v<\beta+R,\\
R&\text{ if }v>\beta+R,\end{cases}

and hence,
Assumption

2
is satisfied.
Again,
the proximal operator of
(\tilde{g}_{L^{1}}+\tilde{g}_{\textrm{box}})/\alpha
is obtained by replacing
\beta
with
\beta/\alpha
in the formula above.

L^{2}
-ball constraint.

Let us now consider
g
to be the indicator function of a ball in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with radius
\gamma>0
:

g(u)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\gamma\\
+\infty&\text{ if }\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}>\gamma.\end{cases}

Again, the corresponding proximal map is the projection on the ball with radius
\gamma
,
\operatorname{prox}_{g}(v)=v\max(1,\frac{\gamma}{\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}})
.
Let us choose
\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g}(v)\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\Omega),L^{2}(\Omega))
by

\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g}(v)h:=\begin{cases}h&\text{ if }\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\gamma\\
\frac{\gamma h}{\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}-\frac{\gamma v\langle v,h\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}}&\text{ if }\|q\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}>\gamma.\end{cases}

Then
T^{\prime}=\operatorname{prox}_{g}
and
T^{\prime\prime}=\partial\operatorname{prox}_{g}
satisfy condition (
3.2
) by
[
23
, Proposition 4.1]
.
In addition,
\operatorname{prox}_{g}
is Bouligand differentiable due to
[
23
, Proposition 3.4]
.

Directional sparsity.

Here,
U=L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(I))
where
I=(0,T)
.

g(u)=\beta\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{I}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,\text{{}{}d}t\right)^{1/2}\,\text{{}{}d}x

The proximal operator associated to
g
is given by

\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(u)(x,t)=\max\left(0,\|u(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(I)}-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\frac{u(x,t)}{\|u(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(I)}}.

Here, we used the notation

\|u(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(I)}:=\left(\int_{I}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,\text{{}{}d}t\right)^{1/2}.

This operator is semismooth from
L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(I))
to
L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(I))
for all
p>2
,

[
27
, Lemma 3.22]
,
[
13
, Lemma 3.2]
.
In addition, it is easy to check that
\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}
is Bouligand differentiable using the chain rule for the Bouligand derivative
[
32
, Corollary A.4]
.

Further examples.

Additional examples that satisfy these assumptions are multi-bang problems
[
4
]
,
where
g
is given as the convex hull of

u\mapsto\begin{cases}\beta u^{2}&\text{ if }u\in\mathbb{Z},\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

In addition, also vector-valued controls can be considered
[
5
]
.
Since
u=\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(v)
if and only if
0\in\alpha(u-v)+\partial g(u)
,
implicit function theorems
[
21
]
can be used to prove the semismoothness of proximal maps.

7
Variational discretization

For computations, one has to choose finite-dimensional approximations of the infinite-dimensional problem (
1.1
).
The application of the semismooth Newton method to discretized problems is straight-forward.
In this section, we will focus on the variational discretization of
[
16
]
.
We will consider the following optimal control problem: Minimize

\frac{1}{2}\|y-z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\tilde{g}(u(x))\,\text{{}{}d}x

over all
(y,u)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)

subject to

\int_{\Omega}\nabla y\cdot\nabla v\,\text{{}{}d}x=\int_{\Omega}uv\,\text{{}{}d}x\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded domain,
z\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is given, and
\tilde{g}:\mathbb{R}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is as in
Section

6
.
The state space is discretized using piecewise linear and continuous finite element functions:
let
V_{h}\subseteq H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the corresponding finite element subspace.
Given
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, let
S_{h}u:=y_{h}\in V_{h}

denote the solution of

\int_{\Omega}\nabla y_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\,\text{{}{}d}x=\int_{\Omega}uv_{h}\,\text{{}{}d}x\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

(7.1)

which gives rise to a linear and bounded operator
S_{h}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to V_{h}
. Note that
the control space
U=L^{2}(\Omega)
is not discretized.

The dual of the discretized problem is now posed in
V_{h}
: find
\xi_{h}\in V_{h}

such that

\xi-z+S_{h}\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(S_{h}^{*}\xi/\alpha)=0.

(7.2)

The crucial observation of
[
16
]
is the following: in this equation
S_{h}
is applied to elements of the type
\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(q_{h})
,
where
q_{h}
is a piecewise linear finite element function. That is, if
\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}
has a favorable structure, then
S_{h}\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(q_{h})
can be evaluated
numerically even if
\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(q_{h})
does not belong to a finite element subspace.
This is the case for the first four examples discussed in
Section

6.2
.
For instance, if
g
models box constraints on the control, then
\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(q_{h})
is piecewise linear.
This piecewise linear function has kinks in the interior of
elements in general, nevertheless the right-hand side for
u=\operatorname{prox}_{g/\alpha}(q_{h})
in (
7.1
) can be computed exactly
[
16
,
15
]
.

In addition, when applying the semismooth Newton method to the dual equation (
7.2
), these kinks do not accumulate.
This is not the case when applying the semismooth Newton to the primal equation as in
Remark

5.26
.
To overcome this difficulty,
[
17
]
suggested to apply the semismooth Newton method to the normal map equation as in
Remark

5.27
.
The variational discretization is not limited to piecewise linear finite elements, in
[
36
]
the application to finite elements of polynomial degree 2 is discussed.

f

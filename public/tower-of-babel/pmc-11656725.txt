Post-transplant complications revealed by mycophenolate mofetil related transporters and metabolic enzymes gene polymorphisms in pediatric patients with hematological disorders
Authors: Qi Ji, Yixin Hu, Minyuan Liu, Lixia Liu, Jiajia Zheng, Zhizhuo Du, Li Gao, Peifang Xiao, Jing Ling, Liyan Fan, Xinni Bian, Feng Lou, Shanbo Cao, Jie Li, Yuanyuan Tian, Jun Lu, Jiayue Qin, Shaoyan Hu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHaploidentical hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (Haplo-HSCT) serves as an important option for patients without an HLA matched donor in treating hematological disorders, while patients may experience various complications after transplantation. Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), a cornerstone drug for graft-versus-host disease (GvHD) prophylaxis, effectively reduces the incidence of acute GvHD, and the efficacy of MMF varies among individuals associated with MMF-related transporters and metabolic enzymes single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). However, limited studies have systematically reported the correlations between the MMF-related SNPs and post-transplant complications.MethodsHere, we conducted a retrospective study involving 90 pediatric patients with hematological disorders who underwent haplo-HSCT at a single center. All patients were subjected to MMF-related SNP testing, combined with common clinical characteristics, to be correlated with post-transplant complications.ResultsWe observed that all 15 MMF-related SNPs were in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium. Based on multivariate Cox regression analysis of post-transplant complications, we discovered that SLCO1B1 (521T > C) variant genotype was an independent protective factor for chronic GvHD (HR = 0.25, 95% confidence interval (CI) (0.08–0.84)). For viral infection, CYP2C8 (1291 + 106T > C) variant genotype was an independent risk factor for cytomegalovirus infection (HR = 2.98, 95% CI (1.18–7.53)). As to hemorrhagic cystitis, SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C) variant genotype was an independent protective factor, while older age was considered as an independent risk factor (HR = 0.41, 95% CI (0.19–0.85); HR = 2.52, 95% CI (1.14–5.54), respectively). No statistical significance was discovered between common clinical characteristics and MMF-related SNPs with other complications, including grade II-IV/III-IV acute GvHD, Epstein-Barr virus infection, peri-engraftment syndrome, and capillary leak syndrome. We also discovered SLCO1B1 (597 C > T) and SLC29A1 (-162 + 228 A > C) variant genotypes are both independent factors for cumulative incidence of relapse after haplo-HSCT (HR = 4.02, 95% CI (1.42–11.44); HR = 0.18, 95% CI (0.07–0.43), respectively).ConclusionsOur findings highlight the significance of MMF-related transporters and metabolic enzymes SNPs in the development of post-transplant complications, contributing to facilitating personalized risk assessment and improving the clinical management in haplo-HSCT patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-13227-0.

Background

Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT) plays an effective role in treating hematological malignancies. However, patients still face various complications after allo-HSCT, including graft-versus-host disease (GvHD), infection, peri-engraftment syndrome (Peri-ES), etc., adversely impairing patient prognosis [1–3]. Haploidentical HSCT (Haplo-HSCT) is considered an important option for patients without an HLA matched donor. As an ester prodrug of mycophenolic acid (MPA), mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) is widely used to prevent acute GvHD (aGvHD) in patients receiving allo-HSCT by blocking the de novo purine synthesis of T- and B-cell lymphocytes [4–6]. With efficacy and safety of MMF in HSCT recipients, increasing emerging evidence suggests the successfulness of utilizing MMF in GvHD prophylaxis [7–9].

MMF is rapidly absorbed after oral medication in the gastrointestinal tract and further transforms to MPA based on esterase. MPA, enterohepatic circulated by organic anion transporting polypeptide (OATP, encoded by SLCO), is metabolized into 7-O-glucoside (MPA glucuronide, MPAG) and acyl-glucuronide (MPA acyl-glucuronide, AcMPAG) by uridine 5ʹ-diphospho-glucuronosyltransferase (UGT) [10]. AcMPAG is highly reactive and concerned with potential adverse effects. Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) involved in MPA disposition help in understanding the interindividual variability of MPA pharmacokinetics [11]. Although well tolerated MPA exhibits no obvious liver and kidney toxicity, it results in a high incidence of adverse events, such as gastrointestinal toxicity, myelotoxicity and infections [12]. The clinical manifestations of these adverse events are diverse in individuals, which may be correlated with the different SNPs.

Cytochrome P450 (CYP450) and UGT are major metabolizing enzymes in the biotransformation of most drugs. The metabolite 6-O-desmethyl-MPA (DM-MPA) is formed by hepatic CYP450 enzymes, mainly CYP3A4 and CYP3A5, and secondary CYP2C8, which undergoes further conjugation to form two glucuronides that constitute minute fractions of MPA [10]. Glucuronidation is an important Phase II metabolic reaction pathway in vivo, which is catalyzed by UGTs and plays a critical role in the elimination and detoxification of endogenous and exogenous compounds. Most UGT genes are polymorphic, and the function or expression of UGTs can be altered by nonsynonymous changes in coding exons and/or changes in regulatory regions [13]. Most UGT-related SNPs affect UGT activity and drug metabolism [14, 15], some of which are associated with increased risk of post-transplant rejection and gastrointestinal side effects [16]. MPAG and Ac-MPAG are substrates for OATPs, which are actively transported from the circulation into hepatocytes via OATP1B1 and OATP1B3, and mainly eliminated by the kidneys [17]. Polymorphisms leading to altered OATP activity may therefore affect MPA pharmacokinetics. ABCB1 (P-glycoprotein; encoded by ABCB1), is a transmembrane transporter belonging to the ATP-binding cassette (ABC) family, primarily expressed in the intestinal epithelium, and plays a crucial role in limiting the absorption of various drugs by actively effluxing them back into the gut lumen. Approximately one hundred SNPs have been identified in the coding region of ABCB1, with 3435 C > T, 2677G > T/A, and 1236 C > T being the most significant in terms of immunosuppressant drugs pharmacokinetics [18, 19]. Several studies, conducted both in vitro and in vivo, have consistently provided evidence that MPA serves as a substrate for ABCB1, illustrating its functional interaction with this transporter protein [20, 21]. Evaluation of ABCB1 genotypes or haplotypes as indirect markers of cellular P-gp have been used to identify patients at higher risk for CNI-associated AE including nephrotoxicity, gingival hyperplasia, and neurotoxicity with variable success [22]. However, limited studies have systematically reported the association between the MMF-related SNPs and post-transplant complications in haplo-HSCT.

This study aims to examine the correlation between common clinical complications, and the SNPs of MMF-related transporters and metabolic enzymes, including CYP, UGT, ABC, and SLC family genes, in 90 pediatric patients with malignant hematological diseases who underwent haplo-HSCT.

Materials and methods

Study cohort

A retrospective cohort study, involving 90 patients who received haplo-HSCT, was performed at a single center in Children’s Hospital of Soochow University (Suzhou, China) between 3st January 2019 and 13th January 2022. This study was approved from the Institutional Review Board at Children’s Hospital of Soochow University (No. 2022CS025) and conducted in compliance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. The study inclusion criteria consisted of (1) first-time transplant; (2) haplo-HSCT; (3) patients aged between 1 and 18 years old; (4) received MMF-based GvHD prophylaxis; and (5) presence of informed consent to MMF-related genotyping.

Conditioning regimen and GvHD prophylaxis

All patients received a myeloablative conditioning regimen based on Busulfan + Cyclophosphamide (BuCy) as the backbone. In some patients, Fludarabine + cytarabine + granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) (FLAG, N = 45), Cladribine + cytarabine + G-CSF (CLAG, N = 19), or other regimen (N = 26) were added as intensified bridging therapies before BuCy transplantation. The drug dosage in the conditioning regimen was adjusted individually based on patients’ height, weight, body surface area, and disease status. The aGvHD prophylaxis for patients receiving halpo-HSCT included MMF and calcineurin inhibitor (CNI) and ATG. The dose of ATG was about 10 mg/kg. MMF was used in all patients for a period of 30 days and a half dose for 15 more days after halpo-HSCT. Regarding the choice of CNIs, 80 patients opted for cyclosporine A (CsA), while the remaining patients used tacrolimus (FK506). Additionally, all patients concurrently received oral methotrexate (MTX) as a prophylactic measure against GvHD. End point definitions

Neutrophil engraftment was defined as achieving an absolute neutrophil count ≥ 0.5 × 109 /L for 3 consecutive days. Platelet engraftment is defined as achieving a platelet count ≥ 20 × 109 /L without transfusion support for 7 consecutive days. Myeloablative conditioning regimen, defined as Bu > 8 mg/kg. Viral DNA ≥ 500 copies/mL in plasma was defined as viremia for cytomegalovirus (CMV) and Epstein-Barr virus (EBV). Hemorrhagic cystitis (HC) was defined as microscopic or macroscopic hematuria and dysuria with a negative bacterial culture in the urine and no other hemorrhagic conditions [23]. Primary graft failure (GF) was defined as the failure of neutrophil engraftment at day + 28 after HSCT, while secondary GF was defined as the development of neutrophil count < 0.5 × 109/L occurring after the initial engraftment. Peri-ES was defined according to the previously described criteria [24]. The diagnosis and grading of aGvHD and chronic GvHD (cGvHD) were assigned by the transplant center using standard criteria [25, 26]. The definition of capillary leak syndrome (CLS), as per previous standards, is characterized by the excessive leakage of fluid and proteins from the capillaries into the surrounding tissues [27]. Overall survival (OS) was calculated from transplant to date of death or last follow-up.

DNA extraction and genotyping

Genomic DNA was extracted from bone marrow diagnostic samples in this study. A targeted sequencing gene panel, comprising 27 genes, was specifically employed for next-generation sequencing (NGS) at Acornmed Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Tianjin, China). The sequencing multiplex library methods, raw data filtration principles, and variant calling standards were same as our previous study [28].

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences software (IBM SPSS Statistics, version 22.0) or R (version 3.5.2). The quantitative data were measured by T-test or Mann-Whitney U test, and categorized data were compared with Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. Event time distributions for the clinical outcomes were estimated using cumulative incidence curve with log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazard regression analysis were used to compare the relative risk for aGvHD, cGvHD, CMV, EBV, Peri-ES, HC, CLS and OS. Univariate and multivariate Fine-Gray proportional hazard regression analysis were adopted for cumulative incidence of relapse (CIR) and non-relapse mortality (NRM). Variables were selected by univariate regression analysis. Those with P < 0.1 were subsequently enrolled in multivariate regression analysis. All tests were two-sided, and statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Patient clinical characteristics

A total of 90 pediatric patients were involved in our study and the patient clinical characteristics are described in Table 1. The median age at haplo-HSCT was 73 months old. Acute leukemia, including acute myeloid leukemia (AML), acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) and mixed phenotype acute leukemia (MAPL), was the most common etiology, accounting for 95.6% (86/90). All patients were administered GvHD prophylaxis based on MMF. All patients achieved hematopoietic recovery from haplo-HSCT. The median time to neutrophil and platelet engraftment was 11 days (interquartile range (IQR), 11–12), and 11 days (IQR, 10–15), respectively.

Correlation between clinical characteristics and MMF-related transporters and metabolic enzymes SNPs with post correlation between clinical characteristics and MMF-related transporters and metabolic enzymes SNPs with post

In our study, we analyzed the association between common clinical characteristics and MMF-related transporters and metabolic enzymes SNPs with post-transplant complications, including GvHD, viral infection, and other complications. Out of 90 patients, 30 (33.3%) patients developed grade II-IV aGvHD, 14 (15.6%) patients developed grade III-IV aGvHD, 54 (60.0%) patients developed cGvHD, 56 (62.2%) patients experienced CMV infection, 57 (63.3%) patients experienced EBV infection, 57 (63.3%) patients developed Peri-ES, 30 (33.3%) patients developed HC, and 16 (17.8%) patients experienced CLS.

From the results of cumulative incidence curves, we concluded that SLCO1B1 (521T > C) wild type was correlated with a higher incidence of cGvHD (P = 0.006, Fig. 2A). Patients with higher mononuclear cell (MNC) dose, higher CD34 + cell dose, CYP2C8 (1291 + 106T > C) mutant genotype, ABCB1 (1000–44 C > T) wild genotype, ABCB1 (1554 + 24 A > G) wild genotype, ABCB1 (1725 + 38 C > T) wild genotype, and SLC29A1 (-162 + 228 A > C) wild genotype, had a higher incidence of CMV infections (all P < 0.05, Additional file 1: Figure S1A, Fig. 2B). As to HC, we found age at transplantation, donor gender, and SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C) showed a correlation with HC (P = 0.010, P = 0.040, P = 0.034, respectively, Additional file 1: Figure S1B, Fig. 2C). Both SLCO1B1 (597 C > T) and SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C) mutant genotypes were found to be associated with CLS (P = 0.013, P = 0.010, respectively, Fig. 2D). The results of statistical differences were not observed in the analysis of grade II-IV aGvHD, grade III-IV aGvHD, EBV, and Peri-ES.

Additionally, we constructed multivariate Cox regression analysis using variables selected by univariate Cox regression analysis with P < 0.1, and the results are presented in Fig. 3 and Additional file 1: Table S2-S9. We discovered that SLCO1B1 (521T > C) variant genotype was an independent protective factor for cGvHD (HR = 0.25, 95% confidence interval (CI) (0.08–0.84), Fig. 3A and Additional file 1: Table S4). For viral infection, CYP2C8 (1291 + 106T > C) variant genotype was an independent risk factor for CMV infection (HR = 2.98, 95% CI (1.18–7.53), Fig. 3B and Additional file 1: Table S5). For HC, SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C) variant genotype was an independent protective factor, while older age was considered as an independent risk factor for HC (HR = 0.41, 95% CI (0.19–0.85); HR = 2.52, 95% CI (1.14–5.54), respectively, Fig. 3C and Additional file 1: Table S8). No statistical significance was discovered between common clinical characteristics and MMF-related transporters and metabolic enzymes SNPs with other complications, including grade II-IV aGvHD, grade III-IV aGvHD, EBV infection, Peri-ES, and CLS (Additional file 1: Table S2, S3, S6, S7 and S9).

We then analyzed the factors that might affect the patient prognosis, including CIR, NRM and OS based on univariate and multivariate analysis. We found that SLCO1B1 (597 C > T) variant genotype was a poor prognostic factor, while SLC29A1 (-162 + 228 A > C) variant genotype and grade III-IV aGvHD were favorable prognostic factors for CIR (all P < 0.05, Additional file 1: Table S10). At the same time, the female donor gender, Peri-ES and CLS were all adverse prognostic factors for NRM (all P < 0.05, Additional file 1: Table S11). In OS analysis, we discovered that HC and CLS were both independent adverse prognostic factors (both P < 0.05, Additional file 1: Table S12).

Discussion

In the field of allo-HSCT, complications are common: GvHD, virus infection, Peri-ES, etc., which significantly contribute to the increased morbidity and mortality [29, 30]. In addition to several clinical factors, drug related SNPs serve as a key tool to improve outcomes and prevent adverse effects [31, 32]. In our study, we analyzed the correlation between MMF-related transporter and metabolizing enzyme gene polymorphisms, and post-transplant complications in 90 pediatric patients. To the best of knowledge, we systematically revealed MMF-related gene polymorphisms were associated with post-transplant complications in pediatric patients with hematological disorders after haplo-HSCT for the first time.

GvHD is a common complication following HSCT in children. Recent evidence suggests that polymorphisms in donor and recipient non-HLA genes, including cytokine genes, innate immunity genes, and pharmacogenetic polymorphisms, are associated with the risk of GvHD [33]. In our cohort, we have observed for the first time that the incidence of cGvHD is significantly higher in patients with SLCO1B1 (521T > C) TT genotype compared to those with TC/CC genotype. cGvHD is a systemic complication that specifically arises after allo-HSCT and is triggered by the engrafted donor immune system. cGvHD involves the dysregulation of both B and T cells, which can be modulated by the inhibitory effects of MMF [34]. OATP, encoding by SLC family, is mainly distributed in the liver and involved in the uptake of MPA and MPAG. Masatomo Miura found that MPAG pharmacokinetics are affected by SLCO1B1 SNPs [35]. Several studies have also shown that patients with SLCO1B1 (521T > C) C allele variant have higher plasma concentrations of MMF [36, 37]. This may explain why patients with the SLCO1B1 (521T > C) TT genotype have a tendency to develop cGvHD.

Virus infections, including CMV and EBV, following on allo-HSCT are a major cause of morbidity and mortality [38]. The use of potent immunosuppressive agents in the prevention strategy of GvHD may lead to an increase in the occurrence of infections [39]. Therefore, we conducted an analysis on the correlation between MMF-related SNPs and viral infections. Our data showed that a total of 62.2% (56/90) individuals had at least one CMV infection, while 63.3% (57/90) individuals had at least one EBV infection. ABCB1 is a member of the ABC superfamily, which encoded P-gp serving as an ATP-dependent efflux pump for substrates, resulting in reduction of systemic exposure and lower intracellular drug accumulation [40]. The ABCB1 (3435 C > T) variant genotype, the CYP2C8 (1291 + 106T > C) variant genotype, and the SLC29A1 (-162 + 228 A > C) wild genotype were identified as independent risk factors for CMV infection. Unfortunately, no significant correlation was observed between these polymorphisms and EBV infection in our study. It is well known that CMV infections commonly occur in immunocompromised individuals. Current research suggests that the polymorphisms of transporter proteins may be associated with variations in the levels of immunosuppressive agents [37]. S. Hoffmeyer et al. discovered that individuals with ABCB1 (3435 C > T) T-allele have more than a 2-fold decrease in ABCB1 expression levels compared to those with the homozygous CC genotype [41]. These results in the accumulation of MPA in the body, leading to increased exposure and ultimately resulted in a more potent immunosuppressive effect compared to the wild type allele. This finding helps explain why patients carrying the ABCB1 wild type genotype are more susceptible to CMV infection.

CYP2C8 is involved in drug metabolism and the 6-O-desmethyl-mycophenolate acid metabolite is produced by CYP3A4/5 and probably CYP2C8 [42]. It was reported that the CYP2C8 variant allele was associated with reduced CYP2C8 metabolic activity compared with wild-type enzyme [43]. There is a limited amount of literature available regarding the association between MMF and CYP2C8. Existing studies have indicated that the CYP2C8 variant allele has previously been associated with an elevated risk of leucopenia. This may be partly influenced by a decreased of MPA metabolism associated to the CYP2C8 variant allele and to a higher exposure to MPA, which is well known to favor leucopenia [42, 44]. Therefore, we speculate that the CYP2C8 (1291 + 106T > C) variant allele may increase MPA exposure, thereby enhancing the immunosuppressive effect and subsequently increasing the risk of CMV infection. SLC29A1, encoding the human equilibrative nucleoside transporter-1 (hENT-1), plays a crucial role in cellular uptake of anti-cancer drugs and nucleosides [45]. However, no evidence currently suggested that SLC29A1 is involved in the transport of MMF, and the impact of SLC29A1 (-162 + 228 A > C) on the transport function of SLC29A1 protein has not been reported yet. Therefore, further investigation is needed to validate this hypothesis through fundamental functional experiments.

Peri-ES is defined as a clinical syndrome encompassing both ES and pre-ES, which is closely correlated with the prognosis. In our cohort, 63.3% of patients (57/90) developed Peri-ES, which is similar to our previous report [28]. Our data showed that there was a lower incidence of Peri-ES in patients with SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C) TT genotype, an intronic variant of the SLCO1B1 gene located at chr12:21229685 [46], compared to those with the TC/CC genotype, which was consistent with our observations in MTX [28]. Currently, no reported studies were focused on the association between MMF and SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C). As SLCO1B1 has been found to be a common bidirectional transporter protein for both MTX and MMF, we suggested that its action mechanism on MMF may be similar to that on MTX. The SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C) C-allele variant might potentially reduce the entry of MMF into cells via transporters and thereby exert its effect [35, 47]. In the future, we plan to validate our findings by evaluating the plasma concentrations of MMF in HSCT patients from a pharmacokinetic perspective.

Next, we investigated the association between polymorphisms in MMF-related SNPs and HC, and found that the SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C) wild genotype is an independent risk factor for HC. As widely recognized, BK virus (BKV)-related HC is a significant contributor to morbidity after HSCT [48]. Based on our previous experiences, early-onset HC is often induced by cyclophosphamide. Late onset HC is often associated with a consequence of virus reactivation, most commonly involving BKV [49]. Due to the lack of specific antiviral drugs effective in children against BKV, and considering economic factors and patient benefit principles, we did not conduct BKV testing. In this study, the median time for the occurrence of HC was 19 days (IQR, 8.5–30 days). Therefore, we think that viral reactivation is more likely than cyclophosphamide-induced HC. Enhanced immunosuppression is associated with an increased susceptibility to BKV infection [50]. OATP1B1, encoding by SLCO1B1 gene, mediates the transport of MPA from blood to hepatocytes, thus reducing the plasma MPA concentration [36]. As hypothesized earlier, the SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C) C allele variant may potentially result in a reduction in the cellular transport of MMF through transporters. Consequently, this polymorphic variant leads to a decreased immunosuppressive effect, reducing the risk of BKV infection and subsequently decreasing the incidence of HC. Hence, further investigation is required to acquire additional information that will facilitate a better comprehension of the underlying biological mechanisms contributing to its linkage with the development of HC.

CLS is characterized by plasma extravasation into the interstitium with resultant hypotension, anasarca, hemoconcentration, and hypoalbuminemia in the absence of albuminuria [51]. Currently, underlying cause of CLS lies in the presence of high levels of proinflammatory cytokines, which subsequently triggers endothelial cell damage and apoptosis [52]. Our results suggest that the SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C) variant as an independent risk factor for the development of CLS, which aligns with our previous speculation regarding the mechanistic role of SLCO1B1 (1865 + 4846T > C), leading to diminished the immunosuppressive effects of MMF on immune cells, thereby increasing the risk of high levels of proinflammatory cytokines.

MMF is commonly associated with adverse effects such as diarrhea, leukopenia, and anemia, particularly in autoimmune diseases and kidney transplantation [36, 53]. As to HSCT, especially haplo-HSCT, various factors contribute to gastrointestinal and bone marrow toxicity, including intensive preconditioning chemotherapy, antibiotics, other adjunct therapies, as well as recipient age and disease status. Furthermore, none of the patients in our study experienced severe diarrhea, and all achieved successful neutrophil and platelet engraftment. Therefore, gastrointestinal and bone marrow toxicities were not included in the analysis of our study.

In this study, the SLCO1B1 (597 C > T) variant type and SLC29A1 (-162 + 228 A > C) wild type were identified as independent risk factors for CIR. Previous research has indicated that the SLCO1B1 (521T > C) variant type increases plasma concentrations of MMF [36, 37]. Therefore, we thought that the SLCO1B1 (597 C > T) variant might exert a similar effect, as elevated MMF levels have been associated with reduced T cell proliferation [54], potentially increasing CIR. There have been no reports linking the SLC29A1 (-162 + 228 A > C) variant type to post-HSCT relapse. SLC29A1 was expressed in 83% of AML patients. Some reports suggest it may be associated with resistance of AML cells to AraC [49, 55]. Therefore, we speculate that the SLC29A1 (-162 + 228 A > C) variant may contribute to decreased CIR risk by mediating Ara-C resistance.

In contrast to the predominant use of HLA-matched allo-HSCT and unrelated donor transplantation patterns abroad, haplo-HSCT holds the leading position in China due to its extensive applicability [56]. However, it is acknowledged that post-haplo-HSCT complications may be influenced by various factors, including other immunosuppressive drugs. This study focused on a homogeneous cohort of patients with hematologic malignancies, maintained consistent GvHD prophylaxis, and employed the same myeloablative conditioning regimen to minimize these impacts. We also implemented thorough pre-transplantation assessments to ensure the pre-transplantation status of donors and recipients, including measures such as preemptive acyclovir administration for virus-positive donors, aiming to minimize these confounding factors influence. In the future, additional types of transplants need to be included to assess the role of MMF-related genetic polymorphisms in HSCT.

This study is limited by its retrospective nature and small sample size. Furthermore, the assessment of MMF-related gene polymorphisms in donors, which may influence the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of MMF, was not included in our study. Importantly, this study did not measure MPA concentrations to assess pharmacokinetics. Therefore, there is a need for large-scale retrospective or prospective studies to further investigate these factors, and efforts may involve using high-performance liquid chromatography and limited sampling strategies to measure MPA and MPAG concentrations. In the future, we plan to combine pediatric HSCT pharmacokinetic models for MPA administered orally with MMF-related genetic polymorphisms, aimed at individualizing dose adjustments based on the interdose area under the curve.

Conclusions

In summary, our study revealed significant correlations between MMF-related transporters and metabolic enzymes SNPs and post-transplant complications following haplo-HSCT, including GvHD, CMV, HC, and CLS, contributing to facilitating personalized risk assessment and improving the clinical management of haplo-HSCT patients.

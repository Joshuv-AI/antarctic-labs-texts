1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the twisted second moment of primitive cubic Dirichlet
L
-functions
L_{q}(s,\chi)
evaluated at the central point
s=\frac{1}{2}
, where
\chi
ranges over primitive cubic characters of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
of fixed genus

1.1.
Background

The study of moments of
L
-functions is a central topic in analytic number theory, offering insight into the value distribution, non-vanishing, and zero statistics of these functions across various families.

In the number field setting, Luo
[
14
]
investigated the first moment of cubic Dirichlet twists over
\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{3})
, while Baier and Young
[
1
]
considered the first moment of central values of Dirichlet
L
-functions associated with primitive cubic characters over
\mathbb{Q}
. Güloglu and Yesilyurt
[
11
]
studied the mollified first moment over the Eisenstein field, and also provided upper bounds for the mollified second moment. More recently, David et al.
[
5
]
analyzed the mollified second moment in the same setting and obtained a power-saving error term, which they used to study the non-vanishing of cubic
L
-functions.

In the function field context, significant progress has been made. David, Florea and Lalín
[
8
]
computed the first moment of cubic L-functions over function fields with characters of fixed genus, assuming
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. Later, in
[
6
]
, they established upper bounds for a mollified second moment to study non-vanishing at the central point. Specifically, they proved that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal{C}(g)}|L(\frac{1}{2},\chi)|^{k}|M(\chi;\frac{1}{\kappa})|^{k\kappa}\ll q^{g}.

where
\mathcal{C}(g)
denotes the set of primitive cubic characters of genus
g
and
M(\chi;\frac{1}{\kappa})
is a Dirichlet polynomial. In their most recent work
[
7
]
, they applied the theory of low-lying zeros to further investigate non-vanishing phenomena. In a complementary direction, Goel and Ray
[
10
]
computed the (untwisted) second moment of cubic
L
-functions and showed that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi~primitive~cubic\\
genus(\chi)=g\end{subarray}}L_{q}(\frac{1}{2},\chi)^{2}=\frac{g(g+2)A_{q}(\frac{1}{q^{2}},\frac{1}{q^{3/2}})\zeta_{q}(3/2)^{2}}{8\zeta_{q}(3)}q^{g+2}+O(q^{g}),

where
A_{q}
is an explicitly defined function. Their method builds upon the framework developed in
[
8
]
.

Despite these advances, the asymptotic of twisted moments in the cubic case has not yet been studied.

1.2.
Our contribution

In this paper, we address this gap and establish the first result in this direction. Specifically, we consider the twisted second moment in the non-Kummer setting. Our main theorem is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q
be an odd prime power such that
q\equiv 2\moda 3
and
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be fixed polynomials. Then, for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\ primitive\ cubic\\
genus(\chi)=g\end{subarray}}\chi(h_{1})\bar{\chi}(h_{2})|L_{q}(\frac{1}{2},\chi)|^{2}=(1-q^{2})SC(h_{1},h_{2})P(q^{-2})q^{g}+O(q^{(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)g}),

where
P(u)
,
S
and
C(h_{1},h_{2})
are given in Section
3.2
.

Here
P(u)
is a function defined by (
3.12
),
S
is a constant from (
3.21
) and
C(h_{1},h_{2})
is a constant depending on the twisting polynomials
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
.
L_{q}(s,\chi)
is the
L
-function associeted to
\chi
.

Our result is consistent with the bound in (
1.1
), and provides new quantitative information about the second moment with nontrivial twists.

1.3.
Methodology

A key innovation in our approach is the use of double Dirichlet series. Using Perron’s formula, we express the twisted second moment as a contour integral involving such a series. By representing the double Dirichlet series in different ways, we identify multiple regions of convergence. Basic tools from complex analysis imply that the convex hull of these regions also lies within the domain of absolute convergence, allowing us to shift contours further and isolate the main term.

This method—originating from studies of moments of L-functions of higher-order characters (see
[
9
,
13
,
12
]
)—proves to be powerful and flexible. In particular, it avoids the intricate analysis of the distribution of cubic Gauss sums, which significantly simplifies the computation and reduces technical complexity.

Compared to the approaches used in
[
8
]
and
[
10
]
, our method is more direct and computationally efficient.

2.2.
Multivarible Complex Analysis Lemmas

Our approach relies on two foundational results from multivariable complex analysis. We begin by introducing the concept of a tube domain.

Definition 2.2
.

An open set
T\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a tube if there is an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
T=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\ \Re(z)\in U\}.

For any set
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a connected open set and
f(z)
be a function holomorphic on
T(U)
. Then
f(z)
has a holomorphic continuation to
the convex hull of
T(U)
.

We denote the convex hull of an open set

by
\widehat{T}
. Our next result is
[
4
, Proposition C.5]
on the modulus of holomorphic continuations of multivariable complex functions.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that
T\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a tube domain,
g,h:T\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are holomorphic functions, and let
\tilde{g},\tilde{h}
be their
holomorphic continuations to
\widehat{T}
. If
|g(z)|\leq|h(z)|
for all
z\in T
and
h(z)
is nonzero in
T
, then also
|\tilde{g}(z)|\leq|\tilde{h}(z)|
for all
z\in\widehat{T}
.

3.2.

From (

\displaystyle\Res_{u=q^{-2}}A(u,q^{-\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Res_{u=q^{-2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1},N_{2}\in\Aq\\
h_{1}h_{2}^{2}N_{1}N_{2}^{2}~is~a~cube\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|N_{1}N_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\zeta_{q^{2}}(u)}{\zeta_{q^{2}}(u^{2})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{2}\in\A 2\\

P_{1}|hN\end{subarray}}\left(1-u^{\deg P_{1}}\prod_{P_{2}|P_{1}}(1+u^{\deg P_{2}})^{-1}\right)^{-1}P(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1},N_{2}\\
h_{1}h_{2}^{2}N_{1}N_{2}^{2}~is~a~cube\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|N_{1}N_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{2}\in\A 2\\
P_{2}|hN\end{subarray}}\left(1+u^{\deg P_{2}}\right)^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{1}\in\Aq\\
P_{1}|hN\end{subarray}}\left(1-u^{\deg P_{1}}\prod_{P_{2}|P_{1}}(1+u^{\deg P_{2}})^{-1}\right)^{-1}P(q^{-2})\Res_{u=q^{-2}}\frac{\zeta_{q^{2}}(u)}{\zeta_{q^{2}}(u^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q^{-4}-q^{-2})S(q^{-\frac{1}{2}})P(q^{-2}),

where

(3.11)

\displaystyle S(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1},N_{2}\\
h_{1}h_{2}^{2}N_{1}N_{2}^{2}~is~a~cube\end{subarray}}v^{\deg N_{1}N_{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{2}\in\A 2\\
P_{2}|hN\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}}\right)^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{1}\in\Aq\\
P_{1}|hN\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{|P_{1}|_{2}}\prod_{P_{2}|P_{1}}(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}})^{-1}\right)^{-1},

and

(3.12)

\displaystyle P(u)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{1}\in\Aq\end{subarray}}\left(1-u^{\deg P_{1}}\prod_{P_{2}|P_{1}}(1+u^{\deg P_{2}})^{-1}\right).

For each prime
P\in\Aq
, we define

(3.13)

\displaystyle ord_{P}(h_{1})=a_{p};

(3.14)

\displaystyle ord_{P}(h_{2})=b_{p};

(3.15)

\displaystyle ord_{P}(N_{1})=c_{p};

(3.16)

\displaystyle ord_{P}(N_{2})=d_{p}.

Next, we rewrite
S(v)
as an Eular product

\displaystyle S(v)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\Aq\\
P|h\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{2}|P\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{|P|_{2}}\prod_{P_{2}|P}(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}})^{-1}\right)^{-1}\sum_{3|a_{P}+2b_{P}+c_{P}+2d_{P}}v^{\deg P(c_{P}+d_{P})}\right)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\Aq\\
P\nmid h\end{subarray}}\left(1+\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{2}|P\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{|P|_{2}}\prod_{P_{2}|P}(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}})^{-1}\right)^{-1}\sum_{3|c_{P}+2d_{P}}v^{\deg P(c_{P}+d_{P})}\right)

and separate the factors related to
h_{1}
,
h_{2}

\displaystyle S(v)=\prod_{P}G_{P}(v)C(h_{1},h_{2}),

where

(3.17)

\displaystyle G_{P}(v)=1+\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{2}|P\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{|P|_{2}}\prod_{P_{2}|P}(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}})^{-1}\right)^{-1}\sum_{3|c_{P}+2d_{P}}v^{\deg P(c_{P}+d_{P})}

and

(3.18)

\displaystyle C(h_{1},h_{2},v)=\prod_{P|h}\frac{\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{2}|P\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{|P|_{2}}\prod\limits_{P_{2}|P}(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}})^{-1}\right)^{-1}\sum\limits_{3|a_{P}+2b_{P}+c_{P}+2d_{P}}v^{\deg P(c_{P}+d_{P})}}{G_{P}(v)}.

For each prime
P\in\Aq
, we distinguish two cases depending on whether
\deg P
is even or odd (noting that
P\in\Aq
splits in
\A 2
if and only if
\deg P
is even). In fact, we have

\displaystyle G_{P}(v)=\begin{cases}1+\sum\limits_{3|c_{P}+2d_{P}}v^{\deg P(c_{P}+d_{P})},&\mbox{if }2\nmid\deg P;\\
1+\left(1+\frac{2}{|P|}\right)^{-1}\sum\limits_{3|c_{P}+2d_{P}}v^{\deg P(c_{P}+d_{P})},&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Using the orthogonality of primitive cubic root

(3.19)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\varpi^{kn}=\begin{cases}1,&n\equiv 0\moda 3;\\
0,&otherwise,\end{cases}

for
2|\deg P
, we have

\displaystyle G_{P}(v)

\displaystyle=1+(1+\frac{2}{|P|})^{-1}\sum_{3|c_{P}+2d_{P}}v^{\deg P(c_{P}+d_{P})}

\displaystyle=1+(1+\frac{2}{|P|})^{-1}(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{P}\geq 0,d_{P}\geq 0\\
\end{subarray}}v^{\deg P(c_{P}+d_{P})}\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\varpi^{k(c_{P}+2d_{P})}-1)

\displaystyle=1+(1+\frac{2}{|P|})^{-1}(\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sum_{c_{P},d_{P}}\varpi^{kc_{P}}v^{\deg Pc_{P}}\varpi^{2kd_{P}}v^{\deg Pd_{P}}-1)

\displaystyle=1+(1+\frac{2}{|P|})^{-1}(\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{1}{1-\varpi^{k}v^{\deg P}}\frac{1}{1-\varpi^{2k}v^{\deg P}}-1)

\displaystyle=1+(1+\frac{2}{|P|})^{-1}\frac{v^{2\deg P}(1+v^{\deg P}-v^{2\deg P})}{(1-v^{2\deg P})^{2}(1+v^{\deg P}+v^{2\deg P})}.

For
2\nmid\deg P
, we have

\displaystyle G_{P}(v)=

\displaystyle 1+\sum\limits_{3|c_{P}+2d_{P}}v^{\deg P(c_{P}+d_{P})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{2}(\sum_{c_{P}\geq 0}v^{\deg Pc_{P}}\varpi^{kc_{P}})(\sum_{d_{P}\geq 0}v^{\deg Pc_{P}}\varpi^{2kd_{P}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{1}{1-\varpi^{k}v^{\deg P}}\frac{1}{1-\varpi^{2k}v^{\deg P}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-v^{\deg P}+v^{2\deg P}}{(1-v^{\deg P})^{2}(1+v^{\deg P}+v^{2\deg P})}.

Using similar method, we have

\displaystyle C(h_{1},h_{2},v)=\frac{\prod\limits_{P|h}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{2}|P\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{|P|_{2}}\prod\limits_{P_{2}|P}(1+\frac{1}{|P_{2}|_{2}})^{-1}\right)^{-1}\frac{1}{3}(\frac{1}{(1-v^{\deg P})^{2}}+\frac{\varpi^{a_{P}+2b_{P}}+\varpi^{2a_{P}+b_{P}}}{1+v^{\deg P}+v^{2\deg P}})}{\prod\limits_{P|h}G_{P}(v)}.

For simplicity, we denote

(3.20)

\displaystyle C(h_{1},h_{2})=C(h_{1},h_{2},q^{-\frac{1}{2}})

and

(3.21)

\displaystyle S=\prod_{P}G_{P}(q^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

3.3.
The second convergence region

Inserting this into

P|F\to P\not\in\Aq\end{subarray}}|q^{2\varepsilon}u|^{\deg F}.

Hence,

We begin by considering the case where

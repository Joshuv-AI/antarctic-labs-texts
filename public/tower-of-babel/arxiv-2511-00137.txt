Introduction

We consider an integral transform
T_{\nu}
given by

(1.1)

T_{\nu}f(s)\coloneqq\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}rsJ_{\nu}(rs)^{2}f(r)\,dr,

where
J_{\nu}
denotes the Bessel function of the first kind of order
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)
.
It is easy to see that the right-hand side exists as a Lebesgue integral for every
s\in(0,\infty)
when

(1.2)

\int_{0}^{1}\lvert f(r)\rvert r^{2\nu+1}\,dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\lvert f(r)\rvert\,dr<\infty

holds, and in this case
T_{\nu}f
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
. In what follows,
we write
L^{1}_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}
for the space of all measurable functions
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
such that the following norm is finite:

(1.3)

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\lvert f(r)\rvert r^{\nu_{1}}\,dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\lvert f(r)\rvert r^{\nu_{2}}\,dr.

The aim of this paper are to establish a certain identity for
T_{\nu}
(Theorem
1.6
) and to derive several inequalities from it (Theorems
1.5
and
1.8
).

As shown by
Walther 2002
, the transform
T_{\nu}
plays an essential role in the study of the following inequality, known as the Kato–Yajima smoothing estimate for the free Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(1.4)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}w(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\psi(\lvert\nabla\rvert)e^{it\Delta}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\lVert u_{0}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
w,\psi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
are some given functions, which will be referred to as a spatial weight and smoothing function, respectively.
For example, it is classically known that the smoothing estimate holds in the following cases:

(A)

\displaystyle d\geq 3,\quad

\displaystyle(w(r),\psi(r))=(

\displaystyle(1+r^{2})^{-1},\quad

\displaystyle(1+r^{2})^{1/4}

\displaystyle),

(B)

\displaystyle d\geq 2,\quad

\displaystyle 1<a<d,\quad

\displaystyle(w(r),\psi(r))=(

\displaystyle r^{-a},\quad

\displaystyle r^{(2-a)/2}

\displaystyle),

(C)

\displaystyle d\geq 2,\quad

\displaystyle a>1,\quad

\displaystyle(w(r),\psi(r))=(

\displaystyle(1+r^{2})^{-a/2},\quad

\displaystyle r^{1/2}

\displaystyle).

See
Kato and Yajima 1989
for (
A
),

Kato and Yajima 1989
,
Sugimoto 1998
,
Watanabe 1991
for (
B
),
Ben-Artzi and Klainerman 1992
,
Chihara 2002
for (
C
).
Furthermore, the exponents in these cases are sharp;
see
Walther 1999
for (
A
)
,

Vilela 2001
for (
B
), and
Walther 2000
for (
C
).
For each
(w,\psi)
and dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, let
\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}(w,\psi)
be the optimal constant of the inequality (
1.4
).

Walther 2002
proved the following formula for this optimal constant.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
33
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Then we have

(1.5)

\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}(w,\psi)=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{r>0}r^{-1}\psi(r)^{2}T_{k+d/2-1}w(r).

Note that Theorem
1.1
immediately implies

(1.6)

\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d+2)}(w,\psi)\leq\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}(w,\psi)

for every pair
(w,\psi)
whenever
d\geq 2
.
On the other hand, interestingly, it is known that

(1.7)

\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d+1)}(w,\psi)\leq\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}(w,\psi)

holds in the cases (
A
), (
B
), (
C
).
This is a consequence of the following results, which gives the explicit values of
\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}
in these cases.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
7
, Theorem 1.7]
)
.

In the case (
A
), we have

(1.8)

\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}=\begin{cases}\pi,&d=3,\\
\pi\sup_{r>0}(1+r^{2})^{1/2}I_{1}(r)K_{1}(r),&d=4,\\
\pi/2,&d\geq 5,\end{cases}

where
I_{\nu}
and
K_{\nu}
are the modified Bessel functions of first and second kinds order
\nu
, respectively.
Here we note that

(1.9)

\sup_{r>0}(1+r^{2})^{1/2}I_{1}(r)K_{1}(r)=0.50239\ldots.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
34
, Corollary 4]
,
[
24
, (3)]
,
[
7
, Theorem 1.6]
)
.

In the case (
B
), we have

(1.10)

\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}=\pi^{1/2}\frac{\Gamma((a-1)/2)\Gamma((d-a)/2)}{2\Gamma(a/2)\Gamma((d+a)/2-1)}.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
24
, (2)]
,
[
8
, Theorem 1.4, Corollary 1.5]
)
.

In the case (
C
), we have

(1.11)

\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}=\frac{\Gamma((a-1)/2)}{2\Gamma(a)}

whenever
d\geq 3
.
More generally, if
d\geq 3
and the Fourier transform of a spatial weight
w\in L^{1}_{0,0}=L^{1}
as a radial function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is non-negative,
then we have

(1.12)

\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}(w,r^{1/2})=\int_{0}^{\infty}w(r)\,dr.

Here, the non-negativity of the Fourier transform should be understood in the distributional sense. Equivalently, it is non-negative if and only if

(1.13)

\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}w(\lvert x\rvert)\exp(-\varepsilon\lvert x\rvert^{2})\exp(-ix\cdot\xi)\,dx\geq 0

holds for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Our first result is a sufficient condition for the inequality (
1.7
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
d\geq 2
.
Assume that both of the Fourier transforms of a spatial weight
w\in L^{1}_{d-1,0}
as radial functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
are non-negative.
Then we have

(1.14)

\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d+1)}(w,\psi)\leq\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}(w,\psi)

for every smoothing function
\psi\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
.

Note that the assumption of Theorem
1.5
is satisfied in the cases (
A
), (
B
), (
C
), since the Riesz and Bessel kernels are non-negative.

Our proof of Theorem
1.5
is inspired by that of Theorem
1.4
given by
Bez et al. 2015
.
In order to explain their proof, we introduce the Hankel transform and the Gegenbauer function of the first kind.

Definition 1

(Hankel transform)
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)
and
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
. The Hankel transform of
f
of order
\nu
is defined by

(1.15)

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f(\rho)\coloneqq\rho^{-\nu}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\nu+1}J_{\nu}(r\rho)f(r)\,dr.

Definition 2

(Gegenbauer function of the first kind)
.

Let
\mu\in[0,\infty)
and
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)
. The Gegenbauer function of the first kind
\mathrm{C}^{(\nu)}_{\mu}\colon(-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(1.16)

\mathrm{C}^{(\nu)}_{\mu}(x)\coloneqq\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-\mu,\mu+2\nu\\
\nu+1/2\end{matrix};(1-x)/2\right),

where
\,{}_{2}F_{1}
denotes the hypergeometric function, which is given by

(1.17)

\displaystyle\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}a,b\\
c\end{matrix};x\right)\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}}\frac{x^{n}}{n!},

(1.18)

\displaystyle(a)_{n}\coloneqq\prod_{m=0}^{n-1}(a+m)=\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(a)}.

We remark that when
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, the Hankel transform
\mathcal{H}_{d/2-1}f
coincides with the Fourier transform of
f
as a radial function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the sense that

(1.19)

\mathcal{H}_{d/2-1}f(\lvert\xi\rvert)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\lvert x\rvert)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,dx

holds whenever
f\in L^{1}_{d-1,d-1}
(
\iff f(\lvert\makebox[4.30554pt]{{$\cdot$}}\rvert)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
).

Now we define another integral transform
U_{k,d/2-1}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
as follows:

(1.20)

\displaystyle U_{k,d/2-1}f(s)\coloneqq\int_{r\in(0,\infty)}\mathcal{K}_{k,d/2-1}(r,s)f(r)\,dr,

(1.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{k,d/2-1}(r,s)\coloneqq\dfrac{\pi^{1/2}}{2^{d/2-1}\Gamma((d-1)/2)}\times\begin{cases}r^{d-2}(1-r^{2}/(4s^{2}))^{(d-3)/2}\mathrm{C}^{(d/2-1)}_{k}(1-r^{2}/(2s^{2})),&0<r<2s,\\
0,&r>2s>0.\end{cases}

Bez et al. 2015
showed that

(1.22)

T_{k+d/2-1}f=U_{k,d/2-1}\mathcal{H}_{d/2-1}f

holds whenever
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
, and
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
is sufficiently nice.
This identity leads us to the following observations:

Observation 1
.

We see that

(1.23)

T_{k+d/2-1}f(s)\leq T_{d/2-1}f(s)

holds if
\mathcal{H}_{d/2-1}f
is non-negative, since

(1.24)

\lvert\mathrm{C}^{(d/2-1)}_{k}(x)\rvert\leq\mathrm{C}^{(d/2-1)}_{k}(1)=\mathrm{C}^{(d/2-1)}_{0}(x)=1

holds for every
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x\in(-1,1]
(see
[
1
, (2.116)]
).

Observation 2
.

We see that

(1.25)

(0,\infty)\ni s\longmapsto T_{d/2-1}f(s)=\dfrac{\pi^{1/2}}{2^{d/2-1}\Gamma((d-1)/2)}\int_{r\in(0,2s)}r^{d-2}(1-r^{2}/(4s^{2}))^{(d-3)/2}\mathcal{H}_{d/2-1}f(r)\,dr

is non-decreasing if
\mathcal{H}_{d/2-1}f
is non-negative and
d\geq 3
,
since

(1.26)

(0,\infty)\ni s\longmapsto\begin{cases}r^{d-2}(1-r^{2}/(4s^{2}))^{(d-3)/2},&0<r<2s,\\
0,&r>2s>0\end{cases}

is non-decreasing for each
r\in(0,\infty)
if (and only if)
d\geq 3
.

Observation 3
.

Using

(1.27)

\pi rJ_{\nu}(r)^{2}=1+\sin(2r-\nu\pi)+O((1+r)^{-1/2})

and the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, we get

(1.28)

\lim_{s\to\infty}T_{\nu}f(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)\,dr

for every
f\in L^{1}
(
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
).

Theorem
1.4
is derived by combining these observations:

(1.29)

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{s>0}T_{k+d/2-1}w(s)\underset{\text{Obs.\ \ref{obs:1}}}{=}\sup_{s>0}T_{d/2-1}w(s)\underset{\text{Obs.\ \ref{obs:2}}}{=}\lim_{s\to\infty}T_{d/2-1}w(s)\underset{\text{Obs.\ \ref{obs:3}}}{=}\int_{0}^{\infty}w(r)\,dr.

Now notice that we have

(1.30)

\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d)}(w,\psi)=\sup_{s>0}s^{-1}\psi(s)^{2}T_{d/2-1}w(s),\quad\bm{C}_{\textup{S}}^{(d+1)}(w,\psi)=\sup_{s>0}s^{-1}\psi(s)^{2}T_{d/2-1/2}w(s)

under the assumption of Theorem
1.5
owing to Observation
1
.
Therefore, to prove Theorem
1.5
, it suffices to show that

(1.31)

T_{d/2-1/2}w(s)\leq T_{d/2-1}w(s)

holds.
We prove this inequality (
1.31
) by extending the identity (
1.22
) to non-integer indices.
In order to define
U_{\mu,\nu}
for arbitrary
\mu\in[0,\infty)
and
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)
, we introduce the Gegenbauer function of the second kind.

Definition 3

(Gegenbauer function of the second kind)
.

Let
\mu\in[0,\infty)
and
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)
.
The Gegenbauer function of the second kind
\mathrm{D}^{(\nu)}_{\mu}\colon(1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(1.32)

\mathrm{D}^{(\nu)}_{\mu}(x)\coloneqq\frac{\Gamma(\mu+1)\Gamma(\nu+1/2)}{\pi^{1/2}2^{\mu}\Gamma(\mu+\nu+1)}x^{-(\mu+2\nu)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\mu/2+\nu,(\mu+2\nu+1)/2\\
\mu+\nu+1\end{matrix};1/x^{2}\right).

Now we define
U_{\mu,\nu}
as follows.

Definition 4
.

Let
\mu\in[0,\infty)
and
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)
.
We define an integral transform
U_{\mu,\nu}
by

(1.33)

\displaystyle U_{\mu,\nu}f(s)\coloneqq\int_{r\in(0,\infty)}\mathcal{K}_{\mu,\nu}(r,s)f(r)\,dr,

(1.34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\mu,\nu}(r,s)\coloneqq\dfrac{\pi^{1/2}}{2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1/2)}\times\begin{cases}\phantom{-\sin{(\mu\pi)}}r^{2\nu}(1-r^{2}/(4s^{2}))^{(2\nu-1)/2}\mathrm{C}^{(\nu)}_{\mu}(1-r^{2}/(2s^{2})),&0<r<2s,\\
-\sin{(\mu\pi)}r^{2\nu}(r^{2}/(4s^{2})-1)^{(2\nu-1)/2}\mathrm{D}^{(\nu)}_{\mu}(r^{2}/(2s^{2})-1),&r>2s>0.\end{cases}

Note that
\mathcal{K}_{\mu,\nu}
does not vanish on the region
r>2s>0
if
\mu\not\in\mathbb{N}
, which is slightly unexpected.
Additionally, in order to state our results simply, we introduce the following convention.

Definition 5

(non-negativity and strict positivity of the Hankel transform)
.

Let
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)
and
f\in L^{1}_{2\nu+1,0}
. We define
f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}_{2\nu+1,2\nu+1}
by

(1.35)

f_{\varepsilon}(r)\coloneqq f(r)\exp(-\varepsilon r^{2})

for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
.

•

We say that
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is non-negative
if
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f_{\varepsilon}(r)\geq 0
holds for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
and
r\in(0,\infty)
.

•

We say that
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is strictly positive
if it is non-negative and

(1.36)

\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f_{\varepsilon}(r)>0

holds for almost every
r\in(0,\infty)
.

Now our second main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

The following hold:

(I)

Let
\mu\in[0,\infty)
,
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)
, and
f\in L^{1}_{2\nu+1,0}
.
Then, for every
s\in(0,\infty)
,

(1.37)

T_{\mu+\nu}f(s)=U_{\mu,\nu}\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f(s)

holds.

(II)

Let
\nu\in[1/2,\infty)
and
f\in L^{1}_{2\nu+1,0}
.
Then

(1.38)

(0,\infty)\ni s\longmapsto T_{\nu}f(s)

is non-decreasing if
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is non-negative, and strictly increasing if
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is strictly positive.

(III)

Let
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)
and
f\in L^{1}_{2\nu+1,0}
. Then

(1.39)

(0,\infty)\ni s\longmapsto T_{\nu}f(s)+T_{\nu+1}f(s)

is non-decreasing if
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is non-negative, and strictly increasing if
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is strictly positive.

(IV)

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
,
\nu\in[0,\infty)
, and
f\in L^{1}_{2\nu+1,0}
.
Then, for every
s\in(0,\infty)
,

(1.40)

T_{\mu+\nu}f(s)\leq T_{\nu}f(s)

holds if
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is non-negative, and the inequality is strict if
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is strictly positive.

(V)

Let
\mu\in(0,1]
and
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)
be such that
\mu+2\nu\geq 0
, and let
f\in L^{1}_{2\nu+1,0}
.
Then, for every
s\in(0,\infty)
,

(1.41)

T_{\mu+\nu}f(s)\leq T_{\nu}f(s)

holds if
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is non-negative, and the inequality is strict if
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is strictly positive.

Notice that the inequality (
1.31
) follows by letting
\mu=1/2
in
(V)
, so that Theorem
1.5
holds.

Theorem
1.6
is also useful to obtain monotonicity results.
In particular,
(V)
can be used to prove monotonicity of
\nu\longmapsto T_{\nu}f
by applying it to a function
f
such that
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f
is non-negative for every
\nu
(e.g., the Gaussian).
Such a function is characterized by the
complete monotonicity
.

Definition 6

(complete monotonicity)
.

A function
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
completely monotone
if it is infinitely differentiable and
satisfies

(1.42)

(-1)^{n}f^{(n)}(r)\geq 0

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
, where
f^{(n)}
denotes the
n
-th derivative of
f
.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
6
,
36
,
23
]
)
.

Let
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. We consider the following conditions:

(CM1)

The function
r\longmapsto f(r^{1/2})
is completely monotone.

(CM2)

The function
f
is bounded.

(CM3)

For each
\nu\in[-1/2,\infty)
, there exists a Borel measure
\lambda_{\nu}
on
[0,\infty)
such that

(1.43)

f(r)=\mathcal{H}_{\nu}\lambda_{\nu}(r)\coloneqq r^{-\nu}\int_{0}^{\infty}a^{\nu+1}J_{\nu}(ar)\,d\lambda_{\nu}(a)

holds for every
r\in(0,\infty)
.

(CM4)

There exists a Borel measure
\lambda
on
[0,\infty)
such that

(1.44)

f(r)=\int_{a\in[0,\infty)}e^{-a^{2}r^{2}/2}\,d\lambda(a)

holds for every
r\in(0,\infty)
.

Then we have

(1.45)

\displaystyle\ref{item:completely monotone}\iff\ref{item:Gaussian mixture}\implies\ref{item:Hankel transform of measure for every order},

(1.46)

\displaystyle\ref{item:bounded}\land\ref{item:Hankel transform of measure for every order}\implies\ref{item:Gaussian mixture}.

The implications
\ref{item:completely monotone}\implies\ref{item:Gaussian mixture}
and
\ref{item:bounded}\land\ref{item:Hankel transform of measure for every order}\implies\ref{item:Gaussian mixture}
are known as the
Bernstein 1929
–
Widder 1931
theorem (
[
6
, §14]
,
[
36
, Theorem 8]
) and the
Schoenberg 1938
theorem (
[
23
, Theorem 2]
), respectively. The remaining
\ref{item:Gaussian mixture}\implies\ref{item:completely monotone}\land\ref{item:Hankel transform of measure for every order}
can be shown by a simple calculation.
Taking into account Theorem
1.7
, it is natural to consider
T_{\nu}f
for a function
f
such that
r\mapsto f(r^{1/2})
is completely monotone.
Our last main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\nu_{0}\in(-1/2,\infty)
, and let
f\in L^{1}_{2\nu_{0}+1,0}
be such that

(1.47)

r\longmapsto f(r^{1/2})

is completely monotone and not identically zero.
Then the following hold:

(1)

For every
\nu\in[\nu_{0},\infty)
,

(1.48)

s\longmapsto s^{-\nu-1/2}T_{\nu}f(s^{1/2})

is completely monotone on
(0,\infty)
.

(2)

For each fixed
\nu\in[1/2,\infty)\cap[\nu_{0},\infty)
,

(1.49)

s\longmapsto T_{\nu}f(s)

is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
.

(3)

For each fixed
\nu\in(-1/2,\infty)\cap[\nu_{0},\infty)
,

(1.50)

s\longmapsto T_{\nu}f(s)+T_{\nu+1}f(s)

is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
.

(4)

For each fixed
\nu\in(-1/2,0)\cap[\nu_{0},\infty)
and
s\in(0,\infty)
,

(1.51)

T_{-\nu}f(s)<T_{\nu}f(s)

holds.

(5)

For each fixed
s\in(0,\infty)
,

(1.52)

\nu\longmapsto T_{\nu}f(s)

is strictly decreasing on
[0,\infty)\cap[\nu_{0},\infty)
.

We note that
(1)
also can be regarded as a sufficient condition for the analyticity of
T_{\nu}f
, since completely monotone functions are analytic owing to
Bernstein 1914
’s little theorem (
[
6
, §1]
).
In other words, we have:

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
\nu_{0}\in(-1/2,\infty)
, and
f\in L^{1}_{2\nu_{0}+1,0}
be such that

(1.53)

r\longmapsto f(r^{1/2})

is completely monotone.
Then
T_{\nu}f
is analytic on
(0,\infty)
for every
\nu\in[\nu_{0},\infty)
.

As
Bez and Sugimoto 2017
pointed out, the analyticity of
T_{\nu}f
can be used to show the non-existence of extremisers for the smoothing estimate (
1.4
) (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
for details).
They established the following sufficient condition for the analyticity, and showed that
T_{k/2}f
is analytic when
f(r)=(1+r^{2})^{-a/2}
for every

Remark 1
.

Our proof is substantially different from that of
Bez et al. 2015
for the case
(\mu,\nu)=(k,d/2-1)
.
Roughly speaking, their proof is as follows.
Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be an even Schwartz function, and let
Y_{k,d}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
be a harmonic homogeneous polynomial of
k
-degree in
d
-variables such that
\lVert Y_{k,d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}=1
.
We consider the left-hand side of the smoothing estimate (
1.4
) with the initial data
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
given by

(2.26)

\widehat{u_{0}}(r\theta)=r^{-(d-1)/2}\varphi(r)Y_{k,d}(\theta),\quad(r,\theta)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
Walther 2002
showed that

(2.27)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}w(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\psi(\lvert\nabla\rvert)e^{it\Delta}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-1}\psi(r)^{2}T_{k+d/2-1}w(r)\lvert\varphi(r)\rvert^{2}\,dr

holds using some identities involving the Fourier transform and the Bessel functions.
On the other hand,
Bez et al. 2015
calculated the same integral using a different method based on the so-called Funk–Hecke theorem, and showed that

(2.28)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}w(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\psi(\lvert\nabla\rvert)e^{it\Delta}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-1}\psi(r)^{2}U_{k,d/2-1}\mathcal{H}_{d/2-1}w(r)\lvert\varphi(r)\rvert^{2}\,dr

holds. Comparing these, we conclude that

(2.29)

T_{k+d/2-1}w=U_{k,d/2-1}\mathcal{H}_{d/2-1}w

holds.

Proposition 4.4

4.4
.

It is well known that

(4.20)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}J_{\nu}(r)

\displaystyle=-J_{\nu+1}(r)+\frac{\nu}{r}J_{\nu}(r),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}J_{\nu+1}(r)

[

\displaystyle=(2\nu+1)J_{\nu}(r)^{2}-2rJ_{\nu}(r)J_{\nu+1}(r)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\left(rJ_{\nu+1}(r)^{2}\right)

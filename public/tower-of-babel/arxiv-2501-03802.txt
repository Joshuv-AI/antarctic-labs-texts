Quasi-optimal cyclic orbit codes
Chiara Castello, Heide Gluesing-Luerssen, Olga Polverino, and Ferdinando Zullo

Abstract
. We focus on two aspects of cyclic orbit codes: invariants under equivalence and quasi-optimality. Regarding the first aspect, we establish a connection between the codewords of a cyclic orbit code and a certain linear set on the projective line. This allows us to derive new bounds on the parameters of the code. In the second part, we study a particular family of (quasi-)optimal cyclic orbit codes and derive a general existence theorem for quasi-optimal codes in even-dimensional vector spaces over finite fields of any characteristic. Finally, for our particular code family we describe the automorphism groups under the general linear group and a suitable Galois group.



1.
Introduction

A network channel can be represented as a directed acyclic graph with three types of vertices: sources (vertices without incoming edges), sinks (vertices without outgoing edges) and inner nodes (vertices with both incoming and outgoing edges).
When inner nodes combine incoming information linearly, this is named
linear network coding
. If the network’s topology is unknown in advance, it is called
random network coding
.
Network coding was developed in the seminal paper
[
1
]

to enhance throughput in multicast sessions. Unlike traditional routing, where intermediate nodes merely store and forward data, network coding enables these nodes to actively combine and encode the data before transmission.
This paradigm shift has driven interest in network coding, with applications in areas such as wireless communications and cloud computing; see e.g.
[
2
]
.
In
[
21
]
, the authors laid the mathematical foundation for random linear network coding, highlighting the effectiveness of subspace codes for reliable transmissions. These codes leverage the model’s ability to transmit linear combinations of vectors while preserving their span. More precisely, codewords are subspaces of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
, for some field
\mathbb{F}
, and a code is a collection of subspaces equipped with the subspace distance.
Since then, intensive research has focused on the theory of subspace codes, aiming to establish bounds, develop constructions, and design efficient decoding algorithms; see
[
19
]
.

In this paper we focus on cyclic orbit codes, introduced in
[
12
]
, as the subspace code analogue of classical linear codes in the Hamming metric.
The set of all
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
is called the
Grassmannian
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and denoted by
{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
.
A
constant dimension subspace code
is a subset
\mathcal{C}
of
{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
endowed with the
subspace distance

d(U,V)=2(k-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap V))

for
U,V\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
.
As usual, the
minimum distance
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is defined as
d({\mathcal{C}})=\min\{d(U,V)\colon\!\!

U,V\in{\mathcal{C}},\,U\neq V\}
.
Clearly, it is upper bounded by
2k
.
A subspace code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
is said to be
cyclic
if
\alpha V\in\mathcal{C}

for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}
and
V\in\mathcal{C}
.
For
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
the
orbit
of
U
is the set
\mathrm{Orb}(U)=\{\alpha U:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}
.
Clearly,
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
is a cyclic subspace code, called a
cyclic orbit code
(or a single-orbit or one-orbit code
[
4
,
7
,
8
]
).
The size of a cyclic orbit code is upper bounded by
(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
and codes with this size are called
full-length orbit codes
.

It is not hard to see that for
k>2
a full-length orbit code cannot attain distance
2k
(see also
[
16
]
and
[
27
]
).
For this reason we call a full-length orbit code
optimal
if its distance is
2(k-1)
, and
quasi-optimal
if it is
2(k-2)
.
In
[
30
]
, the authors conjectured the existence of optimal full-length orbit codes
for all positive integers
n
and
2<k\leq n/2
and any prime power
q
.
A lot of progress has been made on establishing this conjecture, see e.g.
[
3
,
23
,
26
,
25
]
.
However, the case
q=2
and
k=n/2
remains largely open and in particular, the conjecture is not true for
(n,k,q)=(8,4,2)
; see
[
13
]
.
For further details we refer to Remarks
4.3
and
6.1
.
On the other hand, not much is known about quasi-optimal full-length orbit codes.

In order to achieve a finer distinction between cyclic orbit codes than the minimum distance, the notion of distance distribution has been introduced in
[
14
]
.
Therein, it has been shown that the distance distribution of an optimal full-length orbit code only depends on the parameters
(n,k,q)
, whereas this is not the case for quasi-optimal full-length orbit codes.
In the latter case, the distance distribution of
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
depends on yet another parameter, namely the number of fractions
\overline{uv^{-1}}\in{\mathbb{P}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
for
u,v\in U\setminus\{0\}
, denoted by
f_{U}
.

In this paper, we establish a relation between
f_{U}
and the size of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set
L_{U\times U}
, the set of points in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
defined by vectors in
U\times U
.
With the aid of known lower bounds on the size of a linear set (see
[
11
]
and
[
10
]
) we are able to improve upon previous bounds on
f_{U}
provided in
[
14
]
(under some mild constraints on the distance distribution of the code); see
Corollary

5.10
and
Theorem

5.14
.
This allows us to narrow down the possible distance distributions of quasi-optimal full-length orbit codes; see
Corollary

5.15
.
All of this provides answers to some questions posed in
[
14
, Section 6]
.
In particular, for
k=3
, exactly three different distance distributions arise.

Another contribution of this paper is a detailed study of the orbit codes
{\mathcal{O}}_{s,\gamma}=\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U_{s,\gamma})
, where
U_{s,\gamma}=\{u+u^{q^{s}}\gamma\colon u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}\}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
, where
n=2k,\,s\in[k]
such that
\gcd(s,k)=1
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}(\gamma)
.
These codes have been introduced in
[
25
]
, and the authors proved that if
\gamma^{(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)}\neq 1
then
{\mathcal{O}}_{s,\gamma}
is an optimal
full-length orbit code.
Based on this, we show that
{\mathcal{O}}_{s,\gamma}
is always full-length and either optimal or quasi-optimal, and the distinction depends only on the field norm
of
\gamma
; see
Proposition

6.6
.
It is not hard to determine the number of optimal and quasi-optimal orbit codes
{\mathcal{O}}_{s,\gamma}
; see
Proposition

8.7
.
All of this allows us to establish in
Theorem

6.7
the existence of quasi-optimal full-length orbit codes for any
q\geq 2,\,n
even, and

3\leq k\leq n/2
.

As shown in
[
14
]
, if
n
is even, then the distance distribution of a quasi-optimal full-length orbit code
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
depends on whether or
not
U
contains a cyclic shift of the subfield
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
(thus the same is true for the parameter
f_{U}
).
In
Theorem

6.8
we characterize the subspaces
U_{s,\gamma}
containing such a cyclic shift and in
Proposition

8.8
we determine the
number of the resulting orbit codes
{\mathcal{O}}_{s,\gamma}
.

Finally, we study the codes
{\mathcal{O}}_{s,\gamma}
up to equivalence.
The study of equivalence for subspace codes has been initiated in
[
29
]
, and in
[
15
]
special attention has been paid to the
case of cyclic orbit codes.
We consider two types of equivalence:
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-linear isometries and

{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-Frobenius isometries (where
q=p^{h}
and
t
a divisor of
h
); see
Definition

7.3
for details.
In the former case the codes are related via an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-isomorphism on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, while in the latter they are related via an isomorphism from the
Galois group
\mbox{$\text{Gal}$}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\mid{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}})
.
With the aid of a result in
[
15
]
, we prove in
Theorem

7.6
that if
U_{1},\,U_{2}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
are generic subspaces, then
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U_{1})
and

\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U_{2})
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-linearly isometric if and only if they are
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-Frobenius isometric.
This allows us to use
[
6
]
for determining the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-automorphism group of the codes
{\mathcal{O}}_{s,\gamma}
; see
Proposition

8.4
.
Finally, in
Theorem

8.11
we provide, for the codes
{\mathcal{O}}_{s,\gamma}
, a description of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Frobenius-automorphism groups
and thus of the sizes of the orbits of these codes under the action of the Galois group.
Examples illustrate our findings.

2.
Linear sets

In this paper, we will make use of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear sets in the projective line and therefore start with recalling some useful definitions and results regarding linear sets in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
. We refer to
[
22
]
and
[
24
]
for comprehensive references on linear sets in projective spaces of any finite dimension.
Let
V
be a
2
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-vector space and let
\Lambda=\mathrm{PG}(V,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})=\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
.
Let
W\neq\{\mathbf{0}\}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
V
. The pair
(W,L_{W})
where

L_{W}=\{\langle\mathbf{u}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon\mathbf{u}\in W\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}\},

is said to be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear set
of
W
-rank
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(W)
.
Note that the set
L_{W}\subseteq\Lambda
does not uniquely determine the dimension of the subspace
W
: we may have
L_{W}=L_{U}

and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(W)\neq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U)
. We denote the
W
-rank of the linear set
L_{W}
by
W\mbox{-{\rm Rank}}(L_{W})
.

Another important notion is the following. Consider an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set
L_{W}
of
W
-rank
k
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
.
The
W
-
weight
of a point
P=\langle\mathbf{v}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\in\Lambda
is defined as

w_{W}(P):=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(W\cap\langle\mathbf{v}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})
(of course, this dimension depends on the choice of the
ground field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
; we will omit this dependence in our notation).
For
i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
denote by
N_{i}
the number of points of
\Lambda
having
W
-weight
i
.
We call
(N_{0},\ldots,N_{k})
the
W
-
weight distribution
of
L_{W}
.
Note that if
\mathrm{Rank}(L_{W})>n
, then
N_{0}=0
and hence
L_{W}=\Lambda
.
We say that
L_{W}
has
W
-
weight spectrum

(i_{1},\ldots,i_{t})

with
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{t}\leq k
if the
W
-weight of every point
P\in L_{W}
is in
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{t}\}

and each of these integers
i_{j}
occurs as the weight of at least one point of
L_{W}
.

Naturally, the size of the linear set is given by

|L_{W}|=N_{1}+\ldots+N_{k},

and
N_{0}=q^{n}+1-|L_{W}|
.
Counting the pairs
(P,\mathbf{w})
, where
P
is a point of
L_{W}
and
\mathbf{w}\in W
such that
P=\langle\mathbf{w}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
, we obtain

(1)

N_{1}(q-1)+N_{2}(q^{2}-1)+\ldots+N_{k}(q^{k}-1)=q^{k}-1,

and thus
N_{1}+N_{2}(q+1)+\ldots+N_{k}(q^{k-1}+\ldots+q+1)=q^{k-1}+\ldots+q+1
.
From the previous equalities we get

(2)

|L_{W}|\leq\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}.

Moreover, by the Grassmann formula, we also get

w_{W}(P)+w_{W}(Q)\leq W\mbox{-{\rm Rank}}(L_{W}),

for any
P,Q\in\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
with
P\neq Q
. If there exist two distinct points
P,Q\in L_{W}
such that
w_{W}(P)+w_{W}(Q)=W\mbox{-{\rm Rank}}(L_{W}),
then we say that
L_{W}
is a linear set with points of
complementary weights
.
The following result has been proved in
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
(and for
k=n
in
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set
L_{W}
of
W
-rank
k\leq n
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
with at least one point of
W
-weight one has at least
q^{k-1}+1
points.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that Theorem
2.1
also holds when
k=n+1
.
Indeed, in that case we have
L_{W}=\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
and so
|L_{W}|=q^{n}+1=q^{k-1}+1
.

In this paper, we will mainly consider
V=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and linear sets of the form

L_{U\times U}=\{\langle(u_{1},u_{2})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon u_{1},u_{2}\in U,\,\,(u_{1},u_{2})\neq(0,0)\}

for some
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
U
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
Thus
(U\!\!\times\!\!U)\mbox{-{\rm Rank}}(L_{U\times U})=2k
.
Note that the projective space is given by

\Lambda=\mathrm{PG}(V,q^{n})=\{\mbox{$\langle{(1,\alpha)}\rangle$}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\}\cup\{\mbox{$\langle{(0,1)}\rangle$}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\}
.
Now we compute

(3)

w_{U\times U}(\mbox{$\langle{(1,0)}\rangle$}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\times\{0\})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U)=k=w_{U\times U}(\mbox{$\langle{(0,1)}\rangle$}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}).

Furthermore, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
we have

(4)

(U\times U)\cap\mbox{$\langle{(1,\alpha)}\rangle$}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}=\{(u,\alpha u)\colon u\in U\cap\alpha^{-1}U\}

and thus

(5)

w_{U\times U}(\mbox{$\langle{(1,\alpha)}\rangle$}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha^{-1}U)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)\leq k.

In particular,
L_{U\times U}
is a linear set with two points of complementary weights.
Moreover, if
\alpha
is in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
w_{U\times U}(\langle(1,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})=k
, and therefore

N_{k}\geq q+1.

Furthermore, we have
N_{k}=q+1
iff
\alpha U\neq U
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
This last condition means that
U
is not closed under multiplication by any scalars outside
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
We cast the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
W
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
We call
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
the
maximum field of linearity
of
W
if it is the largest subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
W
is
closed under multiplication by scalars from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
.

We have the following upper bound for the size of
L_{U\times U}
, which improves upon (
2
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
U
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
of dimension
k\leq n
and assume that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the maximum field of linearity of
U
.
Let
e=\min\{w_{U\times U}(P)\colon P\in L_{U\times U}\}
.
Then

|L_{U\times U}|\leq\frac{(q^{k}-1)(q^{k}+1)}{q^{e}-1}-(q+1)\frac{(q^{k}-q^{e})}{q^{e}-1}.

Proof.

From (
5
) we know already that the maximum weight of the points of
L_{U\times U}
is
k
.
Furthermore, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
we have

w_{U\times U}(\langle(1,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})=k\Longleftrightarrow\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)=k\Longleftrightarrow\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
where the last step follows from the fact that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the maximum field of linearity of
U
.
Together with (
3
) we arrive at
N_{k}=q+1
.
Since
N_{1}=N_{2}=\ldots=N_{e-1}=0
, we obtain from (
1
)

N_{e}(q^{e}-1)+\ldots+N_{k-1}(q^{k-1}-1)+(q+1)(q^{k}-1)=q^{2k}-1.

Using the previous equality together with
q^{i}-1\geq q^{e}-1
for any
i\in\{e,\ldots,k-1\}
and the identity
|L_{U\times U}|=N_{e}+\ldots+N_{k-1}+q+1
,
we get

(|L_{U\times U}|-(q+1))(q^{e}-1)+(q+1)(q^{k}-1)\leq q^{2k}-1

and therefore

|L_{U\times U}|\leq\frac{(q^{k}-1)(q^{k}+1)}{q^{e}-1}-(q+1)\frac{(q^{k}-q^{e})}{q^{e}-1}.\qed

3.
Cyclic orbit codes

In this short section we revisit some basic notions about cyclic orbit codes.
Recall the notation
{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
for the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
30
, Def. 19]
,
[
13
, Def. 2.1]

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
. The
cyclic orbit code generated by

U
is defined as

\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)=\{\alpha U\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}
, and
\mathrm{Stab}(U)=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\colon\alpha U=U\}
is called
the
stabilizer
of
U
.
It is thus the multiplicative group of the maximum field of linearity; see
Definition

2.3
.
Clearly, a subfield
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
is the maximum field of linearity of
U
if and only if

|\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)|=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q^{t}-1}
.
In the case where
t=1
, we call
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
a
full-length orbit code
.

Note that the minimum distance of
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
reads as

d(\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U))=2k-2\max\{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\setminus\mathrm{Stab}(U)\}.

For the next definition, we need the following notation.
Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
be a subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
The projective space over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
considered as an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
-vector space is denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
.
Its points are of the form
\alpha{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
.
If
t=1
, we will just write
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
and use
\overline{\alpha}:=\alpha{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
for its points.
Suppose
\mathrm{Stab}(U)={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}^{*}
.
Then
\alpha U=\beta U\Longleftrightarrow\alpha\beta^{-1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
, and therefore we have
a one-to-one correspondence between the points of
\mathbb{P}_{t}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})

and the elements of
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
.

For the following definition, we refer to
[
14
, Definition 2.5, Definition 2.6]
.
Note that the defined notions only depend on
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
and not on its generating subspace
U
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
. Define

\omega_{2i}=|\{\alpha U\in\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*},\,d(U,\alpha U)=2i\}|

for
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. We call
(\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{2k})

distance distribution
of
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
.
Furthermore, the minimum distance is
d(\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U))=2(k-\ell)
, where

\ell=\max\{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\setminus\mathrm{Stab}(U)\}.

We call
\ell
the
maximum intersection dimension
of
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
.
For
i\in\{0,\ldots,\ell\}
we define
\lambda_{i}=|\mathcal{L}_{i}|
, where

\mathcal{L}_{i}=\{\overline{\alpha}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})\colon\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)=i\},

and call
(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
the
intersection distribution
of
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
.
Note that if
\text{Stab}(U)={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}^{*}
, then
\lambda_{i}=(q^{t}-1)/(q-1)\omega_{2(k-i)}
for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,\ell\}
.

One can easily see that a full-length orbit code generated by a
k
-dimensional subspace has distance at most
2k-2
; see also

[
14
, Remark 2.7]
.

Definition 3.3

(
see also
[
14
, Definition 2.8]
)
.

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
and suppose that
{\mathcal{O}}=\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
is a full-length orbit code.
Then
{\mathcal{O}}
is called an
optimal full-length orbit code
if
d({\mathcal{O}})=2k-2
, and it is
a
quasi-optimal full-length orbit code
if
d({\mathcal{O}})=2k-4
.

Recall that a subspace
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
is a
Sidon space
if for all
a,b,c,d\in U\setminus\{0\}
the identity
ab=cd
implies
\{a{\mathbb{F}}_{q},b{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}=\{c{\mathbb{F}}_{q},d{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}
.
The interest in Sidon spaces increased especially after
[
25
]
in which the authors proved the following equivalence between optimal full-length orbit codes and Sidon spaces.

Theorem 3.4
.

[
25
, Lemma 34]

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
. Then
U
is a Sidon space if and only if
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
is an optimal full-length orbit code.

In the next section we will see, via a duality argument, that we can always assume
k\leq n/2
for the dimension
k
of a generator of an orbit code.

4.
A bound on
k
for cyclic orbit codes with prescribed minimum distance

In this section, we provide an upper bound on the dimension
k
of the subspace
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
depending on the minimum distance
of
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
.
Moreover, we recall a result from
[
7
]
classifying the cyclic orbit codes generated by
3
-dimensional spaces.

We start with the definition of the orthogonal complement of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
Recall the trace function from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, defined as

\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}:a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\longmapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a^{q^{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

It is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map and gives rise to a nondegenerate, symmetric, bilinear form

\sigma\colon(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\longmapsto\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(ab).

For any subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
the orthogonal complement
U^{\perp}
is defined as

U^{\perp}=\{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\colon\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(ab)=0\text{ for all }b\in U\}.

As for any nondegenerate, symmetric, bilinear form we have
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U^{\perp})=n-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U)
and

(U+W)^{\perp}=U^{\perp}\cap W^{\perp}
.
Furthermore, the following can easily be verified.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
U,W
be
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
. Then

(1)

(\alpha U)^{\perp}=\alpha^{-1}U^{\perp}
;

(2)

d(U^{\perp},W^{\perp})=d(U,W)
;

(3)

d(U^{\perp},\alpha^{-1}U^{\perp})=d(U,\alpha U)
.

As a consequence,
(\mathrm{Orb}(U))^{\perp}=\mathrm{Orb}(U^{\perp})
, and the cyclic orbit codes
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
and
\mathrm{Orb}(U^{\perp})
have the same length and distance distribution.

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
. If
d(\mathrm{Orb}(U))=2k-4
, then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)\leq 2,

for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
U\neq\alpha U
.
This immediately implies that

n\geq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U+\alpha U)=2k-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)\geq 2k-2,

that is

k\leq\frac{n+2}{2}.

We can improve the bound as follows.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
, and suppose that
d(\mathrm{Orb}(U))=2(k-\ell)>2
.
Then

k\leq\frac{n+\ell}{2}.

Furthermore, if
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
is a full-length orbit code, then
k<\frac{n+\ell}{2}
.
In this case we have in particular

(a)

If
\ell=1
then
k\leq\frac{n}{2}
.

(b)

If
\ell=2
and
n
is odd, then
k\leq\frac{n+1}{2}
. Moreover,
k=\frac{n+1}{2}\,

\Longleftrightarrow U^{\perp}
is a Sidon space.

Note that in Case (a)
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
is an optimal full-length orbit code (and
U
is a Sidon space), while in Case (b) it is a quasi-optimal full-length orbit code.

Proof.

d(\mathrm{Orb}(U))=2(k-\ell)
implies
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)\leq\ell
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
U\neq\alpha U
.
This leads to
n\geq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U+\alpha U)=2k-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)\geq 2k-\ell
,
and thus

k\leq\frac{n+\ell}{2}.

Suppose now that
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
is a full-length orbit code and
k=\frac{n+\ell}{2}
. Then
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U^{\perp}):=\hat{k}=\frac{n-\ell}{2}
.
By
Proposition

4.1

d(\mathrm{Orb}(U^{\perp}))=d(\mathrm{Orb}(U))=2(k-\ell)=n-\ell=2\hat{k}
.
Thus
\mathrm{Orb}(U^{\perp})
is a spread.
In particular,
\hat{k}
divides
n
and
U^{\perp}
is a multiplicative coset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{\hat{k}}}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
; see

[
14
, Remark 2.7]
.
But this means that
|\mathrm{Orb}(U)|=|\mathrm{Orb}(U^{\perp})|=(q^{n}-1)/(q^{\hat{k}}-1)
.
Thus, if
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
is a full-length orbit code, then
\hat{k}=1,\,k=n-1
, and
\ell=n-2
.
Hence
d(\mathrm{Orb}(U))=2(k-\ell)=2
, contradicting our assumption.
All of this shows that
k<\frac{n+\ell}{2}
.

Let us now turn to the special cases (a) and (b) assuming that
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
is a full-lenght orbit code.
(a) is clear.
(b) We know already that
k\leq\frac{n+1}{2}
. Next,
k=\frac{n+1}{2}\Longleftrightarrow\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U^{\perp})=\hat{k}=\frac{n-1}{2}
and

2(k-2)=n-3=2\hat{k}-2
.
Hence
d(\mathrm{Orb}(U))=2(k-2)
is equivalent to
\mathrm{Orb}(U^{\perp})
being an optimal full-length orbit code.
The latter is equivalent to
U^{\perp}
being a Sidon space thanks to
Theorem

3.4
.
∎

Let us briefly discuss the existence of optimal and quasi-optimal full-length orbit codes of dimension
n/2
and
(n-1)/2
.

Remark 4.3
.

(a)

Let

5.
Fundamental properties of the intersection distribution

In
[
14
]
the intersection distribution of a quasi-optimal cyclic orbit code
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
has been described.
It has been shown that the distribution depends (a) on the number of fractions,
f_{U}
, that can be formed by the elements in
U
,
and (b) on the fact whether or not
U
contains a cyclic shift of the subfield
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
(if
n
is even); see Equation (
9
) and
Theorem

5.7
.
Making use of the linear set
L_{U\times U}
, we will derive new bounds for
f_{U}
, which in turn will narrow down the possible intersection distributions
of quasi-optimal full-length orbit codes.

Recall the notation
\overline{\alpha}=\alpha{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
for the points in
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
.

Definition 5.1
.

[
14
, Definition 3.1]

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
and
d(\mathrm{Orb}(U))=2k-2\ell
. We define

(1)

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(U)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{L}_{i}
, where
{\mathcal{L}}_{i}
is as in
Definition

3.2
;

(2)

\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(U)=\{\overline{\alpha}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})\colon\alpha\in\mathrm{Stab}(U)\}
;

(3)

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(U)=\{(\overline{u},\overline{v})\colon u,v\in U\setminus\{0\},\overline{u}\neq\overline{v}\}
;

(4)

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(U)=\{\overline{uv^{-1}}\colon u,v\in U\setminus\{0\}\}
.
We call
\mathcal{F}
the
set of fractions
of
U
and set
f_{U}=|{\mathcal{F}}|
.

The above parameters are related as follows. Recall the intersection distribution
(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
from
Definition

3.2
.
Thus,
\lambda_{i}=|{\mathcal{L}}_{i}|
.

Proposition 5.2
.

[
14
, Corollary 3.3]

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
be such that
\mathrm{Stab}(U)={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}^{*}
and let
d(\mathrm{Orb}(U))=2k-2\ell
.
Set
s=|\mathcal{S}|
,
Q=|\mathcal{M}|
, thus
Q=(q^{k}-1)(q^{k}-q)/(q-1)^{2}
.
Then

f_{U}=s+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}\ \text{ and }\ Q=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{q^{i}-1}{q-1}\lambda_{i}+\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}(s-1).

If
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
is a full-length orbit, we have
s=1
and

f_{U}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}\ \text{ and }\ Q=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{q^{i}-1}{q-1}\lambda_{i}.

The intersection distribution of cyclic orbit codes has also been studied in
[
4
]
.
In particular, the following theorem has been proven.

Theorem 5.3
.

[
4
, Theorems 4 and 8]

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
be a subspace with stabilizer
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.

(1)

If
n
is an odd number, then
\lambda_{i}
is a multiple of
q(q+1)
for all
i=0,\dots,k-1
;

(2)

If
n
is even and
U
contains
\frac{q^{2m}-1}{q^{2}-1}
distinct shifts of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
(where
m\geq 0
), then

\lambda_{2m}=q+sq(q+1)

for some non negative
s
. Moreover,
\lambda_{i}
is multiple of
q(q+1)
for
i=0,\ldots,k-1,i\neq 2m
.

Together with Proposition
5.2
we obtain the following consequence.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
be a subspace with stabilizer
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.

(1)

If
n
is an odd number, then
f_{U}-1
is a multiple of
q(q+1)
;

(2)

If
n
is even and
U
contains
\frac{q^{2m}-1}{q^{2}-1}
distinct shifts of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
, then
f_{U}-1-q
is a multiple of
q(q+1)
.

Moreover, we have the following lower and upper bound on
f_{U}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

[
14
, Proposition 3.4]

With the same notation as in
Proposition

5.2
we have

\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}\leq f_{U}\leq Q+1.

Furthermore,

(1)

f_{U}=(q^{k}-1)/(q-1)
if and only if
U=a{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}
, for some
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{k}}
.

(2)

f_{U}=Q+1
if and only if
U
is a Sidon space.

In the case where
U
is a Sidon space, or equivalently
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
is an optimal full-length code, the intersection distribution is completely known and only depends on the parameters
q,n,k
.

Theorem 5.6
.

[
14
, Theorem 3.7]

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
be a Sidon space. Then the optimal full-length orbit
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
has intersection distribution
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1})
, where

\lambda_{1}=Q=\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}\frac{q^{k}-q}{q-1}=f_{U}-1\ \text{ and }\ \lambda_{0}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-\lambda_{1}-1.

One can check straightforwardly that this is compatible with the information given about
\lambda_{0}
and
\lambda_{1}
in
Theorem

5.3
.
For quasi-optimal codes the distance distribution does not just depend on
q,n,k
.

Theorem 5.7
.

[
14
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}(k,n)
generate a quasi-optimal full-length orbit. One of the following cases occurs:

(1)

U
contains a cyclic shift of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
(hence
n
is even). In this case
U
has intersection distribution
(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
where

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=q+rq(q+1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=Q-(q+1)\lambda_{2}=\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}\frac{q^{k}-q}{q-1}-(q+1)(q+rq(q+1)),

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})|-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-1=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}+q^{2}(1+r(q+1))-Q-1.

for some
r\geq 0
.

(2)

U
does not contain a cyclic shift of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
. In this case
U
has intersection distribution
(\lambda_{0}^{\prime},\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime})
where

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=r^{\prime}q(q+1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=Q-(q+1)\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}\frac{q^{k}-q}{q-1}-r^{\prime}q(q+1)^{2},

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})|-\lambda_{1}^{\prime}-\lambda_{2}^{\prime}-1=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}+r^{\prime}q^{2}(q+1)-Q-1.

for some
r^{\prime}\geq 1.

Remark 5.8
.

Note that if
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
and
\mathrm{Orb}(V)
are quasi-optimal full-length codes where
U
belongs to Family (1) of Theorem
5.7
and
V
belongs to Family (2), then
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
and
\mathrm{Orb}(V)
have different distance distributions because
\lambda_{2}\neq\lambda_{2}^{\prime}
(regardless of

). In particular, the orbit codes are not isometric in the sense of

.

5.1.
New bounds on
f_{U}

In this subsection, we point out a relation between the distance distribution of
\mathrm{Orb}(U)
and the points of
L_{U\times U}
. This will allow us to prove better bounds on
f_{U}=|{\mathcal{F}}|
; see
Definition

5.1
.
Recall from Section
2
the
(U\!\!\times\!\!U)
-weight distribution
(N_{0},\ldots,N_{2k})
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
,
\mathcal{O}=\mathrm{Orb}(U)
, and
\mathrm{Stab}(U)={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}^{*}
. Let
L_{U\times U}
be the linear set defined by
U\times U
.
Then
N_{i}=0
for
i>k
,
N_{k}=q^{t}+1
and

\omega_{2i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&\text{if }i=0,\\
\frac{N_{k-i}}{q^{t}-1},&\text{if }i=1,\ldots,k\end{array}\right.\quad\text{ and }\quad\lambda_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{N_{i}}{q-1},&\text{if }i=0,\ldots,k-1,\\
\frac{q^{t}-1}{q-1},&\text{if }i=k.\end{array}\right.

Moreover,

f_{U}=\frac{|L_{U\times U}|-2}{q-1}.

Proof.

We know from (
3
) and (
5
) that
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
and
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
have weight
k
and

w_{U\times U}(\langle(1,\alpha)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})

=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
.
This shows immediately that
N_{k}=|\mathrm{Stab}(U)|+2=q^{t}+1
.
Using that
\omega_{2i}=|\left\{\alpha U\colon\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)=k-i\right\}|
, we obtain

N_{k-i}=|\mathrm{Stab}(U)|\omega_{2i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
.
This proves the first part.
The statement about
\lambda_{i}
follows immediately from the relation between
\lambda_{i}
and
\omega_{2(k-i)}
as described in

Definition

3.2
.
Finally, note that we have a well-defined surjective map
L_{U\times U}\setminus\{\langle(1,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}},\langle(0,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}
given by

\mbox{$\langle{(u_{1},u_{2})}\rangle$}\longmapsto\overline{u_{1}u_{2}^{-1}}
. Its fibers have size
q-1
and thus
we arrive at
f_{U}=(|L_{U\times U}|-2)/(q-1)
, as stated.
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
U\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
with
n\geq 2k-1
.
If
\omega_{2(k-1)}\neq 0
, then

f_{U}\geq\frac{q^{2k-1}-1}{q-1}.

Proof.

Since
\omega_{2(k-1)}\neq 0
,
Proposition

5.9
implies
N_{1}>0
, i.e.
L_{U\times U}
has at least one point of weight one. As a consequence, by Theorem
2.1
and Remark
2.2
the size of
L_{U\times U}
is at least
q^{2k-1}+1
and so the assertion follows from
Proposition

5.9
.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

. Indeed, consider

Theorem 5.12
.

(see
[
10

Let

be an

-linear set of

.

Corollary 5.13
.

Let
n\leq q
and
L_{W}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set of
W
-rank
h
in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})=\mathrm{PG}(V,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
where

and
|L_{W}|>1
.
Let
e=\min\{w_{W}(P)\colon P\in L_{W}\}
. Then there exists an integer

such that

and

|L_{W}|\geq q^{h-t}+1.

Furthermore, if
e=1
or

is a prime number, then
t=1
.

Proof.

If
e=1
, Theorem
2.1
implies the assertion for
t=1
.
Thus, let
e>1
.
Then by Theorem
5.12
there exists an integer
t_{1}\in\{e,\ldots,n\}
such that
t_{1}\mid n
and
L_{W}=L_{V_{1}}
where

.

i) Suppose
t_{1}=n
.
Then
V_{1}=\mbox{$\langle{W}\rangle$}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
and

.
If
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}(V_{1})=1

, contradicting the assumption that

.

, a contradiction to

.

ii) Thus
t_{1}<n
.
Note that

if

Proof.

By

\lambda_{2}=\epsilon q+rq(q+1)\ \text{ for some }\ r\geq 1-\epsilon.

Furthermore, from

q\lambda_{2}=Q-(f_{U}-1)\ \text{ and }\ q\lambda_{1}=(q+1)(f_{U}-1)-Q,\ \text{ where }\ Q=\frac{(q^{k}-1)(q^{k}-q)}{(q-1)^{2}}.

(a) Let

) and (

7.
Semilinear Isometries and Frobenius Isometries

In this short section we introduce and discuss the notions of semilinear and Frobenius isometries.
Throughout we consider
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, where
q=p^{h}
with
p
prime.
Note that if
t
is a divisor of
hn
, then
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-vector space of dimension
hn/t
.
If
t
is a divisor of
h
, then
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
is a subfield of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and a
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
is an

kh/t
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.
Thus, for the Grassmannians we have the containment

{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{p^{t}}(kh/t,nh/t).

Definition 7.1
.

Let
t
be a divisor of
hn
. We define the groups

{\rm GL}_{hn/t}(p^{t})=\{\phi:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\colon\phi\text{ is an ${\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}$-linear isomorphism}\}.

Note that
{\rm GL}_{hn/t}(p^{t})\leq{\rm GL}_{hn}(p)
. Moreover,
{\rm GL}_{n}(q)
is the group of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-isomorphisms on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}
is isomorphic to
the Singer group
S:={\rm GL}_{1}(q^{n})
via
a\longmapsto m_{a}
, where
m_{a}:\mbox{${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}$}\longrightarrow\mbox{${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}$},\ c\longmapsto ac
.
Furthermore, we define

(15)

G_{p^{t}}=\mbox{$\text{Gal}$}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\mid{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}})=\mbox{$\langle{\sigma_{p^{t}}}\rangle$},\text{ where }\sigma_{p^{t}}:\mbox{${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}$}\longrightarrow\mbox{${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}$},\ c\longmapsto c^{p^{t}}.

Remark 7.2
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}=\mbox{$\langle{\omega}\rangle$}
. One easily checks that

\sigma_{p^{t}}^{\ell}m_{\omega^{s}}=m_{\omega^{sp^{t\ell}}}\sigma_{p^{t}}^{\ell}\ \text{ and }\ m_{\omega^{s}}\sigma_{p^{t}}^{\ell}=\sigma_{p^{t}}^{\ell}m_{\omega^{sp^{rn-t\ell}}}\ \text{ for all $\ell\in[hn/t]$ and $s\in[q^{n}-1]$.}

Thus
G_{p^{t}}S=SG_{p^{t}}
, and this is a group of order
|G_{p^{t}}||S|=hn/t(q^{n}-1)
. In fact, it equals the normalizer
N_{{\rm GL}_{hn/t}(p^{t})}(S)
(see
[
9
, bottom of p. 98]
or
[
20
, top of p. 497]
).

Clearly, the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
does not depend on our choice of the ground field (
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
versus
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
), and therefore
the definition of the cyclic orbit code does not depend on this choice either.

Definition 7.3
.

Let
U,\,U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
and
{\mathcal{C}}=\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}=\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U^{\prime})
be their cyclic orbit codes.
Let
t
be a divisor of
h
.

(a)

{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
are called
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-linearly isometric
if there exists
\phi\in{\rm GL}_{hn/t}(p^{t})
such that
\phi({\mathcal{C}})={\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
.
If
t=1
, we call the codes
semilinearly isometric
, and for
t=h
they are called
linearly isometric
.

(b)

{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
are called
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-Frobenius-isometric
if there exists
\phi\in G_{p^{t}}S
such that
\phi({\mathcal{C}})={\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
.

Remark 7.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}},\,{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
be as in
Definition

7.3
and suppose
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-Frobenius-isometric.
Hence there exist
\ell\in[hn/t]
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}
such that
\phi=\sigma_{p^{t}}^{\ell}m_{\alpha}
satisfies
\phi({\mathcal{C}})={\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
.
Since
m_{\alpha}({\mathcal{C}})={\mathcal{C}}
, this implies
\sigma_{p^{t}}^{\ell}({\mathcal{C}})={\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
.
Thus, we may replace the group
G_{p^{t}}S
in
Definition

7.3
(b) by the Galois group
G_{p^{t}}
.

Note that the following definition is stronger than the one in
[
15
, Def. 4.3]
.

Definition 7.5
.

A subspace

is called

if no cyclic shift of

is contained in a proper subfield of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.

We have the following result.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
U,\,U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{q}(k,n)
be generic subspaces and let
t
be a divisor of
h
.
Then
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U)
and
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(U^{\prime})
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-linearly isometric if and only if they are
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-Frobenius-isometric.

Proof.

Consider
U,\,U^{\prime}
as elements of the Grassmannian
{\mathcal{G}}_{p^{t}}(kh/t,nh/t)
.
Then
[
15
, Thm. 6.2(a), Def. 3.5]
applied to

together with

7.2
tells us that being
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-linearly isometric is equivalent to

{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
-Frobenius-isometric.
∎

The following example shows that semilinearly isometric codes are not necessarily linearly isometric.

Example 7.7
.

Let
q=4,\,n=4
, thus
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}={\mathbb{F}}_{4}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}={\mathbb{F}}_{4^{4}}
.
Choose a primitive element
\omega\in{\mathbb{F}}_{4^{4}}
.
Since
(4^{4}-1)/(4-1)=85
, the element
a:=\omega^{85}
is a primitive element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}
.
If we choose the element
\omega
with minimal polynomial
x^{8}+x^{4}+x^{3}+x^{2}+1
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
, then its minimal polynomial over
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}
turns out to be
x^{4}+x^{3}+ax^{2}+ax+a
.

In
{\mathbb{F}}_{4^{4}}={\mathbb{F}}_{2^{8}}
we consider the
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}
-subspaces

{\mathcal{U}}=\mbox{$\langle{1,\omega}\rangle$}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{4}}=\mbox{$\langle{1,\omega,a,a\omega}\rangle$}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}\ \text{ and }\ {\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}=\mbox{$\langle{1,\omega^{2}}\rangle$}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{4}}=\mbox{$\langle{1,\omega^{2},a,a\omega^{2}}\rangle$}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}.

Note that both subspaces are generic and contain

.
Moreover, one can check that
{\mathbb{F}}_{4}
is the maximal field of linearity for
{\mathcal{U}}
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
.
Thus,
|\text{$\mathchar 525$}({\mathcal{U}})|=|\text{$\mathchar 525$}({\mathcal{U}}^{\prime})|=85
for the cyclic orbit codes.

Consider now the
2
-Frobenius

, which

.
In other words,

.
Using that

, we observe that

and even more,

for all

Suppose

and
\text{$\mathchar 525$}({\mathcal{U}}^{\prime})

implies that they are

-Frobenius-isometric and

However, using for instance SageMath, one easily checks that

for each

Hence semilinearly isometric codes are not necessarily linearly isometric.

{\mathcal{O}}_{s,\omega^{\ell}}
, where

\alpha_{i}=\frac{(q^{k}+1)(q-1)}{\hat{\ell}_{i}}-\frac{(q^{k}+1)(q-1)}{{\rm lcm}(\hat{\ell}_{i},q^{k}+1)}.

It is not hard to see that the fractions involved in the expression for
\alpha_{i}
are indeed integers:
we have
\hat{\ell}_{i}(p^{i}-1)=b(q^{k}+1)(q-1)
for some
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\gcd(\hat{\ell}_{i},b)=1
.
Hence
\hat{\ell}_{i}
divides
(q^{k}+1)(q-1)
.
For the second fraction use that
{\rm lcm}(\hat{\ell}_{i},q^{k}+1)=\hat{\ell}_{i}(q^{k}+1)/\gcd(\hat{\ell}_{i},q^{k}+1)
and a Bezout identity for
the greatest common divisor.

Proof.

Fix
(s,\ell)\in\bar{{\mathcal{A}}}
. Then (
19
) and
Proposition

8.10
implies for
i\geq 1

\sigma_{p}^{i}\in\mbox{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{O}}_{s,\omega^{\ell}})\Longleftrightarrow\omega^{\ell(p^{i}-1)}\in\mbox{$\langle{\omega^{(q^{k}+1)(q-1)}}\rangle$}\Longleftrightarrow\hat{\ell}_{i}\mid\ell.

From the definition of the set
\bar{{\mathcal{A}}}
, we know that
q^{k}+1
does not divide
\ell
.
Hence the same is true for every
\hat{\ell}_{i}
dividing
\ell
.
If there is no such
\hat{\ell}_{i}
, we thus have
{\mathcal{F}}_{s,\omega^{\ell}}=\{{\rm id}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\}
.
Otherwise,
{\mathcal{F}}_{s,\omega^{\ell}}=\{{\rm id}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\}\cup\{\sigma_{p}^{i}\colon i\in{\mathcal{I}},\,\hat{\ell}_{i}\text{ divides }\ell\}
.
Since
G_{p}
is cyclic, the subgroup
{\mathcal{F}}_{s,\omega^{\ell}}
is thus generated by
\sigma_{p}^{\iota}
, where
\iota
is the smallest
such value
i
.
This proves the first two statements.

As for the counting, note that for any
i\in\hat{{\mathcal{I}}}

\mbox{$\langle{\sigma_{p}^{i}}\rangle$}\leq{\mathcal{F}}_{s,\omega^{\ell}}\Longleftrightarrow\hat{\ell}_{i}\mid\ell\text{ and }(q^{k}+1)\nmid\ell\text{ and }\ell<(q^{k}+1)(q-1).

This is satisfied for
\alpha_{i}
values for
\ell
.
Moreover,

\mbox{$\langle{\sigma_{p}^{i}}\rangle$}={\mathcal{F}}_{s,\omega^{\ell}}\Longleftrightarrow\mbox{$\langle{\sigma_{p}^{i}}\rangle$}\leq{\mathcal{F}}_{s,\omega^{\ell}}\text{ and }\mbox{$\langle{\sigma_{p}^{j}}\rangle$}\not\leq{\mathcal{F}}_{s,\omega^{\ell}}\text{ for all proper divisors $j\in\hat{{\mathcal{I}}}$ of~$i$.}

This is satisfied by
\alpha_{i}-\sum_{j\in\hat{{\mathcal{I}}}\setminus\{i\},\,j\mid i}\alpha_{j}
many values for
\ell
.
Realizing that the factor
\phi(k)/2
accounts for the possible values for
s
in the index set
\bar{{\mathcal{A}}}
, we arrive at the final result.
∎

Open Problems

(1) For small parameters
q
and
n=2k
we determined all orbits codes
{\mathcal{O}}_{s,\gamma}
, and their distance distribution appears to be quite rigid:
it only depends on

, and the fact whether

contains a cyclic shift of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}

(in other words, the parameters

6.7
and

4.5

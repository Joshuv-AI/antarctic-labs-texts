Pacific Island Fisheries; Annual Catch Limit and Accountability 
Measures for Mariana Bottomfish Management Unit Species in the Guam 
Management Subarea

AGENCY: National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS), National Oceanic and 
Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), Commerce.

ACTION: Final rule.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: NMFS is modifying the annual catch limit (ACL) and 
accountability measures (AM) for Mariana bottomfish management unit 
species (MUS) in the Guam Management Subarea (the fishery). The rule 
increases the ACL from 31,000 pounds (lb) (14,061 kilograms (kg)) to 
34,500 lb (15,649 kg), replaces the in-season AM with a post-season 
overage adjustment if the average catch from the most recent 3 years 
exceeds the ACL, and removes the higher performance standard that 
closes the fishery in Federal waters for any overage. The rule is based 
on the best available scientific, commercial, and other information 
about the fishery, and supports rebuilding of the fishery.

DATES: The final rule is effective November 2, 2026.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: NMFS and the Council manage bottomfish 
fishing in Federal waters around Guam in accordance with the FEP, as 
authorized by the Magnuson-Stevens Fishery Conservation and Management 
Act (Magnuson-Stevens Act). The Council recommended the subject action 
at its 201st meeting in December 2024, based on: (1) a 2024 stock 
assessment update for Mariana bottomfish MUS in Guam; (2) consideration 
of the best scientific, commercial, and other information about the 
fishery; (3) a review from the Council's Scientific and Statistical 
Committee; and (4) input from the public. Accordingly, NMFS implements 
this action under section 303(c) and section 304(b) of the Magnuson-
Stevens Act.
    The fishery targets 13 species of bottomfish that are managed as a 
multi-species stock complex. Accordingly, stock status and management 
measures such as ACLs and AMs are applied to the stock complex rather 
than to individual species. NMFS determined the stock complex was 
overfished but not experiencing overfishing (85 FR 26940, May 6, 2020), 
and it has been subject to a rebuilding plan with an ACL of 31,000 lb 
(14,061 kg) since 2022 (87 FR 9271, February 18, 2022). An updated 
stock assessment completed in 2024 by the NMFS Pacific Islands 
Fisheries Science Center found that the stock was no longer overfished 
and continued to not experience overfishing. However, while stock 
biomass had increased above the ``minimum stock size threshold'' for 
the overfished condition, it had not increased to the biomass that 
would produce maximum sustainable yield. This means the stock is not 
yet fully rebuilt, so NMFS and the Council continue to manage the 
fishery under the rebuilding plan with a target time to rebuild by 
2031. The stock assessment update also provided new projections showing 
the stock could support moderately larger catches and still rebuild by 
2031.
    The subject action will also remove the in-season AM that would 
close the fishery in Federal waters if NMFS projects catch will reach 
the ACL

[[Page 62341]]

because NMFS and the Council found that available data systems do not 
allow for accurate in-season tracking and projections. The rule would 
implement a post-season AM in place of the in-season AM. Under the 
post-season AM, if the average catch from the most recent 3-year period 
exceeds the ACL, the ACL will be reduced in the subsequent fishing year 
by the amount of the overage to mitigate potential effects to the 
stock. NMFS would conduct a subsequent rulemaking action to implement 
the overage adjustment, if necessary. The rule would also remove the 
higher performance standard that requires NMFS to close the fishery in 
Federal waters indefinitely in response to any exceedance of the ACL. 
Fishery management would revert to the performance standard described 
in the FEP, which requires the Council to re-evaluate AMs if the ACL is 
exceeded twice in a 4-year period.
    As described in the Environmental Assessment (EA), NMFS does not 
expect this rule to result in a change in fishing operations, or other 
changes to the conduct of the fishery that would result in significant 
environmental impacts. Additional background information on this action 
can be found in the preamble to the proposed rule published on April 
23, 2026 (91 FR 21782) (the proposed rule).

Comments and Responses

    On April 23, 2026, NMFS published the proposed rule, draft EA, and 
Regulatory Impact Review for public comment (91 FR 21782). The comment 
period ended on May 26, 2026, and NMFS received comments from two 
individuals. NMFS did not receive any comments from fishery management 
agencies, nongovernmental organizations, or from individuals who 
identified themselves as fishery participants.
    Comment 1: One commenter generally expressed concern regarding the 
welfare of fish, stating that fishing causes pain, and requested a stop 
to fishing activities.
    Response 1: The comment did not identify a conservation, 
sustainability, or legal rationale to alter the rule under 
consideration. NMFS acknowledges the need to balance various fisheries 
issues, and is mandated under the Magnuson-Stevens Act to prevent 
overfishing while providing resources to consumers. Catch limits, 
accountability measures, monitoring, and EAs ensure the long-term 
sustainability of the fishery and resource availability for the 
communities that depend on seafood in the Pacific Islands region.
    Comment 2: One commenter suggested that NMFS had not considered or 
documented consideration of the Magnuson-Stevens Act and other 
applicable laws appropriately in the proposed rule.
    Response 2: NMFS disagrees. In developing the proposed rule and 
this final rule, we appropriately considered the Magnuson-Stevens Act 
and applicable laws, regulations and policies. During the rule 
development process, NMFS thoroughly reviewed the proposed action with 
respect to all applicable legal authorities. Prior to publication of 
the proposed rule and this final rule, the NOAA Assistant Administrator 
for Fisheries determined that they are consistent with the national 
standards and other provisions of the Magnuson-Stevens Act and other 
applicable laws. The Classification sections of the proposed and final 
rules documented compliance with all applicable statutes and 
regulations as required by law.

Changes From the Proposed Rule

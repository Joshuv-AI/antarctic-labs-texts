\Phi_{0}(G;\mathscr{H}_{m}\setminus\{0\})

can be fully characterized as follows

(H_{m})\leq(K)\iff(K)=(G)\text{ or }(K)=(H_{m^{\prime}})\text{ with }m\leq m^{\prime}\text{ and }\mathcal{B}(\{m,m^{\prime}\}).

It follows, from the recurrence formula (cf. Appendix (
A
)) for multiplication in the Burnside Ring, that one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{coeff}^{H_{s}}((H_{m})\cdot(K))=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }(K)=(G)\text{ and }s=m;\\

0&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\operatorname{coeff}^{H_{s}}((H_{m})\cdot(K))=[(K)=(G)][s=m]+2[(K)=(H_{m^{\prime}})][s=\gcd(m,m^{\prime})][\mathcal{B}(\{m,m^{\prime}\})].

With these preliminaries out of the way, we can now derive some results characterizing the behavior of the orbit type
(H_{m})
in the Burnside Ring product of any finite collection of basic degrees

(15)

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}

\deg_{\mathcal{W}_{m}^{-}}\cdot\deg_{\mathcal{W}_{m}^{-}}=(G).

Proof.

The result follows from a direct computation of the relevant Burnside Ring product

\displaystyle\deg_{\mathcal{W}_{m}^{-}}\cdot\deg_{\mathcal{W}_{m}^{-}}

\displaystyle=((G)-(H_{m}))\cdot((G)-(H_{m}))

\displaystyle=(G)-(H_{m})-(H_{m})+2(H_{m}).

\square

Next, let’s characterize the coefficients of the orbit types
(H_{s})
of the form (

For any

Again, the result follows from a direct computation of the relevant Burnside Ring product

What follows is the full generalization of the previous results to an orbit type

For any set of distinct indices

\square

4.1.
The Local Bifurcation Invariant and Krasnosel’skii’s Theorem

Formulation of a Krasnosel’skii type local bifurcation result for equation (

(for more details, the reader is referred to
[
8
,
6
]
). Our first definition clarifies what is meant by a bifurcation of the equation (
19

Definition 4.1
.

A trivial solution

for the equation (

(\alpha_{0},0)

\mathscr{S}

It is well-known that a necessary condition for any trivial solution

\mathscrbf A(\alpha_{0}):\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}
is not an isomorphism. This leads to the following definition:

Definition 4.2

A trivial solution
(\alpha_{0},0)\in M

regular point
for the equation (
19

\mathscrbf A(\alpha_{0})
is an isomorphism and a
critical point
otherwise. Moreover, a critical point
(\alpha_{0},0)\in M
is said to be
isolated
if there exists a deleted
\epsilon
-neighborhood

such that for all
\alpha\in(\alpha_{0}-\epsilon,\alpha_{0}+\epsilon)\setminus\{\alpha_{0}\}

(\alpha,0)\in M

The set of all critical points for equation (
19
), denoted
\Lambda

critical set
, i.e.

(21)

\displaystyle\Lambda:=\{(\alpha,0):\text{ $\mathscrbf A(\alpha):\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}$ is not an isomorphism}\}.

Definition 4.3
.

A trivial solution
(\alpha_{0},0)\in M
is said to be a
branching point
for the equation (
19
) if there exists a non-trivial continuum

with

branch
of nontrivial solutions bifurcating from the branching point
(\alpha_{0},0)

Whereas the classical Krasnosiel’skii bifurcation result is only concerned with the existence of a branch of nontrivial solutions for the equation (

Definition 4.4

Given a subgroup

\mathscr{S}^{H}
the corresponding
H
-fixed point space of non-trivial solutions.

Let
(\alpha_{0},0)\in\Lambda

G

It follows, from the homotopy property of the

Equivariant notation.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a compact Lie group. For any subgroup
H\leq\mathcal{G}
, we denote by
(H)
its conjugacy class,
by
N(H)
its normalizer by
W(H):=N(H)/H
its Weyl group in
\mathcal{G}
. The set of all subgroup conjugacy classes in
\mathcal{G}

\Phi(\mathcal{G}):=\{(H):H\leq\mathcal{G}\},

and has a natural partial order defined as follows

(H)\leq(K)\iff\exists_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\;\;gHg^{-1}\leq K.

As is possible with any partially ordered set, we extend the natural order over
\Phi(\mathcal{G})
to a total order, which we indicate by ‘
\preccurlyeq
’ to differentiate the two relations. Moreover, we put

\Phi_{0}(\mathcal{G}):=\{(H)\in\Phi(\mathcal{G})\;:\;\text{$W(H)$ is finite}\},

and, for any
(H),(K)\in\Phi_{0}(\mathcal{G})
, we denote by
n(H,K)
the number of subgroups
\tilde{K}\leq\mathcal{G}
with
\tilde{K}\in(K)
and
H\leq\tilde{K}
. Given a
\mathcal{G}
-space
X
with an element
x\in X
, we denote by

\mathcal{G}_{x}:=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:gx=x\}
the
isotropy group
of
x

and we call
(\mathcal{G}_{x})\in\Phi(\mathcal{G})
the
orbit type
of
x\in X
. We also put

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Phi(\mathcal{G},X):=\{(H)\in\Phi(\mathcal{G}):(H)=(\mathcal{G}_{x})\;\text{for some $x\in X$}\},\\
\Phi_{0}(\mathcal{G},X):=\Phi(\mathcal{G},X)\cap\Phi_{0}(\mathcal{G}).\end{cases}

Given any subgroup
H\leq\mathcal{G}
, we call the subspace

X^{H}:=\{x\in X:\mathcal{G}_{x}\geq H\},

the
H
-fixed-point subspace
of
X
. If
Y
is another
\mathcal{G}
-space, then a continuous map

\mathcal{G}
-equivariant

for each

and

.

The Burnside Ring and Axioms of the
\mathcal{G}
-Equivariant Brouwer Degree.

We call the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module
A(\mathcal{G}):=\mathbb{Z}[\Phi_{0}(\mathcal{G})]
the
Burnside Ring
when it is equipped with the multiplicative operation

(33)

\displaystyle(H)\cdot(K):=\sum\limits_{(L)\in\Phi_{0}(\mathcal{G})}n_{L}(L),\quad(H),(K)\in\Phi_{0}(\mathcal{G}),

where the coefficients
n_{L}\in\mathbb{Z}
are given by the recurrence formula

(34)

\displaystyle n_{L}:=\frac{n(L,H)|W(H)|n(L,K)|W(K)|-\sum_{(\tilde{L})\preccurlyeq(L)}n_{\tilde{L}}n(L,\tilde{L})|W(\tilde{L})|}{|W(L)|}.

Since every Burnside Ring element
a\in A(\mathcal{G})
can be expressed as a formal sum over some finite number of generator elements

a=n_{H_{1}}(H_{1})+n_{H_{2}}(H_{2})+\cdots+n_{H_{N}}(H_{N}),

we can use the notation

\operatorname{coeff}^{H}:A(\mathcal{G})\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\quad\operatorname{coeff}^{H}(a):=n_{H},

to specify the integer coefficient standing next to the generator element
(H)\in\Phi_{0}(\mathcal{G})
.

Let
V
be an orthogonal
\mathcal{G}
-representation and suppose that
\Omega\subset V
is an open bounded
\mathcal{G}
-invariant set. A
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant map

, in which case the pair

\mathcal{G}
-pair
in
V
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{G}}(V)
the set of all admissible
\mathcal{G}
-pairs in

and by
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{G}}
the set of all admissible
\mathcal{G}
-pairs defined by taking a union over all orthogonal
\mathcal{G}
-representations, i.e.

The
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant Brouwer degree provides an algebraic count of solutions, according to their symmetric properties, to equations of the form

where
(f,\Omega)\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{G}}
. In fact, it is standard (cf.

8
]

\mathcal{G}
-equivariant Brouwer degree

\mathcal{G}
-pair

of existence, additivity, homotopy and normalization:

Theorem A.1

There exists a unique map
\mathcal{G}\text{\rm-deg}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{G}}\to A(\mathcal{G})

\mathcal{G}

(Existence)

(Additivity)

(Homotopy)
For any

\Omega

\mathcal{G}
-homotopy,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\text{\rm-deg}(h_{t},\Omega)=\mathrm{constant}.

(Normalization)

For any open bounded neighborhood of the origin in an orthogonal

-representation

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\text{\rm-deg}(\id,\Omega)=(\mathcal{G}).

The following are additional properties of the map

(Multiplicativity)

Genetic elements of a long-standing family business: An analysis of five century-old family businesses based on grounded theory
Authors: Qi Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSuccession is a major threat to the sustainable growth of family businesses. This study proposes that the growth of a family business is a co-evolutionary process involving multiple memes, and a family business undergoes succession through memes. To address this exploratory question, the author analyzes entrepreneurial behavior in five well-known century-old Western family enterprises based on grounded theory, and constructs a deoxyribonucleic acid model of the family business. The model not only reveals the most prominent memes in the complex meme system of a family business, but also illustrates the connectivity of different strengths among the organizational memes. The implications and recommendations derived from this study indicate that family businesses should develop specialization and succession capabilities through memes, and explore different means of innovation and adaptation to realize cross-generational growth.Keywords: Family business, Small business enterprise, Meme, Succession, Sustainable growth

Introduction

The global economic impact on family firms is undoubtedly highly significant. For example, family firms are estimated to account for over 70 % of global gross domestic product [1]. Long-term development and a desire to continue are key goals of family businesses. There are two long-term aspects of a family business. The first is succession and the ability to innovate; the second refers to innovativeness as an aspect of organizational culture [2]. However, the COVID-19 pandemic and digital economy have disrupted the pace of succession and growth of family businesses, especially in China. Whether or not a family business achieves innovative transformation through succession directly threatens its survival. Therefore, more research on sustainability and succession in family businesses is urgently needed to further the understanding of scholars and practitioners alike [3].

Existing research on family business succession follows the logic of “what to handed down and how is it handed down,” and attempts to provide theoretical guidance from the perspective of the content elements needed for succession, considering succession planning and successor training. However, two problems remain unresolved. The first is the primary problem of succession, “what is handed down,” which has long been disputed. For example, some scholars believe that family business succession is a succession of power, whereas others have suggested that the characteristics or resources related to incumbent entrepreneurs should be the primary focus. Another approach ignores the internal relationship between family business succession and company growth. The relevant literature mainly studies the impact of leadership succession on organizational performance. However, the growth of family businesses does not simply equate to an increase in organizational performance.

Family business succession is a long-term process that, in many instances, can take less than a few years, or even more than 20 years. Family business succession does not necessarily involve a handover to the successful incumbent family member(s) or a handing over of particular but unspecified power characteristics or even resources to the successful incumbent. Therefore, this study proposes the following research question:

‘How should a family business be handed down when based on the perspective of enterprise growth?’

Here, the operational definition of family business succession is no longer about the transfer of power from the current holder to the successor, but rather the transfer of multiple factors that determine enterprise growth from the current holder to the successor. The operational definition of family business growth is no longer the change in organizational performance, but rather the change in business scale and quality caused by multiple core factors. However, little is known about family businesses's growth. Generally, the Penrosean approach is the primary research perspective for non-family businesses. Mainstream theories may require adaptation when applied to family businesses. Specifically, the Penrosean approach to firm growth appears insufficient to fully explain the growth of family businesses and would benefit from incorporating non-economic determinants of growth, such as those proposed from the Social Emotional Wealth (SEW) perspective [4]. According to Gomez-Mejia et al. [5], SEW refers to the non-financial benefits that families obtain from family businesses through the identities of their owners, decision-makers, and managers. This includes the ability to exercise power; fulfill the need for belonging, affection, and emotional needs; maintain family values within the business over the long-term; sustain family control; preserve the family's social capital; fulfill family obligations based on kinship relationships; and treat family members with altruism.

Firm growth is a complex phenomenon to define and measure. In the seminal work “The Theory of the Growth of the Firm,” Edith Penrose [6] observes that “The term ‘growth’ sometimes denotes merely increase in amount; for example, when one speaks of ‘growth’ in output, export, and sales. At other times, however, it is used in its primary meaning, implying an increase in size or improvement in quality as a result of a process of development, akin to natural biological processes in which an interacting series of internal changes leads to increases in size accompanied by changes in the characteristics of the growing object.” Although SEW provides a perspective on the particularity of family business growth, family businesses tend to prioritize long-term non-economic goals and avoid potential risks to SEW. However, this is only a partial explanation for the growth of family businesses, as they also have economic goals. Therefore, seeking new perspectives that explain family business growth is necessary. Based on Penrose's biological metaphor of firm growth, this study analyzes the definition of a firm and the determinants of growth from the perspective of organizational evolution.

A family business is an enterprise and organic family unit; therefore, it is not only an economic organization but also a cultural one. As Schein [7] noted, culture is one of the most powerful and stable forces at work in organizations. Evidently, family businesses cannot be explained simply through rational adoption. Memetics helps us understand a phenomenon and acts as a metaphorical framing device with strong explanatory power [8].

From the perspective of organizational culture evolution, memes promote the evolution of family businesses. What are the memes of family businesses?

In the context of this question, there are many world-famous, century-old family business enterprises that may provide inspiration, leading to a second research question:

“What are the internal drivers that allow family business enterprises to continue to grow over many generations?”

There are various approaches to understanding and evaluating organizational cultural phenomena, such as surveys, in-depth interviews, detailed descriptions, and participant observation [9]. However, identifying a family business's memes, which are the cultural traces left by the enterprising family, is difficult, as they are invisible and their structure is unknown. Grounded theory is a methodology for discovering theory from data, specifically through qualitative methods to construct theory. Grounded theory follows rigorously defined research procedures, and its findings can be audited for traceability. Therefore, using grounded theory methodology for theory construction is a scientific process, with research outcomes that are credible and conclusions that are explanatory. The growth of a family business is a co-evolutionary process involving multiple memes, and a family business undergoes succession through memes. To address this exploratory question, this study adopts the method of the bottom-up theory, —namely, the construction-grounded theory, —to reinterpret the family succession progression of five-century-old large family business enterprises. Moreover, this study considers the concept of family business memes, along with a constructed DNA theoretical model, provide new insights into the theory and practices of family business succession and growth.

Literature review

Family business succession

Although the connotations of family business succession have been expanding, existing studies are based on the succession of resources owned by a generation of entrepreneurs who are strongly dependent on the individual entrepreneur and will disappear with the departure of this entrepreneur. Successors who obtain these resources find the difficult to effectively use and control. This study considers that the content elements to be passed on by a family business organization should be objective and not require human willpower in their transfer.

Family business succession and growth

Only a few studies looked at understanding family business growth in depth [10]. The difference between family business and non-family business is familism, obviously the growth of family business is inseparable from the unique role of family involvement. Family dynamics influence company goals, decisions and related outcomes. Previous studies examining the effects of family involvement on firm growth address the dimensions of involvement separately and evaluate their additive impact while overlooking their joint effects. Moreno-Menéndez & Casillas [11] propose that the combined effect of various dimensions of family involvement is responsible for how much family firms grow compared to their non-family counterparts. There is the lack of knowledge and analysis of the relationship between family business succession and enterprise growth.

Succession is the most important process in the life cycle of a family business. Succession activities can continuously impact the innovation, strategy, culture, and viability of the business [12]. Although some scholars have proposed that a family business should evolve through its succession in accordance with changes in the external environment and from the perspective of organizational ecology and enterprise evolution theory, power succession is still considered the starting point. Moreover, existing studies do not clearly consider enterprise growth factors and their joint effects as the objects of evolutionary selection analysis and, therefore, have no systematic evolutionary strategy at the micro level.

Family business culture

There is the lack of studies on how values are formed across generations. Culture (values) is nurtured in relationships between different generations. Moreover, culture is dynamic and evolves both through relationships and discursive processes involving differences and divisions. The relationship between family values and family business growth is differentiated by their complexity and generational aspects. These values can influence professionalization, succession, and innovation processes [13].

The rapidly changing environment has forced family businesses to develop a strong adaptability for sustainable growth. In other words, it does not align with the needs of practical development to study family business succession in terms of a certain element without considering enterprise growth. Therefore, as a primary objective, this study combines a family business's growth with its succession to answer the question of how a family business should “be passed down” with the objective existence of memes in the family business. A further objective is to use the outcome to analyze succession and growth in the dynamic environment of family businesses.

Organizational meme or DNA theory suggests that corporate memes are composed of elements that determine a company or corporate development and rely on the DNA structure of biological memes. Qrganizational meme or DNA theory has relatively strong explanatory power on the growth of enterprises. However, the theoretical perspective has not yet been applied to the study of family businesses. Family business memes are the core elements of a company or corporate development. Therefore, the following research question is proposed:

“What are the essential and unique core elements that determine the development of family businesses?”

Family businesses comprise two systems: the family system and the business system. The essential elements that determine their development include both family and business aspects, with these mutually influencing each other. However, the existing literature does not comprehensively summarize the essential elements of family business development from both family and business perspectives. Colli [14] argued that the essential factors for the long-term survival of family businesses include kinship, property, control, family values, leadership of successors, trust, and networks, but failed to provide a comprehensive summary of the factors at the business level. Landes [15] primarily proposed factors determining the development of century-old family businesses at the family level, including patriarchal governance; a system of mutual trust, responsibility, habits, and emotional connection; passing legal and even personal responsibilities; and the capabilities, mission, and unity of successors. Similarly, their work lacks a comprehensive summary of factors at the business level. Tàpie et al. [16] stated that dynamic innovation is a key factor for the sustained growth of century-old family businesses from the business perspective, but they also failed to provide a summary of the factors at the family level. Following Price [17], considering memes as “a composite mindset, a paradigm, or a mental model” and regarding the organizational culture as a complex pattern of memes or a complex memetic system, this study considers that the memes of a family business should not only reflect the uniqueness of the family but also highlight the unique influence brought by the family involved in the family business. The unique influence of a family's involvement in an enterprise is an intrinsically essential factor that determines the survival and development of the family business, which endure after many generations. Therefore, family business memes are defined as the values formed by the first generation of family entrepreneurs in their management activities, which become essential elements for the survival and development of the family business through the continuous succession and innovation of the family successors [18]. The core aspect of family business memes is value; its nucleus is a rule; and its outer nucleus is a behavior pattern. Family business memes have the characteristics of structure, culture, stability, heredity, variability, and a wide distribution. The memes of a family business constitute the content elements of the family business's succession. Family business growth is a process of inheritance or variation of the family business memes. Therefore, this study identifies a family business's memes and construct a DNA model of a family business's memes as the research objective to determine the content elements of family business succession. Thus, it seeks to enable enterprises to conduct innovative activities to ensure their evolution in a dynamic environment.

Research design

Research methods

Whether Western or Chinese, the current literature shows a limited amount of research specifically conducted on family business memes in related fields. This limitation applies to elements of family business memes and their relationships, which are unknown, highlighting the need to identify this missing link through case studies [19].

When compared with a single case study, multi-case studies can be used to more effectively condense and compare the data to determine the similarity of the cases and provide a more accurate and general conclusion, via mutual confirmation and supplementation between cases [20]. Therefore, on this premise, this study adopts multi-case study method to summarize the common characteristics of well-known, century-old family businesses in the succession process to obtain the genetic elements of each long-standing family business. As the collected cases are more than 100 years old, the collection of their genetic elements involves a large number of concepts and complex relationships between different variables. The significance of the grounded theory method lies in its theoretical construction. It is suitable as a method that involves various concepts together with additional complex relations between variables, including various large-scale forms of construction [21]. Accordingly, the grounded theory method is used to analyze the case data.

Analysis process of grounded theory

The grounded theory method follows inductive rather than deductive logic, eliminating the need for researchers to logically deduce pre-set hypotheses. Instead, it involves the inductive study of sentences and dialogues in the data, followed by theoretical abstraction. Key figures in grounded theory, Corbin and Strauss, introduced the coding techniques of grounded theory in 1990, known as protocol [29]. Glaser believes that if researchers follow a well-established procedure in analyzing data, the conclusions of the study can be highly reliable, thus meeting the criteria for generalizability, replicability, accuracy, rigor, and verifiability of the research findings [30]. The coding techniques proposed by Corbin and Strauss involve three stages: open coding, spindle coding, and selective coding.

The coding of four case companies, the Ford Motor Company, EXOR, Bertelsmann Stiftung, and the Tata Group—was completed using the open coding tool spindle, a coding-selective coding program, and it was ensured that the research results reached theoretical saturation using the BMW case for comparative verification.

Open coding

First, the phenomenon was defined, and then the original material was decomposed into separate stories or events, which were named “Fx―Ex―Tx ―Bx” and were used to identify, the family entrepreneurs of Ford, EXOR, Tata, and Bertelsmann Stiftung, respectively, in past generations. Then, statements about the family's entrepreneurial activity or behavior were obtained, following the structure “Fxcx―Excx―Txcx―Bxcx”. Second, conceptualization was performed: the defined phenomena were further developed into concepts that were identified by “Cx”. Finally, the categorization of the related concepts took place: they were grouped into classes of concepts that were identified by “CCx”. This study obtained 1696 concepts and 125 categories. Spindle coding

The purpose of spindle coding is to clarify the relationships between various concepts and integrate them into higher-level abstract categories. In grounded theory, the paradigm model “conditions/causes - action/interaction strategies - consequences” is typically used to analyze the logical relationships between the categories, further classifying the categories, and refining the main categories. When analyzing the associations among concept categories, researchers should not only consider the relationships between the categories but also place the participants' speech in the context of their social and cultural background at that time, contemplating the intentions and motivations of the participants who expressed these concept categories [31]. Consequently, this study summarizes the entrepreneurial behavior of the Ford Motor Company, EXOR, Tara, and Bertelsmann Group by including the same cause and effect, situation, and function. Following this procedure, we extracted 10 new categories, which were related to higher levels of memeralization and abstraction, and were considered the main categories. These included family control, family unity, family mission, merit-based succession, entrepreneurship, human capital, social capital, power arrangements, crisis management, and competitive strategy.

The purpose of the selective coding was to refine the core categories that required further integration with the main categories obtained in the spindle coding stage. Through the open and spindle coding, many correlations and causality among the concepts, supporting categories, and main categories of family business growth were obtained; however, these relationships were disordered and could not be effectively integrated. Therefore, in the selective coding stage, core categories were selected to establish systematic links with other supporting and main categories [32].

Therefore, the integration line of family business growth was selected and analyzed as follows. This is the dual driving force that determines a firm's development strategy and, in turn, the enterprise's stakeholder management, which ultimately determines the grow of the family business. Theoretical saturation test

Theoretical saturation refers to a situation in a case analysis whereby new data cannot be used to further develop a specific category; that is, a theoretical saturation state is reached. After completing the coding of the three case enterprises - the Ford Motor Company, EXOR, and the Bertelsmann Stiftung Group-this study analyzed the case of the Tata Group to ensure that there were no new categories. Then, through a comparison with the BMW case study, it was found that there were no new categories. Consequently, the results of this study reached theoretical saturation.

Discussion

Elements of family business growth were identified through open, spindle, and selective coding. This study explored the elements of family business growth and their relationships. The “company growth” module in Fig. 1 and its two supporting factors, “competitive advantage” and “expanding business,” are the identified elements of family business growth and, therefore, are not discussed in detail. With reference to the relevant literature on the multi-generational nature of a family business—such as Chrisman et al. Family mission and entrepreneurship are the dual driving forces

According to the modern Chinese dictionary, responsibility refers to an individual's self-conscious desire to do things well [40]. A sense of responsibility is reflective of individual consciousness rather than commitment. Based on this assertion, a “family mission” is defined as “the responsibility of the successor of a family business to continue the tradition of the parents' management.” The family mission requires the successor of the family business to inherit the natural family attributes and to continue the family's management tradition, and realize their self-worth.

“Entrepreneurship” comprises three aspects: values, adventure, and innovation. Compared with entrepreneurship and values [36] or core values that extend beyond profit [37], the concept of “entrepreneurship” in this study considers values as one of the main directions. Although the renowned management scholar Drucker suggested that innovation is the most basic activity and core function of entrepreneurs, purposeful and organized systematic innovation is the essence of entrepreneurship [41].

From the results of the five case studies, it was found that the family entrepreneurs, especially first-generation-entrepreneurs, expressed a value pursuit to change society that led to risk taking, followed by innovation and sound business activity. Based on this, “entrepreneurship” is defined as “the risk-taking and innovation activities of family business leaders based on value pursuit.”

In the examination of family business succession and growth, scholars agree that family entrepreneurs require entrepreneurship, but they ignore the influence of the family system with respect to family entrepreneurs. Family entrepreneurs not only require a memeral sense of entrepreneurship but should also display the firm's pursuit of values and a sense of the family mission. The family mission and entrepreneurship act together, ultimately helping a family business achieve intergenerational growth.

Family control and the competition strategy are the development strategy

From a review of the related literature, little or no research has discussed “family control,” as the subjects of the previous studies were family-controlled enterprises [[35], [36], [37], [38]]. In the process of firm development, family enterprises should always emphasize their control over enterprise growth; otherwise, they cannot exist. The concept of “family control” described in this study includes three aspects.

Shleifer and Vishny [42] argued that control can be divided into two concepts: nominal control associated with ownership and actual control associated with key resources. Additionally, Zhu and Du [43] argued that control focuses not only on explicit decision-making behavior but also on implicit dominance. It is the core of the actual control owner's power to induce the dominant person to obey voluntarily; furthermore, the ownership structure of “family control” is related to nominal control. Moreover, the design of the double-layered ownership structure ensures that the family has the decision-making power within the business operation—that is, actual control. The implicit control contained in “family control” refers to the control of the corporate culture. Through cultural control, the hidden strengthening of the family's influence on the business is possible; this was particularly obvious in the Bertelsmann Stiftung and Tata Group. Based on this, “family control” is defined as “the hidden control of family enterprises through the design of an equity structure and voting rights.”

“Competition strategy” is a common term in management theory research and practice, in which is part of a business strategy in strategic management when referring to business activities [44]. Compared with the competition strategy concept proposed by David [44], the “competition strategy” in this study refers to having a customer-oriented or customer-focused approach and an independent, innovative attitude focused on core corporate activities [30]. This concept also considers financial, R&D, branding, and other aspects related to business activities to ensure business success. Therefore, a “competition strategy” is defined as “a series of actions taken by a family business with key business performance at the center.”

“Family control” is the premise for the survival and continuation of family enterprises, and the formulation of a “competition strategy” in an enterprise must be performed under the constraints of “family control.” In other words, the family must create a competition strategy for the family business; otherwise, a failure to do so or deviation from such a strategy—especially when brought about by the completion of such a strategy—will undoubtedly weaken the family control and strengthen the influence of external forces that may threaten it.

Power arrangement and human capital constitute internal stakeholder management

The element of “command,” as proposed by Miller [35], refers to managers who have a large amount of decision-making power and can flexibly adjust the decision-making and maintain the vitality of the enterprise. Conversely, the category of “power arrangement” includes not only the decision-making power of managers but also the distribution of power between family and non-family executives to form a balanced power structure. The scope of “power arrangement” and concept of corporate governance in the existing literature show similarity. The concept of corporate governance mainly refers to the organizational structure at the “physical level,” associated with shareholders’ meetings, the Board of Directors, Board of Supervisors, manager level, and division of responsibilities and rights, as well as checks, balances, and supporting mechanisms, such as decision-making, incentives, and restraint mechanisms.

“Power arrangement,” when focused on forming a power structure, achieves the goal of mutual checks and balances. Additioally, compared to the concept of corporate governance, the concept of power arrangement is narrower. Based on this premise, “power arrangement” is defined as “the rational distribution of management rights among management by family business leaders to form a power structure to achieve the purpose of power checks and balances.”

The element of “community,” as proposed by Miller, refers to the construction of a close and humane culture to motivate employees and to maintain their loyalty to the enterprise [35]. Moreover, the “human capital” category presented in this study considers that family enterprises not only need to motivate their employees through a humanized culture but must also excel in finding suitable talent, similarly to a business philosophy. The concept of human capital, as presented by the renowned American economist Schultz, refers to the knowledge, skills, experience, and health of individuals [45]. Compared with Schultz's concept of human capital, “human capital” in this research focuses on human capital management activities. Therefore, “human capital” is defined as “family enterprises choosing the talent to match their business philosophy and improving their loyalty by means of incentives to obtain their intellectual resources.” As shown in Table 4, the first generation leader of the Tata Group integrated ethical treatment of employees and concern for their welfare into the Tata management philosophy. As a result, employee loyalty at the Tata Group is very high, with many employees' descendants also joining the Tata Group to work.

Both “power arrangement” and “human capital” are targeted at employees; however, “power arrangement” plays a decisive role in the performance of senior managers. “Human capital” is chiefly directed at the management of middle- and low-level employees. Furthermore, family enterprises are obligated to have a stable high-level power structure to command staff at the middle and low levels, with the intention of achieving excellence and creating profits for the company using their talent.

Family unity and social capital constitute external stakeholder management

De Geus [34] argued that cohesion enables individuals in an organization to exist independently and closely together. Cohesion and a strong identity are important personality characteristics of long-standing companies that are transmitted from generation to generation. In this study, the concept of “family unity” refers to cohesion among family members, including relationship processing, interest maintenance, conflict management, and communication mechanisms. Most family business researchers believe that a family business requires relationship governance, but only when referring to family and non-family employees. It has been found that the relationship between family members who do not enter the enterprise and family members who enter the enterprise directly affects the stability within the family and in turn affects the development of the enterprise. As shown in Table 2, the decision to close the factory by the first-generation leader of Ford Motor Company was influenced by his wife.

This issue requires further investigation through case studies. Based on this finding, “family unity” is defined as “the establishment of a communication mechanism among family members, which coordinates various relationships and in turn protects family interests.”

The element of “relationship,” as proposed by Miller [35], refers to the maintenance of a long-term win–win relationship with external partners for the long-term development of an enterprise. The concept of “social capital” considered in this study is consistent with this. Social capital, as a technical term, generally refers to the sum of the real or potential resources associated with group members; it provides support to each member from the perspective of group ownership [46]. Based on this, “social capital” is defined as follows: “a family business is involved in a social relation network and participates in, sponsors, and supports various activities to gain trust and praise, which are transformed into a relational resource for business development.”

“Family unity” can realize harmony within the family and then lead to a kind of unity power and a respectable reputation, which have a positive influence on the formation of “social capital.” If family enterprises have rich “social capital,” this positively impacts “family unity.”

Merit-based succession and crisis management are succession management

“Merit-based succession” is a new concept introduced in this study that includes not only upbringing but also education, highlighting the self-positioning and leadership of successors [36]. There is no corresponding concept of “merit-based succession” presented in the management or related fields; it is defined as “the choice of a successor who has clear self-positioning and is trained in leadership through various educational methods in the family business.”

The category of “crisis management” also does not appear in the literature mentioned above. This may be because succession activities between two generations span only a few years, at which point the crisis problem is not evident. The long-term succession research presented in this study shows that the successors of the studied family enterprises were challenged by various crises, which were then successfully resolved. Therefore, “crisis management” is also one of the core elements that determine the growth of family enterprises. A crisis is an event that threatens an enterprise's future profitability growth, and even survival of an enterprise [47]. This implies that typical conditions have disintegrated, and disorder overwhelms the previous upholding of rules and good order [48]. Such an outcome often results in the destruction of the experiences, beliefs, and values shared by stakeholders and groups [49]. Based on this, “crisis management” is defined as “the systematic repair and reconstruction of the external environment and internal order, rules, and contracts destroyed by crisis events.”

“Merit-based succession” significantly influences successful “crisis management.” The stronger the abilities of family business successors, the greater the possibility of successfully resolving a crisis. The success of “crisis management” further enhances the possibility of “merit-based succession.”

The family mission drives family control, power arrangement, merit-based succession, and family unity

The five elements of the family mission, family control, power arrangement, merit-based succession, and family unity are the core elements of the family system. As the driving force of the family system, the family mission promotes changes in family control, family unity, power arrangement, and merit-based succession. In other words, the four elements of family control, family unity, power arrangement, and merit-based succession may not exert an effective influence without a family mission. The family mission plays a decisive role in family control, family unity, power arrangement, and merit-based succession.

Entrepreneurship drives the competitive strategy, human capital, crisis management, and social capital

Entrepreneurship, human capital, social capital, competitive strategy, and crisis management are the core elements of the enterprise system. As the driving force of the enterprise system, entrepreneurship strongly drives the formation of human capital, social capital, competitive strategies, and crisis management. Specifically, human capital, social capital, competitive strategies, and crisis management may not be formed without entrepreneurship in the enterprise system. Entrepreneurship plays a decisive role in human capital, social capital, competitive strategies, and crisis management.

A DNA model of a family business

First, this study determined the double helix chain of a family business's DNA. The double helical chain of biological DNA is complementary, and its function is to form a DNA skeleton that connects the bases to form a stable spatial structure. A family business consists of two systems: the family and the enterprise. All family business activities are the result of the interaction between the two systems. Similarly, the double-helical chain of a family business's DNA should reflect the characteristics of the family and business, particularly when there is a strong correlation with other core elements. A family's mission and entrepreneurship are the dual driving forces of family and business systems, which play decisive roles in other core elements. Consequently, the family mission and entrepreneurship constitute a double spiral chain of family business DNA arranged externally. The chain of the family mission connects family control, family unity, power arrangement, and merit-based succession, and includes the chain of entrepreneurship, which connects human capital, social capital, competitive strategies, and crisis management.

Conclusions, implications, and future research directions

Conclusions

Succession is a key threat to the sustainable growth of family businesses, However, research on the relationship between succession and growth is lacking. Therefore, this study presented two research questions: 1) How should a family business be passed down from the perspective of enterprise growth? 2) What internal drivers allow family business enterprises to continue growing for many generations? Based on the perspective of organizational culture evolution, this study proposed that the growth of a family business is a co-evolutionary process involving multiple memes, and that a family business undergoes succession through its memes. Therefore, this study identified a family business's memes and constructed a DNA model of a family business's memes as the research objective, to determine the content elements of family business succession. Thus, the obtained results can help enterprises to conduct innovative activities to ensure their evolution in a dynamic environment.

Based on the analysis of the multi-generational growth processes of five century-old family businesses, this study found that the core elements of family business growth included the family mission, family control, family unity, merit-based succession, entrepreneurship, human capital, social capital, power arrangement, crisis management, and competition strategies. Analysis of the relationships between the core elements revealed that 10 elements had a DNA structure similar to that of biological memes with a double helix. These included the family mission and entrepreneurship with four base pairs, which were as follows.

The DNA model of the family business shows that the research objective has been achieved and provides answers to the two research questions. First, the DNA model of the family business provides a powerful explanation of how family businesses realize cross-generational growth through succession. The DNA model of the family business combines enterprise growth and inheritance, and identifies the content elements with which a family business can “passed downed.” Evidently, the succession of a family business is not achieved with a single element (e.g., entrepreneurship, social capital), as suggested in previous research on family business succession; rather, it is a collaborative succession process comprising multiple elements. This finding helps alleviate confusion regarding the objectives of family business succession.

Second, the DNA model of the family business explains the joint effects of the different dimensions of family involvement on firm growth. Family involvement in family businesses must be studied as a multidimensional construct to capture the potential independence among the dimensions of family involvement and how they relate to firm growth. Most studies examining the effects of family involvement on firm growth address the dimensions of involvement separately and evaluate their additive impacts, while overlooking their joint effects. According to the DNA model of the family business, family involvement includes multiple dimensions: the family mission, family control, family unity, merit-based succession, and power arrangement. These dimensions are not only independent but also operate together with entrepreneurship, human capital, social capital, crisis management, and competition strategies of the enterprise system; they jointly promote the cross-generational growth of family businesses.

Thirdly, family business memes provide a new theoretical perspective for the study of family business heteromemeity. Familyism is the core concept of family business research, which has been used by scholars to explain the heteromemeity of family business [50]. The heteromemeity study of family business lacks a theoretical basis and does not explain why family participation has a positive effect on business [51]. Family business memes can also highlight the decisive influence of family participation of a business and give a reasonable explanation as to why family participation has a positive effect on businesses. When compared with the explanation of familial heteromemeity by familyism, family business memes clearly call attention to the systematic influence of family on business.

Additionally, the DNA model of the family business promotes research on family business culture. The DNA of the family business shows that the culture of the business comprises the effects of the founder's values on business practices, and is formed through the joint efforts of different generations of family members and non-family members. The complex meme system of family businesses and the interactions among the memes are fundamental forces that promote the stability and development of organizational culture.

Implications

To survive and prosper across generations, family businesses must be actively restructured as the family and business evolve in an age characterized by uncertainty. The DNA model of a family business can create important sources of competitive advantage for family businesses. The DNA model demonstrates that values can influence professionalization, succession, and innovation processes. The memes in the family and firm can be skillfully reinterpreted to deal with core managerial issues such as innovation and leadership. Business growth involves the management of a family business's memes to be taken over by the second generation. First, a family business needs to acquire ten memetic elements: family mission, family control, family unity, merit-based succession, entrepreneurship, human capital, social capital, power arrangements, crisis management, and a competition strategies. In the succession process, a family business, as far as possible, should not destroy the relationship between the elements; it should enhance the value orientation of entrepreneurship and conceal family control while implementing a robust competitive strategy combined with the brand name.

Second, family business memes were injected into the successor's role model to complete the cultivation. Through the injection of the family business memes, the successor completes the transformation of the family entrepreneur's roles. In the process of role training, family businesses should cultivate the family mission and entrepreneurship of the successor while simultaneously allowing the successor to deal with crises, which is more conducive to enhancing the successor's abilities.

Third, aligning with the 10 meme elements and their relationship characteristics in the family business DNA model, a weighted family business succession and strategy requires formation. Family enterprises undertake structural and systematic succession an innovation under the dual drivers of family missions and entrepreneurship. Alternatively, succession is adopted to detect, cultivate, optimize, and replicate the memes of the family business. It also considers the environment and innovation as directions to recombine the memes of the family business, to change the rules and behavioral patterns of the meme elements, or adjust the relationship structure between the elements, which helps in updating the endogenous system of the family business and achieve sustainable growth. For example, Hong Kong's Fung Group is a fourth-generation family business. The recombinant path of Fung Group's genetic code is a process driven by competitive strategy to transform other genetic elements. The performance of the competitive strategy gene across different generations in the Fung Group, shows different innovative paths driven by four generations of entrepreneurs. The first generation of the Fung Group established the core business, and the second generation, building on this core, continuously expanded the market and diversified its operations. The third generation led the Fung Group to become a globally renowned multinational conglomerate through a series of strategic innovations. However, around 2010, disruptions in e-commerce led to a persistent downturn in the global retail market. After the fourth generation took over, they faced the most significant operational crisis in the history of the Fung Group. Despite measures like slowing down acquisitions, increasing order production speed, and cutting costs, the financial performance of the Fung Group continued to decline. Therefore, after several years of adjustment, the fourth generation centered their competition strategy on digital reform of their core business [52].

Limitations and future research directions

This study is the first attempt to utilize memetics in a family business context and provides a seminal argument for the use of a combination of organizational DNA theory and memetics to shed light on the evolution of family businesses. However, our study has three limitations that provide opportunities for future research. First, there is a lack of discussion on the relationship between family business DNA and firm performance. Future studies should use family business DNA as an independent variable to test its main effect on firm performance by appropriately adding moderating or mediating variables. Second, there is a lack of discussion on the selection, variation, and retention mechanisms of memes. Family business evolution is a process in which the DNA constantly changes according to the environment. Identifying the family business DNA was only the first step. The relationship between the family business DNA and firm growth varies according to environmental complexity and generational aspects. Future research should include environmental and generational variables to reveal the selection, variation, and retention mechanisms of memes. Third, there is a lack of discussion on the applicability of the family business DNA model across different cultural backgrounds or among various types of family businesses. Future research should focus on family businesses from different cultural contexts or of various types, such as those in East Asian countries, those with female successors, and those in sectors like biopharmaceuticals and finance. Analyzing the key factors that drive the growth of these businesses, and comparing them with the family business DNA model, will aid in refining and improving the family business DNA model.

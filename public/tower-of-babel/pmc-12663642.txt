Late secondary syphilis mimicking anal carcinoma: A case illustration
Authors: Aanika Balaji Warner, John Woller, Philip Wagner
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSyphilis can closely mimic malignancy in both clinical presentation and appearance. We describe a case of a 58-year-old man presenting with painful perianal masses raising concern for anal carcinoma. Serologic testing revealed late secondary syphilis with a rapid plasma reagin titer of 1:128. Treatment with doxycycline resulted in complete clinical resolution and appropriate serologic response, obviating planned biopsy. While condyloma lata were considered as part of the differential, the patient’s history, lesion morphology, and distribution were atypical, prompting initial concern for malignancy, This case underscores the importance of early serologic testing to avoid unnecessary invasive procedures in atypical presentations of syphilis.

Case Presentation

A 58-year-old man with current alcohol, tobacco, and marijuana use and a remote gunshot wound presented to his primary care clinic after a three-year lapse in follow-up. He reported a new gluteal “blistering rash” of an unknown duration that caused discomfort with ambulation and dyschezia for at least three weeks' duration. He denied hematochezia or changes in bowel habits, but he noted clear discharge overlying the rash that could be wiped away, which was worsening over time. He reported unprotected intercourse with multiple female partners in the preceding six months and denied receptive anal intercourse. He additionally denied any prior history of lesions or skin changes to the genital and perineal areas.

Examination revealed there were tender, verrucous, coalescent, hypopigmented, exophytic perianal masses bilaterally anterosuperior to the anus with ulceration, raised borders, and an overlying mucinous layer (Fig. 1A).

Given concern for malignancy, given his risk factors of multiple sexual partners, age, no prior screening colonoscopies, and prolonged tobacco use he was urgently referred for biopsy and underwent sexually transmitted infection (STI) testing. HIV testing was negative. Before the scheduled biopsy, laboratory results revealed a reactive rapid plasma reagin (RPR) titer of 1:128. He was diagnosed with late secondary syphilis and treated with a 14-day course of doxycycline due to a penicillin G shortage. Within one week, the lesions and associated pain resolved (Fig. 1B), and RPR titer demonstrated a fourfold decline (1:32) at three-month follow-up. Biopsy was ultimately deferred.

Discussion

Syphilis remains the “great imitator,” capable of mimicking neoplastic, inflammatory, and other infectious conditions. Although secondary syphilis typically presents with papulosquamous or mucocutaneous lesions, atypical morphologies such as verrucous or annular lesions in intertriginous or anogenital region, can closely resemble carcinoma in both appearance and texture [1]. The firmness, verrucous appearance, and ulceration of the lesions, along with their asymmetric distribution and lack of typical moist, flat morphology, were atypical for condyloma lata and raised concern for anal carcinoma. Although condyloma lata were considered in the differential, the raised, ulcerated, and indurated appearance of the lesions was inconsistent with their usual smooth, flat morphology. These features prompted concern for an underlying neoplastic process and the decision to pursue biopsy.

This case illustrates that syphilitic lesions may clinically and histologically mimic anal cancer, even in patients without classic risk factors such as receptive anal intercourse, HIV infection, or prior STIs [2], [3]. Early serologic testing for syphilis can prevent unnecessary biopsy and oncologic evaluation [4]. Clinicians should maintain a broad differential diagnosis for perianal masses or ulcers and include syphilis testing early in the diagnostic process.

Absence of gravitationally induced entanglement in certain semi-classical theories of gravity
Ward Struyve

Abstract
Bose et al. and Marletto and Vedral proposed an experiment to test whether gravity can induce entanglement between massive systems, arguing that the capacity to do so would imply the quantum nature of gravity. In this work, a class of semi-classical models is examined that treat gravity classically, through some potential in the Schrödinger equation, and it is shown that these models do not generate entanglement. This class includes the Newton-Schrödinger model, where gravity is sourced by the wave function, the Bohmian analogue, where gravity is sourced by actual point-particles, and an interpolating model proposed by Döner and Großardt. These models are analyzed in the context of the proposed experiment and contrasted with the standard Newtonian potential, which does generate entanglement.



1
Introduction

One of the central open problems in theoretical physics is the reconciliation of quantum theory with gravity. The quantum description of matter requires that the classical framework of general relativity must, at some level, be modified or extended. One possibility, which has been mostly investigated, is that gravity itself should be described by a quantum theory. This approach is pursued in for example the Wheeler-DeWitt theory, loop quantum gravity and string theory
[
1
]
. An alternative possibility is that gravity remains fundamentally classical, in which case the question arises of how exactly the gravitational field couples to quantum matter. Various ideas for such semi-classical models have been explored, see e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

To probe this problem experimentally, Bose
et al.

[
8
]
and Marletto and Vedral
[
9
]
(BMV) have proposed a feasible tabletop experiment to test whether gravity can induce entanglement between massive systems (see
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
for recent reviews). They argued that the capacity to do so would imply the quantum nature of gravity. However, this claim has been challenged by several authors, who argued that the generation of entanglement does not necessarily constitutes evidence of quantumness
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. In particular, Döner and Großardt
[
15
]
claimed to present a counterexample in the form of a model that treats gravity classically yet still generates entanglement. The aim of this paper is to show that their model in fact does not generate entanglement and hence does constitute a counterexample.

More generally, in the context of the weak-field Newtonian regime, we will consider a class of semi-classical models where the gravitational interaction is modeled by a potential in the Schrödinger equation. The potential may depend on the wave function, leading to a non-linear dynamics. However, because the potential is additively separable, these models do not generate entanglement. Examples are the Newton-Schrödinger (NS) model
[
17
,
18
]
, its Bohmian analogue
[
19
]
, which will be refered to as the Newton-Schrödinger-Bohm (NSB) model, and the interpolating model proposed by Döner and Großardt.

Since, the BMV experiment has not yet been realized, it is of interest to analyze and compare existing semi-classical models in this context. Here, we will examine the NS and NSB models and contrast them with the standard Newtonian potential which does generate entanglement, using the entanglement witness proposed by Chevalier
et al.

[
20
]
. These models predict a different behaviour of the witness, so that the BMV experiment could in principle discriminate between them.

2
Absence of entanglement generation

In the models considered here, systems are described by a wave function
\psi_{t}(x)
, with
x=({\bf x}_{1},\dots,{\bf x}_{N})
, and a configuration
Q_{t}
, like for example particle positions. The wave function satisfies the non-relativistic Schrödinger equation

\textrm{i}\hbar\partial_{t}\psi_{t}(x)=\left[-\sum^{N}_{i=1}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{i}}\nabla_{i}^{2}+V(x,t;\psi_{t},Q_{t})\right]\psi_{t}(x),

(1)

with a potential that may depend on
\psi_{t}
and
Q_{t}
, so that the dynamics may be non-linear. The dynamics of
Q_{t}
may also depend on
\psi_{t}
. The potentials that will be considered are the Newtonian gravitational potential

V_{\textrm{N}}(x)=-\frac{G}{2}\sum^{N}_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}m_{i}m_{j}\frac{1}{|{\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{j}|},

(2)

the NS potential

V_{\textrm{NS}}(x;{\psi}_{t})=-G\sum^{N}_{i,j=1}m_{i}m_{j}\int d^{3}x^{\prime}_{1}\dots d^{3}x^{\prime}_{N}\frac{|{\psi}_{t}({\bf x}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,{\bf x}^{\prime}_{N})|^{2}}{|{\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}^{\prime}_{j}|},

(3)

where
\psi
is assumed to be normalized, and the NSB potential

V_{\textrm{NSB}}(x;X_{t})=-G\sum^{N}_{i,j=1}m_{i}m_{j}\frac{1}{|{\bf x}_{i}-{\bf X}_{j,t}|},

(4)

where
X_{t}=({\bf X}_{1,t},\dots,{\bf X}_{N,t})
is a configuration of particle positions at time
t
, with dynamics

\dot{\bf X}_{i,t}=\frac{\hbar}{m_{i}}{\textrm{Im}}\left(\frac{{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}_{i}\psi_{t}(x)}{\psi_{t}(x)}\right)\Bigg|_{x=X_{t}}.

(5)

The potentials
V_{\textrm{NS}}
and
V_{\textrm{NSB}}
correspond to Newtonian gravitational potentials sourced by respectively the mass densities

\rho_{\textrm{NS}}({\bf x},t)=\sum^{N}_{i=1}m_{i}\int d^{3}x^{\prime}_{1}\dots d^{3}x^{\prime}_{N}\delta({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}_{i})|{\psi}_{t}({\bf x}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,{\bf x}^{\prime}_{N})|^{2}

(6)

and

\rho_{\textrm{NSB}}({\bf x},t)=\sum^{N}_{i=1}m_{i}\delta({\bf x}-{\bf X}_{i,t}),

(7)

so that gravity is treated classically.

Whether or not the dynamics generates entanglement depends on the form of the potential. If the potential is additively separable, i.e.,

V(x,t;\psi_{t},Q_{t})=\sum^{N}_{i=1}V_{i}({\bf x}_{i},t;\psi_{t},Q_{t}),

(8)

then the dynamics does not generate entanglement. That is, an initially separable wave function

\psi_{0}(x)=\prod^{N}_{i=1}\psi_{i,0}({\bf x}_{i})

(9)

will remain separable at all times. This would be immediately clear if the potential did not depend on
\psi
, neither directly nor indirectly through a
\psi
-dependent dynamics of
Q
. But the statement is also valid in the case of a
\psi
-dependent potential. To see this, consider first a fixed
({\bar{\psi}}_{t},{\bar{Q}}_{t})
(say for
t\geqslant 0
). Then, the Schrödinger equation

\textrm{i}\hbar\partial_{t}\psi_{t}(x)=\sum^{N}_{i=1}{\widehat{H}}_{i}\left(t;{\bar{\psi}}_{t},{\bar{Q}}_{t}\right)\psi_{t}(x),

(10)

with

{\widehat{H}}_{i}\left(t;{\bar{\psi}}_{t},{\bar{Q}}_{t}\right)=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{i}}\nabla_{i}^{2}+V_{i}({\bf x}_{i},t;{\bar{\psi}}_{t},{\bar{Q}}_{t}),

(11)

has a
\psi
-independent potential and does not generate entanglement. The solution for an initially separable wave function
\psi_{0}
is given by

\psi_{t}(x)=\prod^{N}_{i=1}\psi_{i,t}({\bf x}_{i}),

(12)

where
\psi_{i,t}({\bf x}_{i})
is the solution of

\textrm{i}\hbar\partial_{t}\psi_{i,t}(x)={\widehat{H}}_{i}\left(t;{\bar{\psi}}_{t},{\bar{Q}}_{t}\right)\psi_{i,t}(x),

(13)

with initial wave function
\psi_{i,0}
. Consider
({\bar{\psi}}_{t},{\bar{Q}}_{t})
now to be a solution of (
1
) with
V
an additively separable potential and with initial state
({\bar{\psi}}_{0},{\bar{Q}}_{0})
. Then
{\bar{\psi}}_{t}
will also be a solution of (
10
). Since it was just established that (
10
) does not generate entanglement,
{\bar{\psi}}_{t}
will not be entangled if
{\bar{\psi}}_{0}
is not entangled, and hence (
1
) does not generate entanglement either.

Another way to establish the absence of entanglement generation is to consider the formal solution of (
1
) in terms of a Dyson series. This has been applied for particular semi-classical models in
[
13
,
21
]
. For an initial wave function
\psi_{0}
, the solution of (
10
) can be written as the Dyson series

\psi_{t}(x)=U_{t}({\bar{\psi}},{\bar{Q}})\psi_{0}(x)={\mathcal{T}}\textrm{e}^{-\textrm{i}\int^{t}_{t_{0}}dt^{\prime}\sum^{N}_{i=1}{\widehat{H}}_{i}\left(t^{\prime};{\bar{\psi}}_{t^{\prime}},{\bar{Q}}_{t^{\prime}}\right)}\psi_{0}(x).

(14)

Since

\left[{\widehat{H}}_{i}\left(t;{\bar{\psi}}_{t},{\bar{Q}}_{t}\right),{\widehat{H}}_{j}\left(t^{\prime};{\bar{\psi}}_{t^{\prime}},{\bar{Q}}_{t^{\prime}}\right)\right]=0,

(15)

the unitary operator
U_{t}({\bar{\psi}},{\bar{Q}})
can be written as

U_{t}({\bar{\psi}},{\bar{Q}})=\bigotimes^{N}_{i=1}U_{i,t}({\bar{\psi}},{\bar{Q}}),

(16)

with

U_{i,t}({\bar{\psi}},{\bar{Q}})={\mathcal{T}}\textrm{e}^{-\textrm{i}\int^{t}_{t_{0}}dt^{\prime}{\widehat{H}}_{i}\left(t^{\prime};{\bar{\psi}}_{t^{\prime}},{\bar{Q}}_{t^{\prime}}\right)}.

(17)

Because of (
16
), if
\psi_{0}
is separable, then
\psi_{t}
will be as well. Moreover, if
\psi_{0}
is entangled, then the degree of entanglement, as measured by the entanglement entropy, will not change in time
[
22
]
. As before, we can now consider
({\bar{\psi}}_{t},{\bar{Q}}_{t})
to be a solution of (
1
) to establish that the dynamics (
1
) does not generate entanglement and does not change the degree of entanglement in the case of an initially entangled state.

Of the potentials considered above, only the Newtonian potential generates entanglement; the potentials
V_{\textrm{NS}}
and
V_{\textrm{NSB}}
are additively separable and do not generate entanglement. The potential
V_{R}
proposed by Döner and Großardt
[
15
, eq. (23)]
, which forms an interpolation between
V_{\textrm{NS}}
and
V_{\textrm{NSB}}
, is also additively separable and hence does not generate entanglement either.

While the potentials
V_{\textrm{NS}}
and
V_{\textrm{NSB}}
do not generate entanglement, it is not the case that systems evolve independently. In both cases, the dynamics of a single particle wave function will depend on the wave functions of the other particles. This dependence may be non-local and allow for faster-than-light signaling. (This is not an artifact of dealing with the non-relativistic Schrödinger equation, but would also be present in similar theories bases on relativistic wave equations like the Dirac equation.) As such, these models violate the locality assumption that is sometimes invoked to argue that entanglement cannot be created by means of classical mediators
[
8
,
23
,
11
,
12
]
(e.g. in the form of the LOCC assumption that only local operations and classical communication are allowed).

3
Experimental setup

The BMV proposal to test gravitationally induced entanglement concerns two massive systems, separated by a distance
d
, which are each held in a superposition of two states
|L\rangle
and
|R\rangle
, separated by a distance
\delta
. The initial state is of the product form

|\psi_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(|L\rangle_{1}+|R\rangle_{1}\right)\left(|L\rangle_{2}+|R\rangle_{2}\right).

(18)

The centers of the wave functions
\psi_{iL}({\bf x})=\langle{\bf x}|L\rangle_{i}
and
\psi_{iR}({\bf x})=\langle{\bf x}|R\rangle_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are assumed to be aligned along, say, the
x
-direction, represented by the unit-vector
{\bf e}_{x}
. Denoting these centers by
{\bf X}_{iL}
and
{\bf X}_{iR}
, they are taken to be

\displaystyle{\bf X}_{1L}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{d}{2}-\frac{\delta}{2}\right){\bf e}_{x},\quad\>{\bf X}_{1R}=\left(-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\right){\bf e}_{x},

\displaystyle{\bf X}_{2L}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{\delta}{2}\right){\bf e}_{x},\quad\quad{\bf X}_{2R}=\left(\frac{d}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\right){\bf e}_{x}.

(19)

It is assumed that the wave functions
\psi_{iL}({\bf x})
and
\psi_{iR}({\bf x})
,
i=1,2
, are well-localized so that their overlap is negligible and
|\psi_{0}\rangle
is approximately normalized.

Assuming that, during the run of the experiment, the kinetic part in the Hamiltonian can be ignored and that the spatial and temporal variation of the potential
V({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2})
is negligible within the bulk of the support of each term
\psi_{1k}({\bf x}_{1})\psi_{2l}({\bf x}_{2})
in (
18
),
k,l=L,R
, the dynamics will merely produce phase shifts for each term, given by

\textrm{e}^{-\textrm{i}\Delta_{kl}t/\hbar},

(20)

with

\Delta_{kl}=V({\bf X}_{1k},{\bf X}_{2l}),

(21)

so that

|\psi_{t}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l=L,R}\textrm{e}^{-\textrm{i}\Delta_{kl}t/\hbar}|k\rangle_{1}|l\rangle_{2}.

(22)

(In an actual experimental setup, there might be motion orthogonal to the
x
-direction, as in the case of a free-fall setup, but that could easily be taken into account.) In the case of an additively separable potential,

\Delta_{kl}=V_{1}({\bf X}_{1k})+V_{2}({\bf X}_{2l}),

(23)

so that

|\psi_{t}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\textrm{e}^{-\textrm{i}V_{1}({\bf X}_{1L})t/\hbar}|L\rangle_{1}+\textrm{e}^{-\textrm{i}V_{1}({\bf X}_{1R})t/\hbar}|R\rangle_{1}\right]\left[\textrm{e}^{-\textrm{i}V_{2}({\bf X}_{2L})t/\hbar}|L\rangle_{2}+\textrm{e}^{-\textrm{i}V_{2}({\bf X}_{2R})t/\hbar}|R\rangle_{2}\right],

(24)

which remains non-entangled.

In the case of the Newtonian potential, the quantum state is
[
8
]
:

|\psi_{t}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d}}|L\rangle_{1}|L\rangle_{2}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d+\delta}}|L\rangle_{1}|R\rangle_{2}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d-\delta}}|R\rangle_{1}|L\rangle_{2}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d}}|R\rangle_{1}|R\rangle_{2}\right],

(25)

where
\gamma=Gm_{1}m_{2}/\hbar
. This state is entangled, except for particular values of
t
.

The NS and NSB model can easily be analyzed if the self-interaction is dropped from the potential, i.e., by restricting the sums over
i,j
in (
3
) and (
4
) over
i\neq j
. In the two-particle case, these potentials are

{\widetilde{V}}_{\textrm{NS}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2};{\psi}_{t})=-Gm_{1}m_{2}\int d^{3}x^{\prime}_{1}d^{3}x^{\prime}_{2}|{\psi}_{t}({\bf x}^{\prime}_{1},{\bf x}^{\prime}_{2})|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf x}^{\prime}_{2}|}+\frac{1}{|{\bf x}_{2}-{\bf x}^{\prime}_{1}|}\right),

(26)

{\widetilde{V}}_{\textrm{NSB}}({\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2};{\bf X}_{1,t},{\bf X}_{2,t})=-Gm_{1}m_{2}\left(\frac{1}{|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf X}_{2,t}|}+\frac{1}{|{\bf x}_{2}-{\bf X}_{1,t}|}\right).

(27)

Consider first the NS model. Since the spatial variation of
V_{\textrm{NS}}
within the bulk of the support of each term
\psi_{1k}({\bf x}_{1})\psi_{2k}({\bf x}_{2})
, with
k,l=L,R
, can be assumed negligible, there will not be temporal variation either at least over sufficiently short time intervals. This is because the wave functions
\psi_{1k}({\bf x}_{1})\psi_{2k}({\bf x}_{2})
have approximately disjoint report, and hence if the dynamics merely generates relative phases, then
|\psi_{t}|^{2}\approx|\psi_{0}|^{2}
. As a result,

|\psi_{t}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{2\gamma t}{d}}\left(\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d+\delta}}|L\rangle_{1}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d-\delta}}|R\rangle_{1}\right)\left(\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d-\delta}}|L\rangle_{2}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d+\delta}}|R\rangle_{2}\right).

(28)

In the case of the NSB model, the potential depends on the actual trajectories
{\bf X}_{1,t}
and
{\bf X}_{2,t}
. These will be in the support of one of the wave functions
\psi_{1k}\psi_{2l}
, say
\psi_{1m}\psi_{2n}
and will remain so under mere relative phase development. Because of the narrowness of the wave functions
\psi_{1k}\psi_{2l}
, the configuration can be approximated by the center of
\psi_{1m}\psi_{2n}
, i.e.,
({\bf X}_{1,t},{\bf X}_{2,t})\approx({\bf X}_{1m},{\bf X}_{2n})
. There are four possible choices of configurations
({\bf X}_{1m},{\bf X}_{2n})
, each occurring with equal probability 1/4 over an ensemble (assuming that the initial configuration is distributed according to
|\psi|^{2}
). Each choice will lead to particular phases

\Delta^{mn}_{kl}={\widetilde{V}}_{\textrm{NSB},1}({\bf X}_{1k};{\bf X}_{2n})+{\widetilde{V}}_{\textrm{NSB},2}({\bf X}_{2l};{\bf X}_{1m}),

(29)

where

{\widetilde{V}}_{\textrm{NSB},1}({{\bf x}_{1};{\bf X}_{2n}})=-Gm_{1}m_{2}\frac{1}{|{\bf x}_{1}-{\bf X}_{2n}|},\quad{\widetilde{V}}_{\textrm{NSB},2}({{\bf x}_{2};{\bf X}_{1m}})=-Gm_{1}m_{2}\frac{1}{|{\bf X}_{1m}-{\bf x}_{2}|}.

(30)

Denoting the corresponding wave functions by
|\psi^{mn}\rangle
, we have that

\displaystyle|\psi^{LL}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d}}|L\rangle_{1}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d-\delta}}|R\rangle_{1}\right)\left(\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d}}|L\rangle_{2}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d+\delta}}|R\rangle_{2}\right),

\displaystyle|\psi^{LR}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d+\delta}}|L\rangle_{1}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d}}|R\rangle_{1}\right)\left(\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d}}|L\rangle_{2}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d+\delta}}|R\rangle_{2}\right),

\displaystyle|\psi^{RL}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d}}|L\rangle_{1}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d-\delta}}|R\rangle_{1}\right)\left(\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d-\delta}}|L\rangle_{2}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d}}|R\rangle_{2}\right),

\displaystyle|\psi^{RR}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d+\delta}}|L\rangle_{1}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d}}|R\rangle_{1}\right)\left(\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d-\delta}}|L\rangle_{2}+\textrm{e}^{\textrm{i}\frac{\gamma t}{d}}|R\rangle_{2}\right).

(31)

Hence the ensemble is described by a statistical mixture of the (non-entangled) states
|\psi^{mn}\rangle
, each occurring with equal probability.

The potential
V_{R}

Identification of SNPs and INDELS associated with duck egg quality traits through a genome-wide association analysis☆
Authors: Min Zhang, Lizhi Lu, Yan Li, Qiong Wu, Yanhui Liu, Hongfei Liu, Hehe Tang, Rulong Lin, Hongping Chen, Tao Zeng, Yong Tian, Yuting Yan, Yanning Wei, Chenyu Ren, Wenfu Li, Min Liu, Jie Yu, Jiawen Liu, Xin Lin, Guanghua Zeng, Chunmei Cheng, Xiaobing Jiang, Yanfa Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Egg quality traits are economically important in the poultry industry. To explore the genetic architecture and identify potential candidate genes, a genome-wide association study (GWAS) was performed for 13 egg quality traits using data from whole-genome sequencing of 299 Longyan Shan-ma female ducks, including 12 quantitative traits and one qualitative trait, eggshell color (ESC; white, light green, green). From estimation of pedigree genetic parameters, heritability (h2) ranged from 0.022 to 0.996 for the 12 quantitative traits, with the highest h2 (0.996) for eggshell color a* value (ESCA) and the lowest h2 (0.022) for egg yolk percentage relative to EW. A total of 8,874 single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP)-based significant associations (1.0 × 10−6) and 247 insertion-deletion (indel)-based significant associations (1.00 × 10−5) were identified, including 5,980 SNPs and 159 indel markers. From 5,924 SNPs and 143 indels associated with ESC traits, 181 potential candidate genes were identified, and most significant SNPs and indels (P < 1.0 × 10−20) were located at 1.86 Mb (44.29−46.15 Mb) on chromosome 4. The top SNP (chr4:45325309:C>A; P = 7.97 × 10−43) and the top indel (chr4:45299595:delTTCCACTCCAC; P = 4.20 × 10−36) for the ESC a* value were within two known ESC candidate genes; ATP-binding cassette subfamily G member 2 (ABCG2) and protein kinase cGMP-dependent 2 (PRKG2). Of 56 SNPs and 16 indels associated with other egg quality traits, 46 potential candidate genes were identified including synapse differentiation-inducing 1-like (SYNDIG1L) for EW, and core histone macro-H2A.1 (LOC101795967) and neurogenin 1 (NEUROG1) for egg shape index; and four genes including collagen type VI alpha 3 chain (COL6A3), lysine demethylase 7A (KDM7A), LOC101802169, and sperm-associated antigen 16 (SPAG16) for egg yolk weight and the percentage of yolk to total egg weight. Of the 46 genes, the molecular functions of 22 are related to protein binding, indicating important roles in the formation of egg quality traits. Our findings provide new insight into the genetic basis of egg quality traits in ducks.

Introduction

The duck egg industry is second only to chicken egg production (Chang et al., 2024). China has the largest duck egg industry in the world, producing >2.6 million tons of duck eggs annually (Hou and Liu, 2024). Duck eggs are rich in proteins, amino acids, vitamins, fatty acids, and various essential nutrients (Huang and Lin, 2011; Baéza and Huang, 2022). Besides egg production traits, egg quality traits are a major focus. They are economically important and related to commercial egg and hatching breeding values, therefore poultry breeders pay particular attention (Dunn, 2011). Egg quality traits include both internal and external quality traits such as egg weight (EW), eggshell color (ESC), egg shape index (ESI), eggshell thickness (EST), eggshell strength (ESS), albumen height (AH), Haugh units (HU), egg yolk color (EYC), and egg yolk weight (EYW), as well as the percentage of yolk to total egg weight (EYP) (Liu et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2021c). Genetics, feed, intestinal microbes, and other factors have effects on egg quality traits (Goto and Tsudzuki, 2017; Shi et al., 2022), which are complex are controlled by multiple genes in ducks.

In farm animals, genome-wide association studies (GWAS) are widely applied to dissect the genomic variants associated with phenotypes, and explore the genetic and molecular bases underlying various traits (Tan et al., 2023). Using GWAS, many single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and candidate genes of numerous traits have been identified in ducks, including appearance traits (Zhou et al., 2018; Xi et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2023b; Zhang et al., 2023b; Zhang et al., 2024), growth and carcass traits (Zhou et al., 2018; Deng et al., 2019; Zhu et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2021b; Guo et al., 2022b), meat quality traits (Deng et al., 2020; Guo et al., 2022a), and egg production and quality traits (Chen et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021a; Liu et al., 2021b; Liu et al., 2021c; Xu et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2023c). Regarding egg quality traits in ducks, analysis of pedigree-based genetic parameters showed that heritability (h2) ranged from 0.26 to 0.71 in 352 F2 laying ducks generated by reciprocal crosses between mallards and Pekin ducks (Liu et al., 2021c). A similar h2 range (0.21 to 0.70) was observed for egg quality traits in 235 Longyan Shan-Ma ducks (Sun et al., 2023b). For ESC traits, SNP variants located in the cis‐regulatory region of the ATP-binding cassette subfamily G member 2 (ABCG2) gene causing the blue eggshell phenotype were identified through GWAS and other analyses (Chen et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021a). For AU and HU traits, one candidate region at 5.8 Mb (14.7−20.5 Mb) on chromosome 5 was identified in F2 laying ducks via GWAS, and two genes (MUC6 and LDLRAD3) among 111 potential candidate genes were detected using locus zoom analysis and conditional GWAS (Liu et al., 2021c). For ESI, EST, and YC traits, only seven genome-wide significant SNPs were identified in Longyan Shan-Ma ducks using GWAS with 62,706 SNPs genotyped through genotyping-by-sequencing technology (Sun et al., 2023b). Despite the above progress, the genetic mechanisms of egg equality traits besides ESC traits remain unclear.

Longyan Shan-ma ducks, among the most well-known breeds of laying ducks in China, have low body weight and high egg production (Sun et al., 2020), with >300 million raised in southern China (Xia et al., 2015; Xia et al., 2022). Previous GWAS on the egg quality traits of Longyan Shan-ma ducks using a limited number of ducks (235) and SNP markers (62,706) identified only a few egg quality trait-associated SNPs and potential candidate genes (Sun et al., 2023b). Therefore, in the present study, more individuals (299) and more markers, including 4,025,624 SNPs and 178,511 insertions-deletions (Indels), were employed to conduct GWAS on egg quality, using genotyping via whole-genome sequencing (WGS) technology, to identify candidate genes and uncover the genetic basis of egg quality traits in ducks.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

This trial protocol was approved by the Longyan University Ethics Committee (Longyan, China). Blood samples were collected according to the Guidelines for Experimental Animals established by the Ministry of Science and Technology (Beijing, China).

Birds and phenotypic measurements

Female Longyan Shan-ma ducks of the sixth-generation high yield line were obtained from the Longyan Shan-ma Duck Original Breeding Farm (Longyan, China). During the starting and growing periods, 20 birds were raised in a large cage. At ∼80 days old, birds were moved into individual cages under the same routine conditions of nutrition, vaccination, and management.

At 43 week of age, fresh eggs were collected, and egg quality traits were recorded and measured, including EW, ESC, ESI, EST, ESS, AH, HU, EYC, EYW, and EYP. The ESC of each egg was recorded as white, light green, or green. The L*, a*, and b* color values of ESC were measured according to the CIELab color space system using a WSC-S Color Difference Meter (Shanghai Shenguang Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). ESI was determined using a Micrometer (Mitutoyo Precision Instrument (Shanghai) Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) according to the ratio of egg width to length. EST at the blunt end, sharp end, and middle position of the eggshell, and the average value of the shell thickness at the three positions, was used as EST, determined by measuring with a TQ-1A eggshell thickness gauge (Nanjing Ming Ao Instrument Equipment Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China). ESS was measured using an KQ-1A Eggshell Strength Tester (Tenovo International Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Albumen quality was assessed using AH and HU. Two positions of albumen height were evaluated with a tripod (Mitutoyo Precision Instrument (Shanghai) Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China), and average values were recorded as AU. HU was calculated from AH using egg weight and the formula HU = 100log (AH - 1.7EW0.37 + 7.57). EYC was assessed using a Yolk Color Chart (Robotmation Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). Egg yolk was separated using an egg yolk separator and weighed to give EYW. The percentage of egg yolk relative to total egg weight was recorded as EYP.

Genotyping, imputation, and quality control

Blood samples from each duck were collected from the brachial vein of the wing using citrate-based anticoagulant syringes. Genomic DNA (gDNA) was extracted from whole blood via the traditional phenol-chloroform method. Next, paired-end WGS with 150 bp was performed using the Illumina Novaseq6000 platform at Novogene Corporation (Beijing, China). Raw sequence data reported have been deposited in the Genome Sequence Archive (GSA) of the National Genomics Data Center, China National Center for Bioinformation (CNCB; https://www.cncb.ac.cn/) (Partners, 2024) under GSA access code CRA011756.

After elimination of adapters, poly-N reads, and low-quality reads from the raw data, clean data were obtained and aligned against the duck reference genome (ZJU1.0) (https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genomes/all/GCF/015/476/345/GCF_015476345.1_ZJU1.0/GCF_015476345.1_ZJU1.0_genomic.fna.gz) with the parameters “mem -t 4 -k 32 -M” using Burrows-Wheeler Aligner software (Li and Durbin, 2009). The raw SNP sets from the duck population were called by SAMtools software (Li et al., 2009) and annotated using ANNOVAR (Wang et al., 2010).

Quality control of samples and markers was conducted using Plink software version 1.9 (Renteria et al., 2013). Data not meeting one or more of the following criteria were removed: sample call rate < 80%, markers call rate < 90%, minor allele frequency (MAF) < 5%, Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) test P < 10−6, and markers on the Z chromosome. Finally, missing genotypes were imputed using Beagle software version 5.4 (22Jul22.46e) (Browning et al., 2018) with default parameters.

Population structure testing

Principal component (PC) analysis was performed using all autosomal SNPs and Plink 1.9 software. The top 20 PCs were calculated for each sample, and a plot was drawn using PC1 as the x-axis and PC2 as the y-axis with R software (version 4.2.3, https://www.r-project.org/). PC significance tests were conducted using EIGENSTRAT software (version 6.1.4) (Price et al., 2006).

Phenotypic data and genetic parameter estimation

For 12 quantitative traits, descriptive statistics were calculated using Minitab statistical software (version 17.0 for Windows; Minitab, LLC., USA State College, PA, USA), and all phenotypic data within three standard deviations on either side of the mean were retained. One qualitative trait, ESC, was subjected to Chi-square test.

Genetic parameters for 12 quantitative traits based on the pedigree were estimated using the ASReml-R package (version 4.2.0.276) (VSN China Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). The h2 value, genetic correlation (rg), and phenotypic correlation (rp) were estimated using single-trait and two-trait animal models and the following formula:(1)y=Xb+Zu+ewherey is the vector of phenotypic traits; b and μ are the vectors of the fixed effects (population mean) and additive genetic effect, respectively; X and Z is the corresponding matrices of the fixed effects (population mean) and additive genetic effect; and e is the residual effect vector. The Wald function was used to test the significance of fixed effects, and the REML likelihood ratio test (LRT) was used to test the significant of genetic and phenotypic correlations.

Association analysis

The centered kinship matrix was calculated based on all SNPs in autosomes using GEMMA software (version 0.98.4) (Zhou and Stephens, 2012). Markers-trait association tests were performed using a univariate linear mixed model (LMM) based on all SNPs/Indels in autosomes using GEMMA software. The statistical model was calculated using the following formula:(2)y=Wα+Xβ+ewhere y is the phenotypic trait vector; W is the incidence matrix; α is the fixed effect vector (kinship); X is the SNP genotype vector; β is the SNP or indel effect; and e is the residual effect. The phenotypic values of all traits were treated as numerical values.

The P-values of associations were determined by Wald tests. Significant genome-wide association threshold P-values were 1.00 × 10−6 for SNP-based GWAS as suggested in previous studies (Xi et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024) and 1.00 × 10−5 for indel-based GWAS. Quantile-quantile (QQ) and Manhattan plots for GWAS results were generated using the CMplot package (Yin, 2020) within R software (https://www.r-project.org/).

HWE and linkage disequilibrium (LD) D' (|D'|) values for SNPs associated with quality traits were tested and estimated using the confidence intervals method (Gabriel et al., 2002) within Haploview software (version 4.2) (Barrett et al., 2005). Haplotypes were constructed on the basis of SNPs using the PHASE 2.0 program (Stephens et al., 2001). Using the general mixed procedure of SAS version 8.0 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC), associations of diplotypes with traits were analyzed using the following model:(3)y=Xβ+ewhere y is the phenotypic trait vector; X is the diplotypes vector; β is the diplotype effect; and e is the residual effect. The tabulated values were de-transformed into least square means (LSMs). Multiple comparisons were performed with LSMS using Duncan's test.

Function enrichment analyses

For egg quality trait-associated genes, gene ontology (GO) enrichment analyses and pathway enrichment analyses were carried out using web-based KOBAS software (version 3.0) (Bu et al., 2021).

For EST and ESS, strong positive phenotypic and genetic correlations (rp = 0.504 and rg = 0.705) were found. Population structure

From the population structure plot (Fig. S1), it can be seen that there was no obvious population stratification, as verified by the results of PC significance tests (P > 0.05 for all PCs; Table S4). PCs were not fitted as covariates in the GWAS analysis.

For ESC traits, 5,924 SNPs and 143 indels were significantly associated with ESC, ESCL, ESCA, and ESCB, and 181 potential candidate genes were detected (Table S5 and Table S6). For ESC, ESCL, and ESCA, 5,921 SNPs and 143 indels were associated with one or more traits, and 2,833 (83) SNPs (indels) were common markers for both ESC and ESCA, while 57 (5) SNPs (indels) were common markers for these three traits (Fig. S3). The most strongly associated common signals (chr4:45325309:C>A and chr4:45299595:delTTCCACTCCAC) for ESC, ESCL, and ESCA, which were chromosomally located, have been reported in two previous studies (Chen et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021a) and identified PPKG2 and ABCG2 as candidate genes for ESC. For ESCB, only three significantly associated SNPs (chr20:1398470:T>G, chr20:1402321:G>C, and chr22:6825190:T>C) were found, involving in two genes (Table S5): musashi RNA-binding protein 2 (MSI2) and intermediate filament family orphan 2 (IFFO2).

For the other egg quality traits, 56 SNPs and 16 indels were identified, and 46 potential candidate genes were revealed (Table 3, Table 4). For EW, four significantly associated SNPs (chr3:116941355:C>A, chr3:116941357:T>G, chr5:24938356:A>T, and chr5:28462930:A>G) located on chromosomes 3 and 5 were found. Two SNPs on chromosomes 3 formed one haplotype (Fig. S4A), and diplotypes H1H2 (CT/AG) had the highest egg weight (Fig. S5A). These variants were involved in four genes: SRY-box transcription factor 7 (SOX7), RP1-like 1 (RP1L1), synapse differentiation-inducing 1-like (SYNDIG1L), and cytosolic 5′-nucleotidase 1A (LOC101792766, also known as NT5C1A). For ESI, four significantly associated SNPs (chr14:3398602:C>T, chr14:3400514:T>A, chr14:3400609:C>T, and chr14:3405593:C>T) and one indel (chr15:4902386:delCTC) were identified. Four SNPs were located on chromosome 14, and the last three SNPs forming one haplotype (Fig. S4B) with diplotypes H1H1 (ATT/ATT) had the highest trait value, while diplotypes H3H3 (TCC/TCC) had the lowest trait value (Fig. S5B). These SNPs were near two genes: core histone macro-H2A.1 (LOC101795967) and neurogenin 1 (NEUROG1). The single indel in the intron of calpain 15 (CAPN15) was located on chromosome 15.

For ESS, only one significantly associated SNP (chr15:15964067:A>G) was detected, located on chromosome 15 near two genes: lipase maturation factor 1 (LMF1) and uncharacterized gene LOC101797262. For EST, seven SNPs (chr2:26258857:T>C, chr2:26259093:G>A, chr2:26260911:T>C, chr2:26261080:C>A, chr7:31872667:G>A, chr14:691339:C>T, and chr14:1245638:C>G) significantly associated with EST were located on chromosomes 2, 7, and 14, along with two indels (chr2:130010663:insCACT and chr24:5664096:delCAC) located on chromosomes 2 and 24. Four SNPs on chromosome 2 forming one haplotype were identified (Fig. S4C), with diplotypes H1H1 having the lowest trait value and H3H3 the highest (Fig. S5C). These SNPs were related to eight genes: RAD54 homolog B (RAD54B), GTP-binding protein overexpressed in skeletal muscle (GEM), erb-b2 receptor tyrosine kinase 4 (ERBB4), PRELI domain-containing 2 (PRELID2), potassium channel tetramerization domain-containing 16 (KCTD16), Rho GTPase-activating protein 26 (ARHGAP26), sperm microtubule inner protein 4 (C2H7orf31, also known as SPMIP4), and small nuclear ribonucleoprotein U5 subunit 40 (SNRNP40). Two among 13 EST-associated genes, RAD54B and KCTD16, perform key roles in uterine functional maintenance in laying periods (Sun et al., 2023a), ion transportation (Liu et al., 2013), and shell strength (Liu et al., 2023), respectively.

For albumen quality traits, 38 markers significantly associated with AH and HU were detected. In particular, 10 SNPs and one indel were located at 15.57 Mb ranging from 185.42 to 201.00 Mb on chromosome 1 (seven SNPs forming one haplotype; Fig. S4D), and 21 SNPs and three indels 5.22 Mb ranged from 111.32 to 116.55 Mb on chromosome 2 (19 SNPs forming three haplotypes; Fig. S4E). Four diplotype chromosomes 1 and 2 were found (Fig. S5D-5G), and H3H3 (GCGCGTA/ GCGCGTA), H1H1 (GG/GG), H2H2 (ATGGG/ ATGGG), and H2H2 (CAAAACGCTTAT) had the highest HU values. These variants were associated with nine genes: fms-related receptor tyrosine kinase 1 (FLT1), poly(A) specific ribonuclease subunit PAN3 (PAN3), coiled-coil domain-containing protein 81-like (LOC119717116), family with sequence similarity 181 member B (FAM181B), RAB30, RAS oncogene family member RAB30, ataxin 1 (ATXN1), ring finger protein 182 (RNF182), mitochondrial calcium uniporter regulator 1 (MCUR1), and nuclear factor of activated T cells 1 (NFATC1). Moreover, one SNP (chr2:46593766:T>A on chromosome 2), one SNP (chr5:63654522:G>A on chromosome 5), and three SNPs (chr13:2670000:G>A, chr13:2672242:C>T, and chr13:3359102:G>A on chromosome 13) were also found, related to five genes: desmoglein 2 (DSG2), TUB bipartite transcription factor (TUB), contactin-4 (LOC101793345), contactin-6 (CNTN6), and cell adhesion molecule L1-like (CHL1).

For yolk quality traits, five SNPs, and nine indels located on chromosomes 1, 2,7, and 11 were significantly associated with EYW and EYP. Two SNPs (chr7:31261801:T>C and chr7:31261802:G>A) on chromosome 7 form one haplotype (Fig. S4F). Diplotype H1H1 (TG/TG) had the highest trait value and H2H2 (CACA) had the lowest. Of 14 genes near these markers, five were differentially expressed in ovarian tissues between higher and lower egg production in Longyan Shan-ma ducks (Sun et al., 2020), namely collagen type VI alpha 3 chain (COL6A3; log2FC = -1.22, P = 3.12 × 10−3), lysine demethylase 7A (KDM7A; log2FC = -1.32, P = 1.68 × 10−2), LOC101802169 (log2FC = -1.67, P = 9.64 × 10−3), poly(rC) binding protein 3 (PCBP3; log2FC = 1.82, P = 1.96 × 10−2) and sperm-associated antigen 16 (SPAG16; log2FC = 1.69, P = 3.97 × 10−2), indicating that these five genes may affect EYW and EYP. For EYC, there was only one associated indel (chr2:147837215:insCTT) identified within the intron of the MAGUK p55 scaffold protein 7 (MPP7) gene located on chromosome 2.

Function enrichment analysis results

Because some of the genetic bases of ESC traits have been uncovered in previous studies (Chen et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021a), function furnishment analyses were carried out for the 46 non-ESC trait-associated genes. The 22 GO terms of these genes with false discovery rate (FDR) corrected P <0.05 are listed in Table S7. Importantly, 22 genes were significantly enriched in the protein-binding molecular function term (FDR corrected P = 1.76 × 10−2), suggesting their potential role in determining egg quality traits. Moreover, 15 genes were significantly enriched in the nucleus (P = 1.76 × 10−2) and 13 in the cytosol (FDR corrected P = 3.89 × 10−2) cellular component terms, and five genes were significantly enriched in the nervous system development biological process term (FDR corrected P = 7.69 × 10−3). Based on the pathway functional analysis results, seven genes (NFATC1, ERBB4, FLT1, COL6A3, NT5C1A, RAD54B, and ACMSD) were enriched in six pathways (P < 0.05), namely MAPK signaling, PI3K-Akt signaling, focal adhesion, nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism, homologous recombination, and tryptophan metabolism (Table S8).

Discussion

Egg quality traits are economically important, and attract much attention in duck farming and breeding programs. With the development of genome sequencing technology, GWAS has become a powerful tool for improving important economic traits in animals (Tan et al., 2023). GWAS has identified various candidate genes influencing egg quality traits in ducks, including two genes, ABCG2 and PRKG2, influencing ESC traits (Chen et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021a). However, most candidate genes related to egg quality traits remain unknown. In this study, GWAS on egg quality traits was carried out to identify candidate genes and uncover the genetic basis of egg quality traits in ducks.

Genetic parameters

Egg quality traits with high heritability can be improved through genomic selection (Lin et al., 2016), and those with high genetic correlations can be indirectly selected (Li et al., 2020). In this study, genetic parameters were estimated for 12 quantitative egg quality traits. The h2 values (≥0.20) for EW, ESI, ESCA, EST, and AU were in the medium to high range, similar to previous studies (Liu et al., 2021c; Sun et al., 2023b), indicating the potential to improve these egg quality traits through genomic selection. Some traits had low h2, such as EYC (h2 = 0.059), which indicates that it could be mainly influenced by nutritional factors such as carotenoids in feed (Zurak et al., 2022), suggesting that genetic selection had no impact on it. High and medium phenotypic and/or genetic correlations were observed between egg quality traits including EW, EYW, and EYP (rg = 0.948, rp = 0.656, -0.223, and 0.556), EST and ESS (rp = 0.504 and rg = 0.705), and AH and HU (rg = 0.974 and rp = 0.979). Therefore, the indirect genetic selection of some egg quality traits (such as EYW and EYP) can be carried out on difficult-to-measure traits through easily measurable traits (such as EW) based on genetic correlations between these traits.

EW and ESI

In poultry, EW not only has an impact on consumption preferences, but also exerts an influence on the quality of chicks/ducklings, including hatching weight, fitness, and performance (Yi et al., 2015). This trait is a major breeding and research goal. Herein, duck EW exhibited high heritability; Heritability was estimated to be 0.473 at 43 weeks of age, similar to the heritability estimated to be 0.652 at 300 days of age (Lin et al., 2016). In this study, we identified one significantly associated gene (SYNDIG1L) for EW that was related to follicular growth and development. SYNDIG1L, also known as TMEM90A or capucin, encodes a synapse differentiation-inducing 1-like protein that belongs to the interferon-induced transmembrane family of proteins, and is involved in early follicular growth (Bonnet et al., 2015). ESI has an effect on some egg quality characteristics such as EW and egg surface area, and it could be considered for use in future breeding programs (Duman et al., 2016). In this study, there were high h2 (0.483), and high phenotypic correlation (rp = 0.201) between ESI and EW. In previous studies, one SNP (chr20:5947108:G>C) located on chromosome 20 significantly associated with ESI was identified in Longyan Shan-ma ducks (Sun et al., 2023b). In the present study, a further four significantly associated SNPs (chr14:3398602:C>T, chr14:3400514:T>A, chr14:3400609:C>T, and chr14:3405593:C>T) between LOC101795967 and NEUROG1 genes located on chromosome 14, and one indel (chr15:4902386:delCTC) at the intron of CAPN15 located on chromosome 15, were identified. LOC101795967, also known as MACROH2A1, encodes the core histone macro-H2A.1, an unusual histone variant that is critical in stabilizing cell identity, acting as a barrier to reprogramming, and as a tumor suppressor (Sun and Bernstein, 2019). NEUROG1 encodes neurogenin 1, a negative regulator of neocortical neurogenesis (Han et al., 2018). CAPN15 encodes a protein containing zinc-finger-like repeats and a calpain-like protease domain that functions in neurodevelopment (Beaman et al., 2023).

Eggshell quality

Eggshells protect the internal contents and prevent bacterial invasion during the transportation and storage of eggs (Chen et al., 2024). Therefore, eggshell quality is one of the essential selection traits in poultry breeding. In this study, 10 variants associated with ESS and EST were identified, and two trait-function-related genes (RAD54B and KCTD16) were detected. RAD54B belongs to the DEAD-like helicase superfamily, members of which are involved in homologous recombination and DNA repair (Wesoly et al., 2006). RAD54B is highly expressed in uterus at 31 weeks of age (Sun et al., 2023a). Since the avian eggshell forms in the uterus, this gene might be as a potential candidate gene mediating eggshell quality. KCTD16 encodes potassium channel tetramerization domain-containing 16, which is downregulated in the hen shell gland between high and low shell breakage strength (Liu et al., 2013), and might act as a key regulator for eggshell quality by regulating ion transportation. Only one significant variant was identified for ESS. Increasing the number of markers and birds could be an appropriate option to enhance the detection efficiency of significant loci.

In poultry, ESC is mainly determined by three kinds of pigments, protoporphyrin-IX, biliverdin-IX, and biliverdin zinc chelate, and ESC variation results from mixtures of these pigments in different proportions (Zhao et al., 2006; Liu et al., 2009; Liu et al., 2021a). In ducks, ESC varies from white to green. Chen et al. (2020) identified two cis-regulatory SNPs (G to A transitions at positions 45,318,055 and 45,318,024) upstream of ABCG2 that together cause the blue eggshell phenotype (Chen et al., 2020) in ducks. Liu et al. (2021) verified that one SNP (Chr4:47,299,976:G>A) located in the cis-regulatory region of the ABCG2 gene may cause variation in duck ESC, with the AA, GA, and AA genotypes representing green, light green, and white ESC, respectively (Liu et al., 2021a). In our study, 5,239 SNPs and 133 indels associated with ESC traits (ESC, ESCA, and ESCL) were identified (Table S5 and Table S6). Three casual SNPs for ESC identified in previous studies (Chen et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021a) were either not detected or did not reach significant levels in the present study. Interestingly, most of the variants in our study are located within or near two known casual genes, ABCG2 and PRKG2 (Table S5 and Table S6). To confirm whether a genome-wide significant locus on chromosome 4 consisted of multiple independent signals, conditional and joint analyses were performed by GCTA-COJO (Yang et al., 2011). Significant SNPs and indels associated with ESC traits in the ABCG2 and PRKG2 genes were used as conditional SNPs/indels. Condition analyses showed that there was only four top independent signals (chr4:45299595:delTTCCACTCCAC within an ABCG2 intron for ESC [P = 1.02 × 10−19] and ESCA [P = 1.72 × 10−22]; chr4:45308396:G>A within an ABCG2 intron for ESC [P = 2.98 × 10−20] and ESCL [P = 5.72 × 10−10]; chr4:45325309:C>A within a PRKG2 intron for ESCA [P = 1.71 × 10−23]; Table S9), indicating that these three variants (chr4:45299595:delTTCCACTCCAC, chr4:45308396:G>A, and chr4:45325309:C>A) may be the causal SNPs of blue ESC in Longyan Shan-ma ducks. For example, for SNP chr4:45308396:G>A, 95.68% of (light) green ESC cases were AA and AG genotypes. Therefore, determining the casual SNPs of ABCG2 for green ESC in this duck population requires further candidate-gene association studies.

Egg albumen quality

Egg albumen quality is a crucial parameter indicating egg freshness (Qu et al., 2019), and can be measured through AU and HU (Eisen et al., 1962). Among 352 F2 laying ducks, the 5.8 Mb candidate region spanning from 14.7 to 20.5 Mb on chromosome 5 was identified through GWAS for AU and HU, and two genes (MUC6 and LDLRAD3) in this region were proposed as major candidate genes (Liu et al., 2021c). In the present study, two novel candidate regions of 15.57 Mb ranging from 185.42 to 201.00 Mb on chromosome 1 and 5.22 Mb ranging from 111.32 to 116.55 Mb on chromosome 2, were revealed for AU and HU traits, harboring nine genes (FLT1, PAN3, LOC119717116, FAM181B, RAB30, ATXN1, RNF182, MCUR1, and NFATC1). The functions of these nine genes needs further investigation.

Egg yolk quality

Egg yolk quality traits are economically important because consumers usually associate the size and color of the yolk with the nutritional content of eggs. As quantitative traits, many loci have been identified for EYW, EYP, and EYC in poultry (Sun et al., 2015; Liu et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2023a). In this study, four significantly associated genes (COL6A3, KDM7A, LOC101802169, PCBP3, and SPAG16) for EYW and EYP were differentially expressed in ovarian tissue between higher and lower egg production in Longyan Shan-ma ducks (Sun et al., 2020). COL6A3 encodes collagen type VI alpha 3 chain, one of the three alpha chains of type VI collagen. The function of COL6A3 contributes to follicular development, and expression of COL6A3 was downregulated in laying chickens compared with brooding chickens (Yu et al., 2023). KDM7A encodes histone lysine demethylase 7A, involved in normal embryo development (Rissi et al., 2019). PCBP3 encodes poly(rC)-binding protein 3, a member of the KH-domain protein subfamily, which may play key roles in pre-mRNA processing and important pathways regulating embryonic development in chicken (Qiu et al., 2012). SPAG16 encodes sperm-associated antigen 16, a potential driver gene influencing both egg number and clutch size in crossbred laying hens (Isa et al., 2024). LOC101802169 as an uncharacterized gene with an unknown function. These five genes may play key roles in follicular development and embryo development in laying ducks. For EYC, only one significantly associated gene (MPP7) was found. MPP7 encodes MAGUK p55 scaffold protein 7, a member of the p55 Stardust family of membrane-associated guanylate kinase proteins. MPP7 plays an important role in cytoskeletal dynamics and cell-cell interactions (Stucke et al., 2007), which might influence the transport of nutrients or regulatory molecules that could impact pigment synthesis.

Conclusions

In summary, the h2 for 12 egg quality quantitative traits ranged from 0.022 to 0.996 in Longyan Shan-ma ducks. A total of 5,924 SNPs and 143 indels were significantly associated with ESC traits, and 56 SNPs and 16 indels were associated with EW, ESI, ESS, EST, AH, HU, EYC, EYW, and EYP traits. Two ESC trait candidate genes, ABCG2 and PRKG2, were further verified in the Longyan Shan-ma duck population. Additionally, 46 potential candidate genes related to other egg quality traits were detected, including SYNDIG1L for EW, two genes (RAD54B and KCTD16) for eggshell thickness, nine genes (FLT1, PAN3, LOC119717116, FAM181B, RAB30, ATXN1, RNF182, MCUR1, and NFATC1) for AH and HU, five genes (COL6A3, KDM7A, LOC101802169, PCBP3, and SPAG16) for EYW and EYP, and one gene (MPP7) gene for EYC. Our findings provide novel insight into the underlying genetic basis of egg quality traits in laying ducks.

Mapping the evolving trend of research on leukocyte telomere length: a text-mining study
Authors: Yuanjun Lyu, Hongjie Zhao, Guiping Zeng, Jia Yang, Qipeng Shao, Haiyang Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSubstantial evidence indicates that measuring leukocyte telomere length (LTL) is a useful tool that may be considered as a valuable biomarker of individual biological age, correlating with numerous chronic disorders. However, to date, there has been a lack of in-depth understanding regarding the current landscape and forthcoming developments in the LTL field. Therefore, this study aimed to utilize bibliometric methods to summarize the knowledge structure, current focus, and emerging directions in this field.MethodScientific publications on LTL spanning the period from 2000 to 2022 were acquired from the Web of Science Core Collection database. Several bibliometric tools including CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and an online website were utilized for bibliometric analysis. The primary evaluations encompassed investigating the major contributors and their collaborative relationships among countries/regions, institutions, and authors, conducting co-citation analyses of authors, journals, as well as reference, examining reference bursts, as well as performing co-occurrence analyses of keywords.ResultsThere are 1818 papers with 66,668 citations identified. Both the annual publication and citation counts on LTL exhibited significant upward trends. The United States emerged as the most prominent contributor, as evidenced by the greatest volume of papers and the highest H-index value. University of California San Francisco and Aviv A were identified as the most productive institution and author in this domain, respectively. Reference analysis revealed that longitudinal study and mendelian randomization study are the most concerned research method in this field recently. Keywords analysis showed that the most concerned diseases in LTL fields were aging, inflammation, cardiovascular diseases, endocrine diseases, neurological and psychiatric diseases, and cancers. In addition, the following research directions such as “COPD”, “mendelian randomization”, “adiposity”, “colorectal cancer”, “National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHNES)”, “telomerase reverse transcriptase”, “pregnancy” have garnered increasing attention in recent times and hold the potential to evolve into research foci in the foreseeable future.ConclusionThis is the first bibliometric study that provides comprehensive overview of LTL research. The findings of this study could become valuable references for investigators to explore and address the current and emerging challenges in LTL research.Keywords: Bibliometrics, Leukocyte telomere length, Knowledge landscapes, Emerging trends, CiteSpace, VOSviewer

Introduction

Telomeres are protective nucleoprotein structures situated at the termini of linear chromosomes in eukaryotic cells, consisting of repetitive sequences (TTAGGG). They play essential roles in maintaining chromosome integrity, regulating the cell division cycle, and protecting chromosomes from recombination and degradation [1]. In normal cells, it was found that telomeres get shortened progressively over time during each cellular division. Upon reaching a critical telomere length, cells either enter senescence or proceed to apoptosis. Therefore, telomeres and telomerase activity are regarded as biomarkers of cellular senescence and are correlated with various age-related disorders [2, 3]. Telomerase, a ribonucleoprotein enzyme complex, mitigates telomere attrition in dividing cells by synthesizing telomeric DNA. Although telomerase activity is typically present in early embryonic development, its activity is nearly absent in most normal adult tissues, including the kidneys, blood vessels, and peripheral white blood cells. As a consequence, telomeres shorten at a regular rate with age in leukocytes and also in other bodily tissues. However, it has been shown that cancer cells upregulate telomerase expression and evade cellular senescence, enabling them to attain unlimited replicative potential [4]. As somatic cells continuously divide, telomeres gradually shorten, and when they attain a specific degree of shortening, it may induce chromosomal instability and promote tumor development. Multiple earlier studies have demonstrated that human telomerase reverse transcriptase (hTERT) is overexpressed in 85–90% of human cancers and is crucial for sustaining cancer susceptibility [5, 6].

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) are an ideal choice for telomere research due to their ease of collection from blood and convenient handling. A study published in SCIENCE found that telomere length in 15 tissues exhibited a significant positive correlation with telomere length in whole blood cells. Thus, using telomere lengths from readily available whole blood cells could be representative of telomere lengths in tissues that are more difficult to obtain [7]. In addition, it has been established that leukocyte telomere length (LTL) demonstrates a strong correlation with telomere length across various tissues [7]. White blood cells serve as a representative of hematopoietic cells and circulate throughout the body, making them highly sensitive to pathological and physiological changes that occur over time [8]. There is compelling evidence to show that measuring LTL is a useful tool that may be considered as a biological marker of individual biological age and is associated with certain age-related disorders, such as metabolic disorders and cancer, as well as increased overall mortality and limitations in life expectancy [8, 9]. In view of this, LTL has attracted increasingly researchers to focus on this area in recent years, and many studies related to LTL were published over the past two decades, making it very difficult for researchers to identify research hotpots in this area. In addition, despite the increasing number of publications, the research focus has undergone shifts over time and among various research groups. Consequently, it becomes challenging for readers to promptly grasp the key and trending content in the field of LTL. To our knowledge, there is still a lack of comprehensive analysis of publication trend, authoritative authors, institutions, research advancements, and emerging hotspots in relation to LTL. Multiple system reviews and meta-analysis on LTL have been published from different perspectives recently [10, 11]. These reviews detail the relationship between LTL and a variety of diseases. However, literature reviews usually summarize and outline only a specific issue or a particular branch of a particular field of study, rather than covering the entire field. Moreover, reviews and meta-analysis could not acquire quantitative results of above indicators such as publication trends or contributors’ information. Hence, there is a pressing demand to formulate novel literature analysis strategies to facilitate rapid data collation and mining.

Bibliometric analysis entails both the qualitative and quantitative evaluations of existing publications or literature by using statistical and mathematical methods [12]. This kind of analysis is usually used to assess the impact of academic results, research trends, academic communication patterns, and the output and quality of scientific research. The indicators commonly used in bibliometric analysis include the number of published papers, the number of citations, the h-index, etc., through which the current status and development trend of research in a certain field can be objectively reflected. Recently, the utilization of several bibliometric software such as CiteSpace and VOSviewer, has been extensively employed by researchers to identify specific focal areas and research trends in a certain field [13–15]. As far as we know, there has been a lack of scientific quantitative analysis in the field of LTL studies. To address this gap, we undertook an extensive bibliometric analysis, aiming to quantitatively and qualitatively assess LTL research published between 2000 and 2022. In particular, the main objectives of this study were to answer the following questions.

Question one: What is the global development trajectory in the LTL field?

Question two: Which journals are most frequently chosen for publishing research on LTL?

Question three: Who are the primary contributors in the LTL domain?

Question four: What are the key research themes and current hotspots in this field?

Question five: What are the most significant future research frontiers and anticipated hotspots in this area?

Methods

Data sources

We conducted a bibliometric analysis using the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCI-EXPANDED) of the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database hosted by Clarivate Analytics. Previous research has highlighted the benefits of the WoSCC database for bibliometric analysis compared to other databases [16, 17]. First, the literature included in WoSCC undergoes thorough evaluation and consists of high-quality, peer-reviewed academic journals and publications. Second, WoSCC provides extensive citation information and detailed literature records, allowing researchers to perform comprehensive literature analyses and build citation networks. Finally, WoSCC features sophisticated search and filtering options, enabling users to accurately refine and organize search results based on specific criteria. These advantages make WoSCC one of the most popular databases for bibliometric studies.

Retrieval methods

To ensure accuracy and consistency, the document retrieval was conducted on March 11, 2023, to minimize any potential deviations caused by database updates. The publication period considered in this study spanned from 2000 to 2022. Data from 2023 was not included due to incomplete data and most studies were not cited. The retrieval formula used were as follows: TI=((leukocyte telomere length*) OR (leucocyte telomere length*) OR (“white blood cell*” AND telomere length*)) OR AK=((leukocyte telomere length*) OR (leucocyte telomere length*) OR (“white blood cell*” AND telomere length*)) OR AB=((leukocyte telomere length*) OR (leucocyte telomere length*) OR (“white blood cell*” AND telomere length*)).

Inclusion criteria were (i) Papers published between 2000 and 2022; (ii) Written in English; (iii) Original articles and reviews. Exclusion criteria were (i) WOSCC occasionally misclassifies letter-type papers as articles. Therefore, a manual review is necessary to exclude all document types except reviews and articles; (ii) duplicate literatures. Two researchers independently conducted the primary data search, and any discrepancies were resolved through discussion with a third researcher. A total of 1818 papers were ultimately included. Data collection

In this study, the original data were extracted from the SCI-EXPANDED, encompassing various documented data such as the count of publications and citations, year of publication, countries/regions, institutes, authors, journals, H-index, references, and author keywords. Moreover, the H-index is a widely utilized metric that ranks publications in descending order based on the number of citations. The H value corresponds to the number of publications (N) in the list that have been cited N times or more. To ensure data accuracy, efforts were made to manually remove duplicate authors and correct any misspelled elements. Prior to the data analysis using VOSviewer 1.6.16 (Leiden University) [18], a thesaurus file was applied to merge duplicate terms, rectify misspelled elements, and eliminate redundant words. Subsequently, the cleansed dataset was imported into VOSviewer, CiteSpace 6.2. R1 (Drexel University) [19], and online platform for comprehensive bibliometric analysis (https://bibliometric.com).

Bibliometric analysis

CiteSpace and VOSviewer are Java-based software tools developed by professor Chaomei Chen and professor Van Eck, respectively [18, 19]. Both software has gained widespread recognition for their ability to identify the publication trends, cutting-edge research, and collaborations in scholarly research. In present work, we employed CiteSpace to perform the following analysis including co-cited reference analysis, and clusters’ timelines, reference citation bursts, keyword citation bursts. The specific configurations in CiteSpace were set up as follows: (i) Time slicing: The period January 2000 to December 2022 was divided into yearly intervals; (ii) Node types: Keywords and references were selected; (iii) Top N per slice: Set to “Top N per slice = 50”; (iv) Pruning options: pruning sliced networks and minimum spanning trees were applied. In general, different maps featuring various nodes and lines convey distinct meanings, which are thoroughly detailed in the figure legends. Nodes with significant centrality, indicated by a betweenness centrality (BC) value exceeding 0.1, often represent key hotspots or critical points in the field and are highlighted with a purple outer ring. For the co-citation reference cluster map, CiteSpace provides two metrics: modularity Q (Q value) and mean silhouette (S value). A Q value above 0.3 indicates a strong cluster structure, while an S value greater than 0.7 reflects robust clustering. Cluster labels are derived from noun phrases within the keyword lists of articles cited in each cluster using the likelihood ratio test (p < 0.001) [20, 21]. Each cluster was carefully reviewed, and automatic labels were adjusted based on the authors’ expertise when necessary. In addition, for bibliometric analysis, burstness detection is an analytical method used to identify “sudden” events or phenomena in a particular field of study. The main significance of this method is that it can identify significant increases in certain words or topics over a period of time, thus revealing a sharp rise in academic interest in a particular field or topic.

For the visualization of co-authorship networks among researchers, institutions, countries/regions, as well as co-citation networks among authors and journals, we utilized VOSviewer software. VOSviewer, developed by van Eck and Waltman, is another bibliometric tool with text mining capabilities to identify key parameters in large collections of scientific literature. It generates three types of network maps: network visualization, overlay visualization, and density visualization. The primary parameters were configured as follows: type of analysis (chosen one at a time, such as by country/region, author, journal, or keywords), item thresholds (determined by specific criteria), and a thesaurus file (used to combine different keyword variants). The remaining parameters were adjusted according to the guidelines provided in the software manual, tailored to suit the specific requirements of the analysis. Default settings were applied for other parameters. Total link strength measures the overall strength of connections among different subjects and is used to evaluate their position within cooperative or co-citation networks. Moreover, the online platform was used to conduct country cooperation analysis [21].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses and drawing graphs were conducted using R statistical software (R version 3.5.2), Microsoft Excel 2019, and SPSS 21.0. Categorical data were presented as frequencies and percentages. The rate of publication growth was determined using the techniques outlined by Wu et al. [22] Curve fitting was conducted in Microsoft Excel, and the best fitting model was selected according to the maximum value of coefficient value (R2). Pearson’s correlation coefficient was employed to investigate the correlation between two variables. A correlation coefficient (r) exceeding 0.7 was considered indicative of a strong correlation. Statistical significance established if the p-value was below 0.05.

Results

Global publication and citation trend

According to the aforementioned retrieval strategy, a total of 1818 non-duplicate literature, comprising 1741 articles and 77 reviews from the period of 2000 to 2022 were included in this study. The growth of yearly publication volume and the corresponding citation times was illustrated in Fig. 2A-B. As can be observed that the annual number of outputs in the field of LTL exhibited a volatile increasing trend during the study period. The volume of published studies increased from 5 in 2000 to 165 in 2022, with an average growth rate of 17.2%. A statistically significant association between the year and the volume of publications was detected (R2 = 0.9913). With respect to citations, the cumulative volume of citations for all studies reached 66,668, with an H-index of 123. On average, each paper was cited 36.67 times. Similar to the pattern in yearly output, the annual citation count for these publications also showed a significantly increasing trend (R2 = 0.9978). Pearson correlation analysis uncovered a robust positive relationship between the volume of publications and the number of citations (r = 0.982, p < 0.001).

References co-citation and burst references

Reference co-citation analysis is an essential method for assessing the progression and tracing the evolutionary frontiers of any research area. Following the bibliometric analysis conducted with CiteSpace, Fig. 8 presents a timeline network map of cited references. The clustering function was employed to divide the entire network map into distinct clusters, wherein references within the same cluster tend to have similar research themes compared to references in other clusters. It is important to note that the Q value and S value are crucial parameters for evaluating the significance of the community structure. Specifically, a Q value greater than 0.3 and an S value greater than 0.7 suggest significant clustering [15]. In the present study, the Q value was determined to be 0.7668, indicating the reasonable nature of this cluster network. Additionally, the mean S value was found to be 0.9273, thus suggesting a high level of homogeneity within these clusters. As illustrated in Fig. 8, according to time sequence, the clusters received much attention recently were “longitudinal study (#1)” and “mendelian randomization study (#7)”. Moreover, burst detection, an algorithm devised by Kleinberg, proves to be an useful evaluation method for capturing sudden surges in the popularity of references or keywords within a specific time. This function serves as an efficient means of identifying concepts or topics that were actively discussed during a given timeframe. Here, the burst detection algorithm was applied to identify the top 25 references exhibiting the most significant citation bursts related to LTL (Fig. 9). Among these references, the study with the highest burst value was the one published by Brouilette et al. in 2007.

Keyword co-occurrence analysis

In addition to references, keywords play an essential role in representing the main content of a specific subject. In the field of bibliometrics, keyword co-occurrence analysis is a common technique for detecting trending research topics. Co-occurrence analysis assesses the relatedness of keywords based on the frequency of their appearance together in documents. In this research, author keywords were extracted from 1818 documents and analyzed utilizing VOSviewer. After removing irrelevant keywords and merging those with similar meanings, a set of 77 keywords was identified. Figure 10 presents an overlay visualization map generated using VOSviewer. The size of the nodes reflects its frequency of occurrence. It is evident that in addition to the keywords “leukocyte telomere length”, the other most common keywords were “aging”, “telomerase”, “oxidative stress”, “biomarker”, as well as “inflammation”, and so on.

Discussion

The annual number literature serves as indicators of the development speed and research progress in the field under study, as well as the level of research concentration [22, 23]. Our findings revealed that, over the past 23 years, a total of 1818 papers related to LTL were identified and incorporated into the final analysis. Overall, the count of publications exhibited a fluctuating upward trend from 2000 to 2022. The number of documents increased from 5 in 2000 to 165 in 2022, with an average growth rate of 17.2%. Similarly, the annual number of citations received by these publications also showed a significantly increasing trend. Overall, with a thorough understanding of telomeres and telomerase, the significance of LTL has increasingly captured scholarly interest, as evidenced by both the number of annual publications and citation volumes. As we all know that the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded in 2009 to three scientists, including Elizabeth Blackburn, Carol Greider and Jack Szostak, for their remarkable contributions to the field of telomeres and telomerase research [24, 25]. This recognition was bestowed upon them for their discovery of the mechanisms by which telomeres and telomerase protect chromosomes. Since then, research on telomeres and telomerase has emerged as an important field in biomedical science. In addition, one study by Valera-Gran et al. [26] has investigated the research on telomere length in Children from 1991 to 2019 using bibliometric methods. Their findings indicated that since 2012, the number of publications on telomere length in children has markedly risen, with an overall trend showing consistent growth. This suggests that telomere length in children has become a significantly relevant research topic over the past decade. This result further proved the increasing attention of studies related to telomere length.

Over centuries, scholarly papers have remained crucial tools for researchers across all disciplines to communicate their findings. Disseminating research findings through international peer-reviewed journals is an indispensable aspect of establishing effective scientific communication [27]. By sharing their work through these platforms, researchers can disseminate their findings to a global audience, ensuring visibility and credibility within the scientific community. Thus, we have listed the 15 journals with the most publications related to LTL research in Fig. 3A. PLOS One published the most papers, followed by Scientific Reports and Aging US. This result shows that these journals are more willing to accept LTL-related research. At the same time, it can also provide a reference for scholars in this field to submit papers in the future. In addition to the quantity of publications, citation frequency is often regarded as a superior metric for assessing the influence of a journal. Thus, to gain a more comprehensive understanding, we further analyzed the co-citation network among journals using VOSviewer. The top five co-cited journals were PLOS One, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America, Lancet, Nucleic Acids Research as well as Aging Cell. This result revealed that these journals might have published several high-impact studies, which have garnered significant attention from researchers in this field.

Among the top 15 countries, the USA demonstrated a clear lead, as reflected by the highest number of research publications and the highest H-index value. Previous studies revealed that the discrepancies in publication quantity observed among different countries may be attributed more to economic factors rather than differences in population size [28]. National distribution of the top 10 funding agencies in this field further confirmed this. Among them, half of the funding organizations were from the USA and the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services has sponsored the highest number of research projects. Additionally, in terms of institutions in research regarding LTL, the institutions from the USA including University of California San Francisco, Harvard University, and National Institutes of Health occupied the top three positions based on number of publications. As for authors, among top 10 most prolific authors, 70% of them came from the USA. Specifically, Aviv A [29] from Rutgers State University published the most LTL articles in this study period, followed by Lin J [30] from University of California San Francisco, Kimura M [31] from Rutgers State University, and De Vivo I from Harvard University. The fact that these institutions and authors have published a large number of studies in this field is inseparable from the fact that they receive adequate financial support for research and advanced experimental conditions. Adequate financial support enables researchers to purchase advanced equipment, recruit high-quality research talents, and sustain high-level academic exchanges and cooperation. At the same time, advanced experimental conditions can provide reliable technical support and data security, making the research results more accurate and credible. More importantly, these research results not only reflect the leading position of the research institution in the field, but also provide valuable references for other scholars, and promote the sharing of knowledge and exchange of experience in the academic community. These research results can help other scholars identify potential partners and opportunities, promote cross-institutional and interdisciplinary collaborative research, and further promote the development of the field.

In addition, author co-citation analysis is a commonly employed method to unveil the prominent authors within a co-citation network of a specific field. Typically, authors who are frequently cited are considered to possess a greater influence and impact compared to their less-cited counterparts. We identified authors with high influence as those who obtained at least 100 citations. Among the 84 authors included, the top three authors including Cawthon RM, Blackburn EH, and Aviv A are all from the USA. Thus, these findings suggest that the consistent dominance of the United States in terms of both quantity and quality of publications in this field could be attributed, at least in part, to the contributions of these influential researchers and institutions.

Average citations per item (ACI) is another metric that reflects the value and scientific contribution of a paper [32]. As shown in Table 1, China ranks second in terms of the quantity of publications. However, the ACI of China is relatively lower compared to certain European and American countries. Hence, there is a pressing need to focus not only on increasing the quantity but also on enhancing the quality of publications. Of note, the Chinese scientific management agencies have noticed this issue and has successively issued a series of policies to encourage high quality research [33]. Meanwhile, the value of ACI might be associated with the time factor. As depicted in Fig. 2C, the USA, UK and Netherlands are highlighted in purple or blue, indicating that researchers from these countries made early contributions to the field of LTL. That means their publications with more opportunities for citation. Nevertheless, it is important to note that this premise is based on the assumption of high-quality studies from these countries. Furthermore, Fig. 4B illustrates the international collaboration between different countries, with the thickness of the lines representing the strength of cooperation. It is evident that the USA has established close collaborations with China, UK and Canada. China has active cooperation with Canada and Sweden. Generally, collaborative relationships are concentrated mainly in European, American, and East Asian countries. Efforts should be made to further enhance cooperation with less developed nations.

Citation analysis is a crucial methodology in bibliometric studies. It is widely acknowledged that the number of citations can serve as an indicator of a publication’s impact. A higher frequency of citations typically signifies a greater academic influence and also considered a crucial knowledge source and key readings for researchers interested in this area [34, 35]. Figure 7 summarized top 10 most cited papers on LTL. Among them, the study with the highest number of citations is the one authored by Valdes AM and colleagues published in Lancet [36]. In this cross-sectional study, they have investigated 1122 white women aged 18–76 years. Their results revealed that LTL declined consistently with age at an average rate of 27 bp per year, and telomeres in obese women were 240 bp shorter compared to those in lean women. Furthermore, the results indicated that each pack-year of smoking was associated with an additional 5 bp decrease in telomere length compared to the overall cohort. This study emphasized the pro-ageing effects of obesity and cigarette smoking. The second-most cited study was a genome-wide meta-analysis with a large sample [37]. In this study, they identified seven loci, of which five are novel, that exhibit significant associations with mean LTL. Within two of these loci (TERC and TERT), lead single nucleotide polymorphisms are implicated in various cancers and this study also observed a significant association between alleles associated with shorter LTL and an increased risk of coronary artery disease. In additional to the aforementioned highly cited studies of LTL, several studies investigated the role of LTL in cardiovascular disease such as atherosclerosis, premature myocardial infarction and coronary heart disease have also received significant attention from many scholars [8, 38–40]. Multiple lines of evidence, including from recent large genetics-based analyses, indicated that short leukocyte telomeres were a risk factor mainly a contributory role for many cardiovascular diseases [41, 42]. Therefore, there has been a significant amount of work on unveiling the connection between LTL and cardiovascular diseases.

Although the top 10 most cited articles mentioned above hold significant value in their respective contributions, it is important to note that there exists a moderate correlation between the number of citations and the years since publication. It is commonly accepted that articles published earlier tend to accumulate a higher number of citations [13, 14]. Therefore, to monitor and comprehend the dynamic evolution of research hotspots, we employed the technique of document burst detection using CiteSpace. Generally, articles exhibiting citation bursts indicate that they have garnered notable attention from relevant academic circles during a specific time. As can be seen from Fig. 9, the lobal hotspot research at different time periods are also different. Among them, it is worth noting that while the burst in the majority of references has subsided, there are still ongoing bursts in two references, suggesting that these research topics continue to acquire attention in recent years [43, 44]. Of them, Factor-Litvak et al. [44], investigated the role of LTL in newborns. Their findings showed that substantial variation in LTL observed among newborns poses a challenge to the conventional telomeric clock model. Individuals may possess inherently short or long LTL since birth, potentially influencing the development of diseases in adulthood several decades later. Therefore, it is now being questioned by an increasing number of scholars about whether a single assessment of LTL could meaningfully serve as a personal biological aging clock [45].

Reference co-citation analysis is a widely used method to identify prominent research themes within a specific field. In this study, we utilized the publications and their corresponding references to generate homogeneous clusters. References that exhibited strong interconnections were grouped together, while those with weaker connections were assigned to different clusters. Our results revealed the presence of 13 distinct clusters within the co-citation network map. It is important to note that the Q value and S value are crucial parameters for evaluating the significance of the community structure. Specifically, a Q value greater than 0.3 and an S value greater than 0.7 indicate significant clustering. In this study, the Q value was determined to be 0.7668, indicating the reasonable nature of this network. The largest cluster was “framingham heart study” (#0) [46, 47]. Figure 8 presents the timeline view of the major clusters, providing insights into the temporal dynamics and evolutionary patterns of each cluster. The emergence of cluster 4, cluster 10, and cluster 11 could be traced back to earlier periods, indicating their early development and influence. Conversely, “longitudinal study (#1)” and “mendelian randomization study (#7)” represent more recent research topics in the field of LTL, reflecting a shift in research focus over time and both of these clusters received a lot of attention in recent years. Take mendelian randomization study as an example, it is a study design that utilizes genetic variants as instrumental variables to investigate causal relationships between an exposure of interest and an outcome [48]. This approach leverages the random assortment of genetic variants during meiosis, mimicking the randomization process in a randomized controlled trial. In recent years, it has been widely employed to investigate the causal relationship between LTL and various diseases. For example, multiple mendelian randomization studies have investigated the potential causal role of LTL in cardiovascular disease, cancer, neurodegenerative disease [49, 50]. These studies provide evidence supporting a causal relationship between shorter LTL and increased risk of certain diseases and mortality. Meanwhile, there are also several studies investigating the role of LTL in modulating the impact of risk factors such as smoking, obesity, and inflammation on disease outcome [51, 52]. By disentangling causality, it could help reveal the underlying biology and potential therapeutic targets associated with LTL.

Keywords play a vital role in accurately capturing core content of scientific articles. In the domains of bibliometric analysis and data visualization, keyword co-occurrence analysis has emerged as a valuable approach for identifying development trends and research hotspots within specific fields. In our study, we performed an extensive keyword co-occurrence analysis to identify the key research directions and focal areas within the field of LTL. From the results of keyword co-occurrence network visualization analysis, apart from the keywords “leukocyte telomere length”, the other most common keywords were “aging”, “telomerase”, “oxidative stress”, “biomarker”, as well as “inflammation”. In addition, Fig. 11 presented Top 15 diseases related keywords with the most occurrences. It can be seen that the most concerned diseases in LTL fields were aging [2, 3], inflammation [53, 54], cardiovascular diseases such as atherosclerosis, coronary artery disease, and myocardial infarction [41, 42], endocrine diseases including obesity and type 2 diabetes [52, 55], neurological and psychiatric diseases including depression, stress, Alzheimer’s disease and schizophrenia [56–58], and cancers especially breast cancer [59]. This result was consistent with the analysis of most popular research areas. As can be seen from the top 10 most active research areas presented in Fig. 3C, Geriatrics Gerontology, Science Technology Other Topics, Oncology, Neurosciences Neurology, Cell Biology, and Psychiatry yielded the largest number of studies, suggesting these areas have received the most attention.

Moreover, all the keywords were also marked with different colors based on the AAY by VOSviewer. Earlier appearing keywords were represented in blue, while more recent keywords were depicted in red [12]. Through this analysis, we identified key topics that were prominent during the early stages of research, such as “hypertension”, “blood pressure”, “gender”, and “atherosclerosis”. On the other hand, keywords such as “COPD”, “mendelian randomization”, “adiposity”, “colorectal cancer”, “National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHNES)”, “telomerase reverse transcriptase”, “pregnancy” displayed a relatively recent appearance, suggesting a growing interest in these areas and their potential as future research hotspots. Take telomerase reverse transcriptase as an example, telomerase is also believed to have a close relationship with various diseases especially tumors [60]. The study of LTL and telomerase has gained increasing attention in the field of cancer, particularly with regard to targeting the mechanisms of telomere elongation and exploring telomerase inhibitors as potential novel therapeutic targets for anti-tumor treatments. Strategies aimed at disrupting telomere elongation, such as telomerase inhibitors or telomere-targeting agents, have shown promising results in preclinical and clinical studies [61–63]. These interventions aim to induce telomere shortening, which leads to telomere dysfunction and cellular senescence or apoptosis in cancer cells. However, due to differences in the design and methodology of analytical studies, including studies in cell culture and humans, it is still difficult to translate into actual clinical practice. Further investigation of the mechanisms underlying telomere maintenance and telomerase regulation may pave the way for the development of innovative therapies that can effectively inhibit tumor growth and improve patient outcomes. In addition, the NHANES database is an invaluable resource for studying all aspects of health and disease in the U.S. population. This database provides a large and representative sample of the U.S. population, making it an invaluable resource for studying the relationship between LTL and various health factors such as age, sex, race/ethnicity, socioeconomic status, lifestyle behaviors (smoking, physical activity), and the presence of chronic diseases [64]. Moreover, the NHANES database allows for longitudinal analysis, enabling researchers to examine changes in leukocyte telomere length over time and to investigate the effects of interventions or lifestyle changes on telomere dynamics [65]. Encouragingly, the NHANES database is seeing increased adoption in LTL research.

Strengths and limitations

This study offers several notable advantages. Firstly, it presents the first comprehensive analysis of LTL through bibliometrics, providing an important reference for researchers in this field. Secondly, compared to traditional reviews, bibliometric analysis provides a more comprehensive understanding of research trend, main contributors, hotspots and frontiers in this field. Lastly, the use of three bibliometric tools, including the widely used VOSviewer and CiteSpace, enhances the objectivity of our data analysis process. Nevertheless, there are still shortcomings in the present study. Firstly, the data solely relies on the WoSCC database, potentially overlooking relevant studies from other databases. Secondly, the exclusion of non-English publications may result in underestimating the presence of non-English research papers. Additionally, since the study from 2023 is incomplete, this study does not include publications after December 2022. As a result, some important studies may have been missed.

Conclusion

In conclusion, as far as we know, this study constitutes the first thorough examination of global research advancements and key areas of focus in the field of LTL. Our findings indicate that the study of LTL has attracted increasing attention, resulting in rapid advances in this field. The United States emerged as the leading contributor in this field, with the highest number of publications and H-index. Furthermore, the majority of the top 10 countries were from North America, Western Europe and East Asia, thereby significantly driving research development. To bridge the gap with developed countries, scholars in other countries/regions should focus on innovating research methods to enhance the quality of their published papers. In addition, this study not only identifies potential collaborators, research hotspots, and future frontiers, but also provides valuable guidance for exploring new research directions for clinicians, researchers and industry collaborators in the field of LTL. Reference analysis revealed that longitudinal study and mendelian randomization study are the most concerned research method in this field recently. Keywords analysis showed that the most concerned diseases in LTL fields were aging, inflammation, cardiovascular diseases, endocrine diseases, neurological and psychiatric diseases, and cancers. In addition, the following research directions such as “COPD”, “mendelian randomization”, “adiposity”, “colorectal cancer”, “National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHNES)”, “telomerase reverse transcriptase”, “pregnancy” have garnered increasing attention in recent times and hold the potential to evolve into research foci in the foreseeable future. The findings of the present study may also offer a reliable reference for policymakers making investment decisions.

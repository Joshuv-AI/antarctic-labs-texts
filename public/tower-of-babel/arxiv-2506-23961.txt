Abstract
. We prove several results for the Dirichlet, Neumann and Regularity problems for the Laplace equation in graph Lipschitz domains in the plane, considering A ∞ A_{\infty} -measures on the boundary. More specifically, we study the L p , 1 L^{p,1} -solvability for the Dirichlet problem, complementing results of [ 25 ] and [ 10 ] . Then, we study L p L^{p} -solvability of the Neumann problem, obtaining a range of solvability which is empty in some cases, a clear difference with the arc-length case. When it is not empty, it is an interval, and we consider solvability at its endpoints, establishing conditions for Lorentz space solvability when p > 1 p>1 and atomic Hardy space solvability when p = 1 p=1 . Solving the Lorentz endpoint leads us to a two-weight Sawyer-type inequality, for which we give a sufficient condition. Finally, we show how to adapt to the Regularity problem the results for the Neumann problem.

1.
Introduction

From the late 70’s, beginning with the Dirichlet problem for the Laplace equation
[
15
]
, there has been an intensive attempt to study boundary value problems in domains with low regularity. Let us just mention for now the recent work
[
2
]
, where the authors establish the minimal assumptions on the domain for the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian to be well-posed in
L^{p}
for some
p<\infty
, and the works of Mourgoglou and Tolsa
[
32
,
33
]
, where they solve the
L^{p}
-Neumann and Regularity problems in dimensions
3
and higher in a more general context than that of Lipschitz domains.

In his PhD thesis
[
25
]
, Kenig gave an application of weighted theory to this kind of problems. More specifically, he used the theory of Muckenhoupt’s
A_{p}
-weights
[
34
]
, together with results about weighted Hardy spaces, to study the Dirichlet problem in graph Lipschitz domains with
A_{\infty}
-measures on the boundary, obtaining the following theorem. For a detailed exposition of the notation involved, see Section
2
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 4.4 in
[
25
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a graph Lipschitz domain and let
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\to\Omega
the conformal mapping specified in Section
2
. Let
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
and let

(1.1)

p_{\Phi}(\nu)\coloneqq\inf\{q\in(1,\infty):\Phi(\nu)\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R})\}.

where
\Phi(\nu)
denotes
(\nu\circ\Phi)|\Phi^{\prime}|
.
If
p\in(p_{\Phi}(\nu),\infty)
, given any
f\in L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
, there exists
u
harmonic in
\Omega
so that
u
converges to
f
non-tangentially and
\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha_{0}}u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)}
. Moreover, if
p\in(1,p_{\Phi}(\nu)]
, there exists
f\in L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
such that there exists no
u
harmonic in
\Omega
so that
u
converges to
f
non-tangentially and
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha_{0}}u\in L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
.

The idea of the proof is to consider the problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
with boundary datum
f\circ\Phi
. In
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, a explicit solution is the Poisson integral of
f\circ\Phi
, and the non-tangential maximal operator of this Poisson integral can be bounded by
\mathcal{M}_{hl}(f\circ\Phi)
. So if
\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
, this operator is bounded in
L^{p}(\Phi(\nu))
. Therefore, one can solve the Dirichlet problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
with measure
\Phi(\nu)
on
\mathbb{R}\equiv\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, and transfer the estimates back to
\Omega
via
\Phi
. Conversely, if the problem is solvable in
L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
, then it can be proved that the Hilbert transform is bounded in
L^{p}(\Phi(\nu))
, and hence
\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
(
[
22
]
).

This technique reached its peak in
[
24
]
, where the authors stated the
L^{p}
-solvability of the Dirichlet, Neumann and Regularity problems in chord-arc domains in the plane with arc-length measure on the boundary. However, it was superseded later by other methods, such as layer potentials (
[
39
,
16
]
) or harmonic measure (
[
23
,
17
]
), that also worked in higher dimensions and for more general differential operators.

Nevertheless, the conformal mapping technique has recently been recovered in
[
10
]
, where they prove the following endpoint result for the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian in the case of arc-length measure
ds
(i.e.
\nu=1
).

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem 2.4 in
[
10
]
)
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.1
, if
|\Phi^{\prime}|\in A_{p_{\Phi}(ds)}^{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{R})
, then the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian is solvable in
X=L^{p_{\Phi}(ds),1}(\Lambda,ds)
taking
Y=L^{p_{\Phi}(ds),\infty}(\Lambda,ds)
.

Followed by this work, the conformal mapping technique has been used in
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
9
]
to study
L^{p}
-solvability results and obtain new endpoint results for some boundary value problems (B.V.P.’s from now on) for the Laplace equation. This approach provides a way to understand B.V.P.’s via weighted theory and, conversely, it motivates the study of several problems involving weights. However, the results obtained so far, in the case of the Dirichlet and Neumann problems, only treat the case of arc-length measure on the boundary, instead of the more general
A_{\infty}
-measures originally considered by Kenig in Theorem
1.1
.

The main goal of this paper is to address this question for the
L^{p,1}
-endpoint of the Dirichlet problem, the
L^{p}
-solvability for the Neumann problem, and its corresponding
L^{p,1}
and
H_{at}^{1}
-endpoints. As a consequence, we obtain the
L^{p}
and
L^{p,1}
-solvability of the the Regularity problem.

We should mention here that, for the
L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
-Neumann and Regularity problems, we obtain a range of solvability which is empty in some cases, a clear difference with the arc-length case. When it is not empty, it is an interval, and hence it makes sense to consider solvability at two endpoints with boundary data in Lorentz spaces when
p>1
, and in
H_{at}^{1}
when
p=1
. Moreover, solving the Lorentz endpoints leads us to a new two-weight Sawyer-type inequality.

1.1.
Main results

The first result is a quite direct generalization of Theorem
1.2
. We will always work with the Laplacian, so we omit it from the statements. See Section
2
for the definitions involved.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
. If
\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p_{\Phi}(\nu)}^{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{R})
, then the Dirichlet problem is solvable in
X=L^{p_{\Phi}(\nu),1}(\Lambda,d\nu)
taking
Y=L^{p_{\Phi}(\nu),\infty}(\Lambda,d\nu)
.

The
L^{p}
-solvability for the Neumann problem in terms of weights when
p\in(1,\infty)
is given by the following theorem. For the notion of unique solvability for the Neumann problem, see Definition
2.5
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
. If
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
, then the Neumann problem is uniquely solvable in
X=L^{p}(\Lambda,d\nu)
taking
Y=L^{p}(\Lambda,d\nu)
.

In view of this theorem, given
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
, we define the range of
L^{p}
-solvabilty

R(\nu)\coloneqq\{p\in(1,\infty):|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})\}.

When the weight is
\nu=1
, we recover Theorem 1.4 in
[
7
]
. Indeed, in this case,

R(ds)=\{p\in(1,\infty):|\Phi^{\prime}|^{1-p}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})\}=\{p\in(1,\infty):|\Phi^{\prime}|\in A_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})\},

and hence, by the nesting properties of the
A_{p}
-classes,
R(ds)
is an interval of the form
(1,p_{\Phi}^{\prime})
, where
p_{\Phi}=p_{\Phi}(ds)
is as in (
1.1
). We observe that, since
|\Phi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}(\mathbb{R})

[
4
,
25
]
,
p_{\Phi}<2
and therefore
p_{\Phi}^{\prime}>2
. This recovers the classical result that the Neumann problem in a Lipschitz domain is solvable in
(1,2+\varepsilon)
for some
\varepsilon>0
depending on the domain
[
16
]
, giving an explicit formula for such
\varepsilon
.

For a general weight
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
, the range of solvability
R(\nu)
may be empty, as we will see in examples in Section
8
, and, when it is not empty, it is an open interval (see Theorem
4.4
). Therefore, we can consider

p_{+}\coloneqq\sup R(\nu)\quad\text{ and }\quad p_{-}\coloneqq\inf R(\nu).

One may wonder whether the Neumann problem is solvable at
p_{\pm}
for some space smaller than
L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
. For the case
p_{-}=1
, we obtain the following Hardy space result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
. If
(\nu\circ\Phi)\in A_{1}
, then the Neumann problem is uniquely solvable in
X=H_{at}^{1}(\Lambda,\nu)
taking
Y=L^{1}(\Lambda,\nu)
.

Observe that
\nu\circ\Phi\in A_{1}
is the case
p=1
of the condition imposed in Theorem
1.4
. Also notice that, if
d\nu=ds
is arc-length measure,
\nu=1\in A_{1}
, so we have the following direct consequence.

Corollary 1.6
.

The Neumann problem is uniquely solvable in
X=H_{at}^{1}(\Lambda,ds)
with
Y=L^{1}(\Lambda,ds)
for any graph Lipschitz domain in the plane
\Omega
.

Again, this recovers the classical
H_{at}^{1}
-result in
[
16
]
, but now using the conformal mapping technique. In fact, Dahlberg and Kenig combine this endpoint with the
L^{2}
-solvability to prove
L^{p}
-solvability for
p\in(1,2)
, using interpolation. However, we follow the approach in
[
7
]
, and therefore we do not need
H_{at}^{1}
-solvability to prove the
L^{p}
-result.

For the case
p_{\pm}\in(1,\infty)
, we have the following Lorentz-space result, whose hypothesis will be explained afterwards:

Theorem 1.7

(Solvability in
L^{p_{\pm},1}(\Lambda,\nu)
)
.

Let
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
be such that
R(\nu)=(p_{-},p_{+})\neq\varnothing
.

•

If
p_{+}<\infty
and
(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))\in\mathcal{S}_{p_{+}}^{\mathcal{R}}
, then the Neumann problem is solvable in
X=L^{p_{+},1}(\Lambda,\nu)
with
Y=L^{p_{+},\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)
.

•

If
p_{-}>1
,
(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))\in\mathcal{S}_{p_{-}}^{\mathcal{R}}
and (
6.4
) holds, then the Neumann problem is solvable in
X=L^{p_{-},1}(\Lambda,d\nu)
with
Y=L^{p_{-},\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)
.

Condition
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
on the pair of weights
(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))
means that the operator

f\mapsto\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(f|\Phi^{\prime}|)}{|\Phi^{\prime}|}

is bounded from
L^{p,1}(\Phi(\nu))\to L^{p,\infty}(\Phi(\nu))
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}
denotes a sparse operator. In view of Remark
2.32
, this condition is an endpoint case of the one given in Theorem
1.4
.

This kind of weighted estimates are usually known as Sawyer-type inequalities or mixed weak-type inequalities
[
13
,
31
,
36
]
, and they have recently appeared in a similar form under the name of multiplier inequalities
[
14
]
. Here, they are directly motivated by a boundary value problem with a measure on the boundary, showing that applying the conformal mapping strategy to B.V.P.’s gives rise to interesting problems in weighted theory.

Finally, the
L^{p}
and
L^{p,1}
results for the Neumann problem can be adapted to the Regularity problem.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
. The Regularity problem is:

•

Uniquely solvable in
X=L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
taking
Y=L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
if
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}
.

•

Solvable in
X=L^{p_{+},1}(\Lambda,\nu)
with
Y=L^{p_{+},\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)
if
R(\nu)=(p_{-},p_{+})\neq\varnothing
with
p_{+}<\infty
,
(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))\in\mathcal{S}_{p_{+}}^{\mathcal{R}}
. The same holds for
p_{-}
with the same hypothesis if
p_{-}>1
and, besides, (
6.4
) holds.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

2.1.
Graph Lipschitz Domains and Conformal Mapping

Let
\Lambda
be a curve in the complex plane given parametrically by
\eta(x)=x+i\gamma(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\gamma
is a real-valued Lipschitz function with constant
L
, and consider the Lipschitz domain

(2.1)

\Omega=\left\{z_{1}+iz_{2}\in\mathbb{C}:z_{2}>\gamma(z_{1})\right\}.

Note that
\partial\Omega=\Lambda
.

By Rademacher’s theorem, a Lipschitz function is differentiable almost everywhere. Therefore, it makes sense to talk about the tangent vector
\eta^{\prime}(x)
to
\Lambda
at a point
\xi=\eta(x)

dx
-a.e., where
dx
denotes Lebesgue measure. Hence, we can define the outer normal vector
\vec{n}(\xi)
at almost every
\xi\in\Lambda
with respect to arc-length measure. We define arc-length measure on
\Lambda
as

ds(E)\coloneqq\int_{\eta^{-1}(E)}|\eta^{\prime}(t)|\,dt=\int_{\eta^{-1}(E)}\sqrt{1+|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{2}}\,dt,\quad E\subset\Lambda.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
0<\alpha<\arctan(\frac{1}{L})
, define the non-tangential maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
as

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(F)(\xi)=\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)}|F(z)|,\quad\xi\in\Lambda,

where
F
is a complex-valued function defined in
\Omega
and
\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)
is the non-tangential cone

\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)=\left\{z_{1}+iz_{2}\in\mathbb{C}:z_{2}>\operatorname{Im}(\xi)\text{ and }\left|\operatorname{Re}(\xi)-z_{1}\right|<\tan(\alpha)\left|z_{2}-\operatorname{Im}(\xi)\right|\right\}.

Since the region
\Omega
is simply connected, it is conformally equivalent to the upper half-plane

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\coloneqq\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:x\in\mathbb{R},y>0\}.

Let
z_{0}=ix_{0},x_{0}>\eta(0)
, and
\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\rightarrow\Omega
be the conformal mapping such that
\Phi(\infty)=\infty
, and
\Phi(i)=z_{0}
. Let
\Phi^{-1}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
be its inverse.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 1.1 in
[
25
]
)
.

With the above notation:

(1)

\Phi
extends to
\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}
as a homeomorphism onto
\overline{\Omega}
.

(2)

\Phi^{\prime}
has a non-tangential limit
dx
-a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
, and this limit is different from 0
dx
-a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
.

(3)

\Phi(x)
, with
x\in\mathbb{R}=\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, is absolutely continuous when restricted to any finite interval, and hence
\Phi^{\prime}(x)
exists
dx
-a.e. and is locally integrable. Moreover,
\Phi^{\prime}(x)=\lim_{z\triangleleft x}\Phi^{\prime}(z)

dx
-a.e.

(4)

Sets of
dx
-measure 0 on
\mathbb{R}
correspond by
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\Lambda
to sets of
ds
-measure 0 on
\Lambda
, and vice versa.

(5)

At every point where
\Phi^{\prime}(x)
exists and is different from
0
, it is a vector tangent to the curve
\Lambda
at the point
\Phi(x)
, and hence
|\arg\Phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq\arctan{L}

dx
-a.e.

We abuse notation and denote by
\Phi
the conformal mapping on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, the extension to
\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}
and the restriction to
\mathbb{R}\equiv\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
. We will use this conformal mapping and its inverse
\Phi^{-1}
to transfer the boundary value problems from
\Omega
to
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
and viceversa.

Conformal mappings from
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
onto graph Lipschitz domains
\Omega
preserve non-tangential cones in the following sense: for any
\alpha\in(0,\arctan{\frac{1}{L}})
, there exists
\beta,\gamma>0
depending only on
\alpha
such that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}\equiv\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
,

(2.2)

\Gamma_{\beta}(\Phi(x),\Omega)\subset\Phi(\Gamma_{\alpha}(x,\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}))\subset\Gamma_{\gamma}(\Phi(x),\Omega).

This result follows from Proposition 1.1 in
[
24
]
, because Lipschitz domains satisfy the
three point condition
.

2.2.
Dirichlet, Neumann and Regularity problems

Definition 2.3
.

For a locally integrable function
g
on
\Lambda
, a function
v
defined in
\Omega
is a solution of the Dirichlet problem
in
\Omega
with datum
g
, and we write

(2.3)

\Delta v=0\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad v=g\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

if
v
is harmonic in
\Omega
and
v
converges non-tangentially to
g
almost everywhere in
\Lambda
with respect to arc-length measure, that is, there exists
0<\alpha<\arctan(\frac{1}{L})
such that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)\\
z\rightarrow\xi\end{subarray}}v(z)=g(\xi)\quad ds\text{-a.e. }\xi\in\Lambda.

In the sequel, we use
z\triangleright\xi
to denote that
z\rightarrow\xi
with
z\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)
for some
0<\alpha<\arctan(\frac{1}{L})
.

Definition 2.4
.

For a locally integrable function
g
on
\Lambda
, a function
v
defined in
\Omega
is a solution of the Neumann problem
in
\Omega
with datum
g
, and we write

(2.4)

\Delta v=0\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\nabla v\cdot\mathbf{n}=g\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

if
v
is harmonic in
\Omega
and

\lim_{z\triangleright\xi}\nabla v(z)\cdot\mathbf{n}(\xi)=g(\xi)\quad\text{ for }ds\text{-a.e. }\xi\in\Lambda.

Definition 2.5
.

If
X
is a Banach space of measurable functions defined on
\Lambda
, we say that the Dirichlet problem in
\Omega
is solvable in
X
if there exist a Banach space
Y
of measurable functions defined on
\Lambda
and
0<\alpha<\arctan(\frac{1}{L})
such that for every
g\in X
there exists a solution
v_{g}
of the Dirichlet problem in
\Omega
with datum
g
, and satisfies the estimate

\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(v_{g})\right\|_{Y}\lesssim\|g\|_{X}.

The definition for the Neumann problem is the analogous one, but the estimate is instead

\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla v_{g})\right\|_{Y}\lesssim\|g\|_{X}.

We say that the Neumann problem is uniquely solvable if the solution is unique up to additive constants.

The notation
A\lesssim B
means that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq C\cdot B
. If we want to make explicit the dependence of
C
on some parameter
\alpha
, we may write
A\lesssim_{\alpha}B
. We write
A\simeq B
to denote
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

For the Regularity problem, also known as Dirichlet Regularity problem, we need to be a little more careful with the definition in order to be rigurous in our statements. We introduce the following space, which is the biggest class of functions on
\Lambda
for which we can take the Poisson integral after composing with
\Phi
.

Definition 2.6
.

Given
\Lambda
and
\Phi
as above, we define

\widetilde{L}^{1}(\Lambda)\coloneqq\left\{f\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Lambda):\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|(f\circ\Phi)(x)|}{1+|x|^{2}}\,dx<\infty\right\}.

Definition 2.7
.

If
X
is a Banach space of measurable functions defined on
\Lambda
, we say that the Regularity problem in
\Omega
is solvable in
X
if there exist a Banach space
Y
of measurable functions defined on
\Lambda
and
0<\alpha<\arctan(\frac{1}{L})
such that, for every
g\in\widetilde{L}^{1}(\Lambda)
with weak derivative
g^{\prime}\in X
, there exists a solution
v_{g}
of the Dirichlet problem in
\Omega
with datum
g
, and satisfies the estimate

(2.5)

\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla v_{g})\right\|_{Y}\lesssim\|g^{\prime}\|_{X}.

The definition of weak derivative of a function defined on
\Lambda
is clarified in Section
7
.

2.3.
Function spaces and weights on
\mathbb{R}

A weight
w
is a function defined on
\mathbb{R}
that is locally integrable and positive a.e. Given
p\in(1,\infty)
, we denote by
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w)
the space of measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

The main class of weights is Muckenhoupt’s class
A_{p}

[
34
]
, defined for
p>1
as

w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})\ratio\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{hl}\colon L^{p}(w)\to L^{p}(w),

and for
p=1
as

w\in A_{1}(\mathbb{R})\ratio\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{hl}\colon L^{1}(w)\to L^{1,\infty}(w).

Here,
\mathcal{M}_{hl}
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator, defined for
f\in L_{\rm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
as

\mathcal{M}_{hl}f(x)=\sup_{x\in I}\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|f(x)|\,dx,

where the supremum is taken over all intervals
I\subset\mathbb{R}
that contain
x
and, for a measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
,
|A|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
A
.

The class
A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as

A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\coloneqq\bigcup_{p\geq 1}A_{p}(\mathbb{R}).

We will sometimes abbreviate
A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
by
A_{p}
, for any
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Some standard properties of
A_{p}
weights (see page 218 of
[
38
]
and page 546 of
[
21
]
) are the following.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
u\in A_{p}
.

(1)

Then there exists
r>1
small enough such that
u^{r}\in A_{p}
.

(2)

There exist
A_{1}
weights
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
such that

w=w_{1}w_{2}^{1-p}.

Moreover, if
w_{0},w_{1}\in A_{p}
and
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}>0
satisfy
\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}\leq 1
, then
w_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}w_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\in A_{p}
.

To define Poisson and Neumann integrals for functions in weighted spaces, we need the following estimate.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. If
w\in A_{p}
, then
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|}\,dx<\infty
for every
f\in L^{p}(w)
.

For the endpoint results, we need to introduce Lorentz spaces. The Lorentz spaces
L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R},w)
and
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R},w)
are defined as the sets of measurable functions
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R},w)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\lambda_{f}^{w}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dy=\frac{1}{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{w}^{*}(t)t^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\,dt<\infty,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R},w)}=\sup_{y>0}y\left(\lambda_{f}^{w}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{1}{p}}f_{w}^{*}(t)<\infty,

respectively. Here,
\lambda_{f}^{w}
is the distribution function of
f
with respect to the measure
w(A)=\int_{A}w(x)\,dx
, defined for any measurable
A\subset\mathbb{R}
; that is,

\lambda_{f}^{w}(y)=w(\{t\in\mathbb{R}:|f(t)|>y\}).

And
f_{w}^{*}
is the decreasing rearrangement of
f
with respect to
w
, given by

f_{w}^{*}(t)=\inf\left\{y>0:\lambda_{f}^{w}(y)\leq t\right\}.

The class of restricted
A_{p}
-weights
[
26
,
11
]
, denoted by
A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{R})
or simply
A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
, is defined as

w\in A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{R})\ratio\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{hl}\colon L^{p,1}(w)\to L^{p,\infty}(w).

It is known
[
10
]
that

A_{p}(\mathbb{R})\subsetneq A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{R})\subsetneq\bigcup_{\varepsilon>0}A_{p+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}).

These classes of weights can be generalized to consider maximal operators with respect to measures different from Lebesgue measure.

Let
u
and
w
be weights and let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then,
w\in A_{p}(u)
if and only if

\mathcal{M}_{u}\colon L^{p}(wu)\to L^{p}(wu),

and
w\in A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}(u)
if and only if

\mathcal{M}_{u}\colon L^{p,1}(wu)\to L^{p}(wu).

Here,

\mathcal{M}_{u}f(x)\coloneqq\sup_{Q\ni x}\frac{1}{u(Q)}\left(\int_{Q}fu\,dx\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

These classes can be characterized in terms of cubes, as usual. Indeed,
w\in A_{p}(u)
if and only if

\sup_{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{u(Q)}\|\mathds{1}_{Q}\|_{L^{p}(wu)}\|w^{-1}\mathds{1}_{Q}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(wu)}<\infty,

and
w\in A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}(u)
if and only if

\sup_{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{u(Q)}\|\mathds{1}_{Q}\|_{L^{p,1}(wu)}\|w^{-1}\mathds{1}_{Q}\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(wu)}<\infty.

This last condition can be rewritten using Kolmogorov’s inequalities, obtaining that

w\in A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}(u)\iff\sup_{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{E\subset Q}\frac{u(E)}{u(Q)}\left(\frac{uw(Q)}{uw(E)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

2.3.1.
Sparse operators

A class of operators that has gained importance in recent years is that of sparse operators, because they satisfy in most cases the same weighted bounds as
\mathcal{M}_{hl}
and many interesting operators can be bounded by a sparse one. Hence, sparse bounds for an operator imply weighted bounds for it. Gentle introductions to this topic are
[
29
,
35
]
.

Definition 2.10
.

Given
0<\eta<1
, a family
\mathcal{S}
of cubes contained in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
\eta
-sparse if for every
Q\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists a measurable set

such that
\left|E_{Q}\right|\geq\eta|Q|
and the sets
\left\{E_{Q}\right\}_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}
are pairwise disjoint. A family of cubes is sparse if it is
\eta
-sparse for some
0<\eta<1
.

Theorem 2.11

(
[
28
,
5
]
)
.

Given a sparse family
\mathcal{S}
, the corresponding sparse operator is defined as

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(f)(x)\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f(y)\,dy\right)\mathds{1}_{Q}(x).

Then,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\colon L^{p}(w)\to L^{p}(w)
if and only if
w\in A_{p}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\colon L^{p,1}(w)\to L^{p,\infty}(w)
if and only if
w\in A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
.

2.5.
The Atomic Hardy Space

We recall the definition of the (weighted) atomic Hardy space over
\Lambda

\Lambda

. This is a particular case of the general construction for spaces of homogeneous type

Definition 2.17
.

A function

is an

over
\Lambda
with respect to the measure

-atom, if there exists an interval

on
\Lambda

•

.

The atomic Hardy space over

H_{at}^{1}(\Lambda,\nu)\coloneqq\{f\in L^{1}(\Lambda,\nu):f=\sum\lambda_{j}a_{j},\text{ where }a_{j}\text{ is }(\Lambda,\nu)\text{-atom and }\sum|\lambda_{j}|<\infty\}.

\|f\|_{H_{at}^{1}(\Lambda,\nu)}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\sum|\lambda_{j}|:f=\sum\lambda_{j}a_{j},\hskip 2.84544pta_{j}\text{ atoms}\right\}.

2.6.
Hardy spaces over graph Lipschitz domains

Definition 2.19
.

(

2.7.
Poisson integrals

Finally, we introduce some notation about Poisson integrals and prove a result about logarithms of functions in
H^{p}(w)
that we will need in the proof of Theorem

.

Definition 2.23
.

We define the Poisson kernel at
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
as

P_{y}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{y}{x^{2}+y^{2}}.

Given a function
f
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|^{2}}\,dx<\infty
, we define the Poisson integral of
f
at
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
as

(2.8)

(P_{y}\ast f)(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{y}{(x-t)^{2}+y^{2}}f(t)\,dt.

Definition 2.24
.

We define the conjugate Poisson kernel at
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
as

Q_{y}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{x}{x^{2}+y^{2}}.

Given a function
f
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|}\,dx<\infty
, the conjugate Poisson integral of
f
at
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
is

(Q_{y}\ast f)(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{x-t}{(x-t)^{2}+y^{2}}f(t)\,dt.

The following control of the non-tangential maximal operator of Poisson integrals is well-known.

Theorem 2.25
.

Let
f\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|^{2}}\,dx<\infty
. If
u(x,t)\coloneqq P_{t}\ast f(x)
for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, then
u
converges non-tangentially to
f
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}
. Besides

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(u)(x)\lesssim_{\alpha}\mathcal{M}_{hl}(f)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

It is proved for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
-functions in Theorem 1 in page 197 of
[
37
]
. The proof also works with the above generality.
∎

2.8.
Poisson integrals and logarithms

Following
[
20
]
, given
F\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
, the Poisson integral of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
of
F
is defined as

\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{D}}[F](w)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\operatorname{Re}\left({\frac{e^{i\theta}+w}{e^{i\theta}-w}}\right)F(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta,\quad w\in\mathbb{D},

and, for convenience, we denote the Poisson integral (
2.8
) also as

\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}[f](x+iy)=P_{y}\ast f(x),\quad x+iy\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

The Poisson integrals of
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
are related by a simple change of variables.

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
\tilde{F}\colon\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\equiv\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\tilde{F}(x)|}{1+|x|^{2}}\,dx<\infty
. Then

\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}[\tilde{F}](z)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{D}}[\tilde{F}\circ\varphi](\varphi^{-1}(z)),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

where
\varphi\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}

is the conformal mapping

(2.9)

\varphi(w)=i\frac{1-w}{1+w}\quad w\in\mathbb{D}.

This change of variables is going to allow us to transfer the following result about Hardy spaces on the unit disk.

Lemma 2.27

(Theorem II.4.1 in
[
20
]
)
.

If
0<p\leq\infty
and if
f(z)\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
with
f(z)\neq 0
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log{|f(e^{i\theta})|}\,d\theta>-\infty.

If
f\left(z_{0}\right)\neq 0
for some
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
, then

\log\left|f\left(z_{0}\right)\right|\leq\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{D}}[\log{|f|}](z_{0}).

We want an analogue for the upper half-plane. It is stated for Lebesgue measure in page 62 of
[
20
]
, and here we extend it to measures in
A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 2.28
.

Let
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
F\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},w)
. If
F(x,t)\neq 0
for a given
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, then

\log{|F(x,t)|}\leq P_{t}\ast\log{|F|}(x),

where
|F|
on the right-hand side denotes the non-tangential limit of
|F(x,t)|
.

Proof.

Since
F\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},w)
, by Lemma I.9 in
[
19
]
we have

|F(x,t)|\leq\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}[F](x,t).

Denote
u(x,t):=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}[F](x,t)
. Since
|F|\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w)
with
w\in A_{p}
,
u
is a well-defined harmonic function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, and hence
(u\circ\varphi)
is harmonic on
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore, using the above bound and applying the mean value property for harmonic functions, we obtain

\displaystyle\|F\circ\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{D})}\leq\sup_{r\in(0,1)}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(u\circ\varphi)(re^{i\theta})\,d\theta=\sup_{r\in(0,1)}(u\circ\varphi)(0)=(u\circ\varphi)(0).

Since
\varphi(0)=i
, we have

\displaystyle(u\circ\varphi)(0)=u(0,1)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|F|(t)}{1+t^{2}}\,dt\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w)}\leq\|F\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},w)}<\infty,

where the bound of the integral follows from formula (3.10) in
[
7
]
. Since
F\circ\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
and
(F\circ\varphi)(w_{0})\neq 0
for
w_{0}\coloneqq\varphi^{-1}(x,t)
, we can apply Theorem
2.27
, obtaining

\log{|F\circ\varphi(w_{0})|}\leq P_{\mathbb{D}}[\log{(F\circ\varphi)}](w_{0}).

Composing with
\varphi^{-1}

and using Lemma
2.26
, we have

\log{|F(x,t)|}\leq\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{D}}[\log{|(F\circ\varphi)|}]\circ(\varphi^{-1})(x,t)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}[\log{|F|}](x,t).\qed

2.9.
The Neumann integral

Just as the Poisson integral is the standard solution to the Dirichlet problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, the solution for the Neumann problem is the so-called Neumann integral
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.29
.

If
f
satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|}\,dx<\infty

as

(2.10)

u_{f,N}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{(y_{1}-x)^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}}{1+|x|}\right)f(x)\,dx,\quad(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

It is known (
[

]

is a well-defined harmonic function with gradient given pointwise by

(2.11)

\nabla u_{f,N}(y_{1},y_{2})=(-Q_{y_{2}}\ast f(y_{1}),-P_{y_{2}}\ast f(y_{1})),\quad(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

and hence, it is a solution to the Neumann problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
with datum

For the estimates that we need, the key result to control

and

This is contained in

]

2.9.1.
The class
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}

In order to obtain norm estimates for
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla u_{f,N})
when we deal with
L^{p,1}(\Lambda,\nu)
-solvability, we need to introduce the following class of weights.

Definition 2.31
.

We define
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
as the class of ordered pairs of weights
(u,v)
such that

(2.12)

\left\|\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(fu)}{u}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R},v)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R},v)},\quad\forall f\in L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R},v),

for every sparse family
\mathcal{S}
. The implicit constant may depend on the sparseness constant
\eta\in(0,1)
.

Remark 2.32
.

We observe that if we define the class
\mathcal{S}_{p}
by the condition

\left\|\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(fu)}{u}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},v)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},v)},\quad\forall f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},v),

then it is easy to see that

(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}_{p}\quad\iff\quad u^{-p}v\in A_{p}.

However, the complete characterization of the class
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
is a hard open problem in weighted theory.

Some concrete cases can be derived easily from previously known results. We state a few of them.

Proposition 2.33
.

Let
u,v
be weights. Then:

(1)

(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
if and only if
(u^{-1}v,v)\in\mathcal{S}_{p^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{R}}
.

(2)

(1,v)\in\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
if and only if
u\in A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
.

(3)

(u,u)\in\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
if and only if
u\in A_{p^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{R}}
.

Proof.

(1) follows by duality since sparse operators are self-adjoint.

(2) It is known that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\colon L^{p,1}(u)\to L^{p,\infty}(u)
if
u\in A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
; see the proof of Corollary 3.2 in
[
5
]
.

(3) It follows from (1) and (2).∎

A more novel result is the following sufficient codition for
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
, that we will use to construct examples in Section
8.4
. It generalizes items (2) and (3) in Proposition
2.33
.

Theorem 2.34
.

Assume
u
and
v
are weights, with
v\in A_{\infty}
, such that

(2.13)

\sup_{Q}\displaystyle\frac{u(Q)\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)(Q)}{|Q|v(Q)}<\infty,

and such that

(2.14)

\frac{v}{u}\in A_{p}(u)\text{ and }u\in A_{p^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{R}}\left(\frac{v}{u}\right),\quad\text{ or }\quad\frac{v}{u}\in A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}(u)\text{ and }u\in A_{p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{v}{u}\right).

Then
(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
.

Proof.

We consider the first case:
\frac{v}{u}\in A_{p}(u)
and
u\in A_{p^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{R}}(\frac{v}{u})
. The other case is reasoned in the same way. It is also enough to prove the bound for characteristic functions, so let
f=\mathds{1}_{E}
. We reason by duality. Let
g\in L^{p^{\prime},1}(v)
with
\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime},1}(v)}=1
. We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(fu)}{u}g(x)v(x)\,dx=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{u(E\cap Q)}{|Q|}\left(\int_{Q}g\frac{v}{u}\right).

We use that
\frac{1}{|Q|}\lesssim\frac{v(Q)}{u(Q)\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)(Q)}
to bound

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{u(E\cap Q)}{|Q|}\left(\int_{Q}g\frac{v}{u}\right)\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{u(E\cap Q)}{u(Q)}\frac{1}{\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)(Q)}\left(\int_{Q}g\frac{v}{u}\,dx\right)v(Q).

We have
v\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and hence

v(Q)\lesssim_{\eta}v(E_{Q}).

Therefore, using this estimate and the disjointness of the sets
E_{Q}

4.2.

By Theorem

Unlike the case

•

There are examples of

such that

.

•

When

, it is an interval that can have any size and any location. In particular, it can be at a positive distance from

, unlike the arc-length measure case

We leave the examples for Section

and prove now the second item.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
. If

. More specifically, given

Proof.

Let

. This means that

\nu(\Phi(x))|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|^{1-p}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}).

Therefore, by the Factorization Theorem,

(4.9)

\exists u_{0},u_{1}\in A_{1}(\mathbb{R}):\nu(\Phi(x))|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|^{1-p}=u_{0}u_{1}^{1-p}.

We want to check that there exists

(4.10)

\nu(\Phi(x))|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|^{1-p}=v_{0}v_{1}^{1-q}.

We try to use (
4.9
) to obtain (
4.10
). We can write

(4.11)

\nu(\Phi(x))|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|^{1-q}=\nu(\Phi(x))|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|^{1-p}|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|^{p-q}=u_{0}u_{1}^{1-p}|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|^{p-q}.

Since

Substituting this into (

\nu(\Phi(x))|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|^{1-q}=u_{0}u_{1}^{1-p}(\widehat{u}_{0}\widehat{u}_{1}^{-1})^{p-q}=u_{0}u_{1}^{1-p}\widehat{u}_{0}^{p-q}\widehat{u}_{1}^{q-p}.

We have to distinguish the two cases

4.3.
A remark on duality

When
\nu\equiv 1
, the Dirichlet problem is solvable in
L^{p}(\Lambda,ds)
if and only if
|\Phi^{\prime}|\in A_{p}
. On the other hand, the Neumann problem is solvable in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda,ds)
when
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
. Since
|\Phi^{\prime}|\in A_{p}
if and only if
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
, if we knew optimality of these conditions, then we would have that the Dirichlet problem is solvable in
L^{p}
if and only if the Neumann problem is solvable in
L^{p^{\prime}}
. This is why the ranges of solvability are dual to each other: if
(p_{\Phi},\infty)
is the Dirichlet range, the Neumann range is
(1,p_{\Phi}^{\prime})
.

We can reason in an analogous way for a general measure
\nu
. Indeed, by Theorem
1.1
, the Dirichlet problem in
L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
is solvable if and only if
\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}
. And
\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}
if and only if
\Phi(\nu)^{1-p^{\prime}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
. It turns out that

\Phi(\nu)^{1-p^{\prime}}=|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p^{\prime}}\Phi(\nu^{1-p^{\prime}}),

so if the Dirichlet problem is solvable in
L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
, then
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p^{\prime}}\Phi(\nu^{1-p^{\prime}})\in A_{p^{\prime}}
, and hence the Neumann problem is solvable in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda,\nu^{1-p^{\prime}})
. For the reverse implication, we would need to know that if the Neumann problem is solvable in
L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
, then
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}
. This is an open problem.

\xi\in B_{\Lambda}(\xi_{0},r)\iff\Phi^{-1}(\xi)\in\Phi^{-1}(B_{\Lambda}(\xi_{0},r)),

so

\operatorname{supp}\left(a\right)\subset B_{\Lambda}(\xi_{0},r)\iff\operatorname{supp}\left(a\circ\Phi\right)\subset\Phi^{-1}(B_{\Lambda}(\xi_{0},r)),

with
\Phi^{-1}(B_{\Lambda}(\xi_{0},r))=(\Phi^{-1}\circ\eta)(B(\eta^{-1}(\xi_{0}),r))
an interval of
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, we need to prove that

\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)}\leq\frac{1}{\nu(B_{\Lambda}(\xi_{0},r))}\iff\|a\circ\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}\leq\frac{1}{\Phi(\nu)(\Phi^{-1}(B_{\Lambda}(\xi_{0},r)))}.

But this is true because, by a change of variables,

\nu(B_{\Lambda}(\xi_{0},r))=\Phi(\nu)(\Phi^{-1}(B_{\Lambda}(\xi_{0},r))),

and by Theorem
2.2
,
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\Lambda
is a bijective map that preserves sets of measure
0
, so

\|a\circ\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)}.

Finally, the mean
0
property is direct because

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a\circ\Phi)(x)\,\Phi(\nu)(x)dx=\int_{\Lambda}a(\xi)\,\nu(\xi)d\sigma(\xi).\qed

As a trivial consequence of Lemma
5.2
, we obtain:

Corollary 5.3
.

With the above notation,

f\in H_{at}^{1}(\Lambda,\nu)\iff f\circ\Phi\in H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu)),

with

\|f\|_{H_{at}^{1}(\Lambda,\nu)}=\|f\circ\Phi\|_{H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}.

As we explained above, we would like to obtain a relation between
\|f\|_{H_{at}^{1}(\Lambda,\nu)}
and
\|\mathcal{T}_{N}(f)\|_{H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mu)}
for a certain measure
\mu
, and we have proved the relation
\|f\|_{H_{at}^{1}(\Lambda,\nu)}=\|f\circ\Phi\|_{H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}
. Here is where the following theorem enters into play.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
w\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},w)=\{fw^{-1}:f\in H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx)\},

with equivalence of norms. In other words, the map
f\mapsto fw
is an isomorphism between the weighted Hardy space
H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},w)
and the classical (unweighted) Hardy space
H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx)
.

Proof.

See Theorem 2.25 in
[
12
]
or Theorem III 1.2 in
[
19
]
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
and suppose
\nu\circ\Phi\in A_{\infty}
. Then

f\in H_{at}^{1}(\Lambda,\nu)\iff\mathcal{T}_{N}(f)=(f\circ\Phi)|\Phi^{\prime}|\in H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R},(\nu\circ\Phi)),

with the equivalence of norms (
5.2
).

Proof.

By Corollary
5.3
,

f\in H_{at}^{1}(\Lambda,\nu)\iff f\circ\Phi\in H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu)),

with equality of norms. Then, since
\Phi(\nu),(\nu\circ\Phi)\in A_{\infty}
, we can apply Theorem
5.4
twice to obtain

f\circ\Phi\in H^{1}_{at}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))\iff(f\circ\Phi)\Phi(\nu)\in H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R},dx)\iff(f\circ\Phi)|\Phi^{\prime}|\in H_{at}^{1}(\mathbb{R},(\nu\circ\Phi))

with equivalence of norms. ∎

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

The proof is the same as that of Theorem
1.4
, using instead the estimates developed in this section.

We just emphasize that, to prove uniqueness, Theorem 2.5 in
[
9
]
works for
p=1
, because if
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},w)
and
w\in A_{1}
, then
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|}\,dx<\infty
. This, together with the fact that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(x)\,dx=\infty
for
w\in A_{\infty}
and the definition of
A_{1}
-weight, makes their argument valid in this case too.

6.
The Neumann Problem in
L^{p_{\pm},1}(\Lambda,\nu)

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.7
following the strategy of Theorem 1.5 in
[
7
]
. However, new difficulties arise in this more general context. First, the problem that we need to study in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
gives rise to the two-weight estimate (
2.12
). Second, the transference of the non-tangential maximal estimate from
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
to
\Omega
is more subtle in this case, and will make use of the property about logarithms stated in Lemma
2.28
.

6.1.
Equivalent problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}

Let
g\in L^{p,1}(\Lambda,\nu)
and let
\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)=(g\circ\Phi)|\Phi^{\prime}|
be the corresponding Neumann datum in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
. By a change of variables,

\|g\|_{L^{p,1}(\Lambda,\nu)}=\|g\circ\Phi\|_{L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}=\left\|\frac{\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)}{|\Phi^{\prime}|}\right\|_{L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}.

Therefore, if we want to pose a problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
that allows us to solve the
L^{p,1}(\Lambda,\nu)
-problem in
\Omega
by composing with a conformal mapping, the space
X
from which we must take the boundary data for the Neumann problem is
\mathcal{L}^{p,1}(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))
, formed by those
f
with

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p,1}(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))}\coloneqq\left\|\frac{f}{|\Phi^{\prime}|}\right\|_{L^{p,1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}<\infty.

And the space
Y
that we choose is
\mathcal{L}^{p,\infty}(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))
, formed by those
h
with

\|h\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p,\infty}(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))}\coloneqq\left\|\frac{h}{|\Phi^{\prime}|}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}<\infty.

For the Neumann integral of
f
to be well-defined, we need
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|}\,dx<\infty
. By Hölder,

(6.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|}\,dx\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p,1}(\mathbb{R},(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu)))}\left\|\frac{(\nu\circ\Phi)}{1+|\cdot|}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}.

So it suffices to have

(6.2)

\left\|\frac{(\nu\circ\Phi)}{1+|\cdot|}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}<\infty.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
p\in(p_{-},p_{+}]
. If
(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))\in\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
, then
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|}\,dx<\infty
for
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p,1}(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))
.

Proof.

Let
(u,v):=(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))
. We check (
6.2
). Since
(u,v)\in\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
, then
(u^{-1}v,v)\in\mathcal{S}_{p^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{R}}
by Proposition
2.33
. Hence,

(6.3)

\left\|\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(h\cdot u^{-1}v)}{u^{-1}v}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R},v)}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime},1}(\mathbb{R},v)}.

Now, we have that

\frac{1}{1+|x|}\simeq\mathcal{M}_{hl}(\mathds{1}_{[-1,1]})(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

and thus, if we choose
h\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{[-1,1]}uv^{-1}
, we have that

\left\|\frac{uv^{-1}}{1+|\cdot|}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(v)}\simeq\left\|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{hl}(h\cdot u^{-1}v)}{u^{-1}v}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R},v)}\lesssim\left\|\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(h\cdot u^{-1}v)}{u^{-1}v}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(\mathbb{R},v)}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime},1}(\mathbb{R},v)}.

Consequently, it remains to prove that, under our hypothesis,
h\in L^{p^{\prime},1}(v)
. Now, for every
p\in(p_{-},p_{+})
, we have that
vu^{-p}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
and hence the dual weight
v^{1-p^{\prime}}u^{p^{\prime}}\in L^{1}_{loc}
. Therefore,
h\in L^{p^{\prime}}(v)
for every
p\in(p_{-},p_{+})
, and thus
h\in L^{p^{\prime},1}(v)
. Finally, since
p_{+}>p
for
p\in(p_{-},p_{+})
, then
p_{+}^{\prime}<p^{\prime}
and hence, since
h
has compact support,

h\in L^{p_{+}^{\prime},1}(v)
, and the result follows.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

In the case
p=p_{-}
, (
6.2
) has to be imposed in order to have a well-defined Neumann integral. This condition is equivalent to the following inequality for every measurable set
E
:

(6.4)

\int_{E}\frac{|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|}{1+|x|}dx\lesssim\left(\Phi(\nu)(E)\right)^{1/p_{-}^{\prime}}.

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

We assume that
p_{+}<\infty
and
p_{-}>1
. In this way, we can treat both cases at the same time, taking
\bm{p}\in\{p_{-},p_{+}\}
.

Let
g\in L^{\bm{p},1}(\Lambda,\nu)
. We have to solve the following problem in
\Omega

\begin{cases}\Delta v=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\bm{n}}v=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega=\Lambda,\\
\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla v)\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\bm{p},1}(\Lambda,\nu)}.\end{cases}

To do so, consider the problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}

(6.5)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\\
-\partial_{y}u=\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\\
\|\mathcal{M}(\nabla u)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p{+},\infty}(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))}\lesssim\|\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p{+},1}(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))}.\end{cases}

Since
(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu))\in\mathcal{S}_{\bm{p}}^{\mathcal{R}}
, a solution is given by
u=u_{\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)}
which is well-defined as a consequence of (
6.2
) (it is assumed for
\bm{p}=p_{-}
, and for
\bm{p}=p_{+}
it follows from Lemma
6.1
). Therefore, we have

\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla u)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu)))}\lesssim\|\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu)))}=\|g\|_{L^{\bm{p},1}(\Lambda,\nu)}.

We know that
v=u\circ\Phi^{-1}
is a solution to the Neumann problem with datum
g
on
\Omega
, so the only thing we have to prove is the non-tangential maximal estimate

\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla v)\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{\bm{p},1}(\Lambda,\nu)}.

Therefore, it is enough to prove that

\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla v)\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla u)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu)))}.

First, let us rewrite the left-hand side. As usual, let

F\coloneqq\partial_{1}u-i\partial_{2}u\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad G\coloneqq\partial_{1}v-i\partial_{2}v\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

Then
F
and
G
are holomorphic in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
and
\Omega
respectively, and they satisfy

|F|=|\nabla u|\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad|G|=|\nabla v|\quad\text{ in }\Omega

and

G\circ\Phi=\frac{F}{\Phi^{\prime}}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Then, by Theorem
2.20
,

\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla v)\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)}=\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(G)\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)}\simeq\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(G\circ\Phi)\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}=\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{F}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}.

So the inequality that we need to prove is

(6.6)

\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{F}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}\lesssim\left\|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(F)}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}.

To do so, we do an approximation argument. Since
\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)\in\mathcal{L}^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))<\infty
, then
\frac{\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)}{|\Phi^{\prime}|}\in L^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))
. By Lemma 2.2 (b) in
[
7
]
, there exists
\{g_{m}\}\subset C_{c}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))
such that

\left\|\frac{\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)}{|\Phi^{\prime}|}-g_{m}\right\|_{L^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}\xrightarrow{m\to\infty}0.

So
f_{m}\coloneqq g_{m}\cdot|\Phi^{\prime}|\in\mathcal{L}^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu)))
satisfies

\|\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)-f_{m}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu)))}\xrightarrow{m\to\infty}0.

Let
u_{m}\coloneqq u_{f_{m},N}
. Notice that
f_{m}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu))
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, because

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f_{m}|^{p}|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Phi^{\prime}|^{p}|g_{m}|^{p}|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)\,dx\leq\|g_{m}\|_{\infty}^{p}\int_{\operatorname{supp}\left(g_{m}\right)}\Phi(\nu)\,dx<\infty.

Besides, by definition of
p_{-}
and
p_{+}
,
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
for every
p\in(p_{-},p_{+})
. Therefore, we have

\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla u_{m})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu))}\lesssim\|f_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu))},\quad\forall p\in(p_{-},p_{+}).

Let
F_{m}\coloneqq\partial_{1}u_{m}-i\partial_{2}u_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. The previous inequality tells us that

\|F_{m}\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu))}\lesssim\|f_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu))}=\|g_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}.

Since
F_{m}\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu))
with
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)\in A_{\infty}
, by Theorem
2.22
we obtain
\frac{F_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\Phi(\nu))
with

\|F_{m}\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu))}\simeq\left\|\frac{F_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\Phi(\nu))}.

Denote by
h_{m}
the boundary values of
F_{m}
. Since
F_{m}\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu))
with
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
for any
p\in(p_{-},p_{+})
, then Lemma
2.28
gives us that

\log{|F_{m}(y_{1},y_{2})|}\leq P_{y_{2}}\ast\log|h_{m}|(y_{1}),\quad(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Since
\log{|\Phi^{\prime}(y_{1},y_{2})|}=P_{y_{2}}\ast\log{|\Phi^{\prime}|}(y_{1})
(see for example
[
3
]
), we have that

\log{|F_{m}(y_{1},y_{2})|}-\log{|\Phi^{\prime}(y_{1},y_{2})|}\leq P_{y_{2}}\ast\log{|h_{m}|}(y_{1})-P_{y_{2}}\ast\log{|\Phi^{\prime}|}(y_{1}).

That is,

\log{\left|\frac{F_{m}(y_{1},y_{2})}{\Phi^{\prime}(y_{1},y_{2})}\right|}\leq P_{y_{2}}\ast\log{\left|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right|}(y_{1}).

Multiplying by
a>0
,

\log{\left|\frac{F_{m}(y_{1},y_{2})}{\Phi^{\prime}(y_{1},y_{2})}\right|^{a}}\leq P_{y_{2}}\ast\log{\left|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right|^{a}}(y_{1}).

Taking exponentials and using Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{F_{m}(y_{1},y_{2})}{\Phi^{\prime}(y_{1},y_{2})}\right|^{a}\leq e^{P_{y_{2}}\ast\log{|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}|^{a}}(y_{1})}\leq(P_{y_{2}}\ast e^{\log{|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}|^{a}}})(y_{1})=\left(P_{y_{2}}\ast\left|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right|^{a}\right)(y_{1}).

So

\left|\frac{F_{m}(y_{1},y_{2})}{\Phi^{\prime}(y_{1},y_{2})}\right|\leq\left(P_{y_{2}}\ast\left|\frac{F_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right|^{a}(y_{1})\right)^{\frac{1}{a}},

which by Theorem
2.25
implies

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{F_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right)\leq\left(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\left(P_{y_{2}}\ast\left|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right|^{a}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\lesssim\left(\mathcal{M}_{hl}\left(\left|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right|^{a}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{F_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right\|_{H^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\Phi(\nu))}\lesssim\left\|\mathcal{M}_{hl}\left(\left|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right|^{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\right\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}=\left\|\mathcal{M}_{hl}\left(\left|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right|^{a}\right)\right\|_{L^{\frac{\bm{p}}{a},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}^{\frac{1}{a}}.

We choose
a>0
sufficiently small so that
q\coloneqq\frac{\bm{p}}{a}
is sufficiently big so that
\Phi(\nu)\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, we have
\mathcal{M}_{hl}\colon L^{q,\infty}(\Phi(\nu))\to L^{q,\infty}(\Phi(\nu))
. Therefore, the above can be estimated by

(6.7)

\left\|\frac{F_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right\|_{H^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\Phi(\nu))}\lesssim\left\|\left|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right|^{a}\right\|_{L^{\frac{\bm{p}}{a},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}^{\frac{1}{a}}=\left\|\frac{h_{m}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}\leq\left\|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(F_{m})}{|\Phi^{\prime}|}\right\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}

obtaining inequality (
6.6
) for the approximations
F_{m}
.

The limiting argument now follows as in pages 44 and 45 of
[
7
]
with the obvious changes.

7.
The Regularity Problem

In this section, we show how to adapt the results for the Neumann problem to the Regularity problem. As usual, we begin with the case of the upper half-plane.

7.1.
The regularity problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
with weak derivative
f^{\prime}
such that

(7.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|^{2}}\,dx<\infty\quad{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f^{\prime}(x)|}{1+|x|}\,dx<\infty,

and let
u_{f,D}(y_{1},y_{2})\coloneqq(P_{y_{2}}\ast f)(y_{1})
.
Then, we have that

(7.2)

\nabla u_{f,D}(y_{1},y_{2})=\left(-P_{y_{2}}\ast f^{\prime}(y_{1}),Q_{y_{2}}\ast f^{\prime}(y_{1})\right),\quad\forall(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof follows by standard distributional computations.
∎

As a direct consequence of (
2.11
) and (
7.2
), we obtain the following relation between the gradient of the Poisson integral and that of the Neumann integral.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
f
be as in Lemma

. Then

(7.3)

|\nabla u_{f,D}(y_{1},y_{2})|=|\nabla u_{f^{\prime},N}(y_{1},y_{2})|,\quad\forall(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Using (

), the estimates for the Neumann problem can be transferred to the Regularity problem.

.

Consider one of the following pairs
(X,Y)
of spaces:

•

,

These results address the Regularity problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
. To pass to
\Omega
, some care must be taken to deal with the derivatives of the boundary datum. First, we need some technical remarks.

7.2.
Some technical facts about conformal mapping

Since
\Phi(x)\in\Lambda
, it must be of the form

\Phi(x)=(\Phi_{1}(x),\gamma(\Phi_{1}(x))),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}

for some
\Phi_{1}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. Hence, by the Chain Rule,

\Phi^{\prime}(x)=\Phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)\cdot(1,\gamma^{\prime}(\Phi_{1}(x))).

Since
\Phi^{\prime}(x)\neq 0

dx
-a.e., it must be
\Phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)\neq 0

dx
-a.e. in
\mathbb{R}
. Since
|\operatorname{arg}\Phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq\arctan{L}<\frac{\pi}{2}
, then
\operatorname{Re}(\Phi^{\prime}(x))=\Phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)>0
at every point where
\Phi^{\prime}(x)
exists and is different from
0
. In particular,

(7.5)

\Phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)>0\text{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Since it is a bijection from
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, it is strictly increasing.

Besides, by the Inverse Function Theorem on
\mathbb{R}
, we have that
\Phi_{1}^{-1}
has derivative
dx
-a.e., with

(7.6)

(\Phi_{1}^{-1})^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{\Phi_{1}^{\prime}(\Phi_{1}^{-1}(x))}>0,\quad dx\text{-a.e.}

In summary, we have:

Proposition 7.4
.

If
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\Lambda=\partial\Omega
is given by the components
\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
, then
\Phi_{1}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an absolutely continuous homeomorphism from
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, differentiable a.e. with positive derivative. Its inverse
\Phi_{1}^{-1}
is differentiable a.e. with derivative given by (
7.6
).

7.3.
Derivative of a function defined over a Lipschitz curve

To solve the Regularity problem in
\Omega
, we need to deal with estimate (
2.5
), which involves
the weak derivative
f^{\prime}
of the Dirichlet datum
f
appears. This datum
f
is a function defined on the boundary,
f\colon\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}
, so we must clarify what we understand as the weak derivative of a function defined on a Lipschitz curve. This is defined in some articles like
[
39
]
, but here we modify slightly the class of test functions in order to make rigorous some explicit computations that we have to perform. First, we introduce some notation.

Definition 7.5
.

Let
g\colon\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function. We say that
g
is locally integrable, and write
g\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Lambda)
if
(g\circ\eta)(t)=g(t,\gamma(t))\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R},dt)
.

We are going to use functions of bounded variation as test functions, for reasons explained below.

Definition 7.6
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Define

T_{F}(x)=\sup\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|F\left(x_{j}\right)-F\left(x_{j-1}\right)\right|:n\in\mathbb{N},-\infty<x_{0}<\cdots<x_{n}=x\right\}.

T_{F}
is called the total variation function of
F
.
It is an increasing function with values in
[0,\infty]
. If
T_{F}(\infty)=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{F}(x)
is finite, we say that
F
is of bounded variation on
\mathbb{R}
, and we denote the space of all such
F
by
BV
(
[
18
]
). We denote by
BV_{c}
the set of functions in
BV
with compact support.

Definition 7.7
.

Let
\Lambda
and
\gamma
be as above. Given
g\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Lambda)
, we say that
g
has weak derivative
h=g^{\prime}
if for every
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
in
BV_{c}(\mathbb{R})
we have

(7.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t,\gamma(t))\varphi^{\prime}(t)\,dt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(t,\gamma(t))(1+i\gamma^{\prime}(t))\varphi(t)\,dt.

Remark 7.8
.

The reason to choose as test function the class
BV_{c}

is so that
\varphi\circ\Phi_{1}^{-1}
is a test function when
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
g\colon\Lambda=\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
g
has weak derivative
g^{\prime}
in the sense of Definition
7.7
. Then
(g\circ\Phi)
has weak derivative
(g\circ\Phi)^{\prime}
and is given by

(7.8)

(g\circ\Phi)^{\prime}=(g^{\prime}\circ\Phi)\cdot\Phi^{\prime}\quad\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We want to see that if

(7.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t,\gamma(t))\varphi^{\prime}(t)\,dt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(t,\gamma(t))(1+i\gamma^{\prime}(t))\varphi(t)\,dt,\quad\forall\varphi\in\operatorname{BV}_{c}(\mathbb{R}),

then

(7.10)

-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(g\circ\Phi)(t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(g^{\prime}\circ\Phi)(t)(\Phi^{\prime})(t)\varphi(t)\,dt,\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}).

Since
\Phi=\eta\circ\Phi_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(g\circ\Phi)(t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)\,dt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(g\circ\eta)(\Phi_{1}(t))\varphi^{\prime}(t)\,dt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(g\circ\eta)(\Phi_{1}(t))\varphi^{\prime}(\Phi_{1}^{-1}\circ\Phi_{1}(t))\,dt,

Making the change variables
y=\Phi_{1}(t)
and applying the chain rule, the above integral becomes

-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(g\circ\Phi)(t)\varphi^{\prime}(t)\,dt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(g\circ\eta)(y)(\varphi\circ\Phi_{1}^{-1})^{\prime}(y)\,dy.

We would like to apply to this integral the relation (
7.9
) with test function
\varphi\circ\Phi_{1}^{-1}
. We have to check that it is of bounded variation with compact support. The compact support is clear. Besides, since
\Phi_{1}^{-1}
is strictly increasing and locally bounded, we have that
\Phi_{1}^{-1}
is of bounded variation in
[\Phi_{1}(a)-1,\Phi_{1}(b)+1]
. So, given

x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots\leq\Phi_{1}(a)-1<x_{k}<\ldots\leq\Phi_{1}(b)+1<x_{k^{\prime}+1}<\ldots<x_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\sum_{1}^{n}|\varphi(\Phi_{1}^{-1}(x_{j}))-\varphi(\Phi_{1}^{-1}(x_{j-1}))|\leq\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}(T_{\Phi_{1}^{-1}}((\Phi_{1}(b)+1)-T_{\Phi_{1}^{-1}}((\Phi_{1}(a)+1))<\infty,

and thus,
\varphi\circ\Phi_{1}^{-1}
is of bounded variation in
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, applying (
7.9
) using as test function
\varphi\circ\Phi_{1}^{-1}
, we obtain

-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(g\circ\eta)(y)(\varphi\circ\Phi_{1}^{-1})^{\prime}(y)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(\eta(y))(1+i\gamma^{\prime}(y))(\varphi\circ\Phi_{1}^{-1})(y)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{\prime}(\Phi(t))\Phi^{\prime}(t)\varphi(t)\,dt,

having made the change
y=\Phi_{1}(t)
and using that
\Phi_{1}^{\prime}(t)(1+i\gamma^{\prime}(\Phi_{1}(t))=\Phi^{\prime}(t)
and
\eta\circ\Phi_{1}=\Phi
. ∎

7.4.
The regularity problem

Lemma
7.9
, together with Corollary
7.2
, is what allows us to adapt all the work done for the Neumann problem to the Regularity problem. Indeed, if
g
is the Dirichlet datum on
\Lambda
,
\mathcal{T}_{D}(g)\coloneqq g\circ\Phi
is the Dirichlet datum on
\mathbb{R}
. By Lemma
7.9
, it has weak derivative
\mathcal{T}_{D}(g)^{\prime}=(g^{\prime}\circ\Phi)\Phi^{\prime}
. On the other hand, if
f
is the Neumann datum on
\Lambda
, then the corresponding Neumann datum on
\mathbb{R}
is
\mathcal{T}_{N}(g)\coloneqq(f\circ\Phi)|\Phi^{\prime}|
. Therefore,

(7.11)

|\mathcal{T}_{D}(g)^{\prime}|=|\mathcal{T}_{N}(g^{\prime})|.

In particular, their
L^{p}
and
L^{p,1}
-norms will be equal. Hence, the same schemes of proof used for the Neumann problem work as well for the Regularity problem, so we just sketch the arguments.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

We begin with the
L^{p}
-case. The proof is a repetition of Theorem
1.4
. Since
f\in\widetilde{L}^{1}(\Lambda)
, then

u\coloneqq u_{f\circ\Phi,D}=P_{y}\ast(f\circ\Phi)

is a solution to the Dirichlet problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
with datum
f\circ\Phi
. Besides, by Lemma
7.3
, since
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
we have

\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla u_{f\circ\Phi,D})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu))}\lesssim\|f\circ\Phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu))}.

Then, if we define
v\coloneqq u_{f\circ\Phi,D}\circ\Phi^{-1}
, we have that
v
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem in
\Omega
and, reasoning with the norms of the non-tangential maximal operators as in the proof of Theorem
1.4
, it satisfies
\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla v)\|_{L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)}
. Uniqueness follows as in Theorem
1.4
.

For the
L^{p,1}
-result, let
\bm{p}\in\{p_{-},p_{+}\}
be as in the proof of Theorem
1.7
. Let
f\in\widetilde{L}^{1}(\Lambda)
with weak derivative
f^{\prime}\in L^{\bm{p},1}(\Lambda,\nu)
. To solve the
L^{\bm{p},1}(\Lambda,\nu)
-Regularity problem in
\Omega
with datum
f
, consider in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
the Poisson integral

u=u_{(f\circ\Phi),D}=P_{y}\ast(f\circ\Phi).

This is a solution to the Dirichlet problem in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
with datum
(f\circ\Phi)
that satisfies the estimate:

(7.12)

\left\|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla u)}{|\Phi^{\prime}|}\right\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}\lesssim\|(f^{\prime}\circ\Phi)\|_{L^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}=\|(f^{\prime}\circ\Phi)(\Phi^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu)))},

where, by Lemma
7.1
,

(7.13)

\nabla u=(-P_{y}\ast(f\circ\Phi)^{\prime},Q_{y}\ast(f\circ\Phi)^{\prime}).

The delicate step is to transfer estimate (
7.12
) to
\Omega
to obtain

(7.14)

\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\nabla v)\|_{L^{\bm{p},\infty}(\Lambda,\nu)}\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\bm{p},1}(\Lambda,\nu)},

where
v=u\circ\Phi^{-1}
. We have already done this in the proof of Theorem
1.7
. We indicate how to repeat the argument.

Since
(f\circ\Phi)^{\prime}=(f^{\prime}\circ\Phi)(\Phi^{\prime})\in\mathcal{L}^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu)))
, then
f^{\prime}\circ\Phi\in L^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))
. Hence, by Lemma 2.2 (b) in
[
7
]
, there exists a sequence
\{g_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}\cap L^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))
such that

\|g_{m}-(f^{\prime}\circ\Phi)\|_{L^{\bm{p},1}(\Lambda,\Phi(\nu))}\xrightarrow{m\to\infty}0.

Then, defining
f_{m}\coloneqq g_{m}\cdot(\Phi^{\prime})\in\mathcal{L}^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu)))
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\|(f\circ\Phi)^{\prime}-f_{m}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},(|\Phi^{\prime}|,\Phi(\nu)))}=\left\|\frac{(f^{\prime}\circ\Phi)\cdot(\Phi^{\prime})-g_{m}\cdot(\Phi^{\prime})}{|\Phi^{\prime}|}\right\|_{L^{\bm{p},1}(\mathbb{R},\Phi(\nu))}\xrightarrow{m\to\infty}0.

Notice that

F(x,y)\coloneqq\partial_{1}u(x,y)-i\partial_{2}u(x,y)=-P_{y}\ast(f\circ\Phi)^{\prime}(x)-iQ_{y}\ast(f\circ\Phi)^{\prime}(x),\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

is holomorphic in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
with modulus equal to
|\nabla u|
. So if we define, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

F_{m}(x,y)\coloneqq-(P_{y}\ast f_{m})(x)-i(Q_{y}\ast f_{m})(x),\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

these functions are holomorphic and converge uniformly to
F
on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
. Indeed, given
K\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
compact and
z\in K
, we can bound
P_{y}(x-t),Q_{y}(x-t)\lesssim_{K}\frac{1}{1+|t|}
in the convolution to obtain,

|F_{m}(x,y)-F(x,y)|\lesssim_{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f_{m}(t)-(f\circ\Phi)^{\prime}(t)|}{1+|t|}\,dt\lesssim\left\|\frac{f_{m}-(f\circ\Phi)^{\prime}}{|\Phi^{\prime}|}\right\|_{L^{\bm{p},1}(\Phi(\nu))}\left\|\dfrac{\left(\nu\circ\Phi\right)^{-1}}{1+|\cdot|}\right\|_{L^{\bm{p}^{\prime},\infty}(\Phi(\nu))},

which tends to
0
when
m\to\infty
.

Since we have uniform convergence over compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, we can repeat the limiting argument in the proof of Theorem 1.5 in
[
7
]
to obtain (
7.14
). ∎

Lemma 8.1

Let

8.1.

and

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and

with
\nu(\xi)\coloneqq|\xi|^{\beta},

and in

for any

. Moreover, for

We have that

if and only if

while
\Phi(\nu)\in A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
if and only if
\alpha(\beta+1)-1\in(-1,p-1]
, that is if
\alpha(\beta+1)\leq p
. The result follows by Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
.
∎

8.1.1.

One may wonder if the Dirichlet problem is always solvable in

for the endpoint

for

Consider again the cone of aperture
\alpha

\nu(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{|\xi|^{\beta}}{1+\log_{+}{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}},\quad\xi\in\Lambda.

8.2.
L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
-solvability for the Neumann and Regularity problems

Corollary 8.3
.

The Neumann and the Regularity problems are solvable in
L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)
in the following cases:

•

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and for every
p
such that

\max\{1,\beta+1\}<p<\infty.

•

For
\alpha\in(1,2)
and for every
p
such that

\max\{1,\beta+1\}<p<\max\left\{1,\frac{\alpha(\beta+1)}{\alpha-1}\right\}.

Proof.

We have that

|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-p}\Phi(\nu)=|x|^{\alpha\beta+(\alpha-1)(1-p)}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R})\ \iff\ (\alpha-1)(1-p)+\alpha\beta\in(-1,p-1).

This is equivalent to:

•

When
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have
p>\beta+1
.

•

When
\alpha\in(1,2)
, then
p\in\left(\beta+1,\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}(\beta+1)\right)
.

Therefore, we have:

•

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

p_{-}=\max\{1,\beta+1\},\quad p_{+}=\infty.

•

For
\alpha\in(1,2)
,

p_{-}=\max\{1,\beta+1\},\quad p_{+}=\max\left\{1,\frac{\alpha(\beta+1)}{\alpha-1}\right\},

and the result follows by Theorem
1.4
.
∎

We notice here that, in the case
\alpha\in(1,2)
, the range
(p_{+},p_{-})
can be any subinterval of
(1,\infty)
. Indeed, given any
1\leq a<b<\infty
and taking

\begin{cases}\beta=a-1\\
\alpha=\left(\frac{b}{a}-1\right)^{-1}+1,\end{cases}

the range of solvability is
(a,b)
. Also, it can be the empty set if
a<b<1
.

Open problems

The main open problem is to prove that the imposed conditions for solvability are optimal:

(3)

The Finite-Temperature Behavior of a Triangular Heisenberg Antiferromagnet
Cecilie Glittum, Olav F. Syljuåsen

Abstract
We investigate the classical antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model on the triangular lattice with up to third-nearest neighbor couplings using nematic bond theory. This approach allows us to compute the free energy and the neutron scattering static structure factor at finite temperatures. We map out the phase diagram with a particular emphasis on finite-temperature phase transitions that break lattice rotational symmetries, spiral spin liquids and the broad specific heat hump that is ubiquitous in the antiferromagnetic 120° phase.



I
Introduction

The antiferromagnetic (AF) Heisenberg model on the triangular lattice is the eldest daughter of frustrated magnetism.
Beyond its fundamental theoretical importance, there has in recent years been renewed interests in it driven by the discovery of new materials. In particular, the
S=1/2
delafossites NaYbSe
2
, KYbSe
2
and CsYbSe
2
are layered triangular lattice materials with negligible interlayer couplings. They are believed to be described by a
J_{1}
-
J_{2}
AF
quantum
Heisenberg model with
J_{2}
in proximity to a suggested quantum spin liquid (QSL) region
Scheie et al. 2024a
;
Scheie et al. 2024b
;
Xie et al. 2023
.
Recent studies of
S=2
h-Lu
0.3
Y
0.7

3

S=5/2
h-Lu
0.47
Sc
0.53
FeO
3

Yano et al. 2024
.

While these systems are inherently quantum mechanical, important insight can be obtained through their classical analogs. The classical Heisenberg model emerges as the
S\to\infty
limit of the quantum spin model. This limit is not only appropriate for large-spin materials, but serves also as a reference for understanding the landscape of ordered phases and thermal behavior from which quantum fluctuations may induce novel phenomena such as spin liquids. For instance, the QSL regime of the quantum
J_{1}
-
J_{2}
AF Heisenberg model lies close to a phase boundary in the classical model where for small
J_{2}/J_{1}<1/8
, the ground state exhibits 120° order, while a stripe phase is stabilized for
J_{2}/J_{1}>1/8
.

The classical Heisenberg model on the triangular lattice with further-neighbor interactions have mainly been studied in the
T=0
limit
Rastelli et al. 1980
;
Jolicoeur et al. 1990
;
Korshunov 1993
, presenting a ground state phase diagram with a wide range of single-
\vec{q}
orders allowing for breaking of discrete lattice symmetries. Thus, the introduction of further-neighbor interactions can stabilize new phases and expand the parameter space for unconventional magnetic order. Yet, the fate of these phases at finite temperatures remains largely unresolved.

Moreover, beyond the single-
\vec{q}
orders, the ferromagnetic (FM) counterpart of the model exhibits spiral spin liquid behavior upon including both second- and third-neighbor interactions
Glittum and Syljuåsen 2021
. Similar behavior may be expected for the antiferromagnet close to the line
J_{3}=J_{2}/2
, where there is a continuous ground state degeneracy of spiral states.

The classical triangular Heisenberg antiferromagnet thus remains a critical reference point
for understanding the effects of frustration, both as a standalone system and as a baseline for quantum models.

II
Model and Method

II.1
Hamiltonian

We study the classical Heisenberg model on the triangular lattice with up to third-nearest neighbor couplings

H=J_{1}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j}+J_{2}\sum_{\langle\langle i,j\rangle\rangle}\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j}+J_{3}\sum_{\langle\langle\langle i,j\rangle\rangle\rangle}\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j},

(1)

where
\mathbf{S}_{i}
are unit vectors. We will consider AF
J_{1}
and use energy units where
J_{1}=1
.
The corresponding exchange coupling in momentum space takes the form

J_{\vec{q}}=-3J_{3}+(J_{1}-2J_{3})A_{\vec{q}}+2J_{3}A^{2}_{\vec{q}}+(J_{2}-2J_{3})B_{\vec{q}},

(2)

where

\begin{split}&A_{\vec{q}}=\cos{\vec{q}_{1}}+\cos{\vec{q}_{2}}+\cos{\vec{q}_{3}},\\
&B_{\vec{q}}=\cos{(\vec{q}_{1}-\vec{q}_{2})}+\cos{(\vec{q}_{2}-\vec{q}_{3})}+\cos{(\vec{q}_{3}-\vec{q}_{1})},\end{split}

with
\vec{q}_{i}=\vec{q}\cdot\vec{a}_{i}
and triangular lattice vectors
\vec{a}_{1}=(1,0)
,
\vec{a}_{2}=(-1/2,\sqrt{3}/2)
and
\vec{a}_{3}=(-1/2,-\sqrt{3}/2)
.

The characterization of the minima of
J_{\vec{q}}
was first made in Ref.
Rastelli et al. 1980
, and their zero-temperature phase diagram is reproduced in Fig.
2
.
For the pure nearest neighbor model,
J_{\vec{q}}
depends only on
A_{\vec{q}}
. It is minimal on the K points in the Brillouin zone (see Fig.
2
for definitions of high-symmetry points), and the ground state is the AF
120\degree
state.
In contrast,
B_{\vec{q}}
is maximal at the K points. Thus for
J_{3}=0
, a sufficiently positive value of
J_{2}
will cause the minimum to shift away from the K points to the M points.
The energy at M points is lower than at K as long as

J_{2}>\frac{1}{8}\left(1+9J_{3}\right)

(3)

or equivalently

J_{3}<\frac{1}{9}\left(-1+8J_{2}\right).

(4)

In particular, this means that for
J_{3}=0
(
J_{2}=0
) the stripe phase (M) that breaks the lattice rotational symmetry becomes minimal for
J_{2}>1/8
(
J_{3}<-1/9
).
For
J_{3}=J_{2}/2
, the
B_{\vec{q}}
-terms are cancelled, and
J_{\vec{q}}
depends only on
A_{\vec{q}}
with the minimum forming a continuous “ring” for
J_{2}>1/4
.
For both
J_{2}<-1
and
J_{3}<-1
the minimum is at
\Gamma
, i.e. a FM state, and for large positive values of
J_{2},J_{3}
the minima lies on the lines
\Gamma
K or
\Gamma
M. For coupling values where K, M,
\Gamma
K and
\Gamma
M phases meet there is also a small region, see inset of Fig.
2
, where the minima lie on the KM lines on the Brillouin zone boundary.
For illustrations of the ordered spin configurations in each region of the phase diagram, see Ref.
Rastelli et al. 1980
.

II.2
Method

To investigate finite-temperature properties of the model, we employ the NBT
Michael Schecter and Olav F. Syljuåsen and Jens
Paaske 2017
. This method provides a self-consistent treatment of bond correlations, allowing us to compute the temperature dependence of the free energy
Glittum and Syljuåsen 2021
and the static structure factor
Syljuåsen et al. 2019
for large system sizes
L\times L
. The NBT self-consistent equations are solved by iterations until the self-energy converges. Then the free energy and structure factor are calculated. The free energy can be used to determine the thermodynamically stable state for cases where iterations starting from different random (or biased) self-energies converge to different states. For a detailed description of NBT, see Refs.
Syljuåsen et al. 2019
;
Glittum and Syljuåsen 2021
.

III.2
Spiral spin liquids

Along the line
J_{3}=J_{2}/2
,
J_{\vec{q}}
depends only on
A_{\vec{q}}
, and the minima form a continuous “ring” when
J_{2}>1/4
. This ring minimum is centered around K for
1/4<J_{2}<1/3
and around
\Gamma
for
J_{2}>1/3
. For
J_{2}=1/3
, the ring minimum forms a hexagon with corners at the M points.

The ring minimum signifies a continuous degeneracy of spin spirals with different pitch vector orientations. At low temperatures, but still above the phase transition, one expects the spin configuration to be a liquid mainly consisting of such spirals with momenta belonging to the ring, and the static structure factor will therefore be peaked for the ring momenta. While all single-
\vec{q}
spiral states on the ring are degenerate, at finite temperatures, entropy effects and interactions between different spirals will make the intensity along the ring uneven. Fig.
7
(upper panels) show the static structure factor obtained by NBT just above the phase transition for two cases where
J_{3}=J_{2}/2
, one where the ring is around K (
J_{2}=0.3
) and another where the ring is around
\Gamma
(
J_{2}=1
). In the latter case it is clear that the ring intensity is maximal on the
\Gamma
M lines, while the former shows a more even intensity distribution. Yet, a closer inspection reveals slightly higher intensities on the KM lines.

We have further mapped out the directions of maximal ring intensity in the static structure factor along the line
J_{3}=J_{2}/2
for
J_{2}>1/4
. The results are shown in Fig.
8

T_{c}

The phase transition at

approaches

In Fig.

IV
Discussion

As seen in Fig.
3
, NBT gives a specific heat for the pure nearest-neighbor model which has a broad asymmetric hump at
T_{\rm hump}\approx 0.367
. While the existence of the broad hump is in accordance with MC simulations
Kawamura et al. 2010
, NBT overestimates its peak temperature by 16%.
This is in line with NBT typically overestimating critical temperatures by 10-20%
Syljuåsen et al. 2019
, which is likely a consequence of the neglect of vertex corrections, thus all temperature estimates presented in this article should take this into account.

Kawamura and Myashita’s Z
2
vortex theory
Kawamura and Miyashita 1984
interprets features of the specific heat hump as a signature of a finite-temperature topological transition driven by the unbinding of Z
2
vortices
Kawamura et al. 2010
;
Aoyama and Kawamura 2020
. This scenario predicts two distinct length scales and a weak essential singularity in the specific heat. It is unlikely that NBT can capture such unbinding of Z
2
vortices, as that is an effect relying on the topological nature of planar spin configurations with three sublattices. NBT is based on a large-
n
expansion, where
n
is the number of spin components. It is thus unreasonable to expect it to be able to correctly capture topological effects that crucially depend on the precise value of the number
n
. Nevertheless, NBT gives a clear hump feature in the specific heat at approximately the correct temperature. We believe this shows that the broad specific heat hump is mainly associated with the build-up of short-range spin correlations.

The Heisenberg model on the triangular lattice considered here has a continuous global spin-rotational symmetry, and so there cannot be any long-range magnetic order at finite temperatures
Mermin and Wagner 1966
. However, there can still be finite-temperature phase transitions that break discrete lattice symmetries. We have mapped out critical temperatures for these phase transitions, which are all discontinuous, as well as the peak temperature of the specific heat humps for the full range of
J_{2}
-
J_{3}
-couplings. This can aid the inference of specific coupling values from experimental measurements of either the specific heat or phase transition temperatures.

The finite temperature phase diagram of the model studied here contains a multicritical point at
J_{2}=1/4
,
J_{3}=1/8
and
T_{c}=0
where four phases meet: the
T=0
uniformly ordered AF 120° state (K), two incommensurate spiral phases (
\Gamma
K and KM), and the disordered phase.
Our results show that going away from the multicritial point along the line
J_{3}=J_{2}/2
as
J_{2}>1/4
the phase transition becomes discontinuous and the critical temperature increases continuously from zero. The phase transitions along this line separate disordered spiral spin liquid states from low-temperature ordered states that break the lattice rotational symmetry. The pattern of symmetry breaking follows the order-by-disorder scenario and agrees with that predicted by calculations of the entropy within the linear spin wave approximation.

For a FM nearest neighbor interaction, a spiral spin liquid was observed recently in the
S=3/2
quasi-two-dimensional delafossite-like AgCrSe
2

Andriushin et al. 2025
, where the maximal neutron scattering intensites are oriented along the
\Gamma
K lines. There the exchange couplings deviate from the line
J_{3}=J_{2}/2
, with
J_{3}
being slightly higher, causing the energy minima to be on the
\Gamma
K lines, which increase the intensities of the static structure factor in the
\Gamma
K-directions. However, as we have shown in Fig.
8
(for AF couplings), entropy and interactions of spirals can also select a particular modulation of the structure factor intensity, even on the line
J_{3}=J_{2}/2
, where all spiral pitch vectors have the same energy. By continuity, we expect that this entropic effect will extend these regions outside of the
J_{3}=J_{2}/2
line, until
\Gamma
K or
\Gamma
M spiral-
\vec{q}
s become so energetically favored that they will dominate below a certain temperature. It is therefore actually possible to have a spiral spin liquid modulated along
\Gamma
K(
\Gamma
M) despite the minimal energy of
J_{\vec{q}}
being along
\Gamma
M(
\Gamma
K).

In addition to the triangular lattice Heisenberg ferromagnet, spiral spin liquids were previously predicted theoretically for the diamond
Bergman et al. 2007
and honeycomb lattice
Okumura et al. 2010
Heisenberg antiferromagnets and the square lattice XY ferromagnet
Yan and Reuther 2022
;
Gonzalez et al. 2024
, also there with further-neighbor interactions. Here, we have shown that also the archetypical triangular lattice antiferromagnet hosts spiral spin liquids when including second- and third-nearest neighbor couplings. On our wish list is to investigate the real space configurations of this Heisenberg spiral spin liquid, to better understand the underlying excitations, which in the XY case are known to be momentum vortices
Yan and Reuther 2022
.

Our model provides a finite temperature study of the classical analog of the suggested model for
S=1/2
delafossite compounds
Scheie et al. 2024a
;
Scheie et al. 2024b
;
Xie et al. 2023
, where further-neighbor couplings (
J_{2}
) are often sizeable. It could also be relevant for modelling triangular Heisenberg magnets of higher spin. Such “classical” examples include layered compounds such as the
S=5/2
materials RbFe(MoO
4
)
2

Svistov et al. 2003
, Rb

V
Conclusion

Seeing the wood for the trees, the NBT provides a quantitative description of the finite-temperature behavior of the classical triangular Heisenberg antiferromagnet with extended couplings.
In addition to the conventional 120° state, the second- and third-nearest neighbor interactions give rise to phases that break discrete rotational lattice symmetries and also finite temperature spiral spin liquid phases. It is our hope that the quantitative finite temperature results presented here can aid the deduction of possible values of exchange couplings from experimental measurements of specific heat, phase transition temperatures and neutron scattering structure factors.

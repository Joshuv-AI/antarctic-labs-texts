Abstract
We study the interaction between photons, dark photons, and axions at future lepton colliders, focusing on single-photon events with missing energy as the experimental signature. We find that future facilities such as the ILC, CEPC, and FCC-ee will be sensitive to the axion–photon–dark photon coupling down to the order of 10 − 4 ​ GeV − 1 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1} for dark photon masses around 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 ​ GeV ) \mathcal{O}(10\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}) , assuming that the axion is extremely light and escapes detection. Existing constraints from LEP II are analyzed for comparison. Furthermore, the mass of dark photon can be determined by measuring the sharp drop-off in the distribution of the recoil mass.



I
Introduction

The exploration of light and weakly coupled particles beyond the Standard Model (SM) is interesting on its own in the search for new physics. Among the most theoretically motivated candidates are axions, originally proposed to solve the strong CP problem (see, e.g.
Choi et al. 2021
for a review), and dark photons, which arise as gauge bosons of hidden
U(1)
symmetries appearing in many extensions of the SM, including string-theoretic and grand unification models (see, e.g.
Fabbrichesi et al. 2020
for a review).

Both axions and dark photons are typically light and feebly interacting with the visible sector through higher-dimensional operators or a kinetic mixing term. Of particular interest is the possibility of an interaction vertex involving an axion, a photon, and a dark photon, which can arise naturally in UV completions involving heavy fermions charged under both SM and dark gauge groups
Kaneta et al. 2017a
;
Fang et al. 2025
.
This interaction gives rise to a rich phenomenology not only in astrophysical and cosmological contexts
Kaneta et al. 2017b
;
Choi et al. 2020a
;
Kalashev et al. 2019
;
Choi et al. 2020b
;
Hook et al. 2020
;
Arias et al. 2021
;
Hook et al. 2021
;
Domcke et al. 2022
;
Gutiérrez et al. 2021
;
Carenza et al. 2023
;
Hook et al. 2024
;
Hong et al. 2024
;
Broadberry et al. 2024
, but also in collider and reactor experiments, where it leads to distinctive signatures beyond those induced by kinetic mixing alone
Biswas et al. 2019
;
Deniverville et al. 2021
;
Lane et al. 2023
;
Jodłowski 2023
. Moreover, the interaction of the axion–photon–dark photon has also been studied in various other contexts
Chen et al. 2024
;
Ding et al. 2025
.

In this work, we explore the phenomenology of the axion–photon–dark photon interaction at LEP II and future lepton colliders. In particular, we study processes
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{\prime}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{\prime}a
followed by decay
\gamma^{\prime}\to\gamma a
, where
\gamma^{\prime}
and
a
represent dark photon and axion, respectively. These processes yield the same collider signature consisting of a single photon plus missing energy, as the axion escapes detection. The single-photon signature is especially clean at lepton colliders because of the relatively low and well-understood backgrounds.
We analyze existing data from LEP II to place constraints on the axion–dark photon–photon coupling and then project the sensitivity of future machines such as the International Linear Collider (ILC)
Fujii et al. 2019
, the Future Circular Collider (FCC-ee)
Abada et al. 2019
, and the Circular Electron–Positron Collider (CEPC)
Abdallah et al. 2024
. In addition, we demonstrate that the recoil mass spectrum in these events contains a distinctive maximum feature that can be used to reconstruct the mass of dark photon.

III

IV

II
The Model

We consider a dark sector involving a light pseudo-scalar
a
and a dark photon
\gamma^{\prime}
that originate from a broken
\text{U(1)}_{D}
global symmetry and a broken
\text{U(1)}_{X}
gauge symmetry
Kaneta et al. 2017a
;
Fang et al. 2025
, respectively. The relevant interactions can be described by an effective Lagrangian that includes both kinetic mixing and a dimension-five operator involving the axion, dark photon, and SM photon:

\mathcal{L}\supset-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}g_{a\gamma^{\prime}\gamma}aF_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\prime\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}m_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{2}A^{\prime}_{\mu}A^{\mu\prime},

(1)

where
A^{\prime}_{\mu}
is the dark photon field, and
F_{\mu\nu}
and
F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}
are the field strength tensors of the SM photon and the dark photon, respectively, and
\tilde{F}^{\prime\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F^{\prime}_{\rho\sigma}
is the dual field strength. The coupling
g_{a\gamma^{\prime}\gamma}
has a dimension of
1/\text{mass}
, and its magnitude depends on the UV completion, such as heavy fermion loops or anomaly-induced interactions.

Due to kinetic mixing, the dark photon can decay into SM particles.
The partial decay width of the dark photon into a pair of charged leptons is given by:

\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-})=\frac{\epsilon^{2}e^{2}}{12\pi}m_{\gamma^{\prime}}\left(1+\frac{2m_{\ell}^{2}}{m_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\ell}^{2}}{m_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{2}}},

(2)

where
e
is the electromagnetic coupling and
m_{\ell}
is the mass of the lepton
\ell
.

For hadrons, partial decay widths can be related to the muon channel via:

\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime}\to\text{hadrons})=\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})\times R(m_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{2}),

(3)

where the function

R(m^{2}_{\gamma^{\prime}})\equiv\frac{\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to\text{hadrons})}{\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})},

encodes the effects of hadronic resonances and the mixing of the dark photon with QCD vector mesons. The values of
R(m^{2}_{\gamma^{\prime}})
can be taken from measurements
Navas et al. 2024
.

As
\gamma^{\prime}
is heavy enough, it could decay into a SM
W
-boson pair. The partial decay width is

\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime}\to W^{+}W^{-})=\frac{\epsilon^{2}e^{2}}{192\pi}m_{\gamma^{\prime}}\left(\frac{m_{\gamma^{\prime}}}{m_{W}}\right)^{4}\left(1-4\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{m_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)^{3/2}\lambda(x),

(4)

where
\lambda(x)=1+20x^{2}+12x^{4}
with
x=m_{W}/m_{\gamma^{\prime}}
.

In addition, the dimension-five operator allows the decay
\gamma^{\prime}\to\gamma a
when kinematically allowed, with a partial width given by:

\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime}\to a\gamma)=\frac{g_{a\gamma^{\prime}\gamma}^{2}}{96\pi}m_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{3}\left(1-\frac{m_{a}^{2}}{m_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)^{3},

(5)

where
m_{a}
is mass of the axion.
In the case of a heavy dark photon with a extremely light axion,
m_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gtrsim 10
GeV, namely, the dark photon is much heavier than the axion and hadrons,
\gamma^{\prime}\to a\gamma
will be the dominant decay channel if the numerical values for
\epsilon
and
g_{a\gamma^{\prime}\gamma}
are of the same size. This is the parameter space on which we focus in this paper. A heavy dark photon, without the existence of axion, has been searched at the CERN Large Hadron Collider, LHC, using
pp\to\gamma^{\prime}
with
\gamma^{\prime}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
for a dark photon up to 70 GeV by the LHCb collaboration
Aaij et al. 2020
and up to 200 GeV by the CMS collaboration
Sirunyan et al. 2020
. The kinematic mixing parameter
\epsilon^{2}\gtrsim 10^{-6}(10^{-5})
is excluded for
m_{\gamma^{\prime}}\simeq 10
GeV (
70
GeV)
Aaij et al. 2020
, and
\epsilon^{2}\gtrsim 5\times 10^{-5}
as
m_{\gamma^{\prime}}=200
GeV
Sirunyan et al. 2020
.

IV
Conclusion

We have explored the potential of future lepton colliders to probe the interaction between axions, dark photons, and Standard Model photons through dimension-five operators. Such interactions can naturally arise in models with light pseudoscalar particles and dark photons, and may be generated at the loop level via heavy fermions charged under both
U(1)_{\rm EM}
and
U(1)_{D}
.

Focusing on single-photon events with missing energy, we analyzed the signal processes
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{\prime}\to\gamma a
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{*}\to a\gamma^{\prime}\to aa\gamma
, under the assumption of a light axion that escapes detection. The axion–photon–dark photon coupling
g_{a\gamma^{\prime}\gamma}
larger than about
10^{-3}\penalty\ \rm{GeV}^{-1}
is excluded by LEP II data for
m_{\gamma^{\prime}}\lesssim 100
GeV. We studied the relevant kinematic distributions and developed selection strategies to optimize the signal-to-background ratio. In particular, the recoil mass and the angular distribution of the emitted photon were found to be especially effective in discriminating signals from background.

Using these strategies, we projected the sensitivity reach of ILC, CEPC, and FCC-ee. We found that these future facilities can probe the coupling
g_{a\gamma^{\prime}\gamma}
in the range of
\sim 10^{-4}\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
to
10^{-3}\penalty\ \mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
for dark photon masses between 10 GeV and 230 GeV.
Furthermore, we demonstrated that the photon energy spectrum offers a viable method to reconstruct the dark photon mass through recoil kinematics, providing an additional handle on signal characterization.

Our results highlight the importance of high-luminosity lepton colliders in exploring light and weakly coupled new physics, offering complementary coverage to fixed-target, astrophysical, and hadronic collider searches. This work paves the way for probing rich dynamics within the dark sector at next-generation facilities.

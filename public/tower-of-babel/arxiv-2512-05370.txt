A Tight-binding Approach for Computing Subwavelength Guided Modes in Crystals with Line Defects
Habib Ammari, Erik Orvehed Hiltunen, Ping Liu, Borui Miao, Yi Zhu

Abstract
In this paper, we consider waveguide systems operating at subwavelength scales. A key feature of these systems is that they are high contrast periodic resonator systems with line defects, leading to resonant phenomena at subwavelength scales. Their spectral properties at the subwavelength scales can be approximated by using the capacitance matrix formulation. Our main objective is to investigate the exponential decay of the off-diagonal elements of the capacitance matrices associated with these waveguide systems. This decay property rigorously justifies a tight-binding approximation, which in turn enables a novel and efficient approach for computing the spectral properties of subwavelength resonators with non-compact defects. Various numerical experiments are provided to validate the theoretical results, including applications to topological interface modes.

Introduction

The study of line defects in subwavelength bandgap materials has had an immense impact on various technological applications. The capability of subwavelength devices to guide waves significantly below the diffraction limit is considered the key to the development of the next generation of communication, biomedical, quantum, and sensing technologies
Cheben et al. 2018
;
Sorger et al. 2011
;
Lemoult et al. 2013
;
Lemoult et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2000
. Similar interest has been devoted to photonic and phononic crystals, where the guiding of waves due to line defects was investigated both numerically and analytically
Ammari and Santosa 2004
;
Kuchment and Ong 2010
;
Kuchment and Ong 2003
;
Brown et al. 2015
;
Brown et al. 2014
;
Hoang and Radosz 2014
;
Brown et al. 2017
;
Delourme and Fliss 2024
;
Amenoagbadji et al. 2025
;
Delourme et al. 2017
;
Figotin and Klein 1997
. However, this guiding phenomenon occurs only at scales that are comparable to the size of the unit cell of the microstructure.

In the subwavelength regime, as demonstrated in
Ammari et al. 2017
, bandgaps corresponding to wavelengths much larger than the unit cell of the microstructure can be found in a broad class of high contrast periodic resonator systems. A subwavelength resonant frequency of a high contrast periodic resonator structure with a defect is called a subwavelength bandgap frequency if it lies within a subwavelength bandgap of the unperturbed structure. In
Ammari et al. 2022
, it is shown that the introduction of a line defect creates a defect band and that there are no bound modes in the defect direction.
More precisely, using the fictitious source superposition method, originally introduced in
Wilcox et al. 2005
, the approach in
Ammari et al. 2022
to create defect bands entirely within the subwavelength bandgap involves considering circular resonators and modeling the defect resonators as unperturbed resonators with additional fictitious monopole and dipole sources. The size of the perturbation needed to have the entire defect band fall into the bandgap is explicitly quantified. Moreover, in order to have guided waves along the line defect, the defect mode must not only be localized to the line, but also propagating along the line. In other words, we must exclude the case of standing waves in the line defect, i.e. modes that are localized in the direction of the line. Using an explicit formula for the defect band, it is shown in
Ammari et al. 2022
that it is nowhere flat, and hence does not correspond to bound modes in the direction of the line. Based on the formulation of quasi-periodic subwavelength defect modes as characteristic values of a family of quasi-periodic operators, the defect band is numerically computed. These quasi-periodic operators are discretized using a truncated Fourier basis, and their associated characteristic value problems
are formulated as root-finding problems for the determinants of the truncated operators and solved numerically using Muller’s method
Ammari et al. 2018
.

In this paper, we propose a new and more accurate approach than the one introduced in
Ammari et al. 2022
. This method can be applied to general settings of crystals with non-compact defects.
Our strategy is to first reduce the problem to one strip using Floquet?Bloch theory. Then we introduce the discrete approximation of the defect band in terms of a family of quasi-periodic capacitance operators. By establishing a tight-binding approximation for these capacitance operators, we reduce the problem of computing the defect band to finding the eigenvalues of a family of tridiagonal matrices that lie within the bandgap of the unperturbed structure. Our tight-binding approximation generalizes the one first obtained in
Ammari and
Qiu 2025
for the capacitance operator associated with the unperturbed crystal.

The novel approach developed in this paper for efficiently computing band defects in the subwavelength regime can also be applied to calculate point defect modes in crystals of subwavelength resonators and topological interface modes between two half-crystals with different topological invariants.

2.1
Wave guiding in the subwavelength regime

We consider the following eigenvalue problem in the waveguide system:

\kappa(x)\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\rho(x)}\nabla u\right)=-\omega^{2}u,\quad u\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\partial D_{\mathrm{full}}).

(2.2)

Here, the density and bulk modulus functions
\rho(x),\kappa(x)
are given by

\rho(x)\triangleq\rho_{{}_{0}}\chi_{{}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus D_{\mathrm{full}}}}(x)+\rho_{{}_{1}}\chi_{{}_{D_{\mathrm{full}}}}(x),\quad\kappa(x)\triangleq\kappa_{{}_{0}}\chi_{{}_{{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus D_{\mathrm{full}}}}}(x)+\kappa_{{}_{1}}\chi_{{}_{D_{\mathrm{full}}}}(x),

(2.3)

where
\chi_{{}_{D_{\mathrm{full}}}}
and
\chi_{{}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus D_{\mathrm{full}}}}
denote the characteristic functions of
D_{\mathrm{full}}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus D_{\mathrm{full}}

\rho_{{}_{0}},\kappa_{{}_{0}}
and
\rho_{{}_{1}},\kappa_{{}_{1}}
represent the densities and bulk moduli outside and inside the inclusions
D_{\mathrm{full}}
, respectively. We also introduce the wave speeds
\mu_{{}_{0}},\mu_{{}_{1}}
inside and outside the inclusions as

\mu_{{}_{0}}\triangleq\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{{}_{0}}}{\rho_{{}_{0}}}},\quad\mu_{{}_{1}}\triangleq\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{{}_{1}}}{\rho_{{}_{1}}}},

and assume that the contrast parameter
\delta
satisfies

\delta\triangleq\frac{\rho_{{}_{1}}}{\rho_{{}_{0}}}\ll 1.

We now consider Bloch waves in the
v_{{}_{1}}
direction, which satisfy:

\displaystyle\kappa(x)\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\rho(x)}\nabla u^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=-\omega^{2}u^{\alpha},\quad u^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\partial\widetilde{D}),

\displaystyle u^{\alpha}(x+mv_{{}_{1}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha m}u^{\alpha}(x),\quad x\in\widetilde{Y},m\in\mathbb{Z},

where the parameter

denotes the quasi-periodicity. This is equivalent to the following system of partial differential equations:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta u^{\alpha}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{\mu_{{}_{0}}^{2}}u^{\alpha}=0,\quad x\in\widetilde{Y}\setminus\overline{\widetilde{D}},\\
&\Delta u^{\alpha}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{\mu_{{}_{1}}^{2}}u^{\alpha}=0,\quad x\in\widetilde{D},\\
&u^{\alpha}|_{+}-u^{\alpha}|_{-}=0,\quad y\in\partial\widetilde{D},\\
&\delta\left.\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla u^{\alpha}\right|_{+}-\left.\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla u^{\alpha}\right|_{-}=0,\quad y\in\partial\widetilde{D},\\
&u^{\alpha}(x+mv_{{}_{1}})=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha m}u^{\alpha}(x),\quad x\in\widetilde{Y},m\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

In this paper, we focus on subwavelength frequencies (for
\alpha\neq 0

\omega\sim\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\delta})

\quad u^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\partial\widetilde{D}).

The solution can be represented using single-layer potentials:

\displaystyle u^{\alpha}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,k_{1}}_{\widetilde{D}}[\phi](x),\quad x\in\widetilde{D},\\
&\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,k_{0}}_{\widetilde{D}}[\psi](x),\quad x\in\widetilde{Y}\setminus\overline{\widetilde{D}},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

for some densities
\phi,\ \psi\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\widetilde{D})

The single-layer potential

8.4

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,k_{1}}_{\widetilde{D}}[\phi](y)=\mathcal{S}_{\widetilde{D}}^{\alpha,k_{0}}[\psi](y),\\

Theorem 1

For all

Theorem 2

For

satisfy the following estimate:

Proof.

Given the estimate (

\widetilde{U}_{n}^{\alpha}(x)\triangleq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{j,n}(x)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha j}.

(2.13)

By definition and the uniqueness of the solution to (

Clearly, we have

\widehat{C}_{n,q}^{\alpha}=-\int_{\partial D_{0,n}}\nu_{y}\cdot\left.\nabla\widetilde{U}_{q}^{\alpha}(y)\right|_{+}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

From (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\widehat{C}^{\alpha}_{n,q}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(p-m)}\mathrm{d}\alpha&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{\partial D_{0,n}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{j,q}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha j}\Big]\Big|_{+}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(p-m)}\mathrm{d}\alpha\\

By the translation invariance in the

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\widehat{C}^{\alpha}_{n,q}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(p-m)}\mathrm{d}\alpha=-\int_{\partial D_{m,n}}\nu_{y}\cdot\left.\nabla U_{p,q}(y)\right|_{+}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=C_{m,n}^{p,q}.\end{aligned}

This formula is consistent with

\widehat{C}^{\alpha}_{n,q}=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha p}C^{p,q}_{0,n}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha m}C^{0,q}_{m,n}.

(2.15)

From

1
, we directly obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\widehat{C}^{\alpha}_{n,q}|\leq C\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\min\{\rho^{|p|},\rho^{|n-q|}\}\leq\widetilde{C}\rho^{|n-q|}(1+|n-q|).\end{aligned}

For all
n,q\in\mathbb{Z}

\rho^{\frac{|n-q|}{2}}(1+|n-q|)\leq M<+\infty,

holds for some constant

This estimate proves the theorem and establishes the exponential decay of the off-diagonal elements of the quasi-periodic capacitance matrix
\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in[-\pi,\pi)
.

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l>n}\int_{\partial D_{0,l}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U^{0}_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=-\sum_{l>n}\widehat{C}^{0}_{l,0},

\displaystyle\int_{I_{1,0,-n}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U^{0}_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l<-n}\int_{\partial D_{0,l}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U^{0}_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=-\sum_{l<-n}\widehat{C}^{0}_{l,0}.

The smoothness of the harmonic function
U_{0}^{0}
implies the bounds

\displaystyle\left|\int_{I_{1,0,n+1}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U^{0}_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\right|\leq\int_{I_{1,0,n+1}}|\nabla U^{0}_{0}(y)|\mathrm{d}\sigma(y),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{I_{1,0,-n}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U^{0}_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\right|\leq\int_{I_{1,0,-n}}|\nabla U^{0}_{0}(y)|\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

Applying Harnack’s inequality (
Lemma

5
) and noting that
U^{0}_{0}
is positive, we obtain

\bigg|\sum_{l>n}\widehat{C}^{0}_{l,0}\bigg|\leq\frac{4}{r}\int_{I_{1,0,n+1}}U^{0}_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y),\quad\bigg|\sum_{l<-n}\widehat{C}^{0}_{l,0}\bigg|\leq\frac{4}{r}\int_{I_{1,0,-n}}U^{0}_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

(2.18)

By (
2.15
) and
Proposition

1
, we
obtain the following estimates for
n\geq 0
:

\displaystyle|C^{0,0}_{m,n+1}|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\sum_{l>n}C^{0,0}_{m,l}\bigg|\leq\bigg|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{l>n}C^{0,0}_{m,l}\bigg|=\bigg|\sum_{l>n}\widehat{C}^{0}_{l,0}\bigg|,

\displaystyle|C^{0,0}_{m,-(n+1)}|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\sum_{l<-n}C^{0,0}_{m,l}\bigg|\leq\bigg|\sum_{l<-n}\widehat{C}^{0}_{l,0}\bigg|.

Combining these with the previous inequalities (
2.18
) and (
2.16
), we conclude that

|C^{0,0}_{m,n+1}|\leq C_{3}\rho_{0}^{n},\quad|C^{0,0}_{m,-(n+1)}|\leq C_{3}\rho_{0}^{n},\quad n\geq 0.

Defining
C_{4}=C_{3}\rho_{0}^{-1}
, we obtain the final estimates in the
x_{2}
direction:

|C^{0,0}_{m,n}|\leq C_{4}\rho_{0}^{n},\quad|C^{0,0}_{m,-n}|\leq C_{3}\rho_{0}^{n},\quad n\geq 0.

(2.19)

We now turn to estimates (
2.17
). Consider the harmonic function
U_{0,0}
on the regions
\cup_{l>m}\widetilde{Y}_{l}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{l}
and
\cup_{l<-m}\widetilde{Y}_{l}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{l}
. For
m\geq 0
, similar reasoning yields

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U_{0,0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l>m}\int_{\partial\widetilde{D}_{l}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U_{0,0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=-\sum_{l>m}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}C^{0,0}_{l,n},

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,-m}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U^{0}_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l<-m}\int_{\partial\widetilde{D}_{l}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U^{0}_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=-\sum_{l<-m}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}C^{0,0}_{l,n}.

Applying Harnack’s inequality again, we find

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{l>m}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}C^{0,0}_{l,n}\bigg|=\left|\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U_{0,0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{r}\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1}}U_{0,0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y),

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{l<-m}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}C^{0,0}_{l,n}\bigg|=\left|\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,-m}}\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla U_{0,0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{r}\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,-m}}U_{0,0}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

And we have

C^{0,0}_{m+1,n}\leq C_{5}\rho^{n}_{0},\quad C^{0,0}_{-(m+1),n}\leq C_{5}\rho^{n}_{0},\quad m\geq 0.

Defining
C_{6}=C_{5}\rho_{0}^{-1}
, we obtain the estimates in the
x_{1}
direction:

C^{0,0}_{m,n}\leq C_{5}\rho^{n}_{0},\quad C^{0,0}_{-m,n}\leq C_{5}\rho^{n}_{0},\quad m\geq 0.

(2.20)

Combining (
2.19
) and (
2.20
), we arrive at the desired estimate (
2.11
).
∎

The proof of
Theorem

1
is then reduced to proving the estimates (
2.16
) and (
2.17
). This will be done in the next two sections.

3
Proof of estimate (
2.16
)

This section establishes the exponential decay of the integral of the periodic harmonic function
U_{q}^{0}
on
I_{1,0,n}
as
n\to\pm\infty
, i.e., estimate (
2.16
).

For notational simplicity, we define:

Y_{n}\triangleq Y_{0,n},\quad D_{n}\triangleq D_{0,n},\quad I_{n}\triangleq I_{1,0,n},\quad L_{n}\triangleq L_{1,0,n},\quad\omega_{n}\triangleq\omega_{1,0,n},\quad x_{0,n}=x_{n},

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
to simplify the notation. The distance between
x_{n}
and
I_{n}
, denoted by
d(x_{n},I_{n})
, satisfies

d(x_{n},I_{n})\geq r+c>0.

This fact follows from
2
. Throughout this section, we let
q=0
.

By the uniqueness theorem, the restriction of
U_{0}^{0}
to
\cup_{n\geq 1}(Y_{n}\setminus D_{n})
coincides with the unique solution to the following boundary value problem with periodic boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\widetilde{U}_{+}(x)=0,\quad x\in\cup_{n\geq 1}(Y_{n}\setminus D_{n}),\\
&\widetilde{U}_{+}(y)=0,\quad y\in\cup_{n\geq 1}\partial D_{n},\\
&\widetilde{U}_{+}(y)=U_{0}^{0}\big|_{I_{1}}(y),\quad y\in I_{1},\\
&\widetilde{U}_{+}(x+mv_{{}_{1}})=\widetilde{U}_{+}(x),\quad x\in\cup_{n\geq 1}Y_{n},\;m\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

Since
U_{0}^{0}\big|_{I_{1}}(y)
is a positive smooth function, it belongs to

\mathbb{L}^{1}(I_{1})\cap\mathbb{L}^{2}(I_{1})
. In the following, we let
\widetilde{U}_{+}
be
0
inside the inclusions
\cup_{n\geq 1}D_{n}
.

3.1
Truncation method

In this part, we introduce the truncation method to estimate
\widetilde{U}_{+}
on
\cup_{n\geq 1}(Y_{n}\setminus D_{n})
. Before providing details, note that the line
L_{n}
divides the cell
Y_{n}
into two parts, denoted by
Y_{n,+}
and
Y_{n,-}
, as shown in
Figure

2
.

Figure 2
:
Cell
Y_{n}
. The line
L_{n}
divides the cell into two parts, denoted as
Y_{n,+}
and
Y_{n,-}
.

The procedure can be divided into three recursive steps. Given the boundary value
\widetilde{g}_{{}_{1}}=U_{0}^{0}\big|_{I_{1}}>0
on
I_{1}
, for
n\geq 1
,

(1)

First, solve the following boundary value problem for the Laplace equation on the half space:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}(x)=0,\quad x\in\cup_{l\geq n}Y_{l},\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}(y)=\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n}}(y),\quad y\in I_{n},\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}(x+mv_{{}_{1}})=\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}(x),\quad x\in\cup_{l\geq n}Y_{l},\;m\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.2)

Due to the smoothness of
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}
, one can take its trace on
L_{n}

in
Y_{n}
, denoted by
\widetilde{h}_{n}
.

(2)

Next, solve another boundary value problem on the half space:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(x)=0,\quad x\in Y_{n,+}\cup(\cup_{l\geq n+1}Y_{l}),\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(y)=\widetilde{h}_{n}(1-\chi_{{}_{n}}),\quad y\in L_{n},\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(x+mv_{{}_{1}})=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(x),\quad x\in Y_{n,+}\cup(\cup_{l\geq n+1}Y_{l}),\;m\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

Here,
\chi_{{}_{n}}
is a positive smooth function on
L_{n}
satisfying

\chi_{{}_{n}}(y)=1,\quad y\in\omega_{n},\quad\text{and }\operatorname{supp}\chi_{{}_{n}}\subset L_{n}\cap D_{n}.

From this, its trace on
I_{n+1}
can also be taken, denoted by
\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n+1}}
.

(3)

Repeat Steps (1) and (2).

The left and right panels of
Figure

3
illustrate the truncation method and the functions
\mathcal{V}_{n}
and
\mathcal{W}_{n}
, respectively.

Figure 3
:

\mathcal{V}_{n}
and the derivation of
h_{n}
. Right panel: Illustration of
\mathcal{W}_{n}
and the derivation of
g_{n+1}
.

Remark 2
.

We emphasize that the functions
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n},\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
are solved on the half space without inclusions
\{D_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
. Therefore, they can be explicitly represented by Fourier series; see Appendix
9
.

Based on this, we can prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Given the solutions
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n},\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
obtained by the truncation method, it holds that for all
n\geq 1
,

\widetilde{U}_{+}(y)\leq\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n}}(y),\quad y\in I_{n}.

Proof.

We prove this by induction. First,
\widetilde{U}_{+}(y)=\widetilde{g}_{{}_{1}}(y)
on
I_{1}
. Assuming
\widetilde{U}_{+}(y)\leq\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n}}(y)
on
I_{n}
,
consider the harmonic function
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+})=0,\quad x\in Y_{n},\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+}\geq 0,\quad y\in I_{n},\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+}>0,\quad y\in\partial\big(\cup_{l\geq n}D_{l}\big),\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+}\text{ satisfies the periodic boundary condition.}\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

Thus,
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+}\geq 0
for all
x\in Y_{n}
, which implies that

\widetilde{h}_{n}\geq\widetilde{U}_{+}\quad\text{ on }L_{n}.

Since
\widetilde{U}_{+}
vanishes in
D_{n}
, it follows that

\widetilde{h}_{n}(1-\chi_{{}_{n}})(y)\geq\widetilde{U}_{+},\quad y\in L_{n}.

Next, consider the function
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+}
. By definition, it can be verified that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+})=0,\quad x\in Y_{n,+},\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+}\geq 0,\quad y\in L_{n},\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+}\geq 0,\quad y\in\partial D_{n}\cap Y_{n,+},\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+}\geq 0,\quad y\in\partial\big(\cup_{l\geq n+1}D_{l}\big),\\
&\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}-\widetilde{U}_{+}\text{ satisfies the periodic boundary condition.}\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

Hence, we conclude that
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\geq\widetilde{U}_{+}
for all
x\in Y_{n,+}\setminus D_{n}
. Denoting the trace of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}
on
I_{n+1}
by
\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n+1}}
, we obtain
\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n+1}}\geq\widetilde{U}_{+}
. This completes the proof.
∎

A direct consequence of the above theorem is

\int_{I_{n}}U_{0}^{0}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=\int_{I_{n}}\widetilde{U}_{+}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\leq\int_{I_{n}}\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y),\quad n\geq 1.

The theorem also implies that, for
n\geq 1
,

U^{0}_{0}(x)\left\{\begin{aligned} &\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}(x),\quad x\in Y_{n,-},\\
&\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}(x),\quad x\in Y_{n,+}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.6)

In the following, we estimate the
\mathbb{L}^{1}(I_{n})
norm of
\{\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
as
n\to+\infty
.

3.2
Exponential decay of the integral on
I_{n}

In this subsection, we prove the decay of the integrals of
U_{0}^{0}
on
I_{\pm n}
. This relies substantially on the following theorem characterizing the decay of the integrals of
\widetilde{g}_{n}
.

Theorem 4
.

Given the periodic functions
\{\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}
determined by the truncation method, we have

\int_{I_{n+1}}\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n+1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=\int_{I_{n}}\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n}}(1-\widetilde{V}_{n})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

(3.7)

Here,
\widetilde{V}_{n}
is the
auxiliary function
determined by the following boundary value problem on the lower half space:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\widetilde{V}_{n}=0,\quad x\in Y_{n,-}\cup\Big(\cup_{l\leq n}Y_{l}\Big),\\
&\widetilde{V}_{n}=\chi_{{}_{n}},\quad y\in L_{n},\\
&\widetilde{V}_{n}(x+mv_{{}_{1}})=\widetilde{V}_{n}(x),\quad x\in Y_{n,-}\cup\Big(\cup_{l\leq n}Y_{l}\Big),\;m\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.8)

Moreover, the function
1-\widetilde{V}_{n}
satisfies

1-\widetilde{V}_{n}\leq c<1,\quad\forall x\in I_{n},\;n\geq 1.

Proof.

From the truncation method, we have from (
9.3
)

\widetilde{h}_{n}(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{g}_{n}(m)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi|m|d(x_{n},I_{n})}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}2\pi mx_{1}},

where
\widehat{g}_{n}(m)
is the
m
-th Fourier coefficient, given by

\widehat{g}_{n}(m)\triangleq\int_{0}^{1}\widetilde{g}_{n}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}2\pi mx}\mathrm{d}x.

Recall that the distance between
x_{n}
and
I_{n}
is denoted by
d(x_{n},I_{n})
. Thus, the
m
-th Fourier coefficient of
\widetilde{h}_{n}
is

\widehat{h}_{n}(m)=\widehat{g}_{n}(m)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi|m|d(x_{n},I_{n})}.

Suppose that the Fourier coefficients of
1-\chi_{{}_{n}}
are given by
\{\widehat{b}_{n}(m)\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}
, then we can directly compute

\int_{I_{n+1}}\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n+1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{g}_{n}(m)\widehat{b}_{n}(-m)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi|m|d(x_{n},I_{n})}.

From the definition of the auxiliary problem, we have
\widetilde{V}_{n}>0
on
I_{n}
, and

1-\widetilde{V}_{n}(x_{1})=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{b}_{n}(m)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi|m|d(x_{n},I_{n})}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}2\pi mx_{1}}.

It follows that

\widetilde{g}_{n}(1-\widetilde{V}_{n})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{g}_{n}(m)\widehat{b}_{n}(n-m)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi|n-m|d(x_{n},I_{n})}\bigg)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}2\pi nx_{1}}.

Integrating over
I_{n}
gives the desired result.

We now turn to estimating the upper bound of
1-\widetilde{V}_{n}
on
I_{n}
. By the maximum principle,
\widetilde{V}_{n}\in(0,1)
, it only remains to show that there exists a uniform upper bound for
1-\widetilde{V}_{n}
with respect to
n\geq 1
. This follows from the
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}
-continuous dependence of
\widetilde{V}_{n}
on the parameters of the boundary value problem.
∎

Therefore, we have

\int_{I_{n+1}}\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n+1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\leq\rho_{n}\int_{I_{n}}\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

(3.9)

Here, the constants
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
have a uniform upper bound
\rho<1
.
From this and from
Theorem

3
, we are led to

\int_{I_{n+1}}U_{0}^{0}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\leq\int_{I_{n+1}}\widetilde{g}_{{}_{n+1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\leq\rho^{n}\int_{I_{1}}U_{0}^{0}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

Since
\widetilde{g}_{1}
is not larger than 1 by the maximum principle, one can prove the first part of the estimate (
2.16
) for all
q\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For
n\leq-1
, similar estimates can be obtained by considering the restriction of
U^{0}_{0}
in
\cup_{n\leq-1}Y_{n}\setminus D_{n}
, as illustrated in
Figure

4
.

Figure 4
:
Left panel: Illustration of
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}
and the derivation of
h_{n}
for
n<0
. Right panel: Illustration of

and the derivation of
g_{n+1}
for
n<0
.

Thus, we can also define
\{\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n},\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\}_{n\leq-1}
and the related trace
\{g_{{}_{n}}\}_{n\leq-1}
on
\{I_{n}\}_{n\leq-1}
and prove that the following estimate holds

\int_{I_{-n}}U^{0}_{0}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\leq\int_{I_{-n}}\widetilde{g}_{{}_{-n}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\leq\rho^{n}\int_{I_{0}}U_{0}^{0}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

(3.10)

3.3
Estimate on the boundary
\partial\widetilde{Y}

In this subsection, we estimate the trace of
U^{0}_{0}
on the boundary
\partial\widetilde{Y}
, which will be used in
Section

4
. From the previous definitions, it can be expressed as

\partial\widetilde{Y}=\widetilde{I}_{2,0}\cup\widetilde{I}_{2,1}.

We first present a version of the trace inequality which can be easily derived from the fundamental theorem of calculus.

Proposition 2
.

The following estimate holds for all
1\leq p<\infty
and
u\in\mathbb{W}^{1,p}(\widetilde{Y})
:

\int_{\partial\widetilde{Y}}|u|^{p}(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\leq C_{p}\bigg[\int_{\widetilde{Y}}|u|^{p}(x)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\widetilde{Y}}|\partial_{x_{1}}u|^{p}(x)\mathrm{d}x\bigg],

where
C_{p}
is a positive constant that depends only on
p
.

Combining the smoothness of
U_{0}^{0}
and the above results, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 2
.

The trace of
U_{q}^{0}
on
\widetilde{I}_{2,0}
and
\widetilde{I}_{2,1}
belongs to
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,0})\cap\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,0})
and
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,1})\cap\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,1})
, respectively. Moreover, there exists a real constant
M
such that the following estimates hold for all
q\in\mathbb{Z}
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|U_{q}^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,0})}+\|U_{q}^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,1})}&\leq M<+\infty,\\
\|U_{q}^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,0})}+\|U_{q}^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,1})}&\leq M<+\infty,\end{aligned}

(3.11)

Proof.

From the above analysis, we can deduce from the properties of the Poisson kernel that

\sum_{|n|\geq 1}\Big[\|\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(Y_{n,-})}+\|\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(Y_{n,+})}\Big]\leq M<+\infty,

(3.12)

Considering the derivative
\{\partial_{x_{1}}\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n},\partial_{x_{1}}\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\}_{|n|\geq 1}
and slightly modifying the above procedures, we can further show that

\sum_{|n|\geq 1}\Big[\|\partial_{x_{1}}\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(Y_{n,-})}+\|\partial_{x_{1}}\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(Y_{n,+})}\Big]\leq M<+\infty.

(3.13)

Then the trace theorem together with
Theorem

3
implies

\|U_{q}^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,0})}+\|U_{q}^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,1})}\leq M<+\infty.

Combining (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), one has

\sum_{|n|\geq 1}\Big[\|\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,1}(Y_{n,-})}+\|\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{n}\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,1}(Y_{n,+})}\Big]<+\infty.

(3.14)

Then the trace theorem gives

\|U_{q}^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,0})}+\|U_{q}^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,1})}\leq M<+\infty.

Note that the above estimates are uniform with respect to
q
.
∎

(4.1)

U_{0,0}\big|_{\widetilde{I}_{2,1}}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,1})\cap\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,1}).

4.1
Truncation method on

In this subsection, we introduce the truncation method on

to estimate

on the boundary
\widetilde{I}_{2,m}

divides the unit cell

.

Y_{m,n}
. The line

divides the cell into two parts, denoted as

and
Y_{m,n,-}
.

The procedure can also be split into three recursive steps. Given the boundary value

on
\widetilde{I}_{2,1}
, for

,

(1)

First, solve the following boundary value problem for the Laplace equation on the left half space for all

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\mathcal{V}_{m,n}(x)=0,\quad x\in\cup_{l\geq m}\widetilde{Y}_{l},\\
&\mathcal{V}_{m,n}(y)=g_{{}_{m}}(y),\quad y\in I_{2,m,n},\\

(4.2)

We can take its trace on the lines

(2)

Next, solve the boundary value problems for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\mathcal{W}_{m,n}(x)=0,\quad x\in\widetilde{Y}_{m,n,+}\cup\Big(\cup_{l\geq m+1}\widetilde{Y}_{l}\Big),\\
&\mathcal{W}_{m}(y)=h_{m,n}(1-\chi_{{}_{m,n}})(y),\quad y\in\widetilde{I}_{2,m}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.3)

Here,
\{\chi_{{}_{m,n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
are positive smooth functions on
L_{2,m,n}
satisfying

\chi_{{}_{m,n}}(y)=1,\quad y\in\omega_{2,m,n},\quad\text{and }\operatorname{supp}\chi_{{}_{m,n}}\subset L_{2,m,n}\cap D_{m,n}.

Its trace on
\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1}
is denoted by
\{g_{{}_{m+1,n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Taking the summation with respect to
n
gives
g_{{}_{m+1}}

g_{{}_{m+1}}\triangleq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{{}_{m+1,n}}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1})\cap\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1}).

(4.4)

(3)

Repeat Step (1) and Step (2).

Remark 3
.

It is straightforward to check that the trace of
\{\mathcal{V}_{m,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
on
L_{2,m,n}
satisfies
\{h_{m,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{L}^{1}(L_{2,m,n})\cap\mathbb{L}^{2}(L_{2,m,n})
, given
g_{{}_{m}}\in\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,m})\cap\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,m})
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|h_{m,n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(L_{2,m,n})}\leq\|g_{{}_{m}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,m})},\quad\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|h_{m,n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(L_{2,m,n})}\leq\|g_{{}_{m}}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,m})}.\end{aligned}

From the properties of Poisson’s kernel, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|g_{m,n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(L_{2,m,n})}\leq\|g_{{}_{m}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}(\widetilde{I}_{2,m})},\quad\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|g_{m,n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(L_{2,m,n})}\leq\|g_{{}_{m}}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\widetilde{I}_{2,m})},\end{aligned}

which justifies the summation (
4.4
) in Step (2).

We can also prove the following theorem for the truncation method on
\mathbb{R}
:

Theorem 5
.

Given the solutions
\{\{\mathcal{V}_{m,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\{\mathcal{W}_{m,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\}_{m\geq 1}
obtained by the truncation method on
\mathbb{R}
, it holds that for all
m\geq 1
,

U_{0,0}\leq g_{{}_{m}},\quad y\in\widetilde{I}_{2,m}.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by induction. First,
U_{+}(y)=g_{{}_{1}}
on
\widetilde{I}_{2,1}
. Assuming
U_{+}\leq g_{{}_{m}}
on
\widetilde{I}_{2,m}
, consider the harmonic function
U_{+}
on the half space
\cup_{l\geq m}\widetilde{Y}_{l}
. We decompose it as
U_{+}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{+,m,n}
, where the harmonic function
U_{+,m,n}
is defined by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta U_{+,m,n}(x)=0,\quad x\in\cup_{l\geq m}\widetilde{Y}_{l}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{l},\\
&U_{+,m,y}(y)=0,\quad y\in\cup_{l\geq m}\partial\widetilde{D}_{l},\\
&U_{+,m,n}(y)=U_{0,0}(y),\quad y\in I_{2,m,n},\\
&U_{+,m,n}(y)=0,\quad y\in\widetilde{I}_{2,m}\setminus I_{2,m,n}.\end{aligned}\right.\end{aligned}

From this, consider the harmonic function
\mathcal{V}_{m,n}-U_{+,m,n}
. It can be directly verified that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathcal{V}_{m,n}-U_{+,m,n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\big(\mathcal{V}_{m,n}-U_{+,m,n}(x)\big)=0,\quad x\in\cup_{l\geq m}\widetilde{Y}_{l}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{l},\\
&\mathcal{V}_{m,n}(y)-U_{+,m,n}(y)>0,\quad y\in\cup_{l\geq m}\partial\widetilde{D}_{l},\\
&\mathcal{V}_{m,n}(y)-U_{+,m,n}(y)\geq 0,\quad y\in I_{2,m,n},\\
&\mathcal{V}_{m,n}(y)-U_{+,m,n}(y)=0,\quad y\in\widetilde{I}_{2,m}\setminus I_{2,m,n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Thus, it follows that
\mathcal{V}_{m,n}(y)-U_{+,m,n}
is nonnegative for all
x\in\widetilde{Y}_{m}
, which implies
h_{m,n}\geq U_{+,m,n}
on
L_{2,m,n}
. Since
\{U_{+,m,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is equal to zero in
\widetilde{D}_{m}
, it follows that

h_{m,n}(1-\chi_{{}_{m,n}})(y)\geq U_{+,m,n},\quad y\in L_{2,m,n}.

Next, consider the harmonic function
\mathcal{W}_{m,n}-U_{+,m,n}
. Again, by definition, one has

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\big(\mathcal{W}_{m,n}-U_{+,m,n}(x)\big)=0,\quad x\in\cup_{l\geq m}\widetilde{Y}_{l}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{l},\\
&\mathcal{W}_{m,n}(y)-U_{+,m,n}(y)>0,\quad y\in\cup_{l\geq m+1}\partial\widetilde{D}_{l},\\
&\mathcal{W}_{m,n}(y)-U_{+,m,n}(y)\geq 0,\quad y\in L_{2,m,n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Therefore, we conclude that
\mathcal{W}_{m,n}\geq U_{+,m,n}
in the relevant domain. Taking the trace of
\mathcal{W}_{m,n}
on
\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1}
, denoted by
g_{{}_{m,n}}
, it can be verified that
g_{{}_{m,n}}\geq U_{+,m,n}
. Taking the summation with respect to
n
gives the desired result.
∎

4.2
Exponential decay of the integral on
\widetilde{I}_{2,m}

In this subsection, we estimate the decay of
\{g_{{}_{m}}\}_{m\geq 1}
with respect to
m
, where
g_{{}_{m}}
are determined by the truncation method on
\mathbb{R}
. We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

Given the functions
\{g_{{}_{m}}\}_{m\geq 1}
determined by the truncation method on
\mathbb{R}
, we have

\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1}}g_{{}_{m+1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=\rho\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,m}}g_{{}_{m}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

(4.5)

Here, the parameter
\rho<1
for all
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

We first define the corresponding auxiliary problem. For
m\geq 1
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, it is defined by the solution to the following boundary value problems on the left half space:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta V_{m,n}=0,\quad x\in Y_{m,n,-}\cup\Big(\cup_{l\leq n}\widetilde{Y}_{l}\Big),\\
&V_{m,n}=\chi_{{}_{\omega_{2,m,n}}},\quad y\in L_{2,m,n}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.6)

Here,
\chi_{{}_{\omega_{2,m,n}}}
is the characteristic function on the line segment
\omega_{{}_{2,m,n}}
. Then, from the truncation method on
\mathbb{R}
, we have by a direct calculation that

h_{m,n}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(\widehat{g}_{{}_{m,n}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi|\xi|d(x_{m,n},\widetilde{I}_{2,m})}\Big).

The function
\widehat{g}_{{}_{m,n}}
is defined by the Fourier transform of the function
g_{{}_{m}}\chi_{{}_{I_{2,m,n}}}
, where
\chi_{{}_{I_{2,m,n}}}
denotes the characteristic function of the line segment
I_{2,m,n}
. Again, by the properties of Poisson’s kernel and Step (2) of the truncation method, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1}}g_{{}_{m+1,n}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)&=\int_{L_{2,m,n}}h_{m,n}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\\
&\quad-\int_{L_{2,m,n}}\widehat{g}_{{}_{m,n}}(\eta)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi|\eta|d(x_{m,n},\widetilde{I}_{2,m})}\widehat{\chi}_{{}_{\omega_{2,m,n}}}(-\eta)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\eta).\\
\end{aligned}

Here,
\widehat{\chi}_{{}_{\omega_{2,m,n}}}
denotes the Fourier transform of the characteristic function of
\omega_{{}_{2,m,n}}
. Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,m}}g_{{}_{m}}V_{m,n}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)&=\int_{L_{2,m,n}}\widehat{g}_{{}_{m,n}}(\eta)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi|\eta|d(x_{m,n},\widetilde{I}_{2,m})}\widehat{\chi}_{{}_{\omega_{2,m,n}}}(-\eta)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\eta).\end{aligned}

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1}}g_{{}_{m+1,n}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=\int_{I_{2,m,n}}g_{{}_{m,n}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)-\int_{I_{2,m,n}}g_{{}_{m,n}}V_{m,n}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).\end{aligned}

Next, we investigate the upper bound of
1-V_{m,n}
on the compact set
I_{2,m,n}
. From the continuous dependence on the parameters, we have

1-V_{m,n}\leq\rho<1,\quad\forall m\geq 1,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Taking the summation with respect to
n
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,m+1}}g_{{}_{m+1}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\leq\rho\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,m}}g_{{}_{m}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).\end{aligned}

This proves the theorem.
∎

For
n\leq-1
, similar estimates can also be obtained by considering the restriction of
U_{0,0}
in
\cup_{n\leq-1}\widetilde{Y}_{n}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{n}
. Thus, the functions

\{\{\mathcal{V}_{m,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\{\mathcal{W}_{m,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\}_{m\leq-1}

and the related trace on
\widetilde{I}_{2,m}
can also be proved to satisfy

\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,-m}}U_{0,0}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\leq\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,-m}}g_{{}_{-m}}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\leq\rho^{m}\int_{\widetilde{I}_{2,0}}U_{0,0}\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

Combining the above arguments, we obtain the full estimate (
2.17
).

Remark 4
.

Section

3
and
Section

4
have established the exponential decay of the quasi-periodic capacitance matrix
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}
, which implies that
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}
possesses a tight-binding structure. This means that we can only consider the dominant contributions from nearest-neighbor interactions.

Remark 5
.

Note that the overall methodology can be generalized to higher dimensional systems. More specifically, consider resonators
D_{\mathrm{full}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
that extend to infinity, with basis vectors
v_{{}_{1}},v_{{}_{2}},v_{{}_{3}}
spanning the lattice points

\Lambda=\{mv_{{}_{1}}+nv_{{}_{2}}+pv_{{}_{3}}:m,n,p\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

If the resonators are periodic with respect to
v_{{}_{1}}
and
v_{{}_{2}}
, the above approach can be generalized to establish the exponential decay of the full capacitance matrix and the corresponding quasi-periodic capacitance matrix.

5
Subwavelength problem formulation

While it is well-known that finite capacitance matrices can asymptotically characterize the subwavelength frequencies of acoustic systems, this property is not yet well-established for the infinite case. In this section, we prove that subwavelength frequencies
\omega\sim\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\delta})
corresponding to
\mathbb{L}^{2}
localized modes are asymptotically determined from the quasi-periodic capacitance matrix when
\alpha\neq 0
.

Our analysis relies on layer potential theory. The solution to (
2.4
) can be represented by the single layer potential:

\displaystyle w(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,k_{1}}_{\widetilde{D}}[\phi](x),\quad x\in\widetilde{D},\\
&\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,k_{0}}_{\widetilde{D}}[\psi](x),\quad x\in\widetilde{Y}\setminus\overline{\widetilde{D}},\end{aligned}\right.\quad\phi,\psi\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\widetilde{D}).

(5.1)

To present the main theorem, we first introduce some lemmas.

Lemma 3
.

The kernel of the operator
A\triangleq-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}})^{\ast}:\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\widetilde{D})\to\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\widetilde{D})
is given by

\ker\big\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}})^{\ast}\big\}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\psi_{n}^{\alpha},

where
\psi_{n}^{\alpha}=\nu\cdot\nabla U_{n}^{\alpha}\big|_{+}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\widetilde{D})
and
\{c_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Here, we recall that the quasi-periodic harmonic functions
U^{\alpha}_{n}
are defined in (
2.7
).

Proof.

From the jump relation (
8.6
), we have

\operatorname{span}\{\psi_{n}^{\alpha}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\ker\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}})^{\ast}\big).

For all
\psi\in\ker\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}})^{\ast}\big)\subset\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\widetilde{D})
, we have

\left.\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}}[\psi]\right|_{-}(y)=0,\quad y\in\partial\widetilde{D}.

Therefore, if we define
u^{\alpha}(x)=\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}}[\psi](x)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\widetilde{Y}\setminus\partial\widetilde{D})
, then it satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta u^{\alpha}(x)=0,\quad x\in\widetilde{Y}\setminus\partial\widetilde{D},\\
&\left.\nu_{y}\cdot\nabla u^{\alpha}(y)\right|_{-}=0,\quad y\in\partial\widetilde{D},\\
&u^{\alpha}(x+mv_{{}_{1}})=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m\alpha}u^{\alpha}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\partial\widetilde{D}.\end{aligned}\right.

It can be verified that the trace of
\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}}[\psi]
on
\widetilde{D}
is equal to
\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}}[\psi]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\chi_{{}_{\partial D_{n}}}\in\mathbb{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\widetilde{D})
.
Therefore,
\{c_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and it follows that
\psi=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}\psi_{n}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\widetilde{D})\subset\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\widetilde{D})
.
∎

The following lemma from
Ammari et al. 2022
also holds for the infinite domain
\widetilde{D}
.

Lemma 4
.

The operator
\mathcal{K}^{\alpha,\omega}_{\widetilde{D}}:\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\widetilde{D})\to\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\widetilde{D})
admits the following asymptotic expansion:

\mathcal{K}^{\alpha,\omega}_{\widetilde{D}}=\mathcal{K}^{\alpha,\omega}_{\widetilde{D}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\omega^{n}\mathcal{K}^{\alpha,\omega}_{\widetilde{D},n}.

Furthermore, the first order operator satisfies

\int_{\partial D_{n}}\mathcal{K}^{\alpha,0}_{D,1}[\phi]\mathrm{d}\sigma=-\int_{D_{n}}\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,0}_{D}[\phi]\mathrm{d}x,\quad\phi\in\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\widetilde{D}).

We can now derive the asymptotics of the subwavelength frequency that corresponds to the point spectrum.

Theorem 7
.

For
\alpha\neq 0
, the subwavelength frequency
\omega(\alpha)\sim\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\delta})
that corresponds to the point spectrum of (
2.4
) can be approximated by

\omega(\alpha)=\sqrt{\delta\lambda^{\alpha}}\mu_{{}_{1}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta),

(5.2)

where
\lambda^{\alpha}
is the generalized eigenvalue of the following quasi-periodic capacitance matrix that corresponds to the point spectrum:

\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}v^{\alpha}=\lambda^{\alpha}\mathbf{M}v^{\alpha},

(5.3)

with
\mathbf{M}=\operatorname{diag}\{|D_{n}|\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

We seek normalized
\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\widetilde{D})
solutions
(\phi^{\alpha},\psi^{\alpha})
, with

\|\phi^{\alpha}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\widetilde{D})}=\|\psi^{\alpha}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\widetilde{D})}=1.

By the asymptotic expansions in
Lemma

4
, we can verify that
\phi^{\alpha},\psi^{\alpha}
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}}[\phi^{\alpha}]-\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}}[\psi^{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(\omega^{2}),

(5.4)

\displaystyle\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}})^{\ast}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{\mu_{{}_{1}}}\mathcal{K}^{\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D},1}\right\}[\phi^{\alpha}]-\delta\left\{\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{D})^{\ast}\right\}[\psi^{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(\omega^{4}+\delta\omega^{2}).

(5.5)

Since the single layer operator
\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}}
is invertible for each fixed
\alpha
, one has

\psi^{\alpha}=\phi^{\alpha}+\mathcal{O}(\omega^{2}).

Substituting the above approximation into (
5.5
), we have

\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}})^{\ast}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{\mu_{{}_{1}}}\mathcal{K}^{\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D},1}\right\}[\phi^{\alpha}]-\delta\left\{\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{D})^{\ast}\right\}[\psi^{\alpha}]=\mathcal{O}(\omega^{4}+\delta\omega^{2}).

(5.6)

We write

\phi^{\alpha}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{\alpha}_{n}\psi^{\alpha}_{n}+\varphi^{\alpha},

(5.7)

where the series
\{c^{\alpha}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The residue term
\varphi^{\alpha}
lies in the orthogonal complement of
\ker(-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}})^{\ast})
. Therefore, we have

-\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{\alpha}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}})^{\ast}[\varphi^{\alpha}]=\mathcal{O}(\omega^{2}+\delta).

(5.8)

Since the operator
-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+(\mathcal{K}^{-\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}})^{\ast}
is invertible from
Theorem

11
, it follows that

\varphi^{\alpha}=\mathcal{O}(\omega^{2}+\delta).

Thus, integrating on
\partial D_{n}
, we have

-\frac{\omega^{2}|D_{n}|}{\mu^{2}_{{}_{1}}}c^{\alpha}_{n}+\delta\mathbf{C}^{\alpha}c^{\alpha}=\mathcal{O}(\omega^{4}+\omega^{2}\delta),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore, we have proved the theorem.
∎

Remark 6
.

Compared with the derivation of the discrete tight-binding model,
we use combinations of single layer potentials
\{\mathcal{S}^{\alpha,0}_{\widetilde{D}}[\psi_{n}^{\alpha}]\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
to recover the approximate eigenfunction. Here, we recall that the functions
\psi_{n}^{\alpha}
are given in
Lemma

3
. These functions behave similar to the Wannier functions in physics or the atomic orbital in analysis; see, for example,
Marzari and Vanderbilt 1997
;
Pelinovsky et al. 2008
;
Pelinovsky and
Schneider 2010
;
Ablowitz et al. 2012
.

Remark 7
.

In the previous sections, we have proved the exponential decay of off-diagonal elements of quasi-periodic capacitance matrix, when
\alpha\in[-\pi,\pi)
. However, we are unable to prove the correspondence between subwavelength frequencies and eigenvalues of
\widehat{\mathbf{C}}^{\alpha}
for
\alpha=0
, since the corresponding single layer potential
\mathcal{S}^{0,0}_{\widetilde{D}}
is no longer invertible. Therefore, the capacitance matrix formulation cannot be directly deduced. Asymptotic analysis is further required; see previous works
Ammari et al. 2021
;
Miao and Zhu 2024
on this issue.

7
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have introduced a new and very accurate method to compute subwavelength defect bands in crystals of high contrast resonators with line defects. Our method is based on a tight-binding approximation of the capacitance matrix associated with the non-compactly perturbed periodic structure. We have shown numerically that our method can be generalized to compute topologically protected defect bands corresponding to interface guided modes between two half crystals with different topological properties. We expect that our method can be applied to curved defects and will serve as the block building tool for optimizing guiding properties at subwavelength scales in crystals of subwavelength resonators. This will be the subject of a forthcoming work.

Preventive Role of Simvastatin against Infertility: Potential Role of PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs and NF-κβ Signaling Pathways
Authors: Ozra Motaghinejad, Mina Gholami, A. Wallace Hayes, Majid Motaghinejad, Mojgan Javedani Masroor, Arash Mohazzab
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Several inflammatory-related signaling pathways are involved in infertility. A common feature involves activating inflammatory cascades in the mitochondrial respiratory chain. Oxidative stress triggers mitochondrial dysfunction and initiates intrinsic apoptosis pathways, leading to cell damage/death in germ cells. Disruption of the mitochondrial respiratory chain is a leading cause of degenerative disorders of germ cells. However, the signaling pathways responsible for disrupting the mitochondrial respiratory chain function are unclear and require further studies. Among the signaling pathways potentially involved, the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs pathway has been reported to modulate mitochondrial function in germ cells. Although the role of the cell death cascades on germ cell-induced damage has not been unwoven, apoptosis, necrosis, autophagy, and their related signaling cascades play roles in infertility. Thus, therapeutic agents, such as simvastatin, with anti-cell death properties should be considered as possible new therapeutic approaches for managing infertility.

Introduction

Infertility, or not getting pregnant despite having carefully timed, unprotected sex for 1 year, is a significant public health issue globally. According to WHO statistics, about 8% of reproductive-age couples suffer from some form of infertility.[123] Numerous environmental and genetic factors, lifestyle, diet, racial and sex differences, and drugs have been reported to contribute to infertility [Figure 1].[45] In recent years, the increase in the incidence of infertility has become a severe sociomedical problem, and healthcare workers continue to research the pathophysiological causes of infertility and to improve treatments.[67] Mitochondrial disorders have been proposed as one cause of infertility.[89] These authors have postulated that regardless of the stressor, infertility involves mitochondrial dysfunction.[89] Germ cell degeneration is associated with mitochondrial and respiratory chain dysfunction.[1011] Mitochondrial dysfunction initiates destructive cellular processes such as oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis, so it can be expected that these destructive processes play a vital role in infertility.[121314] The PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway, via modulation of mitochondrial function, can inhibit the NF-κβ protein, consequently inhibiting oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis in germ cells.[15161718]

Many risk factors, including internal and external factors such as diseases, various disorders, pathogens, drugs, and toxins, as well as unknown factors, are involved in germ cell degeneration and infertility

Cell protective agents that modulate mitochondrial function by inhibiting the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathways may potentially inhibit or at least reduce oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis in germ cells.[81719202122] Simvastatin, an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor and cholesterol-reducing drug, has been reported to have cell-protective properties,[23] including mitochondrial biogenesis and respiratory chain reaction activation, including induction of oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis.[24252627] The role of simvastatin in infertility through the mediation of the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs pathway, the inhibition of the NF-κβ protein, and the control of destructive processes such as oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis in germ cells require additional study for confirmation.

We review the involvement of simvastatin in this process in this paper.

Oxidative Stress and Infertility

Oxidative stress, a significant event during cell degeneration, results following an imbalance between oxidants such as ROS, RNS, free radicals, reactive oxidative species, and protective agents such as glutathione, antioxidant enzymes, and free-radical scavengers.[282930] Reactive oxygen, nitrogen, and free radicals accumulate after prolonged oxidative stress.[3132] Oxidative stress, including germ cells, also occurs during cellular degeneration.[3334]

Stress can cause infertility in males and females by activating oxidative stress pathways.[3536] Oxidative stress alters the quality and quantity of sperm and ova[3738] and can destroy the number and functional quality of eggs.[3738] The critical underlying cause of infertility may reside in mitochondrial disorders/dysfunction, which can lead to the formation of ROS and RNS.[3940] ROS and RNS consist of unpaired electrons, damaging lipids in cellular membranes and adversely affecting carbohydrates, DNA fragmentation, and protein degradation.[4142]

Malondialdehyde (MDA), a biomarker of lipid peroxidation, was increased in infertile males and females in both clinical and experimental subjects.[43] High lipid peroxidation levels also occurred in germ cells of infertile patients.[43]

Numerous intrinsic and extrinsic factors involved in infertility also decrease glutathione (GSH) and increase glutathione disulfide (GSSG) levels.[4445] In addition, the inactivation of GPx, GR, SOD, and CAT antioxidant enzymes plays a critical role in the disruption and dysfunction of germ cells in both males and females, which can lead to infertility.[4647]

Mitochondrial respiratory chain dysfunction induces oxidative stress-induced germ cell death signals.[48] Chronic diseases and risk factors that are involved in infertility, such as diabetes, thyroid disorders, infectious diseases, endocrinal disorder, some neurological diseases, disorders related to polycystic ovarian disease, obesity, consumption of abused substances such as cigarette smoke, alcohol, stimulant substances (THC), and a pathological or anatomical disorder in the genital system and exposure to toxicants can adversely affect fertility by activating pathways related to oxidative stress [Figure 2].[49]

Several risk factors, disorders, and diseases can initiate mitochondrial dysfunction, which causes the occurrence of events related to oxidative stress, activation of free radicals, disruption of the glutathione cycle, activation of lipid peroxidation, and failure of antioxidant defenses potentially resulting in germ cell degeneration and infertility

The use of antioxidants, such as simvastatin, along with the currently accepted treatment of infertility, is being considered as a potential therapeutic approach. Oxidative stress plays a central role in inducing damage to germ cells, ultimately leading to infertility.[495051]

Inflammation and Infertility

Several inflammatory-related signaling pathways are involved in infertility.[5253] A common feature involves activating inflammatory cascades in the mitochondrial respiratory chain.[14] Increased production of cytokines, chemokines, and activated COX-PG, NOS-NO, NF-κβ, JAK/STAT, TLRs, growth factor, and MAPK signaling pathways play a role in the activation of inflammation in germ cells.[1454555657] Many chronic metabolic, endocrine, and infectious diseases and intrinsic and extrinsic risk factors, including exposure to toxicant and medicinal agents, exert self-inducing effects on infertility by activating various inflammation-related pathways [Figure 3].[14545556] Overproduction of cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 in infertile subjects has been suggested to mediate germ cell degeneration.[5859] The levels of IL-2, IL-4, and IL-10, anti-inflammatory agents, decrease in infertility.[5960]

Several risk factors, disorders, and diseases can activate inflammatory pathways, potentially resulting in germ cell degeneration and infertility

INFγ is a proinflammatory cytokine whose synthesis increases in many infertility cases.[59] Increased levels of chemokines such as CCL2, CCL20, CXCL5, CXCL7, and CXCL12 in germ cells have been reported to contribute to infertility.[59] Overactivation of the COX-PG pathway also plays a role in the induction of inflammation in infertility.[6162] The cytotoxic prostaglandins have also been reported to be involved in cases of infertility.[6263]

NO overproduction occurs in infertility.[6465] Elevated levels of iNOS can cause disproportionate NO synthesis in germ cells, often resulting in changes in the quantity and quantity of germ cells.[6667] Another inflammatory mediator is NF-κβ, a family of transcription factors for most proinflammatory molecules, enzymes, cytokines, and chemokines.[68] Most infertility risk factors induce some alteration of mitochondrial DNA expression, which is responsible for initiating the inflammatory process through the regulation of NF-κβ, which can lead to degeneration of germ cells and, ultimately, infertility.[6970]

JAK/STAT3 activation stimulates those inflammatory cascades involved in neurodegeneration.[7172] The TLRs family of receptors also plays a role in molecular inflammatory events during infertility.[7374]

Although details of these inflammatory cascades or pathway-induced damage on germ cells and the occurrence of infertility are lacking, these proteins, receptors, and their related signaling cascades have been reported to be involved in infertility.[1452] Chemicals with anti-inflammatory properties, such as simvastatin and anti-infertility drugs, may have potential as new therapeutic approaches.[14]

Necrosis/Apoptosis/Autophagy and Infertility

Cell death pathways such as necrosis and apoptosis are involved in infertility, including germ-cell dysfunction.[1475] Necrosis is the death of most or all organ or tissue cells due to disease, injury, or hypoxia.[4176] It is not necessarily an independent process and has been linked to apoptosis.[417677] Various risk factors, including metabolic disorders, other diseases, medications, and toxic agents that cause infertility, often lead to apoptosis followed by necrosis.[1475] Vascular dysfunction caused by a disturbance in blood flow to genital organs and germ cells can trigger necrosis.[527879]

Apoptosis is the orchestrated collapse of a cell characterized by membrane blebbing, cell shrinkage, chromatin condensation, and DNA fragmentation, requiring specific triggering signals to begin an energy-dependent cascade of molecular events. The extrinsic and intrinsic apoptotic pathways activate their respective initiator caspases.[808182] In the extrinsic apoptotic pathway, death ligands, including TRAIL and FAAD, activate the death receptor, consequently triggering caspase 8 and 10. Finally, caspases 3 and 7 are activated, and apoptosis occurs. In the intrinsic pathway, mitochondrial dysfunction, including DNA damage, hypoxia, external stress factors, and diseases, can cause the formation of apoptosomes and the release of cytochrome C, which trigger caspase-9 and caspases 3 and 7 and initiate apoptosis.[8384] Proteins such as Bcl-2 and Bcl-XL inhibit Bax and cause stability of the MOMP protein. SMAC is another essential protein during apoptosis.[8586]

Numerous risk factors cause apoptosis of both male and female germ cells, ultimately leading to the degeneration of these cells [Figure 4].[498788] Inflammation plays a significant role in germ cell death. Similar increases in inflammatory mediators, including cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-1β, activate these same death signals, thereby activating the extrinsic apoptosis pathways.[148889]

Several risk factors, disorders, and diseases cause the activation of intrinsic and extrinsic apoptosis pathways, which may cause germ cell degeneration and the occurrence of infertility

Similar events occur following oxidative stress. Oxidative stress triggers mitochondrial dysfunction and initiates intrinsic apoptosis pathways, leading to cell damage/death in germ cells.[90] In infertile subjects, these risk factors cause the formation or activation of one or more apoptotic proteins, and this event triggers apoptosis in germ cells. The level of the Bax and the caspase family of proteins increased, and Bcl-2 decreased[148889] in experimental and clinical subjects of infertility in both sexes.[369192]

Another degenerative process in infertility is autophagy.[9394] Autophagy is the process that removes unnecessary or dysfunctional cellular components through a lysosome-dependent regulated mechanism involving death signaling cascades.[35899091] Autophagy includes three processes: Macroautophagy, microautophagy, and chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA).[95] Although the exact role of the cell death cascades on germ cell-induced damage has not been unwoven, apoptosis, necrosis, autophagy, and their related signaling cascades play roles in infertility.[527596] Thus, therapeutic agents with anti-cell death properties should be considered a potential new therapeutic approach for managing infertility.

PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs Pathway and NF-κβ

Disruption of the mitochondrial respiratory chain is a leading cause of degenerative disorders of germ cells and ultimately can lead to infertility.[897] However, the signaling pathways responsible for disrupting the mitochondrial respiratory chain function are unclear and require further studies. Among the signaling pathways potentially involved, however, the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs pathway has been reported to modulate mitochondrial function in germ cells and the overall reproduction process.[169899]

Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) is a neurotransmitter modulator that participates in neuronal plasticity and plays an essential role in neuronal survival and growth. BDNF also has a role in regulating mitochondrial function. TrkB, a high-affinity receptor for BDNF, is activated during a positive feedback cycle, resulting in increased levels of BDNF.[100101] TrkB-BDNF regulates protein transcription, translation, and transport in multiple processes, including mitochondrial function.[100101] One of the downstream signaling pathways of the BDNF-TrkB pathway is the proliferator-activated gamma receptor (PPAR- γ) 1-alpha (PGC-1α) coactivator.

The PPAR family has multiple functions [Figure 5]. PGC-1α is the foundation of biogenesis in mitochondria, and these effects are mediated via PPARβ.[16102103104] PGC-1α is a transcriptional coactivator that controls respiratory and energy metabolism in the mitochondrion. The peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPARβ) is activated by PGC-1α, which is key in regulating mitochondrial function and respiratory chain enzyme activity.[100101102103104] PGC-1α via PPARβ, a primary controller of mitochondrial function, including the mitochondrial respiratory chain system, regulates mitochondrial uncoupling proteins (UCPs) and the mitochondrial anion carrier protein (MACP).[16102103104105] The uncoupling proteins are transporters into the mitochondrial inner membrane that mediate a regulated discharge of the proton gradient generated by the respiratory chain. UCPs are involved in functions such as thermogenesis, redox balance maintenance, or ROS reduction.[106107] A disturbance in the production, function, and formation of UCPs may lead to disturbances in mitochondrial function, ultimately leading to cell degeneration.[20108] The PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs pathway is critical in fertility.[20109110] By focusing on the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs pathway in mitochondrial biogenesis, the occurrence of destructive consequences leading to infertility, such as oxidative stress, inflammation, and cell death, is likely.[17111112] This approach makes agents such as thiazolidinedione and fibrates that interact with the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway effective therapeutic drugs in infertility.[17113114] In addition, because of the involvement of the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway in controlling energy metabolism, drugs that influence energy metabolism may have an advantage in managing obesity, diabetes, and thyroid disorders.[17113114]

The effect of different types of PPAR (α,β, and γ) on the function of different organs and their potential physiological role

NF-κβ, a major transcription factor of critical genes, is also involved in regulating apoptosis in human germ cells.[115] A significant correlation between NF-κβ elevation and mitochondrial dysfunction has been observed.[116117] Numerous risk factors, including diseases, drugs, and toxicants involved in infertility pathogenesis resulting from mitochondrial dysfunction, can trigger the elevation of NF-κβ.[81118119] A significant correlation between NF-κβ activity and germ cell denegation involving oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis has been reported,[81120] suggesting that NF-κβ is a critical factor in the molecular pathophysiology of infertility.[118]

There is evidence that the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway can modulate NF-κβ activity.[15121] It has been hypothesized that a significant part of the cell-protective function of the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway and its effects on modulation of mitochondrial biogenesis occurs via inhibition of NF-κβ, leading to a reduction of oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis in germ cells [Figure 6].[121122] The PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway, if not the primary regulator of mitochondrial function, certainly plays a significant role in the process. The evidence also suggests that disrupting mitochondrial function may trigger disturbances in reproduction via increased oxidative stress, inflammation, and cell death in germ cells.[8121497] Modulating the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway via inhibition of NF-κβ activation could be a potential therapeutic strategy for addressing infertility.

The PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway and its effects on the modulation of mitochondrial biogenesis and function following inhibition of NF-κβ. This phenomenon led to the reduction of oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis in sensitive cells such as germ cells

Therapeutic Strategy

Anti-infertility drugs that interfere with PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs activation and/or inhibit NF-κβ activation by exerting their antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-cell death properties may lead to a new generation of infertility drugs. Among the currently approved drugs, simvastatin, besides its cholesterol-lowering property, may be a candidate for infertility treatment because of its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-cell death properties that appear to involve regulation of the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs and NF-κβ pathway. The role of simvastin, however, in activating PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs needs to be experimentally verified.

Simvastatin

Statins, in general, increase glutathione levels and, by this mechanism, exert their cell protection.[123] All statins are free radical scavengers and can decrease the levels of ROS and RNS in multiple degenerative events.[124125126127] Furthermore, statins can increase antioxidant defenses by increasing SOD, GPx, GR, and CAT.[123128129]

Simvastatin, an inhibitor of HMG-CoA reductase,[23] is a member of the class of hexahydronaphthalenes that has the 2-methylbutyrate ester moiety replaced by a 2,2- dimethylbutyrate ester group [Figure 7]. Simvastatin is a potent anti-inflammatory drug.[130] Simvastatin reduces lipid peroxidation, protein carbonyl, and other cell damage biomarkers.[124125] Simvastatin inhibits oxidative stress, inflammation, and cell death in multiple cell types, including germ cells.[23124131132] Although most studies have focused on the metabolic effects of simvastatin, its protective effects against degenerative events have also been studied.[133134] Simvastatin cell protective effects have been reported to be mediated via a reduction or inhibition of inflammatory mediators and cascades,[130135136137] including cytokines, chemokines, COX-PG, NOS-NO, NF-κβ pathways, the JAK/STAT cascade, the TLRs and growth factor cascades, and MAPK signaling in sensitive cells.[130138139140141142143144145] Simvastatin inhibited endothelial cell death and reduced atherosclerotic lesion formation in ApoE-deficient mice.[146] Simvastatin also inhibited several cell death signaling pathways such as necrosis and apoptosis in human coronary artery endothelial cells through upregulation of the JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway.[138146147148149] Simvastatin inhibited intrinsic and extrinsic apoptosis pathways in cells under degenerative events.[138150] Simvastatin reduced Bax, caspases, and other apoptosis parameters and increased Bcl-2, an anti-apoptosis marker.[138150] Simvastatin also modulates the Bax-Beclin-2 complex, a primary regulator of autophagy.[151152]

Chemical structure of Simvastatin

Simvastatin induced its cell protection effects by increasing the activity of hepatocyte mitochondria of hypercholesterolemic mice.[26153] A large part of its effect in reducing cellular degeneration, including oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis, is due to its protective effect on mitochondria function.[2627154]

The Hypothesis

We hypothesized that simvastatin acts against infertility via the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs and NF-κβ signaling pathways.

Literature Review

A literature review was conducted to evaluate the role of simvastatin in infertility and the possible role of PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs and NF-κβ signaling pathways in managing germ cell degeneration processes such as oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis. The keywords simvastatin, infertility, PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs and NF-κβ, oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis were searched in multiple databases, including PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane, Google Scholar, Elsevier Science Direct, and Core Collection. Only English literature papers were included in the review. Studies investigating the role of statins and simvastatin, in particular, in infertility were limited, and no study was found dealing with these signaling platforms.

Simvastatin Effects on Infertility

Although the available data about the role of simvastatin in infertility are limited, and the mechanism and signaling pathways potentially involved in simvastatin effects were not clarified, a few studies did report on the treatment and management of infertility with simvastatin. Both atorvastatin and simvastatin can cause a reduction of inflammation, oxidative stress, hyperandrogenemia, testosterone level, and several metabolic parameters in mice and infertile male patients.[155156157158159160161] These effects were more pronounced in PCOS women.[162163] Simvastatin has a similar effect on gonadotropin in IVF candidates.[164165]

We found several studies that reported on the effects of simvastatin on some events related to infertility, such as PCOD, IVF, gonadal function, and spermatogenesis disorders. We found, however, no evidence supporting simvastatin’s role in activating PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs. Thus, conducting a series of comprehensive studies to evaluate the effects of simvastatin on the processes involving infertility and these signaling pathways is important.

Bustan et al.[166] (2017) reported that different statins affect fertility differently. Simvastatin (20 mg/day) inhibited reduction in spermatogenesis, and this effect was reported to be mediated by inhibition of lipid peroxidation.[156] Simvastatin also inhibited spermatozoal lipid peroxidation and creatine kinase in infertile male patients.[167] Coadministration of α-tocopherol with simvastatin (1 mg/kg) increased reproductive efficiency and performances in hypercholesterolemic male rats.[155] Simvastatin (20 mg/kg per day) increased sperm quality and quantity in fertility impairment induced by experimental unilateral testicular ischemia-reperfusion in mice.[155] Simvastatin and pravastatin (20 and 40 mg/day, respectively) were reported not to have a clinical effect on gonadal testosterone production, testosterone reserve, or semen quality in male hypercholesterikenuc patients.[158] Simvastatin (20 mg/day) decreases testosterone levels and normalizes gonadotropin levels in women with PCOS.[159] Simvastatin protected Sertoli cells from cisplatin-induced cytotoxicity.[160]

Discussion

Our literature review did not support our initial hypothesis that simvastatin acts against infertility via the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs and NF-κβ signaling pathways. However, simvastatin, in addition to its cholesterol-reducing properties, may still have a supportive role in conjunction with standard treatment in infertility patients.

Molecular degenerative events involved in infertility include the activation of inflammatory pathways,[52] cytokine (TNF-α and IL-1β), interferons, chemokines, COX-PG, NOS-NO, the NF-κβ pathways, and the JAK/STAT and TLRs cascades, and the growth factor cascade and MAPK signaling.[1452545556168]

Disruption of the mitochondrial respiratory chain function is involved in oxidative stress, inflammation, apoptosis, and, ultimately, infertility, but the primary mechanism and signaling pathways require further clarification. Free-radical formation in germ cells also plays a role in infertility.[3948169] In infertile patients, lipids, protein peroxidation, ROS, and RNS levels were elevated in germ cells.[39170171] The reduction of antioxidant defenses and glutathione in germ cells is another oxidative stress factor in infertility.[171] An agent that can inhibit inflammation and has a role in the activation of anti-inflammatory genes and proteins has the potential to support infertility management.[62172173] Simvastatin inhibits oxidative stress, inflammation, and cell death in multiple cell types, including germ cells.[23124131132]

In addition, cell death pathways such as apoptosis, necrosis, and autophagy play critical roles in germ cell degeneration and infertility.[174175] Activation of both intrinsic and extrinsic apoptosis pathways, formation or activation of apoptosis mediators such as Bax and caspases, and reduction of Bcl-2 are involved in germ cell degeneration and infertility.[176177] Autophagy meditators such as Bax/Beclin-2 also play a role in germ cell degeneration.[178179] Infertility-associated germ cell death occurred due to activation of necrosis.[174175]

Simvastatin, an anti-hyperlipidemia drug and HMG-CoA reducatase inhibitor,[180181] can inhibit oxidative stress, inflammation, and cell death in multiple cells.[130182183] Simvastatin, like other satins, can act as a free-radical scavenger and reduce ROS and RNS while increasing glutathione levels and antioxidant activity.[123183] Some of the protective effects of simvastatin are mediated via the reduction or inhibition of various inflammatory mediators such as NF-κβ, cytokine, chemokine, COX-PG, NOS-NO, and the JAK/STAT and TLRs cascades.[130138139140141142143144145] Simvastatin also inhibits cell death pathways such as apoptosis, necrosis, and autophagy.[123130182183] It inhibits both intrinsic and extrinsic apoptosis pathways, causes reduction of Bax, caspases, and other apoptotic parameters, and increases Bcl-2, an anti-apoptosis marker.[138] Simvastatin can also modulate the Bax-Beclin-2 complex, an essential regulator of autophagy,[14138] by altering mitochondrial functioning by reducing cellular degenerative hallmarks such as oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis.[123130138139140141142143144145182183]

The PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs pathway is critical in fertility.[20109110] By focusing on the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs pathway in mitochondrial biogenesis, the occurrence of destructive consequences leading to infertility, such as oxidative stress, inflammation, and cell death, are likely.[17111112] This approach makes agents such as thiazolidinedione and fibrates that interact with the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway effective therapeutic drugs in infertility.[17113114] In addition, because of the involvement of the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway in controlling energy metabolism, drugs that influence energy metabolism may have an advantage in managing obesity, diabetes, and thyroid disorders.[17113114]

The PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway, if not the primary regulator of mitochondrial function, certainly plays a significant role in the process. The evidence also suggests that disrupting mitochondrial function may trigger disturbances in reproduction via increased oxidative stress, inflammation, and cell death in germ cells.[8121497] Modulating the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway via inhibition of NF-κβ activation could be a potential therapeutic strategy for addressing infertility.

Anti-infertility drugs that interfere with PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs activation and/or inhibit NF-κβ activation by exerting their antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-cell death properties may lead to a new generation of infertility drugs. Among the currently approved drugs, simvastatin, besides its cholesterol-lowering property, may be a candidate for infertility treatment because of its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-cell death properties that may involve regulation of the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs and NF-κβ pathway. The role of simvastin, however, in activating PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs needs to be experimentally verified.

Conclusions

A common feature in infertility involves activating inflammatory cascades in the mitochondrial respiratory chain. Disruption of the mitochondrial respiratory chain is a leading cause of degenerative disorders of germ cells and ultimately can lead to infertility. Oxidative stress triggers mitochondrial dysfunction and initiates intrinsic apoptosis pathways, leading to cell damage/death in germ cells. In infertile subjects, these risk factors cause the formation or activation of one or more apoptotic proteins, and this event triggers apoptosis in germ cells. Several inflammatory-related signaling pathways are involved in infertility. The PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs pathway has been reported to modulate mitochondrial function in germ cells. Although the role of the cell death cascades on germ cell-induced damage has not been unwoven, apoptosis, necrosis, autophagy, and their related signaling cascades play roles in infertility. Thus, therapeutic agents such as simvastatin with anti-cell death properties should be considered as a potential new therapeutic approach for managing infertility.

Additional fundamental and clinical research is needed to sort out the potential interaction between molecular events, such as the PGC-α/PPARβ/UCPs signaling pathway and its related NF-κβ pathway, and the possible role simvastatin can play in infertility management.

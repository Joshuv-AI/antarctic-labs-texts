Anytime-Valid Conformal Risk Control
Bror Hultberg, Dave Zachariah, Antônio H. Ribeiro

Abstract
Prediction sets provide a means of quantifying the uncertainty in predictive tasks. Using held out calibration data, conformal prediction and risk control can produce prediction sets that exhibit statistically valid error control in a computationally efficient manner. However, in the standard formulations, the error is only controlled on average over many possible calibration datasets of fixed size. In this paper, we extend the control to remain valid with high probability over a cumulatively growing calibration dataset at any time point. We derive such guarantees using quantile-based arguments and illustrate the applicability of the proposed framework to settings involving distribution shift. We further establish a matching lower bound and show that our guarantees are asymptotically tight. Finally, we demonstrate the practical performance of our methods through both simulations and real-world numerical examples.



1
Introduction

Standard prediction problems focus on a point prediction
f(X)
of an outcome variable
Y
. A prediction
set

\mathcal{C}(X)
, which aims to cover
Y
, is often more informative as the set size reflects uncertainty of the outcome. Thus
\mathcal{C}(X)
could be a variable interval or a collection of labels depending on the domain of the outcome. Split conformal prediction is a widely used framework for constructing prediction sets. Using a held out calibration set
\mathcal{D}_{n}=(X_{i},Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}

(
Vovk et al. 2005
;
Angelopoulos et al. 2023
)
, the framework generates a prediction set
\mathcal{C}_{n}(X)
that ensures a miscoverage rate no greater than
\alpha
for the next observation
(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})
, i.e.,

\mathbb{P}\big(Y_{n+1}\not\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(X_{n+1})\big)\leq\alpha.

(1)

While elegant and computationally simple, the achievable miscoverage control (
1
) has certain limitations.

One main limitation about the procedure is that it is a data-marginalized control, meaning that the probability is evaluated
jointly
over the calibration set
\mathcal{D}_{n}
and the out-of-sample test point
(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})
. Consequently, for any realized dataset
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, the miscoverage rate can vary widely from
\alpha

(
Vovk 2012
;
Marques F. 2025
;
Zwart 2025
)
. For instance, when
n=500
there is a 48% chance of drawing
\mathcal{D}_{n}
such that the rate exceeds the specified
\alpha
, see Figure
1(a)
. We want to ensure that the miscoverage rate is controlled with a given probability, say, 90%.

In many circumstances data collection is a cumulative process such that
n
in
\mathcal{D}_{n}
increases and yields a sequence of prediction sets
\{\mathcal{C}_{n}(X)\}
. However, (
1
) only controls the miscoverage rate for an individual
\mathcal{C}_{n}(X)
rather than all the rates for the
entire
sequence of prediction sets with high probability. Overall, we want to ensure that the miscoverage rates approach
\alpha
as
n
increases, while remaining below it for all
n
.

While miscoverage control is important in certain applications, there are other relevant types of error control for
\{\mathcal{C}_{n}(X)\}
that we want to consider, such as false negative rate control in multilabel classification. We consider the chosen error metric as the
risk
of a prediction set
(
Angelopoulos et al. 2024
)
.

In this paper, we propose an approach that jointly address all these limitations. Our contributions are:

1.

We show that one can construct a sequence of prediction sets
\{\mathcal{C}_{n}(X)\}
for which the risks will be no greater than
\alpha
and approach it from below with high probability.

2.

We establish a matching lower bound that allows us to prove our rates are asymptotically optimal.

3.

Finally, we specialize our results to the case of test-time distribution shifts and illustrate them with numerical experiments.

2
Problem Formulation

A prediction set is a subset of the domain of the outcome variable, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}
. We assume that the calibration points
(X_{1},Y_{1}),(X_{2},Y_{2}),\dots
and test point
(X,Y)
are drawn independently and identically. We let
\mathcal{C}_{n}(X)
be a function of the calibration data
\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

2.1
Risk of a Prediction Set

The prediction error of
\mathcal{C}(X)
is quantified by a loss function
\ell(\mathcal{C}(X),Y)
. The
risk
of the
given
prediction set
\mathcal{C}_{n}(X)
is defined as:

\mathbb{E}[\ell\left(\mathcal{C}_{n}(X),Y\right)\>|\>\mathcal{D}_{n}],

(2)

which is a calibration-conditional risk.

Example 2.1
.

In regression and single-label classification problems, the miscoverage event is a common loss function
\ell(\mathcal{C}(X),Y)=\mathbf{1}\{Y\notin\mathcal{C}(X)\}
. In this case, (
2
) is the miscoverage rate of the given prediction set, i.e.,

\mathbb{P}(Y\not\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(X)\>|\>\mathcal{D}_{n}),

(3)

Example 2.2
.

In multilabel classification problems, where
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,K\})
denotes the set of possible label subsets, the fraction of true labels missed by the prediction set

\ell(\mathcal{C}(X),Y)=1-\frac{|\mathcal{C}(X)\cap Y|}{|Y|}

is a suitable loss function. Then
(
2
) is the false negative rate of the given prediction set.

We shall assume that the chosen loss function is
monotone
in prediction set size, i.e.,

\ell(\mathcal{C}(X),Y)\geq\ell(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(X),Y),\quad\forall\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}

and
bounded
in
[0,B]
.

We can also consider the risk under known distribution shifts. That is, when calibration data is drawn as
(X_{i},Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\sim P
but the test point is drawn from different distribution
(X,Y)\sim P^{*}
so that the test-time risk is

\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[\ell\left(\mathcal{C}_{n}(X),Y\right)\>|\>\mathcal{D}_{n}].

(4)

It is assumed that
P^{*}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
P
and the Radon–Nikodym derivative

\omega(x,y)=\frac{\mathrm{d}P^{*}(x,y)}{\mathrm{d}P(x,y)}

(5)

is a known importance weight.

2.2
Conformal Risk Control

We consider a family of prediction sets
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})
indexed by
\lambda\in\Lambda
, where
\Lambda
is totally ordered and the family is nested such that

\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(X),\;\quad\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}

and all
X
. It is assumed that there exists a
\lambda_{\max}
such that

\ell(\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{\max}}(X),Y)\leq\alpha
, for all
(X,Y)
. Note that
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{\max}}(X)
may equal
\mathcal{Y}
and is therefore uninformative.

Example 2.3
.

In regression problems, we may use score function, e.g.,
s(X,y)=|y-f(X)|
given a trained model
f(X)
, and then set

\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}(X)=\{y:s(X,y)\leq\lambda\}.

(6)

Example 2.4
.

In multilabel classification problems, where
f:\mathcal{X}\to[0,1]^{K}
, we may use

\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}(X)=\{k:f_{k}(X)\geq 1-\lambda\},

for
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

In conformal prediction and risk control, we specify a
tolerated
risk level
\alpha
and set a data-dependent threshold
\lambda_{n}
using the calibration set
\mathcal{D}_{n}=(X_{i},Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
. Specifically, choose the smallest
\lambda
subject to a constraint:

\boxed{\lambda_{n}=\inf\left\{\lambda:R_{n}(\lambda)\leq\alpha-\gamma_{n}\right\},}

(7)

where

R_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell\left(\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}(X_{i}),Y_{i}\right)

is the empirical risk and
\gamma_{n}\geq 0
is some
correction
term. The aim of (
7
) is to produce the tightest prediction set
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)
with a risk (
2
) that is no greater than a specified
\alpha
, with high probability. That is, achieve
risk control
.

Example 2.5
.

When the risk is the miscoverage rate, the standard correction term is
(
Angelopoulos et al. 2023
)
:

\gamma_{n}=\frac{\lceil(1-\alpha)(n+1)\rceil}{n}-(1-\alpha).

(8)

This choice only ensures that the miscoverage rate of prediction sets is below
\alpha

when averaged over all
n
-sized calibration sets
, i.e., marginal risk control:

\mathbb{E}\big[\>\mathbb{P}(Y\not\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\>|\>\mathcal{D}_{n})\>\big]\;\leq\;\alpha.

More recently,
Duchi 2025
showed that the correction term

\gamma_{n}=\frac{4\log\frac{1}{\delta}}{3n}+\sqrt{\Big(\frac{4}{3n}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\Big)^{2}+\frac{2\alpha(1-\alpha)}{n}\log\frac{1}{\delta}},

(9)

where
\delta\in(0,1)
, ensures that
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)
has a calibration-conditional miscoverage rate no greater than
\alpha
with probability at least
1-\delta
, i.e.,

\mathbb{P}(Y\notin\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\mid\mathcal{D}_{n})\leq\alpha.

This strengthens the miscoverage control of the prediction set.

Remark 2.6
.

If the set inside the infimum in (
7
) is empty, we define
\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{\max}
. We assume that the empirical risk
R_{n}(\lambda)
is right-continuous in
\lambda
,
meaning that for every
\lambda
and
n
,

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}R_{n}(\lambda+\epsilon)=R_{n}(\lambda).

2.3
Risk Control with Anytime Validity

In this paper, we consider cumulative data collection processes such that
n
in
\mathcal{D}_{n}
increases and yields a sequence of prediction sets
\{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\}
. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\ell
be a loss function and
\alpha
a tolerated level of risk. For any given
\delta\in(0,1)
, a method producing a sequence of prediction sets
\{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\}
is said to achieve
anytime-valid risk control
, if

\boxed{\mathbb{P}\Big(\forall n:\;\mathbb{E}[\ell\left(\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X),Y\right)\>|\>\mathcal{D}_{n}]\>\leq\>\alpha\Big)\;\geq\;1-\delta.}

(10)

That is, with a probability of at least
1-\delta
, we have a sequence of prediction sets for which
all
the risks are no greater than the tolerance
\alpha
. Additionally, we say that the risk control is
asymptotically tight
if the risks approach the specified level
\alpha
from below.

The main question we aim to address is how to construct a
sequence
of prediction sets
\{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\}
using (
7
) that achieves anytime valid risk control and is asymptotically tight.

3
Related Work

Conformal prediction has had a significant impact in machine learning and overviews are provided in
(
Vovk et al. 2005
;
Angelopoulos et al. 2023
;
Angelopoulos et al. 2025
)
.
Vovk 2012
studied miscoverage rate of
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)
when
conditioned
on
\mathcal{D}_{n}
and a distributional characterization is provided in
(
Hulsman 2022
;
Marques F. 2025
)
, which can be used to improve miscoverage control
(
Zwart 2025
)
as alternative to (
9
).

Duchi 2025
showed that using (
9
) when the scores are pairwise distinct also yields a lower bound,

\alpha-\frac{1}{n}-2\gamma_{n}\leq\mathbb{P}(Y_{n+1}\notin\mathcal{C}_{n}(X_{n+1})\mid\mathcal{D}_{n})\leq\alpha,

that holds with probability of at least
1-2\delta
. Calibration-conditional miscoverage control under distribution shifts was recently achieved in

(
Pournaderi & Xiang 2026
)
. Miscoverage control was generalized to marginal and calibration-conditional risk control in
(
Angelopoulos et al. 2024
)
and
(
Bates et al. 2021
)
, respectively.

A large body of work on calibration-conditional conformal prediction under the i.i.d. setting relies on classical concentration inequalities, see, for instance
(
Vovk 2012
;
Duchi 2025
;
Bates et al. 2021
)
.
While most prior work relies on concentration inequalities for a fixed sample size, such tools are not always well suited to sequential settings. Anytime-valid concentration bounds, which hold uniformly over time, provide a natural alternative and have been studied for several decades, dating back to at least
(
Darling & Robbins 1967
)
. These bounds have been refined and extended over the years
(
Howard et al. 2018
;
Howard & Ramdas 2022
;
Waudby-Smith & Ramdas 2023
;
Waudby-Smith et al. 2024
)
. In certain settings, asymptotic rates on the order of
O\left(\sqrt{n^{-1}\log\log n}\right)
are achieved, and this asymptotic rate is known to be optimal
(
Duchi & Haque 2024
)
.

Recently,
Xu et al. 2024
, as part of deriving labeling policies and predictors, showed that if one can construct a suitable martingale for the risk of interest then there exists a
\lambda_{n}
that achieves anytime valid risk control, provided the loss function is bounded. We significantly extend the applicability of anytime risk control by providing an interpretable form of
\lambda_{n}
as in (
7
); establishing the asymptotic tightness of the resulting risk control; providing rates of convergence of the risk towards the chosen
\alpha
; and generalizing the applicability to risk control under distribution shifts.

4
Main Results

We now turn to setting the correction term in (
7
) such that the resulting sequence

\{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\}
achieves anytime-valid risk control and is asymptotically tight. The threshold sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}
will be non-increasing and, when necessary, we use a running minimum. The derivations of the results are provided in Section
5
.

4.1
Anytime-Valid Conformal Risk Control

We let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta\in(0,1)
. For notational convenience, define the following two functions

\displaystyle h_{B,m,\delta}(v)\;

\displaystyle=\;2\,\log\left(\log_{2}\left(\frac{\max\{v,m\}}{m}\right)+1\right)

\displaystyle+\;\log(\pi^{2}/(6\delta)),

and

\displaystyle f_{B,m,\delta}(v)\;=

\displaystyle\;1.44\,\sqrt{v\,h_{B,m,\delta}(v)}\;+\;2.42\,B\,h_{B,m,\delta}(v).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\ell
be an arbitrary loss function bounded in
[0,B]
, monotone in
\lambda
, and right-continuous in
\lambda
. Construct the prediction sets according (
7
) and set the correction term as

\gamma_{n}=f_{B,m^{*},\delta}\big(\alpha(B-\alpha)n\big)/n,

(11)

where

m^{*}\;=\;\min\Bigl\{\,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}:f_{B,m^{\prime},\delta}\big(\alpha(B-\alpha)m^{\prime}\big)/m^{\prime}\leq\alpha\,\Bigr\}.

Then the resulting sequence of prediction sets
\{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\}
achieves anytime-valid risk control (
10
).

Corollary 4.2
.

In conformal prediction, where
\ell
is the miscoverage loss, anytime valid miscoverage rate control is achieved by setting
B=1
in (
11
).

Remark 4.3
.

Note that for
n<m^{*}
, we would have
\gamma_{n}>\alpha
hence in (
7
) we have the infimum of an empty set. By definition, this results in

\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{\max}
and yield an uninformative prediction set at that sample
n
.

Remark 4.4
.

There are alternative ways to construct valid sequences of correction terms. For further details, we refer to Remark
A.3
.

Assuming that the empirical risk
R_{n}
does not exhibit large jumps, we can show that the choice of correction terms
\gamma_{n}
is tight up to a controlled error. For a right-continuous function
l
, we define the jump at
\lambda
by

J(l,\lambda)=\underset{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}l(\lambda-\epsilon)-l(\lambda).

Proposition 4.5
.

In the setting of Theorem
4.1
, further let

d_{n}=\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}J(R_{n},\lambda).

If
d_{n}
converges to
0
, then the sequence
\{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\}
is asymptotically tight with the risk approaching
\alpha
at the rate
O\left(\max\left\{d_{n},B\sqrt{n^{-1}\log\log n}\right\}\right)
.

In particular, the sequence
\{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\}
satisfies

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\big(\forall n:\alpha-k_{n}\leq\mathbb{E}[\ell\left(\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X),Y\right)|\mathcal{D}_{n}]\leq\alpha\big)\\
&\geq 1-2\delta,\end{split}

where we
define

\begin{split}k_{n}&=d_{g(n)}+\gamma_{g(n)}+\gamma^{\prime}_{g(n)}(\delta_{\lfloor\log n\rfloor}),\\
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(\delta)&=\frac{B}{n}\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\sqrt{2\frac{\alpha(B-\alpha)}{n}\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\!\left(\frac{B}{n}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{2}},\end{split}

g(n)=2^{\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor}
and
\delta_{i}=\delta/(i(i+1))
.

Remark 4.6
.

The condition
d_{n}\to 0
is satisfied in several settings. For instance, if
R_{n}
is continuous, then
d_{n}=0
. Alternatively, let
L_{i}(\lambda)=\ell(\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}(X_{i}),Y_{i})
and assume that for any
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
i
,
\mathbb{P}\left(J(L_{i},\lambda)>0\right)=0
. Then
d_{n}\overset{\text{a.s.}}{\leq}B/n
(Lemma 1,
Angelopoulos et al. 2024
). This can be interpreted as follows: for any fixed
\lambda
, the function
L_{i}
is almost surely continuous. In other words,
L_{i}
might have discontinuities, but their locations are random rather than fixed.

4.2
Anytime-Valid Risk Control under Distribution Shift

We generalize Theorem
4.1
to the setting of conformal risk control under test-time distribution shift. The calibration data is drawn from a distribution
P
, which may differ from distribution
P^{*}
for the test data. The goal is to control the test-time risk is (
4
), assuming
\omega(x,y)
is given (
5
).

We begin by defining the function

\displaystyle b_{B,m,\delta}(v)=1.44\sqrt{vh_{B,m,\delta}(v)}.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\ell
be an arbitrary loss function bounded in
[0,B]
, monotone in
\lambda
, and right-continuous in
\lambda
. Define the importance weights
\omega_{i}:=\omega(X_{i},Y_{i})
using (
5
) and
W_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}^{2}
.
Construct the prediction sets according to (
7
) with the correction term as

\gamma_{n}=B\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\right)+\frac{h_{B,m^{*},\delta}(B^{2}W_{n})}{n},

where

\begin{split}m^{*}&=\min\Big\{\,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}:B\Big(1-\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}\omega_{i}\Big)\\
&+\frac{b_{m^{\prime},B,\delta}(B^{2}W_{m^{\prime}})}{m^{\prime}}\leq\alpha\,\Big\}.\end{split}

(12)

Then the resulting sequence of prediction sets
\{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\}
achieves anytime-valid risk control (
10
) under distribution shift (
4
). That is, with probability at least
1-\delta
, we obtain a sequence of prediction sets such that

\mathbb{E}_{P^{*}}[\ell(\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X),Y)\>|\>\mathcal{D}_{n}]\>\leq\>\alpha.

holds for all
n
.

Remark 4.8
.

Since
\mathbb{E}[\omega_{i}]=1
, it follows that the term

B\Big(1-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\Big)

in the expressions above is zero in expectation. Thus classical concentration inequalities ensure that this term is typically small for large
n
, provided finite variance
\operatorname{Var}(\omega_{i})<\infty
.
In contrast, if the variance is infinite, such high-probability guarantees no longer hold, and the term may exhibit significant fluctuations even as
n
grows.

Proposition 4.9
.

In the distribution-shift setting of Theorem
4.7
, let

d_{n}=\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}J(R_{n},\lambda).

If
d_{n}
converges to
0
and

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\to 1\qquad\sqrt{\frac{W_{t}\log\log W_{t}}{t^{2}}}\to 0,

then the sequence
\{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\}
is asymptotically tight.

In particular, the sequence
\{\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X)\}
satisfies

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\big(\forall n:\alpha-k_{n}\leq\mathbb{E}[\ell\left(\mathcal{C}_{\lambda_{n}}(X),Y\right)|\mathcal{D}_{n}]\leq\alpha\big)\\
&\geq 1-2\delta,\end{split}

where we define

\begin{split}k_{n}&=d_{g(n)}+\gamma_{g(n)}+\gamma^{\prime}_{g(n)}(\delta_{\lfloor\log n\rfloor}),\\
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(\delta)&=\sqrt{2\frac{B^{2}W_{n}^{2}}{n^{2}}\,\log\frac{1}{\delta}},\end{split}

g(n)=2^{\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor}
and
\delta_{i}=\delta/(i(i+1))
.

5
Derivations

To establish our main results, we construct suitable processes
(M_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty}
and
(V_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty}
that satisfy a sub-gamma condition, allowing us to apply Corollary
5.2
.

Definition 5.1

(Sub-
\psi
condition, Definition 1
(
Howard et al. 2020
)
)
.

Let
\smash{(S_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty},(V_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty}}
be real-valued processes
adapted to an underlying filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty}
with
\smash{S_{0}=V_{0}=0}

and
\smash{V_{t}\geq 0}
for all
t
. For
c\in\mathbb{R}
, define

\displaystyle\psi_{G,c}(\tau)=\frac{\tau^{2}}{2(1-c\tau)},\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq\tau<\tau_{\max},

where
\tau_{max}=1/\max\{0,c\}
with the convention that
1/0=\infty
. We say that
(M_{t})
is a
sub-gamma process
with scale parameter
c\in\mathbb{R}
and variance process
(V_{t})
if, for each
\tau\in[0,\tau_{\max})
, there exists a supermartingale

(L_{t}(\tau))_{t=0}^{\infty}
w.r.t.
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
such that

\mathbb{E}[L_{0}(\tau)]\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\exp\left(\tau S_{t}-\psi_{G,c}(\tau)V_{t}\right)\leq L_{t}(\tau)\text{ a.s.\ for all }t.

The following corollary is an immediate consequence of Theorem 1 in
(
Howard et al. 2018
)
. For further details, see Appendix
A
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Define

d(v)=2\log\big(\log_{2}(v/m)+1\big)+\log\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\delta}.

Let
(M_{t})
be a sub-gamma process with scale parameter
c\in\mathbb{R}
and variance process
(V_{t})
. Then, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and
m>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\forall t\geq 1:M_{t}<\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\delta}(\max\{V_{t},m\})\big)\geq 1-\delta,

where

\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\delta}(v)=1.44\sqrt{\,v\,d(v)}\;+\;2.42\,c\,d(v).

For notational convenience, we denote the loss associated with the prediction set
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}(x)
by

\ell_{\lambda}(x,y):=\ell\left(\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}(x),y\right).

The population risk is defined as
R(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}[\ell_{\lambda}(X,Y)]
.
To construct suitable sub-gamma processes, we will rely on certain properties of
R
, and we first address the additional considerations that arise when
R
is discontinuous.
By assumption,
\ell_{\lambda}(X,Y)
is right-continuous in
\lambda
and bounded.
Hence, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
R
is right-continuous in
\lambda
. Furthermore,
R
is monotonically decreasing. For
\alpha\in[0,\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda}R(\lambda)]
, define

\hat{\lambda}(\alpha):=\inf\{\lambda:R(\lambda)\leq\alpha\}.

We also define the left-limit of
R
at
\hat{\lambda}(\alpha)
by

R(\hat{\lambda}(\alpha)^{-}):=\lim_{\lambda\uparrow\hat{\lambda}(\alpha)}R(\lambda),

and, similarly, the corresponding left-limit of the loss function by

\ell_{\hat{\lambda}(\alpha)^{-}}(x,y):=\lim_{\lambda\uparrow\hat{\lambda}(\alpha)}\ell_{\lambda}(x,y).

Since
\ell_{\lambda}(x,y)
is monotonically decreasing in
\lambda
and bounded, it follows from the Dominated Convergence Theorem that

\displaystyle R(\hat{\lambda}(\alpha)^{-})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\lambda\uparrow\hat{\lambda}(\alpha)}\mathbb{E}[\ell_{\lambda}(X,Y)]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\lim_{\lambda\uparrow\hat{\lambda}(\alpha)}\ell_{\lambda}(X,Y)\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\ell_{\hat{\lambda}(\alpha)^{-}}(X,Y)].

By monotonicity and right-continuity of
R
, we have

R(\hat{\lambda}(\alpha))\leq\alpha\leq R(\hat{\lambda}(\alpha)^{-}).

Define

7
Conclusion

We proposed an anytime-valid conformal risk control framework that guarantees, with high probability, that the risk of a sequence of prediction sets remains below a target level at all times while converging to it asymptotically. This is particularly useful in problems with cumulative data collection processes. The approach yields interpretable correction terms, matching lower bounds, and extensions to test-time distribution shift.

A natural limitation is that the method is more conservative at small sample sizes, requiring additional data before producing informative prediction sets. Future work includes extensions to online model updates as well as time-series models with uncertainty updates. This is an interesting direction as anytime-valid guarantees are especially relevant for models that need to run over extended periods in online settings.

Exploring Temporal Graphs with Frequent and Regular Edges
Duncan Adamson School of Computer Science University of St Andrews KY16 9SX United Kingdom duncan.adamson@st-andrews.ac.uk

Abstract
Temporal graphs are a class of graphs defined by a constant set of vertices and a changing set of edges, each of which is known as a timestep. These graphs are well motivated in modelling real-world networks, where connections may change over time. One such example, itself the primary motivation for this paper, are public transport networks, where vertices represent stops and edges the connections available at some given time. Exploration problems are one of the most studied problems for temporal graphs, asking if an agent starting at some given vertex v v can visit every vertex in the graph. In this paper, we study two primary classes of temporal graphs. First, we study temporal graphs with frequent edges , temporal graphs where each edge e e is active at least once every f e f_{e} timesteps, called the frequency of the edge. Second, temporal graphs with regular edges , graphs where each edge e e is active at any timestep t t where t ≡ s e mod r e t\equiv s_{e}\bmod r_{e} , with s e s_{e} being the start time of the edge, and r e r_{e} the regularity. We show that graphs with frequent edges can be explored in O ⁡ ( F ​ n ) O(Fn) timesteps, where F = max e ∈ E ⁡ f e F=\max_{e\in E}f_{e} , and that graphs with regular edges can be explored in O ⁡ ( R ​ n ) O(Rn) timesteps, where R = max e ∈ E ⁡ r e R=\max_{e\in E}r_{e} . We provide additional results for public transport graphs , temporal graphs formed by the union of several routes, corresponding to the schedules of some modes of transit, for sequential connection graphs , temporal graphs in which each vertex has a single active in-edge per timestep, iterating over the set of edges in some order, and for broadcast networks , a representation of communication within distributed networks where each vertex broadcasts a message either to all vertices, or none at each timestep.

Introduction

In many real world settings, networks are not static objects but instead have unstable connections that vary with time. Such examples include public transport networks
[
1
]
and infection control
[

. Temporal graphs provide a model for such time-varying networks. Formally, a

Much work on temporal graphs has focused on the problem of

The agent is restricted in being allowed only a single move per timestep, thus as the graph changes, the agent movement may become restricted. This was motivated by extending the well known

,

,

,

The decision version of

-complete problem
[
3
]
. Indeed, this problem remains

-complete even if the underlying graph has pathwidth 2 and every snapshot is a tree
[
14
]
, or if the underlying graph is a star and the exploration has to start and end at the center of the star

.

In the optimisation version of this problem, we ask for the

On the positive side, we mention the landmark paper by Erlebach et. al.

On the negative side, we have, by Erlebach et al.

In this paper, we focus on the exploration of temporal graphs with
frequent

The notions of frequency and regularity, by our definitions, have been studied previously with a particular focus on graph properties

The authors show that determining if the graph has a restless exploration is solvable in polynomial time if the graph has a period of 2, equivalent to a regularity of 2 in our model, and NP-Hard if the period is greater than 2.

Our Results

In Section
3
, we show that graphs where each edge has a frequency of at most
f
can be explored in
f(2n-3)
timesteps, and that graphs where each edge has a regularity of at most
r
can be explored in
r(2n-3)
timesteps. We note that, as our definition of regularity generalises the definition of periodicity for temporal graphs given in
[
23
]

Further, in Section
4

•

Public Transportation Graphs
: A class of temporal graphs defined by a given set of (temporal) walks, with the edges active at timestep
t
corresponding exactly to the edges in the walks used in that timestep. We show that every edge in these graphs has a period of at most the length of the longest walk,
L
, and thus can be explored in
L(2n-3)
timesteps.

•

Sequential Connection Graphs
: A class of temporal graphs where each vertex activates exactly one in-edge at each time step, following a given permutation of the in-edges. We show that these graphs can be explored in at most
4|E|
timesteps, where
E
is the set of edges in the underlying graph.

•

Broadcast Networks
: A class of temporal graphs where the set of out-edges from each vertex are either all active, or all inactive at a given timestep, with the additional constraint that all in-edges to a given vertex must be active between each activation of the out-edges. We show that such graphs can be explored in
O(dn^{2})
timesteps, where
d
is diameter of the graph and, further, in
O(\delta n)
timesteps if the graph is always connected, where
\delta
is the minimum degree of any vertex in the graph.

Temporal Graphs

A
temporal graph
is a generalisation of a graph, herein called a
static graph
whenever confusion may otherwise arise, where, rather than having a single edge set, the graph contains an ordered sequence of timesteps, by convention
E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{T}
, while maintaining a shared set of vertices. We call each edge set a
timestep
(also known as a snapshot). An edge
e
is
active
at timestep
t
if
e\in E_{t}
. We assume, without loss of generality, that each timestep contains at least one active edge. In our definition, an edge
e
may be active in any number of timesteps.

By default, we assume that the temporal graph is undirected, with a temporal graph referred to as a
directed temporal graph
if the edge sets are directed. We assume that either every edge set is directed, or none are. Given a temporal graph
\mathcal{G}
the
lifetime
of the graph is the number of timesteps, thus, if
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{T})
, then the lifetime of the graph is
T
.

The
underlying graph
of a temporal graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{T})
, denoted
U(\mathcal{G})
, is the static graph
U(\mathcal{G})=(V,\bigcup_{t\in[1,T]}E_{t})
, i.e. the static graph formed with the edge set corresponding to the union of all timesteps in the graph. A temporal graph is a
symmetric directed temporal graph
if the underlying graph is a symmetric directed graph. In general, given some property
X
of a static graph, a temporal graph has the property
X
if the underlying graph satisfies the property. A temporal graph
always satisfies
a given property
X
if the static graphs
G_{1}=(V,E_{1})
,
G_{2}=(V,E_{2})
,
\dots
,
G_{T}=(V,E_{T})
also satisfy this property. Notably, a static graph
G=(V,E)
is
connected
if, for each pair of vertices
v_{i},v_{j}\in V
, there exists some walk starting at
v_{i}
and ending at
v_{j}
. Therefore, a temporal graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{T})
is an
always connected temporal graph
if the static graph
G_{t}=(V,E_{t})
is connected, for every
t\in[1,T]
, where

is the lifetime of
\mathcal{G}
.

The
frequency
of an edge
e
, denoted
f_{e}
, is the minimum number of timesteps such that
e
is active at least once every
f_{e}
timesteps. Formally,
f_{e}
satisfies
\forall t\in[1,T+1-f_{e}]

\forall f^{\prime}\in[1,f_{e}-1]
,
\exists t\in[1,T+1-f^{\prime}]
such that
e\notin\bigcup_{t^{\prime}\in[t,t+f^{\prime}-1]}E_{t^{\prime}}
. A temporal graph is

-frequent

,
\forall e\in E
.

An edge

is
r
-regular
if given any

, either

is active in all of
E_{t-r}
,
E_{t}
, and
E_{t+r}
, or
e
is inactive in all of
E_{t-r}
,
E_{t}
, and
E_{t+r}
. The

of an edge

, denoted

, is the value such that

is

regular and,
\forall r^{\prime}\in[1,r_{e}-1]
,

is not
r^{\prime}
regular. A temporal graph is

-regular

. Observe that

. Therefore, every

-regular temporal graph is an

-frequent temporal graph.

A

is an set of edge-timestep tuples,
\mathcal{W}=((v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}}),t_{1})

The

Theorem 3.6

Given some temporal graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{T})
with the underlying graph

is the weight of the minimum weight spanning tree on
FW(\mathcal{G})
. Further, this walk can be determined in

Proof 3.7

First, we construct the minimum weight spanning tree

Let

is active. Formally,
e_{1}\in E_{t_{1}}

\forall t^{\prime}\in[1,t_{1}-1]

e_{1}\notin E_{t^{\prime}}

We set the tuple
(e_{1},t_{1})

\mathcal{W}

We determine the remaining steps on
\mathcal{W}
in an iterative manner, using the value of the

step. Formally, if the
(i-1)^{th}

\forall t^{\prime}\in[t_{i-1}+1,t_{i}-1]

e_{i}\notin E_{t^{\prime}}

To determine the total length of this path, observe that, by definition, if the edge

To determine the time complexity, note first that

4
Restricted classes with Frequent Edges

In this section, we provide a set of results on some subsets of temporal graphs which may be reduced to temporal graphs with frequent edges.

4.1
Public Transport Graphs

We first consider
public transport graphs
. We use such temporal graphs to model public transport timetables. Here, the temporal graph is composed of a set of temporal walks, each representing some transport line with vertices representing stops. For example, one may represent the bus stops and lines in a city by the vertices and walks respectively. We assume that once a walk is completed, it will be repeated immediately, noting that we can simulate a delay by adding a self-loop at the end of the walk, active at an appropriate timestep.

Formally, we define a public transport graph by the set of temporal walks
\mathcal{W}_{1}
,
\mathcal{W}_{2}
,
\dots
,
\mathcal{W}_{m}
over a common set of vertices
V
and given lifespan
T
. Letting
L_{i}=|\mathcal{W}_{i}|
be the last timestep containing some edge in the
i^{th}
walk, and
\mathcal{W}_{i}[j]
denote the
j^{th}
edge in walk
\mathcal{W}_{i}[j]
, we have, at timestep
t\in[1,T]
, the edge
e\in E_{t}
iff
\exists i\in[1,m],j\in[1,|\mathcal{W}_{i}|]
s.t.
e=\mathcal{W}_{i}[j]
and
\mathcal{T}_{i}[j]=t\bmod L_{i}
. Informally,
e
is active in timestep
t
if there exists some
\mathcal{W}_{i}
such that
e
is active in timestep
t
(modulo the length of the walk). Note that an edge may be used in multiple routes, possibly even in the same timestep.

Theorem 4.1

Given a public transport graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{T})
composed of the routes
\mathcal{W}_{1},\mathcal{W_{2}},\dots,\mathcal{W}_{m}
, such that
U(\mathcal{G})
is a single connected component, there exists an exploration of
\mathcal{G}
requiring
(2n-3)\max_{i\in[1,m]}L_{i}
timesteps, where
L_{i}=|W_{i}|
.

Proof 4.2

Observe that, by construction, each edge must have frequency at least
L_{i}
. Therefore, using Theorem
3.6
and Lemma
3.8
, we get the bound.

4.2
Sequential Connection Graphs

We now consider the class of graphs where each vertex activates the set of incident edges in a fixed, repeating order. One can think of these as graphs in which each vertex allows connections to one neighbour in turn. For example, a radio receiver switching between a set of bands in a regular order.

Formally, a symmetric directed temporal graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{T})
is a
sequential connection graph
if there exists, for every vertex
v\in V
there exists a permutation of the incoming edges to
v
,
P_{v}=e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{\Delta(v)}
, such that at timestep
t
,
P_{v}[t\bmod\Delta(v)]\in E_{t}
and,
\forall e\in\inedge(v)\setminus\{P_{v}[t\bmod\Delta(v)]\}
,
e\notin E_{t}
.

Theorem 4.3

Given a sequential connection graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{T})
,
\mathcal{G}
can be explored in
2\sum_{v\in V}\Delta(v)=4|E|
timesteps, where
U(\mathcal{G})=(V,E)
.

Proof 4.4

Note that the frequency of the edge
(v_{i},v_{j})
is
\Delta(v_{j})
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.6
, we get the bound.

4.3
Broadcast Networks

In distributed computing, the
broadcast
model of communication is one in which, at each communication round, every vertex either sends the same message to every neighbour, or sends no message
[
25
]
. We can use a temporal graph to model communication in these networks by representing each communication round as a timestep in the temporal graph, with messages from vertex
v
to vertex
u
in timestep
t
represented by having the edge
(v,u)
in
E_{t}
. We call these graphs
Broadcast Networks
.

We have two additional restrictions on these temporal graphs. First, to model the restrictions on the broadcast model, we require that each time a vertex broadcasts a message, it does so to all neighbours.
Formally,
\forall v\in V,t\in[1,T]
, either
\{(v,u)\mid u\in N(v)\}\subseteq E_{t}
or
\{(v,u)\mid u\in N(v)\}\cap E_{t}=\emptyset
. Second, we assume that a vertex will only send the next message once it has received some sort of acknowledgement from every neighbour, either in the form of a simple acknowledged message, or a full message from the neighbour containing the round in which the neighbour has sent the message. This avoids a desynchronisation of the network, where one vertex progresses despite no follow-up information being given. To simplify the model, we assume that if two neighbours send a message on the same round, both are consider in sync, and thus do not have to wait for the other to send the next message. Formally, we say that an edge
(v,u)
may be active in timesteps
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
, where
t_{1}<t_{2}
iff there exists some
t^{\prime}\in[t_{1},t_{2}-1]
where
(u,v)\in E_{t^{\prime}}
.

For convenience, we say a vertex
v
is active in timestep
E_{t}
iff
\{(v,u)\mid u\in N(v)\}\subseteq E_{t}
, and use the notation
A_{t}
to denote the set of vertices active in timestep
t
. Thus, the second condition can be rewritten as
v\in A_{t_{1}}\cap A_{t_{2}}
where
t_{1}<t_{2}
iff
\forall u\in N(v)
,
\exists t_{u}\in[t_{1},t_{2}-1]
s.t.
u\in A_{t_{u}}
.

We combine these restrictions to derive the frequencies of the edges.

Lemma 4.5

Given a broadcast network
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{T})
, the edge
(u,v)
has a frequency of
dn
, where
n
is the number of vertices in
V
and
d
is the diameter of
U(\mathcal{G})
.

Proof 4.6

Let
v
be some vertex in the graph
\mathcal{G}
that is active at timesteps
t
and
t^{\prime}
, where
v\notin A_{i}
,
\forall i\in[t+1,t^{\prime}-1]
. Observe that each vertex
u\in N(v)
can be active at most once in
A_{t+1},A_{t+2},\dots,A_{t^{\prime}-1}
. Further, given some vertex
u^{\prime}\in N(u)\setminus\{v\}
,
u^{\prime}
can be active at most twice in
A_{t+1},A_{t+2},\dots,A_{t^{\prime}-1}
, once before
u
is active and once after.

Now, consider some vertex
v^{\prime}
at a distance
\ell
from
v
, and let
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{\ell-1}
satisfy
(v,u_{1})\in E
,
(u_{\ell-1},v^{\prime})\in E
and
(u_{i},u_{i+1})\in E
,
\forall i\in[1,\ell-2]
, where
U(\mathcal{G})=(V,E)
. Further, let
\tau_{x}
be the number of timesteps between
t+1
and
t^{\prime}-1
in which the vertex
x
is active. Note that
\tau_{u_{1}}=1
, and
\tau_{u_{2}}\leq 2
. In general
\tau_{u_{i}}\leq\tau_{u_{i-1}}+1
as the vertex
u_{i}
can be active once before
u_{i-1}
, once between each activation of
u_{i-1}
, and once after. Therefore,
\tau_{v^{\prime}}\leq\dist(v,v^{\prime})
.

As
\dist(v,v^{\prime})\leq d
,
\forall v^{\prime}\in V
, no vertex may be active more than
d
times between
t
and
t^{\prime}
. Hence, as there are
n
vertices in
V
, there can be at most
dn
vertices activated between timesteps
t
and
t^{\prime}
, thus
t^{\prime}-t\leq dn
. By extension, the frequency of
v
is at most
dn
.

Corollary 4.7

Any broadcast network
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{T})
with a diameter
d
can be explored in
dn(2n-3)
timesteps.

Always Connected Broadcast Networks

We now consider the restriction to broadcast networks where the temporal graph is connected at each timestep. We show that these temporal graphs can be explored in
(\delta+1)(2n-3)
timesteps where
\delta=\min_{v\in V}\Delta(v)
, i.e.
\delta
is the lowest degree of any vertex in the underlying graph, with
\Delta(v)
denoting the degree of
v
in the underlying graph.

For simplicity, in this section we use the term
frequency
with reference to vertices, denoting the frequency of a vertex
v
by
f_{v}
and defining
f_{v}
as the smallest value such that
\forall t\in[1,T+1-f_{v}]
,
v\in\bigcup_{t^{\prime}\in[t,t+f_{v}-1]}A_{t^{\prime}}
, i.e. the smallest value such that
v
is active at least once every
f_{v}
timesteps. Note that this is analogous to the definition of edges and, indeed, in this model, the frequency of the edge
(v,u)
is equal to the frequency of the vertex
v
.

Lemma 4.8

Given an always connected broadcast network
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{t})
with underlying graph
U(\mathcal{G})=(V,E)
, the edge
e\in E
has frequency
\delta+1
, where
\delta=\min_{v\in V}\Delta(v)
.

Proof 4.9

Consider some vertex
v\in V
that is active in timestep
t
. In order for
v
to be connected, there must, at each time step, be some vertex
u\in N(v)
that is active, or
v
itself must be active. As each vertex in
N(v)
can be active at most once before
v
is active,
v
must be active in at least one timestep in
[t+1,t+1+\Delta(v)]
. Thus
v
has a frequency of
\Delta(v)+1
.
Now, consider some pair
(v,u)
such that
(v,u)\in E
. As
v
has a frequency of at most
\Delta(v)+1
and
u
has a frequency of at most
\Delta(u)+1
, to satisfy the condition that
v
is active at most once between activations of
u
(and
u
is active at most once between the activations of
v
),
v
must also be active at least once every
\Delta(u)+1
timesteps. Hence
f_{v}\leq\min(\Delta(v),\Delta(u))+1
. Extrapolating this, we have that
f_{v}\leq\min_{u\in N(v)}\Delta(u)+1
and
f_{v}\leq\Delta(v)+1
. Further, we have by the same arguments, that
f_{v}\leq\min_{u\in N(v)}f_{u}
. Expanding this across the temproal graph gives that
f_{v}\leq\min_{u\in V\setminus\{v\}}f_{u}\leq\min_{u\in V\setminus\{v\}}\Delta(u)+1
. Hence,
f_{v}\leq\min_{u\in V}\Delta(u)+1=\delta+1
.

Theorem 4.10

Given an always connected broadcast network
\mathcal{G}=(V,E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{t})
,
\mathcal{G}
can be explored in
(\delta+1)(2n-3)
timesteps, where
\delta=\min_{v\in V}\Delta(v)
.

Proof 4.11

Follows from Theorem
3.6
, Lemma
3.8
, and Lemma
4.8

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the set of temporal graphs defined by having
frequent edges
, both in the general case and in several motivating settings, including public transport networks and the broadcast communication model. The primary result of this paper is that any such graph with
n
vertices, where each edge is active at least once every
F
timesteps, can be explored in
F(2n-3)
timesteps.

There are several obvious open questions following this paper. First is if the lower and upper bounds for exploration of temporal graphs with frequent edges can be closed, either through improving the exploration algorithm, or by constructing a stronger worst case example.

Another interesting direction is the question of whether the current results for broadcast networks are optimal. We conjecture that there exists an upper bound of the form
O(nd^{k})
for some constant
k
. Alternatively, lower bounds for this setting would be of great interest. Further, it is of interest as to if these techniques can be applied to other settings.

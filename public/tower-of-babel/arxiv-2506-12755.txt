Introduction

In the pioneering work of
[
20
]
the solutions to linear Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equations, which are parabolic partial differential equations describing a time-dependent probability density on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
have been shown to run along the steepest descent of their corresponding Lyapunov function, measured in relation to the Wasserstein distance. Since then, studies about the connection between
gradient flows in metric spaces and diffusion equations have been conducted in various settings,
see e.g.

,

,

and the textbooks of

.

We fix

and denote the set of all Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
\mathscr{P}
.
Following the approach in
[
1
,
2
,
32
,
13
,
6
,
30
,
27
]
, the differential and the gradient of a function
W:\mathscr{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
at a point
\mu\in\mathscr{P}
may be assigned,
by looking at the family of curves
\mu_{\varphi,\varepsilon}:=\mu\circ(\textnormal{id}+\varepsilon\varphi)^{-1}\in\mathscr{P}
,
\varphi\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
, parameterized by
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then,
W
is differentiable at
\mu
if and only if

(1.1)

\textnormal{diff}\,W(\mu)(\varphi):=\tfrac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}\varepsilon}W(\mu_{\varphi,\varepsilon})\big|_{\varepsilon=0}

acts as a linear functional in its argument
\varphi
which is continuous with respect to the
(trace) topology of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)
, as
it provides an infinite-dimensional Riemannian-like structure on
\mathscr{P}
with tangent bundle
(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu))_{\mu\in\mathscr{P}}

through the inner product

\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)}:=\mu(\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle),\hskip 20.00003pt\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu),

at
\mu\in\mathscr{P}
.
The differential diff
W(\mu)
determines a gradient
DW(\mu)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)
via

(1.2)

\mu\big(\langle DW(\mu),\varphi\rangle\big)=\textnormal{diff}\,W(\mu)(\varphi),\hskip 20.00003pt\varphi\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d}),

which can be taken as definition, regardless of a choice for a metric on
\mathscr{P}
.
We refer to (
1.1
) and (
1.2

A discussion about
the relation between the intrinsic and the extrinsic derivative can be found in
[
28
]

For

\mathscr{P}_{p}:=\big\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}:\ \mu(|\cdot|^{p})<\infty\big\}

rather than

to assign the differential at
\mu
according to (
1.1
) (cf. Definition

This leads to a consistent notion of the differential at
\mu
(the class of differentiable functions
W
will change, but not the value of diff

for
\varphi\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
if defined). (
1.1
) extends to all curves
{(\mu_{\varphi,\varepsilon})}_{\varepsilon}
,
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)
.

Sometimes, it is beneficial to consider instead of the standard structure of
L^{2}
an equivalent inner product at
\mu
, leading to a perturbed value for the gradient
DW(\mu)
. For
a measurable weight function
\gamma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}\rightarrow[c^{-1},c]

D^{\gamma}W(\mu):=\gamma(\cdot,\mu)DW(\mu)

This corresponds to re-defining the gradient w.r.t. the inner product
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma(\cdot,\mu)^{-1}\mu)
.

By the findings in
[
7
]
, under suitable conditions
on the coefficients
\beta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
b:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the generalized porous media equation

(1.3)

\partial_{t}\rho=\Delta\beta(\rho)+\textnormal{div}\big((\nabla\Phi)b(\rho)\rho\big)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

has a mild solution
\rho(t,x)
,
t\in[0,\infty)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, given by a nonlinear semigroup
{(S(t))}_{t\geq 0}
of contractions in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{\rm{d}}x)
, i.e.

\rho(t,\cdot)=S(t)\rho_{0}
for an initial value
\rho(0,\cdot):=\rho_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{\rm{d}}x)
.
In
[
8
]
the uniqueness of this solution is shown in the largest class of solutions, namely the so-called distributional solution.
The positivity
\rho_{0}\geq 0
and the mass
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(x)\text{\rm{d}}x
of an initial value
are preserved by
S(t)
.
(
1.3
) is an example from the class of nonlinear Fokker–Planck equations, which describe the evolution of the time marginal laws of
solutions to distribution dependent stochastic differential equations, known as McKean–Vlasov SDEs. We refer to
[
9
]
for a comprehensive study of this topic.
By virtue of
[
27
]
the curves
\mu_{t}:=\rho(x,t)\text{\rm{d}}x\in\mathscr{P}
,
t\geq 0
, defined by the probability solutions to (
1.3
) correspond
to the unique gradient flow on
\mathscr{P}
satisfying

(1.4)

\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}t}\mu_{t}=-D^{\gamma}W(\mu_{t})

w.r.t.
\gamma(x,\rho\text{\rm{d}}x):=b(\rho(x))
and an explicit energy functional
W
. The latter has a domain within the set of absolutely continuous measures on which

W(\rho\text{\rm{d}}x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x,\rho(x))\text{\rm{d}}x

with

(1.5)

F(x,s):=s\Phi(x)+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{1}^{t}\frac{\beta^{\prime}(r)}{rb(r)}\text{\rm{d}}rdt,\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,s\in(0,\infty)

is well-defined.
The functional

coincides with the Lyapunov function determined in
[
7
]

For
\Phi=0

W(\rho\text{\rm{d}}x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\ln(\rho)-1)\rho\text{\rm{d}}x
being the entropy functional.
If a suitable space of test functions
u

1.4

\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}t}u(\mu_{t})=-\mu_{t}\big(\langle\gamma_{\mu_{t}}DW(\mu_{t}),Du(\mu_{t})\rangle\big)

with
\gamma_{\mu}:=\gamma(\cdot,\mu)
.

We are interested in constructing stochastic gradient flows for energy functionals of the type

W_{F}(\mu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x,\rho_{\mu}(x))\text{\rm{d}}x
,
\mu=\rho_{\mu}\text{\rm{d}}x
, with
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for example as in (
1.5
).
Adding a stochastic process
({R_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
to its right-hand side transforms (
1.4
) into a stochastic differential equation

(1.6)

\text{\rm{d}}\mu_{t}=-D^{\gamma}W_{F}(\mu_{t})\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}R_{t},\hskip 20.00003ptt\geq 0.

Let
{(R_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
be a
\Lambda
-symmetric Markov process with state space
\mathscr{P}_{p}
for some
p\in[1,2]
and a probability measure
\Lambda
on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
. Denoting the generator of
{(R_{t})}_{t\geq 0}

A
we reformulate (
1.6

{(\mu_{t})}_{t\geq 0}

In this article, solutions to (
1.6

(\Omega,\mathscr{F},(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0},(\mathbb{P}^{\mu})_{\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}})

(1.7)

u(\mu_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}Au(\mu_{s})\text{\rm{d}}s+\mu_{s}\big(\gamma_{\mu_{s}}\langle DW_{F}(\mu_{s}),Du(\mu_{s})\rangle\big)\text{\rm{d}}s,\hskip 20.00003ptt\geq 0,

is a martingale for a suitable class of test functions
u:\mathscr{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and starting points
\mu
up to a set of zero capacity.
There is no Brownian motion on the set of probability measures. Hence, we do not have a canonical candidate for
{(R_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
.
A substitute must be chosen with care, because typical examples of
W_{F}
,
as e.g. the entropy functional, do not have an intrinsic derivative
DW_{F}
defined at all points of
\mathscr{P}_{p}
, but only within a certain subset of absolute continuous measures.
It is a priori not clear how
{(R_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
can be defined in a way that martingale solutions to (
1.6

1.7
) exist.

We utilize the method in
[

31

by which

\mathscr{P}_{p}
.
The main finding of this work is that we can take
{(\phi_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
as a reflected Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process on

in such a way that (

being the generator of the induced process

on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
.
The test function

must be an element in the

-domain

of the generator, which makes

a non-positive self-adjoint operator in

.
The Dirichlet form of
{(R_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
is given by

(1.8)

\langle-Au,v\rangle_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}=\int_{\mathscr{P}_{p}}\mu\big(\gamma_{\mu}\langle Du(\mu),Dv(\mu)\rangle\big)\Lambda(\text{\rm{d}}\mu),\hskip 20.00003ptu,v\in\mathscr{D}(A),

and hence of intrinsic gradient-type.

1.7

has been fixed.
We consider a perturbation

\Lambda_{F}(\text{\rm{d}}\mu):=\frac{1}{Z_{F}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-W_{F}(\mu)}\Lambda(\text{\rm{d}}\mu)

of the invariant measure
\Lambda

\infty\ &\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

and

Defining

2
Diffusion processes on Wasserstein spaces

2.1
Preliminaries

Let
E
be a metrizable Lusin topological space. We denote its Borel
\sigma
-algebra by
\mathscr{B}(E)
and fix a probability measure
\Lambda
on
(E,\mathscr{B}(E))
.
Given a measurable function
f:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
its
\Lambda
-class of measurable functions is again denote by
f
.
All vector spaces in this text are assumed to be real.

Let
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
be a dense subspace of
L^{2}(E,\Lambda)
and

\mathscr{E}:\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\times\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

be a non-negative definite, symmetric bilinear map.

(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}))
is called closed if
\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
is complete under
\mathscr{E}_{1}^{1/2}
-norm induced by the inner product

\mathscr{E}_{1}(u,v):=\mathscr{E}(u,v)+{\langle u,v\rangle}_{L^{2}(E,\Lambda)}.

It is called a symmetric Dirichlet form if it is closed and

is defined. For a measurable function

A symmetric Dirichlet form

\mathscr{E}(u,v)=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for }u,v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}):\textnormal{supp}[u]\cap\textnormal{supp}[v]=\emptyset.

\mathscr{E}(u,v)=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for }u,v\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}):u\textnormal{ is constant }\Lambda\text{-a.e.~on }\textnormal{supp}[v].

and
\mathscr{E}(\boldsymbol{1},\boldsymbol{1})=0
. In the conservative case the strong local and the local property are equivalent.

Let

be a Dirichlet form on

. If there exists a bilinear form

(2.1)

\mathscr{E}(uw,v)+\mathscr{E}(vw,u)-\mathscr{E}(w,uv)=2\int_{E}w\Gamma(u,v)\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda,\hskip 10.00002ptu,v,w\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\cap L^{\infty}(E,\Lambda),

We set

According to the results in

and

The generator of

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
(a)
and
(b)
.
We have

\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\cap\mathscr{D}(A)\subseteq\mathscr{D}_{1}(L)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003ptLu=Au+\varrho^{-1}\Gamma(u,\varrho),\hskip 10.00002ptu\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\cap\mathscr{D}(A).

Proof.

We fix
u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\cap\mathscr{D}(A)
. For any
v\in\mathscr{L}
it holds

\int_{E}\Gamma(u,v)\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda=\int_{E}\Gamma(u,v\varrho)-v\Gamma(u,\varrho)\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda_{\circ}=\int_{E}\big(-Au-\varrho^{-1}\Gamma(u,\varrho)\big)v\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda.

Thus, the equality
\mathscr{E}(u,v)=\int_{E}f_{u}v\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda

f_{u}:=-Au-\varrho^{-1}\Gamma(u,\varrho)
holds for all

by density of

.
In particular,

\int_{E}(LT_{t}u)v\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda=\int_{E}LuT_{t}v\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda=-\mathscr{E}(u,T_{t}v)=\int_{E}(-f_{u})T_{t}v\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda=\int_{E}(-T_{t}f_{u})v\text{\rm{d}}\Lambda.

for

,

,
and so,

in

and closedness of
(L,\mathscr{D}_{1}(L))
.

and the image measure under the shift,
\Lambda(\,\cdot\,-\phi)
, are absolutely continuous w.r.t. each other.
The linear space of all elements
\phi
with the property in (

of

.
The corresponding Radon–Nikodym density is given by

(see
[

, Chap. 2]
).
Hence, the
\Lambda
-admissibility of every element in the Cameron–Martin space follows with
[
3
, Prop. 4.2]
.

To conclude the preliminaries, we give the definition of an image form under a transform of the state space.
Let

,

be as in the beginning of this section,

be a closed symmetric form in

, and

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{E}(u\circ\Psi,v\circ\Psi)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for }u,v\text{ of its domain,}

\displaystyle:=\big\{w\in L^{2}(S,\Lambda\circ\Psi^{-1}):w\circ\Psi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})\big\},

2.2
Induced diffusion processes on
\mathscr{P}_{p}

In this section, we specify the basic framework on which Sections
3
and
4
build.
The
p
-Wasserstein space
\mathscr{P}_{p}

is a separable Polish topological space w.r.t. the
p
-Wasserstein distance

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{p}\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x,\text{\rm{d}}y)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\hskip 20.00003pt\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where the infimum rus over all couplings and
p\in[1,\infty)
.
In
[
6
]
, the intrinsic derivative on
\mathscr{P}_{p}
and the class
C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
is introduced, in the spirit of
[
1
,
2
,
32
]
, as follows.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\textnormal{id}(x)=x
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(1)

A continuous function

f:\mathscr{P}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called intrinsically differentiable, if

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)\ni\phi\mapsto D_{\phi}f(\mu):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{f(\mu\circ(\textnormal{id}+\varepsilon\phi)^{-1})-f(\mu)}{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}

is a well-defined bounded linear functional for any
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
.
In this case, the intrinsic derivative
Df(\mu)

of
f
at
\mu
is the unique element in
L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)
such that

D_{\phi}f(\mu)=\mu\big(\langle\phi,Df(\mu)\rangle\big),\ \ \phi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu).

(2)

An intrisically differentiable function
f
is called
L
-differentiable if

\lim_{\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)}\downarrow 0}\frac{|f(\mu\circ(\textnormal{id}+\phi)^{-1})-f(\mu)-D_{\phi}f(\mu)|}{\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)}}=0

for each
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
.
The class
C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
contains all
L
-differentiable functions
f
for which
\mu
-versions of
Df(\mu)
,
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
, can be chosen such that

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{p}\ni(x,\mu)\mapsto Df(x,\mu):=Df(\mu)(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

is bounded and continuous.

Below, we define a Dirichlet form
\mathscr{E}
with state space
\mathscr{P}_{p}
,
p\in[1,2]
, and square-field operator of the type

\Gamma(u,v)(\mu)=\mu\big(\gamma_{\mu}\langle Du(\mu),Dv(\mu)\rangle\big),\hskip 20.00003pt\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p},\,u,v\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p}),

where
\gamma_{\mu}(x):=\gamma(x,\mu)
and
\gamma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{p}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is measurable with
c^{-1}\leq\gamma(\cdot,\cdot)\leq c
for some constant
c\in(0,\infty)
.
This construction is analogous to the classical situation in which the state space
\mathbb{B}
is a Banach space and
\mathbb{H}\subseteq\mathbb{B}
a densely embedded Hilbert space.
Then
\Gamma(f,g)(x)=\langle\nabla f(x),\nabla g(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}
,
x\in\mathbb{B}
, for suitable differentiable functions
f,g:\mathbb{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, is the square-field operator of standard gradient-type Dirichlet forms on
\mathbb{B}

(e.g. see
[
3
]
,
[
23
, Sect. II.3]
).

Let
\mathscr{P}_{p}^{\textnormal{ac}}:=\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}:
a probability density
\rho_{\mu}(x)=\frac{\text{\rm{d}}\mu}{\text{\rm{d}}x}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, exists

.
First, we formulate a condition on a reference probability measure on

,
\lambda\in\mathscr{P}_{p}^{\textnormal{ac}}
.
Subsequently, with the map in (
2.8
) below, we consider its push-forward onto
(\mathscr{P}_{p},\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{P}_{p}))
.
In this manner, we can obtain a gradient-type Dirichlet form and diffusion process on
\mathscr{P}_{p}

(C_{1})

Let
\lambda\in\mathscr{P}_{p}^{\textnormal{ac}}
for some
p\in[1,2]
and
\mathscr{D}\subseteq L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\lambda)
be a linear subspace,
equipped with a locally convex Hausdorff topology which makes it a Lusin space, such that

\mathscr{D}
is densely and continuously embedded into

.
Moreover, let

be a probability measure on

such that

has an orthonormal basis

consisting of

-admissible elements.

Remark 2.7

If

on

In Sections

and hence

\mathscr{D}
. The density of

is trivial, since
\mathscr{D}
is dense in

and for each

there is a measurable transport map

such that
\lambda\circ\phi^{-1}=\mu
.
In the case of
\Psi_{\lambda}({\mathscr{D}}_{1})
, we argue as follows. For an arbitrary element
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}
we can choose an optimal pair

such that

\lambda(\varphi)+\mu(\varphi^{c})=\sup_{(f,g)}(\lambda(f)+\mu(g))=\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\lambda),

where the supremum runs over all
(f,g)\in C_{b}
with
f(x)+g(y)\leq|x-y|^{2}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\varphi^{c}(y):=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{2}-\varphi(x)
.
Then, we know from the theory of optimal transport that
|\cdot|^{2}-\varphi
is convex,

\varphi
has an approximate differential
\nabla\varphi
and

\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni x\mapsto x-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\varphi(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

is an optimal transport map with
\lambda\circ\phi^{-1}=\mu
, see
[
5
, Thm. 6.2.4]
. We can approximate
\phi
by elements in
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}
.
For this purpose, let
{\{k_{n}\}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a smooth approximate identity on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\phi_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni x\mapsto(1+\tfrac{1}{n})x-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla(k_{n}*\varphi)(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \ n\geq 1.

Since
\phi_{n}
is the gradient field of a strictly convex function, the Jacobi matrix
\nabla\phi_{n}
of
\phi_{n}
is symmetric and strictly positive definite, uniformly on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This implies that
\phi_{n}
is injective and the image set Im
(\phi_{n})
is open. Moreover, by Lipschitz continuity of the inverse

(\phi_{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

(\phi_{n})
has a limit inside Im
(\phi_{n})
. Thus, Im
(\phi_{n})=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The map
\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow x\mapsto\phi_{n}^{-1}(x)+\tfrac{x}{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

is bi-Lipschitz by construction and hence a diffeomorphism on

.
So, the claimed density of
\Psi_{\lambda}({\mathscr{D}}_{1})
follows since the family

belong to
{\mathscr{D}}_{1}
and

\Psi_{\lambda}(\phi_{n,m})=\lambda\circ(\phi_{n}^{-1}+\tfrac{1}{m}\textnormal{id})\overset{m\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\lambda\circ\phi_{n}^{-1}\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\lambda\circ\phi=:\mu

w.r.t.
\mathbb{W}_{2}

can be constructed analogously as in the proof of

. The statement follows since

∎

4
Functionals with local weak gradient

This section is motivated by the following observation.
In case of Example
3.6
we cannot show that
W_{F}
has a weak intrinsic gradient in the sense of Definition
3.7
, because the candidate for its gradient
DW_{F}(\mu)=\nabla V+\tfrac{q(\rho_{\mu})\nabla\rho_{\mu}}{\rho_{\mu}}
doesn’t have the desired integrability properties w.r.t.
\Lambda(\text{\rm{d}}\mu)
.
We introduce the notion of a
``
local weak intrinsic gradient”.
Under suitable Lipschitz conditions on
F
and
\partial_{2}F

4.3
below,
W_{F}
has a local gradient in this sense. The application to Example
3.6
is addressed subsequently.

Let
\lambda
,
G_{\mathscr{D}}
satisfy
(C1)
with
(\mathscr{D},d_{\mathscr{D}})
as in (

). Defining

\mathscr{D}_{2}:=\big\{\phi\in\mathscr{D}:\nabla\phi\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\big\}

The linear space

\|\nabla^{2}\phi\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|\nabla\phi(x)-\nabla\phi(y)|_{\textnormal{op}}}{|x-y|}

with
|\cdot|_{\textnormal{op}}
being the operator norm for matrices.

Remark 4.1
.

A choice for
G_{\mathscr{D}}
which fulfills these requirements can always be found in the class of Gaussian-based measures. First, we define a non-degenerate Gaussian measure
on
\mathscr{D}_{2}
analogously as done in Example

for the space
\mathscr{D}

instead and proceeding the exact same way. Then, property

\mathscr{D}_{1}
, which is open in

2.7

So, we end up with a measure

on
\mathscr{D}_{2}
such that

holds true.

To define the local weak intrinsic gradient we introduce the sets

\mathscr{D}^{(n)}:=\Big\{\phi\in\mathscr{D}_{2}\cap{\mathscr{D}}_{1}:\|\nabla\phi^{-1}\|_{\infty}+d_{\mathscr{D}_{2}}(\phi,0)<n\Big\},\hskip 20.00003ptn\in\mathbb{N},

{\mathscr{D}}_{1}\cap\mathscr{D}_{2}

For

It should be mentioned that

and

Definition
4.2
below is analogue to Definition
3.7
, but using the notion of the weak gradient coming from the family of Dirichlet forms
{\{\mathscr{E}^{(n)}\}}_{n}
instead of

.
According to the proof of Proposition

,
for any

we find a sequence
{\{f_{m}\}}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{b}^{1}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\lambda))
such that the analogues of
(a)
,
(b)
,
(c)
at the end of Section

The limit

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\ni\phi\mapsto\tilde{D}^{u}(\phi):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nabla f_{m}(\phi)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{in }L^{2}(\mathscr{D}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\lambda),\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathscr{D}^{(n)}}G_{\mathscr{D}})

only depends on

. Moreover, by the hierarchy of open sets,

\tilde{D}^{u}\in\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}L^{2}\big(\mathscr{D}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\lambda),\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathscr{D}^{(n)}}G_{\mathscr{D}}\big)

Definition 4.2

(Local weak intrinsic gradient)
.

For
u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}^{(\infty)})
the unique element

Du\in\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}L^{2}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\Lambda^{(n)}(\text{\rm{d}}\mu)\big)

such that

Du(\Psi_{\lambda}(\phi))\circ\phi=\tilde{D}^{u}(\phi),\hskip 20.00003ptG_{\mathscr{D}}\text{-a.e.~}\phi,

is called
local weak intrinsic gradient
of

.

It is clear that for
u\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
, respectively
u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E})
, the local weak intrinsic gradient coincides with the (weak) intrinsic gradient.

Let
\nabla_{1}
and
\partial_{2}
denote the
gradient in
x
and derivative in
s
for
(x,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

We assume
(C_{1})
,
(C2)
with
G_{\mathscr{D}}(\mathscr{D}_{2}\cap\mathscr{D}_{1})=1
and
\lambda\in\mathscr{P}_{p}^{\textnormal{ac}}
such that
\rho_{\lambda}
is strictly positive, Lipschitz continuous. Then:

(i)
\rho_{\mu}
is Lipschitz continuous for
\Lambda
-a.e.
\mu
.

(ii) If
F
and
\partial_{2}F
are locally Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
and

\Lambda^{(n)}\Big(\big\||(\nabla_{1}\partial_{2}F)(\cdot,\rho_{\mu})|+\big|(\partial_{2}\partial_{2}F)(\cdot,\rho_{\mu})\nabla\rho_{\mu}\big|+|\partial_{2}F(\cdot,\rho_{\mu})|\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)}\Big)<\infty

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then the functional
W_{F}
defined in (
1.10
) has local weak intrinsic derivative
satisfying

(4.4)

DW_{F}(\mu)(x)=H_{F}(x,\mu):=(\nabla_{1}\partial_{2}F)(x,\rho_{\mu}(x))+(\partial_{2}\partial_{2}F)(x,\rho_{\mu}(x))\nabla\rho_{\mu}(x)

for
\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\Lambda(\text{\rm{d}}\mu)
-a.e.
(x,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{p}
.

Proof.

We complete the proof by four steps. Claim (i) follows from Step (1). Claim (ii) is verified in Steps (2)-(4).

(1) First, we show that
\rho_{\mu}
is Lipschitz continuous for
\Lambda^{(n)}
-a.e.
\mu
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with a Lipschitz constant only depending on
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We recall

(4.5)

\rho_{\mu}=\Big(\frac{\rho_{\lambda}}{|\textnormal{det}\nabla\phi|}\Big)\circ\phi^{-1},\hskip 20.00003pt\phi\in{\mathscr{D}}_{1},\ \mu=\Psi_{\lambda}(\phi)=\lambda\circ\phi^{-1}.

For any
\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{(n)}
, we have

(4.6)

\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\lor\|\nabla^{2}\phi\|_{\infty}\lor\|\nabla\phi^{-1}\|_{\infty}\lor|\phi(0)|\leq n.

Then

|\phi^{-1}(0)|\leq n+|\phi^{-1}(0)-\phi(0)|\leq n+n|\phi(\phi(0))|\leq n+n|\phi(0)|+n^{2}|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 3n^{3}

and hence

|\phi^{-1}(x)|\leq 3n^{3}+\|\nabla\phi^{-1}\|_{\infty}|x|\leq 3n^{3}+n|x|.

This together with (
4.6
) yields

(4.7)

\displaystyle\inf_{|x|\leq r}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\lambda}}{|\textnormal{det}\nabla\phi|}\Big)(\phi^{-1}(x))\geq n^{-d}\inf_{|x|\leq 3n^{3}+nr}\rho_{\lambda}(x)>0,

\displaystyle\sup_{|x|\leq r}\Big(\frac{\rho_{\lambda}}{|\textnormal{det}\nabla\phi|}\Big)(\phi^{-1}(x))\leq n^{d}\sup_{|x|\leq 3n^{3}+nr}\rho_{\lambda}(x)<\infty,\hskip 20.00003ptr\in(0,\infty).

Moreover, for
\mu:=\Psi_{\lambda}(\phi)
,
\phi\in\mathscr{D}^{(n)}
, (
4.5
) implies

(4.8)

\nabla\rho_{\mu}=\Bigg(\frac{(\nabla\phi)^{-1}\nabla\rho_{\lambda}}{|\textnormal{det}\nabla\phi|}-{\rm sgn}(\textnormal{det}[\nabla\phi])\frac{\rho_{\lambda}(\nabla\phi)^{-1}(\nabla\textnormal{det}\nabla\phi)}{|\textnormal{det}\nabla\phi|^{2}}\Bigg)\circ\phi^{-1}

and thus by (
4.6
) we find a constant
c_{n}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(4.9)

\|\nabla\rho_{\mu}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{n},\ \ \Lambda^{(n)}\text{-a.e.}\ \mu.

(2) In this step we calculate the local weak intrinsic gradient
DW_{F}
for
F
satisfying

(4.10)

F\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)),\hskip 10.00002pt\bigcup_{s>0}{\rm supp}[F(\cdot,s)]\subset[-l,l]^{d}\ \text{
for\ some \ }l>0.

Let
\tau\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for some constant
\varepsilon>0

0\leq\tau\leq 1,\ \ \ \tau(x)=1\ \text{if}\ |x|\leq\varepsilon,\ \ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tau(x)\text{\rm{d}}x=1.

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\tau_{m}(x):=m^{d}\tau(mx)
. Define

(\tau_{m}*\mu)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tau_{m}(x-y)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}y),\ \mu\in\mathscr{P},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By (
4.5
)-(
4.9
), we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\tau_{m}*\mu\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \ \inf_{[-l,l]^{d}}\tau_{m}*\mu>0,\ \ m\in\mathbb{N},\ \mu\in\Psi_{\lambda}(\mathscr{D}^{(n)}),

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\mu\in\Psi_{\lambda}(\mathscr{D}^{(n)})}\big(\|\rho_{\mu}-\tau_{m}*\mu\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla\rho_{\mu}-\nabla(\tau_{m}*\mu)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu)}\big)=0,\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, in view of (
4.10
), there exists
\tilde{F}\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
such that

F(x,(\tau_{m}*\mu)(x))=\tilde{F}(x,(\tau_{m}*\mu)(x))

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,m\in\mathbb{N},\,\mu\in\Psi_{\lambda}(\mathscr{D}^{(n)})
, and hence
defining
u_{m}(\mu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{F}\big(x,(\tau_{m}*\mu)(x)\big)\text{\rm{d}}x
,
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}
, we have
u_{m}\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{p})
and

\displaystyle u_{m}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F\big(x,(\tau_{m}*\mu)(x)\big)\text{\rm{d}}x,

\displaystyle Du_{m}(\mu)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big[\partial_{2}F(y,(\tau_{m}*\mu)(y))\big](-\nabla\tau_{m})(y-x)\text{\rm{d}}y

for
\mu\in\Psi_{\lambda}(\mathscr{D}^{(n)})
.
With integration by parts we get

Using (
4.10
) and (
4.11

Hence,
W_{F}
has local weak intrinsic derivative
DW_{F}=H_{F}
as claimed.

(3) We calculate the local weak intrinsic derivative
DW_{F}
for
F
as in the assumptions, but keep the support condition

\bigcup_{s>0}{\rm supp}[F(\cdot,s)]\subset[-l,l]^{d}\ \text{
for\ some \ }l>0

of the previous step. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and

\displaystyle F_{m}(x,s):=\frac{1}{m}\int_{s}^{s+m^{-1}}\text{\rm{d}}r\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(y,r)\tau_{m}(x-y)\text{\rm{d}}x.

satisfies (
4.10

and from (

Consequently, for each

(4.13)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\big\|W_{F_{m}}-W_{F}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{2},\Lambda^{(n)})}+\big\|DW_{F_{m}}-H_{F}\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\Lambda^{(n)}(\text{\rm{d}}\mu))}\Big)=0.

has local weak intrinsic gradient

(4) Let
F

Then

satisfies Condition (

has local weak

4.13

for each

So,

has local weak intrinsic derivative

∎

The nonhomogeneous Cauchy–Riemann equation on families of open Riemann surfaces
Franc Forstnerič

Abstract
. In this paper we use the nonhomogeneous Beltrami equation to give an optimal solution to the nonhomogeneous Cauchy–Riemann equation for continuous or smooth families of complex structures and ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -forms of a Hölder class on a smooth open orientable surface. As an application, we obtain the Oka–Grauert principle for complex line bundles on families of open Riemann surfaces.

Primary 32W05; secondary 32Q56, 32L20

1.
The
\overline{\partial}
-equation on families of open Riemann surfaces

In this paper, we show that the nonhomogeneous Cauchy–Riemann
equation, or the
\overline{\partial}
-equation for short, can be solved for very
general families of complex structures and
(0,1)
-forms of a Hölder class
on a smooth open orientable surface, with the usual gain of one derivative
in the space variable and without any loss of regularity in the parameter.
Our main results are Theorem
1.1
and its global version,
Corollary
1.2
. The proof uses the
nonhomogeneous Beltrami equation
together with the Runge approximation theorem for families
of holomorphic functions on families of open Riemann surfaces
(see
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
). An application
is a Dolbeault cohomology vanishing theorem
(see Proposition
1.5
) and the Oka principle
for complex line bundles on such families (see Theorem
2.3
).

We begin by introducing the setup, referring
to
[
9
]
for more details.
Let
X
be a smooth orientable surface.
A complex structure on
X
is an endomorphism
J
of its tangent bundle

TX
satisfying
J^{2}:=J\circ J=-\mathrm{Id}
. Thus,
J
is a section of the smooth
vector bundle
T^{*}X\otimes TX\cong\mathrm{Hom}(TX,TX)\to X
whose fibre over

x\in X
is the space
\mathrm{Hom}(T_{x}X,T_{x}X)
of linear maps
T_{x}X\to T_{x}X
.
We endow the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}\cong\mathbb{C}
with the standard complex
structure
J_{\mathrm{st}}
given in standard basis by the matrix

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,
and in complex notation by multiplication by
\mathfrak{i}=\sqrt{-1}
.
A differentiable function
f:U\to\mathbb{C}
on an open set
U\subset X

is said to be
J
-holomorphic
(or
(J,J_{\mathrm{st}})
-holomorphic)
if the Cauchy–Riemann equation

df_{x}\circ J_{x}=J_{\mathrm{st}}\circ df_{x}=\mathfrak{i}\,df_{x}
holds
for every
x\in U
. We say that
J
is of local Hölder class

\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
and
0<\alpha<1

if for any relatively compact domain
\Omega\Subset X
, the restriction

J|_{\Omega}\in\Gamma^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega,T^{*}\Omega\otimes T\Omega)

is a section of
T^{*}\Omega\otimes T\Omega
of class

\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega)
, i.e., with finite
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega)

norm. (Hölder norms are defined
with respect to a smooth Riemannian metric on
X
;
see
[
10
, Sect. 4.1]
.
If
\Omega
has
\mathscr{C}^{1}
boundary, as will be the case
in our results, then a function
f\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega)

extends to a unique function in
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
.
The same holds for complex structures.)
For such
J
, there is an atlas
\{(U_{i},\phi_{i})\}_{i}
of
open sets
U_{i}\subset X
with
\bigcup_{i}U_{i}=X
and

J
-holomorphic charts
\phi_{i}:U_{i}\to\phi_{i}(U_{i})\subset\mathbb{C}

of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(U_{i})
;
see
[
2
, Theorem 5.3.4]
.
Since the transition maps
\phi_{i}\circ\phi_{j}^{-1}

are
J_{\mathrm{st}}
-biholomorphic,

J
determines on
X
the structure of a Riemann surface,
and every
J
-holomorphic function is of local class
\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}

in the given smooth structure on
X
. Since the inverse
of a diffeomorphism of local class
\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}

is of the same class (see Norton
[
29
]
and
Bojarski et al.
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
),
the smooth structure on
X
determined by a complex structure
J

of local class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
is
\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}
compatible
with the given smooth structure. In the sequel, we shall often admit
the adjective ”local” when speaking of complex structures or functions of
Hölder classes.

Let
B
be a topological space whose nature will depend
on the integer
l\geq 0
to be specified.
When
l=0
, we assume that
B
is a paracompact Hausdorff space,
and if
l>0
then
B
will be a (paracompact) manifold of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}
.
A family
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
of complex structures on
X

is said to be of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
if
for any relatively compact domain
\Omega\Subset X
, the map

B\ni b\mapsto J_{b}|_{\Omega}\in\Gamma^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega,T^{*}\Omega\otimes T\Omega)
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}

as a map to the Hölder space

\Gamma^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega,T^{*}\Omega\otimes T\Omega)
.
Following Kodaira and Spencer
[
22
]

and Kirillov
[
21
]
, the collection
\{(X,J_{b})\}_{b\in B}

is called a family of Riemann surfaces of class

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
. Such a family
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
can equivalently
be given by a family
\{\mu_{b}\}_{b\in B}
of maps from

X
to the unit disc
\mathbb{D}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\zeta|<1\}

of the same smoothness class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
;
see
[
9
, Sect. 2]
.
A continuous map
f:B\times X\to Y
to a complex manifold
Y

is said to be
fibrewise holomorphic
or
X
-holomorphic

if the map
f_{b}=f(b,\cdotp):X\to Y
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic
for every
b\in B
. Assuming that
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is of class

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
, the space
Z=B\times X

endowed with the complex structure

J_{b}
on the fibre
\{b\}\times X
admits fibre preserving

X
-holomorphic charts of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}

with values in
B\times\mathbb{C}
which are local in
b\in B

and semiglobal in the space variable
x\in X

(see
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
), and
every
X
-holomorphic function
f:B\times X\to\mathbb{C}
of class

\mathscr{C}^{l,0}
(i.e.,
f
admits continuous partial derivatives
of order
\leq l
with respect to the parameter
b\in B
)
is of local class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}

(see
[
9
, Lemma 5.6]
).

Assume now that
X
is a smooth
open
surface endowed
with a complex structure
J
of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}

(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\ 0<\alpha<1)
.
By Gunning and Narasimhan
[
15
]

there is a
J
-holomorphic immersion
z:X\to\mathbb{C}
.
By what was said above,
z
is of local class

\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}
in the given smooth structure on
X
, and
it provides a local holomorphic coordinate on
X
at every point.
Its differentials
dz
and
d\bar{z}
are of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
,
and they trivialise the respective cotangent bundles

T^{*(1,0)}_{J}X
and
T^{*(0,1)}_{J}X

consisting of
(1,0)
-forms and
(0,1)
-forms with respect to
J
.
We have
\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}T^{*}X=T^{*(1,0)}_{J}X\oplus T^{*(0,1)}_{J}X
,
and every
1
-form
\beta
on
X
can be uniquely written
as
\beta=Adz+Bd\bar{z}
for a pair of functions
A,B:X\to\mathbb{C}
.
Note that
\beta
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
if and only if
the functions
A,B
are of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
.
Given a differentiable function
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
, its differential splits
in the
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
parts:

(1.1)

df=\partial_{J}f+\overline{\partial}_{J}f=f_{z}\,\cdotp dz+f_{\bar{z}}\,\cdotp d\bar{z}.

Given a
(0,1)
-form
\beta=u\,d\bar{z}
on a domain
\Omega\subset X
,
the
\overline{\partial}_{J}
-equation asks for a function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}

satisfying
\overline{\partial}_{J}f=\beta
; equivalently,
f_{\bar{z}}=u
.
If
\Omega
is relatively compact and has sufficiently regular boundary
then this elliptic equation is solvable in many function
spaces with a gain of one derivative; see e.g. Ahlfors
[
1
]
and Astala et al.
[
2
]
.

We shall prove the following result, which gives families
of solutions of the
\overline{\partial}
-equation
for families of complex structures and
(0,1)
-forms
on domains in a smooth open surface.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
X
is a smooth open orientable surface,

\Omega\Subset X
is a relatively compact domain with

\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}
boundary for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and

0<\alpha<1
,
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
B
is a paracompact Hausdorff
space if
l=0
and a
\mathscr{C}^{l}
manifold if
l>0
,
and
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is a family of complex structures
of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}(B\times\overline{\Omega})

on
\overline{\Omega}
.
Given a family of
(0,1)
-forms
\{\beta_{b}\}_{b\in B}

on
\overline{\Omega}
of class

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}(B\times\overline{\Omega})
,
there is a function

f\in\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}(B\times\overline{\Omega})
satisfying

(1.2)

\overline{\partial}_{J_{b}}f(b,\cdotp)=\beta_{b}\ \ \text{on $\overline{\Omega}$ for every $b\in B$.}

Theorem
1.1
, together with the Runge approximation theorem in

[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
, implies the following corollary
concerning solutions of families of global
\overline{\partial}
-equations.

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume that
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is a family of complex structures
of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
on a smooth open orientable surface
X
,
where
l,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
l\leq k+1
,
0<\alpha<1
,
and
B
is as in Theorem
1.1
.
Given a family
\{\beta_{b}\}_{b\in B}
of
(0,1)
-forms

\beta_{b}\in\Gamma(X,T^{*(0,1)}_{J_{b}}X)
of class

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
, there is a function
f:B\times X\to\mathbb{C}

of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}
satisfying

(1.3)

\overline{\partial}_{J_{b}}f(b,\cdotp)=\beta_{b}\ \ \text{on $X$ for every $b\in B$.}

The condition
l\leq k+1
in the corollary is due to the use of the Runge
approximation theorem for fibrewise holomorphic functions
on families of open Riemann surfaces

[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
, which
was proved under this assumption.
See also Remark
1.4
concerning the
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
case.
This restriction is unnecessary in Theorem
1.1
.

Except perhaps for the condition
l\leq k+1
in the corollary,
these results are optimal since we have the expected gain of one derivative
in the space variable and no loss of regularity
in the parameter
b\in B
. Note that the 1-forms

\beta_{b}
and the function
f
in (
1.2
), (
1.3
)
are expressed in terms of the smooth coordinates on
X
. Expressing
them in terms of local fibrewise holomorphic coordinates leads
to a loss of
2l
derivatives in the space variable if
l>0
;
see
[
9
, V3, Theorem 9.1]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We shall use the connection between complex
structures and Beltrami multipliers; see

[
9
, Sect. 2]
or any
standard text on quasiconformal maps for the details.

Choose a smooth complex structure
J
on
X
and
a
J
-holomorphic immersion
z:X\to\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\Omega\Subset X
be as in the theorem.
Every function
\mu:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{D}

of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
with values
in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}

determines a complex structure
J_{\mu}
on
\overline{\Omega}

of the same class, with
\mu=0
corresponding to
J|_{\overline{\Omega}}
.
Conversely, every complex structure
J^{\prime}
on
\overline{\Omega}

of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
in the same orientation class
as
J
equals
J_{\mu}
for a unique

\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{D})
.
A differentiable function
f:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies

\overline{\partial}_{J_{\mu}}f=0
, and hence is
J_{\mu}
-holomorphic on

\Omega
, if and only if it satisfies the homogeneous Beltrami equation

f_{\bar{z}}=\mu f_{z}
, where the partial derivatives
f_{z}
and

f_{\bar{z}}
are defined by (
1.1
). The analogous
statements hold for globally defined complex structures on
X

and function
\mu:X\to\mathbb{D}
.

To prove the theorem, it suffices to show that for every point
b_{0}\in B

there is a neighbourhood
B_{0}\subset B
of
b_{0}
such that
equation (
1.2
) is solvable on
B_{0}\times\overline{\Omega}
with

f\in\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}(B_{0}\times\overline{\Omega})
.
By using
\mathscr{C}^{l}
partitions of unity on
B
we then obtain
a solution on
B\times\overline{\Omega}
, thereby proving the theorem.

Note that the complex structure
J_{b_{0}}
on
\overline{\Omega}

extends to a complex structure on
X
of local class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
.
To see this, we represent

J_{b_{0}}
in terms of
J
by a Beltrami multiplier

\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{D})
.
Since
b\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}
,

\mu
extends from
\overline{\Omega}
to a function

\mu^{\prime}:X\to\mathbb{D}
of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}

with compact support
(see Gilbarg and Trudinger
[
10
, Lemma 6.37]
).
The associated complex structure
J_{\mu^{\prime}}
on
X
is of local
class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
and it coincides with
J_{\mu}
on

\overline{\Omega}
. For
k\geq 1
the same conclusion
holds if
b\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
.

This reduces the proof to the following proposition.
In this result, the smooth structure on
X
is induced
by the complex structure
J
, and the Hölder norms are
computed with respect to a fixed smooth Riemannian metric on
X

whose precise choice is unimportant.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
(X,J)
be an open Riemann surface,
and let
\Omega\Subset X
,
k
, and
\alpha
be as in Theorem
1.1
.
For
\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{D})
let
J_{\mu}

denote the associated complex structure on
\overline{\Omega}
,
with
J_{0}=J|_{\overline{\Omega}}
. For
c>0
set

B_{c}=\{\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega}):\|\mu\|_{k,\alpha}<c\}.

There exists
c>0
such that for any map

B_{c}\ni\mu\mapsto\beta_{\mu}\in\Gamma^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega},T^{*(0,1)}_{J_{\mu}}\overline{\Omega})

of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}
,
l\in\{0,1,\ldots,\infty,\omega\}
,
there is a function

f\in\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}(B_{c}\times\overline{\Omega})

such that for every
\mu\in B_{c}

the function
f_{\mu}=f(\mu,\cdotp):\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies

(1.4)

\overline{\partial}_{J_{\mu}}f_{\mu}=\beta_{\mu}.

Proof.

We begin by recalling some
technical tools from
[
9
, Secs. 3-4]
.
Choose a
J
-holomorphic immersion
z:X\to\mathbb{C}
.
There is a Cauchy kernel on
(X,J)
which determines on any
smoothly bounded domain
\Omega\Subset X
a pair
of bounded linear operators

P:\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})\to\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
and

S:\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})\to\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})

(
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
0<\alpha<1
) such that
for any
\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
, the
Cauchy operator
P
solves the equation

P(\phi)_{\bar{z}}=\phi
on
\overline{\Omega}
and
the Beurling operator
S
is given by
S(\phi)=P(\phi)_{z}
.
The properties of these operators are summarised in

[
9
, Theorem 3.2]
.
Although the cited result is stated for domains
\Omega

with
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
boundaries, it is clear from the proof and

[
10
, Lemma 6.37]
that it holds
if
b\Omega
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
if
k\geq 1
,
and of class
\mathscr{C}^{(1,\alpha)}
if
k=0
.

By
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
there are a constant

c>0
and a function
h:B_{c}\times\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
for every
\mu\in B_{c}
,
h_{\mu}=h(\mu,\cdotp):\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}

is a
J_{\mu}
-holomorphic immersion of class

\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
depending analytically on
\mu
.
We recall the proof since we shall use a similar idea
in the sequel. The function
f=h_{\mu}
must solve
the Beltrami equation
f_{\bar{z}}=\mu f_{z}
.
We look for a solution in the form
f=z|_{\overline{\Omega}}+P(\phi)
with

\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
.
Note that
\phi=0
corresponds to
f=z|_{\overline{\Omega}}
.
We have that

f_{\bar{z}}=P(\phi)_{\bar{z}}=\phi\quad\text{and}\quad f_{z}=1+P(\phi)_{z}=1+S(\phi).

Inserting in the Beltrami equation
f_{\bar{z}}=\mu f_{z}
gives
(I-\mu S)\phi=\mu
,
where
I
denotes the identity map on
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
.
For
\|\mu\|_{k,\alpha}
small enough we have that
\|\mu S\|_{k,\alpha}<1
,
so the operator
I-\mu S
is invertible and its bounded inverse
depends analytically on
\mu
:

\Theta(\mu):=(I-\mu S)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\mu S)^{j}\in\mathrm{Lin}(\mathscr{C}^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})).

(See Mujica
[
28
, 29.3 Theorem]
.)
Hence,
\phi=\Theta(\mu)\mu
and we get
the following solution
h_{\mu}=f
to the
Beltrami equation
f_{\bar{z}}=\mu f_{z}
on
\overline{\Omega}
:

h_{\mu}=z|_{\overline{\Omega}}+P(\Theta(\mu)\mu)=z|_{\overline{\Omega}}+P((I-\mu S)^{-1}\mu)\in\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega}).

Note that
h_{\mu}
depends analytically on
\mu
. For
\|\mu\|_{k,\alpha}

small enough,
h_{\mu}
is so close to
h_{0}=z|_{\overline{\Omega}}
in

\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
that it is an immersion.
Hence, for
c>0
small enough,

\{\theta_{\mu}=dh_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in B_{c}}

is a family of nowhere vanishing holomorphic

1
-forms on
(\Omega,J_{b})
of class

\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
with analytic dependence on
\mu
.
(The existence of a family
\{\theta_{b}\}_{b\in B}
of globally defined nowhere vanishing holomorphic
1
-forms on
(X,J_{b})
of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
was proved in
[
9
, Theorem 7.1]
under a stronger assumption on the parameter space
B
.)
The conjugate
\bar{\theta}_{\mu}=d\overline{h_{\mu}}
is a nowhere vanishing
antiholomorphic
(0,1)
-form with respect to
J_{\mu}
for every

\mu\in B_{c}
. Thus, every family
\{\beta_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in B_{c}}

on
\overline{\Omega}
, where
\beta_{\mu}
is a
(0,1)
-form
with respect to
J_{\mu}
, is of the form

\beta_{\mu}=u_{\mu}\,d\overline{h_{\mu}}
for a family of functions

u_{\mu}:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}
,
\mu\in B_{c}
. If the family

\{\beta_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in B_{c}}
is of class

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}(B_{c}\times\overline{\Omega})

then
\{u_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in B_{c}}
is of the same class, and vice versa.

We shall express the
\overline{\partial}_{J_{\mu}}
-equation (
1.4
) as a
nonhomogeneous Beltrami equation with respect to the immersion
z
.
For
\mu\in B_{c}
we can uniquely express any complex
1
-form

\beta
on
\overline{\Omega}
as

(1.5)

\beta=Adz+Bd\bar{z}=A_{\mu}dh_{\mu}+B_{\mu}d\overline{h_{\mu}}.

Note that
\beta
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})

if and only if the coefficients
A,B,A_{\mu},B_{\mu}:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}

are of this class. We now express
A_{\mu}
and
B_{\mu}
in terms of
the functions
A,B,\mu
, and

(1.6)

g_{\mu}:=(h_{\mu})_{z}\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega}).

We have that

dh_{\mu}=(h_{\mu})_{z}\,dz+(h_{\mu})_{\bar{z}}\,d\bar{z}=g_{\mu}\,dz+\mu g_{\mu}\,d\bar{z}

where the second identity follows from the Beltrami equation

(h_{\mu})_{\bar{z}}=\mu(h_{\mu})_{z}
. From this and (
1.5
) it follows that

\displaystyle Adz+Bd\bar{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(g_{\mu}dz+\mu g_{\mu}d\bar{z})+B_{\mu}(\overline{\mu g_{\mu}}\,dz+\overline{g_{\mu}}\,d\bar{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A_{\mu}g_{\mu}+B_{\mu}\overline{\mu g_{\mu}})dz+(A_{\mu}\mu g_{\mu}+B_{\mu}\overline{g_{\mu}})d\bar{z}

and hence

A=A_{\mu}g_{\mu}+B_{\mu}\overline{\mu g_{\mu}},\qquad B=A_{\mu}\mu g_{\mu}+B_{\mu}\overline{g_{\mu}}.

Solving these equations on
A_{\mu}
and
B_{\mu}
gives

A_{\mu}=\frac{A-\bar{\mu}B}{(1-|\mu|^{2})g_{\mu}},\qquad B_{\mu}=\frac{B-\mu A}{(1-|\mu|^{2})\overline{g_{\mu}}}.

Taking
\beta=df
we have
A=f_{z}
,
B=f_{\bar{z}}
,
A_{\mu}=f_{h_{\mu}}
,
and
B_{\mu}=f_{\overline{h_{\mu}}}
. Inserting these quantities in
the above expression for
B_{\mu}
shows that the
nonhomogeneous Cauchy–Riemann equation

\overline{\partial}_{J_{\mu}}f=\beta_{\mu}=u_{\mu}d\overline{h_{\mu}}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ f_{\overline{h_{\mu}}}=\frac{f_{\bar{z}}-\mu f_{z}}{(1-|\mu|^{2})\overline{g_{\mu}}}=u_{\mu}

is equivalent to the nonhomogeneous Beltrami equation

(1.7)

f_{\bar{z}}-\mu f_{z}=(1-|\mu|^{2})\overline{g_{\mu}}\,u_{\mu}

with
g_{\mu}
given by (
1.6
).
The regularity conditions in the proposition imply that the right hand side is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
on
B_{c}\times\overline{\Omega}
.
We look for a solution of (
1.7
) in the form
f=f(\mu)=P(\phi)
with

\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
to be determined.
Inserting
f_{\bar{z}}=P(\phi)_{\bar{z}}=\phi
and
f_{z}=P(\phi)_{z}=S(\phi)

into (
1.7
) gives

f_{\bar{z}}-\mu f_{z}=(I-\mu S)\phi=(1-|\mu|^{2})\overline{g_{\mu}}\,u_{\mu}.

For
\|\mu\|_{k,\alpha}
small enough the operator
I-\mu S
is
invertible and we obtain

\phi=\phi_{\mu}=(I-\mu S)^{-1}\left((1-|\mu|^{2})\overline{g_{\mu}}\,u_{\mu}\right).

Since the bounded linear operator

(I-\mu S)^{-1}\in\mathrm{Lin}(\mathscr{C}^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}))

is analytic in
\mu
and
(1-|\mu|^{2})\overline{g_{\mu}}\,u_{\mu}\in\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}(B_{c}\times\overline{\Omega})
, the map

(\mu,x)\mapsto\phi_{\mu}(x)
belongs to

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}(B_{c}\times\overline{\Omega})
.
Finally, the solution of (
1.7
) is
f_{\mu}=P(\phi_{\mu})
,
and the map
(\mu,x)\to f_{\mu}(x)
belongs to

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}(B_{c}\times\overline{\Omega})
.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Global solvability of (
1.3
) is
obtained by exhausting
X
by an increasing family
of relatively compact, smoothly bounded Runge domains, solving the
equation (
1.3
) on each of them by using
Theorem
1.1
, and applying the Runge
approximation theorem on families of open Riemann
surfaces (see
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
)
at every step of the induction to ensure convergence of solutions.
The straightforward details can be found in the preprint

[
9
, V3, proof of Theorem 9.1]
.
One essentially follows the standard scheme in the proof of
Cartan’s Theorem B
[
16
, Section VIII.14]
.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

(A) If
B
is a manifold of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}
with

0<l\leq k+1
then the space
Z=B\times X
in
Theorem
1.1
, endowed with an atlas
of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}
given by

[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
,
is a
mixed manifold
of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}

in the sense of Jurchescu
[
18
,
19
]
,
and a Levi-flat CR manifold of CR-dimension one in the
sense of Cauchy–Riemann geometry
[
3
]
.
(Mongodi and Tomassini
[
27
]
also call
such manifolds
semiholomorphic foliations
. Apparently they
were not aware of Jurchescu’s work.)
In Jurchescu’s papers, maps which are holomorphic on complex leaves of a mixed manifold are called
morphic
, while in CR geometry they are called CR maps. The Runge approximation theorem

[
9
, Theorem 1.1]

shows that such
Z
is also a
Cartan manifold
of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}

in the sense of
[
19
, Sect. 6]
.
Cartan manifolds are analogues of Stein manifolds in
the category of mixed manifolds.
Solvability of the tangential
\overline{\partial}
-complex on
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}

smooth Cartan manifolds was shown by Jurchescu in

[
20
, Sect. 3]
by using sheaf-theoretic approach,
similar to the one in the classical theory of Stein manifolds.

(B) There are results in the literature concerning the
\overline{\partial}
-equation
on moving families of domains, also in higher dimensional complex manifolds;
see Diederich and Ohsawa
[
7
]
,
Cho and Choi
[
6
]
,
Gong and Kim
[
11
, Theorem 4.5]
,
Simon
[
31
]
,
Kruse
[
25
]
,
among others. By pulling back a complex structure by a
family of diffeomorphisms, the case of moving domains
is changed to the variation of the complex structure on a fixed domain.
Conversely, Hamilton’s theorem
[
17
]

(see also
[
14
, Theorem 1.13]

for a simpler proof and
[
9
, Corollary 4.5]
for
domains in Riemann surfaces) shows that a small smooth
integrable variation of the complex structure on a
smoothly bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain can be realised
by a smooth variation of the domain in the ambient Stein manifold.
In
[
14
]
, Greene and Krantz
used methods introduced by Kohn
[
23
,
24
]

to study stability of the
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann operator and
the canonical (Kohn) solution to the
\overline{\partial}
-equation under small integrable perturbations
of a complex structure
J
on a compact strongly

J
-pseudoconvex domain
\overline{M}
.
Assuming that the boundary
bM
is of class
2s+5
for some
s\geq 1
,
they obtained continuous dependence of the Neumann operator

N_{J}
in the Sobolev
L^{2}
-space
W^{s}

under small variations of
J
of class
\mathscr{C}^{2s+5}
, and hence
continuous dependence of solutions of the
\overline{\partial}_{J}
-equation
in
W^{s-1}

[
14
, Theorems 3.9, and 3.10]
.
It is not clear whether these results are (close to) optimal,
and we could not find results in the literature with better than continuous
dependence of solutions on the complex structure.

Theorem
1.1
implies the vanishing of the
Dolbeault cohomology on families of open Riemann surfaces.
To explain this, assume that

B
,
X
, and
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
are as in Theorem
1.1
,
where the family
J_{b}
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
for some

0\leq l\leq k+1
and
0<\alpha<1
.
Denote by
\mathscr{O}
the sheaf of germs of functions
f
of class

\mathscr{C}^{l}
on
Z=B\times X
which are fibrewise holomorphic,
that is,
f_{b}=f(b,\cdotp)
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic for each
b\in B
.
By
[
9
, Lemma 5.6]
,

\mathscr{O}
is a subsheaf of the sheaf
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}
.
These are sheaves of unital abelian rings, in particular, of abelian groups.

Proposition 1.5
.

(Assumptions as above.)
H^{q}(Z,\mathscr{O})=0
for all
q=1,2,\ldots
.

Proof.

Consider the sequence of homomorphisms of sheaves of abelian groups

(1.8)

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\partial}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}_{(0,1)}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}_{(0,1)}
is the sheaf of germs
of
(0,1)
-forms of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
on the fibres

Z_{b}=(X,J_{b})
and
\overline{\partial}
is the operator
which equals
\overline{\partial}_{J_{b}}
on
Z_{b}
for every
b\in B
.
By Theorem
1.1
the sequence (
1.8
) is exact.
The second and the third sheaf in (
1.8
)
are fine sheaves (they admit partitions of unity), so they are acyclic,
i.e., their cohomology groups of order
\geq 1
vanish. It follows that

H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{O})=\Gamma\big(Z,\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}_{(0,1)}\big)/\overline{\partial}\,\Gamma\big(Z,\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}\big)

and
H^{q}(Z,\mathscr{O})=0
for
q\geq 2

(see
[
16
, Chapter VI]
).
Here,
\Gamma
denotes the space of global sections of a sheaf.
The quotient group on the right hand side above
vanishes by Theorem
1.1
.
∎

2.
The Oka principle for line bundles
on families of open Riemann surfaces

Every holomorphic vector bundle on an
open Riemann surface is holomorphically trivial by the Oka–Grauert principle; see
[
30
,
13
]

and
[
8
, Theorem 5.3.1]
.
We now show that Proposition
1.5
implies
the Oka principle for complex line bundles on families
of open Riemann surfaces; see Theorem
2.3
.
We also discuss vector bundles of higher rank.

Let
B
be a paracompact Hausdorff space,
X
be a smooth
open surface, and
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
be a continuous family of complex
structures on
X
of class
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}
for some
0<\alpha<1
.
We let
Z=B\times X
with the fibre
Z_{b}=\{b\}\times X
endowed
with the complex structure
J_{b}
for each
b\in B
.
Denote by
\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{C}_{Z}
and
\mathscr{O}=\mathscr{O}_{Z}

the sheaves of germs of continuous resp. fibrewise holomorphic functions on
Z=B\times X
.
These are sheaves of unital abelian rings, with
\mathscr{O}_{Z}
a subsheaf
of
\mathscr{C}_{Z}
. Furthermore,
\mathscr{O}^{*}\subset\mathscr{O}
and

\mathscr{C}^{*}\subset\mathscr{C}
denote the subsheaves consisting
of germs with nonzero values;
these are sheaves of (multiplicative) abelian groups.
Likewise, for a complex Lie group
G
,
\mathscr{O}_{Z}^{G}\subset\mathscr{C}_{Z}^{G}

denote the sheaves of germs of maps
Z\to G
of respective classes;
these are sheaves of groups.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
,
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
, and
Z=B\times X
be
as above, and denote by
Z_{b}=(X,J_{b})
the fibre of
Z
over
b\in B
.
A topological complex vector bundle
E\to Z

is said to be fibrewise holomorphic
if for any
b\in B
the restriction
E|Z_{b}\to Z_{b}
is a holomorphic vector
bundle on the Riemann surface
(X,J_{b})
.

Let
Gl(r,\mathbb{C})
denote the group of invertible
r\times r
matrices.
We have the following observation.

Proposition 2.2
.

A complex vector bundle
E\to Z=B\times X
of rank
r

is fibrewise holomorphic if and only if it admits a transition cocycle
consisting of sections of the sheaf
\mathscr{O}_{Z}^{Gl(r,\mathbb{C})}
.

In the case of line bundles we have
r=1
and
Gl(r,\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
so
\mathscr{O}^{Gl(r,\mathbb{C})}=\mathscr{O}^{*}
.

Proof.

A vector bundle
E\to Z
which admits a transition cocycle
consisting of sections of the sheaf
\mathscr{O}_{Z}^{Gl(r,\mathbb{C})}
is
fibrewise holomorphic by the definition of the sheaf
\mathscr{O}_{Z}
.

To prove the converse, it suffices to show that every point
z_{0}=(b_{0},x_{0})\in Z

has a neighbourhood
U\subset Z
such that the restricted
bundle
E|U
admits
r=\mathrm{rank}\,E
linearly independent
continuous fibrewise holomorphic sections. Such
r
-tuples of
sections trivialise the bundle over
U
, and the transition maps
between such local frames are fibrewise holomorphic maps into
Gl_{r}(\mathbb{C})
.

To find such local frames, fix a point
(b_{0},x_{0})\in Z
and
smoothly bounded relatively compact domains

\Omega\Subset\Omega^{\prime}\Subset X
such that
\Omega
is a
contractible neighbourhood of
x_{0}
.
By
[
9
, Corollary 4.5]
there are a neighbourhood

B_{0}\subset B
of
b_{0}
and a continuous map

\Phi:B_{0}\times\Omega^{\prime}\to B_{0}\times X
of the form

(2.1)

\Phi(b,x)=(b,\Phi_{b}(x)),\quad b\in B_{0},\ x\in\Omega^{\prime}

such that
\Phi_{b_{0}}
is the identity on
\Omega^{\prime}
and

\Phi_{b}:\Omega^{\prime}\to\Phi_{b}(\Omega^{\prime})\subset X

is a
(J_{b},J_{b_{0}})
-biholomorphic
map in
\mathscr{C}^{(1,\alpha)}(\Omega^{\prime},X)
which is continuous
with respect to
b\in B_{0}
. Shrinking
B_{0}
around
b_{0}
,
we may assume that
\overline{\Omega}\subset\Phi_{b}(\Omega^{\prime})

for all
b\in\overline{B_{0}}
. The inverse map
\Psi=\Phi^{-1}
is a
homeomorphism defined on
\overline{B_{0}\times\Omega}
,
and
\Psi_{b}=\Psi(b,\cdotp)=\Phi_{b}^{-1}
is
(J_{b_{0}},J_{b})
-holomorphic
for every
b\in\overline{B_{0}}
. Hence, the vector bundle

E^{\prime}=\Psi^{*}(E)|\,\overline{B_{0}\times\Omega}\to\overline{B_{0}\times\Omega}
is fibrewise
holomorphic with respect to the complex structure
J_{b_{0}}
.
Since
\overline{\Omega}
is contractible, the restricted bundle

E^{\prime}_{b}=E^{\prime}|\{b\}\times\overline{\Omega}
is holomorphically trivial
with respect to
J_{b_{0}}
for every
b\in\overline{B}_{0}
.
Furthermore, by the stability theorem
of Leiterer
[
26
, Theorem 2.7, p. 68]
, shrinking

B_{0}
around
b_{0}
there is family
of
J_{b_{0}}
-holomorphic trivialisations depending continuously on

b\in B_{0}
. This gives a local frame for the bundle
E^{\prime}
on
a neighbourhood of
(b_{0},x_{0})

consisting of continuous fibrewise
J_{b_{0}}
-holomorphic sections.
Their pullbacks by the map
\Phi
(
2.1
) is a
local frame for
E
around
(b_{0},x_{0})
consisting of fibrewise
holomorphic sections with respect to the variable complex
structures
J_{b}
,
b\in B_{0}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Denote by
\mathrm{Pic}(Z)\cong H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{O}^{*})
the set of isomorphism
classes of fibrewise holomorphic line bundles on
Z=B\times X
.
We have the following Oka principle.

Theorem 2.3
.

(Assumptions as above.)
Every topological complex line bundle on
Z=B\times X

is isomorphic to a fibrewise holomorphic line bundle,
and any two fibrewise holomorphic line bundles on
Z

which are topologically isomorphic are also
isomorphic as fibrewise holomorphic line bundles.
Furthermore,
\mathrm{Pic}(Z)\cong H^{2}(Z,\mathbb{Z})
.

It follows in particular that if
B
is contractible then
\mathrm{Pic}(Z)=0
.

Proof.

The proof follows the standard argument for complex line
bundles on a Stein manifold, due to Oka

[
30
]
; see
[
8
, Theorem 5.2.2]
.
Let
\sigma(f)=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathfrak{i}f}
. Consider the following
commutative diagram whose rows are
exponential sheaf sequences and whose vertical arrows are
the natural inclusions:

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{*{9}c}0&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{Z}&\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow&\mathscr{O}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathscr{O}^{*}&\longrightarrow&1\\
&&\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow&&\!\!\big\downarrow&&\\
0&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{Z}&\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow&\mathscr{C}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathscr{C}^{*}&\longrightarrow&1\\
\end{array}

Since
\mathscr{C}
is a fine sheaf, we have
H^{q}(Z,\mathscr{C})=0
for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Proposition
1.5
we also have
H^{q}(Z,\mathscr{O})=0

for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, the relevant part of
the long exact sequence of cohomology groups associated
to the diagram (
2.2
) gives

\begin{array}[]{*{8}c}0&\longrightarrow&H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{O}^{*})&\longrightarrow&H^{2}(Z;\mathbb{Z})&\longrightarrow&0\\
&&\big\downarrow&&\big\|&&\\
0&\longrightarrow&H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{C}^{*})&\longrightarrow&H^{2}(Z;\mathbb{Z})&\ \,\longrightarrow&0\\
\end{array}

Thus, all arrows in the central square are isomorphisms.
Since
\mathrm{Pic}(Z)\cong H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{O}^{*})
and

H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{C}^{*})
is the set of isomorphisms classes
of topological line bundles on
Z
, the theorem follows.
∎

The Oka principle for maps from families of open Riemann surfaces
to Oka manifolds (see
[
9
, Theorem 1.6]
)
allows us to extend the first part of Theorem
2.3

to vector bundles of arbitrary rank by using the approach from
the classical Oka–Grauert theory. However, the assumptions
on the parameter space
B
must be more restrictive for
the cited result to imply. We state the following special case
when
B
is a CW complex and refer to the discussion preceding

[
9
, Theorem 1.6]
for more information.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that
B
is a finite CW complex or a countable locally
compact CW-complex of finite dimension,
X

is a smooth open surface, and
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is
a continuous family of complex structures on

X
of local Hölder class
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}
for some
0<\alpha<1
.
Then, every topological vector bundle on
B\times X
is isomorphic
to a fibrewise holomorphic vector bundle.

Proof.

A topological vector bundle
E\to B\times X
of rank
r

is the pullback
f^{*}\mathbb{U}

by a continuous map
f
from
B\times X
to a
Grassmannian
G=Gr(r,N)
(consisting of complex

r
-planes in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
) of the universal rank
r
vector bundle
\mathbb{U}\to G
.
(We take
N
big enough such that
E
embeds as a topological
vector subbundle of the trivial bundle
(B\times X)\times\mathbb{C}^{N}
;
this is possible since
B\times X
is paracompact.)
Since
G
is a complex homogeneous manifold, and hence an
Oka manifold by Grauert
[
12
]
,
the Oka principle in
[
9
, Theorem 1.6]

shows that
f
is homotopic to a fibrewise holomorphic map

F:B\times X\to G
(i.e., such that
F(b,\cdotp):X\to G
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic
for every
b\in B
). The pullback
F^{*}\mathbb{U}\to B\times X
is then a fibrewise
holomorphic vector bundle topologically isomorphic to
f^{*}\mathbb{U}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Under the assumptions in Theorem
2.4
,
it is likely the case that any two fibrewise holomorphic vector bundles on

B\times X
that are topologically isomorphic are also isomorphic
as fibrewise holomorphic vector bundles.
The idea is to follow the classical case of this result for a single
complex structure on a Stein manifold
X
, due to Grauert

[
13
]
; see also the expositions
by Cartan
[
5
]
, Leiterer
[
26
]
, and

[
8
, Theorem 5.3.1]
.
To complete the proof, we would need an Oka principle
for sections of fibrewise holomorphic principal bundles
on
B\times X
, thereby extending the Oka principle
for maps from
B\times X
to Oka manifolds
in
[
9
, Theorem 1.6]
.
Unfortunately I was unable to handle certain technical
issues concerning gluing of families of fibrewise holomorphic
sections in this situation, so this problem must be postponed.

Note that Mongodi and Tomassini
[
27
]

obtained the Oka principle for more general CR vector bundles
on certain real analytic Levi-flat submanifolds of complex
Euclidean spaces by reducing the problem to the
Oka–Grauert theorem
[
13
]
.
Our techniques use considerably less regularity.

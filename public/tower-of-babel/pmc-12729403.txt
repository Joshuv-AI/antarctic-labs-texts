MicroRNAs as the critical regulators of ubiquitin-proteasome system through F-box protein targeting during tumor progression
Authors: Iman Akhlaghipour, Meysam Moghbeli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) as a crucial mechanism for protein degradation is involved in regulation of cellular processes. Dysregulation of UPS can be associated with tumor progression by modulation of tumor suppressors and oncogenes. F-box proteins (FBPs) play a significant role in the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway (UPP) by targeting specific protein sequences and engaging in post-translational modifications, thus affecting various pathophysiological processes. MiRNAs regulate FBP functions, and their deregulation has been observed in numerous cancers. In the present review, we discussed the role of miRNAs in tumor progression by regulation of FBPs. FBXW7 is the main F-box protein that can be modulated by the miRNAs in tumor cells. It has been shown that miRNAs had mainly oncogenic functions in tumor cells by FBPs targeting. MiRNA/FBP axis can be introduced as a reliable diagnostic and therapeutic option in cancer patients.

Background

Ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) serves as a primary modulatory pathway for protein degradation and is essential for biological processes by targeting critical factors for degradation [1]. Given that the UPS is involved in the degradation of various tumor suppressors and oncogenes, any dysregulation within this pathway can be associated with tumor progression [2]. Protein ubiquitination involves a cascade of three enzymes, including the E3 ubiquitin ligases, the E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, and the E1 ubiquitin-activating enzyme [3]. E3 ubiquitin ligases are responsible for determining substrate specificity [4]. E3 ligases have been categorized into four main groups including PHD-finger-type, U-box-type, RING-type E3s, and HECT-type [5]. Skp1-Cul1-F-box (SCF) complex is a well-characterized E3 ligase in which F-box protein has a critical role in determining substrate specificity. SCF ligases mediate various biological conditions, such as gene expression, cell cycle progression, and inflammation [6]. The primary role of F-box proteins (FBPs) is in the ubiquitination-proteasome pathway (UPP), where they regulate degradation of proteins by ubiquitination [7, 8]. Sixty-eight F-box proteins have been recognized in human [9]. FBPs are also known to target specific short peptide sequences and engage in interactions with proteins through post-translational modifications of substrates, thereby participating in different pathophysiological processes [10]. Moreover, elevated expression levels of FBP members have been associated with unfavorable prognoses in various cancers [11, 12]. Therefore, molecular mechanisms of FBPs regulation can be a novel option for diagnostic and therapeutic purposes in cancer patients.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are non-coding RNAs that play crucial roles in modulation of mRNA degradation or translational inhibition [13–16]. They have key roles in various essential cellular and biological processes, such as cell differentiation, stem cell differentiation, cell death, and proliferation [17–19]. They are also involved in tumor progression by modulating oncogenes or tumor-suppressor genes [20–22]. MiRNAs are highly stable in body fluids, which make them suitable biomarkers for early diagnosis of malignancies [23]. They have key roles in modulation of F-box proteins and their subsequent cellular functions [24–29]. Deregulation of miRNA/FBP axis has been frequently observed in different cancers [30–36]. Therefore, we assessed the molecular mechanisms of miRNA/FBP axis to introduce that as a reliable diagnostic and therapeutic option in cancer patients (Table 1).

F-box and WD repeat domain-containing 7 (FBXW7)

FBXW7 is a component of the SCF ubiquitin ligase complex involved in regulating the degradation and ubiquitination of target proteins [37]. FBXW7 inhibits tumor progression by regulating the proteasome-mediated degradation of various oncogenic proteins such as Notch, mTOR, c-Myc, and Cyclin E [38–41]. FBXW7 has been also reported to be a functional target of several microRNAs during tumor progression (Fig. 1). Its deregulation has been observed in several cancer processes, such as epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), cell invasion, and proteasome-mediated oncoprotein degradation [42, 43]. It also has a tumor-suppressive role and its deregulation leads to tumor progression and poor prognosis [44–46]. MiR-27a has been recognized as an oncomiR in osteosarcoma that enhances invasive, proliferative, and EMT activities [47]. FBXW7 expression was notably lower in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) tissues than normal tissues that were associated with poorer OS outcomes. MiR-27a induced OSCC cell migration and proliferation by targeting the FBXW7/PI3K/AKT axis [48]. MiR-92a-3p enhanced RCC cell growth through the regulation of FBXW7 [49]. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) play significant roles in post-transcriptional mRNA modulation, transcriptional regulation, chromatin interactions, and epigenetic modifications [50, 51]. They function as competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs), which compete with miRNAs to enhance the expression of target genes [52]. There was TINCR down regulation in lung tumor cells and tissues. TINCR inhibited lung cancer cell invasion and proliferation through modulating the miR-544a/FBXW7 pathway [53]. Inhibition of miR-92a promoted NSCLC cell apoptosis (with diminished Bcl-2, and elevated caspase-3 and Bax expressions) while inhibited viability (with Ki-67 and PCNA down regulations) through FBXW7 targeting [54]. There was miR-367 up regulation in NSCLC cells and tissues that was significantly correlated with poor prognosis. MiR-367 induced NSCLC cell invasion while inhibited apoptosis via FBXW7 targeting [55]. MiR-92a was up regulated in osteosarcoma cells and tissues that were significantly correlated with poor prognosis. MiR-92a induced osteosarcoma cell cycle progression and growth while inhibited apoptosis via FBXW7 inhibition [56]. There was significant MT1JP down regulation in gastric cancer (GC) tissues compared to normal tissues, which was associated with lymph node metastasis and higher stages. MT1JP inhibited GC cell growth and invasion while promoted cell death by modulating FBXW7 through competing with miR-92a-3p [57]. Circ_0000094 has been demonstrated to inhibit T-ALL development by modulating the miR-223-3p/FBXW7 axis [58]. CircKL reduced renal tumor cell metastasis and growth via regulation of miR-182-5p/FBXW7 axis [59]. TTN-AS1 inhibited ovarian tumor cell growth through miR-15b-5p/FBXW7 axis [60]. There was LINC00173 down regulation in cervical cancer cases, which was correlated with poor prognosis. LINC00173 suppressed cervical tumor cell invasion and proliferation through mediating the miR-182-5p/FBXW7 axis [61].Fig. 1miRNAs have important roles in regulation of FBXW7 during tumor progression. Chemo resistance is a complex and multifactorial phenomenon that encompasses the abnormal expression of microRNAs, reactive oxygen species (ROS), efflux by ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters, and deregulated autophagy and signaling pathways [62–65]. Autophagy is a lysosomal-mediated degradation mechanism responsible for the elimination of cytoplasmic organelles and components [66]. Autophagy exhibits divergent roles even as an oncogene or tumor suppressor across various cell types [67]. For instance, it can enhance tumor cell proliferation and survival by enabling them to cope with environmental and intracellular stresses, including nutrient deprivation, hypoxia, and cancer treatments [68]. Aberrant activation of autophagy plays a significant role in the development of chemo resistance [69, 70]. Aberrant expressions of miRNAs are also involved in chemo resistance through modulation of autophagy-associated factors and genes [71–73]. Therefore, miRNA-mediated autophagy has emerged as a potentially effective approach to mitigate chemo resistance in tumor cells [74]. MiR-25 promoted hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cell autophagy and sorafenib resistance through its targeting FBXW7 [75]. Circ_0001306 knockdown induced HCC cell invasion and proliferation through the activation of the miR-527/FBXW7 pathway [76]. It has been indicated that miR-223 enhanced NSCLC cell autophagy and cisplatin resistance by FBXW7 targeting [77]. Cisplatin (DDP) is a chemotherapeutic agent known for its DNA-damaging properties that is utilized in the systemic treatment of various tumor types [78, 79]. MiR-223 has been identified to decrease DDP-induced apoptosis and affect G1/S cell cycle transition in GC cells through FBXW7 targeting [80]. Gefitinib and erlotinib are effective epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs) utilized in treatment of cancer patients exhibiting elevated levels of EGFR expression or specific EGFR mutations [81–83]. There are various signaling pathways involved in chemo-resistance of NSCLC, such as Notch-1, Wnt, TGF-β, PI3K/Akt, and JAK/STAT [84–89]. Notch-1 is involved in acquired gefitinib resistance via the acquisition of the EMT phenotype. Inhibition of Notch-1 reverses EMT process and reduces cell proliferation, invasion, and drug resistance [85, 90]. There was miR-223 up regulation in the erlotinib-resistant NSCLC cells. MiR-223 induced resistance of NSCLC cells to erlotinib by FBXW7 down regulation. Inhibition of Notch and Akt pathways enhanced NSCLC cell sensitivity to erlotinib via regulation of the miR-223/FBXW7 axis [91]. Sorafenib is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) that inhibits the RAF serine/threonine kinases, PDGFR, VEGFR, and Fms-like tyrosine kinase 3 [92]. Several mechanisms contribute to acquired Sorafenib resistance, including autophagy, epigenetic modifications, EMT, and the PI3K/AKT pathway [93, 94]. It has been demonstrated that miR-223 was up regulated in HCC cells exhibiting sorafenib resistance. Knockdown of miR-223 significantly enhanced sensitivity to sorafenib via FBXW7 up regulation, while Notch-1, c-Myc, and c-Jun down regulations in HCC cells [95]. Radiation therapy, surgical resection, and temozolomide (TMZ) are considered as the standard treatments of Glioblastoma [96]. TMZ is an oral chemotherapeutic drug that alkylates DNA to disrupt cell cycle progression at the G2/M phase and induce apoptosis. Development of acquired resistance to TMZ is frequently observed in glioblastoma patients and serves as a marker of unfavorable prognosis. The exosomal transfer of miR-25-3p has been investigated to induce glioblastoma cell proliferation and TMZ resistance by suppressing FBXW7 and indirectly enhancing the expression of oncoproteins, Notch proteins constitute a family of single-pass transmembrane heterodimeric ligand-activated receptors that play a significant role in cell growth, differentiation, and cell fate [98–100]. It exerts its effects via interactions with other signaling pathways, including NF-κB and Akt/mTOR signaling [101, 102]. Notch intracellular domain (NICD) which is a FBXW7 target functions as a transcriptional activator that positively influences the expression of genes associated with the promotion of cell proliferation [103]. It has been revealed that miR-223 was upregulated in CRC specimens. MiR-223-induced suppression of FBXW7 promoted CRC cell proliferation while inhibited apoptosis through regulating cyclin E, Akt/mTOR, and Notch pathways [104]. NF-κB signaling serves as a principal mediator of Notch-induced malignant transformation of T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL) [103–106]. There was a positive feedback loop of Notch/miR-223/FBXW7 in T-ALL cells. NF-κB and Notch interactions enhanced miR-223 gene expression, which subsequently suppressed FBXW7 and its negative regulatory effect on Notch signaling. Notch3, Notch1, and p65 were directly recruited to the miR-223 promoter region and progressively regulated its expression in T-ALL. Inhibition of miR-223 also decreased γ-secretase inhibitor (GSI) resistance in T-ALL cells [107].

The c-Myc was initially identified as the cellular homolog of the retroviral oncogene v-Myc [108, 109]. It regulates numerous cellular activities that influence cell metabolism, differentiation, and growth [110, 111]. C-Myc is activated in more than 50% of human cancers [112]. Various mechanisms contribute to the activation of c-Myc during carcinogenesis, including point mutations [113, 114], gene amplification [115], and chromosomal rearrangements [116, 117]. C-Myc stability is modulated by several E3 ubiquitin ligases [118], and the SCF-Fbxw7 complex is a well-recognized E3 ligase associated with c-Myc. Degradation of c-Myc is regulated by SCF-Fbxw7 [39, 119]. It has been indicated that lncRNA-MIF suppressed tumor cell glycolysis and progression through down regulation of miR-586 and subsequent promotion of Fbxw7-mediated c-Myc degradation [120]. Aurora kinase A is a serine/threonine kinase that mediates mitotic processes, including spindle assembly, centrosome maturation, and the initiation of mitosis through the phosphorylation of associated proteins, such as Ndel1 and Tacc [121–123]. It is also involved in the protein translation through the phosphorylation of CPEB [124, 125]. Prostate adenocarcinoma and prostatic small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (SCNC) are two histopathological types of prostate cancer. Prostate adenocarcinomas also contain scattered neuroendocrine (NE) cells that are usually quiescent [126, 127]. SCNC consists of NE tumor cells that have high aggressiveness. It has been demonstrated that p53 mutation-induced Aurora kinase A and MYC expression promoted prostatic SCNC cell aggressiveness via activating the miR-25/FBXW7 signaling [128]. FER1L4 reduced prostate cancer progression through miR-92a-3p down regulation and FBXW7 up regulation [129]. There was miR-367 up regulation in NSCLC cells and tissues that was associated with poorer prognosis. MiR-367 induced NSCLC cell cycle progression and growth while decreased apoptosis via the regulation of FBXW7 and its downstream targets c-Jun and c-Myc [130]. There was miR-24 up regulation in tongue squamous cell carcinoma (TSCC) specimens that was correlated with reduced patient overall survival. MiR-24 suppression inhibited TSCC cell invasion via the regulation of FBXW7 and its downstream targets such as c-Jun and c-Myc [131]. Elevated expressions of miR-92a in HCC samples were correlated with reduced patient survival and worse clinicopathological characteristics. MiR-92a impeded HCC cell apoptosis while induced cell growth through down regulating FBXW7 and up regulating cyclin E and c-Myc [132]. Up-regulation of miR-27b in HCC specimens was markedly correlated with decreased patient survival. MiR-27b induced HCC cell proliferation and growth while prevented apoptosis by Fbxw7 targeting [133]. There was significant miR-103a-3p up regulation in cervical cancer cells and tissues that was linked to distant metastasis, FIGO stage, advanced histological grades, and poor overall survival. Knockdown of miR-103a-3p inhibited cervical cancer progression by up regulation of FBXW7, and subsequent c-Myc and cyclin E down regulations [134]. There was significant miR-223 up regulation in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) samples compared to normal tissues that was correlated with poor prognosis. MiR-223 promoted ESCC progression through FBXW7 down regulation and subsequent c-Jun and c-Myc accumulation [135].

EMT process is involved in the regulation of inflammatory responses, embryonic development, tissue regeneration, and tumor invasion, and chemo-resistance [136, 137]. EMT encompasses a series of intricate and reversible processes that lead to the acquisition of a mesenchymal phenotype and a concomitant adhesion loss of epithelial cell [138, 139]. F-box proteins have been implicated in EMT through the regulation of transcription factors and modulation of its inducers [140, 141]. MiR-233 decreased doxorubicin sensitivity of NSCLC cells while induced EMT through FBXW7 targeting. Knockdown of Twist also inhibited EMT process via E-cadherin up-regulation and Vimentin down-regulation in NSCLC cells [142]. There was elevated expression of miR-27a in breast tumor cells and tissues. MiR-27a enhanced EMT process and tumor cell migration through FBXW7 suppression. MiR-27a inhibition significantly down regulated ZEB1 and Snail, and their downstream mesenchymal markers vimentin and N-cadherin while up regulated the E-cadherin in breast tumor cells [143]. MiR-223 enhanced doxorubicin resistance and EMT process via FBXW7 and CDH1 down-regulations and Vimentin up-regulation in CRC cells [144]. There was CASC2 down regulation in HCC cells and tissues that was associated with tumor recurrence and aggressiveness. CASC2 reduced HCC cell migration and EMT process through miR-367/FBXW7 axis in HCC cells [145]. Krüeppel-like factor 5 (KLF5) is a pivotal transcription factor participated in the regulation of EMT process and tumor metastasis [146, 147]. It has been reported that cholesterol accumulation enhanced ccRCC cell invasion, proliferation, and migration through mediating the KLF5/miR-27a/FBXW7 axis [148]. There was miR-223-3p up regulation in breast cancer samples that was correlated with poor prognosis. MiR-223-3p promoted EMT process via FBXW7 targeting in breast tumor cells [149]. CircFBXW7 down regulation was associated with lymphatic node metastasis, larger tumoral size, and worse clinical outcomes in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) patients. CircFBXW7 impeded TNBC cell growth and metastasis via modulation of miR-197-3p/FBXW7 axis [150].

Cancer stem cells (CSCs) are a subset of tumor cells with stem cell-like traits that are crucial for distant metastasis, tumor initiation, and resistance to radiotherapy and chemotherapy [151]. MiR-19b has been implicated in promoting CRC cell stemness characteristics and radio-resistance by FBXW7 targeting and triggering the Wnt/β-catenin pathway [152]. There was miR-500a-3p up regulation in GC samples that was closely associated with tumor recurrence and cisplatin resistance. MiR-500a-3p promoted cisplatin resistance and CSC properties by FBXW7 targeting in GC cells [153]. Activation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling has been indicated to induce HCC cell proliferation via up-regulation of miR-770 and subsequent FBXW7 targeting [154]. Heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein K (hnRNPK) is a critical RNA and DNA-binding protein that plays a pivotal role in gene expression and signal transduction processes, including RNA stability, RNA splicing, DNA transcription, and translation [155]. There was up regulation of miR-223 in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) cells and tissues, which was correlated with worse prognosis. GSK3-mediated hnRNPK phosphorylation promoted PDAC cell invasion and growth through the regulation of miR-223/FBXW7 axis [156]. E2F6-mediated MIR22HG down regulation enhanced laryngocarcinoma cell growth and migration through the miR-5000-3p/FBXW7 axis [157].

F-box and WD repeat domain containing 8 (FBXW8) interacts with cullin 7, ring-box 1, and S-phase kinase-associated protein 1 to form an E3 ligase complex [158, 159]. FBXW8 has been also reported to be a functional target of several microRNAs during tumor progression (Fig. 2). FBXW8 interacts with insulin receptor substrate 1 (IRS-1) as a crucial mediator in insulin and IGF-1 signaling pathways to regulate cell growth and senescence [160]. Cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) are involved in the cell cycle [161, 162]. The activation of CDK2 and CDK1 is primarily linked to cyclin B1 and A during the G2 to M phases of cell cycle. CDC2 and Cyclin B1 play pivotal functions in the initiation of the M phase [163]. Consequently, the p27 negatively regulates progression of the cell cycle via inhibiting cyclin/CDK2 complexes [168, 169]. FBXW11 (also known as β-TrCP2) is involved in cellular development, differentiation, cell cycle, and metabolism through ubiquitinating its phosphorylated substrates such as ATF4 and β-catenin [171, 172]. FBXW11 is a functional target of several microRNAs during tumor progression (Fig. 2). However, β-TrCP1 directly interacts with the β-catenin’s N-terminus to suppress Wnt/β-catenin signaling, implying that β-TrCP1 functions as a dynamic regulator of Wnt signaling in tumorigenesis [174]. Deregulation of FBXW11 has been implicated in carcinogenesis through regulating various signaling pathways. For instance, FBXW11 has critical roles in modulating the IκB-dependent apoptotic pathway in colorectal cancer and melanoma [176]. FBXW11 enhanced lymphocytic leukemia cell cycle development and carcinogenesis by activating β-catenin/TCF and NF-κB signaling [177]. PCGEM1 promoted cervical cancer cell growth and invasion by controlling the miR-182/FBXW11 axis and its downstream pathways β-catenin/TCF and NF-κB [178]. There was significant miR-182 up regulation in NSCLC tissues compared to non-tumoral tissues. F-box and leucine-rich repeat protein family

MicroRNAs have key roles in regulation of F-box and leucine-rich repeat (FBXL) protein family during tumor progression (Fig. 3). FBXL2 is a component of the SCF ubiquitin ligase complex that functions as a tumor suppressor in several malignancies [180, 181]. It has been demonstrated that FBXL2 can impede tumorigenesis by ubiquitinating and targeting different oncoproteins. For instance, FBXL2 functions as a tumor suppressor in lung cancer by inducing cell cycle arrest through the ubiquitin-mediated degradation of cyclin D3 [180]. FoxM1 is a transcription factor that contributes to tumor progression through promoting β-catenin nuclear accumulation [182]. It has been revealed that FoxM1 is a target of FBXL2 in gastric tumor cells [183]. Cyclin E-CDK2 and CDK4/6-cyclin D complexes regulate G1-S transition while CDK1-cyclin B complex is involved in G2-M transition during cell cycle progression [184]. MiR-346-5p has been indicated to promote CRC cell proliferation through the regulation of the FBXL2/FoxM1/β-catenin cascade. MiR-346-5p also induced CRC cell proliferation through up regulation of cyclins and CDK2 while p27 and p21 down regulations [185]. CircSMARCA5 suppressed gastric tumor cell invasion and proliferation by modulating the miR-346/FBXL2 axis [186]. FBXL7 is involved in Aurora kinase A degradation, thus regulates segregation of chromosomes and formation of mitotic spindles [187, 188]. Temozolomide (TMZ) acts as an alkylating agent that induces DNA methylation at the O6 position of guanine, resulting in improper pairing of thymine with O6-methylguanine [189]. This mispairing activates the mismatch repair system, leading to DNA double-strand breaks, cell cycle arrest, and apoptosis [190]. MiR-152-5p has been found to inhibit glioma cell invasion and proliferation while enhanced TMZ sensitivity by FBXL7 targeting [191]. Catabolic glycolysis/oxidative phosphorylation and anabolic gluconeogenesis are primary pathways in the maintenance of glucose homeostasis [192]. Glucose is initially converted to pyruvate in the cytoplasm of normal cells, subsequently leading to ATP production via the tricarboxylic acid cycle in the mitochondria [193]. However, tumor cells preferentially convert pyruvate to lactate in aerobic glycolysis [193]. Heightened rates of lactate production and aerobic glycolysis are not only characteristic metabolic traits of tumor cells but also serve as indicators of metastases [194]. MiR-181d promoted aerobic glycolysis, metastasis, and proliferation of CRC cells through protecting c-Myc from CRY2- and FBXL3-dependent degradation. Autophagy is involved in various pathological and physiological activities, such as degradation of proteins and organelles, cell survival, intracellular homeostasis, and tumorigenesis [66, 196]. Beclin 1 serves as a critical modulator of autophagosome formation and autophagy initiation. It has been demonstrated that approximately 40–75% of ovarian cancer (OC) tissues exhibit monoallelic deletion of Beclin, which results in a reduction in Beclin-1 expression and diminished autophagy levels [197]. There was an up regulation of circRNF144B in OC specimens that was associated with a worse prognosis. CircRNF144B promoted OC progression while reduced autophagy through modulation of the miR-342-3p/FBXL11/Beclin-1 axis [198]. FBXL20 is a FBP family member that has been identified as an oncogene in different malignancies [199]. FBXL20 has oncogenic effects on colorectal adenocarcinoma cell invasion, migration, and adhesion through E-cadherin targeting [199]. There was significant FBXL20 up regulation in OC tissues compared to adjacent non-cancerous tissues that was associated with a poor prognosis. MicroRNAs have key roles in regulation of FBXO protein family during tumor progression (Fig. 4). FBXO11 is an integral component of SCF complex that inhibits p53 transcriptional activity through promoting its ubiquitin-like modification [201]. FBXO11 also induces EMT process and tumor cell metastasis via PI3K/AKT pathway [202]. FBXO11 is involved in the proteasomal degradation and ubiquitination of SNAIL during tumor progression [203, 204]. FBXO11 has also tumor-suppressive properties by inhibiting the synthesis of hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α) [205]. There were significant LINC01436 up regulations in GC specimens that were associated with increased T stage, lymph node metastasis, adverse prognosis, and larger tumor size. There was FBXO11 up regulation in HCC tissues that was associated with advanced disease stages, venous infiltration, larger tumor size, and decreased survival rates. F-box proteins are categorized into three families based on the presence of distinct domains beyond the F-box domain: the FBXL group, which contains leucine-rich repeats; the FBXW group, characterized by multiple WD40 repeats; and the FBXO group, which encompasses proteins with other types of domains. FBXO31 is classified within the FBXO group [9, 209]. FBXO31 has a pivotal role in the assembly of the SCF ubiquitin E3 ligase complex that regulates the cell cycle progression, mitotic fidelity, signal transduction, and centrosome stability [210–212]. It is implicated in DNA damage response [213, 214], neuronal development [215], and tumorigenesis [216, 217]. It has been reported that FBXO31 was down regulated in GC specimens that was significantly correlated with advanced clinical stages, lower survival rates, and poorer prognosis. MiR-20a and miR-17 induced gastric tumor cell proliferation through suppression of growth-inhibitory effects of FBXO31 at G1 phase [218]. FBXO31 has been also indicated to inhibit EMT by facilitating the proteasomal degradation of Snail1 [219]. There was significant miR-210 down regulation in ESCC that was associated with tumor aggressiveness. MiR-210 impeded ESCC cell migration and EMT by inhibiting FBXO31 [220]. The 5-FU and CDDP-based regimens are crucial for multimodal cancer treatment. However, resistance to these conventional chemotherapeutic drugs is a significant challenge in cancer therapy [221, 222]. It has been indicated that the expression levels of miR-29c were markedly reduced in primary ESCC tumor tissues compared to corresponding non-tumor tissues, and elevated tumor miR-29c expression was associated with improved survival outcomes. MiR-29c enhanced the sensitivity of ESCC cells to 5-FU by modulating FBXO31-p38 axis [223]. There was also significant miR-19a-3p up regulation in multiple myeloma specimens. MiR-19a-3p inhibited multiple myeloma cell apoptosis via FBXO32 targeting [224].

FBXO22 is an F-box E3 ligase that is up regulated in different malignancies [225–228]. FBXO22 knockdown impedes melanoma cell invasion and angiogenesis through the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling [227]. FBXO22 induces the HCC progression by ubiquitinating and degrading p21 [228]. Moreover, upregulation of FBXO22 has been revealed to boost colony formation, cell proliferation, and xenograft tumorigenicity in primary breast tumors [229]. It has been reported that SNHG14 was notably up regulated in osteosarcoma cells and tissues. SNHG14 promoted osteosarcoma cell invasion and proliferation through regulation of miR-433-3p/FBXO22 axis [225]. Runt-related transcription factor 3 (RUNX3) is a transcription factor involved in the progression of different malignancies [230, 231]. There was circDYRK1A down regulation in GC specimens, which was correlated with poor prognosis. CircDYRK1A significantly inhibited GC cell growth, invasion, and glutamine metabolism through the RUNX3/miR-889-3p/FBXO4 axis [232]. GABPB1-AS1 reduced NSCLC cell invasion and proliferation through the modulation of miR-566/FBXO47 axis [233]. FBXO45 is classified as a non-typical E3 ubiquitin ligase that plays a role in inhibiting proteolysis and mediating ubiquitin-dependent proteasomal degradation [234, 235]. FBXO45 has been observed to facilitate new blood vessel formation, and supply essential nutrients for tumor cell proliferation [234, 235]. MiR-30a-5p inhibited lung tumor cell proliferation by FBXO45 targeting [236].

Conclusions

Ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) regulates crucial biological processes and tumor progression. E3 ubiquitin ligases, specifically SCF complexes, determine substrate specificity and are involved in cellular functions. F-box proteins (FBPs) as the crucial factors in ubiquitination are associated with cancer prognosis. In the present review, we discussed the role of miRNAs in regulation of UPS by F-box protein targeting during tumor progression. The relationship between miRNAs and F-box proteins represents a complex regulatory network where miRNAs post-transcriptionally regulate F-box protein expression, while F-box proteins conversely influence miRNA activity through degradation of transcription factors and other regulators. It has been reported that, FBXW7 is the main F-box protein that can be modulated by the miRNAs in tumor cells. Majority of miRNAs had oncogenic roles by F-box protein targeting during tumor progression. Therefore, miRNA/FBP axis can be a reliable diagnostic and therapeutic option in cancer therapy. The inhibition of oncogenic miRNAs (oncomiRs) that target tumor-suppressive F-box proteins represents a promising therapeutic strategy. AntagomiRs, miRNA sponges, and small molecule inhibitors have been developed to specifically inhibit overexpressed miRNAs in cancer. Despite the promising potential of targeting the miRNA/FBP axis, several significant challenges should be addressed. Delivery efficiency remains a major hurdle for miRNA-based therapeutics. Ensuring sufficient uptake of miRNA inhibitors or mimics by tumor cells while minimizing off-target effects requires advanced delivery systems with improved tumor specificity and bioavailability. Off-target effects pose another challenge, as miRNAs typically regulate multiple genes. Comprehensive understanding of miRNA target networks and improved bioinformatics predictions are needed to design specific therapeutics with minimal unintended consequences. Tumor heterogeneity can lead to variable treatment responses, as different sub clones within a tumor may have distinct miRNA and F-box protein expression patterns. Single-cell multi-omics technologies can clarify tumor type-specific regulation patterns of F-box proteins and their regulatory miRNAs. These approaches could reveal heterogeneity in miRNA/FBP signaling within tumors and identify rare subpopulations with distinct dependencies on specific regulatory nodes. However, further animal studies and clinical trials are required to use miRNA/FBP axis as a therapeutic and diagnostic target in clinics. Longitudinal clinical studies tracking changes in miRNA and F-box protein expression during treatment and disease progression could identify dynamic biomarkers of response and resistance, facilitating treatment adaptation and personalization. Specific miRNA/FBP signatures offer promise as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for various cancer types, with potential applications in non-invasive liquid biopsies.

The clinical translation of miRNA/FBP axis faces several methodological and conceptual limitations. First, the substrate recognition patterns for many F-box proteins remain incompletely characterized, with more than 70% of F-box proteins lacking a complete substrate lineage map. This knowledge gap hinders our complete understanding of how miRNA-mediated regulation of F-box proteins impacts oncogenesis. Second, most research has focused on K48-linked ubiquitination that targets substrates for proteasomal degradation. However, emerging evidence indicates that F-box proteins can also mediate other ubiquitination types (e.g., K27-linked chains by FBXO32) that stabilize substrates or alter their function rather than promoting degradation [237]. The biological significance of these non-degradative ubiquitination modes in cancer progression requires further investigation. Third, the regulatory dimensions of F-box proteins in the tumor immune microenvironment are underestimated. The mechanisms by which F-box proteins mediate immune escape through reshaping immune cell metabolism (e.g., T-cell mitochondrial function) or modulating immune checkpoints (e.g., PD-L1/CTLA-4) [238]. Dynamic models of the immune microenvironment can systematically analyze new mechanisms through which F-box proteins regulate anti-tumor immunity via metabolic reprogramming and immune checkpoint.

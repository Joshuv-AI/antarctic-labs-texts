Depth of free-by-cyclic groups
Spencer Dowdall, Yassine Guerch, Radhika Gupta

Abstract
For a free group automorphism, we prove that its poset of attracting lamination orbits is a canonical invariant of the associated mapping torus. That is, if a free-by-cyclic group splits as a mapping torus in two different ways, then the corresponding automorphisms have isomorphic posets of lamination orbits. Further, we show that the lamination depth, the size of the largest chain in this poset, is a commensurability invariant of the free-by-cyclic group.



1
Introduction

Every automorphism
\Phi
of a finitely generated free group
\mathbb{F}
gives rise to a
mapping torus extension
of
\mathbb{F}
by
\mathbb{Z}
defined by the presentation

M(\Phi)\coloneq\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{Z}=\langle\mathbb{F},t\mid tgt^{-1}=\Phi(g)\text{ for all }g\in\mathbb{F}\rangle.

Up to isomorphism, this group depends only on the outer class
\phi\coloneq[\Phi]\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
of the monodromy/automorphism, so we often write
M(\phi)
or
\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
instead.
A group
\mathbb{G}
is
free-by-cyclic
if it is isomorphic to such an extension
M(\phi)
for some
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
with nontrivial
\mathbb{F}
. However, it is well known that
\phi
is not uniquely determined by
\mathbb{G}
;
indeed, there may be infinitely many distinct outer automorphisms
\psi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F}^{\prime})
– even of free groups
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}
of distinct ranks! – for which
M(\phi)\cong\mathbb{G}\cong M(\psi)
. Thus, in principle,
\mathbb{G}
loses information about
\phi
.
It is natural to ask what features of the outer automorphism
\phi
are in fact group invariants of the associated free-by-cyclic group
\mathbb{G}\cong M(\phi)
, or even stronger, commensurability or quasi-isometry invariants of
\mathbb{G}
.

For the introduction,
\mathbb{F}
is a finitely generated nontrivial free group and
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
.
By the preceding remarks, the rank of
\mathbb{F}
is not a group invariant of
M(\phi)
since it is possible that
M(\phi)\cong M(\psi)
for some other
\psi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F}^{\prime})
with
\operatorname{rank}(\mathbb{F})\neq\operatorname{rank}(\mathbb{F}^{\prime})
.
In contrast, one can show using
[
MSW03
,
Pap07
]
that
\phi
having finite order in
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
is a quasi-isometry invariant of
M(\phi)
while the precise order of
\phi
is not even a group invariant.
As another example, recent work
[
Mut24c
,
Mac00
,
GL04
,
Gho23
,
DL22
]
shows that
\phi
being
polynomially growing
(see §
2.7
), and moreover the degree of polynomial growth, is a quasi-isometry invariant of
M(\phi)
.
At the other extreme,
\phi
being
atoroidal
(that is, having no periodic conjugacy classes of nontrivial elements) is also a quasi-isometry invariant since Brinkmann proved this is equivalent to the group
M(\phi)
being Gromov hyperbolic
[
Bri00
]
. Finally, recall that
\phi
is
irreducible
if it does not preserve a proper free factor system of
\mathbb{F}
. Using dynamical techniques, it was first shown in
[
DKL15
]
that when
\phi
is both irreducible and atoroidal, then the same holds for many other splittings of
M(\phi)
(for example, those in the same component of the symmetrized BNS-invariant). It was later proven in full generality
[
Mut21
]
that irreducibility of
\phi
is a group
invariant of
M(\phi)
.

In their work proving the Tits Alternative for
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
, Bestvina–Feighn–Handel
[
BFH00
]
showed that every outer automorphism
\phi
of
\mathbb{F}
has a canonically defined (and possibly empty) finite set
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
of
attracting laminations
, each of which is an
\mathbb{F}
–invariant closed subset of the double boundary
\partial^{2}\mathbb{F}
(see §
2.8
for details). These laminations encode essential dynamical information about the automorphism and, since their introduction over two decades ago, have become fundamental tools in many facets of the theory of
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
.

One might hope that the cardinality of
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
is an invariant of the free-by-cyclic group
M(\phi)
. This guess is motivated by prior results in two important special cases: Firstly, the set
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
is empty precisely when
\phi
is
polynomially growing
, which we have seen is a quasi-isometry invariant. Thus
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
being empty is a quasi-isometry invariant.
Secondly,
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
consists of a single lamination whenever
\phi
is irreducible.
Since irreducibility is a group invariant, it follows that
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\psi)
also has cardinality
1
for all outer automorphisms
\psi
with
M(\psi)\cong M(\phi)
. However, this does not prove cardinality
1
is an invariant as there are also reducible automorphisms
\phi
for which
\left|\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)\right|=1
.

It turns out that the cardinality of
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
is not the right quantity. For example, by considering automorphisms arising from homeomorphisms of punctured surfaces,
one may find examples
M(\phi)\cong M(\psi)
where, say,
|\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)|>|\mathcal{L}^{+}(\psi)|=1
.
The full story is more interesting and involved.

The lamination set
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
has two additional structures. For the first structure, attracting laminations may be properly nested inside each other (as subsets of
\partial^{2}\mathbb{F}
), making
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
a partially ordered set. By the
(lamination) depth
of
\phi
, we mean the length
\delta(\phi)
of the longest chain in the poset
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
. Our original goal for this project was to determine whether the depth
\delta(\phi)
is a group invariant of
M(\phi)
, which was posed as a question in
[
Mut24c
]
.
Our first main result (
5.6
) answers this in the affirmative, and we deduce a stronger statement.

Corollary 1

(
5.7
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finitely generated nontrivial free group and
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
.
The depth
\delta(\phi)
of the outer automorphism
\phi
is a commensurability invariant of the free-by-cyclic group
M(\phi)=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
.
That is, if
\psi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F}^{\prime})
is any outer automorphism for which
M(\phi)
and
M(\psi)
are commensurable (i.e. admit finite index subgroups that are isomorphic), then
\delta(\phi)=\delta(\psi)
.

Since commensurable groups are quasi-isometric, this provides evidence for the conjecture posed in
[
Mut24c
, Conjecture 5.5]
that depth is a quasi-isometry invariant of the mapping torus.

The second additional structure is that the lamination set
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
is invariant under the action of
\phi
on the double boundary
\partial^{2}\mathbb{F}
. As this action preserves nesting, the partial order descends to the quotient, turning the set
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
of
\phi
-orbits of attracting laminations into a canonical poset associated to the automorphism
\phi
. Note that the
\phi
-orbits of attracting laminations are in one-to-one correspondence with the exponentially growing strata in any relative train track representative of
\phi

[
BFH00
, Lemma 3.1.13]
.
The length of the longest chain in
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
is also the length of the longest chain in
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
, which is a group invariant by the first result.
We must work a bit harder to show the group invariance of this poset of orbits:

Theorem 1

(
6.3
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finitely generated nontrivial free group and
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
.
The poset
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
of lamination orbits of the outer automorphism
\phi
is a group invariant of the free-by-cyclic group
M(\phi)=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
: For any outer automorphism
\psi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F}^{\prime})
, any isomorphism
M(\phi)\to M(\psi)
induces an order-isomorphism
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}\to\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\psi}
.

We remark that, in contrast to the depth
\delta(\phi)
, the poset
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
associated to
\phi
is not a commensurability invariant of the free-by-cyclic group
M(\phi)
. Indeed, this can be seen by
passing to a power of
\phi
and the corresponding mapping torus extension.

In addition to the depth
\delta(\phi)
, we define the
(lamination) depth spectrum
of
\phi
, denoted
\delta\mathcal{S}(\phi)
, to be the set consisting of the lengths of maximal chains in
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
(equivalently
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
) recorded without multiplicity. As with depth, we obtain invariance of the depth spectrum:

Corollary 2

(
6.4
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finitely generated nontrivial free group and
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
.
The depth spectrum
\delta\mathcal{S}(\phi)
of the outer automorphism
\phi
is a commensurability invariant of the free-by-cyclic group
\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
.

1.1
Contrasting the 3-manifold setting

Much of the theory of free group automorphisms is motivated by parallels with surface homeomorphisms, and the same is true for the results of this paper.
To add context and give a better geometric appreciation of the lamination poset,
let us now briefly describe how the story plays out in the surface setting.

For an oriented compact surface
S
with negative Euler characteristic, the Nielsen–Thurston classification states that every orientation preserving homeomorphism
f\colon S\to S
is isotopic to a homeomorphism that is either finite-order, reducible, or pseudo-Anosov. Recall that
reducible
means the map setwise preserves a collection, termed a
reducing system
, of disjoint essential simple closed curves in
S
. On the other hand,
pseudo-Anosov
means there exists a constant
\lambda>1
and a pair
(\Lambda_{\pm},\mu_{\pm})
of
attracting
and
repelling
arational measured (singular) foliations of
S
such that

f(\Lambda_{\pm},\mu_{\pm})=(\Lambda_{\pm},\lambda^{\pm 1}\mu_{\pm})
.

It follows from the classification that, up to isotopy, every homeomorphism
f
has a canonical
Thurston normal form
with the following structure: There is a canonical finite (possibly empty) reducing system
\mathcal{C}
so that,
for each component
\Sigma
of
S\setminus\mathcal{C}
with an
f
-orbit of size
k\geq 1
, the restriction
f^{k}|{\Sigma}\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
is either finite order or pseudo-Anosov;
\Sigma
is called a finite-order or pseudo-Anosov piece of
f
accordingly.
Since each pseudo-Anosov piece comes with its own attracting measured foliation (for the map
f^{k}|\Sigma
), we see that every homeomorphism
f
has a canonical finite (possibly empty) set
\mathcal{L}^{+}(f)
of attracting measured foliations: one for each pseudo-Anosov piece in the Thurston normal form. These are the analogs of, and were indeed the motivation for, the attracting laminations of a free group automorphism.

Thurston’s geometrization conjecture (now proven by Perelman) posits that every compact oriented
3
-manifold can be canonically decomposed into geometric pieces. In the special case that
M
fibers over the circle with compact fibers of negative Euler characteristic,
this decomposition follows from Thurston’s hyperbolization theorem and has the following structure:
Such an
M
can be cut along a canonical finite (possibly empty) collection of essential tori and Klein bottles so that each complementary component is either: Seifert fibered and admits a complete
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
-structure of finite volume; or atoroidal and admits a complete
\mathbb{H}^{3}
-structure of finite volume.

While this decomposition is intrinsic to the manifold
M
, it can also be read off from the dynamics of
any
monodromy as follows:

To say that
M
is fibered means it is homeomorphic to the mapping torus
M_{f}
of a surface homeomorphism
f\colon S\to S
. This surface homeomorphism has its Thurston normal form—the canonical reduction system
\mathcal{C}
that decomposes
f
into finite-order and pseudo-Anosov pieces. Each
f
-orbit of curves in the reduction system
\mathcal{C}
gives rise to an essential torus or Klein bottle in the mapping torus
M_{f}
. After cutting
M_{f}
along the resulting collection
\mathcal{T}
of tori or Klein bottles, the complementary components exactly correspond to
f
-orbits of components of
S\setminus\mathcal{C}
. Moreover,
f
-orbits of finite-order pieces of
S\setminus\mathcal{C}
give rise to
(\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
-components of
M_{f}\setminus\mathcal{T}
and, by Thurston’s hyperbolization theorem,
f
-orbits of pseudo-Anosov pieces of
S\setminus\mathcal{C}
give rise to
\mathbb{H}^{3}
-components.

By uniqueness, this decomposition of the mapping torus
M_{f}
must be the geometric decomposition of the manifold
M
into
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{H}^{3}
pieces. In particular, the hyperbolic components of
M\cong M_{f}
are in bijective correspondence with the
f
-orbits of pseudo-Anosov pieces of
S\setminus\mathcal{C}
and thus with the
f
-orbits of the set
\mathcal{L}^{+}(f)
of measured foliations; therefore, despite the well-known fact that
M
may fiber as the mapping torus
M\cong M_{g}
for infinitely many essentially distinct surface homeomorphisms
g\colon S^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
, the uniqueness of the geometric decomposition shows that the sets of measured foliation orbits are naturally identified for all such fibrations.

1.2
Sketch of key ideas

The above discussion proves the analog of

Theorem

6.3
for surface homeomorphisms and explains why
orbits
of laminations are relevant. It also highlights important differences and challenges that must be overcome in our free group setting. Chief among these is that the result for surface homeomorphisms rests upon monumental work regarding the structure of
3
-manifolds. Frankly, there is no geometrization theorem for free-by-cyclic groups. Secondly, we see that the attracting measured foliations for surfaces do not interact with each other: they are supported on disjoint subsurfaces and are decidedly not nested. The nesting of laminations is something unique to the free group setting, making it unclear whether the depth or poset structure of
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
should be preserved in different splittings of the group.

Accordingly, our approach to
Theorem

6.3
takes inspiration from the
3
-manifold setting but differs greatly in execution. For example, for a surface homeomorphism
f
, the measured foliations are supported on subsurfaces that give rise to canonical submanifolds of the mapping torus
M_{f}
. One might hope that for a free group outer automorphism
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
, the laminations would be supported by subgroups of
\mathbb{F}
that induce canonical subgroups of
M(\phi)
.
While this turns out to be true, constructing these supporting subgroups is significantly more involved, and proving that they induce canonical objects in
M(\phi)
is rather circuitous (see §
6
).

To start, each lamination orbit
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
gives rise to a canonical
nonattracting subgroup system

\mathcal{N}[\Lambda]
.
Using these (specifically, the systems
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda^{\prime}]
for those
[\Lambda^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{+}
that are not contained in
[\Lambda]
), one may also construct a
supporting subgroup system

\mathcal{S}[\Lambda]
that carries the lamination
[\Lambda]
(see §
3
).
Both
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda]
and
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda]
in turn give rise to their own mapping tori, which are free-by-cyclic subgroup systems of
\mathbb{G}=M(\phi)
(see §
2.5
).
We focus on the mapping tori
\mathcal{M}[\Lambda]
coming from the nonattracting subgroup systems
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda]
, which are much easier to deal with.
Unfortunately, these subgroup systems are generally
not known to be canonical in
\mathbb{G}
.
However, we show in the case when
\mathbb{G}
has
no cyclic splittings
that the subgroup systems
\mathcal{M}[\Lambda]
for

maximal
elements
[\Lambda]
of
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
are canonical in
\mathbb{G}
.
Indeed, they are algebraically characterized as the maximal proper malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup systems of
\mathbb{G}
that contain the
polynomial subgroup system

\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
(see §
5.1
for the definition).
This is the content of the next theorem and
forms a key ingredient in our argument.

Theorem 2

(
5.1
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finitely generated nontrivial free group and
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
.
If
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}

has no cyclic splittings, then the set

\left\{\mathcal{M}[\Lambda]\,:\,[\Lambda]\text{ maximal in }\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}\right\}

is the set of maximal proper malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup systems in
\mathbb{G}
supporting
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
. Moreover, this set is in one-to-one correspondence with the collection of maximal lamination orbits in
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
.

This theorem is essentially a consequence of
Proposition

4.3
, which gives sufficient conditions for a malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
to be supported by the nonattracting subgroup system
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda]
of some lamination orbit
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
.
In order to apply
Theorem

5.1
, one needs the free-by-cyclic group
\mathbb{G}
to have no cyclic splittings. While this is not true in general, we show in
Proposition

5.4
how to pass to a canonical subgroup system of
\mathbb{G}
with no cyclic splittings.

These two key ingredients set up a recursive procedure: First apply
Proposition

5.4
to
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\mathbb{G}
and pass to a canonical free-by-cyclic subgroup system
\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\prime}
with no cyclic splittings, then apply
Theorem

5.1
to
\mathcal{G}_{0}^{\prime}
and extract the canonical subgroup systems corresponding to maximal lamination orbits. Next, take their meet (i.e. intersection)
\mathcal{G}_{1}
, and repeat from the first step until the procedure terminates. In this way, we obtain a canonical filtration in
\mathbb{G}
by free-by-cyclic subgroup systems whose length is the depth of
\phi
(
Theorem

5.6
).

Finally, in §
6
, we use this canonical filtration to prove that the mapping tori of the supporting subgroup systems
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda]
are in fact canonical in
\mathbb{G}
and, moreover, that this canonical poset of subgroup systems of
\mathbb{G}
is order-isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
(
Theorem

6.3
).

A few results in the paper utilize the machinery of relative train track maps and topological trees. In order to avoid the arguments becoming too cumbersome, we postpone these details to the appendices.
Section

A.1
contains the proof of
Proposition

2.17
(invariance of depth under passing to finite index subgroups), which requires the precise definition of attracting laminations and relative train track maps.

Section

A.2
constructs an element that approximates the generic leaves of an attracting lamination (
Lemma

2.19
).

Appendix

B
contains the explicit construction of a canonical topological tree associated to a collection of attracting laminations (
Theorem

3.1
), implicit in the results of
[
Mut24b
]
.

Guide for the reader
.

We note that, due to the recursive nature of our argument, we generally do not work with the groups
\mathbb{F}
and
\mathbb{G}
directly, but rather work within certain subgroup systems. That is, all of the machinery is developed for: the restriction
\phi|\mathcal{A}
of an outer automorphism
\phi
to an invariant subgroup system
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathbb{F}
; and the associated mapping torus
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{M}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
, a free-by-cyclic subgroup system of
\mathbb{G}
. However, to simplify the definitions and proofs, the reader can often pretend that
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{F}
,
\phi|\mathcal{A}=\phi
, and
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{G}
.

Further directions

Given

\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
, in this paper, we focus on the collection
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
of attracting laminations, which is empty if and only if
\phi
is polynomially growing. Nevertheless, one can associate a collection of laminations (not attracting anymore), denoted
\Omega(\phi)
, to a polynomially growing outer automorphism
\phi
as explained by
Feighn–Handel in
[
FH11
]
. Handel–Mosher
[
HM20
]
later referred to
\Omega(\phi)
as the set of
weak accumulation sets of eigenrays and twistors of
\phi
. In the same paper
[
FH11
]
, Feighn–Handel show that
\Omega(\phi)
is a partially ordered set and the length of a maximal chain in
\Omega(\phi)
is equal to the degree of polynomial growth of
\phi
, which is known to be a quasi-isometry invariant of
\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\Omega_{\phi}
denoted the poset of
\phi
-orbits of laminations in
\Omega(\phi)
.
Based on
Theorem

6.3
, we expect a positive answer to the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Is
\Omega_{\phi}
, the poset of lamination orbits associated to a polynomially growing automorphism
\phi
, a group invariant of
\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
?

Moreover, given any
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
, one can look at the union of attracting laminations and weak accumulation sets of eigenrays and twistors, which is again a partially ordered set. We are curious to know if this full poset of laminations associated to
\phi
or its depth is an invariant of
\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
. This invariant would be useful for distinguishing hyperbolic free-by-cyclic groups.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Subgroup systems

Let
G
be a group. A
subgroup system
of
G
is a (possibly empty) set
\mathcal{A}
of conjugacy classes of nontrivial subgroups of
G
;
moreover, we require that if
[A],[B]\in\mathcal{A}
are distinct conjugacy classes, then
A
cannot be a subgroup of
B
.
A subgroup system has
finite type
if it consists of finitely many conjugacy classes of finitely generated subgroups.
It is
proper (resp. cyclic)
if it consists of conjugacy classes of proper (resp. cyclic) subgroups.
A subgroup
A\leq G
is
malnormal
if for any
h\in G\setminus A
, the intersection
hAh^{-1}\cap A
is trivial.
A subgroup system
\mathcal{A}
of
G
is
malnormal
if each
A
with
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
is malnormal in
G
and, for each
A,B
with
[A]\neq[B]
in
\mathcal{A}
, the intersection
A\cap B
is trivial.

There is a natural partial order, denoted
\sqsubseteq
, on the set of malnormal subgroup systems where

\mathcal{B}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{A}
if, for every
[B]\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
with
B\leq A
.
In this case, we say that
\mathcal{A}

supports

\mathcal{B}
.
We will write
\mathcal{B}\sqsubsetneq\mathcal{A}
if
\mathcal{B}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}\neq\mathcal{B}
.
Note that malnormality is needed to ensure this relation is antisymmetric, for otherwise one might have a situation
A^{\prime}\lneq B\lneq A
with
[A]=[A^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{A}
.
This partial order is a meet-semilattice, i.e. every pair
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
of malnormal subgroup systems has a greatest lower bound
\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}
;
specifically, the
meet

\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}\coloneq\{[A\cap B]\,:\,[A]\in\mathcal{A},[B]\in\mathcal{B},\text{ and }A\cap B\text{ is not trivial}\}.

When
G
is a free group, the following lemma extends the partial order beyond malnormal subgroup systems to the collection
G^{\leq}_{\mathrm{fg}}
of subgroup systems of
G
consisting of conjugacy classes of finitely generated subgroups.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a free group. The collection
G^{\leq}_{\mathrm{fg}}
has a partial order defined by
\mathcal{B}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{A}
if, for every
[B]\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
with
B\leq A
.
Moreover, when restricted to subgroup systems with finite type, the partial order is a meet-semilattice.

This lemma (and its proof) will serve as a blueprint for the partial order constructed in §
2.4
.

Proof.

Let
A\leq G
be a finitely generated subgroup.
We first observe that if
A^{\prime}\leq A
is a conjugate of
A
, then
A^{\prime}=A
.
Let
\iota\colon G\to G
be the inner automorphism that maps
A
into itself with image
\iota(A)=A^{\prime}
.
As free groups are subgroup separable and
A
is finitely generated, the automorphism
\iota
must map
A
onto itself, i.e.
A^{\prime}=A
(see
[
BFH00
, Lemma 6.0.6]
).

This observation implies the relation
\sqsubseteq
is antisymmetric.
Indeed, let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in G^{\leq}_{\mathrm{fg}}
, and suppose
\mathcal{A}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{A}
.
Pick an arbitrary
[A^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{A}
.
Then
A^{\prime}\leq B
for some
[B]\in\mathcal{B}
.
Similarly,
B\leq A
for some
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is a subgroup system and
A^{\prime}\leq A
, we must have
[A^{\prime}]=[A]
, i.e.
A^{\prime}
is conjugate to
A
.
By the previous paragraph,
A^{\prime}=A
.
Consequently,
A^{\prime}=B=A
and
[A^{\prime}]=[B]
.
As
[A^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{A}
was arbitrary, we have shown
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
.
The same argument shows
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
; hence
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}
.
It is straightforward to check that the relation
\sqsubseteq
is reflexive and transitive.

Now, suppose
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are subgroup systems of
G
with finite type.
We start with a collection mimicking the meet of malnormal subgroup systems:

\mathcal{A}\hat{\wedge}\mathcal{B}\coloneq\{[A\cap B]\,:\,[A]\in\mathcal{A},[B]\in\mathcal{B},\text{ and }A\cap B\text{ is not trivial}\}.

This need not be a subgroup system since, without the malnormality assumption, distinct conjugacy classes may be nested.
Nevertheless, it is a finite collection
[
Neu90
, Proposition 2.1]
.
For each
[A\cap B]\in\mathcal{A}\hat{\wedge}\mathcal{B}
, the singletons
\{[A\cap B]\}
are subgroup systems partially ordered by
\sqsubseteq
.
Then
\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}
is defined to be the subset of maximal classes in
\mathcal{A}\hat{\wedge}\mathcal{B}
.
One can verify that
\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}
is the meet, i.e. the greatest lower bound of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
.
∎

Subgroup systems arise naturally in the context of relatively hyperbolic groups. For example, if
G
is a hyperbolic group and
\mathcal{A}
is a proper subgroup system of
G
with finite type, then
G
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{A}
if and only if

\mathcal{A}
is malnormal and consists of (conjugacy classes of) quasi-convex subgroups
[
Bow12
, Theorem 7.11]
(see also
[
Osi06
,
DGO17
]
).
For a relatively hyperbolic pair
(G,\mathcal{A})
, we say that an element
g\in G
is
\mathcal{A}
-peripheral
if there exists
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
with
g\in A
;
we similarly define
\mathcal{A}
-peripheral subgroups.

Let
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
be two malnormal subgroup systems of
G
with finite type such that
\mathcal{B}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{A}
.
For
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
, the
restriction
of
\mathcal{B}
to
A
is

\mathcal{B}|A\coloneq\{[B]_{A}\colon[B]\in\mathcal{B}\text{ and }B\leq A\},

where
[\cdot]_{A}
denotes the
A
-conjugacy class.
So the restriction
\mathcal{B}|A
is a subgroup system of
A
.

2.2
Trees and splittings

A
G
-tree
is a simplicial metric tree equipped with a minimal action of
G
by isometries.
The tree is
trivial
if it is a point.
A
splitting
of
G
is a
G
-equivariant isometry class of nontrivial
G
-trees. A splitting of
G
is
free
if edge stabilizers are trivial, and it is
cyclic
if edge stabilizers are cyclic (possibly trivial). Let
S
be a free splitting of
G
. The set of conjugacy classes of nontrivial vertex stabilizers of
S
is a malnormal subgroup system of
G
called a
proper free factor system
.
If
G
is finitely generated, then its proper free factor systems have finite type by Bass–Serre theory.

The automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
acts naturally on the right on the set of splittings of
G
by precomposition of the action. Since we are considering
G
-equivariant isometry classes of trees, the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
factors through an action of
\operatorname{Out}(G)
. Note that
\operatorname{Out}(G)
preserves the set of free splittings and the set of cyclic splittings of
G
.

Let
S
be a set with a
G
-action and
\Phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
.
A function
f\colon S\to S
is
\Phi
-equivariant
if: for every
s\in S
and every
g\in G
, we have
f(g\cdot s)=\Phi(g)\cdot f(s).

If, additionally,
S
is a splitting of
G
that is not isometric to
\mathbb{R}
and
\Phi
preserves
S
, i.e.
S\cdot\Phi=S
, then there exists a unique
\Phi
-equivariant isometry
\iota_{\Phi}\colon S\to S
(for instance, see
[
Lev05
, §2.1]
).

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a subgroup system of
G
. A
splitting
of
\mathcal{G}
is a set
\mathcal{S}=\{T_{G}\,:\,[G]\in\mathcal{G}\}
where each
T_{G}
is a
G
-equivariant isometry class of
G
-trees (one for each conjugacy class
[G]
) and some
T_{G}
is a splitting of
G
, that is, at least one
T_{G}
is nontrivial.
A splitting
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{G}
is
cyclic
(resp.
free
) if each of the splittings in
\mathcal{S}
is cyclic (resp. free).
Now suppose
\mathcal{G}
is a malnormal subgroup system of
G
.
A
proper free factor system
of
\mathcal{G}
is the subgroup system of
G
consisting of the conjugacy classes of nontrivial vertex stabilizers of a free splitting of
\mathcal{G}
.
One can check that proper free factor systems of
\mathcal{G}
are malnormal subgroup systems of
G
.
When
\mathcal{G}
has finite type, then so do its proper free factor systems by Bass-Serre theory again.

We now describe some properties of free factor systems of malnormal subgroup systems.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a malnormal subgroup system of
G
and let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
be a free factor system of
\mathcal{G}
.
If
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is a free factor system of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, then
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is a free factor system of
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{G}
or
\mathcal{F}_{2}=\mathcal{F}_{1}
, then there is nothing to show.
So we may assume both free factor systems are proper.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{1}
be a free splitting of
\mathcal{G}
whose system of nontrivial vertex stabilizers is
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and let
\mathcal{S}_{2}
be a free splitting of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
whose system of nontrivial vertex stabilizers is
\mathcal{F}_{2}
.
Then one can blow-up the splitting
\mathcal{S}_{1}

by equivariantly replacing the vertices with nontrivial stabilizers with the splitting
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
This produces a new free splitting

\mathcal{S}_{1}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{G}
whose system of nontrivial vertex stabilizers is
\mathcal{F}_{2}
.
Hence,
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is a free factor system of
\mathcal{G}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{G}
be malnormal subgroup systems of
G
.
If
\mathcal{F}
is a free factor system of
\mathcal{G}
, then
\mathcal{H}\wedge\mathcal{F}
is a free factor system of
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

We may assume
\mathcal{F}\neq\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}\wedge\mathcal{F}\neq\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a free splitting of
\mathcal{G}
whose system of nontrivial vertex stabilizers is
\mathcal{F}
.
For each
[H]\in\mathcal{H}
, malnormality of
\mathcal{G}
implies there is a unique
T_{H}\in\mathcal{S}
with an
H
-action induced by the free splitting. Let
T_{H}^{\prime}\subseteq T_{H}
be the minimal subtree for
H
. Then the nontrivial vertex stabilizers in
H
of
T_{H}^{\prime}
are precisely the nontrivial intersections of
H
with representatives of classes in
\mathcal{F}
. As
\mathcal{H}\wedge\mathcal{F}\neq\mathcal{H}
, the set
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\coloneq\{T_{H}^{\prime}\,:\,[H]\in\mathcal{H}\}
is a free splitting of
\mathcal{H}
, and its nontrivial vertex stabilizers are
\mathcal{H}\wedge\mathcal{F}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a malnormal subgroup system of
G
with finite type.
If
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of free factor systems of
\mathcal{G}
, then the sequence
(\bigwedge_{n\leq N}\mathcal{F}_{n})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
stabilizes.

Proof.

The
complexity
of a system
\mathcal{A}=\{[A_{1}],\ldots,[A_{k}]\}
is
c(\mathcal{A})=-k+\sum_{i=1}^{k}2\operatorname{rank}(A_{i})
, where
\operatorname{rank}(A_{i})
is the smallest size of a generating set of
A_{i}
.
It follows from Bass–Serre theory that
c(\mathcal{F})>c(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
for any proper nesting
\mathcal{F}\sqsupsetneq\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
of free factor systems of
\mathcal{G}
.
So any nested sequence of free factor systems of
\mathcal{G}
stabilizes.
Note that
(\bigwedge_{n\leq N}\mathcal{F}_{n})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is a nested sequence of free factor systems of
\mathcal{G}
by
2.2
and
2.3
;
therefore, it stabilizes.
∎

For a sequence
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of free factor systems of a malnormal subgroup system
\mathcal{G}
with finite type, the
meet
of
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, denoted by
\bigwedge_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{F}_{n}
, is the free factor system
\bigwedge_{n\leq N}\mathcal{F}_{n}
of
\mathcal{G}
for large enough
N\in\mathbb{N}
given by
2.2
and
2.3
, which is well-defined by
2.4
.

2.3
Free-by-cyclic groups and mapping tori

A group is
free-by-cyclic
if it is a group extension of a finitely generated nontrivial free group by an infinite cyclic group. Note that free-by-cyclic groups are not free.

We fix once-and-for-all a free-by-cyclic group
\mathbb{G}
that is a group extension of a finitely generated nonabelian free group
\mathbb{F}
by an infinite cyclic group.
As the infinite cyclic group is free, the extension splits, and there is a natural isomorphism
\mathbb{G}\cong M(\Phi)
, where
\Phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
and
M(\Phi)
is the associated
mapping torus
group with the relative presentation:

M(\Phi)\coloneq\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathbb{Z}=\langle\mathbb{F},t\mid tgt^{-1}=\Phi(g)\text{ for all }g\in\mathbb{F}\rangle.

Denote by
\pi\colon M(\Phi)\to\mathbb{Z}
the natural projection with
\ker\pi=\mathbb{F}
and
\pi(t)=1
.
Up to isomorphism, the mapping torus depends only on the outer automorphism
\phi\coloneq[\Phi]
in
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
, and we also write
M(\phi)
or
\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
instead.
Throughout the paper,
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
,
\Phi\in\phi
, and
\mathbb{G}=M(\Phi)
.

Let
S
be an
\mathbb{F}
-set, i.e. a set with an
\mathbb{F}
-action, and let
f\colon S\to S
be a bijection.
We chose the relation
tgt^{-1}=\Phi(g)
for
M(\Phi)
so that the
\Phi
-equivariance of
f
is precisely the condition needed to extend the
\mathbb{F}
-action on
S
to an
M(\Phi)
-action by
t\cdot s=f(s)
.

2.4
Poset of free-by-cyclic subgroup systems of
\mathbb{G}

Definition 2.5
.

A subgroup system of
\mathbb{G}
is
free-by-cyclic
if it consists of conjugacy classes of free-by-cyclic subgroups.

The following lemmas extend the partial order beyond malnormal subgroup systems of
\mathbb{G}
to the collection of free-by-cyclic subgroup systems, in a manner similar to
Lemma

2.1
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{G}\coloneq\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
and
\pi\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{Z}
be the natural projection.
If
H\leq\mathbb{G}
is a free-by-cyclic subgroup, then
A\coloneq H\cap\mathbb{F}
is a
\phi
-periodic finitely generated subgroup of
\mathbb{F}
, and
H=\langle A,xt^{k}\rangle
for some element
x\in\mathbb{F}
and integer
k\geq 1
that generates
\pi(H)
;
moreover,
H
is naturally identified with
M(\psi)
for some
\psi\in\operatorname{Out}(A)
.

A subgroup
A\leq\mathbb{F}
is
\phi
-periodic
if the conjugacy class
[\Phi^{k}(A)]=[A]
for some integer
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

As
H
is not free,
\pi(H)\leq\mathbb{Z}
is not trivial.
Let
k\geq 1
be a generator of
\pi(H)
.
Then
xt^{k}\in H
for some
x\in\mathbb{F}
, and
H=\langle A,xt^{k}\rangle
, where
A\coloneq H\cap\mathbb{F}
.
As
A
is normal in
H
, it is preserved by conjugation in
\mathbb{G}
by
xt^{k}
.
In other words,
[\Phi^{k}(A)]=[A]
, i.e.
A
is
\phi
-periodic.
The Feighn–Handel coherence theorem implies
A
is finitely generated
[
FH99
]
(see
[
Mut24c
, Lemma 3.5]
for details).
Since
\pi(H)
is infinite cyclic,
H
is naturally identified with
M(\Psi)=A\rtimes_{\Psi}\mathbb{Z}
, where
\Psi\colon A\to A
is the restriction of conjugation by
xt^{k}
.
The element
x\in\mathbb{F}
is well-defined up to left multiplication by an element of
\ker(\pi|H)=A
.
Thus, the outer class
[\Psi]\in\operatorname{Out}(A)
is well-defined.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathbb{G}\coloneq\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
and
H\leq\mathbb{G}
be a free-by-cyclic subgroup.
If a subgroup
H^{\prime}\leq H
is a conjugate of
H
, then
H^{\prime}=H
.

Proof.

By
2.6
, we have
H=\langle A,xt^{k}\rangle
for the
\phi
-periodic finitely generated subgroup
A\coloneq H\cap\mathbb{F}
, some element
x\in\mathbb{F}
, and the integer
k\geq 1
that generates
\pi(H)\leq\mathbb{Z}
.
By hypothesis,
H^{\prime}=(yt^{n})H(yt^{n})^{-1}\leq H
for some
y\in\mathbb{F}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
(yt^{n})A(yt^{n})^{-1}=H^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{F}\leq A
as
\mathbb{F}
is normal in
\mathbb{G}
.
Note that conjugation in
\mathbb{G}
by
yt^{n}
restricts to an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}
that maps
A
into itself.
As
\mathbb{F}
is subgroup separable
and
A
is finitely generated, the automorphism must map
A
onto itself, i.e.
H^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{F}=(yt^{n})A(yt^{n})^{-1}=A
(see
[
BFH00
, Lemma 6.0.6]
).
Note that
\pi(H^{\prime})=\pi(H)
in
\mathbb{Z}
.
Since both
H^{\prime}
and
H
have the same
\pi
-image and the same intersection with the kernel
\ker\pi=\mathbb{F}
, they must be equal.
∎

As in the proof of
Lemma

2.1
, the property in
Lemma

2.7
is precisely what we need to define the following partial order.

Corollary 2.8
.

The collection of free-by-cyclic subgroup systems of a free-by-cyclic group
\mathbb{G}
has a partial order defined by
\mathcal{H}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{G}
if, for every
[H]\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists
[G]\in\mathcal{G}
with
H\leq G
. ∎

The partial order on free-by-cyclic subgroup systems of
\mathbb{G}
with finite type is a meet-semilattice as well, where we define the meet operation like in
Lemma

2.1
. First, we define the collection

\mathcal{H}\hat{\wedge}\mathcal{G}\coloneq\big\{[H\cap G]\,:\,[H]\in\mathcal{H},[G]\in\mathcal{G},\text{ and }H\cap G\text{ is not trivial}\big\}.

This collection need not be a subgroup system since distinct conjugacy classes may be nested.
An application of
2.6
(and
[
Neu90
, Proposition 2.1]
) shows that
\mathcal{H}\hat{\wedge}\mathcal{G}
consists of finitely many conjugacy classes of cyclic or free-by-cyclic subgroups.
The free-by-cyclic conjugacy classes (treated as singletons) are partially ordered by
\sqsubseteq
.
The
meet

\mathcal{H}\wedge\mathcal{G}
consists of the maximal free-by-cyclic classes in
\mathcal{H}\hat{\wedge}\mathcal{G}
.
When applied to malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup systems, this modification of the previous meet operation just amounts to ignoring cyclic intersections.

2.5
Mapping tori over subgroup systems of
\mathbb{F}

A natural way to construct free-by-cyclic subgroup systems of
M(\phi)=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is to consider the mapping torus of a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type.
A subgroup system
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathbb{F}
is
\phi
–invariant
if
\phi[A]\coloneq[\Phi(A)]\in\mathcal{A}
for all
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
.
So
\phi
permutes the conjugacy classes in
\mathcal{A}
.
Fix a
\langle\phi\rangle
-orbit
\langle\phi\rangle[A]\subseteq\mathcal{A}
, and let
k\geq 1
be the size of the orbit—the orbit is finite since
\mathcal{A}
has finite type.
As
\phi^{k}[A]=[A]
, we get
\Phi^{k}(A)=x^{-1}Ax
for some
x\in\mathbb{F}
.
Let
\Phi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
be the automorphism given by
g\mapsto x\Phi^{k}(g)x^{-1}
, and let
\Psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)
be the restriction of
\Phi^{\prime}
to
A
.
By malnormality of
\mathcal{A}
, the element
x\in\mathbb{F}
is unique up to left multiplication by an element of
A
.
So the
first return
outer class
\psi\coloneq[\Psi]\in\operatorname{Out}(A)
is well-defined and may be denoted
\phi^{k}|A\coloneq\psi
.
We naturally identify
M(\psi)\coloneq A\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{Z}
with the subgroup
\langle A,xt^{k}\rangle\leq M(\phi)
.
Since
A
is malnormal in
\mathbb{F}
, the subgroup
M(\psi)
is the normalizer of
A
in
M(\phi)
.
Note that two such subgroups
M(\psi_{1})
and
M(\psi_{2})
are conjugate in
M(\phi)
if and only if the orbits
\langle\phi\rangle[A_{1}]
and
\langle\phi\rangle[A_{2}]
are equal.
The
mapping torus
of
\mathcal{A}
is the following finite collection:

M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\coloneq\Big\{[M(\phi^{k}|A)]~:~\langle\phi\rangle[A]\subseteq\mathcal{A}\text{ is a }\langle\phi\rangle\text{-orbit of size $k$}\Big\}.

Definition 2.9
.

Expanding on the above notation, define
\phi|\mathcal{A}
to be
the set of first return outer classes
\phi^{k}|A
over all
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
.
We moreover say
\phi|\mathcal{A}

preserves a splitting

\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{A}
if, for each
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
, the restriction
\phi^{k}|A
preserves the corresponding
A
-tree in
\mathcal{S}
.

When
\mathcal{A}
consists of a single
\langle\phi\rangle
-orbit, then the singleton
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
is a subgroup system of
M(\phi)
. The next lemma shows that
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
is always a subgroup system of
M(\phi)
and gives a natural correspondence between nested
\phi
-invariant systems in
\mathbb{F}
with nested mapping tori.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup systems of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type.

1.

Then
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
and
M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
are subgroup systems of
M(\phi)
with finite type; and

2.

\mathcal{A}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{B}
if and only if
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\sqsubseteq M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
.

Proof.

We first prove (2) under the assumption
\langle\phi\rangle
acts transitively on
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\langle\phi\rangle[A]
and
\mathcal{B}=\langle\phi\rangle[B]
, and let
M(\phi^{k}|A)=\langle A,xt^{k}\rangle
and
M(\phi^{\ell}|B)=\langle B,yt^{\ell}\rangle
be the associated subgroups of
M(\phi)=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
as identified above.
Now, if
\mathcal{A}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{B}
, i.e.
A\leq B
, then
x\Phi^{k}(B)x^{-1}\cap B
is not trivial. Since
\mathcal{B}
is malnormal, we get
[\Phi^{k}(B)]=[B]
and
k
is a multiple of
\ell
, the size of
\mathcal{B}
; therefore,
\langle B,xt^{k}\rangle
is a finite index subgroup of
M(\phi^{\ell}|B)
, and
M(\phi^{k}|A)=\langle A,xt^{k}\rangle
is a subgroup of
M(\phi^{\ell}|B)
, i.e.
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\sqsubseteq M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
. Conversely, if
M(\phi^{k}|A)\leq M(\phi^{\ell}|B)
, then
A=M(\phi^{k}|A)\cap\mathbb{F}
is a subgroup of
B=M(\phi^{\ell}|B)\cap\mathbb{F}
.

We can now prove (1). Suppose
[M(\phi^{k_{1}}|A_{1})]
and
[M(\phi^{k_{2}}|A_{2})]
are distinct elements of
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
.
We need to show
M(\phi^{k_{1}}|A_{1})
cannot be a subgroup of
M(\phi^{k_{2}}|A_{2})
.
By definition of
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
, the orbits
\langle\phi\rangle[A_{1}]
and
\langle\phi\rangle[A_{2}]
in
\mathcal{A}
are distinct. In particular,
\langle\phi\rangle[A_{1}]
is not nested in
\langle\phi\rangle[A_{2}]
as malnormal subgroup systems of
\mathbb{F}
. By the previous paragraph,
M(\phi^{k_{1}}|A_{1})
is not a subgroup of
M(\phi^{k_{2}}|A_{2})
.
The same argument proves
M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
is a subgroup system of
M(\phi)
.

Finally, we prove (2) in full generality.
Suppose
\mathcal{A}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{B}
, and let
[M(\phi^{k}|A)]\in M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
.
By
\phi
-invariance, we have
\langle\phi\rangle[A]\sqsubseteq\langle\phi\rangle[B]
for some
[B]\in\mathcal{B}
.
We may assume
A\leq B
. By the first paragraph,
M(\phi^{k}|A)\leq M(\phi^{\ell}|B)
, where
[M(\phi^{\ell}|B)]\in M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
.
As
[M(\phi^{k}|A)]\in M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
was arbitrary, we have
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\sqsubseteq M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
.
Conversely, suppose
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\sqsubseteq M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
, and let
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
.
Then
M(\phi^{k}|A)\leq M(\phi^{\ell}|B)
for some
[B]\in\mathcal{B}
.
By the first paragraph again,
A\leq B
. So
\mathcal{A}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{B}
as
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
was arbitrary.
∎

There is a similar correspondence describing meets of mapping tori.

Lemma 2.11
.

If
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are two
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup systems of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type, then
M(\phi|(\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}))=M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\wedge M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}
is malnormal with finite type
[
Neu90
, Proposition 2.1]
.
It is also
\phi
-invariant by uniqueness of the meet.
Let
[C]\in\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}
, i.e.
C=A\cap B
is nontrivial for some
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
and
[B]\in\mathcal{B}
.
Since
M(\phi^{k}|A)
and
M(\phi^{\ell}|B)
are normalizers of
A
and
B
in
M(\phi)
, their intersection is in
M(\phi^{n}|C)
, the normalizer of
C
.
Conversely, let
M(\phi^{n}|C)=\langle C,zt^{n}\rangle
for some
z\in\mathbb{F}
.
By malnormality and
\phi
-invariance of
\mathcal{A}
, the power
n
is a multiple of
k
, and
zt^{n}
normalizes
A
, i.e.
M(\phi^{n}|C)\leq M(\phi^{k}|A)
.
Thus
M(\phi^{n}|C)=M(\phi^{k}|A)\cap M(\phi^{\ell}|B)
, and
[M(\phi^{n}|C)]
is in
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\hat{\wedge}\mathcal{M}(\phi|\mathcal{B})
.
By the definition of the meet,
\{[M(\phi^{n}|C)]\}\sqsubseteq M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\wedge\mathcal{M}(\phi|\mathcal{B})
.
As
[C]\in\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}
was arbitrary, we have shown
M(\phi|(\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}))\sqsubseteq M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\wedge M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
.

Conversely, let
[G]\in M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\wedge M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
.
By definition of the meet, the free-by-cyclic subgroup
G=M(\phi^{k}|A)\cap M(\phi^{\ell}|B)
for some
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
and
[B]\in\mathcal{B}
.
Moreover, the intersection
M(\phi^{k}|A)\cap M(\phi^{\ell}|B)=\langle C,zt^{n}\rangle
for the nontrivial subgroup
C=A\cap B
.
Therefore,
[C]\in\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}
and
[G]=[M(\phi^{n}|C)]\in M(\phi|(\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}))
.
As
[G]\in M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\wedge M(\phi|\mathcal{B})
was arbitrary, this paragraph shows
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\wedge M(\phi|\mathcal{B})\subseteq M(\phi|(\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}))
.
In particular,
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\wedge M(\phi|\mathcal{B})\sqsubseteq M(\phi|(\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{B}))
.
∎

In general, the subgroup system
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
need not be malnormal in
M(\phi)
. We now characterize this failure.
Following
[
DL22
]
, we say distinct subgroups
A,B
are
\phi
-twins
if there exists
n\geq 1
and
w\in\mathbb{F}
so that the
\mathbb{F}
-automorphism
\Psi\colon g\mapsto w\Phi^{n}(g)w^{-1}
simultaneously preserves the two subgroups.
A
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathbb{F}

has
\phi
-twins
if there are
\phi
-twins
A,B
with
[A],[B]\in\mathcal{A}
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type. Then
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
is malnormal in
M(\phi)
if and only if
\mathcal{A}
has no
\phi
-twins.

Proof.

For
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
, let
M(\phi^{k}|A)=\langle A,xt^{k}\rangle
be the associated subgroup of
M(\phi)=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
identified above.
By construction,
[M(\phi^{k}|A)]\in M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
, and all conjugacy classes in
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
are of this form.
Let
n\geq 1
,
w\in\mathbb{F}
, and
\Psi\colon\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{F}
be the automorphism given by
g\mapsto w\Phi^{n}(g)w^{-1}
.
If
\Psi
preserves
A
, then
\langle A,wt^{n}\rangle\leq M(\phi^{k}|A)
(as
A
is malnormal in
\mathbb{F}
). So
wt^{n}\in M(\phi^{k}|A)
. Conversely, if
wt^{n}\in M(\phi^{k}|A)
, then
\Psi
preserves
A
(as
A
is normal in
M(\phi^{k}|A)
).

We first show that
M(\phi^{k}|A)
is malnormal in
M(\phi)
if and only if the orbit
\langle\phi\rangle[A]
in
\mathcal{A}
has no
\phi
-twins.
Let
h=yt^{\ell}\in M(\phi)\setminus M(\phi^{k}|A)
and set
B\coloneq y\Phi^{\ell}(A)y^{-1}\neq A
.
Note that
[B]=\phi^{\ell}[A]
, the conjugate
hM(\phi^{k}|A)h^{-1}=M(\phi^{k}|B)
, and
M(\phi^{k}|A)\cap M(\phi^{k}|B)\cap\mathbb{F}=A\cap B
is trivial as
B\neq A
and
\langle\phi\rangle[A]
is malnormal in
\mathbb{F}
.
So
M(\phi^{k}|A)\cap M(\phi^{k}|B)
is nontrivial if and only if it contains some element
wt^{n}
with
w\in\mathbb{F}
and
n\geq 1
; equivalently,
A,B
are
\phi
-twins.

To conclude, suppose no
\langle\phi\rangle
-orbit in
\mathcal{A}
has
\phi
-twins.
Choose distinct orbits
\langle\phi\rangle[A],\langle\phi\rangle[B]
in
\mathcal{A}
.
Then the conjugacy classes
[M(\phi^{k}|A)]
,
[M(\phi^{k}|B)]
in
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
are distinct.
By the same argument as in the previous paragraph,
M(\phi^{k}|A)\cap M(\phi^{k}|B)
is nontrivial if and only if

A,B
are
\phi
-twins.
∎

In fact, all malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup systems of finite type arise in this way:

Lemma 2.13
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup system of
\mathbb{G}
with finite type, then
\mathcal{G}=M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
for some
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system
\mathcal{A}
of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{Z}
be the natural projection for the presentation
\mathbb{G}\coloneq\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
.
By
2.6
, for every
[G]\in\mathcal{G}
,
G=\langle A,xt^{k}\rangle
for some
x\in\mathbb{F}
, where
A=G\cap\mathbb{F}
is a
\phi
-periodic finitely generated subgroup of
\mathbb{F}
and
k\geq 1
generates
\pi(G)
.
Notice that
A
is malnormal in
\mathbb{F}
.
Indeed, suppose
hAh^{-1}\cap A
is not trivial for some
h\in\mathbb{F}
.
Then
hGh^{-1}\cap G
is not trivial.
By malnormality of
G
in
\mathbb{G}
, we have
h\in G
and
h\in\mathbb{F}\cap G=A
.
Let
\kappa\geq 1
be the size of the
\langle\phi\rangle
-orbit of
[A]
in
\mathbb{F}
.
We claim that
\kappa=k
.
As
xt^{k}At^{-k}x^{-1}=A
, we have
\phi^{k}[A]=[A]
and
k
is multiple of
\kappa
.
If
\kappa<k
, then
yt^{\kappa}At^{-\kappa}y^{-1}=A
for some
y\in\mathbb{F}
, and
yt^{\kappa}\notin G
(as
\kappa
does not generate
\pi(G))
;
therefore,
yt^{\kappa}Gt^{-\kappa}y^{-1}\cap G
is not trivial, contradicting that
G
is malnormal in
\mathbb{G}
.

Define
\mathcal{A}
to be the collection of
\mathbb{F}
-conjugacy classes
[A]
corresponding to all
[G]\in\mathcal{G}
. Consider any
[A_{1}],[A_{2}]\in\mathcal{A}
, where
A_{i}=G_{i}\cap\mathbb{F}
with
[G_{i}]\in\mathcal{G}
.
If
A_{1}\neq A_{2}
, then
G_{1}\neq G_{2}
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is malnormal, this implies
A_{1}\cap A_{2}\leq G_{1}\cap G_{2}
is trivial. Therefore,
\mathcal{A}
is a malnormal subgroup system.
If
[G_{1}]=[G_{2}]
, say with
G_{2}=(wt^{m})G_{1}(wt^{m})^{-1}
, then
A_{2}=w\Phi^{m}(A_{1})w^{-1}
, and so the
\mathbb{F}
-conjugacy classes
[A_{1}],[A_{2}]
are in the same
\phi
-orbit.
Since
\mathcal{G}
has finite type and each
[A]
has finite
\phi
-orbit, the subgroup system
\mathcal{A}
has finite type.
Note that the
\mathbb{F}
-conjugacy class
\phi[A_{1}]=[\Phi(A_{1})]
corresponds to
[G_{1}]=[tG_{1}t^{-1}]\in\mathcal{G}
,
i.e.
\phi[A_{1}]\in\mathcal{A}
.
So
\mathcal{A}
is
\phi
-invariant.
Finally,
\mathcal{G}=M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
as
k
is the size of the orbit
\langle\phi\rangle[A]\subseteq\mathcal{A}
by the previous paragraph.
∎

2.6
Malnormal within a subgroup system

When
\mathcal{H}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{G}
are nested free-by-cyclic subgroup systems of
\mathbb{G}
, we shall colloquially refer to
\mathcal{H}
as a subgroup system of
\mathcal{G}
. In this context, we shall need the following notion of malnormality:

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}
be free-by-cyclic subgroup systems of
\mathbb{G}
such that
\mathcal{H}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{G}
.
We say
\mathcal{H}
is
malnormal
in
\mathcal{G}
if it is the meet of
\mathcal{G}
with a malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup system of
\mathbb{G}
.
Similarly, let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
be
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup systems of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type such that
\mathcal{B}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{A}
.
Then
\mathcal{B}

has
\phi|\mathcal{A}
-twins
if it is the meet of
\mathcal{A}
with a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type and
\phi
-twins.

The following extends
2.13
, the characterization of malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup systems:

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type.
If
\mathcal{H}
is a malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup system in
M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
with finite type, then
\mathcal{H}=M(\phi|\mathcal{D})
for some
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system
\mathcal{D}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{A}
of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type.

Proof.

We have that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\wedge M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
for a malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup system
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{G}
with finite type.
By
2.13
,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=M(\phi|\mathcal{C})
for some
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type.
Now, by
2.11
,
M(\phi|\mathcal{C})\wedge M(\phi|\mathcal{A})=M(\phi|(\mathcal{C}\wedge\mathcal{A}))
. Then
\mathcal{D}\coloneq\mathcal{C}\wedge\mathcal{A}
is the desired
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type.
∎

2.7
Polynomial growth

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
and
g\in\mathbb{F}
, and
fix a basis of
\mathbb{F}
.
The
length
of the conjugacy class
[g]
of
g
with respect to the basis is

\left\|[g]\right\|\coloneq\min_{g^{\prime}\in[g]}\left|g^{\prime}\right|,

where
\left|g^{\prime}\right|
is the
word length
of
g
with respect to the chosen basis.

We say that
g
has
polynomial growth under (forward)
\phi
-iteration
if there exists a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{R}[X]
such that

\left\|\phi^{n}[g]\right\|\leq P(n)\quad\text{for every }n\in\mathbb{N}.

This property does not depend on the choice of the basis. We say
\phi
is
polynomially growing
if every element has polynomial growth under
\phi
-iteration; otherwise,
\phi
is
exponentially growing
.

In
[
Lev09
, Proposition 1.4]
, Levitt showed that there is a unique subgroup system
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
of
\mathbb{F}
whose subgroup representatives are the maximal subgroups consisting entirely of elements with polynomial growth under
\phi
-iteration; further,
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
is a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system with finite type.
Note that
\phi
is exponentially growing if and only if the subgroup system
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
is proper.
Define

\mathcal{P}(M(\phi))\coloneq M(\phi|\mathcal{P}(\phi)),

which is a free-by-cyclic subgroup system of the mapping torus group
M(\phi)\coloneq\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
.
It follows from the proof of
[
Lev09
, Proposition 1.4]
that
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
has no
\phi
-twins (see also
3.3
below), so
\mathcal{P}(M(\phi))
is malnormal in
M(\phi)
.
We shall observe later in §
5.1
that the subgroup system
\mathcal{P}(M(\phi))
is independent of the presentation
M(\phi)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type. Then
\phi|\mathcal{A}
is
polynomially growing
if every
\mathcal{A}
-peripheral element in
\mathbb{F}
has polynomial growth under
\phi
-iteration; equivalently,
\phi|\mathcal{A}
is polynomially growing if and only if
\mathcal{A}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{P}(\phi)
.
Define
\mathcal{P}(\phi|\mathcal{A})\coloneq\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{P}(\phi)
and
\mathcal{P}(M(\phi|\mathcal{A}))\coloneq M(\phi|\mathcal{A})\wedge\mathcal{P}(M(\phi))
.
Note that
\mathcal{P}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
has finite type too.

2.8
Weak attraction theory and attracting laminations

Let
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{F}
be the Gromov boundary of
\mathbb{F}
and

\partial^{2}\mathbb{F}\coloneq(\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{F}\times\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{F}-\Delta)/{\sim}

be the
double boundary
of
\mathbb{F}
, where
\Delta
denotes the diagonal subset and
\sim
is the equivalence relation generated by the flip
(x,y){\sim}(y,x)
.
The double boundary
\partial^{2}\mathbb{F}
is equipped with the quotient topology.
The (continuous) action of
\mathbb{F}
on
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{F}
induces a diagonal action of
\mathbb{F}
on
\partial^{2}\mathbb{F}
;
moreover, this extends to an action of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
on
\partial^{2}\mathbb{F}
via the natural identification
\mathbb{F}\cong\operatorname{Inn}(\mathbb{F})
.

The
space of lines
of
\mathbb{F}
is the quotient
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})\coloneq\mathbb{F}\backslash\partial^{2}\mathbb{F}
of the double boundary by the
\mathbb{F}
-action.
A
line
of
\mathbb{F}
is an element of
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
.
The conjugacy class
[g]
of a nontrivial
g\in\mathbb{F}
naturally determines a (periodic) line
{[[g]]}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
, called the
axis
of
[g]
.
The quotient topology on
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
is also called the
weak topology
to emphasize that it is non-Hausdorff.
A
lamination
of
\mathbb{F}
is a closed nonempty subset of
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
.
Note that
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
naturally acts on the space
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
of lines by homeomorphisms.

Fix an outer automorphism
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
. Bestvina–Feighn–Handel associated to
\phi
a collection
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
of closed subsets
\Lambda\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
called the
attracting laminations
of
\phi

[
BFH00
, Definition 3.1.5]
.
We give the precise definition of an attracting lamination in
Appendix

A
.
For
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
and
g\in\mathbb{F}
, we say that
g
is
weakly attracted
to
\Lambda
(under forward
\phi
-iteration) if
\Lambda
(as a subset of
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
) is contained in the accumulation points of the sequence
({[[\phi^{n}(g)]]})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(in the weak topology).
The set
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
is finite (see proof of
[
BFH00
, Lemma 3.1.13]
), and it is empty if and only if
\phi
is polynomially growing.

Definition 2.16
.

The set
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
of all attracting laminations for
\phi
is partially ordered by inclusion.
The
(lamination) depth

\delta(\phi)
of
\phi
is the maximum size of a chain in
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
,
and the
depth spectrum

\delta\mathcal{S}(\phi)
is the set consisting of the sizes of all maximal chains in
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
.

We first record the following result on the invariance of the depth of an outer automorphism under passing to positive powers and restrictions to finite index invariant subgroups. We postpone its proof to
Section

A.1
as it relies on the exact definition of attracting laminations.

Proposition 2.17
.

Suppose
A\leq\mathbb{F}
has finite index,
\Phi\colon\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{F}
is an automorphism such that
\Phi(A)=A
, and
\Psi\colon A\to A
is the restriction of
\Phi
to
A
.
Set
\phi\coloneq[\Phi]
in
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
and
\psi\coloneq[\Psi]
in
\operatorname{Out}(A)
. Then
\delta(\phi^{m})=\delta(\phi)=\delta(\psi)
and
\delta\mathcal{S}(\phi^{m})=\delta\mathcal{S}(\phi)=\delta\mathcal{S}(\psi)
for all
m\geq 1
.

In fact, there is a natural surjective strict-order preserving map
\pi\colon\mathcal{L}^{+}(\psi)\to\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
, and chains in
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
can be lifted (via
\pi
) to chains of the same length in
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\psi)
.

The poset
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)

.

Each finitely generated subgroup
A
of
\mathbb{F}
has its own double boundary
\partial^{2}A

\partial^{2}\mathbb{F}
using the embedding induced by the inclusion

.

in
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
under the quotient is a closed subset identified with the intrinsic space of lines
\mathfrak{B}(A)
of
A
. Further, suppose
\mathcal{A}
is a malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type.
Then
\bigcup\{\partial^{2}A\mid[A]\in\mathcal{A}\}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-invariant closed subset of
\partial^{2}\mathbb{F}
whose image
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A})
is a closed subset of
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
and a finite disjoint union of the sets
\mathfrak{B}(A)
for
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
.
Note that, for any malnormal subgroup systems
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}
of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type,
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A}_{1})\cap\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A}_{2})=\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A}_{1}\wedge\mathcal{A}_{2})
, and if
\mathcal{A}_{1}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{A}_{2}
, then
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A}_{1})\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A}_{2})
.
Finally, for any given subset
L\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
of lines in
\mathbb{F}
, we say that
\mathcal{A}

carries

L
if
L\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A})
.

Now assume
\mathcal{A}
is a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type.
An
attracting lamination
of
\phi|\mathcal{A}
, denoted
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
, is an attracting lamination of
\phi
that is carried by
\mathcal{A}
.
So
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
is a subset of
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi)
.
As with
\phi
, the subset
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
is empty if and only if
\phi|\mathcal{A}
is polynomially growing.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is
\phi
-invariant, the
\langle\phi\rangle
-action on
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
will preserve
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A})
.
In particular, the
\langle\phi\rangle
-orbit

is a closed subset of
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A})
for any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
.
Let
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
denote the partially ordered subset of
\langle\phi\rangle
-orbits in
\mathcal{L}^{+}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
.
We will need the following folklore lemma, whose proof is postponed to
Section

A.2
.

Lemma 2.19
.

For
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}
, some
\mathcal{A}
-peripheral element

has the following property:

for any
[\Lambda^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}
,

is weakly attracted to

if and only if

.

2.9
JSJ decompositions of relatively one-ended free groups

This section follows
[
GL15
, §3.3]
(see also
[
GL17
]
for a general presentation of JSJ decompositions of groups). We state the results of the section for the free group
\mathbb{F}
for simplicity but all the statements extend to torsion-free relatively hyperbolic groups.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a proper malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type. Recall that, in this case, the group
\mathbb{F}
is hyperbolic relative to the set
\mathcal{N}
.
Partition
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{P}\sqcup\mathcal{H}
into conjugacy classes of noncyclic and cyclic subgroups respectively. In the general theory, only the noncyclic subset
\mathcal{P}
(not
\mathcal{N}
) is necessarily a subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
. However, in this section, we assume that
\mathcal{N}
is a proper malnormal subgroup system.
A subgroup
H
of
\mathbb{F}
is
(relatively) elementary
if it is cyclic or
\mathcal{N}
-peripheral.
We denote by
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
the setwise stabilizer in
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
of
\mathcal{N}
.

An
(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
-tree
is an
\mathbb{F}
-tree in which every conjugacy class in
\mathcal{N}
is elliptic;
it is
elementary
if edge stabilizers are elementary.
Similarly, a
splitting
of
(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
is a splitting of
\mathbb{F}
consisting of
(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
-trees.
We say that
\mathbb{F}
is
one-ended relative to

\mathcal{N}
if there is no free splitting of
(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
.
In that case, Guirardel–Levitt associate to the pair
(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
a canonical
elementary

(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
-tree
T_{\mathcal{N}}
called the
(elementary) JSJ tree
.

Theorem 2.20

(see
[
GL17
, Theorem 9.18]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a proper malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type and let
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{P}\sqcup\mathcal{H}
be the partition into conjugacy classes of noncyclic and cyclic subgroups respectively.
If
\mathbb{F}
is one-ended relative to
\mathcal{N}
, then there is a canonical
elementary

(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
-tree
T_{\mathcal{N}}
with the following properties.

1.

Any vertex with an elementary stabilizer is adjacent to one with a nonelementary stabilizer.

2.

The tree
T_{\mathcal{N}}
is preserved by
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
.

Let
\operatorname{Out}^{0}(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
be the maximal finite index subgroup of
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
which acts trivially on the quotient graph
\mathbb{F}\backslash T_{\mathcal{N}}
.

3.

For each vertex
v\in VT_{\mathcal{N}}
, exactly one of the following holds:

(a)

The stabilizer
\operatorname{Stab}(v)
of
v
is elementary; moreover, either it is cyclic or its conjugacy class is in
\mathcal{P}
.

(b)

The natural homomorphism

\operatorname{Out}^{0}(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})\to\operatorname{Out}(\operatorname{Stab}(v))

has finite image (see
[
GL15
, Theorem 3.9]
).

(c)

The stabilizer
\operatorname{Stab}(v)
is identified with the fundamental group of a compact surface
\Sigma
. Edge groups and representatives of conjugacy classes in
\mathcal{N}
intersect
\operatorname{Stab}(v)
in the fundamental groups of the boundary components of
\Sigma
and the image
of the natural homomorphism

\operatorname{Out}^{0}(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})\to\operatorname{Out}(\operatorname{Stab}(v))

is infinite and contained in the mapping class group of
\Sigma
. ∎

Remark
.

The tree
T_{\mathcal{N}}
is not necessarily a splitting since it can be a point. This is why we call
T_{\mathcal{N}}
a
JSJ tree
instead of a
JSJ splitting
. However, even when
T_{\mathcal{N}}
is a point, the trichotomy for the vertex stabilizers in
2.20
(3) gives information about
\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
that will be sufficient for our purpose (see, for instance,
4.2
and the proof of
4.3
).

Let
T_{\mathcal{N}}
be the JSJ tree for
(\mathbb{F},\mathcal{N})
as in
2.20
.
A vertex
v\in VT_{\mathcal{N}}
is:
elementary
if it satisfies Condition (3a);
rigid
if it satisfies Condition (3b); and finally,
flexible
if it satisfies Condition (3c).

We will also need JSJ forests for subgroup systems of
\mathbb{F}
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{[A_{1}],\ldots,[A_{k}]\}
be a malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type such that
\mathcal{N}\sqsubsetneq\mathcal{A}
.
A
splitting

\mathcal{S}=\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}\}
of the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{N})
is a splitting of
\mathcal{A}
such that each
T_{i}
is a class of
(A_{i},\mathcal{N}|A_{i})
-trees.
We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
one-ended relative
to
\mathcal{N}
if there is no free splitting of
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{N})
.

When
\mathcal{A}
is one-ended relative to
\mathcal{N}
, there exists a canonical
elementary
forest
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{N}}
for the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{N})
constructed as follows.
Pick a conjugacy class
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
. If
[A]\in\mathcal{N}
, then define
T^{A}_{\mathcal{N}}
to be a point as an
A
-tree.
Otherwise, since
\mathcal{A}
is one-ended relative to
\mathcal{N}
, the subgroup
A
is one-ended relative to
\mathcal{N}|A
.
Let
T_{\mathcal{N}}^{A}
be the JSJ tree for
(A,\mathcal{N}|A)
given by
2.20
. The forest
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{N}}\coloneq\{T_{\mathcal{N}}^{A}:[A]\in\mathcal{A}\}
is the
JSJ forest
for
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{N})
. We keep using the trichotomy rigid, flexible, or elementary to classify vertices of the JSJ forest.

3
Subgroup systems associated to attracting laminations

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
.
To prove that the depth of
\phi
is a group invariant of its mapping torus, we need to translate the dynamical information given by an attracting lamination into algebraic data.
This is done through
nonattracting subgroup systems
.
We will also introduce
supporting subgroup systems
, which will be needed to prove the group invariance of the poset
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
.

3.1
Nonattracting subgroup systems

Handel–Mosher proved that the set of all conjugacy classes of elements of
\mathbb{F}
which are not weakly attracted to
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
determines a
\phi
-invariant proper malnormal subgroup system
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda]
with finite type, called the
nonattracting subgroup system

[
HM20
, Theorem F]
.
Their construction also applies to subsets of
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
, and the following theorem is an alternative construction of these subgroup systems:

Theorem 3.1

(see
[
Mut24b
, §3]
)
.

For
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
and
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
, there is a canonical topological tree
T_{\mathcal{L}}
with a non-nesting continuous action by
\mathbb{F}
such that:

1.

the action is minimal with trivial arc stabilizers;

2.

for any automorphism
\Phi\colon\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{F}
representing
\phi
, there is an expanding
\Phi
-equivariant homeomorphism
h_{\mathcal{L}}\colon T_{\mathcal{L}}\to T_{\mathcal{L}}
; and

3.

an element
g\in\mathbb{F}
is elliptic in
T_{\mathcal{L}}
if and only if it is not weakly attracted to any
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}
.

A topological tree can be thought of as an
\mathbb{R}
-tree without the metric.
Non-nesting actions on topological trees are the analogues of isometric actions; in particular, they allow us to classify elements of
\mathbb{F}
as elliptic/loxodromic.
Finally, the definition of expanding for
\Phi
-equivariant homeomorphisms is a bit technical (due to lack of a metric) but the only property we will use is that their fixed points are unique if they exist; this is analogous to expanding homotheties.

Remark
.

Technically, the stated theorem does not appear in the cited reference;
however, it follows readily from the discussion in the citation. For completeness, we sketch in
Appendix

B
how to deduce
3.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
and
T_{\mathcal{L}}
is the corresponding topological tree given by
3.1
.
The
nonattracting subgroup system

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})
for
\mathcal{L}
is the set of conjugacy classes of nontrivial point stabilizers of
T_{\mathcal{L}}
.
For singletons
\mathcal{L}=\{[\Lambda]\}
in
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
, we recover Handel–Mosher’s definition:
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{N}[\Lambda]
.
When
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
, we recover Levitt’s subgroup system:
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{P}(\phi)
.
The
nonattracting systems
for
\phi
is the collection
\mathcal{N}(\phi)\coloneq\left\{\mathcal{N}[\Lambda]\,:\,[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{+}\right\}
.

More generally, let
T
be a topological tree with a non-nesting action of
\mathbb{F}
that is minimal with trivial arc stabilizers.
Then the set
\mathcal{N}(T)
of conjugacy classes of nontrivial point stabilizers of

is a malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
.
Such subgroup systems include nonattracting subgroup systems as well as free factor systems of
\mathbb{F}
.
Let
\mathcal{N}(T^{\prime})
be another such subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
.
Their meet
\mathcal{N}(T)\wedge\mathcal{N}(T^{\prime})
can also be described as the subgroup system consisting of the conjugacy classes of the maximal subgroups of
\mathbb{F}
that are elliptic in both
T
and
T^{\prime}
.
For instance, if
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{+}
, then
\bigwedge\{\mathcal{N}[\Lambda]:[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}\}=\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})
by
3.1
(3).

In our inductive argument, we will consider nonattracting subgroup systems for restrictions to invariant subgroup systems. To that end,
let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type.
For any
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}
, define
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{A})
to be the meet
\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})
.
Similarly, define
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda|\mathcal{A}]\coloneq\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{N}[\Lambda]
for
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathcal{N}(\phi|\mathcal{A})\coloneq\left\{\mathcal{N}[\Lambda|\mathcal{A}]\,:\,[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}^{+}\right\}
.
By
2.19
, for
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}
, the subgroup system
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}
if and only if
\mathcal{L}
is empty.

3.2
.

Recall from
Example

2.18
the automorphism of the free group
\mathbb{F}=\langle a,b,c,d,e,f\rangle

\Phi\colon a\mapsto ab,~b\mapsto bab,~c\mapsto bcd,~d\mapsto dcd,~e\mapsto aba^{-1}b^{-1}ef,~f\mapsto fef.

The following are some nonattracting subgroup systems of
\phi
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{cd}]

\displaystyle=[\langle a,b,e,f\rangle]

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\{[\Lambda_{cd}],[\Lambda_{ef}]\})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{cd})\wedge\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{ef})=[\langle a,b\rangle]

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{ef}]

\displaystyle=[\langle a,b,c,d\rangle]

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{+})

\displaystyle=[\langle aba^{-1}b^{-1}\rangle]=\mathcal{P}(\phi)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{ab}]

\displaystyle=[\langle aba^{-1}b^{-1},e,f\rangle]

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{ab}|\mathcal{A}]

\displaystyle=[\langle aba^{-1}b^{-1}\rangle]\text{ if }\mathcal{A}=[\langle a,b\rangle].

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
,
\mathcal{A}
be a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type, and
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}
.
Then the following statements hold:

1.

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{A})
is a malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type;

2.

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{A})
is
\phi
-invariant and has no
\phi|\mathcal{A}
-twins; and

3.

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{A})
supports
\mathcal{P}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

Let
T_{\mathcal{L}}
be the topological tree corresponding to
\mathcal{L}
given by
3.1
.
By Condition (1) of the theorem (trivial arc stabilizers) and Gaboriau–Levitt index theory
[
GL95
, Theorem III.2]
(see also
[
Mut24a
, Theorem A.7]
),
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})
is a malnormal subgroup system with finite type.
As the meet of two malnormal subgroup systems of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type,
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{A})
is a malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type too.

Pick an automorphism
\Phi\in\phi
.
By Condition (2) (
h_{\mathcal{L}}
is
\Phi
-equivariant),
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})
is
\phi
-invariant.
For an arbitrary
n\geq 1
and
w\in\mathbb{F}
, define the
\mathbb{F}
-automorphism
\Psi\colon g\mapsto w\Phi^{n}(g)w^{-1}
.
Then the homeomorphism
w\cdot h_{\mathcal{L}}^{n}\colon T_{\mathcal{L}}\to T_{\mathcal{L}}
is
\Psi
-equivariant.
By Condition (2) (
h_{\mathcal{L}}
is expanding), the map
w\cdot h_{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
has at most one fixed point. In particular, the automorphism
\Psi
preserves at most one nontrivial point stabilizer of
T_{\mathcal{L}}
by
\Psi
-equivariance of
w\cdot h_{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
. Thus,
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})
has no
\phi
-twins.
As the meet of two
\phi
-invariant subgroup systems,
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{A})
is
\phi
-invariant.
Consequently, it has no
\phi|\mathcal{A}
-twins.

Finally, by Condition (3),
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})
supports
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
.
When we take the meets of both systems with
\mathcal{A}
, we deduce that
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}|\mathcal{A})
supports
\mathcal{P}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
.
∎

We now characterize the laminations that are carried by the nonattracting subgroup systems.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
[\Lambda_{1}],[\Lambda_{2}]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}
,

[\Lambda_{1}]\nsubseteq[\Lambda_{2}]
(as subsets of
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A})
) if and only if
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}|\mathcal{A}]
carries
[\Lambda_{2}]
.

Proof.

Suppose
[\Lambda_{1}]\nsubseteq[\Lambda_{2}]
.
Let
T_{1}
be the tree associated with the singleton
\{[\Lambda_{1}]\}
given by
3.1
.
By
2.19
, there is an
\mathcal{A}
-peripheral element
g_{2}\in\mathbb{F}
that is weakly attracted to
[\Lambda_{2}]
but not
[\Lambda_{1}]
since
[\Lambda_{1}]\nsubseteq[\Lambda_{2}]
.
By
3.1
(3),
g_{2}
is elliptic in
T_{1}
, i.e.
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}]
carries the axis
[[g_{2}]]
.
Since the
\mathcal{A}
-peripheral element
g_{2}
is weakly attracted to
[\Lambda_{2}]
and since
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A}),\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}])\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
are
\phi
-invariant closed subsets that contain
[[g_{2}]]
, we deduce that
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}|\mathcal{A}]
carries
[\Lambda_{2}]
.

The converse follows from the claim that
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}]
does not carry
[\Lambda_{1}]
.
Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}]
carries
[\Lambda_{1}]
, i.e.
[\Lambda_{1}]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}]}
.
By
2.19
, some
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}]
-peripheral element
g_{1}\in\mathbb{F}
is weakly attracted to
[\Lambda_{1}]
, which contradicts the definition of
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}]
.
∎

As an application, we get
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{N}[\Lambda|\mathcal{A}]}=\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{[\Lambda]\}
when
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}
is maximal.
The next lemma is a key step in tying the poset of laminations with posets of subgroup systems.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
[\Lambda_{1}],[\Lambda_{2}]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}
,

[\Lambda_{1}]\subseteq[\Lambda_{2}]
if and only if
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}|\mathcal{A}]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{2}|\mathcal{A}]
.

Equivalently, the collection
\mathcal{N}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
is naturally order-isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}
.

Proof.

For
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
T_{i}
be the tree associated with singleton
\{[\Lambda_{i}]\}
given by
3.1
.
An element
g\in\mathbb{F}
is loxodromic in
T_{i}
if and only if it is weakly attracted to
[\Lambda_{i}]
by
3.1
(3).
If
[\Lambda_{1}]\subseteq[\Lambda_{2}]
, then any element
g\in\mathbb{F}
that is elliptic in
T_{1}
is also elliptic in
T_{2}
,
or equivalently,
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{2}]
; therefore,
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}|\mathcal{A}]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{2}|\mathcal{A}]
.
Conversely, if
[\Lambda_{1}]\nsubseteq[\Lambda_{2}]
,
then
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}|\mathcal{A}]
carries
[\Lambda_{2}]
but
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{2}|\mathcal{A}]
does not by
3.4
; therefore,
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{1}|\mathcal{A}]\nsqsubseteq\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{2}|\mathcal{A}]
.
∎

3.2
Supporting subgroup systems

For
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{+}
, the
support
of
[\Lambda]
is the subset

\Lambda^{\downarrow}\coloneq\left\{[\Lambda^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{+}\,:\,[\Lambda^{\prime}]\subseteq[\Lambda]~\text{as subsets of}~\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})\right\},

and the
exterior
of
[\Lambda]
is the subset
\Lambda^{\perp}\coloneq\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{+}\setminus\Lambda^{\downarrow}
.
By
3.4
, we get
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{N}(\Lambda^{\perp})}=\Lambda^{\downarrow}
.
The
supporting subgroup system

\mathcal{S}[\Lambda]
for
[\Lambda]
is the meet of all free factor systems of
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda^{\perp})
that carry
\Lambda^{\downarrow}
, which is the free factor system of
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda^{\perp})
defined using
2.2
,
2.4
and
2.3
.
By uniqueness of the meet and
\phi
-invariance of
\Lambda^{\perp}
,
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda]
is
\phi
-invariant.
Note that
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{S}[\Lambda]}=\Lambda^{\downarrow}
.
The
supporting systems
for
\phi
is the collection
\mathcal{S}(\phi)\coloneq\left\{\mathcal{S}[\Lambda]\,:\,[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{+}\right\}
.

The next lemma is an analog of
3.5
for supporting systems.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
[\Lambda_{1}],[\Lambda_{2}]\in\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{+}
,

[\Lambda_{1}]\subseteq[\Lambda_{2}]
if and only if
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{1}]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{2}]
.

Equivalently, the collection
\mathcal{S}(\phi)
is naturally order-isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
.

Proof.

Suppose
[\Lambda_{1}]\nsubseteq[\Lambda_{2}]
.
As
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{i}]}=\Lambda_{i}^{\downarrow}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, we know
[\Lambda_{1}]
is carried by
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{1}]
but not by
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{2}]
.
This shows that
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{1}]\nsqsubseteq\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{2}]
.
Conversely, suppose
[\Lambda_{1}]\subseteq[\Lambda_{2}]
. Then
\Lambda_{1}^{\downarrow}\subseteq\Lambda_{2}^{\downarrow}
and
\Lambda_{2}^{\perp}\subseteq\Lambda_{1}^{\perp}
.
As
\Lambda_{2}^{\perp}\subseteq\Lambda_{1}^{\perp}
, we have
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{1}^{\perp})\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{2}^{\perp})
.
As
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{2}]
is a free factor system of
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{2}^{\perp})
that carries
\Lambda_{2}^{\downarrow}\supseteq\Lambda_{1}^{\downarrow}
,
the meet
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{1}^{\perp})\wedge\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{2}]
is a free factor system of
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{1}^{\perp})

that carries
\Lambda_{1}^{\downarrow}
by

2.3
.
By minimality of
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{1}]
in
\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{1}^{\perp})
,

\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{1}]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{1}^{\perp})\wedge\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{2}]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{2}].\qed

Our supporting subgroup system

is closely related to the supporting free factor systems defined by Bestvina–Feighn–Handel

By uniqueness of the meet,
\mathcal{F}(\phi\Lambda)=\phi\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
for

For

Note that

3.7
.

Let

be induced by a homeomorphism

of a surface

with boundary. Suppose the Thurston normal form for

Lemma 3.8

(see
[
HM20
, Theorem F]
)
.

\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}]=\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{-}]
for paired lamination orbits
[\Lambda_{\pm}]
.

The lemma essentially is due to Handel–Mosher. We include a proof that uses splittings instead of the machinery of completely split relative train track maps.

We start by defining relative growth.
Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\sqsubsetneq\mathcal{A}
is a
\phi
-invariant proper free factor system.
Fix a free splitting
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{A}
whose subgroup system of point stabilizers is
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
The length
\left\|[g]\right\|_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
of an
\mathcal{A}
-peripheral element of
\mathbb{F}
is the translation length of its conjugacy class in
\mathcal{T}
.
An
\mathcal{A}
-peripheral element

has

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
under
\phi
-iteration if the sequence
(\left\|\phi^{n}[g]\right\|_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}})_{n}
is bounded by a polynomial in

.
The property is independent of the choice of the free splitting
\mathcal{T}
, and we recover the original definition of polynomial growth when
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\emptyset
.
The restriction
\phi|\mathcal{A}
is
polynomially growing relative to
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
if every
\mathcal{A}
-peripheral element has polynomial growth relative to
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
under
\phi
-iteration.
For completeness, we insist
\phi|\mathcal{A}
is
polynomially growing relative to
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.8
.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}]\neq\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{-}]
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume some
g\in\mathbb{F}
is weakly attracted to
[\Lambda_{-}]
(under backward
\phi
-iteration) but not
[\Lambda_{+}]
(under forward
\phi
-iteration).
Then the
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}]
carries
[[g]]
.
By
\phi
-invariance,
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}]
carries each periodic line in the sequence
(\phi^{-k}[[g]])_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Since the accumulation set of the sequence contains
[\Lambda_{-}]
, the closed subspace
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}])\subseteq\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
contains
[\Lambda_{-}]
.
Let
\mathcal{F}\coloneq\mathcal{F}[\Lambda_{\pm}]
be the free factor system of
\mathbb{F}
defined above.
Then
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}|\mathcal{F}]
carries
[\Lambda_{-}]
.
However,
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}|\mathcal{F}]=\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{-}|\mathcal{F}]
.
Indeed, choose a maximal
\phi
-invariant proper free factor system
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\sqsubsetneq\mathcal{F}
. By
[
Mut24b
, Proposition 2.2]
,
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}|\mathcal{F}]
(resp.
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{-}|\mathcal{F}]
) is the unique maximum amongst malnormal subgroup systems
\mathcal{H}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{H}
-peripheral elements of
\mathbb{F}
are polynomially growing relative to

under
\phi
-iteration (resp. under
\phi^{-1}
-iteration).
So
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}|\mathcal{F}]
, and the restriction
\phi|\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}|\mathcal{F}]
is polynomially growing relative to
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
By the equivalence of Conditions (
1{\Leftrightarrow}2
) in
[
Mut24a
, Proposition 3.1]
, the restriction
\phi^{-1}|\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}|\mathcal{F}]
is polynomially growing relative to
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
(see also
[
Mut24c
, Corollary 2.5]
).
Uniqueness and maximality of
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{-}|\mathcal{F}]
implies
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{+}|\mathcal{F}]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{-}|\mathcal{F}]
.
By symmetry, the two are equal, and
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda_{-}]
carries
[\Lambda_{-}]
, which contradicts

.
∎

From this lemma and

, we immediately get the following:

Corollary 3.9
.

The induced pairing between
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
and
\mathcal{L}^{-}_{\phi}
is an order isomorphism. ∎

Finally, we show that paired lamination orbits have the same supporting subgroup systems.

Corollary 3.10
.

\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{+}]=\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{-}]
for paired lamination orbits
[\Lambda_{\pm}]
.

Proof.

By
Lemmas

3.8
and
3.9
, we have

\mathcal{N}\coloneq\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{+}^{\perp})=\mathcal{N}(\Lambda_{-}^{\perp})
.
As
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}_{\phi|\mathcal{N}}=\Lambda_{\pm}^{\downarrow}
(

3.4
), the supporting subgroup system
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{+}]
(resp.
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{-}])
is the minimal free factor system of
\mathcal{N}
that carries
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{N}}
(resp.
\mathcal{L}^{-}_{\phi|\mathcal{N}}
).
This is equivalent
to stating that
\phi|\mathcal{N}
is polynomially growing relative to
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{+}]
, and so is
\phi^{-1}|\mathcal{N}
as in the previous proof;
therefore,
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{+}]
carries

.
By minimality,
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{-}]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{+}]
.
Similarly,
\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{+}]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{S}[\Lambda_{-}]
.
∎

4
Invariant malnormal subgroup systems of
\mathbb{F}

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
.
In this section, we relate
\phi
-invariant proper malnormal subgroup systems supporting the polynomial subgroup system

\phi

Suppose

is a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of

We start with the case of
\phi
-invariant proper free factor systems of

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
\phi|\mathcal{A}
does not preserve a free splitting of
\mathcal{A}
.
If
\mathcal{F}\sqsubsetneq\mathcal{A}
is a
\phi
-invariant proper free factor system, then
\mathcal{F}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}[\Lambda|\mathcal{A}]
for some
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}

Proof.

We may suppose that
\mathcal{F}\sqsubsetneq\mathcal{A}
is a maximal
\phi
-invariant proper free factor system.
Equivalently, for every
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
, the outer automorphism
\phi^{k}|A
is irreducible relative to
\mathcal{F}|A
.
Choose
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
such that
[A]\notin\mathcal{F}
.
Let

be a relative train track map for
\phi^{k}|A

f

of
A
(see
Appendix

).
We claim that the top stratum

of

is an EG stratum. Indeed, suppose towards a contradiction that
H_{0}
is NEG.
Then collapsing each connected component of

to a point induces a
\phi^{k}|A
-invariant graph of groups

of
A
with trivial edge groups. By Bass–Serre theory,

is covered by a
\phi^{k}|A
-invariant free splitting of

does not preserve a free splitting of

By
[

, some

. The attracting lamination

Let
\mathcal{N}\sqsubsetneq\mathcal{A}
be a proper malnormal subgroup system with finite type and

be the partition into conjugacy classes of noncyclic and cyclic subgroups respectively. Note that

, the group

, the JSJ forest

for

exists if

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{N}\sqsubsetneq\mathcal{A}
is a
\phi
-invariant proper malnormal subgroup system with finite type such that
\mathcal{P}(\phi|\mathcal{A})\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}
.
If
\mathcal{A}
is one-ended relative to
\mathcal{N}
, then the JSJ forest
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{N}}
has no rigid vertices.

Proof.

Suppose towards a contradiction that there exists
[A]\in\mathcal{A}
such that
T_{\mathcal{N}}^{A}
has a rigid vertex
v
.
Pick an automorphism
\Psi\in\phi^{k}|A
.
Then there is a
\Psi
-equivariant isometry
\iota
of
T_{\mathcal{N}}^{A}
by
2.20
(2); moreover,
\iota^{m}
preserves the orbits of vertices of

∎

We now prove the main proposition of this section.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
and
\mathcal{A}
be a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}

Suppose that
\phi|\mathcal{A}

\phi

\mathcal{N}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}[\Lambda|\mathcal{A}]
for some
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}\sqsubsetneq\mathcal{A}

\phi
-invariant proper malnormal subgroup system with finite type such that
\mathcal{P}(\phi|\mathcal{A})\sqsubseteq\mathcal{N}
.
By

2.4

\mathcal{A}

.
Otherwise,

is one-ended relative to

.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{N}}
be the JSJ forest for

(

).
By
2.20
(1)
, there exists
[A]\in\mathcal{A}

For some

[v]
of

5
Group invariance of lamination depth

Fix an outer automorphism
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
and its mapping torus
\mathbb{G}\coloneq M(\phi)=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
.
We say an object/property associated with
\phi
is
canonical
in
\mathbb{G}
if it has a characterization involving only algebraic statements about
\mathbb{G}
.
In particular, the algebraic statements do not reference the presentation
M(\phi)
.

For the rest of the section,
\mathcal{A}
is a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of
\mathbb{F}
with finite type and
\mathcal{G}\coloneq M(\phi|\mathcal{A})
is the associated mapping torus.
As before, we say an object/property associated with
\phi|\mathcal{A}
is
canonical
in
\mathcal{G}
if it has a characterization involving only algebraic statements about
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathbb{G}
.
Note that canonicity is transitive: if an object is canonical in
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}
is canonical in
\mathbb{G}
, then the object is canonical in
\mathbb{G}
.

5.1
Growth type is canonical

For our first example of a property of
\phi
that is canonical in
\mathbb{G}
, we remark that
\phi
preserves a free splitting of
\mathbb{F}
if and only if
\mathbb{G}
has a cyclic splitting
[
Mut24c
, Lemma 3.1]
.

More generally, the growth type of
\phi
is canonical in
\mathbb{G}
, and the algebraic characterization involves inductively choosing cyclic splittings.
Set
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{[\mathbb{G}]\}
and, for
i\geq 0
, assume
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is a free-by-cyclic subgroup system of
\mathbb{G}
with finite type.
Pick a cyclic splitting
\mathcal{S}_{i}
of
\mathcal{H}_{i}
(if one exists).
By Bass–Serre theory, the conjugacy classes of noncyclic vertex stabilizers of
\mathcal{S}_{i}
form a proper free-by-cyclic subgroup system
\mathcal{H}_{i+1}\sqsubsetneq\mathcal{H}_{i}
.
This inductively produces a filtration
\mathcal{H}_{0}\sqsupsetneq\mathcal{H}_{1}\sqsupsetneq\cdots
,
and we stop when every vertex stabilizer of
\mathcal{S}_{i}
is cyclic or
\mathcal{H}_{i}
has no cyclic splittings.
We will say
\mathbb{G}
is
polynomial
if this process stops when every vertex stabilizer is cyclic (for some/any choices of cyclic splittings).
The growth type of
\phi
is canonical in
\mathbb{G}
:
\phi
is polynomially growing if and only if
\mathbb{G}
is polynomial
[
Mut24c
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Finally, the mapping torus
\mathcal{P}(M(\phi))
(defined in §

) is canonical in
\mathbb{G}
since it is the unique subgroup system of maximal polynomial free-by-cyclic subgroups of
\mathbb{G}

[
Mut24c
, Proposition 3.6]
.
We may now denote it as
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
.
Set
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})\coloneq\mathcal{G}\wedge\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{G})
.
As the meet of
\mathcal{G}
with a canonical subgroup system in
\mathbb{G}
,
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})
is canonical in
\mathcal{G}
.

5.2

For any

, recall that
\mathcal{N}[\Lambda|\mathcal{A}]\sqsubseteq\mathcal{A}
is a
\phi
-invariant proper malnormal subgroup system with finite type;
moreover, it supports
\mathcal{P}(\phi|\mathcal{A})
and has no

-twins by

.
The mapping torus

, is a proper malnormal subgroup system in
\mathcal{G}
supporting
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})
by

,
2.10
and

.

For this section, assume
\mathcal{G}
has no cyclic splittings, and set
\mathcal{L}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}\coloneq\left\{\text{maximal }[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}\right\}

We now give an algebraic characterization of the collection
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{max}}_{\mathcal{G}}
of associated mapping tori

This is the key observation underlying the rest of the paper:

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
and
\mathcal{A}
be a
\phi
-invariant malnormal subgroup system of

Thus, the collection
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{max}}_{\mathcal{G}}

is canonical in
\mathcal{G}
, and we may also say
\mathcal{L}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}
is canonical in
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a proper malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup system in
\mathcal{G}
supporting
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})
.
Since
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})
has finite type, some finite subsystem of

supports it.
For an arbitrary finite subsystem
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}

By

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G})\sqsubseteq\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

By

As

We may assume

\mathcal{L}_{\phi|\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{max}}
by

and

.

Thus, the collection of maximal proper malnormal free-by-cyclic subgroup systems of

is a subset of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{max}}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
This subset is not proper.
Indeed, for

Proposition 5.4

Let

Appendix A

The aim of this appendix is to prove

(in
Section

A.1
) and

(in
Section

A.2
). Both proofs require some general background regarding relative train tracks and attracting laminations, which we recall now.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected finite graph whose fundamental group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}
. A
marking
is an isomorphism
m\colon\pi_{1}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{F}
. The marking induces an equivariant homeomorphism between the Gromov boundary
\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{\Gamma}
of the universal cover
\widetilde{\Gamma}
of

and the Gromov boundary of
\mathbb{F}

and the space
\mathfrak{B}(\Gamma)\coloneq\pi_{1}(\Gamma)\backslash\partial^{2}\widetilde{\Gamma}
of biinfinite, unoriented, reduced paths in
\Gamma
.
We fix
this identification
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})\cong\mathfrak{B}(\Gamma)
.

Let
\phi\in\operatorname{Out}(\mathbb{F})
be a given outer automorphism.
Following
[
BFH00
, Definition 3.1.5]
, a line
\ell\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
is
generic
for
\phi
if the following hold:

•

\ell
is
birecurrent

\ell
occurs infinitely often in both ends of
\ell
; and

•

there is an integer
k\geq 1
and neighborhood

of
\ell
such that
\phi^{k}(U)\subseteq U
and
\{\phi^{kn}(U):n\geq 1\}
is a neighborhood basis for
\ell
;

The cited definition has a third condition (
\ell
is not the axis of an element) that is made redundant by the second condition under our assumption (in §
2.3
) that
\mathbb{F}
is nonabelian.
An
attracting lamination
for
\phi
is the closure in
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
of a generic line for
\phi
.

We now turn to the relevant background regarding relative train track maps. Let
f\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
be a relative train track representing
\phi
. The graph
\Gamma
comes equipped with an

\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}\supsetneq\Gamma_{1}\supsetneq\ldots\supsetneq\Gamma_{K}=\emptyset.

For

be the

of
\Gamma
. For a filtration associated with a relative train track map, a stratum is

(EG),
nonexponentially growing
(NEG), or a

depending on the spectral radius of the transition matrix associated with the stratum. An edge path
\rho

crosses

if
\rho
contains an edge of
H_{r}
.

If
\sigma
is a (not necessarily reduced) edge path in

its associated reduced edge path: it is the immersed path homotopic to
\sigma

If
\sigma
is a closed edge path, we also denote by

its associated cyclically reduced edge path, which is the immersed closed path freely homotopic to

.
Let

be the map sending an edge path

of
\Gamma
to

Bestvina–Feighn–Handel

proved that, for every attracting lamination orbit

such that the highest stratum crossed by any generic line of

obtained as an exhaustion by

-iterates of some (equivalently any) edge

of

[

.

A

is an unordered pair of oriented edges of
\Gamma
originating at a common vertex. A turn has

if the edges defining it are contained in the subgraph
\Gamma_{r}
but not

. A turn is

if it consists of distinct oriented edges, and is

otherwise. An

\rho

crosses a turn

if it contains the subpath

(or
\overline{e}_{2}e_{1}
). For

, let

be the first edge of the reduced (nontrivial) path

. The relative train track

induces a map

of the turns by sending

to

.
A turn is

(with respect to

-iterates are all nondegenerate. A path

A.1

The goal is this section is to prove the virtual invariance of the depth:

Proposition 1

(
2.17
)
.

Suppose

has finite index,
\Phi\colon\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{F}
is an automorphism such that
\Phi(A)=A
, and

is the restriction of

to

in

and

Let

Note that

and

We first describe a natural map

Let

be a relative train track map representing

, and choose a lift

.
Assume that

, the lift

.
For every EG stratum

,

) such that

is the highest stratum it crosses.

For surjectivity of

, let

and

. By

. Hence the

by some periodic line
[[g]]\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{A})
.
First, we prove the standard case

.

Lemma A.3
.

For
[\Lambda]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
, some element
g\in\mathbb{F}
has the following property:

for any
[\Lambda^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
,
g
is weakly attracted to
[\Lambda^{\prime}]
if and only if
[\Lambda^{\prime}]\subseteq[\Lambda]
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
be a relative train track representing
\phi
and
H_{r}
the EG stratum of
\Gamma
associated with
[\Lambda]
.
Pick an oriented edge
e
of
H_{r}
and an integer
M\geq 1
such that the reduced
r
-legal path
f_{\#}^{M}(e)
contains at least two copies of
e
—such an
M
exists since
H_{r}
is an EG stratum.
Note that, for any
n\geq 1
, any finite subpath of
f_{\#}^{n}(e)
is a finite subpath of
f_{\#}^{M}
-iterates of
f_{\#}^{n}(e)
by
A.1
.

Define
\sigma
to be the cyclically reduced subpath in
f_{\#}^{M}(e)
joining the origins of two distinct copies of
e
.
We will consider
\sigma
as a cycle in
\Gamma
and denote by
[g]
the conjugacy class of elements in
\mathbb{F}
represented by
\sigma
.
By construction,
\sigma e
is a subpath of
f_{\#}^{M}(e)
, so it is
r
-legal.
As
e
is the initial edge of
\sigma
,
A.1
implies
f_{\#}^{n}(\sigma)
is cyclically reduced and has
f_{\#}^{n}(e)
as an initial subpath for all
n\geq 1
.

Recall that we identified
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{F})
with
\mathfrak{B}(\Gamma)
, the space of biinfinite unoriented reduced paths in
\Gamma
.
Let
k
be the size of the
\phi
-orbit
[\Lambda]
, and assume generic lines of
\Lambda
cross the edge
e
.
By
[
BFH00
, Lemma 3.1.10]
, an unoriented path
\rho
is a finite subpath of the generic lines of
\Lambda
if and only if it is a subpath of some unoriented
f_{\#}^{k}
-iterate of
e
.

Let
[\Lambda^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\phi}
, and suppose the element
g
is weakly attracted to
[\Lambda^{\prime}]
.
Pick an (arbitrary) unoriented finite subpath
\rho^{\prime}
of a generic line of
\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Then
\rho^{\prime}
is a subpath of the periodic line
\phi^{N}[[g]]
for infinitely many
N\geq 1
.
For large
N
, the reduced path
f_{\#}^{N}(e)
will be longer than
\rho^{\prime}
;
therefore,
\rho^{\prime}
(with some orientation) is a subpath of a cyclic permutation of the oriented path
f_{\#}^{N}(\sigma)
for infinitely many
N\geq 1
.
Since
e
is the initial edge of
\sigma
, we deduce that
\rho^{\prime}
is a subpath of the reduced path
f_{\#}^{N}(\sigma)f_{\#}^{N}(e)=f_{\#}^{N}(\sigma e)
for some large
N\geq 1
—the latter is a subpath of
f_{\#}^{N+M}(e)
as
\sigma e
is a subpath of
f_{\#}^{M}(e)
.
Thus,
\rho^{\prime}
is a subpath of the generic lines of
\phi^{N}(\Lambda)
.
As
\rho^{\prime}
was arbitrary, we have shown that the generic lines of
\Lambda^{\prime}
are in the closure of the generic lines of
[\Lambda]
, and hence,
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq[\Lambda]
.
This extends to the
\phi
-orbit:
[\Lambda^{\prime}]\subseteq[\Lambda]
.

Conversely, suppose
[\Lambda^{\prime}]\subseteq[\Lambda]
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda
.
Pick an unoriented finite subpath
\rho^{\prime}
of a generic line of
\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Then
\rho^{\prime}
is a subpath of a generic line of
\Lambda
.
Equivalently,
\rho^{\prime}
(with some orientation) is a subpath of some
f_{\#}^{k}
-iterate of
e
.
By the observations in the first and second paragraphs,
\rho^{\prime}
is a subpath of infinitely many
f_{\#}
-iterates of
e
, and hence
\sigma
.
As
\rho^{\prime}
was arbitrary, the element
g
is weakly attracted to
\Lambda^{\prime}
, and this extends to
[\Lambda^{\prime}]
.
∎

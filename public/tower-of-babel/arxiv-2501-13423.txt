General Error Estimates of Non Conforming Approximation of System of Reaction-Diffusion Equations
Yahya Alnashri

Abstract
. This paper aims to establish a first general error estimate for numerical approximations of the system of reaction-diffusion equations (SRDEs), using reasonable regularity assumptions on the exact solutions. We employ the gradient discretisation method (GDM) to discretise the system and prove the existence and uniqueness of the approximate solutions. The analysis provided here is not limited to specific reaction functions, and it is applicable to all conforming and non-conforming schemes fitting within the GDM framework. As an application, we present numerical results based on a finite volume method.

Letting
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(

) be a polygonal bounded domain and

, we consider in this work the following system of partial differential equations coupled with generic reaction terms and anisotropic tensors:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\mathop{\rm div}(\Lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t))

\displaystyle=F_{1}(\bar{u},\bar{v}),\quad(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t)-\mathop{\rm div}(\Lambda_{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t))

\displaystyle=F_{2}(\bar{u},\bar{v}),\quad(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.1b)

\displaystyle(\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t),\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t))

\displaystyle=(0,0),\quad(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.1c)

\displaystyle(\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},0),\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},0))

\displaystyle=(u_{\rm ini}(\boldsymbol{x}),v_{\rm ini}(\boldsymbol{x})),\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega.

(1.1d)

With particular choices of reaction terms
F_{1}
and

[

Mathematical studies corresponding to the existence and uniqueness of the solutions to SRDEs are available in different references

[

]

[

, finte element method
[

,

]

[

\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}=I_{d}

[

While benchmarking is typically employed to evaluate the quality of numerical schemes, it is limited to certain specific cases. This study seeks to determine the convergence order of generic approximations of the system (
1.1

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to present general error estimates for the approximation by non-conforming methods of the anisotropic SRDM. To achieve our results, we approximate the studied model in space using the Gradient Discretisation Method detailed in
[
10

The organisation of this paper is structured as follows. In Section

2.
Weak Formulation and Discretisation

Let us start by introducing the following assumptions on data.

Assumptions 2.1
.

We assume that the model data satisfies the following conditions:

•

\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(
d=1,2.3
) with a
C^{2}\mbox{--regular}
boundary
\partial\Omega
and

•

\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}:\Omega\to\mathbb{A}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
are measurable functions, such that, for a.e.
\Lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})
and
\Lambda_{2}(\boldsymbol{x})
are symmetric with eigenvalues in
[\underline{\lambda}_{1},\overline{\lambda}_{1}]
and
[\underline{\lambda}_{2},\overline{\lambda}_{2}]
, respectively, where
\mathbb{A}_{d}({\mathbb{R}})
is the set of
d\times d
matrices,

•

(u_{\rm ini},v_{\rm ini})
are in
H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
,

•

the functions
F_{1},\;F_{2}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constants
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
, respectively, where
L:=\max\{L_{1},L_{2}\}
.

Under the above assumptions, it is proved in
[
22
]
that the problem (
1.1
) admits a unique pair of weak solutions as follows: there exists
(\bar{u},\bar{v})\in(L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)))^{2}
such that
(\partial_{t}\bar{u},\partial_{t}\bar{v})\in(L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega)))^{2}
, and for all a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\bar{u}(\cdot,t),\varphi(\cdot,t)\rangle

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla\bar{u}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\cdot\nabla\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

(2.1a)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F_{1}(\bar{u},\bar{v})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\quad\forall\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\bar{v}(\cdot,t),\psi(\cdot,t)\rangle

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Lambda_{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla\bar{v}(\boldsymbol{x},t)\cdot\nabla\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

(2.1b)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F_{2}(\bar{u},\bar{v})\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\quad\forall\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the duality product between
H^{1}(\Omega)
and its dual space
H^{-1}(\Omega)
.

Concerning the spatial space, we can approximate the problem (
2.1
) by the gradient discretisation method. The first step toward this approximation is introducing generic discrete spaces and operators as in the following definition.

Definition 2.2

(Generic Discrete Elements)
.

A space-time gradient discretisation
{\mathcal{D}}
is given by
{\mathcal{D}}=(X_{{\mathcal{D}},0},\Pi_{\mathcal{D}},\nabla_{\mathcal{D}},J_{\mathcal{D}})
, where

•

X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}
is a finite-dimensional space on
{\mathbb{R}}
, associated with the interior and boundary degrees of freedoms and taking into account the zero boundary conditions,

•

\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}:X_{{\mathcal{D}}}\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is a linear reconstructed function,

•

\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}:X_{{\mathcal{D}}}\to L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
is a linear reconstructed gradient and must be defined so that
\|\cdot||_{\mathcal{D}}:=||\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\cdot||_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}
defines a norm on
X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}
,

•

J_{\mathcal{D}}:L^{\infty}(\Omega)\to X_{\mathcal{D}}
is a linear and continuous interpolation operator for the initial conditions.

The coercivity, the consistency and the dual consistency of the discretisation are respectively controlled by the parameter
C_{\mathcal{D}}
, the interpolation error function
S_{\mathcal{D}}:H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)
, and the conformity error function
W_{\mathcal{D}}:H_{\rm{div}}(\Omega)=\{{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}{\;:\;}{\rm div}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}\to[0,+\infty)
; they are defined by

C_{\mathcal{D}}={\displaystyle\max_{w\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}}},

(2.2)

S_{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi)=\min_{w\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}}\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w-\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}},\mbox{ and }

(2.3)

W_{\mathcal{D}}({\boldsymbol{\varphi}})=\sup_{w\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}}\Big|\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w\cdot{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}+\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w\cdot\mathrm{div}({\boldsymbol{\varphi}}))\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\Big|.

(2.4)

From the definition of
C_{\mathcal{D}}
, we can derive the following discrete Poincaré inequality, for all
w\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0}
,

\|\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\mathcal{D}}\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}.

(2.5)

Using an implicit Euler scheme in time and the space-time gradient discretisation illustrated above, we can define a generic discrete scheme of the problem (
2.1
) called a gradient scheme. In what follows, let
t^{(0)}=0<t^{(1)}<....<t^{(N)}=T

(N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\star})
be a discrete times and we set the time step
\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}:=t^{(n+1)}-t^{(n)}
,
n\in\{0,...,N-1\}

Definition 2.3

(Gradient Scheme)
.

If

is a space-time gradient discretisation defined in Definition

, the corressponding gradient scheme for the problem (
2.1
) is seeking

and for all

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}u^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})-\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}u^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{x}))\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}u^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

(2.6a)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F_{1}(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}u^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x}),\Pi_{{\mathcal{D}}}v^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x}))\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\quad\forall\varphi\in X_{{\mathcal{D}},0},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta t^{(n+\frac{1}{2})}}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}v^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})-\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}v^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{x}))\Pi_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x})+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Lambda_{2}(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}v^{(n+1)}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(\boldsymbol{x})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

(2.6b)

3.
Convergence rates

Here, we state the main results of our contribution along with error estimates. The established convergence rates depend on the space size and the time discretisation. The convergence rates relative to space size depend on the three indicators
C_{\mathcal{D}}
,
S_{\mathcal{D}}
and
W_{\mathcal{D}}
. Let
h_{\mathcal{D}}
denote the space size defined in
[
10
, Definition 2.22]
. With respect to the continuously embedded spaces

and

h_{\mathcal{D}}:=\displaystyle\max\Big(\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{S_{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi)}{\|\varphi\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}},\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{W_{\mathcal{D}}({\boldsymbol{\varphi}})}{\|{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}}}\Big),

and it therefore satisfies

\forall\varphi\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\quad S_{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi)\leq h_{\mathcal{D}}\|\varphi\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)},

(3.1a)

\forall{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d},\quad W_{\mathcal{D}}({\boldsymbol{\varphi}})\leq h_{\mathcal{D}}\|{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)^{d}}.

(3.1b)

It is worth mentioning that

, Lemma 1]
provides us with a linear spatial interpolator

(3.2)

and it meets the following relation

Let

and the interpolant

\displaystyle-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla_{\mathcal{D}}P_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi-\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle v(x,y,t)

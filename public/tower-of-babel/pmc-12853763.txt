Elderly welfare programs and depressive symptoms in China: cross-sectional evidence from CHARLS 2020
Authors: Hui Yang, Zhan Shu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThere is limited evidence in China regarding the relationship between welfare programs and the depressive symptoms of the older adults. This study aims to explore the relationships between three different welfare programs and the depressive symptoms of older adults and to compare the potential pathways through which family support and social participation may operate.MethodsThis study utilized data from the 2020 China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), including 7773 Chinese individuals aged 60 and above. The depressive symptoms were assessed using the 10-item Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CES-D10). Welfare programs were measured through dummy variables indicating individual participation in these programs. The relationship was examined using multiple regression linear models. Mediation analysis was employed to test the mediating roles of family support and social participation.ResultsThe old age allowance was negatively correlated with the depressive symptoms of older adults (r=-0.859, 95% CI [-1.346, -0.371], P < 0.01), mediated by economic support that older adults provide to their adult children. Further investigation revealed that this negative correlation was more evident among older women, agricultural older adults. A negative relationship between the one-child family allowance and older adults’ depressive symptoms(r=-0.696, 95%CI [-1.228, -0.165], P < 0.05) was associated with one child’s visitation and life care. This association was significant among agricultural, married/cohabiting older adults. The subsistence allowance was positively associated with the depressive symptoms of older adults, due to the mediation of economic support to children and children’s visitation (r = 1.737, 95%CI [1.192, 2.282], P < 0.01).ConclusionThe elderly welfare programs were strongly linked to depressive symptoms among older adults, with different welfare programs exhibiting varying degrees of association with these symptoms. Different dimensions of family support played a mediating role, while social participation did not exhibit a mediating effect. Rural and older women’s depressive symptoms were more significantly associated with the welfare programs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40359-025-03811-9.Keywords: Older adult, Depressive symptoms, Welfare program, Family support, Social participation

Introduction

In recent years, China’s aging population has gradually deepened. By the end of 2023, individuals aged 60 and above accounted for 21.1% of China’s total population, with those aged 65 and above comprising 15.4% [1]. The issue of mental health among older adults in China could not be ignored. According to a survey on “The Mental Health Status of Chinese Older Adults”, it was shown that 19.05% of older adults were in a mild depressive state, while 12.17% experienced moderate to high levels of depressive emotions [2]. In order to safeguard the life of older adults effectively, China has established a multifaceted welfare system for the older adults, which primarily includes social pension and medical insurance programs to ensure basic living and healthcare needs. There are various allowance programs like one child family allowance, old age allowance, subsistence allowance program, long-term care insurance, elderly service subsidy, social elderly care service system as well as other support measures involving discounts on transportation and tourism ticket. Welfare programs were identified as a key factor influencing mental health, influencing individuals’ economical status, thereby affecting mental health through material, social, psychological, and behavioral pathways.

Extensive research has investigated the relationship between welfare programs and the depressive symptom. Studies examining welfare system transitions in various countries found that generous welfare programs could enhance the mental health of older adults. Conversely, austerity measures in welfare systems could exacerbate mental health inequalities and worsen mental health outcomes [3]. Among the various elderly welfare programs, national pension insurance is one of the most generous. Older adults who receive pension benefits had higher levels of mental well-being compared to those without such benefits [4–6]. The improvement in mental health was more significant for rural older adults receiving pensions than those only making contributions [7, 8]. Mutual family support between older adults and their living together children has statistical significance in reducing the loneliness of parents [9]. Pension insurance reduced economic transfers from adult children to parents, weakening emotional comfort and replacing familial caregiving [10–16]. Moreover, the poor elderly could face larger reductions in the amount of economic transfers [17]. However, pension insurance increased economic support and care provided by the older generation to the third generation, leading to increased financial assistance and life care from adult children to their parents in return [18]. Pension insurance could promote social participation by increasing personal income alleviating income inequality and improving physical health, and yet it might also inhibit social participation by increasing non-agricultural labor supply [19, 20].

Older adults with medical insurance and access to free healthcare exhibited higher levels of mental health compared to those without such a system [6], particularly in rural elderly populations [21, 22]. Medical insurance released older adults of healthcare service demands and increased economic transfers from adult children to the older adults [23] and had negative effects on social participation [24].

Additionally, providing effective physical and mental health care for older adults and offering palliative treatments could significantly reduce the probability of older adults seeking suicide [25]. In addition to China, among the Nordic social democratic welfare system, longer maternity leave for women, and family models where both spouses share the responsibility of child-rearing can alleviate stress and discrimination, and reduce poverty, thereby improving women’s mental health [26].

However, welfare programs do not always improve mental health. People receiving unemployment or disability benefits tend to have poorer mental health than when they are not receiving benefits. This may be due to the sense of psychological helplessness created by dependence on welfare, or welfare discrimination and other forms of social exclusion [27]. Delayed retirement policies, one means by which countries reduce welfare benefits, have been found to decrease the mental health of older women in the UK [28].

In the context of the rapidly widening income gap between urban and rural areas in China, this article also finds it necessary to discuss the heterogeneous impact of welfare programs on the depressive symptoms of older adults in urban and rural settings. In addition to income, there are significant urban-rural disparities in the allocation of elderly welfare resources. Urban areas have a higher density of elderly care institutions, well-developed community service facilities such as senior dining halls, and higher pension replacement rates. In contrast, rural areas face a shortage of elderly care facilities, and older adults are more reliant on their children for support. Starting from the differences in welfare, the issue can be expanded to the broader perspective of urban-rural development imbalance, which can, in turn, promote greater attention from the government and society towards achieving balanced welfare between urban and rural areas.

There are still three significant limitations, while existing research has already focused on the impact of elderly welfare programs on depressive symptoms. First, existing research mainly focused on welfare programs like social pension and medical insurance with high participation rates and their impact on the depressive symptoms of older adults. There is limited research on the effects of low-coverage elderly welfare programs (old age allowance, the one-child family allowance, and the subsistence allowance) on the depressive symptoms of older adults; Second, researches on welfare programs are mostly focused on rural older adults, with insufficient comparative analysis of the differences in welfare and health effects between urban and rural elderly populations. This limitation makes it difficult to reflect the impact of regional differences in welfare programs. Third, existing research has confirmed that family support and labor supply play mediating roles in the relationship between elderly welfare programs and depressive symptoms. However, few studies have yet explored family support and social participation within the same analytical framework.

To address the current research gap, this study contributed to existing literature in several ways: firstly, it examined the of less-studied welfare programs such as the old age allowance program, the one-child family allowance, and the subsistence allowance on the depressive symptoms of older adults. Secondly, it compared different groups older adults (gender, hukou, marital status) to test the heterogeneous effects of welfare programs on mental health levels. Thirdly, the paper explores the mediating effect of family support and social participation in explaining the relationship between elderly welfare programs and depressive symptoms.

Methods

Data and sample

This study utilized data from the 2020 China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) to examine the association between welfare programs and older adults’ depressive symptoms via multiple linear regression models and mediation analysis, with recognition of its cross-sectional nature. CHARLS is a nationwide longitudinal survey conducted by the China Social Survey Center at Peking University, targeting households and individuals aged 45 and above, providing nationally representative longitudinal data. The first national survey of CHARLS was conducted in 2011, covering 150 county-level units, 450 village-level units, and approximately 17,000 individuals from 10,000 households. CHARLS employed multi-stage sampling using Probability Proportional to Size (PPS) sampling method both at the county/district and village levels [29]. In November 2023, the fifth wave of data collection (2020) was publicly released.

The total number of original samples is 19,395. According to the research needs, 11,622 participants were excluded because they were under 60 years old, the depressive symptoms had missing values and sample matching failure. In addition, missing values were handled as follows: samples with income coded as “−1” (invalid data) and missing values for depressive symptoms or variables with < 5% missing rate (e.g., hukou, social participation, ADL and family support) were excluded; educational level and chronic disease status were imputed using non-missing data from the previous CHARLS wave.

Variables

Depressive symptoms

Depressive symptoms are an indicator of mental health which is primarily assessed using the Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CES-D10) developed by Radloff in 1977, one of the most widely used scales for assessing depressive symptoms [30]. The scale consists of 10 items related to emotions experienced by the older adults such as annoyance over trivial matters, difficulty concentrating, feeling downhearted, among others, along with positive emotions indicating hope for the future and happiness. The reliability of the CES-D10 scale is 0.779, indicating that the scale has good reliability and can be used to accurately assess depressive symptoms. It is common practice in this field to regard depressive symptoms as continuous variables. Each item is scored on a 4-point scale, with total scores ranging from 0 to 30, where higher scores indicate worse depressive symptoms.

Welfare program

The study’s assessment of welfare program participation was primarily based on self-reported items from the CHARLS questionnaire. The study primarily focused on programs such as the old age allowance, the one-child family allowance, and the subsistence allowance. The old age allowance program is a cash transfer program aimed at improving the quality of life for elderly seniors. The criteria for issuing this policy are determined by each locality based on their economic development level and financial situation; The one-child family allowance program is a reward program for families with only one child since the introduction of the family planning policy. The allowance may be paid out to the couples with one child upon retirement either as a monthly allowance or as a lump-sum payment. The subsistence allowance program is a social assistance program aimed at ensuring the basic living standards of impoverished populations. The program provides a monthly subsistence allowance with special assistance for families living below the poverty line.

The CHARLS questionnaire asked respondents: “Have you received the kind of transfer payment in the past year?” “Have you received the subsistence allowance in the past year?” If an elderly individual receives benefits from a particular program, the variable is assigned a value of 1; otherwise, it is assigned a value of 0. To normalize the data, the logarithm of each welfare amount received by the older adults was calculated after adding 1.

Family support and social participation

Mediation variables include family support and social participation. Family support is the multidimensional interaction among family members that encompasses emotional, informational, and instrumental aspects [31]. It encompasses economic support, life care, and children’s visitation. Economic support is represented as a continuous numerical variable, combining the economic support given to and received from adult children by summing all financial and material contributions recorded in the questionnaire. Life care is a binary variable denoting whether family members take care of older adults (assigned a value of 1) or not (assigned a value of 0). Adult children’s visitation represents the concern of children for the older adults, when the children and parents communicate at least once a week including meetings, phone calls and text message, assigned a value of 1; otherwise, assigned a value of 0. Social participation is a person’s involvement in social activities that provide social interactions within his/her community or society [32]. It is measured by the number of social activities participated in, including: “interacted with friends; played mahjong, chess or cards, or went to community club; provided help to family, friends, or neighbors who do not live with you; went to a sport, social, or other kind of club; took part in a community-related organization, done voluntary or charity work, of cared for a sick or disabled adult who does not live with you; attended an educational or training course; others”.

Control variables were selected to account for factors that could influence the mental health of older adults, aiming to avoid endogeneity issues stemming from omitted variables. Variables at the individual level primarily include: age, gender, residence, hukou, education level, marital status, employment status, chronic disease and activity of daily living (ADL) and personal income. Education level is categorized into three types include primary school and below, middle school, high school and college or above. Residence is a dummy variable indicating whether the individual lives at home or not. Hukou is a binary variable including two types: agricultural and non-agricultural. ADL is measured through scoring six activities: bathing or showering, getting into or out of bed, using the toilet, eating, dressing, and controlling urination and defecation with each item scored on a 1–4 scale. Personal income is defined as the sum of wage income and pension income. The household-level variables include family size and household living expenses.

Empirical mode

Least squares method

This study applies the Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) multiple linear regression analysis to investigate the association between elderly welfare programs with depressive symptoms of older adults. The association between welfare programs and depressive symptoms of older adults

Table 2 reports the relationship between various elderly welfare programs and the depressive symptoms of older adults. Older adults receiving the old age allowance had lower depressive symptom scores by 0.859 compared to those who do not benefit from this welfare program (95%CI [−1.346, −0.371]). The connection between the one-child family allowance program and depressive symptoms was relatively weak (β=−0.696, 95%CI [−1.228, −0.165]). Moreover, older adults receiving subsistence allowance had 1.737-point higher depressive symptom score than those without this welfare(95%CI [1.192, 2.282]). The above results indicated that welfare programs were closely related to depressive symptoms, and further exploration of the underlying pathways is needed. This study employed robust standard errors, with a variance inflation factor of 1.19 below 5, indicating that multicollinearity is not a significant concern in our models.

Mediation analysis

Table 5 reported the association between elderly welfare programs on family support and social participation among older adults. The logarithmic value of economic support provided by parents receiving old age allowance to their adult children significantly increased by 0.564(95%CI [0.261, 0.867]). This May indicate that parents become more financially comfortable after receiving the subsidies and are more willing to transfer resources to their adult children. After parents receive the one-child family allowance, the logarithmic value of the financial support provided by adult child to their parents significantly decreased by 2.419 units(95%CI [−2.789, −2.050]). The policy May compensate one-child families, partially replacing the adult children’s financial responsibility for their parents. However, the frequency of children visiting parents weekly for their parents significantly increased by 28.9%(95%CI [0.242, 0.335]). The underlying logic might be that the “one-child family” label reinforces the only child’s sense of filial duty, or that the policy’s promotion encourages emotional care from the child. The receipt of subsistence allowance significantly reduced the logarithmic value of the economic support parents provide to their children by 0.614 compared to those who do not receive the allowance(95%CI [−0.897, −0.332]). The probability of receiving visits from children decreased by 3.9%(95%CI [−0.067, −0.012]). The underlying reasons May be that after receiving the allowance, older adults have some level of self-care ability, leading their children to feel that “parents no longer require frequent visits”. As a result, adult children opt to work away from home, reducing the frequency of visits, and consequently, parents provide less financial support to their children.

In summary, our findings suggest that stronger emotional family interactions and greater social participation are associated with better mental well-being in older adults. In practice, visits and close relationships with children fulfill parents’ emotional needs, and engagement in social activities helps older adults gain friendships and new skills, which further supports their mental health. Conversely, reliance on family life care may lead some older adults to feel that they have “lost the ability to live independently” or have “become a burden to the family”, potentially resulting in negative mental effects.

Mediation analysis suggested that family support may function as a mediation linking elderly welfare programs to depressive symptoms. But it must be pointed out that the above mediation analysis can only prove the correlation rather than confirm the causal path. Parental financial support to children plays a mediating role in the association between old age allowance and depressive symptoms of older adults. In addition, children’s visitation and life care play a mediating role in correlation between the one-child family allowance and the depressive symptoms of older adults. The one-child allowance provides child with holidays or options for family responsibilities, beneficial for improving the mental health of older adults. The positive correlation between subsistence allowance and the depressive symptoms of older adults may potentially be mediated by factors such as visits from children, and financial support provided by parents to their children.

Heterogeneity analysis of gender, hukou, and marital status

To better examine the heterogeneity in the association between elderly welfare programs and depressive symptoms, this study employs a combination of grouped regression and marginal effect plots of interaction terms. Table 7 visually presents the differences in the relationship between elderly welfare programs and depressive symptoms across various dimensions, while Figs. 1, 2 and 3 further quantify the strength and statistical significance of heterogeneity. The old age allowance was negatively associated with depressive symptoms among older adults who are female, rural. Previous studies have indicated a higher prevalence of depression among rural elderly women compared to men, highlighting the significant practical implications of positive effects on the mental health of this group [35]. The one-child family allowance was negatively correlated with the depressive symptoms of married individuals from agricultural areas. Although the subsistence allowance was significantly correlated with the depressive symptoms of participants of different genders, the correlation was stronger among women. Additionally, it significantly correlated with the mental health among rural residents and those who are married or cohabiting.

Compared to older adults from urban areas, older adults from rural areas often face limited economic development and continue engaging in agricultural labor, resulting in constrained income. Consequently, cash transfer payments become a more critical supplement to their economic resources. Rural women, in particular, tend to earn less than men and occupy a disadvantaged position in household resource allocation, making them more reliant on transfer payments. As a result, their depressive symptoms were more closely tied to the elderly welfare programs.

Discussion and conclusion

This study used the OLS method to investigate the relationship between three elderly welfare programs and depressive symptoms among the older adults. Therefore, this study found that receiving the old age allowance was negatively associated with depressive symptoms, and the association was mediated by the older adults’ financial support to their children. The one-child family allowance showed a certain degree of association with depressive symptoms. In contrast, the subsistence allowance was positively associated with depressive symptoms, mainly due to the weakening of economic support and reduced visits from children. Furthermore, heterogeneity analysis reveals that the old age allowance was significantly negatively associated with the depressive symptoms of female, rural older adults. Rural elderly individuals who receive the one-child family allowance have worse depressive symptoms than those in urban areas. The subsistence allowance exhibited a more significant correlation with the depressive symptoms of female, rural, and married or cohabiting older adults.

The correlation between the old age allowance and depressive symptoms of the older adults was negatively significant, particularly among agricultural women. This finding is consistent with a study based on 2011–2018 CHARLS data, further highlighting the importance of the old age allowance program. However, this study differs by discovering that the old age allowance increases the likelihood of older adults participating in outdoor activities [36], whereas our study finds no correlation between the allowance and social participation. Since the background of this study is 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic in China, most of individuals reduced their frequency of going out, thereby decreasing normal social activities. This may lead to the relatively low level of social participation measured in this study among the older adults, which fails to function as social support and effectively alleviate their loneliness.

Research has found that welfare assistance programs in economic surveys across countries generally exhibit the phenomenon of welfare stigma [33, 37]. In China, recipients of the subsistence allowance program have lower mental health than non-recipients [38]. Stigmatization manifests in two aspects: on one hand, self-stigmatization, where applicants often compromise their family privacy and self-esteem when applying for this welfare; on the other hand, social stigma from members of society, who tend to perceive the assisted as having a lower social status in daily interactions. The decline in life satisfaction and happiness caused by welfare stigma may lead to symptoms of severe depression [39].

A critical area requiring further exploration is the complex interplay between welfare stigma and family support in shaping mental health. First, welfare benefits like subsistence allowances are often perceived as indicators of “inadequate capabilities”, creating stigma. Family members may worry that relying on subsistence allowance could diminish their social standing, leading to reduced visits and emotional communication. This stigma consequently weakens familial support, exacerbating depressive symptoms in older adults. Second, the absence of family support and welfare stigma often coexist. Many elderly parents, lacking financial support from children, must rely on subsistence allowance to meet basic needs while enduring social stigma through others’ judgment. This dual pressure limits their social interactions, making emotional support from children particularly vital. However, in order to deeply understand the attitude of family members towards their parents’ receiving subsistence allowance, we need to do more qualitative interviews.

This study focuses on the association and heterogeneity between the elderly welfare programs and depressive symptoms, which not only directly responds to the requirement of “promoting healthy aging and implementing mental health services for the elderly” in the “Outline of the Healthy China 2030 Plan” but also provides empirical support for promoting the goal of healthy aging through the social security system. The findings of this study have significant policy implications. There is a negative correlation between old age allowance and the depressive symptoms of older adults. To leverage this benefit, the government should strive to achieve full coverage of the old age allowance for adults over the age of 80. This can be accomplished by increasing governmental transfer payments to support rural areas, thereby promoting the widespread adoption of this program in rural regions. Currently, older adults receiving subsistence allowances had worse depressive symptoms, and more efforts should be made to provide psychological care services to them. For future policy, the government should improve family welfare policies that encourage visit between parents and children, for example, ensuring the welfare benefits for older adults who relocate to live with their child and providing family visit leave to the only child.

Limitations

This study had several limitations. Firstly, because the study is a cross-sectional study, the estimation results cannot effectively explain causal relationships of the study variables. Secondly, although this study controlled for observable variables such as age, chronic diseases, and family size, some unobservable factors that are difficult to quantify may still influence the results. For example, psychological trauma in the older adults, latent family conflicts, and undiagnosed chronic pain are variables that cannot be directly measured through questionnaires. Moreover, due to constraints within the CHARLS questionnaire, we were unable to examine the impact of other welfare programs on the mental health of older adults, despite previous studies showing the positive effects of the long-term care insurance system [40]. In addition to cash benefits, elderly welfare also includes other services such as daily care, rehabilitation, cultural entertainment and so on. Unfortunately, the questionnaire from CHARLS 2020 did not collect relevant information, so this paper cannot explore its relationship with the depressive symptoms of older adults. Finally, the mediating variables are measured by the respondents’ self-report, but these variables can be biased due to the respondents’ inaccurate memory or deliberate concealment of the truth.

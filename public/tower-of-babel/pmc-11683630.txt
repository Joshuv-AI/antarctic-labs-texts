Analysis of an Unsaturated Seepage Mechanism in Coal Seam Water Injection
Authors: Wan Jiang, Zhenhua Li, Jianping Wei, Leilei Si, Yang Su
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe wetting process of coal seam water injection is a typical unsaturated flow, but the steady-state method for measuring liquid permeability cannot reflect the unsaturated flow process of water in coal. The principle of wetting and expanding process of liquid in coal medium is very complex, and the multiscale pore characteristics of coal make the liquid permeability show multiscale characteristics. Therefore, liquid-phase triaxial seepage experiments under different working conditions are carried out, and a one-way multiscale dynamic apparent permeability coefficient model D(t) = D0 exp(−βt) is established to analyze the influence of pressure, liquid wettability, and pore structure on the unsaturated wetting process of coal seam water injection. The results show that the apparent permeability coefficient of liquid phase decreases gradually with time due to the multiscale pore characteristics of coal. The dynamic apparent permeability coefficient model can well describe the unsaturated seepage process of liquid in coal, which is helpful to characterize the liquid permeability of low permeability coal seam. The water injection pressure has a great influence on the attenuation coefficient of apparent permeability coefficient, and the higher the water pressure is, the smaller the attenuation coefficient is. The surfactant increases the permeability coefficient by enhancing the capillary wetting, while the capillary force, as an internal factor, has less influence on the model attenuation coefficient than the water pressure.

Introduction

Coal seam water injection is one of the important means to prevent dust pollution, coal and gas outburst, cooling, and rockburst in coal mines.1−3 In the process of water injection, water moves along the holes and fractures in coal, so as to achieve the effect of wetting and dust suppression.4 This process is a typical transition from unsaturated flow to steady flow, during which the water saturation of the coal gradually increases.5,6 At present, the characterization of the liquid phase permeability is mostly based on saturated flow. Therefore, exploring the unsaturated flow laws and influencing factors of coal seam water injection is of great significance to enriching the theory of liquid phase flow in porous media and revealing the water injection mechanism.

In general, the gas-phase permeability is used to characterize the inherent permeability of porous media. The liquid phase permeability is much lower than the gas phase permeability because of the liquid–solid interface coupling and the influence of water film to form a larger viscous resistance.7,8 The liquid phase permeability of coal is usually calculated by the steady state method according to Darcy’s law.9 The coal body is composed of fissures and bedrock pores, and the fracture permeability is much higher than that in the bedrock.10,11 Therefore, the measurement of permeability by the steady state method is based on the steady flow described by Darcy’s law, which cannot reflect the diffusion of fluid in pores.12 Liu Zhen13 establishes the permeability model of coal based on the volume fractal dimension of coal, which indicates that the structural fractal dimension increases, the flow resistance increases, and the theoretical permeability decreases. Yang He14−16 and Cheng Weimin17 analyzed the fractal characteristics of the effective seepage channel structure of coal body combined with fractal geometry theory. The results show that the pore structure affecting the seepage characteristics of water injection coal is mainly concentrated in the scale of macropore and medium pore. They built a mathematical model describing nonlinear seepage of water injection in gassy seams based on experimental data. Fu Wei18 studied the relationship between the fractal dimension of coal pores and the seepage and diffusion properties. Xiao Qianhua19 studied the water invasion characteristics using nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) online detection technology. The above research shows that the multiscale characteristics of pores have an important impact on liquid phase flow, but it is difficult to describe this complex impact by the steady-state method, especially for low-permeability coal seams. The process of expanding seepage passage and enhancing wetting degree by coal seam water injection is a unique form of coal medium, and its principle is very complicated.20 Due to the coexistence of fissures and matrix in coal seam, the forms of liquid flow include seepage, capillary diffusion and adsorbed water.21 Therefore, the liquid phase permeability of coal seam water injection shows multiscale characteristics in time and space. It is of great significance to judge the wetting degree of coal by studying the permeability of the unsaturated flow process.

In addition, Zhou22,23 studied the effects of capillary force and salinity on self-absorption. The results show that capillary force is the control mechanism in sandstone and carbonate rocks, and the permeation and diffusion effect in clay shale cannot be ignored. Yang He24 and Li Bo25 studied the effect of surfactant on liquid distribution in coal pores. According to the unidirectional seepage experiment of cylindrical coal samples, when the cracks are well developed, the pressure water can move from one end of the coal sample to the other along the cracks.26 However, in this case, the infiltration of water into the tiny voids is relatively small. On the other hand, when the coal sample fissures are less, the resistance of water migration in the coal is larger, and the capillary infiltration into the microvoid occurs.27,28 In this case, the change of water injection flow is affected by water pressure and capillary permeability, which comprehensively reflects the multiscale seepage characteristics of liquid. Therefore, it is considered that the effect of capillarity in unsaturated flow cannot be ignored.

In order to understand the seepage characteristics of liquid in the process of unsaturated flow, considering the multiscale characteristics of liquid seepage, a unidirectional multiscale dynamic permeability model of cylindrical coal core was established based on the change of moisture content in time and space. The triaxial percolation experiments under different water injection pressures and different wettability liquids conditions were carried out. Through theoretical and experimental studies, the multiscale dynamic apparent permeability coefficients under different experimental conditions were obtained. The effects of the pore structure and liquid wettability on liquid seepage in coal were compared and analyzed.

Experimental Method

Coal seam water injection is a typical process from an unsaturated state to a saturated state, and unsaturated flow is the main process of water injection wetting. The flow information on liquid can be obtained by carrying out triaxial seepage experiment. Meanwhile, the influence of the coal sample and liquid properties should be considered comprehensively. The pore structure of the coal sample was determined by the low-field NMR analyzer. Modeling

Based on the triaxial seepage experiment, the liquid phase unsaturated flow process of cylindrical coal core was considered, and the dynamic apparent permeability coefficient model was constructed based on the change of water content in the axial direction.

Basic Assumptions

Li Zhiqiang29 put forward a new physical model of multiscale pore distribution of coal particles. The new model assumes that the pores in coal particles show heterogeneous multiscale morphological distribution and have a self-similar fractal structure. For coal samples with specific pore structures, it is difficult to distinguish the degrees of seepage and imbibition during unsaturated flow. The mathematical forms of the basic equations of infiltration and seepage are the same, so they are considered to be able to reflect the influence of multiscale characteristics of coal pores.11 In laboratory experiments, water penetrates from one end of the coal sample to the other, and it is usually easy to obtain the cumulative flow into the coal, which is conducive to the solution of the imbition equation. Therefore, the infiltration equation based on the experimental results is more convenient to solve, and the coefficient in the equation is called the dynamic apparent permeability coefficient.

Based on the above analysis, the following assumptions are given: (1) when the liquid flows axially through the cylindrical coal core, it is an unsteady unidirectional flow with or without load. (2) The coal core is anisotropic. The permeability coefficient of the cylindrical coal core is independent of the type of permeable material in coal and has a negative exponential relationship with time. It is defined as unidirectional multiscale dynamic apparent permeability coefficient. (3) According to the experimental conditions, the outlet flow is regarded as a constant equal to 0.

Analysis and Discussion

Experimental Results of Seepage Under Various Water Pressure

Through the triaxial seepage experiment of a cylindrical coal core, the changes of the cumulative flow rate of water into the coal sample under different conditions were obtained. The triaxial seepage experimental results of coal samples #C1 and #C2 are shown in Figure 4a,b. With the increase of water injection time, the cumulative flow rate gradually increases and its growth rate decreases, which means that the instantaneous flow rate decreases gradually. This is because the seepage channel is occupied by degrees and the flow resistance increases with the rising water saturation of coal samples. The movement power of water in the coal seam mainly includes external water injection pressure and internal pore capillary force. The change of water pressure, which is the main driving force, has an obvious influence on the movement of liquid. With the increase of water pressure, the flow rate of injected coal increases, and the flow velocity increases. As shown in Figure 4a, when the water injection pressure is 6 MPa, there is water outflow at the end of the coal sample after 35 min, and the cumulative flow increases linearly. The flow of water in the coal sample enters the stage of steady seepage at this time. When the water pressure is 2 and 4 MPa, the cumulative flow curve shows a convex shape, which means that the coal sample is always in the unsaturated flow stage during the 60 min experimental process. Similarly, the seepage process of coal sample #C2 in Figure 4b is in the unsaturated seepage stage. The volume of water entering the coal body is obviously different, and the pore structures of coal samples #C1 and #C2 are different. For coal samples with low permeability, the permeability in the unsaturated flow stage can better reflect the water injection effect.

The preferential movement of water in the dominant river channel weakens the multiscale effect of water transport, which is reflected in the fact that the permeability coefficient tends to be constant and the attenuation coefficient is small.

Experimental Results of Seepage with Surfactants

In addition to the effect of the water pressure on the process of liquid flow, capillarity is another dynamic factor affecting liquid flow. The purpose of coal seam water injection with a surfactant is to enhance the capillary wetting effect of liquid so that the distribution of coal seam water is more uniform and the wetting effect is better. When the experiments of water injection and surfactant injection are carried out for the same coal sample under the same stress and water injection pressure, to a large extent, the difference only comes from the change of liquid properties. Figure 7a,b shows the cumulative flow for water injection and surfactant injection, respectively.

As shown in Figure 7, the cumulative flow shows an upward convex curve, which means that there is no liquid outflow from the end of the coal sample during the 60 min experiment. Therefore, the flow of liquid in the coal sample belongs to the unsaturated flow process. Under the same conditions, the cumulative flow rate of the surfactant is higher than that of water. It is worth noting that the boost curve shows that the pressure of the surfactant is slightly lower than that of the water during the pressurization process but the flow growth rate of the injected surfactant is higher than that of the water at this stage. Analysis of the Influence of Sample Characteristics

The influence analysis of sample characteristics can be divided into the influence of the pore structure of the coal sample and the wettability of liquid for water injection. Coal exhibits different pore structure distributions and surface physicochemical properties during its formation process.32,33 The pore structure distribution of coal samples #C1 and #C2 is shown in Figure 10. According to the Khodot’s aperture classification method,34 the ranges of micropores, transition pores, medium pores, and macropores are marked in Figure 10. As shown in Figure 10, the total porosity of coal sample #C1 is higher than that of coal sample #C2, while the distribution characteristics of each pore diameter are different. Specifically, the porosity component of coal sample #C1 in the micropore and transition pore ranges is lower than that of coal sample #C2. In the mesoporous range, the porosity component of coal sample C1 is higher. However, the signal intensity of coal sample #C2 in this range is very weak; therefore, the porosity component is nearly close to 0. The porosity component of coal sample #C2 is higher in the macropore range. Macropores are the dominant channels of liquid seepage. From the distribution characteristics of the above pore structure, the transition pores and macropores of coal sample #C2 are basically discontinuous, resulting in relatively poor pore connectivity. Therefore, even if the porosity component of macropores of coal sample #C2 is higher, it is not conducive to the flow of liquid.

Pore size distribution of coal samples.

Figure 11 shows the average instantaneous flow rate (dQ/dt) in the last 30 min, initial apparent permeability coefficient D0, and attenuation coefficient β of coal samples #C1 and #C2 during the triaxial seepage experiment. Combined with the difference analysis of pore structure characteristics in coal samples, it is known that the better the pore connectivity, the greater the instantaneous flow rate, that is, the greater the seepage velocity, and the smaller the attenuation coefficient β of the apparent permeability coefficient. When the apparent permeability coefficient approaches to a constant, it shows that the multiscale characteristic of fluid motion is relatively weak. For the coal sample #C2 with poor connectivity, the instantaneous flow rate of water injection is smaller and the attenuation coefficient is larger, indicating that the multiscale characteristic of fluid movement in a low permeability coal seam is more obvious. In addition, the initial apparent permeability coefficient D0 of coal sample #C2 is slightly higher than that of coal sample #C1, which is due to the higher macropores porosity component of coal sample #C2.

Conclusions

In this paper, the liquid phase triaxial seepage experiment is carried out based on the unsaturated flow process, and a one-way multiscale dynamic apparent permeability coefficient model is proposed. This model was compared with the experimental results, and the apparent permeability coefficients affected by the pore structure and surfactant were calculated. The main conclusions are as follows:

(1)The unsaturated flow process of liquid in a coal sample is regarded as the diffusion process of water, and the one-way apparent permeability coefficient model of liquid is constructed based on the form of diffusion equation. The dynamic apparent permeability coefficient D(t) = D0 exp(−βt) reflects the flow characteristics of liquid in a multiscale pore structure, and it attenuates with the increase of water injection time, which is consistent with the experimental results. The permeability measured by a steady-state method cannot reflect this characteristic.

The unsaturated flow process of liquid in a coal sample is regarded as the diffusion process of water, and the one-way apparent permeability coefficient model of liquid is constructed based on the form of diffusion equation. The dynamic apparent permeability coefficient D(t) = D0 exp(−βt) reflects the flow characteristics of liquid in a multiscale pore structure, and it attenuates with the increase of water injection time, which is consistent with the experimental results. The permeability measured by a steady-state method cannot reflect this characteristic.

(2)For the coal with poor pore connectivity, the seepage speed is slow. At this time, the model describes the experimental results well, and the apparent permeability coefficient decreases obviously with time. The higher the water pressure is, the larger the apparent permeability coefficient is and the smaller the absolute value of the attenuation coefficient is. For the coal with good connectivity, the flow state becomes stable seepage with the increase of water injection pressure, and the apparent permeability coefficient is close to constant. Therefore, the model can better describe the unsaturated flow process of low permeability coal and reflect the multiscale permeability of coal.

For the coal with poor pore connectivity, the seepage speed is slow. At this time, the model describes the experimental results well, and the apparent permeability coefficient decreases obviously with time. The higher the water pressure is, the larger the apparent permeability coefficient is and the smaller the absolute value of the attenuation coefficient is. For the coal with good connectivity, the flow state becomes stable seepage with the increase of water injection pressure, and the apparent permeability coefficient is close to constant. Therefore, the model can better describe the unsaturated flow process of low permeability coal and reflect the multiscale permeability of coal.

(3)For the same coal sample under the same stress condition, the influence of the change of liquid property on the attenuation coefficient is limited. Therefore, the model reflects the intrinsic permeability of coal samples. However, due to the enhancement of capillary permeability, the wetting scale of liquid increases, and the apparent permeability coefficient of liquid under the condition of surfactant injection is relatively large.

However, due to the enhancement of capillary permeability, the wetting scale of liquid increases, and the apparent permeability coefficient of liquid under the condition of surfactant injection is relatively large.

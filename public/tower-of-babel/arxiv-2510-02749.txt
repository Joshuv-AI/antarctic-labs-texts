On d d -distance p p -packing domination number in strong products
Csilla Bujtás, Vesna Iršič Chenoweth, Sandi Klavžar, Gang Zhang

Abstract
The d d -distance p p -packing domination number γ d p ​ ( G ) \gamma_{d}^{p}(G) of a graph G G is the cardinality of a smallest set of vertices of G G which is both a d d -distance dominating set and a p p -packing. If no such set exists, then we set γ d p ​ ( G ) = ∞ \gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty . For an arbitrary strong product G ⊠ H G\boxtimes H it is proved that γ d p ​ ( G ⊠ H ) ≤ γ d p ​ ( G ) ​ γ d p ​ ( H ) \gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes H)\leq\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)\gamma_{d}^{p}(H) . By proving that γ d p ​ ( P m ⊠ P n ) = ⌈ m 2 ​ d + 1 ⌉ ​ ⌈ n 2 ​ d + 1 ⌉ \gamma_{d}^{p}(P_{m}\boxtimes P_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\right\rceil\left\lceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\right\rceil , and that if γ d p ​ ( C n ) < ∞ \gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{n})<\infty , then γ d p ​ ( P m ⊠ C n ) = ⌈ m 2 ​ d + 1 ⌉ ​ ⌈ n 2 ​ d + 1 ⌉ \gamma_{d}^{p}(P_{m}\boxtimes C_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\right\rceil\left\lceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\right\rceil , the sharpness of the upper bound is demonstrated. On the other hand, infinite families of strong toruses are presented for which the strict inequality holds. For instance, we present strong toruses with difference 2 2 and demonstrate that the difference can be arbitrarily large if only one factor is a cycle. Preliminaries and a general bound

In this section, we first provide additional definitions needed in this article. After that we recall some earlier results that will be useful later and give an upper bound on the
d
-distance
p
-packing domination number of arbitrary strong products.

For two integers
a\leq b
, the interval of integers is defined and denoted as
[a,b]=\{a,\dots,b\}
. If

, then
[a,b]=\{a\}
. The interval

Let

\ecc_{G}(u)

. A

so that
\deg_{G}(v_{1})=1
and
\deg_{G}(v_{i})=2
for

. If

and

, then by

we denote the

in

, that is, the set of vertices which are at distance at most

from

. Further, for

let

. Unless stated otherwise, we will assume that
V(P_{n})=V(C_{n})=[n]
, with the natural adjacency.

From the stock of known facts, let us first recall the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
7
, Proposition 1.1]

If
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
and
0\leq p\leq p^{\prime}
, then
\gamma_{d^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}(G)\geq\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)
.

As it was explicitly or implicitly observed in previous papers, the definition of a
d
-distance

-packing dominating set implies the following properties. To be self-sufficient, we also provide arguments for them.

Observation 2.2
.

For every graph
G
and every two nonnegative integers

and

, the following statements are true.

(i)

If
\rad(G)\leq d
, then
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=1
.

(ii)

If
\rad(G)>d
and

, then
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
.

(iii)

If

, then
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)<\infty
.

Proof.

(i) A vertex

\ecc(u)=\rad(G)
forms a
d
-distance dominating set and a
p
-packing set.

(ii) Suppose

is a
d
-distance dominating set and a

-packing set. Let

. Since
\rad(G)>d
, there exists a vertex

with

. As

is a

-distance dominating set, there exists

such that

. But then

, a contradiction.

(iii) First set
S=\{u\}
, where

is an arbitrary vertex of
G
. Then, inductively, if there exists a vertex

which is at distance at least

to every vertex of
S
, then add

to

. After no such addition is possible, the condition

implies that the constructed set is a
d
-distance dominating set and a
p
-packing set.
∎

For a path

, Observation

[

For every three integers

,

.

[

If

,

\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{n})=\begin{cases}\left\lceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\right\rceil;&\frac{n}{p+1}\geq\left\lceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\right\rceil,\\

We conclude this section with the following announced bound.

Theorem 2.5
.

If
G
and

are graphs, and

and

\gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes H)\leq\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)\gamma_{d}^{p}(H)\,.

Proof.

If
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
or
\gamma_{d}^{p}(H)=\infty

\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)<\infty
and
\gamma_{d}^{p}(H)<\infty

Let

be a
\gamma_{d}^{p}
-set of

and
S_{H}

\gamma_{d}^{p}
-set of

-distance

-packing set of

(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})

d_{G\boxtimes H}((g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime}))=\max\{d_{G}(g,g^{\prime}),d_{H}(h,h^{\prime})\}\geq\max\{p+1,p+1\}=p+1\,,

is a

-packing. Let now

be an arbitrary vertex from

. Since

is a

. Similarly, there exists a vertex

. Therefore,

-distance dominating set.
∎

3
A conjecture and supporting results

In this section we investigate the question how the assumption
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
affects the strong product with
G
being its factor. In this regard, we make the following:

Conjecture 3.1
.

If
G
is a graph, and
d
and
p
are integers such that
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
, then
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes H)=\infty
for every graph
H
.

Remark 3.2
.

By Theorem
2.5
we know that the reverse of the implication in the conjecture is true. That is, if
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes H)=\infty
, then
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
or
\gamma_{d}^{p}(H)=\infty
. If Conjecture
3.1
is true, then for any
n
-fold strong product
F=G_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes G_{n}
it holds that
\gamma_{d}^{p}(F)=\infty
if and only if
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G_{i})=\infty
is true for at least one factor.

A
perfect
r
-code
of a graph
G
is a set
X\subseteq V(G)
such that the sets
N_{G}^{r}[u]
,
u\in X
, form a partition of
V(G)
. By definition, the existence of a perfect
r
-code in
G
is equivalent to the fact that
\gamma_{r}^{2r}(G)<\infty
. It is proved in
[
1
]
that the
n
-fold strong product of simple graphs has a perfect
r
-code if and only if each factor has a perfect
r
-code. This result implies that Conjecture
3.1
is true for
p=2d
. By Observation
2.2
(i) and (ii), the statement is true when
p\geq 2d+1
. Further, the condition
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
does not hold if
p\leq d
. Therefore, it is enough to consider Conjecture
3.1
for
d<p<2d
.

A vertex
u
of a graph
G
is
(d,p)
-close
if
d_{G}(x,y)\leq p
holds for every
x,y\in N_{G}^{d}[u]
. We next verify that Conjecture
3.1
holds if
H
contains a
(d,p)
-close vertex.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
d
and
p
be two nonnegative integers with
p<2d
and let
G
and
H
be two graphs. If
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
and
H
contains a
(d,p)
-close vertex, then
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes H)=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
. Observation
2.2
(iii) then implies
d<p
.
Let
u\in V(H)
be a
(d,p)
-close vertex. Suppose, to the contrary of the statement, that
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes H)<\infty
and
S
is a
\gamma_{d}^{p}
-set in
G\boxtimes H
.

Consider the layer
G^{u}
in
G\boxtimes H
and let
X=N_{H}^{d}[u]
. Then,
V(G)\times X
is the set of vertices in the strong product that can
d
-distance dominate
G^{u}
. Projecting the vertices in
S\cap(V(G)\times X)
to the layer
G^{u}
, we obtain a
d
-distance dominating set of
G^{u}
. By assumption,
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G^{u})=\infty
and hence, the projected vertices cannot form a
p
-packing. It means that there exist two vertices
(x,y)
and
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
in
S\cap(V(G)\times X)
such that the projected vertices
(x,u)
and
(x^{\prime},u)
satisfy

p\geq d_{G\boxtimes H}((x,u),(x^{\prime},u))=d_{G}(x,x^{\prime}).

(1)

Since
S
is a
p
-packing, we have

p+1\leq d_{G\boxtimes H}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))=\max\{d_{G}(x,x^{\prime}),d_{H}(y,y^{\prime})\}.

(2)

Since
y
and
y^{\prime}
belong to
X=N_{H}^{d}[u]
, and
u
is a
(d,p)
-close vertex, we have
d_{H}(y,y^{\prime})\leq p
. This inequality, together with (
1
), contradicts (
2
). We may conclude
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes H)=\infty
.
∎

Complete graphs and graphs with diameter at most
p\geq 1
contain
(d,p)
-close vertices. If
p=2d-1
and
H
contains a simplicial vertex (i.e., a vertex with a complete neighborhood), then again,
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
implies
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes H)=\infty
. In addition, if
H\colon z_{1}\dots z_{n}
is a path, then the leaf
z_{1}
is a
(d,p)
-close vertex. Indeed, if
d_{H}(z_{1},z_{i})\leq d
and
d_{H}(z_{1},z_{j})\leq d
, then
d_{H}(z_{i},z_{j})\leq d\leq p
. Theorem
3.3
then directly implies the following statement:

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a graph and
d
,
p
,
n
three nonnegative integers with
p<2d
and
2\leq n
. If
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
, then
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes P_{n})=\infty
.

Assuming that
H
contains a pendant path that is long enough, a more general consequence of Theorem

can be proved.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
d
and
p
be two nonnegative integers with
p<2d
and let
G
and
H
be two graphs. If
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)=\infty
and

contains a pendant path on at least
\frac{2d-p}{2}

\gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes H)=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
k=\lceil\frac{2d-p}{2}\rceil
and let
z_{1}z_{2}\dots z_{k}
be a pendant path in

such that
z_{k}
is adjacent to a vertex

different from
z_{k-1}
. Observe that
d_{H}(z_{1},z)=k\geq\frac{2d-p}{2}
. In light of Theorem

it suffices to prove that

is a
(d,p)
-close vertex.

If
v_{i}
lies on a shortest path between
z_{1}
and
v_{3-i}
, for

, then
d_{H}(v_{1},v_{2})\leq d_{H}(z_{1},v_{3-i})\leq d<p
proves the statement. Otherwise, neither

nor

belongs to the pendant path

, and the distance can be estimated as

\displaystyle d_{H}(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle\leq d_{H}(z,v_{1})+d_{H}(z,v_{2})=(d_{H}(z_{1},v_{1})-k)+(d_{H}(z_{1},v_{2})-k)

\displaystyle\leq(d-k)+(d-k)\leq 2d-(2d-p)=p.

This finishes the proof of the statement.
∎

. Therefore, it suffices to consider the cases with

The vertex set of the layer

In this section we determine the

Theorem 4.1

\displaystyle|S|

Theorem 5.4
.

For every two nonnegative integers
d
and
p
and for every two cycles
C_{m}
and
C_{n}
, the following statements hold.

(i)

\left\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\right\rceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\leq\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{m}\boxtimes C_{n})
.

(ii)

If
n\equiv 0\pmod{2d+1}
, then
\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{m}\boxtimes C_{n})=\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{m})\,\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{n})
.

(iii)

If
n\equiv r\pmod{2d+1}
and
(1-\frac{r}{2d+1})\left\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\right\rceil<1
, then
\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{m}\boxtimes C_{n})=\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{m})\,\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{n})
. In particular, the equality holds if
n\equiv 2d\pmod{2d+1}
and
m\leq 4d^{2}+2d
, and also if
n\equiv 2d-1\pmod{2d+1}
and
m\leq 2d^{2}+d
.

Proof.

By Theorems
2.5
and
3.6
, the statements are valid when
\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{m}\boxtimes C_{n})=\infty
. Hence, we may assume that
C_{m}\boxtimes C_{n}
has a
\gamma_{d}^{p}
-set
S
and further that
\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{m})=\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\rceil
and
\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{n})=\lceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\rceil
.

(i) Let
R_{i}=\{(x,i)\colon x\in[m]\}
and define
A_{i}=\bigcup_{j=i-d}^{i+d}R_{j}
for every
i\in[n]
, as in the proof of Theorem
3.6
(see Fig.
1
). Since the vertices in
R_{i}
are all
d
-distance dominated by the vertices in
S\cap A_{i}
, we observe that
|S\cap A_{i}|\geq\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\rceil
for every
i\in[n]
. Counting the pairs
(i,v)
complying with
i\in[n]
and
v\in A_{i}
, we then infer that this number
a
is not smaller than
n\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\rceil
. On the other hand, every vertex
v
from
S
occurs in exactly
2d+1
such pairs and then
a=|S|\,(2d+1)
. These observations result in the desired inequality

\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{m}\boxtimes C_{n})=|S|=\frac{a}{2d+1}\geq\frac{n}{2d+1}\left\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\right\rceil.

(ii) If
n\equiv 0\pmod{2d+1}
, then
\frac{n}{2d+1}=\lceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\rceil
and therefore, the lower bound in part (i) and the upper bound in Theorem
2.5
coincide. This implies the statement.

(iii) The condition
(1-\frac{r}{2d+1})\left\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\right\rceil<1
implies
r\neq 0
and therefore,
\lceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\rceil=\frac{n+2d+1-r}{2d+1}
.
Since
\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{m}\boxtimes C_{n})
and
\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\rceil\lceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\rceil
are integers, they are equal whenever the lower bound from (i) and the upper bound from Theorem
2.5
satisfy

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle>\left\lceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\right\rceil\left\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\right\rceil-\frac{n}{2d+1}\left\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n+2d+1-r}{2d+1}-\frac{n}{2d+1}\right)\left\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{r}{2d+1}\right)\left\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\right\rceil.

As our condition in (iii) corresponds to this inequality,
\gamma_{d}^{p}(C_{m}\boxtimes C_{n})=\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\rceil\lceil\frac{n}{2d+1}\rceil
follows. For

\frac{1}{2d+1}\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\rceil<1
which is equivalent to
\lceil\frac{m}{2d+1}\rceil\leq 2d
and hence to

. A similar reasoning proves the statement for
r=2d-1

6
Concluding results and problems

By Theorem
5.1
, the difference
\gamma_{d}^{p}(G)\gamma_{d}^{p}(H)-\gamma_{d}^{p}(G\boxtimes H)
over all connected graphs

can be equal to

. We next prove that the difference can be arbitrary.

Theorem 6.1
.

The difference
\gamma_{2}^{2}(G)\gamma_{2}^{2}(H)-\gamma_{2}^{2}(G\boxtimes H)
can be an arbitrarily large integer, even if
G
and
H
are connected graphs. In particular, this also holds when one factor is required to be a cycle.

Proof.

Let
G_{k}
be obtained from
k\geq 2

vertex disjoint
11
-cycles by gluing them together at two adjacent vertices. Formally,
G_{k}
is defined on the vertex set

V(G_{k})=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\cup\{v_{i}^{j}\colon i\in[3,11],\enskip j\in[k]\}

such that
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}^{j}\dots v_{11}^{j}v_{1}
is an
11
-cycle for each
j\in[k]
. We state first that
\gamma_{2}^{2}(G_{k})=2k
. Indeed, every cycle
C_{11}^{j}
contains five consecutive vertices, namely
v_{5}^{j},\dots,v_{9}^{j}
, which can be
2
-distance dominated only by vertices that belong to
C_{11}^{j}
and no other cycles. Supposing that
V(C_{11}^{j})\setminus\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
contains only one vertex from a
2
-distance
2
-packing set of
G_{k}
, this vertex must be
v_{7}^{j}
. However, in this case,
v_{4}^{j}
and
v_{10}^{j}
would be
2
-distance dominated by
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, respectively, and the set would not be a
2
-packing. This contradiction proves
\gamma_{2}^{2}(G_{k})\geq 2k
. For the other direction, observe that

S=\{v_{5}^{1},v_{10}^{1}\}\cup\{v_{4}^{j},v_{9}^{j}\colon j\in[2,k]\}

is a
2
-distance
2
-packing set in
G_{k}
and contains
2k
vertices. Thus
\gamma_{2}^{2}(G_{k})=2k
for every
k\geq 2
. We also know that
\gamma_{2}^{2}(C_{11})=3
.

Consider now the product
F_{k}=G_{k}\boxtimes C_{11}
. For each cycle
C_{11}^{j}
in

, take the strong torus

and apply the construction described in the proof of Theorem
5.3
to get a

-distance

-packing set

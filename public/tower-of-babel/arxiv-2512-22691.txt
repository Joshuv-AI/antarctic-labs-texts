An Improved Lower Bound on Cardinality of Support of the Amplitude-Constrained AWGN Channel
Haiyang Wang, Luca Barletta, Alex Dytso

Abstract
We study the amplitude-constrained additive white Gaussian noise channel. It is well known that the capacity-achieving input distribution for this channel is discrete and supported on finitely many points. The best known bounds show that the support size of the capacity-achieving distribution is lower-bounded by a term of order A A and upper-bounded by a term of order A 2 A^{2} , where A A denotes the amplitude constraint. It was conjectured in [ 1 ] that the linear scaling is optimal. In this work, we establish a new lower bound of order A ​ log ⁡ A A\sqrt{\log A} , improving the known bound and ruling out the conjectured linear scaling. To obtain this result, we quantify the fact that the capacity-achieving output distribution is close to the uniform distribution in the interior of the amplitude constraint. Next, we introduce a wrapping operation that maps the problem to a compact domain and develop a theory of best approximation of the uniform distribution by finite Gaussian mixtures. These approximation bounds are then combined with stability properties of capacity-achieving distributions to yield the final support-size lower bound.



1
Introduction

We consider an additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channel subject to a peak-power constraint. The channel output is given by

Y=X+Z,

(1)

where the input random variable
X
satisfies the constraint
|X|\leq A
, and
Z
is a standard normal random variable independent of
X
.

We are interested in the channel capacity, defined as

C(A)=\max_{X:\,|X|\leq A}I(X;Y),

(2)

where
I(X;Y)
denotes the mutual information between
X
and
Y
. We denote by
X^{*}
a capacity-achieving input random variable and by
Y^{*}
the corresponding induced output random variable. In general, both the exact value of the capacity
C(A)
and the precise structure of the capacity-achieving distribution
X^{*}
remain unknown.

This channel was first studied by Shannon in his seminal work
[
2
]
, where initial upper and lower bounds on the capacity were derived. Shannon also showed that, in the low signal-to-noise ratio regime, the peak-power constrained capacity exhibits the same asymptotic behavior as the capacity under an average-power (second-moment) constraint.

Subsequent progress on characterizing both the capacity and the structure of the optimal input distribution was made by Smith
[
3
,
4
]
. In particular, Smith established that the capacity-achieving input distribution is unique, symmetric about the origin, and—perhaps surprisingly at the time—discrete with finitely many mass points. Moreover, Smith showed that for all
A<0.1
, the capacity-achieving distribution is equiprobable on the two-point set
\{\pm A\}
, which effectively established capacity for this regime.

This result was later sharpened by Sharma and Shamai
[
5
]
, who showed that an equiprobable binary input supported on
\{\pm A\}
is optimal if and only if
A\leq\bar{A}\approx 1.665
, where
\bar{A}
is characterized as the solution to a certain integral equation. Furthermore, they demonstrated that a ternary input supported on
\{-A,0,A\}
is capacity-achieving for all
\bar{A}\leq A\leq\bar{\bar{A}}\approx 2.786
. They also conjectured that, as
A
increases, the support of the capacity-achieving distribution grows by at most one point at a time, with new mass points always appearing at zero.

The work of Zhang
[
6
]
, that is not widely known within the information-theoretic literature, studied the asymptotic behavior of the capacity-achieving input and output distributions. Roughly speaking, Zhang showed that both distributions are close to uniform in an appropriate asymptotic sense. The arguments in
[
6
]
are technically involved and require lengthy derivations. In contrast, in this work we derive more explicit and non-asymptotic bounds using simpler arguments. Zhang
[
6
, p. 91]
also conjectured that the support size of the capacity-achieving input distribution grows on the order of
A\log A
.

Dytso et al.
[
1
]
established rigorous upper and lower bounds on the cardinality of the capacity-achieving input distribution for the amplitude-constrained AWGN channel, of orders
A^{2}
and
A
, respectively. Their upper bounds rely on Karlin’s oscillation theorem
[
7
]
together with complex-analytic zero-counting arguments. The lower bound, on the other hand, relied on the fact that mutual information is bounded by the log of cardinality of input support. Based on these results and supporting numerical evidence, the authors conjectured that the support size scales linearly with
A
.

Subsequent numerical investigations have suggested alternative asymptotic behaviors. In particular, Mattingly et al.
[
8
]
reported an empirical scaling of order
A^{4/3}
as
A\to\infty
, based on experiments involving not only the AWGN channel but also several non-Gaussian models, including the binomial channel and certain two-dimensional channels. A follow-up work by Abbott and Machta
[
9
]
provided a heuristic, physics-inspired argument in support of this scaling. As noted earlier, this is in addition to Zhang’s
A\log A
conjecture
[
6
]
.

The diversity of these conjectured scaling laws can be partly attributed to the extreme numerical sensitivity of the underlying optimization problem. Numerical studies in this regime are highly susceptible to algorithmic bias and rounding errors, particularly for large
A
. In this limit, the nearly optimal output distribution in the interior approaches the uniform distribution, with meaningful deviations occurring only at the scale of numerical precision. Moreover, standard implementations based on the Blahut–Arimoto algorithm
[
10
,
11
]
require nested numerical integrations of log-output probabilities, which further exacerbate numerical instability and bias. As a result, different numerical methodologies can lead to markedly different empirical scaling laws.

In addition to studying the structure of the capacity-achieving distribution, a large body of work has focused on upper and lower bounds on the capacity in (
2
). Broadly speaking, existing capacity upper bounds fall into three categories. The first relies on the maximum entropy principle
[
12
, Chapter 12]
, upper bounding the output differential entropy
h(Y)
under suitable moment constraints
[
13
,
14
]
. The second is based on a dual characterization of capacity, where maximizing mutual information over the input distribution is reformulated as minimizing relative entropy over the output distribution; a suboptimal choice of the latter yields an explicit upper bound. Notable examples in this class include the McKellips bound
[
15
]
and the bound of Thangaraj and Kramer
[
16
]
; see also
[
17
]
for an in-depth exposition. The third approach exploits the representation of mutual information as an integral involving the minimum mean square error (MMSE)
[
18
]
, leading to an upper bound by replacing the optimal estimator with a suboptimal one
[
19
]
.

There also exists extensive literature on extending Smith’s original proof strategy
[
3
]
, as well as the associated cardinality bounds in
[
1
]
, to a wide range of channel models. For complex and vector Gaussian channels, discreteness of the capacity-achieving input distribution and explicit cardinality bounds are established in
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
1
,
23
,
24
]
. For additive noise channels with sufficiently regular noise densities, discreteness of the optimal input distribution is shown in
[
25
,
26
]
. Proofs of discreteness and cardinality bounds for practically relevant Rayleigh fading channels can be found in
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
. Similar results for non-additive channels include the Poisson channel
[
13
,
30
]
and the binomial channel
[
31
]
. A broader survey of optimization-based techniques for establishing discreteness of capacity-achieving distributions is provided in
[
32
]
. Extensions to multiuser channels can be found in
[
33
,
34
,
35
]
. In addition, a comprehensive overview of capacity results for point-to-point Gaussian and related channels is given in
[
36
]
.

Our results also rely on recent advances in the theory of best approximation by finite Gaussian mixtures; see
[
37
]
and references therein. Indeed, our Theorem

1.2
Notation

Throughout the paper, the deterministic scalar quantities are denoted by lower-case letters and random variables are denoted by uppercase letters.

We denote the distribution of a random variable
X
by
P_{X}
. The support set of
P_{X}
is denoted and defined as

{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X})=\left\{x:\text{ for every open set $\mathcal{D}\ni x$ we have that $P_{X}(\mathcal{D})>0$}\right\}.

(3)

The notation
|\cdot|
, depending on the context, denotes either absolute value or cardinality of the set. All logarithm are taken with base
\mathrm{e}
. The density of a standard normal will be denoted by
\phi
.

Given two probability distributions
P
and
Q
with probability densities functions (pdfs)
p
and
q
, respectively, we will require the following distance:

Total Variation:

\displaystyle\mathsf{TV}(P\|Q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int|p(x)-q(x)|\mathrm{d}x,

(4)

Relative Entropy:

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(P\|Q)

\displaystyle=\int p(x)\log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\mathrm{d}x,

(5)

\chi^{2}
Divergence:

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(P\|Q)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{(p(x)-q(x))^{2}}{q(x)}\mathrm{d}x,

(6)

with the understanding that the relative entropy and
\chi^{2}
are equal to infinity if
P
is not absolutely continuous with respect to
Q
.

2
Main Result

The main result of this work is the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Fix some
A>0
and let
P_{X^{*}}
be the capacity-achieving input distribution in (
2
). Then,

|{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X^{*}})|\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\pi}A\sqrt{\log^{+}(c\,A)},

(7)

for some explicit constant
c>0
, where
\log^{+}(x):=\max\{\log(x),0\}
.

A few remarks are in order:

•

The bound in (
7
) improves upon the previously known lower bound of order
A
derived in
[
1
]
. Additionally, it disproves the conjecture made in
[
1
]
that the support scales as
A
.

•

Our result rules out linear growth of the support size and shows that any valid scaling must be superlinear. Whether the correct scaling is

A\sqrt{\log A},A\log A,A^{4/3},A^{2}
or another intermediate rate remains open.

•

In (
7
) the constant can be taken to be
c=\frac{\sqrt{2}\log(\mathrm{e})}{8\sqrt{\pi\mathrm{e}}(5\mathrm{e}+1)^{2}}
.

3
Proofs and Techniques

In this section, we collect all the proofs and techniques needed to show the main theorem. We begin by presenting results related to the best approximation with Gaussian mixtures. Next, we present a few new properties of the capacity-achieving input and output distributions. This section will be concluded with the proof of the main theorem.

3.1
A Best Approximation Theory With Gaussian Mixtures

We begin by introducing a wrapping operation that maps real-valued random variables onto the circle, allowing approximation questions to be studied on a compact domain. Given a continuous random variable
W\in\mathbb{R}
and a parameter
B>0
we define the following wrapping operator:

\left\langle W\right\rangle_{B}=\frac{\pi}{B}\left(W\text{ mod }2B\right)\in(-\pi,\pi),

(8)

and where
W\text{ mod }2B=W-2B\left\lfloor\frac{W}{2B}\right\rfloor
.

The next results summarize some properties of the wrapping operation that will be important in our derivations.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
W\in\mathbb{R}
is a continuous random variable with pdf
f_{W}
. Then, the following statements hold: for
B>0

•

the density of
\langle W\rangle_{B}
is given by

f_{\langle W\rangle_{B}}(\theta)=\frac{B}{\pi}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{W}\left(\frac{B}{\pi}(\theta+2\pi j)\right),\,\theta\in(-\pi,\pi).

(9)

•

Suppose that
U\sim\mathcal{U}(-B,B)
. Then

f_{\langle U+Z\rangle_{B}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi},\,\theta\in(-\pi,\pi).

(10)

•

for any
X\in[-B,B]
, we have the following Fourier transform: for
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{B}}(n)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{B}}(\theta)\,\mathrm{e}^{in\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\pi n}{B}\Big)^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(in\langle X\rangle_{B}\right)\right].

(11)

Proof.

We only show the last statement.
Fix
B>0
and let
V=\langle X+Z\rangle_{B}\in(-\pi,\pi)
.
Since the map
w\mapsto\langle w\rangle_{B}
is a reduction modulo
2\pi
after scaling by
\pi/B
, and since

\theta\mapsto\exp(in\theta)
is
2\pi
-periodic, we have

\exp(inV)=\exp\left(in\frac{\pi}{B}(X+Z)\right)\qquad\text{a.s.}

(12)

Therefore, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{B}}(n)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{B}}(\theta)\,\exp(in\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta

(13)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(inV)\right]

(14)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(in\frac{\pi}{B}(X+Z)\right)\right]

(15)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(in\frac{\pi}{B}X\right)\right]\,\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(in\frac{\pi}{B}Z\right)\right],

(16)

where we used independence of
X
and
Z
. Finally, since
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
,

\mathbb{E}[\exp(itZ)]=\exp(-t^{2}/2),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

and substituting
t=\frac{\pi n}{B}
yields

\widehat{f}_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{B}}(n)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\pi n}{B}\Big)^{2}\right)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(in\frac{\pi}{B}X\right)\right].

(17)

Since
X\in[-B,B]
implies
\langle X\rangle_{B}=\frac{\pi}{B}X
a.s., the last expectation equals

\mathbb{E}[\exp(in\langle X\rangle_{B})]
, which gives (
11
).
∎

The key result for providing a lower bound is the following theorem. Its proof adopts the trigonometric moment method of
[
37
, Thm. 6]
; see also
[
38
, Thm. 3]
. The next theorem quantifies the best possible approximation of the wrapped uniform distribution by a wrapped Gaussian mixture with finitely many components.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X\in[-A,A]
be a discrete random variable with
K>1
mass points and
U\sim\mathcal{U}[-A,A]
. Then,

\chi^{2}\left(P_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}\|P_{\langle U+Z\rangle_{A}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-4\pi^{2}\frac{K^{2}}{A^{2}}\right).

(18)

Proof.

Let us first introduce some preliminary notation. The
n
-th trigonometric moment of a random variable
X
supported on the circle
[-\pi,\pi]
is defined as
t_{n}(X)=\mathbb{E}[\exp(inX)]
. Furthermore, we can define its
n
-th trigonometric moment matrix as follows:

T_{n}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}t_{0}(X)&t_{1}(X)&\cdots&t_{n}(X)\\
t_{-1}(X)&t_{0}(X)&\cdots&t_{n-1}(X)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
t_{-n}(X)&t_{-n+1}(X)&\cdots&t_{0}(X)\end{pmatrix},

(19)

which is an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
Hermitian matrix. Additionally, note that, given a discrete random variable
X
with the distribution
P_{X}
which has
K
mass points, we can see that

T_{n}(X)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}P_{X}(x_{k})T_{n}(x_{k})
is sum of
K
rank-
1
matrices, therefore it has rank at most
K
.

Now, using (
11
) the Fourier transform of the underlying densities is given by: for
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{\langle U+Z\rangle_{A}}(n)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&n=0\\
0&n\neq 0\end{array}\right.,

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}(n)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{\sigma^{2}n^{2}}{2}\right)t_{n}\left(\langle X\rangle_{A}\right),

(20c)

where
\sigma^{2}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{A^{2}}
. Consequently, we have the following characterization of the
\chi^{2}
-distance

\displaystyle\chi^{2}\left(P_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}\|P_{\langle U+Z\rangle_{A}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(f_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}(\theta)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

(21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\,n\neq 0}\exp\left(-\sigma^{2}n^{2}\right)\left|t_{n}\left(\langle X\rangle_{A}\right)\right|^{2},

(22)

where (
21
) follows from (
10
); and (
22
) follows from Parseval’s theorem and Fourier expressions in (
20
).

Furthermore, we have that the
\chi^{2}
divergence can be lower bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\chi^{2}\left(P_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}\|P_{\langle U+Z\rangle_{A}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n\neq 0,|n|\leq 2K}\exp\left(-\sigma^{2}n^{2}\right)\left|t_{n}\left(\langle X\rangle_{A}\right)\right|^{2}

(23)

\displaystyle\geq\exp(-\sigma^{2}4K^{2})\sum_{n\neq 0,|n|\leq 2K}\left|t_{n}\left(\langle X\rangle_{A}\right)\right|^{2}

(24)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\exp(-\sigma^{2}4K^{2})}{2K+1}\sum_{n\neq 0,|n|\leq 2K}(2K+1-|n|)\left|t_{n}\left(\langle X\rangle_{A}\right)\right|^{2}

(25)

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp(-\sigma^{2}4K^{2})}{2K+1}\|T_{2K}(\langle X\rangle_{A})-I\|_{\text{F}}^{2},

(26)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\text{F}}
is the Frobenius norm of a matrix.

Now, as was argued already before,
T_{2K}(\langle X\rangle_{A})
is a matrix with rank
K
at most, therefore, by Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem
[
39
]
, we have that

\|T_{2K}(\langle X\rangle_{A})-I\|_{\text{F}}^{2}\geq\min_{B\in\mathbb{C}^{2K\times 2K}:\,\text{rank}(B)\leq K}\|B-I\|_{\text{F}}^{2}\geq K+1.

(27)

Substituting (
27
) into (
26
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\chi^{2}\left(P_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}\|P_{\langle U+Z\rangle_{A}}\right)\geq\frac{\exp(-\sigma^{2}4K^{2})(K+1)}{2K+1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-4\pi^{2}\frac{K^{2}}{A^{2}}\right),

which concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

If, in addition, we know that the pdf of the wrapped random variable is bounded, then we can upgrade the above result to a total variation.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose that
f_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}\leq M
for some constant
M
independent of
A
. Then,

\mathsf{TV}\left(P_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}\|P_{\langle U+Z\rangle_{A}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2(2\pi M+1)}\exp\left(-4\pi^{2}\frac{K^{2}}{A^{2}}\right).

(28)

Proof.

Starting with the definition of
\chi^{2}
-squared divergence in (
6
), we have that

\displaystyle\chi^{2}\left(P_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}\|P_{\langle U+Z\rangle_{A}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\left(f_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}(x)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{2}}{\frac{1}{2\pi}}\mathrm{d}x

(29)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|2\pi f_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}(x)-1\right|\,\left|f_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}(x)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\right|\mathrm{d}x

(30)

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi M+1)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|f_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}(x)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\right|\mathrm{d}x

(31)

\displaystyle=2(2\pi M+1)\,\mathsf{TV}\left(P_{\langle X+Z\rangle_{A}}\|P_{\langle U+Z\rangle_{A}}\right),

(32)

where the last step follows from the definition of the total variation distance in (
4
) and from (
10
).
∎

3.2
Some Properties of Capacity-Achieving Distribution

In this section, we summarize some of the known properties of the capacity and capacity-achieving distributions. We begin by presenting the following well-known result
[
40
,
41
]
.

Lemma 2
.

Given a channel
P_{Y|X}
, suppose that
P_{X^{*}}
is a capacity-achieving distribution. Then, for any
P_{X}
we have that

\mathsf{D}(P_{Y}\|P_{Y^{*}})\leq I(X^{*};Y^{*})-I(X;Y),

(33)

where
P_{X}\to P_{Y|X}\to P_{Y}
and
P_{X^{*}}\to P_{Y|X}\to P_{Y^{*}}
.

A related result to Lemma
2
are the following KKT conditions
[
4
,
42
]
.

Lemma 3
.

The capacity-achieving distribution
P_{X^{*}}
and induced output distribution
P_{Y^{*}}
satisfy the following: for
A>0

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(P_{Y|X}(\cdot|x)\|P_{Y^{*}})

\displaystyle\leq C(A),\quad x\in[-A,A],

(34)

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(P_{Y|X}(\cdot|x)\|P_{Y^{*}})

\displaystyle=C(A),\quad x\in{\rm supp}(P_{X^{*}}).

(35)

The next result provides upper and lower bounds on the capacity and bound the relative entropy between optimal output distribution
P_{X^{*}+Z}
and output distribution induced by a uniform input
P_{U+Z}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
U\sim\mathcal{U}_{[-A,A]}
. Then, for
A>0

\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{2A^{2}}{\pi\mathrm{e}}\right)\leq I(U;U+Z)\leq C(A)\leq\log\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}A}{\sqrt{\pi\mathrm{e}}}\right).

(36)

Moreover,

\mathsf{D}(P_{U+Z}\|P_{X^{*}+Z})\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi\mathrm{e}}{2}}\frac{1}{A}.

(37)

Proof.

The lower bound in (
36
) is due to Shannon
[
2
, Section 25]
and the upper bound is due to McKellips
[
15
]
. To show the bound on the relative entropy of the output distributions note that

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(P_{U+Z}\|P_{X^{*}+Z})

\displaystyle\leq C(A)-I(U;U+Z)

(38)

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}A}{\sqrt{\pi\mathrm{e}}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{2A^{2}}{\pi\mathrm{e}}\right)

(39)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{1+\frac{\sqrt{2}A}{\sqrt{\pi\mathrm{e}}}}{\sqrt{1+\frac{2A^{2}}{\pi\mathrm{e}}}}

(40)

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\mathrm{e}}}A}\right)

(41)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\mathrm{e}}}A},

(42)

where (
38
) follows from Lemma
2
; (
39
) follows from the bounds in (
36
); and (
42
) follows from using inequality
1+u\leq\mathrm{e}^{u},\,u\geq 0
.
∎

We now produce a few new properties of the optimal distributions.

Proposition 1
.

The capacity-achieving input and output distributions satisfy: For
A>0

•

Suppose that
y_{0}
is a global maximum of
f_{X^{*}+Z}
, then there exists a point
x\in{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X^{*}})
such that

|y_{0}-x|\leq 1.

(43)

•

Let
M_{A}=\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}}f_{X^{*}+Z}(y)
, then

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\mathrm{e}}+2A}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-C(A)-h(Z)}\leq M_{A}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-C(A)-h(Z)+1}\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}}{\sqrt{2\pi\mathrm{e}+4A^{2}}},

(44)

where
h(Z)
is differential entropy of
Z
.

•

For
|y|\geq A

f_{X^{*}+Z}(y)\leq M_{A}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(|y|-A)^{2}}{2}}.

(45)

•

\max_{\theta\in(-\pi,\pi)}f_{\langle X^{*}+Z\rangle_{A}}(\theta)\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}}{2\pi}\left(3+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{2}A}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A>1}}{{\leq}}\frac{5\mathrm{e}}{2\pi}.

(46)

Proof.

We claim that there exists a support point
x_{k}\in{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X^{*}})
such that
|y_{0}-x_{k}|\leq 1
.
Recall
f_{X^{*}+Z}(y)=\sum_{i}w_{i}\phi(y-x_{i})
where
w_{i}>0
.
Suppose
|y_{0}-x_{i}|>1
for all
i
. Then
f_{X^{*}+Z}^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})=\sum w_{i}((x_{i}-y_{0})^{2}-1)\phi(y_{0}-x_{i})>0
, which contradicts the fact that
y_{0}
is a maximum. Thus, there must be at least one support point
x_{k}
with
|y_{0}-x_{k}|\leq 1
.

We now move on to showing the upper bound in (
44
). Let
y_{0}
be a global maximum of
f_{X+Z}
. Using a generalization of Tweedie’s formula we have that
[
43

4
Conclusion

In this work, we derived a new lower bound on the cardinality of the capacity-achieving input distribution for the amplitude-constrained AWGN channel. Our result improves the previously known linear lower bound and establishes that the support size must grow superlinearly with the amplitude constraint, thereby ruling out linear scaling.

The proof is based on a comparison between the output distribution induced by the capacity-achieving input and that induced by a uniform input, using a probability distribution wrapping argument together with recent sharp lower bounds on the approximation of Gaussian mixtures. This approach yields explicit, non-asymptotic bounds.

Several questions remain open. While our result shows superlinear growth, the exact asymptotic scaling —— whether

A\sqrt{\log(A)}
,
A\log A
,
A^{4/3}
,
A^{2}
, or otherwise —— remains unresolved. Further refinements of the constants and a deeper understanding of the boundary behavior of the capacity-achieving distribution are of interest. Extending these techniques to other channel models, including vector Gaussian and fading channels, is another promising direction.

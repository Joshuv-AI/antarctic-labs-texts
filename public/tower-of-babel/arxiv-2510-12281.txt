Analytic characterization of t t -quasicircles and conformal mappings onto t t -quasidisks
Xin Wei

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a new class of mappings, termed ( ρ , t ) (\rho,t) -quasisymmetric mappings, which generalizes the classical concept of quasisymmetric mappings. Using this broader class of mappings, we provide an analytic characterization of t t -quasicircles. This result can be viewed as a t t -quasicircle analogue of a classical theorem by Tukia and Väisälä [ 5 ] . Furthermore, we study conformal mappings from the unit disk 𝔻 {\mathbb{D}} onto t t -quasidisksand show that their boundary values are ”almost“ ( ρ , t 2 ) (\rho,t^{2}) -quasisymmetric. This result extends the Quasicircle Theorem to the case of t t -quasicircles.

1.
Introduction

A Jordan curve
\Gamma
is a quasicircle if it is the image of the unit circle under a quasiconformal mapping of the extended complex plane onto itself. The bounded component of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\Gamma
is called a quasidisk. Quasicircles appear in many different settings in complex analysis and geometric function theory. For example, in complex dynamics, they are the Julia sets of some rational mappings; also quasicircles play an important role in universal Teichmüller space, through quasisymmetric homeomorphism of the unit circle. Besides the original definition, Ahlfors established a geometric characterization of quasicircles in
[
1
]
, which is also called bounded turning condition. Let
\Gamma
be a Jordan curve, for any
x,y\in\Gamma
, let
\Gamma(x,y)
be the subarc of
\Gamma
between
x
and
y
which has a smaller diameter, or, be either subarc when both have the same diameter. The diameter of
\Gamma(x,y)
is denoted by
\operatorname{diam}\Gamma(x,y)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Ahlfors’ Condition)
Jordan curve
\Gamma
is a quasicircle if and only if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that

(1)

\begin{split}\operatorname{diam}\Gamma(x,y)\leq C|x-y|\end{split}

for any
x,y\in\Gamma
.

Example 1.2
.

The Koch snowflake curve is a quasicircle; see Figure
1
.

The third step of the construction of Koch curve

This equivalent characterization of quasicircles is also applied to define quasiarcs, i.e., a planar arc
\Gamma
is a quasiarc if and only if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that (
1
) is satisfied for any
x,y\in\Gamma
. Here
\Gamma(x,y)
is the unique subarc of
\Gamma
connecting
x
and
y
. In
[
4
]
, in order to study the Whitney phenomenon, Norton introduced the definition of
t
-quasiarcs for
t\geq 1
.

A planar arc
\Gamma
is a
t
-quasiarc if and only if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that

(\operatorname{diam}\Gamma(x,y))^{t}\leq C|x-y|

for any
x,y\in\Gamma
.

We extend Norton’s construction to establish the definition of
t
-quasicircles for
0<t\leq 1
. The interior bounded domain of a
t
-quasicircle is called a
t
-quasidisk.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
0<t\leq 1
, and let
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a Jordan curve. Then
\Gamma
is a
t
-quasicircle if and only if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}\Gamma(x,y)\leq C|x-y|^{t}

for any
x,y\in\Gamma
. In particular, a
1
-quasicircle is a usual quasicircle.

Example 1.4
.

In Figure
, the arc
AB
is a circular arc and the arc
CB
is a straight line tangents
AB
at
B
. According to the definition of
t
-quasicircles, the arc
ABC
is a
1/2
-quasiarc.

In
[
3
]
, the authors studied the local properties of self-similar
t
-quasicircles. Also, they provided a method for constructing
t
-quasicircles for any given
t\in(0,1]
. Also, a global flatness condition of
t
-quasicircles was studied in
[
7
]
, and it follows that the constant distance boundary of a
t
-quasicircle is also a
t
-quasicircle.

In
[
5
]
, Tukia and Väisälä provided another analytic characterization of quasicircles of metric spaces by using quasisymmetric mappings. We provide the definition of quasisymmetric mappings between metric spaces firstly. In the following definition, the notation
|a-b|
stands for the distance between
a
and
b
in any metric space.

Definition 1.5
.

[
5
]

Let
f:X\to Y
be a homeomorphism between metric spaces
X
and
Y
, and let
\rho:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a strictly increasing continuous function. The mapping
f
is called quasisymmetric or
\rho
-quasisymmetric if
|a-x|\leq k|b-x|
implies

|f(a)-f(x)|\leq\rho(k)|f(b)-f(x)|

for any
a,b,x\in X
and
k\geq 0
. While, a homeomorphism
f:X\to Y
is called weakly quasisymmetric or weakly
R
-quasisymmetric if for any
a,b,x\in X
,
|a-x|\leq|b-x|
implies

|f(a)-f(x)|\leq R|f(b)-f(x)|

for some constant
R
.

According to the next theorem, we see that quasicircles and quasisymmetric mappings are closely related.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
5
, Theorem 4.9]

A curve is a quasicircle if and only if it is the image of the unit circle under a quasisymmetric mapping.

We would like to create a parallel relationship for
t
-quasicircles. So, in Section
2
, we generalize the definition of weakly
R
-quasisymmetric mappings and obtain weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric mappings (see Definition
2.1
). Moreover, we prove that

any
t
-quasicircle is the image of the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}
under a weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric mapping
(see Theorem
2.7
).
This result provides us an analytic characterization of
t
-quasicircles. We also introduce the definition of
(\rho,t)
-quasisymmetric mappings in this section, and establish the relations between weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric and
(\rho,t)
-quasisymmetric mappings in both Section
2
and the following Section
5
.

Let

be a quasidisk, which is the inner domain of a quasicircle, and then it is a bounded simply connected domain. The Riemann Mapping Theorem guarantees that there exists a conformal mapping

such that

Theorem 1.7
.

[

Let

(1)

(2)

is quasisymmetric on

.

Remark
.

The open ball
\{y\in{\mathbb{C}}:|x-y|<r\}
is written as
{\mathbb{D}}(x,r)
and the closed ball
\{y\in{\mathbb{C}}:|y-x|\leq r\}
is written as
\bar{\mathbb{D}}(x,r)

and

2.
Generalized quasisymmetric mappings

In
[
5
]
, it was proved that any quasicircle is the image of the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}
under a quasisymmetric mapping. So it is natural to find this kind of analytic characterization of
t
-quasicircles. Thus, in this section, we define a class of mappings between metric spaces which can be viewed as a generalization of the quasisymmetric mappings of Definition
1.5
. And then the relationships between this kind of mappings and
t
-quasicircles are studied; the main result states that
every
t
-quasicircle is the image of the unit circle under a mapping we defined in this section.

Let
0<t\leq 1
, and let
X
,
Y
be metric spaces. We use the notation
|a-b|
to denote the distance between
a
and
b
in any metric space.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a homeomorphism, and let
\rho:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a strictly increasing continuous function. The mapping
f
is called
(\rho,t)
-quasisymmetric if
|a-x|\leq k|b-x|
implies

\displaystyle|f(a)-f(x)|\leq\rho(k)|f(b)-f(x)|^{t}

for any
a,b,x\in X
and
k\geq 0
. While, say
f
is weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric if
|a-x|\leq|b-x|
implies

\displaystyle|f(a)-f(x)|\leq R|f(b)-f(x)|^{t}

for some constant
R
.

When
t=1
, a
(\rho,t)
-quasisymmetric mapping is reduced to a
\rho
-quasisymmetric mapping and the weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric is reduced to weakly
R
-quasisymmetric. Also, directly follows from the definition, every
(\rho,t)
-quasisymmetric mapping is weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric with
R=\rho(1)
. The converse situation is not true. However, when
X={\mathbb{T}}
and
Y={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
we show that every weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric mapping is
(\rho,t^{2})
-quasisymmetric mapping in Theorem
5.1
. Immediately, it follows that when
t=1
the concepts of
\rho
-quasisymmetric and weakly
R
-quasisymmetric are equivalent (also see Theorem 2.16 of
[
5
]
).

Example 2.2
.

If
f:X\to Y
satisfies the bi-Hölder condition, i.e., there exist positive constants
K_{1},K_{2}
such that

\displaystyle K_{1}|x-y|^{1/t}\leq|f(x)-f(y)|\leq K_{2}|x-y|^{t}

for any
x,y\in X
, then
f
is weakly
(R,t^{2})
-quasisymmetric.

Proof.

For any
a,b,x\in X
with
|a-x|\leq|b-x|
, then
|b-x|^{1/t}\leq 1/K_{1}|f(b)-f(x)|
. Thus
|a-x|^{t}\leq 1/K_{1}^{t^{2}}|f(b)-f(x)|^{t^{2}}
. Since
|f(a)-f(x)|\leq K_{2}|a-x|^{t}
, it leads to
|f(a)-f(x)|\leq K_{2}/K_{1}^{t^{2}}|f(b)-f(x)|^{t^{2}}
. Let
R=K_{2}/K_{1}^{t^{2}}
. Then the proof is complete.
∎

The next proposition shows that the image of the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}
under a weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric mapping is a
t
-quasicircle. This is our first conclusion about the connection between weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric mappings and
t
-quasicircles.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric mapping. Then
\Gamma:=f({\mathbb{T}})
is a
t
-quasicircle.

Proof.

For any
a,b\in\Gamma
, there exist
x,y\in{\mathbb{T}}
such that
f(x)=a
and
f(y)=b
. Let
{\mathbb{T}}(x,y)
be the subarc of
{\mathbb{T}}
between
x
and
y
with a smaller diameter. Then we obtain that
\operatorname{diam}{\mathbb{T}}(x,y)=|x-y|
. For any
z\in{\mathbb{T}}(x,y)
, it follows that
|z-x|\leq\operatorname{diam}{\mathbb{T}}(x,y)=|x-y|
. The weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric property of
f
yields that

\displaystyle|f(z)-f(x)|\leq R|f(x)-f(y)|^{t}.

Since there are
f(u),f(v)\in f({\mathbb{T}}(x,y))
such that
\operatorname{diam}f({\mathbb{T}}(x,y))=|f(u)-f(v)|
, we know that

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}f({\mathbb{T}}(x,y))=|f(u)-f(v)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(u)-f(x)|+|f(v)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq 2R|f(x)-f(y)|^{t}.

Because
f({\mathbb{T}}(x,y))
is a subarc of
\Gamma
between
a
and
b
, and
\Gamma(a,b)
stands for the subarc of
\Gamma
between
a
and
b
which has a smaller diameter. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}\Gamma(a,b)\leq\operatorname{diam}f({\mathbb{T}}(x,y))\leq 2R|a-b|^{t}.

Therefore, by its definition,
\Gamma
is a
t
-quasicircle.
∎

when

Proposition 2.4
.

Let

The above two propositions established that a weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric mapping maps

Our proof is parallel to that of Theorem 4.9 of
[
5
]
, so we should introduce a definition of
[
5
]
which will be used later. A metric space

is called homogeneously totally bounded, if there is an increasing function
h:[1/2,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that for every
\alpha\geq 1/2
, every closed ball
\{y\in X:|y-x|\leq r\}
of

can be covered with sets

such that
s\leq h(\alpha)
and
\operatorname{diam}(A_{j})<r/\alpha
for

. We care about the special case when

, it follows from Remarks 2.8 of
[
5
]
that every subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is homogeneously totally bounded with
h(\alpha)=4(\alpha\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}
.

The next lemma generalizes Lemma 4.4 of
[
5
]
to the case of
t
-quasicircles. In the following lemma, we assume that the
t
-quasicircle
\Gamma
is oriented, and any subarc has the induced orientation. For a subarc
L\subset\Gamma
, denoted by
A(L)
the initial point of
L
, meanwhile
B(L)
denotes the terminal point of
L
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
t
-quasicircle. Then for any
0<\varepsilon<1
there exist
\delta>0
and
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that there is a subdivision
\Gamma_{1},\dots\Gamma_{p}
of
\Gamma
with

\displaystyle\delta\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma)\leq\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{i})\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma)

for
i=1,\dots,p
.

Proof.

By a change of scale we assume that
\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma)=1
. Select an arbitrary point
a\in\Gamma
. Since
\varepsilon<1
, there exists a subarc
\Gamma_{1}\subset\Gamma
which is the maximal subarc of
\Gamma
such that
A(\Gamma_{1})=a
and that
\Gamma_{1}\subset\bar{\mathbb{D}}(a,\varepsilon/4)
. So
\varepsilon/4\leq\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{1})\leq\varepsilon/2
. Let
\Gamma_{2}
be the maximal subarc of
\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{1}
such that
A(\Gamma_{2})=B(\Gamma_{1})
and that
\Gamma_{2}\subset\bar{\mathbb{D}}(B(\Gamma_{1}),\varepsilon/4)
. Suppose that
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{j}
have been inductively constructed. If
\Gamma_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Gamma_{j}=\Gamma
, let
p:=j
and the process ends. Otherwise let
\Gamma_{j+1}
be the maximal subarc of
\Gamma\setminus(\Gamma_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Gamma_{j})
such that
A(\Gamma_{j+1})=B(\Gamma_{j})
and that
\Gamma_{j+1}\subset\bar{\mathbb{D}}(B(\Gamma_{j}),\varepsilon/4)
. Because of the compactness of
\Gamma
, we could obtain subarcs
\Gamma_{1},\dots\Gamma_{n+1}
of
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Gamma_{n+1}=\Gamma
and that
\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{i})\leq\varepsilon/2
for all
i\leq n+1
and
\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{i})\geq\varepsilon/4
for
i\leq n
. Deleting
\Gamma_{n+1}
and replacing by
\Gamma_{n}
by the subarc with the initial point
A(\Gamma_{n})
and the terminal point
a
, we get a subdivision
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}
of
\Gamma
such that
\varepsilon/4\leq\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{i})\leq\varepsilon
for all
i\leq n
.

Assume that
1\leq i<j\leq n
and let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a subarc of
\Gamma
with endpoints
A(\Gamma_{i})
and
A(\Gamma_{j})
. Then
\Gamma_{i}\subset\Gamma^{\prime}
and then
\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma^{\prime})\geq\varepsilon/4
. While
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}
is also a subarc between
A(\Gamma_{i})
and
A(\Gamma_{j})
. It is not hard to see that
\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})\geq\varepsilon/4
, since
\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
. The
t
-quasicircle property of
\Gamma
yields that

\displaystyle\varepsilon/4\leq C|A(\Gamma_{i})-A(\Gamma_{j})|^{t}.

Here
C\geq 1
is a suitable constant. It follows that
|A(\Gamma_{i})-A(\Gamma_{j})|\geq(\varepsilon/4C)^{1/t}
. By Remark 2.8 of
[
5
]
,
\Gamma
is
h
-homogeneously totally bounded with
h(\alpha)=4(\alpha\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}
. Thus
n\leq h((4C/\varepsilon)^{1/t})=:p
, where
4C/\varepsilon\geq 1/2
since
0<t,\varepsilon<1
and
C>1
. If
n=p
, there is nothing more to be done. Otherwise we divide
\Gamma_{1}
into two subarcs of diameter at least
\varepsilon/8
. Repeating this
p-n
times we obtain a new subdivision written as
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{p}
such that

\displaystyle 2^{-p-1}\varepsilon\leq\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{i})\leq\varepsilon

for all
i\leq p
. Let
p=h((4C/\varepsilon)^{1/t})
and
\delta=2^{-p-1}\varepsilon
, and then the proof is complete.
∎

With Lemma
2.5
, the next lemma follows by adapting the proof of Lemma 4.7 of
[

]
; we omit the details. However, some notations should be introduced. As in Lemma

, let

,
\delta=2^{-p-2}
. Define
\mu=2^{p+1}
, then
\mu\geq 2
and
\mu^{2}\geq 2^{p+2}
. Choose positive integers

and

\displaystyle 2^{n}\geq\mu^{m},

\displaystyle 4n+3\leq p^{m}.

To see the existence of such

and

, find

and

that satisfy the first inequality firstly. If they do not satisfy the second inequality, double them, and repeat this until the second inequality is also satisfied. Let
N=p^{m}+2n(p-1)
. Define
W
to be the set of words which consist of finite sequence

where

and

; also the empty sequence

is in

. The length of a word

, denoted by

, is the number

. Two words

.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
t
-quasicircle. Then there are subarcs
\Gamma_{w}\subset\Gamma
,
w\in W
such that:

(1)
\Gamma_{\emptyset}=\Gamma
.

(2)
\Gamma_{w1},\dots,\Gamma_{wN}
is a subdivision of
\Gamma_{w}
for

.

(3)
\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{w})\leq 2^{-\ell(w)}\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma)
.

(4) If

,
\ell(w)=\ell(v)
, and if
\Gamma_{w}
and
\Gamma_{v}
are adjacent, then

\displaystyle\mu^{-m-2}\leq\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{w})/\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{v})\leq\mu^{m+2}.

We now deduce the principal theorem of this section from Lemmas

-

:

Theorem 2.7
.

If
\Gamma
is a
t
-quasicircle then there exists a weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric mapping
f:{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that
\Gamma=f({\mathbb{T}})
.

Proof.

For any
a,b\in{\mathbb{T}}
, recall that
{\mathbb{T}}(a,b)\subset{\mathbb{T}}
is the subarc between
a
and
b
with a smaller diameter. Let
|a-b|
denote the
1/2\pi
of the arclength of
{\mathbb{T}}(a,b)
. Then this is a comparable metric with the euclidean distance of
a
and
b
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Let
N
be the constant which is defined before Lemma
2.6
. Divide
\Gamma
into
N
subarcs
\{\Gamma_{w}\}
with specific properties of Lemma
2.6
, and equally divide
{\mathbb{T}}
into
N
subarcs
\{I_{w}\}
. There is a homeomorphism
f
such that
f(I_{w})=\Gamma_{w}
for all
w=1,\dots,N
. We show that
f
is weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric. Without loss of generality, we assume
\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma)=1
by scaling. For any distinct
a,b,x\in{\mathbb{T}}
with
|a-x|\leq|b-x|
, let
I:={\mathbb{T}}(b,x)
, while
J:={\mathbb{T}}\setminus I
.

Case 1. Assume that
f(I)=\Gamma(f(b),f(x))
. Let
s
be the smallest integer such that
N^{-s}\leq|b-x|/2
. Then
I
contains an arc
I_{\alpha}
with
\ell(\alpha)=s
. It follows that

(2)

\begin{split}\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{\alpha})=\operatorname{diam}(f(I_{\alpha}))\leq\operatorname{diam}(f(I))\leq C|f(b)-f(x)|^{t}.\end{split}

Here,
C
is the constant in the definition of
t
-quasicircle. Meanwhile, since
|a-x|/2\leq|b-x|/2\leq N^{-s+1}
it follows that
|a-x|\leq 2N^{-s+1}
and
|a-b|\leq|a-x|+|b-x|\leq 4N^{-s+1}
. Hence there is a sequence of arcs
I_{\alpha(1)},\dots,I_{\alpha(j)}
following one another such that
\ell(\alpha(i))=s
,
j\leq 4N+1
,
\alpha(1)=\alpha
and

\begin{split}|f(a)-f(x)|\leq 8Nc^{5N}\operatorname{diam}(\Gamma_{\alpha})\leq 8Nc^{5N}C|f(b)-f(x)|^{t}.\end{split}

, assume that

Since

3.
Preparations of conformal mappings on
{\mathbb{D}}

In this section some auxiliary results on conformal mappings are introduced. Basically, they concern about the estimate of
|f^{\prime}(z)|
for conformal mapping
f:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. For the most part, these results are well-known; however, we establish a more accurate estimate for the case
|z|\geq 1/2
. Through this section, let
M_{1},\;M_{2}\dots
denote suitable constants.

Recall that if
f=u+iv
is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{D}}
, it satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations on
{\mathbb{D}}
. If we rewrite these equations in polar coordinates, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v}{\partial\theta},\;\;\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}=-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta}.

Suppose that
f:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is conformal, thus
\log f^{\prime}=\log|f^{\prime}|+i\arg f^{\prime}
is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{D}}
. So we know that

(5)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log|f^{\prime}(z)|=-r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\arg f^{\prime}(z).\end{split}

Let
z=re^{i\theta}
. Then

\displaystyle z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\log f^{\prime}(z)=r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\log f^{\prime}(z)=r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\log|f^{\prime}(z)|+ir\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\arg f^{\prime}(z).

By (
5
), we find the imaginary part

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\arg f^{\prime}(z)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log|f^{\prime}(z)|.

The Koebe distortion theorem states that

\displaystyle|\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-\frac{2r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}|\leq\frac{4r}{1-r^{2}}.

It implies that

\displaystyle\frac{2r^{2}-4r}{1-r^{2}}\leq\operatorname{Re}\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\leq\frac{2r^{2}+4r}{1-r^{2}},

which is

(6)

\begin{split}\frac{2r-4}{1-r^{2}}\leq\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\log|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{2r+4}{1-r^{2}}.\end{split}

Meanwhile, we also have

\displaystyle-\frac{4r}{1-r^{2}}\leq\operatorname{Im}\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\leq\frac{4r}{1-r^{2}},

which is

(7)

\begin{split}-\frac{4r}{1-r^{2}}\leq\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{4r}{1-r^{2}}.\end{split}

Let
\zeta_{1}=r_{1}e^{i\alpha}
,
\zeta_{2}=r_{2}e^{i\beta}
where
0<r_{1}\leq r_{2}<1
and
\alpha,\beta\in(-\pi,\pi]
. Define
\zeta=r_{2}e^{i\alpha}
. Let
\gamma_{1}:=\{r_{2}e^{i\theta}|\;\theta:\beta\to\alpha\}
and
\gamma_{2}:=\{re^{i\alpha}|\;r:r_{2}\to r_{1}\}
. Here
\gamma_{1}
is the circular curve from
\zeta_{2}
to
\zeta
with radius
r_{2}
and
\gamma_{2}
is the line segment from
\zeta
to
\zeta_{1}
with argument
\alpha
. Then
\gamma:=\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}
is a piecewise smooth curve from
\zeta_{2}
to
\zeta_{1}
. Then (
6
) and (
7
) imply that

(8)

\begin{split}\log\frac{|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{1})|}{|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{2})|}=&\int_{\gamma}d\log|f^{\prime}(z)|=\int_{\gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\log|f^{\prime}(z)|dr+\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log|f^{\prime}(z)|d\theta\\
=&\int_{\gamma_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\log|f^{\prime}(z)|d\theta+\int_{\gamma_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\log|f^{\prime}(z)|dr\\
\leq&\frac{4r_{2}}{1-r_{2}^{2}}|\alpha-\beta|+\int_{r_{2}}^{r_{1}}\frac{2r-4}{1-r^{2}}dr\\
=&\frac{4r_{2}}{1-r_{2}^{2}}|\alpha-\beta|+\log\frac{1-r_{1}}{1-r_{2}}+3\log\frac{1+r_{2}}{1+r_{1}}\\
\leq&\frac{4r_{2}}{1-r_{2}^{2}}|\alpha-\beta|+\log\frac{1-r_{1}}{1-r_{2}}+\log 8.\end{split}

By (
8
), an estimate about
|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{1})|/|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{2})|
is established, which is crucial in the proof of Theorem
4.6
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a
t
-quasidisk and let
f
be the conformal mapping with the induced boundary value
f:{\mathbb{T}}\to\partial\Omega
. Then there exists an increasing injection
\eta:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that

(24)

\begin{split}\frac{|f(a)-f(x)|}{|f(b)-f(x)|^{t^{2}}}\leq\eta(k)\end{split}

for any
a,b,x\in{\mathbb{T}}
satisfy
|a-x|=k|x-b|
with
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
x=1
. Let
a=e^{i\theta_{1}}
and
b=e^{i\theta_{2}}
where
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(-\pi,\pi]
. Since
|a-1|\geq|b-1|
, there are
\zeta_{1}=r_{1}e^{i\alpha}
and
\zeta_{2}=r_{2}e^{i\beta}
with
1/2<r_{1}\leq r_{2}<1
and
|\alpha|=\pi(1-r_{1})
,
|\beta|=\pi(1-r_{2})
such that

\displaystyle I(\zeta_{1})={\mathbb{T}}(a,1)\;\;\text{ and }\;\;I(\zeta_{2})={\mathbb{T}}(b,1).

It is not hard to see that the arclength of
I(\zeta_{1})
is
2\pi(1-r_{1})
while the arclength of
I(\zeta_{2})
is
2\pi(1-r_{2})
. It is well-known that the unit circle is a chord-arc curve with ratio constant
\pi/2
, i.e., for any two points on
{\mathbb{T}}
, the length of the shortest arc between them is comparable to the length of the chord connecting them. Then
2\pi(1-r_{1})\leq\pi/2|a-1|
. It follows that

\displaystyle 4(1-r_{1})\leq|a-1|\leq 2\pi(1-r_{1}).

Similarly,

\displaystyle 4(1-r_{2})\leq|b-1|\leq 2\pi(1-r_{2}).

Then we obtain that

(25)

\begin{split}\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{1-r_{1}}{1-r_{2}}\leq\frac{|a-1|}{|b-1|}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{1-r_{1}}{1-r_{2}}.\end{split}

Lemma
4.4
implies that

\displaystyle|f(a)-f(1)|\leq M(1-|\zeta_{1}|)^{t}|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{1})|^{t},

while Lemma
4.5
implies that

\displaystyle|f(1)-f(b)|^{t}\geq N(1-|\zeta_{2}|)|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{2})|.

It follows from (
25
) that

(26)

\begin{split}\frac{|f(a)-f(1)|}{|f(1)-f(b)|^{t^{2}}}\leq\frac{M}{N^{t}}\frac{(1-r_{1})^{t}}{(1-r_{2})^{t}}\frac{|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{1})|^{t}}{|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{2})|^{t}}\leq\frac{M}{N^{t}}(\frac{k\pi}{2})^{t}\frac{|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{1})|^{t}}{|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{2})|^{t}}.\end{split}

Since
1-r_{1}\geq 1-r_{2}
, then
|\alpha-\beta|\leq\pi(1-r_{1})+\pi(1-r_{2})\leq 2\pi(1-r_{1})
. It follows from (
8
) and (
25
) that

\displaystyle\log\frac{|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{1})|}{|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{2})|}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4r_{2}}{1-r_{2}^{2}}|\alpha-\beta|+\log\frac{1-r_{1}}{1-r_{2}}+\log 8

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi r_{2}(1-r_{1})}{1-r_{2}^{2}}+\log(4\pi\frac{|a-1|}{|b-1|})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\pi^{2}\frac{|a-1|}{|b-1|}+\log(4\pi\frac{|a-1|}{|b-1|})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi^{2}k+\log(4\pi k),

thus

\displaystyle\frac{|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{1})|}{|f^{\prime}(\zeta_{2})|}\leq 4\pi ke^{4\pi^{2}k}.

Therefore we obtain from (
26
) that

\displaystyle\frac{|f(a)-f(1)|}{|f(b)-f(1)|^{t^{2}}}\leq(4\pi ke^{4\pi^{2}k})^{t}\frac{M}{N^{t}}(\frac{k\pi}{2})^{t}=:\eta(k).

It is not hard to verify that the function
\eta
is an increasing injection from
[0,\infty)
onto itself. The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 5.1
.

Every weakly
(R,t)
-quasisymmetric

Proof.

As we have explained that

\displaystyle s\leq\varphi(\frac{R|f(b)-f(x)|^{t}}{|f(a)-f(x)|^{1/t}/(2R)^{1/t}})=\varphi(\frac{(2R)^{1/t}R}{m^{t}}).

\displaystyle\log\frac{1}{k}<\varphi(\frac{(2R)^{1/t}R}{m^{t}})\log 4.

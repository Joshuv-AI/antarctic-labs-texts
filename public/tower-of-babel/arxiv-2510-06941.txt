Ricardo Campos, Bruno Vallette

Abstract
. We apply the effective integration theory of Lie-graph algebras, developed recently by the authors, to the deformation and homotopy theories of types of bialgebras, that is structures controlled by a properad, like associative bialgebras, (involutive) Lie bialgebras, Frobenius bialgebras, double Poisson bialgebras, pre-Calabi–Yau algebras, quantum Airy structures, etc. In these cases, we provide their associated Deligne groupoid with an explicit homotopical description. We settle the Koszul hierarchy and the twisting procedure on the properadic level. We also give a conceptual construction of the homotopy transfer theorem in terms of gauge actions. This work extends the formulas for the deformation theory of operadic algebras.



Introduction

Integration theory of Lie-graph algebras

The integration theory of complete Lie algebras into topological groups is performed via the intricate Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff (BCH) formula, see
[
BF12
]
. When the Lie bracket comes from the skew-symmetrisation of a binary product, this latter one is called
Lie-admissible
. Pre-Lie products are Lie-admissible operations satisfying an even stronger relation which allows one to consider exponential and logarithm isomorphisms mapping the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula to a simpler and effective group structure. In the same way, the action of this
gauge group
on Maurer–Cartan elements admits more efficient formulas in the pre-Lie case
[
DSV16
]
. This integration theory for pre-Lie algebras admits powerful applications to the deformation theory of morphisms of operads, including homotopy algebra structures
[
CPRNW24
,
DSV24
,
Emp24
]
.

We introduced in
[
CV25
]
an intermediate Lie-admissible algebraic structure bridging Lie algebras and pre-Lie algebras, termed
Lie-graph algebras
. This structure comprises an infinite family of operations indexed by directed connected graphs
The leading example for such a new algebraic structure is the deformation complex of morphisms of properads. This includes types of bialgebras that are structures equipped with operations having multiple inputs and multiple outputs. Such rich structures appear more and more nowadays in algebra, geometry, topology, and mathematical physics:
associative bialgebras, (involutive) Lie bialgebras
[
CFL20
]
, (involutive) Frobenius bialgebras
[
Yal18
]
,
double Poisson bialgebras
[
VdB08
,
Ler20
,
LV22
]
, pre-Calabi–Yau (bi)algebras
[
TZ07
,
KTV25
,
KTV23
]
, (balanced) infinitesimal bialgebras
[
Agu00
,
Que23
]
, quantum Airy structures
[
KS18
,
ABCO24
,
BV25
]
, etc.
The integration theory of Lie-graph algebras is fully settled in
[
CV25
]
: graphical exponential and logarithms isomorphisms, effective gauge group, and effective action on Maurer–Cartan elements.
The goal of the present paper is to apply these new fundamental results to universal constructions of homotopy bialgebras.

Deligne groupoid

The largest range of homotopy bialgebras for which all the required nice properties
(
\infty
-morphisms, homotopy transfer theorem, etc.)
have been established so far are the ones given by differential graded properads which are the cobar construction of a coproperad:
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}=\Omega\mathcal{C}
.
The deformation complex
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
of gebra structures over a properad of this form on a chain complex
A
admits a natural Lie-graph algebra structure
[
CV25
, Theorem 1.21]
.
Applying the aforementioned general integration theory of Lie-graph algebras to this example, we are first able to
describe explicitly the associated Deligne groupoid, which is made up of Maurer–Cartan elements related under the gauge group action.

Theorem
1.2
.

The Deligne groupoid associated to the Lie-graph algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
is given by
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-gebra structures on
A
and
\infty
-morphisms with first component equal to the identity of
A
:

\mathrm{Deligne}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A})\cong\left(\Omega\mathcal{C}\textrm{-}\,\mathrm{gebras},\infty\textrm{-}\,\mathrm{isotopies}\right)\ .

Universal constructions

In the operadic context, there exist three universal constructions of homotopy algebras: the homotopy transfer theorem
[
LV12
, Section 10.3]
, the Koszul hierarchy
[
Mar15b
]
, and the twisting procedure
[
DSV24
]
.
The first one, the
homotopy transfer theorem
provides us with explicit formulas for the transport of homotopy gebra structures through homotopy equivalences without any loss of algebraic-homotopical data.
It was recently extended to the level of properads in
[
HLV21
]
.
Generalising the operadic approach of
[
DSV16
]
, we give here the homotopy transfer theorem for
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebras a conceptual interpretation in terms of the gauge group action (
Theorem

2.1
), which emphasizes the perturbative aspect of this theory.

The Koszul hierarchy

In
Section

3
, we generalise to properads the construction known as the
Koszul hierarchy
, which originally is a method that
produces a (shifted)
\mathrm{L}_{\infty}
-algebra measuring the failure of a square-zero operator to be a derivation of a product in a given commutative algebra
[
Kos85
]
.
This construction is intimately related to the Koszul duality
\mathrm{Com}\text{-}\mathrm{Lie}
.
Using the gauge group action, we show that a similar procedure can be applied to any quadratic properad
\mathcal{P}
to construct a

\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structure from the data of a
\mathcal{P}^{!}
-gebra structure, where
\mathcal{P}^{!}
is the Koszul dual properad.
In order to appreciate the range of novelty brought by this new fundamental construction, one has to keep in mind that, contrary to the operadic case, there exists
no
bar-cobar construction between
\mathcal{P}
-gebras and
\mathcal{P}^{!}
-gebras for quadratic properads, for the simple reason that the notion of a free
\mathcal{P}
-gebra does not exist. So the present Koszul hierarchy provides us with the first universal construction of (homotopy)
\mathcal{P}
-gebras from
\mathcal{P}^{!}
-gebras.
To illustrate this phenomenon and to show how effective this new theory is, we make explicit
the homotopy involutive Lie bialgebra structures obtained from Frobenius bialgebra structures in this way (
Theorem

3.3
).
In order to do so, we introduce a general method which can be used to compute efficiently any case. For the first time, this establishes a universal construction of
symplectic field theories
from
topological field theories
.

The twisting procedure

In
Section

4
, we generalise to the properadic level the
twisting procedure
, which originally
takes in the data of an
\mathrm{L}_{\infty}
-algebra and one of its Maurer–Cartan element and
produces another
\mathrm{L}_{\infty}
-algebra structure, see
[
DSV24
]
. Interpreted in terms of the Maurer–Cartan moduli space, this procedure amounts to localise at the given Maurer–Cartan element. From a given quadratic properad
\mathcal{P}
, we explain how to enlarge the deformation Lie-graph algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{P}^{!},A}
controlling
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebras in order to produce a bigger gauge group including this time the elements of
A
.
By definition, the gauge action of these elements is not stable on
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebras, but produces in general
curved

\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebras. We coin the relevant general definition of
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-Maurer–Cartan equations
(
Definition

4.15
), which characterise the elements that produce back a
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra under their gauge group action. As usual with the present approach, this procedure is effective and receives the following formula.

Theorem
4.14
.

For any
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structure
\alpha
on a complete graded module
A
,
the gauge group action

(\mathbb{1}-x)\cdot\alpha=\alpha\lhd(\mathbb{1}+x)

of a
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-Maurer–Cartan element
x
produces a

\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structure .

This fundamental new form of the twisting procedure recovers all the known cases on particular algebras and bialgebra structures, like
homotopy Lie bialgebras
[
MW15
]
, homotopy involutive Lie bialgebras
[
CFL20
]
, and pre-Calabi–Yau algebras
[
LV22
]
for instance. Like the Koszul hierarchy, it is settled in such a way that it admits many degrees of freedom for a wider range of applications. For instance, it is shown that this twisting procedure can now accommodate not only the action of elements but also tensors of elements and more.

Range of applications

The fundamental results developed in the present paper actually apply to a wide range of recently studied properadic structures, some of them already mentioned above:
associative bialgebras (using the bar construction for the cooperad
\mathcal{C}
in order to get a model for their homotopy version), (involutive) Lie bialgebras, (involutive) Frobenius bialgebras,
double Poisson bialgebras, pre-Calabi–Yau (bi)algebras, (balanced) infinitesimal bialgebras, and quantum Airy structures.
This is why we expect that the present universal constructions will find even more applications in these domains in the future.

Conventions

For a uniform and light presentation, we work over a ground field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic
0
though many results hold under weaker hypotheses.
The underlying category is that of differential
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-vector spaces, using the homological degree convention, for which differentials have degree
-1
.
The symmetric groups are denoted by
\mathbb{S}_{n}
.
We use the conventions of
[
MV09a
,
MV09b
,
HLV21
,
CV25
]
for properads: for instance, we draw graphs with inputs at the top and outputs at the bottom. All the graphs present here will be directed by a global flow going from top to bottom.

1.
Deligne groupoid of homotopy gebras

Algebraic structures made up of operations with multiple inputs and multiple outputs can be encoded by the notion of a properad, which admits a Koszul duality theory
[
Val07
]
. Bialgebras over properads
\Omega\mathcal{C}
which are given by the cobar construction of a coproperad
\mathcal{C}
have nice deformation and homotopic properties.
First, they admit a convolution differential graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
whose Maurer–Cartan elements are in one-to-one correspondence with
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-gebra structures on
A
, see
[
MV09a
,
MV09b
]
.
Generalising the operadic case, a higher notion of
\infty
-morphism
was introduced recently in
[
HLV21
, Section 3.3]
for such homotopical properadic structures:
\infty
-endomorphisms are made up of a collection of maps which are packed into a map
\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{End}_{A}
satisfying some relation, see
[
HLV21
, Proposition 3.16]
.
The
\infty
-isotopies
are the
\infty
-endomorphisms whose first component
\mathcal{I}\to\mathrm{End}_{A}
is given by the identity
\mathrm{id}_{A}
.

Proposition 1.1
.

For any conilpotent dg coproperad
\mathcal{C}
, two
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-gebra structures are gauge equivalent if and only if they are
\infty
-isotopic.

Proof.

Recall from
[
CV25
, Proposition 1.27]
that the dg Lie-admissible convolution algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
extends to a complete dg Lie-graph algebra satisfying
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}=\mathrm{F}_{1}\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
, to which we can apply
[
CV25
, Theorem 2.29]
.
So two Maurer–Cartan elements
\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta}\in\mathrm{MC}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}\right)
are gauge equivalent if and only if there exists
\lambda\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\overline{\mathcal{C}},\mathrm{End}_{A}\right)_{0}
such that

\bar{\beta}=\exp(\lambda)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\alpha}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\text{\scalebox{1.2}{$\Join$}}}}}}\exp(-\lambda)-\left(\exp(\lambda);\mathrm{d}\big(\exp(\lambda)\big)\right)\circledcirc\exp(-\lambda)\penalty\ ,

in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
.
If we simply denote
\exp(\lambda)=\mathbb{1}+f
, with
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\overline{\mathcal{C}},\mathrm{End}_{A}\right)_{0}
, this relation becomes

\bar{\beta}=(\mathbb{1}+f)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\alpha}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\text{\scalebox{1.2}{$\Join$}}}}}}(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}-\left((\mathbb{1}+f);\partial(\mathbb{1}+f)\right)\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}\penalty\ ,

in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
.
The formula
(\ast)
given in the proof of
[
CV25
, Theorem 2.6]
shows that

(\mathbb{1}+f)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\alpha}}}{{\vbox{\hbox{\text{\scalebox{1.2}{$\Join$}}}}}}(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}=\left((\mathbb{1}+f)\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+\bar{\alpha})\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}\right)_{-1}\penalty\

since
f
has degree
0
and since
\bar{\alpha}
has degree
-1
; using the same idea, one can also notice that

\left((\mathbb{1}+f);\partial(\mathbb{1}+f)\right)\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}=\left(\big(\mathbb{1}+f+\partial(\mathbb{1}+f)\big)\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}\right)_{-1}\penalty\ .

To summarize, we get

(\mathbb{1}+\bar{\beta})_{-1}=\left((\mathbb{1}+f)\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+\bar{\alpha})\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}\right)_{-1}-\left(\big(\mathbb{1}+f+\partial(\mathbb{1}+f)\big)\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}\right)_{-1}\penalty\ ,

in
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathcal{C},A}
, which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left((\mathbb{1}+\bar{\beta})\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)\right)_{-1}=

\displaystyle\left((\mathbb{1}+f)\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+\bar{\alpha})\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)\right)_{-1}

\displaystyle-\left(\big(\mathbb{1}+f+\partial(\mathbb{1}+f)\big)\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}\circledcirc(\mathbb{1}+f)\right)_{-1}\penalty\ .

The direct implication is straightforward, the converse is obtained similarly by composing on the right-hand side by

(\mathbb{1}+f)^{-1}
.
This last equation is actually equal to

\bar{\beta}\lhd(\mathbb{1}+f)=(\mathbb{1}+f)\rhd\bar{\alpha}-\partial(\mathbb{1}+f)\penalty\ ,

which is the equation in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
of an
\infty
-isotopy
\mathbb{1}+f
between the two
\Omega\mathcal{C}
-gebra structures

\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta}\in\mathrm{MC}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}\right)
by
[
HLV21
, Proposition 3.16]
.
∎

Theorem 1.2
.

For any conilpotent dg coproperad
\mathcal{C}

and any dg module
A
, the Deligne groupoid associated to the complete dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
is isomorphic to the groupoid of

\Omega\mathcal{C}
-gebra structures on
A
with their
\infty
-isotopies:

\mathrm{Deligne}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A})\cong\left(\Omega\mathcal{C}\textrm{-}\,\mathrm{gebras},\infty\textrm{-}\,\mathrm{isotopies}\right)\ .

Proof.

This is a direct corollary of
Proposition

1.1
.
∎

This general result admits many applications, for instance one for each Koszul properad
\mathcal{P}
, see
[
Val07
]
: in this case, one considers the Koszul dual coproperad
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}}
. It shows that the Deligne groupoid associated to the convolution dg Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}},A}
encoding homotopy
\mathcal{P}
-gebra structures on
A
is isomorphic to the groupoid of
\infty
-isotopies.
For more details, we refer the reader
to
[
HLV21
, Section 6.1]
for the case of homotopy Lie bialgebras,
to
[
HLV21
, Section 6.4]
for the case of homotopy involutive Lie bialgebras (also known as
\mathrm{IBL}_{\infty}
-gebras),
to
[
LV22
, Section 4]
and to
[
KTV23
]
for the cases of homotopy double Poisson gebras,
\mathrm{V}_{\infty}
-gebras, pre-Calabi–Yau algebras,
and
to
[
Que23
, Section 5]
for the case of homotopy balanced infinitesimal bialgebras.
Quantum Airy structures introduced by Kontsevich–Soibelman in
[
KS18
]
, see also
[
ABCO24
]
, govern algebraically the
Eynard–Orantin topological recursion
[
EO07
,
EO09
]
. They are shown in
[
BV25
]
to be encoded by a Koszul properad, so

Theorem

1.2
applies to this case and describes the Deligne groupoid of their homotopical generalisation.
The
partition function
that computes the solutions to many problems in the enumerative geometry of complex curves and in low-dimensional quantum field theories
[
Eyn14
,
Eyn16
,
Bor20
]
,
is proved in
[
BV25
]
to be given by a certain gauge element acting on homotopy
quantum Airy structures
by the action formula of
[
CV25
, Theorem 2.29]
.
Finally, considering the bar-cobar resolution, that is
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{B}\,\mathrm{Bialg}
, one can also treat the case of homotopy associative bialgebras.

Corollary 1.3
.

For any conilpotent dg coproperad
\mathcal{C}

and any dg module
A
, the Deligne groupoid associated to the complete dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
is isomorphic to the groupoid made up of morphisms of dg properads from
\Omega\mathcal{C}
to
\mathrm{End}_{A}
and homotopies between them:

\mathrm{Deligne}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A})\cong\left(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{dg\ properad}}\left(\Omega\mathcal{C},\mathrm{End}_{A}\right),\sim_{h}\right)\ .

Proof.

We work in the model category structure on dg properads given in
[
MV09b
, Appendix A]
. This defines the notion of a homotopy between morphisms of dg properads.
For conilpotent dg coproperads
\mathcal{C}
, a cylinder object
\mathrm{Cyl}(\Omega\mathcal{C})
of the cobar construction
\Omega\mathcal{C}
has been given in
[
HLV25
, Proposition 1.12]
, where it has been shown to coincide with the dg properad encoding two

\Omega\mathcal{C}
-gebra structures related by an
\infty
-isotopy.
Theorem

1.2
applies to conclude the proof.

∎

Remark 1.4
.

This result extends
[
DSV16
, Corollary 2]
from the operad level to the properad level.

3.
Koszul hierarchy

The main obstruction to extend the operadic results to the properadic level is the absence of a free
\mathcal{P}
-gebra functor. As a consequence, we have (so far) no bar-cobar adjunction between
\mathcal{P}
-gebras and
\mathcal{P}^{!}
-gebras, which in the Koszul duality case
\mathrm{Com}^{!}\cong\mathrm{sLie}
provides us with the functors of rational homotopy theory of Quillen
[
Qui69
]
and Sullivan
[
Sul77
]
for instance.
An important but different construction between algebras over Koszul dual operads is given by the
Koszul hierarchy
.
Originally due to J.-L. Koszul
[
Kos85
]
and developed by M. Markl in
[
Mar15b
,
Mar15a
]
, the Koszul hierarchy is a method that takes as input a (graded) commutative algebra
A
together with a (degree 1) square-zero operator
\Delta
, and constructs a (shifted)
\mathrm{L}_{\infty}
-algebra on
A
such that the
n
-bracket vanishes if and only if
\Delta
is a differential operator of order
n-1
.
The Koszul hierarchy was interpreted conceptually with the pre-Lie deformation theory in
[
DSV16
, Section 5]
and thus extended to operads beyond the cases of commutative and associative algebras.
We now develop it further to the properadic level using the Lie-graph gauge action settled in the previous sections. This introduces for the first time a universal way to construct
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structures from
\mathcal{P}^{!}
-gebra structures, on the same underlying space.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a quadratic properad and let
(A,\mathrm{d})
be a chain complex viewed as an abelian
\mathcal{P}
-gebra structure.
Notice that the presence of the differential prevents it from being completely trivial.
Let
\theta:\mathcal{P}^{!}\to\mathrm{End}_{A}
be a
\mathcal{P}^{!}
-gebra structure on
A
.
Suppose that we have a (iso)morphism of
\mathbb{S}
-bimodules

\mathrm{K}:{\mathcal{P}}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}}\to\mathcal{P}^{!}

from the Koszul dual coproperad to the Koszul dual properad, which sends the counit to the unit, for instance via a chosen basis.
Such a data gives rise to the following element of the deformation gauge group associated to the convolution algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}}},A}
:

\Theta\coloneq\theta(\mathrm{K})\in\mathfrak{G}_{{\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}}},A}\ .

Definition 3.1

(Koszul hierarchy)
.

The
Koszul hierarchy
associated to the data
(A\,,\mathrm{d}\,,\theta\,,\mathrm{K})
is the
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structure on the chain complex
(A,\mathrm{d})
given by the action of
\Theta^{-1}
on
\mathrm{d}
:

\Theta^{-1}\cdot\mathrm{d}=-\left(\Theta^{-1};\mathrm{d}\left(\Theta^{-1}\right)\right)\circledcirc\Theta=\Theta^{-1}\circledcirc\left(\Theta;\mathrm{d}\Theta\right)\in\mathrm{MC}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}}},A}\right)\ .

The Koszul hierarchy provides us with a well-defined
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structure by
[
CV25
, Theorem 2.29]
.

Remark 3.2

Notice that the Koszul hierarchy retains almost no
homotopical
information since it produces a
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structure which is gauge trivial by definition.
Equivalently, the Koszul hierarchy is
\infty
-isotopic to the underlying chain complex with trivial
\mathcal{P}
-structure
by
Theorem

1.2

4.
Twisting procedure

In this section, we adapt the arguments of
[
DSV24
, Section 4.5]
,
with some slight modifications,
in order to settle the twisting procedure on the properadic level, whose heuristic is as follows.
We work with a filtered infinitesimal dg coproperad
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
and a complete dg module
A
: they give rise to
the complete convolution Lie-graph algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}=\left(\hom_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\overline{\mathcal{C}},\mathrm{end}_{A}\right),\mathrm{F},\partial,\{\mathrm{g}\}_{\mathrm{g}\in\mathsf{dsGra}}\right)

by
[
CV25
, Proposition 1.28]
.
Suppose that the filtered infinitesimal coproperad
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
admits an “extension” filtered counital infinitesimal coproperad
u\mathcal{C}

containing an arity
(1,0)
element
u
of degree
0
and such that
u\mathcal{C}
projects onto
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
under a map
\pi\,\colon\,u\mathcal{C}\twoheadrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{C}}
.
In this case, the original convolution algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
embeds into the convolution algebra associated to
u\mathcal{C}
:

\pi^{*}\ \colon\ \mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}=\hom_{\mathbb{S}}\big(\overline{\mathcal{C}},\mathrm{end}_{A}\big)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{u\mathcal{C},A}=\hom_{\mathbb{S}}\big(u\mathcal{C},\mathrm{end}_{A}\big)\ .

Under the assignment
u\mapsto a
, this bigger convolution algebra contains the elements of
A
; it also contains
the two elements

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{1}\ :\ u\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{end}_{A}}
and
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta\ :\ u\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{end}_{A}\penalty\ ,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}_{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial_{A}}

where
\varepsilon
is the counit of
u\mathcal{C}
.
Any Maurer–Cartan element
\bar{\alpha}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
gives a Maurer–Cartan
\bar{\alpha}\pi
in
\mathfrak{g}_{u\mathcal{C},A}
, that we prefer to view as
\alpha\coloneq\delta+\bar{\alpha}\pi
: notice that here
\delta
is not a formal element, so
\alpha
really denotes a square-zero element of degree
-1
of
\mathfrak{g}_{u\mathcal{C},A}
.
The deformation gauge group of
\mathfrak{g}_{u\mathcal{C},A}
is
\mathbb{1}+\mathrm{F}_{1}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{u\mathcal{C},A}\right)_{0}
, so it gives rise to a bigger deformation gauge group, which, for instance, contains the elements
a\in\mathrm{F}_{1}A_{0}
under the form

\mathbb{1}+a
. Such elements act on
\alpha
in
\mathfrak{g}_{u\mathcal{C},A}
to provide us with elements

(\mathbb{1}+a)\cdot\alpha\in\mathrm{MC}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{u\mathcal{C},A}\right)\ ,

This Maurer–Cartan element in
\mathfrak{g}_{u\mathcal{C},A}
induces a Maurer–Cartan element in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{C},A}
if and only if it factors through
\pi
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{end}_{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathcal{C}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{1}+a)\cdot\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

In practice, this properadic twisting procedure can be performed for instance in the following
way. Given a properadic quadratic data
(E,R)
, that is

R\subset\mathcal{G}(E)^{(2)}
, we consider the data
\chi
of:

•

two linear maps
\{\chi_{i}:E(1,2)\to\mathbb{k}\}_{i=1,2}
,

•

a certain choice of some basis elements
\left\{e^{\chi}_{m,n}\right\}
of

E(m,n)
, for any
(m,n)\neq(1,2)
,

•

for each
e^{\chi}_{m,n}
a choice of some inputs
\left\{i^{\chi}_{m,n}\right\}
.

Let
u
denote an arity
(1,0)
degree
0
element.
This gives rise to a space of relations
R_{\chi}\subset\mathcal{G}(E\oplus\mathbb{k}u)
,
made up of

\mu\circ_{1}^{1}u-\chi_{1}(\mu)\mathrm{id}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu\circ_{2}^{1}u-\chi_{2}(\mu)\mathrm{id}\ ,

with
\mu\in E(1,2)
, and the composites

e\circ_{i}^{1}u

for any
e\in\left\{e^{\chi}_{m,n}\right\}
,
any
i\in\left\{i^{\chi}_{m,n}\right\}
, and any
(m,n)\neq(1,2)
.

Definition 4.1

(Unital extension)
.

The
unital extension
of
\mathcal{P}\coloneq\mathcal{P}(E,R)
by
\chi
is the properad defined by

u_{\chi}\mathcal{P}\coloneq\mathcal{P}\big(E\oplus\mathbb{k}u,R\oplus R_{\chi}\big)\cong\frac{\mathcal{P}(E,R)\vee\mathcal{U}}{\left(R_{\chi}\right)}\ ,

where
\vee
stands for the coproduct of properads and where

\mathcal{U}
stands for the properad spanned
u
.

A
u_{\chi}\mathcal{P}
-gebra is a
\mathcal{P}
-gebra equipped with a degree
0
element which acts as a unit, possibly with coefficients, for some generating binary products and such that its composite at the chosen inputs of the chosen other generating operations vanish.

Definition 4.2

(Extendable quadratic data)
.

A properadic quadratic data
(E,R)
is
extendable
if there exists a data
\chi
with non-trivial
pair of maps
\{\chi_{i}:E(1,2)\to\mathbb{k}\}_{i=1,2}

such that the canonical map

\mathcal{P}\hookrightarrow u_{\chi}\mathcal{P}

is a monomorphism.

For instance, if one chooses to kill all the composites of
u
with any element of
E(m,n)
, for
(m,n)\neq(1,2)
, the extendability condition is equivalent to the maximality of the underlying
\mathbb{S}
-bimodule of the unital extension, that is

u_{\chi}\mathcal{P}\cong u\oplus\mathcal{P}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

(1)

The quadratic properads
\mathrm{Frob}
and
\mathrm{IFrob}
of (involutive) Frobenius bialgebras are extendable.

(2)

The quadratic properads
\mathrm{BiLie}
and
\mathrm{IBiLie}
of (involutive) Lie bialgebras are not extendable.

Proof.

Recall that the involutivity relation states the genus
1
composite of the coproduct followed by the product vanishes.

(1)

The cases of
\mathrm{Frob}
and
\mathrm{IFrob}
can be treated in the same way as the operad
\mathrm{Com}
of commutative algebras, see the proof of Proposition 4.21 in
[
DSV24
]
: in these cases, one requires that
u
is a unit for the binary product and one can require or not the binary coproduct to kill
u
.

(2)

The two cases
\mathrm{BiLie}
and
\mathrm{IBiLie}
contain the operad
\mathrm{Lie}
which is shown in
loc. cit.
not to be extendable regardless of any choices we make.

∎

Recall that a quadratic properad

The cobar construction

when this properad is Koszul.
The

is defined by the arity-wise and degree-wise linear dual, which admits the following presentation

Definition 4.11

(Twisting procedure)
.

Given a curved
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra

\alpha\in\mathrm{MC}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{c\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}},A}\right)

\alpha

\mathbb{1}-a
, associated to any element
a\in\mathrm{F}_{1}A_{0}

produces a
curved
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra

(\mathbb{1}-a)\cdot\alpha=\alpha\lhd(\mathbb{1}+a)\in\mathrm{MC}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{c\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}},A}\right)\ ,

a

This formula is a consequence of
[
CV25

or the identity at the inputs of the operations of the curved
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra
\alpha

[

, Theorem 4.20]
for curved homotopy double Poisson gebras, curved pre-Calabi–Yau algebras, and curved homotopy infinitesimal balanced bialgebras by
[

Remark 4.12

Under the aforementioned terminology,
\mathbb{1}-a

\infty

\alpha^{a}

Proposition 4.13
.

Any curved
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra twisted by an element

\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra twisted by the element

\left(\alpha^{a}\right)^{b}=\alpha^{a+b}

Proof.

This a direct corollary of the fact that we are dealing with a group action whose product satisfies

by

The issue is now to settle a twisting procedure for
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebras. The inclusion of properads

\mathcal{P}^{!}\hookrightarrow u_{\chi}\mathcal{P}^{!}
induces the projection of infinitesimal coproperads

c\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}}\twoheadrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}}
and thus the inclusion of complete Lie-graph algebras

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}},A}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{c\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}},A}
.
So any
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structure on
A
can be viewed as a curved
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structure that can thus be twisted.
But the result will
a priori
produce a curved
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra. It remains to measure the difference between these two notions.
To this extent, we consider the
\mathbb{S}
-bimodule
\mathcal{K}
which is the kernel of the projection map:

\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow c\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{P}}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}}\penalty\ .

Putting all the above arguments together, we finally get the twisting procedure for
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebras.

Theorem 4.14
.

For any complete
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structure
\bar{\alpha}\in\mathrm{MC}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}},A})
on
A
and for any
element
a\in\mathrm{F}_{1}A_{0}
,
the Maurer–Cartan element

(\mathbb{1}-a)\cdot\alpha=\alpha\lhd(\mathbb{1}+a)\in\mathrm{MC}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{c\mathcal{P}^{\scriptstyle\text{\rm!`}},A}\right)

of the extended convolution algebra is a
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra structure on
A
if and only if its
image on
\mathcal{K}
vanishes:

\big(\alpha\lhd(\mathbb{1}+a)\big)(\mathcal{K})=0\penalty\ .

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward consequence of the aforementioned arguments.
∎

This conceptual approach motives the following general definition.

Definition 4.15

(

-Maurer–Cartan equations)

The equations

\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-Maurer–Cartan equations

An element

satisfying them is called a

of the
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}
-gebra
\alpha
.

In the case of the operads
\mathrm{Ass}
and
\mathrm{Lie}
, the kernel
\mathcal{K}
is the one-dimensional module spanned by
u^{*}
.
This way, one recovers conceptually the usual Maurer–Cartan equations

da+\mu_{2}(a,a)+\mu_{3}(a,a,a)+\cdots=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad da+\tfrac{1}{2}\ell_{2}(a,a)+\tfrac{1}{3!}\ell_{3}(a,a,a)+\cdots=0

of
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-algebras and
\mathrm{L}_{\infty}
-algebras respectively. The Maurer–Cartan equation for pre-Calabi–Yau algebras is explicitly given in
[

, Definition 4.21]

-algebra.

Let us now treat in detail the twisting procedure for shifted homotopy involutive Lie bialgebras, using the notations introduced in
Section

3
.
First, the Koszul dual properad
\mathrm{sIBiLie}^{!}\cong\mathrm{Frob}
is extendable: we consider its extension given by
the properad
\mathrm{uFrob}
encoding

unital Frobenius bialgebras
where one unit for the commutative product is added and no extra relation between the unit and the coproduct is required. Its linear dual infinitesimal coproperad
\mathrm{uFrob}^{*}
admits for basis the elements

\left\{c_{m,n}^{g}\right\}_{m\geqslant 1,n,g\geqslant 0}
, which extend the basis of
\mathrm{Frob}^{*}
to
n=0
.
So the complete convolution Lie-graph algebra encoding
shifted curved homotopy involutive Lie bialgebras is the quantum Weyl algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathcal{\mathrm{uFrob}}^{*}},A}\cong\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geqslant 1\\
n,g\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}\hom\left(A^{\hat{\odot}n},A^{\hat{\odot}m}\right)\hbar^{g}

and the kernel
\mathcal{K}
of the canonical projection
\mathrm{uFrob}^{*}\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Frob}^{*}
is the
\mathbb{S}
-bimodule
concentrated in input arity
0
with

\mathcal{K}(m,0)\cong\mathbb{k}\left\{c_{m,0}^{g}\,,\ g\geqslant 0\right\}
. Given a shifted homotopy involutive Lie bialgebra structure

\left\{\nu_{m,n}^{g}\right\}_{m,n\geqslant 1,g\geqslant 0}
on a complete graded module
A
, its Maurer–Cartan elements are the elements

a\in\mathrm{F}_{1}A_{0}
satisfying the Maurer–Cartan equations:

\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\tfrac{1}{n!}\nu^{g}_{m,n}(a,\ldots,a)=0

for
m\geqslant 1
and
g\geqslant 0
.
(Notice that for
m=1
and
g=0
, we recover the Maurer–Cartan equation of the shifted homotopy Lie sub-algebra.)
In this case, the formulas

\left(\nu_{m,n}^{g}\right)^{a}=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\tfrac{1}{k!}\nu_{m,k+n}^{g}(\underbrace{a,\ldots,a}_{k\ \text{times}},-,\ldots,-)

define the shifted homotopy involutive Lie bialgebra twisted by
a
.
For shifted homotopy Lie bialgebras, that is shifted homotopy involutive Lie bialgebras concentrated in
g=0
, this recovers the
twisting procedure described in
[
MW15
, Appendix B]
, which is shown there to admit a nice application to the cohomology classes of the moduli spaces of curves with marked points.

This is the special case of
[
CFL20
, Proposition 9.3]
when
\mathfrak{m}_{l,g}=0
, except for
l=1
and
g=0
where

\mathfrak{m}_{1,0}=a
.
It is worth noticing that the present theory includes the full version of this result as follows. The entire space

\hom_{\mathbb{S}}\left(\mathcal{K},\mathrm{end}_{A}\right)\cong\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geqslant 1\\
g\geqslant 0\end{subarray}}A^{\hat{\odot}m}\hbar^{g}

is a direct summand of the complete convolution Lie-graph algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{{\mathcal{\mathrm{uFrob}}^{*}},A}
whose elements

\left\{a_{m}^{g}\in\allowbreak\mathrm{F}_{1}\left(A^{\hat{\odot}m}\right)_{0}\right\}_{m\geqslant 1,g\geqslant 0}

can be used to twist any shifted (curved) homotopy involutive Lie bialgebra
\alpha
using the deformation gauge group action:

\left(\mathbb{1}-\sum_{m\geqslant 1\atop g\geqslant 0}a_{m}^{g}\right)\cdot\alpha=\alpha\lhd\left(\mathbb{1}+\sum_{m\geqslant 1\atop g\geqslant 0}a_{m}^{g}\right)\penalty\ .

Such a formula amounts to plugging the elements
\left\{a_{m}^{g}\right\}_{m\geqslant 1,g\geqslant 0}
and the identity at the inputs of
\left\{\nu_{m,n}^{g}\right\}_{m,n\geqslant 1,g\geqslant 0}
in all the possible ways, which coincides with the formula of
loc. cit.
.

Remark 4.16
.

The procedure described here twists homotopy gebra structures with elements
a\in A
but it would work as well with “coelements”

f\in A^{*}
: all the arguments are similar under the upside-down (input-output) mirror symmetry.
Since the properads
\mathrm{BiLie}\text{-}\mathrm{Frob}_{\diamond}
and
\mathrm{BiLie}_{\diamond}\text{-}\mathrm{Frob}
are symmetric with respect to the
upside-down mirror symmetry, the formulas for the twisting procedure with a coelement are the same, up to this symmetry, than the formulas for the twisting with an element.
For instance, the notion of a “cocurved” Lie bialgebra can already be found in
[
Saf18
, Definition 2.24]
.

All these arguments show that, like the Koszul hierarchy, the fundamental twisting procedure established here
by means of deformation gauge group action
has many degrees of freedom and a wide range of possible applications.

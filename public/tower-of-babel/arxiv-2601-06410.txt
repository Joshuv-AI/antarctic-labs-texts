An NPDo Approach for Principal Joint Block Diagonalization
Ren-Cang Li, Ding Lu, Li Wang, Lei-Hong Zhang

Abstract
Matrix joint block-diagonalization ( jbd ) frequently arises from diverse applications such as independent component analysis, blind source separation, and common principal component analysis (CPCA), among others. Particularly, CPCA aims at joint diagonalization, i.e., each block size being 1 1 -by- 1 1 . This paper is concerned with principal joint block-diagonalization ( pjbd ), which aim to achieve two goals: 1) partial joint block-diagonalization, and 2) identification of dominant common block-diagonal parts for all involved matrices. This is in contrast to most existing methods, especially the popular ones based on Givens rotation, which focus on full joint diagonalization and quickly become impractical for matrices of even moderate size ( 300 300 -by- 300 300 or larger). An NPDo approach is proposed and it is built on a nonlinear polar decomposition with orthogonal polar factor dependency that characterizes the solutions of the optimization problem designed to achieve pjbd , and it is shown the associated SCF iteration is globally convergent to a stationary point while the objective function increases monotonically during the iterative process. Numerical experiments are presented to illustrate the effectiveness of the NPDo approach and its superiority to Givens rotation-based methods. Keywords: principal joint block-diagonalization, pjbd , principal joint diagonalization, pjd , common principal component analysis, NPDo, SCF Mathematics Subject Classification 62H25; 65F30; 65K05; 90C26

2.2
An Algorithm for NPDo

Theorem

2.2
provides the theoretical foundation of
the NPDo approach for optimization (
2.1
)
and naturally leads to the following fixed-point-type iteration for computing a maximizer:

\mathscr{H}(P^{(j)})=P^{(j+1)}\Lambda_{j}\quad\mbox{for $j=0,1,\ldots$},

(2.7)

where
P^{(j+1)}\in{\rm St}(k,n)
and
\Lambda_{j}\succeq 0
;
that is,
P^{(j+1)}\Lambda_{j}
is a polar decomposition of
\mathscr{H}(P^{(j)})
.
This iterative scheme resembles the well-known Self-Consistent-Field
(SCF) iterations for nonlinear eigenvector problems
and is referred to as the NPDoSCF
[
37

Alternatively,
in terms of the optimization problem (

each SCF step in (

search based on

2.1

In particular, given the current iterate

it follows from the lower bound (

for all

f(P)-f(P^{(j)})\geq\Re\tr\left(P^{\HH}\mathscr{H}(P^{(j)})\right)-\tr\left((P^{(j)})^{\HH}\mathscr{H}(P^{(j)})\right).

(2.8)

Therefore, to achieve a large increase in the objective value

we aim to maximize the right hand side over

P^{(j+1)}=\argmax_{P\in{\rm St}(k,n)}\Re\tr(P^{\HH}\mathscr{H}(P^{(j)})).

(2.9)

This optimization subproblem also arises in the classical

]

This monotonicity immediately implies the following global convergence result:

ca be taken as any lower bound on the eigenvalues of
A_{\ell}
, and numerically
it can be estimated rather efficiently
[
52
]

Reformulating each trace function

\tr((P_{i}^{\HH}A_{\ell}P_{i})^{2})
as

\displaystyle\tr((P_{i}^{\HH}A_{\ell}P_{i})^{2})

\displaystyle=\tr([P_{i}^{\HH}\widehat{A}_{\ell}P_{i}]^{2})+2\delta_{\ell}\tr(P_{i}^{\HH}\widehat{A}_{\ell}P_{i})+k_{i}\delta_{\ell}^{2}

\displaystyle=\tr([P_{i}^{\HH}\widehat{A}_{\ell}P_{i}]^{2})+2\delta_{\ell}\tr(P_{i}^{\HH}A_{\ell}P_{i})-k_{i}\delta_{\ell}^{2},

we obtain

\displaystyle f(P)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{t}\tr\left([P_{i}^{\HH}A_{\ell}P_{i}]^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{t}\tr\left([P_{i}^{\HH}\widehat{A}_{\ell}P_{i}]^{2}\right)+2\tr(P^{\HH}BP)-k\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\delta_{\ell}^{2},

(3.13)

where

B=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\delta_{\ell}\,A_{\ell}.

(3.14)

The expression of

) resembles

),
except that it has an extra constant term and

is not necessarily positive semidefinite.
To address the indefiniteness of

Case

.
Now that

(3.15)

a constant. It follows from (
3.13
) that

f(P)=\underbrace{\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{t}\tr\left([P_{i}^{\HH}\widehat{A}_{\ell}P_{i}]^{2}\right)}_{=:\widehat{f}(P)}+\underbrace{2\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\delta_{\ell}\tr(A_{\ell})-k\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\delta_{\ell}^{2}}_{\mbox{constant}}.

(3.16)

Consequently, optimization problem (
3.6
) is
equivalent to

\max_{P\in{\rm St}(n,n)}\,\widehat{f}(P).

(3.17)

Objective function
\widehat{f}(P)
takes the same form as

in (
3.6
),
with all trace terms
\tr\big([P_{i}^{\HH}\widehat{A}_{\ell}P_{i}]^{2}\big)

involving
\widehat{A}_{\ell}\succeq 0
.
Thus, the problem reduces to the positive semidefinite case as
discussed in subsection

and the NPDo approach applies.

Case

For the case, the term

is no longer a constant as in (

).
Similarly, we shift matrix

, to
a positive semidefinite one:

It then follows from (

) that

f(P)=\underbrace{\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{t}\tr\left([P_{i}^{\HH}\widehat{A}_{\ell}P_{i}]^{2}\right)+2\tr(P^{\HH}\widehat{B}P)}_{=:\widehat{f}(P)}+\underbrace{2k\delta_{0}-k\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\delta_{\ell}^{2}}_{\mbox{constant}}.

(3.18)

Again, by dropping the constant portion,

\max_{P\in{\rm St}(k,n)}\,\widehat{f}(P),

(3.19)

Now the objective function

takes the same form as

in (

In particular,

(3.20)

and for
Algorithm

2

of (

\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(Z):=\frac{\partial\widetilde{f}(Z)}{\partial Z}=4\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\Big[\widetilde{A}_{\ell}Z_{1}(Z_{1}^{\HH}\widetilde{A}_{\ell}Z_{1}),\ldots,\widetilde{A}_{\ell}Z_{t}(Z_{t}^{\HH}\widetilde{A}_{\ell}Z_{t})\Big]+4\widehat{B}Z.

5
Conclusion

We studied the problem of computing the most dominant
joint block-diagonalizer for a given set
\{A_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
of
N
Hermitian matrices
in
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
,
that partially and unitarily block-diagonalizes all
A_{\ell}
approximately and optimally.
To this end, we propose to maximize the total joint
block-diagonal part under a common partial
congruent transformation by an othonormal matrix for all
A_{\ell}
:

\max_{P\in{\rm St}(k,n)}\left\{f(P):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\|\BDiag_{\tau_{k}}(P^{\HH}A_{\ell}P)\|_{\F}^{2}\right\},

(5.1)

where
1\leq k\leq n
and
\tau_{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t})
as in (
3.1
) specifies the joint block-diagonal structure.
In contrast to most existing approaches that focus on the case
k=n

(i.e., the full joint block-diagonalization),
the new model allows for the more general case of
k\leq n
.
This is particularly important in modern applications involving
high dimensional data, where full block-diagonalization can be prohibitively
expensive and wasteful too, and a partial block-diagonalization with
k\ll n
is
really what one needs in practice. We name it
principal joint block-diagonalization
(
pjbd
) in general and

principal block-diagonalization
(
pjd
) which
aligns naturally with the goal of popular principal component analysis (PCA).

We propose to solve the
pjbd
problem (
5.1
) by the NPDo approach
developed in
[
37
]
, which is built on its strong
connection to numerical linear algebra to allow efficient computation.
This approach suits particularly well for large scale problems.
Two numerical solution techniques have been explored,
including the SCF iteration that globally converges
to a stationary point while produces a monotonically increasing convergent sequence
of the objective values,
and a subspace acceleration scheme based on the locally optimal conjugate
gradient technique.
Extensive numerical experiments demonstrated the
effectiveness of our methods and their superiority to Jacobi-type methods
for matrices of sizes
200
-by-
200
or larger in the case of
pjd
.

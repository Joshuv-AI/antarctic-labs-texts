Carleman Estimates and Controllability of Forward Stochastic Parabolic Equations with General Dynamic Boundary Conditions
Said Boulite, Abdellatif Elgrou, Lahcen Maniar and Abdelaziz Rhandi

Abstract
We derive a new Carleman estimate for a general backward stochastic parabolic equation with dynamic boundary conditions, incorporating weak divergence source terms in both the bulk and surface equations. This estimate is obtained through two main steps: first, by refining a known Carleman estimate for backward stochastic parabolic equations to explicitly account for the dependence of the parameters on the final control time T T ; second, by applying a duality technique to address weak divergence source terms. As an application, we prove the null and approximate controllability of forward stochastic parabolic equations with dynamic boundary conditions, which involve both reaction and convection terms with bounded adapted coefficients, as well as general second-order parabolic operators. Additionally, we provide an explicit estimate for the null-controllability cost in terms of the final control time T T and the coefficients of the equation.

Consider the following non-homogeneous linear backward stochastic parabolic equation subject to dynamic boundary conditions

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dz+\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{A}\nabla z)\,dt=(F_{1}+\nabla\cdot F)\,dt+Z\,dW(t)&\text{in}\,Q,\\
dz_{\Gamma}+\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma})\,dt-\partial_{\nu}^{\mathcal{A}}z\,dt=(F_{2}-F\cdot\nu+\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot F_{\Gamma})\,dt+\widehat{Z}\,dW(t)&\text{on}\,\Sigma,\\
z_{\Gamma}=z|_{\Gamma}&\text{on}\,\Sigma,\\
(z,z_{\Gamma})|_{t=T}=(z_{T},z_{\Gamma,T})&\text{in}\,G\times\Gamma,\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
(z,z_{\Gamma};Z,\widehat{Z})
is the state variable,

(z_{T},z_{\Gamma,T})\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2})

F_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))

The operators
\nabla\cdot
(in

\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot
(on

\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{A}\nabla z)
becomes the Laplacian

\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma})

\Gamma
, where the tangential gradient
\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}:=\nabla z-(\partial_{\nu}z)\nu
is used. In this case, if
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}=I_{N}
, the surface diffusion term simplifies to the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}
on
\Gamma
. For further details on these geometric tools and on dynamic boundary conditions, we refer the reader to
[
17
,
20
,
28
,
33
]
.

Similarly to the standard Sobolev spaces
H^{k}(G)
(

), we introduce the Sobolev spaces
H^{k}(\Gamma)
(
k=1,2
) on the boundary
\Gamma
as follows:

H^{k}(\Gamma)=\left\{z_{\Gamma}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\mid\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\in H^{k-1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\},\quad k=1,2,

which are endowed with the following norms:

|z_{\Gamma}|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}=\sqrt{\langle z_{\Gamma},z_{\Gamma}\rangle_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}},\quad\text{where}\quad\langle y_{\Gamma},z_{\Gamma}\rangle_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}=\int_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\,d\sigma+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\,d\sigma,

|z_{\Gamma}|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}=\sqrt{\langle z_{\Gamma},z_{\Gamma}\rangle_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}},\quad\text{where}\quad\langle y_{\Gamma},z_{\Gamma}\rangle_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}=\int_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\,d\sigma+\int_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}y_{\Gamma}\,\Delta_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\,d\sigma.

In the sequel, we also define the following Hilbert spaces:

\mathbb{H}^{k}=\{(z,z_{\Gamma})\in H^{k}(G)\times H^{k}(\Gamma):z_{\Gamma}=z|_{\Gamma}\},\quad k=1,2,

equipped with the usual inner product

\langle(y,y_{\Gamma}),(z,z_{\Gamma})\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{k}}=\langle y,z\rangle_{H^{k}(G)}+\langle y_{\Gamma},z_{\Gamma}\rangle_{H^{k}(\Gamma)}.

In this paper, the following property

(\textbf{z},\textbf{z}_{\Gamma})=(z_{1},z_{2})\quad\text{in}\;G\times\Gamma,\quad\textnormal{a.s.},

is understood as follows:

\textbf{z}=z_{1}\quad\text{in}\;G\quad\text{and}\quad\textbf{z}_{\Gamma}=z_{2}\quad\text{on}\;\Gamma,\quad\textnormal{a.s.}

Remark 1.1
.

In this paper, the concept of vectors on the boundary
\Gamma
is understood in the context of vector fields on the manifold
\Gamma
. More precisely, these vectors are functions
\gamma:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that for each

\gamma(x)
lies in the tangent space

T_{x}\Gamma=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid p\cdot\nu(x)=0\}.

This assumption allows for simplifications in the computation of certain boundary terms via the tangential divergence formula:

\int_{\Gamma}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot\gamma)\,z_{\Gamma}\,d\sigma=-\int_{\Gamma}\gamma\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\,d\sigma,

for
\gamma\in H^{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
z_{\Gamma}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
. However, when the vector field
\gamma:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is not necessarily tangential, one needs to use the

(see
[
16

\int_{\Gamma}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot\gamma)\,z_{\Gamma}\,d\sigma=-\int_{\Gamma}\gamma\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\,d\sigma+\int_{\Gamma}(\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot\nu)\,(\gamma\cdot\nu)\,z_{\Gamma}\,d\sigma.

This identity introduces additional boundary-like terms that need to be carefully treated.

Theorem 1.3
.

Equation (

) is null controllable at time

. That is, for any initial state

Moreover, the controls

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\widehat{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G_{0}))}+\|\widehat{v}_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}+\|\widehat{v}_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}\leq e^{CK}\|(y_{0},y_{\Gamma,0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2})},\end{aligned}

Remark 1.2
.

In (
1.7
), by acting on the system with the controls

,
v_{1}
, and
v_{2}
, we aim to influence the final behavior of the evolution. The conormal derivative

serves as the coupling term between the bulk equation in

and the surface equation on

,

, and

directly affect the drift term through the control

on the small region

of the bulk equation, and the diffusion terms of both the bulk and surface equations via the extra controls

and

, which act throughout the entire spatial domain

, these controls also influence the drift term of the surface equation. This interplay allows us to establish the null and approximate controllability of (

).

This paper investigates the null and approximate controllability of a class of stochastic parabolic equations with dynamic boundary conditions. For results on the controllability of deterministic parabolic equations with such boundary conditions, we refer the reader to
[
28
]
,
[
1
]
, and
[
20
]
. The case of fully discrete parabolic equations is addressed in
[
21
]
. Additionally, numerous results on the controllability of stochastic partial differential equations are available in the literature; for a comprehensive discussion, we refer the reader to the book
[
27
]
and the references therein.

The controllability of stochastic parabolic equations with various boundary conditions—both static and dynamic—has been extensively studied. Specifically, for static boundary conditions, controllability has been investigated for Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin boundary conditions. For Dirichlet boundary conditions,
[
2
]
was among the first to establish controllability results for forward stochastic parabolic equations with a localized control in the drift term, under certain restrictive assumptions on the system’s coefficients. Later,
[
32
]
extended these results using global Carleman estimates to address the controllability of general forward stochastic parabolic equations, introducing an additional control in the diffusion term and considering only bounded adapted zero-order coefficients that may depend on space, time, and random variables. We also refer to
[
14
]
where a general weighted identity for some stochastic operators with complex principal parts is established and then used to study controllability of certain type of stochastic partial differential equations as stochastic complex Ginzburg–Landau equations. In
[
32
]
, the authors achieved these results by introducing a second control acting on the diffusion term across the entire domain. The work
[
3
]
further investigates the controllability of more general stochastic parabolic equations, incorporating both zero- and first-order terms, under the assumption that all coefficients are only bounded and adapted. In
[
24
,
35
]
, the Lebeau-Robiano strategy was applied to explore both approximate and null controllability of the forward stochastic heat equation with a single control in the drift term, assuming space-independent coefficients. Additionally, the controllability of some classes of stochastic parabolic equations with Robin boundary conditions has been studied in
[
7
,
34
]
. For results on the null controllability of a class of stochastic semidiscrete parabolic equations, see
[
37
]
.

For controllability results of stochastic parabolic equations with dynamic boundary conditions, we refer to
[
6
]
for forward and backward stochastic parabolic equations with only reaction terms,
[
5
]
for a class of one-dimensional stochastic heat equations,
[
4
]
for backward stochastic parabolic equations that incorporate both reaction and convection terms, and
[
8
]
for the case of the forward stochastic heat equation with a time-space localized control in the drift term of the bulk equation, without extra forces. For controllability results concerning certain coupled forward-backward stochastic parabolic equations, we refer the reader to
[
23
,
29
]
.

Following the classical duality argument, the controllability of linear stochastic partial differential equations, as explored in the previously cited works, can be proven through an observability problem for the corresponding adjoint equation. This involves unique continuation for approximate controllability and an observability inequality for null controllability. A key tool in proving these observability results (for both parabolic PDEs and SPDEs) is Carleman estimates, which provide energy estimates with exponential weights and parameters sufficiently large to absorb undesired lower-order terms. Carleman estimates, first introduced by Carleman in 1939 to prove the uniqueness of solutions for second-order elliptic partial differential equations with two variables (
[
9
]
), are essential not only for analyzing control problems (see
[
27
]
) but also for solving certain inverse problems (e.g.,
[
25
,
26
]
).

To the best of our knowledge, this paper is the first to investigate the null and approximate controllability of a more general class of linear forward stochastic parabolic equations with dynamic boundary conditions. This class includes both reaction and convection terms, as well as general second-order parabolic operators. Furthermore, we establish the estimate (
1.8
) for the null-controllability cost of (
1.7
), which is expressed in terms of the final control time
T
and the coefficients
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
B_{1}
, and
B_{2}
.

Now, some remarks are in order.

Remark 1.3
.

In equation (

and

influence the diffusion terms in the whole domain

(resp.,

Remark 1.4

The surface diffusion term

Remark 1.5

In

However, for a broad class of nonlinearities, as discussed in

Remark 1.6

Lemma 1.2

For any

for all

\displaystyle\textbf{I}_{7}=-\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}(\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma})_{t}\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}d\sigma dt\leq C\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}|\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}|^{2}d\sigma dt.

(A.28)

It is not difficult to see that

\displaystyle\textbf{I}_{8}=2\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}\partial_{\nu}^{\mathcal{A}}z\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot(\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma})d\sigma dt\leq\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}|\partial_{\nu}^{\mathcal{A}}z|^{2}d\sigma dt+\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}|\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}|^{2}d\sigma dt.

(A.29)

On the other hand, we have that

\displaystyle\textbf{I}_{9}=2\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}\widetilde{A}z_{\Gamma}\partial_{\nu}^{\mathcal{A}}zd\sigma dt

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}\left[-\frac{2}{\beta_{0}}\|\mathcal{A}\|_{\infty}\lambda\mu\varphi\partial_{\nu}^{\mathcal{A}}\psi+\lambda T\varphi^{2}O(e^{2\mu|\psi|_{\infty}})\right]z_{\Gamma}\partial_{\nu}^{\mathcal{A}}zd\sigma dt.

It follows that

\displaystyle\textbf{I}_{9}\leq C\lambda\mu\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}\varphi|z_{\Gamma}||\partial_{\nu}^{\mathcal{A}}z|d\sigma dt+C\lambda Te^{2\mu|\psi|_{\infty}}\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}\varphi^{2}|z_{\Gamma}||\partial_{\nu}^{\mathcal{A}}z|d\sigma dt,

which implies that for large
\lambda\geq C(e^{2\mu|\psi|_{\infty}}T+T^{2})
,

\displaystyle\textbf{I}_{9}\leq C\lambda^{2}\mu\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}\varphi^{2}|z_{\Gamma}||\partial_{\nu}^{\mathcal{A}}z|d\sigma dt.

(A.30)

Applying Young’s inequality for (
A.30
), we derive that

\displaystyle\textbf{I}_{9}\leq C\lambda^{3}\mu^{2}\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}z_{\Gamma}^{2}d\sigma dt+C\lambda\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}\varphi|\partial_{\nu}^{\mathcal{A}}z|^{2}d\sigma dt.

(A.31)

Combining (
A.13
), (

), (

), (

), (

), (

), (

), (

), (

), and taking a large enough

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &2\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}c^{jk}h_{x_{j}}h_{x_{k}}dxdt+\mathbb{E}\iint_{Q}Bh^{2}dxdt\\
&+\lambda^{3}\mu^{3}\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}z_{\Gamma}^{2}d\sigma dt+\lambda\mu\mathbb{E}\iint_{\Sigma}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla_{\Gamma}z_{\Gamma}|^{2}d\sigma dt\\

(A.32)

Step 3. Estimating the non-boundary terms.

, we have that for large

Taking a large

(A.33)

Notice that

Indocyanine green fluorescence for intraoperative detection of liver tumours in minimally invasive surgery: protocol for the LIVERGREEN phase IV multicentre clinical trial

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionLiver tumours are a leading cause of global morbidity and mortality. Indocyanine green (ICG) has emerged as a promising tool for improving liver tumour detection. Background

Liver tumours represent one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide.1 2 There is a well-established consensus on the impact of achieving disease-free surgical resection margins (R0) on patient survival.3 4 Preoperative computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and intraoperative ultrasound (IOUS) are the primary diagnostic tools for detecting liver neoplasms. However, these techniques have limitations, with suboptimal specificity and sensitivity.5,7

Fluorescence imaging using indocyanine green (ICG) has emerged as an innovative tool, gaining popularity in the field of image-guided surgery. ICG is a safe molecule that selectively accumulates in hepatocellular carcinoma tissue and peritumoural areas of adenocarcinomas.8 When exposed to near-infrared light (830 nm), ICG emits fluorescence, potentially facilitating real-time tumour detection.9

Recent studies have suggested that ICG fluorescence may enhance intraoperative identification of neoplastic tissue when combined with other imaging modalities.710,13 However, the clinical significance of newly detected lesions identified solely by ICG fluorescence remains unclear. Current guidelines14 recommend considering surgical resection only if additional evidence, such as visual inspection, digital palpation or intraoperative ultrasonography, supports the presence of a tumour.

The present study aims to test the hypothesis that preoperative administration of ICG improves the intraoperative detection of malignant liver tumours in minimally invasive surgery, compared with the standard use of preoperative imaging test and IOUS. Additionally, a machine-learning algorithm will be developed to enhance liver tumour detection through the photographic analysis of ICG fluorescence images.

Methods and analysis

Objectives

Primary endpoints

The first primary endpoint is the diagnostic performance of intraoperative ICG fluorescence imaging for the detection of malignant liver lesions during minimally invasive surgery. The primary analysis will be performed at the lesion level. Successful detection will be defined as the intraoperative identification of a lesion using ICG fluorescence and/or IOUS that corresponds to a histologically confirmed malignant lesion in the resected specimen. Histopathological examination of resected lesions will serve as the reference standard for confirming the presence and nature of malignant liver lesions.

Design

This study is a prospective, non-randomised, phase IV multicentre clinical trial. Due to its methodological characteristics, the study is classified as a low-intervention clinical trial.

The study framework has been designed to assess differences in liver tumour detection capabilities among ICG fluorescence imaging, IOUS and preoperative imaging tests. The study adheres to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines. The SPIRIT 2025 Checklist is provided as online supplemental material 1.

The planned recruitment period for the study will take place between November 2024 and December 2026. At present, 70 patients have already been enrolled. Data analysis and the development of the artificial intelligence (AI) algorithm for liver tumour detection are expected to be completed by mid-2027.

Study scope

As a multicentre study, the participating institutions include Hospital Universitari Vall d’Hebron, Hospital Universitari Dr. Josep Trueta, Hospital Universitari de Bellvitge and Hospital de la Santa Creu i Sant Pau. These are all academic tertiary medical centres located in Catalonia, Spain.

Presurgery

During the presurgery visit, demographics and comorbidities (Charlson Comorbidity Index) will be recorded. A clinical laboratory assessment, including biochemical and haematological parameters, will be conducted.

Liver cirrhosis will be defined based on previous clinical diagnosis documented in the medical record, compatible radiological findings (eg, nodular liver surface, segmental hypertrophy, splenomegaly, or signs of portal hypertension), or histological confirmation when available.

All patients must undergo contrast-enhanced MRI, multiphasic CT or PET-CT scan prior to surgery. Ideally, Primovist-enhanced MRI 4 weeks prior to surgery will be considered the gold standard preoperative imaging modality. The detailed optimal MRI protocol is provided as online supplemental material 3.

Preoperative phase

ICG will be administered intravenously 24 hours before surgery in an outpatient (day hospital) setting. ICG dosing will be adjusted as follows:

0.2 mg/kg intravenous for patients with cirrhosis or a history of previous systemic chemotherapy.

0.2 mg/kg intravenous for patients with cirrhosis or a history of previous systemic chemotherapy.

0.5 mg/kg intravenous for all other patients.

3–5 weeks follow-up visit

During this visit, early postoperative complications will be assessed, and a histopathological analysis of the resected lesions will be performed.

90-day follow-up visit

During this visit, postoperative complications, morbidity and mortality will be evaluated. Additionally, an imaging test will be performed within 3 months after surgery to assess tumour recurrence (yes/no) and determine the number and location of new lesions, if present, based on the radiology report.

Statistical analysis

Sample size

Paired measurements (repeated within the same group) will be performed. The sample size has been calculated based on results from a preliminary study conducted at the lead centre (Hospital Universitari Vall d’Hebron).13 In the preliminary study, the mean number of histologically confirmed malignant lesions identified per patient preoperatively was 1.7 (SD 1.34), and the mean number of malignant lesions identified per patient after using ICG was 2.2 (SD 1.87). Accepting an alpha risk of 0.05, a 95% power, a correlation of at least 0.4 between the techniques, and anticipating a 20% drop-out rate, a total of 173 subjects will be required to detect a statistically significant difference, with an initial mean of 1.7 and a final mean of 2.2.

Recruitment

The estimated recruitment period is 3 years, starting from the initiation of the study across the four participating centres.

Statistical analysis

The analysis population will include all enrolled patients who undergo surgery and intraoperative fluorescence imaging according to the study protocol. Continuous variables with symmetric distribution will be reported as mean (SD), whereas non-normally distributed variables will be presented as median (IQR). Categorical variables will be expressed as counts and percentages. Comparisons between groups will be performed using the χ² test for categorical variables and the Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test for paired continuous variables, as appropriate. The McNemar test will be used for differences in paired proportions. A p<0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Because multiple lesions may occur within the same patient, the primary analysis will be conducted at the lesion level, accounting for within-patient clustering using mixed-effects models. The study centre will be included as a random effect to account for the multicentre design.

Histopathological examination of resected specimens will be used as the reference standard for diagnostic accuracy analyses. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value and the area under the curve will be calculated at the lesion level. Receiver operating characteristic curves will be generated using quantitative fluorescence intensity measurements obtained from intraoperative imaging.

Lesions identified intraoperatively but not resected will be recorded and followed during the 90-day postoperative follow-up, including postoperative imaging, in order to determine whether they correspond to malignant disease and to reduce potential verification bias.

Exploratory subgroup analyses will be performed according to tumour histology, liver parenchymal background, prior systemic chemotherapy exposure and type of preoperative imaging modality.

No interim analyses are planned. Statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS Statistics V.27.0 (IBM) and Stata V.15.1 (StataCorp).

AI-assisted algorithm

After enrolment of the first 100 patients, an AI-based algorithm will be developed to characterise patterns and intensity of ICG uptake in liver lesions detected during surgery.

The analysis will be performed at the lesion level, using intraoperative fluorescence images as input data. Lesions will be annotated according to intraoperative findings and the corresponding histopathological diagnosis, which will serve as the reference label.

The dataset from the first 100 patients will be divided into training and internal validation subsets. Supervised machine learning methods, including neural network models, will be applied to identify fluorescence patterns associated with malignant lesions and to estimate the probability of malignancy.

Model performance will be iteratively improved as additional cases are incorporated into the dataset. Once trained, the model will be evaluated for its ability to estimate the probability that a detected lesion corresponds to malignant tissue based on intraoperative fluorescence imaging characteristics.

Data management and monitoring

Study data will be collected and managed using Research Electronic Data Capture tools, hosted at Vall d’Hebron Institut de Recerca.

The Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) is appointed by the sponsor and includes the clinical trial manager and three clinical trial monitors. The primary responsibilities of the DMC are to train all researchers during the initial visit, verify the accuracy of the data and ensure compliance with the protocol during subsequent visits throughout the recruitment period. Regular monitoring will be conducted in accordance with ICH guidelines for Good Clinical Practice.

Discussion

The use of fluorescence with ICG for liver tumour detection has gained significant relevance in liver surgery, particularly in minimally invasive procedures. Growing literature on its utility highlights not only its ability to identify new lesions79,13 but also its potential for evaluating surgical margins15 16 and providing a tool for hepatic parenchyma demarcation for anatomical liver resections.17,20 Additionally, ICG fluorescence has proven useful in assessing tissue vascularisation after surgical resection,21 as well as in detecting bile leaks or localising bile ducts.22 23 These features position ICG as a key tool in hepatic resection, contributing to safer procedures and improved postoperative outcomes.

However, there is still no consensus on the use of ICG in liver tumour detection. Previous studies, such as those by Wakabayashi et al,24 have shown variability in dosage, timing of preoperative administration and the effectiveness of fluorescence, highlighting the lack of uniform evidence in this area. Nevertheless, preliminary results on the use of ICG are promising. In the context of this study, we aim to employ a standard dose of ICG administered 24 hours prior to surgery. This dosage is intended to maximise sensitivity in liver tumour detection, which is crucial for proper surgical resection.

Previous analyses conducted at Vall d'Hebron University Hospital between 2019 and 202013 confirmed that the combination of ICG and IOUS yielded the best results in terms of accuracy, sensitivity and specificity compared with traditional techniques like MRI and IOUS, with a sensitivity of 97.7% and specificity of 44% for ICG. These results emphasise the potential improvement in intraoperative detection of liver lesions using ICG as a complementary tool.

The homogeneity in ICG dosage, the strict preoperative and postoperative radiological control, and the use of the same fluorescence camera across the participating centres adds robustness to the study and ensures consistency in the results. This trial will be one of the first of its kind in the field of minimally invasive surgery, providing innovative value by addressing an emerging technology in the context of hepatic resection.

Moreover, the development of artificial intelligence in medicine,25,28 specifically in the interpretation of ICG images, represents a significant advancement. The use of machine learning algorithms and neural networks for the photographic analysis of liver lesions could enhance the accuracy in assessing malignancy and offer additional data regarding the pathological anatomy and behaviour of lesions. The development of this technology in conjunction with ICG could create a synergistic effect, optimising decision-making during surgery and improving long-term outcomes for patients.

This study seeks to provide further evidence on the efficacy of ICG in minimally invasive hepatic surgery and create a platform for integrating emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence, which promises to transform the approach to liver tumours in the surgical setting.

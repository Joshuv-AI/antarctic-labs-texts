Group Knowledge of Hypothetical Values
Alexandru Baltag, Sonja Smets

Abstract
In recent years, epistemic logics have been extended with operators K a ​ x K_{a}x for knowledge of (the value of) a variable x x (by an agent a a ). We study dynamic versions of these logics, enriched with modalities for semi-public data-exchange events (e.g., public announcements, data-sharing within a subgroup, or changing the value of a variable). To obtain a complete axiomatization of data-exchange events, in the presence of equality x = y x=y and K a ​ x K_{a}x , one needs to extend the logic further: first, with an operator for distributed knowledge K A ​ x K_{A}x of the value (by a group of agents A A ); next, with a conditional version of this: distributed knowledge K A φ ​ x K_{A}^{\varphi}x (of the value by a group) given some hypothetical condition (expressed by some proposition φ \varphi ); then, with definite descriptions x A φ x_{A}^{\varphi} , denoting the ‘hypothetical’ value of x x according to A A ’s (distributed) knowledge given condition φ \varphi . In order to deal with common knowledge in the presence of semi-public data exchanges, we also need to add a novel conditional version of the recent concept of common distributed knowledge . We investigate the resulting logic, giving examples and presenting a complete axiomatization and a decidability proof.

6
Completeness and Decidability for the dynamic logic
DLKV

In this section we prove Theorem
2
. We first establish our co-expressivity result:
DLKV
and
LKV
are provably co-expressive
. For this, we need a few preliminary notions and results.

Expressions and subexpression-complexity
An
expression

\varepsilon
of
DLKV
is any formula
\varphi
, term
x
or event
e
of
DLKV
. The
sub-expression complexity order

>
is the natural extension to expressions of the usual notion of subformula-complexity; more precisely,
>
is the least transitive relation on expressions, s.t.: (1) every formula is
>
all its subformulas and all terms occurring in it; (2) every term is
>
all its subterms; (3) every event
e=\Phi/\sigma
is
>
than all formulas
\varphi\in\Phi
and all postconditions
\sigma(v)
(for all
v\in V
); and (4) the formula
[e]\varphi
is
>e
.

It is easy to see that
<
is a well-founded partial order
.

Reducible expressions

A formula
\theta
in
DLKV
is
reducible
if it is provably equivalent to a ’static’ formula; i.e., if there exists some formula
\theta^{\prime}
in the static fragment
LKV
s.t.

\vdash\,\theta\leftrightarrow\theta^{\prime}

is provable in
\mathbf{DLKV}
. A term
x\in Var_{DLKV}
is
reducible
if it is provably equal to a static term; i.e., if there exists some
x^{\prime}\in Var_{LKV}
s.t.
\vdash\,x=x^{\prime}
is provable in
\mathbf{DLKV}
. Finally, an event
e=\Phi/\sigma
is
reducible
if all formulas
\varphi\in\Phi
and all its post-conditions
\sigma(v)
are reducible (for all
v\in V
).

Observation
.
If
e=\Phi/\sigma
is a reducible event, then its precondition
pre_{e}:=\bigwedge\Phi
is a reducible formula
.

Lemma 13
.

The following are provable in
\mathbf{DLKV}
:

1.

(Derived Reduction Axiom for Conjunction)

\vdash\,[e](\varphi\wedge\psi)\leftrightarrow\left([e]\varphi\wedge[e]\psi\right)

2.

(Existential Dynamic Modality)

\vdash\langle e\rangle\theta\leftrightarrow\left(pre_{e}\wedge[e]\theta\right)

Proof.

The first is a standard consequence of
[e]
-Necessitation and
[e]
-Distributivity, that holds for all normal modalities. The second follows immediately from the Partial Functionality reduction axiom, together with the definition of
\langle e\rangle\theta
as an abbreviation for
\neg[e]\neg\theta
.
∎

Lemma 14
.

(Replacement of Equivalents)

Suppose that
\vdash\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\prime}
,
\vdash\theta\leftrightarrow\theta^{\prime}
,
\vdash x=x^{\prime}
,
\vdash y=y^{\prime}
and
\vdash x_{i}=x^{\prime}_{i}
(for all
i=1,n
) are provable in
\mathbf{DLKV}
. Then the following are also provable in
\mathbf{DLKV}
:

1.

\vdash x|_{\varphi}y=x^{\prime}|_{\varphi^{\prime}}y^{\prime}

2.

\vdash F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=F(x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n})

3.

\vdash x_{A}^{\varphi}=(x^{\prime})_{A}^{\varphi^{\prime}}

4.

\vdash e(x)=e(x^{\prime})

5.

\vdash Px_{1}\ldots x_{n}\leftrightarrow Px^{\prime}_{1}\ldots x^{\prime}_{n}

6.

\vdash\neg\varphi\leftrightarrow\neg\varphi^{\prime}

7.

\vdash(\varphi\wedge\theta)\leftrightarrow(\varphi^{\prime}\wedge\theta^{\prime})

8.

\vdash K_{A}\varphi\leftrightarrow K_{A}\varphi^{\prime}

9.

\vdash[e]\varphi\leftrightarrow[e]\varphi^{\prime}

10.

\vdash C_{\mathfrak{A}}^{\theta}\varphi\leftrightarrow C_{\mathfrak{A}}^{\theta^{\prime}}\varphi^{\prime}

We can now prove a preliminary “one-step reduction" result:

Lemma 15
.

Let
e
be any reducible event.
Then, for every ’static’ formula
\theta
in
LKV
and every ’static’ term

, the formula
[e]\theta
and the term

Since
e=\Phi/\sigma
is reducible, its precondition

is also reducible. Let us fix some static formula
\rho

pre_{e}

14

We now prove both claims simultaneously, by induction on sub-expression complexity:

For
\theta:=Px_{1}\ldots x_{n}

we have
\vdash e(x_{i})=x^{\prime}_{i}
for some static terms
x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n}\in Var_{LKV}

[e]\theta
is provably equivalent to

\rho\to Px^{\prime}_{1}\ldots x^{\prime}_{n}

The case
\theta:=\neg\varphi

\varphi
is reducible, so there exists some static formula
\varphi_{e}
s.t.
\vdash[e]\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi_{e}

\vdash[e]\theta\leftrightarrow(\rho\to\neg\varphi_{e})

The case
\theta\,:=\,\varphi\wedge\theta

\varphi_{e}
and
\theta_{e}
s.t.
\vdash[e]\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi_{e}
and
\vdash[e]\theta\leftrightarrow\theta_{e}
are provable. Using now Lemma

.1
(the Derived Reduction Axiom for Conjunction) we obtain
\vdash[e]\theta\leftrightarrow(\varphi_{e}\wedge\theta_{e})
.

For
\theta:=K_{A}\varphi
: by induction, there exists some static formula
\varphi_{e}
s.t.
\vdash[e]\rangle\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi_{e}
is provable. Putting this together with the Knowledge Update reduction axiom, we get
\vdash[e]\theta\leftrightarrow(\rho\to K_{e(A)}\varphi_{e})

For
\theta:=C_{\mathfrak{A}}^{\theta}\varphi

\varphi_{e}
and
\theta_{e}
s.t.
\vdash[e]\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi_{e}
and
\vdash[e]\theta\leftrightarrow\theta_{e}

\vdash[e]\theta\leftrightarrow(\rho\to C_{e[\mathfrak{A}]}^{\rho\wedge\theta_{e}}\varphi_{e})

Moving to terms: the case

\vdash e(c)\,=\,c|_{\rho}\!\!\uparrow

For

For

\vdash e(y)=y^{\prime}

\vdash e(x)\,=\,(y^{\prime}|_{\rho\wedge\varphi^{\prime}}z^{\prime})|_{\rho}\!\!\uparrow

For

For

, s.t.
\vdash[e]\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi^{\prime}

The base cases

The cases

For the case

7
Conclusions

In this paper we axiomatize a decidable but richly expressive logic, that can deal with both “knowledge that" and “knowledge what", in a multi-agent setting in which data is modeled using local variables that can be shared, exchanged or modified. Our investigation builds on prior work on knowledge of values and on data-exchange events, as well as more classical work in Epistemic Logic on public announcements, common and distributed knowledge. But our setting has a number of innovative features, such as definite descriptions for hypothetical values, conditional distributed knowledge of values, a conditional version of common distributed knowledge, etc. The proofs also present a number of challenges (due to the presence of the above features).
Due to page limitations, we confined ourselves to semi-public events, but in future work we plan to extend this study to arbitrary data-exchange events (such as secret hacking).

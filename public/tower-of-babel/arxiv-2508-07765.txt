Isomorphism of almost locally compact Polish metric structures
Maciej Malicki

Abstract
. A topological space is almost locally compact if it contains a dense locally compact subspace. We generalize a result from [ 6 ] , showing that isomorphism on Borel classes of almost locally compact Polish metric structures is always classifiable by countable structures. This allows to remove a gap in the proof presented in [ 6 ] of a positive answer to a question of Gao and Kechris, who asked whether isometry of locally compact Polish metric spaces is classifiable by countable structures.

Introduction

One of the main results of
[
6
]

Theorem
.

Let
F

T
be an
F

\rm{Mod}(T)
is classifiable by countable structures.

In this note, we point out that the assumption of local compactness can be relaxed to almost local compactness. Recall that a topological space is called

if it contains a dense locally compact subspace. In particular, this generalization fixes a gap in the proof presented in
[
6

of a positive answer to the problem posed by Gao and Kechris whether the isometry relation
\cong
on the space
\mathcal{LC}
of locally compact Polish metric spaces is classifiable by countable structures. Here,
\mathcal{LC}
is understood as subset of the standard Borel space
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{U})
of closed subspaces of the Urysohn space
\mathbb{U}
. A meaningful positive answer to this question requires finding a standard Borel space
X
, and an equivalence relation
E
on
X
such that
\mathcal{LC}
is a subset of
X
,
E
restricted to
X
is
\cong
, and
E
is classifiable by countable structures. In the proof, a wrong claim was made that
\mathcal{LC}
itself is Borel in
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{U})
. In fact, it is complete co-analytic (see
[
2
]

\mathbb{U}
, and the isometry relation on

as

Let

Let
\mathbb{U}
be the Urysohn space, and let
\mathcal{ALC}\subseteq\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{U})
be the set of all closed almost locally compact subspaces of
\mathbb{U}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

\mathcal{ALC}
is a Borel subset of
\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{U})
.

Proof.

By the Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski theorem, there exists a Borel
\phi:\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{U})\rightarrow\mathbb{U}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\phi(K)
is a dense sequence in
K
. It is straightforward to verify that each mapping
K\mapsto\rho_{K}(\phi(K)_{n})
is Borel (see the proof of Lemma 6.4 in
[
4
]
for more details). Thus, the condition for
K
being almost locally compact can be formulated as

\forall m\forall q\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}\exists n(d(\phi(K)_{m},\phi(K)_{n})<q\mbox{ and }\rho_{K}(\phi(K)_{n})>0),

which is clearly Borel.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Isometry of locally compact Polish metric spaces is classifiable by countable structures.

Proof.

Every almost locally compact Polish metric space
K
can be coded as
M_{K}\in\rm{Mod}(L)
with the trivial signature
L
, and metric bounded by
1
. Simply, pick a countable tail-dense subset of
K
, and replace the original metric
d
with the metric
1/(1+d)
which does not change the isometry relation. Actually, for
\mathcal{ALC}\subseteq\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{U})
, the coding
\mathcal{ALC}\rightarrow\rm{Mod}(L)
,
K\mapsto M_{K}
, can be defined in a Borel way: the Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski theorem yields a Borel function
f:\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{U})\rightarrow\mathbb{U}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
f(K)
is a tail-dense sequence in
K
. As the signature
L
is trivial, the isomorphism relation is just the isometry relation. Moreover, the property of being almost locally compact can be expressed as a sentence in
\mathcal{L}_{\omega_{1}\omega}(L)
, so the set of all possible codes of almost locally compact Polish metric spaces is of the form
\rm{Mod}(T)
. By Theorem
3.6
and
[
3
, Theorem 13.1.2]
, the isometry relation on
\mathcal{ALC}
is classifiable by countable structures.

Thus, the isometry relation on the (complete co-analytic) subspace
\mathcal{LC}\subseteq\mathcal{ALC}
of locally compact Polish metric spaces is classifiable by countable structures.
∎

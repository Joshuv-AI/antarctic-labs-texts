Impact of Dimethyl Sulfoxide on Early Cardiotoxicity After Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation
Authors: V Lábska, B Mladosievičová, J Dúbrava, B Žiaková, K Nemergutová, L Sopko, J Lukáš, Ľ Harvanová
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDuring the COVID-19 pandemic, the use of cryopreserved grafts in allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) increased substantially. Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), used as a cryoprotective agent, is well recognized for its infusion-related toxicity; however, its impact on cardiac function remains unclear. Methods: This prospective study included 96 consecutive patients (median age 46 years) undergoing allogeneic HSCT. Patients were divided according to graft type into cryopreserved (n = 59, 61.5%) and non-cryopreserved (n = 37, 38.5%) groups. Comprehensive echocardiographic assessment was performed before and after transplantation. Results: Significant changes were observed in diastolic parameters (E/A, and E/E′), structural parameters (left ventricular mass and left ventricular mass index), global longitudinal strain (GLS), and right ventricular ejection fraction following HSCT. In the cryopreserved group, E/A ratio decreased below the normal range and E/E′ ratio increased significantly, whereas no such changes were observed in the non-cryopreserved group. During the 30-day follow-up period, symptomatic cardiovascular toxicity occurred in 5 patients (5.2%), while subclinical cancer therapy-related cardiac dysfunction was detected in 16 patients (16.7%). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated that GLS predicted cardiotoxicity with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.88 (95% CI, 0.79–0.98; p < 0.0001). At a cut-off value of − 16.8%, sensitivity was 100% and specificity was 79%. Conclusion: Cryopreserved graft administration may be associated with early alterations in left ventricular diastolic function, potentially reflecting DMSO-related cardiotoxicity. GLS appears to be a sensitive marker for the early detection and prediction of cardiotoxicity in patients undergoing allogeneic HSCT.Graphical Abstract Keywords: Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, Early cardiotoxicity, Echocardiography, Dimethyl sulfoxide

Introduction

Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) represents a potentially curative therapeutic modality for patients with advanced and high-risk hematologic malignancies, including leukemias, myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), and lymphomas, as well as for patients with bone marrow failure syndromes and certain non-hematologic disorders [1]. The number of HSCT procedures performed worldwide has been increasing at an average annual rate of more than 7% [2]. In recent years, substantial improvements in post-transplant survival have been observed, likely reflecting advances in transplantation protocols and supportive peri-transplant care. It is estimated that the number of long-term HSCT survivors, defined as patients who remain relapse-free for at least 12 months after transplantation, may increase up to fivefold by 2030 [3].

Despite significant improvements in clinical outcomes, HSCT remains associated with several serious acute and chronic complications. These complications contribute substantially to long-term morbidity and mortality among HSCT survivors [4]. Approximately two-thirds of patients undergoing allogeneic HSCT develop at least one long-term complication [5]. Furthermore, patients who survive for at least 5 years after HSCT have a 4- to 9-fold higher mortality rate than the general population during the subsequent 30 years of follow-up [6].

Although early cardiac events following allogeneic HSCT are relatively uncommon, with an incidence of less than 10%, they represent potentially life-threatening complications associated with increased mortality and reduced quality of life among long-term survivors [7]. Therefore, minimizing the risk of cardiovascular complications after HSCT is of considerable importance, given that this procedure represents a potentially curative therapeutic modality for many patients. Risk factors for cardiovascular complications include conditioning-related chemotherapy and total body irradiation, prior exposure to anthracyclines or radiotherapy during induction therapy, pre-existing cardiovascular comorbidities, hemodynamic instability related to sepsis, graft-versus-host disease (GvHD), and its treatment. In addition, the cryoprotective agent dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), used in cryopreserved grafts, may contribute to cardiovascular toxicity [8].

During the COVID-19 pandemic, the European Society for Blood and Marrow Transplantation (EBMT) issued guidelines and recommendations supporting the cryopreservation of hematopoietic stem cell grafts in the setting of allogeneic transplantation. Cryopreservation was implemented to mitigate several risks, including graft procurement failure due to symptomatic donor COVID-19 infection, logistical challenges related to graft transportation, and the potential transmission of SARS-CoV-2 through the graft [9].

The aim of this study was to evaluate selected echocardiographic parameters and the occurrence of both subclinical and clinically overt cardiotoxicity in patients undergoing allogeneic HSCT. As a substantial part of the study was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic, we specifically focused on assessing the potential impact of the cryoprotective agent dimethyl sulfoxide on the development of cardiovascular complications. During the COVID-19 pandemic, several studies investigated the impact of graft cryopreservation on transplantation outcomes; however, these studies primarily focused on engraftment kinetics, graft failure, graft-versus-host disease, and survival outcomes rather than cardiovascular toxicity [10, 11]. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the association between DMSO exposure, echocardiographic parameters, and cardiovascular toxicity in a cohort of patients undergoing allogeneic HSCT.

Methods

Patients Selection

This prospective, single-center study compared echocardiographic parameters before and after allogeneic HSCT. The study was conducted at the Department of Hematology and Transfusiology, University Hospital Bratislava. All adult patients who underwent allogeneic HSCT between January 2020 and January 2022 were included. The study was approved by the Local Ethics Committee of University Hospital Bratislava (approval No. EK/1/9), and all participants provided written informed consent. Most patients underwent HSCT for hematologic malignancies; however, two patients received transplantation for non-malignant hematologic disorders. The distribution of underlying diseases and transplantation characteristics is presented in the Results section.

Myeloablative conditioning regimens primarily consisted of high-dose busulfan (3.2 mg/kg/day administered intravenously for 4 consecutive days) or total body irradiation (TBI) at a total dose of 12 Gy, combined with cyclophosphamide (total dose, 120 mg/kg). Reduced-intensity conditioning regimens mainly consisted of lower-dose busulfan (3.2 mg/kg/day administered intravenously for 3 consecutive days) or TBI at a dose of 8 Gy, combined with standard-dose fludarabine. Additional RIC regimens included fludarabine 150 mg/m² and cyclophosphamide 1200 mg/m², fludarabine 100 mg/m² combined with busulfan 6 mg/kg and cyclophosphamide 40 mg/kg, and fludarabine 150 mg/m² with melphalan 140 mg/m². A subset of patients received conditioning based on fludarabine 150 mg/m² and treosulfan 30 g/m². Unless otherwise specified, all reported doses represent cumulative doses administered during the conditioning regimen. Graft-versus-host disease (GvHD) prophylaxis consisted of calcineurin inhibitors combined with a short course of methotrexate.

Cryopreservation of hematopoietic stem cell grafts was performed in 49 patients (81.7%) receiving grafts from unrelated donors and in 10 patients (27.8%) receiving grafts from related donors. The mean DMSO concentration in the cryopreserved grafts was 7–8%, with a mean volume of 21.6 mL (range: 17.6–26.3 mL). Patients were divided into two groups according to graft type: those receiving cryopreserved grafts (cryo group) and those receiving non-cryopreserved grafts (non-cryo group).

Echocardiography

Echocardiographic examinations were performed by a single experienced cardiologist at University Hospital Bratislava before HSCT and repeated 1 month after transplantation. All examinations were conducted using a Vivid E95 ultrasound system (GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA). Echocardiographic parameters, including left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), global longitudinal strain (GLS), right ventricular functional parameters, diastolic function indices (E/A and E/E′ ratios), pericardial status, and structural cardiac parameters, were assessed.

Cardiac Toxicity Definition

Early cardiovascular events were defined as any new occurrence of arrhythmia, heart failure, acute pulmonary edema, myocardial infarction or ischemia, pericarditis, or pericardial effusion. Subclinical cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction (CTRCD) was defined according to the diagnostic criteria outlined in the 2022 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Cardio-Oncology Guidelines, developed in collaboration with the European Hematology Association (EHA), the European Society for Radiotherapy and Oncology (ESTRO), and the International Cardio-Oncology Society (IC-OS) [12].

Heart failure was diagnosed based on compatible clinical signs and symptoms, including dyspnea, peripheral edema and signs of pulmonary congestion, rather than on NT-proBNP elevation alone. NT-proBNP was used as a supportive biomarker and not as an isolated diagnostic criterion.

Statistical Analysis

Clinical characteristics (sex, underlying disease, and transplant-related variables) are presented as frequencies and percentages. The chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used to compare categorical variables. Echocardiographic parameters that did not meet the assumptions of normality are presented as medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs). The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used for comparisons of paired data, whereas the Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare independent groups. Logistic regression analyses were performed using both univariate and multivariate models. The diagnostic performance of the studied parameters was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The area under the curve (AUC) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. The optimal cut-off value was determined using the Youden index. All statistical analyses were performed using MedCalc statistical software (version 23.4.8). A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical Characteristics

Data from 96 patients were analyzed. The median age was 46 years (range, 18–68 years), and 56 patients (58.3%) were male. The most common indication for allogeneic HSCT was acute myeloid leukemia (AML), accounting for approximately half of all transplant indications. Peripheral blood stem cell grafts were used in all but one patient. Sixty patients received grafts from matched unrelated donors (MUD), whereas 36 received grafts from HLA-identical sibling donors.

The study cohort was divided into two groups according to graft cryopreservation status. Cryopreservation of hematopoietic stem cell grafts was performed in 59 patients (61.5%) (cryo group), whereas the remaining 37 patients received non-cryopreserved grafts (non-cryo group). No significant differences were observed between the cryo and non-cryo groups with respect to age, sex, underlying disease, conditioning regimen intensity, use of total body irradiation, pre-transplant radiotherapy, occurrence of acute graft-versus-host disease, or pre-transplant cardiovascular comorbidities. A statistically significant difference between the groups was observed only in the type of HSCT performed. Analysis of New Onset Cardiac Events

Of the 96 patients, 5 (5.2%) developed newly diagnosed cardiovascular events during the early transplantation period, from the conditioning phase through Day + 30 after HSCT. Three patients developed heart failure, whereas two experienced arrhythmias, including one case of atrial fibrillation and one case of supraventricular tachycardia. Of the five patients who developed new-onset cardiovascular events, four received cryopreserved grafts and one received a non-cryopreserved graft. Accordingly, cardiovascular events occurred in 4/59 (6.8%) patients in the cryopreserved group and in 1/37 (2.7%) patients in the non-cryopreserved group. The difference between the groups was not statistically significant (P = 0.37). Serum creatinine levels were within the normal range in all three patients diagnosed with heart failure, making renal dysfunction an unlikely explanation for the observed NT-proBNP elevation. The main characteristics of patients with clinically significant cardiovascular events are summarized in Table 5. Figure 1 illustrates changes in two-dimensional global longitudinal strain before (a) and after HSCT (b) in Patient 3. GLS decreased from − 16.5% to − 10.8%, representing a 35% relative decline and indicating subclinical left ventricular systolic dysfunction. The incidence of cardiovascular toxicity was higher in patients receiving cryopreserved grafts than in those receiving non-cryopreserved grafts (6.8% vs. 2.7%); however, the difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.37). The odds ratio for symptomatic cardiovascular toxicity in the cryopreserved group was 2.74 (95% CI, 0.29–25.5).

Analysis of Subclinical CTRCD

According to the 2022 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Cardio-Oncology Guidelines, 16 patients (16.7%) met the criteria for asymptomatic cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction at the 1-month follow-up based on echocardiographic findings. Four patients had moderate CTRCD, all characterized by a new reduction in LVEF of ≥ 10% points to a value of 40–49%, whereas twelve patients had mild CTRCD, defined as an LVEF > 50% with a new relative decline in GLS of > 15% from baseline (Table 6).

The incidence of subclinical cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction was 18.6% in the cryopreserved group and 13.5% in the non-cryopreserved group. Although patients receiving cryopreserved grafts had a numerically higher risk of subclinical CTRCD, the difference did not reach statistical significance (relative risk, 1.44; 95% CI, 0.54–3.82; P = 0.46). Similarly, the odds ratio for subclinical CTRCD was 1.54 (95% CI, 0.49–4.86; P = 0.45).

Predictors of Symptomatic Cardiovascular Toxicity

In both univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses, GLS remained the only independent predictor of cardiotoxicity (P < 0.001), whereas structural cardiac parameters, left ventricular ejection fraction LVEF, and right ventricular functional parameters were not significantly associated with cardiotoxicity. ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate the predictive performance of GLS for cardiotoxicity. The analysis demonstrated that GLS predicted cardiotoxicity with an AUC of 0.88 (95% CI, 0.79–0.98; P < 0.0001). At the optimal cut-off value of − 16.8%, sensitivity was 100% and specificity was 79% (Table 7).

Discussion

In this study, we aimed to assess the incidence of both clinical and subclinical cardiotoxicity and to evaluate changes in echocardiographic parameters following HSCT. We found significant deterioration in diastolic function parameters and global longitudinal strain one month after transplantation, whereas no significant decline in LVEF was observed. Notably, alterations in diastolic function were more apparent among patients receiving cryopreserved grafts. In our cohort, the incidence of clinically overt cardiotoxicity was 5.2%, whereas subclinical cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction was detected in 16.7% of patients.

Echocardiography is the most widely used and well-established imaging modality for the assessment of systolic cardiac function, primarily through the measurement of left ventricular ejection fraction. Global longitudinal strain enables the detection of subclinical myocardial and left ventricular dysfunction at an earlier stage than LVEF. Watanabe et al., in a cohort of 85 patients undergoing HSCT, demonstrated a significant decline in GLS over time after transplantation, whereas no significant reduction in LVEF was observed [13]. Similar findings have been reported in other studies, which showed that GLS is a more sensitive marker than LVEF for the assessment of cardiac function in patients with heart failure and CTRCD following various anticancer therapies [14–16]. Consistent with these observations, our cohort demonstrated a significant reduction in GLS after HSCT, whereas LVEF remained unchanged.

The association between impaired GLS and cardiotoxicity has been well documented. According to the 2022 European Society of Cardiology Guidelines, a relative decline in GLS of > 15% from baseline is considered clinically significant for the diagnosis of cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction. A baseline GLS value of approximately − 18% has been proposed as an optimal cut-off for predicting CTRCD [17]. Furthermore, meta-analyses have demonstrated that impaired GLS is strongly associated with the development of CTRCD, with reported cut-off values ranging from − 21% to − 13.8% [18]. In our cohort, early post-transplant assessment of GLS, with a cut-off value of − 16.8%, demonstrated good discriminatory ability for identifying patients with CTRCD.

Several studies have demonstrated that chemotherapy is associated with early impairment of diastolic function, which typically precedes the development of systolic dysfunction. Previous studies have shown that early cyclophosphamide-induced cardiotoxicity is characterized predominantly by left ventricular diastolic rather than systolic dysfunction [19, 20]. El-Cheikh et al. reported a decrease in the E/A ratio; however, the values remained within the normal range, and no significant change in the E/E′ ratio was observed [21]. In our previous study, we also demonstrated a significant reduction in the E/A ratio following HSCT [22]. Consistent with these findings, the present study confirmed a significant decrease in the E/A ratio below the normal range, suggesting impaired left ventricular relaxation and early diastolic dysfunction. Although the E/E′ ratio increased significantly after HSCT, it remained within normal limits. These findings suggest that subclinical alterations in diastolic function may represent an early manifestation of cardiac injury and could become clinically relevant over time. Therefore, our results support the use of serial echocardiographic monitoring following transplantation.

Cardiovascular DMSO toxicity may manifest as bradycardia, hypertension, and cardiac arrhythmia and rarely as coronary vasospasm or myocardial infarction. DMSO has been identified as a major contributor to infusion-related toxicity in HSCT. However, its impact on cardiovascular toxicity in patients undergoing allogeneic HSCT has not been systematically investigated. To the best of our knowledge, however, no published studies have evaluated the cardiovascular subclinical and clinical toxicity in patients who have undergone allogeneic transplantation with cryopreserved stem cell grafts. Prior to the COVID-19 pandemic, cryopreservation was routinely used primarily for autologous grafts, whereas allogeneic grafts were generally administered fresh. Several case reports describing cardiac toxicity following the infusion of cryopreserved grafts have been published [23–26]. In addition, some studies have reported blood pressure fluctuations, arrhythmias, and transient hemodynamic alterations associated with DMSO exposure [27, 28]. Nevertheless, the available evidence is largely derived from small cohorts and autologous transplantation settings, and data regarding the effects of DMSO on cardiac function, particularly echocardiographic parameters, remain limited.

DMSO metabolites can induce direct endothelial irritation, mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress [29–32]. In vitro studies have demonstrated that even low concentrations of DMSO inhibit vascular endothelial cell proliferation by preventing cell-cycle progression from the G1 to the S phase and increase endothelial cell apoptosis in a concentration-dependent manner [33]. Exposure to 3.7% DMSO increased the rate of apoptosis, an effect driven by mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress in cardiomyoblasts [29]. During cryopreservation of hematopoietic stem cells, substantially higher concentrations of DMSO (7–8%) are used, which more profoundly disrupt mitochondrial function and increase oxidative stress, thereby amplifying its potential biological effects on cardiomyocytes and the endothelium. Mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and increased calcium influx collectively influence intracellular Ca²⁺ handling in cardiomyocytes. Diastolic Ca²⁺ overload negatively affects cardiomyocyte relaxation and hence cardiac diastolic function in the human heart [34]. Recipients of cryopreserved grafts demonstrated higher levels of selected inflammatory and endothelial-associated biomarkers, particularly IL-8 and VEGF-D, suggesting a more pronounced early inflammatory and endothelial-associated response [25]. Taken together, mitochondrial oxidative stress appears to contribute to diastolic dysfunction by impairing mitochondrial performance, disturbing mitochondrial dynamics, enhancing the production of pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic cytokines, activating fibroblasts, and promoting extracellular matrix deposition. These processes collectively drive interstitial fibrosis and increase passive myocardial stiffness, providing a plausible mechanistic link between mitochondrial stress and early diastolic impairment [35]. In our study, patients receiving cryopreserved grafts demonstrated a significant decrease in the E/A ratio below the normal range, as well as a significant increase in the E/E′ ratio, although the latter remained within normal limits. In contrast, no significant changes in diastolic function parameters were observed in patients receiving non-cryopreserved grafts. These findings may support a potential association between DMSO exposure and the development of early diastolic dysfunction; however, a causal relationship cannot be established based on the present data. It is important to recognize that DMSO may represent one of several biologically plausible contributors to the early biological and cardiovascular effects associated with the administration of cryopreserved grafts.

Cardiovascular complications following allogeneic HSCT are relatively uncommon during the early post-transplant period, with a reported incidence of clinically overt cardiotoxicity ranging from approximately 3% to 9% within the first 100 days after transplantation [36]. The reported incidence of cancer therapy–related cardiac dysfunction following HSCT ranges from 17% to 27% [37, 38]. In our study, clinically overt cardiotoxicity occurred in 5 patients (5.2%) during the 30-day follow-up period, whereas asymptomatic CTRCD was identified in 16 patients (16.7%). Two patients developed arrhythmias, and three developed new-onset heart failure. Twelve patients met the criteria for mild CTRCD, whereas four patients had moderate CTRCD. These subclinical abnormalities, including impaired global longitudinal strain and alterations in diastolic function parameters, may represent early manifestations of myocardial injury and could precede the development of clinically overt cardiovascular disease.

This study has several limitations. First, LVEF and GLS could not be assessed in some patients because of suboptimal echocardiographic image quality. Second, echocardiographic follow-up was limited to a single assessment at Day + 30 after transplantation. Importantly, the study design does not allow the specific contribution of DMSO to be distinguished from other factors associated with graft cryopreservation, including freezing and thawing procedures, cellular injury, graft composition, and infusion-related characteristics. Furthermore, individualized DMSO exposure was not quantified and dose-response analyses were not performed. Therefore, a DMSO-specific or dose-dependent cardiovascular effect cannot be established. Further prospective studies incorporating individualized DMSO dose assessment, mechanistic biomarkers, larger cohorts, and longer follow-up are warranted.

Conclusion

In conclusion, significant changes in GLS and selected diastolic function parameters underscore the value of echocardiography for the early detection of cardiotoxicity in patients undergoing HSCT. Further prospective studies are needed to clarify the long-term clinical significance of these findings. Cardiovascular toxicity associated with cryopreserved graft administration should be considered in the differential diagnosis of cardiovascular events following HSCT. DMSO may represent one of several biologically plausible contributors to these effects; however, its independent contribution requires further investigation. Strategies aimed at reducing DMSO exposure without compromising graft viability may be considered, particularly in patients with pre-existing cardiovascular risk factors or comorbidities.

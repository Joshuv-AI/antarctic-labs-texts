Abstract
. Given a finite transitive group G ≤ Sym ⁡ ( Ω ) G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega) , the intersection density of G G is defined as the ratio between the size of the largest subsets of G G in which any two permutations agree on at least one element of Ω \Omega , and the order of a point stabilizer of G G . In this paper, we completely determine the intersection densities of the permutation groups PSL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q) , where q q is a power of an odd prime p p , acting transitively with point stabilizers conjugate to ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} . Our proof uses an auxiliary graph, which is a PGL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{PGL}_{2}(q) -vertex-transitive graph, in which a clique corresponds to an intersecting set of PSL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q) . For the transitive action of PSL 2 ⁡ ( q ) \operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q) with point stabilizers conjugate to ℤ r \mathbb{Z}_{r} , where r | q − 1 2 r\mid\frac{q-1}{2} is an odd prime, we show that the auxiliary graph is not regular, and we construct an intersecting set which is sometimes of maximum size.

1.
Introduction

Given a finite transitive group
G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
, a subset
\mathcal{F}\subset G
is called
intersecting
if, for any
g,h\in G
, there exists
\omega\in\Omega
such that
\omega^{g}=\omega^{h}
. We are interested in the Erdős-Ko-Rado type problem of determining the largest intersecting sets of
G
. For any
\omega\in\Omega
, the point stabilizer
G_{\omega}
and its cosets are examples of intersecting sets in the transitive group
G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
. Most of the works in the literature on this Erdős-Ko-Rado type problem aim to classify the transitive groups whose largest intersecting sets are of size at most the order of a point stabilizer (see
[
15
]
for example). In recent years, there has been a growing interest in transitive groups admitting intersecting sets of size larger than that of a point stabilizer. To address this, the authors of
[
11
]
introduced the intersection density of transitive groups. The
intersection density
of a finite transitive group
G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
is the rational number

(1)

\displaystyle\rho(G)=\frac{\left\{|\mathcal{F}|:\mathcal{F}\subset G\mbox{ is intersecting}\right\}}{|G|/|\Omega|}.

Clearly,
\rho(G)\geq 1
for any transitive group
G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
. Moreover, it was proved in
[
14
]
that
\rho(G)\leq\tfrac{|\Omega|}{3}
whenever
|\Omega|\geq 3
. This bound was known to be attained by the four transitive groups given in
[
14
, Theorem 5.1]
. Recently, Cazzola, Gogniat, and Spiga
[
4
]
proved that these are, in fact, the only transitive groups that attain this upper bound. Consequently,
\rho(G)\leq\tfrac{|\Omega|}{4}
whenever
|\Omega|>30
. It is worth mentioning here that the motivation for
[
4
]
comes from the study of Kronecker classes of fields extensions. The intersection densities of transitive groups of particular degrees have also been studied, and in some cases, completely determined
[
2
,
7
,
8
]
. For transitive groups of degree a product of two primes, it was shown in
[
2
,
7
]
that there is a strong connection between the intersection density and the existence of certain cyclic codes whose codewords have non-maximal weights.

The notion of intersection density has also been extended to vertex-transitive graphs. Although the papers
[
1
,
10
,
12
]
define the notion of intersection density of vertex-transitive graphs in different ways, they are essentially the intersection densities of certain transitive subgroups of automorphism of the graph.

1.1.
Motivation

As mentioned earlier, the vast majority of the works in this area focus on transitive groups with intersection density equal to
1
. The only papers studying transitive groups with intersection densities larger than
1
are essentially
[
4
,
11
,
14
]
. Motivated by a deeper understanding of transitive groups with intersection density larger than
1
, Hujdurović, Kovács, Kutnar and Marušič started a program to study such transitive groups, focusing first on those with small point stabilizers. Indeed, it was shown in
[
6
]
that many examples of transitive groups with large intersection densities can be obtained by restricting the point stabilizers to be small. In
[
6
, Problem 7.4]
, Hujdurović et al. posed the following problem.

Problem 1.1
.

Given a prime
r\geq 2
, determine all possible intersection densities of transitive groups with point stabilizers isomorphic to
H=\mathbb{Z}_{r}
.

When
r=2
in Problem
1.1
, the transitive groups with intersection density equal to
1
were completely determined in
[
6
, Proposition 3.4]
using the character theory of the underlying group algebra. However, the case where
H
has order
3
becomes significantly more challenging, but there are many transitive groups with large intersection densities nevertheless. The main hurdle in this case seems to be the automorphism groups of cubic arc-transitive graphs with vertex stabilizers isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. Indeed, when
H=\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, the orbitals of a group
G
acting on the cosets of
H
by right multiplication have length
1
or
3
. Consequently, many cubic arc-transitive graphs arise from transitive groups with such point stabilizers. Studying the intersection densities of these groups was one of the main motivations behind the research
[
6
]
and
[
10
]
.

One family of transitive groups that often occurs as the full automorphism group of cubic arc-transitive graphs of type
\{1,2^{1}\}
(see
[
5
]
for the definition) is the projective general linear group
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(q)
, with point stabilizer isomorphic to
\operatorname{Sym}(3)
. In this case, the
1
-arc regular subgroup of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(q)
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
, with stabilizer isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. The intersection densities of some of these graphs were studied in
[
6
,
10
,
12
]
.
For these graphs, it is natural to study Problem
1.1
from the perspective of
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
, since a transitive subgroup may have a larger density than the underlying group.

The intersection densities of
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(3^{n})
, for
n\geq 1
, acting on the cosets of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
were determined by Hujdurović, Kovács, Kutnar, and Marušič in
[
6
]
. When

, the intersection density of

acting on the cosets of

was also determined in
[

]
. The remaining cases, where

Theorem 1.2
.

If

\displaystyle{\rho}\left(\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)\right)=\begin{cases}3^{k-1}&\mbox{ if $q=3^{k}$ and $k$ odd,}\\

The proof of Theorem

is no longer vertex transitive, and is in fact not even regular.

For
H=H^{-}_{r}
, we will construct an example of large intersecting set which sometimes realize the intersection density of
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(q)
acting on cosets of
H=H^{-}_{r}
. We state the main result below.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
q
is a power of a prime and
r\mid\tfrac{q-1}{2}
is an odd prime, then the intersection density of
G_{q}
acting on cosets of
H_{r}^{-}
is at least
\tfrac{3r-1}{2r}
.

As shown in Table
1
, the lower bound given in Theorem
4.1
is tight for many cases.

Before proceeding with the construction, we first determine the elements that are conjugate to elements in
H_{r}^{-}
. Let
q=p^{k}
where
p
is an odd prime and
k\geq 1
is an integer. Fix a primitive element
\omega\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Since
H_{r}^{-}\leq G_{q}
, we know that
2r\mid(q-1)
. Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
be an element of order
2r
. Define the element of
G_{q}
given by

\displaystyle R={\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{bmatrix}}.

Clearly, the order of
R\in G_{q}
is equal to
r
.
For any
1\leq i\leq\tfrac{r-1}{2}
, we also define

\displaystyle\delta_{i}:=a^{i}+a^{-i}.

Moreover, we also define
\delta_{0}=2.

As
r\mid\tfrac{q-1}{2}
, there is a unique conjugacy class of subgroups isomorphic to
H_{r}^{-}
(see
[
9
]
). Hence, without loss of generality, we assume that
H_{r}^{-}=\langle R\rangle
. The elements of order
r
in
G_{q}
are therefore all conjugate in
H_{r}^{-}
. Further, there are
\tfrac{r-1}{2}
conjugacy classes of elements of order
r
in
G_{q}
represented by
R^{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\tfrac{r-1}{2}
. For any
1\leq i\leq\tfrac{r-1}{2}
, we let
\mathcal{C}_{i}
be the conjugacy class of
G_{q}
containing
R^{i}
.
Let
\Gamma_{q}^{-}
be the subgraph of
\overline{\Gamma_{G_{q}}}
induced by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tfrac{r-1}{2}}\mathcal{C}_{i}.

The next lemma gives a characterization of the elements of
G_{q}
that lie in
\mathcal{D}
. We omit the proof since it is similar to that of Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

An element of
G_{q}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
if and only if its trace is
\pm\delta_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq\tfrac{r-1}{2}
.

Recall that
\Gamma_{q}^{-}
is the graph defined in (
ii
) in §
3.2.1
. Since the graph
\Gamma_{q}^{-}
is clearly the union of
\tfrac{r-1}{2}
conjugacy classes of
G_{q}
, the group
G_{q}
acts intransitively as an automorphism group of
\Gamma_{q}^{-}
. Similarly,
G_{q}^{*}
acts intransitively as a subgroup of automorphism of
\Gamma_{q}^{-}.
In fact, the graph
\Gamma_{q}^{-}
is not even regular in general, except for the case where
r=3
, in which case the vertex set of
\Gamma_{q}^{-}
is the conjugacy classes of elements of order
3
. In order to prove Theorem
4.1
, we will find a clique of the desired size
\tfrac{3(r-1)}{2}
in
\Gamma_{q}^{-}.

We define the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\left\{R^{-i}:1\leq i\leq\tfrac{r-1}{2}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\left\{{\begin{bmatrix}a^{i}&0\\
\left(a^{i}-a^{-i}\right)&a^{-i}\end{bmatrix}}:1\leq i\leq\tfrac{r-1}{2}\right\}\mbox{, and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3}

\displaystyle:=\left\{{\begin{bmatrix}a^{i}&-\left(a^{i}-a^{-i}\right)\\
0&a^{-i}\end{bmatrix}}:1\leq i\leq\tfrac{r-1}{2}\right\}.

Moreover, let
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}\cup\mathcal{F}_{3}
. It is straightforward to show the next lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

The set
\mathcal{F}
is a clique in
{\Gamma}_{q}^{-}
. In particular,
|\mathcal{F}|=\tfrac{3(r-1)}{2}
, and the intersection density of
G_{q}
acting on cosets of
H_{r}^{-}
is at least
\tfrac{3r-1}{2r}
.

Dyadic microlocal partition for anisotropic metrics and uniform Weyl quantization
Vicente Vergara

Abstract
We develop a dyadic microlocal partition adapted to position–dependent anisotropic metrics in phase space and prove uniform bounds for localized Weyl quantization and for Moyal truncation with explicit control of remainders. A semiclassical, per–band renormalization recovers the exact scaling on each frequency band, while a Cotlar–Stein almost–orthogonality scheme ensures global convergence and operator–norm estimates with transparent dependence on finitely many symbol seminorms. The approach is robust in non-homogeneous settings where anisotropy varies with position. As applications, we construct a microlocal parametrix via truncated Moyal expansion and develop a local–global analysis of the Radon transform viewed as a model Fourier integral operator. The results provide a constructive toolset for uniform localization, composition, and recombination of pseudodifferential and Fourier integral operators.



Keywords:
dyadic microlocal partition; anisotropic metrics; Weyl quantization; Calderón–Vaillancourt; Moyal expansion and truncation; Cotlar–Stein almost orthogonality; semiclassical band renormalization; microlocal parametrix; Radon transform; global operator bounds.

Introduction

The goal of this work is to explicitly construct and control a dyadic microlocal partition adapted to anisotropic metrics on phase space, and to exploit this partition to derive quantitative estimates for symbols and pseudodifferential operators (
\Psi
DOs) in the class
S^{m_{1},m_{2}}
.
We focus on block-diagonal metrics whose fiber part depends on the base point; this regime arises naturally when the frequency scale varies with
x
(anisotropic semiclassical settings, non-homogeneous coefficients, etc.). We work on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
under uniform ellipticity and polynomial derivative bounds for the family
G_{x}
.

Starting from the classical calculus for
\Psi
DOs and Fourier integral operators (FIOs)
[
10
,
11
,
19
]
and the semiclassical framework
[
22
]
, we introduce a dyadic partition normalized by
T_{x}=G_{x}^{1/2}
that stabilizes
S^{m_{1},m_{2}}
under multiplication by microlocalizers
\Lambda_{j,k}
, with finite losses uniform in
(j,k)
.
Building on this, we obtain three central ingredients: (i) local Weyl quantization with
H^{s}\to H^{s-m_{2}}
bounds uniform on each patch; (ii) a semiclassical variant (band renormalization
h\sim 2^{-k}
) that recovers the exact bandwise factor; and (iii) truncation of the Moyal expansion with explicit control of the remainder and decay in fiber distance, enabling a global recombination by almost-orthogonality (Cotlar–Stein) with norm convergence. For the oscillatory representation of Moyal and Weyl composition we refer to the classical sources (cf.
[
10
,
11
,
19
,
6
,
13
]
).

The dyadic structure is adopted for substantive reasons: partitions into dyadic bands or rectangles capture anisotropies and product geometries beyond the one-parameter isotropic setting. For instance, in multiparameter analysis, Tanaka–Yabuta establish multilinear embeddings on dyadic rectangles
[
18
]
, and Wang extends Stein–Weiss inequalities to product spaces with multiparameter weights
[
21
]
, both illustrating the effectiveness of decompositions that separate scales by coordinates. In a complementary microlocal direction, global directional frames (curvelets, shearlets) produce near-diagonal matrices for FIOs
[
3
,
8
]
. In this work we propose a different yet compatible route: instead of fixing a global frame, we construct and control a
dyadic microlocal partition normalized by a position-dependent fiber metric

g_{(x,\xi)}(y,\eta)=y^{\top}F\,y+\eta^{\top}G_{x}\,\eta.

Under uniform ellipticity and polynomial control of
\partial_{x}^{\beta}G_{x}
, we build microlocalizers
\Lambda_{j,k}
adapted to
T_{x}=G_{x}^{1/2}
, prove uniform (in
(j,k)
) derivative bounds, develop local quantization, and close the global sum of patches via Cotlar–Stein.

As representative applications, we present: (a) a patchwise microlocal
parametrix
, where the controlled sum
\sum_{j,k}\text{Op}^{w}(q_{j,k})
yields a microlocal inverse with remainder estimated by the truncated Moyal expansion; and (b) a local–global analysis of the Radon transform, viewed as an FIO of order
m_{R}=(n-1)/2
(cf. Section
6
), in which band renormalization delivers the factor
2^{k(m_{2}-m_{R})}
and the global recombination is achieved by almost-orthogonality. Both cases illustrate how the
T_{x}
-normalization underpins robust estimates with explicit constant dependencies.

Our approach fits within current lines of research in anisotropic microlocal analysis: global calculi and anisotropic (Gabor-type) wave front sets in
[
14
,
20
,
4
]
, and function spaces adapted to FIOs with parabolic decompositions in
[
15
,
12
]
. These references highlight that partitions aligned with the underlying metric or canonical geometry improve both local analysis and its transfer to global results. From the perspective of numerical approximation, the adaptive rectangular partitions of
[
1
]
reinforce the usefulness of blocks with variable aspect ratio to capture anisotropy and inhomogeneity.

Littlewood–Paley partitions and global directional frames are standard tools in harmonic analysis and for FIOs. The contribution here differs in two respects: (1) the
spatial dependence of the metric
(the normalization
T_{x}
adapts scale and directionality to
x
); and (2) the
uniform derivative control
of
\Lambda_{j,k}
in terms of powers of
\langle x\rangle
and
\langle\xi\rangle
, a key ingredient that directly feeds into the Moyal truncation and Cotlar–Stein summation in a non-homogeneous setting. In the homogeneous case, the technique recovers classical schemes with a constructive patchwise presentation and explicit tracking of constants.

Organization of the paper.

Section
2
fixes notation and hypotheses on the anisotropic metric: we impose uniform ellipticity and polynomial derivative control for the family
G_{x}
, introduce the fiber normalization
T_{x}=G_{x}^{1/2}
, and establish smoothness bounds for
T_{x}
and its associated norm (cf. Lemma
2.2
). This block fixes the structural constants and parameters (finite overlap
L
, finite collection
\{c_{\gamma}\}
) that remain invariant throughout the paper, setting the stage for a patchwise construction with explicit constant dependencies.

On this basis, Section
3
develops the dyadic microlocal partition adapted to

: we construct annuli,
1/2
-separated nets, and pre-cutoffs
\widetilde{\chi}_{j,k}
, introduce the normalizer
\Sigma

\Lambda_{j,k}=\chi_{j,k}/\Sigma
, and quantify their regularity with derivative bounds uniform in the indices (cf. Proposition
3.3
). The covering/separation geometry of annuli (cf. Lemma
3.1
) is recorded so that it can be invoked later to control interactions between patches.

With the partition under control, Section
4
addresses quantization and composition at the local level and its global recombination. First, we show that multiplication by
\Lambda_{j,k}
preserves the class
S^{m_{1},m_{2}}
with finite losses (cf. Proposition
4.1
); next we establish Weyl estimates with cutoffs: a version with losses towards
H^{s-(m_{2}+N_{\xi})}
(cf. Corollary
4.1
), a conservative
H^{s}\to H^{s-m_{2}}
version with band factor
2^{k(m_{2}+N_{\xi})}
(cf. Proposition
4.3
), and a semiclassical variant which, after the renormalization
h=2^{-k}
, recovers the exact factor
2^{km_{2}}
(cf. Proposition
4.5
). The Moyal expansion is truncated with explicit remainder control and decay in fiber distance for separated patches (cf. Lemma
4.8
); with these inputs, the global recombination via almost-orthogonality closes the sums in norm (cf. Theorem
4.9
) with transparent constant dependencies.

Section
5
illustrates the strength of the scheme with the construction of a patchwise microlocal parametrix: bandwise inversion and a high-order Moyal truncation yield
Q=\sum_{j,k}\text{Op}^{w}(q_{j,k})
such that
Q\,\text{Op}^{w}(a)=\mathrm{Id}+R
microlocally in the elliptic region, with a remainder of lower order or smoothing, depending on the choice of

. This procedure serves as a template for linear applications with real principal symbol. Next, Section
6
views the Radon transform as an FIO of order
m_{R}=(n-1)/2
: we obtain a local bound per patch with factor
2^{k(m_{2}-m_{R})}
and verify global recombination via Cotlar–Stein (cf. Proposition
6.1
, Theorem
6.2
), consolidating the passage from the local to the global in a paradigmatic case.

We conclude in Section
7
with a synthesis of the patchwise operator reconstruction, the role of Moyal truncation, and almost-orthogonality in non-homogeneous contexts, and we point to natural extensions (semiclassical,
L^{p}
, fine detection of singularities). Appendix
A
collects auxiliary lemmas and deferred proofs (multivariable Faà di Bruno, Cotlar–Stein, local and semiclassical versions of Calderón–Vaillancourt (CV)), used throughout the technical sections.

Notation and conventions

We use standard microlocal/semiclassical notation and normalizations throughout

Multi-indices and Leibniz’ rule.

For
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
set

\gamma!:=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}!,\qquad\binom{\gamma}{\alpha}:=\frac{\gamma!}{\alpha!(\gamma-\alpha)!},\qquad\binom{\gamma}{\alpha,\beta}:=\frac{\gamma!}{\alpha!\,\beta!}\quad(\alpha+\beta=\gamma).

The Leibniz rule in multi-index notation reads

\partial^{\gamma}(fg)=\sum_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\alpha}\,\partial^{\alpha}f\,\partial^{\beta}g,

equivalently in the form with
\binom{\gamma}{\alpha,\beta}
.

Euclidean norms and operator norm.

We use
|\cdot|
for the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, i.e.
|v|=(\sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}^{2})^{1/2}
, and

\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2},\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For a linear map
A:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
,

\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{|v|\leq 1}|Av|.

More generally, for
T:(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}

k
-linear,

\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup\{\,|T(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})|:\ |v_{j}|\leq 1\,\}.

If
D^{k}u(x)
denotes the
k
-th Fréchet derivative (a
k
-linear form), then for every
\beta
with
|\beta|=k
,

|\partial^{\beta}u(x)|\leq\|D^{k}u(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}},

which is immediate by evaluating
D^{k}u(x)
on the unit coordinate vectors.

Higher-order derivatives and tensor bounds.

For
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, its
r
-th derivative at
z
is the symmetric
r
-linear form

\nabla^{r}\varphi(z):(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{r}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\|\nabla^{r}\varphi(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{|v_{j}|\leq 1}|\nabla^{r}\varphi(z)[v_{1},\dots,v_{r}]|.

In coordinates, if
\varphi_{a_{1}\cdots a_{r}}(z)
are the symmetric coefficients, then

\nabla^{r}\varphi(z)[v^{1},\dots,v^{r}]=\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}}\varphi_{a_{1}\cdots a_{r}}(z)\,v^{1}_{a_{1}}\cdots v^{r}_{a_{r}}.

We define uniform seminorms

p_{r}(\varphi):=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\|\nabla^{r}\varphi(z)\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\infty.

For controlled composition expansions (multivariable Faà di Bruno, cf. Lemma
A.1
) see
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Anisotropic symbol classes)
.

Let
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. The class
S^{m_{1},m_{2}}
consists of the functions
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\theta})
such that, for every pair of multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
, there exists
C_{\alpha\beta}>0
with

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\theta}^{\beta}a(x,\theta)|\ \leq\ C_{\alpha\beta}\ \langle x\rangle^{\,m_{1}-|\alpha|}\ \langle\theta\rangle^{\,m_{2}-|\beta|},\qquad(x,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

The truncated seminorms are defined by

\|a\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1},m_{2})}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq\imath\\
|\beta|\leq\ell\end{subarray}}\ \sup_{(x,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\frac{|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\theta}^{\beta}a(x,\theta)|}{\langle x\rangle^{\,m_{1}-|\alpha|}\ \langle\theta\rangle^{\,m_{2}-|\beta|}}.

Anisotropic metric and normalization.

When relevant (metric sections and local quantization), we consider a Hörmander metric on
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the form

g_{(x,\xi)}(y,\eta)=y^{\top}F\,y+\eta^{\top}G_{x}\,\eta,

with
F

\partial_{x}^{\beta}G_{x}

T_{x}=G_{x}^{1/2}

2.1
General assumptions

Let

G\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathrm{Sym}^{+}(n)),

with constants
0<\lambda^{\min}_{G}\leq\lambda^{\max}_{G}<\infty
such that

\sigma(G_{x})\subset[\lambda^{\min}_{G},\lambda^{\max}_{G}]
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In addition
we assume derivative bounds

\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}G_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq c_{\beta}\,\langle x\rangle^{|\beta|},\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1)

for some constants
c_{\beta}
.

We denote the principal square root by
T_{x}:=G_{x}^{1/2}
and define

g_{x}(\xi):=\xi^{\top}G_{x}\xi=|T_{x}\xi|^{2},\qquad\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}:=|T_{x}\xi|.

Lemma 2.2

(Derivatives of the matrix square root)
.

Under the previous hypotheses, for every multi-index
\gamma
there exists

C_{\gamma}>0
(depending only on
\lambda^{\min}_{G},\lambda^{\max}_{G},|\gamma|
and on
the constants
c_{\beta}
with
|\beta|\leq|\gamma|
) such that

\|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}T_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C_{\gamma}\,\langle x\rangle^{|\gamma|}\qquad(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

Choose a closed contour
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
that encloses the interval

[\lambda^{\min}_{G},\lambda^{\max}_{G}]
and stays at distance
\mathrm{dist}(\Gamma,\sigma(G_{x}))\geq\delta>0

from the spectrum of every
G_{x}
. The principal square root admits the Dunford representation

T_{x}=G_{x}^{1/2}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\lambda^{1/2}(\lambda I-G_{x})^{-1}\,d\lambda.

For each fixed
x
the integrand is
C^{\infty}
in
x
, hence we may differentiate under the integral sign
(pointwise in
x
); moreover for
\lambda\in\Gamma
one has
\|(\lambda I-G_{x})^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\delta^{-1}
.
Using the resolvent identity iteratively and Leibniz’ rule,
\partial_{x}^{\gamma}[(\lambda I-G_{x})^{-1}]
is a finite sum
(over partitions
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{p}=\gamma
,
1\leq p\leq|\gamma|
) of terms of the form

(\lambda I-G_{x})^{-1}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}G_{x})(\lambda I-G_{x})^{-1}\cdots(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{p}}G_{x})(\lambda I-G_{x})^{-1}.

Hence, for
\lambda\in\Gamma
,

\big\|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}[(\lambda I-G_{x})^{-1}]\big\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sum_{p=1}^{|\gamma|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{p}=\gamma\\
|\alpha_{j}|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\delta^{-(p+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{p}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{j}}G_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

By (
1
) we get
\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{j}}G_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq c_{\alpha_{j}}\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha_{j}|}
, hence

\big\|\partial_{x}^{\gamma}[(\lambda I-G_{x})^{-1}]\big\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\Big(\sum_{p=1}^{|\gamma|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{p}=\gamma\\
|\alpha_{j}|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\delta^{-(p+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{p}c_{\alpha_{j}}\Big)\,\langle x\rangle^{|\gamma|}.

Taking operator norms in the Dunford formula and estimating the
\lambda
-integral by

\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\sup_{\lambda\in\Gamma}|\lambda^{1/2}|\cdot\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)
yields the claim with

C_{\gamma}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big(\sup_{\lambda\in\Gamma}|\lambda^{1/2}|\Big)\,\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)\,\sum_{p=1}^{|\gamma|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{p}=\gamma\\
|\alpha_{j}|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\delta^{-(p+1)}\prod_{j=1}^{p}c_{\alpha_{j}}.

∎

2.2
Phase-space metric (anisotropic model)

Fix a constant matrix
F\in\mathrm{Sym}^{+}(n)
and consider the phase-space metric

g_{(x,\xi)}(y,\eta)=y^{\top}Fy+\eta^{\top}G_{x}\eta.

Its contravariant inverse is

g^{(x,\xi)}((p,q),(p,q))=p^{\top}F^{-1}p+q^{\top}G_{x}^{-1}q.

Uniform ellipticity and temperance hold uniformly thanks to the spectral bounds and (
1
).

Proposition 2.3

(Algebraic properties and comparisons)
.

Under the spectral bounds there exist constants guaranteeing:

1.

\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}\geq 0
, positive homogeneity, and
\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}=0\iff\xi=0
.

2.

\sqrt{\lambda_{G}^{\min}}\,|\xi|\leq\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{G}^{\max}}\,|\xi|
.

3.

Comparability in
x
:

\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{G}^{\min}}{\lambda_{G}^{\max}}}\,\|\xi\|_{g_{y}}\leq\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{G}^{\max}}{\lambda_{G}^{\min}}}\,\|\xi\|_{g_{y}},\qquad\forall x,y,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

4.

Global comparison in phase: there exist constants
c,C>0
such that

c\big(|y|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\big)\leq g_{(x,\xi)}(y,\eta)\leq C\big(|y|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\big),\qquad\forall(x,\xi),(y,\eta).

Proof.

We verify each item in turn.

1. Positivity, homogeneity, and norm property.

By definition
\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}=|T_{x}\xi|\geq 0
. If
\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}=0
then
T_{x}\xi=0
; since
T_{x}
is invertible (all eigenvalues of
G_{x}
are
>0
) we deduce
\xi=0
. Positive homogeneity follows immediately:

\|\lambda\xi\|_{g_{x}}=|T_{x}(\lambda\xi)|=|\lambda|\,|T_{x}\xi|=|\lambda|\,\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}.

Thus
\|\cdot\|_{g_{x}}
is a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2. Inequalities with the Euclidean norm.

Since
\sigma(G_{x})\subset[\lambda_{G}^{\min},\lambda_{G}^{\max}]
, for every
\xi
we have

\lambda_{G}^{\min}|\xi|^{2}\leq\xi^{\top}G_{x}\xi\leq\lambda_{G}^{\max}|\xi|^{2}.

Taking square roots yields

\sqrt{\lambda_{G}^{\min}}\,|\xi|\leq|T_{x}\xi|=\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{G}^{\max}}\,|\xi|,

which proves the claim.

3. Comparability in
x
.

For
x,y
fixed and
\xi\neq 0
,

\frac{\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}}{\|\xi\|_{g_{y}}}=\frac{|T_{x}\xi|}{|T_{y}\xi|}=\frac{|(T_{x}T_{y}^{-1})(T_{y}\xi)|}{|T_{y}\xi|}\leq\|T_{x}T_{y}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

Similarly,

\frac{\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}}{\|\xi\|_{g_{y}}}\geq\|T_{x}T_{y}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{-1}.

Using the uniform spectral bounds we have
\|T_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{G}^{\max}}
and
\|T_{y}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1/\sqrt{\lambda_{G}^{\min}}
, hence

\|T_{x}T_{y}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\|T_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|T_{y}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{G}^{\max}}{\lambda_{G}^{\min}}}.

Inserting these estimates gives, for all
\xi
,

\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{G}^{\min}}{\lambda_{G}^{\max}}}\,\|\xi\|_{g_{y}}\leq\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{G}^{\max}}{\lambda_{G}^{\min}}}\,\|\xi\|_{g_{y}},

which is the stated comparability. The constants are independent of
x,y
because the spectral bounds are uniform.

4. Global comparison in phase.

Let
\lambda_{F}^{\min},\lambda_{F}^{\max}>0
be the extreme eigenvalues of the fixed matrix
F
. For every
(x,\xi)
and every tangent vector
(y,\eta)
,

g_{(x,\xi)}(y,\eta)=y^{\top}Fy+\eta^{\top}G_{x}\eta

and hence by the spectral bounds

\lambda_{F}^{\min}|y|^{2}+\lambda_{G}^{\min}|\eta|^{2}\leq y^{\top}Fy+\eta^{\top}G_{x}\eta\leq\lambda_{F}^{\max}|y|^{2}+\lambda_{G}^{\max}|\eta|^{2}.

Taking

c:=\min\{\lambda_{F}^{\min},\lambda_{G}^{\min}\},\qquad C:=\max\{\lambda_{F}^{\max},\lambda_{G}^{\max}\},

we obtain

c\big(|y|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\big)\leq g_{(x,\xi)}(y,\eta)\leq C\big(|y|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\big),

as required.

This completes the proof of Proposition
2.3
.

\psi(v):=|v|
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
v(x,\xi):=T_{x}\xi
, so

\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}=\psi(v(x,\xi))
. For
r\geq 1
the
r
-th Fréchet derivative

D^{r}\psi(v)
is homogeneous of degree
1-r
, hence there exists a constant

B_{r}=B_{r}(n)
with

\|D^{r}\psi(v)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq B_{r}\,|v|^{\,1-r}=B_{r}\,\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}^{\,1-r},\qquad(v\neq 0).

(4)

(For instance
D\psi(v)=v/|v|
and
D^{2}\psi(v)=|v|^{-1}I-|v|^{-3}vv^{\top}
and
the general bound follows from homogeneity and continuity on the sphere.)

Now apply the multivariable Faà di Bruno (cf. Lemma
A.1
) rule to

\Phi(x,\xi):=\psi(v(x,\xi))
. Any mixed derivative

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\Phi
is a finite linear combination of
terms of the form

\mathcal{T}=\big\langle D^{r}\psi(v(x,\xi)),\ \Theta_{1}(x,\xi),\dots,\Theta_{r}(x,\xi)\big\rangle,

where
1\leq r\leq|\alpha|+|\beta|
and each
\Theta_{j}
equals either

•

T_{x}
(arising from one
\xi
-derivative of
v=T_{x}\xi
), or

•

(\partial_{x}^{\gamma}T_{x})\,\xi
for some multi-index
\gamma\neq 0

(arising from an
x
-derivative of
T_{x}
).

Moreover, the number
s
of factors equal to
T_{x}
is exactly the number of

\xi
-derivatives involved in the given term (hence
s\leq|\beta|
), and the
total sum of the orders
|\gamma_{j}|
of the
x
-derivative factors satisfies

\sum_{j}|\gamma_{j}|\leq|\alpha|
.

Estimate a generic such term. Using (
4
) and the operator
norm bounds,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}|

\displaystyle\leq\|D^{r}\psi(v)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\|\Theta_{j}(x,\xi)\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq B_{r}\,\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}^{\,1-r}\;\|T_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{\,s}\;\prod_{j=1}^{r-s}\Big(\|\partial_{x}^{\gamma_{j}}T_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,|\xi|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq B_{r}\,\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}^{\,1-r}\;\|T_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{\,s}\;\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r-s}A_{\gamma_{j}}\langle x\rangle^{|\gamma_{j}|}\Big)\,|\xi|^{\,r-s}.

Use
|\xi|\leq\|T_{x}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}\leq(\lambda_{G}^{\min})^{-1/2}\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}

to absorb the
|\xi|^{r-s}
factor:

|\mathcal{T}|\leq\widetilde{C}\;\langle x\rangle^{\sum_{j}|\gamma_{j}|}\;\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}^{\,1-s},

where
\widetilde{C}
depends only on
r,n,\lambda_{G}^{\min},\lambda_{G}^{\max}

and the finite collection of
A_{\gamma_{j}}
(hence ultimately on the
c_{\gamma}

from (
1
)). Since
\sum_{j}|\gamma_{j}|\leq|\alpha|
and
s\leq|\beta|
,
summing the finitely many combinatorial terms produced by Faà di Bruno yields
the desired estimate

\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}\big|\leq C^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}\,\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha|}\,\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}^{\,1-|\beta|},\qquad(\xi\neq 0),

with
C^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}
depending only on

n,|\alpha|,|\beta|,\lambda_{G}^{\min},\lambda_{G}^{\max}
and the finitely many

c_{\gamma}
with
|\gamma|\leq|\alpha|
(via the
A_{\gamma}
’s from the square-root
lemma (cf. Lemma
2.2
)).

This completes the proof of (ii) and hence of the proposition.
∎

3
Dyadic microlocal decomposition

Maximal
1/2
-separated nets in the dyadic annulus.

Let the dyadic annulus be

\mathcal{C}_{k}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:2^{k}\leq|\zeta|<2^{k+1}\}.

A set
\{\zeta_{j,k}\}_{j}\subset\mathcal{C}_{k}
is called a
maximal
1/2
-separated net
in
\mathcal{C}_{k}
if

1.

(Separation)
|\zeta_{j,k}-\zeta_{j^{\prime},k}|\geq 1/2
for all
j\neq j^{\prime}
, and

2.

(Maximality / covering) for every
\zeta\in\mathcal{C}_{k}
there exists some index
j
with

|\zeta-\zeta_{j,k}|\leq 1/2
.

Equivalently, the closed balls
\{\overline{B}(\zeta_{j,k},1/2)\}_{j}
cover
\mathcal{C}_{k}
, while the open balls
B(\zeta_{j,k},1/4)
are pairwise disjoint.

Fibre centres via
T_{x}
.

Fix
T_{x}=G_{x}^{1/2}
as in Section
2
. For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and each net point
\zeta_{j,k}
define the fibre centre

\theta_{j,k}(x):=T_{x}^{-1}\zeta_{j,k},\qquad\text{so that }T_{x}\theta_{j,k}(x)=\zeta_{j,k}.

By uniform ellipticity of
T_{x}
and the fact
|\zeta_{j,k}|\simeq 2^{k}
we have
|\theta_{j,k}(x)|\simeq 2^{k}
uniformly in
x,j,k
.

Mother cut and seminorms.

Fix a compactly supported mother cut
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\varphi(\eta)=1\quad\text{for }|\eta|\leq\tfrac{1}{2},\qquad\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi\subset\{|\eta|\leq 1\}.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
denote the seminorms

M_{\ell}(\varphi):=\sup_{|\alpha|\leq\ell}\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi(\eta)|.

Precuts adapted to
T_{x}
.

For each triple
(x,j,k)
set

u_{j,k}(x,\xi):=T_{x}\big(\xi-\theta_{j,k}(x)\big),\qquad\widetilde{\chi}_{j,k}(x,\xi):=\varphi\bigl(u_{j,k}(x,\xi)\bigr).

Hence
\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{\chi}_{j,k}(x,\cdot)\subset T_{x}^{-1}B(\zeta_{j,k},1)
and

\widetilde{\chi}_{j,k}\equiv 1
on
\{\,|u_{j,k}|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\,\}
. Note that, after the radial localization introduced below, the effective support of each
\widetilde{\chi}_{j,k}
lies where
|\xi|\simeq 2^{k}
.

Radial dyadic localization and bounded overlap.

Fix a radial bump
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
with

\operatorname{supp}\rho\subset[1/4,4]
and
\rho\equiv 1
on
[1/2,2]
, and set

\rho_{k}(\xi):=\rho(2^{-k}|\xi|),\qquad\chi_{j,k}(x,\xi):=\rho_{k}(\xi)\,\widetilde{\chi}_{j,k}(x,\xi).

The factor
\rho_{k}
enforces the dyadic annulus
|\xi|\simeq 2^{k}
and makes the family
\{\chi_{j,k}\}_{j,k}
effectively localized to the scale
2^{k}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

With the notation above there exist finite constants
N=N(n)
and
M=M(\rho)

such that

\sup_{x,\xi}\sum_{j,k}\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname{supp}\chi_{j,k}(x,\cdot)}(\xi)\leq M\cdot N.

In particular the double family
\{\chi_{j,k}\}_{j,k}
has uniformly bounded overlap
(independent of
x
). Moreover one may take the explicit bounds

N\leq\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(B(0,5/4))}{\operatorname{Vol}(B(0,1/4))}=5^{n},\qquad M\leq 5.

Proof.

We prove two simple facts and combine them.

(I) Fixed-corona overlap bound.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Define

S_{x,k}(\xi):=\{\,j:\ \xi\in\operatorname{supp}\chi_{j,k}(x,\cdot)\,\}.

If
j\in S_{x,k}(\xi)
then, by the support hypothesis,

T_{x}\xi\in B(\zeta_{j,k},1).

Hence all
\zeta_{j,k}
with
j\in S_{x,k}(\xi)
lie in the Euclidean ball
B(T_{x}\xi,1)
.
Since the net
\{\zeta_{j,k}\}_{j}
is
1/2
-separated, the open balls
B(\zeta_{j,k},1/4)
for

j\in S_{x,k}(\xi)
are pairwise disjoint and are contained in
B(T_{x}\xi,1+1/4)=B(T_{x}\xi,5/4)
.
Therefore

\#S_{x,k}(\xi)\cdot\operatorname{Vol}\big(B(0,1/4)\big)\leq\operatorname{Vol}\big(B(T_{x}\xi,5/4)\big)=\operatorname{Vol}\big(B(0,5/4)\big).

Thus, for every
x,k,\xi
,

\#\{j:\ \xi\in\operatorname{supp}\chi_{j,k}(x,\cdot)\}\leq\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(B(0,5/4))}{\operatorname{Vol}(B(0,1/4))}=:\;N(n).

(II) Uniform bound on the number of active dyadic scales.

Fix
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\rho_{k}(\xi)\neq 0
then

2^{-k}|\xi|\in\operatorname{supp}\rho\subset[1/4,4]
, equivalently

\frac{1}{4}\leq 2^{-k}|\xi|\leq 4\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\log_{2}|\xi|-2\leq k\leq\log_{2}|\xi|+2.

Hence the integer values of
k
for which
\rho_{k}(\xi)\neq 0
lie in an interval of length
4
,
so there are at most
5
such integers. Therefore one may take
M(\rho)\leq 5
.

Combining (I) and (II) gives

\sum_{j,k}\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname{supp}\chi_{j,k}(x,\cdot)}(\xi)\leq M(\rho)\cdot N(n)\leq 5\cdot 5^{n}=5^{n+1},

and taking the supremum over
x,\xi
yields the stated uniform bound.
∎

3.1
Normalized sum and local cutoffs

Throughout this section define the normalization sum

\Sigma(x,\xi):=\sum_{j,k}\chi_{j,k}(x,\xi).

By construction the sum is pointwise finite (cf. Lemma
3.1
).

Lower bound for
\Sigma
.

If
\rho_{k}(\xi)\neq 0
then
|\xi|\simeq 2^{k}
, and by the uniform spectral bounds for
T_{x}=G_{x}^{1/2}
there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
(depending only on
\lambda_{G}^{\min},\lambda_{G}^{\max}
) such that

c_{1}2^{k}\leq|T_{x}\xi|\leq c_{2}2^{k}.

Consequently there is an integer
k^{\prime}
with
|k^{\prime}-k|\leq K
(where
K=K(\lambda_{G}^{\min},\lambda_{G}^{\max})
)
such that
T_{x}\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime}}
. By maximality of the
1/2
-separated net in
\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime}}

there exists some index
j
for which
|T_{x}\xi-\zeta_{j,k^{\prime}}|\leq 1/2
, hence
\widetilde{\chi}_{j,k^{\prime}}(x,\xi)=1
.
Since
\Sigma
sums over all dyadic levels, we obtain the uniform lower bound

\Sigma(x,\xi)\geq 1\qquad\text{for every }(x,\xi)\text{ with }\rho_{k}(\xi)\neq 0.

This positive lower bound (on the effective dyadic support) is the key ingredient to control derivatives of
1/\Sigma
.

Dyadic partition of unity.

Define the normalized local cutoffs by

\Lambda_{j,k}(x,\xi)\;:=\;\frac{\chi_{j,k}(x,\xi)}{\Sigma(x,\xi)},\qquad\text{for }(x,\xi)\text{ such that }\Sigma(x,\xi)>0.

Then
0\leq\Lambda_{j,k}\leq 1
, the sum is locally finite and one has the partition of unity

\sum_{j,k}\Lambda_{j,k}(x,\xi)=1\qquad\text{on }\{\Sigma>0\},

(5)

while outside the effective dyadic support all functions vanish.

Proposition 3.2
.

The sum
\Sigma(x,\xi)=\sum_{j,k}\chi_{j,k}(x,\xi)
is pointwise finite and

\Sigma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\xi})
. Moreover, for every multiindex

\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)
there exists a constant
C_{\gamma}
(depending on
\varphi,\rho
,
n

and the finitely many constants
c_{\gamma}
from (
1
)) such that for all
(x,\xi)

\big|\partial^{\gamma}\Sigma(x,\xi)\big|\leq C_{\gamma}\,\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{\,|\beta|+1}.

Proof.

Pointwise finiteness and smoothness.

By definition
\chi_{j,k}(x,\xi)=\rho_{k}(\xi)\,\widetilde{\chi}_{j,k}(x,\xi)
where

\rho_{k}(\xi)=\rho(2^{-k}|\xi|)
has
\operatorname{supp}\rho_{k}\subset\{2^{k-2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+2}\}
.
Hence for fixed
\xi
only finitely many
k
contribute, and for each such
k
only finitely many
indices
j
have
\widetilde{\chi}_{j,k}(x,\xi)\not\equiv 0
(maximal
1/2
-separated net). Thus the sum defining

\Sigma(x,\xi)
is finite for each
(x,\xi)
; each summand is
C^{\infty}
, so
\Sigma
is
C^{\infty}
.

Derivative bounds for a single summand.

Recall
u_{j,k}(x,\xi)=T_{x}(\xi-\theta_{j,k}(x))
. Using
T_{x}\theta_{j,k}(x)=\zeta_{j,k}
we rewrite

u_{j,k}(x,\xi)=T_{x}\xi-\zeta_{j,k}.

This form is convenient because
\zeta_{j,k}
is constant (independent of
x
) and therefore

\partial_{\xi}u_{j,k}(x,\xi)=T_{x},\qquad\partial_{x}u_{j,k}(x,\xi)=(\partial_{x}T_{x})\,\xi,

and in general

\partial_{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta^{\prime}}u_{j,k}(x,\xi)=\begin{cases}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}T_{x})\,\xi,&\beta^{\prime}=0,\\[4.0pt]
\partial_{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}T_{x},&|\beta^{\prime}|=1,\\[4.0pt]
0,&|\beta^{\prime}|\geq 2.\end{cases}

By Lemma
2.2
there exist constants
A_{\alpha^{\prime}}
with

\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}T_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq A_{\alpha^{\prime}}\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha^{\prime}|}
. Hence for all multiindices
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}

\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta^{\prime}}u_{j,k}(x,\xi)\big|\leq C_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\,\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha^{\prime}|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{\mathbf{1}_{\{|\beta^{\prime}|=0\}}},

(6)

where
\mathbf{1}_{\{|\beta^{\prime}|=0\}}=1
if
|\beta^{\prime}|=0
and
0
otherwise; the constants
C_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}
depend only on the
A_{\alpha^{\prime}}
and the dimension.

Apply Lemma
A.1
to
\varphi\circ u_{j,k}
: since the derivatives of
u_{j,k}
satisfy (
6
), for every multiindex
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)
there is a constant
D_{\gamma}(\varphi)
(depending on finitely many seminorms
p_{r}(\varphi)
and the constants
C_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}
) such that

\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\big(\varphi\circ u_{j,k}\big)(x,\xi)\big|\leq D_{\gamma}(\varphi)\,\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta|}.

(7)

(Notice that derivatives in
\xi
of order
\geq 2
of
u_{j,k}
vanish, which simplifies the combinatorics; the
\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta|}
factor arises only from those terms where zero or one
\xi
-derivative of
u
appears.)

Derivatives of
\rho_{k}(\xi)
.

The radial cutoff
\rho_{k}(\xi)=\rho(2^{-k}|\xi|)
satisfies for each multiindex
\beta_{1}

\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{1}}\rho_{k}(\xi)\big|\leq E_{\beta_{1}}\,2^{-k|\beta_{1}|}\,|\xi|^{-|\beta_{1}|}

on its support. But on
\operatorname{supp}\rho_{k}
we have
|\xi|\simeq 2^{k}
, hence

\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{1}}\rho_{k}(\xi)\big|\leq\widetilde{E}_{\beta_{1}},

uniformly in
k
and
\xi
(and in fact one may bound it by
C_{\beta_{1}}\langle\xi\rangle^{-|\beta_{1}|}
). Thus derivatives of
\rho_{k}
are uniformly bounded by constants depending only on
\rho
and
|\beta_{1}|
; in particular there exists
E^{\prime}_{\beta_{1}}
with

\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{1}}\rho_{k}(\xi)\big|\leq E^{\prime}_{\beta_{1}}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{\,0}\leq E^{\prime}_{\beta_{1}}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta|+1},

(8)

(the last inequality is trivial for bounded
\xi
and power-growth for large
\xi
; we keep the convenient upper exponent
|\beta|+1
for later absorption).

Leibniz and single-summand bound.

Write
\chi_{j,k}=\rho_{k}\cdot(\varphi\circ u_{j,k})
. For a given multiindex
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)
, by Leibniz,

\partial^{\gamma}\chi_{j,k}=\sum_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\,\partial^{\gamma_{1}}\rho_{k}(\xi)\;\partial^{\gamma_{2}}(\varphi\circ u_{j,k})(x,\xi).

Applying (
7
) and (
8
) we obtain, for each term,

\big|\partial^{\gamma_{1}}\rho_{k}(\xi)\;\partial^{\gamma_{2}}(\varphi\circ u_{j,k})\big|\leq E^{\prime}_{\gamma_{1}}\,D_{\gamma_{2}}(\varphi)\,\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha_{2}|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|+1},

and since
|\alpha_{2}|\leq|\alpha|
,
|\beta_{2}|\leq|\beta|
we can absorb combinatorial factors to get a uniform bound (independent of
j,k
)

\big|\partial^{\gamma}\chi_{j,k}(x,\xi)\big|\leq C_{\gamma}^{(1)}\,\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta|+1},

(9)

where
C_{\gamma}^{(1)}
depends only on finitely many seminorms of
\varphi,\rho
and on the constants
A_{\alpha^{\prime}}
coming from derivatives of
T_{x}
.

Summation using bounded overlap.

By Lemma
3.1
there is a finite uniform bound
L:=M\cdot N
such that for each fixed
(x,\xi)
at most
L
terms
\chi_{j,k}(x,\xi)
are nonzero. Therefore summing (
9
) over all
j,k
yields

\big|\partial^{\gamma}\Sigma(x,\xi)\big|\leq\sum_{j,k}\big|\partial^{\gamma}\chi_{j,k}(x,\xi)\big|\leq L\,C_{\gamma}^{(1)}\,\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta|+1}.

Set
C_{\gamma}:=L\,C_{\gamma}^{(1)}
to conclude the claimed estimate.

Remarks on dependence of constants.

The constant
C_{\gamma}
depends only on
n
, finitely many seminorms of the mother-cut
\varphi
and the radial profile
\rho
, the finite collection of constants
c_{\mu}
appearing in (
1
) (through the bounds
A_{\alpha^{\prime}}
on derivatives of
T_{x}
), and on the explicit overlap constant
L=5^{n+1}
coming from Lemma
3.1
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Sigma
and
\Lambda_{j,k}
be as above. Since
\Sigma(x,\xi)\geq 1
on the effective dyadic
support, for every multiindex
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)
there exists
C_{\gamma}>0
(depending on

n
, finitely many seminorms of
\varphi,\rho
, the constants
c_{\mu}
from

(10)

Proof.

Since
\Sigma\geq 1
everywhere, we have
\Sigma^{-1-r}\leq 1
for all

.
The proof then follows the same Faà di Bruno and Leibniz arguments as before,
but without the need to track negative powers of
\Sigma
.

Write
\Lambda_{j,k}=\chi_{j,k}\cdot(\,1/\Sigma\,)

\partial^{\gamma}\Lambda_{j,k}=\sum_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\big(\partial^{\gamma_{1}}\chi_{j,k}\big)\,\big(\partial^{\gamma_{2}}(1/\Sigma)\big).

From Proposition
3.2

and each

(12)

For the reciprocal

). Since
\Sigma\geq 1
,
each derivative
\partial^{\gamma_{2}}(1/\Sigma)
is bounded by a finite linear combination of terms
of the form

4
Quantization and Local–Global Composition

In this section we establish the core ingredients of local quantization on the dyadic decomposition:
(i) symbolic stability under multiplication by the microlocalizers
\Lambda_{j,k}
with finite losses
and uniform control in
(j,k)
;
(ii)
H^{s}\to H^{s-m_{2}}
bounds for cutoff operators —including a semiclassical variant that recovers the
factor
2^{km_{2}}
; and
(iii) composition estimates via truncated Moyal expansion with decay in the fiber distance, which enable
global recombination by almost-orthogonality (Cotlar–Stein).
These results provide the technical scaffolding for later applications (patchwise parametrices, remainder control, and global summation).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
a\in S^{m_{1},m_{2}}
in the sense of Definition
2.1
, and fix derivative cutoffs

\imath,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Define the finite losses

N_{x}:=2\imath+\ell,\qquad N_{\xi}:=2\ell+1+\imath.

Then for every
\imath,\ell
there exists a constant
C=C(\imath,\ell)
(depending on finitely many seminorms of
\varphi,\rho
, on the constants
c_{\gamma}
from (
1
), on the overlap constant
L
, and on the combinatorics of the Leibniz/Faà di Bruno expansions) such that for every index pair
(j,k)
one has

\|\,a\Lambda_{j,k}\,\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1}+N_{x},\;m_{2}+N_{\xi})}\leq C\,\|a\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1},m_{2})}.

In particular
a\Lambda_{j,k}\in S^{m_{1}+N_{x},\;m_{2}+N_{\xi}}
and the bound is uniform in
j,k
.

Proof.

Fix
\imath,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. By definition of the seminorms we must estimate, for all multiindices
\alpha,\beta
with
|\alpha|\leq\imath
,
|\beta|\leq\ell
,

\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\frac{\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\big(a(x,\xi)\Lambda_{j,k}(x,\xi)\big)\big|}{\langle x\rangle^{m_{1}+N_{x}-|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{m_{2}+N_{\xi}-|\beta|}}.

Apply Leibniz:

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}(a\Lambda)=\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha\atop\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta}\binom{\alpha}{\alpha_{1}}\binom{\beta}{\beta_{1}}\big(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{1}}a\big)\,\big(\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{2}}\Lambda_{j,k}\big).

By the hypothesis
a\in S^{m_{1},m_{2}}
,

\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{1}}a(x,\xi)\big|\leq\|a\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1},m_{2})}\,\langle x\rangle^{m_{1}-|\alpha_{1}|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{m_{2}-|\beta_{1}|},

(valid because
|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha|\leq\imath
,
|\beta_{1}|\leq|\beta|\leq\ell
, and the seminorm with indices
(\imath,\ell)
controls these derivatives).

From Proposition
3.3
we have uniformly in
j,k
:

\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{2}}\Lambda_{j,k}(x,\xi)\big|\leq C_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}\,\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha_{2}|+|\beta_{2}|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|+1+|\alpha_{2}|},

where the constants
C_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}
depend only on finitely many data but
not
on the particular
(j,k)
.

Multiply the two bounds and divide by the target denominator:

\displaystyle\frac{\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{1}}a\big|\;\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{2}}\Lambda_{j,k}\big|}{\langle x\rangle^{m_{1}+N_{x}-|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{m_{2}+N_{\xi}-|\beta|}}

\displaystyle\leq\|a\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1},m_{2})}\,C_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}\;\frac{\langle x\rangle^{m_{1}-|\alpha_{1}|}\langle\xi\rangle^{m_{2}-|\beta_{1}|}\;\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha_{2}|+|\beta_{2}|}\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta_{2}|+1+|\alpha_{2}|}}{\langle x\rangle^{m_{1}+N_{x}-|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{m_{2}+N_{\xi}-|\beta|}}.

Simplify exponents using
|\alpha|=|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|
and
|\beta|=|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|
:

\displaystyle\text{power in }x

\displaystyle:\quad-|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|+|\beta_{2}|+|\alpha|-N_{x}=2|\alpha_{2}|+|\beta_{2}|-N_{x},

\displaystyle\text{power in }\xi

\displaystyle:\quad-|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|+1+|\alpha_{2}|+|\beta|-N_{\xi}=2|\beta_{2}|+1+|\alpha_{2}|-N_{\xi}.

Thus the quotient equals

\|a\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1},m_{2})}\,C_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}\;\langle x\rangle^{\,2|\alpha_{2}|+|\beta_{2}|-N_{x}}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{\,2|\beta_{2}|+1+|\alpha_{2}|-N_{\xi}}.

Now choose the losses
N_{x},N_{\xi}
exactly so that the exponents above are non-positive for every admissible pair
(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})
:

N_{x}\geq\max_{0\leq|\alpha_{2}|\leq\imath,\ 0\leq|\beta_{2}|\leq\ell}(2|\alpha_{2}|+|\beta_{2}|)=2\imath+\ell,\qquad N_{\xi}\geq\max_{0\leq|\alpha_{2}|\leq\imath,\ 0\leq|\beta_{2}|\leq\ell}(2|\beta_{2}|+1+|\alpha_{2}|)=2\ell+1+\imath.

With the choice
N_{x}=2\imath+\ell,\ N_{\xi}=2\ell+1+\imath
the residual polynomial factors are bounded by 1. Therefore each summand above is bounded by

\|a\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1},m_{2})}\;C_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}},

and summing over the finitely many decompositions
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha,\ \beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta
yields

\frac{\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}(a\Lambda_{j,k})\big|}{\langle x\rangle^{m_{1}+N_{x}-|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{m_{2}+N_{\xi}-|\beta|}}\leq C^{\prime}(\imath,\ell)\,\|a\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1},m_{2})}.

Taking the supremum over
|\alpha|\leq\imath,|\beta|\leq\ell
proves

\|a\Lambda_{j,k}\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1}+N_{x},\;m_{2}+N_{\xi})}\leq C(\imath,\ell)\,\|a\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1},m_{2})},

uniformly in
j,k
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The choices
N_{x}=2\imath+\ell
and
N_{\xi}=2\ell+1+\imath
are optimal for the worst-case scenario in the Leibniz expansion. They ensure that for all
|\alpha_{2}|\leq\imath
,
|\beta_{2}|\leq\ell

2|\alpha_{2}|+|\beta_{2}|\leq 2\imath+\ell=N_{x},\quad 2|\beta_{2}|+1+|\alpha_{2}|\leq 2\ell+1+\imath=N_{\xi},

which guarantees the residual factors are bounded by 1.

Proposition 4.3

(Local Weyl quantization, uniform control in

— conservative version)
.

Let
a\in S^{m_{1},m_{2}}
and let

(15)

The constant
C
is independent of
j,k
.

Proof.

Since
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
are supported in the compact set

, the derivatives of

and the weights

\langle x\rangle^{p}
are uniformly bounded on

, and all dependence on

is absorbed into

.

By Proposition
4.1
, the symbols
a\Lambda_{j,k}
belong to

S^{m_{1}+N_{x},m_{2}+N_{\xi}}
and satisfy

\|a\Lambda_{j,k}\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1}+N_{x},m_{2}+N_{\xi})}\leq C_{0}\,\|a\|_{\imath,\ell}^{(m_{1},m_{2})},

with

Proposition 4.5

(Semiclassical Weyl quantization with band renormalization)
.

Let
a\in S^{m_{1},m_{2}}
and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}
be compact. Take
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\text{supp}\chi,\text{supp}\chi^{\prime}\subset K
. Fix
\imath,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and define
N_{x}:=2\imath+\ell
,
N_{\xi}:=2\ell+1+\imath
.
For each
(j,k)
set
h:=2^{-k}\in(0,1]
and define

b_{j,k}(x,\xi;h):=h^{-N_{\xi}}\,a(x,\xi)\,\Lambda_{j,k}(x,\xi),

\text{Op}_{h}^{w}(b)u(x)=(2\pi h)^{-n}\!\iint e^{\tfrac{i}{h}(x-y)\cdot\xi}\,b\!\Big(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\xi;h\Big)\,u(y)\,dy\,d\xi.

Then there exist integers
N=N(n)
,
N^{\prime}=N^{\prime}(n,m_{2})
and a constant

\widetilde{C}=\widetilde{C}\!\big(K,\chi,\chi^{\prime},n,m_{1},m_{2},\varphi,\rho,L,\{c_{\gamma}:|\gamma|\leq r\}\big),

with

finite, such that, uniformly in
(j,k)

\big\|\chi\,\text{Op}_{h}^{w}(b_{j,k}(\cdot;\,h))\,\chi^{\prime}\big\|_{H^{s}\to H^{s-m_{2}}}\leq\widetilde{C}\,2^{km_{2}}\!\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq N\\
|\beta|\leq N^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\,\sup_{(x,\xi)\in K\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(x,\xi)|}{\langle x\rangle^{m_{1}-|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{m_{2}-|\beta|}}.

(16)

The constant
\widetilde{C}
is independent of
(j,k)
.

Proof.

Writing
T_{j,k}(h):=\chi\,\text{Op}_{h}^{w}(b_{j,k})\,\chi^{\prime}
, the change of variables
\xi=h\eta
gives

T_{j,k}(h)u(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\chi(x)\!\iint e^{i(x-y)\cdot\eta}\,\widetilde{b}_{j,k}\!\Big(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\eta;h\Big)\,\chi^{\prime}(y)\,u(y)\,dy\,d\eta,

with standard symbol
\widetilde{b}_{j,k}(x,\eta;h):=b_{j,k}(x,h\eta;h)
. For
|\alpha|\leq M_{x}
,
|\beta|\leq M_{\eta}
,

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}\widetilde{b}_{j,k}(x,\eta;h)=h^{|\beta|}\,\big(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}b_{j,k}\big)(x,h\eta;h).

Since
\Lambda_{j,k}\in S^{0,N_{\xi}}
(uniformly in
j,k
) and by Proposition
4.1
,

\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\!\big(a\Lambda_{j,k}\big)(x,\xi)\big|\lesssim\mathcal{S}_{N,N^{\prime}}(a)\,\langle\xi\rangle^{m_{2}+N_{\xi}-|\beta|}\quad(x\in K),

and on the support of
\Lambda_{j,k}
we have
\langle\xi\rangle\simeq 2^{k}=h^{-1}
. Hence

\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}b_{j,k}(x,\xi;h)\big|=h^{-N_{\xi}}\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}(a\Lambda_{j,k})(x,\xi)\big|\lesssim\mathcal{S}_{N,N^{\prime}}(a)\,h^{-(m_{2}+2N_{\xi}-|\beta|)}.

Consequently,

\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}\widetilde{b}_{j,k}(x,\eta;h)\big|\lesssim\mathcal{S}_{N,N^{\prime}}(a)\,h^{|\beta|-(m_{2}+2N_{\xi}-|\beta|)}=\mathcal{S}_{N,N^{\prime}}(a)\,h^{-(m_{2}+2N_{\xi}-2|\beta|)}.

Choosing
M_{\eta}\leq N_{\xi}
(and
M_{x}
large enough for local CV), we obtain

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in K,\ \eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
|\alpha|\leq M_{x},\,|\beta|\leq M_{\eta}\end{subarray}}\big|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}\widetilde{b}_{j,k}(x,\eta;h)\big|\leq C_{\star}\,h^{-m_{2}}\,\mathcal{S}_{N,N^{\prime}}(a),

with
C_{\star}
independent of
(j,k)
and
h
. The local Calderón–Vaillancourt theorem (standard version cf.
[
2
]
) applied to

\text{Op}^{w}(\widetilde{b}_{j,k})
yields

\|T_{j,k}(h)\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C_{CV}\,C_{\star}\,h^{-m_{2}}\,\mathcal{S}_{N,N^{\prime}}(a).

Conjugating by
\langle D\rangle^{s}
and repeating the argument (the additional derivatives of the symbol remain
controlled by the same bound) gives

\|T_{j,k}(h)\|_{H^{s}\to H^{s-m_{2}}}\leq\widetilde{C}\,h^{-m_{2}}\,\mathcal{S}_{N,N^{\prime}}(a)=\widetilde{C}\,2^{km_{2}}\,\mathcal{S}_{N,N^{\prime}}(a),

which is (
16
).

Lemma 4.8

(Composition estimate for separated patches)
.

Let
a\in S^{m_{1},m_{2}}
and, for indices
(j,k)
and
(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})
, define

a_{j,k}(x,\xi):=a(x,\xi)\,\Lambda_{j,k}(x,\xi),\qquad a_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}(x,\xi):=a(x,\xi)\,\Lambda_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}(x,\xi).

Let

be the symbol of the Weyl composition

\text{Op}^{w}(a_{j,k})\text{Op}^{w}(a_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}})=\text{Op}^{w}(r_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}}).
Fix a compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}
. For every

and multiindex
\gamma
there exist integers

and a constant

) such that, writing

\big|\partial_{x,\xi}^{\gamma}r_{jj^{\prime}kk^{\prime}}(x,\xi)\big|\leq C_{\gamma,M}\;2^{km_{2}}\,2^{k^{\prime}m_{2}}\;\mathcal{S}_{N_{0},N_{1}}(a)^{2}\;\big(1+d_{\xi}\big((j,k),(j^{\prime},k^{\prime})\big)\big)^{-M},

(19)

where

\mathcal{S}_{N_{0},N_{1}}(a):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq N_{0}\\
|\beta|\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}\sup_{(x,\xi)\in K\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(x,\xi)|}{\langle x\rangle^{m_{1}-|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{m_{2}-|\beta|}},

and

may be chosen depending only on

and
n
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sup_{\text{eff. dom.}}\Big|\iint e^{\frac{i}{2}(v\cdot y-u\cdot\eta)}\,\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{j,k}}(x+u,\xi+v)\,\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}}(x+y,\xi+\eta)\,du\,dv\,dy\,d\eta\Big|,

5
Microlocal parametrix for symbols with

-dependent fiber scale

In this section we construct a microlocal parametrix for Weyl operators with symbol

p(x,\xi)
whose
fiber scale
depends on

through

.
We work with the anisotropic metric

g_{x}(\xi):=\xi^{\top}G_{x}\xi=|T_{x}\xi|^{2},\qquad\|\xi\|_{g_{x}}:=|T_{x}\xi|,

with

\sigma(G_{x})\subset[\lambda_{G}^{\min},\lambda_{G}^{\max}]

\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}G_{x}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq c_{\beta}\langle x\rangle^{|\beta|}

1

2.3
)

Assumptions and notation

Fix a compact set

and
\chi_{0},\chi_{0}^{\prime}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(K)

1.

with
m_{2}>0
with respect to

\alpha,\beta

2.

Microlocal ellipticity on
K
:
there exist

and

such that

and

on

3.

Matrix smoothness:

T_{x}=G_{x}^{1/2}\in C^{\infty}
on

, with derivatives controlled by
\{\lambda_{G}^{\min},\lambda_{G}^{\max},c_{\beta}\}

(cf. Lemma

).

Theorem 5.1

(Anisotropic microlocal parametrix)
.

Under the assumptions above, there exist

, on
\lambda_{G}^{\min},\lambda_{G}^{\max}
, and on a
finite number of

(j,k)

Proof.

6
Radon transform and its dyadic microlocal decomposition

We consider the Euclidean Radon transform

(Rf)(s,\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\delta\big(s-x\cdot\omega\big)\,dx,\qquad(s,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

We view
R
as a Fourier integral operator in the class

I^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}(\mathcal{C})
associated with the standard canonical relation
\mathcal{C}

between
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\setminus\!0
and
T^{*}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\!\setminus\!0

(cf.
[
11
, Ch. XI, §2–3]
). In particular, the normal operator

N:=R^{*}R

is an elliptic pseudodifferential operator of order
-(n-1)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with principal symbol

\sigma_{\mathrm{pr}}(N)(\xi)=c_{n}\,|\xi|^{-(n-1)}\qquad(\xi\neq 0),

for some
c_{n}\neq 0
(composition of an FIO with its adjoint; see
[
11
, Ch. XI, Thm. 2.3 & Thm. 2.6]
).
Equivalently,
N
is (up to a constant) the Riesz potential of order
n-1
; hence the inversion formula reads

(-\Delta)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\,R^{*}R\,f\;=\;C_{n}\,f\quad\text{in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where, for even
n
,
(-\Delta)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
is understood as the fractional pseudodifferential operator
with multiplier
|\xi|^{\,n-1}
(cf.
[
9
, Ch. I–II]
; see also
[
17
, Ch. VIII]
,
[
7
, Ch. 5]
).

We will work with compact cutoffs in the input and output variables, and with the dyadic microlocal
decomposition introduced earlier.

6.1

Fix a compact set
K_{x}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and cutoffs
\chi_{x},\chi_{x}^{\prime}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(K_{x})

\chi_{s,\omega}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(K_{s,\omega})

and the microlocalizers
\Lambda_{j,k}

(j,k)

The fiber order

of the symbol and the FIO order

of

(Radon: global recombination)

7
Conclusions

Final comments on the patchwise sum
S
and the quantization
\text{Op}^{w}(a)

The results of Section
4
establish the microlocal scaffolding over a dyadic decomposition
(normalized by
T_{x}
) to reconstruct, with uniform control, the quantized operator associated with

a\in S^{m_{1},m_{2}}
on localized regions. We summarize the key points.

(i)

Local reconstruction and norm control.

With
\Lambda_{j,k}
a normalized dyadic partition and
a_{j,k}:=a\,\Lambda_{j,k}
, over a compact set

K
(cutoffs
\chi_{0},\chi_{0}^{\prime}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(K)
), the sum

S\;=\;\sum_{j,k}\chi_{0}\,\text{Op}^{w}(a_{j,k})\,\chi_{0}^{\prime}

converges in
\mathcal{L}(H^{s},H^{s-m_{2}})
and reproduces
\chi_{0}\,\text{Op}^{w}(a)\,\chi_{0}^{\prime}
in the relevant microlocal
region (whenever
\sum_{j,k}\Lambda_{j,k}=1
there (cf. (
5
))). Quantitative control comes from
uniform local quantization, Moyal truncation, and global recombination via almost-orthogonality (Cotlar–Stein).

(ii)

Low frequencies and manageable remainder.

The very low-frequency band (around
\zeta=0
) is handled by an explicit radial cutoff; its contribution is a finite-order
operator amenable to the classical calculus. The rest of the dyadic sum converges in norm, and any discrepancy is absorbed as a smoothing
or lower-order term.

(iii)

Uniformity and dependence of constants.

The global bound

\|S\|_{H^{s}\to H^{s-m_{2}}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N_{0},\;|\beta|\leq N_{1}}\|a\|_{\alpha,\beta}^{(m_{1},m_{2})}

is uniform in
(j,k)
. The constant
C
depends only on
K
,
n
,
m_{1},m_{2}
, on finitely many
seminorms of the mother cut
\varphi
, of the radial profile
\rho
, and on the overlap
L
.

(iv)

Developed applications.

On this framework we presented two representative applications:

•

A
microlocal parametrix
with
x
-dependent fiber scale, where the patchwise sum
allows one to construct
Q=\sum_{j,k}\text{Op}^{w}(q_{j,k})
and control the remainder via Moyal truncation.

•

The
Radon transform
with dyadic microlocal decomposition, obtaining the per-patch scale
factor
2^{k(m_{2}-m_{R})}
and global recombination via Cotlar–Stein.

(v)

Robustness with respect to the partition.

Moderate variations in
\varphi
or in the centers
\zeta_{j,k}
only affect the constants through additional
seminorms; they do not alter the validity of the reconstruction nor the convergence.

(vi)

Perspectives.

The scheme naturally extends to:

•

semiclassical settings (identifying
h\sim 2^{-k}
),

•

L^{p}
and
W^{s,p}
bounds (patchwise kernels + local CV + global sum),

•

fine detection of singularities via positive commutator,

•

variants with directional frames after normalization by
T_{x}
.

These lines are mentioned here as natural extensions for future work.

In sum, the dyadic microlocal partition normalized by
T_{x}
provides a stable and quantitative mechanism for local–global
operator reconstruction, with precise control of constants and remainders, suitable for parametrices and FIOs such as the Radon transform,
and readily extendable to other scenarios.

A.1
Multivariable Faà di Bruno lemma

Lemma A.1

(Multivariable Faà di Bruno: composition estimate)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\xi};\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
Fix a nonzero multi-index
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2n}\setminus\{0\}
with
s:=|\gamma|=|\alpha|+|\beta|
, and suppose that
for every
\gamma^{\prime}=(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
there exists
C_{\gamma^{\prime}}>0
such that

|\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}u(x,\xi)|\ \leq\ C_{\gamma^{\prime}}\,\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha^{\prime}|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta^{\prime}|}\qquad\forall(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

Then, for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,

\big|\partial^{\gamma}(\varphi\circ u)(x,\xi)\big|\ \leq\ C_{\gamma}(\varphi)\,\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha|}\,\langle\xi\rangle^{|\beta|},

where

C_{\gamma}(\varphi):=\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{p_{r}(\varphi)}{r!}\sum_{(\gamma^{1},\dots,\gamma^{r})\in\mathcal{S}_{r}(\gamma)}\ \prod_{j=1}^{r}C_{\gamma^{j}},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{r}(\gamma):=\big\{(\gamma^{1},\dots,\gamma^{r})\in(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2n}\!\setminus\!\{0\})^{r}:\ \sum_{j=1}^{r}\gamma^{j}=\gamma\big\}.

Moreover,
\partial^{\gamma}(\varphi\circ u)\equiv 0
outside
u^{-1}(\text{supp}\varphi)
. The constant
C_{\gamma}(\varphi)
depends on
p_{1}(\varphi),\dots,p_{s}(\varphi)
and on
\{C_{\gamma^{\prime}}:\ |\gamma^{\prime}|\leq s\}
.

Proof.

By the ordered multivariable Faà di Bruno formula (cf.
[
5
]
),

\partial^{\gamma}(\varphi\circ u)=\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{r!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{1}+\cdots+\gamma^{r}=\gamma\\
\gamma^{j}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\nabla^{r}\varphi\!\big(u(x,\xi)\big)\big(\partial^{\gamma^{1}}u(x,\xi),\dots,\partial^{\gamma^{r}}u(x,\xi)\big).

Taking absolute values, using
\,\|\nabla^{r}\varphi\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq p_{r}(\varphi)
and the hypotheses on
u
yields the stated bound; vanishing outside
u^{-1}(\text{supp}\varphi)
is immediate from the support of
\varphi
.
∎

A.3
Cotlar–Stein lemma and Calderón–Vaillancourt theorem

Lemma A.6

(Cotlar–Stein)
.

Let
\{T_{j}\}_{j\in J}
(at most countable) be a family of bounded operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
If

A:=\sup_{j\in J}\ \sum_{k\in J}\ \big\|T_{j}^{\,*}T_{k}\big\|^{1/2}<\infty,\qquad B:=\sup_{k\in J}\ \sum_{j\in J}\ \big\|T_{j}T_{k}^{\,*}\big\|^{1/2}<\infty,

then, for every finite subset
F\subset J
,

\Big\|\sum_{j\in F}T_{j}\Big\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\ \leq\ \sqrt{A\,B}.

In particular, the partial sums are uniformly bounded and, for any ordering of
J
, the series

\sum_{j\in J}T_{j}
converges in the strong topology to some
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
with
\|T\|\leq\sqrt{AB}
.

Corollary A.1

(Tail criterion: norm convergence)
.

Under the hypotheses of Lemma
A.6
, if in addition

A_{N}:=\sup_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in J\\
\mathrm{ind}(k)\geq N\end{subarray}}\big\|T_{j}^{\,*}T_{k}\big\|^{1/2}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0,\qquad B_{N}:=\sup_{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
\mathrm{ind}(j)\geq N\end{subarray}}\big\|T_{j}T_{k}^{\,*}\big\|^{1/2}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0,

then
\sum_{j\in J}T_{j}
converges in norm in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
and

\big\|\sum_{j\in J}T_{j}\big\|\leq\sqrt{AB}
.

Theorem A.7

(Calderón–Vaillancourt, local version)
.

Let
n\geq 1
. There exists
M=M(n)\geq 2n+1
and a constant
C=C(n,M)
such that the following holds.
Let
b\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\xi})
and let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}
be compact and
B>0
such that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq M\\

Then, for any cutoffs
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(K)
, the Weyl quantization

\text{Op}^{w}(b)u(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\!\iint e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}\,b\!\Big(\tfrac{x+y}{2},\xi\Big)\,u(y)\,dy\,d\xi

satisfies

\big\|\chi\,\text{Op}^{w}(b)\,\chi^{\prime}\big\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\ \leq\ C\,B.

More generally, if
b
is of order
m
in
\xi
, then for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\big\|\chi\,\text{Op}^{w}(b)\,\chi^{\prime}\big\|_{H^{s}\to H^{s-m}}\ \leq\ C\,B.

Proof.

By conjugation with
\langle D\rangle^{s}
and Weyl symbolic composition with finitely many derivatives, it suffices to treat
m=0
,
s=0
; this only changes
B
by a dimensional constant (cf.
[
19
, Theorem 18.6.1]
,
[
16
, Theorem 23.1]
).
The kernel of
T:=\chi\,\text{Op}^{w}(b)\,\chi^{\prime}
is

K(x,y)=(2\pi)^{-n}\,\chi(x)\,\chi^{\prime}(y)\int e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}b\!\Big(\eta,\xi\Big)\,d\xi,\qquad\eta:=\tfrac{x+y}{2}\in K.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}=(1+|x-y|^{2})^{N}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}
; integrating by parts
N

\xi
,

Using mixed-derivative bounds for
b
up to order
2N
with
\eta\in K
,

\big|(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}b(\eta,\xi)\big|\ \leq\ C\,B,

and a standard frequency partition step or an additional integration by parts in

|K(x,y)|\ \leq\ C_{n,N}\,B\,|\chi(x)\chi^{\prime}(y)|\,(1+|x-y|^{2})^{-N}.

With

this function is integrable in

\|T\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C\,B
. The

∎

defined from any index enumeration tend to zero uniformly in
h
. By the Cotlar–Stein lemma
(cf. Lemma
A.6
) and its corollary (cf. Corollary
A.1
), the series
\sum_{j,k}T_{j,k}(h)

converges in norm in
\mathcal{L}(H^{s},H^{s-m_{2}})
uniformly on
0<h\leq 1
, with

\Big\|\sum_{j,k}T_{j,k}(h)\Big\|_{H^{s}\to H^{s-m_{2}}}\ \leq\ \sqrt{A(h)B(h)}\ \leq\ C\,\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N_{0},\ |\beta|\leq N_{1}}\ \sup_{0<h\leq 1}\|a(\cdot;h)\|_{\alpha,\beta}^{(m_{1},m_{2})}.

Finally, the dyadic partition of unity recorded in Section 3 yields

\sum_{j,k}\Lambda_{j,k}\equiv 1\quad\text{on the effective region,}\qquad\text{(see \eqref{eq:dyadic-partition-unity})},

and by symbolic linearity and continuity of
\text{Op}_{h}^{w}(\cdot)
we conclude

\sum_{j,k}\chi_{0}\,\text{Op}_{h}^{w}\!\big(a(\cdot;h)\,\Lambda_{j,k}\big)\,\chi_{0}^{\prime}\ =\ \chi_{0}\,\text{Op}_{h}^{w}(a(\cdot;h))\,\chi_{0}^{\prime}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{L}(H^{s},H^{s-m_{2}}),

which proves
(\star_{h})
and the claimed identity.
∎

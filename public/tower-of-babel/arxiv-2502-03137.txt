Positivity of line bundles on general blow ups of Hirzebruch surfaces
Cyril J. Jacob, Bivas Khan

Abstract
. We investigate various positivity properties of line bundles on general blow ups of Hirzebruch surfaces motivated by [ 6 ] , where the author has studied general blow ups of ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} . For each of the properties: ampleness, global generation, very ampleness, and k k -very ampleness, we provide several sufficient numerical conditions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14C20, 14E05, 14J26

1.
Introduction

Positivity properties of line bundles on the blow up of projective varieties have been of significant interest in the literature. There are several notions of the positivity of a line bundle, such as ampleness, global generation, very ampleness, and more generally
k
-very ampleness. We recall the definition of
k
-very ampleness.

Definition 1.1
.

A line bundle
L
on a projective variety
X
is said to be
k
-very ample if the restriction map

H^{0}(X,L)\to H^{0}(X,L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z})

is surjective for all zero-dimensional subschemes
Z\subset X
of length
k+1
.

Note that
0
-very ampleness and
1
-very ampleness of a line bundle are equivalent to the global generation and very ampleness, respectively.

Let
X
be a surface and
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
be
r
distinct points in
X
.
Let
\pi:X_{r}\to X
be the blow up of
r
distinct points on
X
.
Then

\text{Pic }X_{r}=\pi^{\ast}\text{Pic }X\oplus\mathbb{Z}.E_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}.E_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}.E_{r},

where
E_{i}=\pi^{-1}(\{p_{i}\})
is the exceptional divisor corresponding to
p_{i}
.
So, a line bundle
L
on
X_{r}
has the form
L=\pi^{\ast}(\mathcal{L})-m_{1}E_{1}-\dots-m_{r}E_{r}
, where
\mathcal{L}\in\text{Pic }X
. If
m_{i}=m
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, we call
L
a
uniform line bundle
.

The ampleness of a line bundle can be characterized in different ways: geometrically (which we take as the definition), cohomologically (through the theorem of Cartan-Serre-Grothendieck), or numerically (using the Nakai-Moishezon-Kleiman criterion). Determining whether a given line bundle is ample is often challenging. Although the Nakai-Moishezon-Kleiman criterion provides a numerical condition, it involves an infinite number of conditions to check. So, it is natural to look for a finite number of sufficient conditions to check ampleness.

In this article, we restrict our interest to surfaces.
It is known that if the
Nagata-Biran-Szemberg conjecture
(see
[
18
, Section 4.1]
) holds for a surface
X
, then for a uniform line bundle
L
on the blow up of
X
at a sufficiently large number of points is ample if and only if
L^{2}>0
(
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
). Several finite conditions have been proposed for surfaces to determine ampleness of a line bundle. The case of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
has been extensively studied by various authors. Küchle (
[
13
]
) and Xu (
[
19
]
) independently showed that for the general blow up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, the line bundle
L=\pi^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(d))-E_{1}-E_{2}-\dots-E_{r}
is ample if
d\geq 3
. The conditions for global generation and very ampleness can be found in
[
3
]
. A complete characterization for very ampleness is given in
[
5
, Proposition 3.2]
when
m_{i}=1
for all
i
. Later, Hanumanthu developed numerical criteria for the ampleness, global generation, and
k
-very ampleness of line bundles, when blown up points are either general (
[
6
]
) or special (
[
7
]
).

This question has also been explored for various other surfaces in addition to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. For example, on an abelian surface, Lee and Shin provided criteria for ampleness and
k
-very ampleness of uniform line bundles (see
[
15
]
). For a ruled surface, Szemberg and Gasinska provided a sufficient condition for a uniform line bundle with
m=1
to be
k
-very ample when the blown-up points are general (
[
17
]
). In
[
4
]
, Farnik obtained similar results for
k
-very ampleness in the case of hyperelliptic surface.

In this article, we investigate the positivity of line bundles on the blow ups of Hirzebruch surfaces. Our primary motivation comes from
[
6
]
.
In Section
3
, we have obtained three sets of sufficient conditions for ampleness (see Theorem
3.1
, Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.5
). As an application, we have provided certain lower bounds for multi-point Seshadri constants of line bundles on the Hirzebruch surface.

In Section
4
and Section
5
, we have obtained several sufficient conditions for global generation and very ampleness, respectively. The key tool here is the Reider’s criterion (see Theorem
2.2
) along with the conditions for ampleness obtained in Section
3
. In the final section, we have studied
k
-very ampleness. The key ingredient is a criterion by Beltrametti, Francia, and Sommese (see Theorem
2.3
), along with the criteria for ampleness obtained in Section
3
, as before.

Throughout this article, we work over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
e\geq 0
be an integer. Then the projective bundle associated with the rank two vector bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e)
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,

\mathbb{F}_{e}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e))

is called the
Hirzebruch surface
with invariant
e
. Let
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
denote the canonical projection map. Let
C_{e}
denote the image of a section of
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
C_{e}
is the
divisor associated to the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}(1)
. Also, let
f
denote a fiber of the map
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then we have

\text{Pic }\mathbb{F}_{e}=\mathbb{Z}.C_{e}\oplus\mathbb{Z}.f,

with the following intersection products

C_{e}^{2}=-e,\,f^{2}=0\text{ and }C_{e}\cdot f=1.

We are interested in the blow up of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
at finitely many distinct points. Let
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e,r}\to\mathbb{F}_{e}
denote the blow up of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
at
r
distinct points
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
. We know that

\text{Pic }\mathbb{F}_{e,r}=\mathbb{Z}.\pi^{\ast}(C_{e})\oplus\mathbb{Z}.\pi^{\ast}(f)\oplus\mathbb{Z}.E_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}.E_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}.E_{r},

where
H_{e}=\pi^{*}(C_{e})
,
F_{e}=\pi^{*}(f)
, and
E_{i}=\pi^{-1}(\{p_{i}\})
is the exceptional divisor corresponding to
p_{i}
. Moreover, in both
\mathbb{F}_{e}
and
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
, the numerical equivalence coincides with the linear equivalence. Let
K_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}
and
K_{\mathbb{F}_{e,r}}
denote the canonical line bundles of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
and

\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
, respectively. Then, we have

K_{\mathbb{F}_{e}}=-2C_{e}-(e+2)f

and

K_{\mathbb{F}_{e,r}}=-2H_{e}-(e+2)F_{e}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}.

By abuse of notation, we will use
H_{e}
and
F_{e}
to refer both the divisor classes and a specific curve linearly equivalent to these divisor classes.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
12
, Proposition 2.8]

Let
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
denote the Hirzebruch surface with invariant
e
. Then we have

h^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{e},aC_{e}+bf)=(a+1)\left(b+1-\frac{ae}{2}\right),

where
a\geq 0\text{ and }b\geq ae
.

The Nakai-Moishezon criterion provides a numerical criterion for determining the ampleness of a line bundle. Analogously, the following two theorems establish sufficient numerical conditions for global generation, very ampleness, and
k
-very ampleness.

Theorem 2.2

(Reider’s Criterion)
.

[
16
, Theorem 1]

Let
X
be a smooth surface and
N
be a line bundle on
X
. If
N^{2}\geq 5
and
K+N
is not globally generated, then there exists an effective divisor
D
such that any of the following holds:

(1)

N\cdot D=0
and
D^{2}=-1
,

(2)

N\cdot D=1
and
D^{2}=0
.

Also, if
N^{2}\geq 10
and
K+N
is not very ample, then there exists an effective divisor
D
such that any of the following holds:

(1)

N\cdot D=0
and
D^{2}=-1
or
-2
,

(2)

N\cdot D=1
and
D^{2}=0
or
-1
,

(3)

N\cdot D=2
and
D^{2}=0
.

The following theorem by Beltrametti, Francia, and Sommese, generalizes Reider’s Criterion by extending it to
k
-very ample for all
k\geq 0
.

Theorem 2.3

(Beltrametti-Francia-Sommese)
.

[
2
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
X
be a smooth surface and let
N
be a nef line bundle with
N^{2}\geq 4k+5
. Then
K+N
is not
k
-very ample if there exists an effective divisor
D
on
X
such that

N\cdot D-k-1\leq D^{2}\leq\frac{N\cdot D}{2}<k+1.

3.
Ampleness

In this section, we establish some criteria for determining the ampleness of a given line bundle. Recently, in
[
9
, Proposition 3.11]
, the authors have obtained a necessary and sufficient condition for ampleness when the number of blown up points is less than or equal to
e+1
. Here, we provide several sufficient conditions for ampleness independent of the number of blown up points.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
e>0
. Let
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
be
r
distinct points on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
such that for each
i
,
p_{i}\notin C_{e}
, and for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
with
i\neq j
,
p_{i}
and
p_{j}
are not on the same fiber of the map
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e,r}\to\mathbb{F}_{e}
be the blow up of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
at
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
. Let
L=aH_{e}+bF_{e}-m_{1}E_{1}-m_{2}E_{2}-\dots-m_{r}E_{r}
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
such that:

(1)

a>m_{i}>0

\text{for }1\leq i\leq r,

(2)

b>ae,

(3)

b>\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}.

Then
L
is ample.

Proof.

Using Nakai-Moishezon criteria for ampleness, it is enough to show that
L^{2}>0
and
L\cdot C>0
for all reduced irreducible curves on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
. First, we will show that
L^{2}>0
. Note that from
(1)
and
(3)
, we have:

(3.1)

ab>\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}.

Then, we have

\begin{split}L^{2}&=2ab-a^{2}e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}=ab+a(b-ae)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}\\
&>0\,(\text{by $\eqref{Eqnmini-1}$, \eqref{A1C1} and \eqref{A1C2}}).\end{split}

Now, we show that
L\cdot C>0
for any reduced irreducible curve
C
on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
.
Write

C=\alpha H_{e}+\beta F_{e}-n_{1}E_{1}-n_{2}E_{2}-\dots-n_{r}E_{r}.

Now if
\alpha=0
or
\beta=0
, from
[
11
, Corollary V.2.18]
we can see that
C
is a curve in the set
\{E_{i},H_{e},F_{e},F_{e}-E_{i}\}
for some
i=1,\ldots,r
. Hence, from (
1
) and (
2
), we can see
L\cdot C>0
.

Now suppose that
\alpha\neq 0,\beta\neq 0
. Then again from
[
11
, Corollary V.2.18]
, we have
\beta\geq\alpha e
. As
C
and
F_{e}-E_{i}
are distinct curves, using Bezoút’s theorem we have

\alpha\geq n_{i}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,r.

Hence, by (
3
) we have,

(3.2)

\alpha b>\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}.

Thus, we get

\begin{split}L\cdot C&=a\beta+\alpha b-a\alpha e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}=a(\beta-\alpha e)+(\alpha b-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i})>0\penalty\ (\text{by \eqref{Eqnmini}}).\end{split}

Hence
L
is ample.
∎

In
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
, the authors have obtained a bound for the sum of multiplicity of an irreducible and reduced curve at
r
distinct points when
r\leq e+1
. The following lemma provides a bound for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n\geq e
be an integer. Let
C=\alpha C_{e}+\beta f
be a reduced and irreducible curve on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
with
\alpha>1
or
\beta>n
. Then for any distinct points
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{2n-e+1}
in
\mathbb{F}_{e}
, we have

\beta+(n-e)\alpha\geq\sum_{i=1}^{2n-e+1}\text{mult}_{p_{i}}C.

Proof.

We have
h^{0}(C_{e}+nf)=2n-e+2
by Proposition
2.1
. So there exists a curve
D\in|C_{e}+nf|
passing through points
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{2n-e+1}
. Since
C
is reduced and irreducible,
C
and
D
can have a common component only if
C
is a component of
D
. But

D-C
can not be effective as
D-C\equiv(1-\alpha)C_{e}+(n-\beta)f
and either
1-\alpha
or
n-\beta
is negative by the assumption on
C
. Hence,
C
is not a component of
D
.

So, by Bezóut’s theorem, we have

C\cdot D\geq\sum_{i=1}^{2n-e+1}\text{mult}_{p_{i}}C\penalty\ \text{mult}_{p_{i}}D\geq\sum_{i=1}^{2n-e+1}\text{mult}_{p_{i}}C.

∎

We formulate the following sufficient conditions for ampleness.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
e>0
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e,r}\to\mathbb{F}_{e}
be the blow up of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
at very general points
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
. Let
\lambda\geq e
be the least integer such that
2\lambda-e+2>r
and let
k_{i}=2i-e+1
for all
e\leq i\leq\lambda
. Let
L=aH_{e}+bF_{e}-m_{1}E_{1}-m_{2}E_{2}-\dots-m_{r}E_{r}
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
such that:

(1)

a>m_{i}>0

\text{for }1\leq i\leq r,

(2)

b+(i-e)a>\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{any distinct }\\
k_{i}\text{ of }m_{j}\end{subarray}}m_{j}

\text{for }e\leq i\leq\lambda,

(3)

b>a\lambda.

Then
L
is ample.

Proof.

The strategy of the proof is the same as in the previous one, using the Nakai-Moishezon criterion.
Note that
(
1
)
and
(
2
)
together imply:

(3.3)

a(b+(i-e)a)>\sum_{i=1}^{k_{i}}m_{i}^{2}\penalty\ \text{for }e\leq i\leq\lambda.

Then,

\begin{split}L^{2}&=2ab-a^{2}e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}\\
&=a(b-a\lambda)+a(b+a(\lambda-e))-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}\\
&>0\penalty\ \penalty\ (\text{by }\eqref{A2C1},\eqref{A2C3}\text{ and }\eqref{EqnL2}).\end{split}

Now, let
C=\alpha H_{e}+\beta F_{e}-n_{1}E_{1}-n_{2}E_{2}-\dots-n_{r}E_{r}
be a reduced irreducible curve in
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
. It remains to prove
L\cdot C>0
.
We consider the following cases using
[
11
, Corollary V.2.18]
:

Case (i):
\alpha=\beta=0

Since
C
is a reduced and irreducible curve, we have
C=E_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq r
. Thus
L\cdot C=m_{i}>0
(by (
1
)).

Case (ii):
\alpha=1,\beta=0

As
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
are very general points and
e>0
, we know that
C_{e}
passes through none of these points. Hence in this case
C=H_{e}
. So,
L\cdot H_{e}=b-ae\geq b-a\lambda>0

using (
3
)
.

Case (iii):
\alpha=0,\beta=1

In this case,
C
is the strict transform of some fiber
f
in
\mathbb{F}_{e}
. As the blown up points are very general at most one point lies in a single fiber. Hence the curve
C
is either
F_{e}
or
F_{e}-E_{i}
for some
i
. In both cases, we have
L\cdot C>0
by (
1
).

Case (iv):
\alpha=1,e\leq\beta\leq\lambda

Note that
n_{i}=\text{mult}_{p_{i}}\pi(C)
and using Bezóut’s theorem we can see that
n_{i}\leq\alpha=1
. By Proposition
2.1
, we have
h^{0}(C_{e}+\beta f)=2\beta-e+2
. Since
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
are very general points, at most
2\beta-e+1
points of
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
can lie on
\pi(C)
. i.e., at most
k_{\beta}=(2\beta-e+1)
of
n_{i}
are 1 and all others are zero. Hence by (
2
) and the fact
n_{i}=1
for at most
2\beta-e+1
number of
i
, we have

(3.4)

\alpha b+\alpha(\beta-e)a=b+(\beta-e)a>\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}.

Now we have,

\begin{split}L\cdot C&=a\beta+\alpha b-a\alpha e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}=a\beta+b-ae-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}>0\text{ (by \eqref{Eqn2})}.\end{split}

Case (v):

\alpha>1
or
\beta>\lambda

By Lemma
3.2
, we have

\beta+\alpha(\lambda-e)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}.

Combining this together with
(1)
, we have the following:

(3.5)

a(\beta+\alpha(\lambda-e))\geq a\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}\right)>\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}.

Now, we get

\begin{split}L\cdot C&=a\beta+\alpha b-a\alpha e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}\\
&=a(\beta+\alpha(\lambda-e))+\alpha(b-a\lambda)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}\\
&>\alpha(b-a\lambda)\,\text{(by \eqref{Eqn3})}\\
&>0\text{ (by \eqref{A2C3})}.\end{split}

Hence
L
is ample.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

From the above proof we can see that conditions (
1
) and (
2
) are necessary for ampleness.

The following theorem is motivated from
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
, where the author has obtained criterion for ampleness of line bundles on the blow up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose
e>0
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e,r}\to\mathbb{F}_{e}
be the blow up of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
at very general points
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
, where
r\geq 3
. Let
L=aH_{e}+bF_{e}-m_{1}E_{1}-m_{2}E_{2}-\dots-m_{r}E_{r}
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
such that:

(1)

a>m_{i}>0

\forall 1\leq i\leq r,

(2)

b>ae,

(3)

\displaystyle b^{2}-e(2ab-a^{2}e)\geq\frac{3}{s+2}\sum_{j=1}^{s}m_{i_{j}}^{2}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\penalty\ 2\leq s\leq r,

(4)

\displaystyle 2ab-a^{2}e>\frac{s+3}{s+2}\sum_{j=1}^{s}m_{i_{j}}^{2}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\penalty\ 2\leq s\leq r.

Then
L
is ample.

Proof.

Since
\frac{r+3}{r+2}>1
, using (
4
) we have,

L^{2}=2ab-a^{2}e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}>0.

Now, we prove that
L\cdot C>0
for all reduced irreducible curves on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
considering the following cases.
If
C=E_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq r
, then clearly
L\cdot C=m_{i}>0
.
Now suppose that
C\neq E_{i}
, then
C
is the strict transform of the curve
\pi(C)
. Let
n_{i}=\text{mult}_{p_{i}}\pi(C)
, so we can write
C=\alpha H_{e}+\beta F_{e}-n_{1}E_{1}-n_{2}E_{2}-\dots-n_{r}E_{r}
.

Case (i):

n_{i}\geq 2
for some
i
.

It is easy to see that,

(3.6)

(a\beta+b\alpha-a\alpha e)^{2}=(2ab-a^{2}e)(2\alpha\beta-\alpha^{2}e)+(a\beta-b\alpha)^{2}\geq(2ab-a^{2}e)(2\alpha\beta-\alpha^{2}e).

Let
n=\min\{n_{i}|n_{i}\neq 0,1\leq i\leq r\}
and
s
denote the cardinality of the set
\{n_{i}|n_{i}\neq 0,1\leq i\leq r\}
. Then by
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have

2\alpha\beta-\alpha^{2}e\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}-n.

Combining this inequality with (
4
) and (
3.6
), we have

(a\beta+b\alpha-a\alpha e)^{2}\geq(2ab-a^{2}e)(2\alpha\beta-\alpha^{2}e)>\left(\frac{s+3}{s+2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}-n\right).

Using
[
6
, Lemma 2.3]
, we can see that (as
r\geq 3
):

(a\beta+b\alpha-a\alpha e)^{2}>\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}\right)^{2}.

Thus
L\cdot C>0
in this case.

Case (ii):

\alpha\neq 0,\beta\neq 0
and
C=\alpha H_{e}+\beta F_{e}-E_{i_{1}}-E_{i_{2}}-\dots-E_{i_{s}}
for some
1\leq s\leq r
.

As earlier by
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have
2\alpha\beta-\alpha^{2}e\geq s-1
.
Also, note that

(3.7)

\begin{split}(\alpha b-a\beta)^{2}&=\alpha^{2}b^{2}-2ab\alpha\beta+a^{2}\beta^{2}\\
&=\alpha^{2}b^{2}+a\beta(a\beta-2b\alpha)\\
&\geq\alpha^{2}b^{2}+a\alpha e(a\alpha e-2b\alpha)\text{ (using $\beta\geq\alpha e$)}\\
&\geq\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{3}{s+2}\sum_{j=1}m_{i_{j}}^{2}\right)\text{ (by \eqref{A3C3})}\\
&\geq\frac{3}{s+2}\sum_{j=1}m_{i_{j}}^{2}.\end{split}

Now,

(3.8)

\begin{split}(a\beta+b\alpha-a\alpha e)^{2}&=(2ab-a^{2}e)(2\alpha\beta-\alpha^{2}e)+(a\beta-b\alpha)^{2}\text{ (by \eqref{Eqn4})}\\
&>(s-1)\frac{s+3}{s+2}\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}^{2}+(\alpha b-a\beta)^{2}\text{ (by \eqref{A3C4})}\\
&\geq(s-1)\left(1+\frac{1}{s+2}\right)\sum_{i=j}^{s}m_{i_{j}}^{2}+\frac{3}{s+2}\sum_{j=1}m_{i_{j}}^{2}\text{ (by \eqref{Eqn-1})}\\
&=s\sum_{i=j}^{s}m_{i_{j}}^{2}+\left(\frac{s-1}{s+2}-1\right)\sum_{i=j}^{s}m_{i_{j}}^{2}+\frac{3}{s+2}\sum_{j=1}m_{i_{j}}^{2}\\
&=s\sum_{i=j}^{s}m_{i_{j}}^{2}\\
&\geq\left(\sum_{i=j}^{s}m_{i_{j}}\right)^{2}\text{ (by Cauchy-Schwarz's inequality).}\end{split}

Hence
L\cdot C>0
in this case.

The remaining cases are the following (using
[
11
, Corollary V.2.18]
)

•

\alpha=1,\beta=0
,

•

\alpha=0,\beta=1
,

•

\alpha=\beta=0
.

In these cases, we can show
L\cdot C>0
, similar to the earlier cases using (
1
) and (
2
). Thus we get that the line bundle
L
is ample.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

Note that for a line bundle
L
on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
with
L^{2}>0
, we get that
L\cdot C>0
for all curves
C
with
C^{2}\geq 0
using (
3.6
). Hence, if a line bundle
L
with
L^{2}>0
is not ample, there exists a curve
C
with
C^{2}<0
such that
L\cdot C<0
.

The following example shows that the set of line bundles satisfying Theorems
3.1
,
3.3
and
3.5
are different.

Example 3.7
.

Consider the surface
\mathbb{F}_{10,12}
. Note that the line bundle
L=33H_{e}+331F_{e}-32E_{1}-32E_{2}-\dots-32E_{10}-E_{11}-E_{12}
is ample using Theorem
3.1
. But
L
does not satisfy the conditions in Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.5
.

Similarly,
32H_{e}+353F_{e}-31E_{1}-\dots-31E_{12}
is an ample line bundle by Theorem
3.3
, which does not satisfy the conditions of the other two theorems.

Finally, consider the the line bundle
L:=2H_{E}+6F_{e}-E_{1}-E_{2}-\dots-E_{10}
on
\mathbb{F}_{2,10}
, which is ample by Theorem
3.5
. Note that
L
does not satisfy the conditions in either Theorem
3.1
or Theorem
3.3
.

Application to giving bounds on multi-point Seshadri constant

The multi-point Seshadri constant of an ample line bundle
l
on a smooth projective variety
X
at the points
p_{1},p_{2}\ldots,p_{r}
is the real
number

\varepsilon(X,l,p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}):=\sup\left\{m\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,:\,\pi^{*}l-m\,\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}\text{ is ample }\right\},

where
\pi:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
is the blow u of
X
at
p_{1},p_{2}\ldots,p_{r}\in X
(see
[
1
, Definition 1.5 and 1.8]
).

Let
l=aC_{e}+bf
be an ample line bundle on the Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{e}
. Suppose
e>0
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e,r}\to\mathbb{F}_{e}
be the blow up of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
at
r
very general points
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
. Then, by Theorem
3.5
, we see that
\pi^{*}l-m\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}
is ample whenever

\displaystyle m<\sqrt{\frac{r+2}{r+3}}\,\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}}{r}},

\displaystyle m<\sqrt{\frac{r+2}{3r}}\sqrt{[b^{2}-e\,l^{2}]}\text{ and}

\displaystyle m<a.

Thus, we have the following lower-bound

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\mathbb{F}_{e},l,p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})\geq\min\left\{\sqrt{\frac{r+2}{r+3}}\,\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}}{r}},\sqrt{\frac{r+2}{3r}}\sqrt{[b^{2}-e\,l^{2}]},a\right\}.

Note that this recovers
[
8
, Theorem 3.8]
for the Hirzebruch surfaces.

Similarly, using Theorem
3.1
, we get that

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\mathbb{F}_{e},l,p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})\geq\min\left\{\frac{b}{r},a\right\},

where
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
are
r
distinct points on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
such that for each
i
,
p_{i}\notin C_{e}
, and for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
with
i\neq j
,
p_{i}
and
p_{j}
are not on the same fiber of the map
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Using Theorem
3.3
, we get the following lower-bound. Let
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r}
be very general points on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
. Let
\lambda\geq e
be the least integer such that
2\lambda-e+2>r
and let
k_{i}=2i-e+1
for all
e\leq i\leq\lambda
. Let
l=aC_{0}+bf
be an ample line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
satisfying
b>a\lambda
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\mathbb{F}_{e},l,p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})\geq\min\left\{\min_{e\leq i\leq\lambda}\frac{b+(i-e)a}{k_{i}},a\right\}.

4.
Global Generation

In this section, we use the conditions for ampleness proved in Section
3
and Reider’s criterion (see Theorem
2.2
) to get sufficient conditions for the global generation of a line bundle.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
e>0
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e,r}\to\mathbb{F}_{e}
be the blow up of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
at
r
distinct points
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
such that for each
i
,
p_{i}\notin C_{e}
, and for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
with
i\neq j
,
p_{i}
and
p_{j}
are not on the same fiber of the map
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let
L=aH_{e}+bF_{e}-m_{1}E_{1}-m_{2}E_{2}-\dots-m_{r}E_{r}
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
such that:

(1)

a+2>m_{i}+1>0

\text{for }1\leq i\leq r,

(2)

b+e+2>(a+2)e,

(3)

b+e+2>\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1).

Then
L
is globally generated.

Proof.

First note that we have the following.

a)

\eqref{G1C1}\implies a\geq m_{i}\geq 0,

b)

\eqref{G1C2}\implies a(b+e+2)=ab+ae+2a\geq a(a+2)e=a^{2}e+2ae,

c)

\displaystyle\eqref{G1C3}\implies a(b+e+2)\geq a\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}+r+1\right)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}+ra+a
(by (a)).

Let
N=(a+2)H_{e}+(b+e+2)F_{e}-(m_{1}+1)E_{1}-\dots-(m_{r}+1)E_{r}
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
. Then, by the assumption on
L
and by Theorem
3.1
,
N
is ample. Note that
L=K_{\mathbb{F}_{e,r}}+N
.
Now,

(4.1)

\begin{split}N^{2}&=2(a+2)(b+e+2)-(a+2)^{2}e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)^{2}\\
&=(ab+ae+2a)+(ab+ae+2a)+(2b+2e+4)+(2b+2e+4)\\
&-a^{2}e-4ae-4e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}-r\\
&\geq ra+a-2ae-2e+2b+{4}+r\text{ (by (a), (b), (c) and \eqref{G1C3})}\\
&=2(b-ae-e+2)+(r+1)a+r\\
&{\geq r+4\geq 5}\text{ (by \eqref{G1C2} we have $b-ae-e+2\geq 1$)}.\end{split}

So by Reider’s criterion, if
L
fails to be globally generated, then there is an effective divisor
D
with
D^{2}=-1
and
N\cdot D=0
or
D^{2}=0
and
N\cdot D=1
. But the first case cannot occur as
N
is ample.

Suppose that there exists such a
D
with
D^{2}=0
and
N\cdot D=1
. Since
N
is ample,
N\cdot D=1
implies that
D
is a reduced irreducible curve. We will show that
N\cdot D>1
for any reduced irreducible curve
D
with
D^{2}=0
.
Write
D=\alpha H_{e}+\beta F_{e}-n_{1}E_{1}-\dots-n_{r}E_{r}
.
Note that if
\alpha\neq 0
, we have
\alpha\geq n_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. So when
\alpha\neq 0
, by (
3
) we have

(4.2)

\alpha(b+e+2)\geq\alpha\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)+1\right)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}+\alpha.

Case (i):

\alpha\neq 0
,
\alpha>1
or
\beta>\alpha e

\begin{split}\alpha(b+e+2)+\beta(a+2)-\alpha(a+2)e&\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}+\alpha+(\beta-\alpha e)(a+2)\text{ by \eqref{thm4.1eq1}}\\
&\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}+2\text{ (since $\alpha\geq 2$ or $\beta-\alpha e\geq 1,a>0$)}.\end{split}

Hence
N\cdot D\geq{2}
.

Case (ii):

\alpha=1
and
\beta=e
.

Since
D^{2}=0
, we have
D=H_{e}+eF_{e}-E_{i_{1}}-\dots-E_{i_{e}}
.
Then

N\cdot D=b+e+2-\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}(m_{i}+1)=b+e+2-\sum_{j=1}^{e}(m_{i_{j}}+1).

By (
1
), we have
a\geq m_{i}
, and (
2
) gives

\displaystyle b+e+2\geq(a+2)e+{1}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{e}(m_{i_{j}}+2)+1=\sum_{j=1}^{e}(m_{i_{j}}+1)+e+1.

Hence, we have

N\cdot D\geq{2}\text{ (since $e\geq 1$)}.

Case (iii):

\alpha=1
and
\beta=0
.

Here
D=H_{e}
, but
D^{2}=0
suggests
e=0
. But we are not considering
e=0
case.

Case (iv):

\alpha=0
and
\beta=1

Since
D^{2}=0
, we have
D=F_{e}
. Then
N\cdot D=a+2\geq 2
.

Case (v):

\alpha=\beta=0

In this case, as
D
is reduced and irreducible,
D=E_{i}
for some
i
, but
D^{2}=-1
.
So there is no curve
D
with
D^{2}=0
and
N\cdot D=1
. Hence, using Reider’s criterion, we conclude that
L
is globally generated.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
e>0
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e,r}\to\mathbb{F}_{e}
be the blow up of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
at very general points
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
. Let
\lambda\geq e
be the least integer such that
2\lambda-e+2>r
. Also, let
k_{i}=2i-e+1
for all
e\leq i\leq\lambda
. Let
L=aH_{e}+bF_{e}-m_{1}E_{1}-m_{2}E_{2}-\dots-m_{r}E_{r}
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
such that:

(1)

a+2>m_{i}+1>0

\text{for }1\leq i\leq r,

(2)

b+e+2+(i-e)(a+2)>\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{any distinct }\\
k_{i}\text{ of }m_{j}\end{subarray}}(m_{j}+1)+1

\text{for }e\leq i\leq\lambda
,

(3)

b+e+2\geq(a+2)\lambda+1
.

Then
L
is globally generated.

Proof.

The strategy of the proof is similar to the proof of the previous theorem. As before, let
N=(a+2)H_{e}+(b+e+2)F_{e}-(m_{1}+1)E_{1}-\dots-(m_{r}+1)E_{r}
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
. Then by assumption on
L
and by Theorem
3.3
,
N
is an ample line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
. Also note that
L=K_{\mathbb{F}_{e,r}}+N
.

Note that from
(1),(2)
and
(3)
above we have:

\displaystyle a\geq m_{i},

\displaystyle b+e+2\geq(a+2)\lambda+1,

\displaystyle b+e+2+(\lambda-e)(a+2)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}+r+2.

(4.3)

\begin{split}N^{2}&=2(a+2)(b+e+2)-(a+2)^{2}e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)^{2}\\
&=(ab+ae+2a)+(ab+ae+2a)+(2b+2e+4)+(2b+2e+4)\\
&-a^{2}e-4ae-4e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}-r\\
&\geq(a^{2}\lambda+2a\lambda+a)+(\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}+ra+2a-(\lambda-e)(a^{2}+2a))+(2a\lambda+4\lambda+2)\\
&+(2\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}+2r+4-(\lambda-e)(2a+4))-a^{2}e-4ae-4e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}-r\\
&=3a+ra+a^{2}e+2ae+4\lambda+6+r+2ae-4\lambda+4e-a^{2}e-4ae-4e\\
&\geq r+6\geq 5.\end{split}

Since
N
is ample, using Reider’s criterion, if
L
fails to be globally generated, then there exists an effective divisor
D
with
D^{2}=0
and
N\cdot D=1
.

To finish the proof we will show that
N\cdot D>1
for any reduced irreducible curve
D
with
D^{2}=0
.
We consider the following cases:

Case (i):

\alpha=\beta=0

Here
D=E_{i}
, but
D^{2}=-1\neq 0.

Case (ii):

\alpha=0
and
\beta=1

Similarly, since
D^{2}=0
, we have
D=F_{e}
. Then
N\cdot D=a+2>1.

Now we can assume
\alpha\neq 0
and the remaining cases are the following.

Case (iii):

\alpha=1
and
\beta=0

This case is not under consideration with the same reason in the proof of Theorem
4.1
as
e>0
.

Case (iv):

\alpha=1
and
e\leq\beta\leq\lambda

\alpha=1
gives us
0\leq n_{i}\leq 1
. Thus,
D=H_{e}+\beta F_{e}-E_{i_{1}}-\dots-E_{i_{s}}
. Since blown up points are very general we have
s<2\beta-e+2=h^{0}(\mathbb{F}_{e},C_{e}+\beta f)
.

Hence, we have

\begin{split}N\cdot D&=(a+2)\beta+b+e+2-(a+2)e-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(m_{i_{j}}+1)\\
&=b+e+2+(\beta-e)(a+2)-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(m_{i_{j}}+1)\\
&>1\text{ (by \eqref{G2C2})}.\\
\end{split}

Case (v):

\alpha>1
or
\beta>\lambda

By Lemma
3.2
, we have
\displaystyle\beta+(\lambda-e)\alpha\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
. Since
a+2>m_{i}+1
we have,

(4.4)

(a+2)(\beta+(\lambda-e)\alpha)>\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}.

Hence,

\begin{split}N\cdot D&=\alpha(b+e+2)+\beta(a+2)-\alpha(a+2)e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}\\
&=(a+2)(\beta+(\lambda-e)\alpha)-(a+2)\alpha\lambda+\alpha(b+e+2)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}\\
&>\alpha(b+e+2-(a+2)\lambda)\text{ (by \eqref{eqnn})}\\
&\geq 1\text{ (by \eqref{G2C3} and }\alpha\neq 0).\end{split}

Hence, using Reider’s criterion we see that
L
is globally generated.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
e>0
. Let
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{e}
be very general points with
r\geq 3
. Let
L=a\,H_{e}+b\,F_{e}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}E_{i}
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
such that

(1)

a+2>m_{i}+1>2
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
,

(2)

b+e+2>(a+2)e
,

(3)

\displaystyle(b+e+2)^{2}-e[2(a+2)(b+e+2)-(a+2)^{2}e]\geq\frac{3}{s+2}\sum_{j=1}^{s}(m_{i_{j}}+1)^{2}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\penalty\ 2\leq s\leq r,

(4)

\displaystyle 2(a+2)(b+e+2)-(a+2)^{2}e>\frac{s+3}{s+2}\sum_{i=1}^{s}(m_{i_{j}}+1)^{2}
,
\forall 2\leq s\leq r
.

Then
L
is globally generated.

Proof.

Let
N=(a+2)H_{e}+(b+e+2)F_{e}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\,(m_{i}+1)E_{i}
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
. Then by the assumption on
L
and Theorem
3.5
,
N
is ample.

Note that

\begin{split}N^{2}&=2(a+2)(b+e+2)-(a+2)^{2}e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\,(m_{i}+1)^{2}\\
&>\frac{1}{r+2}\,\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)^{2}\,(\text{using \eqref{G3C4} for }s=r),\\
&\geq 5\,\text{as }r\geq 3\text{ and }m_{i}\geq 2\text{ (cf. \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Han}{}{}, Proof of Theorem 3.1]})}.\end{split}

Then using Reider’s criterion, it suffices to show that there is no reduced irreducible curve
D
with
D^{2}=0
and
D\cdot N=1
. Let
D=\alpha H_{e}+\beta F_{e}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}E_{i}
. Then, we have

(4.5)

D^{2}=-\alpha^{2}e+2\alpha\beta-\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}=0,

and

D\cdot N=\alpha(b+e+2)-\alpha(a+2)e+(a+2)\beta-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}.

Using (
3.6
), we have

\begin{split}\left(D\cdot N+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}\right)^{2}&\geq[2(a+2)(b+e+2)-(a+2)^{2}e](2\alpha\beta-\alpha^{2}e)\,\\
&\geq\frac{r+3}{r+2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}\right)\,(\text{by \eqref{G3C4} and \eqref{GG3e2}})\\
&\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}+1\right)^{2}\,(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Han}{}{}, Proof of Theorem 3.1]}}).\end{split}

Thus, we have
N\cdot D>1
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
L
is globally generated.
∎

5.
Very Ampleness

In this section, we provide sufficient conditions to check whether a given line bundle is very ample. Here, we again use the conditions for ampleness that we developed in Section
3
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
e>0
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{F}_{e,r}\to\mathbb{F}_{e}
be the blow up of
\mathbb{F}_{e}
at very general points
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
. Let
L=aH_{e}+bF_{e}-m_{1}E_{1}-m_{2}E_{2}-\dots-m_{r}E_{r}
be a line bundle on
\mathbb{F}_{e,r}
such that:

(1)

a+2>m_{i}+2>2

\text{for }1\leq i\leq r,

(2)

b+e+2>(a+2)e+1,

(3)

b+e+2>\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)+2.

Then
L
is very ample.

Proof.

Let

N=(a+2)H_{e}+(b+e+2)F_{e}-(m_{1}+1)E_{1}-\dots-(m_{r}+1)E_{r},

so that
L=K_{\mathbb{F}_{e,r}}+N
and
N
is ample using Theorem
3.1
.
From the assumptions, we have

(5.1)

\begin{split}&(a+2)(b+e+2)\geq(m_{i}+3)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)+3\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)+3(m_{i}+3),\\
&(a+2)(b+e+2)\geq(a+2)^{2}e+(a+2).\end{split}

Using (
5.1
), we have

\begin{split}N^{2}&=2(a+2)(b+e+2)-(a+2)^{2}e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)^{2}\\
&\geq(a+2)(b+e+2)-(a+2)^{2}e+2\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)+3(m_{i}+3)\\
&\geq(a+2)+2\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)+3(m_{i}+3)\\
&\geq 10.\end{split}

Now applying Reider’s criterion, we get that if
L
is not very ample, then there exists an effective divisor
D
such that one of the following holds:

(a)

N\cdot D=1
,
D^{2}=0
or
-1
,

(b)

N\cdot D=2
,
D^{2}=0
.

The case
N\cdot D=1
,
D^{2}=0
, has already been addressed in the proof of Theorem
4.1
. If
N\cdot D=1
, it is clear that
D
is an irreducible reduced curve, since
N
is ample. When
N\cdot D=2
,
D
is either a reduced irreducible curve or a sum of exactly two reduced, irreducible curves
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, with
N\cdot D_{1}=N\cdot D_{2}=1\text{ and }(D_{1}+D_{2})^{2}=0
.
Let
D=\alpha H_{e}+\beta F_{e}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}E_{i}
.

Note that
(
1
)
gives
a\geq 1
and
a>m_{i}
.
We consider the following cases.

Case (i):

\alpha\neq 0
,
\alpha>1
or
\beta>\alpha e

Similar to the case (i) in the proof of the Theorem
4.1
, we get
N\cdot D>3
.

Case (ii):

\alpha=1
and
\beta=e
.

Here
D=H_{e}+eF_{e}-E_{i_{1}}-\dots-E_{i_{s}}
. Since blown up points are very general and
h^{0}(C_{e}+ef)=e+2
, we have
s\leq e+1
. Then, we have

N\cdot D=b+e+2-\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}(m_{i}+1)\geq b+e+2-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(m_{i_{j}}+1)>2\text{ (by \eqref{V1C3})}.

Case (iii):

\alpha=1
and
\beta=0

Then, we have
D=H_{e}
, so
D^{2}=-e
. Hence, we get

N\cdot D=b+e+2-(a+2)e>1\text{ by \eqref{V1C2}.}

Case (iv):

\alpha=0
and
\beta=1

Then
D=F_{e}
or
D=F_{e}-E_{i}
for some
i
. If
D=F_{e}
, we get

N\cdot D=a+2>2.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
r\geq 4,m_{1},\dots,m_{r},t\geq 1
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}>0
. Suppose that
n_{i}\geq 2
for some
1\leq i\leq r
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}\geq t
. Let
n=\min\{m_{i}|1\leq i\leq r\}
. Then, the following inequality holds

\left(\frac{r+3}{r+2}+t\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}-n\right)>\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}n_{i})+t\right)^{2}.

Proof.

By
[
6
, Lemma 2.3]
we have,

\left(\frac{r+3}{r+2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}-n\right)\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}\right)^{2}.

Now to prove the desired inequality, it is enough to show that

t\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}-n\right)>2t\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}+t^{2}.

Equivalently,

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}-n\right)>2\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}+t.

For the notational convenience, let
\displaystyle A=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2},B=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}^{2}\text{ and }C=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}
. Thus, we need to show:

AB-t>2C+An.

By the assumption, we have
A\geq t
.
So,
AB-t=A(B-1)+A-t\geq A(B-1)
. Now, it is enough to show that
A(B-1)=AB-A>2C+An
. Equivalently, we need to prove that

. Consider the following cases.

Case (i):

A>C

Note that
B-n-1\geq 2
as
r\geq 2
and
n_{i}\geq 2
for some
i
. Hence, the required inequality follows.
Case (ii):

A\leq C

Note that, we have

A(B-n-1)=AB-A(n+1)\geq C^{2}-C(n+1)\,(\text{as $AB\geq C^{2}$}).

So it is enough to prove:

C^{2}-C(n+1)>2C.

Equivalently,

C>n+3\Leftrightarrow\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}n_{i}>n+3.

This is true as

∎

-very ample.

is not
k
-very ample. Then by Theorem
2.3
, there exists an effective divisor

does not exist by considering the following cases:

is a reduced irreducible curve,

\alpha=0

&=b+e+2+(\beta-e)(a+2)-\sum_{j=1}^{s}(m_{i_{j}}+1)\\
&>2k+1\text{ (by \eqref{K2C2})}.\\
\end{split}

Case (v):

\alpha>1
or
\beta>\lambda

Then, by Lemma
3.2
we have
\beta+(\lambda-e)\alpha\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
. Hence,

(6.3)

(a+2)(\beta+(\lambda-e)\alpha)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}.

Now, we have

\begin{split}N\cdot D&=\alpha(b+e+2)+\beta(a+2)-\alpha(a+2)e-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}\\
&=(a+2)(\beta+(\lambda-e)\alpha)-(a+2)\alpha\lambda+\alpha(b+e+2)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(m_{i}+1)n_{i}\\
&\geq\alpha(b+e+2-(a+2)\lambda)\text{ (using \eqref{Eqn-5})}\\
&>a+2>2k+1\text{ using \eqref{K2C3}}.\end{split}

With the same arguments as in the last proof, the line bundle
L
is
k
-very ample.
∎

Theorem 6.3

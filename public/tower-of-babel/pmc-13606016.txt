Optimized Endoscopic Hemostasis in Spontaneous Intracerebral Hemorrhage: A Pilot Study of the “Chopsticks” Technique
Authors: Shuang Liu, Zijiang Yang, Shengyang Su, Shikui Cao, Xiaobiao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES:Endoscopic evacuation for spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage can limit approach-related injury, but hemostasis and visualization within a narrow corridor remain challenging. We evaluated whether a single-surgeon “chopsticks” configuration was associated with improved operative efficiency and early neurological recovery compared with a conventional 2-surgeon, 3-handed technique.METHODS:We performed a single-center retrospective cohort study of supratentorial spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage treated with endoport-assisted endoscopic evacuation (control, n = 25; chopsticks, n = 20) after excluding structured training-phase cases. Primary outcomes were operative time and estimated blood loss. Early neurological recovery (secondary outcome) was assessed using the Full Outline of UnResponsiveness (FOUR) score on postoperative day 3 (D3) and day 7 (D7); ΔFOUR was defined as FOUR_D7 − FOUR_D3. Other outcomes were exploratory.RESULTS:Baseline characteristics were similar between groups. Operative time was shorter with the chopsticks configuration (118.3 ± 14.8 vs 147.3 ± 52.9 minutes; P = .014), and estimated blood loss was lower (100.5 ± 30.1 vs 184.0 ± 117.1 mL; P = .002). Radiographic evacuation efficacy was high in both groups with similar evacuation rate and residual hematoma volume. FOUR improved more from D3 to D7 in the chopsticks group (median [IQR] 3.0 [2.0-3.2] vs 2.0 [1.0-3.0]; P = .005). In an intracerebral hemorrhage score-adjusted linear mixed-effects model, the group × time interaction was significant (β = 1.01; 95% CI 0.42-1.60; P = .001).CONCLUSION:In this pilot cohort, the chopsticks configuration was associated with improved operative efficiency and a steeper early neurological recovery trajectory without differences in gross evacuation extent. Prospective validation is warranted.

Spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (SICH) remains among the most devastating forms of stroke, characterized by abrupt vessel rupture, hematoma formation, and secondary brain injury.1,2 Despite advances in neurocritical care and neuroimaging, no treatment consistently improves long-term outcomes, highlighting the need to refine surgical management.3,4 Early randomized trials of craniotomy evacuation did not demonstrate net benefit and, in some series, were associated with worse outcomes, raising concern that operative tissue disruption may offset the potential benefit of hematoma removal.5

Endoscopic evacuation has emerged as a minimally invasive alternative to craniotomy, offering smaller incisions, reduced disruption of surrounding brain tissue, and improved visualization of the hematoma cavity.6,7 However, reliable hemostasis within a narrow endoscopic corridor remains challenging. Active bleeding can rapidly degrade visualization, prompting repeated irrigation and instrument exchanges that prolong operative exposure and increase manipulation and thermal coagulation of perihematomal tissue.

The “chopsticks” technique, initially described in functional endoscopic sinus surgery and later adapted for endoscopic endonasal neurosurgery,8,9 uses a two-handed configuration in which the nondominant hand co-controls the endoscope and suction, whereas the dominant hand operates the working instrument (typically bipolar forceps). Subtle suction-tip adjustments permit dynamic camera angulation and view-guided hemostasis, potentially reducing instrument conflict and unnecessary tissue manipulation. Although used in transnasal procedures,10 this approach has not been systematically evaluated for endoport-assisted evacuation of supratentorial SICH.

In this pilot retrospective cohort study, we evaluated the feasibility and safety of the chopsticks configuration for endoscopic evacuation of supratentorial SICH and examined its association with operative efficiency and early neurological recovery. Longer-term functional outcomes were considered exploratory. We also characterized the learning curve to inform implementation and to mitigate learning-curve bias in comparative analyses.

METHODS

Ethical Considerations

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of People's Hospital of Jinping Miao, Yao and Dai Autonomous County (Approval No. 2023-LL-0015) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its amendments. All surgical procedures were performed with the written informed consent of the patient or their legal representative. Data were deidentified before analysis.

Study Design

We conducted a single-center, retrospective cohort study at People's Hospital of Jinping Miao, Yao and Dai Autonomous County, comparing the chopsticks configuration with a conventional 2-surgeon, 3-handed technique during endoscopic evacuation of supratentorial SICH. Patients treated between July 1, 2022, and December 31, 2022, comprised the control cohort and underwent endoscopic evacuation using the standard 2-surgeon technique. Patients treated between July 1, 2023, and December 31, 2023, comprised the chopsticks cohort and underwent surgery after the operating surgeon had achieved proficiency with the chopsticks configuration.

To mitigate learning-curve bias, cases performed during a structured training period (January 2023-June 2023; n = 18) were used for skill acquisition and excluded from the comparative cohort. Cases requiring intraoperative conversion from the chopsticks configuration to the conventional method were retained for safety and outcome analyses and were excluded only from the primary learning-curve assessment.

Patient Selection

Eligible patients met all of the following criteria: (1) age 30 to 70 years; (2) supratentorial SICH confirmed by preoperative computed tomography (CT) and computed tomography angiography; (3) hematoma volume ≥30 mL calculated using an approximate method for calculating blood clot volume using the formula (A × B × C)/2; (4) preoperative Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score ≥6; (5) symptom onset within 24 hours before surgery; and (6) intraoperative identification of at least 1 active bleeding point. Exclusion criteria included coagulopathy or underlying vascular malformations, prior cranial surgery, secondary ICH due to tumor or trauma, or incomplete clinical or imaging data.

Surgical Procedure and Hemostatic Techniques

All procedures were performed by a senior neurosurgeon following institutional indications and operative steps described previously 11. The cortical entry point and trajectory were selected according to hematoma location and size. A cylindrical endoport system (LW171107, 17 × 11 mm inner diameter, 7-cm length; Jilin Laiwo Medical Technology Co., Ltd.) was used to establish a minimally disruptive transsulcal corridor. Visualization was provided by a 4-mm 0° rigid endoscope (ShenDa Endoscopy Co., Ltd.).

Chopsticks Configuration (chopsticks cohort)

Within the endoport, the surgeon's nondominant hand simultaneously grasped and controlled the endoscope and a straight suction device, analogous to handling chopsticks (Figure 1A and 1B). The dominant hand operated bipolar forceps. Fine suction-tip adjustments were used to reposition the visual axis and expose bleeding sources while maintaining continuous suction and stable endoscope support. Hemostasis was obtained with low-power bipolar coagulation, followed by careful clot dissection and evacuation.

Conventional 3-Handed Technique (control cohort)

In the control cohort, an assistant managed the endoscope while the primary surgeon operated the suction device and bipolar forceps (Figure 1C). Endoscope positioning and maintenance of the operative field depended on assistant performance.

Data Collection and Outcomes

Demographic, clinical, and imaging variables were extracted from the electronic medical record. Hematoma volume was calculated on preoperative CT using an approximate method for calculating blood clot volume using the formula (A × B × C)/2. Intraoperative variables included operative time (skin incision to skin closure) and estimated blood loss (EBL). EBL was obtained from the anesthesia record using standard institutional calculations (suction canister volume minus irrigation volume plus gravimetric estimates from weighed gauze, when applicable), rather than surgeon self-report. Postoperative variables included neurosurgical intensive care unit (NSICU) length of stay (LOS), total hospital LOS, and perioperative complications.

Primary outcomes were operative time and intraoperative EBL. Early neurological recovery was the secondary outcome and was assessed using the Full Outline of UnResponsiveness (FOUR) score (range 0-16; Eye, Motor, Brainstem reflexes, and Respiration), on postoperative day 3 (D3) and day 7 (D7); ΔFOUR was defined as FOUR_D7 − FOUR_D3.11,12 FOUR scores were abstracted from routine neurocritical care examinations documented before study data abstraction by assessors not involved in the surgical procedure. When multiple values were available, the value closest to the target day was used.

Exploratory outcomes included NSICU LOS, total hospital LOS, perioperative complications, 30-day all-cause mortality, and 90-day functional independence (Glasgow Outcome Scale score ≥4). For contextualization, expected 30-day mortality and expected 90-day functional independence were estimated using validated risk stratification tools: the ICH score13 and the Functional Outcome in Patients With Primary Intracerebral Hemorrhage score,14 respectively, and reported descriptively.

Statistical Analysis and Bias Migration

Continuous variables are reported as mean ± SD or median (IQR) and categorical variables as counts and percentages. Between-group comparisons used the Student t-test or Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables and the χ2 or Fisher exact test for categorical variables. All tests were 2-sided, and P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

Learning curve performance was evaluated using cumulative sum analysis of operative time to identify the proficiency inflection point. To reduce confounding, cohorts were compared at the same institution during the same prespecified calendar window in consecutive years, and cases performed during the interim training period were excluded from comparative analyses (n = 18). Given the pilot sample size, adjusted analyses were prespecified to be parsimonious to limit overfitting, using score-based covariates (ICH score) rather than models with multiple individual components. Analyses were performed in R (version 4.2.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

RESULTS

Patient Characteristics

Analyses were restricted to consecutive postproficiency cases, with structured training-phase cases excluded (n = 18). Of 89 patients undergoing endoscopic surgery for SICH during the study period, 45 met inclusion criteria and had complete data for analysis (chopsticks, n = 20; control, n = 25) (Supplemental Digital Content 1, Figure S1, http://links.lww.com/NS9/A96). Baseline characteristics were comparable between cohorts (Table 1), including age, sex, hematoma volume, time to evacuation, intraventricular extension, and preoperative GCS score (all P > .05). ICH score strata were similarly distributed (control 1/2/3 = 2/14/9; chopsticks 1/2/3 = 2/9/9).

Primary Perioperative Outcomes

Compared with controls, the chopsticks cohort had shorter operative time (mean ± SD 118.3 ± 14.8 vs 147.3 ± 52.9 minutes; P = .014) and lower EBL (100.5 ± 30.1 vs 184.0 ± 117.1 mL; P = .002) (Table 2; Figure 2). Exploratory resource-utilization end points suggested shorter NSICU LOS (3.0 ± 0.9 vs 3.9 ± 1.8 days; P = .039) and total hospital LOS (14 ± 2 vs 16 ± 5 days; P = .029).

Radiographic Efficacy of Hematoma Evacuation

Radiographic evacuation metrics were high in both groups (Table 2). All patients achieved >90% evacuation (median [IQR] 94.1% [92.7%-95.2%] vs 94.7% [93.6%-96.3%]; P = .163), and residual hematoma volumes were similar (3.5 mL [2.7-4.0] vs 2.9 mL [2.2-3.7]; P = .303). Thus, between-group differences in early neurological measures were not accompanied by differences in gross evacuation extent.

Early Neurological Recovery (FOUR score; secondary outcome)

FOUR scores were assessed on postoperative day 3 (D3) and day 7 (D7) (Table 2; Figure 3A). At D3, FOUR scores were numerically higher in the chopsticks cohort (median [IQR] 10.5 [8.0-12.0] vs 9.0 [8.0-10.0]; P = .067). At D7, FOUR scores were higher in the chopsticks cohort (13.5 [11.8-15.0] vs 11.0 [9.0-13.0]; P = .008), with greater improvement from D3 to D7 (ΔFOUR 3.0 [2.0-3.2] vs 2.0 [1.0-3.0]; P = .005).

In an ICH score-adjusted linear mixed-effects model with a random intercept for patient (FOUR ∼ group × time + ICH score + [1|patient]), the group × time interaction was significant (β = 1.01; 95% CI 0.42-1.60; P = .001). In exploratory analyses, operative time correlated inversely with ΔFOUR (Spearman ρ = −0.55; P < .001) (Figure 3C) and remained associated in a parsimonious ICH score-adjusted model (β = −0.013 per minute; 95% CI −0.021 to −0.005; P < .001), with group assignment also significant (β = 0.69; 95% CI 0.10-1.29; P = .023).

Exploratory Clinical Outcomes

Longer-term outcomes were analyzed descriptively as exploratory end points. Observed 30-day mortality was lower than ICH score-based expectations in both cohorts (Supplemental Digital Content 4, Table S1, http://links.lww.com/NS9/A99). For 90-day functional independence, the observed-to-expected ratio was larger in the chopsticks cohort (relative risk 3.67, 95% CI 1.31-10.29) than in the control cohort (Supplemental Digital Content 5, Table S2, http://links.lww.com/NS9/A100); these benchmarked comparisons are hypothesis-generating.

Learning Curve Analysis

In the training-phase cumulative sum analysis, proficiency was reached after an inflection at approximately the fifth chopsticks case, with operative time decreasing by approximately 40% thereafter (162 ± 18 vs 97 ± 12 minutes) (Supplemental Digital Content 2, Figure S2A, http://links.lww.com/NS9/A97, Supplemental Digital Content 3, Figure S2B, http://links.lww.com/NS9/A98). No postoperative rebleeding or intracranial infection was observed, and conversion cases were not associated with adverse outcomes.

DISCUSSION

In this single-center pilot cohort, the endoscopic “chopsticks” configuration was associated with shorter operative time and lower intraoperative EBL compared with a conventional 2-surgeon, 3-handed technique for supratentorial SICH evacuation. These findings support feasibility of a single-surgeon endoport workflow and suggest that improved instrument coordination within a narrow corridor may translate into measurable efficiency gains.

Although minimally invasive and endoscopic evacuation is increasingly used to reduce approach-related injury, effective hemostasis and sustained visualization within a constrained endoscopic corridor remain common workflow-limiting steps.6,7,15,16 The chopsticks configuration is a technical refinement aimed at improving endoscope-suction control and hemostatic efficiency, and our perioperative findings are consistent with an effect on procedural efficiency rather than on the extent of clot removal.

Traditional endoscopic hemostasis often relies on an assistant for endoscope control or a holding device.17 These solutions may be less reproducible in emergency settings and in resource-limited environments, and mechanical holders may not provide the fine, dynamic view adjustments required during hemostasis.18 By integrating endoscope and suction control in the nondominant hand while reserving the dominant hand for bipolar coagulation, the chopsticks configuration may reduce instrument conflict and facilitate rapid, view-guided coagulation. Although causality cannot be established in this retrospective study, the observed reductions in operative time and EBL are consistent with the intended ergonomic rationale of the technique.

Given the retrospective design and limited sample size, we prioritized early neurological recovery and interpreted mechanistic signals as hypothesis-generating. The FOUR score is well suited to this context because it can be fully rated in intubated patients and captures brainstem reflexes and respiratory patterns, with established reliability and validity in neurocritical care settings.11 By contrast, early postoperative consciousness assessment using the GCS may be less informative when patients are intubated (absence of a verbal component) and when examination is influenced by early sedation practices.19 We therefore focused on postoperative day 3 and day 7 trajectories, a timeframe more likely to yield interpretable examinations as sedation is lightened and standardized “wake-up” assessments become feasible beyond the hyperacute postoperative period.20 Within these constraints, the steeper D3-to-D7 FOUR trajectory in the chopsticks cohort—together with the significant group × time interaction after ICH score adjustment and the inverse association between operative time and ΔFOUR in exploratory models—was consistent with, but did not prove, an efficiency-to-recovery pathway. Specifically, more stable bimanual control may reduce instrument conflicts and nonproductive maneuvers, shorten operative exposure, and potentially lessen traction- or thermal-related perihematomal iatrogenic stress. Notably, radiographic evacuation efficacy was uniformly high with similar residual hematoma volumes across groups, suggesting that early neurological trajectories were not accompanied by gross differences in evacuation extent. Previous work also supports the clinical utility of FOUR-based assessments in ICH risk stratification and outcome analyses.21

These considerations may be particularly relevant for patients at a higher risk of hematoma expansion and perioperative bleeding. Computed tomography angiography and noncontrast CT expansion markers (eg, spot sign and related markers) have been associated with hematoma growth and worse outcomes,22,23 and ultra-early evacuation has been linked to rebleeding in some experiences, motivating delayed or modified strategies in selected patients.24,25 Conversely, delayed decompression prolongs exposure to mass effect and blood-breakdown products that may exacerbate secondary injury.17,26 Although we did not stratify by imaging expansion markers, our findings support prospective evaluation of whether more stable endoscopic hemostatic control can facilitate timely evacuation in appropriately selected patients.

From an implementation perspective, the chopsticks configuration may be well suited to endoport-assisted evacuation. A rigid tubular corridor supports and guides the endoscope and suction while facilitating bimanual manipulation.9 Variable endoport diameters and lengths can be tailored to hematoma size, location, and depth,27 and inner diameters around 15 mm may balance tissue preservation with adequate working space.28,29 Structured skills training and attention to ergonomics may flatten the learning curve and reduce fatigue-related errors,30,31 and a low threshold to revert to a 2-surgeon workflow remains prudent during early adoption.

Previous literature shows that endoscopic evacuation can improve perioperative outcomes, while effects on longer-term functional end points have been variable across studies.32,33 Our study was not designed to establish long-term superiority; rather, it provides preliminary evidence that a hemostasis-focused configuration may be associated with efficiency gains and early recovery signals. Mechanistic inference remains limited without standardized postoperative imaging time points and objective measures of edema or secondary injury.4,34 Current guidelines emphasize minimally invasive strategies that balance timely intervention with tissue preservation,2 and the chopsticks configuration represents a technical refinement aligned with this principle.

Limitations

This study has important limitations, including modest sample size, retrospective single-center design, and the possibility of residual confounding. Although we attempted to mitigate learning curve effects by excluding training-phase cases and restricting analyses to postproficiency cases, residual confounding may persist, including unmeasured time-dependent differences in perioperative and neurocritical care practices that could influence resource utilization and early neurological assessments independent of surgical technique. These findings should therefore be considered hypothesis-generating and warrant prospective, ideally multicenter validation with standardized perioperative protocols and prespecified mechanistic end points.

CONCLUSION

In summary, the chopsticks configuration was associated with improved operative efficiency and a steeper early neurological recovery trajectory in this pilot cohort, without meaningful differences in gross evacuation extent. Prospective studies with standardized perioperative protocols and mechanistic end points are needed to assess generalizability and to test whether efficiency-related factors contribute to early recovery after endoscopic SICH evacuation.

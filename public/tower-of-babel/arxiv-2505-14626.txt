Equivariant Chern character operators and Okounkov’s conjecture
Mazen M. Alhwaimel, Zhenbo Qin

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Chern character operators on the equivariant cohomology of the Hilbert schemes of points in the complex affine plane ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} with the action of the torus 𝕋 = ( ℂ ∗ ) 2 \mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2} , and partially verify Okounkov’s Conjecture [ Oko , Conjecture 2] in this setting. Our main idea is to apply the connection between the equivariant cohomology of these Hilbert schemes and the ring of symmetric functions, via the deformed vertex operators of Cheng and Wang [ CW ] , (the integral form of) the Jack symmetric functions and the transformed Macdonald symmetric functions [ GH , Hai ] .

Primary 14C05; Secondary 11B65, 17B69.

1.
Introduction

It is well-known
[
Bri
,
Fog
,
Iar
]
that Hilbert schemes of points on a smooth algebraic surface are irreducible and smooth.
In
[
Oko
]
, Okounkov introduced some reduced generating series
for the intersection numbers among the Chern characters of
the tautological bundles over the Hilbert schemes of points on
a smooth algebraic surface and the total Chern classes of
the tangent bundles of these Hilbert schemes,
and conjectured that they are multiple
q
-zeta values which are
q
-deformations of the usual (multiple) zeta values

[
Bac
,
BK1
,
BK2
,
Bra1
,
Bra2
,
OT
,
Zhao
,
Zud
]
.
This conjecture has been investigated and partially verified in

[
Alh
,
AQ
,
Car1
,
Car2
,
CO
,
Qin2
,
QY
,
Zhou
]
.
The basic idea in these papers is to interpret
the reduced series in terms of the Chern character operators

[
LQW1
]
which act on the cohomology of
the Hilbert schemes via cup products. The Chern character operators
have played a pivotal role in determining the cohomology ring structures
of the Hilbert schemes of points on surfaces

[
Leh
,
LQW1
,
LQW2
,
LQW4
,
Qin1
]
.

In this paper, we study the Chern character operators and
Okounkov’s Conjecture in the equivariant setting which generalizes
the setting in
[
Car1
,
Car2
]
and
coincides with the setting in
[
CO
,
Nak2
,
OP1
,
OP2
,
Zhou
]
.
Let
X=\mathbb{C}^{2}
be the complex affine plane and
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
.
Let
u,v
be the standard coordinate functions on
X
.
Define the action of
\mathbb{T}
on
X
by

\displaystyle(a,b)\cdot(u,v)=(a^{-1}u,b^{-1}v),\quad(a,b)\in\mathbb{T}.

(1.1)

The origin
x\in X=\mathbb{C}^{2}
is the only fixed point.
The action of
\mathbb{T}

on
X
lifts to an action of
\mathbb{T}
on the Hilbert scheme
{X^{[n]}}

which parametrizes length-
n

0
-dimensional closed subschemes of
X
.
The
\mathbb{T}
-fixed points of
{X^{[n]}}
are supported on the origin
x\in X
and
indexed by partitions
\lambda
of
n
. We use
\xi_{\lambda}

to denote the fixed point in
{X^{[n]}}
corresponding to
a partition
\lambda=({\lambda}_{1}\geq{\lambda}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq{\lambda}_{k}>0)
of
n
,
and adopt the convention that
\xi_{\lambda}
is defined by the ideal

\displaystyle I_{\xi_{\lambda}}=\big\langle u^{{\lambda}_{1}},u^{{\lambda}_{2}}v,\ldots,u^{{\lambda}_{k}}v^{k-1},v^{k}\big\rangle.

(1.2)

Put
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}({\rm pt})=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2}]
and
H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\cdot)^{\prime}=H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(\cdot)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2}]}\mathbb{C}(t_{1},t_{2})
. Define

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{X}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}H_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}({X^{[n]}})^{\prime}.

(1.3)

By the localization theorem, the equivariant classes
[\xi_{\lambda}]
form
a linear basis of
\mathbb{H}_{X}^{\prime}
.

The tautological rank-
n
vector bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{[n]}
over
{X^{[n]}}

is
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant. The fiber of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{[n]}
over the fixed point

\xi_{\lambda}\in{X^{[n]}}
is canonically

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{[n]}|_{\xi_{\lambda}}=H^{0}\big(\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{\lambda}}\big)=\mathbb{C}[u,v]/\big\langle u^{{\lambda}_{1}},u^{{\lambda}_{2}}v,\ldots,u^{{\lambda}_{k}}v^{k-1},v^{k}\big\rangle.

(1.4)

It follows that as
\mathbb{T}
-modules, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{[n]}|_{\xi_{\lambda}}=\bigoplus\limits_{\square\in D_{\lambda}}\theta_{1}^{-a^{\prime}(\square)}\theta_{2}^{-\ell^{\prime}(\square)}

(1.5)

where
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are the two standard
1
-dimensional

\mathbb{T}
-modules, and
a^{\prime}(\square)
(respectively,
\ell^{\prime}(\square)
)
is the arm colength (respectively, the leg colength) of the cell
\square

in the Young diagram
D_{\lambda}
of the partition
{\lambda}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
k\geq 0
. The
equivariant Chern character operator

\mathfrak{G}(t_{1},t_{2})\in{\rm End}({\mathbb{H}}_{X}^{\prime})

and the
k
-th equivariant Chern character operator

\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t_{1},t_{2})\in{\rm End}({\mathbb{H}}_{X}^{\prime})
are defined by putting

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}(t_{1},t_{2})[\xi_{\lambda}]={\rm ch}^{\mathbb{T}}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{[|{\lambda}|]}\big)\cup[\xi_{\lambda}]=\sum_{\square\in D_{\lambda}}e^{-a^{\prime}(\square)t_{1}-\ell^{\prime}(\square)t_{2}}\cdot[\xi_{\lambda}],\qquad\qquad

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t_{1},t_{2})[\xi_{\lambda}]={\rm ch}_{k}^{\mathbb{T}}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{[|{\lambda}|]}\big)\cup[\xi_{\lambda}]=\sum_{\square\in D_{\lambda}}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}(a^{\prime}(\square)t_{1}+\ell^{\prime}(\square)t_{2})^{k}\cdot[\xi_{\lambda}]\quad

(1.7)

for every partition
{\lambda}
, and by putting

\mathfrak{G}(t_{1},t_{2})|_{H_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(X^{[0]})^{\prime}}=0=\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t_{1},t_{2})|_{H_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}(X^{[0]})^{\prime}}
.

Our goal is to study the equivariant Chern character operators

\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t_{1},t_{2})\in{\rm End}({\mathbb{H}}_{X}^{\prime})
.
It is known
[
LQW4
,
OP2
,
Qin1
,
Vas
]
that
{\mathbb{H}}_{X}^{\prime}
is
linearly isomorphic to
\mathfrak{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}(t_{1},t_{2})

where
\mathfrak{F}
is the Fock space
freely generated over
\mathbb{C}
by commuting creation operators

\mathfrak{a}_{-k},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
acting on the vacuum vector
|0\rangle
.
The annihilation operators
\mathfrak{a}_{k},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
satisfy

\mathfrak{a}_{k}|0\rangle=0
, and obey the commutation relations

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{a}_{k},\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}]=k\delta_{k,-\ell}\cdot\text{\rm Id}.

(1.8)

The Fock space
\mathfrak{F}
can be further identified with

\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
which denotes the ring (over
\mathbb{C}
) of symmetric functions
in countably many independent variables
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots
.
The following deformed vertex operator is introduced in
[
CW
]
:

\displaystyle V(z;q,t,\tilde{q},\tilde{t})=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}(q^{k}-\tilde{q}^{k})\frac{z^{k}}{k}\mathfrak{a}_{-k}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}(\tilde{t}^{k}-t^{k})\frac{z^{-k}}{k}\mathfrak{a}_{k}\right).

(1.9)

Let
V_{0}(z;q,t,\tilde{q},\tilde{t})={\rm Coeff}_{z^{0}}V(z;q,t,\tilde{q},\tilde{t})

be the
0
-mode of
V(z;q,t,\tilde{q},\tilde{t})
, and define

\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{B}}(q,t^{-1})=\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-t^{-1})}\big(1-V_{0}(z;t,t^{-1},1,qt^{-1})\big).

(1.10)

Let
q=e^{\alpha t_{0}}
where
\alpha
is a formal parameter,
and
t=e^{t_{0}}
. For
k\geq 0
, let

\displaystyle\overline{\mathfrak{B}}_{k}^{(\alpha)}={\rm Coeff}_{t_{0}^{k}}\overline{\mathfrak{B}}(q,t^{-1}).

(1.11)

Theorem 1.2
.

If
0\leq k\leq 2
, then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{2}^{k}t_{1}^{\delta(\cdot)}\overline{\mathfrak{B}}_{k}^{(\alpha)}|_{\alpha=-t_{1}/t_{2}}

(1.12)

where
t_{1}^{\delta(\cdot)}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=t_{1}^{\delta({\lambda})}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=t_{1}^{\ell({\lambda}^{-})-\ell({\lambda}^{+})}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}

for a generalized partition
{\lambda}
, and
{\lambda}^{+}
and
{\lambda}^{-}
are
the positive and negative parts of
{\lambda}
respectively.

We refer to Definition
3.4
(i) for the concepts and notations
related to generalized partitions. When
t_{1}=-t_{2}=1
, the operators

\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t_{1},t_{2})
have been determined in
[
LQW3
,
Car1
,
Car2
]
.
When
k=0
, formula (
1.12
)
yields the trivial identity

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{0}(t_{1},t_{2})=\sum_{i>0}\mathfrak{a}_{-i}\mathfrak{a}_{i}.

(1.13)

When
k=1
, formula (
1.12
) recovers

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{1}(t_{1},t_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j>0}(t_{1}t_{2}\mathfrak{a}_{-i}\mathfrak{a}_{-j}\mathfrak{a}_{i+j}-\mathfrak{a}_{-i-j}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{j})-(t_{1}+t_{2})\sum_{i>0}\frac{i-1}{2}\mathfrak{a}_{-i}\mathfrak{a}_{i}

(1.14)

which has been obtained by
[
OP1
, Theorem 1]
(setting
q=0
there)
and is known as the equivariant boundary operator following
[
Leh
]
.
Using (
1.12
), we obtain an explicit formula for

\mathfrak{G}_{2}(t_{1},t_{2})
in (
4.40
).
For
k\geq 3
, it is unlikely that (
1.12
) holds.
On the other hand, a direct computation reveals that

t_{2}^{k}t_{1}^{\delta(\cdot)}\overline{\mathfrak{B}}_{k}^{(\alpha)}|_{\alpha=-t_{1}/t_{2}}
equals

\sum_{\ell({\lambda})=k+2,\,|{\lambda}|=0}(-1)^{\ell({\lambda}^{+})-1}(t_{1}t_{2})^{\ell({\lambda}^{-})-1}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}+\sum_{\ell({\lambda})<k+2,\,|{\lambda}|=0}b^{(k)}_{\lambda}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}

where
b^{(k)}_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},t_{2})
.
Together with the formula of the Chern character operators in
the non-equivariant setting (see Theorem
3.7
), we propose
the following conjecture regarding the leading term of
\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t_{1},t_{2})

(in other words, our conjecture asserts that
\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t_{1},t_{2})
and

t_{2}^{k}t_{1}^{\delta(\cdot)}\overline{\mathfrak{B}}_{k}^{(\alpha)}|_{\alpha=-t_{1}/t_{2}}

have the same leading term).

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
k\geq 0
. Then,
\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t_{1},t_{2})
is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell({\lambda})=k+2,\,|{\lambda}|=0}(-1)^{\ell({\lambda}^{+})-1}(t_{1}t_{2})^{\ell({\lambda}^{-})-1}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}+\sum_{\ell({\lambda})<k+2,\,|{\lambda}|=0}g^{(k)}_{\lambda}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}

(1.15)

where
g^{(k)}_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},t_{2})
, and
{\lambda}^{+}
and
{\lambda}^{-}
are
the positive and negative parts of
{\lambda}
.

The main idea in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is
to exploit the connection between the Fock space
{\mathbb{H}}_{X}^{\prime}
and
the ring
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
of symmetric functions. Using the transformed

[

,

,

,

]
, we show in

that the integral form

of
the Macdonald symmetric function is an eigenfunction
of the operator
\overline{\mathfrak{B}}(q,t^{-1})
with eigenvalue

\sum_{\square\in D_{\lambda}}q^{a^{\prime}(\square)}t^{-\ell^{\prime}(\square)}
.

An important feature of Theorem
1.2
is the connection
between the equivariant Chern character operators
\mathfrak{G}_{k}(t_{1},t_{2})

where
0\leq k\leq 2
and vertex operators through (
1.9
),
(
1.10
) and (

).
It leads to two interesting applications. The first is to use

to investigate

Okounkov’s reduced generating series is defined by

\displaystyle\langle{\rm ch}_{k_{1}}\cdots{\rm ch}_{k_{N}}\rangle_{m,t_{1},t_{2}}^{\prime}=\frac{\langle{\rm ch}_{k_{1}}\cdots{\rm ch}_{k_{N}}\rangle_{m,t_{1},t_{2}}}{\langle\rangle_{m,t_{1},t_{2}}}

(1.16)

where
k_{1},\ldots,k_{N}\geq 0
,

, and via localization
(see Remark

(ii) and (i)),

\displaystyle{\langle{\rm ch}_{k_{1}}\cdots{\rm ch}_{k_{N}}\rangle_{m,t_{1},t_{2}}}={\rm Tr}_{{\mathbb{H}}_{X}^{\prime}}q^{\mathfrak{n}}\,\Gamma_{-}(1)^{m+t_{1}+t_{2}}\,\Gamma_{+}(1)^{m/(t_{1}t_{2})}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\mathfrak{G}_{k_{j}}(t_{1},t_{2}).

(1.17)

In (

),
\mathfrak{n}
is
the number-of-points operator
(i.e.,
\mathfrak{n}|_{H^{*}(X^{[n]})^{\prime}_{\mathbb{T}}}=n\,{\rm Id}

\Gamma_{\pm}(z)=\exp\left(\sum_{k>0}\frac{z^{\mp k}}{k}\mathfrak{a}_{\pm k}\right).

(1.18)

states that

\langle{\rm ch}_{k_{1}}\cdots{\rm ch}_{k_{N}}\rangle_{m,t_{1},t_{2}}^{\prime}
is
a multiple
q
-zeta value in

of weight at most

\sum_{i=1}^{N}(k_{i}+2)
. We refer to Section
2

the definitions and notations of multiple

-zeta values
and the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras
{\bf qMZV}\subset{\rm q}{\mathcal{BD}}\subset\mathcal{BD}
.
Our next result partially verifies Okounkov’s Conjecture in
the equivariant setting.

Theorem 1.4
.

(i)

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{N}
be nonnegative integers. Then,

\langle{\rm ch}_{k_{1}}\cdots{\rm ch}_{k_{N}}\rangle_{0,t_{1},t_{2}}^{\prime}\in{\rm q}{\mathcal{BD}}[t_{1},t_{2}]

is a degree-
\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}
symmetric homogeneous polynomial in

t_{1}
and
t_{2}
whose coefficients have weights at most

\sum_{i=1}^{N}(k_{i}+2)
.

(ii)

Let

. Then, Okounkov’s Conjecture
[
Oko
, Conjecture 2]

holds for
\langle{\rm ch}_{k}\rangle_{0,t_{1},t_{2}}^{\prime}
.
More precisely,
\langle{\rm ch}_{k}\rangle_{0,t_{1},t_{2}}^{\prime}\in{\bf qMZV}[t_{1},t_{2}]
is a degree-

t_{1}
and
t_{2}
whose coefficients have weights at most

, and the coefficient of

(and

) in

\langle{\rm ch}_{k}\rangle_{0,t_{1},t_{2}}^{\prime}
is equal to

(-1)^{k}\cdot[k+2]+\big(\text{terms with weights $<(k+2)$}\big).

(iii)

If
k_{1},\ldots,k_{N}\in\{0,1,2\}
, then

\langle{\rm ch}_{k_{1}}\cdots{\rm ch}_{k_{N}}\rangle_{m,t_{1},t_{2}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{BD}(t_{1},t_{2})[m]

with both degree (in

) and weight at most
\sum_{i=1}^{N}(k_{i}+2)
.

The second application of the vertex operator feature in

2.
Multiple

In this section, we recall basic materials related

q
-zeta values.
The following definitions are from
[

,
BK3
,
Oko

.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
, and fix a subset
S\subset\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

Let
Q=\{Q_{s}(t)\}_{s\in S}
where each
Q_{s}(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
is a polynomial
with
Q_{s}(0)=0
and
Q_{s}(1)\neq 0
. For
s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\in S

with
\ell\geq 1
, define

Z_{Q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{\ell}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{Q_{s_{i}}(q^{n_{i}})}{(1-q^{n_{i}})^{s_{i}}}\in\mathbb{Q}[[q]].

Put
Z_{Q}(\emptyset)=1
, and define
Z(Q,S)
to be the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear span
of the set

\{Z_{Q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})|\,\ell\geq 0\text{ and }s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\in S\}.

(ii)

Define
\mathcal{MD}=Z(Q^{E},\mathbb{N})
where
Q^{E}=\{Q_{s}^{E}(t)\}_{s\in\mathbb{N}}

and

Q_{s}^{E}(t)=\frac{tP_{s-1}(t)}{(s-1)!}

with
P_{s}(t)
being the Eulerian polynomial defined by

\displaystyle\frac{tP_{s-1}(t)}{(1-t)^{s}}=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}d^{s-1}t^{d}.

(2.1)

We have
tP_{0}(t)=t
.
For
s>1
, the polynomial
tP_{s-1}(t)
has degree
s-1
.
For
s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
with
\ell\geq 1
, define

\displaystyle[s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}]=Z_{Q^{E}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{\ell}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{Q_{s_{i}}^{E}(q^{n_{i}})}{(1-q^{n_{i}})^{s_{i}}}.

(2.2)

This
q
-series is called a
bracket
of depth
\ell
and of weight

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{i}
.

(iii)

Define
\text{q}\mathcal{MD}
be the subspace of
\mathcal{MD}
linearly
spanned by
1
and all the brackets
[s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}]
with
s_{1}>1
.

(iv)

Define
{\bf qMZV}=Z(Q^{O},\mathbb{N}_{>1})
where

Q^{O}=\{Q_{s}^{O}(t)\}_{s\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}}
with

Q_{s}^{O}(t)=\begin{cases}t^{s/2},&\text{if $s\geq 2$ is even,}\\
t^{(s-1)/2}(t+1),&\text{if $s\geq 3$ is odd,}\end{cases}

and
\mathbb{N}_{>1}=\{2,3,4,\ldots\}
.
For
s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
with
\ell\geq 1
, define

Z(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})=Z_{Q^{O}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}).

By the Theorem 2.13 and Theorem 2.14 in
[
BK1
]
,

\text{q}\mathcal{MD}
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{MD}
,
and
\mathcal{MD}
is a polynomial ring over
\text{q}\mathcal{MD}

with indeterminate
[1]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{MD}=\text{q}\mathcal{MD}[\,[1]\,].

(2.3)

By the Proposition 2.2 and Theorem 2.4 in
[
BK3
]
,

Z(\{Q_{s}^{E}(t)\}_{s\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}},\mathbb{N}_{>1})

as well and

{\bf qMZV}=Z(\{Q_{s}^{E}(t)\}_{s\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}},\mathbb{N}_{>1})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle{\bf qMZV}=Z(\{Q_{s}^{E}(t)\}_{s\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}},\mathbb{N}_{>1})\subset{\rm q}{\mathcal{MD}}\subset\mathcal{MD}

(2.4)

are inclusions of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras.
For instance, by
[

\displaystyle Z(2)=[2],\quad Z(3)=2[3],\quad Z(4)=[4]-\frac{1}{6}[2].

(2.5)

Next, we recall bi-brackets from

Definition 2.2
.

(i)

For integers
\ell\geq 0,s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\geq 1
and

r_{1},\ldots,r_{\ell}\geq 0
, define

\begin{bmatrix}s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\\
r_{1},\ldots,r_{\ell}\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}>\cdots>u_{\ell}>0\\
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}>0\end{subarray}}\frac{u_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots u_{\ell}^{r_{\ell}}}{r_{1}!\cdots r_{\ell}!}\cdot\frac{v_{1}^{s_{1}-1}\cdots v_{\ell}^{s_{\ell}-1}}{(s_{1}-1)!\cdots(s_{\ell}-1)!}\cdot q^{u_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+u_{\ell}v_{\ell}}\,\in\,\mathbb{Q}[[q]].

This
q
-series is called a

of depth
\ell
and of weight

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(s_{i}+r_{i})
.

(ii)

Define
\mathcal{BD}
to be the vector space spanned by the bi-brackets
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(iii)

Define
{\rm q}{\mathcal{BD}}
to be the subspace spanned by all the

.

Let

[

Theorem 3.1

The operators

\displaystyle\displaystyle{[\mathfrak{a}_{m}(\alpha),\mathfrak{a}_{n}(\beta)]=-m\;\delta_{m,-n}\cdot\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\cdot{\rm Id}_{{\mathbb{H}}_{X}}}.

(ii)

. Then,

(-t_{2})^{k}t_{1}^{\delta(\cdot)-1}{\rm Coeff}_{s_{1}\cdots s_{k}z_{1}^{0}\cdots z_{k}^{0}}\frac{(Q_{1}^{n}-1)T_{1}^{-n}z_{1}^{-n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-Q_{i})(1-T_{i}^{-1})}\prod_{j=2}^{k}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}f_{i,j}-\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}f_{j,i}\right)

\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{-}(T_{i}z_{i})\Gamma_{-}(z_{i})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{+}(Q_{i}^{-1}T_{i}z_{i})\Gamma_{+}(T_{i}z_{i})^{-1}.

, and

\displaystyle f_{i,j}=\frac{1-(T_{j}z_{j})^{-1}(T_{i}z_{i})}{1-(Q_{j}^{-1}T_{j}z_{j})^{-1}(T_{i}z_{i})}\cdot\frac{1-(Q_{j}^{-1}T_{j}z_{j})^{-1}z_{i}}{1-(T_{j}z_{j})^{-1}z_{i}}.

(6.2)

Proof.

By Theorem

), we have

\mathfrak{G}_{1}(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{2}t_{1}^{\delta(\cdot)}\overline{\mathfrak{B}}_{1}^{(\alpha)}=t_{2}t_{1}^{\delta(\cdot)}{\rm Coeff}_{t_{0}}\overline{\mathfrak{B}}(q,t^{-1})

.
Combining with (

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{1}(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{2}t_{1}^{\delta(\cdot)}{\rm Coeff}_{t_{0}}\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-t^{-1})}\big(1-V_{0}(z;t,t^{-1},1,qt^{-1})\big)

To avoid confusion with the notations, we write the above as

by replacing

Applying (

is equal to

\displaystyle[V(z_{k};T_{k},T_{k}^{-1},1,Q_{k}T_{k}^{-1}),[\ldots,[V(z_{1};T_{1},T_{1}^{-1},1,Q_{1}T_{1}^{-1}),\mathfrak{a}_{-n}]\ldots]

where

and

.
By Lemma

,
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}
is equal to

(-t_{2})^{k}t_{1}^{\delta(\cdot)-1}{\rm Coeff}_{s_{1}\cdots s_{k}z_{1}^{0}\cdots z_{k}^{0}}\frac{(Q_{1}^{n}-1)T_{1}^{-n}z_{1}^{-n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-Q_{i})(1-T_{i}^{-1})}

[V(z_{k};T_{k},T_{k}^{-1},1,Q_{k}T_{k}^{-1}),[\ldots,[V(z_{2};T_{2},T_{2}^{-1},1,Q_{2}T_{2}^{-1}),

Finally, applying Lemma

Our next result determines the leading term of
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}

where
n>0
and
k\geq 1
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
n>0
and
k\geq 1
. Then,
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}
is equal to

n^{k}k!\cdot\sum_{\ell({\lambda})=k+1,\,|{\lambda}|=-n}(-1)^{\ell({\lambda}^{+})}(t_{1}t_{2})^{\ell({\lambda}^{-})-1}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}+\sum_{\ell({\lambda})<k+1,\,|{\lambda}|=-n}f^{(k)}_{\lambda}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}

where
f^{(k)}_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},t_{2})
, and
{\lambda}^{\pm}
denote
the positive and negative parts of
{\lambda}
.

Proof.

We will compute the terms in the expression of
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}
from
Theorem
6.4
. Recall that
Q_{i}=e^{\alpha s_{i}}
with

\alpha=-t_{1}/t_{2}
and
T_{i}=e^{s_{i}}
. We get

\displaystyle\frac{(Q_{1}^{n}-1)T_{1}^{-n}z_{1}^{-n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-Q_{i})(1-T_{i}^{-1})}

(6.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}nz_{1}^{-n}}{\alpha^{k-1}s_{1}\prod_{i=2}^{k}s_{i}^{2}}\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-2)ns_{1}-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}(\alpha^{2}-3\alpha+3)n^{2}s_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}(\alpha-2)(\alpha-1)ns_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{1}{24}(\alpha^{2}+1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{8}(\alpha-1)^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}\right)^{2}+O(3)\right)

where
O(n)
denotes terms whose combined degrees in
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}
are
at least
n
.

Next, by (
1.18
),

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{-}(T_{i}z_{i})\Gamma_{-}(z_{i})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{+}(Q_{i}^{-1}T_{i}z_{i})\Gamma_{+}(T_{i}z_{i})^{-1}
equals

\displaystyle\prod_{r>0}\exp\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(T_{i}^{r}-1)z_{i}^{r}}{r}\mathfrak{a}_{-r}\right)\cdot\prod_{r>0}\exp\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(Q_{i}^{r}T_{i}^{-r}-T_{i}^{-r})z_{i}^{-r}}{r}\mathfrak{a}_{r}\right).

(6.5)

Since
Q_{i}=e^{\alpha s_{i}}
and
T_{i}=e^{s_{i}}
,
the above expression is equal to

\prod_{r>0}\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}rs_{i}+\frac{1}{6}r^{2}s_{i}^{2}+O(s_{i}^{3})\right)z_{i}^{r}\mathfrak{a}_{-r}\right)

\cdot\prod_{r>0}\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha^{2}-2\alpha)rs_{i}+\frac{1}{6}(\alpha^{3}-3\alpha^{2}+3\alpha)r^{2}s_{i}^{2}+O(s_{i}^{3})\right)z_{i}^{-r}\mathfrak{a}_{r}\right).

Thus,
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{-}(T_{i}z_{i})\Gamma_{-}(z_{i})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma_{+}(Q_{i}^{-1}T_{i}z_{i})\Gamma_{+}(T_{i}z_{i})^{-1}
is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}\cdot\alpha^{\ell({\lambda}^{+})}\cdot\left(\prod_{r>0}\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{r}\right)^{m_{-r}}\right.\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{rm_{-r}}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{r}\right)^{m_{-r}-1}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(s_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}rs_{i}^{3}\right)z_{i}^{r}

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{r^{2}}{4}\binom{m_{-r}}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{r}\right)^{m_{-r}-2}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{r}\right)^{2}\right)\cdot\prod_{r>0}\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{-r}\right)^{m_{r}}\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{rm_{r}}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{-r}\right)^{m_{r}-1}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left((\alpha-2)s_{i}^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}-3\alpha+3}{3}rs_{i}^{3}\right)z_{i}^{-r}

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\frac{r^{2}}{4}\binom{m_{r}}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{-r}\right)^{m_{r}-2}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha-2)s_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{-r}\right)^{2}\right)+O(\ell({\lambda})+3)\right)

(6.6)

where
{\lambda}
denotes a generalized partition whose multiplicity of part
n

is
m_{n}
.

To calculate
f_{i,j}
with
i\neq j
, we put
z_{i,j}=z_{i}z_{j}^{-1}

for simplicity. By (
6.2
),

\displaystyle f_{i,j}=\frac{1-e^{s_{i}-s_{j}}z_{i,j}}{1-e^{s_{i}+(\alpha-1)s_{j}}z_{i,j}}\cdot\frac{1-e^{(\alpha-1)s_{j}}z_{i,j}}{1-e^{-s_{j}}z_{i,j}}.

(6.7)

The numerator
(1-e^{s_{i}-s_{j}}z_{i,j})(1-e^{(\alpha-1)s_{j}}z_{i,j})
is equal to

\displaystyle 1-e^{s_{i}-s_{j}}z_{i,j}-e^{(\alpha-1)s_{j}}z_{i,j}+e^{s_{i}+(\alpha-2)s_{j}}z_{i,j}^{2}

(6.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-z_{i,j})^{2}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\frac{1}{\ell!}(s_{i}-s_{j})^{\ell}z_{i,j}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\frac{1}{\ell!}((\alpha-1)s_{j})^{\ell}z_{i,j}

\displaystyle\qquad+\,\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}\frac{1}{\ell!}(s_{i}+(\alpha-2)s_{j})^{\ell}z_{i,j}^{2}+O(5).

For the denominators in (
6.7
),

1/(1-e^{s_{i}+(\alpha-1)s_{j}}z_{i,j})
is equal to

\frac{1}{1-z_{i,j}}+\frac{z_{i,j}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{2}}(s_{i}+(\alpha-1)s_{j})+\frac{1}{2!}\frac{z_{i,j}+z_{i,j}^{2}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{3}}(s_{i}+(\alpha-1)s_{j})^{2}

+\frac{1}{3!}\frac{z_{i,j}+4z_{i,j}^{2}+z_{i,j}^{3}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{4}}(s_{i}+(\alpha-1)s_{j})^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4!}\frac{z_{i,j}+11z_{i,j}^{2}+11z_{i,j}^{3}+z_{i,j}^{4}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{5}}(s_{i}+(\alpha-1)s_{j})^{4}+O(5).

(6.9)

Similarly, we conclude that
1/(1-e^{-s_{j}}z_{i,j})
is equal to

\frac{1}{1-z_{i,j}}+\frac{z_{i,j}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{2}}(-s_{j})+\frac{1}{2!}\frac{z_{i,j}+z_{i,j}^{2}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{3}}(-s_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3!}\frac{z_{i,j}+4z_{i,j}^{2}+z_{i,j}^{3}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{4}}(-s_{j})^{3}+\frac{1}{4!}\frac{z_{i,j}+11z_{i,j}^{2}+11z_{i,j}^{3}+z_{i,j}^{4}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{5}}(-s_{j})^{4}+O(5).

(6.10)

Combining (
6.7
), (
6.8
),
(
6.9
) and (
6.10
),
we see that
f_{i,j}
is equal to

1+\frac{\alpha z_{i,j}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{2}}s_{i}s_{j}+\frac{\alpha z_{i,j}(1+z_{i,j})}{2(1-z_{i,j})^{3}}s_{i}^{2}s_{j}+\frac{\alpha(\alpha-2)z_{i,j}(1+z_{i,j})}{2(1-z_{i,j})^{3}}s_{i}s_{j}^{2}

+\frac{\alpha z_{i,j}(1+4z_{i,j}+z_{i,j}^{2})}{6(1-z_{i,j})^{4}}s_{i}^{3}s_{j}+\frac{\alpha z_{i,j}(-2(1+4z_{i,j}+z_{i,j}^{2})+\alpha(1+6z_{i,j}+z_{i,j}^{2}))}{4(1-z_{i,j})^{4}}s_{i}^{2}s_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha(\alpha^{2}-3\alpha+3)z_{i,j}(1+4z_{i,j}+z_{i,j}^{2})}{6(1-z_{i,j})^{4}}s_{i}s_{j}^{3}+\alpha s_{i}s_{j}O(3).

(6.11)

noting that
f_{i,j}-1
is divisible by
\alpha s_{i}s_{j}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\prod_{j=2}^{k}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}f_{i,j}-\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}f_{j,i}\right)

(6.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{k-1}\prod_{j=2}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}s_{i}s_{j}\left(\frac{z_{i,j}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{2}}+\frac{z_{i,j}(1+z_{i,j})}{2(1-z_{i,j})^{3}}s_{i}+\frac{(\alpha-2)z_{i,j}(1+z_{i,j})}{2(1-z_{i,j})^{3}}s_{j}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{z_{j,i}}{(1-z_{j,i})^{2}}-\frac{z_{j,i}(1+z_{j,i})}{2(1-z_{j,i})^{3}}s_{j}-\frac{(\alpha-2)z_{j,i}(1+z_{j,i})}{2(1-z_{j,i})^{3}}s_{i}+O(2)\right).

We see from Theorem
6.4
, (
6.4
),
(
6.6
) and (
6.12
) that

\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}
is equal to

(-1)^{k}t_{2}^{k}t_{1}^{\delta(\cdot)-1}\cdot{\rm Coeff}_{s_{1}\cdots s_{k}z_{1}^{0}\cdots z_{k}^{0}}\frac{(-1)^{k}nz_{1}^{-n}}{\alpha^{k-1}s_{1}\prod_{i=2}^{k}s_{i}^{2}}

\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-2)ns_{1}-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}+O(2)\right)

\cdot\alpha^{k-1}\prod_{j=2}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}s_{i}s_{j}\left(\frac{z_{i,j}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{2}}+\frac{z_{i,j}(1+z_{i,j})}{2(1-z_{i,j})^{3}}s_{i}+\frac{(\alpha-2)z_{i,j}(1+z_{i,j})}{2(1-z_{i,j})^{3}}s_{j}\right.

\left.-\frac{z_{j,i}}{(1-z_{j,i})^{2}}-\frac{z_{j,i}(1+z_{j,i})}{2(1-z_{j,i})^{3}}s_{j}-\frac{(\alpha-2)z_{j,i}(1+z_{j,i})}{2(1-z_{j,i})^{3}}s_{i}+O(2)\right)

\cdot\sum_{\lambda}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}\cdot\alpha^{\ell({\lambda}^{+})}\cdot\left(\prod_{r>0}\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{r}\right)^{m_{-r}}+\frac{rm_{-r}}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{r}\right)^{m_{-r}-1}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{r}\right)\right.

\left.\cdot\prod_{r>0}\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{-r}\right)^{m_{r}}+\frac{rm_{r}}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{-r}\right)^{m_{r}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha-2)s_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{-r}\right)+O(\ell({\lambda})+2)\right)

where
z_{i,j}=z_{i}z_{j}^{-1}
for
i\neq j
, and
{\lambda}
denotes
a generalized partition whose multiplicity of part
n
is
m_{n}
.
Note that for a fixed generalized partition
{\lambda}
,
the sum of the exponents of
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}

in the above expression is at least

-1-2(k-1)+2(k-1)+\ell({\lambda})=\ell({\lambda})-1.

Since
s_{1}\cdots s_{k}
has degree
k
, the generalized partitions
{\lambda}
with

\ell({\lambda})\geq k+2
do not contribute to
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}
.
Similarly, by considering the exponents of
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}
,
we see that if
|{\lambda}|\neq-n
, then
{\lambda}
does not contribute to
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}
.
Thus,
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}
is equal to

\sum_{\ell({\lambda})\leq k+1,\,|{\lambda}|=-n}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}\cdot(-1)^{\ell({\lambda}^{+})}t_{1}^{\ell({\lambda}^{-})-1}t_{2}^{k-\ell({\lambda}^{+})}\cdot{\rm Coeff}_{s_{1}\cdots s_{k}z_{1}^{0}\cdots z_{k}^{0}}\frac{nz_{1}^{-n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}}

\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-2)ns_{1}-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}+O(2)\right)

\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}s_{i}\left(\frac{z_{i,j}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{2}}+\frac{z_{i,j}(1+z_{i,j})}{2(1-z_{i,j})^{3}}s_{i}+\frac{(\alpha-2)z_{i,j}(1+z_{i,j})}{2(1-z_{i,j})^{3}}s_{j}\right.

It follows that
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}
is of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell({\lambda})=k+1,\,|{\lambda}|=-n}(-1)^{\ell({\lambda}^{+})}(t_{1}t_{2})^{\ell({\lambda}^{-})-1}f^{(k)}_{0,{\lambda}}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}+\sum_{\ell({\lambda})<k+1,\,|{\lambda}|=-n}f^{(k)}_{\lambda}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}

where
f^{(k)}_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},t_{2})
, and
f^{(k)}_{0,{\lambda}}\in\mathbb{Q}

\ell({\lambda})=k+1

{\rm Coeff}_{s_{1}\cdots s_{k}z_{1}^{0}\cdots z_{k}^{0}}\frac{nz_{1}^{-n}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}}\,\prod_{j=2}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}s_{i}\left(\frac{z_{i,j}}{(1-z_{i,j})^{2}}-\frac{z_{j,i}}{(1-z_{j,i})^{2}}\right)\cdot\prod_{r\neq 0}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}z_{i}^{r}\right)^{m_{-r}}.

It is very difficult to evaluate
f^{(k)}_{0,{\lambda}}
directly
via the previous line. Alternatively, by using induction on
k
,

\ell({\lambda})=k+1
and

, then

\displaystyle f^{(k)}_{0,{\lambda}}=n^{k}k!.

(6.14)

When

\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{a}_{-n}
.
Comparing with (

.
Now assume that (

We will show that (
6.14
) holds for

) and our inductive assumption,

\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}

\sum_{\ell({\lambda})=k+1,\,|{\lambda}|=-n}(-1)^{\ell({\lambda}^{+})}(t_{1}t_{2})^{\ell({\lambda}^{-})-1}n^{k}k!\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}+\sum_{\ell({\lambda})<k+1,\,|{\lambda}|=-n}f^{(k)}_{\lambda}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}}{{\lambda}^{!}}.

Putting
t_{1}=-1
and
t_{2}=1
,
since
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k+1)}=\big(\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k)}\big)^{\prime}
, we see that

\mathfrak{a}_{-n}^{(k+1)}
is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell({\lambda})=k+1,\,|{\lambda}|=-n}(-n)^{k}k!\frac{(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})^{\prime}}{{\lambda}^{!}}+\sum_{\ell({\lambda})<k+1,\,|{\lambda}|=-n}f^{(k)}_{\lambda}\frac{(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})^{\prime}}{{\lambda}^{!}}

(6.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell(\mu)=k+2,\,|\mu|=-n}(-1)^{k+1}f^{(k+1)}_{0,\mu}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}}{\mu^{!}}+\sum_{\ell(\mu)<k+2,\,|\mu|=-n}f^{(k+1)}_{\mu}\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}}{\mu^{!}}

where by abusing notations, we let
f^{(k)}_{\lambda}=f^{(k)}_{\lambda}(-1,1)
and

f^{(k+1)}_{\mu}=f^{(k+1)}_{\mu}(-1,1)
. By (
6.1
),

\mathfrak{a}_{m}^{\prime}={m}/{2}\cdot\sum_{i+j=m}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{j}
.
Using the arguments in the proof of
[
Qin1
, Lemma 3.29]
,
we conclude that
f^{(k+1)}_{0,\mu}=n^{k+1}(k+1)!

whenever
\ell(\mu)=k+2
and
|\mu|=-n
.
Hence (
6.14
) is true for
k+1
.

Finally, combining (
6.13
) and (
6.14
) completes
the proof of our proposition.
∎

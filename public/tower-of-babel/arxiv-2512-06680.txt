Quentin Ehret

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the study of Poisson superalgebras over fields of characteristic 2 2 . We investigate their representations, semidirect products, cohomology, formal deformations, and universal enveloping algebras. We also introduce Lie-Rinehart superalgebras in characteristic 2 2 and clarify their connections with Poisson superalgebras. In particular, we show that the universal enveloping algebra of a Poisson superalgebra coincides with the universal enveloping algebra of an associated Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. We also compute examples and initiate the study of pre-Poisson superalgebras.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Poisson (super)algebras in characteristic zero

Poisson brackets occur naturally in many different contexts and have numerous applications, particularly in classical mechanics. Poisson algebras were introduced as an algebraic framework for Poisson brackets and have since played an essential role in areas such as Hamiltonian mechanics, quantum mechanics, integrable systems, quantization, symplectic and differential geometry, see
[
LPV
]
for a thorough survey of these structures. Roughly speaking, a Poisson algebra is an associative commutative algebra equipped with a Lie bracket that is a biderivation for the product. Poisson (co)homology was introduced by Lichnerowicz (
[
Li
]
) and Koszul (
[
K
]
) in the setting of Poisson manifolds. In the purely algebraic context, the construction of the Poisson cohomology relies heavily on the Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology of Lie algebras. The usual Chevalley–Eilenberg cochain space is replaced by the space of skew-symmetric bilinear biderivations of the associative commutative product (see, for instance,
[
LPV
, Section 4.1.2]
). From the point of view of representation theory, Oh defined the universal enveloping algebra of a Poisson algebra in
[
Oh
]
; see also
[
OPS
,
LOV
]
. Poisson algebras are related to Lie-Rinehart algebras; this connection is explored in
[
Hu
]
. A Lie-Rinehart algebra is a triple
(A,L,\rho)
, where
A
is an associative commutative algebra,
L
is both a Lie algebra and an
A
-module, and
\rho:L\to\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)
is a morphism of Lie algebras and
A
-modules, called
anchor map
. This map satisfies a compatibility condition that controls, in particular, the failure of the Lie bracket on
L
to be
A
-linear. Lie-Rinehart algebras first appeared in the works of Herz (
[
He
]
) and Palais (
[
P
]
), and were developed systematically by Rinehart (
[
Ri
]
), in order to provide a formalism of differential forms for general commutative algebras. He also studied representations and cohomology of Lie-Rinehart algebras, which were later thoroughly studied by Huebschmann (see
[
Hu
]
). In particular, Huebschmann showed that any Poisson algebra gives rise to a Lie-Rinehart algebra by considering its module of Kähler differentials, and he used this construction to obtain Poisson (co)homology in terms of the associated Lie-Rinehart algebra. Furthermore, the same construction provides a way to define the universal enveloping algebra of a Poisson algebra via the enveloping algebra of the corresponding Lie-Rinehart algebra.

In the supercase, the notion of Poisson superalgebra appeared in connection with supersymmetric mechanics. Indeed, the generalization of Hamiltonian mechanics to systems admitting “bosonic” and “fermionic” variables naturally led to the notion of Poisson superalgebra, see e.g.
[
Kan
,
Kos
,
KS1
,
S
,
Sh
]
. Note that there is also a notion of
Buttin bracket
(sometimes called
antibracket
or
Schouten bracket
), which is an odd version of the Poisson bracket, see e.g
[
KS2
,
LS
,
S
]
. In a recent paper (
[
La
]
), Lamkin studied the universal enveloping algebra of Poisson superalgebras. In particular, he constructed the universal enveloping algebra for a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra and proved a new version of the PBW Theorem, in order to generalize Huebschmann’s constructions (see
[
Hu
]
) to the supercase. Notably, he showed that the Poisson universal enveloping algebra can be constructed using the corresponding Lie-Rinehart superalgebra of Kähler differentials and proved a PBW Theorem for Poisson superalgebras.

1.2.
The case of the characteristic
p=2

In the case where the base field has characteristic
p=2
, the usual definition of a Lie superalgebra has to be amended. Indeed, a Lie superalgebra is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector superspace
L=L_{\bar{0}}\oplus L_{\bar{1}}
such that the even part
L_{\bar{0}}
is a Lie algebra, the odd part is a two-sided
L_{\bar{1}}
-module due to symmetry, and a map
s:L_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow L_{\bar{0}}
, called
squaring
that satisfies some compatibility conditions, including a modified Jacobi identity; see Section
2.1
. The definition is due to Lebedev (
[
LeD
]
), although earlier versions can be found in
[
BMPZ
,
NR
]
. Due to the presence of the squaring, new methods are required to study those objects, and they have received some proper attention over the last few years. For instance, double extensions and Manin triples have been studied in
[
BB1
,
BB2
]
, while the classification was investigated in
[
BGL1
,
BGLLS
,
BLLS
]
. Derivations and deformations were studied in
[
BGL2
,
BGLL
]
, while a full cohomology theory was introduced in
[
BM
]
and used in
[
BBE
]
to study Lagrangian extensions and left-symmetric structures.

The analogues of several types of superalgebras, such as orthogonal, Hamiltonian, Poisson, and contact superalgebras were studied by Lebedev in
[
LeD
]
. He showed that there are four equivalence classes of non-degenerate symmetric bilinear forms on a linear superspace, denoted
I
,
I\Pi
,
\Pi I
, and
\Pi\Pi
. This gives rise to several Poisson brackets (resp. Buttin brackets) depending on whether the form is even (resp. odd); see
[
LeD
]
for further details.

1

1

Note that two of the Poisson brackets in
[

]
(namely, types

) do

More recently, an algebraic definition of a Poisson superalgebra in characteristic

was introduced by Petrogradsky and Shestakov in
[

. Their definition includes an additional compatibility condition between the squaring map and the associative supercommutative product, see Section

.
We also mention that

[

]
in the characteristic

In this last paper, we notably adapted some of Huebschmann’s constructions; namely, we built a cohomology for restricted Poisson algebras and restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras and showed that under a freeness hypothesis, they are isomorphic when considering the restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra of Kähler differentials. However, we will

1.3.
Outline of the paper and main results

The aim of this paper is to investigate the notion of Poisson superalgebras over a field of characteristic

and to study analogues of classical constructions such as representations, cohomology, formal deformations and universal enveloping algebras. In Section

2.1.2.
Subalgebras and ideals

Let

Lemma
.

Let
(L,[-,-],s)
be a Lie superalgebra. A map
\bar{s}:L_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow L_{\bar{0}}
is a squaring on
L
satisfying the Jacobi identity if and only if there exists a 2-semilinear map
g:L_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow Z(L)_{\bar{0}}
such that
\bar{s}(x)=s(x)+g(x),~\forall x\in L_{\bar{1}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\bar{s}=s+g
is a squaring satisfying the Jacobi identity. Then, for all
x,y\in L_{\bar{1}},
and all
\lambda\in\K,
we have

g(\lambda x+y)=\lambda^{2}\bar{s}(x)+\bar{s}(y)+\lambda^{2}s(x)+s(y)+2[\lambda x,y]=\lambda^{2}g(x)+g(y).

Thus, the map
g
is 2-semilinear. Moreover, since for all
z\in L
, we have

[g(x),z]=[\bar{s}(x)+s(x),z]=2[x,[x,z]]=0,

it follows that
g
maps any
x\in L_{\bar{1}}
into the center of
L
. Next, suppose that
g:L_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow Z(L)_{\bar{0}}
is 2-semilinear and let
\bar{s}=s+g.
Clearly, we have
\bar{s}(\lambda x)=\lambda^{2}\bar{s}(x),~\forall x\in L_{\bar{1}},~\forall\lambda\in\K.
Moreover, for all
x,y\in L_{\bar{1}}
and all
z\in L
, we have

\bar{s}(x+y)=g(x)+g(y)+s(x)+s(y)+[x,y]=\bar{s}(x)+\bar{s}(y)

as well as

[\bar{s}(x),z]=[g(x)+s(x),z]=[s(x),z]=[x,[x,z]].

Therefore, the map
\bar{s}
is a squaring satisfying the Jacobi identity.
∎

Proposition
.

Let
L_{\bar{0}}
be a Lie algebra with a basis
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})

Then,
L=L_{\bar{0}}\oplus L_{\bar{1}}
is a Lie superalgebra.

Proof.

Let
x_{1},\cdots x_{m}
is a basis of
L_{\bar{1}}
and define
s(x_{j}):=y_{j}~\forall~1\leq j\leq n
. Then, for any
\lambda_{i}\in\K

(6)

s\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\Bigl)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{2}s(x_{i})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[x_{i},x_{j}].

Let us prove that the map
s
satisfies the Jacobi identity. For all
y\in L
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[x_{i},[x_{j},y]]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[x_{i},[x_{j},y]]+\sum_{i>j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[x_{i},[x_{j},y]]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[x_{i},[x_{j},y]]+\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[x_{j},[x_{i},y]]=\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[[x_{i},x_{j}],y].

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\Bigl[s\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\bigl),y\Bigl]+\Bigl[\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}x_{i},\Bigl[\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}x_{j},y\Bigl]\Bigl]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\cancel{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}[s(x_{i}),y]}+\sum_{i<j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[[x_{i},x_{j}],y]+\cancel{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}[x_{i},[x_{i},y]]}+\sum_{i\neq j}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}[x_{i},[x_{j},y]]=0.

Therefore, the map
s
satisfies the Jacobi identity. The conclusion follows.

3.1.

Let

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rcll}[x,ay]&=&a[x,y]+\rho_{x}(a)y,&~\forall a\in A,~\forall x,y\in L;\\

(a\cdot D)(b):=aD(b),~\forall a,b\in A,~\forall D\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A).
As anchor map, we take the identity map
\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits:\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A).
It is indeed an
A
-linear superalgebras morphism. The first compatibility condition of Definition
3.1
is readily checked (see e.g.
[
R
]
). Let
(a,b)\in A_{\bar{0}}\times A
and
D\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)_{\bar{1}}
. We have

\displaystyle s\bigl((a\cdot D)\bigl)(b)=(a\cdot D)\bigl(aD(b)\bigl)=aD(a)D(b)+a^{2}(D\circ D)(b)=a^{2}s(D)(b)+\bigl((a\cdot D)(a)\cdot D\bigl)(b).

In the case where
a\in A_{\bar{1}}
and
D\in\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A)_{\bar{0}},
the same computation leads to
s\bigl((a\cdot D)\bigl)(b)=\bigl((a\cdot D)(a)\cdot D\bigl)(b)
, since
a^{2}=0
. The second compatibility condition is therefore satisfied and
\bigl(A,\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A),\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits\bigl)
is a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra.

Let
(A,L,\rho)
and
(A^{\prime},L^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
be Lie-Rinehart superalgebras in characteristic
2
. A morphism of Lie-Rinehart superalgebras is a pair
(\phi,\psi)
, with
\phi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
is a morphism of associative superalgebras and
\psi:L\rightarrow L^{\prime}
is a
A
-linear Lie superalgebras morphism, such that

\phi\left(\rho_{x}(a)\right)=\rho^{\prime}_{\psi(x)}\left(\phi(a)\right),~~\forall x\in L,\forall a\in A.

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. An ideal
I
of the Lie superalgebra
L
is called
ideal of the Lie-Rinehart superalgebra

(A,L,\rho)
if
(A,I,0)
is a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra with the induced action and the anchor identically equal to zero.
With this definition, one can check that
\bigl(A,L/I,\tilde{\rho}\bigl)
is a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra with
\tilde{\rho}
the induced anchor.

Example
.

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. Then, the anchor map induces a Lie-Rinehart morphism
(\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits,\rho):(A,L,\rho)\rightarrow\bigl(A,\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A),\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits\bigl)
, whose kernel is an ideal.

3.1.4.
Lie-Rinehart modules

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra and
V
be a
A
-module. A
weak module
is a pair
(\pi,V)
, where
\pi:L\rightarrow\text{End}(V)
is a Lie superalgebras morphism and
V
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space such that

(12)

\pi_{x}(av)=a\pi_{x}(v)+\rho_{x}(a)v,~\forall a\in A,~\forall x\in L,~\forall v\in V.

If the map
\pi
is
A
-linear, the pair
(\pi,V)
is called
strong module
.

Remark
.

The above definition resembles the classical one (see
[
Ri
,
Hu
]
), but differs from
[
La
, Definition 5.4]
. Our definition is motivated by the following Proposition.

Proposition
.

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra and
V
be a strong Lie-Rinehart module. Then, the triple
(A,L\oplus V,\tilde{\rho})
is a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra with

(i)

the bracket
\bigl[(x+v),(y+w)\bigl]:=[x,y]+\pi_{x}(w)+\pi_{y}(v),~\forall x,y\in L,~\forall v,w\in V;

(ii)

the squaring
s(x+v):=s(x)+\pi_{x}(v),~\forall x\in L_{\bar{1}},~\forall v\in V_{\bar{1}};

(iii)

the anchor
\tilde{\rho}(x+v)=\rho(x),~\forall x\in L~\forall v\in V.

Proof.

Straightforward computations.
∎

3.2.
Universal enveloping algebra

This section is devoted to the construction of the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. Our construction follows the original work of Rinehart (
[
Ri
]
) and
[
La
, Section 5]
. The universal property for Lie-Rinehart algebras was proven later by Huebschmann (
[
Hu
]
). Given a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra
(A,L,\rho)
, the idea is to equip the superspace
A\oplus L
with a Lie superalgebra structure and then to consider its enveloping algebra.

Proposition
.

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. Then, the superspace
A\oplus L
is a Lie superalgebra with

(i)

the bracket
\bigl[a+x,b+y\bigl]:=\rho_{x}(b)+\rho_{y}(a)+[x,y],~\forall a,b\in A,~\forall x,y\in L;

(ii)

the squaring
s(a+x):=\rho_{x}(a)+s(x),~\forall a\in A,~x\in L.

Proof.

Recall that the grading on
A\oplus L
is given by
(A\oplus L)_{{\bar{0}}}=A_{{\bar{0}}}\oplus L_{{\bar{0}}}
and
(A\oplus L)_{{\bar{1}}}=A_{{\bar{1}}}\oplus L_{{\bar{1}}}
. We will only prove the condition on the squaring (for the bracket, see
[
Ri
]
or
[
La
]
). Let
a+x\in A_{{\bar{1}}}\oplus L_{{\bar{1}}}
and
\lambda\in\K
. We have

s\bigl(\lambda(a+x)\bigl)=s\bigl(\lambda a+\lambda x\bigl)=\bigl(\rho_{\lambda x}(\lambda a)+s(\lambda x)\bigl)=\lambda^{2}\bigl(\rho_{x}(a)+s(x)\bigl)=\lambda^{2}s(a+x).

Moreover, for any
b+y\in A_{{\bar{1}}}\oplus L_{{\bar{1}}}
, we have

\displaystyle s\bigl((a+x)+(b+y)\bigl)

\displaystyle=s(a+b+x+y)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\rho_{a+b}(x+y)+s(x+y)\bigl)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\rho_{a}(x)+s(x)\bigl)+\bigl(\rho_{b}(y)+s(y)\bigl)+\bigl(\rho_{a}(y)+\rho_{b}(x)+[x,y]\bigl)

\displaystyle=s(a+x)+s(b+y)+\bigl[a+x,b+y\bigl].

It remains to check the Jacobi identity involving the squaring (see Eq. (
3
)). Let
a+x\in A_{{\bar{1}}}\oplus L_{{\bar{1}}}
and
b+y\in A\oplus L
.
On one hand, we have

\displaystyle\bigl[s(a+x),b+y\bigl]

\displaystyle=\bigl[\rho_{x}(a)+s(x),b+y\bigl]

\displaystyle=\bigl(\rho_{s(x)}(b)+\rho_{y}\bigl(\rho_{x}(a)\bigl)+\bigl[s(x),y\bigl]\bigl)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\rho_{x}^{2}(b)+\rho_{y}\circ\rho_{x}(a)+[x,[x,y]]\bigl).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl[a+x,\bigl[a+x,b+y\bigl]\Bigl]

\displaystyle=\Bigl[(a+x),\bigl(\rho_{x}(b)+\rho_{y}(a)+[x,y]\bigl)\Bigl]

\displaystyle=\bigl(\rho_{x}\bigl(\rho_{x}(b)+\rho_{y}(a)\bigl)+\rho_{[x,y]}(a)+\bigl[x,[x,y]\bigl]\bigl)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\rho_{x}^{2}(b)+\underset{=~0}{\underbrace{\rho_{x}\circ\rho_{y}(a)+\rho_{x}\circ\rho_{y}(a)}}+\rho_{y}\circ\rho_{x}(a)+\bigl[x,[x,y]\bigl]\bigl)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\rho_{x}^{2}(b)+\rho_{y}\circ\rho_{x}(a)+\bigl[x,[x,y]\bigl]\bigl).

Therefore, we have

\bigl[s(a+x),b+y\bigl]=\bigl[a+x,\bigl[a+x,b+y\bigl]\bigl].

It follows that Eq. (
3
) is satisfied and
A\oplus L
is a Lie superalgebra.
∎

3.2.2.
Construction of the universal enveloping algebra.

Let
(A,L,\rho)
be a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra. Denote by
T(A\oplus L)=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(A\oplus L)^{\otimes n}
the tensor algebra of
A\oplus L
.
Let
U(A\oplus L)=\bigl(T(A\oplus L)/I_{1}\bigl)/I_{2}
be the Lie enveloping algebra of
A\oplus L
, with
I_{1}
the two-sided ideal of
T(A\oplus L)
generated by elements

I_{1}:=\Bigl<(a+x)\otimes(b+y)+(b+y)\otimes(a+x)+\bigl[(a+x),(b+y)\bigl],~(a+x),~(b+y)\in(A\oplus L)\Bigl>

and
I_{2}
the two-sided ideal of
T(A\oplus L)
generated by elements

I_{2}:=\Bigl<s(c+z)+(c+z)\otimes(c+z),~(c+z)\in(A\oplus L)_{\bar{1}}\Bigl>,

see also
[
PS
, Section 2.2]
. Let
i:A\oplus L\rightarrow U(A\oplus L)
be the canonical inclusion, let
i_{A}:A\rightarrow A\oplus L
and
i_{L}:L\rightarrow A\oplus L
be the inclusions of
A
and
L
into
A\oplus L
respectively. Moreover, let
\overline{U}(A\oplus L)
be the subalgebra of
U(A\oplus L)
generated by
i(A\oplus L)
. We define the
universal enveloping algebra
of
(A,L,\rho)
, denoted by
U(A,L,\rho)
by

U(A,L,\rho)=\overline{U}(A\oplus L)/J,

with
J
the two-sided ideal generated by elements

J:=\bigl<i(a)i(b+x)+i(ab+ax)\bigl>.

Note that the following relations hold in
U(A,L,\rho)
for all
a\in A
and all
x\in L
:

(13)

\displaystyle i_{L}(ax)

\displaystyle=i_{A}(a)i_{L}(x);

(14)

\displaystyle i_{L}(x)i_{A}(a)

\displaystyle=i_{A}(a)i_{L}(x)+i_{A}\bigl(\rho_{x}(a)\bigl).

The enveloping algebra
U(A,L,\rho)
satisfies the following universal property.

Proposition
.

For any triple
(B,r_{A},r_{L})
where
B
is an associative superalgebra,

r_{A}:A\to B
is an even superalgebra morphism, and

r_{L}:L\to B
is an even Lie superalgebra morphism satisfying for all
a\in A
and all
x\in L

(15)

\displaystyle r_{L}(ax)

\displaystyle=r_{A}(a)r_{L}(x);

(16)

\displaystyle r_{L}(x)r_{A}(a)

\displaystyle=r_{A}(a)r_{L}(x)+r_{A}\bigl(\rho_{x}(a)\bigl),

there exists an unique even associative
superalgebras morphism
f:U(A,L,\rho)\to B
such that
f\circ i_{A}=r_{A}
and
f\circ i_{L}=r_{L}
.

Proof.

Consider the map
r:A\oplus L\to B,~a+x\mapsto r_{A}(a)+r_{L}(x).
It follows from
[
La
, Proposition 5.5]
that

r\bigl([a+x,b+y]\bigl)=r(a+x)r(b+y)+r(b+y)r(a+x).

Moreover, for all
a+x\in(A\oplus L)_{\bar{1}}
, we have

\displaystyle r(a+x)^{2}

\displaystyle=r_{A}(a)^{2}+r_{A}(a)r_{L}(x)+r_{L}(x)r_{A}(a)+r_{L}(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=r_{A}\bigl(\rho_{x}(a)\bigl)+r_{L}\bigl(s(x)\bigl)

\displaystyle=r\bigl(\rho_{x}(a)+s(x)\bigl)=r\bigl(s(a+x)\bigl).

Therefore, the map
r
is a morphism of Lie superalgebras. By the universal property of
U(A\oplus L)
, there exists an unique associative superalgebras morphism
\widetilde{r}:\overline{U}(A\oplus L)\to B
such that
\widetilde{r}\circ i=r
, where
i:A\oplus L\to\overline{U}(A\oplus L)
denotes the canonical map.
Let
a,b\in A
and
x\in L
. We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{r}\bigl(i(a)i(b,x)+i(ab,ax)\bigl)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{r}(i(a))\widetilde{r}(i(b,x))+r(i(ab,ax))

\displaystyle=r_{A}(a)\bigl(r_{A}(b)+r_{L}(x)\bigl)+r_{A}(ab)+r_{L}(ax)=0,

since
r_{A}
is a morphism of associative superalgebras and
r_{L}(ax)=r_{A}(a)r_{L}(x).
Thus, the ideal
J
is contained in the kernel of the map
\widetilde{r}
and it follows that there exists an unique superalgebras morphism
f:U(A,L,\rho)\to B
that satisfies
f\circ i_{A}=r_{A}
and
f\circ i_{L}=r_{L}
.
∎

4.
Poisson superalgebras in characteristic
2

In this Section, we investigate representations, cohomology, formal deformations, universal enveloping algebra and examples of Poisson superalgebras.

4.1.
Poisson superalgebras

Let
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
be a Lie superalgebra equipped with an associative supercommutative product denoted by juxtaposition. Following
[
PS
, Section 2.4]
,
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
is called
Poisson superalgebra in characteristic 2
if

\begin{array}[]{crcll}(i)&\{xy,z\}&=&x\{y,z\}+\{x,z\}y,&~\forall x,y,z\in P;\\
(ii)&s(xy)&=&x^{2}s(y)+xy\{x,y\},&~\forall x\in P_{\bar{0}},~\forall y\in P_{\bar{1}}.\end{array}

A Poisson superalgebra will be usually denoted by a triple
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
, or sometimes by
\bigl(P,\cdot,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
if it is needed to specify the associative supercommutative product
(\cdot)
. In the case where no confusion is possible, we denote it simply by
P
.

Let
P
and
Q
be Poisson superalgebras. A linear map
\varphi:P\rightarrow Q
is called
Poisson superalgebras morphism
if it is a morphism of associative superalgebras and of Lie superalgebras at the same time.

Let
(P,\{-,-\},s)
be a Poisson superalgebra. A linear map
\delta:P\to P
is called a
Poisson derivation
if

\begin{array}[]{crcll}(i)&\delta(xy)&=&\delta(x)y+x\delta(y),&\forall x,y\in P,\\
(ii)&\delta\bigl(\{x,y\}\bigl)&=&\{\delta(x),y\}+\{x,\delta(y)\},&\forall x,y\in P,\\
(iii)&\delta\bigl(s(x)\bigl)&=&\{\delta(x),x\},&\forall x\in P_{\bar{1}}.\end{array}

The space of Poisson derivations will be denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{\mathrm{po}}(P).
The space of linear maps
P\to P
satisfying Condition
(i)
only will be denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{\mathrm{asso}}(P).
From the definitions, it is clear that a Lie superalgebra derivation
\delta
(see Eq. (
5
)) is a Poisson derivation if and only if
\delta
satisfies Condition (
i
) above.

An homogeneous Lie ideal (see Section
2.1.2
)
I\subset P
of a Poisson superalgebra is a
Poisson ideal
if
xI\in I,~\forall x\in P.
With this definition, one can readily check that the quotient
P/I
is a Poisson superalgebra.

Example
.

Consider the Lie superalgebra
P
given by the basis
e_{1},e_{2}|e_{3},e_{4}
(even
|
odd) and the squaring
s(e_{3})=e_{1},~s(e_{4})=e_{2},~s(e_{3}+\lambda e_{4})=e_{1}+\lambda^{2}e_{2},
for all
\lambda\in\K
. The brackets are zero. Consider the associative supercommutative product given by
e_{1}x=x,~\forall x\in P
, and
e_{3}e_{4}=e_{4}e_{3}=e_{2}
. Then,
P
has the structure of a Poisson superalgebra in characteristic
2
.

Proposition
.

Let
(P,\{-,-\},s)
and
(Q,[-,-],s^{\prime})
be Poisson superalgebras. The tensor product
P\otimes Q
is a Poisson superalgebra with

(
i
)

the associative supercommutative product

(p_{1}\otimes q_{1})(p_{2}\otimes q_{2}):=(p_{1}p_{2})\otimes(q_{1}q_{2}),\quad\forall p_{1},p_{2}\in P,~\forall q_{1},q_{2}\in Q;

(
ii
)

the Lie bracket

\bigl[p_{1}\otimes q_{1},p_{2}\otimes q_{2}\bigl]_{\otimes}:=\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\otimes q_{1}q_{2}+p_{1}p_{2}\otimes[q_{1},q_{2}],\quad\quad\forall p_{1},p_{2}\in P,~\forall q_{1},q_{2}\in Q;

(
iii
)

the squaring

\begin{array}[]{rcll}s_{\otimes}(p\otimes q)&:=&p^{2}\otimes s^{\prime}(q),&\forall p\in P_{\bar{0}},~\forall q\in Q_{\bar{1}},\\
s_{\otimes}(p\otimes q)&:=&s(p)\otimes q^{2},&\forall p\in P_{\bar{1}},~\forall q\in Q_{\bar{0}}.\end{array}

Proof.

We prove the identities involving the squaring. Recall that the grading on
P\otimes Q
is given by

(P\otimes Q)_{\bar{0}}=(P_{\bar{0}}\otimes Q_{\bar{0}})\oplus(P_{\bar{1}}\otimes Q_{\bar{1}});~~(P\otimes Q)_{\bar{1}}=(P_{\bar{0}}\otimes Q_{\bar{1}})\oplus(P_{\bar{1}}\otimes Q_{\bar{0}}).

Let
p_{2}\in P,~q_{2}\in Q
,
p_{1}\in P_{\bar{0}}
and
q_{1}\in Q_{\bar{1}}
. We have

\displaystyle[s_{\otimes}(p_{1}\otimes q_{1}),p_{2}\otimes q_{2}]_{\otimes}

\displaystyle=[p_{1}^{2}\otimes s^{\prime}(q_{1}),p_{2}\otimes q_{2}]_{\otimes}

\displaystyle=\{p_{1}^{2},p_{2}\}\otimes s^{\prime}(q_{1})q_{2}+p_{1}^{2}p_{2}\otimes[s^{\prime}(q_{1}),q_{2}]

\displaystyle=\bigl(p_{1}\{p_{1},p_{2}\}+\{p_{1},p_{2}\}p_{1}\bigl)\otimes s^{\prime}(q_{1})q_{2}+p_{1}^{2}p_{2}\otimes[q_{1},[q_{1},q_{2}]]

\displaystyle=p_{1}^{2}p_{2}\otimes[q_{1},[q_{1},q_{2}]].

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\bigl[p_{1}\otimes q_{1},[p_{1}\otimes q_{1},p_{2}\otimes q_{2}]_{\otimes}\bigl]_{\otimes}=

\displaystyle~\bigl[p_{1}\otimes q_{1},\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\otimes q_{1}q_{2}+p_{1}p_{2}\otimes[q_{1},q_{2}]\bigl]_{\otimes}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\{p_{1},\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\}\otimes q_{1}^{2}q_{2}+p_{1}\cancel{\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\otimes[q_{1},q_{1}q_{2}]}

\displaystyle+\cancel{\{p_{1},p_{1}p_{2}\}\otimes q_{1}[q_{1},q_{2}]}+p_{1}^{2}p_{2}\otimes[q_{1},[q_{1},q_{2}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~p_{1}^{2}p_{2}\otimes[q_{1},[q_{1},q_{2}]].

Therefore, the squaring
s_{\otimes}
satisfies the Jacobi identity and
P\otimes Q
is a Lie superalgebra.
It remains to check the compatibility of the squaring
s_{\otimes}
with the multiplication. There are two cases to consider.

The case where
p_{2},q_{2}
are even.
We have

\displaystyle s_{\otimes}\bigl((p_{1}\otimes q_{1})(p_{2}\otimes q_{2})\bigl)=

\displaystyle~s_{\otimes}(p_{1}p_{2}\otimes q_{1}q_{2})=(p_{1}p_{2})^{2}\otimes s^{\prime}(q_{1}q_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~(p_{1}p_{2})^{2}\otimes q_{2}^{2}s^{\prime}(q_{1})+q_{1}q_{2}[q_{1},q_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~(p_{1}p_{2})^{2}\otimes q_{2}^{2}s^{\prime}(q_{1})+(p_{1}p_{2})^{2}\otimes q_{1}q_{2}[q_{1},q_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~(p_{2}\otimes q_{2})^{2}\bigl(p_{1}^{2}\otimes s^{\prime}(q_{1})\bigl)+(p_{1}p_{2}\otimes q_{1}q_{2})\bigl(\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\otimes q_{1}q_{2}+p_{1}p_{2}[q_{1},q_{2}]\bigl)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~(p_{2}\otimes q_{2})^{2}s_{\otimes}(p_{1}\otimes q_{1})+(p_{1}\otimes q_{1})(p_{2}\otimes q_{2})\bigl[p_{1}\otimes q_{1},p_{2}\otimes q_{2}\bigl]_{\otimes}.

The case where
p_{2},q_{2}
are odd.
In that case, we have

s_{\otimes}\bigl((p_{1}\otimes q_{1})(p_{2}\otimes q_{2})\bigl)=0=(p_{2}\otimes q_{2})^{2}s_{\otimes}(p_{1}\otimes q_{1})+(p_{1}\otimes q_{1})(p_{2}\otimes q_{2})\bigl[p_{1}\otimes q_{1},p_{2}\otimes q_{2}\bigl]_{\otimes}.

It follows that
P\otimes Q
is a Poisson superalgebra.
∎

4.2.
Analogs of “classical” Poisson Lie superalgebras from
[
LeD
]

In this Section, we show that the analogs of classical Poisson Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 defined by Lebedev in
[
LeD
]
are indeed Poisson superalgebras according to Definition
4.1
.

Let
k\geq 1
. The associative commutative algebra of divided powers in
k
variables
x:=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})
is defined for
\underline{N}:=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{k})
, where
n_{s}\geq 0,~\forall~1\leq s\leq k
, by (see, e.g,
[
Ko
,
SF
]
)

(17)

\displaystyle\K(x;\underline{N}):=\mathop{\mathrm{{Span}}}\nolimits\bigl\{x^{(\underline{i})}:=x_{1}^{(i_{1})}\cdots x_{k}^{(i_{k})},~(\underline{i})=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}),~0\leq i_{s}\leq 2^{n_{s}}-1\bigl\}.

The multiplication is given by

(18)

x^{(\underline{i})}x^{(\underline{j})}=\Big(\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\underline{i}+\underline{j}}{\underline{i}}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\underline{i}+\underline{j}}{\underline{i}}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\underline{i}+\underline{j}}{\underline{i}}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\underline{i}+\underline{j}}{\underline{i}}$}}\Big)x^{(\underline{i}+\underline{j})},~\text{where}~\Big(\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\underline{i}+\underline{j}}{\underline{i}}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{\underline{i}+\underline{j}}{\underline{i}}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\underline{i}+\underline{j}}{\underline{i}}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\underline{i}+\underline{j}}{\underline{i}}$}}\Big):=\prod_{s=1}^{k}\Big(\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i_s+j_s}{i_s}$}}{\raisebox{0.25pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{0}{i_s+j_s}{i_s}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{i_s+j_s}{i_s}$}}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{i_s+j_s}{i_s}$}}\Big).

We will write
\mathcal{O}(k,\underline{N})=\K(x;\underline{N})
or just
\mathcal{O}(k)
if the value of
\underline{N}
is not important, see
[
LeD
]
for more details. It is shown in
[
LeD
, Section 6]
that there are two Poisson Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 given by the following formulas, for
f,g\in\mathcal{O}(n)
and
h\in\mathcal{O}(n)_{\bar{1}}
.

\textup{(}i\textup{)}

Type
\Pi I
. The bracket is given by

(19)

\{f,g\}_{\Pi I}=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\bar{0}}}\Bigl(\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q_{i}}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial q_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{i}}\Bigl)+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\bar{1}}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\theta_{i}}

and the squaring by

(20)

s_{\Pi I}(h)=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\bar{0}}}\Bigl(\frac{\partial h}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial h}{\partial q_{i}}\Bigl)+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\bar{1}}}\Bigl(\frac{\partial h}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Bigl)^{(2)};

\textup{(}ii\textup{)}

Type
\Pi\Pi
. The bracket is given by

(21)

\{f,g\}_{\Pi\Pi}=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\bar{0}}}\Bigl(\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q_{i}}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial q_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{i}}\Bigl)+\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\bar{1}}}\Bigl(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\xi_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\eta_{i}}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial\eta_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\xi_{i}}\Bigl)

and the squaring by

(22)

s_{\Pi\Pi}(h)=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\bar{0}}}\Bigl(\frac{\partial h}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial h}{\partial q_{i}}\Bigl)+\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\bar{1}}}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\xi_{i}}\frac{\partial h}{\partial\eta_{i}};

where we denoted
n=n_{\bar{0}}+n_{\bar{1}}
, with
n_{\bar{0}}
(resp.
n_{\bar{1}}
) the dimension of the even part (resp. odd), and

\begin{array}[]{lll}p_{i}=x_{i},&q_{i}=x_{k_{\bar{0}}+i},&\text{if $n_{\bar{0}}=2k_{\bar{0}}$ and $1\leq i\leq k_{\bar{0}}$;}\\
\theta_{i}=x_{n_{\bar{0}}+i},&&\text{if $1\leq i\leq n_{\bar{0}}$;}\\
\xi_{i}=x_{n_{\bar{0}}+i},&\eta_{i}=x_{n_{\bar{0}}+k_{\bar{1}}+i},&\text{if $n_{\bar{1}}=2k_{\bar{1}}$ and $1\leq i\leq k_{\bar{1}}$.}\end{array}

Proposition
.

The Poisson Lie superalgebras of type
\Pi I
and
\Pi\Pi
are Poisson superalgebras in the sense of Definition
4.1
.

Proof.

We prove that the Poisson Lie superalgebra of type
\Pi I
satisfies Condition (
ii
) of Definition
4.1
. The case of type
\Pi\Pi
is similar. Let
f\in\mathcal{O}(n)
and let
g\in\mathcal{O}(n)_{\bar{1}}
. We have (for the sake of readability, we drop the subscript
\Pi I
)

\displaystyle s(fg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\bar{0}}}\Bigl(g\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{i}}+f\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{i}}\Bigl)\Bigl(g\frac{\partial f}{\partial q_{i}}+f\frac{\partial g}{\partial q_{i}}\Bigl)+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\bar{1}}}\Bigl(g\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{i}}+f\frac{\partial g}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Bigl)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\bar{0}}}\Bigl(gf\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q_{i}}+fg\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial q_{i}}+f^{(2)}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q_{i}}\Bigl)+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\bar{1}}}\Biggl(f^{(2)}\Bigl(\frac{\partial g}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Bigl)^{(2)}+fg\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Biggl)

\displaystyle=f^{(2)}\Biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\bar{0}}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\bar{1}}}\Bigl(\frac{\partial g}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Bigl)^{(2)}\Biggl)+fg\Biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\bar{0}}}\Bigl(\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q_{i}}+\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial q_{i}}\Bigl)+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\bar{1}}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{i}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Biggl)

4.3.
Representations of Poisson superalgebras

Let
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
be a Poisson superalgebra and let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
be a super vector space. A
representation
of
P
in
V
is given by two maps
\pi,\gamma:P\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(V)
such that

(
i
)

\pi
is a morphism of associative superalgebras;

(
ii
)

\gamma
is a morphism of Lie superalgebras;

(
iii
)

the four following compatibility conditions are satisfied:

(23)

\displaystyle\gamma(xy)

\displaystyle=\pi(x)\gamma(y)+\pi(y)\gamma(x),

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in P;

(24)

\displaystyle\pi\bigl([x,y]\bigl)

\displaystyle=\pi(x)\gamma(y)+\gamma(y)\pi(x),

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in P;

(25)

\displaystyle\pi(y)\gamma(x)\pi(x)_{|V_{\bar{0}}}

\displaystyle=\pi(x)\pi(y)\gamma(x)_{|V_{\bar{0}}},

\displaystyle\forall x\in P_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in P_{\bar{0}};

(26)

\displaystyle\pi(y)^{2}\gamma(x)_{|V_{\bar{1}}}

\displaystyle=\pi(y)\gamma(x)\pi(y)_{|V_{\bar{1}}}+\pi(x)\pi(y)\gamma(y)_{|V_{\bar{1}}}

\displaystyle~~\quad+\gamma(y)\pi(x)\pi(y)_{|V_{\bar{1}}},

\displaystyle\forall x\in P_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in P_{\bar{0}}.

The compatibility conditions (
23
) and (
24
) appeared in
[
Oh
,
LOV
,
La
]
, while (
25
) and (
26
) are specific to the characteristic 2 case. Such a representation will be denoted by a triple
(V,\pi,\gamma)
.

Example

(Adjoint representation)
.

Let
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
be a Poisson superalgebra. Define
\pi(x)(v)=xv
and
\gamma(x)(v)=\{x,v\},\quad\forall x,v\in P
. Then
(P,\pi,\gamma)
is a representation of the Poisson superalgebra
P
into itself, called
adjoint representation
.

Proof.

We prove conditions (
25
) and (
26
). Let
x,v\in P_{\bar{1}}
and
y,w\in P_{\bar{0}}
. Since
[x,x]=0
, (
25
) is immediate. For (
26
), notice that

(27)

y^{2}\bigl(s(x+v)+s(x)+s(v)+[x,v]\bigl)=0=y\bigl(v[x,y]+v[x,y]\bigl).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle y^{2}[x,v]

\displaystyle=y^{2}s(x+v)+y^{2}s(x)+y^{2}s(v)

\displaystyle=y^{2}[x,v]+yv[x,y]+\underset{=~0}{\underbrace{xy[y,v]+xy[y,v]}}+\underset{=~vy[y,x]}{\underbrace{v[y,xy]}},

which is exactly (
26
).
∎

Proposition

(Semi-direct product)
.

Let
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
be a Poisson superalgebra and let
(V,\pi,\gamma)
be a representation. Then,
\bigl(P\oplus V,\cdot,\{-,-\}_{\rtimes},s_{\rtimes}\bigl)
is a Poisson superalgebra, where

(28)

\displaystyle(x+v)\cdot(y+w)

\displaystyle=xy+\pi(x)(w)+\pi(y)(v),

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in P,\forall v,w\in V;

(29)

\displaystyle\{x+v,y+w\}_{\rtimes}

\displaystyle=\{x,y\}+\gamma(x)(w)+\gamma(y)(v),

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in P,\forall v,w\in V;

(30)

\displaystyle s_{\rtimes}(x+v)

\displaystyle=s(x)+\gamma(x)(v),

\displaystyle\forall x\in P_{\bar{1}},\forall v\in V_{\bar{1}}.

Proof.

We prove that
s_{\rtimes}
satisfy condition
(ii)
of Definition
4.1
. For the other conditions involving the squaring, see
[
BM
, Theorem 3.1]
. Let
x\in P_{\bar{1}},y\in P_{\bar{0}},v\in V_{\bar{1}},w\in V_{\bar{0}}
. We have

(31)

\displaystyle s_{\rtimes}\bigl((x+v)\cdot(y+w)\bigl)

\displaystyle=s\bigl(xy+\pi(x)(w)+\pi(y)(v)\bigl)

\displaystyle=s(xy)+\gamma(xy)\pi(x)(w)+\gamma(xy)\pi(y)(v);

(32)

\displaystyle(y+w)^{2}\cdot s_{\rtimes}(x+v)

\displaystyle=y^{2}\cdot\bigl(s(x)+\gamma(x)(v)\bigl)=y^{2}s(x)+\pi(y^{2})\gamma(x)(v);

\displaystyle(x+v)\cdot(y+w)\cdot\{x+v,y+w\}_{\rtimes}

\displaystyle=\bigl(xy+\pi(x)(w)+\pi(y)(v)\bigl)\cdot\bigl(\{x,y\}+\gamma(x)(w)+\gamma(y)(v)\bigl)

(33)

\displaystyle=xy\{x,y\}+\pi(x)\pi(y)\gamma(x)(w)+\pi(x)\pi(y)\gamma(y)(v)

\displaystyle~~\quad+\pi(\{x,y\})\pi(x)(w)+\pi(\{x,y\})\pi(y)(v).

Thus, by summing (
31
)+(
32
)+(
33
), we get

\displaystyle\gamma(xy)\pi(x)+\pi(x)\pi(y)\gamma(x)+\pi(\{x,y\})\pi(x)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\cancel{\pi(x)\gamma(y)\pi(x)}+\pi(y)\gamma(x)\pi(x)+\pi(x)\pi(y)\gamma(y)+\cancel{\pi(x)\gamma(y)\pi(x)}+\underset{=~0}{\underbrace{\gamma(y)\pi(x)^{2}}}=0,

as maps
V_{\bar{0}}\rightarrow V_{\bar{0}}
, since Eq. (
25
) holds. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\gamma(xy)\pi(y)+\pi(y^{2})\gamma(x)+\pi(x)\pi(y)\gamma(y)+\pi(\{x,y\})\pi(y)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\cancel{\pi(x)\gamma(y)\pi(x)}+\pi(y)\gamma(x)\pi(y)+\pi(y)^{2}\gamma(x)+\pi(x)\pi(y)\gamma(y)

\displaystyle+\cancel{\pi(x)\gamma(y)\pi(x)}+\rho(y)\pi(x)\pi(y)=0,

as maps
V_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow V_{\bar{0}}
, since Eq. (
26
) holds. Therefore, we have

s_{\rtimes}\bigl((x+v)\cdot(y+w)\bigl)=(y+w)^{2}\cdot s_{\rtimes}(x+v)+(x+v)\cdot(y+w)\cdot\{x+v,y+w\}_{\rtimes},

and the result follows.
∎

4.4.
Cohomology of Poisson superalgebras in characteristic 2

In this Section, we introduce a cohomology theory for Poisson superalgebras in characteristic 2 that is derived from the cohomology of Lie superalgebras, see Section
2.2
.

Let
A
be an associative supercommutative superalgebra. For
k\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathfrak{X}^{k}(A)
the space of skew-symmetric
k
-derivations of
A
, that is, multilinear maps
\varphi\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\wedge^{k}A,A)
satisfying

(34)

\varphi(xy,z_{2},\cdots,z_{k})=x\varphi(y,z_{2},\cdots,z_{k})+y\varphi(x,z_{2},\cdots,z_{k}),~\forall x,y,z_{2},\cdots,z_{k}\in A.

4.4.1.
Poisson cochains

Let
(P,\{-,-\},s)
be a Poisson superalgebra in characteristic
2
. For
n=0,1
, we set
XC^{0}_{\rm po}(P)=A
and
XC^{1}_{\rm po}(P)=\mathfrak{X}^{1}(P)
. For
n\geq 2
, a pair
(\varphi,\omega)\in XC^{n}(P;P)
is a Poisson superalgebra
n
-cochain if
\varphi\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}(P)
and moreover if for all
\lambda\in\K
and all
x\in P_{\bar{1}},y\in P_{\bar{0}}
and all
z_{2},\cdots,z_{n-1}\in P
, we have (where
2\leq i\leq n-1
):

(35)

\displaystyle\omega(xy,z_{2},\cdots,z_{n-1})=

\displaystyle~y^{2}\omega(x,z_{2},\cdots,z_{n-1})+xy\varphi(x,y,z_{2},\cdots,z_{n-1}),

(36)

\displaystyle\omega(x,z_{2},\cdots,z_{i}z^{\prime}_{i},\cdots,z_{n-1})=

\displaystyle~z_{i}\omega(x,z_{2},\cdots,z^{\prime}_{i},\cdots,z_{n-1})

\displaystyle~+z^{\prime}_{i}\omega(x,z_{2},\cdots,z_{i},\cdots,z_{n-1}).

The space of all pairs
(\varphi,\omega)\in XC^{n}(P;P)
satisfying (
35
) and (
36
) is denoted by
XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P)
. The coboundary operators

{\rm d}_{\rm po}^{n}:XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P)\rightarrow XC^{n+1}_{\rm po}(P)
for

n\geq 0
are induced by the coboundary operators for Lie superalgebras, see Section
2.2.1
and Proposition
4.4.2.  Proposition
below.

We denote by

XZ_{\rm po}^{n}(P)=\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(d^{n}_{\rm po})
the Poisson
n
-cocycles and
XB_{\rm po}^{n}(P)=\text{Im}(d^{n-1}_{\rm po})
the Poisson
n
-coboundaries.

Following
[
BBE
, Section 2.2]
, we can define a graduation on the cochain spaces
XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P)
in the following way. Let
(\varphi,\omega)\in XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P).
In the case where
\varphi
is an even map, then
\text{Im}(\omega)\subset P_{\bar{0}}
; whereas if
\varphi
is odd, then
\text{Im}(\omega)\subset P_{\bar{1}}
. Therefore, we can define a graduation on the space
XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P)
by setting

|(\varphi,\omega)|:=|\varphi|,~\forall(\varphi,\omega)\in XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P).

Therefore, we have a decomposition
XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P)=XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P)_{\bar{0}}\oplus XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P)_{\bar{1}}
, where

(37)

\begin{array}[]{rcll}XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P)_{\bar{0}}&=&\{(\varphi,\omega)\in XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P),~|\varphi|={\bar{0}}\};\\
XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P)_{\bar{1}}&=&\{(\varphi,\omega)\in XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P),~|\varphi|={\bar{1}}\}.\end{array}

Proposition
.

Let
(P,\{-,-\},s)
be a Poisson superalgebra in characteristic
2
, and let
n\geq 0
. For all
(\varphi,\omega)\in XC^{n}_{\rm po}(P)
, we have
d^{n}_{\rm po}(\varphi,\omega)\in XC^{n+1}_{\rm po}(P)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in P_{\bar{1}},~y\in P_{\bar{0}},~Z:=z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}\in P
. We also denote by
\widehat{Z_{i}}:=(z_{2},\cdots,\hat{z_{i}},\cdots,z_{n})
,
\widehat{Z_{i,j}}:=(z_{2},\cdots,\hat{z_{i}},\cdots,\hat{z_{j}},\cdots,z_{n})
, and so on, where the hat means that the term is omitted. We have

\displaystyle\delta^{n}\omega(xy,Z)=~

\displaystyle\{xy,\varphi(xy,Z)\}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{n}\{z_{i},\omega(xy,\widehat{Z_{i}})\}+\varphi(s(xy),Z)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{n}\varphi\left(\{xy,z_{i}\},xy,\widehat{Z_{i}}\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n}\omega\left(xy,\{z_{i},z_{j}\},\widehat{Z_{i,j}}\right)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle y^{2}\delta^{n}\omega(x,Z)+xy\,d_{{\text{CE}}}^{n}\varphi(x,y,Z)

\displaystyle+\{z_{i},y\}y\,\omega(x,\widehat{Z_{i}})+\{z_{i},x\}y\,\varphi(x,y,\widehat{Z_{i}})+\{z_{i},y\}x\,\varphi(x,y,\widehat{Z_{i}}\Big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle y^{2}\delta^{n}\omega(x,z_{2},\cdots,z_{n})+xy\,d_{{\text{CE}}}^{n}\varphi(x,y,z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}).

Moreover, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\delta^{n}\omega(x,z_{2}z^{\prime}_{2},\widehat{Z_{2}})\\[5.69054pt]
=\{x,\varphi(x,z_{2}z^{\prime}_{2},\widehat{Z_{2}})\}+\{z_{2}z_{2}^{\prime},\omega(x,\widehat{Z_{2}})\}\\[5.69054pt]
\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{n}\{z_{i},\omega(x,z_{2}z_{2}^{\prime},\widehat{Z_{2,i}})\}+\varphi(s(x),z_{2}z_{2}^{\prime},\widehat{Z_{2}})+\varphi\left(\{x,z_{2}z_{2}^{\prime}\},x,\widehat{Z_{2}}\right)\\[5.69054pt]
\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{n}\varphi\left(\{x,z_{i}\},x,z_{2}z_{2}^{\prime},\widehat{Z_{2,i}}\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{n}\omega\left(x,\{z_{2}z_{2}^{\prime},z_{i}\},\widehat{Z_{2,i}}\right)\\[5.69054pt]
\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{3\leq i<j\leq n}\omega\left(x,\{z_{i},z_{j}\},z_{2}z_{2}^{\prime},\widehat{Z_{2,i,j}}\right)\\[8.53581pt]
=z_{2}\delta^{n}\omega(x,z^{\prime}_{2},\widehat{Z_{2}})+z^{\prime}_{2}\delta^{n}\omega(x,z_{2},\widehat{Z_{2}}).\end{array}

The result follows.
∎

Since
d_{\rm po}^{n}
is induced by the coboundary operator for the Lie superalgebra
(P,\{-,-\},s)
, we have
d_{\rm po}^{n}(\varphi,\omega)\in C_{\rm po}^{n+1}(P)
and
d_{\rm po}^{\,n+1}\circ d_{\rm po}^{n}=0
. It follows that we obtain a well-defined cochain complex
\bigl(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}XC_{\rm po}^{n}(P),d_{\rm po}^{n}\bigr)
, and the associated
n
-th cohomology group
XH_{\rm po}^{n}(P)
is also well defined, and admits a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graduation induced by (
37
).

Remark
.

From the definition of Poisson derivations in Section
4.1
, it follows that a linear map
\delta:P\mapsto P
is a Poisson derivation if and only of it is a Poisson 1-cocycle.

4.5.
Formal deformations

We briefly consider formal deformations of Poisson superalgebras as an application of the cohomology introduced in Section
4.4
. A formal deformation of a Poisson superalgebra
(P,\{-,-\},s)
is a Poisson superalgebra
(P[[t]],\mu_{t},\omega_{t})
, where
P[[t]]
is the
\K[[t]]
-algebra of formal power series with coefficients in
P
, the maps
\mu_{t}
and
\omega_{t}
are defined by

\mu_{t}(x,y)=\{x,y\}+\sum_{i\geq 1}t^{i}\mu_{i}(x,y);\quad\omega_{t}(z)=s(z)+\sum_{i\geq 1}t^{i}\omega_{i}(z),\quad\forall x,y\in P,~\forall z\in P_{\bar{1}},

with
(\mu_{i},\omega_{i})\in XC^{2}_{\rm po}(P)_{\bar{0}}.
Note that since
(\mu_{i},\omega_{i})\in XC^{2}_{\rm po}(P),
the conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
of Definition
4.1
are automatically satisfied by the maps
\mu_{t}
and
\omega_{t}
.

Two formal deformations
(P[[t]],\mu_{t},\omega_{t})
and
(P[[t]],\mu_{t}^{\prime},\omega_{t}^{\prime})
of a Poisson superalgebra

(P,\{-,-\},s)
are called
equivalent
if there exists an (even) isomorphism of Poisson superalgebras

(38)

\psi_{t}:(P[[t]],\mu_{t},\omega_{t})\to(P[[t]],\mu_{t}^{\prime},\omega_{t}^{\prime}),\quad\psi_{t}=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits+\sum_{i\geq 1}t^{i}\psi_{i},

where
\psi_{i}:P\to P
are linear maps.
Let
k\geq 1.
A formal deformation of order
k
is given by a Poisson superalgebra
(P_{k}[[t]],\mu_{(k)},\omega_{(k)})
, where
P_{k}[[t]]=P[[t]]/\langle t^{k+1}\rangle
and

(39)

\mu_{(k)}(x,y)=\{x,y\}+\sum_{i\geq 1}^{k}t^{i}\mu_{i}(x,y);\quad\omega_{(k)}(z)=s(z)+\sum_{i\geq 1}^{k}t^{i}\omega_{i}(z),\quad\forall x,y\in P,~\forall z\in P_{\bar{1}}.

The formal deformations of order
k=1
are called
infinitesimal deformations
.

Lemma
.

Let
k\geq 1
and let
\bigl(P_{k}[[t]],\mu_{(k)},\omega_{(k)}\bigl)
be a formal deformation of order
k
of a Poisson superalgebra
\bigl(P,\cdot,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
. Then, we have
(\mu_{1},\omega_{1})\in XZ^{2}_{\rm po}(P)
.

Proof.

By expanding Eq. (
39
), we have
(\mu_{1},\omega_{1})\in XC^{2}_{\rm po}(P)
. The 2-cocycle condition is covered by
[
BM
, Theorem 6.3]
.
∎

Next, we discuss the problem of extending a deformation of order
k
to a deformation of order
k+1
. Let
(P,\{-,-\},s)
be a Poisson superalgebra and let
(P_{k}[[t]],\mu_{(k)},\omega_{(k)})
be deformation of order
k
. For
k\geq 1
, we define the maps

\displaystyle{\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(\mu_{i}(x,\mu_{k+1-i}(y,z))+\mu_{i}(y,\mu_{k+1-i}(z,x))+\mu_{i}(z,\mu_{k+1-i}(x,y))\Bigl),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~x,y,z\in P;

\displaystyle{\text{obs}}^{(2)}_{k+1}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(\mu_{i}\bigl(y,\omega_{k+1-i}(x)\bigl)+\mu_{i}\bigl(x,\mu_{k+1-i}(x,y)\bigl)\Bigl),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall x\in P_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in P_{\bar{1}}.

.

We have
\bigl({\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1},{\text{obs}}^{(2)}_{k+1}\bigl)\in XC^{3}_{\rm po}(P)_{\bar{0}}
.

Proof.

By
[
BM
, Theorem 6.3]
, we have
\bigl({\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1},{\text{obs}}^{(2)}_{k+1}\bigl)\in XC^{3}_{\rm po}(P;P)
. Moreover, we have

(40)

{\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1}(xy,z,v)=x~{\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1}(y,z,v)+y~{\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1}(x,z,v),~\forall x,y,z,v\in P.

Let
x\in P_{\bar{1}}
and
y,z\in P
. We show that the pair
\bigl({\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1},{\text{obs}}^{(2)}_{k+1}\bigl)
satisfies Eq. (
35
).

\displaystyle{\text{obs}}^{(2)}_{k+1}(xy,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(\mu_{i}\bigl(z,\omega_{k+1-i}(xy)\bigl)+\mu_{i}\bigl(xy,\mu_{k+1-i}(xy,z)\bigl)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(y^{2}\mu_{i}(z,\omega_{k+1-i}(x))+xy\cdot\mu_{i}(z,\mu_{k+1-i}(x,y))+\mu_{k+1-i}(x,y)\mu_{i}(z,xy)\Bigl)

\displaystyle~+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(x\mu_{k+1-i}(y,z)\mu_{i}(y,x)+xy\cdot\mu_{i}(y,\mu_{k+1-i}(x,z))\Bigl)

\displaystyle~+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(y^{2}\mu_{i}(x,\mu_{k+1-i}(x,z))+y\mu_{k+1-i}(x,z)\mu_{i}(y,x)+xy\cdot\mu_{i}(x,\mu_{k+1-i}(y,z))\Bigl)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~y^{2}{\text{obs}}^{(2)}_{k+1}(x,z)+xy\cdot{\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle~+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigl(x\mu_{k+1-i}(x,y)\mu_{i}(z,y)+y\mu_{k+1-i}(x,y)\mu_{i}(z,x)+x\mu_{k+1-i}(y,z)\mu_{i}(y,x)+y\mu_{k+1-i}(x,z)\mu_{i}(y,x)\Bigl)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~y^{2}{\text{obs}}^{(2)}_{k+1}(x,z)+xy\cdot{\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1}(x,y,z).

Moreover, Eq. (
36
) is readily checked. Therefore, we have
\bigl({\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1},{\text{obs}}^{(2)}_{k+1}\bigl)\in XC^{3}_{\rm po}(P)
.
∎

Proposition
.

Let
\bigl(P_{k}[[t]],\mu_{(k)},\omega_{(k)}\bigl)
be a formal deformation of order
k
of a Poisson superalgebra
\bigl(P,\cdot,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
. Let
(\mu_{k+1},\omega_{k+1})\in XC^{2}_{\rm po}(P)
. Then,
\bigl(P_{k+1}[[t]],\mu_{(k+1)},\omega_{(k+1)}\bigl)
is a formal deformation of order

of
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
if and only if

\bigl({\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1},{\text{obs}}^{(2)}_{k+1}\bigl)=d_{\rm po}^{2}\left(\mu_{k+1},~\omega_{k+1}\right),

Proof.

Suppose that
\bigl(P_{k+1}[[t]],\mu_{(k+1)},\omega_{(k+1)}\bigl)
is a formal deformation of order
k+1
of
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
. It follows from
[

, Theorem 6.3]
and Lemma (

\bigl({\text{obs}}^{(1)}_{k+1},{\text{obs}}^{(2)}_{k+1}\bigl)=d_{\rm po}^{2}\left(\mu_{k+1},~\omega_{k+1}\right).
The equivalence follows from the fact that we are working over

.
∎

In the following Proposition, we investigate equivalent deformations and show that equivalent infinitesimal deformations are classified by the even part of the second cohomology space.

Proposition
.

Let
k\geq 1
, and let
\bigl(P_{k}[[t]],\mu_{(k)},\omega_{(k)}\bigl)
and
\bigl(P_{k}[[t]],\mu_{(k)}^{\prime},\omega_{(k)^{\prime}}\bigl)
be two equivalent deformations of order
k
of
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
. Then,

(
i
)

we have
(\mu_{1},\omega_{1})\equiv(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{1}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\rm po}(P)_{\bar{0}};

(
ii
)

the second cohomology space
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\rm po}(P)_{\bar{0}}
classifies up to equivalence the infinitesimal deformations of
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
.

Proof.

Let us prove the first item. Let

(\mu_{1},\omega_{1})=(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{1}^{\prime})+d^{1}_{\rm po}\psi_{1}.
The second item is a straightforward corollary of the first one.

4.6.
The universal enveloping algebra of a Poisson superalgebra

This section aims at defining the universal enveloping algebra of a Poisson superalgebra in characteristic
p=2
. We follow the constructions of
[
La
]
and show that the universal enveloping algebra can be realized as the universal enveloping algebra of an associated Lie-Rinehart superalgebra.

4.6.1.
Universal enveloping algebra

Let
(P,\{-,-\},s)
be a Poisson superalgebra. The
universal enveloping algebra
of
(P,\{-,-\},s)
is a triple
(U(P),m,h)
, where
U
is an associative superalgebra,
m:P\rightarrow U(P)
is an even superalgebras morphism,
h:P\rightarrow U(P)
is an even Lie superalgebras morphism (where
U(P)
is seen as a Lie superalgebra with the commutator and the square power) such that

(41)

\displaystyle h(xy)

\displaystyle=m(x)h(y)+m(y)h(x),

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in P;

(42)

\displaystyle m\bigl(\{x,y\}\bigl)

\displaystyle=m(x)h(y)+h(y)m(x),

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in P;

(43)

\displaystyle m(y)h(x)m(x)

\displaystyle=m(x)m(y)h(x),

\displaystyle\forall x\in P_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in P_{\bar{0}};

(44)

\displaystyle m(y)^{2}h(x)

\displaystyle=m(y)h(x)m(y)+m(x)m(y)h(y)

\displaystyle~~\quad+h(y)m(x)m(y),

\displaystyle\forall x\in P_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in P_{\bar{0}},

which is universal among triples
(B,m^{\prime},h^{\prime})
satisfying the same properties.
Clearly stated, the universal property reads as follows.

(45)

For any triple
(B,m^{\prime},h^{\prime})
where
B
is an associative superalgebra,

m^{\prime}:P\to B
is an even superalgebra morphism, and

h^{\prime}:P\to B
is an even Lie superalgebra morphism satisfying
conditions (
41
)–(
44
), there exists an unique even
associative superalgebra morphism
f:U(P)\to B
such that
f\circ h=h^{\prime}
and
f\circ m=m^{\prime}
.

Let
(P,\{-,-\},s)
be a a Poisson superalgebra
with unit
(for the associative multiplication). The rest of this section is devoted to construct
\bigl(U(P),m,h\bigl)
explicitly by considering the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie-Rinehart superalgebra of Kähler differentials of
P
.

4.6.2.
Lie-Rinehart superalgebra of Kähler differentials in characteristic
2

Let
A
be an associative algebra
with unit
. We denote by
S
the free
A
-module spanned by symbols
\mathrm{d}a
for all homogeneous
a\in A
. The degree is given by
|\mathrm{d}a|=|a|
. Let

R
be the ideal of

generated by elements of the form

\mathrm{d}(\lambda a+\mu b)+\lambda\mathrm{d}a+\mu\mathrm{d}b,~\mathrm{d}(ab)+a\mathrm{d}b+b\mathrm{d}a,\text{ and }~\mathrm{d}\lambda,~\forall a,b\in A,~\forall\lambda,\mu\in\K.

We denote by
\Omega_{A}:=S/R
the

The Kähler differentials satisfy the following universal property. For every even derivation
D:A\rightarrow A

\hat{D}:\Omega_{A}\rightarrow A
such that
\hat{D}\circ\mathrm{d}=D
. Thus, for any

-module

\mathrm{d}
yields a group isomorphism (see
[
La

(46)

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{A}\bigl(\Omega_{A},M\bigl)\cong\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits(A,M).

Let
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
be a Poisson superalgebra in characteristic
2

with unit
. Then, one can consider its module of Kähler differentials
\Omega_{P}
. In the following, we show that there is a natural Lie-Rinehart superalgebra associated to the pair
(P,\Omega_{P})
, see also
[

and
[

for the characteristic

[

[

Lemma
.

Let
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
be a Poisson superalgebra in characteristic
2
and let
\Omega_{P}
be its module of Kähler differentials. Then
\bigl(\Omega_{P},[-,-],\widetilde{s}\bigl)
is a Lie superalgebra with the bracket

(47)

\bigl[x\mathrm{d}y,z\mathrm{d}w\bigl]:=xz\mathrm{d}\{y,w\}+x\{y,z\}\mathrm{d}w+z\{w,x\}\mathrm{d}y,~\forall x\mathrm{d}y,z\mathrm{d}w\in\Omega_{P};

and the squaring defined by

(48)

\begin{cases}\widetilde{s}(x\mathrm{d}y):=x^{2}\mathrm{d}s(y)+x\{x,y\}\mathrm{d}y,&~\forall x\in P_{\bar{0}},~y\in P_{\bar{1}};\\
\widetilde{s}(x\mathrm{d}y):=x\{x,y\}\mathrm{d}y,&~\forall x\in P_{\bar{1}},~y\in P_{\bar{0}}.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove the Lemma in the case where
x
is even and
y
is odd. Let
z\mathrm{d}w\in\Omega_{P}
. We have

(49)

\displaystyle\bigl[\widetilde{s}(x\mathrm{d}y),zdw\bigl]=

\displaystyle~x^{2}zd\{s(y),w\}+x^{2}\{s(y),z\}\mathrm{d}w+xz\{x,y\}\mathrm{d}\{y,w\}

\displaystyle+x\{x,y\}\{y,z\}\mathrm{d}w+xz\{w,\{x,y\}\mathrm{d}y+z\{x,y\}\{w,x\}\mathrm{d}y.

Moreover, we have

(50)

\displaystyle\bigl[x\mathrm{d}y,xz\mathrm{d}\{y,w\}\bigl]=

\displaystyle~x^{2}z\mathrm{d}\{y,\{y,w\}\}+x^{2}\{y,z\}\mathrm{d}\{y,w\}

\displaystyle+xz\{y,x\}\mathrm{d}\{y,w\}+xz\{\{y,w\},x\}\mathrm{d}y;

(51)

\displaystyle\bigl[x\mathrm{d}y,x\{y,z\}\mathrm{d}w\bigl]=

\displaystyle~x^{2}\{y,z\}\mathrm{d}\{y,w\}+x^{2}\{y,\{y,z\}\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle+x\{y,z\}\{y,x\}\mathrm{d}w+x\{y,z\}\{w,x\}\mathrm{d}y;

(52)

\displaystyle\bigl[x\mathrm{d}y,z\{w,z\}\mathrm{d}y\bigl]=

\displaystyle~xz\{y,\{w,x\}\}\mathrm{d}y+x\{y,z\}\{w,x\}\mathrm{d}y+z\{x,y\}\{w,x\}\mathrm{d}y.

Comparing Eqs. (
49
)-(

\bigl[\widetilde{s}(x\mathrm{d}y),z\mathrm{d}w\bigl]=\bigl[x\mathrm{d}y,\bigl[x\mathrm{d}y,z\mathrm{d}w\bigl]\bigl].

Thus, it follows from Proposition
2.1.4.  Proposition
that
\bigl(\Omega_{P},[-,-],\tilde{s}\bigl)
is a Lie superalgebra.
∎

Proposition
.

Let
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
be a Poisson superalgebra in characteristic
2
and let
\Omega_{P}
be its module of Kähler differentials viewed as a Lie superalgebra with the bracket (
47
) and the squaring (
48
) and endowed with the Lie superalgebras map

(53)

\rho:\Omega_{P}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits_{\rm{asso}}(P),~x\mathrm{d}y\mapsto x\{y,-\}.

Then,
(P,\Omega_{P},\rho)
is a Lie-Rinehart superalgebra.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in P
. There are four cases to consider, depending on the parity of the elements.

The case where
y
is odd and
x,z
are even.
In that case, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{s}(z\cdot x\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=(zx)^{2}\mathrm{d}s(y)+zx\{zx,y\}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=z^{2}x^{2}\mathrm{d}s(y)+z^{2}x\{x,y\}\mathrm{d}y+zx^{2}\{z,y\}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=z^{2}\widetilde{s}(x\mathrm{d}y)+\rho(z\cdot x\mathrm{d}y)(z)x\mathrm{d}y.

The case where
x
is odd and
y,z
are even.
In that case, we have

\widetilde{s}(z\cdot x\mathrm{d}y)=z^{2}x\{x,y\}\mathrm{d}y=z^{2}\widetilde{s}(x\mathrm{d}y)+\rho(z\cdot x\mathrm{d}y)(z)x\mathrm{d}y.

The case where
z
is odd and
x,y
are even.
In that case, we have

\widetilde{s}(z\cdot x\mathrm{d}y)=z^{2}x\{x,y\}\mathrm{d}y=\rho(z\cdot x\mathrm{d}y)(z)x\mathrm{d}y.

The case where
x,y,z
are odd.
In that case, both
\widetilde{s}(z\cdot x\mathrm{d}y)
and
\rho(z\cdot x\mathrm{d}y)(z)x\mathrm{d}y

The conclusion follows from Lemma
3.1.1.  Lemma
.

Theorem
.

Let
\bigl(P,\{-,-\},s\bigl)
be a Poisson superalgebra in characteristic

be its module of Kähler differentials. Then, the universal enveloping algebra

of the Lie-Rinehart superalgebra
(P,\Omega_{P},\rho)
satisfies the universal property (

Proof.

Denote by
i_{P}:P\rightarrow U(P,\Omega_{P})
and
i_{\Omega}:\Omega_{P}\rightarrow U(P,\Omega_{P})
the canonical inclusions and let
j=i_{\Omega}\circ\mathrm{d}

). It follows from the construction of

that
i_{P}
and
j
are even and that
i_{p}
is an associative superalgebras morphism. Let us show that
j
is a morphism of Lie superalgebras. Let
x\in P_{\bar{1}}
. we have

j\bigl(s(x)\bigl)=i_{\Omega}\circ\widetilde{s}(\mathrm{d}x)=i_{\Omega}(\mathrm{d}x)^{2}=j(x)^{2}.

We also have
j\bigl(\{x,y\}\bigl)=j(x)j(y)+j(y)j(x)
for any
x,y\in P
, see
[
La
, Proposition 6.3]
. Thus, the map
j
is a morphism of Lie superalgebras. Similarly to
[
La
, Proposition 6.3]
, we have

(54)

j(xy)=i_{P}(x)j(y)+i_{P}(y)j(x),\quad\forall x,y\in P,

and

(55)

i_{P}\bigl(\{x,y\}\bigl)=j(x)i_{P}(y)+i_{P}(y)j(x),\quad\forall x,y\in P.

Note that taking
x=y
in Eq.(
55

j(x)i_{P}(x)=i_{P}(x)j(x),~\forall x\in P
.
Let
x\in P_{\bar{1}}
and
y\in P_{\bar{0}}
. Let us prove that
i_{P}
and
j
satisfy Eq. (
43

i_{P}(y)j(x)i_{P}(x)=i_{P}(y)i_{P}(x)j(x)=i_{P}(x)i_{P}(y)j(x),

therefore Eq. (
43
) is satisfied. Finally, let us consider Eq. (
44

\forall x,y,z\in P
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(\mathrm{d}x)(y)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=[\mathrm{d}x,y\mathrm{d}z]+y[\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}z]=2y\mathrm{d}\{x,z\}+\{x,y\}\mathrm{d}z=\{x,y\}\mathrm{d}z.

It follows that
\rho(\mathrm{d}x)(y)\mathrm{d}z=\{x,y\}\mathrm{d}z=\rho(\mathrm{d}y)(x)\mathrm{d}z.,

\forall x,y,z\in P
.
Let
x\in P_{\bar{1}}
and
y\in P_{\bar{0}}
. We have

i_{P}(y)i_{P}(y)j(x)=i_{P}(y)\bigl(j(x)i_{p}(y)+i_{P}(\rho(\mathrm{d}x)(y))\bigl)=i_{P}(y)j(x)i_{p}(y)+i_{P}(y)i_{P}\bigl(\rho(\mathrm{d}x)(y)\bigl)

\displaystyle i_{P}(x)i_{P}(y)j(y)

\displaystyle=i_{P}(y)i_{P}(x)j(y)

\displaystyle=i_{P}(y)j(y)i_{P}(x)+i_{P}(y)i_{P}\bigl(\rho(\mathrm{d}y)(x)\bigl)

\displaystyle=j(y)i_{P}(y)i_{P}(x)+i_{P}(y)i_{P}\bigl(\rho(\mathrm{d}y)(x)\bigl).

It follows that

\displaystyle i_{P}(y)i_{P}(y)j(x)+i_{P}(x)i_{P}(y)j(y)=~

\displaystyle i_{P}(y)j(x)i_{p}(y)+i_{P}(y)i_{P}\bigl(\rho(\mathrm{d}x)(y)\bigl)

\displaystyle+j(y)i_{P}(y)i_{P}(x)+i_{P}(y)i_{P}\bigl(\rho(\mathrm{d}y)(x)\bigl)

\displaystyle=i_{P}(y)j(x)i_{p}(y)+j(y)i_{P}(y)i_{P}(x),

44

\bigl(U(P,\Omega_{P}),i_{\Omega},j\bigl)

(B,m,h)

41

B
can be seen as a
P

h:P\rightarrow B

h(xy)=m(x)h(y)+m(y)h(x)=x\cdot h(y)+y\cdot h(x),

thus

is a derivation with values in
B

\widehat{h}:\Omega_{P}\rightarrow B
such that

. Then, the triple
(B,m,\widehat{h})
satisfy the following properties:

\textup{(}i\textup{)}

m:P\rightarrow B
is an even superalgebras morphism,

\textup{(}ii\textup{)}

\widehat{h}:\Omega_{P}\rightarrow B
is an even Lie superalgebras morphism,

\textup{(}iii\textup{)}

we have
m\circ\rho(\mathrm{d}x)(y)=\widehat{h}(\mathrm{d}x)m(y)+m(y)\widehat{h}(\mathrm{d}x),~\forall x,y\in P

and

A.1.
Zinbiel superalgebras

Following
[
Lo
,
CFKN
]
, a superspace
Z=Z_{\bar{0}}\oplus Z_{\bar{1}}
is called (right) Zinbiel superalgebra if it is equipped with a bilinear product
*
satisfying

(56)

\displaystyle x*(y*z)

\displaystyle=(y*x)*z+(x*y)*z,\quad\forall x,y,z\in Z.

Note that by taking
x=y
in Eq. (
56
), we have
x*(x*z)=2(x*x)*z=0,\quad\forall x,z\in Z.

Proposition
.

Let
(Z,*)
be a right Zinbiel superalgebra. Then

(
i
)

(Z,*)
is left-commutative, that is,
x*(y*z)=y*(x*z),\quad\forall x,y,z\in Z
;

(
ii
)

the product
x\cdot y:=x*y+y*x,~\forall x,y\in Z,
is associative supercommutative.

Proof.

Left-commutativity is a straightforward consequence of Eq. (
56
).
Following Loday’s proof (see
[
Lo
]
) for all
x,y,z\in Z
, we have

\displaystyle x\cdot(y\cdot z)

\displaystyle=(y*x)*z+(x*y)*z

\displaystyle+(x*z)*y+(x*z)*y

\displaystyle+(y*z)*x+(z*y)*x=(x\cdot y)\cdot z.

In particular, if
x=y
, we obtain
x\cdot(x\cdot z)=0=(x\cdot x)\cdot z,\quad\forall x,z\in Z.

∎

Theorem

(
[
CFKN
, Theorem 5.11]
)
.

Any finite-dimensional Zinbiel superalgebra
(Z,*)
over any field is nilpotent.

[

]

Proposition
.

Let
(P,*,\triangleright)
be a pre-Poisson superalgebra. Then,
(P,\cdot,[-,-],s)
is a Poisson superalgebra, with the associative supercommutative structure
(\cdot)
defined in Prop.
A.1.1.  Proposition

([-,-],s)

A.2.1.  Proposition

Proof.

It is well known that the bracket

[
A
]
. It remains to check Condition
(ii)
of Definition
4.1
. Let
x\in P_{\bar{1}},~y\in P_{\bar{0}}

\displaystyle s(x\cdot y)=

\displaystyle~(x*y)\triangleright(x*y)+(x*y)\triangleright(y*x)+(y*x)\triangleright(x*y)+(y*x)\triangleright(y*x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~x*(y\triangleright(x*y))+y*(x\triangleright(x*y))+x*(y\triangleright(y*x))+y*(x\triangleright(y*x)),

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\cdot[x,y]=

\displaystyle~(x*y)*(x\triangleright y)+(x*y)*(y\triangleright x)+(y*x)*(x\triangleright y)+(y*x)*(y\triangleright x)

\displaystyle+(x\triangleright y)*(x*y)+(x\triangleright y)*(y*x)+(y\triangleright x)*(x*y)+(y\triangleright x)*(y*x).

\displaystyle=x\triangleright(x*(y*y))

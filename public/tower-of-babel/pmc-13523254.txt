Effects of cold plasma treatment on the development and physiological performance of Eucalyptus maidenii from seedling to sapling
Authors: Zhenmin He, Zhengxiong Zhao, Lingrong Kong, Zai Shi, Zhuangyue Lu, Tingbiao Liu, Shaonian Lv, Yufan Hou, Yulan Xu, Guanben Du, Zilin Cao, Xiaoli Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to evaluate the effects of cold plasma treatment on the germination, seedling growth, sapling development, and physiological responses of Eucalyptus maidenii seeds, and to identify an optimal treatment combination. By integrating germination tests, seedling cultivation experiments, and sapling growth assessments, the study explored both growth performance and underlying physiological mechanisms.The results showed that cold plasma treatment generally promoted seed germination and improved seed vigor. It also enhanced seedling morphological traits and photosynthetic performance, indicating a positive effect on early growth. During the sapling stage, cold plasma treatment further improved several growth characteristics and influenced key physiological and metabolic parameters, including photosynthetic enzyme activity, osmotic regulation substances, chlorophyll accumulation, and oxidative stress indicators. These findings suggest that appropriate cold plasma treatment can stimulate growth not only at the germination stage, but also throughout subsequent seedling and sapling development. Comprehensive evaluation using the TOPSIS model identified S14 (150 W, 2 n, 15 s, 2 d) as the optimal treatment. Overall, appropriate cold plasma treatment can effectively enhance the germination rate of seeds, the growth of seedlings and saplings, as well as their photosynthetic and antioxidant capabilities of Eucalyptus maidenii, showing promising potential for seed enhancement and nursery production.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12870-026-09027-2.

Introduction

E. maidenii, an economically significant timber species introduced to Yunnan Province, demonstrates considerable silvicultural value [1]. In plantation forestry, seeds and seedlings serve as primary propagation materials for stand establishment. However, E. maidenii propagation faces multiple constraints: limited availability of vigorous seedlings, elevated seed costs, inconsistent seedling quality, and variable post-plantation performance [2]. Furthermore, its biological characteristics-small seed size, weak seedling vigor, and slow initial growth-significantly hinder efficient nursery production and plantation success [3]. This study selected E. maidenii due to its dual relevance: practical importance in plantation forestry and distinct propagation bottlenecks during early growth phases that may be ameliorated through seed pretreatment technologies.

Cold plasma (CP), an eco-friendly physical treatment, has demonstrated efficacy in enhancing multiple growth parameters including seed germination, vigor, seedling development, and plant survival rates [4–9]. Emerging evidence indicates CP’s beneficial effects persist throughout later developmental stages [10–11]. In agricultural systems, CP treatment typically enhances hydration capacity, modifies seed coat permeability, and activates metabolic pathways, collectively improving germination efficiency and seedling establishment [12–16]. These findings position CP as a promising seed pretreatment technology with substantial application potential. Beyond germination and growth enhancement, CP treatment improves photosynthetic efficiency and physiological conditioning. CP treatment elevates key photosynthetic indicators including chlorophyll content (Chl), net photosynthetic rate (Pn), stomatal conductance (Gs), and water use efficiency (WUE), while optimizing light energy utilization through improved chlorophyll fluorescence parameters [17–18]. Plasma technologies additionally enhance crop productivity, mitigate biotic or abiotic stress impacts, and induce systemic acquired resistance [19]. Physiologically, osmoprotectants (proline, soluble sugars) mediate stress adaptation through membrane stabilization and metabolic homeostasis, while malondialdehyde (MDA) serves as a biomarker for oxidative membrane damage [20–22]. CP treatment consistently demonstrates dual modulation: upregulating osmoprotectant accumulation while suppressing MDA generation, thereby enhancing seedling physiological resilience [23–26].

Although significant progress has been made, the majority of CP research has concentrated on agricultural crops, primarily examining seed germination and yield-related traits, with comparatively limited investigation in forestry species. Current studies in forestry species have predominantly addressed germination and early seedling stages, leaving the long-term impacts of CP treatment on nursery cultivation and early plantation establishment largely unexplored. Specifically for E. maidenii, a comprehensive understanding of how various CP treatment parameters influence both initial growth and subsequent physiological responses remains incomplete. Therefore, in this study, CP-treated E. maidenii seeds were used to evaluate the effects of different treatment factors, including plasma power, frequency, duration, and storage time, on the morphological and physiological characteristics of seedlings and saplings. Quantified physiological parameters included malondialdehyde (MDA), proline (PRO), starch (ST), and soluble sugar (SS) content. This study had two primary objectives: to determine the optimal CP treatment protocol for E. maidenii seeds, and to establish a scientific foundation for producing superior planting material and sustainable plantation management.

Methods

Experimental site overview

The seedling nursery is located at Southwest Forestry University (102°75′E, 25°05′N elevation 1948.5 m). This region exhibits a subtropical-highland mountain monsoon climate with an average temperature of 15℃, approximately 2200 h of annual sunshine, a frost-free period exceeding 240 days, and annual precipitation of 1035 mm. The rainy season spans May to October, accounting for about 85% of annual precipitation. The predominant soil type is red soil [27].

The afforestation site is located at Jinghong Farm, Jinghong City, Yunnan Province (100°55′E, 21°57′N, elevation 600 m). The region exhibits a tropical humid monsoon climate with an average annual temperature of 23.5℃, abundant sunlight, and a frost-free period. Annual precipitation averages 1214 mm, with the rainy season occurring from May to October, accounting for approximately 85% of the annual total. The predominant soil type is lateritic red soil [28].

Experimental materials and setup

The experiment involving CP treatment seeds was conducted in April 2023 at the laboratory of the School of Forestry, Southwest Forestry University. The experimental materials comprised E. maidenii seeds collected from a plantation in Chuxiong Prefecture, Yunnan Province, China. Seeds were selected based on criteria such as fullness, smoothness, integrity, uniform size, and the absence of obvious defects. The HD-2NCP seed treatment machine, manufactured by Changzhou Zhongke Changtai Plasma Technology, operated on 220 V 50 Hz AC power. The treatment procedure commenced with the activation of the vacuum pump, which was turned off once the vacuum reached 100 Pa. The RF power supply was then activated, and the treatment power was set. Following this, the frequency-controlled motor was turned on, and the conveyor belt speed was verified. Seeds were evenly spread on the glass plate, and the valve was opened (Fig. 1). The glass plate conveyed the seeds through the glow discharge zone for treatment. After treatment, the RF power supply, vacuum pump, and variable-frequency motor were sequentially shut down. The system was vented, the valve was opened (Fig. 1), and the seed treatment process was completed [29]. Experimental methods

This study employed E. maidenii seeds as the experimental material, with 0.5 g assigned per treatment group. Sixteen cold plasma (CP) treatment combinations (S1-S16) were designed by crossing four parameters: (1) Power (P), (2) Frequency (N), (3) Duration (S), and (4) Storage time (T). The tested parameter ranges were: P: 60 W, 90 W, 120 W, 150 W; N: 1 n, 2 n, 3 n, 4 n; S: 5 s, 10 s, 15 s, 20 s; T: 2 d, 4 d, 6 d, 8 d. Each treatment code corresponded to a unique combination of all four parameters. Representative examples include: S1: 60 W, 1 n, 5 s, 2 d; S10: 120 W, 2 n, 15 s, 6 d; S14: 150 W, 2 n, 15 s, 2 d.The specific parameter combinations are detailed in Table 1, with untreated seeds as the control (CK).

Measurement items and methods

Seed germination: CP-treated seeds were placed in 12 cm diameter Petri dishes containing two moistened filter paper layers (100 seeds per dish, with three replicates per treatment combination). The Petri dishes were incubated at 25℃. Germination was defined as radicle emergence ≥ 2 mm, and the test concluded after three consecutive days without new germination. Germination counts were recorded daily over a 30-day period. For the seedling growth experiment, only seeds synchronously germinated (radicle length ≥ 2 mm) were selected to ensure uniform developmental stages.This approach standardized seedling growth assessments by eliminating variability from asynchronous germination.Germination parameters-including germination rate (GR), germination potential (GP), germination index (GI), and vigor index (VI)-were calculated as follows [30–31]:

where N is the total number of seeds tested, Ng is the total number of germinated seeds at the end of the experiment, Np is the cumulative number of germinated seeds on the day of peak germination, Gt is the number of seeds germinated on day t, Dt is the corresponding germination day, and S is the seedling dry weight.

Seedling growth and physiological measurements: In May 2023, seedlings were cultured in 9 cm×12 cm non-woven fabric pots containing a 1:2 (v/v) mixture of red soil and humus soil. CP-treated seeds were sown directly into the containers.The experiment was conducted in a greenhouse under natural light conditions, with temperatures maintained at 22–28℃ and relative humidity ranging from 25% to 55%. Following emergence, a single uniform seedling was maintained per container. Seventeen experimental treatments were established in a completely randomized design with three replicates per treatment (30 seedlings per replicate). At 90 days post-sowing, morphological parameters were measured: ground diameter (GD) with digital calipers, seeding height (SH) with a metric ruler, and seedling crown width (CWs) with a measuring tape.

For physiological analyses, nine plants per replicate (27 plants per treatment) were randomly sampled.Leaf samples were obtained from the third pair of fully expanded leaves from the shoot apex of each sampled plant. On a clear and windless day between 09:00 and 11:00 h, net photosynthetic rate (Pn), transpiration rate (Tr), intercellular CO2 concentration (Ci), stomatal conductance (Gs), and water use efficiency (WUE) were measured using a LI-6400 portable photosynthesis system (LI-COR, USA). Concurrently, chlorophyll fluorescence parameters were determined using a Handy PEA handheld chlorophyll fluorometer, including initial fluorescence (Fo), maximum fluorescence (Fm), variable fluorescence (Fv = Fm-Fo), PSII potential efficiency (Fv/Fo), and maximum quantum efficiency of PSII (Fv/Fm) [32].

Sapling growth and physiological measurements: The E. maidenii plantation was established at Jinghong Farm, Jinghong City, using container-grown seedlings planted in August 2023. The experimental site has the following climatic characteristics: mean annual temperature of 15℃, annual sunshine duration of 2200 h, frost-free period exceeding 240 days, and annual precipitation of 1035 mm (85% occurring between May and October). A completely randomized block design with 17 treatments and three replicates was implemented. Each treatment consisted of 30 plants spaced at 2 × 3 m (within and between rows). Seedlings were planted in 60 × 60 × 60 cm pits using the open-pit method. Planting depth was maintained 2 cm above the seedlings’ original soil mark. After planting, no supplemental irrigation, fertilization, or field management was applied post-planting; plants grew under natural conditions. At 180 days post-planting, saplings were assessed for: total height (TH), basal diameter (BD), sapling crown width (CWsap), diameter at breast height (DBH), and height to crown base (HCB) [33]. Chlorophyll content (Chl) was measured using an OPTI-SCIENCES CCM-200 PLUS chlorophyll meter. For analyses, nine healthy saplings with uniform growth were randomly selected per replicate (27 per treatment), controlling for light exposure. Leaves were analyzed for: malondialdehyde (MDA), proline (PRO), soluble sugars (SS), starch (ST) [34], photosynthetic enzyme activity. Photosynthetic enzyme activities were measured using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), including triose phosphate isomerase (TPI), ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase (Rubisco), fructose-1,6-bisphosphate aldolase (FBA), and glutamine synthetase (GS) [35].

Statistical analysis

Data were statistically analyzed using Microsoft Office Excel 2020 (Microsoft Inc., Washington, USA). Differences between data sets were tested using the Tukey HSD method in R software at a significance level of Sig < 0.05. Visualization of the correlation network analysis results was generated using the Tutu Cloud platform (http://cloudtutu.com.cn/). The TOPSIS model was employed to comprehensively evaluate growth and physiological/metabolic indicators under different CP treatments, identifying the optimal solution for cultivating E. maidenii seeds, seedlings, and saplings under various CP treatments.

Results

Effects of CP treatments on seed germination of E. maidenii

Compared with the control (CK), CP treatment significantly enhanced germination parameters in E. maidenii seeds, including GR, GI, GP, and VI. The control values were 47.68% (GR), 47.98 (GI), 42.33% (GP), and 9.76 (VI), respectively. Statistical analysis revealed significant differences (P < 0.05) in GR and GP among treatments (Fig. 3), while GI and VI showed non-significant but increasing trends. The optimal treatment (S14: 150 W, 2 n, 15 s, 2 d) demonstrated the highest values: 91.67% (GR), 92.28 (GI), 84.33% (GP), and 22.67 (VI), representing increases of 92.26%, 92.33%, 99.22%, and 132.27%, respectively, compared to the control. The consistently superior performance of S14 over both the control and other treatments (e.g., S1, S4, S8) indicates that CP treatment effectively enhances germination performance in E. maidenii.

Effects of CP treatment on the growth and photosynthetic physiology of E. maidenii seedlings

Effects of CP treatment on E. Maidenii seedling growth

90 days after sowing, the CP treatment significantly influenced the SH, GD, and CWs of E. maidenii seedlings (P < 0.05)(Fig. 4). The control (CK) values for SH, GD, and CWs were 5.41 cm, 0.10 cm, and 4.18 cm, respectively. Among the treatments, S7 (90 W, 3 N, 20 s, 2 d) exhibited the highest values for SH (17.07 cm), GD (0.14 cm) and CWs (11.84 cm). Compared to the CK, S7 enhanced SH, GD, and CWs by 215.53%, 40.00%, and 183.25%, respectively. In summary, S7 demonstrated optimal morphological development at the seedling stage.

Effects of CP treatment on photosynthetic characteristics of E. maidenii seedlings

The CP treatment exerted significant effects on photosynthetic and chlorophyll fluorescence parameters in E. maidenii seedlings (Fig. 5). Compared to the control (CK), most CP treatments enhanced the Pn, Gs, Ci, Tr, Fo, Fv/Fm, and Fv/Fo. However, WUE responses showed treatment-dependent variation. Peak values were recorded for Pn (15.74 µmol·m− 2·s− 1, S4), WUE (3.26%, S8), and Fo (350.11, S3). Conversely, treatment S14 exhibited maximal values for Gs (0.59 µmol·m− 2·s− 1), Ci (291.43 µmol· mol− 1), Tr (6.98 µmol·m− 2·s− 1), Fv/Fm (0.84), and Fv/Fo (5.42). WUE showed increases in S2 (3.22%), S3 (2.98%), S4 (3.20%), and S8 (3.26%), while declining in other treatments. The minimal WUE (2.17%, S14) represented a 17.49% reduction compared to CK (2.63%).

RDA analysis and correlation analysis of growth morphological and physiological characteristics of E. maidenii seedling and sapling with treatment factors

Redundancy analysis (RDA) was employed to assess the relationships between CP treatment factors and various growth and physiological parameters. Regarding seedling growth and photosynthetic characteristics, RDA1 and RDA2 accounted for 93.15% of the total variance, with SH and CWs being the primary contributors to RDA1 (Fig. 6A). For sapling carbohydrate content and photosynthetic enzyme activity, RDA1 and RDA2 explained 84.05% of the variance, where TH, SS, PRO, and ST showed the strongest contributions to RDA1 (Fig. 6B).

Correlation analysis revealed significant positive relationships between Pn and Tr, as well as between ST and multiple growth or photosynthetic parameters (SS, PRO, CWsap, DBH, Fv/Fm, and Fv/Fo). Additionally, DBH, Pn, Ci, Tr, Fv/Fm, and Fv/Fo exhibited positive correlations with SS. Conversely, Ci and Tr showed negative correlations with WUE, while PRO, HCB, and Chl were negatively correlated with MDA.

Network analysis further demonstrated strong associations among photosynthetic enzyme activities, chlorophyll content, photosynthetic parameters, fluorescence parameters, and MDA in saplings (Fig. 6A). TPI was closely associated with HCB and WUE, whereas Rubisco was associated with MDA, Tr, Ci, and Chl. TPI displayed close associations with HCB and WUE, whereas Rubisco was linked to MDA, Tr, Ci, and Chl. FBA correlated with Gs, DBH, HCB, Chl, and SS, while GS was associated with BD, HCB, Chl, and SS. These findings indicate coordinated relationships among growth, photosynthesis, and physiological metabolism in E. maidenii under CP treatment.

Analysis of physiological metabolic mechanisms in seedling and sapling of E. maidenii

CP treatment significantly enhances seed germination, seedling growth, and sapling development in E. maidenii. To investigate the physiological mechanisms, this study examined photosynthetic and metabolic responses. This results demonstrate that CP treatment breaks seed dormancy and stimulates germination, leading to significant increases in both germination rate and index (Fig. 7). These effects persist through later developmental stages, as evidenced by enhanced photosynthetic rate, elevated enzyme activity, and increased Chl, SS, and ST content. This metabolic shift boosts carbohydrate synthesis, facilitating greater accumulation of photosynthetic products that drive plant growth. Furthermore, CP treatment reduces MDA accumulation while elevating PRO levels, thereby strengthening antioxidant capacity and stress tolerance-key factors in growth promotion. In conclusion, the growth-promoting effects of optimal CP treatment in E. maidenii are mediated through two primary mechanisms: augmented photosynthetic efficiency and improved stress resilience.

Discussion

Study on the Effects of CP Treatment on Seed Germination in E. maidenii

CP treatment has been demonstrated to enhance seed vigor [36]. In this study, CP treatment at different doses significantly enhanced both germination rate and seed vigor in E. maidenii compared to controls (P < 0.05), consistent with previous reports [37]. This effect appears mediated by both physical modification of the seed coat and biochemical actions of plasma-generated reactive oxygen (ROS) and nitrogen species (RNS). Studies indicate that optimal ROS and RNS levels can etch seed surfaces, enhance hydrophilicity, and improve permeability to water and gases, thereby facilitating imbibition and early metabolic activation [38–40]. Furthermore, ROS serves as a key germination regulator via interactions with nitric oxide, Ca2+ signaling, MAPK cascades, and phytohormone pathways-particularly the gibberellin (GA) and abscisic acid (ABA) balance that controls dormancy-break and germination [41–43]. Thus, CP’s germination-enhancing effects in E. maidenii likely involve both physical seed coat alterations and RNS-mediated modulation of GA, ABA pathways. However, CP treatment showed limited effects on most germination and viability parameters. This limited response may be related to the relatively high initial germination percentage (> 80%) of the seed lot used in the present study under indoor cultivation conditions, suggesting that the effectiveness of CP treatment depends on seed physiological status, species characteristics, and treatment dose 37 [44]. This interpretation is consistent with recent reviews emphasizing that plasma treatment may be either promotive or inhibitory depending on treatment intensity and seed properties [38–39].

Effects of CP treatment on growth during the seedling and sapling stages of E. maidenii

Selcuk indicated that plasma-generated ROS and RNS can alter seed coat structure, increase seed permeability, and stimulate seed germination, thereby promoting subsequent seedling growth [8–9]. In this study, the seedling-stage traits, including SH, GD, and CWs, were all significantly elevated compared with the CK group. Rather than reflecting a single direct mechanism, these responses may represent the cumulative outcome of improved early physiological status, including enhanced water uptake, altered hormonal balance, and activation of metabolic processes during early establishment [38–39, 45]. CP treatment resulted in a smaller increase in GD than in SH and CB during the seedling stage of E. maidenii, suggesting that height growth and canopy expansion were more responsive than radial growth under the present treatment conditions. During the seedling stage, the maximum GD and CWs values were recorded in the S10 group, whereas the highest PH value was observed in the S7 group. In the sapling stage, the maximum values of DBH and HCB were observed in different treatment combinations, and TH exhibited a growth pattern that was not fully consistent with DBH and HCB. This discrepancy may reflect stage-specific priorities in lignification, radial growth, and crown development. During the sapling stage, TH in E. maidenii saplings was significantly greater than that of the CK group in most treatments, whereas HCB showed a different response pattern, suggesting that treatment power and frequency exerted distinct influences on growth traits. Research indicates that frequency has a stronger effect on TH, whereas plasma power showed a closer association with HCB. These differences suggest that distinct CP parameters may regulate apical elongation and crown development through partially different physiological pathways [38, 45].

Effects of CP treatment on photosynthetic performance in E. maidenii seedlings

This study showed that Pn values were higher in CP-treated groups than in the CK group, which is broadly consistent with previous studies reporting that plasma seed treatment can improve subsequent photosynthetic performance [46–47]. Similarly, Gs and Ci were elevated relative to the CK group in several treatments. This might be be because CP priming improved early seed physiological status and subsequently supported chlorophyll accumulation, stomatal regulation, and photochemical activity during seedling growth [39, 45, 48]. As treatment power increased, Pn, Gs, and Tr showed an overall rising trend, suggesting that suitable treatment intensity may enhance photosynthetic efficiency. This enhancement may not result solely from increased seed-surface porosity, but also from ROS and RNS-mediated signaling that stabilizes cellular metabolism, activates antioxidant defense, and supports photosynthetic adjustment under appropriate treatment conditions [38–39]. In addition, CP treatment may influence enzyme activation pathways associated with photosynthesis, including chlorophyll biosynthesis, Calvin-cycle activity, and redox regulation, thereby contributing to improved carbon assimilation [48]. The increase in Tr, together with the decrease in WUE, indicates that CP treatment did not uniformly improve water-use performance in E. maidenii seedlings. This finding contrasts with those of Chi et al. (2005) [17]. Biologically, this pattern is reasonable because increased Gs can simultaneously enhance CO2 uptake and transpirational water loss. Therefore, the increase in Pn does not necessarily result in higher WUE.

Chlorophyll fluorescence (ChlF) serves as an indicator of the photochemical efficiency of photosystem II and reflects plant tolerance to environmental stress as well as possible damage to the photosynthetic apparatus [47]. The ratios Fv/Fo and Fv/Fm are critical metrics for assessing leaf photosynthetic efficiency. Specifically, Fv/Fo reflects the integrity of the photosynthetic apparatus, whereas Fv/Fm indicates the capacity of leaves to utilize absorbed light energy [49]. The relatively small differences in Fv/Fo and Fv/Fm between some treatment groups and the CK group suggest that CP treatment may have improved PSII stability or initial photochemical efficiency under certain conditions, but its effect on maximum photochemical efficiency was limited. This interpretation is more consistent with recent study that plasma treatment modulates photosynthetic performance in a dose-dependent manner rather than uniformly enhancing all fluorescence parameters [39, 45, 48].

Physiological and metabolic Responses in E. maidenii saplings

Malondialdehyde (MDA) is a byproduct of membrane lipid peroxidation and is widely used as an indicator of oxidative damage in plants [49]. Lower levels of MDA are generally associated with enhanced stress tolerance. ST, SS, and PRO are closely related to energy storage and osmotic adjustment [51–53]. In the present study, CP treatment significantly influenced the contents of MDA, SS, ST, and PRO in E. maidenii saplings (P < 0.05), suggesting that seed pretreatment affected redox balance and primary metabolism during later growth stages. The highest levels of SS, ST, PRO, TPI, and Chl were observed in the S14 group. Together with the high values of Ci and Tr, these results suggest that certain high-power treatments enhanced photosynthetic and carbon-assimilation-related processes. At the same time, the low MDA content in S14 may indicate reduced oxidative damage, which may have contributed to the accumulation of SS, ST, PRO, and TPI. In addition, moderate levels of ROS and RNS may trigger adaptive metabolic reprogramming, while excessive amounts of these substances may disrupt the homeostasis of cells [54].

Rubisco is a key enzyme in plant carbon assimilation and regulates photosynthetic CO2 fixation [55–56]. GS is essential for nitrogen metabolism and thus strongly influences photosynthesis and growth [57]. FBA plays an important role in the Calvin cycle and contributes to photosynthetic carbon metabolism [58], whereas triose phosphate isomerase (TPI) participates in the regeneration phase following CO2 fixation [59]. In the present study, Rubisco, FBA, and GS reached their maximum values in groups S1, S3, and S4, respectively, all of which were significantly higher than those in the CK group. These results suggest that CP treatment enhanced the activities of key photosynthetic and nitrogen-metabolic enzymes, these effects were more evident in some lower-power treatment groups, whereas S14, despite its favorable performance in osmotic-regulation-related indices, showed comparatively weaker enzyme responses. This may indicate that different CP treatments promote different physiological strategies, with some favoring growth-related carbon assimilation and others favoring protective metabolite accumulation. Recent studies similarly emphasize that plasma effects on enzyme activity are highly parameter-dependent and may involve both redox regulation and hormone-associated metabolic adjustment [38, 39, 54].

Synergistic enhancement of photosynthesis and physiological metabolism in E. maidenii by CP treatment

Different cold plasma treatments produced varying effects on the photosynthetic and growth indices of E. maidenii, resulting in distinct outcomes in photosynthetic productivity. Significant correlations were identified between photosynthetic enzymes and chlorophyll content, as well as among photosynthetic parameters, fluorescence parameters, and MDA levels in saplings (Fig. 4). Previous studies have shown that photosynthetic rate, physiological metabolism, and plant growth are closely related [60]. In the present study, various CP treatments markedly enhanced the photosynthetic capacity and physiological metabolite accumulation potential of E. maidenii seedlings. Rather than indicating a single dominant mechanism, these relationships support the view that CP acts as a priming stimulus that triggers multiple downstream responses, including ROS and RNS signaling, membrane-associated changes, enzyme activation, and metabolic adjustment [38, 45–48]. In this study improved chlorophyll content and photosynthetic parameters may enhance carbon assimilation, whereas reduced oxidative damage and increased osmotic regulators may help maintain cellular stability during continued growth. Thus, the superior overall performance of S14 may reflect a coordinated improvement in photosynthetic regulation, osmotic adjustment, and oxidative balance, rather than improvement in a single physiological trait alone [43].

Conclusions

In this study, the optimal CP treatment (150 W, 2 n, 15 s, 2 d) demonstrated superior performance in E. maidenii relative to the control. This treatment significantly enhanced seed germination and stimulated growth across both seedling and sapling developmental stages, as evidenced by marked increases in plant height, ground diameter, crown breadth, and diameter at breast height. Furthermore, it significantly elevated the Pn, Fv/Fm, boosted key photosynthesis-related enzyme activities, and facilitated the accumulation of soluble sugars, starch, proline, and chlorophyll. These findings demonstrate that properly optimized CP treatment can effectively enhance both early establishment and subsequent physiological performance in E. maidenii. Nevertheless, because ROS and RNS accumulation, endogenous GA and ABA contents, membrane permeability, and the expression of key metabolic enzymes were not directly measured in the present study, these mechanisms should be regarded as biologically plausible interpretations rather than direct proof. Future studies should combine CP treatment with direct analysis of hormone balance, seed-surface physicochemical properties, membrane integrity, antioxidant systems, and gene expression to further clarify the mechanistic basis of CP-induced growth promotion in E. maidenii.

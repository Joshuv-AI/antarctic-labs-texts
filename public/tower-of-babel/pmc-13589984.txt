Dupilumab Improves Lung Function and Reduces Severe Exacerbations in Patients from China with Type 2 Asthma: A Subgroup Analysis of a Phase 3 Randomized Trial
Authors: Qingling Zhang, Xiuhua Fu, Haohui Fang, Jie Lin, Arman Altincatal, Yvonne Huang, Jason H Kwah, Olivier Ledanois, Nanshan Zhong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeAsthma affects approximately 46 million adults in China. The safety and efficacy of dupilumab, a monoclonal antibody which blocks the shared receptor component for interleukin (IL)-4 and IL-13, were evaluated in a phase 3 study in patients with moderate-to-severe asthma in the Asia–Pacific region (NCT03782532). This is a pre-specified subgroup analysis of patients in China derived from the Asia–Pacific trial.Patients and MethodsPatients (≥12 years) with inadequately controlled asthma despite medium/high-dose inhaled corticosteroids and ≥1 additional controller were included. Patients with type 2 inflammation (eosinophil counts ≥150 cells/µL or fractional exhaled nitric oxide ≥25 parts per billion) without maintenance oral corticosteroids (OCS) (type 2 non-OCS group, for primary efficacy analysis) were randomized 1:1 to receive 200-mg dupilumab every 2 weeks or placebo for 24 weeks. In this pre-specified subgroup analysis, primary efficacy endpoints examined included change from baseline in pre-bronchodilator forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1) to week 12. Additional outcomes assessed included asthma control measures, annualized severe exacerbation rates, and safety.ResultsIn a total of 412 patients randomized from China (206 per treatment group) 347 (84.2%) were included in the primary efficacy analysis. Changes from baseline to week 12 in FEV1 were significantly greater for dupilumab vs placebo (least squares [LS] mean difference: 0.34 L [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.25, 0.43]), as were improvements in 5-Item Asthma Control Questionnaire scores at week 24 (LS mean difference: −0.19 [95% CI: −0.35, −0.03]). Severe exacerbation risk was significantly reduced by 74.2% (risk ratio: 0.258 [95% CI: 0.127, 0.521], P = 0.0002) vs placebo. Treatment-emergent adverse event rates were similar across groups.ConclusionIn this 24-week subgroup analysis, dupilumab improved lung function, asthma control, and exacerbations, and was well tolerated in patients from China with type 2 asthma without maintenance OCS.Keywords: Asia-Pacific, corticosteroids, exacerbation, FEV1, lung function, safety

Introduction

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by variable respiratory symptoms and expiratory airflow limitation.1 Approximately 262 million people worldwide have asthma, with an estimated mortality rate of 455,000 annually.2 In China, asthma prevalence in adults is approximately 4.2%, affecting approximately 46 million individuals,3 with 3–8% having severe disease.4–6 However, asthma in China is thought to be largely underdiagnosed and undertreated, with a recent study identifying that only 29% of adults with asthma had ever been diagnosed by a physician and that only 5.6% had ever been treated with inhaled corticosteroids (ICS).3 The prevalence of asthma has been increasing globally over recent decades,7,8 and the combination of urbanization, aging populations, and changing environmental and lifestyle factors is expected to continue to substantially increase the prevalence of asthma in China in the future.3,9,10 Patients from China remain underrepresented in global clinical trials, and distinctive local features including rising prevalence, air pollution burden, and diagnosis/treatment gaps may affect phenotype distribution and clinical outcomes in this population.11–13 Therefore, substantial public health interventions and therapies are needed to reduce asthma morbidity and mortality in China.

Asthma is a heterogeneous disease with multiple contributing pathways. Type 2 inflammation, characterized by elevated eosinophils, immunoglobulin E (IgE), or fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO), underlies disease in up to 88% of adults with asthma.14 For patients with uncontrolled asthma despite medium-to-high dose ICS with a second controller, biologic therapies are recommended based on biomarker levels: anti-interleukin (IL)-5/IL-4 receptor for elevated eosinophils, anti-IgE for elevated IgE, and anti–IL-4 receptor for elevated FeNO.1

Dupilumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody, blocks the shared receptor component for IL-4 and IL-13, key and central drivers of type 2 inflammation in multiple diseases, including asthma.15,16 In China, dupilumab was approved in 2023 for the treatment of asthma in patients ≥12 years of age.17 In a recent phase 3 study on the efficacy and safety of dupilumab in patients with moderate-to-severe asthma living in the Asia–Pacific region (NCT03782532), dupilumab significantly improved both lung function and asthma control in patients with evidence of either type 2 inflammation or oral corticosteroid (OCS) maintenance use.18 As maintenance OCS use has the potential to significantly influence the levels of type 2 biomarkers, thereby affecting whether patients remain eligible for biologic therapy,19 we present a pre-specified subgroup analysis of the Asia–Pacific phase 3 trial. This analysis evaluated the efficacy and safety of dupilumab in patients from China with moderate-to-severe asthma with evidence of type 2 inflammation and no OCS maintenance (type 2 non-OCS group).

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Participants

This pre-specified subgroup analysis was performed using data from a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 study (NCT03782532) which was conducted at 67 sites, 54 of which were in China and 13 in India, between March 2019 and May 2022. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards laid down in the Declaration of Helsinki, Principles of Good Clinical Practice, and applicable local regulations. The local institutional review board or ethics committee at each study center oversaw trial conduct and documentation. All patients, or their parents/guardians, provided written informed consent before participating in the trial. Assent was also obtained from all adolescents included in the trial.

Full details of the study design and patient inclusion criteria have been previously published.18 Patients were eligible for inclusion in the current analysis if they were enrolled at sites in China, were ≥12 years of age, and had inadequately controlled asthma despite receiving medium/high-dose ICS and ≥1 other controller.18 Eligible patients met one of these two criteria: (1) elevated type 2 inflammatory biomarkers (blood eosinophils ≥150 cells/µL or FeNO ≥25 ppb) without maintenance OCS at baseline (type 2 non-OCS group) or (2) maintenance OCS use at baseline (OCS maintenance group). Of 412 randomized patients, 347 (84.2%) were included in the primary efficacy analysis (type 2 non-OCS group), while 65 patients were excluded as they were either on maintenance OCS (34 patients) or lacked evidence of type 2 inflammation without maintenance OCS (31 patients). Biomarker endpoints were evaluated only in the type 2 non-OCS group (n=347), while safety endpoints were assessed in all randomized patients (overall safety population, n=412) (Figure 1). Across the overall study population, eligible patients were randomized 1:1 to receive add-on subcutaneous dupilumab 200 mg every 2 weeks (q2w) with a one-time 400-mg loading dose (type 2 non-OCS group only), dupilumab 300 mg q2w with a one-time 600-mg loading dose (OCS maintenance group only), or volume-matched placebo (1.14 mL for type 2 non-OCS group and 2 mL for OCS maintenance group) for 24 weeks. Use of background controller medication was permitted throughout the study.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint of the study was absolute change from baseline in pre-bronchodilator forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1) at week 12. Secondary efficacy endpoints included change from baseline to week 24 in 5-item Asthma Control Questionnaire (ACQ-5) and Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire (AQLQ) scores, percentage of patients decreasing in ACQ-5 score by the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) of 0.5 or greater and annualized severe exacerbation rate. A severe exacerbation event during the study was defined as deterioration of asthma requiring either systemic corticosteroids ≥3 days for participants not on OCS maintenance treatment or hospitalization/emergency room visit because of asthma requiring systemic corticosteroids. Safety endpoints included rates of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), serious adverse events (SAEs), adverse events of special interest (AESIs), and TEAEs leading to permanent study discontinuation, treatment withdrawal, or death. AESIs included hypersensitivity reactions or any severe or serious infection. Severity of TEAEs was evaluated as mild, moderate, or severe; potential relatedness to the investigational product (dupilumab or placebo) was determined by the investigators. Pharmacodynamics of biomarkers (blood eosinophil counts, total IgE, and FeNO) were evaluated as exploratory endpoints and analyzed descriptively. Formal statistical comparisons were not performed for these endpoints.

Statistical Analysis

The sample size for the overall study was based on power calculations for the primary endpoint in all patients (including patients outside of China) with evidence of type 2 inflammation and with no maintenance OCS, with 90% power to detect a 0.15-L difference in absolute change from baseline in pre-bronchodilator FEV1 at week 12 at a two-tailed significance level of α=0.05. Additionally, the study also enrolled 48 OCS maintenance participants (approximately 10% of the total population) who received 300 mg q2w regimen (or matching placebo).

Efficacy analysis was performed for the primary analysis population, which included all patients from China with evidence of type 2 inflammation and with no maintenance OCS (type 2 non-OCS group). Change from baseline in FEV1 up to week 24 was evaluated by least squares (LS) mean difference for dupilumab vs placebo using mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) analysis including the change from baseline in pre-bronchodilator FEV1 value as the response variable and intervention, age, sex, height, screening blood eosinophil level, screening FeNO level, visit, intervention by-visit interaction, baseline pre-bronchodilator FEV1 value, and baseline value-by-visit interaction as covariates. Change in ACQ-5 and AQLQ scores were also evaluated using MMRM analysis, with change from baseline in ACQ-5/AQLQ score up to week 24 as the response variable and intervention, age, screening blood eosinophil level, screening FeNO level, visit, intervention by-visit interaction, baseline ACQ-5/AQLQ score value, and baseline value-by-visit interaction as covariates. A negative binomial regression model was used to evaluate differences between dupilumab and placebo in annualized severe exacerbation rates, with the total number of events from randomization up to the week 24 visit or last contact date (whichever was earlier) as the response variable; intervention, age, region, screening blood eosinophil level, screening FeNO level, baseline ICS dose, and number of severe exacerbation events within 1 year prior to the study as covariates; and log-transformed standardized observation duration as an offset variable. Safety was evaluated descriptively for the safety population (patients at Chinese sites who received at least a partial dupilumab dose); biomarker analysis excluded those on maintenance OCS. All analyses were performed using SAS software, version 9.4.

Results

In total, 412 patients from China were randomized (n=206 per treatment group), and 395 (95.9%) of them completed the study (dupilumab n=201 [97.6%], placebo n=194 [94.2%]) (Figure 1). The percentage of participants discontinuing the study intervention was lower in the overall dupilumab group (16 [7.8%]) than in the overall placebo group (20 [9.7%]). The main reason for permanent study discontinuation was “withdrawal by patient” (n=2 [1%] in the dupilumab group, n=11 [5.3%] in the placebo group). Reasons for withdrawal included adverse events (related or unrelated to COVID-19), study procedures, inconvenience, or other (most of which were related to COVID-19).17 In total, 347 (84.2%) patients were included in the primary analysis population (i.e, had elevated type 2 biomarkers and no maintenance OCS; the type 2 non-OCS group); 65 patients who had been included in the study were excluded from the primary analysis population as they were either on maintenance OCS (34 patients) or had no evidence of type 2 inflammation or without maintenance OCS (31 patients).

Demographics and baseline characteristics in the type 2 non-OCS group were broadly similar in the 2 treatment groups at baseline (Table 1). All participants were Asian, and 57.3% were female. Across groups, 12–16% of patients were using high-dose ICS at baseline, and the majority (72.9%) had comorbid atopic conditions such as atopic dermatitis, allergic rhinitis, or food allergy.

Lung Function

At baseline, mean pre-bronchodilator FEV1 in the type 2 non-OCS group was 1.45 L (standard deviation [SD]=0.50) in the placebo arm and 1.52 L (0.50) in the dupilumab arm. At week 12, the LS mean change from baseline was greater in the dupilumab arm (0.46 L [0.03]) than in the placebo arm (0.12 L [0.03]), with an LS mean difference of 0.34 L (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.25, 0.43; nominal p<0.0001). This improvement was observed within 2 weeks of study start and sustained through week 24 (Figure 2). Improvements were also seen in percent predicted pre-bronchodilator FEV1, with an LS mean difference for dupilumab vs placebo of 13.19 percentage points (95% CI: 10.01, 16.38; nominal p<0.0001) at week 12.

LS mean change from baseline in pre-bronchodilator FEV1 (L) up to week 24 in patients with type 2 inflammatory asthma without OCS maintenance (type 2 non-OCS group) in China. ***p<0.0001 vs matching placebo.

Abbreviations: FEV1, forced expiratory volume in 1 second; LS, least squares; OCS, oral corticosteroid; q2w, every 2 weeks; SE, standard error.

Asthma Control and Quality of Life

Baseline ACQ-5 scores in the type 2 non-OCS group were similar in the 2 treatment groups (placebo: 2.20; dupilumab: 2.26). By week 24, ACQ-5 scores had improved in both groups, with a greater reduction in the dupilumab group (LS mean change=−1.32 [standard error [SE]=0.06]) than in the placebo group (LS mean change=−1.13 [0.06]; LS mean difference for dupilumab vs placebo: −0.19 [95% CI: −0.35, −0.03; nominal p=0.0206]). Notably, within-group improvements in ACQ from baseline were clinically relevant in both treatment arms, with each exceeding the Minimal Clinically Important Difference (MCID) of 0.5. Improvements in asthma control were rapid and maintained through to the end of the study (Figure 3A). At week 24, a higher proportion of patients in the dupilumab group (83.8%) achieved the MCID threshold for ACQ-5 reduction (≥0.5) compared with placebo (71.3%), with an odds ratio of 2.03 (95% CI: 1.20, 3.44; nominal p=0.0082). A similar response was also observed at week 12 (nominal p=0.0002). Baseline AQLQ scores were similar in the dupilumab and placebo type 2 non-OCS groups (mean 4.53 and 4.69, respectively). At week 24, the LS mean increase from baseline was greater for dupilumab (1.04 [0.07]) vs placebo (0.71 [0.07]), with an LS mean difference of 0.33 (95% CI: 0.14, 0.52; nominal p=0.0005) (Figure 3B).

LS mean change from baseline to week 24 in (A) ACQ-5 score and (B) AQLQ score in patients with type 2 inflammatory asthma without OCS maintenance (type 2 non-OCS group) in China. *p<0.05, **p<0.01 vs matching placebo.

Abbreviations: ACQ-5, 5-item Asthma Control Questionnaire; AQLQ, Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire; LS, least squares; OCS, oral corticosteroid; q2w, every 2 weeks; SE, standard error.

Severe Asthma Exacerbations

During the 24-week study period, the risk of severe exacerbations in the type 2 non-OCS group was 74.2% lower in the dupilumab group than in the placebo group (relative risk: 0.258; 95% CI: 0.127, 0.521; nominal p=0.0002) (Figure 4). Overall, the annualized rate of severe exacerbations decreased from a mean of 1.6 exacerbations in the year prior to the study to 0.129 during the 24-week treatment period in the dupilumab group, compared with a change from 1.5 per year to 0.501 in the placebo group.

Biomarkers

In the non-OCS safety population (including the 31 patients without evidence of type 2 inflammation or without OCS maintenance), a marked decrease in FeNO was noted from week 2, with a sustained decline through week 24. In the dupilumab group, median percent changes from baseline were −33.81% (interquartile range [IQR]: −52.38% to −14.29%) and −43.75% (IQR: −63.64% to −14.58%) at weeks 2 and 24, respectively, compared with 0% (IQR: −20.41% to 27.27%) and −11.71% (IQR: −31.85% to 23.48%) in the placebo group (Figure 5A).

Median percent change from baseline to week 24 in (A) FeNO, (B) blood eosinophil counts, and (C) total IgE in patients with or without type 2 inflammatory asthma without OCS maintenance (non-OCS safety population) in China.

Abbreviations: FeNO, fractional exhaled nitric oxide; IU, international units; OCS, oral corticosteroid; ppb, parts per billion; Q, quartile; q2w, every 2 weeks.

Median blood eosinophil counts remained stable throughout the study in both the dupilumab and placebo groups in the non-OCS population (Figure 5B). By week 24, median percent change from baseline was −11.43% (IQR: −36.36% to 22.22%) in the placebo group and −20.00% (IQR: −51.52% to 29.41%) in the dupilumab group.

By week 24, the median change from baseline in total IgE was −61.44% (IQR: −73.45% to −45.72%) in the dupilumab group, compared with −3.52% (IQR: −18.19% to 19.95%) in the placebo group (Figure 5C).

Safety

Safety findings in the type 2 non-OCS population were similar to the overall study population20 (Table 2). In the safety population, which also included 34 patients taking maintenance OCS, 80.1% of placebo recipients and 86.4% of dupilumab recipients reported TEAEs, the most frequent of which was upper respiratory tract infection (26.7% and 26.2% of patients in the placebo and dupilumab groups, respectively). Most TEAEs were mild to moderate, with severe TEAEs reported by 5.3% of placebo recipients and 4.9% of dupilumab recipients. Rates of treatment-emergent SAEs were similar across the placebo and dupilumab groups (10.7% vs 9.2%).20 Safety findings were similar in patients on maintenance OCS receiving 300 mg dupilumab (Table 2).

Discussion

In this predefined subgroup analysis, dupilumab versus placebo significantly improved lung function, asthma control, and quality of life, and reduced severe exacerbations in adolescents and adults from China with moderate-to-severe type 2 asthma and no maintenance OCS. Safety findings were consistent with the known safety profile of dupilumab, and biomarker profiles were similar to those observed in previous studies.18,20 This phase 3 randomized, placebo-controlled trial provides comparative efficacy data supporting the use of add-on dupilumab in patients from China with moderate-to-severe asthma with evidence of type 2 inflammation, thereby complemented by real-world effectiveness findings in this population.21,22

Results from the current analysis are similar to those for the overall study population, which also included patients from India,18 and to the results of previous global pivotal clinical trials performed in adults.21,23,24 When efficacy results in the current analysis (patients in China only) are compared with those in the overall study population (patients in India and China), similar degrees of improvement in FEV1 were observed (LS mean difference: 0.31 L, compared with 0.35 L in the current analysis), although the reduction in risk of severe exacerbations vs placebo was lower than that in the current analysis (62.0% compared with 74.2%), which was potentially related to the slightly lower overall rates of severe exacerbations and exacerbations requiring hospitalization in the preceding year for the overall study population.18 In a phase 2b dose-ranging study performed across 17 countries worldwide (NCT01854047), dupilumab 200 or 300 mg q2w or every 4 weeks reduced severe asthma exacerbations and improved lung function and quality of life in patients with uncontrolled persistent asthma, with the greatest increases in pre-bronchodilator FEV1 observed in patients with eosinophil counts ≥300 cells/µL. Rates of reduction in severe exacerbations were similar to those in the current analysis, with reductions of ~70% in annualized exacerbation rate for the study population as a whole and greater reductions of 71–81% in patients with baseline eosinophils ≥300 cells/µL.23 The between-group ACQ improvement, while not meeting the established MCID of 0.5, reflects the MCID’s validation for within-patient, within-group baseline comparisons rather than between-group differences. Similarly, in the global pivotal phase 3 LIBERTY ASTHMA QUEST study (NCT02414854), which included 1902 adolescent and adult patients with moderate-to-severe asthma, with a similar study design and patient enrollment criteria as the current study, patients receiving dupilumab 200 mg q2w also showed significant improvements in lung function, asthma control, and exacerbation rates, with greatest benefits in patients with elevated type 2 biomarkers at baseline.23 These benefits were maintained through up to 3 years of treatment in the LIBERTY ASTHMA TRAVERSE open-label extension study overall population.20 Subgroup analyses in Japanese and Korean populations showed findings consistent with the overall study, demonstrating similar improvements in lung function and exacerbation risk reduction.25,26

Currently, asthma is largely undertreated and underdiagnosed in China, with only 23.0% of patients with asthma ever having undergone pulmonary function tests.3 In a study of patients from Sichuan and Jilin provinces, asthma was uncontrolled in approximately 40.0% of patients, consistent with findings from the previous Asthma Insight and Management survey27 and indicating a need for alternative treatment options for improving symptom control. One of the key goals of biologic therapy for asthma is to achieve disease remission and enable discontinuation of maintenance OCS. Chinese expert consensus recommends the use of add-on biologics for eligible patients with severe asthma that remains uncontrolled despite use of medium/high-dose ICS and additional controller(s).28

Although data on biologics in Chinese populations are limited, a key strength of this analysis is its focus on Chinese patients with type 2 asthma, enabling a comprehensive assessment of dupilumab’s efficacy and safety. Patients from China are often underrepresented in global clinical trials, particularly industry-sponsored trials of new drugs;29 therefore, this analysis provides valuable evidence for patients in this country, where dupilumab is available as an add-on treatment for patients with persistent asthma. These results support add-on dupilumab use in Chinese patients with moderate-to-severe type 2 asthma inadequately controlled on standard therapy, with the potential to achieve on treatment clinical remission and reduce OCS burden in alignment with expert consensus recommendations. Clinical remission as a composite endpoint warrants further focused investigation; the current analysis provides results for individual remission components (exacerbation rates, lung function, asthma control).

Despite being an analysis on a subgroup of patients, enrollment in the phase 3 study was stratified by region (China vs non-China), thus resulting in a balanced population across treatment groups. The study was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic, limiting in-person site visits; contingency measures including remote visits and patient self-administration of treatments were implemented, although findings should be considered within this context. A notable placebo response was observed across efficacy measures; however, the magnitude of improvement with dupilumab relative to placebo demonstrates clinically meaningful benefit. This magnitude of placebo response is consistent with asthma RCTs and may reflect increased adherence to background therapy and intensified monitoring during the pandemic period. As a subgroup analysis, generalizability to broader Asia–Pacific asthma populations may be limited. Longer-term real-world effectiveness studies in patients from China and further research evaluating efficacy in other asthma phenotypes would be valuable.

Conclusions

This pre-specified subgroup analysis derived from a larger Asia–Pacific randomized controlled trial demonstrates that add-on dupilumab significantly improves lung function, reduces the risk of severe exacerbations, increases asthma control and quality of life, and is well tolerated in patients from China with persistent asthma and evidence of type 2 inflammation and no maintenance OCS requirements. The findings from this study contribute to the overall efficacy and safety profile of dupilumab for patients with moderate-to-severe asthma in China, where asthma remains underdiagnosed and undertreated.

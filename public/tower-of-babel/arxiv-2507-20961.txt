Xueqin Peng, Matteo Rizzi

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we find normalized solutions to the following Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}-\Delta u-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}h(x)u+\lambda u=f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\displaystyle u>0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}dx=a^{2},\end{cases}

where
N\geq 3
,
a>0
is fixed,
f
satisfies mass-subcritical growth conditions and
h
is a given bounded function with
||h||_{\infty}\leq 1
. The
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
-norm of
u
is fixed and
\lambda
appears as a Lagrange multiplier. Our solutions are constructed by minimizing the corresponding energy functional on a suitable constraint. Due to the presence of a possibly nonradial term
h
, establishing compactness becomes challenging. To address this difficulty, we employ the splitting lemma to exclude both the vanishing and the dichotomy of a given any minimizing sequence for appropriate
a>0
.
Furthermore, we show that if
h
is radial, then radial solutions can be obtained for any
a>0
. In this case, the radial symmetry allows us to prove that such solutions converge to a ground state solution of the limit problem as
\mu\to 0^{+}
.

Key words:
Schrödinger equation; Normalized solution; Variational methods; Global minimizer.

Introduction

Consider the following time-dependent Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi+V(x)\psi+g(|\psi|)\psi=0\qquad\text{in}~(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.1)

where the function
\psi=\psi(t,x):(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is complex valued,
g
is real valued and
V:{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a given potential. An important topic is to search standing wave solutions of (
1.1
), namely solutions of the form

\psi(t,x)=e^{i\lambda t}u(x),\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad u:{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}.

In this case,
\psi
is a solution to (
1.1
) if and only if
u
solves the following semilinear elliptic equation

-\Delta u+\lambda u+V(x)u=f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

(1.2)

where
f(u)=g(|u|)u
.
Because of its importance in many different physical frameworks, many authors have investigated the
Schrödinger equation. At this point, there are two different ways to approach (
1.2
) according to the role of
\lambda
:

(1)

the frequency
\lambda
is a fixed and assigned parameter,

(2)

the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
-norm of the solution is given and the frequency
\lambda
is an unknown of the problem and appears as a Lagrange multiplier.

In the first case, there are many results in the literature which are focused on existence, nonexistence and multiplicity of solutions to (
1.2
). Some of them can be found, for instance, in
[
3
,
19
,
21
]
and the references therein. In those papers the authors apply variational methods (see e.g.
[
11
,
28
,
32
,
34
]
) to construct such solutions. In fact, it is easy to see that the critical points of the following functional

I(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V(x)+\lambda)u^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx

give rise to solutions of (
1.2
).

On the other hand, the second case has attracted significant attention during the last few years. It is normally referred to as the
fixed mass problem
, which consists in looking for solutions to (
1.2
) on the
L^{2}
-constraint

\mathcal{S}_{a}:=\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):||u||_{2}=a\}.

(1.3)

These kind of solutions, which have recently gained interest in physics, are known as
normalized solutions
. Indeed, from the physical point of view, this approach turns out to be more meaningful since it offers a better insight of the variational properties of the stationary solutions of (
1.2
), such as stability or instability. Another physical motivation to study normalized solutions is that the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
-norm of the standing wave solutions to (
1.1
) is preserved in time.

Compared to the fixed frequency case, the study of normalized solutions is more complicated because the terms in the corresponding energy functional scale differently. The main difficulties and challenges are based on several aspects: (i) the classical mountain-pass theorem is not applicable to construct a
(PS)
sequence; (ii) since
\lambda
is unknown, the Nehari manifold cannot be used anymore, which brings more obstacles to prove the boundedness of the
(PS)
sequence; (iii) the compactness of the
(PS)
sequence is more challenging even if one considers the radial case since the embedding of
H^{1}_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is not compact.

Most of papers are devoted to the autonomous case, that is
V(x)\equiv 0
. For results in this direction, we refer to e.g.
[
1
,
15
,
25
,
30
,
31
,
33
]
. However, the case
V(x)\not\equiv 0
is totally different, especially if
V
is not radial, so that it is not possible to work in the radial setting and use the compactness of the embedding
H_{r}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, for
p\in(2,2^{*})
. As a consequence, it is much more complicated to recover the compactness of (PS)-sequences. From the perspective of physics,
V(x)
represents an external potential that influences the behavior of the stationary waves.

A natural approach to such a question is by variational methods.
Indeed, if
V\in L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for some
r\in[\frac{N}{2},+\infty],r\geq 1
, solutions to (
1.2
)-(
1.3
)
can be found as critical points of the energy functional

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)u^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx

(1.4)

on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
.
Now we recall some existing results in this direction. It turns out that the geometry of
E

on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
changes according to the growth condition of
f
in relation with the mass critical exponent
\bar{p}=2+\frac{4}{N}
. In this respect, among many results, we refer the reader to the
recent paper
[
12
]
by Ikoma and Miyamoto, where the existence of global minimizers is obtained for non-positive potentials. After
that, Ikoma and Miyamoto
[
13
]
applied their argument to a minimization problem
with two constraint conditions and potentials. On the other hand, in the mass-supercritical case, the functional
E
is unbounded
from below on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
, thus the problem cannot be solved by (global) minimization arguments, and
even the appropriate definition of ground state is not clear. For (step well) trapping potentials, i.e. potentials
satisfying

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}V(x)=+\infty,

the trapping nature of the potential provides
compactness, which guarantees the existence of solutions which are local minimizers of
E
on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
, see e.g.
[
24
]
. In
[
2
]
, Bartsch et al. considered weakly repulsive potentials,
i.e.

V(x)\geq\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}V(x)>-\infty

and they applied a new variational principle exploiting the Pohozaev identity which can be used to obtain existence of solutions with high Morse index. For weakly attractive potential, that is

V(x)\leq\limsup_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}V(x)<+\infty,

Molle et al.
[
22
]
obtained a mountain pass solution and a local minimizer, under suitable assumptions about
f
and
V
. For other contributions in this direction, we refer the reader to
[
16
,
26
,
35
]
and the references therein.

It is interesting to mention that, up to now, it has not been considered that problem (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) in the
L^{2}
-subcritical regime involving the “Hardy potential” (or “inverse-square potential”)
V(x):=-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}
in the linear part except
[
17
]
, where they studied the mixed non-linearities by using the Pohozaev manifold approach developed by Soave
[
30
,
31
]
. The potential with this decay rate plays a critical role in non-relativistic quantum mechanics, as it represents an intermediate threshold between two distinct classes of potentials: regular potential and singular potential, we refer to
[
9
]
for more details. Besides, it also arises in many other
areas such as quantum mechanics, nuclear physics, molecular physics and quantum cosmology
[
10
]
.

Due to its broad relevance, one of the interest of the present paper is to investigate this problem. Precisely, we consider the following problem:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}h(x)\color[rgb]{0,0,0}u+\lambda u=f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u>0,~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}dx=a^{2},\end{cases}

(
\mathcal{Q}
)

where
0\leq\mu<\bar{\mu}:=\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}
,
h\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
with
\|h\|_{\infty}\leq 1,\,h\neq 0
and
f
satisfies the following conditions:

(f_{1})

f(t)\in C({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})
is odd and there exist constants
q\in(2,2+\frac{4}{N})
and
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
such that

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{f(t)}{t^{q-1}}=\alpha.

(1.5)

(f_{2})
There is
r\in(2,2+\frac{4}{N})
such that
\frac{f(t)}{t^{r-1}}
is increasing on
(0,+\infty)
.

(f_{3})
There exist
c_{0},t_{0}>0
and
p\in(2,2+\frac{4}{N})
such that

f(t)\leq c_{0}t^{p-1},\quad\forall\,t>t_{0}.

The easiest example of non-linearity
f
satisfying
(f_{1})-(f_{3})
is
f(t)=|t|^{p-2}t
with
p\in(2,2+\frac{4}{N})
. However, it is possible to verify that the function

f(t)=|t|^{q-2}t+|t|^{p-2}t\ln(1+|t|)

satisfies
(f_{1})-(f_{3})
as well, provided
p,\,q\in(2,2+\frac{4}{N})
. The non-linearities which fulfill
(f_{1})-(f_{3})
will be referred to as
mass-subcritical
non-linearities.

Problem (

Q

) has a variational structure. In fact, under the assumptions
(f_{1})-(f_{3})
, the solutions to (

Q

) are the crtical points of the energy functional

J_{\mu}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{h(x)}{|x|^{2}}u^{2}(x)dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}F(u)dx,\quad\forall\,u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})

on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
, where
F(t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(s)ds
. We note that
J_{\mu}
is well defined in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for any
\mu\in[0,\bar{\mu})
, with
\bar{\mu}=\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}
, thanks to the Hardy-Sobolev inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{u^{s}}{|x|^{s}}dx\leq(\frac{s}{N-s})^{s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{s}dx\qquad\forall\,u\in W^{1,s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\,1<s<N

applied with
s=2
.

For
\mu=0
, the existence of solutions to Problem (

Q

) was treated in
[
29
]
. More precisely, setting

m^{0}(a):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}_{a}}J_{0}(u),\qquad\forall\,a>0,

(1.6)

Shibata
[
29
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
f
satisfies
(f_{1})-(f_{3})
. Then there exists
a_{0}>0
such that, for any
a>a_{0}
, there exists a solution
(v_{a},\lambda_{a})\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\times(0,\infty)
to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda u=f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u^{2}(x)dx=a^{2},\,u>0,\end{cases}

(1.7)

such that
J_{0}(v_{a})=m^{0}(a)\in(-\infty,0)
.

Problem (
1.7
), which corresponds to the case
\mu=0

limit problem
. We note that the assumptions about

in
[
29

The solution constructed in Theorem

corresponds to a global minimizer of

on

with negative energy, which exists thanks to the mass-subcritical growth of

for any

, a detailed discussion is given in Remark

.
However, in some special cases, such as for instance
\mu=0
, solutions to (

Q

[

]
.

For
\mu\in(0,\bar{\mu})
, a radial ground state solution
U
to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u-\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}u+u=f(u)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\,u>0,\end{cases}

was constructed in

for a quite wide class of non-linearities

which satisfy

with

. We stress that in the aforementioned paper the frequency is fixed and there is no mass constraint. Moreover, the non-linearity is allowed to have mass-superctical growth.

As a consequence, if we take the pure power non-linearity
f(t)=|t|^{p-2}t
with
p\in(2,2^{*})\setminus\{2+\frac{4}{N}\}
and

, a scaling argument proves the existence of a solution to Problem (

Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we always assume
h\neq 0,\,h\geq 0
and construct a solution
(u,\lambda)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\times(0,\infty)
to (

Q

) such that

is a minimizer of the functional

J_{\mu}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{h(x)}{|x|^{2}}u^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx

(2.1)

on the constraint
\mathcal{S}_{a}

The following lemma is necessary
for this argument.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Lions
[
21

) Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

be a bounded sequence in

\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\int_{B_{1}(z)}|u_{n}|^{2}dx\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

In the next Lemma

is bounded from below and coercive on
\mathcal{S}_{a}

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
(f_{1})-(f_{3})
hold,
\mu\in[0,\bar{\mu})

Proof.

First, we prove
J_{\mu}
is bounded from below on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
.
By
(f_{1})
and
(f_{3})
, we have

|f(t)|\leq C_{1}|t|^{q-1}+C_{2}|t|^{p-1}.

(2.2)

Consequently, applying the Hardy and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities, we have for any
u\in\mathcal{S}_{a}
,

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{h(x)}{|x|^{2}}u^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx

(2.3)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\bar{\mu}-\mu}{2\bar{\mu}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-C_{1}a^{(1-\mu_{q})q}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{\mu_{q}q}{2}}-C_{2}a^{(1-\mu_{p})p}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{\mu_{p}p}{2}},

this implies

is bounded from below and coercive on

since

and
\mu_{q}q,\mu_{p}p<2
. This shows that

.

Now we prove

for any

The assumption
(f_{1})
yields that
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{qF(t)}{|t|^{q}}=\alpha>0
, then there is

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}m_{r}^{\mu-\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{n}}(a)\leq m^{\mu}_{r}(a)\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}m_{r}^{\mu+\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{n}}(a)

(4.1)

for
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
. For
\mu=0
, it is enough to prove the second inequality, while for
\mu=\mu_{0}
, it is enough to prove the first inequality.

Let us prove the first inequality. For any
\varepsilon>0
small enough, there exists
u\in\mathcal{S}_{a,r}
such that

J_{\mu}(u)<m^{\mu}_{r}(a)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<0.

Moreover, by relation (
2.3
), for any
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0}]
there exists a constant
\bar{C}>0
such that,

\text{if}~~v\in\mathcal{S}_{a}~~\text{satisfies}~~J_{\mu}(v)<0,~~\text{then}~~||\nabla v||_{2}\leq\bar{C}.

(4.2)

As a consequence

\displaystyle m^{\mu-\frac{1}{n}}_{r}(a)

\displaystyle\leq J_{\mu-\frac{1}{n}}(u)=J_{\mu}(u)+\frac{1}{2n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{h(x)u^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2}}dx

(4.3)

\displaystyle<m^{\mu}_{r}(a)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\bar{\mu}}{2n}||\nabla u||^{2}_{2}<m^{\mu}(a)+\varepsilon

provided
n>n_{0}
, for some
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon,\mu)>0
. This concludes the proof of the first inequality.

In order to prove the second inequality, we note that, for any
n>0
there exists
u_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{a}
such that

J_{\mu+\frac{1}{n}}(u_{n})<m^{\mu+\frac{1}{n}}_{r}(a)+\frac{1}{n}<0.

As a consequence, using once again (
4.2
), we can see that there exists
\tilde{C}_{\mu}>0
such that

J_{\mu}(u_{n})-\frac{\tilde{C}_{\mu}}{n}\leq J_{\mu}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{2n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{h(x)u_{n}^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2}}dx=J_{\mu+\frac{1}{n}}(u)<m^{\mu+\frac{1}{n}}_{r}(a)+\frac{1}{n},

which yields that

m^{\mu}_{r}(a)\leq J_{\mu}(u_{n})<m^{\mu+\frac{1}{n}}_{r}(a)+\frac{1+\tilde{C}_{\mu}}{n}.

(4.4)

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, we have the statement.

In particular (
4.1
) shows that
m^{\mu}_{r}(a)\to m^{0}_{r}(a)
as
\mu\to 0^{+}
.

Let
\{\mu_{n}\}\subset(0,\infty)
be a sequence such that
\mu_{n}\to 0^{+}
. For the sake of simplicity, we will drop the subscript
n
and write
\mu:=\mu_{n}
. By (
4.2
), we know
\{u^{\mu}\}\subset H^{1}_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is bounded in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and by (
2.30
),
\{\lambda^{\mu}\}
is also bounded in
{\mathbb{R}}
. Then up to a subsequence, there exist

u^{0}\in H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
u^{0}\geq 0
and
\lambda^{0}\in[-\frac{2m^{0}(a)}{a^{2}},0)
such that

u^{\mu}\rightharpoonup u^{0}\quad\text{ in}\quad H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda^{\mu}\rightarrow\lambda^{0}\quad\text{ in}\quad\mathbb{R}.

We note that

L_{0}u^{\mu}=f(u^{\mu})+(\lambda^{0}-\lambda^{\mu})u^{\mu}+\mu\frac{h(x)u^{\mu}}{|x|^{2}}\qquad\text{in}\,{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

where
L_{0}
is defined in (
3.1
), or equivalently

u^{\mu}=L_{0}^{-1}\left(f(u^{\mu})+(\lambda^{0}-\lambda^{\mu})u^{\mu}+\mu\frac{h(x)u^{\mu}}{|x|^{2}}\right).

We stress that
L_{0}
is invertible thanks to the fact that
\lambda_{0}\geq-\frac{2m^{0}(a)}{a^{2}}>0
. Using that
u^{\mu}\in H^{1}_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and the compactness of the embedding
H^{1}_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, for
s\in(2,2^{*})
, we can see that

u^{\mu}=L_{0}^{-1}\left(f(u^{\mu})+(\lambda^{0}-\lambda^{\mu})u^{\mu}+\mu\frac{h(x)u^{\mu}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\to L_{0}^{-1}(f(u^{0})).

Since
u^{\mu}\rightharpoonup u^{0}
in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, one can summarize

u^{\mu}\to u^{0}\quad\text{strongly}\quad\text{in}\quad H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\quad

and

-\Delta u^{0}+\lambda^{0}u^{0}=f(u^{0}).

Since the sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}
is arbitrary, the above results conclude that

(u^{\mu},\lambda^{\mu})\to(u^{0},\lambda^{0})\quad\text{in}\quad H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\times{\mathbb{R}}\quad\text{as}\quad\mu\to 0^{+}.

∎

Now it remains to prove that
J_{0}(u^{0})=m^{0}(a)
.

Remark 4.1
.

Theorem
1.4
follows from Lemmas
4.2
and
4.1
. In fact, due to the continuity of
J_{\mu}
with respect to the parameter
\mu
and to the variable
u
, we have

J_{\mu}(u^{\mu})\to J_{0}(u^{0})
as
\mu\to 0^{+}
. However, due to Lemma
4.2
and
4.1
, one has

J_{\mu}(u^{\mu})=m^{\mu}_{r}(a)\to m^{0}_{r}(a)=m^{0}(a)\quad\text{as}\quad\mu\to 0^{+},

which yields that
J_{0}(u^{0})=m^{0}(a)
. This means that
u^{0}
is a minimizer of
J_{0}
on
\mathcal{S}_{a}
.

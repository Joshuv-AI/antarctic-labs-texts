1.
Introduction

The proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem in 1995 was a monumental achievement in mathematics. Not only was this the end of the famous open problem that remained for more than 300 years, but it also paved the way to a new strategy of solving certain Diophantine equations. This strategy is called the ‘modular method’ which exploits the relation between modular forms and elliptic curves. Fermat’s Last Theorem states that there are no non-trivial solutions to the equation
a^{p}+b^{p}=c^{p}
whenever
p>2
. In his paper
[
20
]
, Wiles proved that semi-stable elliptic curves are modular which gave the required contradiction to prove Fermat’s Last Theorem.

This connection between elliptic curves and modular forms is also conjectured over general number fields. Assuming these conjectures, the modular method can be employed to solve other Diophantine equations over number fields. The modular method was used by Freitas and Siksek in
[
7
]
to show that the equation
a^{p}+b^{p}=c^{p}
has no ‘asymptotic solutions’ over a totally real number field
K
. That is, for a number field
K
, there is a constant
B_{K}
depending on
K
such that whenever
p>B_{K}
, there is no solution to the equation. In
[
3
]
, Deconinck generalized these methods to show that the equation
Aa^{p}+Bb^{p}=Cc^{p}
has no asymptotic solutions over a totally real field
K
with
A,B
and
C
odd. The modular method also applies to asymptotically solve
a^{p}+b^{p}=c^{3}
over certain number fields
K
; in
[
8
]
, Isik, Kara and Özman showed that there are no asymptotic solutions to this equation over certain number fields
K
. Furthermore, in
[
12
]
Mocanu proved that the same equation has no asymptotic solutions for totally real number fields
K
which satisfy a certain condition about
S
-units.

In this paper, we combine and generalize the approaches of
[
8
]
and
[
12
]
to prove that the equation
Aa^{p}+Bb^{p}=Cc^{3}
admits no asymptotic solutions for number fields
K
satisfying a suitable
S
-unit condition. In particular, we show that for certain imaginary quadratic fields, this condition is satisfied.

In addition to this asymptotic result, we also establish an effective result that extends the explicit bounds obtained in
[
8
]
. More precisely, we show that for the equation

a^{p}+db^{p}=c^{3}

over
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
, with
d\in\{7,19,43,67\}
, there exists an explicit bound (depending on
d
) such that there are no solutions of a particular type whenever
p
exceeds this bound.

Our Results

Let
K
be a number field and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
denote its ring of integers and
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
denote the group of units in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. For
A,B,C
nonzero elements of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}

and a prime number
p
, we refer the equation

(1.1)

Aa^{p}+Bb^{p}=Cc^{3}

as
the generalized Fermat equation over K with coefficients A,B,C and signature (p,p,3)
. A solution
(a,b,c)
is
called
trivial
if
abc=0
, otherwise
non-trivial
. A solution
(a,b,c)
of the equation (
1.1
) is called

if
a,b
and
c
are pairwise coprime.

We study solutions
(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{3}
to (
1.1
) such that
(a,b,c)
is primitive. This restriction of solutions has the following reason. We aim to show that there are no solutions to (
1.1
) for
p
larger than a constant depending only on
K,A,B
and
C
. If we do not require a solution to be primitive, then we obtain an infinite set of uninteresting solutions to (
1.1
).
In fact, if
p\equiv-1\pmod{3}
and

satisfy
Aa^{p}+Bb^{p}=Cc
, then
(ac,bc,c^{\frac{p+1}{3}})
is a non-primitive solution to
(
1.1
)
. Observe that if

satisfies
Ax^{p}+By^{p}=Cz
, then
(a,b,c)=(xz)^{p}+(yz)^{p}=(z^{\frac{p+1}{2}})^{2}
satisfies equation
Aa^{p}+Bb^{p}=Cc^{2}
. Hence, if
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
we find that
(ac,bc,c^{\frac{p+2}{3}})
is a non-primitive solution to
(
1.1
)
. Thus, we consider only primitive solutions to the equation (
1.1
). In
[
2
]
, it was shown that equation (
1.1
) has finitely many primitive solutions.

The nature of our results to equation (
1.1
) is asymptotic and explicit. The main asymptotic result is about some type of solutions over general number fields, and the explicit result is about solutions over some imaginary quadratic fields. Before stating the main results, we introduce some notation. Define

T_{K}=\{\mathfrak{P}:\mathfrak{P}\text{ is a prime in }K\text{ and }\mathfrak{P}\mid 3ABC\}.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{T_{K}}
denote the ring of
T_{K}
-integers and let
\mathcal{O}_{T_{K}}^{\times}
denote the group of
T_{K}
-units. Further, let
\text{Cl}_{T_{K}}(K)
denote the quotient
\text{Cl}(K)/\langle[\mathfrak{P}]\rangle_{\mathfrak{P}\in T_{K}}
. Our asymptotic result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a number field with
\textup{Cl}_{T_{K}}(K)[3]=1
satisfying Conjectures
2.1
and
2.2
. Further suppose that for every solution
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathcal{O}_{T_{K}}^{\times}\times\mathcal{O}_{T_{K}}^{\times}\times\mathcal{O}_{T_{K}}

Let

Theorem

[

in the sense that

restricts to totally real number fields. What allows this generality in our case is that we use a slightly modified level lowering technique which is used in

Proof of Theorem

.

\lambda

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

With notation as in Theorem
1.1
, we have
T_{K}=\{\mathfrak{q}\}
Note that since
3\nmid h_{K}
, by Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that for every solution
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\mathcal{O}_{S_{K}}^{\times}\times\mathcal{O}_{S_{K}}^{\times}\times\mathcal{O}_{S_{K}}

satisfies

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
be such a solution. Scale this solution by cubic powers (this is where the assumption that
\mathfrak{q}
is principal comes in) and swap
\alpha
and

if necessary so that
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\beta)=0,1
or

and

Case 1:
Suppose that

which contradicts since

is not a multiple of

v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha)=v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\beta)
. It follows that
|v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha\beta^{-1})|=0\leqslant 3v_{\mathfrak{q}}(3)
.

Case 2:
Suppose that

and that
\beta
is not a cube. Suppose for a contradiction that

so that

. The field

is an abelian extension of degree

of

. We show that

is unramified above
\mathfrak{q}
. The element
\theta=\tfrac{\gamma^{2}+\gamma\zeta_{3}\sqrt[3]{\beta}+\zeta_{3}^{2}\sqrt[3]{\beta}}{3}\in L
has minimal polynomial over

\displaystyle f_{K}^{\theta}(X)

\displaystyle=X^{3}+\frac{\gamma(\gamma^{3}-\beta)}{3}X^{2}-\gamma^{2}X-\frac{(\gamma^{3}-\beta)^{2}}{27}

\displaystyle=X^{3}+\frac{\gamma\alpha}{3}X^{2}-\gamma^{2}X-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{27}.

Since
3^{3}\mid\alpha
and
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\gamma)=\tfrac{1}{3}v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha+\beta)=0
, the polynomial

belongs to
\mathcal{O}_{K}[X]
and has discriminant

\Delta(f^{\theta}_{K})=-\frac{2\gamma^{3}\alpha^{3}}{3^{5}}-\frac{4\gamma^{3}\alpha^{5}}{3^{9}}+\frac{\gamma^{6}\alpha^{2}}{3^{2}}-4\gamma^{6}-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{3^{3}}.

Since

and
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\gamma)=0

\Delta\not\equiv 0\mod\mathfrak{q}

and hence

is unramified at
\mathfrak{q}
. The other primes where

may ramify are the primes dividing

, it follows that
\beta
is supported on
\mathfrak{q}
. It follows that
L/K(\zeta_{3})
is unramified above all places (also the infinite ones since

and

have the same signature) and hence

. This contradicts our assumption. Therefore,
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha)\leqslant 3v_{\mathfrak{q}}(3)
and
|v(\alpha\beta^{-1})|=v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha)\leqslant 3v_{\mathfrak{q}}(3)
.

Case 3:
Suppose that
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\beta)=0
and
\beta
is a cube. By dividing the equation

by

, we may assume that

. We get an equation of the form (

) and by assumption we get that

and hence
|v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha\beta^{-1})|=v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha)\leqslant 3v_{\mathfrak{q}}(3)
.
In all possible cases we find that

∎

4.3.
Quadratic imaginary number fields and proof of Corollary
1.3

The

-integer equation occurring in Theorem

-integer equation.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

be a positive square-free integer such that

. Let

and let
S
be the set of primes in
K
above

. The only solutions to the
S
-unit equation
\alpha+1=\gamma^{3}
with
(\alpha,\gamma)\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}\times\mathcal{O}_{S}
are
(-1,0)
and
(-9,-2)
.

Proof.

Let
(\alpha,\gamma)\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}\times\mathcal{O}_{S}
be a solution to
\alpha+1=\gamma^{3}
. The condition that
d\equiv 1\mod 3
is precisely the condition that
3
is inert in
K
. Therefore
S=\{(3)\}
and
\mathcal{O}_{S}=\mathcal{O}_{K}[\frac{1}{3}]
and
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}\times\langle 3\rangle
. It follows that
\alpha=\pm 3^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. First suppose that
n\leqslant 0
. Then
\pm 3^{n}=\pm\tfrac{1}{3^{k}}
where
k=|n|
. It follows that

(4.3)

\gamma^{3}=\frac{\pm 1+3^{k}}{3^{k}}.

Therefore,
3v_{3}(\gamma)=-k
which shows that
k
is divisible by
3
and that
\gamma=\tfrac{c}{3^{k/3}}
for some
c\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Then (
4.3
) shows that
c^{3}=\pm 1+3^{k}
. We have

\pm 1=c^{3}-3^{k}=\big(c-3^{\tfrac{k}{3}}\big)\big(c^{2}+3^{\tfrac{k}{3}}+3^{\tfrac{2k}{3}}\big).

This is an equality in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and hence it follows that
c-3^{\tfrac{k}{3}}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}
. It follows that
c=\pm 1+3^{\tfrac{k}{3}}
and

c^{3}=\pm 1+3^{\tfrac{k}{3}+1}\pm 3^{\tfrac{2k}{3}+1}+3^{k}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c^{3}-3^{k}=\pm 1+3^{\tfrac{k}{3}+1}\pm 3^{\tfrac{2k}{3}+1}.

The left hand side of the last equation is equal to
1
or
-1
which forces
k=0
and hence
n=0
. We conclude that
n\geqslant 0
. Next, suppose for a contradiction that
n>2
. Since
\gamma^{3}=\pm 3^{n}+1
it follows that
v_{3}(\gamma)=0
and hence
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Write

\pm 3^{n}=(\gamma-1)(\gamma-\zeta_{3})(\gamma-\zeta_{3}^{2})

in
L\coloneqq K(\zeta_{3})
. Define

\displaystyle x=\gamma-1,\quad y=\gamma-\zeta_{3}\quad\text{and}\quad z=\gamma-\zeta_{3}^{2}.

Then
x-y=\zeta_{3}-1
and
y-z=\zeta_{3}(\zeta_{3}-1)
. Let
\sigma\in\Gal(L/K)
be the generator of
\Gal(L/K)
i.e.
\sigma
is the element of
\Gal(L/K)
such that
\sigma(\zeta_{3})=\zeta_{3}^{2}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}=(\zeta_{3}-1)
be the prime above
3
in
L
. Then
\sigma(\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{p}
and since
\sigma(z)=y
we have that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(z)=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(y)\eqqcolon r
. We have

1=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\zeta_{3}(\zeta_{3}-1))=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(y-z)\geqslant r.

We claim that
r
is equal to
1
. Suppose for a contradiction that
r\leqslant 0
. We have

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)\geqslant v_{\mathfrak{p}}(xyz)=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(3^{n})>4.

Using this, it follows that

1=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\zeta_{3}-1)=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x-y)=\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(y)\}=r\leqslant 0,

a contradiction. We conclude that
r=1
. It follows that
2n=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(xyz)=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)+2
and hence
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)>2
. Using this fact, define

u=\frac{x}{\zeta_{3}-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{L}\quad\text{and}\quad v=\frac{-y}{\zeta_{3}-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}.

We have that
\mathfrak{p}^{2}=(3)\mid u
and since
u+v=1
it follows that
v\equiv 1\mod 3
. Let
\tau
be the generator of
\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3}))
. Then, since
\tau(\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{p}
, it follows that
(3)\mid\tau(u)
and hence also
\tau(v)\equiv 1\mod 3
. It follows that
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}(v)=\tau(v)v\equiv 1\mod 3
. Since
v\in\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
we also have that
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}(v)\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}^{\times}=\langle-\zeta_{3}\rangle
. Combining these two facts it follows that
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}(v)=1
. Let
F\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3d})
denote the unique totally real subfield of
L
. Suppose that
v\in\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}\minus\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}=\zeta_{3}\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
. Then
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}(v)
is a multiple of
\zeta_{3}
which contradicts
N_{L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})}(v)=1
. It follows that
v\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
and hence
u=v-1\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Since
x\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
u
is a quotient of
x
and
\zeta_{3}-1
, one readily verifies that this is a contradiction. We conclude that
n\in\{0,1,2\}
. It follows that

\pm 3^{n}+1\in\{0,2,4,-2,10,-8\}.

The only cubes in this set are
0
and
-8
. This concludes the proof.
∎

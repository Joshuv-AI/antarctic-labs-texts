Counting Nonattacking Chess Piece Placements: Bishops and Anassas


Abstract
We derive recurrences and closed-form expressions for counting nonattacking placements of two types of chess pieces with unbounded straight-line moves, namely the bishop (two diagonal moves) and the anassa (one horizontal or vertical move and one diagonal move), placed on a standard square chessboard. Additionally, we obtain explicit expressions for the corresponding quasi-polynomial coefficients. The recurrences are derived by analyzing how nonattacking configurations attack a specific subset of board squares, employing a bijective argument to establish the relations. The main results are simplifications of known expressions for the bishop and a general counting formula for the anassa.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
R_{\mathbf{W}}(m,k)
be the number of possible nonattacking configurations of
k
rooks placed on
\mathbf{W}_{m}
. Similarly, let
R_{\mathbf{K}}(m,k)
be the number of possible nonattacking configurations of

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{W}}(m,k)

\displaystyle=R_{\mathbf{W}}(m-1,k)+(m-k+\pi(m))R_{\mathbf{W}}(m-1,k-1),

(1)

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{K}}(m,k)

\displaystyle=R_{\mathbf{K}}(m-1,k)+(m-k+1-\pi(m))R_{\mathbf{K}}(m-1,k-1),

(2)

with initial conditions

. These recurrences have the following closed-form solutions:

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{W}}(m,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{\left\lceil\frac{m}{2}\right\rceil}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-j}{m-k}\right\},

(3)

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{K}}(m,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\rfloor}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-j}{m-k}\right\},

(4)

valid for all integers

Proof.

First, note that
\mathbf{I}_{m}^{\mathbf{W}}
has
m-1+\pi(m)
squares and each nonattacking rook placed on
\mathbf{W}_{m}-\mathbf{I}_{m}^{\mathbf{W}}
attacks
\mathbf{I}_{m}^{\mathbf{W}}
on exactly one distinct square. Thus, we have two disjoint possibilities for placing
k
nonattacking rooks on
\mathbf{W}_{m}
:

Case 1.

Place all of them on
\mathbf{W}_{m}-\mathbf{I}_{m}^{\mathbf{W}}
. This can be done in
R_{\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{W}}}(m,k)=R_{\mathbf{W}}(m-1,k)
ways, using Theorem

.

Case 2.

Place

and then place the remaining rook on one of the

1.2
.

These cases establish Equation (
1

). In order to prove the validity of the closed-form solution, first check the initial conditions, using the identity
\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-j}{-k}\right\}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k}{j}

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{W}}(m,0)

\displaystyle=\binom{\left\lceil\frac{m}{2}\right\rceil}{0}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m}\right\}=1,

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{W}}(0,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{0}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-j}{-k}\right\}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k}{0}=\delta_{k0}.

Then, replace (
3

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{\frac{1}{2}(m-1+\pi(m-1))}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-1-j}{m-1-k}\right\}+

\displaystyle+(m-k+\pi(m))\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{\frac{1}{2}(m-1+\pi(m-1))}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-1-j}{m-k}\right\}

\displaystyle=\binom{\frac{1}{2}(m-\pi(m))}{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{\frac{1}{2}(m-\pi(m))}{j}\left[\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-j}{m-k}\right\}+\pi(m)\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-1-j}{m-k}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{\frac{1}{2}(m-\pi(m))}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-j}{m-k}\right\}+\pi(m)\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{\frac{1}{2}(m-\pi(m))}{j-1}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-j}{m-k}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k}\right\}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left[\binom{\frac{1}{2}(m-\pi(m))}{j}+\pi(m)\binom{\frac{1}{2}(m-\pi(m))}{j-1}\right]\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-j}{m-k}\right\}

For the black board, note that

Remark 2.3
.

In fact, the approach used to solve recurrence (
1
), for instance, is more natural if we consider
R_{\mathbf{W}}(m,m-k)
, using the boundary condition
R_{\mathbf{W}}(m,m)=\delta_{m1}+\delta_{m0}
. By solving for some small

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{W}}(m,m-k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}(j+1)^{\frac{1}{2}(m+\pi(m))}j^{\frac{1}{2}(m-\pi(m))}}{k!}.

This was essentially the expression found by Arshon
[
1
]
, as stated by Kotěšovec
[
13
, pp. 242–248]
. By expanding
(j+1)^{\frac{1}{2}(m+\pi(m))}
using the binomial theorem, rearranging summation indices and using the identity
\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{k}\right\}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}j^{m}}{k!}
, Kotěšovec was able to refine Arshon’s result, obtaining

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{W}}(m,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{1}{2}(m+\pi(m))}\binom{\frac{1}{2}(m+\pi(m))}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j+\frac{1}{2}(m-\pi(m))}{m-k}\right\}.

However, this can be further simplified by noting that

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{W}}(2m,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=m-k}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j+m}{2m-k}\right\}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{m}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{2m-j}{2m-k}\right\},

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{W}}(2m-1,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=m-k}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{j+m-1}{2m-1-k}\right\}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{m}{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{2m-1-j}{2m-1-k}\right\}.

Combining both equations results in (
3
). A similar approach yields (

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

\displaystyle\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k}\right\}=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k+p}{p}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}\binom{m}{k+p}.

(7)

An interesting property
[
8
]

\displaystyle\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{k}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}=k\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-1}{k}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}+(m-1)\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m-2}{k-1}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\},

with initial conditions
\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0}{k}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}=\delta_{k0}
,
\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1}{k}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}=0
and
\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{0}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}=\delta_{m0}
. Written in the form
\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+k}{k}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}
, these numbers are also referenced as the Ward numbers (

). The related OEIS sequences assume

, but here we have trivially extended the definition of

in order to account for the cases

as well. Lemma

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
p
and
q
be nonnegative integers, and let
x
and
z
be arbitrary complex numbers. Then,

\displaystyle\binom{2x+z-q}{p}\binom{x}{q}=\sum_{i=0}^{p+q}\beta_{i}(p,q,z)\binom{2x+z}{i},

(8)

where

\displaystyle\beta_{i}(p,q,z)=\frac{1}{2^{2q}}\sum_{b=\max(0,i-p)}^{q}2^{b}\binom{2q-b}{q}\sum_{a=0}^{b}\binom{p+b-q-z}{a}\binom{q+z}{b-a}\binom{a-q}{p+b-i}.

(9)

Proof.

We begin with two combinatorial identities, listed by Gould and Quaintance
[
9
, identities (1.40) and (1.42)]
. Let
x
be an arbitrary complex number and let
q
be a nonnegative integer. Then,

\displaystyle(-1)^{q}2^{2q}\binom{x}{q}=\sum_{b=0}^{q}\binom{-q-1}{q-b}\binom{2q-2x-1}{b}2^{b}.

(10)

Now, let
p
be a nonnegative integer and let
z
be an arbitrary complex number. Use the identity
\binom{-x}{q}=(-1)^{q}\binom{x+q-1}{q}
and multiply
\binom{2x+z-q}{p}
on both sides of (

) to obtain

(11)

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k,p)
be the number of possible nonattacking configurations of
k
anassas placed on the square board
\mathbf{S}_{m}
such that exactly
p
of them are placed strictly below the board’s main diagonal. Then,

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k,p)=A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k,p)+(m-k+1)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-1,p)+

\displaystyle+(m-p)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-1,p-1)+(m-p)(m-k+1)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-2,p-1),

(16)

with initial conditions
A_{\mathbf{S}}(0,k,p)=\delta_{k0}\delta_{p0}
,
A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,0,p)=\delta_{p0}
,
A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,1,p)=\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m}\right\}\delta_{p0}+\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-1}\right\}\delta_{p1}
and
A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k,0)=\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m-k+1}\right\}
. This recurrence has the following closed-form solution:

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k,p)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m-k+j+1}\right\},

(17)

valid for all integers
m,k,p\geq 0
.

Proof.

We have the following disjoint possibilities for the placement of
k
nonattacking anassas on
\mathbf{S}_{m}
such that
p
of them are placed strictly below the board’s main diagonal:

Case 1.

Place all of them on
\mathbf{S}_{m}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}
, while keeping
p
of them below
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}
. This can be done in
A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k,p)
ways.

Case 2.

Place
k-1
of them on
\mathbf{S}_{m}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}
, while keeping
p
of them below
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}
, and then place the remaining anassa on one of the
m-k+1
unattacked squares of
\mathbf{D}_{m}
. This can be done in
(m-k+1)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-1,p)
ways.

Case 3.

Place
k-1
of them on
\mathbf{S}_{m}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}
, while keeping
p-1
of them below
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}
, and then place the remaining anassa on one of the
m-1-(p-1)=m-p
unattacked squares of
\mathbf{V}_{m}
. This can be done in
(m-p)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-1,p-1)
ways.

Case 4.

Place
k-2
of them on
\mathbf{S}_{m}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}
, while keeping
p-1
of them below
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}
, then place the first remaining anassa on one of the
m-1-(p-1)=m-p
unattacked squares of
\mathbf{V}_{m}
and then place the other remaining anassa on one of the
m-k+1
unattacked squares of
\mathbf{D}_{m}
. This can be done in
(m-p)(m-k+1)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-2,p-1)
ways.

These cases establish recurrence (
16
). The first two initial conditions are consequences of an empty board, due either to the absence of squares (
m=0
) or to the absence of pieces (
k=0
). The last two initial conditions are consequences of the well-known result that states that
\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m-k+1}\right\}
enumerates the nonattacking configurations of
k
rooks (or
k
anassas, due to the board symmetry) placed on a staircase board, in the shape of a right isosceles triangle, with
m
squares in its longest row.

In order to prove the validity of the closed-form solution, first check the initial conditions

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(0,k,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1}{-k+j+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{k0}\sum_{j=0}^{p}(j)_{j}\binom{-p-1}{j}\binom{-j}{-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1}{j+1}\right\}=\delta_{k0}\delta_{p0},

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,0,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m+j)_{j}\binom{-p-1}{j}\binom{-j}{-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m+j+1}\right\}=\delta_{p0},

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,1,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m-1+j)_{j}\binom{-p}{j}\binom{1-j}{1-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m+j}\right\}

\displaystyle=(m-1)_{0}\binom{-p}{0}\binom{1}{1-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m}\right\}+(m)_{1}\binom{-p}{1}\binom{0}{1-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=(\delta_{p1}+\delta_{p0})\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m}\right\}-m\delta_{p1}=\delta_{p0}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m}\right\}+\delta_{p1}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-1}\right\},

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{0}(m-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m-k+j+1}\right\}=\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m-k+1}\right\}.

Then, in order to replace (
17
) in (
16
), note that

\displaystyle(m-p)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-1,p-1)=

\displaystyle=(m-p)\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j-1}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j+1}\right\},

(18a)

\displaystyle(m-k+1)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-1,p)=

\displaystyle=(m-k+1)\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-p-2}{j}\binom{k-j-1}{k-p-1}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j+1}\right\},

(18b)

\displaystyle(m-p)(m-k+1)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-2,p-1)=

\displaystyle=(m-p)(m-k+1)\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(m-k+j+1)_{j}\binom{k-p-2}{j}\binom{k-j-2}{k-p-1}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j+2}\right\}

\displaystyle=(m-p)\sum_{j=1}^{p}(m-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-p-2}{j-1}\binom{k-j-1}{k-p-1}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j+1}\right\}.

(18c)

Now, sum Equations (
18a
) to (
18c
), simplify the binomial coefficients, and use the recurrence for the Stirling numbers of the second kind to obtain

\displaystyle(m-p)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-1,p-1)+(m-k+1)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-1,p)+

\displaystyle+(m-p)(m-k+1)A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k-2,p-1)=

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m-k+j+1)(m-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j+1}\right\}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{p}(j+1)(m-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j+1}\binom{k-j-1}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m-k+j+1)(m-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j+1}\right\}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{p}j(m-k+j-1)_{j-1}\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m-k+j+1}\right\}-

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{p}j(m-k+j-1)_{j-1}(m-k+j+1)\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j+1}\right\}.

(19)

Then, use the Stirling numbers recurrence again to write

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m-1,k,p)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m-k+j-1)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j}\right\}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m-k+j-1)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m-k+j+1}\right\}-

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m-k+j+1)(m-k+j-1)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j+1}\right\}.

(20)

Now, sum (
19
) and (
20
) and use the identity
(r)_{s}=(r-1)_{s}+s(r-1)_{s-1}
to retrieve
A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k,p)
, proving that recurrence (
16
) holds.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We may verify that, for
p=k
, Equation (
17
) is a telescoping sum, resulting in

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}(m-k+j)_{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{m-k+j+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\left[(m-k+j)_{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j}\right\}+(m-k+j+1)_{j+1}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j+1}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k}\right\}.

This result is expected, since
\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k}\right\}
enumerates the nonattacking configurations of
k
anassas placed on a staircase board of size

.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)
be the number of possible nonattacking configurations of
k
anassas placed on the square board
\mathbf{S}_{m}
. Then,

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}(m-k+j)_{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j}\right\}2^{k-2j}\left[\binom{k-j}{j-1}+\binom{k-j+1}{j}\right],

(21)

for all integers
m,k\geq 0
.

Proof.

First, we use the recurrence for the Stirling numbers of the second kind and manipulate the binomial coefficients to rewrite (
17
) as

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k,p)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(m-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j}\binom{k-j}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j}\right\}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{p+1}(m-k+j)_{j}\binom{k-p-1}{j-1}\binom{k-j+1}{k-p}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p+1}(m-k+j)_{j}\left[\binom{k-j}{k-p}\binom{k-p}{j}+\binom{k-j}{k-p-1}\binom{k-p-1}{j-1}\right]\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p+1}(m-k+j)_{j}\left[\binom{k-j}{j}\binom{k-2j}{p-j}+\binom{k-j}{j-1}\binom{k-2j+1}{p-j+1}\right]\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j}\right\}.

(22)

We also have

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)=\sum_{p=0}^{k}A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k,p).

(23)

Then, replace (
22
) in (
23
) and rearrange the summation indices to obtain

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}(m-k+j)_{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j}\right\}\binom{k-j}{j}\sum_{p=j}^{k}\binom{k-2j}{p-j}+

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}(m-k+j)_{j}\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{m-k+j}\right\}\binom{k-j}{j-1}\sum_{p=j-1}^{k}\binom{k-2j+1}{p-j+1}.

Now, use the identity
\sum_{p=j}^{k}\binom{k-2j}{p-j}=2^{k-2j}
and simplify the binomial coefficients. The upper summation limit can be reduced to
\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil
, as indicated by the remaining binomial coefficients. These manipulations result in (
21
).
∎

Remark 3.4

3.2
Quasi-polynomial coefficients for the anassa

As for the bishop, an explicit expression for the quasi-polynomial coefficients of

can be obtained by using Lemma
2.6

Theorem 3.6
.

The quasi-polynomial coefficients of
A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)
are

\displaystyle[m^{d}]A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)=

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=d}^{2k}\frac{(-1)^{i-d}}{i!}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{i}{d}\sum_{j=0}^{U(i,k)}\alpha(k,j)\sum_{p=L(i,k)}^{k-j}\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p+k-j}{p}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}\sum_{b=0}^{j}\binom{p}{b}\binom{k}{j-b}\binom{b}{k+p-i},

(24)

for
0\leq d\leq 2k
, where
\alpha(k,j)=2^{k-2j}\left[\binom{k-j}{j-1}+\binom{k-j+1}{j}\right]j!
,
U(i,k)=\min(\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil,2k-i)
, and
L(i,k)=\max(0,i-k)
.

Proof.

First, use Lemma
2.6
to expand the Stirling numbers of the second kind in (
21
), obtaining

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}\alpha(k,j)\sum_{p=0}^{k-j}\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p+k-j}{p}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}\binom{m}{p+k-j}\binom{m-k+j}{j}.

Then, use identity (
12
) to expand the binomial product

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}\alpha(k,j)\sum_{p=0}^{k-j}\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p+k-j}{p}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}\sum_{b=0}^{j}\binom{p}{b}\binom{k}{j-b}\binom{m+b}{k+p},

and use the Vandermonde convolution followed by a rearrangement of the summation indices to obtain

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}\alpha(k,j)\sum_{p=0}^{k-j}\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p+k-j}{p}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}\sum_{b=0}^{j}\binom{p}{b}\binom{k}{j-b}\sum_{i=0}^{k+p}\binom{m}{i}\binom{b}{k+p-i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2k}\binom{m}{i}\sum_{j=0}^{\min(\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil,2k-i)}\alpha(k,j)\sum_{p=\max(0,i-k)}^{k-j}\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p+k-j}{p}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}\sum_{b=0}^{j}\binom{p}{b}\binom{k}{j-b}\binom{b}{k+p-i}.

The final step is to use the identity
\binom{m}{i}=\sum_{d=0}^{i}\frac{(-1)^{i-d}}{i!}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{i}{d}m^{d}
and rearrange the summation indices again, giving the result.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Chaiken, Hanusa, and Zaslavsky
[
6
, section 5.4]
conjectured that all quasi-polynomials for the anassa have a factor of
(m)_{k}
. This conjecture can be proved true using Equation (
21
) and Lemma
2.6
:

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}2^{k-2j}\left[\binom{k-j}{j-1}+\binom{k-j+1}{j}\right]\sum_{p=0}^{k-j}\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p+k-j}{p}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}\frac{(m)_{p+k-j}(m-k+j)_{j}}{(p+k-j)!}.

It is straightforward to verify that
(m)_{p+k-j}(m-k+j)_{j}=(m)_{k}(m-k+j)_{p}
, hence,

\displaystyle A_{\mathbf{S}}(m,k)=(m)_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\lceil\frac{k}{2}\rceil}2^{k-2j}\left[\binom{k-j}{j-1}+\binom{k-j+1}{j}\right]\sum_{p=0}^{k-j}\left\{\mskip-8.0mu\left\{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{p+k-j}{p}\right\}\mskip-8.0mu\right\}\frac{(m-k+j)_{p}}{(p+k-j)!}.

Conclusion

Theorem
1.2
provides a way to establish recurrences for the number of possible nonattacking placements of bishops and anassas on the square board. The closed-form solutions to these recurrences are the quasi-polynomials (
5
) and (
21
), for bishops and anassas, respectively. These expressions answer some of the questions raised by Chaiken, Hanusa, and Zaslavsky
[
6
]
and allow the derivation of explicit formulas for the quasi-polynomial coefficients, results proved in Theorems
2.8
and
3.6
.

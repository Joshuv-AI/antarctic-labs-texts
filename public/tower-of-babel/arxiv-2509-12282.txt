AIssistant: An Agentic Approach for Human–AI Collaborative Scientific Work on Reviews and Perspectives in Machine Learning
Sasi Kiran Gaddipati †, Farhana Keya †, Gollam Rabby, Sören Auer

Abstract
Advances in AI-assisted research have introduced powerful tools for literature retrieval, hypothesis generation, experimentation, and manuscript preparation. However, systems remain fragmented and lack human-centered workflows. To address these gaps, we introduce AIssistant , an agentic, open-source Human-AI collaborative framework designed to simplify the end-to-end creation of scientific workflows. Since our development is still in an early stage, we present here the first experiments with AIssistant for perspective and review research papers in machine learning. Our system integrates modular tools and agents for literature synthesis, section-wise experimentation, citation management, and automatic LaTeX paper text generation, while maintaining human oversight at every stage to ensure accuracy, coherence, and scholarly rigor. We conducted a comprehensive evaluation across three layers: (1) Independent Human Review , following NeurIPS double-blind standards; (2) Automated LLM Review , using GPT-5 as a scalable human review proxy; and (3) Program Chair Oversight , where the chair monitors the entire review process and makes final validation and acceptance decisions. The results demonstrate that AIssistant improves drafting efficiency and thematic consistency. Nonetheless, Human-AI collaboration remains essential for maintaining factual correctness, methodological soundness, and ethical compliance. Despite its effectiveness, we identify key limitations, including hallucinated citations, difficulty adapting to dynamic paper structures, and incomplete integration of multimodal content.

III
AIssistant

AIssistant
is a Human-AI collaborative framework that facilitates high-quality scientific review and perspective workflows. It provides structured support across the research pipeline, from literature review to manuscript drafting, while allowing human intervention to ensure accuracy, methodological rigor, and adherence to scholarly standards. III-A

Literature Review Tools

The literature review stage identifies and curates relevant publications for subsequent tasks using literature retrieval APIs like Semantic Scholar
1
1

1

https://www.semanticscholar.org/
and ORKG ASK
2
2

2

https://ask.orkg.org/
. The workflow involves two key actions: i) The agent retrieves abstracts from the top
N
relevant publications based on the research query. ii) Selected abstracts are added to a curated review that informs the following stages. This process is iterative, with the agent refining paper selection until the predefined number of relevant texts (
N_{\text{max}}
) is reached, providing a comprehensive foundation for writing tasks.

III-B

Pipeline Execution

III-B
1
Plan Formulation

During plan formulation,
AIssistant
constructs a structured and actionable outline for the target manuscript. Drawing on the finalized literature review and proposed title, the system defines the scope, thematic organization, and logical flow of the research paper. Sections and subsections are explicitly specified and iteratively refined to ensure alignment with research goals, supporting evidence, and expected argumentation. This blueprint facilitates coherent manuscript construction and maintains thematic consistency throughout.

III-B
2
Data Preparation

In this stage, the system extracts references to datasets mentioned within the curated literature. Dataset descriptions, usage contexts, and relevant metadata are collected to provide context and support reproducibility. Currently, dataset extraction is limited to datasets explicitly cited in the retrieved literature. Future work aims to integrate external repositories, such as HuggingFace Datasets and Kaggle, to expand coverage and enhance comparability.

III-B
3
Implementation

AIssistant
automates the generation of both review and perspective papers through a multi-phase pipeline, while allowing human oversight at each stage. The main phases are as follows:

Ideation

Structured prompts and surprise detection methods generate multiple candidate research directions based on the initial context, such as existing artifacts or thematic prompts. Suggestions are presented in checklist form, enabling researchers to select, refine, or discard options, balancing innovation with domain expertise
[
4
]
.

Title Generation

Titles are generated to reflect research questions, contribution type, and contextual materials. Human researchers retain the ability to edit or replace titles to ensure accurate framing.

Tools Selection

The system identifies relevant tools and resources for literature retrieval, data processing, and citation management. Human supervision ensures that selected tools are appropriate for the research context.

Abstract Generation

The abstract agent produces concise summaries capturing the scope, key claims, and significance of the work. Distinct strategies are applied for review versus perspective papers, reflecting differences in rhetorical structure. Human collaborators can refine abstracts for clarity and accuracy.

Introduction Generation

The introduction agent contextualizes the topic, defines the problem space, and motivates the study. It integrates citations from the curated literature and allows human authors to adjust narrative flow and emphasis.

Related Work Generation

The related work agent synthesizes prior literature to situate the paper within the broader academic landscape. Review papers emphasize thematic analysis and synthesis, whereas perspective papers focus on critical evaluation of supporting and opposing perspectives. Humans may augment or verify the literature base as needed.

Methods & Implementation

For review papers, this component documents literature selection and synthesis procedures. Perspective papers describe conceptual or analytical methodology. Human oversight ensures clarity, reproducibility, ethical, and methodological rigor.

Results & Analysis

For review papers, findings are organized by themes or research questions; for perspective papers, key claims are articulated and supported with evidence. Human collaborators may refine interpretations, verify consistency, and adjust analyses for accuracy.

Conclusion Generation

The conclusion agent consolidates insights, emphasizes contributions, and identifies future research directions. Human authors may further refine conclusions to align with the intended framing and emphasis of the paper.

III-B
4
Research Paper Writing

The Research Paper Writing Agents integrate outputs from previous stages into a coherent, human-readable manuscript while maintaining academic standards.

Initial Manuscript Structure

An initial manuscript is generated with eight standardized sections:
Title
,
Abstract
,
Introduction
,
Related Work
,
Methods
,
Results
,
Conclusion
, and
References
. This framework establishes structured sections to facilitate content generation and ensure immediate LaTeX compilability.

Dynamic LaTeX Processing

To enhance logical flow, cross-referencing, and formatting, the concatenated section text is reprocessed. Researchers can control structure and style via tailored prompts or additional parameters.

Research Paper Editing

Finally, human researchers review and refine the manuscript for factual correctness, methodological accuracy, stylistic clarity, and ethical compliance. This ensures that the final output represents a responsible integration of human expertise and AI assistance while maintaining high academic quality.

IV
Experimentation

IV-A

Dataset Construction via Human-AI Collaboration

To evaluate
AIssistant
, we generated a dataset of 48 research papers generated through Human–AI collaboration, evenly split between 24 perspective papers and 24 review papers. Each paper was produced through a structured Human–AI collaborative workflow, where the assistant generated draft sections that were subsequently refined by expert human input to ensure transparency, minimize hallucinations, and maintain academic rigor. The dataset construction pipeline involved three key steps: i) Initiating the ideation phase to define the research scope. ii) Generating section-level content (
Title
,
Abstract
,
Introduction
,
Related Work
,
Methods
,
Results
,
Conclusions
) via
AIssistant
. iii) Iterative human review and refinement to ensure accuracy, coherence, and overall quality. Metadata for each paper includes category, topic domain, section-level generation logs, and LLM configuration details, providing a diverse and robust benchmark for evaluating AI-assisted academic workflows.

IV-B

Integration of State-of-the-Art LLMs

Content generation leveraged two different LLMs:
OpenAI o1
and
GPT-4o-mini
. Within
AIssistant
,
OpenAI o1
was responsible for complex reasoning, structural planning, and ensuring consistency across sections, while both of the LLMs efficiently generated and refined text segments. This dual-LLM setup balanced output quality, coherence, stylistic consistency, and computational efficiency. Additionally, it enabled a comparative analysis of generation patterns, error tendencies, and stylistic differences between LLMs, highlighting both the strengths and limitations of Human–AI collaborative research assistants.

IV-C

Context Window Management

A key limitation of LLMs is their fixed-size context window, which constrains the amount of information that can be processed
[
12
]
. This limitation is particularly critical for review and perspective papers, where large volumes of literature, notes, and drafts must be integrated. Without effective strategies, LLMs are prone to omissions, redundancy, or hallucinations. Within
AIssistant
, we evaluated
OpenAI o1
, capable of supporting broader reasoning, and
GPT-4o-mini
, which is faster and more cost-effective but more sensitive to prompt length. To address these challenges, we adopt a hierarchical strategy: inputs are segmented into reference clusters and outlines, routed to specialized agents, and compressed into summaries. Drafts are iteratively distilled to maintain continuity, while context allocation remains tunable to prioritize citations, structure, or methods. Automated compression, combined with human oversight, ensures coherent and reliable scientific workflows within context limitations.

IV-D

Implementation and Workflow

IV-D
1
Anchoring with Human-Provided References

Human experts provided seed references to contextualize the research scope. These anchors guided automated retrieval and generation, reducing domain drift and minimizing irrelevant outputs.

IV-D
2
Augmented Literature Retrieval

Literature suggestions were extracted using tools such as ORKG ASK and Semantic Scholar. Users curated relevant papers, which were stored in
AIssistant
’s asset management system. Curated bibliographies, together with titles and abstracts, informed the
Methods
component, while
Implementation
expanded on methodological details, including evaluation metrics.

IV-D
3
Human Roles in the Collaborative Workflow

Human collaborators with graduate-level expertise in machine learning supervised the AI throughout the research lifecycle. Their responsibilities spanned the pipeline: i) Reference Provision and Curation: collaborators provided initial seed references and curated AI-retrieved literature to ensure contextual relevance and domain alignment; ii) Draft Editing and Refinement: humans reviewed and iteratively refined section-level drafts, correcting inaccuracies, enhancing clarity, and ensuring logical flow; iii) Methodological and Epistemic Oversight: for both review and perspective papers, collaborators guided methodological framing, experimental design, and evaluation metrics, while supervising the AI’s reasoning to prevent hallucinations and maintain academic standards. This streamlined description emphasizes human authority in scientific correctness while highlighting AI’s role in scalable content drafting and synthesis, without repeating the detailed pipeline steps already provided in Section
III-B
.

IV-D
4
Independent Human Review

To ensure rigorous evaluation, independent reviewers were recruited according to NeurIPS standards. To mitigate evaluation bias, the researchers who developed
AIssistant
did not participate in the human review process, ensuring that assessment of outputs remained independent. Reviewers were not part of the generation process and were assigned manuscripts based on expertise alignment and conflict-of-interest checks. The review workflow included: i) Reviewer Selection: Ph.D. holders or advanced doctoral candidates with relevant publication records were recruited, with expertise matched to manuscript topics. ii) Conflict of Interest Management: Disclosures were required; conflicts triggered reassignment to maintain impartiality. iii) Double-Blind Review: Author and reviewer identities were anonymized, following NeurIPS double-blind standards. iv) Rubric-Based Assessment: Reviewers scored manuscripts on clarity, originality, technical soundness, significance, reproducibility, limitations, and ethical considerations. Missing or incomplete sections incurred penalties. v) Ethical Oversight: Reviewers flagged hallucinations, fabricated evidence, or ethically sensitive content to ensure scholarly integrity. vi) Redundancy and Aggregation: Each manuscript was reviewed by three independent reviewers; scores and qualitative feedback were aggregated to produce final evaluations. This process ensured that manuscripts generated with
AIssistant
were held to the same rigorous standards as conventional NeurIPS submissions.

IV-D
5
Automated LLM Review

GPT-5 was integrated as an agent-driven reviewer to provide scalable and reproducible evaluation signals. Selected as the latest OpenAI LLM, GPT-5 claims PhD-level reasoning and writing abilities, though independent validation is still required
[
9
]
. The LLM followed the same evaluation headers as human reviewers—clarity, originality, technical soundness, significance, reproducibility, limitations, and ethical considerations—with LLM-specific adaptations: i) Blind Assessment: manuscripts were reviewed without author identities; ii) Domain Adaptation: curated context windows mitigated knowledge cut-off and context-length limitations; iii) Structured Scoring: numerical scores with textual justifications ensured interpretability; iv) Consensus Estimation: multiple GPT-5 passes were aggregated and compared to human averages. By mirroring human review while adapting to LLM constraints, GPT-5 provided a complementary review layer, enabling systematic comparison and highlighting agreement and divergence in AI-assisted scholarly evaluation, and the results largely aligned with human assessments.

IV-D
6
Program Chair Observation

A Program Chair with PhD-level experience and 6–7 years of reviewing PhD-track and perspective/review papers provided expert evaluations complementary to LLM and human reviewer assessments. For each criterion, we report: i) Average: the mean of all scores assigned by the Chair across LLMs and tool-supported variants; ii) Range: the difference between the highest and lowest scores, reflecting variability across manuscripts; iii) Overall Trend: a qualitative judgment of the relative performance between LLM-assisted and human reviews. This presentation clarifies that averages and ranges summarize the Chair’s evaluations, while trends reflect expert judgment across all manuscript types and LLM configurations, highlighting strengths, weaknesses, and alignment without listing individual scores.

IV-D
7
Hardware Configurations

We evaluated the system’s performance across two different hardware configurations: i) Configuration 1: This system features 32 GB of RAM, 512 GB of storage, and an Intel Core Ultra 7 165H processor with 22 CPUs, complemented by an Intel Arc Graphics card. ii) Configuration 2: This system is equipped with 16 GB of RAM, 512 GB of disk capacity, and an 11th Gen Intel Core i5-1145G7 processor with 8 cores and Intel Xe Graphics. Both systems were used to run the OpenAI LLM, which does not require GPU resources. Evaluation results were assessed across both configurations, and no substantial differences were observed, as the currently used LLMs,
OpenAI o1
and
GPT-4o-mini
, do not rely on local GPU acceleration.

VI
Limitations & Ethical Considerations

Workflow Limitations

While
AIssistant
is effective, several limitations remain. Iterative improvement is restricted, and the pipeline currently lacks image-generation capabilities. Managing citations, formatting, and page structure remains challenging under the independent agent design, indicating a need for global parameters or better inter-agent communication. Paper refinement and structured reviews are only partially supported, and integrating a formal review mechanism could improve evaluation fidelity. The system also does not optimize cross-domain LLM usage; leveraging specialized LLMs for reasoning-intensive versus simpler tasks could enhance subsection-specific performance and overall quality. Addressing these limitations is essential for achieving robust, scalable, and adaptable AI-assisted scientific writing.

Dynamic Paper Structure Challenges

The workflow assumes a static, linear pipeline with predefined agents. In practice, researchers often have evolving goals that require flexible adaptation of the paper structure. Current Human-AI workflows have limited ability to dynamically interpret requirements, determine appropriate sections, and iteratively refine content. Human-AI collaboration remains essential: the LLM drafts content, while humans guide structure, adjust parameters, and verify references for accuracy and coherence.

Hallucination Challenges

Human-AI approaches often struggle to generate novel, feasible, and accurate ideas. Hallucinated or missing citations can mislead researchers and compromise workflow integrity. Users must verify that LLM-generated statements and references are correct. When generating bibliographies in
LaTeX
, LLMs may alter titles or formatting despite explicit instructions, necessitating careful human oversight.

Common Failure Modes

Experiments revealed recurrent citation errors, even when using reasoning-capable LLMs such as
OpenAI o1
. Formatting issues also arise with tables and structured elements, as the pipeline does not fully account for diverse conventions. These failures highlight the need for improved citation verification and formatting-aware mechanisms.

Sample Size Limitation
The evaluation covered 48 papers (24 review and 24 perspective), which, while sufficient for identifying key trends, is insufficient for drawing definitive statistical conclusions. Future work will expand this study to a larger set of papers and domains to enhance the reliability and generalizability of the findings.

Domain Coverage Limitations

Our evaluation is based on 48 machine learning papers and does not include subdomain-level analysis. Since results were aggregated across all papers, potential subdomain-specific variations remain unexplored. Future work will expand the dataset and incorporate subdomain-level evaluations to provide a more comprehensive assessment of
AIssistant
’s performance and enhance the generalizability of our findings.

Ethical Considerations

AIssistant
accelerates research by automating time-intensive tasks, but the autonomous generation of reviews and papers may lower barriers to producing substandard or misleading content, potentially overloading the peer-review process. Human-AI collaboration may also amplify biases from training data or prior outputs. Transparent disclosure of LLM involvement is essential. Misuse risks exist in fields such as chemistry, physics, cybersecurity, environmental studies, biomedical research, social sciences, and finance. As AI capabilities expand, governance, bias mitigation, and accountability are crucial to ensure outputs remain ethically sound, scientifically rigorous, and socially responsible. In line with the EU AI Act, responsibility for ethical use is distributed between system providers and humans, underscoring the need for accountable integration of
AIssistant
into scholarly infrastructures

]
.

VII
Conclusion

We introduced
AIssistant
, an open-source Human–AI collaborative framework designed to accelerate scientific workflows in machine learning. Unlike fully automated systems, our framework adopts a human-centered approach, supporting collaborative research exploration. Through experiments, we evaluated the quality, usefulness, and rigor of end-to-end workflows. Our results demonstrate that while both
GPT-4o-mini
and
OpenAI o1
proved highly effective,
OpenAI o1
achieved the highest experimental quality, offering superior efficiency in terms of runtime and cost. However, gaps remain between human expectations and LLM performance, particularly regarding clarity, soundness, and citation accuracy, highlighting the need for structured human oversight in AI-assisted research. We also positioned
AIssistant
within the broader AI-for-science landscape. Unlike previous systems such as Agent Laboratory and AI Scientist, our framework uniquely addresses the need for structured review and perspective workflows in machine learning, providing the first dedicated pipeline for these tasks. As noted in the Limitations and Ethical Considerations sections, challenges persist, including hallucinations, adapting document structures, and the need for formalized review mechanisms. Future work will focus on enhancing the pipeline by integrating specialized reasoning LLMs, reducing hallucinations, enabling multimodal support, and developing adaptive workflows. Additionally, we plan to replace OpenAI LLMs with a
open-source
domain-specific scientific reasoning LLM

AIssistant
represents a significant step toward efficient, human-centered research workflows that combine the scalability of LLMs with the judgment of human researchers, reducing repetitive tasks, fostering collaboration, and accelerating scientific discovery while maintaining accountability and integrity.

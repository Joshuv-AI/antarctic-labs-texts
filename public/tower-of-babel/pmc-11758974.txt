Setting up a transgender-friendly service in a tertiary care hospital: The challenges and solutions
Authors: Manoj K. Sahoo, Chandrima Naskar, Vinita Singh, Akash Panigrahi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Transgender and gender diverse (TGD) individuals face significant barriers to healthcare, necessitating the development of TGD-friendly medical services. In India, healthcare systems have only recently begun addressing the unique needs of TGD individuals, particularly with the advent of the Transgender Persons Act 2019. This article outlines the establishment of a comprehensive TGD clinic within a multidisciplinary framework. Services span psychiatric support, endocrinology, and surgical interventions, including gender-affirming medical and surgical treatments (GAMSTs). Adherence to WPATH Standards of Care (SOC-8) ensures informed, patient-centered treatment for gender incongruence. The clinic also emphasizes pretreatment evaluations, hormone therapy protocols, surgical readiness, and regular monitoring. Additionally, patient education, consent processes, and social transition support are integral to the clinic’s approach. Challenges include stigma reduction, staff training, financial sustainability for long-term treatments, and navigating gendered hospital infrastructure. The clinic’s experience with 50 TGD individuals, including 18 who underwent gender reassignment surgeries, demonstrates the feasibility of such a service. Future goals include expanding services to nonemployees, providing advanced surgeries, and enhancing mental health support. This model serves as a framework for the creation of inclusive, holistic healthcare services for TGD individuals in India, aiming to improve both physical and psychological wellbeing.

INTRODUCTION

Transgender and gender diverse (TGD) is an umbrella term to describe individuals whose gender identity is different from the gender assigned at birth. It also includes those who are gender nonbinary and gender nonconforming, that is, whose gender identity is outside that of the male–female binary. TGDs are different from intersex individuals as they possess no structural genital ambiguity. In the current update of Standards of Care (SOC 8) of World Professional Association for TGD Health’s (WPATH), the newer more inclusive term ‘TGD and Gender Diverse (TGD)’ has been introduced to reduce such confusion.[1] While documented accounts of TGD individuals have been recovered from even ancient texts, the terms transgender, gender identity, gender roles, and so on have emerged in their current form in 1950s. With that, gender discussions came to the forefront in the past 5–6 decades.[2] While gender diversity has been a sociopolitically significant domain, the healthcare system also remains markedly integrated with it. Starting from the debate regarding TGD being a normal variation versus a disorder till making provision for their adequate treatment, management, care, and support, healthcare professionals have been closely involved in this revolution.

In India, up until the past 1–2 decades, medical care did not pay distinct attention to cater to the TGD individuals, be it for the usual access to medical care or providing the medical and surgical care specifically needed for gender reassignment. The lack of dedicated TGD-friendly healthcare services led to their poor treatment seeking. The need for TGD-friendly/TGD-specific healthcare systems is not only to cater to their transitioning related treatment but also to provide safe, individualized medical care for them.

With the advent of Transgender Persons Act 2019,[3] prohibition against discrimination toward TGD persons, recognition of identity of TGD person, obligation of appropriate Government toward them, obligation of educational institutions to provide inclusive education to them, and nondiscrimination in employment have been legally mandated. In terms of medical care, the act mandates the Government to provide HIV surveillance, medical care facility including sex reassignment surgery and hormonal therapy, provision of counseling, a health manual, and provision for coverage of medical expenses for transition by a comprehensive insurance scheme. While the IDEA group consensus statements are available, there is no Indian guideline yet to guide different aspects of gender affirmation treatment, creating apprehension and confusion among clinicians.

WPATH was formed in 1979 by endocrinologist and sexologist Dr. Harry Benjamin, and since then, they have published regularly updated versions of flexible clinical guidelines for care of TGDs called the Standard of Care (SOC). Over the years, currently in its eighth edition, SOC have inculcated progressive research to guide individuals or institutions initiate and maintain TGD-friendly clinics.[1] In India, TGD-focused setups were initially started by individual initiatives or through Non-Government Organizations with overseas or personal funding. A few examples of such initiatives are Mitr Trust in New Delhi, Sakha in Odissa, Dostana Safar in Bihar, and Bandhan and Rista in West Bengal. While Kerala health ministry started the queer-friendly hospital initiative in 2017, the exact details of the same are yet unavailable. At the Central Government level, AIIMS Bhopal has started their TGD Clinic Services since February 2024 as a collaborative model between Department of Psychiatry, Endocrinology, Urology, and Plastic Surgery.

Since Tata Steel Limited has been actively working against stereotypical biases in person recruitment, it targets to increase representation of diverse workforce in Tata Steel to achieve a goal of 25% of diverse workforce by 2025. The Medical Services have been adapted to provide necessary support the needs of TGD persons of its workforce. For a seamless functioning of the same, a detailed protocol has been devised by a multidisciplinary medical and surgical team and has been in place for 24 months with good clinical results. This article attempts to provide insight on how to replicate similar service in different institutions.

CHALLENGES

Stigma remains high and leads to low accessibility to TGD clinics. It is essential to improve the knowledge and awareness of healthcare providers as well as the associated staff in the hospital. Increasing their empathy and flexibility toward the care-seekers requires repeated training and gradual normalization. More objective challenges are to decide in which ward (male or female) the individual should be provided admission, making provision for the finances for long-term regular Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT) as well as surgical interventions, developing an economically sustainable care plan, developing standard operating procedures for dealing with complications and consequences, and providing mental health support at every step of the transition and its consequences. For starting a TGD clinic, challenges can be multifarious starting from the availability of funds, recruiting the required set of specialists, establishing a board for certifications and decision making in complicated scenarios, and deciding the extent of care that can be made available [i.e., only HRT or HRT and Gender Reassignment Surgery (GRS) or HRT with GRS and cosmetic procedures].

Experiences from the clinic

Till now, 50 TGD individuals are seeking treatment from the hospital for various clinical needs. Among these, seven are trans female and the rest are trans male. All of them are on hormone therapy. Amid all, 18 have undergone GRS, of which three were male to female. The rest all were female to male. All the individuals have continued to stay on follow-up with good outcome and adherence.

Future goals

We have yet provided treatment to only our employees in our clinic. Considering the good outcomes and feasibility of the treatment, soon, we plan to make the services readily available to nonemployees too. In our experience, TG clinics need to increase in number to improve the availability and accessibility of care to these unique groups of help-seekers. It is imperative that comprehensive TG clinics might be difficult to establish at one go. Initially starting from providing HRT, then expanding to surgical capacities of different complexities, and ultimately developing provision for associated medical cosmetic facilities like LASER hair removal can be achieved in a step-by-step fashion to support holistic transition. Setting up a new TGD clinic or making one’s clinic TGD-friendly can seem daunting at the beginning, so more publications on the experiences of setting up such clinics can provide first hand narratives and unique ways of navigating challenges. This document thus can help in guiding future clinics at their initiative. The key guiding points for developing a transgender friendly clinic, as per our experiences, have been summarised in Box 1.

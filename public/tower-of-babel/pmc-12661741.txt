Connection between granulosa cell pyroptosis and oocyte endoplasmic reticulum stress in a mouse model of polycystic ovary syndrome
Authors: Yan Zhang, Xianguo Xie, Lei Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionLow oocyte maturation rate was a common clinical manifestation in patients with polycystic ovary syndrome. Whether ovarian granulosa pyroptosis ultimately leads to low oocyte maturation rate through endoplasmic reticulum stress in oocytes is currently lacking in relevant research.Materials and methodsA PCOS model was established by treating mice with dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and a CPA + PCOS ameliorator model was established by treating mice with the androgen antagonist cyproterone acetate (CPA) combined with DHEA. The levels of pyroptosis and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress-related proteins were measured. In addition, an culture system in vitro oocyte was established. After inhibiting pyroptosis of granulosa cells with CPA and the pyroptosis inhibitor disulfiram (DMF), the levels of ER stress-related proteins in oocytes and the oocyte maturation rate were determined.ResultsCompared with the control group, the expression levels of pyroptosis-related proteins such as GSDMD and Caspase-1 in granulosa cells and ER stress-related proteins such as p-IRE1α and p-PERK in oocytes were significantly increased in the PCOS group induced by high androgen levels, while the oocyte maturation rate was significantly decreased. After treatment with CPA and DMF, the expression levels of pyroptosis-related proteins in ovarian granulosa cells and ER stress-related proteins in oocytes were significantly lower than those in the PCOS group, while the oocyte maturation rate was significantly higher than those in the DHT group. In an in vitro oocyte co-culture system, the expression levels of pyroptosis-related proteins in granulosa cells and ER stress-related proteins in oocytes were significantly upregulated in the DHT group compared with the control group. After treatment with CPA and DMF, the expression levels of pyroptosis-related proteins in granulosa cells and ER stress-related proteins in oocytes were significantly lower than in the DHT group.ConclusionHigh androgen induced pyroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells in a mouse model of PCOS, leading to ER stress in oocytes and a decrease of the oocyte maturation rate. Effective alleviation of granulosa cell pyroptosis can inhibit ER stress in oocytes and improve their maturation rate. This study provides new therapeutic targets for improving oocyte maturation in PCOS.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13048-025-01841-w.

Introduction

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a disease mainly occurring in reproductive-aged women, leading to reproductive dysfunction and endocrine metabolic disorders. Clinically characterized by chronic anovulation, hyperandrogenism, hyperinsulinemia or insulin resistance, it presents with menstrual irregularities, infertility, hirsutism, acne, and obesity [1, 2]. Many studies have shown that long-term exposure to a high androgen environment can lead to follicular development disorders and polycystic changes, which are important causes of adverse pregnancy outcomes and infertility in PCOS patients [3, 4]. Studies on assisted reproduction also suggest that although PCOS patients produce more oocytes, their maturity, normal fertilization rate, high-quality embryo rate, usable embryo rate, and blastocyst formation rate are significantly decreased. This indicates that the quality of oocytes is significantly affected in PCOS patients [5–7].

Granulosa cells are the somatic cells that directly contact and provide nutritional support to oocytes through paracrine and autocrine mechanisms, engaging in bidirectional signal transduction between cells, ultimately promoting normal follicular development and maturation. Therefore, the proliferation and death of granulosa cells are necessary for maintaining normal oocyte development during follicular growth and differentiation [8, 9], with studies showing that when the death rate of granulosa cells is ≥ 10%, follicular atresia occurs [10]. Other studies in mice have also shown that high androgen stimulation induces chronic low-grade inflammation in the ovaries, activates the NLRP3 inflammasome, which in turn induces pyroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells, follicular dysfunction, and fibrosis of ovarian stromal cells, resulting in a series of pathological changes [11]. Preliminary studies in this project revealed that patients with a predominance of high androgens exhibit granulosa cell pyroptosis in the ovaries, exacerbating the secretion of pro-inflammatory factors and further worsening the local inflammatory response in the ovaries [12, 13].

The synthesis of new proteins is crucial for the maturation of oocytes and subsequent embryonic development [14, 15]. As the main site for protein and lipid synthesis and secretion, the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) plays a key role in meeting the demand for new proteins during oocyte maturation, prior to embryo implantation [15, 16]. In studies on the culture of porcine oocytes, it was found that GRP78 is present in the GV, GVBD, MI, and MII stages of oocyte development. The expression level of GRP78 mRNA in cumulus-oocyte complexes (COCs) cultured for 44 h was found to be significantly increased compared to 22 h of culture, suggesting that ER stress may lead to delayed maturation of porcine oocytes [17]. In mouse GV oocytes, XBP1 protein is mainly located in the nucleus, which is located on the spindle microtubules in the MI stage, also indicating the crucial role of the ER stress signaling pathway in the maturation of mouse oocytes [18, 19]. However, in studies on PCOS, pyroptosis of granulosa cells was found to exacerbate the local chronic inflammatory response of the ovaries. However, whether this can cause a low maturation rate by inducing ER stress in oocytes has not been reported yet. The aim of this project was to study the correlation of the pyroptosis of granulosa cells induced by hyperandrogenism in PCOS with the oocyte maturation rate, and then explore the mechanisms through which pyroptosis causes low oocyte maturation rates.

Research methods and materials

Research materials

Experimental animals

The C57BL/6 mice used in this study (purchased from Ningxia Medical University, Yinchuan, China) had an age of 4 weeks and an average weight of approximately 15 g. All corresponding experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Ningxia University (ethics approval no. NXU-2020-012).

The mouse models of the PCOS group, control group, and treatment group were constructed as follows: five mice in the PCOS group were injected subcutaneously with dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA; 60 mg/kg, 12 mg/mL in Cremophor EL) for 21 days. Five mice in the control group were injected subcutaneously with the same dose of Cremophor EL as the PCOS group for 21 days; five mice in the treatment group were injected subcutaneously with DHEA and cyproterone acetate (CPA; 20 mg/kg, 4 mg/mL dissolved in Cremophor EL) for 21 days. The rearing conditions of all mice were as follows: a constant temperature of 22 °C, humidity of 50–60%, light/dark cycle of 12/12 h, with standard chow (Ningxia Medical University, China) and drinking water ad libitum.

Isolation of primary granulosa cells from mice

Primary granulosa cells from mice were collected, which were used as the experimental material. ICR mouse were Inject PMSG (Pregnant mares’ serum gonadotropin, with an injection volume of 5 IU/10 g, intraperitoneal injection). After 44 h, remove the ovaries of the ICR mice and place them in a medium-sized dish containing HX-M199 medium(purchased from Gibco Company); puncture the follicles with a needle to allow the granulosa cells to flow out naturally; transfer the culture medium containing the granulosa cells to a centrifuge tube, centrifuge at 3000 rpm for 5 min; discard the supernatant, resuspend the mouse ovarian granulosa cells in HX-M199 medium, and distribute 1 mL of the medium per well into 6-well plate cell culture dishes. After adherence, the culture medium was changed for 1–2 days to about 70% confluence. DHT solution (10 mM DHT dissolved in DMSO, final concentration was 10µM) was added to the DHT group for 24 h. DMSO with the same volume as in the DHT group was added to the control group for 24 h. Then, 10 µM DHT solution was added to the treatment group, and after 2 h of action, CPA solution was added (10 mM CPA dissolved in DMSO, final concentration was 10µM) for 22 h.

The cells of the DHT + DMF group were treated with 0, 25, 50, and 100 µM DMF solutions in DMOS for 24 h, followed by the CCK8 assay. Based on the viability results, 25 µM was selected as the appropriate concentration of DMF.

In vitro co-culture system of oocytes and granulosa cells

Primary mouse granulosa cells were cultured in M199 medium (Gibco Company of the United States).CPA or DMF solution was added. At the same time, cumulus oocyte complexes (COCs) were obtained from mouse ovaries, and digested using trypsin (final concentration was 0.25%; Gibco Company of the United States) to isolate the oocytes, odroplets containing granulosa cells and cultured in an incubator at 37 ℃ with 5% O₂, which were then transferred into M199 medium micrand 5% CO2 for 16 h to establish the co-culture systems.

Methods

Western blot

SDS-PAGE gels (PG113, Yamei, China) were used for electrophoresis, after which the bands were transferred to a PVDF membrane (Millipore, R1JB38278), which was blocked with 5% skim milk (BD, 1053907) for 1 h at room temperature. The primary antibodies (GSDMD, CST, #46451; GSDMD-N, CST, #36425; caspase-1-p20, CST, #89332; P-ATF6, Affinity, #AF4507; ATF6, Affinity, #DF6009; p-IRE1α, abcam, ab124945; IRE1α, CST, #3294; p-PERK, CST, #3179; PERK, CST, #3192) were incubated at 4 °C overnight, followed by washing with PBST (Tween 20: Solarbio, T8220, PBS: Servicebio, G0002) 3 times, for 5 min each time. Then, the membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies (Proteintech, SA00001-1/SA00001-2) and washed with PBST 3 times, for 5 min each time. Finally, ECL (ThermoFisher, XA338899) chemiluminescence reagent was used to develop the specific bands.

Immunohistochemistry

Mouse ovaries were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (Solarbio, P1110) overnight at 4 °C. Samples were cut into 5 μm thick sections, transferred to 3-aminopropyl triethoxysilane-treated slides (ZSBB-Bio, ZLI-9001), dehydrated in ethanol and toluene, embedded in paraffin, and stained according to the instructions of the IHC Kit (ZSBB-Bio, PV-9001). DAB reagent was used to detect antibody complexes according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The antibodies used in the experiment (against GSDMD, Caspase-1, p-ATF6 and P-IRE1α) were all purchased from Affinity Biosciences LTD in the United States.

RNA isolation and qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted using the FastPure Cell/Tissue Total RNA Isolation Kit (Vazyme, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and then reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA with HiScript SuperMix (Vazyme, China). The qRT-PCR analysis was performed using ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, China) on LightCycler 480 (Roche, Basel, Switzerland), and the level of GAPDH was used as a normalization control. The mRNA levels of gene expression were analyzed by the 2-ΔΔCt method. The primers used are as follows (5′−3′),

Mouse GSDMD forward: CTAGCTAAGGCTCTGGAGACAA.

Mouse GSDMD reverse: GATTCTTTTCATCCCAGCAGTC.

Mouse caspase-1 forward: AGAGGATTTCTTAACGGATGCA.

Mouse caspase-1 reverse: TCACAAGACCAGGCATATTCTT.

Mouse GAPDH forward: CGTTGACATCCGTAAAGACC.

Mouse GAPDH reverse: TAGGAGCCAGAGCAGTAATC.

Cellular Immunofluorescence

The cells were fixed with fixing solution (Beijing Soleibao Technology Co. LTD, China) for 15 min and washed with PBS 3 times, 5 min each time, followed by permeabilization with 0.1% TritonX-100 for 15 min, and washing with PBS 3 times, 5 min each time. Then, 10% donkey serum was used for blocking for 30 min, after which the blocking solution was absorbed, and the primary antibodies against GSDMD (1:100, Affinity), p-IRE1α (1:100, Affinity), p-ATF6 (1:100, Affinity), and p-PERK (1:100, CST) were added and incubated at 4 °C overnight. Finally, the slides were incubated with the secondary antibody and Hoechst solution with a concentration of 2 mg/L at room temperature for 60 min, avoiding light exposure. After washing with PBS three times, with a 5-minute interval between each wash, a laser confocal fluorescence microscope was used for observation with an excitation wavelength of 488 nm.

CCK8 assay

The granulosa cells were collected and inoculated into a 96-well plate. After the cells fully adhered to the wall, different concentrations of DMF were added for treatment. After 24 h, the cell viability assay was performed by adding 10µL of CCK-8 solution (Beijing Soleibao Technology Co. LTD, China) to each well. The wells with corresponding amounts of cell culture medium and CCK-8 solution but without added cells served as blank controls. The cells were incubated for another 30 min, and the absorbance was measured at 450 nm.

Statistical analysis

The results were obtained from at least three or more repeated experiments. The ImageJ software 1.8.0 was used to analyze the gray values of the western blot results. GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 software was used for comparisons, and SPSS22.0 software was used for the statistical analysis of clinical data. Measurement data with a normal distribution were expressed as means ± standard deviations (SD), and comparisons between groups were performed using the independent-sample t-test. Counting data were expressed as percentages, and comparisons between groups were performed using the χ2 test. A p-value < 0.05 was taken as the minimal threshold for statistical significance.

Results

Androgen-lowering therapy alleviated the pyroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells and improve the maturation rate of oocytes in a mouse model of PCOS

CPA is a synthetic derivative of 17-hydroxyprogesterone that binds to androgen receptors, exerting an antiandrogenic effect. Additionally, CPA can inhibit 5α-reductase, accelerating the clearance of testosterone. It is commonly used clinically to improve the hyperandrogenic state of patients with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). In this study, western blot analysis was used to detect the expression levels of pyroptosis-related proteins GSDMD, GSDMD-N, and caspase-1-p20. Compared to the control group, the expression of all three pyroptosis-related proteins was significantly upregulated in the ovaries of PCOS model mice. After androgen reduction treatment, the expression levels of GSDMD, GSDMD-N, and caspase-1-p20 were significantly downregulated in the CPA + PCOS group compared to the PCOS group (Figs. 1A-B). In addition, immunohistochemistry was used to determine the expression levels of pyroptosis proteins GSDMD and caspase-1 in the three groups. Compared to the control group, the expression levels of GSDMD and caspase-1 were significantly increased in ovarian granulosa cells of the PCOS model mice. After androgen reduction treatment, the expression levels of GSDMD and caspase-1 in the CPA + PCOS group were significantly decreased compared to the PCOS group (Fig. 1C-D). The results of the maturity assessment of oocytes from the three groups of mice indicated that the oocyte maturity rate of the DHT group was significantly lower than that of the control group, while the oocyte maturity rate of the CPA + PCOS group was significantly higher than that of the DHT group (Figs. 1E-F). Thus, high androgen levels can lead to pyroptosis in ovarian granulosa cells, and after androgen reduction treatment, the phenomenon of pyroptosis in ovarian granulosa cells of PCOS model mice significantly improved.

Androgen-lowering therapy alleviated ER stress in oocytes of PCOS model mice

This study employed western blot analysis to assess the expression levels of ER stress-related proteins. The results indicated that the ratios of P-IRE1α/IRE1α and p-PERK/PERK were significantly higher in the ovaries of PCOS model mice compared to the control group. However, there was no significant difference in the level of p-ATF6 protein between the two groups. Following androgen reduction treatment, the ratios of p-IRE1α/IRE1α and p-PERK/PERK in the CPA + PCOS group were notably lower than in the PCOS model mice. Similarly, there was no statistical difference in the levels of p-ATF6 protein between the two groups (Figs. 2A-B). Additionally, immunohistochemical methods were used to detect the expression and localization of ER related proteins in ovarian tissues of the three groups of mice. The findings revealed that the level of p-IRE1α protein in oocytes of the PCOS group was significantly higher than in the control group. However, there was no difference in the levels of p-ATF6 between the two groups. In the CPA + PCOS group, the protein level of p-IRE1α was significantly reduced compared to the PCOS group, while there was no difference in the levels of p-ATF6 between the two groups of oocytes (Fig. 2C-D). From this, it can be inferred that high androgen levels can induce ER stress and activate the IRE1α and PERK pathways in oocytes, without significant phosphorylation of ATF6. After androgen reduction treatment, the activation of these two pathways in oocytes and the resulting ER stress can be effectively alleviated.

CPA simultaneously alleviated the occurrence of pyroptosis in granulosa cells and ER stress in oocytes co-cultured in vitro

This study employed an in vitro co-culture system of mouse oocytes. Initially, primary mouse granulosa cells were treated with DHT and CPA to establish the control group, DHT group, and DHT + CPA group, respectively. After culturing the cells for 24 h, oocytes were added and co-cultured for 16 h. The mRNA levels of the pyroptosis-related proteins caspase-1 and GSDMD in the three groups of mouse granulosa cells were measured using qRT-PCR. The results indicated that the mRNA levels of caspase-1 and GSDMD were significantly upregulated in the DHT group, while they were significantly downregulated in the DHT + CPA group (Fig. 3A). The protein expression levels of GSDMD detected by immunofluorescence were consistent with the results of qRT-PCR (Fig. 3B-C). The levels of ER related proteins were assessed in the co-cultured oocytes from the three groups. Compared to the control group, the levels of p-IRE1a, p-PERK, and p-ATF6 were significantly increased in the DHT group oocytes. Compared to the DHT group, the levels of p-IRE1a and p-PERK were significantly decreased in the DHT + CPA group, while there was no statistical difference in the expression pattern of p-ATF6 (Fig. 3D-E). The in vitro experimental results demonstrated that high androgen treatment can lead to pyroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells and ER stress in oocytes, while the antagonistic effect of CPA on androgen action can significantly alleviate pyroptosis of granulosa cells and ER stress in oocytes.

Utilizing the pyroptosis inhibitor DMF to specifically inhibit pyroptosis of granulosa cells can alleviate ER stress in oocytes

Using CCK8 to detect the safe concentration of DMF acting on mouse ovarian granulosa cells, the final concentration of DMF was set at 25 µM (Fig. 4A). After treating primary mouse granulosa cells with DMF and DHT, oocytes were added to establish the control group, DHT group, and DHT + DMF, respectively. After 16 h of co-culture, the expression levels of ER related proteins in the oocytes were determined. The results indicated that compared to the control group, the levels of p-IRE1a and p-PERK were significantly increased in oocytes from the DHT group, while the levels of p-IRE1α and p-PERK were significantly decreased in oocytes from the DHT + DMF group oocytes compared to the DHT group (Fig. 4B-C). The above results demonstrate that inhibiting pyroptosis of granulosa cells using the specific inhibitor DMF significantly decreased the phosphorylation levels of IRE1α and PERK in oocytes. This proves that high androgen-induced pyroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells in PCOS can lead to ER stress in oocytes.

Discussion

Pyroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells leads to decreased oocyte maturity in PCOS

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common reproductive endocrine disease in women. Despite the presence of numerous follicles, PCOS patients have a highly reduced number of high-quality mature oocytes, hindering normal meiosis and fertilization [20, 21]. Granulosa cells (GCs) are the largest cell population in the ovary, with the main function of supporting and promoting oocyte growth as well as providing a suitable microenvironment for follicular development and oocyte maturation. Preliminary studies in this project also found that in granulosa cells from PCOS patients characterized by high androgen levels, there is an increase in the expression levels of NLRP3 inflammasome and pyroptosis-related proteins such as GSDMD. This, accompanied by the occurrence of granulosa cell pyroptosis, leads to the release of high levels of IL-1β and IL-18, exacerbating local inflammation in the ovary. Recent studies have shown that high androgen-induced activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and subsequent pyroptosis of GCs in the ovaries of PCOS model mice lead to ovarian fibrosis and dysfunction [22, 23]. In this study, both in vitro and in vivo experiments demonstrated that high androgen levels can indeed induce granulosa cell pyroptosis in the ovary, while after androgen reduction treatment, granulosa cell pyroptosis was significantly alleviated, consistent with the above research results. At the same time, with the occurrence of granulosa cell pyroptosis, the maturation rate of oocytes in PCOS model mice was significantly lower than in the control group, and effective alleviation of granulosa cell pyroptosis through androgen reduction or the use of pyroptosis inhibitors effectively improved oocyte maturation.

Pyroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells leads to ER stress in oocytes

Ovarian follicles consist of an oocyte, granulosa cells, and Zona pellucida cells. Follicular development is a well-coordinated process that includes oocyte maturation and the proliferation and differentiation of somatic cells, which are regulated by gonadotropins and intraovarian local factors that affect the follicular microenvironment [24, 25]. Recently, it has been revealed that ER stress in the follicular microenvironment, followed by activation of the UPR, plays an important role in ovarian physiology and pathology [26–28].In the granulosa cells of PCOS patients and androgen-induced PCOS mice, ER stress and UPR are both activated, working in concert with some local factors to cause chronic inflammatory responses in the follicular microenvironment of PCOS patients [28, 29]. The results of this study reveal that pyroptosis of PCOS model ovarian granulosa cells leads to the activation of the IRE1α and PERK pathways in oocytes, along with ER activation, mitigating granulosa cell pyroptosis, which in turn effectively alleviated ER stress in oocytes.

Relieving ovarian granulosa cell pyroptosis downregulated ER stress in oocytes, which in turn effectively improved oocyte maturation

Research has shown [30] that there are gap junctions between granulosa cells and oocytes, through which granulosa cells transmit various information-carrier molecules and materials to oocytes, promoting their growth and maturation. Some studies have shown [31, 32] that when granulosa cells are stimulated by these external factors, the ratio of pro- and antiapoptotic members in the B-cell lymphoma 2 (Bcl-2) family becomes imbalanced, causing granulosa cells to undergo apoptosis, follicles to lose their biological functions, ultimately leading to the apoptosis of oocytes and follicular membrane cells, resulting in follicular atresia. This study indicates that PCOS induces ER stress in oocytes due to pyroptosis of granulosa cell, leading to a decrease of the oocyte maturation rate. Inhibition of ovarian granulosa cell pyroptosis resulted in a significant amelioration of oocyte ER stress, accompanied by a significant increase of the oocyte maturation rate. Therefore, pyroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells caused ER stress in oocytes, leading to a decrease of their maturation rate, while inhibition of granulosa cell pyroptosis reduced oocyte ER stress, ultimately leading to an increase of the oocyte maturation rate.

Conclusions

Our study found that hyperandrogenism induces pyroptosis of ovarian granulosa cells in PCOS, leading to ER stress in oocytes and a decrease of their maturation rate. By effectively alleviating granulosa cell pyroptosis, ER stress in oocytes can be suppressed, thereby improving the oocyte maturation rate. This study reveals a new therapeutic target, which may inspire potential clinical approaches for improving the maturation rate of oocytes in PCOS patients.

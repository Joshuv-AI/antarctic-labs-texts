Abstract

Introduction and main results

Given two irreducible polynomials
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
over a filed
{\Bbbk}
. The rings
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1}^{n})}
and
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2}^{n})}
are local rings, hence if they are isomorphic then their residue fields
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1})}
and
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2})}
are also isomorphic.

One can wonder if the converse assertion is true. In that case, we get that
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1}^{n})}
and
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2}^{n})}
are isomorphic for all
n\geq 1
.

We show that the converse is true if
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are seprable (irreducible) polynomials. In particular, the converse holds for any two polynomails over a perfect field as fields of characteristic
0
or algebraic extensions of finite fileds. This is show in section
1
. More precisely, we show that for a seprable irreducible polynomial
P
, we have a
{\Bbbk}
-algebra isomorphism between
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{n})
and
({\Bbbk}[X]/(P))[Y]/(Y^{n})
(theorem
1.7
), and deduce the converse from it (theorem
1.8
).

In section
2
, We develop criterias (for
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
irreducible) under wich the existence of certain isomorphisms
f:{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1})}\to{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2})}
or
f_{m}:{\Bbbk}[X]/(P_{1}^{m})\to{\Bbbk}[X]/(P_{2}^{m})
imply that
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1}^{n})}
and
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2}^{n})}
are isomorphic for all
n\geq 1
(corollary
2.9
and theorem
2.10
). These criteria apply to the case where
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are not seprable and they are obtained by constructing lifts of
f:{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1})}\to{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2})}
to morphisms
f_{X,n}:{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1}^{n})}\to{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2}^{n})}
.

1.
Seprable case

Let
{\Bbbk}
be a field and
P
be an irreducible polynomial over
{\Bbbk}
. In this section, we show that if
P
is seprable
(i.e.\ P^{\prime}\neq 0)
then
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{n})
is isomorphic as a
{\Bbbk}
-algebra to
({\Bbbk}[X]/(P))[Y]/(Y^{n})
(theorem
1.7
). From this we deduce that if
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are two seprable polynomials, then the local rings
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1}^{n})}
and
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2}^{n})}
are isomorphic if and only if their residue fields
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1})}
and
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2})}
are isomophic (theorem
1.8
).

Lemma 1.1
.

For
Q\in{\Bbbk}[X]
, we have :

P(X+Q(X))=P(X)+P^{\prime}(X)Q(X)+R(X)Q(X)^{2},

for some
R\in{\Bbbk}[X]
.

Proof.

Set
P(X)=\sum a_{i}X^{i}
. We get

P(X+Q(X))=\sum a_{i}(X+Q(X))^{i}=\sum a_{i}(X^{i}+iX^{i-1}Q(X)+R_{i}(X)Q(X)^{2}),

for some
R_{i}\in{\Bbbk}[X]
. But
\underset{i>0}{\sum}ia_{i}x^{i-1}=P^{\prime}(X)
and
\sum a_{i}X^{i}=P(X)
. This proves the proposition.
∎

Lemma 1.2
.

If
P^{\prime}\neq 0
, then we have an infinite sequence of pairs of polynomials
(Q_{0},R_{0}),(Q_{1},R_{1}),\cdots
such that for
k\geq 0
we have :

P(X+\overset{k}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}Q_{i}(X)P(X)^{i})=R_{k}(X)P(X)^{k+1}.

Proof.

For
k=0
, the equation is
P(X)=R_{0}(X)P(X)
. We can take any
Q_{0}
and we take
R_{0}(X)=1
. We will prove the propostion by induction. Assume that the pairs
(Q_{0},R_{0}),\cdots,(Q_{n},R_{n})
are constructed, we will constuct
(Q_{n+1},R_{n+1})
. Applying the previous lemma we get that for
U
and
S
in
{\Bbbk}[X]
, we have:

P(U(X)+S(X)P(X)^{n+1})=P(U(X))+P^{\prime}(U(X))S(X)P(X)^{n+1}+T(X)P(X)^{2n+2}.

For
U(X)=X+\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}Q_{i}(X)P(X)^{i}
the eqution is reduced by the induction hypothesis to :

P(U(X)+S(X)P(X)^{n+1})=(R_{n}(X)+P^{\prime}(U(X))S(X))P(X)^{n+1}+T(X)P(X)^{2n+2},

and we get a pair
(Q_{n+1},S_{n+1})
if we find
S(X)
such that
R_{n}(X)+P^{\prime}(U(X))S(X)
is zero modulo
P(X)
(i.e. a multiple of
P(X)
). But
P
is irreducible. Hence
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P)
is a field and the class of
P^{\prime}\circ U
in this field is equal to the class of
P^{\prime}
wich is invertible since
P^{\prime}\neq 0
. So
P^{\prime}\circ U
is invertible modulo
P
and therefore we can find an
S(X)
such that
R_{n}(X)+P^{\prime}(U(X))S(X)
is zero modulo
P
. This proves the proposition.
∎

Proposition 1.3
.

If
P^{\prime}\neq 0
, then we have an injective
{\Bbbk}
-algebra morphism from the field
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P)
into
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{k})
, for
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
Q_{0},Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{k-1}
be as in the previous proposition and let
\phi_{k}:{\Bbbk}[X]\to{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{k})
be the
{\Bbbk}
-algebra morphism given by

X\mapsto X+\overset{k-1}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}Q_{i}(X)P(X)^{i}.

By the previous proposition
\phi_{k}
maps
P
to
0
and hence induces an algebra morphism
\bar{\phi}_{k}:{\Bbbk}[X]/(P)\to{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{k})
. To see that
\bar{\phi}_{k}
is injective, notice that
\pi_{k}\circ\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)=X
, where
\pi_{k}
is the projection
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{k})\mapsto{\Bbbk}[X]/(P),\ X\mapsto X
.
∎

Corollary 1.4
.

If
P^{\prime}\neq 0
, then the local ring
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{k})
contains its residue field
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P)
as a
{\Bbbk}
-subalgebra.

Corollary 1.5
.

If
P^{\prime}\neq 0
, then the local ring
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{k})
is a
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P)
-algebra.

Lemma 1.6
.

If
P^{\prime}\neq 0
, then the family
1,P,P^{2},\cdots,P^{k-1}
of
K[X]/(P^{k})
is free over
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P)
.

Proof.

Assume that
a_{0}1+a_{1}P+\cdots a_{k-1}P^{k-1}=0
for given
a_{i}\in{\Bbbk}[X]/(P)
. We want to prove that
a_{0}=\cdots=a_{k-1}=0
. To see that multiply the equation
P^{k-1}
, we get
a_{0}P^{k-1}=0.
. Hence
a_{0}=0
. Since
a_{0}=0
, multuplying the first equation of the proof by
P^{k-2}
, we get that
a_{1}P^{k-1}=0
and hence as before we deduce that
a_{1}=0
. We show that
a_{i}=0
for all the remaining
i
’s by multiplying successively by
P^{k-3},P^{k-4},\dots
.
∎

Theorem 1.7
.

If
P^{\prime}\neq 0
, then
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{k})
is isomorphic as a
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P)
-algebra and as a
{\Bbbk}
-algebra to

({\Bbbk}[X]/(P))[Y]/(Y^{k}).

The isomorphism is given by
Y\mapsto P
.

Proof.

One has a unique
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P)
-algebra morphism
\psi_{k}:({\Bbbk}[X]/(P))[Y]/(Y^{k})\to{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{k})
given by
Y\mapsto P
. This morphism is injective by the previous lemma. The morphism
\psi_{k}
, is also a
{\Bbbk}
-algebra morphism. The dimension of
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P^{k})
over
{\Bbbk}
is equal to the degree of
P^{k}
, hence equal to
k\cdot deg(P)
(
deg(P)
is the degree of
P
). The algebra
({\Bbbk}[X]/(P))[Y]/(Y^{k})
is also of dimension
k\cdot deg(P)
over
{\Bbbk}
. We therefore have that
\psi_{k}
is an injective
{\Bbbk}
-linear map between two vector spaces having the same dimension over
{\Bbbk}
. This proves that
\psi_{k}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be two irreducible polynomials over
{\Bbbk}
and
k
a positive integer. If
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are seprable
(i.e.\ P_{i}^{\prime}\neq 0)
, then the local rings
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P_{1}^{k})
and
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P_{2}^{k})
are isomorphic if and only if their residue fileds
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P_{1})
and
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P_{2})
are isomorphic.

Proof.

If the local rings are isomorphic then the residue fields are isomorphic. Since we assume that
P_{i}^{\prime}\neq 0
, we have by the previous theorem that
{\Bbbk}[X]/(P_{i}^{k})
is isomorphic to
({\Bbbk}[X]/(P_{i}))[Y]/(Y^{n})
. The "only if" part of the statement follows.
∎

Remark 1.9
.

The condition
P^{\prime}\neq 0
is always satisfied if
{\Bbbk}
is a perfect field as caracteristic
0
or algebraic extensions of finite fields. Hence, the last two theorems always hold over those fields.

{\Bbbk}
is a field and

Definition 2.1
.

For
A
and
B
two
{\Bbbk}
-algebras, we say that a ring morphism

stabilizes
{\Bbbk}
if there exists a field automorphism
\sigma_{f}:{\Bbbk}\to{\Bbbk}
such that
f(a)=\sigma_{f}(a)
for

.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be two finite dimensional algebras over
{\Bbbk}
and
f:A\to B
be a ring morphism stabilizing
{\Bbbk}
.

1)

Im(f)
is a vector subspace of
B
.

2)

If
f
is injective then
dim(Im(f))=dim(A)
.

3)

If
f
is injective and

then
f
is an isomorphism.

4)

If
f
is an isomorphism then

stabilizes
{\Bbbk}
and
\sigma_{f^{-1}}=\sigma_{f}^{-1}
.

5)

If
f
is an isomorphism then
dim(A)=dim(B)
.

6)

Let

be a proper ideal of

, and let
\pi:B\mapsto B/I
be the projection. The ring morphism

stabilizes
{\Bbbk}
and
\sigma_{\pi\circ f}=\sigma_{f}
.

7)

Let
J
be an ideal of
A
lying in the kernel of
f
. The morphism
\bar{f}:A/J\to B
factorising

stabilizes
{\Bbbk}
and
\sigma_{\bar{f}}=\sigma_{f}
.

Proof.

This can be proved as for
{\Bbbk}
-algebra morphisms, we only need to use
\sigma_{f}
and
\sigma_{f}^{-1}
.
∎

.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
f:{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1})}\to{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2})}
is a ring isomorphism stabilizing
{\Bbbk}
then :

1)

The degree of
P_{1}
is equal to the degree of
P_{2}
.

2)

There exist a unique polynomial
Q_{f}\in{\Bbbk}[X]
of degree less than the degree of
P_{1}
(the degree of
P_{2}
) and greater or equal to
1
such that
f
is induced by the ring morphism stabilizing
{\Bbbk}

f_{X}:{\Bbbk}[X]\to{\Bbbk}[X]
defined by
X\mapsto Q_{f}
and
\sigma_{f_{X}}=\sigma_{f}
i.e.
P\mapsto\sigma_{f}^{X}(P)\circ Q_{f}
, where
\sigma_{f}^{X}
is as in the previous proposition.

3)

\sigma_{f}^{X}(P_{1})\circ Q_{f}=S_{f}P_{2}
for a given
S_{f}\in{\Bbbk}[X]
.

4)

For
P\in K[X]
, if
\sigma_{f}^{X}(P)\circ Q_{f}=SP_{2}
for some
S\in{\Bbbk}[X]
then
P=RP_{1}
for some
R\in{\Bbbk}[X]
.

5)

The morphism
f_{X}
maps
(P_{1}^{n})
into
(P_{2}^{n})
and hence induces a ring morphism stabilizing
{\Bbbk}
:
f_{X,n}:{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1}^{n})}\to{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2}^{n})}
induced by
P\mapsto\sigma_{f}^{X}(P)\circ Q_{f}
.

Proof.

Point
1)
follows from the fact that the dimension of
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1})}
and
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2})}
as
{\Bbbk}
-vector spaces are respectively the degree of
P_{1}
and the degree of
P_{2}
. Now, there is a unique polynomial
Q_{f}
of
{\Bbbk}[X]
of degree less than the degree of
P_{2}
(degree of
P_{1}
) such that
f(X_{1})=Q_{f}(X_{2})
where
X_{1}
is the class of
X
in
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{1})}
and
X_{2}
is the class of
X
in
{\Bbbk[X]/(P_{2})}
. This polynomial has a degree greater or equal to

otherwise the image of

will lie in
{\Bbbk}
(
f
stabilizes
{\Bbbk}
). For
P\in K[X]
we have
f(P(X_{1}))=\sigma_{f}^{X}(P)\circ Q_{f}(X_{2})
. This proves

. Since
P_{1}(X_{1})=0
,
f(P_{1}(X_{1}))=\sigma_{f}^{X}(P)\circ Q_{f}(X_{2})=0

\sigma_{f}^{X}(P)\circ Q_{f}
lies in the ideal
(P_{2})
. This proves

. The statement in

is equivalent to the injectivity of

. Finally

imples that

Landscape and trends in postoperative cognitive dysfunction: A bibliometric and visualized analysis
Authors: Wei Gan, Qiaoning Li, Jinchun Xie, Liye Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) is a hot research topic in the field of anesthesia. Despite decades of research, its pathogenesis remains complex, and worldwide research trends are unclear. We intended to analyze the relevant researches on POCD in the past 30 years by using bibliometric methods.Methods:Based on Web of Science, literature published from January 1, 1993 to December 31, 2022 was retrieved. CiteSpace and VOSviewer were used for descriptive statistical analysis and visualization.Results:A total of 2290 articles are included, and the number reaches peak in 2022 with 277. China is the country with the largest number of publications, but its centrality is relatively low at 0.06, much lower than that of United States (0.67), which ranks first. Capital Medical University is the institution with the highest number of publications, while Duke University is the institution with the highest centrality. The journal that ranks highest in both citations and centrality is ANESTHESIOLOGY. The most cited literature is Monk TG’s research published in ANESTHESIOLOGY in 2008, while Wan YJ’s research published in ANESTHESIOLOGY in 2007 has the highest centrality. Research hotspots mainly includes 6 aspects: neuroinflammation, cardiac surgery, delirium, general anesthesia, noncardiac surgery, craniopharyngioma and neuron-specific enolase. Burst keywords analysis reveals that recent studies mainly focus on perioperative neurocognitive disorder.Conclusion:The global research on POCD is growing rapidly. Future studies need to pay more attention to the etiology and mechanism of POCD, and the research on perioperative neurocognitive disorder will still be the frontier hot spot.

1. Introduction

Postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) is a common postoperative complication of central nervous system, manifested in changes and abnormalities in attention, memory, perception, personality, psychomotor behavior, and other aspects. It generally occurs in the early postoperative period, with an incidence of up to 9% to 54%, lasting for several days to weeks, and some patients progress to permanent disease or even dementia, significantly increasing the postoperative hospital stay and mortality, which is closely related to poor prognosis.[1] At present, the diagnosis of POCD lacks a unified standard and mainly relies on the assessment of cognitive function by cognitive function scale. The underlying pathophysiological mechanism leading to POCD is still unclear, and it is generally believed that it may be related to anesthesia mode, anesthetic, cerebral hypoperfusion, hyperventilation, neuroinflammation, etc.[2] Additionally, one of most promising directions in POCD occurrence is inflammatory response induced by surgery. A recent randomized controlled trial revealed that preoperative corticosteroid administration attenuated inflammatory response prompted by surgery, and thereby decreased the incidence and severity of POCD development.[3] Various preventive measures have been tried in clinic, including continuous cerebral oxygen monitoring, hormone use, intraoperative blood glucose and blood pressure control, but their effectiveness needs to be further verified.[4] Therefore, comprehensive analysis of the characteristics of POCD related literature research and mining of massive data are helpful to understand the research status, hot spots and changing trends in this field. Based on bibliometrics method, this study intends to use Citespace and VOSviewer software to visually analyze POCD related studies in the core set database of Web of science (WOS) in the past 30 years, comprehensively present the context of POCD research from different aspects, and provide ideas for the prevention and treatment of POCD.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Bibliographic retrieval

A bibliometric review and visualized analysis was performed. WOS core collection was selected for literature search, including the Science Citation Index Expanded, Social Sciences Citation Index, and Arts Humanities Citation Index. The search keyword was set as “postoperative cognitive dysfunction,” and the time range was from January 1993 to December 31 2022. Literature type was Article with no limitation in Language. Through the titles and abstracts, irrelevant and duplicate documents were manually excluded by WG and QL. If there was discrepancy, it was decided by LL, who would approve the final list of included studies. All documents were exported as complete records in plain text files. This study merely involved an analysis of previous literature and did not involve any personal information or samples, so ethical approval was not necessary.

2.2. Data analysis

Microsoft Excel 2019 was used to analyze the annual publication volume of the included literatures. CiteSpace 5.8.R3 was used to analyze the country, institution, co-citation literature and keywords. The timespan (timespan) was set from January 1993 to December 2022, and the slice length was 1 year. Text processing: term source “Title,” “Abstract,” “Author Keywords,” and “Keywords Plus” with the threshold (Top N% per slice) to 10%. VOSviewer 1.6.10 was used to draw a visualization map, and the number of papers was set to ≥ 5 for the analysis of national publications, ≥10 for the analysis of institutional publications, ≥100 for the analysis of journal citations, and ≥ 20 for keyword cluster analysis. Centrality is used to measure the core position, connection role and influence radiation capability of a certain node (such as a country, institution, journal, document, keyword, etc) in the research network. The higher the centrality value of a node, the stronger its hub role in the network.

3. Results

3.1. Literature retrieval process

After searching keywords in the core collection of WOS, a total of 3102 papers were retrieved, and 2376 articles were remained after refining as Article. 2305 articles were included after irrelevant literatures were excluded according to the titles and abstracts. The above literatures were imported into CiteSpace, and 15 duplicate literatures were excluded again by using the function of rechecking. Finally, 2290 literatures were included. The flowchart of literature retrieval was presented in Supplementary Figure 1. A complete search strategy has been uploaded as Supplementary Appendix.

4. Discussion

The present study included 2290 relevant papers on POCD published in WOS core collection from 1993 to 2022. By using bibliometric methods, descriptive statistics and visual analysis was conducted from the perspectives of publication trends, countries, institutions, journals, and keywords with the aid of tools such as CiteSpace and VOSviewer.

4.1. Analysis of publications, countries, institutions, and literature in the field of POCD

In the past 30 years, the number of published papers in the field of POCD has shown an increasing trend year by year, indicating that POCD is attracting continuous attention from scholars worldwide. China is the country with the largest number of POCD publications, but lags far behind the United States in terms of centrality. Even for the UK, China’s publication volume is nearly ten times of that, but it still lags behind the UK in centrality. This shows that although Chinese scholars pay more attention to POCD, they lack articles with greater influence. The systematic research on POCD in China started much later than in developed countries such as the United States, and the participation in international cooperation was insufficient. China rarely took the lead in conducting international multi-center studies. In addition, we also observed that Sweden, South Korea and Switzerland are next only to the United States, the United Kingdom and China in the centrality ranking, but the total number of articles did not enter the top ten, indicating that the quality of articles published in these 3 countries is high, worthy of scholars in this field to cooperate. A similar phenomenon can also be seen in the institutional analysis. China accounted for 3 of the top 5 institutions in the number of publications, but only Capital Medical University entered the top 5 in the central ranking. The journal ANESTHESIOLOGY ranked first in both citations and centrality, indicating that it is the most influential journal in the POCD field. Among the top 10 journals in terms of citations, most of them are in the field of anesthesia or comprehensive journals, while among the top ten journals in the central ranking, the journals in the field of neurology rank higher, which also indicates that the research published in the field of neurology will be more professional and more likely to attract the attention of relevant scholars.

In the present study, we found that Monk TG’s study published in ANESTHESIOLOGY in 2008 was the most cited literature.[5] This study was a prospective cohort study to investigate the effect of age on the incidence of POCD and mortality in the first year after non-cardiac surgery. A total of 1664 adult patients were enrolled, divided into 3 groups based on age. Results revealed that the incidence of POCD was highest in those over 60 years of age. Independent risk factors for POCD at 3 months after surgery were advanced age, low educational attainment, previous cerebrovascular events, POCD at discharge. Patients with POCD at discharge and 3 months after surgery were more likely to die within 1 year after surgery. This study systematically studied the risk factors of POCD, and proposed the significant influence of elder age on the occurrence of POCD, providing important guiding significance for subsequent clinical research. The most central literature was Wan YJ’s study published in ANESTHESIOLOGY in 2007.[6] The study was an animal experiment that investigated the role of cytokine mediated central nervous system inflammation in the development of POCD in a rat model. On the first and third days after splenectomy, hippocampal glial cells were activated, and the levels of inflammatory factors such as interleukin-1 and tumor necrosis factor-α were significantly increased, accompanied by severe impairment of cognitive function. This study confirmed for the first time that POCD was caused by pro-inflammatory cytokine dependent glial cell activation, providing an important basis for studying the mechanism of POCD, and also suggesting that strategies to reduce the inflammatory components of the central nervous system may be a method to prevent POCD.

4.2. Analysis of research hotpots in the field of POCD

Through the cluster analysis of the keywords about POCD, we found that the research hotspots in the field of POCD mainly focused on 6 aspects: neuroinflammation, cardiac surgery, delirium, general anesthesia, non-cardiac surgery, craniopharyngioma, and neuron-specific enolase. Early researches about POCD mainly focused on heart surgery. The American College of Cardiology and the American Heart Association classify nerve damage after heart surgery as type I and Type II.[7] Type I injuries are defined as well-defined focal brain injuries, such as transient ischemic attacks and strokes. In contrast, type II nerve injury describes poorly understood brain damage, such as postoperative delirium or POCD. POCD occurs in 10% to 40% of patients 6 weeks after heart surgery, and only about 45% of patients fully recover from cognitive impairment 1 year after heart surgery.[8] The risk factors of POCD associated with cardiac surgery can be divided into preoperative and intraoperative risk factors. Preoperative risk factors include elder age, low education level, preexisting cognitive impairment, APOE4 genotype carrier, depression, and diabetes. Intraoperative risk factors include surgical approach, use of cardiopulmonary bypass, duration of surgery and anesthesia, arterial pressure management, blood glucose management, and intraoperative hemoglobin concentration.[4] In recent years, the focus on risk factors is shifting from surgery-related factors to patient-related factors, such as genetic polymorphisms.[9] Patients with POCD have slower recovery time and longer hospital stays than those without POCD.[10] In addition, these patients also have an increased mortality rate in the year after surgery.[10] Neurocognitive function is strongly associated with quality of life and a patient’s perception of their own health, with postoperative cognitive changes after heart surgery predicting cognitive decline 6 years after surgery, according to a 5-year prospective study.[11] Monitoring and optimizing preoperative and postoperative management of cardiac surgery patients is critical to reduce the likelihood and potential long-term effects of POCD.[12]

In the past ten years, the research focus on POCD has shifted from the analysis of clinical phenomena and risk factors to the in-depth exploration of the pathological mechanisms. Neuroinflammation has been confirmed as the core mechanism. Related research has also gradually expanded from mechanism verification to the development of prevention and treatment strategies based on inflammatory pathways. Peripheral immune signals are transmitted to the central nervous system through neural and humoral pathways and activate central resident immune cells, namely microglia. As an inherent immune cell in the central nervous system, microglia have a lineage similar to that of peripheral blood macrophages, and the activation of microglia is a key link in the occurrence and development of central nervous inflammatory response.[13] Activation of microglia produces a series of cytokines that induce a central inflammatory response In this process, the activation of microglia and the specific mechanism of cognitive impairment caused by central nervous system inflammation are the 2 core links, and also the breakthrough point of clinical diagnosis and treatment of POCD[14] The occurrence and development of POCD is the result of multiple factors in perioperative period, which cannot be completely explained by a single inflammatory response theory. The clinical treatment of POCD should focus on prevention and minimize the risk factors that may lead to the occurrence of POCD in the perioperative period. Secondly, targeted treatment measures can be taken from the perspective of neuroinflammation, such as the application of anti-inflammatory drugs to reduce inflammatory response and improve cognitive function.[15,16]

In recent years, the research on POCD mainly focuses on perioperative neurocognitive disorder (PND). Although POCD has been in clinical application for a long time, there are still many limitations, it is not a formal clinical diagnosis of disease, and it lacks clear diagnostic criteria. Therefore, in 2018, experts from various related fields such as anesthesiology, neurology, and psychiatry jointly proposed a new professional term PND to describe all perioperative cognitive function changes, including POCD, that occur before and 12 months after surgery.[17] Compared with POCD, the follow-up time of PND is significantly longer, and the subjective evaluation of the patients’ cognitive ability and the assessment of the activities of daily living has been added during the follow-up period. Studies have shown that PND may be related to perioperative oxygenation, inflammatory response, neuronal injury and other factors. The methods to prevent PND include preoperative evaluation, surgical technique improvement, anesthetic drug selection, etc. In terms of treatment, there are no specific drugs, but some studies have shown that nutritional support, cognitive training and other measures may have a certain effect on the prevention and treatment of PND. Existing evidence reveals that although PND is renamed from POCD, there are still great differences between the 2 in terms of pathogenesis, risk factors, treatment and prevention, so the research on PND will still be a frontier hot spot in the future.

4.3. Limitations

Thers still exist some limitations in the present study. Firstly, the literature search only used the single keyword “postoperative cognitive dysfunction,” and did not include related synonyms or derivatives, which may have missed important literature in the related fields, thereby affecting the comprehensiveness of the bibliometric review. Secondly, the research data only came from the WOS core collection and did not cover other important databases, resulting in a limited search scope.

4.4. Outlooks

POCD is one of the hotspots in the field of anesthesia in recent years, and the research about POCD is still increasing year by year. Domestic scholars should lead more high-quality clinical research to enhance China’s influence in this field, and cooperation with other regions should be strengthened. Future studies need to focus more on the etiology and mechanism of POCD. In addition, the treatment of POCD also needs to be more individualized and integrated, including the combined application of various methods such as psychotherapy, drug therapy and cognitive behavioral therapy. In summary, this study applied CiteSpace and VOSviewer to conduct bibliometric and visual analysis of POCD studies based on WOS sources, comprehensively displaying the development trend and research hotspots of POCD in the past 30 years, and providing an important reference for relevant researchers.

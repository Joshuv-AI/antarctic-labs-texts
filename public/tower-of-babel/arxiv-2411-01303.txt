Abstract
We consider the so-called generalized Harish-Chandra morphism, taking the center of the enveloping algebra U ⁡ ( g ​ l ​ ( N ) ) U(gl(N)) to the commutative algebra generated by eigenvalues of the generating matrix of this algebra, and generalize this construction to Reflection Equation algebras. To this end we introduce the eigenvalues of the generating matrix of the Reflection Equation algebra (modified or not), corresponding to a skew-invertible Hecke symmetry and define the generalized Harish-Chandra morphism in a similar way. We use this map in order to introduce quantum analogs of the so-called weight systems.

4
Quantum weight systems

According to the approach from
[
Y1
]
, we consider the weight systems associated with elements of the symmetric group. More precisely, we consider the maps

{w}_{gl(N)}:{S}_{n}\to U(gl(N)),\qquad{\sigma}\mapsto w_{gl(N)}({\sigma}),\quad n\geq 1,

where
\sigma\in{S}_{n}
, is a permutation and the quantity
w_{gl(N)}({\sigma})
is defined as follows

w_{gl(N)}({\sigma})=\sum_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}=1}^{N}\,\,\hat{l}_{i_{1}}^{\,i_{{\sigma}(1)}}\,\hat{l}_{i_{2}}^{\,i_{{\sigma}(2)}}\dots\hat{l}_{i_{n}}^{\,i_{{\sigma}(n)}}.

(4.1)

Here
\hat{l}_{i}^{j}
are the generators of the algebra
gl(N)
(see the Introduction of the paper). It is known that the element
w_{gl(N)}({\sigma})
belongs to the center of the algebra
U(gl(N))
.
Thus, we have a map
{S}\to Z(U(gl(N)))
. Usually, this map is composed with the Harish-Chandra isomorphism, sending the center
Z(U(gl(N)))
to the algebra
U({{h}})
.

Our immediate objective is to present the element (
4.1
) in another form, more convenient for the subsequent generalization. Let
{\sigma}_{k}
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
be the set of
transpositions
6
6

6

They are subject to the relations (
2.2
) at
q=1
.
generating the group
{S}_{n}
. Since any element
{\sigma}\in{S}_{n}
can be presented as a
monomial in the generators
{\sigma}_{k}
, then the image of
\sigma
in the
R
-matrix representation with
R=P
(i.e. the usual flip) is a monomial

\rho_{P}(\sigma)={\cal P}_{\sigma}(P_{1}\dots P_{n-1})
. Observe that we endow the set
\{1\dots n\}
with the left action of the symmetric group.

Proposition 4

Let
\hat{L}=\|\hat{l}_{i}^{j}\|
be the generating matrix of the algebra
U(gl(N))
. The polynomial
(
4.1
)
can be written as follows:

w_{gl(N)}({\sigma})=\mathrm{Tr}_{(1\dots n)}\hat{L}_{\overline{1\to n}}\,{\cal P}_{\sigma}(P_{1},P_{2}\dots P_{n-1})

(4.2)

Here
\hat{L}_{\overline{1\to n}}=\hat{L}_{1}\hat{L}_{2}\dots\hat{L}_{n}
, since for
R=P
one gets
\hat{L}_{\overline{k}}=\hat{L}_{k}

\forall\,k\geq 2
.

Proof.
This claim follows immediately from the fact that the matrix of the usual flip is
P_{i_{1}i_{2}}^{j_{1}j_{2}}=\delta_{i_{1}}^{j_{2}}\,\delta_{i_{2}}^{j_{1}}
.

The way of defining the weight systems exhibited in the Proposition
4
enables us to generalize the notion of the weight system to the algebras
{\cal{L}}(R)
and
\hat{{\cal L}}(R)
.
Let
z
be an element of the Hecke algebra
H_{n}(q)
. It is a polynomial in the generators
\tau_{i}
:
z={\cal P}(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n-1})
. We define the corresponding quantum
weight system as follows:

{w_{{\cal{L}}(R)}(z)}={\mathrm{Tr}_{R(1\dots n)}}{L_{\overline{1\to n}}}\,{\cal P}(R_{1},\dots,R_{n-1})\quad\mathrm{or}\quad{w_{\hat{{\cal L}}(R)}(z)}={\mathrm{Tr}_{R(1\dots n)}}\hat{L}_{\overline{1\to n}}\,{\cal P}(R_{1},\dots,R_{n-1}),

(4.3)

depending on the algebra which we are dealing with.
According to the result of
[
IP
]
the element
w_{{\cal{L}}(R)}(z)
belongs to the center of the algebra
{\cal{L}}(R)
.

Remark 5

It should be emphasized that the claim from
[
IP
]
and the method of its proof are still valid if a braiding
R
is a skew-invertible involutive symmetry (i.e.
R^{2}=I
).
Together with Proposition
4
this claim entails that the elements defined in (
4.1
) are central.

Applying to the element
{w_{{\cal{L}}(R)}(z)}
defined in (
4.3
) the quantum Harish-Chandra morphism, we get a symmetric polynomial in
\mu_{i}
. Computing this polynomial is a
somewhat complicated task even in the classical setting. We constrain ourselves to a few examples.

First, consider the element
z=\tau_{n-1}\tau_{n-2}\dots\tau_{2}\tau_{1}\in H_{n}(q)
. Upon computing the corresponding elements defined in (
4.3
), we
get

w_{{\cal{L}}(R)}(z)=\mathrm{Tr}_{R}L^{n},\quad w_{\hat{{\cal L}}(R)}(z)=\mathrm{Tr}_{R}\hat{L}^{n}.

The proof of these formulae is straightforward and is left to the reader. These and all subsequent formulae can be established by means of the relations

\mathrm{Tr}_{R(k+1)}R_{k}=I,\qquad\mathrm{Tr}_{R(k+1)}\tilde{L}_{\overline{k+1}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{R(k)}\tilde{L}_{\overline{k}},

(4.4)

where
\tilde{L}
stands for
L
or
\hat{L}
.

Let
n=3
and take the element
z=\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\in{{H}}_{3}(q)
. Then the corresponding element
w_{{\cal{L}}(R)}(z)
is

w_{{\cal{L}}(R)}(z)={\mathrm{Tr}_{R(123)}}\,{L_{\overline{1\to 3}}}\,R_{1}R_{2}=\mathrm{Tr}_{R(123)}\,L_{1}\,R_{1}\,L_{1}\,R^{-1}_{1}\,R_{2}\,R_{1}\,L_{1}\,R^{-1}_{1}\,R^{-1}_{2}\,R_{1}\,R_{2}.

The product of the last four
R
-matrices can be simplified with the use of braid relation:

R^{-1}_{1}\,\underline{R^{-1}_{2}\,R_{1}\,R_{2}}=R^{-1}_{1}\underline{R_{1}R_{2}R^{-1}_{1}}=R_{2}R^{-1}_{1}

and we come to

w_{{\cal{L}}(R)}(z)=\mathrm{Tr}_{R(123)}\,L_{1}R_{1}L_{1}R^{-1}_{1}R_{2}R_{1}R_{2}L_{1}R^{-1}_{1}.

Then we again use the braid relation for the product of four
R
-matrices and compute the
R
-trace in the 3-rd space according to the first formula of (
4.4
):

w_{{\cal{L}}(R)}(z)=\mathrm{Tr}_{R(12)}\,L_{1}\underline{R_{1}L_{1}R_{1}L_{1}}R^{-1}_{1}=\mathrm{Tr}_{R(12)}\,L_{1}^{2}R_{1}L_{1}=\mathrm{Tr}_{R}L^{3}.

Here for the underlined product we apply the defining relations (
2.4
) of RE algebra
{\cal{L}}(R)
. Thus, this quantum weight system does not distinguish the elements

z=\tau_{1}\tau_{2}
and
z=\tau_{2}\tau_{1}
. In general, it is possible to show that the weight system associated with the algebra
{\cal{L}}(R)
does not distinguish the elements

z=\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\dots\tau_{k}
and
z=\tau_{k}\dots\tau_{2}\tau_{1}
. But it is not so for the weight system
w_{\hat{{\cal L}}(R)}(z)
associated with the algebras
\hat{{\cal L}}(R)
.

Indeed, with the same calculations as above we arrive at the expression:

w_{\hat{{\cal L}}(R)}(z)={\mathrm{Tr}_{R(123)}}\,{\hat{L}_{\overline{1\to 3}}}\,R_{1}R_{2}=\mathrm{Tr}_{R(12)}\,\hat{L}_{1}\,\underline{R_{1}\hat{L}_{1}R_{1}\hat{L}_{1}}R^{-1}_{1}.

But now for the transformation of the underlined product we have to use the defining relations (
2.5
) of the algebra
\hat{{\cal L}}(R)
and obtain the following result:

w_{\hat{{\cal L}}(R)}(z)=\mathrm{Tr}_{R(12)}\,(\hat{L}_{1}^{2}R_{1}\hat{L}_{1}+\hat{L}_{1}R_{1}\hat{L}_{1}R^{-1}_{1}-\hat{L}_{1}^{2})=\mathrm{Tr}_{R}\hat{L}^{3}+(\mathrm{Tr}_{R}\hat{L})^{2}-\mathrm{Tr}_{R}\hat{L}^{2}.

This result looks like the classical one. The difference becomes transparent after applying the quantum Harish-Chandra morphism

w_{\hat{{\cal L}}(R)}(z)=\sum_{i}(\hat{\mu}_{i}^{3}+\hat{\mu}_{i}^{2})\,\hat{d}_{i}+(\sum_{i}\hat{\mu}_{i}\,\hat{d}_{i})^{2},

where
\hat{d}_{i}
are defined in (
3.7
).

Consider one more example for
n=3
. We choose
z=\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}\tau_{1}\tau_{2}
. Then we have:

\displaystyle w_{{\cal{L}}(R)}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{R(123)}(L_{\overline{1}}L_{\overline{2}}L_{\overline{3}}R_{2}R_{1}R_{2})=\mathrm{Tr}_{R(123)}(L_{1}L_{\overline{2}}R_{2}R_{1}L_{1}R_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{R(123)}(L_{1}R_{1}L_{1}R_{2}R_{1}L_{1})=\mathrm{Tr}_{R(12)}(L_{1}R_{1}L_{1}R_{1}L_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{R(12)}(L_{1}R_{1}L_{1}(R_{1}^{-1}+(q-q^{-1})I)L_{1})=\mathrm{Tr}_{R}(L)\,\mathrm{Tr}_{R}(L^{2})+(q-q^{-1})\mathrm{Tr}_{R}(L^{3}).

Note that we did not use here the defining relations of the algebra, so we get the same result of computations for the corresponding element

w_{\hat{{\cal L}}(R)}(z)\in Z(\hat{{\cal L}}(R))
.

5
General type algebras and concluding remarks

So far we have been dealing with even symmetries of bi-rank
(m|0)
. As for the algebras related to the symmetries of bi-rank
(m|n)
, the Cayley-Hamilton polynomial for their generating
matrices
L
was obtained in
[
GPS1
]
. Its coefficients are also central and (upon multiplying by a central element) the polynomial can be factorized
in a product of two polynomials with central coefficients. This property enables us to introduce two families of quantities
\{\mu_{i}\}
,
1\leq i\leq m
and
\{\nu_{i}\}
,
1\leq i\leq n
,
which are the roots of these two polynomial factors respectively. In this case the parameterization of the power sums
p_{k}(L)
in terms of quantum eigenvalues takes the following form

[
GPS2
]
:

p_{k}(L)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\,\mu_{i}^{k}\,d_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\,\nu_{j}^{k}\,{f_{j}},

(5.1)

where

d_{i}=q^{-1}\,\prod_{p\not=i}^{m}\,\frac{\mu_{i}-q^{-2}\,\mu_{p}}{\mu_{i}-\mu_{p}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\mu_{i}-q^{2}\,\nu_{j}}{\mu_{i}-\nu_{j}},\qquad{f_{j}}=-q\,\prod_{p\not=j}^{n}\frac{\nu_{j}-q^{2}\,\nu_{p}}{\nu_{j}-\nu_{p}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{m}\,\frac{\nu_{j}-q^{-2}\,\mu_{i}}{\nu_{j}-\mu_{i}}.

(5.2)

Observe that the power sums
p_{k}(L)
in this case are the super-symmetric polynomials in the quantities
q^{-1}\mu_{i}
and
q\nu_{i}
as defined in
[
S
]
.

Again, we treat the map, sending the elements
p_{k}(L)
into the above super-symmetric polynomials in eigenvalues
\mu_{i}
and
\nu_{i}
, as a quantum analog of
the Harish-Chandra morphism in the RE algebras of general type. In order to get a similar morphism in the modified RE algebras it suffices to replace

\mu_{i}
,
\nu_{i}
,
d_{i}
and
f_{i}
in the formulae (
5.1
) for respectively
\hat{\mu}_{i}
,
\hat{\nu}_{i}
,
\hat{d}_{i}
and
\hat{f}_{i}
, where the quantities
\hat{d}_{i}
and
\hat{f}_{i}

can be deduced from (
5.2
) by means of the relations (
3.3
) and similar formulae for
\nu_{i}
.

However, the characters of the spectral values
\nu_{i}
in the modules
{V_{\lambda}}
are not yet computed: so far we do not know the relations between the quantities
\nu_{i}
and
{\lambda}_{i}
.

Summing up, we want to emphasize the universal character of our method: it is valid in the RE algebras (modified or not), associated with any skew-invertible
Hecke symmetry. We do not use any object of a quantum group type, instead we use the eigenvalues of the generating matrices arising from the corresponding
Cayley-Hamilton identities.

In this connection we want to mention the paper
[
JLM
]
, where the characters of the power sums (called in that paper the quantum Gelfand invariants) are computed
in the particular case related to the quantum group
U_{q}(gl(N))
. The main tool in this way is the RTT presentations of
U_{q}(gl(N))
and its affine analog going back to the
Leningrad school
[
KS
,
RTF
]
. In fact, this construction enables one to map the RE algebra, corresponding to the Hecke symmetry
R
coming from
U_{q}(gl(N))
, into this
quantum group. This map enables one to realize entries of the generating matrix of the RE algebra as elements from
U_{q}(gl(N))
. Thus, one gets the mentioned in footnote 2
matrix which meets a Cayley-Hamilton identity with central coefficients.

Note that the characters of the power sums
\chi_{\lambda}(p_{k}(L))
computed in
[
GSZ
]
coincide with those obtained in
[
JLM
]
and they do not depend on
the Hecke symmetry
R
. The only difference is that the quantity
N=\dim V
entering the mentioned characters should be replaced by the degree of the Cayley-Hamilton polynomial.
It is not a surprising property since usually numerical quantities arising in
R
-matrix technique can be expressed via the bi-rank of the initial Hecke symmetry.

Asymptotics for the enumeration of commuting matrices over finite fields
Kathrin Bringmann, Shane Chern, Johann Franke, Bernhard Heim

Abstract
. We give asymptotic expressions for the number of commuting matrices over finite fields. For this we use product expansions for the corresponding generating functions.



In honor of James Lepowsky’s 80-th birthday.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

Let
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
be the finite field with
p^{r}
elements, where
p
is a prime and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. The study of the enumeration of commuting square matrix pairs over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
was first launched by Feit and Fine
[
5
]
. Let
Q_{p^{r}}(n)
denote the number of ordered pairs of (not necessarily distinct) commuting
n\times n
matrices with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
. Feit and Fine proved that
Q_{p^{r}}(n)
(re-normalized) admits the following product-like generating function.

Theorem 1.1

(Feit–Fine,
[
5
, p. 91]
)
.

We have

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{Q_{p^{r}}(n)}{p^{rn^{2}}f_{p^{r}}(n)}w^{n}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-p^{r(1-j)}w^{\ell}},

where

(1.2)

\displaystyle f_{p^{r}}(n):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(1-p^{-rj}\right).

Remarks
.

(1)

The product in (
1.1
) converges for
w\in\mathbb{C}
with
|w|<1
not being a pole and continues to a meromorphic function for
|w|<1
. This is due to the absolute and uniform convergence on compact subsets of the double series

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
j\geq 0\end{subarray}}p^{r(1-j)}w^{\ell}

away from a pole.

(2)

Recall that (see
[
8
, (2.1)]
)

(1.3)

\left|\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})\right|=p^{rn^{2}}f_{p^{r}}(n).

Therefore, (
1.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{Q_{p^{r}}(n)}{\left|\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})\right|}w^{n}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-p^{r(1-j)}w^{\ell}}.

(3)

By
[
5
, (1)]
and (
1.3
), we have

(1.4)

Q_{p^{r}}(n)=|\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})|\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{p^{r\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}}}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}{f_{p^{r}}(b_{k})}},

where the sum runs over all partitions of
n
and
\lambda
has the frequencies
b_{k}
given by
n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}k
.

A natural question is to approximate
Q_{p^{r}}(n)
, as
n\to\infty
. It was shown by Motzkin–Taussky
[
12
]
(see also
[
9
, p. 71, Theorem 2]
) that the space of
n\times n
commuting matrices over an algebraically closed field has dimension
n^{2}+n
. Hence, heuristically,
Q_{p^{r}}(n)
should be proportional to
p^{r(n^{2}+n)}
for
n
sufficiently large. This observation was rigorously proved by Fulman and Guralnick
[
7
, Theorem 2.6]
as a byproduct of their new proof of the result of Feit and Fine. More precisely, Fulman and Guralnick established that

(1.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{Q_{p^{r}}(n)}{p^{r(n^{2}+n)}}=\prod_{j\geq 1}\left(1-p^{-rj}\right)^{-j}.

In this paper, we prove more precise asymptotics for
Q_{p^{r}}(n)
.
To start with, we write, for
w\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
|w|<1
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-p^{r(1-j)}w^{\ell}}=P_{0,p^{r}}(w)F_{p^{r}}(w),

where for
x>0

(1.7)

\displaystyle P_{m,x}(w):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\ell\not=m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-xw^{\ell}},\qquad F_{p^{r}}(w):=\prod_{\ell,j\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-p^{r(1-j)}w^{\ell}}.

We show the following asymptotic behavior of
Q_{p^{r}}(n)
, as
n\to\infty
. Throughout, we use the notation
\zeta_{m}:=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m}}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

We have, as
n\to\infty
,

(1.8)

\displaystyle Q_{p^{r}}(n)\sim p^{rn^{2}}f_{p^{r}}(n)\sum_{m\geq 1}C_{m,p^{r}}(n)p^{\frac{rn}{m}},

where

\displaystyle C_{m,p^{r}}(n):=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}P_{m,p^{r}}\left(\zeta_{m}^{-j}p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)F_{p^{r}}\left(\zeta_{m}^{-j}p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)\zeta_{m}^{nj}.

In particular, we have, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle{Q_{p^{r}}(n)=p^{r\left(n^{2}+n\right)}\prod_{j\geq 1}\left(1-p^{-rj}\right)^{-j}}+O\left(p^{rn^{2}+\frac{rn}{2}}\right).

Remark
.

The series in (
1.8
) does not converge, but should be viewed as an asymptotic expansion. Also note that
C_{m,p^{r}}(n)
only depends on
n\pmod{m}
. In particular,
C_{1,p^{r}}(n)
does not depend on
n
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle|C_{m,p^{r}}(n)|\leq\max_{0\leq j\leq m-1}\left|P_{m,p^{r}}\!\left(\zeta_{m}^{-j}p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)\!F_{p^{r}}\!\left(\zeta_{m}^{-j}p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)\right|=\left|P_{m,p^{r}}\!\left(p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)\!F_{p^{r}}\!\left(p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)\right|.

As a result, when truncated at
m=N
, the error in (
1.8
) is
O(p^{\frac{rn}{N+1}})
.

Example.
Let
p=2
and
r=1
. By (
1.1
), we have

(1.9)

\displaystyle(1-2w)\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{Q_{2}(n)}{2^{n^{2}}f_{2}(n)}w^{n}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
j\geq 0\\
(\ell,j)\not=(1,0)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-2^{1-j}w^{\ell}}.

While (
1.1
) has radius of convergence
\tfrac{1}{2}
, the right-hand side of (
1.9
) has radius of convergence
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{1,2}(1)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{Q_{2}(n)}{2^{n^{2}+n}f_{2}(n)}=\prod_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{\left(1-2^{-j}\right)^{j+1}}=34.738723457\ldots.

Further values are given by

\displaystyle C_{2,2}(1)

\displaystyle=-11716.7651425569\ldots

\displaystyle\quad C_{2,2}(2)

\displaystyle=-11716.3960075313\ldots

\displaystyle C_{3,2}(1)

\displaystyle=7970793.64416118\ldots

\displaystyle\quad C_{3,2}(2)

\displaystyle=7970793.59033743\ldots

\displaystyle C_{3,2}(3)

\displaystyle=7970793.67801128\ldots.

Looking at these first values one notes that they are very close to each other for
m
fixed. A further investigation of their behavior would be interesting.

The paper is organized as follows. First we recall some basic definitions. Then we give a proof of Theorem
1.2
, and generalize these ideas to another Cohen–Lenstra series in Theorem
4.1
. In the final section, we propose problems for further research.

2.
Preliminaries

In the following, we recall some required results from complex analysis. We start with a well-known and simple observation that is still very useful for many aspects in the theory of
q
-series, recurrences, and combinatorics. For
\varrho\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
let
B_{\varrho}(a):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a|<\varrho\}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\varrho\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, and
f
be a holomorphic function defined in a neighborhood of
a
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The function
f
extends to an analytic function on
B_{\varrho}(a)
, but not on
B_{\varrho+\varepsilon}(a)
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

(2)

The power series
\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(z-a)^{n}
has radius of convergence
\varrho
.

For a sequence
a_{m}
of non-zero complex numbers

(2.1)

\displaystyle F(w):=\prod_{m\geq 1}\left(1-\frac{w}{a_{m}}\right).

Proposition 2.2

(Wang,
[
13
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
a_{m}
be a sequence of non-zero complex numbers such that
a_{m}\not=a_{n}
for
m\not=n
and such that
\sum_{m\geq 1}|a_{m}|^{-1}
converges. Then the function
\frac{1}{F}
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
w=0
and we have there

\displaystyle\frac{1}{F(w)}=-\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{F^{\prime}(a_{m})a_{m}^{k+1}}w^{k}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we require the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x>1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\varepsilon<x^{-\frac{1}{n}}
, and
f
be a holomorphic function in some region
\mathcal{R}_{x,n,\varepsilon}:=\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon x^{-\frac{1}{n}}-\varepsilon<|w|<x^{-\frac{1}{n}}+\varepsilon\}
. Define

\displaystyle g(w):=\frac{f(w)}{1-xw^{n}}.

Then

\displaystyle g(w)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{f\left(\zeta_{n}^{-j}x^{-\frac{1}{n}}\right)}{1-\zeta_{n}^{j}x^{\frac{1}{n}}w}

continues holomorphically to
\mathcal{R}_{x,n,\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

The only poles of
g
in
\mathcal{R}_{x,n,\varepsilon}
lie in
\{\zeta_{n}^{-j}x^{-\frac{1}{n}}\colon 0\leq j\leq n-1\}
. These are simple. We compute, using l’Hospital,

\displaystyle\lim_{w\to\zeta_{n}^{-j}\!x^{-\frac{1}{n}}}\frac{\left(1-\zeta_{n}^{j}x^{\frac{1}{n}}w\right)\!f(w)}{1-xw^{n}}=\frac{1}{n}f\!\left(\zeta_{n}^{-j}x^{-\frac{1}{n}}\right)\!.

The claim follows.
∎

We have the following corollary to Lemma
3.1
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
f
be a function holomorphic inside the unit disk and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle P_{0,p^{r}}(w)f(w)-\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{P_{m,p^{r}}\left(\zeta_{m}^{-j}p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)f\left(\zeta_{n}^{-j}p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)}{1-\zeta_{m}^{j}p^{\frac{r}{m}}w}

continues holomorphically to the region
\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}:|w|<p^{-\frac{r}{N+1}}\}
.

Proof.

By (
1.7
), we have, for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle P_{0,p^{r}}(w)f(w)=\frac{P_{m,p^{r}}(w)f(w)}{1-p^{r}w^{m}}.

Note that the numerator is holomorphic in the region

\left\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon p^{-\frac{r}{m-1}}<|w|<p^{-\frac{r}{m+1}}\right\}

(where
p^{-\frac{r}{0}}:=0
). By applying Lemma
3.1
iteratively, the claim follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
be arbitrary. We abbreviate

c_{m,j}:=P_{m,p^{r}}\left(\zeta_{m}^{-j}p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)F_{p^{r}}\left(\zeta_{m}^{-j}p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right).

By (
1.1
), (
1.6
), and Corollary
3.2
we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{Q_{p^{r}}(n)}{p^{rn^{2}}f_{p^{r}}(n)}w^{n}-\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{c_{m,j}}{1-\zeta_{m}^{j}p^{\frac{r}{m}}w}

continues holomorphically to
\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}:|w|<p^{-\frac{r}{N+1}}\}
. We can then write

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{c_{m,j}}{1-\zeta_{m}^{j}p^{\frac{r}{m}}w}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{p^{\frac{rn}{m}}w^{n}}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{m,j}\zeta_{m}^{jn}.

Hence we have, for
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{p^{r}}(n)}{p^{rn^{2}}f_{p^{r}}(n)}-\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{m,j}\zeta_{m}^{jn}p^{\frac{rn}{m}}=O_{\varepsilon}\left(p^{\left(\frac{r}{N+1}+\varepsilon\right)n}\right)\quad(n\to\infty).

Equivalently, we have, as
n\to\infty
,

Q_{p^{r}}(n)=p^{rn^{2}}f_{p^{r}}(n)\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{m,j}\zeta_{m}^{jn}p^{\frac{rn}{m}}+O_{\varepsilon}\left(p^{rn^{2}+\left(\frac{r}{N+1}+\varepsilon\right)n}\right).

The result follows after a straightforward calculation.
∎

Finally, we give an upper bound for the coefficients in the asymptotic expansion in Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 3.3
.

We have, for
\varepsilon>0
, uniformly in
m
,
p^{r}
, and
n

\displaystyle C_{m,p^{r}}(n)\ll_{\varepsilon}\exp\left(\frac{\zeta(3)+\varepsilon}{\left(1-p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)^{2}}\right),

where
\zeta
denotes the Riemann zeta function.

Proof.

By (
1.7
) we have

\displaystyle\left|P_{m,p^{r}}\!\left({{p}^{-\frac{r}{m}}}\right)\!F_{p^{r}}\!\left({{p}^{-\frac{r}{m}}}\right)\right|=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\ell\not=m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{1}{1-p^{r-\frac{r\ell}{m}}}\right|\prod_{\ell,j\geq 1}\left|\frac{1}{1-p^{r(1-j)}p^{-\frac{r\ell}{m}}}\right|.

We estimate both products separately. First, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\ell\not=m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-p^{r-\frac{r\ell}{m}}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+1}p^{-\frac{rm(m+1)}{2m}}P_{0,1}\!\left(p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right)\prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{1-p^{-\frac{r\ell}{m}}}

\displaystyle\ll p^{-\frac{r(m-1)}{2}}P_{0,1}^{2}\!\left(p^{-\frac{r}{m}}\right).

For the second product, we find

\prod_{\ell,j\geq 1}\frac{1}{1\!-\!p^{r(1-j)}p^{-\frac{r\ell}{m}}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0\\
\ell\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1\!-\!p^{-\frac{r}{m}(mj+\ell)}}\ll\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 0\\
\ell\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1\!-\!p^{-\frac{r}{m}(j+\ell)}}\\
=\prod_{j\geq 1}\!\left(1\!-\!p^{-\frac{rj}{m}}\right)^{\!-j}.

Applying
[
2
, Lemma 3.2]
, we have, as
w\to 1^{-}
,

\displaystyle P_{0,1}^{2}\left(w\right)\ll_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\zeta(2)+\varepsilon}{|w-1|}},\qquad\prod_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{(1-w^{j})^{j}}\ll_{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\zeta(3)+\varepsilon}{|w-1|^{2}}}

for all
\varepsilon>0
. As a result, for all
\varepsilon>0
, we find the claimed bound.
∎

4.
Cohen–Lenstra Series and the Nilpotent Classes

Since its discovery, Feit and Fine’s generating function (
1.1
) has been reproved by other means such as the motivic Donaldson–Thomas theory
[
3
]
. More recently, Huang
[
10
]
placed such counting problems into
the framework of Cohen–Lenstra series
[
4
]
. Simply speaking, Huang considered
(generalized) Cohen–Lenstra series
, which are defined as

\displaystyle Z_{R}(w)=Z_{R/\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}}(w):=\sum_{M}\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Aut}(M)|}w^{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}}(M)},

where
M
runs over all isomorphism classes of finite
R
-modules and
R
is a commutative ring containing the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
such that the above series is well-defined
1
1

1

The original Cohen–Lenstra series in
[
4
]
are defined for
R
a Dedekind domain over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
.
. A more general setting was later provided by Huang in
[
11
]
. Note that, by (
1.3
), the relation (
1.1
) corresponds to the Cohen–Lenstra series (see also item 3 in Huang’s list in
[
10
, p. 27]
):

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[u,v]}(w)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{|\{(A,B)\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})^{2}:AB=BA\}|}{|\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})|}w^{n}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-{p}^{r(1-j)}w^{\ell}},

where, as usual,
\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
denotes the set of
n\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
. In this setting, we may generalize
R
to count commuting varieties (i.e., subvarieties of
XY-YX=0
) over a finite field; see
[
10
, p. 40, Proposition 4.3]
. However, these Cohen–Lenstra series in general do not admit a product form and mostly behave multisum-like, see
[
10
, (1.14) and (1.15)]
. Therefore, our method of proof used for Theorem
1.2
does not apply. To work out new methods to deal with these products would be worthwhile.

In the meantime, we can restrict the enumeration of commuting square matrix pairs to nilpotent classes. Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{Nilp}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}):=\big\{A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}):\text{$A$ is nilpotent}\big\}.

In the simplest cases, due to Fine and Herstein
[
6
]
and Fulman and Guralnick
[
7
]
, we still have product-like generating functions similar to (
1.1
). In terms of the Cohen–Lenstra series, the two cases may be written as follows:

•

R=\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[[u]]
– Fine–Herstein
[
6
, p. 499, Theorem 1]
(see also item 2 on the list of Huang
[
10
, p. 27]
):

(4.1)

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[[u]]}(w)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{|\mathrm{Nilp}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})|}{|\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})|}w^{n}=\prod_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-p^{-rj}w},

•

R=\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[[u,v]]
– Fulman–Guralnick
[
7
, p. 301, Theorem 2.9]
(see also item 4 on the list of Huang
[
10
, p. 27]
):

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[[u,v]]}(w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{|\{(A,B)\!\in\!\mathrm{Nilp}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})^{2}:AB\!=\!BA\}|}{|\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})|}w^{n}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-p^{-r(1+j)}w^{\ell}}.

The structure of (
4.1
) is particularly simple, which allows us to use Proposition
2.2
to derive not only an asymptotic expression but even a closed-form series representation for its coefficients. For
w\in{\mathbb{C}}
, we define

\displaystyle Z_{m,\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[[u]]}(w):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\geq 1\\
j\not=m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-p^{-rj}w}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have, for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathrm{Nilp}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})|}{|\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})|}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{Z_{m,\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[[u]]}(p^{rm})}{p^{rnm}}.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
2.2
with
a_{j}:=p^{rj}
. As
p^{r}>1
, the series
\sum_{m\geq 1}p^{-rm}
converges and
a_{m}\not=a_{n}
for all
m\not=n
. By the product rule (see (
2.1
) for the definition of
F
)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(p^{rm})=-\frac{1}{p^{rm}Z_{m,\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[[u]]}(p^{rm})}.

Using this, we obtain the theorem.
∎

Example.
The series in Theorem
4.1
is actually convergent. We choose
p=2
and
r=1
in this example. And let

\displaystyle Z_{m,\mathbb{F}_{2}[[u]],N}(w):={\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\geq j\geq 1\\
j\not=m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-2^{-j}w}.}

We have

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{F}_{2}[[u]]}(w)=\prod_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-2^{-j}w}=1+w+\frac{2}{3}w^{2}+\frac{4}{11}w^{3}+\frac{64}{315}w^{4}+\cdots.

On the other hand, for
N=100
, one finds numerically

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq m\leq 10}Z_{m,\mathbb{F}_{2}[[u]],100}(2^{m})

\displaystyle=0.999999999999999667\ldots,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq m\leq 10}\frac{Z_{m,\mathbb{F}_{2}[[u]],100}(2^{m})}{2^{m}}

\displaystyle=0.9999999999999999998\ldots,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq m\leq 10}\frac{Z_{m,\mathbb{F}_{2}[[u]],100}(2^{m})}{2^{2m}}

\displaystyle=0.6666666666666666666\ldots,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq m\leq 10}\frac{Z_{m,\mathbb{F}_{2}[[u]],100}(2^{m})}{2^{3m}}

\displaystyle=0.3809523809523809523\ldots,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq m\leq 10}\frac{Z_{m,\mathbb{F}_{2}[[u]],100}(2^{m})}{2^{4m}}

\displaystyle=0.2031746031746031746\ldots.

These sums represent the cases
0\leq n\leq 4
. Note that also the degenerate case
n=0
is included.

5.
Questions for future research

We propose the following research questions.

(1)

Is there any recursive formula for the
Q_{p^{r}}(n)
(or quotients of it by normalization factors) that is analogous to the divisor recurrence formula/the Euler Pentagonal Theorem
[
1
, (1.3.1)]
:

\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(3n-1)}{2}}?

In the second scenario there is no exact identity but rather an error term smaller than all exponential functions in the main series. Is there a combinatorial interpretation for these recurrences and the error term?

(2)

Can we improve the bounds for
C_{m,p^{r}}(n)
? What can we say about these coefficients for fixed
m
?

(3)

How could the methods in this paper be extended to deal with multisum-like products as (
•
‣
4
)?
